# A codimension threshold for recovering global spectral regularity

The investigation produced a positive reconstruction mechanism and a matching obstruction:

> **For a rotationally invariant probability measure, strong log-concavity of a marginal obtained by removing two coordinates controls the full spectral gap. Removing three coordinates already permits counterexamples.**
>
> **A stronger obstruction survives near the Gaussian limit:** in any fixed dimension \(n\ge5\), every marginal obtained by removing four coordinates can have almost optimal Gaussian curvature, while the full measure is arbitrarily close to Gaussian in relative entropy and nevertheless has an arbitrarily severe, balanced bottleneck.

Complete proofs follow. Historical originality and independently major foundational significance are **not established** by these proofs or by the selective literature check.

## 1. Precise statements

Write
\[
C_P(\mu)=
\sup_f
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)}
{\int |\nabla f|^2\,d\mu}
\]
for the Poincaré constant. The associated spectral gap is \(1/C_P(\mu)\).

A positive density \(p\) is \(\kappa\)-strongly log-concave when
\[
-\nabla^2\log p\succeq \kappa I.
\]

Here, a codimension-\(k\) marginal means an orthogonal projection from \(\mathbb R^n\) onto an \((n-k)\)-dimensional subspace.

### Theorem A — Codimension-two transfer

Let \(n\ge3\), and let \(\mu\) have a smooth, positive, rotationally invariant density on \(\mathbb R^n\). Suppose its codimension-two marginal is \(\kappa\)-strongly log-concave. Then
\[
\boxed{
C_P(\mu)\le \frac{n}{(n-1)\kappa}.
}
\]

The full density need not be log-concave.

### Theorem B — Failure at codimension three

For every fixed \(n\ge4\), there are smooth, positive, isotropic, rotationally invariant measures \(\mu_\varepsilon\) such that every marginal of dimension at most \(n-3\) is \(1/500\)-strongly log-concave, but
\[
\boxed{
C_P(\mu_\varepsilon)\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
}
\]

Moreover, these measures satisfy the covariance-matched Gaussian moment-generating-function bound
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_\varepsilon}e^{\langle t,X\rangle}
\le e^{|t|^2/2}
\qquad(t\in\mathbb R^n).
}
\]

Thus even optimal Gaussian bounds for every linear observable do not repair the failure.

### Theorem C — Balanced failure arbitrarily close to Gaussian

Fix
\[
n\ge5,\qquad 0<\eta<\tfrac12,\qquad K>0.
\]
There exists a smooth, positive, isotropic, rotationally invariant measure \(\mu\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D(\mu\|\gamma_n)&\le\eta,\\
-\nabla^2\log p_E&\succeq(1-\eta)I
&&\text{for every }E,\ 1\le\dim E\le n-4,\\
C_P(\mu)&\ge K.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Here \(p_E\) is the density of the projection onto \(E\), and \(\gamma_n\) is the standard Gaussian.

The large Poincaré constant is witnessed by a function taking values \(0\) and \(1\) on two sets each having probability at least \(1/3\). **The obstruction is therefore not confined to a rare exceptional component.**

## 2. The positive mechanism: adding exponential energy

The useful operation is not a covariance estimate. It reconstructs the full radial distribution from a lower-dimensional marginal by adding an independent exponential amount of potential energy.

### Recovering the radial law

Set \(d=n-2\). Write the full Lebesgue density as \(\rho(|x|)\), and write the marginal density as
\[
p_\nu(y)=c\,e^{-W(|y|)}.
\]

Integrating over the two removed coordinates gives
\[
p_\nu(s)=2\pi\int_s^\infty r\rho(r)\,dr.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\rho(r)\propto \frac{W'(r)}r e^{-W(r)}.
\]

The radius \(R=|X|\) under the full measure therefore has density
\[
q_R(r)\propto r^d W'(r)e^{-W(r)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Strong log-concavity of the marginal gives
\[
W''(r)\ge\kappa,\qquad W'(r)\ge\kappa r.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(Y\sim\nu\), put \(S=|Y|\), and take an independent \(E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)\). Define
\[
R_* = W^{-1}\bigl(W(S)+E\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

Conditionally on \(S=s\), this variable has density
\[
W'(r)e^{-[W(r)-W(s)]}\mathbf 1_{\{r\ge s\}}.
\]
Since \(S\) has density proportional to \(s^{d-1}e^{-W(s)}\), integration over \(s\) yields
\[
q_{R_*}(r)
\propto
W'(r)e^{-W(r)}
\int_0^r s^{d-1}\,ds
\propto r^dW'(r)e^{-W(r)}.
\]
Thus \(R_*\) has exactly the full radial law (1).

### The reconstruction map is contractive

Represent the exponential variable as
\[
E=\frac{\kappa}{2}|G|^2,
\qquad
G\sim N(0,\kappa^{-1}I_2).
\]
Define
\[
F(y,g)=
W^{-1}\!\left(
W(|y|)+\frac{\kappa}{2}|g|^2
\right).
\]

Put \(s=|y|\) and \(r=F(y,g)\). Differentiation gives
\[
|\nabla F|^2
=
\frac{W'(s)^2+\kappa^2|g|^2}{W'(r)^2}.
\]

But (2) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
W'(r)^2-W'(s)^2
&=2\int_s^r W'(u)W''(u)\,du\\
&\ge2\kappa\int_s^r W'(u)\,du\\
&=2\kappa[W(r)-W(s)]\\
&=\kappa^2|g|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{|\nabla F|\le1.}
\tag{4}
\]
The continuous extension at the origin is also Lipschitz, so \(F\) is globally \(1\)-Lipschitz.

The source law
\[
\nu\otimes N(0,\kappa^{-1}I_2)
\]
is \(\kappa\)-strongly log-concave. The standard Bakry–Émery inequality gives its Poincaré constant at most \(1/\kappa\), and its logarithmic Sobolev inequality has coefficient \(2/\kappa\) in the convention \(\operatorname{Ent}(f^2)\le C\int|\nabla f|^2\). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.3421)

Applying these inequalities to \(h\circ F\), using (4), proves
\[
C_P(R)\le\frac1\kappa,
\tag{5}
\]
and, more strongly,
\[
\operatorname{Ent}\bigl(h(R)^2\bigr)
\le\frac2\kappa\,\mathbb E|h'(R)|^2.
\tag{6}
\]

This is the central construction: **strong convexity controls the increase of squared potential slope strongly enough to make exponential-energy lifting contractive.**

### Recovering angular control

Integration by parts in (1) gives
\[
\mathbb ER^2=\frac{d+2}{d}\mathbb ES^2.
\]
Also,
\[
\mathbb E[S W'(S)]=d.
\]
Together with \(W'(s)\ge\kappa s\), this yields
\[
\mathbb ES^2\le\frac d\kappa,
\qquad
\mathbb ER^2\le\frac n\kappa.
\tag{7}
\]

For a rotationally invariant measure, the radial–angular comparison is
\[
C_P(\mu)\le
\max\left\{
C_P(R),\frac{\mathbb ER^2}{n-1}
\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
This comparison is an existing result, appearing as Lemma 3.2 in Bonnefont–Joulin–Ma; it is not a new claim here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.4621v1)

For completeness, write \(X=R\Theta\), with \(R\) independent of the uniform direction \(\Theta\). Decompose
\[
\operatorname{Var}f(R\Theta)
=
\mathbb E_\Theta\operatorname{Var}_R f(R\Theta)
+
\operatorname{Var}_\Theta\mathbb E_R f(R\Theta).
\]
The first term is controlled by the radial Poincaré inequality. For the second, the sphere inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\Theta\mathbb E_R f(R\Theta)
\le
\frac{\mathbb ER^2}{n-1}
\mathbb E|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}f(X)|^2.
\]
This proves (8).

Combining (5), (7), and (8) proves Theorem A.

### This genuinely includes non-log-concave full measures

Take
\[
W(r)=\frac{r^2}{2}+r^4.
\]
The reconstructed full density is
\[
\rho(r)\propto
(1+4r^2)e^{-r^2/2-r^4}.
\]
Its logarithm has Hessian \(7I\) at the origin, so the full density is not log-concave. Nevertheless, its codimension-two marginal is \(1\)-strongly log-concave, and Theorem A gives
\[
C_P(\mu)\le\frac n{n-1}.
\]

## 3. Why removing one additional coordinate breaks the conclusion

The counterexample has an elementary polynomial density.

For \(n\ge4\) and \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), define
\[
d\nu_\varepsilon(x)
=
\frac{(|x|^2-2)^2+\varepsilon}{Z}\,d\gamma_n(x),
\qquad
Z=n^2-2n+4+\varepsilon.
\tag{9}
\]

Its covariance is \(\beta I\), where
\[
\beta
=
1+\frac{4n}{Z},
\qquad
1\le\beta\le3.
\tag{10}
\]
Let
\[
\mu_\varepsilon
=
\mathcal L(X/\sqrt\beta),
\qquad X\sim\nu_\varepsilon.
\]
This makes the measure isotropic.

### Every codimension-three marginal has uniform curvature

Integrating out three standard Gaussian coordinates uses
\[
\mathbb E\chi_3^2=3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\chi_3^2)=6.
\]
Thus the marginal density before rescaling is proportional to
\[
e^{-|y|^2/2}
\left[(|y|^2+1)^2+6+\varepsilon\right].
\tag{11}
\]

Set
\[
q=|y|^2,\quad t=q+1,\quad B=6+\varepsilon,\quad D=t^2+B,
\]
and
\[
\ell(q)=-\frac q2+\log D.
\]
Then
\[
\ell'=-\frac12+\frac{2t}{D},
\qquad
\ell''=\frac{2(B-t^2)}{D^2}.
\]

The tangential eigenvalue of \(\nabla^2\log p\) satisfies
\[
2\ell'
=-1+\frac{4t}{D}
\le -1+\frac2{\sqrt B}
<-\frac16.
\tag{12}
\]

The radial eigenvalue is
\[
2\ell'+4q\ell''
=
-\frac{P_\varepsilon(t)}{D^2},
\]
where the numerator has the exact certificate
\[
\boxed{
P_\varepsilon(t)=
(t^2+2t-6)^2
+12(t-2)^2
+2\varepsilon\bigl[(t-3)^2+1\bigr]
+\varepsilon^2.
}
\tag{13}
\]

For \(t\ge\sqrt B\), we have \(\ell''\le0\), so (12) controls the radial eigenvalue as well.

For \(1\le t\le\sqrt B\), we have \(D^2\le196\). Moreover,
\[
P_\varepsilon(t)\ge\frac{12}{25}.
\]
Indeed, when \(|t-2|\ge1/5\), the second square in (13) suffices. Otherwise \(t>9/5\), so
\[
t^2+2t-6\ge\frac{21}{25},
\]
and the first square suffices.

Consequently,
\[
\nabla^2\log p\preceq-\frac1{500}I.
\tag{14}
\]

Strong log-concavity with the same parameter is preserved by further orthogonal marginalization. This follows by factoring out the corresponding Gaussian density and applying Prékopa’s marginalization theorem to the remaining log-concave factor. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1206.5098v4)

Finally, the isotropic rescaling multiplies negative log-density Hessians by \(\beta\ge1\). Thus (14) holds for every marginal asserted in Theorem B.

### The full spectral gap nevertheless collapses

Put \(w=\sqrt\varepsilon\), and define \(f_\varepsilon(x)\) to be \(0\) when
\[
|x|^2\le2-w,
\]
to be \(1\) when
\[
|x|^2\ge2+w,
\]
and to interpolate linearly in \(|x|^2\) between these regions.

On the transition band,
\[
|\nabla f_\varepsilon|^2=\frac{|x|^2}{\varepsilon},
\qquad
(|x|^2-2)^2+\varepsilon\le2\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, writing \(Q\sim\chi_n^2\),
\[
\int|\nabla f_\varepsilon|^2\,d\nu_\varepsilon
\le
\frac2Z
\mathbb E\!\left[
Q\mathbf1_{\{|Q-2|\le\sqrt\varepsilon\}}
\right]
=O_n(\sqrt\varepsilon).
\tag{15}
\]

As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the inner and outer regions retain strictly positive probabilities for every fixed \(n\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_\varepsilon}(f_\varepsilon)
\longrightarrow p_n(1-p_n)>0.
\]
The Rayleigh quotient in (15), followed by the bounded rescaling (10), proves
\[
C_P(\mu_\varepsilon)\ge c_n\varepsilon^{-1/2}.
\]

The obstruction is a radial bottleneck near \(|x|^2=2\). Integrating out three coordinates smooths it enough to restore uniform strong log-concavity.

### Even covariance-matched Gaussian linear bounds survive

Gaussian exponential tilting gives, with \(u=|t|^2\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_\varepsilon}e^{\langle t,X\rangle}
=
e^{u/2}
\left(1+\frac{2n}{Z}u+\frac1Z u^2\right).
\]
Set \(A=2n/Z\). Since \(Z\le2n^2\),
\[
\frac1Z\le\frac{A^2}{2},
\]
and therefore
\[
1+Au+\frac{u^2}{Z}\le e^{Au}.
\]
Using \(\beta=1+2A\) and rescaling proves
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_\varepsilon}e^{\langle t,X\rangle}
\le e^{|t|^2/2}.
\]

That completes Theorem B.

## 4. Strengthening the obstruction: balanced bottlenecks near Gaussian

The preceding family establishes the exact codimension boundary, but its inner component becomes rare as \(n\) grows. The next construction removes that limitation.

Fix \(n\ge5\). Let \(a>0\) be the median of \(\chi_n^2\), and choose a smooth function
\[
0\le\psi\le1,\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[-1,1],
\qquad
\psi=1\ \text{on }[-1/2,1/2].
\]

For small \(\delta>0\), define
\[
w_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)
=
1-(1-\varepsilon)
\psi\!\left(\frac{|x|^2-a}{\delta}\right),
\]
and
\[
d\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}
=
\frac{w_{\delta,\varepsilon}}c\,d\gamma_n,
\qquad
c=\mathbb E_{\gamma_n}w_{\delta,\varepsilon}.
\tag{16}
\]

This suppresses a thin annulus around a Gaussian median radius. Its width is controlled by \(\delta\); its residual density is controlled independently by \(\varepsilon\).

### Four-coordinate integration preserves almost Gaussian curvature

The density of \(\chi_4^2\) is
\[
h(z)=\frac z4e^{-z/2},\qquad z\ge0.
\]
For \(q=|y|^2\), set
\[
H(q)=
\int_0^\infty
\psi\!\left(\frac{q+z-a}{\delta}\right)h(z)\,dz.
\]

The codimension-four marginal has density proportional to
\[
e^{-q/2}\bigl[1-(1-\varepsilon)H(q)\bigr].
\tag{17}
\]

The relevant bounds are
\[
0\le H\le\frac\delta e,\qquad
|H'|\le\frac\delta2,\qquad
H''\ge-\frac\delta2.
\tag{18}
\]

Here is why the last bound, which matters most, holds. Integration by parts twice gives
\[
H''(q)
=
\frac14\psi\!\left(\frac{q-a}{\delta}\right)
+
\int_0^\infty
\psi\!\left(\frac{q+z-a}{\delta}\right)
\left(\frac z{16}-\frac14\right)e^{-z/2}\,dz.
\]
The boundary term is nonnegative. The negative part of the integrand is bounded by \(1/4\), and its support has length at most \(2\delta\). This proves the third inequality in (18). The first two follow similarly from
\[
\|h\|_\infty=\frac1{2e},
\qquad
\|h'\|_\infty\le\frac14.
\]

Write \(b=1-\varepsilon\). For the logarithm of (17),
\[
\ell'=-\frac12-\frac{bH'}{1-bH},
\qquad
\ell''=
-\frac{bH''}{1-bH}
-\frac{b^2(H')^2}{(1-bH)^2}.
\]
Also, \(H\) and its derivatives vanish for \(q>a+\delta\).

Using (18), both radial and tangential Hessian eigenvalues are at most
\[
-1+\tau_\delta,
\qquad
\tau_\delta=
\frac{\delta[1+2(a+\delta)]}{1-\delta/e}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every codimension-four marginal is \((1-\tau_\delta)\)-strongly log-concave, uniformly in \(\varepsilon\).

Crucially,
\[
\tau_\delta\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{as }\delta\downarrow0,
\]
regardless of how deep the annular bottleneck becomes.

### Relative entropy and isotropic normalization

Let
\[
M_n=\|\text{density of }\chi_n^2\|_\infty.
\]
The Gaussian mass affected by the annulus satisfies
\[
m:=
\mathbb E_{\gamma_n}
\psi\!\left(\frac{|X|^2-a}{\delta}\right)
\le2\delta M_n.
\]

Because \(0<w_{\delta,\varepsilon}\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
&=
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}}
\log\frac{w_{\delta,\varepsilon}}c\\
&\le-\log c\\
&\le-\log(1-m)\\
&\le4\delta M_n
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
for sufficiently small \(\delta\).

Rotational symmetry gives covariance \(\beta I\), with
\[
|\beta-1|
\le
\frac{(|n-a|+\delta)m}{n(1-m)}
=O_n(\delta),
\tag{21}
\]
uniformly in \(\varepsilon\).

Let \(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\) be the isotropic rescaling. Its projected curvature lower bound becomes
\[
\beta(1-\tau_\delta)=1-O_n(\delta).
\]
Moreover, direct entropy calculation gives
\[
D(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
=
D(\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
-\frac n2(\beta-1-\log\beta).
\tag{22}
\]
The subtracted quantity is nonnegative.

Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small therefore establishes the entropy and marginal-curvature requirements of Theorem C, **uniformly over every \(0<\varepsilon\le1\)**.

### A balanced test function with arbitrarily small energy

Define \(f\) to be \(0\) for
\[
|x|^2\le a-\delta/2,
\]
to be \(1\) for
\[
|x|^2\ge a+\delta/2,
\]
and linear in \(|x|^2\) between them.

The sets
\[
A=\{|x|^2\le a-\delta\},
\qquad
B=\{|x|^2\ge a+\delta\}
\]
lie outside the modified annulus. Because \(a\) is a median,
\[
\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}(A),
\ \nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}(B)
\ge\frac12-\delta M_n
\ge\frac13
\]
when \(\delta\) is small. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}}f
\ge\frac19.
\tag{23}
\]

Throughout the transition region, \(\psi=1\), so the density multiplier is exactly \(\varepsilon\). Also,
\[
|\nabla f|^2=\frac{4|x|^2}{\delta^2}.
\]
For \(\delta\le1\) and \(c\ge1/2\),
\[
\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}
\le
8(a+1)M_n\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}.
\tag{24}
\]

After isotropic rescaling, \(\beta\le2\) for small \(\delta\), so (23)–(24) yield
\[
\boxed{
C_P(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon})
\ge
\frac{\delta}{144(a+1)M_n\,\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{25}
\]

First choose \(\delta\) to secure the prescribed Gaussian proximity and marginal curvature. Then choose \(\varepsilon\) small enough to make (25) exceed the prescribed \(K\).

This proves Theorem C.

## 5. Consequence: marginal Gaussian transports cannot be assembled with uniform regularity

The obstruction is not specific to one proposed reconstruction algorithm.

Suppose, for any finite latent dimension \(m\),
\[
T:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
T_\#\gamma_m=\mu,
\]
is globally \(L\)-Lipschitz.

Applying Gaussian Poincaré to \(f\circ T\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)
&=\operatorname{Var}_{\gamma_m}(f\circ T)\\
&\le\int|\nabla(f\circ T)|^2\,d\gamma_m\\
&\le L^2\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\mu.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{L\ge\sqrt{C_P(\mu)}.}
\tag{26}
\]

In contrast, each marginal in Theorem C individually admits a Gaussian transport with Lipschitz constant at most
\[
(1-\eta)^{-1/2},
\]
by Caffarelli’s contraction theorem. This transport result is an imported theorem, not an additional discovery here. [Numdam](https://www.numdam.org/item/CRMATH_2023__361_G9_1471_0/)

Combining it with (26) gives:

> **Every almost-full marginal can have a nearly contractive Gaussian representation, while every exact Gaussian representation of the full law has arbitrarily large Lipschitz constant—even when arbitrarily many latent Gaussian coordinates are allowed.**

This is a genuine obstruction to recovering global geometric regularity from separately regular projections.

It is **not** an information-theoretic claim that all projections fail to determine the law. Exact one-dimensional projections determine the characteristic function in every direction. Nor is it generic sampling hardness: (16) itself has a Gaussian rejection representation, accepting a proposed \(x\) with probability \(w_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)\), whose average acceptance probability approaches one as \(\delta\downarrow0\).

The separation is between **sampling or identifying a distribution** and **representing or exploring it through uniformly regular geometry**.

## 6. What this establishes—and where the escalation remains unresolved

The positive mechanism and the counterexamples fit together rather than merely accumulating unrelated results.

In codimension two, marginalization produces a survival integral whose inverse has an exact exponential-energy representation. Strong convexity makes that reconstruction contractive at the radial level. In codimension three, the polynomial example already defeats uniform spectral recovery. In codimension four, smoothing is sufficient to conceal a balanced bottleneck while retaining almost Gaussian marginal curvature and arbitrarily small full relative entropy.

There is relevant prior art that must not be overwritten. In particular, the simpler assertion that log-concave marginals need not imply a log-concave full density was already explicitly discussed by Figalli–Jerison. The claims above concern quantitative spectral collapse, the positive codimension-two transfer, and the strengthened Gaussian-limit obstruction—not that older observation. [MIT Mathematics](https://math.mit.edu/~jerison/convex5.pdf)

The main unresolved escalation is **removing rotational symmetry from the positive mechanism**. The scalar energy lift controls the radius, but it does not construct a contractive full-vector reconstruction. The radial–angular proof succeeds because the angular law is a fixed uniform sphere; that independence is absent in the general case. No replacement argument establishing a comparably consequential non-radial theorem has been supplied here.

The selective literature check identified the existing convexity inequalities, radial–angular comparison, Gaussian transport theorem, and earlier marginal-convexity counterexamples. It did **not** settle priority for the combined statements proved above.

**The result is therefore a proof-bearing candidate contribution: an exact radial codimension threshold and a robust obstruction to marginal-based spectral and transport regularity. It is not yet an established original, independently major foundational breakthrough, and I would not record the mission’s completion standard as met.**
