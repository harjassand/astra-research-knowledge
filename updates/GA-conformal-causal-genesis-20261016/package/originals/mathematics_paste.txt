1. Reconstructing the actual obstruction
Write
\[
D\phi=
\begin{pmatrix}
f_x&f_y\\
g_x&g_y
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\c&d
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and let brackets denote normalized periodic averages.
For the entire positive family, introduce
\[
\sigma=r+1,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{r(r+2)}3,\qquad
q=\sigma a+d.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
\boxed{
\left\langle J_r(sI+D\phi)-J_r(sI)\right\rangle
=A_r+sC_r+s^2Q_r,
}
\]
where
\[
A_r=\langle J_r(D\phi)\rangle,\qquad
Q_r=\langle q^2+\kappa c^2\rangle,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
C_r=
\left\langle
q\bigl(2aq-(r+2)a^2+rc^2-2bc\bigr)
-\kappa a(a^2+c^2)
\right\rangle.
}
\]
The raw quadratic coefficient differs from \(Q_r\) by \((r+2)\det D\phi\), whose integral vanishes.
At \(r=1\),
\[
q=2a+d,\qquad Q_1=\langle q^2+c^2\rangle.
\]

The obstacle is now visible in the expression itself. Most cubic terms already contain \(q\) or \(c\), which \(Q_r\) controls. The difficult remainder includes

2. An exact cubic compatibility theorem
For fixed \(\sigma>0\), define
\[
T_\sigma(f,g)=(\sigma f_x+g_y,\ g_x)=(q,c).
\]
Its symbol, apart from the factor \(i\), is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma\xi_x&\xi_y\\
0&\xi_x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its rank changes from two to one when a nonzero frequency becomes vertical.
Theorem — Necessary and sufficient cubic selection rule
Let \(P(a,b,c,d)\) be any real homogeneous cubic polynomial. Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\exists K<\infty\ \text{such that, for every compatible periodic }\phi,\\[-2mm]
&\qquad
\left|\langle P(D\phi)\rangle\right|
\le K\|T_\sigma\phi\|_2\|D\phi\|_4^2
\end{aligned}
}
\]
holds if and only if
\[
\boxed{
P(a,b,0,-\sigma a)\ \text{is divisible by }a^2.
}
\]
The same algebraic condition is equivalent to the stronger absolute estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|P(D\phi)\|_1
\le K'\|T_\sigma\phi\|_2\|D\phi\|_4^2,
}
\]
with a possibly different constant.
Both statements hold for the full periodic \(W^{1,4}\) class. Zero extension into a surrounding square gives the corresponding zero-boundary statement.
The restriction must therefore have the form
\[
P(a,b,0,-\sigma a)=\lambda a^3+\mu a^2b.
\]
The forbidden terms are \(b^3\) and \(ab^2\). These are two independent conditions in the twenty-dimensional cubic space, leaving an eighteen-dimensional space of controlled cubic interactions.
Merely vanishing on the exact shear kernel is not sufficient. The polynomial \(ab^2\) vanishes when \(a=c=d=0\), yet it fails the estimate. The order of vanishing transverse to the shear direction matters.
Why sufficiency holds
The central nonlinear estimate is
\[
\boxed{
\|f_x\|_{8/3}^2
\le C_\sigma
\|T_\sigma(f,g)\|_2\|D(f,g)\|_4.
}
\]
It controls two powers of the missing derivative by one power of the conformal defect and one power of the full gradient.
To prove it, take the periodic Hodge decomposition
\[
(\sigma f,g)=\nabla\chi+(\psi_y,-\psi_x),
\qquad \Delta\chi=q.
\]
Then
\[
\sigma a=\chi_{xx}+\psi_{xy},\qquad
\sigma b=\chi_{xy}+\psi_{yy},\qquad
c=\chi_{xy}-\psi_{xx}.
\]
Second-order Riesz estimates control the derivatives of \(\chi\). A mixed-derivative interpolation estimate gives
\[
\|\psi_{xy}\|_{8/3}^2
\le C\|\psi_{xx}\|_2\|\psi_{yy}\|_4.
\]
Combining these identities proves the displayed nonlinear estimate. The report includes a derivation of the interpolation step using complex interpolation, periodic imaginary-power multipliers, and Hilbert transforms.
Now use polynomial division in the coordinates \((a,b,c,q)\). The selection rule is exactly the ability to write
\[
P=qP_2+cR_2+a^2(\lambda a+\mu b),
\]
with \(P_2,R_2\) quadratic.
Hölder controls the first two terms. For the others,
\[
\|a^2b\|_1\le\|a\|_{8/3}^2\|b\|_4,
\qquad
\|a^3\|_1\le\|a\|_{8/3}^2\|a\|_4.
\]
The nonlinear estimate completes the proof.
Why necessity uses genuine gradients
The \(b^3\) obstruction is immediate from
\[
f(y)=\sin y+\tfrac12\sin2y,\qquad g=0.
\]
Here \(T_\sigma\phi=0\), but \(\langle b^3\rangle=3/4\).
For \(ab^2\), take
\[
\psi(x,y)=-\cos(x+y)-\cos y-\tfrac12\cos(x+2y)
\]
and define, for positive integers \(N\),
\[
f_N(x,y)=N^{-1}\psi_y(x,Ny),\qquad
g_N(x,y)=-\sigma N^{-2}\psi_x(x,Ny).
\]
These are actual periodic potentials, with
\[
a_N=N^{-1}\psi_{xy},\quad b_N=\psi_{yy},\quad
c_N=-\sigma N^{-2}\psi_{xx},\quad d_N=-\sigma a_N.
\]
Consequently,
\[
q_N=0,\qquad
\langle a_Nb_N^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2N},
\]
while
\[
\|T_\sigma\phi_N\|_2
=\frac{\sigma}{N^2}\sqrt{\frac58},
\qquad
\sup_N\|D\phi_N\|_4<\infty.
\]
The forbidden cubic decays like \(N^{-1}\), but the proposed controlling quantity decays like \(N^{-2}\). No finite constant can work.
This proves necessity without substituting an arbitrary matrix law for a compatible field.

3. A nonlocal operation that resolves the difficult cubic
The classification establishes boundedness, but its interpolation constants do not identify the sharp quasiconvexity threshold. I therefore constructed an explicit representation with a quantitative estimate.
Let
\[
P_t=e^{t\partial_x^2},
\]
and write \(a_t=P_ta\), and similarly for \(b,c,q\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\langle a^3\rangle
=
\frac6\sigma\int_0^\infty
\left\langle
a_ta_{t,x}q_{t,x}
-a_tb_{t,x}c_{t,x}
\right\rangle\,dt.
}
\]
This is a cubic resolvent identity: the uncontrolled-looking cubic is rewritten as a nonlocal pairing involving the controlled quantities \(q,c\).
It does not require the energy \(\int J_r\) to decrease under heat.
The compatibility identity behind it
Mixed derivatives give
\[
a_y=b_x,\qquad q_x=\sigma a_x+c_y.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
a a_xq_x-a b_xc_x-\sigma a a_x^2
&=a(a_xc_y-a_yc_x)\\
&=\det D(a^2/2,c).
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side has zero integral.
Also,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\langle a_t^3\rangle
=-6\langle a_ta_{t,x}^2\rangle.
\]
Since \(a=f_x\) has zero horizontal mean, \(a_t\to0\). Integrating in time gives the resolvent.
Thus horizontal heat is being used to resolve a cubic functional through a Jacobian identity, not to manufacture pointwise positive dissipation for the quartic energy.
This differs from the energy-monotonicity route in Bruno–Pasqualotto, whose quartic theorem also assumes both left and right rotational invariance. arXiv
An explicit estimate
For \(\theta>0\), define the convex quartic
\[
K_\theta(a,b)
=
\frac{1+\theta}{12}a^4+\frac12a^2b^2+\frac{b^4}{3\theta}.
\]
Its Hessian satisfies the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
&D^2K_\theta(a,b)[(X,Y),(X,Y)]-a^2(X^2+Y^2)\\
&\qquad=
(\sqrt\theta\,aX+2bY/\sqrt\theta)^2+b^2X^2\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Horizontal heat integration therefore bounds
\[
\int_0^\infty
\langle a_t^2(a_{t,x}^2+b_{t,x}^2)\rangle\,dt
\le\langle K_\theta(a,b)\rangle.
\]
Meanwhile,
\[
\int_0^\infty
\langle q_{t,x}^2+c_{t,x}^2\rangle\,dt
\le\tfrac12\langle q^2+c^2\rangle.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz in space and time, followed by optimization in \(\theta\), yields
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|\langle a^3\rangle|^2
\le{}&
\frac{18}{\sigma^2}\langle q^2+c^2\rangle\\
&\times\left[
\frac{\langle a^4\rangle}{12}
+\frac{\langle a^2b^2\rangle}{2}
+\frac{\sqrt{\langle a^4\rangle\langle b^4\rangle}}3
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\]
This is an explicit, all-gradient compatibility estimate. Strong approximation extends it to periodic \(W^{1,4}\) maps.
It is not the required sharp bound. Estimating the remaining terms of \(C_r\) separately by triangle inequalities loses too much to prove \(C_r^2\le4A_rQ_r\).
4. Why the most direct sharp use of the new representation fails
The resolvent gives an explicit bilinear stress \(S=(S_1,S_2)\) such that, at \(r=1\),
\[
C_1=\langle qS_1+cS_2\rangle.
\]
The report gives its full Fourier multipliers, including the natural projections removing components known to pair to zero.
A tempting sufficient condition would be
\[
\boxed{\langle|S|^2\rangle\le4A_1.}
\]
Together with Cauchy–Schwarz, that would prove the desired inequality.
This particular stress contraction is false.
Consider the smooth compatible map
\[
F(y)=\frac{(1+\cos y)^8}{256},\qquad
f(x,y)=\frac14\sin(4x)F(y),\qquad g=0.
\]
For this field, the specified stress simplifies to
\[
S_1=\frac{F^2-\langle F^2\rangle}{2},
\qquad
S_2=\frac{FF'}8\sin8x.
\]
Exact trigonometric integration gives
\[
\boxed{
\langle|S|^2\rangle-4A_1
=
\frac{159381921467360331}
{147573952589676412928}>0.
}
\]
Yet
\[
C_1=0,
\]
so its actual conformal Jensen polynomial is
\[
A_1+s^2Q_1>0.
\]
This is not a quasiconvexity counterexample. It proves that this fully specified stress norm is stronger than the needed signed pairing inequality.
The consequence is methodological: the remaining argument must retain cancellations before taking a stress norm, or construct a different representation whose norm does not charge components irrelevant to the cubic pairing. It cannot simply apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the first exact nonlocal identity.
This limitation is narrower than the earlier fixed-operation obstructions recorded in N874. Neither type of failure determines quasiconvexity. 
5. What happens near the degenerate directions
The compatibility estimate gives a useful, but conditional, structural result.
Fix \(r>0\). Suppose \(D\phi_n\) is bounded in \(L^4\), its fourth powers are uniformly integrable, and
\[
Q_r(\phi_n)\to0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_r(\phi_n)}{\sqrt{Q_r(\phi_n)}}\to0
}
\]
along indices with nonzero denominator.
Indeed, \(q_n,c_n\to0\) in \(L^2\). The determinant-null identity gives
\[
\sigma\langle a_n^2\rangle
=\langle a_nq_n-b_nc_n\rangle\to0.
\]
Thus \(a_n,c_n,d_n\to0\) in measure. Uniform integrability upgrades this to strong \(L^4\) convergence for those components. The quadratic factors paired with \(q_n,c_n\) then tend to zero, and the explicit cubic estimate handles \(\langle a_n^3\rangle\).
After normalization \(A_r=1\), such a nonconcentrating near-kernel sequence has
\[
\frac{C_r^2}{4A_rQ_r}\to0.
\]
Concentration cannot be discarded
For a smooth compact compatible map with nonzero cubic integral, define
\[
\phi_\varepsilon(x)
=
\varepsilon^{1/2}\phi((x-x_0)/\varepsilon).
\]
The exact scaling is
\[
A_r(\phi_\varepsilon)=A_r(\phi),\qquad
Q_r(\phi_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon Q_r(\phi),\qquad
C_r(\phi_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{1/2}C_r(\phi).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_r(\phi_\varepsilon)^2}
{4A_r(\phi_\varepsilon)Q_r(\phi_\varepsilon)}
=
\frac{C_r(\phi)^2}{4A_r(\phi)Q_r(\phi)}.
}
\]
Concentration preserves the quantity that decides quasiconvexity. It can simultaneously send \(Q_r\) to zero.
Thus a proof that treats every small-\(Q_r\) field as an innocuous approximate shear would be invalid. The uniform-integrability qualification is essential.
6. Actual interacting gradients defeat a one-wave reduction
For every one-phase compatible perturbation, the \(r=1\) cubic ratio satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_1^2}{4A_1Q_1}\le\frac12.
}
\]
The proof reduces to the rank-one matrix
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\s&st\end{pmatrix}
\]
and the identity
\[
(1+t^2)\bigl((2+st)^2+s^2\bigr)-4(1+st)^2
=\bigl((1-t^2)s-2t\bigr)^2.
\]
However, the same constant does not control interacting gradients.
The package contains a continuous periodic piecewise-affine map, specified by rational nodal values on a \(12\times12\) grid. Its gradients are derived from the nodal interpolant, not prescribed independently. Exact triangle integration gives
\[
A_1=\frac{14054717889}{640000},\qquad
C_1=\frac{282507}{80},\qquad
Q_1=\frac{16569}{80},
\]
with both mean gradient and mean determinant exactly zero.
Its ratio is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_1^2}{4A_1Q_1}
=
\frac{17735601122000}{25874735633649}
\approx0.685440862976>\frac23>\frac12.
}
\]
The fully optimized scalar conformal background is
\[
s_*=-\frac{94169}{11046},
\]
and the optimized Jensen gap is still strictly positive:
\[
\boxed{
A_1-\frac{C_1^2}{4Q_1}
=
\frac{8139134511649}{1178240000}>0.
}
\]
This example has one precise purpose: it rigorously defeats a reduction to the single-wave constant. It is not a negative-energy construction.
Twenty-six completed signed cubic-ratio local searches found no ratio above one. Their finite resolution, local optimization, and floating arithmetic provide no upper bound on the true supremum. The rational example above—not the optimizer status—is the exact result retained from that exploration.
