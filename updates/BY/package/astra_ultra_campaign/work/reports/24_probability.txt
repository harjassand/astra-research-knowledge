Probability / statistical mechanics investigator 24
Status: a conditional zero-temperature no-gap theorem was reduced to a concrete PDE inequality. The algebraic gate and a sharp test case were reconstructed exactly; the load-bearing PDE inequality remains unproved here. No novelty or external correctness is claimed.

Target and interface

Consider a zero-field Gaussian mixed even p-spin model with finite covariance mixture

  ξ(t) = a0 t^2/2 + Σ_{p≥4 even} λp t^p/[p(p−1)],

where a0>0 and λp≥0. Write a=ξ'' and assume a(t)a''(t)−3a'(t)^2≥0 for every t∈[0,1]. The exact target is that every admissible zero-temperature Parisi minimizer γ has dγ with support [0,1), equivalently γ is strictly increasing on every nonempty subinterval of [0,1). This is a no-overlap-gap guarantee in a spin-glass regime, rather than a high-temperature uniqueness criterion. Inputs are the exact finite coefficient list (and a proof that the polynomial inequality holds); output is a structural theorem about all minimizers. No random disorder sample is needed. For m listed interaction orders and rational coefficients of at most B bits, D=aa''−3(a')² has degree at most 2pmax−6 and can be built with O(m²) exact rational operations; a Sturm/subresultant nonnegativity test has polynomial bit complexity in degree and coefficient size. I did not implement that checker or claim an end-to-end algorithm for computing γ.

Reconstructed reduction

The zero-temperature Parisi PDE is

  ∂tΦ + a(t)[Φxx+γ(t)Φx²]/2 = 0,     Φ(1,x)=|x|.

Set s=ξ'(t), S=ξ'(1), τ=(ξ')^{-1}, and γ̃(s)=γ(τ(s)). Since a≥a0>0, this clock is well-defined. The PDE becomes the unit-diffusion equation ∂sΦ̃ + [Φ̃xx+γ̃Φ̃x²]/2=0, with the same terminal data. The penalty transforms exactly as ∫t a(t)γ(t)dt=∫τ(s)γ̃(s)ds. Differentiating the inverse gives

  τ'=1/a,     τ''=−a'/a³,     τ'''=[3(a')²−aa'']/a⁵,

where a and its derivatives are evaluated at τ(s). Thus the target curvature inequality gives τ'''≤0; coefficient nonnegativity gives a'≥0 and hence τ''≤0.

Let X solve dX=γ̃(s)Φ̃x(s,X)ds+dB with X0=0, and put Q(s)=E[Φ̃x(s,Xs)²]. The first-variation condition for a minimizer gives

  G(s)=∫s^S[Q(u)−τ(u)]du ≥0,

and dγ̃ is supported on {G=0}. Write H=Q−τ. If (r,t) is an interior gap of supp(dγ̃), its endpoints are contacts, so G(r)=G(t)=H(r)=H(t)=0; the one-sided minimum conditions also give H'(r+)≤0 and H'(t−)≤0. On a constant plateau γ̃=c>0, the unit-clock PDE and Itô’s formula give H'''=Q'''−τ'''. If one can prove Q'''>0 on every such plateau, H' is strictly convex, lies below its nonpositive endpoint chord, and therefore has negative integral. But ∫r^t H'=H(t)−H(r)=0, contradiction. For c=0, the corresponding required inequality is Q''>0; then H''=Q''−τ''>0, so H is strictly convex with zero endpoints and H<0 inside, making G increase from zero and fail to return to zero. An initial zero plateau uses Q(0)=τ(0)=0. A terminal gap is excluded if the unit-clock solution has the boundary estimate 1−Q(s)≥C√(S−s) while 1−τ(s)=O(S−s): then H<0 near S and G(s)=∫s^S H<0.

The finite-mixture algebra is solid. With k=p−2 and λk denoting the coefficient of t^k in a, define Z(t)=Σ[k/(k−1)]λk t^k. Direct symmetrization gives

  Za''−(a')² = Σ_{k<ℓ} kℓ(k−ℓ)² λkλℓ t^{k+ℓ−2}/[(k−1)(ℓ−1)] ≥0.

Therefore aa''−3(a')²=(a−3Z)a''+3[Za''−(a')²]≥0 whenever Σ[(2p−3)/(p−3)]λp≤a0. This is an explicit sufficient coefficient region. It is not sharp: for a=a0+bt²+ct⁴, set u=t². Then

  aa''−3(a')² = 2a0b +(12a0c−10b²)u−34bc u²−36c²u³.

This polynomial is concave on [0,1], so it is nonnegative exactly when its endpoint values are nonnegative. For a0=1, b=11/50, c=1/25, the coefficient budget is 61/50>1, but the endpoint at u=1 is 99/1250>0, so the exact curvature condition still holds throughout. This widens the simple budget region with a proof that needs no numerical scan.

The decisive unresolved step and obstruction

For f=Φ̃x, g=Φ̃xx, h=Φ̃xxx, k=Φ̃xxxx, on a plateau γ̃=c the diffusion identities reconstruct as df=g dB, dg=−cg²ds+h dB, and dh=−3cgh ds+k dB. Consequently

  Q'=E[g²],
  Q''=E[h²−2cg³],
  Q'''=E[k²−12cgh²+6c²g⁴].

This exposes the bottleneck: the third-derivative integrand has a negative mixed term. Convexity g≥0 alone does not yield the claimed strict sign. I did not prove the pinned N04 route’s stronger proposed estimate Q'''≥c²E[g⁴]/1000, nor a weaker sign that suffices in the gap argument. The recently posted finite-temperature SK proof by Lopatto proves a more conditional inequality, J>0 only under the additional conditions N≤0 and Q''=0; its proof uses a substantial moment argument. That result cannot be silently substituted for the unconditional unit-clock plateau estimate needed here. The terminal square-root estimate also needs a proof uniform over the admissible γ under consideration.

A separate exact calculation falsifies a tempting shortcut. For the legitimate mixture a(t)=1+t²/2+t^100/200, D(t)=aa''−3(a')² satisfies D(1)=27701/400>0, while

  D(4/5)=−3/5 +(99/2)(4/5)^98 +(4351/200)(4/5)^100 −(201/400)(4/5)^198 < −0.59999997.

The latter comparison is between exact rationals. Hence checking curvature only at the terminal overlap cannot certify τ'''≤0 on the full interval. This refutes the endpoint-only gate, not the target theorem.

Prior art and significance

Auffinger, Chen, and Zeng, “The SK Model Is Infinite Step Replica Symmetry Breaking at Zero Temperature,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06872, prove infinitely many support points for mixed p-spin models at zero temperature; that is weaker than excluding every open support gap. Hong-Bin Chen, “FRSB in the SK spin glass: convergence to full-interval support at zero temperature,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18032, claims full support for pure zero-field SK. Its arXiv record states that the proof arguments were generated by ChatGPT and that the Lean formalization assumes seven identified analytic inputs; I treat it as current source-reported prior art, not independent validation. Patrick Lopatto, “Parisi measure of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model with external field,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00127, claims connected support and a positive interior density for the positive-temperature SK model with h>0, using a strict PDE moment inequality. El Alaoui, Montanari, and Sellke, “Optimization of Mean-field Spin Glasses,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00904, show that the zero-temperature no-overlap-gap condition feeds an incremental approximate-message-passing algorithm achieving any fixed ε ground-state error with high probability; their iteration count depends on the model and ε, and each iteration is comparable to gradient evaluation. Thus a proved mixed-model support theorem would supply a structural certificate with a concrete algorithmic consequence. It would not itself prove the PDE estimate, computationally construct γ, or give finite-bit/worst-case runtime guarantees.
