Investigator 19 — coefficient-rank derandomization attempt
10 October 2026

Question and interface

The target was a deterministic black-box identity test with a new structural invariant, ideally strong enough to touch general arithmetic-circuit PIT. I tested the most direct candidate: the ranks of coefficient matrices across variable cuts. The oracle interface is evaluation access to an n-variate polynomial over a field; a black-box PIT must output either “zero” or a point at which a promised nonzero polynomial is nonzero. The restricted promise considered below is that the polynomial is homogeneous and has a width-r ROABP in some, possibly unknown, variable order.

The proved structural route

For S ⊆ [n], let M_S(f) have rows indexed by monomials in x_S, columns indexed by monomials in the remaining variables, and entries equal to the corresponding coefficients of f. If f has an ROABP of width r in order π, then for every prefix S_i={π(1),…,π(i)},

    f = Σ_{j=1}^r L_j(x_{S_i}) R_j(x_{[n]\S_i}),

so M_{S_i}(f) factors through an r-dimensional space and rank M_{S_i}(f) ≤ r. Conversely, the ranks on all prefixes characterize the minimum width for that fixed order. This is Nisan’s rank characterization, used here as a reconstructed linear-algebra lemma; it is established prior art, not a new result.

A stronger structural fact gives an actual construction. Bisht and Saxena prove that a homogeneous polynomial with a width-r ROABP has a same-width representation in which every edge label is a monomial. The key argument is that, in a minimal-width representation, the coefficient polynomials selected at each cut are linearly independent. Homogeneity of f makes the suffix coefficient polynomials homogeneous; independence then forces the corresponding prefix polynomials to be homogeneous as well. Repeating this at each layer makes each univariate matrix entry homogeneous, hence a monomial. There are at most r^n source-to-sink paths, so

    sparsity(f) ≤ r^n.

This yields a black-box construction for homogeneous width-r ROABPs even when the order is unknown: first apply a deterministic sparse-polynomial hitting set to the sparsity bound. For t-sparse polynomials of individual degree at most d, a deterministic separating-weight family maps x_i to z^{w_i}; one weight map separates all support monomials, so the resulting univariate polynomial remains nonzero. Evaluating that univariate polynomial on D+1 points, where D ≤ nd max_i w_i, gives a hitting set of size polynomial in n,t,log d. Bisht–Saxena state a constructible family of N=nt log(d+1) weights with coordinates bounded by 2N log N. Thus, for constant r and n=O(log s), t≤r^n is polynomial in s and d≤s, giving polynomial-time black-box PIT. Over a finite field whose size is too small for D+1 points, use an extension field of size greater than D and account for its representation cost.

This is a valid small theorem/construction, but it is not novel: both the homogeneous canonicalization and the resulting sparsity bound are in Bisht–Saxena, while sparse PIT by separating weights is older work cited there. Their stronger theorem gives polynomial-time black-box PIT for a sum of a constant number of size-s, O(log s)-variate, constant-width ROABPs even with unknown and differing orders; they also handle unbounded sums when every summand is homogeneous. Their sum result uses a structural map and enumerates candidate prefix sets, costing O(n 2^n), which is polynomial when n=O(log s).

Falsifier for rank-profile-only invariant

The rank trajectory S ↦ rank M_S(f) looks like a compact fingerprint, but it does not determine the polynomial, even for width two. Take

    f = x1 x2 + x3 x4,       g = x1 x3 + x2 x4.

Both have width-two ROABPs in the order x1,x2,x3,x4. For f, one matrix product is [x1,1] diag(x2,1) diag(1,x3) [1,x4]^T; for g it is [x1,1] diag(1,x2) diag(x3,1) [1,x4]^T. Exact Gaussian elimination over Q gives rank 1 for the empty and full cuts and rank 2 for every other subset S, for both polynomials, although f≠g. I checked all 16 subsets with work/reports/19_derandomization/rank_profile_check.py. This directly refutes the proposed rank-profile separator. The coefficient spaces themselves differ at S={1}, but locating and preserving their orientations from evaluation access is precisely extra information the rank numbers discard.

The obstacle to extending this route is concrete. A natural extension of the sum-of-ROABPs algorithm is to find a prefix cut in one summand’s order where the other summand’s coefficient rank exceeds r. For an unknown order, this means finding a chain of low-rank subsets. Bhargava, Dutta, Ghosh, and Tengse prove that deciding whether a polynomial has an ROABP of width at most w in some order is NP-hard, even for constant-degree polynomials supplied in dense or circuit form; their reduction has ROABP-width exactly 2 plus graph cutwidth. This does not make PIT itself NP-hard, but it rules out treating order recovery as an easy prerequisite unless P=NP. Generic-instance order-finding algorithms do not repair this worst-case gap.

Exact landmark gate and significance

For a major derandomization consequence, the sharp nearby gate is already known. Guo, Kumar, Saptharishi, and Solomon prove over characteristic zero that, for every fixed k and δ>0, if size-s^δ k-variate circuits of individual degree s admit an explicit hitting set of size (s+1)^k−1 for every sufficiently large s, then there are polynomial-size explicit hitting sets for s-variate size-s degree-s circuits. This is a one-point saving over the trivial grid, and it suffices for deterministic polynomial-time PIT for general circuits. The restricted ROABP construction above does not meet that premise: it covers a special class and does not supply the required saving for all constant-variate circuits.

If one instead achieved deterministic polynomial-time PIT for general arithmetic circuits over the integers, Kabanets–Impagliazzo give the standard landmark consequence: either NEXP is not contained in P/poly, or Permanent has no polynomial-size arithmetic circuits. The implication is conditional on full general PIT; nothing proved here reaches it. Recent work illustrates that substantial advances remain model-specific: Shpilka–Tal’s 2026 result gives explicit polynomial-size hitting sets for high-degree depth-four powering circuits under d>r^2, not general circuits.

Status: proved here are the coefficient-matrix factorization bound and the exact 16-cut counterexample; the sparse hitting-set route is reconstructed from known results; the cited class-specific PIT and order-finding hardness are source claims. No new derandomization theorem, lower bound, or novelty claim is established. The next proof gate is a deterministic way to preserve coefficient-space information for all relevant cuts without enumerating 2^n subsets, or a construction that achieves the GKSS one-point saving for all constant-variate circuits. Because order recovery is NP-hard, any successful route must avoid solving the full order-finding problem as a subroutine.
