36 — Unconventional scout: noisy computation must pay to acquire its own robust code
2026-10-10

Finding

I pursued a foundational gap in classical reliable computation: whether a fault-tolerant circuit can get around the known n log n cost by beginning with an error-correcting encoding of its input. The key interface question is who produces that encoded input. I proved a simple, quantitative acquisition lower bound: under the standard independent noisy-gate model, any reliable bounded-fan-in circuit that maps n raw bits into a binary code of constant relative distance needs Ω(n log n) gates, even if each codeword coordinate is only required to be individually reliable. This is an elementary corollary of the Gács–Gál sensitivity theorem, not a new noisy-circuit theorem. It rules out an O(n)-gate “bootstrapping” step from raw data to a good code in this model. It does not settle the separate question of how cheaply a computation can run once a trusted or externally generated codeword has been supplied.

Exact model and derived result

Let E:{0,1}^n→{0,1}^m be injective, and suppose its image has minimum Hamming distance d. For each x and input coordinate i, write x^i for x with bit i flipped. Since E(x) and E(x^i) are distinct codewords, d_H(E(x),E(x^i))≥d. For fixed x, count coordinate changes in two ways:

    Σ_{j=1}^m |{i : E_j(x) ≠ E_j(x^i)}|
      = Σ_{i=1}^n d_H(E(x),E(x^i)) ≥ n d.

Therefore some output coordinate j has sensitivity at x at least nd/m. Its maximum sensitivity s_j is at least that large. If E is computed by a noisy circuit and every output coordinate is correct with probability at least 1−p on every input, then the output-j cone is itself a reliable noisy circuit for E_j. Gács and Gál prove that, for fixed gate failure probability ε∈(0,1/2), fixed error p∈(0,1/2), and any fixed finite bounded-fanin basis, a function of sensitivity s requires Ω(s log s) gates. The whole encoder therefore has size at least

    Ω((nd/m) log(nd/m))

when nd/m grows; constants may depend on ε, p, and the basis. In particular, if d/m≥δ>0, encoding raw inputs into any constant-relative-distance code costs Ω(n log n). The proof does not assume linearity, systematic form, a specific decoder, or blockwise reliability. Full-codeword reliability implies the coordinatewise condition used here.

This is an acquisition result, not a lower bound on post-encoding evaluation. It also identifies why allowing “coded inputs” without charging their producer changes the problem. Given any code E and a fixed target linear form a·x, extend it to E'(x)=(E(x),a·x). The old minimum distance cannot decrease, because the appended symbol only adds zero or one to pairwise distance. Once E'(x) is supplied, the target is one input coordinate and can be read at constant cost. But if E' must be generated by a noisy circuit from raw x, that appended coordinate itself has sensitivity wt(a); for parity, wt(a)=n, and the Gács–Gál theorem alone forces Ω(n log n) gates. The apparent saving has put the target computation into the input provider.

Actual attempt and decisive falsifier

The candidate route was: take a good code, arrange for it to contain the parity answer, then compute parity in O(1) from the encoded word. The extension construction above makes the post-encoding part real and exact. It fails as a total-cost improvement: if the encoder is inside the measured computation, it must calculate the parity coordinate reliably, and the established sensitivity lower bound restores Ω(n log n). If the word arrives pre-encoded, the model has assumed an upstream party or trusted mechanism already performed and paid for that work. This precisely falsifies the tempting claim that coded input alone defeats the noisy-parity lower bound.

The distance-to-sensitivity derivation is the strongest result achieved here. I did not construct a target-independent encoder or an online computation with sub-n log n total cost, and I do not claim a new asymptotic lower bound in the standard raw-input model. The bound is potentially useful as a compositional rule: a reliability theorem that starts from a high-distance code should state the code’s acquisition interface and account for its gate cost. A broader landmark would be a general, tight space-time or preprocessing tradeoff for noisy computation from coded intermediate states, without permitting the code to contain the answer as an uncharged coordinate.

Prior art and status

1. Péter Gács and Anna Gál, “Lower Bounds for the Complexity of Reliable Boolean Circuits with Noisy Gates,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 40(2), 579–583 (1994), DOI 10.1109/18.312190. Primary author PDF: https://www.mit.edu/~6.454/papers/gacs_gal.pdf. Their Theorem 2.4 gives the Ω(s log s) sensitivity lower bound under independent fixed gate failures, for any fixed target error below 1/2. In their discussion of coded intermediate inputs, they explicitly say the n log n lower bound was not known to hold when computation starts from a redundant code. They describe cellular-automaton constructions using a more sophisticated code with constant spatial-width overhead but logarithmically increased depth, and say a lower bound on that time increase or the product of spatial and time redundancy was not known. This is the closest primary-source match to the opportunity selected here.

2. Nicholas Pippenger, George D. Stamoulis, and John N. Tsitsiklis, “On a Lower Bound for the Redundancy of Reliable Networks with Noisy Gates,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 37(3), 639–643 (1991), DOI 10.1109/18.79921. Primary DOI page: https://doi.org/10.1109/18.79921. It proves the Ω(n log n) lower bound for reliable parity, repairing an earlier proof. The later Gács–Gál sensitivity theorem subsumes the parity consequence used in this report.

3. Nicholas Pippenger, “On Networks of Noisy Gates,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory (1985). Primary PDF: https://www.mit.edu/~6.454/papers/pippenger.pdf. It records an O(L log L) construction when a function has an L-gate noiseless circuit, and examples where the overhead is necessary or only constant. This sets the raw-input parity lower bound against the general reliable-computation upper bound.

The coded-input product tradeoff was labeled open in the 1994 primary source. A bounded current-source search found that source and related encoded-computation work, but no primary result that resolves the exact acquisition-inclusive model above; I therefore treat its 2026 status as unverified rather than assert that the question remains open. Results in circuit models with noiseless encoders, externally supplied codewords, cellular-automaton geometry, or different fault models are not interchangeable with the model proved here.

Claim ledger

Proved here: the distance-to-sensitivity lemma and its noisy-encoder Ω((nd/m)log(nd/m)) consequence, conditional only on the published Gács–Gál theorem and the stated independent-gate model.

Known from primary literature: Ω(s log s) noisy-gate complexity for sensitivity s; Ω(n log n) for parity; O(L log L) noisy gates suffice in the general raw-input setting.

Constructed but not a breakthrough: target-coordinate extension preserves minimum distance while making one selected query free after codeword supply.

Unproved: a tight total-space/time tradeoff for computation from a target-independent encoded state, including encoder, state repair, evaluation, and final decoding costs.

Precise next proof gate

Fix a finite fan-in basis, independent gate failure ε below the reliable-computation threshold, a family of encoders E_n:{0,1}^n→{0,1}^{m(n)} with explicitly specified distance and rate, and a target family F_n chosen only after E_n is fixed. Count all noisy gates used to form E_n(x), maintain or repair its encoded state while answering T targets, and produce outputs with worst-case error at most p. Prove a lower bound on this total as a function of n, m, d, T, and p, or give an explicit construction beating the raw-input Ω(T n log n) baseline for a nontrivial target family. The proof must rule out answer-bearing coordinates being silently supplied for free.
