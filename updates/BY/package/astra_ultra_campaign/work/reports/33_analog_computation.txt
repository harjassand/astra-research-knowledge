Investigator 33 — Analogue / continuous computation

Finding: no new physically realizable capability established. The strongest defensible result is a boundary classification: once a computable input interface, finite-time readout margin, and effective dynamical bounds are supplied, finite-dimensional computable ODE computation cannot decide a nonrecursive language; with polynomially bounded solution-curve length and the standard precision interface, polynomial ODE computation characterizes P. These facts are known, not a new discovery. A small derivation below makes precise why raw clock time and “bounded noise” do not by themselves establish physical advantage.

Target and interface. The candidate capability was a robust analogue device that decides a language outside digital computability, or gives an asymptotic decision-time advantage while charging energy, precision, noise, and readout. I model an input word w as a rational/computable initial state x0(w), evolve a fixed finite-dimensional system x'=F(x) with rational polynomial F, and read one coordinate at a computable finite time T(w). For a decision, require an effective output margin: the readout is at least +γ(w) on yes instances and at most −γ(w) on no instances, for some computably obtainable γ(w)>0. Also require a computable enclosure of the trajectory on [0,T(w)] (or a certified adaptive bound) and an effective Lipschitz bound for F on that enclosure. These requirements make precision and acquisition explicit instead of treating an arbitrary real initial state or exact threshold as free advice.

Derived finite-time barrier (proved here under those hypotheses). Take T rational, a rational box K certified to contain the exact trajectory, and B=K+[-1,1]^d. Compute rational overestimates M≥sup_B||F|| and L≥max(1,Lip_B(F)). For Euler step h=T/N, the one-step defect is at most LMh²/2: integrate F(x(t+s))−F(x(t)) and use ||x(t+s)−x(t)||≤Ms. As long as Euler iterates stay in B, the error recurrence is e_{k+1}≤(1+Lh)e_k+LMh²/2, e_0=0, hence e_N≤(Mh/2)((1+Lh)^N−1)≤(Mh/2)(exp(LT)−1). Search for N making this bound <min(γ/4,1/2). Induction then validates the estimate for all steps: each Euler iterate is within 1/2 of the true state in K and so remains in B. The rational Euler endpoint therefore has the same sign as the true readout; its updates are a finite exact Turing computation. Computing the certified bounds and positive margin is assumed effective, so this decides the language and proves it recursive. For a perturbed physical run x'=F(x)+ζ(t), ||ζ||≤η, and initial error ε0, Grönwall gives endpoint deviation at most exp(LT)(ε0+ηT); adding measurement error ρ, require exp(LT)(ε0+ηT)+ρ<γ/2. If L·T is large, required analog precision can be exponentially fine. This is a computability barrier, not a polynomial-time bound: the effective enclosure, margin, T, and L may themselves be extremely expensive to obtain.

Zeno falsifier for “constant physical time.” The polynomial ODE u'=u², s'=u, u(0)=1, s(0)=0 has the exact solution u(t)=1/(1−t), s(t)=−ln(1−t), for 0≤t<1. It traverses unbounded internal time s in finite clock time t→1. Reaching internal time S requires t=1−e^(−S), state u=e^S, and endpoint clock resolution e^(−S). Its curve length is at least the total variation of u, e^S−1. Therefore this time compression makes state range, timing precision, and path length exponential in the “work” S, and the flow has no finite state at the proposed deadline t=1. It does not deliver a finite-resource readout. This exact example is a decisive falsifier of using raw ODE clock time as the complexity measure. The 2016 GPAC analysis identifies the same general Zeno defect and measures computation by solution-curve length instead.

Strongest prior art and exact classification. Bournez, Graça, and Pouly, “Polynomial Time Corresponds to Solutions of Polynomial Ordinary Differential Equations of Polynomial Length” (ICALP 2016; https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.109), prove that a language is in P iff it is recognized by a polynomial initial-value problem with polynomial solution-curve length to a stable ±1 decision; their real-function version also charges requested accuracy through the precision parameter. They explain that solution length is invariant to time reparameterization, whereas naive time can be contracted by Zeno phenomena. This is the sharpest relevant result found: the polynomially resource-bounded GPAC offers exactly P, not a known class beyond P. The proof's “space” analogue is not a physical energy law, however, and does not establish a hardware-level energy/noise theorem.

There is also robust universality within ordinary digital power. Graça, Campagnolo, and Buescu, “Computability with Polynomial Differential Equations” (Advances in Applied Mathematics 40 (2008), 330–349; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2007.02.003; open author PDF https://sqigmath.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/pub/GracaDS/06-GCB-Poly.pdf), construct polynomial ODEs simulating each Turing-machine transition. Their robust formulation permits a bounded perturbation of the transition map and retains a fixed error tolerance in the encoded configuration. This is a meaningful robustness result, but only Turing universality. Their tape encoding stores digits as integers in base 10 (the paper's equations (1), pp. 2–3), hence uses an unbounded state space; it is not a bounded-amplitude, bounded-energy, bounded-time physical realization. Moore’s “Unpredictability and undecidability in dynamical systems” (PRL 64 (1990), 2354–2357; https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.2354) establishes undecidable long-term questions for computational dynamical systems. That is a statement about unbounded-time dynamics, not finite-margin finite-resource prediction.

Decisive counterexample to a stronger claim. The universal-ODE/noise papers do not show a super-Turing function: their machine is simulated one transition after another. Conversely, the P characterization does not show that every laboratory analogue circuit is in P under a measured physical cost model: it assumes an abstract polynomial-ODE encoding and counts curve length/precision, not substrate-specific energy, thermal fluctuations, component tolerances, or ADC throughput. I found no theorem that turns “bounded noise” alone into a net speed/energy advantage over the best digital algorithm. The result sought is therefore not achieved, and a physical-resource comparison remains open.

Possible landmark consequence. A genuinely consequential result would be a substrate-explicit theorem: for a fixed analogue device family, show either (i) a robust algorithm for a well-defined task with asymptotic wall-time and energy advantage after ADC/readout, precision, noise, and fabrication tolerances are charged, or (ii) a no-advantage lower bound covering a meaningful family. The computation above rules out Zeno timing as the missing mechanism but does not settle practical analogue acceleration.

Next proof gate. Choose one concrete device family (for example, passive RC networks solving a specified class of sparse linear systems); state how inputs are loaded, how coefficients are represented, a noise model, voltage/current/energy limits, settling-time criterion, ADC resolution and output size. Then derive an end-to-end worst-case resource bound and compare it against the best known digital solver on the same input family. Until that is done, the honest outcome is “known abstract equivalence; no physical advantage demonstrated,” not a new capability claim.

Claim ledger: proved here — finite-time computable ODE with effective enclosure and nonzero computable readout margin is recursively decidable; the explicit Zeno ODE trades clock time for exponential state, path length, and timing resolution. Known — polynomial-length/precision GPAC recognition equals P; polynomial ODEs can robustly simulate Turing machines under the stated bounded perturbation model. Not established — a new asymptotic physical capability, energy advantage, or universal noise/readout bound.
