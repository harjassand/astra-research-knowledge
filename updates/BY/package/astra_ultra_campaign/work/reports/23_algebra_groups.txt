Investigator 23 — algebra/group approximation
Status: algebraic mechanism reconstructed conditionally; decisive geometric-probabilistic existence proof not independently certified.

Target and source claim

The October 4, 2026 manuscript “A Torsion-Free Group Algebra That Is Not Directly Finite” claims a finitely presented torsion-free group G with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex and elements a,b,c in F₂[G] satisfying ab=1, ac=0, c≠0. Hence ba≠1. The pinned source is https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/A-Torsion-Free-Group-Algebra-That-Is-Not-Directly-Finite-October-4-2026/direct-finiteness.pdf. The abstract makes no hyperbolicity claim; this is distinct from the earlier family-197 hyperbolic-group route. The relevant Astra gate is G-GROUP-RING-PREMISE-FAILURE, which tracks N47/N129 and the citation correction N165. N65’s permutation-rank nonsoficity argument is a separate route and does not establish a group-ring counterexample.

The exact target is consequential: Elek and Szabó prove direct finiteness of K[G] over every field K when G is sofic (“Sofic groups and direct finiteness,” Journal of Algebra 280 (2004), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2004.06.023; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305440). If the manuscript’s theorem is correct, its G is therefore nonsofic, and it would refute Kaplansky’s direct-finiteness conjecture in characteristic two for a torsion-free finitely presented group. That is the potential consequence, not a result established by this audit.

Proof mechanism reconstructed here

The paper’s algebraic certificate is valid conditional on its graph and root-protection hypotheses. Let A′ and B′ be the components of the two labeled graphs containing roots x_A and x_B. For vertices x,y define g_x,h_y as the group labels of paths from the respective roots. The cone attachments make these labels path-independent. Put a=Σ_{x∈A′}g_x, b=Σ_{x∈A′}g_x⁻¹, c=Σ_{y∈B′}h_y⁻¹.

For each common outgoing letter t at a pair (x,x′), make a simultaneous step (x,x′)→(x·t,x′·t), treating the reverse step as the same undirected edge. Along every such edge, g_{x·t}g_{x′·t}⁻¹=(g_xt)(g_{x′}t)⁻¹=g_xg_{x′}⁻¹, so each component contributes a constant group element to ab. The degree at (x,x′) is |S_x∩S_x′|. By the prescribed parities, every vertex except (x_A,x_A) has odd degree; that root pair has even degree. Handshaking makes every non-root component have even cardinality, while the root component has odd cardinality. Summing in characteristic two leaves just the root contribution 1. For A′×B′ every degree |S_x∩S_y| is odd, so every component has even size and ac=0. Root protection says h_y≠1 for y≠x_B, making the identity coefficient of c exactly one, hence c≠0. Finally ba=1 would imply c=(ba)c=b(ac)=0. This confirms the finite algebraic implication; it does not establish the needed graph exists.

I also checked the manuscript’s weighted contraction numerically with exact rational arithmetic. With q=128, v=q²+q+1=16513, and p=(q+1)/v=129/16513, its nonnegative transition matrix M has a positive vector f for which the manuscript’s two row bounds are
q/(q+1)+7p²+21/(q+1)² = 71589573988/72026254783 ≈ 0.9939372
and
[6/4+(v+21)p²]/4 = 195474585/311633336 ≈ 0.6272583.
Thus Mf≤λf for λ=0.9939372…<1, and the claimed exponential decay of squared word weights follows. This local estimate is sound and useful, but it is one input to the main graph-exclusion theorem.

The indispensable unresolved step

The claimed group comes from random matchings on two highly regular labeled graphs. The authors prescribe label-intersection parities, condition on logarithmic girth, prove a bounded-path first-moment estimate from the weighted contraction, and use a planar separator to reduce any spherical arrangement to a forbidden bounded system. They then infer absence of spherical arrangements, asphericity, root protection, and torsion-freeness. I reviewed the proof’s architecture and several central counts, including the girth-conditioning switch, the stage/multiplicity bookkeeping, and the parity criterion. I found no decisive defect in this pass. I did not independently verify the full uniform bounded-path estimate or the planar extraction and cone-surgery argument; those are the theorem’s decisive steps, and the proof is too long and interdependent to certify from the sampled checks.

The theorem selects a matching outcome existentially for sufficiently large m≡1 mod 4, then defines the presentation and algebra sums from that outcome. The artifact does not supply a concrete finite matching table, a specific value of m with a verified outcome, or a runnable certificate for “no reduced spherical arrangement.” This does not invalidate a correct existence proof, but it means no explicit witness has been acquired in this investigation. The acquisition interface is: choose the fixed projective-plane/Fano label types; sample the prescribed bijections on A and B; obtain a matching whose labeled graph has girth and no reduced spherical arrangement; attach cones; output the finite presentation and the three sums. The source proves asymptotic existence rather than furnishing that output or a practical recognition procedure. In particular, I cannot offer a finite example or verify the advertised presentation and coefficients.

Prior-source boundary and status

Formanek’s “Idempotents in Noetherian Group Rings” (Canadian Journal of Mathematics 25 (1973), https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/25AF425E2069C808258EA860D2329372/S0008414X00050392a.pdf/idempotents-in-noetherian-group-rings.pdf) states its torsion-free Noetherian idempotent theorem in characteristic zero. Burger–Valette’s “Idempotents in complex group rings: theorems of Zalesskii and Bass revisited” (https://people.math.ethz.ch/~burger/pub/1998_Idempotents.pdf) includes a positive-characteristic trace restriction, but that trace conclusion alone does not exclude the manuscript’s zero-trace one-sided-inverse witness. I therefore found no citation-based refutation. The candidate group in this paper is not claimed hyperbolic, so the separate hyperbolic-group idempotent literature does not directly settle it.

Unproved in this audit: the central uniform random-graph exclusion and its topological consequences. Not acquired: a specific finite presentation and explicit coefficient support.
