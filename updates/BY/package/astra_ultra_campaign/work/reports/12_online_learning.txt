ONLINE SCIENTIFIC LEARNING UNDER ADAPTIVE SHIFTS
Investigator 12 — 10 October 2026

Finding

A useful reusable guarantee is possible when the learner receives complete per-example feedback for a supplied bank of scientific models and is given a transition prior over which model changes are plausible. It tolerates arbitrary, even adaptively chosen, bounded loss vectors and charges a shift according to its prior surprisal. This is a precise information structure, but the theorem is a reconstruction of established fixed-share / Markov-Hedge results, not a new advance. It guarantees sequence regret against a model path in the bank; it does not guarantee correctness on unseen inputs or discovery of a model outside the bank.

Target and interface

Let there be N supplied predictors. On round t, the learner sees the current input x_t, obtains each model's prediction, chooses a distribution p_t over models, and then receives a label y_t from which it can compute every loss ℓ_t(i) in [0,1]. The environment may choose x_t and y_t using the past and the announced p_t. It must not observe a private draw of one model before choosing y_t; equivalently, score the learner by the mixture loss p_t·ℓ_t, or output a convex mixture under a convex loss. This distinction matters when the environment can respond to the learner's realized action.

Before round t the learner also knows a transition kernel Q_t(j|i), supplied by the application, and an initial distribution π. These encode admissible model changes: a forbidden transition has probability zero and cannot be part of the comparator. A scientific instance could use a graph of mechanisms or instrument configurations, with transition probabilities reflecting which changes are plausible. Every round requires evaluating all N models on the observed input and scoring all N predictions after the label arrives. Given a sparse transition graph E, the aggregation update costs O(N+|E|) besides model evaluation and stores O(N) weights. If only the selected experiment's outcome is observed, this full-information interface is absent and the result does not apply.

Reconstructed guarantee

For a model path u=(u_1,...,u_T), define its prior probability

  q(u) = π(u_1) product_{t=2}^T Q_t(u_t | u_{t-1}),
  C(u) = log(1/q(u)).

Exponential weights over paths, updated after each full loss vector, can be implemented as a forward recursion on the N model states. For every loss sequence, including one selected adaptively after each announced mixture, and every path with q(u)>0, the learner's cumulative mixture loss satisfies

  L_A - sum_t ℓ_t(u_t) <= C(u)/η + η T/8,       η > 0.

Proof. Let Z_t be the prior-weighted sum over paths of exp(-η times their losses through t), and let p_t be the next-state marginal under the posterior formed from losses through t-1. Then Z_t/Z_{t-1} = sum_i p_t(i) exp(-ηℓ_t(i)). Hoeffding's lemma for a [0,1]-valued loss gives log(Z_t/Z_{t-1}) <= -η p_t·ℓ_t + η²/8. Summing gives log Z_T <= -η L_A + η²T/8. The one comparator path contributes q(u)exp(-η sum_t ℓ_t(u_t)) to Z_T, so log Z_T >= -C(u)-η sum_t ℓ_t(u_t). Combining yields the stated inequality. No stationarity, independence, or fixed change times enter this pathwise proof.

For a graph with at most d allowed new states from each state, take Q(i|i)=1-ρ and distribute ρ uniformly over at most d outgoing changes. A path with S changes then has

  C(u) <= log N + (T-1-S)log(1/(1-ρ)) + S log(d/ρ).

For known C(u), choosing η=sqrt(8C(u)/T) gives regret at most sqrt(T C(u)/2). Thus a sparse transition graph can reduce the cost per change from dependence on all N models to dependence on the number of plausible next mechanisms. This is a pathwise comparison guarantee, not a claim that the learner identifies the true mechanism. An unknown S or unknown transition kernel requires an additional meta-algorithm or a valid prior; the bound does not grant that adaptivity for free.

Adversarial challenge and boundary

Finite VC dimension by itself is insufficient for robust sequential generalization when the adversary selects inputs after seeing the learner. For thresholds h_θ(x)=1{x>=θ} on the real line, maintain an interval of θ values consistent with all labels so far. Query x at the interval midpoint. Both labels preserve a nonempty half-interval; a deterministic learner can be given the opposite label and forced to err, while for a randomized binary prediction one of the two consistent labels has expected error at least 1/2. Repeating yields T errors while the entire sequence remains realizable by one threshold, a class of VC dimension 1. This is why a claim about arbitrary shifts on unseen inputs needs an input-process or hypothesis-class restriction, or feedback that directly tests the candidate models.

A stronger known information structure is the “dynamic changing process” of Wu, Grama, and Szpankowski. The covariates form a stochastic process whose conditional marginal at each round, given past covariates, is one of at most K distributions along almost every sample path; the distributions are unknown and may be arbitrary, and labels are adversarial after each covariate. For finite-VC binary H of dimension d and bounded convex loss, they prove expected worst-case regret O(sqrt(K T d log T)) under a stated range condition K^3 d <= O(T^(1-epsilon)/log T), with constants depending linearly on 1/epsilon. They also give a lower bound of order sqrt(K d T) in its stated parameter range, and a mixable/log-loss bound of order K d polylog(T). Their definition makes the covariate process a stochastic process chosen independently of the current prediction; it does not cover the threshold adversary above, which chooses the current context from the learner's current response. This result is directly relevant prior art for unknown-distribution adaptation, and already supplies a much broader guarantee than a finite model bank under its process restriction.

Prior-art check

Herbster and Warmuth, “Tracking the Best Expert,” Machine Learning 32 (1998), established tracking regret for piecewise-constant expert paths, including O(S log N + S log(T/S)) dependence for S switches. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007424614876

Mourtada and Maillard, “Efficient Tracking of a Growing Number of Experts,” ALT 2017, PMLR 76, develops efficient shifting/sparse-shifting regret algorithms; its discussion of MarkovHedge treats aggregation over expert paths. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v76/mourtada17a.html

Daniely, Gonen, and Shalev-Shwartz, “Strongly Adaptive Online Learning,” ICML 2015, gives guarantees on every time interval, so local adaptation is also established prior art. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/daniely15.html

Wu, Grama, and Szpankowski, “Online Learning in Dynamically Changing Environments,” COLT 2023, PMLR 195, proves the finite-VC / K-distinct-conditional-marginal result described above, including a matching-order lower bound. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/wu23a.html

Claim ledger and significance

Proved here: the path-prior regret inequality follows by a telescoping exponential-weights potential; the threshold adversary gives an explicit obstruction to finite-VC generalization against input selection based on the learner's current behavior.

Known: fixed-share/Markov-Hedge tracking, strongly adaptive interval regret, and distribution-blind regret under a bounded number of distinct conditional marginals. The proposed graph-prior theorem is not novel.

Conditional: a scientific deployment can claim the path guarantee only if it supplies the expert bank and transition kernel, evaluates all candidate losses from each arriving outcome, and reports regret against paths in the prior's support. It can claim a distribution-level result only with additional sampling assumptions such as the precise process restriction above.

Not established: calibration or risk on future inputs, adaptation to a model absent from the bank, causal identification under interventions, or a single guarantee combining unknown finite-VC hypotheses, adaptive mechanism changes, partial feedback, and arbitrary shifts.

Potential consequence: if a scientific application has a validated finite set of mechanisms, cheap counterfactual scoring on each observed outcome, and a defensible sparse transition graph, the path code gives a reusable post-shift performance contract that survives adversarially selected bounded data. Achieved significance is a precise known guarantee and a boundary diagnosis, not a foundational advance.

Next proof gate: define the minimax expected regret for finite-VC H under the K-distinct-conditional-marginal process class when the comparator is allowed S switches among H. Derive matching upper and lower bounds as functions of (T,K,d,S), or construct a realizable counterexample showing the proposed dynamic comparator cannot have sublinear regret under that information structure. This would test whether model-path coding and distribution-blind sequential covers compose, rather than assume that they do.
