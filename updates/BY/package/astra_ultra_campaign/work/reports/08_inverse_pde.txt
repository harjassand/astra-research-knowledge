Inverse boundary measurements: adaptive control cannot remove the worst-case instability
10 October 2026

Finding

For the Schrödinger inverse boundary problem, selecting boundary excitations adaptively cannot uniformly repair the exponential instability on a smooth, infinite-dimensional prior class, even if the instrument returns the entire Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DN) map at every energy in a fixed finite union of regular energy intervals. The obstruction is already a theorem of Isaev extending Mandache. I derived its consequence for bounded, noisy, adaptive measurement protocols and independently checked the local attenuation mechanism in the unit disk. This is an obstruction and a sharper acquisition-cost statement, not a new inverse theorem.

Interface and prior class

On the unit ball D in R^d, d >= 2, consider

    (-Delta + q - E)u = 0 in D,   u|_boundary D = f,
    Phi_q(E) f = partial_nu u|_boundary D.

Let S be any fixed finite union of sigma-regular energy intervals: for every E in S and every potential in the stated bounded class, E stays away from the Dirichlet spectrum. Take a smooth base q0 supported in B(0,1/3), with ||q0||_infinity <= sigma/3. Isaev's Theorem 2.3 says that, for each smoothness index m and each s >= 0, there are constants beta,c>0 such that for every sufficiently small epsilon there are real C^m potentials q1,q2, also supported in B(0,1/3), with

    ||q1-q2||_infinity >= epsilon,
    ||qi-q0||_infinity <= epsilon,
    ||qi-q0||_{C^m} <= beta,
    sup_{E in S} ||Phi_q1(E)-Phi_q2(E)||_{H^{-s}->H^s}
        <= exp(-c epsilon^(-1/(2m))).

The source states c=1 in its normalization and notes that any chosen beta can be accommodated by changing c and restricting epsilon. At s=1/2 the last, stronger smoothing norm bounds the usual H^{1/2}->H^{-1/2} DN difference by continuous Sobolev embeddings. This is a pair of admissible media whose full data are exponentially closer than the media themselves. In particular, the conclusion already includes every spatial boundary harmonic and every energy in S; it is not a statement about a poor fixed selection of probes.

Adaptive and noisy acquisition consequence (derived here)

At round j, let a protocol choose E_j in S and Dirichlet data f_j after seeing earlier outputs, with ||f_j||_{H^{1/2}} <= A. It may record the full normal trace, or any scalar projection y_j = <g_j,Phi_q(E_j)f_j> with ||g_j||_{H^{1/2}} <= 1. The pair above gives, uniformly over every such choice and every prior transcript,

    |y_j(q1)-y_j(q2)| <= C A exp(-c epsilon^(-1/(2m))).

For deterministic noise bounded by delta in the H^{-1/2} output norm (or by delta per scalar reading), if the right side is at most 2 delta, an adversary can return the midpoint of the two predicted outputs under both potentials. Inductively, both worlds then produce the same transcript, including under adaptive choice of later E_j and f_j. No estimator can guarantee L-infinity error below epsilon/2 on this class.

For independent Gaussian readout noise with variance sigma_n^2 per scalar measurement, the chain rule for relative entropy gives, for N adaptive readings,

    KL(transcript under q1 || transcript under q2)
       <= N C^2 A^2 exp(-2c epsilon^(-1/(2m))) / (2 sigma_n^2).

The bound holds conditionally at each shared transcript, so adaptation does not change it. If this quantity tends to zero, Pinsker's inequality makes the two transcript laws indistinguishable in total variation. Thus reliable worst-case recovery at separation epsilon requires, up to constants, either

    A >= (sigma_n/sqrt(N)) exp(c epsilon^(-1/(2m)))

or a comparable exponential increase in independent shots. In the bounded-noise model, one query requires A >= (delta/C)exp(c epsilon^(-1/(2m))). These are lower bounds on the acquisition budget, not proposed costs of a particular device.

The spatial-frequency cost is explicit in the boundary norm: a Fourier/spherical-harmonic mode of degree n with coefficient a has H^{1/2} size proportional to sqrt(1+n)|a|, which is the trace scale controlled by bulk Dirichlet energy. Isaev's theorem controls the full operator in a norm that includes all such modes, so no choice of arbitrarily high spatial boundary frequency defeats the pair at fixed H^{1/2} actuation. It also covers a continuum of measurements over each bounded regular energy interval, in the sup-over-E operator norm. It does not cover an unbounded energy range or a protocol whose actuation norm grows without limit as energy increases; those would need their own noise and energy accounting.

Local mechanism checked directly

At E=0 and q=0, differentiating the Alessandrini identity gives

    <g, D Phi_0[h] f> = integral_D h u_g u_f dx,

where u_f,u_g are harmonic extensions of the boundary data. In the unit disk write f(theta)=sum f_n exp(in theta), so u_f(r,theta)=sum f_n r^{|n|}exp(in theta), and similarly for g. Fix 0<rho<1 and a smooth radial a supported in an annulus inside r<=rho. For h_k(r,theta)=a(r)cos(k theta), its L^2 norm is independent of k, while only pairs n+m=+/-k contribute to the integral. For each such pair, |n|+|m|>=k, hence

    |integral_0^rho a(r) r^{|n|+|m|+1} dr|
       <= ||a||_infinity rho^(k+2)/(k+2).

Cauchy-Schwarz on each convolution slice gives

    ||D Phi_0[h_k]||_{H^{1/2}->H^{-1/2}}
       <= C ||a||_infinity rho^(k+2)/(k+2).

This proves exponential loss of first-order sensitivity to fixed-size angular perturbations supported away from the boundary, even when all boundary modes are allowed. It is only a derivative estimate: by itself it does not bound the nonlinear DN difference for a fixed perturbation amplitude, because nonlinear terms may contribute lower angular modes. Isaev's energy-interval theorem supplies the finite-amplitude nonlinear obstruction used above.

Strongest constructive alternative and its cost boundary

For d>=3, Alberti and Santacesaria prove that if q is known to lie in a specified finite-dimensional linear subspace W of L-infinity and ||q||_infinity<=R, then a finite number N of boundary excitations gives uniqueness and a Lipschitz estimate. Their Theorem 2 has the form

    ||q2-q1||_{L^2(D)} <= exp(C N^(1/2+alpha))
          ||(Phi_q2 f_l-Phi_q1 f_l)_{l=1}^N||_{H^{-1/2}(boundary D)^N}.

Here C depends on the domain, R and alpha. This is a positive theorem for a strong, known model restriction, not a uniform repair for arbitrary smooth potentials. The measurement profiles in the theorem are constructed from complex geometrical optics solutions and, in the proof, use complex frequency parameter t_k proportional to |k|^s+1. The paper does not impose a trace-amplitude or physical actuator budget; its stability constant already grows exponentially in N^(1/2+alpha). It also chooses profiles relative to a reference q1. Converting this result into an implementable, uniformly budgeted adaptive protocol remains a separate task.

Significance and next gate

The consequential implication is a no-go boundary for boundary-only discovery: recovering every C^m interior potential to worst-case error epsilon from active boundary data on fixed bounded energy bands requires an actuation/readout budget exponential in epsilon^(-1/(2m)) under the stated noise models. A claim of subexponential cost at this accuracy contradicts an established theorem. A credible positive claim must state a narrower prior class, an interior or other new measurement channel, or an energy/actuation budget that grows at least exponentially at the hidden scale. The known finite-dimensional theorem gives a concrete restricted route, while exposing a cost that has not been made physical.

Next proof gate: choose a concrete finite-dimensional W and a physical boundary norm. Derive explicit bounds on each required trace ||f_l||_{H^{1/2}}, the maximum temporal energy, and the number of noisy readings; then determine whether the Lipschitz estimate remains useful under that total budget. Alternatively, prove that a desired structured prior excludes the Isaev pair and establish a stable measurement map on that restricted class. Until one of these gates passes, no general active-measurement breakthrough is supported.
