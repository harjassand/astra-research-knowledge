28 — Two-copy HOM metrology: exact tradeoff and a closed novelty route

Finding

I tested a centroid-independent receiver for two equal, mutually incoherent point sources. Each photon is collected into two apertures separated by baseline B, giving a path qubit; a second photon carrying an independent copy of the same spatial state is interfered with the first at a balanced beam splitter. A coincidence measurement is a SWAP test: it estimates the purity of the one-photon spatial state, which depends on source separation but not centroid. This is physically realizable with mode-matched photons, delay/storage, a beam splitter, and coincidence detectors. The calculation below is exact for that model. The interface is not novel: Parniak et al. already demonstrated two-photon HOM superresolution with spatially resolved detection and simultaneous centroid/separation estimation, and a 2025 paper gives a related two-photon transverse-momentum sampling interface. I therefore found no defensible landmark claim. The useful result is a compact resource/noise audit of the simple purity-only receiver.

Model and ideal information

Use angular coordinates and let β = kB, where k=2π/λ and B is the projected aperture baseline. A photon from angle θ is the path state |ψθ> = (|L> + exp(iβθ)|R>)/√2. For equal incoherent sources at θ0±d/2, the received one-photon density matrix is

ρ = 1/2 [[1, exp(-iβθ0) cos(βd/2)], [exp(iβθ0) cos(βd/2), 1]].

Writing α=βd/2 and c=cos α, its purity is Tr(ρ²)=(1+c²)/2, independent of centroid θ0. HOM coincidence probability for two identical copies is p=(1−Tr(ρ²))/2=sin²(α)/4. Thus the output is a scalar separation statistic requiring no image-plane centroid alignment. This is the proposed interface; it estimates d only and discards centroid information.

For a Bernoulli coincidence outcome, the Fisher information per photon pair is

F_pair(d) = (dp/dd)²/[p(1−p)] = β² cos²(α)/(3+cos²(α)).

The removable limit at d=0 is β²/4 per pair, or β²/8 per incident photon, over the sub-Rayleigh branch 0≤α<π/2. The one-photon quantum Fisher information of this two-aperture state for d is β²/4, so this binary HOM readout retains exactly one half of the local QFI in the d→0 limit. For M independent pairs, the local Cramér–Rao variance bound is 4/(Mβ²), equivalently 8/(Nβ²) for N=2M collected photons; the one-photon QFI benchmark is 4/(Nβ²). This constant local information is the same scaling benefit as the usual mode-sorter result, with a factor-two photon-efficiency penalty in this deliberately reduced readout.

The finite-sample boundary is singular: at d=0 the ideal coincidence probability is exactly zero, so the regular unbiased-estimator interpretation of the CRB does not apply at the boundary. For a fixed false-negative target a, an at-least-one-coincidence test has miss probability (1−p)^M. In the small-d limit p≈β²d²/16, so setting (1−p)^M≤a gives d≈4√(−ln a)/(β√M). This establishes the M^−1/2 separation scale under the ideal, zero-background model without relying on a Gaussian approximation to the estimator.

Noise and acquisition cost

Let V∈[0,1] be the internal-mode indistinguishability (HOM visibility) of the paired photons. The standard partial-overlap model gives pV=(1−V Tr(ρ²))/2 = 1/2−V(3+cos(βd))/8. Its exact FI is

F_pair,V = V²β² cos²(α) sin²(α) / [4−V²(1+cos²(α))²].

At any fixed V<1, pV(0)=(1−V)/2>0 and F_pair,V = V²β⁴d²/[16(1−V²)] + O(d⁴). Hence the apparent constant-information limit disappears: fixed distinguishability error restores a Rayleigh-type local loss. The crossover occurs when the separation signal increment, approximately Vβ²d²/16, is comparable to the baseline coincidence floor (1−V)/2; its scale is d²≈8(1−V)/(Vβ²). Independent accidental coincidences or detector dark events create the same mathematical failure whenever they leave a nonzero d=0 outcome probability. This agrees with the general noisy-superresolution no-go proved by Oh et al.; it is not a loophole around it.

Pairing also has an acquisition cost. If N photons can be stored, synchronized, and paired without loss, M≈N/2 and the above per-photon factor-two loss is the main penalty. In a weak thermal field with mean occupancy μ≪1 per temporal coherence mode and no storage, the fraction of occupied modes containing a pair is O(μ²); over T modes the useful pair count is O(Tμ²), while a one-photon mode sorter can use O(Tμ) events. Thus the centroid-independent interface can lose a factor O(μ) in acquisition rate in the faint-source regime. A memory that queues isolated photons could avoid that flux penalty, but its efficiency, storage time, mode preservation, and dark-event rate must be included in V and pair yield; no such implementation is established here. In comparison, ideal direct imaging of a Gaussian intensity PSF of variance σ² has separation FI F_DI≈d²/(8σ⁴) per detected photon as d→0, while the Gaussian-PSF QFI remains 1/(4σ²). The gain is conditional on low-noise coherent mode handling and enough usable paired photons.

Decisive falsifier and prior art

The candidate’s supposed novelty was “remove centroid calibration by measuring purity instead of mode occupation.” That novelty fails directly. Tsang, Nair, and Lu’s “Quantum Theory of Superresolution for Two Incoherent Optical Point Sources” establishes the one-photon quantum limit and treats unknown centroid/misalignment; Nair and Tsang’s “Interferometric superlocalization of two incoherent optical point sources” gives SLIVER. Rehacek et al. analyze joint centroid, separation, and brightness bounds; Shao and Lu derive a separation/centroid information tradeoff. Most decisively, Parniak et al., “Beating the Rayleigh Limit Using Two-Photon Interference,” experimentally use HOM interference and spatially resolved detection to estimate centroid and separation jointly without prior centroid alignment. Muratore, Triggiani, and Tamma (Phys. Rev. Applied 23, 054033 (2025)) further report two-photon interference sampling of transverse momenta with no additional optics. The present two-aperture purity statistic is a simpler restricted observable inside this already occupied two-copy territory, not a new class of metrology.

The strongest falsifier for any stronger claim is the exact noisy FI above: for every fixed V<1, FI→0 as d→0. Any claim of a constant practical resolution independent of d must specify how indistinguishability, accidentals, and dark counts shrink with d or show a different outcome model that avoids a d=0 floor. A second falsifier is acquisition: without photon storage, the pair rate is quadratic in weak flux. The textbook per-photon QFI comparison omits this event-generation and synchronization cost.

Status and consequence

Proved here: the two-aperture state, centroid-invariant purity, exact ideal and imperfect-visibility Bernoulli FI, half-QFI local efficiency, and ideal fixed-error M^−1/2 detection scaling. Known: single-photon quantum-superresolution bounds and collective two-photon centroid/separation approaches. Conditional: a stored-photon purity receiver could be useful when centroid calibration is expensive and the source yields mode-matched pairs economically. Unproved: any advantage in real acquisition time after storage loss, accidental coincidences, and detector background. Refuted as a novelty claim: HOM-based centroid-free two-photon superresolution is already established. Potential significance is an engineering design point for a purity-only receiver, not a foundational resolution advance.

Next proof gate: derive a full likelihood including finite collection efficiency, weak-thermal pair statistics, memory loss, distinguishability V, and accidental/dark coincidences, then compare its fixed-error separation threshold per unit observing time against direct imaging and adaptive SPADE using the same aperture, total collected flux, and detector budget. If this constrained comparison does not yield a strict regime of advantage, close the route.
