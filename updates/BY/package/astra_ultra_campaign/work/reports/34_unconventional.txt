34 — A finite-grid no-go for Euler vorticity certification

Status. I proved an exact observation-interface obstruction for a standard finite periodic grid, strengthened it to preserve both global mean-square speed and mean-square vorticity exactly, and checked the finite formulas with rational arithmetic. This is a negative capability result about what the stated input can certify. It is not a result about blow-up, and I make no novelty claim for the underlying aliasing mechanism.

Capability and target. Consider an attempted certificate which receives (i) all velocity values on an m-by-m-by-m equispaced grid at time zero, (ii) exact total kinetic energy and enstrophy, and (iii) a time horizon T>0. It must return a finite upper bound, valid for every smooth periodic 3D Euler initial field consistent with those inputs, on

    B_T(u) = integral_0^T ||curl u(t)||_infinity dt.

This is a natural proof interface because the Beale–Kato–Majda continuation criterion makes this integral control smooth continuation. The decisive question is whether finite samples plus even exact low-order global invariants can bound the quantity.

Theorem proved here. For every grid size m>=2 and T>0, there are smooth, divergence-free, time-independent Euler solutions with identical zero velocity samples on the full grid and identical positive energy and enstrophy, but with B_T arbitrarily large. Therefore no finite uniform bound can be computed from this interface without an additional high-frequency restriction or certificate.

Derivation. Work on T^3=[0,2pi]^3 with normalized spatial average <.>. Fix E>0 and set the observed mean-square speed to E and mean-square vorticity to Z=2m^2 E. (Under the common one-half convention, kinetic energy is E/2 and enstrophy is Z/2.) For each integer K>=1, let n_0=m and n_j=m(j+1), j=1,...,K. Put

    S_K = sum_{j=1}^K 1/n_j^2,       d = Z - m^2 E = m^2 E,
    c_K^2 = 2d / (1 - m^2 S_K/K),
    H_K = c_K^2 S_K/(2K),
    a_0^2 = 2(E-H_K),       a_j = c_K/(sqrt(K) n_j), j>=1.

The denominator is positive: m^2 S_K/K = K^(-1) sum_{r=2}^{K+1} r^(-2) <= 1/4. Also H_K<=E/3, so a_0 is real. Define

    u_K(x,y,z) = ( f_K(y), 0, 0 ),
    f_K(y) = a_0 sin(my) + sum_{j=1}^K a_j sin(n_j y).

This field is divergence-free. Its nonlinear term vanishes because u_K dot grad = f_K(y) partial_x and u_K is independent of x; with constant pressure, it is an exact stationary Euler solution. Parseval orthogonality gives

    <|u_K|^2> = (a_0^2 + sum a_j^2)/2 = E,
    <|curl u_K|^2> = (m^2 a_0^2 + sum n_j^2 a_j^2)/2
                   = m^2(E-H_K) + c_K^2/2 = Z.

The last equality is exactly the defining equation for c_K. Every frequency n_j is a multiple of m, so at every grid ordinate y_l=2pi*l/m, every sine vanishes. Thus every one of the m^3 velocity samples is zero for every K. Yet at y=0 all derivative terms align, and

    ||curl u_K||_infinity >= |(curl u_K)_3(0)|
                           = m a_0 + c_K sqrt(K) >= c_K sqrt(K).

Since sum_{r=2}^{K+1} r^(-2) is bounded, m^2 S_K/K tends to zero and c_K^2 tends to 2m^2 E. Therefore B_T(u_K)=T||curl u_K||_infinity tends to infinity like at least T sqrt(2m^2 E K). One fixed observation record (zero samples, E, Z) is compatible with arbitrarily large answers. The script work/reports/34_unconventional/certify_hidden_shear.py checks the Parseval identities and lower-bound growth using exact fractions for K=1,...,128; the proof above establishes the all-K claim.

Decisive falsification and boundary. This refutes the proposed uniform certificate interface, including its stronger version with exact energy and enstrophy, rather than only a noisy-data version. It does not refute BKM, imply singularity, or show that any u_K is dynamically dangerous: every constructed solution is globally smooth and stationary. If a known spectral cutoff is part of the input, this counterexample is excluded. A measured H^s tail with s>5/2 also controls the initial vorticity in L-infinity by Sobolev embedding, but it still does not by itself certify the time integral; a validated evolution enclosure is needed.

Prior art and novelty boundary. Beale, Kato, and Majda, “Remarks on the breakdown of smooth solutions for the 3-D Euler equations” (1984), establish the vorticity continuation criterion: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01212349. Shannon’s “Communication in the Presence of Noise” (1949) is primary sampling-theorem background for why unbandlimited periodic signals can alias on uniform grids: https://doi.org/10.1109/JRPROC.1949.232969. The present construction is an Euler-specific exact aliasing witness, not a new sampling theorem. Chen and Hou, “Stable Nearly Self-Similar Blowup of the 2D Boussinesq and 3D Euler Equations with Smooth Data II: Rigorous Numerics” (SIAM MMS, 2025), report computer-assisted proof of finite-time blowup for axisymmetric 3D Euler with smooth data and boundary, illustrating that rigorously controlled computation can settle structured cases: https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1580395. A 2026 SIAM ICM invited article describes global extension in dimensions d>=3 as a notoriously difficult open problem and states its specific C^{1,alpha} blow-up result; the scope must not be conflated with smooth periodic data: https://doi.org/10.1137/25M1807137. I found no prior source in this bounded search stating the exact combination proved here (all finite-grid samples plus exactly matched energy and enstrophy plus unbounded BKM integral); that is not evidence of novelty, and the proof is close enough to classical aliasing that priority claims would be premature.

Significance achieved vs potential. Achieved: an explicit, replayable no-go theorem that prevents a finite-resolution, invariant-only observation record from being misreported as a uniform BKM certificate. Potential: a general acquisition theorem could identify the weakest certified tail information needed for reliable Euler regularity computations and provide a formal audit standard for simulation-based singularity or nonsingularity claims. This result alone is not a foundational advance in fluid regularity and does not establish a new capability for deciding the Euler problem.

Next proof gate. Extend the construction from the aligned Cartesian grid to arbitrary finite point sensors while preserving a prescribed finite list of low-order integral invariants, or prove the precise obstruction that prevents this extension. In parallel, characterize a sharp necessary-and-sufficient spectral tail functional for bounding ||curl u(0)||_infinity; only after that should one attempt propagation of the acquired tail bound over time. Until those gates pass, retain the conclusion at the stated uniform-grid interface.
