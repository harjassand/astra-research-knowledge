Investigator 18 — exact bilinear matrix multiplication
Date checked: 2026-10-10

Finding

No new asymptotic matrix-multiplication capability was found in this pass. I reconstructed and exactly verified the rank-seven 2-by-2 bilinear algorithm, and then followed its recursive scaling proof. The result is a valid, division-free exact construction over rings, but it is Strassen's 1969 algorithm and its exponent is already far behind the current frontier. The decisive local falsifier is that the 2-by-2 multiplication tensor has border rank seven, so no alternative rank-six bilinear formula at this size can rescue this route.

Target, interface, and model

The consequential target would be an explicit low-rank decomposition of the matrix-multiplication tensor, with exact coefficients over a stated field and a scaling argument that improves the best known exponent or a structured contraction cost. An input consists of supplied exact matrices A and B; the output is their exact product AB. There is no acquisition oracle, learned information, or hidden input. The claims below count arithmetic operations, separating scalar multiplications from additions. They do not claim floating-point stability or a bit-complexity improvement.

For A=[[a,b],[c,d]] and B=[[e,f],[g,h]], define

  m1=(a+d)(e+h)       m2=(c+d)e       m3=a(f-h)
  m4=d(g-e)           m5=(a+b)h       m6=(c-a)(e+f)
  m7=(b-d)(g+h).

Reconstruct

  C11=m1+m4-m5+m7    C12=m3+m5
  C21=m2+m4           C22=m1-m2+m3+m6.

Expanding each output gives respectively ae+bg, af+bh, ce+dg, and cf+dh. The seven products are rank-one bilinear terms: each multiplies one linear form in A's entries by one linear form in B's entries, and each output is a linear combination of those products. This is an exact tensor decomposition of the 2-by-2 multiplication tensor with rank at most seven.

I wrote and ran work/reports/18_matrix_multiplication/verify_strassen.py. It stores each output as a sparse integer coefficient map on monomials (A-entry, B-entry), then compares all four maps with the defining matrix-product polynomials. It passed coefficient-for-coefficient over Z; this is a symbolic identity check, not a sample of inputs and not floating-point evidence. Since the formulas use only addition, subtraction, and multiplication, the identity remains valid over any associative ring with central integer coefficients. In particular, the block recursion is valid when entries are themselves matrices, with the A-factor kept to the left of the B-factor.

Scaling proof and its limit

Partition n-by-n inputs into four equal blocks and recurse on the seven products. For n=2^k this uses exactly 7^k scalar multiplications, hence n^(log_2 7), where log_2 7=2.8073549…. The addition/subtraction recurrence is S(n)=7S(n/2)+18(n/2)^2, S(1)=0, so S(n)=6n^(log_2 7)-6n^2 for powers of two. Padding dimensions to the next power of two preserves O(n^(log_2 7)). Thus the scaling proof is complete for this construction, but does not approach exponent two. Conner, Harper, and Landsberg's paper gives border rank R̲(M2)=7; combined with the explicit rank-seven formula, this makes the local multiplication count optimal. A proof gate for any improvement through this exact 2-by-2 recursion is therefore blocked: it would need a different base tensor, a different asymptotic construction, or a distinct cost model.

Contemporary comparison and barriers

The best frontier source I found by the date above is Dupont et al., “Improving the matrix multiplication exponent with modern optimization and AlphaEvolve” (arXiv:2608.16884, 17 August 2026). It claims omega<2.371177 by reformulating and optimizing combination-loss analysis, improving the prior 2.371339 bound. This is a source-reported current preprint result; I did not reconstruct its optimization certificate. It is an asymptotic algebraic upper bound, not evidence of a practical kernel. The gap between 2.807 and 2.371 makes the reconstructed algorithm immaterial to the exponent frontier.

Finite-size results illustrate why field and interface must be part of every claim. Fawzi et al., “Discovering faster matrix multiplication algorithms with reinforcement learning” (Nature 610, 2022; DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-05172-4), report a 47-multiplication 4-by-4 algorithm over F2, beating the 49 multiplications from Strassen recursion in that field. Novikov et al., “AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery” (arXiv:2506.13131, 2025), report 48 scalar multiplications for 4-by-4 complex-valued matrices. These are stronger small-format alternatives in their stated domains; the F2 decomposition does not establish a characteristic-zero rank improvement, and complex coefficients do not by themselves give a 48-real-multiplication algorithm. A decomposition over a field F would be checked by verifying every tensor coefficient: for each input coordinate pair (A_ij,B_kl) and output coordinate C_pq, the proposed sum of rank-one terms must equal 1 exactly when j=k, p=i, and q=l, and 0 otherwise. Such verification is finite and decisive for a supplied decomposition, but finding a lower-rank one is the hard step.

Lower bounds do not close the asymptotic problem. The elementary output-size bound gives omega>=2, while the value of omega and whether it equals 2 remain open. Christandl, Vrana, and Zuiddam, “Barriers for fast matrix multiplication from irreversibility” (arXiv:1812.06952), show that fixed irreversible intermediate tensors impose method-specific barriers on laser-style routes; their theorem constrains those proof methods, not all possible matrix-multiplication algorithms. The recent exponent improvement still uses a refinement of the laser method, so a genuinely new route would need either a better intermediate construction with a compatible loss analysis or a method outside the bounded families covered by such barriers.

Status ledger

Proved here: exact seven-product identity and exact integer-coefficient verification; recursive multiplication and its arithmetic-operation recurrences.

Known from cited source: rank/border-rank optimality seven for 2-by-2 multiplication; AlphaTensor's 47-product F2 instance; AlphaEvolve's 48-product complex instance; the cited asymptotic upper-bound and barrier results.

Unproved here: any improvement to omega, any new rank decomposition, practical speedup, floating-point stability, and any real-field lift of the F2 result.

Refuted route: improving the scalar multiplication rank by changing the 2-by-2 base formula below seven. The border-rank-seven lower bound blocks it.

Potential landmark consequence: an exact, certifiable decomposition or degeneration whose tensor powers yield an exponent below the 2.371177 frontier would improve a central algebraic-complexity bound. This pass achieves only a reconstruction of a classical algorithm, with no frontier or practical significance.

Next proof gate

To claim new capability, supply explicit coefficient arrays U,V,W for a tensor decomposition of a new base shape over the intended field, verify all tensor coefficients exactly, and prove that the resulting recursion or degeneration yields a valid exponent below 2.371177 with all field and additive-cost assumptions stated. For a 4-by-4 claim, the immediate gate is an independently reconstructed 16-by-16-by-16 coefficient identity and a field-correct recursive proof; a reported multiplication count alone does not pass it.
