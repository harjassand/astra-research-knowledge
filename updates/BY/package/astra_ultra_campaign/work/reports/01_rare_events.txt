Rare-event estimation without a committor: a sharp black-box boundary

Status: proved here (elementary finite-sample theorems); known background (adaptive importance sampling and cross-entropy); conditional positive guarantee; uniform sub-inverse-probability capability refuted for membership-only black boxes. No empirical experiment was needed. No novelty or external-validation claim is made.

Target and interface

The target is p=P(A), where P is a known, efficiently sampleable baseline law and A is recognized by an oracle that returns only 1_A(x). The hoped-for capability is to learn a change of measure while estimating p, without a supplied committor, score, dominating point, or event geometry, and to return a relative-error certificate at cost polynomial in log(1/p). Every event-oracle evaluation is charged. Proposal sampling, density evaluation, likelihood-ratio evaluation, and proposal fitting are additional costs, not free operations.

A valid positive result with an explicit acquisition contract

There is a simple fully adaptive certification wrapper, but it does not in general make an exponentially rare event cheap. Let P have known density p(x). Before draw t, choose a proposal density q_t using only past observations, and require q_t(x) >= epsilon p(x) for all x, for a declared epsilon in (0,1]. Draw X_t from q_t and set Z_t=1_A(X_t)p(X_t)/q_t(X_t). This includes the defensive mixture q_t=(1-epsilon)r_t+epsilon p for any learned, evaluable proposal r_t. It needs no committor and remains valid when r_t is updated after every draw.

The estimate p_hat=n^{-1} sum Z_t is unbiased conditionally at every step: E[Z_t | history]=P(A)=p. Also 0<=Z_t<=B=1/epsilon and E[Z_t^2 | history]<=Bp, since p(x)/q_t(x)<=B. Thus the centered increments have absolute bound B and conditional variance at most Bp. Freedman's martingale inequality gives, for 0<rho<1,

Pr(|p_hat-p| >= rho p) <= 2 exp(- n rho^2 p / (2B(1+rho/3))).

Consequently, if a valid lower bound p>=p0>0 is supplied, then

n >= 2B(1+rho/3) log(2/delta)/(p0 rho^2)

makes [p_hat/(1+rho), p_hat/(1-rho)] a (1-delta) confidence interval for p. The proof is the conditional expectation/second-moment calculation above followed by Freedman; it does not depend on proposal convergence. It certifies adaptive production draws, including reuse of all production observations for future proposal updates.

The guarantee's acquisition costs are exact: for each draw, the implementation must sample q_t, evaluate q_t and p at X_t, evaluate 1_A(X_t), and form the ratio; updating r_t adds its full fitting and normalization cost. The mixture component density must be evaluable as the total mixture density, not just the selected component. If any of these are approximate, their error needs a separate bound. The supplied p0 is also essential to the fixed sample budget. With the defensive mixture alone, B=1/epsilon and n remains proportional to 1/p0. For p0=exp(-cn), this is still exponential in the event's rarity. The wrapper is a certification tool, not a rare-event discovery mechanism.

Sharp falsifier for a universal black-box mechanism

Consider Omega={1,...,N} with known uniform P. The unknown event A is accessible only through membership queries x -> 1_A(x). Promise that its size is either m or 2m, where 1<=m and 2m<=N. The estimator may adaptively select any query point, use arbitrary randomization, and compute any proposal probabilities and likelihood ratios from the points it has queried. This query interface is strictly stronger than drawing points from chosen proposals. It must output a relative-error-1/4 estimate with probability at least 2/3 for every promised A.

The two target probabilities are m/N and 2m/N. Their 25%-accuracy intervals, [0.75m/N,1.25m/N] and [1.5m/N,2.5m/N], are disjoint, so any successful estimator distinguishes the two cases with average success at least 2/3. Couple the hypotheses by drawing B uniformly among all 2m-subsets and then A uniformly among the m-subsets of B. The marginals are the required uniform priors. Couple the estimator's random bits in both runs. Until a query lands in B, both transcripts return zero and remain identical. Along the all-zero transcript, the algorithm's at most T queried points are determined without knowing B. A uniform B intersects this query list with probability at most 2mT/N by a union bound. Therefore the total variation distance between the two transcript laws is at most 2mT/N. Distinguishing with success 2/3 requires total variation at least 1/3, hence T >= N/(6m) = 1/(6p_small).

This proves an Omega(1/p) worst-case event-oracle acquisition cost, even with adaptive changes of measure and stronger-than-sampling point selection. For m=1, p=1/N and the cost is Omega(N). For p=exp(-c d), it is exponential in d. The proof applies to any fixed upper bound T on total membership evaluations; it says nothing against a supplied score, structural description, event-region generator, or verified proposal that reveals useful geometry. Those are precisely extra acquisition channels whose cost and validity must be named. Likelihood ratios alone add no information about the hidden A.

Prior art and boundary

Adaptive importance sampling and cross-entropy already use data-driven proposal updates: Rubinstein, “Optimization of computer simulation models with rare events” (1997), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-2217(96)00385-2; de Boer, Kroese, Mannor, and Rubinstein, “A Tutorial on the Cross-Entropy Method” (2005), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-005-5724-z. Delyon and Portier, “Safe Adaptive Importance Sampling: A Mixture Approach” (2021), https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1983, analyze adaptive proposals mixed with a safe density, with asymptotic convergence and a central limit theorem. Arief et al., “Certifiable Deep Importance Sampling for Rare-Event Simulation of Black-Box Systems” (2021), https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02204, combine rare-set learning with a dominating-point concept and offer a statistical certification framework. These are strong relevant precedents. They establish that useful learning/certification is possible with richer model structure and proposal assumptions; they do not give a universal geometry-free escape from the membership lower bound. The defensive-martingale bound above is a direct consequence of standard importance sampling plus Freedman, not claimed as a new theorem.

Decision and consequence

The assigned universal target is refuted under the explicit membership-only interface. A positive sub-exponential claim needs a named information source beyond event hits: for example, a score with a proved coverage relation, an acquired dominating-point set, or a proposal q with a certified second-moment bound E_q[(1_A p/q)^2] <= C p^2. Under the last contract, ordinary Bernstein concentration gives relative sample complexity O(C rho^-2 log(1/delta)); finding and certifying that proposal is the unresolved acquisition problem. Counting training, simulator calls, likelihood evaluation, and validation can erase any apparent gain.

Possible landmark consequence: this is a stop theorem for claims that an unconstrained adaptive sampler can discover arbitrary exponentially rare event regions from black-box membership feedback alone. It locates the missing ingredient as event-geometry information, rather than the choice among cross-entropy, neural proposals, or other update rules. Achieved significance is a precise lower-bound gate plus a usable conditional certificate; no new rare-event capability or general breakthrough is established.

Next proof gate: state an acquired structural interface that escapes the lower bound, then prove that a concrete learner obtains a proposal with a certified second-moment ratio C=poly(d, log(1/p)) at total acquisition cost poly(d, log(1/p)). Compare against the strongest method with the same score/model access and charge proposal fitting, density normalization, and event evaluations. Until that gate passes, classify the general capability as refuted in the membership-only model and unresolved for structured systems.
