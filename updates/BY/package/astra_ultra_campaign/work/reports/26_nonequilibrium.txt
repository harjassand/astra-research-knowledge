26 — Measurement route: closed-loop generalized-force work

Decision. A control-based measurement can expose a finite nonequilibrium response with a tractable sample count when the experimenter can vary two calibrated energy fields and measure their conjugate forces. The exact null is that a globally equilibrated system has zero quasistatic work around every closed field loop, even after hidden states are integrated out. A driven three-state ring gives a concrete alternative with a nonzero, sign-predicted loop. This is a conditional measurement candidate, not a universal hidden-state test: an unobserved metastable sector can be arbitrarily slow or uncoupled from the controls, and then no finite protocol has a uniform guarantee.

Input and output contract. Take a finite system with controls E1,E2 (E3 fixed at zero), in units of kBT, and measured generalized forces A1,A2. The control Hamiltonian has the form H(x;E)=H0(x)+E1 A1(x)+E2 A2(x); the reported statistic is the quasistatic loop work W=integral(<A1> dE1 + <A2> dE2) along a closed square. The intervention requires calibrated conjugacy and the ability to prepare or sample the stationary distribution at each field point. It does not require reading hidden microstates. Under global equilibrium, F(E)=−log sum_x exp(−H(x;E)) is a state function, <Ai>=partial_i F, and W=0 exactly for any loop. Summing over hidden x only changes F, so this null is independent of hidden-state count. Failure of the null means the tested preparation is not globally equilibrated under the stated controls; it does not by itself decide whether the cause is steady driving or metastable trapping.

Exact model and prediction. Use a three-state ring, states i=1,2,3, with clockwise edges i→i+1 (mod 3) and rates
q_i,i+1(E)=2 exp[−(E_{i+1}−E_i)/2],    q_i+1,i(E)=exp[+(E_{i+1}−E_i)/2].
The rate unit is one inverse model-time unit and E3=0. The log rate ratio on each clockwise edge is log 2−(E_{i+1}−E_i), so the fixed cycle affinity is 3 log 2=log 8. At E=0 the stationary law is uniform, each clockwise edge current is 1/3, and the stationary entropy-production rate is (1/3)(3 log 2)=log 2 nats per model-time unit.

The stationary probabilities at any E are obtained exactly from the three directed spanning-tree weights tau_i: pi_i=tau_i/(tau_1+tau_2+tau_3), where tau_i is the sum of products of two rates over the directed spanning trees pointing to i. This gives a compact exact evaluator. At the origin the generator is Q=[[-3,2,1],[1,-3,2],[2,1,-3]] and pi=(1/3,1/3,1/3). Differentiating pi Q=0 and sum_i pi_i=1 gives d pi/dE1=(-3/14,1/14,1/7) and d pi/dE2=(1/7,-3/14,1/14). Therefore the force curl is partial_E1 pi_2−partial_E2 pi_1=1/14−1/7=−1/14. For a counterclockwise square of side delta about the origin, W=−delta^2/14+O(delta^3). This is an exact local prediction. For side delta=1, the tree formula plus 100-panel composite trapezoid gives W=−0.0664299723730 kBT; 20, 50, 100, 200, and 1000 panels converge respectively to −0.06642809, −0.06642974, −0.06642997, −0.06643003, and −0.06643005. The accompanying standard-library script reproduces these values. Replacing clockwise rate 2 by 1 makes the chain equilibrium with pi_i proportional to exp(−E_i); its loop work is zero, and the symmetric-square trapezoid is zero to rounding at every listed grid size.

Acquisition cost. For this three-state model, measure Ai=1{X=i}. At K=100 subdivisions per edge, take M=100 independent stationary force readings at each of the four edge grids (counting endpoints separately), for 4(K+1)M=40,400 force readings. Each reading lies in [0,1]. The sum of squared sample-weight ranges in the trapezoid estimator is at most 4/(KM). With the threshold halfway between the model predictions, |W|/2=0.033215 kBT, Hoeffding's one-sided bound gives either error probability at most exp(−(0.033215)^2 KM/2)<0.0041. This is a finite-sample calculation for the specified model pair; it gives a sub-1% sum of type-I and type-II bounds under independent stationary sampling. It is not an end-to-end wall-clock guarantee. The ring's rates are order one and a dense scan of the square gives a minimum nonzero spectral decay rate about 4.3975, but that scan is not a certified mixing bound. A physical report must charge field settling, independent-start or autocorrelation costs, force calibration, and the time scale of the actual device. For arbitrary hidden equilibria, finite-grid quadrature bias must also be bounded; the exact null is the continuous quasistatic integral.

What this changes, and what it does not. Astra's N185–186 cards already record why stationary-start entropy-production confidence claims fail without hidden mixing access. The current N511–512 route gives a seven-time reversal-even witness, but its source card reports a sufficient independent-window cost around 1.06×10^93 and temporal resolution 10^−12; those claims remain source-derived and externally unverified. The loop-work route changes the interface by actively varying conjugate fields, and for the explicit ring its margin is about 0.066 kBT rather than the tiny seven-time witness margin. It does not improve the N511 proof or evade N185 for systems whose slow hidden transitions are not accelerated by the intervention. A product hidden cycle that is decoupled from both fields and force sensors is exactly invisible under this protocol. More generally, without a lower bound on response curl and a mixing/escape envelope under the applied fields, the worst-case sample/time cost is unbounded.

Prior art and status. Nonzero geometric response in slowly driven stochastic systems is established prior art: Sinitsyn and Nemenman, “Universal Geometric Theory of Mesoscopic Stochastic Pumps and Reversible Ratchets,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 99, 220408 (2007), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.220408. Quasistatic transformations of nonequilibrium stationary states and their renormalized work were studied by Bertini, Gabrielli, Jona-Lasinio, and Landim, “Clausius Inequality and Optimality of Quasistatic Transformations for Nonequilibrium Stationary States,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 110, 020601 (2013), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.020601. Harada and Sasa's equality separately connects fluctuation-response violation to energy dissipation for Langevin systems: “Equality Connecting Energy Dissipation with Violation of the Fluctuation-Response Relation,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 95, 130602 (2005), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.130602. These establish the mechanism family; the three-state rates, curl, loop value, and sample calculation above were reconstructed here. No novelty or general theorem is claimed. The Astra records are navigation/source claims, not independently certified results.

Decisive falsifier. In a realization calibrated to the stated three-state rates and field coupling, the quasistatic square work must approach −0.06643005 kBT as the field mesh is refined; the matched equilibrium control must approach zero. A stable zero or opposite-sign driven limit after confirming stationarity, the local detailed-balance rate law, and force conjugacy falsifies this model-specific prediction. For metastability, repeat with increasing dwell or a barrier-lowering pulse: a loop that collapses once the escape time is crossed supports hidden slow relaxation; a nonzero plateau after stationarity is established supports a NESS response. Neither pattern is universal without bounds on escape and control coupling.

Achieved vs potential significance. Achieved: an exact, low-dimensional response witness with a transparent observation/control contract, a derived signal, and a conditional 40,400-reading design. Potential: in a system with accessible conjugate forces and a measured mixing envelope, a closed-loop assay could test global-equilibrium assumptions at realistic sample counts without reconstructing its hidden state graph. The experiment, physical-time calibration, and general finite-sample guarantee are unachieved; the foundation-level objective is not met.

Next proof gate. Prove a finite-mesh, finite-time test theorem for bounded conjugate forces: given a certified uniform mixing/escape envelope under all points on the loop, a certified curvature margin |curl <A>|≥c on a known patch, and a field mesh K, derive explicit bounds for stationary-sampling error, quadrature bias, and finite-rate dissipation, then test them on an independently measured small system. If any one of the coupling, curvature, or mixing constants cannot be acquired, retain the result as model-specific and stop the universal claim.
