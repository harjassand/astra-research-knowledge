PRIMARY-SOURCE PRIOR-ART CHALLENGE: AMPLIFIER EPnI
Cutoff: 2026-10-10

QUESTION AND VERDICT

For finite n, independent finite-energy n-mode inputs rho_A and rho_B (arbitrary states within each mode group), gain G >= 1, and the retained two-mode-squeezer output defined by

  c_j = sqrt(G) a_j + sqrt(G-1) b_j^dagger,

is the bound

  N(rho_C) >= G N(rho_A) + (G-1)(N(rho_B)+1),
  N(rho) := g^{-1}(S(rho)/n),
  g(x) := (x+1) log(x+1) - x log x,

already proved or refuted?

In the primary sources checked for this report, I found neither a proof nor a counterexample for this full two-arbitrary-input, all-finite-n statement. The strongest historical status evidence is explicit: De Palma, Mari, and Giovannetti state the amplifier version as a conjecture and say they do not have a proof (2014/2015 paper, Eq. (36)). The 2026 OpenAI Math EPnI result is for passive beam-splitter mixing, not amplification. Thus the exact amplifier statement remains open on the evidence reviewed here; this is a bounded literature conclusion, not a proof that no later or unindexed source exists.

SCOPE COMPARISONS

1. 2026 OpenAI Math result: passive beam splitter only.

The pinned release scope states that the formalized theorem is for two independent finite-energy inputs in any finite positive number of modes, with arbitrary entanglement allowed within either input, and output inequality

  N(rho_C) >= eta N(rho_A) + (1-eta) N(rho_B)

for a beam splitter of transmissivity eta in [0,1]. Its Heisenberg map has two annihilation ports, c = sqrt(eta) a + sqrt(1-eta) b. The target here has an active creation port b^dagger and the spontaneous-emission offset +1; it is not an instance of the passive theorem or a parameter substitution into it.

Primary source: OpenAI, “The entropy photon-number inequality,” pinned Math repository commit fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb, scope note https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/273.md and release manuscript https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/The-entropy-photon-number-inequality-September-24-2026/paper.pdf .

2. One thermal input: exact one-mode result, 2017.

De Palma, Trevisan, and Giovannetti prove that for any one-mode input state rho_A and a one-mode phase-covariant amplifier with thermal environment mean E,

  S(A_{G,E}(rho_A)) >= g(G g^{-1}(S(rho_A)) + (G-1)(E+1)).

This is precisely the requested amplifier bound for n=1 when rho_B is thermal with mean E. It includes the quantum-limited/vacuum environment E=0 and allows arbitrary rho_A. Product thermal inputs attain equality. It does not allow an arbitrary nonthermal second port. Their multimode follow-up proves the corresponding constrained-output-entropy bound for n identical channels when the signal input is diagonal in some product basis; in particular, joint-Fock-diagonal inputs are covered. It explicitly leaves generic multimode inputs for future work. This restriction excludes arbitrary within-group mode entanglement, so it does not give the requested all-state n-mode theorem.

Primary sources: De Palma, Trevisan, Giovannetti, “Gaussian states minimize the output entropy of one-mode quantum Gaussian channels,” PRL 118, 160503 (2017), Theorem 4, Eq. (26): https://arxiv.org/html/1610.09970 . De Palma, Trevisan, Giovannetti, “Multimode Gaussian optimizers for the Wehrl entropy and quantum Gaussian channels,” Theorem 11, Eqs. (41c), (40), and conclusion: https://arxiv.org/html/1705.00499 .

3. Pure inputs: vacuum/coherent port is covered; arbitrary pure pairs are not established by those results.

The 2017 one-mode theorem covers arbitrary signal rho_A when the other port is vacuum, and more generally thermal. Displacing a vacuum environment to a coherent state changes the output only by displacement, which leaves entropy unchanged, so coherent pure rho_B is covered as well. The theorem does not permit an arbitrary noncoherent pure rho_B. The target for two arbitrary pure inputs would reduce to N(rho_C) >= G-1, but I found no primary-source proof or counterexample for arbitrary pure input pairs or their all-n-mode version. Do not conflate this with the quantum-limited amplifier minimum-output-entropy theorem: that theorem fixes the environmental port to vacuum. The 2012 majorization paper formulates the vacuum-environment amplifier optimization as its conjecture; 2017 settles the one-mode fixed-input-entropy version.

Primary sources: De Palma et al. (2017), Theorems 2 and 4, https://arxiv.org/html/1610.09970 . García-Patrón et al., “Majorization Theory Approach to the Gaussian Channel Minimum Entropy Conjecture,” PRL 108, 110505 (2012), especially C2/C3, https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.110505 .

4. Amplifier qEPI: known and weaker; it does not prove this EPnI.

For independent inputs the established quantum entropy power inequality is

  exp(S(rho_C)/n) >= G exp(S(rho_A)/n) + (G-1) exp(S(rho_B)/n).

This is an entropy-power bound, not the affine entropy-photon-number bound above. De Palma, Mari, and Giovannetti explicitly list the amplifier EPnI as a separate conjecture after proving the qEPI. They explain that x -> g^{-1}(log x) is convex, so applying this conversion to qEPI does not yield the desired linear inequality. Their amplifier qEPI also is not saturated by thermal inputs in general, while the proposed EPnI is tight on product thermal inputs. Therefore citing qEPI alone is not a proof of the requested bound.

Primary source: De Palma, Mari, Giovannetti, “A generalization of the Entropy Power Inequality to Bosonic Quantum Systems,” arXiv:1402.0404, amplifier qEPI Eq. (7), amplifier EPnI conjecture Eqs. (36) and lines following: https://arxiv.org/html/1402.0404 .

5. Older special-case EPnI work is passive, not an amplifier theorem.

Das, Sharma, and Muthukrishnan’s “On some special cases of the Entropy Photon-Number Inequality” treats the beam-splitter inequality with one vacuum input and restricted number-diagonal signal states. It is not evidence for the active amplifier target.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1107.2365 .

PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY

The candidate’s exact target is historically identifiable as the 2014 amplifier EPnI conjecture. The 2026 passive theorem supplies no theorem in the active Bogoliubov setting. The 2017 one-mode thermal-environment result and its restricted multimode product-basis extension are genuine neighboring results, but neither fills the arbitrary-independent two-port gap. qEPI supplies a quantitative entropy-power lower bound, not the EPnI. I found no counterexample in the checked primary literature. Accordingly, a proposed proof of the full inequality would be aimed at an historically open conjecture, but this prior-art report does not validate that proposed proof or establish novelty against every source available by the cutoff.
