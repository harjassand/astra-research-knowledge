03 — Linear solvers: locality obstruction, with a sparse GMRES stress case

Status: a proved obstruction for banded approximate-inverse preconditioners; no general sparse-solver breakthrough. The distinction between matrix-free access and an explicitly stored sparse matrix is decisive.

Target and interface

The candidate capability was an adaptive preprocessor that, from a general nonsymmetric sparse matrix A, learns a cheap sparse approximate inverse B and then certifies a fast solve uniformly, without assuming decay or favorable conditioning in advance. I tested the concrete matrix-representation claim: for every nonsingular A, can a banded B with half-bandwidth at most n/4 make either left or right preconditioned operator uniformly close to identity, say ||I−BA||₂≤δ or ||I−AB||₂≤δ for fixed δ<1/(4e)? The input is explicit sparse entries; the claimed output is an explicit banded B. This is only one candidate mechanism, not a lower bound against all sparse solvers.

Decisive counterexample, proved here

Let n=4m and S be the upper shift, Sᵢ,ᵢ₊₁=1, with all other entries zero. Put α=1−1/n and A=I−αS. A is nonsingular upper bidiagonal, has 2n−1 nonzeros, and is nonnormal. Its inverse is

  A⁻¹ = I + αS + α²S² + ⋯ + αⁿ⁻¹Sⁿ⁻¹,

so (A⁻¹)ᵢⱼ=αʲ⁻ⁱ for j≥i. Also ||A⁻¹||₂≤Σₖ αᵏ≤n and ||A||₂≤2, hence κ₂(A)≤2n.

Take any B whose entries vanish whenever |i−j|>w, where w≤m. Set I={1,…,m} and J={3m+1,…,4m}. For every i∈I and j∈J, j−i≥2m+1>w, so Bᵢⱼ=0. Since αⁿ⁻¹>e⁻¹, every corresponding entry of D=A⁻¹−B is at least e⁻¹. With u and v the unit all-ones vectors supported on I and J,

  ||D||₂ ≥ |uᵀDv| ≥ m/e.

For left preconditioning, I−BA=(A⁻¹−B)A=DA, so D=(I−BA)A⁻¹ and ||I−BA||₂≥||D||₂/||A⁻¹||₂≥1/(4e). For right preconditioning, D=A⁻¹(I−AB), giving the same bound. Thus no matter how B is acquired or optimized, bandwidth at most n/4 cannot make either preconditioned operator closer than 1/(4e) to identity on this family. This is an exact-arithmetic theorem for the stated representation and norm. It does not show that GMRES must be slow whenever this residual norm is large; that implication would be invalid.

There is a separate sparse nonnormal stress case for Krylov iteration counts. For even n, define A eⱼ=wⱼeⱼ₊₁ (indices cyclically), with wⱼ alternating between 1 and 2, and let b=e₁. This matrix has one nonzero per row and column, singular values 1 and 2, and κ₂(A)=2. It is nonnormal because AᵀA and AAᵀ have the alternating diagonal weights in opposite positions. For k<n, Kₖ(A,b)=span{e₁,…,eₖ}, while A Kₖ is contained in span{e₂,…,eₖ₊₁}, orthogonal to b. Therefore the GMRES residual after k steps is exactly ||b||₂: x=0 attains residual 1, and every nonzero image A x adds an orthogonal component. GMRES reaches the exact solution at step n. This refutes any claim that a small singular-value condition number alone certifies a short GMRES run. The example is deliberately not a hard explicit sparse solve: its cycle structure gives an inverse action that can be recovered and applied in O(n) time from the stored entries.

Implications for the proposed mechanism

The first family invalidates a uniform locality-limited approximate-inverse guarantee. For the bidiagonal example, the inverse has long-range entries even though the matrix is sparse; a banded inverse representation loses a constant amount in preconditioned residual. Yet the original matrix admits a direct triangular solve in O(nnz(A)) arithmetic and storage. So inverse sparsity is not the right universal measure of solve cost: a sparse operator representation, such as a triangular solve, can be much cheaper than materializing its inverse. The second family shows that a solver must use the sparse graph or other information beyond κ₂(A); generic Krylov iteration can take n steps even when a graph-aware exact solve is linear-time.

These counterexamples do not rule out adaptively acquired nonlocal sparse patterns, hierarchical operators, sparse elimination, or matrix-free methods with additional assumptions. Nor do they establish an end-to-end solver advantage. Any comparison must charge the cost of reading/ordering the entries, choosing the representation, building and storing it, each preconditioner application, and the final solve. For a normwise forward-error target ε, a standard sufficient residual condition is ||b−Ax||₂/||b||₂≤ε/κ₂(A). Here the bidiagonal family has κ₂(A)≤2n, so certifying that forward error from this bound requires a residual tolerance that scales with n. The exact lower bound above is about operator quality, not rounding behavior or final solution error. A finite-precision result would additionally need to bound construction and application errors at the chosen precision.

Strongest relevant prior art and model boundary

Michele Benzi and Miroslav Tůma, “A Sparse Approximate Inverse Preconditioner for Nonsymmetric Linear Systems,” SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 19(3), 1998, https://www.cs.emory.edu/~benzi/Web_papers/ainv.pdf. They describe approximate inverse construction, adaptive pattern selection, and factorized sparse inverse methods. Their introduction explicitly notes that choosing a useful sparsity pattern is difficult for general sparse matrices, and that construction can be costly; the method targets favorable matrix classes and empirical performance rather than a uniform guarantee.

Zhongxiao Jia and Wenjie Kang, “A Residual Based Sparse Approximate Inverse Preconditioning Procedure for Large Sparse Linear Systems,” 2015, https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.04500. RSAI adapts patterns using dominant residual information and dropping. It is close prior art for the proposed adaptive acquisition route, but its reported evidence is numerical and does not prove a universal complexity or conditioning bound.

Anne Greenbaum, Vlastimil Pták, and Zdeněk Strakoš, “Any Nonincreasing Convergence Curve is Possible for GMRES,” SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications 17(3), 1996, https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479894275030. For any prescribed nonincreasing residual sequence, they construct a matrix and right-hand side realizing it, with the matrix eigenvalues chosen arbitrarily. This explains why eigenvalues alone are not a sufficient convergence certificate. Their theorem is not a sparse-input lower bound.

Michał Dereziński, Ethan N. Epperly, and Raphael A. Meyer, “The matrix-vector complexity of Ax=b,” COLT 2026, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/derezinski26a.html (preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04842). In a matrix-vector oracle model, their two-sided lower bound is Ω(κ log(1/ε)); their one-sided bound requires Ω(n) queries even for perfectly conditioned systems. This is important evidence against universal black-box preconditioning gains, but the theorem is for general oracle matrices and does not by itself prove a lower bound for explicit low-nnz CSR inputs where preprocessing may inspect entries and exploit the sparsity graph.

Achieved versus potential significance

Proved here: banded approximate inverses fail by a constant in the stated residual norm on an explicit sparse, nonnormal family; sparse nonnormal GMRES can stagnate for n−1 steps at condition number 2. Known: adaptive sparse approximate inverse and incomplete inverse methods already exist, and unrestricted GMRES convergence is not characterized by eigenvalues or κ alone. Not proved: a lower bound for all O(n)- or O(nnz)-storage preconditioners, a faster general sparse solver, or a finite-precision end-to-end guarantee. There is no landmark advance in this result. A potentially consequential direction is to learn nonlocal inverse actions as sparse operator factorizations or graph/path representations and certify their full build-plus-solve cost without materializing a dense inverse.

Next proof gate: determine whether the 1/(4e) obstruction extends from banded B to every B with at most s nonzeros per row for s=o(n), under ||I−BA||₂ or ||I−AB||₂. Prove such a bound, or give an explicit sparse B family that defeats it. This will distinguish a true storage bottleneck from the narrower locality barrier proved here.
