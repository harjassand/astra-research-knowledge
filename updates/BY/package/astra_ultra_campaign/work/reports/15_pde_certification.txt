Investigator 15 — Observable-specific PDE surrogate certification

Finding and exact target

For a fixed linear output of a parameterized coercive PDE, a rigorous online certificate need not require a uniformly accurate approximation of the whole state. The operative mechanism is classical primal–dual reduced-basis (RB) certification. It is already established for affine coercive elliptic problems relative to a finite-element truth discretization, and later work certifies against the exact infinite-dimensional weak solution. I found no landmark novelty in this route. The concrete potentially useful refinement is to pair the output-corrected dual-residual bound with the true-PDE residual machinery, and to count its full offline/online cost explicitly; this is a modular theorem-level combination, not yet an implemented or novel result here.

Interface: the user supplies a weak form a_mu(u_mu,v)=f_mu(v) on a specified Hilbert space X, a parameter domain D, one fixed bounded linear functional ell_mu, the parameter/coefficient evaluators, and certified stability data. The offline stage may solve high-fidelity primal and adjoint problems and construct residual certificates. A query mu returns an output estimate s_hat_mu and a guaranteed interval [s_hat_mu-Delta_mu,s_hat_mu+Delta_mu]. For an exact-PDE guarantee, the residual bounds must be in X' (or otherwise rigorously dominate those norms), and the stability lower bound must hold on all of D. A certificate relative only to the finite-element truth does not certify discretization error.

Reconstructed mechanism

Let a_mu be bounded and coercive, with alpha(mu)||v||_X^2 <= a_mu(v,v); for a nonsymmetric form use a certified inf-sup lower bound beta(mu)>0. Let u_N approximate u_mu and z_M approximate the adjoint z_mu, where a_mu(v,z_mu)=ell_mu(v). Define r_p(v)=f_mu(v)-a_mu(u_N,v) and r_d(v)=ell_mu(v)-a_mu(v,z_M). Then e=u_mu-u_N satisfies a_mu(e,v)=r_p(v), and the output identity is

ell_mu(u_mu)-[ell_mu(u_N)+r_p(z_M)] = r_d(e).

Indeed ell_mu(e)=a_mu(e,z_M)+r_d(e)=r_p(z_M)+r_d(e). Thus the corrected estimate s_hat=ell_mu(u_N)+r_p(z_M) obeys

|ell_mu(u_mu)-s_hat| <= ||r_d||_(X') ||r_p||_(X') / beta_LB(mu),

where 0<beta_LB(mu)<=beta(mu); in the symmetric coercive case beta may be replaced by alpha. This proof needs no approximation guarantee for u_N in a global output norm: it certifies one named functional through the primal and adjoint residuals. The adjoint is essential for a useful output bound, not decorative.

Two exact finite-dimensional witnesses delimit the gain. First, take A=diag(1,epsilon), f=(1,1), ell=e_1, epsilon>0, and primal/dual reduced spaces span{e_1}. Then u=(1,1/epsilon), u_N=z_M=e_1, r_p=(0,1), r_d=0, and the output is exactly 1 although the unreduced state error has Euclidean norm 1/epsilon. The product certificate is exactly zero. Second, for A=I_2, f=ell=(1,delta), and the same reduced space, the compliant output is s=1+delta^2 while s_N=1. The state error is delta, so the generic continuity bound ||ell|| ||e|| is O(delta), whereas the true output error is delta^2; at delta=10^-6 it is about 10^-6 versus 10^-12. The primal–dual residual product is delta^2. This independently reconstructs the known failure of primal-only output bounds to retain the quadratic goal-error effect.

Offline/online cost and limits

For an affine truth model A(mu)=sum_(q=1)^Qa theta_q(mu) A_q, f=sum_(q=1)^Qf theta_q f_q and ell=sum_(q=1)^Ql theta_q^ell ell_q, let primal and dual RB dimensions be N and M. Residual expansions have Kp=Qf+Qa N and Kd=Ql+Qa M generators. Offline, form N+M high-fidelity snapshots; construct Riesz representatives for these residual generators (Kp+Kd truth-size linear solves, often sharing a fixed Riesz factorization); form/store their Gram matrices; precompute reduced operators and output vectors; and build the stability lower-bound data (for example SCM). This costs truth solves and storage depending on the FE dimension n, the snapshot counts, Kp,Kd, and stability-training size. A useful accounting is T_off = (N+M+Kp+Kd) T_solve(n) + T_Gram(n,Kp,Kd) + T_SCM, with T_Gram=O(n(Kp^2+Kd^2)) for dense inner products of stored Riesz representatives. The campaign must not hide this expensive offline stage.

Online, evaluating the affine coefficients, solving the two reduced systems, evaluating the corrected output, and taking residual Gram quadratic forms costs O(Qa N^2+N^3 + Qa M^2+M^3 + Kp^2+Kd^2 + T_stability); this is independent of n but not of affine rank, reduced dimensions, or SCM size. Exact continuous-PDE certification replaces the finite-dimensional Riesz residual norm by a certified infinite-dimensional upper bound; it does not make its offline construction free. Nonaffine operators require controlled hyperreduction errors. Noncoercive or nearly resonant problems require a positive inf-sup bound and can lose sharpness or online efficiency. If only finitely many snapshots are available and no exact model/coefficient evaluator or regularity restriction is supplied, no arbitrary-query guarantee follows: a smooth parameter bump vanishing at every sampled parameter can change the output at an unsampled query without changing any acquired snapshot.

Prior art and consequence

The core finite-truth capability is explicit in Prud'homme et al., “Reliable Real-Time Solution of Parametrized Partial Differential Equations: Reduced-Basis Output Bound Methods” (2002), DOI https://doi.org/10.1115/1.1448332, and the detailed affine elliptic treatment of Rozza, Huynh, and Patera, “Reduced Basis Approximation and a Posteriori Error Estimation for Affinely Parametrized Elliptic Coercive Partial Differential Equations” (2008), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11831-008-9019-9. The latter gives a primal–dual output bound as a product of primal and dual residual norms over the coercivity bound and explicitly shows that omitting the adjoint can make effectivity diverge as the state error shrinks (Secs. 9.3, 11.2; equations (137)–(140)). This is decisive prior art against novelty for the proposed algebraic mechanism.

The truth-discretization gap is also addressed already. Yano, “A Minimum-Residual Mixed Reduced Basis Method: Exact Residual Certification and Simultaneous Finite-Element Reduced-Basis Refinement” (2016), DOI https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2015039, certifies a residual norm in the infinite-dimensional function space with online cost independent of FE resolution; that paper notes that a stability lower bound is still needed. Yano’s “A Reduced Basis Method for Coercive Equations with an Exact Solution Certificate and Spatio-Parameter Adaptivity: Energy-Norm and Output Error Bounds” (2018), DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1071341, supplies exact-weak-solution functional-output bounds, an infinite-dimensional residual upper bound, and an offline-online SCM stability lower bound. Goal-oriented dual-weighted residual reduction with controlled empirical-quadrature errors extends to nonlinear 3D RANS in Yano (2020), DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.6395. These results already cover the consequential capability in important linear classes and substantially weaken a broad nonlinear novelty claim.

Achieved status: the identity and product bound are proved here from the weak primal/adjoint equations; the two toy examples are exact algebra; the online complexity is a decomposition-level count conditional on affine data, certified residual norms, and stability-bound evaluation. The exact-PDE paired primal/dual implementation is a proposed composition of established components, not implemented here. No new theorem, numerical benchmark, or external validation is claimed.

Next proof gate: build the paired primal and adjoint minimum-residual mixed certificates for one noncompliant coercive elliptic benchmark, prove that both certificates dominate the true X' residuals uniformly over D, include every affine/hyperreduction term in the output identity, and compare its certified online cost and interval width against Yano 2018 and the 2020 nonlinear goal-oriented framework. Only a sharper proved bound or a measurable cost/accuracy advantage beyond those interfaces could support a novelty claim.
