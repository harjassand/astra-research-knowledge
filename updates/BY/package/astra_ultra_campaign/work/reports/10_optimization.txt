Target and outcome

Capability sought: return a certified additive-ε global minimizer of an expensive, nonconvex black-box objective on a high-dimensional box, while paying exponentially in a discoverable intrinsic dimension rather than the ambient dimension. The decisive mechanism tested is the global gradient-span rank

    r(f) = dim span{∇f(x) : x in int(D)}.

If r is small, the objective is exactly constant along the orthogonal complement of this span. The question is whether this global invariant can be cheaply acquired from oracle data without assuming that local samples represent the full landscape.

Conditional theorem reconstructed here

Let D=[-1,1]^n, and let f be real analytic on a connected open neighborhood of D, with ||∇f(x)||₂ ≤ G on D. An exact first-order oracle returns (f(x),∇f(x)); sample n points independently and uniformly from int(D). With probability one, the sampled gradients have rank exactly r(f). Given their span V and an orthonormal basis Q∈R^{n×r}, one can return x̂∈D with f(x̂)−min_D f≤ε using at most

    n + (ceil(2G sqrt(nr)/ε)+2)^r

oracle calls (the n discovery calls plus at most one objective call per reduced grid cell), plus one linear-feasibility problem per reduced grid cell. If r=0 is known, one evaluation suffices because f is constant. This is an oracle/real-arithmetic bound, not a bit-complexity result. For fixed r it is polynomial in n and 1/ε; it remains exponential in r.

Proof. Put V=span{∇f(x):x∈int(D)} and r=dim(V). If r>0, there are r interior points whose gradients are independent. Some r×r minor of the matrix of those gradients is nonzero. The same minor, viewed as a function of the r query locations, is real analytic on int(D)^r and is not identically zero. The zero set of a nonzero real-analytic function has Lebesgue measure zero. Thus r independent uniform locations produce independent gradients with probability one. All sampled gradients lie in V, so the first n samples recover V exactly almost surely. (For r=0, all interior gradients vanish.)

For x,y∈D with Qᵀx=Qᵀy, the segment between them lies in D and y−x∈V⊥. Therefore d f(x+t(y−x))/dt=0 and f(x)=f(y). Hence f(x)=g(Qᵀx) for a well-defined g on Z=QᵀD. On relint(Z), the linear map Qᵀ has local interior lifts; differentiating through any lift gives ∇g=Qᵀ∇f, so ||∇g||₂≤G. Convexity of Z and continuity extend the G-Lipschitz bound to all Z.

Now Z⊂[-sqrt(n),sqrt(n)]^r. Partition this box into cells of side h=ε/(G sqrt(r)). For each cell intersecting Z, find one z_C∈Z in that cell and a lift x_C∈D with Qᵀx_C=z_C. For the box D this is linear feasibility: -1≤x_i≤1 and z_C=Qᵀx. Every projected optimizer shares a cell with a representative, so their objective values differ by at most G sqrt(r)h=ε. Evaluating all representatives and returning the best proves the claim. There are at most (ceil(2sqrt(n)/h)+1)^r cells. When r=0, the segment argument shows f is constant.

This is a complete conditional derivation, not a new global-optimization theorem. The input contract matters: real analyticity, exact gradients and exact rank arithmetic are doing the acquisition work. Analyticity is not itself verifiable from a finite oracle transcript; finite precision can turn a tiny but nonzero gradient direction into an apparent null direction. The box-grid proof also says nothing about efficient bit-level construction of Q or solving its induced LPs when oracle data are arbitrary reals.

Adversarial instance: hidden smooth well

Drop analyticity and retain even a uniform Hessian bound. Choose a nonnegative radial ψ∈C∞_c(B(0,1)) with unique maximum ψ(0)=1 and nonzero radial derivative at some radius s∈(0,1). Pack N pairwise disjoint radius-ρ balls B(a_j,ρ) inside D and define

    f_0(x)=0,
    f_j(x)=−A ψ((x−a_j)/ρ),  j=1,…,N.

Each f_j is smooth, has its unique minimum −A at a_j, and its gradients over its support span R^n (at points a_j+sρ e_i, the gradients point along e_i). Its Hessian norm is at most A||∇²ψ||∞/ρ². Set A=2ε and choose ρ=sqrt(2ε||∇²ψ||∞/M) to keep the gradient Lipschitz constant at most M. For sufficiently small ε, the box contains a packing with N on the order of ρ^{-n}, hence on the order of (M/ε)^{n/2} up to dimension- and bump-dependent constants. Its gradient magnitude is O(sqrt(Mε)), so any fixed positive gradient bound G is also met for small enough ε.

Run any deterministic adaptive algorithm on f_0 with q oracle queries and record its output. If N>q+1, one packed ball contains neither a query nor the output. The matching f_j gives exactly the same values and gradients at every query, so by induction the algorithm follows the same transcript and returns a point of value 0, while min_D f_j=−2ε. It fails the requested ε guarantee. Under a uniform random hidden-ball choice, any randomized algorithm’s success probability is at most (q+1)/N. Thus local smoothness and finitely sampled gradients do not rule out a hidden direction or global well; the rank inferred from sampled gradients can be zero while the true rank is n. This is the decisive falsifier for a sample-rank-stabilization certificate on general C¹/C² objectives.

Prior art and novelty check

The core invariant and its use for global optimization are already directly studied. Cartis, Liang, Massart, and Otemissov, “Learning the subspace of variation for global optimization of functions with low effective dimension” (2024), define effective dimension through the gradient-covariance range, estimate it from sampled gradients, reduce to low-dimensional global subproblems, and give sampling/global-convergence bounds; the paper explicitly identifies independent sampled gradients as sufficient for a successful reduction. Their quantitative sampling bound depends on spectral information including the smallest nonzero covariance eigenvalue. This is stronger practical prior art than the exact-analytic observation above, and rules out novelty for the proposed mechanism. Constantine, Dow, and Wang’s “Active subspace methods in theory and practice: applications to kriging surfaces” (2014) and Constantine and Gleich’s “Computing active subspaces with Monte Carlo” (2015) establish the broader gradient-covariance dimension-reduction framework and its sample estimation. Vinod, Israel, and Topcu’s “Constrained, Global Optimization of Unknown Functions with Lipschitz Continuous Gradients” (SIAM J. Optim. 2022) gives certified first-order global optimization for nonconvex black boxes through covering bounds, at substantially more difficult subproblems.

Status and consequence

Proved here: exact rank acquisition almost surely for analytic f under an exact gradient oracle; exact ridge reduction; a fixed-r global ε-grid algorithm; the compactly supported hidden-well counterexample and its packing-scale obstruction. Known: active-subspace estimation and reduced global optimization with quantitative sampling assumptions. Conditional: the claimed query bound under analytic inputs, exact arithmetic, and G-Lipschitzness. Refuted: the idea that finite gradient-span stabilization certifies global rank for general smooth objectives. Unproved/unvalidated: bit-model efficiency, noisy/finite-precision acquisition, and any practical classwide certificate of the required global rank.

Potential significance would be a robust, cheaply checkable rank-visibility certificate that survives finite precision and excludes hidden wells; that would convert active-subspace heuristics into a sound global solver for a materially larger class. Achieved significance is a bounded proof and a sharp obstruction, not a landmark capability. Next proof gate: formulate and prove a finite-precision acquisition theorem using an explicit lower bound on the smallest nonzero gradient-covariance eigenvalue (or an equivalent visibility margin), then determine whether the resulting reduced-space global certificate has total cost polynomial in n, 1/ε, and the stated condition parameters without importing an unverified prior model.
