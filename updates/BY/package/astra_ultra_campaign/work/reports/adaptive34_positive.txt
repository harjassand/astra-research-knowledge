Constructive follow-up 34 — certify integrated dissipation through actuator power

Status. I derived a finite-sensor certificate for a nonlinear PDE observable, proved its error interval, and checked its sharp storage envelope on an exact Navier–Stokes shear solution. The certificate concerns time-averaged viscous dissipation, not pointwise vorticity or the Euler BKM integral. The energy balance and port viewpoint are established prior art; no novelty claim is made for either.

Capability. On a two-dimensional no-slip channel, certify the average viscous dissipation over a chosen time window without reconstructing the velocity field, computing Fourier modes, or differentiating sampled data. The output is a rigorous interval for

    epsilon_bar_T = (nu/T) integral_0^T < |grad u(t)|^2 > dt,

where <.> is the spatial average. This is a nonlinear, high-gradient quantity used in fluid heating and energy-budget analysis. The high-frequency shear counterexample from report 34 can make instantaneous vorticity arbitrarily large while energy and enstrophy stay fixed, but its total viscous dissipation over a time interval is constrained by the energy supply and storage balance. The new interface measures that balance directly.

PDE and acquisition interface. Let Omega=(R/L_x Z) x (0,L_y), periodic in x and no-slip at y=0,L_y. Take the incompressible 2D Navier–Stokes equations

    u_t + (u.grad)u + grad p = nu Delta u + f(x,t),   div u=0,
    u|_{y=0,L_y}=0.

Use a finite number q of known actuator shapes b_r(x), with f(x,t)=sum_r a_r(t)b_r(x); impose the certified force bound ||f(t)||_2 <= F. The actuator ports expose only the scalar conjugate outputs y_r(t)=<b_r,u(t)>, or directly their signed work rates a_r(t)y_r(t). A work meter returns an interval [W_-,W_+] containing

    W_T = integral_0^T <f(t),u(t)> dt = sum_r integral_0^T a_r(t)y_r(t) dt.

This port is physically realizable: collocated actuator force and velocity measure power. For a pressure-driven periodic channel cell, q=1, f=g(t)e_x, and y_1 is the cross-section-averaged flow rate. If the imposed pressure rise is Delta-p(t) over streamwise length L_x at density rho, then g=Delta-p/(rho L_x); for cross-section A_c and volumetric flow Q, y_1=Q/A_c, so the normalized input power is exactly g y_1=Delta-p Q/(rho L_x A_c). Stationary no-slip walls supply no mechanical power. The PDE state is not sent to the certifier. Exact rest can be replaced by a certified initial-energy cap: if K(0)<=kappa_0, the bound below accounts for that residual. A cap can be produced by turning the forcing off for a reset interval t_r: unforced Poincare decay gives K(0)<=K_pre exp(-2 gamma t_r), so t_r >= (2 gamma)^(-1) log(K_pre/kappa_0) suffices. This reset requires a qualified pre-reset energy bound K_pre, but no state sensors.

Theorem and proof. Set K(t)=<|u(t)|^2>/2 and gamma=nu lambda_1, where the channel Poincare constant is lambda_1=(pi/L_y)^2. Since 2D smooth solutions exist globally for smooth forcing, the energy equality holds on every finite interval. Multiplication of the equation by u and spatial integration cancel the nonlinear and pressure terms and give

    K'(t) + epsilon(t) = <f,u>,
    epsilon(t) = nu <|grad u|^2>.

By Cauchy–Schwarz and Poincare,

    K' + nu lambda_1 <|u|^2> <= F sqrt(<|u|^2>).

For R(t)=sqrt(<|u|^2>)=sqrt(2K(t)), this implies R'+gamma R<=F. If K(0)<=kappa_0, comparison yields

    R(t) <= sqrt(2 kappa_0) exp(-gamma t)
            + (F/gamma)(1-exp(-gamma t)),
    K(T) <= K_*(T;kappa_0) := 1/2 [sqrt(2 kappa_0) exp(-gamma T)
            + (F/gamma)(1-exp(-gamma T))]^2.

Integrating the energy equality gives the exact identity

    integral_0^T epsilon(t)dt = W_T - K(T).

Therefore the certified output is

    epsilon_bar_T in [ max(0,W_- - K_*(T;kappa_0))/T,
                       (W_+ + kappa_0)/T ].

If the reported work interval is centered at W_hat with half-width eta_W, the interval width is at most (K_*(T;kappa_0)+kappa_0+2 eta_W)/T. Exact rest is the special case kappa_0=0. The state-dependent missing storage term therefore costs O(1/T) at long horizons, with an explicit coefficient. No spatial interpolation, tail norm, or temporal derivative of u is needed.

Sharpness and total cost. The storage envelope for exact rest is attained by a one-mode shear. Let b(y)=sqrt(2) sin(pi y/L_y)e_x, normalized so <|b|^2>=1, and force f=F b. The exact solution u=q(t)b has zero convective term and satisfies q'+gamma q=F, q(0)=0. Thus R(T)=(F/gamma)(1-exp(-gamma T)) and K(T)=K_*(T;0). This shows the energy envelope is sharp when only the force norm, viscosity, geometry, and horizon are used; a certifier exploiting more of the actuator response may do better. The companion script work/reports/adaptive34_positive/verify_certificate.py checks the exact-mode energy identity at 60 decimal digits for four horizons; the computed residuals are below 1e-57, and K(T) attains the stated envelope to the same precision.

For q ports with continuous output error |y_hat_r-y_r|<=delta_r and exact control signals, a valid work half-width is eta_W <= sum_r delta_r ||a_r||_{L1(0,T)} + eta_int, where eta_int is the certified power-integrator error. With N digital time bins, use a meter that reports a certified interval for each bin's integrated work; then eta_int is the sum of those N bin errors, with no unaccounted quadrature remainder. The data cost is q actuator commands, q scalar port channels (or one aggregate power channel), and N scalar work intervals (or one running totalizer). For the pressure-driven specialization, this is one pressure-drop channel, one bulk-flow meter, and one power accumulator. Storage parameters needed are nu, L_y, F, and either exact startup rest or K_pre plus reset time; no state-resolution cost grows with spatial dimension or frequency cutoff.

The residual-energy allowance kappa_0 is also necessary if the instrument knows only an upper bound on initial storage. With zero forcing, compare rest with u_N(y,0)=2 sqrt(kappa_0) sin(2 pi N y/L_y)e_x. Each u_N is a no-slip steady-shape shear for Navier–Stokes: it decays by exp(-nu(2 pi N/L_y)^2 t), has zero bulk-flow signal for all t, and dissipates kappa_0[1-exp(-2nu(2 pi N/L_y)^2T)] of energy. Thus the same zero work and zero flow record, plus the same cap K(0)<=kappa_0, permits dissipation approaching kappa_0 as N grows; the upper interval endpoint cannot omit the kappa_0/T term on this interface. As a sensor-error example, if g is known exactly and the bulk-flow sensor has uniform error delta_U, then |W_hat-W|<=delta_U integral_0^T |g(t)|dt + eta_int. If pressure-gradient measurement also has error delta_g, use |U(t)|<=sqrt(2 kappa_0)+F/gamma to add at most T[(sup|g|)delta_U+(sqrt(2 kappa_0)+F/gamma)delta_g+delta_g delta_U]+eta_int. These terms enter eta_W directly. An unbounded force, uncertified power reading, or unqualified initial-energy/reset bound invalidates the stated interval; the report does not hide those costs.

Prior art and scope. Ladyzhenskaia, “Solution ‘in the large’ of the nonstationary boundary value problem for the Navier–Stokes system with two space variables” (1959), is foundational prior art for global 2D solvability: https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160120303. Califano, Rashad, Schuller, and Stramigioli, “Geometric and energy-aware decomposition of the Navier–Stokes equations: A port-Hamiltonian approach” (Physics of Fluids, 2021), develops the energy and power-port structure for fluids: https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0048359. The exact energy identity itself is elementary and standard. The derived contribution here is only the explicit sensing interface, terminal-storage envelope, and total error propagation for this observable; the literature search is not a novelty proof.

Achieved and potential significance. Achieved: a proof-backed, constant-channel-count certificate for total viscous heating in a globally regular nonlinear flow model, independent of hidden high-frequency field detail. This could make energy budgets in pressure-driven channels auditable from control and flow instrumentation. It does not certify local dissipation, enstrophy, maximum vorticity, or 3D regularity. For 3D Navier–Stokes, the same energy calculation yields the interval only on an interval where a smooth solution and energy equality are independently justified; a Leray–Hopf energy inequality alone does not give the lower endpoint. Potential: extend the same supply-storage certificate to compressible fluids, reactive flows, and coupled thermal systems, where total energy is a scalar storage and actuator/boundary power is measurable.

Next proof gate. Establish a robust version with uncertain viscosity and density and independently calibrated pressure/flow sensors. Extend the pressure-power reduction to an open finite channel only after adding and measuring its inlet/outlet kinetic-energy fluxes and pressure work. Until then, the theorem applies to the stated periodic 2D cell and idealized bounded-error port measurements, with residual initial energy handled by kappa_0.
