Chemical mechanism discovery: a finite jump-resolved design, with a hard limit on novelty

Question and interface

The target is the complete set of distinct mass-action reactions and their rate constants from interventions and observations, not merely a vector field that predicts concentration trajectories. Assume a known list of n species, no hidden species, a known upper bound d on reactant molecularity, no self-reactions y -> y, and a well-mixed stochastic mass-action model. A reaction y -> y' has source y in C_d = {y in Z_+^n : |y| <= d}, jump nu = y' - y, and propensity k_(y,nu) (z)_y at molecule-count state z, where (z)_y = product_i z_i(z_i-1)...(z_i-y_i+1). Identical reactions with identical (y,nu) are merged and their constants summed; event data cannot identify a physical multiplicity of indistinguishable parallel copies.

Proposed measurement interface: prepare the exact count state z, record every species count and event time at single-event resolution, and repeat to estimate each outgoing jump rate q_nu(z). This is a precise intervention protocol in the CTMC model. It presumes independent preparation of species counts and no missed or simultaneous events. It is physically plausible only in small-count systems with adequate single-molecule readout; it is not claimed to be routine for arbitrary chemistry. The acquisition interface is stronger than sparse concentration time courses, and that change matters.

No-go result for concentration-only identification

For formula-level chemical balance, let A have elemental composition 2E and let B and C be distinct isomers with composition E. Compare (i) A -> 2B and A -> 2C, each with rate k[A], with (ii) A -> B+C with rate 2k[A]. Their deterministic mass-action vector fields in coordinates (A,B,C) are identical: (-2,2,2)k[A]. Yet the event jumps are respectively (-1,2,0), (-1,0,2), versus (-1,1,1). Thus even exact knowledge of the full vector field on every concentration, and every concentration trajectory from every initial condition, cannot reveal which channel structure generated it. Any concentration-only adaptive experiment has identical answers on this pair. This is a concrete instance of the established dynamic-equivalence obstruction, not a new one. Craciun and Pantea, “Identifiability of Chemical Reaction Networks” (2008), explicitly show non-unique structures and parameters under complete deterministic dynamics: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10910-007-9307-x. Szederkényi et al., “Inference of complex biological networks: distinguishability issues and optimization-based solutions” (2012), likewise show that sparsity alone does not generally select the true structure: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3305990/.

Finite exact design for the richer event interface

For each integer jump nu, the generator's outgoing transition rate is

    q_nu(z) = sum_{y in C_d} theta_(y,nu) (z)_y,

where theta_(y,nu) is the rate for y -> y+nu, or zero if that product is not a nonnegative integer vector. This is a polynomial in the falling-factorial basis. Set N = |C_d| = binom(n+d,d), and query the N initial count states z in C_d. Form P[z,y]=(z)_y. Order rows and columns by total degree. P[z,y]=0 unless y_i<=z_i for every i, which implies |y|<=|z|; within each equal-degree block only the diagonal survives. Hence P is triangular with diagonal P[y,y]=product_i y_i!, so det(P)=product_{y in C_d} product_i y_i! != 0. For every observed nu, solve P theta_nu = (q_nu(z))_(z in C_d). This recovers every rate and source complex without searching over candidate product complexes or subsets of a reaction library. Every positive-rate channel is observed at z=y, because (y)_y>0 and no other source y' not coordinatewise below y can fire there; after solving, theta_(y,nu)>0 gives the product y+nu. The unique output is the network modulo merging duplicate (y,nu) channels.

This proof also supplies an exact algebraic certificate: the N queried rates and reconstructed coefficients obey P theta_nu=q_nu, all coefficients are nonnegative, and the nonzero entries enumerate the reconstructed reactions. If d is fixed, N grows polynomially in n; the solve is triangular and can be done in O(N^2) arithmetic per observed jump, with at most r distinct jump vectors for r reactions. If product molecularity is also bounded by p, the candidate directed-edge library has size O(|C_d||C_p|), and candidate networks are exponential in that size; the design avoids enumerating candidate destinations and networks. Without a product bound, that comparison is not finite, while the transition-based reconstruction still works because destinations are read from observed jumps. It does not yet provide O(s log N) experiments when only s reactions are active: it uses N count states, independent of s. For n=2,d=2, exact calculation gives C_d={(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)} and det(P)=4; the companion check is in work/reports/14_chemical_search/check_example.py.

Finite-data boundary and sparse-design attempt

Real experiments estimate q_nu(z) from dwell times and event counts; they do not return exact real hazards. If the vector of estimates has coordinatewise error at most epsilon, then coefficient error is bounded by ||P^{-1}||_infinity epsilon. With a known separation k_min>0 for every true nonzero rate, support is certified when ||P^{-1}||_infinity epsilon < k_min/2. Without a lower bound on positive rates, no finite sample can certify that a channel is absent: arbitrarily small positive rates approach the null model's data law. This is a true statistical identifiability boundary, not a numerical nuisance. Conditioning and replicate cost therefore need to be reported for any deployment.

I also checked whether sparsity immediately shrinks the design. For a fixed jump nu, known sparsity cap s, and 2s<=N, a generic 2s-point integer-state evaluation matrix is full spark: every set of at most 2s distinct falling-factorial polynomials is linearly independent on the integer lattice (a polynomial vanishing on all of Z_+^n is zero, by induction on n); each required minor is therefore a nonzero polynomial, and their product is nonzero at some integer tuple. Thus 2s evaluations identify an s-sparse coefficient vector information-theoretically. This is an existence argument, not a practical design or decoder. Checking all minors can require binom(N,2s) tests; I have no efficient constructive design with a certified stable decoder under bounded, realizable count ranges. I do not claim a sparse-experiment algorithm from this observation.

Prior-art and status

Enciso, Erban, and Kim, “Identifiability of Stochastically Modelled Reaction Networks” (2021), already establish reconstruction from stochastic transition vectors/rates and study estimation from temporal trajectories, including simulations: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792520000492 (open manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02272). Thus the central insight that jump-resolved stochastic data can distinguish networks hidden by deterministic averaging is known. The triangular count-state design above is a direct derivation of a finite unisolvent acquisition schedule; I found no basis in this bounded pass for a novelty claim, and have not shown it strengthens that paper's identifiability and reconstruction results. Faul, Hoessly, and Xia, “Identifiability of SDEs for Reaction Networks” (2026), further show that even drift plus diffusion of a Langevin approximation may remain non-identifying, and their theorem is for a known complete network with all species observed: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382. This reinforces why event-resolved CTMC jumps, rather than Gaussian noise summaries, are the relevant information in the counterexample.

Proved here: the exact deterministic counterexample and the finite triangular inversion theorem under the stated CTMC interface. Source-reported / independently reconstructed: prior deterministic non-identifiability and stochastic transition-data reconstruction. Conditional: full mechanism reconstruction from exact event rates over C_d; finite-sample certification additionally needs an error model, rate separation, and conditioning bounds. Not achieved: sparse-in-s active design, proof of computational advantage beyond avoiding product-network enumeration, partial-observation reconstruction, or empirical single-molecule validation. Significance achieved is a clean acquisition theorem and a sharp interface boundary, not a landmark capability. Potential significance would be a certified low-budget design that finds rate-separated mechanisms with substantially fewer than binom(n+d,d) initial conditions.

Next proof gate: derive an adaptive, noise-stable O(poly(s,n,d,log(1/delta))) count-state design and decoder for unknown s-sparse positive falling-factorial propensities, or prove a matching lower bound under bounded molecule counts; then compare it directly with Enciso–Erban–Kim's reconstruction conditions on a blinded sparse CRN family. Until that gate passes, retain the N-state theorem as a known, useful construction rather than a discovery claim.
