ASSIGNMENT 05 — GIBBS SAMPLING ACROSS HIDDEN PHASES
Status: sharp black-box obstruction proved; broad novelty not claimed
Date: 2026-10-10

Question and interface

Can an adaptive sampler cross an unknown metastable phase without a supplied phase decomposition? I tested a universal black-box route: query an unnormalized Gibbs weight at chosen configurations, learn where a phase lies, then sample it by adaptive stratification. The hard family below refutes any uniformly sublinear-in-phase-rarity guarantee, even when the phase size and partition function are supplied. The result is a finite-state, worst-case oracle theorem, not a lower bound for local physical Hamiltonians.

Finite hidden-phase theorem

Let Ω have N states and let S⊂Ω be an unknown set of known size m, with p=m/N≤1/16. The input oracle answers a point query x with whether x∈S. Define weights
  w_S(x)=1                         if x∈S,
          p/(1-p)                  if x∉S.
Equivalently, at inverse temperature 1 the energy is 0 on S and Δ=log((1-p)/p) off S. The partition function is exactly
  Z=m+(N-m)p/(1-p)=2m,
so the target Gibbs law π_S gives each phase probability 1/2 and is uniform conditional on either phase. The sampler is told N,m,Z and the two possible weights; it is not told S. It may query any state adaptively, use arbitrary computation and random bits, and must output one state whose law is within ε total variation of π_S for every S.

For any sampler using at most q distinct weight queries, average its output phase mass over S drawn uniformly from all m-subsets. Let a be the probability that one of its queries hits S. Until the first hit, every answer is “outside,” so the queried sequence is independent of the hidden S; a≤qm/N. If it never hits S, conditional on the transcript the remaining hidden set is a uniform m-subset of the N−q unqueried points. Thus any output has conditional chance at most m/(N−q) to lie in S (zero if it is one of the queried points). On a hit, bound the phase chance by 1. Consequently,
  E_S[Pr(output∈S)] ≤ qm/N + m/(N−q).
Accuracy for every S requires the left side to be at least 1/2−ε, since total variation bounds the error on event S. Hence
  1/2−ε ≤ qm/N + m/(N−q).                         (1)
For ε≤1/8 and p≤1/16, any q≤N/(8m) makes the right side at most 1/8+(8/7)p≤11/56<3/8, contradicting 1/2−ε≥3/8. Therefore q>N/(8m)=1/(8p). This is a worst-case query lower bound against every adaptive randomized classical sampler. It already holds with the exact normalizer and phase mass supplied; a free-energy estimate cannot locate the phase.

The order is sharp in this oracle model. Flip a fair coin to choose a phase. For S, draw uniform points from Ω and query until one lies in S; for Sᶜ, do the same until one lies outside. Conditional on acceptance each output is uniform in its phase, so the mixture is exactly π_S. Its expected query cost is
  (1/2)(1/p)+(1/2)(1/(1-p))=Θ(1/p).
For fixed target error, truncation gives O(p⁻¹ log(1/ε)) worst-case queries. This closes the order-of-growth gap for point-evaluation access.

The same family certifies a local-dynamics bottleneck. On Ω={0,1}ⁿ, run single-bit Metropolis with a uniformly chosen coordinate flip. From any x∈S, a proposed move outside S raises the energy by Δ and is accepted with probability exp(−Δ)=p/(1−p). Therefore P_x(Sᶜ)≤p/(1−p). Start from μ₀=π_S conditioned on S. By a union bound, μ₀Pᵗ(S)≥1−t p/(1−p), while π_S(S)=1/2, so
  ||μ₀Pᵗ−π_S||TV ≥ 1/2−t p/(1−p).
In particular the 1/4 mixing time is at least (1−p)/(8p), up to integer rounding. With n even and m=2^(n/2), both the query and local-mixing bounds are exponential, Ω(2^(n/2)); the energy gap is Δ=log(2^(n/2)−1). This is a conductance/retention argument, not a simulation.

Explicit-model escape and boundary

The black-box bound does not rule out structure-aware samplers. For the same p=2^(-d), take the succinctly described phase S={x: x₁=…=x_d=0}. No separate phase list is an input: the predicate is part of the explicit model. The exact sampler flips a fair coin; on the S branch it outputs 0^d followed by n−d fair bits; on the complement branch it draws uniform n-bit strings until the first d bits are not all zero. This is uniform within each phase and assigns probability 1/2 to each. It uses expected at most 2 uniform proposals on the complement branch and O(n) random bits overall, despite the same exponentially slow single-bit Metropolis chain. This is routine exploitation of a succinct sufficient description, not a general discovery algorithm.

The hidden-S construction permits arbitrary high-order, nonlocal energy functions. Its theorem must not be projected onto bounded-degree or finite-range spin systems, where locality may reveal structure. Nor does it rule out cluster moves, tempering, latent-variable samplers, coupling-from-the-past, or model-specific exact reductions. It establishes that a universal adaptive certificate cannot be extracted from pointwise energies alone: a hidden phase carrying constant Gibbs mass can remain invisible for Ω(1/p) queries even with Z known.

Prior art and novelty status

The closest current result is Olivucci et al., “Provable Quantum-Classical Separation for Continuous Gibbs Sampling” (arXiv:2608.24527v2, submitted Aug. 2026, revised Sep. 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24527. Its source-reported theorem hides a translated smooth bump on a torus and proves Ω(α) classical local-oracle queries for a Gevrey class, including access to derivatives of every order; it also gives an annealed quantum sampler with Õ(√α) queries under its stated regularity/interface. Its hide-and-seek proof is substantially stronger in smooth continuous setting than the discrete finite construction here. I read and reconstructed the relevant lower-bound argument (average over a hidden translate; all off-patch oracle responses match a null run), but did not audit the paper’s quantum upper bound. The discrete proof above is therefore not presented as novel: it is a transparent finite Gibbs analogue with exact known Z, a balanced hidden phase, and a matching rejection-sampling upper bound.

Other relevant primary sources:
- J. G. Propp and D. B. Wilson, “Exact Sampling with Coupled Markov Chains and Applications to Statistical Mechanics,” 1996, https://doi.org/10.1002/%28SICI%291098-2418%28199608/09%299%3A1/2%3C223%3A%3AAID-RSA14%3E3.0.CO%3B2-O. Coupling from the past yields exact samples when the coupled chains coalesce; it supplies no barrier-independent runtime.
- J. Ding, E. Lubetzky, and Y. Peres, “The Mixing Time Evolution of Glauber Dynamics for the Mean-Field Ising Model,” https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1906. It establishes the known Curie-Weiss slow-mixing regime, including exponential mixing at fixed β>1.
- E. Viola, “The Complexity of Distributions,” https://doi.org/10.1137/100814998. This studies sampling/representation lower bounds and hidden-set membership data structures in restricted circuit models; it is related context, not the same oracle theorem.

Achieved significance is a compact, exact obstruction and matched oracle upper bound, plus an explicit counterexample to equating slow local mixing with hard sampling. It is not a new universal Gibbs-sampling capability or a new general lower-bound technique. Potential significance would come from transferring the obstruction to a physically relevant local-interaction class, or proving a structure condition that yields a sampler without prelabelled phases.

Next proof gate

Prove or refute the following bounded-interaction extension: for some fixed interaction range and degree, construct a succinct finite-range spin Hamiltonian with a constant-mass hidden metastable phase, known partition function, and local-oracle transcripts that force Ω(exp(cn)) classical queries; or show that the locality constraints enable a subexponential sampler. The arbitrary-subset family above does not meet this gate.
