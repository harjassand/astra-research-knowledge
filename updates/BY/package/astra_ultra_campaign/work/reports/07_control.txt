07 — Safe control and identification under unknown nonlinear dynamics
Status: exact impossibility established for the unrestricted smooth class; no new positive capability established.

Question and interface

I tested the broadest reading of the assigned target: can an online controller safely identify a useful action for an otherwise unknown nonlinear plant without a global dynamics model or expensive reachability calculation? Use a deterministic, discrete-time, fully observed scalar plant

    x[t+1] = f(x[t], u[t]),    x[0]=0,
    safe set X=[-1,1],    input set U=[-1,1].

The controller selects u[t], then receives x[t+1]. Every selection is a physical transition, and only the resulting state is observed; there is no counterfactual query, reset, or free experiment. Give the controller the favorable certified hold promise f(x,0)=0 for all x, so holding u=0 is known safe forever. Its target is to identify, in finite time, whether some nonzero action is safe at x=0 (or its one-step response). The only further model promise is f is C-infinity. This is a deterministic safety requirement at every time, not a high-probability guarantee.

Exact obstruction

The hold action alone reveals nothing beyond f(x,0)=0. More strongly, no deterministic policy can both remain safe for every plant in this class and ever apply a nonzero input. Run any policy on f0(x,u)=0. If it ever selects a first nonzero input u* at time tau, every earlier action was zero and every observed state was zero. Select r with 0<r<|u*| and define the smooth bump

    eta(s) = exp(1 - 1/(1-s^2))  if |s|<1, and 0 otherwise,
    f1(x,u) = 2 eta((u-u*)/r).

The function eta is C-infinity, eta(0)=1, and its support avoids u=0. Thus f1 is C-infinity, f1(x,0)=0, and f1 agrees with f0 on the entire history before tau. The policy must therefore select the same u* under f1. But x[tau+1]=f1(x[tau],u*)=2, outside X. This contradicts uniform safety. Consequently any uniformly safe policy must hold at zero forever, and cannot identify any nonzero action. The proof charges the first probe as an actual transition; it grants no free state/action sampling.

This is not merely a finite-data limitation of one estimator. For any finite set of queried state/action pairs, a smooth bump supported away from them changes the response at an unqueried pair while preserving all observations. So finite exact global identification is impossible for the infinite-dimensional C-infinity class. Approximate identification also has no uniform finite rate without a known modulus of continuity or other complexity bound: the bump can be made arbitrarily narrow while retaining fixed height. A controller may safely do nothing, or identify after being given a safe-experiment contract, but the stated interface cannot yield both guarantees.

The boundary is sharp in the relevant direction. If a known Lipschitz constant L is supplied and f(0,0)=0, then |f(0,u)| <= L|u|, so |u|<=1/L is certified safe for one transition. That creates an initial safe probing neighborhood from known margin and regularity; it does not identify a general L-Lipschitz function exactly. Uniform approximation to error epsilon on a scalar action interval still requires a worst-case O(L/epsilon) cover, and a d-dimensional action box requires order (L/epsilon)^d samples. Without known L, these bounds provide no finite guarantee. A multi-step claim additionally needs a recursively invariant safe region, a known fallback policy, or an equivalent certificate; one-step safety does not imply future feasibility.

Prior-art audit and comparison

The core components of the plausible positive story already have close prior art, but each pays for structure or a safety premise. Nejati, Zhong, Caccamo, and Zamani, “Data-Driven Controller Synthesis of Unknown Nonlinear Polynomial Systems via Control Barrier Certificates” (L4DC 2022), construct data-driven barrier certificates and synthesize a polynomial state-feedback controller from a single trajectory under a rank condition, with a claimed certainty-one guarantee. This is a finite polynomial-type class and an excitation/rank condition, not unrestricted nonlinear identification: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v168/nejati22a.html.

Capone, Cosner, Ames, and Hirche, “Safe Online Dynamics Learning with Initially Unknown Models and Infeasible Safety Certificates” (2023), combine GP/RKHS model learning and a robust CBF-SOCP. Their theorem claims finite recovery of certificate feasibility before reaching the safe-set boundary, without a backup nonlearning controller, under regularity, strict feasibility and sampling-frequency assumptions. It is the closest precedent to “learn until the safety filter becomes feasible”; it does not remove the quantitative assumptions needed to make the bump adversary impossible: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02133.

Koller, Berkenkamp, Turchetta, and Krause, “Learning-based Model Predictive Control for Safe Exploration” (2018), propagate GP confidence sets over multi-step trajectories and use a terminal known-safe set/backup controller for recursive feasibility. This addresses safe exploration but retains set propagation and a safety anchor: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08287. Luo, Sun, and Kapoor, “Sample-efficient Safe Learning for Online Nonlinear Control with Control Barrier Functions” (2022), provide a sample-efficiency/regret and probabilistic-safety analysis for a structured episodic CBF setting: https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14419. Finally, Sawarkar and Jagtap’s 2025 “Control Barrier Function for Unknown Systems: An Approximation-free Approach” avoids online identification by assuming bounded unknown drift and a sign-definite input gain, and confines the true system around a designed virtual system; it illustrates that model-free control is possible under different control authority promises, not with no structure at all: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23022.

I therefore found no novelty in the general proposal to combine learning, CBFs, safe exploration, or polynomial data-driven synthesis. The actual obstruction is access and class complexity: smoothness without a known quantitative bound supplies no safe nonzero probe. Replacing reachability by a barrier constraint changes the computation, but does not conjure the missing safety information. Conversely, a known invariant excitation set can make identification safe by construction, but then its safety is a supplied resource that must be charged to the capability claim.

Finding, falsifier, and significance

The decisive falsifier is a universally safe policy that, from the stated observations and only the C-infinity promise plus known zero-hold, ever applies u!=0. The bump construction refutes every such deterministic policy. Randomization does not rescue a pathwise guarantee: a positive-probability set of first nonzero actions contains a compact interval of positive probability, and a smooth bump can be chosen unsafe throughout that interval while remaining zero at the hold input.

A positive result would be consequential if it gave a computable safe excitation mechanism and near-minimal identification cost for a broad nonlinear class without global models or trajectory reachability. This attempt does not establish that capability. It proves a precise boundary and confirms that current positive routes use rank/finite-dimensional structure, a known quantitative uncertainty model, or a certified safe region. The strongest achieved significance is a no-free-lunch obstruction for the unrestricted interface, not a landmark control method.

Next proof gate

For any positive continuation, first name the exact dynamics class and observation/noise precision, then give a computable nontrivial safe experiment set or a quantitative regularity-plus-margin certificate that survives every model still consistent with data. Prove that each acquisition transition remains inside this set, derive worst-case identification/query cost (including failed or uninformative probes), and compare against polynomial data-driven CBF synthesis and GP safe learning under the same access. Without a theorem showing a strict capability or cost separation, the claim remains unresolved.
