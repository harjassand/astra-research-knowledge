Geometric analysis: spectral-count transfer to 3D Ricci geometry

Finding

There is a concrete candidate extension of the sharp Yau harmonic-dimension bound. Let (M^3,g) be complete with Ric_g >= 0, positive asymptotic volume ratio, and a unique tangent cone at infinity. The exact claim is

    dim H_d(M) <= dim H_d(R^3) = (d+1)^2,  d = 0,1,2,...,

with equality at any d >= 1 forcing M to be isometric to R^3. This appears to follow by composing Huang's unique-cone harmonic bound with the 2026 finite spectral-count theorem for Alexandrov two-spheres. The new transfer is from sectional curvature to Ricci curvature, while preserving an exact finite-degree statement and rigidity. It does not remove the unique-tangent-cone hypothesis.

The exact inequality and its mechanism

For a compact Alexandrov two-sphere X with curvature at least 1, the source theorem states

    N_X(l(l+1)) <= (l+1)^2,

where N_X(L) counts Laplace eigenvalues <= L with multiplicity. Equality for any integer l >= 1 forces X to be the unit round sphere. The comparison is at the full round spectral clusters, not an asymptotic Weyl estimate.

The counting recursion can be reconstructed from the line-bundle ladder in Lin–Wang–Xu. At rung m, a closed first-order operator B_m has partner operators B_m*B_m and B_m B_m*. Their positive spectra agree with multiplicity; ker(B_m*)=0; and dim ker(B_m) <= 2m+1 (equality in the smooth sphere case). The Bochner–Kodaira identity gives the quadratic-form order for the next shifted rung. Min–max therefore gives, for A_m=B_m*B_m+m(m+1)I and C_m=B_m B_m*+m(m+1)I,

    N_{A_m}(L) <= dim ker(B_m) + N_{C_m}(L)
                         <= (2m+1) + N_{A_{m+1}}(L).

Iterating from m=0 through l at L=l(l+1), the terminal operator A_{l+1} has bottom at least (l+1)(l+2)>l(l+1). Thus

    N_X(l(l+1)) <= sum_{m=0}^l (2m+1) = (l+1)^2.

For singular Alexandrov spheres, the source replaces the smooth identity by a weak form order and controls the holomorphic kernel dimensions by the same 2m+1. Its equality proof first excludes curvature atoms and then uses an exact defect identity to force zero curvature defect. I reconstructed the counting algebra, not the full singular-domain argument; the complete theorem is a source claim in a recent preprint, not externally certified here.

Transfer to Ricci-nonnegative three-manifolds

Assume M has a unique tangent cone at infinity C(X). Positive AVR makes the blow-down noncollapsed. Cheeger–Colding cone theory gives the cross-section X dimension two and synthetic Ricci curvature at least 1. The cone measure is the three-dimensional Hausdorff measure, so its polar decomposition gives the link its two-dimensional Hausdorff reference measure. Lytchak–Stadler prove that an RCD(1,2) space with this measure is an Alexandrov space with curvature at least 1.

The cross-section is a sphere, as required by the spectral theorem: C(X) is itself a noncollapsed three-dimensional Ricci limit. Bruè–Pigati–Semola's Theorem 1.6 states directly that the cross-section of such a three-dimensional cone is homeomorphic to S^2; they note that this dimension-three case follows from Zhu, or from Simon combined with Lytchak–Stadler. This topology step matters because the RCD-to-Alexandrov theorem alone allows a boundary.

Huang's Theorem 1.6 gives, under the same Ricci, maximal-volume-growth, and unique-cone hypotheses,

    dim H_d(M) <= N_X(d(d+1)).

Combining it with the exact Alexandrov count yields dim H_d(M) <= (d+1)^2. If equality holds for d >= 1, equality in the count makes X round. Then C(X)=R^3 and AVR(M)=omega_3. Bishop–Gromov volume monotonicity has limiting ratio 1 both at zero and infinity, so it is identically 1; its rigidity case gives M=R^3.

Evidence labels and falsifiers

Reconstructed here: the exact ladder-counting sum and the logical composition of the three stated implications. Conditional candidate: the Ric_g >= 0, unique-tangent-cone corollary above, contingent on the cited source theorems and the noncollapsed-link/topology implications as stated. Source-reported, not independently validated: the 2026 Alexandrov spectral theorem and Zhu's topology theorem. Unproved here: historical priority, external correctness review, and the extension to manifolds with nonunique tangent cones. No finite computation is relevant to this continuum argument.

A decisive warning against a tempting shortcut is the spherical football X_c, 0<c<1, with metric dr^2+c^2 sin^2(r)dtheta^2. It is Alexandrov curvature >=1, is not round, and has lambda_1=2, the first positive round eigenvalue. Therefore single-eigenvalue Obata rigidity is false in the singular class. The whole-cluster counting inequality and its equality condition are essential. This example is explicit in Lin–Wang–Xu, Section 17.

Significance and boundary

If the transfer survives expert review and is not already known, it settles the sharp integer-degree Yau dimension question, with rigidity, for all three-dimensional Ricci-nonnegative manifolds of positive AVR that have a unique tangent cone. That is a substantial geometric consequence, not a constant refinement. It does not settle the unrestricted Ricci problem: nonnegative Ricci curvature admits nonunique tangent cones, and Huang's dimension-to-link comparison uses uniqueness. The preprint's stated three-manifold application is narrower, assuming nonnegative sectional curvature.

The most promising next gate is to replace uniqueness with a uniform spectral-transfer principle over the compact family of all tangent-cone links. The exact count bounds the number of link modes through the round threshold, but a shared gap in growth degrees can fail when lower-index eigenvalues cross that threshold; the football shows why a single-eigenvalue stability argument cannot supply it. A proof would need a cluster-aware three-circles or frequency-transfer theorem that retains the rank budget (d+1)^2 across changing cones. Until that lemma is proved, the nonunique-cone extension remains open.
