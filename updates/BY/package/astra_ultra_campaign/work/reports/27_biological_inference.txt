27 — Biological mechanism inference from lineage and time-series data

Question and scope

Can lineage-resolved time series identify whether a persistent cancer drug-tolerant phenotype is stored inside a cell or maintained by a transferable extracellular niche? This distinction matters because cell-intrinsic memory suggests targeting intracellular state regulators, whereas niche-supported persistence may require disrupting local signals. The interface considered here supplies barcoded cells, a drug pulse and washout, randomized culture manipulations, live phenotype/division/death measurements, and terminal rechallenge. It does not directly read the hidden memory variable. The target is deliberately restricted to soluble or otherwise perfusable extracellular support versus an environment-independent inherited cellular state; it does not cover every possible cancer-resistance mechanism.

Exact nonidentifiability under the baseline interface

Let the observations be a barcode-labelled lineage tree L and a binary live reporter Y_v at each observed node v. The only baseline intervention is a pulse history u (drug type, dose, and duration); the investigator does not independently reset or exchange the local environment. Define a hidden binary state C_v with root law P(C_root=1|u)=q_u, daughter inheritance P(C_child=C_parent)=1−ε, and emission P(Y_v=C_v)=1−η. Keep the lineage-tree and death/division kernels identical in two models.

In model I, C_v is an intracellular, heritable state. In model E, C_v is the state of a private extracellular niche associated with the clone; cells have no persistent intracellular memory. For model E, an isolated clone’s niche is carried forward with the same transition kernel. Both models then induce the same joint law on every observed reporter trajectory and lineage tree, for every pulse schedule and every sample size. This follows by induction over nodes: the root state law, each child transition, and each conditional observation kernel coincide. Thus total variation distance between the models’ observable distributions is zero. With equal prior probabilities, every classifier has error 1/2, even with infinitely many cells and arbitrarily dense time sampling. This is a constructed counterexample to resolution from the stated interface; it is not a claim that all current experiments have that restricted interface.

Discrimination protocol

Use a cancer model with a validated live persister reporter and high-complexity lineage barcodes. After a preregistered targeted-therapy pulse and washout, split cells from each barcode lineage at random into matched branches. In the reset arm, transfer cells to fresh perfused single-cell chambers and continuously remove soluble conditioned factors, with a neutralizing reagent for a prespecified candidate signal where available. In the retain arm, keep the matched branch in its conditioned medium and local support. Include non-pulsed and sham-transfer controls, balance arms across plates, and image division, death, and reporter state through a fixed washout interval. At the endpoint, rechallenge both arms identically and score the fraction of starting cells per barcode that remain viable and reporter-positive. Record the full time series, but define one bounded arm-level fraction per barcode for the primary analysis so that thousands of daughters from one clone do not masquerade as independent samples.

Add a transfer check: give naïve barcoded recipients cell-free conditioned medium from pulsed donor branches, alongside sham medium and neutralized donor medium. Transfer of the phenotype supports sufficiency of a soluble extracellular component; failure to transfer does not rule out matrix-bound, vesicular, contact-dependent, or short-lived signals. The primary randomized contrast estimates the causal effect of reset on the measured persistence outcome. It does not identify a molecular mediator unless a mediator-specific depletion/rescue is also performed.

The restricted hypotheses are explicit. H_I says reset leaves the relevant intracellular state and endpoint phenotype unchanged, so the expected within-barcode branch difference is zero. H_E says the reset removes the relevant extracellular support and reduces persistence by at least Δ. Let Y_i,keep and Y_i,reset be the two branch fractions for independent barcode i, each in [0,1], and D_i=Y_i,keep−Y_i,reset ∈[−1,1]. Under H_I, E[D_i]=0; under H_E, E[D_i]≥Δ. Classify by whether the mean of D_i exceeds Δ/2. Intermediate effects are inconclusive or evidence for a mixed mechanism, not forced into either model.

For n independent barcode pairs, Hoeffding’s inequality gives either error probability at most exp(−nΔ²/8): under H_I, a false positive requires a deviation of Δ/2; under H_E, a false negative requires the same deviation. To make the sum of the two errors at most α, it suffices that

    n ≥ (8/Δ²) log(2/α)

independent barcode pairs are measured. For α=0.05 and a minimum meaningful absolute effect Δ=0.20, this distribution-free bound is 738 pairs (1,476 culture branches). The unit is an independent barcode lineage, not a cell or daughter. Shared plate or batch effects require independent blocking or a more conservative experimental-unit count. The bound is sufficient, not a power calculation based on estimated variance.

The Δ-dependence cannot be improved in order for unrestricted Bernoulli outcomes. For a simple subproblem, let both outcomes be Bernoulli(1/2) under H_I, and let the reset outcome have probability 1/2−Δ under H_E while the keep outcome remains 1/2. The one-pair KL divergence is −(1/2)log(1−4Δ²), for 0<Δ<1/2. The Bretagnolle–Huber testing inequality implies that any test with each error at most α needs

    n ≥ log(1/(4α)) / [−(1/2)log(1−4Δ²)] .

For small Δ this is Ω(Δ⁻² log(1/α)), matching the upper bound’s scaling. Under the baseline interface with no environment intervention, by contrast, the exact equivalence above gives no finite sample size at all.

Decisive falsifiers and interpretation

If reset lowers persistence by at least the preregistered Δ and conditioned medium transfers the phenotype to naïve recipients, H_I in its environment-independent form is rejected and a soluble extracellular component has causal support. If reset has a tight confidence interval excluding effects of size Δ and transfer fails, the specified soluble-factor H_E model is rejected; this does not prove chromatin or any other intracellular mechanism. If reset matters but transfer fails, the result may reflect contact, matrix, intervention toxicity, or a mixed pathway. A sham-transfer viability control is essential: otherwise mechanical handling itself could create the observed effect. Sequencing or matched genomic profiling can help exclude newly selected genetic clones, but cannot by itself establish where the non-genetic memory resides.

Primary-source boundary and status

Existing work is already close on the component techniques and on parts of the biology. Shaffer et al., “Rare cell variability and drug-induced reprogramming as a mode of cancer drug resistance,” Nature (2017), used fluctuation analysis, single-cell measurements, and time-lapse imaging to distinguish transient pre-resistance from treatment-induced reprogramming: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22794. Oren et al., “Cycling cancer persister cells arise from lineages with distinct programs,” Nature (2021), combined expressed barcodes with proliferative and transcriptional state measurements: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03796-6. Goyal et al., “Diverse clonal fates emerge upon drug treatment of homogeneous cancer cells,” Nature (2023), report that resistant fate diversity was largely predetermined by pretreatment molecular differences: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06342-8. Sehgal et al., “Dynamic single-cell RNA sequencing identifies immunotherapy persister cells following PD-1 blockade,” JCI (2021), report exogenous IL-6 expansion and TNF-α vulnerability of persister cells, showing that extracellular cytokine perturbations can alter this phenotype: https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI135038. Rubinstein et al., “Spatiotemporal Profiling Defines Persistence and Resistance Dynamics during Targeted Treatment of Melanoma,” Cancer Research (2025), add spatial transcriptomics and phylogenetic tracing of intrinsic and acquired resistance: https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-24-0690.

These papers establish substantial prior art in lineage tracking, temporal resolution, and causal cytokine perturbation. The present construction does not overturn their model-specific results and makes no priority claim. The mathematical equivalence is proved here for the stated hidden-state interface; the Hoeffding and KL bounds are standard concentration/testing derivations applied to that design. The biological protocol is proposed, not executed. No physical measurements or external validation were obtained. Achieved significance is a precise no-go for this restricted observation interface plus a quantitative intervention design. The potential landmark consequence would require a validated, reusable design that identifies latent mechanism classes across real tissues while preserving the effects of context and selection; that is unproved and not established here.

Next proof gate: prospectively preregister one organotypic model, the reset operation, the reporter/rechallenge endpoint, and Δ; demonstrate that sham transfer preserves viability, that the reset removes the intended extracellular activity, and that independent barcode pairs achieve the predicted error rate. Only then test whether the intervention separates the two specified mechanisms in the biological system.
