Worker 25 — quantum measurement interface

Finding

A direct two-copy measurement can expose a nonlinear property that single-copy classical shadows cannot efficiently certify in the worst case: the purity Tr(rho^2), and hence the second Rényi entropy. The strongest simple physical interface is a parallel Bell analyzer between two independently prepared copies. This is a valid route beyond single-copy shadow barriers, but it is not a new discovery: the swap identity, Bell-basis implementation, optical experiments, and exponential single-copy lower bound are all known. The achievable ideal separation is large; a recent noisy-learning theorem blocks the naive claim that it survives constant local noise.

Interface and costs

Input: an unknown n-qubit density operator rho, supplied as independent, identically distributed preparations; d=2^n. One trial consumes two preparations rho tensor rho. The apparatus must hold both n-qubit registers coherently until the measurement and couple corresponding qubits across the registers. For each i, perform a Bell-basis measurement on the pair (i,i'), using one CNOT, one Hadamard, and two computational-basis readouts. The decoder labels the singlet outcome |Psi^-> by s_i=-1 and the other three Bell outcomes by s_i=+1. Return the parity S=product_i s_i. Thus each trial uses n cross-register entangling gates, 2n readouts, and n qubits of quantum memory beyond the arriving copy; the two-copy state occupies 2n qubits. With all couplers available in parallel, the ideal measurement depth is constant. A serial source must keep the first copy coherent while producing and aligning the second. These are required interface resources, not included in a copy-only comparison.

For one qubit pair, SWAP has eigenvalue -1 on |Psi^-> and +1 on the triplet Bell states. Therefore S is the outcome of the n-qubit register-swap observable V=product_i SWAP_i. The swap trick gives

  E[S] = Tr[V (rho tensor rho)] = Tr(rho^2) =: p.

Since S is in {-1,+1}, Var(S) <= 1. With m independent pair trials, the sample mean estimates p to additive epsilon with failure probability at most delta when m >= 2 epsilon^(-2) ln(2/delta). This follows directly from Hoeffding's inequality. It uses 2m state preparations, plus the n parallel Bell analyzers per trial; it does not reconstruct rho or its spectrum.

For the promise rho pure versus rho=I/d, the expectations are 1 and 1/d. The threshold midpoint has distance G/2 from either mean, where G=1-1/d. Hoeffding gives error at most delta with m >= 8 G^(-2) ln(2/delta) pair trials. Since G>=1/2 for n>=1, a constant number of trials suffices for fixed delta, independent of n. Under the pure case S=+1 on every ideal trial; under I/d, Pr(S=-1)=(1-1/d)/2.

No-go comparison and noise calculation

The correct comparator is adaptive single-copy access, which includes classical shadows: each copy is measured and discarded before the next arrives, while later measurement choices may depend on earlier classical outcomes. Chen, Cotler, Huang, and Li prove that for the promise of a maximally mixed state versus a fixed Haar-random pure state on n qubits, every algorithm without external quantum memory needs Omega(2^(n/2)) copies, and an unentangled algorithm uses O(2^(n/2)). Their paper explicitly gives the two-copy SWAP test as the constant-sample upper bound when n qubits of external memory are available. This is a proved exponential separation in copy count for the ideal interface, not an advantage over arbitrary joint measurements or a result about every property-estimation task.

A direct noise calculation explains a severe failure mode. Write rho=d^(-1) sum_P r_P P in the n-qubit Pauli basis, where r_I=1. Let each qubit undergo depolarization D_eta(A)=eta A+(1-eta)Tr(A)I/2, with fixed 0<=eta<=1, before the Bell measurement. A Pauli string P of weight w is attenuated by eta^w. The expected swap outcome on the corrupted state sigma=D_eta^(tensor n)(rho) is Tr(sigma^2). For Haar-random pure rho, E[r_P^2]=1/(d+1) for every nonidentity Pauli P. There are binomial(n,w)3^w strings of weight w, so

  E_rho[Tr(sigma^2)] - 1/d
    = [ (1+3 eta^2)^n - 1 ] / [ d(d+1) ].

At eta=1 this is 1-1/d, the ideal signal. For any fixed 0<eta<1, the mean signal is asymptotic to ((1+3 eta^2)/4)^n and therefore decays exponentially; at eta=0 it is exactly zero. This is an averaged signal calculation; by itself it is not a worst-case copy lower bound.

The stronger no-go is in Cotler, Gong, and Kannan, “Noisy Quantum Learning Theory” (arXiv:2512.10929, v2, 14 Jan 2026; published in Nature Communications in 2026). Their informal Theorem 2.4 considers the same pure-Haar-versus-maximally-mixed promise, applies a fixed nonzero local depolarizing channel to each qubit of every two-copy input before measurement, and still allows an arbitrary joint POVM on each pair with adaptive classical feedback. They prove that any such procedure needs at least c(lambda)^n samples for a constant c(lambda)>1 depending on the noise. This rules out the ideal O(1) guarantee under that model. It does not, from the informal bound alone, give a matching base against every classical protocol under the same noise parameter. The theorem’s input-noise model is especially relevant because it grants noiseless arbitrary processing after that initial noise, so replacing the Bell circuit by a deeper ideal measurement does not evade the stated lower bound.

Prior art and status

Primary sources:

- Sitan Chen, Jordan Cotler, Hsin-Yuan Huang, and Jerry Li, “Exponential separations between learning with and without quantum memory,” FOCS 2021 / arXiv:2111.05881. https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05881. The paper states the purity-testing bounds and formalizes the memory distinction.
- Jordan Cotler, Weiyuan Gong, and Ishaan Kannan, “Noisy Quantum Learning Theory,” arXiv:2512.10929v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10929. The paper proves the noisy two-copy lower bound and studies a protected HaPPY-code toy setting where structure can restore a constant-copy test under boundary erasures.
- Ting Zhang et al., “Quantification of entanglement and coherence with purity detection,” npj Quantum Information 10, 60 (2024). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-024-00857-2. The experiment compares random-measurement shadow estimation with collective two-copy Bell measurements, including polarization and path encodings.
- Jayadev Acharya, Ibrahim Issa, Nirmal V. Shende, and Aaron B. Wagner, “Measuring Quantum Entropy,” IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Information Theory 1 (2020). https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00814. This gives copy-complexity bounds for entropy estimation under its stated copy-measurement model; it is broader than the simple purity promise and should not be conflated with the dimension-independent swap estimate.

Proved here: the Bell-parity identity, copy/gate/readout count, Hoeffding bounds, and the Haar-averaged depolarizing attenuation formula. Known from primary literature: the exponential ideal separation and the noisy arbitrary-POVM lower bound; the Bell-measurement implementation has already been experimentally demonstrated for small systems. Not established here: a scalable implementation on a generic many-qubit device, a noise threshold at which this protocol beats the best same-noise classical method, or any new mathematical capability. The primary advance is already known, and this route does not support a landmark claim.

Decisive falsifier and next proof gate

The immediate experimental falsifier is to apply calibrated independent depolarization with increasing n to Haar-random pure states and I/d controls, then compare measured Bell parity against the predicted ((1+3 eta^2)^n-1)/(d(d+1)) mean gap, while auditing copy identity, storage, and cross-register gate error. A persistent constant gap at fixed eta<1 would contradict the calculation or its noise model. The next theoretical gate is more consequential: identify a physically supplied code or symmetry structure that protects the relevant logical purity from the noisy system-device interface, state its preparation and decoding costs, and prove an end-to-end advantage against matched classical measurements. The HaPPY toy model in the 2026 source is existing prior art and is only a candidate starting point, not evidence of black-hole microstate access in an experiment.
