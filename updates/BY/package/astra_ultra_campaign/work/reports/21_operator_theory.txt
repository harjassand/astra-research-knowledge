Operator-theoretic route: a two-dilation perturbation lemma and Crouzeix

Finding and status

The sharp Crouzeix numerical-range inequality is now claimed in two independent 2026 preprints. I reconstructed the operator-theoretic proof's decisive perturbation lemma and checked its algebra line by line. The lemma is valid under its stated hypotheses, and the double-layer representation supplies those hypotheses in the advertised functional-calculus application, provided the cited standard double-layer identities and boundedness facts hold. This is a source-derived proof, not a discovery by this investigation, and my check is not a full independent audit of every analytic input or a formal verification. The current pinned Astra analysis route did not contain a Crouzeix-specific card; I therefore used the primary papers directly rather than treating the route's silence or older open-problem wording as a status signal.

The target is: for every complex d-by-d matrix A and polynomial p,

    ||p(A)|| <= 2 sup{|p(z)| : z in W(A)},

where W(A) = {x*Ax : ||x||_2=1}. Lorist and Schwenninger's Theorem 3 states the stronger rational-function version for bounded Hilbert-space operators, with poles off the closure of W(A). The factor 2 is sharp: for J=[[0,1],[0,0]], ||J||=1 and W(J) is the disk of radius 1/2, so p(z)=z gives ratio 2. Indeed x*Jx=conj(x_1)x_2, whose magnitude is at most 1/2, and all points in that disk occur.

Reconstruction of the decisive lemma

Let T be a bounded operator on a Hilbert space H. Assume there are a Hilbert space K, a contraction Q on K, and an isometry V:H->K such that

    E_n = 2 V* Q*^n V - T*^n,  n>=1,

are uniformly bounded and commute with T. I derive ||T||<=2. Put k=||T||. If k<=1 this is immediate. For k>1, choose a unit x with T*T x=k^2 x. In finite dimension such an x exists. In infinite dimension the paper passes to Hilbert-space ultrapowers, preserving the hypotheses and norm, so T*T attains its norm.

Define m_n=Re〈E_n T^n x,x〉 and u=Q*VTx-kVx. The E_n bound and the identity above imply uniform boundedness of T^n, hence m_n is bounded. Commutation lets us write

    k m_n - m_(n+1)
      = Re〈T^n (k E_n - E_(n+1)T)x,x〉.

Substitute the formula for E_n. On this particular x, T*T x=k^2x gives

    (k E_n - E_(n+1)T)x
      = [k(k-1)T*^n + 2V*Q*^n(kV-Q*VT)]x.

Since kV-Q*VT applied to x is -u, moving T^n across the inner product yields

    k m_n - m_(n+1)
      = k(k-1)||T*^n x||^2 - 2 Re〈V*Q*^n u,T*^n x〉
      >= -||u||^2/[k(k-1)].

The last inequality is completion of the square, using ||V*Q*^n u||<=||u||. Now telescope with weights k^(-n+1). Boundedness of m_n makes the terminal term vanish, and the geometric sum gives

    k m_1 = sum_(n>=1) k^(-n+1)(k m_n-m_(n+1))
           >= -||u||^2/(k-1)^2.                         (1)

The isometry and contraction also give the opposing estimate:

    ||u||^2 <= 2k^2 - 2k Re〈V*Q*VT x,x〉
             = 2k^2 - k m_1 - k^3.                    (2)

Here the equality uses E_1=2V*Q*V-T* and T*T x=k^2x. Inserting (1) into (2) yields

    ||u||^2 (1 - 1/(k-1)^2) <= k^2(2-k).

For k>2 the left side is nonnegative while the right side is negative, a contradiction. The key is the full sequence of identities: the n=1 estimate alone only recovers the weaker 1+sqrt(2) bound. This derivation makes no positivity assumption on m_1; earlier shortcuts that require m_1>=0 therefore are not needed.

Functional-calculus interface

For a bounded operator A and a smooth convex domain Omega containing W(A), the double-layer construction defines

    P_Omega(sigma)=(1/pi) Re(n_Omega(sigma)(sigma I-A)^(-1)) >= 0,
    Phi(f)=(1/2) integral_boundary f(sigma)P_Omega(sigma)|d sigma|.

The cited setup gives Phi(1)=I and a bounded antilinear map alpha with 2Phi(f)-f(A)=alpha(f)(A)*. On K=L^2(boundary Omega;H), set Vx=P_Omega(.)^(1/2)x/sqrt(2); then V is an isometry. For ||f||_infinity=1, let Q multiply by f and T=f(A). Directly from the integral, V*Q^nV=Phi(f^n), so

    E_n=2V*Q*^nV-T*^n=alpha(f^n)(A).

Thus E_n commutes with T by the homomorphism property of the holomorphic functional calculus, and ||E_n||<=||theta|| ||alpha|| uniformly in n. The lemma gives ||f(A)||<=2. Approximation by smooth convex outer domains yields the bound on W(A); the rational-function statement follows when poles stay outside its closure.

This is a concrete input/output contract: receive the full operator A and analytic function f (or polynomial coefficients), then bound ||f(A)|| from the scalar supremum on W(A). It is uniform in dimension and uses no eigenbasis or condition-number assumption. It is an inequality, not a complexity result: the papers provide no polynomial-time routine for acquiring a certified exact supremum over W(A) for arbitrary encoded inputs. Finite-bit precision and worst-case optimization costs remain separate.

Consequences and limits

For t>=0, f(z)=exp(tz) gives ||exp(tA)||<=2 exp(t omega(A)), where omega(A)=lambda_max((A+A*)/2). In particular, if omega(A)<=-a<0, the semigroup decays at most 2 exp(-at), a sharp uniform prefactor for the full functional calculus. For lambda outside W(A), f(z)=1/(lambda-z) gives ||(lambda I-A)^(-1)||<=2/dist(lambda,W(A)); hence the epsilon-pseudospectrum lies within the 2epsilon-neighborhood of W(A), with points of W(A) included. These are direct consequences, not separate new theorems here. They sharpen general nonnormal stability and resolvent estimates when the numerical range is available, but do not make a spectrally stable matrix decay if its numerical abscissa is positive.

No counterexample to the reconstructed lemma was found. The decisive falsifier for the application is a failure of one of its exact bridge conditions: uniform boundedness of alpha(f^n)(A), or commutation of these errors with f(A). The source derives both from its bounded functional calculus; an external proof audit should check the double-layer identity and the rational/outer-approximation passage at the full Hilbert-space scope. The sharp 2-by-2 Jordan example prevents any improvement of the universal scalar constant.
