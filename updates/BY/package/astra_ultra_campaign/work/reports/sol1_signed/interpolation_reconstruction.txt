INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION OF THE FOUR-WEIGHT INTERPOLATION CORE
10 October 2026. Scope: an internal mathematical reconstruction, not
independent external validation. The source is OpenAI, The entropy
photon-number inequality, Section 02, pinned at
fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. Its theorem is not counted as
our original discovery. The signed application is documented separately.

0. Exact theorem and domain hypotheses

Each Hilbert space H_j has fixed weighted coordinates, with positive
coordinate measures absorbed into the coordinates. At each coordinate
of each space independently specify m>0 and x,y real. For a weight X_j
write Q_X,j(Z)=sum_alpha X_j(alpha)|Z_alpha|^2. Let T subset H_0 be a
linear test space, and L_i:T -> H_i linear, with w_i>0. Assume:

(D1) each Z in T has support on only finitely many distinct parameter
     tuples (m,x,y); the number of nonzero coordinates may be infinite;
(D2) multiplication by every scalar function of (m,x,y) preserves T;
(D3) each of the four weight families
     m f(x)e^x, m f(x)e^-x, m f(y)e^y, m f(y)e^-y
     obeys Q_X,0(Z) >= sum_i w_i Q_X,i(L_i Z).

Here f(t)=t/sinh t, with f(0)=1. Then the same comparison holds for
F=m f(x)f(y)/f(x+y). There is no common-sign assumption on y, and L_i
need not commute with any multiplier. D2 is indispensable; Section 8
gives an exact thermal-weight counterexample when it is dropped.

1. Elementary comparison calculus

Positive linear combinations preserve comparison. Pointwise limits
preserve it too: group the source sum into the finitely many parameter
tuples in D1; it is then a finite sum of convergent coefficients times
finite coordinate masses. Target Fatou gives the other side. No target
finite-support assumption is needed.

For positive weights X,Y the parallel sum X:Y=(X^-1+Y^-1)^-1 preserves
comparison. Indeed, coordinatewise,
 (X:Y)|z|^2 = min_{u+v=z} (X|u|^2+Y|v|^2).
The source minimizers U=Y/(X+Y) Z, V=X/(X+Y) Z belong to T by D2.
Compare their X and Y forms, add, and use L_iU+L_iV=L_iZ. At each target
coordinate the resulting cost is at least its unconstrained minimum.
One must not replace L_iU by Y_i/(X_i+Y_i)L_iZ; no such relation is
assumed or used.

For a,b>0 put ell(a,b)=(a-b)/(log a-log b), continuously at equality.
The direct one-variable integrals
 ell(X,Y)^-1 = integral_0^1 [(1-t)X+tY]^-1 dt,
 XY/ell(X,Y) = integral_0^1 [t/X+(1-t)/Y]^-1 dt
show that these two weights preserve comparison. A positive quadrature
for the first, followed by inversion, is a parallel sum of positive
multiples of the comparing weights (1-t)X+tY. The second is a positive
linear combination of weighted parallel sums. D1 and Fatou justify
the quadrature limits.

Apply these operations to each opposite-exponent pair in D3. Because
ell(m f(x)e^x,m f(x)e^-x)=m, their product divided by that logarithmic
mean is m f(x)^2. We therefore have comparison for m, ms, mt, where
s=f(x)^2 and t=f(y)^2 lie in (0,1].

2. Real inverse hyperbolic function and the required derivative

Let b(x)=x coth x, b(0)=1. For x>=0, s=f(x)^2 decreases strictly from
1 to 0. Define B(s)=b(x) on (0,1]. Differentiation gives
 ds/dx=2s(1-B)/x, db/dx=(B-s)/x.
For 0<s<1 this yields
 -B'(s)=(B-s)/[2s(B-1)]
        =1/(2s)+R(s)/2,
 R(s)=(1-s)/[s(B(s)-1)].
The sign of -B' on (0,1) is easy, but positivity alone is insufficient
for comparison calculus. What is required is the stronger positive
Stieltjes representation of -B' below.

3. The complex extension: all boundary and injectivity checks

For q>0 define p_*(q) in (pi/2,pi) by
 p_* tan p_*=-q tanh q.
On (pi/2,pi), p tan p increases strictly from minus infinity to zero:
its derivative is (p+sin p cos p)/cos^2 p>0. Thus p_* exists uniquely
and continuously; p_*(q)->pi as q->0. Set
 D={u=p+iq:q>0, 0<p<p_*(q)},
 Q={z:Re z>0, Im z<0},
 a(u)=sin u/u.
D is connected, for example by the coordinates (p/p_*(q),q).

Compute
 Re a=(p sin p cosh q+q cos p sinh q)/(p^2+q^2),
 Im a=(p cos p sinh q-q sin p cosh q)/(p^2+q^2).
The real part is positive if p<=pi/2. If p>pi/2, its positivity is
equivalent, after division by the negative cos p cosh q, to
p tan p<-q tanh q, precisely p<p_*(q). For the imaginary part, when
p<pi/2 use tan p/p>1>tanh q/q; when p>=pi/2 the two displayed terms
have the required signs directly. Hence a(D) subset Q.

The map is proper into Q. Finite boundary pieces map outside Q:
p=0 and q=0 map to the positive real axis; p=p_*(q) maps to the negative
imaginary axis; the corners u=0 and u=pi have limits a=1 and a=0.
At infinity,
 |a(p+iq)| >= sinh q/sqrt(pi^2+q^2) -> infinity
uniformly for 0<=p<=pi. A sequence whose images remain in a compact
subset of Q therefore cannot escape to infinity or to the boundary.

It has no critical point in D. Indeed a'(u)=0 would imply u cot u=1,
whereas
 Im(u cot u)=[q sin(2p)-p sinh(2q)]/[cosh(2q)-cos(2p)]<0.
The denominator is positive and sin(2p)<=2p, sinh(2q)>2q make the
numerator strictly negative.

By local analytic inversion a(D) is open. Properness makes it relatively
closed in Q, so a(D)=Q. Every fiber is compact and discrete, hence
finite. Disjoint local inverse neighborhoods at its finitely many
points cover the full preimage of some small image neighborhood:
otherwise a sequence of extra preimages would accumulate at that fiber
by properness. Consequently a is a covering. A connected covering of
the simply connected quadrant has one sheet. Here is the relevant
reason rather than an injectivity assertion: local inverses lift paths;
successive finite image neighborhoods lift homotopies; every loop in Q
contracts, so its lift starting at a fixed point ends there. A path in
D between two hypothetical inverse points projects to a loop and would
contradict that endpoint property. Thus a is bijective and its inverse
is holomorphic.

For Im s>0 take s^-1/2 in Q and put u=a^-1(s^-1/2), B(s)=u cot u.
This is holomorphic and Im B<0. It continues across the whole positive
axis. For s->s0>0, the bound above prevents q->infinity. Every cluster
point of u must lie on p=0 or q=0; the curved boundary has a purely
imaginary image and the corner pi has image zero. On p=0 the values
sinh q/q increase strictly from 1 to infinity. On q=0 the values
sin p/p decrease strictly from 1 to zero. Thus there is a unique
boundary value: u=iq for 0<s0<1, u=p in (0,pi) for s0>1, and u=0
for s0=1. The derivatives of a on the first two boundary pieces are
nonzero: q cosh q-sinh q>0 and sin p-p cos p>0. Analytic inversion
gives continuation there. At s0=1 invert in the variable u^2:
 s=(u/sin u)^2=1+u^2/3+O(u^4),
 u cot u=1-u^2/3+O(u^4).
Hence B(1)=1 and B'(1)=-1.

On (0,1), this extension agrees with the original real B, since u=ix.
For s>1 it equals p cot p with s=(p/sin p)^2. The latter s increases
strictly in p while p cot p decreases strictly; thus B is real and
strictly decreasing on all of (0,infinity). The complex step is fully
specified; no global inverse or sign property is inferred from a plot.

4. A derivation of the needed positive representation

We need a basic Pick representation, and can derive its relevant form
from the disk Poisson formula. Suppose P is holomorphic with Im P>=0
in the upper half-plane, and extends real-analytically across the
positive real axis with real values. Under s=i(1+z)/(1-z), its imaginary
part is a nonnegative harmonic v in the unit disk. The measures
v(rho e^{i theta})dtheta/(2pi) all have total mass v(0). A weakly
convergent subsequence as rho->1 exists on the compact circle. On each
strictly smaller disk the Poisson formula and uniform convergence of
its kernel identify v as the Poisson integral of the limit measure.
The atom at z=1 gives beta Im s with beta>=0. At other boundary points,
indexed by a real t, the Poisson kernel is
(1+t^2) Im s/|t-s|^2. Absorb 1+t^2 into the pushed-forward measure mu.
Then integral (1+t^2)^-1 dmu(t)<infinity. The measure has no mass on
the arcs corresponding to t>0: v tends uniformly to zero on every
compact such arc by real analytic continuation. Therefore its support
is t<=0. The analytic integral with this imaginary part is locally
uniformly convergent, since its integrand is O((1+t^2)^-1) at infinity.
Its difference from P has zero imaginary part and is a real constant.
We have proved the representation
 P(s)=a+beta s+integral_{t<=0}
       [1/(t-s)-t/(1+t^2)]dmu(t).

Apply this to P=-B, subtract its value at 1, and divide by s-1. The
function
 G(s)=(B(s)-1)/(1-s)
     =beta+integral_{t<=0}[(1-t)(s-t)]^-1 dmu(t)
has removable G(1)=1 and G(s)>0 on s>0. Moreover H(s)=sG(s) has
nonnegative imaginary part in the upper half-plane: each term
s/(s-t) has imaginary part -t Im s/|s-t|^2>=0. H has no zero there.
If it had one, the nonnegative harmonic Im H would have an interior
zero, so it would vanish identically by the harmonic minimum principle;
H would then be a real constant, and a zero constant contradicts its
positive values on s>0. Thus R(s)=1/H(s) is holomorphic, positive on
s>0 and has Im R<=0. It agrees with the R of Section 2.

Apply the just-proved representation to -R, with coefficients
tilde beta>=0, tilde mu supported on t<=0. For real s>1, subtraction
at 1 gives
 0<=R(1)-R(s)=tilde beta(s-1)
   +integral [1/(1-t)-1/(s-t)]dtilde mu(t)<=R(1).
Hence tilde beta=0. Monotone convergence as s->infinity gives
integral (1-t)^-1 dtilde mu(t)<=R(1)=1. Put
c0=R(1)-integral (1-t)^-1 dtilde mu(t)>=0. Then
 R(s)=c0+integral_{t<=0} (s-t)^-1 dtilde mu(t).
The integrability just proved makes this locally uniformly convergent
off the negative axis; the formula follows first on s>1 and then by
analytic continuation.

The identity -B'(s)=(1/s+R(s))/2 on (0,1) extends holomorphically and
hence holds on s>0. Changing variables t=-r proves
 -B'(s)=c+integral_{r>=0} (s+r)^-1 dnu(r),
 c=c0/2>=0,
 nu=(delta_0+pushforward(tilde mu))/2,
 integral (1+r)^-1 dnu(r)<infinity.
In particular the mass 1/2 at r=0 implies -B'(s)>=1/(2s)>0. This is
the precise representation needed for the comparison argument.

5. Construction of the additional comparison weight

For s,t>0 define
 1/E=(2/m) integral_0^1 -B'(theta s+(1-theta)t)dtheta.
It is finite and strictly positive. For s!=t the fundamental theorem
of calculus gives E=m(t-s)/[2(B(s)-B(t))]; at s=t, E=-m/[2B'(s)].

Substitute the representation above. Each reciprocal integrand is
 1/[m(theta s+(1-theta)t+r)]
 =1/[theta(ms)+(1-theta)(mt)+r m].
The denominator is a positive linear combination of the three weights
already known to compare. Positive finite quadratures and inversion
therefore construct E by parallel sums. The constant c contributes
2c/m and is treated by a positive multiple of m. On bounded r intervals
the integrands are continuous in theta,r and nu is locally finite;
positive quadratures converge. The tail tends to zero by
integral (1+r)^-1 dnu<infinity. Consequently all quadratures converge
pointwise at every source and target tuple. D1 and Fatou give comparison
for E. This is a valid comparison-preserving construction, rather than
an unsupported multiplication of comparing weights.

6. Exact final scalar minimum, including both sign degeneracies

For complex z,phi consider
 Q(z,phi)=[m f(x)e^x|z-phi|^2+m f(x)e^-x|z+phi|^2
           +m f(y)e^y|z+phi|^2+m f(y)e^-y|z-phi|^2]/4
           +E|phi|^2.
Since f(x)e^{+-x}=b(x)+-x, writing a=b(x)+b(y), e=E/m gives
 Q=m a|z|^2/2+m(a/2+e)|phi|^2
       -m(x-y)Re(conj(z)phi).
Its unique minimum is at phi=(x-y)/(a+2e)z, with value
 m/2 [a-(x-y)^2/(a+2e)]|z|^2.

If x^2!=y^2, b(x)^2=f(x)^2+x^2 and the formula for E imply
 (a+2e)(b(x)-b(y))=x^2-y^2.
Substitution in the minimum gives
 m[y b(x)+x b(y)]/(x+y)|z|^2
 =m f(x)f(y)/f(x+y)|z|^2,
using coth x+coth y=sinh(x+y)/(sinh x sinh y) and continuous limits
when x or y is zero. If y=x, the minimum is m b(x)|z|^2, which equals
m f(x)^2/f(2x)|z|^2. If y=-x!=0, let s=f(x)^2 and b=b(x). Then
 e=s(b-1)/(b-s), b+e=x^2/(b-s),
so the minimum is m[b-x^2/(b+e)]|z|^2=ms|z|^2. At x=y=0,
E=m/2 and the minimum is m|z|^2. Thus the claimed value is valid for
every pair of signs and every degenerate tuple.

For Z in T let Phi be its coordinatewise source minimizer. D2 places
Phi in T, and D1 makes all source forms finite. Apply D3 to Z-Phi,
Z+Phi with the indicated choices of the four weights; apply the E
comparison to Phi; add. The source side equals Q_F,0(Z). The target
side is at least sum_i w_i Q_F,i(L_iZ), because its second variable
is the actual L_iPhi and each target coordinate cost is at least its
unconstrained minimum. This completes the theorem.

7. Background audit and actual verification status

The following background facts are used: elementary differentiation and
hyperbolic identities; holomorphic local inverse and identity theorems;
the disk Poisson formula; weak compactness of positive measures on the
circle; the harmonic minimum principle; monotone convergence and Fatou;
and path/homotopy lifting for a local homeomorphism with properness.
The relevant covering and Pick-representation deductions are proved
above rather than imported as special new source results. Their standard
background facts are not formalized here. The real/complex signs,
boundary limits, integrability, each form operation and degenerate
scalar minimization have been checked in this reconstruction. No
remaining analytic implication specific to the interpolation theorem
has been identified. This is internal proof reconstruction; a separate
expert or formal check is still external validation, and has not occurred.

8. Exact falsifier to dropping multiplier invariance

Use actual thermal weights with r0=rA=1, h=log 2. Source H0=C^2 has
frequencies v=(10,-10), so m=1/h, x=h v/2, y=-h/2. Restrict the source
test space to T=span{(1,1)}. Target HA=C has vA=-4 with the same m,y.
Set L(alpha,alpha)=alpha, wA=2. Every test has finitely many tuples,
but T is not invariant under arbitrary functions of v.

Both arithmetic source weights on (1,1) sum to 10250/1023. The weighted
target weights R+ and R- are 8/15 and 128/15, respectively, both smaller.
The source and weighted target constant forms are exactly 2 and 4.
Thus all four elementary comparisons hold on T. Nevertheless,
 Q_F,0(1,1)=296650/101277,
 wA Q_F,A(1)=248/75,
and the second exceeds the first by 955982/2531925>0. An extra creation
port can have zero map and positive weight, so this also falsifies the
candidate standalone signed implication without D2. It does not
falsify the repaired theorem or the physical full eigenblock test class.
