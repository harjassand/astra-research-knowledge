32 — Goal-quotient information geometry for acquisition

Verdict

The common structure is the information metric restricted to the directions that change the requested output. I derive an exact finite-sample theorem and a cost certificate for linear Gaussian acquisition. It shows that full parameter conditioning is the wrong objective when the task is prediction: unobservable parameter directions are harmless if they leave the goal unchanged. The core is established linear estimation and is close to published goal-oriented optimal design; I claim no priority or novelty. The theorem does not acquire an unknown feature map or close teacher-free HMM/predictive-state discovery.

Interface and theorem

An unknown fixed parameter is theta in R^d. The learner is given a finite menu A of measurement vectors a and costs c_a>0. One paid use of action a returns Y=a^T theta+epsilon, with independent epsilon~N(0,sigma_a^2). The requested output is z=B theta in R^r, for a supplied matrix B. A design chooses integer counts n_a before observations; its total acquisition cost is sum_a c_a n_a. The only permitted inputs are the menu, costs, noise variances and B. The output is an unbiased estimate of z, with its covariance certificate. This deliberately separates acquisition of measurements from acquisition of the model class: the feature vectors and goal map are supplied.

Whiten every observation by sigma_a and stack its row a^T/sigma_a in a matrix A. Its Fisher information is F=A^T A=sum_a n_a a a^T/sigma_a^2. Then:

1. The requested target is identifiable from this design exactly when ker(A) is contained in ker(B), equivalently row(B) is contained in row(A). If this fails and theta ranges over R^d, two parameters differing by t v for v in ker(A)\ker(B) have identical data laws and targets diverging with |t|; every estimator has infinite worst-case target error.
2. Under that estimability condition, the generalized least-squares estimate B A^dagger y is unbiased and has covariance B F^dagger B^T. No unbiased estimator has a smaller covariance matrix in the positive-semidefinite order. Thus this exact target covariance is both an achievable acquisition certificate and the Cramer–Rao limit.
3. For a target covariance tolerance tau^2 I_r, the minimum cost among unbiased estimators has the exact integer design formulation

   min sum_a c_a n_a, over n_a in Z_{>=0}, subject to
   [ F(n)   B^T ] >= 0.
   [ B      tau^2 I_r ]

The singular Schur-complement condition is part of the constraint: it enforces estimability and B F^dagger B^T <= tau^2 I_r. Replacing integer counts by nonnegative real weights gives a convex semidefinite relaxation and a lower bound on implementable cost; it need not have zero rounding gap.

Proof. The data have mean A theta and identity covariance after whitening. Their score is A^T(Y-A theta), with covariance F. An unbiased estimator of B theta has score covariance B. The matrix Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives Cov(estimator) >= B F^dagger B^T on the estimable subspace. The estimator B A^dagger Y has mean B A^dagger A theta=B theta because A^dagger A projects onto row(A), which contains row(B), and its covariance is B(A^T A)^dagger B^T. This attains the bound. The generalized Schur complement gives item 3, including the range condition when F is singular. This is a finite-sample identity, not an asymptotic approximation.

The resulting primitive is the goal-quotient Fisher certificate B F^dagger B^T. Geometrically, F is the Fisher metric on parameter perturbations; only the quotient R^d/ker(B) matters for the output. A global condition number of F can be infinite while the goal certificate is finite. For example, with d=2, one action a=(1,0), and B=(1,0), theta_2 can never be recovered, but n observations estimate z=theta_1 with exact variance sigma^2/n. A full-parameter inverse solver calls this singular; the task-level inverse problem is well-conditioned.

Decisive falsifier and boundary

Local Fisher rank alone does not imply global acquisition. Let theta range over {0,2pi}, observe sin(theta)+N(0,sigma^2), and request z=theta. The Fisher information at both parameter points is 1/sigma^2, but the observation distributions coincide and the targets differ. Any nonlinear extension therefore needs global separation of goal-distinct hypotheses in addition to a local information margin. The present theorem also gives no efficient algorithm for finding useful actions when the menu itself is unknown or exponentially large. Those are exactly the structural-acquisition obligations a landmark application would need to discharge.

Pinned Astra consequence

The pinned snapshot is work/sources/astra at commit 8aed7fd74eb14622ed5a0a3635a799374296e32a. Its N189 card reports a much narrower positive instance: supplied noiseless GF(2) parity-block contract, secret s in GF(2)^L, N=L+ceil(log2(1/delta)) independent uniform rows X, solve Xs=Y, and return s with a full-rank certificate or UNKNOWN. The stated rank-failure bound is (2^L-1)2^-N<=delta, with O(NL^2) bit operations and O(NL) stored bits. This is a valid exact-rank analogue of the theorem above, but the parity feature map and noiseless block contract are given. The card labels the claim source_derived_unreviewed, with correctness and priority unverified.

N243 supplies a relevant cost obstruction rather than a general extension. Its card reports that, for a noisy parity-reset HMM and a supplied prefix observable, a suffix bank of M bounded functions with population singular margin kappa covers at most K=floor(lambda^2(M-kappa^2)/kappa^2) secret coordinates. It also reports an acquisition lower bound of at least L-log2(K)+log2(1-delta) output bits and a paid-block information lower bound. These are source-reported, unreviewed claims. They support the same lesson: the acquired information geometry and its margin control which predictive directions can be learned; merely naming more hidden states or observables does not solve the coverage problem. They do not give a general unknown-noise HMM learner.

The possible landmark consequence is an output-equivalence acquisition theorem for predictive systems: identify a finite bank of future tests whose measured Fisher metric controls every target-distinct predictive secant, then recover a predictive realization with cost governed by effective predictive rank and the target-restricted margin, not by a chosen hidden-state coordinate system. This would be materially useful because equivalent hidden realizations can be arbitrarily ill-conditioned while their output laws agree. The present result proves only the linear Gaussian design component; it does not prove that bank acquisition, a global separation bound, or a polynomial runtime.

Prior art and status

Rao's 1945 “Information and accuracy attainable in the estimation of statistical parameters” contains the classical minimum-variance linear-estimation problem. The more direct overlap is Attia, Alexanderian and Saibaba, “Goal-Oriented Optimal Design of Experiments for Large-Scale Bayesian Linear Inverse Problems” (2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06517, which optimizes posterior uncertainty in a quantity of interest rather than in the full parameter. Spantini et al., “Goal-oriented optimal approximations of Bayesian linear inverse problems” (2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.01881, derive goal-relevant subspaces and optimal posterior approximations. Hsu, Kakade and Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models” (2012), https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413, already gives efficient HMM learning under singular-value separation assumptions. These primary sources make the proposed information-geometry synthesis non-novel at its present level. They do not establish autonomous discovery of an unknown, well-conditioned predictive feature bank under the N189/N243 interfaces.

Evidence labels: the finite Gaussian theorem and proof above are derived here; target-specific design, Cramer–Rao bounds and spectral HMM learning are known; N189/N243 statements are pinned-source reports, not independently audited here; the nonlinear adaptive extension and landmark consequence remain unproved. No computation was necessary for the exact derivation.

Next proof gate: formulate an HMM or predictive-process query interface with no supplied feature bank; prove that a charged adaptive procedure can acquire tests giving (i) uniform separation of every pair of goal-distinct predictive laws and (ii) a certified positive target-quotient Fisher margin; then prove a finite-sample, finite-bit reconstruction bound and compare total acquisition plus computation against a same-information retaining baseline. If the first two conditions cannot be obtained without supplying the sought structure, this route does not clear the landmark acquisition barrier.
