04 — Observable prediction for chaotic systems: conditional-mixing closure

Finding

A mathematically clean closure for long-horizon observable prediction is “posterior-to-climatology”: if an invariant ensemble is conditioned on a partial observation, predict a smooth target observable at horizon n by its invariant mean, with an error bounded by the rate at which the pushed-forward conditional measure mixes. This predicts an ensemble observable without evolving point trajectories. It is a real and useful closure, but it is already substantially present in the conditional-mixing literature. I found no new theorem connecting it to turbulent Navier–Stokes flows or to a data-only acquisition interface. The exact model below establishes the mechanism and the numerical rate in one chaotic system; transfer to turbulence remains the missing result.

Target and interface

Let T preserve a physical invariant probability measure μ, let H be a partial observation, and let μ_h be the posterior distribution of the state given H=h. For a specified smooth observable A, the requested forecast is

    m_n(h) = ∫ A(T^n x) dμ_h(x).

The candidate closure outputs μ(A) = ∫ A dμ, with a certified bound on |m_n(h) − μ(A)|. Its full input is (T, μ, H, h, A, n), or in a data-assimilation setting a validated measurement likelihood and the corresponding posterior μ_h. Its output is a conditional mean, not a state or a trajectory. An operational certificate additionally needs effective constants C, ξ and regularity norms for which |m_n(h) − μ(A)| ≤ C ||A|| ||1|| ξ^n. This is the exponential conditional-mixing estimate. Ordinary mixing of μ does not imply this estimate for every posterior μ_h.

Exact model and derivation

Use the binary baker map B on [0,1]^2,

    B(x,y) = (2x mod 1, (y + floor(2x))/2),

with invariant probability μ equal to area measure. Condition on the exact partial observation H(x,y)=x−y²=0. The change of variables (h,y)=(x−y²,y) has Jacobian one, so the conditional measure μ_0 is uniform in y on the curve (x,y)=(y²,y), 0≤y≤1. This posterior is acquired exactly from the stated prior and observation model; no point on the curve is selected.

For integer q≥1 take A_q(x,y)=cos(2πqx). Its invariant mean is zero. Since the first coordinate of B^n is 2^n x mod 1,

    m_n(0) = ∫_0^1 cos(2πq 2^n y²) dy.

Thus the forecast is a one-dimensional Fresnel integral, with no trajectory simulation. Put λ=2πq2^n. The classical Fresnel integral gives |∫_0^∞ exp(iλy²)dy|=√π/(2√λ). Integration by parts on [1,∞), using exp(iλy²)=(2iλy)^{-1} d exp(iλy²)/dy, bounds the tail by 1/λ. Therefore

    |m_n(0) − μ(A_q)| ≤ 1/(2√(2q2^n)) + 1/(2πq2^n).

The mean forecast error is explicitly O((q2^n)^{-1/2}); at any chosen tolerance, this expression gives a sufficient horizon. This proves an observable-only, computable-error closure in the exact model. It does not give pointwise prediction, a distributional approximation, or a cost bound for a general high-dimensional system.

What is known and what breaks

Caroline Wormell, “Conditional mixing in deterministic chaos” (2023 preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09291; author manuscript https://wormell.perso.math.cnrs.fr/preprints/W23M-preprint.pdf), proves exponential conditional mixing for a class of modified baker maps. The theorem covers nonlinear stable contractions and linear contractions paired with curved conditioning leaves; its estimate is |ρ_t(A∘b^n B)−ρ_t(B)ρ(A)| ≤ C||A||_{C^{α;β}}||B||_{C^γ}ξ^n. Setting B=1 yields the closure above in that class. The proof uses Fourier decay and non-integrability structure. The theorem supplies existence of C and ξ under stated structural hypotheses, but a finite sensor record alone does not certify those hypotheses or produce a validated mixing rate for an unknown flow.

A decisive falsifier for any unconditional version is the same baker map observed through H(x,y)=x. Given H=x_0, the conditional measure fixes x exactly, and A_q(B^n(x_0,y))=cos(2πq2^n x_0) for every y. This generally does not converge to the area mean zero. Partial observations can retain permanent predictive information when aligned with the expanding dynamics. Wormell also gives a nontrivial failure: a three-branch baker map with contractions v_1(y)=y/9, v_2(y)=(y+2)/9, v_3(y)=(2y+1)/3 and linear leaf x=y has conditional support in an invariant middle-thirds Cantor subset, so the conditional measures cannot converge to the full SRB measure. The needed property is conditional mixing for the specific observation geometry, not merely chaos or global mixing.

Prior art also sets the limit of this route. Philipp, Schaller, Worthmann, Peitz and Nüske, “Error bounds for kernel-based approximations of the Koopman operator,” Applied and Computational Harmonic Analysis 71 (2024), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acha.2024.101657, derive finite-data prediction error bounds for RKHS observables of stochastic differential equations, with truncation and sampling assumptions. Colbrook, Mezić and Stepanenko, “Adversarial dynamical systems characterize when data-driven learning succeeds or fails,” Nature Communications 17, 5397 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74220-8, show that broad data-driven spectral learning needs structural conditions and provide adversarial impossibility results outside them. These works make a generic “learn the closure from finite data” claim untenable without a proved model class and acquisition guarantee.

For turbulent flows, Mori–Zwanzig gives an exact observable equation, but the memory and orthogonal-dynamics terms are not thereby computable; practical finite-memory truncations remain modeled approximations (for example, Stinis, “A dynamic subgrid scale model for Large Eddy Simulations based on the Mori–Zwanzig formalism,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02143). Conditional mixing offers a different long-horizon statistical closure, yet no result found here proves it for a Navier–Stokes observation map or gives constants from real flow data. Mean response under equilibrium forcing also does not cover transient statistics or trajectory-level flow forecasts.

Evidence ledger and significance

Proved here: the exact posterior, Fourier forecast formula, and explicit Fresnel error bound for the stated baker-map/curved-observation model. Known from source: exponential conditional mixing for specified modified baker maps; source theorem and assumptions were checked, not independently reconstructed in full. Refuted: a universal partial-observation washout closure, by H=x (and the source’s Cantor-support example). Conditional: a useful turbulent observable closure if a flow has a physical invariant measure, the chosen observed slices satisfy conditional mixing, and effective constants are certified. Unproved: acquisition of those conditions and constants from finite physical measurements; extension to Navier–Stokes; any new landmark result. The achieved significance is a clean exact proof-of-mechanism with a computable forecast-error envelope. The potential significance would be large if it replaced long turbulent ensemble simulations for specified coarse observables, but that capability has not been established.

Next proof gate

Choose one fully specified turbulent model (preferably a globally well-posed finite-dimensional shell model or a rigorously defined two-dimensional forced-flow discretization), one invariant ensemble, one sensor map H, and one target A. Prove conditional mixing for the posterior measures induced by that H with explicit, effective C and ξ, including the actual observation noise and discretization error. Then demonstrate that μ(A), the posterior, and all constants can be acquired from the permitted inputs with a total cost and error certificate. Failure at the conditional-mixing step blocks this closure for that sensor/target pair; empirical decay alone does not pass the gate.
