Distributed exact inference: boundary matrices and a bit-complexity obstruction

Question and interface. Can a sequence split among m machines be replaced by a short exact message from each machine while preserving a scientific posterior query? I tested the natural finite-state route: use the segment likelihood matrix as an adaptive sufficient message for exact hidden-state inference. The model, transition/emission law, and contiguous partition are public; machine i sees only its length-ell_i observation block; a coordinator must output the exact posterior of the final hidden state. All inputs are acquired locally, computation is charged at each site and at the coordinator, and communication is measured in bits. This last choice matters: an arbitrary-precision rational is not one communication bit or one fixed-size word.

Candidate protocol. For a K-state hidden Markov model, let A_z be the K-by-K nonnegative matrix whose (s,s') entry is the probability of observation z and next state s' given current state s. A site's block w_i sends M_i=A_{w_i,1}...A_{w_i,ell_i}. The coordinator computes M=M_1...M_m and applies the public initial row vector and terminal query vector. For initial distribution pi and terminal-state query e_j, the posterior is

    P(S_N=j | w) = (pi M e_j) / (pi M 1).

The formula is exact. With unit-cost arithmetic, each site sends K^2 scalars and the coordinator combines m matrices in O(m K^3) arithmetic operations; sites take O(ell_i K^3) operations with dense matrices. This changes serial dependence into parallel segment computation followed by a tree reduction. A direct forward recursion takes O(N K^2) operations and O(K) working storage. The matrix protocol does not reduce total arithmetic in the dense case, and it can increase arithmetic; its potential gain is communication latency or arithmetic-word count. A rank-r segment matrix can be sent as U_i V_i^T using 2Kr scalars when r<K/2, but forming and coding that factorization is not free, and rank-adaptive arithmetic alone is not a bit bound.

Decisive exact-bit test. The fixed-K message claim fails to imply sublinear exact communication. Consider two hidden states and binary observations, with joint observation/transition matrices

    T_0 = (1/3) [[1,1],[0,1]],    T_1 = (1/3) [[1,0],[1,1]].

Their sum has every row sum 1, so these define a valid Markov source with transition-emitted observations. Let the initial state be uniform. For a word w of length N, write (a,b)=(1,1)B_w, where B_0=3T_0 and B_1=3T_1. The posterior of final state 1 is q(w)=a/(a+b). Right multiplication updates the integer pair by

    0: (a,b) -> (a,a+b),       1: (a,b) -> (a+b,b).

Starting at (1,1), every step makes the coordinates unequal and preserves gcd(a,b)=1. The last symbol is recovered from the larger coordinate: if a>b it was 1; if a<b it was 0. Subtracting the smaller coordinate reverses that step. Repeating recovers the entire word, so all 2^N words yield distinct coprime pairs and distinct exact posterior rationals q(w). Thus any exact finite-bit encoding of the required coordinator output has at least 2^N possible values and needs at least N bits in the worst case (up to the one-bit convention for variable-length code boundaries). This is a communication lower bound too: with the public model fixed, the coordinator's transcript must distinguish those outputs.

The arithmetic-word accounting hides the result size. In this example a segment matrix has rational entries with denominator 3^ell_i and numerators of O(ell_i) bits. Sending four exact entries therefore costs O(ell_i) bits for K=2, and O(N) bits over all sites, the same asymptotic order as the N-bit raw observation sequence. The posterior output alone already forces that order. Segment matrices can still be useful for parallel depth, but they do not deliver a worst-case total-bit advantage for exact posterior output on this family. The local matrix products use O(N) arithmetic operations for fixed K, while the forward algorithm also uses O(N); no total-operation improvement is established. A 12-step exact enumeration found 2^N distinct q values for each N=1,...,12; the injectivity proof above, not this finite check, establishes the all-N claim.

Prior art and comparison. The transfer/forward recursion is classical, originating in Baum and Petrie's “Statistical Inference for Probabilistic Functions of Finite State Markov Chains” (1966), https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177699147. Thus the boundary matrix is a known exact representation, not a new inference method. Wu and Uribe, “ByM for Distributed Bayes,” JMLR 26 (2025), https://jmlr.org/papers/volume26/23-1504/23-1504.pdf, explicitly represent distributed exponential-family beliefs by additive sufficient statistics and analyze communication graphs; that gives exact finite-dimensional messages in the covered exponential-family representation, while their network efficiency conclusions are statistical contraction results. Jordan, Lee, and Yang, “Communication-Efficient Distributed Statistical Inference,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07689, build an O(dk)-bit surrogate likelihood whose posterior approximates the global posterior; approximation is the reason that result does not contradict the exact-output lower bound here. Recent adaptive work also stays in an approximate/statistical criterion: Kal et al., “Adaptive Divide and Conquer with Two Rounds of Communication,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17073, achieves minimax-rate adaptation for white-noise estimation, and Lau and Scarlett, “Sequential 1-bit Mean Estimation with Near-Optimal Sample Complexity,” https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/lau26a.html, gives a PAC estimator. These are stronger comparators for bounded-error estimation, not for exact HMM posterior reconstruction.

Status. Proved here: the exact segment-matrix identity and the two-state injectivity/output-cardinality lower bound. Known: forward-matrix inference and additive exponential-family sufficient statistics. Finite-tested: uniqueness for lengths through 12. Refuted: a worst-case sublinear-in-N bit claim for exact posterior output from fixed-state sequence blocks, and any total-arithmetic advantage over the ordinary forward recursion on this dense fixed-K example. Unproved: whether rank-adaptive boundary factors yield a useful *bit* reduction under restricted scientific observation families, or a total-compute advantage under a stated cost model. No new landmark capability was established.

Next proof gate. Fix a concrete restricted data family and a finite-precision output contract (or a finite-valued decision query). Then derive matching upper and lower bounds in bits, including rational numerator/denominator growth, message framing, and all local/coordinator arithmetic. A positive result must beat the strongest protocol under that same output contract; scalar-count or real-RAM comparisons are insufficient.
