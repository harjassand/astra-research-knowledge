1. The complete universal theorem
Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) be bounded self-adjoint operators on an arbitrary complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\). Write
\[
A(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_jA_j,
\qquad
\|A(u)\|\le R|u|.
\]
The task is to construct one positive operator-valued probability density \(M_A\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\widehat M_A(t)=\widehat p(t)e^{iA(t)}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
For every state and every direction \(u\), the classical output \(u\cdot X\) must therefore have exactly the spectral distribution of \(A(u)\), plus independent scalar noise with the distribution of \(u\cdot Z\), where \(Z\sim p\).
The same measurement works for all states and directions. This is stronger than merely obtaining the correct noisy coordinate marginals. The underlying all-linear-marginal Wigner distribution is established prior mathematics. arXiv
Reconstructed theorem
For
\[
0<c<\frac1{2R},
\]
define
\[
f(z)=\sqrt{1+|z|^2}\log\sqrt{1+|z|^2},
\qquad
V_L(x)=cL f(x/L),
\qquad
p_L(x)=Z_L^{-1}e^{-V_L(x)}.
\]
For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), there is a finite
\[
L_0=L_0(m,R,c,\varepsilon)
\]
such that, for all \(L\ge L_0\), equation (1) defines a positive normalized operator density and
\[
\boxed{
(1-\varepsilon)p_L(x)e^{A(\nabla V_L(x))}
\preceq M_{A,L}(x)
\preceq
(1+\varepsilon)p_L(x)e^{A(\nabla V_L(x))}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
This holds uniformly over all tuples in the stated norm ball and all Hilbert-space dimensions.
The noise is smooth, radial, strictly log-concave, and satisfies
\[
\int e^{b|x|}p_L(x)\,dx<\infty
\quad\text{for every finite }b>0.
\]
Its tails obey
\[
-\log p_L(x)=c|x|\log|x|+O(|x|).
\]
The quantifiers matter: independence from \(\dim\mathcal H\) does not mean independence from the number \(m\) of observables, a small sufficient scale, or an inexpensive measurement implementation.
2. Reconstruction of the load-bearing analysis
Compact support does not require a finite eigenbasis
Define the operator distribution through the norm-convergent integral
\[
W_A(h)
=
(2\pi)^{-m}\int \widehat h(t)e^{-iA(t)}\,dt.
\]
Unitarity gives
\[
\|W_A(h)\|
\le (2\pi)^{-m}\|\widehat h\|_1.
\tag{3}
\]
For complex arguments, the Hermitian part of
\[
iA(t)-A(s)
\]
is \(-A(s)\). Differentiating the norm of a vector evolving under this generator gives
\[
\|e^{iA(t)-A(s)}\|\le e^{R|s|}.
\]
The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem, applied to every scalar matrix coefficient, therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{supp}W_A\subseteq\overline B_R.
\]
Analytic continuation also gives
\[
W_A(e^{g\cdot y})=e^{A(g)}.
\tag{4}
\]
The support mechanism is classical Weyl-functional-calculus machinery, explicitly developed in the generalized Wigner literature; it is not a new component of the discovery. arXiv
The distribution estimate really is dimension-independent
Choose a fixed cutoff \(\chi\), equal to one near \(B_R\), supported in \(B_a\) with \(a>R\). For \(N=2k>m\),
\[
|\widehat{\chi h}(t)|
\le
(1+|t|^2)^{-k}\|(1-\Delta)^k(\chi h)\|_1.
\]
Integrating and applying the product rule gives
\[
\boxed{
\|W_A(\chi h)\|
\le C\|h\|_{C^N(B_a)}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Every constant here is scalar. No summation over a basis of \(\mathcal H\) appears.
Remote tails are controlled relative to the correct positive operator
Set
\[
g=\nabla V_L(x),
\qquad
F_x(y)=V_L(x-y)-V_L(x)+g\cdot y.
\]
Then, exactly,
\[
p_L(x-y)=p_L(x)e^{g\cdot y}e^{-F_x(y)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{M_{A,L}(x)}{p_L(x)}
=e^{A(g)}+E_x.
\tag{6}
\]
The positive reference is \(e^{A(g)}\), not the identity.
Writing
\[
s=\frac{|x|}{L},\qquad h(s)=1+\log(1+s),
\]
direct differentiation gives
\[
|g|\le ch(s),
\qquad
\|F_x\|_{C^N(B_a)}
\le \frac{C_0}{L}\frac{h(s)}{1+s}.
\]
Equation (5) bounds the remainder. Conjugating by the inverse square root of the leading operator gives the decisive estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-A(g)/2}E_xe^{-A(g)/2}\|
\le
\frac{C'}{L}
h(s)^{N+1}(1+s)^{-\alpha},
\qquad
\alpha=1-c(R+a)>0.
}
\tag{7}
\]
The factor from inverse conditioning has been retained. Omitting it would invalidate the argument.
Since \(c<1/(2R)\), an admissible \(a>R\) exists. Moreover,
\[
B_{\alpha,N}
=
\sup_{u\ge0}(1+u)^{N+1}e^{-\alpha u}
<\infty.
\]
Thus a sufficient scale is
\[
L\ge
\max\{1,2a,C_0,C'B_{\alpha,N}/\varepsilon\}.
\tag{8}
\]
This controls all \(x\) before any integration. It is not a conclusion drawn from a large finite testing region.
Infinite-dimensional normalization is also justified
The resulting \(M_{A,L}\) is norm-continuous and has the integrable majorant
\[
\|M_{A,L}(x)\|
\le
(1+\varepsilon)p_L(x)e^{R|\nabla V_L(x)|}.
\]
It therefore defines a norm-integrable operator density, even on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Fourier evaluation at zero gives
\[
\int M_{A,L}(x)\,dx=I.
\]
No finite-dimensional truncation limit is needed. Conversely, none of this establishes a theorem for arbitrary unbounded observables.
3. The stronger result: an exact regularizability criterion
The earlier abstract theorem required a global lower bound
\[
T(e^{g\cdot y})\succeq \eta e^{-b|g|}I.
\]
That envelope is sufficient, but it is not necessary.
Laplace-coercivity characterization
Let \(T\) be a compactly supported, norm-continuous, self-adjoint \(B(\mathcal H)\)-valued distribution with
\[
T(1)=I.
\]
Define its real Laplace transform
\[
H_T(g)=T(e^{g\cdot y}).
\]
Then the following are equivalent:
A. For every finite real \(g\),
\[
H_T(g)\succeq\lambda_g I
\quad\text{for some }\lambda_g>0.
\]
B. There exists a scalar probability law \(\nu\), having every exponential moment, such that
\[
T*\nu
\]
is a positive normalized operator-valued measure.
Furthermore, whenever this is possible, \(\nu\) can be chosen with an everywhere positive, smooth, radial, strictly log-concave density \(p\), whose every derivative decays faster than every exponential.
The condition in A is called coercivity: positivity with a nonzero lower spectral bound at each finite tilt. The bound may deteriorate arbitrarily quickly as \(|g|\) grows.
Why the condition is necessary
Suppose \(M=T*\nu\) is positive.
A technical issue must first be resolved: one cannot simply interchange a formal distributional convolution with an unbounded exponential test. The proof instead uses the entire continuation
\[
\Phi(z)=\widehat\nu(z)\,T(e^{iz\cdot y}).
\]
After scalarizing against a unit vector, positivity and finite-difference formulas identify all even moments. Cauchy estimates on arbitrary complex circles then establish genuine exponential moments, uniformly over unit vectors. This justifies the bounded positive operators
\[
Q_\pm(g)
=
\int e^{\pm g\cdot x}M(dx)
=
m_\nu(\pm g)H_T(\pm g).
\]
For every unit vector \(v\), scalar Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\langle v,Q_+v\rangle
\langle v,Q_-v\rangle\ge1.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q_+(g)\succeq \|Q_-(g)\|^{-1}I,
\]
which forces \(H_T(g)\) to be boundedly invertible.
This necessity argument covers nonsmooth input noise and weakly countably additive POVMs; it does not assume the conclusion’s smooth density.
Why the condition is sufficient
The same local exponential-tilt operation works for general \(T\):
\[
(T*p)(x)=p(x)\bigl[H_T(\nabla V(x))+E_x\bigr].
\]
For a fixed finite distribution order \(N\),
\[
\|H_T(g)^{-1/2}E_xH_T(g)^{-1/2}\|
\le
C e^{a|g|}(1+|g|)^N
\frac{\eta_x}{\lambda_{\min}(H_T(g))},
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\eta_x\) controls the local derivatives of \(V\) of orders \(2,\ldots,N\).
On each compact set of tilts,
\[
\lambda_n
=
\min_{|g|\le n+2}\inf\sigma H_T(g)>0.
\]
Choose the curvature tolerance for that band small enough to compensate for \(\lambda_n\).
The construction makes the radial slope \(V'(r)\) rise through increasingly delayed, increasingly broad smooth ramps. Two controls are essential:
- Broad ramps make radial curvature and higher derivatives small.
- Large starting radii make the angular Hessian contribution \(V'(r)/r\) small.
The slope still tends to infinity, so \(V(r)/r\to\infty\), giving every exponential moment. But it increases slowly enough that the relative error in (9) stays below a prescribed tolerance everywhere.
The complete ramp construction is in the proof manuscript. It removes the fixed exponential envelope; it does not make the lower bounds \(\lambda_n\) efficiently recognizable or cheap to use.
An exact counterexample makes the infinite-dimensional condition necessary
On \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), take the diagonal operator-valued signed measure with entries
\[
T_n=
\frac{
\frac1{16}\delta_2+\delta_{-2}
+\left(-\frac12+\frac1n\right)\delta_0
}{
\frac9{16}+\frac1n
}.
\]
It is normalized and uniformly bounded, with common compact support. Its Laplace entries are
\[
H_n(g)=
\frac{\sinh^2(g-\log2)+1/n}{9/16+1/n}>0.
\]
Every nonzero vector has a strictly positive quadratic form at every real tilt. Nevertheless,
\[
H_n(\log2)=\frac{16}{9n+16}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus every finite truncation is regularizable, but the infinite direct sum has no common all-exponential regularizer.
This directly falsifies the weaker condition “positive on every nonzero vector.” It does not contradict the Wigner theorem, because \(e^{A(g)}\) for bounded self-adjoint \(A(g)\) always has a positive lower bound.
A consequence beyond quantum observables
Let \(P(g)\) be a Hermitian matrix polynomial satisfying
\[
P(0)=I,\qquad P(g)\succ0\quad\text{for every real }g.
\]
Apply the characterization to
\[
T=P(-\partial)\delta_0.
\]
There exists a radial, strictly log-concave, rapidly decreasing density \(p\) such that
\[
\boxed{
P(-\partial)p(x)\succeq0
\quad\text{for every }x.
}
\tag{10}
\]
This connects the operation to matrix-valued differential inequalities. It does not provide an efficient procedure for recognizing global polynomial positivity.
4. The sharp Pauli obstruction survives dependence
For a two-coordinate noise law, let
\[
\kappa(s,t)=\log\mathbb E e^{sZ_1+tZ_2}.
\]
Exponential reweighting of a proposed positive measurement, followed by the moment-matrix Schur complement, gives
\[
\kappa_{22}(s,0)I+K_2(s)-K_1(s)^2\succeq0,
\tag{11}
\]
where
\[
K_\ell(s)
=
e^{-sA/2}
\left.\partial_t^\ell e^{sA+tB}\right|_{t=0}
e^{-sA/2}.
\]
For
\[
A=R\sigma_z,\qquad B=R\sigma_x,
\]
direct differentiation yields
\[
K_2-K_1^2
=
\operatorname{diag}(-d_R(s),d_R(s)),
\quad
d_R(s)=\frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s^2}.
\]
For radial noise, writing
\[
K(s)=\log\mathbb E e^{sZ_1},
\]
the transverse tilted variance is \(K'(s)/s\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
K'(s)\ge
\frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s}.
}
\tag{12}
\]
This derivation does not assume independence.
The inequality forces cumulant growth with exponential rate at least \(2R\). By contrast, a density bounded above by
\[
Ce^{-c_0r\log r},
\qquad c_0>\frac1{2R},
\]
has a cumulant upper bound with a strictly smaller exponential rate. The contradiction follows from
\[
\sup_{r>0}\{sr-c'r\log r\}=c'e^{s/c'-1}
\]
for \(1/(2R)<c'<c_0\).
Together with the construction,
\[
\boxed{
\sup c_{\mathrm{universal}}=\frac1{2R}
}
\]
for \(m\ge2\) in the specified radial tail class.
This remains a supremum, not universal endpoint attainment.
The mechanism explaining dependence also survives: independent transverse noise retains a fixed variance under the first-coordinate tilt, whereas suitable dependent noise can increase that variance enough to offset the operator defect. Matching individual coordinate laws and covariance does not capture this behavior.
5. Historical priority: what is old, and what is still unresolved
The most consequential prior-art finding is older scalar convolution positivity.
Pomatto, Strack and Tamuz reproduce a theorem credited to Ruzsa–Székely and Mattner: a finite signed measure of total mass one can be convolved with a probability measure to become positive, preserving a prescribed finite order of moments—or all ordinary moments when the input has them. Their appendix also provides a common regularizer for a bounded scalar class. arXiv
Mattner’s original paper was inspected directly, including its quotient-of-characteristic-functions construction and attribution to Ruzsa–Székely. EMS Press
Therefore, “signed objects become positive after convolution” is not a defensible novelty claim.
The distinction here is not cosmetic. All ordinary moments do not imply all exponential moments. The older geometric convolution construction involves
\[
(1-q)\sum_{k\ge0}q^k\pi^{*k},
\]
whose moment-generating series fails when \(q\,m_\pi(g)\ge1\). The new characterization identifies exactly which compact operator distributions permit the stronger all-exponential requirement.
Other comparisons also narrow the claim:
Prior theory	Overlap and remaining distinction
Generalized Wigner/Weyl calculus	The representation and compact-support mechanism are inherited. The precise uniform positive scalar convolution and sharp tail theorem were not found in the inspected statements.
Generalized Husimi representations	The inspected construction uses a general kernel and generally state-dependent normalization. It does not automatically satisfy the state-independent scalar multiplier in (1).
Positive polynomial multipliers	Finite scalar versions have classical antecedents; they cannot be presented as a newly discovered general principle.
Catalytic stochastic order	Exponential-moment criteria already play a central role, but the comparison is between stochastic orders with support/mean constraints, not pointwise operator positivity under this exact contract.


The Husimi distinction follows from the paper’s explicit representation and normalization formulas. arXiv The polynomial and catalytic-order comparisons are supported by the primary statements of Kepka–Korbelář and Fritz. arXiv
What remains uncleared: whether an existing operator-valued convolution or ordered-algebra multiplier theorem already implies the exact characterization above. Anderson’s original 1969/1970 articles were located bibliographically but not obtained in full, so their complete contents have not been excluded as relevant ancestors.
The strongest defensible originality statement is consequently: a precise proof-backed candidate with substantial known antecedents, not a cleared claim of a fundamentally unprecedented operation.
6. General resource costs
Noise variance is not free
For the universal family, with \(m,c\) fixed,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(Z_j)\sim \frac{L}{c}
\qquad(L\to\infty).
}
\tag{13}
\]
This follows by rescaling near the quadratic center of \(V_L\), with dominated convergence controlling the tails.
There is also an implementation-independent necessity. For \(m\) anticommuting Clifford observables of directional norm \(R\), the full second-moment block matrix gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma\ge m(m-1)R^2.
}
\tag{14}
\]
For radial noise, each coordinate variance therefore satisfies
\[
v\ge(m-1)R^2.
\]
This is a direct consequence of standard moment positivity, not a claim of a new general uncertainty principle. It shows why uniformity in Hilbert dimension does not eliminate costs as \(m\) grows.
The sufficient regularization scale is not yet practically compiled
The bound in (8) contains explicitly identifiable but unevaluated derivative and cutoff constants. Even its scalar supremum factor can become large near the sharp boundary.
For \(m=3\), \(R=1\), \(N=4\), the tested choices give approximately
\[
B_{\alpha,N}=2.77\times10^4
\quad(c=0.25),
\]
and
\[
B_{\alpha,N}=2.13\times10^{11}
\quad(c=0.49).
\]
These are not values of \(L_0\). They are only one factor in its sufficient bound. The earlier \(L=20\) Pauli tests cannot be promoted into a universal scale guarantee.
Samples, synthesis, and inversion remain different costs
For \(K\) fixed unit directions, finite-variance robust mean estimation gives the achievable shot bound
\[
n=
O\!\left(
\frac{R^2+\|\Sigma\|}{\epsilon^2}
\log\frac K\delta
\right)
\]
for simultaneous mean accuracy \(\epsilon\) with failure probability at most \(\delta\). This presupposes actual access to the measurement; it does not synthesize it.
A mathematical dilation can be written as
\[
(J\psi)(x)=M_A(x)^{1/2}\psi.
\]
Normalization makes \(J\) an isometry. But this is not a low-cost quantum circuit. Matrix square roots, continuous-output approximation, control synthesis, state preparation, and precision still have to be acquired.
Likewise, the direct Fourier estimator
\[
\frac{e^{it\cdot X}}{\widehat p(t)}
\]
has squared magnitude \(1/|\widehat p(t)|^2\), and is undefined at zeros. A positive forward representation does not supply a stable inverse.
Exact analytic continuation can sometimes preserve uniqueness despite zeros, but uniqueness from exact data is not stable reconstruction from finitely many samples.

Final assessment
The mathematical result is now stronger than an isolated regularizer:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{A compact operator distribution admits positive scalar regularization}\\
\text{with all exponential moments}\\
\Longleftrightarrow\\
\text{every real Laplace transform is positive and boundedly invertible.}
\end{gathered}
}
\]
The universal quantum theorem follows because its Laplace transform is \(e^{A(g)}\). Quantitative control supplies the sharp radial tail supremum. The direct-sum counterexample shows that the infinite-dimensional condition cannot be weakened casually.
What has not been established is that this classification is historically new, that it resolves a recognized major foundational problem, or that it supplies a scalable quantum capability. The explicit qubit implementation actually closes one proposed advantage negatively: it is a different exact representation of an existing measurement’s information.
The remaining decisive work is therefore concentrated: independent verification of the characterization, exact operator-convolution priority clearance, and a consequence or implementation beyond known measurement postprocessing. Those are distinct obligations; none can be replaced by additional positive examples.
