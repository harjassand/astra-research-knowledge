PHYSICAL ALTERNATIVE: OPTICALLY WRITTEN COMPLEX TRANSMISSION
Status: EXISTING_OPERATION_CONFIRMED_FROM_PRIMARY_REPORT; NEW_OPERATION_NOT_ACHIEVED.
Date: 2026-10-15. Research-only source audit; no physical experiment executed here.

I selected a different memory carrier from pressure-written superconductivity:
the amorphous/crystalline atomic arrangement of an encapsulated optical
phase-change patch. The selected operation writes and erases a retained
complex optical transmission coefficient with optical pulses. Both amplitude
and phase are measured in the same fabricated waveguide. This satisfies a
useful physical coexistence test, but the operation already exists.

PRIMARY COUPLED REGIME

Sawant et al. (2025), sections 2-3 and Figures 3-6, report a 1.5-um GST-on-Si
patch: a 400-ps, about 100-pJ pulse produces about 55 degrees of optical phase
change and transmission change; a 1.2-us, 7.4-nJ pulse resets both. Fabrication
uses a 200-mm process with encapsulation. The reported crystalline propagation
loss is 5.7 dB/um. The abstract/table claim 1e8-cycle endurance; a 1e9-cycle
14-hour stress run ends with degradation and phase segregation. Pulses recur
every 50 us. These are source-reported device results, not independent checks.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/adom.202500775

The operation's useful output is the changed complex transmission after the
programming transient. This is stronger evidence than a transition onset or
an inferred barrier. It does not establish a full optical router, a large
lossless phase shift, long dark retention, or energy-efficient computation.
Repeated cycling every 50 us is not a years-long retention experiment.

DECISIVE COST FROM THE SAME MATERIAL AND GEOMETRY

The saved calculate.py reproduces ordinary arithmetic. A full optical
write-erase cycle costs about 7.5 nJ at the paper's programming-energy boundary;
98.7 percent is reset. Threshold energies for the first detectable change are
not substituted for the energies used to obtain full contrast. At the reported
stress rate this is about 0.15 mW of programming optical power, before laser,
EDFA, modulation, routing, readout, calibration, and thermal-control overhead.
Those complete costs are UNKNOWN.

For fixed cross section and constant reported slopes, the phase change and
crystalline attenuation obey

    Delta_phi = beta L;  A_c = alpha L;
    A_c = (alpha / beta) Delta_phi.

This conditional extension yields approximately 28 dB crystalline propagation
loss for a pi change, or about 0.16 percent transmission. A 3-dB propagation
budget permits only about 19 degrees. The script checks both the direct
55-degree/1.5-um slope and the table's rounded 37-degree/um slope.

This calculation is a gate against lengthening this measured patch into a
low-loss pi shifter. It is not a universal theorem about all phase-change
devices: different mode architectures can bypass the lossy phase. It does not
turn the small experimentally measured patch into a demonstrated pi device.

MATCHED EXISTING OPERATIONS, WITHOUT SPLICING THEIR BEST PROPERTIES

The exact all-optical operation is itself prior art, so an Astra claim of
inventing pulse-written retained complex transmission fails directly.

For repeated retained optical control, Dutta et al. (2026), arXiv:2604.08637v1,
Figures 4-5, provide a stronger low-loss, high-endurance same-platform
comparison: eleven tapered Sb2Se3 islands on a Si microring, about 0.53 dB/pi
excess loss and over 1e8 cycles. Figure 5 was visually inspected. Its long-run
contrast is around 0.6; the text rounds the headline contrast to about 0.7.
The actually measured phase shift is about 0.15 pi. The 33-nJ/25-uJ energies
shown for the single segment must not be transferred to eleven islands.
Also, its excess crystalline-minus-amorphous loss is not identical to the
GST paper's absolute crystalline propagation loss. Thus no numerical claim of
whole-device efficiency domination is made.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08637v1
Published version: https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202522895

For routing as the useful output, Xu et al. (2019) already measured retained
GST directional couplers with roughly 1-dB insertion loss in both states for
1x2 routing across 30 nm. The field avoids the lossy crystalline waveguide.
But the demonstrated state change used a 200-C, 10-minute thermal anneal;
nanosecond switching and approximately 2-nJ energy were proposals/estimates.
Their low loss cannot be combined with Sawant's optical pulses and endurance
as though one apparatus demonstrated both. This prevents rescuing the selected
candidate by assembling separate favorable results.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphotonics.8b01628

The same distinction excludes a deceptively strong memristor comparison:
Fang et al. (2023) obtain retained resistance configurations, but the reported
optical contrast is read with an applied negative bias. Their small-pJ energy
and partial phase shift do not establish unbiased pi routing.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/adom.202301178

VERDICT AND DISPOSITION

This mechanism replaces the pressure-state carrier with an actual material
memory and a directly measured functional readout. It does not produce a new
Astra operation or a demonstrated consequential advantage over existing
retained photonic control. The fixed-geometry phase-loss calculation is a
useful negative result, not invention or technological validation.

Stop this candidate as a new-operation claim. Do not continue optimizing an
unmeasured hypothetical efficient router, and do not combine the three device
histories into one fictitious material/device regime. A future qualification
would have to be a new, measured joint regime, with identical output insertion
loss, extinction, bandwidth, retention duration, cycling, and complete energy
against an already nonvolatile competitor.
