## A modest operational reading of “epistemic closure”

The phrase is ambiguous in the source. It can mean proof completeness, model identification, experimental qualification, or an affordable scientific decision. These are distinct. For this audit, use the following decision-level convention, not a new theorem:

> Fix the scientific decision, admissible model class, allowed interventions, uncertainty tolerance, and full resource boundary. Acquired evidence is sufficient for that decision when a valid certificate bounds its error or regret over the models still consistent with the evidence; all acquisition, preparation, calibration, computation, precision, and use costs needed by that certificate have been counted.

This permits incomplete latent identification when it cannot change the declared decision. PR94's actual scalar-response proof (155–173) is a positive mathematical example: the weighted-transpose ambiguity gives identical responses for every permitted diagonal control with fixed source/detector. The ambiguity is harmless for that task. Changing the source, detector, or control family can make it consequential. Its weak-anchor and cyclic indistinguishability examples (185–256) show why a query schedule is not uniformly robust and why low-order calibration cannot certify every collective decision. One full-support measurement distinguishes the displayed cyclic pair; no exponential query lower bound follows.

FS's joint-moment closure similarly integrates calibration histories and targets against the **same acquired joint measure** (decision_closure/PROOFS.md 187–277). Separate marginal calibration would change that admissible set. Sparse optimizing measures do not imply only that many physical experiments suffice, and the type-addressable optimum is an outer bound rather than an available controller. These are precise decision certificates under a supplied physical envelope.

The pressure text and FT lifetime-transport proof display the physical counterpart. A transition onset, low-temperature shielding, or favorable individual barrier can agree across models with different useful outputs. The scientific decision needs same-state released transport/retention, or complete accepted output over lifetimes with all damage channels. No amount of additional optimization over favorable invented coefficients acquires these measurements. Conversely, acquiring a sufficient model does not guarantee the resulting optimization or proof search is computationally easy.

The observed dossiers support these scoped distinctions. They do not prove a universal epistemic-closure hypothesis, a general ceiling of model reasoning, or the claim that every previous failed mission is solely an information deficit.
