3. Exact model and the relative-tail failure of mean-clock compression

Let X~Exp(1), J~Bernoulli(p), Y~Exp(1/M), independent. Then
  T = X + JY.
Put C=pM/(M-1). For the family p=M^(-3), M>=2, 0<C<1. Direct convolution gives
  S(t)=P(T>t)=(1-C) exp(-t) + C exp(-t/M).                 (3.1)
In particular
  S_M := S(M) = (1-C) exp(-M)+C/e ~ p/e.

The mean-clock reduction instead has
  T_hat=(1+pM)X,
  S_hat(M)=exp[-M/(1+pM)],
  E[(T-T_hat)^2]=2pM^2=2/M.
These equalities reproduce the stated obstruction by independent elementary
calculation. S_hat(M)/S_M tends to zero while the bulk clock bound tends to
zero. No statement about the missing incoming rate certificates is used.

4. Theorem: even the best scalar time tilt still has rare-branch cost

For theta<1/M define the exact scalar exponentially tilted trajectory law by
  dP_theta/dP = exp(theta T)/Z_theta,
  Z_theta=E exp(theta T)
         = [1-p+p/(1-M theta)]/(1-theta).                 (4.1)
This law is easy to generate for this model. Its X holding time is Exp(1-theta),
its tilted trap-branch probability is
  [p/(1-M theta)] / [1-p+p/(1-M theta)],
and conditional on the trap branch its Y holding time is Exp(1/M-theta).
Thus the lower bound gives time tilting its exact law, including the induced
branch reweighting. It is not testing an incorrectly implemented tilt.

The usual unbiased likelihood-ratio estimator of S_M is
  W_theta=Z_theta exp(-theta T) 1{T>M},  T~P_theta.
Let R_theta=E_theta[W_theta^2]/S_M^2. Its finite-second-moment domain is exactly
  -1/M < theta < 1/M.
For theta<=-1/M the tilt itself still exists, but the second moment is infinite;
for theta>=1/M the normalizing constant is infinite.

The exact second moment is
  E_theta[W_theta^2] = Z_theta L_theta,
where, by integrating the density corresponding to (3.1),
  L_theta = (1-C) exp[-(1+theta)M]/(1+theta)
          + C exp[-(1+M theta)]/(1+M theta).              (4.2)
Both terms are nonnegative throughout the finite-second-moment domain.

Uniform lower bound, valid for EVERY such theta:
  Z_theta >= (1-p)/(1+1/M).
Writing c=M theta in (-1,1), the function exp[-(1+c)]/(1+c) is decreasing and
has infimum exp(-2)/2. Hence
  R_theta >= B_M := (1-p) C exp(-2)
                    / [2(1+1/M) S_M^2].                 (4.3)

Define r_M=(1-C) exp(1-M)/C, so S_M=(C/e)(1+r_M). Then (4.3) becomes
  p R_theta >= (1-p)(M-1)
                / [2(M+1)(1+r_M)^2].                   (4.4)
Since p=M^(-3), r_M tends to zero, and therefore
  liminf_{M->infinity} p R_{theta_M} >= 1/2              (4.5)
for every choice of finite-second-moment tilts theta_M, including choices
adapted to p and M with arbitrarily precise arithmetic.

The constant is sharp: choose
  theta_M=(1-sqrt(p))/M.
Then 1-M theta_M=sqrt(p), Z_theta_M tends to 1, the second term of (4.2) is
asymptotic to C exp(-2)/2, and its first term is negligible. Consequently
  p R_theta_M -> 1/2,
  inf_theta R_theta = (1+o(1))/(2p).                     (4.6)
The naive Bernoulli estimator has R=1/S_M~e/p. Time tilting improves only the
constant on this family; it does not remove the M^3 sample requirement.

Exact finite-M corollary. For every integer M>=16,
  R_theta >= M^3/4                                      (4.7)
for all finite-second-moment scalar time tilts. To verify this without decimal
exponentials, use 2<e<3 to get
  r_M <= 3M^3/2^M <= 3/16.
The last expression decreases for M>=16 because
  ((M+1)/M)^3/2 <= (17/16)^3/2 = 4913/8192 < 1.
Equation (4.4) is therefore at least
  (4095/4096)(15/17) / [2(19/16)^2]
  = 61425/196384 > 1/4.
All rational inequalities are checked in exact_certificate.json.

For the sample mean of N iid likelihood-ratio draws, relative mean-square error
is exactly (R_theta-1)/N. Thus fixed relative MSE needs Omega(M^3) draws, and
relative MSE <=epsilon^2 needs at least (M^3/4-1)/epsilon^2 for M>=16.
This claim concerns this likelihood-ratio sample-mean estimator, not every
possible algorithm given the generator. The exact two-mode formula already
solves the model deterministically.

A stronger fixed-confidence statement also holds for the standard sample mean.
If N_M p_M->0, then for every fixed epsilon in (0,1), uniformly over the finite
variance tilts,
  P_theta( mean(W_theta) >= (1-epsilon) S_M ) -> 0.        (4.8)
Proof: fix K>1 and split samples into M<T<=KM and T>KM. For the first band,
  P_theta(M<T<=KM) <= exp(K) S_M/Z_theta = O_K(p).
The probability any of N samples lands in it is O_K(Np). The expected weighted
contribution from the second region is exactly S(KM), irrespective of tilt.
Markov's inequality bounds the probability its sample mean exceeds
(1-epsilon)S_M by S(KM)/[(1-epsilon)S_M], which tends to
exp(1-K)/(1-epsilon). First take M to infinity and then K to infinity. This
proves (4.8), and explains why the variance lower bound is not merely caused by
an irrelevant outlier.

Interpretation. Near the slow pole, time tilting makes entering the trap more
likely, but lengthens its mean tilted residence time to M/(1-M theta). The
original tail's main weight still comes from durations of order M, which then
become rare under the tilted law. A scalar clock change moves rarity between
branch selection and residence length.

5. Equally informed comparator: branch conditioning has bounded relative error

Assume p and the trap channel are supplied, with the conditional sampling
interface in section 1. Let
  q_M=P(X+Y>M)=(M/e-exp(-M))/(M-1).
The estimator
  Z_branch=(1-p) exp(-M) + p 1{X+Y>M},
using independent draws X~Exp(1), Y~Exp(1/M), is unbiased for S_M and satisfies
  Var(Z_branch)/S_M^2
    = p^2 q_M(1-q_M) / [(1-p)exp(-M)+p q_M]^2
    <= (1-q_M)/q_M <= e-1.                              (5.1)
The last inequality follows from X>=0, hence q_M>=P(Y>M)=1/e. This bound is
uniform in M>1 and p in (0,1). For p=M^(-3), it tends to e-1.

It therefore takes O(epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta)) conditional draws for a fixed
relative error and failure probability, independent of 1/p. A multiplicative
Chernoff bound on the Bernoulli estimate of q_M gives this directly. This is
standard stratification/conditional Monte Carlo, not a claimed new method.
Moreover, q_M has an explicit two-exponential formula here, so the strongest
equally informed comparator performs no Monte Carlo at all.

For a general known network, a rare channel could be identified by first
entrance to a set D before the product set B. The committor
  h_i=P_i(tau_D<tau_B)
solves Qh=0 outside B union D, h_D=1, h_B=0. On h_i>0, the usual Doob rates
  q^h_ij=q_ij h_j/h_i
condition on entering D first. Robust graph transformation already supplies
these hitting probabilities. Conditioning then continuing the original model
after D is an exact branch interface. The bounded-variance conclusion (5.1)
does NOT transfer automatically: it additionally needs the conditional tail
within that branch to have probability bounded away from zero. Computing the
entry-time law, choosing D, multiple mechanisms, internal cycling, and further
tail rarity can still be difficult. This is a conditional route, not a solved
general-network acquisition or tail problem.

6. Exact semi-Markov elimination exposes the missing pole

For the trap model the exact Laplace transform is
  E exp(-zT) = [1-p+p/(1+Mz)]/(1+z).                    (6.1)
Keeping the excursion factor 1/(1+Mz) preserves the tail exactly. Replacing it
by its mean-clock Markov surrogate does not. The exact transform has two poles
and two dynamic degrees of freedom; eliminating the trap from the state list
does not remove its memory pole.

More generally, for killed generator A partitioned into retained R and removed
I, the retained survival resolvent is
  vbar_R(z) = [zI-A_RR-A_RI(zI-A_II)^(-1)A_IR]^(-1)
               [1_R+A_RI(zI-A_II)^(-1)1_I].             (6.2)
This follows by block Gaussian elimination of (zI-A)vbar=1. It is the ordinary
frequency-dependent Schur complement / exact excursion-memory representation,
not a new reduction. For scalar R it can still have rational degree n. Sparse
elimination fill, repeated frequency solves, numerical precision, and inversion
of the transform remain costs. A smaller retained-state list alone does not
establish smaller total representation cost.

Tilting excursions by exp(theta*time) replaces the removed block inverse with
(-A_II-theta I)^(-1). For the removed trap this is M/(1-M theta), diverging at
the slow tail pole. Near-pole conditioning and preserving that pole are real
requirements. Positive first-moment elimination cannot make it disappear.

Diagnostic only: if w(t)>0, w(0)=1 and
  m(t) <= [w'(t)-Aw(t)]_i/w_i(t) <= M(t)
for every transient state, semigroup comparison gives
  exp[-integral M] w(t) <= exp(At)1 <= exp[-integral m] w(t).
This is an immediate positive-system comparison certificate and is NOT claimed
novel. For the one-clock lift w(t)=exp[-t/(1+pM)]1, its log-interval width at
t=M is (1-p-1/M)M, which diverges. It correctly refuses a useful relative-tail
certificate when the slow trap is absent from the representation. Computing a
good positive lift, certifying its residual for all times, and beating the
known componentwise-relative interval matrix-exponential baseline remain open.

7. Acquisition obstruction: the conditional interface is not free physical data

Suppose the generator is unknown and the only acquisition interface is N
independent source-start trajectories, even with complete state labels. Compare
two models with the same source rate and same rare branch probability p, but
trap means M and 2M. Conditional on taking no trap branch, the entire observed
data have exactly the same distribution. A coupling gives total variation
distance at most 1-(1-p)^N, so any equal-prior binary test has error at least
  (1-p)^N/2.                                           (7.1)
At the fixed deadline t=M the survival probabilities have ratio tending to
exp(1/2)>1.64. Thus, for example, relative error 0.1 with confidence 1-delta on
both models would distinguish them, and necessarily
  N >= log(1/(2delta))/[-log(1-p)]
    = Omega(p^(-1) log(1/delta)).                        (7.2)
For p=M^(-3), passive acquisition still costs order M^3 trajectories. This lower
bound concerns the stated trajectory interface; it does not apply when the
network and exact rates are supplied, or to a different controlled experimental
interface that can deliberately populate the trap.

The branch-conditioned simulation's bounded variance therefore cannot be
promoted to a physical rare-event capability without paying for an actual
rare-channel access/rate interface. Potential-energy landscape methods provide
a different model-acquisition route, but finding the saddles, missing channels,
Hessian factors, calibration, and model adequacy remain separate costs.

Complete costs for the stated interfaces:
  - supplied three-state rates: O(log M) binary bits encode p=M^-3 and 1/M;
  - exact two-mode/Schur solution: constant number of rational operations and
    exponential evaluations, with precision charged to requested relative error;
  - scalar-tilt sample mean: two exponential draws plus branch selection per
    sample, but Omega(M^3) draws for fixed relative accuracy;
  - branch-conditioned estimator: two exponential draws per sample and known p,
    O(epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) samples; direct two-mode evaluation is stronger;
  - passive unknown-rate/channel interface: Omega(M^3 log(1/delta)) trajectories
    merely to distinguish the displayed tail decisions, plus experiment time;
  - arbitrary supplied n-state network: committor/reduction preprocessing can
    require dense O(n^3) arithmetic, O(n^2) storage and sparse fill; exact
    semi-Markov memory need not be lower order; precision and full acquisition
    are not hidden in a cheap retained-state count.

Historical priority of the specific sharp scalar-tilt obstruction is not
resolved. The change of measure, conditional sampling, semigroup comparison,
Schur complement, and statistical indistinguishability tools are standard.
This packet supplies a scoped proof and diagnostic, not a novelty claim.

Decision: preserve the sharp tilt failure and acquisition/interface distinction
as mathematical evidence. Do not pursue scalar elapsed-time tilting or a generic
relative-semigroup residual as the principal breakthrough branch. A new route
would need a physically acquired rare-channel representation or a model-specific
conditional decomposition that genuinely avoids the existing committor,
semi-Markov, and componentwise-relative exponential costs.
