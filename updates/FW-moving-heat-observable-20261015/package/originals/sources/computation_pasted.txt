Result: a checked mathematical mechanism, but not yet new scientific capability
I developed and tested a method for bounding a finite-time linear voltage response directly from measured impedance, without reconstructing a relaxation spectrum or assuming a largest relaxation time.
On one publicly available battery-cell input, the method produces a narrow conditional enclosure for the 10-second response. The same measurements also admit two explicitly constructed, positive circuit models whose 1,000-second responses differ by more than a factor of five. Both models satisfy the stated measurement-error constraints, checked using exact rational arithmetic.
I then investigated a different approach: designing a small set of measurements for the requested response, rather than accelerating reconstruction after acquiring a full spectrum. That produced checked sparse-measurement estimators, but the faster tested measurement sets lost useful precision.
These results do not yet meet Astra’s breakthrough criterion. The central positivity-and-duality strategy has substantial prior art; no finite-amplitude experimental voltage prediction was validated; and no advantage over the strongest relevant incumbent was established.

2. The exact task—and what was actually accessible
The intended output was
\[
Q_T=\frac{\Delta V(T)}{I},
\]
the incremental voltage response per unit current at the end of a pulse of duration \(T\).
This is a useful quantity only after specifying the input waveform, initial condition, operating state and applicable physical model. A sharp numerical answer for a linear-response model is not automatically an accurate prediction for a finite operating pulse.
The public dataset contains characterization measurements for 92 Panasonic NCR18650B cells, including impedance, pulse and pseudo-open-circuit measurements. Its published conditions include 25°C and measurements at 50% state of charge. Zenodo
The executable calculation used one cell’s 70 complex impedance samples. I retained the complete first-row frequency, real-part and imaginary-part vectors from the raw CSV views.
The paired pulse files were located, but a complete aligned current/time/voltage trace and an unambiguous absolute pulse-current contract were not recovered into the executable environment. Consequently:
No held-out experimental pulse-voltage validation was completed.
The distinction matters. The numerical results below concern all models consistent with a stated measurement interface, not an independently validated prediction of the real cell’s operating voltage.
3. The strongest prior-art collision
Before interpreting the construction as an invention, I checked the relevant existing approaches.
Grabovsky’s Stieltjes reconstruction work already provides certified reconstruction and uncertainty analysis in an electrochemical-impedance setting. Lawrence’s spectral work explicitly formulates bounds on linear functionals through positivity and Lagrange duality. Thus, “avoid reconstructing the full positive spectrum” is not itself a new mathematical principle. arXiv
I also inspected the original hybrid-drt fitting code and its prediction interface. It includes substantially more than a simple nonnegative least-squares fit: hierarchical modeling, nuisance components, error treatment and joint-domain interfaces. I did not execute the full package, and the NNLS comparator reported below must not be confused with a reproduction of it.  
Joint time/frequency-domain electrochemical acquisition is also existing research, rather than a new proposal arising here. 
The narrower construction I developed is therefore best described as an explicit, checked implementation of a useful measurement-based normalization, with historical novelty unresolved—not a newly established foundational operation.
4. The constructed mechanism
Reweight unobserved modes using a quantity that was measured
Consider the restricted distributed-resistor–capacitor model
\[
Z(i\omega)
=
R_\infty+i\omega L+\frac{\kappa}{i\omega}
+
\int_0^\infty
\frac{d\mu(\tau)}{1+i\omega\tau},
\]
where
\[
R_\infty,\kappa\geq0,\qquad \mu\geq0.
\]
Here \(\tau\) is a relaxation time. The model includes series inductance and capacitance, but does not include every passive system or general nonlinear battery dynamics.
For \(T>0\), its non-impulsive step response is
\[
Q_T
=
R_\infty+\kappa T+
\int_0^\infty
\left(1-e^{-T/\tau}\right)d\mu(\tau).
\]
The important challenge is that the data need not determine the distribution \(\mu\), its total resistance or its longest relaxation time.
Choose two measured angular frequencies \(0<a<b\), and form
\[
\boxed{
M=
\operatorname{Re}Z(ia)
-\operatorname{Im}Z(ia)
+\frac{a}{b}\operatorname{Im}Z(ib).
}
\]
The unknown series-inductance term cancels exactly:
\[
-aL+\frac ab\,bL=0.
\]
Writing \(x=a\tau\) and \(r=b/a>1\), define
\[
w(x)
=
\frac{1+x}{1+x^2}
-\frac{x}{1+r^2x^2}.
\]
This weight is strictly positive, since
\[
w(x)
=
\frac{1}{1+x^2}
+
x\left(
\frac{1}{1+x^2}
-\frac{1}{1+r^2x^2}
\right)>0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
M
=
R_\infty+
\frac{\kappa}{a}(1-r^{-2})
+
\int_0^\infty w(a\tau)\,d\mu(\tau).
\]
A measured combination of impedance values controls a positive weighted mass of all the unknown modes. No microscopic enumeration or assumed bound on total relaxation resistance is required.
Why this handles arbitrarily slow modes
Introduce
\[
s=\frac{a\tau}{1+a\tau}\in[0,1].
\]
After reweighting by \(w\), both the measurement kernels and the finite-time response kernel extend continuously to this compact interval. The endpoints represent the ohmic and ideal capacitive limits.
In particular,
\[
\lim_{\tau\to0}
\frac{1-e^{-T/\tau}}{w(a\tau)}
=1,
\qquad
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}
\frac{1-e^{-T/\tau}}{w(a\tau)}
=
\frac{aT}{1-r^{-2}}.
\]
Thus a finite-time response remains bounded even when the unweighted total relaxation resistance does not.
There is a corresponding obstruction. Under a measurement box with some strict slack, sufficiently slow positive modes can be added while making their finite-frequency signature arbitrarily small. Their DC relaxation resistance can nevertheless become arbitrarily large.
Finite-time response, long-horizon response and DC resistance are different information problems. A representation that handles one must not be presented as having resolved the others.
The full report gives the compactified kernels and the bounded-observable argument explicitly.
5. What the real-input computation established
A model discrepancy appears before any precision claim
The full 70-point spectrum violates a necessary property of the positive-RC model.
For that model, the real part of impedance cannot increase as frequency increases. But the retained measurements contain
\[
\operatorname{Re}Z(10{,}000\text{ Hz})=38.03\ \mathrm{m}\Omega,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Re}Z(5{,}484\text{ Hz})=37.00\ \mathrm{m}\Omega.
\]
The ordering violation is \(1.03\ \mathrm{m}\Omega\). Consequently, any uniform real-component error allowance must be at least
\[
\boxed{\epsilon\geq0.515\ \mathrm{m}\Omega.}
\]
This is an algebraic necessary condition, independent of how many relaxation modes are fitted. The input values come from the published real-part CSV. Zenodo
The narrow-bound experiment therefore applies only to 58 lower-frequency samples, from 0.01 through 904.7 Hz, with an assumed error budget of \(0.1\ \mathrm{m}\Omega\) for each real and imaginary component.
That restriction is a limitation. It does not establish that the real cell belongs to the model class, and it does not repair the rejected tighter full-band model.

6. How the continuum bounds were checked
A finite relaxation grid can miss an adversarial mode between grid points or beyond the chosen range. I therefore used the grid optimizer only to generate candidate inequalities.
For a lower-bound coefficient vector \(v\), the relevant condition is a pointwise inequality over the entire compactified interval:
\[
H_T(s)-v^\top F(s)\geq-\delta
\qquad
\text{for every }s\in[0,1].
\]
Integrating against the positive reweighted measure gives
\[
Q_T
\geq
v^\top y
-\epsilon\|v\|_1
-\delta M_{\max}.
\]
An analogous inequality supplies the upper bound.
The implementation converts the dual coefficients to rational numbers, enforces exact cancellation of series inductance, and checks the complete interval using interval arithmetic and adaptive derivative bounds. Endpoint formulas handle arbitrarily fast and slow modes. Scalar output bounds are then evaluated rationally.
This separates the roles of the computations:
Optimization proposes a bound; a separate continuum check establishes the stated inequality.
The checks passed, and the mathematical argument is explicit. They remain internal mathematical and software checks, not independent code review, formal proof-assistant verification or validation of the battery model itself.
7. The alternative tested: change the measurement interface
Once the reconstruction approach failed the novelty and capability tests, I investigated acquiring less information in the first place.
The alternative constructs a linear estimate
\[
\widehat Q_T=v^\top y
\]
from a small set of selected complex-frequency measurements. It seeks a uniform approximation to the target response kernel, rather than an identifiable relaxation distribution.
If the modewise approximation has relative error at most \(\eta<1\), positivity gives
\[
\frac{\widehat Q_T-\epsilon\|v\|_1}{1+\eta}
\leq Q_T\leq
\frac{\widehat Q_T+\epsilon\|v\|_1}{1-\eta}.
\]
I implemented three six-frequency protocols and checked their resulting inequalities over the full relaxation-time continuum. These sparse-protocol bounds do not secretly use an additional low-frequency mass measurement.

The tested faster sets lose useful decision precision.
There are three important qualifications. The time column is only a bookkeeping proxy for serial, single-cycle acquisition—not measured instrument time or a universal lower bound. The optimization used a declared response-scale parameter selected during this cell’s exploration, so it was not a blinded prospective design. And three tested sets do not establish an impossibility result for every possible protocol.
A real comparison must include conditioning, settling, repetitions, calibration and validation, and must confront multisine, joint-domain and direct pulse methods. Pulse-based relaxation analysis is already established, and joint-domain methods are an existing acquisition competitor. MDPI

8. The physical obstruction that mathematics alone did not remove
There is a stronger reason not to turn these linear certificates into operating guarantees.
Suppose all characterization currents satisfy \(|I|\leq a\). Add a memoryless voltage contribution
\[
V_{\mathrm{extra}}(I)
=
c\,\operatorname{sign}(I)(|I|-a)_+^3,
\qquad c\geq0.
\]
This contribution is exactly zero throughout the characterization range. It is monotone, and
\[
I\,V_{\mathrm{extra}}(I)\geq0,
\]
so it is passive.
Nevertheless, at an operating current \(I_p>a\), it adds
\[
c(I_p-a)^3
\]
to the voltage.
Therefore, identical bounded-amplitude characterization data and passivity alone cannot determine unrestricted higher-amplitude voltage response.
This is a constructive information counterexample, not a claim that the tested battery literally contains that element. Material-specific restrictions or nonlinear measurements can rule it out—but those restrictions and measurements have to be supplied.
The same separation applies to unknown initial relaxation, changing state of charge, temperature dependence and instrument calibration. A numerical certificate does not automatically bound these physical effects.
The unresolved scientific task is consequently not “solve the positive inverse problem faster.” It is:
Obtain enough physically justified information to constrain the operating response, at a lower total cost or with greater reliability than the best existing characterization procedure.

That task was not completed here.

Assessment for Astra
The useful research result is precise:
A measured positive normalization can support finite-time response certificates without selecting a microscopic relaxation spectrum or assuming a maximum relaxation time. Exact compatible countermodels can also expose when a point prediction is not scientifically identified.
But this session did not establish a fundamentally new operation beyond existing positive-moment methods, a validated nonlinear battery prediction, or superiority under matched scientific acquisition conditions.
The appropriate disposition is to retain the certificate and countermodel machinery as research tools, without promoting this battery pilot as a foundational advance. Further work on the same solver is not the decisive next step. Promotion would require an experimentally validated improvement in the information available for the actual operating decision, against the strongest relevant incumbent under a matched total resource budget.
