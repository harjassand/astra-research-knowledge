Scalar acquisition of a reusable nonreciprocal control-response map
Independent discovery packet, 2026-10-15

Disposition: conditional exact mathematical construction and synthetic checks.
Physical capability advantage, research priority, and mission achievement are
NOT ESTABLISHED. This operation was selected independently of the port-adjoint
framework. It changes the operator through actual controls and learns a reusable
scalar response map. It does not reconstruct an adjoint field.

1. Scientific opportunity and exact interface

The scientific task is to predict a fixed externally observed response under
many future combinations of local controls, through an otherwise unknown linear
network. This is relevant to programmable scattering, circuit response shaping,
and linearized transport. No benefit for an unknown nonlinear plant is claimed.

Assume the apparatus supplies, at a fixed frequency or in steady state,

    A(u) = A + P diag(u) P^T,
    f(u) = c^T A(u)^(-1) b,

where A is invertible, u has m components, and all queried operators are
invertible. The columns of P specify independently calibrated additive rank-one
actuator changes. b is the ordinary source and c specifies a detector. Physical
injection of c, internal field readout, reciprocity, known nonreciprocal support,
and physical reversal are unnecessary. Source and detector may coincide.

Indispensable supplied or separately acquired facts are linearity over the
operating range, fixed A,b,c during calibration and reuse, the additive rank-one
actuator laws and their independence, actual values of each applied u, stable
settling, and repeatable return to the reference configuration. The theorem
does not acquire or certify those premises from scalar responses.

Let

    H = P^T A^(-1) P,
    w = P^T A^(-1) b,
    v^T = c^T A^(-1) P,
    f0 = c^T A^(-1) b.

Woodbury gives, with D=diag(u),

    f(u) = f0 - v^T D (I + H D)^(-1) w.                 (1)

The formula also holds if some u_i vanish. For generic w_i != 0, put W=diag(w)
and use the diagonal gauge Hbar=W^(-1) H W and a_i=v_i w_i. Since W commutes
with every allowed D,

    f(u) = f0 - a^T D (I + Hbar D)^(-1) 1.              (2)

The estimator learns f0,a,Hbar from scalar queries. It does not learn the
original full A. Below, H denotes Hbar for economy.

2. Generic exact theorem

Fix two distinct positive values t1,t2 and another positive value t3. They must
be physically permissible and all proposed inverses must exist. For generic
real coefficients (f0,a,H), the following schedule reconstructs the complete
function (2), for every future u at which its inverse exists, using exactly

    Q(m) = (3 m^2 - m + 4)/2

scalar queries for m>=2. For m=1 it uses three queries. "Generic" means outside
a finite union of explicitly described algebraic exceptional sets. This is
not a uniform conditioning or noisy-data theorem.

The schedule is one baseline, two singleton settings per control, two settings
per pair of controls, m-2 anchored triple settings, and one triple setting for
each pair among controls 3,...,m. No configuration activates more than three
controls. The control hardware needs the reference and two distinct nonzero
values; a strictly binary actuator does not provide this schedule.

2.1 Singleton acquisitions

For one active control i at value t, let delta_i(t)=f0-f(t e_i). Then

    delta_i(t) = a_i t/(1+d_i t),  d_i=H_ii.

Two values identify d_i and a_i:

    d_i = [delta_i(t2)/t2 - delta_i(t1)/t1]
          /[delta_i(t1)-delta_i(t2)],
    a_i = delta_i(t1)(1+d_i t1)/t1.

We exclude a_i=0 and the vanishing denominators. These exclusions are essential.

2.2 Pair acquisitions

For active controls i,j with values s,t define x=H_ij, y=H_ji,
p=xy, q=a_i x+a_j y, and delta=f0-f(s e_i+t e_j). Direct inversion gives

    delta = [a_i s + a_j t
              +(a_i d_j+a_j d_i-q)st]
            /[1+d_i s+d_j t+(d_i d_j-p)st].             (3)

For each measured delta, all quantities in

    q - delta p = r,
    r = [a_i s+a_j t+(a_i d_j+a_j d_i)st
         -delta(1+d_i s)(1+d_j t)]/(st)

are now known except p,q. Two settings, in the implementation (s,t)=(t1,t1)
and (t2,t2), identify p,q provided their deltas differ. The two possible directed
entries are given by the roots of

    a_i x^2 - q x + a_j p = 0,
    y = (q-a_i x)/a_j.

The two possibilities are

    (x,y) and ((a_j/a_i)y, (a_i/a_j)x).                 (4)

The quadratic discriminant is exactly (a_i x-a_j y)^2. Near weighted
reciprocity, root selection is ill conditioned. Complex roots from noisy real
data are a model-consistency warning, not a valid new physical response.

2.3 Aligning pair choices with triples

Choose either solution for edge {1,2}. For each k>=3, retain that edge and
enumerate the four combinations of pair choices for {1,k} and {2,k}. Compare
the four predicted responses to one actual measurement with u_1=u_2=u_k=t3.
Under the generic separation condition, precisely one choice matches.

This establishes all edges incident to controls 1 or 2. For each 3<=i<j<=m,
enumerate the two choices for {i,j}, with {1,i},{1,j} already fixed. One actual
measurement with u_1=u_i=u_j=t3 selects the matching choice, again assuming
nonzero candidate separation.

To justify generic separation, all candidate responses are rational functions
of a,d and off-diagonal entries. Equality of two distinct candidates, after
clearing nonsingular denominators, is an algebraic condition. None of these
equalities is an identically zero polynomial: one witness, also checked with
exact rational arithmetic in the script, is

    a=(1,2,3), f0=10,
       [10   1/5    1/2 ]
    H= [3/10 10    11/10],   t3=1.
       [7/10 13/10 10   ]

Holding edge {1,2} fixed and independently switching the two other edge choices
produces the four distinct scalar responses

    12487600/1311109,
    37462120/3933429,
    37473360/3934847,
    12489080/1311287.

This witnesses every four-choice separation condition after relabelling. It
also witnesses the two-choice conditions. The other inversion denominators
and pair-root separations are nonzero on generic coefficients. A finite union
of proper algebraic exceptional sets has empty interior. Strictly dissipative
plants form an open family, so these generic conditions do not exclude all
physically dissipative plants.

2.4 What ambiguity survives, and why it does not change the allowed decisions

Let R=diag(a) and define

    Hstar = R^(-1) H^T R.

Transposing the scalar expression and using
(I+D H)^(-1)D=D(I+H D)^(-1) proves

    a^T D(I+Hstar D)^(-1)1 = a^T D(I+H D)^(-1)1.        (5)

If the initial edge {1,2} was chosen in the original orientation, uniqueness
of every subsequent triple selection reconstructs H. If it was chosen in the
other orientation, the same argument reconstructs Hstar. Both are compatible
with all acquired data, and (5) proves identical predictions under every
permitted diagonal control. Physical orientation of hidden edges is therefore
unnecessary for this fixed scalar prediction task. Coupled controls, a changed
source, or a changed detector can make the ambiguity relevant and are outside
this theorem.

The query count is

    1 + 2m + 2 C(m,2) + (m-2) + C(m-2,2)
      = (3m^2-m+4)/2.

The reconstruction performs O(m^2) constant-sized algebraic operations and
stores O(m^2) numbers. A dense prediction uses an m-dimensional linear solve,
ordinarily O(m^3) arithmetic. This does not solve global discrete optimization
over m controls in polynomial time.

3. Exact failure of uniform robustness for the fixed-anchor schedule

Let the two anchor nodes be disconnected, in H, from an interior three-node
block. Use the above H for that interior block in one system. In the other,
switch only its edge {1,3} according to (4). Keep a=(1,2,1,2,3) for the
five-control plant. Every queried configuration in the fixed-anchor schedule
contains at most two interior controls. By (3), every acquired scalar response
is therefore identical in the two systems. The unqueried interior triple gives
responses differing by exactly

    5135840960/5159013315323 = approximately 0.0009955083746.

Arbitrarily small generic perturbations of anchor-interior links can leave the
exceptional set while leaving that future difference fixed. Because this is
an open dense algebraic condition, such perturbations exist arbitrarily near
the disconnected example. The illustrative executed epsilon=1e-6 perturbation
differs on all calibration queries by at most 4.02e-9; that particular floating
point instance was not separately certified against every generic-condition
polynomial. Thus there is no uniform
noise-robustness bound for this fixed schedule on all generic plants. Choosing
better anchors or adding diverse joint configurations can fix this particular
example, but their cost and guarantees require separate analysis.

4. Stronger obstruction: all proper-subset experiments can be insufficient

For any even m>=4, let d=1+1/m, f0=2m, and let C_sigma be a directed cyclic
permutation matrix whose cycle product is sigma=+1 or -1. Exactly one edge
changes sign between the two cases. Define

    H_sigma=d I + C_sigma,
    w=v=e_1,
              [2m    e_1^T]
    G_sigma = [e_1   H_sigma],
    A_sigma=G_sigma^(-1).

Here G is the complete response matrix, including the external source/detector
coordinate. Both systems are strictly dissipative. Indeed sym(H_sigma)>=I/m.
The Schur complement of sym(H_sigma) in sym(G_sigma) is at least
2m-m=m>0. More quantitatively, Young's inequality yields
sym(G_sigma)>=I/(3m), and ||G_sigma||<=2m+3. Hence

    cond(A_sigma)=cond(G_sigma)<=3m(2m+3),

so no exponential matrix conditioning is required. Inverting preserves strict
positivity of the symmetric part: sym(A)=G^(-T) sym(G) G^(-1)>0. Adding any
nonnegative diagonal conductance keeps the physical admittance strictly
dissipative.

Let r_i=u_i/(1+d u_i). The directly observed one-port response is exactly

    f_sigma(u)=2m-r_1/[1+(-1)^(m-1) sigma product_i(r_i)]. (6)

If any u_i=0, then product_i(r_i)=0. Consequently the two systems agree under
EVERY configuration that changes fewer than all m controls, at arbitrary
nonnegative amplitudes. This is an exact statement, not a finite random test.

For u_i=m for all i, put r=m/(m+2). The response difference is

    2 r^(m+1)/(1-r^(2m)) -> 1/sinh(2) > 0.

It remains of constant size as m grows. At m=32 the difference is 0.276209.
More strongly, the two candidate actions u=m*1 and u=2m*1 are ranked oppositely
for minimizing the scalar response: at m=32 their response differences are
-0.142825 in the sigma=+1 plant and +0.046862 in the sigma=-1 plant.

This demonstrates genuine missing action information under low-order
calibration, even for a known additive rank-one actuator family and dissipative
operators. It does NOT prove exponentially many experiments are necessary:
one suitable full-support measurement distinguishes this pair. It does show
why a universal claim cannot follow from only singleton, pair, or triple
validation. The collective controls here have amplitudes O(m), total applied
conductance O(m^2), and nonzero switching/reset costs. These are charged inputs.

5. What was executed

check_scalar_acquisition.py used only its scalar oracle in the estimator.
Eight seeded dense strictly dissipative nonreciprocal plants were tested for
each m in {3,8,16,32} and each absolute additive response-noise standard
deviation in {0,1e-10,1e-8,1e-6,1e-4}. There are 100 independent held-out
control configurations per run. Failed real-root estimates were counted.

Noiseless median held-out response RMS errors were 3.90e-15, 4.12e-14,
4.12e-13, and 1.78e-12, respectively. At m=32, Q=1522. Absolute response noise
1e-4 yielded median response RMS error 0.547 and error 0.798 relative to the
control-induced response. At noise 1e-6 those figures were 0.00533 and 0.00802;
however 1137 pair estimates across the eight runs had nonreal candidate roots,
so these complex intermediate models are not qualified physical models.

The separate high-order script checked (6) against direct loaded-plant inverses
for m=4,6,10,16,32,64. Every sampled proper-subset difference was zero; the
largest direct-inverse/formula discrepancy was 8.53e-14. The all-subset equality
itself follows from (6), not from those sampled checks.

These are synthetic normalized examples. They contain no measured seconds,
joules, fabrication response, ADC tolerance, calibration lifetime or physical
demonstration. They establish neither a new hardware capability nor superior
performance over the strongest model-fitting comparator.

A separate executed three-level schedule, t1=1,t2=2,t3=1, is saved in
ternary_acquisition_results.json and check_ternary_acquisition.py. At m=32,
eight noiseless seeds again used 1522 queries, with median held-out RMS error
3.27e-12 and maximum 6.29e-11. Absolute response noise 1e-4 produced median RMS
error 0.684 and error 0.999 relative to the control-induced response. These
results show that the exact construction is executable on ternary controls;
they do not remove its noise failure. The earlier four-level results remain
preserved separately. A complete 94-query ternary m=8 synthetic measurement
receipt and both true/acquired normalized models are saved in
ternary_example_receipt.json. No binary-only acquisition theorem was proved.

6. Full-cost comparison and disposition

See COSTS_AND_COMPARATORS.txt for primary literature, matched interfaces and
cost accounting. The strongest prior physical route already learns a reusable
response model from external measurements. This construction is a possible
closed-form generic initialization or calibration schedule for that route.
Its mathematical contribution is potentially useful, but priority is not
established and the operation does not yet meet the civilization-scale mission.

The decisive next experiment, if this instrument niche is pursued, is a blind
scalar-response calibration of an actual many-control nonreciprocal apparatus,
with known load values and held-out collective configurations, compared at
equal source time, scalar reads, switching, precision and calibration reuse to
physics-compliant model fitting. A simulation cannot substitute for that gate.
