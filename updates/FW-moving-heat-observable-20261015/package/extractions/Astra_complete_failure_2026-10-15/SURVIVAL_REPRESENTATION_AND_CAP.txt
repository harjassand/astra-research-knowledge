Complete failure as a convex empirical-survival event
Original task-specific reconstruction, 15 October 2026. Ingredients include established common time changes, convex duality and exponential tilting. Historical novelty is not established.

1. Exact representation
Let an intact component i have rate lambda_i g(r), where r is the current number failed and g(r)=(1-r/n)^(-rho). Under the independent reference clocks E_i~Exp(lambda_i), let E_(0)=0 and E_(1)<=...<=E_(n). The next identity under both models is chosen with probability lambda_i/sum_alive lambda. Conditional holding times are coupled by division by g(r). Therefore the physical-model complete failure time has exactly the law
 tau = sum_(r=0..n-1) [(n-r)/n]^rho (E_(r+1)-E_(r))
     = integral_0^infty [s_E(t)]^rho dt,
 s_E(t)=n^-1 sum_i 1(E_i>t).
This is an exact simulator of the declared Markov model; it does not require weak heterogeneity. For rho=1 it is n^-1 sum E_i, a standard hypoexponential-sum problem. For integer rho=m, expansion gives n^-m times the sum of min(E_i1,...,E_im) over ordered m-tuples, including repeated indices.
For rho2, tau is a weighted ordered sum with positive weights (2(n-j)+1)/n². These weights sum to1, so tau is 1-Lipschitz in the sup norm of the lifetime vector, even when its order changes.

2. Global proposal cap
Fix T>0, rho>=1, theta>=0 and a nonnegative profile h with finite A=integral h^rho. Define psi_h(x)=rho integral_0^x h(t)^(rho-1)dt and
 q_i(x)=lambda_i exp[-lambda_i x-theta psi_h(x)]/Z_i.
Each Z_i is positive and at most1. Under the independent product Q, the likelihood from Q back to independent reference lifetimes is
 L(E)=product_i Z_i * exp[n theta rho integral h^(rho-1)s_E].
The deterministic time change then makes E_P0[L-related event] the desired interacting-model probability.
Young's inequality gives rho integral h^(rho-1)s_E <= (rho-1)A+integral s_E^rho. On event tau<=T,
 L(E)<=M=(product_i Z_i) exp[n theta((rho-1)A+T)].
Consequently W=1(tau<=T)L/M is in[0,1], and p=M E_Q W. This is valid for any profile; numerical optimization error affects efficiency, not this analytic inequality.
For rho2 the exact gap is
 log(L/M)=-n theta[(T-tau)+||s_E-h||_2²]
on the event. This exposes the missing efficiency issue: the proposal must put enough mass near the boundary and near the selected profile. A valid cap alone does not prove polynomial cost.

3. Convex profile optimization for rho2
Put u=theta h and absorb the profile scale. The tighter Holder cap has log objective
 F(u)=sum_i log Z_i(u)+2 n sqrt(T)||u||_2,
 Z_i(u)=integral_0^infty lambda_i exp[-lambda_i x-2 integral_0^x u]dx,
 u>=0.
Log partition functions of affine functionals are convex, as is the norm, so this is a convex optimization problem. Equivalent Young objective
 sum_i log Z_i(u)+n||u||²/theta+n theta T
is jointly convex for theta>0 by the quadratic perspective. A rational piecewise-constant u gives a finite-dimensional convex problem and piecewise-exponential proposal densities, with analytic positive segment masses and an exponential tail. No discretized probability law is silently substituted for a continuous one.
At a nonzero unconstrained positive-profile optimum, differentiation yields average_q survival = sqrt(T)u/||u||. Thus its squared L2 norm is T. This is a characterization, not a proof that an arbitrary numerical solver found the global optimum.
Existence sketch for the infinite-dimensional formulation: with lambda_min>0 and finite n, integrate Z_i over x in[0,(1+||u||)^(-2)]. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds integral_0^x u<=1, giving Z_i>=c_i/(1+||u||)^2 for constants c_i>0. Hence F is coercive because its linear norm term dominates logarithmic decay. Convex lower semicontinuity and weak compactness of bounded L2 balls give a minimizer in the closed nonnegative cone. When T<||average baseline survival||², a directional derivative along baseline survival at u=0 is negative, so the optimizer is nonzero. Full functional-analytic details and external review remain needed before presenting this sketch as a standalone theorem.

4. Why a common rate-scale comparator can be catastrophically misleading
Take q_i=Exp(c lambda_i), c>2. Its ordinary importance estimator for the same event has second moment
 integral_event product_i (lambda_i/c) exp[(c-2)lambda_i E_i] dE.
Fix 0<a<T and one coordinate j. Restrict all other E_i to[0,a], and E_j to[b,b+1], with b=n²(T-a)-1>a. Then tau<=a+E_j/n²<=T. This region yields the exact lower bound
 [ integral_b^(b+1) (lambda_j/c)exp((c-2)lambda_j x)dx ]
 * product_(i!=j) [integral_0^a (lambda_i/c)exp((c-2)lambda_i x)dx].
For rates uniformly bounded above and away from zero, its logarithm grows as (c-2)lambda_j n²(T-a)-O(n). Thus this particular common exponential tilt can have a super-exponentially large second moment, even if a finite run misses all slow-survivor configurations and reports a small empirical variance. This does not refute c<=2, mixture, permutation or state-dependent importance samplers. The survival-profile tilt preserves the original exponential tail slopes when its integral potential saturates, and its event likelihood cap avoids this specific failure.

5. Scientific boundary
The declared equal-load-sharing Markov law is the input, not an experimentally established universal material model. Stress-indexed independent Weibull-threshold models and local-load-sharing models are different contracts. No extrapolation from a mean stress–strain fit to an ultra-rare physical failure probability is justified. Sampling efficiency, parameter acquisition, model error and novelty are separate obligations.
