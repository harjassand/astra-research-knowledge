# Astra: compatible symmetry breaking and a local-storage obstruction

Research handoff, 15 October 2026.

**Capability:** Construct and certify a compatible three-Fourier-mode deformation that defeats an exact single-mode optimum. Rule out an entire precisely specified local-polynomial representation of the critical trace storage.

**Evidence:** Exact algebraic second variation, compatible finite strip, rational exponential remainder and finite-amplitude bounds. The improved certificate gives a strict action improvement greater than 1.8e-12 over the circular-family value. Run `python certify_improved_gap.py`; SymPy is required. `REPORT.md` contains the original more conservative certificate; the improved certificate changes its numerical parameters, not its mechanism.

**Second theorem:** Every constant-coefficient SO(2)-invariant quartic density with derivative weight three that induces a strongly continuous functional on mean-zero W^{3/4,4} has zero integral. The classification and three exact high-frequency tests appear in `local_polynomial_obstruction`.

**Closest established machinery:** Second-variation instability, optimal-control required supply, fractional trace extension, and heat-flow quasiconvexity certificates. This package does not establish historical novelty. The pinned Astra source and primary literature are linked in the main report.

**Missing operation:** A full-profile computable storage certificate with a controlled stopping error. The single-mode formula is not that operation; the new compatible path proves it cannot be extended unchanged.

**Plausible consequence:** Eliminates two attractive but inadequate representations and supplies a reusable compatible-mode test for future candidates. This is foundational mathematical progress, not a validated technological capability or a solution of the full quartic quasiconvexity problem.

**Decisive next test:** Construct a genuinely nonlocal, chirality-sensitive full-profile bound and test domination on arbitrary compatible strips, with explicit Fourier-tail costs. A finite-mode calculation without those controls remains insufficient.
