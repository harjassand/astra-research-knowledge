# Bounded primary-literature comparison

Research date: 15 October 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not a priority
clearance. Search snippets did not serve as mathematical premises.

1. **Giuseppe Bruno and Federico Pasqualotto, Quasiconvexity for the
   Dacorogna–Marcellini Energy, arXiv:2608.06367v1.**
   [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1).
   Read Theorem 2, the heat/exact-two-form discussion, and Appendix A's
   rank-one-convex cone and boundary decomposition. The theorem requires both
   left and right rotational invariance. Our four-term energies usually have
   only the left invariance: at beta!=0 their values on e11 and e12 differ.
   Appendix A already uses a sharp boundary family plus a nonnegative
   determinant-square remainder. Thus the general heat operation and that
   decomposition strategy are inherited. The source does not directly
   establish the present two-LMI formula, because its coefficient space and
   acoustic tests differ. This is a scope comparison, not an absence or
   priority claim.

2. **A. P. Calderón, A note on biquadratic forms, Linear Algebra and its
   Applications 7 (1973), 175–177.**
   [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379573900529).
   The publisher's abstract was available through search; direct full-text
   opening failed. The abstract states that nonnegative biquadratic forms
   with one factor of dimension two are sums of squares of bilinear forms.
   Consequently producing an acoustic SOS alone is not a new general
   principle. Our proof does not invoke Calderón's theorem: it supplies the
   exact square completion and separately proves positivity. Full-text
   comparison remains incomplete.

3. **G. Aubert, On a counterexample of a rank 1 convex function which is not
   polyconvex in the case N=2, Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh
   A 106 (1987), 237–240.**
   [Publisher record and abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-royal-society-of-edinburgh-section-a-mathematics/article/on-a-counterexample-of-a-rank-1-convex-function-which-is-not-polyconvex-in-the-case-n-2/88EF6A499E2D4941E1D1FC4BEDE2AAB0).
   Read the abstract, metadata, and references. These establish the preexisting
   planar rank-one-convex/non-polyconvex example tradition. No full-text
   coefficient comparison was acquired, so this source cannot clear priority
   of the present family.

4. **B. Dacorogna and P. Marcellini, A variational counterexample.**
   [Author-hosted paper](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~stefanelli/cv/paper5.pdf).
   Read the definitions and the displayed quartic norm–determinant example.
   It concerns the known isotropic family; it does not justify transferring a
   QC theorem to our anisotropic sharp generators. Non-polyconvexity via a
   negative quartic value and lifted variables is an established mechanism.

The search also checked combinations of left invariance, quartic,
rank-one convexity, and the source rational coefficient 35/24. No search
result is treated as proof that an equivalent theorem is absent. Historical
priority for the exact contact-face classification, the extremal family, or
the QC completion remains UNKNOWN.
