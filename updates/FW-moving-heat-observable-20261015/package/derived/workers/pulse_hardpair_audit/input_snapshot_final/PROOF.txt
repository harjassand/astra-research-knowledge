2. Transfer-function construction

Let a = 2*pi*0.01 per second, delta = a/40 = pi/2000 per second, and A = 125
mOhm. Let B(s) be the common positive RC baseline whose exact compact nodes,
positive masses, and positive series-inductance coefficient are retained in
verified_hard_pair.json. Define

  Z_+(s) = B(s) + 2A*delta/(s+delta),
  Z_-(s) = B(s) + 2A*delta/(s+2delta).

The extra term in Z_+ is a positive RC mode of resistance 2A and relaxation time
1/delta. The extra term in Z_- is a positive RC mode of resistance A and
relaxation time 1/(2delta). Both are finite positive RC models; no ideal slow
endpoint or resonant mode is used for the difference. Their transfer difference
is

  D(s) = Z_+(s)-Z_-(s)
       = 2A*delta^2 / ((s+delta)(s+2delta)).                    (1)

Let d(t) be the unit-step-response difference and g(t) its derivative. Direct
integration gives

  d(t) = A*(1-exp(-delta*t))^2,
  g(t) = 2A*delta*(exp(-delta*t)-exp(-2delta*t)) >= 0.         (2)

The first-order early response cancels exactly. In particular d(t) is of order
t^2 at the origin, even though the two positive RC responses are each of order
t. The absence of a signed impulse or a high-frequency discrepancy prevents an
arbitrarily fast switch from exposing this pair immediately.

The common baseline cancels for every input. For zero incremental initial
conditions and any bounded measurable current i, their voltage difference is

  Delta v(t) = integral_0^t g(t-u)*i(u) du.                  (3)

Their common inductance causes no difference, including for finite ramps.
For peak-only ideal switches, its shared switching impulse is excluded or
subtracted using the common known L; the observation here is the regular
incremental voltage. Finite-slew inputs starting from zero avoid that impulse.

3. Sharp waveform discrimination under peak and slew limits

First assume only |i(t)| <= Imax on [0,H]. Since g is nonnegative,

  |Delta v(t)| <= Imax*integral_0^t g(v)dv
               = Imax*d(t) <= Imax*d(H).                  (4)

The constant pulse i(t)=Imax attains (4) at H. Hence d(H) is the EXACT induced
L-infinity to L-infinity separation per unit maximum current for this pair on
[0,H]. This is exact optimality for this pair, not a claim that a constant pulse
is universally optimal for all possible RC identification problems.

Now impose a physically finite rise rate: i(0)=0, |i(t)| <= Imax, and
|i'(t)| <= Imax/rho almost everywhere, where rho>0 is the minimum full-scale
rise time. Any such input satisfies

  |i(t)| <= Imax*p_rho(t),  p_rho(t)=min(t/rho,1).

The nonnegative envelope i_*(t)=Imax*p_rho(t) is admissible and pointwise dominates
every admissible absolute input. Therefore it maximizes the absolute separation
at every time simultaneously. Integration by parts in (3) yields its output
separation at H:

  S(H,rho) = Imax*K(H,rho),
  K(H,rho) = (1/rho)*integral_max(0,H-rho)^H d(v)dv.         (5)

K is nonnegative and strictly increasing for H>0. The exact optimal waveform is
a maximum allowed ramp followed by a full-scale plateau. Formula (5) remains
valid when H<rho, when the waveform has not reached the plateau. The limit
rho=0 is K(H,0)=d(H).

A finite return ramp AFTER the last observation can be appended and charged
separately. The theorem's clock ends at the last observation; it does not count
subsequent reset time as zero. If a charge-balanced final input, a final zero
current at H, an energy bound, thermal constraints, or smaller admissible current
is required, the feasible input set is smaller. The lower bound survives; this
particular exact optimal waveform and its upper bound need not remain feasible.

4. Indistinguishability, including adaptive measurements

Fix a deterministic finite sequential strategy: each chosen input segment may
depend on all previous observed voltage values and on the already supplied EIS
row. Inputs satisfy the same peak/slew constraints. The observer may read the
whole resulting path, so the lower bound also covers any finite sampling plan.
For a continuous-time feedback formulation, require a well-posed causal closed
loop for every model in the convex segment between Z_- and Z_+, including the
midpoint. This excludes undefined instantaneous algebraic loops.

Run the strategy with the midpoint system Zbar=(Z_-+Z_+)/2 and zero measurement
error. Call its input i_bar and output v_bar. By linearity, the outputs v_+ and
v_- under that same input satisfy v_bar=(v_++v_-)/2. Now use the two possible
noise paths

  e_+(t)=v_bar(t)-v_+(t),  e_-(t)=v_bar(t)-v_-(t).

If Imax*K(H,rho) <= 2*epsilon_V, both paths satisfy the required noise bound,
and both generate the identical observed record v_bar. Thus the strategy chooses
the same inputs in both cases. For finite sequential strategies this is also
proved recursively segment by segment; the error at a time uses only the input
history already determined. The argument permits bounded systematic error and
does not assume an independent stochastic noise law.

The fixed EIS record is also identical input to the strategy. Both models obey
its component box exactly, so no extra information has been withheld within
this stated interface.

For a common observed record, any estimate qhat must be at distance at least
|Q_+(T)-Q_-(T)|/2 from one of the two true quantities. For the pair here,

  Q_+(T)-Q_-(T) = d(T).                                    (6)

This proves the claimed absolute-error and binary-classification lower bounds.
Randomization cannot give a uniform almost-sure guarantee; with an equal prior
on the indistinguishable endpoints its worst-case error probability is at least
one half. Independent stochastic noise, averaging assumptions, other sensors,
additional measured histories, or higher currents change the problem and must
be supplied and costed.

5. An exact minimax theorem for the entire hard segment

Suppose the investigator is told the family

  Z_theta = Z_- + theta*(Z_+-Z_-),  0 <= theta <= 1.          (7)

Every family member is positive RC and obeys the same EIS box by convexity. Its
target is Q_theta(T)=Q_-(T)+theta*d(T). With known common baseline/initial state,
the exact minimax worst-case absolute error over all peak/slew-admissible finite
sequential experiments and bounded voltage errors is

  R_*(H,rho,T) = d(T)*min(1/2, eta/K(H,rho)).                (8)

When K=0, interpret (8) as d(T)/2. For the lower bound, choose two parameters
theta=1/2 +/- min(1/2,eta/K). Their full-path separation is at most twice the
noise bound under every admissible strategy; the midpoint argument applies.
For the upper bound, use the ramp/plateau waveform, read its endpoint, subtract
the known Z_- output, divide by Imax*K, intersect the resulting feasible theta
interval with [0,1], and return that interval's midpoint. Its maximum radius is
min(1/2,eta/K), proving equality.

This is a SHARP minimax result for the explicit informed segment. It gives a
lower bound for the much larger actual EIS-feasible class. No claim is made that
the binomial family is a global extremizer over that larger class.

For the binary endpoint promise, certain discrimination is possible exactly
when K(H,rho)>2*eta. At equality the two endpoint observation intervals touch.

  Hcrit = -log(1-sqrt(2*eta/A))/delta,  0<2*eta<A.           (9)

6. General binomial family

For integer n>=1 and delta,A>0, put positive resistance A*binomial(n,k) at rate
k*delta in Z_+ for odd k and in Z_- for even k, k=1,...,n. A common positive
baseline can be added. Then

  d_n(t)=A*(1-exp(-delta*t))^n,
  g_n(t)=A*n*delta*exp(-delta*t)*(1-exp(-delta*t))^(n-1)>=0,
  Z_+(s)-Z_-(s)=A*n!*delta^n / product_(k=1)^n(s+k*delta). (10)

The binomial identity proves the step formula; its derivative proves the
impulse formula. The transfer identity follows by the beta integral, or partial
fractions. The same sharp waveform and segment-minimax results apply with d_n.
This makes the early difference order t^n while retaining two positive mixtures.

For the source normalization, choose delta=a*d with rational d>0. Each mode has
x=a*tau=1/(k*d), compact node s=1/(1+k*d), and normalized mass

  A*binomial(n,k)*w(1/(k*d)),
  w(x)=(1+x)/(1+x^2)-x/(1+10000*x^2)>0.                    (11)

Thus all normalized masses and EIS kernels are rational at rational measured
frequency ratios. This allows exact feasibility checking and includes the full
relaxation half-line without a time-constant cutoff.

8. Strongest-method confrontation and novelty status

Brown and Grabovsky, On feasibility of extrapolation of completely monotone
functions, arXiv:2401.15178v2 / DOI 10.1137/24M1652325, already derive a sharp
asymptotic power-law exponent for relative-L2 extrapolation of positive
exponential mixtures. Their global extrapolation theorem is materially deeper
than the finite-pair construction here. Their exact norm/class is different, so
their exponent is not silently asserted for our acquisition contract.

Casini, Garulli, and Vicino, On input design in l-infinity conditional set
membership identification (Automatica 42, 2006), already formulate optimal
bounded-input worst-case identification through radii of feasible system sets.
Worst-case input design and midpoint information arguments are established
ideas. Grabovsky's Stieltjes reconstruction and Lawrence's spectral duality
already cover positivity-based inverse uncertainty; this is not a novelty claim
for linear-functional bounds.

Huang et al.'s 2024 hybrid time/frequency method, binary multi-frequency EIS,
and closed-loop/Fisher-information designs are entitled to their strongest
versions. The lower bound above explicitly gives them all bounded current
waveforms and the complete voltage path, rather than comparing only NNLS or
three hand-selected frequencies. It is an information bound under deterministic
voltage error, not an empirical finding that those methods fail on real cells.
