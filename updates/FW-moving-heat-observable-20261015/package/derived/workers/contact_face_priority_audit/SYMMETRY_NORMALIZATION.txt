TRANSFORMED C4 SYMMETRY: RECEIVING-HYPOTHESIS REFINEMENT
15 October 2026. Exact algebra; historical priority UNKNOWN.

The sharp J_r is not invariant under the ordinary right quarter-turn in
the original contact coordinates. This fact alone does NOT dispose of
literature on C4-symmetric anisotropic energies: the negative branch has
a conjugated C4 action, made standard by an invertible diagonal stretch.

For r<-2, put k=-(2r+1)/3>1, h=sqrt(k),
A_r=[[0,-k],[1,0]], T_r=A_r/h=[[0,-h],[1/h,0]].
The exact discrete symmetry in GL_ORBIT_ADDENDUM.txt gives

 T_r^2=-I, det T_r=1, J_r(A T_r)=J_r(A).

Let D=diag(1/h,1), Q=[[0,-1],[1,0]]. Then
T_r=D^(-1) Q D. Consequently G_r(B)=J_r(B D) satisfies

 G_r(B Q)=G_r(B).

This is a genuine QC-preserving right change of variables on the global
matrix domain. It retains left-SO(2) invariance and yields ordinary right
C4 symmetry, but does not yield right-SO(2) invariance.

With u=|Be1|^2,v=|Be2|^2,j=det B, direct substitution gives

 G_r(B)=alpha_r [u^2+v^2+(2/k)uv-(4/sqrt(k))(u+v)j
                       +4(5k-1)/[k(3k+1)] j^2].

Equivalently, for t=1/sqrt(k) in (0,1), its normalized form is

 W_t(B)=u^2+v^2+2t^2 uv-4t(u+v)j
                      +4t^2(5-t^2)/(3+t^2) j^2.

The correspondence is r=-(3+t^2)/(2t^2), and G_r=alpha_r W_t.
This explicit W_t is the appropriate receiving family for any proposed
complete theorem about objective homogeneous planar C4 quartics. Merely
observing that J_r breaks the untransformed quarter-turn is insufficient.
It is also not the full material reflection group: the (u+v)j term changes
under a single right reflection. Any theorem using orthotropic, square,
tetragonal or D4 symmetry must specify whether determinant orientation
and reflections are included.

At t=1 the displayed normalized expression limits to H; at t=0 it limits
to u^2+v^2. For 0<t<1 it is nonpolyconvex, because the invertible right
transformation and positive scalar preserve polyconvexity and J_r is
nonpolyconvex. The parent's reported universal negative-branch QC
certificate, if independently validated, would also make each W_t QC.
That QC conclusion is not proved by this algebraic normalization.

For r>0, b=(2r+1)/3>0 instead gives the normalized involution
A_r/sqrt(b), whose square is I and determinant is -1. The sharp-to-sharp
orbit theorem still prevents this reflection case from identifying a
positive sharp parameter with a negative one.
