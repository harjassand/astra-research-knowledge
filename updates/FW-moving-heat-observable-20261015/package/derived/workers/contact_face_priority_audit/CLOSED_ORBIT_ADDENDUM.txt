CLOSED POSITIVE ISOTROPIC-ORBIT CONE EXCLUSION
15 October 2026. Independent structural addendum; historical priority UNKNOWN.

This strengthens the finite-sum exclusion in STRUCTURAL_PROOF.txt, Section 7.
The earlier frozen proof remains unchanged and its literal scope remains
finite sums. The present result explicitly closes the limit argument.

Let V and K be the nine-dimensional left-SO(2)-invariant homogeneous
quartic space and its ROC cone. Let G consist of the following elements:

 (i) left-SO(2) averages of Q(P A D), for arbitrary invertible real P,D
     and arbitrary globally ROC two-sided invariant quartics
     Q(A)=a0|A|^4+b0|A|^2 det A+c0(det A)^2;
 (ii) arbitrary left-SO(2)-invariant convex homogeneous quartics.

CLAIM. No nontrivial sharp ray J_r, r>0 or r<-2, belongs to the CLOSED
conic hull of G (closure in the finite-dimensional coefficient topology).
Consequently neither finite sums nor coefficient limits of this positive
isotropic-orbit-plus-convex route inherit QC for any sharp generator.

1. EXPLICIT LEFT AVERAGE AND A UNIFORM COEFFICIENT BOUND

For any P and B, write t=|P|^2 and d=det P. Averaging over R in SO(2)
with normalized Haar measure gives

 average |P R B|^2=(t/2)|B|^2,
 average |P R B|^4=k |B|^4-[(t^2-4d^2)/2](det B)^2,
 k=(3t^2-4d^2)/8>0 for nonzero P.

The second identity follows directly from the fourth moments of sine
and cosine (3/8,3/8,1/8, with odd mixed moments zero).

For a globally ROC source Q, a0>=0 and |b0|<=4a0. Indeed the e12
acoustic test at diag(x,y) is

 4a0(x^2+y^2)+2b0 x y >=0 for all x,y,

which is exactly this coefficient bound. In particular a0=0 forces b0=0.

Put m=|D|^2, T=D D^T/m, so T is positive definite with trace one,
and q_T(A)=tr(A^T A T). Each average E has the form

 E(A)=A0 q_T(A)^2+B0 q_T(A) j+C0 j^2,
 A0=a0 k m^2,
 B0=b0 d(det D)(t/2)m,

with an unneeded explicit real C0. The preceding source bound yields

 |B0|<=4 A0 sqrt(det T) <=2 A0.

For the first inequality, use
 |d| t/(2k)<=1,
 since 2k-|d|t=(t-2|d|)(3t+2|d|)/4>=0.
Here t>=2|d|. The second inequality uses trace T=1.
These estimates remain valid with either determinant orientation.

2. NO SINGLE-AVERAGE LIMIT CAN BE A SHARP RAY

Suppose a sequence of such averages E_n converges coefficientwise to
lambda J_r, lambda>0. Write each in the preceding form with trace T_n=1.
The positive-semidefinite trace-one matrices have a compact closure,
so pass to a subsequence T_n -> T>=0 with trace T=1.

On rank-one matrices, E_n(x tensor n)=A_n |x|^4(n^T T_n n)^2. In
particular

 E_n(e11)+E_n(e12)=A_n[(T_n)11^2+(T_n)22^2].

The bracket lies between 1/2 and 1. The left side converges to a strictly
positive finite number, because J_r's rank-one restriction is strictly
positive away from zero. Thus A_n is bounded and bounded away from zero.
Pass to A_n -> A>0. The limiting rank-one restriction is the positive
quadratic square A(n^T T n)^2, forcing beta_r=0 and hence r=1, by the
coefficient argument in STRUCTURAL_PROOF.txt, Section 7. Since it is
strictly positive, T is positive definite. At r=1 radiality forces T=I/2.

The bound |B_n|<=2A_n makes B_n bounded. Also
E_n(I)=A_n+B_n+C_n, so C_n is bounded. Passing to convergent subsequences,
the entire polynomial limit has the form

 A |A_matrix|^4/4+B |A_matrix|^2 j/2+C j^2,

which is two-sided invariant. But J_1 contains (u-v)j/2 and is not
right-SO(2)-invariant. Contradiction. Thus no allowed sharp ray is a
coefficient limit of single isotropic orbit averages.

The notation A_matrix in the last display denotes the matrix argument;
A,B,C in that display are the scalar subsequential coefficients.

3. COMPACT-BASE ARGUMENT EXCLUDES CLOSED POSITIVE COMBINATIONS

K is closed and pointed. For completeness, integrate the acoustic
polynomial P_F(A,eta,xi) over unit spheres in its three variables, obtaining
a linear functional L>=0 on K. If L(F)=0, nonnegativity and continuity
force every acoustic evaluation to be zero. In particular all four
coordinate second derivatives of F are zero. A homogeneous quartic
in four matrix entries that is affine in each separate entry must be
c a b c_entry d. Testing the rank-one direction e1 tensor (1,1) gives
2c c_entry d=0 for all backgrounds, so its coefficient is zero. Therefore
L(F)>0 for every nonzero F in K.

The base B_K={F in K:L(F)=1} is compact. Otherwise normalizing an
unbounded sequence by its coefficient norm would produce a nonzero
element of K with L=0. This also directly proves pointedness.

Normalize every nonzero member of G by L. Its closure Z is compact
inside B_K. The base of the closed conic hull of G is conv(Z), which
is compact and closed by finite-dimensional Caratheodory. Each normalized
J_r is an extreme point of B_K, by the independently proved extremality
in STRUCTURAL_PROOF.txt. If it belonged to conv(Z), extremality would
force every point in a finite representing convex combination to be
that same normalized J_r. In particular J_r/L(J_r) would belong to Z.

A converging sequence in the union defining G has a subsequence entirely
from one of its two classes. Limits from class (i) were excluded in
Section 2. The convex quartic cone in class (ii) is closed, whereas
every J_r is nonpolyconvex and hence nonconvex. Limits from class (ii)
are also impossible. Thus J_r/L(J_r) is not in Z, proving the claim.

An arbitrary convex quartic not initially left invariant causes no
additional issue: averaging it under left SO(2) remains convex and lies
in class (ii). Left averaging a putative convergent positive decomposition
is a continuous linear operation fixing J_r, so the same result applies.

SCOPE. This excludes the stated CLOSED POSITIVE CONE of globally ROC
two-sided invariant quartics under real invertible left/right changes,
together with convex quartics. It does not exclude nonlinear changes,
signed decompositions, nonpolynomial sources, or other analytic proofs.
It uses no receiving theorem that transposition preserves QC. For the
isotropic source Q specifically, Q(B^T)=Q(B), so P A^T D reduces to
D^T A P^T and is covered. No quasiconvexity certificate is verified here.
