# An exact positive-branch obstruction for constant input diffusion

Date: 2026-10-15. Independent continuation of the sharp-floor proof. The earlier proof files in the parent output directory are preserved unchanged. No other worker's positive-branch calculations were read.

For the positive sharp branch, at \(r=1\) the energy is

\[
J(A)=\frac18\big((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2\big)^2
+(ad-bc)^2+(a^2+c^2)(ad-bc).
\]

**Theorem.** There is no constant positive definite real input-diffusion matrix \(D\) and no \(C^2\) closed two-form \(\omega\) on matrix space for which

\[
\mathcal P_{D,\omega}(A,z)
=\sum_{i,j=1}^2D_{ij}D^2J(A)[B_i,B_j]
+\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\ge0
\]

for every real matrix \(A\) and every compatible second jet

\[
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}z_0&z_1\\z_3&z_4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_2=\begin{pmatrix}z_1&z_2\\z_4&z_5\end{pmatrix}.
\]

No global sum-of-squares assumption is made. At each fixed background, nonnegativity in the six jet variables alone forces the corresponding six-by-six matrix to be PSD. That necessary property is contradicted below.

This is an obstruction to the stated heat-certificate class. Quasiconvexity of \(J\) remains **UNKNOWN**. General constant output-matrix differential operators, including an output-skew mixed derivative, are **NOT COVERED** by this theorem.

## 1. Exact symmetry reduction and orientation parity

Let

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The energy is invariant under left proper rotations, \(A\mapsto SAS\), and \(A\mapsto AK\). The first two statements follow from the defining invariants. For the last, \(u\leftrightarrow v\), \(j\mapsto-j\), and the change of \(H/8\) is \((u+v)j\), exactly cancelling the change of \(uj\).

For any orthogonal input matrix \(R\) and orthogonal output matrix \(L\) giving a symmetry \(J(LAR)=J(A)\), the transformed jet satisfies

\[
A'=LAR,\qquad B_i'=\sum_k R_{ki}LB_kR.
\]

The transformed certificate is

\[
D' = RDR^T,\qquad
\omega'=(\det R)\Phi^*\omega,
\quad \Phi(A)=LAR.
\]

The factor \(\det R\) follows from alternating bilinearity in \((B_1,B_2)\). It is essential: an input reflection requires anti-invariance of the averaged two-form. Linear pullback preserves closedness, smoothness, and homogeneity. The transformations preserve universal pointwise nonnegativity and act linearly on the certificate pair.

Normalize \(\operatorname{tr}D=2\) by positive scaling. The finite group generated by the paired reflection \((L,R)=(S,S)\) and the input swap \((I,K)\) has the dihedral input action. Its average sends every symmetric \(D\) to \(I\): averaging the paired reflection removes the off-diagonal entries, and the input swap exchanges the two diagonal entries. The full group average makes both reductions simultaneous. Averaging further over left \(SO(2)\) preserves the finite symmetries because the paired reflection normalizes that rotation group.

Thus any certificate would yield one with \(D=I\), a left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant two-form, and

\[
\boxed{\omega=-\Phi_K^*\omega,\qquad\Phi_K(A)=AK.}
\]

The following calculation even drops the extra paired-reflection condition, enlarging the allowed two-form space. A contradiction in this larger space is sufficient.

## 2. Reduction of arbitrary smooth corrections to quadratic corrections

First suppose only that the two-form is \(C^2\). At \(A=0\), the heat term is zero. The jet quadratic form \(\omega_0(B_1,B_2)\) must therefore be nonnegative. It vanishes on every rank-one Hessian jet, because then \(B_1,B_2\) are proportional. Those jets span the six-dimensional jet space: use each target coordinate separately and input vectors \(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\). A PSD quadratic form vanishing on a spanning set is zero.

The map from a constant two-form to its compatible-jet quadratic form is injective. Its six component polynomials, in the component order \((ab,ac,ad,bc,bd,cd)\), are

\[
z_0z_2-z_1^2,\quad z_0z_4-z_1z_3,\quad
z_0z_5-z_1z_4,\quad z_1z_4-z_2z_3,\quad
z_1z_5-z_2z_4,\quad z_3z_5-z_4^2.
\]

They are linearly independent. Thus \(\omega_0=0\). Expand at the origin:

\[
\omega_{tA}=t\omega^{(1)}_A+t^2\omega^{(2)}_A+o(t^2).
\]

The heat term scales as \(t^2\). Nonnegativity for both signs of sufficiently small \(t\) forces \(\omega^{(1)}_A(B_1,B_2)=0\) for every \(A,z\); injectivity gives \(\omega^{(1)}=0\). Dividing by \(t^2\) and letting \(t\to0\) yields a feasible homogeneous quadratic correction \(\omega^{(2)}\). Differentiating closedness at the origin shows \(d\omega^{(2)}=0\). Its symmetries are inherited from the averaged form.

It therefore suffices to exclude homogeneous quadratic closed two-forms.

## 3. Exhaustive finite-dimensional correction space

Use row-major coordinates \(X=(a,b,c,d)\). Represent a two-form by a skew matrix \(\Omega(X)\), so \(\omega_A(B_1,B_2)=B_1^T\Omega(A)B_2\). Initially its six upper-triangular entries have ten quadratic coefficients each, hence 60 unknowns.

Impose:

1. \(d\omega=0\).
2. Left-rotation invariance. With \(V(X)=(-c,-d,a,b)\), this is the polynomial identity
   \(V\cdot\nabla\Omega+(DV)^T\Omega+\Omega DV=0\).
3. Input-swap anti-invariance \(\Omega(X)+P^T\Omega(PX)P=0\), where \(P\) swaps \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\).

Exact linear elimination gives rank 52, so the allowed space has dimension eight. `CERTIFICATE.json` displays an explicit basis \(\Omega_0,\ldots,\Omega_7\). The checker reconstructs all 60 unknowns and all constraints, then verifies that those eight displayed basis vectors span the complete kernel. Write

\[
\Omega=\sum_{i=0}^7t_i\Omega_i.
\]

## 4. Necessary contact constraints without a global Gram assumption

Let \(\mathcal P=\mathcal P_{I,\omega}\), and write \(\mathcal P(A,z)=z^TM(A)z\). At

\[
A_0=I,\qquad z_*=(0,0,1,0,0,0),
\]

the acoustic contact gives \(\mathcal P(A_0,z_*)=0\). Nonnegativity forces \(M(I)z_*=0\).

There is a higher-order contact curve

\[
A_\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\tau\\2\tau&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad z_\tau=(\tau^2,\tau,1,0,0,0).
\]

Its derivative matrices are proportional, so every alternating correction vanishes on it. Direct differentiation gives

\[
\mathcal P(A_\tau,z_\tau)=8\tau^4(1+\tau^2).
\]

The full polynomial \(\mathcal P\) in the ten variables \((A,z)\) is nonnegative and vanishes at \((A_0,z_*)\). Its ordinary Hessian there is PSD. The curve has tangent

\[
w=(0,-2,2,0,\;0,1,0,0,0,0).
\]

Because the curve's second derivative of \(\mathcal P\) is zero, \(w\) has zero Hessian quadratic value and hence lies in that Hessian's kernel. Thus
\(D^2_{(A,z)}\mathcal P(A_0,z_*)w=0\).
This step uses only local nonnegativity, not an ambient bilinear sum-of-squares representation.

The two kernel conditions give five independent affine linear equations. Their exact general solution is

\[
\boxed{(t_0,\ldots,t_7)=
(t_0,\;4t_7+6,\;0,\;3t_7+6,\;4t_7+6,\;t_5,\;0,\;t_7).}
\]

## 5. Contradiction from exact principal minors

The principal block of \(M(I)\) on jet indices \((0,4)\) is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}8&-8(t_7+1)\\-8(t_7+1)&-8(t_7+1)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is \(-64(t_7+1)(t_7+2)\). Positivity forces

\[
-2\le t_7\le-1.
\]

Now set \(A_v=\operatorname{diag}(1,v)\). Let \(p(v)\) be the determinant of the principal block of \(M(A_v)\) on jet indices \((0,2,4)\). This determinant is independent of \(t_0,t_5\). The complete polynomial is in `CERTIFICATE.json`; the two identities needed here are

\[
\boxed{p(-1)=0,\qquad p'(-1)=32t_7(t_7+1)(t_7+2).}
\]

Since every principal minor of a PSD matrix is nonnegative, \(p(v)\ge0\) for all real \(v\). A differentiable nonnegative function vanishing at \(-1\) must have derivative zero there. Combined with \(t_7\in[-2,-1]\), this leaves only \(t_7=-2\) or \(t_7=-1\).

Both fail at the single exact background \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\):

\[
p(0)\big|_{t_7=-2}=-7,
\qquad p(0)\big|_{t_7=-1}=-9/4.
\]

The contradiction exhausts the entire allowed correction space. It is neither a failed optimizer run nor a failure of one chosen Gram support.
