SECONDARY MODEL-CLASS GATE: FINITE EXACT EIS DOES NOT BOUND GENERAL PASSIVE RESPONSE
Classical Foster synthesis consequence; no historical novelty claimed.

This is separate from the frozen positiveRC hard-pair theorem. It explains why
replacing rejected positiveRC assumptions by unrestricted passivity alone does
not rescue finite-time response guarantees.

Let 0<w1<...<wm be any finite collection of measured angular frequencies.
Choose interlacing resonances

  0<O0<w1<O1<w2<...<w_m<O_m.

The real rational impedance

  F(s) = s*product_(j=1)^m(s^2+w_j^2)
           / product_(k=0)^m(s^2+O_k^2)

has a partial-fraction representation

  F(s) = sum_(k=0)^m A_k*s/(s^2+O_k^2),
  A_k = product_j(w_j^2-O_k^2)
           / product_(l != k)(O_l^2-O_k^2) > 0.

For each k, numerator and denominator have exactly k negative factors, so all
A_k are strictly positive. The branch A_k*s/(s^2+O_k^2) is the impedance of a
parallel LC network with C_k=1/A_k and L_k=A_k/O_k^2. Their series connection is
passive, and F(i*w_j)=0 exactly at every measured frequency. No cancellation of
positive resistance by an active element is involved; these are lossless
reactive networks. The unit-step response is

  q_F(T)=sum_k A_k*sin(O_k*T)/O_k.

For every fixed T>0 one can choose the interlacing poles so q_F(T) != 0. To see
this, fix O1,...,Om and vary O0=z over (0,w1). Write

  N(z^2)=product_j(w_j^2-z^2),
  P(z^2)=product_(k=1)^m(O_k^2-z^2).

Then A0=N/P, and multiplying q_F(T) by P(z^2) gives

  N(z^2)*sin(z*T)/z + R(z^2),

where R is a polynomial, since each other residue has only one z-dependent
denominator factor. This even entire function cannot vanish identically: at
z=i*y, the first term grows like a nonzero polynomial times sinh(y*T)/y,
whereas R(-y^2) has only polynomial growth. By analytic uniqueness it cannot
vanish throughout the real interval (0,w1).

For any passive baseline Z0, each Z0+c*F with c>0 is passive and gives EXACTLY
the same finite EIS samples as Z0, yet its response at the selected time differs
by c*q_F(T). Thus finite sample values and unrestricted passivity alone yield no
finite two-sided bound on the ideal-step response. This statement is conditional
on a passive baseline being compatible with the data; no full-band compatible
baseline for the battery row is asserted here.

The escape also works for a finite ramp, so it is not only a switching impulse
artifact. For a unit ramp of rise time rho>0 followed by a plateau, the voltage
at T is

  y_F(T) = (1/rho)*integral_max(0,T-rho)^T q_F(v)dv
         = sum_k A_k *
           [cos(O_k*max(0,T-rho))-cos(O_k*T)]/(rho*O_k^2).

Multiplying by P(z^2) again gives a nonzero entire expression: the z-dependent
term contains cos(z*max(0,T-rho))-cos(z*T), whose exponential growth at i*y
dominates every polynomial because max(0,T-rho)<T. Hence some interlacing pole
choice has y_F(T) != 0; scaling c is again unbounded.

Finally, strictly damped passive RLC systems give the same obstruction with any
strict observation-error margin. Replace each branch by

  A_k*s/(s^2+2*gamma*s+O_k^2),  gamma>0.

It is a parallel RLC network with C_k=1/A_k, L_k=A_k/O_k^2, and R_k=A_k/(2gamma).
At each measured nonpole frequency its difference from the lossless branch is
O(gamma), while its finite-time response tends continuously to the lossless
response as gamma tends to zero. Scale by c and choose gamma=c^(-2). Its EIS
perturbation tends to zero as O(1/c), but its selected finite-time output grows
like c*y_F(T). An interior feasible baseline therefore stays within any fixed
positive observation box for all sufficiently large c. The same statement does
not follow for an exactly saturated error box without an interior margin.

This is not evidence that the cell has these resonances, nor a physical
manufacturability or energy-budget claim. It identifies the missing restrictions:
an independently justified RC/Stieltjes class, a bounded reactive spectral mass,
bounded element/Q/energy parameters, bandwidth controls, direct time-domain
measurements, or other material information must enter explicitly.
