Closed-span ZnS/GaP dislocation transport: physical-control audit
15 October 2026

Decision: UNACQUIRED_SAME_LOOP_RESPONSE. Preserve as a specific
laboratory lead. No physical invention, defect-removal capability,
priority, device benefit, or acquisition-inclusive resource advantage
is established by this work.

The incoming model is mathematically coherent under its stated diagonal,
translation-invariant, reversible-branch assumptions. Its necessary
material interface is not supplied by the cited experiments. The more
consequential refinement is that the nominally neutral full screw arm
contains charged partials. This changes the control design: a generic
365 nm light brake cannot be assumed to select only the mixed arm.

Primary evidence and coupled regime

S1. Montenegro et al., Phys. Rev. Materials 10, 025003 (2026),
DOI 10.1103/c2hc-lyp2. Public author text/appendices:
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07908v2
Publication metadata/abstract:
https://journals.aps.org/prmaterials/abstract/10.1103/c2hc-lyp2

The systematic experiment uses undoped 20 nm ZnS/GaP(001), 350 C
annealing and 365 nm illumination at 0.07--0.67 mW/cm2. ECCI follows
cooling to room temperature (30 kV, 2.4 nA). 148 MDs are quantified;
the longest changes are used as glide indicators because pinning is
prevalent. Length changes are normalized by inferred strain, which
changes during relaxation. The appendix includes larger dark/light
family contrasts on 20/25 nm films. Neither acquisition resolves the
required pair of full-arm outward/inward response branches on one loop.
Al doping is established during growth, not reversible same-loop
electrical actuation. The public author appendices were read; the full
journal-version text was not acquired.

S2. Li et al., Harnessing dislocation motion using an electric field,
Nature Materials 22, 958--963 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41563-023-01572-7.
Author PDF with extended data and SI:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14933v2

This is strong prior for electrically directed, reversible and retained
ZnS partial-dislocation relocation. In FIB-thinned commercial ZnS,
30-degree partials reverse with field polarity; 90-degree partials stay
still. Reported specimen-dependent thresholds are about 2.4e7 and
5.8e6 V/m. Motion persists with the electron beam off, and zero bias
retains displaced positions over the video interval. SI Table 2's
force conversion assumes a line charge of 1.3 electrons/nm; it does
not measure the new film's full-loop charge. TEM geometry, pinning,
partial dissociation and field distribution differ from S1. This prior
supplies a real signed-force candidate and mandatory comparator, not
closed-shape full-half-loop motion or lifetime-qualified retention.

S3. Blackston et al., Photoluminescence excitation spectroscopy of
quantum wire-like dislocation states in ZnS (author preprint, 2025):
https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05357v1

Its geometry states that mixed Zn60 TDs split into Zn30+Zn90, while
the neutral screw TD splits into Zn30+S30. Both arms therefore share a
Zn30 component. Room-temperature absorption peaks assigned to Zn90,
S30 and Zn30 are around 2.24, 2.60 and 2.75/2.81 eV, respectively;
assignment uses spectral/density correlation. PLE is on a 50 nm film
at 5 K, scanning 3.6--3.9 eV; it is not a mobility action spectrum at
350 C. GaP emission contaminates 2.1--2.4 eV and is fitted out. The
source supports a concrete spectral-control lead, not arm-selective
charge preparation or glide.

S4. Genlik et al., Origin of photoplasticity in ZnS, Phys. Rev.
Materials 9, 054604 (2025), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevMaterials.9.054604.
Read author v1 including its supporting-information section:
https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13044v1
Current publisher abstract/metadata were checked. The accepted-final
manuscript link did not download (404), so v1 numerical barriers are
not represented as a re-audit of the final publication.

The charge-state DFT model predicts reconstruction and increased
barriers for Zn-rich 30/90 partials. Unit-line Peierls barriers are not
the finite-segment activation free energies or measured endpoint
mobilities needed here. A separate primary calculation, Shen et al.,
J. Am. Ceram. Soc. 108 e20249 (2025), instead obtains strong S30
hole effects in an unreconstructed e-h-excited model:
https://ceramics.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jace.20249
This model dependence strengthens the need for same-loop trajectories;
it does not by itself falsify S1's experimental family response.

S5. Vurgaftman, Meyer and Ram-Mohan, J. Appl. Phys. 89, 5815 (2001),
DOI 10.1063/1.1368156, Eq 2.13 and Table IV. Paper copy inspected:
https://ursemiconductors.web.illinois.edu/ECE536/Vurgaftman%282001%29.pdf

Its GaP X-gap parameters (Eg0=2.35 eV, alpha=0.5771 meV/K,
beta=372 K) give Eg(623.15 K)=2.1248107 eV. This calculation is a
substrate-absorption warning, not a ZnS/GaP band-offset or injection
model. A 2.24 eV selective optical pulse is above that gap at the
published glide temperature. Carrier creation in GaP is therefore
energetically allowed and cannot be ignored in a selectivity claim.

What mechanism could supply the missing control?

Candidate A: partial-selective spectral brake. The mixed arm contains
Zn90, whereas the screw arm does not. Instead of broadly exciting the
shared Zn30 component, tune narrowband light near the Zn90 feature,
starting around the room-temperature 2.24 eV feature (553.5 nm), and
measure the mobility action spectrum at the actual operating temperature.
If localized Zn90 charging slows the mixed arm while the complete screw
arm remains mobile, it produces the required relative full-arm response.
This is an experimentally addressable control proposal, not a measured
mechanism. It also requires a reversible mechanical return branch.

The coupled-regime obstacles are specific. Thermal shifts, line/core
reconstruction, partial coupling and substrate-generated carriers can
erase selectivity. S3 did not excite at 2.24 eV in its PLE scan. Its
room-temperature absorption and 5 K emission do not establish an action
window at 350 C. A spectral peak in a material ensemble also does not
establish adequate trapped occupation on one moving arm. A bare GaP
control and measurement of carrier transfer/charge settling are needed.
Changing to a freestanding ZnS foil or blocking interface would change
the platform and its forces, strain and baseline; no result transfers
automatically. No favorable cross-section, lifetime or switching ratio
has been invented.

Candidate B: direct electric drive plus shape-force feedback. S2 already
supplies signed reversible partial motion. Acquire the additional full-arm
responses g_C(E),g_N(E), then use mechanical shape-force control to keep
dot{ell}=0. In the diagonal reduction,

  F=(m_C g_C-m_N g_N)/(m_C+m_N),
  dot{c}=m_C m_N(g_C+g_N)/(m_C+m_N).

Unlike mobility switching, this uses a net translational drive. It is a
concrete route to test without needing an optical mobility-ratio change.
Its decisive obstacle remains whole-arm mobility and stable partial
spacing. Opposite field forces on Zn30/S30 can change a stacking-fault
ribbon rather than translate the full screw arm. The 90-degree arrest
observed in S2 is particularly relevant to moving the mixed full arm.
Field screening, leakage, piezoelectric stress and GaP conduction must
be acquired on the heated epitaxial specimen. S2 thresholds cannot be
assigned to it. No direct-field full-loop servo has been demonstrated.

Exact mathematical and accounting improvements

THEOREMS.txt gives the derivations and calculate.py replays rational
identities and dimensionless countermodels. The basic drift formula and
sharp mobility-matching bound survive. Three stronger boundaries follow:

* The four outward/inward optical branches must be measured. With
  reproducible branch maps, d_DL+d_LD=d_DD+d_LL. Phase swapping gives
  exact reversal only when the fixed-state branch drifts sum to zero.
* Fixed-light cycles are not universally null: stress-dependent mobility
  ratios can pump. A dimensionless odd activated-glide countermodel
  gives d/a=-0.28504597 at one optical state with different force
  magnitudes. No ZnS parameters were assigned.
* For constant mobilities and available moving time T, minimum glide
  dissipation for an excursion a is
  a^2(1/sqrt(S_D)+1/sqrt(S_L))^2/T. Increasing contrast can approach
  unit drift per excursion while useful rate tends to zero at the
  displacement-optimal matching point. Switching/heating dead time
  changes the throughput optimum.

The published intensity converts to 0.7--6.7 W/m2; 365 nm photons carry
3.3968 eV. Dwell, illuminated area and wall-plug efficiency remain
unacquired here. No cycle energy or device-area cost follows from those
intensities alone. The returned shape does not remove glide dissipation,
charge-state preparation, external actuation, heating/cooling, metrology,
preparation, failures, guard capacity or requalification costs.

Earliest decisive experiment and rejection rule

Use a same-loop endpoint/partial map on the published 20 nm material,
adding calibrated reversible substrate loading. The first acquisition
is not a defect-clearing machine. Measure both full arms at matched
span/shape and load, under each optical state in both glide directions;
include every pinned or damaged loop in the denominator. Record signed
motion and partial separation, rather than only misfit-segment length.
Either establish a safe contraction branch or reject this platform
before optimizing optical sequences. Critical thickness is not a
measured reverse-glide loading window.

For the spectral route, acquire the response difference versus photon
energy at the SAME glide temperature, with bare-substrate, off-resonance,
equal-dose and matched-temperature controls. For the direct-field route,
acquire polarity-reversed field responses and ribbon/shape closure,
including an E=0 control and a measured force/strain map. Operate with
the imaging beam off where possible; account for all motion during
heating, cooling, switching and imaging interruptions. A feedback claim
requires actual feedback or a separately verified open-loop hold.

Then test D-out/L-in and L-out/D-in alongside D/D and L/L at the same
force/temperature histories. Check the predicted integral using separate
acquisition data and substrate fiducials, full shape, partial-width and
relevant strain closure. Compare to optimized stress-only, best constant
light, direct-field, thermal and spatial-stress protocols that meet the
same output constraints. A noise pilot must set sample size and a useful
drift threshold; no arbitrary n is assumed.

Reject the candidate in that admissible window if either full arm fails
to glide reversibly, return strokes cause cracks/multiplication/twinning
or persistent ribbon change, independently acquired response bounds rule
out useful drift, or matched controls explain the retained displacement.
If a simpler electric-field or mechanical protocol achieves the same
accepted output at equal or lower full cost, retain the scientific
observation but reject the proposed optical advantage.

Selective export outside an active region additionally needs a validated
destination/sink, retained defect counts, strain accommodation, surface
quality and electrical/optical device function. Whole-loop translation
does not annihilate the loop. Neither a few partial trajectories nor any
model replay establishes those endpoints.

The next information that changes the decision is a paired full-arm
response matrix with a safe return branch, or a demonstrated direct-drive
shape servo. Another optimization with guessed mobilities does not.
