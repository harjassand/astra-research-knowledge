1. The capability obtained
Consider the genuinely infinite-spectrum process
\[
X(t)=\mu+Z_0+
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
\bigl(A_j\cos jt+B_j\sin jt\bigr),
\qquad 0\le t<2\pi,
\]
with independent Gaussian blocks of the stationary form used in the original compiler. Assume a certified global envelope
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}\!\begin{pmatrix}A_j\\B_j\end{pmatrix}
\preceq a^2\,4^{-j}I
\qquad(j\ge1),
}
\tag{1}
\]
and positive-definite constant covariance \(S_0\). The required finite prefix of rational covariance blocks must be computable in polynomial time and polynomial bit length from the model description.
Let
\[
K=\{x:a_i^\top x>b_i,\ i=1,\ldots,m\}
\]
be a nonempty, full-dimensional rational polyhedron, and define
\[
p_\infty=\Pr[\exists t:X(t)\in K].
\]
Conditional algorithmic consequence
For \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/4\), there is an explicitly computable cutoff \(D\), polynomial in the model’s bit length and \(\log\varepsilon^{-1}\), such that
\[
\boxed{
\left|\frac{p_\infty}{p_D}-1\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{8},
}
\tag{2}
\]
where \(p_D\) is the crossing probability of the degree-\(D\) truncation.
Consequently, assuming the prior finite-spectrum compiler, the infinite-model probability can be approximated relatively in
\[
\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1},\log\delta^{-1})
\]
bit operations. The same reduction transfers failure-conditioned waveform sampling with explicitly charged approximation and output errors.
The crucial distinction is that the algorithm never counts the crossings of the infinite series. It counts crossings only in a finite approximation, then proves that both the probability and the conditioning operation survive the approximation.
The global envelope in (1) is not learned for free. A concrete model family where it follows from finite, checkable matrix inequalities appears in Section 5.
2. The operation: contraction toward an interior trajectory
The main obstacle in model approximation is a trajectory that nearly touches a failure boundary. Small perturbations can create or remove a crossing, and ordinary mean-square error does not quantify the resulting relative change in a rare probability.
The following operation controls all such trajectories collectively. It does not require a minimum crossing angle, crossing separation, or boundary-density estimate.
Gaussian contraction lemma
Let \(\gamma_n\) be standard Gaussian measure, \(0<\theta<1\), and
\[
T_\theta(z)=(1-\theta)z+\theta h.
\]
For every measurable set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_n(T_\theta A)
\ge e^{-\beta}\gamma_n(A),
\qquad
\beta=
-n\log(1-\theta)
+\frac{\theta\|h\|^2}{2(2-\theta)}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, \(A\) need not be convex. This matters because the coefficient-space event “the trajectory enters \(K\) at some time” is generally a union of sets, not a convex set.
Proof
Put \(c=1-\theta\). The Gaussian density, including the Jacobian of the transformation, satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{c^n\phi_n(cz+\theta h)}{\phi_n(z)}
={}&n\log c\\
&+\frac{\theta(2-\theta)}2
\left\|z-\frac{c}{2-\theta}h\right\|^2
-\frac{\theta\|h\|^2}{2(2-\theta)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Discard the nonnegative square and integrate over \(A\). This proves (3).
The constant is optimal among all measurable sets: shrinking balls about \(ch/(2-\theta)\) approach equality. ∎
Apply it to a failure event
Write a finite Gaussian trajectory as
\[
X_z(t)=\mu+B(t)z,\qquad z\sim N(0,I_n).
\]
Suppose an available coefficient vector \(h\) generates a trajectory uniformly inside the failure region:
\[
a_i^\top X_h(t)-b_i
\ge r\|a_i\|_1
\qquad\text{for every }i,t,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(r>0\).
Define the expanded and contracted regions
\[
K_\eta^\pm
=
\{x:a_i^\top x>b_i\mp\eta\|a_i\|_1\}.
\]
With \(\theta=\eta/r<1\), coefficient contraction gives
\[
X_{T_\theta z}(t)
=(1-\theta)X_z(t)+\theta X_h(t).
\]
Therefore it maps the hitting event for \(K_\eta^+\) into the hitting event for \(K\), and the event for \(K\) into that for \(K_\eta^-\). If their probabilities are \(p_+,p,p_-\), then
\[
\boxed{
p_-\ge e^{-\beta}p,
\qquad
p_+\le e^\beta p.
}
\tag{5}
\]
A nondegenerate constant Gaussian mode supplies an explicit \(h\): choose an interior point \(x_*\) and shift the constant coefficient so that the entire trajectory equals \(x_*\). Its squared Gaussian-coordinate norm is
\[
\boxed{
\|h\|^2=(x_*-\mu)^\top S_0^{-1}(x_*-\mu).
}
\tag{6}
\]
This quantity is computable directly from rational inputs. No conditioned trajectory or unknown committor is assumed.
3. A relative-risk certificate for arbitrary coupled model error
The preceding bound is useful beyond Gaussian spectral truncation.
Let \(Y\) be any continuous process coupled with the finite Gaussian process \(X\), possibly a non-Gaussian physical model or numerical approximation, and suppose
\[
\Pr\!\left[
\sup_t\|Y(t)-X(t)\|_\infty>\eta
\right]\le q.
\tag{7}
\]
On the complementary event,
\[
\{X\text{ hits }K_\eta^-\}
\subseteq
\{Y\text{ hits }K\}
\subseteq
\{X\text{ hits }K_\eta^+\}.
\]
Combining this with (5) proves:
Relative model-transfer theorem
\[
\boxed{
e^{-\beta}p-q
\le
\Pr[Y\text{ hits }K]
\le
e^\beta p+q.
}
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover, under the supplied coupling,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left[
\mathbf1_{\{X\text{ hits }K\}}
\ne
\mathbf1_{\{Y\text{ hits }K\}}
\right]
\le
(e^\beta-e^{-\beta})p+q.
}
\tag{9}
\]
This separates two genuinely different errors.
The ordinary trajectory tolerance \(\eta\) incurs the multiplicative geometric cost \(e^\beta\). It does not have to be as small as \(\varepsilon p\).
The probability \(q\) of violating the trajectory tolerance must be controlled relative to \(p\). The theorem does not make rare unmodelled excursions harmless.
Equation (9) also controls conditioning. For events \(E,F\) on a common probability space,
\[
\operatorname{TV}\bigl(P(\cdot\mid E),P(\cdot\mid F)\bigr)
\le
\frac{P(E\triangle F)}{\min(P(E),P(F))}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the same certificate transfers failure-conditioned laws, rather than merely their normalizing probabilities.
No path-law absolute continuity is required. In particular, a finite Fourier approximation and an infinite Gaussian process may be singular as probability measures on complete paths; that does not invalidate this coupling argument.
4. An explicit cutoff with no inverse-probability runtime
The remaining challenge is to obtain (7) at the necessary confidence without measuring an event of probability comparable to \(p\).
For the certified Gaussian envelope (1), this is possible analytically.
A tail certificate
A Gaussian tail bound, followed by a union bound over frequencies and coordinates, gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left[
\|X-X_D\|_{\sup,\infty}
>
6a\,2^{-D}\sqrt{\log(4d/q)}
\right]\le q,
}
\tag{11}
\]
where \(d\) is the number of observed coordinates.
For completeness, at frequency \(j=D+k\), bound each sine and cosine coefficient by
\[
a\,2^{-(D+k)}
\sqrt{2\log(2^{k+2}d/q)}.
\]
The probability that any bound fails is at most \(q\). Sum the resulting waveform bounds and use
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}\sqrt{k}\le\sqrt2.
\]
This proves (11), without a time grid or a simulated tail event.
A lower probability bound acquired from one time point
Choose a rational interior point \(x_*\) and rational \(r>0\) satisfying
\[
a_i^\top x_*-b_i\ge r\|a_i\|_1.
\]
Let rational covariance bounds satisfy
\[
\lambda_-I\preceq S_0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X_D(0))\preceq\lambda_+I
\quad\text{for all }D.
\]
The envelope permits
\[
\lambda_+\ge\|S_0\|_{\mathrm{op}}+a^2/3.
\]
Put
\[
M_0=\|x_*-\mu\|_1+dr
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
F_0=
1+\frac{M_0^2}{2\lambda_-}
+d\left(2+\lambda_++\frac1r\right).
}
\tag{12}
\]
Integrating the Gaussian density of \(X_D(0)\) over the cube of half-side \(r/2\) about \(x_*\) proves
\[
\boxed{p_D\ge e^{-F_0}\quad\text{for every }D.}
\tag{13}
\]
This is deliberately conservative. Its important computational feature is that \(F_0\) has polynomial bit length even when its numerical value is enormous.
The cutoff calculation
Define
\[
H_0=(x_*-\mu)^\top S_0^{-1}(x_*-\mu),
\qquad
n_D=d(2D+1),
\]
\[
\theta_D=
\frac{\varepsilon}{64(1+n_D+H_0)},
\qquad
\eta_D=r\theta_D,
\qquad
q=\frac{\varepsilon}{64}e^{-F_0}.
\]
Choose \(D\ge1\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
36a^2\,4^{-D}
\left[
F_0+
\left\lceil\log_2\frac{256d}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil+1
\right]
\le
\eta_D^2.
}
\tag{14}
\]
This is an exact rational comparison. The algorithm never writes \(e^{-F_0}\).
The contraction exponent obeys
\[
\beta_D
\le2n_D\theta_D+\frac12H_0\theta_D
\le\frac{\varepsilon}{32}.
\]
Also, \(q/p_D\le\varepsilon/64\). Equation (8) therefore gives the explicit bounds
\[
1-\frac{3\varepsilon}{64}
\le\frac{p_\infty}{p_D}
\le1+\frac{5\varepsilon}{64},
\tag{15}
\]
which imply (2).
The left side of (14) decays exponentially in \(D\); the dependence of its right side on \(D\) is only inverse-quadratic. Hence the necessary degree is polynomial in the input bit length and \(\log\varepsilon^{-1}\), not in \(1/p_\infty\) or the numerical magnitude of \(F_0\).
Conditioned trajectories
For failure-conditioned output, first choose \(D\) using a probability-law error budget. Sample the prefix conditioned on its crossing event. All omitted Gaussian blocks remain independent and unconditional under that prefix event.
Generate additional blocks only far enough to meet the requested waveform tolerance. This second cutoff uses an ordinary tail confidence, not confidence proportional to the failure probability. Equation (9) accounts for the change from conditioning on the prefix event to conditioning on the true event.
The output is a finite waveform coupled to a true failure-conditioned trajectory with the requested uniform error and failure allowance. It is not claimed that a discrete distribution of finite rational outputs has small total-variation distance from a continuous path law.
5. A stronger input model than a supplied Fourier table
The infinite covariance law need not be handed over as an arbitrary infinite list.
Consider the periodic response of a multichannel linear system
\[
x'(t)=-Cx(t)+f(t),
\]
where \(C\) is rational and
\[
\frac{C+C^\top}{2}\succeq\kappa I,
\qquad \kappa>0.
\]
Suppose the specified periodic Gaussian forcing has constant covariance \(U_0\succ0\) and harmonic covariance blocks
\[
4^{-j}\operatorname{diag}(U,U),
\qquad U\succ0.
\]
For
\[
R_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
C&jI\\
-jI&C
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the output covariance block is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_j
=
4^{-j}R_j^{-1}
\operatorname{diag}(U,U)
R_j^{-\top}.
}
\tag{16}
\]
The symmetric part of \(R_j\) is at least \(\kappa I\), so
\[
\|R_j^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\kappa^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathcal C_j
\preceq
\frac{\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\kappa^2}\,4^{-j}I.
\]
The constant covariance is
\[
S_0=C^{-1}U_0C^{-\top}\succ0.
\]
All required prefix covariances, phase invariance, positivity and the infinite tail envelope follow from finite rational linear algebra and matrix inequalities.
This is a checkable infinite-dimensional driven-model specification, rather than a finite-spectrum table. It is not a theorem for arbitrary Brownian forcing, general Matérn fields, nonlinear stochastic differential equations, or unvalidated physical models.
The distinction is essential: the forcing law is a modelling input. Learning or experimentally validating that law has a separate cost.
6. Adverse scaling: small omitted variance is not enough
A simple counterexample demonstrates why the relative transfer theorem is needed.
Let
\[
X_0(t)=Z,
\qquad
X_\sigma(t)=Z+\sigma(A\cos t+B\sin t),
\]
with independent standard Gaussian \(Z,A,B\). Over a complete period,
\[
\sup_tX_\sigma(t)=Z+\sigma R,
\qquad R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}.
\]
Take threshold \(u\ge2\) and \(\sigma=2/u\). The covariance error is at most
\[
\sigma^2=\frac4{u^2}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the coupled uniform trajectory error tends to zero in probability.
Nevertheless,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Pr(\sup X_\sigma>u)}{\Pr(Z>u)}
&\ge
\Pr(R\ge1)\,
\frac{\overline\Phi(u-\sigma)}{\overline\Phi(u)}\\
&\ge
\boxed{\exp\!\left(\frac32-\frac2{u^2}\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The last step integrates the Gaussian hazard bound
\(\phi(x)/\overline\Phi(x)\ge x\).
Thus a vanishing omitted variance can still change the rare probability by a factor bounded far away from one.
The new cutoff does not ignore this scaling. In an exact arithmetic stress test with threshold \(u=2^{1000}\), envelope \(a=1\), \(S_0=1\), \(x_*=2u\), \(r=u\), and \(\varepsilon=1/20\), the certificate selects
\[
\boxed{D=2016.}
\]
That is a cutoff certificate, not a runtime benchmark. It shows that the required approximation degree can remain proportional to the threshold’s bit length even under an extreme rare-event scale.
7. The acquisition barrier: two matching statistical lower bounds
The preceding algorithm presumes a certified stochastic model. It cannot make the cost of learning that model disappear.
Unknown Gaussian variance
Suppose calibration supplies independent exact observations
\[
X_i\sim N(0,v),\qquad v\in[1,2].
\]
The target is
\[
\log p_v(u)
=
\log\overline\Phi(u/\sqrt v).
\]
Require additive error at most \(\varepsilon\), with success probability at least \(2/3\), uniformly over \(v\).
Take
\[
v_0=1,\qquad
v_1=1+\Delta,\qquad
\Delta=\frac{16\varepsilon}{u^2},
\]
for \(u\ge2\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1/8\). Gaussian tail bounds give
\[
\frac{d}{dv}\log p_v(u)\ge\frac{u^2}{2v^2},
\]
so the two risk logarithms differ by more than \(2\varepsilon\).
But the one-observation relative entropy is only
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(N(0,1)\,\|\,N(0,1+\Delta)\bigr)
&=
\frac12\left[
\log(1+\Delta)-\frac{\Delta}{1+\Delta}
\right]\\
&\le\frac{\Delta^2}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The product-law entropy bound and Pinsker’s inequality imply
\[
\boxed{
N\ge\frac{u^4}{288\varepsilon^2}.
}
\tag{18}
\]
This order is attainable: estimate \(v\) by the empirical second moment, clip to \([1,2]\), and evaluate the Gaussian tail. Chi-square concentration gives
\[
N=O\!\left(u^4\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta)\right).
\]
Since \(\log(1/p_v(u))=\Theta(u^2)\),
\[
\boxed{
N=\Theta\!\left(
\frac{\log^2(1/p)}{\varepsilon^2}
\right)
}
\tag{19}
\]
at fixed confidence in this one-parameter experiment.
This is still exponentially cheaper than waiting to observe failures directly. It is nevertheless exponential in the binary length of a threshold \(u=2^b\).
The bound already grants exact Gaussian observations and unlimited processing. Measurement noise cannot improve it. It does not apply when the variance is supplied exactly, nor to arbitrary additional information-rich interventions.
Dots’ deadline compiler has the analogous acquisition boundary
For a single exponential activity,

X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda),
\qquad
p_\lambda(T)=e^{-\lambda T}.
\]
Estimating \(\log p_\lambda(T)\) to error \(\varepsilon\) requires estimating \(\lambda\) to accuracy \(\varepsilon/T\).
Comparing \(\lambda_0=1\) with

\tag{20}

8. Unrestricted physical misspecification restores inverse-\(p\) cost
There is a sharper barrier to interpreting an empirically good Gaussian fit as a rare-event certificate.
Let \(P(E)=p\le1/8\). Define \(Q\) by multiplying the density of \(P\) by
\[
1+4\varepsilon\quad\text{on }E
\]
and by the compensating constant on \(E^c\). Then
\[
Q(E)=(1+4\varepsilon)p,
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=4\varepsilon p.
\]
The two log risks differ by more than \(2\varepsilon\), but
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)
=
\operatorname{kl}\bigl(p\|(1+4\varepsilon)p\bigr)
\le32\varepsilon^2p.
\]
Thus even independent observations of complete trajectories require
\[
\boxed{
N\ge\frac1{144\varepsilon^2p}
}
\tag{21}
\]
for uniform log-risk estimation over this pair. Direct indicator Monte Carlo attains this order up to confidence factors.
This identifies the unresolved physical interface precisely:
A certified spectral or trajectory-tail bound can support efficient relative-risk computation. An empirical fit that leaves arbitrary rare tail contamination possible cannot provide the same guarantee at the same cost.

10. Resource accounting and supported outcome
For the certified infinite-spectrum class, the additional costs are explicit: interior-point and covariance linear algebra, exact cutoff calculation, generation of the required covariance prefix, and any extra output modes requested for waveform accuracy. The earlier compiler’s integration, sampling, root isolation, finite-precision and memory costs remain charged.
The guarantee is not uniform over an unspecified temporal analyticity width, arbitrarily slow spectral decay, unknown tail contamination, or unpriced model learning. Those parameters and assumptions cannot be absorbed into a harmless constant.
The executed package contains 6,129 exact rational assertions covering the contraction identity, polyhedral containment, cutoff certificates under adverse parameter scaling, and a rational driven-filter construction. The supported advance is a relative-error model-transfer operation and its infinite-spectrum computational corollary. The precise obstruction to a universal acquired-model compiler is statistical information: exact-model evaluation can be polynomial-bit while reliable parameter or tail acquisition is not.
