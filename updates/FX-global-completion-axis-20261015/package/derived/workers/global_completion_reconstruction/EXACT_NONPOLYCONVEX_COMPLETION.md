# No finite distortion coefficient repairs ordinary polyconvexity

15 October 2026. Independent internal reconstruction from the defining
polynomial, following the receiving checkout's local support idea. This
statement excludes ordinary polyconvexity, including arbitrary nonsmooth,
nonpolynomial convex lifts. It is not a quasiconvexity counterexample,
external review, formal proof verification, or a priority claim.

Let

\[
P_{\alpha,r}(A)=\alpha H(A)+\frac{r(r-1)}6u(A)^2+j(A)^2+r u(A)j(A),
\quad u=a^2+c^2,\quad j=ad-bc,
\quad H=((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2)^2.
\]

**Theorem.** If \(r(r+2)\ne0\), then \(P_{\alpha,r}\) is not polyconvex for
any finite real \(\alpha\). In particular, this holds throughout both
admissible completion branches \(r<-2\) and \(r>0\). More generally, the
same conclusion holds after adding any everywhere-finite correction which
is \(C^3\) near \(I\) and has zero Taylor jet through degree three there.
Zero second and third derivatives suffice as well, since constant and
affine terms do not affect the argument.

The exact derivative identities below are regenerated by
`verify_no_polyconvex_completion.py`; no exported producer coefficient or
verification script is a premise.

## 1. Ordinary polyconvexity gives a local lifted support

Suppose an everywhere-finite integrand \(F\), smooth near \(I\), is
polyconvex. Then some proper convex \(g\) satisfies
\(F(A)=g(A,\det A)\). Its lift is finite everywhere in the five-dimensional
space. Indeed, for any \((B,s)\), the pair of matrices \(B\pm tI\) has mean
\(B\) and mean determinant \(\det B+t^2\), while \(B\pm tJ_0\),
\(J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), has mean determinant \(\det B-t^2\).
Choose \(t\) to reach \(s\). Thus every lifted point is a convex combination
of two graph points at which \(g\) is finite.

A finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^5\) has a supporting
hyperplane at \((I,1)\). Write its determinant slope as \(\ell\). Pulling
the support back to matrix space and differentiating the smooth left side
at \(I\) gives

\[
F(A)-\ell\det A\ge F(I)-\ell
+[DF(I)-\ell\,\operatorname{cof}I]:(A-I).
\tag{N1}
\]

Consequently the left side minus its affine Taylor polynomial is
nonnegative near \(I\). This step does not require differentiability of
\(g\), a polynomial lift, or any globally smooth determinant-support
selection.

## 2. A zero Hessian row forces the determinant slope

For \(F=P_{\alpha,r}\), put \(G_\ell=F-\ell j\). At \(I\),

\[
\partial_{bb}G_\ell=0,\qquad
\partial_{bc}G_\ell=\ell-r-2.
\]

The Hessian of the nonnegative support gap in (N1) is positive semidefinite.
A positive semidefinite matrix with a zero diagonal entry has its entire
corresponding row equal to zero. Hence necessarily

\[
\ell=r+2.
\tag{N2}
\]

For this uniquely forced slope, define
\(V=\operatorname{diag}(1,-r-1)\). Exact differentiation yields

\[
D^2[P_{\alpha,r}-(r+2)j](I)V=0
\qquad\text{for every finite }\alpha.
\tag{N3}
\]

## 3. The cubic violates the support

Along \(A(t)=I+tV\), subtract the constant and linear Taylor terms of
\(G=P_{\alpha,r}-(r+2)j\). Its exact gap is

\[
G(A(t))-G(I)-tDG(I):V
=-\frac{r(r+2)}3t^3
+\left[\alpha(r+2)^4+\frac{(r+2)(r+3)}6\right]t^4.
\tag{N4}
\]

When \(r(r+2)\ne0\), the cubic is nonzero. Choose the sign of \(t\) so that
the cubic is negative, then choose its magnitude sufficiently small. Every
finite quartic coefficient is dominated by that cubic, contradicting (N1).
Thus ordinary polyconvexity is impossible for every finite \(\alpha\).

The value and all derivatives of \(H\) through degree three vanish at
\(I\). More generally, a correction with zero \(C^3\) Taylor jet contributes
\(o(|t|^3)\) to (N4) and changes neither (N2) nor (N3). It cannot repair the
contradiction. This is a local jet obstruction with a global ordinary
polyconvexity conclusion.

## 4. Entire nonzero curved boundary of the projected cone

For
\(F=\alpha H+\beta u^2+\gamma j^2+\delta uj\), suppose

\[
\gamma>0,\quad\delta\ne0,\quad
6\beta\gamma-\delta^2+\delta\gamma=0,
\quad2\beta+\delta>0.
\tag{N5}
\]

These are precisely the nonzero rank-one boundary triples of the first
acoustic matrix within the finite-completion projection. With
\(r=\delta/\gamma\), equation (N5) gives \(r(r+2)>0\) and

\[
F=\gamma P_{\alpha/\gamma,r}.
\]

Scaling a polyconvex integrand by a positive constant preserves
polyconvexity. Hence **no finite distortion coefficient makes any triple
in (N5) polyconvex**, while the independently reconstructed global
completion theorem makes it quasiconvex for sufficiently large finite
coefficient. On the negative branch, the receiving sharp theorem gives
the optimal quasiconvex coefficient as well.

This proof does not decide ordinary polyconvexity for strict interior
projected triples, and it does not decide the sharp positive-branch
quasiconvexity threshold.
