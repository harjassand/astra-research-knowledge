# Finite H completion in the strict conformal regime is classical

This is a receiving corollary of Iwaniec--Kristensen (2005), not a new general
QC theorem. Its proof is supplied to make the exact boundary visible.

Write C A=(a-d,b+c), H(A)=|C A|^4, and decompose A=U+V orthogonally into
conformal U and anticonformal V. Thus |C A|^2=2|V|^2. No rotational symmetry
of F is needed for the first theorem below.

**Receiving theorem.** Let F be a real homogeneous quartic on R^(2x2).
Assume there is d>0 such that

    D2 F(U)[R,R] >= d |U|^2 |R|^2

for every conformal U and every real rank-one R. Then a finite alpha exists
such that F+alpha H is quasiconvex at every background. In fact a further
fixed positive H subtraction can be reserved by increasing alpha.

**Preliminary strict-LH completion.** Set Q_F(A,R)=D2F(A)[R,R] and polarize
its quadratic dependence on A as B_F(U,V;R). Choose a finite M>=0 satisfying

    |B_F(U,V;R)| <= M |U| |V| |R|^2,
    Q_F(V,R) >= -M |V|^2 |R|^2

for conformal U, anticonformal V and rank-one R. Such M exists by
finite-dimensional compactness. Then

    Q_F(U+V,R)
      >= [d |U|^2 - 2 M |U||V| - M |V|^2] |R|^2
      >= [d |U|^2/2 - (M+2 M^2/d)|V|^2] |R|^2.

For rank-one R, |C R|^2=|R|^2, and direct differentiation gives

    D2H(A)[R,R]
      =4 |C A|^2 |R|^2 +8 (C A . C R)^2
      >=8 |V|^2 |R|^2.

Consequently, for

    c >= (M+2 M^2/d+d/2)/8,

R0=F+cH satisfies the globally strict inequality

    D2R0(A)[R,R] >= (d/2)|A|^2 |R|^2.

The exact Hessian identity is regenerated by the worker verifier.

**Receiving the primary theorem.** Iwaniec--Kristensen's Theorem 3.1 applies
to a C3 positively p-homogeneous R0, p>3, with a globally strict LH bound,
and a P with uniform integrated p-gap. For p=4, their Lemma 4.1 applies to
P=H: ker C is the conformal plane and contains no nonzero rank-one matrix.
It gives some e_H>0 with

    integral [H(B+Dphi)-H(B)] >= e_H integral |Dphi|^4.

Their theorem therefore gives QC of R0+tH, for all sufficiently large finite
t. In the notation of their displayed bound, if delta=d/2 and
T3=sup_(|A|=1) ||D3R0(A)||, one may take t>=T3^2/(2 delta e_H).
This proves the receiving theorem. The proof of the primary theorem uses
Taylor expansion, absorption of the third-order remainder, Fourier
positivity of the frozen strict-LH quadratic form, and the integrated gap
of P. It does not use heat flow.

For left-SO(2)-invariant F, strict LH at every nonzero conformal background
is equivalent to strict LH at I in all rank-one directions, using target
rotation and homogeneity. The strictness is essential to this application:
the shear-contact family has D2F(I)[e12,e12]=0 for every alpha, and H has
zero Hessian at I. Thus no finite preliminary H addition can make that
family receive the strict hypothesis.

## Exact closure of the full completion projection

Let V be any finite-dimensional space of homogeneous quartics containing
H and |A|^4, including the full nine-dimensional left-invariant space. Let
pi:V->V/span{H}. Let Q and R be the all-background QC and ROC cones in V.
Let E be the cone of classes with nonnegative conformal acoustic form:

    E={pi(F): D2F(U)[R,R]>=0 for conformal U and rank-one R}.

This is well defined because D2H vanishes on the conformal plane. E is
closed: it is an intersection of closed linear halfspaces in the quotient.
Necessity gives pi(Q) subset pi(R) subset E. If pi(F) belongs to E, then
F_epsilon=F+epsilon |A|^4 is strictly conformally elliptic for every epsilon>0.
The receiving theorem gives pi(F_epsilon) in pi(Q), and these classes converge
to pi(F). Therefore

    closure pi(Q) = closure pi(R) = E.

This does not give pi(Q)=pi(R), and does not determine their nonclosed
boundary. The supplied r=-2 endpoint is precisely the kind of boundary
obstruction that the closure statement cannot decide.

## A smaller classical SDP for that closure

For the nine-coefficient basis in the main report, write

    D2F([[h,-k],[k,h]])[e1 tensor (n,m), e1 tensor (n,m)]
      = [h,k] (n^2 C11+2nm C12+m^2 C22) [h,k]^T.

The coefficient map has rank six and kernel dimension three. Its matrices
are exported in `derived/RECEIVING_ALGEBRA.json`; their identity and rank
are checked independently. The classical two-factor biform theorem yields

    E membership iff there exists kappa in R with
    [[C11,C12+K],[C12-K,C22]] >=0,
    K=[[0,kappa],[-kappa,0]].

This is a 4x4 PSD test with one free skew scalar. It is only a test for the
closure of the finite-completion projection. The full acoustic test uses
8x8 matrices with six skew freedoms, and exact finite completion on the
degenerate boundary still requires additional information.
