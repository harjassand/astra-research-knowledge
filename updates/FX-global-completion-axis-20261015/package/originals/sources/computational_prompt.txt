The missing operation needed to exclude those unrealistically cheap relaxed solutions without excluding genuine physical solutions.
The construction emerged in two stages. The first uses ordinary convex-envelope mathematics; the second couples loads through a matrix identity.
Write
\[ w_i=W_{ii}=|V_i|^2,\qquad a_i=\underline v_i^2,\qquad b_i=\overline v_i^2. \]
At a bus with fixed complex injection \(s_i\), physical feasibility implies
\[ \ell_i:=|I_i|^2=\frac{|s_i|^2}{w_i}. \]
In the lifted formulation, current magnitude squared is also linear in \(W\):
\[ \ell_i=Y_iWY_i^*. \]
Because the reciprocal function is convex, its secant over \([a_i,b_i]\) gives
\[ \ell_i+ \frac{|s_i|^2}{a_i b_i}w_i \leq \frac{|s_i|^2(a_i+b_i)}{a_i b_i}. \]
This prevents the relaxation from assigning excessive current variance to a fixed-power load.
For generators, whose powers vary over a box, I constructed affine upper envelopes of the convex function
\[ \frac{P_i^2+Q_i^2}{w_i}. \]
No foundational novelty is claimed for these convex-envelope operations. They are nevertheless necessary controls: together, they reduced the numerical gap to 1.7790%. Any subsequent invention must improve beyond that result, rather than take credit for it.
For a fixed, nonzero complex injection, define the normalised current
\[ J_i:=\frac{I_i}{\overline{s_i}}. \]
The power equation gives the exact identity
\[ \boxed{J_i=\frac{1}{\overline{V_i}}.} \]
But \(J_i\) is also a known linear transformation of the voltage vector:
\[ J_i=B_iV,\qquad B_i=\frac{Y_i}{\overline{s_i}}. \]
This provides two descriptions of the same quantity: a linear network transformation and a reciprocal local voltage.
For two fixed-injection buses \(i,j\), define
\[ K_{ij}(W)= \begin{pmatrix}B_i\\B_j\end{pmatrix} W \begin{pmatrix}B_i\\B_j\end{pmatrix}^{*}, \]
and
\[ C_{ij}(W)= \begin{pmatrix} w_j&W_{ij}\\ \overline{W_{ij}}&w_i \end{pmatrix}. \]
Both matrices have entries linear in the existing full voltage Gram matrix.
Every physical voltage vector satisfies
\[ \boxed{C_{ij}(W)=w_iw_jK_{ij}(W).} \]
The proof is direct. The diagonal entries of the physical current matrix are \(1/w_i\) and \(1/w_j\), while
\[ J_i\overline{J_j} = \frac{1}{\overline{V_i}V_j} = \frac{V_i\overline{V_j}}{w_iw_j} = \frac{W_{ij}}{w_iw_j}. \]
Multiplying by \(w_iw_j\) yields the displayed matrix identity.
Since \(K_{ij}\succeq0\) and
\[ a_i a_j\leq w_iw_j\leq b_i b_j, \]
we obtain the valid matrix inequalities
\[ \boxed{ a_i a_jK_{ij}(W) \preceq C_{ij}(W) \preceq b_i b_jK_{ij}(W). } \]
Here \(A\preceq B\) means that \(B-A\) is positive semidefinite.
These inequalities do not require adjacent buses, equal loads, a radial network, zero resistance, or small phase angles. They do require known fixed injections and positive voltage lower bounds.
Individual current envelopes constrain each bus separately. The matrix construction constrains the joint amplitude and phase compatibility of two loads.
A scalar consequence is
\[ |V_i-V_j|^2 = w_iw_j|J_i-J_j|^2. \]
The matrix inequalities are stronger than enforcing this single difference relation: they test every complex linear combination of the pair.
The resulting operation excludes some nonphysical voltage–current covariance patterns that remain feasible in the relaxation and make its cost artificially low.
It introduces no higher-order moment variables in the full-\(W\) formulation. That does not make scaling free: a sparse implementation can require additional non-edge entries and matrix fill-in.

The exact pair identity contains one shared scalar, \(w_iw_j\), multiplying every entry of the normalised-current matrix.
The implemented relaxation only bounds that scalar between two endpoints. It does not enforce the exact shared product consistently across every covariance entry and every overlapping pair. Consequently, nonphysical mixtures can remain.
