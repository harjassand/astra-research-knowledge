Analyzed nitrogen-assisted chemical-axis writing
The most developed candidate from this investigation is a way to permanently change the preferred magnetic axis of an immobilized metal by passing it through a temporary nitrogen-rich structure.
The proposed operation is not ordinary magnetization. It would change the direction of the atomic ordering that determines the metal’s preferred magnetic axis, then retain that change after both the mechanical intervention and the nitrogen are removed.
The intended application is manufacturing high-performance iron–nickel magnets without rare-earth elements. However, this investigation has not established a new physical capability. The broader precursor-conversion mechanism already exists; the narrower controllable operation remains unmeasured. Moreover, the quantitative comparison shows that alignment alone would not establish competitive magnet technology.
The outcome is therefore a specific, physically motivated invention hypothesis, an experimentally accessible test, and a substantial restriction on what its success would mean.
I examined four routes with different useful outputs.
I developed the FeNi route because the underlying transformation does not disappear when the intervention stops. It also allows a sharp distinction between successful atomic manipulation and unsuccessful technology: the final material must deliver an adequate magnetic demagnetization curve, not merely an attractive diffraction pattern.
That distinction became decisive.
In chemically ordered \(L1_0\)-FeNi, iron and nickel occupy alternating atomic layers. The direction of that ordering is associated with the preferred magnetic axis. Nitrogen insertion and topotactic extraction—usually abbreviated NITE—already provide a route from disordered FeNi through an ordered nitride precursor to ordered FeNi. “Topotactic” here means that the conversion preserves a crystallographic relationship between the precursor and product. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-13562-2)
The proposed additional operation is:
Use directional mechanical loading during the nitrogen-enabled transformation to select the chemical-order axis inside immobilized material; unload it; remove nitrogen; retain the selected axis in the resulting metal.
The stronger demonstration would be to write one direction and subsequently write another direction in the same marked material, without mechanically rotating its particles or using an epitaxial substrate to impose the orientation.
Its conditional manufacturing value would be significant: crystallographic orientation could become a controllable property of material already fixed in place, rather than something that must be established by rotating powders or growing crystals on a suitably oriented template.
Nitrogen removal from oriented FeNiN films has already produced oriented \(L1_0\)-FeNi. A 2020 epitaxial-film study reported an order parameter \(S=0.87\) and measured uniaxial anisotropy \(K_u=5.9\times10^5\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\). Thus, “orient a precursor and remove nitrogen” is established prior art, not a new Astra operation. [AIP Publishing](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apl/article/116/24/242404/986293/Epitaxial-L1-FeNi-films-with-high-degree-of-order)
Direct stress-assisted FeNi ordering also has prior art: a patent describes combining thermal, extensional-stress and magnetic-field drivers. That establishes a priority warning, not independent validation of its performance claims. [Free Patents Online](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/11462358.html)
The potentially distinct claim is narrower:
Nitrogen-enabled rewriting of the chemical-order axis in immobilized material, with demonstrated retention after unloading and nitrogen extraction.
I did not find a demonstration of that exact response in the sources examined. This does not establish historical novelty.
The possible leverage is that a temporary chemical state can have a much stronger structural response to stress than the final material.
For two candidate transformation orientations \(i\) and \(j\), a leading-order fixed-stress description contains the term
\[ \Delta g_{ij}(\sigma) = \Delta g_{ij}(0) - \sigma: \left(\varepsilon_i^\ast-\varepsilon_j^\ast\right), \]
where \(\sigma\) is applied stress and \(\varepsilon_i^\ast\) is the transformation strain associated with orientation \(i\).
This suggests a physically intelligible route: directional loading could favor one orientation while the chemically enabled transformation proceeds. Removing the temporary constituent could then leave behind the selected ordering.
But this expression is not a prediction of switchability. The load-bearing unknowns are the actual transformation correspondence, nucleation barriers, interface mobility, stress transmission and orientation retention.
An important experimental result prevents an easy shortcut. The examined FeNi nitridation study describes precursor formation through a massive, interface-mediated transformation, with high-index orientation relationships and preferential nucleation near nanotwinned regions. It does not establish a simple, coherently switching three-variant ferroelastic material. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-07479-8)
Consequently, it would be unjustified to take a tetragonal lattice ratio, multiply it by an assumed stress and grain volume, and announce a switching threshold.
A distorted crystal is not necessarily a switchable crystal.
There is also a competing operating-condition requirement. A specimen must transmit enough directional loading to influence transformation while accommodating chemical expansion and contraction without cracking, slipping or losing orientation. The experimentally convenient geometry for relieving transformation stress may be precisely the geometry that transmits little externally applied stress.
The credible hypothesis is therefore stress-biased transformation or growth, not demonstrated low-force detwinning.
The most useful experimental platform I found is a study of non-epitaxial FeNi islands immobilized on a substrate.
That work provides both encouragement and a warning. After nitrogen removal, a representative island had a dominant chemical-axis orientation over approximately 72% of its examined area, but also contained differently oriented domains. Increasing nominal film thickness from 20 to 30 nm left approximately 16 wt% residual lower nitride under the reported treatment. These observations concern different specimens or stages; they are not a same-island stress-response experiment. [AIP Publishing](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/9/9/091108/122923/Fabrication-of-L1-FeNi-films-with-island)
The consequence is direct:
Successful precursor alignment would not prove successful final-metal alignment.
The operation needs an experimentally measured chain:
\[ \text{applied directional loading} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{precursor orientation} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{retained metal ordering} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{useful magnetic performance}. \]
The retrieved literature establishes pieces of this chain, but not their coexistence under the proposed intervention.
I acquired published text, figures and supplementary information for this assessment. I did not acquire raw instrument records measuring the indispensable stress-to-retained-axis response. The island-study authors make supporting data available on request, rather than supplying the required response as an open dataset. [AIP Publishing](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/9/9/091108/122923/Fabrication-of-L1-FeNi-films-with-island)
The relevant output is not simply chemical order or saturation magnetization. It is the final material’s usable magnetic performance, including the maximum energy product \((BH)_{\max}\), its coercivity, density and temperature-dependent stability.
A reported 2024 FeNi powder result reached approximately 2.3 kOe coercivity and 6 MGOe maximum energy product. The latter converts to approximately 47.7 kJ/m³. I could access the indexed primary abstract, but not the full measurement and normalization protocol, so this is a reported benchmark—not a reproduced or certified current record. [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2283203)
For strong commercial comparison, the inspected Arnold datasheets give nominal energy products of 334 kJ/m³ for N42 and 422 kJ/m³ for its N55 grade. These are manufacturer specifications, not matched experimental comparisons with the FeNi powder. [Arnold Magnetic Technologies](https://www.arnoldmagnetics.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/N42-151021.pdf)
For an ordinary monotone descending magnetic-polarization curve, write
\[ J=\mu_0M,\qquad h=-H\geq0. \]
In the useful second quadrant,
\[ B=J-\mu_0h. \]
Since \(J\leq B_r\), where \(B_r\) is remanence,
\[ -BH \leq h(B_r-\mu_0h). \]
Restricting the field to \(h\leq H_{cJ}\), the intrinsic coercive-field magnitude, gives
\[ (BH)_{\max} \leq h_\ast(B_r-\mu_0h_\ast), \qquad h_\ast= \min\!\left(H_{cJ},\frac{B_r}{2\mu_0}\right). \]
This is an optimistic envelope, not a predicted magnetic loop.
I evaluated it using the reported 2.3 kOe field scale, approximately \(183\ \mathrm{kA/m}\), while explicitly assuming a hypothetical saturation polarization of \(1.5\ \mathrm T\):
These rows are assumptions for a limiting calculation, not demonstrated specimens.
Even the generous perfectly aligned case remains below the commercial comparators. At the same coercivity, reaching the N55 sheet’s nominal 422 kJ/m³ would require an envelope remanence of approximately 2.54 T, incompatible with the assumed 1.5 T saturation polarization.
This does not prove that FeNi cannot become competitive. Orientation changes might also improve coercivity. It proves something narrower and important:
A texture-only improvement, with coercivity left at the reported powder value, cannot support the proposed high-end performance claim under these assumptions.
The physical operation must therefore improve—or at least enable improvement of—orientation and resistance to reversal in the same product. A beautiful alignment result alone would not meet the technological objective.
The 2017 supplementary information includes calculated powder-diffraction intensities. For fully ordered material, its calculated ratio is
\[ \left(\frac{I_{001}}{I_{111}}\right)_{S=1}=0.0163. \]
Using the order-parameter dependence on the squared intensity ratio, an illustrative \(S=0.71\) gives
\[ \frac{I_{001}}{I_{111}} = 0.0163(0.71)^2 \approx0.00822. \]
The order-sensitive peak is therefore only about 0.82% of the reference peak in this calculation. [Springer Nature Media](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-017-13562-2/MediaObjects/41598_2017_13562_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
Now suppose orientation changes double the measured relative intensity in one measurement geometry, without changing chemical order. Applying an uncorrected powder formula would give
\[ S_{\mathrm{apparent}} = 0.71\sqrt{2} \approx1.004. \]
That impossible apparent value illustrates the confound: the proposed orientation-writing operation can corrupt the usual readout used to claim improved ordering.
The experiment consequently needs separate measurements of phase fraction, chemical order and texture. A larger superlattice peak in one geometry cannot establish all three.
The caution is not hypothetical in this material family. The rapid-casting tetrataenite claim was subsequently reinterpreted, and a 2026 study demonstrated that elevated coercivity in FeNi-derived material can arise without \(L1_0\) ordering. Neither an isolated diffraction assignment nor a harder magnetic loop is sufficient identification. [Wiley Online Library](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202408796)
The initial platform should be immobilized, non-epitaxial material with spatial markers, small enough for nitrogen exchange and crystallographic characterization. Published island-film work supplies a starting platform—not proof that externally imposed loading reaches the reacting crystallites. [AIP Publishing](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/9/9/091108/122923/Fabrication-of-L1-FeNi-films-with-island)
The first measurement must therefore establish load transfer into the material during reaction. Substrate bending or fixture loading cannot simply be equated with particle stress.
The proposed causal comparison is compact:
The same marked material should be examined before conversion, in the precursor state, after unloading and after nitrogen removal. Particle displacement, damage and changes in shape must be recorded alongside lattice orientation.
Let \(\mathbf n\) denote the chemical-order axis of an ordered domain. Define the ordered-phase-volume-weighted orientation tensor
\[ Q=\langle \mathbf n\mathbf n^\mathsf T\rangle. \]
A convenient directional contrast is
\[ D=Q_{xx}-Q_{yy}. \]
The central result would be a reproducible difference
\[ \Delta D_{\mathrm{final}} = D_{\text{\(x\)-loaded}} - D_{\text{\(y\)-loaded}} \]
that remains after unloading and nitrogen extraction.
Ordered-phase fraction and degree of order must be reported separately. Where a control contains no measurable ordered phase, its normalized \(Q\) is undefined, not zero; an order- and phase-weighted signal can be reported separately.
The strongest operation-level demonstration would then repeat the cycle under the orthogonal loading direction and show that the chemical axis can be rewritten in the same immobilized material.
A magnetic change that disappears on unloading is not the proposed operation. Neither is whole-particle rotation, a change caused only by particle shape, residual-nitride magnetism, or selective examination of favorable islands.
If the precursor responds directionally but the final metal loses the response, the retention step has failed.
If no response occurs because the crystallites never experienced meaningful loading, the result is an unsuccessful test platform—not evidence that the underlying operation is impossible.
If loading is transmitted across a measured nondamaging range and the experiment excludes a predeclared practically relevant retained response, further optimization of that specimen route should stop.
Finally, a structural pass must be followed by a utility pass: the same resulting material must show improved full demagnetization curves, with density and active-material volume accounted for. Microscopy and magnetic characterization should not be performed on unrelated “best” samples.
There are several unresolved costs that cannot be replaced with favorable assumptions.
Chemical expansion and contraction. The literature reports roughly 30% greater unit-cell volume in the nitride state. Reversing such an expansion corresponds to approximately 23% contraction relative to the expanded volume. That is not automatic densification: an immobilized assembly may instead create gaps, stress or damage. [AIP Publishing](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apm/article/9/9/091108/122923/Fabrication-of-L1-FeNi-films-with-island)
Laboratory gas flow is not process efficiency. The original powder preparation used a 1 g specimen and an ammonia flow of 5 L/min for 50 hours. Multiplication gives 15,000 litres of delivered gas per gram in that laboratory protocol. This is neither net ammonia consumption nor a justified production-scale requirement. Recycling, utilization, heating and off-gas treatment would need actual measurement. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-13562-2)
The material is not iron-only. Equiatomic FeNi is approximately half nickel by mass. Removing rare-earth dependence does not remove feedstock, refining or embodied-energy costs.
Fast chemical access and high final density may conflict. A successful thin or porous specimen does not establish conversion throughout a dense body. Maintaining gas access, load transmission, chemical uniformity and final magnetic volume fraction is a coupled problem.
Retention needs a service-time specification. Supplementary diffraction after four-hour treatments helps identify processing behavior. It does not establish decades of chemical-order retention or magnetic reliability under load and temperature cycling. [Springer Nature Media](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-017-13562-2/MediaObjects/41598_2017_13562_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
I therefore have no defensible net-energy-per-kilogram, manufacturing-cost, bulk-throughput or lifetime prediction for this proposed operation.
The potentially valuable idea is using a removable chemical state to make a persistent structural property externally writable. In this candidate, the property is the direction of chemical ordering in a fixed metal.
But three levels must remain separate:
Established physical basis: nitrogen-mediated creation of ordered FeNi, including precursor-to-product orientation relationships in existing implementations.
Unestablished operation: externally selecting and rewriting that orientation in immobilized, non-epitaxial material, with retention after unloading and nitrogen removal.
Unestablished technological advance: producing a dense, stable magnet whose complete performance and manufacturing costs justify an advantage over strong incumbents.
The investigation also found newer FeNi routes, including 2026 work on indium-assisted bulk formation and mechanically induced tetragonal material. Full access was incomplete, so I am not treating the 2024 powder figure as a certified current frontier. Those retrieval limitations do not remove the central requirement to demonstrate the proposed response in one specimen. [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/5165412/11387009/11393511.pdf)
Current disposition: preserve this as a sharply defined physical-invention candidate, but do not promote it as an extraordinary technological discovery or build downstream hardware around it. The alignment-only performance argument has been restricted quantitatively. The surviving question is whether the chemical transformation provides a controllable, retained axis-writing operation that also enables adequate coercivity.
Available evidence establishes the underlying conversion and exposes its failure modes. It does not measure that indispensable response. That is the specific missing interface—not an unspecified need for “more research.”
