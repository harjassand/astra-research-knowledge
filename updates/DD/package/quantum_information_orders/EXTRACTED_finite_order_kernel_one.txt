A finite criterion for complete information order with a
one-dimensional source kernel
AI-generated mathematical research note

Abstract
For finite-dimensional quantum channels, complete symmetric logarithmic derivative order admits
a necessary and sufficient common Hermiticity-preserving observable lift whenever the source adjoint
has kernel dimension at most one. The lift is characterized by one finite semidefinite program. A
reference of dimension equal to the real dimension of the target Hermitian algebra suffices to test
the SLD order. The proof uses an exact null-vector block score to make one prescribed combination
of complex lift coefficients Hermitian without a limiting Gram penalty. Convex duality then supplies
precisely the combination needed in the one-dimensional-kernel case. The same argument excludes
every strict dual obstruction with imaginary-gradient rank at most one. Combined with complete-
order universality, the finite program also decides unrestricted complete relative-entropy and interior
Petz R´ enyi order. Numerical conditioning and entropy-witness reference sizes require separate
qualifications.

6 Relation to entropy orders and precision
The complete-order universality theorem for modular Petz divergences identifies unrestricted complete
SLD order with complete Umegaki relative-entropy order and, separately, with complete Petz R´ enyi
order for every fixed α∈ (0, 2), including the Umegaki limit at α = 1. Here “unrestricted” means
comparison on all state pairs and all finite references, without imposing equal reference marginals.
It also identifies this preorder with the order of any fixed operator-convex modular Petz generator
having finite f(0+) and a nonzero interior representing measure. This is an additional entropy–metric
theorem; the proof above concerns the finite observable-lifting step.
Corollary 6.1. Under the source-kernel hypothesis of Theorem 1.1, the finite program (3) is necessary
and sufficient for unrestricted complete Umegaki order, for each fixed interior Petz R´ enyi orderα∈ (0, 2),
and for each fixed modular Petz generator covered by the preceding universality theorem.
The equivalence applies first on faithful state pairs and then, by the usual common faithful
regularization, with extended support values. It does not cover the Petz R´ enyi endpoints merely by
continuity, and it does not identify numerical contraction coefficients.
The reference bound b2 is a bound for testing SLD order. It does not by itself give the same bound
for direct entropy-pair witnesses: the conversion from a strict SLD violation to an entropy violation can
introduce additional reference dimensions depending on the violation margin and spectral conditioning.
Finally, (3) is an exact finite semidefinite characterization. A floating-point solver status or a tiny
computed margin is not an exact boundary certificate. Near singular feasibility, validated residual and
eigenvalue bounds, exact algebraic certificates, or an appropriate separation promise are needed. No
unconditional polynomial bit-complexity or uniform lower bound on the witness margin is claimed.
