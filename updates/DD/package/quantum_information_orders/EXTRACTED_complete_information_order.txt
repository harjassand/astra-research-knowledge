Complete quantum information orders
AI-generated mathematical research note
10 October 2026
Abstract
For finite-dimensional channels, unrestricted complete Umegaki relative-entropy order is equiva-
lent to complete symmetric logarithmic derivative order and to simultaneous complete comparison of
all operator-convex modular Petz divergences. More generally, one fixed operator-convex generator
with finite left endpoint and a nonzero interior representing measure already determines this order.
The proof uses a saturated reference-only state pair, second derivatives in the denominator, and
realizable rational filters. Every strict violation has a finite-reference witness; explicit bounds are
given for conversion from an SLD violation to a relative-entropy violation. Single interior Petz R´ enyi
orders and sandwiched R´ enyi order two have the same comparison preorder. Petz orders zero and
two do not; complete Petz order two instead has an exact finite complex-linear lift characterization.
For the interior equivalence class, the argument does not establish a uniform reference bound or a
necessary common HP lift. It does not identify numerical contraction coefficients.

8.3 What the theorem does not identify
The theorem assumes all finite references and unrestricted input pairs. Its saturated pairs have different
reference marginals. An equal-reference-marginal-only hypothesis cannot be substituted in the proof.
The result equates channel preorders, not numerical contraction coefficients. It does not produce a
physical degrading channel. A state-independent HP observable lift with complete-square domination
is sufficient, but the proof gives no general necessity theorem for such a lift. It gives no reference
dimension depending only on channel dimensions, no gap-independent finite SDP, and no general
decision algorithm. The finite-left-endpoint condition is sufficient, not claimed necessary: for example
f(x) =− logx gives reverse relative entropy, which has the same unrestricted pair order by swapping
the two input states.

10 Conclusion
The decisive additional test is a noncommuting auxiliary reference at a saturated state pair. De-
nominator Hessians expose a tunable positive resolvent kernel. Finite simple-pole filters select the
SLD kernel, while signed residues cancel the quadratic endpoint when needed. This yields complete
order equivalence from one admissible divergence without assuming a common lift. Every strict SLD
failure becomes a finite entropy failure, but the required reference size can depend on the gap and
conditioning. These are comparison theorems and finite witness constructions, with the endpoint and
computational limitations stated above.
