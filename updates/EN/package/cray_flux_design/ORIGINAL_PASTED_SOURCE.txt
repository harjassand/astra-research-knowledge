CRAY result — flux-calibrated kinetic design
The strongest surviving construction is a procedure for globally optimising several interventions in a hidden kinetic network using only source-state measurements, a measured output, and tagged transition counts. It does not require reconstructing the network or supplying its response matrix as an oracle.
I derived the mathematical guarantees and implemented the acquisition-to-certificate workflow. I also investigated a different acquisition operation—mixed interventions—after finding that one-at-a-time experiments could be exponentially uninformative.
This is not yet an established fundamentally new scientific capability. The construction has close connections to existing response theory and control methods, and its experimentally demanding assumption—what counts as one controllable source state—remains a decisive limitation. The strongest counterexample shows that unlimited isolated-control data can conceal almost completely different joint responses.

1. The obstruction selected
I compared goal-directed inverse inference, physical inverse design, certification of stochastic predictions, and joint kinetic intervention design.
Avoiding full reconstruction is not itself new: goal-oriented experimental design already targets quantities of interest rather than complete parameter estimates. Likewise, constructing controllers directly from input-output data predates this investigation. Those ideas alone would not satisfy the mission. arXiv
The kinetic-design opportunity was more specific:
Can a small number of experimentally acquired response measurements determine the globally best combination of interventions, even when the underlying dynamics are unknown and the intervention strengths are large?

The useful change of variables is from intervention rates to the probability fluxes those interventions actually carry.
The stationary output is generally nonlinear in the rates. Under a precise intervention structure, it becomes linear in those fluxes, and the physically achievable fluxes form a small, experimentally recoverable polytope.
The whole hidden system need not be low rank. The restriction concerns the changes made by the available actuators.
2. Exact task and operational assumptions
Let an unknown finite-state process have generator
\[
Q(u)=Q_0+\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_j b_j e_{a_j}^{T},
\qquad
b_j=\nu_j-e_{a_j},
\qquad
0\le u_j\le U_j.
\]
The interpretation is that actuator \(j\), when the system occupies source state \(a_j\), adds a transition at rate \(u_j\), with destination law \(\nu_j\).
The inference procedure is not given \(Q_0\), the destination laws, or the stationary distribution. It observes the \(k\) source indicators, an output \(f(X)\in[0,1]\), and labels identifying added-transition events.
The desired output is a rate vector together with bounds
\[
J(u_{\mathrm{out}})\ge L,
\qquad
\sup_{0\le u\le U}J(u)\le U_{\mathrm{cert}},
\qquad
J(u)=\mathbb E_{\pi(u)}f.
\]
Thus \(U_{\mathrm{cert}}-L\) certifies global regret, rather than merely reporting a locally optimal setting or a good fit.
Three assumptions are load-bearing.
Each actuator has a distinct microscopic source. A measured population containing several hidden states is not automatically one source.
Actuator effects remain additive and unchanged under combinations. Single-control experiments cannot establish this by themselves.
For the acquisition guarantees, the target includes a reproducible reset. Specifically,
\[
Q_0=L_0+\rho(\nu\mathbf1^T-I),
\]
where \(\rho>0\) is a calibrated reset rate and the background exit rate is bounded by \(\Lambda_b\). The reset remains present in the system being optimised.
These assumptions hold by construction in the executable simulator. They have not been established for a real experimental apparatus.
3. The central mathematical construction
Run a baseline experiment and \(k\) isolated-control experiments. In experiment \(j\), only actuator \(j\) is enabled.
Write their stationary source populations and outputs as
\[
z_i^j=\pi^j(a_i),
\qquad
w^j=f^T\pi^j.
\]
Measure the actual tagged-event flux \(r_j>0\) in experiment \(j\), and define
\[
F_{ij}=z_i^j-z_i^0,
\qquad
d_j=w^j-w^0.
\]
Measured-data control theorem
The complete achievable source/output set is represented by
\[
\boxed{
\alpha\ge0,\qquad
z=z^0+F\alpha,\qquad
0\le z\le1,\qquad
r_j\alpha_j\le U_jz_j.
}
\]
The corresponding output is
\[
\boxed{J=w^0+d^T\alpha.}
\]
An implementing control is
\[
u_j=\frac{r_j\alpha_j}{z_j}
\]
when \(z_j>0\), with \(u_j=0\) when \(z_j=0\).
Consequently, global optimisation becomes a linear programme whose size depends on the number of actuators, not the number of hidden states.
Proof, including the hidden-positivity obligation
For the proof only, let \(h_j\) solve
\[
Q_0h_j=-b_j,\qquad \mathbf1^Th_j=0.
\]
These vectors are not supplied to the algorithm.
Stationarity gives
\[
\pi^j-\pi^0=r_jh_j
\]
and, for arbitrary controls,
\[
\pi(u)-\pi^0
=\sum_j u_j\pi(u)(a_j)h_j.
\]
Setting
\[
\alpha_j=\frac{u_j\pi(u)(a_j)}{r_j}
\]
proves that every real control produces a feasible point.
The converse is important. Given a feasible point, construct the potentially signed vector
\[
\widetilde\pi
=
\pi^0+\sum_j\alpha_j(\pi^j-\pi^0).
\]
It has mass one, its source coordinates equal \(z\), and the recovered controls satisfy
\[
Q(u)\widetilde\pi=0.
\]
Uniqueness of the normalised stationary kernel forces
\[
\widetilde\pi=\pi(u).
\]
Therefore every unobserved coordinate is automatically nonnegative. There is no missing exponential collection of hidden-state positivity constraints.
The underlying finite-perturbation principle is not a new invention: Bao and Liang already derive exact finite-response identities and reconstruction of a full single-parameter response curve without knowing the dynamical details. The candidate contribution here is the experimentally acquired multi-control compilation and its acquisition/certification analysis. arXiv
4. Acquiring the coefficients without a hidden oracle
For each probe, prepare the reset ensemble \(\nu\), evolve without further resets, and stop at an independent time
\[
T\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\rho).
\]
Record the endpoint measurements and the number \(C_j\) of tagged intervention events.
The endpoint law is
\[
\rho\int_0^\infty
e^{-\rho t}
e^{(L_0+\sum_j u_jB_j)t}\nu\,dt
=
\pi(u).
\]
Thus independent finite episodes produce independent observations of the reset system’s stationary law, without burn-in. This is an established resetting/resolvent identity, not a newly invented resetting principle. arXiv
The required flux is obtained directly:
\[
\boxed{r_j=\rho\,\mathbb E C_j.}
\]
Why tagged events matter
Estimating the same flux from endpoint occupancy would use
\[
s\,1_{\{X=a\}},
\]
whose variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(s\,1_{\{X=a\}}\right)=sr-r^2.
\]
A fast transition can have ordinary flux while its source occupancy becomes extremely small. Multiplying rare endpoint observations by the large rate then becomes statistically expensive.
Counting transitions avoids that particular penalty.
To control the counting cost, impose an ejection condition: every added transition lands outside the entire active source set. Between two controlled transitions, at least one background transition must then occur.
Let
\[
\Lambda=\Lambda_b+\rho.
\]
Competing background and reset clocks give a geometric domination of the episode’s intervention count. From its moments, I derived the simultaneous-error ingredient
\[
\boxed{
|\widehat r_j-r_j|
\le
\Lambda
\left(
2\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}+\frac{t}{n}
\right)
}
\]
for \(n\) independent episodes, with the usual exponentially small failure probability in \(t\).
The bound does not grow with the numerical intervention strength. Expected controlled-event work per episode is at most \(\Lambda/\rho\).
Executed measurement comparison
At intervention rate \(10^8\), a 200,000-episode integer-sampling test produced:

The simulator used integer competing-clock races, with at most 27-bit race integers in this test. It did not perform \(10^8\) control-event steps.
This establishes a finite operational example of the changed measurement statistic. It does not establish that event counting itself is historically new or optimal among all specialised estimators.
5. Strong controls, uncertainty, and the global certificate
Strong rates can be capped with an explicit error
Under the reset, distinct-source, and ejection assumptions, let
\[
v_j=\min(u_j,K).
\]
I derived
\[
\boxed{
|J(u)-J(v)|
\le
\frac{\Lambda(1+\Lambda/\rho)}{K}.
}
\]
The proof uses a stationary Poisson equation. Coupling at the next common reset bounds the oscillation of its solution by \(1/\rho\). At a capped source, the local Poisson equation bounds the output consequence of an additional transition. Summing against actual intervention flux closes the estimate because total added flux is at most \(\Lambda\).
This allows arbitrarily large supplied rate bounds to be replaced by a finite cap, with the approximation charged explicitly.
It does not remove dependence on reset time, background activity, or the cost of informative experiments.
Noisy measurements yield an outer optimisation problem
Suppose all endpoint means have error at most \(\eta\), all measured fluxes have error at most \(\eta_r\), and
\[
r_j^-=\widehat r_j-\eta_r>0.
\]
With \(A=\sum_j\alpha_j\), every true achievable design lies in the LP defined by
\[
\alpha\ge0,\qquad 0\le z\le1,
\]
\[
\left|
z_i-\widehat z_i^0-\widehat F_{i\cdot}\alpha
\right|
\le \eta(1+2A),
\]
\[
r_j^-\alpha_j\le K_jz_j,
\qquad
\sum_jr_j^-\alpha_j\le\Lambda.
\]
Its optimistic objective is
\[
\widehat w^0+\widehat d^T\alpha+\eta(1+2A).
\]
This supplies a global upper bound. A proposed control is tested on fresh episodes to obtain an achieved-output lower bound. Their difference is the regret certificate.
If a calibration flux cannot be resolved above zero, the implementation stops rather than inventing a usable denominator.
The arithmetic check does not trust LP solver tolerances
For a maximisation problem
\[
\max c_0+c^Tx,\qquad Ax\le b,\quad0\le x\le M,
\]
any rational \(y\ge0\) gives
\[
c_0+b^Ty
+
\sum_jM_j\max\{0,c_j-(A^Ty)_j\}
\]
as a valid upper bound.
The final term repairs numerical dual residuals. The main benchmark builds the confidence LP from integer counts and rational error bounds, then evaluates this certificate exactly. Confidence-radius inequalities are also checked using rational Taylor sums and integer-square comparisons.
The statistical and physical assumptions remain separate from that arithmetic guarantee.

Resource boundary
Inference storage is \(O(k^2)\) accumulated statistics plus the small LP. Expected episode duration is \(1/\rho\), with additional preparation and detector costs. The hidden simulator still stores its hidden state and transition mechanism; that storage has not disappeared.
For isolated probes, the sample-to-regret bound also depends on the smallest informative calibration flux. That flux can be exponentially small. Consequently, this is not an unconditional polynomial-in-input-bits acquisition theorem.
7. A different operation when isolated probes fail: mixed flux bases
The rare-calibration problem is not always intrinsic to the target. Another intervention may expose the source that was previously inaccessible.
For arbitrary calibration settings \(u^{(1)},\dots,u^{(k)}\), measure the full flux vectors and form
\[
R_{jm}=u_j^{(m)}\pi^{(m)}(a_j).
\]
Let \(D\) contain baseline-subtracted source responses. Stationarity gives
\[
D=HR.
\]
When the measured basis is informative, one obtains the response coordinates through
\[
H=DR^{-1},
\]
and optimises directly in actual nonnegative fluxes \(\beta\):
\[
z=z^0+H\beta,\qquad
0\le\beta_j\le K_jz_j.
\]
The inverse is computed from measured data, not supplied by an oracle. Its conditioning is explicitly observable and enters the error bound.
The executed rare-pathway test
In a two-control fixture, unassisted access to the second source occurred at rate \(2^{-30}\).
An isolated second-control probe had true flux
\[
9.2210\times10^{-10}.
\]
At 300,000 episodes, it would contain only about \(0.000277\) expected tagged events.
Activating the first control exposed a bridge to the second source. Mixed probes instead produced second-channel flux
\[
0.490148,
\]
with measured value \(0.489963\). The measured flux matrix had inverse one-norm about \(4.08\).
This removed that fixture’s rare-information bottleneck without a source-preparation oracle.
But the stronger completion test failed: the initial conservative confidence calculation returned an upper bound of \(1\). The mean response became informative, while the global certificate remained uninformative at the recorded budget.
The surviving claim is therefore successful acquisition of a useful mixed response basis in this example, not successful certified optimisation of the example. A general adaptive procedure for finding sufficiently informative probes—and obtaining a nonvacuous certificate at realistic cost—remains an exact unresolved obligation.
8. The decisive counterexample: experimental labels can hide the wrong structure
A source-selective actuator in the theorem is not the same thing as a knob acting on an experimentally labelled population.
I constructed two five-state models with states
\[
0,\;1,\;2,\;\text{success},\;\text{failure}.
\]
Both reset to \(0\). Two controls set two hidden flags. In model A, completing both flags reaches success. In model B, it reaches failure.
The models have additive control generators. Their source indicators, tagged event records, and outputs agree under the baseline and either isolated control, at every isolated strength. In fact, the entire observable isolated-control path laws agree.
Yet, at equal joint rate \(s\), their outputs differ by
\[
1-\frac{2}{1+s}+\frac{1}{1+2s}.
\]
At \(s=100\), that difference is
\[
\boxed{0.985173.}
\]
Thus:
Even unlimited isolated experiments cannot establish the intervention structure needed for the proposed global prediction.

This is not a numerical failure or a large-system complexity argument. Five states suffice.
Other explicit failures are preserved in the packet: a pooled source giving the wrong single-knob response, instrument cross-talk invisible to isolated probes, and fast controlled loops whose event-count cost grows with intervention strength.
The reset restriction is equally important. The method optimises the reset system. It does not obtain the original unreset stationary law simply by making experiments easier.
9. Comparison, originality, and the next decisive theorem
The proper comparison is not “reconstruct everything versus this new method.”
Occupation-measure LPs are established specialised algorithms. When the full generator was supplied, the benchmark’s full-model LP solved the problem in approximately 0.00573 seconds—far less than the acquisition cost. The possible advantage here concerns what must be measured or known, not beating that solver on a small known model. arXiv
Single-parameter nonlinear response without full identification is already known. The basic response identity cannot be promoted as an invention of this run. arXiv
Semi-Markov and options-based methods can also avoid representing hidden transient dynamics under suitable event-path access. No separation from the strongest such methods under fully matched access has been proved here. ScienceDirect
The candidate research object is therefore the acquired flux basis together with its global finite-data certificate and explicit acquisition costs. Historical originality of that integrated construction remains unresolved.
The next decisive result must address acquisition and structural validity, not merely improve another LP constant. It would need to establish, for an explicit experimentally meaningful class, that affordable mixed interventions can acquire a stable response basis, certify an output-relevant bound on hidden actuator heterogeneity, and produce a nonvacuous global-regret certificate within a measured resource budget.
The current counterexample rules out obtaining that structural certificate from isolated-control agreement alone. The mixed-probe example supplies a constructive direction, but not the general theorem.
Completion assessment: the run produced a mathematically derived and executable capability for a specified resettable kinetic interface, plus a stronger mixed-probe acquisition mechanism and sharp transfer failures. It did not establish the requested major, historically original scientific capability. The remaining obstruction is now concrete: acquiring and certifying the intervention structure that makes the small global computation legitimate.
