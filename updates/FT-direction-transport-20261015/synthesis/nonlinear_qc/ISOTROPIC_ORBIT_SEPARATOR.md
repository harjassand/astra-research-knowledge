# Positive input-orbit exclusion for the entire isotropic quartic class

Date: 15 October 2026. Internal analytic proof; external correctness review
and historical novelty UNKNOWN.

This strengthens `DM_ORBIT_SEPARATOR.md` to the full both-left-and-right
SO(2)-invariant homogeneous quartic class. It does not classify all positive
syntheses from arbitrary quasiconvex generators.

Let `s(A)=|A|^2`, `j(A)=det A`. Every both-left-and-right SO(2)-invariant
homogeneous quartic has the form

    F_(a,b,c)(A)=a s(A)^2+b s(A)j(A)+c j(A)^2.            (1)

For this class rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity by
[Bruno and Pasqualotto, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1).
We only use the elementary necessary rank-one inequalities below for the
separation proof, not an unverified classification of their feasible region.

Theorem. G lies outside the coefficientwise closed positive cone generated by
all input pullbacks `F_(a,b,c)(A S)` of rank-one-convex energies (1), with S
constant real 2x2, together with arbitrary homogeneous polyconvex quartics.

## 1. Compact reduction of the generator parameters

Since `F(R)=a|R|^4` on rank-one R, rank-one convexity gives a>=0.

For S with rows p,q, the shear probe is

    ell(F(. S))=D^2F(S)[e12 S,e12 S]
       =8a(p.q)^2+[4a|S|^2+2b det S]|q|^2.              (2)

Evaluate it at diagonal S with equal absolute diagonal entries and either
determinant sign. Nonnegativity gives `|b|<=4a`.

At A=I in direction e11, rank-one convexity gives
`16a+6b+2c>=0`. At A=diag(1,-1) in the same rank-one direction it gives
`16a-6b+2c>=0`. Thus

    c>=-8a+3|b|.                                        (3)

If a=0, these conditions give b=0 and c>=0, so F=c j^2 is polyconvex.

If a>0 and c>=4a, the exact decomposition

    F=[(4a+b)/8](s+2j)^2+[(4a-b)/8](s-2j)^2
         +(c-4a)j^2                                    (4)

is polyconvex. Indeed `s+2j` and `s-2j` are squared norms of linear
Cauchy-Riemann combinations of the columns, so their squares are convex.
The coefficients are nonnegative, and j^2 is polyconvex. Constant input
pullbacks preserve polyconvexity, so these generators can be absorbed into
the permitted polyconvex term.

Every remaining generator may therefore be normalized to a=1, absorbing its
positive a into the conic weight. By (3), its parameters belong to the compact
rectangle `b in [-4,4]`, `c in [-8,4]`. Restrict to the closed subset K where
the polynomial is rank-one convex. The exact feasible region is unnecessary.

## 2. Every shear-null pullback is convex

Normalize every nonzero S to |S|=1, again absorbing its fourth power into the
conic weight. From (2) and `|det S|<=|S|^2/2`,

    ell(F_(1,b,c)(. S))
       >=8(p.q)^2+(4-|b|)|q|^2.                         (5)

If |b|<4, vanishing of the shear probe forces q=0. Such a matrix S has rank
one, and the pullback is `|A e1|^4 |p|^4`, hence convex.

It remains to handle |b|=4 exactly. If b=-4,

    F=(s-2j)^2+(c-4)j^2.

At A=I the first term vanishes to fourth order along A+t e11. Its second
derivative is therefore `2(c-4)`, and rank-one convexity forces c>=4. Since
c<=4 in K, c=4 and F=(s-2j)^2 is convex. For b=+4, apply the same argument
at A=diag(1,-1); then F=(s+2j)^2 is convex. Their input pullbacks are convex.

Consequently, at every point of the compact shear-null set in
`K x {|S|=1}`, the generator pullback is convex. This endpoint argument is
essential; a strictly positive uniform shear constant was not assumed near
|b|=4.

## 3. Bounded orbit mass and separation

Suppose for contradiction H_n are such finite positive sums plus polyconvex
P_n, and H_n converge coefficientwise to G. After the preceding absorptions
and normalizations,

    H_n=sum_i w_ni F_(1,b_ni,c_ni)(. S_ni)+P_n,
    w_ni>=0, (b_ni,c_ni) in K, |S_ni|=1.

At E11 and E12, the generator values are respectively the fourth powers of
the first and second row norms of S. Their sum is at least 1/2. Polyconvex
homogeneous quartics are nonnegative on rank-one matrices, so

    sum_i w_ni<=2[H_n(E11)+H_n(E12)],                    (6)

which stays bounded under coefficient convergence.

The shear probe is nonnegative on every summand and on P_n, while ell(G)=0.
Thus `sum_i w_ni ell(F_(1,b_ni,c_ni)(. S_ni))->0`.
Compactness implies that the weight outside any neighborhood of the shear-null
set tends to zero.

Use the exact lifted-barycenter law nu on `+/-36I` and
`+/-diag(1,-1)` from the G proof. Its mean matrix and mean determinant vanish,
but `E_nu G=-1350/1297`. The Jensen functional `E_nu F_(1,b,c)(A S)` is
continuous on the compact parameter space and nonnegative on the shear-null
set by Section 2. Uniform continuity, (6), and vanishing mass away from that
set give

    liminf_n E_nu[sum_i w_ni F_(1,b_ni,c_ni)(A S_ni)]>=0.

The polyconvex term has nonnegative lifted Jensen functional. Coefficient
convergence therefore forces `E_nu G>=0`, contradiction. QED.

The same argument handles a single legal input transform as a special case.
It also excludes arbitrary positive mixtures and their coefficientwise limits;
merely testing whether one explicit transform matches G would be weaker.

This comparison does not undermine the isotropic quasiconvexity theorem. It
shows that the new G certificate reaches beyond positive input-orbit synthesis
from its complete polynomial class and polyconvex addends. The method used to
find and prove the certificate remains the established exact-form/heat method.

The finite algebra supporting (2)-(4) and both endpoint arguments is checked
independently by `verify_orbit_and_soft.py`; the compactness argument is proved
here. No numerical conic infeasibility conclusion is used.
