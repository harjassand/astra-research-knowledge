# Two accessible supports can meet barrier targets that no single support direction can

The retained result is conditional but substantive: in a common-host harmonic model, two independently accessible passive springs can lower one useful barrier while raising two damage barriers when every rank-one support, at every orientation and strength, fails those three signs. The finite rational construction is proved in [PROOFS.md](PROOFS.md), section 4. It uses invented common-model inputs, not measured material coefficients. There is no qualified material, physical experiment, historical originality claim or demonstrated device advantage in this packet.

The general result is an attainable-body calculation. For measured accessible coordinates q=U^T u and common host Hessian H, define G=U^T H^-1 U, a_s=G^-1/2 U^T H^-1 f_s and

    Q=I-(I+G^1/2 S G^1/2)^-1,   0<=Q<I.

A fixed constrained barrier has shift tr(Q C_s), where C_s=(a_s a_s^T-a_A a_A^T)/2. Finite support therefore yields a bounded spectral body, rather than an unlimited cone of barrier improvements. The exact inverse map checks whether a proposed Q belongs to the actual fixture's spring catalog. A force projection outside that fixture's accessible subspace cannot be recruited by choosing a convenient mathematical direction.

For a calibrated full-PSD support cap, the support function and a necessary-and-sufficient simultaneous-target certificate have closed forms. Two strict sign criteria alone admit a rank-one direction if unrestricted PSD support admits them. That statement fails for three signs, and it also fails for two fixed improvement targets under a finite ceiling. The proof includes both exact counterexamples. These are model design distinctions; the matrix tools themselves are standard.

The resource calculation is coupled to support strength. A reversible, isothermal establishment cost in a fixed initial well contains both relaxation work and a logarithmically diverging entropy term. Optimizing a linear objective under that budget rotates the optimal support according to the initial-well cost; simply choosing the largest eigenvector of the desired benefit can be wrong. A separate declared normalized-stiffness budget gives another explicit finite solution. Neither model budget substitutes for measured installation, geometry, fabrication, maintenance or reset cost.

Finite-temperature corrections are decisive. With state-dependent Hessians, entropy need not cancel and there is no common Q. The exact local response uses constrained covariance differences. A synthetic example has a useful barrier decreasing at small stiffness and increasing at larger stiffness, disproving automatic extension of a local favorable sign into an operating window. Prestress, surface chemistry, pressure, moving pathways and transmission/prefactor changes are separate interventions or corrections.

For the technological endpoint, the independent [transport_damage analysis](../transport_damage/PROOFS.md) supplies the proper local objective: cumulative occupation times times event rates times continuation advantages. At fixed preparation, topology, prefactors and complete damage clocks, all well shifts cancel from lifetime jump rewards; they rescale waiting times. The useful design matrix is a signed, network-weighted sum of saddle response matrices. It can be optimized over legal support directions as a first-order proposal, followed by a full finite network and cost calculation. This is not a global concave yield optimizer. A fixed laboratory-time hazard, deadline, time reward or changing prefactor/preparation breaks the stated cancellation premises.

The exact two-port witness proves its simultaneous barrier constraints, not superior lifetime yield over every rank-one device. The weighted full-network objective can favor a different tradeoff, particularly when one damage channel is rare.

## What the primary literature permits

Stiffness-dependent measured kinetics is established. Wen et al. varied trap stiffness and handles in RNA hairpin experiments; rates changed under both feedback and passive measurement conditions. The feedback result is not a free passive-stiffness device, and its apparatus dependence is an attribution warning. [Primary experiment, 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1852336/).

A later optical-tweezer study quantified probe/linker effects and estimated roughly 20% constant-force kinetic artifacts in its DNA-hairpin conditions. Thus instrument response and molecular commitment must be distinguished rather than treating a recorded dwell-time change as a barrier measurement. Its passive force clamp still uses powered optical traps. [Primary study, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4968418/).

Graphene crown-ether work predicts large strain-dependent ionic currents using molecular dynamics, including a nonmonotone optimum as transport barriers change. It supplies a strong strain-control comparator, not an experimentally established zero-preload stiffness operation or damage window. [Sahu et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6625819/). Fang et al.'s related large mechanosensitivity result also comes from room-temperature simulation. [Fang et al., 2019](https://www.nist.gov/publications/highly-mechanosensitive-ion-channels-graphene-embedded-crown-ethers).

Actual graphene nanopore experiments found pressure-enhanced conductance. One same-device example went from 1.68 to 4.51 nA at 100 mV when 2.5 bar was applied. The authors attribute the main effect to transport of capacitively accumulated ions, and explicitly examine why deformation does not explain it. Pressure-dependent conductance therefore cannot by itself identify this packet's barrier-clamp mechanism. Hydraulic pressure work belongs in its comparator budget. The paper's high-field/high-pressure MD is a separate regime and is not combined with the experiment here. [Jiang et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8759738/).

Fluctuating-membrane simulations further show that flexibility can change conductance scaling, so suppressing fluctuations need not improve transport universally. [Noh and Aluru, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-46962-8). Force-spectroscopy inference requires a model of the reaction coordinate, loading and dynamics; it is not a general transition-state oracle. [Dudko, Hummer and Szabo, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18852468/). The source ledger records which full texts, abstracts and blocked access paths were actually inspected.

## The obtainable experiment

Use a real device with two separately calibrated support ports and fixed rest positions. Randomize each individual port, both together, a sham fixture and the strongest matched strain/pressure/chemical baseline at one common temperature, reservoir and electrical/hydraulic task. Acquire compliance, preload, active-area and fabrication effects. Measure channel-specific events and damage exposure where observable; otherwise measure accepted lifetime output directly and refrain from claiming an identified microscopic network.

Small legal perturbations can acquire the operational rate slopes needed for the continuation-value objective. This avoids assuming an inaccessible transition-state force vector. It does not identify which part of a rate change came from barrier, prefactor, recrossing, chemistry or contact response. Confirmation must use a finite clamp proposal and complete lifetimes, not only the selected favorable local slope. Rare damage exclusion can dominate acquisition: zero events over homogeneous at-risk exposure E gives only h<=-log(alpha)/E at confidence 1-alpha.

Even an ideal, identifiable Poisson slope experiment has standard-error scale 1/[delta sqrt(kE)] near zero response, where delta is the legal perturbation amplitude, k the channel rate and E its at-risk exposure. The derivation includes the unknown baseline rate as a nuisance parameter. This makes the rare-channel acquisition cost explicit; hidden pathways and drift make that favorable model insufficient.

| Boundary | Comparison that must be acquired |
|---|---|
| Accepted transport | Net specified output, selectivity and inventory settlement over the full lifetime; entry, exit, return and all damage routes |
| Service | Lifetime, reset/replacement duration, delivered flow/current and accepted renewal throughput |
| Mechanical fixture | Reachable ports, actual stiffness caps, preload, lost active area, access/contact resistance, drift, local stress and new failure routes |
| Energy and resources | Reservoir/electrode/pressure work; support installation and dissipation; any feedback; heating/cooling; calibration/destructive samples; fabrication yield, maintenance and disposal |
| Best baseline | Same task and boundaries with no support, sham support, optimized ordinary strain/pressure or chemistry where actually available |

The decisive falsifier is a lack of a beneficial legal response after all damage, throughput and resource terms are included, or failure of its predeclared finite confirmation. A complete positive result would establish an operational effect in that measured regime. It would not by itself establish microscopic attribution, scalable manufacturing or a civilization-scale capability.
