NEW THEOREMS

For bounded connected Lipschitz planar Omega, a real rank-one-convex
homogeneous quartic F with mu_1=min F(e1 tensor unit xi)>0, affine boundary
g1,g2, and supplied v in g2+W0^(1,4), let a_* minimize the Cartesian first
row and a_R minimize the exact translated radial chart from the source.
Then

  ||a_R-a_*||_(W1,4)=O(R^(-1/3)),
  I_v(a_R)-I_v(a_*)=O(R^(-4/3)),
  m_R=I_v(a_*)+J1(a_*)/R+O(R^(-4/3)),

where J1(a)=integral F_2([grad a;grad v]).grad((a-g1)v).
The proof establishes a uniform C1 energy comparison on bounded Sobolev
sets, uses stationarity after the positivity obstacle becomes inactive,
and applies quartic scalar monotonicity. No smooth minimizer or uniformly
elliptic Hessian is assumed.

For the EXACT fourth mass prescribed by a_ref, let a_*^m minimize the
Cartesian problem with mean integral a_ref. If its mean multiplier is
DI_v(a_*^m)[eta]=lambda_* integral eta, then

  m_R^mass=I_v(a_*^m)
      +[J1(a_*^m)-lambda_* H1(a_*^m)]/R+O(R^(-4/3)),
  H1(a)=(3/2)integral(a^2-a_ref^2).

The optimizer rate is again R^(-1/3). The minus sign and multiplier scale
are derived from the actual normalized mass, not inferred by dropping
the constraint. Its normalized multiplier converges at rate R^(-1/3).

The Cartesian reduced functional is globally C1, with locally Holder-1/3
optimizer dependence and stress derivative. Its derivative at EVERY weak
scalar minimum is integral F_2(A_*) grad w; it does not require
differentiating the optimizer. In particular J1(a_*) is its derivative in
the direction (a_*-g1)v. At fixed mean, the reduced critical equation
retains the first-row constant multiplier. No second derivative is
asserted outside a justified nondegenerate branch.

The scalar residual dual norm equals the L4/3 distance of the first-row
stress to divergence-free fluxes, or to fluxes with constant divergence
for mean-zero tests. Any supplied equilibrated flux therefore gives a
continuum residual certificate. The exact quartic residual bounds control
both energy error (power4/3) and optimizer error (power1/3). No claim that
the flux or its norm is free to acquire, or that a solver has certified
polynomial runtime, accompanies this characterization.

SHARP FRACTIONAL RESPONSE, WITH AND WITHOUT THE ACTUAL MASS

On the unit square set v=x(1-x)y(1-y), g1=g2=0, and use the convex quartic
F_c(rows xi,eta)=|xi-c eta|^4+|eta|^4. Its mu_1 is exactly 1 and the
Cartesian optimizer is a_*=cv. Exact expansion of the same translated
chart under a=cv+R^(-1/3)b gives the variational response functional

  H(b)=integral |grad b|^4
       +4 integral |grad v|^2 grad v.grad(vb).

It has a unique nonzero minimizer and strictly negative minimum. In the
mass-constrained version it is minimized over integral b=0 and still has
a unique nonzero minimizer. The proofs give strong W1,4 convergence of
the actual scaled optimizers and

  m_R=E0+cB/R+(min H)/R^(4/3)+o(R^(-4/3)),

with the corresponding mean-zero minimum for the exact-mass operation.
Here E0=1/1470 and B=101/1091475>0. A polynomial mean-zero force test
b0=v^2-v/25 has exact force -118/3902023125, proving the constrained
response is nonzero. Thus the 1/3 optimizer exponent and first-order
renormalized 4/3 value exponent are sharp, and c=1 also makes the original
unrenormalized 1/R value order sharp.

These F_c examples are NOT left-SO(2)-invariant. Sharpness is established
for the incoming theorem's general class; sharper rates in the narrower
invariant cone remain unproved. All example energies are nonnegative;
negative corrections to a positive minimum are not negative QC gaps.
The unperturbed optimizer a_v=cv is smooth in v. The non-C1/non-C2
behavior concerns the translated epsilon=1/R parameter, not sharpness
of the Holder modulus of the v-envelope.
