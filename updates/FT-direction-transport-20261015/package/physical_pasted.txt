Research result: selective de-trapping through a passive mechanical constraint
This invocation did not establish a fundamentally new physical operation. The strongest intermediate is a specific, testable mechanism: use an accessible mechanical constraint to weaken an ionic trap while simultaneously making a destructive atomic rearrangement harder.

The calculation permits both effects under one shared control, rather than independently assuming favourable transport and stability. However, I have not identified a material–support combination that supplies the required response, and the narrower operation’s novelty remains unresolved.

A significant obstruction also emerged: faster escape from a trap can produce less net transport. In the coupled example below, a 6.9-fold increase in escape rate accompanies a 32% decrease in throughput when entry is limiting. This changes which physical regimes are worth investigating.

1. Opportunity selection and the prior-art screen
I concentrated on controlling useful atomic transport without activating destructive host motion. The intended capability is not merely high ionic conductivity. It is an independently usable control that improves transport and operational lifetime together, without continuously depositing more energy than the resulting process saves.

Several attractive formulations failed the operation-level novelty screen.

Capacitive ion ratcheting is already an operation, not an invention opportunity under another name. Kautz and colleagues’ 2026 work reports a nanoporous capacitive electrochemical ratchet for continuous ion separation. A proposal simply to use asymmetric, time-dependent capacitive fields to move ions would overlap that prior art. 

Driving ions through lattice vibrations is also established prior art. Minami and colleagues reported single-cycle-terahertz-driven ionic flow in sodium β-alumina in 2020. Pham and colleagues subsequently combined calculations and experiments on phonon–ion coupling in LLTO. Their reported factor of ten concerns differential impedance relative to other excitation conditions, not a demonstrated tenfold absolute conductivity improvement in a complete device. 

Mechanical and vibrational control of pore transport cannot be claimed generically as new. Fang and colleagues investigated strain-sensitive crown-ether pores; Sahu and colleagues investigated optimal electromechanically tuned transport. Noh and Aluru studied transport activation through vibrational coupling and fluctuating forces. These are substantial overlaps with any broad “change the lattice mechanics to improve transport” proposal, although simulation evidence is not itself qualification of a practical actuator. 

I also rejected a tempting combination of results from different experiments. McClellan and colleagues’ May 2026 LLTO study found correlations extending across approximately 1,500 repeated ultraviolet excitation shots. That is not a measured lifetime of enhanced conductivity in darkness. It cannot supply the missing retention time for a separate terahertz-driven conducting state. 

These findings redirected the investigation away from stronger excitation and toward a narrower question:

Can a passive boundary constraint remove the lattice relaxation that stabilises an unwanted trap, without removing—and preferably while strengthening—the protection against destructive rearrangement?

That is the mechanism developed below. Its generic ingredients are known; its particular coupled capability remains to be established.

2. The physical mechanism: constrain the trap more than the useful transition state
Consider an ion trapped at an interface or transport site.

The surrounding material can deform around the trapped ion. That deformation can lower the trapped state’s energy, making escape difficult. A mechanical constraint that prevents some of this relaxation could make the trap shallower.

But this helps only under a particular ordering:

The useful transition state must depend less on the constrained relaxation than the trap does.
A destructive transition state must depend more on it.
The same constraint could then lower the useful activation barrier and raise the destructive one.

This is not the assertion that “stiffer materials conduct better.” It is a statement about differences between specific atomic configurations, projected onto a deformation that a real support can constrain.

3. The decisive obstruction: escape, occupancy and entry are coupled

A shallower trap releases ions faster, but it can also contain fewer ions. If the constraint raises the transition-state energy relative to the empty pore, incoming ions encounter a larger entry barrier as well.

These effects can cancel—or reverse—the apparent improvement.

5. Is there a physically obtainable implementation?
A credible experimental class exists; a qualified operating regime does not yet.

The most plausible initial class is an ultrathin transport membrane whose active sites can couple to a nearby mechanical support. Defect-engineered hBN has been investigated for lithium-ion conduction and is a possible screening platform. That work does not provide the required transition-state response ordering or demonstrate the proposed clamped geometry. 

The proposed physical intervention would be an independently adjustable or mechanically latched support that changes local compliance while leaving the transport aperture open.

Several distinctions are essential.

A remote macroscopic clamp may barely affect the local displacement that stabilises the ion trap. An atom-freezing calculation would then predict an inaccessible operation.

Holding the nominal pore shape or applied strain fixed does not guarantee unchanged microscopic geometry. The mechanism intentionally changes local relaxation. A backing contact could also change charge, bonding or solvation more strongly than it changes the relevant mechanics.

Finally, the useful and destructive transition states must be evaluated under the same support condition. Combining favourable conductivity from one specimen with favourable stability from another would not qualify the mechanism.

The decisive next physical result
The next substantive result should be one actual material–support pair, not a complete battery or manufacturing apparatus design.

Its calculation must include the real support, empty and occupied configurations, entry and exit pathways, and credible damaging pathways. Electronic-structure and finite-temperature rare-event calculations provide a possible acquisition path, but they need appropriate chemistry and experimental checks; an arbitrary fitted potential or convenient frozen boundary is insufficient.

Experimental qualification would require net carrier transfer in a charge-balanced device, separation of electronic and displacement currents, and irreversible damage measurements under the same loading. Current alone would not identify the proposed transition-state mechanism.

The relevant comparison is not merely an untreated specimen. It includes optimised pore chemistry, composition or defect engineering, static strain, thinner transport layers and better contacts, at matched accepted output.

The route should be rejected or reclassified when:

The accessible relaxation contrast is too small or has the wrong ordering.
Entry, occupancy, recrossing or another damage pathway cancels the local advantage.
Full resource and active-area costs outweigh the benefit.
The realised mechanism is simply established strain or binding control in a different fixture.
This last condition is important: a new implementation is not sufficient evidence of a fundamentally new operation.

Assessment
The defensible intermediate is:

A physically accessible constraint could improve transport and suppress destruction together if it removes more relaxation energy from the occupied trap than from the useful transition state, and still more from the destructive transition state—but only where the resulting entry and occupancy changes preserve a net process advantage.

The work establishes conditional coexistence in a shared model and identifies explicit failure regimes. It does not establish that an existing material satisfies those conditions, that the narrow operation is historically new, or that its advantage would be technologically transformative.

The most important unresolved input is therefore not a better control waveform. It is the same-regime, boundary-accessible response of the actual useful and destructive transition pathways.
