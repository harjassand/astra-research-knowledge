Result: a conditional forward-only adjoint operation, not yet a new physical capability
I derived an operation that can recover the control sensitivities of a partially unknown physical network without reconstructing its interior or physically reversing its nonreciprocal components.
The central idea is to measure how a small set of accessible ports breaks reciprocity, then use those measurements to reconstruct the missing adjoint response. This response determines how local changes would affect a chosen output.
The result has a precise boundary:
The construction is mathematically exact under an explicit structural assumption, and its implementation passed the synthetic tests performed here. Its historical novelty, integrated physical feasibility, and advantage over the strongest existing methods remain unestablished. It does not yet meet Astra’s threshold for an exceptionally consequential new capability.
The most important accompanying result is an exact counterexample: the proposed measurements can be identical in two well-posed linear systems even though the same intervention helps one and harms the other. The measurements therefore cannot, by themselves, certify the structural assumption that makes the reconstruction correct.
This investigation provides a substantive candidate and a falsification boundary—not a breakthrough claim.
1. Selecting the decision—and confronting existing solutions
Why I did not continue the isotope-history route
I examined Astra at repository revision 846f185763ed3a27310fad4ce9471ba324d1e5f4. Its N820 record already distinguishes neutral isotope ancestry from chemical-state identification and causal intervention. It also records a competitive failure: an investigated chemical-provenance decision was already addressed by established tracing and deliberate population manipulation. Repackaging temporal-label reconstruction would therefore not satisfy this mission. 
I instead pursued this decision:
How should many local controls of a physical network be adjusted to improve a measured output when the network contains unknown couplings and components that prevent ordinary reciprocal backpropagation?

The intended application class is adaptive physical computing and wave-control hardware. However, recovering a gradient is only an intermediate capability. A consequential result would require showing that it improves an actual control or computation task at lower total physical cost.
The baseline is already strong
Physical adjoint experiments already obtain gradients through reciprocal systems without identifying every internal component. Guillamon and colleagues demonstrated this in microwave networks using measured forward and adjoint fields. Their method incorporates the actual network response rather than relying solely on a digital twin. arXiv
Nor is nonreciprocity a universal barrier. The September 2026 revision of Unifying Physical Backpropagation gives sufficient conditions for same-hardware gradients in some nonreciprocal systems through more general transformations called intertwining relations. An August 2026 experimental report also demonstrates time-domain physical adjoint optimization in an RLC network. These results rule out a broad novelty claim such as “physical gradients without physically reversing the system.” arXiv
Direct action comparisons are another serious competitor. When only a few settings are available, applying them in randomized, interleaved order and measuring the objective can answer the decision without recovering a gradient or identifying hidden mechanisms.
Even with many controls, the comparison is not necessarily against two experiments per coordinate. A two-sided random-direction estimate uses two objective evaluations per sampled direction, although it does not recover an exact full gradient from those two readings. Multiplexed-gradient approaches are also established, with an integrated-photonic experimental implementation reported in 2025. arXiv
The candidate therefore needs a narrower advantage: acquiring many useful sensitivities through an unknown, locally nonreciprocal system while keeping the control settings fixed during acquisition. Whether that saves time, energy, or intervention cost must be measured—not inferred from the algebra.
2. The candidate: reconstruct the adjoint from measured port responses
I will call the construction port-completed adjoint acquisition, as a descriptive label rather than a novelty claim.
The quantity the decision needs
Consider a real, static linear system
\[
A(p)x=b(p),
\]
where \(p\) contains adjustable controls and \(x\) is the physical state. Let the objective be
\[
J(p)=F(x(p),p).
\]
Define
\[
c=\nabla_xF,
\qquad
A^T\lambda=c.
\]
The adjoint vector \(\lambda\) gives
\[
\frac{dJ}{dp_j}
=
F_{p_j}
+
\lambda^T
\left(
b_{p_j}-A_{p_j}x
\right).
\]
This is established adjoint sensitivity analysis, not a new identity. Its experimental difficulty is obtaining the response of \(A^T\) when the available apparatus implements \(A\). cseweb.ucsd.edu
For example, if \(p_j\) is the conductance of an edge between nodes \(i\) and \(\ell\), write \(v_j=e_i-e_\ell\). Then
\[
A_{p_j}=v_jv_j^T,
\]
and, with no explicit source or objective dependence,
\[
\frac{dJ}{dp_j}
=
-\left(v_j^Tx\right)
 \left(v_j^T\lambda\right).
\]
Only the forward and adjoint fields at that edge’s endpoints are needed.
The structural assumption
Suppose all nonreciprocity is confined to \(k\) known, accessible port coordinates. After ordering those coordinates first,
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&E\\
E^T&C
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad C=C^T.
\]
Here \(B\) may be nonreciprocal and unknown. The surrounding network \(C\) and its reciprocal coupling \(E\) may also be unknown.
Assume \(A\) and \(C\) are invertible. A positive-definite symmetric part \((A+A^T)/2\) is one sufficient mathematical condition.
This is substantially stronger than assuming that nonreciprocity has low algebraic rank:
The coordinates through which it acts must be physically known and accessible. An unknown low-rank factorization is not an experimental interface.

Let \(P\) inject signals at those port coordinates.
What is actually measured
At one fixed, loaded operating condition, apply:
\[
b,\quad Pe_1,\ldots,Pe_k,\quad c.
\]
These are \(k+2\) source profiles, including the ordinary forward run. Measure the required components of
\[
x=A^{-1}b,
\qquad
Y=A^{-1}P,
\qquad
z=A^{-1}c.
\]
From the port measurements form
\[
H=P^TY,
\qquad
q=P^Tz.
\]
The matrix \(H\) is the loaded port response. It is measured with the actual unknown surrounding network attached; it is not an isolated-component calibration.
Now solve two \(k\)-dimensional systems:
\[
H\alpha=q,
\]
\[
H\beta=(H^T-H)\alpha.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\lambda=z+Y\beta.}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda
=
z+
YH^{-1}(H^T-H)H^{-1}P^Tz.
}
\]
The implementation uses linear solves, not explicit inverses.
Although this expression denotes a full vector, only the sensed rows of \(Y\) and \(z\) are required to recover those same rows of \(\lambda\). Unobserved interior coordinates need not be reconstructed.
There is also a physical synthesis interpretation. Applying the additional source
\[
c+P\beta
\]
to the original network produces the adjoint field itself:
\[
A^{-1}(c+P\beta)=\lambda.
\]
That optional physical realization costs one additional settled experiment. Digital combination of the already measured responses does not.
Why it works
Eliminating the hidden coordinates produces the Schur complement
\[
S=B-EC^{-1}E^T,
\qquad H=S^{-1}.
\]
For the measured forward response \(z=A^{-1}c\), its port coordinates satisfy
\[
Sz_P=c_P-EC^{-1}c_Q.
\]
For the desired adjoint response, the hidden block and reciprocal cross-couplings are unchanged, while the port Schur complement is transposed:
\[
S^T\lambda_P=c_P-EC^{-1}c_Q.
\]
Therefore
\[
\lambda_P=H^TH^{-1}z_P.
\]
The required change in port field is consequently
\[
\lambda_P-z_P
=
(H^T-H)H^{-1}z_P.
\]
A port injection \(P\beta\) creates the port response \(H\beta\). Choosing \(\beta\) as above produces exactly this change. Reciprocity of the hidden block then ensures that its induced change is also the correct one.
The unusual operational feature is that the unknown nonreciprocal component values do not have to be identified separately. Their relevant effect is obtained from the loaded port measurements.
The underlying mathematics is nevertheless a composition of Schur complements and low-rank inverse-update machinery. Standard mathematical ingredients do not exclude operational novelty, but they certainly do not establish it. SIAM
3. What would make this a physically obtainable operation?
A plausible first realization is a low-voltage, low-frequency linear circuit with a deliberately confined nonreciprocal module, permanently connected injection channels, and buffered voltage readout.
That is an acquisition path, not an implemented apparatus. Five requirements remain essential.
Required input	Acquisition path	Main failure mode
Port response \(H\) and sensed responses \(Y\)	Calibrated injections at each declared port	Switching injection equipment changes the network being measured
Objective response \(z\)	Physically apply the source profile \(c\)	The required source is mathematically computable but not physically injectable
Local control derivatives	Known conductance law or same-regime actuator calibration	A knob changes hidden couplings as well as the intended component
Reciprocal exterior	Establish by construction, or independently qualify	Unobserved nonreciprocity outside the declared ports
Common operating condition	Interleave and repeat complete measurement blocks	Drift, heating, saturation, or correlated measurement errors


The fourth requirement is especially important. The protocol measures a correction conditional on the exterior being reciprocal. It does not automatically establish that exterior reciprocity.
The photothermal warning therefore survives in a different form: exact inference can fail when measurements and calibration do not establish the joint relationship that the inference requires. Separately accurate source calibration, voltage measurements, and component specifications do not prove that every profile interrogated the same operator \(A\).
There is also a resource distinction. With \(s\) sensed coordinates, the uncompressed protocol collects
\[
(k+2)s
\]
scalar field readings per operating point. It requires \(O(sk)\) stored response values and small-matrix processing, followed by local gradient calculations. The port response generally changes when the controls change, so acquisition must usually be repeated.
A small number of excitation profiles is not the same as low total experimental cost.
The theorem above is real and static. A radio-frequency implementation requires coherent phase information and correct complex differentiation conventions. Arbitrary nonlinear, chaotic, or time-varying dynamics are not covered by this result.

I also did not use fluctuations in those objective traces as a noise calibration for the new estimator. Objective variability is not a measurement of the joint voltage-response errors entering \(H\), \(Y\), and \(z\).
5. Synthetic tests: exact recovery, noise amplification, and misspecification
I implemented the acquisition protocol separately from the simulated plant.
Each test network had 64 state coordinates, 30 tunable conductance edges, four declared nonreciprocal port coordinates, and 17 sensed coordinates. The estimator received only the measured source-to-sensor responses. The remaining 47 state coordinates were hidden from it.
The full matrix was used only by the simulator and the independent reference calculation.

The uncorrected calculation—treating the nonreciprocal apparatus as though it were reciprocal—had a median gradient error of approximately 19.6% on the 100 noiseless networks.
These results establish that the implementation reproduces the conditional mathematics and that the correction can matter substantially. They also show that modest raw measurement error can become much larger gradient error.
The noise was a deliberately assumed Gaussian field-noise model. Those percentages are not experimentally calibrated error bars or guarantees for an apparatus.
The resource accounting is equally important. This example used six settled source profiles and 102 scalar field readings. Complete central finite differences for 30 controls would use 60 objective readings.
Calling that a tenfold speedup would be unjustified: the quantities measured, sensor infrastructure, and settling costs differ. Moreover, an effective random-direction or multiplexed method need not acquire a complete coordinate-wise gradient.
6. An exact counterexample to automatic experimental closure
The sharpest limitation is not numerical noise.
The following are constructed linear alternatives—not models fitted to the published experiment.
Let \(e_i\) denote coordinate vectors and define
\[
A_\pm=I\pm e_2e_4^T,
\]
with
\[
b=e_1,\qquad c=e_2,\qquad P=[e_3,e_5].
\]
Both matrices have positive-definite symmetric parts, with smallest eigenvalue \(1/2\). They are not singularity or instability tricks.
Every response acquired by the proposed protocol is identical:
\[
A_+^{-1}b=A_-^{-1}b=e_1,
\]
\[
A_+^{-1}c=A_-^{-1}c=e_2,
\]
\[
A_+^{-1}P=A_-^{-1}P=P.
\]
This remains true even with complete five-coordinate readout, rather than partial sensing.
But consider adding a conductance in direction
\[
v=e_1-e_4,
\qquad
A_\pm(p)=A_\pm+pvv^T.
\]
For the measured output
\[
J_\pm(p)=e_2^TA_\pm(p)^{-1}e_1,
\]
direct calculation gives
\[
\boxed{
J_+(p)=-\frac{p}{1+2p},
\qquad
J_-(p)=+\frac{p}{1+2p}.
}
\]
Increasing \(p\) helps one system and harms the other.
The port-completion protocol observes \(H=I\) and would infer no correction. It fails here because the actual nonreciprocity lies outside the declared ports.
This is not a counterexample to the theorem. It is a counterexample to the claim that the theorem’s structural promise has been acquired by those measurements.
One additional probe at the missing coordinate, or a direct comparison after changing \(p\), separates the alternatives.
That distinction is the substantive closure lesson: an experiment may acquire all numerical quantities appearing in a formula while failing to acquire the assumption that makes the formula decision-relevant.
7. A cheaper competing operation for a few decisions
The investigation also exposes a way to avoid making a full-gradient problem unnecessarily difficult.
For one proposed control direction, differentiate
\[
Ax=b
\]
to obtain
\[
A\,\delta x=\delta b-\delta A\,x.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\delta J
=
F_p\,\delta p+
c^TA^{-1}(\delta b-\delta A\,x).
\]
When the source
\[
s=\delta b-\delta A\,x
\]
can actually be synthesized, a single forward tangent-source experiment gives that directional response. Neither a physical transpose nor a complete gradient is necessary.
This competitor has its own physical requirement: access to the support of \(s\). It may be unavailable when the actuator locations can be sensed but not independently driven. Nevertheless, for a few feasible candidate actions it can dominate the port-completion procedure.
This is why the decision must remain primary. “Recover every sensitivity” is not automatically the right scientific task.
8. Novelty, significance, and the closure hypothesis
What survived
A precise conditional acquisition operation survived:
Unknown reciprocal surroundings can be eliminated from an adjoint-acquisition problem by measuring the loaded response of the accessible nonreciprocal ports.

The operation does more than reinterpret an existing measurement conservatively: it prescribes additional injections, produces an additional response quantity, and can synthesize an adjoint field using the original network.
Its potentially distinctive feature is the measured-port construction without separate identification of the nonreciprocal component values or a supplied global transformation.
What did not survive
I did not establish that this particular experimental composition is historically new. Targeted searches are insufficient to settle priority, especially given longstanding circuit-adjoint methods and recent generalizations of physical backpropagation. IEEE Xplore
I also did not establish that a consequential application has all of the required properties simultaneously: a small accessible nonreciprocal region, inexpensive field readout, injectable objective sources, trustworthy local actuator derivatives, and a meaningful advantage over direct or multiplexed action tests.
The decisive experiment is therefore a same-apparatus, matched-cost comparison, not another idealized simulation. Port completion would need to improve held-out actual control decisions against tuned randomized or multiplexed alternatives, accounting for acquisition time, source energy, readout cost, drift, and control-setting changes. Deliberately introducing undeclared nonreciprocity would test whether the claimed operating scope is detectable or merely assumed.
What this says about Astra’s hypothesis
The hypothesis was productive in a limited sense: asking what could physically be injected and measured led to a concrete replacement for part of an unknown internal model.
But this run does not establish that missing information interfaces are the dominant explanation for the gap between AI mathematical progress and physical invention. The strongest relevant literature already exploits that principle, and this candidate’s remaining obstacles are structural qualification, experimental integration, and competitive decision performance—not merely finding another mathematical representation.
For Astra, the defensible classification is: an unvalidated, scoped acquisition candidate with an exact proof and explicit failure boundary. It should not be promoted to a major discovery on the strength of numerical agreement alone.
