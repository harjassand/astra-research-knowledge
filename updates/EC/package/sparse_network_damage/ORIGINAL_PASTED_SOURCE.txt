A classical damage-sensitivity oracle that remains accurate near disconnection
The strongest result from this investigation is a classical data structure for repeatedly evaluating sparse damage to a transport network.
After preprocessing a connected, weighted graph and a fixed pair of terminals, it answers:
What is the new terminal resistance when any \(k\) selected conductances are weakened by arbitrary amounts, including complete removal?

The construction gives relative accuracy even arbitrarily close to disconnection, correctly identifies infinite resistance, and supports arbitrarily many adaptively chosen hypothetical scenarios. Each query involves only the selected edges and a small linear system—not another solve on the entire graph.
Writing
\[
q=O\!\left(
\frac{k\log(em/k)+\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}
\right),
\]
the principal costs are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Expected preprocessing}&:\quad \widetilde O(mq),\\
\text{Stored numerical values}&:\quad O(mq),\\
\text{Worst-case query work}&:\quad O(qk^2+k^3),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]
with the finite-bit factors specified below. Here \(m\) is the number of edges, \(\varepsilon\) is the requested relative error, and \(1-\delta\) is the probability that every admissible query is answered correctly.
The mechanism is to preserve conservation laws exactly while approximating circulating flows. This prevents numerical errors from creating fictitious conductance across a nearly broken cut.
The proof below supports this algorithmic construction. Historical originality and practical superiority remain unestablished. The attached code checks the identities and failure cases using dense reference calculations; it is not a production implementation of the near-linear preprocessing bound.
1. Target selection and prior-art boundary
I screened three non-quantum directions. In stochastic chemical simulation, eliminating repeated visits to trapping states is already an established kinetic-path-sampling operation. In parametric partial differential equations, random sketches of reduced solution and residual spaces are also established. Neither operation, by itself, was a credible new discovery. arXiv
Sparse network damage exposed a more specific opportunity: low-rank updating is inexpensive only after acquiring the necessary inverse information, and approximate inverse information can become unreliable near a cut.
The baseline is already strong. Spielman–Srivastava give near-linear preprocessing for static effective-resistance queries. Ronellenfitsch and collaborators give cycle-space formulas for multiple outages and arbitrary changes in line reactance. Thus neither static resistance sketching, cycle duality, nor the Woodbury identity is claimed as new here. arXiv
The potentially new content is their operational combination with exact conservation in both the sketch and the approximate reference current, yielding one compressed structure that remains valid for all sparse weakening scenarios, without a lower bound on the conductance remaining after damage.
The scoped repository check used Astra revision 142e27d7eb0a0590a8fc649c63a95a818dca95aa and OpenAI search results at fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. It did not establish exhaustive novelty clearance.  
2. Exact computational contract
The input is an explicit connected undirected graph \(G\), with \(n\) vertices, \(m\) edges, positive rational conductances \(w_e\), and distinct fixed terminals \(s,t\).
A query specifies a set \(S\) of at most \(k\) edges and rational damage fractions
\[
0<d_e\le1,\qquad e\in S.
\]
The modified conductances are
\[
w_e'=
\begin{cases}
(1-d_e)w_e,&e\in S,\\
w_e,&e\notin S.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
A zero damage fraction can simply be omitted.
Let \(c_d\) denote the terminal resistance. It is \(+\infty\) when \(s\) and \(t\) become disconnected; disconnected components containing neither terminal do not force an infinite answer.
Derived theorem
For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\) and \(0<\delta<1\), the construction produces a randomized data structure with the costs in (1), such that, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), simultaneously for every query:
\[
\boxed{
c_d<\infty
\implies
(1-\varepsilon)c_d\le\widehat c_d\le(1+\varepsilon)c_d,
}
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\widehat c_d=\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_d=\infty.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The success event covers all supports of size at most \(k\) and all their rational damage fractions. Consequently, later queries may depend on earlier answers—or on the stored sketch itself—without accumulating an additional failure probability.
This is a sensitivity data structure: each query modifies the same reference graph hypothetically. It is not an assertion about unrestricted online updates to an evolving graph.
3. Replace a dangerous subtraction by a positive semidefinite problem
Ground one vertex. Let \(B\in\mathbb R^{(n-1)\times m}\) be the oriented incidence matrix and
\[
W=\operatorname{diag}(w_e),\qquad L=BWB^\top.
\]
Let \(f\) be the grounded unit \(s\)-to-\(t\) load. Define
\[
u=L^{-1}f,\qquad
z=B^\top u,\qquad
j_*=Wz,\qquad
c=f^\top L^{-1}f.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(j_*\) is the electrical current and \(c\) is the undamaged terminal resistance.
Introduce
\[
\Pi=I-W^{1/2}B^\top L^{-1}BW^{1/2},
\]
the orthogonal projector onto normalized circulating flows, and
\[
\Gamma
=
W^{-1/2}\Pi W^{-1/2}
=
W^{-1}-B^\top L^{-1}B.
\tag{6}
\]
For a query, put
\[
D_d=\operatorname{diag}_{e\in S}
\left(\frac{1-d_e}{d_ew_e}\right),
\qquad
M_d=\Gamma_{SS}+D_d.
\tag{7}
\]
Both summands are positive semidefinite.
Exact damage formula
When the damaged resistance is finite,
\[
\boxed{
c_d=c+z_S^\top M_d^+z_S.
}
\tag{8}
\]
It is infinite precisely when
\[
z_S\notin\operatorname{range}(M_d).
\tag{9}
\]
For strictly partial damage, the Woodbury identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_d
&=c+
z_S^\top
\left[
\operatorname{diag}_{e\in S}\frac1{d_ew_e}
-B_S^\top L^{-1}B_S
\right]^{-1}z_S\\
&=c+z_S^\top(\Gamma_{SS}+D_d)^{-1}z_S.
\end{aligned}
\]
Complete removals follow by replacing \(d_e\) with \((1-\tau)d_e\) and taking \(\tau\downarrow0\). The quadratic form converges to the pseudoinverse expression when the load is compatible with the limiting matrix, and diverges otherwise. The same limit on the graph side gives finite resistance exactly when the terminal current remains routable.
Equation (8) is a dual low-rank-update identity, not the claimed discovery. Its useful feature is the positive semidefinite sum in (7), rather than subtracting an approximate transfer-current matrix from a diagonal matrix. Related dual outage identities are prior work. arXiv
Why small absolute matrix error is insufficient
Take two parallel unit-conductance edges between the terminals and retain a fraction \(a>0\) of each. Then
\[
c_d=\frac1{2a}.
\]
The relevant cycle matrix has a zero eigenvalue. Adding a spurious numerical floor \(\tau I\) to that matrix changes the leading inverse response from order \(a^{-1}\) to order \((a+\tau)^{-1}\).
For \(a\ll\tau\), the relative error approaches one. At \(a=0\), the same mistake can report finite resistance for disconnected terminals.
The construction therefore needs multiplicative approximation with exact nullspaces, not an absolute-error estimate whose tolerance was chosen before the damage severity was known.
4. Uniformly compress the cycle information
First consider exact arithmetic. Draw a random sign matrix
\[
R\in\mathbb R^{q\times m},
\qquad
R_{ij}\in\{-1/\sqrt q,+1/\sqrt q\},
\]
and define
\[
Y_0=R\Pi W^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
For a fixed support \(S\), the relevant vectors lie in
\[
\mathcal U_S
=
\operatorname{range}(\Pi W^{-1/2}E_S),
\]
a subspace of dimension at most \(k\).
A sign-matrix subspace embedding, followed by a union bound over the at most \(\binom mk\) maximal supports, gives
\[
(1-\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
\preceq
Y_{0,S}^\top Y_{0,S}
\preceq
(1+\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
\tag{11}
\]
simultaneously for every \(|S|\le k\), using
\[
q=O\!\left(
\theta^{-2}
[k\log(em/k)+\log(1/\delta)]
\right).
\tag{12}
\]
The dimension bound follows directly from a constant-radius net of the unit sphere in each \(\mathcal U_S\): the net has \(\exp(O(k))\) points, while each fixed-vector norm estimate fails with probability \(\exp(-\Omega(q\theta^2))\). Random-projection resistance sketches are established ingredients; the application here is to these sparse restrictions of the complementary cycle projector. SIAM
The remaining issue is whether (11) survives finite-precision acquisition of \(Y_0\). Ordinary rounding does not suffice.
Exact conservation repair
Choose a spanning tree and let \(T\) be its routing operator:
\[
BT=I.
\]
Applying \(T\) means accumulating vertex imbalances along the tree; it costs \(O(n)\), without storing a dense matrix.
For a column \(r\) corresponding to one row of \(R\), form a dyadic approximation
\[
a\approx W^{1/2}r,
\]
and approximately solve
\[
Lv=Ba.
\]
Instead of storing the provisional flow \(a-WB^\top v\), store
\[
\boxed{
j=a-WB^\top v-T(Ba-Lv).
}
\tag{13}
\]
Then, in exact arithmetic on the stored finite numbers,
\[
\boxed{Bj=0.}
\tag{14}
\]
Set the corresponding row of the implemented sketch \(Y\) to \(j^\top W^{-1}\).
This uses one Laplacian solve per row, followed by sparse operations and a tree traversal. Numerically stable, finite-precision, nearly-linear Laplacian solvers are established algorithms and are charged as preprocessing subroutines. arXiv
Why this repair gives relative—not merely absolute—accuracy
Let
\[
E=(Y-Y_0)W^{1/2}.
\]
Both the ideal and repaired normalized rows lie in the cycle space, so
\[
E=E\Pi.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore, if the solves and square roots are accurate enough that \(\|E\|\le a_0\), then for every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(Y-Y_0)x\|
&=\|E\Pi W^{-1/2}x\|\\
&\le a_0\sqrt{x^\top\Gamma x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This remains small even when \(x^\top\Gamma x\) is tiny. In particular, it is exactly zero on every true null direction.
Combining (16) with the ideal embedding yields
\[
\boxed{
(1-\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
\preceq
\widehat\Gamma_S:=Y_S^\top Y_S
\preceq
(1+\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
}
\tag{17}
\]
after reserving constant fractions of the error budget.
Thus the stored sketch has precisely the right local kernels on the common success event. No post-damage spectral-gap bound is used.
5. The reference current must also conserve exactly
Correctly sketching \(\Gamma\) is not sufficient. An approximate load vector can accidentally inject a tiny current into a component that is about to become isolated. Its apparent energy can then diverge even though the physical terminal resistance remains finite.
The repair is to approximate the feasible current, not independently rounded potential drops.
Compute an approximate baseline potential \(v\), and form
\[
\boxed{
\widehat j
=
WB^\top v+T(f-Lv).
}
\tag{18}
\]
Then
\[
B\widehat j=f
\tag{19}
\]
exactly.
Choose the solve accuracy so that
\[
\widehat j^\top W^{-1}\widehat j
\le(1+\eta^2)c.
\tag{20}
\]
Define
\[
\widehat z=W^{-1}\widehat j.
\]
A uniform feasible-current lemma
Because \(\widehat j-j_*\) is a circulation, electrical optimality gives the exact identity
\[
\widehat j^\top W^{-1}\widehat j-c
=
\|W^{-1/2}(\widehat j-j_*)\|^2.
\tag{21}
\]
For every support \(S\), let
\[
e_S=\widehat z_S-z_S.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
e_S\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_{SS}),
\qquad
e_S^\top\Gamma_{SS}^+e_S\le\eta^2c.
}
\tag{22}
\]
Proof. Put
\[
h=W^{-1/2}(\widehat j-j_*).
\]
Conservation implies \(\Pi h=h\), and (21) gives \(\|h\|^2\le\eta^2c\).
For
\[
A_S=E_S^\top W^{-1/2}\Pi,
\]
we have
\[
\Gamma_{SS}=A_SA_S^\top,\qquad e_S=A_Sh.
\]
The minimum-norm solution characterization gives
\[
e_S^\top(A_SA_S^\top)^+e_S\le\|h\|^2.
\]
This proves both claims. ∎
Because \(M_d\succeq\Gamma_{SS}\), the variational characterization of inverse quadratic forms further gives
\[
e_S^\top M_d^+e_S\le\eta^2c.
\tag{23}
\]
This estimate has no factor involving the smallest positive eigenvalue of the damaged graph or of the selected cycle matrix.
Exact detection of disconnected terminals
Every vector in \(\ker M_d\) also lies in \(\ker\Gamma_{SS}\). Equation (22) therefore implies
\[
\widehat z_S\perp\ker M_d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z_S\perp\ker M_d.
\tag{24}
\]
Together with the exact kernel equality from (17), this makes the small linear-system consistency test detect the correct infinite-resistance cases.
Relative error for finite resistance
Write
\[
q_d=z_S^\top M_d^+z_S,\qquad c_d=c+q_d.
\]
By (23),
\[
\left|
\widehat z_S^\top M_d^+\widehat z_S-q_d
\right|
\le
2\eta\sqrt{cq_d}+\eta^2c
\le
(\eta+\eta^2)c_d.
\tag{25}
\]
Adding \(D_d\succeq0\) to (17) preserves its relative bounds:
\[
(1-\theta)M_d
\preceq
\widehat M_d:=\widehat\Gamma_S+D_d
\preceq
(1+\theta)M_d.
\tag{26}
\]
On their common range, inversion changes quadratic forms by at most a factor \(1/(1\pm\theta)\).
Finally, approximate the positive scalar energy
\[
\widehat j^\top W^{-1}\widehat j
\]
to obtain a stored baseline \(\widehat c\). Equations (20), (25), and (26), with internal tolerances chosen as small constant multiples of \(\varepsilon\), prove (3).
The key point is that the baseline approximation error is measured in circulation energy, the same geometry that controls the damage correction.
6. The actual query algorithm and finite-bit costs
The online algorithm is short:
Read the selected edge columns of Y and the selected entries of z_hat.
Form M_hat = Y_Sᵀ Y_S + diag((1-d_e)/(d_e w_e)).
Solve M_hat x = z_hat_S using exact rational linear algebra.

If the system is inconsistent, return infinity.
Otherwise return c_hat + z_hat_Sᵀ x, rounded to the output precision.

When the system is consistent, any solution gives the same quadratic form; computing an explicit Moore–Penrose inverse is unnecessary.
Input acquisition and rounding
The graph, weights, terminal labels, and each damage scenario are explicit inputs. Reading them is charged.
For convenient finite arithmetic, initially replace each positive rational weight by a dyadic number satisfying
\[
(1-\tau)w_e\le\bar w_e\le(1+\tau)w_e,
\]
with \(\tau\) a small fraction of \(\varepsilon\).
This is safe uniformly over all damage queries, because multiplication by \(1-d_e\ge0\) preserves the same inequalities. Zero conductances after complete failure remain exactly zero, so connectivity is unchanged. The resistance error from this rounding is bounded by the corresponding multiplicative energy inequalities.
Use these dyadic weights consistently throughout preprocessing. Store the dyadic physical flows \(j\), rather than independently rounding their divided values \(j_e/\bar w_e\). The divisions needed by a query are performed as rational arithmetic.
Required solve precision
Let \(\rho=w_{\max}/w_{\min}\). The tree repair satisfies
\[
\|W^{-1/2}T\|\,\|BW^{1/2}\|
\le n\sqrt{2m\rho}.
\]
Thus, with
\[
C_T=1+n\sqrt{2m\rho},
\]
a baseline potential error bounded by
\[
\|v-u\|_L\le\frac{\eta}{C_T}\sqrt c
\]
is sufficient for (20).
For each sketch row, solve accurately enough that its normalized flow error is \(O(\theta/\sqrt q)\). Summing those errors in Frobenius norm gives the operator bound used in (16).
These tolerances require additional bits proportional to logarithms of \(n,m,\rho,q,\varepsilon^{-1}\), not to the reciprocal of a damaged-network eigenvalue.
The solve quality can be checked from a residual. For example, the grounded Laplacian satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\min}(L)\ge w_{\min}/n^2,
\]
so
\[
\|v-L^{-1}b\|_L^2
\le
\frac{n^2}{w_{\min}}\|Lv-b\|_2^2.
\tag{27}
\]
A suitably conservative residual threshold is explicitly computable. Solver calls can be repeated until this certificate passes; this is why the preprocessing bound is stated as expected work.
Bit, memory, and randomness bounds
Let \(b_{\rm in}\) bound the initial coefficient bit lengths. Working numbers need bit lengths polynomial in
\[
b_{\rm in}+\log\frac{mnq}{\varepsilon\delta}.
\]
The only large linear systems are the \(q+1\) preprocessing Laplacian solves. Their cost, the \(O(mq)\) sparse/tree operations, and coefficient arithmetic give
\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm bit}
=
\widetilde O(mq)\,
\operatorname{poly}(b_{\rm in}),
}
\tag{28}
\]
with the displayed logarithmic precision parameters included in the suppressed factors.
Storage is \(O(mq)\) working words. The sketch uses \(mq\) independent fair signs; there is no uncharged random-function oracle. Standard solver randomness is included in preprocessing.
If a query’s fractions have at most \(b_d\) bits, forming its Gram matrix costs \(O(qk^2)\) arithmetic operations. Fraction-free elimination handles the remaining \(k\)-dimensional systems with bit complexity
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm bit}
=
O(qk^2+k^3)\,
\operatorname{poly}(k,b_{\rm work}+b_d).
}
\tag{29}
\]
There is no numerical factor \(1/\min_e(1-d_e)\). Specifying a retained fraction such as \(2^{-200}\) costs roughly 200 input bits, and the small rational solve pays for those bits.
Query-time randomness is unnecessary. Checking exact conservation costs \(O(mq)\) preprocessing work; verifying the embedding over every support by exhaustive enumeration is not assumed free. Its uniform guarantee is probabilistic.
7. What computation this makes cheaper
A concrete workload is a supplied contact-network model of a conductive composite: fix its electrodes and undamaged conductances, then evaluate many candidate sets of broken or weakened contacts.
Network/percolation models are used to predict electromechanical resistance in CNT/polymer composites and compare it with sensor measurements. The algorithm here accelerates the prescribed network calculations; it does not establish that a particular network model captures every aspect of the material. Apollo
For \(N_{\rm scen}\) explicit scenarios, the arithmetic work becomes
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left(
\frac{mk}{\varepsilon^2}
+
\frac{N_{\rm scen}k^3}{\varepsilon^2}
\right),
}
\tag{30}
\]
plus input and output bit costs.
For fixed \(k,\varepsilon\), this is nearly linear in the graph size plus the number of requested answers. The result is most useful when many different sparse scenarios must be assessed against one reference network.
Competent comparisons
Method	Relevant cost or limitation
Solve each damaged Laplacian directly	Near-linear work in \(m\) per scenario, rather than a cubic dense solve.
Reuse a base factorization and apply low-rank updates	Avoids refactorization, but still needs the selected inverse interactions; acquiring uncached columns requires additional solves.
Materialize the complete edge-interaction matrix	Supports small updates, but explicitly storing that matrix requires \(\Theta(m^2)\) entries.
Construction here	\(q+1\) base solves, \(O(mq)\) stored values, and \(O(qk^2+k^3)\) query arithmetic, with uniform near-disconnection accuracy.


Cycle-space outage methods already reduce repeated calculations and can be particularly effective when the graph has few independent cycles. The comparison above is not a claim to dominate those especially favorable graphs. arXiv
The August 2026 partially dynamic all-pairs result is another important comparator: it gives approximately constant query time after a total update budget of \(\widetilde O(n^2/\varepsilon^7)\), for monotone insertions or deletions and polynomially bounded weights. It handles arbitrary terminal pairs, unlike the fixed-terminal construction here. Conversely, it is not the same contract as unlimited, reset-to-reference hypothetical damage queries. arXiv
These are comparisons of proved interfaces and upper bounds—not a lower-bound separation from every specialized implementation.
Observable estimation is also charged
The normalized residual conductance
\[
F(d)=
\begin{cases}
c/c_d,&c_d<\infty,\\
0,&c_d=\infty
\end{cases}
\]
lies in \([0,1]\).
Given an actual sampler producing finite descriptions of damage scenarios with \(|S|\le k\), its expectation can be estimated to additive error \(a\) using
\[
O(a^{-2}\log(1/\zeta))
\]
sampled scenarios. Build the oracle to accuracy \(O(a)\), evaluate and clamp each estimated \(F(d)\), and average.
The complete cost is preprocessing plus that many damage-sampling and query operations. With the present sketch, the query portion can scale as \(\widetilde O(k^3a^{-4})\); that statistical-and-sketch accuracy cost is not hidden.
For an explicitly supplied candidate list, selecting the largest estimated resistance requires processing the list. This does not automatically search all \(\binom mk\) possible damage sets or resolve differences smaller than the approximation tolerance.
8. Falsification tests and remaining scope
I executed dense reference diagnostics, including:
- 756 exact rational damage cases, with 16 infinite-resistance cases and 18 cases where the graph disconnected but the selected terminal resistance remained finite.
- Exact conservation restoration after rounding potentials to 20 fractional bits; all 63 tested local kernels remained correct.
- A randomized sketch checked on 9,919 supports and 500 damage queries.
- Exact near-cut cases with retained conductances \(2^{-20}\), \(2^{-80}\), and \(2^{-200}\).
In the adversarial triangle example, a conservation-preserving Gram perturbation of about 2% and an approximate feasible baseline flow kept resistance error below 0.86% down to retained fraction \(2^{-200}\). An unconstrained load approximation instead produced a spurious relative error above \(10^{54}\) for a pair whose true resistance remained close to one. Adding a \(10^{-12}\) false floor to a null direction almost completely hid the diverging resistance of a different terminal pair.
These are checks of the mechanism, not performance measurements. The randomized diagnostic even uses more sketch rows than graph edges to inspect the algebra clearly; it is not evidence of memory compression at scale.

The theorem is restricted to weakening at most \(k\) conductances relative to a fixed reference, with fixed terminals. It does not yet cover arbitrary strengthening and weakening, unrestricted evolving damage, nonlinear constitutive laws, full mechanical fracture dynamics, or inexpensive output of every internal current. A full field still has a graph-sized output cost.
The result also concerns the exact finite network supplied as input. It does not acquire contact conductances experimentally, infer the damage pattern, or eliminate model uncertainty.
Established outcome
The surviving computational principle is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Approximate circulating-flow geometry,
but preserve current conservation exactly.}
}
\]
Applied to sparse network damage, it gives a compressed response calculation whose accuracy does not collapse when the queried network approaches disconnection. The crucial improvement over a generic inverse sketch is that both the compressed matrix and the approximate load retain the exact cut constraints.
This is a substantive classical algorithmic construction independent of the earlier quantum-simulation work. The remaining uncertainties are priority for the combined theorem and its realized performance against optimized solvers—not a hidden assumption that the damaged network stays well-conditioned.
