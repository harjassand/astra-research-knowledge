RECTIFIED KELVIN PUMP: AN OPEN MATERIAL GATE, NOT AN ACHIEVED TECHNOLOGY
Independent technology genesis investigation, 2026-10-12

Outcome

The potentially consequential operation is to capture undersaturated vapor in
an electrically switched nanopore, recover electrical charge, and eject the
water as liquid through a phase-resetting outlet. It could avoid paying to
evaporate an adsorbent's captured water. The sustained quantitative gate did
not identify a demonstrated material that performs the required complete
operation with pure water. The closest actual electrocapillary experiments
fail the air/water release state or rely on concentrated electrolyte and oil.

An optimistic continuum design nearly meets an aspirational 0.10 kWh/kg
all-system target at 25 C, 60% RH and 1 kg per square metre of total equipment
footprint per hour. This is a conditional calculation, not a performance
prediction. Its material states are unbuilt; ionic retention, hysteresis,
transport, manufacturing, and valve losses are unpriced, not zero. Novelty of
the complete proposed architecture is UNKNOWN. Field-controlled hydrophobic
nanopore wetting, electrowetting, and charge recovery are already known.

1. The proposed operation and its physical interface

Use an array of electrically addressable, short nanopores with two true
surface states. A hydrophilic capture state A is favorable to water relative
to vapor. A release state B is hydrophobic to water, including its receding
wetting behavior. Couple their vapor ends to a hydrophobic gas-permeable guard.
Their other ends feed a common hydrophobic outlet throat that can empty and
admit gas after delivery. Collect external droplets beyond that throat.

The complete cycle is:
  (i) With the outlet dry and hydraulically isolated from product, prepare
      state A. Admit humid air across macroscopic channels, not through
      nanometre pores as an assumed low-pressure airflow path.
 (ii) Condense water into the active pores. Reject the condensation heat.
(iii) Change the filled pore to state B while recovering returnable charge.
      A gas front from the vapor side displaces water toward the lower-entry-
      pressure outlet. Deliver water as liquid rather than evaporating it.
 (iv) Empty the outlet throat and restore a gas gap/capillary stop before
      preparing A again. The product droplet must remain outside the dry
      throat. Repeat with staggered arrays for steady macroscopic output.

The new proposed architecture is the conjunction of this phase-resetting
hydraulic rectifier and a reversible surface-state cycle, applied to vapor-to-
bulk-liquid separation. It is not a claim to invent voltage-induced wetting.
Its central advantage remains conditional on finding the surface and valve.

2. Thermodynamics and the charge-recovery trap

For a long cylindrical pore of radius r, surface area As and pore volume Vp,
As/Vp = 2/r. Define the true state-dependent free-energy difference

  Gamma_s = gamma_sv(s) - gamma_sl(s) = gamma_lv cos(theta_s).

This equality is only a continuum parameterization; a macroscopic apparent
contact angle is not proof that a subnanometre pore has this Gamma_s.
For vapor activity h at surface temperature Ts, the chemical-potential cost
per volume of pure water is

  g(h,Ts) = (R Ts / Vm) ln(1/h),      Vm = M/rho.

The filled-versus-empty grand potential in state A is approximately

  DeltaOmega_A = Vp g - As Gamma_A.

Capture requires 2 Gamma_A/r > g. Liquid ejection without an external pressure
source requires Gamma_B < 0 and an available gas front; its ideal pressure
scale is P_B = -2 Gamma_B/r. A pore fully immersed in liquid at both ends can
remain metastably wet even after becoming hydrophobic, because nucleating a
vapor cavity is a separate barrier. The proposed gas-side interface is needed
to avoid relying on spontaneous homogeneous cavitation.

At 25 C and h=.6, g=70.08 MPa. The reversible separation work for an infinitesimal
amount of water drawn from that vapor is g/rho=.01953 kWh/kg. This is not the
full device duty at finite vapor depletion and nonzero heat-transfer approach.
For r=1 nm, an off state at theta=90 degrees requires at least 35.04 mJ/m2 of
true surface-energy contrast. This is comparable to 0.5 C V^2 with C=.1 F/m2
and V near 1 V, which motivates examining actual electrocapillary materials.

Let G_s,dry and G_s,wet be the material free energies. Preparing A when dry
and resetting to B when wet costs, in the reversible surface-state limit,

  W_switch = (G_A,dry-G_B,dry) + (G_B,wet-G_A,wet)
           = As (Gamma_A-Gamma_B).

For an abrupt two-state cycle, this pays both the separation free energy and
the capture/ejection free energy dissipated by finite capillary driving.
It is not legitimate to count the adsorption free energy as free usable
work while declaring the material reset free. If the pressure of the release
stroke were recovered, it would require a real hydraulic energy converter.

A variable-capacitor example makes the electrical accounting explicit.
Let wet capacitance Cw exceed dry capacitance Cd by DeltaC (per active area),
and charge at voltages VL<VH. With reversible voltage ramps and ideal recovery,
the signed source work on four strokes is

  charge dry:     +0.5 Cd (VH^2-VL^2)
  fill at VH:    +DeltaC VH^2
  discharge wet: -0.5 Cw (VH^2-VL^2)
  drain at VL:   -DeltaC VL^2.

Their sum is +0.5 DeltaC (VH^2-VL^2), not zero. The filling stroke draws charge
at the high voltage. Ignoring this stroke manufactures an apparent benefit
from charge recovery. For genuine electrochemical surface chemistry the
correct operational observable is the complete signed integral integral V dQ,
plus any chemical reagents, reference/counter-electrode maintenance, leakage,
conversion losses, and unswitched auxiliary work. The capacitor model is not
a validated model of a particular surface.

3. A conditional numerical point and its narrow tolerances

Assume r=.7 nm, true capture theta_A=45 degrees, true release theta_B=100
degrees, gamma=.072 N/m, rho=997 kg/m3. Take air at 25 C, 60% RH; remove 20%
of its incoming water, so the exhaust is 48% RH at the inlet temperature.
Reject latent heat with a surface 3 K warmer than that incoming air. The
worst local relative humidity is .48 psat(25)/psat(28)=.4022, not .60.

The resulting ledger is:
  Chemical pressure at the depleted, warm interface     126.21 MPa
  Ideal capture drive                                 145.46 MPa
  Ideal release pressure                               35.72 MPa
  Required true surface contrast                       63.41 mJ/m2
  Reversible two-state switching work                    .05048 kWh/kg
  Source air volume                                    361.90 m3/kg
  Source fan, 100 Pa at efficiency .55                    .01828 kWh/kg
  Condensation heat                                      .67778 kWh(thermal)/kg
  Heat-rejection air, 3 K rise, latent heat only         682.84 m3/kg
  Heat fan, 50 Pa at efficiency .55, latent heat only      .01724 kWh/kg

For a hypothetical capacitor with DeltaC=.1 and Cd=.02 F/m2, VH=1.126 V and
VL=0, the signed electrical strokes per kilogram are +.01010, +.10096,
-.06058 and 0 kWh. Their sum is .05048. With 95% efficient charging and 95%
efficient recovery, conversion loss adds .00887 kWh/kg. Closing the extra
heat-rejection load from all electrical work gives .09735 kWh/kg, before
unknown material, valve, ionic separation, and control losses.

Alternatively, for a hypothetical one-electron surface change at 1 site/nm2,
there are .08573 switched electrons per captured water molecule. An excess
round-trip voltage of .05 V beyond the reversible surface work adds .00638
kWh/kg. This separate molecular-switch implementation totals .09479 kWh/kg
with the same fans and heat closure. At 3 sites/nm2 and .10 V excess it totals
.12751 kWh/kg. Do not add these site-based losses to the capacitor model as
though both describe one measured device.

Sensitivity is consequential. With the same assumed angles, r=.5 nm and a
3 K heat approach already totals .1217 kWh/kg in the capacitor case; r=1 nm
gives a negative capture margin of -24.4 MPa. At r=.7 nm and a 1 K heat approach,
heat rejection raises the capacitor estimate to .1401 kWh/kg. A 5 K approach
lowers it to .0893, but leaves only 2.3 MPa capture margin. That corresponds to
only about 1.6% radius tolerance before the ideal equilibrium threshold is
crossed, below a plausible uniform-manufacturing guarantee at this radius.
At 3 K, the largest admissible ideal radius is about .807 nm and the largest
capture angle about 52 degrees, before other margins are included.

At 1 kg/m2/hour, 60 s cycles require .01667 kg/m2 water inventory and 47,762 m2
of active internal surface per m2 footprint. A .5 nm coating at 1500 kg/m3
would alone weigh .0358 kg/m2. Its effectiveness, conformality and displacement
of the water cross-section are unproven; a 1.4 nm water diameter spans only a
few molecular layers. Kelvin theory and bulk viscosity/capacitance cannot be
treated as validated there. The product pressure, support ligaments, confined
ions, and repeated swelling impose additional stability requirements.

Heat duty is roughly .68-.78 kW per square metre at the target flux. A 3 K
approach needs approximately 230-260 W/K of heat-exchanger conductance per m2
collector. At an assumed air-side coefficient of 10-30 W/m2/K this implies
roughly 8-26 m2 of effective heat-transfer area per m2 collector. These fins,
fans, ducts and their entire equipment footprint belong in the rate and cost
denominator. The specified pressure drops are design assumptions, not a
demonstrated exchanger. Quoting a tiny active-membrane footprint would hide
this acquisition and heat interface.

4. Actual demonstrated surfaces do not close the gate

Nanoporous gold is the most direct real-material check requested by root.
Xue et al. measured potential-controlled imbibition in 1 M KOH with an
effective pore radius of 68 nm. The effective water contact angle changes
from about 70 degrees off to about 30 degrees at -1 V. This can speed capture,
but both states retain water. Even extrapolated to r=1 nm, the 70-degree state
would need about 49 MPa to empty against water affinity. At the actual 68 nm
radius, the ideal on-state condensation threshold is RH=.9867. Stop/start
was achieved using cyclohexane in the pore space; reverse water expulsion was
not demonstrated. This is not the required water/air surface switch.
[1] https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms5237

Lomax et al. demonstrate a notably stronger reversible, hysteresis-free
response on freshly cleaved, smooth basal-plane HOPG. In air the equilibrium
angle is 64+/-2 degrees and voltage makes it more water-wetting, so it fails
the off-state again. Under hexadecane, a 6 M LiCl droplet has a 100-degree
contact-angle range over 1.5 V, with liquid/liquid saturation near 47 degrees.
That is a real possible oil/water phase switch, not a pure-water/air switch.
The demonstrated aqueous droplet is connected to a pipette electrolyte
reservoir containing the counter and reference electrodes. A pipette-free
variant uses a conducting organic electrolyte. The paper itself discusses an
ultrathin organic layer making the liquid/liquid case resemble EWOD. Smooth
10-100 micrometre HOPG domains are selected; cheap rough porous carbon cannot
simply inherit this measured reversibility.
[2] https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2016/sm/c6sm01565d
    DOI 10.1039/C6SM01565D; primary abstract also at
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27722442/

Two independent obstacles prevent promoting that oil result to this device:

  Ionic inventory. If a long, same-polarity cylindrical pore stores electrode
  charge sigma=Cdl V using mobile counterions inside its water, local charge
  accounting requires c >= 2 Cdl V/(F r). At r=.7 nm, Cdl=.1 F/m2, V=1 V this
  is 2.96 mol/L of monovalent counterions; at 1.126 V it is 3.33 mol/L. This is
  a lower inventory bound for that specified capacitive implementation, not a
  universal no-go theorem. Oppositely charged electrodes, fixed ionic layers,
  ion-excluding membranes, or molecular redox states change the interface and
  must be modeled explicitly. Pure condensate cannot silently replace the
  paper's concentrated brine. Retaining ions while delivering nearly salt-free
  solvent is an additional selective separation/acquisition gate.

  Oil interface. Replacing oil by water changes solid/oil and water/oil free
  energies, and oil must return during the release stroke. Water-vapor transfer
  through the oil, oil retention, water activity, and the work of the oil loop
  must be measured. An oil-mediated variant could supply an invading phase
  without nucleating gas, but it is a different, unresolved device. No oil
  permeability or product-purity number is assumed in the calculator.

The HOPG paper reports electrical power of order 10 microW/cm2, or .1 W/m2
of planar active area. It does not establish that this is steady leakage;
transient charging may contribute. If that same value were persistent
irrecoverable power over all internal active area, a 30 s hold would cost
2.39 kWh/kg. To keep such a term below .005 kWh/kg requires a held state shorter
than .0628 s. Therefore direct signed current measurements in the actual
nanopores, rather than a macroscopic contact-angle reversal, are essential.
Fast cycling could avoid this particular cost, but its vapor uptake, phase
reset and electrical access have not been demonstrated.

Other nearest experimental checks strengthen the stop decision rather than
provide missing validation. Hydrophobic nanoporous carbon in 1 M KCl expels
droplets under negative polarization in conjunction with hydrogen evolution;
positive-potential uptake correlates with surface oxidation. That is not a
closed reversible capacitive cycle [3]. Light-switched spiropyran nanopores
can wet, but the molecular reverse switch did not spontaneously dewet the
filled pore, exposing the metastability barrier [4]. An actual 2011 experiment
already establishes electrical wetting/dewetting in individual hydrophobic
nanopores through ionic conductance fluctuations; it demonstrates gating,
not sustained net vapor-to-bulk-liquid pumping with this energy balance [5].

5. Why a product reservoir refills the capture pore

The capture state stabilizes water below the vapor chemical potential. A
bulk-liquid reservoir has an even higher water chemical potential than that
vapor. If hydraulically connected to the capture pore, it supplies water
backward; vapor uptake is not the unique source. A permanent wet product
bridge defeats a vapor-harvesting claim. An asymmetric taper alone does not
establish isolation.

A possible passive pressure window is

  P_entry,outlet < P_B < P_entry,vapor_guard,

with BOTH interfaces initially gas-separated on the capture stroke. For
illustrative, independent hydrophobic coatings, a 100 nm outlet with an
advancing angle of 120 degrees has P_entry=.72 MPa, while a 2 nm guard with
advancing angle 150 degrees has P_entry=62.35 MPa. The assumed release-state
pressure 35.72 MPa falls between them. A droplet's pressure plus hydrostatic
head must remain below the dry outlet's entry barrier during capture.

That pressure window is necessary but insufficient. After delivery, gas from
the active pore must invade and empty the common manifold/outlet; the external
product must be pinned beyond a reentrant stop or detach. Receding angles and
pinning govern whether the throat actually dries. If a liquid film persists,
the dry-entry calculation is inapplicable. If the collection drop contacts
the throat so as to wet its interior, refill resumes. The manifold's fill
volume, dead-volume recovery, droplet surface energy, and reset work must be
charged. Hydrophobic outlet vapor leakage and product evaporation also enter
the net mass balance. The suggested window is a falsifiable valve design
condition, not proof that such a rectifier has been built.

6. Decisive counterexample to a direct-field alternative

A tempting alternative is to avoid surface chemistry and lower the liquid
grand potential by direct dielectric stabilization. Even its favorable linear
estimate needs 0.5 epsilon0 (epsilon_l-epsilon_v) E^2 >= g. Actual experiments
find a normal dielectric constant near 2 for a few-molecule interfacial water
layer [6]. This gives E about 4 GV/m at ambient 60% RH. A 2025 primary experiment
finds much larger in-plane epsilon, about 1030+/-350 at 1-2 nm confinement, but
also conductivity of order 3 S/m at 1.5 nm [7]. Combining those reported
orders gives E about .124 GV/m and sigma E^2 about 4.6e16 W/m3. Spending the
entire .10 kWh/kg target permits only 7.8 ns residence under that held field.
Neither this simple dielectric model nor its transport regime is a validated
condensation calculation, but invoking giant permittivity while discarding
the jointly measured conductivity does not support an energy-saving device.

RF does not automatically remove that loss. For a conducting dielectric,
tan(delta) contains sigma/(omega epsilon0 epsilon); increasing frequency
changes reactive storage but does not by itself eliminate sigma E_rms^2 heat.
An actual loss spectrum, nonlinear polarization and molecular kinetics are
needed. Simulation-only dielectrocapillarity results are conditional evidence,
not an experimental realization. Conventional EWOD apparent-angle control
also cannot automatically be substituted for a true nanoscopic surface-energy
change: a direct capillary-condensation experiment found unchanged intrinsic
solid/liquid interfacial energy for its studied EWOD system [8]. This result
does not rule out bare-metal electrocapillarity or all surface chemistry.
[6] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29930134/
    DOI 10.1126/science.aat4191
[7] https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09558-y
[8] https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/la101255t

An amphiphilic nanopore film has recently been observed to condense vapor and
exude droplets [9]. Its authors explicitly note that further condensation
can diminish once pores fill and the system can reach evaporation/uptake
equilibrium. Finite pore filling followed by droplet appearance is not a
closed harvesting cycle. A static isothermal material cannot continuously
turn one undersaturated vapor reservoir into pure bulk water while returning
to its initial state without compensating work, another resource, or a
temperature/chemical-potential gradient.
[9] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12094244/
    Science Advances 11, eadu8349 (2025), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adu8349

7. Falsifiable experimental gates; no large prototype before the material gate

The first useful experiment is a potential-resolved sorption and extrusion
loop in the SAME fabricated pore, with fixed temperature, known vapor water
activity, and independently measured ionic/oil inventory. Measure filled
volume, ejected liquid, complete signed electrical work and any gas evolution.
The material passes only if it captures at the local .40 activity, releases
at least 90% of its usable water inventory as liquid with the same water/ion
composition after reset, and shows positive closed-cycle net production.
Apparent macroscopic angles and simulation alone do not pass this gate.

Next test the rectifier with a separately weighed product reservoir and
isotopically or chemically tagged source water. Cycle with the vapor supply
blocked; any uptake now is product backflow or internal inventory. Image
gas resetting at the outlet and deliberately leave a wet bridge as the
decisive negative control. Increase product head until backflow begins and
compare it with the measured advancing/receding pressure thresholds.

Only after those pass, a 1 cm2 complete module at 25 C, 60% RH has the declared
target of .1 g/hour net pure liquid and <=10 mW average all-system electrical
power. Include source and heat fans, power conversion, controls, product
collection and any ion/oil regeneration. Preregister 20% vapor extraction,
surface temperature, all equipment footprint, and liquid purity requirements.
Measure input/output vapor mass, product mass, sorbed inventory, current and
voltage with return power captured, gas composition, calorimetric heat output,
and salt/oil loss. A thousand cycles must yield mass far beyond the initial
liquid inventory and retain at least 90% of capacity. Humidity/temperature
excursions, chemical depletion and finite initial reservoir drainage must
not masquerade as production. Compare to a conventional condenser with the
same incoming air, outgoing humidity, production rate, heat sink, and complete
electrical boundary; the .10 target is not a claim of demonstrated superiority
over any current commercial device.

Kill or pivot if: no true hydrophobic release state; liquid release requires
evaporation or electrolysis; persistent wet bridges refill the capture pore;
the ionic/oil loop consumes uncounted work or contaminates product; full signed
cycle work plus auxiliaries exceeds the target; or confinement destroys the
assumed wetting/capacitance contrast. Improving an attractive drawing does
not resolve any of these gates.

Reproduction

Run: python3 work/genesis_independent/model.py
Outputs: results.json and sweep.csv beside the script. The script uses a Buck
vapor-pressure approximation and standard constants; it checks dimensional
work identities and the illustrative pressure ordering. These checks verify
calculator consistency only. No experiment, molecular simulation, novel
material synthesis or validated device design was performed in this work.

Strongest opening: electrically driven liquid-phase release could remove a
thermal-regeneration bottleneck if the surface and rectifier exist.
Strongest current conclusion: that material/interface combination has not
been concretized; actual NPG and HOPG results do not close it. Status remains
