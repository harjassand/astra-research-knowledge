FULL SIGNED-CYCLE IDENTITY AND PREREGISTERABLE FALSIFIER

Scope: conditional, isothermal material cycle; pure liquid product; one vapor
reservoir at chemical potential mu_v and one bulk-liquid product at mu_l.
The material returns to the same geometry, chemistry, charge and inventory.
No ionic, chemical, oil, temperature or pressure reservoir may drift without
entering the resource/work balance. This note is a derivation, not experimental
validation or a proof that the required material or valve exists.

Exact state-function identity

Let G_Ad, G_Aw, G_Bd, G_Bw denote the complete free energies of the relevant
material plus any resident ions and internal counter-electrode reservoir.
Subscripts A/B are controlled states; d/w are before/after acquisition of n
moles of water. Resident species must be the same after a full cycle.

Starting in Bd, use four strokes with the following free-energy changes:

  prepare dry A:      D1 = G_Ad - G_Bd
  acquire from vapor: D2 = G_Aw - G_Ad - n mu_v
  reset while wet:    D3 = G_Bw - G_Aw
  export liquid:     D4 = G_Bd + n mu_l - G_Bw.

Adding them cancels every material state exactly:

  D1+D2+D3+D4 = n(mu_l-mu_v).

Let Wj be external nonexpansion work supplied to stroke j, including signed
electrical work. With a common bath T, Wj >= Dj, with equality only in a
reversible realization with correctly specified external chemical/mechanical
work terms. Summing gives

  W_total >= n(mu_l-mu_v) = n R T ln(1/h) > 0.

This is the general cycle requirement. A changed ion reservoir, oxidized
surface, consumed gas, maintained electric field, temperature gradient or
drained liquid reservoir changes the boundary and cannot be discarded.

For the ideal continuum surface parameterization, take

  G_sw - G_sd = n mu_l - As Gamma_s,
  Gamma_s = gamma_sv(s) - gamma_sl(s).

Then

  D2 = n(mu_l-mu_v) - As Gamma_A,
  D4 = As Gamma_B,
  D1+D3 = As(Gamma_A-Gamma_B).

The abrupt passive fill requires D2<0; passive liquid release requires D4<0.
If neither stroke recovers work, the positive preparation/reset duty is

  W_switch,min = As(Gamma_A-Gamma_B)
               = n(mu_l-mu_v) + (-D2) + (-D4).

It pays chemical separation plus both passive dissipation terms. At the
illustrative .7 nm, 45/100-degree point, W_switch=.05048 kWh/kg. At the
worst local activity .4022 and 28 C, the chemical cost g/rho is about .03516
kWh/kg. Capture and release margins account for the remainder at that local
point. Across a flowing gas stream, activity varies; do not multiply the
entire batch by the inlet chemical potential and claim exact reversibility.

Finite ideal-air extraction lower bound

At fixed total pressure P and temperature T, let x be water moles per dry-air
mole. Then p_w=P x/(1+x), h(x)=P x/[(1+x) p_sat(T)]. Reversibly removing water
from x_in down to x_out requires

  W_min = R T N_d integral_(x_out)^(x_in) ln[1/h(x)] dx.

An antiderivative is

  H(x)=x ln[p_sat/P]+(1+x)ln(1+x)-x ln x,

so the minimum work per kilogram removed is

  (R T/M) [H(x_in)-H(x_out)]/(x_in-x_out).

This excludes fan pressure losses and a warmer sorbent's heat rejection.
At atmospheric pressure, 25 C, 60% RH and removal of exactly 20% of inlet
water moles, x_out=.8 x_in and the exit RH is about .482, rather than exactly
.480. The technical note's constant-gas-volume approximation changes this
humidity by less than .4%; the .10 kWh/kg feasibility margin is not evidence
precise enough to depend on such a distinction. Use the exact flow relation
in any prototype analysis.

Electrical identity

For ideal reversible voltage ramps with capacitances Cd and Cw, a held-voltage
change of capacitance draws source work V dQ=V^2 dC, half going to field energy
and half entering the effective mechanical/interface thermodynamic coupling.
Including that stroke gives, per area,

  W_signed = .5 Cd(VH^2-VL^2) + (Cw-Cd)VH^2
             -.5 Cw(VH^2-VL^2) -(Cw-Cd)VL^2
           = .5(Cw-Cd)(VH^2-VL^2).

Thus recovering the wet capacitor's stored energy does not erase the filling
work. For the calculator's DeltaC=.1 F/m2, Cd=.02 F/m2 and VH=1.126 V,
the four contributions per kilogram are +.01010, +.10096, -.06058, 0 kWh.
Signed work is +.05048 kWh/kg. These strokes are an explanatory capacitor
model, not the measured charge loop of NPG or HOPG.

Ionic inventory and held-field checks

For a long cylindrical same-polarity electrode pore, As/Vp=2/r. If mobile
monovalent counterions in its water support charge sigma=Cdl V, charge
inventory alone gives

  c_counter >= (As/Vp)|sigma|/F = 2 Cdl |V|/(F r).

With Cdl=.1 F/m2, V=1.126 V, r=.7 nm, c_counter>=3.335 mol/L. This assumption
can be changed by a different, explicitly built interface; it cannot be
changed by silently calling the reported 6 M LiCl droplet pure condensate.
Ion inventories must be measured before/after cycling and in the product.

If held irreversible power density is p_loss per internal area, its energy
per kilogram is p_loss t_hold 2/(rho r). For r=.7 nm and an allocated .005
kWh/kg loss budget, p_loss t_hold must be below .006281 J/m2. Consequently
p_loss=.1 W/m2 permits t_hold<.06281 s. The planar HOPG reported power is NOT
established to be steady leakage; only a measured time-resolved signed work
loop can resolve this. A direct dielectric-field version using the jointly
reported in-plane epsilon~1030 and sigma~3 S/m gives a much shorter ~8 ns
entire-budget dwell estimate. Neither estimate is a universal physical no-go
theorem for all redesigned material states.

Exact first falsification experiment

Apparatus: one electrically addressable pore ensemble with independently
known pore volume and area; optical or x-ray phase occupancy measurement;
sealed temperature-controlled vapor cell; separately weighed liquid outlet;
four-terminal electrical measurement with a physically monitored recovery
store; temperature probes at sorbent and air; gas analysis for H2/O2; and ion
and oil assays. All counter/reference-electrode chemical reservoirs are weighed
and chemically characterized. Use an external source of isotopically tagged
water vapor where needed to distinguish sources. Unpowered controls receive
identical humidity, airflow and temperature history.

First freeze the actual material, voltages, cycle timing and geometry. Do
not optimize after seeing the held-out mass/work loop. The hypothetical
45/100-degree states are not substitute experimental specifications.

Protocol A: thermodynamic/material gate.
  1. Equilibrate the complete uncharged material at 28.0+/-0.05 C and vapor
     activity .402+/-0.005. Characterize all initial liquid/ion/oil inventory.
  2. Apply its declared capture operation, holding until either occupancy
     plateaus or the declared energy allowance is exhausted. Integrate signed
     V I dt through capture, including held-voltage charge drawn on filling.
  3. Disconnect the vapor-cell weighing boundary from any external liquid
     feed. Apply the declared reset/recovery operation. Collect liquid in a
     sealed product vial. Track vapor loss, pore occupancy and gas evolution.
  4. Return charge, material chemistry and resident ionic/oil composition to
     the measured initial state. Count any restorative work and reagents.
  5. Repeat 1000 cycles at the frozen timing. Quantify cumulative liquid,
     cumulative vapor uptake, residual inventory and complete signed work.

Fail the material gate if full inventory does not fill at the declared
activity; less than 90% of its usable acquired water is exported as liquid;
capacity falls more than 10%; H2/O2 evolution or chemical drift accounts for
the operation; or cumulative apparent yield is attributable to initial
liquid/ion/oil depletion. Product ionic/oil composition must meet the declared
pure-water boundary; otherwise it is a brine/oil separation device and all
subsequent purification/reset work must be included.

Protocol B: backflow falsifier, prior to integrated airflow optimization.
  1. Put distinguishably tagged liquid product beyond the outlet and block
     fresh vapor. Cycle the putative pump. Any water acquired in capture is
     backflow or internal inventory, not harvested vapor.
  2. Compare a visibly dry/reset outlet against an intentionally preserved
     wet bridge. Measure the admitted mass and advancing/receding thresholds.
  3. Increase product head in small steps until backflow starts. The dry
     pressure threshold must exceed product Laplace pressure plus head and
     remain below the measured release pressure.
  4. In actual vapor cycling, image/measure complete gas reset of the outlet
     on every stroke. A hydrophobic equilibrium angle alone is insufficient.

Protocol C: all-system target, only if A and B pass.
Run fixed 25 C, 60% RH incoming air and 20% inlet-water removal, with .1 g/hour
net pure liquid for a module whose TOTAL equipment footprint is 1 cm2. The
aspirational all-system duty is <=10 mW average. Independently measure fan and
control power and include heat rejection, conversion, valve drive, ionic/oil
reset, collection, and product purification. External measurement instruments
are measurement equipment; a control instrument needed to run the technology
is part of the technology's duty. Subscale component efficiency must be
reported rather than extrapolated invisibly to a large device.

Control the collection vial against evaporation and account for retained
water after shutdown. Close source/outlet water mass and heat balances to
measurement uncertainty. Compare with a conventional condenser at the same
incoming/outgoing gas state, output rate, heat sink, and complete equipment
boundary. This is a target and failure protocol, not a demonstrated result.
