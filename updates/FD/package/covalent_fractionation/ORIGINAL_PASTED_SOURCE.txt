# ASTRA NATURAL SCIENCES — Invent Physical Capabilities from Real Experimental Leverage

**Mission:** Discover a genuinely new physical operation capable of enabling a civilisation-changing scientific or technological advance.

Begin independently. Do not default to Astra's earlier ionic pumps, catalytic oscillators, defect repair or phonon-assisted electron irradiation.

## Begin with physical evidence

Investigate exceptionally consequential limitations in energy, chemistry, synthesis, materials, manufacturing and other foundational fields.

Search simultaneously for transformative capabilities blocked by existing technology and experimentally established physical responses whose strongest consequences remain unexplored.

Choose an opportunity with a real material or accessible experimental system, measurable physical inputs and a credible route toward an exceptional advantage.

Do not select a mechanism merely because a consistent model can be written.

## Invent the missing mechanism

Work backward from the desired physical transformation.

Derive the actual competing processes from a common physical description. Do not independently assign favourable couplings, transition rates, excited-state lifetimes or control amplitudes.

Challenge conventional device architectures and representations where a genuinely new operation is needed.

Investigate unfamiliar mechanisms, but distinguish established physical responses from new causal predictions.

## Make the advantage testable

Identify the decisive physical quantity whose measurement would establish or reject the proposed mechanism.

Derive predictions that distinguish it from the strongest competing explanation.

Choose a consequential output: retained material change, radically improved selectivity, new accessible reaction, reduced damage or another clearly defined capability.

Compare with the best existing process for the same outcome.

Account for total energy, stability, fabrication, throughput, selectivity, operating conditions, competing reactions and lifetime.

If the decisive material response is unavailable, preserve the exact experimental question and redirect toward a stronger opportunity. Do not replace missing evidence with optimistically chosen parameters.

2. The experimentally established starting point
Soong and colleagues reported reversible electrochemical hydrogen/deuterium chemisorption on supported graphene in November 2025. Their system used HTFSI/DTFSI in a polyethylene-glycol electrolyte. Deuterated graphene required more positive potentials for oxidative removal, while cathodic loading competed with hydrogen-molecule formation. 

I inspected Figure 2 directly. The oxidative peaks in the separate H and D experiments are separated by roughly half a volt on the plotted gate-potential scale. That is a visual reading of separate-isotope measurements, not a fitted mixed-isotope separation window. 

The proposed causal prediction is therefore specific:

A suitably chosen oxidative pulse may remove most surface-bound H while leaving a substantial fraction of surface-bound D, producing a retained, compositionally enriched population.

The crucial word is may. Different pure-H and pure-D voltammograms do not establish this prediction.

Why the mixed experiment can behave differently
In the proposed device, removing H changes both the surface occupancy and the electrode’s electronic state. Those changes could accelerate subsequent D removal. The mixed population may therefore fail to preserve the apparent separation between the pure-isotope responses.

There are also experimental-reference issues. The source’s Methods report approximately 35% lower conductivity for its deuterated electrolyte. A shared mixed electrolyte and common potential reference are therefore important controls. 

The unresolved question is not whether isotopes behave differently in isolation. It is whether useful differential survival persists when both occupy the same operating electrode.

7. The comparison must be against strong demonstrated processes
Two verified comparators make the standard demanding.

Graphene membrane separation already exists. A 2017 study reported a separation factor of approximately eight using chemical-vapour-deposited graphene despite imperfections. Its industrial output and energy advantages were projections, rather than operating-plant demonstrations. A storage-based operation would need a measured advantage over this existing transport-based approach. 

Recent electrolysis already produces substantial isotope enrichment. The February 2026 PNAS study reports a gas–liquid separation factor around 16.5 at room temperature and 20.11 at 5 °C, alongside multistage enrichment beyond 80% D atom fraction. Its reported kinetic isotope-effect constant of 10,165 is not its separator selectivity. 

That study also requires interstage distillation and electrolyte reconstitution in its demonstrated process. A fair comparison must charge those operations—but must equally charge every capture, collection and regeneration step in the graphene proposal. 

The proposed batch odds enrichment of 171 must not be compared directly with an instantaneous gas–liquid separation factor of 16.5. They measure different things.

The comparison needs matched feed composition, final product purity, total D recovery, throughput, energy and lifetime. No like-for-like advantage has been established here.

8. Research decision
The exact experimental question worth preserving is:

Can a graphene electrode with a measured initial surface D atom fraction of 0.10 be oxidatively stripped to at least 0.95 D atom fraction while retaining at least half its original D, at room temperature, with a closed isotope balance? Can that retained inventory then be recovered without losing the enrichment?

The first question tests the proposed physical operation. The second tests whether it yields a useful product.

The investigation produced three concrete results: a defined mixed-isotope mechanism test, a quantitative selectivity–recovery requirement, and an inventory-based warning against assuming straightforward product collection. It did not establish the essential mixed-isotope response, historical novelty, or a substantial technological advantage.

I would retain this as a narrowly scoped experimental lead, not promote it as an Astra flagship or begin separator engineering. The available literature supports testing it; it does not support claiming that it works.
