SEQUENTIAL VALIDATION FOR PERSISTENT COMPARATORS
2026-10-13. Internal proof/audit. External review and novelty UNKNOWN.

Scope. Read-only inputs are p_s02b_matching_robustness/PROOF.txt and the
q_s09_robust_flag_implementation/robust_learner.py source. This note validates
the matching-score confidence kernel, checks its adaptive budget, and gives a
second sequential implementation with a finite cap that returns UNKNOWN.
Neither kernel recognizes the teacher's degree or flag promises. The degree d,
fixed bad-item bound, partial-flag existence, and a correct acquired prefix are
assumptions; candidate coefficients and own-toggle equations are checked
exactly before fresh validation.

1. Matching-score hypotheses

Fix a correct prefix, its exact uniform fiber sampler, a frozen candidate f of
degree at most d, and a nonzero candidate own-toggle v in the exact prefix
stabilizer. The true next polynomial q has degree at most d and toggles on v.
The source is a fixed map C on ordered pairs: it is correct when both items are
outside a fixed bad set B of density at most epsilon, and arbitrary (including
non-antisymmetric) on incident pairs. It has no fresh response noise.

One batch chooses a fresh uniform prefix fiber and independently samples m
positions from each of F_a intersect {f=0} and F_a intersect {f=1}. Repeated
raw items remain separate sample positions. Greedy left-by-left matching over
observed inversion edges C(y_0,y_1)=0 is maximal and uses at most m^2 ordered
pair requests. Its normalized size Z is in [0,1]. The proof in
p_s02b_matching_robustness/PROOF.txt gives, with

  c_m = binom(2m,m)/(2*4^m),   a = 2 epsilon,
  b = 2^(-d) - 2 epsilon - c_m,

the conditional mean bounds E Z <= a for a true candidate and E Z >= b for a
false candidate. The false bound uses the degree-d minimum distance
Pr[f(X) != q(X)] >= 2^(-d). Thus the validation gate exists only when a < b,
equivalently 2^(-d) - 4 epsilon - c_m > 0. For d=2 these are the exact bounds
in the pinned matching proof. For general fixed d, only the stated RM minimum-
distance substitution is new here; the matching argument is unchanged.

Fresh seeds make batch scores independent conditional on the frozen candidate
and fixed source. Repeated source pairs do not invalidate this: their answers
are repeated deterministic values, while the independently drawn positions
and prefixes remain independent. Reusing one prefix or one sample bank across
batches would invalidate the argument. An ordered cache is safe when it stores
only the exact ordered answer C(x,y); it must not complement C(y,x) for an
arbitrary non-antisymmetric source.

2. Audit of q_s09's fractional-score SPRT and finite fallback

Let u=log(b/a)>0 and w=log((1-b)/(1-a))<0. q_s09 updates

  W_t = sum_{i<=t} [ Z_i u + (1-Z_i) w ].

For every z in [0,1], exp(zu+(1-z)w) lies below the chord joining its endpoint
values. If p=E Z <= a, then

  E exp(zu+(1-z)w)
    <= (1-p) exp(w) + p exp(u)
    <= (1-a) exp(w) + a exp(u) = 1.

So exp(W_t) is a nonnegative supermartingale under every true-candidate law.
Likewise, exp(-zu-(1-z)w) is convex in z and its endpoint-chord expectation
is at most 1 for every p>=b, so exp(-W_t) is a supermartingale under every
false-candidate law. Ville's inequality makes each crossing of
W_t >= log(2/delta) under a true candidate, or W_t <= -log(2/delta) under a
false candidate, have probability at most delta/2.

At the deterministic batch cap, q_s09 decides by whether the empirical mean
of Z is below the equal-KL threshold tau, defined by
  D(tau||a)=D(tau||b),
where D is binary relative entropy. For independent [0,1]-valued batch scores,
the endpoint-chord Chernoff bound gives
  Pr_true(mean Z > tau) <= exp(-N D(tau||a)),
  Pr_false(mean Z <= tau) <= exp(-N D(tau||b)).
The configured cap N=ceil(log(2/delta)/min{D(tau||a),D(tau||b)}) makes each
fallback error at most delta/2. The crossing and fallback events are disjoint,
so a true candidate is falsely rejected with probability at most delta and a
false candidate is falsely accepted with probability at most delta. This is a
valid finite-horizon sequential validator; it does not rely on i.i.d. source
response noise. If a global request budget interrupts it first, that path
returns UNKNOWN and is an extra operational stop unless the configured global
budget is at least the analyzed worst-case query cap.

For a cap-and-stopping bound, the increments are in [w,u], and their mean is
at most -D(a||b) under a true candidate and at least D(b||a) under a false
candidate. Let A=log(2/delta). First-exit overshoots are at most -w below and
u above. Therefore the number of batches before the boundary or the cap has
expectation at most

  true:  (A-w)/D(a||b),
  false: (A+u)/D(b||a).

These bounds apply conditionally after every adaptive candidate selection.
They are upper bounds; the actual expected stopping time can be smaller.

3. A cap that returns UNKNOWN using an exact Bernoulli KL test

There is a second option when a fail-closed validation cap is preferred. After
computing each batch score Z_t, draw a fresh private U_t uniform on [0,1] and
set Y_t=1[U_t<=Z_t]. This private coin randomizes the score, not the teacher's
answer. If batches are independently sampled from the fixed source, then
Y_t are iid Bernoulli(p), p=E Z, even when the same ordered source pair happens
to recur. No claim is made that a cached source pair has fresh randomness.

Use the Bernoulli likelihood ratio
  W_t = S_t log(b/a) + (t-S_t) log((1-b)/(1-a)),  S_t=sum_i Y_i.
Reject f as false at W_t >= A_F=log(1/alpha_F); accept f at
W_t <= -A_T=-log(1/alpha_T). The same one-sided likelihood-ratio argument
gives false acceptance probability at most alpha_F and true rejection at
most alpha_T, uniformly over the composite ranges p>=b and p<=a.

If neither boundary is reached by N, return UNKNOWN. Let u=log(b/a),
w=log((1-b)/(1-a)), and
  W_N(k)=Nw+k(u-w).
No stop by N implies -A_T < W_N < A_F. Hence its probability is bounded by
the exact endpoint tails

  true candidate: Pr[Bin(N,a) >= floor((-A_T-Nw)/(u-w)) + 1],
  false candidate: Pr[Bin(N,b) <= ceil(( A_F-Nw)/(u-w)) - 1].

Choose N so both tails are <= kappa. (The accompanying executable uses one
extra count in each tail as a conservative numerical guard, evaluates the
rational binomial tails by integer recurrence, and verifies the final cap.)
Then a candidate test has false-accept, true-reject, and cap-UNKNOWN
probabilities bounded by alpha_F, alpha_T, and kappa respectively.

For the uncapped first-exit time tau, the log-likelihood drift is at most
-D(a||b) for p<=a and at least D(b||a) for p>=b. The same overshoot argument
gives

  E_true tau  <= (A_T-w)/D(a||b),
  E_false tau <= (A_F+u)/D(b||a).

Truncation at N can only decrease the expected number of batches. This variant
is useful when UNKNOWN is required at the validator cap, but it is not claimed
to dominate q_s09's bounded-score KL fallback in worst-case calls.

4. All-history allocation and adaptive candidates

Suppose at most H candidate validations can be invoked across all stages. Freeze
each candidate after its proposal, before drawing its validation seeds. Allocate
deterministic budgets alpha_F,i, alpha_T,i, kappa_i with
  sum_i alpha_F,i <= beta_v,
  sum_i alpha_T,i <= beta_v,
  sum_i kappa_i <= beta_v.
For the UNKNOWN-at-cap variant, equal allocation
alpha_F,i=alpha_T,i=kappa_i=beta_v/(3H) suffices. Conditional error bounds
remain true after arbitrary adaptive history as long as the prefix is still
correct; union-bound only up to the first wrong candidate acceptance. The
event of any validator UNKNOWN is separately bounded by sum_i kappa_i.
Combine beta_v with candidate-proposal, body-decoder, and suffix budgets from
the surrounding learner. If H is unbounded, use summable allocations such as
const/i^2; never reuse a fixed per-candidate delta indefinitely.

q_s09 currently uses a stronger convenient allocation for its own proposal
schedule: delta=beta/(4*h*Rcap^2), with at most h*Rcap validations, so the
first-wrong-acceptance sum is beta/(4*Rcap). This is valid and leaves slack;
it should be reported as such. The q_s09 validation itself makes a decision at
its finite KL fallback cap, while an independently imposed lower global query
cap returns UNKNOWN.

5. Numerical benchmark and limits

The executable benchmark uses admitted degree d=2, epsilon=0.0331, matching
sample size m=11, H=1000 potential tests, and validation share beta_v=0.05.
Thus alpha_F=alpha_T=kappa=1/60000. Here
  c_m=88179/1048576 ~= 0.08409405,
  a=0.0662, b~=0.09970595, b-a~=0.03350595.
The p_s02b Hoeffding cap is 19601 batches per candidate. The Bernoulli-LR
UNKNOWN cap chosen by exact binomial-tail evaluation is 7443 batches; its
endpoint timeout tail bounds are 1.5200e-5 and 1.2601e-5, below 1/60000.
Finite-state recursion at p=a and p=b gives expected stopping of 1572.02 and
1404.78 batches. Wald upper bounds are 1574.71 and 1437.86. Since one batch
uses at most m^2=121 pair requests, per-candidate worst-case matching requests
are 900603 versus 2371721 for the Hoeffding cap, and expected endpoint request
counts are about 190215 and 169978.

At the same per-test alpha, the q_s09 bounded-score fixed-KL optimizer chooses
m=12 and cap 5226 (752544 pair requests), so the Bernoulli conversion is not a
worst-case call improvement over that fallback. It demonstrates an exact
binomial cap with explicit UNKNOWN semantics and lower endpoint expected work
than its own fixed cap. No large source execution was performed here. The
receipt is a statistical-kernel calculation, not an end-to-end learner result
or a source-comparison experiment.

An original directed-answer cache stores only C(x,y). q_s09's earlier harness
also tested a derived C* that makes one canonical forward source call and
complements its answer for the reverse orientation. C* is still a valid fixed
oracle in this promise class: clean strict-order answers are antisymmetric and
all incident-B answers remain arbitrary and persistent. It is a different
oracle, so count its underlying source calls separately and use the same C*
transformation in controls. Current primary q_s09 evidence reports the
original directed cache path. Neither path may claim that the original teacher
answered a reversed query unless it actually did.

No validation here recognizes the teacher's degree, bijection, flag existence,
or bad-set promise. Without an admitted degree d, a false-candidate distance
lower bound 2^(-d) and therefore this score gap are unavailable.
