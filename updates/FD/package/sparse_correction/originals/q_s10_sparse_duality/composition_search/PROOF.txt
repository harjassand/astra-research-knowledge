Paired affine literals, binary matroid bases, and a silver-ratio proposal exponent
13 October 2026. Internal mathematical proof. Coefficient group GF(2).

1. Construction from a specified binary matrix

Let A=(a_1,...,a_D) be a rank-r matrix with columns in V=F_2^r. Give each column j two Boolean variables w_{j,0},w_{j,1}. Define
  Q_A(w)=sum_{v in V} product_{j=1}^D w_{j,<a_j,v>}  (over F_2),
  H_A(y)=Q_A(1+y).
Thus H_A is an explicit finite Boolean polynomial on n=2D variables. It can be represented by 2^r product terms before expansion, or its ANF can be specified by the unique-solution rule below. No infinite group or unexecuted physical operation is needed for the mathematical counterexample.

Let B(A) be the number of r-element column-position subsets that are bases of V. Let
  P_A(K)=sum_{C subset [D], span{a_j:j in C}=V} K^|C|.

Claim 1 (exact ANF support). A coefficient indexed by S subset [D]x{0,1} is nonzero iff S selects at most one literal for each column, its selected columns C span V, and its selected literal labels epsilon_j are a consistent system
  <a_j,v>=epsilon_j, j in C.
Every nonzero coefficient equals 1.

Proof. Expanding Q_A(1+y), the coefficient of y_S is the parity of the number of v whose chosen literals contain S. Selecting both labels of one column is inconsistent. Otherwise that number is either 0 or 2^(r-rank(A_C)), and is odd precisely when the consistent system has rank r. For a fixed spanning C, exactly 2^r choices of literal labels are consistent, one for each v.

Consequently the coefficient count is m=2^r P_A(1). Its minimal supports have exactly r columns, forming bases, because any spanning subset contains a basis and the unique v gives a consistent restriction to that basis. Every basis admits 2^r literal labels. Thus there are 2^r B(A) minima, all of degree r, and
  S=sum_min 2^-|M|=B(A),
  W=B(A)2^(2D).

Claim 2 (exact truth support). The truth support has k=m. In fact, globally swapping labels 0 and 1 in every variable pair maps ANF support bijectively to truth support.

Proof. In H_A(y)=sum_v product_j(1+y_{j,<a_j,v>}), a pair with both entries y=1 kills every v. A pair with both entries 0 imposes no constraint. A pair selecting the sole 1 at label epsilon_j imposes <a_j,v>=1-epsilon_j. Again parity is nonzero precisely for a unique solution, which means the selected columns span and their opposite labels are consistent. This is Claim 1 with all labels swapped.

The exact minimal-mixture proposal count is therefore
  R(A)=W/k=B(A)2^(2D)/(2^r P_A(1)).
The associated exponent is alpha(A)=ln(R(A))/ln(m).

2. Composition with OR blocks

Replace every y_{j,epsilon} with OR_b on its own b Boolean coordinates. Put K=2^b-1. Distinct outer monomials yield disjoint expanded ANF supports, because an active outer variable must contribute a nonempty subset of its private block. Thus
  m=2^r P_A(K).
Each nonzero outer truth assignment selects one literal in each spanning C, so it has K^|C| preimages. Hence again k=m. Each outer minimal basis/literal set contributes b^r minimal monomials of degree r, so
  n=2Db,
  S=B(A)b^r,
  W=B(A)b^r 2^(2Db),
  R(A,b)=W/m.
All terms have coefficient 1 in GF(2). Disjoint tensor products preserve the coefficient, truth and minimum-support counts, so any strict finite exponent gives an asymptotic universal lower bound.

3. Exact known subfamilies

A single nonzero column, r=D=1, gives H=y_0+y_1: OR-pair construction after substitution.
For r=2 and all three nonzero columns, Q has four degree-3 monomials on six literals, equivalent to the Pasch configuration {123,145,246,356}. H has 12 quadratic terms and 4 cubic terms. Thus
  m=k=4K^2(K+3), S=3b^2,
  q=4a^3-12a^5+8a^6=4a^3(1-a)^2(1+2a), a=2^-b.
At b=7 this gives alpha approximately 1.139140632161617, improving the earlier OR-pair exponent.

For the matrix of all D=2^r-1 nonzero vectors, the bases and spanning polynomial have closed forms
  B=product_{i=0}^{r-1}(2^r-2^i)/r!,
  P_A(K)=sum_{s=0}^r [r choose s]_2 (-1)^(r-s) 2^((r-s)(r-s-1)/2) (1+K)^(2^s-1).
This follows by Mobius inversion on the subspace lattice, since every s-dimensional subspace contains 2^s-1 such columns. It gives a ladder of stronger explicit examples, including r=6,b=1 with exponent about 1.18462074206. No global optimizer over this whole construction class is claimed.

4. Probabilistic existence: universal lower exponent log_2(1+sqrt(2))

Choose D columns independently uniformly in F_2^r, allowing repetitions and zero columns. Each r-position subset is a basis with probability
  kappa_r=product_{i=1}^r (1-2^-i)>1/4.
The final inequality follows from product_{i=2}^r(1-2^-i)>=1-sum_{i=2}^r2^-i>1/2, then multiplying by 1/2. Therefore
  E B(A)=kappa_r binom(D,r).
Some actual finite matrix A has at least this many bases, and therefore rank r. Independently of its detailed structure,
  P_A(1)<=2^D,
  m=k<=2^(D+r),
  W=B(A)2^(2D).
Since alpha=ln(W)/ln(m)-1 decreases with m at fixed W,
  alpha >= 1 + [log2(kappa_r binom(D,r))-2r]/(D+r).

Let D/r approach c>1. The elementary entropy bounds
  2^(D H_2(r/D))/(D+1) <= binom(D,r) <= 2^(D H_2(r/D))
show that the right side approaches
  1+[c H_2(1/c)-2]/(c+1).
To verify the binomial lower bound without Stirling, set p=r/D; the binomial probability of r is its maximum among D+1 terms and hence at least 1/(D+1).

Set x=1/c. The excess exponent is
  delta(x)=[H_2(x)-2x]/(1+x).
Its derivative vanishes exactly when
  log2((1-x)^2/x)=2,
so x=3-2sqrt(2), c=3+2sqrt(2). The endpoints have smaller values. At this point
  delta=log2((1+sqrt(2))/2),
  alpha_limit=log2(1+sqrt(2))=1.271553303... .

Conclusion (precise universal quantifiers). For every alpha<log2(1+sqrt(2)) and every fixed C, there exist finite GF(2) polynomials with every listed coefficient nonzero for which
  W>C m^alpha k.
Indeed the existential matrices above give exponents eventually above alpha, with m>=2^r tending to infinity. Alternatively, once any finite matrix gives a strict exponent above alpha, disjoint tensor copies amplify its ratio beyond C.

This is a proved existential family. It does not identify or individually count the larger matrices used in the limiting proof. A separately specified and exactly counted finite matrix appears below and in matroid_witness_receipt.json.

The chosen construction improves a lower bound on the optimal universal exponent. It does not prove a matching upper bound; the retained m^2 upper bound is still the universal control under its local-floor assumptions, and the true exponent remains UNKNOWN.

Verification enumerates the 417,199 subspaces of F_2^8, not 2^94 truth assignments or the enormous expanded ANF. For each subspace U let n_U be the number of matrix columns in U. Then
  P_A(1)=sum_{U<=V} mu(U,V)2^n_U,

The script independently checks the full 64-point rank-2 example and the coefficient/truth label-swap bijection. Runtime was about 1.14 seconds on one CPU, with streamed subspaces and small memory. No expanded truth/ANF scan is represented as executed.

6. Strong matched conditional sampler

Given A, b=1, sample C subset [D] uniformly until its columns span. Then draw v uniformly from F_2^r and put a 1 at literal (j,1-<a_j,v>) for j in C, with all other literals 0. Every nonzero point has exactly one such pair (C,v), so this samples the truth support exactly uniformly.

The success probability of the rank rejection is rho=P_A(1)/2^D. Expected random bits are D/rho+r. Binary elimination costs O(r^2 D)/rho bit operations, or O(rD)/rho machine-word operations when each r-bit column fits one word, plus n output bits. For the explicit witness rho=0.999558249578854..., so the sampler is inexpensive once the matrix representation is supplied. General b uses independent column inclusion probability 1-2^-b conditioned on spanning, then nonzero OR-block assignments.

This refutes the minimal-mixture proposal scaling, not arbitrary conditional-sampling efficiency. Learning A, recognizing this representation from another oracle, or checking all m expanded terms must be charged separately. No new acquisition advantage or practical service advantage is established.

7. Strong restriction on a neighboring coding route

For an outer polynomial that is simply the indicator of an affine binary code/coset of codimension r, the algebraic degree is exactly r (a nonsingular r-column minor gives a nonzero degree-r coefficient). Its vanishing order h at the all-ones input equals the coset-leader distance from that input and is at most r.

If h=r, choose a minimal representation of the syndrome by r columns. These columns are independent and form a basis in which the syndrome is all ones. Any further column with at least two basis coordinates would replace those coordinates and give a representation of weight at most r-1. Therefore every column is zero or one basis unit. The affine code decomposes into independent parity constraints on disjoint coordinate blocks.

After OR_b substitution, an even-size block contributes a parity factor with m=sK, S=sb/2 and q=[1-(1-2a)^s]/2, s even. This q increases with s, so P/m is no larger than for s=2. If P/m>=1, dividing its log by ln(m)>=ln(2K) also cannot beat the size-2 OR-pair exponent; if P/m<1, the exponent is at most 1. An odd-size block contributes a constant-minimum factor with S=1, m=1+sK and q>=a, so P<=1/a<=m. Products average the exponents. Hence max-vanishing-order affine-coset indicators cannot beat the earlier OR-pair optimum.

The new unique-solution/rank predicate H_A is not an affine-coset indicator, so it escapes this proved restriction. Cases with h<r or more general codes are not ruled out by this argument.
