1. Exact construction, with no torsion restriction

For disjoint nonempty variable blocks A,B, |A|=a, |B|=b, let
  O_A(x)=1-product_{i in A}(1-x_i),
  H_{a,b}=O_A-O_B,
  F=g H_{a,b}
for any nonzero element g of ANY nontrivial finite abelian additive group G. Integer multiplication of g is meant here; G needs no ring multiplication.

Each O block expands into all of its nonempty monomials with coefficients +/-1. The two blocks have disjoint monomial supports. Hence the listed coefficient count is
  M=2^a+2^b-2,
and every listed coefficient is +/-g and nonzero. Every variable is an inclusion-minimal term. Thus, for n=a+b,
  W=n*2^(n-1).
On the Boolean cube H is 0,+1,-1. It is nonzero precisely when exactly one block is all zero. Therefore its exact truth support has
  K=(2^a-1)+(2^b-1)=M.

For a=b=4, (n,m,k,W)=(8,30,30,1024), so mk=900<1024.
This disproves the claimed m bound already with integer coefficients +/-1 and over every finite abelian group containing a nonzero element. It is not a prime-projection or torsion-only obstruction.

Lattice audit: let v be the complete +/-1 coefficient vector of H. Every zero-output zeta row r obeys r dot v=0 over Z. Thus its integer row lattice L is contained in ker(v), which contains no coordinate unit because v_j=+/-1. In fact, the rational zero-row span already omits every coordinate unit. Reducing v modulo any q>=2 supplies an actual cyclic finite-group witness. The previous exact lattice criterion remains correct; the conjectured coordinate-unit implication is false.

2. Tensor products and exact proposal scaling

Take disjoint OR-pair blocks indexed by j. First form the INTEGER polynomial product H=product_j H_{a_j,b_j}, then map every integer coefficient to its multiple of one fixed g!=0. Disjointness makes each coefficient +/-1, with a unique factor choice, so every mapped coefficient is +/-g and nonzero. Values again belong to {0,+g,-g}; support is exactly the Cartesian product of the factor supports. This avoids assuming a multiplication on G.

Writing M_j=2^a_j+2^b_j-2 and W_j=(a_j+b_j)2^(a_j+b_j-1), one has
  m=k=product_j M_j,
  W=product_j W_j,
  P:=W/k=product_j P_j, where P_j=W_j/M_j.
P is the exact expected proposal count of the retained minimal-term mass mixture on the uniform cube, because its total minimal mass is W/2^n and its target mass is k/2^n.

For repeated balanced pairs a=b=t,
  M_t=2(2^t-1),
  P_t=t*2^(2t-1)/(2^t-1),
  P_t/M_t=t/[4(1-2^-t)^2].
After d independent copies,
  m=M_t^d,  P=P_t^d=m^(alpha_t),
  alpha_t=ln(P_t)/ln(M_t).
Consequently any universal inequality W<=C m^alpha k with fixed C and alpha<alpha_t fails as d grows. In particular m times any fixed power of log(m) cannot replace the false m bound.

4. Exact base optimality under the same support (Sol05c theorem, independently checked)

This paragraph credits the separate theorem communicated by p_s05c_integral_uncertainty. If nonzero functions f:X->G and h:Y->G both have at least one zero, with |X|=U, |Y|=V, write z=|f^-1(0)| and w=|h^-1(0)|. Then
  |{(x,y):f(x)+h(y)=0}| <= zw+(U-z)(V-w).
The right side is bilinear on 1<=z<=U-1, 1<=w<=V-1. Its largest corner value is (U-1)(V-1)+1, since the other corner sum U+V-2 is no larger. Thus
