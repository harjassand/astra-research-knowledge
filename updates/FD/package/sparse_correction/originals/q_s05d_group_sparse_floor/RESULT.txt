TORSION-SAFE SPARSE CONSTANT FLOOR, SHARP EQUALITY, AND THE ORIGINAL m^2 SCOPE
13 October 2026. Internal mathematical reconstruction; no novelty or mission-completion claim.

1. The assigned finite-group gate is TRUE

Let G be any abelian additive group; finiteness is unnecessary. Let
  F(x) = sum_{A subset [n]} c_A 1[A subset supp(x)]
be its unique multilinear Boolean polynomial. Combine duplicate supports and remove zero coefficients. Assume c_empty != 0 and let m be the ACTUAL number of nonzero coefficients. Put k=|{x:F(x)!=0}|. Then

  k m >= 2^n,
  Pr_uniform(F!=0) >= 1/m.

For at most s terms the requested 1/s bound follows. No map to a field, rational rank argument, division in G, or choice of a prime is used.

There is a stronger optimal bound for the at-most-s class. Write beta(s)=2^floor(log_2 s). If 1<=s<=2^n, then

  Pr_uniform(F!=0) >= 1/beta(s) >= 1/s.              (A)

If s>2^n the sharp bound is 2^-n; equivalently use 1/min(beta(s),2^n). More generally, if degree(F)<=d, the two floors give

  Pr_uniform(F!=0) >= 1/min(beta(s),2^d).           (B)

The degree floor has a separate direct argument given below. In (A), sharpness for every s with floor(log_2 s)<=n is attained over EVERY nontrivial abelian group: fix any g!=0 and take

  F(x) = g product_{i=1}^{floor(log_2 s)}(1-x_i).

This notation means multiplying g by the integer coefficients of the scalar product; it does not assume a multiplication on G. It uses beta(s)<=s nonzero coefficients and is nonzero on exactly a 1/beta(s) fraction. Consequently neither a cyclic-group restriction nor torsion can improve the universal at-most-s floor.

2. Self-contained proof of the requested 1/m floor

Induct on n, with n=0 immediate. Write, on the final coordinate,

  F(y,x_n)=g(y)+x_n h(y),   u(y)=g(y)+h(y),
  a=|supp(coefficients(g))|, b=|supp(coefficients(h))|,
  N=2^(n-1), K=|supp(g)|+|supp(u)|=k.

Because g(0)=c_empty!=0, a>=1, and a+b=m. If b=0, F is independent of x_n and the induction hypothesis for g proves the claim. Suppose b>0.

If u(0)!=0, both g and u have nonzero constants, and u has at most a+b=m coefficients. The induction hypothesis gives

  K >= N/a + N/m >= 2N/m.

If u(0)=0, then h(0)=-g(0)!=0. Pointwise, h=u-g, so a nonzero h-value requires a nonzero value of g or u. Therefore

  K >= max(|supp(g)|,|supp(h)|)
    >= max(N/a,N/b)
    >= 2N/(a+b)=2N/m.

All statements use only equality to zero, addition, and additive inverses. The support inclusion remains valid for every torsion type and for direct products of cyclic groups. This closes the finite-abelian gap directly.

3. Stronger dyadic floor

Use induction on n and the actual m, with L(t)=1/beta(t). Again write F=g+x_n h and u=g+h, with coefficient counts a+b=m. When b=0 use induction for g. When u(0)!=0, both layer polynomials g,u have nonzero constants and at most m terms, hence each has nonzero probability at least L(m). Their equally weighted average has the same bound.

When u(0)=0, both g,h have nonzero constants. In rates on the (n-1)-cube, the support inclusion h=u-g gives

  Pr(F!=0) >= (1/2) max(Pr(g!=0),Pr(h!=0))
             >= (1/2) L(min(a,b)).

Since min(a,b)<=m/2,
  floor(log_2 min(a,b)) <= floor(log_2 m)-1,
and the last quantity is at least L(m). This proves (A) without any finite-group premise. The n=0 base also covers the ambient cap 2^-n. This argument does not classify every equality case of the dyadic bound when m is not a power of two; the complete equality classification below is for the assigned 1/m bound.

4. Complete all-group equality classification for 1/m

The following are equivalent:

  (i) c_empty!=0 and m k=2^n;
  (ii) for some D subset [n] and g=c_empty!=0,
       F(x)=g product_{i in D}(1-x_i);
  (iii) coefficient support is the full Boolean interval 2^D and
        c_A=(-1)^|A| g for every A subset D.

Thus m=2^|D|, k=2^(n-|D|). In an exponent-two group the signs coincide; there is no additional torsion extremizer.

Proof: Induct using section 2. An unused final coordinate reduces to n-1. If b>0 and u(0)!=0, then a<m, so N/a+N/m>2N/m: equality is impossible. If u(0)=0 and K=2N/m, equality in max(N/a,N/b)>=2N/m forces a=b=m/2. But |supp(g)|>=N/a=K and K=|supp(g)|+|supp(u)|. Hence u is identically zero, h=-g as functions, and by Boolean Mobius inversion h=-g coefficientwise. Therefore F=(1-x_n)g and g is itself an equality case. Induction yields (ii) and (iii). Conversely the product takes the value g precisely when every coordinate in D is zero and vanishes elsewhere, giving the claimed counts.

This is a classification of all coefficients, valid over every abelian group, not a scan over bounded moduli.

5. Product laws p_i<=1/2 and degree

Let independent X_i have Pr(X_i=1)=p_i<=1/2. Generate Z_i independently with probability 2p_i, and fair independent U_i, then set X_i=Z_i U_i. Conditional on Z, the polynomial is obtained by setting coordinates outside Z to zero. Its nonzero constant remains c_empty and its sparsity is at most m. Thus (A) and the requested 1/m floor hold conditionally and after averaging.

For the original 1/m bound, equality under this product law occurs exactly when F has the product form in section 4 and every coordinate in D has p_i=1/2. Indeed, if any variable that occurs in a nonzero term has p_i<1/2, with positive probability thinning removes that variable and strictly lowers the actual term count; the conditional 1/m bound is then strict. If all used coordinates have p_i=1/2, apply the uniform equality classification. Unused coordinates have arbitrary allowed biases.

For completeness, the degree floor 2^-d also holds over all abelian groups. On the uniform cube choose a nonzero coefficient on a support A of maximum size e<=d. After fixing coordinates outside A, the coefficient of the full A-monomial is still c_A, because no strict superset term exists. The A-slice is therefore not identically zero by integer Mobius inversion, so its nonzero rate is at least 2^-e>=2^-d. Thinning proves the same for product p_i<=1/2. Combining with (A) gives (B).

The hypotheses matter. With zero constant, F=g x_1...x_n has m=1 and q=2^-n, so a degree-independent 1/m floor fails. With p_1>1/2, F=g(1-x_1) has two terms and q=1-p_1<1/2. Dependence between bits also invalidates the thinning/product argument.

A separate source-scope trap has an explicit torsion counterexample. The GF(2) no-constant corollary Pr(F=0)>=1/(m+1) does NOT extend to arbitrary coefficient groups: over Z/4, F=x_1+x_2+x_3 has m=3 and is zero only at 000, so Pr(F=0)=1/8<1/4. This concerns a ZERO event for a ZERO-constant polynomial, not the proved NONZERO event with NONZERO constant. The proof above does not justify that other corollary or all characteristic-two compiler steps.

6. Consequence for the structural minimal-incidence bound

For arbitrary coefficient support T and every A in T (not only its minima), define
  s_A=|{B in T:A subset B}|,
  d_A=max_{B in T:A subset B}|B\A|,
  rho_A=min(beta(s_A),2^d_A),
  g_A(y)=sum_{B in T:A subset B} c_B y_(B\A).

The polynomial g_A has nonzero constant c_A, exactly s_A nonzero terms, and degree d_A. In the A-fiber the coefficient of the full A-monomial is g_A(y). If nonzero, some assignment to A makes F nonzero, by Boolean Mobius inversion. Every such assignment has product probability at least p_A=product_{i in A}p_i when p_i<=1/2. Hence

  q=Pr(F!=0) >= p_A Pr(g_A!=0) >= p_A/rho_A.       (C)

For ANY subfamily R subset T,

  sum_{A in R} p_A <= q sum_{A in R} rho_A.        (D)

In particular q_l17's structural theorem is fully self-contained, and sharper:

  sum_{A in Min(T)} p_A
    <= Lambda_dyad(T) q,
  Lambda_dyad(T)=sum_{A in Min(T)} min(beta(s_A),2^d_A)
    <= sum_{A in Min(T)} min(s_A,2^d_A)
    <= sum_{A in Min(T)} s_A <= m^2.

On the uniform cube multiply (D) by 2^n. This proves the original m^2 minimal-mass fallback and preserves the scope of the false stronger claim: it does NOT prove W(T)<=m k(F), which remains refuted by the OR-pair and subsequent constructions.

Taking R=T also gives an all-term bound, matching the type of quantity in the original source:

  sum_{A in T} p_A <= q sum_{A in T} rho_A
    <= q sum_{A in T} s_A
    <= [m(m+1)/2] q <= m^2 q.                     (E)

The penultimate inequality counts self incidences and strictly comparable unordered pairs in a partial order. Minimality is not needed for (C): the full A-fiber coefficient ignores terms that do not contain A. This all-term extension is a separate internally proved statement; the source's field scope must not be silently enlarged merely by citing it.

and checks e_empty in L exactly by HNF membership. This certificate forces c_empty=0 in every group satisfying the requested zeros; no rational-rank or one-prime inference is used.
