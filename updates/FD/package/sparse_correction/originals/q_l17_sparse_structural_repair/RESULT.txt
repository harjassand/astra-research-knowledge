# Sparse minimal-incidence repair: degree-cover bound and exact OR obstruction

Date: 13 October 2026  
Status: the proposed global bound \(W(T)\le m k(F)\) is false. A support-dependent positive bound is proved, is much smaller than \(m^2\) on the counterexample family, and is asymptotically within a factor two of the exact best factor on a second sharp family. The best general structural factor remains open.

## Model and notation

Let \(T\subseteq 2^{[n]}\) be a set of \(m\) distinct monomial supports, with every listed coefficient \(c_B\) nonzero in a finite abelian additive group \(G\). Write

\[
 F(x)=\sum_{B\in T}c_B\,\mathbf 1[B\subseteq X],\qquad
 M=\operatorname{Min}(T),\qquad
 q=\Pr(F(X)\ne0),\qquad
 W_p(T)=\sum_{A\in M}p_A,
\]

where the coordinates of \(X\) are independent Bernoulli variables with \(p_i\le1/2\), and \(p_A=\prod_{i\in A}p_i\). For each minimum \(A\), define its term cover and extension width by

\[
 s_A=|\{B\in T:A\subseteq B\}|,\qquad
 d_A=\max_{B\in T:A\subseteq B}|B\setminus A|.
\]

Put \(\beta(s)=2^{\lfloor\log_2s\rfloor}\). The dyadic degree/cover factor is

\[
 \Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)=\sum_{A\in M}\min\{\beta(s_A),2^{d_A}\}.
\]

Here \(s_A\) is the row degree of the minimal-to-term containment matrix. Its total incidence is \(I(T)=\sum_A s_A=\sum_{B\in T}\nu(B)\), where \(\nu(B)=|\{A\in M:A\subseteq B\}|\).

## Universal positive bound

For every finite abelian \(G\), every nonzero coefficient array on \(T\), and every independent product law with \(p_i\le1/2\),

\[
 \boxed{W_p(T)\le \Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\,q.}
\]

Consequently, on the uniform cube,

\[
 \boxed{W(T)\le\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\,k(F)},\qquad
 W(T)=\sum_{A\in M}2^{n-|A|},\quad k(F)=2^nq.
\]

This improves the blanket \(m^2\) incidence factor because

\[
 \Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\le I(T)\le m|M|\le m^2.
\]

It also exhibits width and cover bounds. With \(r=|M|\), \(\Delta=\max_A d_A\), maximum term width \(w=\max_{B\in T}|B|\), and maximum term cover \(c=\max_{B\in T}\nu(B)\),

\[
 \Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\le \min\!\left\{I(T),\ \sum_{A\in M}2^{d_A},\ r2^\Delta,\ mc,\ m\min(r,2^w),\ m^2\right\},
 \qquad d_A\le w-|A|.
\]

The most informative version is the per-minimum sum \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\); the coarser maxima are convenient when only width or cover data are available. The local degree floor is sharp: \(H(x)=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-x_i)\) has nonzero constant, degree \(d\), and exactly \(2^d\) monomials, while \(\Pr(H\ne0)=2^{-d}\) on fair bits.

### Proof

Fix \(A\in M\). For outside coordinates \(y\), define the coefficient polynomial on the \(A\)-fiber by

\[
 g_A(y)=\sum_{B\in T:A\subseteq B}c_B\,\mathbf 1[B\setminus A\subseteq y].
\]

It has nonzero constant term \(c_A\), exactly \(s_A\) monomials, and degree at most \(d_A\). In the slice \(F(\cdot,y)\), the coefficient of the full monomial on the coordinates of \(A\) is \(g_A(y)\). If this is nonzero, the slice cannot vanish on all \(2^{|A|}\) inputs: the Boolean zeta transform on the \(A\)-cube is invertible over every abelian group, by integer Möbius inversion. At least one assignment to \(A\) therefore makes \(F\ne0\). Each assignment has probability at least \(p_A\), since \(p_i\le1/2\). Averaging over \(y\) gives the exact fiber gate

\[
 q\ge p_A\Pr(g_A(Y)\ne0). \tag{1}
\]

The local sparse floor is stronger than \(1/s_A\): for any abelian group, a polynomial with nonzero constant and \(s\) actual terms has uniform nonzero probability at least \(1/\beta(s)\). A self-contained induction splits \(f=g+x_nh\), with \(u=g+h\) and actual coefficient counts \(a,b\) for \(g,h\), where \(a+b=s\) while cancellations can only reduce the count of \(u\). If \(u(0)\ne0\), both layers \(g,u\) have nonzero constants and their inductive floors are at least \(1/\beta(s)\). If \(u(0)=0\), both \(g,h\) have nonzero constants and \(\{h\ne0\}\subseteq\{g\ne0\}\cup\{u\ne0\}\). Hence the average layer rate is at least \(\tfrac12\max(1/\beta(a),1/\beta(b))\ge1/\beta(s)\), using \(\beta(\lfloor s/2\rfloor)=\beta(s)/2\). The induction uses only additive inverses and allows torsion. Product biases \(p_i\le1/2\) follow by thinning: retain coordinate \(i\) with probability \(2p_i\), otherwise set it to zero, then draw fair bits on retained coordinates. This deletes terms containing omitted variables, creates no collisions, and preserves the constant.

Independently, the degree bound \(\Pr(g_A\ne0)\ge2^{-d_A}\) follows from the maximum-degree monomial. For fixed retained coordinates, choose a nonzero term of maximum degree \(e\le d_A\), on support \(U\). After fixing coordinates outside \(U\), the coefficient of the full \(U\)-monomial remains nonzero: a strict superset would have degree greater than \(e\). The restricted function is therefore nonzero on at least one of the \(2^e\) settings of \(U\), so its uniform nonzero probability is at least \(2^{-e}\ge2^{-d_A}\). Thinning proves the same degree floor under product biases.

Thus \(\Pr(g_A\ne0)\ge\max(1/\beta(s_A),2^{-d_A})=1/\min(\beta(s_A),2^{d_A})\). Substituting in (1) gives \(p_A\le\min(\beta(s_A),2^{d_A})q\). Summing over \(A\in M\) proves the boxed inequality. The proof uses only addition and additive inverses in \(G\).

For the incidence estimates, each \(s_A\le m\), so \(I(T)\le mr\le m^2\). Also \(I(T)=\sum_B\nu(B)\le mc\), and \(c\le\min(r,2^w)\). Finally \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}\le\sum_A2^{d_A}\le r2^\Delta\) and \(d_A\le w-|A|\). These give the displayed comparisons.

## Exact OR-block counterexample and sharp family bound

Take disjoint variable blocks \(A,B\), each of size \(b\ge2\), and let \(T_b\) consist of every nonempty subset of \(A\) and every nonempty subset of \(B\). Then

\[
 m=2(2^b-1),\qquad M=\{\{i\}:i\in A\cup B\},\qquad
 W=2b\,2^{2b-1}=b2^{2b}.
\]

The OR polynomial on a block \(A\) has coefficient \((-1)^{|S|+1}\) on every nonempty \(S\subseteq A\). Thus \(F=g(\mathrm{OR}_A-\mathrm{OR}_B)\), for any fixed nonzero \(g\in G\), has all \(m\) coefficients nonzero and is nonzero exactly when the two block-OR values differ. Hence \(k=m\). In particular, for \(b=4\),

\[
 m=k=30,\qquad W=1024,\qquad mk=900<1024.
\]

The failure is exact even after optimizing coefficients on this support. For arbitrary nonzero coefficients write \(F=f(X)+h(Y)\), where \(|X|=|Y|=2^b=:N\). Since there is no constant term, \(f(0)=h(0)=0\); since their listed coefficient vectors are nonzero, Möbius inversion implies neither function is identically zero. Let \(z=|f^{-1}(0)|\) and \(w=|h^{-1}(0)|\), so \(1\le z,w\le N-1\). A zero of \(f+h\) is either a pair of zero values or a pair of nonzero values, whence

\[
 |(f+h)^{-1}(0)|\le zw+(N-z)(N-w)
 \le (N-1)^2+1.
\]

The last maximum is attained at a corner of \([1,N-1]^2\). Therefore \(k(F)\ge N^2-((N-1)^2+1)=2N-2=m\), and the OR difference attains equality. So the exact largest ratio \(W/k(F)\) over all coefficient arrays on this support is

\[
 \frac{W}{k_{\min}}=\frac{b2^{2b-1}}{2^b-1}.
\]

The correction needed in front of the original \(mk\) grows without bound:

\[
 \frac{W}{m k_{\min}}=
 \frac{b2^{2b}}{4(2^b-1)^2}\sim \frac b4.
\]

This rules out any universal constant \(C\) for a repaired inequality \(W\le Cmk\), even before tensoring. The assignment's fixed \(b=4\) witness also has disjoint tensor powers. For depth \(t\), expanding \(\prod_{j=1}^t(\mathrm{OR}_{A_j}-\mathrm{OR}_{B_j})\) gives coefficients \(\pm g\) on every support in the Cartesian product \(T_4^{\times t}\); no multiplication in \(G\) is used. Its values are \(0,\pm g\), and its nonzero set is the product of the local nonzero sets. Therefore

\[
 (m,k,W)=(30^t,30^t,1024^t),\qquad
 \frac{W}{mk}=\left(\frac{256}{225}\right)^t.
\]

For this family, every minimum has \(s_A=2^{b-1}\), \(d_A=b-1\), so \(\beta(s_A)=s_A\) and \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T_b)=2b\,2^{b-1}=b2^b\). The incidence matrix has rank \(2b\) because its columns at the singleton terms form the identity. Each term covers at most \(b\) minima, so \(I(T_b)=\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T_b)=b2^b\le mb\); the blanket count is \(m^2=4(2^b-1)^2\). At \(b=4\), the comparison is \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}=64\), \(mc=120\), and \(m^2=900\), while the exact necessary factor for \(W\le Ck\) is only \(512/15\). As \(b\to\infty\), \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}/(W/k_{\min})\to2\); the dyadic refinement does not change this obstruction family. It is not claimed optimal for arbitrary supports.

## Why the counterexample evades the existing closure theorems

The nonminimal terms \(E=T_b\setminus M\) are not an upper filter. For example, if \(a_1,a_2\in A\) and \(b_1\in B\), then \(\{a_1,a_2\}\in E\), but \(\{a_1,a_2,b_1\}\notin T_b\). The filter theorem needs every superset of every extra term.

At depth one, \(T_b\) is not a nontrivial Cartesian product on a coordinate partition. Every singleton is listed, so any product decomposition would force every cross-partition singleton pair to be listed. The pair graph of \(T_b\) is \(K_b\sqcup K_b\), with no cross-block edges; no nontrivial coordinate cut has all required cross pairs. At larger tensor depth the family is a Cartesian product of the depth-one supports, but its base fails both premises of the Cartesian-mask theorem: its all-nonzero OR-difference assignment violates the ordinary inequality, and the same assignment violates the minimal-mask bound.

Finally, for any nontrivial variable partition \(X|Y\), the projection of \(T_b\) onto \(X\) contains \(\emptyset\) (use a singleton term from \(Y\)) and a singleton (use a singleton term from \(X\)). The same holds for the other side. Neither outer projection is an antichain, so the ragged-antichain projection theorem does not apply.

These are structural failures, not a finite-scan limitation. The exact base proof applies for every \(b\ge2\), every finite abelian group, and every coefficient array on \(T_b\); the concrete OR-difference assignment gives the tensor counterexample at every depth.

## Evidence and scope

`structural_bound.py` constructs the exact integer ANF at tensor depths one and two, computes the value vector by the Boolean zeta transform, and checks \(m,k,W,s_A,d_A,\Lambda_{\rm dyad},I,c\) exactly. It also checks every nontrivial coordinate cut of the \(b=4\) base for Cartesian factorization. The symbolic formulas for arbitrary \(b,t\), the exact all-coefficient base lower bound, and the class exclusions are proved above; the finite receipt alone does not establish them.

For completeness, applying the same fiber gate to every \(A\in T\) gives an all-term bound. Put \(\rho_A=\min\{\beta(s_A),2^{d_A}\}\). Then
\[
\sum_{A\in T}p_A\le q\sum_{A\in T}\rho_A\le qD_m,
\qquad D_m=\sum_{j=1}^m\beta(j)\le\frac{m(m+1)}2.
\]
The triangular inequality follows because \(\rho_A\le s_A\) and \(\sum_A s_A\) counts reflexive comparable pairs in the inclusion poset. The sharper exact factor maximum \(D_m\) follows from a linear extension, which gives \(s_{A_i}\le m-i+1\); a chain \(\emptyset\subset\{1\}\subset\cdots\subset\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) attains equality in the structural sum. This is exactness of the factor sum, not a claim that the polynomial inequality is sharp for each \(m\). On the uniform cube it yields \(\sum_{A\in T}2^{n-|A|}\le D_m k(F)\).

The global \(W\le mk\) assertion is refuted. The dyadic structural factor \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)\) is a proved universal replacement for minimal mass, and is asymptotically within a factor two of the exact OR-block requirement on \(T_b\), but its optimality for general \(T\) remains open.
