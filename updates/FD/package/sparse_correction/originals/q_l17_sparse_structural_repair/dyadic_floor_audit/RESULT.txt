# Independent audit: dyadic sparse floor and structural-factor update

Date: 13 October 2026. Status: the q_s05d local dyadic floor and its fiber use are independently verified. The dyadic refinement is strict for some row counts, but it does not change the OR-block counterfamily (or the paired-matroid family reported by q_s10).

## Audited local theorem

Let (G) be any abelian additive group, (f:{0,1}^n\to G) a multilinear Boolean polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient, and (s) its actual number of nonzero coefficients. Define

\[
\beta(s)=2^{\lfloor\log_2s\rfloor}.
\]

Then on uniform independent bits

\[
\Pr[f\ne0]\ge 1/\beta(s).
\]

If the degree is at most (d), then also \(\Pr[f\ne0]\ge2^{-d}\), so

\[
\Pr[f\ne0]\ge 1/\min\{\beta(s),2^d\}.
\]

Both inequalities extend to independent product bits with each \(p_i\le1/2\). The proof needs only addition, additive inverses, and integer Möbius inversion; no division, field, torsion restriction, or finite-group assumption enters.

### Independent induction check

For the uniform sparse floor induct on the number of variables. Split
\(f(y,x_n)=g(y)+x_nh(y)\) and put \(u=g+h\). Let \(a,b\) be the actual coefficient counts of \(g,h\); the original supports split disjointly between terms omitting and containing \(n\), so \(a+b=s\), while cancellation can only reduce the actual count of \(u\).

The constant of \(g\) is the original nonzero constant. If \(u(0)\ne0\), both layers \(g,u\) have nonzero constants; their counts are at most \(a,s\), respectively, and the induction bounds their nonzero rates below by \(1/\beta(a)\) and \(1/\beta(s)\). Averaging the two layers gives at least \(1/\beta(s)\).

If \(u(0)=0\), then \(h(0)=-g(0)\ne0\), so induction applies to both \(g,h\). Pointwise \(h=u-g\), hence \(\{h\ne0\}\subseteq\{g\ne0\}\cup\{u\ne0\}\). Therefore
\[
\Pr[f\ne0]=\tfrac12(\Pr[g\ne0]+\Pr[u\ne0])
\ge\tfrac12\max(\Pr[g\ne0],\Pr[h\ne0])
\ge\frac1{2\beta(\min(a,b))}.
\]
Since \(\min(a,b)\le\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) and \(\beta(\lfloor s/2\rfloor)=\beta(s)/2\), this is at least \(1/\beta(s)\). The case \(b=0\) is the induction hypothesis for \(g\). This argument explicitly allows cancellation in \(u\); it never identifies its coefficient count with \(a+b\).

For the degree floor, choose a nonzero coefficient on a maximum-degree support \(D\), of size \(e\le d\). After fixing all other bits, the coefficient of the full \(D\)-monomial in the resulting \(D\)-variable polynomial remains that same coefficient: any term contributing to it would have support containing \(D\), and no strict superset exists by maximality of the degree. Integer Möbius inversion implies this slice is not identically zero. Each outside assignment therefore has at least one nonzero point among \(2^e\) settings, giving rate at least \(2^{-e}\ge2^{-d}\).

For product biases, draw independent \(Z_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(2p_i)\) and fair bits \(U_i\), and set \(X_i=Z_iU_i\). Conditional on \(Z\), this is a uniform cube on retained coordinates with every omitted coordinate fixed to zero. Terms containing an omitted variable vanish; terms contained in the retained set keep their original, distinct supports and coefficients. Thus the constant remains unchanged and the actual sparsity cannot increase. Degree cannot increase either. Apply the uniform result conditionally and average. This checks the only potential restriction/cancellation pitfall: zero-restriction deletes terms and creates no new constant contributions.

## Fiber application and corrected factors

Let (T\subseteq2^{[n]}) be the support of a polynomial whose listed coefficients are all nonzero in (G), and let \(p_i\le1/2\) be independent product biases. For any (A\in T), put
\[
s_A=|\{B\in T:A\subseteq B\}|,\qquad
d_A=\max_{B\in T:A\subseteq B}|B\setminus A|,\qquad
\rho_A=\min\{\beta(s_A),2^{d_A}\}.
\]
The fiber coefficient is
\[
g_A(y)=\sum_{B\in T:A\subseteq B}c_B\,\mathbf1[B\setminus A\subseteq y].
\]
Its constant is exactly \(c_A\ne0\), and it has exactly \(s_A\) distinct nonzero terms. In the slice fixing the outside variables to \(y\), the coefficient of the full \(A\)-monomial is exactly \(g_A(y)\). If this is nonzero, Möbius inversion on the \(A\)-cube says the slice has a nonzero value. Every point in the product law on \(A\) has mass at least \(p_A=\prod_{i\in A}p_i\). Consequently, with \(q=\Pr[F\ne0]\),
\[
q\ge p_A\Pr[g_A\ne0]\ge p_A/\rho_A,
\qquad p_A\le\rho_Aq. \tag{1}
\]
This handles cancellations in the slice explicitly: coefficients of the full \(A\)-monomial receive contributions only from supports containing all of \(A\), and those contributions are precisely the displayed fiber sum.

For the minimal set \(M=\operatorname{Min}(T)\), the corrected support-dependent factor is
\[
\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)=\sum_{A\in M}\min\{2^{\lfloor\log_2s_A\rfloor},2^{d_A}\},
\qquad \sum_{A\in M}p_A\le\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)q.
\]
On the uniform cube this is \(W(T)\le\Lambda_{\rm dyad}(T)k(F)\). The earlier \(\sum_A\min(s_A,2^{d_A})\) factor remains valid but is superseded; \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}\) can be strictly smaller when \(s_A\) is not a power of two.

The same per-term gate (1), now summed over all \(A\in T\), gives
\[
\sum_{A\in T}p_A\le q\sum_{A\in T}\rho_A.
\]
First, \(\rho_A\le s_A\). The sum \(\sum_{A\in T}s_A\) counts reflexive comparable ordered pairs in the inclusion poset on \(T\), so it is at most \(m+\binom m2=m(m+1)/2\). Thus the requested triangular factor is true (and replaces the old \(m^2\) fallback); there is no counterexample to it.

There is an exact sharper support-independent maximum for the dyadic factor. Choose a linear extension \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) of inclusion with every strict superset later than its subsets. Then \(s_{A_i}\le m-i+1\), and therefore
\[
\sum_{A\in T}\rho_A\le\sum_{i=1}^m\beta(m-i+1)=D_m,
\qquad D_m:=\sum_{j=1}^m2^{\lfloor\log_2j\rfloor}.
\]
This bound is attained as a structural factor by the chain \(T=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{1,2\},\ldots,\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\}\): the term with \(s_A=j\) has \(d_A=j-1\), so \(\rho_A=\beta(j)\). Hence \(D_m\) is the exact maximum of \(\sum_A\rho_A\) over all finite Boolean support families when ambient dimension may grow with \(m\). It is strictly below the triangular count for \(m\ge3\). This proves exactness of the structural-factor maximum, not sharpness of the resulting polynomial inequality for every \(m\).

Uniformly, the all-term form is
\[
\sum_{A\in T}2^{n-|A|}\le D_m\,k(F)\le\frac{m(m+1)}2\,k(F)\le m^2k(F).
\]
The original source's \(m^2\) statement was for a different, explicitly stated GF(2) all-term quantity; the all-abelian refinement above is proved here through (1), not inferred from that citation.

## Does the dyadic refinement alter the obstruction?

No for the OR-block family. For \(T_b\), each minimal singleton has \(s_A=2^{b-1}\) and \(d_A=b-1\), both powers of two in the relevant sense, so \(\rho_A=2^{b-1}\) and \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}=b2^b\), exactly the previous factor. For its depth-\(t\) tensor, \(s_A=2^{t(b-1)}\), \(d_A=t(b-1)\), and \(\Lambda_{\rm dyad}=(b2^b)^t\). In particular at \(b=4\), the dyadic bound is \(64^t\), while the exact \(W/k\) is \((512/15)^t\); the per-factor gap remains \(15/8\). Thus the dyadic ceiling improves some structural rows but does not repair the false universal coefficient-one minimal-mass claim or eliminate this counterfamily.

The paired-literal binary-matroid family independently reported by q_s10 is likewise unchanged under the supplied parameters: if \(s_A=2^{D-r}\) and \(d_A=D-r\), then \(\rho_A=2^{D-r}\). With \(2^rB\) minima its dyadic factor is \(B2^D\); compared with the reported exact factor \(B2^{2D}/m\), the ratio is \(m/2^D=2^rB/2^D\). This family can have a much larger gap than the OR family; this audit checks the algebraic effect of the dyadic cap, not q_s10's underlying construction or its independent optimality claims.
