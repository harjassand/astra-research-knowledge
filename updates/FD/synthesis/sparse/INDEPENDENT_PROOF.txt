1. Exact contract and disposition

Let G be an abelian additive group. Combine repeated monomial supports and remove
zero coefficients. For T subset 2^[n], |T|=m, all c_A != 0, set
 F(x)=sum_{A in T} c_A 1[A subset supp(x)], M=Min(T).
For independent Bernoulli coordinates with 0<=p_i<=1/2, write
 p_A=product_{i in A}p_i, S_p=sum_{A in M}p_A, q=Pr(F!=0).
For uniform bits write k=|supp F|, W=2^n S_p.

The exact conjecture archived in N782/N780's continuation context was S_p<=m q,
or equivalently W<=m k. It is FALSE, already over every nontrivial finite abelian
group. This changes that named conjecture, not the complete triangular decoder,
the inherited GF(2) all-term m^2 theorem, or arbitrary conditional sampling.

2. Direct all-group counterexample and arbitrary amplification

Take disjoint A,B of size b, any g!=0 in G, and set
 O_A=1-product_{i in A}(1-x_i), F_b=g(O_A-O_B).
Scalar polynomial notation means expand over integers, then map each coefficient
z to z*g. It assumes no multiplication in G. All 2(2^b-1) coefficients are +/-g,
so each is nonzero, even if g has order two. Every singleton is a minimal term.
The scalar value is 0,+1,-1. It is nonzero precisely when exactly one block is
zero; the other block can be any of 2^b-1 nonzero strings. Consequently
 m=k=2(2^b-1), W=(2b)2^(2b-1)=b2^(2b).
At b=4: m=k=30, W=1024>900. The exact proposal ratio is W/k=512/15>30.
These formulas hold in every nontrivial abelian G, without a torsion restriction.

Also W/(mk)=b/[4(1-2^-b)^2] tends to infinity. Hence no fixed C repairs W<=Cmk.
For t disjoint copies, first take the integer scalar product of the t factors,
then multiply its coefficients by g. Each support has a unique factorization,
its coefficient is +/-g, and its value is 0 or +/-g. Thus
 (m,k,W)=([2(2^b-1)]^t,[2(2^b-1)]^t,[b2^(2b)]^t).
For b=12 any alpha<log(W_12/m_12)/log(m_12) and fixed C fails eventually.
That exponent lies strictly between
 1.121977691996127544277265 and 1.121977691996127544277266.
This lower bound applies for EACH fixed nontrivial abelian group.

The zero-output integer zeta rows annihilate the integer +/-1 coefficient
vector v. Its dot product with each coordinate unit is +/-1. Thus their integer
row lattice contains no coordinate unit, refuting the earlier proposed lattice
implication directly; no rational-rank-to-integral inference is required.

3. Exact coefficient-optimized two-block scope

On the support of all nonempty subsets within A or B, an arbitrary coefficient
assignment gives F=f(X)+h(Y), with f(0)=h(0)=0, neither function identically zero
by integral Boolean Mobius inversion. Put U=2^|A|, V=2^|B|, z=|f^-1(0)|,
w=|h^-1(0)|. Then 1<=z<=U-1, 1<=w<=V-1. A zero pair is either zero/zero or
nonzero/nonzero, so zeros(F)<=zw+(U-z)(V-w). The maximum of this bilinear
function over the rectangle is (U-1)(V-1)+1: the competing corner value U+V-2
is smaller by (U-2)(V-2). Therefore k>=U+V-2=m, attained by the OR difference.
This proves the exact minimum k for all coefficient assignments on this base
support in every G. It makes no corresponding arbitrary-coefficient assertion
for its tensor powers.

4. Local dyadic floor, including arbitrary torsion

If c_empty!=0 and the actual coefficient count is s, let
 beta(s)=2^floor(log2 s).
Then for uniform bits Pr(F!=0)>=1/beta(s). Induct on n. Write F=g+x_n h,
u=g+h and let a,b be the actual coefficient counts of g,h, with a+b=s.
The constant of g is nonzero. If b=0, use induction. If u(0)!=0, both g,u
have nonzero constants and at most s terms, so both layer rates are at least
1/beta(s). If u(0)=0, h(0)=-g(0)!=0 and supp(h) subset supp(g) union supp(u).
Thus the equally weighted layer rate is at least
 (1/2)max(Pr(g!=0),Pr(h!=0))>=1/[2 beta(min(a,b))]>=1/beta(s).
The final inequality follows from min(a,b)<=floor(s/2).
Every operation used is addition, an inverse, or comparison with zero in G.

The maximum-degree monomial argument separately gives uniform rate>=2^-d:
fix all outside coordinates of a maximum-degree support U; its full U-fiber
coefficient cannot change, since there is no strict superset term. Integral
Mobius inversion makes the fiber nonzero at least once. For the nonzero-constant
case both bounds extend to product p_i<=1/2 by X_i=Z_i U_i with independent
Z_i~Bernoulli(2p_i), fair U_i. Conditional on Z, fixing omitted variables to zero
deletes terms, preserves the constant, and cannot raise degree or sparsity.
Therefore, for nonzero constant and degree<=d,
 Pr(F!=0)>=1/min(beta(s),2^d).
The nonzero constant is essential under biased bits: F=g x_1 has rate p_1.

The weaker 1/s floor is sharp exactly for face products g product_{i in D}(1-x_i).
For completeness, equality s k=2^n forces the cancellation branch above,
a=b=s/2, and u identically zero: otherwise |supp(g)|+|supp(u)| exceeds the
inductive minimum. Hence h=-g coefficientwise and F=(1-x_n)g. Induction gives
precisely the face product; the converse is immediate. No new torsion equality
case exists. This does not classify all equality cases of the stronger dyadic
floor when s is not a power of two.

5. Universal support-dependent replacement and a new exact factor maximum

For every A in T define
 s_A=|{B in T:A subset B}|, d_A=max_{B in T:A subset B}|B minus A|,
 rho_A=min(beta(s_A),2^d_A),
 g_A(y)=sum_{B in T:A subset B}c_B y_(B minus A).
It has nonzero constant c_A, s_A actual terms and degree d_A. It is exactly the
full A-coefficient of the A-fiber of F. If nonzero, that fiber has a nonzero
point by integer Mobius inversion. Each point in the product law on A has
probability at least p_A, so q>=p_A Pr(g_A!=0)>=p_A/rho_A.
Summing proves, for ANY R subset T,
 sum_{A in R}p_A <= q sum_{A in R}rho_A.
In particular S_p<=Lambda(T)q, Lambda=sum_{A in M}rho_A, and W<=Lambda k.
This establishes the retained m^2 fallback over all abelian groups by an
explicit new proof rather than by silently extending the old GF(2) citation.

New integration-derived refinement: the exact maximum of this MINIMAL structural
factor over support families with m terms, when dimension is free, is
 E_m=max_{p a power of two, 1<=p<=m} p(m+1-p) <= (m+1)^2/4.
Proof: if r=|M|, a minimal A has at most m-r strict supersets because the other
r-1 minima are incomparable. Hence s_A<=m-r+1 and
 Lambda<=r beta(m-r+1). For fixed beta(s)=p this expression is largest at s=p,
or r=m+1-p, giving E_m. Conversely choose r singleton minima and m-r distinct
common supersets that form a chain, adding a fresh variable at each step. Then
all s_A=m-r+1; if there are supersets, all d_A=m-1, so rho_A=beta(m-r+1).
If m=r, all s_A=1,d_A=0 and the same equality holds.
This is exactness of the STRUCTURAL FACTOR MAXIMUM, not the best polynomial
inequality. It improves the general safe factor, with E_30=240, while the OR4
support has its much smaller support-specific Lambda=64. The true extremal
proposal exponent remains open.

For ALL terms, a linear extension bounds s_{A_i}<=m-i+1, so
 sum_{A in T}p_A <= D_m q, D_m=sum_{j=1}^m beta(j)<=m(m+1)/2<=m^2.
Chains attain D_m as a structural sum, not necessarily as an actual ratio.
The old GF(2) no-constant ZERO-event corollary does not extend this way: over
Z/4, x_1+x_2+x_3 is zero only at 000, rate 1/8<1/(3+1).

6. Paired literals: stronger lower exponent with separate group quantifiers

For a rank-r binary matrix A with D columns a_j, introduce Boolean y_(j,0),
y_(j,1), and set over GF(2)
 H_A(y)=sum_{v in F2^r} product_j(1+y_(j,<a_j,v>)).
For a selected set of literals S, its ANF coefficient is the parity of solutions
to the corresponding affine equations. It is 1 precisely when S selects at
most one literal per column, those columns span, and their labels are consistent.
For each spanning column-position set C there are 2^r such labels. With P(A)
the spanning-subset count and B(A) the basis count,
 m=2^r P(A), minima_count=2^r B(A), minimum_degree=r, S=B(A).
On a truth assignment, a double-one pair kills every v; a double-zero pair
imposes no constraint; a singleton-one pair imposes the opposite label equation.
Thus global label swapping bijects coefficient support to truth support:
 k=m, W=B(A)2^(2D), R=W/k.
These statements hold over GF(2), and after mapping 1 to an element of order two.
No version for odd-order groups is claimed.

Choose D random independent uniform columns in F2^r. A given r-position set is
a basis with probability kappa_r=product_{i=1}^r(1-2^-i)>1/4. Therefore some
actual rank-r matrix has B>=kappa_r binom(D,r). As P<=2^D,
 m=k<=2^(D+r), W>=kappa_r binom(D,r)2^(2D).
Consequently alpha(A)=log(W/k)/log(m) is at least
 1+[log2(kappa_r binom(D,r))-2r]/(D+r).
The binomial entropy estimate gives, as r/D approaches x in (0,1), the limiting
lower expression 1+[H_2(x)-2x]/(1+x). Its derivative has the sign of
 log2((1-x)^2/x)-2, so the unique maximum is at x=3-2sqrt(2).
Substitution gives alpha_*=log2(1+sqrt(2))=1.2715533031636119726...
For EACH fixed C and EACH alpha<alpha_*, a finite GF(2) polynomial therefore
violates W<=C m^alpha k. Tensor powers of any strict finite example also
amplify it. These are finite existential witnesses; matrices in the limiting
argument are not individually identified or enumerated. The same conclusion
holds in each fixed group containing a nonzero order-two element. A universal
bound quantified over ALL finite groups must in particular cover these groups.

The specified rank-8,D=47 matrix is separately identified in INDEPENDENT_RECEIPT.
Independent low-pivot RREF enumeration of all 417199 subspaces gives exactly
 B=96991671, P=140675317510576, m=k=36012881282707456,
 W=1921117968129514859726486638757412864,
 1.191504627393948753655902<alpha(A)<1.191504627393948753655903.
The counting identities are
 P=sum_{U<=F2^r}mu(U,V)2^n_U,
 B=sum_{U<=F2^r}mu(U,V)binom(n_U,r),
 mu(U,V)=(-1)^c 2^(c(c-1)/2), c=r-dim(U).
They follow by Mobius inversion of the unique span of each column subset;
the subspace mu follows inductively from Gaussian binomial identities. The
finite check exhausts subspaces, not the 2^94 truth table or expanded ANF.

7. Matched sampler and cost scope

Assume q>0. The original minimal-term mixture chooses A with probability p_A/S_p, then
samples X conditioned on A subset X. Its proposal density is d(X)/S_p, where
d(X) counts satisfied minimal terms. Reject zero F; otherwise accept with 1/d(X).
The accepted law is exactly the product law conditioned on F!=0. Its expected
proposal count is S_p/q, exactly W/k for fair bits. It is this count, not all
sampler complexity, that the failed conjecture and new exponent obstruct.
Computing minima, weights, rho_A, and evaluating F/d are charged preprocessing
and per-proposal work on the explicit m-term representation. Group operations
and equality to zero must be supplied effectively; abstract G alone is not a
bit-complexity implementation. Sampling rational biases also has its own cost.

The factored OR witnesses have an exact direct sampler: choose which block is
zero with side weights proportional to 2^a-1 and 2^b-1, then draw a nonzero
uniform string in the other block. Repeat independently over factors. In the
balanced case random bits and local work are O(n) expected, after the factor
representation is supplied/verified. Reading the expanded polynomial is paid.

Given A, the paired-literal example also has a direct exact sampler. Draw a
uniform column subset C until it spans, then independent uniform v in F2^r;
put the unique opposite-label one at columns in C, zero elsewhere. The pair
(C,v) is in bijection with the support. Success is rho=P/2^D, expected random
bits D/rho+r, and binary elimination costs O(r^2 D)/rho bit operations plus n
output bits (or O(rD)/rho word operations for r-bit machine-word columns).
For the rank-8 witness rho=8792207344411/8796093022208>0.99955.
No representation acquisition, black-box recognition, arbitrary-sampler lower
bound, physical advantage, or civilisation-changing breakthrough follows.
