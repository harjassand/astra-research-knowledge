Use lattice spacing and hbar equal to one. The physical model is

The implemented case has t=1,U=4,d=2. No diagonal interaction is discarded. No strong-dephasing expansion, artificial monitoring, added physical damping, or supplied relaxation gap is used. The mathematical argument also applies to finite-range affine **diagonal** interaction controls with the same structural hypotheses and an acquired local coefficient bound.

The state defining response is the normalized infinite-temperature product trace, including all particle-number sectors. Let n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, chi=1/4 and
\[
j_i=\frac t2(X_iY_{i+1}-Y_iX_{i+1}),\qquad J_n=\sum_i j_i.

The infinite output is
\[
D(V)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T\chi}
 \sum_x x^2\langle e^{T\mathcal L_V}(n_x-1/2),n_0-1/2\rangle.
\]
The finite output is D_n=4 n^{-1}<J_n,(-L_V)^{-1}J_n>, with the inverse taken outside the conserved fixed points. Neither output is a finite-temperature conductivity or a Gaussian-profile claim.

Exact local coefficients and the structural model are inputs. Physical acquisition, translation invariance, finite range, Markovianity, and calibration are not inferred from the numerical certificates. No laboratory material was tested.

## 2. Why this route was selected

The preceding TT construction repairs a given conservation defect at a controlled representation cost. It does not control the rank of a near-minimum-energy repair or the successive fields generated by inversion. Its full-model pipeline was less accurate than the earlier local certificates. Improving that projection in isolation would not satisfy the current scientific objective.

The alternative retained here is output-specific. Two nearby physical controls can have substantial shared uncertainty in their absolute transport values. Subtracting independent intervals pays that uncertainty twice. A derivative of a **corrected physical output** instead has an exact error involving products of actual response residuals and their derivatives. It can certify an improvement or a near-optimal setting without resolving the common absolute offset.

This is related to established goal-oriented/primal-adjoint correction, not a universal new principle that all inverse problems compress. The substantive questions answered here are: how to make those derivative errors independent of a hidden population relaxation scale; how to preserve exact quantum population balance; how to transfer the bounds to the actual infinite-system response; and whether the resulting full physical decision is stronger at meaningful cost.

## 3. Exact residual identity with degenerate physical dissipation

### 3.1 Parity and regularization used only in the proof

Work first on a finite periodic chain in the real Hilbert space of Hermitian observables, with per-site normalized trace. Write
\[
B_V=-\mathcal L_V=S+A_V,\qquad A_V=-i[H(V),\cdot].
\]
S is nonnegative self-adjoint and A_V skew-adjoint. Real H makes A exchange imaginary-antisymmetric (odd) and real-symmetric (even) Hermitian observables. Define the involution R to be +1 on odd and -1 on even observables. Then
\[
RS=SR,\quad RA=-AR,\quad Rj=j,\quad (RB)^*=RB.
\]
This R is a linear involution on the real observable Hilbert space and its complexification; no anti-linear convention is used in the resolvent differentiation.

Let P be complete occupation dephasing. Choose finite polynomial trials f(V),g(V), odd and even respectively, such that
\[
PA_V f(V)=0
\]
**exactly**, in the translation quotient. Individual Pauli words need not commute with total number, but the implemented even-X/Y sector has nonzero dissipative rate at least d. The current and exact solution lie in the number-neutral invariant subspace. Using a larger trial space cannot invalidate the variational inequalities.

For lambda>0, set S_lambda=S+lambda I and
\[
T_{V,\lambda}=I+S_\lambda^{-1/2}A_VS_\lambda^{-1/2}.
\]
At real V its inverse has norm at most one. This is a normalized finite-dimensional accretivity statement; lambda is removed below. It is not extra physical noise or an algorithmic long-time cutoff.

Let H1=dH/dV=(1/4)sum_i Z_i Z_(i+2). More generally, let
\[
J_*(H_1)=\sup_i\sum_{X\ni i}|(H_1)_X|,
\qquad w=4J_*(H_1)/d.
\]
For two occupation configurations differing on q sites, the H1 energy difference is at most 2J_* q, while the dephasing rate is dq/2. Hence
\[
\|S_\lambda^{-1/2} A_1 S_\lambda^{-1/2}\|\le w,
\qquad A_1=-i[H_1,\cdot],
\]
uniformly in volume and lambda. A1 annihilates diagonal inputs and has no population outputs. Here J_*=1/2 and w=1. No extensive Hamiltonian norm enters.

### 3.2 Corrected response functional

Write y=f+g. Define, initially with S_lambda,
\[
\mathcal F_\lambda(V)=2\langle j,f\rangle-\langle f,S_\lambda f\rangle
 +\langle g,S_\lambda g\rangle+2\langle A_Vf,g\rangle.
\]
This equals 2<j,y>-<y,R(B+lambda)y>. Let x=(B+lambda)^{-1}j and let r_lambda=S_lambda^{-1/2}[(B+lambda)y-j]. Square completion gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\kappa_{n,\lambda}(V)-\mathcal F_\lambda(V)
 =\langle r_\lambda,R T_{V,\lambda}^{-1}r_\lambda\rangle.}\tag{1}
\]
For the per-site convention j is the extensive current normalized through the inner product. All displayed identities can equivalently be written with J_n and an explicit factor 1/n.

Proof: RB is symmetric and Rj=j. The exact stationary value is <j,x>. The functional error is <x-y,R(B+lambda)(x-y)>. Substituting the residual and using B*R=RB gives (1). Normalization commutes with R. No Galerkin orthogonality or accurate optimizer is needed.

The population component of the unnormalized residual is lambda Pg, since PAf=0. Its normalized norm tends to zero as lambda goes to zero. Thus the residual norm has the finite limit obtained by dividing nonzero Pauli damping rates and never dividing an unbalanced population residual by zero.

## 4. Response-error jets: a complete a posteriori derivative theorem

A jet is simply a finite list of derivatives of a chosen trial polynomial. Around V0 write
\[
f(V_0+s)=\sum_{k=0}^{M}f_k\frac{s^k}{k!},\qquad
 g(V_0+s)=\sum_{k=0}^{M}g_k\frac{s^k}{k!}.
\]
Require PA0 f_k=0 for every k. Because the varied interaction is diagonal, this is exactly the balance condition for every real s, not only at V0.

Generate the **full** residual derivatives
\[
r^o_k=S f_k+A_Vg_k+kA_1g_{k-1}-\delta_{k0}j,
\]
\[
r^e_k=S g_k+A_Vf_k+kA_1f_{k-1},
\]
with absent indices interpreted as zero. The residual degree is M+1, so that final order must be retained even when no trial coefficient of that order is present. Put
\[
q_k^2=\|r^o_k\|_{\#,S^+}^2+\|r^e_k\|_{\#,S^+}^2.
\]
Every term is an exact finite translation-covariance sum, including outputs outside the discovery dictionary.

Let
\[
\widehat D(V)=4\mathcal F(V)
\]
be the corrected output polynomial after removing lambda. Then
\[
\boxed{|D^{(m)}(V)-\widehat D^{(m)}(V)|
 \le 4m!\sum_{a+b+c=m}\frac{q_aq_b}{a!b!}w^c.}\tag{2}
\]
At a general real V, q_k means the norm of the kth derivative of the complete residual polynomial evaluated there. In particular,
\[
E_1=4(2q_0q_1+wq_0^2),\tag{3}
\]
\[
E_2=4(2q_0q_2+2q_1^2+4wq_0q_1+2w^2q_0^2).\tag{4}
\]

Proof: At finite volume and lambda, differentiate (1). Since the parameter dependence of T is affine,
\[
\partial_V^c T^{-1}=(-1)^c c!\,T^{-1}(K_1T^{-1})^c,
\quad \|\partial_V^cT^{-1}\|\le c!w^c.
\]
Leibniz differentiation of the two residuals and the inverse gives the multinomial coefficient m!/(a!b!c!). The inverse bound cancels c! in that denominator, yielding (2). R has norm one. This is a real-derivative identity; complexification justifies analytic limiting steps, not the use of a conjugate-dependent function as a holomorphic scalar.

### 4.1 No Taylor remainder of the true response is guessed

The trial fields are finite polynomials. Their corrected output is a polynomial, but is not asserted to be the true response Taylor series. Equation (2) bounds the actual error at every real parameter from the actual residual polynomial there. Real intervals may be wider than a Neumann-series radius; the inverse bound at each real parameter still holds.

The exact residual Gram matrices
\[
G^{o,e}_{ab}=\langle r^{o,e}_a,r^{o,e}_b\rangle_{\#,S^+}
\]
produce the polynomial norms q_k(s)^2. Rational Bernstein coefficients enclose each such polynomial over a control cell. Upward-rounded rational square roots yield certified q_k bounds. This retains cancellations between residual derivative coefficients rather than replacing the residual by a sum of independent absolute errors.

The ordinary value envelopes for the identical trials are
\[
D_{\rm low}(V)=\widehat D(V)-4\|r^e(V)\|_{\#,S^+}^2,
\]
\[
D_{\rm up}(V)=\widehat D(V)+4\|r^o(V)\|_{\#,S^+}^2.\tag{5}
\]
Their width is exactly 4q0^2. These identities are separately verified, not inferred from optimizer convergence.

## 5. Why the derivative certificates apply to infinite-system transport

This section does not assume the later candidate quasipolynomial completeness theorem or a compact tensor inverse.

### 5.1 Finite-packet stabilization

Centered local Pauli densities are identified under translation, with
\[
\langle f,g\rangle_\#=\sum_x\langle f,\tau_xg\rangle_{\rm tr}.
\]
Canonical distinct translation words are orthonormal. For all words of footprint at most W, periodized per-site Gram matrices agree exactly with this covariance Gram matrix whenever n>2W. A unique long empty complementary arc on such a ring identifies the underlying linear word. The statement applies to all trial derivatives and all generated commutator outputs, not only to the source dictionary.

Thus every finite-lambda residual expression has an exactly stable per-site finite representation at all sufficiently large n. Exact quotient balance periodizes to exact ring balance.

### 5.2 Existence and identification of the physical scalar

For completeness, the needed argument from the earlier source is reconstructed here. Put C_n(t)=n^{-1}<J_n,e^{tL_n}J_n>, a=t^2/2. Finite-range Markovian locality and the product trace imply a fixed-time limit C(t), while contractivity gives |C_n(t)|<=a. For lambda>0,
\[
\kappa(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}C(t)dt
 =\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{-1}\langle J_n,(B_n+\lambda)^{-1}J_n\rangle.
\]
The parity Schur operator is
\[
T_{n,\lambda}^{o}=S_o+\lambda+C_{eo}^*(S_e+\lambda)^{-1}C_{eo}.
\]
Its version with the scalar lambda subtracted is at least d and increases as lambda decreases. Resolvent subtraction gives an error at most lambda a/d^2 between the two scalar inverses. This proves existence of finite kappa0=lim(lambda down0)kappa(lambda), with kappa(lambda)<=a/(d+lambda).

The even extension of C_n has nonnegative Fourier density
\[
(\pi n)^{-1}\operatorname{Re}\langle J_n,(B_n-i\omega)^{-1}J_n\rangle\ge0.
\]
On the fixed-point complement the finite semigroup decays: an undamped eigenvector must be diagonal by dissipation, and its hopping commutator is off diagonal, so it can only be a zero mode constant in each charge sector. Contractivity excludes zero Jordan blocks. Thus the Fourier statement follows directly. Pointwise limits preserve positive definiteness, giving a finite even measure nu with C(t)=integral cos(omega t)nu(domega).

For nu_s(E)=nu(sE)/s, its Poisson transforms converge to kappa0. The tails satisfy
\[
\int_{|y|>R}y^{-2}\nu_s(dy)\le\frac{2a}{Rd}.
\]
Local boundedness, uniqueness of the even Stieltjes transform, and that tail estimate show that nu_s converges to (kappa0/pi)dy in the sense needed to integrate 2(1-cos y)/y^2. The continuity equation gives the second-moment second derivative 2C(t). Consequently D=4kappa0. This proves only the second-moment coefficient, not Gaussian hydrodynamics or a physical-time convergence rate.

### 5.3 Derivatives pass through the limits

At any real center Vc the normalized resolvent extends to |s|w<1, uniformly in n and lambda, with
\[
|\kappa_{n,\lambda}(V_c+s)|\le \frac{b_0}{1-w|s|},\qquad b_0=t^2/(2d).
\]
This follows from the norm-one real inverse, the bounded perturbation K1, and the normalized current norm. Pointwise convergence on a real interval and this locally uniform holomorphic bound imply convergence of all derivatives on smaller complex disks (normal-family/Vitali argument). Apply first n to infinity at fixed lambda, then lambda down0. Limits at overlapping disks agree on their real interval.

The finite trial's population residual is lambda Pg and vanishes in the normalized limit. The remaining residual Gram expressions converge to their explicitly calculated S+ norms. Therefore (2) holds for the actual infinite D and, separately, every D_n beyond the common footprint threshold. There is no unregularized exchange of size and time limits.

## 6. Construction: the same two matrices generate every trial derivative

At fixed explicit dictionaries, f=N u uses an exact rational nullspace N of every population-output row. The lower and upper quadratic matrices are
\[
H_f=N^T(S_f+A_f^TS_e^+A_f)N,
\qquad
H_g=S_g+A_g^TS_o^{-1}A_g.
\]
The cross block in the least-squares residual cancels by skew-adjointness and exact balance. At each derivative order, only the right-hand side changes, through k A1 times the previous trial coefficient. The same two matrices are reused. Conjugate-gradient solves are numerical proposals; their stopping residuals are never accepted as a physical error certificate.

Fresh dictionary acquisition starts from every allowed reflection-paired word through footprint eight. The implemented symmetry sector uses even X/Y count, odd global-X parity, and the required Y parity. Ten deterministic enrichment stages add at most 5000 words per parity, selected using full zeroth/first/second error-jet objective gradients. Maximum retained footprint is eighteen. Selection thresholds and caps are heuristic; failure returns a wide certificate or a failed run, not an inaccurate accepted answer.

Free coefficients are rounded to a dyadic grid, and dependent f coefficients are reconstructed through the exact nullspace. All original-physics commutators are regenerated. The verifier checks integer assembly range, integer multiplication overflow bounds, exact population balance, exact scalar functionals, the independently expanded residual gap, full residual Gram matrices, and support metadata.

The final central dictionary has 53,088 f variables and 53,024 g variables. Four trial orders are stored. There are 211,580 nonzero f jet coefficients and 212,096 nonzero g jet coefficients. Each coefficient multiplies a reflection-antisymmetric pair of translation orbits, with squared norm two. The four interaction/derivative commutator matrices contain 3,038,619 nonzeros. Output footprint is twenty, giving n>=41.

Neither an exponential Hilbert-space state nor an exact Hodge projection is supplied. Exact balance is solved as a finite sparse rational constraint. This explicitly pays representation acquisition on the executed instance; it does not bound dictionary size uniformly in requested accuracy.

### 7.1 Global uniqueness is certified, not assumed

Seven newly solved value-anchor packets give
\[
\sup_{V\in[0,2]}D(V)<0.6019497444,
\qquad
\sup_{V\in[2.5,3]}D(V)<0.5982063587.
\]
Both are below Dlow(V0). Each anchor's known upper certificate is a convex quadratic; maxima over rational cells occur at their endpoints. The envelope is checked on 1024 cells per interval and covers the continuous domain, not only grid points.

The central residual jet gives the uniform curvature bound
\[
-0.097059755\le D''(V)\le-0.077258662
\quad(2\le V\le2.5).
\]
Thus D is strictly concave on the only remaining possible optimizer interval. The exact derivative bands prove
\[
D'(2.157)>0.0000900320,\qquad D'(2.175)<-0.0000841448.
\]
A unique stationary point lies between them. It is the unique global maximizer on [0,3]. No statement is made about parameters outside this domain.

### 7.2 Fourth-order decision accuracy from quadratic residuals

If D''<=-mu between a proposed setting and the optimizer, and |D'(V0)|<=g, then integration of the curvature inequality and maximizing the resulting quadratic give
\[
\boxed{\sup D-D(V_0)\le g^2/(2\mu).}\tag{6}
\]
Here the root bracket and V0 lie in [2.15,2.19], where
\[
-0.092064776\le D''\le-0.085972016.
\]
Use the exact g=0.0007155131623414562... and mu=0.08597201641798909... to obtain regret <=2.9774751530472833e-6.

This is the important scaling distinction. When q0,q1=O(eta), the output-derivative error (3) is O(eta^2). Selecting a near-stationary corrected output then gives O(eta^4/mu) design regret, even though the absolute value interval remains O(eta^2). The curvature must itself be certified through (4); it is not a free strong-concavity assumption.

If the actual V setting is within 1/1024 of V0, the additional loss is bounded by g delta+M delta^2/2, using the certified |D''| bound on the same interval. The total global regret remains below 3.721e-6. This covers only control-setting uncertainty in V. U, t, d, and structural model errors are not silently included.

### 7.3 An improvement invisible to independent value intervals

Integrate the derivative error certificate from V0 to the old proposed control 71/32. The resulting correlated difference bound is
\[
D(V_0)-D(71/32)\in[0.0000852564,\ 0.0001633436].
\]
The corresponding independent value subtraction using the **same new polynomial trials** gives the much wider interval
\[
[-0.0001375651,\ 0.0003853014],
\]
which cannot establish the sign of the improvement. The new guarantee uses shared error structure, not artificially more accurate input values.

With all identical new packets, a value-only continuous global envelope gives regret <=0.0002573809386. The residual-jet certificate improves that bound by a factor 86.44. The older published-in-conversation design certificate had regret <0.002524 with different, smaller data; no runtime speedup is inferred from that comparison.

## 10. Matched prior-art assessment

Goal-oriented error correction, corrected output functionals, primal-dual product errors, sensitivity bounds, reduced bases, derivative reuse, and interval global optimization all have substantial prior art. They are not individually renamed as inventions.

- Guo and Zhong, *Strict upper and lower bounds for quantities of interest in static response sensitivity analysis*, arXiv:1611.05253 (2016): the primary abstract explicitly describes strict sensitivity-derivative bounds through constitutive-relation error. This prevents claiming invention of certified sensitivities. Full-text retrieval failed here; no theorem is imported from the abstract.
- Janon, Nodet and Prieur, *Goal-oriented error estimation for the reduced basis method, with application to sensitivity analysis*, arXiv:1303.6618v2 (2014), Sections 1.1–1.4: existing corrected-output bounds and an explicitly proposed probabilistic reduced-output bound. The actual HTML source was inspected. The present bounds are deterministic and address a degenerate dissipator and thermodynamic response; this is a scope distinction, not a claim to supersede reduced-basis theory.
- Wu and Zhang, *Goal-Oriented Error Estimation for Least-Squares Finite Element Methods via Physically Meaningful Adjoint PDEs*, arXiv:2607.23850 (July 2026): primary abstract describes primal-adjoint corrected functionals and product errors without Galerkin orthogonality. The full HTML was not accessible; no result is imported. The analogous algebra here is explicitly reconstructed.
- Von Keyserlingk, Pollmann and Rakovszky, *Operator backflow and the classical simulation of quantum transport*, arXiv:2111.09904 (2022): a strong specialised transport comparator. No head-to-head runtime superiority was measured. The comparison actually executed is with independent value certificates using identical witness data.
- Brehmer et al., *Reduced basis surrogates for quantum spin systems based on tensor networks*, Physical Review E 108, 025306 (2023): uses selected MPS ground-state snapshots and reduced many-query observables. Its state, acquisition and accuracy interface differs from these infinite-temperature transport derivative certificates. No blanket claim that existing quantum surrogates require exponential work is made.

The potentially useful combined contribution is: a population-safe, volume-uniform residual-error-jet certificate; a deterministic compiler of its full commutator data; direct thermodynamic derivative transfer; and a stronger executed continuous physical design result. Historical priority remains unverified.

## 12. What has and has not changed

This result does not establish a bound C_physical^N epsilon^-c for minimum-energy projections or interacting inverse ranks. It would be incorrect to infer such a bound from one successful local dictionary or from the quadratic error identity.

It does establish a stronger **full macroscopic design capability** than the previous local-value certificates. The physical observable is not replaced by a Hodge energy or a surrogate dynamical model. The scalar design error can be orders of magnitude smaller than the uncertainty in the absolute transport coefficient, because common response error need not be paid independently at each control.

A precise general next theorem is output-specific approximation complexity: construct balanced response jets with q0,q1 and curvature-certifying q2 below given tolerances at controlled total dictionary/rank/bit cost across a consequential nonperturbative class. A theorem that these jets are cheaper than a normwise inverse would suffice for design; a small exact representation or cheap matrix-vector multiplication alone would not. The present implementation detects adequacy from a complete residual and may fail or become costly elsewhere.

Finite temperature, arbitrary spatial disorder, unmodeled noise, vanishing physical dephasing, Gaussian hydrodynamics, and unrestricted high-dimensional materials design remain outside the result. The mathematics is a model prediction, not evidence that the model is an established law of a particular device.
