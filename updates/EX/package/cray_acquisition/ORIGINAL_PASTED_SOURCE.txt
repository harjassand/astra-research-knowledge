CRAY GENESIS — Response-cell acquisition
This investigation produced a concrete acquisition mechanism, proofs, and executable tests—not an established civilisation-enabling breakthrough.
The candidate is response-cell acquisition: obtain selected microscopic coefficients, or selected pairs of local responses, and convert them into two-sided bounds on a macroscopic conductance without first reconstructing and solving the entire accurate model.
The strongest mathematical outcome is a connection between two costs usually treated separately:
The same discrepancy controls both the uncertainty in the macroscopic prediction and the sampling variance of the information needed to certify it.

In a favourable synthetic regime, the implementation reached a 5%-width confidence interval after reading 2,014 distinct coefficients from a 130,560-edge network. A stronger local construction repaired a substantially mismatched model, but the executed repair experiment acquired every coefficient. That distinction is central: the mechanism survives, while the consequential end-to-end acquisition advantage remains unestablished.

1. Selecting the obstruction
I screened correlated electronic structure, rare-event kinetics, goal-oriented experimental inference, and acquisition of expensive coefficients underlying macroscopic response.
The important comparison was not against brute force. Selected configuration interaction already combines selective representations with stochastic corrections. Weighted-ensemble methods already target rare transitions without simply waiting for them in an ordinary trajectory. Goal-oriented experimental design already seeks predictive information rather than full parameter identification. Merely adopting one of those objectives would not create a new capability. quantum-package.readthedocs.io
I selected the following question:
Can an accurate macroscopic response be certified while acquiring only a small fraction of the expensive microscopic information—and can the certificate itself determine when that shortcut is trustworthy?

The intended application would be a transport model whose local coefficients require costly microscopic calculations. The mathematical target established here is narrower: effective conductance on a finite, known, positive-coefficient network.
This does not assume that constructing a physically correct network is free. It also does not establish a model of interacting battery materials, discover missing reaction pathways, or certify electronic-structure approximations.
There is substantial nearby prior art. Degree-of-rate-control analysis already directs accurate calculations towards quantities that influence a rate, and probabilistic local methods for effective conductivity predate this investigation. The distinction targeted here is a finite-change, two-sided acquisition certificate, rather than sensitivity ranking alone. Whether the particular combined construction is historically new remains unresolved. Welcome to DTU Research Database
2. First mechanism: acquire the response-relevant corrections
Consider a connected network with cheap reference conductances \(c_e^0>0\) and expensive target conductances \(c_e>0\).
Let \(h_0\) be the reference potential with unit voltage between the terminals. Define
\[
C_0=\sum_e c_e^0(\nabla h_0)_e^2,
\qquad
p_e=\frac{c_e^0(\nabla h_0)_e^2}{C_0},
\qquad
r_e=\frac{c_e}{c_e^0}.
\]
Here \(p_e\) is the fraction of reference dissipation occurring on edge \(e\). It is a probability distribution available from the reference calculation.
Write
\[
A=\mathbb E_p[r],
\qquad
B=\mathbb E_p[r^{-1}].
\]
Then the true target conductance satisfies
\[
\boxed{\frac{C_0}{B}\le C(c)\le C_0A.}
\]
Why this is valid
The reference potential remains an admissible potential in the target network. Its target energy is \(C_0A\), giving an upper bound.
The reference current, normalised to carry unit total flow, remains exactly conserved in the target network. Its target resistance energy is \(B/C_0\), giving the reciprocal lower bound.
These are applications of established Dirichlet and Thomson principles, not a claim to have invented a new variational principle. arXiv
The acquisition consequence is that the entire target field is unnecessary for this enclosure. One needs two weighted averages of coefficient corrections.
An edge with \(p_e=0\) contributes neither trial voltage drop nor trial current. Its target coefficient need not be queried or bounded relative to its reference value, provided it remains positive. The enclosure protects against every possible effect of that coefficient—not because it cannot affect the target response, but because both trial bounds remain valid.
The relevant error is dispersion, not common bias
The deterministic interval’s endpoint ratio is
\[
\frac{C_0A}{C_0/B}=1+D,
\qquad D=AB-1.
\]
There is an exact identity
\[
\boxed{
D=\frac12\,
\mathbb E_{e,f\sim p}
\frac{(r_e-r_f)^2}{r_er_f}.
}
\]
Consequently, a common multiplicative correction \(r_e=\rho\) produces \(D=0\), even when \(\rho\) is far from one. What matters is variation in the correction across response-carrying edges.
This is stronger than requiring the cheap model to be uniformly close in absolute calibration. But it still has a decisive limitation:
More samples cannot fix a large \(D\). They only estimate a wide intrinsic interval more accurately.
That led to the second construction.
3. Stronger mechanism: acquire paired local responses
Instead of treating each coefficient as an independent acquisition object, partition the edges into cells \(K\). Cells may share boundary vertices.
For each cell, retain two pieces of reference information:
\[
E_K^0=\sum_{e\in K}c_e^0(\nabla h_0)_e^2,
\]
and the boundary injection pattern generated by the reference current.
After acquiring the target coefficients inside a requested cell, compute two complementary responses:
\[
D_K=
\min_{\substack{h=h_0\\\text{on the cell boundary}}}
\sum_{e\in K}c_e(\nabla h)_e^2,
\]
\[
N_K=
\min_{\substack{f\text{ has the reference}\\
\text{cell injection pattern}}}
\sum_{e\in K}\frac{f_e^2}{c_e}.
\]
The first allows the cell’s potential to relax while preserving its boundary values. The second allows its current to reroute while preserving its boundary injections.
These are acquired local solves, not supplied exact-response oracles. Every coefficient and both solves must be charged.
A global certificate from compatible local pieces
The resulting bounds are
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_0^2}{\sum_K N_K}
\le C(c)\le
\sum_K D_K.
}
\]
The local potentials agree at shared boundaries, so they assemble into a globally admissible potential. The local currents retain the correct cell injections, so their combination remains globally conserved.
The key local inequality is
\[
\boxed{D_KN_K\ge (E_K^0)^2.}
\]
Its proof is short. For any admissible local potential \(h\) and current \(f\), their boundary-work pairing is fixed:
\[
\sum_{e\in K}(\nabla h)_ef_e=E_K^0.
\]
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives the inequality, including when the two local minimisations are performed independently.
Now normalise:
\[
w_K=\frac{E_K^0}{C_0},
\qquad
u_K=\frac{D_K}{E_K^0},
\qquad
v_K=\frac{N_K}{E_K^0}.
\]
Zero-reference-work cells can be omitted. Then
\[
u_Kv_K\ge1,
\qquad
\boxed{
\frac{C_0}{\mathbb E_wv}
\le C(c)\le
C_0\mathbb E_wu.
}
\]
This creates the proposed interface:
Sample cells according to their reference work; acquire their paired responses; certify the global observable from those acquisitions.

The construction can repair the original approximation
Merging cells enlarges the space of permitted local relaxations. Therefore
\[
\sum_K D_K \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_K N_K
\]
can only decrease under coarsening. The upper conductance bound falls, and the lower bound rises.
Singleton-edge cells recover the first mechanism. One whole-network cell gives the exact answer, after terminal sets are contracted appropriately.
That endpoint is mathematically useful but computationally uninteresting: it acquires and solves the full target problem. Monotone improvement alone does not establish affordability.
4. The joint error–acquisition theorem
Suppose a certified ratio envelope is available on the positive-reference-work support:
\[
0<a\le r_e\le b,
\qquad R=b/a.
\]
No such envelope is required on zero-reference-work edges.
The local responses then obey
\[
a\le u_K\le b,
\qquad
b^{-1}\le v_K\le a^{-1}.
\]
Define
\[
A=\mathbb E_wu,
\qquad B=\mathbb E_wv,
\qquad \Delta=AB-1.
\]
The global deterministic enclosure has endpoint ratio \(1+\Delta\). I derived
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(u)}{A^2}\le R\Delta,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(v)}{B^2}\le R\Delta.
}
\]
Thus, when the acquired local representation gives a tight physical-response enclosure, the quantities needed to estimate that enclosure also have controlled relative variance.
Proof of the variance connection
For any positive random variable \(X\) with mean \(m\),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{(X-m)^2}{X}\right]
=m\left(m\mathbb E[X^{-1}]-1\right).
\]
If \(X\le b\), this implies
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{m^2}
\le
\frac bm\left(m\mathbb E[X^{-1}]-1\right).
\]
Apply this to \(u\). Because \(uv\ge1\),
\[
A\mathbb E[u^{-1}]-1\le AB-1=\Delta,
\]
and \(b/A\le R\). The reciprocal argument proves the bound for \(v\).
No spatial independence or stationarity assumption is needed. The target coefficients can be arbitrary and fixed; the randomness comes from the acquisition algorithm.
Separating two kinds of unresolved error
Define the probability distribution
\[
q_K=\frac{w_Ku_K}{A}.
\]
The discrepancy decomposes exactly as
\[
\boxed{
\Delta=
\chi^2(w\Vert q)
+
A\mathbb E_w\!\left[v-\frac1u\right].
}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative.
The first measures redistribution of response work between cells. The second measures incompatibility between a cell’s prescribed reference potential and prescribed reference current.
This identifies two different reasons a certificate can remain wide. Increasing the sample count addresses neither intrinsic defect. Changing the reference or enlarging the locally acquired response is necessary.
Sampling complexity and its qualification
The implementation uses an established empirical Bernstein confidence bound, with a failure budget distributed across successive stopping stages. The concentration inequality and variance-adaptive stopping are prior art. arXiv
Combining those bounds with the variance theorem gives, up to logarithmic factors, an acquisition count of the form
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(
\frac{R\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+
\frac{R-1}{\varepsilon}
\right).
\]
This does not mean arbitrary \(\Delta\) permits an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate answer. The intrinsic discrepancy must already lie below the accuracy budget, with room for statistical error.
For example, when \(\Delta\le\varepsilon/4\) and \(R\) is controlled, the count becomes approximately
\[
\widetilde O(R/\varepsilon)
\]
rather than a range-only \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) count.
Those are sampled cells. A sampled cell containing thousands of coefficients is not a unit-cost microscopic query.
5. What the executed experiments establish
Large-network coefficient acquisition
The main synthetic network contained 65,536 vertices and 130,560 edges. Its heterogeneous reference channels were chosen so that the reference solution was analytically available.
The stopping requirement was
\[
U/L\le1.05,
\]
using a nominal 99% confidence procedure.
The comparison used the same reference distribution, coefficient envelope, sampled indices, and confidence-budget schedule. The statistical comparator was a matched Hoeffding bound.
Target case	Intrinsic enclosure gap	Distinct reads: variance-adaptive	Distinct reads: Hoeffding	Variance-adaptive outcome
Common axial correction \(1.6\)	\(0\%\), up to rounding	2,014	24,506	3.60% interval width
Heterogeneous small axial mismatch	0.419%	2,014	24,506	4.95% interval width
Axial corrections randomly \(0.5\) or \(2\)	56.25%	51,380	51,380	Failed the requested width


The successful cases used 2,048 draws, compared with 32,768 for Hoeffding. With cached repeated queries, that is 12.2 times fewer distinct coefficient reads.
Relative to reading every coefficient, the successful procedure used approximately 64.8 times fewer reads. That is not a lower bound against all specialised methods: some full-input implementations could omit irrelevant terminal edges, and more sophisticated acquisition methods were not compared.
All successful intervals contained the float64 full-network solution. In 100 additional seeded runs of the heterogeneous case, every reported interval contained that comparator.
Several qualifications matter.
The common-scale case is a control, not evidence of difficult scientific discovery. The small-mismatch case’s actual conductance was already close to the reference answer; the achievement is acquiring a certificate, not uncovering a large unexpected physical effect.
Transverse coefficient corrections spanned approximately eight orders of magnitude, but those edges had zero reference work. This is not evidence of solving arbitrary high-contrast transport.
Finally, the 12.2-fold improvement comes from using a known variance-adaptive confidence rule rather than a range-only rule inside the candidate interface. It is not a new-method-versus-state-of-the-art result.
Local repair of strong mismatch
I also executed the paired-cell construction on a separate 64-by-64 strong-mismatch network.
As cell size increased, the enclosure gap fell:
\[

\;\longrightarrow\;
30.1\%
\;\longrightarrow\;
16.2\%
\;\longrightarrow\;
6.22\%
\;\longrightarrow\;

\]
The first tested scale below 5% used four cells, each containing up to 2,016 edges.
That experiment acquired all 7,938 retained target coefficients. It demonstrates repair of the approximation, but not cheap acquisition of that repair. It does not prove that all coefficients are necessary; it shows that the executed method did not avoid them.
This was the most informative negative result: the local mechanism improved the prediction bounds, while the desired simultaneous resource saving remained unproved.
Exact arithmetic and finite-bit implementation
The packet also contains 3,223 passing exact-rational assertions covering local inequalities, global bounds, variance control, monotone coarsening, and whole-cell exactness on small graphs. These are finite checks, not formal verification of the general proofs.
Separately, I implemented a lazy rational-coefficient acquisition procedure on an implicit 256-by-256 grid. It uses integer/rational arithmetic for moment enclosures, logarithm upper bounds, square-root rounding, and stopping comparisons.
It stopped after 2,014 distinct target-coefficient acquisitions, returning
\[
C(c)\in
[2.1503171602351814,\;2.2339888389129205],
\]
with endpoint-ratio width \(3.891\%\).
Exact rational endpoints and the complete sampled-index stream are retained. No transverse coefficients were queried.
This removes floating-point enclosure ambiguity for that structured demonstration. It does not provide a verified arbitrary-graph solver or physical coefficient oracle. The probability theorem assumes independent random bits; the recorded reproducible run uses a seeded pseudorandom generator.
6. The unresolved resource and originality tests
The complete cost is approximately
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{total}}
={}&T_{\mathrm{graph}}
+T_{\mathrm{reference}}
+T_{\mathrm{sampling\ setup}}
+T_{\mathrm{statistics}}\\
&+\sum_{\text{distinct queried cells }K}
\left[
\sum_{e\in K}T_{\mathrm{acquire}}(e)
+T_D(K)+T_N(K)+T_{\mathrm{precision}}(K)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
A graph-size-independent sample count does not make the graph, reference model, ratio envelope, or local responses free.
The coefficient envelope is a substantive missing physical input. Without information controlling unqueried coefficients, a missed large parallel conductance or weak series conductance can dominate the answer. Agreement on sampled coefficients cannot certify the unsampled tail.
The cost-versus-repair relation is unresolved. What is needed is a physical class in which inexpensive cells reduce \(\Delta\) rapidly enough to outweigh their increasing acquisition and solve costs. Monotonicity does not supply that relation.
Specialised superiority is unestablished. The full sparse solves in the main experiment took approximately 0.27–0.30 seconds. No expensive microscopic calculations were executed. The results therefore show fewer synthetic coefficient reads—not a measured ab initio speedup, and not superiority over degree-of-rate-control selection, goal-oriented adaptive modelling, local probabilistic conductivity methods, or specialised domain decomposition.
Historical novelty remains unknown. The elementary enclosure, complementary variational structure, targeted acquisition objective, and confidence machinery have substantial prior art. The possible contribution is the combined response-cell interface and its joint error–variance accounting. Independent derivation and passing tests do not establish that it is fundamentally new.
7. Assessment against the mission
The useful surviving result is more specific than “another low-rank approximation”:
Paired local responses can be assembled into a global conductance enclosure, and their incompatibility quantitatively controls both prediction uncertainty and acquisition variance.

The investigation also goes beyond identifying an obstruction: it constructs a monotone local repair and executes it. But the strong-mismatch experiment shows why that is insufficient. Repairing the certificate and acquiring the repair cheaply are different achievements. Only the former was demonstrated in that regime.
The scientific breakthrough criterion is therefore not met. What remains is a concrete mathematical and computational candidate, with explicit assumptions, a finite-bit implementation for a structured case, measurable favourable-regime savings, and a preserved failure case.
The decisive remaining question is whether small, genuinely obtainable response cells reduce the certificate gap fast enough on a consequential physical problem to beat specialised methods after every acquisition, solve, and certification cost is counted. The supplied experiments do not yet establish that capability.
