Discovery candidate: an autonomous capacitive chemomotor
The strongest candidate I developed is a chemically powered capacitor that generates alternating electrical work without an externally imposed waveform or sustained ion transport through its separator.
The proposed mechanism is specific: a continuously driven surface reaction creates an inverted charge response. The voltage generated by the reaction then changes its own reaction rates, establishing a cycle that delivers power to a passive resistor.
I derived a thermodynamically consistent model, obtained an experimentally measurable instability criterion, and executed nonlinear simulations with energy-balance and equilibrium controls. I have not identified or demonstrated a catalyst possessing the necessary combination of charge coupling and kinetic response. That is the decisive unresolved physical ingredient—not an implementation detail.
The investigation therefore delivers a quantitative discovery candidate, not an established new physical effect or a demonstrated energy technology.
1. The capability worth pursuing
The target is to extract useful electrical work from a surface chemical reaction through reaction-dependent polarisation, rather than requiring the reaction’s electrons to traverse an electrochemical circuit.
In the proposed device, the feedback loop is:
\[
\text{chemical turnover}
\rightarrow
\text{surface occupancy}
\rightarrow
\text{induced terminal charge}
\rightarrow
\text{voltage}
\rightarrow
\text{changed reaction rates}.
\]
The external electrical current is induced by changing polarisation. The separator remains electrically blocking. Chemical free energy pays for the electrical output.
The potentially consequential capability would be a class of energy-converting catalytic surfaces that does not require sustained ionic conduction through a separator. Whether this could improve cost, durability, fuel flexibility or efficiency remains unestablished.
Why this direction has a concrete experimental foothold
Two relevant capabilities already exist.
A 2022 platinum–graphene catalytic condenser used platinum nanoclusters, graphene, a 70-nanometre hafnia dielectric and a silicon electrode. Applied voltage changed CO binding, while impedance measurements demonstrated electrical charge modulation at 3 kHz. This establishes voltage-to-chemistry coupling and useful experimental hardware—not autonomous power generation. Experts@Minnesota
In 2025, isopotential electron titration directly measured adsorption-induced charge through an external circuit. For hydrogen on platinum, adsorption donated charge and desorption reversed it. The measured amount was \(0.19\%\) of an electron per hydrogen atom, approximately \(0.0019e\), over the reported conditions. This establishes an accessible chemistry-to-charge measurement, but also exposes how small the coupling can be. Figshare
The opportunity is to close these two directions into an autonomous work-producing cycle.
Their separate existence does not establish that the closed loop supplies gain. Determining what additional physical condition is required became the central problem.
2. The missing mechanism: a chemically maintained inverted response
Ordinary adsorption charging is insufficient. A charge pulse followed by its reversal may simply return stored energy or dissipate it.
The proposed mechanism instead requires an unusual response:
Increasing voltage must reduce the steady population of the charge-bearing state—even though that voltage energetically favours the state—because the chemically driven reaction changes its escape and arrival rates more strongly.

This is not an equilibrium instability. The opposing chemical pathways maintain the inverted response.
The design variable is therefore not simply “large adsorbate charge” or “strong voltage-dependent binding”. It is the difference between the voltage responses of the two reaction barriers, relative to the voltage response of the occupied state.
A minimal construction
Consider a surface site with empty and occupied states, \(0\) and \(1\), participating in two reversible channels:
\[
F+0 \rightleftharpoons 1
\rightleftharpoons P+0.
\]
Here \(F\) and \(P\) represent complete fuel and product reservoir combinations. The numerical model below is a generic cycle, not a simulation of CO oxidation or another identified catalytic reaction.
Let
\[
E_1(V)-E_0(V)=-qV,
\]
where \(q\) is the charge conjugate to the measured terminal voltage. It must not be casually identified with a formal oxidation state, a computed atomic charge, or the charge injected per metal atom.
Write
\[
a=\frac{\Delta\mu}{k_BT},\qquad
x=\frac{qV}{k_BT},\qquad
r=e^{-a/2},
\]
and introduce a barrier-response contrast \(\gamma\), with
\[
\theta_F=\frac{1-\gamma}{2},
\qquad
\theta_P=\frac{1+\gamma}{2}.
\]
An explicit rate construction is
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_+&=k e^{\theta_Fx},
&
a_-&=kr e^{(\theta_F-1)x},\\
b_+&=k e^{(\theta_P-1)x},
&
b_-&=kr e^{\theta_Px}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These rates obey local detailed balance:
\[
\log\frac{a_+}{a_-}=\frac a2+x,
\qquad
\log\frac{b_+}{b_-}=\frac a2-x.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\log\frac{a_+b_+}{a_-b_-}=a.
\]
The electrical voltage has not been inserted as an unaccounted chemical-energy source. The full reaction affinity remains \(\Delta\mu\).
This is a deliberately symmetric operating-point model. A real multistate reaction would require independently acquired rates and a justified reduction; the symmetry is not an experimental finding.
3. A quantitative condition for autonomous electrical gain
Let \(p\) be the occupied fraction and \(n\) the site density. Use capacitance and conductance per geometric area:
\[
\dot p=(a_++b_-)(1-p)-(a_-+b_+)p,
\]
\[
Q=C_0V+nqp,
\]
\[
C_0\dot V=-GV-nq\dot p,
\qquad
G=G_{\rm load}+G_{\rm leak}>0.
\]
Here \(C_0\) is ordinary positive geometric and parasitic capacitance. No inductor, active electrical controller or externally programmed switching is present.
At \(V=0\),
\[
p_{\rm ss}=\frac12,
\qquad
\lambda=2k(1+e^{-a/2}).
\]
Differentiating the stationary occupancy gives a reaction-associated charge response
\[
\boxed{
C_r
=
\frac{nq^2}{4k_BT}
\left[1-\gamma\tanh\!\left(\frac a4\right)\right].
}
\]
The small-signal device admittance is
\[
Y(\omega)
=
G_{\rm leak}+i\omega C_0+
\frac{i\omega C_r\lambda}{\lambda+i\omega},
\]
so that
\[
\operatorname{Re}Y(\omega)
=
G_{\rm leak}
+
\frac{C_r\lambda\omega^2}{\lambda^2+\omega^2}.
\]
A negative real part means that, under the maintained chemical conditions, the device supplies electrical power to a small perturbation. A negative imaginary “capacitance” alone would not establish this.
The microscopic requirement
Reaction-associated gain requires
\[
\boxed{\gamma\tanh(a/4)>1.}
\]
The physical meaning becomes clearer by writing
\[
\gamma=
\frac{q_P^\ddagger-q_F^\ddagger}{q},
\]
where the numerator is the difference between the two transition-state charge responses.
At strong chemical driving, that contrast must exceed the stable-state charge difference. If both transition-state responses remain between the two stable-state responses, this symmetric model cannot produce gain.
At equilibrium, \(a=0\), the response is positive regardless of \(\gamma\). Unusual barrier sensitivities do not extract work from a single equilibrium heat bath.
These are conclusions about this explicit construction, not universal impossibility theorems for every catalytic network.
The device-level requirement
Linearisation of the coupled circuit and reaction gives
\[
C_0s^2+
\left[C_0\lambda+G+C_r\lambda\right]s+
G\lambda=0.
\]
The stationary state becomes linearly unstable when
\[
\boxed{
-C_r>C_0+\frac{G}{\lambda}.
}
\]
This is the most useful result of the investigation: it makes the candidate testable using independently measured electrical and kinetic quantities.
It also prevents several misleading shortcuts. Negative reaction response alone is insufficient; it must overcome ordinary capacitance, leakage and the useful load.
An equivalent strong-drive design rule uses the extrapolated full-coverage open-circuit swing
\[
\Delta V_{\rm oc}=\frac{nq}{C_0}
\]
and the differential kinetic sensitivity
\[
S=\frac{\partial}{\partial V}\log\frac{b_+}{a_+}.
\]
Near the symmetric operating point,
\[
\boxed{
\Delta V_{\rm oc}S
>
4\left(1+\frac{G}{C_0\lambda}\right).
}
\]
The decisive quantity is the product of achievable voltage swing and differential reaction-rate sensitivity—not either quantity considered separately.
4. Energy accounting: where the electricity comes from
The two channel currents per site are
\[
j_F=a_+(1-p)-a_-p,
\qquad
j_P=b_+p-b_-(1-p).
\]
Chemical input power is
\[
P_{\rm chem}
=
\frac{n\Delta\mu}{2}(j_F+j_P).
\]
With capacitor energy \(U=C_0V^2/2\), internal occupancy entropy \(S_{\rm int}\), and nonnegative entropy production \(\sigma\), the model satisfies
\[
P_{\rm chem}
-
P_{\rm load}
-
P_{\rm leak}
-
T\sigma
=
\dot U-T\dot S_{\rm int}.
\]
On a periodic cycle,
\[
\boxed{
\langle P_{\rm chem}\rangle
=
\langle P_{\rm load}\rangle
+
\langle P_{\rm leak}\rangle
+
T\langle\sigma\rangle.
}
\]
The chemical drive supplies both the useful electricity and the dissipation.
For a simple fill–empty coverage loop, the electrical work also obeys the charge–voltage budget
\[
\frac{W_{\rm cycle}}{A}
\le nq\,\Delta V\,\Delta p
\le nq\,\Delta V.
\]
This is not a universal efficiency bound for arbitrary reaction networks. It does expose why small terminal charge severely limits useful work per coverage cycle.
The plotted charge–voltage loop above is an executed simulation of this mechanism. It is not experimental data.

Why the faster example is not a technology result
Multiplying every reaction rate and the load conductance by 100 simply rescales time in these equations. It does not establish that a real interface can support the faster chemistry.
The waveform also switches sharply. Its largest elementary reaction rate is approximately \(2.16\times10^4\,{\rm s^{-1}}\) in the baseline and \(2.16\times10^6\,{\rm s^{-1}}\) in the rescaled case. The fundamental cycle frequency therefore understates the required kinetic and electrical bandwidth.
The published 3-kHz condenser response is not sufficient evidence that this waveform can be supported. Experts@Minnesota
A rejected interpretation
An earlier parameter choice produced apparently attractive output but implied a full-coverage open-circuit swing of approximately 12.7 V. I rejected its interpretation as an ordinary platinum-adsorbate device prediction.
That correction matters. Choosing charge, capacitance, site density and voltage independently can create a numerically impressive device that has no credible electrostatic implementation.
Numerical verification
The released calculation passes 27 checks covering detailed balance, voltage-independent affinity, the analytic response coefficient, nonnegative entropy production, integrated energy balance and specified passive controls.
The nominal energy-balance residual is approximately \(6.1\times10^{-8}\,{\rm W\,m^{-2}}\). Tighter tolerances and a different initial seed agree closely, but use the same numerical solver, not an independent implementation.
These checks establish consistency of the calculation—not the existence of the required catalyst.
6. The decisive experiment
The first experiment should test electrical gain under steady chemical conditions, not attempt to build a high-power generator.
Acquire the response before testing oscillation
A platinum/graphene catalytic condenser is a concrete starting platform because its electrical structure and voltage-dependent adsorption have already been demonstrated. CO oxidation is a candidate calibration chemistry; related catalytic nanodiodes have also measured electrical signals during CO oxidation, although through a different mechanism. Experts@Minnesota
Under constant reactant flow and controlled temperature, independently measure the blocking capacitance, leakage, dielectric loss and electrode resistance. Measure adsorption-associated terminal charge, preferably alongside a coverage-sensitive spectroscopic signal.
Then acquire small-signal complex admittance around the intended operating point. The critical observation is
\[
\operatorname{Re}Y(\omega)<0
\]
after correcting for instrument phase shifts and passive parasitics.
Fit the reaction response before observing autonomous operation. If the response needs multiple kinetic modes, use that measured model; do not retrospectively force a one-pole fit onto an oscillating device.
Remove the electrical driving source
Connect only a known passive load and high-impedance measurement. Keep the chemical feed steady.
The experiment should test whether the independently acquired response predicts the onset of an electrical instability and sustained load power.
For the hypothetical strong-coupling parameters above, the calculated linear boundary occurs at approximately
\[
R_{\rm load}=579\,\Omega
\]
for \(1\,{\rm cm^2}\), neglecting leakage.
At the \(2\,{\rm k\Omega}\) operating load, adding approximately
\[
C_{\rm shunt}=12.24\,\mu{\rm F}
\]
reaches the corresponding linear-stability boundary.
These are examples of predictions from the assumed model—not expected values for platinum. They are also linear boundaries: hysteresis or a surviving large-amplitude cycle would require separate nonlinear testing.
The unusually useful discriminant is the shunt-capacitance intervention. It changes electrical feedback without deliberately changing the catalyst or feed.
Exclude alternative energy sources and mechanisms
A convincing result must survive reaction-blocked and near-equilibrium controls, matched temperature histories, instrument calibration and tests for dielectric charging transients. Output must persist well beyond the initially stored electrical and chemical inventory, while product measurements support the chemical free-energy account.
Thermal gradients, heater feedback, direct Faradaic shorts and hot-electron collection are alternative explanations, not confirmation.
Hot-electron catalytic nanodiodes already generate reaction-induced electrical signals through semiconductor junctions. The proposed blocking-capacitor mechanism must be distinguished from that established route. PubMed
An adsorption pulse, open-circuit voltage, oscillating reaction rate or negative imaginary capacitance would not, individually, pass this test.
7. The material challenge that determines whether this matters
The measured hydrogen/platinum coupling of approximately \(0.0019e\) is an important warning against assuming an electron-scale terminal charge. Inserting that magnitude into an illustrative version of the model with modest barrier contrast does not produce oscillation. This is a magnitude control, not a simulation of measured hydrogen-oxidation kinetics. Figshare
The path to useful output likely requires a different interface.
A constructive high-coupling target
A candidate geometry is a field-exposed catalytic redox unit inside a gas-accessible insulating nanogap, rather than a reaction hidden behind a strongly screening continuous metal.
In ideal parallel-plate electrostatics, an internal electron displacement \(\Delta z\) across a gap \(d\) induces terminal charge of order
\[
q_{\rm terminal}\sim e\frac{\Delta z}{d}.
\]
A one-nanometre displacement in a three-nanometre gap therefore provides an electrostatic target near \(0.33e\). With an assumed effective relative permittivity near 8, that gap gives capacitance near the strong-coupling model’s \(0.024\,{\rm F\,m^{-2}}\).
This suggests a fabrication investigation involving insulating spacers, porous or perforated electrodes, immobilised redox-active catalytic units and attachments that prevent a continuous conducting path.
But a molecule that merely switches its equilibrium dipole is insufficient. Its two activated chemical routes must have sufficiently different charge distributions. For the strong-coupling example, the transition-state charge derivatives are \(-0.30e\) and \(+0.60e\), relative to the empty state.
No molecular or interfacial structure satisfying that full specification has been established here. The geometry provides a coupling target, not the missing chemistry.
Engineering constraints remain coupled
At the simulated peak voltage, a three-nanometre gap experiences approximately \(0.24\,{\rm V\,nm^{-1}}\). That is a required operating field, not demonstrated dielectric reliability.
Thinner dielectric is not a free improvement: it changes charge coupling, capacitance, leakage and breakdown together. An ultrathin-condenser preprint reports engineered sub-five-nanometre hafnia structures and explicitly different charge-density/leakage operating points, illustrating the need to measure these properties jointly. ChemRxiv
Porous area multiplication similarly brings additional capacitance, fabrication complexity and reactant-transport costs. A shared electrode voltage couples the model’s sites, but actual correlated domains, chemical fluctuations and surface drift remain uncharacterised.
Ideal thermal voltage noise is small for the nominal micrometre-scale capacitance—approximately \(49\,{\rm nV}\) for the \(1\,{\rm cm^2}\) baseline—but that is not an instrument-noise or chemical-noise specification.
At the faster model’s \(100\,{\rm W\,m^{-2}}\) electrical output, mean entropic dissipation is approximately \(81\,{\rm W\,m^{-2}}\), before auxiliary losses. At the first experiment’s microwatt scale, heating and gas handling could easily dominate net output. Rectifying the alternating output would impose additional losses.
These costs determine whether a successful physics experiment becomes an energy technology.
8. Comparison, novelty and significance
There is no demonstrated performance advantage over fuel cells
Even a published 2019 alkaline polymer-electrolyte fuel-cell study reported power exceeding \(1\,{\rm W\,cm^{-2}}\), roughly 100 times the output of the conditional fast model here. This is only a scale comparison: the fuel, temperature, catalyst and operating conditions are not matched. American Chemical Society Publications
The frontier is also advancing. A preprint submitted on 23 May 2026 reports proton–electron coupled catalysts enabling ionomer-free cathode catalyst layers, with power-density improvements of 34% and 85% at different platinum loadings relative to its controls. Those are preprint claims, and an ionomer-free catalyst layer must not be confused with elimination of proton transport from the complete fuel cell. arXiv
A meaningful comparison would need the same reactants, conversion, pressure, temperature, catalyst inventory, lifetime and total auxiliary power. The present candidate has not passed that comparison.
Broad catalytic energy recovery is already prior art
Energy exchange in programmable catalysts was analysed in Energy Flows in Static and Programmable Catalysts in 2023. Subsequent catalytic-resonance work explicitly discusses energy recovery from exothermic reactions. Therefore, “recover chemical energy through changing catalytic binding” is not a defensible novelty claim here. American Chemical Society Publications
The narrower candidate is:
A continuously driven, electrically blocking catalytic interface whose measured inverted charge response supplies enough electrical gain to destabilise a purely passive load circuit and sustain work-producing oscillation.

The construction, its barrier-response requirement and the independently predicted electrical interventions make this candidate substantive. Historical priority remains unresolved; some relevant full texts were inaccessible, and this was not an exhaustive patent or literature clearance.
What this investigation actually contributes
The useful result is a physical condition that is substantially sharper than “adsorption produces current”:
\[
\boxed{
\text{chemical driving}
+
\text{transition-state charge contrast}
+
\text{sufficient charge–voltage coupling}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{autonomous gain in the constructed model}.
}
\]
It also distinguishes detectable gain from technologically useful conversion. The small-charge example could produce a measurable signal while remaining a poor energy converter. The high-efficiency example requires a presently unestablished interface.
The next scientific decision is consequently not whether to optimise the simulated generator. It is whether a real catalytic surface can satisfy the measured gain inequality while remaining electrically blocking.
That is the decisive unresolved question. A positive, independently predicted passive-load experiment would establish a physical mechanism worth developing. The present calculations identify how to test it, but do not substitute for that observation.
