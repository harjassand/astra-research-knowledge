1. Exact mathematical contract

For every integer n, the exact decimal mathematical rates are

No statement about rounding in an IEEE trajectory implementation is made.

Let S(n)=sum_i n_i. Consider an envelope on continuation states of the form

 g(t,n)=F(t,S(n)), n not in B; g(t,B)=1; g(t,overflow)=0.

Assume F is finite and nonnegative, locally absolutely continuous in time
before the deadline, locally bounded on compact time intervals, with
nonnegative terminal lower limit, and satisfies the actual pointwise
supercaloric inequality partial_t g+Qg<=0 almost everywhere. Positivity is a
permitted stronger assumption. Arbitrary nonlinear dependence on S and
arbitrary time dependence are allowed. In particular no product, exponential,
monotonicity, convexity, or moment-closure assumption is imposed.

This is a lower bound on this class of upper envelopes. It is NOT a lower
bound on P_x0(tau_B<=1), and is NOT an approximation of the physical process
by the chain constructed below. The physical probability remains unacquired.

2. A finite row abstraction that every S-only envelope must dominate

Use abstract continuation states s=16,...,499, success b*, and failure a*.
At each s choose a particular ACTUAL continuation state x(s) of total s.

For 16<=s<=121, put x_0(s)=16 and distribute s-16 among sites 1,...,7,
in that order, filling each to 15 before moving to the next. Thus there is
exactly one count 16 and all seven other counts are at most 15.

For 122<=s<=499, write s=100*k+r, 0<=r<100, and choose

All these selected states have maximum count at most 100. All their immediate
successors have maximum count at most 101, so none of these actual generator
rows contains overflow. The selection is a mathematical test of a proposed
global envelope, rather than a claimed physical rearrangement mechanism.

Define rates beta_s,delta_s,kappa_s by applying the ORIGINAL elementary
generator at x(s), grouping every continuing successor by total count and
every target successor into success. Continuing diffusion has unchanged total
and contributes zero to QF(t,S). Therefore

   delta_s=sum_i a(x_i(s)), kappa_s=0;

3. Exact finite-deadline certificate for Y

Let tau be first absorption in {a*,b*}. Two vectors are constructed and then
checked against every equation using exact fractions.

The failure vector P has P(b*)=0, P(a*)=1 and LP=0 on all continuation states.
It satisfies 0<P_s<1 for every s. Its tridiagonal equations are

 (beta_s+delta_s+kappa_s)P_s
   -delta_s P_(s-1)-beta_s P_(s+1)=0,

with the known upper boundary P_500=1 moved to the right-hand side at 499.
The zero lower coefficient at 16 requires no lower boundary value.

The exponential-clock certificate M has M(b*)=M(a*)=1 and

 LM+100M=0

on continuation states, equivalently

 (beta_s+delta_s+kappa_s-100)M_s
   -delta_s M_(s-1)-beta_s M_(s+1)=kappa_s,

again with beta_499 M_500=beta_499 moved to the right-hand side. Exact
elimination has all strictly positive pivots, and the subsequently replayed
equations establish LM+100M=0 independently of elimination roundoff (there is
no roundoff). The program checks M_s>1 at EVERY abstract continuation state.

For fixed t, exp[100*(t wedge tau)] M(Y_(t wedge tau)) is a true martingale:
its drift is zero, and on each bounded time interval it is bounded because
the abstract state set and rates are finite. On {tau>t} its value is at least
exp(100t). Therefore

 P_400(tau>t)<=M_400 exp(-100t).                         (2)

This in particular proves almost-sure absorption. Bounded optional sampling
for P and then dominated convergence identifies P_400 as the probability of
eventual artificial failure. Finite-deadline failure is contained in the union
of eventual artificial failure and {tau>1}. Thus

 1-P_400(Y reaches b* by 1)<=P_400+M_400 exp(-100).        (3)

The following are exact strict comparisons in the executable certificate:

 P_400 < 1/10^18,
 M_400 < 100,
 100^100/100! > 10^42.

Since exp(100) is strictly larger than the positive Taylor term 100^100/100!,
(3) is strictly less than 10^(-18)+10^(-40). Combine with (1) to prove the
theorem. This is a direct finite-deadline argument; no eventual moment/work
obstruction has been substituted for the deadline-one event.

4. Why the abstraction can be fast when the physical event is rare

The abstract chain discards the spatial profile. In high-total states it
selects concentrated counts near 100, whose cubic death propensity drives
the total down quickly. Near total 121 it selects a nearly all-low state that
can enter the target with a single count-16 death. A physical path cannot
freely make those changes of profile at fixed total. A globally pointwise
S-only envelope must nevertheless obey BOTH rows because it cannot distinguish
their profiles. This produces an impossible optimistic scenario for that
certificate class, rather than a physical fast path.

The argument is an ordinary state-abstraction/adversarial-row comparison and
finite CTMC martingale certificate. The model-specific selected rows and
strict numerical floor are the new internally derived work here. No general
framework novelty or external correctness is claimed.

5. Scope limits and decisive consequence

The theorem covers any nonnegative envelope that is a function solely of
(time,total population) on continuation states, even when the actual target
is separately overwritten to one. It covers unrestricted nonlinear F and
unrestricted permitted time dependence, so changing a scalar basis, adding
many one-dimensional total-count knots, or solving its time equation more
accurately cannot repair this GLOBAL envelope class.

The theorem does not exclude envelopes carrying dispersion, spatial/pair
information, history, local phase labels, sums of spatial tensor products,
general TT states, or restricted-domain solves with a separately charged
outside-risk budget. It does not prove that all algorithms storing a total
population marginal fail: such algorithms could retain additional conditional
information and would then fall outside the stated representation class.

The already established physical overflow allowance is approximately
7.57e-10, far larger than the gap 10^(-18)+10^(-40). Adding that allowance to
this class's best possible finite-box envelope and clipping at one therefore
again gives the trivial upper bound one. No inside probability or practical
capability gate is closed.
