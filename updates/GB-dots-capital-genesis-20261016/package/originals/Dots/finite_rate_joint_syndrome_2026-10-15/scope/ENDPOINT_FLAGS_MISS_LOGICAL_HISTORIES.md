# Exact finite-rate obstruction: zero endpoint syndrome need not be a good history

Date: 2026-10-15. This is a small-code counterexample to a support-certification inference, not to error suppression or a memory threshold. It uses finite-rate stabilizer dephasing, an actual final syndrome measurement, and, in the second calculation, ideal autonomous syndrome correction.

## 1. Genuine distance-three code and local coherent coupling

Use the five-qubit [[5,1,3]] stabilizer code generated by

 XZZXI, IXZZX, XIXZZ, ZXIXZ.

Let P_C be its code projector and take logical Z_L=ZZZZZ. A minimum-weight logical X-type operator is

 X_L=IYYIX.

Every single-qubit Pauli is detected; X_L has weight three. Set

 H=h(Y_2+Y_3+X_5),
 Lrho=-i[H,rho]+gamma sum_{a=1}^4(S_a rho S_a-rho).

The Hamiltonian consists of three bounded one-site terms. A trivial one-dimensional bath is a valid special case of an arbitrary finite bath. Take gamma>0 finite. Initialize the logical +1 eigenstate of Z_L and perform an ACTUAL full syndrome measurement at t.

## 2. An actual zero-syndrome Kraus branch contains a logical rotation

Use the ordinary quantum-jump unraveling of the four dephasers. The record with no dephasing jumps has Kraus operator exp(-2gamma t)exp(-itH). Postselecting its final zero syndrome gives, on the code,

 K_{no jump,0}
 =exp(-2gamma t)[cos(ht)^3 I_L+i sin(ht)^3 X_L].           (1)

Reason: the three Hamiltonian Pauli terms commute, their product is X_L, and no nonempty proper product has zero syndrome. Equation (1) is an actual Kraus operator of the physical measured channel, not a signed Dyson term or an invented trajectory.

Both its identity and logical components have the SAME final zero syndrome. Its effect on the code is

 K^dagger K=exp(-4gamma t)[cos(ht)^6+sin(ht)^6] I_L,

so the conditional no-jump/zero-syndrome branch is a nontrivial logical unitary with tan(theta)=tan(ht)^3. A decoder treating zero syndrome as no correction leaves that rotation intact.

For the initial logical-Z eigenstate, positivity of the full Kraus sum yields the exact lower bound

 P(final syndrome=0 AND final logical Z_L=-1)
 >=exp(-4gamma t)sin(ht)^6>0                             (2)

whenever sin(ht)!=0. Unobserved other jump records cannot cancel this nonnegative event probability.

This does NOT say the unconditional final syndrome is always zero. It says an endpoint support model which declares the zero-syndrome outcome fault-free admits an actual logical-error branch carrying no endpoint flag. The missing history comprises intermediate local syndrome excursions and their coherent or corrected return to zero.

## 3. Add ideal autonomous syndrome correction

The code is perfect: its 15 nonzero syndrome sectors correspond bijectively to the 15 single-qubit Pauli errors E_s. Let P_s=E_s P_C E_s and add time-independent jumps

 J_s=sqrt(kappa) E_s P_s,  s!=0.

They are bounded operators in this fixed small system, stabilizer covariant, and vanish on the code. This is an illustrative autonomous syndrome-recovery generator, not the 4D sweep construction. It changes intermediate syndromes and can turn a two-error history into a zero-syndrome logical error after an intended correction.

The record with neither dephasing nor correction jumps has final zero-syndrome Kraus operator

 K_0=exp(-2gamma t)P_C
       exp{t[-iH-(kappa/2)(I-P_C)]}P_C.                  (3)

Here sum_s J_s^dagger J_s=kappa(I-P_C). Its logical coefficient remains nonzero; near t=0 it is i h^3 t^3+O(t^4), multiplied by the dephasing survival factor. Thus adding these correction jumps does not make final zero syndrome a universal no-logical-error certificate. Other records may also yield zero syndrome with a logical error.

## 4. Exact finite-dimensional channel calculation

The accompanying script computes the full Lindblad evolution using a sparse matrix exponential, then performs the actual final syndrome measurement and standard perfect Pauli recovery. Parameters are h=0.2, gamma=0.4, t=1. The initial state is logical Z_L=+1. All probabilities below are dimensionless.

Without correction, kappa=0:
- Final nonzero syndrome probability: 0.0640291330022.
- Zero syndrome AND logical-Z flip: 0.0000187325330151.
- Logical-Z flip after full ideal final recovery: 0.00165439105579.
- Exact no-jump lower bound from (2): 0.0000124140589192.

With autonomous correction, kappa=0.7:
- Final nonzero syndrome probability: 0.0458063841007.
- Zero syndrome AND logical-Z flip: 0.000203124247377.
- Logical-Z flip after full ideal final recovery: 0.00124848879076.
- Positive no-jump lower bound from (3): 0.00000881012268411.

These numbers illustrate the support issue only. In particular correction lowers the total recovered logical-Z error in this example even though its zero-syndrome logical-error portion increases. There is no adverse lifetime or threshold conclusion.

## 5. Exact remaining composition obstruction

The simultaneous endpoint theorem bounds probabilities that selected monitored parities differ between their start and end. A common-decoder memory proof needs a stronger object: every history in every record labeled good must have correctable support throughout the interval, including changes caused by correction. The zero endpoint label does not enforce that property, as (1)-(3) show.

The one-parity-per-interaction hypothesis of the endpoint subset theorem need not hold for the five-qubit check signatures here; this is not a counterexample to that theorem. It is a counterexample to treating a small probability of endpoint syndrome changes as a complete local-fault history model. Intermediate measurements, independently justified excursion flags, or another positive full-horizon support decomposition would be needed for such a composition.
