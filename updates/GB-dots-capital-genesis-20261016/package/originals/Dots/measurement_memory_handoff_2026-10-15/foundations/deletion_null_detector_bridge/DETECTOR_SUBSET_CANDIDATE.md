# Candidate: deletion-null detector records give subset probability bounds

2026-10-15. Root construction, separate from delivered frame-based results. Pending independent review; no novelty or hardware claim.

## Abstract statement

Use one common finite-dimensional unitary dilation of a finite experiment with a fixed initialization isometry W. Retain protected classical output records until the end. Let Q_i be commuting fixed orthogonal projectors on these record outputs, denoting actual bad flags. Each can compare several protected record fields; it need not be a one-bit projector. They are fixed operators, not state-dependent selections.

For real parameters z_i in [0,1], let U_z be generated by

H_z(t)=H_0(t)+sum_i z_i V_i(t),

with integrable self-adjoint terms and fixed frame-independent unitary interventions/record operations. Put a_i=integral ||V_i(t)|| dt. Arbitrary shared bath dynamics are allowed. The initial W, interventions, controllers, record definitions and projectors must remain the same throughout all counterfactual interpolations.

Assume the strong DELETION-NULL property for every i and every choice of all other interpolation parameters:

Q_i U_(z_i=0) W = 0.

Then for each finite subset S,

W† U_1† (product_{i in S} Q_i) U_1 W <= (product_{i in S} a_i²) I.

This is a full input effect bound for actual joint detector outcomes, allowing arbitrary input-bath-reference correlations. It implies no small conditional probability after postselecting another flag.

## Proposed proof

Let Q_S=product_i Q_i, an orthogonal projection because the Q_i commute. In the multivariate anchored difference for the selected parameters, every boundary term omitting selected i is annihilated by Q_S: since projectors commute, Q_S=Q_(S\{i}) Q_i. Thus

Q_S U_1 W = Q_S integral_[0,1]^S partial_S U_z W dz.

The same mixed Duhamel estimate as in the audited global frame lemma gives ||partial_S U_z|| <= product_i a_i: its temporal-order simplices partition the Cartesian insertion-time cube. Orthogonal projection, W and the intervening propagators have norm one. Therefore ||Q_S U_1 W|| <= product_i a_i. Squaring gives the effect inequality. No Fourier transform, private random frame or classical stochastic domination is used.

The claim also covers partial isometries and discarded ancillas by working first in their common unitary dilation, then summing output Kraus indices. It does not follow from a family of normalized postselected branch models.

## Candidate memory application and its actual cost

At a geometrically clean reserved correction interval, perform an ACTUAL IDEAL premeasurement of sufficiently many commuting stabilizer checks, retain protected true record s, perform the correction selected by s with weak local interaction perturbation V_i, and perform an ACTUAL IDEAL postmeasurement of all checks needed to detect the full syndrome of the entire error patch. Define the true bad flag when the postlabel differs from the label predicted by the intended Pauli Q_{j(s)} acting on the prelabel.

Deleting this interval's perturbation should make its bad flag impossible, even with arbitrary perturbations in other memory-disjoint intervals sharing a bath. The proof obligation is an operator annihilation identity, not an average calibration claim. Full reserved-check-support disjointness ensures remote memory operations cannot change the relevant syndrome during the interval. Bath-only operations preserve the measured sector, including on correlated inputs. The pre/post measurements and protected true records must be unaffected by the deleted/noisy terms.

If the entire local error patch has no logical Pauli and the postchecks detect every local syndrome, a zero-mismatch global record should differ from its intended correction only by patch stabilizers on each encoded Pauli history. A nonzero mismatch charges the entire gadget. This support argument still needs separate proof for the chosen physical instrument; the abstract deletion-null lemma alone provides no such locality.

This route trades private ideal frame wrappers for trusted pre/post syndrome measurement and record protection. It does not establish noisy detectors, noisy protected records, faulty reset, or arbitrary unmeasured continuous memory noise. Ordinary gate-calibration errors can be included only insofar as their complete Hamiltonian perturbation and support are bounded uniformly and deletion-nullness is physically justified.

The existing crossing-echo example is consistent with this result: its actual final syndrome flags have small joint subset probabilities while completion-order conditional probabilities approach one.
