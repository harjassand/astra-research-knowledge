# Independent audit: coherent residuals in the common private-frame dilation

Date: 2026-10-15. Mathematical audit of the same-task coherent-control extension. No canonical source theorem was edited.

## Verdict

The proposed coherent-residual extension is valid as a GLOBAL CHARACTER-EFFECT theorem under the common-dilation, private-dependence and reference-uniformity hypotheses stated below.

For homogeneous deterministic residual action bound b,

`E(actual nonzero characters on every i in S) <= (sqrt(q)+b)^(2|S|) I`.

For independent own-parent private clock bounds `b_i <= u(c+N_i)` with `N_i ~ Poisson(mu)`, the same statement holds with

`b_RMS = u sqrt((c+mu)^2+mu)`.

The mean action `u(c+mu)` is not the justified substitute in this argument. The final homogeneous parent budget can be capped at one.

This does not, by itself, establish the support theorem. Arbitrary own-seed-dependent residuals can create zero-character physical damage. The additional covariance and full-support detection conditions below are essential before importing a memory conclusion.

## 1. Precise hypotheses

There is ONE common joint unitary/isometric dilation in which, after any stipulated ideal control transformations,

`H_actual(f,eta,t) = H_reference(f,eta,t) + sum_i R_i(f_i,eta_i,t)`.

Each residual is Hermitian, measurable and norm-integrable, depends only on its own parent's private parameters and permitted fixed outside data, and has an operator-norm action bound

`integral ||R_i(f_i,eta_i,t)|| dt <= b_i(eta_i)`

uniform in its initial seed and all joint inputs. More generally the bound can depend on independent full own-parent private parameters if its second moment is controlled.

All actual and interpolated evolutions have the same endpoints and common Hilbert-space interpretation. No selected residual changes another parent's private clock law, geometric schedule, or coefficient dependence.

For every subset G, deleting the residuals in G leaves the same exact private additive weak interface on G, even after fixing all outside private parameters, retaining arbitrary interpolated residuals outside G, and inserting selected-G-independent normalized contraction gates. The reference weak effect bound

`q^|G| I`, with `q = min(1,a^2,mB)`,

must hold uniformly for this enlarged baseline class. This follows from the stated direct Fourier theorem when its common-dilation and seed/path independence hypotheses remain satisfied. It must not merely be assumed from a weaker theorem that requires a special baseline.

The ordinary dilation outcomes are summed, not postselected and normalized. No CP-contamination assumption is needed here, but a common Hamiltonian residual representation is: a generic small channel difference is not automatically such a residual.

## 2. Anchored interpolation and exact expansion

For a selected set S interpolate only its residuals:

`H_s = H_reference + sum_(i outside S) R_i + sum_(i in S) s_i R_i`.

The residual operators do not themselves depend on the interpolation parameters. All H_s are Hermitian. Repeated fundamental theorem of calculus gives the exact identity

`U_actual = sum_(F subset S) integral_[0,1]^F partial_F U_(s_F,0_(S\F)) ds_F`.

The empty F term is the evolution with every selected residual deleted.

Let `P_S` denote the orthogonal Fourier projection onto a nonzero initial-frame character for every selected parent. The squared L2 norm of `P_S U_actual psi`, averaged over the private paths and including all ordinary dilation outcomes, is exactly the selected positive effect evaluated on psi.

## 3. Bound on one derivative term

Fix F and put `G=S\F`. A mixed Duhamel derivative is a sum over time orders of products containing one residual insertion R_i for each i in F and unitary propagators between them. The propagators are generated by the same H_s with every G residual absent.

Drop the Fourier nonzero constraints on F by orthogonal-projection contraction. Parseval then converts the F initial bits into an ordinary average; fix those bits and the F paths while bounding the remaining G effect.

At any insertion time with nonzero norm, factor

`R_i(t) = ||R_i(t)|| C_i(t)`, with `||C_i(t)|| <= 1`.

The contraction C_i is independent of every selected G seed and path. Realize it as a block of a unitary on a fresh ancilla. Each such ancilla is untouched afterward, and its selected output is projected only at the end. Thus the desired product of contractions is one unnormalized final record of a reference process with selected-G-independent unitary interventions. The record's positive effect is bounded by the complete effect of that process. The weak theorem therefore bounds its G-Fourier L2 norm by `q^(|G|/2) ||psi||`.

No commutation of the residual insertions with weak interactions is needed. The dilation introduces no physical device requirement; it is a proof representation of the contractions.

Minkowski's inequality over insertion times and interpolation parameters gives

`||P_S X_F psi||_L2(G) <= q^(|G|/2) product_(i in F) b_i ||psi||`,

conditional on the fixed F parameters. Here X_F is the integrated mixed derivative. The time-order simplices, summed over all permutations, partition the product time cube; hence there is no extra factorial.

## 4. Averaging own-parent action bounds

Now average the fixed F private parameters. Their independence yields

`||P_S X_F psi||_L2(all) <= q^(|G|/2) product_(i in F) beta_i ||psi||`,

where `beta_i = sqrt(E b_i^2)`.

The argument needs independence between the F parameter distributions and the remaining selected G streams, and product control of the F second moments. If those bounds are correlated, replace the product by `sqrt(E product_(i in F) b_i^2)`; a one-parent second moment alone would no longer prove the desired subset product.

Finally, Minkowski over F gives

`||P_S U_actual psi||_L2 <= product_(i in S) (sqrt(q)+beta_i) ||psi||`.

For homogeneous beta this proves the stated `(sqrt(q)+beta)^(2|S|)` operator bound. This uses one common reference q; it does not silently assert a product of heterogeneous weak minima.

If `b_i <= u(c+N_i)` and `N_i ~ Poisson(mu)` independently across parents, then

`E(c+N_i)^2 = (c+mu)^2+mu`,

so `beta_i <= u sqrt((c+mu)^2+mu)`.

The same reasoning applies to any explicitly controlled own-parent second-moment action bound. The resulting expression is an upper bound, not a claim of sharpness.

## 5. Support qualification

The above conclusion concerns nonzero Fourier characters only. An arbitrary residual depending on its own seed can violate the correspondence between those characters and physical memory errors. For example, a seed-independent nontrivial memory residual in the common representation contributes to the zero character; a character-effect estimate alone says nothing about that damage.

A sufficient additional structural requirement is covariance of the actual residual with the INITIAL private frame:

`R_i(g_i,eta_i,t) = Ad(g_i)[R_i,eta_i(t)]`,

with `R_i,eta_i` independent of g_i, and with the entire memory support lying in the same detection-qualified local patch. The reference terms and residual terms then have the same initial-character/Pauli correspondence. For a restricted stabilizer frame, the existing zero-character/no-local-logical argument must apply to this FULL support. Merely enlarging the declared support without proving its detection property is insufficient.

The original completion-locality, clean-reservation, common-decoder and bath-ordering hypotheses remain necessary. Neither contraction dilation nor the effect proof establishes them.

## 6. Conditional finite-ramp application

Suppose an exact control-only ramp path C_eta(t) is independent of the initial frame g0, matches the ideal piecewise frame eta(t) outside declared ramp intervals, and has the required exact net endpoint. In a genuinely common toggling/compiler representation the weak-coupling mismatch is

`R(g0,eta,t) = Ad(g0)[C_eta(t)^dagger V(t) C_eta(t) - eta(t)^dagger V(t) eta(t)]`.

If `||V(t)|| <= J`, then

`||R(t)|| <= 2J` on the ramp intervals,

and the action is at most `2J` times their total length (or, conservatively, the sum of individual ramp widths).

This residual has the required initial-frame covariance. It gives a qualified application of the character theorem and, only with the full support/locality assumptions, of the associated support argument.

It does not automatically cover initial or final noisy wrappers, wrong control-only endpoints, controller faults, interactions elsewhere in the compiler, or simultaneous overlapping controls whose toggling residual acquires another parent's private-path dependence. Those need separate exact certification.

Unbounded Poisson counts together with positive physical ramp widths also do not automatically fit a fixed reservation: overlap, overflow, endpoint and latency handling must be specified. An action estimate alone does not resolve that scheduling obstruction.

## Final scope

The coherent extension is mathematically sound as stated under these explicit premises and gives a useful RMS control-action cost. It is not a proof that arbitrary finite-width or noisy controls satisfy those premises, and it is not by itself a new memory-threshold or support theorem.
