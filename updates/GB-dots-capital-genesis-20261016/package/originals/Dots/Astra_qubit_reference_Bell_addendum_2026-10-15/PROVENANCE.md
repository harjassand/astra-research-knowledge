# Provenance correction and established foundations

## Earlier finite Petz order characterization

Complete quantum information orders, an AI-generated mathematical research manuscript dated 10 October 2026, already states the finite endpoint characterization in Appendix A, Theorem A.1. It equates complete all-state-pair Petz Rényi order-two comparison with a finite-reference test and the existence of a complex-linear unital lift satisfying a complete quadratic domination condition. The lift is not required to preserve Hermiticity. Appendix A.4 supplies an explicit finite semidefinite formulation.

This is a predecessor in the existing research record, not an external peer-reviewed priority claim. The present addendum acknowledges its statement and corrects later positioning; it does not re-audit or endorse every other result in that manuscript. The source is identified by title, date, and theorem rather than bundled as raw extraction or source PDF.

The later arbitrary-classical-channel theorem gives a direct real signed matrix D with DS=T and per-input covariance inequalities diag(t_i)-D diag(s_i)D^T >= 0, together with a constructive separation argument. For this subclass it identifies ordinary unsmoothed complete binary-MMSE, SLD, and standard-Petz/RLD comparison. Its proof has been independently reconstructed and is included in proofs/ARBITRARY_CLASSICAL_SPECIALIZATION.md.

The defensible description is a direct constructive classical specialization with explicit witnesses, not a first finite comparison algorithm. Its polynomial-size rational SDP representation is not an exact polynomial-time boundary-decision theorem. The general quantum complex-linear lift should not silently be treated as Hermiticity-preserving or as a physical decoder.

The earlier delivered reverse-radius identification and its sharpness constructions remain mathematically valid. Their broader novelty framing is narrowed by this predecessor and by established radius, conditional-order, and signed-lift theory. External review and historical priority remain open.

## State comparison and Bell readout precedents

Barnett, Chefles and Jex, Comparison of two unknown pure quantum states, PRA 67, 023613 (2003), Eqs. (18)–(20), studies known binary pure-state codebooks grouped by equal and different preparation indices: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0202087 . Jex, Andersson and Chefles, Comparing the states of many quantum systems, Section IV and IV.1, treats comparison via conditional-mixture discrimination and explicitly uses Bell measurement for trine comparison: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0305120 .

Quantum-state comparison and discrimination (2018), Section II Eqs. (7)–(9), establishes local-discrimination-then-comparison optimality for a binary mixed-state minimum-error comparison setting: https://arxiv.org/html/1803.09030 . These are direct structural precedents. Their 0–1 discrimination objectives are distinct from the mixed unequal-codebook quadratic-MMSE score used here.

Yoon-Ho Kim, Single-photon two-qubit entangled states: preparation and measurement, PRA 67, 040301(R) (2003), experimentally reports deterministic Bell preparation and measurement using polarization and spatial modes of one photon: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0303125 . The proposed optical primitive is established, not invented here. Same-photon two-degree-of-freedom Bell analysis must not be confused with limitations for two independently encoded photons.

DiVincenzo, Horodecki, Leung, Smolin and Terhal, Locking classical correlations in quantum states, PRL 92, 067902 (2004), concerns accessible correlation and communication-dependent unlocking: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0303088 . Hiding a binary parity in correlations is not by itself that operational locking phenomenon.

For the Bell family, the two hypotheses are equal-index and unequal-index mixtures of two binary codebooks. Both marginals hide the label. After the output rotation, their difference is proportional to X tensor X, so local X measurement already attains Helstrom 0–1 error. Bell readout refines the parity decision into sectors with different posterior-score magnitudes; those magnitudes matter to MMSE. No new measurement operation or necessity of entangled measurement for binary guessing is claimed.

Established signed-lift and observable-estimation framework references remain Zhu–Wang, https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24693v2 , and Hirche, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03706 . Bounded literature checks have not established priority for the precise channel-order specialization or mixed-state risk formula.
