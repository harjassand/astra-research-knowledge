LAPLACE POSITIVITY, ADAPTIVE CONVOLUTION, AND A DEPENDENCE OBSTRUCTION
Independent construction/consequence worker, 2026-10-15

STATUS

The proposed finite-dimensional criterion is true. The proof below supplies an
explicit radial potential, so the phrase "choose the slope sufficiently slowly"
is not an unproved existence step. Necessity holds for merely positive
semidefinite output and does not require integrability of derivatives of the
input density. A concrete compact smooth signed scalar kernel has strictly
positive real Laplace transform but superexponentially deep positive dips, so
no fixed exponential lower margin follows from the hypotheses.

The scalar operator 1 + D_x^2 D_y^2 supplies a particularly clear consequence:
some radial strictly log-concave density regularizes it, but every strictly
positive smooth product density with all exponential moments fails. The
product obstruction was proposed by the root investigator and independently
proved here by the cutoff lemma.

Novelty is UNRESOLVED. Diamond--Essen (1978) is an immediate prior-work gate;
do not present this as a literature-cleared new theorem.

1. EXACT THEOREM

Let T be a compactly supported, finite-order, Hermitian d-by-d matrix-valued
distribution on R^m, with T(1)=I_d. Define

    L_T(g) = <T(y), exp(g dot y)>,          g in R^m.

The following are equivalent:

    (a) L_T(g) is positive definite for every real g.

    (b) There is a positive radial C-infinity probability density p such that
        Hess(log p) is negative definite everywhere, p has every real
        exponential moment, and Q=T*p is positive definite everywhere.

    (c) There is a smooth probability density p with every real exponential
        moment such that Q=T*p is positive semidefinite everywhere.

The p in (b) can additionally be chosen so that every derivative D^alpha p
has every real exponential moment in absolute value. Consequently Q and all
its derivatives also have every real exponential moment in absolute value.
The normalizations and identities are

    integral Q(x) dx = I_d,
    integral exp(g dot x) Q(x) dx = M_p(g) L_T(g),
    M_p(g) = integral exp(g dot x) p(x) dx > 0.

"Positive definite for every real g" is pointwise matrix positivity, not
positive-definiteness in the Bochner sense of a function on a group.

2. NECESSITY WITH NO DERIVATIVE-INTEGRABILITY ASSUMPTION

Take a nonnegative smooth radial cutoff chi, equal to one near zero and
nonincreasing with radius, and set chi_R(x)=chi(x/R). Then chi_R increases to
one. For fixed g, write

    H_R(y) = integral exp(g dot x) chi_R(x) p(x-y) dx
           = integral p(z) exp(g dot (z+y)) chi_R(z+y) dz.

All derivatives in y can be taken on the exponential and cutoff in the
second expression. For any fixed compact set K and any finite N,

    H_R -> M_p(g) exp(g dot y)       in C^N(K).

Indeed the term with no cutoff derivative converges uniformly on K by
dominated convergence and exponential integrability of p. Every term with
a nonzero cutoff derivative is bounded by a constant times R^(-k) M_p(g)
for k>=1 and converges uniformly to zero. Thus distributional Fubini, valid
against each compactly supported test function, gives

    lim_R integral exp(g dot x) chi_R(x) Q(x) dx
        = M_p(g) L_T(g).

For a nonzero vector v, monotone convergence applies to the nonnegative
function v*Q(x)v. At g=0 this establishes

    integral v*Q(x)v dx = ||v||^2 > 0.

Therefore v*Qv is not zero almost everywhere, and its integral against the
strictly positive function exp(g dot x) is strictly positive. This proves

    v*L_T(g)v > 0

for every nonzero v and every g. The argument simultaneously proves that
these exponential moments of Q are finite. Matrix off-diagonal integrability
follows from positivity, for example |Q_ij| <= (Q_ii+Q_jj)/2. No integration
by parts at infinity and no absolute integrability of D^alpha p was assumed.

3. A CONSTRUCTIVE SLOW-GRADIENT LEMMA

Let q>=2 be an integer and epsilon:[0,infinity)->(0,infinity) be continuous
and nonincreasing. There exists a radial smooth phi with Hess phi positive
definite, |grad phi(x)| tending to infinity, and

    ||D^j phi(x)|| <= epsilon(|grad phi(x)|),     2 <= j <= q.

Construction. Put

    phi(x) = sum_{n>=1} (sqrt(R_n^2+|x|^2) - R_n),

with R_n>=1, R_{n+1}>=2R_n, and the radii chosen as described below. For
each j>=2, a dimension-dependent constant C_j satisfies

    ||D^j sqrt(R^2+|x|^2)|| <= C_j (R+|x|)^(1-j).

Let C be at least max(C_2,...,C_q). All the displayed derivative series
converge uniformly, and the series for phi and its first derivatives converge
locally uniformly, since sum 1/R_n is finite. This gives a C-infinity radial
function. Each square-root summand has strictly positive Hessian, so phi does
as well. Its radial slope is

    a(r) = sum_{n>=1} r/sqrt(R_n^2+r^2).

For every fixed M, the first M terms tend to one as r tends to infinity;
hence a(r) tends to infinity. If R_k <= r < R_{k+1}, then

    a(r) <= k + r sum_{n>k} 1/R_n <= k+2,

and, for 2<=j<=q,

    ||D^j phi|| <= C [k/r + sum_{n>k} 1/R_n]
                <= C [k/R_k + 2/R_{k+1}]
                <= C (k+1)/R_k.

Choose recursively

    R_1 >= max(1, 2C/epsilon(2)),
    R_k >= 2 R_{k-1},
    R_k >= C(k+1)/epsilon(k+2),           k>=1,

enlarging R_1 if both k=1 constraints require it. For r<R_1, a(r)<=2 and
||D^j phi|| <= 2C/R_1 <= epsilon(2) <= epsilon(a(r)). For the other
intervals the previous inequalities give the same assertion. Every
derivative of order >=2 is globally bounded, including orders above q.

4. SUFFICIENCY: LOCAL EXPONENTIAL TANGENTS

Take K={|y|<=R} containing supp T, with R>=1. Choose N and C_T such that

    ||T(f)||_op <= C_T max_{|alpha|<=N, y in K}|D^alpha f(y)|.

Let

    lambda(s) = min_{|g|<=s} lambda_min(L_T(g)) > 0.

It is continuous and nonincreasing. Fix q=max(N,2). If phi is smooth and
all derivatives of orders 2,...,q are bounded by eta on x-K, write

    g = grad phi(x),
    w_x(y) = phi(x)-phi(x-y)-g dot y.

Taylor's theorem and the fundamental theorem of calculus give

    |w_x| <= R^2 eta/2,
    |D w_x| <= R eta,
    |D^j w_x| <= eta for 2<=j<=N.

For eta at most a fixed small constant, the product rule and the finite
Faà di Bruno formula therefore yield

    || exp(phi(x)-phi(x-y))-exp(g dot y) ||_{C^N(K)}
      <= C_* exp(R|g|)(1+|g|)^N eta,

where C_* depends only on m,N,R. Choose a positive continuous nonincreasing
delta(s) such that

    delta(s) <= 1/(R+1),
    delta(s) <= a fixed small constant for the preceding bound,
    C_T C_* exp(Rs)(1+s)^N delta(s) <= lambda(s)/2.

Apply the lemma with epsilon(s)=delta(s+1). The global Hessian bound
implies that the slope changes by less than one across x-K. For z in x-K,

    |grad phi(z)| + 1 >= |grad phi(x)|,

so monotonicity gives

    ||D^j phi(z)|| <= delta(|grad phi(z)|+1)
                  <= delta(|grad phi(x)|),       2<=j<=q.

Consequently

    || T(exp(phi(x)-phi(x-y))) - L_T(g) ||_op <= lambda(|g|)/2,

and hence

    T(exp(phi(x)-phi(x-y))) >= lambda(|g|) I_d/2 > 0.

Set p=Z^(-1)exp(-phi). Then

    (T*p)(x) = p(x) T(exp(phi(x)-phi(x-y))) > 0.

The unbounded radial slope gives phi(r)/r -> infinity, so Z is finite and
p has every exponential moment. Hess phi is positive definite, so p is
strictly log-concave in the strongest Hessian sense. The bounded Hessian
also gives |grad phi(x)|=O(|x|). Since all higher derivatives of phi are
globally bounded, for each multi-index alpha,

    |D^alpha p(x)| <= C_alpha (1+|x|)^|alpha| p(x).

This proves the asserted absolute exponential integrability of every
derivative, justifies Fubini and integration by parts directly for this
constructed density, and proves the normalization and all moments.
For completeness, sup_{|y|<=R}p(x-y) is at most p(x)exp(C_R(1+|x|)),
by the bounded Hessian. Applying the finite-order distribution bound to
translated derivatives proves absolute exponential integrability of Q and
every derivative of Q as well.

5. EXPLICIT STRICT POSITIVITY WITHOUT ANY EXPONENTIAL LOWER MARGIN

For n>=1 define

    r_n = 2^(-n),     a_n = 4^n,     b_n = exp(-a_n^2),
    c_n = exp(-a_n r_n) exp(-i b_n r_n),

and the real signed measure

    Q_n = [delta_0 - 2 Re(c_n) delta_{r_n}
                    + |c_n|^2 delta_{2r_n}] / |1-c_n|^2.

Each Q_n has mass one and support in [0,2r_n]. Since |c_n|=exp(-2^n),

    sum ||Q_n-delta_0||_TV < infinity.

Thus the partial convolution products converge in total variation to a real
signed measure T_0 of mass one, supported in [0,2]. Its Laplace transform is

    L_0(g) = product_{n>=1} |1-c_n exp(r_n g)|^2 / |1-c_n|^2.

For fixed real g, no numerator vanishes, because b_n r_n is positive and
strictly less than 2*pi. The tail product has summable deviations from one.
Hence L_0(g)>0 for every real g.

At g=a_n, the nth numerator is

    |1-exp(-i b_n r_n)|^2 <= r_n^2 exp(-2a_n^2).

For k<n, each numerator is at most 4 exp(2r_k a_n), so their product is at
most 4^(n-1) exp(2a_n). For k>n,

    (a_k-a_n)r_k >= (3/4)2^k,

so the tail numerator product is bounded by the finite constant

    product_{k>=1}(1+exp(-(3/4)2^k))^2.

The product of the inverse denominators is another finite constant. Since
4^(n-1)r_n^2=1/4, there is a constant C independent of n such that

    0 < L_0(a_n) <= C exp(-2a_n^2+2a_n).

In particular L_0 has no positive lower bound of the form c exp(-C|g|),
for any c,C>0. A lower bound allowing a fixed polynomial factor also fails.

To obtain a smooth compact kernel, take any nonnegative normalized smooth
bump B supported in [0,e], e>0, and put

    T = T_0 * B * B.

Then T is a real compactly supported C-infinity function of integral one,
supported in [0,2+2e], and

    L_T(g)=L_0(g) M_B(g)^2 > 0,
    L_T(a_n) <= C exp(-2a_n^2+2(1+e)a_n).

It is necessarily signed: any nonnegative mass-one measure supported in
[0,infinity) has Laplace transform at least one for g>=0, whereas the last
display tends to zero along g=a_n.

The criterion therefore regularizes an explicit smooth signed kernel outside
every fixed exponential-margin hypothesis. The theorem is a mathematical
existence result; this example gives no useful finite scale for the required
regularizer.

6. A SHARP DEPENDENCE CONSEQUENCE FOR A CONSTANT-COEFFICIENT PDE

Consider, in two dimensions,

    T = delta_0 + D_x^2 D_y^2 delta_0,
    L_T(u,v)=1+u^2 v^2 >= 1.

The theorem supplies a radial strictly log-concave density p with every
exponential moment such that

    p + D_x^2 D_y^2 p > 0                 everywhere.

However no strictly positive C^2 product p(x,y)=f(x)g(y) with every
exponential moment can even make this expression nonnegative everywhere.

Proof. Positive integrable g has g''(y_0)<0 somewhere: otherwise g is
convex on the whole line, and a nonzero nonnegative convex function is not
integrable. If f g + f'' g'' >=0 everywhere, then at this y_0,

    f''(x) <= K f(x) for all x,
    K = -g(y_0)/g''(y_0) > 0.

Set h=Kf-f''>=0. Transferring derivatives onto a compactly supported test
function exp(t x)chi_R(x), exactly as in Section 2, gives

    lim_R integral exp(t x)chi_R(x)h(x) dx
       = (K-t^2) M_f(t).

For |t|>sqrt(K) the right side is negative, whereas the left side is a limit
of nonnegative numbers. This contradiction needs no integrability assumption
on f'' and proves the claim.

For centered isotropic Gaussian p_beta proportional to exp(-beta(x^2+y^2)/2),
one can see the failure directly on y=0:

    (p_beta + D_x^2D_y^2 p_beta)/p_beta
        = 1+beta^2-beta^3 x^2.

Every beta>0 therefore fails in sufficiently remote tails. The distinction
is substantive even for a scalar sum-of-squares symbol: dependence between
coordinates is necessary for this regularization class.

In fact every nondegenerate Gaussian fails, including arbitrary correlation,
covariance, and translation. Write

    p proportional to exp(-(z-mu)^T A(z-mu)/2),      A positive definite,
    u=(A(z-mu))_1,       v=(A(z-mu))_2.

Then

    D_x^2D_y^2 p / p
      = u^2v^2 - A_22 u^2 - A_11 v^2 - 4A_12 u v
        + A_11 A_22 + 2A_12^2.

Because A is invertible, points with v=0 and arbitrarily large u exist.
Along this line q/p=1+A_11 A_22+2A_12^2-A_22 u^2 tends to minus infinity.

Every finite centered isotropic Gaussian mixture also fails. Along y=0,
the component with smallest precision beta_* dominates, and the ratio is
-beta_*^3 x^2+O(1). No claim is made here about arbitrary finite mixtures
with nonradial means and covariance matrices.

The product obstruction extends to every pair k,l>=1:

    T=delta_0+D_x^(2k)D_y^(2l)delta_0,
    L_T(u,v)=1+u^(2k)v^(2l)>0.

The criterion supplies radial strictly log-concave regularization, but no
positive sufficiently smooth product with all exponential moments works.
If g^(2l)>=0 everywhere, testing against chi_R and moving derivatives to
the cutoff gives integral g^(2l)=0 by monotone convergence, so g^(2l)=0.
Then g is a polynomial, impossible for a positive integrable function.
Thus g^(2l)<0 somewhere. At that point positivity forces f^(2k)<=Kf;
the exponential-cutoff argument gives (K-t^(2k))M_f(t)>=0 for every t,
which is impossible.

For k=l=1, q=p+D_x^2D_y^2p preserves both one-variable
marginals exactly, because integrating a second derivative in the other
variable gives zero. It also satisfies

    E_q[X^2Y^2] = E_p[X^2Y^2] + 4,

while means, variances and E[XY] are unchanged. All these integrations are
justified by the established absolute exponential moments of derivatives.

6A. AN EXPLICIT REGULARIZER WITH A GLOBAL POSITIVITY MARGIN

The following concrete density was proposed by the root investigator; every
derivative, coefficient, and bound below was independently checked here.
Set L=5 and

    rho = sqrt(1+(x^2+y^2)/L^2),
    p(x,y) = Z^(-1) exp(-L rho log(rho)).

Then q=p+D_x^2D_y^2 p is a strictly positive probability density, and

    q/p >= 1-B_L,
    B_L = 27/(exp(2)L) + (3/2+4/27)/L^2 + 5/(2L^3) < 0.817.

In particular the global lower bound is greater than 0.183. The density is
radial, smooth, has negative-definite Hessian of its logarithm, and has all
real exponential moments. No Gaussian and no strictly positive C^2 product
density with all exponential moments can satisfy even q>=0 for this T.

Verification. Put s=x^2+y^2, t=1+s/L^2, l=log(t), and

    h(s)=(L/2)sqrt(t)log(t),             p=Z^(-1)exp(-h).

The successive derivatives with respect to s are

    h1=(l+2)/(4L sqrt(t)),
    h2=-l/(8L^3 t^(3/2)),
    h3=(3l-2)/(16L^5 t^(5/2)),
    h4=(16-15l)/(32L^7 t^(7/2)).

Write

    A2=h1^2-h2,
    A3=-h1^3+3h1 h2-h3,
    A4=h1^4-6h1^2 h2+3h2^2+4h1 h3-h4.

Direct differentiation gives

    D_x^2D_y^2 p / p = 4A2 + 8s A3 + 16x^2y^2 A4.

Since h1>0 and h2<=0, A2 and the first three terms of A4 are nonnegative.
The negative contributions in 8s A3 are bounded, respectively, by

    27/(exp(2)L),       3/(2L^2),       1/(2L^3).

For the first use
max_{l>=0}(l+2)^3 exp(-l/2)=216 exp(-2), attained at l=4.
For the second use l(l+2)exp(-l)<=2. For the third use
(3l-2)_+exp(-3l/2)<=1. The first inequality follows by differentiation
or elementary comparison; the latter bound is deliberately loose.

Since 16x^2y^2<=4s^2, the negative contribution from 4h1 h3 in A4 is
at most 4/(27L^2): use

    max_{t>=1}(t-1)^2/t^3=4/27,
    (l+2)(2-3l)_+<=4.

The negative contribution from -h4 is at most 2/L^3, by
(t-1)^2/t^(7/2)<=1 and (16-15l)_+<=16. Adding these five bounds yields
B_L as stated.

The tangential and radial eigenvalues of Hess h(|x|^2) are, respectively,

    2h1=(l+2)/(2L sqrt(t)) > 0,
    2h1+4s h2=(2t+l)/(2L t^(3/2)) > 0.

Finally L rho log(rho)/sqrt(s) tends to infinity, giving every exponential
moment. Derivatives have at most polynomial/logarithmic factors times p,
so integration by parts proves integral q=1 without boundary terms.

More generally, for every Hermitian matrix polynomial P with P(0)=I, strict
P(g)>0 for all real g is equivalent to the existence of a radial strictly
log-concave probability p with all exponential moments for which P(-D)p is
an everywhere positive-definite probability operator density. Here
T=P(-D)delta_0, since <D^alpha delta_0,exp(g dot y)>=(-g)^alpha.

7. STRICTNESS AND DEGENERATE NORMALIZATION

Nonnegative L_T(g) is insufficient even in one scalar dimension. Take

    T = delta_0 - 2 D^2 delta_0 + D^4 delta_0,
    L_T(g)=(g^2-1)^2,     T(1)=1.

Although L_T is nonnegative everywhere, it vanishes at g=1 and g=-1.
Necessity proves that no smooth probability p with every exponential moment
can make T*p merely nonnegative. Equivalently, no such p can satisfy

    p-2p''+p'''' >=0 everywhere.

For T(1)=A positive semidefinite but singular, the correct extension is:

    T annihilates ker A on both matrix sides, and
    L_T(g) restricted to ran A is positive definite for every real g.

These conditions are necessary and sufficient for a positive semidefinite
regularization of total mass A. Necessity: if v lies in ker A, positivity
and zero integral force Q(x)v=0 for every x. Since the Fourier transform of
p is continuous and nonzero near zero, Q=T*p implies T_hat(xi)v=0 on a
real neighborhood. Compact support makes T_hat entire, hence T v=0.
Apply the preceding theorem on ran A after congruence normalization by
A^(-1/2). Sufficiency follows by that same reduction and embedding with
zero on ker A. If A=0, necessarily T=0.

Checking only L_T(g)>=0 is not enough when A is singular. The scalar
T=D^2 delta_0 has L_T(g)=g^2>=0 and A=0 but T is nonzero, and p'' cannot
be nonnegative for a probability density on the whole line.
