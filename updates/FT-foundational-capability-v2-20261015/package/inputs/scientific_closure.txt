Research result: the structural premise is realistic; the new-capability claim remains unestablished
Port-completed adjoint acquisition survives as an exact conditional technique. This investigation does not establish that it creates new scientific control power at lower total physical cost than existing methods.
The result is more specific than “the assumptions cannot be verified.” A reciprocal exterior connected to a small, accessible nonreciprocal subsystem is a credible hardware architecture—and a closely matching microwave architecture already appears in the literature. The remaining difficulties are obtaining the required source and sensing access, maintaining a qualified operating regime, acquiring actual actuator responses, and beating strong alternatives.
This round produced three substantive advances: a calibration quantity that can remain invariant across reciprocal control updates; a lower-readout implementation that physically synthesizes the adjoint; and additional adversarial examples identifying precisely which acquisitions are indispensable. The numerical work comprises synthetic circuits and closed-loop simulations, not laboratory measurements.
The appropriate disposition is to retain the theorem and its narrow engineering niche, but stop advancing it as an established new physical capability.
1. A concrete hardware match—and a stronger competitor than previously considered
A particularly relevant architecture is described by Yuan and colleagues in their 11 July 2026 nonreciprocal dynamic-metasurface-antenna study: a reciprocal, fabricated 19-GHz antenna with seven feeds and 96 binary tuners, combined with a single measured three-port circulator. Their model combines experimentally estimated antenna parameters with the circulator response. This closely matches the proposed separation between a reciprocal exterior and a small nonreciprocal interface. It is their architecture, not a newly invented Astra device, and their system-level study is not a port-completion experiment. arXiv
The strongest comparison is therefore not against an unrealistically perfect digital twin. Tapie and del Hougne have already demonstrated experimental estimation of a multiport model for the fabricated antenna and tested configurations optimized using that model. An appropriate baseline must include this measured-model route, with its calibration cost, computational cost, reuse across updates, and sensitivity to environmental change. IEEE Xplore
The consequential task would be configuring many interacting wave controls to obtain useful transmit, receive, or sensing responses despite imperfect knowledge of the hardware. However, transferring port completion to that task exposes four gaps.
The nonreciprocal interface must remain physically accessible while loaded. Knowing where the circulator sits does not supply its installed response. Injection couplers, reference-plane calibration, and invariant source impedances are required; disconnecting it to measure a different circuit does not automatically qualify the operating circuit.
The control sites must be observable. Three circulator-port measurements do not supply the fields at every tunable element. Real metasurface elements are often not connectorized, which is precisely why existing work estimates useful proxy models from end-to-end measurements. Adding a model-based reconstruction to recover those fields would reintroduce an acquisition cost that cannot be called free. arXiv
The objective’s mathematical source must be realizable. Detecting a field at a target does not imply the ability to emit the required adjoint excitation there. Cooperative terminals can provide excitation channels; an arbitrary inaccessible target or distributed absorption objective cannot be assumed to do so.
The available controls may be discrete. The identified antenna uses binary tuners. A derivative with respect to a continuously interpolated load is not itself a physically available action. The published study instead uses finite bit flips and efficient model updates. arXiv
Thus this architecture makes the structural premise credible, but does not yet establish an advantageous implementation.
The cleaner demonstrator
A lower-frequency circuit is easier to qualify: a grounded network of genuinely bilateral resistors or RC elements, an isolated four-terminal nonreciprocal module, permanently connected readers, and calibrated signed current injection.
An important exclusion emerged. A network assembled from components described as “variable resistors” is not automatically reciprocal in its small-signal voltage coordinates. For example, the published transistor-network model of Dillavou and colleagues includes conductance depending on the average voltage across each edge. arXiv
From its stated square-law model,
\[
I=\gamma\left[(V_g-V_{\rm th})(v_a-v_b)-\frac{v_a^2-v_b^2}{2}\right],
\]
the incremental nodal block is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
d_a&-d_b\\
-d_a&d_b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
d_a=\gamma(V_g-V_{\rm th}-v_a),\quad
d_b=\gamma(V_g-V_{\rm th}-v_b).
\]
It is generally nonsymmetric when \(v_a\ne v_b\).
This does not invalidate that learning hardware. It prevents us from importing the present theorem into it merely because its components are called resistors.
2. What the operation actually acquires
The recovered result assumes
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
B&E\\
E^T&C
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad C=C^T,
\]
with all nonreciprocity confined to known accessible coordinates selected by \(P\). At the same loaded operating point, it measures
\[
Y=A^{-1}P,\qquad z=A^{-1}c,\qquad H=P^TY,
\]
and solves
\[
H\alpha=P^Tz,\qquad
H\beta=(H^T-H)\alpha.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\lambda=z+Y\beta=A^{-T}c.}
\]
This reconstructs the adjoint without separately identifying the reciprocal interior or every nonreciprocal component value. It still requires the physical sources, the necessary sensed rows, and the actual control derivatives.    RESEARCH_NOTE(20261015-020428)
Advance 1: not every quantity needs reacquisition after an update
The loaded port response determines
\[
\boxed{D=H^{-T}-H^{-1}=B^T-B.}
\]
Consequently,
\[
A^T=A+PDP^T.
\]
If control updates change only reciprocal elements and the nonreciprocal module’s response remains fixed, \(D\) remains fixed even though \(H\) changes.
That is a useful correction to an overly broad claim that every relevant calibration must be repeated at every setting. The original digital formula needs the new \(H\), but the antisymmetric module response need not change with it.
In the synthetic test, a reciprocal update changed \(H\) by 15.5%, while \(D\) remained invariant to numerical precision.
It also supplies an exact alternative: inject boundary feedback
\[
-PDP^Tv
\]
while applying \(c\). The resulting field satisfies
\[
Av=c-PDP^Tv
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A^Tv=c.
\]
This would physically implement the adjoint.
It is not a free actuator. The feedback needs calibrated gain, reference planes, bandwidth, noise control, voltage/current compliance, and dynamic stability. A static inverse identity does not qualify a delayed active controller.
For a deliberately reversible skew-symmetric core, the simpler comparator is to reverse the core itself. Contemporary physical-backpropagation work already treats nonreciprocal systems with suitable known transformations and explicitly discusses their calibration and dynamic-range costs. arXiv
Advance 2: physical synthesis reduces interior readout
There is a better acquisition schedule than measuring every port-response column at every control site.
Measure \(H\) at the ports, measure \(z_P\) at the ports, compute \(\beta\), then apply
\[
c+P\beta.
\]
The apparatus produces the adjoint field directly. Only that field and the original forward field need full control-site readout.
For \(r\) input patterns sharing one linear operator, \(k\) nonreciprocal ports, and \(s\) sensed coordinates:

Physical synthesis trades more propagations for fewer interior readings. It does not eliminate interior sensing, source calibration, or potentially large correction-source amplitudes.
These counts also expose an important restriction: the reuse across \(r\) inputs requires the same operator. When different inputs produce different nonlinear operating-point Jacobians, that reuse is invalid.
3. A many-degree-of-freedom control test
I constructed a larger synthetic problem rather than repeating the previous small gradient check.
The plant has 144 nodes, 180 adjustable conductances, 100 sensed nodes, 44 unobserved nodes, and four declared nonreciprocal ports. Eight input patterns and sixteen output nodes define a response-shaping objective:
\[
J(p)=\frac{1}{2r}
\left\|Q^TA(p)^{-1}B-T\right\|_F^2,
\qquad r=8.
\]
Every controller receives the target \(T\), but not the configuration that generated it. The estimator receives only its permitted measurements and local control laws.
For the declared design instance, 86 task-Jacobian singular values exceed \(0.1\%\) of the largest. This is a problem with many relevant control directions, not merely many parameters affecting one trivial output.

Why small unobserved defects can matter
Suppose a physically synthesized candidate satisfies
\[
(A+P\widehat D P^T)\widehat\lambda=c.
\]
Define the missed transpose defect
\[
R_{\rm miss}=A^T-A-P\widehat D P^T.
\]
Then exactly,
\[
\widehat\lambda-\lambda
=A^{-T}R_{\rm miss}\widehat\lambda.
\]
For a candidate tangent source \(t\),
\[
\boxed{
(\widehat\lambda-\lambda)^Tt
=(A^{-1}t)^TR_{\rm miss}\widehat\lambda.
}
\]
Thus “the hidden nonreciprocity is small” helps only when it has an independently justified bound and the relevant action margin is large enough. An arbitrarily small defect can reverse a nearly zero slope.
Measurement calibration has an analogous problem. If the observed port response is
\[
\widetilde H=S_{\rm sensor}H M_{\rm source},
\]
its nonsymmetry can reflect source or sensor mismatch rather than physical nonreciprocity. Port locations alone do not supply these calibrations.
5. Three exact ways the required control action remains unidentified
A. Undeclared nonreciprocity
The recovered counterexample uses
\[
A_\pm=I\pm e_2e_4^T,
\]
with ordinary source \(b=e_1\), objective source \(c=e_2\), and declared ports \(e_3,e_5\).
Every field measured by the proposed protocol is identical in the two systems, even with complete state readout. Nevertheless, adding a conductance along \(v=e_1-e_4\) produces
\[
J_\pm(p)=\mp\frac{p}{1+2p}.
\]
The same action helps one system and harms the other. A probe at \(e_4\), or an actual control intervention, separates them. This violates the support assumption, not the identity.    RESEARCH_NOTE(20261015-020428)
More generally, if the acquired fields are \(X=A^{-1}U\), then a perturbation satisfying
\[
\Delta A\,X=0
\]
can remain invisible to those acquisitions while changing an unprobed sensitivity. Additional measurements must address the relevant unobserved directions, or an independently valid structural restriction must exclude them.
B. An inaccessible objective source—even with perfect reciprocity
A new counterexample uses two symmetric positive-definite response matrices whose leading blocks are
\[
G_\pm=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0.2&0\\
0.2&1&\pm0.5\\
0&\pm0.5&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with two additional independent coordinates.
Allow injections only at \(e_1,e_4,e_5\), while measuring the objective at \(e_2\). All available source responses are identical between the two systems, and both baseline objective values are \(0.2\).
For a conductance between nodes \(1\) and \(3\), however, the derivatives are
\[
\boxed{+0.3\quad\text{and}\quad-0.7.}
\]
An objective detector at node \(2\) does not supply the missing source there.
An \(e_2\) injection, a suitable tangent-source injection, or the actual conductance change supplies the missing information. Reciprocity alone does not.
C. Unknown actuator response
Two devices can have exactly the same operator at the measured setting but respond differently to the command:
\[
A_+(u)=A_0+u\,vv^T,\qquad
A_-(u)=A_0-u\,vv^T.
\]
All fixed-setting source measurements agree at \(u=0\), but the command gradients have opposite signs. A sufficiently small range around a positive nominal conductance makes both changes admissible.
Consequently, the desired gradient is
\[
\nabla_uJ
=
\left(\frac{\partial g}{\partial u}\right)^T\nabla_gJ,
\]
not automatically the conductance gradient. Cross-coupling, sign, gain and operating-point dependence in the actuator map must be acquired or guaranteed separately.
No refinement of the port inverse identifies an actuator derivative that never entered the measurements.
6. The alternatives already acquire much of the desired information
Ordinary physical adjoints already operate through unknown reciprocal scattering hardware. Guillamon and colleagues’ microwave work is a relevant experimental baseline; its three-control hardware example must be distinguished from its larger numerical example. arXiv
For nonreciprocal systems, the September 2026 Unifying Physical Backpropagation treatment supplies sufficient same-hardware transformations when an appropriate intertwiner is known and realizable. It explicitly accounts for recalibration and source-amplitude requirements; neither existence nor implementation of an arbitrary transformation is free. arXiv
For a single proposed direction \(d\), the tangent-source route is especially important:
\[
t_d=b_p d-A_p[d]x,\qquad
y=A^{-1}t_d,
\]
\[
J'_d=F_p d+c^Ty.
\]
It requires neither reciprocity nor physical injection of \(c\). It does require the actual actuator derivative and access to the support of \(t_d\). A direct control trial avoids that derivative assumption but pays intervention and reset costs.
Multiplexed and stochastic-gradient methods also avoid a complete exact gradient at every update. Their sample quality, perturbation cost and control-update timescales—not “two experiments per parameter”—are the appropriate comparisons. AIP Publishing

It trades reciprocity for nondegenerate diffusion and transition-score information, including calibrated diffusion and parameter-response quantities. Its validation is simulation, and its trajectory budget is not equivalent to one inexpensive static measurement. It therefore expands the competing methods without supplying a free escape from acquisition requirements. arXiv
7. A fundamentally different acquisition operation—and what it reveals
After reaching the competitive boundary for port completion, I investigated changing boundary couplings rather than reconstructing an adjoint through the fixed operator.
This has a concrete physical precedent: Virtual VNA 3.0 estimates a nonreciprocal multiport network using a few directly accessible ports, known individual terminations, and known coupled loads. Its experimental demonstration uses an eight-port network containing circulators, with four directly accessible ports and four load-connected ports. Its closed-form experiment uses nineteen load configurations—not nineteen scalar readings. This is an existing physical acquisition operation, not an Astra invention. arXiv
Why coupled loads can reveal information that more ordinary measurements cannot
The new illustrative test constructs two strictly dissipative nonreciprocal systems related by
\[
A_2=T A_1T^{-1},
\qquad
T=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1).
\]
Only ports \(1\) and \(2\) are directly driven and read. Every independent diagonal load on hidden ports \(3\) and \(4\) commutes with \(T\).
Therefore the systems have identical external responses under every such independent-load configuration. This follows symbolically; one hundred random-load checks also returned zero difference.
Now introduce a known bridge conductance
\[
p(e_3-e_4)(e_3-e_4)^T.
\]
That physical connection does not commute with the hidden transformation. In the constructed pair, its objective derivatives are
\[
+0.04,\qquad -0.12.
\]
For the finite action \(p=0.1\), the objective changes are
\[
+0.00333,\qquad -0.01000.
\]
The coupled intervention exposes exactly the information needed to distinguish the control action. Merely collecting more independent-load measurements would not.
The converse is equally important
When every allowed future control commutes with the hidden transformation, and the objective is externally observed, this internal ambiguity can be operationally irrelevant. Both models then predict the same outcomes for the available actions.
That changes the interpretation of “closure”:
The necessary acquisition is not whatever uniquely identifies the hidden system. It is whatever distinguishes alternatives that would change the admissible scientific decision.

Sometimes a calibrated coupling is necessary. Sometimes identifying additional hidden parameters is unnecessary work.
The physical coupled-load mechanism is already established prior art. The contribution here is the explicit action-level counterexample and its relevance to Astra’s acquisition criterion, not a priority claim for the mechanism.
Final assessment
The investigation separates four questions that should not be conflated.
Is the identity correct under its assumptions?
Yes. The reconstruction and its extensions survived the analytic and synthetic checks.
Can the structural premise correspond to realistic hardware?
Yes. Engineered reciprocal networks with a small nonreciprocal interface are credible, and closely matching microwave architectures exist. But their full source, sensing, actuator and parasitic-response requirements still need qualification.
Has port completion demonstrated a meaningful total-cost advantage?
No. It has conditional profile-count and control-preservation benefits. Physical synthesis improves its readout burden. Nevertheless, qualified core transposition can provide identical decisions more cheaply, stochastic methods trade fewer readings for more interventions, and calibrated multiport models already control closely relevant hardware.
Does this establish the broader epistemic-closure hypothesis?
No. It establishes a concrete failure mode: exact reconstruction can depend on information absent from the proposed measurements. It also shows why that is not the entire explanation—once the missing structure is supplied, novelty or competitive advantage may still disappear, and some hidden ambiguity need not be resolved at all.
Port completion should therefore remain a conditional instrument technique, not be promoted as newly established scientific control power. Its plausible niche requires a real apparatus where control changes or core reversal are expensive, field acquisition is comparatively cheap, and all necessary sources and actuator responses are available in the same regime. No such matched-total-cost advantage was established here.
