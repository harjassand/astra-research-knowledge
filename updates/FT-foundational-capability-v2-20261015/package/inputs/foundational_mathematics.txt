1. The mathematical obstruction selected
After screening identity testing, quantum recovery, and sampling-related directions, I concentrated on a precise representation problem:
Can one positive measurement produce a classical vector whose every linear projection reproduces the corresponding quantum observable, with a prescribed state-independent additive noise?

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) be bounded self-adjoint operators and write
\[
A(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_jA_j.
\]
For a classical noise density \(p\), seek a positive operator-valued probability density \(M_A\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R^m}e^{it\cdot x}M_A(x)\,dx
=
\widehat p(t)e^{iA(t)}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here
\[
M_A(x)\succeq0,\qquad \int M_A(x)\,dx=I.
\]
For a quantum state \(\rho\), the classical output has density
\[
P_\rho(x)=\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(\rho M_A(x)\bigr).
\]
Equation (1) means that, for every real vector \(u\), the law of \(u\cdot X\) is exactly the spectral law of \(A(u)\), convolved with the law of \(u\cdot Z\), where \(Z\sim p\).
This is stronger than jointly measuring noisy versions of the individual coordinates. The all-linear-marginal Wigner framework is established mathematics; I am not claiming that representation itself as new. Published particle-approximation work also constructs signed measures approaching it. DOI
The opening in Astra’s previous result
The earlier Astra note supplied two contrasting candidates: Gaussian noise permits this exact representation only for commuting bounded tuples, while sufficiently broad independent hyperbolic-secant noise permits it for arbitrary bounded tuples.    Pasted markdown(20261011-124622)
Its stronger obstruction explicitly required independent noise coordinates: if one coordinate has every exponential moment and a transverse coordinate has finite variance, noncommutativity is impossible under that independence assumption.    Pasted markdown(20261011-124622)
The new investigation challenges that assumption rather than trying to improve the old derivative expansion.
The proposed escape is dependent, radially symmetric noise whose tails decay faster than every exponential, but not as fast as a Gaussian.
2. Main theorem: one noise law for every bounded tuple
Suppose a positive bound \(R\) is available such that
\[
\sup_{|u|=1}\|A(u)\|\le R.
\]
Choose
\[
0<c<\frac1{2R}.
\]
Define
\[
f(z)=\sqrt{1+|z|^2}\log\sqrt{1+|z|^2},
\]
and, for a scale \(L>0\),
\[
V_L(x)=cL f(x/L),\qquad
p_L(x)=\frac{e^{-V_L(x)}}{\displaystyle\int e^{-V_L(y)}\,dy}.
\]
Universal regularization theorem
For sufficiently large finite \(L\), depending only on \(m,R,c\), the inverse Fourier transform
\[
\boxed{
M_{A,L}
=
\mathcal F^{-1}\!\left[
\widehat p_L(t)e^{iA(t)}
\right]
}
\tag{2}
\]
is a positive, normalized operator density for every tuple satisfying the norm bound.
Moreover, writing \(g_L(x)=\nabla V_L(x)\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\,p_L(x)e^{A(g_L(x))}
\preceq M_{A,L}(x)
\preceq
\frac32\,p_L(x)e^{A(g_L(x))}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The same scalar noise works uniformly over the operator tuple and independently of Hilbert-space dimension. The argument applies to bounded operators on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces as well as finite matrices.
The density is smooth, radial, and strictly log-concave. Its tails satisfy
\[
-\log p_L(x)
=
c|x|\log|x|+O(|x|),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\int e^{a|x|}p_L(x)\,dx<\infty
\quad\text{for every finite }a\ge0.
}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus this is not a heavy-tailed construction with missing moments. It has all polynomial moments and all exponential moments.
The constants \(1/2\) and \(3/2\) in (3) can be replaced by \(1-\varepsilon\) and \(1+\varepsilon\) by increasing \(L\). However, the proof does not provide an optimized practical scale or a polynomial-time measurement compiler.
3. The mathematical operation behind the theorem
The essential change is what gets expanded.
Astra’s previous successful construction controlled derivatives of \(p\) relative to \(p\), uniformly throughout the tails. That works for its hyperbolic-secant density.    Pasted markdown(20261011-124622)
Here, \(\nabla\log p_L(x)\) becomes unbounded. Comparing the entire operator density with \(p_L(x)I\) is therefore the wrong representation.
Instead, the comparison is with
\[
p_L(x)e^{A(\nabla V_L(x))},
\]
which is positive exactly, without assuming commutativity.
Step 1: retain the generalized Wigner distribution
Let \(W_A\) denote the operator-valued distribution with
\[
\widehat W_A(t)=e^{iA(t)}.
\]
Its support lies in the ball \(\overline B_R\). The relevant exponential-type estimate follows from
\[
\|e^{iA(t)-A(s)}\|\le e^{R|s|},
\]
followed by the standard Paley–Wiener–Schwartz support theorem.
Crucially, its real Laplace transform is
\[
\boxed{
\langle W_A,e^{g\cdot y}\rangle=e^{A(g)}\succ0.
}
\tag{5}
\]
The distribution itself need not be positive. Nevertheless, every real exponential test gives a strictly positive operator.
There is also a distribution estimate independent of operator dimension. For a fixed cutoff \(\chi\) supported in a slightly larger ball \(B_{R_1}\),
\[
\|\langle W_A,\chi h\rangle\|
\le
C\max_{|\alpha|\le N}
\sup_{B_{R_1}}|\partial^\alpha h|,
\tag{6}
\]
where a finite even \(N>m\) suffices.
This is reconstructed directly from
\[
\|\langle W_A,h\rangle\|
\le (2\pi)^{-m}\|\widehat h\|_1,
\]
and integration by parts using \((1-\Delta)^{N/2}\). It does not hide dimension-dependent matrix coefficients.
Step 2: expand the logarithmic remainder, not the exponential tilt
For each \(x\), set
\[
g=\nabla V_L(x),
\]
and define
\[
F_x(y)
=
V_L(x-y)-V_L(x)+g\cdot y.
\]
Then the following is exact:
\[
p_L(x-y)
=
p_L(x)e^{g\cdot y}e^{-F_x(y)}.
\]
Applying \(W_A\),
\[
\frac{M_{A,L}(x)}{p_L(x)}
=
e^{A(g)}+E_x,
\]
where
\[
E_x=
\left\langle W_A,
e^{g\cdot y}\bigl(e^{-F_x(y)}-1\bigr)
\right\rangle,
\]
with a cutoff understood on the compact support.
The whole noncommutative exponential is retained. Only the curvature remainder is treated perturbatively.
Step 3: control that remainder everywhere
Put
\[
s=\frac{|x|}{L},\qquad h(s)=1+\log(1+s).
\]
The chosen potential satisfies
\[
|g|\le c\,h(s),
\]
and, through the finitely many derivative orders needed in (6),
\[
\|F_x\|_{C^N(B_{R_1})}
\le
\frac{C}{L}\frac{h(s)}{1+s}.
\]
The logarithmic slope grows, but its local variation becomes small.
The distribution estimate then yields
\[
\|E_x\|
\le
\frac{C}{L}
e^{R_1|g|}(1+|g|)^N
\frac{h(s)}{1+s}.
\]
An absolute error estimate is insufficient because the smallest eigenvalue of \(e^{A(g)}\) may be small. The error must be measured relative to the positive leading operator:
\[
\left\|
e^{-A(g)/2}E_xe^{-A(g)/2}
\right\|
\le
\frac{C'}{L}
h(s)^{N+1}(1+s)^{-a},
\tag{7}
\]
where
\[
a=1-c(R+R_1).
\]
Because \(c<1/(2R)\), one can choose \(R_1>R\) with \(a>0\). Therefore
\[
\sup_{s\ge0}
h(s)^{N+1}(1+s)^{-a}<\infty.
\]
Taking \(L\) sufficiently large makes (7) at most \(1/2\), proving positivity at every point, including arbitrarily remote tails. Fourier evaluation at zero gives normalization.
A broader principle
The same proof applies to a compactly supported operator distribution \(T\) whose Laplace transform satisfies
\[
T(e^{g\cdot y})\succeq \eta e^{-b|g|}I,
\]
provided a finite distribution seminorm is controlled.
If its support lies in \(B_a\), the construction works for
\[
c<\frac1{a+b}.
\]
This is the reusable candidate operation: turn quantitative Laplace positivity into genuine positivity after a carefully designed scalar convolution.
It is not a claim that Laplace positivity alone, without the stated quantitative assumptions, solves every regularization problem.
4. Matching obstruction: the \(r\log r\) scale is sharp
An existence construction could still be an unnecessarily permissive workaround. To test that, I derived a converse using a noncommuting Pauli pair:
\[
A_1=R\sigma_z,\qquad A_2=R\sigma_x.
\]
Suppose a radial noise law admits the exact representation, and define its directional cumulant generating function
\[
K(s)=\log\mathbb E e^{sZ_1}.
\]
Exponentially tilt the proposed positive measurement in the first coordinate. Positivity of the resulting first- and second-moment block matrix forces
\[
\text{tilted transverse variance}
\ge
\frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s^2}.
\]
For radial noise, the transverse variance under this tilt is exactly
\[
\frac{K'(s)}s.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
K'(s)\ge
\frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
The right-hand side grows exponentially with rate \(2R\).
Now suppose a radial density decays at least as fast as
\[
p(x)\le C e^{-c_0|x|\log|x|}
\]
for some
\[
c_0>\frac1{2R}.
\]
Choose \(c'\) strictly between \(1/(2R)\) and \(c_0\). The elementary optimization
\[
\sup_{r>0}\{sr-c'r\log r\}
=
c'e^{s/c'-1}
\]
bounds the cumulant growth by an exponential rate at most \(1/c'<2R\), contradicting (8).
Exact universal supremum
For \(m\ge2\), consider smooth radial noises satisfying
\[
-\log p(x)=c|x|\log|x|+O(|x|)
\]
that work for every bounded tuple with directional norm at most \(R\).
The construction supplies every \(c<1/(2R)\). The Pauli obstruction excludes every \(c>1/(2R)\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sup c_{\mathrm{universally\ admissible}}
=
\frac1{2R}.
}
\tag{9}
\]
This is a complete supremum statement, not a claim of universal attainment at equality.
It also distinguishes two notions that can otherwise be confused:
The noise can decay faster than every exponential, but cannot have arbitrarily fast radial tails. Radial Gaussian tails, for example, are too fast for this exact interface.
This is an exact-positivity obstruction, not a lower bound on approximation error. The forbidden negativity can reside in regions carrying extremely small probability mass.
5. A stronger separation: identical coordinate laws, opposite answers
The construction gives a direct test of what information covariance and individual marginals omit.
Let \(p_L\) be a successful radial noise and \(q_L\) its common one-coordinate marginal. Form the product density
\[
p_{\mathrm{prod}}(x)
=
\prod_{j=1}^{m}q_L(x_j).
\]
These two noise laws have:
- exactly the same individual coordinate distributions;
- the same mean and covariance matrix;
- every exponential moment.
Nevertheless, \(p_L\) supplies the exact representation for every tuple in the norm ball, whereas \(p_{\mathrm{prod}}\) cannot supply it for a noncommuting finite-dimensional tuple.
The reason is precise. Under exponential tilting, dependence allows the transverse variance to grow. For the product law, that variance stays fixed while the noncommutative covariance defect diverges.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{same coordinate laws and covariance}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{same exact all-linear representation capability}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
There is an important qualification: the noisy coordinate observables themselves still have a joint measurement, witnessed by the successful construction. What fails for the product noise is the stronger requirement that every linear projection have the prescribed noise-convolved law.
The two noises do not have identical distributions in every projection; that would determine their joint law.
6. An explicit endpoint example, rather than only an existence proof
I also obtained a separate exact construction for the Pauli triple
\[
A=(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z).
\]
On \(\mathbb R^3\), take
\[
\boxed{
p(x)=\frac{C}{\Gamma(2+|x|/2)}.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Its leading tail coefficient is \(1/2\), precisely the boundary for \(R=1\), and it has every exponential moment. This particular density is not smooth at the origin.
The proof uses a radial wave formula. Write \(p(r)\) for its radial profile, \(f(r)=rp(r)\), and
\[
I(r)=\int_{|r-1|}^{r+1}f(s)\,ds.
\]
The operator kernel has the form
\[
M(x)=u(r)I+v(r)\widehat x\cdot\sigma.
\]
Its potentially problematic eigenvalue is
\[
u(r)-v(r)
=
\frac{f(r+1)}r-\frac{I(r)}{2r^2}.
\]
For \(r\ge1\), monotonicity of \(p\) and the gamma recurrence give
\[
I(r)\le2r\,p(r-1),
\]
and
\[
\frac{p(r-1)}{p(r+1)}
=
\frac{r+3}{2}
\le r+1.
\]
Therefore \(I(r)\le2r f(r+1)\), proving that eigenvalue nonnegative. On \(0<r<1\), monotonicity of \(f\) on \([0,2]\) proves both eigenvalue inequalities.
The Fourier identity and normalization follow from the spherical wave kernel.
This example tests the mechanism without an unspecified large scale \(L\). It is not a substitute for the universal theorem, a variance optimum, or a proof of universal endpoint attainment.

8. What this contributes—and what remains unestablished
The mathematical capability obtained
The candidate theorem removes the positivity obstruction for the entire class of bounded self-adjoint tuples under a common, state-independent scalar convolution, while retaining all exponential moments.
Its conceptual contribution is not simply “add more noise.” It identifies a different positive reference operator and shows how to control the error relative to it. The matching tail obstruction explains why that operation succeeds where faster-decaying radial alternatives cannot.
This is broader than a restricted qubit certificate: the qubit calculations provide a sharp obstruction and explicit diagnostic, while the construction is uniform over arbitrary bounded tuples.
What is not being claimed as new
Generalized Wigner distributions, Weyl functional calculus, signed approximations, and positive Husimi-type representations are established prior work. The retrieved Husimi construction uses a more general representation framework; it does not by itself establish the exact state-independent scalar-convolution identity required here. arXiv
No theorem from the OpenAI mathematics repository is a premise of this proof. Its material was treated as research capital rather than independent validation, consistent with the repository’s stated variation in verification status. 
The targeted literature search did not resolve historical priority for the universal log-slow regularization theorem, the sharp tail supremum, or the reciprocal-gamma construction. A full comparison with the early Weyl-functional-calculus literature remains necessary.
The remaining consequential obstruction
A positive representation is not automatically an efficient computation.
The investigation has not established inexpensive acquisition of the POVM, efficient evaluation of its density for large operator systems, stable inversion of the added noise, or a separation from the strongest existing measurement method. Inverting the Fourier multiplier can encounter small values or zeros; positivity of the forward map does not remove that difficulty.
The next capability-level result would need to identify a substantial operator family where this measurement can actually be implemented and where its exact linear-readout structure provides an advantage after all preparation, sampling, precision, and reconstruction costs are included.
The preserved advance is therefore a universal positive-regularization operation with a sharp radial tail supremum and a dependence-based separation invisible to coordinate marginals. It is a substantive mathematical candidate—not yet a demonstrated civilisation-scale capability or a cleared claim of historical novelty.
