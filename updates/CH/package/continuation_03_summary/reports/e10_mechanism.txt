E10 mechanism scout: physical input-law importance sampling
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: exact conditional estimator theorem; candidate fails the campaign's novelty/superiority gate. No physical or hardware experiment was run. No independent validation is claimed.

Decision
========

Do not promote this as a landmark path over the main quantum-coding work. The construction has a useful interface clarification—importance-sample the controllable exogenous stress sequence while letting the actual plant supply the response—but the estimator is classical importance sampling. Existing black-box rare-event work already assumes a known, modifiable input law; robot risk-assessment work now demonstrates accelerated risk tests on physical robot hardware. The exact model-free statement below is valid, but it does not solve the expensive step: acquiring a proposal that covers every important failure mode at manageable variance and physical trial cost.

Frozen candidate and theorem
============================

The original derivation was frozen at `work/continuation_03/reports/e10_support/first_derivation.txt` before any local reports or catalogue were read. It proposed applying a change of measure to externally imposed, logged disturbance paths while evaluating the adverse event on the real device, rather than on a digital twin.

Let P be the known target law of a complete exogenous disturbance record X. Given X=x, let K(dz|x) be the unknown physical device's response law, and let F(z,x) be a fixed binary failure predicate. Put m(x)=Pr_K(F=1|x) and

    theta = integral p(x)m(x)dx > 0.

If tests draw X~Q with density q, impose that record, and the device response law remains K conditional on x, then

    theta_hat = (1/N) sum_i [p(X_i)/q(X_i)] F(Z_i,X_i)

is unbiased whenever P is absolutely continuous with respect to Q over event-relevant records. For one run,

    E_Q[Y^2] = integral p(x)^2 m(x)/q(x) dx,
    Var(theta_hat)/theta^2 = (1/N)[integral p^2 m/q / theta^2 - 1].

Cauchy-Schwarz gives the second-moment-minimizing input proposal

    q*(x) = p(x) sqrt(m(x)) / integral p(u) sqrt(m(u))du,

with minimum per-run relative variance

    (integral p sqrt(m))^2/theta^2 - 1.

For a deterministic device conditional on X, m is an indicator and this q* is the failure-conditioned input distribution, giving zero variance if it were known. For a genuinely stochastic response, that zero-variance claim is false when only X is changed. For example, if m(x)=theta everywhere, the optimal proposal is P itself and the relative variance remains 1/theta-1. This is a sharp interface limit: input tilting helps only when the controllable disturbance predicts/causes enough of the event, and proposal-learning must find those directions.

Proof: integrate over the same conditional kernel K under both target and proposal. The first moment is integral q(p/q)m=theta. Since F^2=F, the second moment is integral q(p/q)^2m=integral p^2m/q. Minimize the latter over normalized q by Cauchy-Schwarz. The proof depends critically on preserving K(dz|x): if the physical intervention changes hidden device state, unit calibration, temperature, aging, or any other conditional response mechanism beyond the logged disturbance, the weight is not the target/proposal likelihood ratio and the estimate is biased.

What is and is not acquired
===========================

The mechanism can remove the need to identify the internal plant equations: exact reweighting needs the likelihood ratio of the injected disturbance record, not the plant likelihood. It does not discover the failure surface for free. A finite pilot may miss a disconnected failure mode, a feedback controller may place too little mass on one important mode, and no finite collection of observed failures certifies proposal support by itself. A defensive mixture Q=(1-epsilon)R+epsilon P gives support and bounded weights 1/epsilon, but reverts to sample cost proportional to 1/(epsilon theta) for arbitrary black-box events; it does not yield a general sub-inverse-probability rare-event method.

If the device event contains intrinsic random failure after conditioning on X, its variance remains as shown above. If the target disturbance law P is misspecified or has drifted, the estimate is for that supplied P, not field reliability. If an intervention changes the plant's initial condition or effective dynamics, exactness fails. If F is imperfectly sensed, the sensor's false-positive/false-negative behavior must be calibrated and included in the estimand. If the plant cannot replay inputs or be reset to a repeatable state, each run's dependence and drift need a separate model; the iid variance formula cannot be used.

Closest prior art and local overlap
===================================

1. Arief et al., “Certifiable Deep Importance Sampling for Rare-Event Simulation of Black-Box Systems,” arXiv:2111.02204 (2021), is the closest algorithmic interface. It explicitly defines the black-box setting as unknown/analytically intractable system logic with a known, modifiable simulation input distribution, develops a rare-set-learning plus dominating-point mixture, and studies intelligent-driving safety testing. It also shows why a proposal that misses a failure mode can badly underestimate risk. Our q-versus-p identity is a specialization of standard IS to an unknown conditional response kernel; it does not add a new proposal-learning or coverage certificate. https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02204

2. Capito, Castillo, and Weng, “Repeatable and Reliable Efforts of Accelerated Risk Assessment in Robot Testing,” arXiv:2405.20013 (accepted at ICRA 2025), directly approaches controlled physical testing. The abstract reports accelerated instability-risk assessment under push-over disturbances on a controlled inverted pendulum and a 7-DoF planar biped (Rabbit), with formal repeatability/reliability goals. This is particularly close to the proposed “drive a real system with stressors and estimate risk” application. It addresses procedural repeatability/reliability; the abstract alone does not establish the exact same model-free conditional-kernel theorem, so the overlap should be stated as application/mechanism-level, not theorem-level. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20013

3. Capito et al., “Rethink Repeatable Measures of Robot Performance with Statistical Query,” arXiv:2505.08216 (2025; revised Oct. 21, 2025), proposes a repeatability wrapper for bounded expectation-estimation routines including Monte Carlo, importance sampling, and adaptive importance sampling, with demonstrations covering standardized manipulator tests, autonomous-vehicle risk assessment, and humanoid robot command tracking. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08216

4. Durairaj, Koren, and Krishna, “Importance Sampling to Evaluate Real-time System Reliability: A Case Study,” Simulation 76(3), 2001, studies forcing and failure-biasing IS in a simulator testbed for distributed real-time systems, validated against very long unbiased simulation. It is a simulator, not evidence of physical-hardware deployment, but shows the systems-testing application is longstanding. https://doi.org/10.1177/003754970107600304

5. The local prior report `work/reports/01_rare_events.txt` already contains adaptive importance-sampling and black-box coverage analysis. Its membership-only lower bound shows Omega(1/theta) event queries are necessary for arbitrary rare sets, even with adaptive proposal choice. My candidate does not escape that lower bound unless it acquires additional structure or a useful proposal from physical probes; any such acquisition cost must be charged.

Potential application and bound
===============================

A plausible use is tail-risk testing of a repeatable controller or device under a known environmental-load distribution: inject more stressful but absolutely continuous weather/load/impact sequences, run the physical device, then reweight outcomes to the declared operating distribution. This could avoid a plant simulator's model bias in a specific test regime. It is useful only if the plant is resettable, the actual input law is known and replayable, interventions span all relevant event modes, q has a tolerable second moment, and the expensive device trials saved exceed actuator, preparation, proposal-training, and validation costs.

The high-consequence limitation is direct: the estimator cannot validate its own target distribution, support, device stationarity, or failure detector. It is an uncertainty propagation method conditional on these inputs, not a system-safety certificate. A good effective sample size from observed weighted samples can miss an unobserved failure mode, as prior work emphasizes.

Cost and validation ledger
==========================

- Acquisition: characterize target disturbance law P; implement an actuator that samples/replays Q and logs its full path and density; calibrate sensors and event predicate; obtain access to the actual hardware; specify reset/initial-state procedure.
- Proposal training: pay for all pilot trials, model fitting, proposal normalization, retained failures, and proposal versioning. Freeze Q before the confirmatory phase; pilot data are not confirmatory validation.
- Precision: report independent weighted-trial estimates, actual second moment, ratio tails, and confidence intervals with a declared finite-sample procedure. Do not use effective sample size alone as a certificate.
- Physical preparation: time and consumables for reset, recharge, thermal stabilization, re-calibration, maintenance, drift diagnosis, and rejected runs. Establish that target and tilted input trials share K conditional on logged X.
- Memory/provenance: retain disturbance sequences or reconstructible seeds, p and q evaluations, raw device traces, event labels, timestamps, calibration and reset records, software/proposal hashes, and all pilot and held-out results.
- Validation: compare a frozen-Q estimate on an independent physical run set against a much larger direct-P reference on a bench plant with known truth; add a stochastic-response case, deliberately multimodal failure modes, a target-law baseline, actuator sham controls, drifted-state controls, and a support-hole adversary. Fail if weighted estimates miss the reference beyond the registered interval, if the variance does not deliver a material trial reduction, or if target/tilted response kernels differ after conditioning on the logged inputs.

No physical experiment, simulation study, or source-code test was run for this scout. The estimator theorem is an internal derivation, not a new result in the literature or an externally checked reliability certificate.

Recommendation to campaign lead
================================

Classify E10 as a useful standard-statistical interface with a potentially valuable hardware-in-the-loop deployment angle, but NOT a superior credible landmark path. The theorem is a direct IS calculation; the distinctive plant-agnostic claim assumes exact logged exogenous randomization and unchanged conditional device behavior; high-value rare-mode coverage remains the hard problem; and current robot-risk work already uses accelerated physical disturbance testing. Close this candidate unless the main campaign identifies a specific plant and an acquired structural signal that proves a new, much cheaper proposal-learning guarantee.
