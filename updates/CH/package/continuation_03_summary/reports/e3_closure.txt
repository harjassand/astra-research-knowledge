MAIN CONCLUSIONS

   coherent-information instability at the antidegradability boundary.
   For rho_p=(1-p)|0><0|+p|n><n|, n>=1 fixed, and any 0<eta<1, nu>0,

      Ic(rho_p,Phi_eta,nu)=-(1-tau^n)p log(1/p)+O_n(p),
      tau=eta/[1+(1-eta)nu].

   In particular it is strictly negative for all sufficiently small p.
   There is also a completely explicit finite-p upper bound in section A.

2. The large adjacent-Fock binary repetition mechanism has an exact
   asymptotic crossover that is strictly ABOVE the AD boundary:

      eta_F(nu) = sqrt(1+8nu(nu+1))/(1+sqrt(1+8nu(nu+1))).

   For eta>eta_F, sufficiently large adjacent Fock numbers followed by
   sufficiently long binary repetition have positive coherent information.
   For eta<eta_F, that same ordered asymptotic regime is negative. At equality
   the leading expansion is inconclusive. For nu=1,

      eta_F = sqrt(17)/(1+sqrt(17)) = 0.8048058983988962...,

   so this ansatz does not even improve the thermal threshold eta_G=.8.
   The tempting SLD-QFI comparison DOES cross at the exact AD boundary .75;
   it is not the entropy/repetition criterion. The environment's changing
   pure block vectors contribute twice as much to the Chernoff metric as
   the SLD comparison would require.

3. For every FIXED finite prior on n_j=N+O(sqrt(N)), the exact Fock coherent
   information converges to a fully explicit ccq experiment. This statement
   has a finite-label entropy proof and explicit Chernoff tail bounds;
   no subtraction of separately divergent bosonic entropies is needed.
   The transfer is detailed in section D. A positive finite-prior value in
   the limit would therefore imply an actual finite-energy code. No prior

4. The central obligation is to exhibit and rigorously lower-bound such a

CONVENTIONS AND EXACT CHANNEL FORMULAS

Use natural logarithms throughout; divide Ic by log 2 for qubits. Set

   b=1-eta, s=2nu+1, G=1+b nu, tau=eta/G, a=1-tau=b(nu+1)/G.

The exact factorization is Phi=A_G o L_tau, with pure vacuum ancillas in
both Stinespring factors. Let l be the pure-loss environment photon count,
k the amplifier environment count, and delta=k-l. On input |n>, the
receiver photon number is m=n+delta. Define

 P_n(l,delta) = binom(n,l)a^l tau^(n-l)
                binom(n+delta,delta+l)(G-1)^(delta+l)/G^(n+delta+1),

with 0<=l<=n and delta+l>=0; it is zero otherwise. Then

   v_delta^(n) = sum_l sqrt(P_n(l,delta)) |l,delta+l>,
   b_delta^(n) = ||v_delta^(n)||^2,
   Phi^c(|n><n|) = direct_sum_delta |v_delta^(n)><v_delta^(n)|,
   Phi(|n><n|) = sum_m b_(m-n)^(n) |m><m|.

The complement blocks are orthogonal, and EACH occupied block is pure.
All amplitudes in this representation are positive real.

An elementary probabilistic representation is useful for both limits and
tails. Let (L_i,K_i), i=1,...,n, be iid, where

   (L_i,K_i)=(1,0) with probability a;
   (L_i,K_i)=(0,k) with probability tau (G-1)^k/G^(k+1), k>=0.

Add an independent geometric K_0 with the latter geometric law. Then

   (l,k) = sum_i (L_i,K_i)+(0,K_0),
   delta=k-l,       m=n+delta.

The joint lattice law is aperiodic, has positive-definite covariance for
nu>0, and has exponential moments in an open neighborhood of zero.
Those properties are the exact hypotheses used in the local limit step.

A. RARE VACUUM/FIXED-FOCK PERTURBATION: COMPLETE NEGATIVE RESULT

For vacuum, the receiver is thermal with mean G-1, with probabilities

   a_j=(G-1)^j/G^(j+1), j>=0.

Its complementary support is P=|0><0|_loss tensor I_amp. On input |n>,

   Tr[P Phi^c(|n><n|)]=tau^n,
   kappa_n=Tr[(I-P)Phi^c(|n><n|)]=1-tau^n>0.

For ANY pure-state binary ensemble, Ic=chi_B-chi_E, since the two
conditional output entropies coincide. Here

   chi_B <= p D(B_n||B_0)
          <= p { log G + (eta n+G-1) log[G/(G-1)] } = p C_n.

The first inequality is the exact relative-entropy identity
chi_B=pD(B_n||B_0)-D((1-p)B_0+pB_n||B_0). The second drops S(B_n)>=0.
Measuring {P,I-P} on E gives a binary Z-channel, and consequently

   chi_E >= h(p kappa_n)-p h(kappa_n),
   Ic <= p C_n - h(p kappa_n)+p h(kappa_n).                 (A1)

Here h is binary entropy in nats. This is an explicit bound with no cutoff
or unproved entropy interchange. For example, h(x)>=x log(1/x) gives
the sufficient condition for strict NEGATIVITY

   log[1/(p kappa_n)] > [C_n+h(kappa_n)]/kappa_n.

The coefficient of p log(1/p) is exactly -kappa_n. To see the matching
expansion, in each delta=j>=0 block define

   c_j=tau^n binom(n+j,n)(G-1)^j/G^(n+j+1),
   d_j=b_j^(n)-c_j,

and for -n<=j<0 put c_j=a_j=0, d_j=b_j^(n). The two eigenvalues for j>=0 are

 lambda_(j,+/-) = { (1-p)a_j+p b_j^(n)
             +/- sqrt[((1-p)a_j+p b_j^(n))^2
                         -4p(1-p)a_j d_j] }/2.             (A2)

For j<0 there is the single eigenvalue p d_j. Sum_j d_j=kappa_n. Thus
lambda_(j,-)=p d_j+O(p^2 b_j^2/a_j) and
lambda_(j,+)=a_j+p(c_j-a_j)+O(p^2 b_j^2/a_j).

All ratios b_j/a_j are polynomials in j of bounded degree n, times fixed
constants, and all a_j have geometric tails. With
J=ceil[3 log(1/p)/log(G/(G-1))], Taylor expansion on j<=J and the tail
bounds in section E justify termwise entropy expansion. One safe remainder
is O_n(p^2(1+|log p|)^(2n+3)). Explicitly,

 Ic = -kappa_n p log(1/p)+p A_n+O_n(p^2(1+|log p|)^(2n+3)),

 A_n = [eta n+(G-1)(1-tau^n(n+1))] log[G/(G-1)]
          -kappa_n(1-log G)+sum_(j=-n)^infinity d_j log d_j. (A3)

This result has n FIXED as p tends to zero. It does not exclude a joint
n(p)->infinity limit, varying base pure states, or more general block codes.

B. ADJACENT FOCK CHERNOFF EXPANSION: EXACT FACTOR OF TWO

On the common support l<=N, the exact adjacent likelihood ratio is

 R_N(l,delta)=P_(N+1)(l,delta)/P_N(l,delta)
       =(tau/G)(N+1)(N+1+delta)/(N+1-l)^2.                (B1)

The new all-loss endpoint l=N+1 has total probability a^(N+1), hence is
exponentially negligible in the large-N metric calculation.

Let L=(l-aN)/sqrt(N), D=(delta+bN)/sqrt(N). The limiting covariance is

 Sigma = [ a tau,         -eta a             ;
           -eta a,        eta b s            ].          (B2)

The likelihood expansion on any fixed central region is

 R_N=1+[2L/tau+D/eta]/sqrt(N)+O(N^-1(1+|L|+|D|)^2).

The joint score variance is therefore

   F_joint=Var(2L/tau+D/eta)=b s/eta.

Gaussian regression (or the conditional local limit theorem for the exact
noncentral-hypergeometric conditional law) gives

   E[L|D] -> -a D/(b s),
   E[2L/tau+D/eta|D] -> -D/(eta s),
   F_classE=b/(eta s).

For the receiver, comparison at the SAME receiver number m gives the exact
joint ratio

 P_(N+1)(l,delta-1)/P_N(l,delta)
     =tau(N+1)(delta+l)/[(G-1)(N+1-l)^2].                 (B3)

Its conditional score at fixed delta tends to D/(b s), so

   F_B=eta/(b s).

These score limits hold in L^2: truncate to |L|,|D|<=T, use the local limit
theorem, then remove T using the explicit exponential moments below. One
can instead use T=O(sqrt(log N)) and the analytic-mgf local expansion to
obtain O(N^-3/2 poly(log N)) remainders in the overlaps. No numerical fit
is used to determine the coefficients.

Now let t_delta=sum_l sqrt(P_N P_(N+1)) and

   r_delta=t_delta^2/(b_delta^(N)b_delta^(N+1)).

This is the SQUARED overlap of the two pure environment block states.
The exact environment Chernoff expression is

 Q_E,N(z)=sum_delta (b_delta^(N))^z(b_delta^(N+1))^(1-z) r_delta,
                                                        0<z<1. (B4)

For the conditional expectation under P_N(l|delta), ignoring only the
exponentially small new endpoint,

 r_delta = E[sqrt(R_N)|delta]^2 / E[R_N|delta].

Taylor expansion gives

   1-r_delta = Var(R_N|delta)/4+o(N^-1),
   sum_delta b_delta^(N)(1-r_delta)
        = [F_joint-F_classE]/(4N)+o(N^-1).               (B5)

The receiver outputs commute, so their expression is the ordinary
classical Chernoff expression. Combining the conditional variance term
with the marginal likelihood expansion, uniformly in z in [0,1] up to
the exponentially small support endpoint,

 Q_B,N(z)=1-z(1-z) F_B/(2N)+o(N^-1),
 Q_E,N(z)=1-{z(1-z)F_classE/2
                     +(F_joint-F_classE)/4}/N+o(N^-1).  (B6)

The maximizing exponent has z->1/2. Consequently,

 C_B,N = -log inf_z Q_B,N(z)
       = eta/[8 b s N]+o(N^-1),

 C_E,N = -log inf_z Q_E,N(z)
       = b[1+8nu(nu+1)]/[8 eta s N]+o(N^-1).             (B7)

The SLD-QFI of the environment would instead have coefficient
F_joint/N=b s/(eta N), which crosses F_B/N exactly at eta=b s, i.e.
eta_AD=(2nu+1)/(2nu+2). In (B7), the changing PURE block eigenvectors
give (F_joint-F_classE)/4 rather than /8. Replacing that factor would
incorrectly claim positivity all the way to AD.

C. FROM CHERNOFF OVERLAPS TO COHERENT-INFORMATION SIGN, WITHOUT AN
INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL QUANTUM CHERNOFF ASSUMPTION

Consider the actual rank-two m-mode input

 rho_(N,m)=p |N>^(tensor m)<N|^(tensor m)
             +(1-p)|N+1>^(tensor m)<N+1|^(tensor m),

with 0<p<1 fixed. The conditional codewords are pure and orthogonal, so

   Ic=H(X|E^m)-H(X|B^m).

For E, block labels are delta_1,...,delta_m. In a block write

 A=p product_i b_(delta_i)^(N),
 B=(1-p) product_i b_(delta_i)^(N+1),
 R=product_i r_(delta_i).

The two conditional vectors are pure. Its Helstrom error is exactly

   Pe_block=(A+B-sqrt((A+B)^2-4AB R))/2,

and obeys

   (1/2)min(A,B)R <= Pe_block <= min(A,B)R.               (C1)

Define subprobabilities alpha_delta=b_delta^(N) r_delta and
beta_delta=b_delta^(N+1) r_delta, and let

   S_m=sum_(delta_1,...,delta_m)
                  min(p alpha^tensor m,(1-p)beta^tensor m).

Then S_m/2<=Pe_E,m<=S_m. The conditional entropy also has the direct
bound H(X|E^m)>=S_m in NATS. Here is a short proof: for a normalized
binary pure ensemble with prior t<=1/2 and squared overlap R, its
conditional entropy is

 f_t(R)=h(t)-h((1-sqrt((1-2t)^2+4t(1-t)R))/2).

The derivative is

 f_t'(R)=t(1-t)/d log[(1+d)/(1-d)] >=2t(1-t),
 d=sqrt((1-2t)^2+4t(1-t)R).

Thus (A+B)f_(A/(A+B))(R)>=2AB R/(A+B)>=min(A,B)R.
Fano's inequality after an optimal measurement gives the upper bound
H(X|E^m)<=h(Pe_E,m)<=h(S_m) for sufficiently large m.
Both bounds therefore have the same exponential rate as S_m.

The countable classical subprobability Chernoff formula gives

   lim_(m->infinity) -m^-1 log S_m
       =-log inf_(0<=z<=1) sum_delta alpha_delta^z beta_delta^(1-z)
       =C_E,N.                                          (C2)

For completeness, this does not need a finite-alphabet assumption: under
the tilted probability proportional to alpha^z beta^(1-z), the logarithm
of alpha/beta has finite moments here (geometric tails and polynomial
likelihood ratios). At an interior minimizer its mean is zero. The event
that its m-fold sum has absolute value at most O(sqrt(m)) has nonexponential
probability, giving a lower bound Q(z)^m exp[-O(sqrt(m))]. The Chernoff
inequality is the matching upper bound. The minimizer is interior for all
sufficiently large N by (B6).

The same classical argument and binary posterior-entropy bounds give

   lim_(m->infinity) -m^-1 log H(X|B^m)=C_B,N.

Thus, for each fixed sufficiently large N, C_B,N>C_E,N makes Ic strictly
POSITIVE for all sufficiently large m, and the reverse inequality makes
it strictly NEGATIVE for all sufficiently large m. Inserting (B7) proves
the crossover eta_F stated above.

ORDER OF LIMITS MATTERS. This result first chooses large N, then takes
m sufficiently large for that N. It does not rule out a different joint
N,m scaling, finite m, priors exponentially varying with m, superpositions,
or arbitrary quantum codes. It does decisively rule out the proposed
fixed-prior binary repetition route in the stated regime near eta=.75.

D. FINITE-PRIOR LOCAL FOCK EXPERIMENT AND A RIGOROUS TRANSFER

Fix K<infinity, probabilities pi_j>0, and real u_j. Choose integer

   n_j(N)=N+floor[u_j sqrt(eta s N/b)].

For fixed u_j these are nonnegative for all sufficiently large N. The
actual input is the diagonal finite-rank state sum_j pi_j |n_j><n_j|.

For the receiver use Y_N=(B-eta N)/sqrt(eta b s N). For the environment
use Z_N=-(delta+b N)/sqrt(eta b s N). Constants G-1 in the centers vanish
in this scaling. The local limit theorem gives the limiting experiments

   Y=r U+N_Y,       Z=U+N_Z,        r=eta/b,

where N_Y,N_Z are standard normal marginal noises. They are separate
experiments; no joint independence assertion about receiver/E is needed.

The environment also retains pure block states. The conditional law of
l at fixed central delta has asymptotic variance

   Var(l|delta)=eta b nu(nu+1)N/[G^2 s]+o(N),

and changing n at fixed delta shifts its conditional mean at rate

   d E[l|delta]/dn =2a nu/s+o(1).

Taking square roots of these conditional Gaussian laws yields the limiting
normalized block-vector overlaps

   <phi_(u_i)|phi_(u_j)>
       =exp[-nu(nu+1)(u_i-u_j)^2/2].                     (D1)

The overlap is real positive and independent of the limiting central z.
This follows directly from the exact positive amplitudes, not from a
claim about the Fisher metric. Conditional local-limit convergence is
uniform on compact z and finite sets of u_j. The exponential-moment tail
bounds below remove the compact truncation.

There is an exact finite-label entropy identity before taking the limit.
For each delta set

 W_delta=sum_j pi_j b_delta^(n_j),
 Gamma_delta(i,j)=sqrt(pi_i pi_j)
                         sum_l sqrt(P_(n_i)(l,delta)P_(n_j)(l,delta)).

The nonzero eigenvalues of the complementary mixture in the delta block
are exactly those of the K by K matrix Gamma_delta. Therefore

 Ic_N=H(J|delta)-H(J|B)
                  -sum_delta W_delta S(Gamma_delta/W_delta).   (D2)

Every integrand here lies between 0 and log K. In particular, omitted
environment blocks of total probability epsilon_E alter the two
environment terms by at most 2epsilon_E log K, and omitted receiver
outcomes of probability epsilon_B alter its term by at most
epsilon_B log K. This is a quantified ENTROPY tail bound, independent of
the diverging bosonic output entropy.

For a central finite z interval, the classical local limit theorem and
the Gram-overlap limit imply entrywise convergence of all K by K Gram
matrices. Finite-dimensional entropy continuity then gives convergence of
the integrals/Riemann sums in (D2). Let the interval grow and use the
preceding entropy bound together with section E. Hence

 lim_(N->infinity) Ic_N = F(pi,u;eta,nu),

 F=I(U;rU+N)-I(U;U+N)
          -E_Z S( sum_j P(J=j|Z)|phi_(u_j)><phi_(u_j)| ). (D3)

Equivalently,

 F=H(J|Z)-H(J|Y)-E_Z S(G_Z),
 (G_Z)_(ij)=sqrt(P(i|Z)P(j|Z))
                           exp[-nu(nu+1)(u_i-u_j)^2/2].  (D4)

For FIXED finite prior, a rigorously established strict F>0 automatically
transfers to some finite N and positive coherent information. The theorem
is not uniform over K->infinity, varying priors, vanishing gaps, or true
superpositions. Those would require additional estimates.

Useful consistency checks:

* The phi_u may be represented as coherent states |c u>, c^2=nu(nu+1).
  Homodyne gives W=2cU+N. Combining W with Z gives
  sqrt(1+4c^2)U+N=(2nu+1)U+N. Therefore the limiting Eve experiment can
  simulate Bob whenever r<=2nu+1, exactly the AD boundary, for EVERY prior.

* For a Gaussian prior of variance V, the functional is

  F_G(V)=0.5 log[(1+r^2 V)/(1+V)]
          -g((sqrt[1+4nu(nu+1)V/(1+V)]-1)/2),

  where g(x)=(x+1)log(x+1)-x log x. The infinite-V limit is log r-g(nu),
  the thermal hashing expression. This Gaussian-prior identity is a check
  on the limiting experiment, not a proof of a non-Gaussian optimizer.

The unresolved issue above AD is the extra conditional Holevo information
in the pure states phi_u beyond their homodyne information. Merely having
a larger receiver SLD-QFI does not bound that extra entropy.

E. EXPLICIT PROBABILITY AND ENTROPY TAIL CONTROLS

For z=e^t with z<G/(G-1), define D(t)=G-(G-1)e^t. Exact mgfs are

 M_B,n(t)=D(t)^(-1)[a+tau e^t/D(t)]^n,
 M_delta,n(t)=D(t)^(-1)[a e^(-t)+tau/D(t)]^n,
 M_l,k,n(u,t)=D(t)^(-1)[a e^u+tau/D(t)]^n.                (E1)

For any scalar variable X among these and any admissible t>0,

 Pr[X>=x] <= exp[-tx] M_X(t).

For t<0 the same formula bounds Pr[X<=x]. Thus each cutoff probability is
explicitly bounded by a one-dimensional convex minimization; the lost
count l is exactly Bin(n,a), with mgf [1-a+a e^t]^n.

For central thresholds mean +/- T sqrt(N), these formulas give tails
<=C exp(-c T^2) for T=o(sqrt(N)), with C,c depending only on eta,nu and a
fixed finite prior range. Choosing T=O(sqrt(log N)) gives arbitrarily
high polynomial decay by increasing the constant. The exact formulas
(E1), rather than unspecified constants, are available for any actual N.

For a nonnegative integer X with tail mass epsilon and tail first moment
e=E[X 1_(X>M)], the Shannon tail contribution obeys

 sum_(x>M) -p_x log p_x <= epsilon g(e/epsilon)-epsilon log epsilon. (E2)

This follows by normalizing the tail and using maximum entropy of a
geometric distribution at fixed mean. Valid explicit inputs are
epsilon<=e^(-tM)M_X(t) and e<=e^(-tM)M_X'(t), with the upper bounds inserted
only through a monotone enlarged admissible pair or evaluated as a
variational maximum. For complement blocks in (A2), there are at most
two eigenvalues per delta, adding at most epsilon log 2 to the block-label
tail entropy. For (D2) the stronger finite-label bound avoids (E2) entirely.

CENTRAL REMAINING OBLIGATION

To prove the exact positivity boundary, establish a strict positive lower
bound for an explicit family for every eta>(nu+1/2)/(nu+1), with all entropy
tails controlled. Within the local Fock mechanism one concrete version is:

   For every r>2nu+1, find a fixed finite prior (pi,u) with F(pi,u)>0,
   then rigorously bound the one-dimensional integrals and K by K Gram
   entropies in (D4), and invoke the proven transfer above.

No such all-r prior construction or converse was proved. True Fock
superpositions may offer a different mechanism, but their off-diagonal
environment blocks require a new local limit/entropy transfer; they are
not covered by (D2)-(D4).
