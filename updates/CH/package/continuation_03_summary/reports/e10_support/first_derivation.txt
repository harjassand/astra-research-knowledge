Candidate mechanism: input-law-only importance sampling on a live black-box plant

Core idea

Use controllable, logged exogenous disturbances to force a real physical or hardware-in-the-loop system through inputs that are much more likely to cause a target failure than under ordinary operation. Reweight each observed outcome by the exact likelihood ratio of the ordinary disturbance law to the injected law. This computes a rare-event probability for the actual device without a simulator or a fitted model of its internal dynamics. The plant may be nonlinear, high-dimensional, hybrid, partially observed, and otherwise unknown; only the imposed disturbance distribution and its likelihood ratio need be known.

Target and protocol

Let X be the complete exogenous disturbance record for one run, with known target law P (ordinary operating conditions). Given X=x, let K(dz|x) be the unknown physical plant's conditional law over observed trajectory z. Let F(z,x) in {0,1} be a pre-registered adverse event. The target is

    theta = E_{X~P, Z~K(.|X)}[F(Z,X)].

In a test facility, draw and impose X from a proposal law Q, log the realized record and density ratio p(X)/q(X), run the actual plant, and record F. For independent runs, define

    theta_hat = (1/N) sum_i [p(X_i)/q(X_i)] F_i,   X_i~Q, Z_i~K(.|X_i).

Require P << Q on every disturbance record that can contribute to F. The plant is not reset to a modeled state: every preparation/reset, calibration, input-replay error, and device run is part of cost and validity accounting. If the device has independent intrinsic randomness, it remains under K and need not be modeled for unbiasedness.

Positive capability target

For failure events so rare that direct testing sees no failures, change only the known exogenous input law, not the device's internal law, and estimate the ordinary-operation failure rate from stress-biased physical trials. Aim to reduce trials by concentrating Q near disturbance records that actually trigger failure. A second intended use is to amortize one set of physical runs over a declared family of failure predicates F_j on the same observed trajectory; every estimate gets its own event-conditioned input law and variance audit.

Theorem candidate (exactness and the right metric)

Let m(x)=Pr_K(F=1|X=x), theta=E_P[m(X)]>0, and let R_F be the target-law distribution of disturbance records conditioned on failure, with density r_F(x)=p(x)m(x)/theta. If P<<Q and the likelihood ratio is evaluated exactly, then E_Q[theta_hat]=theta and

    Var(theta_hat)/theta^2 = (1/N) ( integral r_F(x)^2/q(x) dx - 1 )
                           = (1/N) chi^2(R_F || Q).

Thus the ideal proposal q*(x)=r_F(x), if available, gives zero variance; the practical objective is to physically learn a proposal whose failure-conditioned input mismatch is small. This theorem is model-free with respect to K. It does not say the proposal can be found cheaply, that support can be certified from finite data, or that a rare event has become easy under arbitrary physical constraints.

Proof sketch to complete later

Condition on X and integrate Z under the same K used by the target. E_Q[(p/q)F]=integral q(x)(p/q)m(x)dx=theta. For one run, E_Q[((p/q)F)^2]=integral p(x)^2 m(x)/q(x)dx. Since m^2 in r_F^2 is absent, this expression is not equal to integral r_F^2/q unless F is deterministic conditional on X. The exact general relative variance is

    (1/N) [ integral p(x)^2 m(x)/q(x) dx / theta^2 - 1 ].

If the full run outcome is included in a proposal that changes only X and leaves K invariant, conditional event randomness remains. In the special deterministic-plant case m(x) in {0,1}, this reduces to chi^2(R_F||Q)/N. This correction is part of the frozen derivation; do not silently claim zero variance for stochastic plants. For stochastic K, the unconstrained q minimizing the second moment is proportional to p(x)*sqrt(m(x)), with minimum second moment (integral p sqrt(m))^2; zero variance over full runs would require changing the conditional physical outcome law too, which this mechanism does not do.

Exact proof outline

For one sample Y=(p(X)/q(X))F, integrate q(x)K(dz|x). Its first moment equals theta and its second moment equals integral p^2 m/q. Independent runs give Var(mean)=Var(Y)/N. The minimizing proposal follows Cauchy-Schwarz applied to integral p^2 m/q with integral q=1. All claims require finite second moment. If ratios are clipped, the estimate is generally biased; report clipping only as a diagnostic, never as the certified estimate.

Adaptive variant to investigate after freeze

During a pilot, use observed prefixes and adverse outcomes to learn a proposal. Freeze its update rule and reserve an independent validation phase. Alternatively, allow a predictable proposal q_t(X_t|history) at each time and log the product likelihood ratio. The physical plant's response kernel cancels from the Radon-Nikodym derivative only if the imposed policy does not alter hidden plant dynamics except through the logged input channel. Adaptive use would need a martingale proof, support conditions, bounded weights or an explicit sequential confidence procedure, and drift controls. Do not assume the simple iid variance formula applies to adaptive runs.

Required cost ledger

1. Acquisition: ordinary input law P, generator/actuator interface, exact density evaluation, device access, event instrumentation, baseline runs, event-definition agreement, reset and inter-run stabilization, and input replay verification.
2. Proposal training: pilot physical runs, failed-run retention, optimizer/compute, proposal versioning, held-out runs, and any human design effort. Charge this separately; do not count pilot failures as validation.
3. Precision: estimate actual second moment and effective sample size from independent confirmatory trials; confidence intervals must account for weighted Bernoulli outcomes and any sequential stopping. No accuracy claim from effective-sample-size heuristics alone.
4. Physical preparation: per-run reset, calibration, thermal/charge/aging state, actuator fidelity, sensor latency, wear, and failed reset. The system must be repeatable enough that target and proposal runs share K conditional on X.
5. Memory/storage: full stimulus paths or cryptographic identifiers plus reconstructible seed, exact target/proposal densities, raw response traces, event labels, timestamps, calibration snapshots, model/proposal hash, and run history. Storing only weights or summary features can make results unauditable.
6. Validation: calibration of P and q, independent confirmation, matched target-law baseline, sham/actuator controls, a known rare-event bench system, drift/staleness checks, and an adversarial proposal with deliberate support gaps to test whether diagnostics detect failure.

Decisive initial falsifier

Use a small black-box stochastic plant with a known but hidden-to-the-proposal response law: e.g. a programmable RC/thermal bench plus a randomized disturbance waveform and a threshold event. Pre-register P and event F. Train Q only on pilot trials, freeze it, and compare independent weighted-Q estimates with a large direct-P benchmark whose uncertainty is much smaller than the claimed gain. Falsify the capability claim if the weighted estimate misses the direct reference outside the pre-registered interval, if device responses drift between P and Q conditions after conditioning on logged inputs, if the measured second moment predicts no material run reduction, or if rare event modes appear outside Q's support. Include a stochastic plant so that the tempting deterministic chi-square zero-variance claim is explicitly rejected.

Landmark hypothesis and uncertainty

Potential landmark is a no-digital-twin path to tail-risk measurement on costly, nonlinear black-box devices, using the device as the response oracle and randomized external stressors as the only modeled random variable. The likely weak point is novelty: input importance sampling, physical stress testing, hardware-in-the-loop validation, and adaptive rare-event sampling may already contain this combination. The likely feasibility limits are actuator reachability, repeatability, weight degeneracy, support discovery, and unknown/stale operating disturbance laws. Treat as a candidate mechanism, not a validated invention, until primary prior art and a falsifying bench construction are checked.
