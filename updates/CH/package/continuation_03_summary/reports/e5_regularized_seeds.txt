1. FIXED FAMILIES, NOT A BLIND OPTIMIZATION

Intended infinite seed:
  psi_n=sqrt(1-q) q^(n/2) exp[i f(n)], q=13/20.

Three predetermined phase profiles:
  geometric_positive:    f(n)=0
  irrational_quadratic:  f(n)=pi sqrt(2) n^2/8
  irrational_cubic:      f(n)=pi sqrt(3) n^3/128.

For each profile, use either
  phi_n=exp[i pi n/3] psi_n,
or
  phi_n=(n+1) psi_n/sqrt(Z), Z=(1+q)/(1-q)^2.

Every intended coefficient is nonzero. This statement concerns the defined
infinite vectors, not the evaluated finite approximation. The two states are
not orthogonal. Their squared overlaps are 49/309 (phase partner) and 20/33
(number partner), independent of the common profile. The code uses the valid
purification sqrt(1-p)|0>psi+sqrt(p)|1>phi without orthogonalizing the states.
The input entropy is measured from the reference Gram matrix, not replaced
by h_2(p).

Tested p={.01,.001,1e-5,1e-7}, eta={.75,.7501,.76,.78,.8}, nu=1, input
cutoffs N={32,64,96}, output cutoffs M=N+16. This gives 360 fixed trials.
No angles or weights were adapted to the results. A single best-family
refinement uses N128/M144 at eta=.7501 and .8, the same four weights.

2. INPUT AND OUTPUT TAILS ARE CHARGED SEPARATELY

Each finite input is the directly prepared, separately normalized projection
of its intended seed onto 0,...,N. It is a legitimate finite-support input,
regardless of whether it closely approximates the infinite seed. A positive
certified finite result would therefore stand independently of an
infinite-support interpretation. No positive result occurred.

Closed-form intended input tails, with L=N+1:
  t_psi=q^L,
  t_phi=q^L[(L+1)^2(1-q)^2+2(L+1)q(1-q)+q(1+q)]/(1+q)
         for the number-weighted partner,
  t_phi=t_psi for the phase partner.

These probabilities are computed as exact rational numbers and rounded
outward for their displayed float upper bounds. Let
  t_mix=(1-p)t_psi+p t_phi.
The half trace distance between the ideal infinite purification and the
branch-normalized projected purification is at most sqrt(2 t_mix).
For reference dimension 2, the conditional-entropy continuity charge is
  f(delta)=2 delta+(1+delta)h_2(delta/(1+delta)).
The displayed square roots/entropy charges are floating-point diagnostics of
this mathematical bound, not directed-rounding certificates. They exclude
additional coefficient-evaluation or hardware preparation error.

At N96 the input charge is approximately 3.95e-8 bits for p=1e-7. At the
N128 refinement it is 5.2145e-11 bits, with raw tails
  t_psi=7.3421458418e-25,
  t_number=9.4801453376e-22.

The output channel is the original physical cutoff-erasure evaluator. It
uses finite retained Kraus sums and a reference erasure matrix; it does not
truncate the thermal environment or renormalize retained output. Every
erasure entropy and all off-diagonal reference entries are included. Finite
output failure probabilities are recorded per trial. The intended infinite
output-tail diagnostic upper bound adds t_mix to the finite tail; this follows
from contraction of the classical input photon-number distributions.

If the finite states were instead obtained by heralded projections of the
infinite sources, each branch would succeed with probability 1-t_branch,
and mixing weights would generally change after conditioning. No free
heralded preparation, branch rebalancing, or source-projection rate is claimed.
Reported finite rates assume direct ideal preparation and include every
physical channel use and output failure.

5. INTERPRETATION AND REPLAY

Negative physical-cutoff results are lower bounds on unprocessed coherent
information. They do not give a capacity upper bound. The finite-support
tests do not establish or disprove faithfulness of the infinite complementary
state. Exchanging the N->infinity and p->0 limits remains unjustified.
Analytic work must establish the relevant complementary support and control
relative entropy/continuity in those limits. This experiment only checks
whether these fixed seeds provide an immediately visible finite witness.
