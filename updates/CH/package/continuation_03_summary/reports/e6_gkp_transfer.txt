E6 — Finite-energy GKP transfer for a fixed positive coherent-information margin

Scope and status

This note isolates the operational bridge for E2's fixed finite-block index-2 GKP lattice. I use E2's conventions: [q_j,p_l]=iδ_jl; the encoded logical dimension is d=2; the block has K physical modes; and the ideal decoder retains the full analog stabilizer syndrome before logical Pauli recovery. E2's displayed positive lower bound is for the ideal Gaussian-random-displacement model. No finite-envelope or actual thermal-attenuator positivity claim is established here. E2 reports no certified positive margin near the AD point at present.

The useful conclusion is conditional but quantitative: once a valid finite-energy probe has a positive decoded coherent-information margin, a qubit reference gives a dimension-independent continuity estimate, and both finite Fock support and finite-resolution homodyne can be charged explicitly. What is still missing is a justified bound connecting the ideal-lattice score to the output of the actual thermal channel on the chosen finite-energy envelope.

1. The fixed-reference continuity gate

Let R be a qubit purifying the (rank-at-most-two) physical input state, let B be the channel output (possibly infinite-dimensional), and let D:B→Q be any deterministic CPTP decoder to a qubit. Write ω_RQ=(id_R⊗D∘Φ)(ρ_RA). Then

  Ic(ρ_A, Φ) = -S(R|B)_(id⊗Φ)(ρ) ≥ -S(R|Q)_ω = Ic(ρ_A,D∘Φ).

The inequality is data processing / strong subadditivity: processing the receiver's system cannot increase the quantum information conditioned on the reference. Thus a positive coherent information after an explicitly specified deterministic decoder is already a valid lower bound for the physical channel. This avoids conditioning on a heralded success branch; if a proposed decoder postselects, its failure probability and all-use rate must be charged separately.

For any two decoded states ω_RQ and ω*_RQ with T=(1/2)||ω−ω*||_1≤1/2, Alicki–Fannes gives the safe bound

  |S(R|Q)_ω−S(R|Q)_ω*| ≤ F_2(T) := 4T + 2 h_2(T),

where entropies are in bits. A sharper Winter bound may replace F_2 by 2T+(1+T)h_2(T/(1+T)); either way the dependence is only on dim(R)=2, not on the bosonic output dimension. Hence if the target decoded state has margin μ=−S(R|Q)_ω*>0, any verified trace-distance bound T<t_*(μ), where t_*(μ)=sup{t≤1/2:F_2(t)<μ}, proves Ic(ρ_A,D∘Φ)>0. The same statement applies to the decoded output of an actual thermal channel: the bosonic output dimension is not the obstruction.

The premise is a trace-distance estimate between two legitimate RQ states. An ideal GKP codeword is a distribution, not a density operator, so it cannot be inserted into this continuity theorem by asserting that a finite-energy state is “close” to it in trace norm. The ideal logical output may be defined algebraically in the SSS model, but a quantitative bridge from the physical output to that logical state must still be proved.

2. A finite-energy approximate code exists at each fixed envelope width

The SSS decomposition is a unitary factorization of the physical oscillator Hilbert space into a finite logical subsystem and a stabilizer/syndrome subsystem. Its partial trace over the syndrome is the ideal decoding CPTP map. For E2's fixed finite-block lattice Λ_n (logical dimension 2), choose a normalized smooth compactly supported syndrome wavefunction φ_s on its fundamental cell, supported in a neighborhood of the origin of width s, and form

  |Ω_s>_RA = (id_R⊗U^†_{Λ_n})(|Φ_2>_RL ⊗ |φ_s>_S).

Here |Φ_2> is a maximally entangled qubit pair. This is a normalizable physical state for every s>0; the two logical basis states are orthogonal because the SSS map is unitary. A smooth bump, rather than a hard cutoff of a Gaussian envelope at the syndrome-cell boundary, avoids the discontinuity that gives infinite oscillator energy.

Matsuura–Menicucci–Yamasaki give an explicit single-mode construction: choose f^s(x)=exp[-1/(1-(x/s)^2)] for |x|<s and zero otherwise, and use the product f^s(z_1)f^s(z_2) as the syndrome wavefunction. Their inverse-SSS wavefunction contains the Fourier transform f̃^s; its tail decays faster than any polynomial, so both quadrature second moments and mean photon number are finite for every fixed s>0. For E2's fixed multimode lattice, this construction transfers directly if its SSS coordinate map is a fixed metaplectic transformation; if it uses an additional cell/dimension map, one must verify that the pulled-back bump lies in the oscillator energy form domain. The corresponding finite energy is exactly the oscillator quadratic form

  E_s = Σ_{j=1}^K ( <q_j^2+p_j^2>_{Ω_s} − 1 )/2 < ∞.

A scaling estimate for a dilated smooth bump is E_s≤C_Λ(1+s^{-2}) for 0<s≤s_0, with C_Λ depending on the fixed lattice/block and bump. For the square-code formula, f_s(x)=f_1(x/s) gives f̃_s(k)=s f̃_1(sk); smooth compact support makes f̃_1 Schwartz, and the grid envelope spans O(1/s) cells. Bounding the weighted lattice sums by Riemann sums gives O(s^{-2}) for each quadrature second moment. A fixed finite symplectic change of lattice coordinates changes only the constant. The 2026 paper establishes finiteness for its bump state but does not tabulate this asymptotic constant; an instantiation must compute or bound C_Λ for E2's particular Λ_n. This is a true finite-energy SSS envelope construction, not a replacement by random displacement noise.

3. Explicit finite-support and analog-syndrome costs, conditional on a positive finite-envelope score

For the pure probe |Ω_s>, let E_s be its total mean photon number over K modes. Project the physical input onto total photon number ≤M and normalize, obtaining |Ω_{s,M}>. Markov's inequality gives tail δ_M≤E_s/(M+1), and pure-state trace distance is exactly sqrt(δ_M). Contractivity through any channel and decoder therefore gives

  T(ω_{s,M},ω_s) ≤ sqrt(E_s/(M+1)).

So if the untruncated finite-envelope decoded coherent information is μ_s>0, choose ε_cut<t_*(μ_s) and M+1>E_s/ε_cut^2. Then the finite-Fock-support input still has positive coherent information. This supplies an explicit finite input-energy/support cost without requiring a nonorthogonal Gaussian-envelope codeword basis. The normalized truncated probe is a valid rank-at-most-two input; one may prepare it directly. If instead implemented by probabilistic filtering, its success/failure overhead must be included.

For finite-resolution analog syndrome extraction, suppose the fixed SSS-to-homodyne transformation has been specified and the measured modes have per-mode mean energies E_j^meas. A binning error of at most b in each q,p correction can be represented as residual displacement. For a reference-entangled state of energy E_j^meas,

  T(ρ,D(δ_q,δ_p)ρD†) ≤ (|δ_q|+|δ_p|) sqrt(2E_j^meas+1).

This follows by integrating the commutator with G=δ_p q−δ_q p and using <q^2>,<p^2>≤2E_j^meas+1. Thus for K modes with |δ_q|,|δ_p|≤b,

  ε_bin ≤ 2b Σ_j sqrt(2E_j^meas+1)
         ≤ 2b sqrt(K(2E_meas,total+K)).

This controls the residual-displacement part of the finite-precision correction, including an arbitrary reference. A decoder whose Pauli-frame rule is discontinuous at a logical-cell boundary also needs a boundary-strip term unless its syndrome support has a certified margin. If a state/noise model guarantees support distance m_Λ from the decoding boundary, require the transformed bin error <m_Λ. For full-support noise, the candidate must instead bound probability in the boundary strip. A variance bound alone does not provide that probability for an arbitrarily thin strip.

For a K-mode thermal attenuator of transmissivity η and environment mean photon number n_th, the Gaussian covariance rule in the [q,p]=i convention (vacuum covariance I/2) is V↦ηV+(1−η)(n_th+1/2)I. Thus the exact block energy relation is E_out=η E_in+(1−η)K n_th before any decoder symplectic transformation. This supplies a finite mean-energy bound for the homodyne modes; after a fixed Gaussian decoding transform, propagate the covariance/second-moment bound through that transform to obtain the E_j^meas above.

For detector range Γ, the Matsuura et al. energy-constrained homodyne estimate gives, per measured quadrature,

  ε_range,j ≤ (2E_j^meas+1)/Γ^2.

For q and p on all K modes, a union/hybrid bound is ε_range≤(4E_meas,total+2K)/Γ^2. Hence Γ^2≥(4E_meas,total+2K)/ε_range suffices. These costs assume the SSS transformation and every measurement mode's energy bound have been included; any lattice symplectic decoder changes the E_j^meas constants.

Combining the pieces, if the actual decoded finite-envelope state has ideal-target margin μ and one proves

  T(ω_actual,ω*) ≤ ε_env(s)+ε_ch(E_s)+ε_bin(b,E_meas)+ε_range(Γ,E_meas)+sqrt(E_s/(M+1)),

then actual Ic>0 follows whenever the right-hand side is below t_*(μ). Here ε_env is finite-envelope-to-target error, and ε_ch is the difference between the actual channel on that envelope and whichever channel defines ω*. In an actual thermal-channel calculation one can set ω_actual directly and avoid ε_ch; then one must certify Ic(ω_actual)>0 from the finite-envelope logical output itself.

If one proves a Hölder envelope estimate ε_env(s)≤C s^α, the bump-energy estimate gives E_s=O(C_Λ(C/ε_env)^{2/α}) at the chosen error budget. If a positive finite-envelope score μ_s is established directly, the Fock cutoff instead costs M=O(E_s/t_*(μ_s)^2). These are quantitative conditional scalings; neither C, α nor μ_s is supplied by the current E2 calculations.

4. The missing inequality and what primary literature does (and does not) supply

The SSS papers supply the right objects: (i) an exact CPTP ideal logical decoder as partial trace over the syndrome subsystem; (ii) formulas for logical maps of arbitrary CV noise, including Gaussian channels; and (iii) an exact procedure for applying an envelope map and orthonormalizing approximate codewords. The PRX Quantum paper emphasizes that ideal GKP states are not normalizable, writes a finite-envelope map, then applies orthonormalization to recover a CPTP encoder. Its pure-loss analysis is for the square code; its general multimode SSS formalism allows more general lattices, but complicated cells require numerical integration. It also notes that applying pure loss to an ideal codeword is an unphysical case where the ideal characteristic-function sum does not decay, and reports that pure loss and the loss-plus-amplifier displacement channel give different logical performance. Thus one cannot silently identify the thermal attenuator with the random-displacement formula used in E2's ideal gate.

Matsuura–Menicucci–Yamasaki provide a finite-energy smooth compact-syndrome preparation and prove a CV fault-tolerance theorem under their energy-constrained Markovian noise model. Their paper explicitly warns that a Gaussian-approximate GKP codeword is not the ideal GKP word subject to Gaussian random displacement. More directly, for the conventional approximate Gaussian-envelope word they show that simply cutting its SSS wavefunction to a compact syndrome cell creates an infinite-energy discontinuity; smoothing restores finite energy but can increase trace distance. They state that deriving the explicit trace-distance versus preparation-energy tradeoff is future work. Their finite-resolution homodyne model does provide the range and up-to-b displacement estimates used above. That FT theorem is not a positive-coherent-information theorem for a fixed thermal attenuator block, and it does not furnish E2's missing ε_env or ε_ch.

A decisive finite-block verification should therefore avoid the ideal-to-thermal shortcut: choose the explicit smooth bump or a CPTP-orthonormalized finite envelope for Λ_n; propagate it through the actual thermal attenuator in the SSS representation using the exact Gaussian-channel characteristic function; apply the stated binned/range-limited decoder; and certify the four-dimensional RQ output state and its conditional entropy with a rigorous integral/quadrature remainder. The reference-qubit bound then handles any remaining trace error. If one instead wants to infer positivity from E2's ideal random-displacement score, the exact missing theorem is a quantitative bound on the decoded output state for the actual thermal channel, uniform at the selected finite energy and with explicit syndrome-boundary and envelope terms. Without that bound, ideal Ic>0 alone does not imply physical Ic>0.
