# Target-specific source-leaf control for a nonlinear field query

**Frozen investigation — 14 October 2026**

## Result

For the supplied Predictor-B branching estimator, the root source-leaf event is itself a computable unbiased contribution to the selected query. Subtracting that realized contribution and adding its exact resolvent expectation leaves the branching law and tree work unchanged, while reducing the variance of each normalized sample by at least the probability of that source event. This target-specific correction adds one scalar resolvent query at the requested point and requires no other corrected-field values.

The exact source-event probability is

\[
p_0=\frac{R}{\Lambda h}
 =\frac{23520887825899840}{38043270574083423}
 \simeq 0.6182667123768061.
\]

Thus the leading small-error Bernstein sample factor is at most
\(1-p_0\simeq0.3817332876231939\), a 2.62-fold reduction against the package's Hoeffding sample count at the same \((\epsilon,\delta)\). A paired 200,000-tree replay observed a normalized sample-variance ratio 0.29579 (3.38-fold reduction), with exactly the same generated trees and mean node count. The replay is floating-point evidence under the ideal sampling law, not an interval-validated implementation result.

This is an application of the standard control-variate identity, not a historical novelty claim. The reported advantage is specific to this root event, predictor, query model, and certificate. Whether it matters end-to-end depends on the cost and availability of the scalar control; the supplied toy's deterministic Fourier method remains far faster.

## Exact setting and control

Use the supplied periodic problem

\[
-u''+u+25u^4=26+10g(x),\qquad
g(x)=0.45\cos x+0.30\cos(7x)+0.25\sin(13x),
\]

at \(x_*=0.5\), with the supplied linear predictor \(v=1+e_0\),

\[
e_0=\frac3{68}\cos x+\frac1{50}\cos(7x)+\frac1{108}\sin(13x).
\]

Its residual is the finite trigonometric polynomial

\[
r_1=-150e_0^2-100e_0^3-25e_0^4.
\]

Let \(h=11/1000\), and use the exact source certificate's residual bound \(R\), proposal rate \(\Lambda\), and coefficient bounds. At the root the supplied sampler chooses a source leaf with probability \(p_0=R/(\Lambda h)\). Conditional on that event, its normalized output is \(S=r_1(Y)/R\), where \(Y\) is the Brownian endpoint at an independent \(\operatorname{Exp}(\Lambda)\) time. The resolvent expectation is

\[
q_\Lambda(x_*)=\mathbb E_{x_*}\!\left[\frac{r_1(Y)}{\Lambda}\right]
 =\sum_k\frac{\widehat r_1(k)e^{ikx_*}}{\Lambda+k^2}
 =\bigl((\Lambda-\partial_x^2)^{-1}r_1\bigr)(x_*).
\]

Only this scalar Fourier evaluation is needed. The polynomial coefficients are generated by exact rational convolution from the supplied finite Fourier predictor; the resulting control polynomial has degree 52. This is not a global nonlinear solve and does not compute values of \(u\) at other query points.

The predictor, global residual bound \(R\), coefficient bounds, and \(\lambda,\Lambda\) certificate are inherited supplied inputs. This control operation does not acquire or independently verify them for a new model. A complete application must charge predictor construction and all global-certificate acquisition/verification separately; the present receipt establishes the algebra from those certified inputs, not their general availability.

Let \(Z\) denote the supplied normalized full-tree output and \(I_0\) the indicator that its root is a source leaf. Define

\[
C=I_0\frac{r_1(Y)}R,\qquad W=Z-C.
\]

The corrected estimator is

\[
\widehat u_M(x_*)=v(x_*)+q_\Lambda(x_*)+
\frac hM\sum_{j=1}^M W_j.
\]

## Unbiasedness, integrability, concentration, and work

**Unbiasedness.** On the source-leaf event the tree terminates immediately and \(Z=S=C\). On other root events \(C=0\). Therefore

\[
\mathbb E C=p_0\,\mathbb E[S\mid I_0=1]
 =\frac1{\Lambda h}\,\mathbb E[r_1(Y)]
 =\frac{q_\Lambda(x_*)}{h}.
\]

The supplied theorem gives \(\mathbb E[hZ]=u(x_*)-v(x_*)\). Subtracting \(C\) and adding \(q_\Lambda(x_*)\) consequently preserves the exact target expectation.

**Signed-weight integrability.** The source bound gives \(|S|\le1\). The incoming certificate bounds every other normalized branch factor by one; its subcritical offspring condition is \(\Gamma(h)<\lambda\). Hence the full output is absolutely integrable and in fact \(|Z|\le1\) almost surely. The control is also bounded, and

\[
W=0\quad\text{on }I_0=1,\qquad W=Z\in[-1,1]\quad\text{on }I_0=0.
\]

No signed-weight expectation is justified by cancellation: the expectation exists absolutely from bounded outputs, and finite expected tree size follows from the incoming subcriticality certificate. At every node, the expected number of children is at most
\[
1-\frac{V(Y)}\Lambda+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{mB_mh^{m-1}}\Lambda
\le 1-\frac{\lambda-\Gamma(h)}\Lambda=:q<1,
\]
so the expected generation sizes are bounded by \(q^n\) and the expected total node count by \(\sum_{n\ge0}q^n=\Lambda/(\lambda-\Gamma(h))\). The altered estimator inherits absolute integrability and this work bound because it does not alter or reweight the tree.

**Variance and tail.** Directly,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(W)\le\mathbb E[W^2]\le 1-p_0.
\]

Also \(|hW-\mathbb E[hW]|\le2h\). Bernstein's inequality gives

\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|hM^{-1}\sum_jW_j-h\mathbb EW\right|\ge\epsilon\right)
\le2\exp\!\left(
-\frac{M\epsilon^2}{2h^2(1-p_0)+4h\epsilon/3}
\right).
\]

For the same delta, the original package's Hoeffding count is
\(\lceil 2h^2\epsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta)\rceil\); the control-variate Bernstein sufficient count is
\(\lceil(2h^2(1-p_0)\epsilon^{-2}+4h(3\epsilon)^{-1})\log(2/\delta)\rceil\).
At \(\delta=.05\), generated exact-arithmetic certificate counts are:

| \(\epsilon\) | package Hoeffding | source-control Bernstein | count ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(10^{-3}\) | 893 | 395 | 0.4423 |
| \(10^{-4}\) | 89,271 | 34,619 | 0.3878 |
| \(10^{-5}\) | 8,927,089 | 3,413,178 | 0.3823 |

As \(\epsilon\to0\), the displayed sufficient-count ratio tends to \(1-p_0\). This is an upper-bound comparison, not a claim that the actual variance ratio equals that limit.

**Tree work.** No offspring event, probability, or weight is changed, so the supplied per-sample bound remains

\[
\mathbb E N\le\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda-\Gamma(h)}
 =\frac{120557007166832000}{74191688310648511}
 \simeq1.624939530450459.
\]

The additional acquisition is the exact residual polynomial and one resolvent multiplier per Fourier mode; for a degree-\(K\) predictor and a quartic residual this has degree at most \(4K\), with direct convolution cost depending on the implementation (naive repeated convolution is quadratic in intermediate support). Each new target then needs an \(O(K)\) scalar Fourier evaluation. These costs are charged separately from tree work. For a general predictor given only as a black-box evaluator, this finite-polynomial shortcut may not exist; the work claim does not cover acquiring a global residual representation or certifying it.

Exact unbiasedness presumes exact evaluation of \(q_\Lambda(x_*)\), just as the supplied theorem presumes ideal arithmetic and ideal endpoint draws. The receipt stores exact rational Fourier coefficients, but its reported \(x_*=0.5\) trigonometric evaluation is floating point. If a certified deterministic evaluation \(\widetilde q\) obeys \(|\widetilde q-q_\Lambda(x_*)|\le\eta<\epsilon\), then the same Bernstein bound applies to error exceeding \(\epsilon\) with \(\epsilon-\eta\) in place of \(\epsilon\); the center is biased by at most \(\eta\). To retain exact unbiasedness, use an exactly unbiased scalar evaluator. Finite-precision diffusion endpoints and branch selection also need their own unbiasedness or bias bounds; those were not validated here. It is incorrect to call the floating implementation exactly unbiased without these qualifications.

The root-frequency fluctuation is consistent with the exact Bernoulli probability, but no hypothesis test is needed to establish the mathematical variance bound. The two estimates differ by the sample mean of the control, as the identity predicts. The supplied numerical Fourier reference is not an interval proof. ## Comparators and limits

The strongest *matched statistical comparator* is the incoming method with the same Predictor B, same certificates, same query, same branch sampler, and its original Hoeffding confidence bound. The supplied one-correction result already shows a 462.6-fold variance drop relative to its constant-predictor run; that is a predictor improvement within the package, not evidence from this operation. Against the matched Predictor-B sampler, the source control further improves the certified leading sample factor by 2.62× and the observed variance by 3.38× without extra trees.

The nearest broad methodological comparator is [Fan et al.'s ICLR 2026 SCaSML](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/cf8353a0efc3644454a5591a7cb55c17-Abstract-Conference.html): the official abstract describes deriving a defect PDE for a surrogate error and applying stochastic simulation as a targeted correction. That makes “approximate field plus stochastic correction” an established research direction. The present operation is narrower: it analytically removes one known source mark from an already certified branching tree. It does not establish a better general defect solver or a speed advantage over SCaSML. I read the official abstract; its linked PDF timed out during retrieval, so I make no comparison of detailed assumptions or rates.

At the method level, nonlinear branching diffusion representations and moment/integrability certificates are established machinery. For example, [Agarwal and Claisse's 2020 accepted author manuscript](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/210343/7/210343.pdf) develops \(L^q\) criteria for branching PDE estimators, with \(q=2\) controlling finite variance and supersolution-based bounds; [Bossy et al.'s 2015 v3 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2304) studies Monte Carlo for nonlinear Poisson–Boltzmann equations. These support the prior-art boundary, not a claim that either paper contains or lacks this exact root-leaf control. A literature-wide novelty search was not performed.

For this synthetic equation, the source's 512-point deterministic Fourier fixed-point computation is the strongest practical comparator: it returns the full field, agrees at displayed precision across grids through 2048, and was reported at about 0.0016 seconds versus about 0.034 seconds of sampling time, excluding 1.20 seconds of Numba compilation. It is far more accurate and faster here, although its continuum value is numerical rather than interval-certified. Therefore this investigation supplies a sharper stochastic query contract, not a win over the best available solver on the toy.

For high-dimensional semilinear elliptic problems, full-history recursive multilevel Picard methods are also a serious prior comparator. The [Beck–Gonon–Jentzen 2024 result](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42985-024-00272-4) proves polynomial complexity for a stated class with Lipschitz nonlinearities; that assumption does not directly match the quartic reaction here. The source's claim that this toy has a faster deterministic Fourier baseline is therefore not a general comparison against MLP, and no matched MLP benchmark was run.

The capital dossier N737 is a relevant structural comparator: exact finite observable closure alone does not remove a slow inverse, and representation/acquisition cost must be paid. Here the finite Fourier representation is explicitly supplied and the control only evaluates one observable. If its construction or global residual certificate is withheld, expensive, or untrusted, this operation has no zero-cost route around that gate. No conclusion about inaccessible-field acquisition follows.

## Failed shortcut: naive antithetic children

Pairing child trees antithetically does not preserve a nonlinear branch expectation merely because each marginal is correct. In the exact counterexample saved in `antithetic_bias_check.json`, let an unbiased child be \(X\in\{-1,+1\}\) equiprobably. Two required independent copies have \(\mathbb E[X_1X_2]=0\). The antithetic pair \((X,-X)\) has product expectation \(-1\); duplicating the same path gives \(+1\). Either changes a quadratic branch. This falsifies naive antithetic reuse, not a designed covariance correction, unbiased U-statistic, or other coupling that explicitly preserves product expectations.

## Rejected weighted-majorant pilot

An exploratory spatial weighted majorant decreased the nominal radius from \(h=.011\) to about .00731, but its certified tree-node upper bound was about 1.724 and its total nodes-plus-rejection-endpoint-proposals bound was about 2.410, compared with the source tree's 1.625-node bound. It also required evaluation of a degree-104 Fourier mass polynomial. These are upper bounds, not observed mean work, and the cost models are not perfectly matched; the pilot did not establish a net variance/work improvement. It is retained only as a negative design receipt (`weighted_certificate.json`), not as a theorem or recommended operation.

## Frozen scope

The exact rational certificate proves the probability identity, the displayed deterministic sample bounds, and unchanged certified tree-work bound under the supplied Predictor-B hypotheses. The replay establishes only observed float behavior under one seeded 200,000-tree run. Neither artifact establishes end-to-end speedup, novelty, a general high-dimensional gain, physical applicability, global residual acquisition, or a solver lower bound.
