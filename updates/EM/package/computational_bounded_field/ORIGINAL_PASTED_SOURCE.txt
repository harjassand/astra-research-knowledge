1. The computational task
I screened quantum-impurity evolution, high-frequency wave computation, and equilibrium spin-system sampling. The first two did not yield a new, closed compression or stability theorem in this run. Spin-system sampling produced an explicit operation with a tractable error analysis and a concrete computational consequence.
Consider \(n\) spins \(x\in\{-1,+1\}^n\), with distribution
\[
p(x)=\frac{1}{Z}
\exp\left\{
\frac12x^\top J_0x
+\frac12(Ax)^\top K(Ax)
+h^\top x
\right\}.
\]
The input consists of rational matrices and vectors satisfying
\[
0\preceq J_0\preceq(1-\gamma)I,
\qquad
A\in\mathbb Q^{r\times n},
\qquad
K\succ0,
\qquad
h\in\mathbb Q^n.
\]
The residual interaction \(J_0\) is weak. The added interaction \(A^\top KA\) has rank at most \(r\), but its strength is unrestricted. “Positive” means positive semidefinite; individual matrix entries can have different signs.
The required outputs are an \(n\)-spin sample within total-variation distance \(\epsilon\), and an estimate of \(\log Z\) within additive error \(\epsilon\). The latter corresponds to an \(e^\epsilon\)-multiplicative estimate of \(Z\), not merely an extensive free-energy approximation.
Proposed computational theorem
Let \(B\) be the total rational input bit length. The construction gives the bound
\[
\boxed{
\left[
\frac{n^2\{\sqrt n+\log(n/\epsilon)\}}{\epsilon}
\right]^{O(r)}
\operatorname{poly}
\left(
B,n,\gamma^{-1},\epsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta)
\right).
}
\]
Here \(\delta\) is the preprocessing failure probability.
For fixed \(r\) and an inverse-polynomial residual gap \(\gamma\), this is polynomial in the binary input size. There is no numerical factor involving
\[
\|A^\top KA\|,
\qquad
\|h\|,
\qquad
\text{or the reciprocal probability of a Gaussian cell}.
\]
The rank dependence remains substantial. This is not a general high-rank Ising solver, and it does not allow the weak residual to become arbitrarily critical for free.
2. The new operation
The starting Gaussian transform is standard. It introduces \(y\in\mathbb R^r\) and the field
\[
\ell(y)=h+A^\top y,
\]
giving the joint unnormalised weight
\[
F(y,x)=
\exp\left\{
-\frac12y^\top K^{-1}y
+\frac12x^\top J_0x
+\ell(y)^\top x
\right\}.
\]
Its total mass is
\[
\sum_x\int F(y,x)\,dy=C_KZ,
\qquad
C_K=(2\pi)^{r/2}\sqrt{\det K}.
\]
The Gaussian lift itself is not the claimed invention. It is already used in specialised approximately-low-rank Ising algorithms. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research
The proposed operation is to clip the fields supplied to the easy sampler while retaining the discarded field strength analytically.
Define
\[
R=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}|(J_0)_{ij}|
\le \sqrt n.
\]
Choose a threshold \(L>R\). Divide \([-L,L]\) into intervals of width \(\Delta\). The hyperplanes
\[
h_i+a_i^\top y=t
\]
at these interval endpoints partition latent space into cells \(C\).
Inside each cell, define a constant field vector \(c_C\):
- use \(+L\) or \(-L\) for a field beyond the corresponding threshold;
- otherwise use the midpoint of its interval.
The total rounding error on unsaturated coordinates is at most
\[
b=\frac{n\Delta}{2}.
\]
Now retain the excess field energy
\[
D(y)=\sum_i\bigl(|\ell_i(y)|-L\bigr)_+.
\]
Crucially, \(D\) is affine on each cell, regardless of how large the original interactions are:
\[
D(y)=d_C^\top y+e_C.
\]
Define the joint envelope
\[
Q(y,x)=
\exp\left\{
-\frac12y^\top K^{-1}y+D(y)
\right\}
\exp\left\{
\frac12x^\top J_0x+c_C^\top x
\right\}.
\]
Within a cell, this factors into a Gaussian weight and a bounded-field spin model.
The distinction matters: simply deleting large fields is wrong. Moving their excess contribution out of the sampler and into an analytically integrable factor is the useful operation.
3. The central theorem and proof
Write
\[
Z_0(c)=\sum_x
\exp\left\{\frac12x^\top J_0x+c^\top x\right\},
\qquad
\rho=ne^{-2(L-R)}.
\]
Bounded-field envelope theorem
For every latent point away from irrelevant cell boundaries,
\[
\boxed{
(1-\rho)e^{-b}
\le
\frac{Z_0(\ell(y))}
     {e^{D(y)}Z_0(c_C)}
\le e^b.
}
\]
There is also an exact ideal rejection identity. Define
\[
a(y,x)=
\exp\left\{
(\ell(y)-c_C)^\top x-D(y)-b
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
0\le a(y,x)\le1,
\]
\[
Q(y,x)a(y,x)=e^{-b}F(y,x),
\]
and, uniformly in \(y\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{X\sim\mu_{0,c_C}}a(y,X)
\ge (1-\rho)e^{-2b}.
}
\]
Proof
For a saturated coordinate, write
\[
\ell_i=s_i(L+t_i),
\qquad s_i\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad t_i\ge0.
\]
Its contribution to the exponent before subtracting \(b\) is
\[
(\ell_i-c_i)x_i-(|\ell_i|-L)
=t_i(s_ix_i-1).
\]
That contribution is zero when the spin aligns with the saturated field and is \(-2t_i\) otherwise. The unsaturated coordinates together contribute a number in \([-b,b]\). This proves \(a\le1\) and the exact density identity.
Under the bounded-field base law, the conditional field acting on coordinate \(i\) is
\[
c_i+\sum_{j\ne i}(J_0)_{ij}x_j.
\]
For a saturated coordinate, its magnitude in the desired direction is at least \(L-R\). Therefore its conditional—and hence unconditional—wrong-sign probability is at most
\[
e^{-2(L-R)}.
\]
A union bound, requiring no independence assumption, gives
\[
\Pr(\text{all saturated spins align})\ge1-\rho.
\]
On that event, \(a(y,x)\ge e^{-2b}\). This proves the acceptance lower bound. The same argument without the subtractive \(b\) proves the partition-ratio bounds. \(\square\)
Two consequences
Let \(Q_{\mathrm{tot}}\) be the total envelope mass, and let \(q_X\) be its normalised spin marginal.
The exact rejection identity gives a coupling under which
\[
\boxed{
\|q_X-p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le 1-(1-\rho)e^{-2b}
\le \rho+2b.
}
\]
Also,
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\log Z-
\left(\log Q_{\mathrm{tot}}-\log C_K\right)
\right|
\le b-\log(1-\rho).
}
\]
These two inequalities are the core of the compiler. They control the full output distribution and the partition function simultaneously.
A constant-resolution choice,
\[
L=\lceil R\rceil+\lceil\log_2(4n)\rceil,
\qquad
\Delta=\frac1{4n},
\]
gives the ideal rejection guarantee
\[
\Pr(\mathrm{accept})
\ge\frac34e^{-1/4}
>0.5841.
\]
Thus even the rejection version needs fewer than \(1.713\) proposals in expectation, independently of interaction strength.
But the stronger implementation simplification is to avoid rejection altogether.
4. A finite mixture that needs no latent samples
Choose
\[
L=
\lceil R\rceil+
\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8n}{\epsilon}\right\rceil,
\]
and divide \([-L,L]\) into
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{16nL}{\epsilon}\right\rceil
\]
equal intervals.
Then
\[
b\le\epsilon/16,
\qquad
\rho\le\epsilon/8,
\]
so the ideal envelope already satisfies
\[
\|q_X-p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\epsilon/4
\]
and has log-partition bias below \(\epsilon/2\). The remaining tolerance can pay for numerical estimation and base sampling.
There are at most
\[
m=n(N+1)
=O\!\left(\frac{n^2L}{\epsilon}\right)
\]
hyperplanes and
\[
M\le\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom mj
\]
full-dimensional cells.
For each cell, compute
\[
G_C=
\int_C
\exp\left\{
-\frac12y^\top K^{-1}y+d_C^\top y+e_C
\right\}dy
\]
and
\[
W_C=G_CZ_0(c_C).
\]
The compiled distribution is exactly
\[
\boxed{
q_X(x)=
\sum_C
\frac{W_C}{\sum_DW_D}
\,\mu_{0,c_C}(x).
}
\]
After preprocessing, drawing a sample consists of selecting a cell according to its weight and sampling one bounded-field base model.
There is no continuous latent-variable sampling at output time, no rejection, and no need to move a Markov chain between the original system’s phases.
This is the new computational representation: a finite weighted collection of easy spin laws, rather than a chain operating on the difficult global law.
5. Closing the hidden-cost obligations
The two potentially expensive components are acquiring the Gaussian cell weights and implementing the base spin models. Neither is treated as a free oracle.
Gaussian weights: no rare-event rejection oracle
Completing the square gives
\[
G_C=
\exp\left\{
e_C+\frac12d_C^\top Kd_C-q_C(y_*)
\right\}I_C,
\]
where
\[
q_C(y)=
\frac12(y-Kd_C)^\top K^{-1}(y-Kd_C),
\]
\[
y_*=\arg\min_{y\in C}q_C(y),
\qquad
I_C=\int_Ce^{-\phi(y)}dy,
\qquad
\phi(y)=q_C(y)-q_C(y_*).
\]
The potentially enormous prefactor is handled in logarithmic form. Its exponent is rational and can be computed exactly.
The mode \(y_*\) can be acquired by enumerating at most \(r\) active constraints and solving rational optimality systems. This costs \((2n+1)^{O(r)}\) polynomial-bit operations, already within the stated rank-dependent bound.
For the remaining integral, define
\[
V(t)=\operatorname{vol}
\{y\in C:\phi(y)\le t\}.
\]
Convexity gives
\[
V(t)\le t^rV(1)\quad(t\ge1),
\qquad
I_C\ge e^{-1}V(1).
\]
Layer-cake integration yields
\[
I_C=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}V(t)\,dt
\]
and the relative tail bound
\[
\frac{\int_T^\infty e^{-t}V(t)\,dt}{I_C}
\le
e\int_T^\infty e^{-t}t^r\,dt.
\]
Thus a cutoff
\[
T=O\!\left(r^2+\log(1/\eta)\right)
\]
suffices for relative accuracy \(\eta\), without knowing the original Gaussian cell probability.
A particularly useful implementation is the positive sum
\[
(1-e^{-s})
\sum_{k=1}^{J}e^{-(k-1)s}V(ks),
\qquad
s=O(\eta),\quad Js\ge T.
\]
It avoids subtracting nearly equal estimated volumes.
Each sublevel body has rational linear constraints and one convex quadratic constraint. The note constructs rational inner and outer radius guarantees with polynomial encoding length. Classical convex-volume approximation, including its rounding and finite-grid implementation, then supplies the required relative volume estimates. math.cmu.edu
This matters for thin or far-shifted cells: their small widths and probabilities are not ignored, but their bit lengths, rather than their reciprocals, enter the construction.
Base sampling and partition estimation
For the residual interaction, the spectral Poincaré theorem of Eldan–Koehler–Zeitouni gives continuous-time heat-bath gap at least \(\gamma\), or random-site discrete-time gap at least \(\gamma/n\), for arbitrary external fields. arXiv
Here all supplied fields are bounded by \(L\). A conservative restarted sample with TV error \(\tau\) therefore needs
\[
O\left(
\frac n\gamma
\left[n(L+1)+\log(1/\tau)\right]
\right)
\]
single-site updates. A dense update costs \(O(n)\) arithmetic operations.
For \(Z_0(c)\), interpolate the interaction through
\[
0,\frac{J_0}{n},\ldots,J_0.
\]
Each successive partition ratio is the expectation of
\[
\exp(E_0(X)/n),
\qquad
E_0(X)=\frac12X^\top J_0X,
\]
which lies between \(1\) and \(e^{1/2}\). Consequently, ordinary bounded-variable estimation provides relative partition estimates without large-variance importance weights.
A conservative bound is
\[
O\left(
\frac{n^5}{\gamma\eta^2}
\left[n(L+1)+\log(n/\eta)\right]
\log(n/\zeta)
\right)
\]
arithmetic operations, multiplied by polynomial bit-arithmetic overhead. For \(J_0=0\), this annealing cost disappears: the base law is a product distribution.
Preprocessing, precision, memory, and output
The complete accounting is
\[
T_{\mathrm{prep}}
\le
T_{\mathrm{arrangement}}
+
M\left(T_{\mathrm{Gaussian\ weight}}+T_{\mathrm{base\ partition}}\right)
+
T_{\mathrm{normalisation}}.
\]
An incremental arrangement implementation makes \(O(mM)\) rational linear-programming calls. Each cell needs at most \(2n\) interval constraints.
Estimating every log weight to error \(O(\epsilon)\) controls the normalised mixture globally. The required per-cell relative tolerance does not become \(\epsilon/M\); the union bound affects confidence through \(\log M\).
Weights are stored as logarithms. Categorical sampling subtracts the largest log weight and discards sufficiently small terms with an explicit total-mass budget. Memory is
\[
M\,\operatorname{poly}
\left(n,r,B,\log(1/\epsilon),\log(1/\delta)\right)
\]
bits. Each subsequent draw also pays for writing its \(n\) output spins.
The output is \(\log Z\), or a compact scientific-notation representation—not the expanded digits of an exponentially large partition function.
Finally, the rational model factors are supplied inputs. Learning an unknown physical model is not included. Parameter acquisition and calibration require a separate cost model. This is equilibrium computation, not simulation of the physical time needed to reach equilibrium.
6. What changes relative to specialised algorithms?
Koehler, Lee, and Risteski already establish full-TV sampling and relative partition estimation for approximately low-rank Ising models. In their positive-semidefinite case, the displayed guarantees include
\[
(n\|J\|_{\mathrm{op}})^{O(d_++1)}.
\]
The proposed compiler removes that numerical norm dependence for the stated factorised positive-spike class, replacing it with binary input length. Both methods can be compared on the same supplied rational model; no learning advantage is assumed. This is an improvement over the displayed guarantee, not a lower bound on their algorithm or an exhaustive priority claim. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research
For example, with fixed rank and spike strength \(2^n\), a polynomial dependence on strength is exponential in \(n\), whereas the proposed bound remains polynomial in the input size.
That does not establish practical superiority. The compiler may construct many cells. At moderate interaction strengths, a specialised sampler can be much faster.
Nor is this intended to beat elementary structure. An exchangeable model has only \(n+1\) possible up-spin counts, so aggregating by magnetisation can be preferable. The intended broader scope includes heterogeneous pattern columns, inconsistent fields, and a correlated weak residual.
7. Counterexamples and boundaries
Naive clipping fails exponentially. For one spin, \(A=1\), \(h=0\), and \(K=\beta\),
\[
Z=2e^{\beta/2}.
\]
Discarding the excess field instead gives an approximation bounded by \(2\cosh L\). At \(\beta=100,L=4\), the true log partition is about \(50.693\), while that bound is about \(4.00034\). Retaining \(D(y)\) is essential.
Scalar factorisation is insufficient for the correlated case. Piecewise approximations to \(\log\cosh\) provide an approach for independent spins. The joint-envelope theorem replaces that independence-dependent route with a conditional wrong-sign bound, which is why the correlated residual is covered.
Negative spikes cannot be included indiscriminately. The known rank-one family
\[
p_a(x)\propto e^{-4n(a^\top x)^2},
\qquad a\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]
encodes perfect partition: a sufficiently accurate polynomial-time sampler would imply \(\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}\). This is an established obstruction, not a new result here. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research
Adding a sufficiently large diagonal leaves a spin law unchanged while making its interaction positive definite. Thus unrestricted polynomial-time sampling at arbitrary positive full rank would inherit that obstruction too.
Rank growth remains expensive. The arrangement can contain \(m^{\Theta(r)}\) cells. This is a limitation of the representation, not a proof that every algorithm must enumerate them. Any broader result needs a rank-dependent improvement or additional structure—not merely a better saturation constant.
8. What was actually tested
The packet separates proof checks from implementations.
The constant-resolution envelope was tested on 240 random small instances, covering 16,732 spin configurations. All assertions passed. Five four-spin rank-one mass checks, spanning \(K=2^{-40}\) to \(2^{40}\), used the same 611 cells and gave ideal acceptance probabilities between approximately \(0.8772\) and \(0.8824\).
The rejection-free mixture was checked against the complete three-spin output distribution at three interaction strengths. Observed TV errors were approximately
\[
4.26\times10^{-4},
\qquad
4.21\times10^{-8},
\qquad
2.42\times10^{-7},
\]
below the fixture’s proved ideal bound of approximately \(0.03077\).

Completion assessment
The result is more than a constant improvement or a formal extension of the retained Gaussian-crossing family. It supplies a different representation for a discrete equilibrium computation:
\[
\boxed{
\text{arbitrarily strong positive low-rank interaction}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{finite mixture of bounded-field easy models}.
}
\]
The envelope and mixture-error arguments are complete. The proposed fixed-rank bit-complexity theorem has an explicit algorithmic reduction, including acquisition of geometric guarantees, precision, randomness, memory, and output costs.
The remaining validation gates are independent proof review, historical priority, and a certified general implementation with matched benchmarks. The mathematical escalation question is whether weighted-cell aggregation can improve the \(n^{O(r)}\) dependence on a meaningful parameterised or structured growing-rank class; parameterised hardness must be checked rather than assuming such an improvement exists.
The retained Gaussian results are not premises of this construction. The packet is a local research handoff; no GitHub files were changed.
