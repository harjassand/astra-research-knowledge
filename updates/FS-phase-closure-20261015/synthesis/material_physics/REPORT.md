[Yu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-023-01421-3). ## Strongest new physical coexistence constraint

For parallel channels sharing a wall, define `DeltaPi=pi(0)-pi(L)` over the actual shared interaction region. A pressure-driven donor has `DeltaP1>0`. A receiver moving in the donor direction while supplying work has `Q2>0, DeltaP2<0`. The transverse pressure difference `delta(x)=p1(x)-p2(x)` obeys

\[
\boxed{\max(|\delta(0)|,|\delta(L)|)
\ge\tfrac12(\Delta P_1+|\Delta P_2|).}
\]

This follows solely from the endpoint difference and the triangle inequality; it does not need linear profiles. Choosing a relative offset between the channels' mean pressures cannot balance both wall ends while preserving donor pressure drive and an opposing receiver load. For 1 bar donor drop and 0.2 bar receiver load over this region, at least one wall end sees 0.6 bar normal differential pressure. Full port drops require separate access-loss accounting before substitution into this local bound.

The same specimen must therefore sustain that normal loading, retain impermeability and low friction, and preserve the electronic response. Dense support, bonding, wrinkles and substrate scattering cannot be assigned independently of cross-transfer. This is not a prohibition on supported separators or different geometries; it is a missing coupled qualification for this particular loaded architecture.

## Detectable flow ratios can still imply very small work efficiency

The primary paper calls the unloaded velocity ratio `r=vB/vA` a tunnelling efficiency. For identical, steady, passive reciprocal full channels, that ratio gives `q²=r²`, hence

\[
\eta_{\max}=\frac{r^2}{(1+\sqrt{1-r^2})^2}\simeq r^2/4.
\]

Its illustrative `r≈0.1` corresponds to only **0.251% maximal work efficiency** in this symmetric linear reduction. A single velocity ratio cannot determine efficiency for unequal channels or unacquired terminal profiles.

Applying the paper's own approximate carbon ansatz `r≈A exp[-(gamma tau+d/ell)]`, with its discussion values `gamma tau≈1, ell≈4 nm`, gives `r<=0.0302` at 10 nm even with `A=1`. The associated symmetric work ceiling is about **0.0228%**. This is explicitly an ansatz-level screen, not a rigorous material exclusion; neglected interlayer transport and the actual wet spectra/rates may change it. It does show that “non-negligible flow tunnelling” in that estimate is not a high-efficiency pressure exchanger.

## Why the cooling experiment cannot supply the missing drag coefficient

For isotropic passive equilibrium channels with one common, velocity-independent transmission kernel, the derived identity is

\[
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{T h}{2}\left\langle q^2/\omega^2\right\rangle_h,}
\]

where `h` is thermal boundary conductance and the average uses the heat-transfer spectral weight. Heat and momentum weight the same modes differently. An exact model counterexample has identical heat transfer but friction differing by a factor of one million through different momentum supports.

Thus even a measured equilibrium `h` does not acquire drag without the relevant spectral moment or a direct force/flow measurement. The actual hot-electron experiment is outside that equilibrium acquisition contract as well. The identity is not imposed on the full inelastic Keldysh wall theory; it is a restricted transfer obstruction, with its complete hypotheses in `DERIVATIONS.md`.

A separate long-wavelength finite-temperature graphene diagnostic rejects another shortcut: an arbitrarily slow high-momentum Drude oscillator is not automatically an available monolayer graphene plasmon. At 300 K the neutral local Drude model retains a thermal energy scale of 35.8 meV. At `q=2.5 nm^-1` and ordinary frequency `f=0.3 THz`, its locality parameter `q vF/omega` is about 1326, far outside the collisionless local expansion. This is not a bound on multilayer graphite, MXenes, acoustic modes or other nonlocal materials. [Intrinsic-plasmon primary theory](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.87.235418).

## A decisive physical test

The most credible first target is a room-temperature, two-sided few-layer-carbon wall with water on both sides, using the actual supported geometry rather than assigning it a cold/encapsulated lifetime. Begin with its zero-drive leakage, mechanical compliance, wet friction and temperature response. Few-layer graphite is a candidate, not a qualified material. Mica/supercooled glycerol is a valuable direct-friction control platform, but its measured large friction and temperature-dependent viscosity do not supply a low-loss exchanger.

Measure steady donor and receiver pressure/volume-flow response in both directions on the same specimen, then impose a receiver load and measure sustained work `Wout=-integral DeltaP2 Q2 dt`, preferably into a calibrated accumulator whose discharge is independently measured. Nanotube nanojet metrology and tuning-fork drainage measurements provide concrete precedents for resolving small flows and interfacial friction, not guaranteed sensitivity for this new fixture. [Secchi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature19315), [Lizée et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50232-0).

Quantify wall motion and support drift, including continuing mechanical boundary work; one-shot stored elastic energy is insufficient for an oscillating or creeping wall. Bound cross-wall hydraulic leakage by its pressure-times-flow energy, and separately track water/species, ions/protons and electrochemical sources. A small high-pressure leak can power a larger low-pressure circulation, so a volume-only leakage comparison fails. Proton transport through some atomically thin crystals is experimentally established; fabricated impermeability is species-, thickness-, edge- and defect-dependent. [Hu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14015).

Require loaded output to exceed jointly calibrated mechanical, leakage and other counterfeit-work upper bounds with uncertainty. That would establish a missing transport route, but unique electronic/quantum attribution still needs controlled response changes. Role reversal, pressure reversal, long holds, frequency dependence, insulating-wall controls and measured field/chemistry controls should test that attribution. Gating can itself alter wetting, ionic motion, strain and loss; it is not an automatically isolated spectral intervention.

The realistic competitor for isolated nanoscale delivery is a conventional pressure-driven or membrane-coupled micropump achieving the same receiver flow, pressure, purity and lifetime. Bulk pressure recovery has mature pressure-exchange competitors. Requiring a stationary separator is a mechanism condition; its practical value must be demonstrated rather than credited as a performance gain by definition.

## Different intervention considered

Suppressing an existing wall-friction channel is a physically different opportunity from adding a work receiver. It could lower the original pumping requirement directly. The glycerol/mica result establishes a real temperature-dependent spectral/friction effect, but a large slip-length increase alone does not establish lower pumping power because viscosity changes simultaneously:

\[
Q/w=\frac{h^3\Delta P}{12L}(1/\eta+6/(h\lambda)).
\]

A fixed-temperature, same-wetted-face spectral intervention would need a measured pressure-drop reduction at fixed useful flow, with its control energy, chemistry and mechanical changes charged. N802 already supplies a related conditional spectral-substitution discipline; no qualified switching material or extraordinary new operation emerged here. The primary flow-tunnelling mechanism itself has not been refuted, so replacing it with an unrelated speculative apparatus would not improve the evidence.
