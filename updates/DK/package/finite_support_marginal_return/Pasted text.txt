# A finite-dimensional support theorem—and a failure of composability

**The historic objective is not established.** The strongest result obtained is a complete finite-dimensional classification of a particular kind of exact catalytic state preparation:

> A bipartite state can be produced from a separable input, with nonzero probability and an exactly restored finite-dimensional catalyst marginal, **if and only if its support contains a product vector**.

The catalyst may be mixed, and it may become correlated with the output. Failure need not preserve it.

The construction uses no infinite spectra. Conversely, the impossibility proof handles **every finite catalyst dimension and every positive success probability**, however small.

Pursuing the apparent computational power of this result led to a second conclusion:

> These successful marginal-return transformations do not compose as a relation on states. Their transitive closure would declare every state freely obtainable, even though the actual physical compositions consume the catalyst.

I then tested whether approximation repairs that defect. It does not: a finite construction gives arbitrarily accurate output and arbitrarily small output–catalyst correlation at fixed positive success probability, while still failing the necessary compositional return condition.

The proofs below establish these statements under their explicit assumptions. **Their precise historical originality remains unverified.** The support geometry and entropy ingredients have established antecedents; the proposed contribution is their combination into the exact reachability classification and its operational consequences.

## 1. Exact statement

Let \(\sigma_{AB}\) be a finite-dimensional bipartite state. Write
\[
U_\sigma=\operatorname{range}(\sigma).
\]

A *stochastic marginal-return preparation* consists of a finite-dimensional catalyst \(\tau_{C_A C_B}\) and a successful branch \(\mathcal S\) of an LOCC instrument such that
\[
\mathcal S(\tau)=p\,\Omega_{AB,C},
\qquad p>0,
\tag{1}
\]
with
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{AB}\Omega=\tau,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{C}\Omega=\sigma.
\tag{2}
\]

All additional inputs are separable. The joint state \(\Omega\) need not equal \(\sigma\otimes\tau\). The return condition concerns the success-conditioned catalyst marginal, not every finer measurement transcript, and failure is unrestricted.

This is **state preparation**, not a universally usable channel implementation.

### Theorem 1 — Complete support criterion

The following conditions are equivalent:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{\(\sigma\) admits a stochastic marginal-return preparation;}\\
&U_\sigma\text{ contains a nonzero product vector;}\\
&\sigma=w\omega+(1-w)\chi
\text{ for some separable state }\omega,\quad 0<w\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Whenever the conditions hold, a catalyst with local dimensions at most
\[
d_A+1,\qquad d_B+1
\tag{4}
\]
suffices.

The impossibility direction remains valid if LOCC is enlarged to arbitrary separable operations.

There is also a stronger source statement:

> For any fixed input state \(\rho\) whose support is spanned by product vectors, the possible target states are exactly those described in (3).

Thus every full-rank input—including an entangled full-rank input—has the same set of exactly reachable targets in this particular catalytic model. This is a statement about **existence at arbitrary positive probability**, not equal costs or equal success probabilities.

The geometric equivalence between a product vector in the range and a nonzero subtractable separable component belongs to the established theory of best separable approximations. It is not itself the new claim. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707043)

## 2. The finite construction

Suppose
\[
\sigma=w\omega+(1-w)\chi,
\qquad 0<w<1,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\omega\) is separable.

Give each catalyst subsystem an extra orthogonal vacuum vector. Embed \(\chi\) in the active subspace and write
\[
v_C=|\perp\perp\rangle\langle\perp\perp|,
\qquad
\tau=w\chi_C+(1-w)v_C.
\tag{6}
\]

Alice and Bob locally test whether the catalyst is active. They declare failure on the vacuum sector. The success probability is \(w\), and conditioned on success they possess \(\chi\).

They then use shared randomness:

With probability \(w\), they keep \(\chi\) in the catalyst and prepare \(\omega\) on the output. With probability \(1-w\), they transfer \(\chi\) to the output by local swaps and reset the catalyst to vacuum.

The successful joint state is
\[
\boxed{
\Omega
=
w\,\omega_{AB}\otimes\chi_C
+
(1-w)\,\chi_{AB}\otimes v_C.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Its marginals are exactly
\[
\operatorname{tr}_C\Omega=\sigma,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{AB}\Omega=\tau.
\tag{8}
\]

Everything is finite-dimensional and LOCC.

The resource has not been cloned. On one branch it remains in the catalyst; on the other it moves to the output. The restored marginal averages over those alternatives.

To obtain (5) from a normalized product vector
\[
|ab\rangle\in U_\sigma,
\]
choose a sufficiently small \(w>0\) satisfying
\[
w\le
\frac{1}{\langle ab|\sigma^{+}|ab\rangle},
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\sigma^+\) is the inverse on the support. Then
\[
\sigma-w|ab\rangle\langle ab|\succeq0.
\]
Normalizing the remainder gives \(\chi\), with \(\omega=|ab\rangle\langle ab|\).

Conversely, any positive separable component contributes product vectors to the support. This proves the constructive direction and the geometric equivalence.

## 3. Why no finite catalyst works for a product-free support

The decisive step combines two different controls.

A sufficiently entangled support forces an entanglement quantity to grow with the number of output registers. But a finite initial catalyst places a bound on the Schmidt number of **every successful branch**, independently of its probability.

The probability-independent bound is essential. An average-resource inequality alone would not exclude an arbitrarily rare successful branch.

### A support-persistence lemma

Let
\[
U\subseteq A\otimes B
\]
be a nonzero subspace containing no nonzero product vector. Then there exists a constant \(\kappa_U>0\) such that every state \(\rho_n\) supported on \(U^{\otimes n}\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
E_R(\rho_n)\ge n\kappa_U.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Here
\[
E_R(\rho)=\inf_{\gamma\in\operatorname{SEP}}
D(\rho\Vert\gamma),
\]
with natural logarithms and the bipartition
\(A_1\cdots A_n:B_1\cdots B_n\).

The outputs in (10) may be arbitrarily correlated. No independent-copy assumption is made.

#### Proof

Fix a product informationally complete measurement
\[
\mathsf M=\mathsf M_A\otimes\mathsf M_B.
\]
Its effects can be chosen strictly positive.

The compact set of states supported on \(U\) is disjoint from the compact set of separable states. Indeed, a separable state supported on \(U\) would supply product vectors in \(U\).

Informational completeness makes the map from states to outcome distributions injective. Therefore
\[
\kappa_U
=
\min_{\substack{\rho:\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq U\\
\gamma\in\operatorname{SEP}}}
D\!\left(\mathsf M(\rho)\middle\Vert\mathsf M(\gamma)\right)
>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Now compare \(\rho_n\) with any separable \(\gamma_n\). Measure the first output pair and discard it, retaining the classical result. Relative-entropy data processing gives
\[
D(\rho_n\Vert\gamma_n)
\ge
D(p\Vert q)
+
\sum_y p_yD(\rho_{n-1|y}\Vert\gamma_{n-1|y}).
\tag{12}
\]

The first term is at least \(\kappa_U\). Each actual conditional remainder remains supported on \(U^{\otimes(n-1)}\). Each comparator remainder is separable, because the measurement is local.

Induction gives
\[
D(\rho_n\Vert\gamma_n)\ge n\kappa_U.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\gamma_n\) proves (10). ∎

This is an application of the restricted-measurement relative-entropy method developed by Piani, not a newly invented entropy inequality. The role here is to make the bound uniform over all correlated states in a specified tensor-product support. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2705)

### Apply the same successful operation repeatedly

Suppose (1)–(2) hold, but \(U_\sigma\) contains no product vector.

Run the same successful operation on the returned catalyst repeatedly, always with fresh separable inputs, and condition on all \(n\) successes.

After each successful prefix, the catalyst marginal is again \(\tau\). Therefore the probability of the next success remains \(p\), and the complete successful event has probability
\[
p^n>0.
\tag{13}
\]

There is a subtlety: later postselection can change the states of earlier outputs through their correlations with the catalyst. It is **not** valid to say that all earlier marginals remain equal to \(\sigma\).

What survives is their support.

Once an output is supported on \(U_\sigma\), operations on other registers cannot move it outside that subspace. Every new output is also supported there. Hence the final \(n\)-output state \(\rho_n\) obeys
\[
\operatorname{supp}\rho_n\subseteq U_\sigma^{\otimes n}.
\tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
r=\operatorname{SN}(\tau),
\]
the Schmidt number of the finite catalyst. Every successful separable operation, followed by discarding the catalyst, preserves the bound
\[
\operatorname{SN}(\rho_n)\le r.
\tag{15}
\]

Schmidt number and its LOCC monotonicity are established notions. Here the relevant stronger observation follows directly from their definition: each product Kraus operator preserves an upper bound on the Schmidt rank of every vector in a decomposition, even after conditioning on success. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911117)

A state with Schmidt number at most \(r\) satisfies
\[
E_R(\rho_n)\le\log r.
\tag{16}
\]
For each pure vector in a rank-\(r\) decomposition, its Schmidt-diagonal separable comparator gives relative entropy at most \(\log r\); convexity gives the mixed-state bound.

Equations (10), (14) and (16) imply
\[
n\kappa_{U_\sigma}\le\log r
\qquad\text{for every positive integer }n,
\tag{17}
\]
a contradiction.

This proves necessity.

**The contradiction is unaffected by an exponentially small success probability.** Each finite successful branch must obey the same Schmidt-number ceiling.

### Why the stronger source statement also holds

Suppose the consumed source \(\rho\) has support spanned by product vectors. Choose a separable state \(\zeta\) with exactly the same support. There are finite constants \(a,b>0\) such that
\[
a\rho\preceq\zeta\preceq b\rho.
\tag{18}
\]

Assume a successful operation converts \(\rho\otimes\tau\) to \(p\Omega\), with output support \(U\), catalyst marginal \(\tau\), and \(U\) product-free.

Replace the source by the free state \(\zeta\). The resulting output lies between \(ap\Omega\) and \(bp\Omega\). In particular, it remains supported on
\[
U\otimes\operatorname{supp}\tau.
\]

Let \(\Phi\) be its induced, unnormalized map on the catalyst. Then
\[
ap\,\tau\preceq\Phi(\tau)\preceq bp\,\tau.
\tag{19}
\]

For any positive \(X\) supported on \(\operatorname{supp}\tau\), one has \(X\preceq c\tau\) for some finite \(c\). Positivity therefore shows that applying the same operation to \(\zeta\otimes X\) still produces an output supported on \(U\), with memory supported on \(\operatorname{supp}\tau\).

Moreover,
\[
\Phi^n(\tau)\succeq(ap)^n\tau,
\tag{20}
\]
so no finite iteration vanishes.

We have again obtained arbitrarily many outputs in \(U^{\otimes n}\), starting only from the finite catalyst and freely preparable inputs. The same contradiction proves the stronger statement. ∎

For algebraic input matrices, the support criterion is a finite decision problem: finding a product vector in \(U_\sigma\) amounts to solving finitely many polynomial equations for nonzero vectors \(a,b\). No search over unbounded catalyst dimensions is necessary in this model. This does **not** decide the earlier tensor-universality problem, whose nonfree inputs and exact product-return requirements are different.

## 4. The apparent universal operation does not compose

The finite theorem initially suggests a strong new operation: arbitrary full-rank entangled states become preparable from a free input, while the catalyst marginal is restored exactly.

The next question is whether those outputs can be used as ordinary resources without invalidating the return claim.

They cannot, in general.

Let \(\Phi\) be a Bell state in an active \(2\times2\) sector, and let \(v\) be an orthogonal, locally flagged product vacuum. Define the two-qutrit state
\[
\theta=\frac12\Phi+\frac12v.
\tag{21}
\]

The construction above produces \(\theta\) from a free input with catalyst \(\theta\), succeeding with probability \(1/2\). The successful joint state is
\[
\Omega
=
\frac12v_S\otimes\Phi_C
+
\frac12\Phi_S\otimes v_C.
\tag{22}
\]
Both marginals are \(\theta\).

Separately, \(\theta\) can be converted to \(\Phi\) by locally projecting onto its active sector, with probability \(1/2\).

Thus, writing \(\rightsquigarrow\) for the state-level marginal-return relation,
\[
\text{free}\rightsquigarrow\theta,
\qquad
\theta\rightsquigarrow\Phi.
\tag{23}
\]

But Theorem 1 gives
\[
\boxed{\text{free}\not\rightsquigarrow\Phi}
\tag{24}
\]
with any finite catalyst.

The physical composition explains the failure. Conditioning the output of (22) on the active sector selects
\[
\Phi_S\otimes v_C.
\]
The catalyst is vacuum, not \(\theta\). The combined successful protocol has consumed the entanglement that the first marginal description called “returned.”

### A general obstruction to state-only bookkeeping

This is not peculiar to Bell states.

For any finite-dimensional target \(\chi\), form
\[
\theta_\chi=\frac12\chi_{\rm active}+\frac12v.
\]
The support criterion permits
\[
\text{free}\rightsquigarrow\theta_\chi,
\]
and local flag projection permits
\[
\theta_\chi\rightsquigarrow\chi.
\]

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The transitive closure of stochastic marginal-return reachability is universal.}
}
\tag{25}
\]

This is a statement about the **formal closure**, not physical universal generation.

It also rules out a nontrivial scalar resource monotone on states that is simultaneously nonincreasing under every such marginal-return transformation and ordinary stochastic LOCC. Those inequalities would imply
\[
M(\chi)\le M(\text{free})
\]
for every \(\chi\), while discarding gives the reverse inequality.

The role of external correlations in catalysis is already an established research subject. The claim here is the explicit failure of this success-conditioned state relation to support nontrivial transitive resource accounting—not a claim that correlations were previously overlooked. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02376?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 5. Approximation does not restore the missing composition rule

A possible rescue would be to require the output to be almost independent of the catalyst. Perhaps finite marginal-return power would then disappear at a success probability bounded away from zero.

That stronger conjecture is false.

Fix any target state \(\chi\), a separable state \(\omega\), a number \(0<q<1\), and an integer \(K\ge1\).

Let \(\tau_k\) be a locally flagged state holding \(k\) copies of \(\chi\), with \(\tau_0\) a product vacuum. Use the finite catalyst
\[
\tau^{(K)}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}r_k\tau_k,
\tag{26}
\]
where
\[
r_k=(1-q)q^k\quad(k<K),
\qquad
r_K=q^K.
\tag{27}
\]

The protocol is as follows.

On flag \(0\), fail. On a flag \(1\le k<K\), emit one copy of \(\chi\), decrement the flag, and succeed. On flag \(K\), succeed using a shared coin: with probability \(1-q\), emit \(\chi\) and decrement; with probability \(q\), emit \(\omega\) and leave the catalyst at \(K\).

Its total success probability is exactly \(q\). The normalized successful joint state is
\[
\boxed{
\Omega^{(K)}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}r_j\,\chi_S\otimes\tau_j
+
q^K\,\omega_S\otimes\tau_K.
}
\tag{28}
\]

Therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}_S\Omega^{(K)}=\tau^{(K)}
\tag{29}
\]
exactly, while
\[
\operatorname{tr}_C\Omega^{(K)}
=
(1-q^K)\chi+q^K\omega.
\tag{30}
\]

More strongly,
\[
\frac12
\left\|
\Omega^{(K)}-\chi\otimes\tau^{(K)}
\right\|_1
\le q^K.
\tag{31}
\]

Thus one obtains arbitrarily accurate output and arbitrarily small joint deviation from product return, at a fixed positive success probability, with a finite catalyst for every requested accuracy.

The expected number of stored target copies is
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K}kr_k
=
\frac{q(1-q^K)}{1-q},
\tag{32}
\]
which remains bounded as \(K\) increases.

There is nevertheless no free resource generation. The catalyst already contains copies of \(\chi\); failure can destroy its usefulness; and its maximum storage and Hilbert-space dimension grow with \(K\). Its preparation is not supplied for free by the construction.

Most importantly, a subsequent selective operation can condition on the small exceptional part in (28). A small unconditional correlation bound does not guarantee small catalyst disturbance after **every** later postselection.

This construction falsifies a dimension-uniform robust extension of the exact impossibility based only on success probability, marginal return and small unconditioned output–catalyst correlation.

## 6. The exact compositional requirement forces decoupling

The missing condition can be stated without an entanglement measure.

Suppose a successful joint state \(\Omega_{SC}\) has marginals \(\sigma,\tau\). Require that, after every nonzero-probability local product measurement outcome \(E_A\otimes E_B\) on \(S\), the conditional catalyst state remains \(\tau\).

Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}_S[(E\otimes I)\Omega]
=
\operatorname{tr}(E\sigma)\,\tau
\tag{33}
\]
for every product effect \(E\). Zero-probability outcomes satisfy the same equality automatically.

Product effects span the operator space. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Omega=\sigma\otimes\tau.}
\tag{34}
\]

The converse is immediate.

Thus, in this setting, protecting the return guarantee against arbitrary subsequent free postselection is exactly an **uncorrelated-return requirement**.

And with a finite catalyst, that stronger requirement cannot create any entangled state from a free input—even probabilistically.

Indeed, an exact successful transformation
\[
\tau\longmapsto p\,\sigma\otimes\tau
\]
could be repeated to produce \(\sigma^{\otimes n}\) on a nonzero successful branch, while keeping its Schmidt number bounded by \(\operatorname{SN}(\tau)\). But for every entangled \(\sigma\), the same fixed informationally complete measurement argument gives a constant \(\kappa_\sigma>0\) with
\[
E_R(\sigma^{\otimes n})\ge n\kappa_\sigma.
\tag{35}
\]
This contradicts the bounded Schmidt-number ceiling.

The conclusion preserves the earlier results rather than weakening their assumptions:

The complementary-spectrum construction used exact infinite support, so it has no finite Schmidt-number ceiling. The tensor-universality construction consumes a nonfree source state at each conversion. The channel-removal results concern universally usable operations with their stipulated whole-protocol return conditions. None is contradicted by the finite, success-only, correlated state preparations here.

## 7. What is established, and what remains missing

The main mathematical result is the complete criterion
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite stochastic marginal-return preparation}
\iff
\text{a product vector exists in the target support}.
}
\tag{36}
\]

Its converse uses a support-persistence argument that survives arbitrarily rare postselection. Its constructive direction gives an explicit finite catalyst and protocol. The stronger source version covers every source whose support is spanned by product vectors.

The geometric component is inherited from separable-approximation theory; the probability-independent ceiling uses Schmidt number; and the support-growth bound uses restricted-measurement relative entropy. Those antecedents must not be relabelled as new mathematics. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707043)

The targeted prior-art search did not establish whether the precise reachability theorem and source extension have appeared elsewhere. Broad quantum catalysis, including differing return conventions, is already an extensive subject. A comprehensive priority determination and independent verification remain outstanding. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00798)

I also checked the finite constructions using exact rational arithmetic: five flagged protocols and twenty-four capped-reservoir instances passed their normalization, marginal-return and subsequent-conditioning identities. These checks do not certify the general impossibility proof or historical originality.

**The route to a foundational computational breakthrough failed at a precise point:** the new finite operation preserves a marginal, but not the return guarantee under subsequent successful use of its output. Imposing that guarantee forces product return and eliminates its source-free entanglement-generating power. Allowing small error does not fix the problem uniformly, as the finite reservoir construction demonstrates.

Consequently, this investigation does not establish a new complexity separation, solve the earlier algebra-representation problem, or supply a finite, composably reusable universal resource. It establishes a complete support-based reachability theorem and shows exactly why its apparent universality is not such a resource. **That is substantive partial mathematics, not completion of the original mission.**
