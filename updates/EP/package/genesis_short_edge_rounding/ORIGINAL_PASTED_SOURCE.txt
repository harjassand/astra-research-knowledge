5. What this advances—and what remains unresolved
The positive mathematical content is not merely a new sufficient condition. Short edge length is sufficient, and the lower bound shows that it is necessary at the same order for universal exact-marginal rounding.

For matroids, the result would supply dimension-independent control of arbitrarily many normalised linear measurements while preserving exact feasibility and marginals. For graphic matroids, this means distributions over spanning trees with exact edge marginals and bounded errors on the specified measurements.

There are important boundaries. This is not a general cut-thin-tree theorem: including every cut can make the measurement-column norms enormous. It is not an operator-norm sparsification theorem. Nor can arbitrary intersections of matroid constraints be substituted without checking their edge lengths.

The exact-marginal idea itself is not being claimed as new for unconstrained Boolean rounding. Related September work develops laws on good signings and explicitly distinguishes its results from rounding in a single arbitrary matroid base. 

The remaining decisive constructive obligation is:

Replace the compact nonconvex minimisations by a polynomial-bit, oracle-efficient procedure for rational input and a matroid independence oracle, accounting for changing faces, near-binding barriers, small coefficients, numerical precision, and total progress.

The proof provides a strictly positive direction reserve and a local curvature certificate. Those are concrete resources for a constructive attack. They do not establish a polynomial number of numerical steps or affordable access to the required global minima.

A distribution supported on at most 
n
+
1
n+1 good vertices exists, but that compact support is not an algorithm for finding it.
