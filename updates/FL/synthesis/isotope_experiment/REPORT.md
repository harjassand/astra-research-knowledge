# FL — physically obtainable C4 carbon-ancestry experiment

**Status:** A concrete acquisition path exists on the published H-ZSM-5 methanol-to-hydrocarbons (MTH) platform. The published isotope transient does not contain the C4 whole-molecule isotope counts, inlet waveform, or detector covariance needed to calculate the target parity. No experiment was run. Whether parity separates a consequential real mechanism ambiguity at equal dose, time, and accuracy remains **UNKNOWN**.

The target proposed here is the pooled C4H8 butylene product from H-ZSM-5, with every feed carbon entering as one-carbon methanol. This target has an experimentally reported C4 product yield. The result sought is a measured distribution P[k], k=0,...,4, for the number of 13C atoms in an intact C4 product, followed by the even-minus-odd parity q = P[0] - P[1] + P[2] - P[3] + P[4]. Under label-neutral kinetics and conditionally independent feed labels, a four-carbon product with carbon-incorporation times T1,...,T4 has ideal parity E[∏(1 - 2 p13(Tj))], with the product over j=1,...,4. This relation is conditional: the measured inlet waveform, catalyst response history, and product-specific incorporation model determine the expectation. A finite switching edge or dead volume therefore changes the contrast. The scalar can discriminate only pre-specified ancestry models whose predicted response differs under the measured waveform; it does not identify an elementary mechanism by itself.

## What the strongest published transient leaves unresolved

Liu et al. used 100 mg H-ZSM-5 (Si/Al 25, 150–212 μm) at 400 °C and 1 bar. Five pulses of 99 mol% 13C-methanol were used to approach a quasi-steady surface state, followed by 12C-methanol pulses. Each pulse supplied 7.6 μmol of carbon. The interval was either 75 s or 225 s. The online quadrupole MS sampled every 50 ms, but its aliphatic isotope channel was m/z 39–47, described as C3+ fragments. The supporting information explicitly says those fragments contain unknown contributions from a mixture of C3 and larger olefins and paraffins. Its four-component fit uses pure-label pulse responses as references, excludes m/z 40 because of Ar, fixes estimates nonnegative, and assumes the component responses contribute similarly. The authors report that a further refinement could not be extracted.

That leaves a real route-assignment ambiguity for C4 olefins. Prior H-ZSM-5 isotope-switch work supports lower-methylbenzene hydrocarbon-pool involvement in ethene/propene formation alongside a C3+ alkene methylation/cracking cycle. The 2023 paper finds the olefin-based cycle kinetically preferred overall and interprets sequential isotope incorporation in light aromatics as evidence for the paring route. These studies establish relevant route families for light-olefin and aromatic pools, but their pooled C3+ fragment signal does not resolve C4H8-specific carbon ancestry or pathway shares. This is a gap in product-specific evidence, not evidence that mechanisms are equally likely.

There is direct evidence that startup inventory matters. In the first 12C pulse after the five 13C pulses, the article reports 15 at% of the C7 aromatic carbon as fully labelled material displaced from the bed, 48 at% as fully unlabelled material, and 29 at% total 13C in the aromatic response. Thus five preconditioning pulses do not make the old labelled pool negligible. A parity campaign must discard and charge the wash-in transient or estimate its contribution from measured inventory; the exact required wash-in length is not reported.

The published FASPA study can measure C1–C4 product totals quickly, and the isotope paper has much more detailed C7 aromatic isotopologue tracking. Neither produces the needed C4H8 isotopologue counts. In the isotope study the online MS sits before the GC and observes a mixture; the downstream C1–C4 GC channel uses FID, which separates and quantifies products but does not resolve 13C counts. The paper reports selected ion intensities and fitted compositions, not calibrated independent ion counts or a response covariance matrix. The article reports an approximately 1.5 s delay between Ar and hydrocarbon appearance, interpreted as HCP induction; aliphatic products appear later in the pulse response (around 28–36 s), with aromatic responses around 40–60 s. Those are outlet reactor responses, not a measured inlet waveform or a standalone dead-volume calibration.

## Quantitative reanalysis of available isotope results

The SI Table S1 estimates are averages for the fitted C3+ mixture, not C4 product probabilities. I computed the parity q = P[0] - P[1] + P[2] - P[3] and compared it with the independent-label binomial parity (1 - 2x)^3, where x = (P[1] + 2P[2] + 3P[3])/3:

| Methanol pulse interval | fitted (x) | fitted (q) | binomial (q) at same (x) | point residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 75 s | 0.32 | 0.1200 | 0.0467 | 0.0733 |
| 225 s | 0.07 | 0.6600 | 0.6361 | 0.0239 |

The displayed componentwise 95% half-widths are ±0.05–0.07, and the SI gives no parameter covariance. Intersecting those marginal intervals with P[0]+...+P[3]=1 gives only a sensitivity envelope, not a joint confidence interval: q in [-0.12,0.32] at 75 s and q in [0.54,0.78] at 225 s. The article itself describes the fitted distributions as similar to the random-label model. These values do not establish non-random ancestry or a real parity effect.

The product carbon balance shows that C4 material is physically available. Table S2 gives integrated carbon flows of 0.479–0.529 μmolC per pulse for butylenes, equivalent to about 0.120–0.132 μmol of C4 molecules (about 7.2–8.0 × 10^16 molecules) before detector sampling. This is a carbon-balance conversion, not a molecule count at the detector. Butanes have a larger reported C4 output, 1.733–1.805 μmolC per pulse, but C4H8 is the cleaner first route-assignment target. The full calculations and warnings are in [reported_isotope_analysis.json](analysis/reported_isotope_analysis.json), reproduced by [analyze_published_isotopes.py](analysis/analyze_published_isotopes.py).

For scale only, the supplied synthetic E/O ancestry construction predicts ΔQ=0.1161 for a telegraph code at rτ=2. Under an ideal independent-product, known-contrast a=0.98 parity measurement with no detector or catalyst variance, a worst-case normal calculation requires 671 effective product observations per hypothesis for 95% two-sided separation (1,158 at 99%). This is solely a synthetic planning reference. No H-ZSM-5 ΔQ is known, and neither FID yield nor raw MS ion current establishes those effective observations.

## Concrete obtainable interface

The existing interface is suitable for an isotope-coded C4 campaign with a detector modification:

1. Retain the published 4 mm quartz fixed bed, 100 mg H-ZSM-5, 400 °C, 1 bar, 20 mL NTP/min He, and 7.6 μmolC methanol pulses. The feed is single-carbon 12C/13C methanol. The source used two saturators near 18 °C, a roughly 2 mL sample loop, and a four-way valve upstream of that loop to select the isotope stream. Feed each code/control condition the same number of pulses, total methanol carbon, pulse interval, and total run time; use the existing 75 s and 225 s intervals as initial candidate scales.
2. Define the feed boundary at the catalyst-bed inlet. Measure p13(t) and total methanol flow there on a matched bypass, using a fast isotope-sensitive MS/IRMS channel and an inert tracer. Do not infer it from the valve command. The paper gives no measured inlet waveform or line/loop dead-volume response. As an order-of-magnitude check only, sweeping 2 mL at 20 mL/min is 6 s; this is not an observed switching edge.
3. Condition the catalyst under a fixed 12C feed, then run a repeated balanced isotope code until both product isotope distributions and total C4 yield repeat cycle-to-cycle. Measure and charge all startup output; the observed 29 at% 13C aromatic carryover shows that five heavy pulses alone are not a clean start. Monitor total conversion, C4 selectivity, tracer response, and their drift throughout. The source establishes quasi-steady response for its five-pulse switch protocol, not stationarity under a new coded run or immunity to deactivation.
4. Resolve and quantify the pooled C4H8 products with a GC-MS channel on the existing C1–C4 separation (or split the GC effluent between FID and a mass analyser). Measure all five carbon-isotope counts through molecular-ion channels or validated fragments retaining the full carbon skeleton (nominal parent ions m/z 56–60 for C4H8). Calibrate the full response matrix A[m,k], natural-abundance contributions, background, fragmentation/cross-talk, isotope-dependent response, and repeat-injection covariance using known standards and blanks. The current C1–C4 FID gives total species yield only; the current upstream quadrupole's m/z 39–47 mixture cannot substitute.
5. Test isotope neutrality directly. The paper assumes the molar product response composition does not change across the 12C/13C switch, but gives no quantitative carbon-isotope kinetic-effect bound for this catalyst/feed. Compare pure-label and mixed-label states at matched total methanol flow and dose, and test whether conversion, C4 yield, product identity, or code-conditioned selection changes. If it does, the simple ancestry identity needs a calibrated chemistry-response model or fails for this platform.
6. Pre-register the target parity and compare it against a single switch, optimized two/multipulse sequences, and periodic coding at the same total isotope dose, catalyst time, preparation, and achieved C4-count accuracy. Measure independent replicate catalyst runs and the joint covariance of fitted P[k], the inlet waveform, and detector calibration. The raw estimand is q = P[0] - P[1] + P[2] - P[3] + P[4]; correct for measured feed contrast only after testing the label-independence assumptions. A parity difference can resolve a pre-specified pair of ancestry models only if their predicted ΔQ exceeds the measured systematic-plus-statistical error. One scalar cannot uniquely recover the MTH mechanism.

This is a standard, physically obtainable isotope-switch plus GC-MS adaptation, not a built or executed apparatus. The needed calibration standards, inlet response, switch transition, product-count covariance, code-state stationarity, isotope-effect bound, and cost are currently **UNKNOWN**.

## Strongest discriminator and result

The decisive failure in the available evidence is that no P[0]...P[4] distribution exists for the selected C4H8 product under a calibrated multi-boundary code. Published pulse switching constrains aggregate C3+ fragment responses and reports C7 aromatic counts; it does not constrain C4 whole-molecule ancestry. The ideal two-boundary E/O indistinguishability from the inherited synthetic construction does not establish that the real FASPA pulse train is indistinguishable, because its pulse train contains additional feed boundaries and a measured catalyst response history. Conversely, the synthetic coded separation cannot establish that H-ZSM-5 has the assumed E/O histories.

Therefore parity **could** add a higher-order C4 ancestry statistic to this platform, but the present data cannot show that it resolves the olefin-cycle versus aromatic-pool contribution at matched resources. The discriminating experiment is a balanced, inlet-measured C4 code versus the optimized equal-dose multipulse baseline, with intact C4 isotope counts and covariance. Until those measurements exist, the scientific result is **NOT ESTABLISHED** and no new capability or superiority claim is warranted.
