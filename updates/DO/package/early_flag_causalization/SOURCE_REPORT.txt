## Result: input-independent postselection cannot generally be moved before the input

**The strongest result obtained is an exact separation between a quantum operation viewed as a complete block and the same operation required to respect an earlier output deadline. This does not establish the historic objective.**

The separation is substantial enough to rule out a proposed route beyond the previous results:

> An operation can be exactly implementable by LOCC, and its success flag can be statistically independent of every input, while announcing that flag **before receiving the input** requires a positive asymptotic rate of entanglement—even with arbitrarily large, exactly returned catalysts.

The argument also gives an exact simulation-cost identity, not merely a lower bound:
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{early}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N})
=
p\,E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N).
}
\tag{1}
\]

For an appropriate separable channel \(\mathcal N\), the complete block operation \(\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N}\) is LOCC and therefore costs zero entanglement. Its early-flag implementation has strictly positive cost.

The crucial distinction is **statistical independence versus operational availability**. An input-independent outcome need not be available before the input arrives, at the same resource cost.

This is not a claim about backward signalling or indefinite causal order. Both implementations have ordinary, definite time orders; they impose different deadlines.

## 1. The operation and the theorem

Let
\[
\mathcal N:AB\longrightarrow A'B'
\]
be a finite-dimensional bipartite channel, and let \(\mathcal R\) discard its input and prepare a fixed product state.

For \(0<p<1\), define the flagged channel
\[
\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N}(X)
=
p\,|11\rangle\langle11|_{F_AF_B}\otimes\mathcal N(X)
+
(1-p)\,|00\rangle\langle00|_{F_AF_B}\otimes\mathcal R(X).
\tag{2}
\]

Its flag marginal is
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{A'B'}\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N}(X)
=
\operatorname{tr}(X)
\left(
p|11\rangle\langle11|+(1-p)|00\rangle\langle00|
\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the flag distribution is exactly input-independent, including for inputs entangled with a reference.

There are nevertheless two different tasks.

In the **late-flag task**, Alice and Bob receive their quantum inputs and subsequently produce the flag and the remaining outputs.

In the **early-flag task**, they must first issue the common flag. Only afterward do they receive the quantum inputs, which may have been chosen in response to that flag. On flag \(1\), they must apply \(\mathcal N\); on flag \(0\), they apply \(\mathcal R\).

The early-flag task is a two-stage quantum process. Its mathematical consistency follows directly from (3). Its resource cost is another question.

### Theorem — Separation under an early output deadline

Let \(\mathcal N\) be a separable channel outside \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\), and write
\[
D=d_A d_B,\qquad p=\frac1D.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N}\in\mathrm{LOCC}}
\tag{4}
\]
as a late-flag channel, but its early-flag process is not even approximable arbitrarily well by causal LOCC implementations.

Furthermore,
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{early}}(\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N})
=
p\,E_{\mathrm{seq}}(\mathcal N),
}
\tag{5}
\]
and, allowing arbitrary finite-dimensional catalysts returned exactly,
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{early}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal H_{p,\mathcal N})
=
p\,E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N)>0.
}
\tag{6}
\]

For the catalyst-assisted identity, return means preservation of the catalyst’s marginal for **every allowed input interaction** at the end of the complete simulation. Correlations with other retained systems are permitted. There is no prescribed bound on catalyst dimension.

The costs use vanishing error against arbitrary adaptive external experiments. This is the operational sequential-simulation setting, rather than fidelity on one selected input state. The bipartite channel-simulation framework itself is established prior work. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04181)

The positivity in (6) uses the retained sequential-cost classification. The construction and the exact cost identities are proved below.

## 2. Why the complete block operation is LOCC

Write a product-Kraus decomposition
\[
\mathcal N(X)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r}
(A_j\otimes B_j)X(A_j\otimes B_j)^\dagger.
\tag{7}
\]

Discard zero terms, and put
\[
a_j=\|A_j\|_F,\qquad
b_j=\|B_j\|_F,\qquad
\lambda_j=a_j^2b_j^2.
\]

Trace preservation gives
\[
\sum_j A_j^\dagger A_j\otimes B_j^\dagger B_j=I_{AB},
\]
so
\[
\sum_j\lambda_j=D.
\tag{8}
\]

Alice and Bob sample a shared index \(j\) with probability \(\lambda_j/D\). They apply the local success filters
\[
A_j/a_j,\qquad B_j/b_j.
\]
These are valid contractions because the operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm.

If both filters succeed, they announce flag \(1\). Otherwise, they discard their systems, prepare the fixed product output of \(\mathcal R\), and announce flag \(0\).

The success subchannel is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j\frac{\lambda_j}{D}
\left(\frac{A_j}{a_j}\otimes\frac{B_j}{b_j}\right)
X
\left(\frac{A_j}{a_j}\otimes\frac{B_j}{b_j}\right)^\dagger
&=\frac1D\mathcal N(X).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Its success probability is \(1/D\) for every input. After resetting on failure, the failure subchannel is
\[
\left(1-\frac1D\right)\mathcal R.
\]
This proves (4).

The general observation that separable operations can be realized through stochastic LOCC, and its connection with normalization and causal order, has substantial antecedents. The construction is not being presented as a newly discovered equivalence between separability and stochastic implementability. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08835?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Why announcing the flag earlier changes the task

Suppose a causal LOCC implementation issued flag \(1\) before receiving the input.

Conditioned on that flag, all its shared memory would still be separable: it began without entanglement and has used only LOCC. Its subsequent action on the fresh quantum input would therefore be an LOCC channel.

But the required subsequent channel is \(\mathcal N\), which is outside \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\). This is impossible.

The obstruction is also robust. If a sequence of causal LOCC implementations approximated the early-flag process, their probabilities \(q_j\) of flag \(1\) would converge to \(p>0\), and their conditional channels \(\mathcal L_j\) would satisfy
\[
q_j\mathcal L_j\longrightarrow p\mathcal N.
\]
Because the input arrives after the flag, each \(\mathcal L_j\) is a trace-preserving LOCC channel. Dividing by \(q_j\) then gives
\[
\mathcal L_j\longrightarrow\mathcal N,
\]
contradicting \(\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\).

**The same division is not valid operationally for a flag obtained after processing the input.** In that case the normalized successful branch can be separable without being LOCC.

## 3. Exact cost identity: proof by causal routing

The cost identity holds for every channel \(\mathcal N\), not only separable ones.

Let
\[
e_{\mathcal N}(k,m)
\]
be the infimum simulation error for \(k\) sequential uses of \(\mathcal N\), using \(m\) supplied ebits.

Let
\[
e_{\mathcal H}(n,m)
\]
be the corresponding infimum error for \(n\) sequential uses of the early-flag process.

Use either the unassisted definitions throughout or the definitions allowing universally returned exact catalysts throughout.

For
\[
K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p),
\]
the following finite-block inequalities hold:
\[
\boxed{
e_{\mathcal H}(n,m)
\le
e_{\mathcal N}(k,m)+\Pr[K>k],
}
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
e_{\mathcal N}(k,m)
\le
e_{\mathcal H}(n,m)+\Pr[K<k].
}
\tag{11}
\]

These inequalities retain the same entanglement budget. In the catalyst-assisted case, the constructions retain the same catalyst.

### From ordinary channel simulation to the early-flag process

Take a simulator for \(k\) sequential uses of \(\mathcal N\).

For each of the \(n\) requested early-flag calls, sample the flag with probability \(p\) and issue it before accepting that call’s input.

On flag \(0\), apply \(\mathcal R\). On flag \(1\), route the input through the next available call of the \(\mathcal N\)-simulator.

This works unless more than \(k\) successes occur. On that exceptional event, use arbitrary local replacement outputs for the excess calls.

If fewer than \(k\) calls are used, complete the unused calls with dummy product inputs and discard their outputs. This completion matters for an exact catalyst: it ensures that the original simulator reaches its stipulated final return condition.

The sampled flags have exactly the ideal law, independently of the external inputs. Replacing the \(k\)-call simulator by its ideal version changes any external distinguishing probability by at most \(e_{\mathcal N}(k,m)\). With the ideal version, the only possible discrepancy is the overflow event.

This proves (10).

### From the early-flag process to ordinary channel simulation

Now take a simulator for \(n\) early-flag calls and use it to serve \(k\) requests for \(\mathcal N\).

Wait for a flag. If it is \(0\), supply a dummy product input and discard the replacement output. If it is \(1\), supply the next genuine input and return the resulting output as the next \(\mathcal N\)-output.

The genuine input is never submitted to an unsuccessful call.

After serving all \(k\) genuine inputs, complete all remaining early-flag calls with dummy inputs. If the available calls run out before \(k\) successes occur, complete the missing outputs locally.

With the ideal early-flag process, this fails only when \(K<k\). Against an approximate process, the routing procedure is an allowed causal external interaction, so its extra error is at most \(e_{\mathcal H}(n,m)\).

The complete simulated horizon is always finished; hence an exactly returned catalyst is still returned.

This proves (11).

### Passing to rates

For the upper bound, choose
\[
k=\lceil(p+\delta)n\rceil.
\]
The overflow probability in (10) vanishes exponentially. Any achievable channel-simulation rate \(r\) therefore gives an early-process rate at most \((p+\delta)r\).

For the reverse bound, choose
\[
k=\lfloor(p-\delta)n\rfloor.
\]
The shortage probability in (11) also vanishes exponentially. An achievable early-process rate \(R\) gives a channel-simulation rate at most \(R/(p-\delta)\).

Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves (5) and (6).

The proof does not discard unsuccessful executions after they have received genuine inputs. It routes genuine inputs **only after a successful flag has already been issued**. That is why the reduction does not incur the error amplification associated with conditioning on a rare successful output.

### Consequence for exact catalysts

Combining (11) with the retained zero-rate strong converse gives
\[
\boxed{
m_n=o(n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e_{\mathcal H}^{\mathrm{cat}}(n,m_n)\longrightarrow1
}
\tag{12}
\]
whenever \(\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\).

Thus the separation is not merely between zero error and small error. Sublinear supplied entanglement fails maximally in the asymptotic distinguishing metric, despite arbitrary exactly returned catalysts.

The return condition is essential here. The identity is not asserted for catalysts whose return is guaranteed only on one fixed, nonadaptive test ensemble: the routing constructions change the input interaction.

## 4. A concrete example with only classical outputs

An explicit instance uses the nine domino product states in \(\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
|1\rangle|1\rangle,\\
|0\rangle|0\pm1\rangle,\qquad
|2\rangle|1\pm2\rangle,\\
|0\pm1\rangle|2\rangle,\qquad
|1\pm2\rangle|0\rangle,
\end{gathered}
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
|u\pm v\rangle=\frac{|u\rangle\pm|v\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

These states form an orthonormal product basis. Their local indistinguishability originates in the nonlocality-without-entanglement construction of Bennett and coauthors. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9804053)

Write the basis as \(\{|a_jb_j\rangle\}_{j=1}^{9}\), and define
\[
\mathcal M(\rho)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{9}
\langle a_jb_j|\rho|a_jb_j\rangle
\,|jj\rangle\langle jj|.
\tag{14}
\]

Its Kraus operators are product operators:
\[
K_j=
|j\rangle\langle a_j|
\otimes
|j\rangle\langle b_j|.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal M\) is separable.

There is a strictly positive lower bound on the error of LOCC discrimination of this basis, so
\[
\mathcal M\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}.
\]
The robust gap, rather than merely failure of exact discrimination, is established by the state-discrimination analysis of Childs, Leung, Mančinska and Ozols. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5822)

Set \(p=1/9\).

The late-flag implementation is particularly simple. Select a uniformly random basis index \(j\); Alice tests \(|a_j\rangle\), and Bob tests \(|b_j\rangle\). If both tests pass, output flag \(1\) and label \(j\). Otherwise, output flag \(0\) and the fixed replacement label.

For every input \(\rho\),
\[
\Pr[F=1]
=
\frac19\sum_j\langle a_jb_j|\rho|a_jb_j\rangle
=
\frac19.
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore the late-flag channel is exactly LOCC, whereas
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{early}}^{\mathrm{cat}}
\bigl(\mathcal H_{1/9,\mathcal M}\bigr)
=
\frac19 E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal M)>0.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Every external output is classical. Each conditional future-input channel is separable. Starting from separable states, neither the early process nor any causal composition of it with LOCC can generate entanglement.

Nevertheless, implementing the early process requires positive entanglement expenditure.

The complete block channel is LOCC, so its Choi state is separable across the Alice–Bob cut. The early process has the same numerical Choi operator, now equipped with the requirement that the flag precede the quantum input. Its positive implementation cost cannot be inferred from spatial entanglement of that operator.

**The additional resource requirement lies in realizing the stipulated temporal interface.**

## 5. The stronger routes this construction rules out

### A resource-preserving causalization theorem is false

A tempting route to the unrestricted parallel result was:

> If a complete LOCC operation satisfies the no-backward-signalling equations of a causal process, it should admit an LOCC realization respecting that causal order.

The flagged construction is a counterexample.

Equation (3) is exactly the relevant no-backward-signalling condition. Equation (4) supplies an LOCC implementation of the complete channel. But any implementation respecting the earlier flag deadline needs a non-LOCC conditional channel.

This does not contradict the established theorem that semicausal operations are semilocalizable. That theorem permits a quantum system to be transmitted in the causal realization; it does not assert preservation of a zero-entanglement LOCC implementation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0104027?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The gap is therefore not an omitted application of a standard causal-realization theorem. **The unrestricted realization theorem does not preserve the resource restriction needed here.**

### Input-independent success does not justify free normalization

Another proposed bridge was to normalize a postselected branch whenever its success probability is independent of the input.

Here the successful branch satisfies
\[
\mathcal S=p\mathcal N,\qquad
\mathcal S^\dagger(I)=pI.
\tag{17}
\]
It belongs to an LOCC instrument. Its normalized channel \(\mathcal S/p=\mathcal N\) is nevertheless outside \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\).

Thus:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform success probability}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{deterministic LOCC implementation of the normalized branch}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The distinction survives a fixed success probability \(1/9\). It is not caused by arbitrarily rare events or a limiting precision issue.

These two failures are the same obstruction viewed from different directions: **normalizing an outcome obtained after processing the input is not equivalent to selecting a usable operation before receiving that input.**

### This does not refute the original memoryless parallel conjecture

The construction is not a counterexample to
\[
\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}
\quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
E_{\mathrm{par}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N)>0.
\tag{19}
\]

The early-flag object has an additional interaction stage inside each use. Replacing an ordinary memoryless channel by that two-stage process changes the task.

What has been disproved is the **general causalization lemma that could otherwise have been used to infer (19)**. The more restricted statement about arbitrary collective simulations of \(\mathcal N^{\otimes n}\) remains unresolved in this investigation.

## 6. What remains indispensable

A route to (19) must control collective block implementations without assuming that their normalized conditional operations are free.

One sufficient missing result would be a block-level testing theorem: for every
\[
\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}},
\]
construct allowed tests with acceptance events \(A_n\) such that
\[
P_{\mathcal N^{\otimes n}}(A_n)\longrightarrow1
\]
while
\[
\sup_{\mathcal L_n\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}}
P_{\mathcal L_n}(A_n)
\le e^{-c_{\mathcal N}n}
\tag{20}
\]
for some \(c_{\mathcal N}>0\).

The tests would need to remain valid against arbitrary collective LOCC across all \(n\) inputs. Combined with the retained resource-accounting argument, such a separation would rule out sublinear entanglement budgets, including exactly returned catalysts.

**No such general testing theorem is proved here.** In particular, the sequential conditional-probability argument cannot simply be reused: the counterexample above shows why a normalized branch can lie outside LOCC even when its probability is input-independent.

The rank-drop tensor-universality theorem remains intact but does not fill this gap. It concerns existence of exact postselected conversions and finite-dimensional algebra representations. It does not provide a method for issuing success before the input, preserving failed inputs for retry, or implementing the normalized successful branch as a free trace-preserving operation.

Accordingly, the two earlier results do not by themselves establish a new computational capability or an undecidability–decidability dichotomy.

## 7. Originality and completion status

The broad connection between causal restrictions, separable operations and entanglement is **not new**. Akibue, Owari, Kato and Murao explicitly relate the LOCC–separable-operation gap to communication outside a prescribed causal order. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.062331?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Likewise, describing a resource as a channel together with its spacetime interface has established antecedents. Chitambar, Gour, Sengupta and Zibakhsh develop a framework in which input-to-output timing is part of the resource specification. The general assertion that timing affects nonlocal implementation cost cannot be claimed as this investigation’s discovery. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.104.052208?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The precise deductions supplied here are the early-versus-late flag construction, the finite-block routing inequalities (10)–(11), and the resulting exact cost identity with universally returned catalysts. **I have not established that these precise formulations are absent from prior work.**

Their mathematical status is also separate from historical priority: the constructions and reductions are explicit, while the positive-rate conclusion uses the retained sequential-cost theorem. Independent verification of the combined argument remains outstanding.

**The historic mission remains incomplete.** The strongest surviving result is an exact obstruction to resource-preserving causalization: an input-independent flag can conceal a nonfree normalized operation, and requiring that flag before the input can turn a zero-cost block operation into a positive-cost causal process.

That result identifies a substantive failure in a proposed route to the larger theorem. It does not replace the missing collective block obstruction with a weaker result and call the original problem solved.
