# Full-cone quartic rigidity audit and conditional quantitative extension

## Findings

The incoming note's Theorems A–D are structurally coherent under their stated hypotheses after an independent analytic reconstruction. The central hypotheses and boundaries are material:

- Theorem A is a 35-coefficient, homogeneous-quartic result using one indefinite quadratic balance. In the planar application the quadric is determinant. Indefiniteness is needed for the non-strict Finsler lemma. The balance is on the second moment, not a mean-zero assumption. The proof gives Hessian positivity and pointwise cubic closure on the support of a singular averaged Hessian.
- For a rank-one-convex quartic, Theorem B classifies all nine real coefficients in the left SO(2)-invariant quartic space. Its exact exceptional class is polyconvex. Outside that class, every determinant-balanced second moment of rank at least three has positive averaged Hessian.
- For a rank-one-convex quartic, Theorem C proves Jensen on the exactly singular averaged-Hessian face. It uses the exceptional-class result or a covariance range of dimension at most two, followed by the determinant geometry and Fourier compatibility argument. It does not classify nearly singular sequences.
- Theorem D excludes a universal monotonicity rule generated by one fixed, constant-coefficient, uniformly strongly parabolic linear flow. It does not exclude nonlinear, nonlocal, or energy-specific methods.

The package does **not** prove full nine-coefficient rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity, does not settle the positive-definite averaged-Hessian sector, and does not establish uniform control near singular faces. The supplied Fourier optimization and failed certificate searches remain exploratory or representation-scoped. No priority or external-validation claim is made. The analytic reconstruction and provenance details are in `ANALYTIC_AUDIT.md` and `SOURCE_READ_LEDGER.md`.

## Conditional quantitative extension

A further fixed-certificate estimate controls one directional cubic-background component. Let F be a homogeneous quartic on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, and let q be an indefinite quadratic form. Assume
\[
D^2F(Z)[R,R]\ge0\quad(q(R)=0),\qquad \mathbb E q(X)=0.
\]
For a fixed direction A, choose one Finsler decomposition
\[
Q_A(X)=D^2F(A)[X,X]=P(X)+\lambda q(X),\qquad P\succeq0,
\]
and define $H=\mathbb E D^2F(X)$, $b=\mathbb E DF(X)$, $\delta=A^THA$, $U=\ker P$, and $m_j=\mathbb E|X|^j$. If $P=0$, then $U$ is the whole space and the exact indefinite-kernel closure gives $C_A=0$. Otherwise let $\sigma>0$ be the smallest positive eigenvalue of $P$ and set $r=\delta/\sigma$. Quartic reciprocity and balance give
\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(X,U)^2\le r.
\]
Write $C_A(X)=DF(X)[A]$ and let $\kappa$ be the operator norm of its symmetric cubic polarization.

If $q|_U$ is indefinite, the exact closure argument gives $C_A|_U=0$, hence
\[
|b\cdot A|\le7\kappa\sqrt{m_4\delta/\sigma}.
\]
If $q|_U$ is semidefinite, let $U_0=\ker(q|_U)$ and $U_1=U\cap U_0^\perp$. When $U_1\ne0$, let $\mu>0$ be the minimum of $|q|$ on its unit sphere, and let $\|Q\|$ be the operator norm of the matrix of $q$. Then
\[
s:=\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(X,U_0)^2\le r+\frac{\|Q\|}{\mu}(2\sqrt{m_2r}+r),\qquad |b\cdot A|\le4\kappa\sqrt{m_4s}.
\]
If $U_1=0$, use $s\le r$. For $r\le1$, a fourth-moment bound alone yields an $O(r^{1/4})$ directional estimate in the semidefinite case; bounded support improves it to $O(r^{1/2})$. All constants depend on the chosen decomposition and direction. This is a local estimate for one component, not a bound on the full Schur remainder and not a uniform theorem as the Finsler gap degenerates. The full derivation is in `QUANTITATIVE_NULLSPACE_STABILITY.md`.

A two-atom rational sequence verifies an exact fourth-moment identity and a uniform bound for a **reduced** balance/vanishing model. It fails the quartic integration identity $D_A C_A=Q_A$, so it is not an exponent-sharpness example for actual quartics. Optimality under full quartic compatibility, and in particular for the planar ROC cone, remains unknown. The script checks the coefficient mismatch explicitly; its arithmetic check is not proof of the analytic extension.

## Related strict-margin consequence

As a separate composition of Theorem A, suppose $F-\tau N$ is rank-one convex, where $N(A)=|A|^4$ and $\tau>0$. For a determinant-balanced law, Theorem A applied to $F-\tau N$ gives
\[
H_F\succeq\tau\,\mathbb E D^2N(X)
=\tau(8\mathbb E[XX^T]+4m_2I)\succeq4\tau m_2I.
\]
If the coefficient ℓ1 norm of F in the 35 standard row-major quartic monomials is at most K, then 
\[
\|b\|\le4K\,\mathbb E|X|^3\le4K\sqrt{m_2m_4},\qquad
\|H_F^{-1}b\|\le\frac K\tau\sqrt{m_4/m_2}\quad(m_2>0).
\]
This controls the associated linear solve at fixed strict margin and moment ratio. It does not control Fourier complexity, the full nonlinear background minimization problem without additional argument, or limits as \(\tau\downarrow0\) or $m_2\downarrow0$.
