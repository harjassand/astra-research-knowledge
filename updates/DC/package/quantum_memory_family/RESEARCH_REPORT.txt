5. Consequences and strongest falsification
The strongest falsification is of the unrestricted generalization. Even imposing closedness, convexity and arbitrary CP-filter closure does not upgrade individual finite lifetimes into a common finite lifetime. Equation (2) is what makes that falsification survive the most dangerous operation: taking normalized limits after nearly singular filters.

The failure is not caused by an identity channel hidden in the closure. The seed limit is complete dephasing, which is EB. Every map in the constructed cone still eventually becomes EB.

No fixed finite sample detects the full obstruction. Every finite selection from this particular cone has a common filtered-word bound: EB factors already terminate entanglement, and finitely many positive-definite Choi matrices have uniform positive lower bounds. The failure occurs across the entire family approaching the flat boundary.

The positive argument retains the intended arbitrary-composition strength. It does not require stationarity, commutativity, a shared invariant state or trace preservation. It remains restricted to serial operations on a fixed transmitted dimension.

The reconstructed argument also retains N574’s proposed dimension-dependent uniform PPT-word consequence. That consequence was already in the repository; it is not a new priority claim from this session. This work does not prove that two PPT factors suffice.

6. Prior art and exact remaining obligations
Pointwise eventual EB and filtered EB indices are established research subjects. Rahaman–Jaques–Paulsen distinguish pointwise finite indices from a uniform bound in their discussion, while Christandl–Müller-Hermes–Wolf develop a different composition mechanism through Schmidt-number iteration. 

The current comparison also includes the September 2026 preprint by Bhat, Dey and Saha claiming a complete pointwise characterization for unital channels. Its stated target is different from a common length across an entire closed filter-stable cone. I have not treated that preprint as independently certified. 

The focused search did not identify the exact counterexample or the proposed quantum characterization of polynomial boundedness. That is not a novelty certificate.

Three obligations remain decisive:

Independent correctness review. The positive argument needs reconstruction at its rectangular-corner, normalized-boundary and two-sided-anchor interfaces. The counterexample needs independent checking of the entire-cone positive-definite-or-separable classification—not just the seed channels. Written arguments are supplied for each; I have not knowingly deferred an indispensable lemma.

External priority verification. The nearest additional targets are local-filter-invariant cones of two-qubit states, nonuniform EB indices and applications of o-minimal growth to positive operator semigroups. The pre-existing N574 machinery must remain separately attributed.

Consequences commensurate with the historic objective. No practical uniform exponent, PPT-square theorem, tensor-power distillability result or comparable foundational consequence has been established here. The sharp classification does not by itself justify a 9–10/10 rating.

The counterexample can be assessed independently of the longer positive theorem. A defect in the reconstructed N574 extension would not automatically invalidate the explicit closed-cone construction.

1. The operational model matters
A logical qubit starts maximally entangled with an untouched reference. Every quantum register persisting across a noise round must pass through that round’s noise. Between rounds, the protocol may use arbitrary joint gates, measurements, reset, fresh unentangled ancillas, classical randomness and classical feed-forward. Classical memory is unrestricted.

There is no noiseless quantum bypass, access to environmental error labels, operation on the reference, or uncounted entangled resource. The noise must have its stated channel action even on correlated inputs.

At the end, the protocol outputs a logical qubit and a success flag. Its total success probability 
p
p includes every selected event, starting with the encoder. Conditional on success, 
F
F is the output’s overlap with the original Bell state.

For an arbitrary heralded operation, this is a conditional Bell-state test—not automatically a worst-case guarantee after an input-dependent normalization. The explicit construction below has input-independent success and a depolarizing conditional channel, so it also provides a channel-error guarantee.

These permissions are substantial, but they do not model noisy control gates. The conclusions are not a general impossibility theorem for weak-noise fault-tolerant quantum computation.

1. Exact scope of the law
The logical qubit starts maximally entangled with an untouched reference. Every quantum register that persists across a counted round must undergo that round’s noise. Between rounds, arbitrary ideal joint gates, measurements, reset, fresh unentangled ancillas, classical randomness and classical feed-forward are allowed.

There is no noiseless quantum bypass, access to environmental error labels, operation on the reference, or uncounted entangled resource. The success probability includes the encoder and every selected event.

For a fixed width schedule, (1) covers arbitrary adaptive controls and postselection with any strictly positive success probability. For history-dependent widths, the theorem has a branchwise and mixture form; substituting average widths directly into (1) would be unjustified.

The fidelity is a conditional Bell-state test. For a general input-dependent successful operation, it is not automatically a worst-case normalized-channel guarantee. The matching construction discussed below has input-independent success and does provide a channel guarantee.

7. Evidence, novelty boundary and handoff
The final exact verifier includes:

Check	Executed scope
Serial sandwiches with changing widths and filters	24 protocols, including 12 complex-rational cases
Positive-semidefinite certificates for those protocols	1,440 principal minors
Width-profile optimization	3,002 integer profiles across 50 budgets
Deadline-free construction	192 finite-prefix checks
Erasure stopping distributions	24 exact cases
Rank-deficient examples	64 dephasing witnesses, 24 repetition-code cases, 16 dual-rail matrix-unit checks
Tensor-comparison eigenvalue classes, the numerical example, support calculations, a noiseless-bypass counterexample and a positive-but-not-CP order counterexample are also checked.

The verifier uses exact integer and rational arithmetic, including complex rational matrices. Two completed executions matched byte for byte. The prior memory-resource verifier was rerun and matched its archived output.

These checks do not replace the all-size proofs or independent review. The main review targets are the complete-cut condition, preservation of CP order under the residual expansion, normalization in the fidelity comparison, adaptive cost definitions, and terminal-slot accounting in the stopping theorem.

The strongest defensible advance is the cumulative, postselection-resistant accuracy law and its support-based extension beyond finite entanglement-breaking time. The stopping theorem additionally determines a full achievable reliability region for routing erasure. Neither a major open-problem resolution nor a historic-significance rating has been established.
