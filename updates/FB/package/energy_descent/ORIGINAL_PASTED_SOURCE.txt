I derived a candidate operation that repairs interference-driven heating in a quantum cooling channel. It works at the level of the full finite-dimensional quantum dynamics, not a population-only or weak-hopping approximation. Its correction remains nonsingular when two downward transition energies approach one another.
The result has three parts: an exact energy-descent identity, a sharp lower bound on the coherent correction required in a nontrivial family, and a construction reducing the required operators to native time evolution and additional controls.
However, two findings prevent a breakthrough claim. The underlying correction kernel has close published precedents, and explicit counterexamples show that the resulting dynamics can prepare states arbitrarily slowly—or remain permanently trapped above the ground state. Historical novelty, practical implementation on a consequential material, and superiority over the strongest existing methods are not established.

I initially screened three different kinds of scientific operation rather than inheriting Astra’s previous transport representation.
The selected scientific goal was preparing useful low-energy states of interacting quantum systems without first learning their complete eigenstructure.
That is not an empty field. Existing work already provides filtered-jump preparation algorithms, rigorous rapid-mixing results for certain weakly interacting systems, and model-agnostic cooling proposals tested on strongly interacting fermionic models. The unresolved comparison is therefore against those methods and available direct controls—not against full Hamiltonian tomography followed by an unnecessarily expensive controller. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15676)
Let \(H\) be a Hamiltonian and \(B\) a quantum jump operator. Its dissipative action is
\[ \mathcal D[B](\rho) = B\rho B^\dagger-\frac12\{B^\dagger B,\rho\}. \]
Suppose every nonzero matrix element of \(B\) connects a higher-energy state to a lower-energy state.
One might conclude that \(\mathcal D[B]\) cannot increase energy. That conclusion is false for coherent inputs.
Consider
\[ H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,4), \qquad B=|0\rangle\langle1|+|0\rangle\langle2|. \]
Both transitions point toward the ground state. Nevertheless, the operator determining the instantaneous energy change is
\[ \mathcal D[B]^*(H) = \begin{pmatrix} 0&0&0\\ 0&-1&-\frac52\\ 0&-\frac52&-4 \end{pmatrix}. \]
Its largest eigenvalue is
\[ \frac{\sqrt{34}-5}{2}>0. \]
For the particularly simple state
\[ |\psi\rangle = \frac{-3|1\rangle+|2\rangle}{\sqrt{10}}, \]
the energy derivative is exactly
\[ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dt}\langle H\rangle=\frac15>0. } \]
This is not heating caused by an accidentally included upward transition. It comes from interference in the full dissipative evolution.
That distinction matters: filtering out upward frequencies is not, by itself, a complete energy-safety specification.
The repair is to retain the downward jump and add a particular coherent operation.
Write
\[ H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle, \]
and suppose
\[ B_{mn}=0\quad\text{unless }E_n>E_m. \]
Define
\[ C_{mn} = \sqrt{E_n-E_m}\,B_{mn} \]
on downward entries, and zero elsewhere.
Now define the Hermitian correction
\[ \boxed{ G_{nn'} = \frac{i}{2} \sum_{m:\,E_m<\min(E_n,E_{n'})} \frac{ \sqrt{E_n-E_m}-\sqrt{E_{n'}-E_m} }{ \sqrt{E_n-E_m}+\sqrt{E_{n'}-E_m} } B_{mn}^{*}B_{mn'}. } \]
The proposed generator is
\[ \mathcal L(\rho) = -i[H+G,\rho]+\mathcal D[B](\rho). \]
Then
\[ \boxed{ \mathcal L^*(H)=-C^\dagger C. } \]
Consequently, for every density matrix,
\[ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Tr}(H\rho_t) = -\operatorname{Tr}(C^\dagger C\rho_t) \le 0. } \]
The identity includes arbitrary coherences and arbitrary interactions inside \(H\). It does not assume a population equation, a known ground-state energy, a supplied spectral gap, or an initial overlap with the ground state.
Every ground-state density matrix is stationary. Multiple accessible jumps can be handled by summing their corrections and their \(C_a^\dagger C_a\) terms.
The eigenbasis is used here to state and prove the identity. It is not assumed to be available to an experimental controller; operator acquisition is addressed below.
Fix a common destination \(m\), and put
\[ x=E_n-E_m>0,\qquad y=E_{n'}-E_m>0. \]
Its contribution to the dissipative energy drift is
\[ -\frac{x+y}{2}\,B_{mn}^{*}B_{mn'}. \]
The coherent correction contributes
\[ i(E_{n'}-E_n)G_{nn'} = \left(\frac{x+y}{2}-\sqrt{xy}\right) B_{mn}^{*}B_{mn'}. \]
Adding them leaves
\[ -\sqrt{xy}\,B_{mn}^{*}B_{mn'}. \]
Summing over \(m\) gives exactly \(-C^\dagger C\).
The construction replaces an arithmetic-mean interference coefficient by a geometric-mean coefficient that factors into a negative square.
For \(H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,4)\) and the same \(B\),
\[ G_{12}=-\frac{i}{6}, \qquad G_{21}=\frac{i}{6}, \]
and
\[ C=|0\rangle\langle1|+2|0\rangle\langle2|. \]
On the same input state,
\[ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dt}\langle H\rangle: \quad +\frac15 \longrightarrow -\frac1{10}. } \]
Thus the correction changes actual energy increase into energy decrease without splitting the jump into independently resolved transitions.
The correction is not merely one arbitrary repair.
Consider a star-shaped transition system:
\[ H=\operatorname{diag}(0,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N), \qquad B=\sum_i b_i|0\rangle\langle i|, \]
where every \(\omega_i>0\).
For any Hermitian \(G\) making the mean-energy drift nonpositive for every state, a two-dimensional principal-minor argument gives, whenever \(\omega_i\ne\omega_j\),
\[ \boxed{ |G_{ij}| \ge \frac{ |\sqrt{\omega_i}-\sqrt{\omega_j}| }{ 2(\sqrt{\omega_i}+\sqrt{\omega_j}) } |b_i b_j|. } \]
The construction above attains every one of these bounds simultaneously.
This is an entrywise optimality result for repairing a fixed collective jump. It is not a claim of minimum total control energy, minimum operator norm, minimum circuit cost, or fastest preparation.
The important feature is the absence of a near-degeneracy divergence:
\[ \frac{ |\sqrt{\omega_i}-\sqrt{\omega_j}| }{ 2(\sqrt{\omega_i}+\sqrt{\omega_j}) } \longrightarrow 0 \quad\text{as }\omega_i\to\omega_j>0. \]
A naive correction that cancels every off-diagonal energy-drift term would involve division by \(\omega_i-\omega_j\). The derived repair does not need that cancellation and avoids its apparent singularity.
Qualification: bounded individual coefficients do not automatically make the full many-body operator \(G\) cheap. The number of contributions, their operator norm and their physical synthesis costs still matter.
I also derived an alternative using correlated dissipative channels. Its sharp correlation factor is
\[ K(\omega,\nu) = \frac{2\sqrt{\omega\nu}}{\omega+\nu} \]
for unit diagonal transition rates.
There is a precise limitation. For a universal reservoir-only construction on \(N\) distinct positive frequencies, with every diagonal rate nonzero, exact all-state energy descent requires the frequency-correlation matrix to have rank \(N\). The proof is included in the research note.
This is a rank requirement on the dissipative representation—not a requirement for \(N\) simultaneous laboratory reservoirs. Nevertheless, it shows why a formal continuum of correlated jumps can conceal a resource requirement.
The coherent completion avoids that rank obstruction by using a different resource: an additional Hamiltonian operation. Whether that exchange is advantageous is a physical and computational question, not settled by the identity.
This is where the mathematical result must be separated from a working scientific apparatus.
I obtained a synthesis reduction for a controllable quantum register. I did not obtain a demonstrated implementation for an arbitrary crystal, molecule or reactor.
Suppose \(A\) is a known accessible seed operation. Choose a smooth scalar filter \(b(\omega)\) supported on positive energy drops, above a chosen cutoff \(\delta>0\).
Let \(A_\omega\) denote its components satisfying
\[ [H,A_\omega]=-\omega A_\omega. \]
Then the desired jump is
\[ B=\sum_{\omega>0}b(\omega)A_\omega. \]
It has a real-time representation
\[ B = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \widehat b(t)\, e^{iHt}A e^{-iHt}\,dt. \]
Thus the operator can be specified through native time evolution and a known seed, rather than by supplying all eigenvectors and transition matrix elements.
Time-domain acquisition of filtered operators is already part of established dissipative preparation algorithms. It is not a new contribution of this investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15676)
Define
\[ F_s = \sum_{\omega>0} b(\omega)e^{-s\sqrt{\omega}}A_\omega, \]
and
\[ D_s = \sum_{\omega>0} \sqrt{\omega}\,b(\omega)e^{-s\sqrt{\omega}}A_\omega. \]
The correction has the exact representation
\[ \boxed{ G = \frac{i}{2} \int_0^\infty \left(D_s^\dagger F_s-F_s^\dagger D_s\right)\,ds. } \]
Both \(F_s\) and \(D_s\) admit real-time Fourier representations. The expression therefore does not require an apparatus capable of directly applying imaginary-time evolution.
A Hermitian ancilla dilation,
\[ X_{\mathrm{dil}} = \begin{pmatrix} 0&X^\dagger\\ X&0 \end{pmatrix}, \]
allows products and commutators of these acquired operators to be synthesized. In particular, the upper ancilla block of
\[ \frac{i}{2}[D_{\mathrm{dil}},F_{\mathrm{dil}}] \]
is the required integrand.
This provides a constructive reduction, but not evidence that its complete pulse sequence is affordable.
A successful filtered Kraus operator alone would not suffice: discarded failure outcomes could invalidate the claimed cooling.
Instead, initialize an ancilla in \(|0\rangle\), apply
\[ \exp\!\left(-i\sqrt{dt}\,B_{\mathrm{dil}}\right), \]
and discard/reset the ancilla without selecting an outcome. The system channel is
\[ \rho \mapsto \rho+dt\,\mathcal D[B](\rho) +O\!\left(dt^2\|B\|^4\right). \]
Both outcomes remain in the physical evolution. There is no uncharged success probability.
The Fourier construction uses forward and inverse native dynamics. Inverse evolution cannot simply be assumed for an unknown material.
On a register with local Pauli controls and a known candidate set of \(K\) Hamiltonian terms, I supplied an explicit randomized conjugation distribution satisfying
\[ \mathbb E_P[PHP]=-\frac{H}{K} \]
for the traceless Hamiltonian. It acquires inverse evolution at a native-time overhead \(K\), with additional finite-step and pulse errors.
Higher-order transformations of unknown Hamiltonians, including inverse evolution, are already established research directions; they must be credited and charged, not presented as newly invented access. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09788)
The interface distinction is decisive: a quantum register with individually controlled qubits may support these constructions. A material with one voltage electrode or one optical coupling does not thereby acquire them.
For a compactly supported smooth filter \(f\),
\[ \int_{|t|>T}|\widehat f(t)|\,dt \le \frac{\|f''\|_1}{\pi T}. \]
This gives a dimension-independent operator-error bound after multiplying by the accessible seed norm. The relevant derivative norms still depend on bandwidth, cutoff and precision.
Moreover, a finite-duration scalar filter cannot vanish on a whole interval of upward frequencies while remaining nonzero elsewhere: its frequency response is analytic. Exact one-sided filtering over an unknown spectrum is therefore an ideal limit of this construction.
If the implemented generator has error at most \(\eta\) in diamond norm, and the Hamiltonian bandwidth is \(W\), the guarantee becomes
\[ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dt}\langle H\rangle \le -\operatorname{Tr}(C^\dagger C\rho) +\frac{W\eta}{2}. } \]
Near a state with very little cooling margin, this does not guarantee strict nonheating.
The complete cost also includes coherent evolution during the filters, inverse-evolution overhead, control amplitudes and pulse durations, ancilla reset errors, and reset and drive work. I have not obtained measured values or an end-to-end resource estimate for a consequential material. No refrigerator-efficiency claim follows from the decrease in system energy.
The construction survives the algebraic heating objection. It does not survive a claim of general efficient preparation.
Consider
\[ H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1+\epsilon), \qquad B=|0\rangle\langle1|+|0\rangle\langle2|, \]
with unit individual population-decay rates.
For the coherently completed generator, the slow survival-probability decay exponent is
\[ \gamma(\epsilon) = \frac{17}{32}\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3). \]
Thus preparation can require time of order
\[ \epsilon^{-2} \]
at these fixed rates.
Crucially, the gap between the ground state and the first excited state is exactly \(1\) throughout this family. The slowdown comes from nearly indistinguishable excited-state transitions and their interference, not a closing ground-state gap.
The correction remains nonsingular, and its acquisition formulas do not require separating every pair of downward frequencies. Yet the difficult spectral scale reappears in the preparation dynamics.
This is a counterexample to preparation-speed claims for the proposed generator—not a lower bound against every optimized controller.
Consider the periodic spin chain
\[ H = -J\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+1} -h\sum_i Z_i, \qquad J,h>0. \]
The all-down state lies above the all-up ground state by
\[ 2hn. \]
Flipping any nonempty subset of at most \(k<n\) spins costs at least
\[ 4J-2hk. \]
Choose \(hk<2J\). Then every \(k\)-local seed, after downward filtering, annihilates the all-down state. Its coherent completion also annihilates that state.
Consequently, any combination of these repaired local lowering operations leaves an excited state permanently stationary.
This is not merely an exact-ground-state technicality. At fixed \(h>0\), the trapped state has a nonvanishing excess energy density \(2h\).
For the explicit six-spin instance
\[ J=1,\qquad h=0.2,\qquad k=2, \]
I checked all 153 nonidentity Pauli seeds of weight at most two. Every filtered seed annihilates the trap, whose energy is \(-4.8\), compared with ground energy \(-7.2\).
A stronger direct controller also exposes the weakness of the comparison: on this known example, applying \(X\) to every spin maps all-down directly to the ground state. A rich-control implementation cannot fairly advertise its elaborate filtered dynamics while excluding this available direct operation from the benchmark.
Repairing the energy sign does not create missing barrier-crossing transitions.
There are two distinct questions: whether the operation has a mathematically useful property, and whether that property constitutes new scientific power.
The first has a concrete answer here. The exact completion repairs coherent heating, and the star-family bound establishes how much pairwise coherent actuation is unavoidable for that repair.
The second remains unresolved—and the available evidence is restrictive.
Published quantum Gibbs samplers already use coherent corrections derived from square-root/Sylvester expressions closely related to the kernel appearing here. They also address efficient simulation without fully resolving every Bohr frequency. Therefore, neither “add a coherent correction” nor “use native time evolution instead of diagonalizing the Hamiltonian” is a defensible novelty claim. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05998v4)
The potentially distinct contribution is the energy-drop-specific completion for arbitrary strictly lowering jumps, together with its sharp entrywise actuation statement. I have not established whether that combination is already implicit in the existing frameworks.
Against direct controls, the example is also unfavorable. On the three-level heating fixture, two independent downward channels with the same individual unit decay rates give
\[ \frac{d}{dt}\langle H\rangle=-\frac{13}{10}, \]
whereas the repaired collective channel gives
\[ -\frac1{10} \]
on the selected input. This is an instantaneous comparison, not a complete implementation-cost comparison, but it rules out interpreting the repair itself as a generic performance improvement.
Against current many-body preparation algorithms, I established no new rapid-mixing class. Existing work already proves rapid preparation for particular weakly interacting systems and provides substantial numerical investigations beyond them. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15827)
The reproducibility package contains the proofs, the physical-access reduction, the counterexamples and executed diagnostics.
The numerical checks are diagnostics, not independent validation of the proofs or evidence of physical feasibility.
This investigation produced an explicit candidate operation and a sharp design constraint, but it did not meet the civilisation-changing completion standard.
The substantive change in understanding is the separation of three problems that are easy to conflate:
\[ \boxed{ \text{nonpositive energy drift} \;\ne\; \text{obtainable physical actuation} \;\ne\; \text{useful preparation speed}. } \]
The first can be repaired exactly without a singular correction at nearby downward frequencies. The second can be reduced to known primitives on a sufficiently controllable register, but has not been acquired for a consequential material. The third fails on explicit, elementary examples—even with a fixed ground-state gap.
The missing scientific operation is therefore more specific than “better cooling”: an experimentally obtainable intervention that creates quantitatively useful escape from excited, weakly connected or metastable subspaces, without assuming the needed many-body transitions are already available. Reweighting, filtering or coherently repairing the same downward moves does not supply that capability.
