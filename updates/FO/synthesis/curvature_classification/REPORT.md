# Exact profile-space curvature span for two amplitudes

## Result and scope

For two profile amplitudes and degree-four polynomial energies, the six signed
area moments are the exact universal coordinates for cancelling the profile
circulation across all profiles, affine backgrounds, and globally convex
homogeneous quartics. The curvature polynomials actually generated by exact
Fourier profiles and a pair of globally convex quartics span all of
\(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^2)\), with an exact nonzero determinant.

That universal statement does not make all six conditions necessary for a
fixed profile. For the scalar target profile pair
\((\psi_1,\psi_2)=(\cos y,\sin 3y)\), curvature is always a multiple of
\(r^2\); the single condition \(M_{20}=0\) cancels it for every scalar
quartic and every background. An explicit loop has \(M_{20}=0\) while
\(M_{00}=3/4\). Positivity is weaker still: a strictly convex quartic has a
positive Jensen gap with a nonzero circulation equal to \(-8\).

The result classifies this signed term in the supplied profile identity. It
does not prove a new Jensen inequality for arbitrary gradients, complete the
N794 moment cone, or close the N812 connected-gradient gate. External

## Curvature and the six universal moments

Let \(\psi_1,\psi_2:\mathbb T\to\mathbb R^m\) be smooth periodic profiles,
\(a=(r,s)\) a closed amplitude path, and

\[
  \phi(x,y)=r(x)\psi_1(y)+s(x)\psi_2(y),\qquad
  Q=(0\mid r\psi_1'+s\psi_2').
\]

For a polynomial \(F:\mathbb R^{m\times2}\to\mathbb R\) and a constant
background \(B\), use the profile one-form

\[
 \alpha_r(a)=\mathbb E_y DF(B+Q)[(\psi_1\mid0)],\qquad
 \alpha_s(a)=\mathbb E_y DF(B+Q)[(\psi_2\mid0)].
\]

Its scalar curvature is

\[
 K_{F,B,\psi}(r,s)=\partial_r\alpha_s-\partial_s\alpha_r
 =\mathbb E_y\!\left(
 D^2F(B+Q)[(0\mid\psi_1'),(\psi_2\mid0)]
 -D^2F(B+Q)[(0\mid\psi_2'),(\psi_1\mid0)]\right).
\]

If \(\deg F\le4\), then \(\deg K\le2\). Green's theorem with winding
multiplicity gives

\[
 \oint_a\alpha=\int_{\mathbb R^2} w_a(r,s)K(r,s)\,dr\,ds
 =K_{00}M_{00}+K_{10}M_{10}+K_{01}M_{01}
  +K_{20}M_{20}+K_{11}M_{11}+K_{02}M_{02},
\]

where \(K=K_{00}+K_{10}r+K_{01}s+K_{20}r^2+K_{11}rs+K_{02}s^2\) and
\[
 M_{ij}=\int_{\mathbb R^2}w_a(r,s)r^is^j\,dr\,ds,\qquad i+j\le2.
\]

Thus the six moments are sufficient. The supplied source uses this cancellation
inside an identity whose other two terms are nonnegative rank-one Bregman and
acoustic remainders; our result below settles the converse for universal
cancellation, not the independent validity of that source identity.

## Exact attainable-span proof

Write \(P(A)=A_{11}A_{12}^3\), \(G(A)=\lVert A\rVert_F^4\), and
\[
 F_\pm=G\pm\tfrac14P.
\]
These are globally convex homogeneous quartics on every \(\mathbb R^{m\times2}\).
Indeed, for any matrix direction \(H\), put
\(x=A_{11}, y=A_{12}, u=H_{11}, v=H_{12}\). Then
\[
 D^2P(A)[H,H]=6y^2uv+6xyv^2,
\]
so
\[
 |D^2P(A)[H,H]|
 \le 3y^2(u^2+v^2)+3(x^2+y^2)v^2
 \le 6\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2.
\]
Meanwhile
\[
 D^2G(A)[H,H]=4\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2
   +8\langle A,H\rangle^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 D^2F_\pm(A)[H,H]\ge\tfrac52\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2\ge0.
\]
Convexity at the origin follows by continuity of the Hessian. In particular,
these witnesses satisfy the stronger global-convexity condition, not just
rank-one convexity.

Take \(B_{11}=0, B_{12}=1\), all other entries zero, and profiles supported
in the first output coordinate: \(\psi_1=f e_1,\psi_2=g e_1\). For the
perturbation \(P\),
\[
 K_P(r,s)=3\mathbb E_y[(gf'-fg')(1+rf'+sg')^2].
\]
The following six finite Fourier pairs give the displayed curvature
polynomials. Each is the difference of the actual curvatures for \(F_+\) and
\(F_-\), multiplied by 2; hence they lie in the linear span of curvatures
actually attained by globally convex quartics.

| \(f(y)\) | \(g(y)\) | \(K_P(r,s)\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(\cos y\) | \(\sin 3y\) | \(\tfrac32r^2\) |
| \(\cos 3y\) | \(\sin y\) | \(-\tfrac32s^2\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\cos 2y\) | \(-\tfrac92r\) |
| \(\cos 2y\) | \(\cos y\) | \(\tfrac92s\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\sin y\) | \(-3-\tfrac32r^2-\tfrac32s^2\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\cos y+\sin y\) | \(-3-\tfrac32r^2-3rs-3s^2\) |

In the ordered coefficient basis \((1,r,s,r^2,rs,s^2)\), the exact
coefficient matrix has determinant \(-6561/16\). Its rank is six, so the
attainable curvature span is all of \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^2)\).

Consequently, for any fixed closed amplitude loop, its circulation vanishes
for every admissible \(F\), every profile pair, and every background if and
only if all six \(M_{ij}\) vanish. For necessity, if every actual \(F_+\) and
\(F_-\) circulation is zero, their difference annihilates each row in the
table; invertibility then forces every moment to be zero. This proves that no
lower-rank linear compression of the six moments is a complete universal
zero-circulation certificate. It also shows why checking only \(B=0\) would
miss part of the answer: for a homogeneous quartic at \(B=0\), \(K\) is
homogeneous quadratic and only \(M_{20},M_{11},M_{02}\) are tested; nonzero
backgrounds produce the constant and linear curvature terms too.

## Strictly weaker fixed-profile certificate

Now fix the scalar target pair \(f=\cos y, g=\sin 3y\) and put
\(c=gf'-fg'\), \(R=rf'+sg'\). Exact Fourier averaging gives
\[
 (\mathbb E c,\mathbb E cf',\mathbb E cg',
   \mathbb E c(f')^2,\mathbb E cf'g',\mathbb E c(g')^2)
 =(0,0,0,\tfrac12,0,0).
\]
For any scalar polynomial quartic \(F(x,y)\),
\(F_{xy}(B_1,B_2+R)\) is a polynomial of degree at most two in \(R\).
The curvature is its average against \(c\), so the displayed identities give
\[
 K_{F,B,\psi}(r,s)=\tfrac12\lambda(F,B)r^2,
\]
where \(\lambda(F,B)\) is the coefficient of \(y^2\) in
\(F_{xy}(B_1,y)\). Thus \(M_{20}=0\) is necessary and sufficient for
cancellation for this fixed profile, all scalar quartics, and all backgrounds.
It is genuinely weaker than six-moment cancellation; the convex witnesses
above give nonzero \(\lambda\).

An explicit closed current satisfying the weaker condition is the sum of:

* a counterclockwise rectangle \([-1/2,1/2]\times[0,1]\), and
* a clockwise rectangle \([-1,1]\times[0,1/8]\).

Joining their base points by connectors and their exact reversals makes one
closed path without changing its winding current. Its exact moment vector is
\[
 (M_{00},M_{10},M_{01},M_{20},M_{11},M_{02})
 =\left(\tfrac34,0,\tfrac{31}{64},0,0,\tfrac{85}{256}\right).
\]
So the fixed-profile curvature cancels while three other area moments are
nonzero. The profile-specific conclusion is limited to this scalar target and
this pair; it is not a universal replacement for the six conditions.

## Positivity with nonzero circulation

Let \(F(x,y)=(x^2+y^2)^2\), \(\psi_1=\cos y\), \(\psi_2=\sin y\),
and let \((r,s)\) traverse the counterclockwise unit square with unit width
on each of its four edges. At \(B=(1,1)\),
\(F_{xy}=8xy\) and \(gf'-fg'=-1\), so the curvature is the constant \(-8\).
The square has \(M_{00}=1\), hence its profile circulation is exactly \(-8\).

The compatible potential is \(\phi(x,y)=r(x)\cos y+s(x)\sin y\). Exact
normalized integration of \(F(B+D\phi)\) over the four strips gives
edge integrals \(148/15,1349/120,1349/120,148/15\), total
\(2533/60\). Dividing by the period 4 gives mean energy \(2533/240\), while
\(F(B)=4\). The Jensen gap is therefore \(1573/240>0\) despite the nonzero
negative circulation. Convexity also gives positivity for every constant
background. Thus circulation cancellation is a sufficient route to
positivity through the supplied identity, not a necessary condition for
positivity itself.

## Degree, dimension, and gauge

For \(d\) amplitude coordinates and a polynomial energy of degree at most
\(p\), the profile one-form has polynomial coefficients of degree at most
\(p-1\); its curvature is an exact, hence closed, polynomial 2-form with
coefficient degree at most \(p-2\). Pairings with all component moments
\(M_{ij,\beta}=\int_\Sigma a^\beta\,da_i\wedge da_j\),
\(i<j,|\beta|\le p-2\), are a sufficient finite cancellation certificate.
In two amplitude dimensions every polynomial 2-form is closed, so the exact
universal count is
\(\binom{2+p-2}{2}=\binom p2\); degree four gives six.
Equivalently, degree-at-most-three polynomial 1-forms in two variables have
20 coefficients, but the 14-dimensional space of exact forms
\(dG\), \(\deg G\le4\), has zero loop integral; the quotient has dimension 6.

For the abstract class of all polynomial 1-forms, the minimal curvature
space in \(d\) amplitude dimensions has dimension
\[
 d\binom{d+p-1}{d}-\binom{d+p}{d}+1.
\]
Indeed, it is the image of exterior differentiation from polynomial
1-forms of degree at most \(p-1\); the kernel consists of exact 1-forms
\(dG\), where \(G\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(p\), modulo
constants. In dimensions \(d\ge3\), this can be smaller than the raw list of
pair moments because curvature 2-forms obey \(d\Omega=0\). This is an
abstract polynomial one-form count; full attainability by the particular
matrix-profile map is not claimed here. The two-amplitude quartic case is
fully attained by the explicit convex examples above.

Adding an exact gauge \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha+dG\) changes no closed-loop action
and leaves \(d\alpha\) unchanged. Conversely, if another one-form preserves
the action on every closed amplitude loop in \(\mathbb R^2\), its difference
from \(\alpha\) has zero periods and is exact, so the curvature cannot change.
An energy null-Lagrangian that preserves the original Jensen functional
therefore cannot erase this signed obstruction by a coboundary; it can only
redistribute terms in an identity. For the determinant null Lagrangian in
two output dimensions, the induced profile form is
\(\alpha_{\det}(u)[v]=\mathbb E_y\det(v,B_2+u_y)\); integration by parts
shows its curvature is zero directly. Removing a nonzero curvature while
keeping every closed-loop action would require a genuine domination or
positive-remainder argument, not a gauge change.
