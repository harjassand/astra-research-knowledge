The following derivation makes the information boundary explicit.
Consider an \(n\)-carbon product detected at time \(t\). Let \(A_i\) be the age of its \(i\)-th carbon since entry through the specified labelling boundary. Let \(\mu\) describe their unordered joint age distribution.
For a prescribed isotope fraction \(p(t)\), the generating function of the intact product’s heavy-carbon count \(K\) is
\[ \boxed{ G_p(z,t) = \mathbb E[z^K] = \int \prod_{i=1}^{n} \left[1+(z-1)p(t-A_i)\right] \,d\mu(A). } \]
The proof is direct: conditional on the entry times, each carbon receives a Bernoulli isotope label; multiply their generating functions and average over the unknown histories.
Every isotope-count probability is a coefficient of this expression. Parity is its value at \(z=-1\). Factorial moments, nonlinear responses and harmonics derived from the same count record are functions of the same observation map.
