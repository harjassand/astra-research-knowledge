Result: a computational obstruction, but not yet a verified new principle
I derived the following result:
Estimating one stationary expectation to constant additive accuracy is PSPACE-complete for a succinctly described family of strictly positive Markov chains—even when every eigenvalue is real and distinct, the entire spectrum is given by a simple exact formula, and the absolute eigenvalue gap is at least \(7/8\).

For the same family, I also derived an unconditional obstruction to polynomial acceleration:
Every polynomial approximating the stationary projection to constant operator-norm accuracy requires degree proportional to the exponentially long computation encoded in the dynamics. This allows arbitrary signed coefficients, including coefficients chosen with complete knowledge of the matrix.

These statements have written proofs below. Their historical originality is not established. The investigation found substantial prior art behind the central mechanism: fixed-point access already has known PSPACE power, and the spectral transformation used here is an established PageRank identity. Consequently, I am not presenting this as completion of your original mission. arXiv
The meaningful outcome is a precisely specified obstruction, with the input, precision, acquisition, verification and computational costs exposed.
1. The operation investigated
The proposed capability was:
\[
\boxed{\text{Extract a global equilibrium answer without simulating the long dynamics.}}
\]
More precisely, suppose a polynomial-size circuit describes transitions on \(m\)-bit states. There are
\[
N=2^m
\]
possible states, but the full \(N\times N\) transition matrix is not supplied.
Given an efficiently evaluable Boolean observable \(g\), the desired output is just
\[
\pi g=\sum_z\pi(z)g(z),
\]
where \(\pi\) is the unique stationary distribution.
This avoids an artificial output-size obstruction: the answer is one scalar, not an exponentially long distribution.
The candidate shortcut was to use favorable spectral information to compute that scalar efficiently. The construction below defeats that shortcut even after imposing strict positivity, diagonalizability, a known spectrum, finite-bit inputs and constant output accuracy.
The spectral promise here concerns ordinary eigenvalues. It is not a singular-value contraction bound or a reversible-chain spectral-gap assumption. That distinction is essential.
2. A complete hardness construction
Encode computation in a transient path, not an unknown terminal state
Take a deterministic machine deciding an arbitrary PSPACE language on input \(x\). Encode its configurations using
\[
s=\operatorname{poly}(|x|)
\]
bits, and set
\[
H=2^s.
\]
A halting deterministic computation cannot repeat a nonhalting configuration, so it halts within \(H\) genuine transitions.
Let \(G_x\) be its polynomial-size one-step configuration circuit. Make halting configurations remain fixed, with an accepting flag that is set only upon acceptance.
The Markov-chain state is
\[
z=(\tau,w),
\qquad
0\leq \tau\leq 16H-1,
\qquad
w\in\{0,1\}^s.
\]
Thus
\[
m=2s+4,
\qquad
N=16H^2.
\]
Define the known initial state and known sink by
\[
a=(16H-1,w_{\mathrm{initial}}),
\qquad
o=(0,0^s).
\]
The deterministic update is
\[
F(\tau,w)=
\begin{cases}
(\tau-1,G_x(w)),&\tau>0,\\
o,&\tau=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Every state reaches the same known sink within \(16H\) updates.
This detail prevents a misleading shortcut: the unknown answer is not placed in a distinguished terminal state that the reduction secretly needs to identify. It is carried by the transient computation.
Define
\[
g(\tau,w)
=
\mathbf 1\{\tau>0\text{ and }w\text{ has the accepting flag}\}.
\]
Give the dynamics distinct, explicitly known eigenvalues
Index states by
\[
\operatorname{idx}(\tau,w)=\tau H+\operatorname{integer}(w).
\]
For \(z\neq o\), set
\[
d_z=\frac{\operatorname{idx}(z)+1}{8N}.
\]
These holding probabilities are distinct and satisfy \(0<d_z\leq1/8\).
Define
\[
Q(z,\cdot)
=
d_z\delta_z+(1-d_z)\delta_{F(z)}
\quad(z\neq o),
\qquad
Q(o,\cdot)=\delta_o.
\]
Every genuine update decreases the state index. Therefore \(Q\) is triangular, giving
\[
\operatorname{spec}(Q)
=
\{1\}
\cup
\left\{
\frac{j+1}{8N}:1\leq j\leq N-1
\right\}.
\]
The computation changes the off-diagonal entries, but not these eigenvalues.
Add strict positivity without destroying the spectrum
Set
\[
p=\frac1{16H},
\qquad
\alpha=1-p,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{64},
\]
and let
\[
r=(1-\varepsilon)\delta_a+\varepsilon u,
\]
where \(u\) is uniform on all \(N\) states. Finally, define
\[
P=\alpha Q+p\mathbf1r.
\]
Every transition is positive:
\[
P(i,j)\geq\frac{p\varepsilon}{N}
=\frac1{1024HN}
\geq\frac1{64N^2}.
\]
In particular, there is a unique stationary distribution, and
\[
\pi(j)\geq\frac1{64N^2}.
\]
To determine the spectrum, use zero-total-mass row vectors:
\[
V=\{v:v\mathbf1=0\}.
\]
For \(v\in V\),
\[
vP=\alpha vQ.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(P)
=
\{1\}
\cup
\left\{
\alpha\frac{j+1}{8N}:1\leq j\leq N-1
\right\}.
}
\]
All eigenvalues are real and distinct. Every nonprincipal eigenvalue is at most \(1/8\), so
\[
1-\max_{\lambda\neq1}|\lambda|
\geq\frac78.
\]
The entire spectrum is independent of the machine’s answer and is computable by an indexed formula using \(O(m)\) bits. This does not supply the eigenvectors or stationary projection.
The reset-spectrum identity is already established in the PageRank literature; it is not a newly invented mathematical operation. Internet Mathematics Journal
Prove a constant separation between accepting and rejecting inputs
The stationary distribution has the regeneration expansion
\[
\pi
=
p\sum_{t\geq0}\alpha^t rQ^t.
\]
Suppose the machine accepts.
Each genuine \(F\)-advance takes expected time at most \(8/7<4/3\). Therefore, if \(T\) is the number of \(Q\)-steps needed to complete \(H\) genuine advances,
\[
\mathbb E[T]\leq\frac{4H}{3}.
\]
For every integer \(t\in[4H,8H-1]\), Markov’s inequality gives
\[
\Pr(T\leq t)\geq\frac23.
\]
The clock is still positive throughout this interval. Since the accepting flag remains set after halting,
\[
Q^t(a,g)\geq\frac23.
\]
Also,
\[
\alpha^{8H}
=
\left(1-\frac1{16H}\right)^{8H}
\geq\frac12.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi g
&\geq
(1-\varepsilon)p(4H)\alpha^{8H}\frac23\\
&\geq
\frac{63}{64}\cdot\frac14\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac23\\
&=\frac{21}{256}
>\frac1{16}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Suppose instead that the machine rejects.
The trajectory from \(a\) never sets the accepting flag. Only the \(\varepsilon\)-fraction of uniform restarts can contribute, so
\[
\pi g\leq\varepsilon=\frac1{64}.
\]
Therefore the promise problem
\[
\boxed{
\pi g\leq\frac1{64}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\pi g\geq\frac1{16}
}
\]
is PSPACE-hard.
An estimate with additive error \(1/128\), correct with probability at least \(2/3\), distinguishes these cases.
No exponentially small output signal is being amplified. The answer gap is constant.
Why this is PSPACE-complete, not merely PSPACE-hard
The entry lower bound gives a polynomial-space upper bound without requiring the matrix to be expanded.
For any promised matrix satisfying \(P(i,j)\geq1/(64N^2)\), write mathematically
\[
P=\beta U+(1-\beta)R,
\qquad
\beta=\frac1{64N},
\]
where \(U\) has uniform rows and \(R\) is stochastic. This proves contraction by at most \(1-\beta\).
After
\[
T=1024N=2^k,
\qquad
k=m+10,
\]
the stationary bias is at most \(e^{-16}\).
An entry of \(P^{2^k}\) can be computed by recursive squaring, recomputing intermediate entries rather than storing matrices. With downward rounding to \(b\) fractional bits, the row-norm error obeys
\[
e_{j+1}\leq2e_j+N2^{-b}.
\]
Taking
\[
b=m+k+15
\]
makes the final rounding error less than \(2^{-14}\). The recursion has polynomial depth and polynomial-size counters, operands and accumulators.
The computation can take exponential time, but uses polynomial space. This establishes PSPACE membership.
Accordingly, a general polynomial-time randomized algorithm for the stated estimation task would imply
\[
\mathrm{BPP}=\mathrm{PSPACE}.
\]
This is a conditional complexity consequence—not an unconditional proof that such an algorithm cannot exist.
3. Arbitrary polynomial acceleration also fails
Slow ordinary iteration would not be enough: perhaps a carefully chosen combination of powers could cancel the transients.
The following argument rules that out in a precise model.
Write
\[
\Pi=\mathbf1\pi,
\qquad
\|A\|_r=\max_i\sum_j|A(i,j)|.
\]
Polynomial-filter theorem
For every real polynomial \(q\) with
\[
\deg(q)\leq4H,
\]
the constructed simple-spectrum matrix satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|q(P)-\Pi\|_r
\geq
\frac1{1+2e^{1/2}}
>
\frac15.
}
\]
If \(q(1)=1\), the stronger bound is
\[
\boxed{
\|q(P)-\Pi\|_r
\geq
\frac1{2e^{1/2}}
>
\frac14.
}
\]
There is no coefficient-size or positivity restriction.
Proof
Let \(d=\deg(q)\), and follow the deterministic path
\[
z_j=F^j(a),
\qquad
0\leq j\leq d.
\]
These states are distinct and do not include the sink.
Construct a test function \(h\) by
\[
h(o)=0,
\qquad
h(z_0)=1,
\]
and
\[
h(z_{j+1})
=
\frac{\alpha^{-1}-d_{z_j}}{1-d_{z_j}}h(z_j)
=
\left(
1+\frac{p}{\alpha(1-d_{z_j})}
\right)h(z_j).
\]
Set it to zero elsewhere.
Because
\[
\alpha\geq\frac{15}{16},
\qquad
1-d_z\geq\frac78,
\]
we have
\[
\|h\|_\infty
\leq(1+2p)^d
\leq e^{2pd}
\leq e^{1/2}.
\]
The construction ensures
\[
Qh(z_j)=\alpha^{-1}h(z_j)
\qquad(j<d).
\]
A \(k\)-step trajectory from \(a\), for \(k\leq d\), only visits \(z_0,\ldots,z_k\). Hence
\[
Q^kh(a)=\alpha^{-k},
\qquad
Q^kh(o)=0.
\]
Let
\[
v=\delta_a-\delta_o.
\]
Using \(vP^k=\alpha^kvQ^k\), we obtain
\[
vP^kh=1
\qquad(0\leq k\leq d).
\]
Therefore
\[
vq(P)h=q(1),
\qquad
v\Pi h=0.
\]
Set \(\delta=\|q(P)-\Pi\|_r\). Since \(\|v\|_1=2\),
\[
|q(1)|\leq2e^{1/2}\delta.
\]
Also, \(P\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) implies
\[
|q(1)-1|\leq\delta.
\]
Combining them,
\[
1
\leq|q(1)|+|q(1)-1|
\leq(1+2e^{1/2})\delta.
\]
This proves the theorem.
The test function is an analytic lower-bound witness, not an efficiently supplied input or a proposed computational procedure.
What this excludes—and what it does not
The theorem excludes low-degree polynomial approximation of the whole stationary projection in the stated norm. It is stronger than a power-method lower bound.
It does not exclude every nonlinear algorithm, every input-specific symbolic simplification, or a method supplied with an independently acquired inverse. Nor does it automatically give a lower bound for every particular observable.
The broader fact that favorable eigenvalues need not ensure favorable polynomial/Krylov convergence already has substantial prior art. The exact positive-stochastic theorem above still requires a targeted priority review. arXiv
4. The resource audit: where the computation went
The construction does not eliminate the long computation. It shows that the computation can remain expensive while the eigenvalues look favorable.

One transition	Worst-case polynomial bit operations and random bits

The transition sampler only samples one local transition. An equilibrium sampler is not supplied.
The small reset probability is not free
Here
\[
p=\frac1{16H}
\]
has a short binary representation, but
\[
\frac1p=16H
\]
is exponentially large in the workspace parameter \(s\).
A runtime polynomial in \(1/p\) is therefore not polynomial in the succinct input size.
Indeed, the constructed chains have
\[
t_{\mathrm{mix}}(1/4)=\Theta(H).
\]
For the lower bound, compare starts at \(a\) and \(o\), using the event \(B=\{\tau>0\}\). Before the clock can expire,
\[
\|\delta_aP^t-\delta_oP^t\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\geq\alpha^t.
\]
At \(t=4H\), this is at least \(3/4\), so one of the two starting distributions remains at least \(3/8\) from stationarity.
For the upper bound, a shared reset couples the two chains, giving a disagreement probability at most \(\alpha^t\).
A direct regenerative stationary sampler also takes expected \(\Theta(H)\) local steps. Thus the long timescale appears in an explicit upper bound as well as in the obstruction.
The relevant inverse is badly conditioned despite the gap
On zero-mass vectors define
\[
K=(I-P|_V)^{-1}
=\sum_{t\geq0}P^t|_V.
\]
The construction gives
\[
\boxed{
4H\leq\|K\|_{1\to1,V}\leq16H.
}
\]
The upper bound follows from contraction by \(\alpha\). For the lower bound, the difference between the root and sink trajectories retains positive mass on \(B\) for order \(H\) steps; summing those differences gives the stated norm bound.
Yet every eigenvalue of \(I-P|_V\) lies in \([7/8,1]\).
Thus the eigenvalue gap is not a substitute for an operational inverse bound.
This general distinction between nonreversible eigenvalues and mixing behavior is known; Jerison gives explicit examples, including slow mixing with zero nonprincipal eigenvalues. arXiv

Precision is charged separately from runtime
If a stochastic perturbation changes every row by at most \(\eta\) in total variation, the stationary distributions satisfy
\[
\|\pi'-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\leq\frac{\eta}{p}.
\]
Thus row precision of order \(p\) suffices for constant stationary accuracy. Even after accounting for all \(N\) entries in a row, this requires only
\[
O(\log N+\log(1/p))=O(s)
\]
fractional bits.
The obstruction therefore does not require an infinitely precise real number or exponentially many input precision bits.
Conversely, diagonalizing the matrix is an exceptionally ill-conditioned representation. Every diagonalization \(P=S\Lambda S^{-1}\) satisfies
\[
\kappa_r(S)\geq \frac12\,8^{8H}.
\]
That is a statement about the conditioning of the spectral representation, not a universal precision lower bound on all possible algorithms.
5. A separate unconditional acquisition lower bound
The PSPACE reduction must not be confused with an unconditional runtime lower bound. A related oracle-input construction does provide an unconditional query bound.
Let the input contain \(H\) oracle bits
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_H.
\]
Use a countdown state and one parity bit. During the first \(H\) genuine updates, XOR the next input bit into the parity register. Only after those updates does the observable expose the result.
Apply the same holding and reset construction. There are now \(N=32H\) states, and each local transition or transition-entry evaluation uses \(O(1)\) input-bit queries.
The stationary expectation satisfies
\[
\pi g\leq\frac1{64}
\quad\text{for even parity},
\]
and
\[
\pi g\geq\frac{21}{256}
\quad\text{for odd parity}.
\]
The spectrum remains independent of the oracle string.
Consequently, a constant-accuracy stationary estimator computes parity. This requires
\[
\boxed{\Omega(H)}
\]
classical queries, and also \(\Omega(H)\) quantum queries under coherent access to the specified local circuits. The quantum statement follows from the established parity lower bound, not a newly invented lower-bound technique. arXiv
This is an acquisition theorem for the stated interface. It does not assume a free global block encoding or equilibrium oracle, and it does not establish an exponential-time lower bound for arbitrary explicitly supplied circuits. When all \(H\) bits are supplied as an array, their input length is already \(H\).

Result: a broader operational boundary, but not a verified foundational breakthrough
The continuation produced a theorem about simultaneous consistency under arbitrary efficiently implemented stochastic or quantum rules, rather than another restriction on the old stationary matrix.
Its main consequence is:
When several rules share an invariant state with a polynomial relative-entropy bound from an accessible initialization, a state approximately consistent with every rule can be prepared efficiently—even without rapid mixing, commutativity, or access to that invariant state.
Therefore, a computational construction whose answer is correct on every approximately consistent state gains no power beyond ordinary randomized or quantum computation under these resource bounds.

This also gives an unconditional, oracle-model obstruction to compiling hard search into such rules. The obstruction concerns acquiring the common invariant support, not eigenvalues or polynomial filters.
The previous proof and resource contracts remain unchanged. In particular, its small-residual obstruction was never a lower bound against every possible algorithm, and the new argument does not reinterpret it as one.    research_note    research_note
Historical originality of the new theorem formulations is unresolved. The arguments below use established entropy inequalities and search lower bounds. They provide a broader mathematical result, but do not yet establish the genuinely new foundational capability requested.
1. The changed target: satisfying several consistency conditions
Suppose an input supplies efficiently implementable rules
\[
\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_K
\]
acting on a state represented by \(m\) classical bits or quantum bits.
Classically, these are stochastic transitions supplied by actual one-step sampler circuits. Quantumly, they are completely positive trace-preserving maps supplied by physical circuits.
Write
\[
d(\rho,\tau)=\frac12\|\rho-\tau\|_1,
\qquad
r_i(\rho)=d\bigl(\Phi_i(\rho),\rho\bigr).
\]
Thus \(r_i\) measures failure of consistency with rule \(i\).
The computational question is not merely whether a common fixed point exists. It is whether one can acquire a state satisfying
\[
\max_i r_i(\rho)\leq\delta
\]
and extract a useful answer from it.
This distinction matters. Separate easy consistency conditions can jointly encode a hard search problem, and replacing them by their average can produce a completely wrong answer. Section 5 gives an explicit two-rule example.
2. General preparation theorem
Assumptions
Let \(\rho_0\) be an efficiently preparable initial state. Suppose there exists a common invariant state \(\sigma\) satisfying
\[
\Phi_i(\sigma)=\sigma
\quad\text{for every }i,
\]
and
\[
D(\rho_0\|\sigma)\leq B<\infty.
\]
Here \(D\) is classical or quantum relative entropy, using natural logarithms.
The state \(\sigma\) is not supplied. The algorithm does not prepare it, evaluate its entries, compute its logarithm, or acquire its eigenvectors. It uses an available upper bound \(B\).
Define
\[
R=\frac12 I+\frac1{2K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Phi_i,
\qquad
\rho_t=R^t(\rho_0),
\]
and the time-averaged state
\[
\bar\rho_T=\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\rho_t.
\]
Theorem
For every positive integer \(T\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{K}
d\bigl(\Phi_i(\bar\rho_T),\bar\rho_T\bigr)^2
\leq \frac{2KB}{T}.
}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
T\geq\frac{2KB}{\delta^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_i r_i(\bar\rho_T)\leq\delta.
}
\]
There is no spectral-gap, reversibility, normality, mixing-time, or commutativity assumption.
Proof
For each rule, introduce its lazy version
\[
M_i(\rho)=\frac{\rho+\Phi_i(\rho)}2.
\]
Because \(\Phi_i(\sigma)=\sigma\), relative-entropy data processing gives
\[
D(\Phi_i(\rho)\|\sigma)\leq D(\rho\|\sigma).
\]
We also use Pinsker’s inequality in the convention
\[
D(a\|b)\geq 2d(a,b)^2.
\]
These are established inequalities, not new ingredients of this investigation. arXiv
The relative-entropy mixture identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(M_i(\rho)\|\sigma)
={}&
\frac12D(\rho\|\sigma)
+\frac12D(\Phi_i(\rho)\|\sigma)\\
&-\frac12D(\rho\|M_i(\rho))
-\frac12D(\Phi_i(\rho)\|M_i(\rho)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both midpoint distances equal \(r_i(\rho)/2\). Therefore,
\[
D(M_i(\rho)\|\sigma)
\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)-\frac12r_i(\rho)^2.
\]
Since \(R(\rho)=K^{-1}\sum_i M_i(\rho)\), convexity yields
\[
D(R(\rho)\|\sigma)
\leq
D(\rho\|\sigma)
-\frac1{2K}\sum_i r_i(\rho)^2.
\]
Telescoping over \(T\) steps and using nonnegativity of relative entropy gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_i r_i(\rho_t)^2\leq 2KB.
\]
Finally, affineness of the rules, convexity of the norm, and scalar Jensen imply
\[
r_i(\bar\rho_T)^2
\leq
\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}r_i(\rho_t)^2.
\]
Summing over \(i\) proves the theorem.
Why this is an operational result
To prepare \(\bar\rho_T\), choose a uniform integer \(J<T\), initialize \(\rho_0\), and perform \(J\) updates of \(R\). Each update either does nothing or applies a randomly selected rule. Discard the random-choice records.
That produces the stated mixed state directly.
It does not construct an exponentially large density matrix. Nor does it require the individual conditional trajectories to be approximately consistent: the guarantee concerns their resulting mixture.
The output is approximately consistent, not necessarily close to any particular equilibrium. That is the critical limitation.

\[
\boxed{

}
\]
The proof is constructive: prepare \(\bar\rho_T\) using the theorem and apply the decoder.
The characterization is exact under these contracts. Ordinary BPP or BQP computations can be embedded using a one-bit replacement rule: run an amplified decision circuit and add a small amount of fair-bit noise. Its invariant distribution has both probabilities bounded below by a constant, and every sufficiently approximate fixed state retains the correct decision bias. The computation inside the replacement rule is charged; this embedding supplies no speedup.
This is a different statement from the established PSPACE power of unrestricted fixed-point access. Aaronson and Watrous’s result concerns a model in which an exact consistent state is supplied by the model itself. arXiv
The difference here is substantive:
\[
\boxed{
\text{accessible initialization}
+
\text{bounded common-reference cost}
+
\text{correctness on all small-residual states}
}
\]
is enough to simulate the proposed global operation by ordinary computation.
This does not show that the promises are efficiently recognizable. It also does not identify robustness to local physical faults with correctness on every small-residual global state.
4. An unconditional tradeoff for search compilers
The preceding theorem can be applied against a general proposed mechanism, without choosing the old countdown construction.
Consider an oracle marking exactly one item among \(N\) possibilities. Suppose a compiler produces arbitrary rules satisfying the theorem’s assumptions, together with a decoder that outputs the marked item from every jointly \(\delta\)-consistent state.
Let
\[
q_{\mathrm{pre}},\quad q_{\mathrm{init}},\quad q,\quad q_{\mathrm{dec}}
\]
be the oracle queries used in preprocessing, one initialization, one rule application, and decoding. Suppose the decoder consumes \(r\) independently prepared copies.
The ordinary algorithm obtained above uses at most
\[
q_{\mathrm{pre}}
+r q_{\mathrm{init}}
+q_{\mathrm{dec}}
+C\,rq\left(1+\frac{KB}{\delta^2}\right)
\]
queries, for an absolute constant \(C\).
Classical unstructured search requires \(\Omega(N)\) queries; quantum unstructured search requires \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\). The quantum lower bound is established prior art. arXiv
Therefore every such compiler must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
q_{\mathrm{pre}}
+r q_{\mathrm{init}}
+q_{\mathrm{dec}}
+O\!\left(rq\left(1+\frac{KB}{\delta^2}\right)\right)
\geq
\begin{cases}
\Omega(N),&\text{classical},\\[2mm]
\Omega(\sqrt N),&\text{quantum}.
\end{cases}
}
\]
This statement allows arbitrary efficiently implemented affine rules and arbitrary decoders meeting the contract. It is not restricted to spectral methods, polynomial filters, or the previously constructed family.
A consequence for hidden invariant weights
Take the initial state to be uniform or maximally mixed on a \(d\)-dimensional space:
\[
\rho_0=\frac{I}{d}.
\]
For any full-rank \(\sigma\),
\[
D(I/d\|\sigma)
\leq
\log\frac{1}{d\,\lambda_{\min}(\sigma)}.
\]
Suppose preprocessing, initialization, decoding, rule count, rule-query cost, copy count, and \(\delta^{-1}\) are all polynomial in \(n=\log_2N\).
The search bound then prevents a uniformly polynomial common-reference budget. In the worst case, the necessary budget is exponential in \(n\), with the classical and quantum exponents inherited from their respective search bounds.
Consequently, for some oracle instance, every full-rank common invariant state must obey a bound of the form
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}(\sigma)
\leq d^{-1}\exp(-B_{\mathrm{required}}).
}
\]
When \(B_{\mathrm{required}}\) is exponential in \(n\), this is a doubly exponentially small invariant weight. Alternatively, the common invariant states may be singular, giving infinite forward relative entropy from a full-support initialization.
The quantifiers matter: this is a worst-case conclusion about a compiler correct for all promised oracles, not a statement about every instance.
Also, such tiny invariant weights need not be explicitly encoded as tiny transition probabilities. They can arise implicitly from finite-bit dynamics. The conclusion exposes a possible hidden resource; it does not prove a universal precision lower bound.
5. The attempted universal extension fails at common support
The obvious next attempt was to remove the common-reference assumption. That fails even for two deterministic, idempotent rules.
Let \(S\) be the satisfying set of a Boolean verifier. Add two special symbols \(a,b\), and define
\[
F_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S,\\
a,&x\notin S,
\end{cases}
\qquad
F_b(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S,\\
b,&x\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]
Each rule needs at most one verifier query. Individually they have trivial fixed states: \(\delta_a\) and \(\delta_b\).
For a distribution \(\mu\), put \(s=\mu(S)\). Directly,
\[
d(\mu F_a,\mu)=1-s-\mu(a),
\]
\[
d(\mu F_b,\mu)=1-s-\mu(b).
\]
Since \(\mu(a)+\mu(b)\leq1-s\),
\[
d(\mu F_a,\mu)+d(\mu F_b,\mu)\geq1-s.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\max\{d(\mu F_a,\mu),d(\mu F_b,\mu)\}\leq\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu(S)\geq1-2\delta.
}
\]
Even constant-accuracy joint consistency therefore produces a satisfying assignment with constant probability.
But the averaged rule has the easy stationary distribution
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{bad}}=\frac12(\delta_a+\delta_b),
\]
which assigns zero probability to \(S\). Its individual residuals are both \(1/2\).
This isolates the obstruction:
Averaging can satisfy an aggregate equation through cancellation, while satisfying the individual equations requires acquiring the solution support.

Every common invariant distribution of \(F_a,F_b\) is supported on \(S\). There is no common invariant distribution dominating a full-support initialization. The finite-relative-entropy assumption fails precisely at that support restriction.
This is not an exact-equality artifact. Perturb each rule by at most \(\eta\) in maximum-row total variation. Any distribution with residual at most \(\delta\) under both perturbed rules still satisfies
\[
\mu(S)\geq1-2(\delta+\eta).
\]
Therefore, universally “softening” these rules while retaining a robust solution decoder cannot evade the compiler tradeoff without paying elsewhere.
The example is a short reduction, not a new discovery that satisfiability is hard. Its role is to falsify the proposed extension of the preparation theorem.
6. A broader geometric obstruction to repairing the method
I also tested whether a different divergence could remove the support problem while preserving the proof mechanism.
The desired replacement would be finite everywhere, contract under stochastic processing, and retain enough convexity to force progress toward simultaneous consistency.
That combination is impossible.
Theorem: disjoint-support flatness
Let \(\mathfrak D(p\|q)\) be any divergence monotone under all stochastic maps. Fix \(q=\delta_0\).
For any distributions \(p,r\) supported outside \(0\), there are stochastic maps \(A,B\) such that
\[
Aq=q,\quad Ap=r,
\qquad
Bq=q,\quad Br=p.
\]
For example, \(A\) leaves \(0\) fixed and replaces every other input by a sample from \(r\).
Data processing in both directions gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D(p\|\delta_0)
=
\mathfrak D(r\|\delta_0).
}
\]
Thus the divergence is constant—possibly infinite—on the entire face disjoint from the reference support.
If finite there, it cannot satisfy
\[
\frac{\mathfrak D(p\|q)+\mathfrak D(r\|q)}2
-\mathfrak D\!\left(\frac{p+r}{2}\middle\|q\right)
\geq c\,d(p,r)^2
\]
with a uniform \(c>0\). Three classical states already yield a contradiction. The quantum obstruction follows on diagonal states.
This rules out an everywhere-finite, uniformly curved, universally data-processing potential as a repair. It does not rule out adaptive support acquisition, non-data-processing potentials, nonlinear operations, or other algorithms.
Quantitative version
There is also a worst-case lower bound on the cost of rare reference events.
Assume \(\mathfrak D\) is normalized by \(\mathfrak D(p\|p)=0\), is convex in its first argument, satisfies data processing across finite alphabets, and has the displayed midpoint curvature \(c\).
For a uniform reference on \(2^k\) points, repeatedly mixing two distributions uniform on disjoint, equally sized subsets gives
\[
\mathfrak D(\delta_i\|u_{2^k})\geq kc.
\]
For general \(q_i>0\), a stochastic map can send \(i\) to a distinguished output while sending \(q\) to a uniform distribution on \(2^k\) points whenever \(2^k\leq1/q_i\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D(\delta_i\|q)
\geq
c\left\lfloor\log_2\frac1{q_i}\right\rfloor.
}
\]
For \(q_i=0\), the same construction works for arbitrarily large \(k\), forcing an infinite value.
Relative entropy has
\[
D(\delta_i\|q)=\log(1/q_i),
\]
so its worst-case logarithmic rare-event cost has the correct order within this axiomatic class.
This is a point-mass, worst-case comparison—not a claim that every such divergence dominates relative entropy on all pairs. Historical originality of these elementary geometric deductions is not established.
7. Resource and verification accounting
The preparation theorem has the following complete operational scope.
Resource	What is actually required
Input acquisition	Explicit initialization and one-step implementation circuits. An entry-evaluation circuit alone is insufficient.
Representation	One \(m\)-bit or \(m\)-qubit state; no expanded transition matrix or density matrix.
Runtime	\(O(1+KB/\delta^2)\) rule opportunities per prepared output, multiplied by actual rule costs. Numerical \(B\), not merely \(\log B\), is charged.
Memory	One state, one rule’s workspace, initialization workspace, and \(O(\log T+\log K)\) counter bits, plus supplied circuit storage.
Randomness	Padding the rule count and averaging horizon to powers of two permits bounded fair-bit selection with only constant-factor overhead.
Verification	The algorithm does not verify the existence of the common reference, its entropy bound, or universal decoder soundness. These remain explicit promises.


For implementation error, suppose initialization is within distance \(\eta_0\), and every implemented rule is within \(\eta\) of its target in maximum-row TV or half-diamond norm. The prepared-state error is at most
\[
\eta_0+T\eta.
\]
Since each residual changes by at most twice the state error,
\[
r_i(\widetilde\rho)
\leq r_i(\bar\rho_T)+2d(\widetilde\rho,\bar\rho_T).
\]
Taking \(T\geq8KB/\delta^2\), together with
\[
\eta_0\leq\frac{\delta}{16},
\qquad
\eta\leq\frac{\delta}{16T},
\]
leaves a valid residual margin.
These are accuracy requirements, not a claim that arbitrary maps can be synthesized cheaply. Actual synthesis and arithmetic costs must be included.
Finally, estimating an additional bounded observable to error \(\varepsilon\) and failure probability \(\zeta\) requires
\[
O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\log\frac1\zeta\right)
\]
independent prepared outputs and measurements. That multiplies the full preparation cost.
8. What this establishes—and what it does not
The strongest conclusion is a general simulation theorem with a compiler obstruction:
\[
\boxed{
\text{A bounded common-reference cost makes robust joint-consistency computation ordinarily simulable.}
}
\]
The two-rule construction shows that robustness alone does not suffice: a hard answer can be stored in the common invariant support. The divergence argument shows why an everywhere-finite replacement potential cannot simply erase that support obstruction while preserving universal data processing and uniform curvature.
These results are broader than the original spectral example. Nevertheless, the underlying ingredients have substantial prior art. Relative-entropy inequalities are established; iterative Bregman fixed-point methods predate this work; and approximate fixed-point repair is an existing research subject. Those facts do not settle priority of the exact operational theorem, but they prevent presenting its proof mechanism as newly invented mathematical power. arXiv
The original completion standard remains unmet. No result here acquires an arbitrary hard common support, automatically constructs a sound robust decoder, recognizes the promises efficiently, or proves a non-oracle superpolynomial lower bound against general algorithms. The exact historical contribution of the theorem bundle also remains unresolved.
