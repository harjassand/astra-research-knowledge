## Result: bounded exchanges characterize uniformly bounded unbiased rounding

I obtained a **complete proof candidate for a geometric classification of constrained rounding**. The proposed principle is:

> A family of Boolean feasible polytopes admits unbiased rounding with dimension-independent worst-case error under every Euclidean-column-normalized linear map **if and only if its edges change a uniformly bounded number of coordinates**.

The upper bound includes arbitrary matroid bases. The converse identifies an explicit obstruction, rather than merely a limitation of the proof technique.

**This is not yet a verified historic discovery.** Historical novelty and independent correctness remain unverified. The analytic machinery is adapted from Guo–Fang–Lu’s September 2026 spectral-signing work; the additional claim developed here is the geometric transfer and classification, not an independent discovery of their Komlós machinery. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1)

## 1. The mathematical statement

A fractional solution may satisfy every feasibility constraint while not representing an actual discrete choice. Rounding must select a discrete solution without violating feasibility or introducing excessive error.

Let
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V),
\qquad
\mathcal V\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n},
\]
be a nonempty feasible polytope.

Define its **exchange width**
\[
\kappa(P)
=
\max_{[u,v]\text{ an edge of }P}
|\{j:u_j\ne v_j\}|.
\]

Thus \(\kappa(P)\) measures the largest number of binary decisions coupled together by an edge of the actual feasible polytope. It does **not** mean the size of an arbitrarily chosen collection of permitted moves.

Now take any matrix
\[
A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n},
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{ij}^{2}\le1
\quad\text{for every column }j,
\]
and any fractional feasible point \(x\in P\).

We seek a random feasible vertex \(Y\) satisfying both
\[
\mathbb EY=x
\]
and
\[
\|A(Y-x)\|_\infty\le D
\quad\text{with probability one}.
\]

The first condition preserves every marginal exactly. The second bounds every measured error at every output in the distribution’s support—not merely on average or with high probability. The distribution may depend on \(P,A,x\).

Let \(R(P)\) be the smallest universal bound \(D\) that works for every such \(A\) and \(x\).

### Proposed theorem

For \(k=\kappa(P)\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k}{2}\le R(P)\le4096k^{3}+48k.
}
\]

For a one-point polytope, \(R(P)=0\).

Consequently, for any family \(\mathcal C\) of Boolean-vertex polytopes, potentially of unbounded dimension,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}R(P)<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_{P\in\mathcal C}\kappa(P)<\infty.
}
\]

The cubic upper bound is not claimed to be optimal. The principal assertion is the **necessary-and-sufficient classification**: a local property of feasible exchanges controls a universal rounding requirement quantified over all dimensions, all normalized matrices, and all fractional feasible points.

## 2. What this would establish for matroids

For a matroid base polytope, every edge exchanges one included element for one excluded element, so its exchange width is at most two.

The proposed theorem therefore gives, for every fractional basis \(x\), a distribution on actual bases \(B\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\mathbf1_B=x,
\qquad
\|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le32864
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
\]

The constant is deliberately conservative. Its important feature is independence from the number of elements, the matroid rank, and the number of rows of \(A\).

There is also a one-output, objective-aware statement: for every cost vector \(c\), some basis satisfies
\[
c^\top\mathbf1_B\le c^\top x
\]
together with the same error bound. This does not assert that a single unbiased distribution makes every outcome cost-nonincreasing; that stronger combination is generally impossible.

For a \(0\)-\(1\) constraint matrix \(W\) having at most \(\Delta\) ones in each column, normalization gives
\[
\boxed{
\|W(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty
\le32864\sqrt{\Delta}.
}
\]

For comparison, the August 2026 *Weighted Equitability and Matroid-Constrained Discrepancy* paper proves a polynomial-time, cost-nonincreasing rounding guarantee with error linear in column sparsity. The candidate here supplies a square-root-sparsity **existence** guarantee; it does not yet supply an efficient constrained sampler or improve that paper’s practical constants. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13983v1)

## 3. Why the representation changed

My initial approaches tried to select a good discrete output directly, either by minimizing a separable energy or by transferring a distributional operation while retaining matroid structure.

Both proposed mechanisms failed. One exact construction shows that even when
\[
A^\top A=I,
\]
global minimization of
\[
\|A\varepsilon\|_4^4
\]
can select signings with arbitrarily large maximum-coordinate error while a signing with error exactly one exists. The source bundle contains the construction and proof.

The surviving approach instead works with the **geometry of every feasible face**. It asks:

> While many coordinates remain undecided, does the current feasible face contain enough directions to escape a minimum of an error-controlling potential?

The decisive bridge is elementary.

### Face-dimension lemma

If every edge of \(P\) changes at most \(k\) coordinates, and a face \(F\) has \(s(F)\) nonconstant coordinates, then
\[
\boxed{
\dim F\ge\frac{s(F)}{k}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Edge directions of \(F\) span its translation space. Choose \(\dim F\) independent edge directions spanning that space. Their supports together contain at most \(k\dim F\) coordinates. Every coordinate outside this union is constant on \(F\). Therefore
\[
s(F)\le k\dim F.
\]
\(\square\)

This statement persists after every restriction to a face. That persistence is what makes the rounding argument possible.

## 4. The upper-bound proof

The complete analytic proof is in the attachment. The following gives its structure, including the dimension count and the finite termination argument.

### 4.1 Strengthen the target by retaining one objective

Rather than first constructing an unbiased distribution, prove that for every \(c\in\mathbb R^n\), there is a good vertex \(y\) with
\[
c^\top y\le c^\top x.
\]

A separation argument will then produce exact marginals.

Pass to the sign polytope
\[
Q=2P-\mathbf1,\qquad z_0=2x-\mathbf1.
\]
Set
\[
K=16k.
\]

The sign-coordinate target is
\[
c^\top v\le c^\top z_0,
\qquad
\|A(v-z_0)\|_\infty\le2K^3+6K.
\]
Dividing by two recovers the stated Boolean-coordinate bound.

### 4.2 Track row errors without accumulating a loss at every stage

For each row, retain coefficients \(b_{ij}\) and an offset, so that the tracked row value is an affine function \(d_i(z)\). Write
\[
p_{ij}=b_{ij}^{2},
\qquad
q_i=\sum_jp_{ij}.
\]

Whenever
\[
p_{ij}>\frac{q_i}{K},
\]
discard that retained coefficient and put its current contribution into the offset.

The entire history of these discards costs at most \(4K\) in any row. The reason is a telescoping estimate. At a discard,
\[
\sqrt q-\sqrt{q-a^2}
=
\frac{a^2}{\sqrt q+\sqrt{q-a^2}}
\ge\frac{|a|}{2\sqrt K}.
\]
The first discard occurs only when \(q<K\). Hence the total absolute discarded mass is less than \(2K\), and each discarded coordinate can subsequently change by at most two:
\[
\big|[A(z-z_0)]_i-d_i(z)\big|\le4K.
\]

The resulting bound is for the **whole construction**, not a per-stage error summed over a growing number of stages.

### 4.3 Use a spectral barrier with feasible-direction freedom

The analytic part uses energy-corrected row barriers and a positive Gram matrix formed from squared retained coefficients. Those ingredients come from the spectral-signing proof, rather than from the failed final-output quartic objective. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1)

Let \(F\) be the current minimal feasible face, with \(s\) nonconstant coordinates. The construction maintains a twice-differentiable potential
\[
\Phi(z)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(M(z))-1\bigr)_+^3
-\eta\|z\|_2^2,
\qquad
\eta=\frac1{Kn}.
\]

Its invariants imply
\[
\Phi(z)\le0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_{\max}(M(z))<2.
\]

They also keep tracked row errors below \(2K^3\). The negative quadratic term supplies strictly negative curvature when the spectral part can be made locally flat or concave.

The key additional claim is that this curvature argument still works **inside the current feasible face while preserving the objective equation**
\[
c^\top z=c^\top z_0.
\]

At an interior candidate minimum, the constraints on a direction consume at most:

- \(5s/K^2\) dimensions for protected rows and tight row barriers;
- \(4s/K+2/K\) dimensions for the relevant spectral directions;
- one dimension for the objective.

The face-dimension lemma therefore leaves a subspace of dimension at least
\[
\frac{s}{k}
-\frac{5s}{K^2}
-\frac{4s}{K}
-\frac2K
-1.
\]

For \(K=16k\) and \(s>K\), this is strictly greater than
\[
\frac{5s}{K}.
\]

That is sufficient for the restricted-trace curvature argument. In the resulting direction \(h\),
\[
L'[h]=0,\qquad L''[h,h]\le0,
\]
where \(L=\lambda_{\max}(M)\). Consequently, when \(L>1\),
\[
\Phi''[h,h]
=
3(L-1)^2L''[h,h]-2\eta\|h\|_2^2<0.
\]
When \(L\le1\), the spectral term has zero Hessian and the same strict negativity follows directly.

The full proof derives the required matrix inequalities and checks that sufficiently small moves in either sign remain feasible. Thus a relative-interior point cannot be a local minimum while \(s>K\).

### 4.4 Obtain finite progress without assuming convergence

This is where the argument changes from a rounding trajectory to a face-descent proof.

Keep the current row data fixed. Within the compact face \(F\), minimize \(\Phi\) subject to the maintained row constraints, the objective equation, and
\[
L(z)\le2.
\]

The current point is feasible, so the set is nonempty. A global minimum exists by compactness. Its potential is still nonpositive, which ensures \(L<2\).

The preceding negative-curvature argument says that this minimum **cannot lie in the relative interior of \(F\)** when \(s>K\). It must lie in a proper face.

Restrict to that smaller face and update the retained coefficients. The proof checks that these updates cannot increase the potential.

Every stage strictly decreases face dimension. Therefore there are at most \(n\) such stages—even when a stage does not immediately make an individual coordinate integral.

Eventually the current face has at most \(K\) nonconstant coordinates. Choose its minimum-cost vertex. This preserves the objective inequality and adds at most \(2K\) to any row.

The total sign-coordinate error is therefore
\[
\underbrace{2K^3}_{\text{tracked row}}
+
\underbrace{4K}_{\text{all discard errors}}
+
\underbrace{2K}_{\text{terminal rounding}}
=
2K^3+6K.
\]

This establishes the proposed objective-aware upper bound.

**An important limitation remains:** the compact minimization problem is generally nonconvex. Existence of its minimum is not an efficient algorithm for finding it. The proof does not hide that computational problem behind an oracle assumption.

## 5. Why exact marginals follow

Fix \(A,x\), and let
\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}
=
\left\{
y\in\mathcal V:
\|A(y-x)\|_\infty\le4096k^3+48k
\right\}.
\]

The objective-aware result says
\[
\forall c\in\mathbb R^n,\quad
\exists y\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}
\text{ with }c^\top y\le c^\top x.
\]

Suppose
\[
x\notin\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}).
\]
Strict separation would give a \(c\) satisfying
\[
c^\top x
<
\min_{y\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}}c^\top y,
\]
contradicting the objective-aware result.

Thus
\[
x\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}).
\]

A convex decomposition of \(x\) supplies the required distribution. Every support point is feasible and has bounded error, while its expectation is exactly \(x\). At most \(n+1\) support points are needed.

This avoids trying to preserve exact marginals through every intermediate analytic operation.

## 6. Why bounded exchanges are necessary

The converse is independent of the spectral machinery.

Take an edge \([u,v]\) changing \(k\) coordinates, and let
\[
x=\frac{u+v}{2}.
\]

Any distribution on vertices with expectation \(x\) must be supported on this edge. To see this, take a linear functional exposing the edge. Its value at \(x\) is maximal over the polytope. An average of vertex values can equal that maximum only if every positive-probability vertex also maximizes the functional.

The only vertices on the edge are \(u,v\), so the distribution must choose each with probability \(1/2\).

Now use the one-row matrix
\[
a_j=v_j-u_j.
\]
Its columns have absolute value at most one, and
\[
a^\top(u-x)=-\frac{k}{2},
\qquad
a^\top(v-x)=\frac{k}{2}.
\]

Therefore
\[
R(P)\ge\frac{k}{2}.
\]

Combining this with the upper bound proves the proposed uniform classification.

The unbiasedness requirement matters here: it forces rounding to respect the exposed face containing the fractional point.

## 7. A concrete boundary: one matroid versus two

Consider an even cycle with \(2r\) edges. It has exactly two perfect matchings, the two alternating edge sets \(M_0,M_1\).

At their midpoint, assign scalar coefficients
\[
a_e=
\begin{cases}
1,&e\in M_0,\\
-1,&e\in M_1.
\end{cases}
\]

Every feasible integral output has error exactly \(r\).

Each side of the cycle’s bipartition separately defines a partition matroid; the perfect matchings are their common bases. Thus two individual feasible polytopes with width-two exchanges can have an intersection whose exchanges involve all \(2r\) coordinates.

This gives a precise distinction:

\[
\text{bounded exchanges}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\text{feasibility forcing an arbitrarily large coupled decision}.
\]

The proposed theorem therefore does not extend indiscriminately to arbitrary intersections of matroids. In this example, the obstruction applies even without unbiasedness, because there are only two feasible outputs.

## 8. What has been checked—and what has not

The full proof is written through in the attached source; no missing lemma is deliberately deferred in its existence argument.

The accompanying program was executed. It checked symbolic identities used in the proof, rational instances of the failed quartic construction, and the face-dimension inequality across all 255 nonempty vertex subsets of the three-dimensional Boolean cube. Accepted proper faces had rational exposing normals checked exactly; rejected candidate faces relied on numerical linear-programming infeasibility reports.

Eight synthetic 128-variable spectral states also passed local negative-curvature diagnostics on partition-matroid tangent spaces. Those were floating-point checks, **not formal certificates and not runs of a rounding algorithm**.

The remaining distinctions are substantive:

**Correctness:** this is a complete proof candidate, not an independently reviewed or formally verified theorem.

**Novelty:** the spectral engine is prior work. The proposed new contribution is the transfer to sparse-edge feasible polytopes and the resulting classification. Targeted searches did not establish historical priority.

**Computation:** the exact unresolved computational step is replacing the nonconvex compact minimization with an efficiently implementable constrained procedure, with its numerical and oracle costs proved.

**Foundational significance:** the candidate replaces an apparently unbounded collection of rounding questions with a single geometric criterion. That is the strongest outcome of this investigation. I have not established that it meets the requested standard of a historically new, independently validated foundational breakthrough.
