1. Minimum-growth rigidity for matrix multiplication
Let
\[
X\in\mathbb R^{m\times n},\qquad
Y\in\mathbb R^{n\times p},\qquad N=mnp.
\]
Consider an exact bilinear algorithm
\[
XY=\sum_{r=1}^{R}
\langle U_r,X\rangle_F
\langle V_r,Y\rangle_F\,W_r.
\]
Define
\[
w_r=\|U_r\|_F\|V_r\|_F\|W_r\|_F,
\qquad
L=\sum_{r=1}^{R}w_r.
\]
Zero terms can be discarded. The quantity \(L\) is a coefficient-growth measure, not a count of additions or a complete description of floating-point stability.
The underlying norm framework is established prior art: Dai and Lim study bilinear stability through such growth factors, and Derksen computes the matrix-multiplication tensor’s nuclear norm as \(mnp\). In particular, the lower bound \(L\ge N\) is not new. Department of Statistics
Theorem: complete classification of equality
An exact real bilinear algorithm satisfies
\[
L=N
\]
if and only if, after normalizing each nonzero term, there are unit vectors
\[
u_r\in\mathbb R^m,\qquad
v_r\in\mathbb R^n,\qquad
z_r\in\mathbb R^p
\]
such that its normalized coefficient matrices are
\[
A_r=u_rv_r^{\mathsf T},\qquad
B_r=v_rz_r^{\mathsf T},\qquad
C_r=u_rz_r^{\mathsf T},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=1}^{R}
w_r
(u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r)
(u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r)^{\mathsf T}
=I_N.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus minimum-growth algorithms correspond exactly to weighted product-vector tight frames.
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{L=N\ \Longrightarrow\ R\ge N.}
\tag{2}
\]
Every algorithm using fewer than \(mnp\) bilinear products therefore has strictly greater Frobenius coefficient growth than classical multiplication.
Moreover, every weight in a minimum-growth decomposition satisfies \(w_r\le1\). When \(R=N\), all weights equal \(1\), and the vectors \(u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r\) form an orthonormal basis.
Proof
Write the multiplication tensor as
\[
T=\sum_{i,j,k}E_{ij}\otimes E_{jk}\otimes E_{ik}.
\]
Its \(N\) displayed terms are orthonormal, so
\[
\|T\|_F^2=N.
\]
For matrices \(A,B,C\) of the corresponding shapes,
\[
\langle T,A\otimes B\otimes C\rangle
=\langle AB,C\rangle_F.
\]
If each matrix has Frobenius norm \(1\), then
\[
|\langle AB,C\rangle_F|
\le \|AB\|_F
\le \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|B\|_F
\le1.
\tag{3}
\]
Normalize the algorithm as
\[
T=\sum_r w_r A_r\otimes B_r\otimes C_r.
\]
Taking its inner product with \(T\) gives
\[
N=\sum_r w_r\langle A_rB_r,C_r\rangle_F
\le\sum_r w_r=L.
\]
If \(L=N\), every nonzero term must attain equality in (3):
\[
\langle A_rB_r,C_r\rangle_F=1.
\]
For one such term, equality implies
\[
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|A\|_F=1.
\]
Hence \(A\) has rank one:
\[
A=uv^{\mathsf T}
\]
with \(u,v\) unit vectors. Equality in \(\|AB\|_F\le1\) then forces
\[
B=vz^{\mathsf T},
\]
and equality in the final inner-product inequality forces
\[
C=AB=uz^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It remains to use the full tensor identity, rather than only its inner product with \(T\).
Define a reshuffling of the six tensor indices by
\[
\mathcal R(S)_{(i,j',k'),(i',j,k)}
=
S_{(i,j),(j',k),(i',k')}.
\]
This is an invertible permutation of entries, and
\[
\mathcal R(T)=I_N.
\]
For an equality-case term,
\[
\mathcal R\!\left[
(uv^{\mathsf T})\otimes(vz^{\mathsf T})
\otimes(uz^{\mathsf T})
\right]
=
(u\otimes v\otimes z)(u\otimes v\otimes z)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The exact algorithm identity therefore becomes (1).
Conversely, applying \(\mathcal R^{-1}\) to (1) produces an exact bilinear algorithm. Taking traces gives \(\sum_r w_r=N\), proving minimum growth.
Finally, the right side of (1) is a sum of \(R\) rank-one matrices, so \(R\ge N\). Since every summand is positive semidefinite,
\[
I_N-w_rq_rq_r^{\mathsf T}\succeq0,
\qquad q_r=u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r,
\]
which gives \(w_r\le1\). If \(R=N\), the square matrix with columns \(\sqrt{w_r}q_r\) is orthogonal, forcing every \(w_r=1\). ∎
A necessary qualification: an orthonormal product basis need not arise by taking all combinations from three fixed local bases. The theorem does not impose that stronger, unjustified classification.
2. A quantitative version—and why it is insufficient
The equality theorem alone leaves open how little extra growth can purchase a substantial reduction in multiplication count.
The following bound survives that quantitative question.
Theorem: rank–growth gap
Every exact real bilinear matrix-multiplication algorithm satisfies
\[
\boxed{
(N-R)_+\le6L(L-N).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Here \((x)_+=\max(x,0)\).
Proof
For each normalized term, write
\[
q_r=\langle A_rB_r,C_r\rangle_F,\qquad
M_r=\mathcal R(A_r\otimes B_r\otimes C_r).
\]
Then
\[
q_r\le1,\qquad
\sum_r w_rq_r=N,\qquad
\|M_r\|_F=1.
\]
When \(q_r\ge0\), cyclic applications of the trace inequality give
\[
\|A_r\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\|B_r\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\|C_r\|_{\mathrm{op}}\ge q_r.
\]
Up to row and column permutations, \(M_r\) is the Kronecker product of the three coefficient matrices, with one transposed. Its largest singular value is therefore at least \(q_r^3\).
Its best rank-one approximation \(Q_r\) consequently satisfies
\[
\|M_r-Q_r\|_F^2
\le1-q_r^6
\le6(1-q_r).
\tag{5}
\]
For \(q_r<0\), choose \(Q_r=0\); the same final inequality holds.
Because
\[
I_N=\sum_r w_rM_r,
\]
and \(\sum_r w_rQ_r\) has rank at most \(R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(N-R)_+}
&\le
\left\|I_N-\sum_r w_rQ_r\right\|_F\\
&\le
\sum_r w_r\sqrt{6(1-q_r)}\\
&\le
\sqrt{
6\left(\sum_r w_r\right)
\left(\sum_r w_r(1-q_r)\right)
}\\
&=\sqrt{6L(L-N)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Squaring proves (4). ∎
For \(R<N\), this yields
\[
L\ge
\frac{N+\sqrt{N^2+\frac23(N-R)}}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
This is the first precise limitation of the route. When \(R\) is a fixed fraction of \(N\), the guaranteed excess \(L-N\) is only of constant order. The theorem does not force a dimension-growing relative penalty.
In particular, it does not exclude a hypothetical family with substantially subcubic product count and growth only slightly above \(n^3\).
3. A sharp computation–error law
The same structure yields a stronger conclusion when cancellation is exposed to independent product noise.
The assumptions matter. Consider fixed-coefficient bilinear algorithms, with all linear operations exact, whose scalar products are independently corrupted:
\[
\widehat Z
=
\sum_{r=1}^{R}
(1+\xi_r)
\langle U_r,X\rangle_F
\langle V_r,Y\rangle_F W_r.
\tag{7}
\]
Assume
\[
\mathbb E\xi_r=0,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi_r^2=\lambda>0,
\]
with noises independent of one another and of the inputs. Let \(X,Y\) have independent standard Gaussian entries.
The noiseless algorithm is allowed to be biased or approximate: its coefficient tensor
\[
S=\sum_r U_r\otimes V_r\otimes W_r
\]
need not equal \(T\).
Theorem: optimal mean-square error for \(R\ge N\)
Every algorithm in (7) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2
\ge
\frac{\lambda N^2}{R+\lambda N}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
For every integer \(R\ge N\), equality is attained by an explicit real algorithm.
For algorithms required to be exact before noise is added,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2
\ge
\frac{\lambda N^2}{R},
}
\tag{9}
\]
again with equality attainable for every integer \(R\ge N\).
Neither bound can be attained with \(R<N\) within this bilinear class.
Proof of the lower bounds
Independence and centering eliminate the cross-noise terms. Independence of the Gaussian inputs makes their bilinear monomials orthonormal in mean square. Thus
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2
=
\|S-T\|_F^2+\lambda\sum_r w_r^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Set
\[
a=\frac{\langle T,S\rangle}{N}.
\]
Orthogonal projection onto the line spanned by \(T\) gives
\[
\|S-T\|_F^2\ge N(1-a)^2.
\]
The contraction inequality (3) gives
\[
L\ge|\langle T,S\rangle|=N|a|,
\]
and Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_r w_r^2\ge\frac{L^2}{R}
\ge\frac{N^2a^2}{R}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2
\ge
N(1-a)^2+\frac{\lambda N^2a^2}{R}.
\tag{11}
\]
The right side is minimized at
\[
a_*=\frac{R}{R+\lambda N},
\]
with minimum (8).
If \(S=T\), then \(a=1\), giving (9).
Equality requires \(S=a_*T\), equal term weights, and a minimum-growth decomposition of \(T\) after rescaling. The rigidity theorem therefore requires \(R\ge N\). ∎
Explicit real constructions attaining the bounds
It is not enough to attain equality only when \(R\) is a multiple of \(N\), by repeating the classical algorithm. The following construction works for every \(R\ge N\).
Define unit real vectors
\[
f_{2h+1}(t)=
\frac1{\sqrt{2h+1}}
\bigl(
1,\sqrt2\cos t,\sqrt2\sin t,\ldots,
\sqrt2\cos ht,\sqrt2\sin ht
\bigr),
\]
and
\[
f_{2h}(t)=
\frac1{\sqrt{2h}}
\bigl(
\sqrt2\cos(t/2),\sqrt2\sin(t/2),\ldots,
\sqrt2\cos((h-\tfrac12)t),
\sqrt2\sin((h-\tfrac12)t)
\bigr).
\]
For
\[
t_r=\frac{2\pi r}{R},\qquad r=0,\ldots,R-1,
\]
put
\[
u_r=f_m(t_r),\qquad
v_r=f_n(mt_r),\qquad
z_r=f_p(mnt_r).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
(u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r)
(u_r\otimes v_r\otimes z_r)^{\mathsf T}
=
\frac RN I_N.
}
\tag{12}
\]
To prove this, observe that \(f_d(t)\) is a fixed unitary change of coordinates of
\[
\frac1{\sqrt d}
\left(
e^{i(j-(d-1)/2)t}
\right)_{j=0}^{d-1}.
\]
In the threefold tensor product, the mixed-radix frequencies are exactly
\[
\ell-\frac{N-1}{2},\qquad \ell=0,\ldots,N-1.
\]
Their pairwise differences are integers of magnitude below \(N\). Since \(R\ge N\),
\[
\frac1R\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
e^{2\pi i(\ell-\ell')r/R}
=\delta_{\ell\ell'}.
\]
Returning to the real coordinates proves (12).
The rigidity identity now gives the exact algorithm
\[
\boxed{
XY=
\frac NR\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
(u_r^{\mathsf T}Xv_r)
(v_r^{\mathsf T}Yz_r)
u_rz_r^{\mathsf T}.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Every term has weight \(N/R\), so
\[
\sum_r w_r^2=\frac{N^2}{R}.
\]
This attains (9). Scaling the entire output by \(a_*\) attains (8).
These formulas establish optimality for the stated product-count model. Their dense linear transformations are not a claim of an efficient implementation with few additions.
Consequence: an exact high-accuracy resource threshold
Since
\[
\mathbb E\|XY\|_F^2=N,
\]
a target relative mean-square error
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2}
{\mathbb E\|XY\|_F^2}
\le\delta
\]
requires
\[
R\ge\frac{\lambda N(1-\delta)}{\delta}.
\tag{14}
\]
Whenever
\[
0<\delta\le\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},
\]
the required count is at least \(N\), so the construction proves the exact optimum:
\[
\boxed{
R_{\min}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\lambda N(1-\delta)}{\delta}
\right\rceil.
}
\tag{15}
\]
For square matrices, fixed positive noise variance and fixed relative accuracy below \(1\) therefore force \(\Omega(n^3)\) products in this model. Classical multiplication with a constant amount of repetition provides an \(O(n^3)\) upper bound.
That is a sharp model-specific resource law—not a general cubic lower bound for numerical matrix multiplication.
4. Decisive falsification of the broader interpretation
A tempting claim would be:
Fast multiplication necessarily loses the same amount of accuracy whenever every scalar product has multiplicative noise of variance \(\lambda\).

That claim is false without a condition on correlations.
Set
\[
\xi_1=\cdots=\xi_R=\xi,
\qquad
\mathbb E\xi=0,\quad
\mathbb E\xi^2=\lambda.
\]
For any exact bilinear algorithm,
\[
\widehat Z=(1+\xi)XY,
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat Z-XY\|_F^2=\lambda N.
\tag{16}
\]
For an exact algorithm with \(R<N\), this is strictly below the independent-noise lower bound
\[
\frac{\lambda N^2}{R}.
\]
For example, the seven-product \(2\times2\) Strassen algorithm gives
\[
8\lambda
<
\frac{64}{7}\lambda.
\]
The standard seven-product decomposition is discussed in the matrix-multiplication norm literature. arXiv
Thus independence is essential to the proved obstruction, not an assumption that can simply be removed in a stronger theorem.
Nor does the result imply that fast multiplication cannot be numerically stable. Demmel, Dumitriu, Holtz and Kleinberg establish that numerically stable algorithms can attain the matrix-multiplication exponent in the appropriate asymptotic sense. Their stability setting is not the fixed independent-product-noise model above. arXiv

Result: exact catalytic tensor conversion can encode arbitrary matrix algebra
I obtained a different kind of result: a dimension-preserving construction that makes ordinary three-party tensor restriction simulate arbitrary finite-dimensional representations of finitely presented algebras.
The resulting proposed theorem is substantially stronger than an obstruction to coefficient growth:
Exact conversion assisted by a returned bipartite catalyst is recursively enumerable–complete—even with fixed tensor formats, a fixed target, and a source varying along one fixed rank-one affine line. The input tensors can all have strictly positive integer coefficients.

The proof is below. Historical originality and independent mathematical verification remain unresolved. I am not treating this as an independently validated historic breakthrough.
The mechanism does not suppress cancellation. It permits arbitrary complex local maps and uses the locations and sizes of matrix-rank drops to force those maps to respect an encoded algebraic system.
1. Precise statement
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Write a three-tensor in matrix slices:
\[
T=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}e_i\otimes A_i.
\]
Define its amplification by a bipartite auxiliary state:
\[
T[d]=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}e_i\otimes(A_i\otimes I_d).
\]
This places
\[
\Phi_d=\sum_{a=1}^{d}f_a\otimes f_a
\]
between the second and third parties, leaving the first party unassisted.
Write \(T\succeq S\) when three independent local linear maps convert \(T\) exactly into \(S\). Define
\[
T\succeq_{\mathrm{cat}}S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists d\ge1:\ T[d]\succeq S[d].
\]
The auxiliary state is returned exactly and uncorrelated with the target.
Operationally, this is stochastic exact conversion: the conversion succeeds with some nonzero probability, with no lower bound on that probability. It is not deterministic conversion or a requirement that the catalyst survive failed branches. Tensor restriction and stochastic multipartite entanglement conversion have an established connection; stochastic entanglement catalysis itself is not a new notion. arXiv
Theorem A — Representation universality
There is an effective construction taking a finite presentation
\[
\mathcal A=
\mathbb Q\langle X_1,\ldots,X_t\rangle/
(p_1,\ldots,p_m)
\]
to integer three-tensors \(T_{\mathcal A},S_{\mathcal A}\), such that for every positive integer \(d\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A\text{ has a unital representation in }M_d(\mathbb C)
\iff
T_{\mathcal A}[d]\succeq S_{\mathcal A}[d].
}
\tag{1}
\]
The construction has polynomial encoding size when the relations are supplied as arithmetic circuits.
The representations need not be faithful.
Theorem B — Fixed-format undecidability
There exist fixed integer three-tensors \(U,V,S\), with \(U,V\) of the same format and
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{tensor}}V=1,
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\{n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:U+nV\succeq_{\mathrm{cat}}S\}
\text{ is recursively enumerable–complete.}
}
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, \(U,V,S\) can all be chosen to have strictly positive integer entries.
“Recursively enumerable–complete” means that positive instances can eventually be recognized by a search, but deciding all instances is at least as hard as the halting problem.
The distinction from the previous matrix-multiplication result is fundamental: the obstruction is now to decidability itself, not to stability or to a particular noise model.
2. Proof of representation universality
2.1 Encode algebra relations as two-sided matrix equations
First replace higher-degree relations by quadratic relations, introducing a variable for each arithmetic-circuit gate. For example,
\[
Z=XY,\qquad W=Z+Q
\]
become quadratic and linear relations. This preserves representations in every dimension: the auxiliary variables are determined by the original ones.
Clear rational denominators. Put
\[
X_0=I,\qquad s=t+1.
\]
Every resulting relation can be written
\[
p_\nu(X)=
\sum_{a,b=0}^{t}c^{(\nu)}_{ab}X_aX_b=0.
\]
Construct \(s\times s\) integer matrices \(M_i\), with corresponding scalars \(b_i\), as follows:
\[
M_0=E_{00},\qquad b_0=1;
\]
for every variable, include
\[
E_{a0}-E_{0a},\qquad b=0;
\]
for every relation, include
\[
M_{p_\nu}=\sum_{a,b}c^{(\nu)}_{ab}E_{ab},
\qquad b=0.
\]
Finally include a distinguished index \(*\) with
\[
M_*=0,\qquad b_*=0.
\]
Consider rectangular unknowns
\[
P=[P_0\ P_1\ \cdots\ P_t],
\qquad
Q=
\begin{bmatrix}
Q_0\\Q_1\\\vdots\\Q_t
\end{bmatrix},
\]
whose blocks are \(d\times d\), satisfying
\[
P(M_i\otimes I_d)Q=b_iI_d
\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{3}
\]
The normalization equation gives
\[
P_0Q_0=I_d.
\]
Thus \(P_0\) is invertible and \(Q_0=P_0^{-1}\).
Set
\[
X_a=P_0^{-1}P_a,\qquad Y_a=Q_aP_0.
\]
The equations corresponding to \(E_{a0}-E_{0a}\) force
\[
X_a=Y_a.
\]
Consequently,
\[
P(M_{p_\nu}\otimes I_d)Q
=
P_0p_\nu(X)P_0^{-1}.
\]
Therefore (3) is equivalent to a \(d\)-dimensional unital representation of \(\mathcal A\).
Conversely, any representation gives a solution by taking
\[
P=[I_d\ X_1\ \cdots\ X_t],
\qquad
Q=
\begin{bmatrix}
I_d\\X_1\\\vdots\\X_t
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The remaining problem is significant: ordinary tensor restriction does not preserve slice labels or the rectangular blocks used in (3). The next construction forces that preservation without restricting the allowed local maps.

The target is concise in its first factor: if \(B(x)=0\), then every \(\ell_j(x)=0\), hence \(x=0\). Its first local map must therefore be invertible.

B(Gx)\otimes I_d
=
L\bigl(A(x)\otimes I_d\bigr)R.
\tag{6}

\operatorname{rank}B(Gx)
\le
\operatorname{rank}A(x).
\tag{7}

Let

H_j=\ker\ell_j.

\operatorname{rank}A(x)
\le K-h_j+s<K.

G(H_j)\subseteq\bigcup_kH_k.

Over \(\mathbb C\), a vector space cannot be covered by finitely many proper linear subspaces. Consequently,
\[
G(H_j)=H_{\pi(j)}

for a permutation \(\pi\).
Choose a generic \(x\in H_j\), avoiding all other marker hyperplanes. Its image belongs to exactly \(H_{\pi(j)}\). Equation (7) then gives

K-h_{\pi(j)}
\le K-h_j+s,

h_{\pi(j)}\ge h_j-s.

Because consecutive \(h_j\)'s differ by \(s+1\),

\pi(j)\ge j.

A permutation with this property is the identity.

3. From representation universality to the halting problem
Undecidability of unbounded finite-dimensional representation existence is already known; Letzter explicitly records this distinction between unrestricted dimension and fixed-dimensional algebraic decision problems. That source problem is not the proposed new contribution. arXiv

An integer-enforcing matrix system
Take generators \(e,f,h,z\) with

[h,e]=2e,\qquad
[h,f]=-2f,\qquad
[e,f]=h,
\tag{11}

The underlying \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) highest-weight construction is standard. Webusers
Consider a nonzero finite-dimensional simple complex module for an algebra containing these relations. Centrality makes \(z=uI\): an eigenspace of \(z\) is nonzero and invariant, so simplicity makes it the whole module.
Choose an \(h\)-eigenvector and repeatedly apply \(e\) until obtaining a nonzero vector \(v\) with
\[
ev=0,\qquad hv=\lambda v.
\]
This terminates because nonzero successive vectors have distinct eigenvalues.
Let \(m\) be maximal with \(f^mv\ne0\). The commutator identity
\[
ef^kv=k(\lambda-k+1)f^{k-1}v

at \(k=m+1\) forces \(\lambda=m\). Applying (12) to \(v\),
\[

\boxed{u\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.}
\tag{13}

ev_j=j(m-j+1)v_{j-1},
\qquad z=uI,

with boundary vectors zero. Direct substitution verifies (11)–(12).
Encode arbitrary integer polynomial equations
For every integer variable \(x_i\), introduce two commuting copies of this system, with central elements \(u_i,v_i\), and substitute
\[
x_i=u_i-v_i.
\]
Every integer is a difference of two nonzero integers. Thus, for any integer polynomial \(P\), adjoining
\[
P(u_1-v_1,\ldots,u_k-v_k)=0
\tag{14}
\]
gives an algebra having a nonzero finite-dimensional representation exactly when \(P\) has an integer root.
For the reverse direction, take a simple quotient of any finite-dimensional representation. All central elements become scalar nonzero integers, yielding the root. For the forward direction, tensor together the explicit representations above.
The passage to a simple quotient is important: it prevents non-scalar central matrices or nilpotent parts from creating false positive instances.

whose integer-root existence, as \(n\ge0\) varies, represents a recursively enumerable–complete set. Nonnegative-variable requirements can be eliminated using sums of four squares. Universal Diophantine equations are established prior art. Cambridge University Press

Catalyst removal and a positive-rate obstruction
The channel-level lead yields a structural result beyond transferring undecidability:
An LOCC device whose memory loses no relative entropy of entanglement on a maximally mixed input implements exactly the same channel when that memory is replaced by one separable state.
The replacement can be chosen independently of the device. For stationary devices, the same replacement reproduces their entire multi-time input–output process.

Pushing this further gives an asymptotic consequence:
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal N\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}.
}
\]
Here \(E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}\) is the entanglement cost of sequential, universally usable channel simulation, allowing arbitrary finite-dimensional catalysts that are returned exactly. Their dimensions may grow without any prescribed bound.
Thus, exact catalysts cannot turn a genuinely non-LOCC channel into a zero-rate operation. Without additional uncharged catalysts, a positive, channel-dependent entanglement-rate threshold admits an exponential strong converse. Even with arbitrary exactly returned catalysts, sublinear supplied entanglement forces simulation error to approach one.

using natural logarithms. The minimum exists: the separable-state set is compact, relative entropy is lower semicontinuous, and a full-rank separable comparator makes the minimum finite.

The simulation model
A sequential simulator receives an input for one call, produces that call’s output, and only then receives the next input. It may retain arbitrary quantum memory and use arbitrary LOCC protocols between calls.

\]
be its complete preparation-and-measurement outcome distribution for a channel \(\mathcal L\).
The map \(\mathcal L\mapsto p(\mathcal L)\) is affine and injective. Consequently,
\[
p(\mathcal N)\notin
\{p(\mathcal L):\mathcal L\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\}.
\]
The set on the right is compact and convex.
A separating linear functional on these probability vectors can be shifted and scaled so that each outcome is assigned an acceptance probability strictly between zero and one. This produces a physically realizable binary test, using only free preparations, local measurements and classical postprocessing, with
\[
a=\Pr[\text{accept}\mid\mathcal N],
\qquad
b=\sup_{\mathcal L\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}}
\Pr[\text{accept}\mid\mathcal L],
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{0<b<a<1.}
\tag{10}
\]
Define
\[
d(u\Vert v)
=
u\ln\frac uv+(1-u)\ln\frac{1-u}{1-v},
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathcal N}=d(a\Vert b)>0.
\tag{11}
\]
No efficient procedure for optimizing \(b\) is assumed or claimed.
Theorem 2 — Adaptive resource-expenditure bound
Suppose a causal LOCC simulator starts with arbitrary shared memory \(\tau_n\). Run the binary test independently at each call, and let \(\tau_n'\) denote the final retained memory averaged over the test records.
If the simulator’s error satisfies
\[
\varepsilon_n<a-b,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
E_R(\tau_n)-E_R(\tau_n')
\ge
n\,d(a-\varepsilon_n\Vert b).
}
\tag{12}
\]
Every retained shared quantum register is included in the memory. Its dimension is unrestricted apart from being finite for each \(n\).
Proof
Choose one closest separable initialization \(\gamma_n\) to the entire initial memory \(\tau_n\).
Let \(P\) be the actual binary-record distribution and \(Q\) the distribution obtained by running the same simulator from \(\gamma_n\).
The complete experiment is a conditionally separability-preserving instrument on the initial memory. Equation (5) gives
\[
E_R(\tau_n)-E_R(\tau_n')
\ge D(P\Vert Q).
\tag{13}
\]
Under \(Q\), the simulator’s memory remains separable conditioned on every past record. Its next action on a fresh input is therefore an LOCC channel. Hence, for every history \(h\),
\[
q_t(h):=Q(X_t=1\mid h)\le b.
\tag{14}
\]
There is no corresponding restriction on the actual conditional probabilities \(p_t(h)\).
Define the convex function
\[
g_b(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\le b,\\
d(u\Vert b),&u>b.
\end{cases}
\]
The classical relative-entropy chain rule and convexity imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(P\Vert Q)
&=
\sum_{t=1}^{n}
\mathbb E_P\,d(p_t(H_{t-1})\Vert q_t(H_{t-1}))\\
&\ge
\sum_{t=1}^{n}
\mathbb E_P\,g_b(p_t(H_{t-1}))\\
&\ge
n\,g_b\!\left(
\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb E_P X_t
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Under the ideal channel, the acceptance bits are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(a\). Operational error at most \(\varepsilon_n\) bounds the total-variation distance of the complete records, so
\[
\frac1n\sum_t\mathbb E_P X_t\ge a-\varepsilon_n.
\]
Substituting into (13)–(15) proves (12). ∎
The actual outputs need not be independent. The simulator may create temporal correlations and adapt its internal protocol. Those possibilities are included in \(P\).
Arbitrarily large exact catalysts cancel from the budget
Now suppose the simulator starts with \(m_n\) supplied ebits and an additional catalyst \(\chi_n\):
\[
\tau_n=\Phi_{2^{m_n}}\otimes\chi_n.
\]
The catalyst may depend on \(n\), have arbitrarily large finite dimension, and be returned correlated with other outputs.
Assume its final marginal is exactly \(\chi_n\). For the bound, return on the averaged test input already suffices.
Subadditivity and monotonicity under discarding give
\[
E_R(\tau_n)\le m_n\ln2+E_R(\chi_n),
\qquad
E_R(\tau_n')\ge E_R(\chi_n).
\]
Combining these with (12),
\[
\boxed{
m_n\ln2
\ge n\,d(a-\varepsilon_n\Vert b).
}
\tag{16}
\]
The catalyst’s size and entanglement have disappeared from the inequality.
Consequently, vanishing simulation error requires
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{m_n}{n}
\ge
\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal N}}{\ln2}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Conversely, if \(\mathcal N\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\), approximate a single use by an LOCC channel with error \(o(1/n)\) and repeat it. A hybrid argument gives vanishing sequential error with no supplied entanglement.
This proves
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N)=0
\iff
E_{\mathrm{seq}}(\mathcal N)=0
\iff
\mathcal N\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}.
}
\tag{18}
\]
This characterizes the zero-cost boundary. It does not give the optimal positive cost.
4. Strong converses
The expenditure law can be strengthened from “the error cannot vanish” to “the error approaches one.”
Exponential strong converse when all supplied entanglement is counted
Suppose the simulator’s only initial nonfree resource is \(m_n\) ebits. For
\[
K=2^{m_n},
\]
let
\[
\gamma_K=\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^{K}|jj\rangle\langle jj|.
\]
This state is separable, and
\[
\Phi_K\le K\gamma_K.
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore every event in every subsequent experiment satisfies
\[
P(A)\le KQ(A),
\tag{20}
\]
where \(Q\) is the law for the same simulator initialized with \(\gamma_K\).
Choose \(s\) with
\[
b<s<a,
\qquad
A_s=\left\{\frac1n\sum_tX_t\ge s\right\}.
\]
Under \(Q\), the conditional acceptance probabilities are bounded by \(b\). Iterating the conditional moment-generating-function bound gives
\[
\mathbb E_Q e^{\lambda\sum_tX_t}
\le (1-b+be^\lambda)^n
\quad(\lambda\ge0),
\]
and therefore
\[
Q(A_s)\le e^{-n d(s\Vert b)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Under the ideal channel,
\[
P_{\mathrm{ideal}}(A_s)
\ge1-e^{-n d(s\Vert a)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Using (20)–(22),
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_n
\ge
1-e^{-n d(s\Vert a)}
-
\exp\!\left(m_n\ln2-n d(s\Vert b)\right).
}
\tag{23}
\]
If
\[
\limsup_n\frac{m_n}{n}
<
\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal N}}{\ln2},
\]
choose \(s<a\) sufficiently close to \(a\). Both error terms in (23) then decay exponentially, so
\[
\varepsilon_n\longrightarrow1
\]
exponentially.
This is a strong converse below a certified positive threshold, not a claim that the threshold equals the unknown optimal cost.
Zero-rate strong converse even with uncharged exact catalysts
For arbitrarily large exactly returned catalysts, the entropy-budget argument still gives
\[
D(P\Vert Q)\le m_n\ln2.
\tag{24}
\]
Apply binary data processing to the event \(A_s\). Writing \(p=P(A_s)\) and \(q=Q(A_s)\),
\[
D(P\Vert Q)
\ge d(p\Vert q)
\ge p\ln(1/q)-\ln2.
\]
Together with (21),
\[
P(A_s)\le
\frac{m_n\ln2+\ln2}{n\,d(s\Vert b)}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_n
\ge
1-e^{-n d(s\Vert a)}
-
\frac{m_n\ln2+\ln2}{n\,d(s\Vert b)}.
}
\tag{25}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
m_n=o(n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varepsilon_n\to1,
}
\tag{26}
\]
even with arbitrarily large, mixed, exactly returned catalysts.
The exponential positive-rate statement in (23) and the catalyst-assisted zero-rate statement in (26) are distinct; the proof does not justify conflating them.
5. An independent consequence: positive cost without entanglement generation
The theorem applies to separable channels outside the closure of LOCC.
Such channels are not merely hypothetical. The domino-state constructions give orthonormal product bases whose LOCC discrimination error has a strictly positive lower bound. Childs, Leung, Mančinska and Ozols provide a quantitative framework proving such bounds. arXiv
For such a basis \(\{|a_kb_k\rangle\}\), define the measurement channel
\[
\mathcal M(\rho)
=
\sum_k
\langle a_kb_k|\rho|a_kb_k\rangle
\,|kk\rangle\langle kk|.
\tag{27}
\]
Its Kraus operators are
\[
K_k=
|k\rangle\langle a_k|
\otimes
|k\rangle\langle b_k|,
\]
so \(\mathcal M\) is separable. But it cannot lie in \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\): an approximating sequence would contradict the strictly positive discrimination-error bound.
Therefore (18) gives
\[
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{seq}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal M)>0.}
\tag{28}
\]
At the same time, its product Kraus form means it cannot generate entanglement from a separable state, even with local reference systems. Adaptive compositions with LOCC retain this property.
Thus the obstruction detects an operational resource that state-entanglement generation bounds cannot detect: implementing the channel costs entanglement at a positive rate even though using the channel cannot produce entanglement from free inputs.
The positive discrimination gap is inherited from prior work. The consequence here is the universal sequential resource-cost conclusion, including arbitrary exact catalysts.
6. Decisive limits and counterexamples
Approximate marginal return is not enough
There is no dimension-independent extension replacing exact return by small trace distance alone.
Let one entanglement bundle \(\Phi_D\) suffice to implement a fixed bipartite channel by teleportation. Construct locally readable, orthogonally flagged battery states
\[
\omega_k
=
|kk\rangle\langle kk|_{\mathrm{flag}}
\otimes
\Phi_D^{\otimes k}
\otimes
|00\rangle\langle00|^{\otimes(L-k)},
\qquad 0\le k\le L.
\]
Initialize
\[
\tau_L=\frac1L\sum_{k=1}^{L}\omega_k.
\]
Read the flag, consume one bundle to implement the channel, decrement the flag and reset the consumed slot. The final memory is
\[
\tau_L'=\frac1L\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\omega_k.
\]
The channel is implemented exactly, while
\[
\frac12\|\tau_L-\tau_L'\|_1=\frac1L.
\tag{29}
\]
Nevertheless, local flag measurement and the matching block-diagonal separable comparator give
\[
E_R(\tau_L)=\frac{L+1}{2}\ln D,
\qquad
E_R(\tau_L')=\frac{L-1}{2}\ln D.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{E_R(\tau_L)-E_R(\tau_L')=\ln D.}
\tag{30}
\]
Arbitrarily small marginal trace disturbance can conceal a fixed resource expenditure. This is consistent with the established embezzlement obstruction to unrestricted approximate-catalyst claims. Nature
Tomographic completeness is indispensable
The replacement theorem cannot be asserted for every resource theory.
Consider real quantum operations—channels with real Kraus operators—and the non-real state
\[
|+i\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+i|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Let
\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I+
|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes J.
\]
The unitary \(U\) is real, and \(J|+i\rangle=-i|+i\rangle\). Thus, for every input \(|\psi\rangle\),
\[
U(|\psi\rangle\otimes|+i\rangle)
=
\left(
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix}
|\psi\rangle
\right)\otimes|+i\rangle.
\tag{31}
\]
A non-real phase gate is implemented with an exactly returned catalyst.
The failed hypothesis is precise: real preparations and effects do not perform complete tomography of complex quantum channels. The universal version of the proposed principle is therefore false; the operational-tomography condition carries real mathematical content.
Why the tensor theorem is not contradicted
The earlier tensor construction concerns state-specific, stochastic conversion. Its successful local filters need not constitute a trace-preserving channel that returns the catalyst on a free tomographic input.
The removal theorem concerns the whole trace-preserving device, including all outcome branches. Returning a catalyst only on successful branches is insufficient. Neither previous tensor universality nor its stated assumptions need to be altered.
Why the sequential theorem does not settle unrestricted parallel simulation
For a causal simulator, conditioned on the past, the next test input is fresh. This is what makes
\[
q_t(h)\le b
\]
valid.
An arbitrary parallel LOCC block map can interact with all inputs before releasing any output. Conditioning on earlier observed outputs can then filter the remaining input-reference systems. The induced conditional operation need not be a trace-preserving LOCC channel on a fresh input.
Consequently, the current proof does not establish the same zero-cost characterization for unrestricted parallel block simulation. Removing that distinction requires a genuinely stronger direct-product argument, not a change of notation.
7. Originality and remaining obligations
The mathematical antecedents must be separated from the proposed contributions.
The entropy mechanism is established. Piani’s 2009 work supplies the restricted-measurement relative-entropy method. The proposed step is to keep a closest free initialization fixed through the actual protocol, yielding exact membership in the operational class rather than only a free-output conclusion. arXiv
The robust-catalysis question is documented. Son and coauthors explicitly distinguish LOCC from separable operations and stabilizer circuits from their resource-nongenerating enlargements, leaving the corresponding robust-catalysis questions open in the checked January 2026 version. Theorems (2)–(3) and the stabilizer application address those stated questions under the assumptions given here. arXiv
Some narrower no-advantage results already exist. Philip and Streltsov prove that entangled catalysts and reusable quantum memory do not improve the optimal LOCC discrimination probability for separable state pairs. Those discrimination phenomena are not new claims of this investigation. Their results are relevant antecedents to the repeated-testing argument. arXiv
The sequential cost model is also established. The proposed result is its faithful zero-cost boundary for all bipartite channels, the catalyst-independent resource-expenditure bound, and the stated strong converses—not the definition of channel entanglement cost. arXiv
I did not identify these precise operational conclusions in the sources checked. That is not a completed priority determination, and the targeted Astra checks do not substitute for one.
The proofs above have no intentionally deferred mathematical lemma under their stated finite-dimensional assumptions. Independent scrutiny is still required, particularly of the exact operational conventions and the catalyst-assisted rate claim. No efficient method for finding \(\gamma_*\), deciding LOCC membership or computing the optimal entanglement cost is supplied.
The strongest conclusion established here is a replacement-and-expenditure principle: in operational theories with free tomography, a resource-neutral memory can be removed without changing the implemented process; for genuinely non-LOCC sequential channels, even arbitrarily large exactly returned catalysts cannot eliminate positive entanglement expenditure.
That is the result to preserve. Its classification as an original historic breakthrough remains unestablished.
