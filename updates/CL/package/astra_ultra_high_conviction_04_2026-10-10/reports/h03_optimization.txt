H03 — nonlinear optimization: coefficient-discovered active nonconvex subspace
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: OPEN; exact reduction theorem and example established, no claim of a new algorithmic breakthrough.

Frozen first derivation (written before literature comparison)

Target. Find a checkable representation that makes a broad class of nonconvex global optimization problems reliably solvable without a supplied basin or a PL/convexity assumption on the objective.

Candidate mechanism. For a sparse rational polynomial f:R^n→R, write its Hessian by monomials

  ∇²f(x) = C + Σ_{β≠0} H_β x^β,

where C is its constant Hessian and each H_β is a symmetric rational matrix. Define the active space S = span_β im(H_β), and k=dim S. This space is recovered from the input coefficient list by exact rational row reduction; no active basis is supplied. If the restriction of C to S^⊥ is positive definite, choose an orthonormal basis U of S, P=UUᵀ, R=I−P, and a rational λ large enough that

  Q = RCR + RCP + PCR + λP

is positive definite. (An exact positive-definiteness test can choose λ by doubling.) Then C−Q and every nonconstant Hessian coefficient have image in S. Symmetry makes them vanish on S^⊥, so integrating twice gives

  f(x) = ½ xᵀQx + bᵀx + h(Uᵀx) + d,

for a polynomial h:R^k→R. Thus all nonconvexity is confined to a coefficient-discovered k-dimensional quotient; the other n−k directions are conditionally quadratic.

Exact reduction theorem. Let Q≻0 and f(x)=½xᵀQx+bᵀx+h(Uᵀx)+d, where UᵀU=I_k. Put x₀=−Q⁻¹b, G=UᵀQ⁻¹U≻0, and z₀=Uᵀx₀. For each z, the minimum of the quadratic part over Uᵀx=z is attained uniquely at

  x(z)=x₀+Q⁻¹U G⁻¹(z−z₀),

with value −½bᵀQ⁻¹b + ½(z−z₀)ᵀG⁻¹(z−z₀). Consequently

  inf_{x∈Rⁿ} f(x) = d−½bᵀQ⁻¹b + inf_{z∈Rᵏ} [h(z)+½(z−z₀)ᵀG⁻¹(z−z₀)].

Every attained reduced minimizer lifts by x(z). This is exact for arbitrary h, including h with many local minima; existence is equivalent to attainment of the reduced infimum. For rational polynomial input, all subspace and definiteness checks are exact. When k=1, a coercive reduced polynomial can be globally minimized by isolating all real roots of its derivative and comparing its values; for fixed small k, real-algebraic global methods apply, though their constants and dependence on k can be large.

Rational-coordinate note. Orthonormal U is only for a clean formula. From a rational basis B of S, compute the rational projector P=B(BᵀB)⁻¹Bᵀ and use Q above. Then write the residual polynomial as h(Bᵀx) and replace G,z₀,U by BᵀQ⁻¹B, Bᵀx₀, B. The same fiber-minimization identity holds, so the construction can stay over exact rationals rather than introducing square roots through Gram–Schmidt.

Exact example. Let Q₀=diag(2,3,4), u=(1,1,1)ᵀ, t=uᵀx, and

  f(x)=½xᵀQ₀x+t⁴−2t².

The Hessian's nonconstant part is 12t²uuᵀ, so S=span(u), k=1, and the orthogonal quadratic curvature is positive. The known Q₀ decomposition gives G=uᵀQ₀⁻¹u=13/12 and reduced objective

  F(t)=t⁴−(20/13)t².

Its two global minima are t=±√(10/13), with value −100/169, and lift to x=(6t/13,4t/13,3t/13). Thus a nonconvex 3-variable problem with multiple minima is solved by a one-variable global calculation. The purpose of the example is the exact quotient calculation, not a scale claim.

Hard boundary / falsifier. The reduction solves only the active quotient; it does not make arbitrary k-dimensional nonconvex optimization easy. For explicit sparse polynomial coefficients, constructing S is polynomial in coefficient-list size and matrix dimensions, but a compact arithmetic circuit can hide an exponentially large coefficient expansion. An oracle-only certificate is impossible from finitely many local probes: for any finite query set in an open domain, a smooth compactly supported bump can be placed in an unqueried ball, leaving values, gradients, and Hessians at all queried points unchanged while making a new, arbitrarily deep global well. Therefore this mechanism requires the globally inspectable coefficient representation; finite Hessian samples are not a certificate.

Resource interface. In the explicit-input route, provide rational sparse polynomial terms, their bit lengths, and unconstrained domain R^n; charge coefficient acquisition/expansion (M listed monomials), exact span computation, definiteness checks, O(nk) active-basis storage, reduced-objective construction, and exact global solving in k with its dependence on degree and coefficient height. In the compact-circuit route, provide circuit size s and total degree d; charge quadratic-jet construction, randomized essential-variable acquisition poly(n,d,s) algebraic operations, random bits/error, and exact bit costs for rational constants and output transformations. This is not polynomial in compact input length for unrestricted d, and no bit-polynomial claim is made for unbounded coefficient growth. The exact root-isolation interface avoids reliance on floating-point basin finding, but numerical output precision can still be governed by root separation and conditioning. Neither route covers general black-box smooth objectives or arbitrary constraints. No external training or basin is used.

Strongest current assessment. This gives a genuine, exact dimension-reduction certificate that can be discovered from polynomial coefficients, but the reduction pattern is close to established low-rank/separable nonconvex optimization and active-subspace ideas. The main unresolved question is whether the coefficient-span certificate is materially new/useful beyond those frameworks and whether realistic large objectives have small k after honest input-expansion costs. Post-freeze literature and acquisition audit

The coefficient-span reduction is mathematically valid, but neither its optimization pattern nor its polynomial variable-acquisition step is new enough to support a landmark claim:

* Carlini, “Reducing the number of variables of a polynomial” (2005; https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507531), characterizes the minimum number of essential variables of a homogeneous polynomial by a catalecticant rank and recovers the linear span of those variables from derivatives. It explicitly homogenizes general polynomials and gives an algorithmic presentation in fewer variables. Applying this componentwise to polynomial terms of degree at least three already recovers the nonlinear active space used above.
* Kayal, “Algorithms for Arithmetic Circuits” (ECCC TR10-073, 2010; https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/073/download), proves randomized essential-variable reduction from a polynomial arithmetic circuit, with running time polynomial in n, the degree d, and circuit size s (over characteristic zero, or characteristic greater than d). Thus the explicit coefficient list is not necessary for variable acquisition when d is polynomially bounded in the compact input size. For the present objective, the exact quadratic Taylor jet can be separated first; the residual has only degree-at-least-three terms. Kayal also records that extracting an individual coefficient from a general arithmetic circuit is #P-hard, so expansion is the wrong resource model for compact circuits. Important limit: the bound is polynomial in d, not log d; circuits of size s can have exponentially large degree, so this is not a polynomial-in-circuit-size acquisition guarantee for unrestricted circuits.
* Mittal and Schulz, “An FPTAS for optimizing a class of low-rank functions over a polytope” (Mathematical Programming, 2013; https://web.mit.edu/schulz/www/epapers/ms-mp-2013.pdf), define low-rank objectives as g(Aᵀx), already treating k as the dimension of nonconvexity. Their fixed-k FPTAS applies under explicit input/oracle and positivity assumptions and is exponential in k; they also give rank-two hardness of general low-rank minimization over polytopes. The Schur-complement identity here is an exact unconstrained/affine-fiber special case of variable elimination, not evidence of a new global-search principle.
* Golub and Pereyra, “The Differentiation of Pseudo-Inverses and Nonlinear Least Squares Problems Whose Variables Separate” (SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 10(2), 1973; https://doi.org/10.1137/0710036), established variable projection for separable nonlinear least squares. The present quadratic-fiber formula is an elementary exact elimination identity rather than their nonlinear least-squares algorithm, but it belongs to the established variable-elimination family.
* Cen and Xia, “A New Global Optimization Scheme for Quadratic Programs with Low-Rank Nonconvexity” (INFORMS Journal on Computing, 2021; https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2020.1017), give global branch-and-bound algorithms for nonconvex QPs with a small number of negative Hessian eigenvalues. The broader low-rank nonlinear setting is already a recognized research direction.
* Greuet and Safey El Din, “Probabilistic Algorithm for Polynomial Optimization over a Real Algebraic Set” (SIAM Journal on Optimization, 2014; https://doi.org/10.1137/130931308), give exact global infimum representations with complexity exponential in the number of variables, consistent with the usefulness of reducing the active quotient before invoking exact algebraic optimization.

Correction to the frozen resource statement: the direct coefficient-span construction above is polynomial in an explicit sparse-polynomial list, but the class need not be restricted to that input format. Kayal’s circuit result shows that essential-variable reduction can be randomized in poly(n,d,s) time without coefficient expansion; this is polynomial in compact input size only when d is polynomially bounded. The algorithm has a randomized algebraic identity-testing error, so this is not deterministic certification for arbitrary circuits. This removes coefficient expansion as a barrier in bounded-degree circuits, while confirming that the acquisition idea is prior art.

Discriminating compact-circuit instance. For even n≥4, let u=(1,…,1)ᵀ, t=uᵀx, and

  f_n(x)=½||x||²+tⁿ−t².

This has an O(n)-size rational arithmetic circuit and degree n. Its dense expansion contains binom(2n−1,n) monomials, exponential in n. Subtracting the exact quadratic Taylor jet leaves tⁿ, which has one essential variable; Kayal’s randomized circuit procedure therefore discovers a 1D representation in time polynomial in n for this family, without expanding those monomials. The constant Hessian is I−2uuᵀ, positive on u⊥, so the exact decomposition can keep Q=I and place −t² in the one-dimensional residual h(t)=tⁿ−t². The quadratic fiber minimum at fixed t is t²/(2n), so the exact reduced objective is

  F_n(t)=tⁿ−(1−1/(2n))t².

Writing α=1−1/(2n)>0, its two global minimizers are t=±(2α/n)^(1/(n−2)), and its minimum is −α(n−2)/n·(2α/n)^(2/(n−2)); each lifts to x=(t/n)u. This is a concrete success against coefficient expansion, not a new optimizer: the certificate is the known essential-variable reduction, the reduced problem is univariate, and the example’s symmetry makes its active direction transparent. It demonstrates that compact acquisition can matter in a family with exponentially many explicit monomials, while leaving the actual novelty claim falsified. The exact script check gives 68,923,264,410 degree-20 monomials at n=20 and confirms the formulas for n=4,6; it does not implement Kayal’s acquisition algorithm.

Constraint falsifier. The exact elimination formula depends on unconstrained fibers (or affine equalities). It does not extend into a general polytope with a tractable one-dimensional outer search. Indeed, a quadratic objective with one negative Hessian eigenvalue over a polytope is NP-hard (Pardalos and Vavasis, “Quadratic programming with one negative eigenvalue is NP-hard,” Journal of Global Optimization 1 (1991), https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00120662). Such an objective can be written as a positive-definite quadratic plus a rank-one concave quadratic term, so rank-one nonconvexity alone does not remove the constrained search barrier. The unconstrained result therefore has a sharp practical scope limitation.

Hidden-conditioning falsifier for numerical claims. Let ε_B=2^(−2^B), generated by an O(B)-gate rational arithmetic circuit via B repeated squarings from 1/2, and p_B(t)=(t²−1)²+ε_B t. For any ε∈(0,1], every t≥1 has p_ε(t)≥ε; for t∈[0,1], writing s=1−t gives p_ε(t)≥s²+ε(1−s)≥ε−ε²/4. Yet p_ε(−1)=−ε. Thus the best nonnegative-side value exceeds the global minimum by at least 2ε−ε²/4, which is Θ(2^(−2^B)). This explicit one-variable, degree-four objective has a tiny circuit and a unique preferred well, but ordinary numeric comparison needs exponentially many bits in B to certify which well is lower by its objective gap. Exact symbolic reasoning may still solve this special example; the point is that active-dimension reduction alone gives no uniform finite-precision guarantee for compact circuits.

Separate pivot tested: exact permutation-symmetry reduction. Suppose f is a coercive symmetric polynomial of degree D on R^n. Let m=inf f, attained by coercivity. For D≥4, the half-degree principle applied to f−m says that it has a zero with at most max(⌊D/2⌋,2) distinct coordinates. Hence at least one global minimizer has the form (a₁ repeated r₁ times, …, a_j repeated r_j times) for j≤max(⌊D/2⌋,2) and positive integer multiplicities summing to n. Enumerating the O(n^{j−1}) ordered multiplicity compositions and globally minimizing each j-variable restricted polynomial is exact; for fixed D, the high-dimensional search becomes polynomially many fixed-dimensional global problems. A circuit can be symmetry-checked by testing the n−1 adjacent-transposition identities with randomized polynomial identity testing; this carries the PIT error probability and the subsequent real-algebraic solver’s bit/degree costs. For fixed D, direct coefficient reconstruction is also polynomial in n^D.

This is a genuinely different mechanism from low-rank variable elimination, but it is established prior art, not a new result: Timofte’s degree principle (2003, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-247X(03)00301-9) and Riener’s optimization/half-degree formulation (2011, https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.4464) already prove these few-distinct-coordinate reductions. Its hard boundary is also explicit: the multiplicity enumeration scales as n^{Θ(D)}, and exact symmetry is essential; approximate symmetry alone does not justify a global optimum claim. It is a useful bounded-degree symmetric optimization method, not the requested new foundational route.

Final mission assessment. Two exact structural reductions survive mathematics: nonlinear active-space elimination for an unconstrained positive-quadratic remainder, and the degree principle for symmetric fixed-degree polynomials. Both have direct primary-source antecedents. The first also loses its broad consequence under general polyhedral constraints; the second is already a mature theorem. No new 8/10+ foundational route or practical global optimizer was found in this pass. OPEN.
