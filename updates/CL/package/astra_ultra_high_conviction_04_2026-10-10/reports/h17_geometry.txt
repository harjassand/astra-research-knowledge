# h17_geometry — frozen independent derivation (pre-literature)

## Target and candidate mechanism

Target: an exact geometric certificate for when a finite point cloud has no positive-degree Vietoris–Rips persistent homology at any scale. Candidate family: finite subsets of a real tree, with the tree (or a certified tree realization) supplied as the geometric input. The mechanism is that equal-radius balls in a tree form subtrees, and their intersection graph has a perfect elimination ordering. This gives a combinatorial collapse certificate for every scale.

This is a restricted structural theorem, not a claim of a transformative discovery. The possible algorithmic consequence is to replace all positive-degree persistence computations by a short elimination certificate plus the ordinary H0 merge data for tree-metric inputs.

## Frozen lemma

Let T be a real tree, X={x_1,...,x_n} a finite set of points in T, and r≥0. Define G_r on X by ij∈E(G_r) iff d_T(x_i,x_j)≤r. Let VR_r(X) be its clique complex. Then every connected component of VR_r(X) is collapsible to one vertex. Consequently H_k(VR_r(X);F)=0 for every field F and every k>0, at every r.

A more constructive form: let B_i be the closed tree ball centered at x_i with radius r/2. Then ij∈E(G_r) iff B_i∩B_j≠∅. The intersection graph of any finite family of subtrees of T is chordal and admits a perfect elimination ordering. A simplicial vertex v in G_r has a clique as its link in VR_r(X); the star of v collapses onto that link, so deleting v is an elementary simplicial collapse. Iterating a perfect elimination ordering collapses each component to a point.

## Proof sketch, sufficient to audit

1. In a real tree, the unique geodesic [x_i,x_j] is contained in the union of the radius-r/2 balls exactly when the balls meet; specifically, B_i∩B_j≠∅ iff d_T(x_i,x_j)≤r. Thus VR_r(X) is the clique complex of the intersection graph of the B_i.
2. Root T. For any nonempty subtree S, let a(S) be its point closest to the root. Among a finite family of subtrees, choose S whose a(S) has maximal depth. If U intersects S at z, then a(U) and a(S) both lie on the root-to-z arc. Maximality of depth gives depth(a(U))≤depth(a(S)); connectedness of U therefore implies a(S)∈U. So every neighbor of S contains a(S), hence any two neighbors intersect. S is simplicial in the intersection graph. The argument repeats after deleting S, proving a perfect elimination ordering.
3. If v is simplicial, its link in the clique complex is the full simplex on N(v). The closed star is the cone v*link; a simplex cone collapses onto its base link. Since the rest of the complex meets that star exactly in the link, these elementary collapses delete v and all faces containing it. Apply this at each vertex in a perfect elimination ordering. Components remain separate and each ends at one vertex.

Edge cases: for r=0, distinct points give isolated vertices (the conclusion holds); repeated sampled locations can be merged or handled as a clique of duplicate vertices, also collapsible.

## Discriminating falsifier / boundary

The tree assumption is essential. Four vertices with cyclic distances 1 and diagonal distances 2 have VR_1 equal to a 4-cycle, so H_1 is nonzero. This is a concrete failure as soon as the input geometry leaves the tree class. More generally, an arbitrary metric cannot be given this collapse certificate merely from a graph threshold; it needs a common tree realization or another certificate that its threshold balls are subtrees of one tree.

## Resource interface

The proof assumes the tree geometry and sample locations are supplied. It does not charge or solve geometric acquisition from a point cloud. With an explicit weighted tree and vertex/sample locations, each distance can be obtained by tree traversal/LCA preprocessing; all pair distances and threshold edge events take O(n^2) pair outputs in a dense point-cloud interface. For a distance-matrix-only interface, certifying/reconstructing the tree metric must be separately charged; no cost claim is made here. For each threshold, one can construct the intersection graph and elimination order from the tree and ball locations, and emit the collapses. A complete persistence output also needs H0 merge events; this lemma eliminates only positive degrees. No claim is made that the certificate compresses all pairwise distance information or beats the best tree-metric persistence algorithms.

## Frozen status before prior-art check

Proof status: internally derived elementary lemma; no external validation yet. Priority/novelty: UNKNOWN. Foundational consequence: plausibly useful only as a certified reduction for tree-metric point clouds, and likely known. OPEN as to any extension with substantial leverage beyond tree-like inputs.

## Post-literature pivot: distance acquisition lower bound (independent derivation)

The frozen collapse theorem itself is established prior art, so the concrete candidate contribution is now a lower bound on acquiring enough geometry to certify a persistent topological distinction. This section was derived after the first source comparison and before a targeted search for oracle-complexity prior art.

### Promise family and exact barcode witness

Let N=binom(n,2), and use an exact pair-distance oracle on labels [n]. The baseline d_0 has d_0(i,j)=2 for i≠j; it is the n-leaf star tree with unit edge lengths. For each undirected 4-cycle C on four labels, define d_C(i,j)=1 when {i,j} is one of its four cycle edges, and d_C(i,j)=2 on every other distinct pair. This is a metric: all nonzero distances are in {1,2}, so every triangle inequality holds. Its four-point pairing sums are 2, 2, and 4, so it is not a tree metric. At threshold 1, VR(d_C) is precisely a disjoint union of one induced C4 and n−4 isolated vertices. Its flag complex has H_1≅F, and at threshold 2 it is the full simplex. Thus its H_1 barcode has the interval [1,2), of length 1. The baseline d_0 has no positive-degree homology at any scale. The promise task is to distinguish d_0 from the family {d_C} using pair queries.

### Deterministic exact query lower bound

Run a deterministic exact algorithm on d_0 and form the graph U whose edges are the unqueried pairs. If U contains a 4-cycle C, then the same query transcript is possible for d_C: all four changed pairs were unqueried and every queried distance remains 2. Since the two promised inputs have different H_1 barcodes, the algorithm cannot be correct on both. Therefore U must be C4-free on the d_0 path.

For any C4-free graph with m edges, every pair of vertices has at most one common neighbor, so
\[
\sum_v {\deg(v)\choose 2}\le {n\choose 2}.
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
\sum_v {\deg(v)\choose 2}≥ 2m^2/n-m.
\]
Solving gives
\[
m\le \frac n4\left(1+\sqrt{4n-3}\right)=O(n^{3/2}).
\]
Hence the algorithm must query at least
\[
{n\choose 2}-\frac n4(1+\sqrt{4n-3})
\]
pairs in the worst case. This is asymptotically one half of all distances.

### Randomized bounded-error query lower bound

Choose C uniformly from all 4-cycles on [n]. Every fixed pair is a cycle edge with probability 4/N. Consider any randomized adaptive algorithm making at most q queries. Fix its random seed and follow its query path on d_0; that path is independent of C. By a union bound, the random C avoids every pair on that path with probability at least 1−4q/N. Whenever it does, the complete transcript and output are exactly those on d_0.

Put equal prior mass on d_0 and on a uniformly random d_C, and let p be the probability that the algorithm outputs “d_0” on d_0. Its average error is at least
\[
\tfrac12(1-p)+\tfrac12p(1-4q/N)≥ \tfrac12(1-4q/N).
\]
Therefore error at most ε on every promised input requires
\[
q\ge \frac N4(1-2\varepsilon).
\]
In particular, error ≤1/3 requires q≥N/12=Ω(n²). This applies to randomized algorithms with adaptive pair selection, since the no-hit transcript is the baseline path for the same random seed.

### Interpretation, interface, and limitations

The four changed distances are an explicit geometric encoding of one unit-length H_1 bar; the lower bound says that pairwise-distance acquisition cannot locate that encoding with subquadratic worst-case queries under this promise. This blocks a broad claim that one can certify tree-like topology from a small number of arbitrary landmark distances. The bound is about query access, not computation after the full matrix is available.

All n labels are supplied free; one oracle query returns one exact real distance at unit cost. No coordinates, embedding, tree topology, or certificate are supplied. The baseline has a free explicit tree realization, but the algorithm is not told which input it received. The alternate metrics are valid abstract metrics, though not tree metrics. The promise family is deliberately narrow. The result does not rule out subquadratic recovery under coordinate access, distributional assumptions, a known embedding, approximate topology, or structured/active distance access. The deterministic bound concerns exact classification; the randomized bound is for constant-error classification. Extending either bound to general point clouds, other homology dimensions, noisy/approximate oracles, or adaptive landmark-selection models remains OPEN.

## Literature and novelty comparison

- Bauer and Roll, “Gromov Hyperbolicity, Geodesic Defect, and Apparent Pairs in Vietoris–Rips Filtrations,” SoCG 2022, establish the same central tree-metric cancellation direction: their Theorem 1.5 gives filtration-compatible collapses to corresponding subforests for finite weighted tree metrics, and they also prove a collapse theorem for finite δ-hyperbolic, ν-geodesic metric spaces above a scale threshold. They connect the tree collapses to apparent-pair optimizations in Ripser. The source is stronger at the filtration level for its stated finite weighted tree metrics; the frozen per-scale chordal-graph proof is closely related and is not a novelty claim.
- The oracle lower bound above has only been checked internally so far. Its priority is UNKNOWN pending targeted primary-source search. Its candidate consequence is a worst-case acquisition barrier, not a new positive homology algorithm or a demonstrated discovery breakthrough.

## Interim status after the first prior-art check

The tree representation is a close match to prior art and is not a novelty claim. The distance-oracle lower bound is a proved elementary promise-family result with an explicit H_1 witness and resource accounting; whether it is new or consequential beyond this narrow model remains OPEN. No external validation or priority claim.

Targeted primary-source comparison for the oracle pivot:

- Kurlin, “Approximating persistent homology for a cloud of n points in a subquadratic time” (arXiv:1312.1494, 2013), constructs a sublinear-size approximation and subquadratic-time computation, with constants depending on doubling dimension. Its stated input is a point cloud “given by their pairwise distances”; it studies computation/approximation after that input is available, not the pair-query acquisition lower bound here. https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1494
- McCabe, “Lower Bounds for Approximating the Vietoris–Rips Filtration” (arXiv:2607.06524, posted July 2026), proves size lower bounds for finitely presented homotopy-interleaving approximations on general finite metric spaces. That is a representation-size lower bound, not an oracle query lower bound. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06524
- Bauer and Roll, “Gromov Hyperbolicity, Geodesic Defect, and Apparent Pairs in Vietoris–Rips Filtrations” (SoCG 2022), proves filtered collapses for finite tree metrics and high-scale collapses for finite hyperbolic, almost-geodesic metrics, and relates the tree collapses to Ripser apparent pairs. This establishes the core tree-cancellation direction before this work. https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2022.15

Searches for exact persistent-homology distance-query lower bounds found these adjacent but model-distinct primary sources; they do not establish novelty of the promise-family reduction. Priority remains UNKNOWN. The central contribution that survives the first prior-art check is a small explicit metric encoding and a transparent pair-query lower bound, whose broader scientific significance is limited unless generalized or tied to a real acquisition constraint.

## Final status

Exact result proved internally: for the stated promise family, deciding whether a unit H_1 bar is present takes Θ(n²) pair-distance queries; the deterministic exact lower bound is nearly N, and the randomized constant-error lower bound is N/12. This is a rigorous acquisition barrier in a narrow oracle model, not a transformative geometric representation. The initial tree-cancellation mechanism is known in close and stronger forms. OPEN: priority of the query lower bound, extensions to broader metric families or approximate/noisy access, and whether any useful positive representation can evade the barrier under additional geometric structure.
