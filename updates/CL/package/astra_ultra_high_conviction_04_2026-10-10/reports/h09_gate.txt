# H09 gate — finite one-port acquisition of the cubic contraction

**Status: OPEN.** The finite-query route does remove the inaccessible-state
objection at the level of exact one-port input/output data. It yields a
conditional, generic reconstruction theorem for a dense symmetric oscillator,
not yet a practical measurement capability. The physical-kernel acquisition
step has a concrete finite-tone implementation and a numerical port-only
check, but the full noise, calibration, and settling-time budget is still
unclosed. Novelty is also unestablished against recent nonlinear-network
identifiability work and established higher-order FRF measurement.

## Target and exact observable

Use the stable one-port mechanical model

\[
\ddot q+\gamma\dot q+Kq+\operatorname{diag}(\alpha)q^{\odot3}=e_1u,
\qquad y=e_1^Tq,
\]

with real symmetric positive-definite \(K\), common \(\gamma>0\), and
positive nodewise cubic coefficients. Only the force \(u\) and displacement
\(y\) at port 1 are controlled or observed; input/output units and a shared
phase/time reference are supplied. Let

\[
Q(s)=(s^2+\gamma s+K)^{-1}e_1,
\quad K=U\Lambda U^T,
\quad a=U^Te_1.
\]

The linear transfer is \(H_1(s)=\sum_i a_i^2/(s^2+\gamma s+\lambda_i)\).
For simple, visible modes, it fixes \(\lambda_i\), \(a_i^2\), and the common
damping (or that damping can be calibrated separately). Fix modal signs by
\(a_i>0\). As in the linear problem, \(H_1\) alone leaves the hidden orthogonal
orientation undetermined: for generic \(a\), the residual \(O(n-1)\) orbit has
dimension \((n-1)(n-2)/2\). The contraction below supplies a node-basis spectral
frame that fixes this continuous ambiguity under the distinct-\(\beta\) gate.

With the symmetric-kernel convention used here, the cubic Volterra kernel is

\[
H_3(s_1,s_2,s_3)=-\sum_r\alpha_r Q_r(s_1+s_2+s_3)
 Q_r(s_1)Q_r(s_2)Q_r(s_3).
\]

Set \(c=(s-t)/2\), use inputs \((R+c,-R+c,t)\), and let
\(F_R(s,t)=-H_3(R+c,-R+c,t)\). The sum frequency is \(s\). The port term is
\(C(s,t)p(R)\), where

\[
C(s,t)=\alpha_1Q_1(s)Q_1(t),\qquad
p(R)=Q_1(R+c)Q_1(-R+c)=R^{-4}+p_6R^{-6}+p_8R^{-8}+O(R^{-10}).
\]

For \(r>1\), the large-frequency response is
\(Q_r(z)=-K_{r1}z^{-4}+O(z^{-5})\). The opposite-frequency product is even
in \(R\), with leading term \(K_{r1}^2R^{-8}\). Hence

\[
F_R=c_4R^{-4}+c_6R^{-6}+c_8R^{-8}+O(R^{-10}),\quad
c_4=C(s,t),\quad c_8=p_8c_4+B(s,t),
\]

where

\[
B(s,t)=\sum_{r>1}\beta_rQ_r(s)Q_r(t),\qquad
\beta_r=\alpha_rK_{r1}^2.
\]

Thus \(B=c_8-p_8c_4\); \(c_6\) is not needed for the subtraction. If
\(\alpha_1\) is unknown, \(c_4\) also recovers
\(\alpha_1=c_4/[Q_1(s)Q_1(t)]\). The known \(p_8\) can be evaluated from
linear data. In terms of \(d=2c+\gamma\), \(e=c^2+\gamma c\),
\(m_1=\sum_i a_i^2\lambda_i\), and
\(m_2=\sum_i a_i^2\lambda_i^2\), direct expansion gives

\[
p_8=d^4-4d^2(e+m_1)+3e^2+6m_1e+m_1^2+2m_2.
\]

This is an observable contraction, not the full quartic tensor.

## Finite residue recovery and matrix reconstruction

Let \(D(z)=\det(z^2+\gamma z+K)=\prod_i(z^2+\gamma z+\lambda_i)\),
whose coefficients and roots come from \(H_1\). For fixed \((s,t)\),
\(F_R\) is a strictly proper rational function of \(R\) with common
denominator \(D(R+c)D(-R+c)\), of degree \(4n\). In the generic simple-pole
case write

\[
F_R=\sum_{\ell=1}^{4n}\frac{r_\ell}{R-z_\ell},\qquad
z_\ell\in\{p_i^+-c,p_i^--c,c-p_i^+,c-p_i^-\},
\quad p_i^\pm=-\gamma/2\pm i\sqrt{\lambda_i-\gamma^2/4}.
\]

The expansion at infinity yields \(c_4=\sum_\ell r_\ell z_\ell^3\) and
\(c_8=\sum_\ell r_\ell z_\ell^7\). Choose 4n distinct non-pole sample points
\(R_j=i\nu_j\), and form

\[
\mathcal C_{j\ell}=\frac1{i\nu_j-z_\ell},\quad F_j=F_{i\nu_j},\quad
w=\mathcal C^{-T}(z^7-p_8z^3).
\]

Then \(\mathcal Cr=F\) and exactly \(B(s,t)=w^TF\). This is finite rational
interpolation from real-frequency one-port data; no probe at infinity and no
internal coordinate is needed. Since \(F_R\) is even in \(R\), the number of
independent samples can be reduced using \(R^2\), but 4n per low-frequency
pair is a simple conservative count. Exact cancellations merely give zero
residues in this common-denominator representation; repeated poles require a
confluent rational fit. The displayed Cauchy formula assumes a generic simple
pole set and distinct sample locations.

Write \(v_i(s)=a_i/(s^2+\gamma s+\lambda_i)\). Then

\[
B(s,t)=v(s)^TAv(t),\qquad
A=U^T\operatorname{diag}(0,\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_n)U.
\]

For two frequency grids \(s_j,t_k\), let \(V_s,V_t\) have columns
\(v(s_j),v(t_k)\). If both are nonsingular, \(B_{jk}=B(s_j,t_k)\) gives
\(A=V_s^{-T}BV_t^{-1}\). Using the same grid is the special case
\(V_s=V_t\). If all hidden \(\beta_r\) are positive and pairwise distinct,
the eigendecomposition of \(A\) returns the hidden rows of \(U\) up to signed
permutation; its zero eigenvector is the known port row \(a\). Therefore
\(K=U\Lambda U^T\) is recovered up to hidden signed-permutation gauge. If
the \(\alpha_r\) are unknown, recover them as \(\beta_r/K_{r1}^2\) after
reconstructing \(K\).

This is an exact generic finite-data identification statement within the
model. It requires visible simple modes \(a_i\ne0\), a full model order and
known/calibrated common damping, nonzero distinct hidden \(\beta_r\),
invertible modal grids, and a simple known pole set for the residue formula.
It resolves a continuous linear orthogonal ambiguity with one nonlinear
observable contraction, rather than requiring recovery of every quartic
tensor coefficient.

## Physical input/output gate

For \(s=i\omega_s\), \(t=i\omega_t\), \(c=i\omega_c\), and
\(R=i\Omega\), use three real input tones at positive frequencies
\(\Omega+\omega_c\), \(\Omega-\omega_c\), and \(\omega_t\). The targeted
intermodulation component at \(\omega_s=2\omega_c+\omega_t\) uses the signed
frequency triple \((+\Omega+\omega_c,-\Omega+\omega_c,+\omega_t)\). With
generic non-colliding tones, the measured output Fourier coefficient is
proportional to \(H_3\) and has phase signature
\(\phi_1-\phi_2+\phi_3\). The factor is known from the chosen Volterra
convention; for cosine amplitudes \(\rho\) and the displayed kernel it is
\(6(\rho/2)^3=3\rho^3/4\). Every signal in the construction is measured at
the same port. H1 supplies the pole locations; H3 is acquired by forced
intermodulation. The formulas do not use measured \(Q_r\), interior sensors,
or a state observer.

A finite real-amplitude estimator for one H3 value is an eight-run mixed sign
cycle. If \(Z(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) is the complex lock-in coefficient at the target
frequency, use

\[
M(\rho)=\frac1{8\rho^3}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^3}
\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3Z(\rho\sigma_1,\rho\sigma_2,\rho\sigma_3).
\]

For analytic dynamics and a non-colliding target, this approaches
\((3/4)H_3e^{i(\phi_1-\phi_2+\phi_3)}\) with \(O(\rho^2)\) bias. The
two-level estimate \((4M(\rho)-M(2\rho))/3\) cancels the leading
fifth-order term and has \(O(\rho^4)\) residual bias if the next Taylor
coefficient is bounded. A small detuning between the two low-frequency grids
avoids the output coinciding with the third input; generic small perturbations
of sample frequencies avoid accidental tone collisions while preserving a
strictly conditioned interpolation design. Repeated-frequency cases can
instead be extracted as repeated-tone derivatives, but are avoidable with
disjoint low-frequency grids.

The independent numerical check integrates a three-mode oscillator from rest
and uses only input tones and the scalar output. Its measured mixed coefficient
has relative error \(4.19\times10^{-3}\) at drive amplitude 0.1,
\(2.64\times10^{-4}\) at 0.025, and \(6.97\times10^{-6}\) after two-level
Richardson extrapolation at 0.05/0.10. This verifies the factor and interface
on one synthetic example; it is not an experiment, a noise study, or a test of
the full matrix recovery.

## Conditioning and resource charge

The 4n-pole interpolation matrix is Cauchy. A sufficient route to a useful
bound is to require underdamped distinct modes and frequency-pole clusters
consisting of isolated poles or pairs only. Same-side modal spacings must be
large relative to \(n\gamma\); clusters must be separated by large multiples
of \(n\gamma\). Sampling at the imaginary parts of isolated poles gives
diagonal entries of size \(2/\gamma\); a close opposite-real-part pair can be
sampled at its center plus/minus \(\gamma/2\), giving a nonsingular 2-by-2
block. Diagonal dominance then yields \(\|C^{-1}\|=O(\gamma)\) under an
explicit pole-separation constant. The sibling residue checker uses a
conservative \(\delta_\omega\ge 400n\gamma\) regime and rejects clusters of
size greater than two. That condition is a real model gate, not a generic
property of every oscillator. Exact residue identity is established above;
the all-cluster norm constants should still be treated as conditional until
proved for the precise sample design.

My independent two-mode calculation evaluates 8 one-port H3 samples, solves
the residue system, and compares \(c_8-p_8c_4\) to the direct hidden
contraction; the absolute discrepancy is \(8.0\times10^{-10}\). Its arbitrary
sample grid has Cauchy condition number \(6.08\times10^6\), despite the exact
identity. This is a negative check: the algebra does not make sample placement
innocuous. The pole-adapted conditioning design is essential, and the
calculation remains an oracle-value simulation rather than acquired physical
data.

For the low-frequency modal matrix, at \(s_j=i\sqrt{\lambda_j}\),

\[
(V_s)_{ij}=\frac{a_i}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j+i\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_j}}.
\]

After scaling rows by \(1/a_i\) and columns by \(i\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_j}\),
the matrix is \(I+E\), with off-diagonal entries bounded by
\(\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}/\delta_\lambda\), where
\(\delta_\lambda=\min_{i\ne j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\). Thus if
\((n-1)\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}/\delta_\lambda\le1/2\),
\(\|V_s^{-1}\|\le 2\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}/a_{\min}\). A grid detuned by
a small fraction of a linewidth has the same bound up to a constant. For
\(\varepsilon_H\) absolute error per acquired H3 value and residue weights
\(w_{jk}\),

\[
\|\Delta B\|_2\le n\max_{jk}\|w_{jk}\|_1\varepsilon_H,
\quad
\|\Delta A\|_2\le
n\|V_s^{-1}\|_2\|V_t^{-1}\|_2
\max_{jk}\|w_{jk}\|_1\varepsilon_H.
\]

Under the pole-cluster bound, \(|z_\ell|=O(\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}})\) and
\(\|C^{-1}\|=O(\gamma)\) imply
\(\|w\|_1=O(n\gamma\lambda_{\max}^{7/2})\). For the same grid this gives
\(\|\Delta A\|_2=O(n^2\gamma^3\lambda_{\max}^{9/2}
\varepsilon_H/a_{\min}^2)\). Eigenvector perturbation then costs the inverse
hidden eigenvalue gap
\(\Delta_\beta=\min(\min_r\beta_r,\min_{r\ne s}|\beta_r-\beta_s|)\);
when \(\|\Delta A\|<\Delta_\beta/2\), Davis–Kahan gives row error of order
\(\|\Delta A\|/\Delta_\beta\), and \(K\)-error adds the spectral scale
\(\|\Lambda\|\). These explicit margins can be arbitrarily poor even though
the model is generically identifiable.

Before those cubic queries, H1 needs its own low-amplitude frequency sweep.
In exact arithmetic its structured 2n-pole response has O(n) modal parameters
and can be fitted from O(n) generic frequency samples; finite precision still
requires every residue to be visible and pole locations/linewidths to be
calibrated on a scale small compared with \(\gamma\). The algebraic count is
4n H3 samples per low pair and n^2 pairs, or
4n^3 cubic-frequency conditions (evenness can reduce this). A sufficient
finite-amplitude acquisition uses 8 sign runs per H3 sample; two-level
extrapolation makes that 16 records, or up to 64n^3 steady-state records,
before repeats. At n=8 this is 32,768 records. Each needs ring-up and a
frequency-resolved output window, typically at least order 1/\gamma near a
resonance, plus averaging. The sample tones remain moderate—order
\(\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\), not arbitrarily high frequency—but the weak
damping needed by the current pole-separation proof can make the settling time
very long. In the sibling n=8 synthetic scale, \(\gamma\approx2.68\times10^{-5}\)
while modal frequencies are order 1; the damping time \(1/\gamma\) is about
37,000 time units.

The small-drive condition also has a cost. For an isolated modal coordinate
with effective cubic stiffness \(\tilde\alpha_j=\sum_r\alpha_rU_{rj}^4\),
the linear resonant modal amplitude is approximately
\(|a_j|\rho/(\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_j})\). To keep its cubic frequency shift
small relative to the linewidth, require
\(\tilde\alpha_j|a_j\rho/(\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_j})|^2
\ll\gamma\sqrt{\lambda_j}\), or
\(\rho\ll\gamma^{3/2}\lambda_j^{3/4}/(|a_j|\sqrt{\tilde\alpha_j})\).
This is a local single-mode scaling; cross-modal and multitone peaks can make
the true safe amplitude smaller. When all four factors in an H3 term are near
resonance, \(H_3=O(\gamma^{-4})\); at the safe scale
\(\rho=O(\gamma^{3/2})\), the measured cubic output is only
\(O(\gamma^{1/2})\). Since the estimator divides an output coefficient by
\(\rho^3\), fixed output noise is amplified as the drive is reduced. The
sibling checker adds noise directly to already-extracted H3 values, so its
tiny synthetic H3-noise errors do not establish sensor-level robustness. A
useful end-to-end claim needs a noise spectrum, allowed drive,
pole-calibration tolerance (roughly a small fraction of \(\gamma\)), repeated
record count, and model-mismatch bound. None is supplied.

## Falsifiers, prior work, and verdict

The exact boundary is visible. If a hidden block is disconnected from port 1,
write \(K=K_{\rm vis}\oplus K_{\rm dark}\). From zero initial state every
one-port input/output trajectory is independent of \(K_{\rm dark}\) and its
local cubic coefficients; no probe can recover that structure. Within the
contraction route, any \(K_{r1}=0\) gives \(\beta_r=0\), and repeated
\(\beta\)'s give a degenerate eigenspace of \(A\), so this particular inverse
fails. This does not prove all-order non-identifiability for a connected sparse
network; higher-order coefficients may reveal farther layers. It does rule
out an unconditional “one cubic probe recovers every hidden network” claim.

The physical act of measuring nonlinear frequency kernels is old. Boyd, Tang,
and Chua’s primary 1983 paper gives multitone methods for measuring Volterra
kernels and includes an experimental electro-acoustic example
([paper and source PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/volterra_kernel.html)).
Lin and Ng’s 2018 primary paper presents sinusoidal/correlation methods for
accurate higher-order FRFs, explicitly including third order and nonlinear
parameter identification in structural systems
([DOI page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isatra.2018.05.015)). Thus the tone
mixing/phase extraction is a known measurement technique, not the proposed
novelty.

The closest network-level competitor is Vanelli and Hendrickx, arXiv
2412.08472v4 (31 Aug 2026). It proves generic local identifiability of known
edge-topology network weights with known analytic node activations from one
excited node per source component and one measured node per sink component;
it explicitly includes recurrent graphs. Its state update is a discrete-time
static activation of weighted previous node outputs, while this candidate is
a continuous-time, second-order mechanical ODE with symmetric positive
stiffness and componentwise cubic force. The competitor is broader in
activation/topology class and local-identifiability scope; this route is
stronger in giving an explicit global reconstruction modulo signed
permutations for its narrower model. The paper itself distinguishes
identifiability from practical identification and says the direct high-order
coefficient extraction is noise-sensitive and does not give an algorithm
([primary v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08472v4), especially its model and
discussion). This overlap makes the model-specific theorem potentially
interesting but insufficient to claim a foundational breakthrough.

**Verdict:** the finite-frequency map genuinely uses only one-port signals and
does not secretly read hidden states. The exact contraction and matrix inverse
are a real conditional capability, with a finite algebraic count of
\(O(n^3)\) H3 values and explicit generic gates. But the end-to-end physical
claim remains open: H3 values have only been simulated for one small probe,
pole calibration must resolve linewidths, safe drive shrinks like
\(\gamma^{3/2}\), the conservative regime settles slowly, and actual sensor
noise/model mismatch has not been propagated through residue extraction and
the \(\beta\)-eigenvector step. Prior art establishes both nonlinear-kernel
measurement and generic nonlinear-network identifiability. Do not call this
an externally validated landmark.
