Independent coordinator derivation, before literature comparison.

Consider dx/dt=-Kx-alpha*x^{componentwise 3}+b*u, y=b^T*x, with K SPD,
known alpha>0 and known accessible b=e_1. Write K=U diag(lambda) U^T,
a=U^T b. Linear response gives the simple eigenvalues and squared a_i;
choose eigenvector signs so a_i>0 when the pair is observable.

The symmetric cubic Volterra transfer, ignoring the known sign/alpha
normalization, is
 H3(s1,s2,s3) = sum_ijkl T_ijkl a_i a_j a_k a_l /
 ((s1+s2+s3+lambda_i)(s1+lambda_j)(s2+lambda_k)(s3+lambda_l)),
 T_ijkl=sum_r U_ri U_rj U_rk U_rl.

If this rational linear map is injective on symmetric quartic tensors,
all T can in principle be recovered from one-port multitone responses.
The orthogonally decomposable quartic T=sum_r u_r^{tensor4} identifies
its real orthonormal rows up to signed permutations: contraction against
v,v produces U^T diag((Uv)_r^2) U, generically with distinct eigenvalues.
This would remove the orthogonal similarity ambiguity of linear response.

Critical gates: rational-map injectivity (frequency-sum denominators cause
relations), finite precision conditioning, attainable measurement of H3,
degenerate modes/nonobservable a_i, nonlinear conjugacy, strongest previous
nonlinear realization/network-tomography methods. This is not yet a theorem
or novel result. A finite-field rank test will distinguish the first gate
for small n only. No physics or experimental result is claimed.
