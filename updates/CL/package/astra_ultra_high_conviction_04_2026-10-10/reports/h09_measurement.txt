# H09 — one-port nonlinear dynamics as an exact probe of hidden structure

**Status: OPEN.** This is an internally derived, model-specific structural-identifiability result, not an externally validated discovery or a practical measurement protocol. It gives a concrete all-dimension reconstruction mechanism under a supplied local cubic law. Prior art already establishes the broad nonlinear-identifiability/Lie-derivative framework, and the newest network-identifiability result is a close conceptual competitor. The novelty and practical leverage therefore remain unestablished.

The first derivation is preserved unchanged in [first_derivation.txt](h09_measurement/first_derivation.txt). It froze a quadratic-response witness before literature search. The stronger route below replaces that witness as the main candidate; its symbolic checker is [lie_reconstruction_check.py](h09_measurement/lie_reconstruction_check.py).

## Target and baseline

Target: recover the hidden symmetric coupling matrix of a stable (n)-mode network from one driven and observed port, using the system's known nodewise nonlinearity as an additional probe. No interior state is supplied or measured, and no graph sparsity pattern is assumed.

The model is

\[
\dot x=f(x)+gu=-Kx-\alpha x^{\odot3}+e_1u,\qquad y=h(x)=e_1^Tx,
\]

where (K=K^T\succ0), the common α is known and nonzero, the state is reset to (x(0)=0), and input/output units are calibrated. The small input response stays in the stable local regime. The exact ideal resource is the local input-output Taylor/Fliess jet at rest for arbitrary sufficiently small one-port inputs; no numerical noise or finite-precision guarantee is assumed.

For the linearized system, the complete transfer function is only

\[
H_1(s)=e_1^T(sI+K)^{-1}e_1=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{c_i^2}{s+\lambda_i},
\]

with λ_i the eigenvalues and (c_i=u_i^Te_1). It determines the spectrum and port overlaps, but is invariant under (K\mapsto QKQ^T) for every orthogonal (Qe_1=e_1). For generic simple spectrum and overlaps, the remaining orientation orbit has dimension ((n-1)(n-2)/2). Thus linear one-port probing cannot fix the hidden orientation for (n\ge3).

## Finite-jet reconstruction mechanism

Use the vector-field bracket convention ([X,Y]=DY\,X-DX\,Y), and write (L_X\phi=\nabla\phi\cdot X). Let (g=e_1), (h=x_1), and (k=Ke_1). Direct calculation gives the following constant vector field and observable:

\[
k=-[g,f]-3\alpha h^2g,\qquad
z_0=-L_fh-\alpha h^3=k^Tx.
\]

For any constant (v), the componentwise cubic gives

\[
[a,[b,[c,f]]]=-6\alpha\,(a\odot b\odot c)
\]

for constant (a,b,c). Hence the constant directions (v_m=k^{\odot(2m+1)}) can be generated recursively, while their scalar linear observables are

\[
z_m=v_m^Tx,\qquad z_{m+1}=-\frac{1}{6\alpha}L_k^2L_fz_m.
\]

The resulting invariant moments are

\[
p_j=(L_k z_{j-1})(0)=\sum_{a=1}^n k_a^{2j},\qquad 1\le j\le n.
\]

Newton identities recover the unordered roots (r_a=k_a^2). Let (V\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}) have rows (v_0^T,\ldots,v_{n-1}^T). Its determinant is

\[
\det V=\left(\prod_a k_a\right)\prod_{a<b}(k_b^2-k_a^2).
\]

Thus (V) is invertible when all (k_a\ne0) and their squared magnitudes are distinct. The port coordinate is anchored, including its sign, by (L_gz_0=k_1); because the roots are distinct this identifies which recovered root belongs to node 1. Remaining root order and sign choices are exactly hidden signed permutations fixing (e_1).

For (0\le i,j<n), define the measured Lie-jet matrix

\[
B_{ij}=-\left(L_{v_j}L_fz_i\right)(0).
\]

Since (L_fz_i=-v_i^TKx-\alpha\sum_a(v_i)_a x_a^3), the derivative of the cubic term vanishes at (x=0), so

\[
B_{ij}=v_i^TKv_j,\qquad B=VKV^T,\qquad K=V^{-1}BV^{-T}.
\]

This proves the following exact implication: within the stated model class and generic gate, equality of the required one-port Lie jets forces the same (K) up to the unavoidable hidden signed-permutation gauge. The data comprise (n) moments and a symmetric (n\times n) matrix (B), hence at most (n+n(n+1)/2) scalar invariants. They resolve the continuous linear orthogonal ambiguity, rather than merely separating one pair.

These expressions are finite input-output invariants: the controlled system's local response jet supplies iterated (L_f,L_g) coefficients, and the displayed brackets, output multiplications, and product rules express (p_j,B_{ij}) as finite combinations of those coefficients. The direct recurrence uses five additional (f/g) operator levels per increment of (m); assembling (B_{ij}) through (i,j=n-1) has a conservative pre-expansion depth bound (10n-6). This is an order bound for the symbolic jet construction, not a count of a numerically stable experiment or a minimal word set.

## Exact checks and boundaries

The symbolic checker uses

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}5&1&2\\1&3&1\\2&1&6\end{pmatrix},\quad k=(5,1,2)^T,\quad \alpha=2/3.
\]

It verifies the constant-field identity, the first three power moments ((30,642,15690)), the Vandermonde determinant (15120), the triple-bracket law, (B=VKV^T), exact recovery (V^{-1}BV^{-T}=K), the port anchor, and invariance under a hidden signed permutation. The script passes with exact SymPy rationals. This is a finite internal check; the general proof is the algebra above, not the example.

The decisive boundary is the non-orthogonally-invariant node law. If α=0, the linear orthogonal orbit remains indistinguishable. If instead the cubic law were radial, (f_K(x)=-Kx-\alpha\|x\|^2x), then for every orthogonal (Qe_1=e_1), (K'=QKQ^T) and the change (x'=Qx) preserve the entire controlled input-output map. Thus the local *componentwise* law is essential, not “nonlinearity” in general. If (k) has a zero entry or repeated absolute values, this particular (V)-based construction is singular; that invalidates this proof path but does not prove non-identifiability. A possible Krylov/sparse extension is deliberately left conditional and is not part of the theorem.

## Acquisition and practical cost

Supplied resources: the model order (n), calibrated same-port actuation/readout, reset to the zero operating point, known common α, and access to the exact local input-output jet. Acquired resources: (n) even power sums of (Ke_1) and up to (n(n+1)/2) directional drift derivatives (B_{ij}), synthesized from higher-order mixed input-output derivatives. The highest symbolic order grows linearly with (n), but bracket expansion and the number of relevant words can grow rapidly.

The method is algebraically ill-conditioned near (k_a=0) or (k_a^2=k_b^2): δ-reconstruction contains (V^{-1}) on both sides of (B), and κ(V) diverges at these degeneracies. Estimating order-(q) derivatives by finite differences with step δ has noise variance scaling like σ²/(Nδ^{2q}) before accounting for model error, settling, input calibration, or input-energy limits. There is no finite-noise sample-complexity theorem, pulse schedule, conditioning guarantee away from the algebraic gate, or physical-device test here. Consequently the exact identifiability result is not yet a practical reconstruction capability.

## Prior art and novelty assessment

The broad target is established: Bellman and Åström introduced structural identifiability precisely to ask whether internal system structure can be recovered from input-output measurements ([1970 paper, DOI 10.1016/0025-5564(70)90132-x](https://doi.org/10.1016/0025-5564(70)90132-x)). Hermann and Krener developed nonlinear observability and controllability through Lie-algebraic observation structure ([1977 IEEE TAC paper, DOI 10.1109/TAC.1977.1101601](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~krener/1-25/10.IEEETAC77.pdf)); Sussmann proved uniqueness of minimal analytic nonlinear realizations up to isomorphism ([1976 paper, DOI 10.1007/BF01683278](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01683278)). Those works establish the general framework used here, not this model-specific moment/congruence formula.

The closest current network competitor found is Vanelli and Hendrickx, *Local Identifiability of Networks with Nonlinear Node Dynamics*, arXiv v4 dated 31 Aug 2026 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08472v4)). It treats known graph topology and known node activations in discrete-time networks and proves generic local identifiability for any digraph when at least one node per source component is excited and one per sink component measured; it explicitly includes recurrent networks. In a strongly connected graph, that is one excitation and one measurement node. Its update is a static activation of weighted previous states, (y_i^t=f_i(\sum_jw_{ij}y_j^{t-1})+b_i u_i^{t-1}), not the continuous-time additive-cubic drift studied here. Its result is generic local identifiability across analytic activations, whereas this derivation is an explicit finite-jet, global reconstruction modulo gauge for a fixed cubic ODE, with no known topology. This overlap is strong enough that novelty cannot be claimed without a much deeper comparison. The paper also stresses that its theoretical result does not provide a robust identification algorithm and that high-order measurement derivatives are noise-sensitive (Sec. VI), matching the practical limitation here.

Kvaternik and Silva's NASA report already identifies bilinear state-space models from first- and second-order Volterra kernels ([NASA/TM-2008-215320](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20080024181)). It is a different model class and does not yield the componentwise-cubic formula above, but it shows that nonlinear response-kernel identification itself is longstanding. The present search did not establish priority for the exact theorem; it is not a novelty proof.

## Decision and next falsification gate

The mechanism engages the linear hidden-orientation obstruction exactly: known componentwise cubic drift creates Hadamard-power directions, whose moment/Vandermonde frame turns one-port higher-order response into a full congruence measurement of (K). The exact theorem is mathematically substantive and independently checkable, but the campaign target of an original high-conviction physical capability remains **OPEN** because the nearest network-identifiability prior art is strong, the specific ODE theorem is not externally reviewed, and realistic noise/conditioning/probe costs are untested.

The next decisive checks are: (i) compare the proof to the hypotheses and equivalence classes in Vanelli–Hendrickx v4 and related continuous-time structural-identifiability work; (ii) derive a minimal, actually executable set of input waveforms for the required Lie jet; and (iii) prove a stability bound in terms of κ(V), root separation, derivative noise, and model mismatch, then test it on a physical model. Until those pass, treat this as a restricted candidate theorem, not mission completion.
