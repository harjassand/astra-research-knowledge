H07 GATE — ONE-PORT IDENTIFICATION OF A CUBIC NETWORK
Date: 2026-10-10
First derivation frozen before literature comparison.
Status: exact n=2 local realization found; scalable general capability OPEN.

TARGET AND INTERFACE

Study the control-affine system
    xdot = f(x) + g u,    f(x)=-Kx-alpha x^{circ 3},
    g=e1,                 y=h(x)=x1,
where alpha>0 is known and K is unknown symmetric positive definite. The
input amplitude/time scale and output calibration are assumed known. The
ideal information interface is the local input-output Lie jet: values at a
prepared state of words in L_f and L_g applied to h. Physical access would
need enough repeatable input perturbations to infer those coefficients.
Goal: identify K, and possibly hidden state, from a single driven port without
measuring internal coordinates. State dimension n is not assumed small.

FROZEN ALGEBRA

Use L_X q=grad(q) dot X and [g,f]=Df g-Dg f. Set k=K e1. Since K is
symmetric and the nonlinearity is componentwise,
    [g,f]=-k-3 alpha h^2 g,
    L_f h=-k dot x-alpha h^3.
Thus the scalar observable
    z0=-L_f h-alpha h^3=k dot x
is available from the first output derivative. Although k is not a physical
input direction, its Lie derivative on any scalar q is an input-output
operator:
    D q := L_k q
       = -(L_g L_f q-L_f L_g q)-3 alpha h^2 L_g q.
This follows by applying the vector-field bracket identity to q, not by
measuring the vector bracket in state space.

The stated recurrence is algebraically correct. If
    z_m=sum_i k_i^(2m+1) x_i,
then L_f z_m=-sum_i k_i^(2m+1)(Kx)_i-alpha sum_i k_i^(2m+1)x_i^3,
and the linear Kx term vanishes after two L_k derivatives. The cubic term
gives
    z_(m+1)=-(6 alpha)^(-1) L_k^2 L_f z_m
             =sum_i k_i^(2m+3) x_i.
Also
    L_k z_m=sum_i k_i^(2m+2).
Writing lambda_i=k_i^2 and a_i=k_i x_i, z_m=sum_i lambda_i^m a_i
and L_k z_m=sum_i lambda_i^(m+1). For distinct nonzero lambda_i, Newton
identities recover the multiset {lambda_i}; a Vandermonde solve then recovers
the associated {a_i}, up to hidden coordinate labels. This is finite exact
algebra if the relevant Lie-jet coefficients are available. Recovering all
of K for n>2 additionally needs enough prepared states to obtain Kx on a
spanning family; one state generally supplies only one matrix-vector product.
One-port reachability/excitation of such a family is a separate assumption.
This is not yet a scalable or noise-robust identification algorithm.

ACTUAL POSITIVE LOW-ORDER REALIZATION: n=2

Write K=[[a,b],[b,c]], x=(h,r), with a>0 by positive definiteness. Assume
b!=0 and prepare a state with r!=0. The recurrence is not needed beyond z0.
The following three observable quantities suffice:
    a = -L_g L_f h-3 alpha h^2,
    p = D z0 = a^2+b^2,
    z0 = a h+b r.
Thus b^2=p-a^2. The transformation (x2,b)->(-x2,-b) leaves all one-port
input-output behavior invariant, so choose the gauge b=+sqrt(p-a^2). Then
    r=(z0-a h)/b.
Finally q=L_f z0 is also an input-output jet of order at most 2, and
    T := -q-alpha(a h^3+b r^3) = k^T Kx
      = p h+b(a+c)r.
Consequently
    c = (T-p h-a b r)/(b r).
Every entry of K is recovered, modulo the unavoidable hidden sign conjugacy
diag(1,-1) K diag(1,-1). The conditions b!=0 and r!=0 are necessary for this
formula. If b=0, x2 is completely disconnected and unidentifiable from this
port; if r=0 at the chosen state, use another state.

Concrete exact witness: alpha=2/3, K=[[3,1],[1,2]], x=(1/5,1/4). Then
    h=1/5, L_f h=-1283/1500, L_g L_f h=-77/25,
    z0=17/20, p=10, L_f z0=-39317/12000,
and the formulas return a=3, b=1, r=1/4, c=2 exactly. Under the gauge
K'=[[3,-1],[-1,2]], x'=(1/5,-1/4), the same y and all these scalar
quantities are unchanged. This is an exact finite-order realization, not a
numerical fit. From rest, a constant input u=U gives x1(t)=Ut+O(t^2) and
x2(t)=-b U t^2/2+O(t^3), so a nonzero hidden coordinate is locally excited
through the port whenever bU!=0.

JET ORDER AND PHYSICAL ACQUISITION

For n=2 the largest required word length is 3: D z0 uses two additional Lie
operators on z0, which itself contains L_f h. A single ordinary output trace
does not directly reveal each noncommutative Lie word. One needs input
variation (or resettable experiments) sufficient to separate them in the
local input-output/Fliess jet, with calibrated u and time. The identity for
D z0 is a cancellation among third-order terms; finite-difference recovery
requires signal precision at the corresponding scale and repeatable
preparation of the same state. The c formula divides by b r, so error
amplification diverges as coupling b or hidden coordinate r approaches zero.
Small b also makes port excitation of x2 only O(b U t^2). These costs are
not covered by exact jet identifiability.

For the general n recurrence, z0 has Lie-word order 1, D raises maximum word
order by 2, and z_(m+1)=D^2 L_f z_m raises it by 5. Recovering p_n=D z_(n-1)
therefore requires words through order 5n-2 before any numerical differentiation
or conditioning cost. Distinct lambda_i are insufficient for stable
identification: Vandermonde conditioning can be arbitrarily poor as
lambda_i collide, and moments can be ill-scaled. At minimum a scalable claim
needs an explicit compressed jet-extraction procedure, noise/precision bounds,
state preparation cost, an excitation condition, and identifiability under
near-collisions. OPEN.

STRONGEST FALSIFIERS AND SCOPE

1. Exact disconnected-port failure: for n=2 and b=0, any c>0 gives the same
   y(t) for all input signals when initialized with x2=0, and x2 is never
   affected by the input. No one-port experiment identifies c in this case.
2. Exact gauge ambiguity: for any diagonal sign matrix S with S_11=1,
   (K,x)->(SKS,Sx) preserves the model class, input g=e1 and output x1.
   Hidden coordinate signs cannot be recovered even from an infinite exact
   input-output record. Hidden permutations among coordinates 2..n also
   preserve the model if state labels are not externally meaningful.
3. A low-order positive realization does not establish linear-in-n physical
   cost. The recurrence uses increasingly high-order local response data,
   and an ill-conditioned inverse moment problem can erase the formal gain.

At this freeze: candidate mechanism is algebraic Lie-jet moments plus
symmetry quotienting; actual result is the exact n=2 reconstruction above
and a correct formal n-dimensional recurrence. Novelty is UNKNOWN. Strongest
baseline and primary-source comparison are pending. Foundational scalable
one-port network discovery is OPEN.

POST-FREEZE POSITIVE EXPERIMENT: ONE STEP FROM REST (n=2)

The Lie-jet reconstruction above assumes access to local word coefficients. A
separate calculation gives a simpler physical experiment for the same n=2
claim. Start at the known equilibrium x(0)=0 and apply one calibrated constant
step u(t)=U (right-sided at t=0). Write y(t)=sum_j c_j t^j. Direct coefficient
recursion in the ODE gives
    c1 = U,
    c2 = -U a/2,
    c3 = U(a^2+b^2)/6,
    c4 = -U(6 U^2 alpha + a^3+2 a b^2+b^2 c)/24.
For U!=0 and b!=0 these determine
    a = -2 c2/U,
    b^2 = 6 c3/U-a^2,
    c = (-24 c4/U-6 U^2 alpha-a^3-2 a b^2)/b^2.
Only the hidden sign of b remains unidentifiable, matching the exact gauge.
Thus a single output trace and derivatives through order four suffice in the
ideal n=2 model, without separating noncommutative words or preparing a
nonzero hidden state. SPD K and alpha>0 make x=0 globally asymptotically stable
under u=0, so it is a valid mathematical reset state. This improves the
low-order realization, not the scalable claim: the fourth derivative is
noise-sensitive, the inversion is singular at b=0, and finite-time reset and
instrument/input dynamics have not been modeled. The exact-rational verifier
now checks both the local Lie-jet reconstruction and this step-response
formula; it passes.

SCALING AUDIT OF THE FORMAL RECURRENCE

The moment construction can in principle provide Kx, not merely x. For a
fixed state define
    r_m = -L_f z_m-alpha sum_i k_i^(2m+1) x_i^3
        = sum_i lambda_i^m k_i (Kx)_i.
Once the distinct lambda_i and their matching labels are recovered by the
moment/Vandermonde systems, r_0,...,r_(n-1) recover all (Kx)_i because
k_i!=0. Repeating at n linearly independent prepared states recovers K from
its action on a spanning set. Therefore the exact algebraic generic claim can
be stated: distinct nonzero k_i^2, known alpha and scales, and accessible
states spanning R^n are sufficient for finite-jet reconstruction of K modulo
hidden signed permutations. This is conditional structural identification,
not an unconditional one-port theorem; reachability/experiment design and
conditioning remain open.

The word-order bound O(n) hides a serious acquisition-cost issue. The
operator Dq=-(L_gL_f-L_fL_g)q-3 alpha h^2 L_gq is a linear combination of
input-output derivatives. Repeated D applications expand into many
noncommuting words; a naive explicit expansion can grow exponentially even
though the maximum word length is only 5n-2. An efficient compressed
experiment or estimator that extracts the particular nested combinations
without enumerating/acquiring a full truncated Fliess series is not provided.
The physical protocol also needs n spanning states, and the moment Vandermonde
inverse can have arbitrarily poor conditioning as the lambda_i approach one
another. These are the main blockers to calling this a scalable capability.

PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON (AFTER FREEZE)

- Boyd and Chua, “Uniqueness of Circuits and Systems Containing One
  Nonlinearity,” IEEE TAC 30(7), 674-681 (1985),
  https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/one_nonlinearity.html . They prove an
  I/O-equivalence characterization for systems with one memoryless
  nonlinearity and apply it to one-port networks with one nonlinear element.
  This is the closest one-port circuit result located in this scoped search,
  but its model class is not the present network with componentwise cubic
  nonlinearities at every state and unknown symmetric K.
- Villaverde, Evans, Chappell, and Banga, “Input-Dependent Structural
  Identifiability of Nonlinear Systems,” IEEE Control Systems Letters 3(2),
  272-277 (2019), https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/110005/ . This already treats
  identifiability as input- and initial-condition-dependent, uses extended
  Lie derivatives, and designs sufficiently exciting inputs; it also notes
  that multiple constant-input experiments can sometimes replace a
  time-varying input. Therefore Lie-derivative identifiability as a general
  method is prior art; any contribution here would have to be the exact
  model-specific moment/Vandermonde reconstruction and its scalable physical
  acquisition theorem.
- Gray, Venkatesh, and Duffaut Espinosa, “Nonlinear system identification for
  multivariable control via discrete-time Chen-Fliess series,” Automatica 119
  (2020), 109085, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2020.109085 . They use
  noncommutative Chen-Fliess input-output expansions and structured regression
  for nonlinear systems; their introduction states analytic control-affine
  systems admit such an input-output representation. This is a direct prior
  baseline for representing the same kind of finite input-output word data,
  though it does not give this structured K-recovery result.
- Haber, Molnar, and Motter, “State observation and sensor selection for
  nonlinear networks,” IEEE TCNS 5(2), 694-708 (2018),
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6178986/ . Their nonlinear-network
  state estimation framework uses finite observation windows and reports a
  tradeoff between sensor fraction, observation length, and estimation error;
  it also states graph-only criteria do not determine practical state
  estimation. This is state observation with selected sensors, not one-port
  structural matrix identification, but it supports treating practical
  excitation/conditioning as a separate hurdle from formal observability.
- Listmann and Zhao, “A Comparison of Methods for Higher-Order Numerical
  Differentiation,” European Control Conference (2013),
  https://folk.ntnu.no/skoge/prost/proceedings/ecc-2013/data/papers/0709.pdf .
  They study estimating higher derivatives from noise-corrupted measurements
  and note finite differences are inappropriate in general for such data;
  they compare noise suppression, computational cost, and tuning. This directly
  bears on the single-step fourth derivative and the high-order Lie-jet route.

No source in this scoped search states the exact componentwise-cubic,
known-alpha, symmetric-SPD, single-port theorem or the moment recurrence.
That is only “no exact match found,” not a novelty result. General structural
identifiability, Lie-derivative/Fliess representations, and one-port nonlinear
network equivalence all predate this candidate. Current verdict: exact n=2
leverage is verified; general-n algebra is conditionally sufficient; scalable
robust acquisition, genericity margins, and novelty remain OPEN.
