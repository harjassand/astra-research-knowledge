FIRST DERIVATION FROZEN — 2026-10-10
No literature comparison has been consulted yet.

Candidate target
A reliability interface for systems whose catastrophic event is failure of every member of a specified m-component cut set. The intended use is to audit the common assumption that individually rare, pairwise-independent component failures imply an exponentially rarer all-cut failure.

Construction
Let q be a prime power, let alpha_1,...,alpha_m be distinct elements of F_q, and let 2 <= k < m <= q. Draw A_0,...,A_{k-1} independently and uniformly from F_q, set F(x)=sum_{j=0}^{k-1} A_j x^j, and define X_i=1{F(alpha_i)=0}. Interpret X_i=1 as failure of cut-set component i.

Exact derivation
For any t<=k distinct indices i_1,...,i_t, the linear map
(A_0,...,A_{k-1}) -> (F(alpha_{i_1}),...,F(alpha_{i_t}))
has rank t: its first t columns form a t-by-t Vandermonde matrix with determinant product_{a<b}(alpha_{i_b}-alpha_{i_a}) != 0. A uniform input under a surjective linear map gives the uniform distribution on F_q^t. Thus the t evaluations are mutually independent uniform field elements. Consequently, every set of at most k failure indicators is mutually independent Bernoulli(1/q). In particular, for k>=2 every pair has exactly the same law as under fully independent failures of rate p=1/q.

For the catastrophe C={X_1=...=X_m=1}, F has m distinct roots. Since deg(F)<=k-1 and m>=k, the root bound forces F to be the zero polynomial. Conversely, the zero polynomial causes C. Therefore
P(C)=P(A_0=...=A_{k-1}=0)=q^{-k}.
Under the fully independent model with the same marginal p=1/q, P_ind(C)=q^{-m}. The amplification ratio is exactly q^(m-k).

Concrete discriminator
Take q=101, m=20, k=2. Every single and pairwise failure probability agrees exactly with independent Bernoulli(1/101) failures. Yet the all-20 failure probability is 101^-2, about 9.80e-5, versus 101^-20, about 8.95e-41 under independence: an amplification of 101^18, about 1.50e36. The latent driver uses only k log2(q) random bits (about 13.32 bits here), versus m log2(q) bits (about 133.2 bits) to generate fully independent field-valued coordinates. The driver is an explicit mathematical common-cause mechanism, not an estimate from finite data.

Resource and observation interface
The theorem assumes a known finite field, distinct component labels, uniform independent coefficients, and the rule that a component fails exactly when its field evaluation is zero. It charges k uniform field elements to produce one joint trial; it does not charge the physical realization or acquisition of the latent field values. Any observer limited to all marginals of order <=k cannot distinguish this construction from the independent model, because those distributions are identical. Full joint-event frequency inspection needs on the order of q^k trials to encounter the correlated catastrophe once on average; the independent baseline needs q^m. These are expectation-scale diagnostics, not confidence guarantees.

Initial claim boundary
The result is an exact witness that pairwise (or k-wise) failure statistics do not certify the risk of an m-way cut failure. It does not establish that real infrastructure failures follow this mechanism, does not yield a general worst-case risk bound for arbitrary marginals, and does not solve system-level reliability.
