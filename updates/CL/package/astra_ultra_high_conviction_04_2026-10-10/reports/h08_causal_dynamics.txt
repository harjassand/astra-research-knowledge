ASTRA ULTRA HIGH-CONVICTION PASS 04 — H08 CAUSAL DYNAMICS
First derivation frozen before literature comparison (2026-10-10).

Candidate primitive: monotone pulse tomography of boundary reachability.

Foundational target
Make directed dynamical reachability and shortest causal path length identifiable between a realistically actuated compartment and a realistically measured reporter, even when every intermediate state is latent and cannot be intervened on. The target is a boundary invariant, not reconstruction of the full latent graph.

Interface
At a stable operating point, model the local concentration dynamics by

    dx/dt = A x + b_s u(t),       y_t = c_t^T x,

where A is an unknown Metzler matrix (A_ji >= 0 for i != j), the known actuator injects a nonnegative dose into compartment s (b_s = e_s, up to a calibrated positive gain), and the known reporter reads compartment t (c_t = e_t, up to a calibrated positive gain). Off-diagonal A_ji > 0 denotes a directed influence i -> j. A is stable for the equilibrium assay. A nonlinear smooth system is covered locally by taking A to be its Jacobian at the operating point, provided that Jacobian is Metzler and the finite-dose remainder is controlled.

The experiment needs only a randomized low-amplitude pulse versus a matched sham and a timed reporter readout. To avoid a physically ideal Dirac impulse or a peak amplitude that diverges as duration shrinks, hold a small actuator amplitude epsilon fixed and vary duration delta: u(t)=epsilon on [0,delta], then zero. The delivered dose is q=epsilon*delta. Measure at pulse end and normalize the randomized reporter difference by q. No intermediate node must be measured or directly manipulated. Actual actuators, reporters, pulse widths, doses, sham specimens, calibration, and replicates are charged as inputs; none are assumed free.

Structural theorem (ideal linear-response limit)
Let h_ts(t) = e_t^T exp(A t) e_s. The derivative of the mean reporter response with respect to delivered dose q at q=0 for an impulse at s is h_ts(t), up to the positive calibrated actuator and reporter gains. Let G have edge i -> j exactly when A_ji > 0 for i != j.

1. For every t > 0, h_ts(t) > 0 if and only if G contains a directed path from s to t. If there is no path, h_ts(t)=0 for every t >= 0.
2. If the shortest path length is ell, then h_ts^(r)(0)=0 for r < ell, and

       h_ts^(ell)(0) = sum_{P:s to t, |P|=ell} product_{(i->j) in P} A_ji > 0.

Thus the support of the boundary impulse response identifies reachability, and its first nonzero Taylor order identifies the minimum number of causal transitions in the operating-point Jacobian graph. No conditional-independence faithfulness premise is used: the leading path contributions are positive and cannot cancel.

Proof. Pick alpha large enough that M=A+alpha I is entrywise nonnegative. Then exp(A t)=exp(-alpha t) sum_{k>=0} M^k t^k/k!. Every entry of every M^k is a sum of nonnegative walk products. Its (t,s) entry is positive for some k exactly when a directed path exists, since the shift only adds self-loops. For the first nonzero order ell, no diagonal/self-loop term can occur: such a term would imply an off-diagonal path of length less than ell. Therefore (A^ell)_{ts} is precisely the sum of products along shortest paths and is strictly positive. If no path exists, A has the same zero pattern outside the diagonal, so every (A^k)_{ts} vanishes.

Finite-pulse form. For the rectangular pulse, linear response per unit dose measured at pulse end is

       g_ts(delta) = (1/delta) integral_0^delta h_ts(r) dr.

For every fixed delta>0, g_ts(delta)>0 exactly when there is a directed path: the integrand is positive for every r>0 in the reachable case and identically zero in the unreachable case. Thus a finite-duration pulse already tests boundary reachability in the ideal local-response limit; the duration ladder is needed to read off shortest-path length.

For shortest path length ell, with p_ell the positive path-product sum above,

       g_ts(delta) = p_ell delta^ell/(ell+1)! + O(delta^(ell+1)).

For ell=0, take p_0=1 when s=t. This gives a physically implementable duration ladder whose low-duration log-slope is ell. In a smooth nonlinear system, the derivative of the reporter difference with respect to pulse amplitude epsilon at epsilon=0 equals epsilon's linear-response coefficient above. Finite-amplitude data incur a higher-order remainder; estimate the slope from a randomized small-amplitude series and include a bound on that remainder. The duration and amplitude series are charged measurements.

Resource and finite-precision interface
For m usable actuators, r reporters, K durations, and N randomized pulse/sham pairs per actuator-duration setting, the assay uses m*K*N pulse runs and the same number of sham runs if all reporters can be read simultaneously; incompatible reporter channels require separate runs. It also charges actuator/reporter calibration and K pulse-width controls. The theorem is exact with noiseless access to the response function. Finite precision needs an effect floor: if path edge weights can approach zero, no finite sample count uniformly separates a path from no path. Under shortest-path cap L, lower edge bound a_min, upper norm bound ||A||<=M0, pulse range delta<=delta0 and known noise level, the leading per-dose coefficient is at least a_min^ell delta^ell/(ell+1)!. The absolute Taylor remainder is bounded by exp(M0*delta)*(M0*delta)^(ell+1)/(ell+2)!; choose delta0 so this is at most half the leading lower bound. With fixed peak amplitude epsilon, the observed signal floor is then gamma = epsilon*a_min^ell*delta^(ell+1)/(2*(ell+1)!). For sub-Gaussian reporter noise with standard deviation sigma, distinguishing this floor from zero by randomized means needs N = O(sigma^2/gamma^2 * log(1/eta)) pulse/sham pairs for error probability eta. This exposes the severe delta^(-2(ell+1)) replication cost for long hidden paths rather than treating temporal precision or interventions as free. The theorem gives no uniform estimator without these declared bounds.

Strongest baseline
Ordinary observational conditional-independence discovery cannot in general recover hidden directed dynamics from endpoint trajectories, and generic intervention discovery usually assumes node-wise interventions or faithfulness. Standard linear system identification can estimate boundary transfer functions but does not by itself interpret their support as graph reachability without a sign/positivity condition. The proposed interface trades full graph recovery for an exact, computable boundary invariant under an explicit Metzler model. Prior-art status is deliberately open until post-freeze search.

Exact identifiability countertests and scope

A. Sign-indefinite cancellation destroys the support theorem. Take nodes s,a,b,t with all diagonal entries -1 and only off-diagonal entries A_as=A_bs=1, A_ta=1, A_tb=-1. There are two directed length-two paths from s to t, one positive and one negative. Starting from a unit impulse at s gives x_a(t)=x_b(t)=t exp(-t), hence h_ts(t)=x_t(t)=0 for all t even though s reaches t. The Metzler/sign restriction is doing real work; it is not a generic causal-discovery result.

B. Boundary responses do not identify the full latent graph. Use nodes s,a,t,u,v, all diagonal entries -2, actuator at s and reporter at t, and common edges s->a and a->t of weight 1. In model G1 also include s->u and u->v, each of weight 1. In model G2 also include s->u, u->v, v->u, each of weight 1. There are no edges from u or v to a or t. For every input waveform at s, the t trajectory is identical in G1 and G2 (the u,v block is downstream and unobserved); the two latent graphs differ. Therefore this primitive identifies only reachable boundary pairs and shortest path lengths witnessed by a measured reporter. It cannot claim hidden adjacency, path count beyond the leading sum, or structure outside the measured causal cone.

C. If the actuator or reporter mixes unknown compartments, an apparent positive response may not correspond to the named source/target pair. Known positive support and calibrated gains are part of the interface. If the local Jacobian is not Metzler, countertest A applies. Hidden feedback that changes sign with state also lies outside the local theorem unless the operating-point linearization remains Metzler and the dose expansion is validated.

Why this engages the obstacle
The inaccessible objects are latent intermediate mechanisms. A positive, known boundary pulse/readout pair can certify that some directed path exists without intervening on those intermediates, because positivity makes the path sum observable and cancellation-free. The duration ladder adds minimum path length. This replaces the faithfulness-style genericity assumption with an explicit physical monotonicity condition and a finite resource cost. It does not make arbitrary latent causal graphs identifiable; the two-graph counterexample fixes that boundary.

Status before literature
Candidate structural theorem derived. No external validation or novelty claim. Exact countertests establish the sign and coverage limits. Main open question: whether existing positive-systems, compartment-network, and causal response literature already contains this same boundary-reachability interface and finite-pulse formulation. Search only after this derivation is frozen.

Post-freeze literature audit of candidate 1
The central positivity mechanism is established positive-systems theory, not a new causal primitive. Ohta, Maeda, and Kodama's 1984 paper treats reachability and realizability of continuous-time positive SISO systems under nonnegative input/output constraints (SIAM J. Control Optim. 22(2), 171-180, https://doi.org/10.1137/0322013). Coxson and Shapiro's 1987 paper treats positive-input reachability for nonnegative linear systems and explicitly uses chemical and pharmacokinetic examples (Linear Algebra Appl. 94, 35-53, https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(87)90076-0). The exact support statement above follows directly from the classical exponential-of-Metzler walk expansion; the finite-pulse average is an integration of that impulse response. Thus the mathematical core is a standard corollary, and the pulse assay is an application framing rather than a demonstrated foundational advance.

In systems biology, the 2019 primary paper "Robust network inference using response logic" explicitly takes perturbation response patterns as transitive-closure information: a perturbed node is assumed to cause responses at all nodes reachable by a path and no others; it then reasons over networks matching that pattern (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6612863/). That work differs from the Metzler theorem in its Boolean/noisy response model and its node-wise perturbations, but it closes much of the claimed conceptual gap between intervention response support and causal reachability. Candidate 1 is therefore rejected as a landmark claim. It remains a useful scoped lemma for positive compartment models, with realistic hidden intermediates only when source/reporter channels are calibrated.

Replacement candidate 2 first derivation: positive-pulse order commutator
This replacement derivation is frozen before its own prior-art search.

Foundational target
Identify a hidden dynamical interaction that every single intervention leaves invisible, using only two feasible positive treatment pulses, their order randomization, and one endpoint reporter. The computable target is an input-output projection of the commutator of the two treatment-conditioned flows. A nonzero order-only response with two zero first-order single-treatment responses also certifies that a homogeneous autonomous smooth realization needs at least two state dimensions near the operating point.

Interface
Consider an autonomous smooth system with state x, no-treatment vector field f_0 and two physical treatment inputs,

    dx/dt = f_0(x) + u_A(t) g_A(x) + u_B(t) g_B(x),    y=h(x).

Start at a known/reproducible no-treatment equilibrium x_0, f_0(x_0)=0. The finite treatment protocols use bounded positive concentrations, one at a time, for durations alpha and beta. The treatment-phase fields are F_A=f_0+g_A and F_B=f_0+g_B, with local flows Phi_A^alpha and Phi_B^beta. Randomize matched specimens to A-then-B or B-then-A; each receives the same two positive doses and total duration alpha+beta. Measure one scalar reporter at the end. Sham phases preserve elapsed time in the single-treatment controls. This is sequence randomization over available treatments, not an intervention on hidden state coordinates.

The population causal order contrast is C(alpha,beta)=E[Y|AB]-E[Y|BA]. For a fixed initial state and deterministic dynamics,

    C(alpha,beta) = h(Phi_B^beta(Phi_A^alpha(x_0)))
                    - h(Phi_A^alpha(Phi_B^beta(x_0))).

If F_A,F_B,h are C^3 on a compact neighborhood, then

    C(alpha,beta) = alpha*beta*kappa + O(alpha^2*beta + alpha*beta^2),
    kappa = dh_x0([F_A,F_B](x_0)),
    [F_A,F_B] = DF_B*F_A - DF_A*F_B.

The first two terms of the flow expansions cancel exactly because both sequences contain the same A and B durations; the mixed term is the Lie bracket projected through the reporter. Hence kappa is computed by the small-dose limit C/(alpha*beta). No faithfulness assumption is involved. At finite doses, C itself remains an identified causal effect of order assignment; interpreting it as kappa requires a dose ladder and a controlled Taylor remainder.

Single-pulse controls quantify main effects relative to a time-matched sham:

    mu_A = d/dalpha [h(Phi_A^alpha(x_0))-h(Phi_0^alpha(x_0))] at alpha=0 = dh*g_A(x_0),
    mu_B = d/dbeta  [h(Phi_B^beta(x_0))-h(Phi_0^beta(x_0))] at beta=0 = dh*g_B(x_0).

Dimension certificate. If mu_A=mu_B=0 but kappa != 0, no one-dimensional autonomous smooth state model at x_0 can explain the response. For dimension one, h'(x_0)=0 makes kappa=0 immediately. If h'(x_0)!=0, mu_A=mu_B=0 forces g_A(x_0)=g_B(x_0)=0; since f_0(x_0)=0, F_A(x_0)=F_B(x_0)=0, so [F_A,F_B](x_0)=0 and again kappa=0. In dimension two or more this implication fails because interventions can move along output-null directions and their bracket can point into an observed direction.

Exact hidden-interaction example
Take state (x,z), stable no-treatment drift f_0=-lambda*x*d/dx-lambda*z*d/dz with lambda>0, positive treatment A field g_A=d/dx, positive treatment B field g_B=x*d/dz, initial state (0,0), and reporter y=z. Then mu_A=mu_B=0. Applying A alone changes x but never z; applying B alone from x=0 never changes z. In AB, A first makes x positive and B then converts that hidden state into z>0. In BA, B sees x=0 and z remains zero, then A changes only x. Thus the finite positive-pulse order contrast is strictly positive for alpha,beta>0. At the origin [F_A,F_B] has z-component 1, so kappa=1. One endpoint channel detects a two-state hidden interaction that single-pulse outcomes cannot detect.

Resource and precision interface
For each (alpha,beta) dose pair, use five randomized protocol arms: AB, BA, A-only followed by a sham phase, B-only preceded/followed by a sham phase as appropriate, and all-sham. Each arm has N replicate units from the same initialized stratum. AB and BA use two active treatment pulses per unit, A-only and B-only one each; total active administrations are 6N per dose pair, plus 5N endpoint measurements. K dose scales therefore use 5KN trial units and 6KN active administrations, plus initialization/reset, pulse calibration, sham, assay calibration, and K dose-grid controls. If all reporters are compatible with one multiplex measurement, this cost does not multiply by reporter count; otherwise charge extra runs.

If the endpoint noise is sub-Gaussian with scale sigma and the local coefficient obeys |kappa|>=kappa_min, a dose pair alpha=beta=r with Taylor remainder at most (kappa_min/2)r^2 gives order-effect signal at least (kappa_min/2)r^2. Estimating AB-BA to fixed error with probability 1-eta then needs N=O(sigma^2/(kappa_min^2*r^4)*log(1/eta)) units per order arm. The two single-arm slopes and sham are additionally needed for the dimension certificate. If kappa may approach zero or the initial-state distribution is heterogeneous enough to average positive and negative coefficients away, there is no uniform sample bound or per-cell dimension conclusion; stabilize or stratify the initial state and declare a coefficient floor. Intervention order, pulse exposures, reset, replicate count, temporal precision, and endpoint assay are all paid resources.

Strongest falsifiers and scope
The exact example already distinguishes the mechanism from single-dose response summaries. But zero observed C does not imply commuting interventions: dh([F_A,F_B]) may vanish because the reporter is blind, and higher-order order effects can survive even when kappa=0. Conversely, a nonzero order effect need not identify a unique molecular mechanism; it certifies only a projected commutator/order dependence of the treatment-conditioned dynamics. Append an unobserved state or apply a smooth coordinate change and preserve the same output protocol, so full hidden graph recovery is impossible. A non-autonomous one-state model with an external clock can also mimic order effects, which is why the dimension certificate requires autonomous dynamics, a common equilibrium, and within-stratum homogeneous replicates. Pharmacokinetic carryover is part of the measured dynamical state unless separately measured and modeled.

Why candidate 2 engages the obstacle
Two interventions individually silent at a reporter can still expose a hidden state-mediated interaction when ordered. The assay uses accessible positive treatment sequences and one endpoint sensor, rather than free node-wise interventions or faithfulness. The mixed response is a structurally defined scalar and, when single-pulse first derivatives vanish, yields a lower bound on latent dynamical dimension. It does not reconstruct which hidden variable mediated the effect. Candidate-specific novelty and relation to nonlinear control theory remain open until the next search.

Candidate 2 post-search refinement: order-commutator rank tomography
Use m available treatment regimes F_1,...,F_m at the same reproducible initial state x_0, with one scalar endpoint y=h(x). For each unordered pair i<j, randomize matched replicates to i-then-j or j-then-i, using equal duration delta for both phases and measuring only once at the end. Define

    C_ij(delta) = E[Y | i then j] - E[Y | j then i],
    K_ij = lim_{delta -> 0} C_ij(delta)/delta^2,
    K_ji = -K_ij,  K_ii=0.

For smooth autonomous regime fields, the flow expansion gives

    K_ij = dh_x0([F_i,F_j](x_0)).

There is a direct state-dimension lower bound from these data. Let a_i=F_i(x_0) in R^n and let r_i=dh_x0 DF_i(x_0), a row vector in R^{1xn}. Form A=[a_1 ... a_m] and R=[r_1^T ... r_m^T]. Entrywise,

    K_ij = r_j a_i - r_i a_j,
    K = A^T R - R^T A.

Therefore rank(K)<=2n and every n-dimensional smooth autonomous realization must satisfy

    n >= rank(K)/2.

This is a one-sided certificate: a measured rank-r commutator matrix proves at least r/2 state dimensions in any local autonomous realization; rank zero or a low rank does not bound the true dimension from above. The theorem requires all pairwise coefficients to refer to the same state and reporter. It is invariant to smooth changes of hidden-state coordinates because each entry is an input-output Lie-bracket scalar.

Sharp 2-state example. At x_0=(0,0) with output h(x_1,x_2)=x_2, take four local treatment fields

    F_1=(1,0), F_2=(0,1), F_3=(0,x_1), F_4=(0,x_2).

The only nonzero upper-triangle coefficients are K_13=1 and K_24=1, so K has rank 4 and certifies n>=2; the displayed model attains n=2. This is a local algebraic sharpness example; the separate silent-single-treatment example above supplies a stable nonnegative concentration model.

This all-pairs interface does not require zero single-pulse responses. The earlier two-treatment example is the sharp qualitative case where both first-order single responses vanish but the order coefficient is nonzero, excluding every one-state autonomous model. The rank version trades more pairwise interventions for a potentially stronger numerical dimension lower bound.

Finite resources and statistical gate. For m treatments, K dose scales delta_k, and N independent initialized units per order arm, exhaustive pairwise tomography uses m(m-1) K N trial units: there are m(m-1)/2 pairs and two orders per pair at every scale. Each unit receives two active positive pulses, so there are 2m(m-1)KN pulse administrations and m(m-1)KN endpoint measurements. Also charge initialization/reset, treatment concentration and timing calibration, and the K-scale dose controls. If all treatments cannot be held as autonomous fixed regimes for short phases, or if the initial state drifts between trials, this theorem is not identified by the protocol.

Suppose per-trial endpoint noise is sub-Gaussian with scale sigma, the Taylor remainder in C_ij(delta)/delta^2 is bounded by L*delta, and the smallest nonzero singular value of K is at least gamma. Choosing delta <= gamma/(4mL) controls the deterministic matrix error. Since the operator norm of an m-by-m entrywise error matrix is at most m times its largest entry error, estimating each order contrast to O(gamma/m) requires N=O(sigma^2*m^2/(gamma^2*delta^4)*log(m^2/eta)) replicates per arm for failure probability eta. Without a singular-value gap gamma, a derivative/remainder bound L, or a homogeneous initial-state stratum, finite data cannot certify the rank. With heterogeneous units, the estimated mean K may have cancellations and gives a bound only for the aggregate response unless the population is stratified or modeled.

Exact nonidentifiability countertest. Take any model realizing a given K and append a hidden state w with dw/dt=-w under every treatment, leaving h independent of w and the other fields unchanged. Every AB/BA endpoint distribution and every entry of K is identical, although the full state dimension has increased by one. Thus rank(K)/2 is a lower bound only; the assay cannot recover all hidden state or molecular identity. More generally, finite-dose order effects at one scale do not identify K: the contrast can be dominated by cubic and higher terms when K=0, so the dose ladder is essential.

Candidate 2 prior-art audit
The Lie-bracket mechanism is classical geometric control: sequential application of nonlinear control fields exposes bracket directions, including directions inaccessible to either field alone. A primary control-theory treatment states this directly and describes pulse phasing as a physical realization (Nonlinear flight physics of the Lie Bracket roll mechanism, Nonlinear Dynamics, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11071-021-06940-z). A 2026 biological-control article applies the same drug-as-vector-field and order-matters bracket interpretation to gerotherapeutic sequencing (https://www.aging-us.com/article/206422/text); this is a modeling proposal, not experimental validation of our assay.

Closer, a 2026 preprint titled "Latent Confounded Causal Discovery via Lie Bracket Geometry" computes brackets of intervention-induced response fields for causal screening and explicitly treats nonzero brackets as nonspecific order/visible-closure diagnostics, not proof of hidden confounding (v2 dated 25 July 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19610). It also estimates a spectral rank of projected bracket residuals under a low-rank factorization, but does not give the same AB-versus-BA physical-pulse estimator or the algebraic bound rank(K)<=2n for the scalar endpoint commutator matrix. This is close enough that no broad novelty claim is justified. Nonlinear input-output realization theory has long treated minimal state dimension from input-output behavior (e.g. Kotta, Moog, and Tõnso, Automatica 95 (2018), 207-212, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2018.05.007), further lowering the novelty bar for an isolated rank inequality.

Assessment after prior art
Candidate 2 gives a clean, physically interpretable protocol for estimating an observable commutator and a cheap exact latent-dimension lower bound. The bracket expansion and qualitative mechanism are established control theory, and bracket-based causal screening now exists. The precise intervention-order estimator plus rank(K)/2 lower bound may be a useful new interface, but the current result is a short consequence of standard bracket algebra and state-space factorization, with an O(m^2) intervention burden and no upper-identification guarantee. It does not meet the charter's 8/10 foundational target on current evidence. Status: OPEN; no novelty or capability breakthrough established.
