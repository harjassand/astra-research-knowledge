H16 — FINITE-SPEC PASSIVE ONE-PORT DESIGN
Date: 2026-10-10

STATUS: OPEN. I found a useful exact certificate for one small passive-network design problem, but not a missing representation at the campaign's 8/10 foundational level. The mechanism is a direct composition of established Loewner rational realization, passivity, and network synthesis results. No claim of novelty, general inverse-design capability, hardware validation, or breakthrough is made.

FOUNDATIONAL TARGET
Given finite measured/specification intervals for a physical device's terminal response, construct a passive device without supplying its full transfer operator and certify a minimum complexity or controllability property. The concrete restricted target here is minimum dynamic state count for a proper, stable scalar linear one-port with a real-rational positive-real impedance, using only finite Laplace-domain samples.

STRONGEST BASELINE / PRIOR ART
The generalized Loewner framework constructs descriptor realizations from finite rational interpolation data; its Loewner and shifted Loewner matrices encode interpolant order [Mayo and Antoulas, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2007.03.008). Positive-real interpolation already couples Loewner and Pick constructions to obtain stable passive interpolants [Antoulas, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2004.07.007). For strictly passive systems, selected frequency-response data can directly yield port-Hamiltonian realizations [Benner, Goyal, and Van Dooren, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2020.104741). Once a rational positive-real impedance is known, classical Brune synthesis realizes it as a passive RLC network [Brune, 1931](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1931101191). A recent preprint estimates low-order systems from repeated noisy frequency-response data using a Loewner nuclear-norm penalty plus a convex stability constraint; its June 2026 version proves sampled and all-frequency error bounds [Honarpisheh and Sznaier, arXiv:2508.17142v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17142).

The possible local discriminator is narrower: deterministic interval uncertainty yields a proof of a lower bound on every feasible proper realization's state count, and a matching passive circuit can turn that into exact optimality. This is an elementary perturbation certificate around known Loewner structure, not a new synthesis theory. Unlike a full-operator inverse design problem, the input below contains four sample values only. It does not certify the response between those points.

MECHANISM AND MATHEMATICS OBTAINED
For left nodes mu_j and right nodes lambda_k, define the Loewner matrix

    L[j,k] = (y(mu_j) - y(lambda_k)) / (mu_j - lambda_k).

If a scalar transfer function has a realization H(s)=D+C(sI-A)^(-1)B with n dynamic states, the resolvent identity gives

    L[j,k] = -C(mu_j I-A)^(-1) (lambda_k I-A)^(-1) B,

so rank(L) <= n. This is architecture-independent and therefore lower-bounds the states of every passive candidate matching the samples.

For interval constraints |y_i-y_i^0| <= eps_i, the entrywise error in L is at most

    |Delta L[j,k]| <= (eps_mu_j + eps_lambda_k)/|mu_j-lambda_k|.

Let delta be the Frobenius norm of these entrywise bounds. Then ||L-L0||_2 <= delta for every feasible device. Weyl's inequality implies that sigma_(d+1)(L0)>delta certifies at least d+1 states for every device satisfying the intervals. A feasible passive d-state realization gives the matching upper bound and proves optimality of state count. For general inputs, finding such a passive low-order interpolant is still a nonconvex search; this certificate alone does not find one.

EXACT WORKED DESIGN
Use dimensionless x=s*tau and z=Z/R0. Specify four sample centers and closed intervals of radius 0.01:

    x = 1, 2, 3, 4
    z = 3/2, 4/3, 5/4, 6/5

Choose left nodes mu=(1,2) and right nodes lambda=(3,4). Exact rational arithmetic gives

    L0 = [[-1/8, -1/10], [-1/12, -1/15]],    det(L0)=0.

For the four interval radii, the squared Frobenius perturbation bound is exactly

    delta^2 = (1/100)^2 + (1/150)^2 + (1/50)^2 + (1/100)^2 = 29/45000.

Also ||L0||_2 >= |L0[1,1]| = 1/8, and 1/64 > 29/45000. Hence ||L||_2 >= ||L0||_2-delta >= 1/8-delta > 0 for every feasible sample vector. A zero-state proper device has constant impedance and Loewner rank zero, so at least one dynamic state is necessary. This exact inequality avoids relying on a floating-point singular-value threshold.

The full transfer function was not supplied. Solving only the first three center constraints under a one-pole ansatz z(x)=(d*x+q)/(x+a) gives the exact linear system

    z_i*a - d*x_i - q = -z_i*x_i,

whose solution is a=1, d=1, q=2. The fourth sample independently checks: z(4)=6/5. The resulting candidate is

    z(x) = 1 + 1/(x+1).

It is strictly positive real because Re z(x)=1+Re(1/(x+1))>1 for Re x>0. A passive realization is a series resistor R0 followed by a parallel R0-resistor and capacitor C=tau/R0. Taking R0=1 kOhm and tau=1 ms gives two 1 kOhm resistors and a 1 microfarad capacitor. The one capacitor provides one state; the sample-based lower bound proves that no zero-state device can meet the intervals. Thus this device is minimum-state in the declared class. This does not prove minimum component count or a unique circuit.

The reproducible script [analyze_example.py](h16_inverse_design/analyze_example.py) checks the exact rational determinant, bound, one-pole reconstruction, interpolation, and physical-circuit mapping. Its NumPy singular values are diagnostic only; the certificate uses fractions and an exact norm lower bound.

STRONGEST FALSIFIER: FINITE SPECS DO NOT IDENTIFY THE DEVICE
The state-count result does not determine behavior away from the four sample locations. For any integer q>=1, choose a_i=10+i for i=1,...,4 and a_(4+j)=14+j for j=1,...,q, and define

    r_q(x) = product_(i=1)^4 (x-i) / product_(k=1)^(4+q) (x+a_k),
    z_q(x) = z(x) + r_q(x).

The perturbation vanishes at every specified sample. On Re x>=0, |x-i|<=|x+a_i| for the four paired factors and |x+a_(4+j)|>=a_(4+j) for the extra factors, so |r_q(x)|<=product_(j=1)^q 1/(14+j)<=1/15. Hence Re z_q(x)>=1-1/15>0 throughout the right half-plane. Each z_q is real-rational, stable, strictly positive real, and has q+5 distinct poles/states (the original pole -1 and the 4+q added poles). Classical passive synthesis therefore gives a higher-order passive realization with exactly the same four samples. The centers certify that the *minimum* feasible order is one, but they do not bound the order of another compatible device, identify topology, or guarantee any unmeasured band response.

RESOURCE AND PHYSICAL INTERFACE
The formal input is four scalar sample centers, four uncertainty radii, four Laplace nodes, and the device class: proper, stable, scalar real-rational positive-real one-port impedances. Here there are four scalar sample acquisitions, each to +/-0.01 in normalized impedance (about +/-10 ohms at the stated physical scale). The certificate uses a 2x2 Loewner matrix, rational arithmetic, and a closed-form three-parameter fit; the cost is negligible. In an experiment the Laplace-domain values could be estimated from known input/output transforms at four positive-real nodes; no such acquisition was performed here, and the values are synthetic design constraints.

The construction assumes ideal lumped components. It charges no hardware, calibration, parasitic, temperature, component quantization, package-size, or power budget. The one-state circuit is physically ordinary at the chosen scale, but its tolerance and full-band behavior were not measured or simulated. The positive-real family used for the falsifier is passively realizable in the ideal network model; general Brune realizations may require ideal transformers. No outside validation occurred.

WHY THIS DOES NOT CLOSE THE MISSION
The representation engages one slice of the inverse barrier: finite specs yield an exact state lower bound, a synthesized feasible device attains it, and the desired transfer function is reconstructed from samples. The general method still needs a passive rank-constrained interpolation search. Loewner data can have low rank without giving a passive realization; enforcing positive-realness or port-Hamiltonian structure adds constraints, and the global minimum-order search remains nonconvex. The example also optimizes dynamic states only. It does not optimize topology/component count, enforce fabrication limits, infer arbitrary material microstructure, certify a useful response between samples, or provide controllability for a multiport objective.
