I also derived a materially different constructive representation: strong

EXACT FOUNDATIONAL TARGET

 xdot=-Kx-alpha*x^[3]+e_1*u,

STRONGEST EXACT FALSIFIER: AN ARBITRARY HIDDEN GRAPH CAN BE INVISIBLE
Let m=n-1 and let L_G be the Laplacian of any weighted undirected graph on the
hidden vertices, with arbitrary nonnegative edge weights. Define
 K_G = [[a, -b*1^T],[-b*1, d*I+L_G]],
where a,d,b>0 and ad>m*b^2.
The measured node is connected directly to every hidden node, so every full
graph is connected even if the hidden graph G is empty. Offdiagonal entries
are nonpositive. Since L_G*1=0, the bright 2D block in basis e_1,1/sqrt(m) is
 [[a,-b*sqrt(m)],[-b*sqrt(m),d]],
and the dark block is d*I+L_G on 1-perp. These blocks are positive definite.
For a=d=2,b=1/sqrt(m), lambda_min(K_G)>=1 for every G, uniformly in n.

Starting at zero, all hidden coordinates remain equal to s(t), because the
cubic acts identically and L_G annihilates a synchronized vector. The complete
controlled dynamics reduce to
 ydot=-a*y+m*b*s-alpha*y^3+u,
 sdot=b*y-d*s-alpha*s^3.
Neither equation depends on G. Thus every hidden topology, from empty graph to
complete graph and every positive edge weight in between, produces EXACTLY the
same complete nonlinear input/output map. Arbitrarily strong, long, precise,
adaptive scalar control cannot distinguish the networks. Known n and a uniform
stability bound do not repair this failure.

The smallest explicit example, frozen before gate/literature read, is
 K_0=[[4,-1,-1],[-1,4,0],[-1,0,4]],
 K_1=[[4,-1,-1],[-1,5,-1],[-1,-1,5]].
The hidden-hidden edge changes from absent to present. Both are connected SPD
M-matrices. Their hidden antisymmetric eigenvalues are 4 and 6, but every u
generates the same y. These are not equivalent by hidden signed permutation.
This is a synchrony/equitable-quotient obstruction; its conceptual foundation
is established prior art, so it is not being claimed as a new landmark theorem.

POSITIVE RESULT A: LINEARLY DARK DOES NOT ALWAYS MEAN NONLINEARLY INVISIBLE
For a rotated 3-node family, let w=(0,cos(theta),sin(theta)) and
v=(0,-sin(theta),cos(theta)), and let the matrix in basis (e_1,w,v) be
 [[a,beta,0],[beta,c,0],[0,0,mu]], with ac>beta^2 and mu>0.
All values of mu have the same linear boundary response. Put
A=cos(theta)^4+sin(theta)^4 and
B=-sin(theta)*cos(theta)*cos(2theta).
For u=epsilon*u_0, the first and third boundary response coefficients do not
depend on mu. Cubic mixing drives the dark coordinate at order 3:
 r_3dot=-mu*r_3-alpha*B*q_1^3.
It returns to the observed bright coordinate at order 5 through
 -3alpha*B*q_1^2*r_3.
Hence the fifth-order boundary response contains the resolvent of the otherwise
invisible dark mode. The first derivation gives an explicit mu reconstruction
formula; A identifies B^2=(1-A)(2A-1)/2 and the hidden angle up to physical gauges.
This recovers the family for 1/2<A<1, including linearly noncyclic matrices.

Synthetic RK4 checks at theta=pi/8 and mu=3 versus 5 show small-amplitude
output-difference slopes 4.992,4.978,4.948, consistent with order 5. At amplitude
0.03, the maximum boundary difference is only 7.86e-13 over time horizon 8.
As theta approaches pi/4, the distinction scales quadratically in distance to
synchrony; the exact synchronized limit agrees to floating-point roundoff.
These are finite sanity checks of the derived formula, not an external validation
or proof of a general reconstruction algorithm.

POSITIVE RESULT B: STRONG-CLAMP RELEASE REPRESENTATION
Write K=[[a,b^T],[b,A]], m=n-1. Assume all b_i nonzero with distinct magnitudes.
Fix hidden sign gauges so b_i<0 and set c_i=(-b_i/alpha)^(1/3)>0.
Clamp y=r^3 to equilibrium; then release-clamp y=0. With epsilon=r^-2,
z=r*w and fast time tau=r^2*t,
 w'=-alpha*w^[3]-epsilon*A*w,
 w(0)=c-epsilon*(Ac)/(3alpha*c^[2])+O(epsilon^2).
The boundary reaction q=b^Tz has
 q/r=Q_0(tau)+epsilon*Q_1(tau)+O(epsilon^2),
 Q_0=-alpha*sum_i c_i^4/sqrt(1+2alpha*c_i^2*tau),
 Q_1=sum_ij A_ij*F_ij(tau),
where, with d_i=2alpha*c_i^2,
 F_ij=[c_i*c_j/3+alpha*c_i^3*c_j*I_ij(tau)]/(1+d_i*tau)^(3/2),
 I_ij=integral_0^tau (1+d_i*s)^(3/2)/(1+d_j*s)^(1/2) ds.

Q_0 uniquely labels the direct-port couplings through its distinct negative
square-root branch points. For i!=j, F_ij+F_ji contains a nonzero coefficient
of log(sqrt(tau+1/d_i)+sqrt(tau+1/d_j)). Analytic-continuation commutators around
the pair of branch points isolate each offdiagonal coefficient; the remaining
diagonal functions have distinct individual branch points. This gives a
candidate all-m proof that A -> Q_1 is injective. The full derivation includes
the explicit antiderivative and monodromy bookkeeping. Independent proof review
is still needed, and analytical continuation is only a uniqueness argument.

Exact finite-field formal-series ranks are full for m=1,...,12, with unknown
dimensions 1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36,45,55,66,78. A generic set of N=m(m+1)/2 positive
times therefore suffices in principle once c and Q_1 are perfectly supplied.
The method replaces high-order IO jets with scalar nonlinear transient fitting;
it does not make infinite-amplitude limits or perfect coefficient extraction free.

RESOURCE INTERFACE AND FAILURE OF THE PRACTICAL GATE
The clamp can be approximated with smooth finite measured-port feedback.
Preparing y=r^3 requires steady force alpha*r^9+O(r^3). The release bandwidth
is O(r^2). The informative coupling correction has raw force size O(r^-1)
against an O(r) leading reaction. If force is inferred from y through feedback
of bandwidth O(r^2), the coupling signal in y is only O(r^-3). Increasing r
reduces asymptotic model bias but increases force, state range, bandwidth, and
the relative precision required for subtracting Q_0. Dense output alone costs
Theta(n^2) data/memory. Estimating the c_i is itself an unstable mixture problem.

Even granting the direct couplings and exact Q_0 subtraction, 70-digit SVD
for c_i uniformly spaced in [0.7,1.3] gives column-normalized release-design
condition numbers approximately
 m=2:4.3e2; m=3:3.5e6; m=4:4.2e9; m=5:1.0e15; m=6:1.4e22.
These use 121 points logarithmically spanning tau=1e-5 to 1e3, plus tau=0.
Simple time-window changes do not rescue the tested family: 81 uniformly spaced
points over tau in [0,T], T in {0.1,1,10,100,1000}, have their best m=6 condition
about 4.1e25. These figures concern explicit tested designs, not a lower bound
against every possible waveform or optimized experiment. Double-precision
results beyond its conditioning floor are not treated as accurate numbers.

This representation therefore has no demonstrated usable large-network
advantage. It makes the decisive obstacle visible: exact uniqueness is encoded
in unstable distinctions among analytic scalar response functions. Sparse
networks with missing direct-port edges are not covered. No all-control minimax
lower bound beyond the exact synchrony family has been established.

STRONGEST PRIMARY PRIOR ART CHECKED AFTER FIRST FREEZE
1. Vanelli and Hendrickx, Local Identifiability of Networks with Nonlinear Node
   Dynamics, arXiv:2412.08472v4, 31 Aug 2026:
   https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08472v4
   The current version covers recurrent as well as feedforward networks. Its
   model is discrete time, y_i(t)=f_i(sum_j w_ij*y_j(t-1))+b_i*u_i(t-1), with
   known topology and known analytic node nonlinearities. It proves generic
   LOCAL weight identifiability, for almost all nonlinearities, under source/sink
   excitation/measurement conditions. It explicitly distinguishes topology
   recovery and practical identification, and says its high-derivative proof
   does not provide a robust identification algorithm. It does not immediately
   specialize to this fixed continuous-time componentwise-cubic gradient model.
   Nevertheless, "known nonlinearities permit sparse access to recurrent network
   weights" is already a clear predecessor theme and cannot be claimed here.

2. Vizuete and Hendrickx, Nonlinear Network Identifiability with Full Excitations,
   arXiv:2405.07636v2, 28 May 2025:
   https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07636v2
   This covers additive edge dynamics, full excitation, and partial measurement,
   including general digraphs under appropriate separability hypotheses. Its
   interface differs materially from a single actuator. It reinforces that
   recurrent nonlinear network identifiability itself is established territory.

3. Whalen, Sauer, Brennan, and Schiff, Observability and Controllability of
   Neuronal Network Motifs, arXiv:1307.5478v2:
   https://arxiv.org/html/1307.5478v2
   Numerical nonlinear motif observability/controllability analysis already links
   symmetries and dynamical synchrony to lost access, and symmetry-breaking
   heterogeneity to restored access. This is conceptual prior art against
   treating the exact synchronized obstruction as novel.

4. O'Clery, Yuan, Stan, and Barahona, Observability and coarse graining of consensus
   dynamics through the external equitable partition, Phys Rev E 88,042805 (2013):
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.88.042805
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24229224/
   The primary abstract relates quotient graphs, synchronization manifolds, and
   observability for consensus dynamics. Our cubic invariant-subspace example
   is an elementary model-specific extension of this established mechanism.

5. Chatterjee and Chintha, Identification and parameter estimation of cubic
   nonlinear damping using harmonic probing and Volterra series (2020):
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijnonlinmec.2020.103518
   The primary publisher abstract covers cubic damping identification via
   higher-order frequency-response functions and harmonic probing. It does not
   settle the exact unknown-network single-port problem, but blocks a broad
   novelty claim for nonlinear harmonic/cubic response identification alone.
