CANDIDATE MECHANISM AND DERIVATION
For a supplied bipartite graph G=(L,R,E), hard-core activity λ≥0, and S⊆L, choosing S leaves exactly R\\N(S) available on the right. Summing those right-side occupations gives the exact positive identity

  Z_G(λ) = Σ_{S⊆L} λ^{|S|}(1+λ)^{|R\\N(S)|}.

This is a positive marginal but can still have exponentially many terms and can inherit a sampling bottleneck. Group by the neighborhood union U=N(S). For an ordering L={v_1,…,v_m}, define

  A_i(U) = Σ_{S⊆{v_1,…,v_i}: N(S)=U} λ^{|S|},
  q = |{N(S):S⊆L}|.

Start with A_0(∅)=1. For v_i, each prior state U either stays U (exclude v_i) or moves to U∪N(v_i) with weight multiplied by λ (include v_i). At the end,

  Z_G(λ) = Σ_U A_m(U)(1+λ)^{|R\\U|}.

This is a positive dynamic program with at most q states at every layer. It also gives an exact sampler: select terminal U with mass proportional to A_m(U)(1+λ)^{|R\\U|}; backtrack through the stored DP layers by choosing predecessor-union/include pairs in proportion to their DP weights; then independently include each vertex of R\\U with probability λ/(1+λ). This samples the exact hard-core distribution. It does not depend on Markov-chain mixing.

Discriminating family K_{n,n}. At λ=1, the neighborhood-union state space has exactly two states: ∅ and R. The coefficients are A_n(∅)=1 and A_n(R)=2^n−1, so

  Z_{K_{n,n}}(1)=2^n+(2^n−1)=2^{n+1}−1.

The terminal states are the two phases: S=∅ with all right subsets allowed has mass 2^n; S≠∅ with no right vertices has mass 2^n−1. The union-state sampler draws one of these phase labels directly, then samples within it. It bypasses the local chain's phase barrier.

For the random-vertex single-site heat-bath chain at λ=1, let A be the nonempty left-only states. Its stationary mass is (2^n−1)/(2^{n+1}−1). The only transitions leaving A are from a singleton left set to the empty set. Summing those n transitions gives Q(A,A^c)=1/[4(2^{n+1}−1)], hence

  Φ(A)=Q(A,A^c)/π(A)=1/[4(2^n−1)].

The conductance is exponentially small. Even the one-side marginal's obvious single-site heat-bath chain remains slow: conditioned on all other left bits being zero, one specified left bit turns on with probability 1/(2^n+1). Thus elimination alone fails; keeping the union U as a phase state is the useful step.

For general λ, K_{n,n} has Z=2(1+λ)^n−1 and terminal phase masses (1+λ)^n and (1+λ)^n−1. The exact original-chain conductance for the same phase cut is λ/[2(1+λ)((1+λ)^n−1)].

PRIOR ART AND STRONGEST BASELINE
The candidate exact DP is neighborhood-union dynamic programming. This is the core state-counting idea behind boolean-width; boolean-width is defined through the number of distinct unions of neighborhoods across a cut. Prior work gives weighted and counting independent-set algorithms from supplied boolean-width decompositions, with O*(2^{2k}) time for width k and O*(2^k) for a linear decomposition. Our single bipartition DP is the direct one-sided form, parameterized by q=2^k. Therefore it is not a novel representation or algorithmic paradigm.

The strongest directly relevant constructive baseline found is the 2026 paper by Jenssen, Malekshahian, and Park, “A Refined Graph Container Lemma and Applications to the Hard-Core Model on Bipartite Expanders.” For d-regular bipartite α-expanders with fixed α>0, it gives an FPTAS and polynomial-time sampling when λ≥C log²(d)/sqrt(d). The same paper improves the known slow-mixing range for Glauber dynamics down to λ=tilde-Ω(d^{-1/2}) on bipartite expanders. Since the d-regular-tree uniqueness threshold is λ_c(d)=(d−1)^{d−1}/(d−2)^d≈e/d, these results treat a substantial non-uniqueness regime where local mixing is already slow. The construction uses graph containers to identify the two imbalanced phases and a polymer/cluster-expansion analysis; this is precisely the broader phase-resolution mechanism sought here, already established in a substantially stronger form than the K_{n,n} DP.

Earlier constructive baselines include Jenssen, Keevash, and Perkins (SODA 2019): FPTAS and efficient sampling for the high-fugacity hard-core model on bounded-degree bipartite expanders; and Cannon–Perkins (SODA 2020): FPTAS, sampling, and cluster-expansion representations for bipartite graphs with sufficient degree or fugacity imbalance. Their polymer weights express deviations from a one-side phase. At the exact identity level, factoring out (1+λ)^{|R|} gives

  Z_G(λ)=(1+λ)^{|R|} Σ_{S⊆L} λ^{|S|}(1+λ)^{-|N(S)|}.

If S is decomposed into components in the graph on L that joins vertices sharing a neighbor, the neighborhood sets of distinct components are disjoint; this yields the standard positive polymer partition function with polymer weight λ^{|γ|}/(1+λ)^{|N(γ)|}. For K_{n,n}, all nonempty S form one polymer and its total activity approaches one, explaining why a one-phase small-activity expansion is inadequate without resolving the second phase.

Correlation-decay and hardness baselines delimit generalization. Weitz gives a deterministic approximation scheme for maximum-degree-d graphs for λ<λ_c(d). Sly proves that just above λ_c(d), approximate counting is not possible in polynomial time unless NP=RP, on general maximum-degree-d graphs. For bipartite graphs in the non-uniqueness region, the hard-core partition function is #BIS-hard; this is a serious barrier, although #BIS itself is conjectured rather than proved not to have an FPRAS.

WHY THIS ENGAGES THE OBSTACLE, AND WHERE IT STOPS
The K_{n,n} calculation demonstrates that exponential local-chain mixing does not imply counting or sampling hardness: the union state records the phase, and exact backtracking jumps between phases without crossing the empty bottleneck. In an expander family, container/polymer methods turn this same qualitative idea into polynomial approximation and sampling beyond uniqueness.

The candidate's own bottleneck is q. On general bipartite graphs, the number of distinct neighborhood unions can be exponential, and computing every union does not give a general polynomial-time procedure. The positive marginal also does not fix slow mixing unless one preserves phase information; its induced local chain has the same exponential exit probability. This construction has no sign-cancellation theorem and does not address complex or signed partition functions. It therefore engages the mixing obstacle on a small family and recovers a known structural-width method, but it does not remove the decisive general counting/sampling obstacle.

RESOURCE INTERFACE
Input: adjacency lists for G and a bipartition (or a bipartite graph from which BFS finds one), plus rational λ=a/b≥0 encoded in B bits. Graph acquisition and bipartition discovery cost O(|V|+|E|). No oracle, training, or external data are used.

The DP performs O(|L| q) state transitions, each with neighborhood-union bitset and dictionary work; a conservative word-level bound is O(|L| q ceil(|R|/w)) plus map overhead, where w is the machine word size. Exact rational weights have O(|V|B+|V|) bits, so bit complexity is polynomial in |V|, B, and q. Counting needs O(q) live states if only the final sum is required. Exact backward sampling stores all layers, using O(|L|q) states and O(|L|q(|R|+|V|B)) bits. Categorical choices can be made from exact integer weights using rejection sampling, with expected polynomial random-bit cost; the final right-side Bernoulli draws use λ/(1+λ). If λ is a real number rather than a rational input, the required input precision must be specified and charged separately.

On K_{n,n}, q=2 and both count and exact sampling are polynomial in n. In the worst case q can be exponential, so the general worst-case cost remains exponential. The executable check in repro.py compared the DP with brute force on a small irregular bipartite instance and checked the K_{n,n} count and coefficient formulas for n=1,…,8. The conductance is derived analytically above, not independently simulated. These finite checks validate the recurrence implementation only on the tested cases.

STRONGEST FALSIFIER
The first proposal, summing out one side and then locally resampling the resulting marginal, fails on K_{n,n}: it retains the 1/(2^n+1) exit probability from the empty left set. The successful union-state DP works because it preserves a phase label, but the number of such labels can be exponential. More broadly, Sly's maximum-degree hardness result rules out a generic algorithm on all graphs just beyond tree uniqueness unless NP=RP, while #BIS-hardness makes the bipartite non-uniqueness regime an unresolved approximation-complexity problem.
