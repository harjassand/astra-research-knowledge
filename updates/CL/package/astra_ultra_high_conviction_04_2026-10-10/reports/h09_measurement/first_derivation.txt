FIRST DERIVATION FREEZE (before literature search)
2026-10-10

Candidate mechanism: use the leading quadratic susceptibility of a one-port
network with a known, identical local nonlinearity at every hidden node. The
linear port transfer is invariant under orthogonal changes of hidden-state
coordinates that fix the accessible port. Elementwise node nonlinearities do
not share that invariance, so the second-order response can reveal a quantity
that linear probing cannot.

Model and resource interface. A symmetric positive-definite 3-by-3 matrix K
describes a stable hidden linear network, e_1 is the sole driven and observed
port, and the known local quadratic coefficient is normalized to one:

    dx/dt = -K x + u(t)e_1 + x^{\odot 2},   y=e_1^T x,   x(0)=0.

The proposed experiment uses a calibrated small constant input u(t)=epsilon,
waits for the stable local equilibrium, and measures the port output y. It
requires no interior sensor or spatially patterned actuator. It does require
that the local quadratic constitutive law and its common coefficient are
known or independently calibrated.

Linear impossibility. Let

    K  = [[4,1,0],[1,3,0],[0,0,5]],
    Q  = [[1,0,0],[0,1/sqrt(2),-1/sqrt(2)],
          [0,1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(2)]],
    K' = Q K Q^T.

Q is orthogonal and Qe_1=e_1. Therefore, for every complex frequency s where
the resolvent exists,

    e_1^T(sI+K')^{-1}e_1 = e_1^T(sI+K)^{-1}e_1.

Thus every linear input waveform at this one port gives the same output for
these two distinct internal coupling matrices. Both are positive definite;
K' has entries [[4,sqrt(2)/2,sqrt(2)/2],
[sqrt(2)/2,4,-1],[sqrt(2)/2,-1,4]].

Nonlinear discrimination. The equilibrium branch through x=0 exists analytically
for sufficiently small epsilon by the implicit-function theorem, since its
Jacobian there is K. Write x(epsilon)=epsilon x_1+epsilon^2 x_2+O(epsilon^3).
The equilibrium equation Kx=epsilon e_1+x^{\odot2} gives

    x_1=K^{-1}e_1,
    x_2=K^{-1}(x_1^{\odot2}),
    y_2=e_1^T x_2 = sum_i (x_1)_i^3.

For K, x_1=(3/11,-1/11,0)^T and y_2=26/1331. For K', x'_1=Qx_1,
so y'_2=27/1331-sqrt(2)/2662. The linear output coefficient is 3/11
for each system, while

    y'_2-y_2 = (2-sqrt(2))/2662 > 0.

Hence their small-signal steady outputs differ by
((2-sqrt(2))/2662) epsilon^2 + O(epsilon^3). This is an exact, finite
counterexample to the claim that the complete one-port linear transfer
function is sufficient to predict the response of a network with known
nodewise quadratic dynamics. The measured feature is the cubic coordinate
moment of the first-order port-access profile, not a full reconstruction of K.

Initial limitation and resource charge. This proves separation of one
orthogonally equivalent pair only. It does not establish generic recovery,
topology recovery, robustness, or an 8/10 foundational capability. For
independent Gaussian read noise with standard deviation sigma per settled
reading and a fixed small amplitude epsilon, if the exact mean separation is
Delta(epsilon), the equal-prior optimal error is
Phi(-sqrt(N)*abs(Delta)/(2*sigma)); achieving error at most alpha therefore
requires N >= 4 sigma^2 Phi^{-1}(1-alpha)^2 / Delta(epsilon)^2 readings.
As epsilon tends to zero, this scales as sigma^2 epsilon^-4. Each reading also
requires amplitude calibration, settling time set by the slowest stable mode,
and an input-energy cost proportional to epsilon^2 times the dwell time. No
claim of practical gain is made until the coefficient separation is compared
with a measured device's noise, model error, and nonlinear operating range.

Frozen claims before prior-art search:
1. The two matrices have exactly the same scalar linear transfer at the only
   accessible port.
2. The explicitly specified quadratic state dynamics separates their
   equilibrium response at order epsilon^2 with the rational-algebraic gap
   shown above.
3. General measurement principle, novelty, physical practicality, and full
   hidden-structure recovery remain OPEN.
