H21 WILDCARD — PAIRWISE-STEALTH NETWORK OUTAGE
2026-10-10

Target
Can component-level failure rates and all pairwise failure statistics certify the tail probability of a redundant network outage to useful multiplicative accuracy? This matters for safety-critical networks because an outage probability can be many orders of magnitude smaller under the independent-failure model than under another law with the same measured pair statistics.

Status and claim boundary
The candidate is not a new theorem or a validated physical mechanism. The core ingredients—finite-field constructions of k-wise independent variables, extremal probabilities of AND events under k-wise independence, and global graph-property failures under k-wise independence—are established prior art. I found no basis to claim the campaign’s 8/10 significance threshold. The useful outcome is a compact exact stress case and a narrower open engineering question about relative tail certification. Status: OPEN.

Frozen first derivation
The original derivation, frozen before literature comparison, is preserved at reports/h21_wildcard/first_derivation_frozen.txt. It constructs k-wise independent rare failures from evaluations of a uniform random degree-(k−1) polynomial over a finite field. For any t≤k distinct labels, the Vandermonde evaluation map has rank t, so the t failure indicators are independent Bernoulli(1/q). If m>k, all m failures occur exactly when the polynomial is zero, with probability q^(−k), versus q^(−m) under full independence. This is an exact factor q^(m−k).

Network consequence
Let G=(V,E) be connected, have m≤q edges with distinct labels α_e in F_q, and have edge connectivity λ(G)≥k. Draw F uniformly from polynomials of degree <k over F_q, and fail edge e iff F(α_e)=0. Every set of at most k edge states is exactly independent Bernoulli(p), p=1/q. A nonzero F has at most k−1 roots, so it deletes fewer than λ(G) edges and cannot disconnect G. The zero polynomial deletes all edges. Therefore the network outage probability under this law is exactly q^(−k).

Under the fully independent Bernoulli(p) edge-failure model, every particular cut C fails with probability p^|C|. If τ(G) is the number of nontrivial vertex cuts, the independent outage probability satisfies
p^λ(G) ≤ P_iid(outage) ≤ τ(G) p^λ(G).
Thus the k-wise law gives a relative-risk amplification of at least q^(λ(G)−k)/τ(G). This is a lower bound; it is not a general exact formula for the worst-case risk over k-wise laws.

Concrete exact comparison: K_10
K_10 has m=45 edges and edge connectivity λ=9. Take q=47, k=2, and p=1/47. The polynomial law makes every edge pair independent with failure probability 1/47. For a nonzero affine polynomial, at most one edge fails, so K_10 stays connected; when the polynomial is zero, all 45 edges fail. Its outage probability is exactly 1/47² = 1/2209 ≈ 4.5269352648×10^(−4).

For the iid baseline, let c_n be the probability that K_n remains connected when each edge independently fails with probability p. Decomposing by the size j of the connected component containing a fixed vertex gives
c_1=1,
c_n = 1 − Σ_{j=1}^{n−1} binom(n−1,j−1) c_j p^[j(n−j)].
Exact rational arithmetic at p=1/47 yields P_iid(K_10 disconnected) ≈ 8.9355086296×10^(−15). The polynomial-law outage is about 5.0662312046×10^10 times larger. The exact recurrence is reproducible with reports/h21_wildcard/exact_iid_k10.py.

For k=2, the same failure-count law has a simpler description: all edges fail with probability p²; each specified edge alone fails with probability p(1−p); otherwise none fail, with residual probability 1−mp+(m−1)p². For m≤q and p=1/q this residual is nonnegative. Every edge has failure probability p and every pair fails with probability p². This shows how a rare all-network common mode can be exactly balanced by singleton outcomes so pairwise statistics remain independent. It is a synthetic adversarial law, not a claim about how real links fail.

Strongest alternatives and prior art
The direct baseline is exact iid network reliability, not a loose approximation; the K_10 value above is computed by an exact recurrence. A robust engineering alternative also exists: common-cause failure analysis. NRC NUREG/CR-5485 gives procedures for identifying and quantifying dependent failures in nuclear-plant probabilistic risk assessment: https://nrcoe.inl.gov/publicdocs/CCF/NUREGCR-5485_Guidelines%20on%20Modeling%20Common-Cause%20Failures%20in%20PRA.pdf. The construction would be represented as a particular common-cause multiplicity distribution if supported by physical evidence.

The mathematical mechanism is already known. Alon and Nussboim studied connectivity and other global properties of k-wise independent random graphs; they give pairwise-independent graph distributions that fail to preserve connectivity: https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/KWISEf.pdf. Benjamini, Gurel-Gurevich, and Peled study k-wise independent inputs to Boolean functions, including AND and percolation: https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3261. Berend et al. give exact expressions for the maximal probability that all k-wise independent bits are 1 in parameter regimes: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18688. The elementary upper bound P(all m fail)≤p^k and the construction attaining p^k in the displayed small-p regime are therefore not a novelty claim.

Resources and interface
For the polynomial mechanism, one trial takes k uniform field elements, stores k coefficients and m labels, and performs O(mk) field operations to emit the edge-failure vector; memory is O(m+k). For the K_10 instance this is two F_47 coefficients, 45 affine evaluations, and about 11.1 bits of seed entropy. Exact field-uniform sampling, physical realization of the latent cause, and empirical validation have not been implemented or charged. An optimized iid Bernoulli sampler has its own randomness costs, so the seed count is not presented as a cost advantage over iid sampling. The iid comparator’s computation is an exact rational recurrence, not sampled simulation.

Strongest falsifier and what remains
Prior work directly addresses the global-property failure mode, and common-cause risk models already treat dependent multi-component outages. The finite-field representation adds no new physical explanation, resource advantage, or general reliability certificate. It also has a deliberately adversarial outage distribution and says nothing about whether a deployed network’s failure process has that structure. This defeats the proposed mechanism as a landmark discovery.

A narrower potential research target is a relative-tail guarantee: given only k-wise component failure constraints plus a stated physical common-cause model, determine the smallest k and amount of joint failure data needed to certify P(outage) within a multiplicative factor of the iid estimate. The K_10 example proves that a high-probability connectivity guarantee alone is insufficient for multiplicative tail accuracy: both models are connected with overwhelming probability, yet their rare outage probabilities differ by about 5×10^10. No theorem resolving that engineering target is claimed here.
