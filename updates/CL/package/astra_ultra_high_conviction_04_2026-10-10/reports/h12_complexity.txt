H12 COMPLEXITY MISSION — FIRST DERIVATION (FROZEN BEFORE PRIOR-ART SEARCH)
Date: 2026-10-10
Mission: resource-explicit attack on the derandomization barrier.

Target. A deterministic generator G:{0,1}^s -> {0,1}^m that is claimed to fool all Boolean circuits of size at most S. The missing resource in many informal claims is that a short-seed generator has a small, explicitly enumerable support. Can that support itself be represented as a distinguisher?

Candidate representation: replace the distribution G(U_s) by its support-membership predicate. Define
    D_G(x) = 1 iff exists z in {0,1}^s such that G(z)=x.
If G has a Boolean circuit of size g over a fixed fan-in basis, then for each hardwired z, a copy of G with its s input bits fixed has size at most g. Equality with x costs O(m) gates. OR over all 2^s seeds costs O(2^s) gates. Thus D_G has size at most c 2^s(g+m+1) for a basis-dependent constant c. This construction is nonuniform in m and uses the description of G, but has no oracle, advice, or random-bit acquisition cost beyond explicitly paying for the copies.

Lemma (support distinguisher). Let K=|Im(G)|. Under X=G(U_s), D_G(X)=1 with probability 1. Under uniform X in {0,1}^m, Pr[D_G(X)=1]=K/2^m <= 2^s/2^m. Hence the distinguishing gap is exactly 1-K/2^m, and at least 1-2^{s-m}. Consequently, if S >= c 2^s(g+m+1), then G cannot be an epsilon-PRG against size-S circuits for any epsilon < 1-2^{s-m}. This gives the necessary seed-cost inequality s > log_2 S - log_2(c(g+m+1)) up to the integer boundary, whenever the target error is below the displayed gap and m>s.

Proof. D_G is the disjunction, over all seeds z, of the m-bit equality checks x=G(z). The equality is true on every output of G. Exactly K strings satisfy it among the 2^m uniform strings. The circuit-size bound follows by building each fixed-seed copy and comparator and joining their results with a binary OR tree. The seed-cost consequence is the contrapositive of this size bound and gap calculation. No computational assumption is used.

Exact falsifier for a common overreach. Let n>=2 and set G(z_1,...,z_{n-1})=(z_1,...,z_{n-1}, z_1 xor ... xor z_{n-1}). G(U_{n-1}) is uniform on the even-parity strings. Every projection onto at most n-1 coordinates is uniform, so the distribution is (n-1)-wise independent. Yet the O(n)-size parity circuit distinguishes it from U_n with gap 1/2: it outputs 1 on G(U) with probability 0 and on U_n with probability 1/2. At n=3 this is the four-point distribution {000,011,101,110}; every pair is uniform but parity separates it. Therefore matching all marginals of order below n is not a substitute for fooling unrestricted polynomial-size circuits.

Initial assessment (pre-literature). This is a rigorous resource barrier/certificate, not a PRG construction and not a circuit lower bound for an explicit hard function. It says exactly when short explicit support leaks through a circuit distinguisher. It cannot engage the hard case S << 2^s(g+m), or generators whose support is computationally inaccessible at the relevant size. Significance is presently below the campaign threshold; continue by checking whether the representation has a sharper, nontrivial use and by locating strongest primary-source prior art. The parity example is an exact falsifier of the proposed limited-independence shortcut, not a falsifier of the support lemma.

POST-DERIVATION EXTENSION AND PRIOR-ART AUDIT

A stronger feature-space form. Let X={0,1}^m and let V_t be the F_2-vector space of multilinear Boolean polynomials of degree at most t, where 0<=t<=m. Its dimension is M(m,t)=sum_{j=0}^t binom(m,j). If the image A=Im(G) has size <M(m,t), evaluation on A is a linear map V_t -> F_2^A with a nonzero kernel. Thus some nonzero p in V_t vanishes on every generated output. The degree-t Reed-Muller code has minimum nonzero weight 2^(m-t), so Pr[p(U_m)=1]>=2^-t. The test p therefore distinguishes G(U_s) from uniform by at least 2^-t. A circuit for p uses at most M monomials, each an AND of at most t variables, combined by XOR, and has size O(t M) over a fixed complete bounded-fan-in basis. Therefore, if the circuit budget S is at least c t M(m,t), an epsilon-PRG for degree-t polynomial tests with epsilon<2^-t must satisfy 2^s>=M(m,t), or s>=ceil(log_2 M(m,t)). For fixed t and growing m this gives s >= t log_2(m/t) up to lower-order terms.

Construction cost, not just existence. Given a description of G and its seed length s, enumerate all 2^s seeds, evaluate G, form the evaluation matrix of the M monomials, and Gaussian-eliminate to find p. With generator evaluation cost T_G, this costs O(2^s T_G + min(2^s,M) M^2) bit operations using a straightforward elimination implementation, plus polynomial overhead to emit the O(tM)-size test circuit. For fixed t, M=O(m^t), so this is polynomial time whenever G itself is polynomial-time evaluable and 2^s<M. If t grows, the construction may be superpolynomial and that cost must remain charged.

Proof of the weight bound. Induct on m. Write p(x',x_m)=g(x')+x_m h(x'), where deg(g)<=t and deg(h)<=t-1. If h=0, the two m-bit slices are equal to the nonzero g and the weight doubles the inductive lower bound. If h is nonzero, then for each x' with h(x')=1 the two slice values differ, so exactly one contributes 1; by induction h has weight at least 2^((m-1)-(t-1))=2^(m-t). Boundary cases t=0 and t=m are immediate. This yields wt(p)>=2^(m-t).

Exact limitation of feature dimension alone. Let V be the span of indicators of d chosen points P in {0,1}^m. Then dim(V)=d, but every nonzero member has uniform acceptance probability at most d/2^m. Choose a generated support A of d-1 points disjoint from P. A nonzero kernel element exists, but every test obtained from this V has distinguishing gap at most d/2^m. Thus dimension by itself gives no constant-gap distinguisher; a minimum-weight (or analogous anti-concentration) condition is essential. The general feature-annihilator statement is: if V has dimension d and every nonzero f in V satisfies Pr_U[f(U)=1]>=delta, then any distribution supported on fewer than d points is distinguished by some f in V with gap at least delta. Its test-size and witness-construction costs are those of the supplied basis for V.

Primary-source prior art and novelty result.
1. Bogdanov and Viola, “Pseudorandom bits for polynomials,” FOCS 2007 / SIAM J. Comput. 2010, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2007/081/ and https://doi.org/10.1137/070712109. Their Claim 30 gives the finite-field dimension-counting lower bound for generators fooling degree-d polynomial tests; it constructs a nonzero polynomial vanishing on the entire generator image and uses a nonzero-value probability bound to distinguish. This already contains the main feature-annihilator mechanism and seed lower bound.
2. Lee and Viola, “Some Limitations of the Sum of Small-Bias Distributions,” Theory of Computing 13(16), 2017, https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v013a016/. This is relevant prior work on the gap between small-bias guarantees and fooling broader test classes. The n=3 even-parity example above is included as an elementary exact diagnostic; I do not attribute that exact construction to this paper.
3. Hoza, Pyne, and Vadhan, “Pseudorandom Generators for Unbounded-Width Permutation Branching Programs,” ITCS 2021, https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.7; and the primary-source account by Pyne and Vadhan, “Limitations of the Impagliazzo–Nisan–Wigderson Pseudorandom Generator Against Permutation Branching Programs,” Algorithmica 2024, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-024-01251-2. Appendix C proves general seed lower bounds for width-2 permutation branching programs. In particular, their proof uses a nonzero parity relation across the generator's outputs when 2^s<n, directly subsuming the t=1 affine/parity case without the INW restriction.
4. Oliveira and Santhanam, “Pseudorandom Generators and Hitting Set Generators,” APPROX/RANDOM 2018, https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2018.55, explicitly note that the support of a pseudorandom distribution is a hitting set. This confirms the basic support viewpoint is definitional background, not a new representation by itself.

Strongest falsifier of the proposed novelty. The low-degree feature-annihilator lemma is already explicit in Bogdanov–Viola, while the parity special case is already used for general PRGs against width-2 branching programs. The frozen support-membership lemma is a direct circuit implementation of the standard support/hitting-set observation. Thus none of the candidate mechanisms supplies new leverage against the general derandomization barrier. The n=3 exact example is executable at reports/h12_complexity/parity_falsifier.py; its output confirms all pair marginals are uniform while the parity test has exact gap 1/2.

Barrier engagement and status. The results give unconditional lower bounds on seed length against restricted test classes and an algorithm for extracting a concrete low-degree distinguisher when support is too small. They do not yield a PRG for general circuits, a superlogarithmic circuit lower bound, or a new derandomization of BPP. The underlying linear-algebraic method and the relevant PRG lower bounds are established prior art; the only fresh contribution in this note is an explicit, resource-accounted restatement and a finite diagnostic script. Foundational target remains OPEN; this mission does not meet the requested significance threshold.
