PRE-LITERATURE DERIVATION (2026-10-10)

Target. Find a representation or preconditioner for nonsymmetric, nonnormal,
indefinite, or high-contrast Ax=b whose discovery cost is counted, and which
has a guarantee where ordinary GMRES does not. The concrete candidate here is
an exact graph lift. It is a strong robust baseline and a discriminating
mechanism; whether it meets the requested foundational novelty is OPEN.

Mechanism. For any square A, form the symmetric dilation

                 K = [ 0   A  ]
                     [ A*  0  ].

Solve K [u;v] = [b;0] with MINRES. If A is nonsingular, the exact solution is
[u;v]=[0; A^{-1}b]. The eigenvalues of K are plus/minus the singular values of
A. Thus the convergence geometry depends on singular values, not the location
or conditioning of eigenvectors of A. One K product costs one A and one A*
product, the state has dimension 2n, and there is no setup factorization or
probe-based discovery. This charges the representation honestly, but does not
remove high contrast in A.

Exact guarantee. Let alpha=sigma_min(A)>0, beta=sigma_max(A), and kappa=beta/alpha.
For m>=1, use the degree-2m polynomial

 p_{2m}(t) = T_m((2t^2-(beta^2+alpha^2))/(beta^2-alpha^2)) /
             T_m(-(beta^2+alpha^2)/(beta^2-alpha^2)),

where T_m is the Chebyshev polynomial. p(0)=1 and, on the spectrum of K,
|p(t)| <= 1/T_m((beta^2+alpha^2)/(beta^2-alpha^2))
<= 2 ((kappa-1)/(kappa+1))^m.
The MINRES residual is no worse than this polynomial residual. For the augmented
iterate z=[u;v], the original residual satisfies
||b-Av|| <= ||[b;0]-Kz||. Therefore an epsilon-relative residual is guaranteed
in at most 2m K-products when 2((kappa-1)/(kappa+1))^m <= epsilon. For kappa=1,
K has only eigenvalues +/-beta and the degree-2 polynomial 1-t^2/beta^2 solves
exactly. No eigenvector condition number or pseudospectral constant appears.

Discriminating family. Let P_n be the cyclic shift, P_n e_j=e_{j+1} for
j<n-1 and P_n e_{n-1}=e_0. Set
A_{n,gamma}=P_n + gamma e_{n-1}e_{n-1}*, b=e_0, 0<gamma<1.
For n>=3 and gamma nonzero this A is nonnormal. It is invertible (the added
last-column component is a multiple of the preceding column), and by Weyl's
singular-value inequality, 1-gamma <= sigma_min(A) <= sigma_max(A) <= 1+gamma.
Thus kappa <= (1+gamma)/(1-gamma), independent of n. Yet
A^j b=e_j for 0<=j<n, and for k<n, A span{e_0,...,e_{k-1}} is contained in
span{e_1,...,e_k}, orthogonal to b. GMRES's best residual is therefore exactly
||b|| for every k<n; it first becomes zero at k=n. For gamma=.05 the lift's
uniform condition bound gives a <=2*0.05^m residual factor after 2m iterations:
12 K-products suffice for < 1e-8, independent of n. This is a concrete regime
where a familiar Krylov method has a full n-step blind spot and the lifted
method has a dimension-independent guarantee.

Strongest falsifier / unresolved obstruction. If sigma_min(A) is tiny, the
lift has the same condition number kappa(A) and gives O(kappa log(1/epsilon))
iterations. It does not solve rare, high-contrast directions. A diagonal matrix
with entries 1,...,1,delta has kappa=1/delta; the generic bound diverges as
delta->0. Any claim of contrast-independent acceleration must discover and
charge a preconditioner for that direction. A deterministic q<n matvec-query
algorithm cannot guarantee an epsilon<1 solution for every unknown unitary
completion: adaptively answer each new query by an isometric image orthogonal to
b; after q<n queries, two unitary completions can map an unqueried unit vector
w to +b or -b. Both matrices give identical transcript and have condition 1,
while their exact solutions are +w and -w. The same lower bound embeds in a
bounded-condition nonnormal family C direct-sum U, with fixed nonsymmetric
2x2 shear C and b supported in the hidden U block. This rules out a generic
sublinear-query discovery miracle in a black-box product interface; it does
not rule out useful structure-specific preconditioners when the structure is
supplied and its acquisition cost is charged.

Input/resource interface. Matrix-free access to A x and A* x; right-hand side b;
no advice, no factorization, no hidden setup queries. MINRES retains O(n) vectors
with a constant larger than GMRES, and each step uses both operator directions.
The theorem uses the true alpha and beta but the MINRES recurrence does not need
them. If A is explicit, reading the representation and the cost of applying A
and A* must be charged; the lift itself is algebraic and has O(1) setup. For
sparse matrices, one iteration is O(nnz(A)); the lift preserves sparse action.

Claim boundary. The graph lift genuinely removes nonnormal eigenvector and
GMRES stagnation obstructions under a singular-value condition bound. It does
not remove ill-conditioning/high contrast, and the mechanism is likely known
as symmetric augmentation / normal-equation Krylov. No foundational novelty,
external validation, or mission completion is claimed. Next: literature audit
against primary sources, then execute the frozen discriminating test and decide
whether any nonstandard preconditioner extension survives.

POST-FREEZE PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT AND PIVOT

Literature comparison. The lift is not new. Paige and Saunders, “LSQR: An
Algorithm for Sparse Linear Equations and Sparse Least Squares,” ACM TOMS 8(1)
(1982), DOI 10.1145/355984.355989, derive LSQR from Lanczos applied to a
particular symmetric system; the original article states that the method solves
unsymmetric equations and least squares and uses A and A^T actions. Nachtigal,
Reddy, and Trefethen, “How Fast are Nonsymmetric Matrix Iterations?” SIAM J.
Matrix Anal. Appl. 13 (1992), DOI 10.1137/0613049, distinguish CGN's
singular-value-controlled convergence from GMRES's eigenvalue/pseudoeigenvalue
behavior. Greenbaum, Pták, and Strakoš, “Any Nonincreasing Convergence Curve is
Possible for GMRES,” SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 17(3) (1996), DOI
10.1137/S0895479894275030, prove arbitrary nonincreasing GMRES residual curves
are realizable even with any prescribed eigenvalues. These primary sources
establish the relevant prior art and the limitation of spectral intuition.

Exact equivalence and cost. Put S=AA*. Then K^2=diag(S,A*A), and, from
f=[b;0],
K^(2j)f=[S^j b;0], K^(2j+1)f=[0;A*S^j b].
At MINRES step 2m the Krylov space splits into u in K_m(S,b) and v in
A*K_m(S,b). The u component only contributes the second residual block -A*u,
so its minimizing value is zero. Writing v=A*y leaves exactly the problem
min_{y in K_m(S,b)} ||b-Sy||. Equivalently x=v belongs to
K_m(A*A,A*b) and minimizes ||b-Ax||: this is the LSQR residual-minimizing
iterate (and is analytically equivalent in exact arithmetic to CG on the normal
equations). The Chebyshev factor above is precisely the CG bound for S, whose
condition number is kappa(S)=kappa(A)^2, rewritten as
((sqrt(kappa(S))-1)/(sqrt(kappa(S))+1))^m=((kappa(A)-1)/(kappa(A)+1))^m.
Odd dilation steps add no better iterate for this right-hand side. Thus the lift
is a representation of LSQR/CGNE, not a distinct solver or discovery.

A K product costs one A action plus one A* action; 2m K iterations cost 2m of
each action, the same as m LSQR iterations. The implementation stores a
2n-vector state but MINRES/LSQR both use O(n) short-recurrence storage. There is
no setup probe cost in either method. Both require an adjoint action. If only a
forward matrix-free oracle is supplied, an adjoint implementation must be
acquired separately; if A is an explicit sparse matrix, transpose indexing or
transpose storage can cost O(nnz(A)) and must be charged. This cost matters for
nonlocal operators where an adjoint is not furnished by the forward simulator.

Correction to the frozen numerical illustration. For gamma=.05, the simple
uniform bound has q=.05, so 14 (not 12) K iterations guarantee a residual below
1e-8: 2(.05)^7=1.5625e-9. The executable NumPy check in
reports/h05_linear_algebra/test_lift.py observed 4 lifted residual-minimization
iterations for n=24,64,128, while original GMRES stayed at residual 1 through
n-1 and then terminated at n. This check confirms the example only; it does not
alter the literature verdict.

Pivot: hidden high-contrast modes and preconditioner acquisition. The useful
remaining question is whether a reusable all-right-hand-sides preconditioner
can be discovered from fewer than n black-box products without supplied
structure. Here is an exact deterministic oracle obstruction.

Theorem (transcript-preserving high-contrast obstruction). Let a deterministic
setup procedure query q<n vectors of an unknown SPD A in exact arithmetic,
then return an SPD preconditioner P for future arbitrary right-hand sides.
Run it on A0=I. Its adaptive query vectors span a proper subspace Q. Choose a
unit vector u orthogonal to Q and let A1=I+(H-1)uu*, H>1. For every query x in
Q, A1x=x=A0x, so the full transcript and returned P are identical. If the
procedure promised kappa(P^(-1/2) A_i P^(-1/2))<=C for both inputs, A0 forces
kappa(P)<=C. For A1, the generalized Rayleigh quotient along u gives
lambda_max(P^(-1/2)A1P^(-1/2)) >= H/lambda_max(P). A unit vector in u^perp
with P-Rayleigh quotient at least lambda_min(P) gives
lambda_min(P^(-1/2)A1P^(-1/2)) <= 1/lambda_min(P). Hence the preconditioned
condition number is at least H/kappa(P)>=H/C. Taking H>C^2 contradicts the
claimed same constant C. Therefore no deterministic q<n-product setup can
uniformly discover a constant-spectral-equivalence SPD preconditioner for this
black-box class at arbitrary contrast. The hidden projector uu* is generally
nonlocal in the supplied coordinates; A1 is nevertheless a valid SPD inverse
operator and differs from identity only in that unresolved direction.

This is a setup/preconditioner theorem, not a lower bound for solving one fixed
right-hand side. In exact arithmetic a generic random query exposes a rank-one
u, so the deterministic adversarial proof does not establish a randomized
minimax bound. A single hidden eigenvalue also has only two-point spectrum, so
unpreconditioned CG can solve it in two iterations; the theorem is about
uniform spectral equivalence, not that one CG instance. For larger hidden
subspaces, q actions expose at most q directions, but a separate randomized
query-complexity proof for approximate recovery remains OPEN. Known sparse PDE
structure, local coefficients, graph connectivity, or an available adjoint can
invalidate the black-box assumptions and may enable useful preconditioners;
those inputs and their processing costs must be specified before claiming
contrast robustness.

Verdict. The proposed lift is rejected as a discovery: it exactly repackages
LSQR/CGNE and does not remove contrast. The new exact result is a deliberately
narrow information barrier for reusable preconditioner discovery, with
important deterministic and oracle-model limits. No new high-contrast
preconditioner was found. The original foundational target remains OPEN; this
mission is not complete at the requested 8/10 significance threshold.
