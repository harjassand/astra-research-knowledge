S41 ORIGINAL INVENTION — THIRD-ORDER PHYSICAL-COORDINATE RECOVERY
2026-10-10. Self-derived, internally checked, not externally validated.

VERDICT

There is a substantive constructive mechanism beyond specialist uniqueness:
the physical quartic tensor need not be reconstructed in its ambient space.
Its observable contraction supplies an ordinary symmetric matrix whose
eigenvectors are the physical coordinates. This removes the n>=5 ambient
nullspace obstruction on a generic directly coupled physical class, using
H1 and H3 only. An explicit finite moderate-frequency version has O(n^3)
cubic queries and avoids an actual high-frequency experiment.

The original first-order model's broad practical target is nevertheless
OPEN. Its modal inversion can be exponentially conditioned, direct port
couplings can vanish, and low-amplitude cubic acquisition is expensive.
A second-order weakly damped oscillator extension has a conditional
polynomial end-to-end construction, detailed below. This is a restricted
technical result, not an established 8/10 landmark or a demonstrated
scalable physical instrument. Novelty and matched practical advantage are
not established. The oscillator model is a scope extension, not a change
silently made to the original target.

FIRST DERIVATION AND EXACT ORIGINAL-MODEL RESULT

The first derivation was frozen in s41_primitive/first_derivation_frozen.txt
before primary-source comparison. It begins from
  xdot=-Kx-diag(alpha)x^3+e1*u, y=x1,
where K is symmetric SPD. Heterogeneous unknown positive alpha is allowed.
Set q(s)=(sI+K)^-1 e1, k=K e1, H1(s)=q1(s). Define
  F_R(s,t)=-H3(c+R,c-R,t), c=(s-t)/2.
The output frequency is s. Then exactly
  F_R(s,t)=sum_r alpha_r q_r(s)q_r(t)q_r(c+R)q_r(c-R).
The known port factor is P_R=q1(c+R)q1(c-R). For hidden r,
  q_r(c+R)q_r(c-R)=k_r^2 R^-4+O(R^-6),
whereas P_R=-R^-2+O(R^-4). Thus, for known alpha1,
  B(s,t)=lim R^4 [F_R-alpha1*q1(s)q1(t)*P_R]
        =sum_(r>1) beta_r q_r(s)q_r(t), beta_r=alpha_r k_r^2.
Unknown alpha1 can be eliminated at finite R by J_R=F_R/P_R:
  -(4/3)R^2[J_R-J_(2R)]=B+O(R^-2).

Let K=U Lambda U^T, a=U^T e1, and choose spectral signs ai>0.
Distinct eigenvalues and nonzero overlaps allow linear identification of
Lambda and a_i^2. With v_i(s)=ai/(s+lambda_i),
  B(s,t)=v(s)^T A v(t),
  A=U^T diag(0,beta2,...,betan) U.
For n distinct low frequencies V=[v(s1),...,v(sn)] is Cauchy-invertible:
  A=V^-T [B(si,sj)] V^-1.
If all hidden beta_r are nonzero and pairwise distinct, diagonalizing A
recovers all rows of U up to hidden signed permutation. Its zero eigenvector
is the known port row a. Consequently K is recovered, and then
  alpha_r=beta_r/(U_r Lambda a)^2, r>1.
The port alpha is recovered from its leading coefficient or from H3 once
the hidden contributions are known. Homogeneous known alpha is a special
case. No quartic-tensor optimization or order-n nonlinear IO jet is used.

This proof also shows why the ambient linear nullspace is harmless to this
specific contraction: every ambient null tensor satisfying the physical
port contraction identity has zero contraction with Lambda*a twice. The
identity follows from the high-pair asymptotic and does not claim that the
entire ambient tensor is linearly observable.

FINITE MODERATE-FREQUENCY CONSTRUCTION FOR OSCILLATORS

Consider explicitly the different model
  qddot+gamma*qdot+Kq+diag(alpha)q^3=e1*u, y=q1,
with known scalar gamma>0, mass I, K symmetric SPD, n known. Its resolvent
is q(s)=(s^2+gamma*s+K)^-1e1 and the same cubic formula holds. The port
high-pair term starts at R^-4 and hidden terms at R^-8. The same A is
obtained by the order-eight port-subtracted limit. Actually measuring this
limit is unattractive: raw measurement error is amplified by Omega^8.

Precise conditional algorithmic claim: given n, upper/lower bounds m,M,
alpha_max, a_min, delta, b and the known gamma satisfying the conditions
below, every system in this class with eigenvalue gap >=delta, overlaps
>=a_min and hidden beta separation >=b (including zero) can be reconstructed
to any prescribed epsilon, up to hidden signed permutation, with probability
>=1-zeta under the stated independent noise model. The algorithm receives
real scalar drive/readout access, not K, eigenvectors, Lambda, a or H3.
It acquires the latter modal quantities and cubic samples as described
below. Total duration, samples, drive/frequency precision, arithmetic,
storage and inverse signal tolerances are polynomial in n,M,m^-1,
alpha_max,a_min^-1,delta^-1,b^-1,gamma^-1,epsilon^-1 and log(zeta^-1).
For a fully data-independent sufficient choice, use the KNOWN lower bound
Delta0=min(delta/(2sqrt(M)),sqrt(m)) and impose gamma<=Delta0/(400n).
Choose the calibration sample count from Delta0, not an unknown measured gap.
(Coordinator clarification after final report: actual-gap diagnostics can
use less conservative promises, but are separate from this uniform theorem.)
This is a self-audited candidate theorem, with conservative rather than
practically favorable costs. No physical experiment verifies its premises.

Instead, fix low imaginary s,t and c=(s-t)/2. As a rational function of R,
F_R has the known 4n poles
  z_l=p_j,+-c, p_j,--c, c-p_j,+, c-p_j,-,
  p_j,+/-=-gamma/2 +/- i*sqrt(lambda_j-gamma^2/4).
They are distinct for distinct lambda and positive gamma. Therefore
  F_R=sum_l r_l/(R-z_l),
and its R^-m coefficient is c_m=sum_l r_l*z_l^(m-1).
Choose 4n finite imaginary R_j=i*nu_j, form
  C_jl=1/(i*nu_j-z_l), C*r=F.
No tensor is estimated. Define the known scalar moments
  m1=sum ai^2 lambda_i, m2=sum ai^2 lambda_i^2,
  d=2c+gamma, e=c^2+gamma*c,
  p8=d^4-4d^2(e+m1)+3e^2+6m1*e+m1^2+2m2.
This p8 is the R^-8 coefficient of q1(c+R)q1(c-R). Exactly,
  c4=alpha1*q1(s)q1(t),
  c8=p8*c4+B(s,t),
  B(s,t)=[z^7-p8*z^3]^T C^-1 F.
Thus unknown alpha1 cancels linearly. This is the explicit desired-target
functional suggested by the coordinator, with O(n) rather than quartic-
dimensional interpolation per low-frequency pair. It uses 4n^3 complex
H3 values before symmetry savings; all input frequencies are O(sqrt(M)).

CONDITIONING PROOF FOR FINITE RESIDUE RECOVERY

Let m<=lambda_i<=M, gamma<=sqrt(m), and let Delta be the minimum spacing
among imaginary parts of the 2n single-oscillator poles. Thus Delta is at
least min(delta/(2sqrt(M)),sqrt(m)), where delta is the eigenvalue gap.
For this paragraph assume Delta>=400*n*gamma. Cluster the imaginary pole
centers whenever adjacent centers differ by less than h=100*n*gamma.
Any cluster has at most two poles, because same-side centers are Delta-
separated. A two-pole cluster necessarily contains one left and one right
pole, whose real parts are -gamma/2 and +gamma/2.

For a singleton sample its imaginary center. For a pair with imaginary
separation >=gamma, sample both centers. Its scaled Cauchy block has
diagonal entries +/-2 and off-diagonal magnitudes <=1/sqrt(1.25), hence
minimum singular value >1.1. For a closer pair sample the mean center
plus and minus gamma/2. Direct 2x2 Cauchy determinant evaluation gives
|det(gamma*C_block)|>=0.64 and norm<=4, so sigma_min>=0.16. A singleton
has singular value 2. Entries outside these blocks have magnitude at most
gamma/(h-gamma); their row and column sums are less than .041. Therefore
  sigma_min(gamma*C)>=.1, ||C^-1||2<=10*gamma.
All constants are deliberately conservative. The same construction can
be used at less weak damping, with sigma_min(C) checked from calibrated
linear data; the universal bound above then need not apply.

At low frequencies s_j=i*sqrt(lambda_j), put
  V_ij=ai/(lambda_i-lambda_j+i*gamma*sqrt(lambda_j)).
Scaling rows by 1/ai and columns by i*gamma*sqrt(lambda_j) gives I+E,
with ||E||2 <= (n-1)*gamma*sqrt(M)/delta. If this is <=1/2,
  ||V^-1||2<=2*gamma*sqrt(M)/a_min.
Alternatively |E_ij|<=(2/sqrt(3))*gamma/Delta, since
|lambda_i-lambda_j|=|omega_i-omega_j|*(omega_i+omega_j) and
sqrt(lambda_j)<=2omega_j/sqrt(3). Thus the Delta promise itself gives
||E||2<1/2 even when the displayed delta-based bound is loose. Hence uniform H3
error epsilon_H produces
  ||delta B||2 <= n*L_B*epsilon_H,
  ||delta A||2 <= n*||V^-1||2^2*L_B*epsilon_H,
where L_B=max_(s,t)||C^-T(z^7-p8*z^3)||1. It is directly computable from
linear calibration and bounded by C0*n*gamma*(1+M)^(7/2). Consequently
the target functional has polynomial gain; full quartic inversion is
neither needed nor asserted. The beta gap b (including separation from
zero) converts A error to coordinate/K error by ordinary symmetric-matrix
eigenvector perturbation, with a further polynomial factor O(M sqrt(n)/b).
Since beta>=b and alpha<=alpha_max, the nonzero port couplings obey
k_r^2>=b/alpha_max, so unknown alpha recovery also has polynomial gain.

FROM REAL INPUT/OUTPUT TO AN H3 VALUE: EXPLICIT QUERY INTERFACE

No H3 tensor/kernel oracle is supplied in the physical statement. For any
real frequencies omega1,omega2,omega3, command the single real waveform
  u_theta(t)=eta*sum_(j=1)^3 cos(omega_j*t+theta_j),
with independently programmed phase labels theta_j in {0,pi/2,pi,3pi/2}.
Run 64 experiments to a common known snapshot time T, then average outputs
with weight exp[-i(theta1+theta2+theta3)]/64. Its linear contribution is
zero and cubic contribution is exactly
  (3/4)*eta^3 H3(i*omega1,i*omega2,i*omega3)
  *exp[i*(omega1+omega2+omega3)*T].
At degree three, the selected phase character requires one positive-
phase factor from each programmed label, yielding 6*(eta/2)^3. This remains
valid for equal or conjugate frequencies: the 64 phase tuples command
legal resultant scalar waveforms, even when some tuples are redundant.
The identity uses controlled phase variables, not separation by an
infinite-time temporal Fourier transform. Fifth and higher orders cause
an O(eta^5) error, not zero error. Clock, phase and drive calibration must
be accurate to the resulting polynomial error tolerance.

Here is a conservative explicit small-signal resource bound. The causal
oscillator Green operator has position L-infinity gain
  G<=4/(gamma*sqrt(m)).
For bounded three-tone drive, choose eta with
  eta^2 <= 1/(432*alpha_max*G^3).
A contraction argument on the ball ||q||<=6G*eta gives a unique bounded
small response. The cubic Taylor remainder in position is at most
  23328*alpha_max^2*G^7*eta^5,
so after phase normalization the H3 error is at most
  31104*alpha_max^2*G^7*eta^2.
Choose eta^2 <= epsilon_H/(124416*alpha_max^2*G^7) to allocate at most a
quarter of the H3 error budget here. Variation-of-constants plus the same
small Lipschitz constant gives decay at least gamma/4; a conservative
settling time is
  T >= (4/gamma)*log[256G/(eta^2*epsilon_H)].
Waiting with u=0 for a comparable interval supplies an approximate reset;
hidden state resets are not an uncharged operation.

The displayed transient prefactor follows from the explicit Green matrix:
||G'(t)||<=2exp(-gamma*t/2), so steady velocity gain is <=4/gamma.
Total steady forcing is <=6eta on the contraction ball; hence
||q*||<=6G eta and ||v*||<=24eta/gamma. The homogeneous position mismatch
envelope is <=2||q*||+2||v*||/sqrt(m)<=24G eta. The small nonlinear
Lipschitz constant adds a factor at most two after weighting by
exp(gamma*t/4). This gives48G eta exp(-gamma*t/4), or normalized cubic
error <=64G eta^-2 exp(-gamma*t/4), proving the stated settling bound.

With independent zero-mean sub-Gaussian readout noise of scale sigma, repeat
each phase N times, where
  N = O(sigma^2*log(256*n^3/failure_probability)
        /(eta^6*epsilon_H^2)).
This is a sample bound, not a claim that real instrument noise is always
independent. Bounded adversarial noise instead needs hardware precision
O(eta^3*epsilon_H); averaging does not remove it. Total scalar snapshots
are 64*(4n^3)*N before calibration, duration O(T) per preparation/run,
bounded force <=3eta, and bandwidth O(sqrt(M)). All gains are polynomial
in inverse gaps, inverse damping, inverse overlap and target accuracy.
The polynomial can be practically prohibitive. No favorable realistic
sample count is claimed from the theorem alone.

LINEAR MODAL ACQUISITION IS ALSO CHARGED

Known H1/Lambda/a are not free. In the weakly damped model, a small known
short pulse followed by free ringdown gives, to O(pulse_amplitude^3),
  y(t)=sum_(j,+/-) d_j,+/- exp[(-gamma/2 +/- i*omega_j)t].
For pulse duration tau<=1/(10sqrt(M)), modal coefficient magnitude is at
least pulse_amplitude*tau*a_min^2/(4sqrt(M)). Use sampling step
h0=1/(4sqrt(M)) and L=ceil(16n/(h0*Delta0)). The undamped Vandermonde Gram has
diagonal L and off-diagonal <=4/(h0*Delta), so its singular values squared
lie in [L/2,3L/2]. If gamma*L*h0<=1, damping adds only a constant factor.
This is ensured by the conservative Delta promise. A rank-2n Hankel
matrix pencil therefore recovers poles and weights with polynomial
perturbation sensitivity. Explicitly collect 2L+1 samples y_j at j*h0,
form L-by-L H0=[y_(i+j)] and H1=[y_(i+j+1)] for i,j=0,...,L-1.
With z_l=exp(p_l*h0), V_jl=z_l^j, D=diag(d_l), Z=diag(z_l),
  H0=V D V^T, H1=V D Z V^T.
The smallest nonzero singular value of H0 is at least
L*min|d_l|/(2e). Rank-2n SVD projection to orthonormal column/row spaces
reduces H1*H0^-1 to T Z T^-1, where T is the projected V and
cond(T)<=sqrt(3e). Also |z_l-z_k|>=h0*Delta/2, since the sampled phases
lie in [-1/4,1/4] and damping is small. Hankel perturbation norm is at
most L times the maximum readout/model error; the rank gap controls
subspaces, the reduced pencil controls poles, and the displayed pole
gap controls eigenvectors. These are all polynomial factors in min|d|^-1,
max|d|, h0^-1 and Delta^-1. The principal log z/h0 recovers p without
aliasing, and a Vandermonde least-squares solve recovers the weights.
The ratio max|d|/min|d| is polynomial in M,m^-1,a_min^-1 for the known
short pulse. With the data-independent design, duration is O(n/Delta0),
sample count O(n sqrt(M)/Delta0). Shrinking pulse amplitude makes cubic contamination
small; repetitions buy readout precision under the same noise model.
Finite pulse edge/bandwidth approximation and mode-calibration errors are
charged. Conservative pole accuracy much smaller than gamma, e.g.
epsilon*gamma^2 divided by the remaining polynomial condition factors,
suffices for the residue construction. Known n and lower-overlap/model
promises are essential: finite noisy data cannot certify an absent dark
mode in an unrestricted larger system.

SPARSE-NETWORK EXTENSION AND ITS COST

The direct-port beta condition excludes hidden nodes not adjacent to the
port. At exact level this can be repaired without going beyond H3. Suppose
previously recovered physical nodes' cubic contributions have been removed.
If all remaining nodes are at graph distance >=d, then
  q_r(S)~(-1)^d (K^d e1)_r S^(-2d-2)
in the oscillator model. The residual R^(-4d-4) coefficient supplies
  A_d=U^T diag[alpha_r*(K^d e1)_r^2 on unresolved nodes] U.
Its positive simple eigenvalues resolve precisely the next distance layer;
the zero eigenspace is the future. From each recovered row U_r, compute
(K^d e1)_r=U_r Lambda^d a and hence alpha_r. Generic noncancelled walk
coefficients and distinct within-layer weights suffice for exact peeling.
All coefficients are moments of the SAME fitted 4n residues. A similar
first-order procedure uses order 2d+2.

This gives generic exact H3-only recovery of connected physical networks,
with a graph-distance-dependent conditioning cost. Residue weights include
z^(4d+3); attenuation of walk coefficients and error propagation between
layers can be exponential. For fixed maximum distance D, promises on walk
weights/gaps give polynomial resource bounds with D-dependent exponents.
No uniform polynomial guarantee for arbitrary sparse large networks, and
no general precision improvement from this exact extension, is asserted.

DISCRIMINATING COMPUTATION

Run:
  python3 work/high_conviction_04/reports/s41_primitive/check_recovery.py
  python3 work/high_conviction_04/reports/s41_primitive/check_residue_recovery.py
Scripts use numpy, known synthetic K/alpha only to evaluate kernel queries.
These are algebra/conditioning diagnostics, not physical IO experiments.
They preserve unfavorable cases.

1. First-order low-frequency V, lambda in [1,4] and ai=1/sqrt(n): condition
numbers n=4,8,16,32 are approximately 6.28e4,3.40e12,6.65e17,1.26e18.
Even supplied exact B in double precision fails badly by n=8. The oscillator
resonance V condition is approximately 2 under the weak-damping choice.

There is also a general obstruction for this particular first-order modal
inversion. Set lambda0=(m+M)/2, rho=(M-m)/(M+m)<1. For imaginary s,
expand1/(s+lambda) in powers of (lambda-lambda0)/(s+lambda0). Keeping
orders0,...,n-2 gives a rank-(n-1) approximation to any n-column V.
After each column is normalized to norm one, the remainder norm is at
most C*(M/m)*sqrt(n)*rho^(n-1). Hence its smallest singular value is
at most that quantity, and condition is at least its reciprocal. For
fixed bounded spectral ratio this grows exponentially. This is a lower
bound for resolving the scalar modal curve by this matrix, not a general
lower bound against all nonlinear experiments or all representations.

2. The explicit high-frequency method has O(Omega^-2) bias but loses badly
to tiny noise. For one n=8 instance, increasing Omega from 10 to 80 reduces
noiseless K error .229 to .0234, yet adding 1e-16 raw complex H3 noise at
Omega=80 raises K error to .858. This rules out claiming the asymptotic
formula alone as practical recovery.

3. Finite residue method uses 256,2048,16384 cubic queries for n=4,8,16,
all input frequencies <=3 in the [1,4] spectrum examples. Under extremely
weak damping, sigma_min(gamma*C) is approximately 1.999, but double rounding
and pole calibration in enormous resonant H3 values produce A errors around
1e-7 to5e-6. Extra arithmetic/experimental precision remains necessary.

4. At less conservative damping, n=8,gamma=.03 has cond(V)=2.51,
minimum sigma_min(gamma*C)=1.48, and K error 2.23e-11 from exact synthetic
kernel values. At n=16,gamma=.03 these are 4.87,.810,1.04e-10. At n=16,
gamma=.1 the residue condition deteriorates to3.26e-6 and K error2.27e-5.
This is a useful positive finite discriminant and an adverse conditioning
case. It is not an end-to-end acquisition experiment or matched advantage.

PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON (CHECKED AFTER FIRST DERIVATION)

Vanelli and Hendrickx, arXiv:2412.08472v4, 31 August 2026,
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08472v4
studies known-topology discrete-time nonlinear node networks, including
recurrent graphs, and generic local identifiability under minimal source/
sink excitation/measurement. Its Section VI explicitly distinguishes
identifiability from an identification algorithm and notes the fragility
of directly extracting high derivatives. It does not establish this
continuous-time symmetric cubic, unknown-topology, unknown-alpha,
third-order diagonal-contraction algorithm. The source is not just a
feedforward predecessor; earlier characterization that way was stale.

Bouvier, Helie and Roze, DAFx 2017,
https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2017/papers/DAFx17_paper_38.pdf
uses phase relationships to separate homogeneous Volterra orders and
demonstrates them in a simulated loudspeaker. Phase-based order separation
is prior art. Our 64-point phase snapshot is an explicit bounded-order
implementation with an O(eta^5) remainder, not a claimed invention of
phase cycling. Cubic response alone does not remove every higher order.

Boyd and Chua, IEEE TAC 1985,
https://stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/one_nonlinearity.html
studies IO uniqueness for systems with one memoryless nonlinearity,
including one-port networks, modulo transformations. This is an important
historical precursor, but a different class from one unknown cubic at
every physical coordinate. Older Volterra/nonlinear modal physical-
parameter identification is mature; exact prior-art clearance of the
observable contraction and moderate-residue construction remains OPEN.

STRONGEST FALSIFIERS / GATES STILL OPEN

* Exact structural recovery is not stable recovery of arbitrary networks.
  Near-dark modes, small port couplings, colliding beta values, broad damping,
  sparse large distance, and model mismatch all defeat the stated efficient
  promises. The method admits these failures instead of renaming them.
* Polynomial sample complexity may have very large exponents, particularly
  in gamma^-1, beta-gap^-1 and desired accuracy. A real finite physical
  experiment and comparison with direct nonlinear output-error fitting
  are mandatory before calling this broadly usable acquisition.
* Linear-modal calibration and fixed diagonal local cubic constitutive laws
  are substantive assumptions. Unknown masses, unknown nonproportional
  damping, unmodelled quadratic/cross terms and drift are not covered.
* The displayed finite tests use synthetic exact third-order coefficients;
  they do not validate the physical phase/reset implementation, novelty,
  or significance. Independent derivation checks by sibling agents are
  internal checking, not external scientific validation.

RESEARCH DECISION

Retain the observable-contraction and finite-residue primitive as a strong
specific result worth independent mathematical/prior-art review. Reject
the claim that the original broad practical acquisition obstruction has
already been removed. A conditional bounded-frequency polynomial algorithm
for symmetric weakly damped cubic networks is credible from the presented
construction; broad scalable one-port tomography and the campaign's
foundational 8/10+ capability target remain OPEN.
