H19 ALGEBRA — FROZEN FIRST DERIVATION (written before inspecting the Astra catalogue or literature)
Date: 2026-10-10

Candidate mechanism: valuation isolation by random exponent weights.

Input model for the lemma. Let
  f(x_1,...,x_n) = sum_{alpha in S} c_alpha x^alpha in Z[x_1,...,x_n]
be a nonzero polynomial in canonical collected form, with finite nonempty support S and c_alpha != 0. Assume coordinate degree bounds 0 <= alpha_i <= d_i are known. Set D=sum_i d_i. Choose an integer W>=0 and independently sample w_i uniformly from {0,...,W}. Evaluate at x_i=2^{w_i}. For every support exponent define the integer cost
  C_w(alpha) = v_2(c_alpha) + sum_i w_i alpha_i.
If the minimum of C_w over S is unique at alpha*, then
  v_2(f(2^{w_1},...,2^{w_n})) = C_w(alpha*)
and in particular the evaluated integer is nonzero. Proof: divide the sum by 2^{C_w(alpha*)}; the alpha* summand is odd, while all other summands are even.

Isolation bound (derived here). For any finite set S subset product_i {0,...,d_i} and any fixed offsets b_alpha (here b_alpha=v_2(c_alpha)), independent uniform weights in {0,...,W} give
  Pr[minimum of b_alpha + w dot alpha is not unique] <= D/(W+1).
To see this, condition on all weights except w_i. The candidates are finitely many affine functions of w_i, with integer slopes alpha_i in [0,d_i]. As w_i increases, the lower envelope can change active slope at most d_i times. At each tie between distinct active slopes there is at most one integer w_i. A non-unique minimum has two distinct vectors, hence they differ in at least one coordinate i; for that coordinate it occurs at one of at most d_i exceptional weights after conditioning. Union over i gives sum_i d_i/(W+1). Therefore choosing W+1 >= D/delta yields success probability at least 1-delta. (This proof does not depend on |S|, coefficient height, signs, or coefficient valuations.)

Consequent exact one-sided identity test for an explicitly evaluable integer polynomial: zero polynomials always return zero; nonzero polynomials return a nonzero integer with probability at least 1-delta. A nonzero output is a deterministic certificate of nonidentity. A zero output is inconclusive. This is a randomized exact-integer evaluation, not a finite-field test. The output additionally gives the minimum valuation cost, which is a linear optimization query on the support with the coefficient-valuation offset.

Bit/representation costs. Let L be the maximum bit length of an explicitly supplied coefficient, and let D=sum_i d_i. The sampled exponents satisfy w_i<=W, so every substituted variable has at most W+1 bits. Every monomial exponent in the resulting integer is at most W D. The exact output has at most O(L + W D + log |S|) bits. For W=ceil(D/delta), this is O(L + D^2/delta + log |S|). Straight sparse evaluation uses |S| terms and big-integer arithmetic on that many bits; a circuit evaluation has analogous intermediate-value bit costs and must also account for circuit size. Thus the method is polynomial in an explicit dense/unary-degree input when D, coefficient bits, and support size are polynomially bounded, but is pseudo-polynomial in binary-encoded degree. For a succinct arithmetic circuit, coefficient heights and degrees can have exponentially many bits relative to circuit description; this argument gives no polynomial-in-circuit-size bound. It does not presently compete with Schwartz–Zippel PIT on ordinary finite-field circuit models.

Minimal failure diagnostics. (1) The generic slogan “tropical weight gives the least exponent” is false when leading terms tie: f=x-1 and w=0 evaluates to zero. (2) The valuation offset is essential: f=2x-4 at p=2,w=1 has distinct exponent weights (1 and 0), but both summands have valuation 2 and cancel exactly. The correct objective is v_p(c_alpha)+w dot alpha, and the isolation guarantee must apply to this adjusted objective. These examples falsify a simpler uncorrected mechanism, not the lemma above.

Initial disposition before prior-art search: an exact, checkable representation and lemma exist, but major consequence is unproven. The mechanism may only repackage valuation/tropicalization plus randomized isolation and may be too expensive for succinct circuits. No novelty or landmark claim is frozen.

POST-FREEZE LITERATURE AND ADVERSARIAL ASSESSMENT (online primary-source check)

Exact foundational target considered. Deterministic polynomial-time identity testing for arbitrary commutative arithmetic circuits (leaves 0, 1, x_i; gates +, -, ×), under a succinct circuit encoding. This is a major algebraic-computation target, but the present mechanism does not solve it. For context, the 2024 ECCC paper “Launching Identity Testing into (Bounded) Space” states that general PIT derandomization remains unresolved and catalogs efficient PIT only for restricted circuit families. A 2026 STACS primary paper formulates integer-circuit PIT, notes that Schwartz–Zippel gives randomized polynomial time, and records the far-reaching consequences of a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm. These sources support `OPEN`; they do not validate the candidate as new.

Strongest baseline and overlap.

1. Schwartz (JACM 1980) and Zippel (EUROSAM 1979) give the classical randomized identity-testing baseline: evaluate at random points over a sufficiently large field and use the degree bound. This is efficient for circuit representations in the standard field-operation model and does not materialize an exponentially long integer. It is already a more efficient general randomized PIT interface than exact huge-integer evaluation for succinct circuits.

2. Mulmuley–Vazirani–Vazirani (STOC 1987), “Matching is as Easy as Matrix Inversion,” is a very close mechanism-level predecessor. They isolate a unique minimum-weight set, substitute powers of 2 for edge variables in a determinant, and use the 2-adic valuation to identify the minimum-weight matching. Their paper also states an extension allowing additive per-set offsets in the isolation objective. This already establishes the core pattern “isolate a unique lowest-weight term, then read it through a 2-adic leading term” for an important determinant polynomial with multilinear monomials.

3. Klivans–Spielman (STOC 2001), “Randomness Efficient Identity Testing of Multivariate Polynomials,” explicitly combines generalized isolation over bounded-exponent linear forms with polynomial identity testing. Arvind–Mukhopadhyay (2008), “Derandomizing the Isolation Lemma and Lower Bounds for Circuit Size,” states the concrete bounded-coordinate lemma: for any family of linear forms with coefficients in {0,...,K}, random weights in {0,...,2Kn} isolate the minimum with probability at least 1/2. It then substitutes x_i=y^{w_i} to obtain commutative PIT for polynomial-degree circuits. The random-weight bound and the support-isolation part of H19 therefore overlap directly with established prior art.

What survives the comparison. The frozen derivation packages a valuation-offset variant for general bounded nonnegative exponent vectors: its objective is v_2(c_alpha)+w·alpha, and the proof gives failure at most (sum_i d_i)/(W+1), independent of the number of support monomials. This is a clean extension of the lower-envelope proof, and the coefficient-valuation offset makes the integer-evaluation statement correct without a prime larger than the coefficients. But combining the known isolation mechanism with the known 2-adic leading-term mechanism is not, by itself, a new algebraic representation of landmark significance. I found no consequential theorem from this package beyond an alternate exact-integer identity test and a support-valuation query.

Falsification of a succinct-circuit consequence. Let D=2^s and f_s(x)=x^D. Repeated squaring gives an O(s)-gate circuit. With any sampled w>=1, the exact evaluation at x=2^w is 2^(wD), which needs wD+1 bits. Under the generic degree-only choice W=ceil(D/delta), this is at least D+1 bits on almost every draw; D is exponential in circuit size. Even a modular computation that certifies nonzero by reducing modulo 2^k must use k>wD for this example if it follows the valuation-bound route. The exact materialized evaluation thus has pseudo-polynomial degree cost and does not give polynomial-time PIT in circuit-description length. A compressed way to compute the valuation without paying that cost would be a different mechanism and is not provided here. The example is a cost falsifier for this direct representation, not a lower bound against all possible algorithms.

The two exact algebraic falsifiers in the frozen section also matter: x-1 at weight 0 defeats “unique exponent minimum” when weights tie; 2x-4 at weight 1 defeats ignoring coefficient valuations even though the exponent weights differ. Adjusting the cost by v_2(c_alpha) repairs these specific failures, as the lemma proves, but does not repair the succinctness cost.

Discriminating check. `h19_algebra/check_claims.py` exhaustively checked 5,988 small support/offset configurations, all weights in {0,...,8}^2, the stated probability inequality for d=(2,2), and the exact valuation identity whenever the adjusted minimum was unique. It also reproduces both counterexamples and the degree-2^12 bit-cost witness. This is an internal finite check of the written lemma and accounting only; the proof above is the mathematical justification, and the script is not external validation.

Cost interface, stated without hidden unit costs. For the explicit integer polynomial representation: coefficient bit length L, support list size m, coordinate degree bounds d_i, D=sum d_i, random weight range W+1 >= D/delta. Choose W+1 to be a power of two; then the weights use exactly n log2(W+1) random bits. The exact integer output is at most L + W D + ceil(log2 m)+O(1) bits. Sparse evaluation must perform m term evaluations and additions on integers of this length. Circuit evaluation must charge the maximum intermediate integer bit length; for general circuits this can be exponential in circuit size even when constants at the leaves are 0 and 1. If only the 2-adic residue is computed, a certified precision bound still depends on coefficient-height and degree bounds, which can be exponentially large for a succinct circuit. No training, oracle, physical-preparation, or external-data costs arise in this algebraic model.

Decisive obstacle and status. The mechanism does expose an exact leading valuation when a random bounded weight isolates one adjusted support vector. It does not remove the representation barrier between a succinct circuit and the bit length of its integer evaluation or a sufficient p-adic precision. It also leaves randomization in the weight choice. The target—deterministic polynomial-time PIT for general arithmetic circuits—remains OPEN. The candidate is a correct restricted lemma and a negative result about this direct route, not a major discovery.
