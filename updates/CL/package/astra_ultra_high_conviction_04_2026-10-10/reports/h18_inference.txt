H18 INFERENCE — FIRST DERIVATION (frozen before catalogue or literature review)
Date: 2026-10-10

Candidate target: learn a hidden dependence relation from finite observations when each experimental context has an arbitrary unknown increasing sensor calibration. The candidate representation is the within-context order/equality bit, acquired by pairing two channels on the same latent context. The claim is only about identifiability and query value under this explicit interface; it does not claim novelty or a general statistical breakthrough.

Model. Let H be either comonotone (+) or countermonotone (-). On each independent trial t, draw U_t iid Uniform(0,1). Under +, latent channel values are (X_t,Y_t)=(U_t,U_t); under -, they are (U_t,1-U_t). The instrument returns (Z_t,W_t)=(g_t(X_t),g_t(Y_t)), where each g_t is an arbitrary unknown strictly increasing function, shared by both channels on that trial and allowed to change across trials. No parametric calibration is supplied.

Representation. Retain B_t=1[Z_t<W_t], E_t=1[Z_t=W_t], and G_t=1[Z_t>W_t]. Strict monotonicity preserves all three relations. Thus under +, E_t=1 surely, while under -, E_t=0 surely with probability one (since U_t=1/2 has measure zero). One paired observation identifies H with zero error. The scalar marginals of X and Y are both Uniform(0,1) under both hypotheses. If the acquisition interface discards pair membership and provides only unpaired channel values, the two hypotheses induce exactly the same observed law; no number of observations can identify H.

Exact discrimination lemma. Under the stated model, the paired order/equality representation has zero Bayes error after one paired draw for equal prior probabilities, while the unpaired marginal representation has Bayes error 1/2 for every sample size. Proof: pointwise equality under + and almost-sure inequality under - give disjoint supports for E; each individual channel has Uniform law under either H, so the product law of unpaired draws is H-independent.

This is an exact acquisition discriminator, not yet a useful broad learner: it identifies a single binary copula family with noiseless matched contexts. Repeated independent context cost is one paired acquisition (= two channel readings plus context matching); memory is O(1) for the zero-error case. If each paired relation is independently flipped with probability eta<1/2, a majority of n pairs has error at most exp(-2n(1/2-eta)^2) by Hoeffding, and costs 2n channel readings; this noisy extension is a standard bound and is not the intended contribution. Context matching, pairing metadata, and maintaining a common calibration across the pair are indispensable resources and must be charged. If channel-specific calibrations are allowed, the equality bit is not invariant and this mechanism fails.

Frozen unresolved extension: can paired order acquisition be generalized into an adaptive, finite-cost strategy for identifying a sparse conditional-dependence graph over d channels, with unknown trial-specific shared monotone calibration, missing channels, and noisy order bits? A possible representation is an adaptively queried signed comparison graph; candidate elimination uses only within-trial comparisons, never calibrated magnitudes. No theorem for graph recovery is claimed here.

POST-FREEZE REFINEMENT: A HIGHER-ORDER PANEL DISCRIMINATOR

The first example is subsumed by the established copula/rank viewpoint, so I do not advance it as novel. A more informative exact test uses a 4-way parity interaction, where the acquisition unit is a synchronized panel from one latent context.

Let H=b in {0,1}. On each independent context draw X uniformly from
  P_b = Uniform{x in {0,1}^4 : x_1 xor x_2 xor x_3 xor x_4 = b}.
The instrument reports Z_i=g_t(X_i), with an unknown context-specific but channel-shared strictly increasing map g_t. The calibration may differ arbitrarily between contexts. A full panel gives the equality/order signature of the four reported values. If all four agree, X is either 0000 or 1111, both even, so H=0. Otherwise the two observed levels identify the zero and one groups because g_t is increasing; their parity gives H. Thus one complete 4-channel panel identifies b with zero error without estimating any g_t.

Every proper coordinate marginal is identical under P_0 and P_1. For any S subsetneq {1,2,3,4} and any fixed x_S, exactly 2^(3-|S|) completions have either specified parity, so P_b(X_S=x_S)=2^(-|S|). Hence observing at most three channels on each independent context yields exactly the same transcript law under both hypotheses, even if the choice of the next channel depends on previous observations. The last clause follows by induction: for every queried subset of size at most three, the conditional law of the next observed coordinate is the same under both hypotheses. This is an exact acquisition discriminator: synchronized four-way co-observation changes the task from non-identifiable under all lower-order panels to one-sample exact discrimination.

Noisy version and cost. Suppose independent bit-flip noise with rate eta<1/2 occurs before the common increasing map. The parity read from a full panel equals b xor the parity of four noise bits; its error probability is q_4=(1-(1-2 eta)^4)/2. From n independent complete panels, majority vote has error at most exp(-n(1-2 eta)^8/2), by Hoeffding. It therefore suffices to use n >= 2(1-2 eta)^(-8) ln(1/delta) panels for error at most delta. This bound is deliberately simple rather than optimal. Total variable acquisition is 4n sensor readings, n matched context identifiers, O(4n) bit/order processing, and O(log n) bits for the running parity count; there is no calibration fitting, pretrained resource, external oracle, or training phase. At eta=0, one panel costs four readings and one parity bit of working memory. As eta approaches 1/2, sample cost diverges as the model becomes uninformative. The ideal exact result depends on preserving same-context panel membership.

Exact lemma and proof. Under P_0/P_1 and the stated observation map, full-panel rank/equality signatures have disjoint supports: all-equal signatures occur only under P_0, and nonconstant signatures reveal the bit vector up to its known order labels and hence its parity. For every proper subset S, P_0(X_S)=P_1(X_S)=Uniform({0,1}^|S|). The map g_t is injective and increasing, so it preserves equality and the lower-versus-higher group in a nonconstant panel. These facts prove both claims. With no full panel and independent contexts, adaptive coordinate selection cannot change the equality of the transcript distributions.

FOUNDATIONAL TARGET AND ACTUAL RESULT

Exact target: determine when a finite observation protocol must capture a whole synchronized panel to identify an interaction that is invisible in all lower-order marginals, and construct adaptive panel acquisition policies that recover sparse higher-order structure under realistic missingness, sensor noise, and calibration drift at lower total measurement cost than full-panel collection.

Actual mathematics obtained: a complete finite 4-bit example, a proof that all proper panel marginals erase the target bit, a one-full-panel invariant decoder, and a transparent noise/cost bound. This is a concrete finite result, not an algorithm for general sparse interaction recovery. The 4-way parity family and its information-theoretic role are classical; the exact toy lemma is not being claimed as a new theorem or a major capability. The candidate's possible novel direction is the combination of context-varying common-calibration invariance with adaptive panel acquisition for sparse unknown interactions. No result has yet been obtained for that extension.

STRONGEST BASELINES / PRIOR ART (primary sources checked after freezing)

1. Hoff, "Extending the rank likelihood for semiparametric copula estimation," Annals of Applied Statistics 1(1), 2007, DOI 10.1214/07-AOAS107 (https://doi.org/10.1214/07-AOAS107). The extended rank likelihood makes inference about association parameters without specifying arbitrary marginal distributions. This is the strongest baseline against the first paired-order idea and establishes that nuisance-invariant rank-based association inference is established prior art.
2. Liu, Lafferty, Wasserman, "The Nonparanormal: Semiparametric Estimation of High Dimensional Undirected Graphs," JMLR 10, 2009 (https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/beta/papers/v10/liu09a.html). This gives semiparametric Gaussian-copula graph inference under unknown smooth monotone coordinate transformations. It is a strong estimator when its Gaussian-copula model applies, although its fixed coordinatewise transformations and graph assumptions differ from the row-varying common calibration and parity law here.
3. Vats, Nowak, Baraniuk, "Active Learning for Undirected Graphical Model Selection," AISTATS 2014 / PMLR 33 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v33/vats14b.html). It adapts future measured variable subsets to earlier graph estimates and proves measurement savings over passive acquisition for a specified family of Gaussian graphical models. This is the strongest direct alternative for adaptive panel allocation; it does not establish the present parity result because its target family and regularity assumptions differ.
4. Blum, Kalai, Wasserman, "Noise-Tolerant Learning, the Parity Problem, and the Statistical Query Model," STOC 2003 (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/2003-Noise-Tolerant_Learning-STOC.pdf). Noisy parity learning is a long-established problem. Its standard input is random examples labeled by a parity function, not the two parity-conditioned distributions used here, so it is context for the hard noisy extension rather than a direct match.

For the four-bit toy, the strongest baseline is simply observe all four bits in the same context and compute XOR; it matches the proposed invariant decoder exactly. If bit categories are directly available, the rank signature adds no value. For broader graph tasks, use an appropriate established rank-likelihood/nonparanormal estimator or the Vats et al. adaptive measurement policy when its assumptions apply. No head-to-head experiment or empirical advantage was established.

STRONGEST FALSIFIERS AND SCOPE

The central toy mechanism is true only because the four readings share one strictly increasing context calibration and remain joined to the same context. If each channel can instead have an arbitrary per-context offset, Z_i=X_i+C_{i,t}, then any observed row z can be explained under either parity by setting C_{i,t}=z_i-X_i; the data no longer identify b. This is an exact counterexample to relaxing the common-calibration assumption. Losing context IDs similarly reduces observations to marginal streams and makes the two laws identical. Independent bit noise makes the observed parity error q_4 approach 1/2 as eta approaches 1/2; there is no useful fixed-cost guarantee near that boundary. The cost grows exponentially in interaction order d for the direct parity signature under fixed eta, since its parity bias is (1-2 eta)^d. Thus the method does not solve the general finite-observation bottleneck, unknown sparse interaction support, or realistic high-order acquisition at scale.

For common-increasing calibration, within-panel order signatures are a representation choice, not new information relative to the complete raw panel: XOR is the competent baseline when the bits are accessible. The claimed gain is only over protocols that omit synchronized panel members or have unknown context-wise common calibration. The parity example demonstrates a precise interface boundary; it does not meet the campaign's requested 8/10 foundational-significance threshold.

Strongest proposed falsifier for any follow-up: on a preregistered family of sparse k-way interaction distributions with shared but drifting monotone calibration, compare (a) adaptive panel selection using rank/equality signatures, (b) the best passive acquisition with the same measurement and context-matching budget, and (c) a likelihood/Fourier or log-linear learner supplied the same candidate interaction family. Count all attempted readings, failed context matches, calibration overhead, memory, and compute. Freeze the hidden supports and noise before evaluation. If the adaptive policy cannot lower total acquisition cost at matched exact-recovery error over the competent model-aware baseline, or if modest independent channel drift makes the rank decoder non-identifiable, the proposed capability should be abandoned. This experiment has not been run.

RESOURCE INTERFACE

Required: ability to select or retain all four named channels from one shared latent trial; a stable context identifier; binary or resolvable two-level outputs; one common strictly increasing calibration within a trial; independent trial contexts; and either eta=0 or independent bit-flip noise eta<1/2. Supplied: channel names, panel definition, monotonicity, parity-family model, error-rate upper bound for the noisy guarantee. Acquired: four readings and their context ID per panel. Derived: one parity bit per full panel. Memory: O(log n) bits for the known two-model majority test, but O(number of tested candidate supports) or equivalent index structures for unknown sparse-support search. No oracle, preprocessing dataset, pretraining, external service, or training data is assumed. Input acquisition dominates the four-bit decoder. For unknown support among d coordinates the support-search and multiple-comparison costs are unaccounted for; no favorable claim is made.

DECISION

OPEN. The initial pairwise idea is known rank/copula inference. The frozen 4-way parity construction gives an exact capability discriminator under a strong but clear synchronized-measurement interface; it is a classical toy family and does not establish a new foundational statistical capability. The general adaptive sparse-interaction problem remains unsolved. No external validation, implementation comparison, or empirical evidence is claimed.
