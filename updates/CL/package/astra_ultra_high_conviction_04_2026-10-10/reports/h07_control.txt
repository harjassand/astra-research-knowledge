H07 CONTROL — FIRST DERIVATION (FROZEN BEFORE LITERATURE CHECK)
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: scoped exact capability; foundational mission OPEN.

MISSION AND INPUT/OUTPUT CONTRACT

Target: robust finite-horizon safety and existential reachability for large
interacting nonlinear systems, without a global state grid or supplied
Lyapunov function. Inputs are an exact discrete-time map
    x[t+1] = F(x[t], w[t]),
an initial set X0, a disturbance set W allowed independently at every step,
and a safety or target predicate. The system and disturbance dimensions may
grow with network size. This pass seeks a structural case in which all-state
reachability is decided by a number of trajectories independent of state
dimension.

FROZEN MECHANISM: SIGNED-ORDER EXTREMAL REDUCTION

Let S=diag(s_i), R=diag(r_j), with s_i,r_j in {-1,+1}, and define
z=Sx, v=Rw, G(z,v)=S F(Sz,Rv). Suppose on a forward-invariant product box
Z x V every partial derivative of G with respect to every state and
disturbance coordinate is nonnegative. (For differentiable F on a convex box,
this is a sufficient and, up to boundary regularity, standard test for
coordinatewise monotonicity.) Suppose the transformed initial set has an
attained coordinatewise maximum z0+ and minimum z0-, and V has attained
coordinatewise endpoints v+ and v-. Then for every admissible initial state
and every disturbance sequence, the trajectory obeys
    z-[t] <= z[t] <= z+[t]
coordinatewise, where z+[0]=z0+ and z+[t+1]=G(z+[t],v+), and similarly
for z-. Proof is induction using monotonicity of G in both arguments.

Consequences, each exact under the stated admissibility conditions:
1. If safe set K is downward closed in transformed order, all trajectories
   stay in K through horizon T iff the single upper-corner trajectory z+[t]
   stays in K for every t<=T. Necessity holds because that corner trajectory
   is itself admissible; sufficiency follows from z[t]<=z+[t].
2. If target U is upward closed, some admissible trajectory reaches U by T
   iff z+[t] reaches U by T. Any reaching trajectory is dominated by z+ at
   the same time; conversely z+ is admissible.
3. The dual statements use z- for upward-closed safety sets or downward-closed
   targets.

This is not a Lyapunov argument and proves neither asymptotic stability nor
infinite-horizon invariance by itself. A forward-invariant box/domain is part
of the certificate domain, or the map must be checked on each actually
reached box. A target/safety predicate is checked along all t in the finite
horizon; no interpolation is needed for discrete time.

DISCOVERY OF THE ORDER

If signs of partial derivatives are fixed over the box, form signed
constraints on a bipartite graph of state and disturbance coordinates:
sign(dF_i/dx_k)=s_i*s_k and sign(dF_i/dw_j)=s_i*r_j. A derivative with
uncertain sign fails this particular representation. For nonzero fixed-sign
edges, solve the parity constraints by graph traversal. They are feasible
iff every cycle has positive sign product. A self-edge must be positive.
Finding or refuting an orthant gauge takes O(n+m) time after sign certification
for n state/input vertices and m nonzero dependencies. Derivative sign
certification itself is an input cost and can be hard; this does not solve
global nonlinear optimization. The theorem assumes the sign facts are proved,
not sampled. When feasible, each endpoint trajectory costs T evaluations of
F; a sparse implementation costs O(T(n+m)) arithmetic per trajectory if F
has O(n+m) local work. Exact-arithmetic or validated numerical costs must be
charged separately.

UNSCOPED INPUTS AND COSTS

The exact reduction requires independent rectangular disturbances (or at
least attained componentwise extrema whose common corner is admissible), an
attained maximum/minimum in X0, and transformed monotonicity over the entire
reachable region. Correlated disturbance constraints can make v+ infeasible;
then endpoint simulation is an over-approximation only if a separately
validated upper comparison map is supplied, not an exact witness for
existential reachability. Online state observations, policy synthesis,
partial observations, continuous-time integration error, and learning F are
not included. If derivative signs are established via interval arithmetic,
symbolic methods, or formal proof, report that cost; finite probes do not
establish them. No accuracy guarantee is hidden inside trajectory evaluation.

EXACT DISCRIMINATING EXAMPLE

For F(x)=4x(1-x) on X0=[0,1], the upward-closed target U=[0.9,1] is reached
at t=1 from x0=1/2 (F=1), but the upper initial corner x0+=1 maps to 0.
Thus corner reduction fails when monotonicity is removed, even with a scalar
state and a monotone target. This distinguishes the theorem from a generic
state-grid-free reachability claim.

ORTHANT-SPECIFIC FALSIFIER

Consider the smooth three-node cycle, with 0<a<1 and
  x1' = -x1 + a*tanh(x2),
  x2' = -x2 + a*tanh(x3),
  x3' = -x3 - a*tanh(x1).
The three off-diagonal derivative signs have product -1 around the cycle.
Any orthant gauge would require s1*s2=+1, s2*s3=+1, s3*s1=-1; multiplying
gives 1=-1. Hence no coordinate sign flip makes this system cooperative.
Yet its Jacobian has infinity logarithmic norm at most -1+a<0 everywhere,
so it is globally contracting. This exact example shows the representation
misses some easy stable systems; it cannot be advertised as a general
stability discovery method. More general cones or non-monotone reachable-set
representations would be required to cover it.

STRONGEST BASELINE AND CLAIM STATUS (PRE-LITERATURE)

Strongest baseline: direct robust reachable-set over-approximation (interval,
polyhedral, zonotope, or other validated propagation), or a known monotone
systems theorem when the order is supplied. The candidate reduces exact
finite-horizon safety/target reachability to 1 (or 2 for dual objectives)
trajectories and can discover an orthant order from signed dependencies.
It removes dependence on global state-grid size under restrictive exact
monotonicity/order assumptions. It does not remove the cost of acquiring and
certifying the dynamics, verify arbitrary predicates, or establish stability.
Originality and priority are UNKNOWN. The broad foundational objective is
OPEN. The next gate is primary-source comparison and then a stronger attack on
unbalanced feedback or non-orthant orders.

PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON (AFTER FROZEN DERIVATION)

The central order mechanism is prior art, so it is not an original landmark.
Angeli and Sontag (2003), “Monotone Control Systems,” give orthant Jacobian
sign conditions, including the signed-incidence-graph criterion that an
orthant order exists when there are no negative loops. This subsumes the
proposed parity-gauge discovery step. Their source is
https://www.sontaglab.org/FTPDIR/angeli-sontag-monotone-TAC03.pdf .

The one-trajectory reachability mechanism is also prior art. Coogan (CDC
2020), “Mixed Monotonicity for Reachability and Safety in Dynamical Systems,”
uses a 2n-dimensional monotone embedding whose one trajectory encloses the
reachable set in a box, and treats control/disturbance inputs. Source:
https://coogan.ece.gatech.edu/papers/pdf/coogan2020mmtutorial.pdf . Abate,
Dutreix, and Coogan (2021), “Tight decomposition functions for continuous-time
mixed-monotone systems with disturbances,” give constructions for any
Lipschitz continuous-time vector field, at the cost of optimization in the
decomposition; source: https://coogan.ece.gatech.edu/papers/abate2020tightdecomp.html .
Sadraddini and Belta (2016) already study safety control for monotone systems
with bounded uncertainties: https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.07419 .

The strongest recent match found is Galarza-Jimenez, Zamani, and Jafarpour,
“Trajectory-based Safety of Monotone Systems: Verification and Control
Synthesis” (arXiv:2605.16660, submitted 2026-05-15). It constructs
trajectory-based dominance functions and robust/control barrier certificates
for unknown monotone discrete-time systems from trajectory data, with known
Lipschitz bounds in the disturbance case. Its target goes beyond the frozen
one-corner check on certificate construction and unknown-system access, but
still assumes monotonicity and is not a universal arbitrary-system solution.
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16660 .

The frozen contribution is therefore only a concise task-specific synthesis
of known monotone-order reachability and signed-graph balance. No new theorem
of foundational scope is claimed. Strongest baseline is mixed-monotonicity
reachability; strongest counterexample remains the stable, unbalanced cycle.

POST-LITERATURE ATTACK: BLACK-BOX QUERY LOWER BOUND

The need for structural information has an exact one-step obstruction.
Proposition: Let X=[0,1]^n, X0=X, unsafe target
U={y in X : y_1=1}, and let the unknown transition map F:X->X be
L-Lipschitz in Euclidean norm. A deterministic verifier adaptively queries F
at m points. If all observed answers are zero and the query set Q has fill distance
h(Q)=sup_{x in X} min_{q in Q} ||x-q||_2 >= 1/L, choose a maximizer x* and
define
    F0(x)=(0,...,0),
    F1(x)=(max(0, 1-L||x-x*||_2), 0,...,0).
Both maps are L-Lipschitz and agree at every query, but F0 is safe while
F1(x*)=(1,0,...,0) reaches U in one step from the allowed initial state x*. Thus no
sound verifier can certify safety from that transcript. Because m Euclidean
balls of radius h must cover the unit cube, 1 <= m v_n h^n, where v_n is
unit-ball volume. Therefore any uniform guarantee requires query count on
the order of at least L^n/v_n (with constants and boundary effects),
exponential in state dimension for fixed L>1. This is an information lower
bound, not a computation lower bound for explicit formula input. Exact local
structure such as monotonicity can bypass it; finite black-box sampling
alone cannot. The claim is for deterministic point queries and the stated
Lipschitz class; it does not cover richer model oracles, randomized error
criteria, or structural priors.

This proposition does not discover a new certificate representation, but
it narrows the mission: any broad scalable success must exploit and verify
structure, and must charge its acquisition/certification cost. OPEN remains
the original foundational goal: a discoverable, sound representation that
handles important unbalanced/non-monotone interactions without returning to
dimension-exponential sampling or a supplied Lyapunov function.
