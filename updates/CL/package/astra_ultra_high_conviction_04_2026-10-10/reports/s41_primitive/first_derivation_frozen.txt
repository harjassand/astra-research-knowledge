FIRST DERIVATION — frozen before source comparison, 2026-10-10

For xdot=-Kx-diag(alpha)x^3+e1 u, K symmetric SPD, put
q(s)=(sI+K)^-1 e1. The cubic Volterra coefficient is
H3(s1,s2,s3)=-sum_r alpha_r q_r(s1+s2+s3) q_r(s1)q_r(s2)q_r(s3).
The linear transfer H1(s)=q_1(s) is directly observed.

Take two arbitrary low complex frequencies s,t and c=(s-t)/2. The
three input frequencies R+c, -R+c, t have output frequency s.
Let F_R(s,t)=-H3(R+c,-R+c,t).
The port contribution is alpha_1 q_1(s)q_1(t)q_1(R+c)q_1(-R+c).
It is fully known if alpha_1 is known. If alpha_1 is unknown, its
coefficient is recovered first because q_1(R+c)q_1(-R+c)=-R^-2+O(R^-4):
lim R^2 F_R(s,t)=-alpha_1 q_1(s)q_1(t).

For r>1, q_r(z)=-k_r z^-2+O(z^-3), k=K e1. Symmetry of the pair
in R makes the error even, so q_r(R+c)q_r(-R+c)=k_r^2 R^-4+O(R^-6).
After subtracting the known port contribution,
B(s,t)=lim R^4 [F_R(s,t)-port term]
      =sum_(r>1) alpha_r k_r^2 q_r(s)q_r(t).

Write K=U Lambda U^T and a=U^T e1. Linear response gives Lambda and
a_i^2; choose ai>0. Then
B(s,t)=v(s)^T A v(t), v_i(s)=a_i/(s+lambda_i),
A=U^T diag(0,alpha_2 k_2^2,...,alpha_n k_n^2) U.
Evaluate B at n distinct low frequencies. The resulting Cauchy matrix
V is invertible, so A=V^-T Bmat V^-1. If all hidden beta_r=alpha_r k_r^2
are nonzero and pairwise distinct, diagonalizing A gives all rows of U
up to hidden signed permutation. The zero row is the known a. This
recovers K. Unknown positive alpha_r are then beta_r/k_r^2.

Thus the physical quartic tensor is exactly recoverable from H3 alone,
for every n satisfying the nondegeneracy conditions, despite the ambient
linear nullspace. It needs O(n^2) independent bilinear low-frequency
measurements, a constant-order asymptotic coefficient (order four),
matrix inversion, and eigendecomposition. It does not require order-n
Lie jets. This is an arithmetic/identifiability result until precision
and acquisition are charged.

Practical difficulty: the first-order resolvent basis V can be severely
conditioned even when lambda gaps and port overlaps are polynomial.
Sparse networks have zero k_r for all non-neighbors, so the matrix only
resolves the first layer. After removing resolved row contributions,
an analogous higher coefficient peels graph-distance layers, at a cost
that can grow exponentially in distance.

SECOND-ORDER EXTENSION (prompted by coordinator after the first mechanism)
For qddot+gamma qdot+Kq+diag(alpha)q^3=e1 u, replace q(s) by
(s^2+gamma s+K)^-1e1. Port q_1(z)~z^-2 and hidden q_r(z)~-k_r z^-4.
Hence alpha_1 comes from order four, and the same B(s,t) comes from the
port-subtracted order-eight limit. Balanced +/-R again gives O(R^-2)
relative asymptotic error, with bounded c,gamma,K.

At s_j=i sqrt(lambda_j), the modal matrix is
V_ij=a_i/(lambda_i-lambda_j+i gamma sqrt(lambda_j)).
Scale its columns by i gamma sqrt(lambda_j), and rows by 1/a_i.
The result is I+E, where |E_ij|<=gamma sqrt(M)/delta off diagonal,
M=max lambda and delta=min distinct lambda gap. If
(n-1) gamma sqrt(M)/delta <= 1/2,
then ||E||_2<=1/2 and the scaled inverse has norm at most two.
This removes the Cauchy inverse obstruction under explicitly weak
damping and separated resonances. Damping, overlap, beta gaps, modal
calibration, bandwidth, drive amplitude, noise and duration still need
a full end-to-end resource statement and numerical discriminant.
