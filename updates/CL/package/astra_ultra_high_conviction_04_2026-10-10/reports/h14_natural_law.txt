H14 — NATURAL LAW / INFERENCE–DYNAMICS AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: OPEN. No foundational discovery or major-consequence result established.

Target

Test whether there is a universal physical principle that bounds information about a system's dynamics, extractable from its trajectory, by the system's thermodynamic entropy production alone. This would connect inference, dynamics, and physical law without fitting a new law to observations.

Frozen independent derivation

The original derivation was frozen before the literature search in [first_derivation.txt](h14_natural_law/first_derivation.txt). It considers a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain with rates q_ij(theta)=e^theta q_ij. For a fully observed path over [0,T], the likelihood ratio is

  log(dP_theta/dP_0) = theta N_T - (e^theta-1) A_T,

where N_T is the number of jumps and A_T is the integrated escape rate for the base generator along the path. The score is the compensated jump-count martingale, giving the exact path Fisher information

  I_path(theta) = E_theta[N_T].

Any recorded statistic Y of the path has I_Y(theta) <= I_path(theta), by conditional expectation of the score. Thus resolved kinetic information is controlled by activity. It is not controlled by entropy production alone.

Finite-resolution, directly testable counterexample

Take a two-state system {0,1} with q_01=q_10=r=k e^theta. Its stationary distribution is uniform and detailed balance holds for every theta. The steady entropy-production rate is exactly zero. Suppose the accessible observable is only the state sampled at t_m=m Delta, with a known sampling interval Delta. The sampled transition matrix has

  p := Pr(X_{m+1} != X_m) = (1-exp(-2x))/2,   x := r Delta.

The sample-to-sample flip indicators are iid Bernoulli(p). From n intervals the Fisher information for the dimensionless log-rate theta is therefore

  I_sample(theta) = n (partial_theta p)^2/[p(1-p)]
                  = 4 n x^2/(exp(4x)-1) > 0.

For any two distinct finite rates r and r0, the sampled path KL divergence is

  D(P_r || P_r0) = n[p_r log(p_r/p_r0)
                     +(1-p_r) log((1-p_r)/(1-p_r0))] > 0,

while both systems have zero entropy production. Hence even finite-resolution state samples identify a reversible kinetic law from its relaxation time, provided the observer has a calibrated clock. As x grows, the sample information tends to zero because multiple hidden jumps erase timing and parity; relative to the fully resolved path, I_sample/(n x)=4x/(exp(4x)-1). This makes the resolution limitation explicit rather than assuming an ideal jump detector.

Dimensional and thermodynamic checks

- theta is dimensionless; k has units 1/time and Delta is a supplied time standard. Estimating theta also assumes k is a known reference rate. Without a calibrated clock or known k, only the dimensionless product r Delta (equivalently k exp(theta) Delta) is identifiable, not an absolute rate or theta separately.
- Changing the common barrier-controlled rate leaves the equilibrium probabilities and local detailed balance ratio unchanged. It is a kinetic perturbation, not a sustained thermodynamic drive. Every fixed-theta run has zero steady entropy production.
- The KL divergence here measures distinguishability between candidate path laws; it is not thermodynamic entropy production and does not indicate an arrow of time.
- The result concerns information present in a supplied physical record. It does not claim that a real observer can acquire, store, or erase that record without cost. Sensor work, clock calibration, sampling bandwidth, state preparation, and memory reset are outside the bound. Any total-cost question must specify these resources.

Primary-source prior-art audit

The strongest direct baseline found is Liu and Gu, “Dynamical activity universally bounds precision of response in Markovian nonequilibrium systems,” Communications Physics 8, 62 (2025), [journal article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-01982-w), [arXiv:2410.20800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20800). Their Methods give the path Fisher-information formula

  I(theta) = integral dt sum_{i!=j,nu} W_ij^nu p_j (partial_theta log W_ij^nu)^2,

and bound it by a rate-sensitivity factor times expected dynamical activity. For the uniform rescaling above, the log-rate derivative is one, so their formula immediately gives I=E[N_T]. Their Example 1 parameterizes rates by a symmetric kinetic-barrier parameter theta, separately from the antisymmetric driving parameter alpha; setting alpha=0 gives unbiased, zero-drive kinetics. The activity/Fisher mechanism is therefore prior art, and the present result is a direct specialization plus a finite-sampling calculation, not a new principle.

A second relevant primary source is Nicoletti, Di Terlizzi, and Busiello, “Balancing Information and Dissipation with Partially Observed Fluctuating Signals,” Physical Review Letters 137, 127101 (2026), [journal article](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/8gx2-rbns), [arXiv:2512.04877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04877). It studies a specified sensor/signaling coupling and proposes a model-dependent way to balance information and dissipation with finite-time and finite-resolution observations. This is consistent with the no-go scope: task-specific tradeoffs can hold after the sensing interface and resources are fixed; no universal bound from specimen entropy production alone follows.

Strongest baseline and falsifier

The standard thermodynamic uncertainty relation bounds precision of certain currents using dissipation under its assumptions. The stronger general Markov-jump path-Fisher/activity result above covers arbitrary trajectory observables and directly supplies the relevant inference geometry. The exact two-state family is the falsifier of any proposed inequality of the form “trajectory information about a kinetic law is at most a universal function of the system's entropy production” when that function vanishes at zero production. The finite-sample KL is strictly positive at zero production, and the path KL can grow without bound with observation duration.

What was obtained and what was not

Obtained: an exact no-go counterexample to dissipation-only limits on trajectory inference, an activity-based Fisher identity, and an explicit finite-sampling prediction with a dimensionless crossover x=r Delta. The prediction is operational: measure sampled occupancy at known intervals, estimate the flip probability, and compare its rate information to the formula above.

Not obtained: a new law, a new bound beyond the 2025 activity result, or a bound for the full thermodynamic cost of an embodied observer. The essential mechanism is a symmetric change in kinetic rates, which preserves equilibrium while changing correlations and transition timing. This exposes why dissipation alone misses kinetic information, but it does not overcome the main obstacle of specifying the observer's acquisition and memory costs. The numerical significance is low (about 2/10 as a novel result; useful as a scope falsifier only). The foundational target remains OPEN.
