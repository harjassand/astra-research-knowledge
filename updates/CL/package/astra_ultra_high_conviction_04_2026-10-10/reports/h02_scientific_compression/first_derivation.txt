FIRST DERIVATION (frozen before literature/catalogue comparison)
2026-10-10

Question. Can a trajectory-only learner replace a long fine-scale run by an
observable-level closure for a broad class of multiscale systems, without a
uniform mixing/closure promise?

Exact adversary: unresolved communicating classes.
Let Z_s be a continuous-time symmetric two-state chain on {+1,-1}; in fast
time s it flips at rate gamma from either state. Start at +1. The frozen model
gamma=0 is also admitted. Let the requested output be the horizon-averaged
observable

    A_H = H^{-1} integral_0^H Z_s ds.

For gamma=0, A_H=1 exactly. For gamma>0, E[Z_s]=exp(-2 gamma s), hence

    E[A_H] = (1-exp(-2 gamma H))/(2 gamma H).

Choose H=gamma^{-2}. Then E[A_H] tends to 0 as gamma tends to 0, while the
frozen model's value remains 1. Yet a training trajectory of fast-time length
B is exactly the all-+ path under gamma=0 and has the same path under gamma>0
with probability exp(-gamma B). The total-variation distance between the two
training-path laws is therefore 1-exp(-gamma B). For equal prior probabilities
any test has error at least exp(-gamma B)/2. Thus, for gamma B <= log 2, no
trajectory-only procedure can reliably distinguish closures whose target
outputs differ by nearly one. This is an exact acquisition obstruction, not
just a failure of a chosen estimator.

Multiscale embedding. In physical time t, take dZ_t to have flip rate
gamma/epsilon and requested observable X_T/T, where dX_t/dt=Z_t. Then the
fast-time horizon is H=T/epsilon. Selecting epsilon=T gamma^2 gives H=gamma^-2.
The expensive fine trajectory resolves O(H) fast-time evolution, while a
short frozen-x burst of length B misses the rare transition with probability
exp(-gamma B). A representation that replaces the hidden mode by its
stationary average 0 is excellent after mixing and wrong by order one in the
frozen model. The hidden communicating-class identity is precisely the
closure variable that has not been acquired.

What this does and does not establish. It rules out a uniform guarantee over
trajectory-oracle multiscale systems if the interface supplies neither a
lower bound on the relevant mixing gap nor a way to inspect/prepare the full
fast generator. It does not rule out compression on a promised uniformly
mixing family, or when exact generator access reveals gamma. It also does not
rule out using O(1/gamma) fast-time data; that is the required acquisition
cost in this example.

Candidate representation after imposing a checkable/promised gap. For a
requested observable g, retain the projected reward and its relaxation
certificate, rather than a generic state reconstruction: its stationary
mean mu_x(g), an upper bound on the integrated correlation tail, and the
mixing/initial-layer error. If the fast process has known gap >= lambda and
mixing bound C exp(-lambda s), then an initial fast burst of length
S >= lambda^-1 log(C/eta) suppresses initialization bias to eta. For a
uniformly mixing Markov fast process, empirical reward estimates have cost
depending on lambda^-1 and requested error, not epsilon^-1. Interpolating the
projected closure over a slow-state grid of spacing h costs O(h^-d) burst
sites in d slow dimensions (or an adaptive number only with additional
regularity/certificate assumptions). Integrating the coarse system then
avoids the O(T/epsilon) fine-time evolution. This is a conditional mechanism;
the rare-transition example says its gap premise cannot be silently inferred
from a short no-transition record.

Cost ledger to preserve: fast-burst duration and reset/preparation; number of
slow-state sites; requested observable count; precision and confidence
repetitions; gap/mixing assumptions or generator queries; coarse integration;
and a validation holdout. A short trajectory alone cannot certify an unseen
rare transition. A deterministic homogenization ansatz, finite Koopman basis,
or fitted Mori-Zwanzig memory kernel does not remove this specific acquisition
ambiguity unless it imports structure/data that distinguishes gamma=0 from
gamma>0.
