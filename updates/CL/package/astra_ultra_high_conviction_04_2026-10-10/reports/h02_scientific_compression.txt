H02 | Scientific compression with acquired observable closure
2026-10-10

Verdict: OPEN. I did not find a new broad simulation representation that
removes the closure/acquisition obstacle. I derived an exact trajectory-only
lower bound: smooth multiscale systems can agree on every sublinear observed
prefix and still have a constant difference in a requested long-horizon
observable. In a finite-state rare-mode example, distinguishing the correct
closure requires horizon-scale data when no uniform mixing gap is supplied.
Classical averaging, HMM/equation-free methods, and projected response models
do give conditional compression when their closure, lift, mixing, or memory
information is supplied or acquired at the required cost.

Target and interface

The target is a compact predictive representation that advances requested
slow observables of nonlinear fast-slow dynamics with error delta using
asymptotically less fine-trajectory work than direct simulation. The exact
lower bounds below use a black-box trajectory interface: one may restart only
from the declared initial state, observe state trajectories over intervals
whose total fast-time exposure is B, and query no off-trajectory vector-field
values, generator entries, source code, or future jump times. The requested
output is either a normalized terminal observable or an ensemble-mean time
average, as specified in each example. All restarts and repeated records are
charged to B. A solver that receives the RHS or the generator has a stronger
interface, which these lower bounds do not cover.

Exact smooth nonlinear discriminator

The file deterministic_falsifier.txt gives the full construction. A hidden
slow phase r advances once over [0,T]. Two C-infinity systems have identical
trajectories until T/2. In one, the requested coordinate x remains zero; in
the other, a smooth response activates during the second half, while its
velocity also oscillates at fast scale epsilon. For the explicit bump chi,
the normalized outputs at T differ by more than 0.061, despite exact agreement
of every state observed before T/2. Therefore any predictor based only on a
prefix ending before T/2 has worst-case error greater than 0.030. A fixed-step
trajectory integrator resolving the oscillatory velocity costs O(T/epsilon)
over the target horizon, while reaching the first discriminating prefix costs
at least a constant fraction of that work. The omitted phase is exactly a
hidden slow closure variable.

The conclusion is scoped to trajectory-only acquisition over a class that
allows hidden slow modes. If source equations are supplied, the two systems
are distinguishable by source inspection; charge that access and any
compiler. If a uniform mixing/closure promise excludes slow hidden modes, the
counterexample is excluded.

Exact rare-mode statistical lower bound

Let Z_s be a symmetric two-state continuous-time Markov chain on {+1,-1},
starting at +1 and flipping at rate gamma in fast time. Compare gamma=0 with
gamma>0. The target is m_gamma(H)=E[H^-1 integral_0^H Z_s ds]. Since
E[Z_s]=exp(-2 gamma s),

  m_0(H)=1,
  m_gamma(H)=(1-exp(-2 gamma H))/(2 gamma H).

Choose gamma=1/H. Then the target values differ by
Delta=(1+exp(-2))/2 > 0.567. Over a trajectory record of length B, the
gamma>0 chain has no flip with probability exp(-gamma B), giving the same
observed path as gamma=0. Thus the path-law total-variation distance is
exactly 1-exp(-gamma B). Le Cam's two-point bound gives minimum equal-prior
testing error at least exp(-gamma B)/2. If an estimator predicts the target
mean to absolute error delta<Delta/2, its estimate identifies which of the
two disjoint target intervals is correct. Hence its worst-case failure
probability is at least exp(-B/H)/2. For confidence 1-alpha with alpha<1/2,
one needs B >= H*log(1/(2 alpha)); uniform high-confidence acquisition is
horizon-scale. The proof and bounded numerical formula checks are in
checker.py. These checks verify this exact calculation only.

This chain is also a diagnostic for the promised-gap case. If gamma>=lambda
is known, probability of seeing no transition in a burst of length
B=lambda^-1 log(1/alpha) is at most alpha. When H is much larger than
lambda^-1, this gives sublinear acquisition. But a short no-transition path
cannot itself certify that an unknown gamma is above lambda.

Conditional representation and costs

A useful representation for a requested observable is its projected response
law: its stationary mean together with the observable-specific relaxation
rates or memory kernel and a bound on the unresolved tail. In the two-state
example this reduces exactly to (m_infinity=0, relaxation rate 2 gamma), and
computing the long-horizon mean then costs O(1). That is genuine compression
once gamma and the correct communicating class are known. Acquiring them is
the bottleneck: a record with no flips is consistent with gamma=0 and with
gamma about 1/H. A finite-dimensional response fit does not remove this
identifiability cost.

With an explicit uniform mixing contract, a burst can burn in for
lambda^-1 log(C/eta) fast-time units, then estimate the observable-specific
mean. For a slow-state grid with M sites and R replicates per site, the
micro-acquisition cost is at least represented as M*R times (burn-in plus
measurement length), with slow-state resets/lifts charged. Interpolation,
coarse-solver, and averaging errors must be added to the final observable
error. Savings require this total to be o(T/epsilon); high slow dimension,
poor conditioning, uncertain gap, and precision requirements can erase the
gain. No new uniform error theorem for that algorithm is claimed here.

Strongest baselines and adversarial comparison

Homogenization replaces fast forcing by coefficients averaged under a frozen
fast invariant law. Averaging theorems supply a limit under structural
conditions; the acquisition question remains how to obtain that law and
control the initial layer. The rare-mode construction violates any uniform
gap promise; the smooth construction hides an omitted slow phase. A
homogenization claim that assumes these modes away is valid within its scope,
but does not solve blind closure acquisition for the whole target class.

Heterogeneous multiscale methods and equation-free computation already use
short, appropriately initialized microbursts to estimate unavailable coarse
equations and advance macro variables. This is the closest computational
baseline and captures the conditional mechanism above. It presumes a usable
coarse variables/lifting/restriction interface and a microburst long enough
to estimate the local closure. In the rare-mode case, a short burst can miss
the only transition; in the smooth case, all trajectories before T/2 agree.
The exact examples therefore expose their acquisition boundary rather than
refute their promised-regime results.

Mori-Zwanzig gives an exact projected equation with memory and a fluctuating
term. For the two-state mode, the hidden response relaxes as exp(-2 gamma s),
so a memory truncation short compared with 1/gamma misses the relevant mode.
The formal identity is exact but does not provide the kernel or certify its
tail from a short record. Acquiring a horizon-scale low-frequency feature
remains necessary without a gap promise.

Koopman theory yields exact finite linear closure only when the selected
observable span is invariant. The two-state chain has a small exact operator
if gamma is known; discovering its rate from trajectory-only observations
still faces the testing bound. A learned finite dictionary adds a feature
selection and conditioning problem; it does not turn a prefix that is
identical on both systems into identifying data.

Multilevel Monte Carlo reduces the cost of estimating expectations when
coupled levels have suitable weak/strong error and variance decay. It still
uses fine-level paths and addresses sampling variance, not whether an
unobserved generator has a rare transition or a smooth future activation.
The present lower bound is about information in the simulator interface,
not about variance reduction under a known model.

Evidence, limitations, and status

The exact bump and two-state calculations are internal derivations; the
Python checker ran successfully and confirms the stated finite examples and
closed-form testing bound. No physical simulation, external empirical test,
proof assistant, or independent expert audit was performed. The stochastic
bound is exact for the stated trajectory oracle. The smooth ODE pair is exact
for a C-infinity trajectory-only interface and an explicit target-horizon
family; it is not an impossibility result for supplied source code or
uniformly mixing systems. An event-driven simulator that reveals transition
rates/times beyond the queried window is a stronger oracle and must be charged
as such.

The strongest falsifier of a proposed universal short-burst closure is the
exact indistinguishable pair: its requested outputs differ by a fixed amount,
while every allowed pre-horizon observation is identical. It proves that a
universal representation cannot be acquired from sublinear trajectory
prefixes over this class. It does not prove that conditional compression is
unhelpful. The founding objective remains OPEN: no new representation and
end-to-end speedup over a matched HMM/homogenization/Mori-Zwanzig/Koopman or
multilevel baseline was obtained.
