**I did not establish the requested matching reliability law.** The strongest completed result is a theorem characterizing the full quantum process near the broadcasting success ceiling, together with an exact counterexample that invalidates one candidate route to a stronger converse. Neither closes the depolarizing reliability gap, and I am not treating them as the requested breakthrough.

## 1. Completed theorem: near-ceiling success forces exclusive routing

The new theorem describes **where the preserved quantum information must be**, rather than counting physical memory.

Consider a complete channel with a \(q\)-dimensional input, \(K\) output receivers, and a classical success flag for each receiver. Its normalized Choi state is \(\omega\). Suppose every receiver has success probability \(p>0\) and, conditional on success, Bell fidelity at least \(1-\varepsilon\). The flags may be arbitrarily correlated.

An *exclusive uniform routing channel* sends the intact input to exactly one receiver, chosen uniformly:
\[
\mathcal R(\rho)=
\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^{K}
\rho_{B_j}\otimes\theta_j^{B_{\ne j}}
\otimes |e_j\rangle\langle e_j|.
\]
Here \(e_j\) marks only receiver \(j\) as successful. Each \(\theta_j\) is independent of the input; it may contain correlations among the other receivers.

### Routing-rigidity theorem

If
\[
Kp\ge1-\delta,\qquad
0\le\delta<1,\qquad
0\le\varepsilon\le\frac1{20},
\]
then there exists such a routing channel satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\omega-\omega_{\mathcal R}\|_1
\le
\delta+2\sqrt{\varepsilon}+12\varepsilon.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The bound does **not** grow with \(K\). For the complete channels themselves,
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\mathcal B-\mathcal R\|_\diamond
\le
q\bigl(\delta+2\sqrt{\varepsilon}+12\varepsilon\bigr).
}
\tag{2}
\]

At \(\delta=\varepsilon=0\), the full extension is exactly an exclusive uniform router. Near that endpoint, almost all its weight lies in histories with one successful receiver, a nearly perfect Bell pair at that receiver, and the remaining output nearly independent of the logical input.

The full argument is in the RIGIDITY_AT_THE_BROADCAST_CEILING.md[routing-rigidity manuscript](sandbox:/mnt/data/astra_reliability_rigidity/RIGIDITY_AT_THE_BROADCAST_CEILING.md). It does not depend on the earlier CP mapping-cone theorem.

### Application to the depolarizing memory

The preserved complete-instrument construction supplies \(K=4^N\) receivers for the \(N\)-round memory: the established four-output universal cloner has the required depolarizing marginals, and its extensions can be composed while retaining all measurement outcomes. The cloner is existing mathematics, not a new construction here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9804001)

Consequently, a code satisfying
\[
p\ge(1-\delta)4^{-N},
\qquad F\ge1-\varepsilon
\]
must induce a mathematical broadcast extension close to exclusive routing, with error bounded by (1)—**without a hidden \(4^N\) factor**.

This is a converse statement. It does not give the real memory user access to the extra receivers or the environment, nor does it construct a code realizing the routing process.

## 2. Proof: retain the complete success distribution

The proof’s useful feature is that it controls correlated postselection directly.

Let \(P_j\) be the Bell projector on the reference and receiver \(j\), tensored with identities elsewhere. For any subset of \(s\) receivers,
\[
\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{s}P_j\right\|_\infty
\le \frac{s+q-1}{q}.
\tag{3}
\]

To verify this, let \(V_j\) insert a normalized Bell vector into those two registers. Then
\[
P_j=V_jV_j^\dagger,\qquad
V_j^\dagger V_j=I,\qquad
V_i^\dagger V_j=\frac1qU_{ij}\quad(i\ne j),
\]
where \(U_{ij}\) relabels the remaining tensor factors. The block Gram matrix therefore has operator norm at most \(1+(s-1)/q\).

### Too many simultaneous successes consume infidelity

Let \(S\) count the successful receivers in a complete flag pattern. Define the total *unnormalized* successful infidelity
\[
e=
\sum_h\sum_{j\text{ successful in }h}
\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(I-P_j)\omega_h\bigr].
\]
Marginal fidelity gives
\[
e\le Kp\varepsilon.
\]

Set \(c=q/(q-1)\le2\). Applying (3) within each complete flag pattern yields
\[
\mathbb E[(S-1)_+]\le ce.
\tag{4}
\]
Since
\[
\mathbb E[S]
=\Pr(S\ge1)+\mathbb E[(S-1)_+]
=Kp,
\]
we obtain
\[
\Pr(S\ne1)\le\delta+2ce,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E[S\,\mathbf1_{\{S\ge2\}}]\le2ce.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus near-ceiling success controls both kinds of unwanted histories: none succeeding and several succeeding.

### Projecting the unique-success sectors exposes the routing structure

Let \(\omega_j\) be the subnormalized state in the sector where only receiver \(j\) succeeds. Project it onto its Bell pair:
\[
\sigma_j=P_j\omega_jP_j
=\Phi_{RB_j}\otimes\eta_j\otimes|e_j\rangle\langle e_j|.
\]
The product form follows because the Bell projector has rank one on \(RB_j\).

Write
\[
a_j=\operatorname{tr}\sigma_j,\qquad
a=\sum_j a_j.
\]
Then
\[
a_j\le p,\qquad
Kp-a\le(2c+1)e.
\tag{7}
\]

A subnormalized gentle-projection estimate gives
\[
\|\omega_{\mathrm{unique}}-\textstyle\sum_j\sigma_j\|_1
\le2\sqrt e.
\]
Including the bad flag sectors and normalization therefore gives
\[
\frac12\left\|\omega-\frac{\sum_j\sigma_j}{a}\right\|_1
\le
\delta+\sqrt e+\left(2c+\frac12\right)e.
\tag{8}
\]

The normalized projected state already routes perfectly, but with weights \(a_j/a\). Replacing these by \(1/K\) costs at most
\[
\frac12\sum_j\left|\frac{a_j}{a}-\frac1K\right|
\le\frac{Kp-a}{a}
\le\frac{(2c+1)e}{a}.
\tag{9}
\]

Finally, the inherited broadcasting inequality implies
\[
Kp\le\frac1{1-c\varepsilon},
\]
while (7) gives
\[
a\ge Kp\bigl(1-(2c+1)\varepsilon\bigr).
\]
Substitute these into (8)–(9). For \(\varepsilon\le1/20\), the resulting expression is bounded by
\[
\delta+2\sqrt\varepsilon+12\varepsilon,
\]
proving (1). The fixed-input-dimensional Choi-to-diamond inequality supplies (2).

The \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\) orders cannot generally be removed. A small coherent phase rotation after ideal routing has Bell infidelity \(\varepsilon\) but trace distance \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) from ideal routing. Independently aborting with probability \(\delta\) changes the success-pattern distribution by exactly \(\delta\).

## 3. Why this still does not determine the exponent

The theorem characterizes **near-prefactor-ceiling** codes, which is stronger than achieving a particular leading exponent.

A hypothetical law
\[
p_N=\frac{4^{-N}}{N}
\]
would satisfy
\[
-\frac1N\log p_N\longrightarrow\log4,
\]
but
\[
4^Np_N=\frac1N\longrightarrow0.
\]
It would therefore have exponent \(\log4\) without approaching the routing ceiling required by (1). **No achievability of this hypothetical sequence is asserted.**

There is also an exact limitation on what broadcasting alone can prove. Over abstract complete \(K\)-receiver channels, write \(x=pF\). The upper boundary
\[
x\le p,\qquad
qx-p\le\frac{q-1}{K}
\tag{10}
\]
is attainable: below \(p=1/K\), use ideal routing followed by independent aborting; above it, mix routing with an all-success optimal-overlap symmetric channel.

Those abstract channels need not be realizable by the prescribed physical depolarizing noise. Their existence shows that **the retained broadcasting property alone cannot imply an extra logarithmic penalty in \(-\log p\)**. A decisive converse must exploit additional structure of the encoded tensor noise.

Distributed monogamy, heralded extraction and erasure-channel simulation already have close prior art, particularly Ahmed–Smith’s work. The routing-stability theorem’s historical originality remains unverified; it is not being presented as the discovery of a new no-cloning principle. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08591v2)

## 4. Exact falsification of a candidate stronger converse

A promising approach was to combine the small probability of two receivers both recovering the qubit with a lower bound on their joint acceptance. The most direct proposed lower bound,
\[
\Pr(\text{both accept})
\ge
\Pr(\text{first accepts})\Pr(\text{second accepts}),
\]
is false for entangled block inputs and tests.

Take two physical input qubits in a Bell state. Apply the four-output universal cloner separately to each qubit. Two receivers each collect one clone from each physical input and test their two-qubit bundle with the Bell projector \(M\).

Each marginal acceptance probability is
\[
p=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
M\,\Lambda_{1/2}^{\otimes2}(M)
\right]=\frac7{16}.
\]
Direct calculation gives
\[
\Pr(\text{both accept})=\frac{17}{96},
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\text{both accept})-p^2
=-\frac{11}{768}<0.
}
\tag{11}
\]

For completeness, the joint-test effect on the two physical inputs is
\[
K_{\rm test}
=\frac{I_4}{12}
+\frac{X\otimes X-Y\otimes Y+Z\otimes Z}{32}.
\]
Its Bell expectation is \(17/96\).

The verifier independently reconstructs the symmetric-projector cloner and traces its outputs before evaluating this example. It also verifies the Pauli calculation.

This is **not** a high-fidelity memory code. It rejects the specific universal positive-correlation premise; it does not refute every possible higher-power or fidelity-sensitive collision inequality.

## 5. An exact endpoint reduction, with the missing conversion exposed

I also established a probability-preserving reduction that removes ambiguity about input-dependent successful operations.

Let \(T_s\) be any allowed successful logical-qubit subchannel, with physical success \(p\) and Bell fidelity \(F\). Apply a uniformly random single-qubit Clifford before encoding, retain only its classical label, and undo it after successful decoding. Averaging over the label gives exactly
\[
\boxed{
\overline T_s=p\Lambda_\lambda,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{4F-1}{3}.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Both \(p\) and \(F\) are preserved. The successful effect becomes \(pI\), so success is genuinely input independent.

Replacing failure by an orthogonal erasure flag gives a full channel whose distance from ideal erasure with transmission probability \(p\) is
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\mathcal E_{p,\lambda}-\mathcal E_{p,1}\|_\diamond
=p(1-F).
}
\tag{13}
\]
This follows exactly from the Pauli probability differences; the triangle bound is attained on a Bell input.

This changes only the endpoint description. It does **not** require decoding to a logical qubit between rounds, restrict accepted syndromes, or remove adaptive quantum memories.

Consequently, the unassisted one-round conversion remains a precise unresolved subproblem. Define
\[
s(\eta)=
\sup\{p:\text{one exposed layer, arbitrary finite width, accepted error}\le\eta\}.
\]
The limit
\[
s_0=\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}s(\eta)\in[0,1/4]
\]
exists, but I did not determine it.

Concatenation supplies the valid inequality
\[
p_{\rm opt}(N,\varepsilon)
\ge s(\varepsilon/N)^N.
\]
Thus \(s_0=1/4\) would suffice for leading exponent \(\log4\). That value is not proved. Conversely, even proving \(s_0=0\) would not establish an adaptive \(N\log N\) converse: its decay rate and protocols retaining larger intermediate quantum codes would still require analysis.

Existing exact results for **entanglement-assisted or nonsignalling-assisted** postselected communication cannot fill this gap, because those resources are excluded here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02583)
