## 1. The all-copy target was attacked directly

On \(H_n=(\mathbb C^3)^{\otimes n}\), define
\[
q_n(C)=
\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}
\left(-\frac12\right)^{|S|}
\left\|\operatorname{Tr}_S C\right\|_F^2.
\]

The foundational endpoint is
\[
\boxed{
q_n(C)\ge0
\quad\text{for every }n\ge1
\text{ and every complex matrix }C\text{ of rank at most two}.
}
\]

This would establish all-copy undistillability of the negative-partial-transpose Werner state
\[
\rho_*=\frac{2I_9-F_3}{15},
\]
and therefore establish NPT bound entanglement. The connection uses the standard rank-two distillation criterion, not a new reduction invented in this continuation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02647v1)

I compared the attempted argument with the recent two-copy result and the three-copy Plücker inequalities. The latter cover positive semidefinite and normal rank-two operators at three copies, with additional restricted nonnormal cases. Those statements do not supply the unrestricted all-copy inequality. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21367v2)

### The apparent negative witnesses were false signals

The numerical search optimized over rank-two projections at three through six copies. It produced values near
\[
-4.7\times10^{-15}
\]
at four, five and six copies.

Those values required exact verification.

For each stored two-column matrix, I converted every real and imaginary floating-point entry **exactly** into Gaussian-integer columns \(V\) with a common denominator. The orthogonal projection onto precisely that stored subspace is
\[
P=V(V^*V)^{-1}V^*.
\]

Writing
\[
d=\det(V^*V),\qquad
N=V\,\operatorname{adj}(V^*V)V^*,
\]
gives \(P=N/d\), and therefore
\[
q_n(P)=
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}
(-1)^{|S|}2^{n-|S|}
\left\|\operatorname{Tr}_S N\right\|_F^2
}{
2^n d^2
}.
\]
The numerator and denominator are integers. Their signs require no tolerance, floating-point logarithm, or inferred convergence.

The results were:

| Copies | Exact sign | Decimal display of the exact rational value |
|---|---|---:|
| \(4\) | Positive | \(1.8714166203730534\times10^{-19}\) |
| \(5\) | Positive | \(2.0220234922151658\times10^{-21}\) |
| \(6\) | Positive | \(6.6152047468041424\times10^{-16}\) |

I also checked the **raw operators \(UU^*\)** separately, without replacing them by projections or assuming that the stored columns were exactly orthonormal. All three raw operators are positive semidefinite of rank two, and their exact \(q_n\)-values are also strictly positive.

**Neither interpretation of these candidates gives a counterexample.**

There are genuine zero projections at every copy count:
\[
P=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)\otimes |w\rangle\langle w|.
\]
Indeed, the form factorizes across this tensor product and
\[
q_1(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0))=2-\frac12(2)^2=0.
\]
Thus a search reaching values indistinguishable from zero is not evidence of a negative witness.

## 2. A proposed all-copy proof fails an exact test

I then tested whether the known inequalities could control the all-copy expression through subsystem purities.

For a normalized rank-two projection state \(\rho=P/2\), let
\[
p_k=
\binom nk^{-1}
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\\|S|=k}}
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_S^2).
\]
Then \(p_0=1\), \(p_n=\tfrac12\), and
\[
q_n(P)=
4\sum_{k=0}^{n}
\binom nk
\left(-\frac12\right)^{n-k}p_k.
\]

At four copies, consider the rational vector
\[
\boxed{
(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)
=
\left(1,\frac7{16},\frac38,\frac12,\frac12\right).
}
\]

An exact rational checker verifies that it satisfies **all 53 margins in the specified relaxation**. These include physical and purifying-qubit Pauli-sector nonnegativity, dimension bounds, the implemented two-copy shadow inequalities, linear-entropy subadditivity constraints, and the inequalities obtained by grouping the four sites into three factors and applying the positive rank-two three-copy result. The grouped inequality is an input from the existing Plücker paper. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02647v1)

Nevertheless, its target value is
\[
\begin{aligned}
4\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom4k
\left(-\frac12\right)^{4-k}p_k
&=
4p_4-8p_3+6p_2-2p_1+\frac14p_0\\
&=
2-4+\frac94-\frac78+\frac14\\
&=\boxed{-\frac38}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This establishes a precise limitation:

> **The 53 specified constraints cannot, by themselves, imply the required four-copy nonnegativity.**

It does **not** establish that this purity vector comes from a quantum state. No corresponding state was found. Consequently, it is not a negative Werner witness and not a distillability result.

The missing information is the compatibility of the marginals with **one actual global rank-two operator**. Treating compatible-looking marginal statistics as independently realizable would manufacture a false breakthrough.

Targeted optimization toward this profile, exact tests of sampled qutrit graph-state codes, and boundary-curvature tests around zero projections did not remove that obstruction.

## 3. The Fourier investigation also retained its unrestricted endpoint

For a real Boolean function \(f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}\), the target remains
\[
\boxed{
\sum_S\widehat f(S)^2
\log_2\frac1{\widehat f(S)^2}
\le
C\sum_S |S|\widehat f(S)^2
}
\]
with one constant \(C\) independent of the function and dimension. The recent paper examined in this continuation establishes further restricted cases, not this universal conclusion. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00246v2)

I worked with the **actual entropy increment under coordinate restriction**, rather than replacing it by absolute Fourier products. That distinction matters because Astra already contains a recursive counterexample to the stronger absolute-product coordinate criterion; it is not a counterexample to Fourier entropy–influence itself. 

Writing
\[
A_T=\widehat f(T),\qquad B_T=\widehat f(T\cup\{i\}),
\]
and \(e(x)=-x\log_2x\), the exact increment is
\[
\Delta_i(f)=
\sum_T\left[
e(A_T^2)+e(B_T^2)
-\frac12e((A_T+B_T)^2)
-\frac12e((A_T-B_T)^2)
\right].
\]

A dimension-free choice of an essential coordinate satisfying
\[
\Delta_i(f)\le C I_i(f)
\]
would close the usual induction. **I did not prove that choice exists.** Exhaustive checks through four variables and larger random tests do not establish it.

A broader phase-based relaxation fails outright. For
\[
F(x)=
\prod_{j=1}^{n}
\left(\sqrt{1-\epsilon}+i\sqrt{\epsilon}\,x_j\right),
\]
one has \(|F(x)|=1\), but
\[
H(F)=n h_2(\epsilon),\qquad I(F)=n\epsilon,
\]
so \(H(F)/I(F)\to\infty\) as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). This is **not Boolean** and hence is not an FEI counterexample. It shows why normalization and unit modulus alone cannot support the proposed proof: the real Boolean constraint must do indispensable work.
