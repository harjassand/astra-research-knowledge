# Crossed-plaquette execution surface: finite candidate transition table

**Status (16 October 2026).** A modified finite controller with a proved clean-history invariant, one-onsite-register-fault exact-control repair, and a two-counter-fault bounded quantum-debt theorem. The latter still does not give a recursive damage theorem: an explicit three-fault trap survives. Protected waiting and the hierarchy remain open. None of these assertions transfers to the original `AsyncR` of arXiv:2601.20818v3.

## Geometry, registers, and predicates

Work on an even square torus of side at least six for the local proof. Let `P_0(v)` and `P_1(v)` be the unique 2×2 even and odd brickwork plaquettes containing site `v`. They intersect only at `v`. The union of their supports is a seven-site crossed plaquette in a 3×3 box. A physical site carries quantum data `Q_v`, execution residue `c_v ∈ Z_q` for **even q ≥ 8**, static two-bit coordinate color `χ_v=(x_v mod 2,y_v mod 2)`, and two local copies `π_{v,p}` of the finite gate word for its incident parity-`p` tile. All four sites of each tile start with identical copies of its word. The word has period dividing `q`; pad `q` if needed. The integer `h_v` is a proof-only count of successfully completed quantum gate events; it is never a physical register. This fixed, preloaded program is a one-level model, not yet a self-simulating mutable program.

For a plaquette `P` of parity `p∈{0,1}`, define `Valid_p(P)` as follows. Its four counter residues are either all equal, or all lie in `{a,a+1 mod q}` for one residue `a` with `a mod 2 ≠ p`. (For q≥8 this `a` is unique when the residues differ.) Define `StarValid(v)=Valid_0(P_0(v)) ∧ Valid_1(P_1(v))`. A gate tests `StarValid(v)` for **all four** participants; hence its counter read support fits in their incident-tile union, a 4×4 box. Its color guard checks every nearest-neighbor affine offset in that entire 4×4 box and that the candidate anchor color is `00` (even tile) or `11` (odd tile). The jumps are placed at every translated 2×2 anchor; the color guards choose the two intended brickwork layers. A single altered color fails every affected 4×4 pattern check and cannot enable a wrong translated tile: the other three tile vertices already force the anchor's true parity. A counter repair at `v` reads its seven-site crossed plaquette and requires a valid 3×3 color pattern.

`Candidate(v,z)` means that replacing `c_v` by residue `z`, with the six neighboring counter residues fixed, makes *both* incident plaquettes valid. `Unique(v)` means exactly one residue satisfies `Candidate`. A repair is allowed to change only `c_v`; it never touches quantum data or any completion flag. Counter progress is inseparable from the quantum gate jump below. No independent timing clock may select a quantum gate label or change `c_v`.

For a tile `P`, its **moat** `N_2(P)` consists of tiles at overlap-graph distance at most two from `P`; it fits in a 6×6 box. `Bulk(P)` is enabled if `P` is invalid, no site of `P` has an effective `R`, and there exists a field differing at no more than two sites of `P` in which all moat tiles are valid. The fixed jump searches the finite alphabet, then takes the minimum-Hamming, lexicographically first such assignment. It writes only those at most two counters. It does not infer the true past from quantum data, apply an inverse gate, or purge data. The chosen assignment can represent one omitted or repeated tile gate, and that is explicitly charged as quantum debt.

## Ideal rate-one local jumps

| Type | Guard read before the jump | Atomic action on the same jump | Data support and control support |
|---|---|---|---|
| `G(P)` for every translated 2×2 plaquette | Correct affine color pattern on entire 4×4 star and correct anchor color; all four `c_v=k` with `k mod 2=p(P)`; `StarValid(v)` for every `v∈P`; at least three of four tile program words equal `π_P`, which selects `K_{P,k}` | Apply prescribed local CPTP `K_{P,k}` on four quantum registers **and** set all four `c_v←k+1 mod q` in the same jump | Four data sites; 4×4 classical read box |
| `R(v)` | Valid 3×3 color pattern; `Unique(v)` with unique value `z≠c_v` | Set `c_v←z`, dephasing only classical controls; leave all `Q` untouched | Seven counter sites in a 3×3 box; one counter write |
| `B(P)` | `Bulk(P)` and valid color pattern throughout `N_2(P)` | Write deterministic selected assignment on at most two sites of `P`; leave all `Q` untouched | At most two counter writes in `P`; 6×6 classical read box |
| `C(v)` for static color | Both north and east neighbors infer the same `χ_v` by subtracting their affine checkerboard offsets, and this differs from `χ_v` | Write their common inferred value to `χ_v`; leave `Q,c,π` untouched | Three classical color sites |
| `A(v,p)` for a copied program word | Correct tile colors; the other three copies on `P_p(v)` agree on a word different from `π_{v,p}` | Set `π_{v,p}` to that word; leave `Q,c,χ` untouched | Four sites of one tile |

Each guarded attempt is a CPTP channel on all classical sectors: dephase the finite classical guard registers, apply `K_{P,k}` in accepted sectors and identity to data otherwise, then write the specified classical sector. A Kraus sum over sectors gives trace preservation and a CP lift with any untouched reference. The jump family is time independent and translationally repeated; the color and program pattern lives in the initialized state. Every jump has fixed support and a finite alphabet once `q`, the one-level gate word, program alphabet, and local quantum dimension are fixed. A physical onsite unitary noise jump may act arbitrarily on `Q_v,c_v,χ_v,π_{v,0},π_{v,1}`. The one-fault **control** theorem covers all these classical registers; its quantum conclusion still charges the local data action of that onsite jump.

The `G` rule is the only **commit**. Its four counter writes are the completion certificate for all four shared quantum wires. There are no request, acknowledge, done, release, or reset flags to recycle. Reuse occurs by `c mod q` only after actual gate jumps, so clean wrap does not create an old-message ABA problem. No claim about stale program/control buffers under faults follows from this observation.

## Clean causal invariant and one-site repair target

Begin with every `h_v=0`, `c_v=0`, correct colors and program. A successful `G(P)` at label `k` has all four *actual* heights `k` and increments them together. Inductively, for every site `v` with actual height `h_v=k`:

1. The **current** tile `P_{k mod 2}(v)` has all other heights in `{k−1,k}`.
2. The **previous** tile `P_{1−k mod 2}(v)` has all other heights in `{k,k+1}`.

Base case is immediate. When a tile at `k` fires, its four sites become `k+1` together. For each of them this tile becomes the previous tile, while the opposite tile's other sites were in `{k,k+1}` and become the new current-tile permitted set. For a site outside the fired tile but sharing an opposite tile with a fired site, its height is `k` or `k+1`; raising that neighbor from `k` to `k+1` remains inside the respective permitted set. All other sites are unchanged. This proves the induction. In particular every clean tile satisfies `Valid`, and the four participants of a ready tile really have equal absolute gate counts: their clean height spread is at most one, so equal residues cannot hide a difference of `q`. Therefore successful clean gates form a linear extension of the intended per-wire gate DAG. Disjoint incomparable channels commute, including nonunitary reset channels on disjoint data/ancilla supports. At any completed causal cut, their composition equals the prescribed circuit channel **on all inputs entangled with any reference**.

The same invariant pins a site's counter from its six neighbors. If clean `h_v=k`, then keeping all other counters fixed, `Valid` on its current tile permits only `c_v=k−1` or `k` (mod q), while `Valid` on its previous tile permits only `k` or `k+1`. Their intersection is `k` for even `q≥8`. This remains true at wrap because parity is well-defined modulo even `q` and the neighboring range is at most two. Thus a single onsite counter change to another residue makes `StarValid(v)` false, and `R(v)` has the unique correct value `k mod q`.

For another site `w≠v`, each of its incident tiles has at least one untouched member. A clean member of the current tile restricts any valid candidate at `w` to `{h_w−1,h_w}`; a clean member of the previous tile restricts it to `{h_w,h_w+1}`. The only intersection is `h_w`. Thus no ideal `R(w)` changes another counter while there is exactly one counter fault. At least one incident tile of `v` is invalid, so no gate containing `v` fires. A clean neighbor may still advance through a different tile while the fault is pending. That accepted gate is also ready in the ghost clean execution, and the pinning argument applies again to the updated ghost surface. The repair target at `v` remains its unchanged ghost height; after `R(v)`, the state is a reachable clean counter state with unchanged accepted gate log.

This proves an **isolated one-counter-fault exact-history lemma**, conditional on correct colors/program and no second fault: an arbitrary onsite alteration of one counter can stall nearby gates until its rate-one repair, but cannot cause a skipped, repeated, or reordered quantum gate. The repair time is exponential of rate one from the fault time because `R(v)` remains enabled and its attempt clock is independent. If one onsite jump also alters color, `C(v)` first restores it from north/east neighbors; every gate whose guard sees this site is disabled by its 4×4 color check. If the same jump alters program copies, `G` still takes the correct three-of-four word, and `A(v,p)` restores each damaged copy. After color restoration, `R(v)` restores any counter damage. Thus one onsite **classical-control** fault repairs exactly. If the jump also touches `Q_v`, its data effect is a single local CP fault; dephasing the damaged controls and conditioning on their classical outcomes preserves a reference-uniform CP description, but generally not a scalar ideal subchannel without an actual code-correction argument. Additional data noise during the random stall is **additional faults** and must be counted over the actual physical-time exposure.

## Two counter faults and the first genuine quantum debt

Start with all counters zero. Change two sites of one even tile from `0` to `1`, without a quantum gate. Every `R` on the tile is inert, but `B(P)` is enabled and can restore either the two false advances or advance the other two. The latter is a **skipped gate**, not exact history. Conversely, start after a real even-tile gate and corrupt two sites from `1` to `0`: the counter pattern is identical, but the data contains `K_P`. No local classical decoder can distinguish these histories. One of them must incur a skipped/replayed gate unless the channel happens to be harmless. The bounded-debt theorem below proves this is only one tile location for two counter faults.

For at most two counter faults before retirement, `R` never writes a value different from the proof-only clean count. If the sites do not share a tile, both `R`s are enabled. If they share `P` and `P` is valid, both `R`s are enabled. If `P` is invalid and no `R` is enabled, the true clean assignment differs from the corrupted field at at most two sites of `P`, validates the moat, and enables `B(P)`. An off-target `B` cannot act: its moat would make a field globally valid at Hamming distance at most four from the clean one, and the only such nonidentical field is an entire tile's one-step advance or rollback. Thus within at most two repair events, with total enabled repair rate at least one, the counters reach the clean surface or an adjacent one-tile surface. Any allowed exterior `G` while faults are pending extends the clean ghost history because every dirty site has an invalid incident tile and cannot participate; the repair argument reapplies under arbitrary interleavings. The corresponding quantum channel differs from the intended gate DAG at at most one local tile operation: a forward move omits `K_P`; a backward move causes an extra `K_P` when the tile catches up, or replays the last `K_P` if one has already occurred. At the initial boundary the extra `K_{P,q−1}` is a preparation-location debt, not a duplicate of a real earlier gate. Primitive onsite data-fault maps are charged separately. Disjoint exterior channels commute even when `K_P` is nonunitary. This is a **one-location quantum debt**, not a correct logical channel unless the code/gadget corrects that location. See `REPORT.md` for the proof and limits.

Three faults defeat this rule. From the all-zero clean field, set three sites of one even tile to `1,2,3` respectively. No `R` or `B` is enabled in the resulting state; its invalid even tile blocks all gates on its four sites. The two opposite odd tiles containing the `2` and `3` sites are also invalid and freeze their neighbors, so exterior progress cannot remove this trap. An all-four-site counterfeit gate also passes validity while omitting `K_P`. Consequently this table has no general eroding damage rule or recursive threshold as stated.

Two faults in **program copies** already defeat the restricted one-fault program protection: at a ready tile, change two of its four identical program words from `W` to the same distinct word `W′`. The tile now has a `2–2` split, so `G(P)` has no three-copy majority. For each site, its other three copies also fail unanimity, so no `A(v,p)` can repair the split. `R`, `B`, and `C` do not change program words. The tile remains unable to execute its scheduled gate without a new fault or a new program-eroder rule. This is separate from the proved two-**counter**-fault theorem.

## Scope boundary for the hierarchy

The clean and bounded-fault lemmas supply fixed-support atomic gate ownership, modulo flag reuse, and a strict history/counter coupling. They do **not** prove fault-tolerant self-simulation, a joint all-set badness bound, bounded participation under arbitrary waiting, physical-time progress on an open system, or a reference-uniform no-logical-leak decomposition after multi-fault repair. A finite 6×6 or larger closed torus has a clean progress word: choose any global minimum-height site; its current tile is uniform and can fire, so repeatedly firing minimum-height tiles raises the minimum. Since clean neighboring heights differ by at most one, finite graph diameter bounds the number of such waves before a selected target advances. This gives a positive finite-domain Poisson completion probability, **not** an exterior-uniform rate for an open neighborhood whose gate guards read outside it. Protected identity/EC while a block waits and the random number of those rounds remain separate costs.
