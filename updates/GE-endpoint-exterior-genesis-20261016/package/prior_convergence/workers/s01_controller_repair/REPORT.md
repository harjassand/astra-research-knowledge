# A crossed-plaquette execution surface for exact gate history

**Independent local result, 16 October 2026.** I constructed a finite, translation-repeated asynchronous controller in which a quantum gate and the increment of all four shared-wire execution counters are one atomic local CPTP jump. The crossed even/odd 2×2 tile geometry makes a single corrupted counter uniquely reconstructible. A radius-two tile-overlap decoder repairs every episode containing at most two counter faults, allowing **at most one explicitly charged local quantum gate omission or extra execution**. One onsite fault in any classical counter, color, or fourfold-copied static program register leaves the intended gate history exact after local repair; an arbitrary onsite unitary's data action is still a data fault. These are one-level propositions for the **modified** controller. They do not establish the original paper's asynchronous hierarchy or a noise threshold. Three counter faults produce a permanent local trap for the proposed decoder.

The machine-level transition table is [TRANSITION_TABLE.md](TRANSITION_TABLE.md). Counterexample and finite-check code are in this worker directory. The deterministic tests falsify finite instances only; proofs and open interfaces are identified below.

## Source and departure

I read the locally pinned TeX source of the full [arXiv:2601.20818v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20818), especially supplementary Sec. VII (`SM.tex`, lines 1254–1336), the discrete damage argument (roughly lines 930–1235), and the self-simulation setup. Sec. VII couples successful asynchronous updates to a modulo clock and quantum brickwork channel, then repairs invalid clocks by a consensus/Toom-like rule. It states a continuous-time threshold outline and explicitly leaves detailed proof for future work. Its local clock-repair move may change a clock without executing the corresponding quantum channel; returning to a valid clock field alone does not certify the actual noncommuting channel history. The finite damage geometry and numerical overlap parameters of the discrete proof do not transfer automatically to a 4×4/6×6 read-footprint controller. The campaign handoff additionally identifies physical-time exposure, flag recycling, and exterior-uniform service as unresolved. I am not claiming a gap in the discrete theorem or a counterexample to the original asynchronous construction.

For predecessor scope, [Wang's 1991 primary conference abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/185379/) already claims an asynchronous reliable two-dimensional classical automaton. [Cook, Gács and Winfree's 2008 primary draft](https://www.cs.bu.edu/faculty/gacs/papers/3Dasync.pdf) studies self-stabilizing modulo synchronization and removal of topological inconsistencies of arbitrary size; its introduction also separates fault repair from rigorous local slowdown. I did not obtain Wang's full proof and make no priority claim for a new classical synchronization mechanism. Neither source is used as a proof of the quantum-history lemmas here.

The distinct idea here is to put the **quantum operation and its four execution-counter writes in one indivisible accepted jump**. The redundancy is spatial: a site lies in one even and one odd tile, and the two tile constraints pin its count. The counter is not a separate scheduler allowed to run ahead of the data. A collective repair may nonetheless reconstruct the wrong side of an ambiguous two-fault event, so it is charged as a faulty quantum gate rather than declared exact.

## Registers, local rules, and what is actually finite

For an even torus of side at least six, (P_0(v)) is the unique 2×2 tile with even-even anchor containing (v), and (P_1(v)) the analogous odd-odd tile. The two intersect only at (v). A site stores finite-dimensional data (Q_v); a counter (c_v\in\mathbb Z_q), even (q\geq8); a two-bit affine coordinate color (\chi_v=(x_v\bmod2,y_v\bmod2)); and two copies (\pi_{v,0},\pi_{v,1}) of finite static tile gate words. Each tile's four sites initially agree on its word. The ideal word has period dividing (q). The *actual successful gate count* (h_v\in\mathbb N) is used only in the proof and is not stored. The preloaded program handles a fixed finite-depth one-level circuit or periodic hold rule. Mutable self-simulating program/structure control is not implemented by this result.

For tile (P) of parity (p), `Valid_p(P)` means its four counter residues are uniform or use only adjacent residues (a,a+1\pmod q) with (a\bmod2\ne p). A gate on tile (P) is ready at (k) if all four counters equal (k\pmod q), (k\bmod2=p), the two incident tiles of **every** participant are valid, the entire 4×4 counter/color star has the correct affine color pattern, and at least three of the four copies of the tile's program word agree. The jump selects their majority channel (K_{P,k}), applies it to all four data registers, and increments all four counters *atomically*. The (K_{P,k}) may be a general CPTP channel, including reset or EC subroutine, provided its input/output are within this four-site port and its schedule is fixed. Any reset must be a scheduled gate label; there is no independent reset or completion-flag channel that can race with a gate. Four data wires are owned by exactly one accepted tile jump at each local phase. No request/acknowledge flag is retained or recycled; modulo reuse follows real successful jumps.

Every translated 2×2 anchor has a jump attempt. The color guard enables exactly even-even and odd-odd anchors in a clean checkerboard. A single color alteration breaks affine edges in every 4×4 read box containing it. It cannot make a wrong anchor valid because at least three unaltered vertices of that tile force the true anchor parity. The color repair `C(v)` takes the common value inferred from its north and east neighbors and writes it at (v) when both agree. A single color fault is uniquely repaired; an uncorrupted site's north/east inferences disagree if just one of those neighbors was altered, so it is not overwritten. The counter repair `R(v)` requires a correct local 3×3 color pattern and replaces (c_v) by the **unique** residue that makes its two crossed tiles valid, if one exists and differs from (c_v). The static program repair copies the common word of the other three sites of each tile into a damaged site; gate majority already gives the correct word with one onsite program fault. Two program faults can create a tie or ambiguity and are outside that theorem.

The collective `B(P)` is enabled on an invalid tile (P), only if no site of (P) has an effective `R`. It enumerates assignments modifying at most two counters in (P), tests validity of every tile at overlap-graph distance at most two from (P), and chooses the minimum-Hamming, lexicographically first candidate. The read box is at most 6×6 sites, the write support is at most two counters in (P), and quantum data is untouched. The entire search is a finite local truth table once (q) is fixed. It is not an oracle: it does not know which sites faulted, read quantum data, or invert (K). A counter sector jump is implemented by dephasing finite classical controls, applying the guarded CPTP branch, and writing the new classical basis state; rejected sectors receive identity. This gives a local CPTP map on arbitrary data-reference inputs. All jump attempt clocks can be independent rate-one Poisson processes. The checks below concern ideal jumps plus the specified onsite noise jumps, not noisy implementation of the engineered jump operators.

The largest classical read diameter is six. Fixed (q), fixed gate alphabet, and fixed local data dimension therefore give finite physical alphabet and support. This says nothing about whether the same local alphabet can encode the entire hierarchy with the necessary program and code capacity; choosing a huge constant (M) is not a recursive construction.

## Exact clean history

Let a clean trajectory start with every (h_v=c_v=0). Successful `G(P)` is the **only** transition that changes (h), and it increments the corresponding four (c)'s in the same jump. The following induction is the key local invariant. If (h_v=k), then every other site in its current tile (P_{k\bmod2}(v)) has height (k-1) or (k), and every other site in its previous tile has height (k) or (k+1). The base state is immediate. When a ready tile at (k) fires, its four sites become (k+1); the fired tile changes from current to previous for them, while their other tile's sites remain in the required interval. A site outside the fired tile but sharing one opposite tile with a fired participant has height (k) or (k+1); lifting that participant from (k) to (k+1) preserves the interval. Other sites are unchanged.

Consequently every clean tile is `Valid`. Height spread within one tile is at most one, so modulo-equal counters of a ready tile represent equal *absolute* heights, despite wrap. An accepted `G(P)` therefore executes the next gate on each of its four wires. Two incomparable accepted gates have disjoint data support; their CPTP channels commute as superoperators, including disjoint resets. Hence every accepted history is a linear extension of the fixed per-wire circuit DAG. At a cut where all predecessor gates of the target have occurred, the data channel is the prescribed composition on **all** data-reference inputs. This is a channel equality, not merely agreement on basis states or outcomes. The random Poisson proposal order changes only which commuting incomparable channels are interleaved. It does not establish that a desired cut occurs by any specified time.

## Local pinning and one full onsite fault

The invariant yields a stronger pinning fact. Fix clean (h) and a site (v) with (h_v=k). If at least one *unchanged* other member of its current tile remains, validity of that tile restricts any proposed (c_v) to (k-1) or (k). If at least one unchanged other member of the previous tile remains, it restricts (c_v) to (k) or (k+1). The intersection is only (k\pmod q). The parity orientation in `Valid` excludes the opposite neighbor in each case; (q\ge8) avoids accidental aliasing among (k-1,k,k+1).

With one altered counter, restoring it to (h_v\pmod q) validates both tiles, and the pinning fact says no other residue does. At every other site, both tiles have untouched members and a repair cannot write a false value. The altered site's crossed star must be invalid, so no gate containing that site can fire. Gates on neighboring clean sites may still fire through their other tiles; if one does, it is also ready in the clean ghost execution and the same pinning statement reapplies to the extended ghost. Thus `R(v)` remains enabled and correct until it fires. The target is the true actual-gate count, not a merely valid modulo clock. If colors and program are sound, a single counter fault changes no quantum gate history at all. Its repair delay is exactly bounded by an independent rate-one attempt at `R(v)`; noise accumulated during that delay is additional physical noise, not free idling.

For one arbitrary onsite unitary on ((Q_v,c_v,\chi_v,\pi_{v,0},\pi_{v,1})), dephase the finite classical controls and work branchwise. If color changed, every affected gate guard fails; `C(v)` restores it from unchanged north/east neighbors. Counter repair then acts as above. A corrupted program copy cannot select a wrong gate because each tile has three unchanged copies; `A(v,p)` restores it. These repairs need not occur in a fixed order except that `R(v)` waits for its color pattern. No ideal jump at another site overwrites a sound register in this one-fault episode. Control-induced skipped, duplicated, reordered or mislabeled gates are therefore absent. The unitary's action on (Q_v) is a separate local quantum fault. Conditioning on dephased control outcomes yields a reference-uniform CP instrument with that local fault, but branch weights may depend on the logical input when the unitary entangled control and data. A scalar ideal logical subchannel requires an actual code-correction proof, such as the accepting-span/decoder test in the root's `ALL_HISTORY_CHANNEL_CONTRACT.md`.

## Two counter faults: local bounded-debt theorem

**Theorem (one-level, counter-only, pathwise).** Start from a reachable clean configuration with correct colors and static replicated program. Allow at most two onsite counter alterations at arbitrary distinct or equal sites and times, with arbitrary ideal `G/R/B` attempts interleaved, then no further faults. Before the first `B`, every accepted `G` also extends a reachable clean ghost execution with the same gate (K_{P,k}), and every effective `R` restores one corrupted site to the ghost counter value. Within at most two effective repair events, with total enabled repair rate at least one until retirement, the counter field is either the ghost field or differs from it by exactly the four sites of a single uniform tile, each shifted by (+1) or (-1) modulo (q). After a `+1` discrepancy the one gate at that tile is omitted; after a `-1` discrepancy the tile's next gate is an extra execution, either a replay of its last gate or a boundary-phase gate before the initialized circuit. No other quantum gate is skipped, duplicated or reordered. Arbitrary onsite data effects of the two primitive noise jumps must be included separately.

Here is the proof, including separated fault times. Define the *ghost* surface as the clean actual gate-count field obtained by following every accepted `G`, ignoring counter-only fault writes. Initially it is the real field. While at most two sites differ from it, the pinning fact shows every dirty site has an invalid incident tile. Thus no accepted gate touches a dirty site's data or counter. Any accepted gate has all four counters equal to the ghost on its tile and is a legitimate clean ghost gate; after it fires the ghost remains reachable and the dirty set does not expand. The pinning fact also shows any unique `R(x)` that changes a counter must set it to the ghost value: each of the two incident tiles of (x) has at least one member untouched by the at most two dirty sites. This proves the invariant under **all** interleavings before `B`, not just a simultaneous corruption snapshot. If the first fault was already repaired before the second, the same one-fault argument simply restarts.

If the two dirty sites share no tile, each site's two incident tiles contain only its own damage; restoring it makes its star valid, so both `R`s are enabled. Suppose they share one tile (P). Their other incident tiles are distinct and each contains only its own fault. If (P) itself is valid, restoring either dirty site keeps (P) valid. To verify this, hold its two unchanged counter values fixed. If those values differ, they fix the only allowed adjacent pair, containing the original values of both dirty sites. If they are equal to (x), tile validity permits only (x) and the single parity-allowed neighbor of (x); both the clean and current tile use that pair. Thus both `R`s are enabled. If (P) is invalid, either an `R` is enabled, or the clean assignment changing just the two dirty sites of (P) validates every tile in the radius-two moat, so `B(P)` is enabled.

An emergency `B` cannot repair an off-target tile. An invalid tile containing only one dirty site (v) has `R(v)` enabled unless the other dirty site lies in (v)'s *other* tile. In that exceptional case, the second dirty site and all tiles incident to it are within the overlap-distance-two moat of the proposed `B` tile. Any candidate `B` output would therefore be globally valid: all changed sites are in the moat, while all unaffected tiles were valid in the clean ghost. It would differ from the ghost on at most four sites—two primitive dirty sites and at most two `B` writes. The distance-four lemma below forces its changed sites to be exactly one 2×2 tile, impossible here because one dirty site lies outside the `B` tile and that site plus at most two other changed sites in the tile cannot fill any 2×2 tile. Hence no off-target candidate exists. The same reasoning allows `B(P)` only when both dirty sites lie in (P). After `R`, one dirty site remains and is repaired by `R`; after `B`, the surface is globally valid. There are at most two effective repair events. At every pre-retirement state at least one effective repair jump is enabled. Even if exterior gates alter which repair is enabled, the conditional total repair hazard is at least one, so the no-new-fault retirement time is dominated by an Erlang-two rate-one time. This is a **local counter-retirement** tail, not physical-time completion of a macro-operation.

**Distance-four lemma.** Let (h) be any clean reachable integer surface, and (t) a globally tile-valid residue field differing from (h\pmod q) on at most four sites. If a site (v) differs, then at least one of its two incident tiles has *all three* other members changed; otherwise an unchanged member in each tile pins (t_v=h_v\pmod q). Hence at least four sites change, and their set is one complete tile (P). The other tile of each changed site has three unchanged members, so its new value is exactly (h_v+1) if (P) is its current tile or (h_v-1) if (P) is its previous tile. If (P) were mixed in clean (h), its lower sites would move down and upper sites up, producing residues separated by three, which violates `Valid`. Thus (P) was uniform and all four sites shift in the same direction by one. A forward shift is a genuinely ready tile gate; a backward shift is a formal immediate predecessor. This combinatorial lemma is independent of the quantum gates' inverses or unitarity.

The quantum comparison is a **local circuit replacement**, not a claim that control repair restores quantum data. A forward shift of (P) without applying (K_{P,k}) is a skipped (K_{P,k}) location, represented by identity in its place. A backward shift means the counter has one pending gate on (P) that the quantum data has already seen if the ghost height is positive. The next accepted `G(P)` applies the corresponding (K_{P,k-1}) again; relative to the intended circuit this is the replacement (K_{P,k-1}\circ K_{P,k-1}) at that location. If the ghost was at initial height zero, the formal predecessor is count (-1), so the catch-up jump instead inserts (K_{P,q-1}) **before** the initialized circuit. It must be charged to a preparation/boundary location; calling it a replay would be false. While the shared tile (P) is invalid before `B`, every `G` touching any of its four sites is blocked because that site's `StarValid` includes (P). Earlier accepted gates during the first isolated fault can touch other sites of (P), but cannot touch the dirty site, so they commute with that onsite data-fault map. After a backward `B` move, the pending (P) gate remains ready until it fires: an overlapping opposite-parity tile has a lower (P) site and cannot be uniform at its next phase. Hence the quantum debt occupies one causally convex local circuit port. Gates disjoint from that port commute even for nonunitary CPTP maps. An onsite data fault at a dirty site can likewise be slid past disjoint gates, but is an additional local CP fault. At a later accepting cut, all histories have the same intended circuit except this one local gate debt and any primitive data faults. Whether a decoder removes those errors on an entangled logical input is unproved here.

The unavoidable ambiguity is concrete. Start with all counters zero, then corrupt two sites of an even tile from 0 to 1 without applying its gate. Alternatively, apply the real gate on that tile, then corrupt the same two counters from 1 to 0. The observed counter pattern is the same two-zero/two-one pattern. Its quantum data differs by (K), which may be noninvertible. No classical local repair can know which history occurred. A conservative theorem must count an omitted or repeated gate. Four false advances can counterfeit a whole completed tile and pass local validity; that is another fault packet, not exact history.
For the specified minimum-Hamming/lexicographic `B`, a concrete replay occurs after the first even-tile gate from the zero state: corrupt two of its new `1` counters to `0`; `B` selects the all-zero tile rather than all-one, so the next tile gate repeats (K_{P,0}). The program need not be unitary, and the calculation never reverses it.

## Decisive three-fault obstruction and missing hierarchy

The present `R+B` rule is not an eroding multi-fault repair. From the all-zero field, choose one even tile and set three of its counters to (1,2,3), leaving the fourth at zero. The even tile is invalid. At every one of its four sites, the other three tile values already fail the adjacent-pair condition, so no `R` there can repair the tile. The opposite odd tiles at the `2` and `3` sites are invalid with patterns (2,0,0,0) and (3,0,0,0). Their three clean neighbors cannot advance: every gate touching them reads the invalid odd tile. No single-site repair of one such neighbor can validate a tile that still contains (2,0,0) or (3,0,0), and `B` on either odd tile cannot validate the even tile, which it intersects at only one site. To make both invalid odd tiles valid, `B` on the even tile would have to use its two allowed writes on the `2` and `3` sites, setting each to 0 or 1; then the untouched (0,1) pair in the even tile itself violates even-tile validity. Hence no local repair can change any of those four counters; exterior gate progress does not break the trap. The finite code finds no initial `R/B`, and the argument shows why the core stays frozen.

There is also a **two-onsite-program-fault** trap, outside the two-counter theorem. At a ready tile with four identical copied words (W), corrupt two copies to the same different (W'\). The result is a 2–2 split: `G(P)` requires at least three agreeing words, and for each site the other three copies do not agree, so every `A(v,p)` is inert. Counter and color repairs leave program words unchanged. The scheduled gate on that tile remains blocked even while counters and colors are valid. The one-onsite full-register theorem is therefore sharp for this simple fourfold program code; an independently designed multi-fault program eroder would be needed for a hierarchy.

Adding an emergency reset over a larger finite region is possible as a *new* rule, but its boundary must be proved to have a compatible execution surface without recursively widening the quarantine. A reset of counters alone can introduce multiple skipped/reordered gates and can break a noncommuting quantum computation. A data purge must be an explicit noisy erasure/EC packet with bounded fanout and accepting-channel proof. No such finite directed release or full retirement protocol is provided here. The three-fault trap therefore blocks the damage-shrink premise needed to iterate a threshold. One cannot simply call the trapped region a bad macro-operation while allowing it to freeze clean neighbors forever.

The source's discrete Toom triangle argument includes a specific eroding rule, finite badness cells, bounded participation, and an encoded channel lift. Here the 6×6 moat changes the direct influence and exRec overlap constants; none of the source values (including (R=4) or its (t_{\rm bad})) can be reused without a new combinatorial count. A two-fault local packet theorem alone does not imply the exponent ((t_{\rm bad}+1)/R>1), and three-fault deadlock makes any present recursive claim false. The fixed preloaded program also omits dynamic higher-level cell structure, syndrome-dependent control, spatial seams, and all boundary-crossing operations of a self-simulating block. Its one-level clean partial order is a useful interface, not a hierarchy.

Physical time remains material. A rate-one counter-repair hazard gives only the exposure of that local episode under no further faults. Each dirty site stalls gates on its wire, and all physical data and ancillas continue to suffer Poisson noise while waiting. The number of protected identity/EC rounds and their random duration must be charged to the packet; ordinary identity outside the completed output is not protected memory. A final accepting cut must wait for all required gates and any backward catch-up gate, then run a specified decoder. The root's finite all-history accepting-span contract could verify one closed finite gadget *after* all exterior ports and noise edges are supplied, but it cannot supply the missing retirement and service guarantees. The source's finite-state waiting assertion is not a substitute for an exterior-uniform sealed service certificate.

## Falsification checks and resource record

`check_execution_surface.py` enumerated 76 clean 4×4 integer surfaces through maximum height five and checked 20,076 clean/random trace samples with one-site alterations of ±1 and ±2. `check_modulo_surface.py` checked all 8,512 one-site alternative residues on those 76 reachable surfaces for correct unique repair and no false repair, and recorded the two-site `R` deadlock and four-site counterfeit. A separate exhaustive loop over 446,880 two-site residue alternatives found no globally valid false surface at Hamming distance two and no wrong unique `R`; the shared-tile classification covered 178,752 cases: 3,648 with a valid shared tile and correct `R`, 43,776 with invalid shared tile but correct `R`, and 131,328 needing the collective case. `check_moat_repair.py` performed 1,000 randomized 6×6, modulo-8 two-fault episodes: all retired in at most three tested iterations to the ghost or one tile step, and two produced single-gate debt. `check_bulk_repair.py` found a trap for the simpler direction-only bulk rule, prompting the moat decoder. A targeted three-fault script found the (1,2,3) trap above. These checks do not establish the lemmas for all lattice sizes or fault histories; the proofs carry that burden. They used local CPU/Python only. I did not use a GPU, external compute, publication service, or another agent.
`check_color_program.py` additionally checked all 108 single-color alterations on a 6×6 torus against every 4×4 gate color guard, verified north/east color repair cannot overwrite a sound site, and checked fourfold majority under one program-copy corruption. `check_interleavings.py` ran 2,000 randomized separated two-fault episodes with 364 accepted gates close to a dirty tile: every accepted gate extended the clean ghost, every `R` wrote the ghost value, and every sampled `B` outcome was either exact or one tile step. These are implementation checks of the written predicates, not a proof of the hierarchy.
Wall and child CPU times for the final script replay are recorded in [CHECK_RECEIPTS.md](CHECK_RECEIPTS.md). They are host-specific execution receipts, not evidence of quantum or threshold performance.

## Claim boundary

The strongest defensible claim is a finite, exact one-level gate-history controller with full one-onsite **classical-control** fault repair and a two-counter-fault bounded *single-gate-debt* theorem. There is no recursive noisy autonomous quantum computer, positive threshold, practical memory lifetime, or correctness claim for the original source's asynchronous rule. The decisive next invention would be an arbitrary-cluster eroder or bounded-port quarantine whose effect on actual quantum circuit history remains within code-correctable accepting spans, plus a physical-time service proof and a fixed-alphabet self-simulation. The current three-fault trap is a concrete design test for that invention.
