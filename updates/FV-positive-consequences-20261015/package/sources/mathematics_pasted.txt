1. What remains valid—and what the new proofs actually use
The inherited characterization concerns a normalized, compactly supported, self-adjoint operator-valued distribution \(T\). Its condition is
\[
H_T(g):=T(e^{g\cdot y})\succeq \lambda_g I,
\qquad \lambda_g>0,
\]
for every finite real \(g\).
Here, positivity is uniform over unit vectors at each fixed tilt. It does not mean that one positive lower bound works for all \(g\). The preceding manuscript claims this is equivalent to the existence of scalar noise with every exponential moment making \(T*\nu\) a positive normalized operator measure.    PROOF
I found no new defect requiring withdrawal of that compact theorem. However, the principal new results below do not rely on its difficult sufficiency construction. They use a necessary condition that I reconstructed independently:
If a compact operator distribution becomes positive after scalar convolution with all-exponential noise, its real Laplace transform must already be positive—and boundedly invertible—at every finite tilt.

The analytic justification matters. A formal substitution of imaginary arguments into a Fourier transform is insufficient. The reconstructed proof obtains genuine exponential moments of the positive output measure from its entire characteristic function: finite differences establish even moments, Cauchy estimates bound them, and summing the hyperbolic-cosine series gives exponential integrability. Only then is the identity
\[
\int e^{g\cdot x}M(dx)=m_\nu(g)H_T(g)
\]
justified.
Since
\[
m_\nu(g)=\int e^{g\cdot z}\nu(dz)>0,
\]
scalar noise cannot change the sign of an operator Laplace value.
That observation leads to a stronger structural consequence than another regularizer formula.
2. The main theorem: changing the ordering changes regularizability
For two bounded self-adjoint operators \(A,B\), consider three characteristic operators.
Representation	Characteristic operator	Possibility of positive scalar regularization
Full Weyl/Wigner ordering	\(e^{i(sA+tB)}\)	The inherited theorem supplies all-exponential noise for every bounded pair.
Symmetrized product, or Margenau–Hill ordering	\(\frac12(e^{isA}e^{itB}+e^{itB}e^{isA})\)	All-exponential noise works if and only if \(A\) and \(B\) commute.
Ordered product	\(e^{isA}e^{itB}\)	Any scalar probability noise works only if \(A\) and \(B\) commute; no moment assumption is needed.


The representations and ordering conventions are established mathematics. The generalized Wigner distribution uses the full exponential of a linear combination, whereas the Margenau–Hill construction uses symmetrized products of separate exponentials. They agree for commuting observables but are different otherwise. arXiv
Theorem: all-exponential noise cannot remove Margenau–Hill negativity
Let \(A,B\) be bounded self-adjoint operators on any complex Hilbert space. Define
\[
J(s,t)=\frac12\left(e^{isA}e^{itB}+e^{itB}e^{isA}\right).
\]
There exists a scalar probability law \(\nu\), with every exponential moment, and a normalized POVM \(M\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\widehat M(s,t)=\widehat\nu(s,t)J(s,t)
}
\tag{1}
\]
if and only if
\[
\boxed{AB=BA.}
\tag{2}
\]
The noise coordinates may have arbitrary dependence. There is no restriction to matrices, eigenvectors, finite dimension, Gaussian noise, or small noise.
Proof: positivity under every exponential weighting forces commutation
The moment-transfer argument gives
\[
\int e^{sx+ty}M(dx\,dy)
=
m_\nu(s,t)
\frac{e^{sA}e^{tB}+e^{tB}e^{sA}}2.
\tag{3}
\]
The left side is positive. The scalar multiplier is strictly positive. Therefore
\[
e^{sA}e^{tB}+e^{tB}e^{sA}\succeq0
\qquad\text{for every real }s,t.
\tag{4}
\]
The decisive operator lemma is:
If bounded self-adjoint \(A,C\) satisfy
\[
Ce^{sA}+e^{sA}C\succeq0
\quad\text{for every }s\ge0,
\]
then \(C\succeq0\) and \(AC=CA\).

Its proof avoids spectral localization entirely.
Set
\[
S_r=e^{-rC},\qquad r\ge0.
\]
At \(s=0\), the hypothesis gives \(C\succeq0\). For every fixed \(s\ge0\),
\[
\frac{d}{dr}\left(S_re^{sA}S_r\right)
=
-S_r(Ce^{sA}+e^{sA}C)S_r
\preceq0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
S_re^{sA}S_r\preceq e^{sA},
\]
which implies
\[
\left\|e^{sA/2}S_re^{-sA/2}\right\|\le1.
\tag{5}
\]
For fixed \(r\), consider the norm-entire operator function
\[
F_r(z)=e^{zA/2}S_re^{-zA/2}.
\]
Because \(S_r\) is self-adjoint, \(F_r(-s)=F_r(s)^*\), so the bound also holds for negative real \(s\). Imaginary shifts merely conjugate by unitaries:
\[
F_r(s+it)=e^{itA/2}F_r(s)e^{-itA/2}.
\]
Thus \(F_r\) is bounded on the entire complex plane.
Applying scalar Liouville’s theorem to every matrix coefficient makes \(F_r\) constant. Differentiating at \(z=0\) gives \([A,S_r]=0\); differentiating in \(r\) at zero gives
\[
[A,C]=0.
\]
Return to (4), fix a nonzero \(t_0\), and take \(C=e^{t_0B}\). The lemma gives \([A,e^{t_0B}]=0\). Since
\[
B=t_0^{-1}\log(e^{t_0B}),
\]
continuous functional calculus gives \([A,B]=0\).
Conversely, commuting bounded observables possess a joint spectral measure, and convolving that measure with any all-exponential scalar law supplies (1).
This proves the theorem.
An exact witness—not a numerical positivity search
For
\[
A=\sigma_z,\qquad B=\sigma_x,\qquad s=t=\log3,
\]
the operator Laplace value is
\[
H_J(s,t)=\frac19
\begin{pmatrix}
45&20\\
20&5
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the unit vector \(v=(1,-2)/\sqrt5\),
\[
\boxed{\langle v,H_J(s,t)v\rangle=-\frac13.}
\]
Any proposed scalar noise would multiply this by \(m_\nu(\log3,\log3)>0\). It cannot turn the negative value into an integral of a positive function against a positive measure.
The witness illustrates the general theorem; it is not a replacement for the arbitrary-Hilbert-space proof.
What this establishes structurally
The class of compact operator distributions admitting all-exponential scalar regularization is not closed under symmetrized convolution on the same Hilbert space.
The inputs can already be positive spectral measures. Their symmetrized composition nevertheless leaves the regularizable class whenever they fail to commute.
This is the most important consequence obtained here:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Positive regularization of linear combinations}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{positive regularization preserving operator composition}.
}
\]
It does not mean actual sequential quantum measurements lack positive outcome probabilities. Actual instruments include measurement disturbance and satisfy a different transform. It also does not forbid signed estimators, nonlinear reconstruction, or a different measurement kernel.
For the unsymmetrized ordered product, the obstruction is even simpler. Every scalar characteristic function is nonzero near zero. Dividing it out of the Hermitian-symmetry condition for a POVM forces
\[
e^{-isA}e^{-itB}=e^{-itB}e^{-isA}
\]
near the origin. Mixed differentiation gives \(AB=BA\), without requiring any noise moments.
3. A stronger unbounded counterexample: smooth commutators are insufficient
A natural attempt to extend the bounded theorem is to replace boundedness by regularity of commutators.
That extension fails under substantially stronger conditions than merely having a bounded first commutator.
Theorem: an infinitely smoothing interaction can still defeat every exponential-neighborhood regularizer
On
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb C^2,
\]
define
\[
\boxed{
A_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
3n&0\\
0&n
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_n=e^{-\sqrt n}\sigma_x,
}
\]
and let \(A=\bigoplus_n A_n\), \(B=\bigoplus_n B_n\).
These operators satisfy strong regularity properties.
The operator \(A\) is positive, \(A\ge I\), and has compact resolvent. Its thermal trace is finite for every \(\beta>0\):
\[
\operatorname{tr}(e^{-\beta A})
=
\sum_{n\ge1}(e^{-3\beta n}+e^{-\beta n})<\infty.
\]
The operator \(B\) is bounded and trace class. Indeed, \(A^kB\) is bounded and trace class for every fixed \(k\), so \(B\) maps the whole Hilbert space into every domain \(D(A^k)\).
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\operatorname{ad}_{A_n}^{\,k}(B_n)\right\|
=
(2n)^k e^{-\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{6}
\]
For every fixed \(k\), this sequence is bounded and summable. Thus every iterated commutator extends to a bounded trace-class operator, and
\[
t\mapsto e^{itA}Be^{-itA}
\]
is smooth in operator norm to every order.
Nevertheless, no scalar noise having exponential moments in even a neighborhood of zero can make
\[
\widehat M(s,t)
=
\widehat\nu(s,t)e^{i(sA+tB)}
\tag{7}
\]
the characteristic function of a normalized POVM.
This is stronger than excluding noise with every exponential moment.
Proof: tiny high-energy couplings become decisive under tilting
Suppose such a measurement exists. Compress it to the \(n\)-th two-dimensional block. Each compression is a normalized POVM with the same scalar noise.
Write
\[
\kappa(s,t)=\log\mathbb E e^{sZ_1+tZ_2}.
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(s>0\) inside the noise’s exponential neighborhood. Then
\[
v_s:=\partial_t^2\kappa(s,0)<\infty
\]
is the transverse variance under exponential weighting of the noise.
For a finite Pauli pair
\[
A_0=a\sigma_z,\qquad B_0=b\sigma_x,
\]
positivity of the tilted first-and-second-moment block matrix gives
\[
\boxed{
v_s\ge
\frac{b^2}{2a^2s^2}
\bigl[\sinh(2as)-2as\bigr].
}
\tag{8}
\]
I reconstructed this identity directly from the \(2\times2\) matrix exponential. The scalar shift \(2nI\) in \(A_n=2nI+n\sigma_z\) cancels in the normalized tilt.
Therefore the common noise must satisfy
\[
v_s\ge
\frac{e^{-2\sqrt n}}{2n^2s^2}
\bigl[\sinh(2ns)-2ns\bigr]
\qquad\text{for every }n.
\tag{9}
\]
But the right side is asymptotic to
\[
\frac{e^{2ns-2\sqrt n}}{4n^2s^2},
\]
which diverges.
No finite \(v_s\) can satisfy these inequalities.
The mathematical boundary exposed
Every fixed commutator order measures a polynomially weighted coupling,
\[
n^k e^{-\sqrt n},
\]
which remains controlled.
Exponential tilting measures an exponentially weighted coupling,
\[
e^{sn-\sqrt n},
\]
which does not.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{smoothness under every commutator order}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{the exponential control required for exact regularization}.
}
\]
The example can also be scaled so that any prescribed finite collection of commutator norms is arbitrarily small, while the impossibility persists.
More generally, for separated Pauli blocks with gap parameter \(a_n\to\infty\) and coupling \(b_n\), a successful common noise admitting a fixed positive tilt \(s\) must satisfy
\[
\sup_n\frac{|b_n|e^{sa_n}}{a_n}<\infty.
\tag{10}
\]
This is a necessary condition, not a sufficient unbounded classification.
Every finite truncation of the example lies within the inherited bounded existence theorem. There is therefore no contradiction. Nor does this prove a lower bound for finite-resolution or energy-cutoff approximation: the obstruction concerns the exact state-independent representation.
4. The exponential-moment requirement is a genuine dividing line
The preceding impossibilities are not evidence that positive smoothing never works outside the original characterization.
In fact, a direct finite-order argument gives:
Every normalized, compactly supported, self-adjoint operator distribution can be made positive by sufficiently broad product hyperbolic-secant noise.

Take
\[
p_s(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac1{\pi s}\operatorname{sech}(x_j/s).
\]
On any fixed compact support,
\[
\left\|
\frac{p_s(x-\cdot)}{p_s(x)}-1
\right\|_{C^N}
=O(s^{-1})
\]
uniformly in \(x\). Applying the distribution’s finite-order bound gives
\[
(T*p_s)(x)=p_s(x)\bigl[I+O(s^{-1})\bigr],
\]
so sufficiently large \(s\) gives positivity everywhere.
This noise has all polynomial moments and a nonzero exponential neighborhood, but its coordinate exponential moments exist only for
\[
|g_j|<1/s.
\]
A scalar example makes the difference exact:
\[
T=\delta_0-\delta_0'',\qquad H_T(g)=1-g^2.
\]
Its negative Laplace values exclude all-exponential regularization. Yet
\[
p_s-p_s''\ge0\qquad(s\ge1)
\]
for the hyperbolic-secant density.
This broad smoothing mechanism is not being promoted as a new foundational discovery. Scalar convolution positivity has substantial older theory. The point is that all ordinary moments, some exponential moments, and all exponential moments are mathematically different contracts, not interchangeable descriptions of “well-behaved noise.” arXiv
There is also an exact limitation on finite testing. For even \(q\),
\[
T=\delta_0-\varepsilon(\delta_{1/q}-\delta_0)^{*q}
\]
matches \(\delta_0\) through moment \(q-1\), can be arbitrarily close in signed total-variation norm, and can have positive Laplace transform throughout any preselected bounded interval. Nevertheless,
\[
H_T(g)=1-\varepsilon(e^{g/q}-1)^q
\]
eventually becomes negative.
This rules out unrestricted inference from finitely many moments and a bounded tilt window. It does not establish computational undecidability or exclude certificates using additional global structure.
5. Historical assessment: substantive antecedents, unresolved exact priority
The literature comparison now supports a narrower assessment than “a fundamentally unprecedented positivity operation.”
Scalar convolution positivity is old. Pomatto, Strack and Tamuz reproduce a result credited to Ruzsa–Székely and Mattner making normalized signed measures positive after convolution, with preservation of ordinary moments. They also give a common regularizer for a bounded scalar class. The precise operator-valued, all-exponential criterion is different, but the broad positivity idea cannot be claimed as new. arXiv
Anticommutator negativity and powers are also established tools. Fazio and collaborators investigated the use of anticommutators and repeated powers to reveal noncommutativity. The proof above uses a different argument that works on arbitrary Hilbert spaces without eigenvalue nondegeneracy, but an independent derivation does not establish historical originality. APS Journals
Margenau–Hill positivity already has a substantial joint-measurement literature. The inspected 2022 construction introduces unsharp effects; the September 2026 Patra–Pan work relates Margenau–Hill positivity to compatibility for unsharp dichotomic observables. These are relevant comparators, but they do not impose the same contract as fixing the sharp characteristic operator and multiplying it by one additive scalar noise transform. arXiv
In particular, replacing a sharp effect by an unsharp effect is not generally equivalent to adding an outcome-independent random displacement. Those results therefore do not contradict the new noise-immunity theorem.
The unresolved historical question remains specific:
Does an existing operator-convolution, ordered-algebra, or positive-multiplier theorem already imply the complete compact Laplace-coercivity characterization or the exact arbitrary-dimensional noise-immunity statement?

The inspected sources did not settle that question. The original Anderson articles and the full relevant operator-convolution literature have not been exhaustively checked.
Consequently, neither of the following is justified:
- declaring the whole characterization historically subsumed;
- declaring it a cleared, exceptionally original foundational discovery.
The proper status is a proof-backed candidate with substantial antecedents and unresolved exact priority.
6. What this does—and does not—add operationally
No improved measurement information, sampling complexity, general circuit compiler, or stable inversion procedure was obtained in this tranche.
The new theorem gives a precise reason one tempting route fails: making a representation positive does not make its natural product operation positive. A classical compositional model cannot simply be read off from the regularized one-shot distribution.
There are also important limits on the negative conclusions.
The proofs use unbounded exponential tests. A negative exponential moment gives an exact contradiction, but it does not automatically give a lower bound in ordinary total variation or in the number of experimental shots. Extremely small far-tail discrepancies can carry large exponential weight. Approximation requires separately specified tail bounds, precision, and error metrics.
Likewise, the unbounded counterexample does not forbid useful finite-energy approximations or prove that every unbounded observable pair is problematic.
Finally, a nontrivial positive convolution does not generally have a probability-preserving convolution inverse. For the present finite-variance noises, if two independent probability noises convolved to a point mass, covariance additivity would force both to be point masses. Positivity after smoothing is therefore not, by itself, a way to recover positivity of the original object.
The previous covariant qubit implementation was not elaborated here. Its lack of an information advantage remains unchanged. The new \(2\times2\) calculation serves only as an exact witness for the general composition theorem.
7. Why the compatible-heat direction currently offers a stronger target
I read the current N850 dossier and the complete proof text for the specific integrand
\[
G(A)=3(u+v-2j)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+j^2-\frac52ju,
\]
where, for \(A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\),
\[
u=a^2+c^2,\qquad v=b^2+d^2,\qquad j=ad-bc.
\]
The repository supplies an internally proved, all-affine-background quasiconvexity argument using a rational compatible-heat certificate. It explicitly leaves the general cone and external verification unresolved. I reviewed the analytic implication, but did not rerun the complete encoded Gram certificate in this tranche. 
The important distinction is what the positivity correction preserves.
In scalar regularization,
\[
T\longmapsto T*p
\]
changes the distribution and its measurement laws.
In the compatible-heat argument, an exact differential-form correction changes the pointwise density but has zero integral on the admissible gradient fields. Positivity of the corrected density therefore proves an inequality for the original integral. The heat flow then connects that inequality to quasiconvexity without abandoning compatibility.
That is direct mathematical leverage: the correction preserves the quantity the theorem needs to control.
The method itself is not an Astra invention. Bruno–Pasqualotto explicitly use heat monotonicity and exact two-form corrections in their Dacorogna–Marcellini work. The potential Astra contribution lies in the exact additional certificates, their scope, and a substantial extension beyond the imported symmetry class. arXiv
The worthwhile next target is therefore the relation between the compatible-heat certificate cone and the full normalized left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant rank-one-convex planar quartic cone, rather than more nearby examples of the already certified \(G\).
A failure of the certificate ansatz would be a limitation of that method—not a counterexample to quasiconvexity. An actual compatible negative field would be needed for the stronger conclusion.
