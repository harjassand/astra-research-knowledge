It does not retroactively validate the record's loader, execute the LJ38 benchmark, or supply a matched performance result.

## Full-network versus tutorial scale

The acquired full hosted archive has 100,000 minima and 138,888 saddle rows. A separate supplied producer report at `research/2026-10-15-regularization-clock-pressure-v1/sources/clock_pasted.txt` describes its expanded tutorial input as 64 minima and 70 saddle rows, with a source–target component of 47 states and 57 undirected pairs (minima 8 and 2). Its raw data bundle is absent from this workspace, so those tutorial counts remain producer-reported rather than independently re-read here. By raw row counts, the hosted archive has 1,562.5 times as many minima and about 1,984 times as many saddle rows. In the acquired raw-table graph, the largest component has 71,887 minima and 119,853 distinct non-self pairs, about 1,529.5 and 2,102.7 times the tutorial's reported component counts. These are scope ratios, not performance or scaling results.

The Wales [OPTIM LJ38 examples](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/examples/OPTIM/) are separate pathway demonstrations: they produce `path.info` records for selected double-ended or single-ended searches, including one connected example path with 207 transition states. Those path examples and the separate 64-min tutorial do not substitute for the 100,000-minimum PATHSAMPLE archive. The official PATHSAMPLE page also lists a short-barrier-run example separately from its 100,000-minimum archive.

“Full” here means the entire public archive linked by the Wales group, not a proven exhaustive catalogue of every LJ38 minimum or saddle. The source paper describes LJ38 as a double-funnel landscape associated with FCC and icosahedral structures ([Doye, Miller & Wales, 1999](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/pdf/JCP.110.6896.1999.pdf)); the downloaded tables contain no explicit per-node FCC/ICO labels. An exact energy crosswalk gives provisional singleton endpoints: archive ID 1 has energy `-173.9284265906`, matching the paper’s global FCC truncated-octahedron minimum (`-173.928427`, point group Oh); archive ID 7 has energy `-173.2523784157`, matching the second-lowest incomplete Mackay icosahedral minimum (`-173.252378`, point group C5v). The paper’s third-lowest incomplete Mackay icosahedral energy (`-173.134317`) similarly matches archive ID 16. These are paper-to-table energy/rank matches, not independent geometry classifications; the coordinate members are available for reviewer inspection. IDs 1 and 7 are therefore provisional single-state endpoints, not an asserted FCC/ICO basin partition. The archive’s `min.A`/`min.B` files contain respectively IDs 1–5 and IDs 6–400, with no structural-family annotations; they are PATHSAMPLE terminal sets, not funnel labels. The tutorial’s IDs 8 and 2 likewise cannot be transferred to this archive by row number.

## Structural receipt and conventions

The extracted tables have exactly six numeric columns per `min.data` row and eight per `ts.data` row. The official [PATHSAMPLE input guide](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/chris/PATHSAMPLE.2.1.doc/node4.html) defines minima fields as energy, log product of positive Hessian eigenvalues, point-group order, and three sorted inertia-tensor eigenvalues. Transition-state rows are energy, the Hessian log product, point-group order, the two connected minimum IDs, and three inertia values. Endpoint IDs are one-based and table order must agree with minima coordinates. The archive endpoints span 1 through 100,000.

A topology-only scan of the extracted table rows found 616 self-transition rows, 135,137 distinct undirected non-self pairs, 2,640 pairs with multiple saddle records (maximum 30 saddle rows for one pair), and 12,830 connected components when self-loops are ignored. The largest raw-table component has 71,887 minima and 119,853 distinct non-self pairs; 22 minima are isolated. IDs 1, 2, 7, and 16 lie in that component. These are file-structure counts only; no rates, coordinate identities, or physical quantities were validated. PyGT selects a component after constructing a floating-point rate matrix, so its retained mask must be checked for the chosen `beta` because exponentiation may underflow. No PyGT loader or rate calculation was run here.

PyGT’s pinned `PyGT/io.py` documents the table interface: `load_ktn(path, beta=...)` reads `min.data` and `ts.data`, decrements one-based saddle endpoint IDs, applies optional `Nmax`/`Emax` filters, calculates harmonic unimolecular TST weights from energy barriers, Hessian log-products, and point-group-order factors, sums parallel saddle contributions, and chooses the largest component of its rate matrix. It returns the Boolean `retained` mask along with the matrices and thermodynamic arrays. `load_ktn_AB(path, retained=retained)` reads the one-based IDs in `min.A` and `min.B` and maps those sets through that mask. The source definition is available at [the pinned PyGT `io.py`](https://github.com/tomswinburne/PyGT/blob/fa0e106958d9948209daff04360290d50de82572/PyGT/io.py); the archive is directly loadable from `full-network/` after choosing `beta` and terminal sets. For provisional singleton endpoints at original IDs 1 and 7, construct one-hot masks in retained-array order from `np.flatnonzero(retained)+1`; the supplied A/B masks are broader PATHSAMPLE sets and do not express this pair. Record `beta`, filters, component mask, endpoint mapping, and objective. Do not silently describe the retained component as the whole archive.

The `min.A` and `min.B` lists are PATHSAMPLE terminal-set inputs, not supplied funnel labels. To make an FCC-to-ICO benchmark exact, first define the terminal states or basin sets by a documented geometric/classification rule and preserve that mapping as a separate input. The PyGT rate model is harmonic transition-state theory in reduced LJ units, not an experimentally measured rate model; `beta`, units, and physical interpretation must be stated. No PyGT code was executed and no rates were produced in this acquisition task.

The exact loader interface available to a reviewer is `load_ktn(".../full-network", beta=<declared inverse temperature>, Nmax=None, Emax=None)`, followed by an explicit terminal mask. For the provisional single-state test, map original one-based IDs 1 and 7 into the returned rows using `np.flatnonzero(retained)+1`, then create one-hot endpoint masks; do not substitute `load_ktn_AB` because that returns the broader archived PATHSAMPLE sets. The archive `pathdata` contains `TEMPERATURE 0.02`, but that producer control setting is not silently translated here into PyGT `beta`; the reviewer must state the rate-model convention and check underflow and the resulting component mask. Basin-to-basin FCC/ICO comparison still needs a documented geometry-based labeling rule and a defined observable/objective. The original Wales archive access blocker is resolved; the separate 64-min tutorial raw bundle remains unavailable, and the archive data license remains unresolved.
