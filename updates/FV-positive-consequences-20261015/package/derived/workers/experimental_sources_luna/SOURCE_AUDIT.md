## NH4SCN–water pressure/dissolution study

Kun Zhang et al., “Extreme barocaloric effect at dissolution,” *Nature* 649, 1180–1185 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41586-025-10013-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-10013-1). The publisher record gives online publication 2026-01-21 and issue publication 2026-01-29. The exact publisher page is <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10013-1>. The saved response `salt/nature_article_page.html` is 475,932 bytes, SHA-256 `83105d0eeacb1a82cf1ae33f47575e2dc85430f331d9cef37f4c5c8496cc1b8d`. Read the article record, abstract, figure captions, and data-availability statement; the page is a subscription preview, not the complete article. Its abstract reports the 26.8 K in-situ temperature decrease and describes the 67 J/g, 77% result as a *designed* Carnot-like cycle. Data availability says plotted/finding data are available from corresponding authors and that Source Data are supplied. The publisher PDF endpoint returned HTML, so no main-article PDF bytes are claimed.

The official SI is saved as `salt/nature_si.pdf` (13 pages; 1,461,035 bytes; SHA-256 `a703658e36d0800ebb36f9e87fe7344db766e154f779aa8ae0383d4be5fa40a0`), from <https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-10013-1/MediaObjects/41586_2025_10013_MOESM1_ESM.pdf>. All pages were text-read. The SI says pp. 2–13 contain Supplementary Figs. 1–7 and Tables 1–2; videos 1–2 are separately listed. Supplementary Table 2 (p. 13) gives the efficiency inputs including ΔS_mix = 607.26 J kg−1 K−1, pressure hysteresis 40 MPa, 60 wt% solution density 1.12 g cm−3, solid density 1.31 g cm−3, 300 K saturation concentration 63 wt%, and pressure-eutectic concentration 15 wt%. These are cycle-calculation parameters, not stress-dependent contact measurements.

The official Source Data bundle is saved as `salt/nature_source_data.rar` (20,835,966 bytes; SHA-256 `99e5d7144ad2706b4ee46a9ed1d40eb733ceeebbbb2242ea77f3a5c1e84ccd2e`). I inspected workbook tabs/headers and relevant sheets for Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, and Extended Data Fig. 7. Fig. 3 includes pressure-varied Raman data and DTA conditions; Fig. 4 includes temperature traces and modeled cycle outputs; Extended Data Fig. 7 includes pressure-step data. These are plotted Source Data tables, not claimed as raw instrument files. The Extended Data Table 3 image, which reports dissolution enthalpy/entropy estimates, is `salt/nature_table_3.jpg` (105,141 bytes; SHA-256 `53bf2dea7546bb60143ac539aef083221e02c44889a44d00231ee56bc30341da`); read it alongside the SI. Its reported solution-mass-specific ΔS values are not a partial molar dissolution entropy as a function of local contact stress. No analysis code was present in the source-data bundle.

## Calcite loaded-growth experiment

Lei Li et al., “Limits to Crystallization Pressure,” *Langmuir* 38(37), 11265–11273 (2022), DOI [10.1021/acs.langmuir.2c01325](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.2c01325), published online 2022-09-09. The public arXiv record is [1904.04491](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04491): v1 submitted 2019-04-09, v2 revised 2021-05-25. The current v2 PDF was acquired from <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.04491v2> as `calcite/arxiv_1904.04491_v2.pdf` (16,835,975 bytes; SHA-256 `61efc776b4345d1c14f2c75317753e3a94bf911386646db546f7c09bc58a8b18`). Read methods/setup pp. 4–6, the pressure/chemical-potential calculation p. 7, and loaded-growth result p. 11. The earlier v1 PDF is also saved for revision comparison as `calcite/arxiv_1904.04491_v1.pdf` (2,404,131 bytes; SHA-256 `4e8fc4f7fc61514ee5dbf6b8ea70fd6348a84ec963c9719fdb266e66108f7a11`).

The version-of-record full text was acquired as JATS XML from the Europe PMC REST endpoint <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9494941/fullTextXML>, saved as `calcite/pmc_fulltext.xml` (110,435 bytes; SHA-256 `7bf33fd0af165ac91855c6dd82c50f17b12aa05bff68088ccd32eca9e4a16094`). Read sections 2–5 (Experimental Section through Conclusions), especially Figs. 2–4 and the thermodynamics/kinetics discussion. The publisher SI is `calcite/figshare_si.pdf` (15 pp.; 5,376,183 bytes; SHA-256 `3f4dc695a25e0e37d2d6b410682a496f295db95a86844c8cd7531e9f3b6bba66`), item DOI `10.1021/acs.langmuir.2c01325.s001`, official record <https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Limits_to_Crystallization_Pressure/21126395>; all 15 pages were read. The related supplementary movie (`.s002`, DOI `10.1021/acs.langmuir.2c01325.s002`) is acquired at `calcite/figshare_growth_movie.avi` (2,832,164 bytes; SHA-256 `0c244e593aba050bb26a53b3592abd4bc8ec307d4d1bd521831d9585cf0682bc`), but is not represented here as visually reviewed. No separate raw instrument dataset or executable analysis code was identified in the article/SI/movie payload.

The experiment controls a calcite–glass contact in a microfluidic cell, uses 0.801 ± 0.002 mM CaCO3 solution at Ω = 0.44, and measures the contact geometry and vertical growth through RICM while changing applied load. In arXiv v2, upward growth is 2.6 nm/min before loading and falls below the 0.04 nm/h detection bound after loading; lateral growth continues. These co-measured contact/geometry/kinetic observations are from calcite in aqueous CaCO3 against glass, not NH4SCN–water. The equilibrium pressure threshold is calculated, not measured as a solubility curve. **Version-specific value:** arXiv v2 p. 7 gives 35 MPa at Ω = 0.44, matching the version linked by the supplied text; the 2022 version-of-record XML gives 29 MPa for the same stated Ω. Keep the citation/version with the number. Neither version measures partial entropy or a closed refrigeration-cycle work balance.

## Pressure-solution thermodynamic/model baseline

Luca Gagliardi and Olivier Pierre-Louis, “Thin film modeling of crystal dissolution and growth in confinement,” arXiv:1801.02552 v1 (submitted 2018-01-08), journal version *Phys. Rev. E* 97, 012802 (2018), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevE.97.012802](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.97.012802). The v1 PDF from <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.02552v1> is `pressure_solution/gagliardi_pierre_louis_v1.pdf` (1,981,126 bytes; SHA-256 `2d338913666dc1800c7d4b4af93256ee0db93162ad47891edd81c4efc4c6936c`); read pp. 1–4, including Eqs. 10–12 on p. 3. The source archive <https://arxiv.org/e-print/1801.02552v1> is `pressure_solution/gagliardi_pierre_louis_v1_source.tar.gz` (1,582,528 bytes; SHA-256 `284582cb9300fc5efd2a06a32b0f247af4843a68ba85e80791766d0805eca729`); its 13 members are the manuscript TeX/BibTeX and figures, with no executable code or data. This is a continuum/thin-film model, not an NH4SCN experiment. It models stress contributions to the crystal chemical potential together with confined-film transport and an assumed interface reaction law; its predictions depend on those interaction/kinetic assumptions. It supplies no same-specimen measurement of the quantities below.
