FINITE MEASUREMENT AND SAMPLING CONTRACT

This contract concerns implementation of the certified upper POVM, not
verification of an untrusted physical device. Its assumptions are a
known input qubit, calibrated Pauli axes Z and X, fresh identically
prepared copies when repeated estimation is desired, unbiased classical
random bits, exact rational classical arithmetic for the ideal model,
and access to projective qubit measurement along a selected known axis.
No quantum-information advantage follows merely from this construction.

1. Exact classical noise sampling from a finite table

Use the final N=10, rho=1/5 table p in
certified_upper_N10_mixture.json. Its full law is

  P{N_noise=(2m,2n)} = rho^(|m|+|n|) p_clamp / Zq.

Choose clamp indices A,B in {0,...,10} with exact probabilities

  W_AB = rho^(A+B) p_AB D_A D_B / Zq,

where D_0=1, D_A=2 for 0<A<10, and D_10=2/(1-rho). There are 121
weights. They sum exactly to 1, and all share an integer denominator

  D = 30784115231534218671915170354675,

which has 105 bits. A uniform integer in {0,...,D-1}, generated by
105-bit rejection sampling, selects the clamp pair by cumulative
integer weights. The expected random-bit cost of that step is <210.

For each coordinate independently conditional on its clamp index:

  A=0: output m=0;
  0<A<10: choose a fair sign and output m=+/-A;
  A=10: choose a fair sign and L with
        P(L=l)=(1-rho)rho^l, l=0,1,..., then m=+/-(10+L).

The two tail variables are conditionally independent. Dependence is
carried by the nonproduct finite clamp table. Geometric L can be sampled
as the number of zero base-5 digits before the first nonzero digit.
Its expected number of digits is 5/4; a base-5 digit from 3-bit rejection
costs 24/5 bits in expectation. The two possible tails therefore cost
at most 12 bits in expectation, and the signs cost at most two bits.
This particular exact noise sampler uses <224 unbiased random bits in
expectation. This is a randomness count, not a claim that arithmetic,
table preparation, or physical calibration is free.

The label size and running time are unbounded but almost surely finite,
with finite expectations. This exact law has countably infinitely many
reported lattice outcomes, not a finite outcome set.

2. At most one binary projective qubit measurement per run

For any output lattice point v, write its POVM effect as

  E_v = a_v (I+r_v dot (Z,X)), a_v>=0, |r_v|<=1.

Reflection symmetry gives a_-v=a_v and r_-v=-r_v. Choose one
representative v from each antipodal pair. Its two effects sum to
2a_v I. Thus the measurement can be implemented as follows:

  (i) Classically sample an antipodal pair with probability 2a_v.
  (ii) On the qubit perform the binary POVM
       F_+/-=(I+/-r_v dot (Z,X))/2.
  (iii) Report +v or -v according to its outcome.

To sample the pair in (i), sample the noise above, add an independent
uniform S in {(+/-1,+/-1)}, and fold V=N_noise+S to its antipodal pair.
The trace law of E is exactly law(V), so this has the required pair
weights. It uses <226 random bits in expectation before step (ii).

The binary POVM in (ii) needs no quantum ancilla in this ideal model:
with probability |r_v| perform a projective measurement along
r_v/|r_v|; otherwise report an independent fair bit. This gives exactly
F_+/-. The visibility |r_v| is the square root of an exact rational,
and its direction has algebraic coordinates. An exact classical
Bernoulli coin for a computable algebraic probability can be generated
by comparing unbiased random-bit prefixes with successively certified
probability intervals; this terminates almost surely. Physical
implementation still needs a chosen finite precision for its rotations
and coins.

Although v is unbounded, the clamped local formula gives only finitely
many r_v. Exact enumeration of this saved table gives 484 oriented Bloch
vectors, or 242 types after identifying r with -r. Hence a finite catalog
of at most 242 binary qubit measurement types suffices, with classical
tail labels. Equivalently, one calibrated rotation followed by one
projective Z measurement suffices when the projective branch is chosen.
Catalog preparation and calibration are separate supplied resources.

3. A finite-label approximation has an explicit tail contract

For an integer K>=0, retain only output labels with

  |v_i| <= 2(10+K)+1 for both coordinates,

and merge the remaining outcomes into one overflow flag. This is a
positive finite coarse-graining of the exact POVM, with at most

  [2(10+K)+2]^2+1

reported labels. Because the overflow set is antipodally symmetric,
its total effect is a scalar multiple of I. Its probability is therefore
independent of the input qubit state and is bounded by

  P(overflow) <= 2*(1/5)^(K+1).

The finite coarse-grained POVM remains valid, but its finite-label
Fourier characteristic is not the exact full scalar convolution
characteristic. An overflow approximation must keep this distinction.

If the implemented Bloch vectors satisfy sup_v |r_v-r_tilde_v|<=delta
and the classical pair law is sampled within total variation eta, the
complete output distributions differ from the ideal untruncated law
by at most eta+delta/2 in total variation for every input state. Adding
the overflow approximation changes this bound by at most the tail
probability when comparing ordinary labels with a chosen tail handling
convention. Actual precision, calibration, reset, and copy acquisition
costs are not estimated or experimentally validated here.

4. Finite-copy mean-estimation contract and matched comparator

Let the input state have r_z=Tr(rho_state Z), r_x=Tr(rho_state X), and
write C for the certified upper law's exact covariance trace. The
construction is centered and reflection symmetric, so for a single
reported pair (V_z,V_x),

  E[V_z]=r_z, E[V_x]=r_x,
  E[V_z^2]=E[V_x^2]=1+C/2,
  E[V_z V_x]=0.

With n independent identical qubit preparations and independent
measurement randomization, the vector sample mean has exact trace
mean-square error

  MSE_joint = (2+C-r_z^2-r_x^2)/n.

A distribution-free Chebyshev/union bound gives

  P(max_i |sample_mean_i-r_i|>=epsilon)
    <= (2+C)/(n epsilon^2).

Thus n >= (2+C)/(delta epsilon^2) is one explicit sufficient finite-copy
contract for error epsilon with failure probability at most delta.
It is a conservative bound, not an asserted optimal sampling rate.

For even n, a matched baseline that measures Z projectively on n/2
copies and X projectively on n/2 copies has

  MSE_separate = (4-2r_z^2-2r_x^2)/n.

Their difference is

  MSE_joint-MSE_separate
    = (C-2+r_z^2+r_x^2)/n > 0.

Consequently this particular dependent POVM gives no advantage over
that standard matched-copy baseline for trace mean-square error in
estimating the two Pauli means. Its established benefit is the reduced
scalar-convolution covariance relative to the supplied independent-noise
benchmark 4. A different operational task would need its own comparison.

Finite physical samples cannot independently certify the full
all-frequency characteristic identity without a trusted model or
calibration assumption. The universal positivity statement here is a
mathematical certificate for the specified construction.
