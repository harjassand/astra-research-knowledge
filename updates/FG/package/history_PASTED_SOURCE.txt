Ground-state-protected, direction-coded molecular-history spectroscopy
The original independently addressed two-branch sequence should be replaced. A better experimental construction is to protect the atomic path label during infrared exposure, use travelling-wave pulses to encode molecular histories through photon momentum, and measure the complete interferometer visibility with a matched reference that does not require molecular coherence.
This investigation produced a concrete readout repair and a multilevel—not merely two-level—measurement construction. It also exposed a serious limitation: the calculated nonlinear signal at the original energy budget is too small to support the earlier optimistic acquisition estimate.
The defensible outcome is therefore an experimentally testable operation that removes the leading atomic Stark path-mixing mechanism and makes the remaining visibility measurable. Affordable acquisition of consequential molecular dynamics is not yet established.
The proposed sequence is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Create spatial cat}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{store its labels in ground-state spin}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{apply momentum-coded IR histories}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{restore labels and recombine}
}
\]
A companion sequence applies the same infrared histories with zero axial photon momentum. That reference is central: it tests the readout under illumination without assuming that the molecule retains the coherence we are trying to discover.
1. The first correction: absorption is not the required coherent response
The demonstrated CaOH\(^+\)/Ca\(^+\) apparatus provides a cat amplitude around \(6.5\), infrared Lamb–Dicke parameter around \(0.03\), and recoil-transfer contrast \(0.52(3)\). Its source operates at \(100\ \mathrm{kHz}\); successive source pulses lack the phase coherence required here. The published excitation model also misses the absolute absorption magnitude. These observations do not determine the coherent single-pulse response of the proposed experiment. arXiv
Consequently, the previous illustrative excitation probability cannot be promoted into an experimental prediction. Nor can the measured recoil-transfer contrast be treated as visibility after additional optical fields, memory transfers and phase cycling.
The authors’ analysis code supplies a reference intensity of \(1.1\times10^8\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), giving a source-pulse fluence of approximately
\[
F_{\mathrm{source}}=0.11\ \mathrm{J\,cm^{-2}},
\]
and a calculated transition-dipole parameter
\[
d=4.81\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{C\,m}.
\]
I used these as reference-model inputs, not newly measured molecular parameters. 
The central experimental quantity must instead be measured jointly:
\[
\boxed{
\text{usable molecular signal}
\times
\text{visibility retained under the complete sequence}.
}
\]
Optimising either factor in isolation is insufficient.
2. Remove the leading atomic Stark error by changing where the path label lives
Why the previous encoding was vulnerable
In the original construction, the two spatial branches are labelled by superpositions of the atomic \(S\) and \(D\) states. A differential infrared Stark shift between those states rotates the labels and can corrupt recombination.
Equal-intensity pulse patterns suppress some systematic differences, but do not remove that operator.
The replacement: ground-Zeeman parking
Before applying infrared pulses, coherently map the two labels into the two \(S_{1/2}\) Zeeman states of \(^{40}\mathrm{Ca}^{+}\):
\[
\frac{|+\rangle|\alpha\rangle+|-\rangle|-\alpha\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\longrightarrow
\frac{
|S,-\tfrac12\rangle|\alpha\rangle+
e^{i\phi}|S,+\tfrac12\rangle|-\alpha\rangle
}{\sqrt2}.
\]
For linearly polarised light, the vector electric-dipole light shift vanishes. A \(J=\tfrac12\) state has no rank-two tensor contribution. To this order, the infrared interaction within the parked doublet is therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_{\mathrm{IR,parked}}^{(2)}
=
h_{\mathrm{scalar}}(\mathbf x,t)\,I_{\mathrm{ancilla}}.
}
\]
The leading differential Stark term disappears from the path-label degree of freedom. This is a symmetry-based removal, not subtraction of an uncertain large phase or selection of a convenient coherence time. The relevant atomic polarizability structure and coherent ground-Zeeman control have established precedents. arXiv
A possible mapping uses \(D_{5/2},m=-3/2\) as a shared intermediate. Its quadrupole connections to the two ground Zeeman states have allowed \(\Delta m=-1,-2\). A direct transfer from \(D,-5/2\) to \(S,+1/2\), by contrast, would require forbidden \(\Delta m=3\). The exact transfer sequence must respect the apparatus’s implemented sublevels.
What this does not remove
Ellipticity, finite-field corrections, magnetic-field gradients, unwanted Raman coupling, residual \(D\)-state population and imperfect transfers remain.
In particular, mapping fidelity must be measured on the cat, not borrowed from carrier rotations on a motional ground state. Carrier coupling depends on motional occupation; a large coherent displacement changes that distribution. A nearly transverse mapping beam reduces axial recoil, but adds requirements on spectator-mode preparation.
This is not a cosmetic qualification. Spectator modes have experimentally altered the response of molecular quantum-logic readout, demonstrating why a one-mode visibility calculation is insufficient. Nature
The experimental gate is the complete forward-and-back parked-memory operation under infrared exposure—not an assumed product of favourable component fidelities.
3. Measure loaded visibility without first knowing molecular coherence
This is the most useful new control operation.
Take the cat separation along \(z\), with infrared polarisation along \(y\). Apply the reference pulses perpendicular to \(z\), so their axial wavevector is zero:
\[
k_z=0.
\]
Both branch centres then receive the same molecular drive:
\[
E_A(t)=E_B(t).
\]
In the ideal axial model, the internal histories are identical. The molecule may undergo complicated coherent evolution, population redistribution or common internal decoherence; none of those alone creates an axial path distinction.
Run this illumination sequence with a known electrical calibration kick, then measure the ancilla return fringe. This determines the readout response while the real molecule experiences the real infrared dose.
The reference must include the actual pulse phases and delays, rather than only a laser-on/laser-off comparison.
Molecular coherence does not have to last until fluorescence detection
There is a useful separation between two requirements:
- Molecular coherence must survive the femtosecond-to-picosecond history being interrogated.
- The encoded interferometer signal must survive until readout.
For any common trace-preserving molecular channel \(\mathcal E\),
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E(K)=\operatorname{Tr}K,
\]
including when \(K\) is the non-Hermitian cross-block between the branches. Thus, internal relaxation after encoding does not automatically erase the ancilla signal.
This does not excuse photon recoil while the branches remain spatially separated. Emission carrying which-path information, state-dependent trapping forces and motion-dependent relaxation must remain in the model.
The transverse reference also changes radial photon momentum. All-mode recoil and route-dependent pulse differences therefore require calibration. A single multiplicative contrast \(C\) is permissible only if the measured transfer response supports it; otherwise the analysis needs a phase-dependent transfer matrix or a full likelihood model.
What becomes obtainable is the loaded readout response without assuming the unknown molecular \(T_2\). That breaks the circular dependence in the previous proposal.
4. Replace independent branch addressing with momentum-coded histories
Independent optical control at both branch centres is more control than the first scientific measurement needs.
Instead, use four non-overlapping travelling-wave pulses and change the axial momentum of one pulse. Photon momentum supplies a second weighting of the nonlinear molecular response.
A broadband-compatible geometry
For a normally illuminated grating with reciprocal vector along \(z\), diffraction gives
\[
k_z=qK,\qquad K=\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda},\qquad q=0,\pm1.
\]
Crucially, \(K\) is independent of optical frequency. The spatial field can be represented as
\[
E_q(z,x,t)=
\int d\omega\,\widetilde E_q(\omega)
e^{iqKz}
e^{i\sqrt{(\omega/c)^2-q^2K^2}\,x-i\omega t}.
\]
This avoids silently treating the broadband pulse as monochromatic when calculating its branch phase.
As a design point, choosing \(K=0.9k_0\) near \(3783\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) gives
\[
\Lambda\approx2.94\ \mu\mathrm m,
\qquad
s=Kd\approx0.702,
\]
using the reference cat geometry. This requires neither a larger cat nor simultaneous counterpropagating fields during molecular interrogation.
The actual diffraction efficiency, spectral phase, delivered fluence and waveform matching must be measured. The grating is a field construction, not an assumption of lossless optics.
Construct all four pulses from replicas of one source pulse. Their common optical phase cancels in the selected phase harmonic; relative phase and delay stability remain necessary.
A specific scientific question
The target is:
Does a waiting-time oscillation arise from a ground-band rotational/Raman coherence, or from dynamics initiated in the excited vibrational band?

Those mechanisms can contribute to overlapping nonlinear signals. Separating them matters before interpreting an oscillation as excited-state energy redistribution.
For CaOH\(^+\), this is a test of what the observed dynamics represent—not a claim that an important reaction pathway or strong vibrational coupling has already been identified.
5. The measurement survives beyond an ideal two-level model
Let \(G\) and \(E\) be two pump-connected energy bands, each containing multiple rotational and internal states. They need not correspond exactly to O–H occupation numbers: dark combination states can belong to the upper energy band.
For a coherent, phase-covariant pulse in these two bands,
\[
U(\phi)=U_d+e^{i\phi}U_++e^{-i\phi}U_-,
\]
where \(U_d\) is block diagonal and \(U_\pm\) transfer amplitude between bands.
This decomposition is exact for arbitrary pulse strength within its stated model. It does not require an impulsive two-level rotation.
Apply four pulses with intervals \((t_1,T,t_3)\), and extract the complex phase harmonic
\[
\ell=(-1,+1,+1,-1).
\]
Hold the first, third and fourth pulse momenta fixed, while switching the second between \(+K\) and \(-K\).
After the first selected interaction, the second pulse produces two contributions:
\[
K\longmapsto
e^{iq_2s/2}U_+KU_d^\dagger
+
e^{-iq_2s/2}U_dKU_-^\dagger.
\]
They occupy the \(E\!-\!E\) and \(G\!-\!G\) blocks, respectively, at the beginning of the waiting interval. Their subsequent native dynamics contribute signals \(X_E\) and \(X_G\).
After calibrated instrumental and motional corrections,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_+&=e^{ia}X_E+e^{-ia}X_G,\\
S_-&=e^{-ia}X_E+e^{ia}X_G,
\qquad a=s/2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{
X_E=
\frac{e^{ia}S_+-e^{-ia}S_-}{2i\sin s},
\qquad
X_G=
\frac{-e^{-ia}S_++e^{ia}S_-}{2i\sin s}.
}
\]
For the proposed geometry, the inversion has condition number approximately \(2.73\): imperfect, but not close to singular.
A third setting, with only pulse 2 having zero axial momentum, provides the consistency test
\[
\boxed{
S_++S_-=2\cos(s/2)\,S_0.
}
\]
This is distinct from the all-four-pulses transverse visibility reference.
I checked the separator against explicit phase cycling for strong pulses acting on multiple states in both bands. The numerical assertions passed.
The important failure boundary
Excited-state absorption into additional bands can invalidate this construction. A three-band counterexample in the accompanying code violates the consistency relation.
Conversely, passing that relation does not prove that leakage is absent: different errors can cancel.
If strong-pulse band closure cannot be established, a controlled alternative is to use weak first and second write pulses, extract their bilinear response by amplitude extrapolation, and retain stronger final read pulses. That preserves the distinction between the two origins at leading write order.
It does not grant free sensitivity. Extrapolation errors, smaller signals and higher read-pulse phase harmonics must all be counted.
Also, a four-pulse coefficient is not automatically a connected correlation. Connected responses require subtraction of measured lower-order products with propagated uncertainty. Thermal mixtures can produce non-Gaussian signals without establishing anharmonic energy transfer.
6. One physical model—not separately favourable error budgets
The common model should begin with
\[
H=
H_{\mathrm{rv}}
+\sum_\nu\hbar\omega_\nu a_\nu^\dagger a_\nu
+H_{\mathrm{Ca}}+H_{729}(t)
-\mathbf d_m\!\cdot\!\mathbf E(\hat{\mathbf x}_m,t)
-\mathbf d_a\!\cdot\!\mathbf E(\hat{\mathbf x}_a,t)
+H_{\mathrm{environment}}.
\]
Here \(H_{\mathrm{rv}}\) includes rotation, the relevant vibrations, anharmonic couplings and internal energy redistribution. The polarizability description of the atom is an effective reduction of its dipole coupling, not an extra interaction to double-count.
For the branch coherence, the relevant open-system evolution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot K={}&-\frac{i}{\hbar}(H_AK-KH_B)\\
&+\sum_\lambda\left[
J_{\lambda A}KJ_{\lambda B}^{\dagger}
-\frac12J_{\lambda A}^{\dagger}J_{\lambda A}K
-\frac12KJ_{\lambda B}^{\dagger}J_{\lambda B}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Molecular emission operators include recoil. Their radiative rates follow from the same transition dipoles used for excitation. Internal redistribution represented explicitly in \(H_{\mathrm{rv}}\) must not be counted again as an independently selected dephasing time.

Writing this common model is not equivalent to having solved it for the actual molecule. The numerical calculation below intentionally does not fill missing molecular parameters with favourable values.

The reference calculation uses the cation rotational constant \(B_0=0.3655\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}\), all rotational magnetic sublevels, and a \(300\ \mathrm K\) rotational mixture. The rotational constant comes from CaOH\(^+\)-specific calculations. arXiv
The Gaussian pulse duration is set to \(116.2\ \mathrm{fs}\), the transform-limited assumption associated with the reported spectrum—not a measured temporal waveform. arXiv
Important remaining assumptions are explicit: \(B_1=B_0\), two vibrational bands, no hot-band shifts, no intramolecular redistribution and no instrumental noise. Thus this is a reference calculation, not a calibrated performance forecast.

8. The corrected acquisition cost changes the conclusion
The earlier two-pulse estimate did not include extracting a small four-pulse Fourier coefficient and then separating its two contributions.
For a uniform phase-cycling estimator and equal allocation between the two momentum settings, I obtain the conservative planning bound
\[
\operatorname{MSE}(\widehat X)
\le
\frac{4}{N C^2\sin^2s}.
\]
A five-standard-error criterion gives
\[
\boxed{
N\gtrsim
\frac{100}{C^2\sin^2s\,|X|^2}.
}
\]
This is a budget for the specified estimator, not a fundamental lower bound. Better inference may improve constants; it does not justify omitting phase cycling or inversion noise.
For an assumed loaded visibility \(C=0.4\), \(s=0.702\), and assumed rate of 50 independent complete trials per second:
Coefficient magnitude	Required trials	Time for one complex coefficient
\(0.0018\)	\(4.6\times10^8\)	Approximately 107 days
\(0.0077\)	\(2.5\times10^7\)	Approximately 5.9 days
\(0.03\)	\(1.7\times10^6\)	Approximately 9.3 hours


These are conditional laboratory acquisition times, not measured throughput.
For the two-band construction, a \(5^3\) optical phase grid, two momentum settings and two ancilla quadratures provide 500 settings. Their samples are already allocated within \(N\); multiplying the table by 500 again would double-count.
Controls and delay scans are additional. Measuring a 10% dynamical change uses \(|\Delta X|\), not the full \(|X|\), and can require roughly 100 times more trials before accounting for the second delay’s variance.
This calculation does not support affordable multidimensional spectroscopy at the original reference energy.
Survival must be counted fairly
It is unnecessary to require one particular molecule to survive the entire experiment unless single-individual dynamics are the objective.
With irreversible loss probability \(q_{\mathrm{loss}}\) per trial, a simple replacement model gives
\[
T_{\mathrm{total}}
\approx
\frac{N}{f_{\mathrm{trial}}}
+
Nq_{\mathrm{loss}}t_{\mathrm{reload}}.
\]
This avoids an artificially stringent single-specimen survival requirement, but does not make reloading free. Preparation reproducibility, ion-order changes, drift and correlated trials must enter the effective sample count.
No loss probability for the new sequence has been measured.
9. The smallest decisive experiment
First: test the readout repair, not a full nonlinear spectrum
Create the cat, park its labels, apply the complete infrared sequence through the zero-axial-momentum reference, restore the labels and recombine. Add calibrated electrical kicks and measure the full phase-dependent return.
This directly answers whether the apparatus retains usable visibility under the required intervention.
A provisional requirement such as
\[
C_{\mathrm{loaded}}\ge0.4
\]
is an engineering gate, not an observed value. Lower visibility can be accepted only with its corresponding acquisition penalty.
The test must include actual pulse-envelope and overlap-pedestal measurements, memory return on the displaced motional state, all-mode thermometry, residual polarisation sensitivity and readout calibration.
Second: acquire a small number of pathway-sensitive points
Use four replicas with \(t_1\approx t_3\approx1\ \mathrm{ps}\), then probe a limited set of waiting times rather than attempting a full spectrum.
Interleave \(q_2=+1,-1,0\) with the all-pulses transverse reference. The necessary discriminants are:
- Removing any one pulse or randomising the relevant relative phase must eliminate the selected four-pulse coherent component.
- The direction-coded data must satisfy the calibrated third-port relation within the stated band model.
- Detuning, full momentum reversal and comparison with the unparked encoding must distinguish molecular response from atomic backgrounds and optical artefacts.
Momentum reversal has a specific Fourier consequence,
\[
S_\ell(-\mathbf q)=S_{-\ell}(\mathbf q)^*,
\]
not an unrestricted assertion that the same coefficient simply conjugates.
For a prospective \(|X|=0.03\), an illustrative systematic-error requirement is that the raw targeted Fourier background remain below
\[
0.1\,C\sin(s)|X|\approx8\times10^{-4}
\]
at \(C=0.4\). Smaller scientific changes require tighter control.
The calibration must also determine \(s\) more accurately than a rough cat-amplitude estimate; approximately \(0.005\ \mathrm{rad}\) is a reasonable provisional target, subject to the actual required uncertainty.
Rejection criteria
If parking plus illumination destroys the return contrast, reject this implementation. If the signal follows pulse overlap or atomic backgrounds, reject the molecular interpretation. If visibility survives but the measured coefficient remains too small, reject the claim of affordable nonlinear spectroscopy at that resource budget.
None of those outcomes should be repaired by assuming a longer molecular coherence time or a larger noiseless cat.
10. What existing methods already do—and what remains distinctive
The comparison substantially narrows the novelty claim.
Recoil pump–probe spectroscopy is prior art. Schindler’s earlier proposal already considered ultrafast molecular interrogation through recoil, including stimulated-return measurements. General recoil access to molecular dynamics is therefore not a new contribution here. arXiv
Phase-sensitive molecular readout and coherent rovibrational control are established capabilities. Molecular-force interference has distinguished states and monitored inelastic processes; a March 2026 report describes quantum-logic spectroscopy and coherent control of forbidden vibrational transitions in individual nitrogen molecular ions. Those achievements are strong benchmarks, but do not supply CaOH\(^+\) femtosecond coherence or the fidelity of this proposed apparatus. Nature
Multidimensional spectroscopy already accesses richer molecular dynamics. Gas-phase two-dimensional infrared experiments on glutathione measured coherent vibrational behaviour across several modes. Furthermore, the 2025 mixed frequency–time pulse-shaping work explicitly selects nonlinear pathways, including two-quantum coherences. “Pathway selection” is not, by itself, an invention. American Chemical Society Publications
The candidate distinction is narrower:
Use protected single-ion recoil readout to obtain two branch-origin weightings of an overlapping nonlinear response, without a molecular tag and without resolving every contributing rotational transition.

The inversion establishes a measurement distinction relative to one fixed response combination. It does not prove that all existing pulse-shaping, polarisation or action-detection methods cannot recover equivalent information.
A convincing advantage would require a matched experiment showing that the added momentum weighting distinguishes competing molecular mechanisms with lower total cost—or in a specimen where the strongest alternative readout is unavailable. That comparison has not been demonstrated.
Research outcome
The leading atomic Stark path-mixing problem now has an explicit symmetry-based repair. More importantly, the remaining infrared-loaded readout can be tested without assuming the molecular coherence being sought. This is the most credible experimental advance from the investigation.
The science extension also goes beyond the original two-level algebra: it separates ground- and excited-band origins for strong pulses in a multilevel model, has a measurable consistency relation, and has an explicit counterexample identifying where it fails.
However, the finite-bandwidth rotational calculation exposes a substantial signal-cost problem. Neither affordable consequential molecular spectroscopy nor a civilisation-changing capability is established by these results. The next decisive evidence is a measured joint response, loaded visibility and loss rate under the complete protected sequence—not another assumed overlap amplitude.
