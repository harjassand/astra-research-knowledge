2. The actual local-to-global construction
The constructive object is a positive representation of heat dissipation after adding an exact differential form.
At a point of a smooth map \(f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2\), write
\[
x=f_{x_1},\quad y=f_{x_2},\qquad
X=f_{x_1x_1},\quad Y=f_{x_1x_2},\quad Z=f_{x_2x_2}.
\]
Then
\[
A=(x\mid y),\qquad
B_1=(X\mid Y),\qquad B_2=(Y\mid Z).
\]
The shared \(Y\) is essential: these are derivatives of a compatible gradient, not independent matrix-valued fields.
Define two six-dimensional vectors of bilinear quantities:
\[
z_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
x\cdot X\\
x\cdot Z\\
y\cdot Y\\
x\times Y\\
y\times X\\
y\times Z
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
z_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
-y\cdot Z\\
-y\cdot X\\
-x\cdot Y\\
y\times Y\\
x\times Z\\
x\times X
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(a\times b=a_1b_2-a_2b_1\).
On matrix space, introduce the linear one-forms
\[
\theta=x\times dx+y\times dy,
\qquad
\zeta=x\cdot dy-y\cdot dx,
\qquad
\theta_a=x\times dx-y\times dy.
\tag{6}
\]
The \(dx,dy\) in this display are coordinate differentials of the columns.
The additional anisotropic correction is built from
\[
\boxed{(u-v)\theta_a.}
\tag{7}
\]
It retains input anisotropy that a fully rotationally invariant correction would discard.

Why this proves quasiconvexity
Work first on a square torus \(Q\). For a smooth periodic perturbation, set
\[
\varphi_t=e^{t\Delta}\varphi,
\qquad
A_t=A_0+D\varphi_t.
\]
The heat flow preserves gradient compatibility.
Define
\[
\mathcal F(t)
=
\int_Q[F_*(A_t)-F_*(A_0)].
\]
Differentiation and integration by parts yield
\[
-\mathcal F'(t)
=
\int_Q\sum_{i=1}^2
D^2F_*(A_t)[\partial_iA_t,\partial_iA_t].
\]
The exact-form correction has zero integral:
\[
\int_Q(d\alpha_{\mathrm{int}})_{A_t}
(\partial_1A_t,\partial_2A_t)
=
\int_Qd(A_t^*\alpha_{\mathrm{int}})
=0.
\]
This is the existing heat/exact-form mechanism; the specific correction in (8) and positive identity (10) supply its anisotropic application here. arXiv
Consequently,
\[
-\mathcal F'(t)
=
\int_Q(z_+^TGz_++z_-^TGz_-)\ge0.
\]
As \(t\to\infty\), the periodic gradient perturbation vanishes. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\int_Q[F_*(A_0+D\varphi)-F_*(A_0)]
=
\int_0^\infty\!\int_Q
(z_+^TGz_++z_-^TGz_-)\,dx\,dt
\ge0.
}
\tag{12}
\]
This is the required positive construction: a nonnegative space-time integral representing the original variational gap.
For a compactly supported perturbation in a bounded domain, extend it by zero into a larger periodic square. Approximation in \(W^{1,4}\) then covers the stated zero-boundary class.
There is no restriction to a Fourier bandwidth, a number of waves, or nonresonant interactions. Arbitrary compatible multidirectional oscillations are included.

4. Why the threshold is sharp
The upper bound is not a limitation of the correction.
For the rank-one direction \(e_1\otimes(1,k)\), direct differentiation produces an acoustic matrix \(M_\ell(k)\) such that
\[
D^2F_\ell(A)
[e_1\otimes(1,k),e_1\otimes(1,k)]
=
\operatorname{vec}(A)^TM_\ell(k)\operatorname{vec}(A).
\]
Put
\[
v_\ell=\ell^2,\qquad
z=\frac{k^2}{(1+k^2)^2}.
\]
Its determinant satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\det M_\ell(k)}{(1+k^2)^4}
={}&3(32-v_\ell)(28-3v_\ell)\\
&+(504v_\ell-5952)z
+768(v_\ell-5/2)z^2.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{18}
\]
The discriminant of this quadratic in \(z\) is
\[
-576\bigl(
48v_\ell^3-2545v_\ell^2+29712v_\ell-97344
\bigr).
\tag{19}
\]
This is the origin of the algebraic endpoint.
At \(\ell=\kappa\), the relevant double zero occurs at
\[
z_*=
\frac{248-21\kappa^2}{64\kappa^2-160}
\in(0,1/4).
\tag{20}
\]
Choose \(k>0\) satisfying
\[
\frac{k^2}{(1+k^2)^2}=z_*.
\]
Then \(M_\kappa(k)\) is singular, so take a nonzero vector \(a_*\) in its kernel.
At \(\ell=0\), the acoustic matrix is positive definite. Its two diagonal blocks have determinants
\[
96(k^2-1/4)^2+90>0,

28+64k^2+28k^4>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Therefore
\[
E_0=a_*^TM_0(k)a_*>0.

The acoustic expression is affine in \(\ell\), and it vanishes at \(\kappa\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
a_*^TM_\ell(k)a_*
=
E_0\left(1-\frac{\ell}{\kappa}\right)<0
\qquad(\ell>\kappa).

\]
Rank-one convexity therefore fails for every parameter beyond the endpoint. Reflection handles \(\ell<-\kappa\).
The full proof also supplies an explicit algebraic null vector and verifies it exactly. Thus both sides of the classification are constructive: a positive compatible-gradient certificate up to the endpoint, and a rank-one witness beyond it.

6. What supplied the decisive mathematical leverage
The useful step was to use local equality cases to design the global positive correction.
For a plane-wave second derivative,
\[
H=a\otimes b\otimes b,
\]
the two derivatives of the gradient are proportional:
\[
B_1=b_1(a\otimes b),\qquad
B_2=b_2(a\otimes b).
\]
Every two-form vanishes on this pair.
Consequently, when
\[
D^2F(A)[a\otimes b,a\otimes b]=0,
\]
any positive corrected heat representation must also vanish on the associated bilinear jet vector. Its Gram matrix must annihilate that vector.
At the endpoint, these acoustic equality cases forced two independent kernel directions in the six-dimensional scalar representation. That identified a rank-four face on which the correction could be constructed by exact linear algebra over \(\mathbb Q[\kappa]\).
The resulting matrix was not merely approximately positive: its remaining four-dimensional block was strictly positive, and its Schur complement vanished identically.
This is an application of PSD kernel constraints and facial reduction, not a claim to have invented those general facts. Its demonstrated value here is that a local degeneracy determined the correction proving a sharp global theorem.
There is also a useful check against merely repackaging the known isotropic result. The proof establishes that \(F_*\) cannot be obtained by positive sums of the known isotropic boundary energies after invertible input-coordinate transformations, even with additional nonnegative determinant squares. Such a representation would require
\[
\frac{841}{1600}\le\frac5{12},
\]
which is false. This excludes that particular shortcut—not all conceivable representations or all uses of prior quasiconvexity results.
7. What remains unresolved
The result does not show that every rank-one-convex left-\(SO(2)\) quartic has a positive compatible heat correction. Unequal \(qu\) and \(qv\) coefficients, the \(qw\) term, and the rest of the nine-coefficient cone remain outside the classification proved here.
In particular, the universal statement
\[
\text{gradient compatibility}
\Longrightarrow
\text{the required nine-moment PSD completion}
\]
has not been established.
The strongest preserved extension mechanism is now more specific: use the complete acoustic equality geometry to construct the compatible correction on its forced matrix face. It remains unproved that this succeeds throughout the cone, including when another heat metric or tensor components are required. The mathematically distinct alternative remains an actual resonant gradient construction with exact moments and independently certified rank-one convexity; no such counterexample was produced here.
Historical priority is also separate from correctness.
