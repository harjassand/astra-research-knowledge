1. Reflection-invariant classification for every m >= 2

The six-term Gram invariant representation is valid. Restrict to a fixed
two-dimensional output plane. SO(2) invariance gives the nine-dimensional
basis (u^2,v^2,uv,w^2,uw,vw,qu,qv,qw); output reflection deletes precisely
the last three. For arbitrary m, an orthogonal map carries span(x,y)
into that plane, so the same six coefficients describe every matrix.
The source's exact finite dimension calculation supports the planar step;
the arbitrary-m step is a written geometric argument, not a numerical test.

For positive binary rank-one restriction P, real root pairing factors P
into positive-definite quadratics. Whitening and simultaneous congruence
diagonalization reduce it to s^4+2r s^2t^2+t^4 with r>=1. Convexity of
P at (1,1) in direction (1,-1) forces r<=3. Restriction to rank-one
matrices leaves the unique undetermined even term lambda(uv-w^2).
The orthogonal-output acoustic test forces lambda>=r-1.

The displayed decomposition into ((3-r)/2)H1+((r-1)/2)H3+
(lambda-r+1)Delta is exact. H1=tr((A^T A)^2) has the stated positive
Hessian for every output dimension; H3 is the half-sum of two fourth
powers of Euclidean norms. Therefore the convex-plus-squared-minors
decomposition proves polyconvexity and quasiconvexity without a heat flow.

The degeneration argument avoids any unproved closedness of a polyconvex
cone. P^(1/4) is the Minkowski functional of a convex balanced unit ball,
hence a seminorm. Its zero direction is a translation-invariance direction
by the triangle inequality applied in both directions. Thus a degenerate
nonzero P is a fourth power of one linear coordinate; P=0 is included.
The remaining F=a u^2+lambda Delta has lambda>=0 by an acoustic test.

Coordinate transport is correct: if Fhat(A)=c F(AS), c>0, then
F(X)=c^-1 Ghat(X S^-1)+c^-1 gammahat det(S)^-2 Delta(X).
Both convexity and the correction sign survive. The factor det(S)^2
was independently checked using S=[[2,1],[-1,3]], giving exactly 49.
No universal counterexample emerged from this proof audit.

2. Orientation terms, Calderon, and the moment cone

The two-slack coefficient inequalities follow from the stated 3 by 3
principal acoustic blocks. For example the p-,z+,z- block gives
(S+nu)^2/(1+r)+4(ell-mu)^2/D<=16 epsilon; the z-,p+,p- block gives
3(S+nu)^2/(1+r)+4(ell-mu)^2/epsilon<=16D. The minus direction replaces
S+nu by S-nu. Maximizing gives |S|+|nu|. At either zero slack, PSD null
rows force all three odd coefficients to vanish. This argument does not
assume the necessary inequalities are sufficient. In the degenerate
rank-one restriction case, fresh symbolic zero-row calculations also
force ell=mu=nu=0 before testing lambda>=0.

The full acoustic reduction is valid specifically because left SO(2)
invariance rotates each output vector to e1 while preserving backgrounds.
The resulting form is biquadratic in four background entries and two
input-direction entries. Calderon's established binary biquadratic SOS
theorem applies. Every Gram diagonal block is fixed; the only invisible
off-diagonal change is a skew 4 by 4 matrix. The criterion therefore
has six auxiliary variables and is exact. It must not be transferred
to arbitrary 35-coefficient planar quartics by deleting output directions.

The source's moment-completion proof has a real strict feasible point.
Fresh differentiation gives eigenvalues 1,4,4,4,4,7,7,17 for its
|A|^4 Gram with K12=1. Thus the primal objective bounded below by
zero satisfies Slater; zero is attained at the origin, and the dual
matrix exists. Pairing with all skew K forces Y12 to be symmetric.

One can additionally check the relevant closure explicitly. Let D be
the image of Y>=0, <Cj,Y>=0 under Y -> (<Bi,Y>)_i. For a convergent
sequence mn in D choose Yn. Since Qnorm>=I,
tr Yn <= <Qnorm,Yn> = pnorm.mn, where pnorm=(1,1,2,0,...,0).
The right side is bounded. PSD matrices with bounded trace are bounded,
so a subsequence yields a feasible limit Y. Hence D is closed. This
rules out the generic unclosed-semidefinite-image pitfall in this instance.

None of these arguments proves that curl-compatible gradient moments
belong to D. Mean A=A0 and mean determinant=det A0 alone do not suffice:
the source's non-polyconvexity measure is an exact counterexample to
that weaker replacement, separated by the already certified Fstar.
The source does not make that replacement.

3. Original Fstar checks and Fourier compatibility

Fresh symbolic differentiation independently confirms the rational acoustic
certificate and positivity of Qstar-(1/16)I via all eight leading minors.
Fstar(I)=-3/10, Fstar(diag(1,-1))=113/10. The four-atom probability law
has zero barycentre and mean determinant zero, and mean Fstar=-833/250.
This is a valid non-polyconvexity witness. It is not a quasiconvexity
counterexample. Indeed, an exact periodic diagonal gradient must have
its two diagonal entries depending separately on the two coordinates;
its joint law is a product, while this four-atom law is not. This simple
observation concerns exact periodic realizations; stronger gradient-Young-
measure exclusions require their actual compensated-compactness premises.

The Fourier derivative convention is correct: for potential
c cos(k.x)+s sin(k.x), its positive-frequency gradient coefficient is
(s+i c) outer k / 2. Thus k2 D1 phi_i(k)-k1 D2 phi_i(k)=0 for every
mode, including arbitrary different vector cosine/sine polarizations.

independent_audit.py reconstructs coefficients from differentiating the
potential, then uses a separate direct fourfold convolution/constant-term
algorithm, without importing the source's moment construction. Twelve
exact rational cases agree in all nine moments, covering resonant waves,
mixed polarizations, duplicate/opposite/zero frequencies, backgrounds,
and frequencies of magnitude 10^12. Modewise curl is checked exactly.
These finite checks corroborate the reviewed code; they are not a proof
of quasiconvexity or an exhaustive software-input validation.

The stated O(M^2) arithmetic count is consistent with four quadratic
pair-frequency convolutions and nine zero-frequency inner products;
rational bit costs and dictionary/frequency arithmetic remain charged.
Nonresonant degree-four phase matching and iterated rank-one Jensen are
valid; resonance is necessary for that counterexample attack, not sufficient.

Analytic implication, reconstructed independently:
Take v_tau=A0 x+exp(tau Delta)psi with psi smooth and compactly supported
on R^2. For each tau>0, its perturbation and derivatives are Schwartz.
The pullback (Dv_tau)^*alpha is Schwartz because every coefficient
contains a derivative of Dv_tau and alpha is polynomial. Stokes gives
integral omega(Dv_tau)(partial1 Dv_tau,partial2 Dv_tau)=0. All terms are
integrable. Integrating the certificate makes the heat dissipation
nonnegative.

Define the relative energy by subtracting G(A0) and DG(A0):Dpsi_tau.
Integration by parts shows its derivative is minus that dissipation.
For a quartic G the absolute relative integrand is bounded by a fixed
multiple of |A0|^2|Dpsi_tau|^2+|A0||Dpsi_tau|^3+|Dpsi_tau|^4.
The two-dimensional L1-to-Lp heat bounds, p=2,3,4, make its integral
tend to zero at infinity. Strong Lp continuity gives the initial value.
Consequently the initial relative energy is nonnegative, and the linear
term integrates to zero. Approximation in W1,4 extends the inequality
to Lipschitz zero-trace perturbations on bounded Lipschitz domains.
This proves quasiconvexity of G.

Finally gamma>=0 and the determinant null-Lagrangian mean identity,
followed by scalar Jensen, prove quasiconvexity of gamma(det A)^2.
Their sum is Fstar. The earlier exact non-polyconvexity witness persists.
The heat method, exact-form mechanism and determinant separation are
prior work; the newly acquired certificate and neighbourhood are scoped
to this example and the explicitly bounded coefficients in Section 5.
The certificate does not assert monotonicity of the full Fstar heat energy.

5. NEW uniform coefficient neighbourhood and quantitative gap

The independent verifier additionally proves Q-(1/500)I positive definite
by exact rational LDL. Write rho=1/500. In the FIXED basis

  f=(u^2,v^2,uv,w^2,uw,vw,qu,qv,qw),
  pstar=(1,1,7/2,1/2,0,0,-29/10,-29/10,0),

let Fdelta=Fstar+sum_i delta_i f_i for arbitrary real delta satisfying
max_i |delta_i|<=eta=1/82000. This is a coefficient cube in the stated
basis, not an invariant ball or a ball in an alternative normal form.
The same gamma=83/1000 and the same closed omega apply to every Fdelta.

For each f_i, independent differentiation and deterministic monomial
pairing produce a symmetric rational R_i with
Z^T R_i Z=sum_j D2f_i(A)[Bj,Bj]. Every one of the nine identities is
checked exactly. The maximal absolute row sum of sum_i |R_i| is 82.
For any real allowed delta, the symmetric perturbation therefore has
operator norm at most 82 eta=1/1000. Hence

  Q+sum_i delta_i R_i >= (1/1000)I.

This is a universal norm bound over an infinite family; the exact finite
calculation establishes its fixed coefficients, not a finite sampling
of delta. The heat argument proves quasiconvexity for the whole cube.

The original non-polyconvexity law has exact basis moments
(49,49,49,0,0,0,1176/25,1176/25,0), whose absolute sum is 6027/25.
Consequently its mean Fdelta is at most
-833/250+(1/82000)(6027/25)=-166453/50000<0 for every allowed delta.
Thus every member of this full-dimensional cube is non-polyconvex.

For H(A)=|A|^4 and the six compatible jet coordinates z, the exact SOS
identity independently checked in the verifier proves

  E_H <= 24 |A|^2 |z|^2.

Indeed, 24|A|^2|z|^2-E_H equals
12|A|^2(z0^2+z2^2+z3^2+z5^2)
+8 sum_{B=B1,B2} sum_{i<j}(A_i B_j-A_j B_i)^2.
Since |Z|^2=|A|^2|z|^2, the same heat correction proves
Fstar-(rho/24)H quasiconvex; its residual still adds the nonnegative
gamma determinant square. This uses the certificate's universal Gram
margin, not an approximate eigenvalue estimate.

For arbitrary matrices A,B, direct expansion gives

  H(A+B)-H(A)-DH(A):B - |B|^4/3
  =6(A:B+|B|^2/3)^2
   +2 sum_{i<j}(A_i B_j-A_j B_i)^2 >=0.

Integrating kills the linear term for every zero-boundary gradient.
Therefore, for every A0, every bounded Lipschitz domain Omega, and every
phi in W0^{1,4}(Omega;R^2) (in particular the standard Lipschitz tests),

  integral [Fstar(A0+Dphi)-Fstar(A0)]
     >= (1/36000) integral |Dphi|^4.

Replacing rho by the cube's margin rho/2 gives the uniform bound

  integral [Fdelta(A0+Dphi)-Fdelta(A0)]
     >= (1/72000) integral |Dphi|^4.

For periodic potentials the same proof uses heat flow on the torus:
the gradient has mean zero and decays to zero, and the exact-form
pullback integrates to zero without boundary. Thus both displayed
inequalities hold with normalized torus averages in place of integrals.
When average |Dphi|^4=1 the scalar gaps are at least 1/36000 for Fstar
and 1/72000 throughout the coefficient cube. No upper bound on A0,
bandwidth restriction, or finite Fourier representation is assumed.

These conclusions supply a robust class of uniformly quasiconvex,
non-polyconvex quartics. They remain a bounded mathematical result,
without historical novelty clearance or an externally validated breakthrough.
