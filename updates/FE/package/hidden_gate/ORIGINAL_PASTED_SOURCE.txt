Research outcome: completion-directed discovery of hidden gates
I replaced the requirement to learn a global transport map with a different operation:
Maintain every unmeasured microscopic transition as uncertain, bound the kinetics of every model consistent with the observations, and query the transition whose measurement gives the greatest guaranteed reduction in kinetic uncertainty.

The central mathematical result is an exact endpoint principle for choosing the next query. It reduces a continuous worst-case acquisition problem to two explicit calculations. I implemented the resulting procedure and tested it on a published hierarchical-gating model with the facilitating-coordinate ordering withheld.
The strongest experimental finding is a missing-pathway test: one unqueried transition changed the mean residence time by more than 11,961-fold while leaving every observation of a sparse rule learner unchanged. The broader completion-directed procedure found that transition and certified the altered lifetime.
This is not yet the requested general molecular capability. The implementation enumerates microscopic configurations, uses known equilibrium masses, and receives local microscopic rates through an explicit query interface. It saved rate queries, but a fully informed sparse solver—and a learner with a valid stronger structural assumption—were faster.

1. Why replace the transport-map approach?
The previous construction required a full-dimensional map covering the entire modeled pre-exit domain. A map covering only observed transition tubes could not certify omitted configurations. That was the acquisition problem, not merely an implementation detail.    RESEARCH_NOTE    RESEARCH_NOTE
I examined three mechanisms before concentrating on model completion.
More expressive, branching currents
Branching can fix geometric deficiencies in a single transport coordinate. It does not, by itself, reveal the conductance of an unknown gate.
For any source-conserving current and any transient region \(S\), conservation and Cauchy–Schwarz give
\[
\mathcal E(J)
\ge
\frac{\left(\sum_{i\in S}m_i\right)^2}
{\sum_{e\in\partial S}c_e}.
\]
An unmeasured cut can therefore dominate the current cost regardless of how flexibly the current is represented.
This does not rule out multichart methods. It identifies the missing component: a means of acquiring or bounding the conductance across regions that have not been explored.
Infer long kinetics from short-time information
Consider two survival laws:
\[
S_0(t)=e^{-t},
\qquad
S_1(t)=(1-p)e^{-t}+p e^{-t/T}.
\]
Their absolute difference never exceeds \(p\), but their mean lifetimes are
\[
1
\quad\text{and}\quad
1-p+pT.
\]
For \(p=10^{-6}\) and \(T=10^{12}\), survival measurements with absolute uncertainty \(10^{-6}\) can be compatible with a mean near one or near one million.
This is an explicit hidden-tail obstruction, not a universal impossibility theorem for spectroscopy or experiments. Such methods need an additional tail or observability guarantee before small signal errors imply small lifetime errors.
Interrogate uncertain dynamics directly
The surviving approach does not first decide which pathways exist in the learned representation.
Instead, it asks:
Among all still-admissible microscopic dynamics, which unresolved transition could most consequentially change the answer?

A pathway that has never been observed remains represented through uncertainty in its constituent transitions. The stopping criterion concerns their collective possible effect, not whether a plausible mechanism has been found.
2. The operation: a sharp interval over unmeasured kinetics
Consider a specified finite reversible microscopic model. Let \(m_i>0\) be known unnormalized invariant masses, and let \(d_e\) denote the incidence vector of a possible transition, with the absorbing target grounded.
Write
\[
A(c)=\sum_e c_e d_ed_e^\top,
\qquad
M=\sum_i m_i.
\]
For the initial distribution \(m/M\) over the transient domain,
\[
\tau(c)=\frac{F(c)}{M},
\qquad
F(c)=m^\top A(c)^{-1}m.
\]
The information acquired so far supplies intervals
\[
\ell_e\le c_e\le u_e.
\]
An unqueried transition is not deleted. Its whole allowed interval remains active.
Assume \(A(\ell)\) is positive definite. Define
\[
F_{\mathrm{hi}}=F(\ell),
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{lo}}=F(u).
\]
Matrix monotonicity immediately gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{\mathrm{lo}}}{M}
\le \tau(c)\le
\frac{F_{\mathrm{hi}}}{M}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Within the independent edgewise uncertainty class, this interval is sharp: both extremal models are admissible. No procedure using the same observations and no additional structural assumptions can guarantee a smaller interval.
This distinction matters. When independent intervals merely enclose a more constrained physical family, the interval remains valid but need not be sharp for that smaller family.
What information is assumed?
The procedure needs a microscopic move alphabet, masses, and globally valid conductance bounds. It does not need a committor, gate ordering, reaction coordinate, equilibrium samples, or successful transition trajectories.
However, a known microscopic move alphabet is not free global coverage. Enumerating every possible single-coordinate move over every conformation can be exponentially expensive.
Likewise, positive lower bounds cannot be invented. Some may be zero if the remaining known-positive transitions connect every transient component to absorption. Otherwise the pessimistic lifetime may be infinite, and establishing connectivity becomes an additional acquisition task.
3. A candidate new acquisition theorem
The useful result is not simply interval propagation. It is an exact rule for deciding which conductance to measure next.
For an unresolved edge \(e\), write
\[
\Delta=u_e-\ell_e,
\]
and calculate
\[
v_- = d_e^\top A(\ell)^{-1}m,
\qquad
R_- = d_e^\top A(\ell)^{-1}d_e,
\]
\[
v_+ = d_e^\top A(u)^{-1}m,
\qquad
R_+ = d_e^\top A(u)^{-1}d_e.
\]
Define
\[
G_{\mathrm{high}}
=
\frac{\Delta v_-^2}{1+\Delta R_-},
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{low}}
=
\frac{\Delta v_+^2}{1-\Delta R_+}.
\tag{2}
\]
The second denominator is positive because removing this edge’s uncertainty increment from \(A(u)\) still leaves a matrix at least as large as \(A(\ell)>0\).
If the measured conductance equals its upper endpoint, the interval becomes
\[
[F_{\mathrm{lo}},\,F_{\mathrm{hi}}-G_{\mathrm{high}}].
\]
If it equals its lower endpoint, the interval becomes
\[
[F_{\mathrm{lo}}+G_{\mathrm{low}},\,F_{\mathrm{hi}}].
\]
Endpoint principle
No interior measurement outcome produces a worse remaining interval width—or a worse upper/lower ratio—than these two endpoint outcomes.
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
Q_e=
\max\left\{
\frac{F_{\mathrm{hi}}}{F_{\mathrm{lo}}+G_{\mathrm{low}}},
\frac{F_{\mathrm{hi}}-G_{\mathrm{high}}}{F_{\mathrm{lo}}}
\right\}
}
\tag{3}
\]
is the exact worst-case remaining ratio after querying edge \(e\).
For equal-cost queries, choosing the edge minimizing \(Q_e\) is therefore one-step minimax for relative kinetic uncertainty. For additive uncertainty, the guaranteed reduction is
\[
\min\{G_{\mathrm{high}},G_{\mathrm{low}}\}.
\]
This is not a theorem that a greedy sequence minimizes the total number of queries.
Proof
At the queried edge’s lower endpoint, the two extremal matrices can be written as
\[
B_-=A(\ell),
\qquad
B_+=A(u)-\Delta dd^\top,
\]
with \(B_+\succeq B_->0\). Any measurement outcome corresponds to the same update \(pdd^\top\) applied to both, where \(0\le p\le\Delta\).
The two remaining extremal quadratic forms have the Sherman–Morrison expressions
\[
f(p)=f(0)-\frac{pa^2}{1+pr},
\qquad
g(p)=g(0)-\frac{pb^2}{1+ps},
\]
where inverse ordering gives \(r\ge s>0\).
For every \(\lambda\ge0\),
\[
\frac{d}{dp}[f(p)-\lambda g(p)]
=
-\frac{a^2}{(1+pr)^2}
+
\frac{\lambda b^2}{(1+ps)^2}.
\]
Because
\[
\frac{1+pr}{1+ps}
\]
is nondecreasing, this derivative can change sign only from negative to positive—not from positive to negative. Thus \(f-\lambda g\) attains its maximum at an endpoint.
Taking \(\lambda=1\) proves the width statement. Taking \(\lambda\) equal to the larger endpoint ratio \(f/g\) proves the ratio statement. Equation (2) follows from the rank-one identities.
For a fixed batch of \(k\) distinct edge queries, the same argument reduces the worst remaining ratio to one of \(2^k\) endpoint combinations. That is an exact reduction, not an efficient general batch algorithm.
Novelty status: monotonicity, network inverses and rank-one updates are established ingredients. The endpoint principle and its exact kinetic acquisition criterion are the candidate contribution. Historical novelty and independent proof review remain unresolved.
4. Executed test: hierarchical gates with their ordering withheld
I used the softened East model, a published kinetically constrained model in which neighboring configurations control local dynamics and constraint violations remain possible at small rates. It provides an independently specified gating mechanism rather than a favorable transport map manufactured for this method. arXiv
The experiment used an open chain of ten binary variables. The last variable becoming excited was absorbing, leaving
\[
N=2^9=512
\]
transient configurations and
\[
E=9\,2^8+2^9=2816
\]
possible microscopic transitions.
The rates were
\[
k_i(0\to1)=q\,a_i,
\qquad
k_i(1\to0)=(1-q)a_i,
\]
with
\[
a_1=1,
\qquad
a_i=\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)n_{i-1}.
\]
The non-target coordinates were relabeled before acquisition. The procedure received the microscopic states, possible single-bit moves, masses, \(q,\epsilon\), and rate bounds—but not the facilitating-parent ordering. A separate callback answered each selected local rate query.
The equilibrium masses are analytically known product weights. The kinetics is interacting and hierarchical, but the thermodynamic acquisition problem is deliberately absent. This is therefore a test of kinetic information acquisition, not a demonstration of affordable general molecular integration.

5. A hidden pathway that defeats an otherwise successful representation
A strong specialized comparator materially changed the assessment.
Suppose each microscopic flip is either constantly facilitated or depends monotonically on one unknown neighboring bit. Under that promise, all-zero probes identify constant rules, and binary group probes locate the unknown parents.
The implemented learner required only 37 rate queries to reconstruct the complete softened-East generator. It then used a sparse solve.
It decisively beat the general completion procedure on the genuine model family.
However, those 37 observations do not establish that the structural promise is globally true.
Off-model shortcut test
For a separate falsification experiment, I changed one unqueried target-flip conductance from its slow endpoint to its fast endpoint. The perturbation preserved detailed balance and equilibrium masses.
Every one of the sparse learner’s 37 queries and answers remained identical.
Yet the certified true lifetime changed from

943{,}914{,}405.32057]
\]
to
\[
[78{,}913.166407819,\;
78{,}913.167817368].
\]
Thus the change exceeded
\[
\boxed{11{,}961.38\text{-fold}.}
\]
The broader completion-directed procedure queried the exceptional transition and returned
\[
[78{,}106.473943106,\;
85{,}912.735819174]
\]
after 890 local queries, again with upper/lower ratio below \(1.1\).
This perturbation was selected deliberately after examining the learner and model. It is an adversarial falsification test, not an independent performance benchmark.
The sparse learner is not wrong under its stated promise. The failure would be treating that promise as established merely because its observed rates fit perfectly.
Learning a plausible generator and excluding a consequential missing pathway are different tasks. The completion procedure addresses the second within its declared move alphabet and global bounds.

Confrontation with stronger molecular methods
Optimized weighted ensemble already uses estimated local first-passage information to allocate trajectories more effectively, with published high-dimensional molecular tests. The present candidate cannot claim novelty merely from directing effort toward kinetically important configurations. arXiv
Descriptor-free AIMMD combines learned committors and path sampling. Its 16 July 2026 preprint reports applications across systems spanning roughly seventeen orders of magnitude in residence time. I inspected its abstract, not a replicated implementation; no performance comparison against it was executed here. arXiv
Most importantly, TAMMBER already uses uncertainty about missing transitions to direct kinetic-model exploration and temperature acceleration. Uncertainty-guided discovery is therefore not itself new. The candidate distinction is the exact deterministic completion/query criterion under explicit conductance bounds—not atomistic exploration machinery. arXiv
Temperature-accelerated dynamics also has an established mathematical framework; it must not be treated as a method forced to wait at the target temperature. And graph transformation already computes finite-network first-passage quantities without simulating paths once the network is known. arXiv
I did not complete an optimized-WE, AIMMD or TAMMBER head-to-head benchmark. The implemented exact comparators already show why the current experiment cannot support a claim of superior molecular computation.
7. The remaining obstruction is more precise than “find a better map”
Three limitations now determine whether this can become consequential molecular capability.
A local rate query must not hide a rare-event calculation
In this experiment, querying a rate means evaluating a specified microscopic generator at an arbitrary selected configuration.
For an atomistic molecule, “return the rate between these metastable basins” could require discovering the basin, finding its exits and solving the original rare-event problem. Such a query is not an acceptable free input.
A transfer to atomistic physics needs a derivation from obtainable energies, forces, local dynamics or experimentally measured quantities, with uncertainty preserved. Finite measurement errors must remain intervals; observing an exponentially rare transition to estimate its rate is not made cheap by equation (3).
The initial preparation matters
The monotonic interval applies to
\[
\tau=\frac{m^\top A^{-1}m}{M},
\]
the equilibrium-conditioned mean over the specified transient domain.
For an arbitrary prepared distribution \(\rho\), the mean is instead
\[
\rho^\top A^{-1}m,
\]
and increasing a conductance need not shorten it.
For example, connect two transient states of equal mass to a sink with conductances \(1\) and \(\epsilon\), and connect the states with conductance \(t\). Starting from the first state,
\[
u_1(t)=
\frac{\epsilon+2t}
{\epsilon+(1+\epsilon)t}.
\]
For \(0<\epsilon<1\), this increases with \(t\): opening access to a slow trap can lengthen the mean.
A binding-prepared molecular ensemble therefore needs a separate treatment. It cannot silently inherit the present extremal certificate.
Compression must preserve hidden residence, not just boundary currents
The full-state implementation is the main scaling failure.
To identify what a compact replacement must retain, partition the network into represented exterior \(O\) and hidden interior \(H\). Exact elimination gives
\[
K=A_{OO}-A_{OH}A_{HH}^{-1}A_{HO},
\]
\[
b=m_O-A_{OH}A_{HH}^{-1}m_H,
\qquad
\gamma=m_H^\top A_{HH}^{-1}m_H,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
F=\gamma+b^\top K^{-1}b.
}
\tag{5}
\]
This is a standard Schur-complement identity, not a claimed new theorem. Its implication is important: a hidden-region representation needs a source load and an internal residence contribution, not merely a boundary conductance map.
Attach a dead-end state of mass \(\delta\) to an exterior state of mass one through conductance \(\delta\epsilon\), with unit exterior-to-sink conductance. Elimination gives
\[
K=1,\qquad b=1+\delta,\qquad\gamma=\delta/\epsilon,
\]
so
\[
\tau
=
1+\delta+
\frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)\epsilon}.
\]
The homogeneous boundary conductance remains unchanged while hidden residence becomes arbitrarily large.
Thus a compressed discovery operation must obtain bounds on the whole source-aware response, including \(\gamma\), without already solving the hidden region’s kinetics. That operation remains unconstructed.
8. What this investigation establishes
The mathematical and computational advance is an acquisition rule that does not require a supplied favorable transport map:
It chooses unmeasured microscopic transitions by their exact worst-case effect on aggregate kinetic uncertainty, and it can certify that the remaining unmeasured transitions collectively cannot overturn the result.
That was demonstrated on an independently specified gating model and on a consequential off-model shortcut. The certificate did not rely on the learned representation being correct everywhere.
The boundary is equally clear. For an unrestricted local query interface, a single exceptional transition can remain hidden among many possibilities until queried. Avoiding exhaustive coverage therefore requires genuine physical structure—symmetry, locality, regularity or a validated compressed description—that constrains entire unvisited regions. A sparse fit alone is not that guarantee.
The retained objective is now to acquire compact, source-aware bounds on unvisited molecular regions from physical information, without enumerating their configurations. The endpoint principle supplies an exact refinement rule once those bounds exist. It does not yet supply the bounds.
The investigation has moved from “which pathways have been found?” to the stronger question: “Can any dynamics still consistent with the unmeasured regions overturn the kinetic conclusion?” The present procedure answers that question for small explicitly represented microscopic models. Extending that coverage to real molecular configuration spaces is the decisive remaining operation
