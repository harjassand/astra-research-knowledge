6. What this demonstrates about discovery methods
The productive representation change came from restricting to the difficult directions first. Factoring the rank-one restriction supplied a coordinate system in which the complete reflection-invariant quartic became a three-term positive decomposition. The normalization did mathematical work; it was not merely a relabelling.

Boundary degeneracy supplied information that generic searches missed. Zero eigenvalues forced all orientation-sensitive couplings to vanish on two boundary regimes. The inequalities then quantified how much interior positivity is required before those couplings can survive.

Exact certification separated an apparent discovery from a valid challenge. The sampled LP candidate failed. The retained example has a rational global certificate. Its remaining difficulty is genuinely quasiconvexity, rather than an unnoticed failure of rank-one convexity.

These results do not establish a general method for producing foundational breakthroughs. They establish a constructive classification, explicit restrictions on the remaining coefficients, and an exact formulation of the unresolved local-to-global step.

Priority also remains unresolved. Older quartic research already explored mathematical-programming approaches to the quadratic and fourth-degree problems; the retrieved abstract alone is insufficient to determine overlap with every result above. 

The strongest preserved mathematical objective is therefore the gradient-to-positive-completion theorem (15). The reflection-invariant sector is handled by the decomposition; the interior orientation-sensitive sector remains. Establishing that completion—or producing a compatible field that violates it—is the exceptional step this investigation did not establish.
