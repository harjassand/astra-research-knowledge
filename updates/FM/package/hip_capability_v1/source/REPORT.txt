The selected scientific task

Repair fatigue-relevant defects in additively manufactured metal parts when a
CT-visible pore may be sealed or connected to the surface through an unresolved
throat. This matters for reliable complex metal components, including parts with
internal channels where complete coating, machining and inspection are difficult.
The desired capability is durable bonded repair at an improved complete cost or
yield; a smaller CT pore alone is insufficient.

Current primary evidence establishes the problem. du Plessis and Macdonald
(2020, DOI 10.1016/j.addma.2020.101191) find connected/near-surface defects resist
HIP or open as surface notches. Their specific reported sample outcomes do not
come with a complete pressure-ramp trace. Tammas-Williams et al. (2016, DOI
10.1007/s11661-016-3429-3) already document sub-XCT connections and surface-pore
exceptions. Later pore tracing and reheating studies show that compressed pores
can remain unbonded or regrow. These are established source findings, not ours.

The action ambiguity is concrete: an apparently identical cavity can need only
qualified HIP, or first need a gas-tight surface seal/encapsulation, a different
process or rejection. External gas entering the open cavity removes the pressure
difference needed for pressure-driven closure. The correct input is hot gas
access plus evolving throat/contact/bond behavior, not room geometry alone.

1. Rapid pressure before gas entry: constructed, then preempted

Root investigated a pressure ramp that collapses or seals a connected throat
before the gas equalizes. Under explicitly stated ideal-gas, common-temperature,
cylindrical, continuum, no-slip assumptions,

  n=pV/(RT),
  n'=pi a^4(P^2-p^2)/(16 mu L R T),
  q'=CP(1-q^2)+(c+theta-r)q,
  C=pi a^4/(16 mu L V), q=p/P,
  c=-V'/V, theta=T'/T, r=P'/P.

Cavity compression and heating increase internal pressure and cannot be omitted.
The near-equilibrium filling time is 8 mu L V/(pi a^4 P), with no extra RT.
Unmeasured throat radius causes a fourth-power uncertainty. Sensitivity examples
with illustrative geometry/properties are preserved; none predicts an actual
AM throat. Slip, rarefaction, real-gas behavior and hot property uncertainty are
material gates, particularly in the very narrow throats favored by the proposal.

Root established a conservative conditional slew exclusion: if P'<=Rmax,
C>=Cmin>0 and c+theta>=0, then

  P-p <= max{(P-p)(0), sqrt(Rmax/Cmin)}.

This is an internally derived scalar barrier in the stipulated model. Applying
it requires a conductance lower bound that remains valid while the throat evolves.
It excludes a required deficit Y only when the bound is below Y; it does not
exclude independent capillarity/chemistry or arbitrarily slow subthreshold creep.
The maths lead also developed conditional closure-strain bounds and demonstrated
that rapid loading need not lower admitted gas per unit closure. Linear-flow and
full compressible-flow claims are deliberately kept distinct.

This operation has decisive prior art. EP1024911B1 / US5816090A explicitly
disclose pneumatic isostatic forging that rapidly closes surface voids before
gas penetration erases the driving difference, with 2.07–27.6 MPa/s ramps.
Experimental gaseous isostatic forging also exists (Dunstan et al., 2018,
DOI 10.1016/j.jmatprotec.2018.04.039). The patent is disclosure, not independent
proof of the exact uncoated AM pore outcome. Nevertheless it preempts our generic
mechanism. Strong matched competitors include rapid forging with its actual
pretreatment, verified complete encapsulation/coating plus HIP, optimized HIP
cycles, and material-specific alternatives. A comparison with only slow unsealed
HIP would be inadequate.

2. Gas-tight closure still does not establish useful repair

If a pore seals with insoluble gas and no later gas loss, conservation gives
  p_f=p_s(V_s/V_f)(T_f/T_s).
Without additional inward traction, reaching V_f requires
  p_s/P_f <= (V_f/V_s)(T_s/T_f).
Sealing at high internal pressure can leave too much gas for useful densification.
Capillarity, gas dissolution, nonquasistatic deformation and bonding change this
balance and must be measured rather than ignored. Reheat/regrowth and fatigue
must be tested independently from CT disappearance and surface finishing.

Root and the adversaries gave exact worlds with identical complete room tracer
responses but different unmeasured hot deformation, and hence opposite attainable
geometric criteria or preferred schedules under declared losses. These are model
counterexamples, not identified real alloy pairs. A finite positive throat radius
is NOT a gas-tight seal: the initial draft incorrectly called it one. Review
forced an explicit additional contact/snap/bond law or the narrower wording
'geometric criterion'. This correction, initial-condition correction and a
numerical integrator repair are retained in root/CORRECTIONS_v0001.md.

3. Selectively preseal hidden narrow entrances: redesigned, still unacquired

Root next proposed Kelvin-selective vapor condensation: close nanothroats at low
gas pressure while leaving intended macrochannels open, then apply HIP. This
would physically change access rather than merely reconstruct topology.
Curvature-selective vapor deposition already exists experimentally (Lovikka
et al., 2019, DOI 10.1039/C8MH01523F). Its materials and later oxide/polymer
implementations do not supply an alloy-compatible, hermetic plug surviving HIP.

The liquid-meniscus Kelvin calculation favors truly nanometric wetting throats,
not arbitrary 'subvoxel' paths. A low-melting liquid plug may be displaced or
alloy; a refractory solid cannot simply inherit the liquid Kelvin law. Reaction
fixation can leave a porous, oxidized or intermetallic seam that defeats bonding
or fatigue. Full encapsulation, coating and vacuum/sinter-then-HIP are established
stronger comparators. No suitable plug chemistry or useful repair was obtained.

4. Direct hot-process isotope query: precise consequences and limitations

To avoid room-to-hot extrapolation, root and the maths lead constructed a
time-coded argon-isotope query followed by registered vacuum pore-gas extraction
and noble-gas MS. A simpler constant isotope code throughout the cycle could
separate retained pre-HIP gas from retained HIP-source gas. In a calibrated
two-source model, lambda=(f-f0)/(fH-f0). The executable interval inversion
returns UNKNOWN if effective endmembers/fractionation/background are unacquired.
This measures retained provenance, not cumulative net uptake or sealing time.

Generic isotope-tagged HIP gas, trapping in microporosities and post-process MS
readout are already explicitly disclosed in EP3243585A1. Root inspected the
primary disclosure. Ordinary isotope inversion is the strongest equally informed
decoder and has the same information/cost contract. Specific CT-registered
single-AM-cavity source attribution remains an unvalidated application, with
priority UNKNOWN; absence of another located paper is not novelty clearance.

Exact finite-code obstruction: two positive ingress histories with total mass 1,
mean time 1/2 and second moment 1/3 have last ingress at 5/6 and 1, yet identical
three-isotope counts for a valid code. Bidirectional exchange gives another
counterexample with identical total gas and isotope endpoint but different
cutoff times. Three-bin mass decoding needs a validated transport model, acquired
initial inventory and well-conditioned source compositions; tiny enrichment
amplifies assay errors. A null late label cannot prove a seal without a hot
open-path flux lower bound.

Mineral inclusion laser extraction and sensitive noble-gas MS establish component
possibilities, not an AM-metal assay. A calculated 10-um-radius cavity at 100 MPa,
1200 K contains ~4.2e-11 mol ideal gas. Blank, fractionation, extraction yield,
matrix/surface uptake, registration, carryover and detector dynamic range must
be acquired in the actual metal. The assay destroys the coupon, so any resulting
policy applies to independently validated future/batch parts, not that same part.
root/ISOTOPE_OPERATION.md gives the conservation, exact counterexamples and test.
