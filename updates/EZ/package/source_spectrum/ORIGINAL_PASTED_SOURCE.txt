2. An exact invariant: the minimum source rank
Let \(T:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) and \(B:\mathcal E\to\mathcal H\) be bounded operators on separable Hilbert spaces. Fix \(a>0\), and define
\[
g_a(t)=\frac{t}{1+at}.
\]
Suppose
\[
\boxed{
T^*T\preceq g_a(TT^*+BB^*).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\preceq\) denotes the positive-semidefinite order. Think of \(B\) as the source that permits the inequality to hold.
Write
\[
A=T^*T,\qquad C=TT^*,\qquad P=BB^*.
\]
Boundedness implies \(a\|A\|<1\). Inverting the positive operators shows that (1) is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
f_a(A)\preceq C+P,
\qquad
f_a(t)=\frac{t}{1-at}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
The exact equivalence—not a relaxation—is useful because \(A\) and \(C\) have the same nonzero eigenvalues, while \(P\) is the source perturbation.
Theorem: reciprocal-window classification
Prescribe a finite or infinite sequence of positive singular-value squares,
\[
a^{-1}>\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots,
\]
tending to zero in the infinite case. Allow arbitrary additional zero singular values and enlargement of the Hilbert space.
Among all operators \(T\) with that prescribed singular spectrum, the least source rank permitting (1) is
\[
\boxed{
r_a(\lambda)
=
\sup_{x>0}
\#\left\{
j:
x\le\frac1{\lambda_j}<x+a
\right\}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The answer may be infinite.
Interpretation: place the reciprocal squared singular values on the positive line. The minimum source rank is exactly the largest number that fit inside any half-open interval of length \(a\).
This is a necessary-and-sufficient classification, not merely a proposed compatibility condition.
Proof of necessity
If \(\operatorname{rank}P=r\), rank-\(r\) eigenvalue interlacing gives
\[
\lambda_{j+r}(C+P)\le\lambda_j(C)=\lambda_j.
\]
Combining this with (2),
\[
f_a(\lambda_{j+r})\le\lambda_j.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{j+r}
\le
\frac{\lambda_j}{1+a\lambda_j}
}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{
\frac1{\lambda_{j+r}}
\ge
\frac1{\lambda_j}+a.
}
\tag{4}
\]
Thus no interval of length \(a\) can contain more than \(r\) reciprocal values.
Proof of sufficiency
Suppose the count in (3) is \(r<\infty\). On an enlarged sequence space, define
\[
Te_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,e_{j+r},
\]
and introduce \(r\) source directions:
\[
Bu_j=\sqrt{f_a(\lambda_j)}\,e_j,
\qquad 1\le j\le r.
\]
Then \(T^*T\) has the prescribed diagonal. In the first \(r\) coordinates, \(TT^*+BB^*\) equals \(f_a(\lambda_j)\). In subsequent coordinates, it equals \(\lambda_{j-r}\), which dominates \(f_a(\lambda_j)\) by (4).
Hence (2), and therefore (1), holds.
This construction preserves singular values. It does not promise to preserve the eigenvalues of an existing nonnormal operator, locality, tensor structure, or a differential interpretation.
A consequence for spectral density
Iterating (4) yields
\[
\lambda_{j+kr}
\le
\frac1{\lambda_j^{-1}+ka},
\qquad 1\le j\le r.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus a rank-\(r\) source forces a \(j^{-1/2}\)-type singular-value bound.
For the inverse operator \(H=A^{-1}\) on the support of \(A\), this also gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}
\le
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{-\beta/\lambda_j}}
     {1-e^{-a\beta}}.
\tag{6}
\]
The certificate therefore controls an entire spectral counting problem, not only one extremal eigenvalue. Acquiring that certificate for a difficult target remains a separate task.
3. A sharp source-to-spectrum law
Source rank is only one measure of complexity. A more flexible measure is singular-value summability:
\[
\|X\|_{S_p}
=
\left(\sum_j s_j(X)^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]
For \(p<1\), this is a quasi-norm; the theorem does not require it to satisfy a triangle inequality.
Theorem: optimal Schatten transfer
Under (1), for every \(0<p<\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
B\in S_p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T\in S_{\max\{p+2,\,2p\}}.
}
\tag{7}
\]
More precisely,
\[
\boxed{
\|T\|_{S_{p+2}}^{p+2}
\le
\frac{2}{pa}\|B\|_{S_p}^{p},
\qquad 0<p\le2,
}
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\|T\|_{S_{2p}}^{4}
\le
\frac1a\|B\|_{S_p}^{2},
\qquad 2\le p<\infty.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Both the exponents and the displayed constants are optimal.
At the transition \(p=2\), the two formulas coincide:
\[
a\|T\|_{S_4}^{4}\le\|B\|_{S_2}^{2}.
\]
Why the proof has two regimes
For \(0<p\le2\), put \(\alpha=p/2\). Finite-dimensional trace subadditivity gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(X+Y)^\alpha
\le
\operatorname{Tr}X^\alpha+\operatorname{Tr}Y^\alpha.
\]
This is a classical ingredient, not a new result; a broader matrix subadditivity theorem is due to Bourin and Uchiyama. The attached note also proves the precise trace inequality needed here directly. arXiv
Applying it on finite spectral subspaces, followed by (2), gives
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\left[
f_a(\lambda_j)^\alpha-\lambda_j^\alpha
\right]
\le
\|B\|_{S_p}^{p}.
\]
The scalar inequality
\[
(1-u)^{-\alpha}\ge1+\alpha u
\]
implies
\[
f_a(\lambda)^\alpha-\lambda^\alpha
\ge
\alpha a\lambda^{\alpha+1}.
\]
Letting \(N\) increase proves (8). This argument does not subtract two potentially infinite traces.
For \(p\ge2\), instead weight (2) by \(A^{p-2}\) on a finite leading spectral subspace. Spectral rearrangement cancels the contribution of \(C-A\), while
\[
f_a(A)-A
=
aA^2(I-aA)^{-1}
\succeq aA^2.
\]
Trace Hölder then gives
\[
a\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_j^p
\le
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_j^p\right)^{(p-2)/p}
\|B\|_{S_p}^{2},
\]
which yields (9).
Two genuinely different mechanisms force the exponents
For a normal diagonal operator, choose
\[
Te_j=t_je_j,
\qquad
Be_j=
\frac{\sqrt a\,t_j^2}{\sqrt{1-at_j^2}}e_j.
\]
Then (1) is an equality. For small \(t_j\), the source singular values are proportional to \(t_j^2\), so
\[
B\in S_p\iff T\in S_{2p}.
\]
This forces the \(2p\) exponent.
A different obstruction comes from repeated nonnormal propagation. On \(\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\), define
\[
\lambda_k=\frac{b^2}{1+ab^2(k+1)},
\]
\[
Te_k=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,e_{k+1},
\qquad
Bz=bz\,e_0.
\]
Again (1) is an equality, but now \(B\) has rank one and
\[
s_k(T)\asymp k^{-1/2}.
\]
In particular, a rank-one source does not force a Hilbert–Schmidt response.
Take orthogonal sums of these shifts, with source strengths \(b_i\to0\). For \(q>2\), integral comparison gives
\[
\sum_{i,k}
\left[
\frac{b_i^2}{1+ab_i^2(k+1)}
\right]^{q/2}
<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_i b_i^{q-2}<\infty.
\]
Choosing \(b\in\ell^p\) but not in smaller summability classes forces \(q\ge p+2\).
The two examples explain the split in (7): the larger exponent comes from either ordinary spectral mass or repeated nonnormal propagation.
4. A constructive operation: generate the representation from the source
The preceding results classify certificates. The next step addresses construction.
Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_d\) be bounded operators satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iV_i^*\preceq I.
\]
Define
\[
\Phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iXV_i^*,
\qquad
\mathcal F(X)=g_a(\Phi(X)+BB^*).
\]
Theorem: constructive positive recursion
There is a unique bounded positive solution
\[
X_*=\mathcal F(X_*).
\]
Starting with \(X_0=0\) and iterating
\[
X_{k+1}=\mathcal F(X_k),
\]
one obtains the explicit bound
\[
\boxed{
0\preceq X_*-X_k
\preceq
\frac1{a(k+1)}I.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover, every positive subsolution
\[
A\preceq\mathcal F(A)
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
A\preceq X_*
\preceq X_k+\frac1{a(k+1)}I.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the iteration constructs a spectral majorant from the source and propagation operators. It does not require the target’s eigenvectors.
The essential estimate
For positive \(X,Y\), let \(\delta=\|X-Y\|\). Positivity and the condition on the \(V_i\) imply
\[
\Phi(X)+BB^*
\preceq
\Phi(Y)+BB^*+\delta I.
\]
For every positive \(Z\),
\[
g_a(Z+\delta I)-g_a(Z)
\preceq
\frac{\delta}{1+a\delta}I.
\]
Exchanging \(X\) and \(Y\) therefore proves
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal F(X)-\mathcal F(Y)\|
\le
\frac{\|X-Y\|}{1+a\|X-Y\|}.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Iterating this scalar modulus proves existence, uniqueness and (10). The full proof is in the research note.
The representation cost is explicit
If \(\operatorname{rank}B=r<\infty\), define the generated space
\[
W_k=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
V_{i_\ell}\cdots V_{i_1}u:
u\in\operatorname{ran}B,\quad 0\le\ell<k
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{ran}X_k\subseteq W_k.
\]
Writing \(D_k=\dim W_k\), equation (11) gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{D_k+1}(A)\le\frac1{a(k+1)}.
}
\tag{13}
\]
This identifies the quantity that must be controlled: the growth of the source-generated spaces.
If the propagation operators commute,
\[
D_k\le r\binom{d+k-1}{d}.
\]
Without relations, the automatic bound is instead
\[
D_k\le r(1+d+\cdots+d^{k-1}).
\]
The iteration has a dimension-independent error bound, but its representation may still grow exponentially.
There is also no hidden logarithmic accuracy claim: the universal bound needs
\[
k=O((a\varepsilon)^{-1})
\]
iterations. Applying the operators, constructing the generated span, handling its conditioning, and controlling rounding errors all remain charged.
For the original single-operator inequality, the polar decomposition \(T=U(T^*T)^{1/2}\) supplies a one-channel mathematical representation. This does not make acquisition of the polar factor \(U\) cheap.
5. A decisive test: bounded norm can conceal enormous source rank
Consider the explicitly defined operator
\[
Ae_\alpha=\frac1{|\alpha|+2}e_\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^n.
\]
Its norm is always \(1/2\). At total degree \(m\), however, its eigenvalue multiplicity is
\[
M_{n,m}=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.
\]
The reciprocal eigenvalues are the integers \(m+2\). Consequently, the exact source-rank theorem gives, for the cutoff \(|\alpha|\le m\) and \(a=1\),
\[
\boxed{
r_{\min}=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.
}
\tag{14}
\]
At \(n=m=20\),
\[
r_{\min}=68{,}923{,}264{,}410.
\]
For the full operator with \(n\ge2\), every scalar-source representation of the specified type requires infinite source rank.
This falsifies the proposed implication
\[
\text{dimension-free norm}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{small scalar-source representation}.
\]
Replacing the failed representation
The same operator admits a rank-one source with \(n\) propagation channels. Define
\[
V_i e_\alpha
=
\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i+1}{|\alpha|+1}}
\,e_{\alpha+e_i}.
\]
These are standard symmetric multivariable shifts, not newly invented objects; their operator-theoretic setting is established in Arveson’s work. arXiv
Direct calculation gives
\[
\sum_iV_iV_i^*
=
I-|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|.
\]
With \(P=|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|\),
\[
\boxed{
A=g_1\left(\sum_iV_iAV_i^*+P\right).
}
\tag{15}
\]
So the huge scalar source can be replaced by one source vector and \(n\) explicit propagation formulas.
But the multiplicities have not disappeared. The generated space consists of degrees below \(k\):
\[
D_k=\binom{n+k-1}{n},
\]
and the exact iteration error is
\[
\|A-X_k\|=\frac1{k+2}.
\]
This also proves that the universal \(1/k\) convergence order is sharp.
The distinction is consequential:
A small number of formulas can generate an enormous space. Removing source rank does not automatically remove computational complexity.

Indeed, in this example,
\[
A^{1/2}\in S_q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q>2n.
\]
Thus the sharp single-operator law in Section 3 cannot simply be transferred to arbitrary multichannel recursions.
The note gives an even stronger counterexample using free-word shifts: a rank-one source produces a compact fixed point belonging to no finite Schatten class. Its obstruction is exponential growth of generated spaces.
The product example validates the representation change, not a computational speedup. Its direct diagonal formula is a stronger specialised baseline than the iteration.
6. Numerical certification has a sharp fourth-root floor
Suppose the comparison is only established with residual tolerance:
\[
T^*T
\preceq
g_a(TT^*+BB^*)+\varepsilon I,
\qquad B\text{ compact}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then the norm remaining after all compact contributions are removed—the essential norm—satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|T\|_{\mathrm{ess}}^2
\le
\frac{\varepsilon+
\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon/a}}2.
}
\tag{17}
\]
The proof reduces the operator inequality modulo compact operators to
\[
m\le\frac{m}{1+am}+\varepsilon,
\qquad m=\|T\|_{\mathrm{ess}}^2,
\]
and solves the quadratic.
This bound is attained by
\[
B=0,\qquad T=\sqrt{m_*}\,I,
\]
where \(m_*\) is the right-hand side of (17).
Therefore the unresolved singular-value scale is
\[
\|T\|_{\mathrm{ess}}
\asymp
(\varepsilon/a)^{1/4}
\]
for small \(\varepsilon\).
An arbitrarily small nonzero residual does not certify compactness. In finite dimensions, an identity block at this scale can have arbitrary multiplicity. This is a mathematical limit on what the certificate establishes, not merely numerical pessimism.
7. What survives the investigation
The preserved mathematical content is the exact reciprocal-window classification, the optimal \(\max(p+2,2p)\) transfer law, the constructive source-generated recursion, and sharp counterexamples separating norm, rank, generated-space growth and precision.
The strongest conceptual conclusion is:
For source-based spectral computation, the relevant complexity is not merely the size of the source. It is the growth of the algebra acting on that source, together with the cost of acquiring and verifying that algebra.

This does not establish historical novelty for the package. Source-to-singular-spectrum methods already exist in displacement-structured matrix analysis, and Hilbert-module growth and curvature invariants also have substantial prior theory. The comparison here is nonlinear, but that distinction alone is not a priority argument. arXiv
The remaining capability gap is concrete: no economically acquired construction has been proved for a consequential general interacting system, and no end-to-end advantage over its strongest specialised method has been demonstrated. Assuming such a construction would simply move the original difficulty into the input specification.
The supplied diagnostics passed: they include exact rational rank-optimal constructions, exact source recursions and multiplicity checks, 150 noncommuting finite-matrix cases, and 36 positive-recursion instances. These checks are separate from the analytic proofs; the floating-point tests are not interval-certified, and none constitutes independent validation.
This should be retained as a candidate operator-theory mechanism with explicit acquisition and complexity boundaries—not promoted as a historic breakthrough.
