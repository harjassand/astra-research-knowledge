MAIN THEOREM AND CONSEQUENCE

There is a stationary irreducible finite all-even CTMC, with positive finite entropy production and six deterministic observed labels, whose observed path law P has all of the following properties:
  (a) P is exactly invariant under ordinary time reversal;
  (b) EVERY finite reflected future/past probability kernel, at arbitrary times and including the reflection boundary, is classically completely positive, with a single common ten-atom nonnegative factorization;
  (c) its observable reflection Hilbert space has finite rank eight and a self-adjoint positive contraction semigroup;
  (d) nevertheless P is outside the rational-cylinder closure of observations of ALL finite stationary reversible CTMCs, with no bounds on hidden dimension, rates, activity, stationary masses, or reducibility.

Thus even globally compatible complete positivity of every reflected word table does not characterize hidden equilibrium. A genuine positive common semigroup/detailed-balance obstruction survives ALL reflection CP tests, not merely individual pair/covariance tests. The construction uses a nonreducing observable subspace: the forward generator is invariant and self-adjoint there, while the adjoint leaks out of that subspace.

A general rigidity theorem below also gives a necessary-and-sufficient observable characterization of equilibrium closure for a finite-rank class whose static positive factors are forced onto finitely many state vectors. In that class equilibrium closure equals exact finite reversible realizability, and a finite nonnegative edge-flow linear program decides the remaining compatibility once the forced vectors/weights are known. This is a subclass characterization, not a universal solution for arbitrary path laws.

1. OBSERVABLE SETUP AND THE REVERSIBLE FRAME CONDITION

Use the reflection Hilbert space H, unit Omega, semigroup T_t=exp(-t A), A symmetric positive semidefinite, and boundary output projections E_a from v1. Its vectors are equivalence classes [F] of bounded future-cylinder functions; <[F],[G]>=E[F(reversed past)G(future)]. Rational times suffice when the target is stochastically continuous, because finite-alphabet cylinders at other times are limits in ordinary L2 and hence in reflection norm.

Let C be the cone generated by classes of nonnegative rational future-cylinder functions. Let
 D_a={x in H: <Omega,x>=1, E_a x=x, <x,c>>=0 for every c in C},
 D=union_a D_a,
 Gamma={mu in Prob(D): integral_D xx^T dmu(x)=I_H}.

D is compact. Choose finitely many nonnegative future-cylinder indicators whose classes span H. Each is between zero and Omega in the cone order. Every x in D therefore evaluates each chosen indicator in [0,1], bounding all coordinates of x; the defining conditions are closed. This also supplies an order-unit proof of compactness, without relying on a preferred CP decomposition.

Any exact finite reversible realization gives a frame satisfying F1–F4 of v1. Equivalently it supplies a finitely supported mu in Gamma with labels and symmetric nonnegative flows
  sum_j w_ij(x_j-x_i)=-pi_i A x_i.                       (1)
Conversely (1) constructs the common reversible generator Q_ij=w_ij/pi_i. For a fixed frame, feasibility is equivalent to
  sum_i pi_i <Z_i,A x_i>>=0
for every pairwise monotone field Z_i satisfying
  <Z_i-Z_j,x_i-x_j>>=0.                                 (2)
This is finite Farkas separation, proved in v1.

Global reflection CP is exactly the static condition Gamma nonempty at finite rank. The forward implication can be derived from T13's common-factor gluing: choose a finite basis [F_j]. All zero reflection-norm linear relations hold almost surely in its common nonnegative feature factor; these relations identify a vector x in H evaluating all future cylinders. The constant is one. Boundary0 events have disjoint second moments and probabilities summing to one, so their conditional probabilities are deterministic zero/one; all boundary multiplication relations give E_a x=x. The feature second moments are I. Conversely any mu in Gamma gives every reflected kernel as integral <x,[F]><x,[G]> dmu, entrywise nonnegative for nonnegative F,G. Finite-dimensional Caratheodory can make the STATIC factorization finite. It does not infer (1).

2. GENERAL FINITE-SUPPORT RIGIDITY THEOREM

Theorem. Assume the target P is stationary, stochastically continuous, reflection-invariant, reflection-positive, and has finite-dimensional H as above. Suppose there is one finite set S subset D such that every mu in Gamma is supported on S. Then the following are equivalent:
  (i) P lies in the rational-cylinder closure of finite stationary reversible CTMC output laws;
  (ii) P has an exact reversible CTMC realization with at most |S| states;
  (iii) some probability weights on S with second moment I admit the symmetric nonnegative edge flows (1).
Zero-weight points may be discarded. This does not require irreducibility of the comparison models. Equivalence (ii)<->(iii) is the frame theorem. The important implication is (i)->(iii).

Proof of (i)->(iii), including the unbounded-dimension/rate issue.

Let P_n be equilibrium outputs converging to P on all rational cylinders. In each hidden model let pi_n, Q_n be its stationary distribution and reversible generator. Choose a basis of H consisting of linear combinations of finitely many bounded future-cylinder indicators. For v in H, define j_n v to be the same linear combination of the corresponding CONDITIONAL future probabilities under model n, given its hidden state at time0. All these functions are uniformly bounded, independently of hidden dimension/rates. Choose an orthonormal basis e_1,...,e_r of H and define the feature vector x_n(i) by (j_n v)(i)=<x_n(i),v>. The x_n lie in one fixed compact box.

For any fixed bounded future cylinder F, write [F]=v_F in H. The reflection norm in model n of F minus the basis combination for v_F tends to zero, because its square is a finite linear combination of observed reflected-cylinder probabilities. Under equilibrium this reflection norm is exactly the hidden L2(pi_n) norm of the conditional future expectation. Hence
  ||h_F^n-j_n v_F||_(L2(pi_n)) ->0.                     (3)
The same applies to the constant function, boundary multiplication M_a, and shifted future functions U_t F at any fixed rational t. Consequently
  ||j_n Omega-1||_2 ->0,
  ||j_n E_a v-D_a^n j_n v||_2 ->0,
  ||j_n T_t v-exp(t Q_n)j_n v||_2 ->0.                  (4)
Here D_a^n is the actual hidden output indicator. The last identity uses the Markov property before taking the limit. No derivative, rate, activity, or state-count convergence is invoked.

Let mu_n be the stationary distribution of (x_n(i),g_n(i)), with the finite label included. Compactness gives a subsequential limit. Gram convergence gives integral xx^T dmu=I. Equations (3)-(4) imply <Omega,x>=1, the label projection relation E_a x=x, and <x,[F]>>=0 for every nonnegative rational cylinder F. For the last step use the continuous negative part: its squared expectation is bounded by the squared error in (3). A countable intersection over rational cylinders is sufficient. Thus mu belongs to Gamma and, by rigidity, is supported on S. The label is determined by x because x is nonzero and belongs to exactly one orthogonal projection block.

For each rational t>0 take the stationary two-time joint distribution kappa_n,t of (x_n(X0),x_n(Xt)). It is symmetric by reversibility and has both marginals mu_n. A diagonal subsequence yields a limit kappa_t with both marginals mu, supported on S x S. For bounded continuous f on the compact feature box and v in H, (4), the Markov property, and Cauchy-Schwarz show
  integral f(x)<y,v> dkappa_t(x,y)
    =integral f(x)<x,T_t v> dmu(x).
Indeed the difference before the limit is bounded by ||f||_infty times the L2 error in the last relation in (4). Since S is finite, continuous f can select each x_i, giving the row regression identity
  sum_j kappa_t(i,j) x_j=pi_i T_t x_i.                 (5)

There are two equivalent ways to finish. A fixed monotone field satisfies
  sum_i pi_i<Z_i,x_i-T_t x_i>
   =1/2 sum_ij kappa_t(i,j)<Z_i-Z_j,x_i-x_j>>=0.        (6)
Dividing by t and taking t down to0 gives every inequality (2). Finite Farkas separation gives (1).
Alternatively the feature energy is
  sum_ij kappa_t(i,j)||x_i-x_j||^2
    =2 tr(I-T_t)<=2t tr(A).
Distinct points of S have a positive minimum separation, so all off-diagonal kappa_t(i,j)/t are bounded. A subsequence at small rational t converges to symmetric nonnegative w_ij, and dividing (5) minus pi_i x_i by t yields (1) directly. Both arguments construct a genuine common generator at the target; neither assumes that the approximating generators have bounded rates or converge.

4. THE OBSERVABLE SUBSPACE AND ALL-WORD CP

Let H0 be the eight-dimensional subspace of hidden functions spanned by
  phi=(1_internal, x_internal, y_internal,
       1_marker0,...,1_marker4),
where x_internal(i)=v_i.x and y_internal(i)=v_i.y on internal states and both are zero on markers. The 10-by8 evaluation matrix is H (named H in the JSON; this matrix should not be confused with the abstract Hilbert space). Its rows are
  xi_i=(1,v_i.x,v_i.y,0,0,0,0,0),     internal i,
  xi_(5+k)=(0,0,0,e_k),               marker k.
M=H^T H/10 is the Gram matrix of this basis.

Every boundary label projection preserves H0 and therefore reduces it. Q preserves H0: equation (7) preserves internal affine functions; the constant total marker rate makes cross-killing -kappa times such a function; and each marker rate as a function of the internal point is affine. Define L by QH=HL. Its restriction is self-adjoint in the M inner product, ML=L^T M. The internal restriction is symmetric by C D^T=D C, and the marker links are reversible as full hidden operators. A stationary generator has nonpositive quadratic form, so -L is positive semidefinite in this inner product.

H0 is exactly the reachable observable subspace. Marker indicator functions are available at the boundary. Differentiating their future conditional probability at t=0 produces eta l_k on the internal block plus a marker diagonal term. The five facet affine functions span all three internal affine coordinates; their coefficient matrix has rank3 (checked exactly). Thus the closure of the span of future words contains all of H0. Conversely Q and the emissions preserve H0, so it contains every future word. The observable reflection rank is8.

Here is the essential all-word CP proof, without an invalid pointwise equality of conditional past/future laws. Let Pi be orthogonal projection onto H0 in L2(pi). In the decomposition H0 plus its orthogonal complement, invariance gives
  Q=((L,B_leak),(0,C_hidden)),
  Q*=((L,0),(B_leak*,C_hidden*)).
Thus Pi Q*=L Pi, and emissions commute with Pi. Starting from1 in H0, induction over the reversed-past conditional-word recursion gives
  Pi h_F^(Q*)=h_F^Q,
where h_F^Q is the actual conditional forward future-word probability and belongs to H0. It follows that
  P(F reflected past,G future)
    =<h_F^(Q*),h_G^Q>_pi
    =<h_F^Q,h_G^Q>_pi
    =(1/10)sum_(i=0)^9 h_F^Q(i)h_G^Q(i).               (8)
All factors h_F^Q(i) are actual nonnegative conditional probabilities and are common across every future-cylinder partition. Hence EVERY reflected word kernel is CP, with ten globally compatible atoms. Equation (8) also identifies the observable reflection Hilbert space isometrically with H0.

Every full word probability is computed on H0 from the self-adjoint semigroup and self-adjoint boundary projections. Taking adjoints reverses the order of that product and leaves its scalar expectation unchanged. Thus the entire stationary observed path law is time-reversal invariant. Conditional past and future laws need not agree pointwise on hidden states; the only equality used in (8) is after projection. This distinction prevents additive reversibilization from producing the same law: Q* leaks out of H0, so (Q+Q*)/2 does not preserve H0.

5. ANY STATIC POSITIVE FRAME IS FORCED ONTO THE TEN POINTS

It is convenient to use the evaluation coordinates xi of the basis phi instead of orthonormal Riesz coordinates. The two are related by the invertible Gram matrix M. A static positive factor measure therefore has first/second moments
  E xi=(1/10)sum_(i=0)^9 xi_i,
  E xi xi^T=M.
Its deterministic boundary label forces exactly the following form:
  label0: xi=(1,v.x,v.y,0^5),
  label k+1: xi=(0,0,0,e_k).
This follows from boundary multiplication, normalization by1, and orthogonal output blocks. Thus marker points are unique; label0 leaves a planar point v to determine.

For every t>0, the conditional future event Y_t=k+1 has nonnegative probability. Its canonical coefficient vector is exp(tL) applied to the marker indicator. For a label0 frame point, dividing the evaluation by t and taking t down to0 gives eta l_k(v)>=0. Consequently v belongs to the five-vertex polygon. No hidden generator was assumed in taking this limit; it is a coefficient limit in the supplied finite-rank observable semigroup.

Since all polygon vertices lie on the unit circle, every polygon point has ||v||^2<=1, with equality only at a vertex. Strict convexity of squared Euclidean norm proves this: any proper convex combination of distinct unit vectors has strictly smaller squared norm.
The prescribed moments give
  P(label0)=1/2,
  E[1_label0 ||v||^2]=M_xx+M_yy=1/2.
Thus equality holds almost surely and every label0 factor point is one of v0,...,v4. The five weights are uniquely determined by the moments of (1,x,y,x^2,xy). Their coefficient determinant is
  -15163556658355961856/10160806025773960625 !=0.
The known uniform model gives the solution, so each internal vertex has unconditional weight1/10. Every marker also has weight1/10. This proves the finite-support rigidity hypothesis, for one fixed set S of ten distinct evaluation points, and fixes its weights. Duplicated hidden states at a feature point only split that point's weight and cannot evade the argument.

6. EXACT MONOTONE FIELD EXCLUDES THE COMMON REVERSIBLE SEMIGROUP

Use the following rational planar field:
 z0=(10591/10600,-13257/13250),
 z1=(24730307/41735000,-1357669/5503750),
 z2=(4181663/6285800,13257/13250),

Every one of the ten pairings (z_i-z_j) dot(v_i-v_j) is strictly positive; their minimum is
  54355124699/139080625016250 >0.
But the exact internal drift pairing after the reset addition is

  =-3230342770615041346411154110862042812888496486195459
    /270241173284150175466366810508793688616783670000000000
  <0.                                                     (9)

Extend this to an eight-coordinate field on ALL ten evaluation points by
  Z_i=(0,z_i.x,z_i.y,(z_i dot v_i)1_5), internal i;
  Z_(5+k)=0, marker k.
Internal-internal monotonicity is the positive planar pairing. An internal-marker pairing is z_i dot v_i minus its added marker coordinate z_i dot v_i, exactly zero. Marker-marker pairings are zero. Thus
  (Z_i-Z_j) dot(xi_i-xi_j)>=0 for all i,j.              (10)

At an internal point the negative-generator drift of the basis coordinates is
  -q phi=(kappa, D(v_i-m)+kappa v_i, -eta l_0(v_i),...,-eta l_4(v_i)).
Taking its dot product with Z_i cancels kappa z_i dot v_i against the sum of the marker terms, leaving z_i dot D(v_i-m). Marker fields are zero. Therefore
  (1/10)sum_i Z_i dot[-(xi_i L)]
  =one half of (9) <0.                                (11)

Any reversible flow on the forced ten evaluation points would instead give this pairing as sum_(i<j) w_ij (Z_i-Z_j) dot(xi_i-xi_j)>=0. This contradicts (11). The frame theorem rules out every exact finite reversible completion. Splitting each forced point into arbitrarily many duplicate states does not help: summing their symmetric edge flows gives a symmetric nonnegative flow on the forced ten points with the same drift.

The general rigidity theorem now rules out every cylinder limit of finite reversible models. For a direct fixed-time proof, the symmetric coupling (5) of any purported limit would imply
  F(t)=(1/10)sum_i Z_i dot[xi_i-xi_i exp(tL)]>=0.
But F(0)=0 and F'(0) is the strictly negative quantity (11), so F(t)<0 for all sufficiently small positive t, including a rational t. This contradiction uses a fixed lag in the LIMIT target, not convergence of approximating generator derivatives.

7. DISSIPATION CONSEQUENCES AND EXACT LIMITS

Let E be the rational-cylinder closure of all finite reversible outputs. The preceding theorem proves P notin E. A cylinder neighborhood of P therefore stays a positive distance from E and uses finitely many observable cylinder probabilities on one finite window [0,T]. Select the finite joint visible-word distribution on the union of all times in that neighborhood. Its total-variation distance to all equilibrium comparisons is bounded below by some epsilon>0. Additive reversibilization gives, for EVERY finite all-even CTMC exact completion of P,
  T sigma/4 >= D(P_word || R_word) >=2 epsilon^2,
so sigma>=8 epsilon^2/T>0. There is at least one finite positive-EPR completion, namely Q above. Consequently the unrestricted identified minimum satisfies 0<V_infinity(P)<=sigma(Q)<infinity.

This positivity result is existential at v2: the finite cylinder list, epsilon, numerical optimal lower bound, and sample complexity are not computed here. Root assigned a distinct Sol continuation to construct explicit robust finite observable inequalities and a numerical EP floor. No claim that (9) itself equals physical minimal dissipation is made. Every scalar autocorrelation of a visible real function is completely monotone because it belongs to the self-adjoint observable subspace; every reflected CP witness is nonnegative. Thus those entire families of equilibrium tests cannot detect this P, while the common reversible flow condition can.
