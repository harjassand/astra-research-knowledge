Sparse exact clocks: root reconstruction and extensions
12 October 2026. Internally derived; priority and external correctness unresolved.

Provenance. The frontier_inventor cell proposed a positive compound-Poisson/gamma
clock after reading Petrack--Doty's Section 5. gamma_clock_adversary independently
checked the law and found the simpler independent-geometric representation used
below. reaction_clock_integration owns the conditional reaction/terminal-time
bridge. This note reconstructs the identity and adds the general block compiler
and deterministic rate-query cap. Classical uniformization, negative-binomial
mixtures, sparse Bernoulli skipping and gamma splitting are not claimed new.

1. Exact oracle contract and identity

Given k>=1 positive deterministic rates lambda_0,...,lambda_(k-1), random-access
rate evaluation, and a certified b>=max lambda_i and q>=max(1-lambda_i/b), q<1,
sample H=sum independent Exp(lambda_i). Envelope acquisition is charged separately.
For q_i=1-lambda_i/b, let G_i be independent geometric failures with
Pr(G_i=r)=(1-q_i)q_i^r, r=0,1,... . Independently conditional on the G_i, take
H~Gamma(k+sum G_i,rate b). For s>=0,

 E[(b/(b+s))^(1+G_i)]
   =(1-q_i)b/(b+s) / (1-q_i b/(b+s))=lambda_i/(lambda_i+s).

Products identify the exact nonnegative law, including repeated rates. More
strongly, conditional on G_i the stage clocks are independent Gamma(1+G_i,b),
so retaining sparse G_i supports exact conditional prefix queries.

Generate independent candidate Bernoulli(q) indices by geometric skipping. At a
candidate i accept with probability q_i/q; if accepted assign
G_i=1+GeomFailures(1-q_i). All other G_i are zero. Independence across indices
and Pr(G_i>0)=q_i prove the target product law. The q=0 case uses Gamma(k,b).
There are exactly Binomial(k,q) candidate rate evaluations, with mean kq, plus
one final skipping draw and the cost of acquiring the envelope. Accepted-marker
count is sum Bernoulli(q_i); expected correction shape is sum q_i/(1-q_i).

This is expected cost, with unbounded geometric values and rejection loops. A
constant expected number of rate queries is not a constant worst-case bit bound.
An explicit expected O(1) large-shape gamma rejection algorithm is supplied by
gamma_clock_adversary. Ideal continuous samples use the same random-real model as
the motivating algorithm; floating diagnostic output has no exact-TV claim.

2. Binomial-rate clocks without enumerating rates

Let lambda_i=c*binom(n+g*i,o), c>0, fixed integers o>=1,g>=0, n>=o. Acquire b at
i=k-1 and lambda_min at i=0; take q=1-lambda_min/b. Then

 log(b/lambda_min)
   =sum_(j=0)^(o-1) log(1+g(k-1)/(n-j))
   <=o*g*(k-1)/(n-o+1),

 q<=log(b/lambda_min), hence kq<=o*g*k*(k-1)/(n-o+1).
For k<=C sqrt(n), fixed o,g,C, the expected rate-query count is bounded uniformly
in n. An individual rate takes O(o) integer factors (and charged integer bits).
The multiplier c cancels in q and is applied to the final time scale. g=0 needs
no corrections. Decreasing positive populations have the same bound using their
minimum population and the appropriate endpoint as b; they are not assumed to
fall under Petrack--Doty's transformed increasing-population contract.

3. Monotone block compiler (root extension)

For a monotone positive oracle, partition the index set into supplied blocks I_j.
Evaluate their two endpoints to get b_j and a_j; set q_j=1-a_j/b_j. Sample each
block by Section 1 and sum independently. This samples the exact original H, with
expected rate queries <=2J+sum_j |I_j|q_j. This is an acquisition statement for
monotone random-access rates, not for an arbitrary unsupplied rate array.

Suppose |log lambda_(i+1)-log lambda_i|<=L (certificate supplied). Equal block
length m gives q_j<=L(m-1); thus expected cost O(k/m+J+ k L m). Choosing
m~min(k,max(1,L^(-1/2))) gives O(1+k sqrt(L)) for 0<L<=1 and O(k) for L>1;
L=0 gives one gamma draw. No o(k) claim follows when L is large. Endpoint-based
adaptive blocks may improve constants; constructing an optimal partition is not
assumed free. For polynomial population rates log increments are O(1/n) on a
range with minimum population n, yielding O(1+k/sqrt(n)) blocks/corrections.
The exact elementary cost also includes J gamma draws and summing J outputs.

4. Every-execution RATE-QUERY cap, with TV error

For a fixed block set mu=kq. Candidate count X~Binomial(k,q). For every t>0,
E exp(tX)=(1+q(e^t-1))^k<=exp(mu(e^t-1)). At t=1,
Pr(X>=j)<=exp(mu(e-1)-j). Given epsilon in (0,1), set
j=ceil(mu(e-1)+log(1/epsilon)). Run exact sparse skipping until j candidates have
been evaluated. If another candidate exists, return any fixed fallback law
without evaluating its rate. Couple all other executions to the exact algorithm.
Their output laws differ in TV by at most Pr(X>j)<=epsilon. Hence every execution
uses <=j rate queries plus envelope acquisition. The bound does NOT cap geometric
integer lengths, gamma-rejection work, or bit precision. With J blocks allocate
epsilon_j and apply the union bound. Nothing here makes finite floating output
close in TV to a continuous law.

5. Scientific and novelty boundary

This supplies a concrete operation for exact aggregate clocks, not a new physics
principle. Its candidate leverage is removal of a stated exact-timestamp overhead
in an existing reaction batching algorithm. It does not simulate a full event
trace in sublinear output time, infer reaction propensities from experiments,
remove null-reaction overhead, or control explosive/unbounded-population models.
Matched comparison must use Petrack--Doty's exact Section 5 method, not only
Gillespie or an approximate tau-leap. The conditional prefix bridge and complete
complexity substitution require their own proof; see reaction_clock_integration.
