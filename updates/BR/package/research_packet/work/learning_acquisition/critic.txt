1. EXACT STATEMENT AND VERDICT

Let d >= 1, 0 <= rho < 1. Let a scalar sequence g_0,g_1,... satisfy a homogeneous recurrence with characteristic polynomial
  q(x) = product_{i=1}^d (x-lambda_i),  |lambda_i| <= rho,
from index d onward. A recurrence of smaller order can be padded with zero roots.
Let epsilon_j >= 0 and |g_j| <= epsilon_j for 0 <= j < d.
Then for every k >= d,
  |g_k| <= sum_{j=0}^{d-1} epsilon_j B_d(k,j;rho),
  B_d(k,j;rho) = binom(k,j) binom(k-j-1,d-j-1) rho^(k-j).
This is correct. The upper bound is attained simultaneously for all k >= d by q(x)=(x-rho)^d and g_j=(-1)^(d-1-j)epsilon_j. For rho=0 all tails vanish, so sharpness is trivial.

The exact coordinate weights and uniform summed constant are
  W_j(d,rho) = sum_{k>=d} B_d(k,j;rho),
  A(d,rho) = sum_j W_j(d,rho)
           = ( ((1+rho)/(1-rho))^d - 1 ) / 2.
Consequently sum_{k>=d}|g_k| <= sum_j W_j epsilon_j; uniform epsilon yields epsilon A(d,rho). There is NO parity correction. Sharpness survives summation.

2. ELEMENTARY POSITIVITY PROOF (NO UNPROVED SCHUR CLAIM)

Let e_l denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree l in lambda_1,...,lambda_d, with e_0=1. Let
  Q(z)=product_i(1-lambda_i z), H(z)=1/Q(z).
The contribution of initial datum g_j to the generating function G(z)=sum_k g_k z^k is
  g_j z^j E_r(-z)H(z),
  r=d-1-j, E_r(-z)=sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l e_l z^l.
This follows directly by multiplying G by Q and collecting its first d coefficients.

For r >= 0, the following rational identity holds:
  E_r(-z)H(z)
   = 1 + (-1)^r z^(r+1)
       sum_{i=1}^d [lambda_i e_r(lambda_{i+1},...,lambda_d)
                    / product_{h=i}^d(1-lambda_h z)].                 (1)
To verify (1), multiply by Q. In
  sum_i lambda_i e_r(lambda_{i+1},...,lambda_d)
                product_{h<i}(1-lambda_h z),
the coefficient of z^p is (-1)^p e_{r+1+p}(lambda_1,...,lambda_d).
Indeed, for each selected set of r+1+p indices, there is exactly one distinguished index i: the (p+1)-st smallest; select p indices before it and r after it. Thus this sum is
  sum_{p=0}^{d-r-1} (-1)^p e_{r+1+p} z^p.
Substitution gives E_r-Q, proving (1).

Write h_n(x_i,...,x_d) for the complete homogeneous polynomial: the coefficient of z^n in product_{h=i}^d(1-x_h z)^(-1). For k >= d put n=k-j and m=k-d+1. Equation (1) shows that the initial-data coefficient is
  a_kj = (-1)^r S_{m,r}(lambda),
  S_{m,r}(lambda)
    = sum_{i=1}^d lambda_i e_r(lambda_{i+1},...,lambda_d)
                         h_{m-1}(lambda_i,...,lambda_d).
Every coefficient of S is a nonnegative integer and S is homogeneous of degree m+r=k-j. Therefore, including for complex lambda,
  |a_kj| <= S_{m,r}(|lambda|) <= rho^(k-j) S_{m,r}(1,...,1).
No cancellation/positivity theorem is being assumed.

At all ones, set t=d-i. The count is
  S_{m,r}(1^d) = sum_{t=r}^{d-1} binom(t,r) binom(m+t-1,t)
              = binom(m+r-1,r) sum_{t=r}^{d-1} binom(m+t-1,m+r-1)
              = binom(m+r-1,r) binom(m+d-1,m+r)
              = binom(k-j-1,d-j-1) binom(k,j).
The middle equality is the elementary hockey-stick identity. This proves the bound. When every lambda_i=rho, equality holds in the polynomial bound and a_kj=(-1)^r B_d(k,j;rho). Alternating initial signs align every contribution and prove simultaneous sharpness.

An independent check of sharpness: for rho>0, repeated-root solutions have g_k=rho^k p(k), deg p<d. Lagrange interpolation at 0,...,d-1 gives
  g_k = sum_j g_j rho^(k-j) product_{0<=m<d,m!=j}(k-m)/(j-m)
      = sum_j (-1)^(d-1-j) B_d(k,j;rho) g_j.

3. SUMMATION AND COMPUTABLE WEIGHTS

The elementary identity
  binom(k,j) binom(k-j-1,d-j-1)
   = d binom(d-1,j) binom(k,d)/(k-j)
and sum_{k>=d} binom(k,d)t^k=t^d/(1-t)^(d+1) imply
  W_j(d,rho)
    = d binom(d-1,j) integral_0^rho t^(d-j-1)/(1-t)^(d+1) dt.       (2)
All terms are nonnegative, justifying termwise integration.
Summing (2) over j gives
  A(d,rho)=d integral_0^rho (1+t)^(d-1)/(1-t)^(d+1) dt
          = ( ((1+rho)/(1-rho))^d -1 )/2.

For implementation, let a=rho/(1-rho). The change of variables x=t/(1-t) yields the finite positive polynomial
  W_j(d,rho)=d binom(d-1,j)
             sum_{l=0}^j binom(j,l) a^(d-j+l)/(d-j+l).             (3)
Formula (3) avoids an infinite tail sum. Numerical overflow/rounding still requires normal numerical treatment; the real-arithmetic proof alone does not validate floating-point certification.

4. SHIFTED DECIMATION

For integers L>=0 and s>=1, each subsequence h^(r)_q=g_(L+r+qs), 0<=r<s, satisfies a recurrence of order at most d with characteristic roots lambda_i^s, counted with multiplicity. One elementary proof represents the original recurrence by its d-dimensional companion matrix C. The subsequence is a linear functional of (C^s)^q times a starting vector; Cayley-Hamilton for C^s supplies the recurrence. Its eigenvalues are lambda_i^s. This argument handles repeated roots and zero roots.

Applying the theorem and summing the s residue classes proves
  sum_{k>=L+ds}|g_k|
   <= sum_{r=0}^{s-1} sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
          W_j(d,rho^s) |g_(L+r+js)|.                            (4)
The indexed taps in (4) are exactly the contiguous block L,...,L+ds-1, rearranged. Thus if their magnitudes are at most epsilon,
  sum_{k>=L+ds}|g_k| <= s A(d,rho^s) epsilon.                   (5)

For 0<rho<1 choose
  s=ceil(log(2d)/(-log rho)).
Then rho^s<=1/(2d), and a stronger constant than e^2 is available:
  A(d,rho^s)<=1,
so (5) is simply <=s epsilon. To prove it, monotonicity reduces to r=1/(2d), and
  d log((1+1/(2d))/(1-1/(2d)))
    =sum_{n>=0} 1/[(2n+1) 2^(2n) d^(2n)]
    <=sum_{n>=0} 1/[(2n+1) 2^(2n)] =log 3.
Hence the ratio to the d-th power is <=3 and A<=1. The universal constant 1 is attained at d=1,r=1/2. For rho=0 set s=1 and A=0.

Note: (4) is valid, but the s>1 aggregate need not be jointly sharp: separate residue-class extremizers can be incompatible with one original order-d recurrence. No sharpness is claimed for decimation beyond s=1 or separately valid subsequence bounds.

5. PRECISE ACQUISITION INTERFACE

Assume the order cap d and pole bound rho are supplied and correct. Assume an exactly reset zero-state experiment can deliver an impulse of known amplitude U>0. For the relevant tap convention, let
  z_k=U g_k+e_k, |e_k|<=delta.
Then substituting |g_k|<=(|z_k|+delta)/U in (4) gives a deterministic certificate
  tail after N=L+ds <=
    sum_{r,j} W_j(d,rho^s)(|z_(L+r+js)|+delta)/U.                (6)
For any subsequent scalar input with sup norm <=V, the output discrepancy from truncating the impulse response before N has sup norm <=V times (6). For unrestricted scalar bounded inputs this induced convolution norm is exactly the impulse l1 tail, since input signs can align a finite partial sum at a chosen time and then pass to the supremum.

Feedthrough/indexing caveat: in the conventional realization
  x_(t+1)=Ax_t+Bu_t, y_t=Cx_t+Du_t,
the taps are g_0=D and g_k=CA^(k-1)B for k>=1. State dimension d gives the order-d recurrence for the shifted sequence g_(k+1), not automatically for g starting at 0. Therefore use L>=1 in (4)-(6), or use order d+1 and an added zero root. This remains necessary when D=0: for d=1, g_0=0,g_1=CB need not satisfy the one-step recurrence at index1. Do not silently identify recurrence order including feedthrough with state dimension.

Acquisition accounting:
- One exact-reset impulse run measuring taps through N-1 costs N sampling periods, N observations, one reset/preparation, and input amplitude U. Reset/preparation costs and any required settling time must be added explicitly.
- For bounded adversarial noise, repeating a run does not justify reducing delta.
- Since true stable taps approach zero, certificate (6) is eventually <=2s A(d,rho^s)delta/U plus an arbitrarily small amount. Thus this particular test is guaranteed eventually to certify tolerance tau if tau>2s A delta/U. This is a sufficient guarantee for the test, not a universal information-theoretic necessity.
- There is no uniform finite stopping horizon without an initial-amplitude or transfer-magnitude bound; stable repeated-root transients can be arbitrarily large. A supplied initial-tap bound and the pointwise theorem provide a computable horizon. In practice one may collect a predetermined bounded horizon or stop with a valid simultaneous noise guarantee.
- If instead measurement noise is zero-mean sigma-sub-Gaussian and independent across exactly reset repetitions for each tap, M repetitions give normalized tap errors <=eta simultaneously for N taps with probability >=1-alpha when
    eta=(sigma/U) sqrt(2 log(2N/alpha)/M).
  Then use |mean(z_k)/U|+eta in (4). Total physical sampling periods are MN, plus M reset costs; accumulating all tap sums uses O(N) memory. Independence across taps is unnecessary for this union bound. For an adaptive unbounded stopping horizon, use a valid confidence sequence or preregister a finite maximum horizon; a fixed-N union bound does not automatically cover optional stopping.
- Any state-reset residual or impulse calibration error needs its own quantitative error term. It cannot be counted as zero by assumption in a physical claim.

6. ACQUISITION OBSTRUCTIONS

Unknown stability margin, bounded noisy finite experiment:
Let zero-state scalar impulse response G_r(k)=(1-r)r^k for k>=0, 0<=r<1. Its DC gain and l1 norm equal1. Under causal inputs |u_t|<=U,
  y_t=(1-r)sum_{s=0}^t r^(t-s)u_s,
  |y_t|<=U(1-r^(t+1))<=U(1-r^T), t=0,...,T-1.
This bound is attained by constant-sign input. Suppose observations are z_t=y_t+e_t with |e_t|<=delta. If U(1-r^T)<=2delta, the zero system and G_r have an identical possible transcript: recursively choose z_t=y_t/2, with noises y_t/2 for the zero system and -y_t/2 for G_r. Causal adaptive policies receive the same past and therefore select identical inputs. Every DC-gain estimator consequently has worst-case absolute error at least1/2 on the two systems. A shared zero transcript holds under the stronger condition U(1-r^T)<=delta.

For 0<2delta<U, the indistinguishability condition is exactly
  r >= (1-2delta/U)^(1/T).
The simpler sufficient condition is UT(1-r)<=2delta. This obstruction requires positive noise/finite precision; it does not establish impossibility with noiseless exact-real observations and a known finite order cap. It does establish that the positive known-margin theorem does not solve unrestricted acquisition merely by writing rho in its hypotheses.

Unknown order, even with a known radius bound:

It is stable, has l1/DC gain1, and can be realized by a nilpotent shift (all poles0), with order growing with T.
