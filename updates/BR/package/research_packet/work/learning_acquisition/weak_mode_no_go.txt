FINITE-DATA STABILITY CERTIFICATION: ACQUISITION GATES
2026-10-10. Internal elementary reconstruction plus primary-source survey.
Status: no novelty claim; elementary consequences of finite Markov parameters and Gaussian testing.

1. EXACT HORIZON OBSTRUCTION, KNOWN ORDER

Fix n>=1 and r>1. Let J e_j=e_(j+1) for j<n, J e_n=0;
let P e_j=e_(j+1) for j<n, P e_n=e_1. Define
  Stable:   A_s=r J, B=e_1, C=e_n^T.
  Unstable: A_u=r P, B=e_1, C=e_n^T.
In both systems x_(t+1)=A x_t+B u_t, y_t=C x_t+z_t, x_0=0.
The observation noise laws are identical and independent of the system.
The inputs may be arbitrary common randomized, adaptive functions of past outputs.

Both systems are controllable and observable: the controllability columns
B,A B,...,A^(n-1)B are r^j e_(j+1), and observability rows are
r^j e_(n-j)^T. Thus neither construction uses an invisible mode.
Both n-step controllability and observability Gramians have least eigenvalue 1.
Both have ||A||_2<=r, ||B||_2=||C||_2=1.
Yet rho(A_s)=0 and rho(A_u)=r>1.

For Markov parameters h_k=C A^k B,
  h_k(s)=r^(n-1) if k=n-1, and zero otherwise;
  h_k(u)=r^k if k congruent to n-1 modulo n, and zero otherwise.
Therefore h_k(s)=h_k(u) for 0<=k<=2n-2. Since
  y_t=sum_(j=0)^(t-1) h_(t-1-j) u_j+z_t,
the joint input-output laws through y_(2n-1) are exactly identical.
This remains true for adaptive inputs by induction on time. The first possible
difference is y_(2n), through Markov parameter h_(2n-1).

Consequence: no test can distinguish this pair with maximum error below 1/2
by horizon 2n-1. More operationally, if a stability certificate is alpha-sound
on the unstable member, its probability of certifying the stable member at
this horizon is at most alpha. Unlimited input amplitude and zero noise do
not remove the delay. Assumptions essential: zero initial state and no process
noise revealing hidden coordinates; partial output rather than full states.
The stable system has large transient amplification, so a uniform transient
bound would rule out this family as n grows. Matrix-norm bounds alone do not.

Exact integer computation for r=2,n=1,...,8 verified matching Markov sequences
and first differing Markov index 2n-1. This computation checks indexing only;
the proof above is algebraic.

2. UNKNOWN ORDER: ANY FIXED FINITE HORIZON

Fix T>=1,r>1,g!=0. An unstable scalar (A,B,C)=(r,1,g) and the stable
T-dimensional nilpotent shift (A,B,C)=(J,e_1,g(1,r,...,r^(T-1))) have
the same h_k=g r^k for k=0,...,T-1. Their output histories y_1,...,y_T
are therefore identical for every input policy under the conditions above.
Both realizations are minimal: controllability of the shift is the identity,
and its observability determinant is +/- (g r^(T-1))^T, which is nonzero.
Taking g=r^(-T) gives ||C||_2^2=(1-r^(-2T))/(r^2-1), uniformly bounded.
An order cap, quantitative tail promise, or a physical observation primitive
that reveals the missing coordinates is necessary for uniform finite-horizon
certification. This does not prohibit instance-dependent sequential methods.

3. KNOWN SCALAR ORDER: WEAK MODES AND A TIME-ENERGY-NOISE TRADEOFF

Take x_(t+1)=a x_t+b u_t, y_t=c x_t+z_t, x_0=0,
z_t iid N(0,sigma^2), known b,c!=0, and a in {0,r}, r>1.
Both systems are observable and controllable. Put g=bc. Observe y_1,...,y_T
under a common adaptive policy (include its independent random seed in the
transcript), with E_0 sum_(s=0)^(T-1) u_s^2 <= E.

Define the T by T matrix D with row t=1,...,T and column s=0,...,T-1:
  D[t,s]=r^(t-1-s) when s<=t-2, and zero otherwise.
The conditional Gaussian means differ by g(Du)_t. The KL chain rule gives
  KL(P_0||P_r)=g^2/(2 sigma^2) E_0 ||D u||_2^2
              <=g^2 E ||D||_op^2/(2 sigma^2).
Pinsker consequently yields
  TV(P_0,P_r)<=|g| sqrt(E) ||D||_op/(2 sigma).
For any certification event S, P_r(S)<=alpha implies
  P_0(S)<=alpha+|g| sqrt(E)||D||_op/(2 sigma).
If desired completeness is P_0(S)>=q>alpha, binary KL contraction also gives
the necessary condition
  g^2 E ||D||_op^2/(2 sigma^2) >= kl(q,alpha),
where kl(q,alpha)=q log(q/alpha)+(1-q)log((1-q)/(1-alpha)).

An explicit norm bound is
  ||D||_op^2 <= ||D||_F^2
    =sum_(k=1)^(T-1) (T-k)r^(2k)
    =r^2 (r^(2T)-T r^2+T-1)/(r^2-1)^2
    <=r^(2T+2)/(r^2-1)^2.
Thus at fixed horizon and finite energy, arbitrarily small nonzero g defeats
uniform useful certification even at fixed stability margins. At fixed r,
necessary time-energy scaling is E r^(2T)=Omega(sigma^2/g^2) for fixed
soundness/completeness. This is not an assertion that unstable modes are
always hard: waiting lets their signal grow exponentially.

A deterministic input sqrt(E) times the leading right singular vector of D
gives two Gaussian output vectors with mean separation
|g|sqrt(E)||D||_op and equal covariance sigma^2 I. Their optimal equal-prior
testing error is exactly Phi(-|g|sqrt(E)||D||_op/(2 sigma)). This matches the
lower-bound information scale. No claim is made here about the exact optimum
over adaptive policies.

4. PRIMARY SOURCES AND LIMITS

Bakshi, Liu, Moitra, Yau (2023), A New Approach to Learning Linear Dynamical
Systems, arXiv:2301.09519v1, Lemmas 9.8-9.9 and Theorem 9.11.
https://arxiv.org/html/2301.09519v1#S9
For ||C A^s v||<=delta all s, perturbing B to B+v u^T gives trajectory TV
O(T^2(m+p)||u||delta). Assumptions include Gaussian unit input/measurement
noise, x_0=0,D=0, common process covariance at least BB^T, and a smallness
condition T^2(m+p)||u||<=1/(100delta). A separate genericity condition yields
constant parameter-recovery error up to similarity for o(1/sqrt(delta(m+p)))
samples. This is a passive-Gaussian-input parameter-identification result,
not by itself a stability-certification lower bound.

Tsiamis, Ziemann, Morari, Matni, Pappas (COLT 2022), Learning to Control
Linear Systems can be Hard, Theorem 5 and Assumption 2.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/tsiamis22a/tsiamis22a.pdf
With adaptive exploration and E||u_t||^2<=sigma_u^2, stabilizing the class of
mu-robustly coupled systems of controllability index kappa requires
  N sigma_u^2 >= (1/2) mu^(-2kappa+2) ((1-mu)/mu)^2 log(1/(3delta)).
Here mu<1; the positive numerical bound is useful for delta<1/3.
This is a controller-construction lower bound even with full state observations.
It prevents hiding exponential dependence inside a system-theoretic constant.

Sarkar, Rakhlin, Dahleh (JMLR 2021), Finite Time LTI System Identification,
Section 5.4 and Theorem 5.4; proof construction in Section 15.
https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume22/19-725/19-725.pdf
For their stable-system order-estimation hard pair, active inputs satisfying
E||U_t||^2<=1 do not eliminate the inverse-square weak-Hankel-mode dependence.
The stated bound has T<=C R^2 zeta^(-2) log(2/delta), with order error at least
delta for one member of the pair; zeta controls the weak-to-strong Hankel
singular-value ratio and R the noise-to-signal bound. This concerns model
order and stable approximation, not an unstable hidden-mode guarantee.

Zhang, Nakahira, Qu (2025), Stabilizing Linear Systems under Partial
Observability: Sample Complexity and Fundamental Limits, arXiv:2503.16756v1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16756v1
This contemporary comparator estimates only the unstable subsystem. Its
Theorem 5.3 retains quantitative controllability/observability conditioning,
stable/unstable spectral margins, and a robustness budget. Assumption 5.2
requires a controller for the unstable subsystem whose small-gain tolerance
exceeds the stable tail plus 3 epsilon_*. Replacing full identification with
unstable-subspace recovery is therefore an existing method; eliminating its
acquisition and stable-tail promises would be the substantive new target.

Research implication: finite Hankel data can support predictions over the
measured horizon yet fail to certify the infinite-horizon physical claim.
Any proposed advance must explicitly acquire an order/tail promise, overcome
the demonstrated information scale with an additional physical primitive,
or weaken the claim to a finite-horizon guarantee. Improved post-processing
alone cannot distinguish identical laws.
