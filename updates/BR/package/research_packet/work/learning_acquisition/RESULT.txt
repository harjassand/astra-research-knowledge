DECISIVE OUTCOME

One can certify an unobserved scalar LTI impulse-response tail using acquired
noisy taps, a SUPPLIED order cap d, and a SUPPLIED pole-radius bound rho<1,
without supplying a realization, Hankel singular-value margin, eigenbasis
condition number, impulse-response envelope, or known observability matrix.
An elementary decimation certificate is proved below. However its mathematical
engine is classical Turan/de Bruijn extrapolation. Existing rational Bernstein
inequalities give a better asymptotic horizon. This is useful transferable
research capital, not a frontier breakthrough. Unknown order or unknown stability
margin cannot simply be inferred from finite noisy taps; explicit no-gos follow.

EXACT INTERFACE AND INDEXING

The scalar sequence g_k, k>=0, obeys a homogeneous order <=d recurrence whose
characteristic roots, counted with multiplicity, have modulus <=rho<1. Padding
by zero roots gives exactly d. For an LTI state-space system this applies to
g_k=C A^k B. Under x_(t+1)=A x_t+B u_t, y_t=C x_t+D u_t, these are the physical
impulse taps y_(k+1), NOT y_k including arbitrary D. The direct tap D must be
measured separately or the order cap increased by one. Even D=0 does not justify
including the leading zero in an order-d recurrence.

Allow complex roots and nonnormal/repeated-pole systems. No minimum residue,
root separation, or observability margin is assumed. Exact reset to zero and
unit impulse access are supplied experimental operations. Noise assumptions
are specified separately; adversarial bounded noise cannot be averaged away.

THEOREM 1: SHARP FINITE-WINDOW EXTRAPOLATION (CLASSICAL ENGINE)

For k>=d and |g_j|<=epsilon_j, 0<=j<d,

 |g_k| <= sum_(j=0)^(d-1) epsilon_j binom(k,j)
                 binom(k-j-1,d-j-1) rho^(k-j).

Consequently sum_(k>=d)|g_k| <= sum_j W_j(d,rho) epsilon_j, where

 W_j(d,r) = r^(d-j) sum_(m=d-1-j)^(d-1)
                         binom(m,d-1-j)/(1-r)^(m+1).

This positive finite formula avoids cancellation. All pointwise and summed
inequalities are simultaneously attained by the recurrence (E-rho)^d g=0,
with g_j=(-1)^(d-1-j) epsilon_j. Therefore, for uniform epsilon,

 sup sum_(k>=d)|g_k| = epsilon A(d,rho),
 A(d,r) = 1/2 [((1+r)/(1-r))^d - 1].

This is an exact minimax statement for the stated d-initial-coordinate box,
not a lower bound for arbitrary longer experiments or arbitrary input design.

PROOF OUTLINE (full elementary proof in critic.txt)

Let lambda_1,...,lambda_d be the roots and D(z)=prod_i(1-lambda_i z).
The generating function for initial coordinate j is
z^j E_(d-1-j)(-z)/D(z), where E_b(-z)=sum_(l=0)^b(-1)^l e_l(lambda)z^l.
An elementary telescoping identity gives

 E_b(-z)/D(z) = 1 + (-1)^b z^(b+1)
     sum_(i=1)^d lambda_i e_b(lambda_(i+1),...,lambda_d)
                  /prod_(h=i)^d(1-lambda_h z).

Thus every coefficient after the initial window is, up to the common sign
(-1)^b, a polynomial in the roots with nonnegative integer coefficients.
Its modulus is maximized by setting every root to rho. At all roots 1, the
coefficient is
sum_(m=b)^(d-1) binom(m,b) binom(k-d+m,m),
which simplifies by the hockey-stick identity to
binom(k,j)binom(k-j-1,d-j-1). At roots rho its homogeneous degree is k-j.
This proves the pointwise bound and equality construction. Summing the positive
generating function at z=1 proves W_j. Summing j gives A, equivalently
W_j=d binom(d-1,j) integral_0^r t^(d-j-1)/(1-t)^(d+1) dt and then the binomial
theorem. This proof does not assume diagonalizability.

THEOREM 2: DECIMATED ACQUIRED TAIL CERTIFICATE

Choose integer s>=1, put R=rho^s and L=d*s. For any offset m>=0,

 sum_(k>=m+L)|g_k|
 <= sum_(r=0)^(s-1) sum_(j=0)^(d-1)
                  W_j(d,R) |g_(m+r+js)|.                          (1)

Reason: for each residue r, h_q=g_(m+r+qs) has recurrence order <=d with
roots lambda_i^s (use the companion matrix raised to power s). Apply Theorem 1
and sum disjoint residue classes. This argument includes collisions of roots.

If observations satisfy simultaneous deterministic bars
|hat_g_k-g_k|<=b_k, replace each |g_k| in (1) by |hat_g_k|+b_k.
This is a data-acquired finite certificate of an infinite tail, conditional
on d,rho being correct. It does not identify poles or estimate a realization.

For 0<rho<1 choose s=ceil(log(2d)/(-log rho)); for rho=0 choose s=1.
Then R<=1/(2d), A(d,R)<=1, and the sum of all weights in (1) is
V=s A(d,R)<=s. Hence a recent L-window bounded by epsilon certifies tail
<=s epsilon. Proof of A<=1: for d>=1,
d log((1+1/(2d))/(1-1/(2d)))
=sum_(n>=0)1/[(2n+1)2^(2n)d^(2n)]<=log 3.

Thus the exponential precision cost of a d-tap window is traded for a window
L=O(d log(2d)/(1-rho)). This is NOT proven optimal; stronger prior art below
removes log d from the horizon order.

COROLLARY: FINITE-PREFIX GLOBAL IDENTIFIABILITY

For the same L,V, ||g||_1 <= ||g_(0:L-1)||_1+V||g_(0:L-1)||_infinity
<= (L+V)||g_(0:L-1)||_infinity. For differences of two systems of order <=d,
use order cap 2d, since their difference has at most 2d poles. Therefore any
two bounded-pole d-order models agreeing within epsilon on that longer prefix
agree globally in l1 within (L+V)epsilon. This is information-theoretic;
finding a compatible bounded-order model can be nonconvex and is NOT provided
as an efficient algorithm. The FIR stopping algorithm below avoids that fit.

THEOREM 3: COMPLETE RESET/NOISE ACQUISITION GUARANTEE

Assume exact zero resets and independent runs returning g_k+xi_(run,k), where
for each fixed k, the noises across runs are independent centered sigma-
subgaussian. Independence across times within a run is unnecessary. sigma is
known; arbitrary process noise is NOT automatically covered by this promise.
No hidden queries to g or its poles are permitted to the estimator.

At epoch a=0,1,... let H_a=L*2^a, delta_a=delta/2^(a+1),
b_a=eta/[4(H_a+V)],
N_a=max(1, ceil(2 sigma^2 b_a^(-2) log(2 H_a/delta_a))).
Acquire N_a FRESH reset/impulse runs, each for H_a taps, and average each tap.
Output the length-H_a FIR estimate if

 H_a b_a + sum_(r,j) W_j(d,R)
                  (|hat_g_(H_a-L+r+js)|+b_a) <= eta.             (2)

On an event of probability >=1-delta, every epoch has all coordinate errors
<=b_a, by subgaussian concentration and two union bounds. On that one event,
any output, including adaptive stopping, obeys ||g-hat_g_FIR||_1<=eta.
Consequently all causal convolutions driven by |u_t|<=U have output error
<=U eta, uniformly over all times. This is a stronger metric than H-infinity
but its straightforward acquisition implementation has higher sample cost.

Since every stable finite-order recurrence tends to zero, the exact right side
of (1) for the trailing window tends to zero. If it is <=eta/2, (2) succeeds:
observed statistic <= exact tail-bound functional +(H_a+2V)b_a<=eta.
Thus stopping is eventually finite on the confidence event. There is NO
uniform finite stopping time over unbounded initial amplitudes/nonnormal gain.

FULL COSTS

At stopping epoch M, sensor samples/physical time steps are exactly
Q=sum_(a=0)^M N_a H_a; resets and unit-energy impulses are sum_a N_a.
The final horizon is H_M and memory is O(H_M) streaming sums plus observations
needed for audit. Computation is O(Q+M d^2+sum_a H_a), excluding physical reset
latency, which adds (sum N_a)*reset_cost. All failed epochs are charged.
Q=O(H_M + sigma^2 H_M(H_M+V)^2 eta^(-2)
                  [log(H_M/delta)+M]).
Large sigma, long memory, or small eta can make this expensive; no advantage
over optimized frequency-domain/coarse FIR experimental design is claimed.
Measuring b precision and exact reset/preparation costs are part of the access
contract. Positive rational weights permit exact arithmetic with rational tap
intervals; the Gaussian acquisition prototype uses floating arithmetic and is
labelled a numerical diagnostic, not a machine-verified certificate.

DECISIVE NO-GOS / NEGATIVES

1. No known order cap: g_k=1{k=T} is a finite-dimensional nilpotent delay,
all poles zero, invisible to any zero-state experiment up to the delay. It has
DC and l1 gain 1, whereas zero has gain 0. Arbitrarily long finite prefixes
do not rule it out. Arbitrary adaptive bounded inputs do not change causality.

2. No known pole margin: g_k=(1-r)r^k, 0<r<1, is scalar, stable, and has DC1.
Its first T unit-impulse taps tend uniformly to zero as r tends to 1. More
generally |u_t|<=U implies outputs up to T bounded by U(1-r^T). For adversarial
noise radius nu, if this is <=2nu, there is a common transcript with the zero
system, so minimax DC error is >=1/2. For Gaussian sensor variance sigma^2
and total finite adaptive input energy E under the zero system, chain-rule KL
is <=(1-r)^2 T^2 E/(2sigma^2), hence tends to zero; testing cannot uniformly
certify the pole margin from finite noisy data. See weak_mode_no_go.txt.

3. The sharp d-window amplification is not a universal lower bound on every
identification algorithm: decimation and longer-prefix primary-source bounds
defeat that interpretation. A previous candidate novelty interpretation was
therefore rejected, with the negative retained here.

TRANSFER HANDOFF / REMAINING OBLIGATIONS

Useful mechanism: finite-order stable dynamics permit acquired tail bounds
without acquiring a well-conditioned state representation; time-scale choice
can replace an apparent precision barrier by an explicit observation-horizon
cost. This may be useful for experimental stopping rules and validating weak
stable remainders, when d,rho come from trusted physical structure.

Essential unsolved acquisition: obtaining valid structural order/radius promises
without merely importing the original hard problem. Neither finite simulation
nor confidence bars solve this. Unknown initial state, imperfect resets,
uncontrolled process noise, unknown sensor scale, nonlinear drift, and MIMO
resource scaling require further work; no transfer is asserted for them.

Prior-art dominance warrants PARKING this as a transformative lead. An efficient
strong-comparator experimental campaign would be appropriate only if a concrete
application offers externally justified d,rho and a real missing certification
capability, rather than another theorem searching for an application.
