DISPLACED COEFFICIENT AUDIT: UNIFORM POSITIVE-INTEGRAL EVALUATION
Status: internally reconstructed constructive theorem; not externally reviewed.
No frozen Gaussian product code is changed by this work.

TASK AND OUTPUT INTERFACE

For rational a,c>=0, binary integer n>=0 and p>=1, evaluate
  H_n=[x^n] exp(a*x^2/2+c*x)
with relative error <=2^-p. The intended output is a certified logarithmic
interval, or a binary floating enclosure with a binary-encoded exponent.
For the logarithmic interface require interval width <=2^-(p+1); choosing
any point in that interval and exponentiating then gives relative error
at most exp(2^-(p+1))-1<=2^-p.
An ordinary exact rational expansion may require Omega(n) output bits and
is NOT the promised interface. Let B=bitlength(n) and L be the input bit
length of a,c. This note constructs an arithmetic procedure polynomial in
B+L+p. It does not claim that an existing special-function implementation
automatically has this complexity.

The positive real integral below removes the tiny-displacement parity
cancellation. A two-saddle local CLT is not necessary.

1. EXACT POSITIVE INTEGRAL

Assume a,c>0 and n>=1. Put d=c/sqrt(a), s=(-1)^n, and
  b_s(t)=1+s*exp(-2*d*t),
  m=(d+sqrt(d^2+4*n))/2.
Then m>=sqrt(n)>=1 and m-d=n/m. Gaussian moments give
  H_n = a^(n/2)/(n!*sqrt(2*pi))
        * integral_0^infty t^n exp(-(t-d)^2/2) b_s(t) dt.
For even n the integrand is a positive sum. For odd n it is a positive
difference; evaluate b_-(t) as -expm1(-2*d*t), or analytically strip d.

Define the entire function (n is an integer)
  F(z)=(z/m)^n
       * exp[-n*(z-m)/m-(z-m)^2/2]
       * b_s(z)/b_s(m),
and I=integral_0^infty F(t) dt. Exactly,
  H_n = a^(n/2) m^n exp[-(n/m)^2/2]
        * b_s(m) I / (n!*sqrt(2*pi)).                 (1)
The corresponding log expression avoids huge intermediate powers.

Special cases: H_0=1; a=0 gives c^n/n!; c=0 gives zero for odd n and
(a/2)^(n/2)/(n/2)! for even n. With a=c=0 and n>0 the result is zero.

2. REAL BOUNDS UNIFORM IN d

Set phi(t)=n*log(t)-(t-d)^2/2. Then phi'(m)=0 and
  phi''(t)=-1-n/t^2<=-1.
Thus for x=t-m with t>0,
  exp(phi(t)-phi(m)) <= exp(-x^2/2).                 (2)
On t in [m,m+1], n/t^2<=1, so phi(t)-phi(m)>=-(t-m)^2.

For even n, 1/2 <= b_+(t)/b_+(m) <=2 for all t>0.
For odd n, b_-(t) is positive, increasing, concave, and zero at t=0.
Consequently
  min(1,t/m) <= b_-(t)/b_-(m) <=max(1,t/m).         (3)
It follows that
  I >= (1/2)*integral_0^1 exp(-x^2) dx
    >=1/(2*e)>1/6.                                (4)

For R>=1, the omitted integral outside |t-m|<=R is at most
  4*exp(-R^2/2).                                  (5)
For even n, use twice the two Gaussian tails, each at most exp(-R^2/2)/R.
For odd n, (3) bounds the left multiplier by 1 and the right by
1+x/m; their tail sum is at most (2/R+1/m)exp(-R^2/2)<=3exp(-R^2/2).
These bounds are uniform as d tends to zero or infinity.

3. UNIFORM COMPLEX DISK BOUND

Take p>=1 and integer R=ceil(sqrt(2*(p+10))). If
  n < 16*(R+1)^2,
use the direct positive coefficient sum instead:
  H_n=sum_{j=0..floor(n/2)} (a/2)^j*c^(n-2j)/(j!*(n-2j)!).
This has O(p) terms, so it is a polynomial-cost branch.

Otherwise m>=4*(R+1). Partition [m-R,m+R] into panels of length at most
1/2. On the complex disk of radius 1/2 about any panel midpoint, put
z=t+i*y. Then t>=m/2 and |y|<=1/2. The modulus of the first two factors
in F is
  exp(phi(t)-phi(m))
  * exp[(n/2)*log(1+y^2/t^2)+y^2/2]
  <= exp[-(t-m)^2/2+(1+n/t^2)*y^2/2]
  <= exp(5/8).                                    (6)

For even n,
  |b_+(z)/b_+(m)|<=2.
For odd n, the exact integral identity
  b_-(z)=2*d*z*integral_0^1 exp(-2*d*z*v) dv
implies
  |b_-(z)| <= (|z|/t)*b_-(t).
Combining with (3),
  |b_-(z)/b_-(m)| <= |z|/min(t,m) <=2              (7)
on these disks. In the t<=m case, t>=m/2>=2 and |y|<=1/2 suffice.
In the t>=m case, t<=m+R+1/2<=5*m/4 and |y|/m<=1/8 suffice.
Therefore uniformly in all allowed parameters,
  |F(z)| <=2*exp(5/8)<4.                          (8)

This entire-function bound avoids logarithmic singularities of cosh or
sinh and does not shrink as d grows. It also preserves the common linear
zero in d in the odd case before error bounds are applied.

4. CERTIFIED QUADRATURE AND COMPLEXITY

Expand F about each panel midpoint to degree K. Cauchy's estimate on its
radius-1/2 disk gives |Taylor coefficient_j|<=4*2^j. On the panel,
|z-midpoint|<=1/4, so truncation error is at most 4*2^-K. Exact integration
of the Taylor polynomial therefore has total absolute truncation error
at most 8*R*2^-K.

For example choose
  K=p+ceil(log2(192*R))+10.
Together with (4)-(5), these choices leave more than enough margin for
relative error 2^-p after allocating a fixed fraction to certified finite
precision arithmetic and the prefactor. There are O(sqrt(p)) panels and
degree O(p+log(p)) on each. Even naive truncated-power-series arithmetic
has polynomial cost. This deliberately conservative bound is not a claim
of practical optimality.

Taylor coefficients are directly constructible, not an oracle. Around a
positive panel midpoint w, expand (z/m)^n*exp[-n(z-m)/m-(z-m)^2/2] by
formal power series with binary powering, or exponentiate its local log
series. The latter has coefficients for j>=2 derived from
n*(-1)^(j+1)/(j*w^j), plus the quadratic exponential term. The linear
coefficient is best computed as
  -(w-m)*(1+n/(w*m)),
which avoids subtracting nearby large quantities. The constant factor
uses n*(log1p((w-m)/m)-(w-m)/m)-(w-m)^2/2, with a stable series if needed.
The b factor uses expm1 for its constant term; for j>=1 its coefficients
are s*exp(-2*d*w)*(-2*d)^j/j!, divided by b_s(m).

For bit conditioning, |w-m|<=R, w>=m/2, and n/w^2<=4. The local log
series therefore has constant O(R^2), linear coefficient O(R), and
degree-j coefficients bounded by O(4*2^(j-2)/j) for j>=2. Formal
exponentiation to degree K needs polynomial(K+R) guard bits; it does not
hide guard precision proportional to the numerical magnitude of n.
Concretely, take exactly 4*R panels of width 1/2, so their offsets from m
are dyadic. If q_j are the local log-series coefficients and C_j the
exponential coefficients, use
  C_0=exp(q_0),
  C_k=(1/k)*sum_(j=1)^k j*q_j*C_(k-j).
A crude roundoff-amplification bound of [C*K*(R+1)]^K for an absolute
constant C gives sufficient working precision
  O(p+K*log(K*(R+1))+B+L).
Tight optimization of this guard-bit bound is unnecessary for polynomial
complexity. The exponentially damped b-factor derivatives can similarly
be evaluated from their logs before multiplication.

When d is tiny, analytically cancel the common d or use expm1 with a
relative enclosure. When d is huge, bound exponentially small terms by
their logarithms and discard only under a certified error allocation.
Rational input bit length bounds the nonzero d and b_-(m) below by
2^-O(L), so no reciprocal-exponent precision is hidden. Intermediate
Taylor computation may require polynomial(p+B+L) guard bits; the analytic
bound alone is not a claim that fixed machine precision is sufficient.

Compute the logarithm of (1) using sqrt, log, expm1 and logGamma. All
arguments are algebraic of fixed degree or elementary expressions in
them. Certified elementary evaluation is polynomial in requested
precision and input length. LogGamma at an integer is evaluable by
shifting to an argument O(p) if needed and using a Stirling expansion
with its explicit remainder. The prefactor's large cancellations require
absolute log accuracy; O(p+B+L) guard precision suffices up to standard
polynomial overhead. The exponent of the final binary floating result
has only polynomial(B+L) bits even when its magnitude is exponential.

Conclusion: within the stated floating/log output interface, the
coefficient H_n has a deterministic relative evaluator with bit cost
polynomial in B+L+p. The proof uses a positive Gaussian moment integral
and uniform certified Taylor quadrature; it does not rely on an
unproved uniform Hermite asymptotic expansion.

5. WHAT THE NAIVE PARITY-CORRECTED CLT DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

For independent X~Pois(mu), Y~Pois(nu), 2*mu+nu=n, the saddle tilt gives
  H_n=exp(mu+nu)*lambda^-n*Pr(2*X+Y=n),
where a*lambda^2+c*lambda=n, mu=a*lambda^2/2 and nu=c*lambda.
The elementary parity-corrected normal approximation is
  q_n=(1+(-1)^n*exp(-2*nu))/sqrt(2*pi*(2*n-nu)).
It is not a certified relative evaluator. A concrete exact failure at
n=1, nu=1/2, mu=1/4 is
  true p=exp(-3/4)/2=0.23618327637050734,
  q=(1-exp(-1))/sqrt(3*pi)=0.20590379524607383,
an underestimate by 12.8203% relative to p.

However, the odd-n nu->0 limit does NOT produce a divergent relative
error. The exact ratio tends to
  sqrt(pi*n)*exp(-n/2)*(n/2)^((n-1)/2)/Gamma((n+1)/2)
  =1+1/(12*n)+O(n^-2).
Separately bounding two saddle errors and subtracting in the odd case
can introduce a spurious 1/nu factor. This is a failure of that error
argument, not a lower bound against uniform relative evaluation.
The full independent calculation, with explicitly nonuniform first-order
expansions, is in work/gaussian_transfer/parity_counterexample.txt.

6. SCOPE AND PRIOR-ART STATUS

The Gaussian-moment identity and certified analytic quadrature are
standard mechanisms; this note does not claim their historical novelty.
A primary-source search found existing compound-discrete local-limit
and Hermite-asymptotic literature, including:
  A. N. Timashev, Local Limit Theorems for Compound Discrete Distributions,
  https://doi.org/10.1137/S0040585X97T990538
  Wei Shi, Gergő Nemes, Xiang-Sheng Wang, Roderick Wong,
  Error bounds for the asymptotic expansions of the Hermite polynomials,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01054
No theorem from those works is used as a premise in the proof above.
The new operational implication for the parent programme is narrowly
that coefficient evaluation itself need not reinstate O(n) work.
The displaced aggregate distribution can still have separated modes;
evaluation alone does not establish an efficient full displaced sampler.

7. FOLLOW-UP: PARITY-RESTRICTED AGGREGATE LOG-CONCAVITY

The remaining multimodality obstruction has an exact parity resolution.
For j>=0 define E_j=H_(2j), O_j=H_(2j+1). Their generating functions are
  sum_j E_j*y^j = exp(a*y/2)*cosh(c*sqrt(y)),
  sum_j O_j*y^j = exp(a*y/2)*sinh(c*sqrt(y))/sqrt(y).
Thus both are convolutions of
  A_j=(a/2)^j/j!
with respectively
  B_j=c^(2j)/(2j)!,   D_j=c^(2j+1)/(2j+1)!.
Each of A,B,D is nonnegative log-concave and has no internal support gap:
successive positive ratios are respectively
  (a/2)/(j+1),
  c^2/[(2j+1)(2j+2)],
  c^2/[(2j+2)(2j+3)],
and decrease with j. Convolution preserves this property. Therefore
  H_n^2 >= H_(n-2)*H_(n+2)                         (9)
for every n>=2, separately on each parity (zeros handled directly).

For completeness, the convolution assertion has a short algebraic proof.
Extend a sequence U by zeros at negative indices and let
T_U(i,j)=U_(j-i), i,j nonnegative. Log-concavity plus no internal zeros
implies all order-two minors of T_U are nonnegative: if the outer terms
are nonzero, monotonicity of adjacent ratios shows that the product of
the two more central terms is at least the product of the outer terms;
zero endpoint cases are immediate. If C=A*B then T_C=T_A*T_B. For any
fixed order-two minor the relevant entries involve only a finite range
of intermediate indices. Cauchy--Binet expresses it as a sum of products
of nonnegative order-two minors of T_A and T_B. Hence it is nonnegative.
Taking consecutive rows/columns yields C_j^2>=C_(j-1)*C_(j+1).

Now for the displaced Gaussian aggregate law
  w(k)=(b^k/k!)*H_(h-k)^2,   k=0,...,h,
restrict k to either parity. Its adjacent ratio on that sublattice is
  w(k+2)/w(k)
   = b^2/[(k+1)(k+2)] * [H_(h-k-2)/H_(h-k)]^2.    (10)
By (9), H_(n-2)/H_n increases as n advances by 2. As k increases by 2,
n=h-k decreases, so the H ratio in (10) decreases; its factorial factor
also decreases. Thus each nonzero parity subsequence is log-concave and
unimodal. This is stronger than the full-sequence unimodality assertion,
which is false by the preserved h=2 counterexample in displaced_gate.txt.

Operational consequence: construct a one-dimensional rejection envelope
on each parity using the uniform H evaluator above and combine their
unnormalized envelope masses. A common scaling reference permits parity
selection from envelope masses; exact parity normalizers need not be
evaluated. The parent programme must still specify and verify its finite
precision envelope, sampling, and total-error accounting before this
observation can be called a complete implemented displaced sampler.

8. FINITE CHECKS AND INDEPENDENT INTERNAL CRITIC

The independent internal critic checked the exact prefactor, endpoints,
Gaussian tails, midpoint disk bounds and guard-bit construction without
finding an analytic counterexample. This is internal review, not external
verification. The reproducible finite checks are
  work/gaussian_transfer/displaced_parity_audit_checks.py
  work/gaussian_transfer/displaced_parity_audit_checks.json.
They check exact rational parity log-concavity over a finite parameter

the uniform claim rests on the proof, not on these finite checks.
