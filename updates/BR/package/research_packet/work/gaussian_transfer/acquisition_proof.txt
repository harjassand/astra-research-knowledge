EXACT PHYSICAL ACQUISITION LOWER BOUND
Independent audit of the construction supplied by the root research branch
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: internally reconstructed complete mathematical argument; not externally or formally verified.

THEOREM (RAW-COPY ACQUISITION OBSTRUCTION)
Let m>=1 be an integer and h=4m^2. Let a=1/4, t=1/(4m), and consider the two normalized pure two-mode Gaussian states
 |psi_j> = Z_j^(-1/2) exp[(a/2)(A^dagger)^2+t_j A^dagger B^dagger]|0,0>,
where t_0=0, t_1=t. Equivalently their real entrywise nonnegative pair matrices are
 B_0=[[a,0],[0,0]],   B_1=[[a,t],[t,0]].
An algorithm is given h and the promise that an unknown input is one of these two states, but not the selector j. Its only access to the actual input is at most N independent copies of |psi_j>. It may perform arbitrary collective or adaptive quantum operations, use unlimited classical computation and independent ancillas/randomness, and must deliver an unconditional classical output on each invocation.
Suppose for both j its output distribution is within total variation epsilon<1/6 of the photon count in physical mode B conditioned on observing exactly h photons in physical mode A. Then
 N >= (15h/8)(1/3-2epsilon)^2.
In particular, epsilon=1/12 requires N>=5h/96.
The copy count is linear in numerical h and therefore exponential in the bit length of h along this family, although all promised state parameters and h have O(log h) bit encodings.

1. PHYSICALITY AND FINITE ENERGY
For m>=1, t<=1/4. The positive eigenvalue of B_1 is
 (a+sqrt(a^2+4t^2))/2 <= (1+sqrt(5))/8 <1.
The magnitude of the negative eigenvalue is smaller; B_0 has norm 1/4. Thus both are normalizable physical pure Gaussian states, uniformly away from the singularity. Their energy is bounded independently of h. The lower bound does not use a high-energy unconditioned input.
The two states can be prepared by standard pure-Gaussian transformations in principle. This theorem grants copies as the input resource; it does not charge for designing the two known possible preparations or assert a hardware realization.

2. EXACT NORM AND OVERLAP WITHOUT IMPORTING A DETERMINANT IDENTITY
For arbitrary a,t with 0<=a<1-t^2, expansion in the Fock basis gives
 |phi(a,t)> = sum_{r,k>=0} [a^r t^k sqrt((2r+k)!k!)/(2^r r! k!)] |2r+k,k>.
Consequently
 Z(a,t)=sum_{r,k>=0} (2r+k)! a^(2r)t^(2k)/(4^r(r!)^2 k!).
First sum over k using
 sum_{k>=0} (2r+k)! x^k/k! = (2r)!/(1-x)^(2r+1),
and then use the central-binomial series. This proves
 Z(a,t)=[(1-t^2)^2-a^2]^(-1/2).
In particular Z_0=(1-a^2)^(-1/2).
The mode-B vacuum component of phi(a,t) equals phi(a,0), hence
 <phi(a,0)|phi(a,t)>=Z_0.
For the normalized states the squared fidelity F is therefore
 F=|<psi_0|psi_1>|^2=Z_0/Z_1
  =sqrt( ((1-t^2)^2-a^2)/(1-a^2) ).
Since t^2=1/(4h)=a/h and 1-a^2=15/16,
 F=sqrt(1-(8h-1)/(15h^2)).
This independently confirms the supplied determinant-fidelity expression.

3. CONDITIONAL OUTPUT SEPARATION
The herald h is even and has strictly positive probability under both states. Under state 0, mode B is vacuum, so its conditional count law Q_0 is the point mass at 0.
For state 1, only k=0,2,...,h are possible, and the exact unnormalized conditional photon-count weights are
 q_k = h! a^(h-k)t^(2k)/[k! 2^(h-k)((h-k)/2)!^2].
The common global normalization and herald probability cancel. Thus
 q_2/q_0 = (t^2/a)^2 h^2/2 = 1/2.
All further weights are nonnegative, so
 Q_1(K>=2) = (sum_{k>=2}q_k)/(q_0+sum_{k>=2}q_k)
             >=q_2/(q_0+q_2)=1/3.
Therefore TV(Q_0,Q_1)>=1/3. This is an exact finite-h argument; it uses no CLT, tail approximation, or parameter-estimation asymptotics.

4. INPUT INDISTINGUISHABILITY AND DATA PROCESSING
For N independent pure-state copies, trace distance is exactly
 D_N = (1/2)||(|psi_0><psi_0|)^tensor N-(|psi_1><psi_1|)^tensor N||_1
     = sqrt(1-F^N).
Let x=(8h-1)/(15h^2). Since 0<=x<1,
 1-F=1-sqrt(1-x)<=x<=8/(15h).
The elementary inequality 1-F^N<=N(1-F) gives
 D_N<=sqrt(8N/(15h)).
Any allowed algorithm is a quantum channel followed by a classical readout; trace-distance contraction therefore gives
 TV(P_0,P_1)<=D_N,
where P_j denotes its unconditional output law. Adaptive processing and joint measurements do not evade this inequality. A worst-case copy bound of N can be padded by unused copies and has the same bound.
On the other hand, the triangle inequality and the two epsilon-accuracy conditions imply
 TV(P_0,P_1)>=TV(Q_0,Q_1)-TV(P_0,Q_0)-TV(P_1,Q_1)
             >=1/3-2epsilon.
Combining and squaring proves
 N>=(15h/8)(1/3-2epsilon)^2.
For epsilon=1/12 this is N>=15h/(8*36)=5h/96.

5. EXACT INPUT PROMISE AND LIMITS
This is an acquisition lower bound for an unknown state accessible through raw independent copies. It is not a lower bound for the explicitly supplied rational-kernel sampler. If the actual B_j, the selector j, a preparation oracle identifying j, or an already heralded state is supplied, the premise fails and the lower bound need not apply.
Giving the algorithm both candidate descriptions and h is allowed: only the selector must remain unknown. Side information independent of j is allowed. More informative oracles require a separate analysis.
The theorem assumes unconditional sample delivery and a fixed or worst-case copy budget. Free postselection on an uncounted successful run is not allowed. A protocol with failures must include the failure flag in its output/error or explicitly account for success probability and raw copies. The theorem as written does not by itself give an expected-stopping-time lower bound for arbitrary unbounded protocols.
It establishes a necessary input-resource cost even with unbounded computational power. It does not establish an upper bound, does not show that order h copies suffice for all unknown Gaussian states, and does not rule out efficient acquisition under stronger structural information.
The target herald is rare, but no inverse-herald-probability argument is used. The lower bound follows from indistinguishable raw inputs inducing distinguishable conditional outputs.

INTERPRETATION
The complete split is now rigorous: the supplied-model conditional-count computation can have polynomial dependence on log h, while obtaining a usable model/sample from an unknown physical source can require Omega(h) raw copies even for two nonnegative Gaussian modes uniformly below instability. A capability claim must state which interface is provided.
