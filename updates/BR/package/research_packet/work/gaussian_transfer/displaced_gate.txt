ATTEMPTED DISPLACED EXTENSION: EXACT NEW OBSTRUCTION TO NAIVE 1-D UNIMODAL ROUTE

For one observed physical mode and effective boundary S=[[a,b],[b,a]], singleton t=(c,c), define H_n=[x^n] exp(a x^2/2+c x). After conditioning on h photons, the aggregate cross-pair count k has weight
w(k) proportional to (b^k/k!) H_(h-k)^2, k=0,...,h.
Given k, each side's own-pair count q has weight proportional to (a/2)^q c^(h-k-2q)/(q!(h-k-2q)!), so the conditional q law is unimodal in q. However, evaluating H_n through its natural recurrence takes O(n), which reinstates the binary-large-h obstacle.

Moreover, the k marginal is not generally unimodal, so the undisplaced proof does not extend by merely replacing its log-weight evaluator. Exact counterexample: h=2,a=1/4,b=7/40,c=1/100.
w0=(a+c^2)^2/4=6255001/400000000;
w1=b*c^2=7/400000;
w2=b^2/2=49/3200.
Both endpoint weights are more than 800 times the middle weight. Thus there are two separated local maxima. All parameters are positive, and a+b=17/40<1. The one-mode Gaussian kernel itself is physical (nonnegative thermal-squeezed family); no sign cancellation caused this obstruction.

Parity splitting or a larger-dimensional conditional-Poisson construction may still help. Neither is ruled out. A real extension must acquire/evaluate the relative parity masses and H_n in bit-polynomial time, or produce another proved sampler without that evaluation. No such algorithm was established in this session. This is a scoped failure of the naive unimodality transfer, not a lower bound against all displaced binary-herald samplers.
