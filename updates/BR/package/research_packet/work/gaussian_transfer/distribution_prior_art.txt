FINITE PARITY LAW: DISTRIBUTION IDENTIFICATION AND PRIOR-ART CHECK
Date: 2026-10-10. Status: algebra internally derived; cited methods are prior art.

Given h>=0 integer and nonnegative rational a,b, K has weights
  a^(h-k) b^k / [k! 2^(h-k) (((h-k)/2)!)^2],
for k in {0,...,h}, k congruent to h mod 2, when their sum is positive.

IDENTIFICATIONS (direct algebra)
Set M=(h-K)/2 and n=floor(h/2). Then
  w_m=(a^2/4)^m b^(h-2m)/[(h-2m)! (m!)^2], 0<=m<=n.
This is the equal-up/down count law of a symmetric trinomial bridge:
  (U,V,K) ~ Multinomial(h; a/[2(a+b)],a/[2(a+b)],b/[a+b])
conditioned on U=V. Equivalently it is a conditional product-Poisson law
(an A-hypergeometric law). Conditioning naively can be inefficient.

For a,b>0 the successive ratio is
  w_(m+1)/w_m = a^2(h-2m)(h-2m-1)/[4 b^2(m+1)^2].
It decreases strictly wherever defined. Thus M is discrete log-concave,
and a mode can be found by O(log h) exact rational ratio comparisons.
Each such comparison involves only O(L+log h) bits, where L is the input
bit length of a,b; no factorials or probability normalization is needed.

The partition sum is
  Z_h=(b^h/h!) 2F1(-h/2,(1-h)/2;1;a^2/b^2)
     =[z^h] exp(bz) I_0(az).
Also h! Z_h = (1/pi) integral_0^pi (b+a cos(theta))^h dtheta.
For b>a, it equals (b^2-a^2)^(h/2) P_h(b/sqrt(b^2-a^2)); polynomial
continuation gives the other parameters without numerical complex choices.
For h=2n, w_m/w_0=binom(n,m)binom(n-1/2,m)(a/b)^(2m);
for h=2n+1, use binom(n,m)binom(n+1/2,m). This resembles Fisher's
noncentral hypergeometric law but has a half-integer population parameter:
standard integer-urn implementations are not a direct exact sampler.

For t>0, an independent Bessel(0,at) variable M and Poisson(bt) variable K,
conditioned on 2M+K=h, also give exactly this law. The finite law itself is
not an ordinary Bessel distribution; one must retain the factorial (h-2m)!.

STRONGEST DIRECT SAMPLING COMPARATOR
Chewi, Gerber, Lu, Le Gouic, Rigollet (2021), Rejection sampling from
shape-constrained distributions in sublinear time, arXiv:2105.14166v1.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14166
Definition 7 assumes a known endpoint mode; Theorem 17 and Algorithm 3
construct a constant-acceptance rejection envelope using O(log log N)
queries to unnormalized weights. Apply their construction separately on
the two sides of our mode, keeping only one copy of the mode. Combined
with the rational-ratio mode search above, this already gives polylog(h)
setup in an exact-real weight-oracle model and O(1) expected proposals.
The envelope has a constant plateau and geometric tails. It does not need
Z_h, Legendre or hypergeometric evaluation. This is a direct prior-art
overlap with the proposed compressed-count rejection mechanism.
Do not import the source's oracle query bound as a bit-complexity theorem.

OTHER PRIMARY COMPARATORS
Devroye (1987), A Simple Generator for Discrete Log-Concave Distributions.
https://luc.devroye.org/devroye-discretelogconcave-1987.pdf
Uniformly bounded expected arithmetic cost, but its envelope uses the
normalized probability at the mode. Supplying that quantity hides Z_h.

Hörmann (1994), A universal generator for discrete log-concave distributions.
https://research.wu.ac.at/ws/files/18959636/document.pdf
Prior constant-center/geometric-tail universal rejection construction.

Sheldon (2013), Discrete Adaptive Rejection Sampling.
https://people.cs.umass.edu/~sheldon/papers/discrete_ars.pdf
https://people.cs.umass.edu/~sheldon/resources.html
Direct unnormalized log-concave sampling and C implementation; useful
practical baseline. No general uniform runtime theorem in this report.

Devroye (2002), Simulating Bessel random variables.
https://luc.devroye.org/bessel.pdf
P(M=m) proportional z^(2m)/(m!)^2 is Bessel(0,2z). Uniformly bounded
expected arithmetic cost, avoids Bessel functions/ratios; Gamma evaluation
is treated as unit cost. A Bessel sampler alone is not a new mechanism.

Good (1958), Legendre polynomials and trinomial random walks.
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100033168
Establishes the longstanding trinomial-walk/Legendre connection. The
identities above were independently obtained from coefficient expansion.

BIT COMPLEXITY: WHAT IS NEEDED AND WHAT IS NOT
1. Exact rational weights generally have Theta(h log h + hL) output bits.
   Computing or returning them exactly is exponential in log h. The same
   obstruction applies to naive exact rational mode-normalized weights.
   A unit-cost exact weight oracle is therefore not the input bit model.
2. Log-factorial evaluation does NOT require h-bit precision. Every
   log-factorial argument is at most h+1. To obtain absolute error 2^-t
   in a log weight (or log ratio to the mode), its elementary terms have
   magnitude O(h(L+log h)); a sufficient significand precision is
     p = t + O(log h + log(L+log h)).
   Thus cancellation costs about log h guard bits, not h bits. L still
   appears in the input reading and rational arithmetic costs.
3. Stirling with a rigorous remainder, shifting only small arguments up
   to O(t+log h+log L), gives log Gamma at positive integers to that
   absolute precision in polynomial time in L+log h+t. Full factorials
   are unnecessary. Primary numerical reference:
   Johansson, Arbitrary-precision computation of the gamma function:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08392
   This reference supports certified log-Gamma machinery; the precise
   specialization/complexity bound above is an internal derivation.
4. An epsilon-TV result needs a robust envelope setup under interval
   comparisons, exact or controlled-error uniform/geometric proposals,
   and a bound on all accumulated acceptance/proposal/truncation errors.
   Near a half-height comparison, an algorithm must allow a constant
   uncertainty band rather than demand an arbitrarily precise equality
   decision. Fixed double lgamma subtraction at enormous h is not proof.
5. An exact sampler needs lazy random-bit comparisons and certified
   probability enclosures, plus expected-cost analysis of refinements.
   Finite expected random bits alone does not pay for those enclosures.
   Relevant distinction: Devroye-Gravel, arXiv:1502.02539 and 1511.02273.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02539
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02273

DEGENERACIES
a=0,b>0 gives K=h. b=0,a>0 and even h gives K=0. b=0 and odd h has
zero normalizer, so the conditional distribution is undefined (a limiting
K=1 law does not repair the zero-probability conditioning event). h=0 is
the point mass at 0 under the usual zero-exponent convention.

ASSESSMENT
The distribution identities and polylog(h) oracle sampling are familiar
tools or direct specializations of existing methods. A complete certified
bit implementation may be useful technical work; this search does not
establish its novelty, and it is not by itself a new feasible capability.
