INVERSE-SCIENCE SCOUT — PRIOR-MISMATCH-ROBUST ADAPTIVE SENSING
Date: 2026-10-10 (Australia/Brisbane)
Disposition: REJECT AS A BREAKTHROUGH CANDIDATE IN ITS PRESENT FORM.
Internal proof reconstruction and finite counterexample; no novel result claimed.

1. CAPABILITY BARRIER AND CANDIDATE

Task: reliably acquire/reconstruct an anomalous scientific specimen at much lower
scan time or dose than full measurement, including structures absent from training.
The difficult step is acquisition of evidence for the omitted structure. A learned
posterior can have negligible or zero variance in the omitted direction, so an
acquisition rule based only on that posterior has no reason to observe it.

Candidate mechanism: devote additional measurements to prior-independent audits
of the current frozen reconstruction; enlarge the prior only when audits fail.
This is useful, but the core certificate is already compressed-sensing cross
validation (Ward 2009). Under physically restricted coordinate/Fourier-line
queries, absent structural assumptions the acquisition burden can approach a full
scan. Dense random probes do not automatically correspond to one feasible scanner
measurement, and energy normalization changes the noisy cost.

3. EXACT POSTERIOR-BLIND COUNTEREXAMPLE

Let x in R^n and permit noiseless coordinate queries. The supplied prior is
X_i iid N(0,1) for i<n and X_n=0 surely. Actual x=A e_n, with A>0.
A maximum posterior-variance rule queries the first n-1 coordinates in any order;
each observed value is zero. Its posterior then collapses exactly to xhat=0.
All acquired measurements fit exactly; posterior variance is exactly zero;
actual Euclidean error is A. The construction holds even with exact posterior
sampling and unlimited inference computation. This isolates model mismatch and
information acquisition, rather than a diffusion sampler implementation defect.
It is a linear-coordinate model, not an empirical claim about every CT dataset.

4. SHARP NOISELESS ADAPTIVE COORDINATE AUDIT LOWER BOUND

Theorem. Let any randomized adaptive algorithm query at most q distinct
coordinates of an unknown vector. It tests H0:x=0 versus H_j:x=A e_j,
j=1,...,n. If false-positive probability under H0 is at most alpha, then its
average detection probability across the n alternatives is at most

    alpha + (1-alpha) q/n.

Consequently, if every alternative must be detected with probability >=1-beta,
then q >= n[1-beta/(1-alpha)], for alpha<1. In particular alpha=0 requires
q >= (1-beta)n. The bound is sharp for q<=n.

Proof. Freeze all random bits, including test randomization. Run the algorithm on
the all-zero transcript, giving query set Q (|Q|<=q) and decision D0 in {0,1}.
For each j outside Q, the transcript under A e_j remains identical at every
adaptive step, hence D_j=D0. Thus pointwise in the random bits,

    sum_j D_j <= n D0 + q(1-D0).

Take expectation and divide by n. Since E D0<=alpha and q<=n, the asserted
bound follows. A uniformly random q-subset followed by rejection whenever a
nonzero is seen, and independent rejection with probability alpha otherwise,
has power q/n+(1-q/n)alpha for every j and FPR alpha. This attains the bound.

Applicability: Fourier coefficient queries after a fixed unitary transform obey
the identical theorem. If an acquisition reveals one disjoint block/line, replace
coordinates by disjoint blocks and choose one unobserved block direction. A real
image imposes conjugacy, so pair conjugate blocks or work in a real orthogonal
basis. Single-coil ideal Cartesian sampling fits; multi-coil sensitivity mixing,
non-Cartesian encoding, positivity, or restricted anomaly classes require their
own argument. This theorem must not be advertised as a universal MRI/CT bound.

5. GENERAL NOISY SENSING ENERGY LOWER BOUND

Theorem. At round t choose any a_t adaptively and observe
Y_t=<a_t,x>+epsilon_t, epsilon_t iid N(0,sigma^2), sigma>0. Include the
algorithm's internal randomness in the transcript. Let total expected sensing
energy under H0 be E0 sum_t ||a_t||^2 <= B. To distinguish x=0 from EVERY
x=A e_j with both type-I and type-II errors <=delta<1/2 necessarily

    B >= (2 n sigma^2/A^2) kl(1-delta,delta),
    kl(1-delta,delta)=(1-2delta) log((1-delta)/delta).

For m rounds with ||a_t||<=1 almost surely, B<=m gives the same lower bound on m.
For random stopping, the formula needs the usual finite-energy/stopping
justification; the claimed proof here is for a fixed finite horizon.

Proof. For each j, chain rule for Gaussian likelihoods, under the null adaptive
history, gives
KL(P0||Pj)=A^2/(2sigma^2) E0 sum_t a_tj^2.
Summing over j gives total <=A^2 B/(2sigma^2), so one j has KL at most this
quantity divided by n. Data processing to the accept-null decision, monotonicity
of binary KL for probabilities >=1-delta and <=delta, gives
KL(P0||Pj)>=kl(1-delta,delta). Rearrangement proves the claim.

Scope: this is already a one-sparse-anomaly obstruction. Arbitrary anomalies
cannot be easier uniformly. It allows arbitrary adaptive dense sensing, not just
coordinate sampling. It does not forbid improvement under a genuine signal
class, a stronger amplitude, or additional physical resources.

For positive one-sparse anomalies the order is attainable: repeatedly measure
a=(1,...,1)/sqrt(n). The null mean is zero and every alternative mean A/sqrt(n).
Threshold the average at A/(2sqrt(n)); Gaussian tails give error <=
exp[-m A^2/(8 n sigma^2)]. Thus m >=8 n sigma^2 A^-2 log(1/delta)
suffices. This upper bound is for the stated positive-one-spike class and does not
cover arbitrary signed multi-coordinate residuals (which can cancel).

6. WHAT THE CLASSICAL POSITIVE AUDIT GUARANTEE ACTUALLY BUYS

Freeze reconstruction xhat using all pre-audit data. Let r=x-xhat be fixed
conditional on those data. Take fresh g_i iid N(0,I/n) and independent noise
N(0,sigma^2). The audit residuals Z_i=<g_i,r>+noise are iid normal with variance
v=||r||^2/n+sigma^2, marginalizing the fresh design randomness. Thus
sum_i Z_i^2/v is chi-square with m degrees of freedom. An exact lower quantile
c_delta gives the (1-delta) upper confidence bound

    ||r||^2 <= max{0, n[sum_i Z_i^2/c_delta - sigma^2]}.

This is a standard chi-square pivot / cross-validation mechanism, NOT novel.
Its independence premise matters: if the reconstruction may adapt arbitrarily
to the same exposed audit matrix with m<n, choose a nonzero residual in ker(G)
and the noiseless audit reports zero. Use fresh audits or prove a suitable
uniform guarantee for a fixed admitted residual family.

Costs: m genuinely new physical measurements; fresh design generation and dense
application nominally O(mn); no new training required for the audit itself.
E||g_i||^2=1, so expected sensing energy is m. Using N(0,I) instead would spend
n times this energy per row, which must not be hidden. Scanner hardware must
implement these dense probes; synthesizing them numerically from a full scan
already pays the full acquisition cost. Noise variance must be supplied or
estimated with its own measurement cost and confidence accounting. The Gaussian
row design does not impose an almost-sure unit-row norm; it has unit EXPECTED
energy. No energy or dose equivalence to a real scanner is claimed here.

8. DECISIVE NEXT GATE / HANDOFF

Do not fund further work merely to add a posterior-independent random audit to a
learned reconstruction: prior art already has the mechanism. A consequential
advance would need (i) a physically implementable mixing/encoding protocol that
changes the feasible acquisition family under a matched energy/dose budget, or
(ii) a scientifically justified anomaly class with provably acquired covering /
incoherence information, enabling a strictly better detection-reconstruction
tradeoff than existing sensing theory. Supplying the true tangent, residual
incoherence, or anomaly support as an oracle hides the original acquisition
problem. Learning those conditions from the same blind measurements cannot
remove the indistinguishability counterexample.

Transferable principle for other branches: distinguish recovery using a prior
from certifying a failure of that prior. The latter needs information channels
which observe the excluded alternatives. Computational sophistication of the
posterior does not supply that evidence.
