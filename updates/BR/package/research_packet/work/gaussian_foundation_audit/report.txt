1. CONDITIONAL GAUSSIAN IDENTITY
Let the Bargmann series be R(u)=c exp(u^T K u/2+ell^T u), K symmetric.
The diagonal physical Fock coefficient for n is
  (partial_(ket)^n partial_(bra)^n R)(0) / product_i n_i!.
There is one factorial per physical mode, not two. For unobserved modes,
tracing with physical mark z_i is the formal contraction operator
  exp[(1/2) partial_U^T C_z partial_U],   C_z=D_z X_U=X_U D_z.
The equality requires the same z_i on ket and bra copies. It follows directly
from the coefficient of (partial_ket_i partial_bra_i)^n/n!.

For any symmetric formal covariance C, the Gaussian contraction identity is
  exp[(1/2)partial^T C partial] exp(x^T A x/2+b^T x)|_(x=0)
  = det(I-C A)^(-1/2) exp[b^T (I-C A)^(-1) C b/2].
This does not assume that C is a positive real covariance. An independent proof
uses the formal heat equation in time t with quadratic-exponential ansatz:
A_t'=A_t C A_t, b_t'=A_t C b_t,
c_t'=(tr(C A_t)+b_t^T C b_t)/2. Their rational solutions give the identity.
Equivalently, formal Wick contractions decompose into cycles and open paths.
The nonnegative expansion and rho(X_U K_UU)<1 ensure absolute convergence at
0<=z_i<=1. The positive determinant root is selected continuously from z=0.

Take b=ell_U+K_UH u_H and A=K_UU. Then
  J(z)=(I-D_z X_U K_UU)^(-1)D_z X_U
      =D_z(I-X_U K_UU D_z)^(-1)X_U.
Expanding b^T J b/2 gives exactly S,t,v in source equations (3.1)-(3.3).
Applying observed derivatives gives the labeled pair/singleton polynomial
L_(h,h)(S,t), divided by product h_i!. Dividing by its z=1 expression cancels
that factorial and c and proves source equation (3.4). No inverse herald
probability enters the computation. Nonzero conditioning is equivalent to
L_(h,h)(S(1),t(1))>0 because all other factors are positive.

2. ORIENTATION, HALF FACTORS, AND PHYSICAL COUNT IDENTIFICATION
For positive physical z,
  J(z)^(-1)=X_U D_z^(-1)-K_UU,
which is symmetric because D_z commutes with X_U. Thus J,S and the augmented
boundary block [[S,t],[t^T,v]] are symmetric as functions of the physical
markings. A matched pair can be assigned either fixed directed orientation:
its normalized endpoint path PGF is S_ab(z)/S_ab(1), equal to the reverse
orientation PGF. At zero markings this extends by continuity.

This statement is false for independent ket/bra marks. For example, K_UU=0
and D=diag(x,y) give J=D X=[[0,x],[y,0]], which is nonsymmetric unless x=y.
The sampler is therefore proved for physical occupations, not a particular
separately marked auxiliary orientation law. That restriction is sufficient
for the claimed output and is explicitly used, not omitted.

The determinant exponent is alpha=1/2. Independent Poisson(v(1)/2) source
paths give exp[(v(z)-v(1))/2]. Two controls pin both halves:
(a) one coherent mode K=0, ell=(g,g): v(z)/2=g^2 z, hence Poisson(g^2);
(b) one squeezed mode B=b: M=[[0,b],[b,0]], det(I-zM)=1-b^2 z^2, hence
    sqrt((1-b^2)/(1-b^2 z^2)), with exactly even physical photon counts.
The occupation of each physical mode is the SUM of its two auxiliary vertex
occupations, not their average. These controls would detect the common factor
of two error.

3. REVERSE SCHUR DISTRIBUTION
See reverse_schur.txt for a separate investigator's complete all-edge PGF
proof. The decisive one-vertex identity is restated here.
Set a=W_vv, p=1-a, V_ij=W_iv W_vj/p, W'_ij=W_ij+V_ij.
Given reduced edge multiplicities c_ij, the prescribed binomial routes and
negative binomial loops yield the all-edge marked conditional PGF
  [p/(1-a t_vv)]^alpha * product_(ij)
  [(W_ij t_ij + W_iv W_vj t_iv t_vj/(1-a t_vv))/W'_ij]^(c_ij).
This follows by first conditioning on routes R_ij; NB(alpha+sum R,p)
contributes one p/(1-a t_vv) per routed edge plus an independent alpha factor.
The expression is exactly the marked Schur substitution. The determinant
identity det(I-W_UU)=p det(I-W'_U'U') telescopes its alpha factors. Induction
therefore proves the full joint edge-count/occupation law, not just moments.

Necessary conventions: alpha>0; boundary vertices are endpoints and never
internally traversed; positive sampled boundary multiplicities require
positive corresponding final Schur weights. Starting from a fixed compatible
boundary multiset yields independent normalized endpoint bridges plus an
independent alpha loop soup. Averaging over the matching and Poisson laws
then gives Section 1's conditional PGF. Arbitrary nonnegative nonreversible
internal matrices are allowed; row-substochasticity is unnecessary.
Pivots remain positive because I-W_UU is a nonsingular M-matrix and its
principal Schur complements are nonsingular M-matrices.

4. PHYSICALITY IS A REAL SEPARATE PREMISE
An explicit counterexample to any stronger physicality inference is
m=2, ell=0, A=0, C=[[0,1/2],[1/2,0]], K=[[A,C],[C,A]].
It is symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, exchange symmetric, and rho(XK)=1/2.
Its one-photon density block is a positive scalar times C, with eigenvalues
+1/2 and -1/2. It is not a positive density operator. Its diagonal count law
is nevertheless normalized and nonnegative: n_1=n_2 geometrically with
mass (1-c^2)c^(2n), c=1/2. The theorem can sample that formal diagonal law,
but cannot label it a physical state's photon law absent the promise.
This is a boundary counterexample, not a defect in the frozen theorem,
which explicitly retains the physicality premise.

5. FROZEN SINGLE-HERALD MATCHING LAW
Global ell=0 implies effective singleton t=0. Exchange symmetry is preserved
under eliminating an exchange-invariant set, so S=[[a,b],[b,a]]. If k is the
cross-pair count, q=(h-k)/2 is the number of same-type pairs in EACH type.
The number of labeled matchings is
  binom(h,k)^2 k! [(h-k)!/(2^q q!)]^2,
giving w(k)=h!^2 a^(h-k)b^k/[k! 2^(h-k) q!^2]. This proves the frozen formula.
Support k=h mod2,h mod2+2,...,h. For a,b>0,
  w(k+2)/w(k)=(b/a)^2 (h-k)^2/[(k+1)(k+2)],
which decreases along support. A mode can be located by O(log(h+1)) exact
rational comparisons; ties are harmless. All comparisons use integers of
polynomial length. The zero-weight degeneracies in the frozen statement are
complete. An aggregate seed C_11=C_22=q,C_12=k contains h edges and has
exactly the endpoint law required in Section 3; no labeled matching or h
individual edge insertions are needed.

Caution for later extensions: ell_H=0 alone does NOT imply t=0 when ell_U
is nonzero and K_HU J ell_U is positive. The frozen theorem correctly assumes
global ell=0. A sufficient more general condition is effective t(1)=0.

6. ENCODED RATIONAL PIVOT AND COUNT-LENGTH BOUNDS
Here is an explicit route that avoids concealing a spectral-gap oracle.
Let d=2(m-1), let L include ALL rational numerator/denominator encodings, and
let Q be the product of positive input denominators. log2 Q<=L. Multiplying
I-M by Q gives an integer matrix with entries of O(L) bits. Any minor of
order at most d has O(d(L+log(d+1))) bits by determinant expansion/Hadamard.
Every exact Schur entry is a ratio of such minors (with at most polynomial
extra rational factors for boundary entries). In particular there is an
explicit B=poly(L,m) bounding every pivot numerator/denominator length, and
  0<p_v<=1,  p_v>=2^(-B).
Routing probabilities and a,b also have polynomial rational length. Thus no
separate numerical inverse spectral-separation promise is required for the
encoded instance theorem. Exact rational acquisition remains an input promise.

Let E_j denote the sum of ALL current aggregate edge counts just before
reverse stage j. Initially E_0=h. Binomial routing gives K_j<=E_j. The
negative binomial's mean is
  mu_j=(K_j+1/2)(1-p_j)/p_j <= (E_j+1)2^B.
For scalar error budget eta<=1, use a cap
  T_j=ceil(4 mu_j/eta)
(or the larger explicit bound ceil(4(E_j+1)2^B/eta)). Markov bounds the omitted
tail by eta/4. A truncated draw, and any chosen zero fallback, obey L_j<=T_j.
Replacing each routed edge by two gives
  E_(j+1)=E_j+K_j+L_j <=2E_j+T_j.
Consequently a safe deterministic support bound is
  E_(j+1)+1 <= (8*2^B/eta)(E_j+1),
  log2(E_d+1) <= log2(h+1)+d[B+log2(1/eta)+3].
The same bound covers physical outputs because their counts are at most
E_d. Eta of order epsilon/O(m^3) gives polynomial binary output length in
L,m,log(h+1),log(1/epsilon). This argument bounds every truncated output,
including adaptive later shapes; it is stronger than an expected count bound.
An ideal untruncated output need not have a worst-case bit bound; the theorem
is an epsilon-TV sampler and MUST use charged tails/caps for worst-case cost.

7. SCALAR PRECISION AND TIME
The compact herald law has finite support of size at most h+1. The source's
dyadic envelope has O(log(h+1)) blocks and mass at most three times the
unscaled mass. Choose dyadic precision b so 2(h+1)2^(-b)<=eta/16. Then b is
O(log(h+1)+log(1/eta)). Bound g(k)=w(k)/w(mode) in an integer grid of width at
most two, clamp lower heights at zero, and sample from lower heights through
upper-envelope rejection. Summed mass loss is <=eta/16 and success is >1/5.
O(log(1/eta)) attempts leave <=eta/4 failure probability after adjustment of
the constant. The method needs no partition function and forms no h!.

Log mass ratios use a constant number of rational multiples of logarithms
and logGamma of positive integer/half-integer arguments. Each argument has
polynomial bit length by Section 6. Although logGamma(n) can be numerically
of order n log n, its binary magnitude is O(log n+log log(n+1)); requesting
small ABSOLUTE error therefore needs only that many extra precision bits.
Likewise multiplying log(a/b) by h needs O(log(h+1)) extra guard bits, not h.
A constructive polynomial enclosure route shifts small positive arguments
by O(P) to >=cP, then uses O(P) Stirling terms with a rigorous next-term bound;
range-reduced logarithms and rational arithmetic give P requested bits in
polynomial work. Bernoulli coefficient generation has polynomial bit cost.
Here P itself is polynomial in the encoded parameters. Extremely negative
log ratios should be clamped below the dyadic grid using a certified upper
bound, rather than demanding a floating representation of an irrelevant
tiny exponential. Exact zero cases must be handled before taking logarithms.

Binomial and half-integer negative binomial ratios admit the same route.
Binomial support is bounded by current E; NB uses Section 6's T. The rational
p,1-p and positive a,b may be very small, but their nonzero bit lengths are
polynomial, so they do not introduce an uncharged conditioning parameter.

Integer-ticket rejection has acceptance >=1/2. Cap each ticket's bit-rejection
loop at O(log(N_calls/epsilon)) attempts, charging its fallback separately.
The scalar envelope proposal caps make N_calls itself polynomial and known
in advance. Adaptive maximal coupling/union bounds apply if each reached
conditional scalar law is within its allocated error uniformly over the
bounded support. Reserving portions of epsilon for matching, NB tails,
dyadic rounding, proposal caps, and ticket caps completes the argument.
A claim of bounded worst-case polynomial cost without these caps would not
follow from the source's merely expected-time integer generation.
