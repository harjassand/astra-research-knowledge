PRIMARY-LITERATURE AUDIT: NORMALIZED-BOX ROBUST IMPORTANCE SAMPLING
Research date: 2026-10-10, Australia/Brisbane
Status: no novelty established; core fixed-q approximation mechanism covered by prior art.
Scope: literature comparison and mathematical implications only. No implementation, remote writes, or external expert verification.

PROBLEM AND ASSUMPTIONS

Given rational P_i>0, B_i>=0, q_i>0 with sum q_i=1, and 0<=l_i<=u_i with sum P_i l_i>0, evaluate
 R(q)=max_{z in [l,u]} [sum_i B_i(P_i z_i)^2/q_i]/[sum_i P_i z_i]^2,
then minimize R(q) over proposals q. Remove coordinates identically zero; the completely zero numerator is trivial. These assumptions matter for positive denominators and polynomial-bit arithmetic.

MAIN PRIOR-ART COMPARISON

1. Direct fixed-q FPTAS precedent.
Shashi Mittal and Andreas S. Schulz (2013), A General Framework for Designing Approximation Schemes for Combinatorial Optimization Problems with Many Objectives Combined into One, Operations Research 61(2):386-397, DOI 10.1287/opre.1120.1093.
Primary author-hosted paper, downloaded and text inspected:
https://web.mit.edu/schulz/www/epapers/ms-or-2013.pdf
Publisher record:
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.1120.1093?journalCode=opre

Section 7, Theorem 7.5 supplies an FPTAS for a ratio of positive affine objectives on a binary feasible set when exact weighted feasibility has a pseudo-polynomial algorithm. Page 395 explicitly extends the framework to f1*f2/(f3*f4). At our box corners, N(x)=N0+sum n_i x_i and D(x)=D0+sum d_i x_i, where n_i=B_i P_i^2(u_i^2-l_i^2)/q_i and d_i=P_i(u_i-l_i), are nonnegative affine objectives. Minimizing D^2/N is directly the stated extension with f1=f2=D, f3=N, f4=1. Binary exact weighted feasibility is SUBSET SUM and has the required pseudo-polynomial DP. Rational data can be integer-scaled with polynomial encoding length. A mixed approximate Pareto set gives D' <=(1+delta)D and N'>=N/(1+delta), hence at most a cubic relative loss. Therefore existence of a fixed-q FPTAS is a routine corollary, not an original mechanism. Theorem 3.3 alone is not the correct citation because the objectives have opposite monotonicity; Section 7 is decisive.

2. Generic minimax center is known.
Baris Nakiboglu, The Renyi Capacity and Center, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 65(2):841-860 (2019), preprint first submitted 2016.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.02424
https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02424

Define p_i(z)=P_i z_i/sum_j P_j z_j. For B_i=1, our value is exp(inf_q sup_p D_2(p||q)), exactly the order-2 Renyi radius of the normalized-box family. Theorem 1 and Definitions 4-7 give capacity/radius duality and the center structure; the order-2 mean has the square-root mixture form. Consequently the minimax formulation, generic dual interpretation, and square-root optimal proposal must not be claimed as new. The normalized-box representation and its implicit computational oracle remain additional structure. Nonconstant B is a weighted generalization and is not literally the same divergence between probability measures.

3. Closest interval uncertainty comparator has a different denominator.
Sebastian U. Stich, Anant Raj, Martin Jaggi (2017), Safe Adaptive Importance Sampling, NIPS 2017.
https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/7025-safe-adaptive-importance-sampling.pdf

Equation (7) treats min_q max_{c in [l,u]} (sum_i L_i c_i^2/q_i)/(sum_i c_i^2), with an O(n log n) exact algorithm. The denominator is squared Euclidean norm, whereas our denominator is squared one-norm for nonnegative c. This difference changes computational structure. It is a strong baseline for uncertainty-aware sampling but does not imply an exact fast oracle here.

4. Other directly relevant robust-sampling precedents.
Tyler B. Johnson and Carlos Guestrin (2018), Training Deep Models Faster with Robust, Approximate Importance Sampling, NeurIPS 2018.
https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/967990de5b3eac7b87d49a13c6834978-Paper.pdf
Section 4 minimizes max_{v in U} sum_i v_i^2/q_i using a learned positive-orthant ellipsoidal uncertainty set. This is relevant to information acquisition and stale importance estimates, but differs from normalized box targets.

Baik, Ko and Byon (2023), Robust Importance Sampling for Stochastic Simulations with Uncertain Parametric Input Model, Winter Simulation Conference.
https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc23papers/033.pdf
Equation (5) minimizes worst-case importance-sampling variance under uncertain input distributions. Its uncertainty model is parametric and its objective is absolute variance; its numerical optimization is not an exact global normalized-box solver.

5. Outer convex optimization is also a standard tool.
Shaddin Dughmi and Haifeng Xu, Algorithmic Bayesian Persuasion, April 2, 2015 version.
https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/shaddin.pdf
Section 2.3, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.3 give weak convex optimization using a separation oracle with one-sided error: feasible points are accepted; any returned cut is valid. Accepted points may violate the original constraints. The theorem includes affine-hull and positive-volume/outer-ellipsoid hypotheses. The authors themselves identify this approach as previously known. Our approximate worst-case witness creates just such an oracle for the epigraph, so citing 'approximate separation' alone does not create a new outer minimax method. A complete claimed algorithm must still verify quantitative domain, bit-complexity, and accuracy assumptions.

6. DP trimming lineage.
Gerhard J. Woeginger (2000), When Does a Dynamic Programming Formulation Guarantee the Existence of a Fully Polynomial Time Approximation Scheme (FPTAS)?, INFORMS Journal on Computing 12(1):57-74.
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/ijoc.12.1.57.11901
The paper provides generic structural and arithmetic conditions converting suitable dynamic programs into FPTASes. It reinforces that state-space trimming itself is standard. The Mittal-Schulz application above is the more direct novelty comparison.

INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL CHECKS (INTERNALLY DERIVED, NOT LITERATURE NOVELTY CLAIMS)

A. Why corners suffice.
Freeze every coordinate except z_i=x. The objective becomes (a x^2+b)/(c x+d)^2 with a,b,c,d>=0 and positive denominator. Its derivative is 2(ad x-bc)/(cx+d)^3. The numerator is nondecreasing, so an interior stationary point is a minimum (or the function is constant). Replacing x by one of its endpoints cannot decrease the value. Repeating proves a maximizing corner exists. This does not make corner enumeration polynomial.

B. Clean weak NP-hardness reduction for exact evaluation.
Take B_i=1, q_i=P_i, sum P_i=1 and every z_i in [1,3]. Given a PARTITION instance a_1,...,a_n of positive integers with A=sum a_i, define P_i=a_i/(2A) and append P_{n+1}=1/2. Let m=sum P_i z_i. Since z_i^2 <=4z_i-3,
 R_at_z <=(4m-3)/m^2 <=4/3.
The first equality requires all z_i in {1,3}, and the second requires m=3/2. Equivalently, the coordinates at the upper endpoint have total P-mass 1/4. The appended coordinate cannot be among them, so equality holds exactly when some original a_i sum to A/2. Thus deciding whether the maximum reaches 4/3 is NP-hard even for this restricted family. This proves a weak NP-hardness obstruction; it does not prove strong NP-hardness or hardness of approximating a fixed positive relative accuracy. The underlying extremal moment bound is classical, not an invention.

C. Elementary one-dimensional trimmed DP specialization.
Start from the full lower-endpoint totals (D0,N0), with D0>0. At coordinate i, extend each retained (D,N) state either by (0,0) or by (d_i,n_i). Partition positive D values into geometric bins of ratio 1+delta, and retain a state with largest N per bin. A retained state corresponding to any original partial state has N no smaller and D at most (1+delta)^k times larger after k stages. Consequently maximizing N/D^2 over final states loses at most (1+delta)^(2d). Taking delta proportional to epsilon/d gives an FPTAS with polynomial state count governed by (d/epsilon) log(Dmax/Dmin). Include masks/predecessors if a witness is required. This is a simple application of established trimming, with particularly favorable one-exact objective behavior; it must not be branded a fundamental new algorithm.

D. A routine route to polynomial outer approximation.
Write a_z,i=B_i p_i(z)^2 and C=max_{i,z} a_z,i. The coordinate maximum p_i is P_i u_i/(P_i u_i+sum_{j!=i}P_j l_j), so C is directly computable. For C>0, R*>=C, and uniform q has R<=d^2 C. Mixing any q with eta times uniform ensures q_i>=eta/d and multiplies R by at most 1/(1-eta). On this truncated simplex, each scenario gradient coordinate has magnitude <=C d^2/eta^2. Scaling by C therefore produces polynomial gradient bounds. An approximate maximizing scenario yields an approximate subgradient of the convex maximum. Standard projected subgradient methods can then give polynomial additive accuracy, which converts to relative accuracy using R*>=C. This is an argument outline, not a checked implementation or optimized complexity theorem. It strongly limits novelty of merely composing the fixed-q FPTAS with outer minimax optimization.

WHAT SURVIVES THIS AUDIT

- No direct publication of this exact normalized-box specialization was located in the bounded search. That is not evidence of novelty.
- A carefully stated box-specific hardness/FPTAS corollary might be a useful technical observation or engineering contribution, but the underlying mechanism is standard and cannot support a foundational-breakthrough claim.
- A genuine advance would require a special structural family allowing a markedly stronger complexity guarantee, tighter acquisition bounds under a realistic information interface, or measured end-to-end benefits over robust/approximate importance-sampling baselines.
- Acquisition of valid P, l, u and B remains unaddressed by optimizing over supplied numbers. If obtaining tight intervals hides the expensive computation, this must be included in the baseline cost.
- Preserve NP-hardness and failed exact-transfer attempts as negative evidence. Do not conflate an FPTAS for relative second moment R with a relative FPTAS for variance R-1 near zero.
