EXACT BOX CERTIFICATION REMAINS HARD WITH ARBITRARILY TIGHT POSITIVE INTERVALS
Internal derivation, 10 October 2026. Historical priority UNKNOWN.
This is a local certificate-computation result, not a solution to rare-event
certificate acquisition or a historic applied breakthrough.

1. Exact problem and source interface

At a node of Astra N486/N487, fix positive rational P_i,q_i with sums one,
continuation second-moment multipliers B_i>=1, and rational intervals
0<=l_i<=u_i<=1. The local certificate must upper-bound

    R* = max_z [sum_i B_i P_i^2 z_i^2/q_i] / [sum_i P_i z_i]^2,

over the box and positive denominator. The source proves that a corner
attains the maximum. This note proves hardness even in the special case
q=P, B_i=1, and all intervals equal the same fixed strictly positive box.

2. Scalar inequality, with exact equality conditions

For 0<a<1 and z_i in [a,1], let mu=sum_i P_i z_i. Since
(z_i-a)(1-z_i)>=0,

    sum_i P_i z_i^2 <= (1+a)mu-a.

Therefore R(z)<=((1+a)mu-a)/mu^2. For mu in [a,1], the right side has
its unique maximum at mu=2a/(1+a), with maximum

    C(a)=(1+a)^2/(4a).

Because every P_i>0, equality in the first inequality forces every z_i
to equal a or 1. If r is the total P mass set to 1, equality holds precisely
when r=a/(1+a). This is the familiar scalar reverse-Cauchy-Schwarz /
Kantorovich-type mechanism. The inequality itself is not claimed new.

3. Positive-box NP-hardness theorem

Fix ANY integer t>=2 and a=t/(t+1). Then exact computation of R* is
NP-hard, even for q=P, B_i=1 and every [l_i,u_i]=[a,1]. The interval
width 1/(t+1) can consequently be an arbitrarily small fixed constant.

Proof. Start with PARTITION on positive integers b_1,...,b_n of total 2B.
Restriction to even total preserves NP-hardness (multiply any input by 2).
Choose integer K>B with t not dividing B+K; one can select either
K=B+1 or K=B+2, as two consecutive numbers cannot both be divisible by
t>=2. Append two copies of K and set B'=B+K. The new list totals 2B'.
Any subset summing to B' uses exactly one K: no K leaves total available
2B<B+K, while two K exceed B+K. Thus the augmented instance is equivalent
to the original partition instance.

Multiply each weight in the augmented list by t, and append one additional
weight B'. Normalize these positive integer weights to probabilities by
their total D=(2t+1)B'. Take q=P and all B_i=1.

By Section 2, R*=C(a) iff some endpoint selection has upper-endpoint mass
r=t/(2t+1), equivalently its selected integer weights sum to tB'. The
extra weight B' cannot be selected: every other weight and the target are
divisible by t, but B' is not. The remaining endpoint selection is exactly
a subset of the augmented original list summing to B', hence solves the
original PARTITION instance. All constructed rationals have polynomial
binary encoding length. This proves the reduction.

For fixed t, threshold-attainment decision R*>=C(a) is in NP: a corner
is a polynomial-length rational witness. It is therefore NP-complete.

4. Verification rather than equality testing

For completeness, corner attainment in this special case follows without
trusting the archived source. Fix all but z_i=x and write
f(x)=(A+p*x^2)/(b+p*x)^2, with p=P_i>0,
A=sum_{j!=i} P_j z_j^2 and b=sum_{j!=i}P_j z_j>0. Then
f'(x)=2p(b*x-A)/(b+p*x)^3. This derivative changes sign only from
negative to positive, so an endpoint is at least as good as any interior
choice. Replace coordinates successively. For a one-coordinate problem
the ratio is constant; the reduction has at least two coordinates.

At a corner with upper mass r, direct substitution gives

    R(r)=[t^2+(2t+1)r]/(t+r)^2,
    C(a)-R(r)=[(2t+1)r-t]^2/[4t(t+1)(t+r)^2].

Here r=y/[(2t+1)B'] for integer y. In a no instance y!=tB', so the
gap is at least g=1/[4t(t+1)^3 B'^2], since 0<=r<=1. Corner attainment
ensures the same upper bound R*<=C(a)-g for the entire continuous box.
Set rational theta=C(a)-g/2. Then all-box verification R(z)<=theta
holds exactly for no-PARTITION instances; a yes instance violates it.
The threshold has polynomial encoding length. All-box verification is
coNP-hard and belongs to coNP, because a violating rational corner is
a certificate. Hence this restricted verification problem is coNP-complete.

Scope: This is weak NP-hardness. The gap can be exponentially small in
input bitlength; the result does not exclude an FPTAS or a practical
polynomial approximate upper certificate. It does not prove strong
NP-hardness, exponential lower bounds, or hardness under P=NP.

5. Simple constant-width special case

For a=1/3, one can omit the modular construction. Given partition weights
of total 2B, append weight 2B, normalize by 4B, and take P=q.
At a corner R=(1+8r)/(1+2r)^2<=4/3, equality iff r=1/4. The extra
weight has mass 1/2 and cannot participate in equality, so the equality
condition is the original partition. The no-instance gap is >=1/(27B^2).

6. Relationship to approximate certificates

This obstruction is compatible with standard multiplicative Pareto
trimming. Write endpoint contributions x_i=P_i z_i and
v_i=B_i x_i^2/q_i. Enumerate partial pairs (S,V), S=sum x_i,V=sum v_i,
while retaining maximal V in each fixed geometric S bin of ratio rho>1.
After d stages, each true corner has a retained representative with
S'<=rho^d S and V'>=V. The best retained ratio L is attainable, and

    L <= R* <= rho^(2d)L.

With epsilon in (0,1] and rho=1+epsilon/(4d), rho^(2d)<=1+epsilon.
For example, (1+x)^m<=1/(1-mx) for mx<1 follows by comparing binomial
coefficients with m^k; put m=2d,x=epsilon/(4d), and use
1/(1-epsilon/2)<=1+epsilon for epsilon<=1.

If h is the smallest positive endpoint x_i and M=sum_i max x_i, the
number of bins is O((d/epsilon) log(M/h)); zero has its own bin.
All retained pairs are actual choices. With rational inputs and exact
geometric thresholds, the bitlength of rho^j is linear in j times the
bitlength of rho; endpoint products, pair sums, and witness bitmasks have
polynomial length. Thus the arithmetic-operation scheme can be implemented
in polynomial Turing time in input length and 1/epsilon. This is a standard
approximate-Pareto / knapsack-DP mechanism; historical priority for this
specialization is unestablished. Implementation and proof audit are owned
by the separate variance_algorithm agent, not duplicated here.

Important: obtaining the verified intervals [l_i,u_i] remains a separate,
potentially intractable high-dimensional acquisition problem. Approximate
verification addresses outdegree of a supplied local certificate, not the
state-space, continuation-learning, or hidden-mode discovery bottleneck.

7. Primary literature for baseline and novelty interpretation

trade-offs and optimal access of Web sources, establishes the broad
approximate-Pareto context. Primary full paper:
https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/yexiang/courses/18fall-cs590/papers/papadimitriou2000.pdf

Herzel, Bazgan, Ruzika, Thielen, Vanderpooten (2021), One-exact approximate
Pareto sets, Journal of Global Optimization 80, 87-115. Author institution:
https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/one-exact-approximate-pareto-sets/

These precedents preclude calling multiplicative Pareto trimming a new
general algorithmic principle. Neither a complete prior-art search nor
historical novelty clearance of the specific reduction has been obtained.

8. Internal checks

check_positive_box_hardness.py uses exact Fraction arithmetic to check
508 independently generated reduction instances at t=2,3,9,99, including
both yes and no partition cases. Twenty-eight small constructed instances
also compare the simplified expression to direct exhaustive box-corner
evaluation. The retained JSON is hardness_checks.json. These are finite
diagnostics; the preceding proof, not the finite checks, establishes the
mathematical reduction.

An independently tasked model critic reconstructed the proof and identified
that the quantitative continuous gap requires the corner-attainment lemma,
which is now explicitly included above. The critic confirmed padding,
modular exclusion, algebra, bit size, and the coNP-completeness conclusion.
This is internal adversarial model review, not external expert or formal
proof-assistant validation. The universal threshold is instance-dependent;
verification at C(a) alone is trivially true for every instance.
