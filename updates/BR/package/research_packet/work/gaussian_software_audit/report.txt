FINDING 1 — UNCAPPED RANDOM INTEGER REJECTION (REPAIRED AT ENDPOINT)
The original DyadicUnimodal.draw caps outer proposals but directly calls Python Random.randrange three times per proposal. Its underlying bit rejection is unbounded. A legal finite rejection-prefix bit source caused 10,003 getrandbits calls inside a single outer proposal, with outer cap44. Arbitrarily longer finite prefixes are possible. Thus the original endpoint did not meet a strict bounded random-bit claim; expected-time correctness was not refuted.
The revised endpoint wraps all matching and reverse-Schur scalar draws in CappedTickets. With S=engine.max_scalar_calls, matching tolerance3eps/8, scalar tolerance3eps/(8 max(1,S)), T the larger scalar proposal cap, R=3(S+1)T is a safe bound on randrange calls. Every nontrivial ticket uses k=bit_length(width-1) bits per try and accepts with probability>1/2 (or1 for powers of two). The per-ticket cap K=bit_length(ceil(R/(eps/4))) gives R*2^-K < eps/4. Any exhausted ticket aborts the whole output to the fixed zero vector. Matching and reverse errors add to at most3eps/4; the ticket event contributes at mosteps/4. No independence between adaptive ticket failures is needed for this union bound, only conditionally unbiased independent input bits.
Independent exhaustive test: width3 with cap2 yields counts[5,5,5,1] over all16 equiprobable two-ticket bit sequences: each integer gets5/16, fallback1/16. Forced all-one bits in a real sample terminate after552bits with documented fallback, rather than hanging. Final endpoint instrumentation records ticket count/bound, bit count, cap and fallback.
Standalone bigint_sampler_arb scalar APIs and AggregateMatching.draw_k still have uncapped Python randrange when called with a bare Random; do not transfer the endpoint's bounded-bit claim to those APIs.

FINDING 2 — NONINTEGER INPUT TRUNCATION (REPAIRED AT ENDPOINT)
Original LargeHeraldGaussian accepted h=-1/2 as0, h=3/2 as1, and fractional/float herald_mode as0. Revised LargeHeraldGaussian and AggregateMatching use operator.index and reject bool. Tested24 invalid inputs across these APIs, including floats, Fractions, bool, strings and None; all were rejected. Negative integer counts and invalid modes are rejected. OUTPUT SIZE AND RESOURCE ACCOUNTING
Let T be total current edge counts, initially h. At vertex v, routed Rv<=T, and with scalar tolerance tau,
 L_v <= ceil(4(Rv+1/2)(1-p_v)/(p_v*tau)).
Thus conservatively T_new+1 <= [3+6/(p_v*tau)](T+1). Every output count is bounded by final total edge counts, so
 T_final+1 <= (h+1) product_v[3+6/(p_v*tau)].
This gives an explicit output bit-length bound polynomial in encoded h, rational pivot bit lengths, dimension and log(1/tau). The fixed fallback is also within this bound. The graph stores O(m^3) rational routing entries and invokes at most S scalar samplers. Finite timing/scaling results do not replace a proof of rational-arithmetic and loggamma complexity.
The author's Python tracemalloc field is accurately labeled Python-traced allocation; it omits native FLINT allocation and is not total resident memory. Validation matching precision/evaluation counters alone do not account for all reverse scalar evaluations. Complete process-resource claims should retain these limitations.

The revised validator computes the finite Fock weight Z exactly with rational amplitude recurrence and uses the exact Schur determinant d and effective herald matching partition P to enclose
  Zfull = P / (h! sqrt(d)),
  omitted conditional mass = 1 - Z/Zfull.
The factorial is correct: the effective kernel's z_H^h w_H^h coefficient is P/(h!)^2, and the diagonal Fock weight contributes one h!, leaving P/h!. The determinant factor is the unnormalized trace over the unobserved modes. Thus Z and Zfull share the same unnormalized original-state convention, and their ratio legitimately measures retained conditional mass. This check uses the source Schur/DP normalizer and an independent original-state amplitude expansion; it is not an independently derived second normalizer.
