1. Exact obstruction with analyticity, known range, and finite derivative observations

Let D>0, c>0, and S be a nonempty finite subset of [0,D]. Suppose the only observations are f(s)=c for s in S. For epsilon>0 define
  Q(x)=product_{s in S}(x-s)^2,
  f_epsilon(x)=c[2 exp(-Q(x)/epsilon)-1].
Each f_epsilon is real analytic, lies in [-c,c], is Lipschitz on the compact interval, and exactly matches all observations. At any z outside S, Q(z)>0, so f_epsilon(z)<0 for epsilon<Q(z)/log(2). The constant function c is also compatible. Therefore finite observations cannot certify safety f(z)>=0 at any unobserved z, even with known range, analyticity, and knowledge that some finite Lipschitz constant exists.

No finite upper bound on Lip(f) is identifiable either. Given M, choose s in S and z outside S sufficiently close that |z-s|<c/M. Choose epsilon so f_epsilon(z)<0. Its secant slope exceeds M. This defeats any proposed finite upper bound computed from the common data. Observed secant maxima provide lower bounds, not upper bounds, on the true Lipschitz constant.

Replacing each square in Q by power 2m also matches the constant function's derivatives through order 2m-1 at every sampled point. Thus observing any predetermined finite collection of derivative jets does not remove the obstruction without a quantitative regularity bound.

Randomization does not create a uniform escape. Let mu be any next-query distribution conditional on this exact transcript, and p=mu([0,D] minus S). As epsilon decreases to zero, the unsafe sets {f_epsilon<0} increase to [0,D] minus S. Hence sup_epsilon Pr_mu[f_epsilon(X)<0]=p. Any rule uniformly delta-safe over this function class must put probability at most delta on making a novel query. This is a conditional one-step statement, not an unsupported theorem about arbitrary noisy adaptive transcripts.

2. The same hidden direction exists in a known RKHS

Let k be strictly positive definite, K=k(S,S), and z outside S. Define p(x)=k(x,S)K^{-1}y and r_z(x)=k(x,z)-k(x,S)K^{-1}k(S,z). Then r_z(s)=0, r_z(z)=||r_z||_k^2>0, and p is orthogonal to r_z. Consequently f_a=p+a r_z matches every observation for every real a, while
  f_a(z)=p(z)+a||r_z||_k^2,
  ||f_a||_k^2=y^T K^{-1}y+a^2||r_z||_k^2.
Values at z and RKHS norms are unbounded over the consistent class. RKHS membership alone, including a known Gaussian kernel, supplies neither an upper norm bound nor off-sample safety. The interpolation norm is the exact minimum compatible norm. Finite-dimensional hypotheses become identifiable only after observations annihilate their nullspace; this is an actual additional resource.

3. Parent candidate: exact minimax radius is correct under stated quantifiers

Known L>0, eta>=0, D>0; exact seed f(0)=c>0; unknown model class all L-Lipschitz functions on [0,D]; actual test function f=c; safety means f>=0; n additional point observations y_i=f(x_i)+e_i with adversarial |e_i|<=eta. Each query must be safe for every consistent function. Put a=c-2eta. The maximum worst-case certifiable right endpoint is
  R_n=min(D,c/L+n max(a,0)/L).

Upper bound: the fixed admissible adversary e_i=-eta gives y_i=c-eta. After any legal query sequence the function
  g_n(x)=max(c-Lx, max_{i<=n}[a-L|x-x_i|])
is L-Lipschitz, has g_n(0)=c, and satisfies a<=g_n(x_i)<=c, exactly the required noisy consistency interval. Every consistent function lies above g_n, so this is the exact pointwise lower envelope. If a<=0, no noisy cone extends the initial safe interval. If a>0, every legal x_i is at most the previous certified endpoint, and its cone extends that endpoint by at most a/L. Induction gives the bound; g_n is negative beyond the resulting endpoint.

Attainment: under every admissible noise sequence on the actual constant function, y_i-eta>=a. Query the current rightmost certified endpoint. If a>0 this extends the safe interval by at least a/L until D; the low-noise adversary above attains equality. If a<=0, preserve the original interval. This proves the stated minimax value.

Scope qualifications: n excludes the exact seed; strict safety f>0 changes endpoint attainment into a supremum; L=0 is a separate trivial case; knowing beforehand that f is constant defeats the lower bound; stochastic centered-noise assumptions permit averaging and are a different experiment. The exact seed is a supplied resource. The 2eta obstruction is information-theoretic for adversarial bounded errors, not a GP artifact.

4. Primary-source audit and unavoidable premises

Fiedler et al., On Safety in Safe Bayesian Optimization, arXiv:2403.12948v1, Section 6.2, equation (11), Proposition 1: LoSBO uses a known L, known deterministic noise bound E, and an initial safe set. Its safe-set update is the union with {x:y_i-E-Ld(x_i,x)>=h}. The proof explicitly relates this envelope to established set-membership identification; Remark 2 permits known continuity moduli. This directly covers the candidate's safety mechanism. The radius formula above is best described as an elementary sharp special-case analysis, not a new invention.
