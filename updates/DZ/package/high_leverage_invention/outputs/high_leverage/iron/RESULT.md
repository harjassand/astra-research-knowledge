# Matter invention: solid-state iron export across a gangue-retaining boundary

2026-10-12. Status: **a physical architecture candidate, with a demonstrated nanoscale transport antecedent and several decisive unresolved transfer gates. No ore-extraction device, novelty clearance, economic advantage, experiment, or major capability has been established.**

The intended large consequence is iron from fine, 45–60 wt% Fe ores without agglomeration, acid dissolution, or melting the gangue. The candidate operation is to reduce iron at the upstream face of a nanoporous boundary, deconstruct the resulting metal at an admitted contact, transport its surface atoms through a confined path, and rebuild/harvest metal downstream. Gangue stays upstream. The strongest surviving mutation uses independently conducting, confined nanocapillaries and a moving/regrowing solid Fe plug, rather than assuming a permanently continuous 3 nm Fe film.

This is a proposed new ore-handling architecture built around old physical operations. Atomic transport through nanotubes and even the possibility of refining nanoparticles during their reconstruction are explicitly prior art. Novelty of the integrated raw-ore architecture is **UNKNOWN**. The construction has not escaped its atom-admission and electrical/material boundary.

## What is actually demonstrated

[Coh et al., PRL 110, 185901 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.185901), [author PDF](https://coh.ucr.edu/pubs/peg.pdf), experimentally move crystalline iron through a CNT constriction whose internal diameter decreases from roughly 20 to 5 nm. Diffraction supports a largely solid state; their heat estimate is at most about 440 K. The model removes atoms at the trailing end, moves them along surfaces/interfaces, and rebuilds the leading end. Their force/current conversion is fitted with explicitly large uncertainty. Their discussion already proposes possible nanoparticle refinement by repeated reconstruction. This validates a nanoscale causal operation, not ore extraction, separation selectivity, or the bulk throughput model below.

[Melinte et al., Nature Communications 6, 8071 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9071), [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4560798/), add two useful interfaces: transfer of Fe from CNTs onto graphene, and reverse collection of external nanoparticles into a CNT when polarity reverses. Reverse collection needs higher bias and acts near the contact. The study reports 0–3 V experiments, including a 2.5 V, approximately 110 μA high-current case. Tube activation can reduce encapsulated oxide by reacting with graphitic carbon, form carbides, and damage walls. These observations prevent treating the CNT cage as an inert, inexhaustible reductant-free scaffold.

Prepared Fe nanoparticle admission is therefore real; admission of Fe from unbeneficiated ore is not demonstrated.

## Construction and required source/sink interface

The upstream compartment holds raw fine ore against a replaceable, supported nanocapillary array. H2 reaches the feed and removes oxygen as H2O. A mechanically retained, exposed metallic Fe domain must touch a pre-existing Fe root at a capillary entrance. The wall carries the drive current independently of the Fe payload. Surface atom removal at the feed contact and deposition at the downstream face allow the confined metal to pass through a smaller opening than the original metallic domain. Downstream plugs are grown into a collection layer and detached while retaining a root or restart inventory. The upstream gangue is continuously discharged, including any unrecovered iron.

For each capillary, the exact birth/harvest contract is:

1. Acquire a metallic, oxide-free contact from an actual ore domain; no supplied pure Fe nanoparticle is silently substituted.
2. Maintain H2/H2O activity that reduces the admitted iron oxide without consuming the conducting wall or growing an insulating scale at the contact.
3. Admit fresh ore Fe faster than the drive exports it; maintain contact as that domain shrinks.
4. Reconstruct Fe downstream without permitting gangue fragments or mobile impurity atoms to accompany it above steel chemistry limits.
5. Detach the product, preserve/restart the conducting transport path, discharge gangue, and repeat. Product removal and repeated contact renewal cannot be omitted from costs.

The particle-contact and atom-admission operations are the largest missing service. A macroscopic bed pressing on a forest of nanotubes is not a proof that those operations occur on 94% of its iron.

## Conditional transport model and the material gate

Let capillary radius be a, active path length L, open-area fraction f, surface molar density Γ, Fe surface diffusivity Ds, effective electromigration charge Z*, field E, molar volume Vm, and T temperature. Positive compression raises the molar chemical potential. Define

μ = μ0 + γ Vm κ + Vm σh + RT ln(activity).

An ideal connected path has molar transport per projected area

J = (2f/a) Ds Γ/(RT) [Z* F E − ∂μ/∂x].

This equation is a supplied-coefficient model. Uniform mobility, all circumference active, persistent Fe contact, open source/sink boundaries, and no leakage/poisoning are assumptions. Neither CNT experiment establishes these coefficients for the proposed industrial interface.

[Nii–Yoshihara 1979](https://doi.org/10.2320/matertrans1960.20.523), [published abstract mirror](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001205117608320), measured α-Fe surface self-diffusion by grain-boundary grooving in H2 at 1073, 1123, and 1163 K. The dry, Pd-purified H2 law is Ds = 10 exp[−228000/(RT)] m²/s. Moist H2 changes the kinetics, with equilibrated oxygen adsorption decreasing mobility. This free-surface law cannot be imported as a measured Fe/CNT interface law or dirty-ore mobility. Its use below is an optimistic conditional arithmetic case.

For T=1163 K, a=L=30 nm, f=0.2, Γ obtained from a 0.3 nm atomic layer, a 3 nm conducting shell, conductivity 10^6 S/m, and Z*=10, the executed calculation gives:

| Conditional quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Ds from the stated free-Fe law | 5.75274e−10 m²/s |
| Passive flux for an assumed maintained 100 J/mol drop | 22.49 kg Fe/m²/h |
| Drive field for 10 kg Fe/m²/h with zero opposing gradient | 1536 V/m |
| Active-layer voltage | 46.09 μV |
| Projected current with exact annular metal fraction 0.042 | 64.53 MA/m² |
| Active-layer electrical loss | 0.297 MWh/t Fe |
| Additional loss for assumed contact RA=10^−12 Ω·m² | 0.416 MWh/t Fe |
| Initial shell inventory | 9.92 mg Fe/m² |
| Shell discharge time without fresh admission | 3.57 ms |

Radius and length 100 nm each lower the passive value by a factor (30/100)^2. A micrometre channel is roughly 1111 times slower than the 30 nm case. These are model scalings, not validated fluxes.

The shell is not a physically justified permanent conductor. [Yang et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep46725) intentionally transform Fe films on alumina into nanoparticles in a hot reducing atmosphere. [Bedewy et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.6b00798) observe iron catalyst-film restructuring under H2 and rapid completion of dewetting after carbon-source introduction. Thus moving to the demonstrated confined-plug representation is more credible than assuming a stable unsupported hot 3 nm Fe film. At unchanged hot free-surface coefficients, a fully conducting Fe plug has metal cross-section f rather than 0.042: its local electrical loss is approximately 4.76 times larger, about 1.42 MWh/t. The conducting CNT wall is a different model; CNT current, hot free-Fe Ds, and an assumed Fe-shell Z* cannot be mixed to claim a lower result.

## Exact failure modes and surviving scope

**Finite reservoir masquerading as extraction.** With admitted source flux S, exported flux J, and material losses Q, continuity gives dNpath/dt = S−J−Q. Therefore integral(J dt) ≤ integral(S dt)+Npath(0), regardless of how large Ds is. Millisecond discharge of the shell is compatible with zero ore atom admission. Label the ore Fe isotopically and account for every Fe seed/collector inventory; a pure-Fe product burst alone proves no extraction.

**Topological counterexample to grade/metallization sufficiency.** Consider reducible Fe oxide domains inside a mechanically intact gangue cage that admits H2/H2O but has no metal-transport connection to the capillary mouth. Within the stated allowed transport model, all such domains may metallize while zero Fe is exported. The accessible connected Fe fraction facc bounds recovery by R≤facc. Hence grade and metallization alone cannot guarantee R≥0.94; facc≥0.94 is an acquired interface obligation. Actual fracture, surface transport over gangue, or mechanical liberation could change facc, but must be demonstrated and charged. This is a counterexample to the overly broad contract, not physical impossibility for porous, accessible real ores.

**Backstress.** The optimistic EM work across the 30 nm path is only 44.47 J/mol. A hydrostatic stress difference of 6.27 MPa cancels it in the simple model. The [Blech primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1359-6454(97)00446-1) demonstrates backflow/stalling in short metal strips. Free growing source/sink surfaces can relieve stress; a constrained collar or blocked product collector can restore it. The Fe/CNT experiment demonstrates motion in its own open geometry, not absence of stress stalls in this reactor.

**Curvature and coarsening.** For assumed γ=1 J/m² and 30 nm radius, γVm/a≈236 J/mol and 2γVm/a≈473 J/mol. A maintained 100 J/mol feed-to-product drop is not automatic. Feed-side Fe coarsening can erase it; an empty narrow throat can have a greater capillary entry penalty than the modeled 44 J/mol drive. Feeding a pre-filled plug may avoid creating the entire throat afresh, but must prove continued source contact and sink withdrawal. Extrapolated planar Mullins smoothing is extremely fast at nanoscale lengths; it is a warning about preserving shape, not a measured dewetting lifetime.

**Stopped whisker is a contrary control.** [Murakami et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.2355/isijinternational.ISIJINT-2025-330) observe H2-induced Fe whiskers, including pressurized low-temperature cases. Their in-situ observation says vertical growth stops after surrounding root material becomes metallic while lateral reduction also occurs. Whiskers do not establish continuous ore-to-export feeding. The proposed drive must produce a measured difference from that stopped-growth control.

**Purity.** A small pore geometrically rejects large gangue fragments. It does not reject surface-mobile S/P, dissolved/metallic Ni/Cu, carbon, or scaffold species. Each impurity has its own mobility and driving force. The cited CNT work does not provide a steel-grade impurity guarantee and sometimes observes carbide phases. Reject any claim that atomwise reconstruction automatically purifies all contaminants.

## Mass/energy boundary and consequence gate

On the illustrative hematite-plus-inert-gangue basis, 1 t exported Fe at 45% Fe grade and 94% export recovery needs 2.364 t ore and leaves 0.843 t gangue. At 97% metallization, 31.9 kg metallic Fe and 31.9 kg unreduced Fe remain outside the product. The H2 floor is 54.15 kg/t exported Fe if only exported Fe is reduced, rising to 55.87 kg for the declared metallization; purge, recycle, heating, and incomplete utilization add costs.

Using deliberately simple assumed heat capacities and fusion duty, heating this gangue from 890 to 1600°C plus 400 kJ/kg melting costs approximately 0.260 MWh/t Fe. Heating the entire ore from 25 to 890°C costs approximately 0.454 MWh/t before recuperation. The optimistic 0.297 MWh/t export-drive cost already matches the avoided high-temperature gangue duty before contacts or membrane replacement. Thus avoiding gangue melting alone has not established an energy advantage. Ore/pellet premiums, yield, flux, waste quality, availability, and whole-process costs could still change the comparison; they are currently UNKNOWN.

Count H2 generation, reductant consumption, H2O removal, all heat and heat recovery, electricity in the active path and every contact/lead, ore preparation and contact renewal, support/nanotube production and replacement, any carbon consumed into CO/CO2 or carbide, purity finishing, gangue discharge and lost Fe, and final melting of the recovered Fe if the comparator output is molten metal.

## Decisive next test, frozen before any claimed performance

First test a 1 cm² or smaller supported capillary coupon with all electrical contacts inside the metered boundary. Match source/seed temperature and total power across controls: bias off, reversed bias, blocked pore, preloaded Fe with no ore, prepared pure Fe source, and isotope-labelled oxide mixed with quartz. Only after atom admission is established move to a real untreated low-grade ore.

Require isotope-resolved downstream Fe from the feed, export exceeding 100 times all accessible preloaded transport-path Fe, sustained replenishment over repeated source contacts and product harvests, measured carbon/scaffold balance, full upstream/downstream Fe/gangue mass balance, and an impurity assay. Observe contact and path continuity; measure actual Ds/drive response in the relevant wet H2/ore environment rather than inserting a clean-surface value. Measure V, I, actual temperature, and net product simultaneously.

Provisional capability gate: ≥94% metallization and ≥94% Fe recovery on the declared real fine ore, steel-usable chemistry, no uncounted agglomeration/beneficiation/stoichiometric reagent, and lower fully accounted cost than an equally scoped HYFOR+Smelter or electrowinning comparator. The 10 kg/m²/h and 0.6 MWh/t total export-electricity numbers are exploratory campaign targets, not sourced industrial facts. They cannot close the gate without the acquired contact and scaffold interface.

**Decision:** retain the confined, independently conducting plug as the most credible representation mutation for a tightly bounded interface experiment. Do not advance a major technological claim. The thin-shell model is useful as an optimistic scale calculation and has failed as an established physical device; the bulk ore admission, purity, lifespan, and full energy/cost obligations remain open.
