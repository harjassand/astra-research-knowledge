# FK repair observable: a target-local certificate and its boundary

**Result.** A repair can be qualified without reconstructing every dislocation in a crystal, but only as a *target- and operating-envelope-specific* claim. The useful replacement for mobility, disappearance of one diffraction contrast, or zero signed Burgers sum is a calibrated endpoint certificate that measures (i) the target line's 3D Burgers class, (ii) stacking-fault displacement and area, (iii) all target-component crossings/reaction nodes at a closed local boundary, (iv) path-integrated plastic slip, and (v) the retained device output after the controls are removed. A global claim that no harmful defect remains is not available from those cheap summaries: neutral loops/dipoles and unobserved branches lie in their nullspace. The assay must return `INCONCLUSIVE` when a boundary path, defect class, or spatial scale is blind.

This is a measurement design and exact counterexample, not evidence that any crystal has been repaired. The ZnS, GaAs/Si, SiC, and other literature platforms in the supplied physical ledger are not merged into one material or one operating regime.

## Exact obstruction: endpoint sums lose both topology and history

Represent a dislocation network in a volume by its Burgers-labelled line current

\[
\alpha(x)=\sum_j b_j\otimes t_j\,\delta_{\Gamma_j}(x),
\]

where \(b_j\) is the signed Burgers vector, \(t_j\) is the oriented line tangent, and \(\delta_{\Gamma_j}\) is line measure. With a fixed orientation convention, the Burgers circuit around an oriented surface \(S\) reads

\[
B(S)=\oint_{\partial S}\beta^p\,dx=\int_S\alpha n\,dA
    =\sum_{p\in S\cap\Gamma_j}\!\operatorname{sgn}(p,S,\Gamma_j)b_j.
\]

This is a **signed** intersection count. Two opposite lines crossing the measurement surface contribute \(+b-b=0\), and a closed line can cross a spanning surface with cancelling signs. Thus zero circuit closure is necessary for the absence of a net enclosed Burgers charge, but it is not a bound on unsigned line length, fault area, or the topology of a network. At a true junction, the oriented incident Burgers vectors must sum to zero; measuring only their total over a large surface does not identify which junction or branches produced it.

An exact partial-reaction example is

\[
b_1=\tfrac16[1,-2,1],\qquad b_2=\tfrac16[2,-1,-1],\qquad
b_1+b_2=\tfrac12[1,-1,0]\ne0.
\]

The two partials may approach, removing their intervening fault ribbon, while leaving the perfect dislocation with Burgers vector \(b_1+b_2\). Consequently, *fault-contrast disappearance is not line removal*. This exact rational identity is rechecked in [exact_checks.py](exact_checks.py) and [exact_checks.json](exact_checks.json); it is also preserved as N1 in the supplied physical-operation ledger.

A second exact counterexample isolates plastic history. Take two parallel, opposite-Burgers lines \(+b\) and \(-b\), both length \(L\), on the same slip plane, separated by glide distance \(d\). Their signed net Burgers vector is zero both before and after they annihilate. The sweep area is \(Ld\). For specimen volume \(V\) and slip-plane normal \(n\), the accumulated volume-averaged plastic distortion in the ideal parallel geometry is

\[
\Delta\beta^p=\frac{Ld}{V}\,b\otimes n,
\qquad \Delta\varepsilon^p=\operatorname{sym}(\Delta\beta^p).
\]

The pair is gone at the endpoint, but its plastic shear need not be. If the same opposite pair exits at free surfaces, the endpoint can again have zero internal net Burgers content while retaining a surface step and the same swept-area contribution. Endpoint elastic strain can relax; it is not a measurement of the accumulated plastic distortion.

These examples give three distinct checks: signed Burgers balance, unsigned residual line/fault content, and path-integrated slip. None substitutes for the other two.

## Scope and definition of “harmful”

Before acquiring data, specify a crystal and lattice basis, the harmful line/fault classes, the active volume, a target region \(U\), its guard collar, the spatial cutoff \(r\), the minimum reportable line/fault size, and the service conditions under which performance matters. “Harmful” is not a crystallographic label: a nonzero \(b\) alone does not establish a device penalty. The operation needs a measured functional target (for example a specified leakage, carrier lifetime, or transport metric) or a separately validated map from defect class and location to that target. Without it the result can certify structural change only.

The global active volume \(\Omega_{\rm act}\) can be larger than the locally resolved target assay. A target-local pass says that this defect/component and any products in its guard collar satisfy the stated bounds. It does not say that all remote defects in \(\Omega_{\rm act}\) were removed. A device-level retained-performance pass can qualify performance under the tested service cycle, but it cannot identify the microstructure elsewhere or promise behavior under an untested condition.

## Proposed certificate: targeted 3D closure + slip + retained output

The certificate is a four-state decision (`PASS`, `FAIL`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, `NOT RUN`) with immutable pre/post acquisition records. It deliberately does not require tracing unrelated network branches. It does require following the target component and any branch that reaches the guard-collar boundary; an unresolved crossing makes the result inconclusive.

1. **Predeclare the observable and blind set.** From the material's allowed finite Burgers catalog \(\mathcal B\) and stacking-fault translation catalog \(\mathcal R/\Lambda\), enumerate all crystallographically legal target and substitute products. For each physically available diffraction vector \(g\), record the cost of a registered image/scan and the classes it can see. In the ideal kinematic contrast rules, a dislocation is extinct only when both \(g\cdot b=0\) and \(g\cdot(b\times u)=0\); a stacking fault with translation \(R\) is extinct when \(g\cdot R\in\mathbb Z\). Pick a minimum-cost reflection cover that gives each nonzero \(b\in\mathcal B\) and each non-lattice \(R\in\mathcal R\) at least one non-extinct condition. Add opposite \(\pm g\) conditions where sign/dipole polarity is needed. Then verify these conditions under the actual foil thickness, excitation error, detector and material structure factors. If there is no such set of allowed reflections, add an independent measurement mode or report that class as blind. For finite catalogs an ideal finite cover exists if every reciprocal-lattice vector is available: nonzero \(b\) cannot be orthogonal to a spanning reciprocal basis, and \(R\notin\Lambda\) means some reciprocal vector has \(g\cdot R\notin\mathbb Z\). Real instruments provide only a subset of reflections with usable structure factor and signal, so the material-specific cover can still fail.

   For the ZnS/f.c.c. pilot, an exact cover fixture for the supplied partials uses \(G=\{(0,2,-2),(2,0,-2),(3,1,1)\}\). The first two conditions occur in the cited ZnS electric-glide microscopy; the third is an added out-of-plane allowed f.c.c. reflection whose measured structure factor and usable contrast must be checked in the actual foil. The matrix has determinant \(-20\). For \(b_1=\frac16[1,-2,1]\), \(b_2=\frac16[2,-1,-1]\), and \(b_1+b_2=\frac12[1,-1,0]\), the signed \(g\cdot b\) triplets are \((-1,0,1/3)\), \((0,1,2/3)\), and \((-1,1,1)\). Thus each line class is visible in at least one condition and the triplets distinguish the three. The fault phases \(g\cdot R\bmod1\) are \((0,0,1/3)\) and \((0,0,2/3)\) for the two partial shifts; they are detectable but identify only the shift modulo a perfect-lattice translation. Since \(b_1+b_2\in\Lambda\), \(R=b_2\) aliases \(R=-b_1\), and \(R=b_1\) aliases \(R=-b_2\). Planar fault contrast alone therefore cannot assign the signed bounding partials; the line contrast/Burgers circuit is still required. This exact table chooses a finite test set; it does not certify that these reflections have adequate experimental signal or that all ZnS substitute classes were covered.

2. **Register the target in 3D before intervention.** Place fiducials around \(U\), measure orientation, local thickness, strain and temperature, then map the target line and its fault boundaries. For near-surface bulk screening, ECCI can image individual dislocations over wider areas; it remains diffraction-condition and near-surface limited. For an electron-transparent target, use 4D-STEM/CBED or multi-\(g\) weak-beam/diffraction-contrast TEM, with a small targeted tilt set where depth/connectivity is ambiguous. Atom-resolved displacement phase or a Burgers circuit around the target core is a local cross-check. Map only the target component and the routes from it to the guard collar; do not infer connectivity from a crossing in a projection.

3. **Track boundary flux and reaction topology.** At each detected junction, assign line sense and verify \(\sum_i\epsilon_i b_i=0\) against the predeclared reaction catalog. For each fault patch, measure its translation class and verify that its boundary partials match the mapped lines. At the closed boundary of the target-plus-collar volume, record every crossing with position, signed \(b\), and line direction. Follow an outgoing line to a measured free-surface sink or to a mapped product; otherwise fail closed as `INCONCLUSIVE`. If the active device boundary itself is not fully covered by a bulk-appropriate assay, no claim about boundary export from the whole active volume is made.

4. **Measure the path-dependent strain term.** Track specimen fiducials or an in-situ marker field during the operation and integrate signed swept areas by slip system:

   \[
   \Delta\beta^p_{\rm path}=V^{-1}\sum_s b_s\otimes n_s\,\Delta A_s.
   \]

   Compare this with the independently registered displacement field and surface step. Report the uncertainty/tolerance on the tensor (or engineering shear) separately from the post-treatment elastic-strain map. For the simple opposite pair above the predicted shear scale is \(|b|Ld/V\), even though signed net \(b=0\). A final strain snapshot alone cannot close this path integral.

5. **Run the retained operating readout.** Restore controls and return the specimen to the declared duty temperature, stress, illumination/electrical bias, and ambient. Re-measure the declared functional metric after a fixed dwell and over a fixed cycle count, against same-specimen baseline and matched dark, constant-drive, dose/heating, and readout-beam controls. Pre-register a minimum improvement \(\Delta P_{\min}\), allowed drift/damage, and a non-inferiority margin for the other required outputs. Use the actual product output: for a semiconductor device this could be four-terminal I–V/Hall transport or time-resolved photoluminescence lifetime, but only if that is the property the dislocation is alleged to harm. Record wall-plug optical/electrical energy, heating, mechanical work, dose, readout time, preparation and control costs. “Retained” means the lower confidence bound on the improvement persists through that stated horizon after the repair controls have been removed; it does not imply indefinite lifetime.

6. **Report bounds, not zero by fiat.** Calibrate a worst-case detection probability over the allowed \(b,R,u\), target locations, foil thicknesses and overlap conditions using reference specimens whose line/fault content is independently known, and a blinded second reader. Image insertions can measure analysis-pipeline sensitivity only; they do not calibrate physical detector sensitivity. Report upper confidence bounds on undetected harmful line length and fault area at resolution \(r\), with assay sensitivity and field coverage. A zero image count without such a calibration is only “none detected.” A literature workflow that extracts 80% of line content on its example cannot be silently treated as a 100%-complete detector.

The retained certificate is the bounded vector

\[
\mathbf z_{\rm post}=\big(\{L_b(U)\}_{b\in\mathcal B},\{A_R(U)\}_{R\in\mathcal R},N_{\rm substitute\ junctions},\mathcal X_{\partial U},\Delta\beta^p_{\rm path},P_{\rm service}(0{:}T)\big),
\]

where line length is unsigned by Burgers class, fault area is resolved by translation class, \(\mathcal X_{\partial U}\) lists individual boundary crossings rather than just their signed sum, and the last coordinate is the measured performance trajectory after controls are removed. The thresholds and confidence bounds on each coordinate are specified before treatment.

**Pass rule.** A `PASS` requires all of: (a) a registered pre-treatment harmful target; (b) post-treatment target-class line and fault upper bounds below the predeclared tolerance in \(U\) plus its guard collar; (c) no unresolved boundary crossings and no above-tolerance substitute junction/fault/surface step in the assayed region; (d) accumulated plastic distortion below the threshold; and (e) retained operating improvement over the matched controls for the stated horizon. A clean endpoint circuit, a disappearing line in one reflection, a reduced fault area, a fast glide trace, or a first-hit repair event alone is a `FAIL` for certification. An uncalibrated/badly oriented measurement, missing boundary path, below-cutoff defect class, or unresolved overlap is `INCONCLUSIVE`, not `PASS`.

## Why this avoids a whole-network reconstruction, and when it cannot

The necessary reconstruction domain is the target's **connected reaction neighborhood**, not all dislocations in the crystal. The boundary record detects a line that has left the local field; only that line's route must be followed to a sink. A complete graph of unrelated dislocations is unnecessary if the target's local closure and a separately measured device performance envelope suffice. In practice, if the target is joined to a specimen-spanning junction network, following the products can become nearly a whole-network reconstruction. The certificate then correctly loses its hoped-for acquisition advantage.

There is an exact observation-equivalence obstruction to a stronger global claim. Let the structural assay image only \(U\), report only the signed net Burgers content of \(\Omega_{\rm act}\), and take one terminal electrical measurement on a current path that excludes a mechanically inactive region \(W\subset\Omega_{\rm act}\). State A contains no line in \(W\). State B is identical in \(U\) and contains an unobserved closed perfect-dislocation loop in \(W\); its signed flux through a complete outer circuit is zero, and it can have no stacking-fault area if it is a perfect loop. In the ideal readout geometry, the loop is outside the measured current path, so the chosen scalar output is exactly identical. Yet the internal defect networks differ. Thus this finite observation map is non-injective even before finite resolution/noise is added. To certify that *no* harmful defect remains in a global claim, either widen the structural assay to the entire relevant active volume at sufficient class/resolution, or narrow the claim to the tested target and service envelope. No new scalar observable avoids that missing information universally.

There is also a practical, not just topological, blind space. TEM contrast depends on diffraction vector, thickness, tilt and overlap. A fault can vanish when \(g\cdot R\) is integral; a line can be invisible for a given \(g\); a single projection confounds depth. Therefore, the presence of multiple selectable reflections and a calibrated detection floor are part of the certificate, not optional improvements.

## Acquisition budget and present method limits

The budget is target-class and specimen specific; the primary-method sources do not provide a universal scan time, dose, or cost per area. The correct report is measured instrument time, electron/X-ray dose, preparation loss, analysis effort, beamtime, control count, and functional cycling for the actual run. A useful work-plan cost expression is

\[
C_{\rm assay}=C_{\rm selection+preparation}+2\sum_{g\in G}C_{\rm registered\ scan}(A,r,\tau)
+C_{\rm local\ tilt/4D-STEM}+C_{\rm boundary\ follow-up}
+C_{\rm fiducial\ strain/path}+C_{\rm performance\ cycles}+C_{\rm matched\ controls}+C_{\rm analysis}.
\]

The factor 2 denotes baseline and post-treatment; dynamic tracking adds frames and beam dose. For the finite ZnS/f.c.c. example, the structural cover starts with three reflections, one additional to the cited two-beam ZnS set, and may add opposite conditions for sign, plus a separate scan for each registered target/timepoint. The catalog-wide minimum is an explicit weighted set-cover problem over visible line/fault classes; the weights are scan time/dose and orientation-change cost. It can be solved exactly for a small known material catalog, but set-cover optimality does not certify real-world detectability.

**Equal-target-information comparator.** To test whether this certificate actually saves acquisition over full reconstruction, process the same pre/post target-plus-collar volume by (a) conventional intensity tomography and (b) targeted object-based line tomography, supplemented by the same multi-g/fault and path-strain measurements. Require both pipelines to return the same decision variables — target line lengths by Burgers class, fault areas by translation class, junction products, every collar-boundary crossing, and uncertainty at the same spatial cutoff — with matched calibration and blinded review. Charge all tilts, setup, dose, analysis and failed scans. Conventional tomography is reported at roughly 50–100 projections, while the object method reports selected line reconstruction with 2–3 views; these literature figures do not establish equal sensitivity or lower cost for this certificate. The cited object pipeline's incomplete/false detections make that comparison an experiment still to be run. If the lower-view path cannot meet the same blind-set and sensitivity thresholds, it has not delivered equal target information and this proposed acquisition advantage fails.

Relevant primary-method limits are:

- **ECCI/SEM:** A [primary 2017 ECCI study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-09756-3) describes few-nanometre lateral resolution, a visibility depth of order 100 nm, diffraction-condition dependence, and a required local orientation accuracy better than 0.1°. Its dipole analysis used five distinct \(g\) vectors and ±\(g\) pairs for selected reflections. The [ZnS/GaP v2 optical-glide methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07908v2) provide a closer platform-specific count: eight ECCI micrographs for pre/post images at four 365 nm intensities, 148 misfit dislocations, 30 kV/2.4 nA imaging, and 350 °C cycles. That work also measures relaxation with HRXRD symmetric (400) and asymmetric (224)/(\(\bar2 24\)) reciprocal-space maps; use those for population/strain baselines, not to assign a single line's topology. ECCI is cheaper over broad near-surface areas than making a TEM foil, but is not a whole-thickness assay and its longest-line mobility sample is selection-limited.
- **4D-STEM/CBED:** [Niermann et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10866901/) show 3D location/type classification from one 4D-STEM measurement by comparing measured pattern variation with multi-beam calculations. In their GaN example, thickness and line depth were fit to about 5 nm calculation steps for a 132 nm foil. The paper identifies a mid-plane mirror ambiguity, and the method remains subject to the \(g\cdot b\) coverage and fidelity of the assumed strain/elastic model. Cost is one diffraction pattern at each probe position, detector/data volume, and model fitting; a single projection is not a 3D network map.
- **Targeted dislocation tomography:** A [primary object-based TEM study](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/28/3/633/6889418) says conventional intensity tomography can require roughly 50–100 projections; its node/line method reports good positional accuracy with as few as three images and can reconstruct selected objects from as few as two tilts. Its example pipeline recovered about 80% of true line content while also producing false lines; manual matching of lines across views was the slowest step. It explicitly warns that projected crossings can be fake junctions, and the demonstrated network omitted extended/partial line detail from its simplified reconstruction. These costs/evidence argue for target-local use plus explicit sensitivity and junction checks, not a claim of completeness.
- **Bulk X-ray option:** Dark-field X-ray microscopy penetrates bulk material and is an appropriate complement when a TEM foil cannot represent subsurface motion. The [2025 DFXM Burgers-vector paper](https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2025/01/00/nb5385/nb5385.html) reports multi-peak comparison for one edge-dislocation example and develops single-peak Burgers-vector extraction from rocking-tilt scans; it also states practical difficulty of multi-peak scans. Treat the single-peak assignment as a modality-specific method, not an already validated general assay. A bulk survey's volume, reflections, rocking angles and time must be charged.
- **Growth/strain comparator:** The [forward-graded ZnS\(_y\)Se\(_{1-y}\)/GaAs study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11664-010-1101-z) is credited by the supplied ledger for X-ray-based dislocation-density changes and an overshoot-interface annihilation/coalescence route. The source does not supply a same-defect before/after topology, and a growth-stack result cannot be used as evidence for a post-growth carrier-gated reaction.
- **Beam and thermal regime:** In the [2023 ZnS electric-field study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-023-01572-7), the authors report dislocation motion during an interval with the electron beam off. The optical-glide methods instead anneal at 350 °C and image the same location after cooling. Accordingly, charge a matched beam-only/readout exposure control, do not assume the beam is passive, and keep the electric-field and optical/350 °C arms separate unless a joint same-specimen control set is acquired.
- **Fault contrast:** In diffraction, the fault phase shift is \(2\pi g\cdot R\). A fault contrast can disappear when \(g\cdot R\) is an integer. The [4H-SiC primary methods paper](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jap/article/116/10/104905/386748/A-method-to-determine-fault-vectors-in-4H-SiC-from) notes that phase-based fault-vector solutions may not be unique; couple fault contrast with the line Burgers circuit and a different g condition.

A sample-level no-defect statement cannot be obtained by multiplying nominal microscope resolution by an unmeasured detection sensitivity. Report the imaged volume, spatial/contrast threshold, orientations, failed acquisitions, thickness, missing region, and detection calibration. If a boundary or interior is outside all modalities, preserve it as `UNKNOWN`.

## Intervention compatibility and retained-reward mapping

The source physical ledger calls for testing carrier-gated reaction selection on one mapped specimen and actual platform, with dark/constant-drive/dose-matched controls and heating, drift, beam and mechanical work measured. The closest concrete pilot is the supplied ZnS/GaP optical-glide platform: the v2 report uses 20 nm ZnS films, a 365 nm LED, ECCI in an environmental SEM, 350 °C anneal cycles, cooling to room temperature for same-location imaging, eight micrographs across four light levels, and 148 counted misfit lines. It analyzes only the longest dislocations for the carrier-mobility comparison because pinning biases glide. Those are useful acquisition and selection facts, not a qualified repair result: the endpoint survey must retain all predeclared target classes, follow boundary motion, and measure fault/strain/function, rather than keep only the longest lines. It specifically warns that this platform does not establish a room-temperature electric-drive/optical-lock combination. The proposed certificate therefore does not assume a universal temperature or conflate ZnS, GaAs/Si, SiC, and SrTiO3 results.

Use the ECCI same-location/low-drift setup as the first structural screen for this thin-film platform, then acquire the costlier multi-\(g\) target line/fault assay only for registered candidates and their guard collars. For a growth-engineering comparator, the credited forward-graded ZnSSe/GaAs work uses high-resolution X-ray diffraction and asymmetric dislocation-density/strain profiles; its overshoot interface promotes annihilation/coalescence at the population level. That is a meaningful growth/strain baseline, but rocking-curve density and optical quality do not assign a given line's Burgers vector or prove retained device benefit. Compare it as a distinct grown architecture, not as the same post-growth optical reaction experiment.

The current capital route N777 is compatible with the *measurement contract* (same specimen, legal controls, causal endpoint, and complete energy/damage ledger) but supplies no acquired dislocation-reaction control pair. N783 suggests a useful distinction between first repair and retained reward. The explicit mapping here is: configuration = registered defect network plus measured carrier/environment state; event = target reaction; immediate reward = newly qualified functional output; damage/cost = new defect/fault, plastic slip, and full intervention energy; retention horizon = the specified operating cycles after controls are removed. N783's Markov-reward inequalities do **not** transfer to the crystal: this task has no measured full-state transition kernel or conservative potential. Therefore the required output is a directly measured retained performance trajectory, not an imported committor guarantee.

## Scoped conclusion

For a specified harm target, a closed and observed target neighborhood, a material-specific reflection cover, calibrated detection bounds, path-integrated slip measurement, and a persistent functional endpoint, the proposed assay can certify target-local retained repair without reconstructing unrelated network branches. This is conditional on acquiring the listed measurements; no physical pass has occurred here.

For unrestricted “all harmful Burgers/fault content removed from the entire crystal” claims, the answer is no unless the structural survey covers the whole relevant active volume or the claim is narrowed. Zero signed Burgers content, a fault-contrast null, mobility, and a scalar terminal device readout each have exact or resolution-limited indistinguishable alternatives. No device-level throughput, energy advantage, new repair operation, priority, or long-term durability is established.
