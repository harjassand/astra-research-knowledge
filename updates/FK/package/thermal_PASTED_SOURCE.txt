The target is an observable-specific thermal response,
\[ \chi_{\beta,A}(i\eta), \]
given an explicit interacting Hamiltonian \(H\), inverse temperature \(\beta\), observable \(A\), and small positive frequency \(\eta\).
This is narrower than reconstructing the complete spectral density. It is also not automatically a DC conductivity: conserved contributions, diamagnetic terms, and limiting procedures require separate treatment.
For ordered energy levels \(E_n\), thermal probabilities \(p_n\), and Hermitian \(A\), define
\[ d\mu_{\beta,A}(\Delta) = \sum_{E_m>E_n} (p_n-p_m)|A_{nm}|^2\, \delta_{E_m-E_n}(d\Delta). \]
Then
\[ \boxed{ \chi_{\beta,A}(i\eta) = \int_0^\infty \frac{2\Delta}{\Delta^2+\eta^2}\, d\mu_{\beta,A}(\Delta). } \]
The task is to obtain this scalar with controlled error without receiving the eigensystem or transition measure as an oracle.
A scalar-only formulation would not itself be an advance. Mutzel and Tilloy’s 2026 preprint already bounds linear spectral functionals directly from Euclidean Monte Carlo data using a hierarchy of semidefinite programs. That is the pertinent certification comparator when the acquired information consists of Euclidean correlations. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09791)
Direct-frequency computation is also not new. Tupitsyn and collaborators construct real-frequency diagrammatic calculations, with convergence, diagram complexity, and cancellations remaining important costs. Correction-vector and dynamical density-matrix renormalization methods likewise target dynamical responses rather than an unrestricted many-body reconstruction. The following analysis shows why that operation fails for a substantial, precisely specified class.
The issue is not insufficient sampling. It is that a fixed amount of localized thermal forcing has vanishing influence on the normalized bulk response.
Consider an auxiliary lattice with \(N\) time slices and an arbitrary finite periodic spatial lattice.
Let
\[ K=\alpha I+\kappa_\tau L_\tau+\kappa_xL_x, \qquad \alpha>0, \]
where the \(L\)'s are nonnegative graph Laplacians. The positive constant \(\alpha\) provides a uniform restoring force. Discretization is fixed; a continuum limit cannot silently treat all these constants as unchanged.
Write \(g=\lambda/6\geq0\). The ordinary positive-action sampler is
\[ dX^0 = -\left[KX^0+g(X^0)^3\right]ds+\sqrt2\,dW. \]
Its stationary density is
\[ \pi_N^0(dx) \propto \exp\!\left[ -\frac12x^\top Kx-\frac g4\sum_i x_i^4 \right]dx. \]
Now select \(r\) fixed auxiliary-time slices. Let \(P\) indicate their coordinates, and let \(Y\) supply thermal configurations there. The anchored sampler is
\[ dX = -\left[ KX+g(I-P)X^3+gPY^3 \right]ds+\sqrt2\,dW. \]
Thus the thermal source replaces the cubic interaction drift at the anchors.
Couple \(X\) and \(X^0\) using the same noise, and define
\[ B= \sup_{j\in\mathrm{anchors}} \left\| g\left(Y_j^3-(X_j^0)^3\right) \right\|_{L^2}. \]
The requirement is that \(B\) remain bounded as the auxiliary and spatial lattices grow. For a compact thermal source with the same strongly convex quartic structure, this follows from dimension-independent moment bounds; it need not be assumed as an unexplained oracle.
For a fixed spatial site \(x\), define the normalized temporal Fourier observable
\[ F_{x,N}(\omega) = \frac1{\sqrt N} \sum_{t=0}^{N-1}e^{-i\omega t}X_{t,x}, \]
and define \(F^0_{x,N}\) similarly.
Under the stated assumptions,
\[ \boxed{ \|F_{x,N}-F^0_{x,N}\|_{L^2} \leq D_N:=\frac{rB}{\alpha\sqrt N}. } \]
Consequently,
\[ \boxed{ \left| \mathbb E|F_{x,N}|^2-\mathbb E|F^0_{x,N}|^2 \right| \leq \frac{2D_N}{\sqrt\alpha}+D_N^2. } \]
The bound is independent of the number of spatial sites and uniform in \(\omega\). It holds for every \(\lambda\geq0\), not just weak coupling.
Therefore, fixed \(r\), fixed \(\alpha\), and bounded \(B\) imply that the thermal-anchor correction vanishes as \(N\to\infty\).
For a fixed finite spatial system, the unanchored long-imaginary-time path integral approaches its zero-temperature response. The anchored sampler approaches that same reference limit, rather than preserving an independently prescribed finite temperature.
Set \(Z=X-X^0\). Away from the anchors,
\[ X_i^3-(X_i^0)^3 = \left[X_i^2+X_iX_i^0+(X_i^0)^2\right]Z_i. \]
The bracket is nonnegative. The difference therefore satisfies
\[ dZ=-(K+D_s)Z\,ds+f_s\,ds, \]
where \(D_s\) is nonnegative diagonal and \(f_s\) is supported only at the anchors.
The nonnegative heat kernel of \(K\) dominates the propagation of this forced difference. This gives
\[ \|Z_i\|_2 \leq B\sum_{j\in\mathrm{anchors}}(K^{-1})_{ij}. \]
For one anchor time \(s\), the product-lattice Green function obeys
\[ \sum_{t,y} (K^{-1})_{(t,x),(s,y)} = \frac1\alpha. \]
Summing over the \(r\) anchor slices and applying the \(N^{-1/2}\) normalization proves the first bound.
The reference potential has Hessian at least \(K\). The established Brascamp–Lieb covariance inequality then gives
\[ \mathbb E|F^0_{x,N}|^2\leq\frac1\alpha. \]
Expanding the squared observable and applying Cauchy–Schwarz proves the second bound. The covariance inequality is imported mathematics, not a new result of this run. [Princeton University](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/on-extensions-of-the-brunn-minkowski-and-pr%C3%A9kopa-leindler-theorem/)
This does not rule out all positive response representations, nonlocal updates, correlated auxiliary constructions, or different ways of transmitting thermal information. It does not cover a vanishing restoring constant, arbitrary nonconvex interactions, or moving anchors.
Within this class, an order-one thermal correction requires at least
\[ rB\not=o(\sqrt N). \]
That is a necessary condition, not a sufficient repair. Increasing the number or strength of anchors does not establish that the resulting response is correct.
The nonlinear bound identifies the limiting problem. An exact first-order calculation identifies the wrong physical quantity.
Consider the spatially coupled quantum oscillator lattice
\[ H= \frac12\sum_xp_x^2 +\frac{m^2}{2}\sum_xq_x^2 +\frac{\kappa}{2} \sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(q_x-q_y)^2 +\frac{\lambda}{24}\sum_xq_x^4. \]
Its free normal-mode frequencies are
\[ \Omega(k)^2 = m^2+4\kappa\sum_{j=1}^d\sin^2(k_j/2). \]
Define
\[ c_\beta = \frac1{|\Lambda|} \sum_k \frac{\coth(\beta\Omega(k)/2)}{2\Omega(k)}, \qquad c_\infty = \frac1{|\Lambda|} \sum_k\frac1{2\Omega(k)}. \]
The exact first right derivative of the physical response is
\[ \boxed{ \left. \partial_\lambda\chi_\beta(i\eta,k) \right|_{0^+} = -\frac{c_\beta} {2[\eta^2+\Omega(k)^2]^2}. } \]
The thermal factor \(c_\beta\) comes from the internal equal-site contraction at the quartic interaction.
For the independently driven finite-anchor construction, the first-order covariance instead satisfies a Lyapunov equation. At zero coupling the thermal source and auxiliary field are independent, so the source contribution to that first derivative vanishes. In the long auxiliary-domain limit, the resulting coefficient contains \(c_\infty\), not \(c_\beta\).
This is an exact derivative statement, not an assumption that first-order perturbation theory solves the strongly interacting system.

One proposed repair would be to add an independent stationary thermal field to a vacuum response field:
\[ X=X_{\mathrm{vac}}+Y. \]
Its covariance is
\[ C_X=C_{\mathrm{vac}}+C_Y. \]
An independent stationary field can only add a nonnegative spectral contribution.
But the exact weak-quartic calculation gives
\[ \chi_\beta(i\eta)-\chi_\infty(i\eta) = -\lambda\, \frac{c_\beta-c_\infty} {2[\eta^2+\Omega(k)^2]^2} +o(\lambda), \]
which is negative at finite temperature for sufficiently small positive \(\lambda\).
Therefore independent additive thermal noise cannot repair this response family. A successful construction would have to alter correlations or the dynamics, not simply add positive fluctuations.
This does not exclude correlated fields, modified drift operators, or other acquisition mechanisms.
The obstruction separates two tasks that the candidate treated as interchangeable:
Sampling thermal configurations is not the same as acquiring thermal transition weights.
The desired response already has a positive spectral representation. The unresolved difficulty is constructing that representation efficiently from the Hamiltonian and temperature.
To make the missing interface precise, suppose the sum rule is finite:
\[ M_1 = \langle[A,[H,A]]\rangle_\beta = 2\int\Delta\,d\mu_{\beta,A}(\Delta). \]
For \(M_1>0\), define
\[ d\nu_{\beta,A}(\Delta) = \frac{2\Delta}{M_1}\, d\mu_{\beta,A}(\Delta). \]
This is a probability measure, and
\[ \boxed{ \chi_{\beta,A}(i\eta) = M_1\, \mathbb E_{\nu_{\beta,A}} \left[ \frac1{\Delta^2+\eta^2} \right]. } \]
A useful new operation could acquire samples from this observable-weighted thermal transition distribution—or obtain an equivalent response estimator—without reconstructing the whole spectrum.
The identity is not such an algorithm. Treating \(\nu_{\beta,A}\) as an available sampler would hide the original scientific problem.
Even the conditional sampling cost must be stated carefully. With exact independent samples and known \(M_1\),
\[ R_\eta = \frac{\eta^2\chi_\beta(i\eta)}{M_1} \]
is the expectation of a variable in \([0,1]\). Estimating \(R_\eta\) is straightforward. But for fixed absolute susceptibility error \(\varepsilon_\chi\), that elementary bounded-estimator argument gives a sample requirement scaling as
\[ O\!\left( \frac{M_1^2} {\eta^4\varepsilon_\chi^2} \log\frac1\delta \right), \]
before paying for transition acquisition or energy resolution. This is a conditional upper bound, not an optimal lower bound and not an achieved speedup.
The genuinely different operation required is therefore temperature-correct response acquisition with controlled low-frequency cost. It cannot be replaced by a thermal snapshot source whose influence disappears under the computation used to read the answer.
A local update on \(N\) auxiliary slices and \(M\) spatial sites costs \(O(NM)\) arithmetic per sweep. A temporal Fourier implementation adds transform costs. Neither count includes equilibration, thermal-source generation, effective sample size, discretization, numerical stability, or verification.
