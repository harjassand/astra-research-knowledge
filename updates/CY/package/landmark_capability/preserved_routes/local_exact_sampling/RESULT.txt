RESULT: exact one-site sampling on a positive 1D chain; no novel capability

Verdict
-------
The requested general mechanism is already present in prior work. Anand and
Jerrum's 2021/2022 algorithm is explicitly outside CFTP: it recursively guesses
a spin without reading a surrounding sphere and resolves that sphere only when
the guess is inconclusive. Under strong spatial mixing (SSM) and subexponential
neighborhood growth it returns an exact finite window from the unique infinite
Gibbs measure in expected time linear in the window size, hence constant per
queried site. Feng, Guo, and Yin give a second non-CFTP approach: a local Bayes
filter repairs a configuration while preserving the conditional Gibbs law,
with linear expected time under their SSM hypotheses. These are stronger
baselines than a new backward-clan construction for the proposed target.

I therefore pivoted once to a distinct, deterministic-refinement mechanism.
It gives a self-contained exact sampler for one spin in a homogeneous
one-dimensional finite-state chain with strictly positive rational transfer
weights. The construction composes classical transfer-matrix contraction
with classical lazy inverse-CDF sampling. It is narrower than the cited
samplers, can be exponentially costly in the interaction bit length, and is
not a landmark or a novelty claim.

Model and inputs
----------------
Input is a supplied q-by-q matrix T of positive rational edge weights. Clear
denominators to make T integer; the common factor cancels from every law. On
the chain Z, a finite string x[-n..n] has free-boundary weight

    product_{i=-n}^{n-1} T[x_i,x_{i+1}].

Strict positivity makes T primitive and defines a unique infinite-volume Gibbs
measure. The target observable is X_0 (or any fixed finite-valued function of
X_0). Let 1 be the all-ones vector,

    L_n = 1^T T^n,       R_n = T^n 1,
    p_n(a) = L_n[a] R_n[a] / sum_b L_n[b] R_n[b].

This is the exact rational marginal at the center of [-n,n] with free
boundaries. If u and v are the positive left and right Perron vectors of T,
the infinite-volume marginal is p(a)=u[a]v[a]/sum_b u[b]v[b].

Certified error bound
--------------------
Write m=min(T_ij), M=max(T_ij), kappa=M/m, and rho=(kappa-1)/(kappa+1).
For kappa=1 the finite-volume marginal is already exact for n>=1. Otherwise,
Hilbert's projective metric and Birkhoff's positive-matrix contraction give
projective diameter Delta(T)<=2 log(kappa) and contraction factor at most
rho=tanh(log(kappa)/2)<1. Since L_1 and the Perron left vector are both in
the image of the positive cone under T, d_H(L_n,u)<=2 log(kappa)rho^(n-1);
the same holds for R_n and v. The log-likelihood ratio of the two central
marginals has oscillation at most the sum of those two distances. The standard
projective-to-total-variation bound therefore gives

    d_TV(p_n,p) <= log(kappa) rho^(n-1)
                  <= (kappa-1) rho^(n-1) =: eps_n.

Use eps_n capped at 1. Thus every cumulative probability F_j=sum_{a<=j}p(a)
lies in the exact rational interval

    [ max(0,F_{j,n}-eps_n), min(1,F_{j,n}+eps_n) ],

where F_{j,n} is computed from p_n.

Exact sampler
-------------
Generate a uniform U lazily from iid fair bits. After n bits, its dyadic
prefix gives an interval [U_n^-,U_n^+) of width 2^-n. Compute p_n and the
rational enclosures above. Return symbol a as soon as [U_n^-,U_n^+) is wholly
between the upper enclosure for F_{a-1} and the lower enclosure for F_a
(omit the left test for a=0 and the right test for a=q-1). Every U is then
inside the true inverse-CDF interval for a, so the output law is exactly p.
Since U equals any one of the finitely many CDF thresholds with probability
zero and eps_n -> 0, the algorithm halts almost surely.

If N is the number of generated bits/iterations, an unresolved prefix at
stage n must intersect one of q-1 CDF uncertainty intervals. Hence

    Pr[N>n] <= min(1, (q-1)(2 eps_n + 2^(1-n))).

The tail is exponential, so every moment of N is finite. With fixed q and
integer entries of at most B bits, each stage uses O(q^2) integer operations
on O(n(B+log q))-bit values; elementary schoolbook arithmetic gives total
bit work O(q^2 (B+log q)^2 N^3). Its expectation is finite and independent
of the length of the chain. The bound can be exponential in B: kappa may be
2^B, making rho close to one. This is an exact system-size-independent local
query, not a polynomial-in-input-length guarantee.

Acquisition and hidden costs
----------------------------
The sampler assumes the full interaction matrix is supplied exactly. Reading
it costs q^2 B bits (after rational denominator clearing); parameter
estimation, physical measurement, model selection, or uncertainty in T is
outside the theorem and must be charged separately. It uses an ideal stream
of iid fair bits, with expected count E[N] bounded by the tail above, plus
exact integer/rational arithmetic. No physical preparation or observation of
an interacting system is demonstrated. Floating-point arithmetic would
invalidate the exactness claim unless every comparison were replaced by a
certified interval comparison.

Falsifier / boundary
--------------------
Strict positivity and the uniquely specified infinite-volume law matter. If
they are dropped, even T=identity has multiple Gibbs measures; free-boundary
finite windows do not identify which target measure is intended. The method
also does not give useful input-size efficiency when the transfer matrix is
ill-conditioned (large M/m). For the broader SSM setting, Anand--Jerrum's
recursive sampler dominates this pivot in scope and practical query cost.
