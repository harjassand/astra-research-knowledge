Pivot note: a positive extension beyond the one-sided convolution
==================================================================

The requested broader operation is available for homogeneous (or finitely many exchangeable types of) independent two-state CTMC coordinates. It handles arbitrary independent initial probabilities and multi-state target sets, and its cost need not be the inverse target rarity in number of simulated jumps.

Construction for one homogeneous class
---------------------------------------

Let n independent coordinates each jump 0 -> 1 at rate alpha>0 and 1 -> 0 at rate beta>0. The initial bits are independent, with P(X_i(0)=1)=r_i, with no ordering assumption on r_i and p=alpha/(alpha+beta). Put K_t=sum_i X_i(t). Exchangeability of the coordinate generators makes K itself a birth-death CTMC, even when the initial r_i differ. Its rates are

  k -> k+1 at b_k=(n-k)alpha,
  k -> k-1 at d_k=k beta.

The initial count K_0 has the Poisson-binomial law w_k=[z^k] product_i((1-r_i)+r_i z), computable by a nonnegative O(n^2) recurrence.

For the multi-target set A_m={x: sum_i x_i >= m}, let H_m=inf{t>=0: X_t in A_m}. The count chain hits m before entering A_m from below, and if K_0>=m then H_m=0. On C={0,...,m-1}, form the m-by-m killed subgenerator T by restricting the birth-death generator to C, retaining its full diagonal (so the rate b_{m-1} out to the target remains in the diagonal loss). Then for every t>=0,

  P(H_m>t) = w_C exp(t T) 1,
  F_m(t) = 1 - w_C exp(t T) 1.

This is an exact phase-type law with a nonnegative initial row w_C and a Metzler subgenerator T. It covers arbitrary independent starts, the single corner target (m=n), and the whole family of threshold targets. For any target predicate depending only on K, replace C by the count levels outside that target and use the same killed-subgenerator formula.

This is more than the earlier H_r=H_p+D identity: it does not require r_i<=p, keeps the initial law in a positive Poisson-binomial mixture, and supports targets containing many configurations. The earlier identity remains a useful special convolution when its monotonicity condition holds.

Positive evaluation and sampling without rare-event paths
---------------------------------------------------------

The matrix exponential has the nonnegative uniformization expansion. Choose Omega>=max_k(-T_kk), set P=I+T/Omega (nonnegative substochastic), and write

  exp(tT)=exp(-Omega t) sum_{j>=0} ((Omega t)^j/j!) P^j.

Directly truncating this series costs O(Omega t), which is poor when the target is rare. A positive scale-and-square evaluation avoids that linear dependence: choose s with mu=Omega t/2^s<=1; approximate exp(tT/2^s) by the above Poisson series through J, with its omitted Poisson tail at most delta; then square the resulting substochastic matrix s times. Since all factors are nonnegative contractions in row-sum norm, the final error is at most 2^s delta. Taking delta<=epsilon/2^s gives a certified epsilon evaluation. Per CDF evaluation, the work is O((J+s)m^3) with dense matrix products, s=O(log(Omega t)), and J selected from a Poisson-tail bound; sparse methods may reduce the matrix cost. Use one uniform U and invert F_m(t) by bisection (return zero immediately if U<=P(K_0>=m)). The quantile search adds repeated CDF evaluations; a finite mean w_C(-T)^{-1}1 gives a Markov-inequality upper bracket.

Thus the construction avoids simulating a path through order 1/P_pi(A_m) jumps. Its cost is controlled by the lumped state dimension m and logarithmically by the time scale for scale-and-square evaluations. In the homogeneous corner-target case this is O(n)-dimensional despite stationary target mass p^n. This is a deterministic numerical sampler for the exact phase-type CDF, up to the stated CDF tolerance; it is not an exact-arithmetic sampler. Conditioning and precision still need to be reported, especially for extremely small target mass.

Several exchangeable types
--------------------------

If coordinates fall into G types, with type g containing n_g coordinates sharing rates (alpha_g,beta_g), the vector of type counts is a CTMC on product grid E=product_g {0,...,n_g}, with independent birth-death coordinate generators. Arbitrary independent initial bits induce independent Poisson-binomial initial counts. Every target set invariant under permutations within each type is a subset B of E. With C=E\B and killed subgenerator T=Q_CC,

  P(H_B>t)=mu_C exp(tT)1.

All entries are still nonnegative in the phase-type representation; the matrix dimension is M=product_g(n_g+1). The one-type threshold construction is the especially useful case M=n+1. If every coordinate has distinct rates, M=2^n, so the positivity identity still holds but the computational advantage can disappear. The honest complexity claim is therefore for exchangeable or few-type systems, not arbitrary heterogeneous products.

Limits and a simple failure outside the one-sided case
------------------------------------------------------

For one coordinate, target 1, alpha=beta=1, p=1/2, compare r=3/4 to stationarity. The hitting time from r has survival (1-r)e^{-t}=e^{-t}/4, while stationary-start hitting has survival e^{-t}/2. Thus H_r is strictly stochastically smaller than H_p for t>0. It cannot equal H_p+D for any independent nonnegative D. The positive phase-type construction above handles this case directly.

Scope / novelty note
--------------------

The killed-generator phase-type formula and uniformization are standard finite-state CTMC machinery; the useful structural step here is exact count-state lumping for product chains, combined with arbitrary Poisson-binomial starts and targets invariant under within-type permutations. Treat this as a candidate algorithmic extension, not a landmark theorem. The main measured improvement is replacing rare-event path simulation by a positive CDF computation in the lumped dimension; for fully heterogeneous coordinates that dimension can be exponential, and numerical conditioning at extreme rarity remains a real limitation.
