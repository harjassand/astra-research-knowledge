## Verdict

There is a mathematically valid positive algorithm for a sharp metastable
class: a known, state-independent rare reset clock, a small explicit mode
chain, and local kernels with exact stationary samples and finite-moment
meeting couplings. It computes an *exactly unbiased* steady-state response
without a transition-count or variance factor of `1/delta`, even though the
full chain has a slow mode with eigenvalue `1-delta` in the example. It uses
mode aggregation plus coupled *observable differences*, and has no path
likelihood products.

This is a scoped synthesis, **not an established new method or landmark**.
Stochastic complementation/aggregation, measure-valued phantom derivatives,
and coupled Poisson estimators are prior work. For N641's four-state instance,
the direct exact 4-by-4 transfer solve is stronger and simpler. A simulation
oracle that hides rare transition probabilities still has a `1/delta` lower
bound for fixed-confidence response accuracy (given below). No claim covers
arbitrary state-dependent escapes, unknown metastable partitions, or smooth
chaotic maps.

## Native contract and exact theorem

Let modes be `i=1,...,k`, with local state spaces `E_i`. The row-stochastic
transition kernel is

    P_theta((i,x),(j,dy))
      = (1-delta) 1{i=j} B_i(x,dy)
        + delta R_theta(i,j) nu_{j,theta}(dy),      0<delta<=1.

At each step a Bernoulli(`delta`) clock either runs one step of the fixed
within-mode kernel `B_i`, or chooses a new mode by `R_theta` and draws its
state afresh from `nu_{j,theta}`. A reset may select the current mode. Assume
`R_theta` is finite, differentiable, and irreducible near theta=0; `B_i` is
ergodic; `nu_{i,theta}` is differentiable as a finite signed measure; and
`|A_i(x)|<=M`. For finite spaces these conditions make the calculations
elementary. Exact entries of `R_0,R'_0` and `delta` are supplied or acquired;
they are not inferred from a short full-chain trajectory.

Let `alpha_theta R_theta=alpha_theta`, `sum_i alpha_theta(i)=1`, and define

    mu_{i,theta} = delta sum_{t>=0} (1-delta)^t nu_{i,theta} B_i^t.

Then the unique stationary law is **exactly**

    Pi_theta(i,dx) = alpha_theta(i) mu_{i,theta}(dx).

This follows by substitution: the nonreset inflow into mode `j` is
`(1-delta) alpha_j mu_j B_j`; reset inflow is
`delta sum_i alpha_i R_theta(i,j) nu_j = delta alpha_j nu_j`.
Their sum is `alpha_j mu_j`. This is a stationary factorization, not an
approximation in `delta`. Differentiating the *small* mode chain gives

    alpha' (I-R_0) = alpha R'_0,       alpha' 1 = 0,

with no division by `delta`. For fixed observable `A`, the exact response is

    J' = sum_i alpha'_i mu_i(A_i) + sum_i alpha_i mu'_i(A_i),
    mu'_i(A_i) = delta sum_{t>=0} (1-delta)^t nu'_i B_i^t A_i.

The prime on `mu` assumes only `nu_i` changes with theta; `B_i` and `delta`
are fixed. If `A` changes, add `sum_i alpha_i mu_i(A'_i)`.

Suppose the signed reset derivative has an *acquired* Hahn-Jordan sampler

    nu'_i = kappa_i (nu_i^+ - nu_i^-),        kappa_i>=0.

For each `i`, draw `X_0~nu_i` and `Y_0~pi_i`, where `pi_i B_i=pi_i` is an
exact local stationary draw. Couple both `B_i` chains faithfully until their
meeting time `tau_i^0`; after meeting, keep them identical. Independently,
couple `U_0~nu_i^+` and `V_0~nu_i^-` until `tau_i^pm`. Set `r=1-delta` and

    H_i = A_i(Y_0)
          + delta sum_{t=0}^{tau_i^0-1} r^t [A_i(X_t)-A_i(Y_t)],
    G_i = kappa_i delta
          sum_{t=0}^{tau_i^pm-1} r^t [A_i(U_t)-A_i(V_t)],
    Z = sum_i alpha'_i H_i + sum_i alpha_i G_i.

If the two meeting times have finite first moments, `E Z=J'` exactly. The
proof is term-by-term expectation: after meeting every summand is identically
zero, and the marginals give `nu_i B_i^t A_i-pi_i A_i` and
`nu_i^+ B_i^t A_i-nu_i^- B_i^t A_i`. The first correction plus
`E A_i(Y_0)=pi_i A_i` is the resolvent formula for `mu_i A_i`. The second
is its derivative. Dominated convergence follows from
`|sum_{t<tau} delta r^t difference|<=2M delta tau`.

With finite second moments and `R_0,R'_0,kappa_i` and the local coupling
moment bounds held fixed as `delta->0`, the estimator has finite variance
uniformly in that limit; explicitly

    |H_i| <= M + 2M delta tau_i^0,
    |G_i| <= 2M kappa_i delta tau_i^pm.

If `pi_i(A_i)` is available exactly, replace `A_i(Y_0)` by that constant;
then the standard deviation of the *whole* estimator is at most

    2M delta [sum_i |alpha'_i| sqrt(E(tau_i^0)^2)
               + sum_i alpha_i kappa_i sqrt(E(tau_i^pm)^2)].

The expected work per copy is one exact mode solve, the cost of exact local
stationary draws, and `sum_i E(tau_i^0+tau_i^pm)` coupled pair updates;
there is no `1/delta` term. Expected work, not worst-case work, is bounded.
For exact rational inputs, discount arithmetic has bit cost polynomial in
the sampled meeting time and input bit length, including `log(1/delta)`.
Without exact local stationary draws, an unbiased local stationary estimator
is needed and must be charged. Without a usable `nu'_i` sampler, the claimed
gradient algorithm is unavailable.

The full chain can nevertheless have mixing time of order `1/delta`:
on block-constant functions its transition is `(1-delta)I+delta R_0`.
For the example `R_0` has nontrivial eigenvalue zero, so `1-delta` is an
*exact eigenvalue* of the full chain. The cancellation is structural: the
rare clock affects residence times equally, so mode stationary weights are
those of `R_0`; all remaining corrections concern fast local kernels.

## Independently checked four-state test

Use N641's within-block matrix in row convention

    B = [[9/10,1/10],[2/5,3/5]],   pi_B=(4/5,1/5),

two modes, `R_theta` with both rows
`((1-theta)/2,(1+theta)/2)`, and reset laws
`nu_0=(1/2,1/2)`, `nu_{1,theta}=(1/2-beta theta,1/2+beta theta)`.
Let `A=(0,1,0,2)`, which varies *within* each mode. At `beta=1/4`,

    J' = (1/10 + 9 delta/10)/(1+delta).

The local conditional probability of state 1 is
`mu_delta({1})=(1/5+4delta/5)/(1+delta)`. The mode derivative contributes
`mu_delta({1})/2`; the reset-law derivative contributes
`delta/[2(1+delta)]`. Both are exact for every `0<delta<=1` in the stated
parameter neighborhood.

A maximal coupling of two unequal `B` states coalesces next step with
probability `1/2`. For the base pair `X_0~nu,Y_0~pi_B`, its expected pair
updates are 1; for the plus/minus derivative pair starting at states 1 and 0,
they are 2. Thus the complete response draw uses exactly 3 pair updates in
expectation (6 single-chain updates), independent of `delta`. A direct
geometric-age draw from `mu_delta` uses `(1-delta)/delta` local transitions
per chain in expectation. This comparison is to a natural direct trajectory
baseline; exact full transfer and local perfect sampling remain stronger
comparators.

The variance calculation is independently auditable without Monte Carlo.
For a pair that starts unequal, `T~Geom(1/2)` and its discounted difference
has magnitude `H=1-(1-delta)^T`. The base pair starts with signs `+1,-1,0`
with probabilities `2/5,1/10,1/2`; the derivative pair has sign `+1`.
These pairs are independent, and the output is
`1/10 + (base difference)/2 + beta*(derivative difference)`. With
`r=1-delta`, `E H=1-r/(2-r)` and
`E H^2=1-2r/(2-r)+r^2/(2-r^2)`, which yield every displayed exact mean
and variance.

`verify.py` constructs the full four-state `P_0,P'_0` as exact Fractions,
solves both stationary and derivative linear equations, proves exact equality
with the coupled-estimator expectation for four deltas, and computes its
variance from the geometric coupling law. Selected outputs:

    delta       exact response    E pair updates  E direct age   Var(Z)
    1/100       109/1010          3               99             7.55186955e-5
    1/1000      1009/10010        3               999            7.81939081e-7
    1/10000     10009/100010      3               9999           7.84693091e-9

For `delta=1/1000`, 100,000 exact-rational sampled outputs averaged

The code's pseudorandom generator is an implementation surrogate for the

## Large-state operational class and limits

For each mode let `E_i={0,1}^d` and let `B_i` refresh a uniformly chosen
coordinate to an independent fair bit. Its stationary distribution is the
uniform product law, sampled exactly with `d` fair bits. Couple two copies
with the same coordinate and new bit; they meet once every initially
different coordinate has been refreshed. A coupon-collector bound gives
`E tau=O(d(1+log d))` and `E tau^2=O(d^2(1+log d)^2)`, independent
of `delta`.
Take easy reset samplers such as a mixture of the all-zero and all-one
vectors with an explicit signed derivative. For *any* bounded black-box
observable on the `k*2^d` states, the theorem yields exact unbiased response
with `O(k d log d)` expected local transition/observable calls after a
`k`-mode solve; arbitrary observable evaluation cost is charged separately.
Exact rational arithmetic also costs polynomially in `d`, `log(1/delta)`,
and the random meeting time. This demonstrates a real large-state regime
where full matrix acquisition is exponential, though local perfect-sampling
methods may achieve similar scaling and must be benchmarked before any
algorithmic-priority claim.

## Stronger state-carrying extension: finite stationary ancestry

Fresh local state on a mode reset is sufficient for the simple product
factorization above, but is not necessary for all `delta`-independent
algorithms. Consider instead

    P_theta((i,x),(j,dy))
      = (1-delta)1{i=j} B_i(x,dy)
        + delta R_theta(i,j) C_ij(x,dy),

where a mode switch can carry and transform the old local state through
`C_ij`. Assume each fixed `B_i` has a *known* state-independent atom with
common strength `eta>0`:

    B_i(x,dy) = eta nu_i(dy) + (1-eta) Btilde_i(x,dy).

Assume `R_theta` is positive with exactly acquired entries and derivatives;
`C_ij,Btilde_i,nu_i,eta,delta` are theta-independent. The mode marginal is
still `alpha_theta`, the stationary law of `R_theta`, although the local
conditional laws no longer factor as the simple `mu_i` above.

There is an exact finite-ancestry sampler for the full stationary state.
Sample the present mode `j~alpha_theta`. Moving backward one step at a
time, select a no-reset event with probability `1-delta`, in which case the
previous mode is `j`; otherwise select a reset event with probability
`delta` and previous mode `i` from the *acquired reversed mode kernel*

    Q_theta(j,i) = alpha_theta(i) R_theta(i,j)/alpha_theta(j).

For a no-reset event, reveal its `B_i` atom coin. Stop at the first atom,
draw its output from `nu_i`, and then run the stored residual `Btilde` and
state-carrying `C` kernels forward to the present. This reconstructs the
stationary law exactly: `alpha_i R_ij/alpha_j` is the true backward reset
probability, and the atom makes all earlier local histories coalesce.
The number `L` of examined backward transitions is geometric with success
probability `(1-delta)eta`, so
`E L = 1/[(1-delta)eta]`. It is bounded as `delta->0`.

Differentiating this *finite random construction* gives an exactly unbiased
response estimator for any bounded fixed observable:

    Z_ancestor = A(M_0,X_0) [alpha'_0(M_0)/alpha_0(M_0)
                   + sum_{backward reset events j<-i} Q'_0(j,i)/Q_0(j,i)].

Only the present-mode draw and backward reset choices depend on theta;
the atom clock and forward local kernels do not. On any parameter
neighborhood where the displayed scores are bounded, `|Z_ancestor|<=
M C(1+L)`, so its variance and expected work are bounded uniformly for
`0<delta<=delta_max<1`. This uses a finite score over the relevant
ancestry, never products of long-horizon path likelihoods. It is a local
regenerative perfect-sampling construction; general CFTP and phantom
methods remain the relevant prior art. If the atom strength `eta` is tiny
or unknown, the work guarantee is lost or must charge its acquisition.

`ancestor_verify.py` checks a nontrivial four-state state-carrying instance.
The same `B` above decomposes exactly as
`B=(1/2)(4/5,1/5)+(1/2)I`, so `eta=1/2`. A mode-switch event sets the
local bit to the destination mode; a reset event retaining the mode carries
the bit unchanged. With both rows of `R_theta` equal to
`((1-theta)/2,(1+theta)/2)` and `A=(0,1,0,2)`, the full rational
stationary/derivative solve gives `J'=1/10+9 delta^2/10`. The ancestor
sampler uses `E L=2/(1-delta)` steps and
`E reset events=2delta/(1-delta)`, exactly. At `delta=1/1000`, the
response is `1000009/10000000`, `E L=2000/999`; 300,000 sampled scores
averaged `0.09998667` with standard error `0.001279`, consistent with
the exact `0.10000090`. This Monte Carlo check is deliberately weak
compared with the exact algebraic identity; it only tests the implementation.

The assumptions are essential. If reset probability depends on local state,
the simple product stationary factorization fails. If `R_theta` itself mixes
slowly, its small solve and derivative can be ill-conditioned. If local
coupling has long tails, work or variance can diverge. If the rare mode
transition law is hidden behind only a simulator, it cannot in general be
acquired in constant cost: consider two-state chains with
`P_theta(0,1)=delta exp(theta)` and `P_theta(1,0)=b delta`, with hidden
`b in {1,2}`. For the state-1 observable, the stationary response at zero is
`b/(1+b)^2`, namely `1/4` or `2/9`, a gap of `1/36`. In `n` oracle samples,
the two transcript laws have total variation at most `n delta`, since each
query from state 1 can be coupled to disagree with probability `delta` and
all other row/derivative queries agree. Achieving fixed-confidence accuracy
better than half the response gap therefore needs `Omega(1/delta)` oracle
samples. Explicit `R,R'` or an equivalent rare-event interface is the paid
source of the positive algorithm's speed.

## Strong baselines and novelty check

* Exact transfer solve: form the full finite transition matrix and solve
  `(I-P^T)pi'=P'^T pi` with normalization. It dominates for N641's four
  states; `verify.py` uses it as the decisive comparator. Certified transfer
  response computation for suitable expanding maps is established in
  Bahsoun et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.08661 .
* Stochastic complementation and aggregation of nearly reducible Markov
  chains are established: Meyer, *SIAM Review* 31 (1989), 240-272,
  https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1031050 . The exact factorization
  here is a particularly simple reset specialization.
* Measure-valued differentiation already uses plus/minus phantom chains,
  regeneration, and coupling for stationary gradients: Heidergott, Hordijk,
  Weisshaupt, *Mathematics of Operations Research* 31 (2006), 154-172,
  https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.1050.0171 . The local
  `G_i` estimator is a specialized phantom construction.
* Coupled unbiased Poisson estimators and their meeting-time conditions
  are established: Douc, Jacob, Lee, Vats, *Annals of Statistics* 54 (2026),
  201-225, https://doi.org/10.1214/25-AOS2564 . The local `H_i` is a
  discounted coupled-difference identity in that spirit.
* Exact stationary sampling through coupling from the past is established:
  Propp and Wilson, *Random Structures & Algorithms* 9 (1996), 223-252,
  https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/206-RWG/RWGpapers/propp_wilson.pdf .
  It is a serious comparator wherever local perfect sampling is easy.

No source located certifies the exact combined class theorem or the concrete
four-state extension as historically new; no exhaustive priority search was
done. A next research gate would be a state-dependent escape model with a
fully acquired fast interface and a matched test against stochastic
complementation, weak derivatives, local perfect sampling, and exact
transfer. That gate is open, not a claimed result.
