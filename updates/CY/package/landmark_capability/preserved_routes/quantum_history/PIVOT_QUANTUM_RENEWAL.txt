SPARSE MONITORED QUANTUM FIRST DETECTION: CONSTRUCTIVE PIVOT
10 October 2026

Status: a correct scoped exact algorithm for the explicit family below,
finite-checked against direct state evolution. The matrix renewal mechanism
extends established quantum first-detection equations; originality and a
win over the strongest tensor/MPS baseline are NOT established. This does
not satisfy the requested broad-simulation landmark gate.

MODEL AND ACQUISITION
The input is n, a starting n-bit computational basis string x, a set A of k
listed n-bit target strings, and a horizon T. All input bytes, including kn
target bits, count. The free one-step unitary is

  U = u tensor ... tensor u,      u=(3 I-4 i X)/5.

Each u has Gaussian-rational entries of O(1) bits. After each application of
U, perform the binary projective test {P,Q}, where

  P=sum_{a in A}|a><a|,          Q=I-P.

On a hit, optionally measure the position within A and stop. On a miss,
continue coherently in Q's subspace. The detector is not an exponentially
large supplied matrix: comparison with each listed target, OR of the k
comparison flags, a measured flag, and uncomputation implement the test with
poly(n,k) local reversible gates and ancillas. We have not optimized that
gate count. The simulation result is a list of the exact first-detection
probabilities p_t, 1<=t<=T. A physical implementation would also pay T
rotation layers and tests; the classical algorithm uses their succinct model.

NEW OPERATION FOR THIS FAMILY
Let a_t,b_t in Q(i) be defined by u^t=a_t I+b_t X, initialized at
(a_0,b_0)=(1,0) and updated by

  a_{t+1}=(3 a_t-4 i b_t)/5,
  b_{t+1}=(-4 i a_t+3 b_t)/5.

For strings y,z at Hamming distance h,

  W_t(y,z)=<y|U^t|z>=a_t^(n-h) b_t^h.

This free propagator can be acquired exactly from n,T and the two strings;
no 2^n state or dense Kraus operator is involved. Define the k-component
first-hit amplitude vector

  f_t = P U (Q U)^(t-1)|x>,

written in the target basis. The observed first-hit probability is
p_t=sum_{a in A}|f_t(a)|^2. Define g_t(a)=W_t(a,x) and the k-by-k
free boundary kernel H_t(a,b)=W_t(a,b). A path decomposition by its first
visit to P yields the exact matrix renewal equation

  f_t = g_t - sum_{s=1}^{t-1} H_{t-s} f_s.                 (1)

Proof: insert I=P+Q after each free step and group the unmonitored
amplitude U^t|x> by the first time P occurs. This gives
U^t|x>=(QU)^t|x>+sum_{s=1}^t U^(t-s) f_s. Multiplication by P kills the
first term and gives g_t=f_t+sum_{s<t}H_{t-s}f_s. All amplitudes can
interfere, and equation (1) retains those phases exactly. It is not a
classical path-probability renewal equation.

After the O(k^2 n) input-distance calculations, precompute all a_t,b_t and
the needed W_t(h) for 1<=t<=T, 0<=h<=n in O(nT) Gaussian-rational
arithmetic operations. Equation (1) then costs O(k^2 T^2) operations and
O(kT+nT+k^2) stored scalars (less is possible). The unoptimized script
implements this recurrence with exact Python Fractions. All f_t components
are entries of P U(QU)^(t-1)|x>, hence have denominator dividing 5^(nt)
and numerators of O(nt) bits. p_t and their cumulative sum have O(nT)-bit
rational encodings. The result is polynomial bit complexity in the *local*
description n,k,T, not merely in d=2^n.

MATCHED EXPERIMENT AND BASELINE GATE
The script sparse_detector_renewal.py checks n=3,4,6 examples, with k=2
or 3 and T=5, against exact direct evolution of all 2^n amplitudes after
each measured step. Every first-hit amplitude and probability agrees.
Saved records: sparse_detector_renewal_result.json. Run the same script with
--benchmark for the larger case; its record is
sparse_detector_benchmark_result.json. A 100-qubit, three-target, T=20 exact
run with three targets all at Hamming distance 50 from the initial zero
string took about 0.08 seconds in one local run; the cumulative
event probability was approximately 10^-29.64 and its exact numerator and
denominator had 9,190 and 9,288 bits. Runtime is an implementation
observation, not a portable performance theorem or a matched MPS benchmark.

For Bernoulli trajectory sampling without importance tilting, relative-
error estimation of an event of probability p needs Omega(1/p) samples.
This instance has p around 10^-30, so straightforward trajectories fail
badly. The full statevector method uses Theta(2^n) amplitudes. But the exact
surviving state is a sum of at most 1+kT product states: use the same
first-hit decomposition, then U^(t-s)|a> is a product state. Consequently an
exact MPS exists with bond at most 1+kT across every cut. A capable MPS/TN
implementation is therefore also polynomial. Its constant and polynomial
factors were not benchmarked here, and an optimized tensor contraction can
implement essentially the same boundary recurrence. This candidate does
NOT prove an advantage over the strongest tensor/MPS method.

RELATION TO N636 AND PRIOR WORK
Upon detection of a specific a, the hit operator |a><a|U has rank one.
The no-hit branch QU remains high rank; N636's every-history rank-collapse
condition is not met during the useful dynamics. The gain comes from
restricting a *query-specific causal boundary* to k target amplitudes while
using an efficiently acquired free propagator. It is a real operational
pivot away from emitting a terminal channel's positive Kraus list.

The quantum renewal equation for a single detection site was derived by
Friedman, Kessler and Barkai in 2016/2017:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02046 . Multisite/matrix renewal is the natural
projector version, so equation (1) alone is not a novelty claim. Quantum
Markov-chain reachability and resolvent methods were developed earlier by
Feng, Yu and Ying and colleagues:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2187 and https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0060 .
General MPS efficient simulation for bounded entanglement is Vidal's prior
result: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0301063 .

FURTHER GATE
A broader useful result would need a nontrivial free kernel family whose
matrix elements can be *acquired* from a local description with certified
cost/error, plus sparse monitored targets or interventions. It must then
beat optimized tensor/MPS/trajectory methods on the same explicit inputs,
including basis changes, detector compilation, accuracy and repeated-query
amortization. Replacing W by an unexplained oracle, hiding exponential
Fourier sums, or relying on rare-event Monte Carlo as the only comparator
would fail this gate. No such broader family was established in this task.
