SPARSE NON-GAUSSIAN DETECTION IN A FREE-FERMION CIRCUIT
10 October 2026

Status: internally derived scoped exact algorithm; finite exact checks pass.
Its components (free-fermion determinant kernels and quantum first-detection
renewal) are established prior mathematics. Novelty of their combination,
performance against the strongest tensor/trajectory/Slater-mixture methods,
and broad applicability are UNKNOWN. This is a constructive candidate, not
a landmark-simulation claim.

INPUT AND QUERY
There are n fermionic modes, m particles, a starting occupation set
S_0 subset [n] with |S_0|=m, a set A={S_1,...,S_k} of k listed occupation
sets of the same size, a horizon T, and an explicit sequence of G local
number-conserving two-mode Gaussian-rational gates defining one free step
U=Gamma(V). Here V is the acquired n-by-n one-particle unitary, computed
from those G gates in polynomial time. Nearest-neighbor mode rotations are
ordinary local matchgates in the qubit encoding. Each subsequent step is
followed by the two-outcome test {P_A,I-P_A}, with
P_A=sum_{a=1}^k |S_a><S_a|. A reversible equality circuit over the n
occupation bits and k supplied target strings implements this projector
with poly(n,k) local gates/ancillas; this acquisition is part of the input
cost. On a hit the particular target may also be read, and evolution ends.

The requested output is the exact probability p_t of first detection at
each t<=T. This is a coherent monitored evolution: a no-hit event projects
onto the complement of the k listed many-body configurations. That
coarse-grained projector generally destroys Gaussian/Slater closure. No
entire-channel Kraus compression or full Fock-state representation is
supplied.

EXACT ALGORITHM AND PROOF
Let V^t be the one-particle t-step matrix. For two m-particle occupation
sets S,R, the unmonitored transition amplitude is the Slater minor

  W_t(S,R)=<S|Gamma(V)^t|R>=det((V^t)_{S,R}).

Acquire V from the local gate list, calculate V^t for t=1..T, then evaluate
only W_t on pairs (S_a,S_b) and (S_a,S_0). Define

  g_t(a)=W_t(S_a,S_0),    H_t(a,b)=W_t(S_a,S_b).

The k-vector f_t of first-hit amplitudes satisfies

  f_t = g_t - sum_{s=1}^{t-1} H_{t-s} f_s,               (1)
  p_t = sum_{a=1}^k |f_t(a)|^2.

Equation (1) follows by inserting P_A+(I-P_A) after each free step and
grouping the free amplitude by its first P_A visit. It is identical to the
operator proof in PIVOT_QUANTUM_RENEWAL.txt and retains the complex phases
that a classical first-passage probability recurrence would lose. This
works despite the non-Gaussian no-hit branch because the recursion needs
only unmonitored matrix elements between the listed configurations.

Naive exact arithmetic costs O(G n + T n^3 + k^2 T m^3 + k^2 T^2)
field operations, up to straightforward matrix multiplication conventions.
The local rational gate bit size B, n,G,T,m,k are explicit parameters.
Products, minors, and the physical first-hit amplitudes have polynomial
bit length in those parameters (one can clear gate denominators per local
layer). Thus the method is polynomial in n for fixed polynomial k,T and
G, even though the m-particle Hilbert sector has binom(n,m) states. It
does not cover interacting evolution unless its unmonitored transition
kernels are also acquired at the stated cost.

CHECKS AND MATCHED BASELINES
fermion_boundary_renewal.py builds V from rational local rotations with
entries 3/5 and 4/5, computes minors and recurrence with Fraction, and
checks all first-hit amplitudes/probabilities against direct evolution on
the exterior-power Fock basis for n=4,m=2 and n=6,m=3. Records:
fermion_boundary_renewal_result.json. Its larger n=16,m=8,k=2,T=6
run used 60 local two-mode rotations per step; the Fock sector contains
12,870 basis states, while the boundary has 2. The exact boundary run took
about 0.11 seconds locally. No optimized Fock/MPS/TN baseline was run for
this case. Record: fermion_boundary_benchmark_result.json.

The same script's --entanglement-check mode uses n=8,m=4 and four
forward/reverse sweeps of rational local rotations. It verifies that all
70 balanced 4-row minors of the occupied-orbital matrix W are nonzero.
For any balanced cut A|A^c, both W_A and W_Ac are invertible, so all four
singular values of W_A lie strictly between 0 and 1. The Slater state has
Schmidt rank 2^4=16 across *every* balanced cut. Hence every exact MPS
ordering needs bond at least 16 at its middle cut for this finite fixture.
Record: fermion_entanglement_check_result.json. This finite observation
does not establish an all-size lower bound, an approximation lower bound,
or a lower bound for general tensor contraction; free-fermion Gaussian
simulators can already bypass MPS entanglement for unmonitored dynamics.

A generic full-spark occupied-orbital matrix would give Schmidt rank
2^(n/2) across every balanced cut, suggesting an asymptotic exact-MPS
separation. A constructive all-size rational local family with checked
input/gate/bit bounds and a matched approximate-MPS statement was not
proved here. More importantly, a simulator that maintains a sum of O(kT)
Slater determinants can also remain polynomial: applying the first-hit
decomposition expresses the surviving state as one unmonitored Slater state
minus at most kT further unmonitored Slater states. Such a baseline uses
the same sparse-defect structure. The proposed operation may save the cost
of maintaining those full states for a narrow first-hit query, but no
decisive advantage over the strongest representation-aware baseline has
been established.

INTERFACE AND NOVELTY BOUNDARY
This is not an application of N636's all-history rank-one theorem. The
specific hit branches have rank one; the repeatedly surviving no-hit
branch stays high rank. The new useful interface is a sparse many-body
detector boundary plus efficiently acquired free kernels. It answers a
multitime monitored first-hit question that terminal forgotten-outcome
compression cannot answer.

Terhal and DiVincenzo (2002) established efficient simulation of
noninteracting fermionic circuits and several measured/feed-forward
extensions: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0108010 . Jozsa and Miyake
(2008) established nearest-neighbor matchgate simulation:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.4050 . Friedman, Kessler and Barkai
(2016/2017) derived the quantum first-detection renewal equation:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02046 . These are direct antecedents; a
bounded search found no priority clearance for the present sparse
many-body-detector combination. The exact algorithmic statement is a
scoped reconstruction from those ingredients, not a historic discovery.

NEXT PASS GATE
For a stronger claim, produce an explicit all-size locally generated V
and targets A for which the first-hit query remains informative, prove
polynomial acquisition and error, and compare against an optimized
Gaussian/Slater-mixture implementation as well as tensor contraction,
MPS, and importance-sampled trajectories. Merely comparing with a dense
Fock vector or an un-tilted rare-event Monte Carlo estimate would be
insufficient. No such matched performance win is claimed here.
