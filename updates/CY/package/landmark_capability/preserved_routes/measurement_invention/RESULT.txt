MEASUREMENT INVENTION SCREEN — LOW-BANDWIDTH STOCHASTIC-PUMP READOUT
Date: 2026-10-10
Status: independently derived standard identity; synthetic finite-state computation only; no physical acquisition or novelty clearance.

DECISION

The generic idea “use nonlinear response to see hidden dynamics without resolving trajectories” is already a mature measurement family. A first-principles mixed-response identity is useful, but it does not establish a new capability. I made one pivot to a different interface: drive two calibrated physical parameters around a closed loop and read only the net integrated current through one edge. This works in an exact three-state model and has a clean one-knob null control. The geometric-pumping identity and its null theorem are also established prior art, so this is a well-specified experiment concept, not a novel measurement principle or a transformative capability.

TASK CONTRACT

Model: a finite irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on a three-state ring, with rates satisfying local detailed balance at every fixed control setting. Two externally controlled parameters are a site energy E1 and one symmetric edge barrier B01. A stationary cycle current is zero at every frozen parameter value.

Input: repeat the closed protocol (E1,B01)=(a cos(2 pi t/T), b sin(2 pi t/T)) in both orientations, then repeat matched protocols in which only E1 or only B01 varies. The energy and barrier controls, period, temperature scale, and edge-count transduction must be calibrated.

Acquired output: one scalar per protocol block, the signed number of 0-to-1 minus 1-to-0 crossings integrated over complete cycles. The proposed measurement does not record internal states or transition times. It does still require an observable current/counting channel associated with that edge.

Cost: control and readout require repeated cycles, periodic-state preparation, current calibration, and uncertainty accounting. The numerical example below requires roughly 14,400 cycles in each orientation for a 5-sigma Gaussian planning estimate under the model's intrinsic counting noise alone. This is not an acquisition-complete guarantee; detector noise, drift, initialization, and biological/material variability are omitted.

EXACT IDENTITY

Use column probabilities p and a generator Q(lambda) with Q pi=0 and 1^T pi=1. Assume Q(lambda) is smooth, irreducible, and has a uniform spectral gap along a smooth closed parameter loop lambda(s), s in [0,1]. Let Q# be its group inverse on the zero-mass subspace:

  Q Q# = Q# Q = I - pi 1^T,   Q# pi = 0,   1^T Q# = 0.

Let j_e(lambda)^T p be the signed instantaneous flux across the chosen oriented edge e. Detailed balance gives j_e(lambda)^T pi(lambda)=0. Drive lambda at physical time t=T s and observe the periodic steady regime. The adiabatic expansion is

  p_T(t) = pi(lambda(s)) + T^(-1) Q#(lambda(s)) d_s pi(lambda(s)) + O(T^(-2)).

Therefore

  lim_{T -> infinity} integral_0^T j_e(lambda(t))^T p_T(t) dt
      = integral_Gamma A_e(lambda) . d lambda,
  A_e,a(lambda) = j_e(lambda)^T Q#(lambda) partial_a pi(lambda).

This is an exact asymptotic identity under the stated smoothness and uniform-gap conditions. For a small oriented parameter loop in coordinates (lambda_a,lambda_b), the leading current is its signed area times the curvature partial_a A_b - partial_b A_a, up to higher loop-area terms. Reversing the loop reverses the leading geometric contribution. The convergence error is O(1/T) under the stated assumptions; at finite T the exact periodic master equation, not just the geometric approximation, should be used.

For thermally activated rates k(i->j)=exp(E_i-B_ij) with beta=1, the no-pumping theorem gives an exact control: in the periodic steady state, changing site energies alone with fixed barriers produces zero net integrated edge current; changing barriers alone with fixed site energies also produces zero. Simultaneously cycling an energy and a barrier can produce a nonzero current while every frozen generator remains detailed-balanced.

Proof sketch: write p=pi+delta p in dp/dt=Qp. At leading order, d pi/dt=Q delta p; the normalization constraint makes delta p=Q# d pi/dt. The equilibrium current term vanishes, so integrate j_e^T Q# d pi/dt over one cycle and change variables from t to lambda. The next term is O(1/T) after integration if the gap and derivatives are uniformly bounded.

NUMERICAL DISCRIMINATOR

The runnable model uses three states and both directions on each ring edge, with

  k(i->j) = exp(E_i - B_ij),   E=(0, 0.7 cos(theta), 0),
  B=(0.7 sin(theta), 0, 0),   theta = +/- 2 pi t/T,   T=15.

All energies are in units of k_B T and the attempt rate is set to one. The script integrates the periodic master equation and the first two derivatives of the tilted generator for the edge count. It does not generate or analyze state trajectories. The one-cycle periodic-state residual is below 4e-16 in L1.

Observed synthetic results:

  forward loop mean net count:       -0.05255625 per cycle
  reversed loop mean net count:      0.05394724 per cycle
  orientation difference:           -0.10650349 per cycle
  even leakage (mean of both):        0.00069550 per cycle
  energy-only null:                  1.0e-15 per cycle
  barrier-only null:                 2.8e-14 per cycle

The orientation-even leakage is about 1.3% of the orientation-odd half-contrast at this finite period. The exact finite-period model predicts it; a raw forward-minus-reverse contrast should not be interpreted as a pure adiabatic curvature without checking this correction.

The count variance for one cycle is 3.37 (forward) and 3.38 (reverse). A tilted-generator calculation over 25 consecutive cycles gives variance rates 3.254 and 3.265 per cycle, retaining covariance within those blocks. Using these block variance rates, the Gaussian planning formula

  M >= 25 (v_forward + v_reverse) / (mu_forward - mu_reverse)^2

gives M about 14,368 cycles per orientation, about 28,736 total cycles, for a nominal 5-sigma difference of sample means. This estimate is conditional on the simulated Markov model, long-run variance being close to the 25-cycle estimate, and no additional sensor noise. It is not a finite-sample confidence theorem. The count mean is modest compared with the intrinsic count noise, so a single molecule/device would need a long acquisition; parallel identical systems could reduce wall-clock time only by adding hardware and calibration cost.

The cycle frequency is 1/T=0.0667 per time unit and the largest total departure rate on the loop is 4.44 per time unit. Thus the drive is roughly 10.6 times slower than even the fastest local departure rate. The proposed output is cycle-integrated current, so event timestamps are not needed. A detector still needs stable current integration and protocol synchronization; its noise and transfer function are not modeled here.

CONTROLS, CALIBRATION, AND FAILURE MODES

The one-knob cycles are physically motivated nulls from the no-pumping theorem. Randomized/interleaved forward and reverse blocks cancel additive offset and slow drift to first order. The sign of the current transduction must be calibrated; absolute curvature additionally needs gain calibration. The field-to-energy and field-to-barrier couplings must be independently characterized, because unknown cross-coupling can create a false two-parameter loop. A hidden-state ensemble that violates local detailed balance, changes parameters during the run, or fails to reach periodic steady state can invalidate the stated theorem or the null control. The count-statistics calculation excludes amplifier noise, missed charge, state-preparation cost, heterogeneous subpopulations, and parameter uncertainty.

PRIOR ART AND NOVELTY SCREEN

1. The first idea, a generic second-order response that extracts coarse-grained hidden dynamics, overlaps directly with M. Muller, U. Basu, P. Sollich, and M. Kruger, “Coarse-grained second-order response theory,” Phys. Rev. Research 2, 043123 (2020), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043123. They derive second-order response of coarse-grained observables and an extrapolation from linear-response measurements. This is direct mathematical overlap.
2. T. Holsten and M. Kruger, “Thermodynamic nonlinear response relation,” Phys. Rev. E 103, 032116 (2021), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.103.032116, relate arbitrary-order response to lower-order entropy-production correlations and study a four-state process coarse-grained to a non-Markovian two-state process.
3. A. Sazhin et al., “Observation of nonlinear response and Onsager regression in a photon Bose-Einstein condensate,” Nat. Commun. 15, 4730 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-49064-9, experimentally measure the condensate response to a reservoir perturbation and observe nonlinear relaxation.
4. M. Sifft et al., “Revealing Hidden States in Quantum Dot Array Dynamics: Quantum Polyspectra Versus Waiting Time Analysis,” arXiv:2412.14893 (2024; version dated March 2026), report an experimental hidden third-state inference from higher-order statistics of a low-SNR charge-detector current without identifying quantum jumps. This already challenges a broad “hidden state without trajectories” capability claim.
5. The pivoted stochastic-pump identity is established by S. Rahav, J. Horowitz, and C. Jarzynski, “Directed Flow in Nonadiabatic Stochastic Pumps,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 101, 140602 (2008), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.140602. It gives an integrated-flow formula, a no-pumping theorem, and the adiabatic geometric current. N. A. Sinitsyn and I. Nemenman, Phys. Rev. Lett. 99, 220408 (2007), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.220408, give the geometric stochastic-pump framework. K. Takahashi et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 150602 (2020), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.150602, treat exact nonadiabatic pumping. Experiments on mechanically interlocked molecular rotors had demonstrated stimulus-driven directional motion earlier (D. A. Leigh et al., Nature 424, 174–179 (2003), DOI 10.1038/nature01758).

The only defensible contribution here is a transparent acquisition contract and a reproducible low-bandwidth numerical example with explicit nulls and a shot-noise estimate. The identity, no-pumping controls, and basic measurement concept are established. No claim of priority, external validation, hardware feasibility, or new physical capability is made.
