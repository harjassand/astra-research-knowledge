PRESERVED SCALAR FEEDBACK BASELINE, NOT A NEW CAPABILITY

For u'(t)=Au(t)+F(ell(u(t)))b, u(0)=0, with A a Kronecker sum,
b a product vector, and ell a product observable, m=ell(u) obeys the
classical scalar input-output Volterra equation

 m(t)=int_0^t K(t-s)F(m(s))ds,  K(t)=ell(exp(tA)b).

The product kernel is cheap to evaluate and, for bounded short time,
Taylor coefficients of m can be computed by scalar recurrences with
rigorous geometric truncation. Even if the exact u(t) has maximal
bipartite matrix rank, its integral representation can be cheaply
approximated by a short sum of product vectors, so maximal exact rank
does not establish a genuine separation from tensor methods. This is
the established Lur'e/Volterra reduction and is not advanced here as
a distinct computational operation.
