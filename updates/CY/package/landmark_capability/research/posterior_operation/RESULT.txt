ADAPTIVE SPARSE POISSON-SENSOR POSTERIOR OPERATION
2026-10-10. Constructive internal candidate; no priority claim or publication.

The operation
-------------
An n-bit hidden configuration X evolves as independent, controlled two-state
continuous-time Markov coordinates. During one experiment, a detector clicks
at rate v_b>0 only while X=b for one of m EXPLICIT listed configurations
B={b_1,...,b_m}. It disables after its first click. The click does not
change X, which continues to evolve freely to the experiment's terminal
time T. The scientist observes only CLICK or NO CLICK, and may choose the
next detector and coordinate rates from a finite explicit menu after each
observation. All rates, times, initial product Bernoulli parameters, and
menu entries are binary rationals. The latent posterior has 2^n entries.

For each candidate experiment, the algorithm acquires signed rational
product mixtures for BOTH unnormalized posterior branches, and from them
chooses an experiment maximizing the expected accuracy of predicting a
specified terminal hidden bit. The per-choice regret can be made at most
epsilon, conditional on the observed history, in time polynomial in
input bits, n, m, menu size, history length K, log(1/epsilon), and
log(1/w), where w>0 is the probability of that history. The latter is
an explicit rare-evidence cost; it can be ACQUIRED by precision doubling
and replay of the actual history, not supplied as an ideal posterior or
oracle. The algorithm forms no 2^n-state object and has no 1/w loop.

The operation is myopic Bayes experiment selection for a concrete loss;
it does not solve arbitrary multistep planning. Its novelty relative to
ordinary conjugacy or particle filtering is the aggregate first-click
boundary measure: detector likelihoods are not assumed to preserve a
product family, and the correction generated by ALL old signed product
components is compressed into one age interpolation per detector state.
Rank grows additively along an adaptive observation path. A general
prior-art novelty comparison remains open.

Algebra of the two observation branches
---------------------------------------
For one segment let Q be the unrestricted product generator, P_t=e^{tQ},
V=diag(v_x), where v_x=v_b for x=b in B and zero otherwise, and
M_t=e^{t(Q-V)}. For any signed initial row measure zeta,

    zeta^0_T = zeta M_T,
    zeta^1_T = zeta P_T-zeta M_T.                         (1)

These are the NO CLICK and CLICK terminal-state measures. For a positive
zeta, both are nonnegative and their sum is the free terminal law. Let

    dG_b^zeta(s)=v_b [zeta M_s](b)ds                     (2)

be the signed first-click measure by detector state. Duhamel's formula is

    zeta M_T = zeta P_T-
       sum_b int_0^T delta_b P_(T-s) dG_b^zeta(s).     (3)

Thus the CLICK branch is precisely the sum of boundary product kernels
in (3), and the NO CLICK branch is the free product propagation minus
that same sum. There is no time-zero atom: even when X_0=b, its first
click clock has a continuous exponential waiting time. The detector
does not perturb X before clicking, so (1) is the correct physical
posterior split.

The map from initial state to (first-click label,time) is a
substochastic Markov kernel. Therefore for every signed zeta,

    sum_b |G_b^zeta|([0,T]) <= ||zeta||_1.             (4)

The two-branch kernel from X_0 to (Y,X_T) is stochastic, so it also
contracts L1 on signed measures. Equation (4), not the sum of absolute
mixture coefficients, controls the number and total weights of appended
terms after signed contributions are AGGREGATED into the marked CDFs.

Acquiring the marked first-click sub-CDFs
----------------------------------------
This step is not an ideal supplied oracle. Let pi be the product
stationary law, pi_*=prod_i min(p_i,1-p_i), gamma=min_i lambda_i,
Lambda=sum_i lambda_i, q=pi(B)>0, and vmin,vmax the minimum/maximum
positive listed detector gains. Let D=diag(pi). Since Q is reversible,
A_V=-D^{1/2}(Q-V)D^{-1/2} is symmetric positive definite. If
E_Q(f,f)>=gamma Var_pi(f), then, writing m=E_pi f,

    m^2 <= (2/q) sum_(b in B) pi(b)f(b)^2 + (2/q)Var_pi(f),

and hence

    A_V >= kappa I,
    kappa=min(gamma*q/3, vmin*q/2)>0.                (5)

All these quantities are computable rationals; log(1/kappa) and
log(1/pi_*) are polynomial in input length even if their values are
enormous.

For real s>0, put R(s)=(sI-Q)^(-1), H(s)=R(s)_{B,B},
w_zeta(s)=zeta R(s)_{:,B}, and W=diag(v_b). The Laplace transform of
the m marked first-click densities is the SMALL boundary solve

    f_zeta(s)=w_zeta(s)[I+W H(s)]^(-1) W.             (6)

The entries of H and w for product zeta are positive-time integrals
of unrestricted product heat kernels. The finite-bit product-resolvent
quadrature in work/target_set_resolvent/MARKED_EVENTS.txt acquires these
entries in time polynomial in input bits, m, bitlength(s), and requested
log precision. Equation (6) adds only an m by m solve. To charge its
conditioning, symmetrize H by D_B: its spectrum lies in
[1/(s+Lambda),1/s]. After also scaling by W^{1/2}, the matrix being
inverted is positive definite with eigenvalues in [1,1+vmax/s]. The
similarity factors have logarithms bounded by the bit lengths of
pi(b),vmin,vmax. Thus target accuracy alpha requires only polynomial
extra bit precision, even for rare clicks.

More explicitly, for product zeta let a=D^{-1/2}zeta,
u_b=D^{1/2}v_b e_b, c_b=A_V^{-1}u_b. Then

    G_b(t)=p_b-a^T exp(-t A_V)c_b,
    p_b=a^T c_b,
    ||a||||c_b|| <= M_b:=vmax/(kappa*pi_*).       (7)

The spectral-polynomial/real-resolvent finite-difference construction
of MARKED_EVENTS.txt, equations (5)-(9) there, applies to (7) with
A_V in place of its hard-killed operator and (6) as transform oracle.
Its degree and all requested precisions are polynomial in
log(M_b/alpha), the model input bits, and bitlength(t). To obtain p_b,
take s0=alpha*kappa*pi_*/4: the first-click survival bound
Pr_zeta(tau>t)<=pi_*^(-1/2) exp(-kappa t) implies
E_zeta tau<=1/(kappa*pi_*), so p_b-f_b(s0)<=alpha/4 for a positive
product zeta; evaluate f_b(s0) to the remaining tolerance. Signed
mixture CDFs are acquired by linear combination of product calls,
choosing per-component accuracy inversely proportional to the known
coefficient l1. No wait until a physical rare click is simulated.

Additive-rank posterior compiler
--------------------------------
Suppose the input signed state is an R-term rational product mixture
tilde_zeta=sum_r c_r mu_r, within L1 error eta of the true unnormalized
history state zeta. Its coefficient l1 C=sum_r|c_r| can exceed one, but
||tilde_zeta||_1<=||zeta||_1+eta<=1+eta.

The free term tilde_zeta P_T retains R product terms. Approximate the
boundary integral in (3) by age kernels delta_b P_u. The elementary
geometric grid from work/controlled_product/RESULT.txt yields
G=O((n/delta)log(1+T Lambda/delta)) bins for L1 state error delta,
and at most mG correction products. More strongly, use the fixed-ellipse
two-level interpolation there. Omit first-click times in the first and
last tau=delta/[64 vmax(1+eta)] units; their total marked mass is at
most 2vmax*tau(1+eta). Partition the remaining ages into
L=O(log(1+T/tau)) balanced rational cells. On each cell the vector
kernel delta_b P_u has complex l1 norm at most 3^n on an ellipse of
parameter 2, so degree

    D0=O(n+log(1/delta))                              (8)

Chebyshev interpolation gives uniform error O(delta). Its signed
coefficient for a target b, cell I=[a,beta], and node j is

    C_(b,I,j)=int_(T-beta,T-a] L_j(T-s)dG_b(s).       (9)

Compute this coefficient from marked CDFs by Stieltjes integration by
parts. Interpolate the analytic scalar G_b(T-u) on the same cell from
finite-bit rational-time CDF queries; degree
O(log(C M_b m L K D0/delta)) suffices. The resulting integral of two
rational polynomials is exact rational arithmetic. The proof and node
precision bounds are in work/controlled_product/RESULT.txt; (7) is the
new analytic bound that transfers them to soft sensing. The Chebyshev
Lebesgue constant is O(log(D0+2)), so (4) gives

    sum_(b,I,j)|C_(b,I,j)|
        <=O(log(D0+2))||tilde_zeta||_1.               (10)

The CLICK mixture is the sum of these mL(D0+1) correction products;
the NO CLICK mixture is the R free products minus them. All acquired
CDF, exponential, node-rounding, and polynomial-integration errors can
be budgeted to make the COMBINED two-branch L1 error <=delta. Along
any realized adaptive history, the rank recurrence is

    R_next <= R+mL(D0+1),                            (11)

and after a CLICK it can be reset to mL(D0+1). Equation (10) bounds the
coefficient l1 increment by O(log(D0+2))(1+eta); hence unnormalized
history-state coefficient l1 is O(K log(D0+2)). Exact two-branch
stochastic kernels contract L1, so propagated approximation errors add
rather than multiply. All quantities have rational encodings of
polynomial bit length in input, K, log(1/delta), and the charged CDF
precision. There is no exponential dependence on n or on physical T.

Acquired adaptive experiment decision
-------------------------------------
For a specified target coordinate i and a finite explicit menu E, let
sigma^e_y be the unnormalized terminal hidden-state measure under
candidate experiment e and outcome y in {NO CLICK,CLICK}. If, after
seeing y, the scientist predicts X_i optimally, the exact probability
of correct prediction is

    A_e=sum_y max{sigma^e_y(X_i=0),sigma^e_y(X_i=1)}.   (12)

No posterior normalization appears in (12). For a product mixture,
the two masses are the coefficient-weighted sums of 1-q_(ri) and q_(ri),
computed in O(R) rational operations. If the two branch mixtures for
each e have total combined L1 error <=delta, then
|Ahat_e-A_e|<=delta. Choosing the largest Ahat_e incurs at most
2delta regret against the best experiment in the EXPLICIT menu.
The cost is menu size times the branch-compiler cost. This is a
constructive active choice, not an entropy or arbitrary-planning claim.

If the current history has probability w, evaluate (12) on its
unnormalized state. Every A_e is multiplied by the same w, so the
argmax is unchanged; conditional regret is at most 2delta/w. To
guarantee epsilon conditional regret, acquire a lower bound w0<=w and
set delta<=epsilon*w0/2. A supplied w0 is not necessary: replay the
actually observed history from its initial product law at precision
2^-r, doubling r until its computed evidence mass exceeds twice its
certified absolute error. For w>0 this halts after
O(log(1/w)) precision levels and yields a rational positive lower
bound. The polylog-accuracy compiler makes the total work polynomial
in log(1/w), unlike physical rejection on a rare history. If w=0,
conditional posterior and regret are undefined; the theorem excludes
that event.

Numerical fixture and limits
----------------------------
Run `python3 work/posterior_operation/demo.py`. It uses exact-size
2^3-state uniformization only as a floating diagnostic for the marked
CDF. With three candidate detectors, it selects detector A first,
matching the exact Bayes bit-classification choice (exact utility
0.8542818451 versus 0.8397576855 for B and 0.5 for C). After NO CLICK,
the exact and compiled choices are A; after CLICK they are B. The
fine-grid realized branch L1 errors are about 0.00376 with at most
101 signed product terms. The coarse grid gives about 0.00897 error
with at most 37 terms. Both choices match the exact small-state
calculation. At s=0.71, the marked transform from the boundary solve (6)
agrees with the direct full soft-killed resolvent to 1.4e-17 in this
floating example. These checks do not certify the general CDF solver or the
fixed-ellipse finite-bit implementation.

This is a structured sensor interface: arbitrary spatial hazards,
unbounded detector support, unknown generator rates, arbitrary utility
tables, or optimal multistep design are outside the theorem. The
full-state posterior can be sampled by the exact rational positive-part
or prefix methods in work/controlled_product/RESULT.txt once its
signed mixture is acquired, with rare-evidence precision explicitly
charged. No external priority review or implementation of the certified
large-n resolver has been completed.
