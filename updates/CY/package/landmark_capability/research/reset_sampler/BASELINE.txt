RARE-CYLINDER TRAJECTORY COMPARISON FOR THE ADDITIVE-HAZARD MODEL
Internal mathematical comparison, 2026-10-10. No historical novelty claim.

For every n>=1, let each coordinate flip 0<->1 at rate 1. There are two
reset channels. For each coordinate i, each channel has hazard 1/(8n) in
state 0 and 1/(4n) in state 1. Channel 1 resets every coordinate to an
independent Bernoulli(1/4); channel 2 resets every coordinate to an
independent Bernoulli(3/4). Thus the global hazard is state dependent and
lies in [1/4,1/2]. All rational input bit lengths total O(n log n).

Let pi be the unique stationary law, and A={1^n}. Then

             4^(-n)/(32n) <= pi(A) <= (3/4)^n.          (1)

Proof of the upper bound. Write L_i=Q_i-diag(a_{1i}+a_{2i}) and
L=sum_i L_i. The stationarity equation pi L + pi U V=0 gives

  pi = sum_{j=1}^2 alpha_j nu_j (-L)^(-1)
     = sum_j alpha_j int_0^infinity nu_j exp(tL) dt,
  alpha_j=pi a_j >=0.                                  (2)

Here -L is invertible because total killing is at least 1/4. Each
nu_j exp(tL) is a nonnegative product subprobability. The conditional
one-bit probability p_j(t), after normalizing a local row, obeys

  p_j'(t)=1-2p_j(t) - [1/(4n)]p_j(t)[1-p_j(t)].         (3)

Initially p_j(0) is 1/4 or 3/4. At p=3/4 the right side is strictly
negative, so p_j(t)<=3/4 for all t. Therefore the mass assigned to A
by each product subprobability is at most (3/4)^n times its total mass.
Apply this pointwise in (2), integrate, and use pi(whole space)=1.

Proof of the lower bound. The total reset firing rate alpha_1+alpha_2
is pi a >=1/4. Each reset law puts at least 4^(-n) mass on A. Starting
at A, the probability of no local transition or reset before time t is
at least exp[-(n+1/2)t]. Retaining just paths of this kind for
0<=t<=1/n in (2) yields

  pi(A) >= (1/4)4^(-n) int_0^{1/n} exp[-(n+1/2)t] dt
        >= e^(-3/2)4^(-n)/(4n)
        >= 4^(-n)/(32n),                               (4)

since e^(3/2)<8.

An ordinary stationary continuous-time trajectory that estimates pi(A)
by visits to A has a rare-event obstruction. In stationarity, the
expected number of exits from A during a length-T trajectory is at most
T(n+1/2)pi(A), and stationarity equates the expected entry count. Thus

  Pr{trajectory hits A by T}
     <= pi(A)+T(n+1/2)pi(A)
     <= [1+T(n+1/2)](3/4)^n.                            (5)

For T=o((4/3)^n/n), this probability tends to zero, so even seeing one
occurrence by direct event-counting takes exponential physical time.
To obtain relative additive error, set the explicit rational tolerance
epsilon_n=4^(-n)/(256n), which is at most pi(A)/8 by (1).
The product-resolvent algorithm in RESULT.txt evaluates this rank-one
product observable to epsilon_n in polynomial bit time: the input and
log(1/epsilon_n) have polynomial length, and the Doeblin precision bound
has log(1/eta)=O(n log n). Thus its number of local heat-kernel nodes is
polynomial in n, whereas the specific direct trajectory event-counting
baseline has exponentially long physical waiting time for a hit.

Scope: (5) is a lower bound for event-counting trajectories started in
stationarity. It is not a lower bound against model-aware importance
sampling, analytic renewal/resolvent methods, exact stationary samplers,
or all regenerative algorithms. Such specialized methods may use the
given rational structure without waiting for an actual visit to A.
