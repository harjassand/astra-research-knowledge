MARKED FIRST-HIT EVENTS AND FINITE-BIN FEEDBACK SIMULATION
2026-10-10. Internal construction; no priority or independent-validation claim.
Companion to RESULT.txt. The same supplied product-chain interface and all its bit-cost conventions apply here.

Claim
-----
Let n independent two-state CTMC coordinates have binary-rational p_i in (0,1) and relaxation rates lambda_i>0. Let B={b_1,...,b_m} be distinct explicit configurations, and let the initial law mu be product Bernoulli with binary-rational parameters. For j in [m] define

  F_j(t)=Pr_mu(H_B<=t, X_{H_B}=b_j),
  p_j=Pr_mu(X_{H_B}=b_j)=F_j(infinity).

Given a binary-rational t>=0 and alpha in (0,1/2), each F_j(t) and p_j can be returned as rationals with absolute error at most alpha in deterministic time polynomial in input bits, m, and log(1/alpha). No 2^n-state object is formed. Consequently, for any explicit finite list of binary-rational time-bin endpoints, a rational joint law of (first-hit label, time bin) can be produced within total variation eta in time polynomial in the input, m, bin count, and log(1/eta). The distribution can be sampled exactly from fair bits at expected polynomial cost.

For a K-event feedback experiment, suppose the policy sees only previous first-hit labels and time bins. At each event it chooses new binary-rational coordinate parameters and either starts from the just-hit configuration or supplies a new binary-rational product reset law. Assume this policy is computable in polynomial time and that all generated parameter bit lengths, target-list lengths, bin counts, and transcript encodings remain polynomially bounded in the stated input and K. Approximating each conditional marked-event kernel within eta/K total variation gives a simulator whose complete length-K observed transcript law is within eta total variation of the true policy experiment. Runtime is polynomial in K, n, m, the policy/input bit lengths, maximum bin count, and log(K/eta). The physical waiting times may be exponentially large; runtime has no factor proportional to them or to 1/pi(B).

This theorem concerns the discrete observations of labels and supplied bins. It does not supply exact continuous hit times or a total-variation approximation to their continuous law. A policy inspecting time more finely than the supplied bins, hidden pre-hit trajectories, or a nonproduct posterior/reset law falls outside the stated interface.

Setup and marked renewal vector
------------------------------
Use the notation of RESULT.txt: pi is the product stationary law, C=B^c, D=diag(pi), A_C=-D_C^{1/2}Q_CC D_C^{-1/2}, q=pi(B), gamma=min_i lambda_i, Lambda=sum_i lambda_i, and pi_*=prod_i min(p_i,1-p_i). The spectral bounds gamma q I<=A_C<=Lambda I follow from the product Poincare inequality. Set a=D_C^{-1/2}mu_C, so ||a||<=pi_*^{-1/2}<=M:=1/pi_*.

For s>0, let R_B(s)=[(sI-Q)^{-1}]_{B,B}, w_mu(s)=mu^T(sI-Q)^{-1}[:,B]. The vector version of the strong-Markov renewal identity gives

  f(s)=(f_1(s),...,f_m(s))=w_mu(s)R_B(s)^{-1},
  f_j(s)=E_mu[exp(-sH_B) 1_{X_{H_B}=b_j}].             (1)

If X_0=b_j, this includes its unit discount at H_B=0. The m x m resolvent and cross row have the product heat-kernel integrands in RESULT.txt. The symmetrized K_B=D_B^{1/2}R_BD_B^{-1/2} satisfies (s+Lambda)^{-1}I<=K_B<=s^{-1}I. Positive log-time quadrature therefore evaluates every f_j(s) to arbitrary requested absolute beta>0 in deterministic time polynomial in input bits, m, bitlength(s), and max(0,log(1/beta)). Explicitly, with c=w_mu D_B^{-1/2} and e_j the jth basis vector, f_j=c K_B^{-1}(sqrt(pi(b_j))e_j). The perturbation estimate in RESULT.txt applies with beta_j=sqrt(pi(b_j))e_j; its norm is at most one. Thus the cross-row amplification, matrix inverse, square roots, and cancellation are all charged. In particular, (1) is not being treated as a free oracle.

Killed-chain absorption formula and its norm bound
--------------------------------------------------
For x in C let kappa_j(x)=Q_{x,b_j}, the instantaneous direct entrance rate into target j, and let v_j=D_C^{1/2}kappa_j. The density of a hit at b_j at time u>0 is

  g_j(u)=mu_C^T exp(u Q_CC)kappa_j=a^T exp(-u A_C)v_j.

Therefore

  F_j(t)=mu(b_j)+a^T A_C^{-1}(I-exp(-t A_C))v_j,       (2)
  p_j=mu(b_j)+a^T A_C^{-1}v_j,
  F_j(t)=p_j-a^T exp(-t A_C)c_j,
  c_j:=A_C^{-1}v_j.                                       (3)

The flux formula is valid even when initial mass lies on B: that mass appears only in the atom mu(b_j) at time zero. At any x, the total jump rate is <=Lambda, so 0<=kappa_j(x)<=Lambda. Hence ||v_j||^2=sum_{x in C}pi(x)kappa_j(x)^2<=Lambda^2. The Dirichlet gap yields

  ||c_j||<=Lambda/(gamma q),
  ||a||||c_j||<=M_j:=Lambda/(gamma q pi_*).             (4)

M_j is a rational, computable upper bound; log M_j has polynomial input bit length. Its potentially huge numerical value enters only precision and degree parameters.

Computing p_j without an s=0 resolvent
---------------------------------------
Since H_B is almost surely finite, p_j=lim_{s downarrow0} f_j(s). For every s0>0,

  0<=p_j-f_j(s0)
    =E[(1-exp(-s0 H_B))1_{X_H=b_j}]
    <=s0 E H_B.

The survival bound in RESULT.txt gives E H_B=int_0^infty S_mu(u)du<=M/(gamma q). To compute p_j within any rational beta>0, choose the rational s0=beta gamma q/(2M), evaluate f_j(s0) within beta/2, and use the result as phat_j. Then |phat_j-p_j|<=beta. The bit length of s0, and the required quadrature/inversion precision at s0, is polynomial in the input and log(1/beta). This step explicitly charges the rare hitting time through log(1/(gamma q)), not through a wait or 1/q iterations.

The precision beta used for p_j inside a high-order moment calculation must be far smaller than the final requested alpha. It is selected below together with the per-resolvent precision.

Marked Laplace transform for the residual probability
-----------------------------------------------------
Put h_j(s)=a^T(sI+A_C)^{-1}c_j. From (1)-(3), or by integrating the residual p_j-F_j(t),

  h_j(s)=(p_j-f_j(s))/s
        =int_0^infty exp(-st)[p_j-F_j(t)]dt.             (5)

The equality can also be checked from f_j(s)=mu(b_j)+a^T(sI+A_C)^{-1}v_j and the resolvent identity [A_C^{-1}-(sI+A_C)^{-1}]/s=(sI+A_C)^{-1}A_C^{-1}.

Given an absolute target zeta for h_j(s') at positive s'>=s, approximate p_j and f_j(s') each to beta=min(zeta s/4,1/4). Their difference divided by s' then has error at most 2beta/s'<=zeta/2. If a final p_j value of precision alpha/10 is also needed, use beta=min(zeta s/4,alpha/10,1/4). The s0 used in the previous section depends on this beta and still has polynomial bit length. Thus (5) is an effective real-axis transform oracle despite the unknown eventual absorption probability.

Single-time sub-CDF algorithm
----------------------------
For t=0 return F_j(0)=mu(b_j) exactly. For t>0 choose either the fully elementary Gevrey-Chebyshev polynomial P_d from RESULT.txt, with degree d=O(log^2(M_j/alpha)), or the known Saff-Schonhage-Varga/Sachdeva-Vishnoi negative-pole polynomial with d=O(log(M_j/alpha)). In either case, with s=1/t for the elementary polynomial (or s=d/t for the sharper prior-art polynomial), Y=s(sI+A_C)^{-1}, and g(y)=exp(1-1/y), one has

  ||exp(-t A_C)-P_d(Y)||<=xi,
  xi:=alpha/(10 M_j).                                     (6)

The polynomial can be constructed with rational coefficients P_d(y)=sum_{r=0}^d alpha_r y^r. Its coefficient l1 norm A_pow=sum_r|alpha_r| has logarithm polynomial in d. For the elementary construction A_pow<=4*7^{d+1}; the sharper prior-art construction gives A_pow<=d^{O(d)}. Formula (3) and (4) show that the spectral-polynomial error in F_j(t) is <=M_j xi<=alpha/10.

The zeroth moment a^T c_j=p_j-mu(b_j) is obtained from phat_j. For r>=1, recover the moment a^T Y^r c_j using finite differences of (5):

  Mtilde_{r,j} = s^r/[(r-1)!Delta^{r-1}]
                 sum_{l=0}^{r-1}(-1)^l binom(r-1,l) h_j(s+l Delta)
               = a^T [prod_{l=0}^{r-1}s(s+l Delta+A_C)^{-1}]c_j.   (7)

The scalar product differs from (s/(s+x))^r by at most r(r-1)Delta/(2s) for every x>=0. Thus its bilinear error is at most M_j r(r-1)Delta/(2s). Set the rational difference step to satisfy

  Delta/s <= alpha/[100 M_j A_pow d^2].                 (8)

The total bias after weighting all d moments by |alpha_r| is at most alpha/200. Write U=2s/Delta and request each h_j(s+l Delta) to absolute precision

  zeta=alpha/[100 A_pow s U^{d-1}].                    (9)

The binomial amplification in moment r is at most s U^{r-1}/(r-1)!, so the weighted numerical error is <=alpha/100. Approximate p_j once to beta=min(zeta s/4,alpha/[100 A_pow],1/4), and every f_j(s+l Delta) to beta. This supplies (9), controls the zeroth moment contribution by <=alpha/100, and controls the final p_j term in (3). The output

  Fhat_j(t)=phat_j-sum_{r=0}^d alpha_r Mtilde_{r,j}

has absolute error less than alpha after adding the polynomial, finite-difference, transform-evaluation, zeroth-moment, and p_j errors. All quantities are rational after certified approximation. The number of transform evaluations is O(d^2); their required bit precision is polynomial in input length and log(1/alpha), because log M_j, log A_pow, d, log U, and bitlength(s) are polynomial. This is the promised polylog-accuracy sub-CDF query.

Binned marked law and exact rational sampler
--------------------------------------------
Sort explicitly supplied nonnegative rational bin endpoints 0=t_0<t_1<...<t_L; include the outcomes {H=0}, (t_{l-1},t_l] for l=1,...,L, and (t_L,infinity). For each j the true cell masses are

  w_{j,0}=mu(b_j),
  w_{j,l}=F_j(t_l)-F_j(t_{l-1}) for 1<=l<=L,
  w_{j,L+1}=p_j-F_j(t_L).                               (10)

All are nonnegative and their total over j,l is 1. Compute each p_j and F_j(t_l) to absolute alpha, with alpha=eta/[16m(L+2)] (or smaller). Form rational approximate cell masses by (10). Their aggregate l1 error is at most 2m(L+2)alpha<=eta/8, after harmless duplicate-endpoint bookkeeping. Clamp negative masses to zero, then divide by the resulting positive total. Clamping cannot increase l1 distance to the true nonnegative vector; normalization increases it by at most another factor two. Therefore the resulting rational probability vector is within eta total variation of the true marked-bin law. Its common denominator and support list have polynomial bit length. An exact uniform integer on that denominator, generated by rejection from fair bits, samples this rational vector with expected fewer than two attempts.

The TV guarantee is for the finite set of labels and prescribed bins. If a representative timestamp is also output, it denotes the sampled bin rather than a TV approximation to the original continuous time inside the bin.

Feedback composition
--------------------
For every possible prior observed transcript h, let P_h be the exact conditional law of the next (label,bin), and Q_h the algorithm's rational approximation. The construction above applies because the policy supplies the next coordinate rates and product initial law or the previous hit state, all within the interface. Choose its accuracy eta_step=eta/K, so sup_h TV(P_h,Q_h)<=eta/K. Couple the exact and simulated next outcomes maximally whenever their transcript prefixes agree. The probability of any first disagreement by time K is at most sum_{k=1}^K eta/K=eta. Thus the joint observed transcript laws have TV distance at most eta. This proof does not rely on independence across episodes; it uses uniform conditional-kernel accuracy and the fact that both policies make the same next-model choice while observed prefixes agree.

The simulator performs K kernel builds and K rational samples, each with polynomial work in the current model/policy encoding, m, the bin count, and log(K/eta). If per-episode parameter bit lengths grow exponentially under a policy, or if the policy asks for an unbounded number of bins, this polynomial conclusion does not apply; those sizes are explicit parts of the input/resource boundary. No wall-clock simulation of the potentially rare physical waiting is performed.

Validation status and boundaries
--------------------------------
Equations (1)-(10) are a mathematical derivation with finite-bit budgets, not an implemented interval-certified solver or independently verified theorem. The background exponential approximation is prior art; the marked-event boundary compiler, target-set numerical conditioning, and feedback composition require a targeted literature review before any novelty claim. The stronger source supplied for the rational approximation is Sachdeva and Vishnoi, Faster Algorithms via Approximation Theory, Section 7, Theorem 7.1, https://theory.epfl.ch/vishnoi/Publications_files/approx-survey.pdf .

A small floating-point identity check used n=3, p=(.21,.64,.37), lambda=(1.1,.4,2.3), initial Bernoulli parameters (.8,.2,.7), and the two target states with integer encodings B={1,6}. At s=.71, the marked-renewal values (0.4425018075406458,0.1565811582419432) agreed with the killed-chain Laplace values (0.4425018075406460,0.15658115824194316); the computed absorption probabilities sum to one. This checks the algebra on one instance only, not finite-bit certification or general correctness.
