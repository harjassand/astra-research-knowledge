NONREVERSIBLE PRODUCT-CHAIN TARGET-HITTING THEOREM
Internal proof candidate, 2026-10-10. No priority or external certification claim.
See COUPLED_EXTENSION.txt for recurring nonreversible global-gate jumps and
synchronized stationary resets handled by a further small Woodbury solve.

1. Interface and result

Input: n independent irreducible continuous-time chains, with explicitly given
d_i by d_i rational row-generators Q_i, rational product initial law
mu=otimes_i mu_i, a deduplicated list B of m distinct product configurations,
a nonnegative rational time t, and rational epsilon in (0,1/2). Write
d=max_i d_i, N=prod_i d_i, Q=sum_i Q_i (tensor-coordinate sum),
tau_B=inf{t>=0:X_t in B}. Degenerate one-state coordinates may be removed.

Theorem. There is a deterministic certified algorithm returning rational Fhat
with |Fhat-Pr_mu(tau_B<=t)|<=epsilon. Its bit complexity is polynomial in the
entire binary input length, m, and log(1/epsilon), and does not enumerate N
states. The polynomial may depend on the explicitly supplied maximum local
dimension d, but d itself is bounded by input length. Its operations are
small local matrix exponentials, products of their selected entries, scalar
quadratures, and m by m linear solves. In particular the running time has no
factor polynomial in the possibly exponential inverse product spectral gap,
inverse stationary mass of B, or inverse hitting rate: only their bit lengths
enter. This is an additive CDF query, not exact symbolic output or an unbiased
single-sample simulator.

Status: the argument below is a complete mathematical bit-complexity route,
including explicit norm and precision budgets. A prototype numerical check is
separate; no full certified implementation has been built here. This result
does not assert novelty against all prior product-chain hitting algorithms.

2. Sharp local numerical-range sector

For an irreducible d-state generator Q with stationary pi, put
c_xy=pi_x q_xy for x!=y. Stationarity makes c a nonnegative divergence-free
flow: sum_y c_xy=sum_y c_yx. Repeatedly take a directed simple cycle in its
positive support and subtract the minimum edge flow on that cycle. An edge
vanishes each step; therefore this gives a finite positive sum of directed
simple cycles of lengths ell<=d. For complex f, with inner product linear in
the second slot, the cycle contribution to <f,-Qf>_pi is

    w sum_{j=0}^{ell-1} conj(f_j)(f_j-f_{j+1}).

The cyclic shift is normal. On a Fourier mode its eigenvalue in this form is
1-exp(2*pi*i*k/ell). For k!=0 the ratio |imaginary part|/real part is at most
cot(pi/ell), hence at most cot(pi/d). Its k=0 mode contributes zero. Adding
cycles preserves this closed cone. Consequently

    |Im <f,-Qf>_pi| <= cot(pi/d) Re <f,-Qf>_pi
                         <= (d/3) Re <f,-Qf>_pi.          (2.1)

For d=2 the sharp coefficient is zero. A one-way directed d-cycle saturates
cot(pi/d) on its first nonconstant Fourier mode. The rational d/3 bound follows
from cot x <=1/x and pi>3; it is used for the algorithm, so no transcendental
constant needs to be computed.

For product stationary pi=otimes pi_i, the global Q form is a sum of local
forms applied to every fixed slice of the other coordinates. It inherits
(2.1) with d=max_i d_i. Set D=diag(pi), A=-D^{1/2}QD^{-1/2}. If C=B^c,
the killed A_C is its principal compression. Extending any vector on C by
zero to B proves that A_C inherits precisely the same sector bound. All
eigenvalues of A_C lie in the right sector of half-angle at most
arctan(d/3)<pi/2. In particular this step does not assume reversibility or
diagonalizability.

3. Explicit accretivity, norms, and bit sizes

For each nontrivial local chain let

    g_i(x,y)=[pi_i(x)q_i(x,y)+pi_i(y)q_i(y,x)]/2

on each unordered edge with a positive term, and let gmin_i be the least
positive g_i. The undirected support is connected. Choose one path of length
at most d_i-1 for every pair x,y. Cauchy-Schwarz along each path and
Var_pi(f)=1/2 sum_{x,y} pi_x pi_y |f_x-f_y|^2 show

    Var_pi_i(f) <= [(d_i-1)/2] sum_edges |f_x-f_y|^2.

Since Re<f,-Q_i f>_pi_i=sum_edges g_i(x,y)|f_x-f_y|^2,
the local symmetric gap is at least gamma_i=2 gmin_i/(d_i-1). Tensor
Poincare gives global gamma=min_i gamma_i. Let q=pi(B)>0, exactly
computable from the m listed states. For any f zero on B,
|<1,f>_pi|^2 <=pi(C)||f||_pi^2, so Var_pi(f)>=q||f||_pi^2. Thus

    Re <f,A_C f> >= lambda0 ||f||^2,
    lambda0=gamma q>0.                                  (3.1)

Stationary pi_i are rational and computable by a d_i by d_i exact linear
solve. gamma, q and lambda0 are positive rationals whose bit lengths are
polynomial in input length, despite possibly being exponentially small.

Put pi_star=prod_i min_x pi_i(x) and H=1/pi_star, both with polynomial bit
length. Let r_i=max_x[-q_i(x,x)] and Lambda=sum_i r_i. Uniformization writes
Q_i=r_i(P_i-I) for stationary stochastic P_i. Jensen and stationarity give
||P_i||_{L2(pi_i)}<=1, hence ||A||<=2 Lambda. If a=D_C^{-1/2}mu_C and
b=D_C^{1/2}1_C, then ||a||||b||<=H (a deliberately loose rational bound).
Equation (3.1) implies ||exp(-t A_C)||<=exp(-lambda0 t).

If C is empty, F(t)=1. If t=0, F(0)=mu(B). For t>0, if
lambda0*t>=ceil(log_2(8H/epsilon)), output 1, with error <epsilon/8.

4. Boundary renewal from the FREE product process

For complex s with Re s>0, let R(s)=(sI-Q)^(-1), K(s)=R(s)_{B,B}, and
w(s)=mu^T R(s)_{:,B}. First-hit renewal gives

    f(s):=E_mu[exp(-s tau_B)] = w(s) K(s)^(-1) 1_B,
    h(s):=int_0^infty exp(-st)Pr_mu(tau_B>t)dt=(1-f(s))/s. (4.1)

This includes the atom at time zero when mu(B)>0. Analytic continuation
extends (4.1) to the contour below. The desired survival is
S(t)=Pr_mu(tau_B>t)=a^T exp(-t A_C)b. Thus h(s)=a^T(sI+A_C)^(-1)b.

The apparently N-dimensional free resolvent needs only m^2+m entries.
For any complex z where the integral converges, every target pair a,b has

    [exp(zQ)]_{a,b}=prod_i [exp(zQ_i)]_{a_i,b_i},

and every product-source to target b has

    [mu^T exp(zQ)]_b=prod_i [mu_i^T exp(zQ_i)]_{b_i}.       (4.2)

This is the crucial affordable observable interface; no killed evolution is
computed in the product state space.

5. Rational sector contour and rotated free resolvent

Write d=max d_i>=2 and define rational complex numbers

    c=1/(2d)+i,            u=1+i/d.

For every real rho>0, use s=-c*rho and its conjugate. From (2.1),

    Re<x,u(A_C-c*rho)x>
      >=(2/3)lambda0||x||^2+[rho/(2d)]||x||^2.          (5.1)

Therefore ||(A_C-c*rho)^(-1)||<=3/(lambda0+rho/d).
For the full A the same argument, without lambda0, gives
||(A-c*rho)^(-1)||<=3d/rho. These are singular-value estimates, and remain
valid for arbitrarily nonnormal generators. The ray lies strictly outside
the sector containing spectrum(A_C).

More strongly, Re<x,u A x>>=0 and Re[(-c*rho)u]=rho/(2d)>0. Hence

    R(-c*rho)=u int_0^infty exp(c*rho*u*v) exp(u*v*Q) dv. (5.2)

The scalar exponential decays in magnitude as exp[-rho*v/(2d)]. Equation
(4.2) makes the integrand a product of local complex-time heat entries.
The same statements hold on the conjugate ray.

The sectorial Dunford contour, with upper ray from infinity to zero and
lower conjugate ray from zero to infinity, yields the absolutely convergent
real formula for every t>0:

    S(t)=(1/pi) Im { c int_0^infty exp(-c*t*rho) h(-c*rho) d rho }. (5.3)

The small circle at zero vanishes by (3.1). A large connecting arc vanishes
because t>0, Re z>0 in the wedge, and ||A_C||<=2Lambda. No spectral
decomposition or product-space matrix is required.

6. Certified quadratures with polynomial bit cost

The following elementary quadrature fact fixes the quantitative issue.
Suppose g is analytic and bounded by M on the Bernstein ellipse E_R of a
real interval I, R>1. The n-point Gauss-Legendre rule has error at most

    8 |I| M R^(-2n+1)/(R-1).                            (6.1)

Indeed Chebyshev coefficients are bounded by 2 M R^(-k), so a degree
2n-1 truncation uniformly approximates g by its geometric tail. Gauss is
exact on that polynomial; positive weights summing to |I| bound both the
integral and quadrature remainder. Nodes and weights are computable to p
bits in polynomial time in n,p by real-root isolation of the Legendre
polynomial and its explicit weight formula. Their finite-precision errors
are handled by increasing p polynomially.

For both (5.2) and (5.3), split the retained positive half-line into dyadic
intervals [r,2r], with a possibly shorter last interval. On every such
interval E_R for R=1+1/(64d) lies in

    Re v >=r/2,       |Im v| <=Re v/(32d).              (6.2)

The same statement holds when the integration variable is rho. For the
inner integral, if v is in (6.2), Re[(-c*rho)u*v]>=rho*Re(v)/(3d), and
Re<x,u*v*A x>>=0 by (2.1). Thus the local and full complex semigroups
stay contractive throughout the ellipse, and the integrand is analytic and
bounded by 2H. For the outer integral, a complex rho in (6.2) satisfies
Re(c*rho)>=Re(rho)/(3d) and the rotated accretivity estimate (5.1) with
rho replaced by Re(rho) and a modest constant loss. Thus its integrand is
analytic and bounded by 6H/lambda0 throughout that ellipse. By (6.1),
O[d(log(1/eta)+log(H)+log(endpoint magnitude)+log d)] Gauss points per
dyadic interval suffice for absolute quadrature tolerance eta. All the
logarithms here have polynomial size in the binary input.

One explicit outer truncation and precision budget is as follows. Set

    rho_min=epsilon*lambda0/(32H),
    Lout=ceil(log_2(64 H d/epsilon)),
    rho_max=(2d/t)*Lout.

The bounds |h(-c*rho)|<=3H/(lambda0+rho/d) and
|exp(-c*t*rho)|=exp[-t*rho/(2d)] make each discarded outer tail at most
epsilon/8. If rho_max<rho_min, the previous large-t shortcut applies;
otherwise the number of dyadic intervals is O(log(rho_max/rho_min)).
At each outer node compute h(s) to absolute
delta_h=epsilon/(32*rho_max); positive Gauss weights then cause at most
epsilon/8 additional error in (5.3). Quadrature itself uses tolerance
epsilon/4, leaving slack for finite precision.

For any required free-resolvent entry precision delta at an outer node of
modulus rho, truncate (5.2) to

    v_min=delta/(16H),
    Lin=max(1,ceil(log_2(32 H d/(rho*delta)))),
    v_max=(2d/rho)*Lin.

The bounds above make each inner tail at most delta/8. Dyadic Gauss rules
with tolerance delta/2 compute the retained part. The number of intervals,
quadrature order, and required working precision are polynomial in input
bits plus log(1/delta), even if v_max is numerically enormous.

For completeness, the boundary solve has a polynomial BIT condition bound.
Use weighted coordinates K_B=D_B^(1/2)R_B D_B^(-1/2),
v_B=w D_B^(-1/2), beta=D_B^(1/2)1_B. Then f=v_B K_B^(-1) beta.
The Schur-complement identity gives

    K_B^(-1)=(sI+A)_{BB}-A_{BC}(sI+A_C)^(-1)A_{CB}.

On the retained contour, ||K_B^(-1)|| is bounded by the rational number

    B0=2*rho_max+2*Lambda+12*Lambda^2/lambda0.

Also ||v_B||<=3H*d/rho_min and ||beta||<=1. If each of the m^2+m
free entries is computed to weighted absolute error delta with

    m*delta*B0 <=1/2,
    delta <=delta_h*rho_min/
             [8*m*B0+24*H*d*m*B0^2/rho_min],        (6.3)

then the inverse-perturbation identity makes the resulting h error at most
delta_h. Additional arithmetic error is made smaller by ordinary guard
bits. The reciprocal and logarithm of every quantity in (6.3) have
polynomial bit length; converting raw entries to weighted entries also
costs only polynomial bit precision because each pi_b is explicit.

At a complex-time inner node, form each local exp(u*v*Q_i) by scaling and
squaring in L2(pi_i). Scale until ||u*v*Q_i/2^k||<=1; k is
O(log(|v|*r_i)+1). Taylor order O(k+log(1/delta)) gives initial error
small enough to absorb the at most 2^k squaring amplification; L2(pi_i)
contractivity of the exact exponential bounds the amplification. Matrix
entries and the products in (4.2) need polynomial guard bits, since their
weighted norms are at most one and 1/pi_star has polynomial bit length.
Consequently every operation above has polynomial bit cost, and the nested
node counts multiply only polynomials.

7. Interpretation and caveats

The key new mathematical step relative to a reversible spectral-measure
route is the sharp directed-cycle sector lemma plus a fixed rational pair of
contour/time rotations. It supplies a uniform analytic strip for geometric
quadrature without knowing any nonreversible eigenbasis or pseudospectrum.
The method spends precision proportional to log(1/lambda0) and log H,
which are input bit quantities; it does not simulate a trajectory of
expected duration 1/lambda0. Numerically it can still be impractical when
d, m, or bit lengths are large. It assumes explicitly given rational local
generators, an explicit small target list, independent coordinates, and
product initial law. Correlated initial laws with a polynomial-size sum of
products can be handled linearly; unrestricted succinct correlations are
not covered.

cot(pi/d) bound and algorithmic composition are reconstructed here and
