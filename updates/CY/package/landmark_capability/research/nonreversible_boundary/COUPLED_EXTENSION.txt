PERSISTENT COUPLING EXTENSION: FINITE GLOBAL GATES AND SYNCHRONIZED RESETS
Internal theorem candidate, 2026-10-10. Algebraic composition, no novelty claim.

The base theorem in RESULT.txt computes an additive-epsilon CDF of the first
hit of m explicit product configurations by independent rational finite CTMCs.
This extension permits genuine, indefinitely recurring interactions while
retaining polynomial bit complexity in the succinct input and log(1/epsilon).

Operational input class

Let Q0 be the tensor-coordinate sum of explicitly supplied irreducible local
generators Q_i, with product stationary law pi0 and product initial law mu.
Give k distinct global configurations G={g_1,...,g_k} and an explicitly
supplied rational k by k row-generator Gamma. Check exactly that

    sum_{j=1}^k pi0(g_j) Gamma_{j,l}=0  for every l.        (1)

Thus the restriction of pi0 to G is a strictly positive, not necessarily
normalized stationary measure for Gamma. Optionally supply a nonnegative
rational global reset rate r. The complete generator on the N=prod_i d_i
product states is

    Q=Q0+E_G Gamma E_G^T+r(1 pi0^T-I).                  (2)

Here E_G selects the listed configurations. The gate term triggers a jump
between global configurations when the chain visits one, possibly changing
every coordinate at once. Gamma can be nonreversible; directed cycles of
three or more gates are allowed. The reset term, when present, independently
resamples *all* coordinates at one common Poisson event time from pi0. These
events recur indefinitely; (2) does not merely modify an absorbing target.
Both terms are valid generators and preserve pi0. Q remains irreducible.

For an explicit target set B of m configurations, rational t>=0, and rational
epsilon>0, Pr_mu(tau_B<=t) can be computed to additive epsilon in bit time
polynomial in the entire input length, k, m, and log(1/epsilon), without
enumerating N. The reset rate may be zero; the gate list may be empty.

Proof: sector and killed lower bound

Put D=diag(pi0), A0=-D^(1/2)Q0D^(-1/2), and b=D^(1/2)1. On the gate subspace,
T=-D_G^(1/2)Gamma D_G^(-1/2). Equation (1) permits the same stationary-flow
cycle decomposition as for a local chain, even if Gamma is reducible. Thus
T has nonnegative real quadratic form and

    |Im<x,Tx>| <=cot(pi/k) Re<x,Tx> <=(k/3)Re<x,Tx>

for k>=2. The reset term becomes r(I-bb^T), a real positive projection.
Consequently

    A=-D^(1/2)QD^(-1/2)
      =A0+E_G T E_G^T+r(I-bb^T)                       (3)

has numerical range in the sector |Im|<=(Dmax/3)Re, where
Dmax=max(2,max_i d_i,k). The sector inequality is inherited by the killed
compression A_C, C=B^c. All added real parts are nonnegative, so the
explicit strong accretivity bound lambda0=gamma*pi0(B) from RESULT.txt,
computed only from the local additive reversibilizations and B, still holds.
The norm bound ||A||<=2 sum_i r_i+2 r_Gamma+r follows by uniformization of
Gamma in its stationary weighted norm. Therefore the rational contour
c=1/(2Dmax)+i, u=1+i/Dmax has exactly the same full/killed resolvent
bounds and outer CDF inversion as in RESULT.txt, with d replaced by Dmax.

Proof: product free resolvent plus a (k+1)-dimensional solve

Let s=-c*rho on the contour and define the shifted product resolvent

    K0(s)=(sI+A0+rI)^(-1).

It has the convergent rotated-time representation

    K0(s)=u int_0^infty exp[-(s+r)u v]exp(-u v A0)dv. (4)

The scalar factor has decay exp[-(r+rho/(2Dmax))v]; all needed entries of
exp(-u v A0) are products of small local heat entries. Let W=[b,E_G] and
J=diag(-r,T), of size (k+1) by (k+1). Equation (3) becomes
A=A0+rI+W J W^T, and the Woodbury identity gives

    K(s):=(sI+A)^(-1)
       =K0-K0 W (I+J W^T K0 W)^(-1)J W^T K0.         (5)

The matrix inverse in (5) is only (k+1) by (k+1); no inverse of Gamma or J
is required. Entries of K0 between listed gates/targets, and product-source
rows, follow from (4). Factors involving b are even simpler:

    K0 b=b/(s+r),       b^T K0=b^T/(s+r),
    (D^(-1/2)mu)^T K0 b=1/(s+r).                       (6)

Thus at most O((m+k)^2) selected product free entries are needed per s.
The target renewal formula applied to K(s) then uses the same m by m
boundary solve as RESULT.txt, with source row a^T K(s) E_B and
K_B=E_B^T K(s)E_B. This computes the hitting transform for the *interacting*
process, after which the same outer contour yields its CDF.

Proof: no hidden conditioning in the small interaction solve

The full K(s) obeys ||K(s)||<=3Dmax/rho from the inherited sector bound.
Although J may be nonnormal and the finite matrix in (5) may look poorly
conditioned, the exact identity

    (I+J W^T K0 W)^(-1)=I-J W^T K W                (7)

and ||W||^2<=2 give

    ||(I+J W^T K0 W)^(-1)||
       <=1+6Dmax||J||/rho.                            (8)

The right side has polynomial *bit length* on the truncated contour.
Equation (8), the product base-resolvent bound, and the m-boundary Schur
bound from RESULT.txt give polynomial guard-bit requirements. Inner and
outer dyadic Gauss quadratures have the same fixed rational ellipse width
O(1/Dmax). All matrix dimensions, node counts, and bit lengths are
polynomial in the explicit input and log(1/epsilon).

Concrete behavior beyond absorbing boundaries

Take n symmetric two-state independent rate-one flip chains, pi0 uniform,
G={0^n,1^n}, and Gamma that flips these two global states at rate alpha in
both directions. From 0^n, the extra edge hits target 1^n at rate alpha.
The immediate competing-event lower bound is

    Pr(tau_{1^n}<=t)>=alpha/(alpha+n)*(1-exp[-(alpha+n)t]).

Under Q0 alone, the probability of a first hit by t is at most t^n:
the instantaneous influx to 1^n is n*p_s^(n-1)*(1-p_s), where
p_s=(1-exp(-2s))/2<=s, and integration from 0 to t gives the bound.
At alpha=n and t=1/n, the gate lower bound is
(1-exp(-2))/2>0.43 while the product-only first-hit probability is at most
n^(-n). After each such
jump the chain continues, so this is a persistent coupled dynamics. The
optional r term similarly makes jumps that resample all n coordinates at
shared event times, creating time correlations between coordinates even
though their stationary joint law remains product.

Boundary of the extension and familiar baselines

The weighted stationarity check (1) is material: without it, pi0 need not
be stationary, the local/gate sector forms no longer add, and this proof
does not give a polynomial-width contour. General state-dependent reset
rates a(x) to a product law have a diagonal loss diag(a) which is usually
full rank and do not fit (5); factorization of a(x) alone is insufficient.
Likewise an arbitrary succinct interaction cannot be inferred to be easy.

This construction combines standard finite-rank resolvent algebra,
finite-target first-hit renewal, and a sectorial contour. Direct familiar
baselines are an N-state killed exponential/resolvent, uniformization with
cost proportional to the physical horizon, and Monte Carlo with
epsilon^(-2) sampling dependence plus trajectory cost. Woodbury by itself
reduces a resolvent solve but does not supply the product entry oracle,
contour inversion, or finite-precision bounds; the base theorem does that.
Finite-rank perturbations of Markov chains and stochastic resetting have
extensive prior literature, so no priority claim is made. Relevant primary
examples: Hunter, The Computation of the Mean First Passage Times for Markov
Chains (2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07781 ; Evans and Majumdar,
Diffusion with Stochastic Resetting (2011),
https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2704 .
