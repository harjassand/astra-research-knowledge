Exact accounting statements for the parked electrothermal separation lead

1. Pooling bound

Let j index collected fractions. Let m_j >= 0 be each fraction's total mass on
one common, complete assay basis, and p_j in [0,1] its target mass fraction.
Assume sum_j m_j > 0. If pooling causes no selective loss, reaction or further
separation, conservation gives

  p_pool = (sum_j m_j p_j)/(sum_j m_j).

Writing alpha_j = m_j/(sum_k m_k), alpha_j >= 0 and sum_j alpha_j = 1 yields

  min_j p_j <= p_pool <= max_j p_j.

This is a convex-average identity. It has no historical-novelty claim.
Applied to the mean values in Source_Data_ED_Fig.6.xlsx, 'Fig. 6e'!B3:B5, its
largest mean input is 0.9918872. Mere pooling of those mean-composition outputs
cannot produce nominal p_pool = 0.995. This numerical application assumes the
common complete denominator above. The deposited standard deviations are not
hard error bounds, so 0.9918872 is not a certified future-response ceiling.

This proof does not constrain a new waveform, serial treatment of an evolving
residue, selective product reprocessing, feedback, a new feed, chemical reaction
or a different purification step. Those operations need their own measurements.
Fresh-feed responses cannot be added as if the second pulse acts on the original
feed after the first has changed composition, particle state and temperature.

2. Recovery and residue disappearance are distinct

Let n0 be feed target inventory, np the collected accepted target product, nr
the residual solid target, ng the gas/trap target, na net target uptake by all
auxiliaries and ns target removed in sampling. A closed elemental inventory is

  n0 = np + nr + ng + na + ns + R,

where R is the unexplained residual and must be bounded by independent assay
uncertainty before claiming closure. The recovery is y = np/n0. Even nr = 0
does not imply y = 1, since ng, na, ns or R may remain. It also does not establish
the intended product identity or purity.

For the reported means, y = 0.8641 at 130 V leaves 13.59% of initial Ga outside
the collected product. At 110 V, the corresponding value is 48.00333%. These
are simple complements of the reported yields, not measurements assigning that
missing share to a particular pathway. The source's near-absence-in-residue
observation can support conversion while leaving recovery separate.

3. Chlorine is a reagent inventory

The chemically balanced conversion identity

  2 GaN + 3 Cl2 -> 2 GaCl3 + N2

exports 1.5 mol of supplied Cl2 equivalent per mole of Ga in the chloride
product. With the explicitly declared conventional atomic masses Ga = 69.723
and Cl = 35.45 g/mol, the exported chlorine is

  3(35.45)/69.723 = 1.525321629878... g chlorine per g Ga.

This is not a measured utilization ratio; additional unused Cl2 could be present.
Reclaiming unused Cl2 does not reclaim the chlorine in the collected GaCl3.
If an oxide output is required, the formal inventory identity

  2 GaCl3 + 3 H2O -> Ga2O3 + 6 HCl

moves that chlorine to HCl. It does not regenerate Cl2. No rate, favorable
equilibrium, heat demand or efficient regeneration mechanism is asserted from
these identities. A demanded closed chlorine loop requires another operation
and its measured work, losses, product specification and consumables.

4. Source values and clocks

Source_Data_Fig.4.xlsx, 'Fig. 4f'!A3:C4 labels 50 mg and 1 g. Its purity/yield
values are fractions, unlike several other source sheets that store percentages.
Therefore 1 g gives 95.1% purity and 88.2% yield. This unit conversion was checked
against the primary preprint text.

The preprint PDF page 14 discusses 1 g per roughly 10 minutes. The arithmetic
1 g * 24*60/10 = 144 g/day assumes uninterrupted repetition. It does not acquire
24-hour endurance, isolated product mass/day, feed variability, collection loss
or a regenerated reactor. PDF page 17 also states a 2-hour calcination for the
capacitor feed. The best small-scale Ta condition in Fig. 3g (PDF page 10) has
60 s ETC plus 40 s ETCC. Preparation is not erased by counting only those 100 s.
The ratio 7200/100 = 72 describes this recipe's clocks. It is not an industrial
latency lower bound; continuous equipment, shared heat and parallel preparation
could change the process while incurring their actual capital and energy costs.

5. What the executed checks can validate

scripts/calculate.py reopens the preserved XLSX source bytes. It checks the eight
indium mean calculations against their three deposited inputs within source
rounding; retains exact source cell coordinates; verifies all eleven repository
MD5 checksums and every saved SHA-256 pin; and checks the modeled-energy header
discrepancy arithmetically. This validates extraction and arithmetic scope only.
The checks cannot validate the chemical experiment, repair missing inventories,
turn analytical repeats into independent process repeats, establish a useful
whole-cycle advantage, or establish historical priority.
