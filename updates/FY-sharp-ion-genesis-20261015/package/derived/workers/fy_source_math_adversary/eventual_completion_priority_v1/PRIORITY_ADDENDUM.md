## Strictness is a substantive boundary

For a retained conformal acoustic zero `(I,R)`, both
`D^2F(I)[R,R]=0` and `D^2H(I)[R,R]=0`. Thus no finite H coefficient can create
the uniform positive constant demanded by Iwaniec–Kristensen Theorem 3.1 at
that contact. Their proof's displayed coefficient bound depends inversely on
the strict constant. Replacing F by `F+epsilon*|A|^4` permits a strict argument
for each positive epsilon, but the resulting coefficients need not remain
bounded as epsilon tends to zero. That establishes at most closure membership
without a separate uniform argument.

Consequently a statement about finite weak-boundary completion is a stronger
claim than strict completion or equality of closures. The new determinant-zero
exclusions are exactly the kind of missing boundary information that strict
arguments do not provide. This observation does not certify the new internal
equations; it identifies why the listed classical theorem does not already
prove them.
