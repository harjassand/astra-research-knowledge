DECISION

The Kautz publisher source-data-access gap is closed: the original Fig. 2, 3, 4,
and 5 XLSX files and publisher Supplementary Information were downloaded,
hashed, and read without modification. The data support the reported KCl
deionization response. They do not supply Li/Na flux vectors, collected lithium
product, or a restored-device cation-exchange cycle for a ratchet.

Two corrections matter when carrying forward the supplied physical report:
1. The efficiency is maximum electrical load-power efficiency for a separate
   Au-Au/HCl experiment. It is not measured KCl electrodialysis separation
   free-energy efficiency.
2. Kong's strong lithium product benchmark used synthetic geothermal feeds.
   Its reported 99.6 +/- 0.2% LiOH solution purity does not directly reproduce
   as the unweighted mean of all nonzero publisher figure rows under Eq. 6.
   Preserve the reported result and the narrower data replay together.

No new physical experiment or independent replication was performed here.
Agreement between a calculation and published figures is a source consistency
check, not external experimental verification of the proposed new operation.

GROSSMAN: ACTUAL MEASUREMENT AND ELECTRODE INVENTORIES

Source locations: G1 Methods; III.2; III.4; Appendices A-C (PDF pp. 4-8, 12-14,
18-22). The device has 40 nm pores, 50 um AAO thickness, 40 nm planar-equivalent
Au on both faces, active area 0.159 cm2; electrolyte is 1 mM KCl throughout.
Complementary bipotentiostatic signals remain within -0.05 to +0.45 V versus a
shared Ag/AgCl quasi-reference, with a shared Pt counter electrode.

Reported peak: -5.17 uA/cm2 at 100 Hz, duty 0.7; voltage output -53.96 mV.
The total current corresponding to that density is -0.82203 uA. Its magnitude
is equivalent to 30.6711 nmol of monovalent charge per hour (F=96485.33212 C/mol).
That is a charge-imbalance equivalent; it is not measured K, Cl, or Li throughput.

The output is measured between two Ag/AgCl wires. Current is supplied by
AgCl(s)+e -> Ag(s)+Cl-(aq) on one side and Ag(s)+Cl-(aq) -> AgCl(s)+e on the other.
One Faraday shifts one mole of chloride between solution and solid inventory
at each wire. Sustained electrical output therefore participates in the local
Ag/AgCl inventories; 'capacitive' describes the pumping contacts, not every
component of the electrical measurement circuit.

With orientation fixed and all auxiliary currents/storage accounted for,
I/F measures the difference of K and Cl transfer rates. It supplies one scalar,
not two species fluxes. A sign reversal can occur while both species still move
in the same direction. The authors infer likely Cl reversal from positive AAO
pore charge at neutral pH; they do not present a direct signed species assay.

CV controls support a capacitive contact window for this KCl regime. Absence of
redox peaks supplies neither a quantified total-cell chemical inventory nor a
leakage bound for a changed Li/Na electrolyte. Sample degradation required
different samples across experiments. Parameter points use 60-120 s ON segments
and adjacent OFF drift correction; the drive comparison uses duplicate scans,
with extrema shown as error bars. These are short operating-characteristic
measurements, not collected-product or lifetime validation.

KAUTZ: TWO DIFFERENT DEIONIZATION EXPERIMENTS MUST STAY SEPARATE

Three-compartment ED: Au-Pt ratchet, CEM/AEM ionic return circuit, initially
1 mM KCl; dilution volume 0.63 mL, two reservoirs 4.1 mL each, reference cell
0.66 mL, active ratchet area 0.16 cm2. Drive: 1 V amplitude, 11 Hz, duty 0.5.
The CEM/AEM are Fumasep FKS-PET-75/FAS-PET-75. Ag/AgCl wires sense voltage;
they are not the shorted current-carrying shunt used in the two-compartment
experiment. The author v3 Methods identify the Au-Pt/ED membranes as 60 nm
pores with 50 nm metal contacts. This geometry is attributed to that version.

Measured variable: conductance from small-amplitude Ti-wire AC measurements,
with a nearby reference cell and external wall temperature probe. Publisher
SI Note 8 (PDF pp. 22-26) states: 5013 Hz, 20 mV excitation; 2 s acquisition,
last 0.8 s used; eight 0.1 s segments; within-record segment amplitude SD below
0.5%. Calibration compares four prepared KCl solutions against a calibrated
Mettler Toledo conductivity meter (147 and 1410 uS/cm standards). The plotted
calibration has R2=0.99021. That SD is not an inventory-error or species-assay
bound. The visible calibration fit need not pass through the origin; its R2
does not independently establish proportional concentration versus normalized
conductance. Evaporation, uptake, volume and complete species balances are not
provided in the source workbook.

Arithmetic in the supplied report is correct under its extra assumptions:
  0.5 * (1e-3 mol/L) * (0.63e-3 L) = 0.315 umol KCl pairs.
  0.315 umol / 37 h = 8.5135135 nmol KCl per elapsed hour.
Using the source minimum gives a conditional 0.3191106 umol and 8.6234209
nmol/elapsed hour. These require constant volume, KCl dominance, proportional
conductance/concentration, and negligible unaccounted inventories. They are
not measured collected salt and are not lithium throughput. Counting both
individual ions doubles the particle-mole count, not the moles of KCl pairs.

Two-compartment HCl demonstration (publisher SI Note 9, pp. 27-30): Au-Au,
1 mM HCl, 40 nm pores, area 0.32 cm2, 0.3 V, 125 Hz, duty 0.5. Shorted Ag/AgCl
meshes explicitly provide finite solid-electrode inventory. Resting voltage is
calibrated against HCl concentration ratio; the reported -7 mV offset is mapped
to ratio about 0.78. This is a different electrolyte, circuit and measurement
from the 50% KCl ED experiment. It is not a species-resolved, restored-auxiliary
lithium exchange demonstration.

KONG: USEFUL-OUTPUT BENCHMARK WITH EXPLICIT BOUNDARIES

Sources: L1 Results, Eq. 6, Methods and Table 1; L2 Tables S1/S5/S6; L3 Fig.5a,
Fig.6b, Fig.S8 and Fig.S9.

The process combines FePO4/LiFePO4 intercalation extraction, a second
purification stage, BMED, and collection/crystallization. Feed A and B are
synthetic geothermal brines matching selected Salton Sea compositions. A has
30 mM Li, 2.3 M Na, 690 mM Ca, 430 mM K, 1.5 mM Mg; B has 42 mM Li, 3.1 M Na,
1.07 M Ca, 540 mM K, 36 mM Fe and 47 mM Mn. Actual Simbol brine analysis in SI
Table S1c includes further species not present in B. Do not call this a tested
unpretreated acquired geothermal-brine operation.

The purification assay starts from a 90% Li/10% Na feed. The BMED methods use
10 mM LiCl and DI water, 5 mL/min, 2 V for 2 h, and continuous N2 to limit CO2.
Li is analyzed by ICP-OES, Cl by IC; OH is calculated from pH. The paper reports
99.6 +/- 0.2% LiOH solution purity, followed by collected LiOH.H2O solid and XRD
phase confirmation. Rotary evaporation at 80 C and freeze drying for 48 h are
part of the reported crystallization method. These staged tests establish much
more useful output than a ratchet current trace, but not a documented single
closed mass-balanced acquired-brine-to-certified-solid production run.

Exact bounded recalculation of L3 Fig.5a rows 3:15 under the paper's Eq. 6,
100*OH/(OH+Cl), gives:
  Unweighted mean: 99.4571241466%.
  Across-time-point sample SD: 0.0825235754 percentage points.
  Range: 99.3494863110% to 99.5834843203%.
The time-point SD is not experimental uncertainty. The averaging/replicate
records used to obtain 99.6 +/- 0.2% are not specified by those figure cells.
Do not conclude fraud, failed chemistry, or a universal purity bound from this
limited difference. Equally, do not independently certify >99.5% for every
sample or all trace contaminants in dry solid. Eq. 6 distinguishes OH from Cl;
XRD confirms a phase, not every impurity limit. The paper's author-reported
battery-grade claim must remain attributed and scoped.

Cost replay: L3 Fig.6b B4:G4 sums to 4.59941 USD/kg LiOH.H2O with an ASSUMED
0.5-year electrode lifespan. Rows for 0.2 and 1 year sum to 10.41966 and 2.71663
USD/kg. Table 1 baseline: extraction efficiency 18%, Li conversion 25%,
SECextraction 4 kWh/kg Li, SECBMED 2.45 kWh/kg LiOH.H2O, electricity 0.07 USD/kWh,
IEM life 8 years, plant life 20 years. This is TEA, not measured plant economics
or a demonstrated 0.5-year electrode lifetime. It includes modeled electrodes,
membranes, replacements, hydraulics, controls, labor, maintenance and washing.
Do not silently assume the laboratory evaporation/freeze-drying and every
feed pretreatment/product certification cost are included in that number.

SAME-REGIME GATES STILL MISSING

The records cannot be combined into a Li/Na device specification. Grossman
KCl bipotentiostatic reversal, Kautz Au-Au/HCl electrical efficiency, Kautz
Au-Pt/KCl ED depletion, and Kong staged lithium extraction/product are distinct
specimens, electrolytes, boundary circuits and outputs. No ratchet source
provides signed Li, Na and Cl fluxes in one mixture, auxiliary inventories,
mixed-waveform prediction/validation, product isolation, reset, and repeated
closed cycles. Kautz SI Note 12 additionally reports Au/Al dissolution, contact
restructuring and delamination; its ICP-MS statement of about 35 ng/mL Au/Al
after a separate exposure cannot be transferred into a closed impurity budget
for another device or mixture.

The defensible physical result is experimentally observed driven KCl depletion
and electrical ratchet control, with a substantial raw-source acquisition and
reanalysis improvement here. Programmable near-zero-chloride Li/Na exchange,
historical novelty and exceptional technological advantage remain UNESTABLISHED.
