## Result of the investigation

I obtained a **structural theorem about the strength of entropy-proof methods**:

> **Maximum-entropy marginal completion adds no new inequalities beyond the closed consequence cone of iterated conditional copying.**
>
> This includes the generalized construction that preserves several overlapping copies of an original entropy profile.

The qualification **closed consequence cone** is essential. The argument gives arbitrarily accurate finite-copy certificates and an exact mathematical implication after taking a limit. It does **not** establish that every maximum-entropy certificate has an exact, finite-copy certificate.

Below is the mathematical argument, including a quantitative version and the obstruction encountered when pursuing the stronger conclusion. I have not established the independently major breakthrough specified in the mission, and originality of the structural theorem remains unverified.

## 2. Mathematical setting

Let \(E\) be a finite set. A polymatroid is a function

\[
h:2^E\longrightarrow \mathbb R_{\ge 0}
\]

with \(h(\varnothing)=0\), monotonicity, and submodularity:

\[
h(S)+h(T)\ge h(S\cup T)+h(S\cap T).
\]

These are the Shannon constraints on an abstract entropy profile. Write

\[
I_h(A;B\mid C)
=
h(A\cup C)+h(B\cup C)-h(C)-h(A\cup B\cup C).
\]

For disjoint \(A,B,C\), this quantity is nonnegative.

### Conditional copying

Let \(A,C\subseteq E\) be disjoint and let \(A'\) be a fresh, labelled copy of \(A\). A conditional-copy extension of \(h\) is a polymatroid \(\widetilde h\) on \(E\cup A'\) satisfying:

\[
\widetilde h|_E=h,
\]

\[
\widetilde h|_{A'\cup C}\cong h|_{A\cup C},
\]

and

\[
I_{\widetilde h}(A';E\setminus C\mid C)=0.
\]

The marginal equality means equality on **every subset** of the indicated coordinates, not merely equality of total rank.

For actual random variables, such an extension is obtained by sampling a fresh copy of \(A\) conditionally on \(C\), independently of the remaining variables given \(C\).

### The marginal-completion rule

Fix a family \(\mathcal F\subseteq 2^E\) of protected subsets.

A partition

\[
E=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}C\mathbin{\dot\cup}B
\]

*separates* \(\mathcal F\) when every protected set lies entirely in \(A\cup C\) or entirely in \(B\cup C\).

The MAXE rule asks for a new profile \(g\) such that

\[
g|_F=h|_F\qquad(F\in\mathcal F)
\]

and

\[
I_g(A;B\mid C)=0
\]

for every separating partition. This is the entropy-profile rule in Csirmaz’s formulation; it does not specify every feature of the underlying maximum-entropy distribution. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12439v2)

That distinction matters: **the theorem below concerns this precise rule, not every conceivable use of maximum-entropy optimization.**

## 3. The central theorem

### Theorem — Closed copy-stable classes admit simultaneous marginal completion

For each finite ground set \(E\), let \(\mathcal C_E\) be a class of polymatroids. Assume that the family \(\mathcal C\) is:

1. closed in the ordinary finite-dimensional topology;
2. preserved by restricting and relabelling coordinates;
3. closed under conditional copying, with the extension required to belong to the corresponding class \(\mathcal C_{E\cup A'}\).

Then, for every \(h\in\mathcal C_E\) and every protected family \(\mathcal F\), there is \(g\in\mathcal C_E\) such that

\[
g|_F=h|_F\qquad(F\in\mathcal F),
\]

\[
I_g(A;B\mid C)=0
\]

for **all** partitions separating \(\mathcal F\), and additionally

\[
g(E)\ge h(E).
\]

No representability, probability distribution, convexity, or differentiability assumption on \(\mathcal C\) is needed.

### Proof

First add all singletons to \(\mathcal F\). This does not change which partitions separate the family.

Consider the feasible set

\[
K=
\left\{
g\in\mathcal C_E:
g|_F=h|_F\text{ for every }F\in\mathcal F
\right\}.
\]

It is nonempty because it contains \(h\). It is closed by assumption.

Moreover, fixing the singleton ranks gives

\[
0\le g(S)\le\sum_{i\in S}h(\{i\})
\qquad(S\subseteq E).
\]

Thus \(K\) is bounded and consequently compact. Choose \(g\in K\) maximizing \(g(E)\).

Suppose that a separating partition \(E=A\dot\cup C\dot\cup B\) has

\[
d:=I_g(A;B\mid C)>0.
\]

Take a conditional-copy extension of \(A\) over \(C\), inside \(\mathcal C\). Restrict it to \(A'\cup B\cup C\), and relabel \(A'\) as \(A\). Call the resulting profile \(g'\).

The copy properties give

\[
g'|_{A\cup C}=g|_{A\cup C},
\qquad
g'|_{B\cup C}=g|_{B\cup C}.
\]

Every protected set lies in one of these two sides, so \(g'\in K\).

The copy independence also implies

\[
I_{g'}(A;B\mid C)=0.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\begin{aligned}
g'(E)
&=g(A\cup C)+g(B\cup C)-g(C)\\
&=g(E)+I_g(A;B\mid C)\\
&=g(E)+d.
\end{aligned}
\]

This contradicts maximality of \(g(E)\). Hence every required conditional mutual information is zero.

Finally, \(h\in K\), so the maximizing \(g\) satisfies \(g(E)\ge h(E)\). ∎

### What the mechanism actually is

The decisive identity is

\[
\boxed{
\text{increase in total rank}
=
\text{conditional-independence defect removed}.
}
\]

A single scalar objective therefore enforces all the separator conditions simultaneously.

The familiar maximum-entropy argument for distributions is not new. The contribution being investigated is that **the same argument works inside any closed class that can perform conditional copying internally**. It does not require actual distributions.

That observation becomes more consequential when applied to the maximal class compatible with finite copy proofs.

## 4. Applying the theorem to the entire copy hierarchy

For precision, a *finite-copy program* here permits Shannon constraints, finitely many conditional-copy steps, and ordinary deterministic coordinate operations such as duplication, grouping, restriction, and relabelling. **Taking a limit is not counted as a finite-copy program.**

For a program \(P\) starting on \(E\), let

\[
Q_P(E)
\]

be the cone of initial profiles admitting a polymatroidal realization of that program.

Each \(Q_P(E)\) is a polyhedral cone: the program introduces finitely many rank coordinates, Shannon inequalities, and homogeneous linear equalities, after which the auxiliary coordinates are projected out.

Define

\[
\mathcal K_E=\bigcap_P Q_P(E).
\]

Thus \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) means:

> Every prescribed finite copy construction can be realized starting from \(h\).

This is stronger than merely admitting one copy, or all copies up to some fixed depth.

The important point is to prove that \(\mathcal K\) itself admits copies **whose resulting profiles remain in \(\mathcal K\)**. That does not follow by swapping quantifiers without justification.

### Lemma — A copy can be chosen consistently with every finite continuation

The family \(\mathcal K\) is closed and stable under conditional copying.

### Proof

Closedness follows because each \(Q_P(E)\) is closed.

Now fix \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) and a desired first copy step. Let \(W\) be the set of possible polymatroidal witnesses for that first step.

The set \(W\) is nonempty. All original singleton ranks are fixed, and each copied singleton has the rank of its source. Subadditivity therefore bounds every witness coordinate. Consequently, \(W\) is compact.

For each finite continuation program \(R\), let

\[
W_R\subseteq W
\]

be the first-step witnesses that admit continuation \(R\). This is closed: the relevant extension constraints form a finite polyhedral system, and their projection onto the first-witness coordinates is polyhedral.

The family \(\{W_R\}\) has the finite-intersection property. Indeed, take finitely many continuations \(R_1,\ldots,R_s\). Perform the required first copy and then run the continuations using disjoint fresh auxiliary labels.

When processing a later continuation, requiring a new copy to be independent of all previously introduced variables is stronger than its original requirement. Restricting back to that continuation therefore preserves the required copy constraints.

This combined construction is itself a finite-copy program. Since \(h\in\mathcal K_E\), it has a realization. Its first-step witness lies in

\[
W_{R_1}\cap\cdots\cap W_{R_s}.
\]

Compactness now gives

\[
\bigcap_R W_R\ne\varnothing.
\]

A witness in this intersection admits every finite continuation and hence belongs to \(\mathcal K\). ∎

Restriction and relabelling are inherited by running a program while retaining any unused original coordinates, then restricting its witness. Deterministic coordinate operations are included explicitly in the program definition; their rank pullbacks are fixed rather than freely chosen.

The central theorem therefore applies to \(\mathcal K\).

### Consequence — MAXE does not shrink the closed copy model

Every \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) has the prescribed MAXE completion **inside \(\mathcal K_E\)**.

For generalized MAXE, first duplicate coordinates according to the prescribed map and then protect the designated transversals. Applying the theorem preserves each transversal’s original profile and enforces every separating independence. This is precisely the duplication-plus-completion structure of GMAXE. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12439v2)

It follows that allowing MAXE and GMAXE, even interleaved with further copies, does not exclude any initial profile in \(\mathcal K\).

Equivalently, if \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{copy}}\) denotes the cone of finite-copy-provable linear inequalities, and \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{mixed}}\) additionally permits these MAXE rules, then

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{copy}}}
=
\overline{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{mixed}}}.
}
\]

The closures here are in the finite-dimensional space of inequality coefficients.

**This is the main structural conclusion.** The specific marginal-gluing rules cannot distinguish a profile that survives the entire finite-copy hierarchy.

## 5. What can be obtained with finitely many copies?

The closure statement has a concrete finite-certificate consequence.

Let

\[
u(h)=\sum_{i\in E}h(\{i\}).
\]

### Finite approximation theorem

Suppose a rational linear inequality

\[
L(h)\ge0
\]

is valid on \(\mathcal K_E\). Then, for every rational \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
\boxed{L(h)+\varepsilon u(h)\ge0}
\]

has a finite-copy certificate.

### Proof

Normalize by \(u(h)=1\), and consider

\[
B_\varepsilon=
\left\{
h\in\Gamma_E:
u(h)=1,\ L(h)\le-\varepsilon
\right\},
\]

where \(\Gamma_E\) is the Shannon cone.

This set is compact. It is disjoint from \(\mathcal K_E\). Hence every \(h\in B_\varepsilon\) fails some finite-copy program.

Because each program-feasibility cone is closed, the complements of these cones form an open cover of \(B_\varepsilon\). A finite subcover suffices.

Combine those finitely many programs using fresh auxiliary variables. The combined program excludes every member of \(B_\varepsilon\). By homogeneity, every initial profile admitting it satisfies

\[
L(h)+\varepsilon u(h)\ge0.
\]

The resulting implication is a finite rational polyhedral implication, so a rational Farkas certificate exists. The case \(u(h)=0\) is trivial because the polymatroid must then be identically zero. ∎

This also shows that any inequality **strictly positive on the normalized nonzero copy model** already has an exact finite-copy certificate. The unresolved issue concerns boundary inequalities.

### An explicit bound for a one-step MAXE certificate

The approximation need not always be obtained through compactness alone.

Suppose a one-step MAXE proof has been reduced to a rational certificate

\[
L(f)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_jd_j(g)\ge0,
\qquad \lambda_j\ge0,
\tag{1}
\]

valid whenever \(f,g\) satisfy the Shannon constraints and agree on the protected marginals. Here \(d_j(g)\) are the required separator conditional mutual informations. Setting all \(d_j(g)=0\) proves \(L(f)\ge0\).

Such a form is obtained from a Farkas certificate by moving the separator-equality terms to the left and using \(d_j\ge0\) to make their coefficients nonnegative.

Put

\[
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_j.
\]

For every positive integer \(T\), a fixed finite-copy construction proves

\[
\boxed{
L(f)+\frac{\Lambda}{T}\bigl(u(f)-f(E)\bigr)\ge0.
}
\tag{2}
\]

To prove this, build a tree of depth \(T\). At each node, include one conditional-copy replacement for each of the \(q\) separators.

Every node profile agrees with \(f\) on the protected marginals. If \(L(f)=-\delta<0\), equation (1) implies that at every node some separator has

\[
d_j(g)\ge\frac{\delta}{\Lambda}.
\]

Following such a child increases total rank by at least \(\delta/\Lambda\). After \(T\) levels,

\[
g_T(E)\ge f(E)+\frac{T\delta}{\Lambda}.
\]

But all singleton ranks remain fixed, so \(g_T(E)\le u(f)\). Rearrangement gives (2). If \(\Lambda=0\), the original certificate already proves \(L\ge0\) without enforcing the separator equalities.

This construction is finite but expensive. With \(m=|E|\), it uses at most

\[
m\sum_{t=0}^{T}q^t
\]

ground-set labels. A full Shannon formulation on \(N\) such labels has \(2^N-1\) rank coordinates.

Thus the result is **not** a polynomial-time entropy algorithm. It is a resource-explicit conversion from a one-step MAXE certificate to finite-copy certificates with controlled error.

## 6. The attempted stronger routes and their obstructions

### A. Exact finite proof does not follow from arbitrarily small error

It would be incorrect to erase the \(\varepsilon u(h)\) term merely because \(\varepsilon\) can be made arbitrarily small.

For an elementary illustration, consider

\[
x+\frac{1}{k}y\ge0,\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,
\]

together with \(y\ge0\).

All these inequalities imply \(x\ge0\). No finite subcollection does: if its largest index is \(K\), then

\[
y=1,\qquad x=-\frac{1}{2K}
\]

satisfies that finite subcollection while violating \(x\ge0\).

In coefficient space, \((1,0)\) belongs to the closure of the generated cone but not to its finite conic hull.

This is **not** an entropy counterexample. It identifies the logical gap in claiming that the conservativity theorem automatically supplies an exact finite-copy certificate.

To close that gap, one needs an additional property of the actual copy-proof cones—not general compactness or rationality.

### B. Small rank gain does not control the change of the whole profile

I also examined a possible convergence argument: repeatedly remove separator defects, hoping that a small total-rank increase forces a small change in the remaining entropy coordinates.

That stability premise is false.

Let \(U,V\) be independent fair bits, and define three coordinates

\[
A=U,\qquad B=U,\qquad C=(U,V).
\]

Then

\[
I(A;B\mid C)=0.
\]

Introduce \(A'=V\). At the entropy-profile level,

\[
(A',C)\quad\text{and}\quad(A,C)
\]

have identical profiles:

\[
H(A')=H(A)=1,\qquad
H(C)=2,\qquad
H(A',C)=H(A,C)=2.
\]

Moreover,

\[
I(A';A,B\mid C)=0,
\]

because all these variables are determined by \(C\). Thus this is a valid conditional-copy witness under the entropy constraints.

Nevertheless,

\[
H(A,B)=1,
\qquad
H(A',B)=2.
\]

The replacement changes a marginal entropy by one bit while its total-rank gain is zero.

Consequently, no general estimate of the form

\[
\|g'-g\|
\le C\,I_g(A;B\mid C)
\]

can hold for arbitrary entropy-level copy witnesses.

This falsifies that proposed stability mechanism. It does not rule out a more carefully selected convergent construction. The maximum-rank proof avoids needing such stability altogether.

### C. Quantized ranks explain one boundary—but do not remove it

When a class admits copy extensions with all ranks in a fixed lattice \(\eta\mathbb Z\), every positive defect costs at least \(\eta\). Repeatedly removing a positive defect must then terminate after at most

\[
\frac{u(h)-h(E)}{\eta}
\]

updates.

That gives exact finite completion in such a class.

The unresolved step is obtaining an analogous uniform quantization or finite-termination principle for general copy-consistent real profiles. Neither rational input data nor the existence of rational witnesses to individual finite programs establishes it.

## 7. Does this reach the foundational objective?

The decisive entropy-cone target remains

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_E
\stackrel{?}{=}
\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}},
}
\]

where \(\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}\) is the almost-entropic cone.

Actual entropy profiles admit conditional copying, so

\[
\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}\subseteq\mathcal K_E.
\]

The missing result is either a reverse-inclusion theorem or a rigorously constructed profile in

\[
\mathcal K_E\setminus\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}.
\]

The investigation established neither.

What it does establish, subject to the proof-system definitions above, is a restriction on one proposed route:

> **Repeatedly adding the specified MAXE and GMAXE separator rules cannot bridge a gap between the closed copy hierarchy and the almost-entropic cone. Those rules already hold throughout the copy hierarchy’s model class.**

This is more than a new inequality or another representable example: it concerns an entire method of generating inequalities. But it is not a characterization of information, a separation from the copy hierarchy, or an efficient general entropy procedure.

**The supported output is therefore a candidate original conservativity theorem, with the proof and finite approximation mechanism given above.** The targeted sources checked did not provide the same closed-hierarchy theorem, but that is not an exhaustive priority determination, and the argument has not received independent verification.

The mission’s stronger completion standard remains unmet for a specific mathematical reason: **the construction never leaves the class of profiles already compatible with arbitrary finite copying.** It identifies why this proposed new mechanism cannot, by itself, deliver the larger theorem.
