## Outcome

The strongest result from this investigation is a **closure-preserving uniformization theorem**: an increasing probability distribution on a union-closed family can be represented, to arbitrarily small local error, by the uniform distribution on a larger, genuinely union-closed family. The construction has a polynomial-time randomized realization for explicitly given rational probabilities.

This yields an exact consequence:

> **Uniform and increasing-weighted union-closed families have the same optimal universal element-frequency bound.**

It also identifies exactly which distributions resist every entropy-increasing self-coupling.

**I did not obtain the decisive half-frequency theorem, a better universal frequency constant, or an independently major application of the construction. External novelty is not established either.** What follows is a proved intermediate capability, an exact failed continuation, and the remaining mathematical obstruction—not a claim that the mission is complete.

## 1. Selected obstruction

The initial candidates were linear-size sample compression, dimension-free concentration for log-concave measures, and union-closed abundance. I selected the third because it exposes a particularly sharp mismatch between a simple algebraic operation and a difficult global consequence.

The target is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F\subseteq 2^{[n]}\text{ finite and union-closed},\quad
\mathcal F\ne\{\varnothing\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_i\frac{|\{A\in\mathcal F:i\in A\}|}{|\mathcal F|}
\ge\frac12.
}
\tag{UC}
\]

Here the family is nonempty. This is Frankl’s union-closed sets conjecture. Entropy growth under union has already produced constant lower bounds. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09055)

The independent-copy entropy method has a sharp threshold at

\[
\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.381966,
\]

and dependent coupling methods already go beyond it. Thus merely proposing correlated copies would not constitute a new mechanism. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08824) Recent work also explicitly constructs global couplings, rather than only coordinatewise protocols. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08291)

The question I pursued was more structural:

**Can one remove the uniform-distribution restriction without losing the actual union operation, distinctness of sets, or coordinate frequencies?**

An increasing-weighted version had already been proposed in the Polymath discussion. Its condition is

\[
p(A)\le p(B)\qquad(A\subseteq B,\ A,B\in\mathcal F).
\tag{1}
\]

The weighted formulation itself is therefore prior art, not a discovery here. [Gowers's Weblog](https://gowers.wordpress.com/2016/02/13/func3-further-strengthenings-and-variants/)

The construction below shows how to move back from that weighted formulation to actual uniform families.

## 2. Closure-preserving uniformization

All entropies and relative entropies below use natural logarithms, except where base two is explicitly indicated.

### Theorem 1

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a finite union-closed family with \(m\ge2\) members. Let \(p\) be a strictly positive probability distribution on \(\mathcal F\), increasing under inclusion as in (1).

For each integer \(k\ge1\), set

\[
b=\lceil\log_2m\rceil,
\qquad
L_k=2kb+1.
\]

There exists a family

\[
\mathcal U_k\subseteq\mathcal F^k
\]

that is closed under coordinatewise union and invariant under permutations of its \(k\) blocks, such that, for \(Q_k=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal U_k)\),

\[
\boxed{
Q_k(x)\le 4L_k\,p^{\otimes k}(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal F^k.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Moreover, for every \(1\le\ell\le k\),

\[
\boxed{
D\!\left((Q_k)_{1:\ell}\,\middle\|\,p^{\otimes\ell}\right)
\le
\frac{\ell}{k}\log(4L_k)
}
\tag{3}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}\!\left((Q_k)_{1:\ell},p^{\otimes\ell}\right)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{\ell\log(4L_k)}{2k}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, every fixed number of blocks converges jointly to independent samples from \(p\).

The construction is a product-likelihood superlevel family:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U_k=
\left\{(A_1,\ldots,A_k)\in\mathcal F^k:
\prod_{j=1}^k p(A_j)\ge\tau_k
\right\},
}
\tag{5}
\]

for an appropriately selected dyadic threshold \(\tau_k\).

### Proof

Write

\[
\mu=p^{\otimes k}.
\]

For \(X\sim\mu\), the nonnegative random variable

\[
Z=-\log_2\mu(X)
\]

satisfies

\[
\mathbb EZ=kH_2(p)\le k\log_2m\le kb.
\]

Markov’s inequality gives

\[
\Pr(Z\ge L_k)\le\frac{kb}{2kb+1}<\frac12.
\]

Thus more than half the probability lies among points satisfying

\[
\mu(x)>2^{-L_k}.
\]

Partition those points into the \(L_k\) bands

\[
B_j=
\left\{
x:2^{-(j+1)}<\mu(x)\le2^{-j}
\right\},
\qquad 0\le j<L_k.
\]

At least one band has

\[
\mu(B_j)\ge\frac1{2L_k}.
\tag{6}
\]

Choose such a \(j\), put \(\tau=2^{-(j+1)}\), and define

\[
\mathcal U=\{x:\mu(x)\ge\tau\},
\qquad N=|\mathcal U|.
\]

Every point of \(B_j\) has mass at most \(2\tau\), so

\[
\frac1{2L_k}
\le\mu(B_j)
\le2\tau|B_j|
\le2\tau N.
\]

Therefore

\[
\tau N\ge\frac1{4L_k}.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(Q=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal U)\) and \(x\in\mathcal U\),

\[
\frac{Q(x)}{\mu(x)}
=
\frac1{N\mu(x)}
\le\frac1{N\tau}
\le4L_k.
\]

This proves (2), and hence

\[
D(Q\|\mu)\le\log(4L_k).
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to establish closure and the local bounds.

If \(x=(A_1,\ldots,A_k)\) and \(y=(B_1,\ldots,B_k)\) belong to \(\mathcal U\), then inclusion-monotonicity gives

\[
p(A_j\cup B_j)\ge p(A_j)
\]

for every \(j\). Consequently,

\[
\mu(x\cup y)\ge\mu(x)\ge\tau.
\]

Thus \(\mathcal U\) is union-closed. Its permutation invariance follows immediately from the product defining \(\mu\).

For an exchangeable random vector \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), the conditional entropies

\[
h_j=H(X_j\mid X_1,\ldots,X_{j-1})
\]

are nonincreasing: conditioning reduces entropy, and exchangeability identifies the corresponding conditional distributions. Therefore

\[
H(X_1,\ldots,X_\ell)
\ge\frac{\ell}{k}H(X_1,\ldots,X_k).
\]

Since all one-block marginals coincide,

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(Q_{1:\ell}\|p^{\otimes\ell})
&=-H(Q_{1:\ell})-\ell\,\mathbb E_{Q_1}\log p(X_1)\\
&\le-\frac{\ell}{k}H(Q)-\ell\,\mathbb E_{Q_1}\log p(X_1)\\
&=\frac{\ell}{k}D(Q\|p^{\otimes k}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Combining this with (8) proves (3). Pinsker’s inequality gives (4). ∎

### Why these are actual set families

Encode a tuple by

\[
\Phi(A_1,\ldots,A_k)
=
\{(j,i)\in[k]\times[n]:i\in A_j\}.
\]

This map is injective and preserves unions. Hence \(\Phi(\mathcal U_k)\) is an ordinary union-closed family of **distinct sets** on \(kn\) elements.

There are no multiplicity weights and no extra selector element that becomes frequent merely because it records which copy was chosen.

### Scope of the operation

The underlying argument is slightly more general than union closure. For a finite binary operation \(f\), the same construction preserves closure whenever

\[
p(f(a,b))^2\ge p(a)p(b).
\]

Indeed, on product spaces this implies

\[
p^{\otimes k}(f^{\otimes k}(x,y))
\ge
\sqrt{p^{\otimes k}(x)p^{\otimes k}(y)},
\]

so every product-likelihood superlevel set is \(f\)-closed.

For union, this condition is precisely positive union-closed support together with increasing weights.

## 3. The construction is computational, not only existential

The first proof chooses a sufficiently populated likelihood band by pigeonhole. That choice can be made using sampling rather than enumeration.

Assume that \(\mathcal F\) is explicitly listed and its probabilities are rational, with numerators and denominators of at most \(B\) bits.

Fix \(k\) and a failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\). Write \(L=L_k\).

### Selecting the threshold

Draw independent samples from \(\mu=p^{\otimes k}\), count their occurrences in the \(L\) bands above, and select a band with the largest count. Samples below all the retained bands are ignored in this comparison.

There is a band of true probability at least \(1/(2L)\). Standard binomial tail bounds show that

\[
T=
\left\lceil
64L\log\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}
\right\rceil
\]

samples suffice to select, with probability at least \(1-\delta/2\), a band of true probability at least \(1/(4L)\).

For completeness, compare counts with \(3T/(8L)\). A band of probability at least \(1/(2L)\) falls below that count with probability at most

\[
e^{-T/(64L)}.
\]

A band of probability at most \(1/(4L)\) exceeds that count with probability at most

\[
e^{-T/(40L)}.
\]

A union bound establishes the claim.

For the selected threshold \(\tau\), the resulting family then satisfies

\[
\tau|\mathcal U|\ge\frac1{8L}.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus the preceding density and relative-entropy bounds hold with \(8L\) in place of \(4L\).

### Sampling uniformly without knowing the family’s cardinality

Use fresh samples \(x\sim\mu\). Reject when \(\mu(x)<\tau\). Otherwise accept with probability

\[
\frac{\tau}{\mu(x)}.
\]

For every \(x\in\mathcal U\),

\[
\Pr(\text{propose and accept }x)
=
\mu(x)\frac{\tau}{\mu(x)}
=
\tau.
\]

Therefore the accepted output is **exactly uniform on \(\mathcal U\)**. Its cardinality is never computed.

On a good threshold, the acceptance probability is at least \(1/(8L)\). Capping the rejection procedure at

\[
R=
\left\lceil8L\log\frac2\delta\right\rceil
\]

trials makes its additional failure probability at most \(\delta/2\). Combining threshold selection and capped rejection gives total failure probability at most \(\delta\). Conditional on any fixed returned threshold and successful rejection, the output distribution is exactly uniform on its specified family.

### Resource accounting

Membership in \(\mathcal U\) is checked by comparing a product of rational probabilities with a power of two. The integers involved have \(O(kB+L)\) bits; exponentially fine numerical approximations are unnecessary.

The preprocessing and sampling use

\[
O\!\left(kL\log\frac{L}{\delta}\right)
\]

draws from the original one-block distribution, and polynomially many bit operations in the explicit input size, \(k\), and \(\log(1/\delta)\). The family need not be enumerated or stored.

**The limitations are important.** The family itself may have exponentially many members. The theorem preserves local distributions, not total-variation closeness of the entire \(k\)-block law to \(p^{\otimes k}\). It does, however, provide the global domination bound (2), so an event exponentially unlikely under the product law remains exponentially unlikely under the lift, up to a polynomial factor.

This is a sampling and representation capability—not a polynomial-time procedure for listing an exponentially large family.

## 4. Exact consequence: increasing weights do not change the universal abundance constant

For \(c\in[0,1]\), consider two statements.

**Uniform abundance:** Every finite nontrivial union-closed family has an element occurring in at least a \(c\)-fraction of its members.

**Increasing-weight abundance:** For every probability distribution \(p\) with nontrivial union-closed support and increasing weights,

\[
\max_i\Pr_{A\sim p}(i\in A)\ge c.
\]

### Theorem 2

These statements are equivalent.

### Proof

The weighted statement immediately implies the uniform one.

For the converse, apply Theorem 1 to \(p\). Let

\[
r_k(i)=\Pr_{Q_k}(i\in A_1),
\qquad
p_i=\Pr_p(i\in A).
\]

The one-block bound gives, simultaneously for every \(i\),

\[
|r_k(i)-p_i|
\le
\varepsilon_k
:=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(4L_k)}{2k}}.
\]

Permutation invariance means that every lifted coordinate \((j,i)\) has frequency \(r_k(i)\). Applying uniform abundance to the actual lifted set family therefore yields

\[
c\le\max_i r_k(i)\le\max_i p_i+\varepsilon_k.
\]

Letting \(k\to\infty\) proves the weighted statement. ∎

**This is a universal equivalence across all finite families and ground-set sizes. It is not a claim that reweighting one fixed family cannot reduce its largest frequency.**

There is also a quantitative counterexample reduction. A weighted counterexample satisfying

\[
\max_i p_i\le c-\eta
\]

produces a finite uniform counterexample once the local error is smaller than \(\eta\). A block length of order

\[
k=
O\!\left(
\eta^{-2}\log\frac{b+1}{\eta}
\right),
\qquad b=\lceil\log_2m\rceil,
\]

suffices. The randomized construction above can find the corresponding compact family description with controlled failure probability.

The increasing-weight condition cannot simply be weakened to

\[
p(A\cup B)\ge\min\{p(A),p(B)\}.
\]

A September 2026 preprint constructs distributions satisfying that weaker condition, with zero weight at the empty set, for which every element has arbitrarily small weighted frequency. That result concerns a different weighted class and does not contradict Theorem 2. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25101v1)

## 5. Exactly when can coupling increase entropy?

The uniformization result becomes more informative when combined with a complete characterization of entropy stagnation.

For a probability law \(p\) on subsets of a finite ground set, define

\[
\Delta(p)=
\max_{\substack{X\sim p\\Y\sim p}}
\bigl[H(X\cup Y)-H(p)\bigr],
\]

where the maximum is over all joint distributions with those marginals.

The diagonal coupling \(X=Y\) shows that \(\Delta(p)\ge0\).

### Theorem 3

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(p)=0
\iff
\operatorname{supp}p\text{ is union-closed and }
p(A)\le p(B)\text{ whenever }A\subseteq B
\text{ in the support}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

### Proof: the stated conditions prevent entropy gain

Let \(Z=X\cup Y\), with law \(q\). Union closure puts \(Z\) in the support of \(p\), while monotonicity gives

\[
\log p(Z)\ge\log p(X).
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(q)+D(q\|p)
&=-\mathbb E\log p(Z)\\
&\le-\mathbb E\log p(X)\\
&=H(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(H(q)\le H(p)\).

### Proof: a failure of either condition gives an explicit improving coupling

Start with the diagonal coupling.

If \(A,B\) have positive mass but \(C=A\cup B\) has zero mass, subtract \(\epsilon\) from the cells \((A,A)\) and \((B,B)\), and add \(\epsilon\) to \((A,B)\) and \((B,A)\). Both marginals remain unchanged.

The union law loses mass \(\epsilon\) at each of \(A,B\) and gains \(2\epsilon\) at \(C\). Writing \(a=p(A)\), \(b=p(B)\), its entropy derivative is

\[
\log\frac{(a-\epsilon)(b-\epsilon)}{4\epsilon^2}.
\]

It is positive throughout

\[
0<\epsilon\le\frac{\min(a,b)}4.
\]

Thus a finite, explicitly specified swap increases entropy.

Alternatively, suppose \(A\subsetneq B\) but \(p(A)>p(B)\). The same coupling modification transfers output mass \(\epsilon\) from \(A\) to \(B\). Choosing

\[
0<\epsilon=
\min\left\{
\frac{p(B)}2,\,
\frac{p(A)-p(B)}4
\right\}
\]

moves the two probabilities toward equality without crossing it, and strictly increases entropy. ∎

Given an explicitly listed distribution, this also decides whether an entropy-improving coupling exists: inspect support closure and comparable pairs. No optimization over the full coupling polytope is required.

### Consequence for the decisive objective

Combining Theorems 2 and 3 gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{Frankl’s conjecture}
\iff
\left[
\max_i\Pr(i\in X)<\frac12
\Longrightarrow
\Delta(p)>0
\right],
}
\tag{11}
\]

with the law concentrated on \(\varnothing\) excluded.

This identifies the remaining difficulty precisely. It is not merely a failure to search a rich enough coupling class. **The distributions that resist every coupling are exactly the increasing-weighted union-closed distributions, and their universal abundance problem is equivalent to the original one.**

Equation (11) is an exact reformulation, not a proof of either side.

## 6. A continuation that failed: projected maximum-entropy repair

I next tried to combine upward entropy improvements on coordinate projections.

For \(J\subseteq[n]\), let \(p_J\) be the projected law and \(\mathcal F_J\) its support. Define

\[
R(J)=
\max H(Y),
\]

where \(Y\) is supported on \(\mathcal F_J\) and can be coupled with \(X\sim p_J\) so that \(X\subseteq Y\).

The intended route required this repaired entropy profile to satisfy the usual submodularity inequality. It does not.

Take

\[
\mathcal F=
\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\},\{a,b,c\}\},
\]

with the uniform distribution.

On all three coordinates, the original law already maximizes entropy on its five-point support, so

\[
R(abc)=\log5.
\]

On coordinate \(c\), the initial probability of presence is \(1/5\), which can be increased to \(1/2\). Thus

\[
R(c)=\log2.
\]

On \(ac\), the projected support is the chain

\[
\varnothing\subset\{a\}\subset\{a,c\},
\]

with probabilities

\[
\left(\frac25,\frac25,\frac15\right).
\]

The uniform law on those three points stochastically dominates this law: it can be obtained by moving mass upward. Therefore

\[
R(ac)=\log3,
\qquad
R(bc)=\log3.
\]

Consequently,

\[
R(ac)+R(bc)=\log9
<
\log10=R(abc)+R(c).
\]

This is an exact counterexample to the required submodularity. **The projected entropy-repair quantities cannot be assembled as an ordinary entropy rank function.** The failed step cannot be restored by changing constants.

## 7. The strongest surviving proof obligation

The representation theorem makes a global mass-transport formulation available, but I did not establish its universal feasibility.

For a fixed family \(\mathcal F\), seek nonnegative coordinate weights

\[
\lambda_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\lambda_i=1,
\]

nonnegative upward flows \(f_{AB}\) for \(A\subsetneq B\) in \(\mathcal F\), and nonnegative residuals \(s_A\), satisfying

\[
\boxed{
2\sum_{i\in A}\lambda_i-1
=
s_A
+
\sum_{B\subsetneq A}f_{BA}
-
\sum_{A\subsetneq C}f_{AC}
\qquad(A\in\mathcal F).
}
\tag{12}
\]

Such a certificate would prove the increasing-weighted half-frequency statement. Indeed, multiply by an increasing probability \(p(A)\) and sum:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\sum_i\lambda_i\Pr_p(i\in A)-1
&=
\sum_A s_Ap(A)
+\sum_{A\subsetneq B}f_{AB}\bigl(p(B)-p(A)\bigr)\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

At least one coordinate must therefore have probability at least \(1/2\).

For a fixed family, finite-dimensional minimax and the dual cone of nonnegative increasing functions show that this certificate is also necessary for its universal increasing-weighted statement. This is consistent with the earlier Polymath linear-programming formulation; it is not an independently new target introduced here. [Gowers's Weblog](https://gowers.wordpress.com/2016/02/13/func3-further-strengthenings-and-variants/)

On a full Boolean cube, the certificate is explicit: put flow \(\lambda_i\) on each edge \(A\to A\cup\{i\}\) and take all residuals zero. The divergence is exactly

\[
\sum_{i\in A}\lambda_i-\sum_{i\notin A}\lambda_i
=
2\lambda(A)-1.
\]

For a general union-closed family, those coordinate-addition edges need not exist. Replacing them with available unions creates merges and unequal multiplicities. **A general construction that balances those merges is the surviving mathematical requirement.** Neither the uniformization theorem nor the entropy characterization supplies it.

## 8. Assessment against the mission

The established output is a **specific representation and sampling capability**: increasing weighted laws can be converted into uniform union-closed models while preserving local statistics, with explicit error bounds and without uncharged enumeration or rare-event conditioning. The accompanying arguments give an exact universal weighted–unweighted equivalence and a complete characterization of entropy stagnation under self-union couplings.

The prior-art check limits what can be claimed. Increasing-weighted formulations and coupling approaches predate this investigation. Zargar also developed non-uniform versions for particular product-weighted distributions; that work is distinct from the arbitrary increasing-weight conversion proved above, but it rules out treating “non-uniform union-closed distributions” as a new subject. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19338v2) I have not established priority for the exact uniformization theorem or its combination of consequences, and these proofs have not been independently reviewed or formally verified.

**The decisive objective remains open in this investigation.** The construction removes a genuine representational restriction, but it does not yet provide the structural inequality needed to reach \(1/2\). The five-set counterexample eliminates the projected-submodularity continuation; the remaining route requires a universal structural construction such as (12), rather than another reformulation or another restricted entropy calculation.
