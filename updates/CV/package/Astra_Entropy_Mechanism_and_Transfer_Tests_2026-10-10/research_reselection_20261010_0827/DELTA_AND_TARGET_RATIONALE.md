Source: [`COORDINATOR_COMPLEX_LY.txt`](https://github.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/blob/f20b87499f0a44bfffeb182d6c162a5e4716cb39/updates/CT/package/work/COORDINATOR_COMPLEX_LY.txt), SHA-256 `bb09e4c18861995c61953abca717f9e3318ebf83b8eda4837c513862d8047a46`.

when EVERY whole n-qubit factor lies in LY(r), r>1. Lines 162–183 explicitly separate this from efficient promise recognition, boundary-vector computation, scalar normalization and physical application. Idle identity wires have radius exactly one. Low global rank does not bound the spatial tensor-network ranks of either boundary.

Three possible native interfaces were screened:

1. **Dense matrices / logarithmic-width strips.** A sufficient, efficiently checkable zero-free certificate is positive real K_00 exceeding the sum of absolute values of all other coefficients weighted by r to their multilinear degrees. It follows directly from the triangle inequality. With dense dimension polynomial in input size, or strip width O(log input size), boundary propagation and scalar normalization are already direct polynomial-cost transfer computations. The new contraction rate is a bound, not an identified qualitative computational improvement.
2. **Succinct bounded-arity all-wire layers.** Locally certified LY blocks tensor into a certified layer; this genuinely addresses recognition for that restricted class. However successive boundary vectors can require exponentially many coefficients. N638 gives no spatial truncation algorithm, error estimate for such truncation, or efficient normalized-observable estimator. Calling the local gates efficient does not pay for their action on the many-body boundary. A quantum application also needs a success-amplitude or alternative preparation bound.
3. **Bounded-degree zero-free partition functions.** Existing deterministic approximation frameworks are a necessary baseline. Patel–Regts already converts appropriate graph-polynomial coefficient access plus zero-freeness into polynomial approximation. Yao–Yin–Zhang gives a bounded-degree local quantum-Hamiltonian version under temperature zero-freeness. The Ising Lee–Yang algorithm already covers bounded-degree ferromagnetic systems off the zero-field circle. Boundary-polynomial LY zero-freeness is NOT automatically temperature zero-freeness, nor does it establish the coefficient interface. Thus these sources neither subsume every N638 input nor supply its missing algorithm. No concrete class outside these baselines with all interfaces acquired was found.

The precise indispensable next construction, if revisited, would be an explicit succinct family and an algorithm for a specified normalized quantity (for example Tr(OM)/Tr(M)) that obtains all certificates, boundary access and normalization with proved polynomial bit/runtime/sample cost. It must avoid dense 2^n vectors and inverse exponentially small success amplitudes, and demonstrate an actual advantage on the same input/error contract over transfer and applicable zero-free expansions. **No such construction is currently supplied; do not launch on the conditional theorem alone.**

## Decisive failed thermal transfer: exact check

Let K=[diag(1,a)]^tensor n with a=1/4 and r=3/2. Each factor has polynomial product_i(1+a z_i w_i), strictly zero-free in radius r because a r^2=9/16<1. After T factors, M=[diag(1,b)]^tensor n, b=a^T.

Its best rank-one relative operator error is b. But its normalized Gibbs-like state has trace distance

D(M/Tr M, |0^n><0^n|)=1-(1+b)^(-n).

For n=4096, T=4, the operator error is exactly 1/256 while trace distance is approximately 0.999999883902; the lower bound D>999/1000 was checked with exact rational arithmetic. The omitted partition mass is exponential in n at fixed b. Splitting a fixed total imaginary time into more slices leaves the total b unchanged. Therefore small relative operator error alone cannot establish normalized finite-temperature sampling, partition-function accuracy or state preparation.

This is an elementary semantic counterexample, not a claimed novel result, not an objection to Theorem B, and not a lower bound on every algorithm for local observables. Product systems themselves are easy. It is a decisive rejection of the direct proposed inference.
