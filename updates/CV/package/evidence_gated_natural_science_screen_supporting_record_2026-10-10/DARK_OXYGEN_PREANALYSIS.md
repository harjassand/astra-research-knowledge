# Prospective test: dark oxygen at the abyssal seafloor

Date: 2026-10-10 (UTC)

## Evidence status

This protocol was frozen before opening the primary dark-oxygen paper or its data. The hypothesis is not selected as a discovery. Any published outcomes consulted after this timestamp are exploratory context, not prospective confirmation.

## Mechanism under test

Mixed-valence metal-oxide nodules act as natural galvanic cells. Their electrochemical potential drives water oxidation at one surface and a coupled reduction reaction elsewhere, producing molecular oxygen in darkness.

## Observable and prediction

The primary observable is the time-integrated number of moles of newly produced O2 in anoxic, dark nodule incubations, accompanied by simultaneous current/charge measurements and independent gas/isotope measurements. For water oxidation, charge conservation requires

`n(O2) = Q/(4 F)`

for the charge specifically routed through the oxygen-evolving reaction; parasitic currents make this an upper bound on O2 from a measured total charge. The ideal water-splitting reaction also predicts `n(H2)/n(O2)=2`, while the minimum reversible electrical work is `4 F × 1.229 V` per mole of O2 at standard conditions. Real seawater cells require additional overpotential and incur losses. If the charge is internal to the nodule, the corresponding electron transfer must instead be bounded by a measured change in nodule oxidation state; an unmeasured internal circuit cannot be assumed.

## Alternatives

1. Residual/trapped oxygen, air contamination, leakage, or pressure-chamber artifacts.
2. Oxygen transport from ambient seawater or a sediment microenvironment rather than local production.
3. Non-electrolytic release from stored oxidants or oxygen-bearing phases (for example, peroxide decomposition), with no net water oxidation.
4. Biological oxygen production or biologically mediated release in darkness.

## Nuisance assumptions

Closed-system integrity, pressure and temperature stability, blank subtraction, sensor calibration and cross-sensitivity, gas partitioning between water and headspace, microbial viability, spatial heterogeneity of nodule surfaces, current-capture efficiency, and the initial/final oxidation-state inventory of the nodules.

## Falsification criterion

Reject the galvanic water-splitting mechanism for a reported incubation if independently calibrated O2 exceeds the maximum Faradaic amount permitted by the complete measured electron-transfer budget (external charge plus quantified solid-state redox change), or if the isotopic/chemical source of oxygen is inconsistent with water oxidation. A result that merely correlates O2 with nodule presence, or reports a local voltage without a charge/energy budget, does not pass. Select the mechanism only if an independent primary record closes both electron and energy budgets, excludes the four alternatives with controls, and shows the predicted O2 source and coupled reduction product within uncertainty.

## Scope boundary

No physical experiment is authorized in this task. This is a test of accessible published primary evidence and its conservation constraints. An unclosed budget is a reason to reject the claimed mechanism or mark the evidence non-identifying, not to infer a different mechanism.
