# Primary sources checked

Checked on 2026-10-10 UTC. The sources below are journal papers or author-posted primary research manuscripts. Claims from abstracts were checked against article text where available. The list is selective, not an exhaustive novelty search.

## Physical burst and common-noise evidence

1. **Acharya et al., “Quantum error correction below the surface code threshold,” Nature 638 (2025).** [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08449-y). Evidence used: distance-3/5/7 surface-code memory result; a repetition-code floor around (10^{-10}) attributed to correlated bursts, initially with unknown origin; paper was corrected in April 2026, so no precision beyond the stated noise-floor observation is claimed.
2. **Kurilovich et al., “Correlated Error Bursts in a Gap-Engineered Superconducting Qubit Array,” Phys. Rev. X 16, 021025 (2026), arXiv:2506.18228v1.** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1103/1bl4-b2f7), [pinned author manuscript v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18228v1). Evidence used: 60 monitored qubits from a 72-qubit Willow array; quasi-static, nonuniform QP frequency shifts up to 3 MHz, about 1-ms recovery; initial T1 burst about 10 µs; modified repetition-code schedule reduced shift sensitivity and shortened detection bursts. The manuscript says this echo strategy may not transfer directly to other codes.
3. **Li et al., “Cosmic-ray-induced correlated errors in superconducting qubit array,” Nature Communications 16, 4677 (2025), arXiv:2402.04245v3.** [Pinned author manuscript v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04245v3). Evidence used: 63-qubit experiment directly detects QP bursts and correlates a subset with muons; gamma rays also contribute; measured burst and recovery rates are device-specific.
4. **Wu et al., “Mitigating cosmic ray-like correlated events with a modular quantum processor,” Phys. Rev. Applied 24, 044022 (2025), arXiv:2505.15919v1.** [Journal article](https://journals.aps.org/prapplied/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.24.044022), [pinned author manuscript v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15919v1). Evidence used: a motherboard plus two two-qubit daughterboard modules; >85% event coincidence within a module and about 2% between modules. This is a four-qubit physics study, not an outer-code demonstration.
5. **Hjerpekjøn et al., “A quasiparticle-protected superconducting qubit,” arXiv:2610.12227v1 (submitted 2026-10-08).** [Pinned author manuscript v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12227v1). Evidence used: one Nb/Au transmon with gap-engineered Al junctions; >10× optical-power tolerance before excess relaxation and no resolved QP frequency shift in the tested range; residual dephasing and SPAM degradation remain. It is not a QEC-array or cosmic-ray-impact experiment.
6. **Rojas-Arias et al., “Scaling of silicon spin qubits under correlated noise,” arXiv:2603.03051v1 (2026).** [Pinned author manuscript v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03051v1). Evidence used: five-qubit silicon-array correlation characterization; global magnetic-field drift and short-range, tunable charge-noise correlations; logical-code calculations assume ideal syndrome extraction and omit relaxation/hardware faults.

## Existing theoretical boundaries and prior mechanisms

7. **Bombín, “Resilience to Time-Correlated Noise in Quantum Computation,” Phys. Rev. X 6, 041034 (2016).** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.6.041034), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.03679). Evidence used: single-shot quantum-memory threshold under spatially local stochastic noise with arbitrary temporal correlations.
8. **Aharonov, Kitaev, and Preskill, “Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation with Long-Range Correlated Noise,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 96, 050504 (2006).** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.96.050504), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0510231). Evidence used: weak pair-coupled system-bath terms with summable spatial strength; algebraic decay faster than dimension suffices. This does not permit constant-strength global replacer events.
9. **Kempe, Bacon, Lidar, and Whaley, “Theory of Decoherence-Free, Fault-Tolerant, Universal Quantum Computation,” Phys. Rev. A 63, 042307 (2001).** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.63.042307), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0004064). Evidence used: universal computation can be done within DFS/subsystem encodings for specified collective noise symmetries.

## Pivot: decoder/controller latency

10. **Chamberland, Iyer, and Poulin, “Fault-tolerant quantum computing in the Pauli or Clifford frame with slow error diagnostics,” Quantum 2, 43 (2018).** [Journal article](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2018-01-04-43/), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06662). Evidence used: a fault-tolerant strategy for slow syndrome measurement or decoding using Pauli/Clifford frames.
11. **Skoric et al., “Parallel window decoding enables scalable fault tolerant quantum computation,” Nature Communications 14, 7040 (2023).** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42482-1). Evidence used: parallel-window decoding handles latency/throughput in surface-code computation.
12. **Kurman et al., “Controller-decoder system requirements derived by executing a non-Clifford circuit,” Quantum (2026).** [Journal article](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-07-22-2170/), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00289). Evidence used: a primary system-level analysis reports that controller-decoder closed-loop latency must remain within tens of microseconds for its compiled workload/model.
