Result: a sharp, constructive criterion for counting continuity
The strongest outcome is a finite criterion that determines when approximating a reversible Markov kernel controls a specified nonlinear graph count.
The criterion has both directions. A positive certificate gives a quantitative continuity bound. A negative certificate produces an actual nonnegative, symmetric kernel with every row integral exactly one—not merely an abstract entropy obstruction.
It also yields an explicit irrational threshold:
\[
\boxed{
p_c(\text{two triangles sharing one vertex})
=
\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2
\approx 2.5615528128.
}
\]
Below this threshold, kernels can converge strongly in \(L^p\) and in operator norm while the corresponding graph count diverges.
The candidate original contribution is the sharp equivalence and its counterexample construction. The proof uses a published normal-polymatroid theorem, credited below. I have not established historical priority or obtained independent review. This is a general analytic classification, not a claim to have resolved a major named conjecture.
1. The obstruction
Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph, with \(n\) vertices and \(m\geq1\) edges. For a nonnegative symmetric kernel \(W:[0,1]^2\to[0,\infty)\), define
\[
t(H,W)
=
\int_{[0,1]^V}
\prod_{uv\in E}W(x_u,x_v)\,dx_V.
\]
Assume exact normalization:
\[
\int_0^1W(x,y)\,dy=1
\qquad\text{for almost every }x.
\]
Thus \(W\) describes a reversible Markov transition density with uniform stationary measure. Unlike an ordinary bounded graphon, \(W\) may take values much larger than one. Graph densities in Markov spaces are an established framework, not introduced here. arXiv
The question is:
How much moment control makes a global approximation of \(W\) sufficient to approximate \(t(H,W)\)?

For unrestricted \(L^p\) graphons, the classical counting lemma works when \(p>\Delta(H)\), and the unrestricted assertion fails at and below the maximum degree. Exact marginal normalization removes some of those counterexamples, but it does not eliminate concentration. arXiv
The surviving difficulty is multiscale concentration: different edges of the same pattern can exploit different large-value scales. Tracking only maximum degree, average density, or a single concentration scale misses this interaction.
The representation developed here records the probability cost of concentrating every subset of the pattern’s vertices.
2. The concentration number
A set function \(d:2^V\to\mathbb R\) is admissible when \(d(\varnothing)=0\), it is increasing, and it is supermodular:
\[
d(S)+d(T)
\leq
d(S\cup T)+d(S\cap T).
\]
For a fixed \(p>1\), define \(\gamma_H(p)\) by the following linear program:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_H(p)=\min\quad &d(V)\\[2mm]
\text{subject to}\quad
&k_e\geq0,\qquad \sum_{e\in E}k_e=1,\\
&d(\{u,v\})\geq p\,k_{uv},\\
&d(\{u,v\})-d(\{u\})\geq k_{uv},\\
&d(\{u,v\})-d(\{v\})\geq k_{uv}
\qquad(uv\in E),\\
&d\text{ is increasing and supermodular.}
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(k_e\) represents an edge’s amplification scale. The quantity \(d(S)\) represents the probability cost of concentrating the vertices in \(S\). Normalizing \(\sum k_e=1\) makes the objective a cost per unit total amplification.
For fixed rational \(p\), this is an ordinary rational linear program. It has
\[
2^n-1+m
\]
variables, and \(O(n^2 2^n+m)\) elementary inequalities suffice.
The consequential part is that its optimum controls the original analytic problem exactly.
3. The theorem
For \(p>1\) and \(B>1\), let
\[
\mathcal M_{p,B}
=
\left\{
W\geq0:
W=W^\top,\ 
\int W(x,y)\,dy=1,\ 
\|W\|_p\leq B
\right\}.
\]
Use the cut norm
\[
\|F\|_\square
=
\sup_{S,T}
\left|
\int_{S\times T}F(x,y)\,dx\,dy
\right|.
\]
Theorem — entropy-deficit completion
If \(\gamma_H(p)>1\), every \(H\)-density is finite on \(\mathcal M_{p,B}\), and
\[
\boxed{
|t(H,U)-t(H,W)|
\leq
C_{H,p,B}\,
\|U-W\|_\square^{
\frac{\gamma_H(p)-1}{m-1+\gamma_H(p)-1}
}
}
\tag{2}
\]
for cut-norm differences at most one.
If \(\gamma_H(p)\leq1\), then for every \(B>1\) there are bounded, finite-step kernels \(W_L\in\mathcal M_{p,B}\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\|W_L-1\|_1\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\liminf_L t(H,W_L)>1=t(H,1).
}
\tag{3}
\]
The strict inequality in the positive criterion is therefore necessary, including at the boundary.
If \(\gamma_H(p)<1\), the counterexamples can satisfy the stronger conclusions
\[
\boxed{
\|W_L-1\|_p\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\|T_{W_L}-J\|_{2\to2}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
t(H,W_L)\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(T_W\) is the integral operator of \(W\), and \(J\) is the constant-kernel operator.
The assumptions \(p>1\) and \(B>1\) matter. At \(B=1\), normalization and Jensen’s inequality force \(W=1\). The theorem does not classify the distinct endpoint question of strong-\(L^p\) continuity when \(\gamma_H(p)=1\).
4. Why a positive certificate works
The proof must control unequal edge scales and mixed products. Allow each edge \(e\) its own kernel \(W_e\), with row and column integrals at most one and \(\|W_e\|_p\leq B\).
For each edge, partition its values into
\[
A_{e,0}=\{W_e\leq2\},
\qquad
A_{e,k}=\{2^k<W_e\leq2^{k+1}\},\quad k\geq1.
\]
Each slice has row and column measures at most \(2^{-k}\), and total measure at most
\[
B^p2^{-pk}.
\]
Fix an edge-level vector \(\mathbf k\). Let \(J_{\mathbf k}\) be the set of vertex tuples satisfying every selected slice constraint, and write
\[
q=\Pr(J_{\mathbf k}).
\]
When \(q>0\), condition product Lebesgue measure on \(J_{\mathbf k}\), obtaining a law \(Q\). Define the base-two relative-entropy deficits
\[
D(S)
=
D_{\mathrm{KL},2}\!\left(Q_S\middle\|\mathrm{Leb}^{S}\right).
\]
These deficits are increasing and supermodular. In particular,
\[
D(S\cup T)+D(S\cap T)-D(S)-D(T)
\]
is conditional mutual information.
A distribution supported on a set of reference probability \(a\) has relative entropy at least \(\log_2(1/a)\). Applying this to pair supports and conditional sections gives
\[
D(uv)\geq pk_{uv}-p\log_2B,
\]
\[
D(uv)-D(u)\geq k_{uv},
\qquad
D(uv)-D(v)\geq k_{uv}.
\]
The modular correction
\[
d(S)=D(S)+\frac p2\log_2(B)\,|S|
\]
removes the additive loss without disturbing supermodularity. Homogeneity of (1), together with \(D(V)=\log_2(1/q)\), yields
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(J_{\mathbf k})
\leq
B^{pn/2}
2^{-\gamma_H(p)\sum_e k_e}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
On the same event,
\[
\prod_eW_e\leq2^m2^{\sum_e k_e}.
\]
Consequently, when \(\varepsilon=\gamma_H(p)-1>0\),
\[
\int\prod_eW_e
\leq
2^mB^{pn/2}
\sum_{\mathbf k\geq0}
2^{-\varepsilon\sum_e k_e}
<\infty.
\]
The same geometric sum gives a uniform tail estimate
\[
\int\prod_eW_e\,
\mathbf1_{\{\max_eW_e>R\}}
\leq C R^{-\varepsilon}.
\tag{6}
\]
The truncation step that cannot be skipped
Pointwise truncation is not automatically Lipschitz in cut norm. The proof therefore does not truncate both kernels and assume their truncations remain close.
Instead, telescope \(t(H,W)-t(H,U)\) over the edges. In each term, leave the distinguished factor \(W-U\) untruncated and restrict every other factor to values at most \(R\).
After fixing the other vertex variables, the remaining factors separate into a function of each endpoint of the distinguished edge. This uses simplicity of \(H\). The bounded portion is controlled by the cut norm; the complementary portion is controlled by (6):
\[
|t(H,W)-t(H,U)|
\leq
4mR^{m-1}\|W-U\|_\square
+
C R^{-\varepsilon}.
\]
Balancing the two terms proves (2).
5. Why a negative certificate produces a real kernel
This direction needs a substantive import. Abo Khamis, Nakos, Olteanu and Suciu prove that, under entropy bounds conditioned on at most one variable, optimization over polymatroids is attained by normal polymatroids. Their result credits earlier work on bag-query containment. This is the precise reason an abstract relaxed certificate can be realized here; I do not assume that arbitrary Shannon relaxations are tight. arXiv
A normal polymatroid has the form
\[
h(S)
=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V}
\alpha_A\,\mathbf1_{\{A\cap S\ne\varnothing\}},
\qquad \alpha_A\geq0.
\tag{7}
\]
From deficits to finite relations
Take an optimum \((d,\mathbf k)\) with \(d(V)=\gamma\leq1\). Choose a sufficiently large integer \(M\) and set
\[
h(S)=M|S|-d(S).
\]
This is a polymatroid. Its constraints become
\[
h(u)\leq M,\qquad h(uv)\leq2M-pk_{uv},
\]
\[
h(uv)-h(u)\leq M-k_{uv},
\qquad
h(uv)-h(v)\leq M-k_{uv}.
\tag{8}
\]
All conditioning sets are empty or singletons. The imported theorem supplies a normal \(h'\) satisfying these bounds with
\[
h'(V)\geq nM-\gamma.
\]
At scale \(L\), realize each coefficient \(\alpha_A\) by a shared coordinate with approximately \(2^{L\alpha_A}\) symbols. Vertex \(u\) stores the coordinates whose sets contain \(u\). Pad every vertex alphabet to size approximately \(2^{LM}\).
The resulting joint relation \(T_L\) has probability at least
\[
c\,2^{-L\gamma}.
\]
Its projection \(R_{e,L}\) onto an edge satisfies
\[
\Pr(R_{e,L})\leq2^{-pLk_e},
\qquad
\text{every row and column probability}\leq2^{-Lk_e}.
\tag{9}
\]
The shared-coordinate realization is established mathematics; the bounds above are its application to the present concentration certificate. arXiv
Combine the relations, then complete the marginals
Partition \([0,1]\) into \(n\) equal vertex-type blocks. On the block pair corresponding to an edge with \(k_e>0\), put
\[
A_L=\theta\,2^{Lk_e}\mathbf1_{R_{e,L}},
\]
and use its transpose on the reversed block pair. Set \(A_L=0\) elsewhere.
For sufficiently small fixed \(\theta>0\), (9) gives
\[
\sup_x\int A_L(x,y)\,dy\leq\theta,
\qquad
\|A_L\|_p=O_H(\theta),
\qquad
\int A_L\longrightarrow0.
\]
Now define
\[
r_L(x)=1-\int A_L(x,y)\,dy,
\qquad
\bar r_L=\int r_L,
\]
and perform the positive completion
\[
\boxed{
W_L(x,y)
=
A_L(x,y)
+
\frac{r_L(x)r_L(y)}{\bar r_L}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Every row integral is exactly one. All terms are nonnegative. The completion is bounded above and away from zero, and tends to one in \(L^1\).
Choosing \(\theta\) sufficiently small ensures \(\|W_L\|_p\leq B\) eventually for any prescribed \(B>1\).
The amplified contribution survives completion
Expand the product defining \(t(H,W_L)\).
The term using only the dense completion tends to one. Another nonnegative term uses \(A_L\) on every positive-scale edge and the dense completion on zero-scale edges.
Restricting that term to the correctly typed copy of \(T_L\) gives
\[
\text{contribution}
\geq
c_{H,\theta}\,
2^{L(\sum_e k_e-\gamma)}
=
c_{H,\theta}\,2^{L(1-\gamma)}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus an extra positive contribution survives whenever \(\gamma\leq1\).
Zero-scale edges are supplied by the dense completion; they are not removed by an unjustified perturbation. The final object is one ordinary symmetric kernel, not a collection of colored edge oracles.
For \(\gamma<1\), replace \(\theta\) by
\[
\theta_L
=
\theta\,2^{-sL},
\qquad
s=\frac{1-\gamma}{2m_+},
\]
where \(m_+\) is the number of positive-scale edges. Then the \(L^p\) norm of \(A_L\) and all its row integrals tend to zero, but the exponent in (11) remains
\[
1-\gamma-sm_+
=
\frac{1-\gamma}{2}>0.
\]
This proves the strong-convergence/divergence statement (4).
6. An explicit irrational threshold
Let \(F_k\) consist of \(k\geq2\) triangles sharing one central vertex and otherwise disjoint. The criterion gives
\[
\boxed{
p_c(F_k)
=
q_k
:=
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8k}}2.
}
\tag{12}
\]
This family also has an elementary proof that does not require the general normal-polymatroid import.
Upper bound
For a forest with one distinguished root edge in each nontrivial component, integrate leaves toward that root. A root edge uses the total support-mass bound; every other forest edge uses a conditional section bound.
Average two constructions over the symmetries of \(F_k\):
- The star consisting of all \(2k\) spokes, with one spoke chosen as root.
- One rooted two-spoke tree, together with the \(k-1\) remaining rim edges as separate root components.
Mix the second construction with weight \(z\). Every edge obtains a coefficient strictly greater than one precisely when one can choose
\[
\frac{k}{p(k-1)}
<
z
<
\frac{p-1}{2(k-1)},
\qquad 0<z<1.
\]
Such a choice exists for
\[
p(p-1)>2k.
\]
The preceding dyadic summation therefore proves continuity for \(p>q_k\).
Matching lower bound
Set \(q=q_k\), let \(b\downarrow0\), and put
\[
a=b^{q-1},\qquad c=1-a-b,\qquad u=b^{-2/q},
\]
\[
z=\frac{1-a/b-bu}{c},
\qquad
w=\frac{1-bz}{c}.
\]
On three blocks \(A,B,C\) of measures \(a,b,c\), define
\[
W_b=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & b^{-1} & 0\\
b^{-1} & u & z\\
0 & z & w
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{13}
\]
For sufficiently small \(b\), all entries are nonnegative. Direct substitution gives row integral one on all three blocks.
Moreover,
\[
\|W_b\|_q^q
=
2ab\,b^{-q}
+b^2u^q
+2bc\,z^q
+c^2w^q
\longrightarrow4.
\]
For every \(1\leq r<q\),
\[
\|W_b-1\|_r\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(q>2\), the associated operators converge to \(J\) even in Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
Nevertheless, put the central vertex of \(F_k\) in \(A\) and all outer vertices in \(B\). Its contribution is
\[
ab^{2k}(b^{-1})^{2k}u^k
=
b^{q-1-2k/q}
=
1.
\]
The all-\(C\) contribution tends to one. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\liminf_{b\downarrow0}t(F_k,W_b)\geq2.}
\]
This proves failure at the exact threshold.
For the bowtie \(F_2\), every proper edge-subgraph reduces, after integrating out degree-one vertices, to a triangle or forest. Their densities tend to one in this construction. The full six-edge pattern retains a defect that every proper edge-subpattern misses.
The representation that failed
Equal-scale rooted-forest tests suggest the simpler quantity
\[
\max_{F\subseteq H}
\left(
2+\frac{|E(F)|-|V(F)|}{\nu(F)}
\right),
\]
where \(\nu(F)\) is matching number.
For the bowtie this is \(5/2\), whereas the exact threshold is
\[
\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2>\frac52.
\]
The discrepancy is not a numerical issue. It comes from two different concentration scales. This is why the final criterion retains separate \(k_e\) and the full subset-deficit structure.
7. The mechanism extends to multiway interactions
The same proof gives an exact theorem for every finite simple \(r\)-uniform hypergraph, \(r\geq2\), and nonnegative symmetric kernels \(W:[0,1]^r\to[0,\infty)\) with each single-coordinate marginal equal to one.
The concentration constraints become
\[
d(e)\geq pk_e,
\qquad
d(e)-d(\{v\})\geq k_e
\quad(v\in e).
\tag{14}
\]
These still involve only singleton conditioning, so the same imported realization theorem applies.
The discrepancy norm must change with the interface. For \(r>2\), use
\[
\|F\|_{r-1,\square}
=
\sup_{0\leq f_i\leq1}
\left|
\int
F(x_1,\ldots,x_r)
\prod_{i=1}^r f_i(x_{[r]\setminus\{i\}})
\,dx
\right|.
\tag{15}
\]
The corresponding optimum being greater than one is again necessary and sufficient for uniform counting continuity. Negative certificates again produce finite-step kernels converging to one in \(L^1\); strict negative certificates give strong-\(L^p\) convergence with diverging counts.
The exact positive completion is now
\[
W_L(x_1,\ldots,x_r)
=
A_L(x_1,\ldots,x_r)
+
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^r r_L(x_i)}
{\bar r_L^{\,r-1}}.
\]
This extension does not substitute pairwise observations for higher-order information. The stronger discrepancy in (15) is an explicit part of the theorem, and no efficient procedure for acquiring it is claimed.
8. Computational resources, status, and remaining limits
For rational \(p\), deciding whether \(\gamma_H(p)>1\) requires rational linear programming of size exponential in the pattern’s vertex count:
\[
2^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(\operatorname{bit}(p))
\]
bit time, with the input-graph factor understood.
A positive result supplies a quantitative continuity certificate. A negative result supplies a finite counterexample description. However, at construction scale \(L\), its explicit label domain can have size \(2^{LM}\). The compact description is not a polynomial-size expanded matrix, and the theorem does not provide a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary graph-count integration.
For each fixed graph, the critical exponent is a computable real algebraic number: the bounded feasibility problem has coefficients polynomial in \(p\), so its parameter boundary is determined by finitely many polynomial sign conditions. This is an effective statement, not an efficient symbolic-threshold algorithm.
The executed checks include 13 exact rational primal–dual LP certificates, three exactly checked normal-relation descriptors, and five exact rational critical kernels for \(F_3\). They test the implementation and constructions; they do not independently validate the general proof or establish novelty.
The final proof does not depend on the recent OpenAI Sidorenko construction examined during direction selection. That source’s repository reports a formalized counterexample statement, but no replay of that formalization was performed here. 
The remaining boundaries are precise. A polynomial-size replacement for the exponential criterion is not supplied. Strong-\(L^p\) behavior exactly at \(\gamma=1\) is a different endpoint problem. Extending the construction to bounds conditioned on several vertices would require additional mathematics: the cited entropy work includes non-simple settings where the polymatroid relaxation is not tight. Simply replacing the finite criterion with a general entropy cone would relocate the difficulty. arXiv
What has been established here is the sharp marginal-normalized counting criterion, its constructive converse, and its multiway extension, relative to the stated published import. What remains unestablished is historical priority, independent validation, and a basis for calling it an independently major foundational breakthrough.
