2. First mechanism: spectral growth does not certify enough distinct neighbours
A natural attempted strengthening was
\[
\rho(A)\ge \frac n3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{a directed triangle}.
\]
If true, it would imply the desired outdegree theorem because \(\rho(A)\ge\delta^+(A)\). It is false.
Exact spectral substitution theorem
Let \(G\) be a strongly connected loopless digraph with adjacency matrix \(B\), order \(N\ge2\), and spectral radius \(r>0\).
Replace each vertex by a transitive tournament \(T_m\), and replace each original arc by all possible arcs between the corresponding blocks. The resulting adjacency matrix is
\[
C=B\otimes J_m+I_N\otimes T_m.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\rho(C)=
\frac{1}{(1+1/r)^{1/m}-1}.
}
\]
Moreover, the construction preserves directed girth and satisfies
\[
\delta^+(G[T_m])=m\delta^+(G).
\]
Proof. Choose a positive vector \(b\) with \(Bb=rb\). Put
\[
q=(1+1/r)^{1/m},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac1{q-1},
\qquad
z_a=q^{m-1-a},\quad 0\le a<m.
\]
The geometric-series identities give
\[
\sum_a z_a=\frac{\lambda}{r},
\qquad
T_mz=\lambda(z-\mathbf1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
C(b\otimes z)
=b\otimes\left(r\Bigl(\sum_a z_a\Bigr)\mathbf1+T_mz\right)
=\lambda(b\otimes z).
\]
This eigenvector is positive. Diagonal conjugation therefore turns \(C\) into a nonnegative matrix with every row sum equal to \(\lambda\). Its spectral radius is exactly \(\lambda\).
A vertex in position \(a\) of block \(i\) has outdegree
\[
m\,d_G^+(i)+(m-1-a),
\]
which proves the minimum-degree formula.
No directed cycle lies within a transitive block. The inter-block transitions of any directed cycle project to a nonempty closed directed walk in \(G\), while every shortest cycle of \(G\) lifts to the substituted graph. Thus directed girth is preserved. \(\square\)
A 20-vertex counterexample
Take \(G\) to be the directed four-cycle and write \(D_m=C_4[T_m]\). Then
\[
|V(D_m)|=4m,\qquad
g(D_m)=4,\qquad
\delta^+(D_m)=m,
\]
but
\[
\rho(D_m)=\frac1{2^{1/m}-1}.
\]
For \(m=5\),
\[
\rho(D_5)>\frac{20}{3}.
\]
This does not depend on numerical approximation:
\[
23^5=6\,436\,343
>
6\,400\,000=2\cdot20^5
\]
implies \(2^{1/5}<23/20\), hence the claimed inequality.
In fact,
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\rho(D_m)}{|V(D_m)|}
=\frac1{4\log2}
\approx0.3606737602>\frac13.
\]
The positive eigenvector above has largest-to-smallest coordinate ratio
\[
2^{(m-1)/m}<2.
\]
Thus even a bounded ratio of Perron-vector coordinates does not rescue this spectral implication.
These are not Caccetta–Häggkvist counterexamples: their minimum outdegree density is only \(1/4\). They distinguish walk growth from the minimum number of distinct outneighbours.
The transitive-tournament substitution itself is prior mathematics: the OpenAI Seymour source explicitly uses this construction and cites an earlier application. I therefore do not count the construction or this spectral calculation as an established original advance. 
3. Replacement: distinct-neighbour mass and an exact composition law
For a real \(n\times n\) matrix \(M\), define
\[
\beta(M)=
\max_{w\in\Delta_n}\min_i(Mw)_i,
\qquad
\Delta_n=\{w\ge0:\mathbf1^Tw=1\}.
\]
For a graph adjacency matrix, \((Aw)_i\) is the total mass of the distinct outneighbours of \(i\).
The dual form is
\[
\beta(M)=
\min_{p\in\Delta_n}\max_j(M^Tp)_j.
\]
In particular, probability vectors satisfying
\[
Mw\ge b\mathbf1,\qquad M^Tp\le b\mathbf1
\]
certify \(\beta(M)=b\).
Exact substitution law
Replace vertex \(i\) of \(G\) by any nonempty finite digraph \(H_i\). As before, inter-block arcs are complete exactly where \(G\) has an arc. Set
\[
c_i=\beta(A_{H_i}).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\beta(G[H_1,\ldots,H_n])
=
\beta\!\left(A_G+\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\right).
}
\]
Proof. A probability vector on the expanded graph determines block masses \(w_i\) and conditional probability vectors \(q_i\) within the blocks.
For a vertex \(u\) in block \(i\), the outneighbour mass is
\[
(A_Gw)_i+w_i(A_{H_i}q_i)_u.
\]
The minimum over all vertices is therefore
\[
\min_i\left[
(A_Gw)_i+
w_i\min_u(A_{H_i}q_i)_u
\right].
\]
For fixed \(w\), each internal minimum is at most \(c_i\). These bounds can all be attained simultaneously, because the conditional vectors \(q_i\) can be chosen independently. Thus the best value for fixed \(w\) is
\[
\min_i\bigl[(A_Gw)_i+c_iw_i\bigr].
\]
Maximizing over \(w\) proves the identity. A zero block mass causes no difficulty because its internal contribution vanishes. \(\square\)
Consequence: invariance under acyclic substitution
Every finite acyclic digraph has a sink, so its \(\beta\)-value is zero. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\beta(G[H_1,\ldots,H_n])=\beta(G)
}
\]
whenever every \(H_i\) is acyclic, even when the block sizes differ.
For the spectral counterexamples,
\[
\beta(D_m)=\frac14
\]
for every \(m\), despite the spectral-radius density eventually exceeding \(1/3\).
This identifies a genuine difference between the representations: acyclic internal paths can increase spectral growth, but they cannot increase this worst-case distinct-neighbour mass. It still supplies no universal one-third bound.
4. Second mechanism: optimal input and output weights cannot simply be identified
A possible next step was to use one probability measure on both sides of the matrix game, then seek a balanced in/out-neighbourhood argument.
That step is also false in general.
Explicit finite counterexample
Use vertices \(i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\). Include the arcs
\[
i\to i+1,\qquad i\to i+3
\]
for every \(i\), and also include \(i\to i+2\) when
\[
i\bmod5\in\{0,1,3\}.
\]
Every arc advances by \(1\), \(2\), or \(3\) modulo \(10\). A closed walk of length at most three would have positive total advance at most \(9\), so none exists. The graph contains the directed ten-cycle and the four-cycle
\[
0\to3\to6\to9\to0.
\]
It is therefore strongly connected, oriented, and of directed girth four.
Let \(A\) be its adjacency matrix and define
\[
w=\frac1{18}(3,1,2,2,1,3,1,2,2,1)^T,
\]
\[
p=\frac1{18}(2,2,1,3,1,2,2,1,3,1)^T.
\]
Exact multiplication gives
\[
Aw=\frac5{18}\mathbf1,
\qquad
A^Tp=\frac5{18}\mathbf1,
\qquad
\det A=5.
\]
Hence
\[
\beta(A)=\frac5{18}.
\]
Both optimizers are strictly positive, and both are unique.
To prove primal uniqueness, let \(w'\) be another optimal vector. Then
\[
Aw'\ge\frac5{18}\mathbf1.
\]
But
\[
p^TAw'
=(A^Tp)^Tw'
=\frac5{18}.
\]
Since every coordinate of \(p\) is positive, every coordinate inequality must be equality:
\[
Aw'=\frac5{18}\mathbf1.
\]
Invertibility of \(A\) gives \(w'=w\). The dual argument uses positivity of \(w\) and invertibility of \(A^T\).
Since \(p\ne w\), there is no common optimal measure.
Again, this is not a counterexample to the original conjecture: \(5/18<1/3\). It rejects a specific balancing step, not all weighted or stationary approaches.
5. The exact remaining theorem—and why the representation has not solved it
Define
\[
F_A(t)=\beta(A+tI).
\]
The proposed universal inequality is
\[
\boxed{
F_A(1/3)\le\frac13
\quad
\text{for every finite triangle-free oriented adjacency matrix }A.
}
\tag{*}
\]
Equivalently:
\[
\boxed{
\forall w\in\Delta_n\ \exists i:
\quad
(Aw)_i\le\frac{1-w_i}{3}.
}
\]
I proved that \((*)\) is equivalent to the directed-triangle case of Caccetta–Häggkvist. I did not prove \((*)\).
Why \((*)\) would settle the problem
Apply it to the uniform vector. Some vertex satisfies
\[
\frac{d_i^+}{n}+\frac1{3n}\le\frac13,
\]
so
\[
\delta^+(G)\le\frac{n-1}{3}<\frac n3.
\]
Thus a triangle-free oriented graph cannot have minimum outdegree at least \(n/3\).
Why the converse also holds
Assume the directed-triangle case of Caccetta–Häggkvist.
Let \(H_k\) be the \(k\)-fold lexicographic power of the directed four-cycle. Induction gives
\[
|H_k|=4^k,
\qquad
d^+(v)=d^-(v)=\frac{4^k-1}{3},
\qquad
g(H_k)=4.
\]
Uniform primal and dual measures show that
\[
c_k:=\beta(H_k)
=\frac{1-4^{-k}}3
\longrightarrow\frac13.
\]
Also, \(F_A\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz: changing \(t\) by \(\varepsilon\) changes each coordinate of \((A+tI)w\) by at most \(|\varepsilon|\), and taking a minimum followed by a maximum preserves that bound.
Suppose, contrary to \((*)\), that \(F_A(1/3)>1/3\). For sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
F_A(c_k)>\frac13.
\]
The substitution theorem gives
\[
\beta(G[H_k])>\frac13.
\]
This graph remains triangle-free. Choose a rational probability vector \(q\) whose weighted outdegree at every vertex exceeds \(1/3\); rational approximation preserves the strict inequalities.
With a common denominator \(L\), replace vertex \(v\) by \(Lq_v\) independent clones, omitting zero-sized classes. Every surviving vertex has outdegree
\[
L\sum_{v\to u}q_u>\frac L3.
\]
The resulting graph is still triangle-free and has \(L\) vertices, contradicting the assumed conjecture. Therefore \((*)\) follows. \(\square\)
This equivalence exposes the limitation precisely. The matrix game retains the finite endpoint, handles substitutions exactly, and avoids the demonstrated spectral failure. But the universal inequality still contains the full original difficulty. Naming or computing the quantity does not establish that inequality.
