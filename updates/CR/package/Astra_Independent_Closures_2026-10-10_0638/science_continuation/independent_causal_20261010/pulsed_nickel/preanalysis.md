# Finite-pulse diffusive-memory test
Locked before any numeric current values or FE tables are opened, 2026-10-10.

Candidate physical mechanism: an anodic pulse releases a reduced/poisoned molecular population near the electrode, and the subsequent cathodic transient remembers the diffusion profile built during that pulse. In the minimal reversible-diffusion limit, a boundary concentration excursion of duration a leaves a post-pulse flux proportional to K_a(t) = t^(-1/2) - (t+a)^(-1/2), t>0. At periodic steady state, sum K_a(t+nP) over previous pulses, P=5+a seconds. Unlike a fitted exponent, this predicts the complete change of transient shape with the intervention a.

Alternative: a finite interfacial inventory reset by the pulse and then consumed with one first-order rate. Its post-pulse transient is A*exp(-t/tau)+B; after baseline/amplitude normalization, its shape is independent of a. The release extent may vary with a, but tau does not unless a second mechanism is added.

Use only the -0.3 V / 0.2 s condition (all three replicates) to characterize acquisition, select fixed baseline/time alignment, and fit tau for the alternative. Discovery interval 1800-3600 s. Validation conditions -0.3 V / 0.04 s and -0.3 V / 1 s remain unopened until extraction and scoring choices are fixed. For validation use the same 1800-3600 s interval. Report acquisition limitations honestly, especially if the 40 ms pulse is faster than sample spacing. Compare full normalized cathodic profiles from 0.2 to 4.8 s; exclude the first 0.1 s to avoid double-layer charging and timing errors. Use 0.2 s and 4.8 s values only as two-point affine calibration, consistently for both mechanisms. Evaluate all remaining resolved times without refitting tau or the diffusion law. Score on per-replicate curves, not thousands of correlated cycle samples treated as biological replication.

The original paper already concluded that oxidation regenerates catalyst and showed approximate Cottrell behavior within the anodic pulse. This analysis is only potentially new evidence for *cross-pulse spatial concentration memory*, not a claim to rediscover catalyst regeneration. A good fit is insufficient to identify the carrier, exclude mixed reactions, or demonstrate a fundamental new mechanism. A failure rejects this minimal mechanism as a description of the observed total-current transient, not every diffusion-coupled mechanism.

The instrument records current but the deposited long-run files do not include measured voltage. Do not invent pulse timing or electrochemical reaction identity; verify timing from record timestamps and repeated current transitions. If timing is insufficient for the fixed test, stop at the measurement boundary.

## Acquisition decision frozen after training, before validation
The 0.2-s training sheet has runs 1/2 sampled at 0.2 s with clear positive anodic points once per 5.2 s. Run 3 is sampled at 0.1 s but has no positive anodic current and its phase-locked signal is only 0.23 microamp versus approximately 1.48 microamp within-phase noise. Therefore run 3 does not resolve the imposed waveform and cannot validate sub-cycle kinetics. This is an acquisition gate, not an inference that the experiment lacked pulses.

Validation acquisition gate: analyze only runs with a resolved positive anodic phase at the prescribed period and a cathodic phase-locked range larger than the within-phase standard deviation. Report all failures. Do not silently discard traces. Current windows remain 1800–3600 s. Use medians of observations within 0.005 s of the 0.2 s-spaced prescribed phase grid (t modulo 5+a), allowing timestamp rounding below 0.002 s. If a phase bin is unobserved, do not interpolate across it to create resolution. Fit no validation shape parameters. The exponential alternative uses tau = arithmetic mean of training run 1/2 estimates, approximately 0.4151 s. Both laws are affinely calibrated only at 0.2 s and 4.8 s in each run. Interior points from 0.4 to 4.6 s are prediction targets. Report normalized RMSE for each model and each run; no claim of independent replication at the individual cycle level.
