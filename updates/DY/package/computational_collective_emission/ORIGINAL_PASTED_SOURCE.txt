A concrete advantage for driven collective-emission models
The strongest continuation is a gate-efficient simulator for interacting emitter arrays with collective photon emission, coherent driving, incoherent pumping, and local dephasing.
The additional mathematical step is an exponential-tail bound on the number of simulated events. It depends on the initial excitation supply and the driving, pumping, and dephasing budgets, not on the much larger maximum collective-emission rate.
For a specified regime of \(n\) emitters, this yields
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O(n^{5/2})
}
\]
elementary gates for full-channel simulation over one single-emitter lifetime, versus
\[
\widetilde O(n^3)
\]
from the current general gate-efficient Lindblad algorithm, using the same explicitly constructed operator circuits for both. Estimating a specified bounded observable to additive error \(\delta\) costs
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O(n^{5/2}/\delta)
}
\]
when coherent input preparation and its inverse are available.
This is an improved upper bound for the physical model specified below—not a demonstrated hardware advantage or a lower-bound separation from every specialized classical or quantum algorithm. The earlier time-history compiler and this extension remain independently unverified; historical originality is also not established.
1. The physical task
Consider \(n\) two-level emitters. Write
\[
q_i=\sigma_i^+\sigma_i^-,
\qquad Q=\sum_{i=1}^n q_i.
\]
The model is
\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]
+\sum_{\mu=1}^{r}\mathcal D[L_\mu](\rho)
+\sum_{i=1}^n\mathcal D[\sqrt{p_i}\sigma_i^+](\rho)
+\sum_{i=1}^n\mathcal D[\sqrt{\kappa_i}Z_i](\rho),
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
\mathcal D[L](\rho)=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}.
\]
The collective emission operators are
\[
L_\mu=\sum_{i=1}^n c_{\mu i}\sigma_i^-.
\tag{2}
\]
The \(r\times n\) complex matrix \(C=(c_{\mu i})\) is supplied as an ordinary finite-precision classical table—not as a free quantum oracle.
The Hamiltonian is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H&=H_0+H_{\mathrm{ex}}+H_{\mathrm d},\\
H_0&=\sum_i d_iq_i+\sum_{(i,j)\in E_0}u_{ij}q_iq_j,\\
H_{\mathrm{ex}}
&=\sum_{(i,j)\in E_{\mathrm{ex}}}
\left(J_{ij}\sigma_i^+\sigma_j^-+\overline{J_{ij}}\sigma_j^+\sigma_i^-\right),\\
H_{\mathrm d}
&=\sum_i\left(\Omega_i\sigma_i^+
+\overline{\Omega_i}\sigma_i^-\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the model includes inhomogeneous collective couplings, detunings, diagonal interactions, excitation exchange, coherent drives, pumping, and dephasing. No permutation symmetry is assumed.
This is an established scientific setting rather than an artificial oracle family. Work on programmable interacting qubit arrays reports controllable collective decay, long-lived subradiant population tails, and spatially resolved correlations—the kinds of quantities targeted here. The theorem below concerns the specified Markovian model, not every imperfection of those experiments. arXiv
Output contract
Given an input-preparation circuit, a time \(T\), and accuracy \(\varepsilon\), construct a channel approximating (1) in diamond norm. The guarantee also permits an external reference entangled with the input.
Additional output registers can record photons in specified emission modes and time bins. From these registers and the final spin state, the algorithm can estimate, for example, the retained excitation fraction, a spin correlation, a photon-count threshold probability, or a specified value of the photon-count characteristic function.
It does not output the full density matrix, all photon wavepackets, or a normalized state conditioned on a rare detection pattern.
2. Comparison with current algorithms
Chen–Gao–Wang–Zhou give a general, gate-efficient Lindblad simulator whose query complexity is essentially linear in
\[
\tau=(\alpha_H+\alpha_B^2)T,
\]
with optimal joint time–precision dependence. Their result includes coherent time-dependent access and charges additional gates beyond oracle calls. This is the main general comparator used below. arXiv
There are important stronger algorithms under different structural assumptions. Kitchen obtains nearly linear space-time gate complexity for finite-range Lindblad dynamics; that locality hypothesis does not hold for a collective operator (2) supported across the array. Related local-lattice algorithms also impose locality and, in their relevant Markovian results, commutation conditions involving jump adjoints. arXiv
The observable-oriented randomized algorithms of Kato and collaborators trade substantially smaller ancillary space for quadratic or quartic dependence on their normalized time. Their stated bounds do not give a better uniform gate bound in the dense collective-emission regime considered here. arXiv
Finally, symmetric and disorder-averaged all-to-all models can admit much smaller representations. The present task is an individual, potentially inhomogeneous instance—not a permutation-invariant or disorder-averaged calculation. Those faster special cases should be used when applicable. arXiv
The scoped repository check did not establish priority. In particular, Astra’s retrieved Dicke-compiler result concerns decomposing promised separable Dicke-diagonal states, a different input/output contract from the dynamical task here. 
3. The new mathematical step: a jump-count certificate independent of collective decay strength
A mean-activity estimate is insufficient for the next task. To build a fixed-length coherent circuit suitable for amplitude estimation, we need a reliable event cutoff. Applying Markov’s inequality to a mean bound would introduce an inverse-error cutoff and spoil the precision dependence.
An exponential-moment argument avoids that cost.
Let \(J_T\) count all events in the chosen simulation unraveling: emitted photons, pumping events, and the specified \(Z_i\)-dephasing events. Suppose the initial state is supported on \(Q\le k_0\). Taking \(k_0=n\) requires no restriction on the input.
Define
\[
\omega=\sum_i|\Omega_i|,
\qquad
p_*=\sum_i p_i,
\qquad
\kappa_*=\sum_i\kappa_i.
\]
Jump-count theorem
For every \(\theta>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E e^{\theta J_T}
\le
\exp\!\left[
\theta k_0+
T\left(
2\sinh(\theta/2)\omega
+(e^\theta-1)\kappa_*
+(e^{2\theta}-1)p_*
\right)
\right].
}
\tag{4}
\]
The collective coupling matrix \(C\) does not occur in this bound. Its entries may produce a maximum instantaneous emission rate of order \(n^2\), but that rate cannot continually supply new excitations.
Proof
Let \(\rho_\theta(t)\) be the positive, unnormalised tilted state obtained by weighting a trajectory containing \(j\) events by \(e^{\theta j}\). Its evolution is
\[
\dot\rho_\theta
=-i[H,\rho_\theta]
+\sum_a\left(
e^\theta K_a\rho_\theta K_a^\dagger
-\tfrac12\{K_a^\dagger K_a,\rho_\theta\}
\right),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(K_a\) ranges over all jump operators in (1). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\rho_\theta(T)=\mathbb E e^{\theta J_T}.
\]
Consider
\[
M_\theta(t)=
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[e^{\theta Q}\rho_\theta(t)\right].
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(Q\ge0\),
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\rho_\theta(t)\le M_\theta(t),
\qquad
M_\theta(0)\le e^{\theta k_0}.
\]
For a collective loss operator, \([Q,L_\mu]=-L_\mu\). Consequently,
\[
L_\mu^\dagger e^{\theta Q}L_\mu
=e^{-\theta}L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu e^{\theta Q}.
\]
The counting factor \(e^\theta\) cancels this \(e^{-\theta}\), giving
\[
e^\theta L_\mu^\dagger e^{\theta Q}L_\mu
-\tfrac12\{L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu,e^{\theta Q}\}=0.
\tag{7}
\]
Each photon decreases the remaining excitation number by precisely the amount it increases the event counter. This cancellation holds for the collective operator itself, including all interference terms.
For dephasing, \(Z_i\) commutes with \(Q\), so its contribution is
\[
(e^\theta-1)\kappa_i e^{\theta Q}.
\tag{8}
\]
A pump raises \(Q\) while also increasing the event count. Since
\[
(\sqrt{p_i}\sigma_i^+)^\dagger
(\sqrt{p_i}\sigma_i^+)=p_i(1-q_i)\preceq p_iI,
\]
its contribution is bounded above by
\[
(e^{2\theta}-1)p_i e^{\theta Q}.
\tag{9}
\]
The terms \(H_0\) and \(H_{\mathrm{ex}}\) commute with \(Q\). To handle the coherent drive, put
\[
A_+=\sum_i\Omega_i\sigma_i^+,\qquad H_{\mathrm d}=A_++A_+^\dagger.
\]
Then
\[
e^{-\theta Q/2}i[H_{\mathrm d},e^{\theta Q}]e^{-\theta Q/2}
=
2i\sinh(\theta/2)(A_+^\dagger-A_+).
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\|i(A_+^\dagger-A_+)\|\le\sum_i|\Omega_i|=\omega.
\]
Thus, in operator order,
\[
i[H_{\mathrm d},e^{\theta Q}]
\preceq
2\sinh(\theta/2)\omega\,e^{\theta Q}.
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (7)–(11) gives a scalar differential inequality for \(M_\theta\). Grönwall’s inequality proves (4). The same argument remains valid with a reference system attached.
The exponentially weighted operator in (6) is only a proof device. The circuit never prepares, measures, or block-encodes it.
A computable cutoff
Let
\[
\bar\omega=\sum_i
\bigl(|\operatorname{Re}\Omega_i|
+|\operatorname{Im}\Omega_i|\bigr)\ge\omega.
\]
Taking \(\theta=1\) and using
\[
2\sinh(1/2)<2,\quad e-1<2,\quad e^2-1<7
\]
shows that
\[
R=
\left\lceil
k_0+2T\bar\omega+2T\kappa_*+7Tp_*+
\log(32/\varepsilon)
\right\rceil
\tag{12}
\]
satisfies
\[
\Pr(J_T\ge R)\le\varepsilon/32.
\tag{13}
\]
All quantities in (12) are computed from the classical input. No trajectory pilot run or estimate of the unknown state’s activity is required.
Stopping with an explicit overflow flag on event \(R\) changes the channel by at most twice the overflow probability. This is a trace-preserving error branch, not rejection followed by renormalisation.
4. Constructing the operator circuits from physical coefficients
Set
\[
s^2=\|C\|_F^2.
\]
Collective annihilation without random-access memory
Define
\[
\mathsf A=\sum_{i=1}^n |i\rangle\otimes\sigma_i^-.
\]
Then
\[
\mathsf A^\dagger\mathsf A=Q,
\qquad
\mathsf L_{\mathrm{col}}=(C\otimes I)\mathsf A.
\tag{14}
\]
There is an explicit encoding of \(\mathsf A/\sqrt n\). For a computational-basis string \(x\) of Hamming weight \(q\), its success component is
\[
|x\rangle\longmapsto
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\sum_{i:x_i=1}|i\rangle|x-e_i\rangle.
\tag{15}
\]
A circuit computes \(q\), applies a success rotation of amplitude \(\sqrt{q/n}\), coherently selects a uniform occupied position, and clears that position. A reversible scan implements the selection in \(O(n\,\mathrm{poly}(b))\) gates at working precision \(b\). The input is recoverable from the output pair \((i,x-e_i)\), allowing the auxiliary counters to be uncomputed. The \(q=0\) case goes entirely to the failure flag.
This is a global unitary encoding; it does not normalise an operator using a promise that fails outside the input subspace.
Encoding the mode matrix
For row norm \(s_\mu\), prepare
\[
|\phi_\mu\rangle
=\sum_i\frac{\overline{c_{\mu i}}}{s_\mu}|i\rangle,
\qquad
|w\rangle=\sum_\mu\frac{s_\mu}{s}|\mu\rangle.
\]
The two isometries
\[
P|\mu\rangle=|\mu\rangle|\phi_\mu\rangle,
\qquad
Q_C|i\rangle=|w\rangle|i\rangle
\]
satisfy
\[
P^\dagger Q_C=C/s.
\tag{16}
\]
The states are compiled from the explicit coefficient table using \(O(rn\,\mathrm{poly}(b))\) gates and comparable preprocessing. Zero rows carry zero weight and cause no singularity.
Combining (15) and (16) gives a projected unitary encoding of
\[
\mathsf L_{\mathrm{col}}/(s\sqrt n).
\tag{17}
\]
The emitter label must be erased coherently inside this encoding. Measuring it would replace collective emission by different channels. For example,
\[
(\sigma_1^-+\sigma_2^-)
\frac{|10\rangle-|01\rangle}{\sqrt2}=0,
\]
whereas separately labelled local-loss channels decay that same state. Our circuit retains the collective dark-state interference.
The pump and dephasing stacks have encodings with normalisations \(\sqrt{p_*}\) and \(\sqrt{\kappa_*}\), using explicit weighted selection of local operators. Combining the three families yields
\[
\boxed{g^2=ns^2+p_*+\kappa_*.}
\tag{18}
\]
For a fixed number \(r\) of collective modes,
\[
\|C\|\le s\le\sqrt r\,\|C\|,
\]
so using the Frobenius norm does not conceal a growing matrix-factor normalisation penalty.
Hamiltonian and time dependence
A Pauli linear-combination encoding of \(H_{\mathrm{ex}}+H_{\mathrm d}\) has normalisation
\[
h=
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_{\mathrm{ex}}}
\bigl(|\operatorname{Re}J_{ij}|+|\operatorname{Im}J_{ij}|\bigr)
+\bar\omega.
\tag{19}
\]
The diagonal Hamiltonian \(H_0\) is implemented directly:
\[
U_0(t)=e^{-itH_0}.
\]
Its commuting one- and two-site phases require
\[
O\!\left((n+|E_0|)\,\mathrm{poly}(b)\right)
\]
gates at a binary-encoded time \(t\).
Work in the interaction picture, using
\[
\widetilde L_a(t)=U_0(t)^\dagger L_aU_0(t),
\qquad
\widetilde H_{\mathrm{res}}(t)
=U_0(t)^\dagger(H_{\mathrm{ex}}+H_{\mathrm d})U_0(t).
\tag{20}
\]
Their circuits are obtained by conjugating the encodings above. No expansion of the dressed operators into an exponentially large term list is performed.
The baseline algorithm receives this same interaction-picture improvement.
5. Turning the cutoff into a coherent full-channel circuit
This is where the loss-complete time-history lemma is used.
For an interval of duration \(d\), that lemma implements the complete first-event instrument at cost
\[
\widetilde O(1+g\sqrt d+hd).
\tag{21}
\]
Its output contains survival, the first physical event, or the controlled numerical-error branch. The output is an isometry, so its implementation does not divide by a survival probability.
Choose a power of two \(W\) with
\[
R+1\le W<2(R+1),
\]
and divide \([0,T]\) into \(W\) windows.
Within a window, the first-event circuit acts on its remaining suffix. A survivor advances to the next window. An event updates the current time and event counter. Reaching the cutoff or a numerical-error label activates a terminal flag.
There are at most \(W\) window completions and \(R\) events. Consequently, the whole computation fits into a fixed schedule of \(W+R\) controlled first-event calls.
All labels and updates are retained coherently. No measurement is needed until the final output. The simulation circuit therefore has an available inverse.
A suffix is padded with identity steps to a common window length. Its selector computes the absolute time by reversible addition and returns zero operators beyond the suffix. This avoids constructing a separate circuit for every possible event time.
Each call costs at most the bound (21) for \(d=T/W\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\mathrm{sim}}
=
\widetilde O\!\left(
R+1+g\sqrt{T(R+1)}+hT
\right).
}
\tag{22}
\]
Unlike the earlier input-dependent expected bound, this is a fixed execution-cost bound with a known error guarantee.
Discretisation and precision
Let
\[
\Lambda_0=\sum_i|d_i|+\sum_{E_0}|u_{ij}|,
\quad
a=g^2/2+h,
\quad
D=a^2+2\Lambda_0(g^2+h).
\]
The interaction-picture encodings have Lipschitz bounds
\[
\ell_B\le2\Lambda_0g,\qquad
\ell_H\le2\Lambda_0h.
\]
Use a power-of-two grid size satisfying, for a sufficiently large numerical constant,
\[
M\ge
\max\left\{
W,\;2aT,\;\frac{1024T^2D}{\varepsilon}
\right\}.
\tag{23}
\]
The rational CPTP step from the preceding construction has local channel error \(O(D(T/M)^2)\). Telescoping gives total discretisation error \(O(T^2D/M)\). This analysis also applies after adding an absorbing event counter; its size does not multiply the operator-norm bounds.
Allocate circuit errors across the \(W+R\) calls and include the overflow error (13). The resulting channel has diamond error at most \(\varepsilon\).
Although \(M\) may be large, its cost is through binary registers and arithmetic. A sufficient working precision is polynomially bounded in
\[
B_0+\log M+\log(R+1)+\log(r+n)
+\log\!\frac{1+g^2T+hT+\Lambda_0T}{\varepsilon},
\tag{24}
\]
where \(B_0\) bounds the input coefficient bit lengths. There is no \(M\)-entry operator table.

Let
\[
D_{\mathrm{in}}=rn+n+|E_0|+|E_{\mathrm{ex}}|.
\]
Up to fixed-degree working-precision factors, one invocation of the required encoded operators costs \(D_{\mathrm{in}}\) elementary gates.
The complete simulation therefore has
\[
\boxed{
G_{\mathrm{sim}}
=
O\!\left[
D_{\mathrm{in}}\,\mathrm{poly}(b)
\left(R+1+g\sqrt{T(R+1)}+hT\right)
\right].

\tag{25}

This includes coefficient-state preparation, local-operator selection, controlled calls and adjoints, interaction-picture conjugations, clock arithmetic, and final application of \(U_0(T)\).
Reading the input costs \(\Omega(D_{\mathrm{in}}B_0)\) bit operations. The required classical preprocessing costs \(O(D_{\mathrm{in}}\mathrm{poly}(b))\); circuit generation is polynomial in the generated gate count and working precision. No quantum random-access memory is assumed.
The coherent implementation uses
\[
O\!\left(n+(R+1)\mathrm{poly}(b)\right)

quantum workspace, including retained event and control records. Long-time calculations with large \(R\) must pay for those records.
Photon statistics
To record photons from mode \(\mu\) in a chosen time bin, append a counter and replace that loss by
\[
L_\mu\otimes\mathrm{INC}_{\mu,\mathrm{bin}(t)}.
\tag{27}
\]
The counter increments are unitary, so the jump normalisation is unchanged. Pump and dephasing labels are not misreported as emitted photons.
For a fixed number of dyadic time bins, their boundaries can be aligned with the simulation grid. With \(B\) bins, the explicit counter-selection cost and \(rB\log(R+1)\) storage must also be included.
This constructs the prescribed photon-count channel, not merely a convenient alternative unraveling.
One bounded observable
Let an input circuit and its inverse cost \(C_{\mathrm{in}}\), and let a bounded effect \(0\le F\le I\) have a coherent implementation costing \(C_F\). Compile the channel to \(\varepsilon=O(\delta)\).
Because the simulator has a fixed coherent implementation and inverse, amplitude estimation gives an estimate of the physical expectation with additive error \(\delta\) and failure probability at most \(\zeta\), at total cost
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{pre}}
+\frac{G_{\mathrm{sim}}+C_{\mathrm{in}}+C_F}{\delta}
\log\frac1\zeta
\right].
}
\tag{28}
\]
The amplitude-estimation step is standard; the new requirement met here is the bounded-cost coherent physical simulator to which it can be applied. arXiv
For the initial fully inverted state, \(C_{\mathrm{in}}=O(n)\). The retained excitation fraction \(Q/n\) has an \(O(n\,\mathrm{polylog}\,n)\)-gate effect circuit. Fixed two-spin correlations and bounded functions of the photon counters also have explicit inexpensive readouts.
If only independently prepared copies are available, use ordinary sampling instead; the corresponding statistical dependence is \(O(\delta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta))\), not \(O(\delta^{-1})\).
A full spatial correlation map
The result also supports a conventional classical output rather than one scalar. Choose independent random local Pauli measurement axes. For a two-site Pauli observable \(P_i^\alpha P_j^\beta\), the estimator
\[
9m_im_j\,
\mathbf 1\{a_i=\alpha,\ a_j=\beta\}
\]
is unbiased and bounded. Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound show that
\[
O\!\left(\delta^{-2}\log(n/\zeta)\right)

shots suffice for all one- and two-site Pauli expectations.
The classical postprocessing costs \(O(n^2)\) per shot. Writing the complete map requires \(O(n^2)\) output numbers and, if accumulated in memory, quadratic classical storage. These costs are not hidden in the simulation bound.

The large collective rate is real
It is not merely a poor normalisation convention. For example, include a mode
\[
L_1=\sqrt{\gamma/2}\sum_i\sigma_i^-.
\]
On a \(q\)-excitation Dicke state,
\[
\langle L_1^\dagger L_1\rangle
=\frac{\gamma}{2}q(n-q+1),
\]
which is \(\Theta(\gamma n^2)\) for \(q\) near \(n/2\). A second nonuniform mode, disordered diagonal interactions and local exchange can break the permutation-symmetric dynamics without removing this large rate.
The useful distinction is between that instantaneous collective rate and the much smaller sustained excitation-supply budget established by (4).
The advantage is not guaranteed for the initial cooperative flash alone. At \(T=\Theta(1/(\gamma n))\), the bounds can be comparable. The stronger application is late-time populations, delayed emission, and driven correlations on longer physical times. Nor does the result certify that a chosen \(T\) is sufficient to reach a stationary state.
8. Input accuracy, physical scope, and status
The result does not make model acquisition free. If the supplied coefficients approximate another model with
\[
\|C-C'\|_F\le e_C,\qquad
\|H-H'\|\le e_H,
\]
and total pump/dephasing rate discrepancies \(e_p,e_\kappa\), then
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi_T-\Phi'_T\|_\diamond
\le
T\left(
2e_H+4ns\,e_C+2n e_C^2+2e_p+2e_\kappa
\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]
This follows from the jump-stack perturbation bound and the Duhamel formula for channels. It supplies an explicit accuracy requirement for input coefficients; it does not obtain laboratory calibration data or certify a microscopic Markov approximation.
The event bound is also representation-specific. It uses collective lowering, local raising and the stated \(Z_i\)-dephasing representation. Unitary mixing of the photon-output modes preserves the total loss budget, but arbitrary Lindblad gauge changes need not preserve the event count.
Several limitations remain important. Strong pumping or driving can enlarge \(R\) enough to remove the advantage. A dominant non-diagonal Hamiltonian leaves the \(hT\) cost intact. Symmetric, independent-decay, or otherwise exactly solvable subclasses may have better algorithms. Retardation, non-Markovian field dynamics and hardware fault-tolerance overhead are outside the stated logical-gate theorem.
For an unnormalised observable of norm \(S\), an absolute target error \(\delta_{\mathrm{abs}}\) requires using \(\delta=\delta_{\mathrm{abs}}/S\) in the estimation bound. Rare conditional states and relative accuracy on tiny photon probabilities therefore do not become free.
The new tilted-number inequality was checked on a driven three-spin model with pumping and dephasing while varying collective-loss strength from zero to \(10^3\); the relative operator bound remained independent of that strength, as the proof predicts. Separate small-system calculations checked the collective-factor and photon-balance identities. These are algebraic diagnostics, not a quantum-circuit execution or independent proof review.
The substantive advance is the combination of an excitation-supply tail certificate, an explicit interference-preserving jump circuit, and a fixed coherent history simulation. Together they give a total-resource improvement for specified driven collective-emission workloads—including actual observable estimation—rather than only a favourable abstract oracle count. Historical priority and independent correctness verification remain outstanding.
