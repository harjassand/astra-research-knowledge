# Separate attack: small bounded-range Pauli proposal faults

Date: 16 October 2026. This corollary was assigned after the original
Sections 1–2 audit and is not used to validate that audit.

## Verdict

The envelope and block argument extend to small, instantaneous,
bounded-range Pauli-string proposal faults, including acceptance depending
jointly on both CSS syndromes and finite-range correlations produced by
shared local proposal primitives. The proof does not require either actual
CSS marginal to be Markov. For the full lifetime consequence, the original
later cluster and decoder arguments are still required, with the fixed
guard and local-action diameters enlarged as below.

The proposal-level freshness hypothesis is substantive. Merely saying
“finite-range spatial correlations” and that each individual stream is
Poisson does not imply independent joint time slabs. A concrete delayed
neighbor-copy process below disproves that inference. The intended
fresh local Poisson uniformization model satisfies the stronger hypothesis.

Additive bad local correction actions are covered directly. Missing ideal
correction actions require a reliable base clock, or an explicit marked
clock modification; they cannot be concealed as an identity Pauli proposal
while retaining the original envelope's death at that missing action.

## 1. Precise proposal model and quantifiers

Index primitive proposal streams by fine cell anchor `a` and one of a
fixed finite number of types. A stream has constant rate `gamma_(a,tau)`,
and

    sum_tau gamma_(a,tau) <= nu

for every anchor, uniformly in `L`, time and syndrome. The roots are fresh
Poisson random measures with independent increments in disjoint time
slabs, jointly with the ideal sweep clock primitives. Independent roots
are sufficient. More generally the joint primitive field may have a
fixed spatial dependence range and independent increments as a field in
disjoint time slabs. The ideal vertex clocks themselves must retain the
independence and rate needed by their original clearance calculation.

At each proposal, a finite mark selects a Pauli string whose qubit bases
lie in the anchor's radius-`q` fine-cell box. A syndrome-dependent acceptance
probability in `[0,1]` decides whether to apply it. It may inspect both CSS
syndromes. It must not inspect the encoded logical state or reference.
For a time-independent local Lindbladian corollary, the acceptance is a
fixed function of a bounded-range joint stabilizer pattern; its read
radius is another fixed constant. The uniformly bounded rate is realized
by the state-independent stream and a fresh uniform acceptance mark.

Spatially correlated instantaneous faults may be generated by one root
proposal that writes at several nearby delivery anchors, within fixed
radius `r` of its root. Group their product into one Pauli string. Its
support is within radius `q+r` of the root; correlated outcomes within
that root mark are allowed. The total intensity `nu` is charged per root
anchor, counting each shared primitive once. Equivalently, if using a
more general finite-dependent random-measure construction, charge the
proposal intensity and correlation support explicitly. An uncharged
remote common source is not a finite-range primitive model.

All `q,r`, type counts, read radii and dependence ranges are fixed before
`b,L,T`. Put `Q=q+r+1`; it bounds the fine-cell radius of potential syndrome
vertices from a root proposal. If `q` is already defined as a bound on
both sectors' syndrome vertices rather than on qubit bases, the extra
one is unnecessary.

## 2. Exact envelope despite non-Markov sector marginals

Write a proposal Pauli, up to phase, as `X^A Z^B`. Its primal syndrome
increment is `partial B`, and its dual syndrome increment is `partial A`.
At every proposal, accepted or rejected, set the vertices of these two
increments to one in their respective analysis envelopes. A retained
syndrome support changes only at those vertices if accepted, and does
not change if rejected. Thus the same pathwise domination proof works.
There is no need to compute an accepted fault intensity in either sector.

The proposal Pauli may be selected by its fresh mark. If its selection
also depends on the state, use the union of all possible boundary-vertex
sets for that marked candidate, or simply its whole radius-`Q` potential
vertex box. This conservative update is monotone and independent of the
input. The original stated fixed-string/variable-acceptance formulation
already has this property without the union.

Example disproving a hidden marginal-Markov assumption: propose `Z_f`
at rate `nu`, but accept only when one specified dual `X`-sector syndrome
edge is occupied. The same all-zero primal sector has Z-transition rate
zero or `nu`, depending on the other sector. The primal marginal does
not have an autonomous transition rule. Its proposal-driven envelope
nevertheless sees the same rate-`nu` births in both cases. The test
enumerates 65,536 joint state/proposal combinations and verifies both
sector support inclusions under an acceptance function reading both
syndromes.

A finite Pauli string may have empty syndrome increment. Then it creates
no envelope infection. In a sufficiently large torus its fixed-radius
support lies in a contractible box; if it is closed in a CSS sector it is
a stabilizer boundary there. It cannot silently cause a macroscopic
logical flip. A six-face 3-cube boundary is the exact regression example.

## 3. Literal enlarged primitive support and probability

Keep the original forced-input guard and the original clock-chain and
clearance flags. Let `G_z` be the union of radius-`R+2` coarse cells, with
fine vertex side length

    N = (2R+5)b.

Declare proposal-bad for target `(z,n)` if *any root proposal* with anchor
in

    H_z = G_z + [-Q,Q]^4

occurs during the slab. This is deliberately stronger than testing
accepted proposals or testing their realized boundary. Every proposal
capable of creating a syndrome vertex in `G_z` is included. The box
contains exactly `(N+2Q)^4` fine-cell anchors. Linearity of expected counts
and the per-root-anchor intensity bound give

    P(proposal-bad) <= nu (N+2Q)^4 Delta.

This union bound requires no spatial independence of proposal streams.
The same bound holds conditional on the complete primitive past, because
the proposal increments, not their accepted actions, are fresh.

The proposal flag reads only increments rooted in `H_z`. Its coarse
support radius is at most `R+2+ceil(Q/b)`; `R+Q+3` is a simple safe radius
uniform for every `b>=1`. Clock prefixes and the finite clearance cone
are already smaller. If the primitive field has a further spatial
dependence radius `r_0`, add that fixed radius in the overlap/dependence
calculation, or absorb its local-root fan-out into `Q`. This fixes a
spatial dependence bound independent of `b,L,T`. For example one can
use coarse support radius `K=R+Q+r_0+4` as an intentionally conservative
uniform bound and color at separation greater than `2K`.

All three flags are functions of the *same slab's raw joint increments*.
Therefore distinct slab fields are independent even though accepted
faults, actual syndromes, and their rates are correlated across slabs.
No acceptance history is conditioned into the bad flags. On a guard
with no proposal, the original uniform localization and halfspace
forcing proof applies unchanged. Exterior proposals are excluded from
target influence by the original no-m-clock-chain event; a proposal
with a direct boundary vertex in the guard would already be flagged.

## 4. Noncircular positive threshold

Fix the physical radii and the joint primitive dependence range, then
fix the original `C,R` and the enlarged dependence support `K`. The
coarse rule is unchanged. Choose the relevant small primitive bad-field
target `delta_*` for these fixed constants. Choose fixed `b` sufficiently
large that each of the original clock terms is at most `delta_*/3`.
Finally choose

    nu/kappa <= delta_* / [3 C b (((2R+5)b+2Q)^4)].

Then the proposal term is at most `delta_*/3`. This upper bound is
strictly positive and independent of system size and storage time.
If original face noise is also retained, either include it in the
per-cell proposal budget, or allocate another fixed fraction of
`delta_*` and keep its separate positive rate bound. No term may be
counted twice as independent when it shares a root event.

Thus the proof's local parameters preserve a fixed positive fault
density, cold code-space start, and a time-independent generator.
The local primitive guard in a torus replay is enlarged by the fixed
constant `K`. For sufficiently large `M`, its injective lift still
exists and the exponential-in-`L` scale is unchanged.

## 5. Decoder and quantum-channel interfaces for this corollary

The whole-slab support cover still traces an occupied coordinate to an
initial occupied ancestor or a proposal-bad flag; it does not use
single-face births. The original fine-to-coarse connectivity constants
may be retained because the good-block trace uses the same m-clock
prefix. Newly born vertices in a bad block are assigned to its next
coarse zero layer, regardless of endpoint clearance.

At a proposal the actual local 2-chain `F` in a sector can contain many
faces, but its support diameter is bounded by a fixed constant `d_Q`.
Every changed old syndrome component touches the boundary-vertex box of
`F`. If all old components have diameter less than `r=L/32`, their union,
the changed new components, `F`, and all component-box fillings lie in
the fixed support box expanded by `r` in every coordinate, of side at
most `2r+d_Q`. For sufficiently large `L`, this is below `rho=L/8` and
embeds in the torus. Hence

    F + D(s) + D(s+partial F)

is a closed 2-chain in a contractible box, and is a stabilizer boundary.
The same unaffected-component filling cancellation used by the original
decoder applies. Disconnected parts of `F` cause no difficulty because
all lie in the one bounded support box. Empty-boundary local strings
are covered by the same argument.

Finally, a jump `sqrt(gamma) Q f(stabilizers)` acts on each code-initial
Pauli-label state with a scalar syndrome-dependent amplitude before
the Pauli action. Its dissipator induces the corresponding joint Pauli
label transition rate. Construct the classical forward equation on the
*joint* labels `(A,B)` and solve the quantum master equation by its Pauli
mixture, exactly as for the ideal generator. Joint transition rates may
depend on both sectors. They remain independent of the encoded logical
state and reference. Good trajectories leave only stabilizers after the
fixed decoder, and twice the bad-trajectory probability bounds the
unhalved diamond norm. Marginal-sector Markovness is never invoked.

## 6. Bad correction actions and two precise limitations

An additional small-rate syndrome-conditioned wrong Pauli correction is
exactly a proposal fault above. The condition may be an erroneous joint
syndrome rule; the proposal envelope ignores acceptance and pays for all
its potential writes. An arbitrary bounded coherent jump or Hamiltonian
perturbation has not been reduced to this model.

If an intended ideal attempt is omitted, applying the original envelope
death is unsound: at the lower-left corner of a `2x2` membrane perimeter,
the physical center remains in the syndrome but that envelope clock
erases it. An identity Pauli proposal has empty boundary and does not
repair the discrepancy. This exact one-event countermodel is checked.

There is a useful valid repair when the per-attempt failure probability
has a uniform bound `p_max<1` and is fresh at proposal level. Split each
rate-`kappa` attempted clock by an independent uniform mark. Marks above
`p_max` give an independent always-correct base clock of rate
`kappa_eff=kappa(1-p_max)`. Treat *all* remaining marks, including ones
that happened to act correctly, as bounded-range Pauli proposals of
total per-vertex intensity `kappa p_max`; on these the envelope preserves
old support and adds the potential boundary. Their realized action may
be a wrong Pauli, correct Pauli, or identity. This supplies an exact
base-sweep-plus-small-proposals decomposition. Apply the threshold with
`kappa_eff` and charge the total rare-mark intensity per cell. Similarly,
syndrome-dependent good rates bounded uniformly below by `kappa_eff`
can be decomposed into that common base plus uniformly bounded residual
proposal rates. Small additive rates alone do not justify arbitrary
suppression of the base clock.

The second limitation concerns temporal freshness. On two neighboring
cells let `N_0` be a stationary Poisson process and define
`N_1(t)=N_0(t-Delta)`. Each individual stream is Poisson of the same
constant intensity. Different neighbor pairs may be independent, so
the spatial dependence range is one. Nevertheless

    A={N_0 has a mark in slab 0},
    B={N_1 has a mark in slab 1}

are the same event. If `p=1-exp(-nu Delta)`, then `P(A and B)=p`, not `p^2`.
The slab fields are not independent. The future copied mark is already
known from the joint past, so this process is not a bounded-hazard fresh
joint uniformization primitive. It disproves only the implication from
per-stream Poisson marginals and spatial finite dependence to joint
freshness. Genuine instantaneous shared local Poisson roots, as in
Sections 1–4, do not have this defect.
