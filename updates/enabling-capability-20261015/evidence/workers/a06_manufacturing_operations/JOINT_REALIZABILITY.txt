Conditional joint realizability of spring-and-stop rotor networks
2026-10-15. Internal derivation; not externally reviewed. Novelty UNKNOWN.

Purpose

A candidate control word is useless if its threshold matrix, hysteresis widths,
and fabricated couplings cannot coexist. This note gives an exact gate for a
restricted, explicit mechanism. It does not assert a gate for all passive
mechanisms or for the finite-rest-length hardware in the source experiment.

Model

N rigid planar rotors have symmetric stops theta_i = +/- a_i, with
0 < a_i < pi/2. All driving springs have common attachment lever L > 0 and
rod-to-pivot distance y > 0. Rotor i has positive driving stiffness k_i and
horizontal offset x_i. Each coupled pair has two identical springs with
stiffness k_ij = k_ji > 0, common lever ell > 0, and sign g_ij = g_ji in {+1,-1}
selected by parallel versus crossed attachment. All spring rest lengths are
zero. Springs obey Hooke's law, bars and stops are rigid, and stability is
quasistatic. Uncoupled pairs have k_ij = 0. Physical collision avoidance and
fabrication are additional obligations.

These hypotheses imply the exact torques

tau_drive_i = L k_i cos(theta_i) [gamma + x_i + y tan(theta_i)],
tau_pair_ij = 2 g_ij k_ij ell^2 sin(theta_j-theta_i).

For neighbours at their stops theta_j=s_j a_j, solving zero torque at the two
stops of i gives

gamma_i^+(s) = c_i + h_i - sum_j J_ij s_j,
gamma_i^-(s) = c_i - h_i - sum_j J_ij s_j,

c_i = -x_i,
J_ij = [2 g_ij k_ij ell^2 / (L k_i)] sin(a_j),
h_i = tan(a_i) [y - sum_j J_ij cot(a_j)].                 (1)

Thus the target centers, widths, and matrix are not independently adjustable.

Theorem (exact in this ideal model)

Let desired c_i be real, h_i > 0, and J have zero diagonal. The conditional
thresholds above can be realized in this model if and only if both conditions
hold:

(A) There exists a positive diagonal matrix D such that DJ is symmetric.
(B) There exists v with every v_i > 0 such that
             [diag(h) + J] v = 1.                       (2)

This is an existence theorem with unbounded allowed geometric and stiffness
parameters. It is not a bounded-parts or precision theorem.

Necessity

Set D_ii=k_i sin(a_i). Then

D_ii J_ij = (2 g_ij k_ij ell^2/L) sin(a_i)sin(a_j)
           = D_jj J_ji.

Set q_i=cot(a_i)>0. Multiplying (1) by q_i gives

             [diag(h)+J]q = y 1.

Therefore v=q/y is positive and satisfies (2).

Sufficiency

Choose D and v satisfying (A),(B). Choose any y>0 and set

a_i=arctan(1/(y v_i)),  k_i=D_ii/sin(a_i),  x_i=-c_i.

For a nonzero pair use g_ij=sign(J_ij) and

k_ij = L |J_ij| D_ii / [2 ell^2 sin(a_i)sin(a_j)].

Condition (A) makes the two formulas for k_ij and k_ji identical and ensures
the two directions have the same sign. Substitution gives the desired J.
Equation (2) gives the desired h. Centers follow from x_i.

The construction can also exclude stable equilibria with any free rotor
strictly between its stops. For arbitrary neighbour angles in their allowed
intervals, write tau_i=A_i cos(theta_i)+B_i sin(theta_i). A lower bound is

B_i/(L k_i) >= h_i y v_i
              - sum_{j:J_ij>0} J_ij [sqrt(1+y^2 v_j^2)-y v_j].

Because sqrt(1+q^2)-q <= 1/(2q), this is positive when

y^2 > max_i {sum_{j:J_ij>0} J_ij/(2 v_j) / (h_i v_i)}.

At an interior torque zero, the angular derivative is B_i/cos(theta_i)>0.
It is unstable along coordinate i, so it cannot be a stable equilibrium.
This does not prove a unique avalanche path or bound dynamic overshoot.

Consequences and exact counterexamples

1. Opposite pair signs and one-way nonzero edges violate (A).
2. On every nonzero cycle, the product of couplings in the forward direction
   equals the product in the reverse direction. Conversely, pair sign/support
   consistency and these cycle equalities give positive diagonal symmetry on
   each connected component.
3. The sign-compatible matrix

       J = [[0,2,1], [1,0,2], [2,1,0]]

   fails: its directed triangle products are 8 and 1.
4. Cycle symmetry alone is insufficient. Take h=(1,1), J_12=3, J_21=1/10.
   It is diagonally symmetrizable, but the unique solution of (2) is
   v=(-20/7,9/7), which is not positive. These abstract parameters cannot be
   jointly realized by the stated spring-and-stop mechanism.

The supplied audit.py verifies the two arithmetic counterexamples with exact
fractions. The proof, not finite checking, establishes the stated theorem.

Scope and adversarial qualifications

The real public experiment has finite spring rest lengths of several cm,
finite angles, friction, and tolerances. The exact source kinematics do not
obey all the simplifying hypotheses above. Finite rest length, unequal arms,
extra components, or active/local control can evade this theorem. This note
does not contradict the demonstrated latching or period-two behavior.

Large y in the construction shrinks the stop angles and changes spring
stiffness and precision requirements. The formulas do not establish a cheap,
scalable, manufacturable compiler. Actual standard spring ranges, angular
resolution, crossings, swept volumes, dissipation, fatigue, and ramp rates
remain part of the acquisition-inclusive problem.

For a fixed chosen diagonal symmetrizer d_i=D_ii, the exact construction costs
in parameters are

  a_i = arctan(1/(y v_i)),
  k_i = d_i sqrt(1+y^2 v_i^2),
  k_ij = [L |J_ij| d_i/(2 ell^2)]
         sqrt(1+y^2 v_i^2) sqrt(1+y^2 v_j^2).

Thus this particular fixed-d construction has angles O(1/y), driving
stiffnesses O(y), and coupling stiffnesses O(y^2). Multiplying every stiffness
by a common factor leaves the ideal thresholds unchanged, but changes force
and energy relative to friction, manufacturing error and noise. Consequently
these formulas are not a universal lower bound on physical stiffness or
energy. They show why a realizability proof alone is not a full-cost result.

Even a realizable matrix does not guarantee all 2^N patterns are stable at a
common drive gamma_0. A sufficient and necessary condition for every corner
pattern to be stable there, away from equality, is

       h_i > |gamma_0-c_i| + sum_j |J_ij|   for every i.

Strong interactions can destroy common-drive storage states. The measured
two-rotor latch has only two stable corner states at gamma=0.
