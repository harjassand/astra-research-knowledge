Independent consequence audit, 2026-10-15.

Exact statement audited
Let H=⊗_{i=1}^k C^{d_i}, k>=2, with all finite d_i>=1. Let K be any complex
operator on H of rank at most two. Suppose either ran K or ran K* contains
a nonzero vector u which, after permuting sites, is
 u=u_12⊗u_3⊗...⊗u_k,
where u_12 has Schmidt rank at most two across site 1 versus site 2.
If the grouped co-Choi lemma gives -3C12<=S12<=C12 for every such u_12,
then q_k(K)>=0, with q_k(K)=Σ_J(-1/2)^|J| ||Tr_J K||²_HS.
For k=3, this closes support rays product across one site versus the
remaining pair, PROVIDED that pair has Schmidt rank at most two. It does
not close every biseparable ray in arbitrary dimensions, nor all rank-two
coefficient operators. The independently reported stronger grouped bound
S12>=-(11/9)C12 implies the required lower bound.

1. General complex reference reduction is valid
For Φ=Λ_p⊗Λ_q, Λ_d(X)=Tr(X)I-X/2, and V=V1⊗V2 unitary,
 Φ(VXV*)=VΦ(X)V*.
For the reference maps M_u(E_ab)=L_a A_u^{-1}L_b*, with
 L_a=Φ(|a><u|), A_u=Φ(uu*), direct basis expansion gives
 M_{Vu}(X)=V M_u(V*XV)V*.
With coChoi(E)=Σ_ab E_ba⊗E(E_ab), this becomes
 S_{Vu}=(V⊗V)S_u(V*⊗V*).
C is fixed by this conjugation. Therefore Loewner bounds proved for real
nonnegative Schmidt coefficients are valid for arbitrary complex u.
A nonzero Schmidt-rank-two reference is a nonzero scalar times a local
unitary image of |00>+t|11>, with t>=0. Rank one is t=0 after relabeling.
There is no missing infinite-t reference: choose a nonzero first Schmidt
coefficient before rescaling. If p=1 or q=1, the reference is fully product.

2. The co-Choi convention and positivity bridge have the right transpose
For any linear map E,
 coChoi(E)=Choi(E composed with T).
Consequently coChoi(E)>=0 implies E composed with T is completely
positive, so E=(E composed with T) composed with T maps PSD matrices to
PSD matrices. Complete copositivity, not complete positivity of E itself,
is the conclusion. This is exactly sufficient here.
A_u=(<u|⊗I)C(|u>⊗I)>0 since C>=2^{-k}I.
No conjugate reference is inserted in this compression formula.
For product reference blocks, both A_u and the L_a factor, hence M_u
factors. The same permutation identifies their co-Choi matrices with
products C12⊗C3⊗... and S12⊗S3⊗....
The independently reconstructed single-site normalized spectrum is
{1,-1/3}, omitting -1/3 only at dimension one. Thus the product of all
remaining sites has spectrum in [-1/3,1]. Multiplying this interval by
[-3,1] never yields an eigenvalue greater than 1. Congruencing by the
positive square roots of C_i gives coChoi(Φ-M_u)>=0.
Applying Φ-M_u to xx* proves
 D-L A_u^{-1}L*>=0,
 D=Φ(xx*), L=Φ(xu*).
The order is L A^{-1}L*, not the ordinary reversed Schur order.

3. Exact complex crossed contraction, with no orthogonality assumption
Define the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product conjugate-linearly in its first
argument. For each J, direct coefficient summation proves
 <Tr_J |u><v|,Tr_J |x><y|>_HS
   = <y| [Tr_{J^c}(|x><u|)⊗I_{J^c}] |v>,
where the tensor factors on the right are restored to their original order.
The term with J empty is <u,x><y,v>; the term with J all sites is
<u,v><y,x>. This checks both boundary contractions and the absence of an
extra transpose. Summing with (-1/2)^|J| gives
 b_k(|u><v|,|x><y|)=<y,Φ(xu*)v>.
If ran K contains u, choose x completing u to a basis of ran K (or omit
it at rank one). There exist unrestricted complex v,y with
 K=|u><v|+|x><y|.
The v,y vectors do not need to be orthogonal, normalized, or product.
With a=<v,A_u v>, d=<y,Dy>, g=<y,Lv>, the exact identity is
 q_k(K)=a+d+2Re(g).
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 |g|² = |<A_u^{-1/2}L*y,A_u^{1/2}v>|²
       <= a <y,L A_u^{-1}L*y> <= ad.
Both a,d are nonnegative. Therefore q_k(K)>=(sqrt(a)-sqrt(d))²>=0.
Since every partial trace commutes with adjoints,
 q_k(K*)=q_k(K).
This proves the same result for a qualifying ray in ran K*. There is no
need to align the input/output support planes or to assume K is normal.

4. Exact scope separation, including dimensions above three
Use the following unnormalized vectors in (C^4)^⊗3:
 u=000+011,
 x=101+212+323+330,
 v=100+111,
 y=002+210+021+332+303,
and K=uv*+xy*. These four vectors have disjoint computational supports.
They form two orthogonal two-planes, so rank K=2, K²=0, K is nonnormal,
and the combined input/output support has dimension four. The reference
u=|0>⊗(|00>+|11>) has paired Schmidt rank two, as does v.
All six minimal one-site input/output support projections equal I_4.
Thus every local output/input support overlap is 4, exceeding the cited
incumbent threshold 2, and no local unitary compression to qutrit local
spaces can represent this operator.
Neither support plane contains a fully product ray: projecting αu+βx
onto first-site |0> gives α(|00>+|11>), forcing α=0 for any fully product
ray; x itself has first-site Schmidt rank at least two. For the input
plane use first-site |1>, v and y.
Neither plane is contained in any larger locally orthogonal product code:
in such a code every vector's three local Schmidt ranks equal the number
of nonzero codeword coefficients, while u and v have ranks (1,2,2).
Every single-site partial trace is nonzero. Exact squared norms in site
order 0,1,2 are
 ||K||²=24;
 ||Tr_0 K||²,||Tr_1 K||²,||Tr_2 K||² = 5,7,7;
 ||Tr_01 K||²,||Tr_02 K||²,||Tr_12 K||² = 2,1,4;
 |Tr K|²=0.
Hence q_3(K)=24-(5+7+7)/2+(2+1+4)/4=65/4.
This example proves the hypothesis has cases outside the cited individual
normal/common-plane/product-ray/local-overlap/local-traceless/product-code
sectors and outside a purely qutrit theorem's stated local dimensions.
It is not by itself proof of a sector, nor exhaustive novelty evidence.
