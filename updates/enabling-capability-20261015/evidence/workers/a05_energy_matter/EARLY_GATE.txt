Primary paper: Miličić et al., Pulsed electrolysis – explained,
Faraday Discussions 246 (2023), 179–197, DOI 10.1039/D3FD00030C.
Strongest initial incumbent: equally informed steady-state operation and
time-sharing of measured operating points; the source's existing nonlinear
frequency response (NFR) method already analyzes DC output shifts. A later
power-aware NFR study (Applied Energy 396, 2025, 126312,
DOI 10.1016/j.apenergy.2025.126312) explicitly warns that lower mean voltage
under periodic current does not imply lower mean power. That water-electrolysis
result is prior art and a comparator requirement, not a proof about CO2 cells.

Candidate operation: source-grounded test of whether a pulse produces a
target-output/power vector outside the convex envelope of measured static
operating modes, retaining peak current, switching, preparation, electrode
aging, gas assay, and full cell power. This is an audit construction, with
novelty unestablished. A strict envelope violation would still require
prospective independent validation and hardware delivery.

GO: inspect and replay actual released data.
HOLD: scientific/industrial physical advantage, pending availability of

STOP trigger: the public record measures only working-electrode potential or
proxy currents and therefore cannot identify the target, or a matched static
