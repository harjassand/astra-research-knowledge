One-state electrochemical pulse audit: exact static-occupation comparator

Status: internally derived conditional statement; no novelty claim. Puteanus
et al. (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.ijhydene.2025.02.348, already use circuit power
balance and Jensen's inequality to exclude an efficiency gain in their PEM
water-electrolyzer model. The construction below makes the comparator explicit
for multiple chemical products and does not assume convex power as a function
of one desired-product rate. It is not evidence that a real CO2 electrode has
only one internal state.

Assumptions and scientific target

Let x(t) be the electrode polarization in a fixed sign convention. Let f(x) be
total faradaic current, r(x) in R^d the vector of product rates, C(x)>=0 the
differential double-layer capacitance, and R>=0 a constant series resistance.
The only memory is the stored double-layer charge. The source equations are

    i(t)=f(x(t))+C(x(t))*x'(t),     u(t)=x(t)+R*i(t).

Assume x is absolutely continuous and periodic, all stated integrals exist,
and C(x)*x' is square integrable. f, C and r are single-valued functions of x
on the visited compact interval. No concentration, coverage, morphology,
temperature, catalyst aging, or path-dependent hidden state is included.
Every static comparison state must be admissible under the same actuator,
material, feed and environmental constraints. The electrical quantity in this
model must be distinguished from unmeasured whole-cell and wall power.

At a static state x, the source voltage is u_s=x+R*f(x), power is

    p_s(x)=x*f(x)+R*f(x)^2,

and product vector is r(x). The scientifically meaningful comparator matches
the desired product and the constrained coproducts/purity, rather than only
matching mean potential. Other requirements (throughput, surface area, aging,
peak input, gas feed, etc.) remain separate matching constraints.

Exact identity and proof

With <.> denoting the time average over a complete period,

    <u*i> = <p_s(x)> + R*<C(x)^2*x'(t)^2>.                 (1)

Indeed, expand (x+R*i)*i with i=f+C*x'. The difference from the right side is

    <[x*C(x)+2*R*f(x)*C(x)]*x'>.

The bracket is a function of x alone. Its primitive returns to the same value
after one closed period, so the average is exactly zero. Similarly <i>=<f(x)>.
No small-signal, sinusoidal, perturbation-order, or constant-capacitance
assumption is used. If R>0 and C(x)*x' is nonzero on a set of positive measure,
the excess in (1) is strictly positive.

Let mu be the distribution of time spent at each x by the periodic trajectory.
A static ensemble (or a limit of sufficiently long static holds with
transition costs charged) using the same mu has exactly the same average
product vector int r(x)dmu and power int p_s(x)dmu. Equation (1) shows the
periodic trajectory cannot have a smaller electrical power in this model.
The equivalent finite-source computation is a linear program: minimize
sum lambda_j p_j subject to sum lambda_j r_j=target, sum lambda_j=1, lambda_j>=0.
For one scalar product target, all pairs of measured static modes can be
enumerated, so no general optimizer is required. replay_audit.py implements
that computation for the public CO2 source labels, using current as a proxy
cost only; it never promotes those unmatched historical mixtures to a full
energy comparison.

This is an ideal static-occupation lower boundary, not a free physical switch.
Finite hold length, transition time, extra gas volume, catalyst response and
loss of stationarity must be measured for an implementable comparator.
If full static curves are unknown, beating a finite sampled envelope does
not establish that the one-state model is false: an unmeasured static state
can be better. A measured feasible static mixture that dominates a pulse is
a sufficient local comparator only after matching preparation, product vector,
peak constraints and all transition costs.

Why the water-electrolysis DC theorem must not be overgeneralized

Equation (1) does not say that one constant operating point is always optimal
for a selected chemical product. A simple dimensionless example is x in [0,1],
f(x)=x, r_CO(x)=x^3, r_other(x)=x-x^3. The partial currents are nonnegative and
sum to f. With R=0, static power is x^2. For desired CO current 1/2, a single
static point uses x=2^(-1/3) and power 2^(-2/3), whereas half time at x=0 and
half at x=1 gives power 1/2. The latter has a different coproduct rate. This
example is algebra only, not a model fitted to silver or a proposed device.
It shows why a correct comparator must use the target product vector and
why the convex-power water-electrolysis result cannot alone settle selective
CO2 chemistry. For R>0 the same static costs acquire factor (1+R), while
periodic capacitor charging adds the nonnegative term in (1).

Reference dependence of the released half-cell energy proxy

Write J=-<I>>0 for net cathodic current and q_CO>0 for CO-equivalent current.
If a reported electrode-power proxy P is shifted by changing the reference
zero of cathodic voltage magnitude by c, then

    e_c = (P+c*J)/q_CO.

For two protocols A and B,

    e_c(A)-e_c(B) = [P_A/q_A-P_B/q_B]
                    +c*[J_A/q_A-J_B/q_B].                (2)

Thus that contrast is reference-invariant if and only if J_A/q_A=J_B/q_B,
equivalently the CO faradaic efficiencies agree. A change of electrode
reference cannot change real two-terminal work, because both terminal
potentials shift together. Equation (2) therefore diagnoses an invalid
promotion of a single-electrode proxy to total energy. It does not invalidate
GC CO observations, and it does not assert that the source authors intended
the proxy as a complete plant accounting.

The V2 source values for the 0.3 V RMS protocols at 0.1 Hz and 100 Hz give an
explicit sign-changing witness saved in RESULTS.json. Their normalized
reported powers change ordering under a reference shift smaller than the
source's own Ag/AgCl-to-RHE conversion (0.6562 V at pH 7.8). Full terminal
voltage is not present in that release, so neither ordering supplies the
desired full energy ranking.

Even reference-invariant contrasts do not identify unobserved losses.
In potentiostatic three-electrode control an added positive series resistance
outside the working-reference sensing path can leave the controlled
electrode trajectory and product outputs unchanged while adding R_extra*<I^2>
to supplied power, provided source compliance suffices. This is a family of
physically passive observational completions, not a claim that the source
apparatus contained such an extra resistor. It proves why full terminal
power or an independently validated loss bound is needed. The experiment's
uncompensated working-reference resistance is not all counter-electrode,
separator, cabling, converter and auxiliary loss.

Decisive prospective interface

The smallest useful test acquires simultaneous cell-terminal voltage and
current with validated bandwidth/phase, calibrated CO and H2 output plus
liquid-product and carbon balance, and a same-electrode steady curve spanning
the pulse's actual interfacial-potential range. Compare complete product-rate
vectors at the same feed, temperature, area and cumulative aging. Fit no
unmeasured dynamic product label from current alone. Measure wall/converter,
fluid pumping, heating, gas handling and reset costs separately. A pulse that
survives that comparator may exploit coverage/concentration/morphology memory
outside the null model; current source access does not implement this test.
