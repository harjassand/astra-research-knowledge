Photon emitter census: conditional nuisance cancellation, exact failures, and cost
2026-10-15 — a09_photon_invention — internally derived, not externally reviewed

Decision
========

No new scientific measurement capability has been demonstrated. There is a
precise conditional four-setting operation that removes arbitrary independent
Poisson backgrounds and an unknown common fluorescence time profile at the
level of exact observation laws. Its emitter-count identifiability proof is
given below. The necessary settings were not acquired in the public archive.
The underlying physical action is known detector-decoy attenuation, not a new
detector or a new physical contrast. Novelty of the particular self-calibration
argument is UNKNOWN. No finite-sample stability or end-to-end advantage is proved.

An attempted simplification using only earliest photon times fails decisively:
an unknown, plausible biexponential lifetime can counterfeit emitter count.
Even with a perfectly known lifetime, any adaptive attenuation/earliest-time
policy needs at least 71,403 pulses for a stated binary decision, whereas an
ideal two-detector coincidence policy succeeds in 1,663 pulses. These are
mathematical bounds for explicit ideal hypotheses, not measured experiment costs.

For the acquired 15-channel raw data, the incumbent retains all timestamps,
coincidences, and calibrated detector response. It can calculate every proposed
contrast. Digital thinning, first-arrival selection, or cumulant calculations
cannot add statistical information to these same records. A computational or
hardware saving could still be useful, but neither is established here.

Scientific target and evidence boundary
=======================================

The target considered is the number of optically active single-photon emitters
within an observation region. Molecular stoichiometry additionally requires
labeling specificity, labeling efficiency, and a way to exclude optically dark
labels. Localization additionally requires a spatial response calibration that
connects detector marks to physical coordinates. Neither is supplied by a count
theorem alone. An arbitrary antibunched contaminant cannot be distinguished from
a target emitter by assuming that all background is Poisson.

1. Conditional four-setting operation
=====================================

Physically randomize a calibrated, passive, spectrally and spatially uniform
transmission a in {0,r,s,1}, with 0<r<s<1. For example r=1/3 and s=2/3.
Keep excitation fixed. Reset the detector before each isolated excitation and
record its first arrival time, including trials with no arrival. The operation
requires actual observations at every setting. Deleting recorded photons after
an unresolved detector event does not reproduce a physical attenuation trial.

Model premises:
  * A fixed finite number N>=1 of independent emitters contributes at most one
    potentially detected photon each per trial, with probabilities p_i>0.
  * Their conditional arrival-time CDF is the same unknown H(t), independent of
    brightness and transmission. H(0)=0 and its range contains an interval near 0.
  * Optical background is an independent Poisson process with unknown cumulative
    intensity B(t), attenuated by a. Dark background is an independent Poisson
    process with unknown cumulative intensity D(t), unaffected by a.
  * The first-event detector is live at trial start, with known time origin.
    Afterpulsing, crosstalk, uncertain carryover, correlated blinking, varying
    emitter lifetimes, and nonlinear attenuation are not silently included.

Let F(x)=product_{i=1}^N(1-p_i x), and let S_a(t) be the probability of no
detection by time t at transmission a. Independence gives the exact identity

  S_a(t) = exp[-D(t)-a B(t)] F(a H(t)).                         (1)

Define the observable contrast

  J_a(t)=log S_a(t)-a log S_1(t)-(1-a)log S_0(t).               (2)

Then both arbitrary Poisson backgrounds cancel exactly:

  J_a(t)=A_a(H(t)),
  A_a(x)=log F(a x)-a log F(x)
        =sum_{k>=2} c_k(a) m_k x^k,
  c_k(a)=(a-a^k)/k,     m_k=sum_i p_i^k.                      (3)

The scalar time coordinate remains unknown. The paired observable curve
(J_r(t),J_s(t)) nevertheless identifies the positive brightness multiset up
to a common scale, and in particular identifies N at the level of exact laws.

Proof of the paired-curve statement
-----------------------------------

Suppose another finite positive multiset q and unknown common CDF K generate
the same pair of curves. Since A_r starts with the strictly positive term
c_2(r)m_2 x^2, sqrt(A_r(x)) has a positive linear term and a real analytic local
inverse. Hence the equality of the r-curves implies K(t)=phi(H(t)), where phi
is analytic near zero, phi(0)=0, and phi'(0)=c>0. Equality of both curves yields

  A_a^p(x)=A_a^q(phi(x)) for a=r,s.                           (4)

If phi is not linear, let d_n x^n, n>=2, be its first nonlinear term. Comparing
orders 2 through n gives m_k(p)=c^k m_k(q). At order n+1, (4) gives

  c_{n+1}(a)[m_{n+1}(p)-c^{n+1}m_{n+1}(q)]
       =2 c_2(a)m_2(q)c d_n,   for a=r,s.                    (5)

But for every k>=3,

  c_k(a)/c_2(a)=(2/k)(1+a+...+a^{k-2})                     (6)

is strictly increasing in a on (0,1). Equation (5) at r and s is therefore
inconsistent with d_n nonzero. Thus phi(x)=c x, and every power sum of order
k>=2 satisfies m_k(p)=c^k m_k(q).

The rational functions

  sum_i p_i^2/(1-p_i x),    sum_j (c q_j)^2/(1-c q_j x)       (7)

then have identical Taylor series near zero and are identical rational
functions. Their poles and nonzero residues identify each distinct positive
brightness and its multiplicity. Thus the multisets {p_i} and {c q_j} agree,
so the counts agree. This also covers repeated brightness values. Exactly
zero brightness contributes no pole and remains unidentifiable. QED.

This proof is asymptotic identifiability, not a consistent finite procedure
with a usable rate. It invokes an exact continuous curve and all orders. The
instrument samples finite timing bins and finite trials, and recovering tiny
high-order coefficients can be severely unstable. No min-brightness lower bound
means no uniform finite count certificate. The proof also does not identify
positions or the biological identity of emitters.

The common-H premise is substantive. Excitation spectral shifts, differing
lifetimes, heterogeneous dark-state kinetics, and brightness-lifetime coupling
can invalidate it. An apparent paired-curve fit cannot independently establish
that all those physical alternatives are absent. A fresh calibration or
target-selective physical response is still required for a scientific census.

2. One first-arrival curve has an exact lifetime counterfeit
===========================================================

With no background, one detector and one transmission, the earliest detection
has survival S(t)=F(H(t)). Even though a known H turns time into a continuum of
effective collection fractions, an unknown H can absorb the emitter count.

For any finite independent-emitter F, let a=1-F(1) and

  H_star(t)=[1-F(H(t))]/a.                                  (8)

This is a valid CDF when H is a CDF. One emitter with detection probability a
and conditional arrival CDF H_star has exactly the same first-arrival law,
including its no-click mass. If all emitters share the same channel mark law,
the first channel mark can be coupled identically as well.

An explicit eta=0.12 example avoids a merely dark hidden emitter. Two emitters
each have detection probability .06 and exponential conditional lifetime tau.
Their first-arrival survival, with z=exp(-t/tau), is

  (.94+.06 z)^2 = .8836+.1128 z+.0036 z^2.                  (9)

One emitter of probability .1164 has the same law if its conditional lifetime
survival is (94 z+3 z^2)/97. This is a positive mixture of lifetimes tau and
tau/2. Thus an uncalibrated biexponential time profile exactly counterfeits the
entire first-arrival record, not just its mean or fitted decay constant.
The two-emitter model still generates coincidences that the ideal single
emitter cannot generate. The acquired full multichannel timestamps keep that
additional information, so discarding it creates the ambiguity.

3. Shot-cost defeat of earliest-time plus adaptive attenuation
=============================================================

Grant the proposed operation more favorable conditions: no background,
independent isolated pulses, common perfectly known continuous H, perfect
timestamps and reset, no crosstalk or afterpulse, and two known hypotheses.
At total collection efficiency eta, use

  H0: one emitter with detected probability a=eta-eta^2/4;
  H1: two independent emitters, each detected with probability eta/2.
  b=eta^2/4.

At eta=.12 the underlying emission probabilities can be .97 for H0 and
(.5,.5) for H1. These are exactly the matched hypotheses in premise_challenge.
Both hypotheses have the same unattenuated total click probability a=.1164.

Let u in [0,1] be any transmission chosen adaptively from earlier observations.
Write x=H(arrival time), so x ranges from 0 to 1. The unconditional earliest-time
densities and empty-trial masses are

  f0(x|u)=a u,             e0=1-a u;
  f1(x|u)=eta u-2b u^2 x,  e1=1-eta u+b u^2.                (10)

At u=0 both laws are the same empty trial. For u>0,

  KL(P1^u || P0^u) <= chi-square(P1^u || P0^u)
    = b^2 u/a [1-2u+4u^2/3]
      + b^2 u^2(1-u)^2/(1-a u)
    <= b^2/(3a).                                          (11)

The last inequality holds for every 0<a<1 and 0<=u<=1. Subtract the middle
expression from b^2/(3a); the remainder is

  b^2(1-u)[(1-u)^3+(1-a)u(u^2+u+1)]/[3a(1-a u)] >= 0.     (12)

For any fixed n-pulse adaptive policy, including policies that randomize u
continuously, conditional KL chain rule bounds the complete transcript KL
by n b^2/(3a). If a final test has both error probabilities at most alpha<1/2,
data processing through that binary decision requires

  n b^2/(3a) >= (1-2alpha)log[(1-alpha)/alpha].              (13)

At eta=.12, b^2/(3a)=9/242500. With alpha=.05 this gives n>=71,403.
It covers four-setting first-arrival protocols as a special case, even if an
oracle had already supplied the backgrounds and H. Unknown nuisances cannot
make the statistical problem easier.

An ideal balanced beamsplitter plus two detectors instead observes a coincidence
with probability zero under H0 and eta^2/8=.0018 under H1. Deciding H1 upon the
first coincidence has false-positive probability zero and false-negative
probability (1-.0018)^n. Thus n=1,663 suffices. The first-arrival lower bound is
more than 42.9 times this constructive coincidence budget.

The comparison is shot/dose complexity for these explicit simple hypotheses.
It does not charge the price, energy, reset circuitry or calibration of an
extra detector. It does not survive arbitrary background without modification.
It does not show that every possible first-arrival hardware implementation is
less economical. For the actual archive, fifteen detector channels already
exist, so the same-raw-data comparator has their coincidences at no additional
retrospective acquisition cost.

4. Finite saturated click interfaces can hide positive emitters exactly
======================================================================

Assume each detector produces one bit per isolated trial, and all emitters have
the same known spatial routing probabilities. The chance that no detector in
a subset S clicks is F(s_S), where s_S is the total calibrated detection
fraction routed to that subset. The full click-pattern distribution is
equivalent to these no-click probabilities by inclusion-exclusion.

Consider any finite menu of such settings and let s_1,...,s_m be all its
distinct nonzero evaluation values. Choose an m-emitter polynomial F0 with
distinct reciprocal-brightness roots strictly greater than 1. Set

  F_epsilon(x)=F0(x)+(-1)^(m+1)epsilon*x*product_j(x-s_j),
  epsilon>0.                                               (14)

It agrees with F0 at every observed value and at zero. For sufficiently small
epsilon, continuity of simple roots keeps m roots strictly above 1. The new
root is positive and tends to infinity as epsilon tends to zero: its leading
balance is x~product_i p_i/epsilon. Equivalently, retain the signs at rational
interval endpoints around the old roots and compare with the opposite leading
sign at positive infinity. There are m+1 distinct positive roots above 1 and
degree m+1; F_epsilon(0)=1 normalizes it to a product of m+1 factors (1-p'_i x)
with all p'_i strictly between zero and one. All complete click laws agree,
but emitter counts differ. Roots can be chosen above 1/eta to enforce p'_i<eta.

This theorem covers fixed finite menus and deterministic finite-depth adaptive
menus (their finitely many reachable controls can be included). It does not
give exact equality for arbitrary continuously randomized control policies.
The separate dim-emitter coupling bound covers the lack of a uniform finite
guarantee in that setting. A finite menu cannot simply be substituted for a
continuous first-arrival curve, and time-resolved deadtime recovery is outside
the threshold-bit model.

The exact two-detector witness
-----------------------------

For a balanced pair of saturated detectors, two emitter probabilities (.5,.5)
and three probabilities ((311+sqrt9169)/684,(311-sqrt9169)/684,.1) both yield

  P(none,only A,only B,both)=(1/4,5/16,5/16,1/8).           (15)

The extra emitter is not exactly dark or infinitesimal in this example. The
models have different full photon-number laws; the three-photon event has
probability 16/855 in the second model and zero in the first. New transmission
settings or a third detector can separate them. Thus neither universal
invisibility nor equivalence to the acquired fifteen-channel timing record is
being asserted.

A second exact witness fits total detection efficiency eta=.12. Start with
two probabilities (3/50,3/50). The alternative has probabilities (p1,p2,1/100),
where p1+p2=54184/492525 and p1*p2=50/19701. Numerically these are
(.07709153758595941,.03292115212522278,.01), all below .12. Both full balanced
two-detector click laws equal (2209/2500,573/10000,573/10000,9/5000). The
three-photon event in ideal PNR has probability 1/39402 in the second model.
It requires 118,037 trials for 95% chance of at least one such event.

verify_photon_boundaries.py also gives a rational sign certificate for fifteen
versus sixteen independent positive emitters in a balanced fifteen-threshold-
detector instrument. This is a mathematical interface example, not a fitted
model of the archive. In particular its new root can imply a very dim emitter.

5. Positive boundary: ideal full PNR is identifiable under its model
===================================================================

For independent Bernoulli emitters of positive probabilities p_i and independent
Poisson background of mean beta, the probability generating function is

  G(z)=exp[beta(z-1)] product_i[1+p_i(z-1)].                 (16)

If two finite parameter sets yield the same complete law, put x=1-z and take
their logarithmic derivatives. The difference of the two rational logarithmic
derivatives of their polynomial factors is a constant equal to the difference
of their Poisson means. At infinity both rational logarithmic derivatives
tend to zero, so the constant is zero. The Poisson means agree and normalized
polynomials agree. Their roots and multiplicities identify the positive
brightness multiset and hence the count. This proof allows unequal brightness.

Therefore a blanket claim that arbitrary unequal brightness prevents all
photon-based count identification would be false. Finite-data precision,
independent emission, background class and actual detector response still
matter. An exactly dark emitter is invisible; an emitter with total detection
probability epsilon per trial changes an n-trial law in total variation by at
most n epsilon, even if a rich detector records every photon. Consequently
uniform reliable finite count certification needs a brightness or response
lower bound, or a differently scoped target such as a brightness-weighted count.

6. Same-raw-access comparison and minimum validation interface
=============================================================

Let X denote the entire acquired time-tag record and Y=K(X,U) any proposed
statistic, image, split stream, first-arrival record or contrast, with independent
randomization U. For any two physical models, TV and KL cannot increase under
K. Any decision based on Y can be implemented from X. This is a statement
about information and achievable risk; it does not deny possible reductions
in computational cost or improved numerical conditioning.

If a physical intervention acquires additional records Z, both the candidate
and the strongest comparator must receive X,Z and the same calibration. The
full joint likelihood can use the contrast (2). Calling the contrast itself
a new source of information would double-count the physical acquisition.

To test the conditional operation scientifically, the missing interface is:

  (i) Actual randomized interleaved transmissions 0,r,s,1 on the same fixed
      specimen, with calibrated relative transmission and detector-live state.
  (ii) Independent controls that constrain common lifetime response, Poisson
       optical and dark backgrounds, carryover, afterpulsing and blinking.
       Merely fitting the four curves does not validate those premises.
  (iii) Blinded independently established positive-emitter count and a declared
        minimum brightness. For localization, independently calibrated PSF and
        positional truth are additionally required.
  (iv) A prespecified count error/coverage target and abstention rule, compared
       against full timestamp/coincidence likelihood using the same settings.
  (v) Account for all illumination dose, discarded trials, calibration exposures,
      setting changes, settling, reset, detector hardware, computation and drift.

This is a concrete validation specification, not authorization to procure or
operate apparatus. None of those new joint measurements was performed here.

Uses fibre-bundle time tags, correlation-based selection and localization;
the supplied software and example archive are the empirical interface audited
by s08. Its supplementary threshold is a brightness-dominance criterion with
declared assumptions, not a universal molecular census.

Moroder et al., detector decoy method (2008):
https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0027
Variable attenuation with a threshold detector to estimate photon-number
statistics is established prior art. The four-setting proposal retains that
old physical operation; only its stated unknown-common-time-profile nuisance
argument is developed here. A failed search for the identical formula would
not establish novelty.

Li et al., emitter counting with photon-number-resolving detectors (2022):
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9363020/
Full photon-number likelihood and its information limits are an essential
incumbent. The displayed simplified treatment uses equal-brightness emitters;
our positive identifiability boundary is an elementary ideal-model extension,
not evidence of experimental advantage.

Muller, cumulant analysis in fluorescence fluctuation spectroscopy (2004):
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1304299/
Factorial cumulants already address molecular brightness/number mixtures and
their uncertainties. Cumulants or Poisson-background cancellation alone are
not a new operation.

Fiurasek, Phys. Rev. Research 7, 033147 (2025), revised preprint March2026:
https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17480v2
Finite multiplexed click observations do not uniquely specify arbitrary photon
number distributions; the paper obtains bounds by linear programming. Our
independent-Bernoulli polynomial witnesses specialize this general limitation
to an emitter model. The limitation itself is known.

Banner et al., Optica Quantum (2024):
https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13603v3
Demonstrates calibrated detector-model maximum-likelihood number-state
reconstruction with a single SPAD, including time-dependent detector effects.
Its stated temporal applicability conditions must be checked, rather than
assuming that every timestamp sequence has ideal photon-number resolution.
Timing-based reconstruction itself is established prior art.
