Materials-output investigation: actual measurement acquired; no new enabling operation
2026-10-15

Decision: HOLD the proposed transferable structural operation. STOP treating phase
partitioning, NMF phase fractions or a phase-informed EI search as a new operation.
The useful retained result is an actual-data auxiliary-measurement benefit and a
precise functional/calibration gate. No new material has been synthesized, no
scientific control interface has been acquired, and no practical advantage is
established. The wave's breakthrough objective is NOT_ACHIEVED by this route.

Actual evidence acquired

NIST REMI Fe-Co-Ni benchmark, source commit
596b04435fb577175264a70c4434f5539b809510: 921 unique compositions, 921 x 89 XRD
intensities across 42.6--47 degree two-theta, source coercivity [mT] and Kerr [mrad].
The MATLAB file is 289480 bytes. It contains no phase labels, measurement repeats,
raw magnetic loops, instrument standard, uncertainty or separately varied process
history. The source paper's cleaning removes substrate and thin-film edge points.
The public field description does not state composition column order; calculations
retain C1,C2,C3 rather than silently assigning element names.

Also acquired a compact real raw-functional dataset: Kac (2026),
doi:10.48733/IFJPAN/VV0KPY, version 1.0, FeCoNiCu nanowires. All seven public text
exports and four original source workbooks are preserved. They include actual
normalized SQUID loops in two directions, magnetic parameter tables, deposition
current/charge, elemental composition and Cu K-alpha XRD. The original magnetic
workbook has six sheets; the Dataverse tabular export exposes only its first sheet.

Strongest incumbents and novelty

Wang et al., 2022, doi:10.1093/oxfmat/itac006, already compares GP Expected
Improvement with CAMEO, which first maps phase boundaries and segments property
optimization. EI wins overall; the authors specifically suggest EI within phase
regions as a repair of CAMEO's UCB stage. Their result is a within-library benchmark.

Iwasaki et al., 2020, doi:10.1080/14686996.2019.1707111, already uses the same
Fe-Co-Ni library's experimental XRD, NMF structural rates and phase-specific
KKR-CPA magnetic moments to map Kerr response. Thus the proposed structural
transport mechanism has close direct prior art. Its agreement is not an
independent calibration of magnetization or coercivity.

Our equally informed incumbent is an ordinary GP/EI supplied with the same complete
diffraction information as the partition mechanism. We test composition Matérn
kernels, continuous composition-plus-spectrum kernels, and partition kernels;
hyperparameter/representation-family selection uses only observed target labels.
The union arm may choose any of these kernels. It does not dominate each family
at finite data because selection itself has an estimation cost. The strongest
tested spectrum incumbent is the continuous-spectrum arm, not the union arm.
This is not a faithful reproduction of CAMEO or a claim against all possible GPs.

Substantive actual-data result

The frozen diagnostic uses measured target values, never synthetic labels.
All 921 spectra are supplied upfront to spectrum arms. Their complete acquisition
is therefore charged as 921 scans, not presented as free information.
Prediction uses 128 magnetic labels per split (96 fitting, 32 validation) and
six held-out composition regions, with separate six random holdouts.

Mean of region-held-out coercivity RMSE:
  composition GP                     1.4913 mT
  composition + continuous XRD GP    1.0911 mT
  computational partition GP         1.5402 mT
  union GP selector                  1.0911 mT
Random-held-out RMSE is 0.9499 vs 0.8006 mT for composition vs continuous XRD.
These are one-library prediction results, not new fabrication/process transfer.
Nominal latent 95% GP intervals cover only about 81.6% for the region-held-out
continuous coercivity arm, so they are not calibrated confidence certificates.

Paired EI search uses 12 fixed seeds, identical eight random initial observations,
80 unique observations per arm. At observation 32 the mean stored-Hc regret is:
  composition EI                     1.1193 mT
  continuous-spectrum EI             0.4475 mT
  partition EI                       1.3100 mT
  union selector EI                  1.0193 mT
  uniform random                     1.2206 mT

Both composition and continuous-spectrum arms reached 95% of the stored Hc maximum
in all 12 runs. Mean observation count is 50.75 vs 31.75, a mean saving of 19
magnetic observations (10 wins, one tie, one loss). A descriptive paired-seed
bootstrap interval for that saving is [9.33,28.83]. It is conditional on this one
library and excludes instrument, process and across-system uncertainty. The
partition arm had one failure within 80 and no clear paired saving. All raw
trajectories and row indices are saved in results_v2/ACQUISITION_RUNS.json.

The secondary actual Kerr target does not repay the structural channel: mean
95%-source-maximum counts are 10.583 composition vs 11.833 continuous spectra.
Kerr and coercivity can arise from the same magnetic/optical loop; future joint
campaigns must charge that shared acquisition once, and retain both outputs for
every comparator. These separate-target runs are not a joint multi-property
controller or a proof of amortized benefit across discoveries.

Complete cost condition

Let c_X be the full mean XRD scan cost, including motion and routine processing,
c_M the full magnetic loop cost, and Delta_other all differential calibration,
analysis, model computation, validation and reset costs. The observed mean Hc
saving gives the necessary comparative condition

    921*c_X + Delta_other < 19*c_M.

Even with Delta_other=0, c_X/c_M must be below 0.02063. No acquisition-time, energy,
instrument availability or amortization evidence establishes that condition here.
Common library synthesis/composition costs cancel in this particular comparison,
but remain in an end-to-end bill. They do not cancel in comparison with another
fabrication strategy or a newly synthesized specimen. A sparse-XRD learner could
avoid all 921 scans, but it is a different untested acquisition policy.

Targets/calibration are load-bearing

The source's top Hc values are 10.934 and 10.567 mT, gap 0.367 mT. Common positive
affine calibration preserves ranking; no missing-scale false alarm is asserted.
Specimen-specific error bounds and repeated preparations are absent. Certifying
99.9% of the true maximum under a uniform additive error bound would require about
0.00547 mT or better. This is a requirement calculation, not a measured error.

Maximum Kerr is not maximum saturation magnetization without material-specific
magneto-optical coefficients. Two algebraic countermodels preserve every actual
Kerr observation, composition and XRD value yet swap the inferred magnetic maximum.
The top two stored Kerr values need only a >4.1047% differential optical gain to
reverse their inferred magnetic ranking. No physical realization of those chosen
gains is claimed. They expose the missing coefficient/independent magnetometry.

A single prepared library cannot identify changed-preparation response. FA=F0 and
FB=F0+p*psi agree on every observed p=0 value, but may have different optima at p=1.
Smoothness alone does not exclude such countermodels. A structural sufficiency law
could exclude them, but that law needs independent validation under intervention.

Raw-loop repair and its decisive limitations

The FeCoNiCu source supplies four deposition potentials. Direct linear zero-crossing
reconstruction from stored normalized SQUID loops yields the following Hc [Oe]:
                     -1.0 V     -1.2 V     -1.4 V     -1.8 V
  out of membrane     346.16     406.84     477.78     445.01
  in membrane         132.33     535.63     526.38     229.37
These are calculations from actual stored curves, not independent field calibration.
They demonstrate why direction and function cannot be silently abstracted into a
phase label. The curve sampling brackets overlap for some rankings; point estimates
do not certify a unique true optimum.

The source parameter table lists 478 Oe at -1.8 V out of plane, while its stored loop
zero-crossing bracket permits only [400.84,451.02] Oe under the continuous-crossing
definition. Processing or run-matching provenance is unresolved. Other tabulated
values lie in the coarse crossing brackets but differ from linear interpolation.
All eight stored crossing pairs are exactly symmetric, so opposite branches cannot
be treated as independent repeat measurements without processing provenance.

The original XRD workbook itself has 20 date-formatted angle cells and six invalid
numeric angle values. The angular table has values equal to one at zero angle under
an Oe header, plus B11=475548. No source cells were repaired or imputed. Absolute
saturation M is unavailable from normalized M/Ms loops alone. The file labels and
the existence of an instrument do not establish calibration or independent truth.

The producer's related paper (Nykiel et al., 2026,
doi:10.1016/j.matchar.2026.116814) already reports fcc structure, texture and
defect/interaction effects of Cu in this series. This supplies relevant physical
context, not a newly discovered control operation. Four conditions are useful for
validating a target interface; exhaustive measurement is a strong incumbent there.

Required next physical interface

Resolve specimen/run/processing mapping for raw loops and tabulated outputs.
Acquire field/rotation standards, repeatability, temperature, field direction and
sweep-rate metadata. If magnetic moment is the target, acquire calibrated absolute
VSM/SQUID moments and specimen-volume/mass normalization, or independently identify
the material-specific optical coefficients. Validate matched repeated compositions
under varied preparation with co-registered diffraction and blinded refabrication.
Give the same measurements, calibration, prior knowledge and accumulated observations
to GP/EI and physics comparators. Measure complete time/energy/material/error/reset
costs before claiming benefit. This interface was not acquired by this investigation.
