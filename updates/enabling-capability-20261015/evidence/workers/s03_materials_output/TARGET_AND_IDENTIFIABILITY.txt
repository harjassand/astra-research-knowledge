Target and measurement logic

1. Source output and scientific target are different objects.
The acquired labels are Coer [mT] and Kerr [mrad]. The finite search can identify
the largest stored Coer value. It cannot certify a new material's coercivity at a
specified temperature, field orientation and sweep rate without repeatability and
measurement details. It cannot establish permanent-magnet utility without absolute
magnetization, remanence, energy product, stability and process/form information.

Kerr rotation is an optical functional target in its own right. Its maximum is
not automatically the maximum saturation magnetization. In theta_i=k_i*m_i,
an unknown material-specific magneto-optical coefficient k_i remains even after
common instrument calibration. Using the measured top two Kerr values, a relative
coefficient change just above their ratio can invert their inferred magnetic
ranking. identification_gates.py constructs two algebraic models with exactly
the same observed rotation values and different magnetic maxima. No fabricated
physical response or realized material is claimed by these countermodels.

2. Source optimum precision has an unacquired calibration premise.
The observed top coercivities are 10.934 and 10.567 mT (gap 0.367 mT). A common
positive affine field calibration preserves ranking, so missing absolute scale
alone does not invalidate search. Specimen-specific errors are different: if each
value has error at most delta, top-two ordering is not certified when 2*delta is
at least the observed gap. This data file contains no bound on delta.

To certify the observed maximum is within a relative epsilon of the true maximum
under a uniform additive error bound, sufficient worst-case calibration requires
(ymax-delta)/(ymax+delta) >= 1-epsilon, i.e.
delta <= epsilon*ymax/(2-epsilon). At epsilon=.001 this is about .00547 mT.
This is a requirement calculation, not an assertion of the instrument's error.

3. Preparation transfer is not identified by one library.
The source preparation is a single process history according to the original
CPD description; the acquired data contain no treatment/batch coordinate. For
any fitted response F0(c,z), models FA=F0 and FB=F0+p*psi agree at the observed
p=0 and can have different optima at p=1. Even smoothness does not resolve this:
smooth disjoint bumps around finitely many distinct compositions allow arbitrary
finite psi values. These are unrestricted-model logical counterexamples; physical
laws may restrict psi, but no independently validated structural sufficiency law
is supplied here. Measuring a new spectrum under p=1 is itself an additional
operation and still needs the same target relation validated under that process.

4. Structure helps prediction without establishing structural sufficiency.
Only 42.6--47 degree two-theta intensities were acquired. The file does not supply
wavelength, a reference-pattern calibration, expert phase labels or uncertainties.
The computational XRD partitions in our test are not certified phases or phase
fractions. Very similar normalized spectrum shapes can accompany substantially
different Coer values; composition and other microstructural coordinates matter.
Neither that near-similarity nor a prediction gain proves exact physical
nonidentifiability: a noise tolerance and measurement model would be needed.

5. Novelty gate.
CAMEO already uses phase structure to segment property optimization. The 2022
benchmark specifically suggests EI inside phase regions. Iwasaki et al. already
use this same library's diffraction, NMF phase rates and phase-specific KKR-CPA
magnetic moments to map Kerr response. Recasting these as a new transport or
acquisition operation would not establish novelty. The finite observed benefit
of continuous XRD in our GP/EI test is useful scoped evidence for an ordinary
auxiliary measurement channel, not a new scientific enabling operation.
