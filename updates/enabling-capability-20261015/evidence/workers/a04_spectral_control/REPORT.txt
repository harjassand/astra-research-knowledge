1. Scientific target and the independently proposed operation

The desired output is a calibrated, gauge-invariant cross-controlled response
showing coherent transfer from one observable bright polarization to another
through a shared intermediate sector. It is not the sign of an individual
transition dipole, a unique dark-state wavefunction, or a complete Hamiltonian.

Ideal construction: prepare bright p_x coherence with XUV. Apply weak, separately
sign-reversible NIR gates along x and y, in both orders. Read the complex emitted
XUV amplitude along y. For basis (p_x,p_y,d), let

 V_x=|d><p_x|+|p_x><d|, V_y=|d><p_y|+|p_y><d|,
 U_j(s)=exp(-i s theta_j V_j), s in {+1,-1}.

The exactly evaluated ordered amplitudes at zero free-evolution interval are

 <p_y|U_y(s_y)U_x(s_x)|p_x> = -s_x s_y sin(theta_x)sin(theta_y),
 <p_y|U_x(s_x)U_y(s_y)|p_x> = 0.

The mixed sign projection is

 Q_xy = (1/4) sum_{s_x,s_y} s_x s_y P_xy(s_x,s_y).

For a general intermediate sector and separated weak gates, its leading term
contains the causal kernel V_y U_D(t_2,t_1) V_x, with the known bright evolution
before and after the gates and calibrated input/output dipoles. The full optical
path is g -> p_x -> d -> p_y -> g; the product of its dipoles is invariant under
rephasing the internal basis states. Isolated dipole phases are not the target.

This is a phase-cycled Raman/FWM construction. Independent derivation does not
make the construction new. The close published operations listed in section 6
substantially consume the mechanism and readout ingredients.

2. Exact null scope, correction, and overlap bound

Pulse-order reversal alone is insufficient. Within an unresolved bright sector,
anisotropic effective Stark generators A_x and A_y may not commute. The models
exp(-i a A_y)exp(-i b A_x) and exp(-i b A_x)exp(-i a A_y) can therefore produce
different bright outputs without the proposed intermediate. Orthogonal LAB
polarizations do not establish commuting material generators in a disordered
or oriented microscopic sample.

A stronger conditional statement survives arbitrary bright-sector mixing.
Suppose the control-dependent generator is invariant under independent sign
flips of two temporally disjoint pulses. It can contain arbitrary state-specific
shifts, rates, anisotropy, mixing, free evolution and nuisance parameters, as
long as those parameters are shared across the four sign settings and the
generator depends on each pulse only through quantities even under its sign.
Then the four evolutions are identical and Q_xy=0 exactly, regardless of whether
the two even-control propagators commute. This is a symmetry statement, not an
assumption that all bright states experience a common scalar phase.

Finite pulse overlap is decisive. For an instantaneous quadratic Stark model
with total field s_x E_x(t)x+s_y E_y(t)y, write

 H_s(t)=H_even(t)+s_x s_y C(t),
 C(t)=E_x(t)E_y(t)(A_xy+A_yx).

For unitary propagators, Duhamel's identity gives

 ||(U_+-U_-)/2|| <= integral ||C(t)|| dt / hbar.

Thus |Q_xy| is bounded by the right-hand side times the norms of the prepared
state and calibrated readout vector. The analogous contraction-semigroup bound
uses the norm of the full generator difference, including dissipative terms.
Calibration imperfections add the corresponding integral of generator errors.

The bound remains valid after refitting ALL nuisance parameters in a class with
an independently established common upper bound on this integral. If the tensor
norm is unconstrained, a small pulse-overlap integral alone gives no finite
useful exclusion threshold. A fitted pulse width or argon timing trace does not
by itself bound the material-specific Stark tensor or local odd-field terms.
Permanent-dipole effects, inversion breaking, odd-control terms, environmental
memory and an arbitrary control-dependent causal susceptibility lie outside
this null and need explicit treatment. A nonzero Q would exclude the stated
even-control class, not uniquely establish a named dark exciton.
The bright/dark partition also requires care: a nominally dark, s-like state
may gain weak XUV oscillator strength through symmetry breaking. Allowed linear
NIR coupling within the resulting unresolved 'bright' set can generate the same
parity-sensitive pathway. That is a competing physical assignment to refit,
not something the sign projection automatically eliminates.

Complex field detection is necessary to evaluate signed Q directly. Squaring
the field can erase the sign: |s_x s_y A|^2=|A|^2. Noncollinear phase matching
can instead select the corresponding emitted field mode and measure |Q|^2.
That suffices for a calibrated zero-versus-nonzero witness in the selected mode,
but does not recover Q's phase or distinguish all interfering pathways. Local
instantaneous FWM during pulse overlap is an equally informed incumbent null.

Exact informational obstruction in the isotropic third-order response model:

4. Bounded test on existing data

inspect_naf.py reads processed arrays directly from the source zip. It profiles
an exponential mean lifetime tau over {0,.025,.05,.1,.2,.3,.5,.75,1,1.5,2,3,5,8} fs.
At each tau it refits Gaussian IRF width, time zero, amplitude and constant
baseline. tau=0 is the exact Gaussian boundary, avoiding an infinite decay-rate
parameter. Spectral windows and time offsets match Make_Figures.py.

                    best sampled tau     SSE improvement over tau=0
 Bright X1          0.75 fs               0.1020 percent
 Bright X2          0 fs                  0
 Dark X1            0 fs                  0
 Dark X2            0 fs                  0

The fitted tau=0 FWHMs are 7.18, 7.43, 9.17 and 7.92 fs respectively. Exact
values and the complete profiles are in LIFETIME_PROFILE.json. The four-channel
inspection and profile code took 0.5593 seconds in the recorded run.

This is a descriptive sensitivity check on deposited processed data. It is not
a confidence interval, noise-calibrated model rejection, raw-data holdout test,
statement that the physical lifetime is zero, or a contest between complete
refitted microscopic Hamiltonians. Source preprocessing chooses iteration
ranges, per-iteration time shifts, baseline windows and final display offsets;
those calibration choices are not promoted to independently established truth.
The result supplies no new support for a resolvable finite dark dwell time.

5. Decisive experiment and matched incumbent

A valid future experiment must jointly obtain: both NIR delays; the two pulse
orders; independent optical sign/phase settings or equivalent separately
calibrated phase-matched field modes; preparation/output polarization channels;
pulse fields including overlap tails and jitter; material tensor/odd-term
constraints; and interleaved backgrounds/calibration repeats. Only an observed
response exceeding the supremum of the null overlap/calibration/error bound,
on a preselected withheld control, rejects the local-even class. Additional
detuning and decay measurements are needed to interpret its spectral content.

The strongest same-input method is standard polarization-selective FWM plus
vectorial heterodyne spectral interferometry and globally refitted causal
response-tensor or microscopic models. It receives the identical fields,
phase calibration, readout quadratures, raw repeats and prior material bounds.
There is no demonstrated information or cost advantage over that method.

6. Primary-source comparison

Gannan et al. (2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18083v3:
LiF transient absorption with one NIR pulse; its stabilized time scan and
polarization scan are distinct experiments. Four bright contributions and two
phenomenological dark contributions make scalar-dipole inference inadequate.

Xiong et al. (2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06112v1,
published https://doi.org/10.1103/dn88-cg3j:
Two independently delayed NIR pulses and polarization-selective core-exciton
FWM are already implemented. The paper leaves multiple spectral-pathway
interpretations open and notes that decay is faster than the instrumental
response. Its public data enabled the acquisition and diagnostic above.

Kasprzak and Langbein (2008), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.78.041103,
author repository https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/7315/:
Dual-polarization heterodyne spectral interferometry already measures both
polarization components of excitonic FWM in amplitude and phase. This is a
direct incumbent for the proposed vector complex-field readout, although a
specific XUV implementation has additional experimental costs.
