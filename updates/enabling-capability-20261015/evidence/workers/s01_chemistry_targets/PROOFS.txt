Conditional decision statements. These statements are elementary existing robust-optimization/estimable-function reasoning. They are not claimed as novel and do not establish the measurement assumptions.

1. All-penalty treatment comparison.
Let the true treatment-control differences be dT and dS, and utility difference g(lambda)=dT-lambda*dS for lambda>=0. Then g(lambda)>=0 for every lambda>=0 if and only if dT>=0 and dS<=0. Necessity follows by taking lambda=0 and then lambda arbitrarily large. Sufficiency follows term by term. Strict preference for every finite lambda requires additionally handling a possible zero at lambda=0. The audit reports Pareto dominance separately from strict all-lambda preference: dT=0,dS<0 is Pareto dominance but ties at lambda=0. Thus the dominance counts do not assert a strict preference for an objective that gives side product no weight.

2. Sharp bounded-error certificate.
Suppose every action's T and S error has magnitude at most epsilon. With utility coefficient vector w=(1,-lambda), each utility error has magnitude at most epsilon*(1+lambda) by the triangle inequality. The difference between two actions has error at most 2*epsilon*(1+lambda). A mean of arbitrarily dependent control observations still has each coordinate error at most epsilon; no independence or square-root-of-n gain is assumed. Thus a positive observed gap g certifies strict preference when g>2*epsilon*(1+lambda). Without extra joint constraints, assigning action errors (-epsilon,+epsilon) to the reported winner and (+epsilon,-epsilon) to the competitor attains the lower endpoint, so the bound is sharp. Under imposed physical simplices the bound stays sufficient but its necessity can change; the actual near-tie target example remains interior enough for its stated target-order radius.

3. Positive affine calibration invariance.
Let T'=a*T+b, S'=c*S+d with common a,c>0 across actions. All target and side pairwise difference signs are preserved, so Pareto dominance is invariant. For lambda'=a*lambda/c, T'-lambda'*S'=a*(T-lambda*S)+(b-lambda'*d). The final term is action-independent, proving envelope-equivalence after penalty reparameterization. Separate strictly increasing transforms likewise preserve Pareto order, but need not preserve a linear utility form. Sample-specific gains, censored intervals or target-side mixing do not satisfy this result's premises.

4. Source-label envelope.
Sort distinct source-label vectors by increasing S and retain successive strict records in T. All discarded vectors have no larger T and no smaller S than a retained vector, hence cannot improve U for lambda>0. The exposed points of the upper concave hull of retained vectors give all nonnegative-penalty optimizers. At each finite switching penalty the adjacent utilities are equal. For a proposed point and competitor, the utility difference is affine in lambda; nonnegativity at two finite interval endpoints proves nonnegativity throughout. On an unbounded interval, additionally requiring smallest S makes the difference nondecreasing. audit_targets.py checks these endpoint inequalities as exact rational arithmetic on all retained numerical Pareto vectors. Lambda=0 can also tie a dominated vector with the same T; such utility-equivalent zero-penalty ties are not claimed to disappear from the source action set.

5. Why same-data prediction alone cannot acquire the output.
Let E be the acquired record set and f(E) an arbitrary deterministic proposed action-selection computation. An equally informed incumbent receiving E can execute f and return exactly the same action; a randomized incumbent can also share the same randomization law. Therefore this comparison cannot show added statistical information. Finite compute costs, genuine out-of-sample extrapolation, additional measurement interfaces or independent priors could change a different comparison, but none is demonstrated by relabeling or repredicting this fully acquired finite action table. Robust optimization of E is useful bookkeeping and conditional decision logic, not independent chemical truth.

6. Contrasts and the missing interface.
For z=A*c+B*n, q^T*z=(q^T*A)c+(q^T*B)n. If q^T*A=w^T and q^T*B=0, the scalar contrast q^T*z equals w^T*c. Paired differences cancel an additional shared additive offset. If no q exists, then the desired contrast is not estimable under the supplied linear model: w is outside the row image of A after quotienting the nuisance columns, so some signal null direction changes w^T*c. If q exists and the noise covariance is known, ordinary generalized least squares gives the minimum-variance linear contrast. The existence, conditioning, noise model and calibration must be established with actual acquired spectra/standards; those were not supplied here. A matched incumbent has access to the same q and covariance.

7. No globally certified selection without an error or acquisition contract.
If each unvalidated action's true product vector can range freely over the physical simplex independently of its producer label, then any unvalidated action can be assigned (100,0) while retaining the same acquired label table. A competing latent state can assign that action (0,100), again with the same labels. Whenever these states imply different optimal decisions, no method using only that table can certify one globally. A simultaneous error contract or broader independent measurements must restrict the admissible states. This is not a claim that arbitrary errors occur; it identifies what the current evidence alone fails to exclude.
