1. SETUP AND SOURCE STATUS

Let F be a finite union-closed family, N=|F|>1, and let X,Y be independent
uniform members. All logarithms are natural. Define

  r(S) = #{(A,B) in F^2: A union B=S},
  c(A,B)=log r(A union B),
  R(A,B)=c(A,B)-E[c(A,Y)]-E[c(X,B)]+E[c(X,Y)],
  V(F)=E[R(X,Y)^2],
  nu_0(S)=r(S)/N^2.

These are ordered-pair counts. Every r(S)>0 and sum_S r(S)=N^2.
Define Q(A,B)=1/[N r(A union B)]. It is a probability law with uniform
union output, but not generally uniform inputs. Let P_* minimize D(P||Q)
over the couplings with uniform input marginals, and let nu_* be its union
law. Put G(F)=H(nu_*)-H(nu_0).

This construction and a finite positive gain are in Jiang,
arXiv:2609.08291v1, Section 10. That preprint explicitly discloses GPT-6
Astra assistance. Its arXiv listing and its author claims are not treated

2. INDEPENDENT DERIVATION OF THE GLOBAL PROJECTION IDENTITY

The feasible transport polytope is compact and contains the strictly
positive product law P_0=1/N^2. Strict convexity gives a unique minimizer.
If a minimizer has a zero entry, moving a sufficiently small distance
toward P_0 changes the zero-entry contribution by a negative multiple of
t log t, dominating the O(t) changes at positive entries. Thus P_*>0.
Lagrange multipliers give

  P_*(A,B)=a(A)a(B)/r(A union B),  a(A)>0.

Symmetry and uniqueness justify taking the same scaling factor on both
coordinates. Equal input marginals imply the exact Pythagorean identity

  D(P_0||Q)=D(P_0||P_*)+D(P_*||Q).

Indeed log(P_*/Q) is a sum of a function of A, a function of B, and a
constant, so its expectations under P_0 and P_* agree. Grouping by union
fibers gives

  D(P_*||Q) = log N-H(nu_*)
    + sum_S nu_*(S) D(P_*(.|union=S)||Unif(fiber S)),
  D(P_0||Q) = log N-H(nu_0).

Subtracting yields

  G(F) = D(P_0||P_*)
    + sum_S nu_*(S) D(P_*(.|union=S)||Unif(fiber S)) >= 0.

This equality also shows exactly where the weaker lower bound discards
information. It does not supply a dimension-free frequency estimate.
The finite nonzero-gain theorem is prior art in the cited preprint, not a
new result claimed here.

3. EXACT TENSORIZATION

Theorem A. For union-closed F_1,...,F_k on disjoint ground sets, write
F=F_1 x ... x F_k under the identification with unions of one set per block.
Then

  log|F| = sum_j log|F_j|,
  V(F) = sum_j V(F_j),
  P_*(F) = tensor_j P_*(F_j),
  G(F) = sum_j G(F_j),
  max_frequency(F)=max_j max_frequency(F_j).

Proof. Each union fiber is a Cartesian product, hence r=product_j r_j,
Q=tensor_j Q_j, and c=sum_j c_j. Under the product input law, row and
column centering gives R=sum_j R_j. The R_j are independent with mean zero,
so their variances add. The tensor product of the positive scaled couplings
has uniform full marginals and has the required scaling form for Q.
By uniqueness it is P_*(F). Union-output laws also tensorize, so their
Shannon entropies and the gains add. Frequencies are unchanged within each
block. QED.

Consequence. For powers F^k of any fixed F,

  V(F^k)/(log|F^k|)^2 = V(F)/[k(log|F|)^2] -> 0,
  G(F^k)/log|F^k| = G(F)/log|F|.

Thus a lower bound proportional to V/(log N)^2 in the abundance conclusion
loses all uniform force under powers, even when the actual mechanism has
extensive gain. This is a defect of that lower bound, not a refutation of
the global coupling itself.

Exact calibrating example. For the one-coordinate Boolean family
B_1={empty,{1}}, r=(1,3). The scaled law is

  P_* = [[a,b],[b,a]],
  a=sqrt(3)/[2(1+sqrt(3))], b=1/[2(1+sqrt(3))].

Its union is zero with probability a. For B_d=B_1^d,

  V(B_d)=d (log 3)^2/16,
  G(B_d)=d [h(a)-h(1/4)],
  G(B_d)/log|B_d|=[h(a)-h(1/4)]/log 2 = 0.0897969932588... .

The variance identity follows by centering log(3) times the OR table:
the residual entries are (-1,+1;+1,-1)log(3)/4.

4. ORDINAL CUBE--CHAIN FAMILY AND EXACT COUNTS

Let M=2^d. Let B_d be all subsets of [d]. Adjoin L>0 new chain members

  T_j=[d] union {d+1,...,d+j},  1<=j<=L,
  F_{d,L}=B_d union {T_1,...,T_L},   N=M+L.

This is exactly union-closed: a union inside B_d stays in B_d; a union with
T_j is the larger relevant chain member. The multiplicities are

  r(S)=3^{|S|},                          S in B_d,
  r(T_j)=2(M+j)-1.                      1<=j<=L

For the first formula, each included coordinate has the three pair states
10,01,11, while absent coordinates force 00. For the second, T_j must be
one of the two inputs and the other may be any of the M+j members at or
below it; count the diagonal once.

The exact maximum frequency is

  p(d,L)=(M/2+L)/(M+L)=1/2+L/[2(M+L)].

It is attained by each cube coordinate. A new chain coordinate belongs to
at most L members, which is smaller than M/2+L.

Set w=M/N and

  a_0=(3/4)log 3-log 2 >0.

The strict positivity is exact because 3^3>2^4. Numerically a_0 is about
0.13081203594.

Let I=1_{X in B_d}, J=1_{Y in B_d}. These are independent Bernoulli(w).
The four conditional means of c are

  g_11=(3d/4)log 3,
  g_10=g_01=(1/L) sum_{j=1}^L log[2(M+j)-1],
  g_00=(1/L^2) sum_{j=1}^L (2j-1)log[2(M+j)-1].

Let Delta=g_11-2g_10+g_00. Conditional expectation commutes with the
row/column centering under the independent input law, hence

  E[R|I,J] = Delta (I-w)(J-w).

Conditional Jensen therefore gives the exact lower bound

  V(F_{d,L}) >= w^2(1-w)^2 Delta^2.                       (1)

Every upper-chain log lies between log(2M+1) and log(2N-1), so

  Delta >= a_0 d-log 2-2log(1+L/M).                      (2)

When the right side is positive, its square may be used in (1).

5. NO DIMENSION-FREE LINEAR VARIANCE REPAIR, EVEN NEAR ONE HALF

Theorem B. Fix any alpha>0 and take L=floor(alpha 2^d) (for sufficiently
large d that L>0). Then, with w_alpha=1/(1+alpha),

  p(d,L) -> (1/2+alpha)/(1+alpha),
  V(F_{d,L})/d^2 -> a_0^2 w_alpha^2(1-w_alpha)^2 >0.

For ANY coupling with uniform marginals and output A union B, its entropy
gain over independent sampling is at most log N=O(d). Therefore its gain
divided by V(F_{d,L}) tends to zero.

Proof of the variance limit. Under LL, c/d=(log 3)|X union Y|/d converges
in L^2 to (3/4)log 3, since |X union Y| is Binomial(d,3/4). If at least
one input is in the chain, c/d converges uniformly to log 2, since its
deviation is bounded by a constant depending only on alpha, divided by d.
Thus c/d differs in L^2 by o(1) from

  log 2+a_0 I J.

Double centering is a bounded orthogonal projection on the finite product
probability space, so R/d differs in L^2 by o(1) from
a_0(I-w)(J-w). Taking squared norms proves the limit. The entropy upper
bound follows only from union closure: the output has at most N values.
QED.

Corollary. For EVERY epsilon>0 and EVERY kappa>0, there is a union-closed F
with max_frequency(F)<=1/2+epsilon for which

  G(F) < kappa V(F).

Choose small fixed alpha so alpha/[2(1+alpha)]<epsilon, then large d.
The same failure holds if G is replaced by the largest entropy gain
achievable by ANY uniform-marginal coupling. It is not an artifact of
numerical scaling or of the particular exponent-one construction.

Limit of this obstruction: these families have maximum frequency ABOVE
one half. They do not refute a gain theorem conditioned on p<=0.39 or
p<=1/2, and they are not counterexamples to Frankl. Establishing such a
low-frequency theorem is still a genuinely new missing step.

6. VARIANCE CAN DIVERGE WHILE ALL AVAILABLE ENTROPY GAIN STAYS BOUNDED

Theorem C. Take L=ceil(2^d d^{3/4}). Then V(F_{d,L}) tends to infinity, but
every uniform-marginal union coupling has gain at most log 2+o(1).

Proof. Here w=(1+d^{3/4}+o(1))^{-1}. By (2),
Delta >= a_0 d-O(log d), so (1) gives

  V(F_{d,L}) >= (a_0^2+o(1)) d^{1/2} -> infinity.

Because nu_0 is supported on F,

  log N-H(nu_0)=E_{nu_0}[log(r(S)/N)].

For chain outputs, r(T_j)<2N, so their total contribution is at most
log 2. The cube-output event has probability w^2; conditional on it,
the mean of log r is (3d/4)log 3. Since log N>=d log 2,

  log N-H(nu_0) <= log 2 + a_0 d w^2 = log 2+o(1).

No supported output can have entropy larger than log N. Its gain over
nu_0 is therefore at most this deficit. QED.

This disproves not only G>=kappa V but any universal lower bound on G
that diverges as a function of V alone. It also disproves the possible
repair G>=kappa min{V,log N}, because V grows while log N is of order d.
In this counterexample p tends to one; no low-frequency extrapolation is
asserted.

7. INVERSE-MULTIPLICITY KERNEL NEED NOT BE POSITIVE SEMIDEFINITE

Theorem D. For every d>=3, the positive-entry matrix

  K(A,B)=1/r(A union B)

of F_{d,1} is indefinite, although its maximum frequency tends to 1/2.

Proof. Restrict to the cube top U=[d] and the added top T_1. Their 2x2
principal submatrix is

  [[1/3^d, 1/(2^{d+1}+1)],
   [1/(2^{d+1}+1),1/(2^{d+1}+1)]].

Its determinant has the sign of 2^{d+1}+1-3^d, which is negative for d=3
and remains negative under increasing d (or by direct induction).
The positive diagonal gives a positive direction and negative determinant
gives a negative direction. Hence the full matrix is indefinite.
Meanwhile p(d,1)=1/2+1/[2(2^d+1)] ->1/2. QED.

Therefore union closure, even together with a maximum-frequency cap
1/2+epsilon, does not justify a Gram-matrix/positive-kernel proof for K.
