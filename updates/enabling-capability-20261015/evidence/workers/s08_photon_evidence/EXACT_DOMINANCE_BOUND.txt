Elementary ideal-model dominance bound; no novelty claim

Let a finite number of independent single-photon Bernoulli emitters have
fixed positive detected probabilities q_i per pulse. Let mu=sum_i q_i>0,
w_i=q_i/mu, and let g=E[K(K-1)]/E[K]^2=1-sum_i w_i^2 for IDEAL full
photon number K. This identity does not by itself describe detector-saturated,
background-contaminated or blinking-normalized sidepeak statistics.

If g<1/2, the brightest fraction m=max_i w_i obeys
m >= (1+sqrt(1-2g))/2.
Proof: c=sum_i w_i^2=1-g <=m*sum_i w_i=m; hence m>1/2.
The remaining weights sum1-m and have sum of squares <=(1-m)^2.
Thus c<=m^2+(1-m)^2, which for m>1/2 implies the displayed bound.
Equality is attained by the two weights (m,1-m). In particular g<3/8
implies m>3/4, irrespective of the unspecified finite number of weights.
It does not imply emitter count1. Arbitrarily many positive dim weights
can be appended while retaining such dominance. A background/detector
response calibration is needed before substituting measured coincidences.

Raw timestamp and photon-number law distinctions
A Bernoulli-threshold countermodel constrains the full law of that declared
binary experiment only. Our real archive has15 detectors, multiple counts
in a detector within a reconstructed pulse, spatial marks and arrival times.
A first-two-moment countermodel need not match any of those retained data.
No equality of raw likelihoods for competing emitter counts is proved here.
In an ideal finite Bernoulli-emitter plus Poisson-background model, full PNR
laws can be identifiable by generating-function factorization. Detector
response, blinking and unknown brightness floors set the finite-operation
gates, not a universal algebraic inability to infer count.
