Premise audit and competing research protocol
2026-10-15. Author role: independent adversary. Scientific status: no qualifying new capability established.

The hypothesis needs three different readings
The proposition under review is: mathematical invention succeeds when a proof/verification loop closes, while technology also requires jointly realizable inputs, interventions and evidence.

As an admission rule it is useful: do not promote an unchecked proof or a technology whose required measurement/control has not been obtained. As a sufficient condition for valuable invention it is false: a checkable theorem may be known, irrelevant, vacuously formalized, or exponentially expensive; a reproducible apparatus may be inferior to the incumbent or answer the wrong question. As a causal explanation of research productivity it has not been tested by observing only completed projects. It becomes nearly tautological if 'closure' is defined retrospectively as all conditions needed for success.

There is no clean mathematics-versus-technology boundary here. Mathematics also has an interface between the human problem and the formal statement. A proof checker accepts exactly the statement supplied, not the claim that the statement captures the desired problem. Technology can work with a bounded, empirically validated decision rule before its microscopic mechanism is proved. Both domains need a target contract, admissible evidence and a comparator; what differs is the kind and cost of the evidence.

Counterexamples to sufficiency
1. Semantic vacuity: formalize 'for every x satisfying A, P(x)' when A has no instances. The proof loop closes but the intended constructive task is untouched. Concrete operational check: exhibit an admissible instance and require a witness of the originally requested output on it.
2. Proxy closure: two optical sources have exactly the same threshold-detector record but different emitter counts. A perfect likelihood fit and unlimited independent record verification cannot identify the target. The accompanying proof specifies the missing measurement and its attainable alternatives.
3. Summary closure: two-vs-three unequal-brightness emitter models have identical intensity and g2 summaries. A proof correct for those summaries cannot certify the actual count. Third-order data can separate the two models, but its sampling/calibration cost is real.
4. Comparator closure: variable attenuation is mathematically valid and uses an obtainable optical component, yet a strong two-detector incumbent can require far fewer excitation trials. Realizability alone gives no advantage.
5. Generalization closure: a target can be verified on a fixed specimen or fixture while drift, bleaching, interactions or deployment distribution change its behavior. The validated scope must be part of the claim, not silently extended after a passed benchmark.
6. Target inaccessibility: arbitrarily dim added emitters defeat uniform finite count guarantees, even for ideal photon-number measurements. The missing item is a lower response bound or a new action that makes the alternatives observably different, not additional inference on the same records.

These are not claims that all discovery requires exhaustive proof
Useful conjectures, representations, empirical laws and procedures can precede complete validation. Their status must remain provisional. The present work distinguishes generation, certified validity in a model, real-world target adequacy, novelty, and comparative usefulness. Closing one of those loops does not close the others.

Operational replacement: target separation per complete cost
Before proposing a mechanism, specify target T(theta), allowed actions U, observation kernels P_theta(.|u), nuisance set, loss/tolerance, and resource budget. Form the observational equivalence class of all models consistent with the data and allowed interventions. If target values differ within that class, return the unresolved target interval/set and identify which attainable action can separate the surviving alternatives. If no such action exists under the budget, report HOLD or STOP rather than inventing a sharper estimator.

The construction is not sufficient for invention either. It is a disciplined search rule. A candidate is interesting only when its intervention produces a better target decision than the strongest available comparator after acquisition, calibration, precision, preparation, detector losses, computation, reset and scaling costs. Count information alone is insufficient if the user ultimately needs localization or molecular stoichiometry; define that downstream loss separately.

A falsifiable prospective test of the hypothesis
Preregister a cohort of mathematical and physical research tasks before solutions are known. Include all admitted candidates and all failures; do not select completed projects. Fix the original desired target, independent final adjudication, available sources, and resource ceilings. Randomly allocate development access to: A) ordinary constructive search; B) an explicit proof/checking loop; C) a target-separation and observation/control redesign step; D) both B and C. All arms receive the same final independent validation, so the intervention concerns the development workflow and does not weaken acceptance criteria.

Primary endpoint: independently accepted, novel target-output improvements per fixed total budget, measured on held-out tasks/specimens. Secondary endpoints: correct early STOP/HOLD decisions, time/resources to first decisive counterexample, false capability promotions, and calibration of preregistered success probabilities. Report the denominator of all tasks and the count of UNKNOWN outcomes. Worker agreement and repeated runs from the same source are correlated evidence, not independent replications. If inference telemetry is unavailable, report that cost UNKNOWN rather than inventing token estimates.

Disconfirming predictions
If workflow B increases proof closure but not independently accepted target improvements, closure alone does not explain useful invention. If workflow C merely rejects more projects without increasing value per budget or correctly predicting infeasibility, target-separation screening is only conservative triage. If D's apparent advantage disappears when comparator/source access and total budgets are matched, the explanation is extra resources or information rather than loop design. If tasks with acquired interfaces still fail systematically through non-identifiability, model mismatch or incumbent dominance, 'joint realizability' needs those explicit additional variables.
