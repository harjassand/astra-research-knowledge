Premise challenge: early checkpoint, 2026-10-15

Status: NO NEW CAPABILITY ESTABLISHED. A concrete validation-interface candidate exists; its elementary mathematical mechanism is prior art.

Central premise under test
"Mathematical invention succeeds when the proof/verification loop closes; technology additionally needs jointly realizable inputs, interventions and evidence."
This is a useful admission rule but not yet an explanatory or predictive hypothesis. It conflates the intended target, a checked proxy, novelty, and comparative utility. If success means an accepted proof or an adequately verified technology, closure is close to definitional. If success means useful invention, closure is not sufficient. Selection on completed projects also removes the failed-loop denominator.

Initial independent survey
1. Photon-count microscopy: actual raw integer data are public. Poisson thinning can give independent training/audit streams only under the declared statistical model. Incumbents are coupled bootstrap risk estimation, general data thinning, and GAP photon denoising. Therefore stop novelty claims for photon splitting itself.
2. Scientific image targets: a lower pixel MSE does not identify emitter number, separation or molecular count. The target must be an explicitly declared observable and resolution, with alternative physical models included.
3. Quantum correlation microscopy: real multichannel time-tag data are publicly acquired (34,749,720 byte Figshare archive downloaded and checksum matched). It directly supports an audit of count inference beyond pixel MSE. Incumbents already use g2, higher correlations, photon number resolution and variable attenuation.

Exact countermodels and constructive repair
A. One binary threshold detector, one independent trial per excitation pulse. One emitter with emission probability 3/4 and two independent emitters with probabilities (1/2,1/2) both give iid Bernoulli(3/4) click records. Their entire observed laws agree for every sample size. Emitter count differs. Calibrated transmission t=1/2 gives click probabilities 3/8 versus 7/16, or a beamsplitter and two independent detectors can acquire a coincidence that a true ideal single emitter cannot produce.
B. Two emitters (1/2,1/2) versus three (2/3,1/6,1/6) have equal mean count 1 and equal factorial second moment 1/2, so equal g2(0)=1/2. They have different third factorial moments, 0 versus 1/9. This is equality of first-two-moment summaries, NOT equality of their full raw-count laws. A third-order coincidence observable separates them.
C. Arbitrarily dim extra emitters prevent uniformly reliable finite-sample certification of total active-emitter count without a lower brightness bound. Exactly dark extra labels make all optical observation laws identical. An assay of active optical emitters is not a molecular stoichiometry assay.

Substantive lead
Develop a count-certification interface that returns a set of compatible emitter counts under explicitly bounded detector/background/brightness nuisances, chooses the next attainable observation that separates surviving models, and stops when no such observation is available. The immediate task is source-code/data decoding, followed by a bounded replay and exact countermodel checks. This is an operational benchmark proposal, not a novelty claim. Comparator must be source g2 criterion plus full photon-count likelihood and variable attenuation, not merely an intensity threshold.
