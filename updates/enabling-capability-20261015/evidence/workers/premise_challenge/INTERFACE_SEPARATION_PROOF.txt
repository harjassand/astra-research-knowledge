An explicit target-separating optical interface, and a bound on adaptive attenuation
2026-10-15. Status: internally derived; independently review before promotion. Not novelty-cleared.

Target and physical model
The target is the number of optically active independent single-photon emitters in one unresolved spatial collection mode during a fixed stationary acquisition. It is not total molecule count. Each emitter emits at most one photon per excitation pulse, independently of the others and across pulses. A photon is detected with common efficiency eta, independently. Dark counts, fluorescence carryover between pulses, deadtime, crosstalk, multi-photon emission by a single source, excitation-induced interactions and sample drift are excluded in this ideal model. These are explicit experimental gates.

Two candidate specimens
H0: one emitter, emission probability 1-eta/4 per pulse.
H1: two emitters, emission probability 1/2 each per pulse.
Here 0<eta<=1 is a fixed detector efficiency. Both parameter choices are physically admissible in the model. Let a=eta(1-eta/4) and b=eta^2/4.

Existing interface
The detector reports one bit C per pulse: at least one detected photon or no detected photon. At transmission t=1, H0 gives P(C=1)=a. H1 gives 1-(1-eta/2)^2=a. Thus the complete recorded process is iid Bernoulli(a) in both specimens. No algorithm, denoiser, proof loop, source prior learned from these records, or arbitrarily large sample can distinguish the specimens using this interface. The two physical target values differ.

Available intervention: downstream attenuation
Insert a calibrated neutral attenuation with photon transmission t in [0,1] after emission and before the detector. Excitation and source physics must be unchanged. Then
 p0(t)=a*t,
 p1(t)=eta*t-(eta^2/4)*t^2,
 p1(t)-p0(t)=b*t*(1-t).
Any t strictly between 0 and 1 separates the ideal models. For eta=1 and t=1/2 these click probabilities are 3/8 and 7/16. This intervention is an instance of established variable-efficiency photon-statistics reconstruction / detector decoy; it is not new here.

Information limit for ALL adaptive attenuation-only policies
Allow each transmission t_k to depend arbitrarily on all previous clicks and private randomness, but require the same policy under H0 and H1 and a maximum of n pulses. The sole output on each pulse remains one binary click. This includes arbitrarily sophisticated adaptive learning/search on exactly the same interface.

For any Bernoulli probabilities p,q, KL(Ber(p)||Ber(q)) <= (p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]. This follows directly by applying log(u)<=u-1 to both terms of p log(p/q)+(1-p)log[(1-p)/(1-q)] and simplifying. On t in (0,1], each of p0(t),p1(t) lies between a*t and a. Consequently the KL divergence in either direction on one pulse is bounded by
 U = [b^2/(a(1-a))] max_{0<=t<=1} t(1-t)^2
   = 4 b^2/[27 a(1-a)],
because the maximum is 4/27 at t=1/3. At t=0 the laws agree and divergence is zero. The chain rule for KL, conditioned on the action/history, gives KL(P0^n||P1^n)<=n*U even for adaptive transmissions.

If a final decision has both error probabilities at most alpha<1/2, data processing under that decision implies
 KL(P0^n||P1^n) >= kl(1-alpha,alpha)
                        = (1-2alpha) log[(1-alpha)/alpha].
Therefore every such policy must have
 n >= (1-2alpha) log[(1-alpha)/alpha] / U.
This is a necessary shot budget; it is not an exact optimal sample complexity. It assumes a bounded fixed maximum number of trials. An expected stopping-time version would require a separate optional-stopping argument and is not asserted here.

Competing intervention: two detector coincidence
Send the same collected photons through a lossless balanced beamsplitter to two independent ideal threshold detectors, each with the same photon efficiency eta as above. Total photon detection efficiency remains eta; the extra detector is a charged hardware resource. Under H0 a pulse can never give two clicks. Under H1 both emitters emit with probability 1/4; both photons are detected with probability eta^2; they reach different detectors with probability 1/2. Thus the coincidence probability is eta^2/8.
The rule "declare H1 after one same-pulse coincidence, otherwise H0 after n pulses" has false-positive probability zero and false-negative probability (1-eta^2/8)^n. It attains both errors <=alpha for
 n >= log(alpha)/log(1-eta^2/8).

Concrete matched optical example
At eta=0.12 (the overall efficiency reported in the 2017 source experiment, used only as an illustrative parameter), H0 source probability is 0.97. Both unattenuated click probabilities are 0.1164. At t=1/2, probabilities become 0.0582 and 0.0591.
The supplied exact script computes the rigorous adaptive attenuation lower bound and the ideal coincidence upper bound at alpha=0.05. No timing or exposure duration is inferred from the experiment's laser repetition rate: reset, detector recovery, fluorescence lifetime, source stationarity, and processing must first be measured.

Interpretation
The construction makes the validation-interface claim operational. It is possible to prove that a particular recorded interface cannot identify the target, then specify an attainable additional observation and compare information budgets to a strong incumbent. It also defeats the tempting argument that a cheap software/attenuator operation automatically wins: an extra detector can save many shots. The proof gives no new imaging theorem beyond a scoped combination of standard ingredients, and the literature screen finds established competing operations.

Physical failure modes
One spurious coincidence invalidates the ideal zero-false-positive test. Required controls include blocked source, known single-emitter control, optical crosstalk/detector afterpulse characterization, interdetector delay calibration, same-pulse assignment, detector deadtime/live-time measurement, source blinking/drift, multi-exciton emission and background fluorescence. Those controls should provide nuisance bounds used inside the decision rule; subtraction of a fitted mean alone does not yield a finite-sample certificate.

Scope beyond the pair
For arbitrary unequal brightnesses p_i, g2(0)=1-sum p_i^2/(sum p_i)^2 measures an effective count. Equality to 1-1/N requires identical p_i. Cauchy-Schwarz gives N >= 1/(1-g2) when the ideal assumptions hold, but does not give an upper bound. The published 2017 method explicitly uses a brightness-dispersion condition and a conservative 0.375 threshold; this report does not claim the authors ignored that issue.
Adding emitters whose total detection probability per pulse is at most epsilon changes an n-pulse full photon record in total variation by at most n*epsilon: couple all original emissions and observe that the records can differ only if an added emitter is detected at least once. This extends to adaptive controls only when that bound holds under EVERY allowed control, conditional on every history. A new excitation/label/chemical intervention that violates this bound can invalidate the obstruction and is exactly the kind of new interface worth acquiring. Therefore no method has uniformly bounded finite count-identification cost over a model class with no lower detectable brightness and such uniformly dim alternatives. Completely dark emitters under every allowed action are exactly unobservable optically.
