1. Exact elimination of unobserved levels

Let b(t) be the vector of bright amplitudes and d(t) the vector of unobserved amplitudes. In units hbar=1, assume
  i b'(t) = H_B(t)b(t) + C(t)d(t) + f(t),
  i d'(t) = H_D(t)d(t) + C(t)^*b(t).
The blocks may depend on the applied control. Let U_D(t,s) solve i dU_D/dt=H_D(t)U_D, U_D(s,s)=I. Variation of constants gives exactly
  d(t)=U_D(t,0)d(0)-i integral_0^t U_D(t,s) C(s)^* b(s) ds.
Substitution yields
  i b'(t)=H_B(t)b(t)+f(t)+C(t)U_D(t,0)d(0)
           -i integral_0^t C(t)U_D(t,s)C(s)^* b(s) ds.
The converse reconstructs d(t) by the first displayed integral. Thus an explicit finite hidden-level model and its control-dependent causal-memory representation have identical b trajectories for every admissible control. If the output also contains d, substitute the same integral in the output map. No observable is changed.

Scope: this is an exact representation equivalence, not a demonstration that a different physical sample with no excitonic modes reproduces the experiment. It does not show that a memoryless Stark-only null fits. It does show that choosing an explicit hidden-state representation is not independently observable evidence. A scientifically meaningful target can instead be required resonant kernel poles, minimum realization order in a specified model family, or controlled transfer. These remain conditional on the admissible kernel family and calibration.

For H_B=0, H_D=Delta, C=g real and b(0)=1,d(0)=0:
  b(t)=exp(-i Delta t/2)[cos(Rt/2)+i(Delta/R)sin(Rt/2)],
  R=sqrt(Delta^2+4g^2),
  b'(t)=-g^2 integral_0^t exp[-i Delta(t-s)] b(s) ds.
check_causal_ambiguity.py checks the latter identity numerically at 25 times for g=.7,Delta=1.1, with max residual 4.28e-16. The algebra is the proof; the floating-point check only guards the implementation/sign convention.

2. Finite-band absorption does not bound total causal response energy

Use H(w)=integral_0^infinity h(t)exp(iwt)dt. For
  h_gamma(t)=1_{t>=0} exp(-gamma t) sin(Omega t), gamma>0,
we have
  H_gamma(w)=Omega/[(gamma-iw)^2+Omega^2],
  Im H_gamma(w)=2 gamma w Omega /
      [(Omega^2+gamma^2-w^2)^2+4gamma^2 w^2].
This is a causal stable response with nonnegative absorption for w>0. On a fixed measured band [0,W] with W<Omega, Im H_gamma tends uniformly to zero as gamma tends to zero. In contrast,
  integral_0^infinity |h_gamma(t)|^2 dt
       = Omega^2/[4gamma(gamma^2+Omega^2)] -> infinity.
Therefore any positive absorption-error tolerance on a finite band allows this passive causal family to have unbounded total response L2 energy, unless an out-of-band oscillator/lifetime/energy constraint is additionally supplied. This is true even though every gamma>0 member is stable and square integrable.

Numerical example in normalized units: Omega=100; measured band20–72. Reducing gamma from1e-3 to1e-6 reduces max in-band imaginary response6.21e-7→6.21e-10 while time-domain energy rises250→250000. The real in-band response tends to about0.02076 at the band edge; an independent phase/quadrature measurement can reveal it. These are analytic synthetic countermodels, not a claim that LiF has such an arbitrary high-Q mode. Specimen-specific physics may exclude them, but that exclusion must be an explicit premise supported by measurement or theory. Causality and passivity alone do not exclude them.

Consequences: applying an untruncated Kramers–Kronig/Hilbert transform to a cropped absorption spectrum silently chooses an out-of-band completion. Window stability is useful sensitivity analysis but cannot prove a universal completion bound. This gate blocks an unrestricted full-response-norm certificate from the deposited LiF DeltaOD arrays alone. It does not prove that every constrained null-discrimination statistic is impossible.

3. What a phase-only norm certificate would actually require

For a single isolated response channel, or an explicitly common-phase null, assume
  h_tau(t)=exp[i phi_tau(t)] h_0(t), phi_tau real.
Then |h_tau(t)|=|h_0(t)|, and Parseval gives equality of full spectral L2 energies. Define the band norm using measure dw/(2pi). Suppose a measurement supplies complex Hhat_j on band B with rigorous L2 error eps_j, and an independent baseline-tail bound
  ||H_0||_{B^c}^2 <= T_0.
Writing q_j=||Hhat_j||_B, the sufficient rejection condition is
  max(q_tau-eps_tau,0)^2 > (q_0+eps_0)^2 + T_0.
Proof: the left side is a lower bound on the dressed full energy; the right side is an upper bound on the baseline full energy. A phase-only multiplication preserves that energy, contradiction.

This rejects the stated null only. It does not uniquely establish a dark exciton: dephasing, ionization, other amplitude changes, unresolved channel interference, sample damage, or transfer-function changes can also reject it. Appropriate controls and a physical alternative family are still required. Neither a complex response measurement nor the necessary tail bound is present in the acquired LiF absorption archive. CATS/SPRINT supply established phase-sensitive measurement concepts; they are incumbents, not inventions by this investigation.

4. Multiple bright channels defeat the unrestricted scalar amplitude claim

If the allowed null instead has known baseline channels h_i(t) and independent diagonal phases,
  h_tau(t)=sum_i exp[i phi_i(t)] h_i(t),
total |h_tau| need not equal |sum_i h_i|. Interference changes the composite amplitude without population transfer. At every t the necessary bounds are
  L(t)<=|h_tau(t)|<=U(t),
  U(t)=sum_i |h_i(t)|,
  L(t)=max(2 max_i|h_i(t)|-U(t),0).
The upper bound is the triangle inequality; the lower bound follows by separating the largest term and applying the reverse triangle inequality. For unrestricted independent phases, every magnitude in this interval is obtainable by arranging vectors of those lengths in the complex plane. This pointwise sufficiency does not assert that arbitrary time-dependent phase functions satisfy a given physical Stark model or smoothness constraint.

Hence a common-phase invariant must not be advertised as excluding every diagonal/no-transfer model. LiF has four bright levels in the source fit. A stronger certificate needs individually resolved/calibrated channels or a defended relationship among their phase shifts. Decomposing a static absorption line into four Gaussians supplies a model-dependent channel representation, not direct independent observation of each bright amplitude.

5. Angle-only controls have a separate restricted information limit

In an isotropic medium, in the electric-dipole weak-dressing regime with a rank-four response tensor and real linear polarizations, rotational invariance gives
  T_ijkl=a delta_ij delta_kl+b delta_ik delta_jl+c delta_il delta_jk.
Contracting with two XUV and two dressing polarization vectors yields a response of the form
  S(theta,w,t)=A(w,t)+B(w,t)cos(theta)^2
for the usual parallel-readout pump/probe geometry, after relabeling a,b,c if necessary. Thus parallel and perpendicular spectra fix the two scalar functions for this geometry; intermediate angles only test that assumed angular family. They cannot distinguish every microscopic model sharing it.

Scope: this rank-four result requires the stated perturbative/isotropy assumptions. The actual LiF field is strong and the source includes nonperturbative dynamics and solid-state orbital mixing; this note does not assert that its full response is exactly rank-four. Higher-order response, polarization-resolved detection, independently phased pulses, and frequency controls can add information. Established polarization-selective TA and phase-sensitive FWM already use such interfaces. Any new operation must specify what independent observable it gains beyond those methods.

6. Coupling signs require a gauge-invariant scientific target

Under a basis-phase change |j> -> exp(i theta_j)|j>, every Hamiltonian/dipole matrix transforms as
  M_jk -> exp(-i theta_j) M_jk exp(i theta_k),
and state coordinates and measured operators transform consistently. All physical predictions are identical. In a real-matrix model this includes independent vertex sign flips. Thus individual coupling signs/phases cannot be inferred as convention-independent physical quantities. Products around closed directed cycles are invariant because vertex phases cancel. For a connected graph with E nonzero edges and V states, a spanning tree fixes V-1 relative vertex phases, leaving E-V+1 independent cycle phases.

The source LiF graph has ground-to-four-bright edges (4), dark1-to-three-bright edges (3), and dark2-to-three-bright edges (3): V=7,E=10, cycle rank4 when all listed edges are genuinely nonzero. Ground-to-bright dipoles chosen positive fix four bright gauges; one edge per dark state fixes the two dark gauges. The four remaining relative signs can equivalently be represented by four independent closed-loop products. If a weak edge is zero, the graph/cycle count changes; the source fitted mu_(3,d2)=0.025 with confidence interval0.13 does not establish its nonzero sign. A physically meaningful new operation must target supported cycle products or another explicitly invariant observable, not an absolute sign of a matrix element.

This is standard gauge reasoning, not a novelty claim. Transition-dipole phase effects in ATAS already have explicit prior art: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6796354/ .

7. An oscillator-strength sum alone does not repair the L2-tail gate

For the causal family in section2,
  integral_0^infinity w Im H_gamma(w) dw = pi Omega/2,
independent of gamma. This follows either from the usual contour sum rule (H(z)=-Omega/z^2+O(z^-3)) or the time-domain initial derivative h_gamma'(0+)=Omega. Thus the same fixed oscillator-strength moment is compatible with arbitrarily large total L2 energy as gamma tends to zero. A specimen-specific linewidth lower bound, stronger spectral model, direct quadrature, or another norm-tail bound is needed; supplying only a total oscillator strength is insufficient. This is a statement about the stated response family, not about attainable core-exciton linewidths in LiF. The source's assumed40meV Auger-Meitner rate is additional physical input and must be retained if used to restrict this family.

8. Specific intensity-only angular gate for the stronger NaF control archive

A later lead from worker a04_spectral_control is polarization-selective NaF FWM, arXiv2604.06112v2(2026), DOI10.1103/PhysRevB.114.105120. I independently read the primary source and verified public Zenodo19443441 API metadata. The raw archives are being handled by a04; this lane did not acquire duplicate NaF raw observations.

Under the source's leading third-order, isotropic electric-dipole model, write the complex response tensor as in section5. Fix XUV=NIR1=x and rotate the real linear polarization of NIR2 to e2=cos(theta)x+sin(theta)y. Contraction gives
  P(theta)=(a+b+c)cos(theta)x+c sin(theta)y.
For polarization-unresolved emission intensity, the orthogonal components do not interfere, so
  I(theta)=|a+b+c|^2 cos(theta)^2+|c|^2 sin(theta)^2.
Consequently parallel and crossed intensities reveal only two squared tensor combinations for this geometry. Intermediate linear angles test this law but add no phase information within this model. Suppressed crossed emission constrains |c|, rather than uniquely identifying every microscopic pathway contributing to a,b,c. This is not a criticism of the source's scoped orbital interpretation, which also uses physical selection rules and DFT/BSE. It prevents inflating that observable into a complete mechanism determination.

The exact linearly independent extra interfaces could include rotating NIR1 independently, controlled elliptic/relative-phase inputs, polarization-resolved output/Stokes measurements, or a coherent output reference. Which is sufficient depends on the precise target and symmetry/model family. Intensity-only full-control tomography can have different identifiability from the two recorded angles; do not assert that a heterodyne reference is universally necessary for every gauge-invariant target. For an independently calibrated absolute complex field response, a phase reference or a defended equivalent constraint is necessary.
