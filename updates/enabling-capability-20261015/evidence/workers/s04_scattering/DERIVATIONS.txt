Exact ambiguity and measurement-change analysis
2026-10-15; established physics, no novelty claim

1. Inversion ambiguity of kinematical, nonresonant scattering

For reciprocal vector q, let F_X(q)=sum_j f_j(q) exp(i q.r_j), with each
f_j(q) real. Occupancies and real displacement factors can be absorbed into
f_j. Let X^- have every coordinate -r_j. Then

F_(X^-)(q) = conjugate(F_X(q)), so I_(X^-)(q)=I_X(q).

This holds for every q, not just a finite sampled grid. Any observation law
depending on X only through these intensities, including a fixed calibrated
Poisson detector, gives identical data distributions for X and X^-. Under
equal hand priors, no estimator can have error below 1/2 on this two-point
subproblem. A learned structure prior can select a hand but does not acquire
that hand from these measurements. This theorem does not apply to anomalous
complex form factors or dynamical multiple scattering.

2. A different forward model alone does not cure an unknown scalar gain

Even if two dynamical structures predict distinct positive scalar intensities
s_+(theta), s_-(theta), observing y=a*s_h(theta) with unknown a>0 leaves the
hand nonidentified: a_h=y/s_h(theta) fits either hand exactly at any fixed
theta where both intensities are positive. Independent unknown gains at each
energy repeat this obstruction; additional energies need calibrated relative
responses or linked reference channels.

Acquired cinchonine example, reflection (0,0,1), supplied t=100 A predictions:
y=1083; s_+=0.00005592562288054204, s_-=0.00005098601068648734.
Gains 19365005.595258262 and 21241120.562645312 respectively both produce
zero residual. These are source-computed predictions and an acquired measured
reflection, not newly computed dynamical truth.

Thus at least two positive intensity outcomes sharing a known relative gain
are needed to remove a completely free scalar exposure scale in this model.
Two outcomes are not universally sufficient: thickness, tilt, shape, detector
response and background can leave the normalized predictions overlapping.

3. Conditional compact selector from the actual supplied data

For two observations sharing scale, d=(I_h-I_-h)/(I_h+I_-h) removes scale.
For h=(2,-2,-2) in the acquired cinchonine tables, the ten supplied thicknesses
100,200,...,1000 A give d_+ in [0.4846178343,0.9665955013] and d_- in
[-0.9485851045,-0.4849668682]. The intervals are disjoint on that finite grid.
Measured intensities 583.0 and 238.4 give d=0.4195276357. Source errors 76.33
and 33.54 give delta-method sigma(d)=0.0791795657 under uncorrelated errors.
The two intensity centroids are separated by 2 source frames, about 0.5044 deg.

This is not an interval enclosure over continuous thickness or orientation.
It also assumes relative detector response, background correction and
indexing. It is selected after examining a full incumbent reconstruction and
simulation grid. It establishes neither a prospective compact experiment nor
acquisition/dose advantage. The 2023 incumbent already tests reducing data
completeness, and single-pattern/single-reflection chirality methods pre-exist.

4. Geometry calibration is a physical premise

Elastic scattering is covariant under a simultaneous orthogonal transformation
of the specimen and the complete experimental geometry. For a reflection M
with det(M)=-1, replacing V(r) by V(M^T r), the incident/detected wavevectors
by M times those vectors, and the full rotation trajectory by its transformed
trajectory preserves the intensities. This follows by the coordinate change
r'=M r in the wave equation. It includes multiple scattering, so it is not a
failure of the kinematical approximation.

When the detector viewing convention and the goniometer rotation sense are
unknown up to such a transformation, an algorithm's preferred hand is only
relative to its assumed geometry. Fix the convention with independently known
geometry or a known-hand calibration reference. A reference used to choose
the convention cannot also count as an independent validation sample.

Klar et al.'s primary supplementary discussion explicitly describes a
detector-view flip causing the wrong enantiomorph assignment in two earlier
data sets. The paper corrected the data orientation. This calibration issue
and its repair are known prior art, not a new detector operation.

5. Correlated supplied frames are not independent measurements

The HPM-1_RT data audit finds frame 20m, for m=1,...,22, exactly equals
ceil((frame(20m-1)+frame(20m+1))/2) pixelwise. If B is the collection of 429
nonfill files and Z these 22 fills, then Z=g(B) deterministically. Therefore
the sigma-algebra generated by (B,Z) equals the sigma-algebra generated by B.
For any structural parameter X, I(X;(B,Z))=I(X;B) whenever these mutual
informations are defined. The fills add no acquired structural information.
They may still be useful numerical inputs to a quadrature/integration rule.
Their uncertainty and reuse must be carried into any inferential confidence.
The same principle applies to overlapping virtual frames. This statement
does not imply the source's resulting structural assignment is incorrect.
