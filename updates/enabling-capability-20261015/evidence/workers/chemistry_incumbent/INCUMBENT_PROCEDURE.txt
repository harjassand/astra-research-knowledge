OBSERVED EXACT INTERFACE
The source code (SHA256 980806cc113608a03878054a519e9ef1ef486a020893771505b3680269d56b40)
extracts UV channels in the inclusive 255-305 nm window. Actual channel count and
grid must be read from MZML; assuming 51 independent channels is not justified.
It applies asymmetric least-squares baseline correction (lambda 1e5, p .005,
10 iterations), then independently fits Gaussian peaks at each wavelength.
Product window is 1.0-1.5 min, initial center 1.14; caffeine window .3-.6 min,
initial center .48. Caffeine normalization is specifically its 270 nm peak area.
The supplied is_nm argument is ignored in the function body, so the actual
normalizer stays 270 nm. Instrument-specific pure calibrations are pivoted by
wavelength from .pkl tables with columns nm and ext_coef. The two columns are
CF3_DP_<INSTRUMENT> and CF3_SP_<INSTRUMENT>. Each integrated spectrum y is fit as
y = A c, c=(desired,side), using scipy least_squares without nonnegativity bounds.
The residual covariance calculation uses s^2 (J'J)^-1 and marginal t widths.

SI40 restricts the reported spectral procedure to mixtures above Product/IS .2,
described there as 20% combined assay yield. Lower-signal mixtures generally gave
large fit errors. Authentic 3 and 13 standards and distinct UV shapes resolve this
specific pair; this is not arbitrary structure identification. S17 plots fitted
outcomes and error bars, including negative estimates; it is not a table of
independently known mixture concentrations.

STRONGER CONVENTIONAL BASELINE AND DERIVATION
Let A have the two authentic-standard response columns on the acquired grid; y
is the measured normalized mixture. Let Sigma be covariance determined from
measurement replicates, and N contain any predeclared baseline/interference
directions. Include all channels already acquired by the proposed operation.
The conventional objective is
  min_{c>=0,b} (y-Ac-Nb)' Sigma^-1 (y-Ac-Nb).
For positive-definite Sigma, whiten with its Cholesky factor and project out the
whitened N columns. This gives ordinary NNLS on two residualized columns. With
two analytes, enumerate the four possible active sets: neither, desired only,
side only, both. For each, solve its small least-squares problem and retain the
feasible solution with the smallest objective. This is the global optimum of
the specified convex problem. No new chemical information is created.

Compute singular values of the whitened, residualized A. A zero smallest singular
value is nonidentifiability. A small value means large concentration uncertainty.
If the columns are proportional and the compounds coelute exactly, y contains
only one response-weighted combination of concentrations. Positivity, a better
optimizer, or a language model does not recover the absent independent contrast.
Likewise an unmodeled interferent lying in span(A,N) need not leave a residual.

Correlated noise matters. Every wavelength is divided by the same caffeine peak
area, so denominator noise induces cross-wavelength covariance. Baseline fitting,
spectral detector resolution, and peak fitting add further dependencies. Diagonal
weights are acceptable only if supported by the acquired covariance. Ordinary
independent-wavelength t widths do not establish empirical coverage.

For a fixed correct A and Gaussian noise with KNOWN Sigma, the nonnegative set
  {c: SSE(c)-SSE(c_hat) <= chi2_2(.95)=5.991464547107979}
has at least 95% joint coverage. After nuisance projection the unconstrained
least-squares improvement at the true c is chi-square with two degrees of
freedom; constrained SSE is at least unconstrained SSE, yielding the conservative
bound. Coordinate projections are obtained by fixing one c and profiling the
other (its one-variable minimizer is clamped at zero). This is a CONDITIONAL
statement. It does not cover estimated A, estimated Sigma, wrong nuisance
models, contamination, detector drift, nonlinear matrix effects, or dilution
error. conditional_gls_nnls.py implements this conventional component and an
optional measured-data comparison. It is not the full calibrated assay.

The full strongest practical comparator must additionally use actual calibration
replicates and instrument/day variation, jointly propagate standard preparation,
pure-spectrum, peak integration, internal-standard and dilution uncertainty,
and verify the resulting intervals against held-out independently quantified
mixtures. An errors-in-variables/hierarchical likelihood or calibration-plus-
measurement resampling is conventional. Any bootstrap needs its own assumptions;
calling intervals calibrated requires observed held-out coverage, not their name.
An independently established limiting-reagent budget can add concentration
upper bounds or a two-product sum constraint to this convex fit. It requires
reconciled amounts, dilution and stoichiometry; clipping source yields above100%
does not resolve an uncertain normalization or establish an accurate assay.

If raw time-by-wavelength arrays exist, the comparator should also fit the full
matrix instead of discarding temporal information into independent Gaussian
areas. Authentic-spectrum nonnegative curve resolution can estimate component
elution profiles and integrate them. Constrained MCR-ALS / PARAFAC2 are existing
alternatives for partial coelution, shifted peaks, background and batch structure.
Any rank, shape, shared-spectrum, and peak-number assumptions must be selected
without seeing test truth. If matrix response differs, measured multivariate
standard additions are an incumbent control, with their injections/preparation
charged. Standard addition does not separate two exactly indistinguishable
initial analytes merely by adding known amounts: independent response contrast
or a physical separation/selective intervention remains necessary.

Actual fair selection: fit source OLS, weighted NNLS, and applicable full-matrix
methods on identical acquired information. Choose/tune within the calibration
set. Freeze preprocessing, rejection rules, units and calibration; then compare
correct-product concentration error, side-product error, false structure calls,
interval coverage and width, failure/abstention rate, and all costs on blind
mixtures. Include pure endpoints, near-zero minority isomer, low total amount,
varied ratios and the same reaction matrices. A producer's fitted concentrations
or recipe-derived predictions cannot serve as independent assay truth.
