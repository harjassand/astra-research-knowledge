2. PHYSICALLY ADMISSIBLE COMPOSITE NULL

Let b_j be the four XUV-created bright coherence channels. A no-discrete-dark-
resonance response can evolve under a diagonal Hamiltonian with shifts
delta_j(t,theta)=E_a(t) alpha_j,ab E_b(t), together with nonnegative Lindblad
dephasing or loss rates gamma_j(t,theta). It may also require calibrated
phonon dephasing, unresolved bright-manifold tensor mixing, continuum
photoionization, pulse satellites, delay jitter, and instrumental transfer.
The latter ingredients make this a composite physical null, not the source's
single common scalar phase with all parameters held fixed.

Important distinction: arbitrary fit parameters are not automatically
qualified for LiF. The acquired interface supplies no independent bounds on
each state-specific polarizability/rate, its frequency dependence, or
field-induced changes to the phonon response. Thus this worker constructs
valid causal response models and tests their predictions, but cannot certify
that their parameter sets are the complete admissible LiF class. Conversely,
there is no acquired basis for excluding them by assuming the source's one
scalar polarizability is exhaustive.

Why polarization suppression is insufficient by itself. For an isotropic
ensemble, the rank-four optical response has tensor form

 T_ijkl = a delta_ij delta_kl + b delta_ik delta_jl
                                + c delta_il delta_jk.

Contracting two XUV polarization vectors and two NIR vectors gives
a+(b+c)cos(theta)^2. XUV excitation creates an aligned responding ensemble
even when the initial sample is polycrystalline. Therefore a large endpoint
contrast can occur in an anisotropic nonresonant response. This statement
does not assert that such a model explains all LiF observations; it removes
the invalid inference from angular contrast alone to a unique resonant
mechanism.

An explicit passive counter-pattern is a bright Lorentzian

 A(E;delta,Gamma)=S Gamma/((E-E_b-delta)^2+Gamma^2), S>0.

A positive extra dephasing rate makes

 partial_Gamma A = S[(E-E_b-delta)^2-Gamma^2]
                   /[(E-E_b-delta)^2+Gamma^2]^2.

The center decreases while sufficiently remote wings increase. Let the extra
rate be gamma_parallel*cos(theta)^2+gamma_perp*sin(theta)^2, both nonnegative.
Then the positive-wing/negative-center pattern can be strongly suppressed
for crossed fields without introducing a new electronic pole. Signed state-
specific shifts provide another independent line-shape deformation. This is
an analytic physical pattern, not a synthetic substitute for the real-data
test below.

The strongest broad null can include a continuum self-energy. The exact
dark-state elimination/memory equivalence and finite-band norm obstruction
are developed independently in
../measurement_search/MATHEMATICAL_ACCESS_GATES.txt. An eliminated resonant
state and its causal-memory representation are not distinct observational
mechanisms. Arbitrarily labeling that memory a Stark phase is also invalid:
an unrestricted memory kernel is broader than an instantaneous phase model.

3. EXECUTED CAUSAL DIAGONAL REFIT

To make a concrete, reviewable comparator, equilibrium absorption was fitted
using four positive Voigt peaks plus a linear continuum background. The
Lorentzian HWHM was fixed at 0.04 eV; Gaussian widths and positive peak
strengths were fitted only to the separate static experiment. Four peak
centers were bounded near source-selected positions. The fitted static
RMSE is 1.8991 mOD over 92 pixels; fourteen free parameters. This is not the
source's exact Mahan phonon-response fit. In particular, the highest weak
Voigt center is 62.7963 eV, not an independently identified new level.

For an impulsive XUV excitation, define causal baseline channel responses

 k_j(t)=Theta(t) exp[-i mu_j t/hbar-Gamma_0 t/hbar
                         -sigma_j^2 t^2/(2 hbar^2)].

An ensemble of static Gaussian detunings produces the last factor. A
diagonal Hamiltonian and nonnegative Markov dephasing produce the fitted
field factor

 R_j(t,tau,p)=exp[-(gamma_j,p+i delta_j,p)Q(t,tau)/hbar],
 Q(t,tau)=integral_0^t h(u-tau)du, h>=0.

This is a causal response with no added discrete electronic dark state.
The differential absorption is the real Fourier transform of
k_j(t)[R_j(t,tau,p)-1], normalized to its fitted static peak. The implemented
h consists of a main positive Gaussian and two nonnegative satellite
Gaussians. Four coefficients are shared between polarizations: time shift,
main FWHM, and two satellite weights. Unknown broad differential background
has an affine spectral shape times the same field envelope, with two
coefficients per polarization. The latter are nuisance terms, not a new
exciton assignment.

Models fitted under the same transforms:

  common shift/rate: one shift and one positive extra rate per polarization,
                    shared across bright channels; 12 fitted parameters.
  state-specific:   four shifts and four positive extra rates per
                    polarization; 24 fitted parameters total.
  Stark template:   source no-dark output with amplitude per polarization,
                    shared time offset/stretch and energy offset, plus the
                    affine envelope background; 9 fitted parameters.
  full template:    same 9-parameter recalibration of source full output.

The last two are calibrated output templates, not TDSE parameter refits.
Different parameter counts and model restrictions preclude interpreting
their ranking as a fair selection between dark and no-dark physical models.

The state-specific physical fit uses two deterministic starting points.
Only local optimization is claimed. Delta bounds are +/-1 eV; rates 0--1 eV.
The strict-reference selected solution has main FWHM 6.2090 fs and time
offset -1.2661 fs. Parallel shifts are [0.01426,0.00095,-0.01137,-0.20236] eV;
parallel extra rates [0.05021,0,0.05533,0] eV. Crossed shifts are
[0.00978,-0.01399,-0.00371,0.00983] eV and extra rates
[0.01593,0.00959,0.01273,0.02674] eV. They are fitted phenomenological
parameters, not acquired microscopic LiF constants.

4. EXACT DISCRIMINATORS, AND THEIR REQUIRED INTERFACES

4A. Statistical set-exclusion gate

Let F_0(theta,nu) be the complete raw-observation prediction of a specified
no-resonant-dark model, including instrument calibration nu in an independently
bounded set V. Suppose an independently justified observation confidence
region is C(y)={m:||W(y-m)||<=r}, with W invertible. The exact decision
condition is

  inf_{theta in Theta_0,nu in V} ||W[y-F_0(theta,nu)]|| > r.

Only then is that composite null excluded at the region's stated coverage.
A local nonlinear least-squares minimum is an UPPER bound on this infimum,
so it cannot certify exclusion. A residual against a single ablation has
no such implication. To claim a coupling lower bound rather than simple
exclusion, optimize the target over every physical model in C(y) and V and
show its infimum exceeds a declared, gauge-invariant threshold.

A valid convex outer relaxation K_0 of the complete null observable set can
support an exact dual witness: find w such that

  w^T y > sup_{m in K_0} w^T m + r ||W^{-T}w||.

This separates the confidence ellipsoid from every admissible null. The
outer inclusion and calibration/noise bounds must be proved. They are not
supplied by this execution. Optimizing a convenient synthetic proxy, a
template distance or an uncalibrated pixel MSE does not fill this interface.

4B. Exact local-diagonal response identity

Assume a known impulsive pump, separately resolved complex bright channel
response k_j(t), and an NIR perturbation local to the field time s=t-tau.
After dividing out the known equilibrium response, write

  r_j(t,tau)=exp[integral_0^t G_j(u-tau)du], Re G_j<=0.

For all positive t1,t2, direct division of the integral gives the cocycle

  r_j(t1+t2,tau)=r_j(t1,tau) r_j(t2,tau-t1).

This is exact for any local field-only diagonal phase/loss law, not just a
Gaussian pulse or constant alpha. Equivalently partial_t log r_j(t,tau)
depends only on t-tau. A resolved violation excludes this specific local
model, once finite-pump, calibration, detector and baseline effects are
bounded. It detects additional memory/mixing; it does not uniquely identify
a dark exciton. Field-dependent phonon memory or bright-manifold mixing can
also violate the chosen scalar identity.

The acquired data give one finite-band absorption quadrature of the SUM of
channels, with unknown incident spectral phase and unresolved channel
decomposition. They do not supply r_j. Adding an arbitrary Hilbert completion
of the measured band is not an acquired quadrature. This gate currently
fails at the measurement interface.

4C. Independent-sign double-pulse control

For nonoverlapping independently sign-reversible gates E1,E2, any local model
that depends evenly on EACH gate has the same prediction for all four sign
settings. Thus the mixed contrast

  C11=[S(+,+)-S(+,-)-S(-,+)+S(-,-)]/4

is exactly zero, including even-control bright tensor mixing. Coherent
linear-in-field excursions with memory between gates can produce nonzero
C11. Pulse overlap contributes a local E1*E2 term and must be independently
bounded; odd local terms, permanent dipoles, symmetry breaking and calibrated
bright-bright pathways must also be included or bounded. This distinguishes
an even local-control null from coherent odd-control memory, not a unique
upper dark exciton. a04_spectral_control independently audited this control
and its tensor/missing-table failure modes; see that worker's REPORT.txt.
No new pulse preparation, full factorial phase cycle, channel-selective
readout or hardware access was obtained here.

4D. A finite-precision obstruction even with better complex readout

An isolated electronic dark pole is not uniformly distinguishable from an
arbitrarily narrow unobserved continuum without a lower continuum-width or
regularity bound. This can be demonstrated with a positive-energy bath.
Take an excited bright space coupled with strength g to a normalized bath
vector f on L2([E_d-epsilon,E_d+epsilon]), E_d>epsilon>0. At epsilon=0 let
the bath Hamiltonian be E_d I: only f is reached, exactly reproducing one
discrete dark level. For epsilon>0 let it multiply by energy. It then has
continuous spectrum and no atomic pole. The Hamiltonian difference has
operator norm<=epsilon. With the same bounded external controls, Duhamel's
formula gives, for finite observation time T,

  ||U_epsilon(t)-U_0(t)|| <= epsilon |t|/hbar, |t|<=T.

For any bounded observable O and normalized initial state, its expectation
difference is <=2||O|| epsilon T/hbar. For a uniform spectral weight the bath
memory kernel is

  K_epsilon(t)=g^2 exp(-i E_d t/hbar) sinc(epsilon t/hbar),
  |K_epsilon-K_0| <= g^2 epsilon^2 t^2/(6 hbar^2).

Therefore the no-atomic-pole observable set has the discrete-pole set in its
closure for finite-time, finite-precision measurements. Any claimed robust
discrete-pole discriminator needs an independently justified restriction
excluding arbitrarily narrow continua. This mathematical construction is
not a claim that such a bath is the actual LiF material. It demonstrates
the required interface and why a pole label cannot be inferred from a
finite residual alone. Orbital/excitonic character needs further physical
information, not just minimal-realization system identification.
