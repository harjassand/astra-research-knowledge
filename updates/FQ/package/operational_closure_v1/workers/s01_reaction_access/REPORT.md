# Finite-amplitude atom-ancestry port audit for catalytic selectivity

15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. **Status: conditional construction; physical capability and priority UNKNOWN.** No laboratory experiment was available. The original element here is the exact task contract joining finite chemical-potential challenges, isotope source-to-product transfer, conservative carbon accounting, and a control decision. Isotope transients, operando product analysis, and pulsed catalytic control themselves have strong experimental prior art. This report does not claim a new assay or a general solution to unknown reaction-network control.

## Decision and physical interface

The target is a flow catalyst with two independently adjustable, carbon-bearing feed ports, such as CO2 and CO in a co-feed electroreduction cell, and a specified carbon-containing desired product. Choose a finite, preregistered family of *actual* feed partial pressures/potentials and disturbances that includes the proposed operating point plus finite-amplitude log-activity challenges on both sides of it. At each point: (i) wait for a measured stationary window; (ii) switch the isotope enrichment at one carbon feed port while holding its total chemical feed fixed; (iii) reverse the port label in another run; (iv) measure isotope-resolved gas and liquid product rates, unreacted feeds, current/energy, and an inert residence-time tracer; (v) repeat the sequence in randomized order to expose drift. The intended decision is whether the target-feed carbon yield and selectivity at *those challenged points* clear predeclared bounds. A failed label mass balance, changed total rates on isotope switching, or non-repeatable catalyst state rejects the certificate.

For an electrochemical CO2/CO example, electrolyte bicarbonate/carbonate, catalyst carbon inventory and any carbon-containing impurities must be treated as extra ports or bounded inventory. Bulk feed composition is not automatically the interfacial chemical potential. The 2019 CO2/CO co-feed experiment establishes that isotope-labelled cross-coupling and time-resolved products can be measured in a capillary flow cell; it does **not** establish that the full accounting protocol proposed here has been achieved for a useful catalyst [S2]. The 2017 thermal Pd/Al2O3 study directly demonstrates a chemically steady ^12CO2 to ^13CO2 switch, product MS, operando FTIR and an Ar hold-up control [S1].

Finite amplitude matters. If an unknown branch has a rate proportional to both feed activities, a baseline with one feed absent can show no branch even when the operating co-feed activates it. A two-by-two rectangle of low/high activities can expose its nonadditive output through `Y(hi,hi)-Y(hi,lo)-Y(lo,hi)+Y(lo,lo)`. This difference detects a coupled response, **not** a unique elementary reaction: adsorption competition, transport, or phase change can also generate it. Isotope switches at each corner distinguish which feed supplies product atoms. Small-signal fluctuation/response at one baseline is not promoted to a finite-amplitude guarantee; the proposed operating state itself is challenged.

## Exact network-free observable

Fix a chemical control `u` and a reproducible catalyst state. Let `f_i(t)` be the fraction of tracer isotope at feed port `i`, and let `L_j(t)` be the *rate of labeled carbon atoms*, not merely labeled molecules, exiting in product bin `j`. For ethylene, for example, count one unit for a singly ^13C-labelled molecule and two for a doubly labelled molecule. Assume the isotope is a passive tag: changing `f` does not change the unlabeled physical trajectories or total rates, each relevant carbon atom keeps its identity, and all carbon sources crossing the reactor boundary are included. Then, conditional on any unlabeled history, the expected label of each exiting atom equals the enrichment at its source port and entry time. Summing atoms gives

`E[L_j(t;f)-L_j(t;f0)] = sum_i integral K_ji(t,s;u) [f_i(s)-f0_i(s)] ds`, with `K_ji >= 0`.

This **atom-ancestry kernel** exists even with unobserved intermediates, nonlinear chemistry, C-C coupling and a large reaction network. The argument is linearity of *expected isotope count*, not linearity of chemical kinetics. Under stationary operation, `K(t,s)=K(t-s)` and its time integral `A_ji(u)` is the rate of output-j carbon atoms sourced from feed i. Two stationary isotope fractions differing only at port i give

`A_ji(u) = [L_j(f_i^high;u)-L_j(f_i^low;u)] / (f_i^high-f_i^low)`

after isotope baseline, residence, detector-response and pool-inventory corrections. A transient pulse yields the lag distribution and flags long storage. This identifies a **source-to-output port map**, not a microscopic path. Two hidden networks with identical ancestry kernels are indistinguishable by this assay, which is acceptable only if the stated decision depends on their common output behavior.

Let `F_i` be incoming carbon atoms/time from the target feed, `U_i` its carbon returned as unchanged feed species, and `P_i=A_{Pi}` its carbon in the desired product. At a stationary challenged point, the target-feed carbon selectivity among net converted atoms is `S_i=P_i/(F_i-U_i)`. If calibrated measurements give `P_i >= P_i^-`, `F_i <= F_i^+`, and `U_i >= U_i^-`, a valid conservative bound is

`S_i >= P_i^-/(F_i^+-U_i^-)`, provided the denominator is positive.

Unidentified products and irreversible storage cannot inflate this lower bound; they count against selectivity through the denominator. For a finite set `D` of operating disturbances, require the lower bound and an absolute desired-product throughput bound at every tested `(u,d)`. This is a task-specific certificate without full mechanism reconstruction. It says nothing about untested concentrations, disturbances, arbitrarily long time or catalyst states. To extend to a continuous neighborhood of radius `r`, one would need an independently justified uniform response bound, e.g. `|P_i(u)-P_i(u')| <= L_P ||u-u'||`; secants from finitely many tests do not prove such a bound. Ongoing isotope sentinels can detect drift but cannot make it impossible.

## Dormant hidden exchange: exact synthetic calculation

Consider feed-accessible pools `B,C`, each with a unit-rate exit to desired `P` and byproduct `Q`, respectively, and an unlisted reversible exchange `B <-> C` at per-molecule rate `k` in each direction. The two-state tracer absorption matrix (rows `P,Q`, columns injections at `B,C`) is

`T = 1/(1+2k) [[1+k,k],[k,1+k]]`.

At `B=C=2`, the net exchange is zero and chemical stationary output rates can equal those in a model omitting the exchange. Yet a fully labeled B feed at rate 2 produces labeled Q carbon at `2k/(1+2k)`. With synthetic `k=1/100`, the cross-output fraction is `h=1/102`, and labeled Q rate is `1/51`; each gross exchange direction runs at `1/50`. The port map detects a chemically silent coupling. If the two-state, unit-exit model is independently credible, `k=h/(1-2h)`. At the proposed relocation `B=3,C=3/2`, net hidden exchange is `k(B-C)=3/200`, matching the prior campaign's dormant-channel stationarity residual. A hidden-exchange budget of `1/200` would permit only `|B-C|<=1/2`, so this relocation fails. The exact arithmetic is independently recomputed by `verify_port_audit.py` and saved in `toy_check_results.json` in this directory; it is synthetic, not experimental.

The exchange also links isotope and chemical-potential response under this **restricted** model. A symmetric dimensionless chemical-potential split `v` around `B=C=b` gives `B=b exp(v/2)`, `C=b exp(-v/2)`, and net exchange `J(v)=2kb sinh(v/2)`. Thus `J'(0)=kb`, the gross one-way exchange rate at balance. The isotope-inferred `k` predicts a finite-amplitude response `J(v)`, which can be checked against a measured concentration challenge. Failure rejects the restricted model; agreement at a few points does not rule out other channels. In an arbitrary network, `h/(1-2h)` is **not** a valid rate inference: a serial route or another transfer mechanism can have the same port map.

## Small adversarial failure and redesign limit

Even a perfect port map at all finite challenged points cannot certify indefinite operation without a catalyst-state assumption. Add just one latent catalyst fraction `z` and one hidden sink `B -> W` with rate `kappa z B`. Start `z(0)=0` and let `dz/dt=a(1-z)` under the proposed operating condition. Let `T` be the total cumulative exposure to that condition across all tests, with no reset. The extra integrated loss during that exposure is

`kappa B [T-(1-exp(-aT))/a] <= kappa B a T^2/2`.

For any finite detection threshold `delta` and test duration, choose `a < 2 delta/(kappa B T^2)`. The branch then sits below the assay's detection threshold during every test yet reaches half its eventual rate at time `ln(2)/a`. Its isotope and chemical signals become material during longer production. This is an exact smooth counterexample to an unlimited reliability claim, not evidence that a real catalyst has this gate. Catalyst restructuring and irreversible morphology changes under potential pulses are experimentally real, which makes aging controls operationally necessary [S3]. A longer challenge horizon, carbon-inventory accounting, periodic sentinel measurements and an explicit acceptable run length are the substantive redesign; no finite package yields a universal all-time guarantee.

There is a second irreducible limit: a fast internal futile cycle returning every carbon atom to its original output can have the same ancestry map and product selectivity while consuming extra electrical/chemical energy. The proposed selectivity certificate therefore separately measures current, heat and reagent use; isotope data alone do not certify energy efficiency or catalyst lifetime.

## Cost, comparator, and decisive test

If there are `m` feed ports, `n` challenged chemical states and `r` repeats, a straightforward serial pair of enrichments for each port uses `2mnr` runs, plus blanks and washout; a shared baseline can reduce the algebraic minimum to `(m+1)nr` runs if the catalyst remains unchanged. A four-corner concentration challenge and randomized return-to-baseline checks add runs. The total time is set by catalyst settling plus the slowest relevant tracer tail, not by inference time. Charge ^13C feed cost, valve mixing/dead volume, MS/GC calibration and isotope overlap, gas/liquid sample recovery, carbon inventory or carbonate exchange, interfacial concentration sensing, electrode power, catalyst preparation/aging, and false-acceptance risk from finite detection. High enrichment improves signal but increases material cost and can introduce isotope effects. A changed total rate on isotope switching invalidates passive labeling; an unchanged total rate alone cannot exclude compensating, route-specific isotope effects, so reversed enrichment and independent isotope-effect controls remain necessary. The published isotope-tracer reliability study documents contamination, separation and assignment pitfalls, so isotope peaks alone are insufficient [S4].

The strongest equally informed incumbent is ordinary factorial feed/potential design plus online GC/MS product optimization and steady-state isotopic transient kinetic analysis, given the **same** labeled feeds, controls, detector, budgets and time. That incumbent can measure the same port map and select the same certified point. With only one carbon source and complete product measurements, direct product-rate optimization may be cheaper. This construction offers a clean control contract and a diagnostic of zero-net source mixing; it has no established cost or capability advantage. Historical novelty remains UNKNOWN and likely overlaps strongly with tracer/SSITKA practice.

The decisive experiment is a blinded two-carbon-feed catalyst test with a hidden exchange/cross-coupling channel introduced by a controlled feed or catalyst intervention. Preregister the operating setpoint, finite ±log-activity challenges, isotope switches, product/atom-balance tolerances, energy and catalyst-state limits, and a production horizon. Compare decisions against the equally informed factorial/SSITKA incumbent on the same specimens and full resource ledger. A pass requires a materially better *measured* target-feed product rate/selectivity with bounded unassigned carbon and sustained behavior through the stated horizon. If the ordinary incumbent makes the same decision at equal or lower cost, or carbon/energy closure fails, the proposed capability advantage is absent. No such experiment was performed here.
