# S03 — geometric route memory with vacancy-gated regrowth

15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. **Status: proposed construction and exact conditional analysis; no physical experiment, demonstrated advantage, or priority claim.** The objective is to restore the *routed load-bearing graph* of an architected lattice after an unknown local member is lost. Repair must use one common feedstock and local surviving structure, with no defect-specific replacement library or map supplied during repair. The first target is a millimetre-scale polymer lattice, where route and mechanical function can both be measured. Translation to DNA origami or a nanoscale frame is not claimed.

## One construction

Fabricate a lattice with sacrificial load-bearing struts and persistent **oriented node collars**. At each potentially repairable edge, two thin, non-load-bearing guide ribs preserve a shaped path between its collars. The collars retain the port positions, outward directions, depth layer at a crossing, and anchoring surface. This *negative geometry*, not a copied strand sequence, is the local record of the missing edge. An intact strut occludes a feed inlet. Its complete loss releases a passive inlet shutter; a shared, externally replenished two-component repair fluid enters the now empty guide, forms a retaining gel, and cures into a new strut bonded to both collars. On successful refill, the new strut reseats the shutter. The guide and vascular supply remain available for another cycle. A first bench fixture can use a fixed inlet rather than claiming the shutter works before its actuation is measured.

This is a proposed *co-design*, not an existing demonstrated device. The only chemically supported components found here are broad ones: vascular delivery and two-stage gel-then-polymer restoration of lost polymer, and dynamic-covalent joining of an apposed broken metamaterial strut. Neither source demonstrates an oriented-collar guide that correctly recreates an erased, selected lattice edge from a common feed. The narrow potential contribution is that surviving mechanical geometry specifies **which** connection and spatial route should return, while a common material supply builds it. The collars and guides are costly, damageable physical memory; they must be included in mass, manufacturing and survival comparisons. An already load-bearing guide would make the lost strut partly redundant and may defeat the point of repair.

The four-arm obstruction from the preceding campaign is decisive. If the survivor leaves arms A,B,C,D but has erased every feature distinguishing AB/CD from AD/CB, both target worlds present the same input and no common repair process can identify the target. Here the two products are deliberately manufactured with different surviving guide paths or port orientations. If those features are also erased, the old one-half success bound returns. This construction is therefore a **bounded-erasure** design, not a solution to arbitrary destruction.

## Exact local mechanism and its limit

Let `S` be the actual surviving port poses, guide geometry, exposed chemistry and feed conditions around a damaged cavity. Form a compatibility graph `G(S)` on the exposed ports: an edge `ij` exists only if a replacement link can physically seat between ports `i,j` without violating measured distance, direction, guide clearance, chemical compatibility or an already occupied connection. A complete local topology is a perfect matching of this graph. If the intended matching `M*` is the **unique** perfect matching, then a local process restricted to the compatible edges has one topologically correct completed endpoint. In a finite graph, uniqueness is equivalent to the absence of an alternating even cycle relative to `M*`: the symmetric difference with any second perfect matching decomposes into such cycles, and flipping any such cycle gives a second completion. The familiar four-port wrong swap is the smallest relevant alternating cycle. Geometric keying must remove at least one of its wrong edges; merely retaining four generic reactive ends does nothing.

For a simple planar illustration, place A=(-1,1), B=(1,1), C=(-1,-1), D=(1,-1). Horizontal ports A/B and C/D face one another, so two horizontal guides specify AB/CD. All four ports may have identical chemistry and all repair fluid may be identical. A competing diagonal AD/CB needs incompatible bend or orientation at these collars. At a crossing, separate depth layers are also needed: restored covalent connectivity alone does not certify the original physical embedding or linking state. This toy shows how *surviving pose* can carry the erased pairing bit; it is not a fabricated measurement of alignment tolerances. If damage lets the collars rotate freely, even this bit may disappear.

Real guides leak selectivity. For a **modular-link implementation** that reversibly seats a replacement before irreversible lock, suppose the correct matching has free energy `E*`, every one of `m-1` wrong matchings is at least `Delta` higher, and seating equilibrates before cure. Boltzmann weighting gives

`P(correct | intact guide, equilibrium) >= 1/[1+(m-1) exp(-Delta/kBT)]`.

For three possible four-port matchings, a **hypothetical** 100 independently repaired cavity frame with at least 90% exact-topology yield would need per-cavity success at least `0.9^(1/100) = 0.998947...`; the energy-margin sufficient condition is `Delta/kBT >= ln[2/(0.9^(-1/100)-1)] = 7.548...`. This is a design threshold, not an acquired interaction energy. For the proposed **fluid regrowth**, no equilibrium seating law is assumed. If correct and wrong irreversible bridges form as competing first-event Poisson processes with rates `k_c` and aggregate `k_w`, then `P(correct)=k_c/(k_c+k_w)`, so the same frame target requires `k_w/k_c <= 0.9^(-1/100)-1 = 0.001054...` under independent identical sites. Actual polymer flow and cure may violate that race model; the rates and full wrong-route yield must be measured. A geometric free-energy margin alone does **not** imply the kinetic ratio.

The finite arithmetic was executed with [thresholds.py](thresholds.py); its raw output is [RESULTS.txt](RESULTS.txt). This is a calculation on stipulated targets, not a simulation or experimental observation.

This also states the information boundary exactly. For a target variable `T` and survivor `S`, deterministic exact recovery across an admitted target family requires `H(T|S)=0` under that family; this is necessary and by itself physically insufficient. If two targets induce identical `S`, no chemistry, computation or repeated cycles can distinguish them. The guide geometry must carry the missing route bits and costs storage/fabrication capacity proportional to the amount of independently programmable routing. Multiscale repair can enlarge the intact survivor region, but each level must store recoverable route information and a final erased outer boundary remains an obstruction. No immortal guide or template is assumed.

## Coexisting physical constraints

The following conditions must hold **together**; establishing one separately would not establish the operation.

1. **Geometry and access.** After member removal, surrounding cells must keep collar positions and directions inside the intended guide tolerance under service preload, thermal cycling and repeated impacts. The guide must admit fluid, release displaced debris and exclude wrong links. A cutter may damage the guide and vascular network in the same event. For an erasure of a complete junction, surviving neighbouring collars must still produce a unique compatibility matching; otherwise the trial is an information failure, not a slow cure.
2. **Delivery and chemistry.** Feedstock flow must fill the chosen path before gelation blocks its inlet (`t_fill < t_gel`); gel retention must beat drain or washout; cure must anchor at both collars and finish before the next damaging load. Off-target polymerization in intact cells, guide occlusion, incomplete mixing, shrinkage, oxygen inhibition, catalyst depletion and residual uncured fluid all enter the failure denominator. The cited two-stage polymer system supplies a *possible chemistry class*, not proof that these inequalities hold in a thin route-selective guide.
3. **Mechanical service.** Restored geometry must give adequate cross-section, modulus, tensile and compressive strength, fatigue life, and connection strength. Restoring a visual line or merely filling missing volume is insufficient. Repeated cure must not progressively thicken the frame or change its unit-cell angles. The published aromatic-disulfide metamaterial repair needed manual contact and 140 °C for 30 minutes; using that exact chemistry in this guide could relax/reprogram the geometry that stores the route. A lower-temperature compatible chemistry or a thermally stable guide would need separate evidence.
4. **Control and repeatability.** A vacancy-specific shutter must open after complete strut loss but remain closed during elastic deformation, and close after refill. If external pumping, illumination or heat is used, every pump, sensor, controller and energy input is counted. Repeated local repair requires the vascular branch and shutter to survive every cycle, not merely enough reagent in a reservoir.

Let `K` critical guide/routing records each be lost at independent hazard `rho` over time `T`. Even granting perfect strut repair, the intact-record probability is `exp(-K rho T)`; correlated impact damage can be worse. This is the same survival burden exposed in the prior DNA study, now applied honestly to mechanical route memory. It cannot be made favourable by conditioning reported yield on guide survival and silently dropping damaged-guide specimens. The total programme cost includes guide and collar fabrication, lost structural volume, vascular mass and reservoir, feedstock replenishment, actuation energy, maintenance, assay and failure cost. The relevant comparator is the same lattice with the same route information and resource allowance, using direct protection, modular part replacement, or generic vascular healing.

## Strongest incumbent and what would count as an advance

The nearest incumbents are substantial. Microtubules can incorporate new tubulin into damaged lattice sites and recover stiffness, a biological example of local geometric repair. Cheung and Gershenfeld made reversibly interlocking, locally constrained cellular composite parts, so geometry-directed assembly and replaceable modules are established. Toohey and colleagues demonstrated repeated vascular healing; White and colleagues later used vascular two-stage gel/polymer chemistry to restore lost material volume and mechanical function. Abbasoglu and colleagues repaired a cut architected polymer strut by apposing the ends and heating to activate dynamic disulfide exchange. A same-information competitor may combine any of these known affordances with the **same** guide geometry. This report has no evidence that our combination is cheaper or more accurate, or that its combination is novel.

A measurable added capability would be repeated, *unaddressed* recovery of the **correct routing and physical path after complete loss of one of several geometrically different struts**, without manually presenting or aligning its mate, while beating an optimized guide-aware refill/replacement baseline in full cost at the same exact-topology and service-function yield. Generic crack sealing, manual end apposition, recovered fluorescence, or restoration on selected intact-guide lesions would not establish that result. The likely first output is a bounded demonstration, not a general nanoscale self-repair platform.

## Decisive experiment

Build blinded A and B lattice coupons with the same bulk polymer, common feed and four exposed chemically identical port ends, but different physical route keys (AB/CD versus AD/CB, including a 3D crossing). Pre-register a damage distribution with full strut removal, blocked inlets, cut-only controls, guide damage, and paired neighbouring lesions. Randomize coupon identity and damage. Run one globally applied repair cycle with no site selection, then several damage/repair cycles. Count *every* assigned coupon. Three-dimensional tomography or destructive serial sectioning must check route and embedding; mechanical load/reload and fatigue tests must check service function; chemistry and vascular imaging must check attachment, complete cure, leaks and inlet resetting. Report repair time, mass, reagent volume, flow/pressure, heat/electricity/light, guide overhead, and maintenance per successful exactly restored coupon.

Compare against (i) the same guided coupon with no refill, to measure protection and geometric redundancy; (ii) optimized modular replacement using the same local socket geometry and allowed sensing/actuation; (iii) best practical vascular two-stage fill using the same guide, material and energy/time budget but no vacancy gating; and (iv) an ordinary dynamic-covalent cut-strut repair on its supported cut-only class. The experiment kills the proposal if wrong-route cures persist, guides fail with the struts, repeated inlets clog, function is not recovered, or a matched incumbent has equal/better complete yield and cost. No laboratory work or physical rate/energy measurement was performed in this investigation.

## Primary evidence actually inspected

- [Schaedel et al., *Nature Materials* 2015, DOI 10.1038/nmat4396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4620915/): inspected author manuscript abstract and the reported laser-damage/two-colour tubulin-incorporation and bending recovery passages. New tubulin was observed at damaged sites; this supports the *principle* of lattice-embodied local repair, not branched programmed routing.
- [Aher et al., *Current Biology* 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2020.03.070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32359430/): inspected PubMed figure/method excerpt for CLASP-assisted complete versus partial lattice repair and its tubulin incorporation controls. It does not demonstrate the proposed polymer guide.
- [Cheung and Gershenfeld, *Science* 2013, DOI 10.1126/science.1240889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23950496/): inspected primary abstract describing mass-produced interlocking composite parts and locally constrained site locations. This is a strong mechanical assembly comparator.
- [Toohey et al., *Nature Materials* 2007, DOI 10.1038/nmat1934](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17558429/): inspected primary abstract of repeated autonomous crack healing by three-dimensional microvascular supply.
- [White et al., *Science* 2014, DOI 10.1126/science.1251135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24812399/): inspected primary abstract reporting a two-stage gel-then-polymer vascular system that filled impact regions over 35 mm diameter and restored 62% absorbed impact energy. Thus even lost-volume regeneration is prior art, though not exact routed-strut restoration.
- [Abbasoglu et al., *Advanced Science* 2024, DOI 10.1002/advs.202407746](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/advs.202407746): inspected full primary PDF, especially pp. 3–5. A cut strut was manually pressed together and heated at 140 °C for 30 min; an approximately 0.6 Poisson ratio at 20% strain was regained. The source also reports shape reprogramming through heating, which is a direct compatibility warning for a heat-cured geometric memory.
- [Scheckenbach et al., *Angewandte Chemie* 2021, DOI 10.1002/anie.202012986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33230933/) and [Agarwal et al., *ACS Nano* 2018, DOI 10.1021/acsnano.7b08345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29451771/): inspected primary abstracts for DNA-origami self-healing after photo/enzymatic damage and designed polymerase gap filling. They delimit the prior DNA route and do not validate geometric route recovery after junction erasure.
