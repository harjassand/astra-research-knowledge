# A buried delamination as its own acoustic beacon and heat target

**15 October 2026 (Brisbane). Status: conditional construction; operation NOT ACHIEVED. Novelty: UNKNOWN.** This is an original proposed combination and an analytic channel test, not a laboratory result or a claim that time reversal, nonlinear guide stars, or ultrasonic repair are new. No experiment or numerical simulation was run for this report.

## Exact proposed operation and substrate

Repair one isolated, **buried matrix delamination with intact reinforcing fibres** in a removable 2–4 mm continuous-fibre thermoplastic *test coupon*. The candidate substrate is manufactured with a thin, resin-rich, weldable interleaf at the susceptible ply boundary, plus accessible surface or edge locations for a reversible piezoelectric array and a compressive fixture. The interleaf is a deliberate material input and may affect pristine strength, mass, and fatigue life; those penalties must be measured. The panel is not an arbitrary already-built composite and the scheme cannot repair severed fibres. A coarse conventional ultrasonic C-scan can restrict the region of interest, but its acquisition is charged to both arms of the comparison.

The **proposed acceptance test**, not a demonstrated specification, is that a delamination at the specified interleaf is rebonded without opening another defect or degrading the surrounding laminate, and matched post-repair Mode-I interlaminar fracture toughness reaches at least 80% of undamaged interleaf controls. The Mode-I test is destructive qualification on coupons, not a per-part certificate. A C-scan or CT indication that a gap has disappeared is insufficient: it can result from mere closure under the clamp. Measure retained compression/fatigue properties as secondary outcomes and count all failed panels. The consequential application is local repair of an enclosed bondline without opening the laminate, if a qualified nondestructive acceptance test is developed and total equipment/fabrication cost beats direct repair.

## Closest incumbents, credited up front

The wave-access primitive itself is established. Ulrich, Johnson and Guyer [focused processed nonlinear elastic emissions back onto a crack-like scatterer](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.104301) in 2007. Le Bas and colleagues [focused on an interior glass–aluminium contact used as a nonlinear scatterer](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3638926) in 2011. Smagin and colleagues [combined a pump, a probe coda, and time reversal to detect local damage](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultras.2020.106226) in 2020. Thus detecting a nonlinear contact, extracting a nonlinear component, and replaying it is **not** a novel mechanism by itself. A 2018 [contact-nonlinearity time-reversal analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsv.2017.12.019) also warns that narrowband retransmission does not reverse the *full* nonlinear scattering process; the present construction only reverses propagation of a selected emitted component through the approximately linear surroundings.

The repair primitive is also established. Hargou and colleagues [ultrasonically activated a mendable polymer inside carbon–epoxy laminates](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compositesb.2012.07.016) and measured Mode-I recovery, while a 2026 [delamination-repair patent application](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20260048561) reports a surface horn repairing embedded thermoplastic delaminations, with CT before/after and an example nominal weld setting of 1500 J. The patent's CT report is the applicant's evidence, not independent strength validation; its stated 1500 J weld setting is not whole-system electrical energy. Levy, Le Corre and Villegas [modelled dry-friction and bulk viscoelastic heating](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmatprotec.2014.02.009) and found that their particular welding apparatus had 13% global efficiency; that number is not transferable to this proposed array. These are the strongest baselines and physical constraints, not minor references.

## Conditional construction: same-mode sideband replay

1. Couple an \(N\)-channel, phase-calibrated **shear-sensitive** array to the panel. Apply a low-amplitude carrier near a selectable guided-wave mode \(\omega\), plus a slow normal-load or acoustic dither \(\Omega\). Sweep frequency, preload, and source position inside a pre-specified region; log every probe shot and couplant condition. A contacting delamination may generate a coherent sideband at \(\omega+\Omega\). Measure phase-resolved complex array response, subtract linear electronic/acoustic backgrounds using pump-off, reversed-pump and intact controls, and test repeatability. A bulk nonlinearity, transducer nonlinearity, another crack, or a clamp contact can otherwise masquerade as an internal beacon.
2. Select the sideband component attributed to the target interface and transmit its conjugate from the same calibrated array at **that sideband frequency and shear channel**. This is one experimentally acquired column of the Green operator; a full 3-D inverse reconstruction is not needed to form the matched focus. The phase conjugate of a harmonic at \(2\omega\) cannot simply be used for a therapeutic carrier at \(\omega\), because propagation and modal content differ. A near-carrier sideband reduces but does not remove this mismatch; driving the *same* \(\omega+\Omega\) mode is the clean construction.
3. Raise power in short pulses while a calibrated clamp holds intimate contact. The resin-rich interleaf is intended to act as a heat director and weldable feedstock. Alternate high-power pulses with low-power sideband and linear ultrasonic readout. Reacquire the beacon whenever clamp pressure, temperature, or couplant changes enough to alter propagation. Do not interpret beacon disappearance as proof of bonding: pressure closure, thermal softening, damage growth, or loss of acoustic coupling can each suppress it.
4. Cool under load, then inspect without preload and mechanically test blinded coupons. The operation ends in a *measured bond* only if the independent test passes.

The co-obtained internal information is limited but real: the measured phase vector of a current nonlinear source and how its response changes under probe/preload. It can serve as a local action guide without Cartesian coordinates. It does **not** by itself report absolute interface temperature, frictional dissipation, polymer diffusion, strength, or even which of several contacts emitted the strongest signal. Coarse C-scan targeting, single-defect preparation, and post-repair inspection remain explicit inputs.

## Original mathematical screen and decisive boundary

Let \(h_a(\omega)\in\mathbb C^N\) map array drive amplitudes \(u\) to the *repair-relevant local shear displacement* \(s=h_a^T u\) at a buried interface. Let \(h_b(\omega')\) be the array vector received from the nonlinear beacon's traction mode at \(\omega'=\omega+\Omega\). Linear reciprocity in the surrounding medium means a **same-frequency, same-traction** beacon can reveal the adjoint of its own input channel. With an input norm budget \(\|u\|^2\le P\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
 |s|^2\le P\|h_a\|^2,
 \qquad u_{\rm opt}=\sqrt P\,h_a^*/\|h_a\|.
\]

If instead \(u=\sqrt P\,h_b^*/\|h_b\|\) is made from the observed sideband, the fraction of this maximum local shear intensity is

\[
 \eta=\frac{|h_a^T h_b^*|^2}{\|h_a\|^2\|h_b\|^2}\in[0,1].
\]

This is a **necessary channel-alignment test** for the promised action. Strong, clean beacon SNR alone puts no lower bound on \(\eta\). For two channels, a normal-symmetric beacon vector \(h_b=(1,1)/\sqrt2\) and an antisymmetric shear-heating vector \(h_a=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\) give \(\eta=0\), despite both being perfectly observable/controllable. This is a mathematical counterexample to the claim that *any* internal nonlinear guide star automatically provides the control mode needed for repair. The intended rescue is same-mode sideband emission and matched shear reception/actuation; actual composites may still mix modes or change under load. Neither \(h_a\) nor \(\eta\) was measured here.

An independent thermal gate follows. Write \(K''\) for the loss component of the interleaf's *patch stiffness*, \(\delta\) for its shear displacement, \(F_t\) for total frictional shear force, and \(f=\omega/(2\pi)\). A schematic cycle-averaged input is \(P_{\rm int}\simeq\tfrac12\omega K''(T,p)|\delta|^2+f\!\oint F_t\,d\delta\), from interleaf viscoelastic loss and interfacial slip, while surrounding resin/fibres dissipate \(P_{\rm bulk}\simeq\int_V\tfrac12\omega\,\epsilon:C''\epsilon\,dV\). This is a bookkeeping split, not a fitted constitutive law, and overlapping losses must not be double-counted. Successful focusing of shear is insufficient unless integrated interface heat supplies the melt/softening enthalpy and bond dwell time **before** off-target bulk heating or fibre/matrix damage. At least \(E_{\rm int}\ge m_{\rm weld}[c_p(T_{\rm weld}-T_0)+L_{\rm eff}]\) is needed in an idealized thermal budget, plus conduction loss and hold/cooling; all parameters must be measured for the chosen interleaf. Conventional thermoplastic welding often uses 20–40 kHz vibration in the inspected [CF/PEEK study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612518304821). For an *illustrative, unmeasured* guided-mode phase speed of 3000 m/s, 40 kHz gives \(\lambda=75\) mm, much wider than a 5–15 mm defect. A sub-wavelength nonlinear beacon does not make the *actuation field* sub-wavelength. Heat could still localize through interleaf loss, but this is the unproven part; moving to higher frequency trades spatial selectivity against attenuation and available power.

There is also an **identifiability obstruction**: a reactive contact nonlinearity can create the same sideband as a lossy slipping contact while dissipating much less heat. Mathematically, the sideband measures a nonlinear scattering coefficient/derivative, whereas heat depends on loss modulus and hysteresis area. Distinct pairs \(K'(\delta,p),K''(T,p)\) can have the same low-power sideband but different \(P_{\rm int}\) and final bond strength. Hence the sideband cannot independently certify the thermal dose or completed weld. This rules out promoting a beacon-only closed loop as operational closure; the redesign uses the same beacon **for aiming**, an independently calibrated energy/temperature window for actuation, and independent outcome tests.

## Why optical and bubble guide stars do not fill the missing interface

Acoustically induced bubble emissions were already [time-reversed through an excised human skull to improve a therapy focus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3081822/) in a phantom experiment; the authors first used CT-derived focusing to create the bubble. [TRUME optical focusing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4673873/) used selective microbubble destruction and optical phase conjugation to focus light at a bubble in a scattering phantom. These are concrete demonstrations of conditional internal guide stars, and they also show the preparation cost of creating/selecting the beacon. For a carbon-fibre laminate, an optical playback path through the opaque load-bearing plies and a mobile microbubble at the *dry* bondline are not available assumptions. Seeding a bubble would alter the bond and count as new material preparation. The acoustic contact beacon is the physically closer route for this substrate. For tissue applications the bubble methods are strong incumbents, so moving the same idea there would not establish novelty.

## Full-cost matched comparison

The comparator is **C-scan localisation plus a calibrated conventional ultrasonic horn under the same fixture and on the same interleaf substrate**, with oven/resistance repair admitted if a horn cannot access the site. It gets the same pre/post inspection, acceptance rule, measured electricity, set-up time, and rejected coupons. The patent gives a concrete horn baseline with reported CT closure; the 2013 polymer study gives mechanical-strength evidence, although on a different laminate. No advantage follows from array pulse energy alone.

| Resource or risk | Beacon/time-reversal candidate | Strong conventional comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate and preparation | Resin-rich interleaf; array attachment/couplant; clamp; possible permanently installed channels; pristine mechanical penalty | Same interleaf and clamp for matched comparison; horn couplant/support or oven/blanket |
| Information acquired | Coarse C-scan, nonlinear sideband sweeps, phase calibration, rejection of equipment/clamp nonlinearity, remeasurement after each geometry/temperature change | C-scan and registration to horn; process-window calibration and post-repair inspection |
| Actuation and heat | \(N\) power amplifiers, beamforming, pulse energy, cooling, bulk attenuation and unwanted focus/sidelobes; true wall-plug energy unknown | Horn generator and pressure fixture, or whole-part oven/resistance energy; measured rather than nominal settings |
| Reliability and verification | Beacon failures, multiple sources, mode mismatch, delamination that becomes acoustically silent, heating/couplant drift, destructive strength coupons or qualified NDT | Registration error, horn footprint/access limits, off-target heat, incomplete fusion/overheating, identical qualification tests |
| Scaling | Repeated scans/recalibration per flaw; fixed array may amortize only over enough panels | Horn/oven fixture time and repositioning per flaw; can process many similar panels |

The 2026 horn patent example describes 1500 J nominal weld energy and seconds of sonication/hold with CT-reported gap disappearance, but supplies no matched input electricity or Mode-I comparison to this candidate. The 2014 13% global-efficiency number applies to its own modelled apparatus only. Thus **no full-cost or performance advantage is established**.

## Decisive experiment and status gate

Make a pre-registered randomized set of otherwise identical laminates with one blind, buried delamination at the interleaf, plus intact, open-gap and deliberately linear-scatterer controls. Hold the conventional C-scan, operator information, clamp, substrate, and outcome tests equal. First, verify that the sideband persists at the *actual welding preload* and originates at the intended defect rather than array/clamp contacts. In instrumented sacrificial coupons, measure interface shear displacement and mode content under beacon replay versus a directly calibrated optimal drive to estimate \(\eta\); separate calibration coupons are necessary because embedded sensors can themselves change an interface. Map interface and bulk temperature/heat-affected zones with calibrated witness sensors and sectioning, record wall-plug energy and elapsed time, then test healed Mode-I toughness blinded after unloading. Repeat across flaw sizes, depths, fibre directions, and couplant resets. Compare against C-scan-plus-horn and, where relevant, oven repair under the same acceptance rule.

The candidate should be **parked** if the beacon is absent under bond-forming preload, \(\eta\) is too small for an attainable interface heat dose, bulk damage occurs before weld formation, or bonded strength/full-system cost fails to beat the incumbent. If these pass, novelty still requires a separate detailed prior-art and patent review. Nothing here establishes a repair, a cost saving, or a new time-reversal principle.
