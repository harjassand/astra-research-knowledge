# S19 — Challenge-gated nitrate conversion with a direct outlet witness

**Status, 15 October 2026:** unbuilt experimental proposal plus an original, conditional finite-sample theorem. No laboratory work was done. It does **not** establish a new water-treatment capability, potability, novelty, or a full-cost advantage. The strongest useful idea is to put a sacrificial, actively challenged reactor beside a production reactor, choose a setting from actual feed-water response, and release a *finished local lot* only after direct assays of its own output. The labeled nitrate challenge diagnoses fate; it is not the success witness.

## Intended operation and embodiment

Target: a variable, nitrate-contaminated groundwater feed for a contained pilot; the immediate useful output is a batch with low **measured** nitrate, nitrite and ammonium under thresholds fixed before the run. Electrode-metal release in water and N₂O in captured reactor off-gas are separate required physical gates. The theorem below addresses the sampled water parcels; it does not certify the exhaust stream. This is a nitrogen-treatment claim at the defined sampling resolution, not a statement that water is safe to drink. A full drinking-water release needs its other required tests and legal approval.

Build **two identical sacrificial** gas-tight flow cells fed by a continuously stirred source reservoir, plus a separately sized production cell. Each has a PdCu cathode on a replaceable support, a separated anode, controlled current/voltage, measured flow and temperature, and sampling at feed, cathode out, final out and both chamber hold-ups. The sacrificial pair receives brief, known, low-amplitude pulses of Na¹⁵NO₃ and randomized electrical settings, including power-off sham periods with the same wetted materials and residence-time schedule; the production cell remains isolated during this work. Source-water nitrate is simultaneously measured; the label is never used as a stand-in for bulk removal. Record ¹⁵NO₃⁻, ¹⁵NO₂⁻, ¹⁵NH₄⁺, ²⁹N₂, ³⁰N₂ and labeled N₂O where feasible, with mass recovery across liquid, gas, both chambers and retained solids. The isotope pulse is sacrificial and its water is not released. An optional UV/photo-oxidation polish is a separately metered actuator, included only if the side-product panel warrants it.

Use these data to choose one controller setting or a bounded schedule; freeze it before a *fresh*, blinded split-feed active/sham comparison in the two identical sacrificial cells. Flush/replace reactive hold-up between assignments. The paired split receives the same current source-water batch, with randomized choice of which physical branch is powered. Assay actual unlabeled outlet nitrate, nitrite and ammonium by calibrated ion chromatography/colorimetry and N₂O, metals, pH and other prespecified side products by suitable validated methods. If the active-minus-sham improvement clears the bound below, run a separate contained production lot, divided into equal-volume numbered parcels. Draw random parcels for final direct assays. Failed lots are retained or reprocessed, and each retest spends fresh error probability. Selection, pair comparison and lot release must use separate water and assay data to avoid choosing a setting because of a lucky noise realization and then certifying it with the same noise. The paired-cell causal result does not itself transfer across scale; only the final production-lot assays address production output.

This architecture exploits a physical affordance: electricity can be switched without changing source-water supply, and an isotope-labeled nitrate pulse follows a chemically similar substrate into N products, while the production stream can be held until direct measurement. A deliberately wrong setting may create nitrite or ammonium even when nitrate falls; that is an observable failure, not an inferred success. The strongest claim is *locally tested operation for that source matrix and lot*. No reactor model, rate law or complete nitrogen reaction network is required for the statistical certificate, but chemical assay coverage and honest parcel sampling are required.

## One exact conditional theorem (original derivation)

Fix any policy chosen from arbitrarily adaptive **training** challenges. For each of \(n\) later, fresh split-feed blocks \(i\), define potential outcomes \(Y_{ib}(a)\in[0,B]\), where \(b\in\{L,R\}\), \(a=1\) means powered treatment and \(a=0\) means power-off sham, and \(Y\) is a prespecified harmful-N score in the outlet (for example a fixed, nonnegative weighted sum of NO₃-N, NO₂-N and NH₄-N, clipped at \(B\)). The weights and \(B\) are fixed before the holdout; the theorem's causal effect concerns this bounded score, while uncapped concentrations remain in the release gate. Within block \(i\), randomly assign one branch to \(a=1\), the other to \(a=0\), using a fair independent coin. Potential outcomes may depend on all earlier history and on feed drift but are fixed before this block's coin; assume no current-block interference and enough washout to make these potential outcomes meaningful. Let \(D_i=Y_{i,\mathrm{sham}}(0)-Y_{i,\mathrm{active}}(1)\), and let

\[
\Delta_n={1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^n {Y_{iL}(0)-Y_{iL}(1)+Y_{iR}(0)-Y_{iR}(1)\over2}.
\]

Suppose each measured \(Y\) has a deterministic error at most \(\eta\) on a separately validated calibration event \(C\), with \(\Pr(C^c)\le\gamma\). The observed paired mean is \(\bar D_{\rm obs}\). Independently, take a **finished** lot of \(N\) equal-volume parcels, of which fraction \(q\) violates at least one prespecified outlet criterion. Draw \(m\) indices independently and uniformly with replacement; an aliquot from the chosen parcel is assayed. Conditional on the full earlier record and on choosing any truly failing parcel, the probability that the assay flags it is at least \(s>0\). This sensitivity premise includes matrix spikes, handling, instrument drift and any repeated sampling. Accept the policy and lot only if

\[
\bar D_{\rm obs}-2\eta-B\sqrt{2\log(1/\alpha_C)/n}>\delta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{all }m\text{ lot assays flag no failure},
\]

where \(\delta\ge0\) is the minimum worthwhile improvement, \(0<\alpha_C,\alpha_Q<1\), and \(0<sq_*<1\). If \(m\ge\log(\alpha_Q)/\log(1-sq_*)\), then the probability of accepting while either \(\Delta_n\le\delta\) **or** \(q\ge q_*\) is at most \(\alpha_C+\alpha_Q+\gamma\), for every physical response law satisfying these experimental premises. The inequality is about the randomized blocks' average causal effect and this lot's nonconforming parcel fraction; it says nothing about future feed or unassayed chemicals.

**Proof.** Given all history before coin \(i\), fair assignment makes \(E(D_i)=\Delta_i\), its indicated term in \(\Delta_n\), and \(D_i\in[-B,B]\). The conditional Hoeffding exponential bound, iterated over the \(n\) coins, gives \(\Pr(\bar D_{\rm true}-\Delta_n\ge t)\le\exp[-nt^2/(2B^2)]\). On \(C\), paired measurement moves each \(D_i\) by at most \(2\eta\), giving the first error probability \(\alpha_C\). If \(q\ge q_*\), each uniform parcel draw has conditional probability at least \(sq_*\) of being flagged, even though assay results may be adaptive; iterated conditioning gives probability of no flag at most \((1-sq_*)^m\le\alpha_Q\). Add \(\Pr(C^c)\le\gamma\) and union bound. The training policy can be arbitrary because both audits are fresh after selection. QED.

At \(q_*=0.05,\ s=0.99,\ \alpha_Q=0.025\), this requires **73** final assays if all pass; at \(q_*=0.01\), **371**, and at \(q_*=0.001\), **3,725**. For \(n=64,\ \alpha_C=0.025\), the causal margin is \(0.340B\) before assay error. These are calculated design consequences, not experimental observations; design_numbers.py reproduces them. Sampling without replacement or using quantitative concentrations can improve efficiency, but needs a separately proved rule. Repeated tuning/lot attempts need preassigned \(\alpha\)-spending (e.g. summable \(\alpha_r\)); reusing a failed lot and calling it a fresh independent success is invalid. Calibration sensitivity \(s\) is itself an acquired empirical premise, not a theorem output.

## Why isotope transit cannot certify useful action

An exact countermodel is a reactor with a treated route carrying fraction \(p\) of source water and an untreated bypass carrying \(1-p\). If a labeled pulse enters only the treated route, its nitrate-to-N₂ conversion can be 100% while source-water nitrate remaining at the mixed outlet is \((1-p)C_0\). At \(p=0.1\), the label reports complete conversion although 90% of original nitrate remains. This is a construction, not a measured failure. Even with upstream injection, finite pulse timing, unmixed bypasses, isotope effects and side-product formation defeat an unvalidated equality between tracer fate and useful output. The local theorem does not use label conversion. It uses the actual finished lot's endogenous harmful-N assays. Isotope accounting is retained for mechanism and leak diagnostics, and failure to close its inventory blocks any assertion of N₂ yield.

The same logic applies to a rising fluorescence signal, a reactor current, or disappearing nitrate alone: each can accompany unwanted ammonia, nitrite, N₂O, sorption, anode migration or incomplete sampling. Direct N₂ evidence requires ²⁹/³⁰N₂ against blanks *and* a checked isotope inventory; label recovery in one product is not full bulk-N conservation.

## Acquisition, preparation, energy and reliability ledger

* **Information:** each feed matrix requires nitrate, nitrite, ammonium, pH, major competing ions (especially carbonate/Ca/Mg), chloride, conductivity and organic/inorganic N background. Calibration needs matrix-spike recovery at every threshold and blinded duplicate/blank standards. The randomization list and decision thresholds are fixed before holdout data. A missing route or analyte is an uncovered failure mode, not a zero.
* **Preparation and control:** two matched sacrificial catalyst cells plus a production cell, membranes, pumps, sealed gas collection, isotope pulse stock of known enrichment, leak test, controller and valve timing, measured dwell/flush, possible photo-polish, effluent hold tank, and numbered equal-volume lot parcels. Pulse amplitude must be tested for nonperturbation; if it changes kinetics, it can still be a stress test but cannot be extrapolated to baseline operation.
* **Material/energy:** charge \(E_{\rm cell}=\int VI\,dt\), pump and cooling/heating energy, UV energy if used, catalyst/electrode and membrane manufacture/replacement, Pd/Cu leaching, isotope reagent and its disposal, gas analysis, all paired/final assays, failed-lot rework, operator time, residence time and unprocessed water. Report kWh/m³ and cost/m³ on *accepted output*, with N₂O emissions and concentrated residuals. The twin challenge cell can erase any apparent benefit if it consumes too much resource.
* **Reliability:** matrix-specific assay sensitivity and sample preservation, branch crossover leakage, anode transfer, gas leaks, hydrodynamic short circuits, catalyst drift, parcel mixing resolution and serially correlated instrument error are testable gates. The theorem fails if its sensitivity/representative-frame premise fails. Off-gas N₂O needs a separately validated, complete capture/measurement gate; water aliquots cannot certify it. The confidence bound is deliberately expensive for rare failures.
* **Scaling:** a pilot passing a 100 L source-water lot cannot certify a municipal stream under later source chemistry. Continuous operation needs rolling contained lots or a new drift model and sampling design. The cost of retaining water while assays finish must be charged. Equal-volume parcel testing supports a vessel-mean property only if each vessel is mixed and sampled representatively; subparcel hot spots require a finer sampling frame.

## Nearest prior art, strongest comparator and discriminating experiment

The closest physical incumbent is the continuous/batch PEM electrolysis work of **Ampurdanés, Bunea and Urakawa (2021)**. It already uses PdCu/SnO₂, potential sweeps, nitrate/nitrite/ammonium outlet chromatography and combined photo-oxidation. Its continuous mode reached reported ~97% nitrate conversion and ~96% N₂ selectivity under synthetic feed, while the combined batch still reported about 2.4 ppm nitrate, 0.6 ppm nitrite and 0.5 ppm ammonium after eight hours. Thus neither the reactor nor adding outlet assays is a novelty claim. Ordered PdCu catalysts have also been reported at 91% N₂ selectivity and 480 h stability. A 2026 primary study found complex-groundwater carbonate/cation pairing suppresses a Cu reactor's nitrate reduction, making changing-matrix challenges scientifically motivated. ¹⁵N product tracing in groundwater is decades old. Novelty of the *assembled* challenge-gated protocol is **UNKNOWN**.

The strongest **equally informed** comparator is a single optimized PdCu production reactor that receives the same source-water assays, the same number of calibration samples and the same allowed policy choices, with routine direct outlet QA; for practical deployment, also compare ion exchange and reverse osmosis with brine, energy and full life-cycle cost. The proposed system's possible advantage is that a separate challenged cell can select a changed setting before risking a production lot; that benefit must exceed its extra cell, isotope, control and testing costs. The finite-sample theorem is standard conditional-randomization/assay logic and would be available to the comparator. It provides honest local certification but no exclusive capability.

**Decisive test.** In a blind, preregistered pilot, use real groundwater plus a held-out sequence of source-matrix shifts (Ca/Mg/HCO₃, chloride, nitrate concentration) and induced catalyst aging, with identical candidate settings and total assay/energy budgets. Randomize periods between (A) challenge-cell selection plus the above holdout and lot gate and (B) an equally informed optimized single-cell controller with direct QA; include ion-exchange/RO economics on accepted water. Primary endpoint: accepted liters meeting every nitrogen/side-product/metal threshold per complete resource cost, with false-release fraction independently measured by exhaustive or denser audit. Secondary endpoints: N₂/N₂O inventory closure, off-spec liters detained, recovery time after shifts and catalyst life. A failure to reduce cost or off-spec volume at matched assurance kills the proposed operational advantage. No part of this experiment was run.

It is the nearest process comparator; synthetic 100 ppm feed and 80 °C do not prove this proposal works on real groundwater.
2. Full publisher article fetch was blocked, so no uninspected details are assumed.
3. [Arrazolo et al., *Environmental Science & Technology* 2026, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.6c04945](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6c04945): publisher abstract and indexed method/results inspected; complex Ca/Mg/HCO₃ matrix, 100 h reactor experiment, performance suppression attributed to cation–carbonate pairing. Attribution is the authors' interpretation, not a proven universal mechanism here.
4. [Bengtsson & Annadotter, *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 1989, DOI 10.1128/aem.55.11.2861-2870.1989](https://doi.org/10.1128/aem.55.11.2861-2870.1989): publisher abstract inspected; ¹⁵NO₃ groundwater microcosms measured labeled N₂, N₂O, NH₄ and assimilated N by GC-MS. This establishes tracer-method prior art, not electrochemical reactor performance.

[US EPA Method 300.0](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=9400419N.TXT) and [Method 350.1](https://www.epa.gov/esam/epa-method-3501-determination-ammonia-nitrogen-semi-automated-colorimetry): official method text/pages inspected for nitrate/nitrite ion chromatography, ammonium colorimetry, matrix interferences and QA. [Cerrón-Calle et al., *Applied Catalysis B* 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.apcatb.2025.125984](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2025.125984): publisher abstract inspected; primary techno-economic benchmark versus RO/ion exchange, strong cost sensitivity to electrode and feed assumptions. This is a modeled study, not demonstrated cost superiority.

[Green et al., *Water Resources Research* 2010, DOI 10.1029/2009WR008903](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009WR008903): primary paper abstract and indexed text inspected; heterogeneous mixing changed apparent reaction rates and isotope fractionation estimates, supporting the caution against treating a tracer pulse as bulk reaction truth. It is an aquifer study, not evidence of a fault in this proposed reactor.
