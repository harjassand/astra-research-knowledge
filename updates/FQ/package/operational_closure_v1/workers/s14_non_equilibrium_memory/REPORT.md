# Poison-written local regeneration in a driven Pt electrode array

15 October 2026 (Brisbane). **Status: candidate operation, not a demonstrated invention.** The bare phenomenon—CO-poisoning-induced autonomous oscillation of formic-acid oxidation on Pt under resistive or galvanostatic control—is prior art. The narrower proposed operation is *spatially selective regeneration*: electrically and ionically partition a Pt microelectrode array so that each patch's own adsorbed-CO history controls its cleaning excursion while its neighbors continue producing current. It could matter only if heterogeneity makes common pulses wasteful, and only if its full power, segmentation, control and lifetime costs beat branch-resolved electronics and a poison-resistant catalyst. That has not been shown. The simple resistor implementation fails a useful energy bound below; an individually current-fed version is the substantive redesign left for experiment.

## Exact task and physical memory

The scientific access problem is to keep a flow-fed formic-acid anode productive when local Pt patches acquire different, mostly unobserved adsorbate histories. The desired output is net electrical energy per feed and Pt area over an eight-hour run, with measured CO2 and catalyst retention, not merely current oscillations. The available inputs are a fabricated array, formic-acid/acid electrolyte, a common power/load interface, and each branch's electrical terminals; CO coverage is *not* initially supplied. The array must acquire its own control signal from the reaction. A three-electrode diagnostic can be used during calibration and adjudication, but its reference and spectroscopy are not free components of the deployed controller.

Choose lithographic Pt islands in parallel flow channels, with a counter electrode near each island and individually wired branches. Formic acid has a productive direct pathway and a CO-forming pathway on Pt. Adsorbed CO blocks active sites and persists across moments of the applied drive: coverage \(\theta_j\) is the local memory. A heavily blocked island passes less productive current. Under a local series impedance or an imposed branch current, its interfacial potential rises. At sufficiently positive potential CO oxidizes/desorbs; current recovers and the potential returns to a productive range. **The marking reaction changes the catalyst's rate and the circuit's current; it consumes sites, fuel and charge.** There is no spectator reporter with cost-free, one-way information flow. Other adsorbates, fuel starvation and solution resistance also change current, so the mark is not specific without calibration.

A minimal coupled model for island \(j\) is

\[
 C_j\dot E_j=I_{\mathrm{feed},j}(E_j,u_j)-I_{\mathrm{prod},j}(E_j,\theta_j,c_j)-I_{\mathrm{strip},j}(E_j,\theta_j),\qquad
 \dot\theta_j=k_{\mathrm{form},j}(E_j,c_j)(1-\theta_j)^q-k_{\mathrm{strip},j}(E_j)\theta_j.
\]

Here \(C_j\) includes double-layer and lead capacitance; \(I_{\rm strip}\) is the Faradaic charge of CO removal and must be separated from direct-path current. It may still convert stored fuel carbon to CO2 and contribute electrical output, but at a different interfacial potential after blocking productive sites. For a resistive branch \(I_{\rm feed}=(V_b-E_j)/R_j\), so the written \(\theta_j\) changes the load line. The control-relevant times are \(t_{\rm poison}\), \(t_{\rm strip}\), \(R_j C_j\), flow residence and inter-island ionic coupling. The equations are a **proposal**, not a fit to the cited experiments. In real Pt formic-acid oxidation, water/OH and formate coverages also enter; the one-variable model must be rejected if it cannot predict the measured phase traces and stripping charge.

## A quantitative obstruction for the easiest implementation

Let \(I_p>I_c\) be branch currents just before poisoning and just before cleaning at the same bus voltage, and let the necessary interfacial-potential rise be \(\Delta E=E_c-E_p>0\). The resistor load law gives exactly

\[
R(I_p-I_c)=\Delta E,\qquad
\frac{P_{R,p}}{I_p}=R I_p=\frac{\Delta E}{1-I_c/I_p}\geq\Delta E.
\]

Thus the resistor burns at least \(\Delta E\) joules per productive coulomb, even with nearly zero poisoned current; partial poisoning makes the cost larger. This is a circuit identity, not an empirical no-go for other controllers. For an *illustrative design target* \(E_p=0.55\) and \(E_c=0.75\) V vs RHE, \(I_p=0.20\) and \(I_c=0.02\) microampere, \(R\) must be at least \(1.11\) megohms and the productive-state drop is at least \(0.222\) V. The dissipated energy is at least \(0.222\) J/C, or \(42.9\) kJ per mole of formic acid if two electrons per molecule pass through that branch. The example currents and required threshold gap were **not measured** for a particular chip; the 0.2-V gap is motivated only by the Pt(111) productive/CO-stripping windows below. Chip geometry and electrolyte must establish the actual windows. A shallow current response to \(\theta\) only worsens the bound.

This also explains why a claimed free chemical reporter would be misleading: the useful signal is a finite loss of reaction current, and converting it to a 0.2-V cleaning swing with a passive resistor charges the productive operation continuously. The old two-electrode microchip experiment used a 10-Mohm branch resistor and observed single-electrode oscillations at about 0.53 Hz, but on its stated no-Bi condition the oscillations lasted roughly 2–5 minutes before the electrode fell into a low-current poisoned state. Those circuit voltages are not RHE interfacial voltages and cannot be substituted into the illustrative bound without a measured local potential.

## Redesign: local current-fed cleaning, not a hidden sensor

Replace each continuously dissipative resistor with an individually regulated branch-current feed and a bounded compliance voltage. With \(I_{\rm feed,j}=I_{0j}\), poisoning drives \(E_j\) upward by charging \(C_j\); CO oxidation returns the branch to its lower potential. In a fuel cell this stage must be an efficient, regulated load/power converter; in a driven half-cell it is a current source. Either way, its energy balance is measured at the external bus. The *chemical memory remains* \(\theta_j\), not an electronic estimate of it. The electronic stage is an actuator whose power conversion loss, leakage, compliance headroom and fabrication area must be charged. Total two-terminal voltage does not by itself bound the Pt interfacial potential when cathode polarization and solution resistance drift; a local reference or a validated conservative estimator is needed for a dissolution limit, and its cost belongs to the design. The circuit cannot manufacture information about whether a low current means CO, starvation, OH blocking or a dry channel. A local high-potential probe/strip excursion can make that discrimination after the fact via its measured stripping charge, but the probe consumes charge and changes the surface. A subsequent local refractory interval can be stored in a finite capacitor/latch to prevent repeated fruitless excursions. This added two-timescale memory is a proposed engineering extension, with branch electronics explicitly counted; its novelty remains **UNKNOWN**.

The physical prediction worth testing is limited: when poisoning hazard varies substantially across islands, local regeneration should avoid oxidizing still-clean neighbors, whereas a common waveform exposes every island. This is not an inherent advantage over a branch-resolved controller. If a local event has cleaning energy \(e_i\) and event rate \(\lambda_i\), ideal selective actuation costs \(\sum_i\lambda_i e_i\) per unit time. A common pulse at rate \(f\) costs roughly \(f\sum_i e_i\), but an equally informed branch-resolved electronic controller approaches the same \(\sum_i\lambda_i e_i\), subject to its own sensing/drive cost. The proposed material-circuit coupling earns a capability claim only through measured savings in apparatus, latency, energy, reliability or scale after those costs are included. It does not have an information-theoretic advantage over an equally informed controller.

There is also an exact ambiguity: construct two islands with the same terminal \(I(t),E(t)\) until a trigger time, one blocked by CO and one temporarily starved of HCOOH (or altered by OH/flow). Any controller whose only input is that terminal history must take the same action in both worlds. A cleaning excursion can rescue the first and needlessly oxidize the second. Coupled chemistry may distinguish them *after* a perturbation, but there is no zero-cost pre-trigger inference. This counterexample is independent of a particular kinetic fit.

## Real evidence inspected and what it does (and does not) support

1. [Kiss, Munjal & Martin, *Electrochimica Acta* 55 (2009), 395–403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2772206/), DOI [10.1016/j.electacta.2009.02.094](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.electacta.2009.02.094). **Full article and methods inspected.** The authors fabricated Pt dual microelectrodes under a PDMS flow channel, used formic acid/sulfuric acid and external resistors, and observed single-electrode oscillations with 10 MOhm. They reported roughly 0.439 and 0.581 Hz for isolated front/rear electrodes under one set of conditions and synchronized 0.485 Hz when jointly operated with distant reference/counter electrode. Bringing reference/counter electrodes close broke synchrony, implicating shared electrical coupling rather than downstream mass transport. In the no-Bi single-electrode demonstration, oscillations died into poisoning after about 2–5 minutes; the paper does not demonstrate eight-hour autonomous throughput. Their Bi pretreatment and acid/flow protocols are preparation costs, and the Bi may itself change poisoning chemistry. This paper strongly blocks novelty of the simple oscillator and provides an experimentally grounded isolation lever.

2. [Chen, Granda-Marulanda, McCrum & Koper, *Nature Communications* 13 (2022), article 38](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-27793-5), DOI [10.1038/s41467-021-27793-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-27793-5). **Full article and methods inspected.** On Pt(111) in 0.1 M perchloric acid with 50 mM HCOOH, oxidation began around 0.35 V vs RHE, and adsorbed CO was stripped above about 0.70 V; the stripping peak was near 0.72 V. Current fell about fourfold across 12 cycles when the potential was capped at 0.6 V. More damaging to a Pt-rescue claim, an epitaxial Pd monolayer on Pt(111) gave an approximately 11.0 mA/cm2 peak at 0.38 V versus a 2.2 mA/cm2 Pt(111) peak, with little low-potential hysteresis. The authors attribute this to formate coverage blocking the ensemble needed for CO production. These are well-defined single-crystal comparisons, not a matched microchip or fuel-cell device budget, but a serious competing substrate. The catalyst choice may remove the very memory the proposal exploits while improving the useful reaction.

3. [Nogueira, Lopes, Markovic & Varela, *Electrochemistry Communications* 121 (2020), 106853](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1388248120302046), DOI [10.1016/j.elecom.2020.106853](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.elecom.2020.106853). **Publisher abstract/highlights and available manuscript snippets inspected; complete methods were not recovered here.** The authors used in situ Pt-dissolution measurement and report higher efficiency under autonomous oscillatory formic-acid/methanol electro-oxidation, with no detectable dissolution during the observed oscillatory state; the reported excursion was around 0.60–0.85 V vs RHE and they warn of dissolution at higher potentials. This supports physical plausibility of a safe window but not a universal zero-dissolution guarantee, branch-selective superiority or long-run reliability.

4. [Hülsey et al., *Molecular Catalysis* 509 (2021), 111482](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468823121000997), DOI [10.1016/j.mcat.2021.111482](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcat.2021.111482). **Publisher abstract and article preview inspected.** Imposed oscillating potentials from open circuit to about 0.8 V vs NHE were used to control CO coverage and infer coverage-dependent turnover across Pt, Pd and Rh catalysts at 0.1–10 Hz. This establishes deliberate pulsing as a close comparator and reinforces that the pulse itself perturbs coverage. Its potential scale and electrolyte must not be silently interchanged with the RHE values above.

The literature search was targeted, not exhaustive. No reported rate in this report is inferred from an article's oscillation frequency. No original wet-lab observation or device simulation was performed. The resistor inequality and terminal-history ambiguity are my derivations; the reported experiments and numerical values above belong to the cited authors.

## Matched comparator and complete-resource contract

The strongest **control** comparator uses the same segmented Pt islands, flow, electrolyte, wiring and per-island power stages, measures each branch's current/potential, and applies optimized local pulses or galvanostatic cycles using the same calibration budget. It may use operando stripping-charge calibration and can reproduce any successful potential history generated by the material coupling. Compare also (i) common optimized pulses on the same Pt array, (ii) no dynamic control, and (iii) Pd-monolayer/Pt or the best measured poison-resistant electrode at equal catalyst area, catalyst mass, preparation and life. An advantageous Pt scheme must beat the best of these under the declared objective; beating common pulses alone is too weak.

For each arm report, per useful mole and per net Wh: acquired calibration time/charge; Pt/Pd/Bi inventory and deposition yield; channel/electrode/counter/reference area; branch wiring and converter fabrication; feed pumping and supporting-electrolyte losses; resistor or power-stage dissipation; capacitive, CO-stripping and other nonproductive charge; counter-electrode reaction; controller standby energy; failed branches/replacements; product selectivity and CO2 balance; Pt dissolution and morphology; and uncertainty across chips. A monitored laboratory reference/SEIRAS apparatus is an adjudicator and belongs in acquisition cost if its information is used to tune the deployed scheme. A shared fuel source or imposed current is an energy input, not a free driver.

Before fabrication, the minimum kinetic contract is a measured branch-resolved \(E,\theta,I\) map with a region where \(\partial I_{\rm prod}/\partial\theta<0\), a reachable strip region with \(k_{\rm strip}\) fast enough to outrun poisoning, and an excursion below the measured dissolution limit. The electrical contract requires enough compliance for every branch, \(R C\) or current-source response faster than the relevant chemistry, and measured isolation despite common electrolyte resistance. Reliability requires the distribution of time-to-permanent-poisoning and time-to-unrecoverable-Pt loss across at least hundreds of cycles and multiple chips; survival of one oscillator for minutes is insufficient. A predeclared engineering target is CO-challenge recovery within 10 seconds in at least 99% of trials, at least 95% of branches still productive at eight hours, and no more than one unnecessary cleaning excursion per branch-hour during CO-free starvation. These numbers are proposed acceptance thresholds, not measured properties. Scaling is **O(N)** branch wires/actuators for \(N\) independently controlled islands, plus counter-electrode and flow capacity, until an architecture actually reduces these terms. The hard test target is an eight-hour paired run with at least a 20% increase in net energy per consumed formic acid and no worse Pt loss than the strongest comparator, across predeclared spatially heterogeneous feeds. The 20% threshold is likewise an engineering decision, not a literature result.

## Decisive experiment and falsifiers

Fabricate randomized, blind paired chips with identical Pt island geometry and local counter/reference placement. Introduce independent, time-varying HCOOH concentration profiles to create heterogeneous local poisoning histories; include CO-free starvation intervals designed to mimic low terminal current. Pre-calibrate \(E\)-dependent Pt(111)-like stripping windows on the actual chip, then freeze all policies. Arms: resistive local oscillation, current-fed local oscillation, the optimized branch-resolved electronic controller, optimized common pulses, static Pt, and Pd-monolayer/Pt where fabrication succeeds. Measure individual \(E_j(t), I_j(t)\), operando CO coverage on representative islands (SEIRAS or equivalent), high-potential stripping charge, effluent CO2, fuel use, net load power, converter/resistor heat, pump power and ICP-MS Pt dissolution. The spectroscopy is for validation; do not feed it into only one policy unless all policies receive it at the same cost. Follow at least eight hours, then test post-run activity and morphology.

The mechanistic discriminator is whether a patch's CO coverage rises *before* its terminal-current fall and local excursion, then falls with measured CO-stripping charge while clean neighbors remain in their productive window. A starvation-only low-current trace should expose false triggering. The capability discriminator is paired net output and lifetime versus the best equally informed comparator after full costs. Failure of the CO temporal ordering, inability to isolate branches, high false-trigger rate, a persistent-voltage/energy penalty, or a Pd/control arm matching or exceeding the proposed arm closes the claimed advantage. A positive result would establish a device-level scoped advantage under that feed and lifetime, not a new general law of nonequilibrium matter or priority.

**Current disposition:** the memory mechanism is real and established; the proposed spatially selective operation is physically testable but unbuilt. The passive resistor version has an unavoidable voltage cost and the cited microchip did not sustain long operation. Current-fed selective regeneration remains an engineering hypothesis with an equally informed controller and a poison-resistant material as decisive rivals. Novelty, device advantage and eight-hour reliability are **UNKNOWN**.
