# Storage intervention: constant-throughput local current transfer in a segmented graphite cell

**Status (15 October 2026): conditional design and falsifiable test; no laboratory result; novelty not established.** The generic architecture is substantially anticipated. The narrower useful hypothesis is that deliberately transferring charge current from a locally plating-prone region to a healthier region, while keeping total charge current fixed, can delay plating in a large pouch cell. The transfer is valuable only if the cell has a controllable spatial differential mode and the healthier region has spare anode *and cathode* margin. A control experiment must measure those facts; cell-level voltage or a fitted impedance alone cannot establish them.

## Physical problem and chosen operation

During fast charging of graphite cells, local intercalation kinetics, electrolyte transport, temperature, state of lithiation, and current-collector geometry differ across the electrode. Lithium metal may deposit in one location before the aggregate voltage, current, or temperature signals become distinctive. Direct imaging has found plating preferentially on fast-lithiating particles and redistribution of plated lithium during rest [Chen et al., 2021]. In a segmented LFP/graphite pouch cell, measured current across ten positive-electrode segments was nonuniform and changed pattern during discharge [Zhang et al., 2013]. Neither measurement licenses a particular fast-charge controller, but both defeat the assumption that one terminal current describes every local electrochemical load.

Build a *laboratory* two-to-four-zone graphite/NMC pouch cell with electronically isolated anode current-collector/coating islands, independent bidirectional current channels, individual current shunts, and spatially registered reference-electrode contacts. Keep the separator and cathode chemistry otherwise matched to an ordinary pouch. An isolated anode coating is essential: merely cutting the copper while leaving a continuous conductive coating could bridge the islands. The electronics deliver a total charge current (I=\sum_i I_i), but can change the vector (I_i). While charging, apply a short compensated transfer, (I_1\mapsto I_1-\delta), (I_2\mapsto I_2+\delta). This preserves immediate total charge delivery. Record each region's potential relative to its local reference, current, and temperature before and after the transfer. If the vulnerable region's calibrated plating margin rises and the receiving region remains within its own anode/cathode limits, retain the new split. If not, lower total current. The useful operation is **current transfer conditional on demonstrated spatial control authority**, not a renamed impedance classifier.

The primary physical target is the local Li deposition reaction overpotential at the graphite/electrolyte interface, not the pack voltage. Xiong et al. experimentally separated plating reaction overpotential from graphite surface equilibrium potential and reported plating when the former became negative; they explicitly cautioned that the common graphite-vs-Li potential criterion is incomplete [Xiong et al., 2023]. An embedded reference reads an electrode/electrolyte potential at its finite location, not the minimum interfacial overpotential over all pores and particles. Thus a controller can use a *calibrated conservative lower bound* on plating margin, not pretend that a reference-electrode number directly certifies every microscopic site. A cathode local-potential and temperature bound must be checked too: sending current to the apparently healthy anode region can overdelithiate or heat the corresponding positive electrode.

## Original conditional mechanism

The following is a local mathematical model and derivation, **not** a measured battery law. At a fixed cell state and over a short control interval, let (m_i>0) denote a conservative lower bound on the no-plating margin in zone (i), after accounting for sensor error and unresolved through-plane polarization. Linearize its dependence on the zone current vector:

\[
m(I+u)\simeq m(I)-A u,\qquad A_{ij}:=-\frac{\partial m_i}{\partial I_j}.
\]

The matrix (A) is a physical response matrix, not assumed diagonal or time invariant. Electrolyte coupling, common cathode potential, in-plane conduction, heat and adjacent particle states can create large off-diagonal terms. A brief current transfer probes a column difference while still delivering useful charge. For two zones, take (u=(-\delta,+\delta)), so (u_1+u_2=0). Then

\[
\Delta m_1\simeq \delta(A_{11}-A_{12}),\qquad
\Delta m_2\simeq-\delta(A_{22}-A_{21}).
\]

If zone 1 needs a margin increase (d_1>0), zone 2 has spare margin (s_2>0), and both differential coefficients are positive, a constant-throughput correction exists in this linear interval precisely when

\[
\frac{d_1}{A_{11}-A_{12}}\leq\delta\leq
\frac{s_2}{A_{22}-A_{21}},
\]

also subject to current limits, a cathode margin, temperature, and the finite validity interval of the linearization. If the left endpoint exceeds the right one, no current transfer satisfies both local margins; reduce total current or stop. If (A_{11}=A_{12}), moving current from zone 1 to 2 has no first-order protective effect on zone 1. This exposes a decisive **controllability** gate, distinct from whether a voltage trace contains a detectable feature. With (n) zones, the same gate is whether the measured/validated response (A) has an effective component on the zero-sum subspace \(\{u: \mathbf 1^Tu=0\}\) large enough, at actual noise and safety margins, to improve a constrained charge objective.

An illustrative *dimensionless calculation* makes the conditional gain explicit. Suppose an invented two-zone margin law is (m=\mathbf 1-AI) with

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.1\\0.1&0.5\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_i\geq0.
\]

An equal-current bus (I_1=I_2=t) must use (t\leq1/1.1=0.9091), total current at most (1.8182). Independent currents can meet both active constraints at (I_1=0.8163, I_2=1.8367), total (2.6531), a 45.9% increase *in this fabricated model only*. If instead (A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}), every margin depends only on total current and segmentation gives **zero** charging advantage. Real values, uncertainty, drift, and boundary reactions decide which regime a cell occupies; the numerical example is not a performance prediction.

There is also a limited information gain. One aggregate terminal voltage and current cannot uniquely report (n) local currents or local interfacial states: distinct spatial current/temperature/plating configurations can produce the same scalar sum. Segmented shunts directly report (I_i), and distributed reference contacts report spatially sampled electrode potentials. Compensated transfers reveal whether a local response is causally steerable at unchanged total current. They do *not* uniquely identify all pore-scale lithium metal, SEI, transport, or temperature states. The response can drift as SOC and temperature change. Crucially, turning a zone's external branch off may not extinguish internal ionic currents or pre-existing plated lithium; tomography has measured such currents during open-circuit rest after fast charge [ACS Nano study, 2023]. The operation aims to prevent new plating during charge, not guarantee that existing metal disappears or thermal runaway is impossible.

## Closest work and the same-information comparator

This is a crowded architecture. Zhang et al. (2013) built a ten-segment pouch with a continuous graphite negative electrode, segmented LFP positive electrode, individual 2 mΩ shunts, one bus, and one-second local current readings. Their device measured nonuniform current but did not independently control charging zones. The distinction matters: it gives experimental precedent for local acquisition, not a demonstration of the proposed intervention. US9343781B2 explicitly describes local collector pads, individual transistors, current/temperature sensors, and coordinated reduction of problematic-region current while increasing healthy-region current. This directly anticipates the general control architecture. Wang et al. (2025) report distributed reference contacts resolving local negative-electrode potential and using it to set fast-charge current boundaries. These are primary reasons to mark **architectural novelty as doubtful and specific protocol novelty UNKNOWN**, pending a proper patent/literature search. No advantage over the patent's implementation has been shown.

The first matched comparator is a conventional single-current pouch using **the same distributed reference contacts, sampling, calibration, temperature measurements, and controller computation**. It follows the most restrictive measured zone and globally derates to keep all margins positive. Since its feasible controls are (I_i=w_i I), a subset of the ideal segmented controls, the segmented optimizer cannot be worse in the *ideal model* before hardware costs. This is a mathematical feasible-set fact, not an empirical advantage. The second and stronger systems comparator is a bank of smaller, sealed cells occupying the same gross volume, each with its own measurements and charge channel. It has the same spatial information and at least as much independent electrical control, with less cross-zone ionic coupling, but more casing, tabs, and pack assembly. A segmented pouch is consequential only if its retained active-material fraction, mass, heat handling, wiring reliability, and cost beat this modular incumbent at equal delivered energy, lifetime and plating risk. Existing potential-controlled fast charging and distributed-reference protocols already provide a serious global-current baseline; comparing against simple CCCV would be too weak [Rangarajan et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025].

## Acquisition, physical costs, and failure channels

* **Preparation:** patterned/insulated collector and graphite coating, edge sealing and pouch feedthroughs, calibrated distributed references, per-zone shunts and current channels. Trenches may create edge current crowding or consume capacity. Segmentation demonstrated for measurement does not establish manufacturable, durable, independently driven large-format cells.
* **Acquisition:** every pulse consumes time and charge redistribution, plus reference calibration and a plating ground truth. A reference sample can miss through-plane minima or drift. The physical claim requires uncertainty bounds from paired operando/post-mortem studies, not fitting a voltage threshold on the same traces later used to claim success.
* **Energy and control:** switches and shunts dissipate approximately \(\int\sum_i I_i^2 R_i\,dt\), before multi-channel conversion, sensing and cooling energy. A linear-pass transistor additionally wastes \(\int\sum_i I_i V_{\rm drop,i}\,dt\); a switched converter could lower that at the cost of size and complexity. Moving current to a healthy region can increase its heat and cathode stress. Total charger power and bus voltage constraints still apply.
* **Reliability:** a stuck-on channel, short between coating islands, failed reference, broken seal, or fast state drift can invalidate local control. A fail-safe design reverts to globally conservative derating, but the failure probability and diagnostic coverage must be measured. Region isolation must be verified electrically and under electrolyte soak, swelling, aging and abuse.
* **Scaling:** channel count, wiring, reference leads, feedthrough sealing, and controller power grow with zone number. Too few zones cannot isolate a small hot spot; too many shrink net capacity and raise failure opportunities. The optimum zone scale is an experimental question.

## Decisive experiment

Make randomized matched graphite/NMC pouch prototypes from the same lot: (A) ordinary single-current geometry with distributed reference contacts and the strongest validated global-margin controller; (B) independently driven two/four-zone geometry with the same contacts and controller budget; (C) same gross-volume modular small-cell bank with per-cell control. Include an ordinary segmented prototype with its channels hard-bussed to quantify manufacturing penalties separately from control benefit. Introduce one reproducible local handicap, such as a controlled colder region or a measured transport variation, while keeping global thermal conditions and initial SOC matched. First make small compensated current transfers at sub-plating rates. Estimate (A_{11}-A_{12}), (A_{22}-A_{21}), repeatability and uncertainty while recording both electrode potentials and temperature. If the differential authority is absent or reverses under SOC/temperature variation, the mechanism fails before risky cycling.

Then compare preregistered **charge-time versus plating-risk Pareto curves** at equal delivered usable energy, with the same maximum cell temperature, voltage, cathode margin, charger power, and energy overhead accounted for. Quantify local metallic Li immediately after selected charges using a validated destructive local assay, with microscopy/cross-section or operando spectroscopic confirmation and sham controls; track irreversible Li loss and capacity fade over repeated cycles. Do not infer zero plating from absence of a voltage feature. Randomize cell assignment; report failed sensors, dead channels, region-to-region variance, false-negative local margins, reference drift and the cost of all pulses and sensing. A successful result requires (i) measured differential response of the predicted sign and sufficient size, (ii) less local plated Li at equal total charge/time or faster charge at equal measured plating/cathode/thermal risk, **and** (iii) net system benefit against the modular comparator. If (i) or (ii) fails, the physical intervention is refuted for that cell family. If (iii) fails, it remains a useful laboratory diagnostic/control demonstration but not a consequential storage capability.
