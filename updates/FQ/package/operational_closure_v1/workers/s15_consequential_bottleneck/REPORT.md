# Capillary-relay copper removal from solid steel scrap

**15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. Status: UNTESTED CONSTRUCTION; no demonstrated capability advantage. Novelty: UNKNOWN.** This report proposes an operation for a consequential but carefully restricted feed: fragmented steel with copper *attached to or exposed at its surface* before remelting. It does not purport to remove copper already dissolved through millimetre-thick steel. No laboratory experiment was performed here.

## Decision and physical access

Copper carried into an electric-arc-furnace (EAF) melt cannot be removed by routine steel refining, and it limits high-grade flat-steel recycling. Daehn, Serrenho and Allwood report about 0.4 wt% Cu in typical shredded end-of-life scrap versus below 0.1 wt% for many flat products, and note that hammer shredding entangles copper wiring with steel. Their assessment reports an older estimate that 90% of contaminating Cu was surface-associated for the particular scrap feedstocks studied; it explicitly warns that this need not cover Cu already alloyed into recycled steel. A 2018 Japanese material-flow study independently distinguishes externally mixed Cu from alloyed Cu and finds that the latter contribution had risen in its studied system. Thus the starting measurement must quantify *accessible metallic Cu mass*, not merely total elemental Cu. [Daehn et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01537-9); [Nakamura et al. 2018](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tetsutohagane/104/8/104_TETSU-2018-009/_article/-char/en).

The physical affordance is a temperature window above Cu's melting point and below steel's, together with differential surface energy. Daehn et al. directly saw Cu liquate on solid low-carbon steel, sometimes as a stable droplet; on medium-carbon steel, scale blistering and oxide/metal gaps allowed the liquid to spread under scale and into grain boundaries. This makes simple preferential melting a serious existing method but also identifies its failure mode: Cu stays attached or disappears under scale. A 2026 experiment on *clean, porous iron in vacuum* directly measured molten-Cu wetting (contact angle about 9 ± 5 degrees at 1100 °C) and rapid infiltration from finite droplets (reported 420 ± 50 mm/s at 1150 °C). It demonstrates the collector interaction, **not** transfer from heterogeneous scrap, a Cu–Sn alloy, an oxidized foam, or a micrometre-thin interfacial film. [Daehn et al. 2019, wetting experiments](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01578-0); [Zhevnenko and Petrov 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.surfin.2026.108970).

## Proposed operation: a capillary relay during scrap preheat

Build a short hot stage upstream of an EAF or as an experimental batch cell. Classify a lot by composition, carbon level, surface oxide and externally attached Cu fraction using representative sectioning plus bulk ICP/OES or equivalent assays. Preheat steel pieces to a calibrated temperature just above the Cu-rich phase liquidus while keeping the steel solid, in a controlled low-oxidation-potential gas. A mechanically compliant, replaceable porous-Fe cassette is reduced/cleaned separately, then briefly pressed against the liquated Cu-bearing surfaces and withdrawn. Connected pores pull accessible liquid Cu into the cassette by capillarity. Repeated contact positions, not higher heating, address irregular fragment geometry. Copper-filled cassettes leave as a separate Cu–Fe feedstock; their regeneration or sale is part of the process cost. The steel proceeds to the EAF only if a representative post-treatment assay clears the Cu specification.

The process is **selective by location and phase, not by a claim that Fe and Cu have magically different chemistry in the melt**. The steel core remains solid. On low-carbon pieces a thin oxide may reduce adhesion of Cu to the base while the freshly prepared Fe collector is Cu-wetting. This is a difficult two-surface state to maintain at a common gas potential; it is a design hypothesis requiring direct measurement. For medium-carbon or heavily scaled scrap, a shallow mechanical scale-opening step may expose trapped Cu, but this can tear the steel, oxidize Fe, or drive Cu inward. These feeds require a separate pass/fail gate; do not average them into a successful low-carbon result.

**Measurement and control are part of the operation.** Calibrate furnace pyrometry against embedded thermocouples; track O\(_2\) potential/dew point; use high-speed optical/thermal imaging on witness contacts (the observation method is demonstrated for porous-Fe melt uptake in the 2026 study) and a calibrated four-terminal conductance change across each cassette as *candidate* fill signals. A Cu-filled Fe pore should add a parallel conductive path, but temperature, Fe oxidation, pressure and solid-state contact may imitate it. Therefore those signals set contact time and cassette replacement only after blinded calibration against weighed and sectioned cassettes; they never certify product purity. Periodic destructive cross sections of scrap and cassette, ICP/OES Cu/Fe mass balances, and EAF melt assays are the independent verification. The controller stops or diverts a lot if Cu uptake stalls, Fe loss increases, or accessible-Cu uncertainty is too large. No opaque classifier is needed.

The mechanism can be expressed by an ideal pore competition condition. For a cylindrical collector pore of radius \(r\), molten-Cu surface tension \(\gamma\), dynamic wetting angle \(\theta_w\), and viscosity \(\mu\), its ideal capillary pressure is \(2\gamma\cos\theta_w/r\). Transfer from the scrap requires this pressure to exceed the effective retention/pinning pressure on the base plus any oxide-film rupture or entrance barrier. For an ideal isolated pore, Lucas–Washburn predicts \(\ell^2\simeq (\gamma r\cos\theta_w/2\mu)t\); real porous-Fe data themselves show that dynamic contact angles and geometry matter, so this is a *design inequality*, not a predicted production speed. The missing, load-bearing quantity is the probability that a Cu-bearing surface actually forms a liquid bridge to an unsaturated, clean pore before it wets a steel grain boundary or becomes trapped beneath scale. [Zhevnenko and Petrov 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.surfin.2026.108970); [Daehn et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01578-0).

## Exact mass gate and a failed broader mechanism

For an illustrative one-tonne lot at 0.4 wt% Cu, the initial Cu mass is 4 kg. If \(R\) kg Cu and \(L\) kg Fe are removed, the output assay is

\[
 c_{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{4-R}{1000-R-L}.
\]

Meeting \(c_{\mathrm{out}}\leq 0.001\) requires \(R\geq(3+0.001L)/0.999\). With zero Fe loss this is **3.003 kg Cu**, or 75.08% of all incoming Cu. Let \(f\) be the fraction of Cu in an accessible surface phase and \(\eta\) the *measured* fraction of that phase captured. Necessarily \(f\eta\geq0.7508\) before any Fe loss. If \(f=0.90\), the required capture is \(\eta\geq0.8342\); if \(f\leq0.75\), the 0.1 wt% goal is impossible for any surface-only device. These are arithmetic consequences of the stated feed and target, not empirical efficiencies. The accompanying `bounds.py` prints the calculations.

The initially broader idea was to oxidize Fe so that Cu dissolved in steel is rejected as a surface liquid, then wick it away. High-temperature experiments do show Cu enrichment at the oxide/steel interface near 1100–1150 °C, and an older oxidation paper found Cu in scale as well as at the interface. However, that access is extremely shallow at practical hold times. Majima and Mitani measured the Cu lattice tracer diffusivity in austenite as \(D_l=4.16\times10^{-4}\exp[-305{,}000/(RT)]\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}\). At 1150 °C, direct substitution gives \(D_l\approx2.66\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}\) and \(\sqrt{2D_lt}\approx4.4\) micrometres in one hour. A deliberately generous ideal absorbing-boundary calculation for a 1-mm slab exposed on both sides gives about 0.7% of initially uniform lattice Cu reaching a face in that hour. Grain boundaries, advancing oxidation, and local liquid penetration change the real profile, but they do not justify claiming bulk de-coppering from this lattice-only estimate. If the front instead exposes bulk Cu by oxidizing successive Fe layers and no deeper diffusion helps, recovering 75% of uniformly alloyed Cu requires oxidizing roughly 75% of the Fe, then handling or reducing that oxide. The useful redesign is therefore to restrict the operation to attached or already exposed Cu and acquire \(f\) for every lot. [Majima and Mitani 1978](https://doi.org/10.2320/matertrans1960.19.663); [high-temperature oxidation experiment, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12897733/); [2004 Cu distribution experiment](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/isijinternational1989/44/9/44_9_1576/_article/-char/en).

The ideal sink calculation is only a mechanism check. It assumes constant \(D_l\), a planar uniform slab, two perfectly absorbing faces, and no moving oxide front. It is **not** a measured bound on real scrap, which has variable phase, geometry, grain boundaries and Cu-bearing attachments. Its purpose is to expose the invalid jump from observing a surface Cu film to claiming removal of alloyed bulk Cu.

## Strongest equally informed comparators

The production comparator is the best available combination of pre-shred/fragmentation, magnetic/density/optical separation and manual or automated removal, followed by EAF blending to specification. This cannot be dismissed as merely conceptual: Koinig et al. report a 2026 industrial trial of optical ejection on rusty post-shredder material above 3 t/h. In a 490-kg validation run, their retained 452.98-kg fraction contained 0.42 kg of *visibly* Cu-bearing particles, and they report 99.907 wt% of retained particles without visible Cu. They also report 33.84 kg of iron co-ejected with the reject fraction. These are substantial operational results, but **99.907 wt% visibly Cu-free particles is not a bulk elemental-Cu assay or a claim that 0.1 wt% melt Cu was reached**. An equally informed dry-sorting line given the same feed and assay might be cheaper and faster than any hot contact process; the decisive comparison includes both residual Cu and Fe yield. [Koinig et al. 2026, industrial trial and mass breakdown](https://doi.org/10.3390/pr14050746).

The direct closest physical comparator is **preferential Cu melting followed by mechanical shaking or molten-salt assisted removal**, with the same feed characterization, temperature profile, oxygen control and post-assay. Daehn et al. already proposed precisely that physics and observed the wetting/entrapment problem. A capillary cassette wins only if it collects the same accessible Cu at lower total cost, Fe loss and contamination, or reaches Cu that optimized shaking cannot access. [Daehn et al. 2019 comparative assessment](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01537-9); [Daehn et al. 2019 wetting study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01578-0).

The strongest immersion comparator for irregular pieces is a hot extraction bath, especially sulfide matte or molten Al, because it contacts surfaces a pressed foam may miss. A primary 1991 molten-Al study reported externally Cu-contaminated solid scrap becoming Cu-free after 5–10 min at about 745 °C, but the downstream Al carryover must be charged. A reported sulfur-matte pilot on copper tubing wrapped around steel rods removed the attached Cu in minutes at 900–1000 °C, but Fe oxidation and sulfur control must be charged. Daehn et al.'s assessment estimates approximately 20 kg Al adhering per tonne of steel in one Al-extraction route and 20–30 kWh/t extra desulfurization for one matte route; these are *model estimates from specified routes*, not measured universal constants. The capillary proposal must beat the best of these after matched feed preparation and full system accounting, not an artificially weak no-treatment baseline. [Iwase and Tokinori 1991](https://doi.org/10.1002/srin.199101282); [sulfur-matte primary report](https://p2infohouse.org/ref/25/24371.pdf); [Daehn et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11663-019-01537-9).

Oxidation and machining of Cu-rich *steel surfaces* have also already been proposed and tested for hot-shortness mitigation. That is not the same feed as attached copper on irregular scrap, but it defeats any claim that creating and removing an oxidized Cu-rich skin is in itself new. [Yeşiltepe and Şeşen 2021](https://hrcak.srce.hr/246101). The 2026 Landi–Sridhar–De Moor paper also reports a mass-balance oxidation model for separated Cu layer thickness, so the mass-balance insight alone has unknown priority. Its full methods were not acquired here. [Primary article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/srin.202501128).

## Full-resource ledger and decisive test

No end-to-end resource measurement exists for this construction. A valid comparison must record per tonne of *accepted, specification-grade steel*, including rejected lots:

| Resource or loss | Proposed operation's charge | Matched comparator's charge |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition and preparation | Statistical sampling, sectioning/assay of external versus alloyed Cu, fragmentation/cleaning, carbon/oxide map; uncertainty and rejected lots | Same information and equivalent feed conditioning |
| Heat and atmosphere | Sensible heat to >Cu liquidus, holding/radiation and transfer losses, gas supply/purification, oxygen/dew-point control; credit recovered preheat only when measured | Full heat, bath or mechanical energy, atmosphere and transfer losses |
| Collector and product | Fabrication, cleaning/reduction, pore degradation, Fe foam mass, captured-Cu grade, replacement/regeneration and Cu recovery from Cu–Fe cassette | Shaking/salt losses or Al/matte make-up, regeneration, disposal and product recovery |
| Steel quality | Fe oxidation, fines/dust, added collector fragments or Cu penetration, residual Cu/Sn/S/Al, yield and downstream rolling defects | Same downstream assays and steel yield |
| Reliability/scale | Contact probability across irregular fragments, throughput, cassette saturation, fouling, maintenance, off-spec diversion and unobserved buried Cu | Equal throughput and off-spec/rework treatment |

An ideal pore-volume capacity calculation gives \(M_{\rm Cu,cap}=M_{\rm Fe,foam}[\phi/(1-\phi)](\rho_{\rm Cu}/\rho_{\rm Fe})\xi\), where \(\phi\) is open porosity and \(\xi\in[0,1]\) is *actual* pore filling. It is only a sizing ceiling. The dominant uncertainty is not whether enough void volume can be made; it is whether copper reaches that void from each real scrap fragment without excess Fe loss or Cu penetration.

**Fatal test:** Before any pilot claim, randomize real shredded scrap lots spanning low/medium carbon, rust/oxide, small Cu wires, Cu-plated pieces and pieces with independently measured alloyed Cu. Pre-register a target below 0.1 wt% Cu in the accepted EAF melt, at least 90% Fe yield, complete Cu/Fe/Sn mass closure within pre-specified assay uncertainty, and noninferior throughput and total energy/material cost to the best optimized comparator on the same lots. Run (i) thermal/preheat-only, (ii) optimized preferential melting plus mechanical/salt removal, (iii) hot immersion if viable, and (iv) capillary relay with live signals and post-assay. Blind downstream bulk Cu assays and metallographic checks for grain-boundary Cu, scale entrapment and steel rolling defects. The first coupon-scale gate is simpler: show directly that a clean Fe collector removes Cu from an actual oxidized low-carbon steel contact **against the base's wetting/pinning**, then repeat for medium-carbon, Cu–Sn and dirty surfaces. If no favorable pressure-transfer window exists, the capillary mechanism fails even though clean Cu droplets imbibe clean porous Fe in vacuum. If the coupon works but \(f\eta\) cannot meet the mass gate or the optimized comparator is cheaper, the operation still fails as a consequential capability.

## Evidence/provenance boundary

**Established literature inspected:** the 2019 Cambridge assessment's full accessible article and wetting-study methods/discussion, the 2026 industrial optical-ejection trial's abstract/results/mass breakdown, the 1978 diffusion abstract and measured equation, the 2018 Japanese material-flow abstract, the 2004 oxidation abstract, the 2021 machining abstract, the 1991 Al-extraction abstract, and search-indexed author/publisher text for the 2026 porous-Fe infiltration and oxidation studies. The sulfur-matte report's indexed test/result excerpts were inspected; its full PDF was not independently audited. The 2026 analytical oxidation model was seen only as an abstract/metadata summary, not full methods.

**Original derivation here:** the Cu/Fe mass gate, required \(f\eta\), the diffusion substitution and ideal-sink consequence, and the conditional capillary-transfer inequality. `bounds.py` runs only arithmetic on stated inputs; it is not a physical simulation or empirical result.

**Untested hypotheses:** a clean porous Fe cassette can outcompete the actual scrap surface for liquid Cu under one controlled atmosphere; collector-fill signals remain interpretable; heterogeneous contact coverage and Cu–Fe product handling can scale without an adverse resource ledger. The literature proves none of these. The operation is a specific experimental proposal with a strong falsifier, not a demonstrated breakthrough or novelty-cleared invention.
