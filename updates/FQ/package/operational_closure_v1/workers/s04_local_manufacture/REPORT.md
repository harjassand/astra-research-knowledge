# S04 — Defect-latched metallization for buried interconnect opens

15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. **Status: untested construction; consequential manufacturing advantage NOT_ACHIEVED; novelty UNKNOWN.** This is an original proposed *combination and test contract*, not a report of fabrication. The mathematical voltage-collapse obstruction below is derived here under its stated circuit model. Literature results are attributed to their authors. No laboratory experiment was available.

## Exact manufacturing task and why local information exists

Target a narrow class: isolated open gaps (initially 4–100 nm, to be measured, not assumed repairable) in Cu micro-joints or low-current interconnect test structures inside an otherwise sealed 3D package. The endpoint is a *durable electrical connection between the two intended terminals* under specified current, temperature, and cycling, while adjacent intended-isolated nets remain isolated. It is not restoration of the original grain structure, a general cure for buried porosity, or repair of arbitrary shorts, delamination, wrong routes, missing pads, or blocked ion paths.

The proposed substrate writes the binary target locally. Each vulnerable joint has two intended mating Cu electrodes surrounded by its own ion-conducting repair sleeve, Cu reservoir, and insulating barrier to every other net. The sleeve and electrode geometry say *which two ends may connect*. A temporary wafer-sort bus applies a bounded test bias to each known net; it need not image or locate the bad joint. A sound joint almost shorts the two ends, whereas an open places a large fraction of the test voltage across its local gap. Cu oxidation, ion migration and reduction can then nucleate a bridge at that gap. That is a physical selection operation, with the netlist, material deposition and isolation sleeve carrying information acquired during original manufacture. The location is selected by the defect-induced potential distribution rather than external full-volume tomography.

This is not a free information oracle. Per-joint lithography, paired-electrode alignment, initial sleeve continuity, per-net addressability, isolation, electrolyte chemistry, and a voltage/current recipe are all supplied. A gap outside a sleeve or a broken reservoir is inaccessible. Multiple opens on one net divide voltage; shorts can redirect it. A process that merely reconnects a wire with an atomic filament may fail the actual endpoint.

## First construction and a sharp self-limitation

The first construction is a Cu-ion-conducting jacket (an oxide/solid electrolyte or confined gel to be qualified) around each preselected joint, with one Cu source electrode, an opposing reduction surface, a dielectric wall between neighboring nets, and a series-compliant test pulse. An open triggers electrochemical metallization; when Cu first bridges, its resistance falls and the gap voltage collapses. The field acts as a local sensor, power source and stop signal. The central idea is **already demonstrated in a different substrate**: [Gao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.202529989) report flexible Ag printed circuits with an isolation layer and colloidal electrolyte; damage draws electrolyte into a crack and normal operating voltage drives electrochemical migration to reconnect the trace. Their abstract reports approximately 100% current recovery in hours and up to 350,000 bending cycles for the metal trace; the detailed repaired-joint life data were not available in the inspected abstract. Separately, Cu/HfO2/Pt cells with a **4 nm** HfO2 layer show field-assisted Cu-rich filaments and abrupt conductance changes, with current compliance essential to control formation [Lv et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13311). An in-situ Cu/Al2O3 study directly saw filament growth and a post-connection no-growth period [Hubbard et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.5b00901). These experiments do **not** establish repair of 100 nm buried interconnect gaps, long-term current capacity, or a reliable Cu joint.

Here is the decisive elementary circuit calculation. If a source `V0` drives an emerging bridge resistance `Rb` through a safety/series resistance `Rs`, then

```text
Vgap = V0 Rb/(Rs + Rb).
```

Assume, illustratively, growth requires `Vgap >= V*`. For `0 < V* < V0`, growth must stop as `Rb` falls below `Rstop = V* Rs/(V0-V*)`. A passive resistor chosen to cap bridge current at `Imax` must obey `Rs >= V0/Imax`, hence `Rstop >= V* V0/[Imax(V0-V*)]`. With **assumed**, not measured, `V0 = 1 V`, `V* = 0.2 V`, and `Imax = 100 microampere`, `Rs >= 10 kilohm` and `Rstop >= 2.5 kilohm`. A 100 nm-long, 100 nm × 100 nm Cu bridge would have only `~0.168 ohm` if bulk Cu resistivity `1.68e-8 ohm m` applied. Maintaining 0.2 V across it would require `~1.19 A`; the passive resistor needed to let growth continue to that resistance would be `<=0.672 ohm`, allowing `>=1.49 A` in the source model. This is a conflict between safe forming current and passive voltage-driven *thickening*. It is not a universal electrochemical impossibility; its assumptions must be measured and alternative mechanisms can bypass it.

The bulk-resistivity cylinder equivalent at 2.5 kilohm and 100 nm length has radius only `~0.46 nm`. Bulk resistivity and continuum geometry are **not valid predictions at atomic scale**; the estimate merely shows that the stop resistance is in the atomic-filament regime, where contact resistance, quantized conductance and stochastic rupture matter. At 100 microampere, a hypothetical 0.5 nm-radius bridge would carry `~1.3e14 A/m^2`, versus `1e10 A/m^2` in a 100 nm × 100 nm cross-section. Neither figure establishes a safe current limit without accelerated-life data. In a measured particle-trapping circuit, electrical contact caused the local field to disappear and stopped further particle attraction; excessive post-bridge current destroyed the bridge through heating [Suetsugu and Iwase 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9967765/). The particular chemistry differs, but the endpoint tension is directly relevant.

For a 100 nm × 100 nm × 100 nm Cu bridge, Faraday's law gives the *ideal stoichiometric* `Q = 2 F rho_Cu volume/M_Cu = 2.72e-11 C` (27 pC), or 27 pJ of electrical work at 1 V. This counts only Cu atoms: no ion transport inefficiency, reservoir preparation, electrode reactions, test overhead, heat, leakage, or substrate processing. At 1 nA of 100%-efficient Faradaic current it would take 27 ms, but actual geometry, kinetics and parasitic electronic current are unmeasured. There is no measured energy or time advantage here.

## Substantive redesign: field-write a repair catalyst, then chemically thicken

To address the skinny-filament obstruction, separate *selection* from *load-bearing growth*. The proposed redesign puts a finite Cu precursor/reductant supply in a sealed annular repair chamber around each joint. A high-gap-field step would create an active metal/catalyst seed only at an open, effectively latching the local defect decision. A subsequent electroless Cu reaction would thicken that seed after the electrical voltage has collapsed. Reagent volume would be bounded by the chamber; a passivating wall and the eventual closure of the gap would limit spread. A final low-energy electrical challenge would reject tenuous bridges before the package is accepted. In symbols, the hoped-for gate is `open -> high local field -> catalyst seed -> chemical growth -> thick connection`, while `intact -> low local field -> no seed`.

This is a hypothesis with hard missing interfaces. Electrochemical catalyst writing must depend on the *gap field*, not merely the absolute potential of an intact Cu pad in the bath. Electroless chemistry must remain shelf-stable through bonding and thermal history, reach an actually buried crack, keep reaction products or gas from trapping voids, and stop before Cu shorts neighboring nets. If cracks themselves expose Cu catalyst, a purely chemical incumbent may start plating without the field-write stage; the additional electrical stage then supplies no advantage. If the reaction continues on all intact exposed Cu, local selectivity is lost. No published source inspected here demonstrates all these gates simultaneously in a sealed, dense 3D package.

The nearest primary evidence cuts both ways. A 2024 chip-to-chip study used microfluidically delivered electroless Cu to join aligned Cu bumps and directly observed voids at gaps as low as **10 micrometres**, attributed to trapped hydrogen and uneven reagent flow. Intermittent flow and inhibitor changes reduced voids, but the paper also describes extraneous Cu on Si [Ren et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11123229/). This establishes relevant deposition and concrete transport/side-reaction hazards, not our sealed-chamber repair. An earlier microcapsule experiment restored nearly full circuit conductance in under 1 ms after a crack released liquid Ga–In [Blaiszik et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.201102888); it demonstrates local fracture-triggered reagent access but at a much larger device scale and with a liquid-metal endpoint. A paper on area-selective Ni electrodeposition reports full tensile-strength recovery in selected fractured metal samples, including a 3D-printed shellular structure [Akbari et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36933269/), but those electrolyte-accessible fractures do not establish dense, sealed Cu interconnect repair.

## Strongest equally informed manufacturing comparator

For the proposed chip-to-chip joint manufacturing stage, the strongest direct comparator is **optimized electroless all-Cu interconnection**, using the *same* pad layout, alignment tolerances, precursor access, microfluidic or chamber infrastructure, test contacts, thermal budget and acceptance criteria. The [2024 primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11123229/) shows the method and its voiding mechanisms. Give it the same geometry and reagent budget; it may connect weak joints without field-written catalyst, eliminate per-net repair pulses, and exploit improved flow/inhibitors. If the setting is post-seal or flexible circuit repair, [Gao et al.'s operational-voltage-driven electrochemical repair](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.202529989) is the closest mechanism comparator; it must get the same addressability and repair-medium access. A further benchmark is preinstalled repair channels plus electroless chemistry triggered by a fracture-exposed surface; the proposed sleeve and reagent cannot be charged only to the competitor. For low-current lines, [field-assisted particle bridging](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-55801-8) and [electrochemical metallization](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13311) are also known active local selectors. Plain field-directed reconnection has no plausible priority claim.

Thus the candidate only earns attention if field-writing a durable catalytic latch improves *total* good-joint yield, low-ohm fatigue survival, and adjacent-net isolation at equal manufacturing and test cost. Merely avoiding site-by-site imaging is not a unique advantage: pad geometry, bath exposure, continuity testing, and self-aligned electroless growth already exploit local information. Conventional electrical redundancy/rerouting during package test is another equally informed functional comparator when physical Cu restoration is not needed; its masks, spare wires and test time would have to be counted rather than dismissed.

## Selectivity, endpoint and scaling contract

Let `M` be intact repair-capable joints, `D` be open joints, `p_false` the per-intact-joint probability of a new short or damaging deposition, and `p_repair` the per-open probability of a connection meeting the *aged* endpoint. If failures were independent, a necessary system-yield condition is `(1-p_false)^M p_repair^D >= Y`. Even ignoring open-repair failures, for `M = 10^6` and `Y = 0.9` we require `p_false <= 1 - 0.9^(1/10^6) = 1.0536e-7`. Even ignoring false deposition, for `D = 10` we require `p_repair >= 0.9^(1/10) = 0.98952`. Correlations and common-mode chemistry can make the independence formula optimistic. With zero false events, about `2.84e7` representative intact-joint trials would be needed merely to put a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound below `1.0536e-7`; a small coupon study cannot certify million-joint yield. These numbers are original arithmetic on a hypothetical yield contract, not measured rates. The circuit, charge and yield arithmetic is reproduced by [`verify_circuit.py`](./verify_circuit.py).

Endpoint verification must be staged. During wafer sort, per-net four-terminal resistance and neighboring-net leakage can establish functional connectivity and isolation for the addressed nets. Stress at the specified current, thermal cycles, repeated pulse/read cycles and noise/drift measurements probe whether the connection persists. Destructive cross-sections or X-ray/TEM sampling on blinded coupons must check whether growth made a dense joined cross-section or a filament plus hidden void. Terminal resistance alone cannot distinguish two internal states with identical `R` but very different neck geometry, electromigration hazard or crack resistance. The production endpoint must state allowable `R`, leakage, drift, current density, temperature and lifetime *before* looking at results.

Full resource accounting includes: extra mask/alignment steps to make each sleeve and reservoir; lost area and added capacitance; Cu contamination barriers; ionic/chemical stability during all subsequent heat steps; precursor and reductant manufacture, loading and waste; test-bus routing and per-net pulse energy/time; bad-gap localization implicitly performed by the field; any chemical bath/transport and gas removal; rejected dies; accelerated testing; and reliability under operation. A bridge formed in an unqualified sleeve does not waive these costs. The physical target is stored in lithographic geometry and therefore pays a preparation cost up front.

## Decisive next test and disposition

Fabricate blinded arrays of intentionally intact, intentionally open, partially resistive and adjacent-short Cu joint coupons in the *same* package-relevant stack, with calibrated gap distributions and independent measurement of sleeve/reagent access. Randomize coupon assignment to: (A) proposed field-write plus chemical growth, (B) the strongest tuned electroless-only process with the same channels/reagents, (C) field-write/filament alone, and (D) untreated control. Record all applied voltages, currents, charge and time. Dissect selected joints after stress to measure metal thickness, trapped voids, contamination and shorts. A first gate is that A materially beats B on aged low-resistance yield and isolation at matched total cost; if it only produces fast high-resistance filaments, or B matches it, stop this line. Scale to representative package arrays only after the coupon gate; the intact-joint false-action bound above is a separate large-sample qualification problem.

**Disposition:** the local-field idea is physically motivated but old in its basic form. The two-stage latch-and-thicken construction addresses a real mathematical obstruction, but its key selectivity, access, stable chemical storage and endpoint claims are untested, and an equally informed electroless process may absorb the same benefit. This is a falsifiable manufacturing research proposal, **not** a demonstrated new capability.
