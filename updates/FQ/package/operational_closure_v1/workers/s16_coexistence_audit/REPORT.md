# Coexistence audit: a DNA-origami nanoruler that reports and repairs while it survives

**Status (15 October 2026): conditional obstruction and testable redesign; no new manufacturing capability established.** The concrete substrate is the experimentally made 12-helix-bundle (12HB) DNA-origami nanoruler of Scheckenbach *et al.* It has replaceable fluorescent imager strands on docking sites and can take up matching replacement staples after deliberately omitted or nuclease-damaged staples. This is a manufactured nanomaterial with local molecular recognition; it is not an atomically universal repair machine. The strongest defensible output is retained **whole-object geometry and optical function** over repeated imaging/challenge cycles, normalized to material and energy inputs. A bright spot alone is not that output.

## Primary-source observations actually inspected

1. [Scheckenbach, Schubert, Forthmann, Glembockyte & Tinnefeld, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 60, 4931–4938 (2021), doi:10.1002/anie.202012986](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/anie.202012986), indexed full-text Results/Discussion and methods passages. A 12HB carrying 100 docking sites was imaged on a passivated coverslip. Continuous 75 W cm^-2 illumination for 3 minutes bleached a 20-nt permanently bound label. A 13-nt exchangeable label recovered 20–60% of brightness over 3 hours without photoprotection; oxygen removal, ROXS and a dye change allowed initial full recovery and >60% after five bleach cycles. The authors explicitly attribute incomplete unprotected recovery partly to photo-generated reactive oxygen species damaging DNA docking sites. For a structural challenge, omitting nine central staples kinked the 12HB. Incubation with a 300-fold excess of missing staples from 50 °C to room temperature lowered the AFM kinked fraction from 63% to 32%; a DNA-PAINT defective fraction fell from 72% to 38%. For unknown nuclease challenge, 0.2% FBS over 11 days changed three-mark fractions from 87% to 12% without added strands, 83% to 56% with nonmatching strands, and 85% to 76% with matching strands. In 10% FBS over 2 hours, matching and nonmatching strands stabilized similarly: the authors say damage was too fast for selective self-healing to dominate. These are different endpoints and cohorts, not measured single-site repair rates.
2. [Eklund, Comberlato, Parish, Jungmann & Bastings, *ACS Nano* 15, 17668–17677 (2021), doi:10.1021/acsnano.1c05540](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsnano.1c05540), indexed full-text coating/results/methods passages and [author-hosted article PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3370692_1/component/file_3371294/content). On a different DNA-origami disk, K10PEG coating at 1:1 N:P left the measured hybridization kinetics of protruding DNA-PAINT handles indistinguishable from uncoated structures. The handles extend about 7 nm while estimated coating thickness is about 1 nm. The same coating hindered attachment through core-integrated biotin enough that roughly tenfold higher origami concentration was needed for similar field occupancy. This is a direct counterexample to an unconditional protection-versus-access no-go, and a direct warning that **the position and type of access site matter**. It did not test access to a damage-exposed, core-adjacent scaffold toehold or structural repair through the coating.
3. [Ponnuswamy *et al.*, *Nature Communications* 8, 15654 (2017)](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15654), abstract, introduction and discussion. An oligolysine-PEG coating increased resistance to serum nucleases by up to about 1,000-fold in its tested systems; coating composition, architecture and assay differ from Eklund's accessibility measurement. These numbers **cannot be multiplied** into a joint protection-and-repair claim for the 12HB.
4. [Agarwal, Matthies, Joffroy & Schmidt, *ACS Nano* 12, 2546–2553 (2018), doi:10.1021/acsnano.7b08345](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29451771/), PubMed primary-paper abstract. DNA-polymerase-assisted gap filling of designed wireframe origami has already been reported. The abstract does not establish polymerase access, end chemistry, or whole-frame recovery for randomly damaged, coated 12HBs.

## Four conditional bounds and their physical meaning

**1. Report strength versus disturbance.** If photons from two candidate states are Poisson with rates (Lr_0,Lr_1), where (L) is excitation scale and background is included, an observation lasting (T) has

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_1\Vert P_0)=LT\{r_1\log(r_1/r_0)+r_0-r_1\}=LTk.
\]

For equal prior odds, the optimal classification error is at least \(\tfrac14 e^{-LTk}\). Thus error at most \(\epsilon<1/4\) requires \(LT\geq\log(1/(4\epsilon))/k\). If illumination independently produces a damaging lesion with hazard \(dL\), the reporter-induced lesion probability at this necessary exposure is at least \(1-\exp[-d\log(1/(4\epsilon))/k]\). This is a **model bound**, not a measured (d/k), and photoprotection can change both rates. Scheckenbach's bleaching/ROS and ROXS rescue show why same-substrate calibration is necessary. More decisively, if an intact frame and a frame with an unlabelled interior break produce the same observation law, (k=0): increasing illumination cannot certify structural integrity. Dynamic imager exchange can diagnose a damaged label or available docking site; it need not diagnose every backbone lesion.

**2. Protected information versus repair access.** For a single repairable structural site, let damage occur at rate \(\lambda\) and repair at rate \(\mu\). A protective layer multiplies them by \(a\) and \(r\), respectively. Under the two-state constant-rate model its stationary intact fraction is \(r\mu/(a\lambda+r\mu)\). It improves over the uncoated fraction \(\mu/(\lambda+\mu)\) exactly when \(r>a\). A barrier can therefore coexist with repair when it excludes a damaging species more than it excludes the repair species. Eklund measured one favorable geometry: protruding handles remained accessible while buried biotin attachment suffered. This does **not** measure (r) for a damage-exposed scaffold toehold, a large enzyme, or an interior template. A protected route/template must remain physically legible to the reaction at the damaged site; otherwise apparent retention is merely slower damage. The key experiment must measure (a,r) on the *same coated 12HB and same challenge*, not splice together the Ponnuswamy and Eklund magnitudes.

**3. Measurement versus action latency.** Let a lesion become irreparable at hazard \(\gamma\). Assume damage arrival phase is uniform relative to optical sampling period \(\Delta\), the diagnostic and reagent-delivery delay is \(\tau\), local repair completion is exponential at rate \(\mu\), and an event is detected with probability \(s\). Then the one-shot rescue probability is

\[
p_{\rm rescue}=s\,e^{-\gamma\tau}\frac{1-e^{-\gamma\Delta}}{\gamma\Delta}\frac{\mu}{\mu+\gamma}.
\]

This follows by averaging survival over the waiting time to the next observation, then competing repair against irreversible loss. Even with arbitrarily fast imaging, (p_{\rm rescue}\leq s e^{-\gamma\tau}\mu/(\mu+\gamma)\). The high-FBS result is qualitative evidence that an actual damage-rate regime can overwhelm this chemistry; it is **not** a measurement of \(\gamma\) or \(\mu\). Autonomous damage-exposed toehold recognition removes external image-to-command delay from the control loop, leaving imaging for quality assurance. That chemical gate is the published incumbent, so bypassing optical latency does not itself establish novelty.

**4. Repair selectivity versus collateral action.** Suppose \(K\) defective sites each repair at hazard \(u\) and \(N\) initially intact sites each suffer an unacceptable false repair/action at hazard \(v\), independently while reagents remain present. In this model newly repaired sites are not susceptible to that harm before the run ends. The exact probability of all defects being repaired before the first harmful action is

\[
p_{\rm clean}=\prod_{j=1}^{K}\frac{ju}{ju+Nv}.
\]

For one defect, (N=100) vulnerable intact features, and 90% clean success, (u/v\geq900\). For (K=9,N=100), the threshold is (u/v\geq2658.068548843\); [the deterministic calculation](selectivity_bound.py) reproduces this. These are **illustrative assumed site counts and hazards**, not measured nanoruler selectivity. Identical-staple exchange at a good site may be harmless, so (v) must mean actual harmful collateral action, not every molecular binding event. Conversely, FBS protection by nonmatching DNA is a competing sacrificial effect: apparent survival does not isolate damage-specific repair. Off-target staple replacement, dimerization, impaired docking or coating removal must be measured as collateral outcomes.

## Redesign that survives the audit as a hypothesis

Co-design a **protected-core, exposed-port 12HB**: keep the structural DNA under a calibrated K10PEG layer, place replaceable fluorescence handles on spacers outside that layer, and put a defined subset of sacrificial staple edges where damage exposes a scaffold toehold reachable by a preloaded matching-staple bath. Use transient imager strands, oxygen scavenging and low-duty optical acquisition for reporting. Let the molecular toehold gate initiate local replacement without waiting for a camera or external controller. The premanufactured scaffold sequence and the complete matching-staple library carry the repair information; neither is free. Eklund supports accessible exterior handles, Scheckenbach supports label renewal and toehold-dependent strand replacement separately. **No source inspected demonstrates all three coexisting on the same coated 12HB**, nor repair of erased scaffold/routing, blocked ends, or arbitrarily buried sites. Those remain outside the proposed repair class.

This redesign is worth a small matched test because it exploits a real size/geometry separation (nuclease attack versus protruding-handle binding) rather than asserting a universal tradeoff. Its strongest equally informed comparator is the published uncoated 12HB with transient labels, ROXS and matching staples, plus a protection-only coated 12HB and a coating tuned by the same screening effort. The proposed design must improve **chemically and geometrically intact, bright objects per unit full manufacturing and operating cost**, not merely recover fluorescence. It may lose if added ports are preferential nuclease targets or if coating excludes repair staples at the structural toehold.

## Decisive physical test and full cost boundary

Randomize identical 12HB batches to coated/uncoated, exposed/occluded repair ports, matching/nonmatching replacement staples, and transient/permanent imagers, with controlled ROXS and excitation. Challenge with blinded, prespecified clean staple gaps, nuclease damage in 0.2% and 10% FBS, and unrepairable blocked/scaffold/route lesions. Keep the original damaged-object denominator. Record per-object photon counts, photodamage, site-resolved toehold binding and staple exchange kinetics, false actions on undamaged sites, repair time, label brightness, AFM or cryo-EM morphology, chemical continuity/sequence and a predeclared function. An independent structure endpoint is essential because three fluorescent marks can persist despite other damage. Estimate (k,d,a,r,\gamma,\mu,u,v) with intervals in the actual joint operating conditions, then test the four displayed inequalities before any scale claim. If the coating suppresses nuclease attack yet leaves **actual repair-port** access high and clean whole-object recovery beats the matched incumbent, the restricted co-design survives; a result confined to label renewal or clean preselected gaps does not establish general manufacturing repair.

Charge scaffold/staple sequence design and synthesis, purified-fold yield, port spacers and coating yield, the matching-staple species/concentration and sacrificial consumption, photoprotection reagents, surface immobilization, illumination energy, image acquisition and computation, incubation/heating/cooling, damage challenge, microscopy/sequencing, false repairs, throughput, and discard/rework. Source reports used 5 µM total matching staples (22.5 nM per species) for the 0.2% FBS comparison and a 300-fold excess of missing staples plus a temperature ramp in its deliberately damaged structural example; those are nonzero inputs, not free autonomous repair. No complete-resource advantage, scale yield, novelty, or physical coexistence of the proposed combination has been established.
