# Reaction control with incomplete networks: the incumbent and the information gate

15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. Scope: heterogeneous/cell-free reaction control with incomplete pathway support. **Finding:** A complete microscopic reaction network is often unnecessary to optimize a measured product under repeatable, safe actuation. The strongest incumbent is direct input-output control, with transient/isotope measurements and standard system identification available when they improve the decision. Conversely, neither an isotope step nor a phase-locked operando signal by itself certifies pathway support. An exact pair of parallel-pathway models below has the same product isotope response for every isotope waveform but opposite preferred actions. No candidate here establishes a new consequential operation or a cost advantage. Historical novelty is **UNKNOWN**.

## The strongest existing route, as an actual operating method

Fix the admissible actuators (feed composition, temperature, flow, potential, or genuinely independently adjustable catalyst gates), an online product/byproduct assay, a safety envelope, and a monetary/time/material budget. Use a small, safe, persistently exciting set of perturbations to estimate the **decision-relevant** input-output map and delay/dispersion; retain an uncertainty set for untested regions and drift. For a batch task, choose the next conditions by constrained response-surface/Bayesian optimization using measured yield and selectivity. For a continuous reactor, use extremum seeking on product output when safe dither and time-scale separation hold, or a fitted input-output predictor with robust receding-horizon control for dynamic constraints. Fall back to a *verified* safe operating point when predicted constraints cannot be certified. If chemistry-specific information is useful, add isotope-switch SSITKA, operando spectroscopy, or pulsed transient experiments *inside the same observation budget* and use their raw data to refine the predictor. This is a strong incumbent, not a claim of a universal guarantee for unknown nonlinear reactors.

The evidence is concrete but scoped. Biloen et al. switched isotope feeds without altering chemical steady-state catalysis and inferred which carbidic population participates in Fischer-Tropsch/methanation turnover [1]. Urakawa et al. gave periodic concentration/temperature modulation and phase-sensitive detection, explicitly treating the input-output response as amplitude and phase versus frequency [2]. López Cámara et al. combined operando SSITKA, DRIFTS and mass spectrometry in water-gas shift catalysis and used surface/product isotope timing to distinguish carbonate and formate assignments under one specified condition [3]. In an actual CO preferential-oxidation reactor, Lee et al. used measured outlet CO and a temperature actuator to track an optimum with extremum seeking; their paper also reports the importance of delay/phase compensation [4]. Shields et al. optimized directly observed synthetic reaction yields by Bayesian optimization, including real Mitsunobu and deoxyfluorination campaigns [5]. Berberich et al. prove a robust data-driven MPC result from measured input-output trajectories for an **LTI** plant with order/noise assumptions [6]; it must not be promoted to arbitrary catalytic chemistry.

When product, impurity and safety quantities are observable, interventions can be sampled safely, and the plant is stable or adaptively trackable over the decision horizon, feedback can learn which actuator settings work without naming every internal path. This still pays for product assays, exploration, settling, calibration and off-spec material. Microscopic pathway acquisition becomes decision-relevant when an unseen reaction creates an unmeasured hazard, the first intervention is irreversible, safe exploration is unavailable, the plant changes faster than it can be measured, or deployment requires extrapolation outside tested conditions. Even then, the minimum need is a *distinguishing observation sufficient for the action*, not necessarily full network reconstruction.

## Exact countermodel: isotope dynamics do not identify the flux split

Consider two physically distinct but unresolved catalytic intermediate pools, `I1` and `I2`, which both convert feed `A` into the same measured product `P`. At the baseline, their steady product fluxes are `F1 = alpha F` and `F2 = (1-alpha) F`, where `0 < alpha < 1`; each pool turns over with the same rate constant `k > 0`, so its inventory is `Fi/k`. Assume the isotope substitution has no kinetic isotope effect, does not move the chemical steady state, and only changes feed label fraction `z(t)`. These are favorable SSITKA assumptions. If `xi(t)` is the fraction of labelled material in pool `i`, then

```text
d xi/dt = k [z(t) - xi(t)],    i = 1,2
labelled product fraction p(t) = alpha x1(t) + (1-alpha) x2(t).
```

With identical initial label fractions, `x1(t)=x2(t)` for **every** waveform `z(t)`. A unit isotope step yields `p(t)=1-exp(-kt)`; a sinusoidal input has the transfer function `H(i omega)=k/(k+i omega)`. These entire step and frequency responses are independent of `alpha`, despite the internal split ranging arbitrarily close to either pure pathway. This is an exact analytic obstruction to inferring pathway flux support or a unique structural assignment from an unresolved product isotope trace. Different time constants or separately resolved chemical bands can help, but their resolution and attribution must be measured rather than assumed.

Now suppose an actuator `u` increases pathway 1 and decreases pathway 2, with product rate

```text
R_alpha(u) = F [alpha exp(u) + (1-alpha) exp(-u)],   u in [-h,h].
```

At `u=0`, all models have the same baseline rate `F` and the same isotope response above. For `alpha=0.9`, the preferred endpoint is `+h`; for `alpha=0.1`, it is `-h`. The gap between correct and wrong endpoint rates is `0.8 F (exp(h)-exp(-h))`. Thus a controller that observes only the baseline and any number of baseline isotope waveforms cannot select the correct first irreversible action. A fair randomized controller has worst-case expected regret at least half this gap, `0.8 F sinh(h)`. An ordinary **small actuator perturbation with product readout** distinguishes the worlds because `R'_alpha(0)=F(2 alpha-1)`, so direct feedback solves the decision when that perturbation is safe and affordable. The example distinguishes information needed for control from full microscopic pathway identity; it does not assert that all real pools share exactly equal rates.

A second exact false-attribution case concerns spectroscopy. Let a nonproductive adsorbed spectator pool `S` exchange isotope with the feed, with fraction `s'(t)=k[z(t)-s(t)]`, while a separate productive pool has the same response. An IR band from `S` and the product isotope signal have identical phase and amplitude dynamics after normalization, yet no product molecule passes through `S`. In [2], the phase-sensitive suppression calculation explicitly assumes the spectator is *unaffected by the perturbation* and hence constant. A responsive spectator violates that assumption; lock-in detection removes constant backgrounds, not causal ambiguity. Multiple labelled atoms, site-selective perturbations, mass balance and independent poisoning/knockout controls may discriminate this case, with their costs charged.

This is also the relevant limit of the earlier Astra rate-free relocation idea. N817's ideal complete-network rule `S diag(gamma) ker S = 0` depends on knowing the full reaction support. Its supplied dormant `B <-> C` exchange has zero baseline net current at `B=C=2`, but at the proposed relocated `(B,C)=(3,3/2)` gives stationarity residual `(-3/200,+3/200)` for exchange constant `1/100` [7]. A baseline zero-flux observation does not certify absence of the edge; a properly chosen tracer/exchange assay might reveal it. This inherited countermodel is separately labelled `source_derived_unreviewed`; the analytic isotope model above is my own derivation.

## What would count as a new probe?

Replacing `12C` feed by `13C` feed at unchanged chemical composition and recording labelled effluent versus time is SSITKA [1]. Recording surface IR and product mass signals together is operando SSITKA-DRIFTS-MS [3]. Periodic composition/temperature forcing followed by Fourier/phase-sensitive detection is modulation-excitation spectroscopy [2]. Short feed/product pulses are in the established TAP/transient-response family [8]. Estimating a transfer matrix or local state from such perturbations and then selecting controls is system identification/output-feedback control [4,6]. A new pulse code, isotope ordering, or subtraction may improve signal-to-noise, but it is not a new operational capability by naming alone.

The decision criterion is precise. Let `M` be all plants consistent with the observations bought so far, and let `Pi_epsilon(m)` be the policies that are safe and within `epsilon` of the best achievable value in plant `m`. If `intersection_{m in M} Pi_epsilon(m)` is nonempty, resolving the hidden pathways is unnecessary for that decision, provided the common policy can be found from the available decision-level data. If it is empty, an action guarantee requires either a paid probe that splits `M` into decision-consistent classes or acceptance of robust/minimax loss. If two plants give the same distribution for *every admissible probe* but require incompatible safe actions, no observation-only method can guarantee both. This is a direct indistinguishability argument, not a novelty claim. A putative new probe must demonstrate a decision-relevant distinction inaccessible to the standard probe/control portfolio at the same or lower full cost, or an independently verified new physical actuation/measurement interface.

## Decisive benchmark, including a hard same-information competitor

No laboratory experiment was performed. A credible test would use a cell-free or fixed-bed reactor with online product/byproduct mass balance and a prepared family of *blinded* parallel pathways, slow spectators, reversible cross-exchange, and thermal/transport delays. Predeclare the product and impurity targets, maximum temperature/pressure, allowable transient excursions, run horizon, actuator set, detector channels, isotope and spectroscopy menu, isotope effects/transport calibration, and a hard cumulative budget. Include at least (a) a pair whose isotope responses are nearly coincident but actuator derivatives differ, (b) a dormant exchange activated only off baseline, and (c) a responsive nonproductive surface pool. Ground truth is retained by the organizer for post-run analysis only. A real reactor is needed for a physical capability claim; simulation may debug the protocol but would establish only a model result.

Give every controller the **same initial design information, raw data, actuator authority, instrument access and right to retain data**. If the candidate buys an SSITKA/IR run, the competitor can use the same run; if it claims a dedicated probe, price instrument build/validation and give the comparator the resulting measurement as available information. The hard comparator is selected on development runs and frozen before blind evaluation: (i) calibrated isotope/transient experiments and operando spectral analysis when informative, (ii) a nonlinear input-output predictor with measured delay and uncertainty plus constraint-aware receding-horizon control, and (iii) a direct yield optimizer (batch Bayesian optimization or continuous extremum seeking) using exactly the same product data. Report the component comparators as well; do not pick their winning result retrospectively from blind trials. A static direct gate-allocation/enzyme-cost optimizer is also included when the candidate is N769/N817-like and is supplied the same network/gate facts.

Score *qualified integrated product*, selectivity, transient hazard violations, and failure probability on fresh feed conditions and aging/drift. Also report cost-to-first-safe-target and a cost/performance frontier over multiple budgets, with confidence intervals from independently prepared reactors or campaigns. Charge isotope reagent synthesis, feed switching, equilibration/settling and washout, catalyst preparation and regeneration, spectroscopy/GC/MS capital amortization, calibration standards, reactor occupation, off-spec product, energy/heat, actuator wear, computation/tuning, failed experiments, and total elapsed time. Separately state what the candidate knew by assumption versus what it acquired. A proposed method passes this lane only if it preserves safety and delivers reproducibly better qualified output at matched **complete** cost, or the same qualified output at lower complete cost, especially on blinded hidden-pathway cases. Merely reconstructing a pathway, reproducing an SSITKA trace, or improving an uncharged probe count does not pass.
