# Centered midpoint mechanism for unbounded strongly log-concave sources

Status: internally derived candidate extension. Root independently reconstructed the centered mass-to-moment and centered adjoint algebra without finding a flaw. This is written-proof evidence, not external validation or a priority claim. The compact log-concave theorem and its weighted interpolation proof are in `transport_theorem.md`; all norm and optimal-map conventions there are retained. Source repository remains untouched.

## Statement and exact source class

Let `kappa>0`, `d>=2`, and let `rho dx` be a full-dimensional probability density such that

\[
\rho(x)=e^{-\kappa|x|^2/2}h(x),
\]

where `h` is a nonnegative log-concave function, possibly zero outside a proper convex support. No bounded support or positive density lower bound is assumed. This is the extended-support meaning of `kappa`-strong log-concavity. In particular, the support need not be all of `R^d`. Put

\[
A_j=\int_{e_j^\perp}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_t\rho(z+te_j)dz,
\qquad A=\sum_j A_j.
\]

These constants are finite: an affine majorant of the concave `log h` at an interior point gives `h(x)<=C exp(b dot x)` on its support, hence a Gaussian envelope for `rho` after completing the square. Integrating the slice supremum of that envelope proves finiteness of each `A_j`. The constant is allowed to depend on the supplied source; no dimension-free bound on `A` is asserted.

For one target with `int |y|^p dmu<=M`, `p>2`, and an arbitrary `nu in P_2`, the candidate theorem is

\[
\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le w+Cw^{(p-2)/(3p-2)},\qquad w=W_2(\mu,\nu),
\]

with one numerical constant

\[
C=\max\left\{\sqrt{24d/\kappa+128A+40dM+80d},\ 2M^{1/p}\right\}.
\]

The same general one-sided radial-moment profile holds:

\[
D^2\le C_0\left[w^2+\inf_{L>0}\left\{w^{2/3}L^{4/3}+M L^2/\Phi(L)\right\}\right],
\]

provided `Phi` is finite and nondecreasing, `Phi(0)=0`, and `Phi(t)/t^2` is nondecreasing. A vanishing modulus follows if the ratio tends to infinity. The proof uses a radius-independent finite-potential estimate

\[
\|u_1-u_0\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}\,W_2(\mu,\nu),
\tag{P-kappa}
\]

with potentials normalized to have source mean zero. We establish this for finite targets on compact truncations, which is sufficient for all map statements by limits. No assertion about computing the supplied density, optimal coupling, or map is made.

The moment exponent and the whole variational profile are sharp even for one fixed genuinely unbounded strongly log-concave source: a Gaussian density restricted to a slab. That source has proper convex support, and does not have full support `R^d`. The full-support sharpness problem must not be conflated with this statement.

## 1. Strong midpoint surplus

First work with a smooth positive strongly log-concave density on the interior of a compact convex support, allowing a density jump to zero at its boundary. Use the same opposite-cell coupling as in the main note. Write `alpha_i,beta_i` for opposite masses, `gamma_i=sqrt(alpha_i beta_i)`, `X_i,Z_i` for conditional cell samples from the common triangular quantile parameter, and `H_i=(X_i+Z_i)/2`. The midpoint lies in the central cell `P_i`, of mass `M_i`.

Strong log-concavity improves the pointwise density inequality to

\[
\rho(H_i)\ge\sqrt{\rho(X_i)\rho(Z_i)}
\exp\left(\frac{\kappa}{8}|X_i-Z_i|^2\right).
\]

The diagonal Jacobian inequality for the triangular midpoint is unchanged:

\[
J_{H_i}\ge\frac{\gamma_i}{\sqrt{\rho(X_i)\rho(Z_i)}}.
\]

The midpoint map is triangular and one-to-one almost everywhere on its parameter image. Successive one-dimensional substitutions, integrating only over its image in `P_i`, give

\[
M_i\ge\gamma_i E\exp\left(\frac{\kappa}{8}|X_i-Z_i|^2\right).
\tag{surplus}
\]

Let `D_i=E|X_i-Z_i|^2`. Since `exp z>=1+z`,

\[
\frac\kappa8\sum_i\gamma_iD_i\le\sum_i(M_i-\gamma_i).
\]

All three cell families partition the same probability source. Therefore

\[
\sum_i(M_i-\gamma_i)
=\sum_i\left(\frac{\alpha_i+\beta_i}{2}-\sqrt{\alpha_i\beta_i}\right)
\le\frac18\sum_i\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{\gamma_i}.
\]

We obtain the exact global displacement estimate

\[
\sum_i\gamma_iD_i\le\kappa^{-1}\sum_i\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{\gamma_i}.
\tag{strong-displacement}
\]

No source radius or raw first moment enters. The surplus replaces the bounded concave quadratic test used in the compact log-concave proof.

## 2. Centered mass-to-moment differential

Fix the sites and perturb intercepts from `c` to `c+-s q`. Let `bar{x}_i=B_i/M_i` be the central cell's conditional mean, and let `b_i^-,b_i^+` be the opposite conditional means. Jensen gives `|b_i^--b_i^+|^2<=D_i`. Divide (strong-displacement) by `(2s)^2` and let `s downarrow0`. Finite-cell differentiability gives `gamma_i->M_i`, `(alpha_i-beta_i)/(2s)->-DM_i[q,0]`, and

\[
\frac{b_i^+-b_i^-}{2s}\longrightarrow
\frac{DB_i[q,0]-\bar x_iDM_i[q,0]}{M_i}.
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_i\frac{|DB_i[q,0]-\bar x_i DM_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}
\le\kappa^{-1}\sum_i\frac{|DM_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}.
\tag{centered-MM}
\]

The centering is essential. On Gaussian halfspace cells the raw ratio in the main note is the threshold squared and diverges; (centered-MM) does not assert or imply a bound for that uncentered quantity.

## 3. Conditional covariance bound from convolution log-concavity

We use the classical fact that a strongly log-concave probability has covariance at most `kappa^(-1)I`, including conditioning on a convex set. Here is a derivation from the already imported preservation of log-concavity under convolution.

Let a conditional cell density have unnormalized form `exp(-kappa|x|^2/2)g(x)` with `g` log-concave, including its cell indicator. Define

\[
Z(a)=\int e^{a\cdot x-\kappa|x|^2/2}g(x)dx.
\]

Completing the square gives

\[
Z(a)=e^{|a|^2/(2\kappa)}
\int e^{-\kappa|x-a/\kappa|^2/2}g(x)dx.
\]

The last integral is a Gaussian convolution of `g`, evaluated at `a/kappa`, hence log-concave in `a`. Thus `log Z(a)-|a|^2/(2kappa)` is concave. Quadratic tails justify two derivatives under the integral; its Hessian at zero is `Cov(X)-kappa^(-1)I`. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_i}(x\cdot v)\le |v|^2/\kappa.
\tag{cell-covariance}
\]

This covariance fact is classical, not a claimed new lemma. It is also a linear-function consequence of the Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality: H. J. Brascamp and E. H. Lieb, JFA 22 (1976), 366–389, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(76)90004-5>. The convolution derivation states precisely the premise used here and avoids additional boundary smoothness assumptions for cells.

## 4. Centered adjoint cancellation and finite potential bound

Represent an optimal finite target coupling by masses `m_i>0` and moving sites `y_i(t)=(1-t)y_i^0+t y_i^1`; write `v_i=y_i^1-y_i^0` and `w^2=sum_i m_i|v_i|^2`. Interior sites are distinct as in source 374. Choose intercepts keeping all cell masses equal to `m_i`, and write `r_i=c_i'(t)`. On cell `i`, the potential derivative is `x dot v_i+r_i`. Define

\[
b(t)=\int\partial_tu_t\,d\rho,
\qquad a_i=r_i+\bar x_i\cdot v_i-b(t).
\]

Since `sum_i m_i a_i=0`, the connected cell graph has an intercept direction `q` solving `DM_i[q,0]=m_i a_i`. The face-symmetry adjoint identity is exactly the one from the main note:

\[
\sum_i r_iDM_i[q,0]=-\sum_i v_i\cdot DB_i[q,0].
\]

Subtracting conditional cell means gives

\[
\sum_i m_i a_i^2
=-\sum_i v_i\cdot\bigl(DB_i[q,0]-\bar x_iDM_i[q,0]\bigr).
\]

Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and (centered-MM) imply

\[
\left(\sum_i m_i a_i^2\right)^{1/2}\le w/\sqrt\kappa.
\]

Conditional centering is orthogonal within each cell. Using (cell-covariance),

\[
\|\partial_tu_t-b(t)\|_2^2
=\sum_i m_i\operatorname{Var}_{P_i}(x\cdot v_i)+\sum_i m_i a_i^2
\le 2w^2/\kappa.
\]

Normalize each potential to source mean zero and integrate from `t=0` to `1` by Minkowski. This proves (P-kappa). Endpoint repeated sites are handled on each compact truncation by the original finite-cell endpoint argument; the temporary radius-dependent intercept bound is used only for existence of individual endpoint intercepts, and does not enter (P-kappa).

## 5. Weighted interpolation and one-sided moment bound

The toward-mode interpolation proof requires slice unimodality and finite slice-peak integrals, not compact support as such. On each compact truncation it gives

\[
D^2\le12dF^2/h^2+32AL^2h+20d\max\{\tau_\mu(L),\tau_\nu(L)\}.
\]

With (P-kappa), the same optimal-coupling transfer `max(tau_mu(2L),tau_nu(2L))<=2tau_mu(L)+4w^2` yields

\[
D^2\le\frac{24d}{\kappa}\frac{w^2}{h^2}+128AL^2h+40d\tau_\mu(L)+80dw^2.
\tag{strong-one-sided}
\]

For a `p>2` moment budget, use `tau_mu(L)<=M L^{-(p-2)}` and choose `L=w^(-2/(3p-2))`, `h=w^(2p/(3p-2))` for `0<w<=1`. For `w>1`, use `D<=w+2M^(1/p)`. For a general radial budget use `tau_mu(L)<=M L^2/Phi(L)` and optimize `h`. These are exactly the displayed theorem and profile.

## 6. Source truncation, nonsmooth source limits, and target limits

For a smooth strongly log-concave source, condition on `B(0,R)`. Write `m_R=rho(B(0,R))` and `rho_R=rho 1_{B_R}/m_R`. For all sufficiently large `R`, the support is a compact convex body with interior; strong log-concavity parameter `kappa` is unchanged. Crucially,

\[
A_j(\rho_R)\le A_j(\rho)/m_R.
\]

The finite-potential proof and (strong-one-sided) have no source radius in their constants. Apply them on `rho_R`, where ordinary compact finite-cell calculus is already justified. Strong Gaussian tails give `rho_R->rho` in `W_2`, and `m_R->1`.

First remove finite target restrictions on each truncation: use finite norm-decreasing quantizers of the constrained target, preserving its `p` or `Phi` budget; use any finite `W_2`-convergent quantizers of the arbitrary `P_2` target. Their squared norms are uniformly integrable because their laws converge in `W_2`; no extra moment of the unconstrained target is assumed. Joint graph-plan compactness, optimal-cost convergence and Brenier uniqueness identify the limiting maps over the same source.

Next let `R->infinity` with the targets fixed. Joint laws `(x,T_mu^R(x),T_nu^R(x))` are tight; every limiting pair of transport plans is optimal from the limiting source because both source and target laws converge in `W_2`. Absolute continuity of `rho` and uniqueness identify the common-source joint limit with `(x,T_mu(x),T_nu(x))`. The two target laws are fixed in `P_2`, which makes `|y-z|^2` uniformly integrable under all these joint laws. Therefore squared map distances converge. The bounds on `A_j(rho_R)` pass to `A_j(rho)`.

For a nonsmooth strongly log-concave source, first convolve with the centered Gaussian of covariance `epsilon I`. Completing the square inside the convolution shows the result is `kappa_epsilon=kappa/(1+kappa epsilon)` strongly log-concave: after removing `exp(-kappa_epsilon|x|^2/2)`, the remaining integral is log-concave by Prékopa. It is smooth, positive, converges in `W_2`, and satisfies

\[
A_j(\rho*\gamma_\epsilon)\le A_j(\rho).
\]

The last inequality follows by moving the slice supremum inside the convolution and integrating transverse coordinates. Apply the preceding truncation argument to this smooth source. Then let `epsilon downarrow0`; `kappa_epsilon->kappa`, and fixed-target joint-plan uniqueness/uniform integrability gives the same limit. Thus compact support, full support, and density smoothness are all unnecessary in the stated strongly log-concave source class.

### 6a. Passing the potential bound itself to the full source

The preceding graph-plan argument passes map inequalities directly, but the Gaussian interpolation application also needs (P-kappa) for potentials on the full Gaussian source. This additional passage is elementary for finite targets and must be stated explicitly.

Fix two finite targets, and let `B` bound the norms of all their sites. On every truncation choose both endpoint finite affine maxima to have `rho_R` mean zero. Their global Lipschitz constants are at most `B`; hence

\[
|u_R(0)|\le B\int|x|d\rho_R,\qquad
|u_R(x)|\le B\int|x|d\rho_R+B|x|,
\]

and the same holds for `v_R`. The right side is bounded uniformly for all sufficiently large `R`, because the sources converge in `W_2`. Arzelà–Ascoli supplies a subsequence converging locally uniformly on `R^d` to convex `B`-Lipschitz potentials `u,v`. Their graph plans are the fixed-target optimal limits already identified above; equivalently, local uniform convex convergence identifies their almost-everywhere gradients on the source interior. Thus they are potentials for the prescribed targets. The common quadratic growth bound for `|u_R-v_R|^2`, Gaussian source tails, and `rho_R=rho 1_{B_R}/m_R` justify dominated convergence of the squared potential differences. The source means pass to zero by the same linear bound. Consequently

\[
\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}\,W_2(\mu,\nu)
\]

on the unbounded source. The temporary site bound `B` is used only to justify this limit; it does not enter the inequality. For the smoothing passage, local uniform convergence plus this common quadratic growth bound and `W_2` convergence of the sources gives exactly the same conclusion. This proves the full-source finite-target potential bound required by the Gaussian note, without any target-moment assumption beyond finiteness of the target support.

For completeness, the potential bound also extends to arbitrary `P_2` targets. Finite `W_2` approximations give mean-zero potentials `u_n`; pairing each with the zero potential for `delta_0` in (P-kappa) proves `||u_n||_2<=sqrt(2/kappa)||T_{mu_n}||_2`, so the potentials are uniformly bounded in `L^2(rho)`. Their gradients converge in `L^2(rho)` to the optimal target map by common-source optimal-plan stability and uniform integrability. Extract a weak `L^2(rho)` subsequence. On each compact subset of the source interior, `rho` is bounded below by a positive number, so distributional differentiation passes to the limit using test functions divided by `rho`. The weak limit has gradient `T_mu`, is convex on the source interior (distributional Hessian positivity passes to the limit), and retains mean zero by weak convergence. Applying the same argument simultaneously to the two target approximations and using weak lower semicontinuity proves (P-kappa). No strong convergence of arbitrary-target potentials or extra target moment is needed. The finite-target version alone suffices for all Gaussian map statements, since those map statements are then passed by graph-plan limits.

## 7. Rigorous fixed unbounded-slab lower family

Fix `kappa>0`. Let `X` have density

\[
f(x)=Z_1^{-1}e^{-\kappa x^2/2}1_{[-1,1]}(x),
\qquad Z_1=\int_{-1}^1e^{-\kappa t^2/2}dt,
\]

let `Y~N(0,kappa^(-1))` be independent, and add independent Gaussian coordinates for `d>2`. This fixed source is strongly log-concave on the unbounded convex slab `[-1,1] times R^(d-1)`. Its slice constants are

\[
A_1=Z_1^{-1},\qquad A_j=\sqrt{\kappa/(2\pi)}\ (j>=2).
\]

Write `f_*=f(1)>0` and define the cap function

\[
g(t)=P(X>1-t).
\]

It vanishes for `t<=0`, equals one for `t>=2`, is globally Lipschitz with constant `||f||_infinity`, and satisfies `g(t)/t->f_*` as `t downarrow0`. For each sufficiently small `a>0`, choose `c_a` uniquely with `P(X>c_a)=a`. Then `c_a->1` and `(1-c_a)/a->1/f_*`.

For a real `C`, define `m_a(C)=E g(a(C+Y))`. It is continuous and strictly increasing in `C`, with limits zero and one, so there is a unique `C_a` with `m_a(C_a)=a`. For fixed `C`,

\[
\frac{m_a(C)}a\longrightarrow f_* E(C+Y)_+.
\]

Indeed `0<=g(a(C+Y))/a<=||f||_infinity(|C|+|Y|)`, an integrable dominating function. The limiting function is continuous and strictly increasing from zero to infinity. Let `C_*` be its unique value satisfying `f_* E(C_*+Y)_+=1`. For any fixed `eta>0`, the limiting values at `C_*+-eta` bracket one, hence monotonicity brackets `C_a` there for all small `a`. Therefore `C_a->C_*`, and the sequence is bounded. Put `c'_a=1-a C_a`.

The source cells

\[
A_a=\{X>c_a\},\qquad B_a=\{X>c'_a-aY\}
\]

both have mass exactly `a`. Their symmetric-difference mass is

\[
\rho(A_a\triangle B_a)=E\left|a-g(a(C_a+Y))\right|.
\]

Pointwise, `g(a(C_a+Y))/a -> f_*(C_*+Y)_+`. The bound `1+||f||_infinity(sup_a|C_a|+|Y|)` is integrable. Dominated convergence therefore gives

\[
\frac{\rho(A_a\triangle B_a)}a\longrightarrow
c_*:=E|1-f_*(C_*+Y)_+|>0.
\tag{slab-switch}
\]

Positivity follows, for example, because `P(C_*+Y<=0)>0` and the integrand equals one on that event. This proof includes the rare Gaussian slices where the affine threshold leaves `[-1,1]`; no hidden compact transverse restriction is used.

For arbitrary `L>0`, use the potentials

\[
u(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-c_a)\},
\quad
v(x)=\max\left\{0,\frac{L(x_1+a x_2-c'_a)}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right\}.
\]

Their outlier slopes are `q=L e_1` and `r=L(e_1+a e_2)/sqrt(1+a^2)`, both of norm exactly `L`. Convexity proves the gradients are the Brenier maps to `(1-a)delta_0+a delta_q` and `(1-a)delta_0+a delta_r`. The same two-by-two coupling calculation as in the main note gives

\[
w^2=a|q-r|^2
=2aL^2\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right)\asymp a^3L^2,
\]

\[
D^2=L^2\rho(A_a\triangle B_a)+|q-r|^2\rho(A_a\cap B_a)
\asymp aL^2.
\]

All comparison constants depend only on this fixed source, and hold for sufficiently small `a`, uniformly in `L`. Taking `L=a^(-1/p)` gives both `p` moments exactly one, `w asymp a^((3p-2)/(2p))`, and `D asymp a^((p-2)/(2p))`. This proves sharpness of the exponent on a single fixed unbounded strongly log-concave source. Setting `L=1` proves the compact-target `1/3` exponent there.

For a general radial moment budget, choose `a=min{M/Phi(L),(w/L)^(2/3)}` at sufficiently large `L`. Equation (slab-switch) gives `D^2>=c aL^2`, while the exact target coupling has distance at most `w`. The increasing/decreasing min-max argument in Section 10 of the main note therefore proves the same sharp variational profile for this fixed unbounded source. The second-moment-plus-log and no-modulus endpoints follow with exactly the same target interface.

## Prior comparison and remaining checks

The exact moment/profile comparison remains the one in the main note: Wang-Wang 7 October 2026 gives one-sided `W_2` exponent `(p-2)/(4p-2)` for BV source densities, including this slab; the candidate gives `(p-2)/(3p-2)`. Their broader weighted-reference source class includes disconnected supports and is not subsumed. Ruan's sharp power-log theorem uses objective gap as its input; it is not an exact theorem about target `W_2` perturbation. Source 374 provides the compact uniform-source endpoint mechanism.

Primary comparison checked on 11 October 2026: [Letrouit–Mérigot, arXiv:2411.04908v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04908v3), Theorem 1.4, equations (1.6)–(1.7), and Remark 1.5. Its source has full support, `rho=exp(-U-F)`, `D^2 U>=kappa I`, and bounded `F`; both targets share a compact support. Its potential estimate is `W_1^(1/2)(1+|log W_1|)^(1/2)`. With the additional upper Hessian bound `D^2 U<=kappa' I`, its map exponent is `kappa/(2kappa'+7kappa)`, replaceable by `kappa/(2kappa'+6kappa)-epsilon`. Remark 1.5 discusses extending to unbounded targets but does not state such a theorem. On the pure strongly log-concave subclass (`F=0`), (P-kappa) supplies a target-radius-independent `W_2` Lipschitz potential estimate; the bounded-target conversion `W_2<=sqrt(2L W_1)` also removes the logarithm in its displayed potential modulus on this narrower source subclass. The bounded perturbations allowed by that paper are not covered here.

The classical strong-concavity covariance fact and a possible prior radius-independent potential Lipschitz theorem require a targeted priority check. Neither is called novel here. The newly derived centered cancellation is logically separated from that priority question. Full-support sharp moment optimality is not proved by the slab example and is not required for sharpness in the stated arbitrary-convex-support source class.
