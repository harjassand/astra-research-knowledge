# A fixed cloner has a universal EB surrogate with at most `floor(8d/7)` preparations

Date: 11 October 2026. Status: internal complete proof candidate. The separate investigator `openai_miner_a` reconstructed the gauge invariance, rank bound, compact classes, globally constrained optimization, and two finite-intersection steps without finding a flaw. This is internal evidence, not external validation or a historical-priority claim. The argument uses the finite Schur-class and decision-amplification lemmas proved in `linear_cloning_estimation.md`.

## Strong pointwise representation theorem

Let `A` be any complex Hilbert space of finite dimension `d>=1`, and let `B:A->A tensor A` be any CPTP map. There exists an EB channel

`Lambda_B(rho)=sum_(c=1)^k Tr[M_c rho] P_c`,

where `{M_c}` is a POVM, each `P_c` is pure, and `1<=k<=floor(8d/7)`, such that **every pure input** `P` satisfies

`1-Tr[P Lambda_B(P)] <=2800 [1-Tr[(P tensor P)B(P)]]`.

Thus the quantifiers are `for every finite d, for every B, there exist one finite POVM and one finite prepared-state list, for every pure input P`. The channel and prepared-state list depend only on `B` and `A`; neither depends on an input prior or a chosen ensemble. The representation has `O(d)` outcomes, each given by a `d by d` effect and a pure state. This theorem is existential and supplies no algorithmic bound for finding its continuum-constrained POVM or its globally minimizing representatives.

## 1. A canonical finite partition of all well-cloned pure states

Write

`e(P)=1-Tr[(P tensor P)B(P)]`, `q(P)=1-e(P)`, `tau=1/2800`,

and let `S={P pure:e(P)<=tau}`. This is a compact subset of finite-dimensional pure-state space. If `S` is empty, a one-outcome constant-output EB channel proves the theorem because `2800e(P)>1` for every pure `P`. Assume `S` is nonempty.

For unit representatives `psi` of `P` and `phi` of `Q`, put

`L(P,Q)=<psi,phi> <psi|B^*(|psi tensor psi><phi tensor phi|)|phi>/sqrt(q(P)q(Q))`.

This is the same `C circ F` entry from the finite proof. It is well-defined on rays: replacing `psi` by `exp(i alpha)psi` and `phi` by `exp(i beta)phi` multiplies the first factor by `exp(i(beta-alpha))` and the channel matrix element by `exp(i(alpha-beta))`. The phases cancel. The denominator is bounded away from zero on `S`, so `L` is continuous on `S tensor S`.

The finite Schur lemma uses the same universal constants for every finite subset of `S`:

`delta=1/sqrt(350)`,

`eta=(3/2)[(1-4delta)^(-1/2)-1]<1/5`,

`a=delta+eta<69/280<1/4`.

On every finite subset its zero/one partition mask is uniquely determined by

`Q(P,Q)=1 if and only if Re L(P,Q)>1/2`,

because `|L(P,Q)-Q(P,Q)|<=eta`. Every finite triple therefore satisfies transitivity of this relation. Reflexivity and symmetry also hold, so it defines an equivalence relation on all of `S`.

Every class is compact. Fixing one point `P_0` of a class, it equals

`{P in S:Re L(P_0,P)>1/2}`

`={P in S:Re L(P_0,P)>=1-eta}`.

The second set is closed. Equality follows from the uniform gap: same-class entries have real part at least `1-eta`, and different-class entries have real part at most `eta`.

## 2. A dimension bound on the number of classes

Choose any finite collection of `m` distinct classes and one representative from each. With uniform prior, its weighted Gram density is `G=C/m`, with `rank(G)<=d`. The partition pinching is `G_0=I_m/m`. The finite Schur-to-trace estimate gives

`||G-I_m/m||_1<=a<=1/4`.

If `m>d`, let `Z` project onto the kernel of `G`. Its rank is at least `m-d`, and

`Tr[Z(G-I_m/m)]<=-(m-d)/m`.

For a traceless Hermitian matrix `D`, any projection satisfies `||D||_1>=2|Tr(ZD)|`. Hence

`2(m-d)/m<=a<=1/4`,

which implies `m<=8d/7`. The same conclusion is automatic when `m<=d`. There cannot be infinitely many classes, since otherwise one could choose `m>8d/7`. Their total number `k` therefore satisfies

`k<=floor(8d/7)`.

The stricter reconstructed estimate also yields `k<560d/491`, but the simpler displayed bound suffices.

Enumerate the classes as `S_1,...,S_k`. Since each class is compact and `e(P)` is continuous, choose a representative `P_c in S_c` minimizing `e` over its whole class.

For every `P in S_c`, the weighted pair lemma from the finite proof applies to the pair `P,P_c` and gives

`1-Tr(P P_c)<=25[e(P)+e(P_c)]<=50e(P)`.

This is pointwise and involves the original input's own defect.

## 3. One globally feasible constrained POVM

Let `K` be the set of `k`-outcome POVMs on `A` satisfying

`r_M(P):=1-Tr[M_c P]<=1/4 for every P in S_c and every c`.

The space of all `k`-outcome POVMs is compact and convex. Each displayed constraint is closed and affine, so `K` is compact and convex. It is nonempty by the finite intersection property: every finite collection of constraints concerns a finite good family, and the finite uniform class-measurement lemma supplies a POVM satisfying it. Missing global classes can be given zero effects; the finite family's labels retain exactly the same classes by the threshold definition. Thus every finite collection of constraints is feasible in the same compact `k`-outcome POVM space, and all constraints are simultaneously feasible.

## 4. Amplification stays inside all global constraints

Take any finitely supported prior on `S`, and minimize its average wrong-class error `R` over `K`. An optimizer `M` exists. Let `E` denote the prior-average cloning defect.

Use `B` twice to make three approximate copies, measure `M` on each, and output a repeated class when one exists; use any fixed class when all three outcomes differ. The finite proof gives, now for **every** `P in S`,

`r_(M')(P)<=(sqrt(3)r_M(P)+sqrt(8e(P)))^2`.

The inequality uses only the original pointwise defect, and not membership in the finite support of the chosen prior. Since `r_M(P)<=1/4` and `e(P)<=tau` everywhere on `S`,

`sqrt(3)r_M(P)+sqrt(8e(P))<=sqrt(3)/4+1/sqrt(350)<1/2`.

Consequently `M'` belongs to the same globally constrained set `K`. Optimality and the finite prior's scalar second-moment estimate give

`R<=(sqrt(3R/4)+sqrt(8E))^2`,

`R<=(224+128sqrt(3))E`.

No uniformity is inferred by optimizing an average alone: the global error cap is explicitly imposed before optimizing, and the majority operation preserves it on the entire good set.

## 5. Simultaneous pointwise decision and preparation

Put `C_dec=224+128sqrt(3)`. For any finite list `P_1,...,P_m` from `S`, the preceding bound holds for every prior on that list, with the optimizer constrained to the same compact convex set `K`. Finite minimax therefore supplies one `M in K` with

`r_M(P_j)<=C_dec e(P_j)` for all j.

Each required inequality is closed in `K`. The finite intersection property gives one fixed `M in K` with

`r_M(P)<=C_dec e(P)` for every `P in S`.

On outcome `c`, prepare the globally minimizing representative `P_c`. For `P in S_c`, affinity of pure-target infidelity in the prepared mixture gives

`1-Tr[P Lambda_B(P)]<=r_M(P)+[1-Tr(P P_c)]`

`                       <=(274+128sqrt(3))e(P)<500e(P)`.

For `P` outside `S`, its loss is at most one, whereas `e(P)>1/2800`. Hence its loss is at most `2800e(P)`. Combining both cases proves the theorem, with at most `floor(8d/7)` pure prepared states.
