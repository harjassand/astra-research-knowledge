## 1. A complete geometric classification under a group gap

Let G be a compact Lie group, and let `ρ:G→O(V)` be a smooth orthogonal action on a finite-dimensional real space V. Define

\[
 k=\min_{x\ne0}\dim(Gx).
\]

Assume `k>0`. Let ν be a fixed finite gate law whose averaging operator on the full group regular representation has absolute mean-zero L² norm at most `λ<1`. For arbitrary gate-aware row-stochastic m-state kernels define `F_m(T)` and `ρ_m` exactly as in `RESULT.md`, equation (6) and Section 4.

**Minimum-orbit theorem.** There are positive constants `c,C,m_0`, depending only on the action and ν, such that

\[
 F_m(T)\le2\exp[-cT/m^{2/k}]
 \quad(m\ge1,T\ge0),
 \tag{R1}
\]

and for every `m≥m_0` there is one stationary positive lift with

\[
 F_m(T)\ge(1-Cm^{-2/k})^T
 \quad(T\ge0).
 \tag{R2}
\]

In particular,

\[
 1-\rho_m=\Theta_{G,V,\nu}(m^{-2/k}).
 \tag{R3}
\]

This is a classification of the exponent by the smallest orbit, rather than by ambient vector-space dimension or by the generic orbit. The action need not be irreducible. The matching lift can live in the invariant linear span of one minimum-dimensional orbit.

The full regular group gap is a hypothesis. A gap only on linear functions in V does not suffice. Nor is a finite-group action a positive-k example: every finite-group orbit has dimension zero, and a finite orbit itself supplies a lossless permutation lift.

### 1.1 Uniform caps follow from the minimum differential rank

Fix an invariant Riemannian metric on G and an inner product on its Lie algebra `𝔤`; write `n=dim G`. For a unit vector x define

\[
 L_x:\mathfrak g\to V,\qquad L_x\xi=d\rho(\xi)x.
\]

Its rank is `dim(Gx)≥k`. The kth largest singular value is continuous in x, so compactness of the unit sphere gives

\[
 \sigma_*:=\min_{\|x\|=1}\sigma_k(L_x)>0.
 \tag{R4}
\]

The orbit map `f_x(g)=ρ(g)x` has the same singular values at every g, using the chart `g exp ξ`. Its first and second derivatives have uniform bounds on a fixed sufficiently small exponential neighborhood, for all unit x, all g, and all orthonormal choices of Lie algebra coordinates. Haar density in these charts is also uniformly bounded.

Here are the local measure details. At any chart center g and unit x, choose the first k input coordinates along the top k right singular vectors of `L_x`. Project V orthogonally onto their k-dimensional image after multiplying by `ρ(g)`. At the center the resulting k-by-k derivative A has smallest singular value at least `σ_*`. Choose a uniform coordinate-cube radius η so small that throughout the cube this derivative differs from A in operator norm by at most `σ_*/4`. Uniform second derivative bounds permit this choice. Keep the other `n−k` coordinates fixed. For two points s,t in the first k coordinates, integrating the derivative along their line segment gives

\[
 \|F(s)-F(t)-A(s-t)\|
 \le(\sigma_*/4)\|s-t\|,
\]

so `F` is injective there and its Jacobian determinant is at least `(3σ_*/4)^k` in absolute value. If the original orbit map lies within b of a specified y, its projection lies in the radius-b Euclidean ball in `R^k`. Change of variables bounds the k-dimensional measure of this preimage by

\[
 (4/(3\sigma_*))^k\,\omega_k b^k.
\]

Integrating the other `n−k` coordinates and multiplying by the uniform Haar-density bound gives a constant times `b^k` on this cube.

Each such cube contains a metric ball of a uniform positive radius. A compact metric-ball cover of G can therefore be chosen with a bounded number of centers independent of x; the singular-vector coordinates and output projection may be chosen separately at each center. Equivalently, a maximal separated set and the positive Haar mass of a sufficiently small metric ball give a uniform finite covering bound. Summing the local estimates proves

\[
 \Pr_{g\sim dg}(\|\rho(g)x-y\|\le b)
 \le\min\{1,C_{G,V} b^k\}
 \quad(\|x\|=1).
 \tag{R5}
\]

Homogeneity gives `C_{G,V}(b/||x||)^k` for arbitrary nonzero x. All estimates hold for arbitrary cap centers y, including centers outside the orbit. This closes the abstract cap hypothesis of `RESULT.md` without assuming a fixed stabilizer type or using a generic orbit dimension. Applying its transfer theorem proves (R1).

The constants in this argument can be specified from `σ_*`, uniform second derivative and chart-density bounds, and a finite metric-ball covering number. Acquiring good bounds for these quantities in a varying family of representations is a separate computational problem; it is not being charged as a free oracle.

### 1.2 A minimum orbit gives the matching positive polytope

Choose a unit `x_*` whose orbit `M=Gx_*` has dimension k. Let

\[
 W=\operatorname{span}M,\qquad K=\operatorname{conv}M\subset W.
\]

W is invariant. Since `k>0` excludes every nonzero fixed vector, the Haar mean of `ρ(g)x_*` is zero. The origin lies in the relative interior of K: if a nonzero `H∈W` supported K at zero, then the continuous nonnegative function `〈H,ρ(g)x_*〉` would have integral zero, hence vanish everywhere, contradicting `W=span M`. Thus some `r>0` satisfies

\[
 rB_W\subset K\subset B_W.
 \tag{R6}
\]

M is a compact smooth homogeneous manifold of dimension k. For all sufficiently small δ it has a geodesic δ-net `{v_1,…,v_N}` with `N≤C_Mδ^{-k}`. This follows from finitely many bounded coordinate charts, or disjoint small metric balls and a uniform lower bound on their volume. Each connected component can be covered; there are finitely many components.

Let `B_M` bound the Euclidean norm of the second fundamental form on unit tangent vectors. For any `H∈W`, choose a maximizer v of `〈H,·〉` on M and a net point within geodesic distance δ. Along a shortest geodesic the first derivative at v is zero and the second derivative has absolute value at most `B_M||H||`. Therefore the net polytope `L=conv{v_i}` satisfies

\[
 h_L(H)\ge h_K(H)-(B_M/2)\|H\|\delta^2
 \ge\left(1-\frac{B_M\delta^2}{2r}\right)h_K(H).
\]

Set `α=1−B_Mδ²/(2r)>0`. Support functions give

\[
 \alpha K\subset L\subset K.
 \tag{R7}
\]

For each gate g and each i, `αρ(g)v_i∈L`. Choose stochastic barycentric rows with

\[
 \sum_jP(g)_{ij}v_j=\alpha\rho(g)v_i.
 \tag{R8}
\]

The dual positive-lift operator then has the nonzero vector family `{v_i}` as an eigenvector with eigenvalue α. A finite alphabet requires only finitely many barycentric problems. Taking `δ=(C_M/m)^{1/k}` for sufficiently large m gives at most m vertices and `α≥1−Cm^{-2/k}`. Padding unused states yields (R2), and (R1)–(R2) prove (R3).

The upper exponent uses ordinary smooth convex approximation. Its role is to match the all-controller lower bound; no novelty of net approximation or barycentric interpolation is claimed.

## 2. A new operation: the coherent SU(2) product, with exponent 3/2

Represent a unit quaternion `q=(q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3)∈S^3` by

\[
 U(q)=q_0I-i(q_1\sigma_x+q_2\sigma_y+q_3\sigma_z)\in SU(2).
\]

Quaternion multiplication agrees with matrix multiplication. Left multiplication is an orthogonal action on `R^4`, and Haar SU(2) acts transitively on `S^3`. Thus `D=4` and `k=3`, rather than the two-dimensional adjoint/Bloch orbit of a bare qubit.

Use the fixed five-letter algebraic alphabet

\[
 \mathcal A=\{I,U_x,U_x^{-1},U_z,U_z^{-1}\},\quad
 U_x=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2&-i\\-i&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 U_z=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2-i&0\\0&2+i\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{R9}
\]

Identify these matrices with their unit quaternions when writing their action on `R^4`. Their density and imported full regular gap are the same established SU(2) facts credited in `RESULT.md`, Section 8, and Astra CC, Section 7. Let ν be uniform, and `β=1−λ²>0`.

Consider the single-terminal-query process

\[
 p_*(+\mid q,g_1,\ldots,g_t,u)
 =\frac{1+\tfrac12\langle u,g_t\cdots g_1q\rangle}{2},
 \qquad q,u\in S^3,\quad t\le T.
 \tag{R10}
\]

The simulator has arbitrary preparation distributions, arbitrary time-dependent gate-aware stochastic kernels, and arbitrary time/deadline/query-dependent terminal responses. Its only persistent record of past inputs is its m-valued state; clock and deadline are supplied and fresh independent randomness is allowed. A stored word, accumulated quaternion, preparation label, or correlated seed outside this state changes the resource contract.

**Controlled-product theorem.** Uniformly for `0≤ε≤1/32`,

\[
 m_*(T;\epsilon)=\Theta_\nu((T+1)^{3/2}),\qquad
 \log_2m_*(T;\epsilon)=\tfrac32\log_2(T+1)+O_\nu(1).
 \tag{R11}
\]

The upper bound has zero probability error. The lower bound needs only the eight quaternion preparations `q=±e_0,…,±e_3` and the one fixed query `u=e_0` at time T. The quantum realization below uses two qubits, algebraic controlled gates, eight explicit full-rank preparation states, and one fixed binary measurement.

An exact prefix reduction, contributed by the root worker and proved in Section 2.4, strengthens the operational statement: enlarge the alphabet by the eight quaternion units. Then **one fixed preparation and one fixed measurement** already have the sharp state law (R11). The quaternion-unit prefix supplies the finite preparations internally as gate words.

### 2.1 An explicit sphere cap constant

For `x≠0`, the orbit of x is the radius-`||x||` three-sphere. Write `η=b/||x||`. For `0<η<1`, any radius-η Euclidean ball intersected with the unit three-sphere is contained in a spherical cap of angular radius at most `arcsin η`. To verify this, put its center at distance a from the origin. The cap threshold is `(1+a²−η²)/(2a)≥√(1−η²)` by minimizing over `a>0`; a zero center gives an empty cap for η<1.

The normalized volume of a cap of angular radius θ on `S^3` is

\[
 \frac2\pi\int_0^\theta\sin^2s\,ds
 =\frac{\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\pi}
 \le\frac{2\theta^3}{3\pi}.
\]

For `0≤η<1`, `arcsin η≤πη/2`, so this is at most `(π²/12)η³≤η³`. For `η≥1`, probability at most one is enough, including the case of a radius-one ball centered at the origin. Hence the abstract cap hypothesis holds with `C=1,k=3,D=4`. The explicit transfer constant can be taken as

\[
 A=4096\cdot4\cdot64^{2/3}=262144,\qquad
 c_\nu=\frac{\beta\log2}{262145},
\]

and

\[
 F_m(T)\le2e^{-c_\nu T/m^{2/3}}.
 \tag{R12}
\]

### 2.2 Eight preparations and one readout prove the lower bound

Choose `X_0` uniformly on the eight signed coordinate vectors in `R^4` and generate independent ν gates. For every fixed gate word, `E[X_TX_T^T]=I_4/4`. Put `f=〈e_0,X_T〉`; then `E f²=1/4` and `|f|≤1`.

For a terminal outcome `Y∈{−1,+1}`, (R10) has conditional mean `f/2`, so

\[
 \mathbb E[Yf]=1/8.
\]

Uniform absolute probability error at most `1/32` changes this score by at most `1/16`, leaving score at least `1/16`. Since the fixed-query terminal readout depends only on `S_T`,

\[
 \frac1{16}\le\sum_i\|\mathbb E[X_T1_{S_T=i}]\|
 \le F_m(T).
\]

Combining this with (R12) yields

\[
 m\ge\left(\frac{c_\nu T}{\log32}\right)^{3/2}.
 \tag{R13}
\]

No affine preparation assumption or regularity of the simulator is used.

### 2.3 A rational positive compiler gives the exact upper bound

Set

\[
 n=\lceil\sqrt{3(T+1)}\rceil,
 \qquad \alpha=1-\frac3{2n^2}.
\]

For integers `a=(a_1,a_2,a_3)` with `−n≤a_j≤n`, use both signs of

\[
 u_a=\frac{(2na_1,2na_2,2na_3,n^2-\|a\|^2)}{n^2+\|a\|^2}.
 \tag{R14}

\[
 N=2(2n+1)^3=O((T+1)^{3/2})

vertices. The stereographic map has differential operator norm `2/(1+||s||²)≤2`. A point on the upper hemisphere has inverse coordinates in `[-1,1]^3`; rounding them to the `1/n` grid changes the parameters by at most `√3/(2n)` and the sphere point by at most `√3/n`. The opposite sign chart covers the lower hemisphere. Thus this is a chord `√3/n` net.

Let `L=conv{u_i}`. Every unit support direction has a net point with inner product at least `1−3/(2n²)=α`, hence

\[
 \alpha B^4\subset L\subset B^4.

Define `w_i=u_i/α`; their convex hull contains the unit ball. Encode q by barycentric coordinates in that inflated hull. For each alphabet gate, choose row-stochastic weights such that

\[
 \sum_jP(g)_{ij}w_j=\alpha g w_i.
 \tag{R16}

Such rows exist because `αg u_i∈αB^4⊂L`. After t gates, `E[w_{S_t}]=α^tG_tq`. Use terminal probability

 h_{t,u}(i)=\frac{1+\tfrac12\alpha^{-t}\langle u,w_i\rangle}{2}.

### 2.4 The two-qubit experiment and the measured quantity

For `δ=1/2`, define the four-dimensional density matrix and binary effect

\[
 \varrho_q=\frac14
 \begin{pmatrix}I_2&\delta U(q)^*\\\delta U(q)&I_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 E_u=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}I_2&U(u)^*\\U(u)&I_2\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{R18}
\]

The density matrix eigenvalues are `(1±δ)/4`, each twice, so these are full-rank physical preparations. Direct multiplication gives `E_u²=E_u`, making it a valid projective binary effect. A gate is the controlled unitary

\[
 C(g)=\operatorname{diag}(I_2,U(g)),
\]

which sends `ϱ_q` to `ϱ_{gq}`. The Born probability is

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(E_u\varrho_{G_tq})
 =\frac12+\frac\delta4\operatorname{ReTr}(U(u)^*U(G_tq))
 =\frac{1+\delta\langle u,G_tq\rangle}{2}.
 \tag{R19}
\]

These controlled matrices are explicit members of the supplied finite gate library. Controlled access is not inferred from an unknown black-box channel or acquired from samples.

For the fixed query `u=e_0`, this measures the real normalized trace of the coherent product `U(g_t)⋯U(g_1)U(q)`. The eight required preparations correspond to `±I` and `±iσ_x,±iσ_y,±iσ_z`, so they are completely explicit algebraic states. The effect is the fixed `|+〉〈+|` measurement on the control qubit, tensored with target identity.

The distinction from the adjoint/Bloch task is operational: `U` and `−U` are distinguishable in this controlled interferometer. Its active coherence representation is quaternionic left multiplication with orbit dimension three; the adjoint conjugation representation identifies those phases and has orbit dimension two. Applying a dense-SU(4) theorem to the entire four-dimensional register would also be incorrect here: these controlled gates lie in a proper subgroup with invariant diagonal blocks. The transfer is applied to the four-dimensional active coherence space, which has no fixed vectors.

This is an exact streaming probability-simulation result for a fixed-size interferometer. It is not a DQC1 hardness theorem or a general time advantage.

For uniform absolute error `0≤ε≤1/32` on all words of length at most H, its optimal classical m-state streaming simulation satisfies

 m_{\mathrm{fixed}}(H;\epsilon)

To prove the lower bound, for `H≥2` restrict to words whose first gate q is in Q₈ and whose remaining `T=H−1` gates lie in 𝒜.  m\ge\left(\frac{c_\nu(H-1)}{\log32}\right)^{3/2}.

**A full-rank qutrit implementation, with minimal quantum dimension.** The same probability process also has a three-level implementation. Let `ψ_q=U(q)e_1∈C²` and

### 2.5 Costs, varying memory, and numerical range

\[
 O(|\mathcal A|N^6\operatorname{poly}(\log(T+2)))
\]

bit operations and `O(|𝒜|Nlog(T+2))` sparse output bits. These costs are included in the implementation, separately from the persistent state register. Arbitrary explicit preparation/query parameters require their own input description and barycentric/evaluation costs.

Exact sampling of algebraic rows uses independent fair bits and certified threshold intervals, with expected polynomial arithmetic cost and unbounded refinement. Gate/preparation acquisition, physical precision, and noise costs remain outside the abstract state-count comparison. If the implemented controlled gates have operator errors `η_t`, telescoping bounds terminal probability error by `Σ_tη_t`.

The general occupation argument in `RESULT.md`, Section 11, gives for every variable state allocation capable of the same terminal score

\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log_2m_t
 \ge\tfrac32\log_2(T!)-O_\nu(T)

 \tag{R20}
\]

The exact upper construction matches the leading coefficient. It follows by taking retained terminal norm `r_0=1/16`, `k=3,D=4,C=1` in that argument.

Likewise, every positive exactly unbiased stochastic interpolation of coherent quaternion products on arbitrary m-point time-varying grids must spend numerical range

\[
 B_T/B_0\ge\tfrac12\exp[c_\nu T/m^{2/3}],

with a matching `exp[O(T/m^{2/3})]` construction from a fixed sphere net, once m is sufficiently large. This covers all positive stochastic interpolators, including time-varying irregular grids, and counts rare extreme coordinates. It asserts no variance or arbitrary signed-arithmetic lower bound.

## 3. Why two apparent alternatives do not contradict the operation

The primary-source [Montina correlated-noise model](https://arxiv.org/html/1108.5138v2) reproduces qubit unitary evolution using four visible states and a continuous time-correlated noise trajectory. Its equations (3)–(4) rotate the noise vectors by the accumulated unitary; the paper explicitly notes that evaluating a noise realization requires solving the Schrödinger evolution. Such gate-history-dependent correlated noise is an additional persistent resource. The model demonstrates why the fresh-independent-randomness clause matters.

The 2026 primary-source [strict-cutpoint automata conversion](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07058v1), Theorem 3.2, preserves the sign of the acceptance margin with finitely many states. Its equations (2)–(3) and final readout give a margin proportional to `(CN)^{−|w|}(f_G(w)−λ)`. Thus it supplies threshold-language equivalence while exponentially attenuating probability differences. The present operation requires a fixed positive visibility and uniform absolute probability approximation. This concrete attenuation formula identifies the interface distinction; no correctness of unrelated claims in that preprint is required here.

The matching polytope construction is standard smooth geometry. The substantive candidate is the full transfer: the minimum orbit dimension controls the optimal positive-composition gap for arbitrary stochastic encoders, and that gap controls an exact observable interferometric operation and its varying-memory resource integral.
