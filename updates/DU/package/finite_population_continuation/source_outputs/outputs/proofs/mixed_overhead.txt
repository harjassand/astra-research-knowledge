# Mixed states retain measurement overhead at arbitrarily small cloning defect

11 October 2026. Complete internal analytic counterexample. This concerns the representation by one isometry, one fixed projective measurement, and conditional preparation; it does not lower-bound arbitrary adaptive apparatuses or an uncharged random selection among many measurement settings.

## Statement

Fix any finite reconstruction constant `K>0`. Choose an integer `q>2K+1` and any integer `b>=1`. Put

`n=b(q+1)`, `D=b(2q+1)`.

There is an explicit family of `n` mixed states in dimension `D` and explicit EB product cloners `B_w`, parameterized by `w>0`, such that

`max_a [1-F(B_w(rho_a(w)),rho_a(w)^tensor2)] <=epsilon_w=w/(q+1) ->0`,

but, for all sufficiently small positive `w`, every measure-and-prepare channel with

`1-F(T(rho_a(w)),rho_a(w))<=K epsilon_w` for all `a`

requires Naimark measurement dimension

`N>=D+b`.

Thus the necessary excess is at least `D/(2q+1)` even while `D epsilon_w->0`. In particular, the pure-state vanishing-overhead statement `N-D=O(D epsilon)` and its zero-ancilla basis regime cannot extend to unrestricted mixed states with any fixed universal constant. A one-qubit upper bound remains compatible with this example.

The phrase "for all sufficiently small" is established by compactness below; an explicit numerical threshold `w0(q,b,K)` is not derived.

## 1. States and cloner

Let the input space be the orthogonal direct sum of an `n`-dimensional tag space with `b` separate `q`-dimensional minority spaces. In minority block `g`, choose a regular real simplex of `q+1` unit vectors `psi_(g,c)` with

`<psi_(g,c),psi_(g,j)>=-1/q` for `c!=j`.

Vectors from different minority blocks are orthogonal. Write `P_a=|psi_a><psi_a|` and `C_a=|a><a|` for the corresponding orthogonal tag projector, where `a=(g,c)`. Define

`rho_a(w)=(1-w)C_a+wP_a`, `0<w<1`.

The simplex tight-frame identity is `sum_(c=1)^(q+1)P_(g,c)=((q+1)/q)I_g`. Therefore

`E_a=C_a+(q/(q+1))P_a`

form a POVM on the whole input space. The explicit EB cloner is

`B_w(X)=sum_a tr(E_a X) rho_a(w)^tensor2`.

On input `rho_a(w)`, the correct preparation occurs with probability

`(1-w)+w q/(q+1)=1-w/(q+1)`.

Separate concavity of squared fidelity consequently gives

`F(B_w(rho_a(w)),rho_a(w)^tensor2)>=1-w/(q+1)`.

No claim that this cloner is optimal is needed.

## 2. Support tests impose a minority reconstruction requirement

Let `Pi_a=C_a+P_a`, the support projector of `rho_a(w)`. If `T_w` meets the claimed loss bound, fidelity monotonicity under the binary support test implies

`tr Pi_a T_w(rho_a(w))>=1-Kw/(q+1)`.

By linearity and positivity,

`tr(I-Pi_a)T_w(rho_a(w))`

`=(1-w)tr(I-Pi_a)T_w(C_a)+w tr(I-Pi_a)T_w(P_a)`

`>=w tr(I-Pi_a)T_w(P_a)`.

Thus every such surrogate must satisfy

`tr Pi_a T_w(P_a)>=1-K/(q+1)` for every `a`. (2.1)

For different labels,

`||Pi_a+Pi_j||_infinity<=1+1/q`. (2.2)

Within a minority block this is the two-rank-one-projector norm `1+|<psi_a,psi_j>|`; the tag projectors contribute eigenvalue `1`. Across blocks the supports are orthogonal and the norm is `1`.

## 3. The compact limiting measurement

Suppose there are arbitrarily small positive `w` with a surrogate using measurement dimension `N<D+b`. By padding codomains and zero outcomes, it suffices to set the common dimension `N=D+b-1`.

Write each channel in the architecture

`T_w(X)=sum_y tr[Q_(w,y) U_w X U_w^*] sigma_(w,y)`,

where `U_w:C^D->C^N` is an isometry, the `Q_(w,y)` form a PVM, and the conditional preparations `sigma_(w,y)` are density matrices on `C^D`. At most `N` PVM outcomes are nonzero; pad by zero projections to use exactly `N` indexed outcomes. Isometries, such indexed PVMs, and density matrices belong to compact sets. Take a subsequence converging to `U_0,Q_(0,y),sigma_(0,y)` and hence to a channel `T_0`.

Because `rho_a(w)->C_a` and the allowed fidelity loss tends to zero,

`T_0(C_a)=C_a` for every `a`. (3.1)

Set `v_a=U_0|a>`. These are `n` orthonormal vectors in the measurement space. A convex combination of density matrices equals the pure state `C_a` only if every preparation with positive coefficient is `C_a`. Consequently, whenever `Q_(0,y)v_a` is nonzero, its preparation is exactly `C_a`.

Merge all outcomes whose preparation equals the same `C_a`, obtaining a projection `F_a`. Then

`F_a v_a=v_a`, `F_a v_j=0` for `j!=a`.

The `F_a` are mutually orthogonal, and every other outcome projection annihilates all `v_a`. Let `V_tag=span{v_a}`, of dimension `n`. Each merged projection splits as

`F_a=|v_a><v_a|+R_a`,

where `R_a` is a projection on `V_tag^perp`. Together the nonzero `R_a` and the other outcome projections form a PVM on `V_tag^perp`, whose dimension is `N-n`.

The embedded minority input space `U_0(direct_sum_g C^q)` lies in `V_tag^perp`. Hence the restriction of `T_0` to minority inputs is a measure-and-prepare channel using at most `N-n` effective prepared states. Some of these may be tag states; that does not affect the count. Compression of the residual PVM to the minority input space can make its effects nonprojective, but cannot increase its number of effective outcomes/preparations.

## 4. Pigeonhole contradiction

Taking the limit in (2.1),

`tr Pi_a T_0(P_a)>=1-K/(q+1)` for all `n` labels.

For each input choose a residual preparation `sigma_y` with

`tr Pi_a sigma_y>=1-K/(q+1)`;

such a preparation exists because the output expectation is a convex combination of these expectations. If there are fewer than `n` residual preparations, two different labels share the same chosen preparation. Equations (2.2) and positivity then imply

`2(1-K/(q+1))<=tr[sigma_y(Pi_a+Pi_j)]<=1+1/q`.

This is impossible because `q>2K+1` implies

`K/(q+1)<(1-1/q)/2`.

Therefore the residual measurement must have at least `n` effective preparations, so

`N-n>=n`, `N>=2n=D+b`,

contradicting the assumption. No arbitrarily small violating sequence exists; this proves the claimed sufficiently-small-`w` lower bound.

## Interpretation

The difficult quantum sector has arbitrarily small weight. Its error budget shrinks by the same weight, while the limiting measurement must simultaneously preserve all classical tags exactly. The classical tags occupy measurement directions that cannot be reused to resolve the minority simplex. This is why a fixed amount of extra measurement space survives even when the global product-cloning defect tends to zero.

The construction itself is explicit and uses only supplied states and an explicit EB cloner. The lower-bound threshold in `w` is existential. Arbitrary mixed output preparations are included in the argument, and there is no assumption that the surrogate prepares the input representatives or identifies their labels exactly.
