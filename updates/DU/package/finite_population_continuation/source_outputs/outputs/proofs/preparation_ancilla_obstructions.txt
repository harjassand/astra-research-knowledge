# Preparation-count and measurement-ancilla obstructions

Date: 11 October 2026. Status: internally reconstructed complete counterexamples. The direct-sum simplex construction was independently derived by the parent investigator; the harmonic-frame construction below was independently derived by the obstruction investigator. Neither invokes optimality of a cloner. These results concern representations and a precisely specified measurement architecture.

## 1. A general finite preparation-cover obstruction

Suppose pure targets `P_1,...,P_n` have pairwise overlaps `|<psi_i,psi_j>|<=gamma<1`. If an EB channel represented using `m` distinct prepared density matrices satisfies

`1-Tr[P_i Lambda(P_i)]<=epsilon<(1-gamma)/2` for every i,

then `m>=n`.

Proof: write `Lambda(rho)=sum_(y=1)^m Tr[M_y rho] sigma_y`, merging identical prepared states if needed. The output target fidelity is a convex average of the numbers `Tr(P_i sigma_y)`, so at least one prepared state has `Tr(P_i sigma_y)>=1-epsilon`. Associate one such prepared state to each target. If `m<n`, two targets `i!=j` share a prepared state `sigma_y`. But

`Tr[sigma_y(P_i+P_j)]<=||P_i+P_j||_infinity=1+|<psi_i,psi_j>|<=1+gamma`,

whereas their two fidelity bounds give a sum at least `2(1-epsilon)>1+gamma`, a contradiction.

The prepared states may be mixed, and there is no purity assumption on `sigma_y`. Thus free classical randomization after a measurement outcome does not evade the result: replace all randomized outputs conditional on that outcome by their average density matrix. The bound concerns the number of effective conditional preparations, and therefore also lower bounds the number of measurement outcomes in any finite measure-and-prepare representation.

## 2. An isometric simplex cloner with exact defect `1/q`

In dimension `q>=2`, choose a regular simplex of `q+1` real unit vectors with

`<psi_i,psi_j>=-1/q` for `i!=j`.

Set `t_i=psi_i tensor psi_i` and `mu=(1/(q+1))sum_i t_i`. Then

`<t_i,t_j>=1/q^2` for `i!=j`,

`||mu||^2=<t_i,mu>=1/q`.

The centered target vectors

`x_i=(t_i-mu)/sqrt(1-1/q)`

have unit norm and satisfy `<x_i,x_j>=-1/q` for `i!=j`. They therefore have exactly the same Gram matrix as the input simplex. An isometry `U:C^q->C^q tensor C^q` sends each `psi_i` to `x_i`. Its global cloning fidelity on every simplex state is

`|<t_i,x_i>|^2=1-1/q`.

Thus the physical isometric cloner `B(rho)=U rho U^*` has exact individual global defect

`e_B(P_i)=1/q`.

No claim that this cloner is optimal is needed.

Take an orthogonal direct sum of `b` such blocks, so `d=bq`. Send each block through its simplex isometry, with output supported on the corresponding block squared. Distinct output blocks are orthogonal, so the direct sum map is again an isometry into `C^d tensor C^d`. The target list has

`n=b(q+1)=d+b=d+d/q`

states, every individual cloning defect is `epsilon=1/q`, and all distinct overlaps have modulus at most `gamma=1/q` (they are zero across blocks).

Fix any finite constant `K>0`. If `q>2K+1`, then

`K/q<(1-1/q)/2`.

The preparation-cover obstruction therefore shows that **every** EB channel obeying the pointwise reconstruction requirement

`1-Tr[P_i Lambda(P_i)]<=K e_B(P_i)=K/q`

on this family needs at least

`n=d+d epsilon`

distinct effective prepared states, hence at least that many outcomes. This remains true for arbitrarily small positive `epsilon=1/q`, and arbitrarily large multiples `d=bq`.

For the candidate's universal constant `K=2800`, choose any integer `q>5601`; then `epsilon<1/2800`, so the example lies inside its well-cloned regime.

## 3. Why the overhead order `d epsilon` is necessary

For a fixed cloner and any pure family whose individual defects are at most `epsilon<=1/2800`, the global class construction supplies a POVM with pointwise wrong-class probability

`r(P)<=(224+128sqrt(3)) e_B(P)`.

Write `C_dec=224+128sqrt(3)`. Choose the minimum-defect representative of each class. Since `Tr(M_c P_c)<=Tr(M_c)` and the effects sum to identity,

`k-C_dec sum_c e_B(P_c)<=sum_c Tr(M_c P_c)<=d`.

Thus `k(1-C_dec epsilon)<=d`, or

`k<=d/(1-C_dec epsilon)`.

The denominator is positive throughout the stated regime. Polar compression of this class POVM, independently developed in `../independent_inventor`, gives a rank-one POVM with

`N=max(d,k)`,

`N-d<=C_dec d epsilon/(1-C_dec epsilon)`.

Its pointwise pure-target infidelity on the promised family is less than `2500e_B(P)`. The direct-sum simplex example proves that for any fixed finite pointwise constant the excess cannot, in general, be smaller than `d epsilon`. Therefore the **order** of the worst-case extra measurement dimensions is `d epsilon`; the multiplicative constants are not sharp.

This family-specific overhead statement has different quantifiers from the all-pure surrogate theorem. Restricting construction to the good set at threshold `epsilon` guarantees the reconstruction bound on that set. It does not automatically retain the all-pure constant `2800` on states whose defects lie between `epsilon` and `1/2800`.

If `C_dec d epsilon/(1-C_dec epsilon)<1`, integrality forces `N=d`, so a von Neumann basis measurement suffices for the promised family. The sufficient crossover constant is not claimed optimal.

## 4. Exact one-qubit measurement-ancilla contract

Consider this architecture only: attach one fixed pure ancilla of dimension `a` to the `d`-dimensional input; apply an arbitrary unitary; perform one orthogonal projective measurement on the combined `da`-dimensional system; and prepare an output state controlled by its classical outcome. The charge here is the additional coherent ancilla present at the measurement stage. Arbitrary unitary synthesis, classical outcome storage, and conditional output preparation are separately charged and are not asserted to be dimension independent.

An ancilla-free instance (`a=1`) has at most `d` nonzero orthogonal measurement outcomes. Even if it uses classical randomization afterward, it has at most `d` effective conditional output density matrices. The direct-sum simplex example therefore rules out every finite dimension-independent pointwise constant for this ancilla-free architecture.

Conversely, the candidate's global all-pure theorem, together with the independently reconstructed polar compression, gives a rank-one POVM with `N<=floor(8d/7)<=2d`. Its isometry into `C^N` embeds into `C^d tensor C^2`; complete that isometry to a unitary on the latter space and measure its basis. The remaining `2d-N` outcomes have zero probability on the initialized input subspace. Therefore **one ancillary qubit suffices**, and some cloners require a nontrivial ancilla, within this architecture.

This is a worst-case statement about the specified single-projective-measurement realization. It is not a lower bound on every conceivable adaptive instrument, on apparatus degrees of freedom, on total laboratory memory, on circuit depth, or on the resources needed to acquire the supplied cloner.

## 5. A general constant-versus-preparation-rate tradeoff from harmonic frames

The following separate construction quantifies excess preparations without an optimal-cloner premise. Let `n=d+r` and `omega=exp(2 pi i/n)`. Define `n` vectors in `C^d` by

`psi_j=(1/sqrt(d))(omega^(ell j))_(ell=0,...,d-1)`, `j=0,...,n-1`.

Fourier orthogonality gives `sum_j P_j=(n/d)I`. For `i!=j`, the complete sum over all `n` Fourier rows is zero; the omitted `r` rows therefore bound the retained inner product by

`|<psi_i,psi_j>|<=r/d=:alpha`.

The PGM effects `M_j=(d/n)P_j` form a POVM. The explicitly defined EB cloner

`B(rho)=sum_j Tr[M_j rho] P_j tensor P_j`

has correct-label probability `d/n` on every target. All other global-fidelity contributions are nonnegative, so

`e_B(P_j)<=r/n=alpha/(1+alpha)`.

Any EB surrogate satisfying pointwise loss at most `K e_B(P_j)` must therefore use at least `n=(1+alpha)d` distinct prepared states whenever

`K alpha/(1+alpha)<(1-alpha)/2`,

equivalently `0<alpha<sqrt(K^2+1)-K`.

Every rational `alpha` in that range occurs for infinitely many dimensions. Hence a universally valid preparation-rate upper bound `(1+beta)d` with finite pointwise constant `K` must have

`beta>=sqrt(K^2+1)-K`.

Equivalently, for `0<beta<1`, it must satisfy `K>=(1-beta^2)/(2beta)`. This lower tradeoff does not determine the sharp frontier. In particular, `beta=0` admits no finite universal constant.

For the concrete constant `K=2800`, take `d=5601b`, `r=b`, and `n=5602b`. Then `2800/5602<(1-1/5601)/2`, so at least `(1+1/5601)d` prepared states are necessary. This gives a linear excess above dimension, independently of the simplex isometry construction.
