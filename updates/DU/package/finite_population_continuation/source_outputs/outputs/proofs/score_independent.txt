# Full-support score interpolation: independent reconstruction

Status: complete internal proof of the interpolation and its map consequence. The strip lemma was proposed by OpenAI miner B; the root and cross-corpus researchers independently reconstructed it and the constants. The centered potential estimate below was independently reconstructed by the root and is retained here as an explicit interface. This is mathematical reconstruction, not external validation or a priority claim. No repository files are changed.

## 1. Exact source and target contract

Fix `d>=2`. Let `rho(x)=exp(-V(x))` be a probability density on all of `R^d`, where `V` is finite and `C^1`, and

\[
V(x)-\frac\kappa2|x|^2\quad\hbox{is convex},\qquad \kappa>0.
\]

Thus the density is positive everywhere. No upper Hessian bound is required, and no derivative of the score is used. Fix `p>2`, put `q=p/(p-2)`, and assume the supplied score budget

\[
S_q:=\sum_{j=1}^d\|\partial_jV\|_{L^q(\rho)}<\infty.
\tag{S}
\]

For one target `mu` assume `int |y|^p dmu(y)<=M<infinity`; the other target `nu` is arbitrary in `P_2(R^d)`. Let `T_mu,T_nu` be the unique quadratic optimal maps from `rho`, and write `w=W_2(mu,nu)`.

Then

\[
\boxed{\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le w+Cw^{1/3}},
\tag{T}
\]

where one admissible constant is

\[
\begin{split}
C&=\max\{C_*,2M^{1/p}\},\\
C_*^2&=\frac{24d}{\kappa}
+10S_q\left[M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\right]
+20d(2^{p-1}M+4).
\end{split}
\tag{C}
\]

The numerical split `w<=1` fixes a length convention. Constants are not asserted uniform as `p` decreases to two. Neither acquisition of the source or maps nor a computational procedure is supplied by this analytic theorem.

## 2. The boundary-free strip lemma

Fix a coordinate fiber. Let `r(t)>0` be its unnormalized density, with unique mode `m`. Strong convexity implies that `r` is increasing to `m`, decreasing after `m`, and tends to zero at both infinities. Let `g` be any bounded nondecreasing real function, and put

\[
J(r,g):=\int_{\mathbb R}|r'(s)|g(s)^2\,ds.
\]

For almost every `t`,

\[
\boxed{r(t)g(t)^2\le J(r,g).}
\tag{strip-point}
\]

Indeed, if `g(t)>=0`, set `a=max(t,m)`. For `s>=a`, `g(s)^2>=g(t)^2`, and

\[
J(r,g)\ge\int_a^\infty(-r'(s))g(s)^2\,ds
\ge g(t)^2r(a)\ge g(t)^2r(t).
\]

If `g(t)<0`, set `a=min(t,m)` and use the increasing-density left tail, where `g(s)<=g(t)<0` for `s<=a`. This gives the same estimate. Consequently, for every `h>0`,

\[
\int_{|t-m|<h}r(t)g(t)^2\,dt\le 2hJ(r,g).
\tag{strip}
\]

There is no restriction on `h`, no density distortion estimate, and no source curvature upper bound.

## 3. Exact interpolation inequality

Initially let `u,v` be finite affine maxima, and define

\[
F=\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)},\qquad
D=\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^2(\rho)},
\]

\[
\tau(L)=\max_{z\in\{u,v\}}
\int_{|\nabla z|>L}|\nabla z|^2\,d\rho.
\]

Clip coordinate derivatives to obtain the fiberwise nondecreasing functions

\[
g_{z,j}=\operatorname{clip}(\partial_jz,[-L,L]),\qquad
J_{z,j}=\int |\partial_jV|g_{z,j}^2\,d\rho.
\]

The inequality is

\[
\boxed{D^2\le\frac{12dF^2}{h^2}
+10h\sum_{j=1}^d(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})+20d\tau(L)}
\qquad(h,L>0).
\tag{WI}
\]

Here is the complete good/bad accounting for one coordinate. On a transverse fiber let

\[
O=\{t\le m-h\}\cup\{t\ge m+h\},\qquad
N=\{|t-m|<h\},
\]

and on `O` set `S(t)=t+h` on the left branch and `S(t)=t-h` on the right. These shifts stay on their respective sides of the mode. Their images are disjoint up to one point, and `r(t)<=r(S(t))`. Therefore, with `f=u-v`,

\[
\int_O\left|\frac{f(S(t))-f(t)}{S(t)-t}\right|^2r(t)\,dt
\le\frac4{h^2}\int_{\mathbb R}|f(t)|^2r(t)\,dt.
\tag{difference}
\]

The mode depends on the transverse coordinate; all statements are integrated by Fubini. Its continuous dependence follows from the continuous, strictly increasing fiber derivative of `V`, so the sets and shifts are measurable.

Use ONE bad set `B` on the whole space: a point is bad if either current coordinate derivative has magnitude greater than `L`, or if it belongs to `O` and either derivative at `S(t)` has magnitude greater than `L`. Every current event has mass at most `tau(L)/L^2`. Every shifted event has the same bound, by the disjoint-image and density-domination properties. Hence

\[
\rho(B)\le4\tau(L)/L^2.
\]

For each `z`, its current-coordinate energy on `B` is at most `L^2 rho(B)+tau(L)<=5tau(L)`. Thus

\[
\int_B|\partial_ju-\partial_jv|^2\,d\rho\le20\tau(L).
\tag{bad}
\]

This includes all bad points in the mode strip; there is no additional strip tail charge.

On `O\B`, convexity gives

\[
\delta_z:=|D_hz-\partial_jz(t)|\le |g_{z,j}(S(t))-g_{z,j}(t)|.
\]

For real `a<=b`,

\[
(b-a)^2\le2(b|b|-a|a|).
\tag{square}
\]

Apply this to the endpoints of `g`, writing `H=g|g|`, which is bounded and nondecreasing. Tonelli on the left branch gives

\[
\int_{t\le m-h}|H(t+h)-H(t)|r(t)\,dt
\le h\int_{s\le m}r(s)\,dH(s).
\]

Indeed all intermediate points satisfy `t<=s<=m`, so `r(t)<=r(s)`, and their integration interval has length at most `h`. The analogous right branch uses only `s>=m`. Their Stieltjes ranges are disjoint, and consequently

\[
\int_O|H(S(t))-H(t)|r(t)\,dt
\le h\int_{\mathbb R}r(s)\,dH(s).
\]

Integration by parts has zero boundary term because `H` is bounded and `r` tends to zero at both infinities. As `r'=-V_jr`,

\[
\int r\,dH=\int V_jH r\,dt\le\int |V_j|g^2r\,dt=J(r,g).
\tag{IBP}
\]

Together with (square), this yields `int_{O\B}delta_z^2 d rho<=2hJ_{z,j}`. Applying `(a+b+c)^2<=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)` to the difference quotient and the two errors gives

\[
\int_{O\setminus B}|\partial_ju-\partial_jv|^2\,d\rho
\le12F^2/h^2+6h(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j}).
\]

On `N\B` the derivatives equal their clips. Use `|g_u-g_v|^2<=2g_u^2+2g_v^2` and (strip), obtaining

\[
\int_{N\setminus B}|\partial_ju-\partial_jv|^2\,d\rho
\le4h(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j}).
\]

Adding (bad) and summing coordinates proves (WI). The Stieltjes measures accommodate the derivative jumps of affine maxima directly; smoothing the potentials is unnecessary.

Finally, Hölder with conjugate exponents `q` and `p/2` gives

\[
J_{z,j}\le\|\partial_jV\|_{L^q(\rho)}\|g_{z,j}\|_{L^p(\rho)}^2.
\tag{score}
\]

## 4. Centered-potential interface, with its cancellation exposed

The required interface is the centered strongly log-concave estimate

\[
\boxed{\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}\,W_2(\mu,\nu)}
\tag{P}
\]

for finite-target potentials normalized to have source mean zero. The detailed full-source proof is in `work/openai_miner_b/strong_logconcave_extension.md`, Sections 1–4 and 6a. Its load-bearing centered algebra is as follows.

For a central Laguerre cell and its opposite intercept perturbations, let the opposite masses be `alpha_i,beta_i`, set `gamma_i=sqrt(alpha_i beta_i)`, and couple their conditional source samples `X_i,Z_i` by their common triangular quantile parameter. Their midpoint lies in the central cell, whose mass is `M_i`. The triangular Jacobian arithmetic-geometric mean bound and strong log-concavity give

\[
M_i\ge\gamma_i E\exp\!\left(\frac\kappa8|X_i-Z_i|^2\right).
\]

The three cell families partition the probability source. Writing `D_i=E|X_i-Z_i|^2`,

\[
\sum_i\gamma_iD_i
\le\kappa^{-1}\sum_i\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{\gamma_i}.
\]

Differentiate the opposite intercept perturbations. If `B_i=int_{P_i}x d rho` and `bar x_i=B_i/M_i`, the result is

\[
\sum_i\frac{|DB_i[q]-\bar x_iDM_i[q]|^2}{M_i}
\le\kappa^{-1}\sum_i\frac{|DM_i[q]|^2}{M_i}.
\tag{centered-MM}
\]

The subtraction by `bar x_iDM_i` is essential. An uncentered moment derivative does not satisfy a radius-independent bound.

Along an optimal finite-target coupling, move sites at velocities `v_i`, with fixed masses `m_i` and `sum_i m_i|v_i|^2=w^2`. Choose intercept derivatives `r_i` keeping masses fixed. The potential derivative on cell `i` is `x dot v_i+r_i`. Set

\[
b=E_\rho\dot u,\qquad a_i=r_i+\bar x_i\cdot v_i-b.
\]

Then `sum_i m_i a_i=0`. Solve the intercept mass derivative equation `DM_i[q]=m_i a_i`. The symmetric face adjoint identity, after the conditional means are subtracted, is exactly

\[
\sum_i m_i a_i^2
=-\sum_i v_i\cdot\{DB_i[q]-\bar x_iDM_i[q]\}.
\]

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and (centered-MM) imply `sum_i m_i a_i^2<=w^2/kappa`. Conditional source densities on convex cells remain `kappa`-strongly log-concave and have covariance at most `kappa^{-1}I`. Thus the within-cell variance is at most another `w^2/kappa`, and

\[
\|\dot u-b\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2\le2w^2/\kappa.
\]

Integrating the source-mean-normalized potentials gives (P). Compact truncation justifies finite-cell calculus. Full-source finite-target potentials have a common global Lipschitz bound from their finitely many sites; the common linear growth bound, local uniform convex convergence, and Gaussian source tails pass both means and squared potential differences to the full source. This is the additional full-source limit needed by (WI), not just a limit of transport graphs.

## 5. One constrained target suffices

Let `(Y,Z)` be an optimal coupling of `mu,nu`, so `E|Y-Z|^2=w^2`. Pointwise, for every `L>0`,

\[
\min\{|Z|,L\}^p
\le2^p|Y|^p+4L^{p-2}|Y-Z|^2.
\tag{clip-transfer}
\]

If `|Z|<=2|Y|`, the first term suffices. Otherwise `|Y-Z|>|Z|/2`, and the second term suffices. Therefore

\[
\|g_{u,j}\|_p^p\le M,\qquad
\|g_{v,j}\|_p^p\le2^pM+4L^{p-2}w^2.
\]

Coordinate clips are pointwise dominated by the radial clip. This bounds clipped moments of the arbitrary target; it does not assume or infer an actual finite `p`-moment for `nu`.

The same coupling gives

\[
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2,
\qquad
\tau(L)\le2^{p-1}ML^{-(p-2)}+4w^2.
\tag{tail-transfer}
\]

For `0<w<=1`, choose

\[
L=w^{-2/(p-2)},\qquad h=w^{2/3}.
\]

Then the clipped moments are bounded by `M` and `2^pM+4`, while `tau(L)<=(2^{p-1}M+4)w^2`. Substitute (P) and (score) in (WI):

\[
D^2\le\frac{24d}{\kappa}\frac{w^2}{h^2}
+10hS_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}
+20d(2^{p-1}M+4)w^2
\le C_*^2w^{2/3}.
\]

For `w>1`, the target second-moment triangle inequality gives `D<=w+2M^{1/p}`. At `w=0` uniqueness gives `D=0`.

To remove finite targets, use finite norm-decreasing quantizers of `mu`, preserving its `p`-moment budget, and arbitrary finite `W_2` approximations of `nu`. Common-source optimal graph-plan stability and uniqueness identify the joint limit of the maps. The target squared norms are uniformly integrable under `W_2` convergence; hence squared map differences converge as well. The constants are unchanged. This proves (T).

## 6. Explicit failure if a physical source boundary is suppressed

The zero endpoint density is a genuine interface, not a technical convenience. Fix

\[
r(t)=Z^{-1}e^{-\kappa t^2/2}1_{[-1,1]}(t).
\]

For `0<a<1` let `u_a(t)=(t-(1-a))_+`, `v=0`, and `g_a=u_a'=1_{t>1-a}` on the source interior. Write `r_1=Z^{-1}e^{-kappa/2}>0`. Then, as `a` tends to zero,

\[
D_a^2=\int_{1-a}^1r(t)dt\sim r_1a,
\]

\[
F_a^2=\operatorname{Var}_r(u_a)\sim r_1a^3/3,
\qquad
J_a^{\mathrm{interior}}=\int_{1-a}^1\kappa t\,r(t)dt
=r(1-a)-r(1)\sim\kappa r_1a.
\]

The potentials may be mean-normalized without changing the gradients or these asymptotics. At a point in the outlier interval, `r(t)g_a(t)^2` is asymptotically `r_1`, whereas the interior score energy is of order `a`. Thus (strip-point) fails.

More decisively, take a derivative cutoff larger than one, so `tau=0`, and set `h=a^{2/3}`. A putative version of (WI) using only the interior score would have right side `O(a^{5/3})` and left side asymptotic to `r_1a`; it is false.

The exact missing integration-by-parts term is

\[
\int_{[-1,1]}r\,d(g_a|g_a|)
=[r\,g_a|g_a|]_{-1}^1+\int_{-1}^1\kappa t\,g_a|g_a|r\,dt
=r(1)+J_a^{\mathrm{interior}}=r(1-a).
\]

If the indicator boundary is included in the distributional density derivative, this is a boundary atom, and its quadratic energy is of order one. Suppressing it invalidates the argument. Taking the product with transverse Gaussians gives precisely the strongly log-concave slab setting, with all interior score moments finite, in any `d>=2`.

The companion slab construction further gives, with one `p`-moment budget, `D asymp w^{(p-2)/(3p-2)}`. Since that exponent is less than `1/3` for every finite `p>2`, extending (T) to proper convex supports on interior score control alone would also contradict an explicit transport-map family.

## 7. Limits of what has been established

The exponent is sharp over the admitted source class, since the fixed full Gaussian source belongs to it and the three-atom family in `work/openai_miner_b/gaussian_sharp_moments.md`, Section 3, has `D asymp w^{1/3}` with both targets in one fixed compact set. This statement does not claim that a matched lower family has been proved for every individual admitted non-Gaussian source.

The analytic inputs have separate roles: lower source curvature supplies (P); full support supplies the zero-boundary strip and Stieltjes identities; finite score `L^q` supplies the quadratic variation budget; one target's higher moment supplies the clipped transfer and tails. No source upper Hessian, no second target higher moment, no retained boundary contribution, and no computational speed claim is implicit.
