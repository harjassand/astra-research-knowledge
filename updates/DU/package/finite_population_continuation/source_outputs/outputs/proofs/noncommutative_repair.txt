# Ordered products: repair couplings and persistent endpoint memory

Status: newly derived in this worker from the mechanism below. The finite-dimensional separation theorem, Haar invariance, and primitive-digraph exponent theorem are imported standard facts. These arguments are complete mathematical candidates; historical originality and independent specialist validation remain unresolved. No repository file has been changed.

The earlier commutative notes solve arbitrary missing support and exact eventual periodicity by count fibers. That proof cannot simply be applied to ordered products. Here a repair mechanism supplies a different bridge, including a complete eventual classification for arbitrary-support finite groups and a boundary-sensitive classification for Rees matrix semigroups.

## 1. A fractional repair lemma for arbitrary constraint languages

Let A be a finite alphabet, let L be a nonempty subset of A^n, and prescribe coordinate laws μ_i on A. Put α_i=min_a μ_i(a). A set J of coordinates is **universally repairable** if, for every w in A^n, some v in L agrees with w outside J.

Suppose there is a probability distribution θ on universally repairable sets J such that

    Pr_{J~θ}(i∈J) ≤ α_i        for every i.

Then there is a joint law supported on L with coordinate marginals μ_i.

**Proof.** The marginal vectors of laws supported on L form the finite polytope

    C_L = conv{(δ_{w_1},…,δ_{w_n}):w∈L}.

By finite-dimensional separation, membership of (μ_1,…,μ_n) in C_L is equivalent to

    min_{w∈L} Σ_i c_i(w_i) ≤ Σ_i E_{μ_i} c_i

for every additive cost array c_i:A→R. Subtract min_a c_i(a) from each coordinate cost, and set M_i=max_a c_i(a) after this normalization. Choose a zero-cost word w by minimizing each coordinate separately. Repairing on J gives an accepted word of cost at most Σ_{i∈J}M_i. Therefore

    min_{v∈L} Σ_i c_i(v_i)
      ≤ E_J Σ_{i∈J}M_i
      ≤ Σ_i α_i M_i
      ≤ Σ_i E_{μ_i}c_i.

The last inequality uses the mass at any maximizing letter. This proves membership and the claimed coupling. □

In particular, if k disjoint coordinate blocks are each universally repairable and μ_i=μ, the uniform distribution over those blocks works whenever k min_a μ(a)≥1. More generally blocks J_j can receive weights θ_j provided their coordinate loads stay below α_i. This argument uses no position symmetry or commutativity.

The lemma is a sufficient condition, not a characterization. Its computational content is a finite marginal LP. It does not claim that arbitrary accepted languages have a succinct representation.

## 2. Full-support group products: a direct constructive theorem

Let G be a finite group of size g, with no commutativity assumption. Let U denote its uniform law. Prescribe possibly different laws μ_1,…,μ_n on G and set

    α_i=min_x μ_i(x),        p_i=g α_i.

If

    Σ_i p_i ≥ 2,

then, for every probability law ν on G, there is a coupling of the μ_i whose ordered product has law ν.

Consequently, for one full-support law μ,

    P_n(μ)=Δ(G)       whenever n≥ceil(2/[g min μ]).

The statement also holds for arbitrary compact groups if μ_i dominates p_i times normalized Haar measure and Σ_i p_i≥2; the finite case below has an entirely discrete exact sampler.

### Correlated flags with at least two marked coordinates

Write

    μ_i=p_i U+(1−p_i)ρ_i.

If p_i=1, choose any ρ_i; its coefficient is zero. Let s_0=0, s_i=Σ_{j≤i}p_j, and take V uniform on [0,1). Mark i when the half-open interval [s_{i−1},s_i) contains V+k for some integer k. Since each interval has length at most one,

    Pr(i marked)=p_i.

The number of marked indices is the number of points V+k in [0,Σ_i p_i), and is at least floor(Σ_i p_i)≥2, apart from null endpoint events. For rational p_i this law has a finite exact representation: partition [0,1) at the fractional parts of s_0,…,s_n and sample one of the resulting rational intervals by its length.

### Completion sampler

Sample Z~ν and the flags independently. At unmarked coordinates sample X_i independently from ρ_i. Among marked coordinates let j be the last; sample all other marked X_i independently from U. Set the remaining letter uniquely to

    X_j=(X_1⋯X_{j−1})^{-1} Z (X_{j+1}⋯X_n)^{-1}.

The product is Z exactly. Each non-final marked coordinate is uniform by construction. The final marked coordinate is uniform too: condition on all variables except one other marked uniform coordinate. The map from that coordinate to X_j is a composition of left/right translations and inversion, hence a permutation of G. Thus every coordinate has its prescribed mixture p_i U+(1−p_i)ρ_i, even conditional on the target Z. This proves the theorem.

This requires no invariance of μ_i under conjugation. A fixed noncentral target is allowed. Output ν may be arbitrary rather than conjugacy-invariant.

The constant 2 in the uniform-component criterion is sharp as a universal sufficient threshold: in G=Z/2Z with identical μ(1)=ε≤1/2, a coupling with product 1 requires nε≥1 by the union bound. Here Σ_i p_i=2nε. This sharpness does not assert optimality for each larger group.

### Exact coalition privacy as a transparent corollary

More generally, if Σ_i p_i≥k+1 for an integer k≥1, use the same flags: there are at least k+1 marked coordinates surely. The product can encode an arbitrary random secret Z while every coalition C of at most k coordinates has a joint law independent of Z. The individual share marginals remain the prescribed μ_i.

For proof condition on the flags, which are independent of Z. If the final completion coordinate is outside C, every observed share was sampled independently of Z. If it is in C, there is an unobserved free marked coordinate. Conditional on all other sampled values, that coordinate's Haar randomness makes the completion share Haar-uniform regardless of Z. Thus, conditional on the flags and Z, the joint law observed by C is exactly the product of its flagged component laws (U or ρ_i). It does not depend on Z. Averaging over flags proves perfect coalition privacy.

This uses the standard uniform group-sharing mechanism, diluted by correlated uniform-component flags. It is recorded as an explicit quantitative corollary, not claimed as a new secret-sharing paradigm. The coalition law need not be a product of the μ_i; the claim is independence from the secret, rather than independent shares.

## 3. Arbitrary-support finite groups: exact eventual coset classification

Let μ have arbitrary nonempty support A⊆G, put α=min_{a∈A}μ(a)>0, and let H be the subgroup generated by A. Fix a_0∈A. Let K be the normal closure in H of

    {b a_0^{-1}:b∈A}.

Then H/K is cyclic, generated by the common coset of all a∈A. Write h=|H/K| and M=|K|.

There is an effectively computable integer ℓ, divisible by h, with

    A^ℓ=K.

For any such ℓ and all

    n ≥ ℓ ceil(1/α),

the exact set of attainable ordered product laws is

    P_n(μ)=Δ(K a_0^n).

Thus missing support creates only a cyclic quotient obstruction in finite groups; once the horizon is large enough, there are no further restrictions on the output law. In particular exact membership for a prescribed ν is eventually periodic, with period h≤|H|.

### A self-contained finite bound for ℓ

Let p=lcm{ord(a):a∈A}, and let B=A^p. Because a^p=e, B contains e. Let K' be the subgroup generated by B.

For a,b∈A, b a^{-1}=b a^{p−1} belongs to B. Also, for every c∈A,

    c B c^{-1} ⊆ A^{2p}=B² ⊆ K',

because c^{-1}=c^{p−1}. Since conjugation preserves cardinality, cK'c^{-1}=K'. Thus K' is normal in H and contains every ratio b a_0^{-1}; hence K⊆K'. Conversely H/K is cyclic with all A mapping to a_0K, and a_0^p=e implies A^p⊆K. Therefore K'=K.

The increasing sets B^j stabilize at K after at most |K|−1 strict growth steps. Hence if |K|>1, one may take

    ℓ=p(|K|−1).

If |K|=1, all letters of A are the same a_0; μ is a point mass and P_n(μ)=δ_{a_0^n} at every horizon, so no mixing argument is needed.

This proves existence without importing a graph exponent bound. The value p can be large, but its binary length is O(|H| log |H|).

### A polynomial bound using an imported classical theorem

One may instead take

    ℓ=h[(M−1)²+1] ≤ |H|²       when M≥2.

Here is the reduction to the imported theorem. In the directed Cayley graph of H with steps A, let d be the gcd of return lengths at e. A positive word for every element exists because the positive monoid generated by A is a finite group. If two words represent the same element, append any positive word for its inverse: their length difference is divisible by d. Therefore word length modulo d defines a homomorphism H→Z/dZ sending every a∈A to 1. The kernel contains K, so d divides h. Every return length is divisible by h, since H/K has order h, so h divides d. Hence d=h.

The h-step Cayley graph on K, with steps A^h, is strongly connected and aperiodic. Strong connectivity follows from any positive word representing k∈K having length divisible by h. Its return-length gcd is d/h=1. The classical primitive-digraph exponent theorem gives (A^h)^{(M−1)²+1}=K. This is the only imported ingredient in the polynomial bound. See [Schneider's publication of Wielandt's original proof](https://people.math.wisc.edu/hans/paper_archive/scanned_papers/hs149.pdf), pp. 8–10; the standard exponent inequality is M²−2M+2.

### Support cosets and repair

Once A^ℓ=K, every n≥ℓ satisfies

    A^n=K A^{n−ℓ}=K a_0^n,

because all A have the same image in H/K and a_0^ℓ∈K. Therefore all attainable outputs lie in this coset.

Fix a target z∈K a_0^n. For any word w∈A^n and any contiguous block of length ℓ, let P and Q be the ordered products before and after that block, using e for an empty part. The required replacement block product is

    P^{-1} z Q^{-1}.

Its image in H/K is (a_0K)^ℓ=K. It therefore belongs to K=A^ℓ and can be realized by a replacement word of exactly length ℓ. Thus every contiguous length-ℓ block is universally repairable for the language of product-z words.

There are floor(n/ℓ) disjoint such blocks. Since floor(n/ℓ)α≥1, the fractional repair lemma supplies a coupling with all marginals μ and product exactly z. Mixtures over z give every law on the coset, proving the classification.

All finite algebra is supplied by the multiplication table. H, K, the quotient, the Cayley graph, and replacement blocks can be obtained by finite graph searches. A direct finite-horizon flow LP has O(n|H||A|) variables; this proof does not claim a new logarithmic-horizon LP for arbitrary ordered targets. Generic LP and primitive-graph algorithms are not new primitives here.

## 4. Arbitrary support in every finite completely simple semigroup

Let S be a finite completely simple semigroup, let A=supp μ be arbitrary nonempty support, and let T=A^+ be the subsemigroup it generates. Let I and Λ index the R-classes and L-classes of T, respectively; in a Rees matrix representation these are the first and last coordinates. Denote their projections by π_I and π_Λ. Set α=min_A μ.

There is a canonical cyclic length homomorphism

    φ:T→Z/hZ,        φ(a)=1 for every a∈A,

where h is the period of a right Cayley graph on any one R-class of T. For an effectively computable multiple ℓ of h, with ℓ≤2|T|², we have A^ℓ=φ^{-1}(0). At every horizon

    n ≥ 2+ℓ ceil(1/α),

the exact attainable output polytope is

    P_n(μ)={ν∈Δ(T): (π_I)_*ν=(π_I)_*μ,
                         (π_Λ)_*ν=(π_Λ)_*μ,
                         supp ν⊆φ^{-1}(n mod h)}.

Thus every sufficiently long horizon is classified by two persistent endpoint projections and a cyclic group quotient. There is no remaining restriction on correlations between those projections and the group component. The support need not contain any whole group fiber.

### 4.1 The generated subsemigroup remains completely simple

Use any Rees representation of the ambient S. Every t∈T belongs to a finite local group of S, so positive powers of t include that group's identity and the inverse of t. Consequently T is completely regular. For a,b∈T, the element aba has the same first and last indices as a and hence belongs to the same ambient local group. For some positive r,

    (aba)^r=e_a,
    a=(aba)^r a∈T b T.

Every nonempty two-sided ideal of T therefore contains every a∈T. Hence T is simple; being finite with idempotents, it is completely simple. The standard Rees representation theorem applies to T. In particular its first and last indices are still endpoint projections of products. The generation closure and Green classes are computable directly from the supplied finite multiplication table. For a primary research source explicitly stating the imported Rees theorem and a constructive representation algorithm, see [Kambites and Otto, Section 6, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.7](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0509349). The original attribution is [Rees, *On semi-groups* (1940)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100017436). The stability of finite subsemigroups is standard; its short proof above is included to make the reduction checkable, not claimed as a new algebraic theorem.

### 4.2 Length modulo the right-Cayley period is well-defined

Work now in a Rees representation of T, and regard A as generating all of T. A right Cayley graph restricted to first index i has vertex set {(i,g,λ)} and arcs x→xa for a∈A. It is strongly connected: for x=(i,g,λ) and y=(i,u,κ), some b=(j,p_{λj}^{−1}g^{−1}u,κ) satisfies xb=y, and b has a positive A-word. The transition rule on (g,λ) does not depend on i, so all these row graphs are isomorphic and have the same period h.

Fix a state x in one row graph. If two A-words u,v represent the same t∈T, their paths from x end at the same state xt. Append any common path back to x. The resulting two return lengths differ by |u|−|v|, and both are divisible by h. Thus word length modulo h is well-defined on T. Concatenation gives the homomorphism φ, and φ(a)=1. Since an A-letter maps to 1, φ is onto Z/hZ.

For computation, h is the gcd of d(x)+1−d(xa) over all Cayley arcs, where d gives shortest-path distances from one root. This is the standard graph-period computation and uses only the supplied table and A.

### 4.3 Normalize the Rees coordinates and identify the phase fibers

A Rees matrix can be normalized so a chosen row and column of its sandwich matrix are identities. Explicitly, with base indices i_0,λ_0 and c=p_{λ_0 i_0}, change coordinates to

    g'=u_i g v_λ,
    u_i=p_{λ_0 i},        v_λ=p_{λ i_0}c^{−1},
    p'_{λj}=v_λ^{−1}p_{λj}u_j^{−1}.

Then p'_{λ_0 j}=p'_{λ i_0}=e, and this change is an isomorphism. Drop the primes in these normalized coordinates. Every (i,g,λ) factors as

    (i,e,λ_0)(i_0,g,λ_0)(i_0,e,λ).

The outer factors are idempotents, so their φ-values are zero. The middle local group identifies φ with a surjective group homomorphism χ:G→Z/hZ:

    φ(i,g,λ)=χ(g).

Moreover (i_0,e,λ)(j,e,λ_0)=(i_0,p_{λj},λ_0), a product of idempotents, so every p_{λj} lies in K=ker χ. Thus

    T_r=φ^{-1}(r)=I×χ^{-1}(r)×Λ.

Each cyclic class in one row Cayley graph has the same number

    M=|K||Λ|=|G||Λ|/h

of states. This is why the cyclic quotient can be repaired without altering the endpoint indices.

### 4.4 A bounded block fills an entire zero-phase fiber

Put W=(M−1)²+1 and ℓ=h(W+1). The h-step Cayley graph on each cyclic class is strongly connected and aperiodic. By the imported primitive-graph exponent theorem its W-th power has every arc.

Fix a desired first index i and choose an A-letter with that index; its phase is 1. Append any h−1 letters, reaching some phase-zero state in that row. Then hW further letters reach every phase-zero state of that row. The resulting word length is ℓ. Since all length-ℓ words have phase zero,

    A^ℓ=T_0.

Also ℓ≤2hM²≤2|T|². This bound invokes only the classical primitive exponent inequality, cited above; it is not a new estimate.

In fact A^n=T_{n mod h} for every n≥ℓ. For n=ℓ+r, r≥1, take a word b∈A^r with the target last index. A suitable left multiplier in T_0, obtained by solving the group coordinate, reaches any target of phase r and any first index; all sandwich factors have χ-value zero. The case r=0 is A^ℓ=T_0.

### 4.5 Repair an interior block while retaining the arbitrary ordered target

Let ν satisfy the three displayed constraints. Sample Z~ν. Conditional on Z, choose the first letter from μ conditioned on first index π_I(Z), and the last letter from μ conditioned on last index π_Λ(Z). Both unconditional endpoint laws are μ.

Fix these endpoints and the target z. For any tentative interior A-word, select any contiguous interior block of length ℓ. Let P and Q be the products before and after that block, including the fixed endpoints. They are nonempty. Write P=(i_P,g_P,λ_P), Q=(i_Q,g_Q,λ_Q), and z=(i_P,g_z,λ_Q). Choose any indices j,κ and solve

    g_P p_{λ_P j} b p_{κ i_Q} g_Q=g_z

for b∈G. Applying χ shows χ(b)=0, since target phase equals total length n and the omitted block has length ℓ≡0. Hence (j,b,κ)∈T_0=A^ℓ. Replace the block by an A-word realizing this element. The first/last target indices and the exact group component are all correct.

Every contiguous interior length-ℓ block is therefore universally repairable. There are floor((n−2)/ℓ) disjoint blocks. Under the stated horizon bound, their uniform repair distribution has coordinate load at most α. The fractional repair lemma supplies an interior coupling with every marginal exactly μ and exact fixed product z. This holds conditional on every endpoint pair and z. Mixing completes the proof of sufficiency; necessity follows directly from product endpoint indices and φ-additivity. □

The proof is not an exchangeable construction, and no rotation preserves the target. The exact output is retained by solving one block equation inside a zero-phase Rees fiber.

## 5. Full group fibers admit a simpler explicit sampler

Consider a Rees matrix semigroup with no zero,

    S=I×G×Λ,
    (i,g,λ)(j,u,κ)=(i,g p_{λj}u,κ),

where every sandwich entry p_{λj} belongs to the finite group G. This formula is associative directly. No structural representation needs to be acquired: the theorem is stated for this supplied representation. By the classical Rees representation theorem it also covers finite completely simple semigroups, once that representation has been supplied or computed separately.

Assume A=supp μ is a union of complete group fibers: for some E⊆I×Λ,

    A={(i,g,λ):(i,λ)∈E, g∈G}.

Let α=min_A μ>0. Then, for every

    n ≥ 2+ceil(1/α),

we have the exact classification

    P_n(μ) = {ν∈Δ(S): ν_I=μ_I and ν_Λ=μ_Λ}.

The endpoint equalities are necessary at every n: the first index of a product is the first letter's index, and the last index is the last letter's index.

For sufficiency take any ν with those projected marginals. Sample Z=(I_Z,G_Z,Λ_Z)~ν. Conditional on Z, sample the first letter from μ conditioned on first index I_Z and the last letter from μ conditioned on last index Λ_Z. These letters each have unconditional law μ. Fix any realized endpoints and target Z. The interior language consists of words whose insertion between those endpoints has group component G_Z. Any single interior coordinate can repair that group component, because all other factors are group elements and the coordinate's group entry is unrestricted inside its existing (i,λ) fiber. Thus every singleton interior position is universally repairable. There are n−2 of them; applying the fractional repair lemma gives an interior coupling with each coordinate law exactly μ. Its existence holds conditional on every endpoint pair and target. Mixing gives the claimed full coupling.

There is also a more efficient explicit sampler. Let g=|G| and put

    β=Σ_{(i,λ)∈E} min_{u∈G} μ(i,u,λ),
    p=gβ.

Decompose μ=p σ+(1−p)ρ, where σ first samples (i,λ) with probability proportional to min_u μ(i,u,λ), then samples its group coordinate uniformly. If

    (n−2)p≥2,

use the same correlated-flag construction on the interior coordinates. Sample all their (i,λ) pairs first; unmarked letters follow ρ, marked group coordinates are uniform except the last, which is completed to the required total group component. The last marked group coordinate is still uniform because there is another uniform marked coordinate and all sandwich factors have already been fixed. Therefore every marked letter has law σ and every interior marginal is μ. This yields the same classification at the alternative sufficient cutoff

    n ≥ 2+ceil(2/[gβ]).

When g=1, the semigroup is a rectangular band and the endpoint conditions are sufficient already for every n≥2: sample the output's first/last indices and endpoints as above; all interior coordinates may be arbitrary with law μ. This is an exact family-wide obstruction, not a finite numerical example: endpoint information persists for arbitrarily long products, even under full support.

## 6. What this establishes and what remains open

The ordered finite-group classification and the completely simple semigroup classification both work for arbitrary support. Complete group fibers permit the particularly short explicit sampler in Section 5. These results show that noncommutativity itself does not obstruct arbitrary prescribed output laws after sufficient redundancy; persistent left/right ideal information and a cyclic length quotient describe the restrictions in the completely simple case.

The exact arbitrary-support classification for general finite semigroups remains open here. The subsequent `NONEVENTUAL_PERIODICITY.md` proves that eventual length phases cannot give such a classification in general: one fixed binary marginal and one fixed target already have a nonperiodic feasible-horizon set. A general semigroup can retain endpoint information and fall into smaller ideals at times depending on the word. The count-fiber normal form from the commutative theorem and the repair argument solve different parts of this problem, and the transient-target obstruction explains why a general finite-phase merger cannot work.

The general notion of semigroup-valued mixability is pre-existing; [Wang's survey](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.6190v3) explicitly formulates it. The 2025 paper [Mixability of finite groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17806) concerns independent two-option factors producing the uniform law, a different interface. Those sources do not establish historical priority for the results here; targeted literature search has not settled priority.

The nearest Astra source is `updates/DE/package/finite_commutative_semigroup_classification/RESEARCH_REPORT.md`, pinned at `b2a7a8fa91a8fcf6da05f36100ae4ce64a72a729`: lines 11–119 give the commutative full-support theorem, lines 518–538 already report an exact finite-horizon binary LP, and lines 542–566 discuss missing support and noncommutative scope. The present ordered-product arguments are not imported from that source.

## 7. Independent exact diagnostics

`verify_noncommutative.py` uses only integer tables and `fractions.Fraction`. It checks all nonempty supports in two 8-element Rees matrix semigroups over Z/2Z, all nonempty supports in S_3, and twenty fixed-seed supports in a 24-element Rees matrix semigroup over S_3: 593 generated-support structures total. It independently computes Green classes and Cayley periods, verifies the phase homomorphism on every product, verifies A^ℓ=T_0 at the stated cutoff, and checks all sandwich repair images in each generated semigroup. There were 133,467 individual admissible sandwich targets.

The same script enumerates the direct correlated-flag sampler exactly for several S_3 marginal choices, including unequal marginals and noncentral targets, and verifies 1,440 elementary group outcomes. For four biased shares with p_i=3/4 it also checks all six pair-coalition laws against all six possible secrets, making 30 exact equality comparisons beyond the reference secret. It enumerates 10,368 elementary outcomes for the Rees sampler, with incomplete row/column support and nontrivial endpoint output law, and checks every coordinate marginal and ordered product exactly. All checks passed; see `noncommutative_verification_receipt.json`. These are finite diagnostics, separate from the universal arguments and from historical novelty.
