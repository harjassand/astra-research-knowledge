# Convex conditioning obstructs a curvature-free inverse Monge representation

Status: new complete written argument, pending independent reconstruction and scoped priority review. This is a necessary-condition theorem and a counterexample. A converse characterization is not proved. No algorithmic impossibility or computational hardness is inferred.

## 1. Perfect-success target and actual conclusion

The motivating target was a source-only criterion for uniformly stable inverse Monge coordinates, replacing the lower curvature assumption in

\[
\|u_\mu-u_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le K_\rho W_2(\mu,\nu),
\tag{PL}
\]

where each convex Brenier potential is normalized to have source mean zero. Such a representation would support stable transport continuation and inverse computation without regularity assumptions on the targets.

The conclusion below rules out a tempting replacement by global Poincare, finite moments, and finite score moments. It shows that (PL), even restricted to finite atomic targets in one fixed compact ball, requires a uniform covariance bound after convex-cell conditioning. An explicit fixed smooth log-concave source has bounded score, all polynomial moments, and a finite global Poincare constant, but violates this necessary condition already for halfspaces. Its potential condition numbers grow like the square root of the bit length of a rare target mass.

This does not settle which source-only condition is sufficient. It also does not show computational intractability: the quantified conditioning deterioration is only polynomial in the mass encoding length.

## 2. A necessary convex-cell covariance bound

Let `rho` be a positive smooth probability density on `R^d`, with an exponential upper envelope `rho(x)<=C exp(-c|x|)` for fixed positive constants. Fix a target radius `B>0`. Suppose (PL) holds with constant `K` for every pair of finitely supported target probabilities contained in `B(0,B)`, with arbitrary positive weights. Then for every full-dimensional convex polytope `P` with `rho(P)>0`,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid X\in P)\preceq K^2I_d.
\tag{necessary-covariance}
\]

Unbounded polytopes and halfspaces are included. For the whole space the same conclusion follows by approximation or by a one-atom target variation.

Here is a local proof with the required finite-target realization. Write the nonredundant facets of `P` as `n_j dot x<=b_j`, with unit normals. Choose `0<s<B` and set

\[
u(x)=\max\{0,\ s(n_j\cdot x-b_j):1\le j\le N\}.
\]

The central Laguerre cell is `P`, with site `y_0=0`; the other sites are `y_j=sn_j`. Every nonredundant facet has a neighborhood on its outer side where its affine branch is uniquely maximal, so the associated cell has positive source mass. Give each site its cell mass `m_i`. This is a finite atomic target and `grad u` is its Brenier map.

Move only the central site to `y_0(epsilon)=epsilon v`, leaving all weights fixed. For sufficiently small `epsilon`, the sites remain in the fixed target ball. Locally one can choose differentiable intercepts `r_i(epsilon)` so that every cell mass remains exactly `m_i`. This standard finite-dimensional implicit step can be seen directly: the intercept mass derivative is the graph Laplacian

\[
\partial_{r_j}m_i=-w_{ij}\ (i\ne j),\qquad
\partial_{r_i}m_i=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij},\qquad
w_{ij}=\int_{F_{ij}}\frac{\rho(x)}{|y_i-y_j|}\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}(x).
\]

The positive-cell adjacency graph is connected: join interior points by a generic segment avoiding face intersections of codimension at least two. Hence the Laplacian has kernel exactly the constants, and is invertible after fixing the additive gauge. The exponential envelope dominates all local facet integrals and their first site/intercept variations. One may first use generic diagrams; arbitrary polytopes follow by generic facet approximation and dominated convergence of their conditional second moments.

Inside each original cell, the pointwise derivative of the affine maximum exists almost everywhere and is

\[
\dot u(x)=r_i'(0)+\mathbf1_{i=0}\,v\cdot x.
\]

The difference quotients are dominated in `L^2(rho)` by `C(1+|x|)`, since there are finitely many affine branches and their parameters vary differentiably. After mean-zero normalization they converge to `dot u-E dot u`. The law of total variance gives

\[
\|\dot u-E\dot u\|_2^2
\ge m_0\operatorname{Var}(v\cdot X\mid X\in P).
\]

The diagonal coupling of the fixed-weight targets costs only `m_0epsilon^2|v|^2`, because only the central atom moved. Apply (PL), divide by `epsilon^2`, and let `epsilon` tend to zero. This proves

\[
m_0\operatorname{Var}(v\cdot X\mid P)\le K^2m_0|v|^2,
\]

which is (necessary-covariance). Neither the number of atoms nor a lower bound on their masses entered the assumed interface.

## 3. A fixed smooth log-concave source

For `d>=2`, fix

\[
\rho_d(x)=Z_d^{-1}\exp\{-V(x)\},\qquad
V(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^2}.
\]

It is a fixed positive smooth log-concave density. The radial and tangential Hessian eigenvalues are respectively `(1+|x|^2)^(-3/2)` and `(1+|x|^2)^(-1/2)`, so no positive global lower curvature exists. Its score satisfies `|grad V|<=1`; hence every finite coordinate score moment is at most one. Its exponential envelope gives every polynomial source moment.

It also has a finite explicit global Poincare constant. Let `L=Delta-grad V dot grad` and `W=exp(V/2)`. Then

\[
-LW/W=\tfrac14|\nabla V|^2-\tfrac12\Delta V,
\quad |\nabla V|^2=|x|^2/(1+|x|^2),
\quad\Delta V\le d/\sqrt{1+|x|^2}.
\]

Take `R=4d`. Outside `B_R`, `-LW/W>=1/16`, while everywhere it is at least `-d/2`. Weighted integration by parts and completing the square yield the elementary ground-state inequality

\[
\int(-LW/W)f^2\rho\le\int|\nabla f|^2\rho.
\tag{ground-state}
\]

Indeed the left side equals `int[2f grad f dot grad log W-f^2|grad log W|^2]rho`, which is bounded by the displayed Dirichlet energy. This identity first holds with cutoff functions; bounded `grad log W` and the exponential source tails pass the cutoffs.

Subtract the `rho`-mean of `f` on `B_R`. A standard ball Poincare inequality and density comparison give

\[
\int_{B_R}f^2\rho\le C_{\rm loc}\int_{B_R}|\nabla f|^2\rho,
\qquad C_{\rm loc}=4R^2e^{\sqrt{1+R^2}-1}
\]

(a deliberately loose constant). Equation (ground-state) now bounds the outside integral and proves

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\rho(f)\le
\{16+(1+8d)C_{\rm loc}\}\int|\nabla f|^2\rho.
\tag{global-Poincare}
\]

Thus failure below is not explained by lack of a global spectral gap, score integrability, or source moments.

## 4. Rare caps have unbounded tangent covariance

Write `X=(X_1,Y)` with `Y in R^{d-1}`, and let `a_t=P(X_1>t)`. Under the change of variables `X_1=t+s`, `Y=sqrt(t)z`, `s>=0`, the density divided by `exp(-t)` is

\[
\exp\{-\sqrt{1+(t+s)^2+t|z|^2}+t\}
\longrightarrow e^{-s-|z|^2/2}.
\]

This convergence passes both the mass and the tangent second moments by an integrable domination uniform in `t>=1`. To see that, the exponent difference is at least `s` and also at least

\[
\sqrt{t^2+t|z|^2}-t
=\frac{|z|^2}{\sqrt{1+|z|^2/t}+1}
\ge\frac{|z|^2}{|z|+2}.
\]

Therefore it is at least half the sum of these two bounds. The function `exp(-s/2-|z|^2/(2(|z|+2)))`, multiplied by `1+|z|^2`, is integrable. Dominated convergence gives

\[
a_t\sim Z_d^{-1}(2\pi)^{(d-1)/2}t^{(d-1)/2}e^{-t},
\]

\[
E[X_2^2\mid X_1>t]/t\longrightarrow1.
\tag{cap-covariance}
\]

The cap tangent mean is zero by radial symmetry. Hence its conditional covariance is unbounded.

## 5. Exact two-atom potential instability

Fix any outlier radius `s>0`. For small `theta`, let `n_theta=cos(theta)e_1+sin(theta)e_2` and define

\[
u_{t,\theta}(x)=s(n_\theta\cdot x-t)_+,
\qquad
\mu_{t,\theta}=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{sn_\theta}.
\]

Radial symmetry makes the active cell mass exactly `a_t` for every angle; these convex potentials give exactly the displayed Brenier maps. Their source means are independent of `theta`, so differences of the raw potentials already equal differences of their mean-zero normalizations.

For `|theta|<pi/2`, matching the two nonzero atoms is the optimal two-atom target coupling: switching a positive amount through the zero atom would add cost `2s^2cos(theta)>0` per unit switched. Consequently

\[
w_{t,\theta}^2=W_2(\mu_{t,0},\mu_{t,\theta})^2
=2a_ts^2(1-\cos\theta).
\tag{exact-target-distance}
\]

At every point off the measure-zero cap boundary,

\[
\frac{u_{t,\theta}-u_{t,0}}\theta
\longrightarrow sX_2\mathbf1_{X_1>t}.
\]

The quotients are bounded by `2s|X|` for small angles, so convergence holds in `L^2(rho)`. Thus

\[
\lim_{\theta\to0}
\frac{\|u_{t,\theta}-u_{t,0}\|_{L^2(\rho)}}{w_{t,\theta}}
=\sqrt{E[X_2^2\mid X_1>t]}
\sim\sqrt t.
\tag{condition-number}
\]

This disproves (PL) for this one fixed source and one fixed target ball, even using only two-atom targets. For any prescribed `p>2,M>0`, choose fixed `s` with `s^p<=M`; both targets then satisfy the same moment budget for every `t,theta`. No hidden loss of target moment control is involved.

## 6. Encoding and acquisition limits

The asymptotic `log(1/a_t)~t` shows that the local condition number is of order `sqrt(log(1/a_t))`. To formulate dyadic target inputs, choose `a_t=2^{-m}` by taking its unique source cap quantile. Parametrize the unit circle rationally as `((1-epsilon^2)/(1+epsilon^2),2epsilon/(1+epsilon^2))` with dyadic `epsilon`; rational `s` then gives rational sites of bit length `O(log(1/epsilon))`, while the rare mass has bit length `O(m)`. The analytic condition number grows as `sqrt(m)`.

The source is supplied as the fixed explicit density. The quantile is a derived description of the corresponding optimal potential, not an oracle value supplied to a transport algorithm. The argument does not charge or bound the cost of computing this quantile, evaluating the normalizing integral, or approximating an optimal potential; it therefore makes no algorithmic complexity claim. In particular polynomial growth in `m` is compatible with polynomial bit algorithms.

## 7. Remaining question and primary scope

The necessary convex-conditioning criterion is not shown sufficient. Establishing a non-tautological sufficient criterion between uniform conditioned covariance, conditioned functional inequalities, and the centered mass-to-moment operator would be the next mathematical step. Simply replacing lower curvature by the global Poincare constant is already falsified.

Primary literature search on 11 October 2026 identified [Letrouit–Merigot, arXiv:2411.04908v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04908v3), which supplies quantitative stability for full-support log-concave sources and logarithmically bounded perturbations, but does not in the checked main statements give the uniform `W_2` potential-Lipschitz criterion above. [Letrouit's Peccot course notes](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~cyril.letrouit/teaching/Peccot123.pdf), final discussion of Section 5, pose a related informal question about simple test families for source-dependent stability. Exact prior status of the present necessary criterion and radial example remains unchecked; no originality claim is made.
