# High-dimensional mixed-state counterexample probe: covariant projector and spectrum families

11 October 2026. This is an independently reconstructed negative control for the unrestricted mixed product-cloning question, not a claimed new theorem of independent significance or a substitute for the missing general class measurement.

## 1. The nearly full-rank projector route cannot produce an unbounded quotient

Let `P` range Haar-uniformly over rank-`r` orthogonal projectors on `C^d`, `1<=r<d`, and put `rho_P=P/r`. Squared Uhlmann fidelity obeys

`F(B(rho_P),rho_P tensor rho_P)<=tr[(P tensor P)B(rho_P)]`.

This follows by applying the support-vs-complement binary measurement: the target has support probability one, and fidelity is monotone under measurement.

For every channel, its averaged support score is `tr(J_B M)` with unnormalized Choi matrix `J_B>=0`, `tr_out J_B=I`, and

`M=(1/r)[integral P tensor P tensor P dP]^(T_1)`.

Therefore it is at most `d ||M||_infinity`. The following elementary third-moment calculation gives the exact upper bound

`d ||M||_infinity=(r+1)/(d+1)`.

The bound is achieved as a support score by the universal symmetric cloner; achievement of this support bound is not asserted as achievement of the same fidelity.

### Explicit third moment and spectrum

For `d>=3`, define

`t_S=r(r+1)(r+2)/[d(d+1)(d+2)]`,

`t_A=r(r-1)(r-2)/[d(d-1)(d-2)]`,

`t_M=r(r^2-1)/[d(d^2-1)]`.

The Haar third moment is

`alpha I+beta(F12+F13+F23)+gamma(F12F23+F23F12)`,

where `alpha=(t_S+t_A+4t_M)/6`, `beta=(t_S-t_A)/6`, and `gamma=(t_S+t_A-2t_M)/6`. This follows by projecting onto the symmetric, antisymmetric and standard `S_3` representations: the moment is scalar on each and its scalar is the rank-`r`/rank-`d` Schur dimension ratio.

After partial transpose of register one, `F12` becomes the unnormalized maximally entangled projector `Omega12`, similarly for `F13`, while `F23` is unchanged. Thus

`r M=alpha I+beta(F23+Omega12+Omega13)+gamma(Omega12 F23+F23 Omega12)`.

For a vector `v`, write `u_v=|Omega>12 tensor v_3` and `w_v=|Omega>13 tensor v_2`. Output swap interchanges them, their norms squared are `d||v||^2`, and their inner product is `||v||^2`. The spaces spanned by `u_v+w_v` and `u_v-w_v` each have dimension `d`. On these spaces, `Omega12+Omega13` has eigenvalues `d+1` and `d-1`, respectively, while `Omega12 F23+F23 Omega12` has eigenvalues `d+1` and `-(d-1)`.

The remaining output-symmetric and output-antisymmetric subspaces are annihilated by the Omega operators. Consequently the four eigenvalues, multiplied by `d`, are

`(r+1)/(d+1)`,

`(r-1)/(d-1)`,

`(r+1)(dr+r-2)/[(d-1)(d+1)(d+2)]`,

`(r-1)(dr-r-2)/[(d-2)(d-1)(d+1)]`.

The first is largest for `1<=r<=d`. For the third this is equivalent to `r(d+1)-2<=(d-1)(d+2)`; the fourth is at most the second. The case `d=2,r=1` is the pure-state symmetric-moment calculation and gives `2/3` directly.

It follows that the average product-cloning infidelity is at least `(d-r)/(d+1)`. The EB replacer `T(X)=tr(X)I/d` has fidelity `r/d` on every `rho_P`, hence

`e_EB <= (d-r)/d <= [(d+1)/d] e_clone`.

In particular `r=d-1` gives cloning error at least `1/(d+1)` and EB loss at most `1/d`. The potentially dangerous high-dimensional one-hole family therefore does not refute a universal linear mixed bound.

This is a continuum-family control. It is not being used to prove a corresponding bound for arbitrary finite subsets; restricting the orbit can make cloning easier and invalidate its averaged lower bound.

## 2. General covariant fixed-spectrum probe

For an entire unitary orbit of a fixed spectrum `lambda`, averaging a channel over `U(d)` and output swap cannot decrease its orbit-average fidelity. A covariant, swap-symmetric Hermiticity-preserving channel has Choi matrix

`J=a I+b(Omega01+Omega02)+c F12+h(Omega01 F12+F12 Omega01)`.

Its four Choi eigenvalues are

`j_+=a+c+(d+1)(b+h)`, `j_-=a-c+(d-1)(b-h)`,

`j_0+=a+c`, `j_0-=a-c`.

Complete positivity is their nonnegativity and trace preservation is

`d^2 a+2d b+d c+2h=1`.

For `d>=3` this is a four-vertex simplex. The vertices are the normalized projectors onto the four invariant Choi spaces. The first two are symmetric and antisymmetric universal cloners. The remaining two live on the orthogonal complements of their dimension-`d` Choi spaces. For `d=2` the antisymmetric complement is absent and the simplex has three vertices.

Every output commutes with `rho tensor rho`. In the output-symmetric basis, the first vertex gives eigenvalues `z/(d+1)`, where `z=2lambda_i` on `|ii>` and `z=lambda_i+lambda_j` on `|ij+>`. Its complementary vertex gives

`[1-z/(d+1)]/[d(d+1)/2-1]`.

The analogous antisymmetric vertices give `z/(d-1)` and

`[1-z/(d-1)]/[d(d-1)/2-1]` on `|ij->`.

Thus fixed-spectrum global fidelity reduces to two independent one-dimensional concave maximizations and one optimal symmetric/antisymmetric mixing weight. If the optimized root contributions of the two sectors are `f_+` and `f_-`, the optimized squared fidelity is `f_+^2+f_-^2`.

A covariant EB channel is depolarizing. Its maximal positive shrinking factor is `1/(d+1)`; choosing it maximizes fidelity to every spectrum because the output moves monotonically toward the target along the line from `I/d` to `rho`. Its exact single-copy squared score is

`F_EB=[sum_i sqrt(lambda_i(lambda_i+1))]^2/(d+1)`.

The script `covariant_mixed_probe.py` evaluates 486 spectra at dimensions `2,3,4,8,16,32,64`, using seed `20261011`, Dirichlet concentrations from `.001` through `100`, every flat rank, and nearly pure limits. No quotient exceeding about `1.956` occurred. All within-sector numerical optima were the ordinary symmetric/antisymmetric cloner vertices. The largest quotients occurred near the maximally mixed state at high dimension. These are ordinary floating-point finite checks, not certificates or a general-orbit inequality.

## Primary context and scope

[Fei–Wang, arXiv:2609.35986v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35986v1), Sections 4.1–4.3, studies many-input projector cloning by Schur-sector compression. Its Proposition 4.2 is a finite-dimensional converse. The one-input third-moment calculation above was reconstructed directly, rather than imported from that proposition.

[Fanizza–Grinko–Scharnhorst–Spilecki, arXiv:2608.27298](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27298), Section 1.2, distinguishes state cloning sample complexity from learning sample complexity. Classical sample amplification does not refute the present EB simulation target: common-basis dephasing is EB and fixes all commuting density matrices exactly. Their random-purification method concerns tensor-power inputs and does not furnish a reference-access lift for an arbitrary given mixed-state cloner.
