# Marginal broadcasting does not imply dimension-free classical simulation

11 October 2026. Team-derived candidate boundary theorem; independently reconstructed by this agent and root. This is an internal analytic proof, not a priority claim or external validation.

## Exact statement

For every integer `L>=2`, there are a finite-dimensional Hilbert space `H_L`, a family of mixed states `rho_psi` on it, and one explicitly defined full-domain channel `B_L:H_L -> H_L tensor H_L` such that both marginals obey, uniformly in `psi`,

`F((B_L)_a(rho_psi),rho_psi)=((L-1)/L)^2`, `a=1,2`.

Here `F` is squared Uhlmann fidelity. Thus both marginal infidelities are `2/L-1/L^2 ->0`.

Nevertheless every EB channel `Lambda:H_L->H_L` has Haar-average fidelity at most

`beta_L=(K+1)(K+2)/[(K+d)(K+d+1)] <=4/d`,

where `K=2^L` and `d=K^2=4^L`. Its average infidelity is at least `1-4/d ->1`.

The same assertions hold for a finite weighted ensemble formed from any complex projective `2K`-design. Such a finite positive weighted design exists by finite-dimensional convexity of the Haar moment operator. No efficient design acquisition or construction is being asserted.

Consequently **no dimension-independent function `g(t)->0` can bound EB simulation infidelity by marginal broadcasting infidelity** for arbitrary mixed state ensembles. In particular no universal linear constant can do so.

## 1. Supplied state family and explicit full-domain broadcaster

Let `h=C^d`, `D_n=dim Sym^n(h)=binom(n+d-1,d-1)`, and

`H_L=directsum_(j=0)^L Sym^(2^j)(h)`.

The physical input dimension is

`dim H_L=sum_(j=0)^L binom(2^j+d-1,d-1)`.

For a unit vector `psi in h`, write `P_psi=|psi><psi|` and define

`rho_psi=(1/L) directsum_(j=1)^L P_psi^tensor(2^j)`.

Its rank is `L`; all nonzero eigenvalues are `1/L`. Sector zero belongs to the ambient Hilbert space but has zero input probability.

Let `Pi_j` project onto sector `j`. For `j>=1`, the canonical inclusion obtained by splitting the tensor registers is an isometry

`V_j:Sym^(2^j)(h) -> Sym^(2^(j-1))(h) tensor Sym^(2^(j-1))(h)`,

with

`V_j psi^tensor(2^j)=psi^tensor(2^(j-1)) tensor psi^tensor(2^(j-1))`.

This map is explicit in normalized occupation bases: if `a=b+c`, `|a|=2n`, `|b|=|c|=n`, its coefficient is

`sqrt[prod_r binom(a_r,b_r) / binom(2n,n)]`.

The multivariate Vandermonde identity verifies preservation of norms. Choose any fixed density matrix `omega_0` in sector zero and set

`B_L(X)=sum_(j=1)^L V_j Pi_j X Pi_j V_j* +tr(Pi_0 X) omega_0 tensor omega_0`.

This is completely positive and trace preserving on the entire ambient operator algebra. It first measures the copy-count sector and then divides its supplied quantum copies into two groups. It does not infer the unknown `psi` or acquire additional copies.

On the promised family its output is

`B_L(rho_psi)=(1/L) sum_(j=1)^L [P_psi^tensor(2^(j-1))] tensor [P_psi^tensor(2^(j-1))]`,

where the two output sectors are equal. Each marginal has weight `1/L` on sectors `0,...,L-1`, whereas the target has that weight on sectors `1,...,L`. Their common rank-one blocks agree exactly. Therefore their root fidelity is `(L-1)/L`, proving the stated marginal score.

## 2. Elementary EB support bound

The target support projector is

`A_psi=directsum_(j=1)^L P_psi^tensor(2^j)`.

The binary measurement `{A_psi,I-A_psi}` accepts the target with probability one. Fidelity monotonicity therefore gives

`F(rho_psi,Lambda(rho_psi)) <=tr[A_psi Lambda(rho_psi)]`.

This does not assume that the channel output is block diagonal.

Write an arbitrary EB channel in measure-and-prepare form

`Lambda(X)=sum_x tr(M_x X) sigma_x`,

where `M_x>=0`, `sum_x M_x=I_H`, `sigma_x>=0`, and `tr sigma_x=1`. For input sector `i` and output sector `j`, put `M_xi=Pi_i M_x Pi_i` and `sigma_xj=Pi_j sigma_x Pi_j`.

The Haar moment identity is

`integral P_psi^tensor t dpsi=Pi_sym^t/D_t`.

For `n=2^i` and `q=2^j`, the averaged overlap contribution satisfies

`integral sum_x tr(M_xi P_psi^tensor n) tr(sigma_xj P_psi^tensor q) dpsi`

`=1/D_(n+q) sum_x tr[(M_xi tensor sigma_xj) Pi_sym^(n+q)]`

`<=1/D_(n+q) sum_x tr(M_xi) tr(sigma_xj)`.

The inequality uses only `Pi_sym^(n+q)<=I` on `Sym^n(h) tensor Sym^q(h)`.

Define the average output-sector probability

`q_ji=(1/D_n) sum_x tr(M_xi) tr(sigma_xj)`.

For every input sector, `sum_(j=0)^L q_ji=1`. The averaged support score is therefore bounded by

`(1/L) sum_(i=1)^L sum_(j=1)^L q_ji D_(2^i)/D_(2^i+2^j)`

`<=max_(n<=K,q>=2) D_n/D_(n+q)`

`=D_K/D_(K+2)`

`=(K+1)(K+2)/[(K+d)(K+d+1)]`.

Monotonicity of the dimension ratio in `n` and `q` follows directly from its product formula. Since `d=K^2` and `K>=2`, its numerator is at most `4K^2` and its denominator is at least `K^4`, yielding the convenient bound `4/d`.

This bound holds for every EB channel, including collective measurements across the entire input register, arbitrary classical postprocessing, and arbitrary mixed prepared output states. It is not restricted to estimation followed by independent-copy preparation.

## 3. Exact finite ensemble existence and the charged interface

Take `T=2K`. The Haar moment `Pi_sym^T/D_T` belongs to the convex hull of the compact set `{P_psi^tensor T}` in a real vector space of dimension at most `D_T^2`. Caratheodory's theorem gives finitely many unit vectors `psi_s` and positive weights `p_s` summing to one such that

`sum_s p_s P_(psi_s)^tensor T=Pi_sym^T/D_T`,

with at most `D_T^2+1` support points. Partial traces give the exact Haar moment identities for every degree at most `T`. Every degree used in the EB support proof is `n+q<=2K`, so the complete inequality holds for this finite ensemble without approximation. The marginal score is pointwise in `psi`, hence also holds for these points.

This is a finite ensemble existence argument. The design can be enormously large; neither its acquisition nor the preparation of `rho_psi` is being granted for free. The interface supplies one mixed state whose conditional sector contains up to `K` existing copies of an unknown `d`-dimensional pure state. Both coherent and EB protocols receive that same supplied state. The broadcaster uses the copy reservoir already present in its input. No efficient learning or computational speedup is claimed.

## 4. Why the joint-product theorem is unaffected

The output copy-count registers are perfectly correlated, while `rho_psi tensor rho_psi` has independent uniform sectors. Their common support consists only of equal sectors `1,...,L-1`, and the quantum states on those blocks agree. Thus the exact joint root fidelity is

`(L-1)/L^(3/2)`,

and the squared joint-product fidelity is

`F(B_L(rho_psi),rho_psi tensor rho_psi)=(L-1)^2/L^3 ->0`.

The product-cloning infidelity therefore tends to one. This family satisfies the marginal premise with vanishing error while strongly violating the joint independent-product premise. It isolates the independence requirement in the mixed classicalization theorem.

## Short alternative proof of the EB benchmark

One may give the EB protocol the stronger input `P_psi^tensor K`. A channel `R` obtains `rho_psi` from it by choosing `j` uniformly from `1,...,L`, discarding `K-2^j` tensor factors, and attaching the corresponding sector label. For any EB `Lambda`, the composite `Lambda R` is EB. Write its POVM on `Sym^K(h)` as `{N_x}` and prepared output as `tau_x`.

For each output sector `q=2^j`, the Haar moment gives

`integral tr(N_x P_psi^tensor K) tr(tau_xj P_psi^tensor q) dpsi <=tr(N_x)tr(tau_xj)/D_(K+q)`.

Sum over `j>=1`, use `D_(K+q)>=D_(K+2)`, `sum_j tr(tau_xj)<=1`, and `sum_x tr(N_x)=D_K`. This gives the complete all-support acceptance bound `D_K/D_(K+2)` immediately. The stronger K-copy input is used only for a mathematical upper bound; both actual protocols in the comparison receive the identical mixed input `rho_psi`.

## Trace-distance consequence and failure mechanism

Fuchs–van de Graaf gives marginal half trace distance at most `sqrt(2/L)`, uniformly on the family. For every EB channel the average half trace distance is at least

`1-average sqrt(F)>=1-sqrt(average F)>=1-2/sqrt(d) ->1`.

Thus dimension-free classicalization fails in trace distance as well as fidelity. The broadcaster can split a copy reservoir, but its output sector labels remain equal. This correlation is exactly what prevents independent-sample majority amplification. Marginal accuracy supplies no replacement for the product-target Bures bound used in the successful mixed theorem.
