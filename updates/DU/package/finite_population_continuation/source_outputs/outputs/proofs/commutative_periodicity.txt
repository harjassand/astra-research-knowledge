# Exact eventual periodicity of prescribed correlated product laws

Status: newly derived in this worker, independent of the general proof in the companion note except for standard eventual power periods. Historical originality and external validation are unresolved. The generic facts about finite-dimensional polytopes and signs of polynomials are established mathematics; the application to arbitrary-support semigroup product couplings is the candidate result.

## The theorem

Let S be a finite commutative semigroup with m elements. Fix probability laws μ and ν on S, with arbitrary zero entries. For n≥1, let P_n(μ) be the laws of X_1⋯X_n under arbitrary joint distributions with every coordinate marginal μ. Put A=supp μ, q=|A|. For each a∈A let τ_a and p_a be its eventual power index and period, and set

    τ=max_a τ_a,   p=max_a p_a,   L=lcm_a p_a,
    D=τ+p²,       N_v=q(D−1)+1,
    H=max{1,(q−1)(D−1)},    r=q+m.

**Exact eventual-periodicity theorem.** For every fixed real μ,ν there is N such that

    ν∈P_n(μ) ⇔ ν∈P_{n+L}(μ)     for every n≥N.

Thus the horizons attaining exactly the prescribed output law are eventually a union of residue classes modulo L. In particular infinitely-often attainability and eventual attainability along a fixed residue class are equivalent. This does not say every law in the limiting transport polytope is exactly attainable; the V-shaped join example in the companion note is a counterexample.

For rational μ,ν choose a positive integer Q with all Qμ(a),Qν(z) integers. Define

    W=r!(2H)^r,
    V=Q(2rH+1)W,
    N_s=2(r+2)V+1,
    N=max{N_v,N_s}.

Then this explicit N is a sufficient stabilization cutoff. Its binary length is O(log Q+m log m). Constants are deliberately loose. The result is not a polynomial-time claim in m: an explicit universal construction below can use m^{O(m)} word patterns and enumerating their basic supports can take m^{O(m²)} arithmetic operations.

## 1. Exchangeable count fibers

For a count vector k=(k_a)_{a∈A}∈Z_+^A with Σ_a k_a=n, define its product

    π(k)=∏_{a∈A}a^{k_a},

using a fresh formal identity for zero powers. The final product belongs to S because n≥1. Let

    K_{n,z}=conv{k: Σ_a k_a=n, π(k)=z}.

The polytope is empty if the fiber is empty. Exact feasibility of (μ,ν) is equivalent to the existence of a probability distribution on these count vectors with mean nμ and product law ν. One direction takes empirical counts of any coupling; the converse randomly permutes a sampled multiset. Commutativity is essential to the converse.

## 2. Every count-fiber vertex has a bounded defect

Suppose a fiber vector k has two coordinates k_a,k_b≥D. Put ℓ=lcm(p_a,p_b)≤p². Both

    k⁺=k+ℓ(e_a−e_b),    k⁻=k−ℓ(e_a−e_b)

are nonnegative, retain total count n, and have the same product. Indeed each decreased exponent is at least D−ℓ≥τ, and shifts by ℓ preserve both powers in their periodic ranges. Hence k=(k⁺+k⁻)/2 is not a vertex of K_{n,z}.

Therefore every vertex has at most one coordinate at least D. If n≥N_v=q(D−1)+1, it has exactly one, say its base a; all non-base counts lie in {0,…,D−1}. It consequently has the form

    k=n e_a+δ,
    δ_b=d_b∈{0,…,D−1} for b≠a,
    δ_a=−Σ_{b≠a}d_b.

The non-base total is at most (q−1)(D−1), and the base exponent is at least D.

Conversely every such pattern with product z is an actual integer point in the fiber. Since all fiber vertices occur in this finite pattern list,

    K_{n,z}=conv{n e_a+δ : the finite pattern (a,δ) has product z}.

For n≥N_v, the product of each fixed pattern depends only on n modulo p_a, and hence only on n modulo L. Along one residue class the list of patterns assigned to each z is fixed. This is a stronger finite-n statement than an asymptotic approximation of count fibers.

## 3. Exact coupling is a one-parameter bounded LP

Fix a residue class c modulo L. Index the finite patterns by j; write their base as a_j, offset as δ_j, and fixed product as z_j. Introduce nonnegative weights λ_j. For n≥N_v in this residue, exact feasibility is equivalent to

    Σ_j λ_j(e_{a_j}+tδ_j)=μ,         t=1/n,
    Σ_{j:z_j=z}λ_j=ν(z)             for every z∈S,
    λ_j≥0.

The output equalities imply Σ_jλ_j=1, so the feasible region is bounded. This LP has r=q+m equality rows, with a matrix A(t) whose entries are affine polynomials in t. There are at most qD^{q−1} patterns.

If feasible at a given t, the LP has a feasible point with linearly independent active columns: if active columns are dependent, a dependence vector has coordinate sum zero (sum the output rows), so one can perturb in both directions until one active weight becomes zero. Repeating produces independent support of size k≤r.

Choose k equality rows making that active k×k matrix invertible. Cramer's rule gives every active λ_j as a ratio of two polynomials in t. The remaining equalities become polynomial equalities after multiplying by the determinant. Therefore feasibility is a finite union, over column and row choices, of conditions of the form

    polynomial(t)=0,   polynomial(t)>0,   polynomial(t)≥0.

Every nonzero real polynomial has constant sign on a sufficiently short interval (0,ε), and every zero polynomial vanishes identically. Finitely many polynomials are involved. Thus each candidate basic solution is either feasible throughout some interval (0,ε) or infeasible throughout it. Their finite union has the same property. This proves eventual constancy of exact feasibility on each residue, and hence the theorem for real μ,ν.

No quantifier-elimination oracle is assumed. The elementary leading-coefficient test gives an exact rational algorithm: compute the relevant determinants and residual polynomials, reject nonzero residual equalities, and test signs using their first nonzero coefficients at zero.

## 4. Explicit rational stabilization bound

All constant and linear coefficients of A(t) have absolute value at most H. A determinant of size k≤r has degree at most r and coefficients bounded by

    W=r!(2H)^r.

Use the common denominator Q. Multiplying each Cramer numerator by Q makes it an integer polynomial with coefficients at most QW. After substitution in a remaining equality, the resulting integer residual polynomial has degree at most r+1 and coefficients at most

    V=Q(2rH+1)W.

This same V also bounds all denominator and scaled numerator polynomials. For any nonzero such polynomial, divide out its lowest power of t. Its constant coefficient is a nonzero integer and hence has absolute value at least one. If

    0<t<1/[2(r+2)V],

the sum of all higher terms has absolute value less than 1/2. Its sign is therefore the sign of that constant coefficient. Every candidate's signs and equality truth values stabilize on this one common interval. Taking n≥N_s gives the claimed uniform cutoff, simultaneously for all residue classes because the coefficient bounds do not depend on the residue.

Since q≤m, τ,p≤m, H≤m(m+m²−1), r≤2m, and log(r!)=O(m log m), the binary length of N is O(log Q+m log m).

## 5. Consequences and limits

For rational μ,ν, exact infinitely-often attainability is decidable by checking one sufficiently large n in each residue class. Exact attainment at every sufficiently large n is decidable by requiring all classes to pass. One may use the existing compact finite-horizon LP reported by Astra N674 for each check; that LP itself is prior to this worker's result.

The new content is the bounded-defect vertex reduction and its eventual-periodicity consequence for exact prescribed output laws with missing input support. The result uses a supplied finite multiplication table and exact marginals; it does not acquire an algebraic representation, solve arbitrary multi-marginal transport efficiently, or establish historical priority.

The companion note supplies a simpler polynomial-size local-unit relation for approximate asymptotic optimization and a sharp 1/n approximation rate. The present finite pattern list can be exponentially larger; its purpose is exact boundary and horizon classification.
