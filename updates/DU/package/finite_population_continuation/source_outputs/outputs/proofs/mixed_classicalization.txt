# Mixed product-cloning classicalization: complete internal reconstruction

11 October 2026. Independent reconstruction of the class-gap induction suggested by the independent inventor, with root's rational constants and spectral-polar measurement rounding. This is an internally reconstructed analytic argument, not a formal verification or priority claim. The physical interface is one supplied full-domain channel; no purification access, environment access, acquisition oracle, or copies of the unknown input are assumed.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let `H` have finite dimension `d`, and let `B:L(H)->L(H tensor H)` be any CPTP map. For every density matrix `rho`, define

`e(rho)=1-F(B(rho),rho tensor rho)`,

where `F(sigma,tau)=||sqrt(sigma)sqrt(tau)||_1^2` is squared Uhlmann fidelity. Thus the premise concerns the full output and the independent product target, not only its marginals.

There is one entanglement-breaking channel `T_B:L(H)->L(H)`, depending on `B` but independent of the input state and its prior, such that for **all density matrices**

`1-F(T_B(rho),rho) <= 2800 e(rho)`.

Moreover, it can be implemented by an isometry `U:H->C^N`, one coordinate projective measurement, and conditional preparation of fixed density matrices, with

`d <= N <= 2d`.

Equivalently, one fixed pure ancilla qubit suffices for a unitary extension and one orthogonal measurement. The acquisition of `B`, the construction of the continuum good-set partition, and the numerical synthesis of `U` are separate costs; this existence theorem does not give a computational bound for them.

For any finite mixed ensemble, minimizing the two losses gives the universal average tradeoff `e_EB <= 2800 e_clone` with the same global product-cloning score. No dependence on dimension, cardinality, rank, eigenvalue lower bounds, or prior occurs in the constant.

## 2. Pair geometry

Set `tau=1/2800`, and retain the compact good set `S={rho:e(rho)<=tau}`. Write

`r(rho,sigma)=sqrt(F(rho,sigma))`.

If `S` is empty, any fixed-output channel and any `d`-dimensional projective measurement suffice, because the right side of the theorem exceeds `1` on every input. The remainder assumes `S` nonempty.

Fidelity monotonicity through `B` and the Bures-angle triangle inequality give

`g(r):=acos(r^2)-acos(r) <= asin sqrt(e(rho))+asin sqrt(e(sigma))`. (2.1)

For `0<=x<=sqrt(tau)`, `asin x <=x/sqrt(1-tau)<(6/5)x`. Also

`g(r)=integral_(r^2)^r (1-t^2)^(-1/2)dt >=r(1-r)`.

Consequently `r(1-r)<(12/5)/sqrt(2800)<1/20`. On `[1/16,15/16]` the left side is at least `15/256>1/20`, so every good pair obeys

`r<1/16` or `r>15/16`. (2.2)

The high-overlap relation is transitive: if `r(rho,sigma),r(sigma,omega)>15/16`, Bures-angle triangle implies `r(rho,omega)>97/128>1/16`, hence (2.2) puts it above `15/16`. Thus `r>3/4` defines equivalence classes of `S`.

Each class is closed in `S`, because the gap in (2.2) replaces the strict threshold by a closed threshold. There are finitely many classes: one representative from each would otherwise give an infinite uniformly separated subset of a compact metric space. Each class therefore has a representative `sigma_c` minimizing `e` in that class.

For same-class states let `s=sqrt(1-r^2)` and `D=e(rho)+e(sigma)`. The elementary estimate

`g(r)>=r(1-r)/sqrt(1-r^4)`

`=[r/((1+r)sqrt(1+r^2))]s >=(12/35)s`, for `r>=3/4`,

together with (2.1), gives

`s <=(7/2)(sqrt(e(rho))+sqrt(e(sigma)))`,

`1-F(rho,sigma) <=(49/2)D <25D`. (2.3)

The coefficient `r/((1+r)sqrt(1+r^2))` is increasing on `[0,1]`: its logarithmic derivative has numerator `1-r^3`; its value at `3/4` is `12/35`.

In particular, minimum-error representatives satisfy the pointwise bound

`1-F(rho,sigma_c)<=50e(rho)` for `rho` in class `c`. (2.4)

## 3. Three-copy decision transformation

Define the twice-used channel `B3=(id tensor B)B`. Its output is on three copies of `H`. For Bures distance `d_B(x,y)=sqrt(2-2sqrt(F(x,y)))`, contraction, triangle inequality, and tensor invariance give

`d_B(B3(rho),rho^tensor3)<=2d_B(B(rho),rho^tensor2)<=2sqrt(2e(rho))`. (3.1)

For every effect `A`,

`|sqrt(tr A x)-sqrt(tr A y)|<=d_B(x,y)`. (3.2)

This follows by Uhlmann purification and the reverse triangle inequality for the projected vectors; the purifications are used only to prove the scalar inequality, not as an operational interface.

Given any class POVM `M`, measure it independently on the three output systems of `B3`, then output the repeated label if any label repeats. If all three labels differ, output a fixed label. On the ideal product state, an incorrect answer requires at least two incorrect individual outcomes, hence has probability at most `3r_M(rho)^2`. By (3.1)-(3.2), the pulled-back POVM has

`r'_M(rho)<=(sqrt(3) r_M(rho)+sqrt(8e(rho)))^2`. (3.3)

No independence of the actual outputs is asserted or needed.

## 4. Uniform weak classification by a forbidden-error interval

Put `a=1/200`, `b=sqrt(8tau)=1/sqrt(350)`, and `f(x)=(sqrt(3)x+b)^2`. We will use the exact gap

`f(x)<x` for `a<=x<=13/50`. (4.1)

Indeed `sqrt(3)<7/4`, `b<3/56`, and

`f(1/200)<(7/800+3/56)^2=(349/5600)^2<1/256<1/200`,

`f(13/50)<(91/200+3/56)^2=(89/175)^2<13/50`.

The quadratic `f(x)-x` is convex, so it is negative throughout the interval between these two points.

For any chosen `m` classes let `R_m` be the smallest possible worst-state wrong-class probability, maximizing over all states in those classes. The optimization attains its minimum because the finite-outcome POVM set is compact and the worst-state risk is continuous. Applying (3.3) to an optimizer gives

`R_m<=f(R_m)`. (4.2)

Thus `R_m` cannot belong to `[a,13/50]`.

For `m<=3`, first classify the representatives. The standard mixed-state pretty-good-measurement error bound, valid for every prior `p`, is

`P_error^PGM <=sum_(c!=j) sqrt(p_c p_j) sqrt(F(sigma_c,sigma_j))`

` <(m-1)/16 <=1/8`.

The sum of `sqrt(p_c p_j)` is at most `m-1`. Finite-dimensional minimax, applied to the affine decision error, therefore gives one representative POVM with all representative errors at most `1/8`.

Purified distance `D_P(x,y)=sqrt(1-F(x,y))` is a contractive metric. Applying it to the POVM and the point mass on the correct label, and then using (2.4), yields for every state in the corresponding whole class

`sqrt(r_M(rho))<=sqrt(1/8)+sqrt(50tau)`.

Thus

`R_m <=(1/sqrt(8)+1/sqrt(56))^2`

`=1/7+1/(4sqrt(7))<1/4<13/50`.

The last strict inequality follows from `sqrt(7)>7/3`. Combining with (4.1)-(4.2) gives `R_m<a` for `m<=3`.

Inductively, suppose every collection of `m-1` classes has a POVM with all its errors at most `a`. Choose uniformly at random one of the `m` classes to omit and use that collection's POVM, extended with a zero effect for the omitted class. Averaging these POVMs gives

`R_m<=1/m+(1-1/m)a <=1/4+(3/4)a=203/800<13/50`, for `m>=4`.

Again (4.1)-(4.2) force `R_m<a`. This proves that one POVM on **all states of all good classes** has uniform error at most `a=1/200`.

This whole-class induction avoids any need to coherently purify mixed states, construct a scalar fidelity Gram matrix, or acquire an unknown label.

## 5. Tight-cap optimality turns weak classification into linear loss

Let `K` be the compact convex set of all class POVMs with `r_M(rho)<=a` for every `rho` in `S`. Section 4 proves `K` nonempty. Equation (3.3) preserves this entire constraint set, since `f(a)<a`.

For any finite prior on `S`, minimize `R=sum p_i r_M(rho_i)` over `K`; put `E=sum p_i e(rho_i)`. Optimality under the transformed POVM, (3.3), and the `L2` triangle inequality give

`R <=(sqrt(3sum p_i r_i^2)+sqrt(8E))^2`

` <=(sqrt(3aR)+sqrt(8E))^2`.

Therefore

`R<=C E`, where `C=8/(1-sqrt(3/200))^2<512/49<11`. (5.1)

The rational estimate uses `sqrt(3/200)<1/8`.

For each finite list of good states, minimax over its priors, still optimizing over the same global set `K`, produces one POVM with `r_M(rho_i)<=C e(rho_i)` for every state in that list. The subsets of `K` defined by these finitely many linear inequalities are closed and have the finite-intersection property. Compactness gives one `M` satisfying

`r_M(rho)<=C e(rho)` for **every** `rho` in `S`. (5.2)

Prepare `sigma_c` after class outcome `c`. Squared fidelity is separately concave: for fixed `rho`, this follows for instance from the variational identity

`F(rho,sigma)=inf_(H>0) tr(rho H) tr(sigma H^-1)`.

Hence (2.4)-(5.2) imply

`1-F(T_M(rho),rho)<=r_M(rho)+50e(rho)<61e(rho)` on `S`. (5.3)

Outside `S`, every output has loss at most `1<2800e(rho)`. This already proves the fixed-channel pointwise theorem. Taking a cloning optimizer and then averaging proves the ensemble theorem.

There are at most `floor(d/(1-Ctau))` prepared class representatives: since every density matrix is at most `I`,

`k(1-Ctau)<=sum_c tr(M_c sigma_c)<=sum_c tr(M_c)=d`.

This counts prepared states, not the ranks of measurement effects.

## 6. Mixed-state spectral-polar rounding to one ancilla qubit

For the pointwise POVM `M` from Section 5 let

`P_c=1_[1/2,1](M_c)`.

Spectral calculus gives `P_c<=2M_c` and `I-P_c<=2(I-M_c)`. Consequently `S0=sum P_c<=2I`, and

`N0=sum rank(P_c)=tr(S0)<=2d`.

Define `T:H->direct_sum_c ran(P_c)` by `T psi=direct_sum_c P_c psi`. Pad its codomain by zero rows to dimension `N=max(d,N0)<=2d`. Its polar decomposition can be completed to an isometry `U:H->C^N`, with

`T=U sqrt(S0)`.

Such completion exists because `N>=d`; it agrees with the polar partial isometry on the support of `S0`. In particular

`(U-T)^*(U-T)=(I-sqrt(S0))^2 <=(I-S0)^2`.

The last inequality is scalar functional calculus, `|1-sqrt(s)|<=|1-s|` for `s>=0`; no assumption that `T` is a contraction is made.

Fix good `rho` in class `c`, with `r=tr rho(I-M_c)`. Put `A=sum_(j!=c)P_j` and `Q=I-P_c`. Then

`A<=2(I-M_c)`, `Q<=2(I-M_c)`, `||A||<=2`, and `Q^2=Q`.

Since `S0-I=A-Q`,

`(S0-I)^2<=(2A^2+2Q^2)<=4A+2Q`,

so

`tr rho (U-T)^*(U-T)<=12r`.

The probability that `T` lands outside the `c` block is `tr rho A<=2r`. The Hilbert-Schmidt triangle inequality for the wrong-block projection therefore gives

`r_U(rho)<=(sqrt(2r)+sqrt(12r))^2`

`=(14+4sqrt(6))r<24r`. (6.1)

Measure coordinates of `U rho U^*` and prepare `sigma_c` for coordinates in block `c`; padded coordinates prepare an arbitrary existing representative. This is a rank-one-effect POVM with `N<=2d` outcomes. Separate concavity and (2.4), (5.2), (6.1) imply its good-state fidelity loss is

`<=24C e(rho)+50e(rho)<314e(rho)<2800e(rho)`.

The bad-state accounting is unchanged. Embed `C^N` into `H tensor C^2`; `U` extends from the fixed input-ancilla subspace to a unitary on the `2d`-dimensional space. One coordinate PVM and the fixed conditional preparations realize the claimed channel.

## 7. One fixed commuting core after dilation

Let `Pi_c` denote the orthogonal coordinate-block projector used by the rounded isometry `U` in Section 6. For each representative put

`p_c=tr(Pi_c U sigma_c U^*)`,

`Omega_c=Pi_c U sigma_c U^* Pi_c /p_c`.

The denominator is positive: `p_c>=1-24C e(sigma_c)>=1-24Ctau>0`. The `Omega_c` have mutually orthogonal supports and therefore commute.

A purification of `U sigma_c U^*`, projected by `Pi_c` on its system and normalized, purifies `Omega_c` and has overlap `sqrt(p_c)` with the original purification. Uhlmann therefore gives

`F(U sigma_c U^*,Omega_c)>=p_c>=1-24C e(sigma_c)`.

For every good `rho` in class `c`, purified-distance triangle, isometric invariance, (2.4), and representative minimality give

`sqrt(1-F(U rho U^*,Omega_c))`

`<=sqrt(1-F(rho,sigma_c))+sqrt(1-F(U sigma_c U^*,Omega_c))`

`<=(sqrt(50)+sqrt(24C))sqrt(e(rho))`.

Thus

`1-F(U rho U^*,Omega_c)<550 e(rho)`.

Every good mixed input is close, in a single fixed dilation, to one member of a finite orthogonal family of mixed templates. The template count is at most `floor[d/(1-Ctau)]`. This is a dilation representation: `Omega_c` need not lie in `U(H)`, and pulling it back by `U^*` need not yield normalized commuting density matrices in the original space. The physical EB implementation from Section 6 prepares `sigma_c` on `H`.

## 8. Resource and boundary accounting

The implementation uses one supplied physical input, one fixed pure qubit ancilla at the measurement stage, a unitary, one orthogonal measurement, and conditional preparation. Output preparation, circuit synthesis, classical outcome storage, and model/ensemble acquisition are charged separately. The proof's twice-used cloner is a mathematical compiler device establishing the measurement's existence; executing the resulting EB channel does not require calls to `B`.

The pure regular-simplex construction in `preparation_and_ancilla_obstructions.md` shows that, for every fixed universal reconstruction constant, some supplied cloner requires more than `d` effective prepared states/outcomes. Thus a single fixed projective measurement on the `d`-dimensional input alone cannot universally achieve the pointwise guarantee. This lower statement uses the same fixed-measurement architecture: arbitrary uncharged random choice among many premeasurement bases would change the outcome/resource interface, and is not ruled out by a count of `d` outcomes.

The sharp-order `O(d epsilon)` excess Naimark dimension from the pure rank-one representative construction remains a pure-state refinement. The mixed spectral-polar construction proves `N<=2d`; no mixed `O(d epsilon)` claim is made here.

## 9. Elementary derivation of the mixed-state PGM bound

For prior `p_i` and states `rho_i`, put `Gamma_i=sqrt(p_i)sqrt(rho_i)`, form the block row `Gamma=[Gamma_1 ... Gamma_m]`, and write

`sigma=Gamma Gamma^*=sum p_i rho_i`, `G=Gamma^*Gamma`,

`G0=diag(p_1rho_1,...,p_mrho_m)`.

The PGM is `M_i=sigma^(-1/2)p_i rho_i sigma^(-1/2)` on the support of `sigma`; extend it arbitrarily to a POVM on its orthogonal complement. Pseudoinverses are only taken on this support. Polar decomposition gives

`Gamma^*sigma^(-1/2)Gamma=sqrt(G)`.

Consequently its success probability is `sum_i tr[(sqrt(G))_(ii)^2]`. Since `trG=1`, its error is

`sum_(i!=j)||(sqrt(G))_(ij)||_HS^2`

`<=||sqrt(G)-sqrt(G0)||_HS^2`

`<=||G-G0||_1`

`<=sum_(i!=j)||Gamma_i^*Gamma_j||_1`

`=sum_(i!=j)sqrt(p_i p_j)sqrt(F(rho_i,rho_j))`.

The penultimate inequality embeds each off-diagonal block as a rectangular coordinate block and uses the trace-norm triangle inequality.

For completeness, the square-root inequality in this calculation has the following short proof. For positive `X,Y` put `D=X-Y` and `S=sign(D)`. Trace-norm duality and cyclicity imply

`||X^2-Y^2||_1>=tr[S(XD+DY)]=tr[|D|(X+Y)]>=tr[D^2]`.

For the last inequality diagonalize `D`: in each eigenvector its diagonal expectation `x+y` is at least `|x-y|=|D_jj|`, since `X,Y` are positive. Apply this with `X=sqrt(G)` and `Y=sqrt(G0)`. Thus no external PGM theorem is a missing mathematical premise.

The other standard ingredients are Uhlmann's theorem, fidelity monotonicity, the Bures-angle and purified-distance metrics, finite-dimensional minimax, compactness, and the separate-concavity variational formula for squared fidelity. None grants physical access to a purification or an environment.
