# Independent proof of quantitative operational replication rigidity

Cross-corpus reconstruction, 11 October 2026. Status: complete written mathematical reconstruction under the exact closure hypotheses below. This does not establish priority or external certification.

## 1. Statement and operational contract

Each system has a compact normalized convex state space `Omega_A` spanning a finite-dimensional ordered real vector space `V_A`. Allowed finite measurements consist of affine effects, separate states, and form a compact convex set for each fixed outcome number. They are closed under classical randomization, relabeling and outcome processing. Systems compose associatively; product states and product measurements, discarding, preparation of fixed states, and classical conditional preparation are allowed. Physical maps can act on arbitrary correlated spectators. Pullback of any allowed measurement by a physical map is allowed. Appending a fixed allowed state and discarding it are physical.

The proof does **not** assume purification, self-duality, local tomography, unrestricted effects, or that all abstract positive maps are physical. It does require the stated closure of the actual operational devices. In particular, a merely positive affine map with no allowed spectator extension would not suffice as the supplied cloner.

Let `S` be any nonempty compact subset of `Omega_A`, and `B:A->AA` any supplied physical transformation. Define

\[
 r(x,y)=\inf_N\sum_j\sqrt{N_j(x)N_j(y)},\quad
 F(x,y)=r(x,y)^2,\quad A(x,y)=\arccos r(x,y),
\]

\[
 b(x,y)=\sqrt{2(1-r(x,y))},\qquad
 e(x)=1-F(Bx,x\otimes x).
 \tag{1}
\]

The defect compares the **entire output** with two independent copies. A statement about accurate marginals alone does not provide this premise.

**Theorem.** There are an allowed measurement `M` with `k<=dim span(S)` outcomes and representatives `s_1,...,s_k in S` such that one and the same proper estimator satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\sum_cM_c(x)[1-F(x,s_c)]\le2800e(x)\qquad(x\in S).}
 \tag{2}
\]

The estimator depends on `B,S`, not on the unknown input or a prior. The output representatives are affine independent whenever the good set below is nonempty. The theorem is an existence/compilation reduction with supplied operational descriptions; it includes no efficient acquisition or optimization guarantee.

## 2. Geometry that replaces quantum structure

For a fixed measurement, the square-root probability vector lies on a unit sphere. Its spherical angle and Euclidean distance are metrics. Because `acos` and `sqrt(2(1-.))` decrease, `A` and `b` are the suprema of these metrics over measurements. Therefore they are metrics too. Pullback of measurements implies contractivity under physical maps.

Appending a fixed state preserves both metrics: appending and discarding give the two opposing contractivity inequalities. Product measurements give

\[
 r(x^{\otimes2},y^{\otimes2})\le r(x,y)^2.
 \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, use twice a measurement whose affinity approaches the single-copy infimum. No minimizer for the fidelity infimum is needed. Exact product multiplicativity is not asserted.

Define operational total variation distance

\[
 d(x,y)=\sup_N\operatorname{TV}(Nx,Ny).
\]

Coarse-graining makes this the supremum over binary effects. Classical inequalities imply

\[
 1-r(x,y)\le d(x,y)\le\sqrt{1-r(x,y)^2},
 \qquad |\sqrt{f(x)}-\sqrt{f(y)}|\le b(x,y)
 \tag{4}
\]

for every allowed event effect `f`. To justify the right-hand total-variation bound without assuming a common optimizing measurement: every measurement has root affinity at least `r`, so its total variation is at most `sqrt(1-r^2)`; now take the supremum. For the left bound, every measurement obeys `1-affinity<=TV`, and take the affinity infimum.

All allowed effects lie in the compact finite-dimensional order interval `0<=f<=u`, so `d` is continuous in the state topology. Thus `d(x_n,x)->0`, implying `r(x_n,x)->1` and `A(x_n,x)->0`. The angle triangle then gives joint continuity of `A` and `r`. Consequently `e` is continuous, including on boundary states.

Use the cloner twice, on one of its outputs:

\[
 C=(B\otimes\mathrm{id})B:A\longrightarrow A^3.
\]

Insert `Bx tensor x` as an intermediate state. Triangle, contraction and fixed-ancilla invariance give

\[
 b(Cx,x^3)\le2b(Bx,x^2)\le\sqrt{8e(x)}.
 \tag{5}
\]

The last inequality uses `b(Bx,x^2)^2=2(1-sqrt(1-e))<=2e`. Combining (4),(5), any event having probability `q` on the ideal product has probability at most `(sqrt(q)+sqrt(8e))^2` on the actual output.

## 3. Good states split into compact, separated classes

Set `tau=1/2800` and `G={x in S:e(x)<=tau}`. If `G` is empty, choose any representative and a trivial measurement; then the loss is at most one, which is less than `2800e(x)` everywhere. Assume `G` is nonempty.

For `x,y in G` write `r=r(x,y)`. Contractivity of `B`, (3), and the angle triangle give

\[
 g(r):=\arccos(r^2)-\arccos r
 \le\arcsin\sqrt{e(x)}+\arcsin\sqrt{e(y)}.
 \tag{6}
\]

This inequality uses tensor submultiplicativity in the correct direction: the target product angle is at least `acos(r^2)`.

Since

\[
 g(r)=\int_{r^2}^r{dt\over\sqrt{1-t^2}}\ge r(1-r),
\]

and `asin z<6z/5` for `0<=z<=sqrt(tau)`,

\[
 r(1-r)\le{6\over5}(\sqrt{e(x)}+\sqrt{e(y)})
 \le{12\over5\sqrt{2800}}<{1\over20}.
\]

For `r in [1/16,15/16]`, however, `r(1-r)>=15/256>1/20`. Every good pair therefore obeys the gap

\[
 \boxed{r<1/16\quad\hbox{or}\quad r>15/16.}
 \tag{7}
\]

Define `x~y` by the second alternative. If `x~y` and `y~z`, angle triangle gives

\[
 r(x,z)>\cos(2\arccos(15/16))=97/128>1/16,
\]

so (7) forces `x~z`. This is an equivalence relation. Its classes are compact: the strict high-overlap relation agrees, on `G`, with the closed relation `r>=15/16`. Distinct classes have a common positive angle separation, so compactness of `G` gives finitely many classes.

For a same-class pair, `r>3/4`. The sharper integral bound is

\[
 g(r)\ge{r(1-r)\over\sqrt{1-r^4}}
 ={r\over(1+r)\sqrt{1+r^2}}\sqrt{1-r^2}
 \ge{12\over35}\sqrt{1-r^2}.
\]

The coefficient is increasing on `[3/4,1]`; its logarithmic derivative has the sign of `1-r^3`. Combining with (6) gives

\[
 \sqrt{1-r^2}\le{7\over2}(\sqrt{e(x)}+\sqrt{e(y)}),
 \qquad
 1-F(x,y)\le25(e(x)+e(y)).
 \tag{8}
\]

The endpoint `r=1` is trivial. Select a minimum-defect representative `s_c` of each compact class. Then

\[
 e(s_c)\le e(x),\qquad 1-F(x,s_c)\le50e(x)
 \quad\hbox{for }x\hbox{ in class }c.
 \tag{9}
\]

This minimum selection is what preserves the pointwise, nonuniform defect interface.

## 4. A forbidden decision-risk interval and deletion induction

For any finite choice of `m` distinct class representatives, let `R_m` be the optimal worst-case classification risk among allowed `m`-outcome measurements. Compactness gives a minimizer. Apply that classifier independently to the three ideal copies and take the majority label; if there is no majority, output arbitrarily. Under a true label, ideal failure requires at least two erroneous copies, so its probability is at most `3R_m^2`. Pulling this procedure back through `C` and using (5) gives

\[
 R_m\le f(R_m),\qquad
 f(z)=(\sqrt3\,z+1/\sqrt{350})^2.
 \tag{10}
\]

Let `a=1/200` and `b0=13/50`. The convex quadratic `f(z)-z` is strictly negative on `[a,b0]`. For explicit endpoint checks, `sqrt3<7/4` and `1/sqrt350<3/56` yield

\[
 f(a)<(349/5600)^2<1/256<a,\qquad
 f(b0)<(89/175)^2<b0.
\]

Thus any classifier bringing `R_m` below `b0` forces the optimal risk below `a`.

For each distinct pair, (7) and the left inequality in (4) give an allowed binary test with sum of conditional errors less than `1/16`. Each individual error is therefore less than `1/16`. Three representatives can be classified on three ideal copies by assigning one copy to each pair test; output the label winning its two relevant tests, when it exists. Under a true label, the union bound gives error less than `1/8`. After the two cloner calls,

\[
 R_3<(\sqrt{1/8}+1/\sqrt{350})^2<(3/7)^2<b0.
\]

The cases `m=1,2` are easier and also enter the interval. This establishes `R_m<a` for `m<=3`, without a quantum pretty-good measurement or any positivity-of-matrices representation of the state cone.

For `m>=4`, choose a label uniformly to omit and classify the remaining `m-1` by induction, always counting the omitted true label as an error. Every true label then has risk at most

\[
 {1\over m}+\left(1-{1\over m}\right)a
 \le{203\over800}<b0.
\]

Equation (10) forces `R_m<a`. Induction proves this for every finite `m`.

### The exact dimension bound

For a measurement classifying all representatives with error less than `a`, the response matrix

\[
 A_{ic}=M_i(s_c)
\]

is column stochastic and has `A_cc>1-a>1/2`. It is strictly column diagonally dominant, hence invertible. Yet it factors through `span{s_c}`, so

\[
 \boxed{k=\operatorname{rank}A\le\dim\operatorname{span}\{s_c\}
 \le\dim\operatorname{span}S.}
 \tag{11}
\]

In fact, the representatives are linearly independent and hence affine independent. This avoids any trace bound, packing constant, or special cone geometry.

## 5. One constrained compact set yields pointwise proper learning

Transfer the representative classifier to every good state using (4),(9). Its wrong-class probability is bounded by

\[
 a+d(x,s_{c(x)})\le a+\sqrt{50\tau}=a+1/\sqrt{56}<b0.
\]

Minimize worst-case risk over all of `G`. The same majority inequality (10) applies to this minimizer, since every defect is at most `tau`. The risk gap therefore lowers the globally optimal worst risk below `a`.

Let `K` be the nonempty compact convex set of allowed class measurements having wrong-class risk at most `a` at **every** state in `G`. Fix any finite prior on good states, and minimize its average risk `R` over this same set `K`. Write `E` for the prior-average defect. For individual risks `r_i<=a`, the majority pullback obeys

\[
 r'_i\le(\sqrt3\,r_i+\sqrt{8e_i})^2.
\]

It belongs to `K`, since `f(a)<a`. Optimality, `sum p_i r_i^2<=aR`, and Cauchy--Schwarz imply

\[
 R\le\left(\sqrt{3aR}+\sqrt{8E}\right)^2,
\]

so

\[
 R\le C_*E,\qquad C_*={8\over(1-\sqrt{3/200})^2}<11.
 \tag{12}
\]

Finite minimax on any finite subset of `G` yields an element of `K` whose risk at each listed state is at most `C_* e_i`. Each pointwise constraint is a closed subset of the **same** compact `K`. The finite-intersection property therefore supplies one measurement satisfying

\[
 r(x)\le C_*e(x)\quad(x\in G)
 \tag{13}
\]

simultaneously on the whole good model. This step does not exchange the representative dictionary or the globally constrained feasible set when the prior changes.

Prepare `s_c` on outcome `c`. Correct-class loss is at most `50e(x)` by (9); every wrong-class loss is at most one. Therefore

\[
 \sum_cM_c(x)[1-F(x,s_c)]\le(C_*+50)e(x)<61e(x)
 \quad(x\in G).
 \tag{14}
\]

For `x outside G`, loss is at most one, which is less than `2800e(x)`. Combining this with (14) proves (2), including the zero-defect case.

## 6. Reverse transfer and arbitrary copy numbers

Squared operational fidelity is separately concave. To see this directly, for fixed `q`, classical squared affinity is

\[
 F_{\rm cl}(p,q)=\sum_ip_iq_i+
 2\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{p_ip_jq_iq_j},
\]

a concave function of `p`. Its pullback through each measurement is concave, and the infimum over measurements remains concave. The same holds in the other argument. No joint concavity of squared fidelity is asserted.

Given any proper estimator, prepare two copies of its selected representative. The hybrid through `x tensor s` gives `A(x^2,s^2)<=2A(x,s)` and consequently

\[
 1-F(x^2,s^2)\le4[1-F(x,s)].
\]

For angles at most `pi/4`, use `sin(2t)<=2sin t`; for larger angles the right side already exceeds one. Separate concavity averages this inequality over the estimator outcome. Thus the minimax risks obey

\[
 R_{\rm proper}\le2800e_{\rm replicate},\qquad
 e_{\rm replicate}\le4R_{\rm proper}.
 \tag{15}
\]

With exact tensor fidelity multiplication, the second constant improves to `2`. That property is not automatic in GPTs.

More generally, `A(x^m,s^m)<=m A(x,s)`, and

\[
 1-F(x^m,s^m)\le\min\{1,m^2[1-F(x,s)]\}.
 \tag{16}
\]

If `m A<=pi/2`, use `sin(mt)<=m sin t`; otherwise `m sin t>=m sin(pi/(2m))>=1`, so the bound is trivial. The same measurement and dictionary as above therefore compile `B` into all finite replica numbers:

\[
 C_m(x)=\sum_cM_c(x)s_c^{\otimes m},\qquad
 1-F(C_mx,x^m)\le\max\{2800,C_*+50m^2\}e(x).
 \tag{17}
\]

In classical and quantum composites, exact product fidelity changes `50m^2` to `50m`. The companion `DISK_QUADRATIC_BOUNDARY.md` proves that the quadratic GPT order is unavoidable under the present axioms, even for a compact binary model and an optimal supplied two-output cloner.

## 7. Fractional sample amplification without extra unknown copies

Suppose a supplied amplifier `A` takes `n` unknown independent copies to `n+k`, with pointwise defect `epsilon_x` against the joint `(n+k)`-copy product target. Apply it repeatedly to the first `n` outputs while retaining the surplus, for exactly `q=ceil(n/k)` calls. Compare each stage to the independent ideal product. Contractivity and fixed-spectator equality give the exact angle bound

\[
 A(\text{actual output},\text{ideal output})
 \le\min\{q\arcsin\sqrt{\epsilon_x},\pi/2\}.
\]

Discard excess outputs. The resulting doubling channel has pointwise defect at most

\[
 \boxed{\sin^2\left(\min\{q\arcsin\sqrt{\epsilon_x},\pi/2\}\right)
 \le\min\{1,q^2\epsilon_x\}.}
 \tag{18}
\]

There is no factor two from converting via squared Bures distance. Apply the theorem to the compact promised model `S_n={x^n:x in S}`. Its proper-estimation loss is the **n-copy** loss `1-F(x^n,hat{x}^n)`, not single-copy parameter loss, and its minimax value is bounded by `2800q^2 sup_x epsilon_x`. Only the original `n` unknown inputs are consumed. Calls, fixed-state preparation and optimization/acquisition costs remain separate resources.

## 8. Primary comparison and proof boundaries

The operational root fidelity and exact generic no-cloning are established notions:

- [Barnum, Barrett, Leifer, Wilce, *Cloning and Broadcasting in Generic Probabilistic Theories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611295), Theorem 1: exact finite-family cloning and joint discrimination are equivalent. Their proof iterates exact cloning and reads empirical frequencies.
- [Kimura, Nuida, Imai, *Distinguishability Measures and Entropies for General Probabilistic Theories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0994), Propositions 4--7: operational root fidelity, monotonicity, tensor submultiplicativity, fixed-ancilla equality and the generalized fidelity/TV inequalities. Their cloning applications are exact.
- [Girling, Cîrstoiu, Jennings, *Simple formulation of no-cloning and no-hiding that admits efficient and robust verification*](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.023090): robust verification via channel unitarities and compatible-unitarity sets; this is a different score and interface from the joint product-target/proper-estimation theorem here.

A targeted primary-source search found no theorem identical to the quantitative pointwise proper-estimation statement (2), but this is not a complete priority search. The independent substantive bridges verified here are the quantum-free three-label base, dimension-exact response-matrix argument, global constrained minimax, and the correct tensor-fidelity direction. The supplied channel, measurement optimization and representative preparation are not automatically acquired computational resources.
