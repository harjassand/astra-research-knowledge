# Quadratic-score interpolation without an upper curvature bound

Status: the main score-only sharp one-third theorem was internally reconstructed by both root and `cross_corpus`, as directly confirmed by root on 11 October 2026. The independent score proof is in `work/cross_corpus/SCORE_ONLY_TRANSPORT.md`, including the corrected common bad-set coefficient `20d`. Root also directly confirmed the centered-potential chain, full-source finite-potential limit, one-sided clipped moment transfer, and Gaussian lower construction. This is written-proof reconstruction, not external validation or a priority claim. This note removes the upper Hessian assumption of Section 5 in `gaussian_sharp_moments.md`. The proper-support Sobolev variant in Section 6 was subsequently derived here and is frozen pending independent reconstruction.

## 1. Exact theorem

Let `d>=2`, `kappa>0`, and let

\[
\rho(x)=e^{-V(x)},\qquad V\in C^1(\mathbb R^d),\qquad
V(x)-\kappa|x|^2/2\text{ convex},\qquad\int\rho=1.
\]

Thus the source has full support, is strongly log-concave, and has Gaussian upper tails. Fix `p>2`, put `q=p/(p-2)`, and assume the explicit source score moment

\[
S_q:=\sum_{j=1}^d\|\partial_jV\|_{L^q(\rho)}<\infty.
\tag{score-assumption}
\]

Let one target satisfy `int |y|^p dmu<=M` and let the other target be any `nu in P_2`. Set `w=W_2(mu,nu)` and `D=||T_mu-T_nu||_{L^2(rho)}`. Then

\[
\boxed{D\le w+Cw^{1/3},}
\tag{score-map}
\]

where one admissible constant is

\[
C=\max\left\{2M^{1/p},\left[
24d/\kappa+10S_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}
+20d(2^{p-1}M+4)\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\]

The exponent is sharp over the source class because it contains the standard Gaussian and the matching compact three-atom target family in `gaussian_sharp_moments.md` Section 3. No claim of a matching lower exponent for each fixed member of the source class is needed or asserted.

The lower curvature enters only through the radius-independent finite-potential estimate

\[
F:=\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}\,w
\]

for source-mean-zero potentials, proved in `strong_logconcave_extension.md` Sections 1–4 and 6a. The interpolation argument below requires only unimodal absolutely continuous fibers, zero density at their ends, and finite score moments. It does not require an upper Hessian bound or controlled density distortion for shifts across a mode.

## 2. A mode-strip domination lemma

Let `r` be a nonnegative absolutely continuous density on the real line, nondecreasing up to a mode `m` and nonincreasing after it, with `r(-infinity)=r(+infinity)=0`. It need not be normalized. Let `g` be bounded and monotone nondecreasing. Define

\[
J(g,r)=\int_{\mathbb R}|r'(s)|g(s)^2ds.
\]

At every point where the functions are represented by their monotone/continuous values,

\[
r(t)g(t)^2\le J(g,r).
\tag{point-strip}
\]

If `g(t)>=0`, take `s_0=max(t,m)`. For `s>=s_0`, `g(s)^2>=g(t)^2`, the density is nonincreasing, and `r(s_0)>=r(t)`. Hence

\[
J(g,r)\ge\int_{s_0}^{\infty}(-r'(s))g(s)^2ds
\ge g(t)^2r(s_0)\ge r(t)g(t)^2.
\]

If `g(t)<0`, take `s_0=min(t,m)` and integrate over `(-infinity,s_0]`. There `|g(s)|>=|g(t)|`, `r'>=0` almost everywhere, and `r(s_0)>=r(t)`. This gives the same conclusion. In particular, for any `h>0`,

\[
\int_{|t-m|<h}r(t)g(t)^2dt\le2hJ(g,r).
\tag{strip-energy}
\]

This is the step that replaces the old `L^2 h sup r` strip bound. It uses vanishing density at the fiber endpoints. A uniform source or a Gaussian abruptly restricted to a slab has nonzero boundary trace, so this argument does not apply to those counterexamples.

## 3. Noncrossing quadratic-score interpolation

Work first with finite affine-max convex potentials `u,v`. For a cutoff `L>0`, put

\[
\tau(L)=\max_{z\in\{u,v\}}\int_{|\nabla z|>L}|\nabla z|^2d\rho,
\qquad g_{z,j}=\operatorname{clip}(\partial_jz,[-L,L]).
\]

Fix a coordinate fiber and let `m` be its mode. Use a forward difference on `t<=m-h` and a backward difference on `t>=m+h`. The endpoints stay on their own monotone-density side. Density does not decrease under either shift, and the two shifted images have disjoint interiors. Therefore

\[
\int_{allowed}|D_h(u-v)|^2d\rho\le4F^2/h^2.
\tag{difference}
\]

Call an allowed point bad if either coordinate derivative of either potential, at the point or its shifted endpoint, exceeds `L` in magnitude. In the excluded strip call it bad only if a current derivative exceeds `L`. The four current/endpoint events have total mass at most `4tau(L)/L^2`, using density monotonicity and the disjoint endpoint images. Each map's current coordinate energy on the full bad set is at most `L^2 mass(bad)+tau(L)<=5tau(L)`. Consequently

\[
\int_{bad}|\partial_j u-\partial_jv|^2d\rho\le20\tau(L).
\tag{bad-energy}
\]

On the good allowed set, convexity and clipping give

\[
\delta_z:=|D_hz-\partial_jz|
\le|g_{z,j}(t\mathbin{\pm}h)-g_{z,j}(t)|.
\]

For real numbers `a<=b`, `(b-a)^2<=2(b|b|-a|a|)`. Thus, writing `H=g|g|`,

\[
\int_{good\ allowed}\delta_z^2d\rho
\le2h\int r(s)dH(s)d z_\perp
\le2h\,J_{z,j},
\tag{allowed-error}
\]

where

\[
J_{z,j}:=\int|\partial_j\rho(x)|\,g_{z,j}(x)^2dx.
\]

For the first inequality, Tonelli on the Stieltjes derivative of `H` gives `h int r dH` for the union of the two halves: each intermediate point stays on its own side of the mode, where `r(t)<=r(s)`; the two halves use disjoint parts of the variation measure. For the second, integration by parts has zero boundary term since `H` is bounded and the density vanishes at both fiber ends:

\[
\int r\,dH=-\int r'H\le\int|r'|g^2.
\]

On the good excluded strip, the derivatives equal their clips. By (strip-energy),

\[
\int_{good\ strip}|\partial_ju-\partial_jv|^2d\rho
\le4h(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j}).
\tag{strip-difference}
\]

On the good allowed set, apply `(a+b+c)^2<=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)` to the difference quotient and its two errors. Equations (difference), (allowed-error), (strip-difference), and (bad-energy) prove

\[
\int|\partial_ju-\partial_jv|^2d\rho
\le12F^2/h^2+10h(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})+20\tau(L).
\]

Summing coordinates yields the interpolation primitive

\[
\boxed{D^2\le12dF^2/h^2+10h\sum_j(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})+20d\tau(L).}
\tag{score-WI}
\]

For the stated source, `|partial_j rho|=|partial_jV|rho`. Hölder gives the admitted resource interface

\[
J_{z,j}\le\|\partial_jV\|_{L^q(\rho)}\|g_{z,j}\|_{L^p(\rho)}^2,
\qquad 1/q+2/p=1.
\tag{score-pairing}
\]

Thus source score moments, rather than an upper source curvature, control the interpolation. Finite score moments are an explicit additional hypothesis and are not inferred from lower curvature alone.

## 4. Insert the one-sided target bounds

Let `(Y,Z)` be an optimal coupling of the targets, with `Y~mu`, `Z~nu`, and `E|Y-Z|^2=w^2`. As proved in the Gaussian note,

\[
E\min(|Z|,L)^p\le2^pM+4L^{p-2}w^2,
\]

\[
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2.
\]

For `0<w<=1`, choose `L=w^(-2/(p-2))`. Then every coordinate clip has bounds

\[
\|g_{u,j}\|_p^p\le M,\qquad
\|g_{v,j}\|_p^p\le2^pM+4,
\qquad\tau(L)\le(2^{p-1}M+4)w^2.
\]

Use (score-pairing), `F<=sqrt(2/kappa)w`, and then `h=w^(2/3)` in (score-WI). This proves

\[
D^2\le\left[24d/\kappa+
10S_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}
+20d(2^{p-1}M+4)\right]w^{2/3}.
\]

For `w>1`, use `D<=w+2M^(1/p)`. Finite norm-decreasing target quantizers preserve the constrained moment budget; arbitrary finite `W_2` quantizers handle the other target. Common-source graph-plan uniqueness and uniform integrability of squared target norms pass the map inequality to the limits. No limiting-potential assertion is needed at this final step.

## 5. Scope and imported premises

The statement uses a full-support `C^1` strongly log-concave source and a finite `L^{p/(p-2)}` score, a moment on exactly one target, and no map regularity of either target. A theorem stated under a bounded upper source Hessian is a corollary: it ensures all finite score moments by linear score growth and Gaussian tails. For `p=4`, finite source Fisher information suffices, since `q=2` and `sum_j||partial_jV||_2` is bounded by `sqrt(d int|gradient V|^2rho)`.

The mode-strip argument more generally works for absolutely continuous unimodal fibers with zero endpoint traces, and the score pairing can be stated using dual Orlicz norms. Such versions require their exact integrability and approximation interfaces to be written before they are claimed. They are not needed for the present theorem.

Imported: the centered strongly log-concave potential estimate, classical Brenier uniqueness/stability, Gaussian lower-source example, basic Stieltjes/Fubini calculus, and Hölder. Newly derived here: pointwise mode-strip domination, its combination with quadratic monotone variation, and the one-sided sharp map theorem under score moments. Exact primary comparison remains the scoped one in `gaussian_sharp_moments.md` Section 4; no additional originality claim is made.

## 6. Proper supports with zero Sobolev boundary trace

The full-support `C^1` assumption can be replaced by an explicit condition that also admits proper convex supports. Let `rho` be a full-dimensional `kappa`-strongly log-concave probability density on `R^d`, allow its support to be proper, and assume

\[
\rho\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb R^d),\qquad
S_q=\sum_j\left(\int_{\{\rho>0\}}
\left|\frac{\partial_j\rho}{\rho}\right|^q\rho\right)^{1/q}<\infty,
\qquad q=p/(p-2).
\tag{Sobolev-score}
\]

The derivatives here are weak derivatives of the globally defined zero-extended density. The ratio is defined as zero on the zero-density set. Then the theorem and its exact constant remain valid with this `S_q`.

To justify the extension, `W^{1,1}` gives an absolutely continuous representative on almost every coordinate fiber, with integrable derivative and zero trace at both infinite endpoints. At a finite support endpoint its value is also zero because the representative is globally absolutely continuous and vanishes beyond that endpoint. Log-concavity makes each nontrivial fiber unimodal. Thus the mode-strip lemma, the noncrossing translations, and Stieltjes integration by parts all apply to these representatives, with no boundary measure. Their derivative energy integrates by Fubini to `J_{z,j}=int|partial_jrho|g_{z,j}^2`, and Holder gives exactly (score-pairing). The centered-potential estimate for strongly log-concave sources on proper supports was proved in `strong_logconcave_extension.md`, including conditioning, smoothing, and the finite-potential limit. The target approximation passage is unchanged.

For an explicit family, take

\[
\rho(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-\kappa|x|^2/2}
\prod_{j=1}^m(1-x_j^2)_+^{a_j},\qquad1\le m\le d,
\qquad a_j>q-1.
\]

This density is `kappa`-strongly log-concave. It belongs to global `W^{1,1}` and its scores on the support are `kappa x_j+2a_jx_j/(1-x_j^2)` for `j<=m`, and `kappa x_j` otherwise. Near a finite boundary the score integrand has order `distance^(a_j-q)`, which is integrable exactly when `a_j>q-1`. Therefore this fixed source admits the `1/3` one-sided `p`-moment bound. It is compactly supported when `m=d` and unbounded on a proper slab when `m<d`. The abrupt slab source with positive boundary trace fails global `W^{1,1}`; its jump measure is precisely the term excluded by (Sobolev-score).

This variant is a written consequence of the same primitive, not a new independent sharpness claim for each individual source. Its added hypotheses and boundary distinction are explicit; they must not be omitted when comparing it with compact convex sources having nonzero density at the boundary.
