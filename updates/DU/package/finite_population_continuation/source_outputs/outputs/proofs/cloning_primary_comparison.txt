# Compact primary-source comparison for the linear average theorem

11 October 2026. This is a targeted overlap check, not an exhaustive novelty search. No originality conclusion follows from absence of an exact match in these papers.

- [Fuchs–Sasaki, *Squeezing Quantum Information through a Classical Channel*, quant-ph/0302092v3](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302092), Sections 2–4 and 5: defines precisely the accessible single-copy fidelity optimized over measure-and-prepare channels, gives its binary formula, and discusses global binary cloning fidelity in the introduction. The displayed binary formulas supply the asymptotic lower constant `3+2sqrt(2)`. The paper's general bounds concern accessible fidelity itself; I found no universal comparison of arbitrary finite-ensemble accessible infidelity to two-copy global cloning infidelity. Relevant locations: HTML lines 69–95, 98–170, 233–392, 657–711.
- [Zhang–Li–Guo, *Quantum Clone and States Estimation for n-state System*, quant-ph/9908003v4](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9908003), equations (6)–(12): gives a Procrustes/trace-square-root **lower bound on achievable cloning fidelity** for a finite pure-state list, then an asymptotic identification bound as the number of outputs tends to infinity. This does not compare the two-copy cloning error to accessible reconstruction error from above. Relevant locations: HTML lines 48–87. Some claims about optimal economical outputs in the source are stated without proof; none is needed for the new linear theorem.
- [Bae–Acín, *Asymptotic quantum cloning is state estimation*, quant-ph/0603078v1](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0603078), equation (2) and its theorem: proves the limit of optimal **single-clone marginal** fidelity as the number of outputs tends to infinity equals the estimation fidelity. Its proof uses shareability and EB Choi states; it does not furnish a dimension-independent relative-error bound from a fixed two-output global score. Relevant locations: HTML lines 33–54 and 67–103.
- [Chiribella–D’Ariano, *Quantum information becomes classical when distributed to many users*, quant-ph/0608007v3](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0608007), Theorems 1–2 and equation (16): constructs measure-and-prepare approximations to symmetric distribution channels. The finite bound is additive and depends on output dimension, with single-user trace error of order `d/M` for symmetric-subspace output and `d^2/M` in general. This is a many-output result, rather than a relative-infidelity comparison at two outputs. Relevant locations: HTML lines 90–115 and 117–147.

The new internal proof uses only two calls to the supplied two-output cloner. Its class-measurement bootstrap avoids the dimension factor incurred by generic many-copy/de Finetti approximation. This is a mathematical distinction between the displayed interfaces, not a priority assertion. The universal sharp constant remains unresolved.

## Compact mixed-state follow-up after the analytic extension

This is targeted primary comparison, not an exhaustive priority audit.

- [Lemm–Wilde, arXiv:1608.07569](https://arxiv.org/html/1608.07569), Definitions 1–2 and Theorem 4: pairwise information-theoretic restrictions on approximate cloning/broadcasting. Their approximate-clone outputs are themselves exact tensor powers of modified states; this differs from the fidelity-to-product premise of the new finite arbitrary-ensemble comparison. The inspected theorem does not supply a global class POVM or EB relative-error surrogate.
- [Piani, arXiv:1501.06855](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.06855), Section III: hierarchy for EB measurement recoverability and local broadcasting. Its Bose-symmetric extension requirement is stronger than exchange symmetry; a mixed product `rho tensor rho` generally has antisymmetric support. Its many-output convergence does not provide the present two-output product-target relative-fidelity bound.
- [Li–Theil–Harrow–Chuang, arXiv:2605.21457](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21457), Sections S2–S3, especially Eqs. S36–S41: the cloning separation uses fixed added output count and one-site marginal loss, whereas the all-site guarantee is a different symmetric-dimension ratio. Its EB limit uses many outputs and dimension-dependent de Finetti estimates. These inspected claims neither prove nor contradict the finite two-output joint-product theorem. The marginal counterexample developed here likewise concerns mixed-state marginals, not the product premise.
- [Fanizza–Grinko–Scharnhorst–Spilecki, arXiv:2608.27298](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27298), Section 1.2: cloning-vs-learning sample complexity is a separate issue. Classical sample amplification can outperform learning, while EB dephasing exactly fixes every state in a commuting family. Its tensor-power random-purification construction does not turn an arbitrary given mixed cloner into a coherent purification cloner.
- [Fei–Wang, arXiv:2609.35986v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35986v1), Sections 1.2 and 4: fixed-gain, many-input mixed-state cloning and projector-state converses. The inspected results do not state a finite arbitrary-ensemble EB relative-error comparison. Our one-input projector support calculation was reconstructed directly as a negative counterexample control.

No primary source inspected here was found to contain the forbidden-risk omission induction or the stated mixed all-input EB surrogate. This is limited search evidence only. The copy-count reservoir marginal separation also requires its own priority review; no originality claim is made from search silence.
