## 1. Exact one-sided theorem and full-class modulus

Fix the standard Gaussian source `gamma_d`, `d>=2`, a finite `r>0`, and `M>0`. Write

\[
\Phi_r(t)=t^2\log(e+t)^r,\qquad
\beta_r=\min\{1/3,r/(r+1)\}.
\]

For any targets satisfying exactly

\[
\int\Phi_r(|y|)d\mu(y)\le M,\qquad \nu\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d),
\]

set `w=W_2(mu,nu)` and `D=||T_mu-T_nu||_{L^2(gamma_d)}`. Then

\[
\boxed{D\le w+C_{d,r,M}w^{\beta_r}.}
\tag{upper}
\]

No logarithmic moment is assumed for `nu`. Define the actual class modulus by

\[
\Omega_{r,M}(\varepsilon)=\sup\{D:\mu\text{ satisfies the above budget},
\ \nu\in\mathcal P_2,\ W_2(\mu,\nu)\le\varepsilon\}.
\]

There exist positive constants `c,C,epsilon_0`, depending only on `d,r,M`, such that

\[
\boxed{c\varepsilon^{\beta_r}\le\Omega_{r,M}(\varepsilon)
\le C\varepsilon^{\beta_r}\qquad(0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0).}
\tag{sharp}
\]

The lower bounds remain true when both targets must satisfy the same budget. At `r=1/2` the exact exponent is `1/3`, without an extra logarithmic factor. At `r=0`, a second-moment budget alone admits no vanishing class modulus, even with both targets constrained and this one fixed Gaussian source.

## 2. The Gaussian pairing, with dependence allowed

Let `A=|G|` be one Gaussian source coordinate and let `Z` be ANY nonnegative function of all source coordinates with `Z<=L^2`. Put

\[
B_L=\log(e+L),\qquad s=(1/2-r)_+.
\]

Split according to `A<=3 sqrt(log(e+sqrt Z))`. On this event,

\[
AZ\le3Z\log(e+\sqrt Z)^{1/2}
\le3B_L^sZ\log(e+\sqrt Z)^r.
\]

The last step uses `log(e+sqrt Z)>=1`: for `r>=1/2` the extra power is unnecessary, and otherwise the missing power is at most `B_L^s`. On the complementary event, `sqrt Z<exp(A^2/9)`, and therefore

\[
AZ\le A\exp(2A^2/9),\qquad
E[A\exp(2A^2/9)]=\frac{18}{5\sqrt{2\pi}}<2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{E[AZ]\le5B_L^s\{1+E[Z\log(e+\sqrt Z)^r]\}.}
\tag{pairing}
\]

There is no independence of `A,Z` in this argument. The event split is pointwise, and the second term uses only the Gaussian marginal of `A`.

## 3. One-sided clipped budgets and tails

For an optimal target coupling `(Y,Z)` with `E|Y-Z|^2=w^2`, let

\[
a_r=4(1+\log2)^r.
\]

The following pointwise inequality holds:

\[
\Phi_r(\min\{|Z|,L\})
\le a_r\Phi_r(|Y|)+4B_L^r|Y-Z|^2.
\tag{clip}
\]

If `|Z|<=2|Y|`, use monotonicity of `Phi_r` and
`log(e+2t)<=log(e+t)+log2<=(1+log2)log(e+t)`.
Otherwise `|Y-Z|>|Z|/2`, and `min(|Z|,L)^2<=4|Y-Z|^2`, while the clipped logarithm is at most `B_L`.

Thus the constrained target's clipped budget is at most `M`, and the arbitrary target's clipped budget is at most `a_rM+4B_L^r w^2`. This is a clipped budget only; it does not infer a finite logarithmic moment for `nu`.

Write

\[
\tau_\eta(L)=\int_{|y|>L}|y|^2d\eta(y).
\]

The budget gives `tau_mu(L)<=M B_L^{-r}`, and the same optimal coupling gives

\[
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2.
\]

Choose `B>=1`, `L=exp(B)`. Then

\[
B\le B_L\le2B,
\qquad
\log(e+L/2)\ge B-\log2\ge(1-\log2)B.
\]

With `b_r=2(1-log2)^{-r}` this proves

\[
\tau(L):=\max(\tau_\mu(L),\tau_\nu(L))
\le b_rMB^{-r}+4w^2.
\tag{tails}
\]

## 4. Interpolation and an alternative view of the phase transition

For finite targets choose source-mean-zero convex potentials `u,v`. The previously reconstructed centered-potential bound gives `F=||u-v||_2<=sqrt2 w`. Clip their coordinate derivatives at `L`, writing `g_{z,j}`. The exact interpolation is

\[
D^2\le12dF^2/h^2+10h\sum_j(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})+20d\tau(L),
\qquad J_{z,j}=E|X_j|g_{z,j}^2.
\]

Every coordinate clip is dominated by the radial clip of the corresponding target. Applying (pairing), (clip), and (tails) gives, for all `h>0,B>=1`,

\[
\begin{split}
D^2\le{}&24d\,w^2/h^2\\
&+50d\,2^s hB^s\{2+(1+a_r)M+4\cdot2^rB^rw^2\}\\
&+20db_rMB^{-r}+80dw^2.
\end{split}
\tag{interpolation}
\]

When `B^r w^2<=1`, optimizing just the first two terms in `h` shows the essential scalar profile

\[
D^2\ \lesssim_{d,r,M}\
w^{2/3}B^{(1-2r)_+/3}+B^{-r}+w^2.
\tag{profile}
\]

For `0<r<1/2`, balancing its first two terms requires
`B=w^{-2/(r+1)}` and gives `D^2=O(w^{2r/(r+1)})`.
For `r>=1/2`, the score term no longer grows with `B`; taking
`B=w^{-2/(3r)}` makes the tail of order `w^{2/3}` as well. This explains the phase transition directly.

For the exact constants, when `0<w<=1` take

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&B&h\\\hline
0<r<1/2&w^{-2/(r+1)}&w^{1/(r+1)}\\
r\ge1/2&w^{-2/(3r)}&w^{2/3}
\end{array}
\]

In the first case all three principal terms equal a constant times `w^{2r/(r+1)}`, and `B^r w^2=w^{2/(r+1)}<=1`. In the second case all three have order `w^{2/3}`, and `B^r w^2=w^{4/3}<=1`. The two choices coincide at `r=1/2`.

One admissible all-distance constant is

\[
C_{d,r,M}=\max\left\{2\sqrt M,
\left[104d+50d\,2^s\{2+(1+a_r)M+4\cdot2^r\}
+20db_rM\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\]

For `w>1`, `D<=w+2sqrt M`, since `Phi_r(t)>=t^2`. At zero distance the maps coincide. Finite norm-decreasing quantization preserves the constrained budget; arbitrary finite `W_2` approximation handles `nu`. Common-source graph-plan uniqueness and uniformly integrable squared target norms pass the inequality to the original targets. This proves (upper) with exactly its one-sided interface.

The cutoff `L=exp(B)` is an analytic proof parameter. This argument asserts no bounded-cost acquisition, representation, or computational use of that cutoff.

## 5. Rare caps: a complete lower reconstruction and explicit constant

Let `X,Y` be independent standard Gaussians, `a_t=Q(t)=P(X>t)`, `H_t=log(1/a_t)`, and choose a fixed `c>0`. For large real `t`, set

\[
\theta_t=1/t,\qquad
L_t=c\,a_t^{-1/2}H_t^{-r/2},
\]

\[
q_t=L_te_1,\qquad
v_t=L_t(\cos\theta_t e_1+\sin\theta_t e_2).
\]

The halfspaces

\[
A_t=\{X>t\},\qquad
B_t=\{X\cos(1/t)+Y\sin(1/t)>t\}
\]

both have probability exactly `a_t`. The convex hinge potentials with these gradients transport the Gaussian optimally to

\[
\mu_t=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{q_t},\qquad
\nu_t=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{v_t}.
\]

Their common moment is

\[
a_tL_t^2\log(e+L_t)^r
=c^2H_t^{-r}\log(e+c e^{H_t/2}H_t^{-r/2})^r
\longrightarrow c^22^{-r}.
\]

Choosing `c` with `c^22^{-r}<M` respects both budgets for all sufficiently large `t`.

Conditioning on `Y`,

\[
\frac{P(A_t\cap B_t)}{a_t}
=E\frac{Q(t+(s_t)_+/t)}{Q(t)},
\quad
s_t=t^2(\sec(1/t)-1)-tY\tan(1/t)
\longrightarrow\tfrac12-Y.
\]

For `s>=0`, the exact Gaussian change of variables gives

\[
Q(t+s/t)=\frac{\varphi(t)}t
\int_s^\infty e^{-z-z^2/(2t^2)}dz.
\]

For every fixed `Y`, the tail ratio therefore tends to `exp(-(1/2-Y)_+)`. It lies between zero and one, so dominated convergence applies over the ENTIRE transverse Gaussian, with no rare-slice exclusion. Moreover the limiting expectation can be evaluated exactly:

\[
\begin{split}
E e^{-(1/2-Y)_+}
&=P(Y\ge1/2)+e^{-1/2}E[e^Y1_{Y<1/2}]\\
&=Q(1/2)+\Phi(-1/2)=2Q(1/2).
\end{split}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\frac{P(A_t\triangle B_t)}{a_t}
\longrightarrow c_*:=2-4Q(1/2)>0.}
\tag{switch}
\]

For the two-atom targets, direct atom matching is optimal: replacing a matched outlier pair by two crossed pairs increases cost by `2 q_t dot v_t>0`. Hence

\[
w_t^2=a_t|q_t-v_t|^2
=4c^2H_t^{-r}\sin^2(1/(2t)).
\]

The exact common-source map distance is

\[
D_t^2=L_t^2P(A_t\triangle B_t)
+|q_t-v_t|^2P(A_t\cap B_t).
\]

The second term is negligible compared with `a_tL_t^2`. The same Gaussian tail formula gives `H_t~t^2/2`, so the asymptotics are explicitly

\[
\boxed{w_t\sim c\,2^{r/2}t^{-(r+1)},\qquad
D_t\sim c\,2^{r/2}\sqrt{c_*}\,t^{-r}.}
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{D_t}{w_t^{r/(r+1)}}\longrightarrow
\sqrt{c_*}(c2^{r/2})^{1/(r+1)}>0.
\]

This proves the lower exponent for `0<r<=1/2`. It proves a FULL small-distance class lower bound: `w_t=2cH_t^{-r/2}sin(1/(2t))` is positive, continuous, strictly decreasing for all sufficiently large `t`, and tends to zero. Both positive factors decrease, and the sine factor decreases strictly. Thus every sufficiently small prescribed distance is realized, rather than only a sequence of distances.

## 6. The compact-target branch and endpoints

For `r>=1/2`, use the Gaussian three-atom thin-strip family from `gaussian_sharp_moments.md`. In brief, with `m_a=P(|X|<a)`, select the unique `C_a` making

\[
E\,P(|X|<a(C_a+Y/2)_+\mid Y)=m_a.
\]

The conditional notation simply evaluates the Gaussian interval probability. The scaled convex maxima

\[
u_a=\eta\max\{-x_1,x_1,a\},\qquad
v_a=\eta\max\{-x_1,x_1,aC_a+(a/2)x_2\}
\]

have exactly matched outer masses and exactly matched central mass `m_a`; only the central target atom moves from zero to `eta(a/2)e_2`. The Gaussian exact-mass argument gives bounded `C_a` and a positive limiting symmetric-difference fraction of the thin central strips. Therefore

\[
w_a^2=\eta^2m_aa^2/4\asymp a^3,\qquad D_a^2\asymp a.
\]

Take fixed `eta>0` small enough that `Phi_r(eta)<=M`; then both targets remain in the ball of radius `eta` for small `a`, so both obey the requested budget. The exact function `w_a=(eta a/2)sqrt(m_a)` is continuous and strictly increasing, proving the full lower modulus with exponent `1/3`.

At `r=1/2`, both the rare-cap and compact-target families match the same exponent `1/3`; the upper choices and constants introduce no logarithmic loss.

At `r=0`, choose `L_t=c a_t^{-1/2}` with `c^2<=M`. Both second moments are exactly `c^2`, while

\[
w_t\sim c/t\longrightarrow0,\qquad
D_t\longrightarrow c\sqrt{c_*}>0.
\]

The same continuous strictly decreasing distance parameter realizes every sufficiently small distance. A vanishing modulus on the uniformly second-moment-bounded class is therefore impossible. For `M=0`, which is excluded from the nontrivial phase statement, the constrained target is `delta_0` and `D=w` exactly; no positive lower obstruction is claimed there.
