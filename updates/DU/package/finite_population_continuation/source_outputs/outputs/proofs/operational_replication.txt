QUANTITATIVE REPLICATION RIGIDITY IN FINITE-DIMENSIONAL OPERATIONAL THEORIES
Root derivation, 11 October 2026. Current status: complete written argument;
independent cross-corpus reconstruction completed for the finite-dimensional core. Historical originality and
external correctness certification remain UNKNOWN. This is a theoretical
existence theorem, not an acquired-input compiler or a computational speedup.

1. Exact operational assumptions

A system A has a compact normalized state space Omega_A spanning a finite-
dimensional ordered real vector space V_A. Put D=dim V_A. Effects are allowed
affine probability functionals that separate states. For every fixed finite
outcome number, the set of allowed measurements is compact and convex.
Measurements are closed under classical randomization and outcome processing.
Systems admit associative composites with continuous independent products,
discarding, preparation of any fixed state, product measurements, and application
of a physical transformation locally with a spectator. Pullbacks of allowed
measurements by physical transformations are allowed. Classical measurement
outcomes can control state preparation. These closure assumptions are explicit;
the theorem does not assume all positive maps are physical or every abstract
effect is available. It does not assume quantum Hilbert spaces, purification,
local tomography, a self-dual cone, or multiplicative composite fidelity.

For states x,y of the same system define

  r(x,y)=inf_M sum_j sqrt(M_j(x) M_j(y)),
  F_op(x,y)=r(x,y)^2,  theta(x,y)=acos r(x,y),
  b(x,y)=sqrt(2(1-r(x,y))).

F_op is squared operational fidelity. In quantum theory this equals squared
Uhlmann fidelity. The root fidelity itself and exact generic no-cloning are
established predecessor notions, not definitions claimed as new here.

Let S be a nonempty compact promised model in Omega_A and let B:A->AA be a
physical transformation. Its joint independent-product defect is

  e_B(x)=1-F_op(Bx,x tensor x).

Theorems concern this JOINT target. Accurate output marginals do not suffice.

2. Elementary operational geometry, with proofs

(i) theta and b are metrics. For a classical probability vector p, sqrt(p) is a
unit vector in the positive orthant. Its spherical angle and Euclidean distance
are metrics. Since acos and sqrt(2(1-.)) are decreasing, theta and b are the
suprema of these classical metrics over all measurements. A supremum of metrics
satisfies the triangle inequality. Effects separate states, so zero distance
implies equality. Measurement pullback gives contraction under physical maps.

(ii) Adding a fixed state preserves r, theta and b: appending the state and
discarding it are both physical maps. Product measurements imply

  r(x tensor x,y tensor y) <= r(x,y)^2.

For any eta>0 choose one measurement with root overlap within eta of the infimum,
use it on both copies, then let eta tend to zero. The inequality direction is
important: equality is not asserted for a general theory.

(iii) Let d_op(x,y)=sup_M TV(Mx,My), equivalently the supremum over binary
effects after outcome processing. Classical Hellinger/TV inequalities give

  1-r(x,y) <= d_op(x,y) <= sqrt(1-F_op(x,y)).

For a binary effect f,

  |sqrt(f(x))-sqrt(f(y))| <= b(x,y).

The latter is one coordinate of the classical Hellinger vector. Finite-
dimensional bounded effect sets make d_op continuous in the state topology;
the displayed inequalities and metric triangle make r, theta, b continuous.

(iv) Squared operational fidelity is separately concave. For fixed q, classical
F(p,q)=sum_i p_i q_i+2sum_{i<j}sqrt(p_i p_j q_i q_j) is concave in p, because
geometric mean is concave. Each measurement therefore gives a concave function
of x; the infimum of concave functions is concave. The same argument applies
to the other variable. No joint concavity of squared fidelity is used.

(v) Two uses of B yield a physical three-output channel C=(B tensor id)B with

  b(Cx,x tensor x tensor x) <= 2sqrt(2 e_B(x))=sqrt(8 e_B(x)).

Insert (B tensor id)(x tensor x) between the two states and use triangle,
contraction, and fixed-spectator invariance. Also b(Bx,x^2)^2=2(1-sqrt(1-e))<=2e.

3. Main theorem: one proper estimator, pointwise for every promised state

There are k<=D representatives s_1,...,s_k IN S and one fixed allowed k-outcome
measurement M such that for every x in S,

  sum_c M_c(x)[1-F_op(x,s_c)] <= 2800 e_B(x).             (R1)

The measurement and representatives depend on B and S, not the input state or
prior. If the good set below is empty, choose any s_1 in S and any trivial
measurement. Reconstructing the average state is a weaker conclusion than R1.

Proof. Put tau=1/2800 and S_tau={x in S:e_B(x)<=tau}. Assume it is nonempty.

Step A: separated compact classes. For x,y in S_tau write r=r(x,y). By (i),(ii),

  g(r):=acos(r^2)-acos r <= asin sqrt(e_B(x))+asin sqrt(e_B(y)).

Here the product overlap may be smaller than r^2, which strengthens this
inequality. The elementary bounds g(r)>=r(1-r) and asin z<(6/5)z in this range
give r(1-r)<1/20. Hence r<1/16 or r>15/16. The high-overlap relation is
transitive: two high overlaps imply the third exceeds
cos(2acos(15/16))=97/128>1/16, so it must be high. Its compact classes are
uniformly separated in theta, and compactness of S_tau gives finitely many.

Within a class, r>3/4 and

  g(r)>=r(1-r)/sqrt(1-r^4)
       =r sqrt(1-r^2)/[(1+r)sqrt(1+r^2)]
       >=(12/35)sqrt(1-r^2).

Squaring the upper bound using (sqrt(e_x)+sqrt(e_y))^2<=2(e_x+e_y) gives

  1-F_op(x,y)<=25(e_B(x)+e_B(y)).                       (R2)

Choose a minimum-defect representative s_c in each compact class. Then

  1-F_op(x,s_c)<=50e_B(x),  e_B(s_c)<=e_B(x).           (R3)

Step B: a weak classifier without a quantum pretty-good measurement. Let R_m
be optimal worst-case risk for m representatives from distinct classes. It is
attained by compactness. Measure an optimal classifier on the three ideal
copies and take majority. At most two wrong answers is the failure event, so
the ideal error is <=3R_m^2. Pull the rule back through C and use (v),(iii):

  R_m <= f(R_m),  f(z)=(sqrt(3)z+sqrt(1/350))^2.        (R4)

Set a=1/200, b0=13/50. The convex polynomial f(z)-z is negative on [a,b0].
Indeed sqrt(3)<7/4 and 1/sqrt(350)<3/56 give
f(a)<(349/5600)^2<1/256<a and f(b0)<(89/175)^2<b0.
Consequently any R_m<=b0 forces R_m<a.

For two distinct representatives r<1/16. A measurement with root overlap
<1/16, coarsened by the classical likelihood-ratio rule, yields a binary test
whose SUM of conditional errors is <=root overlap<1/16. Thus each conditional
error is <1/16. For three representatives, perform one such test for each pair
on its own ideal copy. Declare the label winning both its pair tests if there
is one; assign any outcome on cycles. For every true label, its two relevant
tests both succeed except with probability <2/16=1/8. After C the risk is

  <(sqrt(1/8)+sqrt(1/350))^2<(3/7)^2<13/50.

The cases m<=3 therefore enter the risk gap. For m>=4, randomly omit one label
and use the already constructed (m-1)-label measurement. Its risk is
<=1/m+(1-1/m)a<=203/800<b0. Induction using (R4) gives R_m<a for every m.

Apply this to all representatives. Their response matrix A_{ic}=M_i(s_c) is
column stochastic and A_cc>1-a>1/2, so every column is strictly diagonally
dominant. It is invertible (equivalently apply the elementary diagonal-
dominance argument to its transpose). But A factors through V_A, so rank(A)<=D.
Therefore the number k of classes is at most D. This dimension bound uses no
trace, orthogonality, self-duality, or purification.

Step C: extend and amplify on one globally constrained compact set. The
representative classifier has error on every x in class c bounded by
a+d_op(x,s_c)<=a+sqrt(50tau)=a+1/sqrt(56)<1/4<b0.
Minimize the worst risk over S_tau. Equation (R4) applies to that minimizer as
well, hence the optimal worst risk is <a.

Let K be the compact convex set of allowed class measurements having risk
<=a on EVERY good state. It is nonempty. For any finite prior on S_tau,
minimize average risk R over K and put E=average e_B. The majority pullback
remains in K, since f(a)<a. Its error at state i is

  r'_i<=(sqrt(3)r_i+sqrt(8e_i))^2.

Optimality, r_i^2<=a r_i, and Cauchy-Schwarz imply

  R<=(sqrt(3aR)+sqrt(8E))^2,
  R<=C_* E,  C_*=8/(1-sqrt(3/200))^2<11.             (R5)

Finite minimax on any finite subset of good states supplies one element of K
with r(x)<=C_*e_B(x) on that subset. The corresponding closed subsets of the
SAME compact K have the finite-intersection property. Thus one M satisfies
this pointwise bound for all good states. On the correct outcome its loss is
<=50e_B(x) by (R3); on a wrong outcome it is <=1. Consequently the expected
proper estimation loss is <61e_B(x) for good states. Bad states have loss<=1
<2800e_B(x). This proves (R1), including zero defects exactly.

4. A universal risk equivalence and the tensor-fidelity caveat

For a compact model S define minimax independent replication risk e_rep and
proper-estimation risk R_prop by infimizing the respective worst defects or
expected losses above over allowed channels/measurements and estimates in S.
Then

  R_prop <=2800 e_rep,       e_rep <=4 R_prop.          (R6)

The first inequality follows pointwise from R1 and taking infima. For the
second, estimate s and prepare s tensor s. Separate concavity of F_op and
the angle hybrid through x tensor s give

  theta(x^2,s^2)<=2theta(x,s),
  1-F_op(x^2,s^2)<=4[1-F_op(x,s)].

If theta>pi/4 the last bound is trivial since its right side is >2; below that
threshold it follows from sin^2(2theta)<=4sin^2theta. Average over the estimate.
For classical and quantum products the exact fidelity multiplication improves
4 to 2. Using 2 for an arbitrary operational composite would be an unjustified
import. If an infimum uses general outcome estimators, continuous losses on
compact S permit finite quantization of the estimate range before taking the
limit, provided the relevant classical outcome processing is allowed.

R6 does not equate this loss with ordinary parameter loss at a different scale.
For S={rho_theta^n}, the loss is 1-F_op(rho_theta^n,rho_estimate^n). It is not
single-copy infidelity. This distinction prevents an incorrect amplification-
versus-learning claim for continuous Gaussian location models.

5. Deterministic label experiments and asymptotic rigidity

When sup_{x in S} e_B(x)<=epsilon<=tau, the proof supplies a deterministic
class c(x), k<=D representatives inside S, and one measurement with

  Pr[M(x)!=c(x)]<=C_*epsilon,
  1-F_op(x,s_{c(x)})<=50epsilon.                      (R7)

Let L denote the purely classical experiment returning c(x) deterministically.
Forward simulation S->L has TV error<=C_*epsilon; reverse simulation L->S
by preparing s_c has operational TV error<=sqrt(50epsilon). Hence an
asymptotically product-replicable sequence of compact experiments admits
increasingly accurate deterministic finite-label reductions. The statement is
uniform over states and all allowed terminal measurements, not only one prior
or one observable. Conversely any finite-label reduction with errors r and
representative infidelity ell yields replication error<=r+4ell.

This is a quantitative operational extension of the exact generic no-cloning
principle. It creates a proper-learning and approximate-label conclusion that
the earlier EB reconstruction statement by itself does not express. It still
does not give an efficient algorithm to acquire the measurement or dictionary.
It has not been shown to resolve an independently major open problem.

6. Fractional independent amplification: a universal lower-bound transfer

Let a quantum or operational model have n-copy inputs and let A map n copies
to n+k copies with pointwise defect e_A(theta). Repeatedly apply A to the first
n current outputs while retaining the existing surplus. After m=ceil(n/k)
calls there are at least 2n outputs; discard extras. At each step the ideal
product input incurs angle at most asin sqrt(e_A(theta)), and physical maps
contract the accumulated angle. Thus the resulting doubling channel has

  e_double(theta)<=m^2 e_A(theta).                    (R8)

If m asin sqrt(e)<=pi/2, use sin(m t)<=m sin t. Otherwise sin t>=1/m
(as m sin(pi/(2m))>=1), so the right side is >=1 and the bound is trivial.
Applying R1 to the n-copy promised experiment proves

  R_prop,n <=2800 ceil(n/k)^2 sup_theta e_A(theta).    (R9)

No input copies are secretly supplied to the construction: only the original
n and m calls to the given amplifier are used. This is an existence reduction;
the estimator compilation cost remains unknown. Its bounded loss saturates1,
so the inequality does not recover dimension-sharp Gaussian amplification or
replace the established general statistical-amplification theory.

7. Provenance and exact remaining gap

The operational root fidelity is an established construction in C. Zander and
A. R. Plastino, Fidelity measure and conservation of information in general
probabilistic theories, EPL86 (2009)18004:
https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstreams/2519298d-70dc-4f12-b90a-4d324796c49e/download
Barnum-Barrett-Leifer-Wilce, quant-ph/0611295, Theorem1, establishes EXACT
co-clonability iff joint distinguishability, using iterated exact replication:
https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0611295
The present deduction retains quantitative defects, uses a bounded three-copy
base and deletion/risk gap, and gives pointwise proper estimation and R7.

Sample amplification and learning already have a substantial general theory:
Axelrod-Garg-Han-Sharan-Valiant, arXiv2201.04315v2, especially sections2,6,7:
https://arxiv.org/html/2201.04315v2
The Gaussian amplification obstruction and broad amplification/learning link
are not claimed new here. No inspected statement establishes this exact
operational pointwise theorem, but this is not an exhaustive priority proof.

Surviving mechanism: a nonlinear risk improvement plus convex deletion and
constrained minimax converts weak distinguishability into uniform linear
proper-estimation control, across operational theories. Remaining decisive
obstruction: acquisition of the classifier/dictionary and a consequence that
changes a major unresolved theorem or computational capability. Broadening
the state-space formalism alone does not certify that significance.
