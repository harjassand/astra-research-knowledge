# Exact quadratic replication in an operational disk theory

Independent derivation by the cross-corpus worker, 11 October 2026.
Status: complete mathematical argument under the explicit theory below; historical originality and external validation remain unknown. This is a counterexample to a universal linear-in-copy-number GPT replication law, not a computational claim.

## 1. The theory, including the composite interface

Let `V=R^3`, with normalized states

\[
 \Omega=\{(1,x,y):x^2+y^2\le1\},\qquad u(1,x,y)=1.
\]

For every integer `n>=1`, take

\[
 V_n=V^{\otimes n},\qquad
 \Omega_n=\operatorname{conv}\{a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_n:a_j\in\Omega\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

These are the minimal state composites. They are compact, normalized, and span `V_n`. Grouping factors gives an associative composite rule. An allowed finite measurement is **any** tuple of linear functionals `e_j` nonnegative on the cone over `Omega_n`, with `sum_j e_j=u_n`. Allowed transformations are all linear normalized cone-positive maps `V_n -> V_k`.

This is a fully compositional finite-dimensional operational theory:

- Product states and measurements are allowed by construction.
- Every allowed transformation extends with any spectator: on an elementary product state, apply the transformation to its selected block; its output is a convex combination of elementary product states. Tensoring the remaining factors preserves membership in the appropriate minimal composite. Extend by convexity.
- Appending a fixed state, discarding, state preparation, and classical measurement followed by conditional preparation are allowed.
- Pullbacks of allowed measurements are allowed because transformations are positive and normalized.
- Currying a joint effect against a fixed ancillary state gives a bounded positive effect on the remaining system.
- Every fixed-outcome measurement set is closed and bounded in finite dimension, hence compact; it is convex and closed under classical processing.
- Conditional states are allowed: applying a positive effect to one block of a convex combination of elementary product states leaves a nonnegative combination of allowed states in the other block.

The construction has separable states and a large composite effect set. No entangled state preparation is required. The collective effects used below are positive on all allowed composite states but would not generally be valid quantum effects.

For every system use operational root fidelity and its angle:

\[
 r(a,b)=\inf_M\sum_j\sqrt{M_j(a)M_j(b)},\qquad
 F_{\rm op}(a,b)=r(a,b)^2,\qquad
 A(a,b)=\arccos r(a,b).
 \tag{2}
\]

Allowed channels contract `A`; appending a fixed ancillary state preserves it. Both follow directly from measurement pullback and discarding. The angle is a metric because it is the supremum of the classical spherical angles of the square-root probability vectors.

## 2. Exact single-copy overlap

Put

\[
 \omega_\theta=(1,\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
\]

For `|alpha-beta|<=pi`,

\[
 r(\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta)
 =\cos\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2},\qquad
 A(\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta)=\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Here is an elementary proof, without importing a quantum postulate. Associate to a disk state the real symmetric matrix

\[
 R(x,y)=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+x&y\\y&1-x\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every affine disk effect `e=a+b x+c y` corresponds to

\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}a+b&c\\c&a-b\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad e(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}[E R(x,y)].
\]

Nonnegativity on the disk is precisely `a>=sqrt(b^2+c^2)`, equivalently `E>=0`. Thus an allowed disk measurement corresponds to positive real matrices `E_j` summing to the identity. Moreover,

\[
 R(\omega_\theta)=v_\theta v_\theta^T,
 \qquad v_\theta=(\cos(\theta/2),\sin(\theta/2))^T.
\]

For every such measurement, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \sum_j\sqrt{(v_\alpha^T E_jv_\alpha)(v_\beta^TE_jv_\beta)}
 \ge\sum_j|v_\alpha^TE_jv_\beta|
 \ge|v_\alpha^Tv_\beta|
 =\cos\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2}.
\]

The binary effect `e=(1+cos(alpha)x+sin(alpha)y)/2` has probability one on `omega_alpha` and probability `cos^2((beta-alpha)/2)` on `omega_beta`, attaining this lower bound.

## 3. Exact Heisenberg growth of product-state angle

On elementary product states define the multilinear functional

\[
 H_{n,\alpha}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^n(1,x_j,y_j)\right)
 =\operatorname{Re}\left[e^{-in\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^n(x_j+iy_j)\right].
 \tag{4}
\]

Its absolute value is at most one, since each factor has modulus at most one. It therefore extends linearly to a functional in `[-1,1]` on all of `Omega_n`. Consequently

\[
 E_{n,\alpha}=\frac{u_n+H_{n,\alpha}}2
 \tag{5}
\]

is an allowed binary effect. On identical product inputs,

\[
 E_{n,\alpha}(\omega_\theta^{\otimes n})
 =\frac{1+\cos(n(\theta-\alpha))}2
 =\cos^2\frac{n(\theta-\alpha)}2.
 \tag{6}
\]

Take `Delta=|alpha-beta|` with `n Delta<=pi`. This effect has probability one on `omega_alpha^n`, so

\[
 r(\omega_\alpha^n,\omega_\beta^n)\le\cos(n\Delta/2).
\]

Conversely, change the tensor factors one at a time. Fixed-ancilla invariance and the angle triangle inequality imply

\[
 A(\omega_\alpha^n,\omega_\beta^n)
 \le n A(\omega_\alpha,\omega_\beta)=n\Delta/2.
\]

Combining both inequalities proves the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{A(\omega_\alpha^n,\omega_\beta^n)=n\Delta/2,\qquad
 1-F_{\rm op}(\omega_\alpha^n,\omega_\beta^n)=\sin^2(n\Delta/2)}
 \quad(n\Delta\le\pi).
 \tag{7}
\]

Thus the general angle hybrid is sharp. In particular, for fixed `n` as `Delta->0`, the ratio of product infidelity to single-copy infidelity tends to `n^2`, rather than `n`.

## 4. Exact optimal one-input replication of a binary model

Fix an integer `m>=2` and `0<Delta<=pi/m`. The promised input model is

\[
 S_\Delta=\{\omega_0,\omega_\Delta\},\qquad
 \gamma=\omega_{\Delta/2}.
\]

For an arbitrary allowed one-input, `m`-output channel `C`, let

\[
 \eta(C)=\max_{i\in\{0,\Delta\}}
 \left[1-F_{\rm op}(C\omega_i,\omega_i^{\otimes m})\right].
\]

By contractivity, `A(C omega_0,C omega_Delta)<=Delta/2`. By (7), the target angle is `m Delta/2`. The triangle inequality therefore gives

\[
 \arcsin\sqrt{e_0(C)}+\arcsin\sqrt{e_\Delta(C)}
 \ge\frac{(m-1)\Delta}2,
\]

and hence

\[
 \eta(C)\ge\sin^2\frac{(m-1)\Delta}4.
 \tag{8}
\]

This lower bound is attained exactly by retaining the unknown original and appending `m-1` copies of the fixed midpoint:

\[
 C_m(a)=a\otimes\gamma^{\otimes(m-1)}.
\]

Indeed, fixed-ancilla invariance and (7) show, for either promised input,

\[
 A(C_m\omega_i,\omega_i^{\otimes m})
 =A(\gamma^{\otimes(m-1)},\omega_i^{\otimes(m-1)})
 =\frac{(m-1)\Delta}4.
\]

Therefore the optimal deterministic replication risk is

\[
 \boxed{\eta_m^*(S_\Delta)=\sin^2\frac{(m-1)\Delta}4.}
 \tag{9}
\]

In particular, the supplied two-output cloner `B(a)=a tensor gamma` is itself optimal on the binary model and has defect

\[
 \epsilon=\eta_2^*(S_\Delta)=\sin^2(\Delta/4).
\]

Equation (9) becomes

\[
 \boxed{\eta_m^*(S_\Delta)
 =\sin^2\bigl((m-1)\arcsin\sqrt\epsilon\bigr),}
 \quad 4m\arcsin\sqrt\epsilon\le\pi.
 \tag{10}
\]

Reusing `B` exactly `m-1` times attains this risk. Thus even with a supplied optimal two-output cloner, the best possible `m`-replication loss grows as `(m-1)^2 epsilon` when `m sqrt(epsilon)->0`.

No universal constant `C` can make `eta_m^*<=C m epsilon` hold for all compact GPT models satisfying the operational closure axioms. For example, choose `Delta=m^-2` and let `m->infinity`; then the ratio `eta_m^*/(m epsilon)` is asymptotic to `m`.

## 5. What this separates

The forward replication-to-proper-estimation theorem needs only tensor **submultiplicativity** of operational root fidelity and the angle metric. Those properties hold here. They therefore cannot support a linear-in-`m` replica law in every operational theory.

For classical and quantum composites, exact product fidelity gives `1-F(a^m,b^m)<=m[1-F(a,b)]`. That extra tensor property supports linear replica loss after proper estimation. Equation (7) explicitly fails that property in this theory, identifying a load-bearing structural difference.

The construction charges its resources explicitly: one unknown input; a supplied model parameter `Delta`; preparation of the known state `gamma`; `m-1` uses of the two-output appending channel, or equivalently `m-1` fixed-state preparations; and an allowed `m`-system collective effect for the lower-bound witness. `dim V_m=3^m`. No efficient acquisition or implementation of an arbitrary GPT measurement is asserted.
