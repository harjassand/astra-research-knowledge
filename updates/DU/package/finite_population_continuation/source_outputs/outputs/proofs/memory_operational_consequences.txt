# Operational consequences and a Shannon-memory extension

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: new deductions from the internally reconstructed orbit-cap/Gaussian-potential theorem in `work/cross_corpus/RESULT.md` and the fixed SU(2) trace experiment in `REACH.md`. The regular spectral gap remains an imported hypothesis. No external validation or priority claim.

## 1. Fixed dimension prevents an exponential-in-horizon bit separation

For any fixed quantum dimension d and length-T stream of supplied CPTP maps, choose a trace-distance eta-net of the density matrices, of size at most `(C_d/eta)^(d^2-1)`. A deterministic classical controller stores a nearest net representative; after each map it rounds again. Trace-distance contractivity bounds the accumulated error by T eta, uniformly over all terminal POVMs. With eta=epsilon/T this uses `O_d(log(T/epsilon))` persistent bits. Arithmetic, net compilation, and supplied gate descriptions are separate costs. Consequently an exponential-in-T classical *bit* lower bound is impossible for fixed d in this unrestricted streaming model.

A single communication cut is easier still: Alice may compute the actual accumulated density matrix locally and send one epsilon-net index to Bob. Its bit cost is `O_d(log(1/epsilon))`, independent of T; Bob applies every suffix channel. Thus horizon-growing lower bounds concern repeated compositional compression, rather than an ordinary one-cut communication problem.

## 2. Promise decision reduction with two qubits

Represent a quaternion q by U(q) in SU(2). A controlled-U Hadamard test with maximally mixed two-dimensional target has acceptance probability `1/2 + Re Tr U(q)/4 = (1+q_0)/2`. Two qubits suffice. Appending U(u)^* turns the scalar into `<u,q>`.

Promise yes if `<u,q> >= 1/3`, no if `<= -1/3`. The test succeeds with probability at least 2/3. No correctness requirement is placed outside the promise.

Let q=X be any unit quaternion at the end of a stream, and choose an independent Haar direction u. Its scalar t=<u,X> has density `(2/pi)sqrt(1-t^2)` on [-1,1]. If a classical controller's final answer Y in {-1,+1} is correct on promised inputs with probability at least 2/3, then

`E[Yt | X] >= (1/3) E|t| - (4/3) E[|t| 1_{|t|<1/3}]`

`= (4/(3pi))[(4/3)(8/9)^(3/2)-1] > 0.049`.

On the other hand, when the late query u is independent of the prefix and its persistent state S,

`E[Y<u,X>] <= E ||E[X|S]||`.

Thus the orbit-memory lower bound applies even to this constant-gap yes/no task, permitting arbitrary behavior off promise. A fixed finite algebraic approximation to Haar queries suffices: a deterministic angular grid coupled within Euclidean distance eta=1/1024 gives a score exceeding 1/32 by the following crude bounds. Put M=4/(3pi)>2/5 and `B(a)=M[1-(1-a^2)^(3/2)]`. The finite query score is at least

`(1/3)(M-eta) - (4/3)(B(1/3+eta)+eta)`.

Since `1/3+eta<17/50`, `sqrt(1-(17/50)^2)>47/50`, this exceeds `(2/5)[(4/3)(2211/2500)(47/50)-1] -5/(3*1024) >1/32`. Grid angles that are rational multiples of pi give algebraic gate entries. This construction is explicit but gives a large fixed alphabet; no small efficient alphabet claim is made.

For the fixed nu stream of REACH (finite full-gap SU(2) law), the resulting classical state lower bound is `Omega_nu(T^(3/2))`, and the dynamic worst-case bit-time lower bound is `(3/2)T log_2 T - O_nu(T)`. The quantum task uses two qubits and one acquired finite-alphabet symbol per step, plus one acquired terminal query. This is a consequence of the source mechanism, not a claim that constant-memory quantum promise separation itself is new. Primary overlap already found: Zheng–Li–Qiu–Gruska arXiv:1411.3870; Geffert–Yakaryilmaz arXiv:1405.6671; their broad qualitative promise-separation results must be distinguished from the quantitative horizon/dynamic-memory consequence here.

## 3. New extension: conditional Shannon memory, with countably many states

This extends the source atom-count proof rather than merely restating it. Let R be arbitrary input-independent public randomness. A controller may have countably many persistent states and may choose gate-aware stochastic kernels depending on R and time. Put

`v_i(t,r)=E[X_t | S_t=i,R=r]`, `q_i(t,r)=Pr(S_t=i | R=r)`,

`mu_(t,r)=sum_i q_i(t,r) delta_v_i(t,r)`, and `M_t=E_R integral ||v||^2 dmu_(t,R)`.

The source convex-order evolution and matched Gaussian potential hold conditional on R and then after integration. If the terminal retained norm is at least r0, Jensen gives `M_T>=a:=r0^2`; hence `M_t>=a` throughout. Let H_t be conditional Shannon entropy `H(S_t|R)` in nats. For a threshold h, consider a time with H_t<=h. Truncate atoms with

`q_i(t,r)<exp(-2h/a)`.

Markov's inequality applied to conditional surprisal discards total joint mass at most a/2. In each fiber r there are at most `m_h=exp(2h/a)` retained atoms. Atoms with norm at least `s=sqrt(a)/2` have total joint mass at least 3a/4, because `M_t<=a/4+Pr(||v||>=s)`. Consequently retained atoms of norm at least s have joint mass at least a/4.

Let L=2 sqrt(D),

`b=(s/2)[a/(32 C m_h)]^(1/k)`, `r_G=b/L`.

In each fiber take E_R to be the union of radius-b balls around its retained high-norm atoms. Gaussian smoothing places mass at least `3a/16>=a/8` in this joint set. Every orbit meeting E_R has norm at least s/2, and the uniform cap bound gives orbit fraction at most a/32. Therefore, applying reverse triangle inequality in the joint space `(R,z)`,

`E_R ||sqrt(f_mu) - J sqrt(f_mu)||_2^2 >= (sqrt(a/8)-sqrt(a/32))^2 = a/32`.

Every time with H_t<=h spends at least beta a/32 of the *same* integrated Gaussian potential. Every other time decreases that potential nonnegatively. The initial potential is at most

`1/(2 r_G^2) = (32D/a)(32 C/a)^(2/k) exp(4h/(ak))`.

It follows that

`#{t<T : H_t<=h} <= K exp(4h/(ak))`,

`K = [1024D/(beta a^2)](32 C/a)^(2/k)`.

Sorting the entropies yields, in bits,

`sum_(t<T) H_2(S_t|R) >= (ak/4) log_2(T!) - (ak/4) T log_2 K`.

In particular it is `Omega(T log T)`. This covers countably infinite alphabets and rare enormous states, not just a finite maximum state count. Any conditional prefix-free representation of the persistent state has expected length at least this conditional entropy, so the same bound applies to summed expected code length. No sharp leading coefficient is claimed: deliberate probabilistic dropout can reduce entropy while leaving a positive retained score. Public randomness is free only when independent of the acquired input; an input-dependent correlated noise reservoir is a persistent resource and falls outside this premise.

The proof requires the orbit-cap and full regular spectral-gap hypotheses of the source transfer theorem. All its additional steps are written above. It does not establish an unrestricted Turing-machine running-time lower bound or include quantum gate-precision hardware costs.

## 4. Other alternatives, not new theorems

Late-query Hidden Matching gives a known growing-dimension alternative: an acquired n-bit string produces `n^(-1/2) sum_i (-1)^(x_i)|i>` by indexed phase updates, then a later matching query is answered exactly with log n qubits; classical public-coin one-way summaries require Omega(sqrt(n)) bits. The latter is an imported prior theorem, not proved or claimed new here. An orbit/Hidden-Matching hybrid with a stronger multiplicative separation was not closed.

The one-dimensional growing-Hilbert-space gap mechanism and fixed-gate finite-precision accounting remain open development routes. The current orbit process establishes persistent-state and conditional-entropy consequences, not a total-device superiority claim.
