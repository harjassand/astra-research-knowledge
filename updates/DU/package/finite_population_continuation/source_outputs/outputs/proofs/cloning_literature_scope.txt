# Primary-literature scope check for the linear cloning–estimation theorem

Date accessed: 11 October 2026. This is a targeted source comparison, not an exhaustive priority determination. The inspected sources below do not state the new candidate's exact universal linear average-infidelity bound. Failure to locate it in this search is not proof that it is historically new.

## Exact proposed interface

For every finite pure-state ensemble in finite dimension, compare optimal global `1->2` product-target cloning infidelity with optimal single-copy EB reconstruction infidelity. The candidate proves `e_EB<=2800 e_cl`, independent of dimension, number of labels, and smallest positive prior. For a fixed cloner, compactness strengthens the statement to one EB surrogate satisfying the loss inequality on all pure inputs simultaneously. Known-state representation and unknown-state acquisition are separate interfaces.

## Sources inspected

1. **Christopher A. Fuchs and Masahide Sasaki, “Squeezing Quantum Information through a Classical Channel: Measuring the Quantumness of a Set of Quantum States” (2003).** [Primary arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302092v3). Section 2 defines accessible fidelity for arbitrary pure-state ensembles using measurement and preparation; this is exactly our `F_EB` quantity for a fixed prior. Section 4.1, Eq. (73), gives the balanced binary formula `(1+sqrt(1-c^2+c^4))/2`, used to verify the lower-constant example. Section 1 compares several notions of binary clonability, emphasizing sensitivity to the chosen cloning score. We rely on the accessible-fidelity terminology and formula, and independently rederive the binary formula. The inspected results do not give the proposed all-ensemble linear relation.

2. **K. M. R. Audenaert, C. A. Fuchs, C. King, and A. Winter, “Multiplicativity of Accessible Fidelity and Quantumness for Sets of Quantum States” (2003/2004).** [Primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308120). The abstract states multiplicativity under tensor products and a connection with entanglement-breaking channels. This confirms accessible fidelity is an established operational quantity; neither the quantity nor its EB interpretation is invented here. Only the record/abstract was inspected in this pass, so no broader noncontainment assertion is made.

3. **Joonwoo Bae and Antonio Acín, “Asymptotic Quantum Cloning Is State Estimation” (2006).** [Primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0603078). The abstract establishes equivalence as the number of clones tends to infinity using monogamy and EB channels. This is an asymptotic output-number result, whereas the candidate compares a two-output global score with one-output accessible fidelity near unit score. Only the record/abstract was inspected here; the more quantitative comparison below uses Chiribella's full text.

4. **Giulio Chiribella, “On Quantum Estimation, Quantum Cloning and Finite Quantum de Finetti Theorems” (2010/2011).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1010.1875v1). Theorems 2.1–2.2 approximate the `k`-output restriction of a symmetric `M`-output broadcast channel by a measure-and-prepare channel, in diamond norm, with bounds of order `dk/M` for symmetric-subspace output and `d^2k/M` for arbitrary symmetric output. Section 2.2 applies this to asymptotic cloning/estimation. These are strong channel-level results, but their displayed constants depend on output dimension and the number of clones, rather than on a supplied family's small two-copy global infidelity. Iterating an imperfect cloner and then applying such estimates introduces additional errors and does not directly yield the candidate's dimension-free linear premise-to-conclusion estimate.

5. **Yuxiang Yang and Giulio Chiribella, “Is Global Asymptotic Cloning State Estimation?” (2013/2014).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.6427v2). It compares `N->M` global cloning and measure-and-prepare scores as `M` tends to infinity, for equatorial qubits and an economical covariant maximally-entangled-state model. It permits prepared multipartite entangled states, and warns that preparing `M` identical estimated copies is not generally optimal for a global score. Our score comparison instead uses global fidelity only for a `1->2` cloner, and reconstructs one input copy through an EB channel. Thus asymptotic global equivalence and the proposed universal finite near-unit bound are different claims.

6. **Wei Xie, Kun Fang, Xin Wang, and Runyao Duan, “Approximate Broadcasting of Quantum Correlations” (2017).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.06071v2). Section V, Eqs. (26)–(27) and Theorem 12, optimizes average marginal broadcasting fidelity for a supplied set of potentially mixed states using an SDP. Its score averages one-clone marginal fidelities; the candidate's hypothesis is joint product-target fidelity on both clones. These scores agree at exact perfect pure-state cloning but are distinct away from zero error. The paper supplies useful optimization machinery, not the inspected universal accessible-fidelity loss bound.

7. **Yaroslav Herasymenko, Junqiao Lin, Philip Verduyn Lunel, and Dmitry Grinko, “Approximate Cloning of Structured Pure States” (5 October 2026).** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06723v1). This recent paper studies structured `N->N+K` cloning. For its resolving-orbit families, Corollary IX.2 equates optimal cloning fidelity `d_N/d_(N+K)` with learning to prepare `K` fresh copies. In contrast, measuring and replacing all consumed `N` copies as well as the extra `K` caps fidelity at `d_N/d_(2N+K)`. This is relevant score discipline: small error from adding copies need not permit EB reconstruction of all consumed copies with the same error. It does not contradict our two-copy input-family theorem, which reconstructs a single input rather than all input copies of a large-`N` learning task.

8. **Zhenyu Du, Siyuan Cheng, Qi Zhao, Xiongfeng Ma, and Xiao Yuan, “No Cloning of Quantum Ensembles” (27 June 2026).** [Primary Nature Communications article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74924-x). The introduction and operational setting supply repeated copies of a purification whose random measurement outcomes generate conditional states; the target is to produce repeated copies of the same conditional state or estimate its nonlinear properties. The results address sample and computational acquisition barriers, including known preparation circuits. Our theorem assumes the family and cloner as mathematical inputs and supplies an existential reconstruction channel; it therefore does not bypass that paper's acquisition bottlenecks. The full paper's complexity proofs were not reconstructed in this pass.

## Search scope and remaining uncertainty

Search phrases included combinations of “cloning,” “accessible fidelity,” “global fidelity,” “infidelity,” “linear,” “approximate broadcasting,” “measure-and-prepare,” and “state estimation.” Results were checked against their primary sources where used above. The closest inspected general channel-simulation theorem is the finite de Finetti bound, and the closest exact target quantity is accessible fidelity. A specialist priority review and citation-chain search are still needed before claiming the universal linear inequality or the constrained-decision bootstrap is new.

The archived Astra inputs were separately reconstructed from pinned sources; their unpublished/source-derived status is recorded in `linear_cloning_estimation.md`. No primary source in this note is treated as external validation of that candidate proof.
