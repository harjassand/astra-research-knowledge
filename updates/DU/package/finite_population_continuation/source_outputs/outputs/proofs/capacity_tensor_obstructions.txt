## 1. Major target: computability of ordinary quantum capacity

Input: a finite-dimensional memoryless CPTP channel given by an explicit computable rational/algebraic representation, with a rational accuracy request. Target: a terminating algorithm approximating its ordinary unassisted quantum capacity, or a fixed-gap undecidability reduction. A circuit representation and an explicit Choi matrix have different computational sizes and must not be conflated.

The primary-source status check found [Bhattacharyya–Mehta–Zhao, arXiv:2601.22471v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22471v3): QMA-hardness is established for ordinary capacity approximation; their undecidability statement concerns a restricted entanglement-assisted zero-error quantity. Their introduction explicitly keeps ordinary computability unresolved. That source status is not a proof of what every later paper might contain.

The first attempted operation was an explicit threshold amplifier: convert arbitrarily small positive capacity into a fixed normalized gap without supplying its unknown witness blocklength. The following bound prevents a broad black-box version.

### Reference-weighted hybrid bound for adaptive stopping

Fix a quantum protocol `G` with a finite-dimensional input `A` and a fixed finite-dimensional output `B`. Between calls to its channel oracle it can use arbitrary quantum instruments, memory, classical randomness and classical stopping decisions. The instructions are independent of which oracle is installed. Let `T` count oracle calls. Assume it halts almost surely for every input with either channel `M` or `N`, and

\[
K=\sup_{\rho_A}\mathbb E_{M,\rho_A}T<\infty.
\]

Only the reference channel `M` requires this finite expected-use bound. Then

\[
\|G(N)-G(M)\|_\diamond
\leq \min\{2,K\|N-M\|_\diamond\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Proof. Truncate after `L` calls, replacing any unfinished run by one orthogonal failure output. Telescope from the all-`N` protocol to the all-`M` protocol, replacing oracle calls in chronological order. In the term replacing call `j`, its entire prefix is `M`. The subnormalized live input to that call has trace

\[
p_j=\Pr_{M,\rho_A}(T\geq j).
\]

For an arbitrary entangled reference, the diamond norm bounds the change at that call by `p_j ||N-M||diamond`. The rest of the truncated computation is a channel and cannot increase trace norm. Thus the sum is at most

\[
\|N-M\|_\diamond\sum_{j=1}^L p_j
\leq K\|N-M\|_\diamond.
\]

For completeness, almost-sure halting is uniform over the finite-dimensional input unit sphere. The unfinished probability after `L` calls equals `Tr(E_L rho_A)` for a decreasing sequence of positive effects `E_L`. Almost-sure halting for every input implies `E_L -> 0`; finite-dimensionality gives convergence in operator norm. Hence both truncated channels converge in diamond norm to their actual stopped channels. Taking `L -> infinity` proves (1). External entanglement does not alter unfinished probability, which depends only on the input marginal.

This is a stopping-time form of the usual channel-discrimination hybrid argument. No priority claim is made.

### Consequence for a normalized capacity gap

Let `D = dim B >= 2`. The finite-output continuity theorem of [Leung–Smith, Corollary 14](https://arxiv.org/html/0810.4931) gives, when `x = K ||N-M||diamond <= 1/2`,

\[
|Q(G(N))-Q(G(M))|
\leq 8x\log_2D+4h_2(x).
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose separately that `Q(G(M))=0` and `Q(G(N)) >= c log_2 D`, for `0<c<=1`. Define `gamma_c` as the unique root in `(0,1/2)` of

\[
8\gamma_c+4h_2(\gamma_c)=c.
\]

Then (including the case `x>1/2`)

\[
K\|N-M\|_\diamond\geq\gamma_c.
\tag{3}
\]

For the `d`-dimensional erasure channels with erasure probabilities `1/2` and `1/2-eta`, their diamond distance is `2 eta`: the difference on any input is `eta rho` on the transmitted sector and `-eta |e><e|` on the erasure sector. These sectors are orthogonal, giving norm `2 eta` even without a reference. Consequently any such amplifier needs

\[
K\geq\frac{\gamma_c}{2\eta}.
\]

This does not prove any general capacity undecidability statement, and it does not prohibit reductions on discrete instance families that are not arbitrarily close to one another. It excludes a uniform finite-cost black-box gap amplifier around a zero-capacity output reference.

The assumption `Q(G(M))=0` is essential. It does not follow from `Q(M)=0`: feedback and stopping can turn a half-erasure channel into a reliable link by retrying until a flagged success. Also, an unbounded retained transcript gives an infinite-dimensional output and cannot use the finite-`D` step. A postselected success branch is not a deterministic channel unless failure is included. For two subnormalized successful outputs of trace at least `s`, normalizing can magnify their trace distance by as much as `2/s`; the success cost is therefore load-bearing. In particular, conditioning on a nonzero success whose reference probability is zero escapes the hypotheses rather than refuting the bound.

### Second attempted operation: noise-induced entropy cutoff

The alternative was to add a small known amount of local input noise, use continuity to preserve capacity to the requested accuracy, and then use noise-induced Fourier truncation or dimension reduction to acquire a finite blocklength cutoff. The analogy was [Qin–Yao's decidability theorem for fully quantum games supplied with noisy maximally entangled states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10613). Their theorem has a different input interface. The attempted capacity transfer requires control of an extensive output entropy, which the following example rules out for marginal/low-degree truncations.

Let `C <= F_2^n` be a linear code, `X` uniform on `C`, and `Z` have independent `Bernoulli(p)` coordinates. Put `Y=X+Z`. If the dual code has minimum nonzero Hamming weight `d_perp`, then

\[
Y_S\text{ is exactly uniform for every }|S|<d_\perp.
\tag{4}
\]

Proof. Projection of `C` onto coordinates `S` is surjective unless a nonzero linear functional on `F_2^S` annihilates it. Such a functional would extend by zero to a dual word supported on `S`, contrary to its minimum weight. Uniform `X` therefore has a uniform projection; adding independent noise preserves it.

On the other hand,

\[
H(Y)\leq H(X,Z)=\dim C+n h_2(p).
\tag{5}
\]

Thus a rate-`R` code gives an entropy deficit at least `n(1-R-h_2(p))`, despite exact agreement with the uniform distribution on all the marginals in (4). Such families exist with `d_perp >= delta n` whenever `h_2(delta)<R`: choose an `Rn x n` binary generator matrix randomly. For each nonzero vector of weight at most `delta n`, the probability that it is annihilated by the matrix is `2^{-Rn}`. The union bound is at most

\[
2^{-Rn}\sum_{j\leq\delta n}\binom nj
\leq 2^{-n(R-h_2(\delta))}
\]

up to rounding, and is below one for large `n`. Full generator rank is unnecessary: the resulting code dimension is at most `Rn`, which only strengthens (5).

For instance choose `R=1/2`, `delta=0.1`, `p=0.01`. Here `h_2(delta)<1/2` and the entropy deficit is at least approximately `0.4192 n` bits. The argument also gives diagonal quantum density operators. Qubit depolarization of strength `eta=0.02` acts on these diagonal states as bit flips with `p=0.01`. Every marginal below the dual distance remains maximally mixed after that fixed local noise. Equivalently, every nonconstant Fourier coefficient below that degree vanishes, while the entropy rate differs from the maximally mixed state by a constant.

Consequently, a generic operation recovering entropy rate from all sublinear-order marginals, bounded Fourier degree, or a fixed-size local junta cannot supply the proposed capacity cutoff. This is not a counterexample to the cited noisy-game theorem, nor an impossibility result for every conceivable global entropy representation.

Acquisition was charged in the finite receipt: `check.py` generates dual generator matrices, checks exact binary rank, enumerates all nonzero dual vectors, and records the actual minimum weight. The fixtures have `(n, dim C, d_perp) = (16,8,3), (24,12,4), (32,16,6)`. Their entropy-deficit lower bounds are respectively about `6.7073`, `10.0610`, and `13.4146` bits at `p=0.01`. These are finite receipts, not evidence of an efficient growing-family compiler. Exact minimum-distance verification costs exponentially many dual vectors in this implementation.

### Why continuity and compactness do not finish the cutoff

For fixed input and output dimensions define

\[
F_m(N)=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\frac1j\max_\rho I_c(\rho,N^{\otimes j}).
\]

Each `F_m` is continuous, the sequence increases, and its pointwise limit is `Q`. The fixed-dimension channel set is compact. The continuity theorem plus Dini's theorem therefore proves that a finite uniform cutoff exists for every requested error. It supplies no computable cutoff. This distinction cannot be repaired solely by the known continuity modulus: a computable increasing rational sequence converging to a noncomputable lower-semicomputable real, treated as constant functions on `[0,1]`, has a computable zero continuity modulus and still has no computable convergence rate. The capacity functions may possess additional structure that changes the answer; neither attempted operation acquired it.

## 2. Major pivot target: matrix multiplication exponent two

After the entropy route failed, I tested whether the source's new polynomial-multiplication profile could force the optimal arithmetic exponent by stronger use of its disclosed scalar inequalities.

Source: OpenAI Math `preprints/Matrix-Multiplication-Nine-Fourths-October-2-2026/build/paper.tex` at the pinned revision. The scalar profile and inequalities appear around lines 417–440, 442–527, 529–603, and 605–704. The source status is model-produced, not automatically certified. The following result needs only the displayed scalar hypotheses, so it does not assume that the source's tensor proof is correct.

Define

\[
\ell_1=1,\qquad
\ell_a=\prod_{m=1}^{a-1}\left(1+\frac1{3m}\right),\qquad
P_*(a,b)=\ell_{\min(a,b)}\left[\max(a,b)+\frac{\min(a,b)-1}{2}\right].
\tag{6}
\]

This exact rational profile has all of the following properties:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_*(a,b)&=P_*(b,a),&P_*(1,b)&=b,\\
2P_*(a,b)&\geq P_*(a,b-1)+P_*(a,b+1),& b&\geq2,\\
P_*(a,3h+a-1)&\geq3P_*(a,h),\\
P_*(a,b)&\leq(a+b-1)^{4/3},\\
P_*(a,a)&=\Theta(a^{4/3}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus every one of these scalar premises remains compatible with the source parameter `t=3/4`, hence with its final `3t=9/4`. No rearrangement or iteration of these premises alone can force an exponent strictly below `9/4`. In particular, they cannot establish exponent two. This is a scalar method-scope countermodel; it does not construct a tensor character with `t=3/4`, and it is not a lower bound on matrix multiplication.

Proof of discrete concavity. Fix `a`. For `b>=a`, the increment is `ell_a`. For `b<a`, the recurrence `ell_{b+1}/ell_b=(3b+1)/(3b)` gives

\[
P_*(a,b+1)-P_*(a,b)=\ell_b\frac{2b+a}{3b}.
\]

The last such increment, at `b=a-1`, equals `ell_a`. Earlier increments are decreasing because

\[
3(b+1)(2b+a)-(3b+1)(2b+a+2)=2(a-b-1)\geq0.
\]

This proves separate discrete concavity, including the diagonal join.

Proof of shifted tripling. If `h>=a`, substitution in (6) gives equality. If `h<a`, the required inequality is

\[
\ell_a(a+2h-1)\geq\ell_h(2a+h-1).
\]

For fixed `h`, the function of integer `a>=h`

\[
R_a=\ell_a\frac{a+2h-1}{2a+h-1}
\]

is nondecreasing. After clearing positive denominators in `R_{a+1}>=R_a`, the difference is exactly `2(a-h)(a-h+1)`. Since `R_h=ell_h`, the claim follows.

Proof of the profile upper bound. Induction gives

\[
\ell_a^3\leq\frac{3a-1}{2}.
\]

Indeed the inductive ratio inequality, writing `y=3(a-1)`, reduces to

\[
(y+1)^3(y-1)\leq y^3(y+2),
\]

whose right side minus left side is `2y+1`. At `b=a` this yields

\[
P_*(a,a)\leq\left(\frac{3a-1}{2}\right)^{4/3}\leq(2a-1)^{4/3}.
\]

For `b>=a`, the function `(a+b-1)^(4/3)/(b+(a-1)/2)` is increasing in `b`: its logarithmic derivative has the sign of `b-(a-1)>0`. The diagonal inequality therefore proves the claimed bound everywhere, by symmetry. Finally `ell_a = Gamma(a+1/3)/(Gamma(4/3) Gamma(a)) = Theta(a^(1/3))`, proving the diagonal order in (7).

The attempted new tensor operation was to couple the determinant filtrations on all three legs, instead of using the one-leg Clebsch–Gordan filtration repeatedly. The formal leading sectors are indexed by determinant orders `i,j,k` with `k=i+j`; this leaves a coupled convolution grid rather than independent full direct-sum blocks. Extracting a diagonal matching reproduces an entropy/rank constraint and does not beat the countermodel. No global sector-separation operation with a favorable charged cost was acquired. This is the exact remaining step, not an assumed lemma.
