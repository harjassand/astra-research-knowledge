# Replication necessarily spends a linear global-error budget

Research date: 2026-10-11. Independent derivation by OpenAI miner A. Status: elementary analytic reconstruction; no priority claim. All fidelities below are squared Uhlmann fidelity. The lower bounds concern one supplied input copy and arbitrary CPTP output maps; any uses of a supplied doubling channel are subsumed by such a map. They do not prohibit preparing many outputs with good single-copy marginal fidelity.

## 1. One fixed supplied doubling channel is enough

Let H=C^2 and define the isometry

`V|0> = |00>`, `V|1> = (|01>+|10>)/sqrt(2)`.

The fixed, completely explicit CPTP doubler is `B(X)=VXV*`. For `0<=t<1` and any phase phi, put

`|psi_(t,phi)> = sqrt(1-t)|0> + exp(i phi)sqrt(t)|1>`,

`P_(t,phi)=|psi_(t,phi)><psi_(t,phi)|`, `r=sqrt(2)-1`, `alpha=1/r^2=3+2sqrt(2)`.

There is no acquisition oracle in B: append one known |0> ancilla and apply the fixed two-qubit unitary whose actions are `|00>->|00>`, `|10>->(|01>+|10>)/sqrt(2)`, `|01>->(|01>-|10>)/sqrt(2)`, `|11>->|11>`. Its only nontrivial coefficients are algebraic `+-1/sqrt(2)`.

Its joint product fidelity is

`F(B(P_(t,phi)), P_(t,phi)^(tensor 2)) = (1-t)(1+r t)^2`,

because the amplitude overlap equals `sqrt(1-t)[1+r t]`, independently of phi. Consequently its exact loss is

`epsilon(t) = r^2 t + (4sqrt(2)-5)t^2 + r^2 t^3`.

In particular `epsilon(t)/t -> r^2` and `epsilon(0)=0`. This is one channel B for all t, phases, copy counts, and error scales. There is no family of optimally tailored supplied doublers hidden in this example.

Consider any conversion assigning an m-output CPTP map C_m to this fixed B and satisfying a pointwise finite relative-error guarantee

`1-F(C_m(rho),rho^(tensor m)) <= K_m [1-F(B(rho),rho^(tensor 2))]`

on a model containing P_0 and arbitrarily small P_(t,phi). Evaluating at P_0 forces

`C_m(P_0)=P_0^(tensor m)=:Q_0`.

The map C_m may depend on B and m, may use B repeatedly, and may employ arbitrary ancillas and computation. The zero-error anchor is the operative requirement.

## 2. Classical channels kill tangent coherence at a pure anchor

**Lemma.** If an entanglement-breaking channel C sends a pure input `|0><0|` to a pure output Q, then `C(|0><v|)=C(|v><0|)=0` for every v orthogonal to 0.

Write a finite measure-and-prepare decomposition `C(X)=sum_y Tr(M_y X) sigma_y`; such a decomposition exists in finite dimensions. Since `sum_y <0|M_y|0> sigma_y=Q` is pure, every y with positive `<0|M_y|0>` has `sigma_y=Q`. For the remaining y, positivity implies `M_y|0>=0`. Therefore

`C(|0><v|)=Q sum_(active y)<v|M_y|0> = Q <v|I|0> =0`.

The proof does not require the output states sigma_y to be pure or product, nor the effects M_y to have rank one.

Apply this lemma to C_m. With `sigma_1=C_m(|1><1|)`,

`C_m(P_(t,phi))=(1-t)Q_0+t sigma_1`.

As the target P_(t,phi)^(tensor m) is pure,

`F(C_m(P_(t,phi)), P_(t,phi)^(tensor m))`

`= (1-t)^(m+1)+t Tr[sigma_1 P_(t,phi)^(tensor m)]`

`<= (1-t)^(m+1)+t`.

Thus every EB conversion obeys the exact nonasymptotic bound

`e_Cm(t,phi) >= (1-t)[1-(1-t)^m]`.  (1)

For fixed m, the right side is `m t+O_m(t^2)`. Dividing by the exact supplied doubling error and letting t decrease to zero proves

`K_m >= alpha m = (3+2sqrt(2))m`.  (2)

This lower bound applies in particular to a proper estimator that measures once and prepares a fixed state sigma_y to the mth tensor power. It also applies to the larger class of EB output channels that may prepare arbitrary entangled m-partite states. Hence the linear dependence on m in the replication compiler cannot be improved to o(m).

The coefficient in this local anchored obstruction is attained by the constant replacer `C_m(X)=Tr(X) Q_0`: its loss is `1-(1-t)^m=m t+O_m(t^2)`. This is sharp for the local example, and is not an assertion that alpha m is the optimal universal upper constant.

## 3. A stronger global fast-forward obstruction for every channel

The zero-error anchor alone, without an EB assumption, gives

`D(C_m(P_(t,phi)),Q_0) <= D(P_(t,phi),P_0) = sqrt(t)`

by trace-distance contractivity. Testing against the pure target yields

`|F(C_m(P_(t,phi)),P_(t,phi)^(tensor m))-(1-t)^m| <= sqrt(t)`.  (3)

This holds uniformly over m, phi, and all CPTP C_m fixing the anchor. In particular, along any sequence t->0 and m=m(t) with `m t -> s` in [0,infinity],

`e_Cm(t,phi) -> 1-exp(-s)`.

If `m t -> infinity`, the error tends to one. Rewriting in terms of the *actual supplied doubling error* epsilon(t), whenever `m epsilon(t)->lambda` in [0,infinity],

`e_Cm(t,phi) -> 1-exp(-alpha lambda)`.  (4)

For `m epsilon(t)->infinity`, the error tends to one even though epsilon(t)->0. Thus no all-state pointwise converter can retain a fixed small *global product* error while producing asymptotically more than order 1/epsilon copies. This limitation holds even if the converter remains quantum.

The exact zero-error anchor is essential to this version. A guarantee only on a finite-prior *average* error need not fix the center exactly; do not silently apply (3)-(4) to that weaker interface.

For fixed m, Bures angle gives a finer quantum local bound. Put `gamma=asin(sqrt(t))` and `Phi_m=acos((1-t)^(m/2))`. Contractivity and triangle imply

`e_Cm(t,phi) >= sin^2(max{0,Phi_m-gamma})`

`= (sqrt(m)-1)^2 t+o_m(t)`.

Therefore arbitrary quantum converters satisfying the anchored pointwise guarantee require

`K_m >= alpha (sqrt(m)-1)^2`.  (5)

This fixed-m coefficient is attained locally by the W-state isometry

`W_m|0>=|0>^(tensor m)`,

`W_m|1>=(1/sqrt(m)) sum_(j=1)^m |0...010...0>`.

Indeed its exact target fidelity is

`(1-t)^(m-1)[1+(sqrt(m)-1)t]^2`,

whose loss has the stated coefficient and whose joint scaling also agrees with (4). The distinction between (2) and (5) isolates coherent tangent transmission as the operation that classical measurement removes.

## 4. Independent balanced binary reconstruction without an anchor

This section gives a lower bound even when the only promised model is a known pair and the criterion is average error. Let two input pure states have overlap c=cos(theta), theta in (0,pi/2). Their m-copy targets have overlap c^m and Bures angle `phi_m=acos(c^m)`. For arbitrary output channel C, put `e_i=1-F(C(P_i),P_i^(tensor m))` and `a_i=asin(sqrt(e_i))`.

Bures triangle and contractivity give `a_0+a_1>=Delta_m:=phi_m-theta`. Since Delta_m is in [0,pi/2],

`(e_0+e_1)/2 >= sin^2(Delta_m/2)`.

To see the last step, at fixed sum a_0+a_1<=pi/2 use

`sin^2 a_0+sin^2 a_1 = 1-cos(a_0+a_1)cos(a_0-a_1)`;

the minimum occurs at equality a_0=a_1=Delta_m/2. If the angle sum exceeds pi/2, the average error is at least 1/2 and the claim is automatic. A centered isometry of the two-dimensional input span into the target span attains equality for both labels. Thus the balanced average and minimax optima coincide exactly:

`e_m^opt = sin^2([acos(c^m)-acos(c)]/2)`.

Taking the supplied B to be the optimal doubler for this pair gives

`lim_(theta->0) e_m^opt/e_2^opt = [(sqrt(m)-1)/(sqrt(2)-1)]^2`.

Unlike Section 1, this comparison allows the supplied doubler to vary with theta; Section 1 removes that dependence for the pointwise theorem.

## 5. Exact EB binary global score

For the same balanced input pair with overlap c and arbitrary target pair with overlap d, the optimum EB global fidelity is

`F_EB=(1+sqrt(1-c^2(1-d^2)))/2`.  (6)

Here d=c^m for m-copy targets. This proof needs no discrimination or accessible-fidelity theorem as a black box. Choose real bases in the input and target spans so the input Bloch vectors are `(+-sqrt(1-c^2),0,c)` and target Bloch vectors are `(+-sqrt(1-d^2),0,d)`. Write a POVM effect as `M_y=w_y(I+v_y.sigma)`, where `sum w_y=1`, `sum w_y v_y=0`, and `||v_y||<=1`.

Optimizing the prepared output for each outcome gives fidelity

`F=(1+sum_y w_y sqrt[d^2(1+c v_(y,z))^2+(1-c^2)(1-d^2)v_(y,x)^2])/2`.

Let `A=sqrt[d^2+(1-c^2)(1-d^2)]`. The pointwise inequality

`sqrt[d^2(1+c z)^2+(1-c^2)(1-d^2)x^2] <= A+(d^2 c/A)z`

follows from `x^2<=1-z^2`: after squaring, the difference is at least `(1-c^2)^2(1-d^2)z^2/A^2>=0`. The affine right side is nonnegative. Averaging removes z, proving (6). The two effects with `v=(+-1,0,0)` attain equality; the corresponding optimal prepared states are the top eigenvectors of the posterior target mixtures.

These prepared m-partite states need not be products when m>1. Formula (6) therefore optimizes a larger class than the proper-product compiler and supplies a valid lower bound for it. With d=c^m,

`e_EB,m = [1-sqrt(1-c^2(1-c^(2m)))]/2`.

For fixed m as theta->0, `e_EB,m=m theta^2/4+O_m(theta^4)`, whereas `e_2^opt=(sqrt(2)-1)^2 theta^2/4+O(theta^4)`. Hence the same necessary EB coefficient alpha m also holds for balanced finite-prior models without invoking an anchor.

This is a reconstruction and extension of the familiar binary accessible-fidelity calculation, not a claim of a new general accessible-fidelity formula. The structural new use here is to certify the necessary m dependence of the supplied-doubler replication interface.
