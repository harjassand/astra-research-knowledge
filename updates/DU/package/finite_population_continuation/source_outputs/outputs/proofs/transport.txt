# Candidate theorem: sharp moment-dependent stability from a log-concave source

Status: internally derived candidate, awaiting independent reconstruction and full prior-art review. No claim of external validation or originality. This note changes no source repository file.

Pinned source: `work/repos/openai-math`, commit `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`. Source family 374, `preprints/Sharp-One-Third-Stability-of-Brenier-Maps-September-25-2026/build/source/sections/{cells,moments,finite_stability,gradient,sharpness}.tex`. The source proves the compact-target uniform-source case. The operation extracted here is midpoint domination of the geometric mean of opposite cell masses; log-concavity transfers that operation to nonuniform sources. A weighted interpolation argument and a two-atom heavy-target family give a new candidate moment-dependent theorem.

## Exact statement

Let `d >= 2`, let `rho(x) dx` be a full-dimensional log-concave probability on `R^d` with compact convex support `K subset B(0,R)`, and put

\[
A_j=\int_{e_j^\perp}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in\mathbb R}\rho(z+te_j)\,dz,
\qquad A=\sum_{j=1}^d A_j.
\]

These constants are finite. There is no positive lower bound on the density. Let `p > 2`, `M < infinity`, let `mu` have the moment bound below, and let `nu` be an arbitrary Borel probability in `P_2(R^d)`:

\[
\int |y|^p\,d\mu(y)\le M.
\]

Write `T_mu,T_nu` for the quadratic optimal maps from `rho dx`, unique `rho dx` almost everywhere. Set

\[
\beta_p=\frac{p-2}{3p-2},\qquad c_P=1+\sqrt{162}.
\]

Candidate upper theorem:

\[
\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le W_2(\mu,\nu)+C W_2(\mu,\nu)^{\beta_p},
\]

where one valid numerical choice is

\[
C=\max\left\{\sqrt{12d c_P^2R^2+128A+40dM+80d},\;2M^{1/p}\right\}.
\]

The formula uses the numerical split `W_2 <= 1` versus `W_2 > 1`; its constants therefore depend on the displayed coordinate/length convention. It is not an affine-invariant normalization. The exponent is independent of `d`; the constant is not.

For the fixed uniform source on `[-1,1]^d`, no larger small-distance exponent can hold uniformly, even if both targets have a fixed bound on their `p`th moments. Two atoms suffice for the lower bound. Either target may be the one with the moment bound. Section 9 proves this one-sided strengthening; Sections 1–6 first prove a two-sided precursor with the smaller constant `max{sqrt(12d c_P^2R^2+32A+16dM),2M^(1/p)}` and no linear term.

The bounded-target endpoint has exponent `1/3`: if both target supports lie in `B(0,L_0)`, the tail term below vanishes and

\[
\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le
\sqrt{12d c_P^2R^2+32L_0^2 A}\, W_2(\mu,\nu)^{1/3}.
\]

This endpoint is also sharp on the fixed cube with two-atom targets.

The second-moment endpoint has a different behavior: on a class with only a uniform second-moment bound, the map embedding need not be uniformly continuous at all. The two-atom family below has `W_2 -> 0` but map distance bounded away from zero when `L=a^(-1/2)`.

## Imported premises versus newly derived steps

Imported classical premises: existence and almost-everywhere uniqueness of the quadratic Brenier map from an absolutely continuous `P_2` source; stability/compactness of optimal plans under `W_2` convergence; preservation of log-concavity under convolution. The finite Laguerre-cell calculus is reconstructed below by the same surface-integral proof as source 374, with `rho` inserted in every face integral.

New candidate steps in this note: weighted midpoint domination; source-374 mass-to-moment transfer for log-concave density; interpolation toward slice modes with an `L^p` gradient budget; sharp two-atom family and the exponent `beta_p`.

No computation, sampling, or oracle is asserted by this theorem. The density and target laws are supplied mathematical objects. Acquiring a density, integrating face weights, solving the cell system, computing a coupling, and approximating a map all remain separate computational costs. No bit-complexity bound follows from the condition-free norm estimate.

## 1. Weighted midpoint coupling

First assume `rho` is smooth, supported on a compact convex body, and positive in its interior. Let `U,V` be compact convex subsets of positive source masses `alpha,beta`. There is a coupling `X ~ rho 1_U/alpha`, `Z ~ rho 1_V/beta` such that, with `H=(X+Z)/2`,

\[
\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\operatorname{Law}(H)\le \rho(x)\,dx.
\tag{MC}
\]

If all pointwise midpoints of `U,V` lie in a set `P`, the right side can be replaced by `rho 1_P dx`.

Proof. Use the same sequential conditional-quantile parameter `s in (0,1)^d` for both conditional densities. Their triangular maps have positive diagonal derivatives satisfying

\[
\prod_j\partial_{s_j}X_j=\alpha/\rho(X),\qquad
\prod_j\partial_{s_j}Z_j=\beta/\rho(Z)
\]

almost everywhere. (The successive conditional density ratios telescope.) The midpoint map is triangular and strictly increasing in each new coordinate. Arithmetic-geometric mean and log-concavity give

\[
\prod_j\partial_{s_j}H_j
\ge\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{\rho(X)\rho(Z)}}
\ge\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}{\rho(H)}.
\]

For a nonnegative Borel test `g`, successive one-dimensional substitutions therefore give

\[
\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,\mathbb E g(H)
\le\int g(H(s))\rho(H(s))\prod_j\partial_{s_j}H_j\,ds
\le\int g(x)\rho(x)\,dx.
\]

No differentiability in earlier quantile parameters is used. For smooth density on convex supports, conditional fiber integrals are continuous on their positive interiors, so the single-coordinate substitutions have the same justification as in source `moments.tex`. Boundary/tie sets have zero Lebesgue and source measure. This proves (MC).

## 2. Log-concave mass-to-moment differential

Fix distinct sites `y_i` and intercepts `c_i`, with all Laguerre cells

\[
P_i=K\cap\bigcap_{j\ne i}\{x:x\cdot(y_i-y_j)+c_i-c_j\ge0\}
\]

of positive source masses `M_i`. Put

\[
M_i=\int_{P_i}\rho(x)dx,\qquad B_i=\int_{P_i}x\rho(x)dx.
\]

For an intercept direction `q`, the candidate differential estimate is

\[
\sum_i\frac{|DB_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}
\le162R^2\sum_i\frac{|DM_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}.
\tag{MM}
\]

Proof in detail. Let `P_i^-`, `P_i^+` be the cells at `c-sq,c+sq`, write their masses `alpha_i,beta_i`, and set `gamma_i=sqrt(alpha_i beta_i)`. Couple their conditional source laws by (MC), writing `X_i,Z_i` and `D_i=E|X_i-Z_i|^2`. The averaged cell inequalities cancel `+-s(q_i-q_j)`, so `H_i=(X_i+Z_i)/2 in P_i`. Thus

\[
\gamma_i\operatorname{Law}(H_i)\le\rho 1_{P_i}dx.
\]

Use `Phi(x)=R^2-|x|^2 >=0` on the source support, and `A_i^-=E Phi(X_i)`, `A_i^+=E Phi(Z_i)`. The identity

\[
\Phi(H_i)=\tfrac12(\Phi(X_i)+\Phi(Z_i))+\tfrac14|X_i-Z_i|^2
\]

and all three source partitions give

\[
\frac14\mathcal D\le
\sum_i\left[
\left(\frac{\alpha_i+\beta_i}{2}-\gamma_i\right)
\frac{A_i^-+A_i^+}{2}
+\frac{\alpha_i-\beta_i}{4}(A_i^--A_i^+)\right],
\]

where

\[
\mathcal D=\sum_i\gamma_iD_i,\qquad
\mathcal B=\sum_i\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{\gamma_i}.
\]

Since `0 <= A_i^+- <= R^2`,

\[
\frac{\alpha_i+\beta_i}{2}-\gamma_i
=\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{2(\sqrt{\alpha_i}+\sqrt{\beta_i})^2}
\le\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2}{8\gamma_i},
\]

and `|A_i^- - A_i^+| <= 2R sqrt(D_i)`, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
\mathcal D/4\le R^2\mathcal B/8+(R/2)\sqrt{\mathcal D\mathcal B}.
\]

Using `(R/2)sqrt(DB) <= D/8+R^2 B/2` yields `D <= 5R^2 B`.

Writing `delta_i=alpha_i-beta_i`, the moment difference obeys

\[
|\Delta B_i|=|\alpha_i EX_i-\beta_i EZ_i|
\le R|\delta_i|+\alpha_i\sqrt{D_i}.
\]

For sufficiently small `s`, all `alpha_i,beta_i,gamma_i` lie in `[M_i/2,2M_i]`. With `E=sum delta_i^2/M_i`, we have `B <= 2E` and `alpha_i^2/M_i <=8gamma_i`. Hence

\[
\sum_i\frac{|\Delta B_i|^2}{M_i}
\le2R^2E+16\mathcal D
\le162R^2E.
\]

Divide by `(2s)^2` and let `s downarrow0`. The mass and moment surface derivatives exist, giving (MM). Smallness thresholds may depend on the full finite configuration and `q`; the differential constant does not.

## 3. Finite-target potential estimate

For smooth positive-interior `rho`, the cell differentials are source-374's formulas with

\[
g_{ij}=\frac1{|y_i-y_j|}\int_{F_{ij}}\rho\,dS,
\qquad b_{ij}=\frac1{|y_i-y_j|}\int_{F_{ij}}x\rho\,dS.
\]

The mass derivative in intercepts is the connected weighted graph Laplacian `G`; strict positivity in the source interior makes the geometric connectivity proof apply. The joint site/intercept/moment differentials are `C^1` by the same hyperplane substitution and dominated-convergence argument, now with a continuous source density factor. The convex coercive objective `int max_i(x.y_i+c_i)rho - sum m_i c_i` gives prescribed masses and the implicit-function theorem gives differentiable intercepts when the sites are distinct.

For two finite targets choose an optimal coupling `sum_i m_i delta_(y_i^0,y_i^1)`, deleting zero weights and merging repeated endpoint pairs. Let `y_i(t)=(1-t)y_i^0+t y_i^1`, `v_i=y_i^1-y_i^0`, `w=W_2(mu,nu)`. Optimality gives `(y_i^0-y_j^0).(y_i^1-y_j^1)>=0`, so interior sites are distinct and

\[
\sum_i m_i|v_i|^2=w^2.
\]

Choose intercepts with masses `m_i` and `sum m_i c_i(t)=0`; put `r_i=c_i'(t)`. The differentiated mass constraints and symmetry give, for every `q`,

\[
\sum_i r_i DM_i[q,0]=-\sum_i v_i\cdot DB_i[q,0].
\]

As `sum m_i r_i=0`, connectedness permits `q` with `DM_i[q,0]=m_i r_i`. Thus (MM) gives

\[
E_r:=\sum_i m_i r_i^2\le
w\left(\sum_i|DB_i[q,0]|^2/m_i\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt{162}Rw\sqrt{E_r}.
\]

The affine maximum `u_t(x)=max_i(x.y_i(t)+c_i(t))` has derivative `x.v_i+r_i` on active cell `i`; therefore

\[
\|\partial_tu_t\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le c_P Rw.
\]

Each intercept has finite endpoint limits, by `|r_i|<=sqrt(162)Rw/sqrt(m_i)`. The maxima converge uniformly at endpoints. Their gradient laws are the two endpoint targets; finite-max convex duality identifies them with the unique optimal maps. Consequently there are endpoint potentials `u,v` such that

\[
\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le c_P Rw.
\tag{P}
\]

This bound has no target-radius or target-moment constant. The calculation is the exact source proof after (MM), with the density factor in all face integrals. It does not bound the inverse cell Laplacian or the time/precision needed to solve it.

## 4. Weighted convex-gradient interpolation with only a pth-moment budget

Lemma. Suppose the density has unimodal one-dimensional slices along each coordinate and the slice peak integrals `A_j` above are finite. Let `u,v` be convex, finite in the source interior, with

\[
\max\{\int|\nabla u|^p\rho,\int|\nabla v|^p\rho\}\le M.
\]

Then for every `h>0` and cutoff `L>0`,

\[
\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2
\le \frac{12d}{h^2}\|u-v\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2
+32 A L^2h+16dM L^{-(p-2)}.
\tag{WI}
\]

The lemma does not require log-concavity beyond slice unimodality.

Proof. Fix a coordinate slice and let its unnormalized density be `r(t)`, supported on an interval, with a mode `m` and peak `S`. On `t<=m-h` take the forward difference, and on `t>=m+h` the backward difference. Both shifts go toward the mode, so `r(t)<=r(t+/-h)`. The two shifted image intervals have disjoint interiors. With `f=u-v`, this gives, after integrating over transverse coordinates,

\[
\int_{\rm allowed}|D_h f|^2\rho\le4h^{-2}\|f\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2.
\tag{Q}
\]

Call an allowed point bad if either coordinate derivative of `u` or `v`, at that point or its shifted endpoint, has absolute value greater than `L`. In the excluded strip `|t-m|<h`, call it bad only when a derivative at the point exceeds `L`. Density monotonicity and the injective shifts bound the full bad-set mass by `4M/L^p`. Also `int |partial_j f|^p rho <= 2^p M`. Hölder therefore bounds its squared-gradient contribution by

\[
(2^pM)^{2/p}(4M/L^p)^{1-2/p}
\le16M L^{-(p-2)}.
\tag{T}
\]

On the good allowed set, monotonicity of the convex derivative gives the finite-difference error `delta_u=|D_hu-partial_j u| <= |partial_j u(t+/-h)-partial_j u(t)| <=2L`, and the same for `v`. Let `g` be the derivative clipped to `[-L,L]`; it is monotone and has total variation at most `2L`. At good endpoints the derivative differences equal those of `g`. Fubini with its Stieltjes derivative gives

\[
\int_{t\le m-h}(g(t+h)-g(t))dt
+\int_{t\ge m+h}(g(t)-g(t-h))dt
\le h\,\operatorname{Var}(g)\le2Lh.
\]

The two terms use disjoint halves of the variation measure. Multiplication by `r(t)<=S` and `delta_u^2<=2L delta_u` shows, on this slice, `int_good delta_u^2 r <=4L^2hS`. Integrating slices gives `4L^2hA_j`; the same holds for `v`.

On the good part of the excluded strip, `|partial_j f|<=2L`, and strip mass is at most `2h A_j`, giving contribution `8L^2h A_j`. On the good allowed set, use `(a+b+c)^2<=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)` with (Q) and the two difference errors, obtaining `12F^2/h^2+24L^2hA_j`. Adding the strip and (T) gives

\[
\int|\partial_jf|^2\rho\le12F^2/h^2+32L^2hA_j+16M L^{-(p-2)}.
\]

Summing over coordinates proves (WI). Slice modes and derivatives can be taken measurably for smooth log-concave densities; the argument can alternatively be phrased fiberwise before the transverse integration. Null boundary/derivative sets do not contribute.

## 5. Optimize and remove smooth/finite restrictions

For finite targets and a smooth log-concave source, combine (P) and (WI). If `0<w<=1`, choose

\[
L=w^{-2/(3p-2)},\qquad h=w^{2p/(3p-2)}.
\]

All three terms have power `w^{2(p-2)/(3p-2)}`. This proves the stated upper bound. For `w>1`, use `||T_mu-T_nu||_2 <=2M^{1/p}`; for `w=0`, uniqueness gives zero. For targets supported in `B(0,L_0)`, choose `L=L_0` without a bad set and `h=w^{2/3}`.

Remove finite-target restriction first. Quantize/truncate targets by finite Borel maps `Q_n(y)` tending to `y`, with `|Q_n(y)|<=|y|` (coordinate rounding toward zero followed by bounded coordinate truncation is one explicit choice). Their targets converge in `W_2` and retain the same pth-moment bound. Compactness of joint graph laws `(x,T_mu_n(x),T_nu_n(x))` and Brenier uniqueness identify every limiting pair with `(T_mu,T_nu)` over the same source. Uniform `p>2` moments make `|y-z|^2` uniformly integrable, so the squared-map norms converge and the estimate passes to the limit. For bounded targets, use quantizers staying in their enclosing ball, or first allow radius `L_0+epsilon_n` and let it decrease.

Remove source smoothness next. Convolve `rho` with a compactly supported smooth log-concave approximate identity `kappa_epsilon`. This stays log-concave, is smooth and positive on its interior support, has support in `B(0,R+epsilon)`, and converges in `W_2`. Moreover

\[
\int\sup_t(\rho*\kappa_\epsilon)(z+te_j)dz\le A_j
\]

by moving `sup` inside the convolution and integrating transverse coordinates. Apply the estimates to these smooth sources and the fixed targets. Joint graph-plan compactness, continuity of quadratic optimal costs and source-limit Brenier uniqueness again identify the limit. Target laws are fixed and have finite second moments, which is enough to give uniform integrability of `|y-z|^2`; pth moments are already available. Let `epsilon downarrow0` to retain `R` and `A`.

For completeness, a compactly supported full-dimensional log-concave density is bounded: choose a small interior ball on which it is bounded below; an unbounded sequence of density values, combined with that ball by midpoint log-concavity, would force arbitrarily large total integral on balls of fixed volume. Thus `A_j <= ||rho||_infinity |proj_(e_j^perp)K| < infinity`. The required positivity and continuity on the interior are standard elementary properties of a finite log-concave density.

## 6. Exact two-atom sharpness family

Fix source `rho=2^{-d}1_[-1,1]^d`, `0<a<1/4`, `L>0`, and set `b=aL`, `c=1-2a`. Define

\[
u_{a,L}(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-c)\},\qquad
v_{a,L}(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-c)+b x_2\}.
\]

Their active outlier cells are `A={x_1>c}` and `B={x_1>c-a x_2}`. Every slice boundary stays strictly between `-1` and `1`, since `0<a<1/4`. Averaging the uniform `x_2` coordinate gives `rho(A)=rho(B)=a`. Finite-max convex duality proves the gradients are the optimal maps to

\[
\mu_{a,L}=(1-a)\delta_0+a\delta_{Le_1},\qquad
\nu_{a,L}=(1-a)\delta_0+a\delta_{Le_1+be_2}.
\]

The coupling fixing zero and moving the other atom costs `ab^2`; every coupling has at least that cost from the second coordinate. Hence

\[
W_2(\mu_{a,L},\nu_{a,L})^2=ab^2=a^3L^2.
\tag{S1}
\]

On an `x_2=t` slice, symmetric-difference probability is `a|t|/2`; averaging gives `rho(A triangle B)=a/4`. The coordinate components of the map difference are orthogonal, so

\[
\|T_{\mu_{a,L}}-T_{\nu_{a,L}}\|_2^2
=L^2\rho(A\mathbin\triangle B)+b^2\rho(B)
=\frac{aL^2}{4}+a^3L^2.
\tag{S2}
\]

Now put `L=a^{-1/p}`. The pth moments are exactly `1` and `(1+a^2)^{p/2}`, so they are uniformly bounded, e.g. by `2^{p/2}`. Equations (S1)-(S2) become

\[
w=a^{(3p-2)/(2p)},\qquad
D=a^{(p-2)/(2p)}\sqrt{1/4+a^2}.
\]

For every `alpha>beta_p`, `D/w^alpha` tends to infinity. This proves sharpness of the exponent on this fixed source and moment class. If a target moment bound `M_0>0` is prescribed, multiply both target families by a fixed small constant; the same exponent obstruction remains. For compact targets set `L=1`, which gives `D~sqrt(a)/2` and `w=a^{3/2}` with only two atoms.

At `p=2`, set `L=a^(-1/2)`. The two second moments are `1` and `1+a^2`, `w=a`, and `D^2=1/4+a^2`. Therefore a second-moment bound alone admits no uniform modulus `omega(w)->0`. This does not contradict qualitative stability at each fixed target pair or under convergence of both targets to a common fixed `P_2` limit: the outlier target sequences have no `W_2`-convergent subsequence because their second moments do not converge to the second moment of their weak limit `delta_0`.

## 7. Equal-radius lower family and general moment budgets

The old family in Section 6 changes the outlier radius from `L` to `L sqrt(1+a^2)`. This is harmless for power moments, but need not be harmless for arbitrary increasing moment functions. Use instead

\[
q=L e_1,\qquad r=\frac{L(e_1+a e_2)}{\sqrt{1+a^2}},\qquad c=1-2a,
\]

\[
u(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-c)\},\qquad
v(x)=\max\left\{0,\frac{L(x_1-c+a x_2)}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right\}.
\]

The active cells remain `A={x_1>c}` and `B={x_1>c-a x_2}`, each of mass `a`, with symmetric-difference mass `a/4` and intersection mass `7a/8`. The target laws are `(1-a)delta_0+a delta_q` and `(1-a)delta_0+a delta_r`. Since `q dot r>0`, an alternative two-by-two coupling moving an amount `k` through zero adds `2k q dot r` to the diagonal coupling's cost. Hence diagonal matching is optimal and, exactly,

\[
w^2=2aL^2\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right),
\qquad
D^2=\frac{aL^2}{4}+\frac{7a}{8}\,2L^2\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right).
\tag{SR}
\]

For `0<a<1/4`, these imply `w asymp L a^(3/2)` and `D asymp L sqrt(a)` with absolute constants. Both nonzero atoms have radius exactly `L`. For a nonnegative increasing moment function `Phi` with `Phi(0)=0`, setting `a=1/Phi(L)` for sufficiently large `L` gives both moment integrals exactly one. No doubling assumption is needed for this lower family.

Root's synthesis of the upper interpolation inequality is as follows. Assume `Phi` is increasing, `Phi(t)/t^2` is nondecreasing on `(0,infinity)`, and both `Phi` moments are at most `M`. The bad set used in (WI) has source mass at most `4M/Phi(L)`. For either map, split its squared norm there at radius `L`; the part below `L` is at most `4M L^2/Phi(L)` and the part above at most `M L^2/Phi(L)`. Thus the two-map squared-difference contribution is at most `20M L^2/Phi(L)` per coordinate. The same good-set argument gives

\[
D^2\le \frac{12dF^2}{h^2}+32 A L^2h+20dM\frac{L^2}{\Phi(L)}.
\tag{WI-Phi}
\]

Combine with `F<=c_P Rw` and optimize `h` to obtain

\[
D^2\le C\inf_{L>0}\left\{w^{2/3}L^{4/3}+M\frac{L^2}{\Phi(L)}\right\}.
\tag{O}
\]

For a vanishing class-uniform modulus from this formula, assume in addition `Phi(L)/L^2 -> infinity`; this also supplies the uniform integrability needed for target approximation. A convex Young-function hypothesis is unnecessary for the argument. If `Phi(t)=t^p [log(e+t)]^r`, `p>=2`, `r>=0`, with `p>2` or `r>0`, the balanced scale and resulting map modulus are

\[
L\asymp w^{-2/(3p-2)}[\log(1/w)]^{-3r/(3p-2)},
\]

\[
D\lesssim w^{(p-2)/(3p-2)}[\log(1/w)]^{-2r/(3p-2)}
\quad (w\downarrow0).
\]

Equation (SR) with `a=1/Phi(L)` matches this order. At `p=2,r>0`, the order is `[log(1/w)]^(-r/2)`. This is a sharp target-perturbation candidate on the fixed cube, whereas Ruan's endpoint theorem is stated for objective-gap conditioning. Do not conflate their inputs.

## 8. Failure of a plain second-moment replacement in (MM)

The bounded-source proof cannot simply replace `R^2` by a source second moment. This proposed generalization is false even for a fixed standard Gaussian source. Let `rho=gamma_d`, sites `0,e_1`, intercepts `0,-t`; the cells are `P_1={x_1<=t}` and `P_2={x_1>t}`. Perturb the second intercept by `s`, so the dividing coordinate becomes `t-s`. Let `a=gamma_1((t,infinity))` and let `varphi` denote the standard Gaussian density. At `s=0`,

\[
DM_2=\varphi(t),\quad DM_1=-\varphi(t),\qquad
DB_2=t\varphi(t)e_1,\quad DB_1=-t\varphi(t)e_1.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{\sum_i|DB_i|^2/M_i}{\sum_i|DM_i|^2/M_i}=t^2\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

while every source moment is fixed and finite. No constant depending only on fixed Gaussian moments can make this exact form of (MM) uniform over all cells. This obstruction does not rule out a centered/cancellation-sensitive estimate, a tail-sensitive modification, or another argument for unbounded log-concave sources. Those require a different proof, and are not established here.

## 9. One target moment suffices (root's strengthening)

Define each target's quadratic tail by

\[
\tau_\eta(L)=\int_{|y|>L}|y|^2\,d\eta(y),\qquad L>0.
\]

The proof of (WI) has the following tail version, which does not require any moment above two:

\[
D^2\le \frac{12dF^2}{h^2}+32AL^2h+20d\max\{\tau_\mu(L),\tau_\nu(L)\}.
\tag{WI-tail}
\]

Indeed the bad set for coordinate `j` has mass at most `4 max(tau_mu(L),tau_nu(L))/L^2`, including shifted endpoints because the shift toward a mode does not decrease the density. Each unshifted derivative's squared integral over that bad set is at most `L^2` times this mass plus its own tail integral, hence at most `5 max(tau_mu(L),tau_nu(L))`. The squared difference is at most twice the sum for the two maps, giving `20 max(tau_mu(L),tau_nu(L))`. All good-set and shifted-difference steps are unchanged.

Let `(X,Y)` have an optimal coupling of `mu,nu` and let `w^2=E|X-Y|^2`. On `{|X|>L/2}`, `|Y|^2<=2|X|^2+2|Y-X|^2`; on `{|X|<=L/2,|Y|>L}`, `|Y|<=2|Y-X|`. The two events are disjoint, so

\[
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2.
\tag{tail-transfer}
\]

In particular the maximum of the two tails at `2L` is at most `2 tau_mu(L)+4w^2`. Combining (WI-tail), (P), and this inequality gives, for all `h,L>0`,

\[
D^2\le\frac{12d c_P^2R^2w^2}{h^2}+128AL^2h+40d\tau_\mu(L)+80dw^2.
\tag{one-sided}
\]

If only `int |y|^p dmu<=M`, `p>2`, then `tau_mu(L)<=M L^{-(p-2)}`. For `0<w<=1`, the choices of `L,h` in Section 5 give

\[
D\le\sqrt{12d c_P^2R^2+128A+40dM+80d}\,w^{\beta_p}.
\]

For `w>1`, the triangle inequality in `P_2` gives `||T_nu||_2<=||T_mu||_2+w`, so `D<=w+2M^(1/p)<=w+2M^(1/p)w^(beta_p)`. This proves the main one-sided statement and its displayed constant. The additive `w` term accounts for the arbitrarily distant second target and cannot in general be dropped at large distances. At small distances the sharp exponent remains `beta_p` by Section 6 or (SR).

Finite approximation remains valid with only one higher moment: choose norm-decreasing finite quantizers of `mu`, preserving its moment budget, and any `W_2`-convergent finite quantizers of the fixed `nu in P_2`. Convergence in `W_2` supplies uniform integrability of the squared target norms; no higher moment of `nu` is imported. Apply the one-sided inequality before passage to the limit. Source smoothing proceeds as before.

For a one-sided `Phi` moment bound under Section 7's ratio hypotheses, `tau_mu(L)<=M L^2/Phi(L)`. Optimizing `h` in (one-sided) yields

\[
D^2\le C\left[w^2+\inf_{L>0}\left\{w^{2/3}L^{4/3}+M\frac{L^2}{\Phi(L)}\right\}\right].
\tag{O-one-sided}
\]

For `Phi(t)=t^p[log(e+t)]^r`, this has the same sharp small-distance power-log modulus stated in Section 7. Only one target is constrained; the other is arbitrary `P_2`. Equal-radius (SR) proves sharpness even in the smaller class where both targets satisfy the moment budget. Thus the current candidate strengthens Wang-Wang's exponent on compact log-concave sources at the same one-sided target interface; its scope does not subsume their unbounded-source and weighted-reference classes.

## 10. Sharp variational modulus for arbitrary radial moment functions

Regular variation is unnecessary to make the profile (O-one-sided) sharp. Assume `M>0`, `Phi:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity)` is finite and nondecreasing, `Phi(0)=0`, `Phi(t)/t^2` is nondecreasing on `(0,infinity)`, and `Phi(t)/t^2 -> infinity`. Fix the cube source. Define the one-sided class modulus

\[
\Omega_{\Phi,M}(w)=\sup\left\{\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_2:
\int\Phi(|y|)d\mu\le M,\ \nu\in\mathcal P_2,\ W_2(\mu,\nu)\le w\right\}.
\]

Choose a fixed `L_0>0` so large that `M/Phi(L_0)<1/4`. Put

\[
Q(w)=\inf_{L\ge L_0}\left\{A_w(L)+B(L)\right\},\quad
A_w(L)=w^{2/3}L^{4/3},\quad B(L)=M L^2/\Phi(L).
\]

The upper bound (O-one-sided) gives `Omega(w)^2<=C(w^2+Q(w))`. Since `Q(w)>=A_w(L_0)`, the `w^2` term is absorbed for sufficiently small `w`. The theorem therefore gives `Omega(w)^2<=C' Q(w)`.

For the reverse inequality, use (SR) with any `L>=L_0` and

\[
a=\min\left\{\frac{M}{\Phi(L)},\left(\frac{w}{L}\right)^{2/3}\right\}.
\]

Then `0<a<1/4`, both `Phi` moments are at most `M`, and the exact formula implies `W_2^2<=a^3 L^2<=w^2`. Also `D^2>=aL^2/4`, so

\[
\Omega(w)^2\ge\frac14\sup_{L\ge L_0}\min\{A_w(L),B(L)\}.
\]

Write the supremum on the right as `S`. It is finite because `B` is nonincreasing. For small `w`, `A_w(L_0)<B(L_0)`, so `S>=A_w(L_0)`. For every `epsilon>0`, continuity and unboundedness of the increasing function `A_w` give an `L>=L_0` with `A_w(L)=S+epsilon`. Since `min(A_w(L),B(L))<=S` and `A_w(L)>S`, necessarily `B(L)<=S`. Thus `Q(w)<=2S+epsilon`; let `epsilon downarrow0`. We conclude

\[
\frac18 Q(w)\le\Omega_{\Phi,M}(w)^2\le C' Q(w)
\qquad(w\downarrow0).
\tag{sharp-profile}
\]

This proof permits jump discontinuities in `Phi`; neither doubling nor regular variation is required. The lower examples in fact satisfy the budget on both targets. Power-log asymptotics are a readable special case of this variational statement. A proposed arbitrary quadratic-tail envelope would require checking that its lower examples satisfy the envelope at every radius; the radial-moment class here has been checked exactly and avoids that additional issue.

Within the finite nondecreasing ratio class, this also gives the exact criterion for a vanishing uniform modulus: `Phi(L)/L^2 -> infinity` if and only if `Omega_Phi,M(w)->0`. Sufficiency follows by first fixing a large cutoff to make `B(L)` small, then taking `w` small. For necessity, if `c=sup_{L>0}Phi(L)/L^2<infinity`, choose a fixed `kappa>0` with `kappa c<=M` (any positive `kappa` if `c=0`) and let `a=kappa/L^2`. Equation (SR) has both moment budgets at most `M`, `D^2>=kappa/4`, and `W_2<=kappa^(3/2)/L^2 ->0`. Thus no vanishing modulus exists. This uses `M>0` and sufficiently large `L` so that `a<1/4`.
