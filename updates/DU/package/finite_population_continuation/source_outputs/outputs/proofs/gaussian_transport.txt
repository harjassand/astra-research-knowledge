# Full Gaussian source: sharp one-third stability with one higher moment

Status: newly derived complete written argument, internally reconstructed by root on 11 October 2026. Root independently checked the centered midpoint/adjoint/conditional covariance chain, the full-source finite-potential limit, quadratic-score interpolation, one-sided clipped transfer, and exact-mass Gaussian lower construction. This is written-proof reconstruction, not external validation or an originality claim. The weaker upper exponent `(p-2)/(3p-4)` in `gaussian_interpolation_refinement.md` remains valid but is superseded here. The arbitrary-convex-support theorem in `strong_logconcave_extension.md` retains its sharp exponent `(p-2)/(3p-2)`.

## Exact statement

Let `gamma_d` be the standard Gaussian probability on `R^d`, `d>=2`. Fix `p>2` and `M<infinity`. Assume only

\[
\int|y|^p d\mu(y)\le M,\qquad \nu\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d).
\]

For the unique quadratic optimal maps from `gamma_d`, put `w=W_2(mu,nu)`. Then the candidate theorem is

\[
\boxed{\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)}\le w+C_{d,p,M}w^{1/3}.}
\tag{Gaussian-p}
\]

The exponent `1/3` is sharp for this single fixed full-support Gaussian source, even when both targets have three atoms in one fixed compact set. Constants depend on `p` and are not asserted uniform as `p downarrow2`. In particular the theorem does not assert continuity on the entire uniformly second-moment-bounded class.

Let `q=p/(p-2)` and `m_q=(E|G|^q)^(1/q)` for a scalar standard Gaussian `G`. One admissible constant is

\[
C_{d,p,M}=\max\left\{2M^{1/p},\ 
\left[60d+20d\,m_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}
+40d(2^{p-1}M+4)\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\]

The constants use the numerical split `w<=1` versus `w>1` and thus fix a length convention. No algorithm or acquisition/precision bound is supplied.

## 1. Quadratic variation replaces linear variation

For all real `a<=b`,

\[
(b-a)^2\le2\{b|b|-a|a|\}.
\tag{square-increment}
\]

If the two numbers have one sign, the right side is at least `2(b-a)^2`; if they straddle zero, it is `2(a^2+b^2)>=(b-a)^2`. The function `s mapsto s|s|` is monotone. This inequality is the additional operation needed beyond the linear clipped-variation argument in the earlier Gaussian note.

For finite convex potentials `u,v`, define

\[
F=\|u-v\|_2,\quad D=\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_2,\quad
\tau(L)=\max_{z\in\{u,v\}}\int_{|\nabla z|>L}|\nabla z|^2d\gamma_d.
\]

For `0<h<=1`, shift each coordinate fiber by `S(t)=t+h` for `t<=0` and by `S(t)=t-h` for `t>0`. The scalar Gaussian density satisfies `r(t)<=c r(S(t))`, with `c=e^(1/2)<5/3`. The map has two injective branches and multiplicity at most two. Consequently

\[
\int|D_h(u-v)|^2d\gamma_d<10F^2/h^2.
\]

Use the same good/bad decomposition as the earlier Gaussian note: a point is bad when either coordinate derivative at the point or shifted endpoint has magnitude greater than `L`. The bad-set mass is below `9tau(L)/L^2`, and its contribution to the squared difference of coordinate derivatives is at most `40tau(L)`.

Write `g_{z,j}=clip(partial_j z,[-L,L])`, a monotone function on every coordinate fiber. At a good point convexity implies

\[
\delta_z:=|D_hz-\partial_jz|\le|g_{z,j}(S(t))-g_{z,j}(t)|.
\]

Apply (square-increment) to `g_{z,j}` and put `H_{z,j}=g_{z,j}|g_{z,j}|`. This is a bounded monotone function on each fiber. The elementary weighted Stieltjes estimate from the earlier note is

\[
\int|H(S(t))-H(t)|r(t)dt\le2ch\int r(t)dH(t).
\]

It follows by Tonelli: on the forward branch, the inner interval is `[s-h,s] intersect (-infinity,0]` and `r(t)<=cr(s)` there; the backward branch is analogous. Integration by parts has no boundary term, because `H` is bounded and `r` tends to zero at both infinities. Thus

\[
\int_{good}\delta_z^2d\gamma_d
\le4ch\int r(t)dH_{z,j}(t)d\gamma_{d-1}
=4ch E[X_jH_{z,j}(X)]
\le4ch\,m_q\|g_{z,j}\|_p^2.
\tag{quadratic-score}
\]

Hölder uses conjugate exponents `q=p/(p-2)` and `p/2`, and Gaussian moments are finite for every finite `q`. In particular, the estimate can use a uniform clipped `p`-moment bound even when the original target has no `p`-moment.

Using `(a+b+c)^2<=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)` on the difference quotient and two errors, `12c<20`, and summing coordinates gives

\[
D^2\le30dF^2/h^2
+20h\,m_q\sum_{j=1}^d
\{\|g_{u,j}\|_p^2+\|g_{v,j}\|_p^2\}
+40d\tau(L).
\tag{GWI-p}
\]

No compact-source boundary or excluded mode strip is present. It is essential that the weighted integration by parts has no boundary term. A slab source contributes a boundary term and therefore is consistent with the different sharp profile in the main strong-source theorem.

## 2. One-sided clipped moments and tails

Let `(Y,Z)` be any optimal target coupling, with `Y~mu`, `Z~nu`, and `E|Y-Z|^2=w^2`. For every `L>0`,

\[
E\min\{|Z|,L\}^p\le2^p E|Y|^p+4L^{p-2}w^2.
\tag{clipped-transfer}
\]

To prove this pointwise, when `|Z|<=2|Y|` use `min(|Z|,L)^p<=2^p|Y|^p`. Otherwise `|Y-Z|>=|Z|/2`, while `min(|Z|,L)^p<=L^{p-2}|Z|^2`; the second term bounds it. This transfer is about clipped target moments, and does not incorrectly assign a finite `p`-moment to `nu`.

Also the one-sided quadratic tail transfer already established in the main note is

\[
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2.
\]

For `0<w<=1`, choose

\[
L=w^{-2/(p-2)}.
\]

Then

\[
\|g_{u,j}\|_p^p\le M,\qquad
\|g_{v,j}\|_p^p\le2^pM+4,
\]

\[
\tau(L)\le(2^{p-1}M+4)w^2.
\tag{uniform-clipped-budgets}
\]

The coordinate clips are dominated by the radial clip, so the displayed bounds are valid for every coordinate. The constants are independent of `L` and `w`.

For finite targets, mean-normalize potentials and apply the companion strong-source proof with `kappa=1`, yielding `F<=sqrt(2)w`. Combining with (GWI-p) and (uniform-clipped-budgets) gives

\[
D^2\le60d w^2/h^2
+20d\,m_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}h
+40d(2^{p-1}M+4)w^2.
\]

Choose `h=w^(2/3)`. For `w<=1` the right side is bounded by the displayed constant squared times `w^(2/3)`. For `w>1`, the triangle estimate is `D<=w+2M^(1/p)`. Equality `w=0` is covered by uniqueness.

Finite norm-decreasing quantizers preserve the constrained target's `p`-moment budget. Approximate the other target arbitrarily in `W_2`. Stability and uniqueness of common-source optimal graph plans identify the limits, and `W_2` convergence makes both target squared norms uniformly integrable. Squared map distances converge. This proves the stated candidate theorem for all admitted targets.

## 3. Sharpness on the fixed full Gaussian source

This adapts the three-atom thin-strip mechanism of source 374, whose proof is for a cube. We give the required Gaussian exact-mass adjustment and dominating functions.

Let `X,Y` be independent scalar standard Gaussians, with the remaining coordinates independent as well. Write `phi_0=(2pi)^(-1/2)` and define

\[
H(s)=P(|X|<s)\quad(s>=0),\qquad H(s)=0\quad(s<0).
\]

The function is globally Lipschitz with constant `2phi_0`, and `H(s)/s ->2phi_0` as `s downarrow0`. Put `m_a=H(a)` and `b_a=a/2`.

For each small `a>0`, choose `C_a` uniquely so that

\[
E H(a(C_a+Y/2))=H(a).
\tag{exact-central-mass}
\]

The left side is continuous and strictly increasing in `C_a`, ranging from zero to one. For fixed `C`, division by `a` and dominated convergence gives

\[
a^{-1}E H(a(C+Y/2))\longrightarrow2\phi_0 E(C+Y/2)_+.
\]

A dominating function is `2phi_0(|C|+|Y|/2)`. Since `H(a)/a->2phi_0`, monotonic bracketing shows `C_a->C_*`, where `E(C_*+Y/2)_+=1`. In particular `C_a` is bounded. Set `c_a=aC_a`.

Use convex potentials

\[
u_a(x)=\max\{-x_1,x_1,a\},\qquad
v_a(x)=\max\{-x_1,x_1,c_a+b_ax_2\}.
\]

The central cells are `A_a={|X|<a}` and `B_a={|X|<(c_a+b_aY)_+}`. Their masses are exactly `m_a`. Symmetry in `X` makes each outer cell have mass `(1-m_a)/2`. Therefore the gradient laws are exactly

\[
\mu_a=\tfrac{1-m_a}2(\delta_{-e_1}+\delta_{e_1})+m_a\delta_0,
\]

\[
\nu_a=\tfrac{1-m_a}2(\delta_{-e_1}+\delta_{e_1})+m_a\delta_{b_ae_2}.
\]

Finite convex-max duality identifies the gradients as the unique optimal maps. The target coupling fixing the two outer atoms has squared cost `m_ab_a^2`. Every coupling has at least this cost from its second coordinate alone, so

\[
w_a^2=m_ab_a^2\asymp a^3.
\]

Fiberwise the two central intervals are nested. Hence

\[
\gamma_d(A_a\triangle B_a)
=E|H(a)-H(a(C_a+Y/2))|.
\]

After dividing by `a`, dominated convergence applies with bound `2phi_0(1+sup|C_a|+|Y|/2)`. It gives

\[
a^{-1}\gamma_d(A_a\triangle B_a)
\longrightarrow2\phi_0 E|1-(C_*+Y/2)_+|=:c_*>0.
\]

The limit is positive because the integrand equals one on the positive-probability event `Y<=-2C_*`. The two coordinate components of the map difference are orthogonal, giving the exact identity

\[
D_a^2=\gamma_d(A_a\triangle B_a)+m_ab_a^2\asymp a.
\]

Thus `D_a asymp w_a^(1/3)`. Both targets stay in the unit ball for small `a`, so they satisfy every `p`-moment bound `M=1`. For any prescribed `M>0`, rescale all slopes by a fixed positive factor at most `M^(1/p)`; the source cells are unchanged and the same sharp exponent follows. This proves sharpness in (Gaussian-p).

## 4. Scope and priority

Imported ingredients are Brenier uniqueness/stability, the companion centered strongly log-concave potential bound, elementary Gaussian integration by parts, and the source-374 three-atom mechanism. Newly derived operations here are the quadratic-score interpolation combined with a one-sided clipped-moment transfer and the Gaussian exact-mass lower adaptation. None is claimed original without a further primary-source comparison.

The directly checked one-sided `W_2` theorem of [Wang–Wang, arXiv:2610.09640v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09640v1) gives exponent `(p-2)/(4p-2)` for Gaussian sources as part of a much larger source class, and the bounded-target exponent `1/4`. It does not state this `1/3` conclusion. Its optimality is for its broader class of weighted BV references and uses disconnected supports. [Letrouit–Mérigot, arXiv:2411.04908v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04908v3), Theorem 1.4, treats compact targets and gives a weaker map power for full strongly log-concave sources. Exact wider priority remains open.

Additional primary receipt: [Chewi–Eichinger–Pooladian, arXiv:2606.23383v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23383v1), Section 2.3, distinguishes the general `1/3` lower obstruction from the available `1/4` upper estimate and proves its main stability results for Kim–Milman maps, not Brenier maps. Its cited lower obstruction is [Letrouit, Unstable optimal transport maps, DOI 10.5802/crmath.834](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/articles/10.5802/crmath.834/), published 18 May 2026; that construction uses a disconnected bounded open source, rather than a Gaussian or convex source. These sources do not certify novelty of the present upper theorem. No novelty is claimed for the general `1/3` obstruction itself.

## 5. General score-moment consequence for log-smooth strongly log-concave sources

The Gaussian proof extends to the full-support class

\[
\rho(x)=e^{-V(x)},\qquad V\in C^2(\mathbb R^d),\qquad
\kappa I\preceq D^2V(x)\preceq K I,
\quad0<\kappa\le K<\infty.
\tag{smooth-source}
\]

For a fixed `p>2`, set `q=p/(p-2)` and

\[
S_q=\sum_{j=1}^d\|\partial_jV\|_{L^q(\rho)}<\infty.
\]

Finiteness follows directly from Gaussian source tails and `|gradient V(x)|<=K|x-x_*|`, where `x_*` is the unique minimizer. The main centered-potential theorem gives `F<=sqrt(2/kappa)w`.

On each coordinate fiber there is a unique mode `m_j(z)`. Shift forward on `t<=m_j(z)` and backward on `t>m_j(z)`. For `0<h<=K^(-1/2)` the density distortion is at most `c=e^(1/2)`: away from a mode crossing, monotonicity gives distortion at most one; in a crossing, the upper second derivative bound gives `V(z,t+/-h)-V(z,t)<=Kh^2/2`. The fiber map again has two injective branches with multiplicity at most two. The same bound `rho(z,t)<=c rho(z,s)` holds for the intermediate points used in the Stieltjes proof: on one side of the mode it follows from monotonicity, and on the other side from distance at most `h` to the mode and the upper curvature bound.

The only change in (quadratic-score) is the integration by parts identity

\[
\int\rho\,d(g|g|)=\int(\partial_jV)\,g|g|\,d\rho
\le\|\partial_jV\|_q\|g\|_p^2.
\]

There are no boundary terms: the fibers have full real-line support, `g|g|` is bounded, and the density has Gaussian decay. The derivative can be a Stieltjes measure, so no smoothness of the finite convex potential is needed. The resulting interpolation inequality is

\[
D^2\le30dF^2/h^2+
20h\sum_j\|\partial_jV\|_q
\{\|g_{u,j}\|_p^2+\|g_{v,j}\|_p^2\}+40d\tau(L).
\tag{score-WI}
\]

The one-sided clipped-target and tail transfers in Section 2 are unchanged. Choose `L=w^(-2/(p-2))` and `h=K^(-1/2)w^(2/3)` for `0<w<=1`. Then

\[
D^2\le C_*^2 w^{2/3},
\]

\[
C_*^2=60dK/\kappa+
20K^{-1/2}S_q\{M^{2/p}+(2^pM+4)^{2/p}\}
+40d(2^{p-1}M+4).
\]

The same target approximation yields `D<=w+max{C_*,2M^(1/p)}w^(1/3)` for every admitted target pair. Sharpness over this source class follows from its Gaussian member. A claim that every fixed source in the class has the same sharp lower exponent would require a separate lower construction; it is not needed here and not asserted.

This is a general score-moment theorem with explicit sufficient source conditions. The proof uses lower source curvature only through the centered potential estimate, upper coordinate curvature through controlled shifts across slice modes, and finite source score moments through quadratic variation. Merely assuming a Poincaré constant or a second moment would not establish these three independent inputs.
