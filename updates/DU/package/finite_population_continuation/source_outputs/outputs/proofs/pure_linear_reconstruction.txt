# A universal linear cloning-to-estimation infidelity bound

**Status, 11 October 2026:** complete internal proof candidate. The parent investigator and a separate worker have reconstructed the load-bearing steps without finding a gap; the separate reconstruction is saved in `../openai_miner_a/linear_cloning_estimation_reconstruction.md`. This is internal analytic evidence, not external certification, a historical-priority claim, a sharp-constant theorem, an unknown-state acquisition algorithm, or a historic-breakthrough claim.

## Theorem and exact resource interface

Let `A` be any finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space and let `E={(p_i,P_i)}_{i=1}^n` be any finite ensemble of pure states, with `P_i=|psi_i><psi_i|`, `p_i>=0`, and `sum_i p_i=1`. Define

`F_cl(E) = max_{B CPTP:A->A tensor A} sum_i p_i Tr[(P_i tensor P_i) B(P_i)]`,

`F_EB(E) = max_{Lambda EB:A->A} sum_i p_i Tr[P_i Lambda(P_i)]`.

The first score is **global two-copy product-target fidelity**. The second score is **single-copy measure-and-prepare fidelity**. Put `e_cl=1-F_cl` and `e_EB=1-F_EB`. Then

`e_EB <= min{1,2800 e_cl}`.

More strongly, for any specific supplied cloner `B`, with individual global losses `e_i=1-Tr[(P_i tensor P_i)B(P_i)]`, there exists an EB channel with average infidelity at most `2800 sum_i p_i e_i`.

The bound is independent of dimension, ensemble size, and smallest positive prior. The proof is existential from explicitly supplied mathematical states and channel. It does not give a dimension-independent algorithm for acquiring the states/channel or implementing the POVM. Finite-dimensional compactness supplies the optimization steps. Zero-prior labels may be deleted throughout.

## 1. A uniform class-measurement lemma reconstructed from the Schur argument

Fix a finite pure family and one cloner with every global infidelity at most `tau=1/2800`. Take a finite Stinespring isometry `V:A->A tensor A tensor E` and write

`u_i=V psi_i`, `q_i=1-e_i`,

`u_i=sqrt(q_i) v_i+w_i`, `v_i=psi_i tensor psi_i tensor f_i`,

where `f_i` is a unit environment vector, `w_i` is orthogonal to the entire subspace `psi_i tensor psi_i tensor E`, and `||w_i||^2=e_i`. In particular

`||u_i-v_i||^2=2(1-sqrt(1-e_i))<=2e_i`.

Write `C_ij=<psi_i,psi_j>`, `F_ij=<f_i,f_j>`, and `L=C circ F`. These are correlation matrices, and their Schur multipliers are UCP with cb norm one. Diagonal Stinespring isometries on the label space give

`||S_C-S_{C circ C circ F}||_cb <= 2 sup_i ||u_i-v_i|| <= delta`,

`delta=2 sqrt(2 tau)=sqrt(1/350)`.

Composing by `S_F` therefore gives

`||S_L-S_L^2||_cb<=delta`.

Here powers of a Schur multiplier are compositions, so its symbol is raised entrywise.

In the finite-dimensional unital Banach algebra of Schur multipliers, put

`Z=2S_L-I`, `D=4(S_L^2-S_L)`,

`R=(I+D)^(-1/2)`, defined by its convergent binomial series,

`S_Q=(I+ZR)/2`.

Because `Z^2=I+D` and the factors commute, `S_Q^2=S_Q`. Its symbol `Q` is Hermitian, has diagonal one, and has only zero/one entries. Also

`||S_Q-S_L||_cb <= eta = (3/2)((1-4delta)^(-1/2)-1)`.

The following rational bounds suffice and avoid reliance on numerical decimals:

`delta<=27/500`, `eta<=49/250`, `a:=delta+eta<=1/4`.

For the second bound, `1-4(27/500)=98/125`, and

`sqrt(125/98)<=424/375=1+(2/3)(49/250)`

because `125*375^2=17578125 <17618048=98*424^2`. The first bound follows by squaring `1/350<(27/500)^2`.

The ones of `Q` form an equivalence relation. Otherwise a three-by-three principal submask is

`[[1,1,0],[1,1,1],[0,1,1]]`.

Its action on the all-ones rank-one density matrix of dimension three has trace norm `(1+2sqrt(2))/3>5/4`. This lower bounds its cb norm by trace duality, whereas `||S_Q||_cb<=1+eta<5/4`. Contradiction. Thus `Q` is a class-partition mask and its Schur map is block pinching.

Since

`S_C-S_{C circ Q}=(S_C-S_{C circ L})+S_C(S_L-S_Q)`,

we obtain `||S_C-S_{C circ Q}||_cb<=a<=1/4`. Within each class, the entrywise bound `|L_ij-1|<=eta` implies

`|C_ij|>=1-eta>=201/250>3/4`.

For any prior `s` on this fixed family, define the weighted Gram density `G=diag(sqrt(s)) C diag(sqrt(s))`, and `G_0=G circ Q`. Trace duality for Schur maps gives

`||G-G_0||_1<=a`.

For clarity, the trace-class dual Schur symbol is the transpose/conjugate. Entrywise conjugation preserves cb norm, so the displayed estimate also holds for complex matrices.

Powers–Størmer gives `||sqrt(G)-sqrt(G_0)||_2^2<=a`. On positive-prior labels, the canonical vectors

`alpha_i=sqrt(G)|i>/sqrt(s_i)`, `beta_i=sqrt(G_0)|i>/sqrt(s_i)`

are unit, `alpha_i` have Gram matrix `C`, and `beta_i` lie in their own coordinate class. Pull the coordinate class projections back through the isometry `psi_i->alpha_i`. Extend to a POVM on all of `A` by assigning the orthogonal input complement to one class. The prior-average wrong-class probability is at most

`sum_i s_i ||alpha_i-beta_i||^2<=a`.

This works for every prior on the same fixed family and the same fixed partition. Finite minimax over the compact convex set of class POVMs gives one full-domain class POVM with every individual wrong-class probability at most

`a_0=1/4`.

This proves the uniform class-measurement lemma. No measurement of the physical Stinespring environment is required.

## 2. A weighted pair lemma inside a class

Let two good states have overlap `r=|<psi_i,psi_j>|>3/4`, and put `s=sqrt(1-r^2)` and `E_ij=e_i+e_j`. Preservation of the input overlap and the decomposition above imply

`r <= r^2 + (sqrt(e_i)+sqrt(e_j)) sqrt(1-r^4) + sqrt(e_i e_j)`.

Indeed the main target-target term has modulus at most `r^2`; the target-residual cross terms have modulus at most `sqrt(e_j) sqrt(1-r^4)` and `sqrt(e_i) sqrt(1-r^4)` because the residual is orthogonal to the complete corresponding target-output subspace; and the residual-residual term is at most `sqrt(e_i e_j)`.

Since `sqrt(1-r^4)=s sqrt(1+r^2)`, we get

`(3/7)s^2 <= (r/(1+r))s^2 <= 2 sqrt(E_ij) s + E_ij/2`.

If `E_ij=0`, then `s=0`. Otherwise put `x=s/sqrt(E_ij)`. The resulting inequality `(3/7)x^2<=2x+1/2` forces `x<5`: if `x>=5`, division by `x` would give `15/7<=3x/7<=2+1/(2x)<=21/10`, which is false. Therefore

`1-|<psi_i,psi_j>|^2 <=25(e_i+e_j)`.

Choose in each class a representative whose `e_i` is minimal. Its correct-class replacement loss obeys the pointwise estimate

`1-Tr[P_i P_rep(class(i))] <=25(e_i+e_rep(class(i))) <=50e_i`.

The last inequality uses `e_rep(class(i))<=e_i`. In particular its average is at most `50 sum_i p_i e_i` for every prior on the same good family.

## 3. The new bootstrap: constrained optimality plus three-copy majority

Keep the good family and its fixed class labels. For its conditional prior `p`, minimize average wrong-class probability

`r=sum_i p_i r_i`, `r_i=1-Tr[M_class(i) P_i]`,

over class POVMs `M=(M_c)` satisfying **every constraint** `r_i<=a_0=1/4`. This compact convex feasible set is nonempty by Section 1, so an optimizer exists.

Use the original cloner twice, splitting its second output on the second use. A Stinespring isometry is

`U_3=(I_A tensor V tensor I_E)V`,

with output ordering relabelled to `A tensor A tensor A tensor E tensor E`. Set `d_i=||V psi_i-psi_i tensor psi_i tensor f_i||`. Isometric propagation and the triangle inequality give

`||U_3 psi_i-psi_i tensor psi_i tensor psi_i tensor f_i tensor f_i||<=2d_i<=2sqrt(2e_i)`.

This is a finite physical one-to-three channel, using two calls to `B`.

Measure `M` independently on each of the three outputs. Return a class occurring at least twice if one exists; when all three outputs are different, return a fixed predetermined class. On the ideal product input `P_i^{tensor3}`, an incorrect majority decision requires at least two individual incorrect class labels. The independent-copy ideal error is therefore at most `3 r_i^2` (the exact at-least-two probability is `3r_i^2-2r_i^3`). This implication is valid for any number of classes and every deterministic tie rule.

Let `R_i` be the wrong-decision POVM effect on the three outputs, tensored with the environment identity. Taking its square root and applying the vector estimate gives the actual pulled-back wrong-class error

`r'_i <= (sqrt(3) r_i+sqrt(8e_i))^2`.

The resulting procedure is a POVM on the original input space. It is feasible for the constrained optimization: since `r_i<=1/4`, `e_i<=tau`, and `sqrt(8tau)=delta<1/16`,

`sqrt(3)r_i+sqrt(8e_i) <=sqrt(3)/4+delta <7/16+1/16=1/2`,

so `r'_i<1/4`. (The loose exact bound `sqrt(3)<7/4` suffices.)

Consequently optimality gives `r<=sum_i p_i r'_i`. Write `e=sum_i p_i e_i` for the conditional good-family average. Because `r_i^2<=a_0 r_i`, Cauchy–Schwarz yields

`r <=3a_0 r+8e+2sqrt(24a_0 r e)`

`  =(sqrt(3a_0 r)+sqrt(8e))^2`.

Taking square roots and rearranging, since `3a_0=3/4<1`, proves

`r <= [8/(1-sqrt(3)/2)^2] e =(224+128sqrt(3))e`.

This is the averaging step missing from a simple Markov-trimming argument. It keeps the individual `e_i` through an admissible decision-amplification operation. No independence assumption is made about the actual cloned outputs: independence is used only for the ideal product target, and the Stinespring bound transfers its test probability to the actual outputs.

## 4. Constructing one EB channel and charging bad labels

On the good-family optimized POVM outcome `c`, prepare the pure class representative `P_rep(c)`. This is one full-domain EB channel. For label `i`, its infidelity is at most its wrong-class probability plus the correct-class representative infidelity. Thus its conditional good-family average infidelity is at most

`(274+128sqrt(3)) e <500e`.

Now return to the original ensemble and delete as bad the labels with `e_i>tau=1/2800`. If all labels are bad, the claimed bound is trivial. Otherwise the preceding construction acts on the original full Hilbert space and can be used on all labels. Let

`E_good=sum_good p_i e_i`, `E_bad=sum_bad p_i e_i`.

The good contribution is at most `500E_good`, while the bad contribution is at most their prior mass, bounded by `2800E_bad`. Therefore total EB infidelity is at most

`500 E_good+2800 E_bad <=2800(E_good+E_bad)`.

Apply this to an optimal cloner to prove the theorem. The zero-error case is included: there are no bad positive-prior labels; the amplification inequality gives `r=0`, and the pair lemma gives zero within-class infidelity.

## 5. One EB surrogate for a fixed cloner, simultaneously on all pure states

Fix a finite-dimensional input space `A` and a single channel `B:A->A tensor A`. There is **one** EB channel `Lambda_B:A->A` such that for **every** pure state `P` on `A`,

`1-Tr[P Lambda_B(P)] <=2800(1-Tr[(P tensor P)B(P)])`.

The quantifier order is `for every finite A, for every B, there exists Lambda_B, for every pure P`. The surrogate depends on `B` and `A`, but not on a prior, a chosen finite ensemble, or the tested pure state.

Proof: on any finite list `P_1,...,P_n`, put `f_i(Lambda)=1-Tr[P_i Lambda(P_i)]-2800e_i`. The theorem for the supplied `B` says that for every prior `p` there exists an EB channel with `sum_i p_i f_i(Lambda)<=0`. Finite minimax applies because the finite-dimensional set of EB channels is compact and convex, and the payoff is affine in the channel and the prior. It yields one EB channel with `max_i f_i(Lambda)<=0` on the whole list. For each pure `P`, its required inequality defines a closed subset of this compact EB set. Every finite collection of these subsets has nonempty intersection by the preceding minimax argument. Compactness and the finite intersection property give a channel in their intersection over all pure states. This proves the claim.

Compactness of EB channels needs no infinite-dimensional assumption: their Choi matrices are bounded positive matrices with a fixed partial trace, and separable matrices form a closed set in finite dimension. The pointwise corollary is existential; no finite computational-cost claim for its continuum optimization is supplied.

A stronger representation theorem is proved separately in `global_surrogate_representation.md`: the same pointwise surrogate can be chosen to use at most `floor(8d/7)` POVM outcomes and pure prepared states. It extends the channel-defined good-state partition to all pure states, bounds its number of classes by a Gram-rank argument, and performs the constrained bootstrap on the same compact globally feasible POVM set. The computational cost of the resulting continuum optimization remains unknown.

## 6. Consequences and unresolved sharpness

1. The prior-average half-trace reconstruction error of the constructed EB channel is at most `min(1,sqrt(2800 e_cl))`, by the pure-target fidelity bound and Jensen.
2. The same linear infidelity estimate applies to any finite pure-state ensemble in any finite dimension. It is not a mixed-state or diamond-norm statement.
3. Combined with the separate standard Gram/Uhlmann lift in `gram_physical_lift.md`, a supplied abstract Gram residual `||G-G circ C circ H||_1<=epsilon` implies an EB channel with average infidelity at most `min(1,2800epsilon)` and average half-trace error at most `min(1,sqrt(2800epsilon))`.
4. A balanced binary ensemble forces any universal constant to be at least `3+2sqrt(2)`. The elementary lower-bound proof below needs only an explicit achievable cloner and exact optimal EB fidelity; it makes no optimal-cloner assertion. The present proof does not establish the sharp upper constant.
5. The bootstrap is an existential finite decision-amplification argument. It uses a constrained optimal POVM and supplied per-label errors, and supplies no unknown-state tomography, acquisition, experimental, or dimension-independent time bound.

## 7. A rigorous obstruction to constants below `3+2sqrt(2)`

Take the balanced qubit pair `psi_+=cos(theta/2)|0>+sin(theta/2)|1>` and `psi_-=cos(theta/2)|0>-sin(theta/2)|1>`, where `0<theta<pi/2`, `c=cos(theta)`, and `s=sin(theta)`. Its Bloch vectors are `(s,0,c)` and `(-s,0,c)`.

Write any qubit POVM effect as `M_y=t_y(I+m_y dot sigma)`, with `t_y>=0`, `|m_y|<=1`, `sum_y t_y=1`, and `sum_y t_y m_y=0`. A fixed measurement's best prepared state on outcome `y` is a top eigenvector of the unnormalized posterior operator

`A_y=(1/2)Tr[M_y P_+]P_+ +(1/2)Tr[M_y P_-]P_-`.

Direct Bloch calculation gives

`lambda_max(A_y)=(t_y/2)[1+c m_yz + sqrt(s^4 m_yx^2+c^2(1+c m_yz)^2)]`.

Consequently, after summing the affine part, its best EB fidelity is at most

`1/2+(1/2)sum_y t_y g(m_yz)`,

where, using `m_yx^2<=1-m_yz^2`,

`g(z)=sqrt(D+2c^3 z+(2c^2-1)z^2)`, `D=1-c^2+c^4`.

For `0<c<1`, the expression inside the square root is strictly positive on `[-1,1]`, and differentiation gives

`g''(z)=[(2c^2-1)D-c^6]/g(z)^3=-(1-c^2)^3/g(z)^3<=0`.

Jensen's inequality and `sum_y t_y m_yz=0` give the upper bound `(1+sqrt(D))/2`. The two-outcome `sigma_x` measurement, with each outcome's top-posterior eigenvector prepared, attains every step. Thus

`F_EB=(1+sqrt(1-c^2+c^4))/2`.

This familiar binary accessible-fidelity formula is also proved by Fuchs and Sasaki, Section 4.1, Eq. (73), in [arXiv:quant-ph/0302092](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302092v3).

For the cloning comparison let `t_+=psi_+ tensor psi_+`, `t_-=psi_- tensor psi_-`, and `phi=arccos(c^2)`. The two ideal target vectors have overlap `c^2`. The centered vectors

`T_0=(t_++t_-)/sqrt(2(1+c^2))`,

`T_1=(t_+-t_-)/sqrt(2(1-c^2))`

are orthonormal, and `t_+=cos(phi/2)T_0+sin(phi/2)T_1`, `t_-=cos(phi/2)T_0-sin(phi/2)T_1`. The isometry `U|0>=T_0`, `U|1>=T_1` is an explicit physical cloner with the same global fidelity on both labels:

`F_U=cos^2((phi-theta)/2)`.

Therefore

`e_cl<=e_U=sin^2((phi-theta)/2)`

`     =[1-c^3-(1-c^2)sqrt(1+c^2)]/2`.

As `theta` tends to zero, `phi/theta` tends to `sqrt(2)`, `e_EB=theta^2/4+o(theta^2)`, and `e_U=((sqrt(2)-1)^2/4)theta^2+o(theta^2)`. If a universal inequality `e_EB<=K e_cl` held, then `e_EB<=K e_U`; taking the ratio limit forces

`K>=1/(sqrt(2)-1)^2=3+2sqrt(2)`.

No claim that `U` is the optimal cloner is required for this lower bound.
