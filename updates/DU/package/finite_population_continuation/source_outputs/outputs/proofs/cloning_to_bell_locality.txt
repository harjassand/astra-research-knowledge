# Quantitative pure-state cloning implies one universal parent measurement

Independent consequence derivation by the cross-corpus worker, 11 October 2026.
Status: written proof under the operational hypotheses below, using the independently reconstructed replication-rigidity theorem. No historical originality, specialist validation, or efficient acquisition claim is made.

## 1. Exact input theorem and hypotheses

Use a finite-dimensional operational system with compact convex normalized state space `Omega`, spanning a real vector space `V`, `D=dim V`. Allowed measurements separate states; each fixed finite-outcome measurement set is compact and convex, and measurements are closed under classical processing. Composites permit product states and measurements, discarding, fixed-state preparation, physical transformations on arbitrary correlated spectators, and classical outcomes controlling preparations. Pullbacks of allowed measurements are allowed.

Define

\[
 r(x,y)=\inf_N\sum_a\sqrt{N_a(x)N_a(y)},\quad
 F(x,y)=r(x,y)^2,\quad
 d(x,y)=\sup_N\operatorname{TV}(Nx,Ny).
\]

For a supplied physical `B:A -> AA`, its defect is the **joint independent-product** defect

\[
 e_B(x)=1-F(Bx,x^{\otimes2}).
\]

The input theorem, independently reconstructed in `GPT_REPLICATION_RIGIDITY.md`, gives for every nonempty compact model `S` one allowed measurement `E` and representatives `s_c in S`, with at most `D` outputs, such that

\[
 \sum_cE_c(x)[1-F(x,s_c)]\le2800e_B(x),\quad x\in S.
 \tag{1}
\]

More precisely, on `e_B<=tau=1/2800`,

\[
 \Pr[E(x)\ne c(x)]\le C_*e_B(x),\qquad
 1-F(x,s_{c(x)})\le50e_B(x),
 \quad C_*={8\over(1-\sqrt{3/200})^2}<11.
 \tag{2}
\]

The good classes and `E` are common to the whole model. Their representatives are affine independent: the response matrix of a weak class measurement is strictly column diagonally dominant.

## 2. From uniform pure-state cloning to all-state classicalization

Assume

\[
 \sup_{x\in\operatorname{Ext}\Omega}e_B(x)\le\epsilon\le\tau.
 \tag{3}
\]

Let `S=closure(Ext Omega)`, a compact model. Continuity of operational fidelity extends (3) to `S`. Apply (2), and set

\[
 T(x)=\sum_cE_c(x)s_c.
\]

Squared fidelity is separately concave; hence for `x in S`,

\[
 F(x,Tx)\ge\sum_cE_c(x)F(x,s_c)
 \ge1-(C_*+50)\epsilon>1-61\epsilon.
 \tag{4}
\]

The extension to arbitrary mixed states uses **joint concavity of root fidelity**, not joint concavity of squared fidelity. For completeness, the classical affinity `sum_a sqrt(p_a q_a)` is jointly concave. Infimizing the resulting concave functions over measurements remains concave, so

\[
 r\left(\sum_ip_ix_i,\sum_ip_iy_i\right)
 \ge\sum_ip_ir(x_i,y_i).
\]

Every `x in Omega` is a finite convex combination `x=sum_i p_i x_i` of extreme states. Since `T` is affine, (4) gives

\[
 r(x,Tx)\ge\sum_ip_ir(x_i,Tx_i)\ge\sqrt{1-61\epsilon}.
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{F(x,Tx)\ge1-61\epsilon,\qquad
 d(x,Tx)\le\delta:=\sqrt{61\epsilon}\quad(x\in\Omega).}
 \tag{5}
\]

The image of `T` lies in the inscribed simplex `Delta=conv{s_1,...,s_k}`, `k<=D`; therefore `Omega` is within operational Hausdorff distance `delta` of `Delta`.

If desired, the vertices can be selected from the actual extreme states, even when `Ext Omega` is not closed. Every class is relatively open in `S`, so it contains an extreme state. Choose one such vertex per class. The uniform within-class bound remains `50 epsilon`, the same class measurement `E` still works, and the response matrix again proves affine independence. Minimum-defect representatives are necessary for the general pointwise bound (1), but not for this uniform consequence.

## 3. One parent measurement approximates every allowed measurement

For every allowed measurement `N=(N_a)`, define its classical postprocessing of `E` by

\[
 N'_a=\sum_cN_a(s_c)E_c.
 \tag{6}
\]

The coefficients form a probability kernel. Equation (5) gives the uniform statement

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{x\in\Omega}\operatorname{TV}(Nx,N'x)\le\delta.}
 \tag{7}
\]

The **same** finite parent `E` works for every allowed measurement, independently of its outcome number and of the number of measurement choices. Given any finite list of measurements, their approximations are jointly measurable: after measuring `E`, sample all requested outputs independently conditional on its outcome. The kernel in (6) depends only on the evaluated effect and the representative, so it respects effect equivalence and coarse-graining.

This is stronger than constructing a separate approximate joint measurement for each fixed pair of observables. No all-positive-effects or no-restriction assumption is used.

## 4. The conditional-state bridge to Bell locality

For this section add the usual causal conditional-state closure: applying an allowed local outcome effect to an allowed joint state produces a subnormalized allowed state on the other system, and positive-probability normalization is allowed. This property must be explicit for restricted-effect theories; product-effect positivity alone does not replace it.

Let `z` be any allowed state of `AB`. Alice uses measurements `N^x=(N^x_a)` and Bob arbitrary measurements `Q^y=(Q^y_b)`. Write

\[
 p(a,b\mid x,y)=(N^x_a\otimes Q^y_b)(z).
\]

Assume only Alice's system satisfies (5)--(7). Define

\[
 q(a,b\mid x,y)=\sum_cN^x_a(s_c)(E_c\otimes Q^y_b)(z).
 \tag{8}
\]

For fixed Bob setting and outcome, let

\[
 p_{b\mid y}=(u_A\otimes Q^y_b)(z),\qquad
 x_{b\mid y}={(\mathrm{id}_A\otimes Q^y_b)(z)\over p_{b\mid y}}
\]

when the denominator is positive. The conditional state belongs to `Omega_A`. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{TV}(p(\cdot,\cdot\mid x,y),q(\cdot,\cdot\mid x,y))
 &=\sum_{b:p_{b\mid y}>0}p_{b\mid y}
       \operatorname{TV}(N^x x_{b\mid y},N^xT x_{b\mid y})\\
 &\le\delta_A\sum_bp_{b\mid y}=\delta_A.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]

Crucially, `q` is already Bell local after classicalizing **one side only**. Put

\[
 \pi_c=(E_c\otimes u_B)(z),\qquad
 A(a\mid x,c)=N^x_a(s_c),\qquad
 B(b\mid y,c)={(E_c\otimes Q^y_b)(z)\over\pi_c}
\]

for positive `pi_c`; choose arbitrary Bob responses for zero-weight labels. These are probability kernels, and `pi` does not depend on either measurement setting. Then

\[
 q(a,b\mid x,y)=\sum_c\pi_cA(a\mid x,c)B(b\mid y,c).
 \tag{10}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{x,y}\operatorname{TV}(p(\cdot,\cdot\mid x,y),
 q(\cdot,\cdot\mid x,y))\le\sqrt{61\epsilon_A},
 \quad q\text{ Bell local},\quad |\operatorname{supp}\pi|\le D_A.}
 \tag{11}
\]

Bob need not admit a cloner. If both systems do, use the smaller of their two bounds. The result is uniform over the joint state, the numbers of settings and outcomes, and the chosen local measurements. It bounds the statistics of product measurements on arbitrary shared states. It does not claim a diamond norm or closeness under unrestricted collective measurements on `AB`.

### Multipartite form

For `r` parties, replace the measurements on any `r-1` parties by their parent-measurement postprocessings. The conditional-state argument (9), applied at each hybrid step while holding every other local outcome fixed, bounds the total variation change by the sum of those `r-1` local `delta` values. The final behavior is fully local: the hidden variable is the joint list of parent outcomes, and the unreplaced last party responds according to its conditional state.

If all `r` systems satisfy (5), the best stated bound is

\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm TV}(p,\mathrm{Local})
 \le\sum_{j=1}^r\delta_j-\max_j\delta_j.
 \tag{12}
\]

For common defect `epsilon`, this is `(r-1)sqrt(61 epsilon)`.

## 5. An inverse theorem from a Bell game

Let a Bell game have a setting distribution fixed independently of the state, and payoff in `[0,1]`. Suppose a behavior beats its optimal local payoff by `v>0`. Total variation changes such a payoff by at most the total variation distance, so (11) yields

\[
 v\le\sqrt{61\epsilon_A}
 \qquad\text{whenever }\epsilon_A\le1/2800.
\]

Without a small-defect promise, the valid global lower bound is

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{x\in\operatorname{Ext}\Omega_A}e_B(x)
 \ge\min\left\{\frac1{2800},\frac{v^2}{61}\right\}}
 \tag{13}
\]

for every candidate local product cloner `B`. The minimum is essential because the rigidity proof uses the fixed small-defect threshold.

For a CHSH value `S>2`, with the usual uniform-input binary game normalization, `v=(S-2)/8`; hence the right-hand side becomes `min{1/2800,(S-2)^2/(64*61)}`. The significance is a dimension- and setting-independent structural obstruction, not a competitive numerical cloning constant.

## 6. Resources and predecessor scope

The operation consumes a supplied physical cloner and produces the existence of one parent measurement and at most `D` supplied-model representatives. Its proof uses finite-dimensional optimization and compact minimax. It does not acquire unknown measurements, give an efficient algorithm for that optimization, or promise a small description of an arbitrary GPT effect. The Bell hidden variable has at most `D_A` values in (11).

Exact generic cloning/discrimination and classicality precede this argument: [Barnum--Barrett--Leifer--Wilce](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611295), Theorem 1; [Kimura--Nuida--Imai](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0994), Theorem 1 and Proposition 9. General links between incompatibility, steering and nonlocality also precede it: [Jenčová](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08008). No-cloning and nonlocal shareability in nonsignaling theories are also established: [Masanes--Acín--Gisin](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0508016). The consequence proved here is the quantitative chain from the stated joint product-cloning defect to a single finite parent measurement for **all** allowed measurements, and then the explicit one-sided conditional-state estimate (9). A targeted primary-source comparison has not established historical novelty of this chain.
