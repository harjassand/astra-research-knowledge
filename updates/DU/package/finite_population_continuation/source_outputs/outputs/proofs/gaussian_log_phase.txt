# Gaussian source: a sharp logarithmic-moment phase transition

Status: complete written argument, internally reconstructed independently by both root and `cross_corpus` on 11 October 2026. Root explicitly confirmed the full modulus quantifiers, both exponent regimes, and the zero-logarithm endpoint. The proof imports the internally reconstructed centered Gaussian potential bound and score-WI from `score_interpolation_theorem.md`. This is written-proof reconstruction, not external validation or a priority claim. The second-moment failure by itself is known in broader conditioning contexts and is not claimed original. The argument is frozen for the current account.

## 1. Exact target interface and modulus

Fix a standard Gaussian source `gamma_d` with `d>=2`, a number `r>0`, and `M>0`. Assume exactly one target obeys

\[
\int\Phi_r(|y|)d\mu(y)\le M,
\qquad\Phi_r(t)=t^2\log(e+t)^r,
\]

while the other target `nu` is arbitrary in `P_2`. Put `w=W_2(mu,nu)` and `D=||T_mu-T_nu||_{L^2(gamma_d)}`. The candidate theorem is

\[
\boxed{D\le w+C_{d,r,M}w^{\beta_r},\qquad
\beta_r=\min\left\{\frac13,\frac r{r+1}\right\}.}
\tag{log-phase}
\]

The exponent is sharp, with matching bounds for the entire small-distance class modulus, even if both targets obey the same moment budget. At the critical value `r=1/2`, the modulus reaches the compact-target exponent `1/3`. For `0<r<1/2`, a two-atom rare Gaussian cap family gives exponent `r/(r+1)`. For `r>=1/2`, the Gaussian three-atom compact-target family supplies the `1/3` obstruction. For `r=0`, the class with only a uniform second moment has no vanishing modulus, even for this fixed full-support Gaussian source.

This is different from the arbitrary-convex-support source profile, which can have only a logarithmic modulus under the same target budget. The distinction is supplied by the source score and vanishing fiber boundary traces, not by an extra target hypothesis.

## 2. A Gaussian score estimate for clipped logarithmic moments

Let `A=|X_j|` for a standard Gaussian coordinate, and let `Z` be any nonnegative function of the source, bounded by `L^2`. There is no independence assumption on `A,Z`. Put

\[
B_L=\log(e+L),\qquad s=(1/2-r)_+.
\]

Then

\[
E[AZ]\le5\left(1+E[Z\log(e+\sqrt Z)^r]\right)B_L^s.
\tag{log-score}
\]

On the event `A<=3sqrt(log(e+sqrt Z))`,

\[
AZ\le3Z\log(e+\sqrt Z)^{1/2}
\le3B_L^s Z\log(e+\sqrt Z)^r.
\]

On the complementary event, `sqrt Z<=exp(A^2/9)`, so `AZ<=Aexp(2A^2/9)`. The latter expectation is

\[
E[|G|e^{2G^2/9}]=\frac{18}{5\sqrt{2\pi}}<2.
\]

Adding the two terms and using `B_L>=1` proves (log-score). In particular this is an explicit Orlicz pairing argument, without requiring a power moment of `Z`.

## 3. One-sided clipped logarithmic moments

For an optimal target coupling `(Y,Z)` with `Y~mu`, `Z~nu`, let `w^2=E|Y-Z|^2`. Set

\[
a_r=4(1+\log2)^r.
\]

The pointwise two-case split `|Z|<=2|Y|` or `|Z|>2|Y|` gives

\[
E\Phi_r(\min\{|Z|,L\})\le a_rM+4B_L^r w^2.
\tag{log-clip-transfer}
\]

Indeed the first case uses `log(e+2t)<= (1+log2)log(e+t)`, and the second uses `|Y-Z|>=|Z|/2`. The constrained target's clip has budget at most `M`.

Also

\[
\tau_\mu(L)\le M B_L^{-r},\qquad
\tau_\nu(L)\le2\tau_\mu(L/2)+4w^2.
\]

Choose an auxiliary `B>=1` and `L=e^B`. Then `B<=B_L<=2B` and

\[
\max(\tau_\mu(L),\tau_\nu(L))
\le b_rM B^{-r}+4w^2,
\qquad b_r=2(1-\log2)^{-r}.
\tag{log-tail-transfer}
\]

For each coordinate derivative clip `g`, apply (log-score) with `Z=g^2`. Its logarithmic moment is bounded by that of the corresponding radial target clip. Thus, for the two potentials,

\[
\sum_j(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})
\le5d\,2^s B^s\{2+(1+a_r)M+4\cdot2^r B^r w^2\},
\]

where `J_{z,j}=E|X_j|g_{z,j}^2` is exactly the source-score energy in score-WI.

## 4. Optimize the score interpolation

For finite targets, choose mean-zero potentials with `F<=sqrt2 w`. The already reconstructed score interpolation yields, for all `h>0`,

\[
D^2\le24d\frac{w^2}{h^2}
+50d\,2^s hB^s\{2+(1+a_r)M+4\cdot2^r B^r w^2\}
+20db_rM B^{-r}+80dw^2.
\tag{log-WI}
\]

For `0<r<1/2`, choose

\[
B=w^{-2/(r+1)},\qquad h=w^{1/(r+1)},\qquad0<w<=1.
\]

Then `B^r w^2=w^{2/(r+1)}<=1`, while `w^2/h^2`, `hB^s`, and `B^{-r}` all equal `w^{2r/(r+1)}`.

For `r>=1/2`, choose

\[
B=w^{-2/(3r)},\qquad h=w^{2/3}.
\]

Here `s=0`, `B^r w^2=w^{4/3}<=1`, and the first, second, and third terms have power `w^{2/3}`. A single admissible constant for both ranges is

\[
C_{d,r,M}=\max\left\{2\sqrt M,
\left[104d+50d\,2^s\{2+(1+a_r)M+4\cdot2^r\}
+20db_rM\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\]

For `w>1`, use `D<=w+2sqrt M`, since `Phi_r(t)>=t^2`. Finite norm-decreasing quantizers preserve the constrained moment budget; finite `W_2` quantizers handle the arbitrary target. Common-source graph-plan uniqueness and squared-norm uniform integrability pass the map inequality to the limits. This proves the candidate upper bound at the exact one-sided interface.

The large cutoffs `L=exp(B)` are proof parameters only. No claim is made that they can be represented, acquired, or used by an algorithm at bounded bit cost.

## 5. Matching rare-cap lower family

Let `X,Y` be independent scalar standard Gaussians. Let `Q(t)=P(X>t)`, take `t->infinity`, and put

\[
a_t=Q(t),\qquad \theta_t=1/t,\qquad
L_t=c\,a_t^{-1/2}\{\log(1/a_t)\}^{-r/2},
\]

where `c>0` is a fixed small constant. Let

\[
q_t=L_te_1,\quad r_t=L_t(\cos\theta_t e_1+\sin\theta_t e_2),
\]

\[
A_t=\{X>t\},\qquad
B_t=\{X\cos\theta_t+Y\sin\theta_t>t\}.
\]

Both cells have mass exactly `a_t` by Gaussian rotation invariance. The convex potentials `L_t(x_1-t)_+` and `L_t(x_1 cos theta_t+x_2 sin theta_t-t)_+` give optimal maps to

\[
\mu_t=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{q_t},\qquad
\nu_t=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{r_t}.
\]

The radii are equal. Their common logarithmic moment is

\[
a_tL_t^2\log(e+L_t)^r\longrightarrow c^2 2^{-r}.
\]

Thus any prescribed `M>0` is respected for all sufficiently large `t` by choosing `c` small enough.

We prove a positive symmetric-difference fraction. Conditioning on `Y`,

\[
\frac{P(A_t\cap B_t)}{a_t}
=E\frac{Q\left(\max\left\{t,\frac{t-Y\sin(1/t)}{\cos(1/t)}\right\}\right)}{Q(t)}.
\]

For fixed `Y`, if the second threshold is `t+s_t/t`, then

\[
s_t=t^2(\sec(1/t)-1)-tY\tan(1/t)\longrightarrow\tfrac12-Y.
\]

The exact change of variables in the Gaussian tail gives, for `s>=0`,

\[
Q(t+s/t)=\frac{\varphi(t)}t
\int_s^\infty e^{-z-z^2/(2t^2)}dz.
\]

Hence `Q(t+s_t^+/t)/Q(t)->exp(-(1/2-Y)_+)`. The ratio is between zero and one, so Gaussian dominated convergence applies to the `Y` expectation. It follows that

\[
\frac{P(A_t\triangle B_t)}{a_t}
\longrightarrow2\left(1-E e^{-(1/2-Y)_+}\right)=:c_*>0.
\tag{cap-switch}
\]

Positivity follows from the positive probability of `Y<1/2`. This proves the cap-switch asymptotic without any uncontrolled approximation of rare transverse slices.

In its independent reconstruction, `cross_corpus` simplified the constant using Gaussian exponential tilting:

\[
E e^{-(1/2-Y)_+}=Q(1/2)+e^{-1/2}E[e^Y\mathbf1_{Y<1/2}]
=2Q(1/2),\qquad c_*=2-4Q(1/2)>0.
\]

The two-atom target matching is optimal because `q_t dot r_t>0`, so

\[
w_t^2=2a_tL_t^2(1-\cos(1/t))\asymp a_tL_t^2/t^2.
\]

The exact map formula is

\[
D_t^2=L_t^2P(A_t\triangle B_t)+|q_t-r_t|^2P(A_t\cap B_t)
\asymp a_tL_t^2.
\]

The same Gaussian-tail integral with `s=0` proves `log(1/a_t) asymp t^2`. Consequently

\[
D_t\asymp t^{-r},\qquad w_t\asymp t^{-(r+1)},
\qquad D_t\asymp w_t^{r/(r+1)}.
\]

For `0<r<=1/2` this matches the upper exponent. The function `w_t=2c(log(1/Q(t)))^(-r/2) sin(1/(2t))` is continuous and strictly decreasing for large `t`, so the family realizes every sufficiently small target distance, not only a discrete subsequence. This proves a lower bound on the full class modulus.

For `r>=1/2`, the compact Gaussian three-atom family in `gaussian_sharp_moments.md` gives the matching `1/3` lower exponent. Scaling its slopes by a fixed small factor respects any prescribed `M>0`. Its target distance is also continuous and monotone in its small parameter, which yields the full small-distance lower modulus.

At `r=0`, choose `L_t=c a_t^(-1/2)`. Both second moments equal `c^2`, `w_t asymp c/t ->0`, and `D_t` stays bounded below by a positive constant. This verifies the no-modulus endpoint on one fixed full-support Gaussian source.

Its Theorem 14 (Appendix B.1) assumes a Gaussian upper source envelope but both targets compact, and gives `W_1^(1/4)log(c/W_1)^(1/8)`. These are different interfaces from a fixed Gaussian source, a one-sided logarithmic moment, and target `W_2` input. [Wang–Wang, arXiv:2610.09640v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09640v1), Theorem 2, treats one-sided power moments above two, and does not state this logarithmic-moment phase law. The exact phase theorem still requires a wider priority review before any originality statement. The known fact that a second-moment budget alone can fail to give a uniform modulus is not claimed new.
