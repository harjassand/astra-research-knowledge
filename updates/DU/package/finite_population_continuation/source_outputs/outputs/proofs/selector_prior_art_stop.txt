## 1. The operation extracted from the source

An exact selector is a triple `(R,e,alpha)` where `R` is a finitely presented unital algebra, `e` is a central idempotent, and `alpha` is an endomorphism. It acts on finite-dimensional modules by

\[
H(Y)={}_{\alpha}(eY).
\]

The unfamiliar operation is to compile this module-dependent extinction process into projective dimensions over **one fixed finite-dimensional algebra**. It does not seek a scalar numerical invariant of the source algebra. It preserves the sequence of iterates of a selected submodule, up to controlled shifts and direct sums.

The source's precise compiler is `04-tensor-realization.tex:8–24`: for any field and any such triple there is a finite-dimensional directed algebra `B`, an exact representation encoding `M`, and one bounded right-projective bimodule complex `P` with

\[
P\otimes_B^{\mathbb L}M(Y)\simeq M(HY)\oplus M(HY)[3]
\]

for every finite-dimensional `Y`. All choices precede `Y`.

It then replaces `P` by an ordinary bimodule `X` over a larger directed algebra `D`, with

\[
(X\otimes_D^{\mathbb L}-)^2M(Y)
\simeq M(HY)[b]\oplus M(HY)[b+3],\qquad b\ge0.
\]

Finally `A=D\ltimes X` has square-zero ideal `X`, and its relative bar complex splits by the number of `X` factors:

\[
D\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}N
\simeq\bigoplus_{r\ge0}(X\otimes_D^{\mathbb L}-)^rN[r].
\]

The last identity is `06-square-zero-and-conclusion.tex:19–39`, with a complete chain-level proof in lines 41–152. Extinction of the tensor iterates is equivalent to finite projective dimension for these inflated modules.

## 2. A prime-order generalization of the selector group

Fix **any prime** `ell`. Let `I={1,2,3}`. Use generators `T_i` and families

\[
U_i(r),\ V_i(r),\ W_{ij}(r),\quad i\ne j,\quad r\in\mathbb Z.
\]

The `T_i` commute. The weights under conjugation by `T^n`, `n in Z^3`, are

\[
\lambda_{U_i}=-\varepsilon_i,\quad
\lambda_{V_i}=\varepsilon_i,\quad
\lambda_{W_{ij}}=\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j,
\]

meaning `T^n S(r) T^{-n}=S(r+lambda_S(n))`. Impose:

1. `U_i(r)^ell=1`.
2. `U_i(r),U_j(s)`, `V_i(r),V_j(s)`, and `U_i(r),V_j(s)` commute for distinct `i,j`.
3. `W_ij(s)` commutes with `U_h(r)` if `h != i`, and with `V_h(r)` if `h != j`.
4. With `[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}`,

\[
[U_i(r),W_{ij}(s)]=U_j(r+s),\qquad
[W_{ij}(s),V_j(r)]=V_i(s+r).
\]

Call this group `G_ell`. It differs from the source group only in replacing its relation `U_i(r)^2=1` by the displayed `ell`th-power relation. No relation asserting same-index commutativity of the `U` or `V` families has been inserted.

### Finite presentation

Keep the fifteen generators `T_i,u_i=U_i(0),v_i=V_i(0),w_ij=W_ij(0)`.
Require `u_i,v_i` to commute with `T_h` for `h != i`; require `w_ij` to commute with `T_iT_j` and with the remaining `T_h`. These centralizers give the entire kernel lattice of each weight. Consequently

\[
U_i(r)=T_i^{-r}u_iT_i^r,\quad
V_i(r)=T_i^rv_iT_i^{-r},\quad
W_{ij}(r)=T_i^rw_{ij}T_i^{-r}
\]

is independent of the chosen conjugating exponent with the required weight. Impose the argument-zero version of each remaining relation and `u_i^ell=1`.

For every pair of types appearing in a relation, their two weights map `Z^3` onto `Z^2`. For the only nontrivial output relations, this can be seen explicitly: for inputs `(U_i(r),W_ij(s))` choose `n_i=-r,n_j=-r-s`; for `(W_ij(r),V_j(s))` choose `n_j=s,n_i=r+s`. The output weight is the sum of the input weights. Thus conjugation supplies all integer arguments. The power relation propagates because its weight is onto `Z`. This proves finite presentation exactly as in `02-selection-process.tex:87–163`, without dependence on the prime being two.

### Central prime-order coordinates

Define

\[
z_N=[U_i(a),V_i(b)],\qquad a+b=N.
\]

This expression is independent of `i,a,b`. Here is the transfer identity, including its sign convention. Set

\[
x=U_i(r),\ y=W_{ij}(q),\ v=V_j(s),\quad
u=[x,y],\quad w=[y,v].
\]

The relations give `[x,v]=[w,u]=[w,v]=1`. Reordering `xyv` gives

\[
xyv=uwvyx=(xwx^{-1})vuyx.
\]

Writing `c=[x,w]` and cancelling yields `uwv=cwvu`, so

\[
[x,w]=[u,v],\quad
[U_i(r),V_i(q+s)]=[U_j(r+q),V_j(s)].
\]

No involution identity was used. Choose `r=a,s=d,q=c-a=b-d` to compare any splittings `a+b=c+d=N` at distinct indices; use a third index to compare splittings at one index.

To commute `z_N` with `U_h` or `V_h`, express it with an index other than `h`; to commute with `W_hj`, use the index outside `{h,j}`. Conjugating by `T_l` changes the two arguments by opposite amounts, so it preserves their sum. Hence `z_N` is central. Centrality implies

\[
1=[U_i(a)^\ell,V_i(b)]=z_N^\ell.
\]

The substitution `U_i(r) -> U_i(r+1)` fixing all other types preserves all relations. Its inverse shifts by minus one. It defines an automorphism `alpha_G` satisfying `alpha_G(z_N)=z_{N+1}`.

### Finite quotients preserving arbitrarily many independent coordinates

For `m>=1`, put

\[
S_m=\mathbb F_\ell[t]/(t^m-1).
\]

Map to `GL_5(S_m)`, with matrix indices `0,1,2,3,4`, by

\[
T_i\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,t_i,\ldots,1),
\quad U_i(r)\mapsto1+t^rE_{0i},
\quad V_i(r)\mapsto1+t^rE_{i4},
\quad W_{ij}(r)\mapsto1+t^rE_{ij}.
\]

Here `t_i` means the entry `t` at position `i`. Every elementary inverse is `1-t^rE_ab`, **not** the original matrix when `ell` is odd. The commutator identities remain

\[
[1+aE_{0i},1+bE_{ij}]=1+abE_{0j},\quad
[1+aE_{ij},1+bE_{j4}]=1+abE_{i4};
\]

the potentially higher products vanish by matrix-unit multiplication. Also `(1+aE_ab)^ell=1` in characteristic `ell`, and diagonal conjugation gives exactly the stated weights. Thus all defining relations hold.

Let `F_m` be the finite image. Then

\[
z_N\mapsto1+t^NE_{04}.
\]

These are central in `F_m`, and

\[
\prod_{q=0}^{m-1}(1+t^qE_{04})^{c_q}
=1+\left(\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}c_qt^q\right)E_{04},\quad c_q\in\mathbb F_\ell.
\]

The monic degree-`m` quotient polynomial makes `1,t,...,t^{m-1}` a basis even when `ell` divides `m` and `S_m` is nonreduced. Thus the images of `z_0,...,z_{m-1}` generate a central subgroup `Z_m=(C_ell)^m`. In particular each `z_N` has exact order `ell` in `G_ell`, since an appropriate finite quotient detects it.

## 3. Selectors over every coefficient field, with no roots of unity

Let `k` be any field with `char(k) != ell`; this coefficient field is distinct from the field defining the finite matrix quotients. Put

\[
R=k[G_\ell],\qquad
e=\frac1\ell\sum_{a=0}^{\ell-1}z_0^a,
\qquad\alpha=k[\alpha_G].
\]

The group algebra is finitely presented as a unital algebra: add an inverse generator and two inverse equations for every generator of the finite group presentation, then its relators. The element `e` is a central idempotent, because each coefficient in its convolution square receives exactly `ell` contributions. Put `e_q=alpha^q(e)`.

In `k[F_m]`, let `E_q` denote the average of the cyclic subgroup generated by the image of `z_q`, and put

\[
p_m=\left(\prod_{q=0}^{m-2}E_q\right)(1-E_{m-1}),
\qquad Y_m=k[F_m]p_m.
\]

This works over fields without a primitive `ell`th root and without an inverse of `|F_m|`. Since the coordinates of `Z_m` are independent, the coefficient of `z_{m-1}` (with every preceding coordinate trivial) in `p_m` is `-ell^{-m}`, which is nonzero. **Do not use the identity coefficient**: it equals `(ell-1)/ell^m` and can vanish, specifically for `ell=3` over characteristic two. Thus `Y_m` is nonzero.

All `E_q` are central, and

\[
E_qY_m=Y_m\ (q<m-1),\qquad E_{m-1}Y_m=0.
\]

For every `R`-module `Y`, repeated pullback gives the actual vector-space identification

\[
H^jY={}_{\alpha^j}(e_0e_1\cdots e_{j-1}Y).
\]

Therefore `H^jY_m !=0` for `j<m`, and `H^mY_m=0`, with the same `R,e,alpha` for all `m`.

Choose `ell=2` when `char(k) !=2`, and `ell=3` when `char(k)=2`. This proves a characteristic-uniform selector theorem over **every field**. The characteristic-two construction is already defined over `F_2`, rather than requiring transcendental parameters.

The induced-module dimension is exactly

\[
\dim_kY_m=(\ell-1)|F_m|/\ell^m\le(\ell-1)\ell^{24m},
\]

because `|F_m|<=|S_m|^{25}=ell^{25m}`. The exact formula follows by decomposing `k[F_m]` into copies of the regular `k[Z_m]` module and computing the ranks of the commuting averaging projections. No efficiency claim is made; even enumerating the quotient can be exponential in `m`.

## 4. Reconstructing why the source compiler works

This part identifies the nontrivial mathematical dependency instead of hiding it behind the word compiler.

**Quadratic directed encoding.** Replace every long monomial in a presentation of `R` by auxiliary generators for its partial products. Use the three-vertex quiver `0 -> 1 -> 2` with arrows `s,a_h` and `s',a'_h`, and relations `a'_hs=s'a_h` together with the homogenized presentation relations. There are no length-three paths. Its algebra `B` is finite-dimensional with left and right global dimensions at most two: the kernel of a projective cover supported on vertices at least `i` is supported on vertices at least `i+1`.

An `R`-module `Y` becomes `M(Y)` by using `Y` at each vertex, identity arrows for `s,s'`, and generator operators for `a_h,a'_h`. This encoding preserves nonzero modules.

**Localize to recover `R`.** In `K^b(proj-B)` for right projectives, quotient by the thick subcategory generated by the cones of the two `s` arrows. These arrows become invertible, so `x_h -> s^{-1}a_h` gives a unital action of `R` on the vertex-zero object `L`. Tensor evaluation at `M(Y)` kills those cones and factors through the quotient. It sees exactly the original `R` action.

**Replace a missing retract by an odd double.** The formal retract `U=(L,e)` need not be an object of the quotient. The cone of `1-e` realizes `U+U[1]`. A second cone cancels the common summand `U[1]` between a shift of this object and itself, realizing `U+U[3]`. This uses neither a splitting of `e` inside the quotient nor a stable-flatness assertion.

**Clear finite diagrams by roofs.** Use one common denominator for the arrows from vertex one to two, then one for arrows from zero to one. Every relation is a map from zero to two that vanishes after localization. A final common denominator at vertex zero kills all relation maps. There is no incoming arrow at zero and no outgoing arrow at two, so this correction creates no new relation. This yields a fixed diagram in the homotopy category with chosen relation null-homotopies, independent of `Y`.

**Rectify with no higher coherence obstruction.** For complexes `D_i`, chain maps `f_a`, and degree-minus-one relation homotopies `h_rho`, form the three columns

\[
L_0=\bigoplus_iBe_i\otimes D_i,\quad
L_1=\bigoplus_{a:i\to j}Be_j\otimes D_i,\quad
L_2=\bigoplus_{\rho}Be_2\otimes D_0.
\]

The corrected differential on `L_0+L_1[1]+L_2[2]` is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}d&p&h\\0&-d&q\\0&0&d\end{pmatrix},
\quad pq=-f_\rho,\quad dh+hd=f_\rho.
\]

Its square is zero. The source-index filtration reduces the comparison at each vertex to the exact length-two path/relation sequence. Consequently the vertex complexes are homotopy equivalent to `D_i`. No relation can be composed with a positive-length path; therefore no fourth coherence column is necessary. Evaluating gives cohomology `M(HY)` only in degrees `0,-3`. The truncation triangle splits because its connecting map lies in `Ext_B^4=0`. This proves the stated derived realization pointwise. A natural choice of splitting is unnecessary for iterating it.

**Make the bimodule ordinary.** For support `[a,b]` of `P`, with `a<=0<=b`, let `C` be a linearly oriented radical-square-zero quiver algebra with `b-a+1` vertices. Place `P^{a+i}` at vertex `i` of an ordinary `(B tensor C,B)` bimodule `O`, making each quiver arrow act by `d_P`. Let `W` be the right simple at the final vertex and `T=B tensor W`. Over `D=B times(B tensor C)`, let `X=O+T` be the off-diagonal ordinary bimodule. The projective resolution of `W` yields

\[
T\otimes_{B\otimes C}^{\mathbb L}O\simeq P[b].
\]

The differential sign is corrected in degree `n` by `(-1)^{bn}`; this is valid in characteristic two as well. Hence `F^2M(Y)=M(HY)[b]+M(HY)[b+3]`, where `F=X tensor_D^L -`. Both global dimensions of `D` are finite.

**Square-zero survival records Tor.** For `A=D semidirect X`, expand each interior bar factor as `D+X`. Nonzero faces preserve the number `r` of `X` entries; the `r` component is exactly the multi-derived tensor `F^rN[r]`. This gives the direct-sum decomposition stated above, locally finite in each degree. If an iterate survives, it gives nonzero `Tor` in a degree at least the iterate index. If the iterates vanish eventually, `D tensor_A^L N` is bounded; tensor further with `S=D/rad D` using finite global dimension. The minimal `A`-resolution has zero differential after tensoring with `S`, so boundedness forces finite projective dimension.

All these arguments are field-independent. This reconstruction has not supplied a printed multiplication table for the existentially constructed `A`, nor a resource bound for searching the quotient lifts.

## 5. A quantitative theorem stronger than the source's iterate-index bound

Let `m(Y)` be the least `m>=1` with `H^mY=0`, when it exists, and assume `Y!=0`. The same fixed compiled algebra gives

\[
F^{2j}M(Y)\simeq\bigoplus_{q=0}^j
M(H^jY)[jb+3q]^{\binom jq}.
\]

Because `O` is right-`B`-projective, applying `F` once more replaces the module in each summand by the ordinary module `O tensor_B M(H^jY)` on the second block, without adding cohomological amplitude. If `g` bounds both global dimensions of `D`, put `c=b+5`. Then

\[
c(m(Y)-1)\le\operatorname{pd}_A M(Y)
\le c(m(Y)-1)+g+1.
\]

**Lower bound.** At `j=m-1`, the `q=j` even-iterate summand contributes nonzero cohomology of `D tensor_A^L M(Y)` in degree `-[j(b+3)+2j]=-c(m-1)`.

**Upper bound.** All surviving summands have `j<=m-1`. The even bar contribution has lowest possible degree `-jc`; the odd contribution has lowest possible degree `-(jc+1)`. Thus `D tensor_A^L M(Y)` is zero below `-[c(m-1)+1]`. Applying a right `D`-projective resolution of `S` of length at most `g` adds at most `g` further negative degrees. Minimal-resolution detection bounds the projective dimension by `c(m-1)+g+1`.

If `H^jY` survives for every `j`, the even bar summands survive in degrees tending to minus infinity, so the projective dimension is infinite. Therefore this is an actual extinction/finite-projective-dimension equivalence, with a bounded-error linear encoding of finite extinction times.

## 6. Proposed consequence and its status

**Proposed theorem.** For every field `k` there exists one finite-dimensional unital `k`-algebra `A_k` and finite-dimensional modules `N_m` for all `m>=1` with

\[
5(m-1)\le\operatorname{pd}_{A_k}N_m<\infty.
\]

The `5` follows from `c=b+5>=5`; the construction actually supplies the sharper affine lower and upper bounds above. Thus the little finitistic dimension is infinite over an algebra defined over each prime field. Taking scalar extensions preserves these finite projective dimensions: a finite projective resolution gives the upper bound, and nonzero finite-dimensional `Ext` in the last degree remains nonzero after faithful field extension.

The new ingredient here is a complete prime-order selector with cyclic averaging complements. The remaining theorem is conditional on the correctness of the source's general-field realization, ordinary simulation, and square-zero arguments, which were reconstructed above but have not been independently formally checked in this run. The source already claims the complex case and a separate transcendental characteristic-two case; the field-uniform consequence must not be advertised as the first counterexample to the underlying conjecture.

## 7. Boundaries and failure tests

- Choosing `ell=char(k)` invalidates the averaging idempotent; use a different prime.
- A character-eigenspace construction would improperly assume roots of unity. The complement of the trivial averaging projection avoids this.
- The identity coefficient of `p_m` can vanish; the nonidentity coordinate coefficient is the reliable witness.
- The finite quotients do not need to be preserved by `alpha_G`. Each scalar pullback uses the same vector space with a new `R` action.
- The group presentation uses only finitely many weight-orbit relators; this depends on pairwise weight maps being onto `Z^2`, which was explicitly checked.
- Odd-characteristic elementary matrices have inverses `1-aE`, and the commutator transfer was checked with that sign.
- `R,e,alpha,B,P,D,X,A` are fixed before `m`. The groups `F_m` and modules `Y_m,N_m` vary.
- Higher homotopy coherences would become a real obstruction if relations overlapped along length-three paths. The three-level quiver prevents exactly that failure.
- No efficient construction of the large compiled algebra is asserted. This is a mathematical existence construction with an explicit selector input, not a polynomial-time acquisition claim.
- No universal finite-dimensional algebra deciding arbitrary Turing halting has been obtained. The explicit finite-field selector instances can be simulated in a finite matrix state space, so their extinction is decidable by iteration with a finite-state bound.
