# Global log-Sobolev control does not stabilize inverse Monge potentials

Status, 11 October 2026: complete new written counterexample, pending independent reconstruction and exact prior review. It strengthens `conditional_covariance_obstruction.md`: the source now has Gaussian tails, an explicit finite global log-Sobolev constant, a bounded upper Hessian and every finite score moment. No computational hardness or failure of all Hölder estimates is claimed.

## 1. Exact statement

For each fixed `d>=2`, let

\[
 V(x)=\sqrt{1+\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^4},\qquad
 \rho_d(dx)=Z_d^{-1}e^{-V(x)}dx.
\tag{1}
\]

This fixed source is positive, smooth, strictly log-concave, and satisfies

\[
 0\preceq\nabla^2V\preceq6I,
 \qquad |\nabla V(x)|\le2|x|,
 \qquad V(x)\ge |x|^2/\sqrt d.
\tag{2}
\]

It satisfies a global log-Sobolev inequality with one explicit finite constant depending on `d`. Nevertheless, for every fixed target radius `s>0`, there is no finite `K` such that

\[
 \|u_\mu-u_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho_d)}\le K W_2(\mu,\nu)
\tag{3}
\]

for all two-atom probabilities supported in `B(0,s)`, where `u_mu,u_nu` are their convex Brenier potentials normalized to have source mean zero. For every `p>2,M>0`, the counterexample can additionally satisfy `int|y|^p dmu,int|y|^p dnu<=M`, by choosing the fixed `s` small enough.

Thus a lower source curvature bound in the inverse-potential mechanism cannot be replaced by the combination of global LSI, Gaussian upper tails, an upper Hessian bound and finite score moments. This is failure for one fixed source with finite constants, not merely deterioration along a class of sources.

## 2. Smooth log-concavity, tails and score bounds

Put `Q=1+sum_i x_i^4`. Direct differentiation gives

\[
 \partial_iV=2x_i^3/Q^{1/2},
 \quad
 \nabla^2V=6Q^{-1/2}\operatorname{diag}(x_i^2)
       -4Q^{-3/2}(x_i^3)_i(x_i^3)_i^T.
\]

For any `v`, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
 (\sum_i x_i^3v_i)^2
 \le (\sum_i x_i^4)(\sum_i x_i^2v_i^2).
\]

Consequently

\[
 v^T\nabla^2Vv
 \ge[6-4(Q-1)/Q]Q^{-1/2}\sum_i x_i^2v_i^2\ge0.
\]

For the upper bound, drop the negative rank-one term and use `x_i^2<=sqrt Q`; this gives `nabla^2V<=6I`. The function is strictly convex because it is the squared `ell_4` norm of `(1,x)` and distinct augmented vectors `(1,x),(1,y)` cannot be positively collinear. It is smooth because `Q>=1`. It is not strongly convex: at a point with `x_2=0`, its quadratic form in `e_2` is zero.

Also `sum_i x_i^6<=(sum_i x_i^4)^{3/2}` implies `|grad V|<=2|x|`. Norm equivalence gives

\[
 |x|^2/\sqrt d\le(\sum_i x_i^4)^{1/2}\le V(x)
 \le1+|x|^2.
\]

Thus the normalizer is finite and positive, all source polynomial moments and all finite score moments exist, and a Gaussian upper envelope holds.

## 3. An explicit global log-Sobolev constant

Use the convention

\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_\lambda(f^2)
 \le2C_{\rm LS}(\lambda)\int|\nabla f|^2d\lambda.
\tag{4}
\]

The sole classical imported functional inequality is Gaussian LSI: for `gamma(dx) proportional to exp(-|x|^2)dx`, `C_LS(gamma)=1/2`. The rest is derived below.

Let `N(x)=||x||_4`, and define the radial bijection

\[
 S(x)=\frac{|x|}{N(x)}x\quad(x\ne0),\qquad S(0)=0.
\]

Its radial factor `a(x)=|x|/N(x)` lies in `[1,d^{1/4}]`, is homogeneous of degree zero, and is smooth away from zero. Since `|grad N|<=1`,

\[
 |x|\,|\nabla a(x)|\le d^{1/4}+d^{1/2}.
\]

Therefore `S` is globally Lipschitz with, for example,

\[
 L_d=2d^{1/4}+d^{1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]

The pointwise derivative bound away from zero implies the global bound by integration along segments, splitting a segment at zero if needed. The pushforward `lambda=S_#gamma` then satisfies (4) with constant at most `L_d^2/2`: apply Gaussian LSI to `f composed with S` and use the chain rule for Lipschitz maps.

Euler homogeneity gives `x dot grad a=0`, hence the matrix determinant lemma gives

\[
 \det DS=a^d.
\]

Also `|S^{-1}y|=N(y)`. Thus the density of `lambda` is proportional to

\[
 a(y)^{-d}e^{-N(y)^2}.
\]

Let `rho_0(dx)=Z_0^{-1}exp(-N(x)^2)dx`. Its density relative to `lambda` is a fixed normalizing factor times `a^d`; the ratio of its maximum to its minimum is at most `d^{d/4}`.

For completeness, the bounded-density comparison of LSI constants is immediate from the nonnegative entropy integrand

\[
 H(h,c)=h\log(h/c)-h+c\ge0,
 \qquad \operatorname{Ent}_\lambda(h)=\inf_{c>0}\int H(h,c)d\lambda.
\]

If `m<=deta/dlambda<=M`, then `Ent_eta(f^2)<=M Ent_lambda(f^2)` and `int|grad f|^2dlambda<=m^{-1}int|grad f|^2deta`. Hence `C_LS(eta)<=(M/m)C_LS(lambda)`. Applying this to `rho_0` gives

\[
 C_{\rm LS}(\rho_0)\le d^{d/4}L_d^2/2.
\]

Finally

\[
 0\le V(x)-N(x)^2
 =\frac1{\sqrt{1+N(x)^4}+N(x)^2}\le1.
\]

The relative density of `rho_d` and `rho_0` consequently has oscillation ratio at most `e`. A second comparison proves the explicit bound

\[
 \boxed{C_{\rm LS}(\rho_d)\le
 \tfrac12 e\,d^{d/4}(2d^{1/4}+d^{1/2})^2<\infty.}
\tag{6}
\]

This is deliberately loose; no optimal or dimension-free LSI constant is asserted. In particular it is an absolute fixed finite constant for the counterexample in dimension two. Expanding (4) at `f=1+epsilon g` also gives a finite global Poincare constant with the same bound.

## 4. Exact rare-cap asymptotics with domination

Let `a_t=rho_d(X_1>t)`, `t>=1`, and write `X=(X_1,Y)` with `Y in R^{d-1}`. Substitute

\[
 X_1=t+s/t,\qquad Y=\sqrt t\,z,\qquad s\ge0.
\]

The exponent difference converges pointwise:

\[
 V(t+s/t,\sqrt t\,z)-t^2
 \longrightarrow2s+\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}z_i^4.
\tag{7}
\]

For dominated convergence, call this difference `D_t(s,z)`. First,

\[
 D_t\ge(t+s/t)^2-t^2\ge2s.
\]

Second, setting `A=sum_i z_i^4`,

\[
 D_t\ge\sqrt{t^4+t^2A}-t^2
 =\frac A{\sqrt{1+A/t^2}+1}
 \ge\sqrt{1+A}-1.
\]

Taking half the sum of these two lower bounds gives

\[
 e^{-D_t(s,z)}
 \le e^{1/2}\exp\{-s-|z|^2/(2\sqrt{d-1})\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The right side remains integrable after multiplication by `1+|z|^2`. The Jacobian is `t^{(d-3)/2}`. Define

\[
 I_4=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-z^4/2}dz,\qquad
 c_4=\frac{\int z^2e^{-z^4/2}dz}{I_4}
     =\sqrt2\,\frac{\Gamma(3/4)}{\Gamma(1/4)}>0.
\]

Equations (7)-(8) prove

\[
 a_t\sim\frac{I_4^{d-1}}{2Z_d}
         t^{(d-3)/2}e^{-t^2},
\tag{9}
\]

\[
 \mathbb E[X_2^2\mid X_1>t]/t\longrightarrow c_4.
\tag{10}
\]

The tangent mean is zero by symmetry. This supplies unbounded conditional covariance after halfspace conditioning, even though the global LSI and Gaussian tail constants are fixed.

## 5. Fixed-mass rotation, without radial symmetry

Unlike the earlier radial exponential example, this source is not rotationally invariant. The target masses are kept exact by adjusting the threshold.

Fix `t`, put `n_theta=cos(theta)e_1+sin(theta)e_2`, and let `q_t(theta)` be the unique threshold satisfying

\[
 \rho_d(n_\theta\cdot X>q_t(\theta))=a_t.
\tag{11}
\]

Near zero this is a continuously differentiable function, with `q_t(0)=t` and `q_t'(0)=0`. Here is the differentiability justification. In rotated coordinates the cap mass is

\[
 A(\theta,q)=\int_{s>q}\rho_d(R_\theta(s,z))\,ds\,dz.
\]

The density is smooth, `|grad rho_d|<=2|x|rho_d`, and its Gaussian envelope controls this integrand and its first angle derivative uniformly for small angles. Thus one may differentiate under the integral. The derivative in `q` is the negative, strictly positive projection density at `q`; the implicit-function theorem applies. At `(theta,q)=(0,t)`, symmetry under `x_2 -> -x_2` makes the angle derivative zero (equivalently it is the integral of `x_2 rho_d(t,x_2,z)` over the boundary hyperplane). Hence `q_t'(0)=0`.

Define the convex potential and its target by

\[
 U_{t,\theta}(x)=s(n_\theta\cdot x-q_t(\theta))_+,
\]

\[
 \mu_{t,\theta}=(1-a_t)\delta_0+a_t\delta_{s n_\theta}.
\tag{12}
\]

Its gradient has exactly the displayed two-atom law. Since it is a convex gradient, it is the Brenier map from the absolutely continuous source to that law. All targets lie in the fixed radius-`s` ball; their `p`-moments are `a_t s^p<=s^p`.

For `|theta|<pi/2`, the optimal coupling of these two-atom targets matches equal labels. A switched amount from the nonzero atom to zero and vice versa adds `2s^2 cos(theta)>0` cost per unit. Therefore

\[
 w_{t,\theta}^2=W_2(\mu_{t,0},\mu_{t,\theta})^2
        =2a_ts^2(1-\cos\theta).
\tag{13}
\]

For each fixed `t`, off the cap boundary,

\[
 \frac{U_{t,\theta}-U_{t,0}}\theta
 \longrightarrow sX_2\mathbf1_{X_1>t}.
\]

The difference quotients are bounded by `C_t+sC|X|` for small angles, because `q_t` is differentiable; the source has finite second moment. They therefore converge in `L^2(rho_d)`. The limiting derivative has source mean zero by symmetry. Subtracting the source mean of each `U` leaves the same limiting derivative. With normalized potentials `u`, equations (10) and (13) yield

\[
 \lim_{\theta\to0}
 \frac{\|u_{t,\theta}-u_{t,0}\|_{L^2(\rho_d)}}{w_{t,\theta}}
 =\sqrt{\mathbb E[X_2^2\mid X_1>t]}
 \sim\sqrt{c_4t}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{14}
\]

This proves the failure of (3). The order of quantifiers is: one fixed source, one fixed radius `s`, arbitrary proposed finite constant `K`; choose a large finite `t`, then choose a sufficiently small nonzero angle for that `t`. No limit in the source or target moment budget is used.

## 6. Encoding, acquisition and scope

Equation (9) gives `log(1/a_t)~t^2`, so the local inverse-potential condition number is of order `(log(1/a_t))^{1/4}`. One may choose the target mass to be exactly dyadic `2^{-m}` by taking its unique source cap quantile; this yields deterioration of order `m^{1/4}`. Rational circle parametrization gives finite-bit target sites. The optimal-potential threshold is derived from the source and target mass, not provided free to a solver. No cost is claimed for computing it, and this polynomial condition deterioration does not imply computational hardness.

The counterexample does not disprove the previously proved sharp map-stability theorems for strongly log-concave sources. It also does not show failure of all weaker potential moduli for this source. It precisely disproves a uniform **linear `W_2` to `L^2` potential estimate** under the global source assumptions above.

The necessary convex-conditioned covariance criterion is in `conditional_covariance_obstruction.md` Section 2. The explicit proof here needs only two atoms and does not depend on the finite-cell mass-Jacobian argument in that criterion.

Primary scope checked on 11 October 2026: [Letrouit–Merigot, arXiv:2411.04908v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04908v3), Theorem 1.4, gives `W_1^{1/2}(1+|log W_1|)^{1/2}` potential control for bounded logarithmic perturbations of a strongly log-concave source; it is not the linear `W_2` interface disproved here. [Ruan, arXiv:2610.07577v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07577v1), studies objective-gap-to-map conditioning and common-source map stability, with source/target density and moment profiles; its main statements do not supply the potential-Lipschitz assertion (3). These scope comparisons do not establish originality of the present example or criterion.

The one imported functional inequality in Section 3 is classical Gaussian log-Sobolev. The density-comparison and Lipschitz-pushforward steps are elementary derivations of standard principles and are not claimed new. A specialist/primary-priority comparison for the exact fixed-source counterexample remains required.
