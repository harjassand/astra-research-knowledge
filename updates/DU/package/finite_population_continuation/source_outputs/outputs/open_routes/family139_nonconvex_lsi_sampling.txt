## 1. Perfect-success target and actual conditional theorem

Fix finite known constants `C_0>0,L_0>=1`. Let `V in C^2(R^d)` satisfy

\[
 V(0)=0=\min V,\qquad\nabla V(0)=0,
 \qquad\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla V)\le L_0,
\tag{1}
\]

and suppose `pi(dx)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}dx` is a probability satisfying

\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_\pi(f^2)
 \le2C_0\int|\nabla f|^2d\pi.
\tag{2}
\]

No convexity of `V` is assumed. Thus its Hessian may have negative eigenvalues; only `-L_0 I<=Hess V<=L_0 I` is guaranteed. A valid upper bound on the global log-Sobolev constant and the global minimizer at zero are **supplied promises**, not inferred from local oracle data or found without cost. For a nonconvex input, acquiring a global minimizer or certifying LSI can be difficult; this theorem does not perform either task.

**Conditional theorem.** Assume the local primitive and deterministic circuit/count contract in Section 2 are valid. For every fixed `epsilon>0`, there is a randomized exact-real gradient-oracle algorithm using at most

\[
 q\le C_{\varepsilon,C_0,L_0}d^\varepsilon
\tag{3}
\]

queries on every execution and producing a law within TV `1/10` of `pi`, uniformly over (1)-(2). If the bit-compiler argument in the companion note is also valid, then for every integer `B>=0` a finite-bit algorithm outputs a dyadic vector whose law is within TV `1/10` of the `B`-bit rounded target, with

\[
 P=B+O_{\varepsilon,C_0,L_0}(\log(d+1))
\]

fractional bits and non-oracle bit cost

\[
 d^{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{poly}_{\varepsilon,C_0,L_0}
       (B+\log(d+1)).
\tag{4}
\]

Every approximate vector oracle call at that precision and polynomial query radius is charged separately. No finite-bit continuous-TV assertion is made.

The significance, if the imported primitive survives independent proof reconstruction, is a change of admissible target class: strong convexity is replaced by a fixed global functional inequality even when the potential is nonconvex. This is not an independent proof of family 139's high-order local sampler.

## 2. Exact imported local premise

\[
 \nu_{x,r}(dz)\propto
  \exp\{-|z|^2/2-F(x+rz)\}\,dz,
 \qquad\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla F)\le\delta=d^{-e},
 \quad0<r\le1.
\]

They require `F in C^2`, but do **not** require `F` convex. The local tilted law is nevertheless strongly log-concave because its Hessian lies between `(1-delta r^2)I` and `(1+delta r^2)I`.

For fixed integer `K_0>=2` and fixed `t>0` with `40(K_0+2)t<1/4`, the source claims a primitive `S(x,r,1)` using at most `B_d delta^{-t}` gradient calls and Gaussian vector slots on every execution, where `B_d=C(1+log d)^C`. Its output approximates `nu_{x,r}*N(0,I)` in TV and `W_2`, with errors at most

\[
 B_d\delta^{K_0}\{\sqrt d+|g(x)|\},
 \qquad g=\nabla F.
\tag{5}
\]

All constants are uniform over the centers, admissible `F`, and history. The primitive has a finite affine/oracle circuit, fixed routine height, bounded expression depth, deterministic scalar data, rank-one Gaussian slot transformations and an independent final Gaussian reserve. These are the precise structural premises inspected in the companion compiler note.

## 3. Consequences of the supplied LSI and known global mode

Write `m=E_pi X`. LSI implies Poincare with constant `C_0`, and the usual Herbst argument gives, for every unit vector `e`,

\[
 \mathbb P(e\cdot(X-m)\le-r)\le e^{-r^2/(2C_0)}.
\tag{6}
\]

These statements follow from the assumed inequality itself. Expanding at `f=1+u g` gives Poincare. Applying LSI to `exp(tg/2)` for bounded one-Lipschitz `g` and integrating the differential inequality gives `log E exp(t(g-Eg))<=C_0t^2/2`. Truncated coordinates have variance at most `C_0`; their means remain within `sqrt(2C_0)` of a fixed median, so the truncation limit gives finite moments and (6). In particular `E|X-m|^2<=C_0d`.

Since `V>=0`, the density is at most `1/Z`. Chebyshev gives probability at least `1/2` to `B(m,sqrt(2C_0d))`; the ball-volume bound therefore gives

\[
 Z\le2(4\pi\mathrm e C_0)^{d/2}
    \le2(4\pi\mathrm e(C_0+1))^{d/2}.
\tag{7}
\]

The gradient Lipschitz bound and `grad V(0)=0` imply `V(x)<=L_0|x|^2/2`, hence

\[
 Z\ge(2\pi/L_0)^{d/2}.
\tag{8}
\]

For an explicit source moment bound put

\[
 A=L_0/2+\tfrac12\log(4\pi\mathrm e(C_0+1)).
\]

The cube `[-1,1]^d` lies in `B(0,sqrt d)` and has `V<=L_0d/2`. Equations (7) and this cube give

\[
 \pi(B(0,\sqrt d))\ge\tfrac12e^{-Ad}.
\]

If `m!=0`, use (6) in direction `m/|m|`; if `|m|>sqrt d`,

\[
 |m|\le\sqrt d+\sqrt{2C_0(Ad+\log2)}.
\]

It follows, including `m=0`, that

\[
 E_\pi|X|^2\le M_*d,
 \quad
 M_*=C_0+\{1+\sqrt{2C_0(A+\log2)}\}^2.
\tag{9}
\]

All constants in (7)-(9) depend only on the supplied `C_0,L_0`. Thus no extra moment oracle or source-moment promise is hidden in the subsequent ideal-history estimates.

## 4. Entropy contraction of the exact proximal Gibbs chain

Let `H_h` be convolution with `N(0,hI)`, `pi_h=pi H_h`, and

\[
 Q_h(y,dx)=\kappa_y(dx)\propto
    e^{-V(x)-|x-y|^2/(2h)}dx.
\]

Set `P_h=Q_h H_h` on the noised `y` state space. It preserves `pi_h` by the joint distribution `pi(dx)N(x,hI)(dy)`.

First, `pi_s` has LSI constant at most `C_0+s`: apply LSI tensorization to `f(X+sqrt(s)G)`, where `X~pi`, `G~N(0,I)` are independent. The two squared-gradient terms have coefficients `C_0` and `s`.

For an input law `eta` with finite `KL(eta||pi)`, put `eta_s=eta H_s`. The relative heat-flow identity is

\[
 \frac d{ds}\operatorname{KL}(\eta_s\|\pi_s)
   =-\tfrac12 I(\eta_s\|\pi_s).
\]

It is a classical integration-by-parts identity for two heat solutions; positive-time smoothing and truncation justify it for finite relative entropy. Applying LSI of `pi_s` to the square root of the relative density gives

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\eta_s\|\pi_s)
 \le\tfrac12(C_0+s)I(\eta_s\|\pi_s).
\]

Integrating the resulting differential inequality yields

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\eta H_h\|\pi_h)
 \le\frac{C_0}{C_0+h}\operatorname{KL}(\eta\|\pi).
\tag{10}
\]

For any `mu` with finite `KL(mu||pi_h)`, posterior data processing gives `KL(mu Q_h||pi)<=KL(mu||pi_h)`. Combine this with (10):

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{KL}(\mu P_h\|\pi_h)
 \le\frac{C_0}{C_0+h}\operatorname{KL}(\mu\|\pi_h).}
\tag{11}
\]

No convexity of `V` was used in (10)-(11). This entropy route replaces the original paper's strong-convexity `W_2` contraction. The heat/proximal reduction under functional inequalities is a known framework, not individually claimed original here.

## 5. Cold start, exact-chain moments and approximate-kernel errors

Choose fixed small rational `e>0`, set `delta=d^{-e}` and `h=delta/L_0`, and work first in sufficiently large dimensions that `delta<=1/2`.

### 5.1 One exact step makes finite entropy

At `y=0`, the posterior is `kappa_0 proportional to exp(-V(x)-|x|^2/(2h))`. Its normalizer is at least `(2pi/(L_0+1/h))^{d/2}` by (1), and

\[
 \frac{d\kappa_0}{d\pi}
 =\frac Z{Z_{\kappa_0}}e^{-|x|^2/(2h)}.
\]

Therefore, by (7),

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\kappa_0\|\pi)
 \le\log\frac Z{Z_{\kappa_0}}
 \le\overline D_d
 :=\log2+\frac d2\log\{2\mathrm e(C_0+1)(L_0+1/h)\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Heat data processing also bounds `KL(delta_0 P_h||pi_h)` by `Dbar_d`. Thus, for

\[
 T=1+\left\lceil\frac{C_0+h}{h}
                \log\{1600(\overline D_d+1)\}\right\rceil,
\tag{13}
\]

the ideal chain from zero has final `KL<=1/1600`, and hence TV at most `1/(40sqrt2)`. Increasing the logarithm in (13) to target a sequence tending to zero gives the uniform `o(1)` version. In either case `T<=C_T delta^{-1}log d`, with `C_T` depending only on fixed parameters.

### 5.2 Polynomial moments suffice; no contraction of approximate paths is needed

The posterior potential has Hessian at least `1/h-L_0=(1-delta)/h`. Its mode `x_y` solves `x_y=y-h grad V(x_y)`, so

\[
 |x_y|\le |y|/(1-\delta).
\]

For a strongly convex density with mode `x_y`, integration by parts gives `E|X-x_y|^2<=d/(1/h-L_0)=hd/(1-delta)`: integrate `(x-x_y) dot grad W` and use strong monotonicity. Adding the fresh heat Gaussian and applying Minkowski gives, for the ideal-chain root second moments `m_j`,

\[
 m_{j+1}\le(1-\delta)^{-1}m_j+3\sqrt{hd},
 \qquad m_0=0.
\tag{14}
\]

Hence

\[
 m_j\le3j\sqrt{hd}\exp(2\delta j)\le d^{A_*}
       \quad(0\le j\le T),
\tag{15}
\]

for a fixed finite `A_*` depending on `C_T,L_0,e`. This deliberately coarse polynomial estimate is enough. We do not assume the nonconvex chain has the source paper's contractive second-moment recursion.

### 5.3 Implement the transition by the imported local primitive

Use `F_y(z)=V(y+sqrt(h)z)`, so `g_y(z)=sqrt(h)grad V(y+sqrt(h)z)` has Lipschitz constant at most `hL_0=delta`. Calling the primitive at center zero and scale one, then returning `y+sqrt(h)S`, gives an approximate kernel `P_hat` with

\[
 \|\widehat P_h(y,\cdot)-P_h(y,\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}
 \le B_d\delta^{K_0}
       \{\sqrt d+L_0\sqrt h\,|y|\}.
\tag{16}
\]

Compare hybrids `delta_0 P_h^j P_hat_h^{T-j}` and `delta_0 P_h^{j+1} P_hat_h^{T-j-1}`. They share an **ideal** prefix of length `j`; all later computation is the same kernel. TV data processing therefore gives

\[
 \|\delta_0\widehat P_h^T-\delta_0P_h^T\|_{\rm TV}
 \le B_d\delta^{K_0}
       \sum_{j<T}\{\sqrt d+L_0\sqrt h\,m_j\}.
\tag{17}
\]

Equations (13), (15) and `delta=d^{-e}` make this uniformly `o(1)` if the fixed integer `K_0` is chosen large enough, for example `eK_0>A_*+e+2`. Crucially, no moment claim is made about histories of the actual approximate chain. Raising `K_0` changes fixed constants and the permitted small `t`, but not the possibility of an arbitrarily small query exponent.

## 6. Removing the Gaussian noise for a nonconvex target

This part uses the source's conditional variance-halving architecture, whose Bayes identity does not require convexity. It is included to make clear that (17) alone approximates `pi_h`, not `pi`.

Fix a noised observation `y`, use the same `F_y` throughout, and set `x_0=0`, `r_j=2^{-j/2}`. At level `j`, an ideal observation is

\[
 Y_j=Z_j+N_j,\qquad Z_j\sim\nu_{x_j,r_j},
 \qquad x_{j+1}=x_j+r_jY_j/2.
\tag{18}
\]

Conditioning on `Y_j` changes the Gaussian reference variance from `r_j^2` to `r_j^2/2`; completing the square proves that the next normalized posterior is exactly `nu_{x_{j+1},r_{j+1}}`. This identity works for any integrable tilt `exp(-F_y)`.

To bound ideal-prefix moments under `y~pi_h`, generate a single latent `X_*~pi` and `y=X_*+sqrt(h)G_0`. Put `Z_*=(X_*-y)/sqrt(h)=-G_0`. An ideal history can be generated with fresh independent `N_j` through

\[
 Y_j=(Z_*-x_j)/r_j+N_j.
\]

Thus `x_{j+1}=(x_j+Z_*)/2+r_jN_j/2`, and

\[
 x_j=(1-2^{-j})Z_*+G_j,
 \qquad\operatorname{Cov}(G_j)
       =(2^{-j}-2^{-2j})I,
\]

where `G_j` is independent of `X_*,G_0`. Consequently

\[
 y+\sqrt h x_j\ \stackrel{\rm law}=\ X_*+\sqrt h r_jG,
\tag{19}
\]

with `G` independent of `X_*`. Equations (1), (9) give

\[
 E_{\rm ideal}|g_y(x_j)|
 \le\sqrt h L_0\sqrt d(\sqrt{M_*}+\sqrt h).
\tag{20}
\]

Replace each ideal observation in (18) by its primitive approximation. Comparing hybrids with the same ideal prefix and the same subsequent actual kernel, exactly as in (17), bounds the cumulative TV error by

\[
 C J B_d\delta^{K_0}\sqrt d=o(1)
\tag{21}
\]

for `J=ceil(C_1 log d)` and the already large fixed `K_0`.

At depth `J`, the exact conditional density has Hessian between `(1-l)I` and `(1+l)I`, where `l=delta r_J^2`. Start at zero and make `m_0=ceil(C_2 log d)` iterations

\[
 z^{k+1}=-r_Jg_y(x_J+r_Jz^k).
\]

Their contraction factor is `l`; the fixed point is the unique conditional mode `z_0`. Return `z^{m_0}+G` for a fresh standard Gaussian. The mode-centered conditional law is within TV `Cl sqrt d` of `N(z_0,I)`, even though `F_y` is nonconvex. One proof translates to the mode and interpolates between the Gaussian potential and its perturbation `J(w)`: `grad J(0)=0`, `Lip(grad J)<=l`; each interpolated potential is `(1-l)`-strongly convex, so its variance of `J` is `O(l^2d)`, and the second derivative of its log normalizer yields KL `O(l^2d)`. Pinsker gives the displayed TV bound. This is the source's mode-Gaussian argument and uses only the local bounded Hessian.

Choose the fixed `C_1` large enough that `delta 2^{-J}sqrt d=o(1)`. Equations (20) and the contraction estimate control the mode-iteration shift error as well. Thus the approximate base rule has TV error `o(1)` averaged over ideal histories. An exact conditional base would recover the latent marginal `pi`; (21) and the Gaussian base estimate therefore remove the initial heat noise. Data processing first transfers the noised-chain TV error through this actual descent kernel.

All local-law analytic claims in this section either use elementary Bayes completion of squares or the already specified local strong-convexity Gaussian comparison. They do not restore global strong convexity of `V`.

## 7. Query cap, finite precision and small dimensions

The outer chain uses `T=O(delta^{-1}log d)` primitive calls, and descent uses `J=O(log d)` calls plus `m_0=O(log d)` gradient iterations. Each primitive has deterministic call/slot count at most `B_d delta^{-t}`. Hence

\[
 q\le\operatorname{polylog}(d)\delta^{-(1+t)}
 =d^{e(1+t)+o(1)}.
\]

Choose `e` sufficiently small after the requested fixed exponent, for example `0<e<epsilon/4`; choose `K_0` as above; then choose fixed `t,w` and interpolation order in the source's permitted order. Since `t<1`, the exponent is strictly below `epsilon/2`, leaving room to absorb logarithms. This proves (3) conditionally.

For the bit interface, the companion sharpening of fixed-seed sensitivity gives `Lip_x S<=Kr delta` for arbitrary transformed seeds, using only `Lip(g)<=delta`. The global scaled oracle `g(z)=sqrt(h)grad V(sqrt(h)z)` has `g(0)=0`, so all pathwise polynomial range and error estimates in that note apply. The larger outer mixing constant changes the exponent in the polynomial amplification, not its type. The final base Gaussian reserve is still independent, with variance scale `sqrt(h)r_J>=d^{-C}`. The same grid anti-concentration argument then gives (4), conditional on the companion compiler's scalar/bit analysis.

The original paper's Gaussian rejection fallback cannot be reused blindly: nonconvex `V` need not dominate a fixed quadratic. Instead, for each of the finitely many dimensions below the uniform asymptotic threshold, use a uniform proposal on a sufficiently large cube `[-R,R]^d`. Equation (9) allows fixed `R` with `pi(outside cube)<=1/100`. Acceptance probability is `exp(-V(x))<=1`, because the supplied mode is global. By (8), its mean is at least

\[
 p_{\min}=\frac{.99(2\pi/L_0)^{d/2}}{(2R)^d}>0.
\]

Cap the number of trials at `ceil(log(100)/p_min)`, returning zero on failure. Accepted proposals have exactly the cube-conditioned target law, so total TV error is below `1/50` in exact arithmetic. There are only finitely many such dimensions, and all constants can be absorbed into `C_{epsilon,C_0,L_0}`. Potential values are approximated, if needed, from

\[
 V(x)=\int_0^1\nabla V(tx)\cdot x\,dt,
\]

whose integrand has Lipschitz constant at most `L_0|x|^2`. A fixed sufficiently fine quadrature and fixed sufficiently precise gradient answers control capped-trial acceptance mismatches. Finite-bit uniform proposals and acceptance uniforms have a coupling with fixed small error, and grid-boundary probability for each uniform proposal is `O(2^B zeta/R)` per coordinate. Thus `B+O(1)` fractional bits suffice in the finite fallback dimensions. The fallback is gradient-only and has a deterministic query cap.

## 8. A concrete nonconvex admitted family

This class is genuinely larger than the source paper's strongly convex class. For example,

\[
 V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d\{x_i^2/4+1-\cos x_i\}
\]

has supplied global minimizer zero, `V(0)=0`, and Hessian eigenvalues `.5+cos x_i` in `[-.5,1.5]`; it is nonconvex and globally gradient-Lipschitz with constant `1.5`. In one dimension it is a bounded logarithmic perturbation, of oscillation at most two, of the Gaussian density proportional to `exp(-x^2/4)`. Gaussian LSI and elementary density comparison give one-coordinate LSI constant at most `2e^2`. Tensorization gives the **same** bound for every dimension, not an exponential-in-d comparison bound. Thus this is an explicit dimension-uniform nonconvex example with fixed `C_0,L_0`.

No exact-normalizer or conditional-mean oracle is supplied for this example. The algorithm still acquires all potential information through gradient calls; verifying its displayed promise is an analytic calculation, separate from the unknown-`V` oracle model.
