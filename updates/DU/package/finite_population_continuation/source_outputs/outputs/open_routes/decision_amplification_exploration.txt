# Mixed-state decision amplification from product self-replication

**Status:** internal derivation candidate. This note proves a finite-dimensional mixed-state extension of the decision-amplification step and an application to label-relevant classicalization. It makes no novelty or prior-art claim; a primary-source comparison is still required before making one.

## Statement

Let \(\mathcal H\) be finite-dimensional, let \(\{\rho_i:i\in I\}\) be a finite family of density operators, and let \(c:I\to\mathcal C\) be a finite class map. A class POVM is \(M=(M_c)_{c\in\mathcal C}\), with risk

\[
r_i(M)=1-\operatorname{Tr}(M_{c(i)}\rho_i).
\]

Suppose one class POVM \(M^0\) has \(r_i(M^0)\le a\) for every \(i\), where \(0<a<1/2\). Let \(B:\mathsf D(\mathcal H)\to\mathsf D(\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal H)\) be a supplied channel and define its per-state **joint product** defect by

\[
e_i=1-F\!\left(B(\rho_i),\rho_i\otimes\rho_i\right),\qquad
F(\sigma,\tau)=\|\sqrt\sigma\sqrt\tau\|_1^2.
\]

Put

\[
f(r)=3r^2-2r^3,\qquad \beta(a)=3a-2a^2,\\
\tau(a)=\frac{(\sqrt a-\sqrt{f(a)})^2}{8}
       =\frac{a(1-\sqrt{\beta(a)})^2}{8},\\
C(a)=\frac{8}{(1-\sqrt{\beta(a)})^2},\qquad
K(a)=\frac{1}{\tau(a)}=\frac{C(a)}{a}.
\]

Then:

1. For any prior \(p\) supported on labels with \(e_i\le\tau(a)\), there is a class POVM \(M^p\) with \(r_i(M^p)\le a\) on that support and
   \[
   \sum_i p_i r_i(M^p)\le C(a)\sum_i p_i e_i.
   \]
2. For an arbitrary prior and arbitrary defect vector, there is a class POVM with
   \[
   \sum_i p_i r_i(M^p)\le K(a)\sum_i p_i e_i.
   \]
3. There is one class POVM \(M^\star\), independent of the prior, such that simultaneously
   \[
   r_i(M^\star)\le K(a)e_i\quad\text{for every }i.
   \]

The constants depend only on the weak-classifier error bound \(a\), not on dimension, number of states, priors, or the smallest nonzero prior. The premise in the prompt \(a<1/3\) is therefore sufficient; the exact three-vote majority calculation extends the result to every \(a<1/2\). If \(a=0\), the supplied POVM already has zero class error and no amplification is needed.

## Mixed-state transfer through the cloning tree

Use the Bures distance with the squared-fidelity convention above:

\[
d_B(\sigma,\tau)=\sqrt{2(1-\sqrt{F(\sigma,\tau)})}.
\]

For each \(i\), set \(d_i=d_B(B(\rho_i),\rho_i^{\otimes2})\). Then

\[
d_i^2=2\left(1-\sqrt{1-e_i}\right)\le 2e_i.
\]

Define the three-output tree by

\[
\Omega_i^{(2)}=B(\rho_i),\qquad
\Omega_i^{(3)}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes B)(\Omega_i^{(2)}).
\]

The intermediate state can be correlated. Bures contractivity under channels, the triangle inequality, and multiplicativity of fidelity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_B(\Omega_i^{(3)},\rho_i^{\otimes3})
&\le d_B\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes B)\Omega_i^{(2)},
                 (\mathrm{id}\otimes B)\rho_i^{\otimes2}\big)
   +d_B(\rho_i\otimes B(\rho_i),\rho_i^{\otimes3})\\
&\le d_B(\Omega_i^{(2)},\rho_i^{\otimes2})+d_i
 =2d_i\le 2\sqrt{2e_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the point that makes the mixed-state argument work: the second use of \(B\) acts on a register correlated with the first output, but contractivity compares the entire correlated state with the ideal product input before the second use. No independence of the actual outputs is assumed.

For any effect \(0\le Q\le I\) and any two states \(\sigma,\tau\), Uhlmann's theorem gives the square-root measurement transfer

\[
\left|\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(Q\sigma)}-
       \sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(Q\tau)}\right|
\le d_B(\sigma,\tau).
\]

Indeed, choose purifications with overlap \(\sqrt{F(\sigma,\tau)}\), align their phase, and apply the contraction \(\sqrt Q\otimes I\); the norm difference of the purifications is exactly \(d_B(\sigma,\tau)\). Thus for every decision effect on the three outputs,

\[
\sqrt{q_i^{\rm actual}}\le \sqrt{q_i^{\rm ideal}}+\sqrt{8e_i}.
\]

## Proof of the risk bound

Fix a prior on a set \(G\subseteq I\) for which \(e_i\le\tau(a)\). Among all class POVMs satisfying the pointwise constraints \(r_i\le a\) for every \(i\in G\), choose one minimizing prior-average risk. The feasible set is nonempty by \(M^0\), and it is compact and convex in finite dimension.

Apply this optimizer independently to the three registers of \(\Omega_i^{(3)}\). Return the class appearing at least twice; if the three outcomes are all distinct, return a fixed class. On the ideal product state \(\rho_i^{\otimes3}\), the three measurement outcomes are independent. A wrong final decision requires at least two single-register errors, so its probability is at most

\[
f(r_i)=\Pr[\mathrm{Binomial}(3,r_i)\ge2]=3r_i^2-2r_i^3.
\]

The square-root transfer above bounds the actual error \(r_i'\) of this new class POVM by

\[
r_i'\le\left(\sqrt{f(r_i)}+\sqrt{8e_i}\right)^2.
\]

Because \(f\) is increasing on \([0,1]\) and \(f(a)<a\) for \(a<1/2\),

\[
\sqrt{r_i'}\le\sqrt{f(a)}+\sqrt{8\tau(a)}=\sqrt a.
\]

The amplified POVM is therefore feasible for the same constrained optimization. If \(r=\sum_i p_i r_i\) and \(E=\sum_i p_i e_i\), optimality and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
r&\le\sum_i p_i\left(\sqrt{f(r_i)}+\sqrt{8e_i}\right)^2\\
 &\le \left(\sqrt{\beta(a)r}+\sqrt{8E}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(f(r_i)/r_i=3r_i-2r_i^2\le3a-2a^2=\beta(a)<1\); the ratio is increasing throughout \(0\le r_i\le a<1/2\). Taking square roots and rearranging yields

\[
r\le C(a)E.
\]

For a general prior, split \(I\) into \(G=\{i:e_i\le\tau(a)\}\) and its complement. Apply the good-set result to the conditional prior on \(G\); the bad labels contribute at most their mass, and

\[
\sum_{i\notin G}p_i\le \tau(a)^{-1}\sum_{i\notin G}p_i e_i.
\]

Since \(K(a)=\tau(a)^{-1}>C(a)\), the resulting full-prior average risk is at most \(K(a)\sum_i p_i e_i\). If \(G\) has zero prior mass, the same inequality follows from \(1\le K(a)\sum_i p_i e_i\).

Finally, the prior-average bound holds for every prior \(p\). Let \(r(M)=(r_i(M))_i\), a compact convex risk set. Finite minimax gives

\[
\min_M\max_i(r_i(M)-K(a)e_i)
=\max_{p\in\Delta(I)}\min_M\sum_i p_i(r_i(M)-K(a)e_i)\le0.
\]

The minimizing POVM is the prior-independent \(M^\star\) in item 3.

## Decision-relevant classicalization

The POVM \(M^\star\) is a measurement channel from the quantum experiment \(\{\rho_i\}\) to a classical output \(Y\in\mathcal C\). For each parameter \(i\), its output law \(q_i(y)=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y^\star\rho_i)\) obeys

\[
\operatorname{TV}(q_i,\delta_{c(i)})=r_i(M^\star)\le K(a)e_i.
\]

Thus the measured experiment approximates the perfect classical experiment that reveals the target class, with an error charged label by label to the supplied product-replication defect. In particular, for every class-dependent loss \(\ell(c,u)\in[0,1]\), choose an action \(u_c\) optimal for each revealed class and use \(u_Y\) after the measurement. Its expected loss at parameter \(i\) exceeds the loss under perfect class revelation by at most \(K(a)e_i\); for any prior, the excess is at most \(K(a)\sum_i p_i e_i\).

This is a finite, decision-specific classicalization theorem for mixed-state experiments. It does not claim that the full quantum experiment is close to an entanglement-breaking reconstruction, or preserve tasks that depend on distinctions inside a class. It also does not provide a method for learning \(B\), acquiring the states, or implementing a measurement efficiently.

The same proof applies to finite classical experiments: replace density operators by distributions, channels by stochastic maps, and quantum fidelity by classical fidelity. The result then says that a supplied stochastic product-replicator plus any uniformly weak class predictor yields one class statistic whose error is pointwise proportional to the replicator's product defect.

## Why a weak-classifier premise is necessary

Take two labels with the same state, \(\rho_0=\rho_1=|0\rangle\langle0|\), and balanced prior. The constant channel \(B(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)|00\rangle\langle00|\) has \(e_0=e_1=0\), but every measurement has average label error at least \(1/2\). Therefore no universal bound of the form

\[
\text{class error}\le C\sum_i p_i e_i
\]

can follow from product self-replication alone. Here the weak-classifier premise fails: a POVM on the identical states cannot have worst-label error below \(1/2\). The premise supplies the missing information-bearing interface; replication alone cannot create labels that are absent from the input states.

## Scope and comparison boundary

The argument assumes joint approximation to \(\rho_i\otimes\rho_i\), measured by squared Uhlmann fidelity. It is not an approximate-marginal-broadcasting result. The operation count for the three-output construction is two calls to \(B\), and the final prior-independent POVM follows from finite minimax, not an acquisition algorithm. No literature comparison was performed here, so the result should be treated as an internal derivation and not a novelty claim.
