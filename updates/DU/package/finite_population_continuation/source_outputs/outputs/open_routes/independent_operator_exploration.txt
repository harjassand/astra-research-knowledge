# Direct operator routes: exact obstruction and an unresolved Gram inequality

Date: 11 October 2026. Status: preserved negative research. The task was redirected to the structural frame consequence after these attempts. None of the numerical probes below proves a universal cloning/classicalization inequality.

## 1. Notation and the PGM candidate

For an ensemble {p_i,psi_i}, put P_i=|psi_i><psi_i|,

    G_ij=<psi_i,psi_j>,    A_ij=sqrt(p_i p_j)G_ij.

Let R=sqrt(A). The original-ensemble pretty-good POVM, followed by preparation of P_j, has reconstruction loss

    D_PGM = sum_ij |R_ij|^2 (1-|G_ij|^2).

A cloner Stinespring isometry V admits normalized environment vectors e_i with

    sum_i p_i ||V psi_i - psi_i tensor psi_i tensor e_i||^2
      =2 sum_i p_i(1-sqrt(f_i)) <=2 e_cl,

where f_i is its global cloning fidelity on P_i. The target Gram density is

    B=A circ G circ E,    E_ij=<e_i,e_j>.

Uhlmann/Gram alignment therefore gives Bures_squared(A,B)<=2 e_cl. A dimension-free inequality controlling D_PGM by this Bures defect would be an independent direct proof, but I did not establish it. The obstruction investigator had independently formulated the same candidate. The near-identical equal-prior binary case requires a PGM/Bures coefficient approaching 6+4sqrt(2), approximately 11.656854.

The naive scalar pairwise argument loses control when summed over arbitrarily many labels. Concavity of the square root under Schur multipliers gives a Jensen inequality in the wrong direction for the desired bound. A raw Bures/trace-norm triangle introduces square-root losses. These are unresolved routes, rather than evidence that the bound is false.

## 2. An exact failure of affine EB-dual interpolation

Let H=C^2 and use the equally likely real states

    psi_+=c|0>+s|1>,    psi_-=c|0>-s|1>,
    c^2+s^2=1,    g=c^2-s^2 in (0,1),    k=1-g^2=4c^2s^2,
    t=sqrt(g^2+k^2)=sqrt(1-g^2+g^4),
    rho=diag(c^2,s^2).

Accessible fidelity has a dual over Hermitian Y satisfying, for every output pure state Q,

    Y >= (1/2) sum_(i=+,-) Tr(QP_i)P_i.

Define

    alpha=(1+g^2/t)/2,    b=k^2/(4t),
    Y=alpha rho+bI.

This Y is feasible and optimal. To verify feasibility, write Q's Bloch vector as (x,y,z), x^2+y^2+z^2=1. The right-hand side is

    T(Q)=((1+gz)/2)rho+(kx/4)sigma_x.

Direct expansion gives

    Tr[Y-T(Q)] = (t-gz)/2 >0,
    det[Y-T(Q)] = (k/16)[(z-g/t)^2+k y^2] >=0.

So the 2-by-2 matrix is positive. The input X-basis measurement, with output Bloch vectors (plus_or_minus k/t,0,g/t), saturates the dual: Y-T(Q_+) annihilates |+x>, and Y-T(Q_-) annihilates |-x>. Thus

    F_EB=TrY=(1+t)/2.

The cloning loss operator is

    L_cl=(1/2) sum_(i=+,-) P_i^T tensor (I-P_i tensor P_i).

Set xi_+=s|0>+c|1> and xi_-=s|0>-c|1>, and

    w_+=xi_+ tensor psi_+ tensor psi_+,
    w_-=xi_- tensor psi_- tensor psi_-.

Both w_+ and w_- lie in the kernel of L_cl: the matching term has zero output loss, while the other input projector annihilates xi_i.

Put Delta=rho-Y. On their two-dimensional span its quadratic-form matrix is

    <w_i,(Delta tensor I)w_j> = [[a,h],[h,a]],
    a=(k/4)(1-1/t),    h=g^3 k^2/(4t).

In particular,

    a+h = (k/(4t))[t-1+g^3 k].

Take g=4/5. Then k=9/25 and t=sqrt(481)/25. Here

    t-1+g^3 k = [125sqrt(481)-2549]/3125 >0,

since sqrt(481)>21 and 125*21>2549. Therefore w_++w_- has strictly positive Delta quadratic form but zero cloning loss.

It follows that for EVERY positive c_0,

    L_cl - c_0 (rho-Y) tensor I

fails to be positive. Equivalently, proposing a cloning dual

    Z=(1-c_0)rho+c_0Y

fails, even though Y is an exact optimal EB dual. This kills that specific affine interpolation mechanism; it does not kill more sophisticated operator dual constructions or the universal linear bound itself.

## 3. Numerical diagnostics and their limits

`dual_gap_probe.py` and `dual_gap_probe.json` first exposed the failure above. Their floating-point generalized eigenvalues use a small kernel regularization and are not a proof. Section 2 replaces the numerical observation by an exact witness.

`gram_rigidity_probe.py` reduces the singular Gram fidelity SDP to the input support: if A=Gamma*Gamma with rank r, then

    F_root(A,B)=Tr sqrt(Gamma B Gamma*).

For a correlation variable E, this is represented by maximizing TrS subject to

    E>=0, diag(E)=1,
    [[Gamma B Gamma*,S],[S,I_r]]>=0,
    B=A circ G circ E.

The reduction often avoids the singular 2n-by-2n block in the original formulation. However, several near-coincident cases remain ill-conditioned, and one returned SDP objective exceeded 1 even though fidelity cannot. The logs also recompute the actual root fidelity from the returned correlation matrix; near the pure boundary, numerical residuals materially affect the ratio. No reliable counterexample or universal evidence is claimed from this dataset.

The exact binary EB-dual calculation is consistent with the established accessible-fidelity framework of [Fuchs and Sasaki](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302092). The unresolved Gram inequality should be reopened only with a new operator mechanism or a numerically certified falsification attempt.
