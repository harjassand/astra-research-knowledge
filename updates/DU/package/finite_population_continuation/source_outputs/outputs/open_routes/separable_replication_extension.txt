# Countable classical programs for separable quantum systems

11 October 2026. New internal proof extending the linear-output replication compiler beyond finite dimension. M1's finite-dimensional statement and measurement budget are unchanged. The extension below has no finite record, ancilla, energy, acquisition, or synthesis bound.

## Statement

Let H be any separable Hilbert space, and let B:T(H)->T(H tensor H) be a normal quantum channel, expressed on trace-class states. Let M be a nonempty trace-norm closed family of density matrices; the full state space is permitted. Define squared fidelity F, e(rho)=1-F(B(rho),rho^(tensor2)), tau=1/2800, and C=8/(1-sqrt(3/200))^2<11.

There is one finite or countable POVM (M_y), and representatives sigma_y in M, such that for all rho in M and every integer m>=1, simultaneously,

    sum_y Tr(M_y rho)[1-F(rho,sigma_y)^m]
       <= min{1,max(2800,C+75m)e(rho)}.                    (S1)

The countable measure-and-prepare channels

    Gamma_m(rho)=sum_y Tr(M_y rho) sigma_y^(tensor m)

are normal, trace-preserving, exactly consistent under output partial trace, and satisfy the same upper bound on global infidelity with rho^(tensor m). The measurement and dictionary depend on B and M, not on m, rho, or a prior. For the full state space, they depend only on B.

If the good subset S={rho in M:e(rho)<=tau} is empty, any fixed representative and the trivial measurement prove the assertion. Assume S nonempty below.

## 1. Countable class geometry and representatives

All pair geometry from the mixed-state M1 proof remains valid in arbitrary Hilbert dimension: Bures angle contractivity and triangle give

    acos(r^2)-acos(r) <= asin(sqrt(e(rho)))+asin(sqrt(e(sigma))),
    r=sqrt(F(rho,sigma)).

For good states either r<1/16 or r>15/16, and the high-overlap relation is transitive. Same-class states obey

    1-F(rho,sigma)<=25[e(rho)+e(sigma)].                    (S2)

Each class is relatively trace-norm closed in S because of the fidelity gap. The state space T(H) is trace-norm separable. Representatives of different classes have trace distance at least 1-r>15/16; hence there are at most countably many classes. This uses separability rather than compactness.

For a class c, put alpha_c=inf_{rho in c} e(rho). If alpha_c>0, choose sigma_c in c with e(sigma_c)<=2alpha_c. Then e(sigma_c)<=2e(rho) for every rho in c. If alpha_c=0, choose rho_n in c with e(rho_n)->0. Equation (S2) and trace distance <=sqrt(1-F) make (rho_n) a trace-norm Cauchy sequence. Its limit is a density matrix in the closed model M, has e=0 by continuity of B and fidelity, and remains in c by relative closedness. Choose this limit as sigma_c.

In either case,

    e(sigma_c)<=2e(rho),
    1-F(rho,sigma_c)<=75e(rho).                             (S3)

For the initial coarse classifier a sharper uniform estimate is available without minimization: both rho and sigma_c are good, so (S2) gives 1-F(rho,sigma_c)<=50tau.

## 2. Finite class classifiers in infinite dimension

Fix finitely many classes. The effects of a finite-outcome POVM lie in the ultraweak compact unit ball of B(H), and the conditions M_c>=0 and sum_c M_c=I are ultraweak closed. For each normal state rho, Tr(rho M_c) is ultraweak continuous. A supremum of the wrong-class risks is lower semicontinuous, so its minimum is attained even when the set of input states in a class is noncompact.

The finite mixed PGM estimate still holds:

    average error <=sum_(i!=j) sqrt(p_i p_j) sqrt(F(sigma_i,sigma_j)).

One rigorous bounded construction avoids unbounded inverse square roots. Set Gamma=(sqrt(p_1 rho_1),...,sqrt(p_k rho_k)), a Hilbert-Schmidt block-row operator, and take its polar decomposition Gamma=V sqrt(G), where G=Gamma*Gamma. The effects V E_i V* are bounded, positive, and sum to the support of Gamma Gamma*. Add the complementary projection to any effect. Their error is the off-diagonal Hilbert-Schmidt mass of sqrt(G), exactly as in finite dimension. Powers-Stormer applies to the positive trace-class G and its block diagonal G_0; its elementary proof by the compact Hilbert-Schmidt difference sqrt(G)-sqrt(G_0) also passes to a countable eigenbasis. Thus no inverse of a possibly singular or non-bounded-below density operator is used.

For every prior on at most three representatives this gives error <=1/8; finite minimax over the compact effect set gives worst error <=1/8. The two-call Bures/majority transformation is unchanged:

    r'_rho <=(sqrt(3)r_rho+sqrt(8e(rho)))^2.                (S4)

Optimal worst representative risk R obeys R<=f(R), f(x)=(sqrt(3)x+1/sqrt(350))^2. As proved in the finite M1 argument, f(x)<x on [a,b]=[1/200,13/50]. Deletion induction extends representative risk <a to any finite number of classes: omit a uniformly random label, giving R<=1/k+(1-1/k)a<=203/800<b for k>=4, then use the gap.

The representative classifier extends to its entire classes with error at most

    a+sqrt(50tau)=1/200+1/sqrt(56)<1/4<b.

Optimize worst risk on those whole classes and apply (S4) and the gap once more. Therefore every finite collection of whole classes admits one finite POVM whose risk is at most a on every state of those classes.

## 3. A countable sub-POVM and no escape of probability

Let the class set be C, finite or countable. Consider

    X={(M_c)_(c in C): M_c>=0, sum_(c in F) M_c<=I
                               for every finite F subset C}.

With the product ultraweak topology, X is compact by Banach-Alaoglu and Tychonoff; positivity and all finite partial-sum conditions are closed. It consists of sub-POVMs. The missing effect I-sum_c M_c, with the sum taken strongly, is regarded as a failure outcome.

Each constraint Tr(rho M_c)>=1-a is closed. Any finite list of such constraints involves finitely many classes, for which Section 2 supplies a POVM; set all other effects to zero. The finite-intersection property therefore gives a nonempty compact convex subset

    K={M in X: 1-Tr(rho M_(c(rho)))<=a for every rho in S}.  (S5)

Retaining a failure outcome is essential: equality sum_c M_c=I is not closed in the product topology, and mass could otherwise escape to labels at infinity.

For a sub-POVM, the ideal wrong-class probability r=1-Tr(rho M_c) includes failure. On three independent inputs the correct majority label is c whenever at least two outcomes are c, so the ideal wrong probability remains <=3r^2. If there is no repeated non-failure label, output failure. This defines a countable sub-POVM after pulling through the two-call B channel. Equation (S4) remains valid. In particular, it maps K into K because f(a)<a.

## 4. Linear pointwise decision error

For any finite prior on S, the average class risk R attains its minimum over K. Let E be the corresponding average cloning error. Apply the majority transformation to the minimizer; r_i^2<=a r_i and Cauchy-Schwarz give

    R<=(sqrt(3aR)+sqrt(8E))^2,
    R<=C E.                                                (S6)

For any finite list of states, finite minimax/separation now gives a member of K satisfying r_i<=C e_i on the whole list: otherwise a nonnegative normalized separating vector would be a prior contradicting (S6). These are closed constraints on compact K, so another finite-intersection argument gives ONE sub-POVM with

    1-Tr(rho M_(c(rho)))<=C e(rho) for every rho in S.       (S7)

Finally add its residual effect to any one class. This makes a genuine countable POVM and can only improve the success probability on that class; all other correct effects are unchanged. No probability is lost.

## 5. All output counts and normality

Prepare sigma_c for outcome c. For rho in class c, wrong outcomes cost at most r(rho), and (S3) gives correct-outcome m-copy loss <=75m e(rho). Hence the expected proper m-copy loss is <=(C+75m)e(rho). Outside S it is at most 1<=2800e(rho). This proves (S1).

Each series defining Gamma_m converges in trace norm on positive inputs, since its terms are positive and their traces sum to one. Equivalently, the channel factors through the normal countable measurement map to l^1(C), followed by the countable preparation channel from l^1(C). It is therefore a normal CPTP channel on trace-class inputs. Tensor fidelity multiplicativity and separate concavity give the stated global infidelity bound. Normalization of every sigma_c gives consistency under partial trace. Only finite output counts are asserted; an infinite tensor-product Hilbert representation is unnecessary.

## 6. Closedness and record-size limitations are real

Closedness of a promised proper model cannot simply be omitted. Let H=C^2, psi_t=cos(t)|0>+sin(t)|1>, and M={P_(psi_t):0<t<=t_0}, with 0<t_0<pi/4. Let B be the replacer with output P_0^(tensor2). Its defect on P_(psi_t) is 1-cos(t)^4->0 as t->0. Suppose any proper estimator, including a continuous-outcome POVM, had expected infidelity <=K e for finite K. At input P_0 its output distribution is supported on strictly positive t, so its expected loss is the strictly positive integral of sin(t)^2. The expected loss at input P_(psi_t) tends to this positive number: measurement outcome distributions converge in total variation, and the pure-state fidelity loss converges uniformly over 0<s<=t_0. This contradicts a right side tending to zero. The closure adds P_0 and removes the obstruction.

A finite record cannot replace the countable record in general. On H=l^2(N), exact orthogonal basis states P_n admit exact cloning by measuring n and preparing P_n^(tensor2). Zero-loss proper reconstruction on every P_n requires distinguishable program outputs for every n. Any finite measurement alphabet has only finitely many fixed preparations and cannot reproduce infinitely many distinct pure P_n with zero loss. Thus no uniform finite ancilla or bit bound follows in infinite dimension.

## 7. Classical dominated models

The same proof applies to a total-variation closed model of probability measures dominated by one probability measure mu on a standard Borel space, with a supplied Markov kernel B:X->X^2. Replace squared quantum fidelity by squared Hellinger affinity, trace-class states by L^1(mu) densities, and effects by L^infinity(mu) functions. L^1(mu) is separable and the unit effect ball is weak-* compact.

For finite priors the classical PGM is the posterior rule M_i(x)=p_i f_i(x)/(sum_j p_j f_j(x)) where the denominator is positive. Its error is at most the same pair-affinity bound (in fact one half that ordered sum), since sum_j p_j f_j>=2sqrt(p_i p_j f_i f_j). All other arguments, including the sub-POVM residual and fidelity tensorization, are unchanged. The resulting countable label kernel and representative distributions give (S1) and a projectively consistent product-sampling generator.

The domination hypothesis scopes this classical version. Uncountably many mutually singular Dirac measures admit exact replication with an uncountable observed label, so a universal countable program cannot cover all nondominated classical experiments.
