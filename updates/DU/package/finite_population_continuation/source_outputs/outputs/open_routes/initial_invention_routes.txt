## Attack 1: force budgets for an onto logical clock

Source family 376, especially
`preprints/Incompressible-Box-Transport-and-Finite-Computation-October-6-2026/build/clocks.tex:98-135`, equation `bc:input-offset-force` and its onto-clock discussion. The companion geometric construction transports finitely many disjoint rational boxes by smooth divergence-free fields; finite compilation and positive routing margins are supplied structural premises, not an uncharged general acquisition algorithm.

Extracted operation: an autonomous processor `W` can be slowed by a scalar clock `alpha`, with logical time `g(t)=int_0^t alpha`, velocity `U=alpha W`, and force

\[
f=\alpha'W+\alpha^2(W\cdot\nabla)W-\nu\alpha\Delta W.
\]

The source's eventual `alpha(t)~1/(1+t)` gives `g(t)~log(1+t)`, so every finite logical step is reached at finite physical time, but the time needed for `n` units is exponential in `n`. There is no physical polynomial-time simulation consequence. Its force and spatial derivatives decay as `O(1/t)` and belong to every fixed temporal `L^p`, `p>1`.

Derived exact endpoint obstruction. Let `W` be a fixed nonzero smooth divergence-free field, either compactly supported on `R^d` or periodic, with

\[
K=\|W\|_2^2>0,\qquad D=\|\nabla W\|_2^2>0.
\]

Fix viscosity `nu>0`, assume `alpha in C^1`, `alpha>=0`, `alpha(0)=0`, and require `g(t)->infinity`. Then incompressibility and integration by parts give

\[
\langle(W\cdot\nabla)W,W\rangle=0,
\quad -\langle\Delta W,W\rangle=D,
\]

and therefore

\[
\int_0^T\langle f(t),W\rangle dt
=K\alpha(T)+\nu Dg(T)\ge\nu Dg(T).
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz implies

\[
\int_0^T\|f(t)\|_2dt\ge\frac{\nu D}{\|W\|_2}g(T)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Thus no onto nonnegative scalar clock for this fixed nonconstant autonomous viscous processor has a finite temporal `L^1 L^2` forcing budget. Any added scalar pressure gradient is orthogonal to `W` and does not change the proof. The condition `D>0` excludes constant/harmonic zero-dissipation fields.

Conversely, fix a flat-start clock `alpha(t)` which agrees with `(1+t)^(-1)` after a finite startup, and set `alpha_T(t)=T^(-1)alpha(t/T)`. Its logical clock is still onto. The acceleration and nonlinear force factors have temporal `L^p` size `O(T^(-2+1/p))`; the viscous term has size `O(T^(-1+1/p))`. Every fixed `p>1` force norm can therefore be made arbitrarily small while retaining all finite logical steps, at the cost of arbitrarily long physical delay. This is a complete, elementary critical-budget statement for a supplied fixed processor.

Limit of the attack: the obstruction does not cover changing the spatial processor with time, shrinking signals, increasing spatial precision, a signed clock, or approximate finite-horizon observation. It gives no lower bound on such broader simulation interfaces. It is closely tied to the standard forced energy pairing, so it is not a credible foundational novelty candidate by itself. Retain as a useful resource-cost audit and deprioritize.

## Attack 2: adaptive small-rank exclusions in independent prediction blocks

Source family 093,
`preprints/A-dimension-free-logarithmic-Sobolev-inequality-for-subgaussian-log-concave-measures-September-23-2026/build/sections/04-prediction.tex:317-425`, especially `eq:entropy-loss-block-bound`, `eq:entropy-loss-ui`, and `eq:entropy-loss-rank-error`. The source constructs special conditional residuals of independent observations; its entropy and curvature assumptions are not erased by extracting the covariance operation.

Derived abstraction. Let `(W_j,H_j)`, `1<=j<=N`, be independent copies of a square-integrable vector and its local observation. Let `F=sigma(H_1,...,H_N)`, and put

\[
G_j=W_j-E[W_j\mid H_j],\qquad G=(G_1,\ldots,G_N).
\]

Let `P_N` be any `F`-measurable orthogonal projection with deterministic rank bound `k_N`. It may be chosen adaptively from all local histories. Conditional independence gives a block-diagonal covariance `Sigma=Cov(G|F)`, whose block traces satisfy

\[
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_j\le M_j:=E[|W_j|^2\mid H_j].
\]

For any cutoff `K>0`, on blocks with `M_j<=K` the covariance operator is at most `K I`; on the remaining blocks use `0<=P<=I`. Therefore

\[
E\|P_NG\|^2=E\operatorname{tr}(P_N\Sigma)
\le K k_N+\sum_jE[M_j1_{M_j>K}].
\]

If `Z=|W_1|^2` and `M=E[Z|H_1]`, then on `{M>K}`, the conditional contribution of `{Z<=K/2}` is at most `K/2<M/2`, giving

\[
E[M1_{M>K}]\le2E[Z1_{Z>K/2}].
\]

Hence, uniformly over these adaptive projections,

\[
\frac1N E\|P_NG\|^2
\le K\frac{k_N}{N}+2E[|W_1|^21_{|W_1|^2>K/2}].
\tag{rank-tail}
\]

If `k_N=o(N)`, first let `N->infinity` at fixed `K`, then let `K->infinity`; the captured residual energy is `o(N)` under only a finite second moment. More generally independent nonidentical blocks work with a uniformly integrable family of squared norms.

With `E|W_1|^(2+epsilon)<=C`, the second term is at most `C'K^(-epsilon/2)`. Balancing the cutoff gives normalized energy at most `C''(k_N/N)^(epsilon/(2+epsilon))`. The asymptotic and rate are ordinary covariance-truncation consequences; no assertion of new information-theoretic optimality is warranted.

Interface restriction and counterexample direction. This bounds energy captured by projections of residuals which remain conditionally independent after the supplied observations. It does not bound information acquired by an arbitrary rank-`k_N` real-valued observation or a nonlinear encoder. A single exact real linear measurement can injectively encode a finite discrete vector using sufficiently separated/infinite-precision coefficients. Conditioning on such a pooled measurement generally destroys the conditional block independence. Any proposed general adaptive compression theorem without explicit precision and factorization hypotheses therefore fails at the interface, before the rank-tail proof can apply. Retain the scoped lemma; deprioritize as a major theorem candidate.

## Attack 3: moment-sensitive transport map stability

Source family 374's midpoint coupling and cell adjoint argument survived the substantive generalization attempt. The full proof is in `transport_theorem.md`.

Extracted source operations: midpoint geometric-mean mass domination; comparison of first-moment and mass derivatives independent of inverse graph-Laplacian bounds; transport interpolation along a supplied optimal target coupling. New derived steps: transfer midpoint domination to a compact log-concave density, weighted convex-gradient interpolation by shifts toward slice modes, radial moment truncation, a two-atom equal-radius sharpness family, and an exact variational class modulus. Root independently reconstructed the principal written-proof steps and added the one-sided quadratic-tail transfer.

Current strongest candidate: for compact full-dimensional log-concave source, one target with `p>2` moment bound, and the other arbitrary `P_2`, the Brenier-map difference is at most `w+Cw^((p-2)/(3p-2))`, `w=W_2`. The exponent is sharp already for two-atom targets on a fixed cube. The general radial-moment class admits a sharp variational modulus, including second-moment-plus-log endpoints. One-sided moment acquisition and the target distance are kept exactly the same when comparing with Wang-Wang 2026. No algorithmic or bit-complexity result is inferred from a mathematical norm bound.

An attempted plain source-second-moment replacement for the compact-support constant fails exactly for Gaussian halfspace cells: the ratio in the differential mass-to-moment inequality equals the squared threshold and diverges. This is recorded in Section 8 of the detailed proof and prevents silently extending the current proof to unbounded sources.

Priority comparison completed directly for Delalande-Mérigot 2023, Letrouit-Mérigot 2026, Mischler-Trevisan 2026, Mérigot May 2026, Ruan 6 October 2026, and Wang-Wang 7 October 2026. No checked theorem has the exact sharp one-sided compact-log-concave `W_2` exponent. This is a scoped result of reading those exact statements, not an originality certificate or an exhaustive literature proof.
