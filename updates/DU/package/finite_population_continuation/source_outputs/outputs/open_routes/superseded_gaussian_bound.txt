# A separate full-Gaussian interpolation refinement

Status: superseded intermediate branch, preserved for provenance. Its written proof was not independently reconstructed by root. The stronger sharp one-third Gaussian theorem in `gaussian_sharp_moments.md` and score-only theorem in `score_interpolation_theorem.md` replace its weaker upper exponent. The sharp exponent for arbitrary convex source supports remains `(p-2)/(3p-2)`; the slab lower family does not establish sharpness for a source with full support. No external validation or priority claim is made.

Let `gamma_d` be the standard Gaussian probability on `R^d`. For two finite convex potentials `u,v`, put

\[
F=\|u-v\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)},\quad
D=\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)},\quad
\tau(L)=\max_{z\in\{u,v\}}\int_{|\nabla z|>L}|\nabla z|^2d\gamma_d.
\]

For every `0<h<=1` and `L>0`,

\[
D^2\le 30dF^2/h^2
+20\sqrt d\,Lh\bigl(\|\nabla u\|_2+\|\nabla v\|_2\bigr)
+40d\tau(L).
\tag{GWI}
\]

The gain over the general slice-unimodal bound is `Lh` in place of `L^2h`. Two properties specific to the full Gaussian source enable this: shifts crossing its mode have uniformly bounded density distortion, and weighted variation can be integrated by parts against its score. There is no excluded mode strip.

## Proof of GWI

Fix coordinate `j` and a transverse point. On its real-line fiber write `r(t)=(2pi)^(-1/2) exp(-t^2/2)`, and define

\[
S(t)=\begin{cases}t+h&t\le0,\\t-h&t>0.\end{cases}
\]

This map has two injective branches and image multiplicity at most two. Direct calculation gives

\[
r(t)\le e^{h^2/2}r(S(t))\le c\,r(S(t)),\qquad c=e^{1/2}<5/3.
\]

For `f=u-v`, let `D_hf=(f(S(t))-f(t))/(S(t)-t)`. After transverse integration,

\[
\int|D_hf|^2d\gamma_d\le 2(1+2c)F^2/h^2<10F^2/h^2.
\tag{GQ}
\]

Call a point bad when either coordinate derivative of either potential, at the point or at its shifted endpoint, has magnitude greater than `L`. Each current-point event has mass at most `tau(L)/L^2`. Each shifted event has mass at most `2c tau(L)/L^2`. Thus the union has mass at most `(2+4c)tau(L)/L^2<9tau(L)/L^2`.

For each potential `z`, split its coordinate energy on this bad set according to `|partial_j z|<=L` or `>L`. The bound is at most `L^2 mass(bad)+tau(L)<=10tau(L)`. Hence

\[
\int_{bad}|\partial_j u-\partial_j v|^2d\gamma_d\le40\tau(L).
\tag{GB}
\]

On the good set, convexity along the fiber gives

\[
\delta_z:=|D_hz-\partial_j z(t)|
\le |g_z(S(t))-g_z(t)|\le2L,
\]

where `g_z` is the monotone derivative `partial_j z` clipped to `[-L,L]`. The endpoint derivatives equal their clipped values on this set.

For any monotone bounded `g`, Tonelli against its Stieltjes derivative gives

\[
\int_{t\le0}(g(t+h)-g(t))r(t)dt
\le ch\int r(s)dg(s).
\]

Indeed the inner `t` interval is `[s-h,s] intersect (-infinity,0]`; on that interval `r(t)<=cr(s)`. For `s<=0` the factor can even be replaced by one; for `0<s<=h` it follows from `r(0)/r(s)<=c`. The backward branch obeys the same bound. Consequently

\[
\int|g(S(t))-g(t)|r(t)dt\le2ch\int r(s)dg(s).
\]

Since `delta_z^2<=2L delta_z`,

\[
\int_{good}\delta_z^2d\gamma_d
\le4cLh\int r(s)dg_z(s)\,d\gamma_{d-1}.
\]

Integration by parts is justified by boundedness of `g_z` and Gaussian decay, and yields

\[
\int r(s)dg_z(s)\,d\gamma_{d-1}
=\int x_jg_z(x)d\gamma_d(x)
\le\|g_z\|_2\le\|\partial_jz\|_2.
\tag{variation-score}
\]

On the good set use `(a+b+c)^2<=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)` on the difference quotient and the two errors. By (GQ), (GB) and `12c<20`,

\[
\int|\partial_j u-\partial_j v|^2d\gamma_d
\le30F^2/h^2+20Lh(\|\partial_j u\|_2+\|\partial_j v\|_2)+40\tau(L).
\]

Sum coordinates and use `sum_j||partial_j z||_2<=sqrt(d)||gradient z||_2` to obtain (GWI). This proof initially applies to finite affine maxima, for which all directional derivative and Stieltjes statements are immediate. The map consequence below passes to arbitrary targets by finite-target approximation, so no potential limit theorem is required here.

## One-sided moment consequence for Gaussian Brenier maps

Let `int |y|^p dmu<=M`, `p>2`, and let `nu` be arbitrary in `P_2`. Write `w=W_2(mu,nu)`. For finite targets, choose mean-normalized potentials with the strong-source estimate `F<=sqrt(2)w` established in the companion note. Also

\[
\|T_\mu\|_2\le M^{1/p},\qquad
\|T_\nu\|_2\le M^{1/p}+w.
\]

The one-sided optimal-coupling tail transfer gives `tau(2L)<=2tau_mu(L)+4w^2`. Applying (GWI) at cutoff `2L`, for `0<w<=1` we obtain

\[
D^2\le60d\frac{w^2}{h^2}
+40\sqrt d(2M^{1/p}+1)Lh
+80dM L^{-(p-2)}+160dw^2.
\]

Choose

\[
L=w^{-2/(3p-4)},\qquad h=w^{2(p-1)/(3p-4)}\le1.
\]

The first three terms have power `w^{2(p-2)/(3p-4)}`. Finite norm-decreasing quantization of `mu`, finite `W_2` approximation of `nu`, and common-source graph-plan uniqueness plus uniform integrability prove the same estimate for the original targets. With

\[
C=\max\left\{\sqrt{220d+40\sqrt d(2M^{1/p}+1)+80dM},\,2M^{1/p}\right\},
\]

the resulting bound for all `w` is

\[
\boxed{\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)}\le w+Cw^{(p-2)/(3p-4)}.}
\]

This exponent exceeds the general convex-support exponent for every finite `p>2`. No matched lower bound is supplied for the full Gaussian source. The result does not undermine sharpness over the larger strongly log-concave class: the slab source has a physical boundary, whose weighted variation integration by parts includes a boundary contribution of order `L` and restores the `L^2h` term.

For a general one-sided radial moment budget with superquadratic `Phi`, the same calculation gives a supplementary Gaussian upper profile proportional to

\[
w^2+\inf_{L>0}\{w^{2/3}L^{2/3}+M L^2/\Phi(L)\},
\]

with the constant also depending on the uniform second-moment bound implied by that budget. Restricting to small `w` and sufficiently large cutoffs ensures the optimizing `h` lies in `(0,1]`. This profile is only an upper estimate; the min-max lower argument for the slab does not establish Gaussian sharpness.
