## 1. Exact class and profile

Fix `d>=2`, `M>0`, and a finite, continuous, nondecreasing function `Psi:[0,infinity)->[1,infinity)`. Put

\[
\Phi(t)=t^2\Psi(t),\qquad
k(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\log(e+t)}}{\Psi(t)},\qquad
K(L)=\max_{0\le t\le L}k(t).
\]

For the fixed standard Gaussian source `gamma_d`, let

\[
\mathcal M_{\Phi,M}(w)=\sup\left\{
\|T_\mu-T_\nu\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)}:
\int\Phi(|y|)d\mu\le M,\ \nu\in\mathcal P_2,
\ W_2(\mu,\nu)\le w\right\}.
\]

Only `mu` has a moment assumption. Define the squared profile

\[
\mathcal H(w)=\inf_{L\ge1}\left\{
w^{2/3}(1+MK(L))^{2/3}+\frac M{\Psi(L)}\right\}.
\tag{profile}
\]

The candidate theorem is that for all sufficiently small `w>0`,

\[
\boxed{c_{d,M,\Psi}\,\mathcal H(w)
\le\mathcal M_{\Phi,M}(w)^2
\le C_{d,M}\,\mathcal H(w).}
\tag{sharp-profile}
\]

The same lower bound is achieved with both targets satisfying the moment budget. In particular the class has a vanishing modulus if and only if `Psi(t)->infinity`. No doubling condition on `Phi` or `Psi` is assumed. The constants and the sufficiently-small-distance threshold are fixed once the displayed class is fixed.

## 2. Imported interpolation interface

For finite atomic targets, use source-mean-zero potentials `u,v` and put

\[
F=\|u-v\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)},\quad
D=\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^2(\gamma_d)},\quad
w=W_2(\mu,\nu).
\]

The centered midpoint/covariance chain gives `F<=sqrt(2)w`. For every cutoff `L>0` and step `h>0`, the score interpolation in `score_interpolation_theorem.md` gives

\[
D^2\le12dF^2/h^2+10h\sum_j(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})+20d\tau(L),
\tag{WI}
\]

where `g_{z,j}=clip(partial_j z,[-L,L])`,

\[
J_{z,j}=E|X_j|g_{z,j}^2,\qquad
\tau(L)=\max_{z=u,v}E[|\nabla z|^2\mathbf1_{|\nabla z|>L}].
\]

These premises require no limiting-potential assertion for arbitrary targets: the final map inequality is passed by target quantization.

## 3. A fixed Gaussian pairing and one-sided transfer

Write `Q_*(t)=t^2 sqrt(log(e+t))`. For any bounded scalar function `g` of the Gaussian source, without any independence hypothesis,

\[
E|X_j|g^2\le5\{1+E Q_*(|g|)\}.
\tag{pairing}
\]

Indeed split at `|X_j|=3sqrt(log(e+|g|))`. Below the threshold the integrand is at most `3Q_*(|g|)`. Above it, `g^2<=exp(2X_j^2/9)`, and

\[
E|G|e^{2G^2/9}=18/(5\sqrt{2\pi})<2.
\]

For the constrained target and any radial clip at `L`,

\[
E Q_*(\min\{|Y|,L\})\le MK(L).
\tag{moment-pairing}
\]

For `|Y|<=L` this is the definition of `K`; for `|Y|>L` use `Q_*(L)<=K(L)Phi(L)<=K(L)Phi(|Y|)`.

Let `(Y,Z)` be an optimal coupling of the targets, with `Y~mu`, `Z~nu`. The auxiliary function `Q_*`, unlike `Phi`, has an absolute doubling constant

\[
Q_*(2t)\le c_*Q_*(t),\qquad c_*=4\sqrt{1+\log2}.
\]

Split at `|Z|<=2|Y|`. On that event, `Q_*(min(|Z|,L))<=c_*Q_*(min(|Y|,L))`. On its complement, `|Z|<=2|Y-Z|`, so

\[
E Q_*(\min\{|Z|,L\})
\le c_* MK(L)+4\sqrt{\log(e+L)}w^2.
\tag{one-sided-pairing}
\]

Apply (pairing) to each coordinate clip, bounded pointwise by the radial clip. Therefore

\[
\sum_j(J_{u,j}+J_{v,j})
\le5d\{2+(1+c_*)MK(L)+4\sqrt{\log(e+L)}w^2\}.
\tag{score-total}
\]

The general one-sided quadratic-tail transfer gives

\[
\tau(2L)\le2\tau_\mu(L)+4w^2
\le\frac{2M}{\Psi(L)}+4w^2.
\tag{tail}
\]

There is no moment acquisition or implicit doubling step for the unrestricted target.

## 4. Optimize at the profile crossing

For `L>=1`, monotonicity of `Psi` gives

\[
K(2L)\le c_KK(L),\qquad c_K=\sqrt{1+\log2}.
\]

For points below `L` this is immediate; for points in `[L,2L]` compare their numerator to `sqrt(log(e+2L))` and their denominator to `Psi(L)`.

Let

\[
A_w(L)=w^{2/3}(1+MK(L))^{2/3},\qquad B(L)=M/\Psi(L).
\]

The first is continuous and nondecreasing; the second is continuous and nonincreasing. For sufficiently small `w`, `A_w(1)<B(1)`. There is a finite crossing `L_w` with `A_w(L_w)=B(L_w)`: if `Psi` is unbounded then `B` tends to zero, and if `Psi` is bounded then `K` tends to infinity. Write the common value as `H_w`. Monotonicity proves

\[
H_w\le\mathcal H(w)\le2H_w.
\tag{crossing-profile}
\]

There is a useful automatic resource bound at this crossing. Since `K(L_w)>=sqrt(log(e+L_w))/Psi(L_w)`,

\[
B(L_w)\ge w^{2/3}\{B(L_w)\sqrt{\log(e+L_w)}\}^{2/3},
\]

and hence

\[
\log(e+L_w)\le B(L_w)/w^2\le M/w^2.
\tag{cutoff-bound}
\]

Consequently the unrestricted target term in (one-sided-pairing) satisfies

\[
w^2\sqrt{\log(e+2L_w)}\le c_K w\sqrt M.
\]

For `w<=1` this is absorbed by a constant depending on `M` times `1+MK(L_w)`. Use cutoff `2L_w`, set `N_w=1+MK(L_w)`, and take `h=(w^2/N_w)^{1/3}` in (WI). Equations (score-total) and (tail) give

\[
D^2\le C_{d,M}\{w^{2/3}N_w^{2/3}+B(L_w)+w^2\}
\le C'_{d,M}H_w.
\]

Here `w^2<=w^{2/3}<=A_w(L_w)`. This proves the upper bound in (sharp-profile).

The cutoff bound controls its logarithm; this remains an analytic proof parameter. No algorithm, acquisition procedure, or finite bit complexity is asserted.

## 5. Uniform calibration of a rare three-atom strip

Let `X,Y` be independent scalar standard Gaussians and let `Q(t)=P(X>t)`. There are universal positive constants `eta_0,c_0,C_0` such that the following construction works for every `t>=2` and `0<eta<=eta_0`. Put `delta=eta/t` and let `C_*` be the unique number with `E(C_*+Y)_+=1`.

There are numbers `rho=rho(eta,delta^2)` and `C=C(eta,delta^2)`, with

\[
|\rho|+|C-C_*|\le C_0\eta,
\tag{calibration-bound}
\]

such that the following two convex affine maxima have exactly equal three cell masses:

\[
u(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-t),(L/2)(x_1-t)+L\delta/2\},
\]

\[
v(x)=\max\{0,L(x_1-t'),(L/2)(x_1-t')+(L\delta/2)(C+x_2)\},
\qquad t'=t+\delta\rho.
\tag{calibrated-potentials}
\]

Here the middle cell of `u` is `A={|X-t|<delta}`. If `H=(C+Y)_+`, the middle cell of `v` is `B={|X-t'|<delta H}` with the convention that it is empty when `H=0`. The left and right cells of `v`, conditioned on `Y`, have thresholds `t'-delta H` and `t'+delta H`.

For completeness, the exact calibration follows from a uniform implicit function theorem, not an asymptotic equality of the target weights. For parameters `(eta,epsilon)` near zero define

\[
G_+(\rho,C;\eta,\epsilon)=
E\int_1^{\rho+(C+Y)_+}e^{-\eta z-\epsilon z^2/2}dz,
\]

\[
G_-(\rho,C;\eta,\epsilon)=
E\int_{-1}^{\rho-(C+Y)_+}e^{-\eta z-\epsilon z^2/2}dz.
\]

These are continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of `(rho,C,eta,epsilon)=(0,C_*,0,0)`. Gaussian domination applies even for small negative `epsilon`: on a fixed box in `rho,C`, all integrands and their derivatives are bounded by a polynomial in `|Y|` times `exp(c|Y|+cY^2)` for a fixed `c<1/2`. The positive-part derivative is an indicator and its discontinuity has zero Gaussian mass. At the base point, `G_+=G_-=0`, and the Jacobian in `(rho,C)` is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1& P(C_*+Y>0)\\1&-P(C_*+Y>0)\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is nonzero. The ordinary implicit function theorem therefore gives a solution and the linear bound in `|eta|+|epsilon|`. Take `epsilon=delta^2=eta^2/t^2`, choose `eta_0` small, and obtain (calibration-bound) uniformly for `t>=2`.

The Gaussian identity

\[
\varphi(t+\delta z)=\varphi(t)e^{-\eta z-\delta^2z^2/2}
\]

shows that `G_+=0` is exactly `E Q(t'+delta H)=Q(t+delta)`, while `G_-=0` is exactly `E P(X<t'-delta H)=P(X<t-delta)`. Thus the outer masses agree exactly and so do the middle masses. This explains why both intercept parameters are needed.

Write

\[
a=P(|X-t|<\delta),\qquad b=Q(t+\delta).
\]

The corresponding optimal target maps have common weights `(1-a-b,a,b)` at the three atoms

\[
\mu:\quad 0,\ (L/2)e_1,\ Le_1;
\qquad
\nu:\quad0,\ (L/2)e_1+(L\delta/2)e_2,\ Le_1.
\]

All `mu` atoms have second coordinate zero. The second-coordinate transport cost is `a(Ldelta/2)^2`, and matching the equal-weight atoms realizes it. Therefore the target distance is exactly

\[
w_{t,\eta,L}^2=aL^2\delta^2/4.
\tag{exact-w}
\]

The Gaussian tail rescaling

\[
Q(t)=\frac{\varphi(t)}t\int_0^\infty e^{-z-z^2/(2t^2)}dz
\]

and its interval analogue give uniform bounds

\[
c_0\eta Q(t)\le a\le C_0\eta Q(t),\qquad
Q(t-\delta)\le C_0Q(t).
\tag{mass-bounds}
\]

Indeed the denominator integral is bounded above by one and below by its integral over `[0,1]`, uniformly for `t>=2`; the interval integrand over `[-eta,eta]` is bounded above and below by positive constants. The extra tail interval `[-eta,0]` is uniformly bounded as well.

By (calibration-bound), `C` is uniformly bounded. On the fixed positive-probability event `Y<-sup C-1`, the middle cell of `v` is empty. At every point of the middle cell of `u` on this event, its map value `(L/2)e_1` differs from either outer map value of `v` by at least `L/2`. Independence therefore proves

\[
D_{t,\eta,L}^2\ge c_0L^2Q(t)\eta,
\qquad
w_{t,\eta,L}^2\le C_0 L^2Q(t)\eta^3/t^2.
\tag{strip-lower}
\]

For `eta_0` small, `delta<=sqrt3`, so all target atom radii are at most `L`. Both moment budgets are at most

\[
Q(t-\delta)\Phi(L)\le C_0Q(t)\Phi(L).
\tag{moment-cost}
\]

All constants in (mass-bounds), (strip-lower), and (moment-cost) are independent of `t,eta,L`.

## 6. Lower profile and its minmax identification

For every sufficiently large `L`, choose `t>=2` by

\[
Q(t)=\frac{M}{C_0\Phi(L)},
\]

enlarging the universal constant `C_0` if necessary. Both target moment budgets are then at most `M`. Put `S=L^2Q(t)=M/(C_0Psi(L))`. Standard elementary Gaussian tail estimates imply `t>=c sqrt(log(e+L))` for sufficiently large `L`: `Psi>=1` gives `Q(t)<=M/(C_0L^2)`, while integrating the density from `t` to `t+1/t` gives `Q(t)>=c exp(-t^2)` for `t>=2`.

Given any allowed small distance `w`, choose

\[
\eta=\min\left\{\eta_0,
\left(\frac{wt}{\sqrt{C_0S}}\right)^{2/3}\right\}.
\]

Then (strip-lower) ensures `w_{t,eta,L}<=w`, and it gives

\[
\mathcal M_{\Phi,M}(w)^2
\ge c\min\left\{\frac M{\Psi(L)},
w^{2/3}\left(\frac{M\sqrt{\log(e+L)}}{\Psi(L)}\right)^{2/3}\right\}.
\tag{cap-profile}
\]

In addition the compact Gaussian three-atom family from `gaussian_sharp_moments.md`, scaled by a fixed small slope so that `Phi` of its bounded radius is at most `M`, gives `mathcal M(w)^2>=c_{M,Psi}w^{2/3}` for all sufficiently small `w`.

It remains to show these lower bounds match (profile), including irregular increasing `Psi`. Let `a_w(L)=w^{2/3}(Mk(L))^{2/3}`. Since the maximum defining `K(L)` is attained,

\[
A_w(L)\asymp\max\{w^{2/3},\sup_{s\le L}a_w(s)\}.
\]

As `B` is nonincreasing, for an attaining point `s<=L`,

\[
\min\{a_w(s),B(L)\}\le\min\{a_w(s),B(s)\}.
\]

Using the crossing characterization consequently yields

\[
H_w\asymp\max\left\{w^{2/3},
\sup_{L\ge1}\min\{a_w(L),B(L)\}\right\}.
\tag{minmax}
\]

For the reverse inequality, `a_w(L)<=A_w(L)` and the continuous monotone crossing implies `min(A_w(L),B(L))<=H_w`. Also `H_w>=w^{2/3}`. The bounded range of radii excluded in the rare-strip construction contributes at most a fixed multiple of `w^{2/3}` and is covered by the compact lower family. Thus (cap-profile), the compact lower family, (minmax), and (crossing-profile) prove the lower bound in (sharp-profile). Continuity of the moment profile prevents a jump gap in this minmax comparison; it is an explicit theorem hypothesis.

## 7. Limits and examples

Finite norm-decreasing target quantizers preserve `Phi` moments because `Phi` is increasing. Arbitrary finite `W_2` quantizers approximate the other target. Optimal graph-plan uniqueness for the common absolutely continuous source, together with uniform integrability of squared target norms, passes the upper map inequality to all stated targets. The lower families are already finite atomic targets and need no passage to a limit.

If `Psi` tends to infinity, first fix a large cutoff to make `M/Psi(L)` small and then let `w` decrease in (profile); this proves continuity. If `Psi` has finite limit, let `L` tend to infinity in the lower profile; its first argument stays positive and the second tends to infinity for fixed `w>0`, proving failure of any vanishing modulus. These statements concern uniform classes, not pointwise continuity of the map at a fixed target.

For `Psi(t)=log(e+t)^r`, the theorem recovers

\[
\mathcal M(w)\asymp w^{\min\{1/3,r/(r+1)\}},\qquad r>0.
\]

For `Psi(t)=\{log log(e^e+t)\}^r`, `r>0`, it yields

\[
\mathcal M(w)\asymp\{\log(1/w)\}^{-r/2}.
\]

For a bounded moment ratio, including `Psi=1`, it gives no uniform continuity. If `Psi(t)` eventually dominates a positive multiple of `sqrt(log(e+t))`, then `K` is bounded and the sharp profile is `w^{1/3}`. Profiles with long plateaus or varying growth are described by (profile) and do not require a regular-variation hypothesis.
