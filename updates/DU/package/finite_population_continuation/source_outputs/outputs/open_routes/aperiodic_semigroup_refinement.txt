# Group-free refinement of the nonperiodic coupling obstruction

Status: **FROZEN — PENDING INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION**, at the parent's explicit request. A proposed full argument is preserved below, but the aperiodicity claim is not promoted as a completed team result. This strengthens `NONEVENTUAL_PERIODICITY.md` only if the pumping lemma in section 4 survives independent reconstruction. The already independently reconstructed theorem there is sufficient for the general-semigroup obstruction.

## 1. Statement

There is a finite **aperiodic** semigroup `S` generated by distinct `a_0,a_1`, with zero `0` and a nonzero element `z` satisfying `zs=sz=0` for every `s in S`, such that for the fixed marginal

\[
\mu=\tfrac12(\delta_{a_0}+\delta_{a_1})
\]

the full set of horizons at which the product can be `z` almost surely is

\[
\{87+70t:t\ge0,\quad\operatorname{Re}(((-1+3i\sqrt3)/8)^t)\ge0\}. \tag{A1}
\]

In fact `s^{180}=s^{181}` for every semigroup element `s`. Thus nonperiodicity of exact coupling horizons can occur even when every individual semigroup power stabilizes and the semigroup has no nontrivial subgroup.

## 2. A framed version of the same checker language

Use the 14-state maps, checker word `w(j,b)` of length `L=15`, start state, and terminal acceptance test from `NONEVENTUAL_PERIODICITY.md` sections 2–3. The word `w(j,b)` consists of branch bit `b` followed by the fourteen required checkers. Its first bit reveals `b`, and its unique checker bit different from `b` reveals `j`; hence `(j,b)` is uniquely recoverable from it.

Let `h(0)=01,h(1)=10`. For a fair selector bit `u`, define the inner delimiter

\[
D_u=(1-u)uuu(1-u),
\]

and the two outer markers

\[
P_u=uuuu(1-u),\qquad Q_u=(1-u)uuuu.
\]

A complete frame is

\[
E_{u,j,b}=D_u h(w(j,b)),
\]

of length `M=5+2L=35`. A terminal frame is

\[
T_{u,b}=D_u h(b),
\]

of length seven.

Define the regular language `L_f` to consist of exactly the words

\[
P_v E_{u_1,j_0,b_1}\cdots E_{u_r,j_{r-1},b_r}
T_{u,b} Q_w, \tag{A2}
\]

where `r>=0`, `j_0` is the fixed start state, `j_i=f_{b_i}(j_{i-1})`, and `b=0` or `j_r in F`. All delimiter and outer-marker selectors are free. Regularity follows by using the finite state `j` to check successive frames; fixed finite words label the transitions.

Every such word has length

\[
N=5+35r+7+5=17+35r. \tag{A3}
\]

At this length, reading the first bit of each Manchester pair in its specified frame positions recovers exactly a raw accepted checker word of length `15r+1`. Fair marginals in the framed word imply fair marginals in the raw word. Conversely, given any fair-coordinate law on raw words, take every marker selector independently and fairly. Every coordinate of every marker is then fair, as are both bits of every encoded raw input. Consequently fair-coordinate feasibility is preserved in both directions.

The raw Markov calculation already proved shows that the only feasible values are `r=2+2t`, with the cosine sign criterion. Equations (A3) and `17+35(2+2t)=87+70t` give (A1).

## 3. Unique markers and one target

Manchester payloads have no constant triple. Every inner delimiter has a constant run of length exactly three, separated from its neighbors by opposite guard bits. Payloads start and end in runs of length one; any merger with a neighboring guard therefore has length at most two. Outer markers have a constant run of exactly four, also separated from the neighboring content by a guard.

Thus the only occurrences of `0000` or `1111` are the first four and last four symbols. Also, the occurrences of a pattern `D_u` are exactly the starts of the complete and terminal frames, at positions

\[
6,\ 6+M,\ldots,\ 6+rM. \tag{A4}
\]

The outer runs do not create a `D_u` occurrence: any triple inside a run of length four has either its left or right neighbor equal to the run symbol. This prevents the required two opposite guards.

The unique outer runs imply that `L_f` is an infix code. As in section 4 of the main note, all accepted words form one class `z` in its finite syntactic semigroup. The two letter classes are distinct by replacing only the first symbol of an accepted word; and `zs=sz=0` for every nonempty-word class `s`.

## 4. Uniform pumping lemma: the syntactic semigroup is aperiodic

Set `K=4M+40=180`. We claim that, for all contexts `x,y`, every nonempty word `v`, and every integer `k>=K`,

\[
xv^k y\in L_f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad xv^{k+1}y\in L_f. \tag{A5}
\]

Here are the details of the only nontrivial implication, starting with an accepted word `W=xv^k y`. Put `m=|v|` and let the repeated region be the `km` symbols of `v^k`.

**Step 1: `M` divides `m`.** Choose a position inside the repeated region at distance at least `2m+M+10` from its left edge. By the spacing (A4), there is a delimiter start within the next `M` positions, as long as this interval is before the final terminal frame. This holds because `k>=4M+40`, even when `x,y` are empty and `m=1`. Both the entire five-symbol delimiter and its translate by `m` lie inside the repeated region, far from either whole-word endpoint. Periodicity of `v^k` makes the translated five-symbol pattern the same `D_u`. Both occurrences are therefore genuine frame starts by section 3. Their difference `m` is an integer multiple of `M` by (A4).

For a numerical margin, one may choose the first candidate position `|x|+2m+M+10`. The delimiter begins no later than `|x|+2m+2M+9`; its translate ends no later than `|x|+3m+2M+13`. This is before the repeated region ends because `(k-3)m>=2M+13`. It is also away from the terminal frame because the repeated region alone has at least `km` symbols.

**Step 2: insert one copy deep inside the repeated region.** Insert a copy of `v` between two central copies. This shifts the following content by the multiple `m` of the complete-frame length `M`. It preserves the fixed prefix and suffix, the unique initial/final runs, and the alignment of all existing delimiters. No new run of length four occurs inside the periodic region: any such run would already have occurred in the accepted original word. The boundary substrings next to `x` and `y` remain identical.

Every new complete-frame code and every new pair of adjacent frame codes already occurs in the periodic region of `W`. This follows because a frame uses `M` symbols, adjacent frames use `2M` symbols, `m` is a positive multiple of `M`, and at least four central copies are available on each side. Any new window of length at most `2M+10` near the insertion seam is a window of the same bi-infinite periodic word `v^Z` and occurs in an old central stretch. The unchanged outer stretches cover all remaining windows.

The entire definition (A2) is checked by these data: the initial marker and first state; valid complete-frame codes; agreement of the next state between adjacent complete frames; and the terminal frame/acceptance test next to the last complete frame. The insertion changes none of the endpoint checks and introduces only already accepted local codes and state transitions. Hence `xv^{k+1}y` belongs to `L_f`.

The reverse implication is the same argument applied to an accepted `xv^{k+1}y`, deleting a central copy. The remaining periodic region still has at least `K` copies, and every newly joined local window already occurred before deletion. This proves (A5).

By definition of syntactic congruence, (A5) with `k=K` says `[v]^K=[v]^{K+1}` for every nonempty word `v`. Every semigroup element is such a class. Thus `s^{180}=s^{181}` for all `s`, proving aperiodicity without importing the star-free language theorem.

## 5. Scope

The already independently checked checker forcing and irrational-rotation calculation are imported internally from the main note. The balanced frame delimiters and the uniform pumping lemma are new here. No quantitative claim about a small multiplication table is made; the finite semigroup remains explicitly constructible from the regular language. This refinement does not replace the independent-review status of the main theorem.
