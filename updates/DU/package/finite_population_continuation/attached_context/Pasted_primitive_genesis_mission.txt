Result: exact positive compilation for all claw-free graphs
I obtained a complete reduction from binary-weighted independent-set counting in arbitrary claw-free graphs to unweighted perfect-matching counting.
The reduction acquires its decomposition from the input graph, computes every local quantity exactly, uses no negative weights, and has output size controlled by the bit lengths of the activities—not their numerical magnitudes.
Combined with the separately published general-perfect-matching approximation theorem, it gives polynomial-time relative counting and approximate sampling for every prescribed independent-set cardinality, including maximum cardinality, with explicitly specified vertices forced in or out. That perfect-matching algorithm is an imported result, not a discovery of this investigation. 
The possible original contribution is the complete, automatically constructed claw-free reduction and its resource bounds. The underlying matching-circuit framework has prior art; historical priority for the complete result remains unestablished.
1. Precise theorem
A graph is claw-free when no vertex has three pairwise nonadjacent neighbours. For a finite claw-free graph \(G=(V,E)\), define
\[
Z_G(\lambda)
=
\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq V\\I\text{ independent}}}
\prod_{v\in I}\lambda_v,
\]
including the empty set with weight one.
The activities are nonnegative rationals
\[
\lambda_v=\frac{p_v}{q_v},\qquad q_v\ge1,
\]
specified in binary. Put
\[
S=
1+|V|+
\sum_{v\in V}
\bigl(\operatorname{bit}(p_v)+\operatorname{bit}(q_v)\bigr).
\]
Theorem A — exact compilation
There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm constructing a simple unweighted graph \(H\) and a positive integer \(\Gamma\) such that
\[
\boxed{\#\operatorname{PM}(H)=\Gamma Z_G(\lambda).}
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
|V(H)|+|E(H)|=O(S),
\qquad
\operatorname{bit}(\Gamma)=O(S).
}
\tag{2}
\]
An ordinary labelled adjacency-list encoding therefore has \(O(S\log(S+2))\) bits. The preprocessing is polynomial in the full input length, including the original graph.
No decomposition, local counting oracle, bounded degree, or bounded numerical activity is assumed.
This is a numerical-input theorem: the constructed graph depends on the specified activities. It is not one fixed symbolic graph representing the partition polynomial for all activities simultaneously.
2. The representation change
I selected this direction after examining sample compression, local-constraint sampling, and combinatorial counting. The promising obstruction was the gap between structural optimization and structural counting: a decomposition that preserves the best solution need not preserve the total weight of all solutions.
Earlier work on weighted independent sets distinguished polynomial dependence on unary weights from dependence on their binary encoding, obtained binary-weighted algorithms under additional odd-hole exclusions, and explicitly questioned whether the available claw-free decomposition supported counting. arXiv
The construction replaces each structural piece by its complete boundary partition table, then realizes that table by a small nonnegative matching gadget. The crucial point is that the gadget topology may change with the table; forcing every table into one topology would introduce negative weights.
The auxiliary matching model
For a graph \(K\), assign nonnegative edge weights \(w_e\) and nonnegative monomer weights \(\mu_v\). Define
\[
M(K)=
\sum_{F\text{ matching}}
\prod_{e\in F}w_e
\prod_{v\notin V(F)}\mu_v.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mu_v=0\) forces \(v\) to be covered.
With two terminals \(t_1,t_2\), a boundary signature records deletion:
\[
F_K(T)=M(K-T),
\qquad T\subseteq\{t_1,t_2\}.
\]
Deleting a terminal represents covering it by an edge to the outside.
Lemma: every nonnegative two-terminal table is realizable
For every
\[
(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{4},
\]
there is a gadget with at most four vertices satisfying
\[
F_K(\varnothing)=a,\quad
F_K(\{t_1\})=b,\quad
F_K(\{t_2\})=c,\quad
F_K(\{t_1,t_2\})=d.
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a complete construction.
When \(d>0\) and \(ad\ge bc\): give the terminals monomers \(c/d,b/d\), join them by an edge of weight
\[
\frac{ad-bc}{d^2},
\]
and add an isolated scalar vertex with monomer \(d\). The full partition is
\[
d\left(\frac{bc}{d^2}+\frac{ad-bc}{d^2}\right)=a,
\]
and the deletion values are \(b,c,d\).
Otherwise, when \(c>0\): use the path \(t_1-u-t_2\), with
\[
\mu_{t_1}=0,\qquad \mu_u=d,\qquad \mu_{t_2}=\frac ac,
\]
\[
w_{t_1u}=c,\qquad
w_{ut_2}=b-\frac{ad}{c}\ge0.
\]
The forced covering of \(t_1\) gives full partition \(a\). Deleting \(t_1\) gives
\[
d\frac ac+b-\frac{ad}{c}=b;
\]
the other deletion values are \(c,d\).
The remaining cases are exact, not limiting constructions. If \(c=0,b>0\), the first case’s failure implies \(d=0\); the symmetric forced-path construction works. If \(b=c=d=0\), give each terminal a private leaf, set all monomers to zero, and give the two private edges weights \(a,1\).
A unary table \((a,b)\) uses a terminal of monomer zero, a leaf of monomer \(b\), and an edge of weight \(a\).
The table \((1,1,1,0)\), arising from the two endpoints of an ordinary graph edge, shows why a single fixed edge-gadget topology is inadequate: \(ad-bc<0\). The path construction removes that obstruction without signed cancellation.
Matching circuits and positive weight simulation are established tools, particularly in McQuillan’s work. Their existence is not claimed as a new primitive here. The question is whether they can be made to cover the entire acquired claw-free decomposition with exact counting and controlled resources. arXiv
3. Exact composition, including the troublesome boundary cases
Suppose vertex-disjoint pieces \(G_j\) each have one or two designated clique extremities. Partition the extremities into groups, and make each group’s union a clique. Call these groups hubs.
Two details cannot be ignored.
When two extremities of the same piece belong to the same hub, merge them into one boundary set and require that it contain at most one selected vertex.
When extremities belonging to different hubs overlap, a selected intersection vertex occupies both hubs, but its activity is multiplied only once.
For piece \(j\), let \(B_{jh}\) be its merged boundary set for hub \(h\). Define
\[
f_j(x)=
\sum_{\substack{
I\text{ independent in }G_j\\
|I\cap B_{jh}|\le1\ \forall h\\
1_{I\cap B_{jh}\ne\varnothing}=x_h\ \forall h
}}
\lambda^I.
\]
There are at most two distinct boundary bits per piece. Directly partitioning global independent sets by their local intersections gives
\[
\boxed{
Z_G(\lambda)
=
\sum_{\substack{
x_j\in\{0,1\}^{r_j}\\
\sum_{j:h\sim j}x_{jh}\le1\ \forall h
}}
\prod_j f_j(x_j).
}
\tag{5}
\]
Now replace every \(f_j\) by its unary or binary gadget. Give each hub one vertex with monomer weight one, and connect it to its incident gadget terminals by unit edges.
A matching can use at most one connecting edge at a hub. Conditional on those connecting edges, each gadget contributes exactly the corresponding deletion-table entry. Summing yields (5).
Therefore the composed independent-set partition is exactly a nonnegative matching partition.
This is not an approximate factorization. Neither repeated boundaries nor overlapping boundaries are discarded.
4. Why the local tables are actually computable
The imported structural theorem is the algorithmic decomposition of Faenza, Oriolo and Stauffer. On a connected claw-free graph, it produces strips whose graphs either have independence number at most three or have clique breadth-first layers from an extremity, apart from explicitly handled exceptional components. The pieces are vertex-disjoint and reconstruct the original graph by the composition above. The decomposition is computable in polynomial time. G-SCOP
The counting step needs its own proof; one cannot simply replace “maximum” by “sum” in an optimization algorithm.
Small-independence pieces
When \(\alpha(G_j)\le3\), enumerate subsets of sizes zero through three, test independence and boundary capacities, and sum their activity products into the appropriate table entry. There are \(O(|V(G_j)|^3)\) candidates.
Clique-layer pieces
Root breadth-first search at an extremity. Every distance layer is a clique, so an independent set chooses either no vertex or one vertex per layer. An edge cannot skip a layer.
Use a dynamic program whose state contains the current layer’s choice and the at-most-two-bit record of occupied hubs. A transition rejects adjacency to the previous choice and reoccupation of a hub, then multiplies by the new activity.
Every independent set has one unique sequence of layer choices. Thus this computes all boundary entries exactly in polynomial time.
Exceptional net-free components
A net is a triangle with three pendant leaves. For a claw- and net-free graph, deleting a closed neighbourhood leaves a claw- and asteroidal-triple-free graph. Each connected residual has a breadth-first root whose layers beyond the first are cliques; claw-freeness limits an independent subset of the first layer to two vertices. These are the structural facts needed for another polynomial layer computation. G-SCOP
One must still avoid double-counting. Fix an order \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) and use the least selected vertex:
\[
\boxed{
Z_G(\lambda)
=
1+
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\lambda_{v_i}
Z_{G[\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_n\}\setminus N(v_i)]}(\lambda).
}
\tag{6}
\]
Every nonempty independent set appears exactly once. Every residual is counted by the preceding layer computation, splitting disconnected components multiplicatively.
There is no recursively branching general independent-set search hidden in (6).
Bit complexity of the tables
For a piece \(U\), all entries have common denominator dividing
\[
D_U=\prod_{v\in U}q_v.
\]
After multiplication by \(D_U\), every entry is an integer bounded by
\[
\prod_{v\in U}(p_v+q_v).
\]
Its bit length is therefore linear in the activity encoding of that piece. The gadget construction uses only constantly many exact rational operations.
Since the pieces partition the vertices, the resulting matching graph has \(O(n)\) vertices and edges and total weight encoding \(O(S)\). No approximate subtraction is used to decide the sign of \(ad-bc\).
5. Removing all numerical weights
Two further constructions complete Theorem A.
Monomers become perfect matchings through unique completion
For every \(m\), construct an unweighted graph \(R_m\) with \(m\) terminals such that
\[
\#\operatorname{PM}(R_m-T)=
\begin{cases}
1,&|T|\equiv m\pmod2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Start with a single terminal. To increase the terminal count by one, remove an old terminal \(t\) from the boundary, add a triangle \(x,a,b\), join \(t\) to \(x\), and make \(a,b\) new terminals.
Whether the joining edge is used is determined uniquely by the two parity conditions. This proves (7) inductively. The construction has \(3m-2\) vertices for \(m\ge1\).
For a monomer-dimer graph on \(m\) vertices, attach each original vertex \(v\) to its corresponding reservoir terminal by an edge of weight \(\mu_v\), then set all monomers to zero.
The unmatched vertices of an original matching must use these attachment edges. Their number has parity \(m\), and the reservoir completes them uniquely. Hence this is a weight-preserving bijection for the full partition function.
It is not a claim that arbitrary mixed-parity boundary signatures survive the lift. All boundaries have already been composed before this operation.
Integers use logarithmically many vertices
For an even-order two-terminal graph, write its full and double-deletion counts as \((A,D)\); either single deletion has count zero.
Connecting two such gadgets in series by an edge between their inner terminals gives
\[
(A,D)\ \text{and}\ (B,E)
\quad\longmapsto\quad
(AB,DE).
\tag{8}
\]
With outer terminals present, parity forces the joining edge to be absent. With both deleted, parity forces it to be present. Adding a fresh terminal leaf at each end swaps \((A,D)\) to \((D,A)\).
A single edge has signature \((1,1)\). A four-cycle with adjacent terminals has signature \((2,1)\). Thus serially appending the four-cycle doubles the first entry. Adding an edge between the new outer terminals then increases that entry by exactly one, because the double-deletion count is one.
Reading the binary digits of \(N\) therefore constructs
\[
(N,1)
\]
using exactly
\[
2+4(\operatorname{bit}(N)-1)
\]
vertices. Combining this with swapping and serial multiplication realizes \((q,p)\) in \(O(\operatorname{bit}p+\operatorname{bit}q)\) vertices and edges.
Replace each positive rational edge \(p/q\) by this gadget, with two unit attachment edges to its original endpoints. An unused original edge contributes \(q\); a used edge contributes \(p\). Exactly one attachment is forbidden by parity.
Consequently,
\[
\#\operatorname{PM}(H)
=
\left(\prod_e q_e\right)Z_{\mathrm{PM}}(K).
\]
Taking \(\Gamma=\prod_e q_e\) proves (1). The total previous weight encoding was \(O(S)\), proving (2).
Polynomial rational-weight simulation already appears in the matching-circuit literature. The explicit binary construction above supplies the stated linear vertex/edge bound without replicating an edge \(p\) times. arXiv
6. The acquired algorithmic capability
Relative counting and ordinary sampling
Apply the imported general-perfect-matching approximation algorithm to \(H\), then divide by \(\Gamma\). Exact positive scaling does not alter relative error. Its stated guarantee is worst-case polynomial bit time, including unsuccessful random tapes. 
For sampling, use
\[
Z_J
=
Z_{J-v}+\lambda_vZ_{J-N[v]}.
\tag{9}
\]
Approximate both branch weights and normalize. There are at most \(n\) decisions. Taking each count’s relative error and failure probability of order \(\eta/n\), and using dyadic coins with \(O(\log(n/\eta))\) bits, gives final total variation at most \(\eta\).
Every output is independent even on a numerical failure: inclusion always deletes the selected vertex’s closed neighbourhood. No unbounded rejection loop is required.
Every prescribed cardinality
Write
\[
P(t)=Z_G(t\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha}a_jt^j,
\qquad
a_j=\sum_{\substack{I\text{ independent}\\|I|=j}}\lambda^I.
\]
The claw-free independence polynomial is real-rooted. Math at Princeton The rational-weight extension follows by conceptually replacing each vertex with a clique of appropriately many true twins after clearing denominators. That possibly enormous graph is used only in the proof, never constructed algorithmically.
Thus the tilted size law
\[
p_t(j)=\frac{a_jt^j}{P(t)}
\]
is log-concave and therefore unimodal. Using large activities and this structure to pass from weighted counting to prescribed cardinality is an established strategy; the contribution here is making its required binary-weighted counting interface available on the whole claw-free class. arXiv
The remaining resource issue is making the target cardinality sufficiently probable.
A target-mass lemma
For any unimodal law \(p_0,\ldots,p_n\) with mean \(\mu\),
\[
|\mu-k|\le\frac14
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{p_k\ge\frac{3}{4(n+1)^2}.}
\tag{10}
\]
If \(k\) is a mode, \(p_k\ge1/(n+1)\). If the mode is to the right, then \(p_j\le p_k\) for \(j\le k\), so
\[
\mu\ge(k+1)\bigl(1-(k+1)p_k\bigr).
\]
If the mode is to the left, similarly
\[
\mu\le k-1+(n-k+1)^2p_k.
\]
Either inequality proves (10).
Tuning requires only polynomially many bits
Delete zero-activity vertices. Suppose positive activity numerators and denominators have at most \(B\) bits. Every positive coefficient obeys
\[
2^{-nB}\le a_j\le2^{n(B+1)}.
\]
Choose
\[
L=2nB+n+2\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil+8.
\]
The mean size at \(t=2^{-L}\) is below \(1/256\); at \(t=2^L\), its deficiency from \(\alpha\) is below \(1/256\). These follow by bounding coefficient ratios and summing at most \(n\) tail terms.
For an interior target, use the entirely rational grid
\[
r=1+\frac1{8n^2},\qquad
t_i=r^i,\qquad
|i|\le16n^2L.
\]
It brackets those endpoint fugacities. Also,
\[
\frac{d\mu}{d\log t}
=
\operatorname{Var}(K)\le\frac{n^2}{4},
\]
so successive grid means differ by at most \(1/32\).
Estimate each mean to accuracy \(1/16\) using approximate Boltzmann samples. With controlled total failure probability, a point whose estimated mean is within \(1/8\) of \(k\) exists, and its true mean is within \(3/16\). Equation (10) then gives target probability
\[
p_t(k)\ge p_*:=\frac{3}{4(n+1)^2}.
\]
The grid has polynomially many points, all with polynomial bit length. It uses no coefficient-ratio oracle or exact real arithmetic.
Now use
\[
\boxed{a_k=P(t)\,\frac{p_t(k)}{t^k}.}
\tag{11}
\]
Estimate \(P(t)\) relatively and estimate \(p_t(k)\) from
\[
O\!\left(n^2\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta)\right)
\]
indicator samples, with the underlying sampler’s total variation chosen \(O(\varepsilon p_*)\). This gives relative approximation to \(a_k\).
For sampling, reject tilted samples unless their size is \(k\). The acceptance probability is inverse-polynomial, so a cap of
\[
O\!\left(n^2\log(1/\eta)\right)
\]
trials suffices. On the rare cap or tuning failure, return a \(k\)-subset of a computed maximum independent set. Every output remains feasible; the fallback probability is included in the total variation bound.
Maximum cardinality is covered by the large-fugacity endpoint. Infeasible cardinalities are detected exactly using the established claw-free optimization algorithm.
Explicit pinnings
If \(A\) is forced in and \(B\) forced out, first check that \(A\cap B=\varnothing\), that \(A\) is independent, and that its activity product is positive. Otherwise the weighted count is zero.
On feasible inputs, delete
\[
A\cup B\cup N(A),
\]
multiply the residual count by \(\lambda^A\), and replace target cardinality \(k\) by \(k-|A|\).
These are exact reductions. Their cost contains no inverse probability of the original pinning event.
7. What has—and has not—been established
The theorem supplies a concrete capability on an entire graph class: relative weighted counting and fixed-cardinality sampling without numerical-weight restrictions, odd-hole exclusions, or a supplied decomposition, using the credited perfect-matching algorithm.
The proof does not establish a new general-perfect-matching algorithm, a fundamentally new matching-circuit framework, or historical priority for the complete claw-free result. Nor does it extend to arbitrary graphs, arbitrary conditioning predicates, or counting maximizers of general binary-encoded additive utilities.
The implementation status is narrower than the theorem. The exact-arithmetic checker passed tests covering 756 binary boundary tables, 240 supplied-strip compositions, all 422 claw- and net-free graph-atlas graphs through seven vertices, and 20 complete supplied-strip reductions to simple unweighted perfect-matching graphs, alongside the integer and parity gadget checks. The full structural decomposer and the imported approximation algorithm were not implemented or independently reverified here. Finite tests support falsification; they do not replace the proof or establish novelty.
