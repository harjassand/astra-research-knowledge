The obstruction is not simply finding a low-energy state. A system can have many equilibria, and its transient dynamics determine which one it reaches. Replacing a long evolution by an optimization problem can therefore return the wrong state.
There are also two independent computational difficulties: a large final time may be supplied using only a few binary digits, and an implicitly defined convex energy may have many active regimes that should not be expanded.
The result addresses both:
\[ \boxed{ \text{Explicit implicit-energy model} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{finite rational trajectory} +\text{an all-future error certificate}. } \]
The representation retains the latent optimization variables, but does not assign artificial dynamics to them.
Let
\[ Y=\{y\in\mathbb R^D:Ay\le b\}, \qquad q(y)=\frac12y^\top Hy+g^\top y, \]
where all entries are rational, \(H=H^\top\succeq0\), \(Y\neq\varnothing\), and \(q\) is bounded below on \(Y\).
Let \(P\) select \(d\) observed coordinates. Define
\[ \boxed{ F(x)=\min_{\substack{y\in Y\\Py=x}}q(y), } \tag{1} \]
with \(F(x)=+\infty\) when the fiber is infeasible.
The supplied input is the matrices defining this quadratic program—not an expanded list of regions of \(F\). The latent dimension and all constraints count toward input size.
For a rational initial state \(x_0\in\operatorname{dom}F\), consider
\[ \dot u(t)+\partial F(u(t))\ni0, \qquad u(0)=x_0. \tag{2} \]
Let \(z_*\) be a minimizer of \(F\), and choose a positive rational radius \(R\) with
\[ \|x_0-z_*\|\le R. \]
A suitable minimizer and radius can be acquired from the supplied quadratic program.
For every rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), there is a deterministic algorithm returning rational times and states
\[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T_*, \qquad v_0=x_0,\ldots,v_N=s, \]
such that the polygonal curve \(v\), extended by \(v(t)=s\) after \(T_*\), satisfies
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{t\ge0}\|v(t)-u(t)\|\le\epsilon R. } \tag{3} \]
Moreover,
\[ s\in\arg\min F, \qquad \boxed{\|s-u_\infty\|\le\epsilon R,} \tag{4} \]
where \(u_\infty\) is the equilibrium selected by the exact flow.
The running time and output length are polynomial in the explicit input bit length and \(\epsilon^{-1}\). This uses the established polynomial-bit algorithm for rational convex quadratic programming as an imported computational primitive. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0041555380900981)
The accuracy dependence is on reciprocal error, not merely the number of accuracy bits. For absolute accuracy \(a\), the charged ratio is \(R/a\). This is not a claim of arbitrary precision at logarithmic cost.
No bound on the number of active-regime changes is assumed.
The proof starts with a classical variational object, then makes its construction and computational cost explicit.
For a candidate path \(v\) starting at \(x_0\), define
\[ \mathcal A_F(v)= \int_0^T \left[F(v(t))+F^*(-\dot v(t))\right]dt +\frac12\|v(T)\|^2-\frac12\|x_0\|^2, \tag{5} \]
where \(F^*(a)=\sup_x(a^\top x-F(x))\).
Fenchel’s inequality makes the corresponding pointwise gap nonnegative. If \(u\) is the true flow, subgradient and Fenchel inequalities give
\[ \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|v-u\|^2 \le F(v)+F^*(-\dot v)+\langle v,\dot v\rangle. \]
Consequently,
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|v(t)-u(t)\|^2 \le2\mathcal A_F(v). } \tag{6} \]
This is the Brézis–Ekeland–Nayroles mechanism. A recent paper by Antil, Kaltenbach and Kirk explicitly develops related duality-based subgradient-flow error identities. The action-error principle is therefore credited prior mathematics, not the proposed invention. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17845)
The next step is to evaluate a rigorous upper bound on this action without explicitly constructing \(F\) or \(F^*\).
On a mesh with \(h_i=t_i-t_{i-1}\), choose feasible latent states \(y_i\in Y\), and put \(x_i=Py_i\). Introduce dual variables \(w_i\in\mathbb R^D\) and \(\lambda_i\ge0\) satisfying
\[ Hw_i+g+A^\top\lambda_i = P^\top\frac{x_{i-1}-x_i}{h_i}. \tag{7} \]
For any \(c\), the relation \(Hw+g+A^\top\lambda=P^\top c\), \(\lambda\ge0\), implies
\[ \begin{aligned} c^\top Py-q(y) &=w^\top Hy-\frac12y^\top Hy+\lambda^\top Ay\\ &\le\frac12w^\top Hw+b^\top\lambda \qquad(y\in Y). \end{aligned} \]
Thus
\[ F^*(c)\le\frac12w^\top Hw+b^\top\lambda. \tag{8} \]
Interpolating feasible latent states remains feasible. Convexity bounds the primal energy along each interpolated segment by its endpoint average. Therefore the entirely rational quantity
\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathcal J={}& \frac12\|x_N\|^2-\frac12\|x_0\|^2\\ &+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_i \left[ \frac{q(y_{i-1})+q(y_i)}2 +\frac12w_i^\top Hw_i+b^\top\lambda_i \right] \end{aligned} } \tag{9} \]
satisfies
\[ 0\le\mathcal A_F(v)\le\mathcal J. \]
Every feasible assignment therefore certifies
\[ \boxed{\sup_{[0,T]}\|v-u\|\le\sqrt{2\mathcal J}.} \tag{10} \]
The verifier does not need to trust that an optimizer found the optimum. Feasibility and the returned objective value are sufficient.
Minimizing \(\mathcal J\) subject to (7), \(Ay_i\le b\), and \(\lambda_i\ge0\) is a convex quadratic program. All quadratic terms have positive-semidefinite Hessians.
For \(N\) time intervals it has
\[ N(2D+m) \]
variables, \(ND\) equalities and \(2Nm\) inequalities, where \(m\) is the number of original inequalities.
No active-region enumeration or conjugate oracle has been introduced. The representation in (1) survives directly into the trajectory certificate.
Let \(m_*=\min F\), and let
\[ E_0=q(y_0)-m_*>0 \]
be an initial energy upper bound from a feasible lift of \(x_0\). Define
\[ a=\frac{R^2}{2E_0}, \qquad h_i=\min\{\eta(a+t_{i-1}),\,T-t_{i-1}\}, \quad 0<\eta<1. \tag{11} \]
Before the final truncation,
\[ t_i=a\bigl((1+\eta)^i-1\bigr). \]
Hence
\[ \boxed{ N= \left\lceil \frac{\log(1+2TE_0/R^2)} {\log(1+\eta)} \right\rceil. } \tag{12} \]
The important question is whether such rapidly increasing steps preserve the actual trajectory.
Consider exact proximal states
\[ x_i= \arg\min_x \left[ F(x)+\frac{\|x-x_{i-1}\|^2}{2h_i} \right]. \tag{13} \]
Each proximal problem is itself a convex quadratic program in the supplied latent variables. Its optimality conditions provide feasible dual variables in (7).
Set \(e_i=F(x_i)-m_*\), with \(e_0=E_0\). The subgradient inequality against \(z_*\) gives
\[ 2h_i e_i \le \|x_{i-1}-z_*\|^2-\|x_i-z_*\|^2 -\|x_i-x_{i-1}\|^2. \]
Therefore
\[ \sum_i h_i e_i\le\frac{R^2}{2}. \tag{14} \]
For this witness, substitution into the global objective yields the exact identity
\[ \mathcal J= \frac12\sum_i \left[ h_i(e_{i-1}-e_i)-\|x_i-x_{i-1}\|^2 \right]. \tag{15} \]
The energy decreases. Using the mesh definition and summation by parts,
\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_i h_i(e_{i-1}-e_i) &\le \eta a e_0+ \eta\sum_i t_{i-1}(e_{i-1}-e_i)\\ &\le \eta a e_0+\eta\sum_i h_i e_i\\ &\le \eta R^2. \end{aligned} \]
Thus the global quadratic program has a feasible point satisfying
\[ \boxed{\mathcal J\le\frac{\eta R^2}{2}.} \tag{16} \]
Taking \(\eta\) proportional to \(\epsilon^2\) supplies the required trajectory accuracy with
\[ N=O\!\left( 1+\epsilon^{-2}\log(1+2TE_0/R^2) \right) \]

Let \(y_*\) and \(\lambda_*\ge0\) be rational global optimality witnesses:

Both terms are nonnegative. Therefore the complete latent minimizer set is the rational polyhedron
\[ Y_*= \left\{ y: Ay\le b,\; H(y-y_*)=0,\; \lambda_*^\top(b-Ay)=0 \right\}. \tag{18} \]
Its observed projection is \(S=\arg\min F\). That projected polyhedron need not be expanded.
Write (18) as inequalities \(By\le c\), using both signs for equalities. Clear denominators with an integer \(D_0\), and set
\[ U=\max\!\left(1,\max_{ij}|(D_0B)_{ij}|\right), \qquad K=D_0(DU)^{D-1}. \]
Then
\[ \operatorname{dist}(y,Y_*) \le K\|(By-c)_+\|_2. \tag{19} \]
This is a deliberately loose Hoffman bound, not a claim to compute the optimal constant. Hoffman’s error-bound principle is established prior work. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/nist-research-library/journal-research-volume-49)
For completeness, the explicit constant follows from integer linear algebra. Every independent row submatrix of \(D_0B\) has a positive integer Gram determinant, hence determinant at least one. Its largest singular value is at most \(DU\), which bounds its smallest singular value below by \((DU)^{-(D-1)}\). Applying this to independent active normals at the nearest point proves (19).
Let \(\overline H=\sum_{ij}|H_{ij}|\). Equation (17) then gives

The numerical value of \(C\) may be enormous. Its binary encoding length is polynomial, and its ingredients are acquired from the original rational quadratic program.

Construct a finite-horizon candidate with error at most \(\epsilon R/4\), and project its terminal state \(v_N\) onto \(S\). This projection is another convex quadratic program, performed in the latent representation (18). Call the result \(s\). Then
\[ \|v_N-s\|\le\frac{\epsilon R}{2}. \]
Replace only the last endpoint of the polygonal candidate by \(s\), and continue constantly thereafter. The modification costs at most \(\epsilon R/2\) in state error.
For every later time, distance to the fixed minimizer \(s\) is nonincreasing. Thus
\[ \|u(t)-s\| \le \|u(T_*)-s\| \le\frac{3\epsilon R}{4}, \qquad t\ge T_*. \]
This proves the all-future theorem.
Since only \(\log T_*\) enters the mesh size, and \(\log C\) is polynomially bounded in the input encoding, the complete construction is polynomial in input length and reciprocal accuracy.

The input representation accommodates quadratic energies, unilateral linear constraints, threshold penalties, and internal variables eliminated through convex optimization. The mathematical pathway is to certify long-time behavior of an already validated convex relaxation model without resolving every time scale or expanding every active regime.
However, acquiring a physically faithful energy model and controlling spatial discretization error are separate tasks. The theorem does not establish that a given material, circuit, or continuum equation is adequately described by such a model.
Three attempted extensions expose the remaining obstruction.
Latent variables cannot simply be given their own dynamics. For
\[ F(x)=\min_z\left[\frac12(x-z)^2+\frac12z^2\right]=\frac{x^2}{4}, \]
the observed flow is \(x(t)=e^{-t/2}\) from \(x_0=1\). Joint gradient descent in \(x,z\), even initialized at the minimizing \(z=1/2\), has a different second derivative and therefore a different trajectory. The primal–dual construction avoids this representation failure.
An unknown future input still has to be acquired. For \(x'=u(t)\), where \(u\) is either zero or a unit pulse in one of \(N\) intervals, distinguishing terminal state zero from one requires \(\Omega(N)\) interval queries under that access model. Bounded total input or work does not eliminate the query cost. Supplying the pulse location explicitly would be a different input model.
Persistent oscillation requires a different representation. A rotation can maintain unit norm forever; an eventually constant trajectory cannot approximate it. This does not prove rotations are hard to simulate—their matrix-exponential representation is easy. It shows that dissipation-based time allocation and a constant tail cannot simply be transferred to phase-sensitive dynamics.
Similarly, small multiplicative changes in a nonconvex energy can change which branch a trajectory follows. A small energy discrepancy is not, by itself, a reliable dynamical error measure outside the convex setting.
The completed mathematical consequence is an end-to-end, finite-bit approximation and certification theorem for the entire future of implicitly represented convex relaxation systems. It preserves dynamical equilibrium selection and yields all-time, margin-based observable verification.
The unresolved escalation is a comparably acquired representation that preserves oscillatory phase, nonconvex branch selection, and controlled responses without hiding their computation in an oracle. That is necessary before this approach could support claims about substantially broader technological systems.
