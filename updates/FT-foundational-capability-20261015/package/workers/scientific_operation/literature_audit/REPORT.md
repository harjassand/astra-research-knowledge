# Targeted literature audit: saturable-loss bucket feedback

## Finding

Closest papers establish both absorption-saturation focusing and focusing from scalar nonlinear buckets. I found no primary paper in this targeted search using the specific low/high-power, launched-power-normalized total-loss decrease of a homogeneous saturable absorber as the optimization bucket. This is a scoped observation, not a novelty claim.

## Exact overlaps

- **Absorption nonlinearity and an extended absorber layer:** Cui, Kahraman & Wang, “[Optical focusing into scattering media via iterative time reversal guided by absorption nonlinearity](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63095-w),” *Nature Communications* 16, 7807 (2025), show speckle-scale focusing with ground-state-depletion eosin/gelatin between diffusers. Their feedback is **complex backscatter measured by four-step digital holography**: scaled low/high fields are subtracted and phase-conjugated. This establishes absorber focusing, not scalar total-loss feedback. Their apparatus uses an SLM, camera/reference path, AOMs and monitors; the loop takes about 14 s/iteration. Eosin saturation intensity is near 0.6 W cm⁻², with photobleaching and oxygen-quenching limits.
- **Scalar nonlinear bucket and focusing:** Katz, Small, Guan & Silberberg, “[Noninvasive nonlinear focusing and imaging through strongly scattering turbid layers](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.1.000170),” *Optica* 1, 170–174 (2014), maximize epi-detected total 2PF with one integrating detector and a genetic algorithm, without image feedback. A thin homogeneous fluorescent screen behind a diffuser forms a diffraction-limited focus; a second demonstration focuses through 1 mm brain tissue onto crystallites. This is scalar nonlinear-bucket focusing, with emitted 2PF rather than normalized absorption loss. For thick homogeneous 3D 2PF, total signal may be waist-independent, so target-plane geometry matters. The planar bucket rose only about 50% despite higher peak intensity.
- **General theory and scalar feedback test:** Osnabrugge, Amitonova & Vellekoop, “[Blind focusing through strongly scattering media using wavefront shaping with nonlinear feedback](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.27.011673),” *Optics Express* 27, 11673–11688 (2019); [author-hosted full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.02718v1). They define \(S=\sum_b |E_b|^{2n}\) and analyze when summed nonlinear feedback can favor a bright target. Their camera records target-plane speckle; software sums pixel intensities to orders 1–3, explicitly to mimic multiphoton fluorescence. This is not a physical nonlinear absorber or detector. The theory and experiments show that too many competing targets relative to controlled SLM modes can prevent a focus.
- **Nonlinear acoustic bucket:** Lai et al., “[Photoacoustically guided wavefront shaping for enhanced optical focusing in scattering media](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphoton.2014.322),” *Nature Photonics* 9, 126–132 (2015), use dual-pulse Grüneisen-relaxation nonlinearity and detected PA amplitude to focus to one 5–7 μm speckle grain, about 10× below the acoustic focus. This requires a pulsed source, ultrasound transducer and iterative SLM optimization; their nonlinear run took about eight hours (1,600 iterations, 15 measurements per iteration, roughly 1.2 s/measurement). It is a strong feedback precedent with added acoustic hardware and localized acoustic sensitivity.

## Mathematical and physical gate

For \(q>0\), \(f(x)=q x^2/(1+qx)\) has \(f''(x)=2q/(1+qx)^3>0\). Hence \(D=\beta\sum_j f(I_j)\) is strictly Schur-convex: **at fixed delivered target-plane sum** \(\sum_j I_j\), concentrating the intensities increases \(D\), with a one-hot vector the maximum over the unconstrained nonnegative simplex. This does not show that a finite, phase-only modulator can realize that vector, or that an optimizer reaches it. The 2019 analysis is the relevant reminder that control fidelity and the number of contributing speckle modes matter.

Normalizing by launched power does not itself fix delivered target-plane power. A real scattering sample can change transmission/reflection and collected delivery with the input wavefront; the bucket may then reward open transmission channels as well as concentration. The proposed equivalence needs the stated fixed-delivery condition or a way to separate delivered power from absorption. “Complete output” must capture all relevant transmitted and reflected modes (or otherwise close the energy balance); finite numerical aperture, polarization loss, clipping, parasitic absorption, or detector-efficiency drift make missing light indistinguishable from target absorption. In a multiple-scattering resonator or absorbing volume, saturation can also change the internal field distribution, invalidating the fixed linear-speckle map used in the derivation.

The metric contrast is largest when the controlled speckle intensities sample the absorber’s saturation knee. If all are far below saturation, the differential loss is weak; if all are deeply saturated, \(f(x)\) approaches a linear function and the relative advantage of concentration becomes small. Low/high subtraction of two large bucket readings therefore needs stable launched-power calibration and a reversible absorber response.

## Hardware comparison

The proposed readout could avoid reference-beam interferometry and a camera if a high-SNR, calibrated integrating power detector captures the full output. It still needs SLM wavefront control, repeated metric measurements, power switching/monitoring, and output collection. These are design inferences; no measurement-time or mode-count benchmark for this particular loss bucket was found.

**Assessment:** the proposed metric is a narrower detector/feedback variant with a plausible convexity argument under the stated conditions. The decisive experimental gate is whether its normalized loss change can be measured with enough SNR and separated from delivery and collection changes. No priority conclusion follows from this search.
