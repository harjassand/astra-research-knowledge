# Isotope-selective graphene knock-on: adversarial physical audit

**Status:** the isotope-selective *ejection* effect is experimentally measured; selective isotope *exchange/enrichment* is not demonstrated by the cited evidence. Physical demo and novelty remain **UNKNOWN**.

## Finding

Susi et al. measured a large isotope dependence in the displacement cross section of initially clean graphene. At 95 kV the measured point estimates are 56.6 mb for 12C and 2.1 mb for 13C, about a 27:1 point-estimate ratio. Supplementary Table 1 reports asymmetric standard errors for these cross sections (56.6 +24.0/−16.7 mb and 2.1 +1.0/−0.7 mb), not 95% confidence intervals. The 95% confidence intervals for the Poisson means shown in Supplementary Figure 2 are a distinct uncertainty report; I have not propagated them jointly into uncertainty on the cross sections or their ratio. The point estimates support preferential knock-on of 12C from a pristine lattice.

The same paper's example shows a vacant lattice site in one 2.2-second STEM frame and an occupied site in the next. It does not identify the isotope or history of the atom at the reoccupied site, establish a controlled carbon feed, or report the fraction of vacancies repaired from a reservoir. The measurements establish ejection statistics and one rapid occupancy recovery; they do not establish a repeated exchange cycle.

The strongest countermodel consistent with the evidence is that most apparent recoveries are self-return or local rearrangement of beam-generated carbon, while some vacancies remain, capture uncontrolled carbonaceous adsorbates, or become part of larger defects. Self-return gives no isotope exchange. Local rearrangement conserves isotope count in the processed lattice and can smear a spatial pattern. An external feed can produce exchange, but its isotope composition, arrival rate, return fraction, and effect on lattice quality are currently unmeasured in this process.

## Explicit countermodel

Represent one lattice site as 12C or 13C. A beam hit ejects the occupied atom with pristine-lattice rates proportional to the measured σ12 and σ13. Afterward, three outcomes are allowed:

1. The displaced atom returns to the same site. This restores occupancy with no isotope change.
2. A nearby lattice atom or adatom fills the vacancy. This can move isotope labels locally, but conserves isotope counts apart from any atoms the beam ejects from the processed sheet; defect motion and random exchange can erase a written pattern. If the beam simply removes more 12C than 13C and no carbon returns, the remaining material can become isotope-enriched by selective etching, but the operation leaves vacancies or other damage rather than demonstrating occupancy-preserving exchange.
3. A carbon atom from an external reservoir fills the site. This can change the patch's isotope ratio, but only if the atom does not simply return, the reservoir has a known isotope fraction and adequate flux, and the new site returns to the desired bonding topology.

This model reproduces all reported observations: a 12C site is more likely to be displaced; a vacancy can be visible in one frame and occupied in the next; adsorbates can supply replacement carbon; and sustained irradiation can grow or rearrange defects. It does not produce verified exchange because the observed refill's source and isotope are latent. Without external carbon feed, the only way to change the isotope fraction of the remaining material is isotope-selective loss (which must be counted as damage/material removal); local rearrangement alone cannot enrich it.

An idealized reservoir model shows why the proposal is not ruled out in principle. Let q be the 13C fraction in a large external carbon reservoir and let R = k12/k13 be the measured ejection-rate ratio. If every ejection is followed by perfect refill from that reservoir, with each filled site again behaving like pristine graphene, the two-state exchange process has stationary 13C fraction

\[
x_* = \frac{qR}{qR+(1-q)}.
\]

For the natural abundance q = 1.109% used in the 2016 paper and the 95 kV point ratio R ≈ 27, this idealization gives point-model output x* ≈ 23%. At 90 kV, R ≈ 5.45 gives about 5.8%. These are model outputs, not measured enrichment or uncertainty intervals; no uncertainty propagation is performed. The model assumes a large, well-mixed external bath, no isotope-dependent return, immediate healing, no byproduct accumulation, pristine cross sections after each cycle, and zero structural cost. Each assumption is a physical gate. With a 99% 13C reservoir, a 12C lattice could in principle be driven toward 13C while the lower 13C ejection rate protects incorporated atoms; that is a viable controlled-feed mutation of the proposal, not evidence for the stated natural-reservoir operation.

## Decisive small experiment

Run a source-labelled, event-level repair test before pursuing a large enrichment campaign:

1. Prepare a clean, suspended 12C graphene region over a hole and introduce a sparse, measured 13C-rich carbon reservoir beside the target region. A label opposite to the starting lattice makes a new-source repair distinguishable from a returning 12C atom. Record the reservoir's isotope composition and the initial lattice topology.
2. At 95 kV, expose matched 1 × 1 nm² fields and collect the same 2.2 s/frame ADF time series used in the Susi displacement study. Stop after the first unambiguous vacancy/occupancy-recovery event in each field. Include no-source, source-present, and source-isotope-swapped controls under matched beam dose and vacuum conditions.
3. After four source-labelled occupancy-recovery events have formed a small local cluster, switch to a calibrated 60 kV vibrational-EELS protocol and map that cluster. First calibrate isotope contrast, registration, signal-to-noise, and structural change under the readout on adjacent known 12C/13C domains. The demonstrated 0.35 nm pixel averages about four atoms, uses 34 pA for 0.5 s, and therefore costs about 1.1 × 10⁸ electrons per pixel; it does not resolve the isotope of one repaired atom. Record before/after defect topology and test whether the EELS scan itself moves or changes defects.
4. Repeat enough matched events to estimate source-labelled incorporation and persistent damage, then compare the isotope fraction before and after over the entire irradiated patch. Preserve atom counts and source mass balance so that an apparent local enrichment cannot be explained by unmeasured carbon deposition, material loss, or movement into the support.

**Pass condition:** repaired sites acquire the deliberately supplied isotope at a rate above the no-source/background control; the isotope contrast survives the calibrated readout; the site has the intended bonding topology; and a measurable patch-level isotope shift is accompanied by bounded vacancy/coalescence damage and a closed carbon/isotope balance. **Fail condition:** occupancy recovery occurs without source-labelled incorporation, source-swapped controls give the same result, the isotope readout cannot resolve the repaired region at tolerable dose, or defect accumulation outpaces completed repairs.

This experiment separates the main alternatives with few events. It directly tests the missing conjunction—preferential ejection, identified new replacement, and restored lattice—and gives a readout-dose and damage-per-cycle estimate. A successful result would still establish only a local isotope-exchange demonstration; practical scaling and novelty would remain separate questions.

## Gate disposition

- **Measured:** isotope-dependent displacement from pristine graphene; one next-frame occupancy recovery; isotope-sensitive vibrational EELS; beam-driven damage and carbon restructuring in graphene.
- **Unmeasured:** isotope identity and provenance of the 2016 refill; same-atom return probability; controlled reservoir flux and composition in the operation; isotope exchange per completed cycle; one-atom post-repair readout dose; fraction of occupied sites that are pristine six-membered lattice; damage per useful exchange.
- **Disposition:** keep the proposal as a physically plausible conditional mutation. Do not promote “selective isotope enrichment” or “pattern rewriting” to demonstrated capability until the source-labelled repair test passes. Novelty and physical demonstration remain **UNKNOWN**.
