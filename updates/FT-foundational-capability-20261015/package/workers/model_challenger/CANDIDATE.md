# Early candidate — coupler-inclusive continuous erasure monitor

**Status:** candidate for a falsifiable experiment; not an achieved result or Astra novelty claim.

A dual-rail cavity qubit stores one photon across two modes, so ordinary photon loss maps either logical basis state to the shared vacuum state and can be output as a known erasure. The 2026 SWS CZ uses a transmon coupler as a temporary third rail. If the photon is lost while that coupler–target cross-Kerr is active, the partner qubit receives an unknown partial controlled phase. A late erasure flag identifies which gate failed but does not say when the phase stopped accumulating.

**Mutation:** extend a logical-state-blind, continuously monitored number measurement to the complete active control rail, including the temporary coupler, and feed its analog time record into a loss-aware decoder. Required dispersive response: identical for the control photon in either cavity or in the temporary coupler, and identical for the target's two logical rails; distinct for the zero-occupancy erased sector. This should flag and time-localize a loss during the SWS interaction, allowing a conditional phase correction or a decoder to condition on the partial-CZ angle. The SWS gate remains about 500 ns in the demonstrated design.

**Evidence basis:** Mehta et al. demonstrated SWS in dual-rail microwave cavities (Nature, 5 Aug 2026), with ~0.53% erasure per CZ, ~0.1% postselected residual gate error, and bit flips bounded around 10^-6. They measured a loss-conditioned target dephasing channel and simulated delayed end-of-round checks under a simplified erasure model. Hung et al. demonstrated symmetric-resonator continuous erasure monitoring during 24 ns single-qubit gates in a *transmon* dual-rail device (arXiv:2604.16292, 17 Apr 2026), but not during a cavity SWS two-qubit gate. These are separate platforms/results.

**Kill criteria:** kill the timing-correction path if (i) monitoring distinguishes logical rail/coupler paths enough to add >10^-3 residual logical error per SWS gate; (ii) it raises the erasure rate enough that full-circuit survival/code performance is worse than delayed checks; (iii) detected-event time uncertainty exceeds ~8 ns at χ/2π=1.51 MHz for a 10^-3 small-angle correction budget; or (iv) common-resonator backaction creates target or control errors that erase the measured erasure bias. The 8 ns figure is a first-order estimate from δφ=χδt and infidelity ≈δφ²/6, not a demonstrated readout capability.

**Fast falsification experiment:** first simulate the measured SWS correlated loss channel with realistic check and idle errors against the published delayed-check path. If no full-circuit limitation remains, kill the monitor work. Otherwise, prepare all 16 two-dual-rail computational basis states and six single-qubit superpositions; run the SWS gate with injected/conditioned loss across the interaction window; compare ordinary end-of-gate erasure flag, continuous monitor record, and monitor-conditioned target process tomography. Measure event-time posterior width, missed/false flags, extra loss, rail dephasing, and residual target channel. Continue only if loss-time information reduces the conditional target error after correction/decoder without increasing total cost beyond the saved check overhead.
