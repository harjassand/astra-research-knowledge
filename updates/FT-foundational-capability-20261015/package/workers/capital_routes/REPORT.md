## 1. N702 — signed electrostatic trace measurement for closed-void acceptance

**Potential operation.** Use a four-sign field sequence on one held, fixed-orientation conductive particle to extract its polarizability tensor contribution, then use a covered upper bound on $R=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)/m$ to accept it against a closed-void threshold. A particle that fails the acceptance cutoff is unresolved by this test; a high response is not a porosity diagnosis. The unexpected part is the two-stage certificate: an exterior, shape-independent trace lower bound becomes a mass/void upper certificate, while a signed field projection removes finite-body multipole terms exactly under its affine-field model. This could yield a single-particle quality-control measurement without reconstructing the particle surface. The scope is shielded *closed* cavities in a connected conductor; it says nothing about open pores or general print defects.

## 2. N610 — exact local sampler for finite-depth layered random systems

**Potential operation.** For a supplied finite-field stochastic network whose same-level updates are affine and whose nonlinear forcing uses only strictly lower levels, sample the exact *joint* stationary law of a chosen finite batch of coordinates at specified times by querying only the backward dependency structure. The source theorem's expected number of newly revealed space-time categories is

\[
\mathbb E Q\le \frac{m}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}(1+kc^2/s)^j,
\]

so at fixed depth (c), the stated expected computation depends polynomially on the requested batch, parent bound, reset probability, field size, and acquired forcing descriptions, rather than the total number (N) of sites. The key move is *distributional* masking: a newly exposed unknown term inside an already uniform Haar span can be discarded without pathwise coupling. This makes an exact local-output simulator a possible primitive for sparse-observation studies of layered noisy finite-field systems.

## 3. N739 — one-port exact-null certificate with a tolerant lower-bound boundary

**Potential operation.** Given a known candidate $K=\sum_x n_x$, a fresh vacuum outside a controlled port, and an initial one-excitation superposition at that port, use a short real-time evolution and one-qubit $X$ readout to issue a one-sided certificate of local trajectory agreement. If $p$ is the minus-outcome probability after undoing the candidate phase at time $h\le1/2$, the source proves

\[
E_T(H)\le \min\{1,(2T/h)\sqrt p\}.
\]

Observing no minus outcome in the stated number of shots gives an upper certificate with error probability $\alpha$. The same access model contains connected, reflected, exactly isospectral Hamiltonians for which the one-port local trajectory is either close or far; at bounded native time $\tau$, tolerant certification needs at least

\[
N\ge \frac{(2R+1)(R!)^2}{144\tau^{2R+1}},\qquad R=d-r,\quad 0<\tau\le R/2,
\]

in the source's chain construction, for $d\ge1$, $0<\eta\le1/8$, $0\le r<d$, and a tolerant test that accepts every $E_T\le\eta$ and rejects every $E_T\ge3/4$ with probability at least $2/3$. The striking transfer is therefore a *decision-interface* result: a very small exact-null certificate can be useful, but it cannot be advertised as a robust tolerant simulator or model selector.

## Selection judgment

For a physical measurement lead, N702 is the clearest operation because it maps an actual signed field/readout sequence to a conditional particle-acceptance statement with a blinded ground-truth test. For a genuinely different computational lead, N610 is the strongest candidate, but it first needs independent proof reconstruction and same-interface perfect-sampler comparison. N739 is valuable as an exact-versus-tolerant capability boundary and a candidate verifier, with no claim of robust approximate simulation. These should remain three separate work plans because they require different acquired interfaces and decisive tests.
