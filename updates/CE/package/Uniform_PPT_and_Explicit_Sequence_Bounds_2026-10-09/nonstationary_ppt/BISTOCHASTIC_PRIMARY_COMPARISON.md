# Narrow primary comparison for arbitrary bistochastic PPT sequences

9 October 2026. Statement-level comparison for BISTOCHASTIC_VARYING_WORD_THEOREM.md. This is not an exhaustive priority search or an external proof certification.

## What the candidate says

For each fixed complex dimension d, one finite word length suffices for every serial sequence of bistochastic PPT maps on M_d. Each factor may vary independently. The proof combines a finite moving-support boundary window, semialgebraic vanishing orders, a largest-margin anchor usable from either side, and strictly positive product spans. It does not assert a two-step bound or cover nonunital factors.

## Established components and nearby results

Rahaman, Jaques and Paulsen, arXiv:1801.05542v2 (11 June 2018), Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.1, already establish the PPT splitting associated with projections transported to projections by a unital map. That structural input is credited. Their Theorem 4.4 proves eventual EB under repetition of one unital channel; it does not state the universal arbitrary-sequence conclusion above. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1801.05542v2 .

Hanson, Rouze and Stilck Franca, arXiv:1902.08173v2, Theorem 3.14, extend eventual EB to faithful PPT channels and appropriate faithful CP maps. Their stated discrete index is an equal-map iteration index. This is a nearby equal-map result, not the arbitrary-word theorem asserted by the candidate. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.08173v2 .

Davidson, Marcoux and Radjavi, arXiv:0706.2449v1, Theorem 4.9, provide the exact operator-space product-transitivity input. Applying it to Kraus spaces gives full Choi rank after d strictly output-positive factors. The candidate uses this theorem directly and makes no novelty claim for that bridge. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/0706.2449v1 .

Park, arXiv:2608.13551v2 (17 August 2026), treats eventual equal-map EB in full CP scope and gives compositional bounds under low-entanglement-dimensionality assumptions. The inspected statements in Sections 1.1–1.2 distinguish those assumptions from unrestricted PPT input. Those statements do not give the arbitrary bistochastic word bound claimed here. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13551v2 .

Targeted searches for uniform arbitrary bistochastic PPT compositions did not identify a matching primary theorem during this pass. This negative search result is not proof of novelty. No present-tense claim about the overall status of the PPT-square conjecture is needed for the candidate or made here.
