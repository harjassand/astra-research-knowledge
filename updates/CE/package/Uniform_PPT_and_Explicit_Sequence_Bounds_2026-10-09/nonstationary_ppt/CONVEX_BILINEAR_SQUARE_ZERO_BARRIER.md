# Why the interval example cannot be copied into a convex bilinear square zero model

9 October 2026. A short structural barrier for the active nonstationary gate. This is an elementary observation, not a new quantum theorem or priority claim.

Let S be a convex subset of an associative real or complex algebra whose multiplication is bilinear. Suppose x^2=0 for every x in S and S is multiplicatively closed. Then every product of three elements of S is zero.

Indeed, for a,b in S the midpoint lies in S, so

 0=4((a+b)/2)^2=a^2+ab+ba+b^2=ab+ba.

Thus any two elements of S anticommute. For a,b,c in S, closure gives ab in S, whence

 (ab)c=-c(ab).

But two uses of pairwise anticommutation give

 c(ab)=(ca)b=(-ac)b=-a(cb)=a(bc)=(ab)c.

Therefore 2abc=0, proving the claim.

The compact convex interval-label semigroup in COMPACT_SEMIALGEBRAIC_NIL_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md has nonzero products of every length. Consequently its multiplication cannot be bilinear in its displayed convex coordinates, nor can it have an injective semigroup representation whose image is convex in any associative real or complex algebra. This independently identifies where its max/rational product formula matters.

This argument does not settle the PPT question. Collapsing the EB ideal is a Rees quotient, not a linear quotient algebra. The EB cone is not a face of the CP cone and not a linear ideal, so one cannot transfer the midpoint or anticommutation argument to that quotient. Nor does this observation rule out a nonconvex matrix representation or a higher-index construction without further reasoning.
