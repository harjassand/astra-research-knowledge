# Explicit finite-bit version of the compressed clock

This closes the computational-model gap in the ideal-real construction. It is an algorithm specification and proof, not a claim that the floating-point prototype implements it.

## Theorem

For rational input p_i in (0,1), lambda_i>0, and rational epsilon,delta in (0,1/2), a randomized Turing algorithm outputs a nonnegative rational Hhat that can be coupled to the stationary product-chain first-hitting time H with

    P(|Hhat-H|>epsilon H)<=delta.

Expected bit complexity is polynomial in the total input bit length, n, epsilon^-1, and log(delta^-1). It does not depend polynomially on q^-1 or on numerical rate ratios; those enter through input bit lengths. No real-number or large-parameter Poisson oracle is assumed.

All rational arithmetic below may be done exactly. Its intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in the number of bounded operations and input lengths. Standard power-series/range-reduction algorithms evaluate exp and log to a specified absolute error in polynomial bit time at the bounded arguments/precisions used here.

## Constants and finite draw budget

Use the strata, q,w_i,lambda_i,kappa_i from the main proof. Let

    h=epsilon/16,
    ell=ceil(log_2(1024 n/delta)),
    R_i=M_i=ceil(6 kappa_i h^-2 ell),
    K_i=12 R_i+ell+1,
    Rsum=sum_i R_i,
    J=1+sum_i(R_i+K_i),
    rho=min(epsilon/64,delta/(128 Rsum)),
    tau=min(rho,epsilon/32),
    a=delta/(64 J),
    b=ceil(log_2(16/(tau a^2))).

The ceiling logarithms need no transcendental comparison: find the integer using comparisons to powers of two. J is a deterministic upper bound on all exponentials generated by the finite algorithm. Exact rational Bernoulli draws may use rejection from a power-of-two integer range, with expected fewer than two attempts.

## Approximating unit exponentials

For each generated exponential, draw b random bits, let U_b be the corresponding dyadic interval midpoint, and compute a rational approximation Etilde to -log(U_b) with absolute error <=tau a/16, then clip a negative computed value to zero. This clipping cannot occur on the good event below and keeps all outputs nonnegative even on a failure event.

Couple U_b to an ideal U uniform in that interval. On U in [a,1-a], E=-log U>=a and U_b>=a/2. The midpoint discrepancy is <=2^-b/2, so

    |log U_b-log U|<=2|U_b-U|/a<=tau a/16.

Together with numerical log error this gives |Etilde-E|/E<=tau/8<tau. The union probability that any of at most J generated uniforms falls outside [a,1-a] is <=2aJ=delta/32. It does not matter that the number of draws is adaptive: expose a fixed list of J independent uniforms and use its prefix.

Set Wtilde=Etilde_0/q. Its exact latent comparison variable is W=E_0/q, so |Wtilde-W|<=rho W on the good event. All subsequent increment exponentials are Etilde/Lambda_i, with exact rational Lambda_i; their relative error is at most epsilon/32.

## Branches with slack

Compute rtilde_i=w_i Wtilde exactly as a rational.

- If rtilde_i>=2R_i, take the large branch and output rtilde_i times the mean of M_i finitely approximated increments.
- Otherwise, take the small branch. Its simulated Poisson mean is rtilde_i<2R_i.

On the W-good event, rho<1/2. A large branch has true r_i=w_iW>=R_i; a small branch has r_i<=4R_i. Thus no unstable exact-real threshold decision is required, and every branch is valid with slack.

## Finite small-Poisson generator

For r=rtilde_i<2R_i, compute p_k=exp(-r)r^k/k! for 0<=k<K_i to absolute error <=zeta=delta/(4096 n K_i), clip negative approximations to zero, normalize their rational sum, and sample the resulting finite rational categorical distribution. There is no category >=K_i.

The omitted Poisson tail is bounded by

    P(Poisson(r)>=K_i)<=(e r/K_i)^K_i<=2^-K_i<=delta/(1024n),

because K_i>=12R_i>2e r. Rounding, clipping, and normalization change total variation by at most 2K_i zeta plus twice the omitted tail, which is <delta/(256n). (This bound deliberately includes slack.)

The probability table is computable in polynomial bit time. For example, approximate p_0=exp(-r) to absolute error at most zeta/[ (K_i+1) max(1,2R_i)^K_i ], then use exact rational multiplication by r/k. This requires only O(K_i log(R_i+1)+log(nK_i/delta)) extra precision bits. The exponent r is bounded by 2R_i, itself polynomial in the accuracy parameters. The denominators of all rational table entries and their sum also have polynomial bit length. Exact categorical sampling follows by scaling to integer weights and power-of-two rejection.

Couple this finite count to an exact Poisson(rtilde_i) count with failure probability <delta/(256n). Across all strata this costs less than delta/256.

## Coupling small counts across W rounding

Conditional on W,Wtilde, couple Poisson(r_i) and Poisson(rtilde_i) by evaluating one unit-rate Poisson process at those two endpoints. The probability that the counts differ is at most |r_i-rtilde_i|. In a small branch,

    |r_i-rtilde_i|<=rho r_i<=4rho R_i.

The total mismatch probability over all small branches is at most 4rho Rsum<=delta/32. When counts agree, use the same exact stratum rates and ideal unit exponentials for the true and approximated increments. Their sums differ only by the stated relative exponential error.

## Large-stratum concentration and total error

For each large branch, true intensity r_i>=R_i. The two concentration failures from the main proof (true compound-Poisson sum around r_i mu_i, and the M_i-increment sample mean around mu_i) each have probability at most

    2 exp(-ell)<=delta/(512n).

Their union over all strata is at most delta/256. On their complement, the unrounded large output A_i=rtilde_i Vbar_i obeys

    |A_i-S_i|/S_i <=[2h+rho(1+h)]/(1-h) < epsilon/4.

Replacing the sample increments by their finite exponential approximations changes A_i by relative at most epsilon/32. Consequently the total relative large-stratum error is less than epsilon/2. Small-stratum relative error is at most epsilon/32 whenever counts couple. Summing preserves the epsilon bound.

The union of all failure events is bounded by

    delta/32 (uniform endpoints)
    +delta/32 (small counts after W rounding)
    +delta/256 (finite Poisson tables)
    +delta/256 (large-stratum concentration)
    <delta.

This intentionally leaves ample slack. The true large-stratum compound-Poisson sums are only proof variables; the algorithm never generates their possibly huge counts or marks.

## Complexity and output size

There are at most J=poly(n,epsilon^-1,log(delta^-1)) sampled increments. Each draws at most n exact input-rational Bernoullis. All explicitly simulated Poisson means and table sizes are polynomial in the same parameters. Exponential input uniforms use b=O(log(J/delta)+log(1/rho)) bits, and q and w_i have polynomial input-derived bit lengths. Thus representing Wtilde or a rare-event time of order q^-1 adds O(log(q^-1)) bits, not q^-1 work.

Exact rational sums may have denominators whose bit length grows with J; J is polynomial, so this does not hide an exponential bit-size issue. Power-of-two rejection has constant expected attempts for each finite rational draw. A polynomial worst-case budget can alternatively be imposed with an additional failure allocation, but only expected polynomial bit complexity is claimed here.

The same CDF and finite-bin total-variation corollaries as in the main proof now apply to the finite-bit output, with epsilon and delta as given in this theorem.

### Elementary-function implementation detail

No special real-function oracle is needed. For log of a positive dyadic U_b, multiply by a power of two to obtain v in [1,2], then use

    log v = 2 sum_{j>=0} z^(2j+1)/(2j+1),  z=(v-1)/(v+1) in [0,1/3].

The geometric tail makes the number of terms linear in requested precision. Compute log 2 by the same series at z=1/3, and account for its multiplication by the range-reduction exponent, which is at most b+1. For exp(-r) with r<2R_i, range-reduce to an argument of magnitude at most one, use the factorial-tail Taylor bound, then repeated squaring with sufficient guard bits. The number of reductions is O(log(R_i+1)); both precision and rational intermediate sizes are polynomial in the bounds already charged.
