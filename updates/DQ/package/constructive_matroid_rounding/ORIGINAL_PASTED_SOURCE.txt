## Result: a polynomial-bit constructive rounding theorem candidate

I obtained a **complete constructive proof candidate for general matroid bases**, including an explicit exactly unbiased output distribution.

The previous nonconvex minimization is no longer an operation in the algorithm. Its replacement is a local negative-curvature procedure with **face-preserving precision resets**: small linear programs that control numerical encoding length while preserving matroid feasibility and the required linear equations exactly.

This has not received independent review or a historical-priority determination. The full rounding algorithm has not been implemented end to end.

## 1. The decisive computational statement

Let \(M\) be an arbitrary matroid on \(n\) elements, supplied by an **independence oracle**. Its base polytope is
\[
P(M)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_B:B\text{ is a basis of }M\}.
\]

The input is a rational matrix \(A\in\mathbb Q^{m\times n}\) and a rational point \(x\in P(M)\), with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{ij}^{2}\le1
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,n).
\]

### Proposed theorem: constructive exactly unbiased matroid rounding

There is a deterministic algorithm that constructs bases
\[
B_1,\ldots,B_t,\qquad t\le n+1,
\]
and nonnegative rational probabilities \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_t\), satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\lambda_\ell=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\lambda_\ell\mathbf1_{B_\ell}=x,
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\|A(\mathbf1_{B_\ell}-x)\|_\infty\le C
\quad\text{for every support point}.
}
\]

Here \(C\) is an absolute constant. The proof supplies the conservative value \(C=262336\); its magnitude is not the advance.

The algorithm uses **polynomially many independence-oracle queries and polynomially many ordinary bit operations**, measured in \(n,m\), and the total binary input length. Its output distribution has polynomial encoding length.

The proof first establishes the stronger directional statement needed to construct that distribution:

> For every rational cost vector \(c\), a deterministic polynomial-bit algorithm returns a basis \(B\) with
> \[
> \boxed{
> c^\top\mathbf1_B\le c^\top x,
> \qquad
> \|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le C.
> }
> \]

This does not assume a matrix representation of the matroid, an oracle for discrepancy-constrained optimization, or an oracle for nonlinear minimization.

The spectral engine remains inherited from the previous proof and Guo–Fang–Lu’s **20 September 2026** preprint. Their stated algorithmic model is exact real arithmetic. The additional claim here is the transfer to general matroids with rational bit complexity and constructive exact marginals. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1)

## 2. The operation that removes the computational obstruction

The preceding manuscript explicitly left a global minimum over a generally nonconvex set as its computational limitation. A finite number of face descents did not make those descents efficiently computable. proof

There are actually two problems to remove.

First, negative curvature supplies a direction, but a feasible step may be arbitrarily short. Second, even polynomially many exact rational moves can produce an unacceptable recurrence in denominator size.

The new construction separates these problems:

**Short moves are charged to irreversible face loss. Numerical precision is reset inside the face already reached.**

### The precision-reset lemma

Suppose an exact local move reaches \(z^*\) in a matroid face \(F^*\). Suppose it also satisfies certain exact linear equations
\[
Hz=b,
\]
whose coefficients come from the original input.

For a prescribed rational tolerance \(\delta>0\), construct a small dyadic box containing \(z^*\):
\[
\mathcal B
=
\prod_{j=1}^{n}[a_j,a_j+2^{-p}],
\qquad
a_j=2^{-p}\lfloor 2^p z_j^*\rfloor,
\]
with \(2^{-p}\le\delta/(n+1)\).

Now find a vertex of the **linear** feasible set
\[
\boxed{
F^*\cap\{z:Hz=b\}\cap\mathcal B.
}
\]

This set is nonempty because it contains \(z^*\). Any returned point \(z^+\) satisfies
\[
\|z^+-z^*\|_2\le\delta,
\]
while retaining the face and equations exactly.

More importantly, its encoding length depends on the small-box precision and the encoding lengths of the linear constraints—not on the old denominator of \(z^*\). This follows from determinant bounds for rational vertices.

Exact rational linear optimization from a separation oracle is classical; the construction uses that machinery only for linear problems, including lower-dimensional ones. It does not assume that arbitrary nonlinear feasible sets can be optimized efficiently. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4)

A critical distinction is that **the reset point need not be dyadic**. An exact slice might require \(z_1=1/3\), which no dyadic grid contains. The dyadic object is the enclosing box; the point returned inside it is rational.

### Why the exact equations do not themselves accumulate numerical complexity

The retained-row machinery supplies a useful fact.

A row is protected exactly while its retained squared norm satisfies
\[
q_i>K^2.
\]
A coefficient is deliberately discarded only when
\[
p_{ij}>q_i/K.
\]

But column normalization gives \(p_{ij}\le1\). For a large row,
\[
q_i/K>K\ge1,
\]
so deliberate deletion is impossible.

Therefore, **a currently large row has never undergone a deliberate deletion**. Its exact equation is simply
\[
\boxed{A_i(z-z_0)=0,}
\]
using the original input.

The reset consequently preserves only
\[
z\in F^*,\qquad
c^\top z=c^\top z_0,\qquad
A_i(z-z_0)=0\quad(i\text{ currently large}).
\]

It does **not** try to preserve the moving spectral constraints exactly. Those constraints receive a globally bounded numerical-error allowance. That separation is what makes the precision reset work.

## 3. Turning the curvature argument into a finite algorithm

Work in sign coordinates:
\[
Q=2P(M)-\mathbf1,\qquad z_0=2x-\mathbf1.
\]

Retain the previous coefficient-removal accounting and spectral potential
\[
\Phi(z)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(M(z))-1\bigr)_+^3-\eta\|z\|_2^2.
\]

For the constructive proof, take
\[
K=64,\qquad \eta=\frac1{Kn},
\]
and replace exact square-root row scales by rational upper scales satisfying
\[
q_i\le r_i^2<4q_i.
\]

These scales can be chosen as dyadic multiples of original coefficients. No exact square-root operation is needed.

### Enough curvature survives all the algorithmic constraints

Let the current minimal matroid face have \(s\) nonconstant coordinates. Its dimension is at least \(s/2\).

Protect every medium-row barrier whose value satisfies \(u_{i\sigma}\ge-1\). Under the spectral invariant \(L=\lambda_{\max}(M)<2\), the dimension costs are bounded as follows:
\[
\text{large and protected-row equations}
\le\frac{129s}{K^2},
\]
and
\[
\text{spectral equations used in the proof}
\le\frac{4s}{K}+\frac2K.
\]

Reserve two additional equations: one for the objective and one for an approximate potential gradient.

The surviving dimension is at least
\[
\frac{s}{2}
-\frac{129s}{K^2}
-\frac{4s}{K}
-\frac2K
-2
>
\frac{5s}{K}
\qquad(s>K).
\]

That is enough for the inherited restricted-trace argument. It supplies a nonzero feasible direction with
\[
\boxed{
h^\top\nabla^2\Phi(z)h
\le-2\eta\|h\|_2^2.
}
\]

The important improvement is quantitative: the negative curvature has an inverse-polynomial margin.

The exact high-eigenvalue subspace is used **only in this existence proof**. The algorithm does not compute an exact spectral cutoff. Instead, it forms the rational constraint space and uses a certified approximate Hessian. Rational symmetric elimination finds a negative quadratic-form direction within that space.

### Full steps and short steps have different certificates of progress

Normalize the resulting rational direction so that
\[
\frac12\le\|h\|_2\le1.
\]

Compute the exact maximal feasible lengths in both directions:
\[
t_+=\max\{t\ge0:z+th\in Q\},
\qquad
t_-=\max\{t\ge0:z-th\in Q\}.
\]

For a fixed inverse-polynomial step size \(\tau\), put
\[
t=\min\{\tau,t_+,t_-\}.
\]

When \(t=\tau\), take a full step. When \(t<\tau\), choose the sign that reaches the corresponding endpoint. That short step hits a **proper matroid face exactly**.

Apply the precision reset inside the reached face, then perform coefficient cleanup.

Thus every iteration has one of two outcomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a full step decreases the potential by a fixed polynomial amount;}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\text{a short step strictly decreases face dimension.}
}
\]

There can be at most \(n\) short steps. This remains true when a face shrinks without immediately making a coordinate integral.

### Explicit precision and termination budgets

The manuscript gives the following conservative choices:
\[
R=16K^2(m+n+1),\qquad
H=2^{16}R^6\eta^{-3},\qquad
\tau=\frac{\eta}{128H},
\]
where \(H\) bounds the relevant smoothness constants.

Choose derivative accuracy
\[
\epsilon=\frac{\eta\tau}{1024(n+1)},
\]
and set
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{256n}{\tau^2}\right\rceil+n+1,
\]
\[
\delta=
\min\left\{
\frac{\eta\tau^2}{2^{16}H(n+1)},
\frac1{128N}
\right\}.
\]

These parameters are fixed before the walk.

For an exact local move, Taylor’s theorem gives
\[
\Phi(z+\sigma th)-\Phi(z)
\le
\epsilon t-\frac{\eta t^2}{32}.
\]

The precision reset increases the potential by at most \(H\delta\), and cleanup cannot increase it. Consequently, a full step decreases the potential by at least
\[
\gamma=\frac{\eta\tau^2}{64},
\]
while a short step increases it by at most
\[
\frac{\eta\tau^2}{512(n+1)}.
\]

Since there are at most \(n\) short steps,
\[
-\frac1K\le\Phi\le\frac1{512K}.
\]
This also preserves \(L<2\).

The number of full steps is therefore less than
\[
\frac{65n}{\tau^2}.
\]

For fixed \(K\), the resulting local-update bound is
\[
O\!\left(n^9(m+n)^{12}\right).
\]

That is an extremely conservative polynomial, not a practical running-time claim. Each update also contains polynomial-time linear-algebra and oracle subroutines.

### The reset does not create a cumulative discrepancy loss

A protected row barrier has zero first derivative in the chosen direction and nonpositive second derivative, so it does not increase along the exact move.

An unprotected barrier starts below \(-1\) and remains safely below zero over the short prescribed step. The precision reset changes any barrier by at most \(2\delta\). Over the entire computation,
\[
u_{i\sigma}\le2N\delta\le\frac1{64}.
\]

This small positive allowance still implies the same tracked-row bound
\[
|d_i|<2K^3.
\]

The earlier coefficient-removal error is a whole-history bound, not a per-iteration cost. Precision resets do not change that accounting: each removed coefficient contributes only its coefficient times the difference between its removal-time and current coordinates. The inherited bound is \(4K\) per row. proof

At termination, at most \(K\) coordinates remain nonconstant. A minimum-cost vertex of the final face adds at most \(2K\) sign-coordinate error. Hence
\[
\|A(v-z_0)\|_\infty
\le
2K^3+4K+2K.
\]

Returning to Boolean coordinates gives
\[
\|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le K^3+3K,
\]
and
\[
c^\top\mathbf1_B\le c^\top x.
\]

That completes the proposed one-basis construction.

## 4. Why the oracle and numerical interface is polynomial

There are three places where a seemingly constructive proof could still conceal an unavailable resource. The manuscript addresses each.

### Matroid faces and line endpoints require no additional oracle

For a rational point \(y\), separation over the base polytope reduces to minimizing
\[
S\longmapsto r(S)-y(S).
\]

A rank evaluation takes at most \(n\) independence queries by greedy augmentation. Exact submodular minimization is polynomial-time, so the separation procedure is explicitly reducible to the stated independence interface. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089)

The minimal face is also recoverable without enumerating tight sets.

After deleting zero coordinates and contracting one coordinates, let \(T_j\) be the smallest tight set containing \(j\). It is found by minimizing
\[
r(S)-y(S)+\varepsilon_0|S|,
\qquad j\in S,
\]
where a rational denominator bound for \(y\) determines a sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon_0\).

Two coordinates belong to the same block when they occur together in every tight set. These blocks can be recovered from the \(T_j\). The face’s tangent equations are exactly
\[
\sum_{j\in C}h_j=0
\]
for the recovered blocks \(C\), together with zero motion on fixed coordinates.

Their right-hand sides in the face description are integers. No fractional-coordinate block can be a singleton, giving the dimension bound \(s/2\).

Exact line endpoints are one-variable rational linear programs using the same separator. Precision resets are linear programs with that separator plus explicit equations and box constraints.

### Tiny coefficients do not force an uncharged precision requirement

A medium-row matrix contribution is evaluated in the normalized form
\[
\boxed{
w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^\top
=
\frac{K^2}{2}e^{u_{i\sigma}/(2K)}
\left(\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right)
\left(\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right)^\top.
}
\]

Here
\[
\left\|\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right\|_2^2\le\frac1K,
\qquad
\|\nabla u_{i\sigma}\|_2\le2.
\]

Thus the calculation does not multiply an enormous \(r_i^{-4}\) by an underflowing exponential. Very negative exponents can be truncated with a certified error; the remaining exponentials need only polynomially many precision bits.

The spectral matrix also has a known gap:
\[
\lambda_1(M)-\lambda_2(M)\ge\eta.
\]

The positive rank-one term \(\eta\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\) supplies this gap. It bounds the required eigenpair and reduced-resolvent precision. Approximate derivatives can consequently be computed by rational arithmetic to the prescribed tolerances; no exact exponential or exact eigenvector oracle is used.

### Denominator bounds do not recur through the whole trajectory

After each reset, state coordinates have a fixed polynomial encoding bound determined by the original input and the chosen grid precision.

A single unreset direction and endpoint may have a larger polynomial encoding length, but they are reset before the next iteration.

Offsets remain bounded-bit as well: each row absorbs at most \(n\) removed-coefficient contributions, and cleanup occurs **after** reset. The absorbed coordinates therefore already have the uniform encoding bound.

This is the distinction between a polynomial real-arithmetic operation count and the polynomial-bit claim made here.

## 5. Constructing exact marginals—not just proving they exist

The walk itself need not be unbiased. Exact unbiasedness is constructed afterward from the objective-aware algorithm.

For fixed \(A,x\), define the good bases
\[
\mathcal G=
\left\{
\mathbf1_B:
B\text{ a basis},
\ \|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le C
\right\}.
\]

The one-basis algorithm supplies the response rule
\[
\forall c\in\mathbb Q^n,\qquad
\text{return }y\in\mathcal G
\text{ with }c^\top(y-x)\le0.
\]

An ellipsoid decomposition converts that rule into an explicit convex combination. This is a classical metarounding-type conversion, not a newly invented part of the spectral method. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rsa.10033)

Here is the constructive argument.

Suppose a finite collection \(S\) of returned bases does not contain \(x\) in its convex hull. Then a strictly separating vector can be scaled to satisfy
\[
(y-x)^\top c\ge1
\qquad(y\in S).
\]

Because the coordinates of \(y\) are Boolean and \(x\) has a known rational denominator, determinant bounds give a uniform polynomial-bit bound on such a separating vector. After another constant scaling, its feasible region contains a ball of explicitly bounded radius inside a known outer ball.

At each ellipsoid center \(c\), the rounding algorithm returns a basis violating the proposed separating inequality:
\[
(y-x)^\top c\le0.
\]

Record that basis and make the corresponding ellipsoid cut. Polynomially many cuts reduce the enclosing volume below the ball that would exist if \(x\notin\operatorname{conv}(S)\). Therefore the recorded bases must contain \(x\) in their convex hull.

Finally solve the explicit rational linear program
\[
\sum_{y\in S}\lambda_y y=x,\qquad
\sum_{y\in S}\lambda_y=1,\qquad
\lambda_y\ge0.
\]
A basic feasible solution has at most \(n+1\) support points.

This constructs **exact** marginals. It does not invoke an optimizer over the unknown set of all good bases.

The resulting distribution is also a compact certificate: basis feasibility, rational probabilities, exact marginals, and every discrepancy inequality can be checked without replaying the spectral walk.

There is one unavoidable sampling qualification. A probability such as \(1/3\) cannot be implemented with a uniformly bounded number of fair random bits. The distribution is constructed deterministically in polynomial time; exact sampling from it uses **expected** polynomial time through rational-probability rejection sampling.

## 6. Consequences and boundaries

### Square-root discrepancy under arbitrary matroid constraints

For a Boolean constraint matrix \(W\) with at most \(\Delta\) nonzero entries in each column, compute the integer
\[
q=\lceil\sqrt{\Delta}\rceil
\]
and apply the theorem to the rational matrix \(A=W/q\). This yields
\[
\boxed{
\|W(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty
\le C\lceil\sqrt{\Delta}\rceil
=O(\sqrt{\Delta}),
}
\]
with either a nonincreasing prescribed cost or an explicit exactly unbiased distribution.

The relevant August 2026 matroid-discrepancy paper provides a constructive cost-nonincreasing guarantee linear in column sparsity. The proposed theorem changes the asymptotic guarantee, although its large constants and polynomial degree do not make it a practical replacement. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13983)

### A broader algorithmic transfer principle

The same precision-reset argument applies to Boolean-vertex polytopes with bounded exchange width, a polynomial-time separation procedure, and a controlled rational description.

Bounded exchange supplies enough face directions. The polyhedral interface supplies exact endpoints and linear resets. The spectral argument supplies quantitative descent.

For general matroids, the required interface is derived from independence access; it is not an extra assumption.

The broader operation is therefore:

> **Preserve combinatorial feasibility and fixed input-defined equations exactly, while allowing bounded drift in the analytic certificate and resetting precision inside every reached face.**

Neither negative curvature alone nor an existential chain of faces gives this conclusion.

### The long-edge obstruction remains intact

Nothing here removes the obstruction from large coupled feasible exchanges.

An even cycle has two alternating perfect matchings. At their midpoint, an appropriate scalar row gives unavoidable error proportional to the cycle length. Each side of the bipartition separately defines a partition matroid, so arbitrary matroid intersections remain outside the dimension-independent theorem. proof

The proof also explicitly rejects a uniform lower bound on feasible step length and rejects independent dyadic coordinate rounding as an exact feasibility repair.

## 7. What is established at this stage

The attached argument supplies a complete proposed route from the inherited curvature machinery to a **deterministic polynomial-bit basis algorithm and a deterministic construction of its exactly unbiased output distribution**. It does not leave global nonconvex minimization, exact transcendental operations, approximate marginal correction, or a stronger matroid oracle as deferred operations.

The accompanying program passed exact checks of the dimension and progress inequalities, a precision-reset example, and 80 face-recovery and terminal-cost cases across five small matroid families. Those are limited diagnostics, not verification of the full theorem or an implementation of the full algorithm.

**The substantive advance is a paper-level computational construction, not another existential extension.** Its correctness as a whole and its historical novelty still require independent scrutiny. The conservative algorithm also remains far from a practical solver.
