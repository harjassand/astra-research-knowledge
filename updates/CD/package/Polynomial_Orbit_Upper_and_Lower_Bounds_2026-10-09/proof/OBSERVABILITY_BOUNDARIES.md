# Three distinct finite-realization obligations

These are elementary independently reconstructed boundary examples. Novelty is not asserted.

## 1. A finite observational equality test does not give a finitely generated observation algebra

On X=[-1,1]^2 let F(x,y)=(x,xy) and h(x,y)=y. The t-th output is x^t y. Its observation algebra is

A=R[y,xy,x^2 y,...] subset R[x,y].

A is not finitely generated as an R-algebra. Every nonconstant element is divisible by y. Modulo y^2, a product of two nonconstant elements vanishes. Thus the image of the algebra generated by finitely many elements has finite-dimensional nonconstant part modulo y^2. But the classes x^k y, k>=0, are linearly independent modulo y^2. This is a contradiction.

By contrast the fraction field of A is R(x,y): it contains y and x=(xy)/y. Finite rational generation therefore does not imply finite polynomial generation.

Despite that failure, just the two observations q=(y,xy) determine every future output. If y!=0 they determine x, and if y=0 all future outputs vanish. Thus observational equality is finitely determined.

For paired states (x,y),(X,Y), the equality ideal generated by all future-output differences is exactly

I=<y-Y, xy-XY>.

Indeed x^k y-X^k Y = x^k(y-Y) + Y(x^k-X^k), and modulo y-Y the second generator is y(x-X). The usual factorization x^k-X^k=(x-X)sum_{j=0}^{k-1}x^{k-1-j}X^j finishes membership. Alternatively y(x-X)=(xy-XY)-X(y-Y).

## 2. A nonpolynomial canonical update can still be regular

The image of q is K={(u,v): |u|<=1, |v|<=|u|}. Its exact update is

G(u,v)=(v,v^2/u) for u!=0, and G(0,0)=(0,0).

This is continuous and semialgebraic. It is Lipschitz on K: for u,u' of the same sign, along the convex cone segment the scalar function v^2/u has partial derivatives -v^2/u^2 and 2v/u, bounded by 1 and 2. If the signs differ, |v^2/u-v'^2/u'|<=|u|+|u'|=|u-u'|. Consequently one can take a finite global Lipschitz constant (for example 3 in the l1 norm).

It cannot be replaced by a polynomial update on these two observable coordinates. If a polynomial P(u,v) agreed with v^2/u on the two-dimensional open interior u>0, |v|<u, then uP(u,v)=v^2 would be a polynomial identity. Substituting u=0 contradicts 0=v^2.

Allowing more finitely many polynomial observables from A does not close A under polynomial updates and recover h: if it did, all iterated h would lie in the finitely generated invariant observation subalgebra, hence generate A, contradicting Section 1.

This is not an impossibility of finite polynomial realization with arbitrary hidden coordinates: the original pair (x,y) already gives one. It is an obstruction to a finite canonical polynomial realization using only functions in the observation algebra.

## 3. Finite equality and one-step Lipschitz control do not yield horizon-free robustness

Take two initial states (1,1) and (1-delta,1), delta>0. Their two-coordinate representations are (1,1) and (1,1-delta), at distance delta in l1. Their t-th outputs differ by

1-(1-delta)^t,

whose supremum over t is 1. Thus there is no modulus omega(delta)->0 that bounds all future output differences in this coordinate metric. This remains false although the system is bounded, polynomial, its observation equality ideal closes at depth one, its canonical rational update is globally Lipschitz, and all exact equality questions have finite certificates.

For every fixed t, the error is <=t delta. The lack of a horizon-free modulus is genuinely a uniform-in-time obstruction. It is not a proof against finite-horizon simulation or exact arithmetic.

## Consequence for the proposed primitive

Noetherian termination, rational field closure, global semialgebraic realization, and feedback-safe quantitative robustness are different claims. Solving the first three does not automatically provide the fourth. A new compiler would need an additional mechanism with a noncircular, checkable stability and computational-cost guarantee.
