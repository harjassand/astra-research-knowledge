PAIR-FLUORESCENCE MECHANISM: INDEPENDENT MODEL-SENSITIVITY GATE
2026-10-13

Status
------
The RF-modulated pair-fluorescence proposal does not beat the favorable
same-duration ancilla comparator in the tested finite models. A second
frequency-resolved partial-secular reconstruction changes the modeled error
slightly but also loses. This is a model-scoped negative gate, not a universal
receiver lower bound, a hardware result, or proof that the mechanism cannot be
improved.

At 2.0 us, using the current Sol01 script's corrected continuous-envelope
midpoint schedule, RF RMS amplitude 4 MHz, sign modulation 10 MHz, 0.1 us ramps,
1.0 us flat drive, 0.8 us passive tail, cavity collection efficiency 0.5, and
the threshold “flag if at least one cavity photon is detected”:

  same harmonic-grouped model: accepted Choi infidelity 3.40688%;
  <=1 MHz frequency-cluster control: accepted Choi infidelity 3.46868%;
  favorable 2.0 us ancilla model: accepted Choi infidelity 2.88266%.

The split control is 0.06180 percentage points worse than the grouped model
and 0.58602 points worse than the ancilla comparator. The grouped model is
0.52422 points worse than the comparator. The pair control's accepted
probability is 98.14484% under frequency splitting; its accepted outside-GF
mass per mixed logical input is 3.06163%; its prepared-e flag probability is
43.99050%. The grouped values are 98.42495%, 2.976997%, and 41.33909%.
Thus fewer missed prepared-e flags do not translate into a better accepted
logical channel. The ancilla model has acceptance 97.13735%, accepted leakage
2.65784%, and prepared-erasure flag probability 92.17649%.

The ancilla control is the explicit favorable abstraction in
`work/continuation/sol01_quantum/alternative_control_gates.py`: a 0.2 us
Gaussian effective syndrome SWAP, 1.4 us readout, 0.4 us pre-SWAP idle,
data relaxation, and readout false-positive/false-negative probabilities
0.02/0.07. The two routes are compared using the same accepted-Choi convention,
which includes accepted leakage in the denominator. This is not an acquired
complete hardware channel or a fit to the source experiment.

What is being compared
----------------------
The current Sol01 source is
`work/continuation/sol01_quantum/pair_fluorescence_test.py` (SHA-256 recorded in
`SOURCES_AND_HASHES.txt`). It uses a five-level qutrit truncation and six cavity
Fock states. It keeps all positive-frequency Floquet `a` (cavity) and `b`
(intrinsic transmon) output transitions in its dissipative model. It monitors
all modeled cavity harmonics with eta=0.5 and no dark counts, while intrinsic
`b` output is unobserved. The photon-count map retains counts 0 through 3;
the tested threshold accepts count 0 and flags count >=1. The unobserved
intrinsic channels, uncollected cavity fraction, and all represented terminal
qutrit/cavity branches remain in the accepted/flagged maps.

The accepted metric is formed from the full accepted g/f output traces, with
the logical overlap evaluated only in the target GF subspace. It is not
renormalized by a hidden terminal-qutrit projection. The accepted outside-GF
mass is reported explicitly. For the tested grouped model, represented
unconditional traces for g/f/e inputs are
0.999999999993645/0.999999986350482/0.999999356280816; for the split model they
are 0.999999999993517/0.999999983897807/0.999999352916058. Small nonzero trace
loss and finite cavity truncation remain recorded, not silently normalized
away. Accepted mixed-code residual cavity photon means are 0.002313 (grouped)
and 0.002667 (split).

The 2.0 us schedule contains a 0.8 us passive cavity tail, not an active reset.
The RF sign drive is ramped, modulated, then turned off in the model. The
periodic Floquet flux modulation used to define the dressed states is not
switched off or charged as a finite pump control; pump/flux transfer,
turn-on/turn-off, bias-dependent coherence, and real output spectra remain
unacquired. The channel-metric comparison therefore cannot establish a
physical cost or reset advantage.

Frequency grouping and the model counterexample
-----------------------------------------------
For each transition the script computes

  nu = 7153 MHz + E_source - E_target - ell*Omega,
  k  = ell + q_source - q_target.

The source model forms one collapse operator for each bath and k by summing
all positive-frequency transition amplitudes with that k. Its cavity output
recycling term is the sum over those grouped collapse operators; the detected
jump map is eta times that cavity recycling. The driven Hamiltonian, including
the RF transitions, remains coherent and unchanged in the control below.

I independently rebuilt the same transition table and formed an alternative
partial-secular model. Within each bath and k, lines whose complete cluster
span is at most 1 MHz share a collapse operator; every other cluster receives
its own operator. This retains the code carrier lines

  g, n=1 -> n=0, k=0: 7158.003500 MHz;
  f, n=1 -> n=0, k=0: 7157.901375 MHz;

whose separation is only 0.102125 MHz. It creates 890 frequency-cluster
channels versus 24 harmonic-grouped channels.

The grouping choice changes real cross terms, not merely labels. From the h0
source, two a-output k=0 lines are

  h0 -> e1: 7155.701400 MHz, rate 0.8131735/us;
  h0 -> f0: 7065.543407 MHz, rate 0.0068870/us.

Their 90.157993 MHz gap is about 429 cavity linewidths (kappa/2pi=0.21 MHz).
The grouped collapse operator produces a post-jump off-diagonal amplitude
sqrt(r1*r2)=0.0748355/us between e1 and f0 for unit h0 population, or 9.126%
normalized coherence (18.251% visibility); the frequency-separated channels
remove that cross term.

There is also a pair-mechanism route into the same e1 target:

  (q=1,n=2) -> (q=1,n=1), ell=0,k=0: 7157.478352 MHz, rate 1.808496/us;
  (q=3,n=0) -> (q=1,n=1), ell=-2,k=0: 7155.701400 MHz, rate 0.813174/us.

The 1.776952 MHz gap is 8.46 cavity linewidths. Grouping gives a source-state
cross amplitude 1.212692/us and 92.513% normalized jump visibility; a 1 MHz
partial-secular control resolves these lines. The frequency sensitivity is
therefore especially relevant to the proposed dressed pair path.

This split-frequency model is an explicit Davies-like sensitivity scenario,
not a correction proven to describe the chip. The grouped model represents a
flat broadband Markov input-output limit; a coherent finite-bandwidth detector,
the actual bath spectrum, and the 10–20 MHz RF modulation can alter which
cross terms survive. An undriven-frequency split alone does not model
modulation-induced Rayleigh sidebands. The physical device remains unknown
until bath/detector spectra and a driven Floquet treatment including the
finite modulation are acquired or validated.

Modulation and convergence gate
-------------------------------
The current code samples the actual rise/flat/fall envelope times
sqrt(2)*A*cos(2*pi*f_mod*t) at midpoints. My 2 us run uses 12 steps per
10 MHz period. It is a finite-time numerical model, not an exact modulation
Floquet expansion. The stored earlier `PAIR_MODULATED_RESULTS.json` reports
3.41467% but predates the current code by file timestamp; replaying the current
default path gives 3.40688% for the grouped model. Both values lose to the
2.88266% same-duration comparator.

Sol01's later same-schedule 3.8 us saturated refinement provides a grid warning:
for qdim=5,nf=8, RMS amplitude 8 MHz, 20 MHz modulation, 2.8 us flat drive,
0.1 us ramps and 0.8 us tail, changing from 24 to 48 modulation steps per
period changes accepted Choi error from 3.89607% to 3.73848%. The 0.15759-point
shift exceeds the 0.06180-point grouped-versus-split shift found here. That
3.8 us setting still loses to the same-duration 3.19754% ancilla model, and
the 2 us candidate has not had its own step-refinement check. This is an
unclosed numerical/model gate, not a claimed correction to the 2 us result.
