2. Ito, Kato & Ono (2021), “Transfer of Copper from Carbon Saturated Molten
Iron to Molten Silver via Solid Iron under Temperature Gradient,” Advanced
Experimental Mechanics 6, 71–76. DOI: 10.11395/aem.6.0_71.
Primary landing page: https://doi.org/10.11395/aem.6.0_71
Primary PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aem/6/0/6_71/_pdf
Evidence: §4 / p. 73, equations (1)–(8), reports derived infinite-dilution
gamma_Cu(liquid Ag)=3.76 at 1273 K and 3.26 at 1473 K. The 0.050 mass% Fe-Cu
endpoint is calculated under the source's carbon-saturated iron and oxidation
assumptions, not a measured electrostripping result. The paper measures Cu
transfer through Fe-C/solid gamma-Fe/liquid Ag; it does not report an
electrolyte, charge efficiency, Ag electrochemical loss, or electronic
transference for Ag-Cu electrolysis.

3. Ag-Cu phase diagram, NIST Metallurgy / Phase Diagrams & Computational

The page identifies the CALPHAD assessment (Hayes et al. 1986, adapted by
Kattner/NIST 1999), liquid model (Ag,Cu), and eutectic at 779.1 C. At the
Ag-rich shuttle compositions and 1235–1300 K, the candidate is in the liquid
field. This is an assessed phase diagram, not a direct electrolysis test.

3a. Copper stable-solid/liquid standard-state correction: NIST-JANAF Cu(l)
table https://janaf.nist.gov/tables/Cu-003.html . It tabulates the Gibbs
formation function for Cu(l) relative to stable Cu(s): 1.523 kJ/mol at 1200 K
and 0.561 kJ/mol at 1300 K. Linear interpolation to 1273 K gives about

interpolated standard-state correction, not a measured molten-electrolysis
datum.

4. Common molten chloride redox data: Gaur & Jindal (1970), “Standard
electrode potentials in molten chlorides—II,” Electrochimica Acta 15,
1113–1126, DOI 10.1016/0013-4686(70)85004-6.
Primary landing page: https://doi.org/10.1016/0013-4686(70)85004-6
The primary abstract reports Nernst behavior for Ag(I)/Ag and apparent
standard potentials including Cu(I)/Cu in MgCl2-KCl (32.5:67.5 mol%) at
475 C (748 K). This is 525 K below the 1273 K target. The accessible abstract
lists 16 potential values for 15 named couples; because their mapping is
ambiguous there, no Ag-Cu delta-E0 is assigned from that list. The symbolic
non-overlap proof below is independent of delta-E0, so this ambiguity does
not affect the bound. Aqueous potentials were not used.

5. Lu et al. (2024), “Feasibility of Electro-Reduction of Metals in
Sulfide-Based Copper Removal Slag for Steelmaking,” ISIJ International 64,
1339–1344, DOI 10.2355/isijinternational.ISIJINT-2024-035.
Primary PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/isijinternational/64/9/64_ISIJINT-2024-035/_pdf
Evidence: pp. 1340–1344, §§3.1–3.4. In Na2S-FeS-Cu2S at 1350 C (1623 K),
the source calculates that Cu cannot be independently reduced without Fe
reduction; it reports a 0.18 A background current attributed to electronic
conduction, 323 C total charge, 0.0194 g metallic Cu, 29 C theoretical Cu
charge, and 9.1% Cu current efficiency. This is spent sulfide regeneration,
not dilute Ag-Cu anodic stripping and not the target temperature.

6. Paeng, Judge & Azimi (2024), “Electrorefining for copper tramp element
removal from molten iron for green steelmaking,” Resources, Conservation and
Recycling 206, 107654, DOI 10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.107654.
Primary article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.107654
The accessible primary record/abstract reports Cu reduced from 0.43 wt% to
790 ppmw (0.079 wt%) with an Al2O3-CaO-MgO-FeS oxysulfide electrolyte and
simultaneous carbon removal. It is a direct molten-steel process but has no
Ag shuttle. The accessed record did not provide a Cu current-efficiency or
Ag-loss value; these are not filled in by inference.
