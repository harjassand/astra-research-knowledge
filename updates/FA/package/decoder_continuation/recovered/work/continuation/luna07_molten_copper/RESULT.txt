DECISIVE GATE: COMMON-BATH Ag-Cu STRIPPING / Cu SINK
13 October 2026

Finding
No qualifying primary measured dataset was found for anodic Cu stripping from
dilute liquid Ag-Cu into a molten electrolyte at 1235–1300 K that reports
Cu current efficiency, Ag loss and electrolyte electronic conduction. This
is a bounded source-search result, not a proof that no experiment exists.

A separate thermodynamic gate is decisive for the simplest proposed cell.
For a single homogeneous bath carrying Cu+ and Ag+, Cu-selective oxidation
from an Ag-rich liquid alloy and Cu-only deposition at a pure-Cu cathode have
nonoverlapping equilibrium windows. The result does not require a numerical
Ag/Cu standard-potential gap and does not use aqueous potentials.

Proof with conventions
Use dimensionless activities in one and the same molten electrolyte, with
consistent pure-metal standards at the same temperature: pure liquid Ag and
stable pure solid Cu at 1273 K. Let
  DeltaE0 = E0(Ag+/Ag(l)) - E0(Cu+/Cu(s)),
  VT = RT/F,
  R = a(Ag+)/a(Cu+).
For M+ + e- <-> M, the reduction equilibrium potential is
  E_M = E0_M + VT ln(a(M+)/a(M)).
At the Ag-Cu alloy anode, selective Cu dissolution before Ag requires
E_Cu(anode) < E_Ag(anode), giving
  R > [a_Ag(alloy)/a_Cu(alloy)] exp[-F DeltaE0/(RT)].
At a cathode where pure Cu deposition must precede pure Ag deposition,
a_Cu(cathode)=a_Ag(cathode)=1 and E_Cu(cathode)>E_Ag(cathode), giving
  R < exp[-F DeltaE0/(RT)].
The lower anode bound divided by the upper cathode bound is exactly
a_Ag(alloy)/a_Cu(alloy). Hence there is no shared-bath value of R whenever
a_Ag(alloy)>a_Cu(alloy). This conclusion holds for every value of DeltaE0.

Quantitative scenario bound
The inherited shuttle case spans 0.372557 mass% Cu loaded to 0.040909 mass%
Cu lean. Ito et al. report-derived gamma_Cu^infinity(liquid Ag)=3.76 at
1273 K, with Cu activity referenced to pure liquid Cu. At 1273 K cathodic Cu
is solid, so use NIST-JANAF ΔG°[Cu(l)-Cu(s)]≈0.821 kJ/mol (linear
interpolation of its 1200/1300 K entries) to convert to the stable-solid
standard: a_Cu(solid std)=a_Cu(liquid std) exp(ΔG°/RT). With a_Ag≈1:
  loaded: x_Cu=0.00630769, a_Cu(solid std)≈0.0256292,
          a_Ag/a_Cu≈39.02;
  lean:   x_Cu=0.000694225, a_Cu(solid std)≈0.00282075,
          a_Ag/a_Cu≈354.5.
Under the dilute-activity approximations, the anodic required R is about
39 times the maximum cathodic Cu-only R after the common exp(-F DeltaE0/RT)
factor is removed at the loaded composition and about 355-fold at the lean
endpoint. These are numerical illustrations at 1273 K, not measured
composition-dependent electrochemical windows over 1235–1300 K.
At 1273 K, RT/F=0.10970 V, so the lean-end activity-ratio separation is
VT ln(354.5)=0.6440 V on the Nernst scale. These numerical factors use the
dilute-Ag approximation a_Ag=1, report-derived dilute gamma_Cu, interpolated
Cu fusion correction, and inherited process compositions; the exact theorem
is in terms of same-standard-state measured a_Ag/a_Cu.

Scope and escape routes
This is thermodynamic non-overlap, not a claim that kinetics cannot suppress
Ag dissolution/deposition. No measured kinetics/current efficiency/Ag loss
was found for the proposed Ag-Cu interface. The theorem also does not rule out
co-depositing Ag with Cu and then refining that product, or using a separate
chemical Cu sink; those are different product/recycle routes with added
separation duties. A divided cell with distinct anolyte and catholyte activity
ratios, other Cu/Ag speciation or valence, or a selective membrane could evade
the common-bath premise; each requires its own measured selective transport
and salt-regeneration balance. The accessible
primary standard-potential source is at 748 K, not 1273 K, and its abstract's
potential list has an ambiguous assignment, so no standard-potential number
is used. Unknown salt speciation and kinetic overpotentials remain open.

Phase / primary experimental checks
The NIST Ag-Cu CALPHAD assessment has a liquid phase and eutectic at 779.1 C;
the candidate Ag-rich alloy lies in the liquid field across 1235–1300 K (the
lower edge is close to pure Ag's melting point). This does not supply
electrochemical selectivity. No electrolyte composition/speciation or
Ag-Cu-in-that-salt phase-equilibrium measurement was found for the proposed
electrolytic regenerator.

Closest measured controls remain chemistry-specific. Paeng, Judge & Azimi
(2024; DOI 10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.107654) report direct oxysulfide
electrorefining of molten iron from 0.43 wt% Cu to 790 ppmw (0.079 wt%); this
is a strong steel-process control but does not use Ag, and the accessed
primary record gave no verified Cu CE for the Ag gate. Lu et al. (2024; DOI
10.2355/isijinternational.ISIJINT-2024-035) report spent Na2S-FeS-Cu2S
electrolysis at 1350 C: 0.18 A background current attributed to electronic
conduction, 323 C total charge, 0.0194 g metallic Cu (29 C theoretical) and
9.1% Cu CE, with Cu/Fe co-reduction. It is a different electrolyte, feed and
temperature.

Yamaguchi & Ono (2012; DOI 10.2355/isijinternational.52.18) provide adjacent
chemical, not electrochemical, Ag-carrier data at 1523 K. Table 5 reports
about 0.06–0.12 mass% Ag in the iron phase and in Run 14 a flux composition
of 17.9 mass% Ag versus 1.73 mass% Cu. This confirms Ag partitions into both
metal and flux in that process; it does not quantify Ag loss in the proposed
electrolytic regenerator.

Decision
The evidence gate fails for the one-bath monovalent Ag shuttle plus Cu cathode:
it has no equilibrium selective window even before charge efficiency, Ag
loss, conductivity, corrosion or throughput are considered. Do not call this
a universal impossibility; an experimentally demonstrated separator,
speciation change, kinetic selectivity, or alternate sink would reopen it.
No experiment was performed and no physical validation is claimed.
