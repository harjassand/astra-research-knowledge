Gate: test whether a compact all-prefix fractional-completion representation can support causal binary actions with scalar postdecision noise.

Inherited contract: a fixed supplied schedule v_1,...,v_N in the Euclidean unit ball; choose epsilon_t in {-1,+1}, then adversary chooses zeta_t in [-gamma,gamma], gamma<1; s_t=s_(t-1)+(epsilon_t+zeta_t)v_t; exact past-state feedback. The inherited source theorem is internally reconstructed with radius O((1-gamma)^-3 sqrt(d), while the available general compiler is exponential in d. Prior failed shortcuts include future covariance whitening and commuting coordinate erosion/projection; neither is a general hardness result.

Decisive result: pointwise convexification of all-prefix winning states is not selector-closed. Exact two-step rational Euclidean examples in d=2 and d=3, gamma=1/2 and radius 11/10, make a first action pass because both next states lie in conv(W_2), while an allowed scalar-noise endpoint reaches a state outside W_2 with no safe next action. The result extends to continuous zeta because the final safe ball is convex and each failing endpoint is allowed. Full proof, code, and exact receipts are in RESULT.txt, verify_euclidean_selector_counterexamples.py, and euclidean_selector_exact_receipt.json.

A sufficient property is recorded: if safe convex polytopes Q_t have selector regions covering each Q_t and each region's chosen sign sends both endpoint successors into Q_(t+1), then a constant number of separation/membership queries per round selects a robust action by induction. No construction of such a polynomially separable selector-closed family is established. This rejects one natural fractional hull only; general compact oracle existence and mission achievement remain UNKNOWN.
