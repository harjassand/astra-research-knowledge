LUNA06 — FINITE-SAMPLE CERTIFICATE FOR THE RARE-PATH ESTIMATOR
13 October 2026

Status: an exact, computable confidence interval is derived for a path-level
defensive mixture. It is rigorously valid but vacuous at the inherited budget.
An event-scale likelihood envelope would repair the variance bound; I give an
exact finite-horizon Doob-residual construction and its numerical admission
thresholds. No mixture trajectories or large simulations were run here.

The probability scale is the inherited reported estimate
p0 = 4.304224792713203e-10, not newly certified ground truth. The exact target
is the discrete Euler-grid event “hit x>=0.8 before x<=-0.8 and by T=2” from
(x,y)=(-0.75,-1), dt=0.0005, so there are at most N=4000 x/y force-point
evaluations per stopped path. The source report records 19.4M acquisition
force points and 1,438,768 propagation force points for 10,000 guided paths.
These inherited facts and the exact path likelihood are pinned below.

1. Global defensive mixture gives a valid bounded estimator

Let P be the target stopped-path law, Qc the full-support controlled path law,
and Q=epsilon P+(1-epsilon)Qc, with 0<epsilon<=1. Densities are with respect
to the same stopped-path measure. For one Q path omega define

  Y(omega) = 1_A(omega) dP/dQ(omega).

Then, without any likelihood-tail assumption,

  E_Q Y = P(A)=p,
  0 <= Y <= M=1/epsilon,
  E_Q Y^2 = integral_A f_P^2/f_Q <= p/epsilon.

The last two claims use f_Q>=epsilon f_P. Thus Var_Q(Y)<=p/epsilon. If
Y_1,...,Y_n are independent draws from this *global path mixture*, standard
Bernstein applied to both tails yields, for L=log(2/delta),

  |Ybar-p| <= sqrt(2 L p/(epsilon n)) + 2 L/(3 epsilon n)

with probability at least 1-delta. This is a valid finite-sample certificate,
not an asymptotic normal interval. It is computable by inversion even though
p is unknown. Put M=1/epsilon, a=2 M L/n, b=2 M L/(3n), and observe Ybar=x.
The following interval has at least 1-delta coverage:

  lower = 0                                              if x<=b,
          ((sqrt(a+4(x-b))-sqrt(a))/2)^2              otherwise;
  upper = ((sqrt(a)+sqrt(a+4(x+b)))/2)^2.

This follows by solving the lower-tail inequality for p to obtain the upper
endpoint, and solving the upper-tail inequality for p to obtain the lower
endpoint. certificate_arithmetic.py implements these equations without
simulating paths. Since the inherited controlled path has positive Gaussian
density, its exact path ratio Lc=dP/dQc is positive, and the mixture weight is
computable from the recorded path log ratio as

  dP/dQ = 1/(epsilon+(1-epsilon)/Lc).

At delta=0.05 and a hypothetical observed mixture mean x=p0, epsilon=1 (the
best global-range case), the inverted interval is [0, 1.1783e-3] for n=10,000,
[0, 2.2620e-3] for n=5,209, and [0, 3.2822e-2] for n=359. Those upper ends
are respectively 2.74e6, 5.26e6 and 7.63e7 times p0. The 10,000-row calculation
is a same-count diagnostic, not a claim that defensive-mixture data exist.
Even the impossible accounting that treats every 20.84M force point as an
independent sample gives upper 5.6615e-7, over 1,315 times p0. Lowering epsilon
only increases M and worsens the range certificate.

For 95% coverage and 10% relative radius, write s=sqrt(2L/(epsilon*n*p0)).
The Bernstein relative radius is s+s^2/3, so it is <=0.1 if and only if

  n*epsilon*p0 >= 786.1917.

At epsilon=1 this sufficient sample count is 1.8266e12 independent paths,
or 7.3062e15 force points at the 4,000-step path cap. This is about 3.51e8
times the entire 20,838,768-force-point candidate budget, even before rerunning
the acquisition. If that 19.4M acquisition is charged inside the budget,
only 1.438768M points remain, enough for at most 359 maximum-length paths.
Without acquisition, the whole budget permits at most 5,209 such paths.
Shorter realized paths can raise the observed count; a hard cost stop then
requires a time-uniform confidence sequence, and does not improve the
bounded-increment scale.

This scaling is not just an artifact of the Bernstein constants. For any
range-only procedure, take the admissible rare-spike variable Y=M with
probability epsilon*mu and zero otherwise; its mean is mu. Compare mu=p0 with
mu=(1+3r)p0, r=0.1. Their relative-r intervals are disjoint. The rescaled
observations are Bernoulli(epsilon*mu), whose one-sample total variation is
3r*epsilon*p0; coupling n draws gives TV<=3nr*epsilon*p0. A confidence
procedure that jointly covers and has relative radius r with probability at
least 1-delta under both laws would distinguish them with TV>=1-2delta.
Therefore n>= (1-2delta)/(3r*epsilon*p0), which is 6.97e9 paths at epsilon=1,
over 334 times the full force budget even if each path used only one force
point. This rules out a uniform relative certificate from the defensive range
bound alone; a certificate may still exploit additional structure of the
known path laws.

2. Why the usual refinements do not close this inherited case

Empirical-Bernstein bounds can replace an unknown variance by the sample
variance, but retain an additive term of order M log(1/delta)/n. At n=10,000,
epsilon=1, the usual Maurer–Pontil range term is 8.61e-4, about 2.00e6 p0.
A small observed sample variance or the inherited 0.64% empirical relative SE
does not rule out an as-yet-unseen rare spike. Bounded-observation test
martingales and time-uniform confidence sequences remain valid under
cost-dependent stopping, but the same obstruction applies: under the
range-only rare-spike law, zero informative draws are overwhelmingly likely.
For direct P paths, the zero-hit probability at n=359 is 0.999999845 and at
n=5,209 is 0.999997758. If all draws are zero, exact one-sided 95% binomial
upper bounds are 0.008310 and 0.00057494, respectively, still many orders
above p0. These are finite-sample statements; they do not treat the inherited
normal-approximation SE as a certificate.

3. Path-mixture placement matters

The useful defensive construction mixes complete stopped paths once:
Q=epsilon P+(1-epsilon)Qc. It gives dP/dQ<=1/epsilon independently of the
number of steps. By contrast, if each of N conditional kernels is mixed,
q_k=epsilon p_k+(1-epsilon)qc,k, then p_k/q_k<=1/epsilon at every step and

  dP_path/dQ_path = product_{k<tau} p_k/q_k <= epsilon^{-tau} <= epsilon^{-4000}.

For epsilon=0.1 the bound is 10^4000; for epsilon=0.5 it is 2^4000. To keep a
whole-path bound at most 10 (or 2), the stepwise epsilon must be at least
10^(-1/4000)=0.9994245 (or 2^(-1/4000)=0.9998267). This nearly removes the
controlled component. A global path mixture avoids that exponential factor,
but its 1/epsilon bound still has the rare-event sample complexity above.

4. The available Gaussian energy bound is finite but useless

For the inherited controlled Euler x-step, with control u_k predictable from
the current state/history, the source code gives

  log L_k = -u_k sqrt(dt/2) xi_k - u_k^2 dt/4,  L_k=p_k/qc,k.

Under Qc, conditional Gaussian integration gives
E[L_k^2 | history]=exp(u_k^2 dt/2). If |u_k|<=80 and tau*dt<=2, then
H=sum_{k<tau}u_k^2 dt<=80^2*2=12,800 and
E_Qc[L_tau^2]<=exp(H/2)<=exp(6,400)=10^2779.5. Consequently

  E_Q[Y^2] <= min(p/epsilon, exp(6,400)/(1-epsilon)).

The second term is far looser than p/epsilon at the rare probability scale.
The observed maximum successful weight is not a pathwise bound because every
Gaussian step has unbounded noise.

Localization does not rescue this control-energy envelope. Under P,
log L_tau=H_tau/4+M_tau, where M has predictable variance H_tau/2. The
exponential-martingale bound gives, for s>Hmax/4,

  P_P(log L_tau>s) <= exp(-(s-Hmax/4)^2/Hmax).

Clipping the Qc likelihood at B=e^s has absolute bias at most this tail
probability, since E_Qc[1_A(L-B)_+] <= P_P(log L>log B). To make that generic
bias bound <=0.1 p0 with Hmax=12,800 requires log B>=3,752.74, or
B>=10^1629.79. This cap is useless for finite-sample concentration. Stopping
at first hit/horizon makes tau<=4,000 and makes H finite; it does not make
the Gaussian likelihood ratio bounded or the event-conditioned tail small.

5. Constructive tighter envelope: certified finite-horizon Doob residual

The inherited target's physical (x,y) Euler state is Markov; the eight z
coordinates are independent and do not enter its drift. Let K_k(s,ds') be the
exact 2D one-step kernel on s=(x,y), and let h_k(s) be the success probability
from s with N-k steps left, with absorbing values 1 on B, 0 on A, and terminal
value 0 in transient states. Then h_k=K_k h_{k+1} on transient states and
h_0(-0.75,-1)=p.

For a positive candidate g_k with the same A/B/terminal boundary values and
g_k>0 wherever h_k>0, define the exactly normalized success-supported kernel

  Z_k(s)=integral K_k(s,ds') g_{k+1}(s'),
  Q^g_k(ds'|s)=K_k(s,ds') g_{k+1}(s')/Z_k(s).

On every successful path, its exact likelihood is

  dP/dQ^g = g_0(s_0) product_{k<tau} r_k(s_k),
  r_k(s)=Z_k(s)/g_k(s).

This identity is obtained by multiplying the step ratios Z_k/g_{k+1} and
telescoping g_k. If g=h, each r_k=1 and every Q^h path succeeds with weight
p: the exact finite-horizon Doob transform has zero variance. It is a standard
known-method comparator, not a new discovery.

For an approximate g, if interval integration proves for every reached
transient state and time that exp(-eta_k)<=r_k(s)<=exp(eta_k), and
sum_{k<tau}eta_k<=h, then every sample weight lies in
[g_0 exp(-h), g_0 exp(h)]. Since p=E_{Q^g}Y is in the same interval,

  Y <= R p,  R=exp(2h),  Var(Y)<=R p^2.

The resulting 95% two-sided Bernstein relative-radius bound is
sqrt(2 R L/n)+2 R L/(3n), L=log(40). At n=10,000 it is <=10% if
R<=12.72, equivalently h<=1.272 or a uniform per-step log residual
eta<=3.18e-4 over 4,000 steps. With n=359 even R=1 does not meet this
particular 10% Bernstein certificate. A deterministic 10% enclosure from g_0
alone needs h<=log(1.1)=0.09531, or eta<=2.383e-5 per step.

This is the requested tighter moment envelope: it is event-scaled, Y<=Rp,
and would make the existing order-10k path count statistically useful if
certified. A tiny finite example checks the arithmetic: for a two-atom event
with Qc(A)=0.5 and epsilon=0.1, the global bound is Y<=10 while the exact
event-specific bound is Y<=p/0.45=2.2222p; at n=10,000 the event-scaled
Bernstein relative radius is 4.10%. The example is a bound demonstration, not
the molecular result.

The inherited proposal does not meet this gate: it uses a clipped numerical
infinite-horizon 2D committor gradient on an observed projection, not the
finite-horizon positive g above; no Z_k/g_k interval bound was computed. Its
10,000-path empirical weight maximum and ESS cannot substitute for that
bound. An actual certificate would need verified 2D kernel integrals and
residual ranges over all transient states (including unbounded y or a
separately certified truncation tail), a sampler for Q^g, and charged
numerical/checking costs. Direct certified dynamic programming of h is an
alternative. Until one of those is done, the finite-sample mixture guarantee
is valid but vacuous, and the inherited p estimate remains uncertified.
