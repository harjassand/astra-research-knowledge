1. Late-event information gate

For two stipulated pure coherent carrier fields with squared overlap
c^2=exp(-eta J), a binary quantum receiver with false-flag probability at most
alpha has minimum miss

 beta=[sqrt((1-alpha)c^2)-sqrt(alpha(1-c^2))]^2, if alpha<c^2;
 beta=0 otherwise.

The timing-aware receiver is granted the exact event time separately for each
alternative. Averaging these individual minima is a lower bound on one actual
unknown-arrival receiver with the same whole-window alpha. For finite-energy,
finite-duration continuous carrier response, J(tau)->0 as tau approaches the
decision endpoint. Consequently beta(tau)->1-alpha. A positive unobserved
f->e arrival density near the endpoint implies positive accepted terminal-e
mass in this restricted model. A finite passive tail does not remove this
boundary: the tail itself contains new erasures, especially after the cavity
has been reset close to vacuum. This is not a universal hardware impossibility
theorem: jump-photon monitoring, a different encoding, nonlinear output states
or a different physical interface may evade the restricted carrier model.

Independent executable derivation and quadrature are in sibling directory
../luna03_quantum_record/terminal_change_point_bound.py and NUMERICAL_RESULTS.json.

2. A concrete alternative: carrier-suppressed two-photon erasure fluorescence

In the slow first sideband frame put delta=Delta+Omega. A Duffing ladder has
E_n=n delta+alpha n(n-1)/2. At delta=-3alpha/2=270MHz, states e and h have
equal energy, as do h,0 and e,2 for the cavity. A two-photon qubit drive can
excite e<->h, while h->e releases a cavity photon pair. Code g/f is off the
two-photon resonance. Full source-calibrated G106 Floquet diagonalization moves
the local e2/h0 avoided-crossing minimum toOmega2481MHz at epsilon217.280MHz;
the sampled gap is1.776952MHz. This is a candidate construction, not new prior-
art clearance or a source-reported experiment.

Plain continuous qubit drive also creates a code-dependent linear converted
cavity field and fails badly. Apply instead a real RF envelope

 u(t)=sqrt(2) u_rms E(t) cos(2pi nu t),

where E has100ns cosine turnon/turnoff ramps. The linear converted field moves
to cavity sidebands +/-nu. In a one-pole linear conversion approximation its
power is reduced relative to unmodulated rms drive by

 R_linear=(kappa/2)^2/[(kappa/2)^2+(2pi nu)^2].

With kappa/2pi=.21MHz and nu20MHz this is about2.76e-5. In second-order
perturbation theory through the f state detuned by d=90MHz, the resonant
two-photon e/h matrix element is

 lambda_2=-sqrt(6)*u_rms^2*d/(d^2-nu^2).

It remains nonzero; its ratio to the static-rms value is d^2/(d^2-nu^2),
about1.052 at nu20MHz. Thus sign modulation suppresses the unwanted linear
carrier without nulling the desired quadratic excitation. These are exact
algebraic statements within the displayed linear/second-order models. Strong
drive, higher levels, bath spectra and actual pump/RF transfer require the
executed full-model test and then physical acquisition.

The test succeeded only at this mechanism level: plain RF gives about21%
accepted Choi error, while the finite modulated variant reaches3.73848% in the
best completed grouped-bath model. It still loses the favorable equal3.8us
finite-SWAP ancilla abstraction3.19754%, and has accepted residual cavity
photons. The correct inference is a specified suppression construction plus a
failed complete-operation comparison, not a working new erasure qubit.

3. A different encoding does not grant free erasure readout

For EF storage, f->e is a logical amplitude decay. Even with a perfect
ground-only erasure flag, rates a=f->e,b=e->g and holdT give

 sf=exp(-aT), se=exp(-bT), u=a(sf-se)/(b-a),
 p_accept=(se+sf+u)/2,
 F_numerator=(se+sf+2sqrt(se sf))/4.

Accepted Choi error is1-F_numerator/p_accept. Twenty-seven independent9x9
matrix-exponential cases agree with this formula to1.11e-16. AtT1.4us, source
a1/26,b1/55, the ideal instantaneous-shelving error is2.62646%; an explicit
finite80ns Gaussian DRAG GE pulse followed by a granted ideal flag/return gives
2.70078%. The ideal flag is used solely as a favorable negative gate. No
positive claim uses an ideal terminal projector.
