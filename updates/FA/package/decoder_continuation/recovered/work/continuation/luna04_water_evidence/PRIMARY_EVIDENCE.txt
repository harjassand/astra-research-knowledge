Primary evidence review for sol04_water
Retrieved 2026-10-13. This is a primary-source audit of the physical interface gate, not an independent validation of the inherited transport model.

Inherited target to be acquired (from the water-separation model)
The required reversible wall law is J_pair = ks [C - H*B/Keq], with C, H, and B in mol m^-3, Keq = 4.72e-4 mol m^-3, and ks = 3.3464052288e-4 m s^-1. The final 50 um model point asks for source pCO2=345 Pa, pH=5.19608, (C,H,B)=(0.0876073,0.00636680,0.00645037) mol m^-3 and J=+2.00239e-7 mol m^-2 s^-1; sink pCO2=10.4 Pa, pH=5.82941, (C,H,B)=(0.00354772,0.00148113,0.00133672) mol m^-3 and J=-2.16473e-7 mol m^-2 s^-1. Added background salt is 0.1-1 uM NaCl. Both signs must be shown on the same catalytically active wet surface. The separate same-skin gate is humid gas permeance >=1.025e-6 mol m^-2 s^-1 Pa^-1 (=3063.0 GPU), ion release within the modeled <=1 uM budget, and effective irreversible particle adsorption below approximately 1.0055e-11 m s^-1 for <1% loss. The adsorption velocity is a model-derived target, not a measured material property. Inputs are in `work/continuation/sol04_water/CHECKPOINT.txt` and the authoritative exact wall/adsorption numbers are in `work/replay/fresh_handoff/work/phase5/water_separation/interface_assay_best50_results.json` (the source checkout equivalent is listed in SOURCE_RECEIPTS.json).

1. Direct adverse control: CA and net CO2 flux without buffer (not an immobilized coating)
Gutknecht, Bisson & Tosteson, “Diffusion of Carbon Dioxide through Lipid Bilayer Membranes: Effects of Carbonic Anhydrase, Bicarbonate, and Unstirred Layers,” Journal of General Physiology 69 (1977), 779-794, DOI 10.1085/jgp.69.6.779. Official article: https://rupress.org/jgp/article/69/6/779/26852/Diffusion-of-carbon-dioxide-through-lipid-bilayer?redirectedFrom=fulltext . The searchable scan is https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/6457/255ae20a283081b14cccad8fb204e8eade76.pdf and is saved at `work/continuation/luna04_water_evidence/evidence/Gutknecht_Bisson_Tosteson_1977.pdf`.

The abstract (scan pp.779-780; PDF lines 12-31) reports 10-100x stimulation of one-way/tracer CO2 flux by about 1 mg/mL CA at pH 7-8, but explicitly says CA enhanced *net* CO2 flux in well-buffered and not unbuffered solutions; buffer supplied protons upstream and absorbed them downstream. In the net-flux experiment (scan pp.788-789, Table II; PDF lines 375-410), the rear solution had 1.7 mM CO2, 40 mM HCO3-, pH 7.5; front side was CO2-free. Table II prints an unbuffered CA-both-sides condition at 1.1 +/- 0.9 (n=3), a buffered no-CA condition at 0.7 +/- 0.5 (n=4), and a buffered CA-both-sides condition at 12.7 +/- 4.3 (n=5), in the scan's OCR-damaged unit string. Since the first two rows differ in both CA and buffer, I do not treat them as a matched unbuffered CA/no-CA contrast or convert the units. The authors' explicit conclusion is that CA stimulates net flux only when solutions are well buffered. This is decisive against transferring buffered CA or isotope-exchange gains to a net low-buffer wall law. It does not prove that every surface design fails, and the membrane/chemistry differs from sol04_water.

2. Closest immobilized CO2 gas/liquid measurements found remain outside the contract
Fu et al., “Ultra-thin enzymatic liquid membrane for CO2 separation and capture,” Nature Communications 9, 990 (2018), DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-03285-x. Primary article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-03285-x . Local extracted article and correction are `work/continuation/sol04_water/evidence/Fu2018.txt` and `Fu2018_correction.txt`.

The CA membrane was loaded by soaking in a 0.05 mM enzyme bath and drying/evaporation into an approximately 18 nm aqueous layer (article para. 0026). FTIR-derived loading was 8.0e11 CA cm^-2, corresponding to about 3.7 mM effective CA in the confined liquid (para. 0038). Its separation demonstration was at 37 C, pH 7.5, 20 vol% CO2 in N2 at 0.48 bar total feed pressure, with aqueous Ca(OH)2 as the downstream sink; peak permeance was 2600 GPU (para. 0037). The pH/temperature measurements used saturated humidification and pure CO2 at relative pressure 0.48 bar (para. 0058). Thus measured throughput is an integrated gas membrane result at a much higher CO2 partial pressure, and is not the net Robin response of an externally low-salt catalytic surface. Its peak is 0.84884 of the inherited permeance requirement (15.12% below); the two materials and operating points differ, so this ratio is a screen rather than a failure certificate.

The authors themselves distinguish CO2 capture, HCO3- transport, and CO2 release across their membrane (para. 0038). Their later rate argument is a calculation: reported EAD times an assumed native CA activity of 1e6 reactions/s predicts 833 GPU, compared with measured 500-2600 GPU (para. 0039). That assumed activity is not the measured low-salt `ks`. Fu's EAD is 1.3284e-8 mol m^-2, 0.20 of the hypothetical 25 nm^2/site full monolayer used for the most favorable CAIX surface conversion below.

Meng et al., “In situ layer-by-layer assembled carbonic anhydrase-coated hollow fiber membrane contactor for rapid CO2 absorption,” Journal of Membrane Science 514 (2016), 556-565, DOI 10.1016/j.memsci.2016.05.020. Primary report: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0376738816303441 . It immobilizes CA on PP/PDMS hollow fibers and reports approximately threefold absorption enhancement for CO2 absorption into K2CO3 on PP. This establishes relevant immobilized-contactor prior art, but the carbonate solvent and integrated absorption rate do not instantiate the low-salt reversible surface law. A separate 2012 immobilized-HCAII scrubber report includes buffer-free feed with in-situ pH control and gas-phase conversion / outlet dissolved-carbon / pH measurements (DOI 10.1016/j.ces.2012.01.019, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2012.01.019); it is still a scrubber-scale pH-controlled assay, not a same-skin two-direction measurement at the target gas pressures and chemistry. Neither report supplies an `ks` fit for the requested contract.

3. Closest unbuffered aqueous capture report is still free enzyme, alkaline, and bulk-integrated
Ferreira et al., “Biocatalytic CO2 Absorption and Structural Studies of Carbonic Anhydrase under Industrially-Relevant Conditions,” International Journal of Molecular Sciences 21 (2020), 2918, DOI 10.3390/ijms21082918. Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7215295/ . Local primary XML/PDF and Figure 5 render are in this directory's `evidence/`.

Figure 5 reports unbuffered mineral-water absorption at pH 9, 10, 11; 2 g sample; initial total pressure 1.5 bar absolute; 30 C; 0.5 mg protein per g solution (therefore 1 mg free BCA in a 2 g sample). Text reports +76% initial capture rate with BCA only at pH 10 and no positive rate effect at pH 9 or 11. The water's measured conductivity was 0.472 mS cm^-1 and initial pH about 8.77 before adjustment (Results 2.1); this is not the 0.1-1 uM background-salt feed. The rate readout is headspace pressure decay in a batch cell; no catalytic surface area, wall loading, or wall C/H/B profile is reported. The paper's 76% cannot be normalized into a surface conductance and does not establish performance at pH 5.20/5.83 or reverse-direction net flux.

4. CAIX low-pH kinetics: reversible exchange and a bounded transfer sensitivity, not wall conductance
Mahon et al., “The Structure of Carbonic Anhydrase IX Is Adapted for Low-pH Catalysis,” Biochemistry 56 (2017), 464-474, DOI 10.1021/acs.biochem.6b00243. Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5322481/ . Methods / assay paragraph and results are paragraphs corresponding to PMC lines 188-190, 424-429, 437-438; Table 1 is PMC lines 199-233.

The hCA IX-c kinetic assay measured 18O exchange at chemical equilibrium by membrane-inlet MS in unbuffered solution with 25 mM bicarbonate, 25 C, ionic strength fixed at 0.2 M using Na2SO4 (PMC lines 188-190). It reports peak kcat/KM = 13.0 +/- 0.1 uM^-1 s^-1 and Zn-water pKa 6.6 +/- 0.2 in Table 1; the body text gives 13 +/- 1, so the uncertainty is internally inconsistent and the calculation uses the wider +/-1 text interval. The rate profile is bell-shaped (line 424), and activity is reported as retained as low as pH 5.5 (line 429); source pH 5.196 is below that specific activity statement, while sink pH 5.829 is near it. This assay measured equilibrium isotope interconversion, not net H+/HCO3- flux across an immobilized interface, and its ionic strength is 2e5-2e6 times the nominal 1-0.1 uM background-salt concentration; the carbon substrate concentration (25 mM) is about 266 times the final model's 0.09406 mM total source CO2 species.

A transparent optimistic conversion is: for a *hypothetical* fully active monolayer with site footprint 25 nm^2, Gamma=6.64216e-8 mol m^-2; if its specific catalytic efficiency were 13e6 M^-1 s^-1 at the target interface, `ks=Gamma*(kcat/KM)/1000=8.6348e-4 m/s`, or 2.58 times target. This assumes peak-pH free-enzyme specificity transfers with no immobilization, access, proton-transfer, or low-salt loss, and is not a measured surface maximum. To show how much pH matters, an explicitly hypothetical single-ionization factor `1/[1+10^(6.6-pH)]` yields at source pH 5.196 an effective `ks=3.2774e-5 m/s` (9.79% target; 3.03e-5-3.53e-5 using the body's 12-14e6 range), and at sink pH 5.829 `ks=1.2521e-4 m/s` (37.42%). This is a sensitivity only: the actual reported CAIX profile is bell-shaped and not represented by this one-ionization formula. Literature therefore provides no defensible measured `ks` bound at the target interface. The code `analyze_evidence.py` reproduces these conversions and writes `analysis_results.json`.

5. Particle evidence supplies mobility/profiles, not same-skin adhesion
Temprano-Coleto et al., “Upper Bounds on the Colloid Separation Efficiency of Diffusiophoresis,” Langmuir (2026), DOI 10.1021/acs.langmuir.5c06768. Primary article / full-text XML: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13192318/ and https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13192318/fullTextXML . The source checkout's saved primary XML is `work/phase5/water_separation/diffusiophoresis_bounds.xml` (hash in SOURCE_RECEIPTS.json); the RQA fresh-handoff copy is under `work/replay/fresh_handoff/work/phase5/water_separation/`.

Its gas-permeable PDMS microchannel experiments used 13.8 kPa gauge inlet pressure for CO2 and N2; the source wall had about 29.5 mM dissolved CO2, estimated ionic concentration about 0.118 mM and Debye length about 29 nm. This is an actual CO2-driven particle near-match, but its CO2 source is about 337 times the model's 0.0876 mM neutral source CO2 and its pCO2 is 40 times the target source pCO2. Table 2 reports for uncoated PS of diameter 0.08 um `Gamma_p=-2.9e-10 m^2/s`, `Dp=5.4e-12 m^2/s`, and `Pe=53`; the paper calculates mobility from reported particle properties and measures fluorescent concentration profiles. It does not measure adsorption velocity or close particle mass on a CA-coated wall. APTES pretreatment was used to prevent adhesion of amine-modified particles to PDMS; that is not a bound for uncoated PS on CA/proton-relay skin. The inherited `k_ads <=1.0055e-11 m/s` is only a model perturbation ceiling and must not be reported as measured adhesion.

Missing-contract conclusion
Across the primary reports examined, there is no acquired surface that simultaneously has (i) two-direction net pair-flux data fitting the specified Robin law near both target states in 0.1-1 uM added salt, (ii) post-coating humid CO2 permeance >=3063 GPU at the specified low partial pressures, and (iii) same-material particle mass closure/adsorption velocity <=1.0055e-11 m/s plus ion release within budget. The decisive evidence is absent, not simply uncertain: reported buffered, alkaline, high-salt, free-enzyme, equilibrium-isotope, or integrated membrane rates do not identify this response.

Testable mechanism and experiment
A plausible, already proposed mechanism is a tethered proton antenna: co-immobilized CA with a His/imidazole-like relay and hydrated spacer may speed exchange of proton equivalents between the CA active site and the very thin adjacent liquid, relieving the same supply/sink bottleneck that appears in the 1977 unbuffered net-flux control. This is a hypothesis, not a validated solution or novelty claim; a 2026 perspective explicitly treats proton antennas as a missing design variable/hypothesis (Wiśniewski, DOI 10.1002/cbic.70519, https://doi.org/10.1002/cbic.70519).

Discriminating experiment: fabricate CA-only, relay-only, active-CA+relay, inactive-CA+relay, and bare/inactive-film controls on one gas-contact substrate. At 25 C, impose the 50 um model's source and sink gas activities (345 Pa and 10.4 Pa CO2), 0.1-1 uM added NaCl, and target pH/C/H/HCO3 states. Reverse the direction and sweep the measured thermodynamic drive through positive and negative values. Measure net carbon/proton-equivalent pair flux and actual near-wall C, H, B, then fit one shared `ks` with residuals and report its uncertainty; isotope exchange alone is insufficient. On the same coupons before and after enzyme/relay coating, measure humid CO2 permeance, water breakthrough/wetting, released ions and enzyme, and activity decay. In the same 50 um channel with 80 nm PS, quantify both outlets and digest/extract wall-retained particles to fit `k_ads`. The proton-antenna hypothesis passes only if CA+relay increases the two-direction fitted `ks` over CA-only while meeting the permeance, ion-release, and adsorption gates without relying on soluble buffer. If it changes only integrated gas throughput or requires measurable salt release, it fails this gate.
