RESULT: no matching measured catalytic water interface found
Date: 2026-10-13

The material gate for `sol04_water` remains unacquired. The primary reports I located give useful warnings and near-matches, but none measures the required reversible net pair-flux conductance on one low-salt catalytic surface, together with coated humid CO2 permeance and particle adhesion.

The physical contract is the 50 um operation's pair law

  J_pair = ks ( C - H B / Keq ),
  Keq = 4.72e-4 mol m^-3,  ks >= 3.3464052288e-4 m s^-1,

with concentrations in mol m^-3. At the source, pCO2=345 Pa, pH=5.19608, total dissolved CO2 species=0.094058 mM and target net J=+2.00239e-7 mol m^-2 s^-1. At the sink, pCO2=10.4 Pa, pH=5.82941, total species=0.00488444 mM and target J=-2.16473e-7 mol m^-2 s^-1. Added background NaCl is 0.1-1 uM. The other same-skin requirements are humid gas permeance >=1.025e-6 mol m^-2 s^-1 Pa^-1 (=3063.0 GPU), ion release within the modeled 1 uM budget, and irreversible particle loss <1% in the final geometry, corresponding to model ceiling k_ads <=1.0055e-11 m s^-1. These numbers are targets from the inherited model, not measurements of a real material.

The strongest directly relevant control is Gutknecht, Bisson & Tosteson (1977): CA increased one-way/tracer transport but, in the reported pH 7.5 unbuffered net-flux assay, did not increase net CO2 flux; the 50 mM HEPES condition did. This isolates the proton supply/sink obstacle and rejects direct transfer of buffered CA activity. It is not a no-go theorem for a newly engineered surface.

The strongest quantitative enzyme conversion found is a sensitivity, not a measured wall conductance. CAIX-c gave peak free-enzyme kcat/KM=13.0 uM^-1 s^-1 (Table 1; the text instead says 13 +/- 1) from 18O equilibrium exchange at 25 mM bicarbonate, 25 C and 0.2 M ionic strength. The target source has 0.09406 mM total carbon species, about 266 times less substrate; the assay ionic strength is 2e5-2e6 times the 0.1-1 uM added-salt window. If the peak specificity transferred unchanged to a fully active 25 nm^2/site monolayer, the conversion would give ks=8.6348e-4 m/s (2.58x target), but this is an optimistic peak-pH premise, not measured in the target chemistry. Applying a one-ionization pH sensitivity using the paper's Zn-water pKa=6.6 instead gives source ks=3.2774e-5 m/s (9.79% of target) and sink ks=1.2521e-4 m/s (37.42%). The paper reports a bell-shaped activity profile, so this one-ionization calculation is only a sensitivity. There is no direct empirical `ks` bound to transfer.

Closest membrane data are similarly incomplete. Fu et al. measured up to 2600 GPU on an approximately 18 nm CA-containing liquid membrane with 8.0e11 CA cm^-2 (=1.3284e-8 mol m^-2 EAD, or 0.20 of the hypothetical monolayer) and 3.7 mM confined CA. The membrane operated near pH 7.5 and 37 C; the main demonstration used 20% CO2 in gas at 0.48 bar total pressure and a Ca(OH)2 sink. Its permeance is 84.9% of the inherited 3063 GPU target, but it does not identify surface `ks`, and its pressure, surface, pH and liquid membrane differ. Ferreira et al. reported +76% initial CO2 capture rate from free BCA at pH 10 in mineral water, but the assay was a 2 g batch at 30 C and 1.5 bar absolute with 0.5 mg protein/g solution; water conductivity was 0.472 mS/cm. It is not low-salt, immobilized, or at target pH. No measured rate from it normalizes to a wall coefficient.

For particles, a 2026 CO2-driven PDMS microchannel report supplies a useful mobility and concentration-profile near-match: 80 nm uncoated PS had Gamma_p=-2.9e-10 m^2/s, Dp=5.4e-12 m^2/s, Pe=53, at a source with about 29.5 mM dissolved CO2 and estimated 0.118 mM ions. The source pCO2 was 13.8 kPa gauge, versus 345 Pa target. The paper did not measure CA-skin adhesion or close particle mass on the wall; its APTES pretreatment was to reduce adhesion of amine-modified particles to PDMS. The model ceiling k_ads=1.0055e-11 m/s therefore remains unverified.

A testable mechanism is a tethered His/imidazole proton antenna on a hydrated spacer co-immobilized with CA. It could reduce the interfacial proton-equivalent transfer resistance that limits unbuffered net flux. This is a published hypothesis (Wiśniewski 2026 perspective, DOI 10.1002/cbic.70519), not a validated material or a novelty claim. Compare bare, inactive-coated, CA-only, relay-only, active-CA+relay and inactive-CA+relay surfaces on the same gas-contact substrate. At 25 C, use the exact target gas activities, pH, dissolved species and 0.1-1 uM salt; sweep `C-HB/Keq` through both signs and fit net pair flux, measuring actual near-wall C/H/B. On the same coupons measure post-coating humid permeance, wetting, ion/enzyme leaching, and rate stability. In the same 50 um channel, quantify both particle outlets and digest/extract the wall to fit `k_ads`. The mechanism passes only if the active CA+relay coating raises two-direction fitted `ks` over CA-only and satisfies all three same-surface gates without soluble buffer.
