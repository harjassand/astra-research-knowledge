1. ANCHOR-FIXED FILTER DECODING (R24 and R23, independently)
-----------------------------------------------------------
This removes the section42 decoder obstruction at the structural level.
Keep the FULL factor spaces on which filtered rows have their actual caps.
For every positive local filter F_j with 0<F_j<=I on a proper support S_j,
add the orthogonal anchor A_j=Proj Ran(I-F_j). Do NOT compress the other
rows into ker A_j and then renormalize their ranges.

For clause a, let F_(a,1),...,F_(a,l_a) be any such local filters inside
X_a; they need not commute or be shared by other clauses. Define

 R'_a=F_(a,l_a)...F_(a,1) R_a.

Then the exact common kernel of {R'_a,A_j} equals the exact common kernel
of {R_a,A_j}. Indeed A_j psi=0 implies F_j psi=psi; moving the filter
product through the inner product reverses its order, and that reverse
product still fixes psi. No inverse filter, success probability, or
state-dependent normalization is needed. The same proof gives equality
of GLOBAL bad row sums after adjoining all lifted anchor row spaces.
This is stronger than only mapping a chosen state.

For an arity-two instance, assume the section40 structural data on V_i:
beta_i=sum_(a containing i)r_a/s_(a,i), S_i=2sum_a s_(a,i),
codimV_i+rank(I-H_i)<=beta_i, F_i=sqrt(H_i).
Set A_i=Proj Ran(I-H_i) inside V_i. Its relative rank is at most
beta_i/(d_i-beta_i). The filtered clause relative ranks are at most
r_a/[(d_i-beta_i)(d_j-beta_j)], and every proper normalized marginal
is at most max(S_i/(d_j-beta_j), S_j/(d_i-beta_i)).

For equal original dimensions d, site incidence at most nu, r_a<=p d^2,
and s_(a,i)=sqrt(r_a), put b=nu sqrt(p)<1. Then all anchors have
relative rank <=b/(1-b), every filtered clause has relative rank
<=p/(1-b)^2, and every proper normalized marginal is <=2b/(1-b).
Unary anchor guards are exactly their relative ranks. Every original
clause overlaps at most two new unary constraints, so augmented overlap
degree is <=max(Delta+2,nu). Taking p sufficiently small makes the
augmented system satisfy the ordinary symmetric QLLL condition. Thus a
nonzero anchor-fixed exact common kernel exists; identity decoding cannot
be vacuous. This does not supply an algorithm preparing it.

Input acquisition and exact rank of the anchor supports remain charged.
Approximate numerical filters with tiny nonzero attenuation in every
direction cannot be used as if their support ranks were small.

2. SINGLE MAXIMAL DENSE CUT (ONE-SIDED BIT-HEIGHT ROUTE)
--------------------------------------------------------
Fix R subset A tensor B, dimension r, represented rationally. Put
h(L)=dim[R intersect(L tensor B)] and g(L)=h(L)-s dimL.
The function h is supermodular on the subspace lattice: the sum of the
two row intersections for L,M lies in the intersection for L+M, while
their intersection is the row intersection for L intersect M. Therefore
g-maximizers are closed under sum and intersection. There is a unique
largest maximizing L_* (and a unique smallest one).

Since g(0)=0, h(L_*)>=s dimL_*. Orthogonally remove L_* in ONE cut.
For E subset L_*^perp, the projected residual satisfies

 h_res(E)=h(L_* directsum E)-h(L_*).

For nonzero E, maximality by inclusion forces
g(L_* directsum E)<g(L_*), so h_res(E)<s dimE. Thus the single cut
has the same strict post-cut condition as section39's adaptive cuts,
and dimL_*<=h(L_*)/s. A single acquired rational L_* and one exact
projection avoid adaptive coefficient-height multiplication.

Weighted shrunk-subspace reduction: write row slices K_b:C^r->A so
h(L)=dim{u:K_bu in L for every b}. For integer s, use Kraus maps
K_(b,j):u -> K_bu tensor e_j into A tensor C^s, j=1,...,s. Then
dim span_(b,j) K_(b,j)M=s dim span_b K_bM, and maximum deficiency is
max_M[dimM-s dim span_b K_bM]=max_L g(L).

For rational s=t/u with t,u positive integers, use domain C^r tensor C^u,
codomain A tensor C^t, and all maps K_b tensor |j><l|. Uniqueness of
the largest maximum-deficiency input subspace forces invariance under
the full GL(u) symmetry, hence it has form M_* tensor C^u. Its image
has form L_* tensor C^t. The maximized deficiency equals u max_L g(L).
Largest maximizers can be obtained from smallest canonical maximizers
for the adjoint Kraus family: for maps C_j:n->m and smallest optimal
shrunk W in the adjoint input C^m, set V=W^perp and
U=(span_j C_j^*W)^perp. This is the largest optimal original input.

Primary algorithmic source found: Franks--Soma--Goemans, Shrunk subspaces
via operator Sinkhorn iteration, arXiv:2207.08311, sections3,6 and
AppendicesA--B. Their canonical smallest optimal shrunk-subspace theorem
includes polynomial rational bit complexity and exact rounding. Applying
it through the displayed polynomial-size rational amplification is a
concrete candidate acquisition theorem for THIS one-sided dense cut.
Exact theorem hypotheses and rectangular/adjoint conventions still need
line-by-line verification before importing the running-time claim.

Update: Theorem3.15 was opened in the primary PDF. It explicitly treats
m-by-n Kraus operators and bounds intermediate bit complexity by
poly(m,n,number_of_Kraus_maps,logM), with Gaussian-integer input. Its
Theorem3.14 gives rational canonical-projector denominator exp(poly).
Clearing all rational Kraus denominators costs polynomial total bits.
The adjoint construction above gives the largest optimal input, including
the zero-deficiency case; no generic arbitrary optimal shrink space is
silently substituted for this canonical maximizer.

3. ARBITRARY-ARITY OPEN INTERFACE
--------------------------------
A guard for Tr_i(P)/d_i is a marginal on the COMPLEMENT of site i, and
is sufficient for all fresh subsets containing i. Capping marginals on
individual sites after tracing all other legs is insufficient at k>2.
One-sided scaling on the proper block X\{i} acquires the required
site-i guard, but filters for distinct i overlap and need not commute.
Composing them can invalidate earlier normalized guard caps even though
anchor-fixed identity decoding remains exact. The unresolved task is
simultaneous proper-block guard acquisition, or a global encoder able to
choose an equivalent one-sided guarded representative at each first touch.

5. POLYNOMIAL CONDITIONING OF THE ONE-SIDED OBJECTIVE
-----------------------------------------------------
This part reduces quantitatively to an explicit primary-source theorem.
Let s=t/u be positive rational, with t,u of polynomial MAGNITUDE in the
explicit local input dimensions (our use s=constant*d_B has this form).
After the canonical cut, h(E)<s dimE for every nonzero E. Since h is
integer, choose delta=1/(2u d_A). Then

                 h(E)<(s-delta)dimE

still holds for every nonzero E. The section39 flag/coercivity proof
therefore shows inf_(0<C<=I) f_(s-delta)(C)>-infinity.

Let Phi(C)=G_C be the rational completely positive map defined by the
row slices. The majorization-scaling objective of Franks--Soma--Goemans
section4, with input vector alpha=(s-delta,...,s-delta), output vector
beta=(1,...,1) of length r_0, and size parameter k=r_0, is

 Tr[e^z Phi(X^-1)Y^-1]+(s-delta)logdetX+logdetY-r_0 z,
                           X>=I, Y>=I, z>=0.

For fixed C=X^-1, AM-GM gives its infimum over Y,z as exactly
r_0+f_(s-delta)(C). Equality follows by choosing Y=e^z Phi(C)
with z large enough that Y>=I. Thus its capacity is finite. Their
Theorem4.16 gives a lower bound -L for f_(s-delta)(C), where L is
polynomial in rational input bit height, explicit dimensions, t,u;
clear Kraus denominators first and account for the scalar shift.

Consequently, for 0<C<=I,

               f_s(C)>=-L+delta(-logdetC).               (23.4)

For arbitrary H>0 put v=max(1,||H||op) and C=H/v. Since
f_s(H)=f_s(C)+(r_0-sd_A)logv, equation(23.4) gives

 Q_s(H)>=-L+delta(-logdetC)
                    -(sd_A-r_0)logv+sTrH.              (23.5)

On the sublevel Q_s(H)<=Q_s(I)+1, this first bounds v by a polynomial:
the linear sv term dominates sd_A logv. It then bounds -logdetC by
a polynomial, and hence

       exp(-poly(input_size)) I <=H<=poly(input_size) I. (23.6)

These are BIT/conditioning bounds, with no promised inverse angle.
They prove coercivity and existence of the unconstrained minimizer.
They also bound the Riemannian diameter of the entire relevant sublevel
by a polynomial. Using the full ambient A-space after the dense cut
avoids introducing irrational orthonormal coordinates for L^perp.
Setting H to Pi_V H Pi_V+Pi_L never increases Q_s: the row Gram
depends only on its VV block, determinant block inequalities remove
cross terms, and -logdetH_LL+TrH_LL is minimized at I_L. Thus the
algorithm may keep H block diagonal with H_L=I exactly.

6. POLYNOMIAL ITERATIONS AND A RATIONAL SPARSE OUTPUT
----------------------------------------------------
Primary optimization source: Allen-Zhu--Garg--Li--Oliveira--Wigderson,
arXiv:1804.01076, Theorem4.3 and finite-arithmetic AppendixC.4.
The displayed theorem is a general second-order geodesic minimization
result; its use below needs only a polynomial rescaling of trust radii.

For any geodesic H(z)=H_0^(1/2) exp(zD)H_0^(1/2), diagonalize D.
Cauchy--Binet writes logdetG_H(z) as log(sum_I c_I exp(z w_I)),
c_I>=0, with each w_I a sum of r_0 eigenvalues of D. Thus the
log-determinant has nonnegative second derivative, and its absolute
third derivative is at most2r_0||D||op times its second derivative.
The trace term has the same properties with factor||D||op. The
-slogdet term is affine. Therefore Q_s is geodesically convex and
second-order robust on trust balls ||D||op<=1/(2r_0) (r_0=0 is trivial).
The general proof of Theorem4.3 then has polynomially many iterations
O(r_0 R log(initial_gap/eta)), where R is the polynomial diameter
from(23.6). Its quadratic trust subproblem is a convex quadratic SDP
over -I/(2r_0)<=D<=I/(2r_0), with polynomial-size coefficients.

All derivatives use explicit local Gram inverses. By(23.6) and the
rational determinant bound for S*S, their magnitudes and Lipschitz
constants have polynomial LOGARITHMS. Matrix square roots/exponentials,
convex quadratic solves, objective comparisons and rounding therefore
need polynomially many bits. Round each iterate to a rational Hermitian
matrix with error much smaller than its exp(-poly) positive lower bound;
accept within a poly-iteration error budget. The exact objective proof
then loses at most eta/2. No exponentially many iterations are hidden
in the conditioning bound.

Only inverse-polynomial objective accuracy is needed for a sparse output.
At an eta-minimizer H_0 on the bounded sublevel, the full geodesic
gradient is

                Z=rho_0-sI+sH_0.

The geodesic Hessian in a unit Frobenius direction is at most
M=r_0^2+e*s*TrH_0 on a unit neighborhood, a polynomial bound.
A short step in -Z shows ||Z||F<=sqrt(2M eta). Choose eta so
||Z||op<=s/32. Then H_0<=(33/32)I and rho_0<=(33/32)sI.

Choose a rational threshold tau in[1/4,1/2] at inverse-polynomial
distance from the spectrum of H_0. Among2d_A+1 equally spaced
candidates at spacing1/[8(d_A+1)], at least one is at distance
>=1/[24(d_A+1)] from every eigenvalue; certified eigenvalue
approximations locate such a candidate. Let E={H_0<tau} and c=1/tau.
For its rank ell, approximate stationarity gives

 r_0>=Tr(E rho_0)>=s(1-tau-1/32)ell,
                         hence ell<3r_0/s.              (23.7)

Now Hhat=min(cH_0,I). The ratio C=Hhat/H_0 lies between
(32/33)I and4I. Row-Gram comparison gives

                  ||Tr_B P_Hhat||op<5s.                 (23.8)

Acquire an exact RATIONAL projector Q_E close to E: approximate its
gapped spectral subspace, round a basis, project it exactly into V,
and use its rational Gram inverse. Its rank is exactly ell. On its
range approximate sqrt(cH_0) at exp(-poly) absolute precision by a
rational Hermitian contraction B; set

            F=I-Q_E+Q_E B Q_E,           0<F<=I.

The spectral gap at tau keeps B strictly below I on Q_E, and the
positive bound(23.6) permits rational rounding while retaining F>0.
Choose the error relative to the exp(-poly) smallest singular value
of sqrt(Hhat) so the row projectors differ in operator norm by less
than s/d_B. Positive partial trace then changes the marginal by less
than s. Thus the rational output row space

             R'=(F Pi_V tensor I_B)R

has actual marginal ||Tr_B P_R'||op<=6s. Its row projector is
EXACT rational, obtained by a rational Gram inverse. Its combined
proper-block anchor A=Pi_L+Q_E has rank at most3r/s, and

 R+(RanA tensor B)=R'+(RanA tensor B).                   (23.9)

Every matrix dimension, numerator/denominator bit count, iteration,
and spectral precision here is polynomial in the explicit local
input and in the magnitudes t,u. This is a reconstructed one-sided
guard acquisition theorem using the two cited primary algorithms;
it still needs an independent proof audit, and it is not yet the
global satisfying-state preparation algorithm.

For a clause of relative rank p and fresh site i, set A=H_(X\{i}),
B=H_i and s=alpha*d_i/6. Then(23.8)--(23.9) yield a rational
representative with normalized Tr_i guard<=alpha and a proper-block
anchor of relative rank at most18p/alpha. Such anchors have arity
one less than the original clause and can be treated recursively.

7. ALL-ARITY RECURSIVE PRESENTATIONS WITH IDENTITY DECODING
----------------------------------------------------------
This gives a concrete polynomial guard-acquisition interface for the
full encoder, without simultaneously composing overlapping filters.
It is NOT itself an acquired global kernel-state preparation algorithm.

Fix maximum original arity k and overlap degree Delta. Define
N_1=1, N_t=1+t N_(t-1); hence

 N_t=t! sum_(j=1)^t 1/j! < e t!.

Set D_*=(Delta+1)N_k-1. Choose any positive rational gamma with
e(D_*+1)gamma<1 (a downstream cluster theorem may demand a still
smaller fixed gamma). Define rational thresholds

 q_1=gamma,
 q_t=q_(t-1)^2/18              for2<=t<=k,
 alpha_t=q_(t-1)              for2<=t<=k.

Equivalently q_k=18(gamma/18)^(2^(k-1)). Assume every original local
projector has relative rank at most q_k. Smaller-arity original clauses
then automatically obey their corresponding q_t bound.

For a node v of arity t>=2 carrying rational row space R_v on S_v,
and EVERY i in S_v, apply the one-sided compiler to

 A=H_(S_v\{i}), B=H_i, s=alpha_t*d_i/6.

Acquire its rational representative R'_(v,i), with rank at most rankR_v,

 ||Tr_i(P'_(v,i))/d_i||op<=alpha_t<=gamma,                (23.10)
 relative_rank(R_(v,i))<=18 q_t/alpha_t=q_(t-1),          (23.11)

          =R'_(v,i)+(R_(v,i) tensor H_i).                (23.12)

Equation(23.12), with all child anchors present, and bottom-up

 B_fixed = sum_(original roots v) lifted R_v
                       +sum_(nonroot nodes v) lifted R_v. (23.13)

Thus EVERY choice of representatives has the SAME exact joint kernel

Decoding is the identity on the original physical registers: there is

 W_t=t! sum_(j=0)^(t-1)1/j! < e t!.

site set Y, choose any i in Y and use its representative R'_(v,i).

                  ||W||_(2,tau)<=gamma^[q/(2(D_*+1))].   (23.14)

canonical Q_v has every fresh-subset guard<=beta, replace gamma by
gamma+beta. This uses effects without normalizing their range joins.

The SAME anchor hierarchy works for every causal choice. No new
constraint, filter, support, or rank debt is acquired at runtime when

words only. A sum of arbitrarily repeated words, normalized joined

10. EXACT SOURCE-ONLY SINGULAR SCHUR STEP
------------------------------------------------
This is a local positive interface for the global construction, not a
global acquisition theorem. It allows consistency deletion but excludes
an autonomous spectator sector. All displayed spaces below are explicit
finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces; their FULL dimensions must be charged.

Split a current local input as X directsum T (old good coordinates and
available tag coordinates). One incoming row equation has source space S
and map C=[A B]:X directsum T -> S. No rank or angle assumption on B.
Put Q=BB^+ (the orthoprojector onto Ran B), D=(I-Q)A, X0=ker D,
and L=-B^+ A|X0:X0 -> Ran B^*. Then

 rank D+rank B=rank[A B]=:r,                              (23.15)
 ker[A B] = graph L directsum ker B.                     (23.16)

Proof of(23.15): projection of Ran[A B] onto (RanB)^perp is RanD,
and its projection-kernel is exactly RanB. For(23.16), projecting
A x+B t=0 onto (RanB)^perp gives x in X0; the remaining equation
gives t=Lx+t0 uniquely with t0 in kerB. These summands are orthogonal
because RanB^* is orthogonal to kerB.

On X0 directsum RanB^*, the canonical graph unitary is

 U_L = [ (I+L^*L)^(-1/2)  -L^*(I+LL^*)^(-1/2)
         L(I+L^*L)^(-1/2)  (I+LL^*)^(-1/2) ].            (23.17)

Extend it by identity on X0^perp within X and on kerB. It sends the
canonical selected subcode X0 directsum kerB onto the EXACT new kernel.
It fixes kerL within X0 and kerL^* within RanB^*. Consequently

 rank(U_L-I)<=2rankL<=2rankB,
 (U_L-I)^*(U_L-I)<=4 Proj_(RanL^* directsum RanL).         (23.18)

The deleted consistency sector has dimension rankD. The UNION of the
deleted and moved sectors has dimension at most rankD+2rankB<=2r.
In particular, if A=0, then D=L=0 and U_L=I: no zero-source old-good
spectator is moved. Small singular values of B may make L enormous but
do not change any rank count or the norm-one property of U_L.

If rational bases and C are supplied explicitly with D_tot dimensions
and bit height H, Gaussian rank calculations, rational pseudoinverses,
and rational projections have polynomial bit cost in D_tot,H. Every
nonzero singular value is at least exp[-poly(D_tot,H)] by rational minor
bounds. Formula(23.17) can therefore be approximated to operator error
zeta with polynomial classical bit/circuit-synthesis cost in D_tot,H,
log(1/zeta); a generic dense unitary synthesis charges poly(D_tot) gates.
No black-box inverse-angle implementation is inferred. A support of
L physical sites can have D_tot=d^L; the bound is then exponential inL.

This resolves the algebraic 'hidden zeroth-order spectator' issue for a
SINGLE explicit Schur block. It does not provide the linked global block
decomposition, a source-rank decay invariant, or succinct implementation
when elimination spreads across an unbounded connected support.

11. TELEPORTING WORDS SEPARATE SOURCE RANK FROM FRESH-TOUCH HS DECAY
------------------------------------------------
Let n>=2 and local dimension d>=2. Let P_j be the Bell rank-one projector
on sites j,j+1, lifted by identities elsewhere, with Bell vector
Omega=d^(-1/2) sum_a |a,a>. Every proper normalized marginal is I/d^2;
the relative rank is p=1/d^2 and overlap degree at most2. For rational
input projectors no irrational Bell coefficients are needed: P_j entries
are exactly0 or1/d.

For the chronological product W=P_1 P_2 ... P_(n-1), direct contraction
of Bell indices gives

 W=d^[-(n-2)] V,
 V^*V=P_(n-1), VV^*=P_1,                               (23.19)

where V is a partial isometry shifting the n-2 free input site indices
to the n-2 free output sites. Thus

 rankW=d^(n-2), rankW/d^n=1/d^2,
 W^*W=d^[-2(n-2)] P_(n-1),
 tau(W^*W)=d^[-2(n-1)].                                 (23.20)

For example on a computational input x1,...,xn, the product is zero
unless x_(n-1)=x_n, and otherwise equals
d^[-(n-1)] sum_a |a,a,x1,...,x_(n-2)>.
This proves all identities, including n=2. There are n-1 distinct fresh
gates and exponentially decaying normalized HS weight. Nevertheless
normalizing either the final source effect or the range erases all that
weight and leaves a projector of relative rank p at every length.

Therefore a proposed positive long-channel decomposition cannot obtain
exponential RANK decay merely by replacing a small chronological word
with its normalized source/range projector, even when every original
guard is arbitrarily small. Fixed-support source-rank preservation from
section10 does not repair this: source rank is retained, not multiplied.
This is an obstruction to that proof interface, not a hard state-choice
instance and not a no-go theorem for a linked encoder using additional
anchor selection or different exact cancellation.

12. NORMALIZED-JOIN DICHOTOMY WITH A GUARDED OLD-SOURCE TRANSPORT
------------------------------------------------
This gives a direction-sensitive way to normalize a newly exposed row
without normalizing a tiny tail at its original incoming source. It is
an exact acquired local interface when the old projector is explicit.
Its global implementation is still an open gate.

Let C be ANY old bad orthoprojector on A, K=I-C, and let B be genuinely
fresh. Let P be the ACTUAL original/new row projector on A tensor B with

             Tr_B P/d_B <= alpha I_A.                  (23.21)

P may also act trivially on old spectator sites included in A. No cap
or locality assumption on C is needed for the algebra. On R=RanP split
the compression P(K tensor I_B)P at some0<eta<1. Let P_hi and P_lo
be its spectral subspace projectors for eigenvalues>=eta and<eta.
Define E_hi=Proj Ran((K tensor I)P_hi), and E_lo similarly, omitting
the zero residual modes. Because the split diagonalizes the compression,
the two residual images are orthogonal, and

 Proj(RanC tensor B + RanP)
       = C tensor I_B + E_hi + E_lo.                    (23.22)

The high channel has a genuine normalized marginal guard:

 E_hi <= eta^(-1) (K tensor I)P(K tensor I),
             Tr_B E_hi/d_B <= (alpha/eta) K.            (23.23)

Its row-normalizing inverse uses only singular values>=sqrt(eta).
Thus given succinct circuits for C and P, its spectral/polar filter can
be approximated without resolving tiny nonzero residual singular values.
This assertion still charges the cost of the supplied C circuit.

For the low source space, ell=rankP_lo, every unit row has OLD bad
overlap at least1-eta. Taking its trace and using P_lo<=P gives

 (1-eta)ell <= Tr[P_lo(C tensor I)]
             <= Tr[P(C tensor I)]
             <= alpha d_B rankC,
        ell <= alpha d_B rankC/(1-eta).                (23.24)

This is a real RANK charge to the old source, not a small unnormalized
tail. If C has fractional rank q on A, the newly exposed low residual
has fractional rank at most alpha q/(1-eta) on A tensor B. Repeated
fresh low-channel extensions would therefore multiply this rank budget,
PROVIDED a causal decomposition isolates each parent's old channel and
does not recharge the entire old join at every step.

More strongly there is a norm-one creation map with GUARDED ACTUAL
NORMALIZED INPUT. On R_lo let

 T=polar((C tensor I)P_lo),
 V=polar((K tensor I)P_lo),
                         B_trans=V T^*.               (23.25)

Here T is an isometry on all R_lo because its Gram is at least1-eta;
V is a partial isometry whose initial space omits exactly zero residual
modes. Therefore RanB_trans=RanE_lo, ||B_trans||<=1, and

 B_trans^*B_trans <= T T^*
    =Proj Ran((C tensor I)P_lo)
    <=(1-eta)^(-1)(C tensor I)P_lo(C tensor I)
    <=(1-eta)^(-1)(C tensor I)P(C tensor I).              (23.26)

Consequently

     Tr_B(B_trans^*B_trans)/d_B
                        <= alpha/(1-eta) C.            (23.27)

Thus normalization of an arbitrarily tiny residual does not erase its
fresh guard WHEN it is fed from the normalized OLD bad projection of
the same source row. The outgoing range E_lo can have a heavy marginal.
The reverse/annihilation direction has effect B_trans B_trans^*=E_lo
and has no asserted cap. This distinction is essential for a causal
encoder: one cannot replace every constraint effect by(23.27).

For an orthogonal old-channel decomposition C=sum_j C_j, equation
(23.26) implies the exact positive per-source charge

 Tr[(C_j tensor I) B_trans^*B_trans]
             <= alpha d_B rankC_j/(1-eta).              (23.28)

It does NOT provide an integer-rank partition of E_lo into local C_j
descendants. Coherent low source vectors can involve many old channels;
assigning fractional trace credit to a parent does not make the resolver
local on that parent's original support. This is the remaining linked
decomposition issue, rather than an angle loss in the one-step bound.

If desired, apply section6's one-sided compiler to the actual E_lo with
target cap a_target. Its proper-old-block anchor has rank at most

       18 alpha/[a_target(1-eta)] rankC.                (23.29)

For alpha much smaller than a_target this is a contractive propagation
budget. This requires acquiring the residual row space and the old C,
and so does not yet give a global polynomial dynamic-anchor algorithm.

Finite precision: when C,P are explicit rational matrices, choose a
gapped threshold eta in[1/3,2/3] using the rational candidate-threshold
method of section6. Approximate its spectral subspace by an exact rational
row subspace of RanP to operator accuracy below1/24. Both its old-overlap
Gram and its complementary high residual Gram are then at least1/4.
The exact two residual images need not stay orthogonal after this rational
rounding, but their SUM is exactly Ran(KP), since the rational row split
still spans RanP. All guard/rank/source bounds hold with factor4 in place
of1/eta and1/(1-eta). Rational range calculations determine exact zero
modes; rational minor bounds give nonzero singular-value logarithms
polynomial in explicit dimension and bit height. Dense acquisition and
polar approximation are polynomial in those charged explicit quantities.
For a succinct old C on an unbounded support, none of these dense-matrix
or minor bounds is a polynomial acquisition guarantee.

This strengthens the normalized-join interface beyond section11's Bell
word obstruction: Bell transport has high residual singular values and
falls in(23.23), whereas genuinely near-contained rows fall in the
old-source transport(23.25). Full preparation still needs a positive
causal decomposition and charged implementation of the low resolvers.

13. WHAT A GUARDED HISTORY LIFT DOES AND DOES NOT SUPPLY
------------------------------------------------
Let J_B:x -> x tensor Omega_(B,B') append a normalized Bell purification
on a TRULY fresh physical site B. For any acquired T on old A tensor B,

 J_B^*(T^*T tensor I_(B'))J_B = Tr_B(T^*T)/d_B.          (23.30)

Thus an actual effect guard Tr_B(T^*T)/d_B<=alpha I_A makes the lifted
creation (T tensor I)J_B an OPERATOR contraction of norm<=sqrt(alpha).
For section12's low transport, the bound holds on the old source C with
alpha replaced by alpha/(1-eta). No tiny-tail normalization is present.

For a rooted chronological history tree with at most b children per node,
retain orthogonal history labels. Suppose EACH edge appends its genuinely
fresh Bell factors and applies an acquired map with this guard. Define L
by the sum of child-creation maps between history sectors. Distinct edges
have orthogonal output labels, so

              L^*L<=b alpha I, ||L||<=sqrt(b alpha).     (23.31)

If b alpha<1, the lift has ||(I-L)^(-1)||<=1/(1-sqrt(b alpha)). This is
a genuine bounded-source homotopy interface, stronger than a physical
word's normalized HS bound. An exponentially large history Hilbert space
can be stored with polynomially many qubits, but an efficient circuit
still requires acquired edge maps and computable history controls. The
statement is NOT an implementation of the low polar V in(23.25).

Two further obstructions must stay explicit. First, a mixture obtained
by tracing history labels cannot exploit cancellation between different
source histories. Every terminal decoded branch would have to lie in the
exact original kernel. If instead the satisfying decoder coherently sums
histories, its normalization and success probability must be charged;
orthogonal labels by themselves do not perform that sum for free.
Second, low/near-contained constraints may remove an entire old logical
direction even though its fresh bad probability is tiny (R24 section4).
Consequently a lifted creation contraction alone does not prove a full
old-code-preserving satisfying extension. Free diagonal directions or
an acquired selected subcode must absorb this consistency condition.

There is a simple gap audit for a proposed unchanged source complex. Let
d0=(P_a)_a be the original degree-zero row map and let h d0=I-ProjK on
degree zero. For a unit vector psi orthogonal to K,

      1=||h d0 psi|| <=||h|| ||d0 psi||.

Hence gap(sum_a P_a)>=||h||^(-2). Adding higher syzygy/source degrees
without changing this degree-zero identity cannot produce a uniformly
bounded homotopy in the no-gap target. A successful auxiliary lift must
alter the physical embedding/metric, use a strict selected subcode, or
acquire a different decoder; its logical sector cannot be silently taken
to be the unchanged degree-zero physical K with bounded identity decoder.

14. WEIGHTED COHERENT ERASURE OF AN ACQUIRED GUARDED HISTORY TREE
---------------------------------------------------------------
This refines section13: for a bounded-branch rooted tree, coherent erasure
need NOT cause an exponential loss. It supplies a charged decoder for an
already acquired guarded source recurrence. It does not supply the missing
exact K-valued recurrence or normalize a general disconnected polymer gas.

Let a rooted history tree have at most b children at every node. Its history
sector E_h is an acquired Hilbert space, embedded in a common work register,
and every edge h->hc has an acquired contraction T_hc:E_h->E_hc satisfying

                         ||T_hc|| <= sqrt(alpha).       (23.32)

The actual fresh-site effect guard gives precisely(23.32): append a Bell
pair on the genuinely fresh physical factors, or equivalently include its
Bell-state projector before the physical map. Including this projector
makes(23.32) hold as a FULL operator bound rather than only on an informal
reachable state. It acts identically on the valid freshly purified inputs.
Already visited interactions do not automatically satisfy(23.32).

Let A_empty=I and A_h be the chronological edge product to h. Let J_h:E_h->H
be acquired isometric padding maps into one common decoded output H. Padding
may prepare the remaining untouched Bell factors. Define the desired sum

                         M=sum_h J_h A_h.              (23.33)

All sums may first be taken over a finite depth m; the estimates are uniform
in m. Root input is a declared E_empty, not a silently enlarged physical
space. The root may be one-dimensional when preparing a specified purified
product input. If an exact algebraic recurrence proves RanM subset K tensor
reference, then erasing its histories produces an exact satisfying state.
The theorem here does not prove that algebraic premise.

On the ORTHOGONAL sum of history sectors define

 L=sum_(h->hc) |hc><h| tensor T_hc.

Every child has a unique parent, so the output sectors from distinct edges
are orthogonal and L*L is block diagonal by parent. Therefore

 L*L <= b alpha I,                   ||L||<=sqrt(b alpha).

Choose a scalar t with

                    sqrt(b alpha)<t<1/sqrt(b).         (23.34)

Such t exists iff alpha<1/b^2. The reweighted history map is

 N_t=(I-L/t)^(-1)J_root
      =sum_h t^(-|h|)|h> tensor A_h,
 ||N_t|| <= [1-sqrt(b alpha)/t]^(-1).                  (23.35)

Define the coherent eraser

 F_t=sum_h t^|h| J_h <h|,
 Z_m=sum_(ell=0)^m b^ell t^(2ell)
                         <=[1-bt^2]^(-1).

The row-operator Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives ||F_t||<=sqrt(Z_m),
regardless of overlaps among the decoded J_h ranges. Crucially,

                             F_t N_t=M.                (23.36)

Thus the histories can be amplified during their preparation and then
erased with compensating weights, without changing the decoded sum.
For alpha<=1/(16b^2), take t=1/(2sqrt(b)). Then

 ||N_t||<=2,             ||F_t||<=2/sqrt(3),
             ||F_t|| ||N_t||<=4/sqrt(3).               (23.37)

LOWER NORMALIZATION CERTIFICATE. Suppose an acquired physical coordinate
projection Pi_0 satisfies Pi_0 J_empty=J_empty and Pi_0 J_h A_h=0 for every
nonempty h. This is the orthogonal empty logical chart block, when actually
present. Then M*M>=I. Combining(23.35)--(23.37), prepare a normalized
N_t x and postselect F_t/sqrt(Z_m). Its success probability is at least

                    1/(Z_m ||N_t||^2) >=3/16.          (23.38)

For a more general certified s_min(M)>=mu>0, replace the numerator1 by
mu^2. This certificate is required: a general sum of coherent histories
may cancel even though its lift is contracting.

There is also a sufficient small-sum certificate that does not require
an orthogonal root block. Since the number of length-ell histories is at
most b^ell and every edge obeys(23.32),

 ||M-J_empty|| <= sum_(ell>=1)(b sqrt(alpha))^ell
                  = b sqrt(alpha)/(1-b sqrt(alpha)).   (23.39)

Under alpha<=1/(16b^2), this is at most1/3 and hence s_min(M)>=2/3.
This estimate uses an acquired common output and declared root embedding;
it must not be substituted for a norm estimate on the unacquired selected
graph coefficients D_U^+ b_U.

CHARGED CIRCUIT IMPLEMENTATION. Assume the history controls, each T_hc,
and each J_h have polynomial-cost controlled block encodings, with known
normalizations polynomial in the explicit input. With fixed b and alpha,
I-L/t has bounded inverse and norm: its singular values lie in
[1-sqrt(b alpha)/t,1+sqrt(b alpha)/t]. A block encoding obtained by the
identity plus the acquired edge circuit therefore has polynomial known
normalization and constant actual singular gap. Quantum singular-value
transformation of the adjoint gives its inverse with polynomial cost in
the charged normalization, log(1/delta), and edge-circuit cost. No tiny
original physical singular value is inverted here.

For F_t, embed an irregular tree into the complete b-ary tree, declare
invalid histories to have A_h=0, and use

 |w_t>=Z_m^(-1/2)sum_(|h|<=m)t^|h||h>.

This state is acquired by a truncated geometric length register with
ratio bt^2 and uniform b-ary child labels. Controlled padding J_h followed
by projection of the history register onto |w_t> implements F_t/sqrt(Z_m).
The padding isometries must be extended by acquired controlled unitaries
on specified zero ancillas; their existence is not an acquisition oracle.
The resulting block encoding of M has normalization at most4/sqrt(3)
in(23.37). Under either lower certificate above, its nonzero singular
values on the declared root sector are bounded below by a fixed constant.
Consequently a second singular-value transformation prepares polar(M)
with polynomial cost and no global postselection loss. Uniform input on
E_empty then gives the uniform state on RanM, PROVIDED that uniform root
input itself has an acquired preparation. An unacquired logical kernel D
cannot be hidden in that proviso. For a one-dimensional explicitly prepared
root, constant-success preparation already suffices.

TRUNCATION AND DIMENSION ACCOUNTING. The SAME hypotheses imply

 ||M-M_m|| <= (b sqrt(alpha))^(m+1)/(1-b sqrt(alpha)).   (23.40)

With a constant lower certificate, normalized output-state trace error is
O((b sqrt(alpha))^(m+1)). Thus m=O(log(1/epsilon)) suffices. History storage
is O(m logb) qubits; acquiring a full explicit short-history table costs
b^m, which is polynomial in1/epsilon for fixed b,alpha. A succinct acquired
edge/control circuit can avoid that enumeration. Neither implementation
uses a dense d^m physical matrix. Precision must be distributed over the
polynomial number of acquired gates in the ordinary way.

EXACT REMAINING PREMISES. The current arbitrary-instance constructions do
NOT supply(23.33) as an acquired K-valued map satisfying these assumptions:

* R24's selected graph is K-valued only on logical consistency D, and its
  coefficients contain potentially tiny-angle D_U^+ resolvers.
* Only truly fresh edges get(23.32); arbitrary internal old-site resolution
  has not been converted into bounded guarded creation maps.
* A complete selected encoder sums incompatible/disconnected polymers.
  Arbitrary many disconnected components are not one bounded-branch tree.
  Genuinely disjoint tree encoders compose independently, but overlapping
  hard-core compatibility has not been converted into that situation.
* Section12's low transport has the correct old-source effect guard but
  its residual polar V still requires an acquired resolver. Orthogonal
  history labels do not acquire V or its support for free.

Therefore(23.35)--(23.40) close an actual history-erasure normalization
interface when its recurrence is supplied. They do not claim that the
general satisfying-state algorithm has been obtained.
