1. THE ORACLE THEOREM AND OUTPUT CONVENTION

Let g=grad F:R^d->R^d be globally L-Lipschitz, with F only C^{1,1}; convexity
is unnecessary. Let mu=E g(G), G standard Gaussian. Fix sigma>=L>0.
For each fixed a>0 the construction below is intended to give, on EVERY
execution, at most C_a d^a poly_a(1/epsilon) exact g-value queries and
poly_a(d,1/epsilon) real arithmetic, returning a law within TV epsilon of
N(mu,sigma^2 I). The constants may depend on a and sigma/L.

The continuous real-oracle convention is essential: independent exact
standard Gaussian vectors and exact arithmetic on oracle-returned reals are
allowed. A literally finite-bit output is discrete and has TV distance one
from a continuous Gaussian. Section 9 additionally explains polynomial-bit
scalar constants and the rounded-target convention; it does not call a
finite-bit vector TV-close to an unrounded continuous law.

For L=0, query g(0) once and add sigma G. Below L>0.

2. GAUSSIAN MEAN FAMILY AND ITS SYMMETRIC STEIN KERNEL

Fix a physical center x, scale r>0, and noise S>0. Put
 f(z)=g(x+r z), ell=Lr, u=E f(G), kappa=ell/S.
For 0<=rho<1 define a_rho=1-rho^2 and
 M_rho(y)=E f(rho y+sqrt(a_rho) Z).
Thus M_0=u and M_rho has mean u under y~gamma_d. For a smoothing cutoff
 b=sqrt(1-R^2), 0<R<1/2, define
 K(y)=int_0^b D M_rho(y) d rho/rho.                    (2.1)
The zero endpoint is its smooth limit. Weak Gaussian integration by parts
or differentiation of the OU semigroup gives
 d_rho M_rho=delta_gamma(D M_rho)/rho,
where rowwise (delta_gamma K)_i=sum_j(y_j K_ij-partial_j K_ij).
Consequently
 delta_gamma K=M_b-u.                               (2.2)
Conservativity makes D M_rho, K, and every higher derivative of K fully
symmetric in ALL indices (including the vector-output index). Moreover
 ||K||op<=int_0^b ell d rho=ell b.                    (2.3)

This is the actual use of integrability in the supplied coordinates.
For a general field the stationary position velocity requires K^T p.
Directional value evaluations give Kp, not K^T p. For a gradient K=K^T.

3. DIMENSION-FREE ONE-SLOT REGULARITY AND STATIONARY DERIVATIVES

A one-slot split regards one tensor index as matrix input and all other
indices as output. For j>=0,
 |D^j K|one-slot <= C_j ell R^{-j}                    (3.1)
pointwise, for EVERY choice of isolated slot. Here and below fixed-order
constants are allowed to depend on their indicated orders.

Proof of (3.1). For a scalar ell-Lipschitz phi, Gaussian convolution at
radius s has
 ||D^q(phi*gamma_s)||F <= sqrt((q-1)!) ell s^{-(q-1)}, q>=1. (3.2)
Indeed write q-1 derivatives by Gaussian integration by parts and retain
one weak derivative of phi. Orthogonality/Bessel for the tensor Hermite
polynomials bounds the sum of squared coefficients by
(q-1)! E||grad phi||^2 <=(q-1)! ell^2. This argument is pointwise in the
convolution center. For unit v, the scalar v.f is ell-Lipschitz. Symmetry
allows the isolated input v in D^j K to be moved into f's output slot.
Apply (3.2) to D^{j+1}P_rho(v.f), divide by rho, and integrate:
 C_j ell int_0^b rho^j a_rho^{-j/2} d rho <= C_j ell R^{-j}.
This proves every one-slot split. No Hessian is acquired by the algorithm.
The same argument gives D^j M_b Frobenius bound C_j ell sqrt(d) R^{-j}
for j>=1 (a deliberately conservative power).

Let s=S/2. On independent Gaussian anchors (Y_0,P_0) solve
 Ydot=-K(Y) P/s,
 Pdot=(M_b(Y)-u)/s.                                  (3.3)
All coefficients are smooth, K is bounded, and M_b is Lipschitz, so the
joint velocity has linear growth and the locally unique flow is complete
in both time directions. Relative to gamma_d(Y) gamma_d(P),
 div_Y(p Ydot)=p P.(M_b-u)/s,
 div_P(p Pdot)=-p P.(M_b-u)/s.
The complete flow thus preserves that product density, by its Jacobian
formula. Integrating the momentum equation gives the exact POSITIVE law
 s P_0+int_0^1 M_b(Y_t) dt = u+s P_1 ~ N(u,s^2 I).     (3.4)
The unknown u in (3.3) is retained as an exact mean analysis node and is
eliminated in Section 6. Omitting it is not valid, even for affine g.

For every fixed k>=1 the stationary trajectory obeys
 ||d_t^k(Y_t,P_t)||L2 <= C_k sqrt(d) (kappa R^{-2})^k,
 ||d_t^k M_b(Y_t)||L2 <= C_k ell sqrt(d) (kappa R^{-2})^k. (3.5)

Proof. With Gaussian adjoints the Lie derivative is delta(A D), where
 A=[[0,K^T/s],[-K/s,0]] is skew symmetric. Its one-slot derivative bounds
are C_j kappa R^{-j}. The elementary Gaussian divergence identity is
 E||delta V||F^2 = E||V||F^2+E sum_ij (partial_i V_j)(partial_j V_i)
 <= E||V||F^2+E||DV||F^2.                             (3.6)
It applies to each remaining tensor output and then is summed. If
phi_k=[delta(A D)]^k phi_0, induction using Leibniz and
D delta V=delta DV+V proves
 ||D^j phi_k||F,L2 <= C_jk a0 sqrt(d) kappa^k R^{-(j+2k)}. (3.7)
For phi_0=(Y,P), use a0=1: its first derivative has norm sqrt(2d) and
higher derivatives vanish. For phi_0=M_b use a0=ell and (3.2).
In each product isolate the A-slot contracted with D phi: (3.1), rather
than a Frobenius bound for A, multiplies the other factor's Frobenius norm.
Thus a new factor sqrt(d) is never paid. Applying (3.6) introduces at most
one further derivative and the stated R^{-2} per Lie derivative. Gaussian
cutoffs justify each integration by parts. Stationarity makes these
fixed-measure L2 norms the derivative norms at every deterministic time.

4. A FINITE VALUE-OF-MEAN CIRCUIT FOR THE KERNEL ACTION

This section uses exact Gaussian means
 m(c,v)=E g(c+v Z)
as temporary analysis nodes. Its final formulas have only scalar
coefficients, affine centers, and such nodes.

For independent standard Gaussian Y,P define
 H(rho,z;Y,P)=M_rho(cos(z)Y+sin(z)P).
Symmetry and the chain rule give
 K(Y)P=int_0^b [partial_z H(rho,0;Y,P)] d rho/rho.     (4.1)
Here every value is exactly the Gaussian mean
 m(x+r rho(cos(z)Y+sin(z)P), r sqrt(1-rho^2)).         (4.2)
There is no simulated harmonic motion: (Y,P) has a Gaussian reference,
so its rotation is explicitly (cos(z)Y+sin(z)P,...).

Useful L2 bounds, for fixed j,k with j>=1, are
 ||partial_rho^k partial_z^j H||L2
 <= C_jk ell sqrt(d) (1-rho^2)^{-(j+k)},              (4.3)
and when k=0 the right side has an additional factor rho.
Proof: expand f-Ef=sum_{n>=1} f_n in vector Wiener chaoses.
Sum_n n||f_n||L2^2=E||Df||F^2<=ell^2 d. The OU operator multiplies the
nth chaos by rho^n. Gaussian rotation in R^{2d} is unitary and its
infinitesimal generator has norm at most n on that chaos. Therefore the
square of the left side is bounded by
 sum_n (n)_k^2 rho^{2(n-k)} n^{2j} ||f_n||L2^2.
The scalar supremum after dividing by n is at most
C_jk(1-rho^2)^{-2(j+k)}, with the stated rho^2 factor when k=0.
This proves (4.3) without a dimension factor per derivative.

Choose fixed interpolation order m, mesh ratio h0, and stencil scale psi.
Use an order-m finite difference for partial_z at zero: nodes z_j=psi c_j,
weights d_j/psi, sum d_j=0, exact on polynomials of degree <m. Its absolute
weight sum is C_m/psi. The angle remainder in (4.1), divided by rho,
has L2 at most
 C_m ell sqrt(d) psi^{m-1} R^{-2m}.                  (4.4)

For the rho integration use polynomial interpolation on geometric cells
whose lengths are at most h0(1-rho_right), up to b. There are
O_m h0^{-1}(1+log(1/R)) nodes. On the first cell include rho=0, impose
the exact value partial_z H(0,0)=0, and integrate the interpolation
polynomial divided by rho exactly. No query at the zero node is needed.
On all other cells integrate the polynomial divided by rho. These are
explicit deterministic scalar weights q_i. Fixed-order interpolation
remainders and (4.3) give
 ||int_0^b partial_z H(rho,0) d rho/rho
       -sum_i q_i partial_z H(rho_i,0)||L2
 <= C_m ell sqrt(d) polylog(1/(h0 R)) h0^{m-2}.       (4.5)
For clarity, on a nonzero cell the order-m remainder is bounded by
C_m ell sqrt(d) h0^m (1-rho_right^2)^{-1}; integration of this bound
against d rho/rho costs only logarithms. On the first cell the remainder
has the additional factor rho because zero is an interpolation node;
its division by rho is harmless. The weaker h0^{m-2} bound allows all
endpoint conventions. The absolute rho weights satisfy the safe bound
 sum_i |q_i| <= C_m polylog(1/(h0 R)) h0^{-1}.        (4.6)
(A sharper logarithmic bound is possible but is not needed.)

Let Khat(Y)P denote the double finite sum of (4.2) with weights
q_i d_j/psi. Then
 ||Khat(Y)P-K(Y)P||L2
 <= B ell sqrt(d)[h0^{m-2}+h0^{-1} psi^{m-1}R^{-2m}], (4.7)
where B is a fixed power of logarithms. More conservatively retaining
h0^{-1} in the second term avoids relying on a weighted angle estimate.
As a deterministic function of (Y,P), Khat(Y)P is globally Lipschitz
with constant <=B ell psi^{-1} h0^{-1}.               (4.8)
This follows directly from the ell*rho Lipschitz bound of each H-value.
The sum of all coefficients is exactly zero; constant shifts of g
therefore cancel algebraically.

5. THE EXACT-MEAN AFFINE CIRCUIT AND ORDER OF CHOICES

Fix a desired power J and use parameters
 R=delta^t, psi=delta^{4t}, h0=delta^w,
0<w<t, with t sufficiently small. Assume
 kappa<=B delta^c, c>=1/2.                           (5.1)
All logarithmic factors B below have fixed exponents; in the final use
delta is an inverse fixed power of d/epsilon.

Approximate the centering velocity by
 vhat_Y=-Khat(Y)P/s, vhat_P=(M_b(Y)-M_0)/s.           (5.2)
By (4.7) its stationary-input RMS error can be made <=B delta^J sqrt(d)
by choosing the fixed order m after w. Its global Lipschitz constant is
 <=B kappa delta^{-4t-w} <=delta^{1/3}                (5.3)
for sufficiently small delta and fixed c>=1/2, provided e.g. 10t<1/10.
Use ordinary fixed-order polynomial interpolation on [0,1] for the outer
centering ODE. With partial integral rows w_ij, start all nodes at anchor
Z0=(Y0,P0), and use N Picard layers
 Z_i^[0]=Z0,
 Z_i^[k+1]=Z0+sum_j w_ij vhat(Z_j^[k]).               (5.4)
The row sums are C_m. Use the final positions in the full-integral rule
 C_M=(S/2)P0+sum_j omega_j M_b(Y_j^[N]),
 sum_j omega_j=1, sum_j |omega_j|<=C_m.               (5.5)

The exact stationary derivatives (3.5), Taylor's integral remainder,
(4.7), and the global bound (5.3) prove
 W2(Law(C_M),N(u,S^2 I/4))<=B S delta^J sqrt(d).      (5.6)
Here is the parameter dependence that matters. Outer quadrature errors
are bounded by C_m sqrt(d)(kappa R^{-2})^{m+1}; the initial displacement
is C kappa sqrt(d); every Picard iteration contracts by delta^{1/3}.
Thus N=O(J+1) suffices, independently of w and m. Choose m so that
 w(m-3)>J+10, t(m-3)>J+10, m>4(J+10).                (5.7)
Then all remainders, including (4.7)'s exponent
(4t(m-1)-2tm-w)=(2m-4)t-w, have a strict surplus over J.

Expand only affine state updates, stopping at earlier mean values.
This yields a finite acyclic circuit
 M_i=m(E_i,r_i), E_i=b_i+sum_{j<i}a_ij M_j,
 E_f=(S/2)P0+sum_i a_fi M_i,                        (5.8)
with the following deterministic interface:
 r_i=r tau_i, tau_i=sqrt(1-rho_i^2)>=R;
 b_i=x+r rho_i(cos(z_i)Y0+sin(z_i)P0),
 0<=rho_i<=b, |z_i|<=C_m psi.                       (5.9)
The node M_0 has rho=0, r_i=r, b_i=x. The output nodes in E_f ALL
have rho=b,z=0 and hence r_i=rR. Every center's absolute coefficient
sum is bounded by the positive declared bound
 A=B(r/S)delta^{-4t-10w},                            (5.10)
and the output sum by A_f=C_m. A may be enlarged to ensure A>=r/S.
Each direct expression has at most B delta^{-10w} terms. Every mean
dependency path has length D0=O(N+1), INDEPENDENT of w,m and mesh size.
All scalar data and the entire circuit shape can be generated before
observing any oracle value or anchor. This interface follows simply by
substituting the additive updates (5.4) into (4.2): each update has the
original anchor as direct term, and mean coefficients bounded by
B S^{-1}psi^{-1}h0^{-1}; multiplication by r rho gives (5.10).

6. ELIMINATING EVERY EXACT MEAN: THE POSITIVE NOISE-ABSORPTION COMPILER

We construct M(x,r,S;c,p) targeting m(x,r)+S G. Each routine has a
finite fixed list of Gaussian seed slots and reserves an independent
final S G_res/2, unused by its own queries. Before adding that reserve
the comparison target is N(m(x,r),3S^2 I/4). Its error budget is
 B S delta^p sqrt(d).                               (6.1)
The size label requires Lr/S<=B delta^c, c>=1/2.

Terminal rule (p<=c):
 M_pre=g(x+rZ)+(sqrt(3)S/2)G, M=M_pre+(S/2)G_res.    (6.2)
Its pre-reserve W2 error from the target is <=Lr sqrt(d), within
(6.1); its fixed-seed center Lipschitz constant is L. On moving x by
Delta, shift its Z seed by -Delta/r: every physical query and output
is unchanged. This is a deterministic identity for arbitrary seeds.

Nonterminal rule: use the exact-mean circuit (5.8) with the parameter
S, and compile its output with extra noise n=S/sqrt(2). Finally add
the separate S G_res/2. In the analysis (5.6)'s S^2/4 plus n^2 makes
3S^2/4 before the reserve, as required.

Every term of an affine expression E=b0+sum_i a_i M_i is evaluated
independently, including an uncached copy of its center-supplying tree.
For expression noise n, take A'=A for center expressions or A_f for
the output, and assign the child mean noise S_i=n/(2A'). To call its
mean at intended center E_i and scale r_i, first evaluate E_i with
Gaussian noise n_i=r_i/(2D_s), where D_s=delta^{-t}. If child shift
vector is v_i, apply to its fresh standard seed array the slot-space
matrix
 P_i=(I-v_i v_i^T/(4D_s^2))^{1/2}.                  (6.3)
Denote this child output Q_i, and return
 Eval(E,n)=b0+sum_i a_i Q_i
       +sqrt(n^2-sum_i a_i^2 S_i^2) G_E.            (6.4)
All term blocks, center subtrees, residuals, anchors and reserves are
disjoint. Since sum|a_i|<=A', the radicand is >=3n^2/4.

LABELS. Direct output terms of E_f get (c+t,p). EVERY other mean
occurrence, at any depth of the parent's center-expression tree, gets
(c-10t,p-1/4), with labels relative to the SAME parent routine.
Expression depth and routine depth are different. Iterating p-loss
once per expression tier would be the wrong algorithm.

BOUNDED ROUTINE HEIGHT. Start at (c,p)=(1,K), K fixed. Before 4K+1
decreasing transitions, c has lost at most 10t(4K+1). Choose
 10t(4K+1)<1/4.                                    (6.5)
At that many decreasing transitions p<0, so termination has already
occurred. Between decreases, output transitions increase c by t and
a nonterminal has c<p<=K. Thus they too are bounded in number. All
reachable c>3/4, and routine height H=H(K,t) is fixed independently
of d,epsilon,w,m and the oracle.

EXACT ABSORPTION IDENTITY. Suppose a child has a shift vector v with
||v||<=D_s and satisfies
 Q(x+Delta;z+v tensor Delta/r)=Q(x;z),                (6.6)
preserving all query points. For independent standard Gaussian Z,G,
 Q(x+r Z/(2D_s);P G)
 =Q(x;P G-v tensor Z/(2D_s))                        (6.7)
pathwise. The right-hand seed covariance is exactly I because
PP^T+v v^T/(4D_s^2)=I. Therefore the left-hand output has EXACTLY
the standalone law at deterministic center x. No conditional mean
is supplied by this identity; it is solely a transformation of fresh
Gaussian seeds in the finite routine.

SHIFT VECTORS. For a nonterminal parent, on moving x by Delta, shift
its Gaussian position anchor by -Delta/(rb), leaving momentum and
final reserve fixed. The direct center in (5.9) then changes by
 k_i Delta, k_i=1-rho_i cos(z_i)/b.
For all circuit nodes
 |k_i|/tau_i<=C_m,                                 (6.8)
because 1-rho_i/b<=C tau_i, tau_i>=R, and
(1-cos z_i)/tau_i<=C_m psi^2/R=o(1).
For each direct child term shift its UNTRANSFORMED seed block by
 (k_i/tau_i) P_i^{-1}v_i tensor Delta/r.             (6.9)
The smallest eigenvalue of P_i is >=sqrt(3)/2. Induction from leaf
expressions upward now preserves every mean output and every physical
query. Center subtrees are separate direct children and are charged
once; the factor k_i/tau_i multiplies only the child in (6.9), not its
center-supplying siblings. Output means have k_i=0, and the direct
output (S/2)P0 is unchanged. This proves (6.6) for the parent.

QUERY CAP AND PARAMETER ORDER. Expand a direct circuit into an
uncached expression tree: its size is at most
(D0+1)(1+B delta^{-10w})^{D0} = B' delta^{-C0 w}.
Expanding through the fixed routine height H gives B' delta^{-C1 w}
seed slots and terminal queries, with C1 depending on K,t,N but NOT
on w or interpolation order m. The same bound holds for the norm of
every shift vector: terminal norm is one; (6.8)-(6.9) bound each
parent by a fixed constant times one plus its direct child norms.
Now choose w>0, AFTER K,t,N,H,D0, with C1 w<t/2 and 10w<t. Only
THEN choose the fixed order m via (5.7). For sufficiently small
delta the logarithmic factor fits the remaining power, giving
 Q,slots<=B delta^{-t}, ||v||<=D_s=delta^{-t}.        (6.10)
This is a bottom-up construction: child shifts are computed before
their P_i and the parent shift. Its shape, noises and labels are fixed
without using oracle magnitudes, accuracy events, or stochastic stops.

SIZE PROPAGATION. Output child scales are rR and child noises are
comparable to S, so Lr_child/S_child<=B delta^{c+t}.
An internal center call has S_i=n_i/(2A), hence
 L r_i/S_i=4L D_s A
 <=B (Lr/S)delta^{-5t-10w}<=B delta^{c-10t}.         (6.11)
Thus every label's required size condition holds.

FIXED-SEED CENTER STABILITY. Terminal stability is L. In a circuit,
center expressions propagate earlier mean Lipschitz constants through
coefficient sum A, while the output sum is A_f. By (5.10) and (6.11),
 LA<=B delta^{c-4t-10w}<=delta^{1/3}.                (6.12)
Expression depth and routine height are bounded, so induction gives
fixed-seed center Lipschitz constant B L for every routine. Seed
transforms are fixed deterministic matrices and do not affect this
argument. The constants/powers in B depend only on the fixed orders.

ERROR PROPAGATION. Condition on the parent anchors, so its intended
exact mean circuit is deterministic. Couple a supplied center to
E_i+n_i Z with its conditional W2 error. Use identical child seeds:
fixed-seed stability costs B L times that center error. At the ideal
noisy center, invoke (6.7), then the child's standalone guarantee.
Term blocks are independent conditional on the anchors, so their
ideal outputs and the residual in (6.4) have exactly covariance n^2 I.
For an expression the resulting recurrence is
 eps_E<=sum_i |a_i|[B L eps_{E_i}
                  +B S_i delta^{p_i}sqrt(d)].        (6.13)
Direct output children have p_i=p and sum|a_i|S_i<=n/2. All deeper
expression-tier children have the single loss p_i=p-1/4. Their noise
units are <=C S because A>=r/S and n_i<=r_i/(2D_s). Each additional
expression tier brings the factor B L A<=delta^{1/3}. The first such
factor pays the loss delta^{-1/4}, leaving delta^{1/12}; all further
tiers form a finite sum. Therefore (6.13), (5.6), and J=K+2 imply
the pre-reserve error (6.1) by induction over finite routine height.
All guarantees are UNIFORM in x: Gaussian mean fluctuations around
g(x) have RMS <=Lr sqrt(d), and constant shifts of g cancel in every
velocity and center update. No ideal-history moment bound or unknown
mean magnitude is used.

Adding the common independent S G_res/2 and Gaussian smoothing gives
 TV(Law(M),N(m(x,r),S^2 I))<=B delta^p sqrt(d).       (6.14)
This is a positive finite randomized algorithm, not a signed density
series or a mean estimator.

7. RESTORING THE FIXED REQUESTED NOISE WITH MANY CHEAP COPIES

The small parameter need not be L itself. Choose an integer
 M=ceil(C_a (d/epsilon)^{2e}), delta=M^{-1/2},
with e>0 arbitrarily small and fixed. Run M independent copies of
the above mean routine on the ORIGINAL oracle g at x=0,r=1, with
noise S=sigma sqrt(M), root labels (1,K), and return their average.
Since L/S<=delta for sigma>=L, the root size hypothesis holds.
The ideal average of the M Gaussian targets is exactly
 N(mu,sigma^2 I). By product TV and data processing, its error is
 <=M B delta^K sqrt(d)=B M^{1-K/2}sqrt(d).           (7.1)
Choose fixed K with e(K-2)>2. The right side is at most epsilon
for sufficiently large d/epsilon, after choosing C_a and absorbing
fixed logarithms. There are no random failures to condition away.

Total queries on every execution are
 <=M B delta^{-t}=B M^{1+t/2}
 <=B C_a (d/epsilon)^{e(2+t)}.                      (7.2)
Choose e so that e(2+t)<a/2; one can choose e<a/8 first and then t
by (6.5). Fixed logarithms fit the remaining d^{a/2} and a fixed
power of 1/epsilon. Thus (7.2) is C_a d^a poly_a(1/epsilon).

The finite low-dimensional/low-(d/epsilon) range can use ordinary
Monte Carlo: query g(G_j), average n values, and add sigma G. Gaussian
Poincare gives RMS mean error <=L sqrt(d/n), hence smoothing error
<=L sqrt(d/n)/sigma. Taking n=O(d/epsilon^2) suffices. In the bounded
range d<=D_a this is absorbed by C_a d^a epsilon^{-2}. No F-value
fallback is needed.

8. TOTAL COMPUTATION, NOT ONLY QUERY COMPLEXITY

The runtime algorithm performs only vector additions, scalar
multiplications, Gaussian draws, terminal g calls, and fixed scalar
coefficient generation. It NEVER forms a derivative tensor, Hessian,
Jacobian, or full d-by-d matrix. All tensors in Sections 2-4 are only
proof objects. The number of expression nodes and seed slots is bounded
by (6.10), and total parent/child levels are fixed.

The apparently dense seed transformation (6.3) is rank one. If v!=0,
 P G=G+[sqrt(1-||v||^2/(4D_s^2))-1]
             v tensor (sum_j v_j G_j)/||v||^2.       (8.1)
It costs O(d times number_of_slots) arithmetic, with eigenvalues
uniformly >=sqrt(3)/2. Use identity for v=0. Shift vectors, scales,
noises, and transformations are computed bottom-up once from scalar
data. Even quadratic bookkeeping in the number of nodes is polynomial.
Per-copy and aggregate arithmetic/storage are therefore
poly_a(d,1/epsilon), with an explicit safe bound O_a(d Q_total^3)
plus polynomial scalar coefficient work. A linear-in-node optimized
implementation should be possible; it is unnecessary for the theorem.

All interpolation weights integrate known fixed-degree polynomials
against 1/rho; the first-cell polynomial has zero constant term.
Their generation uses rational operations and logarithms of positive
scalar endpoints. Angles use fixed-order sine/cosine constants. No
optimization, multidimensional integration, or search is done between
oracle calls. This removes the unrestricted-computation loophole of
the general source theorem in this Gaussian specialization.

9. BIT PRECISION AND UNBOUNDED ORACLE OFFSETS

The exact-real algorithm above is finite. For a polynomial-bit scalar
implementation, mesh counts and all fixed orders depend only on a;
delta^{-1},R^{-1},psi^{-1},A and all finite-circuit scalar coefficients
are bounded by fixed powers of d/epsilon (and fixed powers of L,sigma

analysis on an event of probability <=epsilon/10 using a polynomial

On this event bounded-height Lipschitz propagation gives polynomial

Use exact coefficient identities for every finite difference's zero
sum, every velocity difference, and the output quadrature's sum one,
enforcing the last coefficient by subtraction after rounding others.
This prevents numerical error proportional to an arbitrarily large
constant offset g(0). Alternatively query g(0) once, work throughout
with g-g(0), and add the exact returned vector g(0) to the output.
The latter is the simplest real-oracle implementation. Physical query

bit lengths are not falsely charged as bounded finite strings.

Approximating covariance transforms breaks the exact identity (6.7),
