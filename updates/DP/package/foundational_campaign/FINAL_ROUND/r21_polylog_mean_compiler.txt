1. PRECISE TRANSFERABLE CLOUD MECHANISM

For a globally ell-Lipschitz Phi:R^m -> R^n, Clark-Ocone gives
 Phi(G)-E Phi(G)=int_0^1 A_t dB_t,
 A_t=E[D Phi(B_t+sqrt(1-t)Z)|B_t],
 Sigma(B)=int_0^1 A_t A_t^T dt, 0<=Sigma<=ell^2 I.
When ell<1, the POSITIVE comparison construction
 Y=Phi(B_1)+(I-Sigma(B))^{1/2}Z
has exactly law N(E Phi(G),I). This follows from the complex exponential
martingale identity, not from covariance matching alone.

The cloud paper acquires this construction by likelihood tilting an initially
independent Z. It estimates Sigma actions with independent derivative pairs,
then estimates the truncated log density
 w_K(B,Z)=(1/2)sum_{k=1}^K [tr(Sigma^k)/k-Z^T Sigma^k Z].
For ell<1/2, truncation error is O(n ell^{2K+2}); K=O(log(n/epsilon))
suffices. The leading random estimator nevertheless has Lp size
 O(ell^2(sqrt(np)+p)+ell^4 n).
Increasing K removes truncation error but DOES NOT remove that leading term.
Thus using g/sigma directly with fixed ell=L/sigma cannot use their existing
bounded-FORS correction with polylog query complexity. This is a limitation of
this transferred gadget and analysis, not a lower bound for the target theorem.

2. EVERY-EXECUTION QUERY CAPS FROM SUBCRITICAL FORESTS

An exact bounded FORS call with clipping level B uses proposal retries and a
Poisson(2B) number of factors per attempt. A zero Poisson count always accepts.
The potential schedule extending until the first zero has exponential tails
uniformly for B<=1. After contracting each mandatory predecessor chain, suppose
a vertex creates at most m auxiliary children and has conditional expected
number at most 1/4. Convexity on [0,m] then gives
 E exp(s zeta | past) <= exp{lambda(exp(ms)-1)}, m lambda<=1/4.
For q roots, total contracted vertices T obey
 E T<=4q/3, P[T>C(q+mu)]<=exp(-u).
If mandatory depth, local query count, m, and q are polylog(d/epsilon), cap
T and all local schedules at these deterministic polynomial-log values with
u=C log(d/epsilon). Return a fixed point upon cap breach. The capped program
has an EVERY-execution polylog query cap; TV changes by at most the cap-event
probability. Conditional-kernel replacement errors telescope through the cap.
No expectation-to-worst-case conversion can be used unless the required tail
bound has first been proved for the actual complete adaptive forest.

This is the useful forest interface. It does not by itself reduce the number
of mean centers or make the existing fixed-a compiler uniform in its orders.

3. A PROVED DETERMINISTIC FULL-REFRESH CONTRACTION ESTIMATE

Let h:R^d->R^d be a-Lipschitz. Let T:R^{2d}->R^d be globally b-Lipschitz
in the Euclidean joint norm; T approximates the K(Y)P action and is allowed
here only as an explicit ANALYSIS interface. Consider, in normalized time,
 Y'=-T(Y,P), P'=h(Y)-Q, Q'=P.
Run for t*=pi/2. For two solutions with identical Y0,P0 but distinct Q0,
put DeltaQ0=q. Variation of constants in the P,Q harmonic subsystem gives
 Delta Q(t)=cos(t)q+int_0^t sin(t-s)[h(Y(s))-h(Ytilde(s))]ds,
 Delta P(t)=-sin(t)q+int_0^t cos(t-s)[h(Y(s))-h(Ytilde(s))]ds.
Hence at t*, the direct q term vanishes. Writing
 U(t)=sup_{s<=t}||Delta Y(s)||, V(t)=sup_{s<=t}||Delta P(s)||,
 gives U(t)<=b int_0^t[U(s)+V(s)]ds and
 V(t)<=||q||+a int_0^t U(s)ds.
Consequently U(t)<=b t exp((a+b)t)||q||, and
 ||Delta Q(t*)||<=a b t*^2 exp((a+b)t*)||q||.
(The nonsharp U bound follows by bounding U+V by exp((a+b)t)||q||.)
Thus this approximate refresh kernel is a strict W2 contraction in its Q
input whenever a b t*^2 exp((a+b)t*)<1, regardless of d. Its invariant law
is not identified merely by this contraction. To identify an approximate
Gaussian invariant law, one must compare T to the exact Stein action on the
stationary product reference and charge the complete acquired implementation.

For the scaled mean-state kernel in r16_mean_state_refresh_kernel.txt set
Qphysical=S Q, h=(M_b-mu)/S (analysis only), and normalize time by S/s.

4. THE LIVE GAP

The full refresh suggests O(log(d/epsilon)) iterations from a crude mean-state
law, but each refresh still requires local smoothed means at many distinct
centers. Exact Gaussian absorption plus retained innovation can acquire a
finite DAG with Gaussian noise; it cannot collapse distinct physical oracle
queries. With the present common child-noise allocation, internal children
lose a factor polynomial in quadrature order and shift norm in their noise
to scale ratio. Iterating this for logarithmic depth defeats a fixed polylog
root inflation. A successful construction must replace these internal
children by a subcritical random correction forest, or otherwise obtain a
joint positive field at the required local centers with polylog total queries.
This missing operation is not supplied by Sections 1-3.

5. CURRENT INVENTION TESTS

(a) Random covariance completion. A symmetric unbiased matrix estimator
Shat of Sigma with ||Shat||op<1 makes I-Shat positive even if Shat is not PSD.
Sampling Phi(G)+(I-Shat)^{1/2}Z is therefore a positive proposal. However,
E Shat=Sigma only matches the first covariance term. It does not imply the
Gaussian target; its fourth and higher characteristic cumulants remain.
An arbitrary random scalar covariance can leave almost disjoint high-
dimensional shells. Therefore this cannot be asserted as a theorem without
a dimension-uniform correction of the mixture marginal.

(b) Node-specific l2 noise allocation. The common l1 coefficient sum A used
in the retained compiler may be too crude. If a center uses sum_i a_i M_i
with child scale r_i=r tau_i and independent Gaussian noise S_i~S tau_i,
its center noise variance is S^2 sum_i a_i^2 tau_i^2. Since a_i are roughly
(r/S) times quadrature/derivative weights, a sufficiently distributed rule
could satisfy this budget with polynomial-log many terms while preserving
Lr_i/S_i~Lr/S. This must be checked jointly over all centers, including
shared mean occurrences, and against the translation norm. Even a valid
l2 allocation does not by itself remove recursive branching.

(c) Derivative acquisition. Hessian-vector actions of g can be approximated
by smoothed symmetric secants using only g values. Symmetry supplies the
transpose action. Uniform pointwise derivative approximation of an arbitrary
C^{1,1} field is unavailable; smoothing and explicit average-error estimates
are needed. No analysis derivative is to be presented as supplied data.

8. EXACT BLOCK-GRADIENT REDUCTION FOR CORRELATED QUERY INPUTS

This is an exact algebraic interface for correlated clouds, not the missing
polylog sampler. Fix q deterministic centers c_i in R^d, and let V be a
positive definite q-by-q scalar covariance with V_ii=r_i^2. Choose any
invertible A with AA^T=V, acting identically on physical coordinates. Let
z=(z_1,...,z_q) be standard Gaussian in R^{qd}, X_i=c_i+(Az)_i, and define
 H(z)=sum_i F(X_i),
 f(z)=grad_z H(z)=A^T (g(X_i))_{i=1}^q.
Then f is conservative and globally L||A||op^2=L||V||op-Lipschitz. Its mean
is A^T m, where m_i=E g(c_i+r_iG) regardless of the cross-correlations in V.
One f-value query costs q original whole-vector g-value queries, with only
O(q^2d) surrounding arithmetic.

If a positive compiler outputs N(E f,S^2 I_{qd}), applying A^{-T} gives the
joint Gaussian cloud
 N(m,S^2 V^{-1} tensor I_d).
Thus a correlated INPUT query covariance V produces the dual OUTPUT noise
covariance S^2 V^{-1}; a scalar affine combination a^T of the mean nodes has
noise variance S^2 a^T V^{-1}a. A deterministic common physical translation
u_i Delta of all query centers is canceled by shifting z by -A^{-1}u tensor
Delta, whose squared slot norm is u^T V^{-1}u. This covariance/translation
tradeoff is observable and exactly computable. It does not reuse distinct
physical queries, and it does not assert that the required joint compiler
already has polylog complexity.

In particular, setting V close to a rank-one shared-seed covariance can make
a common translation inexpensive, but makes output differences very noisy.
It cannot produce a noise-free mean-difference oracle. The Lipschitz size
L||V||op also increases along a heavily correlated mode, and must be charged.

The remaining possible improvement is to use covariance adapted to the LOW-
DIMENSIONAL quadrature parameter manifold and its analytic spectral decay,
rather than diagonal noises at all value nodes. High-frequency cloud errors
could be large if every downstream quadrature and center update suppresses
those modes. A valid proof would need uniform positive-definite covariances,
all joint fan-out/retention norms, and a recursive anisotropic error estimate;
none of these follow merely from the block identity.

10. EXACT AUTONOMOUS-OU GAUSSIAN-MEAN DIFFUSION

NEW POSITIVE MATHEMATICAL INTERFACE; acquisition remains unproved. Suppose
h is a smooth globally ell-Lipschitz gradient of Gaussian mean mu and K is
symmetric, bounded by ell, and delta_gamma K=K(y)y-div K(y)=h(y)-mu.
The truncated resolvent K_b, h=M_b, is an example. Let S>0 and choose
a>0 with 2 a S^2>=ell^2. On (Y,Q), define symmetric diffusion matrix

 D(Y) = [ a I       -K(Y) ]
        [ -K(Y)    2 S^2 I ],

and antisymmetric matrix Gamma(Y)=[0,K;-K,0]. Then D is PSD, and
D+Gamma=[aI,0;-2K,2S^2I]. For the density
 rho_mu(Y,Q)=gamma_d(Y) N(mu,S^2I)(Q),
the divergence-form stationary drift is
 b=div(D+Gamma)-(D+Gamma) grad[-log rho_mu].
Its two blocks are exactly
 b_Y=-aY,  b_Q=2(h(Y)-Q).
The unknown mu cancels because delta_gamma K=h-mu. Therefore the SDE

 dY=-aY dt+sqrt(2a) dB,
 dQ=2(h(Y)-Q)dt-sqrt(2/a)K(Y)dB
       +(4S^2I-2K(Y)^2/a)^{1/2}dW,                 (10.1)

with independent B,W preserves rho_mu. This can also be verified directly:
the Q drift and diagonal diffusion leave -2(h-mu)partial_Q rho; the two
cross-diffusion terms leave +2(h-mu)partial_Q rho, and the autonomous Y
OU terms cancel separately. Smooth cutoffs/integration by parts justify
the generator calculation, and bounded K plus linear-growth h give a
nonexplosive diffusion. Taking strict inequality gives uniform ellipticity.

The useful new feature is AUTONOMY: Y starts standard Gaussian and stays
exactly stationary at all deterministic times, independently of the chosen
initial Q. For two synchronously coupled executions with the same Y_0,B,W,
their Q differences equal e^{-2t}(Q_0-Q'_0) PATHWISE. This uses no global
Lipschitz bound for DK(Y)P and no perturbed Y trajectory.

Even stronger, put Q_0=0 and denote the innovation at time T by R_T. From
variation of constants,

 R_T=int_0^T 2e^{-2(T-t)}h(Y_t)dt
      -int_0^T e^{-2(T-t)}sqrt(2/a)K(Y_t)dB_t
      +int_0^T e^{-2(T-t)}(4S^2I-2K(Y_t)^2/a)^{1/2}dW_t. (10.2)

Let instead Q'_0~N(mu,S^2I), independent of Y and the driving noises. Then
Q'_T=e^{-2T}Q'_0+R_T has its invariant Gaussian law. R_T is independent
of Q'_0 because Y is autonomous. Dividing characteristic functions gives

 R_T ~ N((1-e^{-2T})mu, S^2(1-e^{-4T}) I).        (10.3)

Consequently R_T/(1-e^{-2T}) is EXACTLY
 N(mu,S^2 coth(T) I).
For any fixed T>0, taking S=sigma sqrt(tanh T) and a>=ell^2/(2S^2)
gives the requested noise sigma in this exact mathematical interface. No

This is an exact Gaussian marginal, not covariance matching alone; the

12. EXACT OU GRID TRANSLATION AND A CRITICAL COVARIANCE BALANCE

For fixed ordered times 0=t_1<...<t_q=T, let
 C_ij=exp(-a|t_i-t_j|). The Markov innovation factorization of a stationary
OU path gives, for any scalar vector u,
 u^T C^{-1}u=u_1^2+sum_{j=2}^q
   (u_j-e^{-a Delta_j}u_{j-1})^2/(1-e^{-2a Delta_j}).
In particular
 1^T C^{-1}1=1+sum_{j=2}^q tanh(a Delta_j/2)<=1+aT/2.
A common physical translation therefore has a slot norm independent of
the number of time samples. On a uniform grid, a FULL smooth quadrature
vector w_j=O(q^{-1})v(t_j), with uniformly bounded v and first differences,
satisfies w^T C^{-1}w=O(q^{-2}). Prefix quadrature vectors have a jump
at the integration endpoint and only O(q^{-1}) under this OU precision;
the full-vector estimate must not be used for all partial integral rows.

There is an exact alternative for balancing all integration rows. For any
invertible real q-by-q integration matrix W, put
 C0=(W^T W)^{1/2}+(W W^T)^{1/2},
 v=max_i(C0)_ii,
 V=C0/v+diag(1-(C0)_ii/v).
Then V is positive definite and has unit diagonal. Polar decomposition
and Cauchy-Schwarz give, for every x,y,
 |x^T W y|^2
 <=[x^T(WW^T)^{1/2}x][y^T(W^TW)^{1/2}y]
 <=(x^T C0 x)(y^T C0 y).
Consequently
 ||V^{-1/2}W V^{-1/2}||op<=v,
 W V^{-1}W^T<=v^2 V,
 ||V||op<=1+2||W||op/v.                           (12.1)

For the uniform left-sum integration matrix
 W_ij=q^{-1}1_{j<=i},
one has ||W||op=O(1) and v=Theta(log(2q)/q). The latter follows from
the explicit singular values [2q sin((2k-1)pi/(4q+2))]^{-1} and the
O(q^{-1/2}) bounds on both left and right singular-vector entries.
The lower bound is trace(C0)/q. Thus
 ||V||op=O(q/log(2q)),
 ||V^{-1/2}W V^{-1/2}||op=O(log(2q)/q),
and their product is O(1), uniformly in q. All partial-integration row
noise variances under V^{-1} are O(log(2q)^2/q^2), by(12.1).

14. EXACT GAUSSIAN SHIFT IMPLEMENTATIONS FORCE AFFINE MEANS

Let Z~N(0,I_n), let v be a FIXED finite-dimensional vector, and let H be
measurable. Suppose for every u in an open interval,
 H(Z+u v) ~ N(m(u),S^2)
with the SAME S>0. Then m is affine on the interval. Indeed, putting
 M(t,u)=E exp(t H(Z+u v)), Gaussian translation gives
 F(t,u)=log M(t,u)+(u^2/2)||v||^2
       =log E exp(t H(Z)+u v dot Z).
This is a joint log moment generating function. F is convex in u for
every real t, and the exact normal law makes all the required local
joint exponential moments finite. Consequently
 0<=partial_u^2 F=||v||^2+t m''(u)
for EVERY real t. Hence m''(u)=0. The same argument along input lines
and scalar output projections proves: an exact fixed-covariance Gaussian
family implemented by a fixed FINITE linear Cameron--Martin translation
of one Gaussian seed can only have an affine mean map.

Thus the nonlinear exact OU innovation cannot simultaneously possess the
finite fixed-linear seed-translation identity used in Section8. This is
an interface theorem, NOT a lower bound for approximate samplers or for
the requested polylog-query theorem. Rare output tails can invalidate
log-mgf comparison while having arbitrarily small total variation.

A quantitative finite-difference version records the required tail gate.
Assume the same implementation Q_u=H(Z+u v) and, at u-h,u,u+h,
 |log E exp(t Q_w)-[t m(w)+S^2 t^2/2]|<=eta
for t=+T and t=-T. Convexity of F yields
 |m(u+h)+m(u-h)-2m(u)|
             <= (||v||^2 h^2+4 eta)/T.             (14.1)
This requires only convexity, not differentiability of H.

For example, suppose Law(Q_w) is within TV epsilon of N(m(w),S^2), and
the ACTUAL acquired output has the controlled centered exponential tails
 E exp(t[Q_w-m(w)])<=exp(V^2 t^2/2), V>=S,
for all |t|<=2T. Maximal coupling and Holder give, for |t|<=T,
 |E exp(t[Q_w-m(w)])-exp(S^2 t^2/2)|
        <=2 sqrt(epsilon) exp(V^2 t^2).
Taking T=sqrt(log(1/epsilon))/(2V) gives relative mgf error at most
2 epsilon^(1/4), and hence eta<=4 epsilon^(1/4) when that error<=1/2.
Substitution in(14.1) allows a squared Cameron--Martin budget growing
as sqrt(log(1/epsilon)) times the actual curvature and output tail scale.
It does not forbid a polylog budget. The exponential-tail bound is an
extra joint-program receipt; TV alone does not supply it.

15. ONE-SHOT BALANCE FOR A COMPLETE CAUSAL INTEGRATION OPERATOR

The polar bound in Section12 is valid for ANY square real operator W,
not only an integration-prefix matrix. To avoid invertibility issues put
 C=|W|+|W^T|+tau I, tau>0,
 v=max_i C_ii,
 V=C/v+diag(1-C_ii/v).
Then V is positive definite with unit diagonal, and
 ||V^(-1/2) W V^(-1/2)||<=v,
 v||V||<=v+||C||<=4||W||+2tau.                   (15.1)
The first inequality follows from the polar Cauchy--Schwarz inequality
 |x^T W y|^2<=(x^T|W^T|x)(y^T|W|y)
and C<=v V. The last inequality uses v<=2||W||+tau and ||C||<=2||W||+tau.
Therefore a complete FINITE causal stencil/retention DAG, once it has
actually been expressed as a single fixed scalar linear operator W on
equal-scale vector mean slots, can be balanced without an additional
operator-norm cost depending on the number of slots. Polynomial matrix
arithmetic suffices to construct a padded numerical covariance.

The qualifier matters: this does not supply a joint mean cloud at
adaptive centers, does not make an arbitrary DAG conservative, does not
remove the q original-g queries in a q-slot block-gradient value, and
does not establish a subcritical forest. Applying(15.1) to an already
assumed exact cloud would leave the acquisition argument circular.

16. TARGETED POSITIVE STEIN NORMAL FORM: EXACT CUBIC CANCELLATION

This section is a new exact ANALYSIS identity for smooth fields; it is
not yet an acquired all-order compiler. Normalize the target noise to 1.
Let h be smooth, E_gamma h=mu, and let delta_gamma K=h-mu. Let Z be an
independent standard Gaussian. Put
 Gamma=sym(K Dh^T), A=(I-Gamma)^(1/2),
assuming ||Gamma||<1. K need not be a derivative oracle in this identity.
The positive random vector X1=h(G)+A(G)Z has mean mu. A valid Stein
matrix before conditioning on X1 is
 T1=K[Dh+D_G(AZ)]^T+A^2.
Thus sym T1=I+R, where
 R(G,Z)=sym[K D_G(AZ)^T]=sum_k R_ij,k(G) Z_k,
and R_ij,k=R_ji,k. Every coefficient is explicitly determined by K,A
and their first derivatives; no unknown mu occurs in it.

Define a centered quadratic-Hermite correction
 u_i(G,Z)=sum_jk C_i,jk(G)(Z_j Z_k-delta_jk),
 C_i,jk=-(R_ij,k+R_ik,j-R_jk,i)/3.                 (16.1)
The coefficient is symmetric in j,k. Let J=(1/2)D_Zu. Since u is pure
Gaussian chaos two, delta_Z J=u exactly. Moreover
 (3/2)sym D_Zu=-R.                                (16.2)
Indeed C_i,jk+C_j,ik=-(2/3)R_ij,k. Consequently the POSITIVE pushforward
 X2=h(G)+A(G)Z+u(G,Z)
still has mean mu and has the valid Stein matrix
 T2=K[Dh+D_G(AZ)+D_Gu]^T+(A+J)[A+D_Zu]^T.
After using(16.2), its exact symmetric residual is
 sym T2-I
  =sym[K(D_Gu)^T]
   +sym[(A-I)(D_Zu)^T+J(A-I)^T+J(D_Zu)^T].       (16.3)
There is NO order-one Gaussian baseline left in(16.3). This explicitly
answers the local baseline problem for this first targeted correction;
it does not assert a generic quadratic law-map contraction.

For the formal scaled family h=lambda h0, K=lambda K0, with bounded
smooth coefficients on the relevant region, Gamma=O(lambda^2),
A-I=O(lambda^2), R=O(lambda^3) times a linear polynomial in Z, and
u=O(lambda^3) times a centered quadratic polynomial. Thus(16.3) starts
at order lambda^4 (then lambda^5 and lambda^6). This is an exact
third-to-fourth order cancellation, not a proved dimension-uniform TV
estimate. Polynomial Z tails and all derivative contractions must be
included in any quantitative all-order construction.

A useful formal extension avoids demanding that every symmetric tensor
be a pointwise symmetric gradient. If a symmetric matrix polynomial
R_m(Z) is pure Gaussian chaos m, write delta_Z delta_Z R_m for its
twice-divergenced scalar chaos m+2. Put
 phi=-(delta_Z delta_Z R_m)/(m+2)^2,
 u=grad_Z phi, J_u=(1/(m+1))D_Zu.
Then delta_Z J_u=u, and
 delta_Z delta_Z [R_m+sym(J_u+(D_Zu)^T)]=0.        (16.4)
For every smooth scalar f with sufficient integrability this implies
 E_Z[(R_m+sym(J_u+(D_Zu)^T)):Hess_Z f(Z)]=0.
The proof uses delta_Z D_Zu=(m+1)u and delta_Z u=(m+2)phi. Equation
(16.4) is a weak Gaussian projection, NOT pointwise Stein cancellation
at the perturbed output X. Evaluating Hess f at h+AZ+u introduces new
higher-order terms, which would have to be generated and bounded in a
full normal-form recursion. No convergent all-order recursion, positive
subcritical acquisition forest, or every-execution query bound is yet
established.

ACQUISITION BOUNDARY. The displayed formulas require h,K,Dh,DA and
D_Gu as coupled fields. R22's exact two-query action acquires conditional
mean matrix words, not a realized common matrix nor these joint tensor
fields. Randomizing a correction creates new covariance cumulants and
must be included in the next residual. For raw C^{1,1} g, the higher
derivatives in(16.1)-(16.3) also require a heat/weak interpretation and
charged value-query acquisition. The full target remains OPEN.

17. GAUSSIAN HODGE GAUGE AND AN EXACT NEWTON RESIDUAL IDENTITY

NEW: the weak projection in(16.4) can be upgraded to a POINTWISE baseline
right inverse by choosing the otherwise free divergence-free part of a
Stein kernel. This avoids a false symmetric-gradient obstruction.

Use standard Gaussian divergence delta on Z, the number operator
N=delta grad, and Dv_ij=partial_j v_i. Let R=R^T be a matrix polynomial
whose entries lie in pure Gaussian chaos m. All coefficients may depend
on G as an external parameter. Define
 s=delta delta R,
 phi=-s/(m+2)^2,
 u=grad phi,
 w=delta R+grad(delta u),
 B=(Dw-(Dw)^T)/(m+2),
 J=-R-(Du)^T+B.                                   (17.1)
Then EXACTLY
 delta J=u,      sym[J+(Du)^T]=-R.                (17.2)
These are polynomial identities, not merely identities after expectation.

Proof. s is chaos m+2, phi is chaos m+2, u,w are chaos m+1, and
delta u=(m+2)phi=-s/(m+2). Thus
 delta w=s+N(delta u)=0.
For any chaos-n vector v, Gaussian differentiation gives
 delta Dv=n v,
 delta(Dv)^T=grad(delta v)-v.
Using n=m+1 and delta w=0 proves delta B=w. Therefore
 delta J=-delta R-[grad(delta u)-u]+w=u.
Finally B is skew, so sym[J+(Du)^T]=-R. Sum(17.1) over matrix chaos
components to obtain a baseline right inverse on every finite matrix
polynomial. Only the choice J=(1/(m+1))Du would have imposed the
symmetric-gradient range restriction; that choice is unnecessary.

Here is the exact nonlinear Newton algebra. Write
 X=h(G)+psi(G,Z), E_Z psi=0,
 delta_G K=h-mu, delta_Z Jpsi=psi.
A valid Stein matrix before conditioning on X is
 T_X=K(D_GX)^T+Jpsi(D_ZX)^T.
Let R_X=sym T_X-I. For ANY centered perturbation u with delta_Z Ju=u,
the new pushforward X+=X+u has Stein residual
 R_X+ = R_X + L_X(u,Ju)+sym[Ju(D_Zu)^T],           (17.3)
 L_X(u,Ju)=sym[K(D_Gu)^T+Jpsi(D_Zu)^T
                                      +Ju(D_ZX)^T].
Thus if the LINEAR equation L_X(u,Ju)=-R_X is solved, the residual is
EXACTLY quadratic in the correction. The map X->X+u is positive and
preserves the mean whenever E_Zu=0. No unknown mu enters L_X.
At the Gaussian baseline psi=Z, Jpsi=I and K=0, (17.1) is a complete
right inverse of this linear equation. Near that baseline, the remaining
linear operator contains K D_Gu and the perturbations Jpsi-I,D_ZX-I.

POSSIBLE COMPILER MECHANISM, NOT PROVED. A uniformly controlled right
inverse of L_X could be formed by a Neumann series around(17.1). If each
small operator factor admits a charged unbiased value-query representation,
its linear series might be evaluated by a subcritical random chain rather
than an exponentially branching expression tree. The residual quadratic
identity would then require only loglog(d/epsilon) Newton stages. Random
unbiased correction estimates can cancel the first-order weak defect, but
their second-order moments, changes in the actual test argument, and the
extra Gaussian marks must be included explicitly; an unbiased coefficient
alone does not prove(17.3) for the implemented random program.

UNRESOLVED GATES. No dimension-controlled function space/right-inverse
bound for the perturbed L_X has been proved. No g-only implementation of
(17.1) and the Neumann factors with a subcritical cost/tail bound has been
proved. Higher source derivatives, joint adaptive centers, common-source
retention and random covariance cumulants remain charged. Hence there is
no acquired Newton compiler, no full-law TV theorem, and no claimed
polylog every-execution query cap in this file. Section17 is the exact
algebraic opening left by this round, not completion of the hard theorem.

18. DIMENSION-FREE L2 BOUND FOR THE BASELINE HODGE RIGHT INVERSE

The operator in(17.1) has a genuine dimension-independent baseline bound
in ordinary Gaussian L2, with Frobenius norm for matrix fields. For one
chaos-m component, Gaussian creation/annihilation norms give
 ||delta R||_2<=sqrt(m+1)||R||_2,
 ||s||_2<=sqrt((m+1)(m+2))||R||_2.
Hence
 ||u||_2<=sqrt(m+1)/(m+2)||R||_2,
 ||Du||_2<= (m+1)/(m+2)||R||_2<=||R||_2.          (18.1)
Furthermore w is the orthogonal divergence-free projection of delta R:
 w=delta R-grad(N^-1 s),
 ||w||_2^2=||delta R||_2^2-||s||_2^2/(m+2)
                                           <=(m+1)||R||_2^2.
Since delta w=0,
 <Dw,(Dw)^T>_2=-||w||_2^2,
 ||Dw-(Dw)^T||_2^2=2(m+2)||w||_2^2.
Thus ||B||_2<=sqrt(2)||R||_2 and
 ||J||_2<=(2+sqrt(2))||R||_2.                    (18.2)
Orthogonality of chaos components gives the same constants for finite
polynomials of arbitrary degree. These bounds extend conditionally in G
and therefore also to joint Gaussian L2 whenever the displayed fields
are square integrable. No factor d or chaos degree is concealed here.

This does NOT prove a bounded inverse for L_X. In particular its term
K D_Gu differentiates in the source variable; that derivative is unbounded
on plain Gaussian L2, even when ||K||op is small. A Neumann argument in
this norm would therefore be invalid. The exact baseline bound narrows
the remaining analytical gate to the coupled source derivative and the
acquisition-compatible control of the nonlinear factors.
