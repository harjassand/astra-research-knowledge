# Exact superactivation from complementary Schmidt spectra

The strongest result from this investigation is a construction with the following property:

> **Two fixed pure entangled reservoirs can each be incapable of catalytically implementing any nonseparable channel—even when any finite number of copies is available—while one copy of each, used together, permits every finite-dimensional bipartite channel.**
>
> Both reservoirs are normalizable, computably specified states with finite entanglement entropy and finite mean oscillator energy. They are returned exactly on the successful branch.

The mechanism is not causalization. It uses a partition of the prime numbers to construct two complementary Schmidt spectra. Every finite tensor power of either spectrum has arbitrarily severe tail bottlenecks. Their tensor product, however, is exactly a regular power-law spectrum that permits repeated heralded extraction of Bell pairs.

The proof also gives a precise consequence for the retained tensor-universality result: with one fixed countable-dimensional reservoir, exact three-party catalytic conversion reduces to an ordinary flattening-rank comparison.

**These are mathematical results proved below under the stated model. Their historical originality is not established, and I am not presenting them as completion of the historic objective.** In particular, exact infinite support and failure-branch resource loss are indispensable.

## 1. The theorem and its resource contract

Let \(c\) be a fixed bipartite pure state, possibly on countably infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Define \(\mathfrak C(c)\) to be the class of finite-dimensional bipartite channels \(\mathcal N\) for which a successful LOCC operation satisfies
\[
\mathcal S\!\left(X\otimes |c\rangle\langle c|\right)
=
p\,\mathcal N(X)\otimes |c\rangle\langle c|,
\qquad p>0,
\tag{1}
\]
for every input operator \(X\), including the corresponding identities with reference systems.

Here the success probability is independent of the input. The complete instrument is trace-preserving, but **the catalyst need only be returned on success**. Failure is unrestricted.

The protocols have finitely many communication rounds, with no uniform bound on that number. Finite or countable outcome sets and bounded local Kraus operators are allowed. There are no additional uncharged entangled ancillas. No closure under infinite-round limits or nonnormal operations is assumed.

Write \(\mathrm{SEP}\) for finite-dimensional channels with product Kraus decompositions. The retained stochastic implementation of separable channels gives
\[
\mathrm{SEP}\subseteq\mathfrak C(c)
\]
for every \(c\): leave the catalyst untouched and use the unassisted postselected implementation. This is a different operational class from deterministic LOCC; the distinction is important in the existing literature on separable operations and causal implementation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08835)

### Theorem — Maximal activation despite arbitrary finite replication

There exist two computably specified pure states \(c_P,c_Q\) such that, for every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C(c_P^{\otimes k})
=
\mathfrak C(c_Q^{\otimes k})
=
\mathrm{SEP},
}
\tag{2}
\]
but
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C(c_P\otimes c_Q)
=
\{\text{all finite-dimensional bipartite channels}\}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Both states have finite entropy of entanglement and finite mean energy for the nondegenerate local Hamiltonian
\[
H|n\rangle=(n-1)|n\rangle,\qquad n\ge1.
\]

In particular, the pair can emit a Bell pair with probability \(1/4\), while returning both reservoirs exactly and uncorrelated with the Bell pair.

The individual states are not unentangled or unusable when consumed. Equation (2) concerns their power **when the complete supplied reservoir must be returned exactly on success**.

## 2. A tail obstruction that excludes every nonseparable channel

The first task is to prove more than inability to extract a Bell pair. A reservoir might otherwise enable a channel with an entangled but nondistillable Choi state.

Let the squared Schmidt coefficients of a pure state \(c\) be
\[
\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots,
\qquad
t_n(c)=\sum_{j\ge n}\lambda_j.
\]

### Tail obstruction

If
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\{n:t_n(c)>0\}}
\frac{t_{2n}(c)}{t_n(c)}=0,
}
\tag{4}
\]
then
\[
\mathfrak C(c)=\mathrm{SEP}.
\tag{5}
\]

This will apply separately to every finite tensor power of each constructed reservoir.

### Proof

For a pure bipartite state \(\psi\), define
\[
e_r(\psi)=1-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_j(\psi).
\]
Extend this to mixed states by the convex roof
\[
E_r(\rho)
=
\inf_{\rho=\sum_i p_i|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|}
\sum_i p_i e_r(\psi_i),
\tag{6}
\]
using probability-measure ensembles when necessary.

These quantities are strongly monotone under the stated LOCC operations. The reason is elementary: the sum of the largest \(r\) eigenvalues is convex, so its complement is concave. For a local instrument on a pure input, the opposite party’s conditional marginals average to its original marginal. Concavity bounds the average output quantity. Applying that argument to ensembles approaching the infimum in (6) proves the mixed-state assertion. This is the established concave-function/convex-roof construction of entanglement monotones; the construction itself is not new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9807077)

Now let \(\sigma\) be any entangled state on a finite-dimensional bipartite space, and set
\[
d=\min\{\dim A,\dim B\},
\qquad
\delta_\sigma
=
\inf_{\omega\in\mathrm{SEP}}\|\sigma-\omega\|_1>0.
\]
Define
\[
a_\sigma=\frac{\delta_\sigma^2}{4(d-1)}>0.
\]

I claim that
\[
\boxed{
E_{2n-1}\!\left(\sigma\otimes|c\rangle\langle c|\right)
\ge a_\sigma\,t_n(c).
}
\tag{7}
\]

To prove this, every pure vector in a decomposition of
\(\sigma\otimes|c\rangle\langle c|\) must factor as
\[
v_i\otimes c,
\]
because the catalyst marginal is pure.

Let
\[
\beta_{i,1}\ge\beta_{i,2}\ge\cdots
\]
be the squared Schmidt coefficients of \(v_i\). The tensor-product spectrum contains two copies of the sequence
\(\beta_{i,2}\lambda_j\), dominated respectively by the contributions from the first and second Schmidt coefficients. Removing its largest \(2n-1\) entries therefore leaves at least
\[
\beta_{i,2}\bigl(2t_n(c)-\lambda_n\bigr)
\ge \beta_{i,2}t_n(c).
\tag{8}
\]

For any ensemble \(\sigma=\sum_i p_i|v_i\rangle\langle v_i|\), approximate each \(v_i\) by its closest product vector. This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_\sigma
&\le 2\sum_i p_i\sqrt{1-\beta_{i,1}}\\
&\le 2\sqrt{(d-1)\sum_i p_i\beta_{i,2}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_i p_i\beta_{i,2}\ge a_\sigma.
\]
Combining this with (8) and taking the infimum over ensembles proves (7).

Suppose a successful LOCC operation produced
\[
\sigma\otimes|c\rangle\langle c|
\]
from \(c\) and free local inputs with probability \(p>0\). Strong monotonicity and (7) imply
\[
p\,a_\sigma\,t_n(c)
\le
E_{2n-1}(c)
=
t_{2n}(c).
\]
Thus, for every \(n\),
\[
p\,a_\sigma
\le
\frac{t_{2n}(c)}{t_n(c)},
\]
contradicting (4).

Finally, feed local maximally entangled input–reference states into a purported implementation of a nonseparable channel. Those preparations are free across the Alice–Bob cut. The successful output would be its entangled normalized Choi state tensor \(c\), which has just been ruled out.

Therefore only separable channels are possible. The reverse inclusion was already available. ∎

**The obstruction excludes all entangled Choi states, not only distillable ones.** That is why it suffices for the full channel statement.

## 3. Prime factorization supplies the positive mechanism

For a set \(P\) of primes, let
\[
M(P)=\{a\ge1:\text{ every prime divisor of }a\text{ belongs to }P\},
\]
including \(1\). Define
\[
Z_P=\sum_{a\in M(P)}a^{-3},
\qquad
|c_P\rangle
=
Z_P^{-1/2}
\sum_{a\in M(P)}a^{-3/2}|a,a\rangle.
\tag{9}
\]

The sum converges because \(Z_P\le\zeta(3)\).

Suppose \(P,Q\) partition all primes. Every positive integer factors uniquely as
\[
n=ab,\qquad a\in M(P),\quad b\in M(Q).
\]
Hence
\[
Z_PZ_Q=\zeta(3).
\]

At both laboratories, relabel the supported basis by
\[
|a\rangle|b\rangle\longmapsto |ab\rangle.
\]
These are bounded partial isometries, reversible on the states’ supports. They transform \(c_P\otimes c_Q\) exactly into
\[
|Z\rangle
=
\frac1{\sqrt{\zeta(3)}}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-3/2}|n,n\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]

The point is not merely that the joint state is entangled. It has a spectrum admitting an exact self-returning extraction.

### Exact Bell-pair extraction

Put
\[
j(n)=\lceil n/2\rceil.
\]
Both parties relabel
\[
|n\rangle\longmapsto |j(n)\rangle|b(n)\rangle,
\]
where \(b(n)\in\{0,1\}\) records whether \(n\) is odd or even.

Alice additionally applies the diagonal success filter
\[
F|n\rangle
=
\left(\frac{n}{2j(n)}\right)^{3/2}|n\rangle.
\tag{11}
\]
Every diagonal entry lies in \((0,1]\), so this is a bounded contraction with a valid failure completion.

The successful coefficient becomes
\[
n^{-3/2}
\left(\frac{n}{2j(n)}\right)^{3/2}
=
(2j(n))^{-3/2}.
\]
Therefore the exact vector identity is
\[
\boxed{
|Z\rangle\longmapsto
\frac12\,|Z\rangle\otimes|\Phi_2\rangle.
}
\tag{12}
\]

The success probability is \(1/4\). Reversing the prime-factor relabelling returns \(c_P\otimes c_Q\) exactly.

Repeating this operation \(m\) times supplies \(m\) Bell pairs with probability \(4^{-m}\), while returning the same reservoirs. A finite supply of Bell pairs implements any fixed finite-dimensional bipartite channel: teleport Bob’s input to Alice, perform the channel locally, and teleport Bob’s output back.

All entanglement extraction can occur before the unknown input arrives. This is not an attempt to move the flag of a previously unassisted operation backward in time: the reservoirs are genuine entangled inputs, and they can be damaged on failure.

It remains to construct \(P,Q\) so that neither individual reservoir, nor any finite tensor power of it, has this power.

## 4. Constructing two individually obstructed spectra

The following elementary counting argument makes the construction possible.

### Finite-prime lemma

Fix a finite nonempty prime set \(F\) and an integer \(k\ge1\). List in nondecreasing order the products
\[
a_1a_2\cdots a_k,
\qquad a_i\in M(F),
\]
counting each ordered \(k\)-tuple separately:
\[
b_1\le b_2\le\cdots.
\]

Define the unnormalized spectral tails
\[
A_n=\sum_{j\ge n}b_j^{-3}.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{\inf_n A_{2n}/A_n=0.}
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed, every tuple of product at most \(X\) is specified by \(k|F|\) nonnegative prime exponents, each at most \(\log_2X\). Thus
\[
\#\{j:b_j\le X\}
\le
(1+\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor)^{k|F|}.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that \(b_n\) grows faster than every fixed power of \(n\), and \(A_n\) decays faster than every inverse power.

If \(A_{2n}\ge\varepsilon A_n\) held for all \(n\), iteration along \(n=2^j\) would give a polynomial lower bound on those same tails. That is a contradiction. ∎

### Protecting a tail bottleneck against all future primes

A finite-prime witness must survive after infinitely many additional primes are assigned.

Suppose, for the current finite set \(F\),
\[
A_{2n}<\varepsilon A_n.
\tag{15}
\]
Choose an integer cutoff \(R\ge b_{2n}\). Ensure that every prime subsequently added to this reservoir is greater than \(R\).

The normalization constant of the new-prime part is at most
\[
1+\sum_{m>R}m^{-3}
\le 1+\frac1{2R^2}.
\]
Consequently, the total extra unnormalized mass in the \(k\)-copy spectrum is at most
\[
\Delta_R
=
Z_F^k
\left[
\left(1+\frac1{2R^2}\right)^k-1
\right].
\tag{16}
\]

Choose \(R\) large enough that
\[
\Delta_R<\varepsilon A_n.
\tag{17}
\]

No new spectral entry appears before the chosen cutoff. If \(\Delta\le\Delta_R\) is the actual added mass, the final tail ratio therefore obeys
\[
\frac{A_{2n}+\Delta}{A_n+\Delta}
<
2\varepsilon.
\tag{18}
\]

Thus a finite construction can enforce a permanent bottleneck despite all later additions.

### The alternating construction

Start with \(2\in P\), \(3\in Q\), and all other primes unassigned.

At stage \(r\), consider the current finite set of primes assigned to \(P\). For each
\[
1\le k\le r,
\]
use the finite-prime lemma to choose a witness with
\[
A_{2n}/A_n<2^{-r}.
\]
Choose a common cutoff large enough to protect every selected witness by (16)–(17). Assign **all still-unassigned primes up to that cutoff to \(Q\)**.

Now reverse the roles. Obtain protected witnesses for every \(k\le r\) using the current finite \(Q\), and assign all newly encountered primes up to the next cutoff to \(P\).

At each half-stage, also move the cutoff beyond the next unassigned prime. Both sets consequently receive infinitely many primes, and every prime is eventually assigned.

For a fixed \(k\), infinitely many later stages protect a \(k\)-copy bottleneck for each reservoir. Equation (18) gives
\[
\inf_n
\frac{t_{2n}(c_P^{\otimes k})}
     {t_n(c_P^{\otimes k})}
=
\inf_n
\frac{t_{2n}(c_Q^{\otimes k})}
     {t_n(c_Q^{\otimes k})}
=0.
\tag{19}
\]

The tail obstruction proves (2). Unique prime factorization and the extraction identity prove (3). This completes the superactivation construction.

At the level of sequences, the result says:

> Two summable spectra can each retain arbitrarily severe doubling-tail bottlenecks under every finite tensor power, while their tensor product is exactly the regular spectrum \(n^{-3}/\zeta(3)\).

The power comes from complementary arithmetic support, not from increasing either reservoir’s number of copies.

## 5. The states have finite energy and an effective specification

These qualifications prevent the construction from hiding an infinite entropy or an uncomputable state description.

For the ordinary oscillator Hamiltonian stated above,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle H\rangle_{c_P}
&=
\frac1{Z_P}
\sum_{a\in M(P)}(a-1)a^{-3}\\
&\le
\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}(a-1)a^{-3}
=
\zeta(2)-\zeta(3)<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The same holds for \(c_Q\).

Their entanglement entropies are finite because
\[
S(c_P)
=
\log Z_P+
\frac3{Z_P}\sum_{a\in M(P)}a^{-3}\log a,
\tag{21}
\]
and the corresponding sum over all positive integers converges.

The prime partition is computable. For finite \(F\),
\[
Z_F=\prod_{p\in F}(1-p^{-3})^{-1}
\]
is rational. The tails used in the searches are a rational total minus a finite rational sum. Each witness search therefore uses exact arithmetic and terminates by the lemma. Each cutoff search also terminates.

To decide whether a given prime belongs to \(P\), execute stages until it has been assigned. The state coefficients and normalization constants can then be approximated with an effective tail bound
\[
\sum_{a>N}a^{-3}\le\frac1{2N^2}.
\]

**Computable specification is not efficient preparation.** The construction supplies no practical runtime bound for the prime partition, no finite circuit preparing the exact states, and no bounded-time physical realization of the infinite-support filters.

Infinite-dimensional Schmidt-tail phenomena under finite-energy conditions already have substantial antecedents. Owari, Matsumoto and Murao established that infinite-dimensional SLOCC order can differ radically from finite-dimensional order even with bounded energy and finite information exchange. The general observation “finite energy does not remove infinite-dimensional effects” is therefore not new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406141)

## 6. Consequence for the catalytic tensor construction

Place the fixed state \(Z\) from (10) between parties \(B,C\). For any nonzero finite-dimensional three-tensors \(T,S\),
\[
\boxed{
T\otimes Z\succeq S\otimes Z
\iff
\operatorname{rank}T_{A|BC}
\ge
\operatorname{rank}S_{A'|B'C'}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

The restriction maps here are bounded local linear maps, and the conversion is exact on a nonzero successful branch.

**Necessity.** The catalyst lies entirely on the \(BC\) side of the spectator cut \(A|BC\). It does not change that cut’s finite Schmidt rank, which local maps cannot increase.

**Sufficiency.** The rank inequality gives a bipartite pure-state restriction consisting of a map on \(A\) and a joint finite-dimensional map on \(BC\). Extract enough Bell pairs from \(Z\), teleport \(C\)’s input to \(B\), apply the joint map as a local filter, and teleport \(C\)’s output back. Fix a successful classical transcript. The resulting transformation is a product of bounded local maps and returns \(Z\) exactly.

Thus the earlier finite-catalyst algebraic universality and this countable-catalyst conversion order behave very differently. In particular, the equal-first-flattening-rank instances of the retained compiler become convertible with this single fixed reservoir.

**This does not decide the old undecidable problem.** It changes its allowed resource. A finite catalyst and a normalizable infinite-support catalyst are not interchangeable, even when the latter has finite mean energy and a computable description.

## 7. Decisive falsifications and limits

### Exact return on success is not deterministic embezzlement

The factor \(1/2\) in (12) cannot be discarded. Repeated extraction succeeds \(m\) times with probability \(4^{-m}\). A failed extraction need not return the reservoir.

Therefore there is no deterministic production of entanglement from an unchanged catalyst, and no repeat-until-success procedure with a guaranteed unchanged resource.

The established impossibility of perfect embezzlement in the tensor-product framework concerns a different normalization requirement. Cleve, Liu and Paulsen’s result does not permit replacing a subnormalized successful vector by a normalized deterministic output. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05061)

This also preserves the earlier channel-removal and sequential-cost results: their complete operations return the resource under their stated conditions. The present complete instrument need not do so.

### Purity cannot be removed from the obstruction

A stronger proposal—that an exactly returned reservoir must either add nothing beyond separable channels or enable everything—is false for mixed reservoirs.

Let \(\sigma\) be a finite-dimensional PPT entangled state. Such states are supplied, for example, by unextendible-product-basis constructions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9808030)

Form a locally flagged direct-sum reservoir
\[
\Xi_{\sigma,q}
=
(1-q)\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}q^k\,\sigma^{\otimes k},
\qquad 0<q<1.
\tag{23}
\]
The \(k=0\) block is a product vacuum.

Project onto \(k\ge1\), split off one copy of \(\sigma\), and relabel the remainder. This gives the exact successful transformation
\[
\boxed{
\Xi_{\sigma,q}\longmapsto
q\,\sigma\otimes\Xi_{\sigma,q}.
}
\tag{24}
\]

So this mixed reservoir can implement a nonseparable constant-output channel.

But the reservoir is PPT, and every local successful operation preserves PPT. It cannot emit a Bell pair. Hence its class lies strictly between the two extremes.

The pure-catalyst proof fails at an identifiable point: a pure returned marginal forces every vector in a decomposition to factor through the same catalyst. A mixed returned marginal does not.

### Finite support cannot realize the extraction mechanism

Every finite-Schmidt-rank pure state has a zero tail beyond some index and satisfies the obstruction (4). Equivalently, exact Bell extraction with the same finite pure catalyst would double its Schmidt rank.

Thus every finite-support truncation of the constructed reservoirs loses the exact universal-return property. Finite energy does not provide a finite-support substitute.

An approximate truncation analysis could still yield useful protocols, but it would be a different theorem. No efficient finite-resource advantage, and no algorithm for the retained finite-catalyst undecidability problem, follows from the exact construction.

## 8. Originality, verification and the remaining objective

Several ingredients have clear predecessors. The convex-roof monotones are standard. Infinite-dimensional SLOCC classification through Schmidt tails—including the distinction between exact reachability and \(\varepsilon\)-convertibility—was developed by Owari and coauthors. Those results preclude claiming the entire tail-based viewpoint as new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0609167)

Superactivation itself is also established: Smith and Yard showed that two zero-quantum-capacity channels can have positive capacity jointly. The present theorem concerns a different task—exact catalytic implementation classes of fixed pure states, with individual nonactivation under every finite tensor power—not the discovery of superactivation in general. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4935)

The candidate contribution is the **complementary-prime construction**, together with the tail obstruction strong enough to exclude every nonseparable channel and the resulting maximal exact activation theorem. I did not identify this precise construction in the sources checked. The targeted Astra repository searches did not provide a comprehensive priority audit.

I also performed exact finite checks of the extraction identity, unique-factorization routing and six protected tail certificates. These are diagnostics, not a proof of the infinite diagonal construction.

**The historic mission remains unresolved.** The strongest established structural result is that exact catalytic power can be absent under every finite replication of either resource and become universal when two complementary, finite-energy spectra are combined.

The obstruction to turning this into finite-resource computational power is not merely an unoptimized constant: the mechanism depends on exact infinite support and accepts irreversible loss on failure. Finite support forbids its defining extraction identity. The mixed-state counterexample also blocks an unrestricted all-or-nothing extension.

Those boundaries are part of the result, not assumptions to be removed by assertion.
