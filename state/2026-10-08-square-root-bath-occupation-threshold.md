## Setup and notation

For `N` qubits and a possibly `N`-dependent number `nu_N>=0`, let `Phi_s` be the CPTP semigroup generated by

`L(rho)=(nu_N+1)D[J_-](rho)+nu_N D[J_+](rho)`, where `J_a=sum sigma_a/2`, `rho_N(0)=I/2^N`, `s=Gamma*t` and the jumps are **unnormalized** `J_±`.

All sectors of fixed total spin `j=M/2` are conserved, with maximally-mixed initial weights `w_{N,M}=[(M+1)^2/(N/2+M/2+1)]binom(N,(N-M)/2)/2^N`. In sector `M`, the classical excitation-level chain has `k=0,...,M`, birth `lambda_k=nu_N(M-k)(k+1)`, death `mu_k=(nu_N+1)k(M-k+1)`, initial uniform `u_M(k)=1/(M+1)`, and stationary geometric `pi_M(k)=q^k/sum_{ell=0}^M q^ell`, `q=nu_N/(nu_N+1)` (at nu0 delta0). Sector weights satisfy `M/sqrtN=>Maxwell(f(x)=sqrt(2/pi)x²e^(-x²/2))`, E[M]=O(sqrtN), all fixed moments controlled.

## Theorem A: subcritical bath occupation STILL creates almost maximally distant transient entanglement

Suppose `nu_N=o(sqrtN)`, without requiring ν fixed, and define

`L_N=log[sqrtN/(nu_N+1)] -> infinity`, `c_N=sqrt(L_N)`, `s_N=c_N/sqrtN`.

Then both

`inf_{sigma in SEP_N} (1/2)||Phi_{s_N}(I/2^N)-sigma||_1 ->1`, AND

`(1/2)||Phi_{s_N}(I/2^N)-rho_N(infty)||_1 ->1`.

Indeed, on spin-sector scale `M=x sqrtN` with x in any compact `[a,R]` in `(0,infinity)`, let y=k/M, c=s sqrtN. The exact generator drift is `-x y(1-y)+[nu_N-(2nu_N+1)y]/sqrtN` and quadratic-variation rate is `O((nu_N+1)/sqrtN)=O(e^(-L_N))`. Couple with logistic deterministic solution `z_x(c;u)=u/[u+(1-u)e^(xc)]`. Doob+Gronwall gives the uniform compact-window error bound

`E sup_{c<=c_N}|y(c)-z_x(c;y(0))| <= C e^(R c_N)[c_N e^(-L_N)+sqrt(c_N e^(-L_N))] = o(e^(-R c_N))`,

because `c_N=sqrt L_N`. Initial k/M tends to Uniform[0,1] in sectors M~sqrtN. The logistic y(c_N) tends to0 for almost all u. Using uniform integrability of `M²/N` over the Maxwell law, obtain

`[Var J_x+Var J_y]/N <= E[M]/(2N)+E[(M²/N)y(s_N)] ->0`.

The explicit random-common-equatorial-axis, local-qubit-measurement test of `state/2026-10-08-far-from-equilibrium-near-unit-entanglement.md` then has target accept probability→1 and uniform fully-separable false-accept probability→0. This proves the first limit and a diverging (also regularized) multipartite relative entropy to fullSEP via the same sequential local testing argument.

To establish the SECOND limit independently of the earlier fixed-temperature mixing law, keep only typical sectors `a<=x<=R` and typical initial fractions `u>=delta>0`. Logistic evolution obeys `z_x(c_N;u)>=delta e^(-R c_N)`; the coupling error is asymptotically MUCH smaller, so the target at `s_N` has `y >= (delta/2)e^(-R c_N)` with probability tending to1 on this typical set. Meanwhile stationary distribution has tail bound for large N:

`Pr_pi_M(k>=M delta e^(-R c_N)/2) <= 2exp[-M delta e^(-R c_N)/(2(nu_N+1))] <= 2exp[-(a delta/2)e^(L_N-R sqrt L_N)] ->0`.

Here `-log q=log(1+1/nu_N)>=1/(nu_N+1)` and `q^(M+1)->0` in typical sectors since `M/(nu_N+1)->infinity`. The event distinguishes actual and stationary populations. Let δ↓0, a↓0,R↑∞ to obtain full TV→1 after averaging over sectors. No high-temperature approximation, exact PPT conclusion or full input-space mixing theorem is used.

This is an **existence of an intermediate time** depending on ν_N, not a universal fixed-c scaling or a necessary/exact onset threshold.

## Theorem B: supercritical bath occupation PREVENTS global entanglement far from the initial free state

Suppose `nu_N/sqrtN -> infinity`. Then

`sup_{s>=0} inf_{sigma in SEP_N} (1/2)||rho_N(s)-sigma||_1 ->0`.

In fact a stronger global comparison holds:

`sup_{s>=0} (1/2)||rho_N(s)-I/2^N||_1 ->0`.

Proof. For each sector, `pi_M(k) ∝ exp(-beta_N k)` with `beta_N=log(1+1/nu_N)`. Relative density to uniform differs by at most `exp(beta_N M)-1`, hence `TV(pi_M,u_M)<=min(1, exp(beta_N M)-1) <=2 beta_N M` (using linear bound if beta M<=1 and trivial TV<=1 otherwise). Therefore, from exact orthogonal sector decomposition,

`eps_N:= D_T(rho_N(infty), I/2^N) = sum_M w_{N,M} TV(pi_M,u_M) <= 2 beta_N E_w[M] = O(sqrtN/nu_N)->0`.

The Lindbladian fixes `rho_N(infty)` and is CPTP, so by contractivity

`D_T(rho_N(s),rho_N(infty))<=D_T(I/2^N,rho_N(infty))=eps_N`, and triangle inequality gives `D_T(rho_N(s),I/2^N)<=2 eps_N` uniformly over all `s>=0`. Since I/2^N is fully separable, this proves the claimed uniform upper bound. **No transient strong entanglement of any duration** is possible in trace distance when ν_N grows faster than √N, within this exact model. It is a robust information-theoretic obstruction, not merely failure of a chosen witness.

## Theorem C: critical scaling ν_N~γ√N gives a nontrivial Wright-Fisher diffusion

Let `nu_N/sqrtN->gamma∈(0,infinity)`. Condition on `M/sqrtN->x>0`. In macroscopic time `c=s sqrtN` and fraction y=k/M, the generators acting on smooth functions `phi` converge to

`(L_{x,gamma}phi)(y) = [gamma(1-2y)-x y(1-y)] phi'(y) + gamma y(1-y) phi''(y)`, `0<=y<=1`.

Equivalently the process solves the Jacobi/Wright-Fisher SDE

`dY_c = [gamma(1-2Y_c)-xY_c(1-Y_c)] dc + sqrt(2gamma Y_c(1-Y_c)) dB_c`, initial `Y_0~Uniform[0,1]`, with natural inward/entrance behavior at boundaries.

Derivation: Taylor expansion of the rescaled birth-death generator gives drift `b_N=(lambda-mu)/(M sqrtN)->gamma(1-2y)-x y(1-y)` and second local moment `a_N=(lambda+mu)/(M² sqrtN)->2gamma y(1-y)`. Third-order Taylor remainder is uniformly O(N^-1/2) on compact positive x, since jumps 1/M shrink and the rescaled total jump intensity is O(N). Bounded-state compactness, convergence of drift and quadratic variation, and uniqueness of the Wright-Fisher martingale problem yield weak path convergence. This result does **not** automatically give TV convergence between discrete and diffusion distributions without a local limit; only weak observables and stationary Riemann sums are asserted.

The invariant density of this diffusion is exactly

`p_{x,gamma}(y)= (x/gamma)exp[-xy/gamma]/(1-exp[-x/gamma])`, `0<=y<=1`.

Verify zero-flux identity: if `a(y)=2gamma y(1-y)`, then `b(y)=a'(y)/2 + (a(y)/2)(log p)'`, giving gamma(1-2y)-x y(1-y). It agrees with the geometric discrete stationary law in the Riemann-sum limit.

### Critical stationary-state distance and an explicit witness crossover

Write `p_a(y)=a exp(-ay)/(1-exp(-a))` for `a>0`, `p_0=1`, and

`C(gamma)=integral_0^infinity f(x) [.5integral_0^1 |p_{x/gamma}(y)-1|dy] dx`.

Then **exactly by sector orthogonality + dominated Riemann sums**,

`D_T(rho_N(infty),I/2^N)-> C(gamma)`.

Here `C(gamma)∈(0,1)`, with limits `C(gamma)->1` as gamma↓0 and `C(gamma)->0` as gamma↑infty. It gives a continuous critical boundary between the two extreme regimes. For computation the unique density crossing with 1 occurs at `y0(a)=log(a/[1-exp(-a)])/a` and `TV(Unif,p_a)=[1-exp(-a*y0)]/[1-exp(-a)]-y0`. For gamma→infinity, `C(gamma)~sqrt(2/pi)/(4gamma)` from the small-a expansion TV≈a/8.

`P_infty(gamma)=integral_0^infinity f(x)x[1/2-mu_stat(x/gamma)]dx`,

with `mu_stat(a)=1/a-1/(e^a-1)` and `mu_stat(0)=1/2`. The conserved-Casimir spin-variance FULLSEP witness `-E Jz>sqrtN/2` applies whenever `P_infty(gamma)>1/2`. Numerical quadrature (not symbolic closed form) yields a unique **witness** crossing
