Question: when can a two-coordinate energy estimate that says nothing about the full pair gap nevertheless provide a polynomial-time sampler for one desired law? The answer needs a refreshable bottom law, many ordered guides, and the inequality for **every** additive pair function. A bound for selected observables is insufficient.

## Theorem and legal interface

Let T be a nonnegative integer. For each tier t=0,...,T let Ω_t be a nonempty finite set with strictly positive probability law μ_t. The laws may live on different sets. For each t>=1, let K_t be a μ_t⊗μ_(t-1)-stationary Markov kernel on Ω_t×Ω_(t-1), acting on both coordinates. Assume its Dirichlet energy satisfies, for all real a on Ω_t and b on Ω_(t-1),

    Var_(μ_t)(a) <= C E_(μ_t⊗μ_(t-1),K_t)(a(x)+b(y)),       (1)

for a known C>0. Reversibility of K_t is assumed when a spectral-gap/mixing conclusion is wanted; stationarity alone suffices for the energy comparison proved below. No full pair gap is assumed.

Take an integer q>=max(1,16C). Put q independent slots at every tier and

    ν = product_(t=0)^T μ_t^q,       M = T q² + q.

For every upper slot x at t>=1 and every guide y at t-1 there is one update K_t on that pair. Each of the q bottom slots has a refresh update replacing it by an independent μ_0 draw. Define P to hold with probability 1/2, otherwise choose uniformly one of these M updates. Then P is ν-stationary and reversible, and

    Var_ν(f) <= (8M/3) E_(ν,P)(f).                         (2)

Thus its spectral gap is at least 3/(8M), irrespective of whether the individual K_t have positive full gaps. For T=0 this remains valid, although ordinary independent refresh gives a sharper elementary bound.

Every update must be implemented from the stated input. For a computational consequence, require: encoded states and relevant update parameters of length at most b; computable legal initial states; an explicit fixed-time sampler for each update within TV error ρ at cost B_update(b,log(1/ρ)); and a similarly charged sampler for μ_0. These may be built from polynomial-length finite rational proposal lists and exact rational acceptance ratios. A proved rational upper bound on C suffices to choose q; a numerical real constant with no computational representation is not a free input. Unknown partition functions, matching counts, or a supplied target sampler are not implicit in this interface. The theorem does not acquire C, T, or the update implementation.

If the ideal initial law has χ² divergence at most D-1 from ν, then

    ||ν_initial P^s - ν||_TV <= (1/2) sqrt(D-1) exp(-3s/(8M)).  (3)

For a deterministic initial state z one may use D=1/ν(z). A proved bound log(1/ν(z))<=B permits s=ceil((8M/3)(B/2+log(2/η))) to get ideal TV at most η. In the absence of a warm start or a lower stationary-mass bound, (2) alone does not supply a bit-complexity mixing bound.

No transcendental oracle is needed to choose the step count: if B is a proved rational upper bound, compute k=min{j:2^j>=2/η} by doubling and exact rational comparison, replace log(2/η) by k, and take the rational ceiling for s. This is conservative because log x<=log2 x for x>=1. The magnitude of this acquired B, rather than only its binary encoding length, contributes to s.

Simulating s updates with conditional per-update TV error at most η/s incurs at most η further TV error, by a sequential coupling. Use fresh bits at every call. Including initialization, replace s by s+q+1 in this allocation. A fixed-time finite-precision implementation has charged time

    O(s * (state-scan/update-selection cost + B_update(b,log((s+q+1)/η))))

plus q(T+1) stored states and the acquired bottom sampling/preprocessing cost. The formulas count the replicas and the Tq² potential updates; they do not hide them inside an oracle. To implement the uniform update index with conditional TV error at most ρ/2, choose k with 2^k>=2M/ρ, draw k fair bits as J, and return floor(MJ/2^k). Every index has either floor(2^k/M) or ceil(2^k/M) preimages, so total variation from uniform is at most M/2^k<=ρ/2. Combine this with an update sampler of error ρ/2 and one exact half-hold bit. Integer comparison/multiplication and O(log M+log(1/ρ)) random bits are included in the selection cost. The ideal refresh is exact in the mathematical proof; an actual finite-bit refresh is approximated and its TV cost included. Exact rational choices with non-dyadic probabilities cannot in general be made by a fixed bounded number of fair bits.

## Complete energy proof

Order slots from highest tier to lowest, breaking ties arbitrarily. Every guide y comes after its upper slot x. Let U_x denote all predecessors of x. Conditional expectations are under the independent product law ν. Define

    h_x = E[f | U_x,x] - E[f | U_x].

The h_x are martingale increments, so Var_ν(f)=sum_x ||h_x||². For an allowed pair (x,y), put

    k_xy = E[f | U_x,y] - E[f | U_x],
    g_xy = E[f | U_x,x,y],
    r_xy = g_xy - E[f | U_x] - h_x - k_xy.

For each fixed U_x, the independent laws of x and y are μ_t and μ_(t-1). Applying (1), then integrating U_x, gives

    ||h_x||² <= C E_xy(h_x+k_xy).

Jensen shows E_xy(g_xy)<=E_xy(f): the pair transition is independent of all coordinates being integrated out. A stationary Markov kernel obeys E_xy(v)<=2||v||², since (v(z)-v(z'))²<=2v(z)²+2v(z')² and both transition endpoints have the same law. Constants depending only on U_x have zero pair energy. Therefore

    ||h_x||² <= 2C E_xy(f) + 4C ||r_xy||².               (4)

The crucial sum is not estimated by the number of pairs. Write the orthogonal product decomposition

    f = sum_S f_S,      E[f | A] = sum_(S subset A) f_S,

where f_S is centered separately in every coordinate it contains. Expanding r_xy gives

    r_xy = sum_(S subset U_x union {x,y}; x,y in S) f_S.

Every S in this sum has y last and x penultimate in the slot order. Those two positions determine (x,y) uniquely. Thus different allowed pairs contain disjoint collections of orthogonal components, even when the pairs share a slot, and

    sum_(allowed x,y) ||r_xy||² <= Var_ν(f).             (5)

This is why the guide must follow x and U_x must contain only predecessors. If the context were all other slots, (5) would generally be false.

For a bottom slot i, independent refresh has energy equal to the variance over i. Jensen and the martingale definition give ||h_i||²<=E_i(f). Average (4) over all q guides of each nonbase x and sum over x. By (5),

    Var_ν(f) <= (2C/q) sum_pairs E_xy(f)
                + (4C/q) Var_ν(f) + sum_bottom E_i(f).

As q>=16C, absorption yields

    Var_ν(f) <= (1/6) sum_pairs E_xy(f)
                 + (4/3) sum_bottom E_i(f)
              <= (4/3)(sum_pairs E_xy(f)+sum_bottom E_i(f)).

The lazy scan has E_(ν,P)=(sum_pairs E_xy+sum_bottom E_i)/(2M), proving (2). The spectral mixing estimate (3) follows by diagonalizing the finite reversible lazy kernel on the centered subspace, then applying Cauchy–Schwarz to total variation. No irreducibility premise is needed separately: (2) implies it on the strictly positive state space.

## A control excluding a disguised full-pair-gap premise

Take Ω_0=Ω_1={-1,+1}, both uniform. Let K(x,y)=(-x,-y) deterministically. It is reversible but preserves xy, so its full pair gap is zero. Every additive function is a constant plus ax+by; its pair energy is 2(a²+b²), while Var(a(x))=a². Thus (1) holds with C=1/2 for every additive a,b. Choosing q=8 meets 16C; bottom refresh and all upper-guide flips have a strictly positive full product gap. A sharper finite control with q=4 also works, but uses the general absorption q>4C rather than the displayed theorem's q>=16C.

For any q>=3, the Walsh character with u upper slots and v bottom slots has scan eigenvalue

    1 - [2(u(q-v)+(q-u)v)+v]/(2(q²+q)).

The pair flip contributes energy 2 precisely when the character contains exactly one endpoint; a bottom refresh contributes 1 when that bottom coordinate is present. All nonconstant characters have positive loss. The exact checker records this and the unique last/penultimate ANOVA allocation. This is a falsifiable primitive test, not performance evidence for the matching algorithm.

## Consequence, prior, and exact remaining gate

The source's real application is weighted perfect-matchings on tree bags: two-edge switches, color changes, and alternating-cycle swaps furnish an **additive** two-coordinate bound. Adjacent clamped weight tiers are within factor two in density, transferring it to (1). This lemma removes a need to prove a full pair Poincaré inequality and instead obtains full mixing only after building a ladder with a refreshable unit-activity bottom law. The unit law is acquired by an explicit tree dynamic program, not supplied for free.

For a new model, the reusable route is to establish (1) via local repairs plus a product-preserving two-replica move; acquire a chain of nearby laws and a base sampler; then use this lemma. A bound limited to a selected observable subspace, such as family114's defect-type observables, cannot be plugged into (1) without a new proof for all additive functions. The current exercise proves the replication step; it does not prove a new additive comparison for an unrelated spin, matroid, or discrepancy model.
