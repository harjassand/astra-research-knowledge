## 1. Original interface and complete guarantee

Input only the expanded rational coefficients of f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2), with unknown rational invertible L and unknown rational Q>0. Assume Q has a hidden representation

    Q=D[diag(a_1,...,a_n)+bJ_n]D,

where D is positive real diagonal, b>0, a_i>=0 and at most one a_i is zero. These parameters and this representation are not supplied. The matrix in brackets is PD: its quadratic form is sum a_i z_i^2+b(sum z_i)^2, whose only zero is z=0 under the stated zero-count condition. Positive definite diagonal plus a positive rank-one term is the principal practical subclass; the single-zero residual boundary is also included.

Then the canonical coefficient-derived F=adj(Hess f)grad(det Hess f) has nonzero coordinate extactic. The already gated exact line/reflection/rational-metric/axis-selector construction acquires all rational hidden axes and a checked positive weighted-square representation with expected polynomial bit cost. No new implementation or hidden parameter recognition is required.

More generally the proof covers Q of the same form with any b!=0 and Q>0 whenever all ordered pairs satisfy

    beta_ij=3a_j/b^2+(n+1)a_i/[b(a_i+b)]>0.       (1)

This is an explicit sufficient promise, not a claim that every PD negative rank-one or negative residual input obeys it. In the principal b>0,a_i>=0 family, positivity is automatic because i,j cannot both have zero residual.

## 2. Hereditary family with every induction parameter retained

In the proof coordinates D=I, for an active index set I of size N define

    R_I=sum_(i in I)y_i^2,
    f_I=sum_(i in I)a_i y_i^4+bR_I^2,
    H_I=Hess f_I, h_I=det H_I,
    F_(I,k)=adj H_I grad(R_I^k h_I),  k>=0,

and require N+k=n. Every principal squared-contrast block is PD, so a_i+b>0. If coordinate j in I is removed, the full Hessian at y_j=0 splits as H_(I\{j}) and the scalar4bR_(I\{j}). The normal derivative of the even R_I^k h_I is zero on that face. Consequently

    F_(I,k)|face=(4b)^2 R_(I\{j}) F_(I\{j},k+1).       (2)

This is the same restriction law as the exchangeable case; heterogeneity affects diagonal fourth-power coefficients but does not affect omitted-row coupling bR. The scalar is a nonzero polynomial when b!=0. The lower-dimensional proof field need not be supplied or built by the learner.

## 3. Exact transverse slope at every derived axis

Fix i in I, put A_i=a_i+b>0 and c_i=12A_i(4b)^(N-1). At e_i, H_I=diag(12A_i,4b,...,4b), h_I=c_i. Sign symmetry makes F_(I,k)(e_i) radial and

    F_i(e_i)=(N+k)c_i^2/(6A_i).

For j!=i, direct Hessian logdet differentiation gives

    (partial_jj h_I)(e_i)/c_i
       =6(a_j+b)/b+2(N-2)-2b/A_i.

Indeed tr(H^-1 H_jj) contributes 2b/(3A_i)+6(a_j+b)/b+2(N-2), while tr(H^-1 H_j H^-1 H_j) contributes8b/(3A_i). Also

    partial_j(adj H)_ji(e_i)=-c_i/(6A_i),
    partial_i(R_I^k h_I)(e_i)=2(N+k)c_i,
    partial_jj(R_I^k h_I)(e_i)=partial_jj h_I(e_i)+2kc_i.

Substituting these identities, without assuming equal a_i, yields

    [partial_j F_j(e_i)-F_i(e_i)]/(c_i^2/2)
       =3a_j/b^2+(N+k+1)a_i/[b(a_i+b)]
       =beta_ij.                                  (3)

All mixed transverse linear derivatives vanish by independent sign flips. Under(1), the projective field -F_(I,k) has a strictly negative diagonal linearization at e_i. It therefore has an open local basin converging to this axis. No numerical basin, rate or condition number is needed by the algorithm. For b>0 and a_i>=0 with at most one zero, (3) is positive for every active pair, including all principal restrictions. The dependence N+k=n is essential: it keeps the same beta promise throughout induction.

## 4. Nonzero extactic by induction, without restricting rational poles

Let D_(I,k)=-F_(I,k) dot grad. For N=1 the coordinate extactic is y_i!=0. Suppose the theorem holds for every active set of size N-1 at k+1. Fix j in I. The polynomial minor B formed from coordinate columns i!=j and rows y,Dy,...,D^(N-2)y restricts on y_j=0, by(2), to the lower extactic multiplied by

    [(4b)^2R_(I\{j})]^[(N-1)(N-2)/2].

For a rescaled derivation eta D, (eta D)^r=eta^rD^r plus lower-order rows; this gives the displayed factor exactly. Thus B is a nonzero polynomial.

If the full N-coordinate extactic were zero, solve its first N-1 rows over the rational-function field using B. There are rational functions c_i with

    D^r y_j=sum_(i!=j)c_i D^r y_i,  r=0,...,N-2.

The zero full determinant supplies the same relation at r=N-1. Differentiation and subtraction, followed by invertibility of B, gives D c_i=0. The coefficients are homogeneous of degree zero because every coordinate column has the same row degrees; hence they are also constant under projective normalization.

Choose a generic initial point in the open attracting basin of e_j where B!=0. The rational c_i are finite there and constant locally along the projective trajectory. Its fixed-coefficient linear identity

    y_j=sum_(i!=j)c_i(initial)y_i

extends for all finite normalized flow time by analytic continuation. This avoids restricting a c_i to a coordinate face or following it across a pole. Divide by y_j on the attracting chart and let time tend to infinity. Every y_i/y_j tends to zero, producing1=0. Contradiction. Thus the full extactic is nonzero, and induction reaches I={1,...,n},k=0.

Changing -F back to the implemented F changes the determinant by the nonzero constant(-1)^[N(N-1)/2]. Tied attraction rates cause no difficulty: the contradiction uses only convergence of every transverse ratio.
