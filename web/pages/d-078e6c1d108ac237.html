## Conditional transfer obtained

Fix an irreducible primitive positive-leading polynomial f of degree 4≤d≤8 and put k=d−2. Assume the source's already-audited modules at their stated interfaces: Theorem 3.1's affine count; Lemmas 4.1–4.2 and Propositions 5.2, 6.1–6.3 for balanced factorization, paired boxes, determinant cuts and (in quartic mode Q) fiber deletion; Propositions 7.1–8.2 and Lemma/Proposition 9.1–9.2 for the coefficient-uniform Hilbert and curve alternatives; and the exact parameter certificate in §10. Then the §11 composition gives a uniform positive power saving for

    #{n : X<n≤2X, p^k | f(n) for some prime p>X}.

More explicitly, using the printed coarse margins, choose the Theorem 3.1 loss ε=10^−5, absorb the O(log X) prime bins by another X^(10^−5), and retain the bound O_f(X^(1−1/20000)) for sufficiently large X. The quartic global exceptional values O_f(X^(1/5)) are charged once and fit this bound. Proposition 2.1 then transfers this tail to the exact Euler-product density for f, retaining its original content-prime factors. This is conditional capital from the source modules; it is not a claim that this programme independently proved those modules or externally validated the theorem.

The geometry doing the highest-value work is the quartic surface alternative, Proposition 8.1. Under its stated arithmetic-box hypotheses it raises the dimension-two Hilbert coefficient to

    min{(2U+V)D0/(U^2 V^2), 3/U^2},   D0=min(3U,2V,U+V),

which is exactly the special δ2 threshold used in quartic mode Q. Once this is transferred to every dimension-two descendant, the global counting tree receives a δ2 strong enough to close the quartic number-field parameter cells. The proof depends on the degree-at-least-three/quadric/plane split; in the plane case, failure for all independent pairs leads either to four critical points for a degree-at-most-four polynomial or to a degree-two map with four distinct ramification directions. This remains a conditional high-ceiling geometric transfer, not a new independent proof of Proposition 8.1.

## Integrated descendant interface

For each number-field box group, the ambient integer variables are (x′,y′)∈Z^(2d), with block weights U,V and coordinate sizes O_f(X^U), O_f(X^V). Integral unimodular transformations preserve the block filtrations. The initial algebraic sets have bounded degree and dimension ≤d; the dimension-d sets are exactly the mixed components from the first cut. The underlying arithmetic variety is Q-defined in rational coordinates after the fixed affine slice L0=1, while individual determinant components need not be. Selected points all have a_i≠0. Hence any C-irreducible, Q-defined descendant meeting the selected set meets the open arithmetic variety and is eligible for the §7–9 arguments. When an initial determinant component is not Q-defined, Theorem 3.1's patch descent intersects it with Galois conjugates; the descendants used by its hypotheses are C-irreducible and Q-defined.

In every Hilbert row the source coefficient c_h is δ_h^(−h), so Theorem 3.1's requirement H_A(T)≥T^h/(h!δ_h^h)−C_eT^(h−1) has the right direction. The fixed-degree graph/projection bounds control the degrees of F1, curves and all descendants; coefficient heights may vary, but the weighted Hilbert coefficients and errors are claimed uniformly in those heights. Lemma 9.1 is coefficient-independent: interpolation at a+b+1 integer pairs gives a polynomial-size resultant, whose divisors bound all integral values of the moving nonpolynomial rational function. All weights belong to a finite set (interval index and lattice-group index are bounded), inside the compact ratio range required by Proposition 9.2 and the common-shift estimate.

The quartic exceptional set from Proposition 8.2 is removed globally before boxes, curves or prime intervals. It costs O_f(X^(1/5)) once. Lemma 6.3 is a different deletion: per initial box its exceptional a-base has dimension ≤2 and bounded degree, hence O_f(X^(2U)) selected points. After lattice grouping, its total exponent in quartic mode Q is

    m−4j+2U = m+2u−2j+2τ < 3122/3125 < 1.

No later descendant is charged for either deleted set.

## Resource/exponent join

For a number-field group j, Proposition 6.2 gives O_f(X^(m−dj)) box pairs and weights U=u+j+τ, V=v+j+τ. Theorem 3.1 and the common-shift estimate yield

    m−dj + Σ_(h=1)^d E_h(U,V)
      ≤ m + Σ_(h=1)^d E_h(u,v) + dτ
      < 999/1000 + 8/50000
      = 24979/25000 = 0.99916.

With ε=10^−5 this branch is below exponent 0.99917 per prime bin. The direct surface range uses Z={f(x)=yz^k}⊂A^3_Q with weights (1,b′,η′); its surface Hilbert coefficient gives δ2=√(b′η′/D)<0.4999 and every curve has either δ1=1/2 or O(X^α) selected points. Thus that branch is below 0.99991 per bin after the same ε. The quartic fiber cost is <0.99904 per bin. There are O_f(log X) dyadic prime bins because p≪_f X^(d/k); absorbing them by X^(10^−5) leaves exponent at most 0.99992. The one global O(X^(1/5)) exceptional set is smaller. Therefore the coarse common choice 1−δ=0.99995, i.e. δ=1/20000, is safe under the listed source-module assumptions.

The parameter cells cover all η=log(P)/log(X): φ_d(η)=η(1−kη/d) is nonincreasing on η≥1; the exact stopping index places all later cells in the direct range, and the quartic Q/ordinary switch meets at η=4/3. The endpoint overhang η=d/k+O_f(1/log X) is covered by the separate finite positive weight pair in §10.1. No prime or parameter gap was found.

For the sieve transfer, primes Y<p≤X contribute at most dX Σ_(m>Y)m^(−k)+dπ(X); divided by X this is O(Y^(1−k))+o(1), which vanishes as Y→∞ after X→∞. For p>X, the tail for f equals that for its primitive positive-leading part once X exceeds the prime divisors of the content. At p^e||content with e<k, ρ_f(p^k)=p^eρ_g(p^(k−e)); elsewhere the root counts agree. This gives the exact original Euler factors, and the local-admissibility condition plus Σ_p O_f(p^(−k))<∞ makes their product positive. The fixed-Y Chinese-remainder count, then the two limits, prove density on (X,2X]; dyadic input intervals extend it to [1,N].
