THEOREM

Let d>=1 and let sigma>0 with Tr sigma=1. Let Phi:M_d->M_d be CPTP with
Phi(sigma)=sigma, and suppose one CPTP B:M_d->M_d tensor M_d has both
channel marginals Phi. Write H=Phi^*. Assume H is self-adjoint for
 <A,C>_sigma=Tr sigma^(1/2) A^* sigma^(1/2) C.
Then there is ONE finite pure ensemble sigma=sum_z p_z pi_z for which
 Psi(X)=sum_z p_z pi_z Tr[sigma^(-1/2) pi_z sigma^(-1/2) X]
is CPTP and entanglement breaking, preserves sigma, and K=Psi^* is a
positive self-adjoint contraction in KMS geometry satisfying
 K >=(2H^2-I)_+                                             (W1)
on the ENTIRE real Hermitian KMS space. Consequently
 I-K<=2(I-H^2)<=4(I-H).                                    (W2)
The ensemble and comparator are independent of the observable/frame. The
comparison has no dimension factor or minimum-eigenvalue factor. Existence
does not assert efficient acquisition of the exact finite ensemble.

0. REAL HILBERT SPACE AND STATIONARITY

All operator inequalities in the theorem refer to self-adjoint operators
on the real Hermitian Hilbert space with the KMS inner product above,
not to the CP/Choi order. Let H_0={A=A^*:Tr sigma A=0}. The identity has
KMS norm one and is orthogonal to H_0. Since Phi is TP, H is unital;
since Phi preserves sigma, Tr sigma H(A)=Tr sigma A. Thus H fixes I and
preserves H_0. Its eigenvalues are real by the assumed self-adjointness,
and lie in [-1,1] by the contraction lemma in Section 1.

Replace B by its average with the output-swapped B. This retains complete
positivity, trace preservation, and both equal marginals. It makes the
two-register stationary output S=B(sigma) swap symmetric. This is a legal
symmetrization of a supplied broadcaster, not an assumption of independent
outputs or a covariance property of Phi. If d=1 the theorem is immediate;
below d>=2.

1. KMS CONTRACTION FROM TWO WEIGHTED KADISON INEQUALITIES

This lemma applies to ANY unital CP T with Tr sigma T(A)=Tr sigma A,
without any self-adjointness assumption on T.

On the ordinary complex Hilbert-Schmidt space M_d write L(A)=sigma A,
R(A)=A sigma, and G(A)=sigma^(1/2) A sigma^(1/2). L,R are positive
definite commuting operators, G=(LR)^(1/2), and the KMS quadratic form is
<A,G A>_HS. Write T^dagger for the ordinary HS adjoint in this section.
Kadison's CP Schwarz inequalities T(A)^*T(A)<=T(A^*A) and
T(A)T(A)^*<=T(AA^*) follow by applying T to the positive 2x2 block and
taking the Schur complement of its unital bottom-right entry. Stationarity
then gives
 T^dagger R T<=R,   T^dagger L T<=L.                         (W3)
The positive operator block [[L,G],[G,R]] is positive semidefinite because
L,R commute and G^2=LR. Congruence by diag(T,T), followed by increasing
the diagonal blocks using (W3), gives
 [[L,M],[M,R]]>=0,    M=T^dagger G T>=0.
The Schur complement of L>0 gives R>=M L^(-1) M. Put
 X=L^(-1/2)M L^(-1/2)>=0, D=L^(-1/2)R L^(-1/2)>0.
Then X^2<=D. Monotonicity of the positive square root implies
 X<=D^(1/2), hence M<=L^(1/2)D^(1/2)L^(1/2)=G,
where the last equality uses commuting L,R. This proves
 ||T(A)||_KMS<=||A||_KMS.                                  (W4)

For completeness, square-root monotonicity used here follows directly from
 sqrt(C)=(1/pi)integral_0^infinity t^(-1/2) C(tI+C)^(-1) dt.
The scalar identity is obtained by t=x u^2 and elementary integration,
and the matrix identity follows by spectral decomposition. If C<=D, then
(tI+C)^(-1)>=(tI+D)^(-1); thus C(tI+C)^(-1)<=D(tI+D)^(-1), proving
monotonicity after integration. No interpolation or data-processing
theorem is being imported as a premise.

2. OUTPUT CORRELATION IS A KMS CONTRACTION

Let S>=0 be any bipartite density matrix with BOTH marginals sigma. Define
 gamma(A,C)=Tr S(A tensor C) and the Hermitian-preserving map
 Gamma(C)=sigma^(-1/2) Tr_2[S(I tensor C)] sigma^(-1/2).
Then <A,Gamma C>_sigma=gamma(A,C). For the symmetric S=B(sigma) used here,
gamma is symmetric on Hermitians, so Gamma is KMS self-adjoint. Moreover
Gamma(I)=I and Tr sigma Gamma(C)=Tr sigma C.

Choose a physical orthonormal basis diagonalizing sigma and let theta be
matrix transpose in that basis. Gamma composed with theta is CP: the map
C -> Tr_2[S(I tensor C^T)] has Choi matrix equal to the register swap of
S, which is positive, and the sigma^(-1/2) sandwich is CP. It is unital
and sigma stationary, since theta fixes sigma and preserves its trace
functional. Section 1 therefore makes Gamma theta a KMS contraction.
Transpose theta is itself a KMS isometry: in the chosen basis the norm
is sum_{i,j} sqrt(sigma_i sigma_j)|A_ij|^2, which is unchanged by transpose.
Thus Gamma=(Gamma theta)theta is a KMS contraction. On the real Hermitian
space its self-adjointness gives the decisive dimension-free bound
 -I<=Gamma<=I.                                             (W5)
Neither CP of Gamma itself, modular covariance, nor commutation of Gamma
with H is needed. This lemma concerns KMS geometry; a Jordan Cauchy-Schwarz
bound alone would be insufficient for the argument below.

3. LEGAL TREE AND EXACT WEIGHTED SECOND MOMENTS

Set T_0=identity and T_n=(T_(n-1) tensor T_(n-1))B for n>=1. This CPTP
tree has N=2^n leaves with marginal Phi^n. Under input sigma, every leaf
is sigma. Its Heisenberg marginal is H^n. Define the leaf average
 M_n(A)=(1/N)sum_ell A^(ell).
Let Q_n be the real KMS-coordinate operator for the Jordan second moment
of M_n under T_n(sigma). Its depth-zero value Q_0=J is the Jordan metric
 J(A,C)=(1/2)Tr sigma(AC+CA),
relative to KMS coordinates. In particular Q_0 is NOT generally I.
Conditioning on the two child subtrees and keeping their correlation gives
 Q_n=(1/2)Q_(n-1)+(1/2)H^(n-1)Gamma H^(n-1),
so the exact matrix identity is
 Q_n=2^(-n)[J+sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^k H^k Gamma H^k].            (W6)
Here the transfer through both H factors uses KMS-self-adjointness of H.
Gamma and H need not commute.

Let P be the spectral projection in H_0 onto |lambda|>1/sqrt(2), with
rank m. If m=0, any spectral pure ensemble of sigma gives an admissible
positive K whose traceless target in (W1) is zero, proving the theorem.
Otherwise choose KMS-orthonormal Hermitian eigenoperators E_1,...,E_m,
H(E_a)=lambda_a E_a, and write F=diag(lambda_a). Complete them to a
KMS-orthonormal basis E_1,...,E_D of H_0, where D=d^2-1. Define
 q_a=Tr sigma E_a^2, D_q=diag(q_1,...,q_m), and L_f=2F^2-I_m>0.
Each q_a is finite and >=1, but it is NOT presumed uniformly bounded as
sigma varies. Spectral rescaling by signed lambda_a^(-n) is legitimate.

On each leaf choose a label a independently and uniformly from 1,...,m,
measure the spectral PVM of lambda_a^(-n)E_a, and record its eigenvalue
t_ell. This is a single physical product POVM with label selection included.
Define the classical vector
 Z_a=(m/N)sum_ell 1_{a_ell=a} t_ell.
Its first output moment is M_n(lambda_a^(-n)E_a), and its input first
moment after pulling back through T_n is EXACTLY E_a.

Under stationary input sigma let K_n=E[ZZ^T]. Different-leaf label choices
are independent, so their moments equal the quantum leaf-average moments.
On a single leaf, mutually exclusive labels instead contribute
m delta_ab q_a lambda_a^(-2n). The unrandomized same-leaf Jordan block is
F^(-n)J_fast F^(-n). Thus the same-leaf correction cancels precisely the
J term in (W6), leaving the exact identity
 K_n=m D_q(2F^2)^(-n)
          +sum_{r=1}^{n}2^(-r)F^(-r)Gamma_fast F^(-r).       (W7)
The cancellation is matrix-valued: Jordan off-diagonal terms are not
silently treated as KMS orthogonality. By (W5) and congruence,
 K_n<=R_n:=m D_q(2F^2)^(-n)+sum_{r=1}^{n}(2F^2)^(-r).       (W8)
On the fast space the finite same-leaf term vanishes, and
 R_n -> (2F^2-I)^(-1)=L_f^(-1).                            (W9)
No minimum-eigenvalue factor remains in this limit.

4. WEIGHTED POSTERIORS AND FULL INPUT GRAM

Pull this complete output POVM back to input effects M_y>=0, sum M_y=I.
Set p_y=Tr sigma M_y and
 rho_y=sigma^(1/2)M_y sigma^(1/2)/p_y,
omitting zero-probability effects. These are density matrices and
 sum_y p_y rho_y=sigma.                                    (W10)
For x_alpha(y)=Tr rho_y E_alpha and z(y)=Z(y), the means vanish. The
exact cross moment is
 sum_y p_y x_alpha(y)z_a(y)
  =Tr sigma^(1/2)[sum_y z_a(y)M_y]sigma^(1/2)E_alpha
  =<E_alpha,E_a>_sigma=delta_(alpha,a).                    (W11)
This is the place where replacing rho_y by M_y/Tr M_y would be wrong.

Let C_n=sum_y p_y x(y)x(y)^T and let U:R^m->R^D be the fast-basis inclusion.
The full classical Gram matrix gives
 [[C_n,U],[U^T,K_n]]>=0.                                   (W12)
Also K_n>=I. To see this, for any v set
G=sum_y(v dot z(y))M_y=sum_a v_a E_a. Expanding the positive sum
sum_y[((v dot z(y))I-G)M_y((v dot z(y))I-G)] gives
sum_y(v dot z(y))^2 M_y>=G^2. Taking Tr sigma yields
v^T K_n v>=Tr sigma G^2>=||G||_KMS^2=||v||^2.
The middle inequality is exact: for Hermitian G,
 Tr sigma G^2-||G||_KMS^2
  =(1/2)Tr [sigma^(1/2),G]^*[sigma^(1/2),G]>=0.
The Schur complement in (W12) and inverse order in (W8) therefore give
 C_n>=U K_n^(-1)U^T>=U R_n^(-1)U^T.                       (W13)
This holds on the ENTIRE H_0, with all slow/fast cross terms retained.
It is not merely a compressed inequality, and no pinching or physical
projection onto the fast spectral subspace is assumed.

5. PURE REFINEMENT, PHYSICAL CANONICAL MAPS, AND COMPACT LIMIT

Refine each weighted posterior rho_y spectrally as
rho_y=sum_l r_(l|y) pi_(yl). Use pure weights p_(yl)=p_y r_(l|y).
Their barycenter remains sigma exactly. Classical conditional Jensen gives,
for every Hermitian A,
 sum_{y,l}p_(yl)Tr(pi_(yl)A)^2>=sum_y p_y Tr(rho_y A)^2.
Form the ACTUAL pure sigma-Petz canonical map Psi_n from this ensemble.
For K_n^can=Psi_n^*, direct cancellation of sigma^(1/2) and sigma^(-1/2)
in the KMS inner product gives
 <A,K_n^can C>_sigma=sum_{y,l}p_(yl)Tr(pi_(yl)A)Tr(pi_(yl)C).
Thus K_n^can is KMS self-adjoint and positive, and
 K_n^can|H_0>=C_n>=U R_n^(-1)U^T.                          (W14)
The symbol K_n^can denotes the channel, not the classical m-by-m moment
matrix K_n from (W7).

For any pure ensemble sigma=sum p pi, the displayed canonical map is
physical: if pi=|psi><psi|, its rank-one Kraus operators are
 V_z=sqrt(p_z)|psi_z><sigma^(-1/2)psi_z|.
Their squared sum is sigma^(-1/2)(sum p pi)sigma^(-1/2)=I. They produce
exactly Psi(X)=sum p pi Tr(sigma^(-1/2)pi sigma^(-1/2)X).
Moreover Psi(sigma)=sum p pi=sigma because Tr(pi)=1. Hence Psi is CPTP,
rank-one entanglement breaking, and sigma preserving; its unital adjoint
is a KMS contraction by Section 1. Its KMS positivity was already shown
by the Gram form, so 0<=K<=I. It fixes identity and has no identity/H_0
cross terms.

The body of all these KMS covariance operators with barycenter sigma is
compact. Indeed the continuous map on pure states
pi ->(x(pi),x(pi)x(pi)^T), with x_alpha(pi)=Tr(pi E_alpha), has compact
image in R^q, q=D+D(D+1)/2. Every convex combination reduces to <=q+1
points by eliminating affine dependences. This makes its convex hull a
continuous image of a compact simplex times <=q+1 copies of the compact
pure-state set. First moment zero is closed and equivalent to barycenter
sigma, since {I,E_1,...,E_D} is a basis of Hermitians. Thus the pure
canonical maps with exact barycenter sigma form a compact set.

Take a convergent subsequence of K_n^can. Positive order is closed; using
(W9),(W14), its limit K satisfies
 K|H_0 >=U L_f U^T=(2H^2-I)_+|H_0.
All maps fix I, and the target on I is also 1, so the comparison holds on
the entire real Hermitian space, proving (W1). The compactness construction
retains a finite ensemble of at most
 1+(d^2-1)(d^2+2)/2
pure atoms. No observable-dependent comparator remains after this full
Gram and compactness step.

6. DIRICHLET CONSEQUENCE AND EXACT FIXED-SIGMA SUPPORT INTERFACE

Since positive part dominates the underlying self-adjoint operator,
K>=2H^2-I, so I-K<=2(I-H^2). The elementary identity
 4(I-H)-2(I-H^2)=2(I-H)^2>=0
proves (W2). For any finite Hermitian frame A_i define
 V=sum_i ||A_i||_KMS^2,
 s_H=sum_i <A_i,H A_i>_sigma,
 k_sigma=sup_{sum p pi=sigma} sum_z p_z sum_i Tr(pi_z A_i)^2,
where the ensemble is unrestricted, pure, and common to the whole frame.
Our ONE K contributes s_K<=k_sigma. Consequently
 V-k_sigma<=V-s_K<=4(V-s_H).
The stronger intermediate bound is V-s_K<=2 sum_i <A_i,(I-H^2)A_i>_sigma.
These formulas use KMS variance/Dirichlet geometry; ordinary
Tr sigma A_i^2 is not silently substituted for V.

7. ACQUISITION, FINITE COST, AND DECISIVE FALSIFIERS

Supplied: faithful finite sigma, exact CPTP Phi, one actual CPTP joint
broadcaster B with both marginals Phi, stationarity, and KMS detailed
balance. No selected-observable compatibility, modular covariance, output
independence, GNS self-adjointness, or tracial reference is substituted.

A depth-n tree uses 2^n-1 B calls and d^(2^n) output dimension. The local
randomized spectral PVM has <=m d labelled outcomes, so the complete joint
POVM has <=(m d)^(2^n) outcomes; posterior pure refinement adds <=d atoms
per joint outcome. Rescaled outcomes, physical shots, sigma diagonalization,
sigma inverses, channel/broadcaster acquisition, eigenoperators, exact
arithmetic, dense tensors, and compactness extraction are separate costs.

For a fixed admitted sigma and H, put
 g=min_fast(2lambda_a^2-1)>0, q_max=max_a Tr sigma E_a^2<infinity.
Then ||R_n-L_f^(-1)|| <=(m q_max+1/g)(1+g)^(-n).
Because K_n>=I, R_n>=I and L_f^(-1)>=I, the same bound controls
||R_n^(-1)-L_f||. Hence a finite pure comparator has domination within
epsilon once this bound is <=epsilon. q_max may grow as sigma becomes
ill-conditioned, and the required depth grows as g tends to zero. These
are finite-construction costs, not a minimum-eigenvalue comparison constant.
The exact limiting finite ensemble has no efficient extraction guarantee.

Decisive falsifiers are: failure of the two-weighted-block contraction
lemma; false CP of Gamma theta or lost sigma stationarity; replacing the
Jordan same-leaf block by I; an omitted randomized-label correction;
unweighted posterior states; a failed full-input cross Gram; loss of sigma
under pure refinement; or a noncompact finite ensemble limit. Every gate
is proved explicitly above. No numeric scan, SDP, external validation,
formal verification, or historical priority claim underlies this proof.
