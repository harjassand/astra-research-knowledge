# Primary-source audit: PI thermal qubits and central Werner sectors

Audit date: 2026-10-08. Scope is targeted source priority, not an exhaustive literature review. The Schur-sector construction below is an exact mathematical consequence of cited sources; I did not locate a peer-reviewed paper that states the combined operational sampler verbatim.

## 1. Collective thermal qubits: exact stationary-state interface

For $N$ qubits, Schur-Weyl/Clebsch-Gordan decomposition gives
\[
(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes N}=\bigoplus_j \mathcal M_j\otimes V_j,
\qquad m_j=\dim\mathcal M_j.
\]
Under the collective weak-coupling thermal master equation of Yadin, Morris, and Brandner, a permutation-invariant initial state has conserved sector weights $p_j$, identity multiplicity factors, and stationary spin-$j$ Gibbs states:
\[
\rho_\infty=\bigoplus_j p_j\,\frac{I_{\mathcal M_j}}{m_j}\otimes
\gamma_{j,\theta},\qquad
\gamma_{j,\theta}=\frac{e^{-\theta J_z}}{Z_j(\theta)},\quad
Z_j(\theta)=\sum_{m=-j}^j e^{-\theta m}.
\]
The source assumes non-interacting constituents, collective bath coupling, weak-coupling/Born-Markov/secular approximations, detailed balance, full-rank bath correlation matrices on nonzero Bohr modes, and simple roots. It does not assert finite-time convergence or a universal sector-weight law. For an initially global Gibbs state at inverse temperature $\theta_0$, it gives $p_j=m_j Z_j(\theta_0)/Z_{\theta_0}$; for a general PI initial state, the weights are inherited from that state.

Write $K=2j$, $b=N/2-j$, and $q=e^{-\theta}\in(0,1)$. Fix a seed with $b$ disjoint singlets and $K$ up spins,
\[
|\Xi_j\rangle=|S\rangle^{\otimes b}\otimes|0\rangle^{\otimes K},
\qquad |S\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
This is a highest-weight vector in the $j$-sector. Uniform $S_N$-twirling makes its multiplicity factor $I_{\mathcal M_j}/m_j$; a spin-$j$ coherent-state mixture then supplies $\gamma_{j,\theta}$. Explicitly, with
\[
|\psi(x,\phi)\rangle=\sqrt{x}|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sqrt{1-x}|1\rangle,
\]
the block state is exactly
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi,x,\phi}\left[
U_\pi\left((|S\rangle\langle S|)^{\otimes b}\otimes
(|\psi(x,\phi)\rangle\langle\psi(x,\phi)|)^{\otimes K}\right)U_\pi^\dagger
\right],
\]
where $\pi$ is uniform over $S_N$, $\phi$ is uniform on $[0,2\pi)$, and $x$ has density
\[
f_{K,q}(x)=\frac{(K+1)(1-q)}{q(1-q^{K+1})}
\left(1+\frac{1-q}{q}x\right)^{-K-2},\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
The coherent-state mixture has Dicke weights $(1-q)q^n/(1-q^{K+1})$, $n=0,\ldots,K$, exactly those of $e^{-\theta J_z}/Z_j$. Its CDF is
\[
F(x)=\frac{1-(1+(1-q)x/q)^{-K-1}}{1-q^{K+1}},
\]
so an operational sample uses $U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ and
\[
x=\frac{[1-U(1-q^{K+1})]^{-1/(K+1)}-1}{(1-q)/q}.
\]
At $q=1$, take the limiting uniform density; $q=0$ is the point-mass limit at $x=0$. The formula follows by integrating the spin-coherent resolution against this density; Sidles supplies a closed-form positive coherent-state (P)-representation for spin-$j$ Gibbs operators.

Thus every such stationary PI qubit state is 2-producible: each sample has only two-qubit singlet clusters and single-qubit factors. It is not thereby proved fully separable; if it is entangled, its depth is exactly 2, and it has no genuine $N$-partite entanglement for $N>2$. This sampler is a state decomposition/preparation procedure. It is not a sample of bath trajectories, a finite-time representation, or a convergence-rate statement. Without PI (identity multiplicity) input, the stationary multiplicity state may be nontrivial, and this uniform random-pair construction does not follow.

## 2. Source audit and priority boundary

- **Stationary block form (primary, directly applicable under stated model):** B. Yadin, B. Morris, K. Brandner, “Thermodynamics of Permutation-Invariant Quantum Many-Body Systems: A Group-Theoretical Framework,” *Phys. Rev. Research* 5, 033018 (2023), [arXiv:2206.12639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12639), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.033018). Sections II.3, III.1–III.2 give Schur-Weyl blocks, conserved weights, and Gibbs states within each irrep, with the conditions summarized above.
- **Positive coherent-state Gibbs representation (primary):** J. A. Sidles, “Positive P-Representations of the Thermal Operator from Quantum Control Theory” (2004), [arXiv:quant-ph/0401165](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0401165). Theorem 1 gives a positive spin-$j$ thermal $P$-sequence proportional to the reciprocal coherent-state $Q$-sequence. The $x,\phi$ density/CDF written above is the qubit-coordinate specialization/derivation used here; the exact full-$N$ random-pair sampler was not located as a quoted theorem.
- **Exact Young-column orbit construction (close direct match, workshop notes rather than a journal article):** H. Maassen, joint work with B. Kümmerer, “Entanglement of symmetric Werner states,” Lorentz Center workshop notes (2019), [slides/handout](https://www.bjadres.nl/MathQuantWorkshop/Slides/SymmWernerHandout.pdf), [workshop page](https://www.bjadres.nl/MathQuantInformation.html). The notes define symmetric Werner states by both particle-permutation and collective-unitary symmetry, form Young vectors as tensor products of antisymmetric column vectors, and state that the span of their joint $S_N\times SU(d)$ orbit is the corresponding minimal central projection. Each column vector is a single cluster of size its column height. Twirling the product seed therefore already implies the $\ell(\lambda)$-producible (hence $d$-producible) upper bound for a normalized central Schur projector. The notes do not, in the inspected material, formulate that consequence as an exact entanglement-depth theorem.
- **Valence-bond precedent:** A. Beach and A. Sandvik, “Some formal results for the valence bond basis,” [arXiv:cond-mat/0605103](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0605103), *Nucl. Phys. B* 750 (2006), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2006.05.032), establish the singlet-pair building blocks and their role in spin-zero sectors. This alone is not the thermal random-pair sampler.
- **Adjacent entanglement-in-Young-sector result:** R. Reuvers, “Lower bound on entanglement in subspaces defined by Young diagrams,” *J. Math. Phys.* 60, 012201 (2019), [arXiv:1805.00364](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00364), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5050904), gives particle-entanglement entropy bounds for vectors in a fixed Schur-Weyl sector. It does not state the projector's exact producibility depth.
- **Recent nearby priority:** A. Rico, D. Grinko, R. Krebs, L. H. Zaw, “Entanglement Structure and Matrix Inequalities from Isotypic Measurements,” *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 137, 100203 (published 2026-09-04), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/nvk2-h8d5). Its abstract reports complete entanglement-partition characterization for three- and four-partite unitary/permutation-symmetric states and witnesses, plus a family of larger-system multipartite witnesses. The inspected abstract does not state the all-$N,d$ normalized-Schur-projector depth formula.
- **Terminology caution:** D. W. Lyons and S. N. Walck, “Entanglement classes of symmetric Werner states,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2012/463610), treats $n$-qubit states invariant under collective $SU(2)$ and their symmetric subfamily. Do not conflate a general collective-unitary-invariant (“Werner”) state with one additionally invariant under $S_N$: only the latter is a central mixture of normalized Schur projectors to which the column-orbit $d$-producibility argument applies.

## 3. General (SU(d)) central Werner boundary

For $\mathcal H=(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes N}$, collective-$SU(d)$ invariance alone allows arbitrary multiplicity operators in the Schur blocks; adding full permutation invariance forces scalar multiplicity blocks. Thus a central Werner state has the form $\rho=\sum_{\lambda\vdash N,\,\ell(\lambda)\le d}p_\lambda P_\lambda/\operatorname{Tr}P_\lambda$.

For $\lambda\vdash N$ with $\ell(\lambda)\le d$, let $P_\lambda$ project onto the full Schur-Weyl isotypic component. The central state $P_\lambda/\operatorname{Tr}P_\lambda$ is the joint $SU(d)\times S_N$ twirl of the column-product seed
\[
|\Psi_\lambda\rangle=\bigotimes_{c}|e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{h_c}\rangle,
\qquad h_c=\lambda'_c\le d.
\]
Each seed factor occupies one column cluster; joint collective-unitary/permutation twirling preserves cluster-size bounds. Hence the literature construction yields $\ell(\lambda)$-producibility (in particular $d$-producibility) for each central projector, and mixtures over $\lambda$ are $d$-producible. Root's separate lower-bound argument for exact depth $\ell(\lambda)$ is not established by these sources. Nor does the upper bound transfer to arbitrary $SU(d)$-invariant states with nonidentity multiplicity operators; permutation centrality is essential. Mixtures can have strictly smaller depth (the mixture with weights $\operatorname{Tr}P_\lambda/d^N$ is $I/d^N$, fully separable), so no exact-depth conclusion for a general central mixture follows just by taking the largest row count.

## Bottom line for priority

The stationary-block and positive-$P$ ingredients are established. The random singlet-pairs plus coherent-rest expression is a short exact composition of those ingredients with the established Schur-Weyl/Young-column orbit construction; the explicit $x$-sampler above is independently derived. I found no source in this bounded search stating that full operational identity in one theorem. For central Werner projectors, Maassen–Kümmerer notes give a direct Young-column orbit construction that already implies the producibility upper bound; exact depth $\ell(\lambda)$ appears to be a distinct lower-bound claim and was not located in sources inspected. These are source-status findings, not global novelty claims.
