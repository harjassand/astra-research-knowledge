ALL-MATRIX NEAR-FULL RANK CASIMIR PROFILE AND MEMORY PRECISION
2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; external validation and novelty UNKNOWN.
This supplement closes the near-full-rank order gate left in RESULT.txt.
It does not prove coherent-state extremality or an exact higher-m constant.

1. The complete order profile

Fix m>=2, put s=m-1, H_N=Sym^N(C^m), d=binom(N+s,s), and use the
generator normalization Tr_fund(t_a t_b)=delta_ab/2 and T_a=dGamma(t_a).
Let L(X)=sum_a[T_a,[T_a,X]], E(X)=<X,LX>_HS, and

  Lambda(Q)=min_{rank X<=Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X).

All matrices, including nonnormal ones and arbitrary kernel subspaces,
are allowed in this minimum. For fixed m, N>=max{s,8}, and 1<=Q<d,
the following profile holds with constants depending only on m:

  Q<=d/2:     Lambda(Q) = Theta_m(N Q^(-1/s));

  Q>d/2, k=d-Q:
    s=1:       Lambda(d-k) = Theta(1/log(e d/k));
    s>=2:      Lambda(d-k) = Theta_m((k/d)^((s-1)/s))
                           = Theta_m((N/d) k^((s-1)/s)).       (1.1)

At Q=d the minimum is zero. All logarithms in this supplement are natural.
The constants are uniform in the integer Q,k and N in the displayed range.
The two formulas agree in order at the midpoint. For finitely many smaller
N one may enlarge constants depending only on m; no asymptotic result
depends on omitting them.

The near-full lower bound is proved by a Green functional for an arbitrary
kernel projection and rank-sensitive spectral-projector overlaps. It needs
no assertion that the coherent kernel is optimal. The upper uses explicit
radial cluster potentials when the deficit is small, and the banked
occupation cap when the deficit is a moderate fraction of d.

For SU(2) the proof also gives the exact leading asymptotic

  Lambda_{N,2}(d-k) ~ 1/log(d/k)
       along EVERY sequence 1<=k=o(d), d=N+1.                 (1.2)

In particular Lambda_{N,2}(d-1)~1/log N. For m>=3 the conclusion here is
sharp in order, Lambda(d-1)=Theta_m(N/d); its exact global leading
constant and exact optimizing kernel remain UNKNOWN.

2. Arbitrary right-kernel Green inequality and positive upper filters

The positivity replacement in RESULT.txt Section 5 supplies positive upper
filters. It is not needed for the Green lower below. For the
singular-value decomposition A=sum_i sigma_i |u_i><v_i|, put
U^a_ij=<u_i,T_a u_j> and V^a_ij=<v_i,T_a v_j>. Scalar carrier Casimir gives

  E(A)-[E(sqrt(AA dagger))+E(sqrt(A dagger A))]/2
      =sum_{a,i,j}sigma_i sigma_j |U^a_ij-V^a_ij|^2 >=0.       (2.1)

The two moduli have the same rank and HS norm as A. Thus the minimum in
Lambda can be attained by positive F for the filter upper. No kernel is
restricted to an occupation subspace, a coherent state, or a special orbit.

For the lower, let A be ANY matrix with ||A||_HS=1, rank(A)<=d-k. Choose
ANY rank-k orthogonal projection P into its right kernel, so AP=0. Write

  a=Tr A/d (possibly complex),   X=A-aI,   Tr X=0,
  P0=P-(k/d)I,
  G(P)=<P0,L^+ P0>_HS,                                  (2.2)

where L^+ is the inverse on traceless matrices and zero on I. Since AP=0
and Tr(PA)=Tr(AP)=0,

  Tr(PX)=-ka.

Spectral Cauchy-Schwarz on the traceless subspace gives

  k^2 |a|^2=|<P0,X>|^2 <= G(P) E(A).                     (2.3)

The Casimir's first positive eigenvalue is m, so

  ||X||_HS^2<=E(A)/m,
  1=d |a|^2+||X||_HS^2
       <=E(A)[d G(P)/k^2+1/m].

Therefore the exact, orientation-free inequality is

  E(A)>=1/[d G(P)/k^2+1/m].                              (2.4)

This uses only a necessary scalar consequence of AP=0, not an assertion
that the full operator constraint is equivalent to that scalar constraint.
The lower applies directly to nonnormal matrices. More generally the same
argument works for any positive invariant HS operator L with ker L=span I
and a first gap gamma>0, replacing 1/m by 1/gamma. Scalar carrier Casimir
and positivity replacement are needed for the positive-filter upper in
the operational equivalence, not for this broader lower principle.

3. Universal Green control from rank-projector uncertainty

End(H_N)=direct-sum_{j=0}^N H_{j,j} is multiplicity-free. Its Casimir
eigenvalues, shell dimensions, and first gap are

  c_j=j(j+s),
  b_j=binom(j+s,s)^2-binom(j+s-1,s)^2,
  c_1=s+1=m,     b_0=1.                                 (3.1)

Let Pi_j be the orthogonal HS projection onto H_{j,j}. For any invariant
orthogonal projector Pi of dimension b, Schur's lemma gives
Tr_2 Pi=(b/d)I under vectorization. The vector vec(P)/sqrt(k) is unit norm
and has range projection P and Schmidt rank k. Hence

  ||Pi vec(P)||^2 <= k Tr[(P tensor I)Pi]=k^2 b/d.         (3.2)

This applies to EACH shell Pi_j, not merely a cumulative projector. It
follows from the operator inequality
|vec(P)><vec(P)| <= k(P tensor I); it does not assume an entrywise
localization or a preferred kernel orientation.

Put u_j=||Pi_j(P)||_HS^2. Then

  u_0=k^2/d,    sum_{j>=1}u_j=k-k^2/d<=k,
  u_j<=k^2 b_j/d,
  G(P)=sum_{j=1}^N u_j/c_j.                             (3.3)

For every integer 1<=K<=N, split the Green sum into its low and high
parts. If K<N the high part is bounded by k/c_{K+1}; if K=N it is zero:

  G(P)<= (k^2/d)sum_{j=1}^K b_j/c_j + k/c_{K+1}.        (3.4)

The high term is omitted at K=N. This is the load-bearing uncertainty
step: summing shell dimensions all the way to N would lose the result
in dimensions s>=2. The cutoff must balance the rank-mass bound against
the total HS mass k.

Set

  R=(d/k)^(1/(2s))>=1,       K=min{N,floor R}.            (3.5)

If K<N, then K+1>R, so c_{K+1}>R^2. If K=N, there is no tail. No
assumption R<=N is necessary. Every estimate below is valid for all
1<=k<d, including the finite cases N small relative to fixed s.

For s>=2 factor the shell dimension as

  b_j=binom(j+s-1,s-1)
            [binom(j+s,s)+binom(j+s-1,s)].

For j>=1, j+s-1<=sj and j+s<=(s+1)j. Thus

  b_j<=A_s j^(2s-1),
  A_s=2 s^(s-1)(s+1)^s/[(s-1)!s!].                     (3.6)

Since c_j>=j^2 and 2s-3>=1,
sum_{j=1}^K j^(2s-3)<=K^(2s-2)<=R^(2s-2). Inserting this and the tail
bound in (3.4) gives the uniform estimate

  G(P)<=(A_s+1) k^(1+1/s) d^(-1/s),     s>=2.           (3.7)

For s=1, b_j=2j+1 and

  b_j/c_j=1/j+1/(j+1).

Its sum is H_K+H_{K+1}-1<=2H_K<=2+2log K<=2+log(d/k).
The tail in (3.4) is at most k/R^2=k^2/d. Therefore

  G(P)<=(k^2/d)[3+log(d/k)],              s=1.           (3.8)

All constants were fixed before N,k. For k=1 these give O_m(1/N) when
s>=2 (because d^(1/s)=Theta_m(N)) and O(log N/N) when s=1. No coherent
maximizer of the Green function or heat return probability is used.

4. All-matrix rank-deficit lower bounds

For s>=2, substitute (3.7) into (2.4). Put p=(s-1)/s. Because d/k>=1,

  E(A)>=1/[(A_s+1)(d/k)^p+1/m]
        >=L_s(k/d)^p,
  L_s=1/[A_s+1+1/m].                                   (4.1)

For s=1, (3.8) gives the slightly sharper explicit bound

  E(A)>=1/[log(d/k)+7/2]
        >=2/[7 log(e d/k)].                            (4.2)

These bounds hold directly for Lambda(d-k) over ALL matrices via their
right kernels, not only for a diagonal or positive restricted infimum.
No modulus replacement or scalar-Casimir identity is required for this
lower step. Positivity replacement is retained for the matching filter
upper and operational construction.

The dimension comparison

  N^s/s!<=d<= (N+s)^s/s!

makes (4.1) equivalent to the requested lower of order
(N/d)k^p at fixed s. In particular

  Lambda(d-1)>= [L_s/(s!)^(1/s)] N/d,     s>=2.          (4.3)

This closes the arbitrary-kernel orientation gate in order. It does not
assert that the best projection is coherent or that (4.3)'s constant is
sharp.

5. Explicit radial upper for small deficits, s>=2

This is the same exact occupation graph as the spectral lead's independent
near_full/explicit_polytope_models.txt. Its radial layer index l=N-n_1 has

  g_l=binom(l+s-1,s-1),
  D_l=binom(l+s,s),
  C_l=s(N-l)D_l,              0<=l<N.                   (5.1)

For a radial diagonal F with entries f_l,

  E(F)=sum_{l=0}^{N-1} C_l(f_{l+1}-f_l)^2,
  ||F||_HS^2=sum_{l=0}^N g_l f_l^2.                    (5.2)

The layer edge formula follows by summing the root conductances
(N-l)(beta_i+1) over all off-axis occupations. It includes both endpoint
edges and uses the same generator normalization as (3.1).

Let N>=max{s,8}, and define

  beta_s=1/[s! 8^s].

When 1<=k<=beta_s N^s, choose

  r=ceil((s!k)^(1/s)),    B=2r.

Then r<=N/8+1<=N/4 and B<=N/2. The cluster l<r has D_{r-1}>=r^s/s!>=k
holes. A filter vanishing there consequently has rank at most d-k; it is
legal to have more than k holes because Q is a maximum rank budget.

Put f_l=0 for l<r, f_l=1 for l>=B, and for r<=l<B use the resistance
potential

  R_{r,B}=sum_{j=r-1}^{B-1}1/C_j,
  f_l=[sum_{j=r-1}^{l-1}1/C_j]/R_{r,B}.                 (5.3)

The increments across the intervening edges are (1/C_j)/R_{r,B}, giving
exact energy E(F)=1/R_{r,B}. There are at least r summands, and each
C_j<=sN(2r+s)^s/s!<=sN(s+2)^s r^s/s!, so

  R_{r,B}>=s! r^(1-s)/[sN(s+2)^s].

There are d-D_{B-1} states in the unit-valued region. For N>=s and
B<=N/2, each factor in D_{B-1}/d is at most
(N/2+s)/(N+s)<=3/4. Thus

  ||F||_HS^2>=d-D_{B-1}>=d/4.

With b_s=(s!)^(1/s)+1 and r<=b_s k^(1/s), this proves

  Ebar(F)<= [4s(s+2)^s b_s^(s-1)/s!]
                    (N/d)k^p
          <=U_small,s (k/d)^p,
  U_small,s=[4s(s+2)^s b_s^(s-1)/s!] (s!)^(1/s).        (5.4)

This is an explicit polynomial-description filter, and is positive. Its
function of the single occupation number interpolates to degree <=N;
the exact finite positive projective cubature in RESULT.txt Section 10
therefore implements its covariant channel with
[(N+1)(2N+1)]^s labels and maximum quantum branch dimension <=d-k.

6. Moderate deficits and matching s>=2 near-full profile

Suppose Q=d-k>d/2 and k>beta_s N^s. The banked positive occupation cap
of rank <=Q has

  Ebar(F_cap)<=[s(s+2)/(s!)^(1/s)] NQ^(-1/s)
               <=s(s+2)2^(1/s),                       (6.1)

using N/d^(1/s)<=(s!)^(1/s). Also, for N>=s,

  d<= (2N)^s/s!,
  k/d>beta_s s!/2^s=16^(-s),
  (k/d)^p>16^(-(s-1)).

Thus (6.1) is bounded by

  U_mod,s (k/d)^p,
  U_mod,s=s(s+2)2^(1/s)16^(s-1).                       (6.2)

Combining (4.1), (5.4), and (6.2) gives an explicit uniform near-full
two-sided bound for every N>=max{s,8}, Q>d/2:

  L_s(k/d)^p<=Lambda(d-k)<=U_s(k/d)^p,
  U_s=max{U_small,s,U_mod,s}.                           (6.3)

The constants are intentionally loose. The small-deficit set may be empty
at some finite N; (6.2) then handles the entire near-full regime.
No hole-placement theorem is assumed. A radial cluster is merely a
constant-factor comparator to the arbitrary-subspace lower.

7. Uniform spin near-full upper and exact dilute-deficit asymptotic

For s=1, d=N+1, the radial graph is the path l=0,...,N with conductances

  C_l=(N-l)(l+1).

For N>=8 and k<=N/8, put r=k, B=floor(N/2), and use (5.3). There are
exactly k zero layers and ||F||_HS^2>=d-B>=d/2. Also

  R_{k,B}>= [H_B-H_{k-1}]/N
          >=log((B+1)/k)/N>=log(N/(2k))/N.

For t=N/k>=8, log(t/2)>=[1+log(2t)]/4>=log(e d/k)/4. Consequently

  Ebar(F)<=8N/[d log(e d/k)]<=8/log(e d/k).              (7.1)

For k>N/8 and Q>d/2, d/k<=9. The cap bound is at most 6, so it is at
most 6(1+log9)/log(e d/k). With

  U_1=6(1+log9),

(4.2) and this construction prove

  2/[7 log(e d/k)]<=Lambda(d-k)<=U_1/log(e d/k).         (7.2)

For an exact leading asymptotic, consider any sequence k=o(d), k>=1.
Let T=N/k ->infinity, L=log T ->infinity, and
B=floor(N/L). Then B/N->0 and B/k->infinity. Use the same harmonic
potential from k-1 to B and leave f=1 beyond B. Its norm satisfies
d-B<=||F||_HS^2<=d, hence ||F||_HS^2~d. Partial fractions give the exact
resistance

  R_{k,B}=[H_B-H_{k-1}+H_{N-k+1}-H_{N-B}]/(N+1).

The first bracket is log(B/k)+O(1)=L-log L+O(1); the second is O(B/N)=
O(1/L). Thus R_{k,B}~L/N and

  Lambda(d-k)<=Ebar(F)=(1+o(1))/log(N/k).

The lower in (4.2) is (1-o(1))/log(d/k), and log(N/k)~log(d/k).
This proves (1.2). The o(1) holds along every stated sequence; no
uniform error estimate outside k=o(d) is asserted. The filter has rank
exactly d-k. Even the global leading asymptotic did not require choosing
or identifying the exact optimal kernel at finite N.

8. Completing the full rank profile and operational consequences

For Q<=d/2 the independent spectral theorem and the first positive gap
give the banked small-rank law, with kappa=1/2:

  c_low,m NQ^(-1/s)<=Lambda(Q)<=C_low,m NQ^(-1/s),
  C_low,m=s(s+2)/(s!)^(1/s),
  alpha_m=s!/[2(2s)^(2s)],
  c_low,m=min{(s!/2)^(1/s)/8, (m/2)alpha_m^(1/s)}.

Together with Sections 6-7, this proves (1.1) throughout 1<=Q<d. The
piecewise choice of explicit cap or harmonic radial filter gives a
constant-factor all-Q comparator, with finite cubature known explicitly.
Thus no acquired nonconvex optimizer is needed to achieve the full ORDER
profile. Efficient exact minimization and sharp finite-N constants are
still separate open questions. Precision-certified circuit acquisition
and gate-time optimality are not supplied by a polynomial-size description.

The exact single-carrier operational equivalence in RESULT.txt remains:

  [eta/(m+1)] Lambda(Q)/N^2<=e*_Q<=K_m,h1 Lambda(Q)/N^2,
  eta=h0 exp(-2h1)/[m(m+1)],
  K_m,h1=exp(h1)[m h1/(2s)+h1^2/(2m(m+1))].

Its contract is blind full-density recovery of
tau_h,v=exp[(h/N)dGamma(P_v)]/Z on the supplied H_N, with h in fixed
positive compact [h0,h1], maximum quantum branch dimension Q, unlimited
classical labels in the lower, no entanglement assistance, and output H_N.
The cap and harmonic-filter upper channels use one finite grid with at
most C=[(N+1)(2N+1)]^s positive labels. Quantum memory has dimension <=Q,
classical storage is <=ceil(log_2 C) bits, total direct-sum memory dimension
is <=QC. Code parameters do not depend on unknown h or v.

Accordingly the whole-state precision profile is, in the near-full regime,

  m>=3: e*_{d-k}=Theta_{m,h0,h1}(k^((s-1)/s)/[Nd]);
  m=2:  e*_{d-k}=Theta_{h0,h1}(1/[N^2 log(e d/k)]).      (8.1)

For k=1 these give

  m>=3: e*_{d-1}=Theta_{m,h0,h1}(1/[Nd])=Theta(N^(-m));
  m=2:  e*_{d-1}=Theta_{h0,h1}(1/[N^2 log N]).           (8.2)

The asymptotic coefficient one for the spin ENERGY profile does not
automatically make the operational trace-error coefficient one; the
score lower and full trace upper have different h-dependent constants.

By monotonicity in Q, an error smaller than a sufficiently small fixed
multiple of (8.2)'s scale forces Q=d. The identity code then has zero
error. Thus these are the precision thresholds below which every
non-full quantum branch dimension is inadequate, even with unlimited
classical information. This statement concerns a single supplied carrier;
no iid Schur-sector transport has been inferred.

For m>=3 and intermediate near-full errors, the permitted deficit has
scale k~(epsilon N d)^(s/(s-1)), within 1<=k<=d/2 and constant-factor
integer clipping. For m=2 the logarithmic formula gives exponential
dependence of the deficit on 1/(N^2 epsilon); only constant-factor bounds
on that logarithm follow from the operational comparison.

10. Falsifiers and runnable finite diagnostics

Decisive mathematical falsifiers: an arbitrary rank-k projector violating
(3.2), a positive rank-deficient F violating the exact mean/kernel bound
(2.4), an incorrect shell dimension/cutoff at (3.5), or a rank/norm or
boundary-edge failure in the resistance filter (5.3). Coherent-state
orientation optimality is not a premise and cannot falsify this proof
merely by failing.

verify_near_full.py checks small full matrix Casimir spectra and Green
functionals for noncoherent projections, the universal shell mass bounds,
the mean/kernel inequality, exact radial energy/rank/norm identities,
and the stated explicit near-full comparator bounds. It records
NEAR_FULL_CHECKS.json. Tests are FINITE-EVIDENCE only; the analytic
derivation above, not a numerical search for an optimizer, is the claim.
