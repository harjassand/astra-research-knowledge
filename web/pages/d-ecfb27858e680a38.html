STATUS

An internally derived, explicit counterexample to dimension-uniform archive
estimates based only on the Fisher covariance spectrum. For every finite d, two
one-observation experiments have analytic, full-support, uniformly strongly
log-concave densities and the same tangent Fisher matrix

    diag(m2 s_i^2),    s_i = 10^-3 exp(-i),    m2 = E[tanh(Z)^2], Z~N(0,1).

Their full Fisher metrics are uniformly equivalent to this same diagonal
matrix, with constants independent of d and the parameter. Yet at a common
precision epsilon, with d allowed to grow with epsilon, one family requires
Omega(log^2(1/epsilon)) archive bits even for arbitrary hybrid/quantum memory,
while a positive classical archive for the other uses only
0.5 log2(1/epsilon)+O(1) bits. The second family is a one-dimensional
observation whose normalized score vector has identity covariance but lies on
a one-dimensional analytic curve. Thus neither the Fisher spectrum nor the
joint score covariance determines the archive order without assumptions on
the joint score law.

This is a scoped counterexample to covariance-only, dimension-uniform
two-sided information estimates. It is not a new universal compression
theorem, a physical quantum-memory experiment, or a priority/novelty claim.
The proof and constants below are self-contained apart from standard
data-processing, quantum entropy, and differential-entropy inequalities.

INTERFACE AND COST BOUNDARY

For each finite d and each fixed, known model P_v (or C_v), the unknown
parameter v lies in the Euclidean unit ball of R^d. The encoder receives one
sample X and is blind to v. It retains a classical label or a hybrid state of
total dimension D (all input-dependent registers count); independent shared
randomness is allowed. A decoder must output a fresh Y with

    sup_{||v||_2<=1} TV(Law_v(Y), P_v) <= epsilon.

The lower bound grants the decoder a later coordinate query and an
arbitrary query-specific measurement, which can only help it. There is no
free input-dependent side record or entangled receiver. The classical upper
uses one label and fresh continuous randomness. Exact model coefficients,
normal CDF/inverse CDF evaluation, random-bit generation, finite-precision
simulation, and the cost of acquiring the declared model are outside this
memory-only interface. No finite-bit implementation is claimed.

THE PAIRED EXPERIMENTS

Write phi for the standard normal density, Phi for its CDF, g(x)=tanh(x),
and m2=integral g(x)^2 phi(x) dx. Then 0<m2<1. Set a=10^-3,
s_i=a exp(-i), and r_i=sqrt(m2) s_i.

Product experiment P (d-dimensional observation):

    p_v(x) = product_{i=1}^d phi(x_i) [1+s_i v_i g(x_i)].

Each factor integrates to one because g is odd. Since |s_i v_i g(x_i)|<=a,
the density is positive on all of R^d. It is real analytic. The Hessian of its
log-density is diagonal, with coordinate i at most

    -1 + a ||g''||_infinity/(1-a),

where ||g''||_infinity=4/(3 sqrt(3))<1. Hence P_v is uniformly strongly
log-concave, with log-Hessian at most -0.998 I (the displayed bound is
stronger).

Shared-scalar experiment C (one-dimensional observation):

    psi_i(x) = sqrt(2) cos(2 pi i Phi(x)),
    q_v(x) = phi(x) [1 + h_v(x)],
    h_v(x) = sum_{i=1}^d r_i v_i psi_i(x).

Under X~N(0,1), T=Phi(X) is uniform on (0,1), so E psi_i=0 and
E psi_i psi_j=delta_ij. Thus q_v integrates to one. Also

    |h_v(x)| <= sqrt(2) ||r||_2 ||v||_2
              <= a sqrt(m2/3) < a/sqrt(3),

using sum_{i>=1} exp(-2i)=1/(exp(2)-1)<1/6. In particular q_v is strictly
positive. Phi is entire, so each finite-dimensional q_v is real analytic.

For log-concavity, direct differentiation gives

    psi_i''(x) = sqrt(2)[-(2 pi i)^2 cos(2 pi i Phi(x)) phi(x)^2
                         + (2 pi i) x sin(2 pi i Phi(x)) phi(x)].

Using sup phi=(2 pi)^(-1/2), sup |x|phi(x)=(2 pi e)^(-1/2), and i>=1,
    ||psi_i''||_infinity <= 9 sqrt(2) i^2.
The identity
    sum_{i>=1} i^4 q^i = q(1+11q+11q^2+q^3)/(1-q)^5, q=exp(-2),
and exp(-2)<1/7 imply sum i^4 exp(-2i)<70/81<1. Cauchy-Schwarz therefore
gives

    ||h_v''||_infinity <= 9 sqrt(2) a sqrt(m2) < 0.012728.

Consequently
    (log q_v)'' = -1 + h_v''/(1+h_v) - (h_v'/(1+h_v))^2
               <= -1 + 9 sqrt(2) a/(1-a/sqrt(3)) < -0.987.
Thus C_v is also uniformly strongly log-concave, with a dimension-independent
constant. These estimates cover every v in the unit ball.

SAME FISHER COVARIANCE, INCLUDING GLOBAL COMPARABILITY

At v=0, the P score coordinates are s_i g(X_i); they are independent,
centered, and have variance m2 s_i^2. The C scores are r_i psi_i(X), whose
Gram matrix is diag(r_i^2)=diag(m2 s_i^2). Both tangent Fisher singular-value
sequences are exactly

    sigma_i = sqrt(m2) a exp(-i).

This is not merely a local coincidence. For P, the score in coordinate i is
s_i g(x_i)/(1+s_i v_i g(x_i)); cross terms vanish, and

    (m2 s_i^2)/(1+a) <= I^P_{ii}(v) <= (m2 s_i^2)/(1-a).

For C, the Fisher quadratic form in direction w is

    I^C_v(w,w) = integral phi(x) [sum_i r_i w_i psi_i(x)]^2/(1+h_v(x)) dx,

which lies between (1+a/sqrt(3))^-1 sum_i m2 s_i^2 w_i^2 and
(1-a/sqrt(3))^-1 times that same sum. Therefore both full Fisher metrics are
uniformly equivalent to the same covariance matrix, uniformly over d and v.

The normalized score-feature vector for P at the reference is
    (g(X_1)/sqrt(m2),...,g(X_d)/sqrt(m2));
its coordinates are independent and have covariance I. For C it is
    (psi_1(X),...,psi_d(X));
it also has covariance I, but is the image of one scalar X under a smooth
one-dimensional map. This joint-support collapse is exactly what the
covariance omits.

PRODUCT-FAMILY MEMORY LOWER BOUND

Fix i and compare v=+e_i with v=-e_i. Their densities relative to the common
reference mu= N(0,1)^d are exactly

    dP_{+e_i}/dmu = 1+s_i U_i,    dP_{-e_i}/dmu = 1-s_i U_i,
    U_i(x)=g(x_i).

The equal mixture is exactly mu, and the posterior sign probability is
(1+s_i U_i)/2. The bounded proper-score regret argument gives, for the actual
encoder/decoder coupling and decoded coordinate Y_i,

    E_mu[(g(X_i)-g(Y_i))^2] <= 4 epsilon/s_i.                 (1)

For completeness, one may take a convex Savage potential whose derivative is
clip((2p-1)/s_i,[-1,1]). Its loss lies in [0,1]; on the posterior interval
generated by U_i in [-1,1], its Bregman regret is exactly
s_i (U_i(X)-U_i(Y))^2/4. Uniform TV error at the two signs changes this
seed-independent bounded risk by at most epsilon, giving (1).

Under mu, the U_i are independent and have finite differential entropy

    h_g = h(Z) + E log(sech^2 Z),

which is finite because |log(sech^2 Z)|=O(|Z|). The entropy-distortion
inequality applied to (1), followed by data processing and the Holevo bound,
gives

    I(U_i; g(Y_i),S) >= (1/2) log(s_i/(C_g epsilon)),
    C_g = 8 pi e exp(-2 h_g),

whenever the right side is positive. Here S is any independent shared seed.
For any finite active index set I, independence of U_I and strong
subadditivity imply

    sum_{i in I} I(U_i;g(Y_i),S)
       <= I(U_I;Q | S) <= log D,

where Q is the retained hybrid memory. This permits a different decoder
measurement for each i; the query is chosen only after storage.

For d at least floor(L), where L=log(a/(C_g epsilon)), choose all i<L.
Then

    log_2 D >= (1/(2 ln 2)) sum_{i<L} ln(s_i/(C_g epsilon))
             = (ln(1/epsilon))^2/(4 ln 2) - O(ln(1/epsilon)).       (2)

The dimension is allowed to grow with precision, as in any dimension-uniform
claim. For each epsilon, d=ceil(L) is finite. The lower includes all
input-dependent classical records and arbitrary unassisted quantum/hybrid
encoders.

SHARED-SCALAR CLASSICAL MEMORY UPPER BOUND

Set T=Phi(X). Under C_v, T has density

    f_v(t)=1+sqrt(2) sum_{i=1}^d r_i v_i cos(2 pi i t),  0<t<1.

This is periodic, has integral one, and uniformly obeys

    ||f_v''||_infinity <= M2,
    M2 = sqrt(2)(2 pi)^2 (sum_i r_i^2 i^4)^(1/2)
       <= sqrt(2)(2 pi)^2 a.

For J>=5, let eta_j be the periodic triangular hat centered at j/J,
j=0,...,J-1. The encoder returns j with probability eta_j(T). Given j,
the decoder draws T' with density J eta_j(t') and outputs Y=Phi^-1(T').
The induced density operator on t is

    Kf(u)=J sum_j eta_j(u) integral_0^1 eta_j(t) f(t) dt.

Its kernel J sum_j eta_j(t)eta_j(u) is a positive Markov kernel fixing the
uniform law. Given u, it is a mixture of neighboring hats whose mean is u;
its displacement is at most 2/J. Taylor's theorem and the zero first moment
therefore yield

    ||Kf_v-f_v||_infinity <= 2 M2/J^2,
    TV(Kf_v,f_v) <= M2/J^2.

Taking J=max(5,ceil sqrt(M2/epsilon)) proves

    B_cl^C(epsilon) <= log_2 J
                      = (1/2)log_2(1/epsilon)+O(1),              (3)

uniformly in d and v. Since a classical archive is allowed, (3) also upper
bounds the optimal quantum/hybrid archive for C. The codec is positive,
unseeded, and exactly preserves the zero-parameter Gaussian reference.

CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS

For the same sequence sigma_i=sqrt(m2) a exp(-i), the usual spectral sum
sum_i [log_2(sigma_i/epsilon)]_+ has order log^2(1/epsilon), while (3) is
only order log(1/epsilon). Equation (2) shows that P also has at least the
log-squared order. Thus a two-sided dimension-uniform archive estimate that
uses only Fisher eigenvalues, or only the covariance of normalized score
features, cannot hold across these analytic strongly log-concave experiments.

The construction does not refute bounds that additionally assume independent
scores, Gaussian score laws, posterior conditional-entropy controls, or a
specific positive reconstruction operator. It does not show that covariance
is useless for the product family. The exact gap is a joint-law issue: under
the reference, one normalized score vector is independent across d
coordinates, while the other has one-dimensional support.
