## Partial quantitative framing (unverified against cell rows)

For gene `g`, donor `d`, training condition `c`, and restimulation time `t`, the measured cross-sectional marginal is `P(Y_g(t) | d,c,t)` for single-cell spot count `Y`. A candidate common-clock null would require a monotone `phi_d(t)` that maps untrained response distributions to trained distributions across time, with explicit gene-specific alternative models. A mean-level special case is `mu_T,g,d(t) = mu_U,g,d(phi_d(t))`. Two positive/negative endpoint contrasts alone do not identify a response trajectory, a same-cell path, or a common clock. The source's 2 h and 6 h summaries therefore motivate a test but do not establish speed-up, equal cumulative output, or a within-cell mechanism.

Required controls include donor-blocked train/untrained pairs, training and challenge identity, plate/date, measured cell density and morphology, an explicit responder-mixture model, and equal-data held-out donor/time evaluation. Because RNA-FISH is destructive/cross-sectional, no individual-cell transition or lineage is observed. A decisive capability would need held-out distribution prediction (not merely refitting endpoints), uncertainty by donor, acquisition/probe/cell costs, and comparisons to gene-specific and mixture baselines. No model fit, power estimate, or capability claim exists yet.
