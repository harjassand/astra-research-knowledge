# Cycle 3: direct bounded-trace RGO and the unchanged hidden likelihood

Status: independently reconstructed direct gradient-only FORS primitive **proved**, with a trace-leading rather than square-root-trace step restriction. The source's unchanged hidden-RGO dimension substitution is **false** when a known quadratic remains inside the estimator. A full residual hidden-RGO Proposition 4.8 is not proved here.

## Interface and provenance

Let V(x)=q||x||²/2+U(x), q>=0 known. U is C², convex, with 0<=H_U<=hI and tr H_U<=tau h uniformly. Take tau>=1 without loss when h>0 (if tau<1, replace h by tau h and tau by 1). Gradients are exact; Gaussian draws and real arithmetic are supplied. No Hessian, value, proximal, projector, or known residual subspace is supplied. The target at an explicit center y is R^V_{eta,y}(dx) proportional to exp[-V(x)-||x-y||²/(2eta)] dx. The known q can be subtracted from each supplied V-gradient. Query counts exclude vector arithmetic, storage and precision cost, as in the source model.

Source locations read: arXiv:2609.38710v1, Algorithm 3.2/Lemma 3.3, Algorithm 3.8 and identity (3.6), Proposition 4.8 and proof, Lemma 4.1, Appendix A.1 Lemma A.2. Source A.2 (the lemma is numbered A.2 in section A.1) cites prior FORS work for the stronger ambient-dimension condition eta[sqrt(d log(1/epsilon))+log(1/epsilon)]<=c with one exact proximal query. Our trace-leading construction below is a deduction using this already-existing correction mechanism, not an independently validated novelty claim. The cited prior theorem itself was not reread.

## 1. Unchanged Algorithm 3.8 fails the residual-dimension substitution

Take U=0, y=z=0, b=eta-2theta>0, beta=(1+bq)^-1, a=q beta. Then R^V_{b,u}=N(beta u,b beta I). Its retained control gradient is g=q sqrt(b beta) xi0. Under the center proposal, independently of g,

M=-2theta g+sqrt(theta)H, Delta=sqrt(theta)D,

where H,D are independent standard Gaussians. In the source's two auxiliary RGO draws, their summed gradient minus 2g is

-2(1+2a theta)g + 2a sqrt(theta)H + q sqrt(2b beta)xi,

with xi independent standard Gaussian. The uniform segment variable cancels exactly. Consequently

E W² = d {theta q² b beta [4(1+2a theta)²+2]+4a² theta²}. (1)

The residual Hessian trace is zero, and the source anchor/mean-score bounds are zero, but (1) grows linearly with d for fixed q,b,theta>0. This is an actual Algorithm 3.8 likelihood counterexample, not an unrelated Gaussian quadratic. Both control noise and auxiliary RGO quadratic noise contribute. Thus replacing d by tau while leaving the algorithm unchanged is invalid. It does not disprove a revised algorithm that integrates q analytically.

## 2. Exact quadratic removal preserves hidden-center access

k=(1+q eta)^-1, abar=k eta, ybar=k y. Completing the square gives R^V_{eta,y}=R^U_{abar,ybar}. A supplied draw N(y,theta I) scales to N(ybar,thetabar I), thetabar=k²theta. The anchor scales to zbar=kz.

Running the hidden algorithm for U uses bbar=abar-2thetabar>0. A requested explicit call R^U_{bbar,u} is exactly R^V_{bbar/(1-q bbar),u/(1-q bbar)}. Its variance is below eta since bbar<abar and x/(1-qx) is increasing. However, it differs from the original eta-2theta: recursive cost and all likelihood identities must be stated for the transformed parameters. The score assumption must concern the smoothed residual U, not the original V score. The unknown center itself is never evaluated by this transformation.

The source ratio premise theta<=(eta-2theta)/4 implies eta>=6theta. It therefore implies thetabar<=bbar/4, since 6k theta<=eta. Also bbar-b=(1-k)[-eta+2theta(1+k)]<=0; hence bbar<=b<=1. These checks preserve the source's variance hypotheses for the transformed residual, apart from whatever smoothness rescaling h requires. Anchor error becomes k epsilon0.

## 3. The load-bearing nonlinear quadratic lemma

Fix c, sigma>=0, s in [0,1], and let T(z)=sigma[grad U(c+s sigma z)-grad U(c)]. Put a_s=s sigma² h. T(0)=0, DT is PSD, ||DT||op<=a_s, tr DT<=tau a_s, and ||DT||F²<=tau a_s².

Co-coercivity of a convex h-smooth U, followed by Gaussian integration by parts, gives

E||T(G)||² <= a_s E[G·T(G)] = a_s E tr DT(G) <= tau a_s². (2)

This establishes the norm energy directly; T need not be odd. Smoothness permits Stein and Gaussian concentration despite no bound on third derivatives. For |t|a_s<=1/8, F_t(x)=x-tT(x) is a global C¹ diffeomorphism. Since y=x-tT(x), ||T(x)||<=||T(y)||/(1-|t|a_s), and

E exp[t G·T(G)]
 = E_y {exp[t²||T(x(y))||²/2]/det(I-t DT(x(y)))}.

The determinant is positive, with log det^-1 <= |t|tau a_s + t²tau a_s²/[2(1-|t|a_s)]. For R=||T(G)||, Gaussian Lipschitz concentration plus an auxiliary scalar Gaussian yields, whenever 2lambda a_s²<1,

E exp(lambda R²) <= (1-2lambda a_s²)^-1/2 exp[lambda (ER)²/(1-2lambda a_s²)].

Using (2), tau>=1, and lambda=t²/[2(1-|t|a_s)²], this proves

E exp[t G·T(G)] <= exp[|t|tau a_s+2t²tau a_s²]. (3)

Bernstein integration then gives ||G·T(G)||p <= C a_s[tau+sqrt(tau p)+p], p>=2. The absolute linear term is essential in this proof; (3) is not a centered sqrt(tau) theorem. At s=0, T=0 and the formulas hold by continuity.

## 4. Direct FORS with a finite approximate proximal center

For the residual target R^U_{a,v}, let c be any explicit point. Define e=grad U(c)+(c-v)/a and zeta=sqrt(a)||e||. Proposal Q is X=c+sqrt(a)G. In whitened coordinates define

H(G)=U(c+sqrt(a)G)-U(c)-sqrt(a)grad U(c)·G+sqrt(a)e·G.

The target relative to Q is exactly exp[-H(G)]/E_Q exp[-H]. Thus c need not be an exact prox point. We do not evaluate H; values occur only in the analysis.

For each proposal G, draw a fresh standard G', and independent S,T uniform on [0,1]. Form

D_s(g)=sqrt(a)[grad U(c+s sqrt(a)g)-grad U(c)]·g,
W=D_S(G')-D_T(G)+sqrt(a)e·(G'-G). (4)

Two fresh gradient queries implement (4), reusing grad U(c). The fundamental theorem of calculus gives E[W|G]=E_Q H-H(G). Hence this is an exactly unbiased likelihood estimator even with e!=0. The normalizer of w=E_Q H-H is >=1 by Jensen.

From (3) and Gaussian linear moments,

||W||p,Q <= C{a h[tau+sqrt(tau p)+p]+zeta sqrt(p)}. (5)

Let H0 denote H minus its linear term. Convexity gives H0>=0. Gaussian heat integration gives E H0<=a tau h/2. Jensen therefore yields E_Q exp[-H]>=exp[-a tau h/2]. The joint density ratio of the target/auxiliary law to the proposal/auxiliary law is bounded by exp[a tau h/2-sqrt(a)e·G]. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the precise tilted moment estimate

||W||p,target <= exp[a tau h/(2p)+zeta²/p] ||W||2p,Q. (6)

The reference draw G', and S,T, retain their proposal laws under this tilt. Equations (5),(6) are uniform in v,c,d except through the displayed proximal residual; they include the target tilt rather than only static proposal moments.

Choose B in (0,1], p>=2, and ensure a h(tau+p)+zeta sqrt(p)<=c B for a sufficiently small universal c. Then (5),(6) bound the sum of both joint Lp norms by B/2. Clipping W to [-B,B], the source clipping proof (also reconstructed directly by comparing normalized exponentials) gives

TV(target, FORS output) <= (1+e) B 2^-p. (7)

Here each individual norm can be bounded by B/2, so each tail expectation <=B 2^-p; source Lemma 4.1's sum-norm convention only changes c. Choosing p>=log2((1+e)/delta), plus p>=2, proves TV<=delta. A safe statement is p=C log(2/delta), a h(tau+p)<=c B, zeta<=c B/sqrt(p).

For any implemented proposal history, bounded clipping yields acceptance >=exp(-2B). Expected proposals <=exp(2B), likelihood estimators <=2B exp(2B), and **additional gradient queries <=4B exp(2B)**. This includes every rejected proposal. It does not include the finite center computation below. There are no RGO recursion calls or Hessian queries in this primitive.

## 5. Computing c by gradients alone

For F(x)=U(x)+||x-v||²/(2a), mu=1/a and L=h+1/a. Start x0=v and take x_{j+1}=x_j-[a/(1+a h)]grad F(x_j). Integration of the Hessian along each update shows

||grad F(x_{j+1})|| <= rho ||grad F(x_j)||, rho=a h/(1+a h).

Consequently, if r0=||grad U(v)||, then k=max(0,ceil(log(sqrt(a)r0/zeta)/log((1+a h)/(a h)))) updates suffice for sqrt(a)||e||<=zeta. Use the exact residual as a stopping test. At most k+1 gradient queries include the initial/final gradient and its reuse in (4). If h=0, U is affine and one gradient and one explicit update gives the exact prox. If r0=0, no update is needed. Taking zeta=cB/sqrt(p) only requires logarithmic dependence on p and the initial gradient norm; **there is no requirement to approximate the center to TV accuracy delta**, because (4) corrects its residual exactly.

The full expected gradient count is <=k+1+4B exp(2B). It is not uniformly O(1) in arbitrary center v: the acquisition term k retains its initial-gradient dependence. Exact gradients/arithmetic are the stated interface. Finite numerical gradient errors require an additional certified promise and are not covered by this theorem.

## 6. Scope, costs and comparisons

Apply the primitive to a=eta/(1+qeta), v=y/(1+qeta) for an explicit original center. Known Gaussian curvature is integrated exactly and never charged to tau. For U=0, the target is already an explicitly generated Gaussian; FORS is unnecessary. This is a competent retaining baseline.

The baseline step condition is a h[tau+log(1/delta)]<=c, weaker than the hoped-for a h[sqrt(tau log(1/delta))+log(1/delta)]<=c. Replacing tau by d therefore produces a weaker theorem than source Lemma A.2. This loss is not hidden as an improvement. An outer proximal sampler can use this dimension-free but slower primitive, with each invocation's local precision, center computation and rejected-call ledger charged. This note does not prove its iteration/mixing theorem or any residual cloud speedup.

The leading tau is real for this particular independent-time estimator: if U=h||x_{1:tau}||²/2 at its exact prox, with integer tau, then W=a h[S||G'_{1:tau}||²-T||G_{1:tau}||²] and E W²=a²h²[tau²/6+4tau/3]. A shared time S=T removes this term for quadratics, yielding 4a²h²tau/3. This suggests a sharper algorithm, but does not prove its nonlinear or tilted moment bound. Our fallback explicitly keeps the certified weaker implementation.

The unknown-center hidden RGO is not implemented by this direct primitive alone: its explicit-center requests can call the primitive, but its outer likelihood still requires a separate residual moment proof and supplied anchor/score bounds. The quadratic counterexample in section 1 is resolved by section 2's rewrite, but the general residual Proposition 4.8 is left open here. In particular, conditioning on Delta and substituting a trace bound in the source's scalar auxiliary-noise Lipschitz estimate is unjustified because the RGO centers depend on Delta.
