1. FAMILY 361 — ANGULAR EIGENLINE CYCLES WITH RADIAL REALIZATION

Source and exact claim

Revision-pinned paper (39 pp., front matter dated 2026-09-26):
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/A-Three-Dimensional-Counterexample-to-Integer-Degree-Harmonic-Dimension-Comparison-September-26-2026/paper.pdf

Theorem 1.1, pp. 1-2: for each v in (4/9,1) and c in (1,9v/4), there is a
threshold k0 such that for every integer k >= k0 there is a metric g_k on R^3,
Euclidean near the origin, with Ric(g_k) >= 0, AVR(g_k)=v, and
h_k(R^3,g_k) >= c(k+1)^2 > (k+1)^2. Here h_k is the dimension of real harmonic
functions with pointwise growth at most k. Corollary 1.2, p. 2, strengthens
this by allowing the metric to be arbitrarily close to Euclidean in global
bi-Lipschitz distance for any 1<c<9/4. The theorem's metric is selected after
k; it does not give one metric working at infinitely many degrees.

Proof mechanism inspected

* Proposition 3.1, pp. 15-18, constructs for each sufficiently large k a
  periodic path of near-round metrics on S^2 with fixed area form. A band of
  the first p=(M+1)^2 angular modes is continued through isolated, linearly
  split eigenvalue crossings. The returned eigenlines cycle through the band,
  and each line's mean cone frequency d(a^-2 lambda_i(s)) is strictly below k.
  The source notes the fixed-link comparison still holds at every limiting
  link; the gain comes from changing links along radius.
* Proposition 4.1, pp. 19-22, realizes the path as links H_z(log r) in
  g_z=dr^2+f(r)^2 H_z(log r). It supplies a complete smooth metric with a
  Euclidean core, Ricci >= 0, AVR=a^2, and controlled crossing perturbations.
* Proposition 5.2, pp. 27-29, constructs an invariant graph over the first p
  modes for the exact inward Dirichlet trace maps, contracting the infinite
  high-mode complement. Proposition 5.3, pp. 29-30, computes a nonzero signed
  first-order coefficient by which each crossing control changes a selected
  off-diagonal entry. Sections 6-7, pp. 30-37, choose all controls by a
  product-space fixed-point argument, obtain exact invariant mode traces, and
  prove their pointwise growth bound. The decisive dependency is finite p:
  Proposition 5.2 fixes p before moving the radial start J; the proof uses a
  positive minimum outer spectral gap tau_* for that fixed block.

Nearest unfinished target / recommendation

Ask for one fixed metric g (rather than a family g_k) with Ric(g)>=0,
AVR(g)=v in (4/9,1), and

    limsup_{k -> infinity} h_k(R^3,g)/(k+1)^2 > 1.

One may ask for the same Euclidean-core and near-flat conditions, but the
limsup statement is the core target. This is the sharp source-explicit gap:
pp. 1-2 and 37 say the metric is chosen after k and no common metric is
asserted. The load-bearing obligation is to combine increasingly large
eigenline cycles in one radial link path while retaining enough spectral
separation and preventing later blocks from destroying the already matched
harmonic modes. The fixed-p / tau_* step in Proposition 5.2 is the immediate
obstruction to simply taking a limit of the present proof. The current
construction already has uncountably many nonisometric tangent cones at
infinity (p. 2 and Section 4), which is consistent with the obstruction from
unique-cone results.

Prior-art boundaries checked

* Colding-Minicozzi proved finite dimensionality and dimension bounds of the
  optimal order in k: “Harmonic functions on manifolds,” Annals of Mathematics
  146 (1997), pp. 725-747, https://doi.org/10.2307/2952459; “Weyl type bounds
  for harmonic functions,” Inventiones Mathematicae 131 (1998), pp. 257-298,
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050204. These are the fixed-degree / growth
  order baseline, not an exact coefficient comparison.
* Donnelly's 2001 examples yield an excess at a real growth degree between 1
  and 2 in dimensions at least 5, not the integer-degree 3D claim here:
  https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792801000216.
* Huang, arXiv:2109.07534v1, Theorem 1.6 and its coefficient corollary,
  bounds dimensions using the link spectrum when the tangent cone is unique;
  in dimension 3 the asymptotic coefficient is AVR v. The 3D release paper
  explicitly cites this unique-cone restriction and builds metrics with many
  tangent cones: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07534v1.
* The release's own Lean scope file for family 361 describes an even-dimensional
  example (dimension 16, degree 50000), tied to the earlier paper, not the
  3D construction audited here:
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/361.md.

The single-metric strengthening is an explicit unresolved extension of this
paper's construction. I did not establish that it is globally open, novel, or
unobstructed by later work; do not claim priority on this scout alone.

2. FAMILY 247 — PHASE-BASED DETECTION OF THE STEINBERG CENTRAL KERNEL

Source and exact claim

Revision-pinned paper (25 pp., front matter dated 2026-10-05):
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/An-infinite-finitely-presented-residually-finite-2-group-October-5-2026/residually-finite-torsion.pdf

Theorem 1.1, pp. 1-2 and proof pp. 23-25: constructs an infinite, ordinarily
finitely presented, residually finite group in which every element has finite
2-power order (orders are unbounded). The paper takes a graded companion
algebra R=F_2 direct-sum I with finite-dimensional graded components and
nilpotence of every finite square matrix over I; the companion also provides
compatible start/end phases modulo arbitrarily large odd L for sufficiently
long nonzero word classes.

Proof mechanism inspected

* Proposition 6.2, pp. 17-18: if a central-kernel element z has a long-entry
  representative satisfying Lemma 6.1, evaluating the graded entries at the
  cyclic shift C_L yields W_z(C_L)=z^L. Phase labels split this evaluation into
  L coordinate offsets; the off-diagonal/strip lemmas make different offsets
  commute, and every offset represents the same central element z.
* Theorem 6.4, pp. 18-19: proves
  intersection_{d>=1} ker(St(R)->St(R/R_{>=d}))={1}. For z in the intersection,
  choose a sufficiently large odd phase L after cyclic-shift transfer kills
  z, then use a long-entry representative to get z^L=1. The earlier
  characteristic-2 calculation gives z^(2^a)=1. Coprimality of L and 2^a
  forces z=1. This is the paper's extra step beyond ordinary matrix detection:
  it separates elements in the stable Steinberg central kernel.
* Theorem 7.2, pp. 19-23, returns from the stable group to rank 12; Proposition
  8.1 and Theorem 1.1, pp. 23-25, turn finite degree quotients into finite
  separating groups and extract the finite-index 2-torsion subgroup.

Nearest target

Try an odd-prime analogue: build a finitely presented infinite residually
finite p-group whose every element has p-power order, for one fixed odd p.
The exact next gate is not merely replacing F_2 by F_p: one needs a finite-
presentation graded algebra with matrix-nil positive ideal and long-word
phase hierarchy, plus characteristic-p analogues of the opposite-root
commutator, stable central-kernel, and fixed-rank stability steps. This is a
concrete transfer target, but I did not verify that the odd-p case is a
literature-wide open problem; label it unresolved rather than established
novelty.

Prior-art boundaries checked

* Golod's 1964 construction gives infinite finitely generated residually
  finite p-groups via nil-algebra methods, for primes p:
  https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im2956. It does not provide the ordinary finite
  presentation added here.
* Ol'shanskii-Sapir construct finitely presented torsion-by-cyclic groups,
  which the release paper correctly distinguishes from periodic groups:
  https://numdam.org/articles/10.1007/s10240-002-0006-7/.
* The source invokes Krstic-McCool's finite-presentability theorem for
  Steinberg groups over finitely presented rings (J. Pure Appl. Algebra 141
  (1999), Theorem 3) and Suslin-Tulenbaev stability. These support the
  presentation/rank bridge, not the phase-detection argument:
  https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01091768 (Suslin-Tulenbaev English translation).
* The current Lean scope page for family 247 links only to the companion
  periodic-group paper and expressly excludes residual finiteness. Thus it is
  not evidence that Theorems 6.4 or 1.1 of this paper are formally verified:
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/247.md.

3. FAMILY 090 — ATOMIC GAUSSIAN MINORANTS AND DENSITY-ONLY FOURIER LP

Source and exact claim

Revision-pinned paper (31 pp., front matter dated 2026-09-26):
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/An-atomic-certificate-for-triangular-lattice-universal-optimality-September-26-2026/paper.pdf

Theorem 1.1, p. 1: the unit-covolume triangular lattice minimizes the lower
energy per particle of every smooth nonnegative completely monotone function
of squared distance, among all locally finite planar configurations of
centered-disk density one. It permits infinite energy and does not classify
the minimizers. Theorem 1.2, p. 2, supplies sharp radial Schwartz Gaussian
minorants f_alpha: f_alpha <= exp(-pi alpha |x|^2), Fourier transform >=0,
equality at every nonzero triangular-lattice point, and Fourier zeros at every
nonzero dual-lattice point.

Proof mechanism inspected

* Proposition 2.3, pp. 6-7, is the density-only LP step: for even Schwartz f
  with nonnegative Fourier transform and a locally finite density-one set C,
  the lower limit of the off-diagonal sum of f(x-y), per point, is at least
  f-hat(0)-f(0). It uses only centered-disk density through concentration of
  the exponential sum near frequency zero; pointwise minorants can replace f
  off the diagonal.
* Theorem 1.2 is built from two 20-by-20 interpolation blocks. Lemma 4.1,
  p. 18 and Appendix A pp. 28-31, supplies the finite interval-certified
  inequalities. Proposition 5.1, pp. 19-21, solves the full summable
  infinite correction; Proposition 6.4, pp. 25-28, proves the exact global
  signs between all interpolation nodes. The paper says the finite block uses
  modulo-36 nodes; the infinite correction and between-node proof remain
  necessary. Positive Laplace mixtures are applied to the energy inequalities,
  not directly to the auxiliary functions.

Nearest target

The paper explicitly leaves equality classification open. A strong next
problem is a quantitative stability theorem: for a fixed strictly completely
monotone potential, bound the density of local deviations from a triangular
pattern by the thermodynamic energy excess, with a formulation robust to
translations and sparse grain boundaries. The source's certificate has exact
contact and strict off-contact signs, but the density-only LP as stated gives
minimality without a rigidity estimate. This is a research direction, not a
claim that a specific stability theorem is known to be open or that the
certificate already implies it.

Prior-art boundaries checked

* Cohn-Kumar formulate the Euclidean universal-optimality program and
  Conjecture 9.4; their earlier linear-programming framework is the direct
  comparator: JAMS 20 (2007), pp. 99-148,
  https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-06-00546-7.
* Cohn-Elkies introduced the Fourier-bound framework for sphere packing:
  Annals of Mathematics 157 (2003), pp. 689-714,
  https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2003.157.689. Cohn et al. later proved the
  E8 and Leech universal-optimality cases using sharp Gaussian interpolation:
  Annals of Mathematics 196 (2022), pp. 983-1082,
  https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2022.196.3.3.
* The release paper itself distinguishes lattice-restricted theta/Epstein
  minimization and prior restricted periodic/local comparisons from its
  density-based, nonperiodic competitor class (pp. 2-3). Its Lean scope note
  says the formalization includes Gaussian minorants and the universal energy
  minimum, but this scout did not replay it:
  https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/090.md.

OTHER CANDIDATES READ, NOT SELECTED AS MECHANISMS

Family 377, Uniform Interior C^{1,alpha} Estimates for Infinity-Harmonic
Functions, is 46 pp. Theorem 1.1, p. 2, gives a dimension-dependent
nonexplicit alpha_d in (0,1/3]; the paper states no endpoint or boundary
regularity claim. The internal route includes an extremal-envelope contact
(Proposition 5.4) and a multiscale propagation (Proposition 7.1), but I did
not check enough of those proofs to rank a transfer target.

Family 362, Global Classical Solutions of the 3D Relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell
System, is 49 pp. Proposition 2.1 (signed momentum increments) is the stated
load-bearing estimate; Sections 3-8 build the retarded-force cancellation and
occupation estimates around it. I did not audit this proof chain, so it is
listed only as a consequential candidate, not as a mechanism or result.
