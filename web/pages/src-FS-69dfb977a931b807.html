Research outcome
No candidate in this investigation passed both the novelty test and the complete physical-capability test. 2. The strongest candidate—and its novelty failure
The proposed operation was:

A driven liquid transfers momentum to a physically separated liquid across a wall, allowing the receiving liquid to move against a load without mass exchange or macroscopic movement of the separating wall.

The attractive feature was not merely unusual flow. It was the possibility of transmitting mechanical power between isolated streams without an intervening conventional pump.

However, both obvious versions were already covered by prior work.

Hydroelectronic generation and its reciprocal, “quantum osmosis,” were analysed in 2024. That work already includes a transport matrix and load-dependent energy-conversion efficiency; simply proposing to turn liquid–electron coupling into a generator would not be new. 

More decisively, Coquinot and colleagues’ “Momentum tunnelling between nanoscale liquid flows,” published on 2 January 2025, covers both direct liquid–liquid coupling through a passive wall and transfer mediated by the wall’s internal excitations. The liquids remain separate; their fluctuations couple their motion. The work uses many-body theory and molecular simulations, rather than demonstrating a loaded technological device. 

Adding wall excitations therefore does not rescue novelty. It does, however, leave a meaningful physical question:

Can a real material route enough momentum into a useful load, rather than into other dissipative channels, to improve a complete technological task?

I investigated that question rather than renaming the existing operation.

Establish the response without assuming ideal isolation
The proposed platform is a supported thin wall between independently driven liquid channels. Its fabrication and sensitivity have not been qualified here.

Pressure-driven wall deflection could act as an ordinary diaphragm pump. Shared plumbing, evaporation, electrical effects and leakage could also mimic the desired response. Those contributions must be measured and bounded.

An atomic wall cannot simply be declared impermeable to every species: proton transport has been demonstrated through tested monolayer crystals, and hBN–water interfacial charging is itself experimentally supported. These are reasons to measure leakage and chemistry, not assume an inert separator. 

Randomised positive and negative pressure drives, role reversal, zero-drive controls and fixed electrical terminations would provide the initial transport matrix. Its uncertainty must be fitted jointly; independently optimistic error bars on coupling and losses would recreate the original accounting problem.

Establish task advantage against the right competitor
For bulk hydraulic-energy recovery, the strongest practical benchmark identified was existing isobaric pressure exchange. Energy Recovery’s March 2026 Q650 announcement claims peak efficiency up to 99% and volumetric mixing as low as 2%. Those are manufacturer figures, not independent verification in this investigation. 

That is not an equivalent competitor for a strictly isolated, nanoscale, stationary-wall operation. Conversely, nanoscale novelty does not justify ignoring mature pressure recovery when making a bulk-energy claim.

A successful implementation must specify a task where its isolation or integration advantage actually matters, then compare complete input, output, throughput, leakage and lifetime with a conventional implementation meeting that same task. A globally optimal microscopic comparator was not established here.

8. What should be retained—and what should not be promoted
The retained results are the fixed-task loss-replacement condition, the distinction between unloaded motion and useful power, the bidirectional acquisition of the efficiency parameter, and the reproducible screening calculation.

The unresolved physical opportunity is:

Redirect an existing interfacial momentum-loss channel into a useful receiver, using a real material whose cross-transfer dominates its added losses.

That has credible physical leverage, but the broad wall-mediated operation is already prior art. No material satisfying the required combined response was acquired in this investigation, and no experiment demonstrated net task advantage.

Accordingly, there is no justified civilisation-scale output projection and no established new physical power to promote from this run. The appropriate next scientific step for this particular lead is a material-specific response-and-work test—not another nominally novel device assembled from favourable coefficients.
