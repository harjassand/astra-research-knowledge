NO THREE-ACTIVE-MOVE EQUALITY FOR 4-SITE HARD-BCS ENDPOINT EXCHANGE
Worker c02_l09; 7 October 2026

For the hard-BCS hole coefficients on E={1,2,3,4}, consider the exchange
from S=E to T=empty (with any anchor a). Let

    g_{ij}=|F_{ij}|^2+|F_{ji}|^2,  i<j.

Then f(U)=g_{E\U} for every two-hole set U, and

    f(empty)=sum_{|I|=2}|det F[I,E\I]|^2.

There are three perfect matchings of E:
M1=12|34, M2=13|24, M3=14|23. Let D be the vector in C^6 whose
coordinates are the six complementary minors det F[I,E\I]. Expand each
2-by-2 determinant into its two signed products. Each product uses two
directed edges from one of M1,M2,M3. For r=1,2,3, collect the signed products
belonging to Mr into a vector v_r in C^6, with zero in coordinates where Mr
does not occur. Then D=v1+v2+v3. Each orientation of the two edges of Mr
occurs exactly once among the six determinant expansions, hence

    ||v_r||^2 = g_e g_e' = p_r^2,

where {e,e'}=Mr and p_r is the corresponding exchange term
sqrt(f(U)f(V)) for the complementary hole-pair matching. Thus the exchange
bound here is the vector triangle inequality

    sqrt(f(empty))=||D|| <= ||v1||+||v2||+||v3||=p1+p2+p3.

This also gives an equality obstruction. v1 is zero in the two coordinates
I=12,34; v2 is zero in I=13,24; and v3 is zero in I=14,23. These three
zero-coordinate pairs partition the six coordinates. If all p1,p2,p3 are
positive and equality held, equality in the Hilbert-space triangle
inequality would require v1,v2,v3 to be positive scalar multiples of one
common nonzero vector. Their common vector would have to vanish on the union
of their zero-coordinate sets, hence on all six coordinates, a
contradiction. Therefore this 4-site hard-BCS endpoint exchange is strict
whenever all three exchange terms are positive.

The result is scoped to n=4 and S=E,T=empty. It explains why the symmetric
all-positive generic stable equality polynomial in the neighboring note
cannot be realized in the 4-site hard-BCS class, and is stronger than the
coefficient-specific bound there. It is not a classification of equality
for one- or two-active-move cases, general endpoint sets, or larger n.
