Leaf 06 — finite-graph transport, mesh uniformity, and continuum limits
Date: 2026-10-08

Finding

There are two different finite-graph arguments in the supplied source package.

1. The deterministic affine-barrier corollary does give a mesh-uniform
   concentration bound when every species uses the same scalar coefficient on
   each transport edge. Its barrier lives in the fixed local species dimension
   d, and its invariance proof is independent of the graph size and the size of
   the edge coefficients. With standard shape-regular finite-volume meshes,
   the resulting L-infinity bound plus the usual discrete diffusion energy
   estimate gives subsequential compactness and a PDE limit that remains in
   the same positive convex set. This is a conditional PDE consequence; mesh
   consistency, energy coercivity, and convergence of reaction controls still
   have to be supplied.

2. The stochastic graph exit certificate is only for common species hopping.
   On a mesh with hopping rate D_h = kappa h^(-2), its displayed collars,
   exponential jump parameter, and exponent scale as h^2, h^2, and h^4,
   respectively, for facets seen by transport. The exit estimate is useful
   uniformly over N_h about h^(-p) sites only if the per-cell system size
   V_h h^4 dominates log N_h + log(V_h T). At fixed physical concentration
   scale, V_h is proportional to h^p, so the required large-volume premise
   fails and the exponent tends to zero. This finite-time stochastic safety
   theorem therefore supplies no fixed-density continuum PDE or stochastic
   permanence result.

Neither result covers arbitrary species-dependent transport. The exact local
counterexample below leaves the proposed product polytope in time O(h^2)
under diagonal diffusion diag(1,3) h^(-2), despite a mesh-independent positive
initial facet gap. A sufficient unequal-diffusion replacement is a
transport-normal compatibility condition: every halfspace normal used by the
barrier must be a nonnegative left eigenvector of each edge transport matrix.
The affine barrier supplied by the reaction theorem is not known to satisfy
that condition in general.

Source and exact interfaces

The public primary source is OpenAI math release 149, “Permanence for weakly
reversible reaction networks,” dated 5 October 2026, at pinned repository
commit adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a. It supplies the affine
approximation Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 for fixed positive rates and weakly
reversible mass-action networks. It is not the source of the broader
all-endotactic measurable-rate theorem or its graph corollaries. Those are
contained in the local 7 October reconstruction, whose own status remains
internal and externally unvalidated.

The local stochastic source is the 7 October “Finite graph reaction-diffusion
safety from the same facet certificate” report. For a graph with N vertices,
maximum degree Delta, d species per site, and equal local volume V, its state
has dN integer counts. Local reaction intensities are V a_vr^V with bounded
rates and actual inward collar drift. A hop of species j from v to w has
intensity V D_vw X_vj and changes one molecule at each endpoint. The graph
theorem requires symmetric edge coefficients common to all species, a
full-dimensional positive polytope Q shared by all sites, bounded degree, and
uniform local reaction margins. Its result is a finite-time exit-probability
bound, not infinite-time survival or stochastic permanence.

The volume/rate rescaling is essential. A reaction jump changes concentration
by nu/V_h and has intensity V_h k phi^V_y(X); its concentration drift is
k phi^V_y(X) nu, tending to k X^y nu as V_h grows. The finite-volume
factorial correction loses at most D_i/V_h from a supplied local facet margin
gamma_i, where

    D_i = K sum_r |a_i dot nu_r| E_(y_r)(U).

Thus V_h >= V_0 = max(1, max_i ceil(2 D_i/gamma_i)) gives the actual margin
delta_Ri=gamma_i/2. This is a local threshold, not multiplied by N_h, but it
still fails when physical voxel scaling makes V_h -> 0. A molecule hop changes the
two endpoint concentrations by +/-1/V_h and has intensity
V_h D_h X_(v,j), so its mean concentration drift is
D_h(X_(w,j)-X_(v,j)). Setting D_h=kappa h^(-2) therefore approximates a
diffusion Laplacian without changing the O(1) local reaction drift. The
parameter V_h controls local noise and particle counts; it is not the mesh
spacing. If voxel physical volume is h^p and the particle scale per unit
physical volume is Omega_h, the corresponding local system size is
V_h=Omega_h h^p.

The separate deterministic source treats d-dimensional local reaction fields
and an affine minimum

    F_s(x) = min_{lambda in Lambda} (r_lambda dot x + b_lambda(s)),

where the finite slopes r_lambda are fixed before the auxiliary scale s is
chosen. In the reconstructed endotactic argument, the finite direction set is
D = {coordinate vectors, nonzero reaction-polytope vertex differences};
E(r) is the minimum of 1/2 and the nonzero ratios
|r dot v|/(2 ||v||_1), and H is the corresponding positive minimum of
|r_lambda dot v|/2 over nonzero pairings (capped by 1). With
L = sum_e ||nu_e||_1 and M = max_e ||y_e||_1, the weighted essential-source
estimate gives, for active non-neutral slopes in [s,s^(-1)]^d,

    r dot f(t,x) >= x^w (2 kappa H - K L s^H)
                 >= kappa H s^M =: delta_s > 0,

when K L s^H <= kappa H. The plateau K_s = {F_s = F_s(c)} is compact,
convex, and lies in [sqrt(s), s^(-1/2)]^d; the classwise construction selects
a final s_P and yields coordinate bounds s_P <= x_i <= s_P^(-1). Its
intermediate entry bound is

    W_s = (F_s(c) - min_[s,s^(-1)]^d F_s)/(kappa H s^M).

The source gives no useful universal lower bound for H or s_P, nor upper
bounds on family size, coefficient precision, or W_s across changing
reaction systems. For one fixed local reaction family these quantities are
independent of the number of mesh cells. Applying the affine construction to
the whole dN-dimensional graph instead would make the dimension and direction
set grow with N, for which the source supplies no uniform H_N, s_N, or entry
bound.

Uniformity calculation for the stochastic graph certificate

Write h for the spatial mesh size, so it is distinct from the affine scale s.
Take a quasi-uniform p-dimensional mesh with N_h and |E_h| both of order
h^(-p), bounded degree Delta, and common symmetric edge rate
D_h = kappa h^(-2). Let the local reaction collar width and inward drift be
ell_i and delta_Ri, fixed with h. The graph proof chooses a new collar

    eta_i(h) <= min(ell_i, delta_Ri/(2 D_h Delta)),
    delta_i(h) = delta_Ri - D_h Delta eta_i(h) >= delta_Ri/2.

Thus eta_i(h) is O(h^2). In the graph theorem's notation,

    Q_i(h) = Q_Ri + 2 D_h Delta R sum_j n_(i,j)^2,
    theta_i(h) = min(1/J_i, 2 delta_i(h)/(e Q_i(h))),
    rho_i(h) = theta_i(h) eta_i(h),
    Lambda_loc(h) = Lambda_R + 2 D_h Delta d R,
    B_i(h) = Lambda_loc(h) [exp(theta_i(h) J_i)-1].

For a facet with nonzero normal on a transported species, Q_i(h) = Theta(h^(-2));
therefore theta_i(h) = Theta(h^2), rho_i(h) = Theta(h^4), and B_i(h) = O(1).
The source's explicit five-facet example has this property for every facet.
Facets orthogonal to the transported species can have better constants; this
does not repair the minimum exponent when even one required facet has the
displayed scaling.

For initial gaps g_i(X_v(0)) >= zeta_i > 0, the exact source bound is

    P(tau_Q <= T) <= N_h sum_i exp(-V_h theta_i(h) zeta_i)
                    + V_h T N_h sum_i B_i(h) exp(-V_h rho_i(h)).

A sufficient mesh-uniform condition is

    V_h theta_min(h) zeta_min - log N_h -> +infinity,
    V_h rho_min(h) - log(V_h T N_h max_i B_i(h)) -> +infinity,
    V_h >= V_0.

In the generic transported-facet regime this reduces to
V_h h^4 >> log N_h + log(V_h T). For N_h about h^(-p) and fixed T, a
polynomial V_h must satisfy V_h h^4/log(1/h) -> infinity. The weaker
initial-gap term requires only V_h h^2 >> log(1/h), so the second term is the
load-bearing one.

If physical voxel volume is proportional to h^p and the molecule/system-size
scale per unit physical volume is Omega_h, then V_h = Omega_h h^p. At fixed
Omega_h = Omega, V_h -> 0, contradicting V_h >= V_0; the formal exit exponent
V_h rho_min is only O(Omega h^(p+4)). A fixed V_h >= V_0 also fails to suppress
the exit estimate because V_h h^4 -> 0. To make this particular certificate
vanish, one needs Omega_h h^(p+4) >> log(1/h) (for fixed T and polynomial
scaling). At fixed Omega, the total particle inventory V_h N_h stays O(Omega)
while mean occupancy per voxel vanishes; the diffusion event envelope grows
like O(Omega h^(-2) T). To meet the certificate condition with
V_h=h^(-4)L_h and L_h/log(1/h)->infinity, total particle inventory must grow
as h^(-p-4)L_h and the event envelope as O(h^(-p-6)L_h T). This is a
high-density diagonal limit, not the fixed-density continuum limit.

The same scaling charges the computation. Counts require O(d N_h log(1+V_h R))
bits and the listed graph O((N_h+|E_h|) log(1+N_h)) bits. The physical count
inventory is O(V_h N_h). Before exit, the source bounds the total event
intensity by

    V_h [N_h Lambda_R + 2 D_h d R |E_h|]
       = O(V_h h^(-p-2)),

so a direct trajectory over time T has an event envelope of
O(V_h T h^(-p-2)); rate evaluation, event selection, random-bit precision,
controller history, and output costs are additional. At the minimal
sufficient choice V_h of order h^(-4) log(1/h), this envelope is of order
h^(-p-6) log(1/h) T. The certificate evaluates a probability bound; it does
not simulate or accelerate the trajectory.

Deterministic uniformity criterion and PDE compactness

For the deterministic transport corollary, fix a finite local reaction
family in R^d and a compact positive initial range Q0. Its affine barrier has
the same slope set at every site. For any finite graph, define

    G(x_1,...,x_N) = min_(v,lambda) [r_lambda dot x_v + b_lambda(s)].

If (v,lambda) is active, then for every w,
L_lambda(x_w) >= F_s(x_w) >= G = L_lambda(x_v). With a common scalar edge
coefficient m_vw >= 0,

    r_lambda dot sum_w m_vw (x_w-x_v)
      = sum_w m_vw [L_lambda(x_w)-L_lambda(x_v)] >= 0.

The local reaction contribution is nonnegative at the active barrier. The
resulting compact positive K is independent of N, graph topology, and the
values of m_vw. In particular, taking m_vw proportional to h^(-2) does not
weaken invariance. This proves a uniform-in-h pointwise bound for all-time
deterministic approximations whose initial concentrations lie in Q0.
It is an invariant range statement from bounded positive initial data, not by
itself a classwise absorbing theorem for arbitrary spatially unbounded data.

An explicit sufficient criterion also allows unequal species diffusion.
Suppose Q = {x: a_i dot x + b_i >= 0} is a common compact positive reaction
invariant polytope, and edge v-w transports concentrations by a diagonal
matrix A_vw^h (or any matrix with the property below). If each facet normal
satisfies

    (a_i)^T A_vw^h = mu_i,vw^h (a_i)^T,   mu_i,vw^h >= 0,

for every edge, then at a site on facet i,

    a_i dot sum_w A_vw^h (x_w-x_v)
      = sum_w mu_i,vw^h [g_i(x_w)-g_i(x_v)] >= 0.

The product Q^N is invariant whenever the local reaction field points into Q.
For constant diagonal diffusion D=diag(D_1,...,D_d), this asks that each
facet normal be supported only on species with a common D_j. Coordinate-box
facets always meet the condition; mixed normals such as (1,1) do not when
D_1 != D_2. This is a sufficient condition, not a necessary characterization
of all possible invariant regions.

To obtain an actual continuum subsequential limit, add the following standard
discretization assumptions: the meshes are shape regular and quasi-uniform
on a fixed bounded domain; the symmetric conservative graph Laplacian is
consistent with the desired no-flux (or periodic) Laplacian; edge conductances
give a discrete H^1 energy equivalent to the piecewise-constant gradient;
diffusivities are uniformly elliptic; and initial interpolants converge in
L^2. If reactions are uniformly bounded on K and their finite collection of
coefficient fields has a weak-* convergent subsequence, then

    1/2 d ||u_h||_(L2,h)^2/dt + c0 ||grad_h u_h||_2^2
       <= ||u_h||_2 ||f_h||_2 <= C(K) |Omega|.

Integration gives uniform L-infinity-in-time L2 and L2-in-time discrete-H1
bounds. The equation bounds time derivatives in L2-in-time H^-1_h; discrete
Rellich/Aubin-Lions compactness gives a strongly L2-convergent subsequence.
Since every u_h(t,x) lies in the same closed convex K, a further almost-
everywhere convergent subsequence has u(t,x) in K. Strong convergence on K
passes polynomial reaction terms, while weak-* convergence passes bounded
rate fields. The limit solves the corresponding reaction-diffusion PDE and
retains the positive lower and finite upper bounds defining K.

This compactness conclusion is conditional on the listed mesh and coefficient
convergence assumptions. The affine theorem supplies neither a mesh family,
nor discrete Rellich estimates, nor convergence of arbitrary h-dependent
rate controls. For common scalar diffusion, the facet eigencondition is
automatic. For species-dependent diffusion, it must be checked for the actual
barrier; the available affine source does not provide it generally.

Concrete unequal-diffusion falsifier

At each site take the linear reactions 0 <-> A and 0 <-> B with birth rate 2
and per-molecule death rate 1. Their concentration drift is (2-x, 2-y). The
local full-dimensional polytope

    Q = {1 <= x <= 3, 1 <= y <= 3, x+y >= 3}

has strict inward reaction margin 1 on each of its five proper facets. On two
sites joined by an edge, put initial states (1+epsilon,2) and (2,1+epsilon).
Both are interior to Q, with both sum-facet gaps exactly epsilon. With species
A hopping at alpha/h^2 and species B at beta/h^2, where 0 < alpha < beta,
the first site's lower-sum-facet gap

    g_h(t) = x_(1,A)(t) + x_(1,B)(t) - 3

is exactly

    g_h(t) = 1 - (1-epsilon) exp(-t)
             - (1-epsilon)/2 exp(-(1+2 alpha/h^2)t)
             + (1-epsilon)/2 exp(-(1+2 beta/h^2)t).

Take alpha=1, beta=3, c=log(3)/4, and epsilon=1/1000. Then

    g_h(c h^2) = 1 - (1-epsilon) exp(-c h^2)
                     (1 + 1/(3 sqrt(3))).

This is negative whenever

    h^2 < (4/log(3)) log[(999/1000)(1 + 1/(3 sqrt(3)))],

whose right-hand side is about 0.63720237. In particular,

    lim_(h->0) g_h(c h^2)
      = epsilon - (1-epsilon)/(3 sqrt(3)) < 0.

Since g_h(0)=epsilon>0, continuity gives a first exit time tau_h through this
facet with tau_h <= c h^2 for every h satisfying the displayed condition.
Thus the initial gap and the certified exit-time upper bound are exact; no
claim is made that the first zero itself occurs at c h^2. At h=1 and
t=log(100/99), the exact gap is
-1621541844091699/200000000000000000 < 0, independently rechecked by rational
arithmetic. This refutes uniform invariance of this local certificate and
shows why the common-coefficient sign calculation cannot be extended by
inspection. It does not refute the existence of another invariant set or
permanence for this linear network; its linear dynamics remain bounded.

The same obstruction occurs in the continuum PDE, so it is not only a graph
artifact. On the one-dimensional periodic domain, let D_A=3, D_B=1 and set

    x(0,z) = 3/2 - (1/10)(1-cos(4z)),
    y(0,z) = 3/2 + (1/5)(1-cos(4z)).

These smooth positive initial values lie in Q and touch its sum facet at
z=0. At that point x_zz=-16/10 and y_zz=16/5, while the reaction sum drift
is 1. Hence

    partial_t(x+y-3)(0,0)
      = 1 + 3(-16/10) + 1(16/5) = -3/5 < 0.

The PDE solution immediately leaves Q at that point. Again this only falsifies
the proposed mixed-facet barrier for unequal diffusivities; it is not a
failure of positive boundedness for this stable linear example.

Best comparator, source dates, and status

The best deterministic comparator is Chueh, Conley, and Smoller, “Positively
Invariant Regions for Systems of Nonlinear Diffusion Equations,” Indiana Univ.
Math. J. 26(2), 373-392 (1977). Its invariant-region/max-principle framework
is the established antecedent for the scalar-diffusion and compatible-normal
criterion; neither is claimed novel here. For the parabolic mesh step,
Bradji and Fuhrmann, “Some abstract error estimates of a finite volume scheme
for a nonstationary heat equation on general nonconforming multidimensional
spatial meshes” (Applications of Mathematics 58 (2013), 1-38), is a closer
energy/discrete-gradient comparator. Gallouet, Herbin, and Vignal,
“Error Estimates on the Approximate Finite Volume Solution of Convection
Diffusion Equations with General Boundary Conditions,” SIAM J. Numer. Anal.
37(6) (2000), and Eymard, Gallouet, and Herbin, “Convergence of finite volume
schemes for semilinear convection diffusion equations,” Numer. Math. 82
(1999), are standard mesh-convergence comparators. Engblom, Ferm, Hellander,
and Lotstedt’s 2009 stochastic reaction-diffusion master-equation work
documents the mesoscopic graph-to-macroscopic PDE modeling interface. For
stochastic large-volume context, Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann (2017) prove a
sample-path LDP and Wentzell-Freidlin theory for chemical jump models; that
does not furnish the mesh-uniform stopped-exit estimate missing here.

Scientific status: `reconstructed` for the scaling and counterexample;
`conditional` for the deterministic PDE compactness statement; `UNKNOWN` for
general unequal-species-diffusion permanence and for a fixed-density
continuum limit of the stochastic graph certificate. The local N33 theorem,
transport corollary, and C06 stochastic proof are internal reconstruction
artifacts with source-reported rather than external validation. The exact
rational h=1 exit gap was independently checked; no trajectory simulation
was performed, and no novelty or priority claim is made.
