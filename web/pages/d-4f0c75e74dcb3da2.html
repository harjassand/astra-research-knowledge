# A full-Hessian-preserving global-coordinate orbit that cannot create a pivot

Cycle16, 7 October2026. Worker hessian_bridge12, root-verified GPT-6.1 Sol/max; no nested workers. These are programme deductions from the explicitly verified published formulas. The inverse cubic is a previous cycle12 deduction, now rechecked and used in a new obstruction. Neither historical novelty nor external validation is claimed.

**Outcome.** A large genuinely base-moving family preserves the full nonzero constant five-variable Hessian determinant and can retain the published collision, but cannot create any constant scalar pivot. More sharply, all constant isotropic covectors of its inverse Hessian are exactly the two-dimensional base plane. This characterizes every determinant-compatible nonlinear elementary shear driven by one linear form. Every finite word of affine changes and such shears, **if each intermediate potential retains constant full Hessian determinant**, remains in the same family up to an affine change. No affine pivot in this orbit admits a global polynomial critical section at any selected level. The mixed-direction strengthening uses irreducibility of every actual derivative fibre, not only of a generic fibre.

Words that lose determinant constancy and later restore it, elementary shears with genuinely multivariate drivers, and variable dual-linear source coefficients are not excluded. Selected-level sections for charts outside the stated orbit remain open. The same obstruction survives arbitrary base-only added potentials; the scalar quadratic-repair corollary below distinguishes these from coordinate changes.

## 1. Prior starting object, exact formulas and interface

Use Meng–Yang arXiv2607.22198v2, Section6 Theorem6.1, source label thm:psi5:
\[
 f(x,y,z)=A^2+13A+2B,\qquad A=g(x,y)\cdot z,\quad B=h(x,y)\cdot z,
\]
where \(z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)\), \(u=1+xy\),
\[
 g=(u^3,3xu^2,-x^3),
\]
\[
 h=(y^2u(4+3xy),\
 y+3xy^2(4+3xy),\
 2x-3x^2y).
\]
Their displayed underlying Alpöge map is
\[
 F(x,y,t)=h(x,y)+t g(x,y),
\qquad \det JF=-2.
\]
The actual author collision for \(f\) is
\[
 q_\pm=(\pm1,\mp3/2,0,0,0),\qquad
 \nabla f(q_\pm)=(0,0,-1/2,0,0).
\]
All these identities are independently recertified in the cycle16 checker. The v1 coefficient11 and its witness are not used.

The global-coordinate family considered here is
\[
 T(X,Z)=\bigl(\phi(X),\ D Z+H(X)\bigr), \tag{1}
\]
where \(\phi:\mathbb A^2\to\mathbb A^2\) is any polynomial automorphism, \(D\in GL_3(\mathbb C)\) is **constant**, and \(H\in\mathbb C[X]^3\) is arbitrary. It has the global polynomial inverse
\[
 X=\phi^{-1}(x,y),\qquad
 Z=D^{-1}\bigl(z-H(\phi^{-1}(x,y))\bigr).
\]
The old base is moved nonlinearly; old dual translations may have arbitrary degree. The constant dual-linear coefficient is an essential restriction.

An arbitrary affine source change may follow (1). The theorem will track how it transports the inverse-Hessian isotropic plane.

## 2. Full determinant preservation, with the actual Hessian correction included

First, an elementary all-free block identity:
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}E&B\\B^T&\lambda gg^T\end{pmatrix}
 =\lambda\det\begin{pmatrix}B\\g^T\end{pmatrix}^{\!2}, \tag{2}
\]
where \(E\) is symmetric2×2, \(B\) is2×3 and \(g\) is a3-vector. In particular, the determinant is independent of \(E\). If the matrix is invertible, its inverse has zero2×2 base block: its three relevant cofactors are zero. The checker verifies (2) and all three cofactors with free indeterminates. Equivalently, a base-block correction gives a relative square-zero matrix and cannot change this determinant.

For a direct proof of constancy under the nonlinear chart, write
\[
 \widetilde F(X,t)=F(\phi(X),t),\qquad
 \overline F(X,t)=D^T\widetilde F(X,t),\qquad
 \overline g=\partial_t\overline F.
\]
The six-variable lift
\[
 G(X,t,Z)=2(DZ+H(X))\cdot\widetilde F(X,t)-(t-13/2)^2 \tag{3}
\]
has constant scalar corner \(G_{tt}=-2\), and critical section
\[
 t_*=(DZ+H)\cdot g(\phi(X))+13/2.
\]
Its critical value is exactly \(f\circ T\).

In coordinates \(((X,t),Z)\), its Hessian is
\[
 H_G=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E&2J\overline F^T\\
 2J\overline F&0
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(E\) contains **all** nonlinear base/translation corrections:
\[
 E=2\sum_i Z_i H_{\overline F_i}
 +H_{(X,t)}(2H\cdot\widetilde F)
 -2e_te_t^T.
\]
It is not replaced by a congruence alone. Since \(\overline F\) is Keller,
\[
 \det H_G=-(\det(2J\overline F))^2
 =-256(\det D\det J\phi)^2.
\]
Eliminating \(t\) by its constant corner gives
\[
 \boxed{\det H_{f\circ T}
 =128(\det D\det J\phi)^2\in\mathbb C^\times.} \tag{4}
\]
Thus this family really satisfies the full-source determinant premise, unlike the earlier failed nonlinear residual charts. The same argument can also be obtained from (2) and the complete nonlinear chain rule: every second-derivative correction of (1) is supported in the base block.

The inverse of the six-dimensional Hessian has blocks
\[
 H_G^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&K\\K^T&-K^TEK
 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad K=(2J\overline F)^{-1}. \tag{5}
\]
The inverse of \(H_{f\circ T}\) is the principal submatrix on \((X,Z)\) of (5), evaluated at \(t=t_*\), by the block inverse/Schur formula. Hence its base2×2 block is identically zero. The base plane consists of constant isotropic covectors for this inverse Hessian.

## 3. No nonzero constant target direction has a locally nilpotent lift for F

This is the load-bearing exact obstruction, proved without any plane-Jacobian conjecture or low-sheet theorem.

Let \(a=F_1,b=F_2,c=F_3\) and work in the original source fraction field
\[
 K_0=\mathbb C(x,y,t).
\]
The previous inverse-variable certificate \(w=y+1/x\) satisfies
\[
 P_0(w)=cw^3-2w^2+bw-2a=0,\qquad
 x(b-4w+3cw^2)=2. \tag{6}
\]
These identities are freshly checked by clearing denominators. The nonzero Keller Jacobian implies that \(a,b,c\) are algebraically independent.

For a fixed nonzero vector \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)\in\mathbb C^3\), let \(\delta_q\) be the polynomial derivation whose coordinate vector is \((JF)^{-1}q\). Its entries are polynomial since \(\det JF=-2\), and
\[
 \delta_q F=q.
\]
If \(\delta_q\) were locally nilpotent, its exponential would give a polynomial source flow in a variable \(s\). Then
\[
 w(s)=y(s)+1/x(s)\in K_0(s)
\]
would satisfy
\[
 P_0(w(s))+sQ(w(s))=0,\qquad
 Q(W)=q_3W^3+q_2W-2q_1. \tag{7}
\]

But \(Q\ne0\), and \(P_0,Q\) are coprime in \(K_0[W]\). To see this directly, every root of \(Q\) lies in \(\mathbb C\); for any such fixed root \(\alpha\),
\[
 P_0(\alpha)=c\alpha^3-2\alpha^2+b\alpha-2a\ne0
\]
by algebraic independence of \(a,b,c\). Equivalently, the exact resultants have leading coefficient \(8q_3^3\) in degree3 of \(a\) when \(q_3\ne0\); \(2q_2^3\) in degree1 when \(q_3=0,q_2\ne0\); and value \(-8q_1^3\) otherwise. These three exhaustive certificates are checked symbolically.

The polynomial \(P_0(W)+sQ(W)\) is primitive and linear in \(s\). A factor with zero \(s\)-degree would divide both \(P_0,Q\); thus it is irreducible in \(K_0[W,s]\). It is also primitive as a polynomial in \(W\) over \(K_0[s]\), since its \(W^2\) coefficient is the nonzero constant \(-2\). Gauss's lemma makes it irreducible over \(K_0(s)\), of degree3 in \(W\). It has no rational root, contradicting (7).

Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\delta_q\text{ is not locally nilpotent for every }q\ne0.} \tag{8}
\]
The known source root of \(P_0\) at \(s=0\) does not factor the generic line deformation. This distinction is essential.

## 4. Exact classification of constant inverse-Hessian isotropic covectors

Let \(\ell=(p,q)\in\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb C^3\) be a constant covector for \(f\circ T\). Suppose
\[
 \ell^T H_{f\circ T}^{-1}\ell\equiv0. \tag{9}
\]
Set \(r=Kq\). From (5), this isotropic contraction is
\[
 2p\cdot r_X-r^TEr
\]
on the critical graph. For a fixed generic \(X,t\), the allowable \(Z\)'s form the affine plane
\[
 \overline g(X)\cdot Z=t-13/2-H(X)\cdot g(\phi(X)).
\]
All terms of \(E\) except \(2\sum Z_iH_{\overline F_i}\) are independent of \(Z\) at fixed \(X,t\). Varying \(Z\) on this plane forces
\[
 \bigl(r^TH_{\overline F_i}r\bigr)_{i=1}^3
 \text{ to be proportional to }\overline g. \tag{10}
\]
This is an identity over the base/source function field; the plane substitution is valid because \(\overline g\) is nowhere zero and unimodular.

Let \(\delta\) have coordinate vector \((J\overline F)^{-1}q=2r\). Differentiating \(J\overline F\,\delta=q\) gives
\[
 \delta^2(X,t)
 =-(J\overline F)^{-1}
 \bigl(\delta^TH_{\overline F_i}\delta\bigr)_i.
\]
Since \(J\overline F\,e_t=\overline g\), equation (10) implies
\[
 \delta^2X_1=\delta^2X_2=0. \tag{11}
\]

The vector \(g\) is unimodular over \(\mathbb C[x,y]\). An explicit certificate, with \(v=xy\), is
\[
 (1-3v+6v^2)g_1
 +y^3(10+15v+6v^2)g_3=1. \tag{12}
\]
It remains unimodular after \(\phi\) and constant \(D^T\). Thus some polynomial row \(k(X)\) has \(k\overline g=1\). Writing \(\overline F=\overline h+t\overline g\), we get
\[
 t=k(X)\cdot(\overline F-\overline h(X)).
\]
Consequently the source ring \(\mathbb C[X,t]\) is generated by \(X_1,X_2,\overline F_1,\overline F_2,\overline F_3\). Every generator is individually \(\delta\)-nilpotent: (11) handles \(X_i\), while \(\delta\overline F=q\) handles the remaining three. Leibniz's rule then makes \(\delta\) locally nilpotent on the entire source ring. No claim that \(\delta^2t=0\) is needed.

If \(q\ne0\), source conjugation by \(\phi\) and the constant target change \(D^T\) would give a locally nilpotent \(\delta_{D^{-T}q}\) for the original \(F\), contradicting (8). Hence \(q=0\).

Conversely every \((p,0)\) is isotropic because the inverse base block is zero. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\text{The constant inverse-Hessian isotropic covectors are exactly }
 \mathbb C^2\oplus0.} \tag{13}
\]
This holds for arbitrary-degree \(\phi,H\), and every constant invertible \(D\).

In fact the same proof excludes any **constant value**, not merely zero, of \(\ell^TH^{-1}\ell\) for a nonbase \(\ell\): variation on the fixed \((X,t)\) dual plane is still zero, and the no-LND argument is unchanged. A constant inverse-Hessian contraction therefore necessarily has \(q=0\), and its value is then zero.

## 5. Every compatible one-linear-form elementary shear stays inside the family

For any constant nonzero vectors \(v,\ell\) with \(\ell^Tv=0\) and any polynomial \(P\) of degree at least2, the elementary source shear
\[
 S(U)=U+vP(\ell^TU)
\]
has polynomial inverse \(U-vP(\ell^TU)\) and determinant1.

For a potential \(p\) with \(\det H_p=d\in\mathbb C^\times\), its complete Hessian chain is
\[
 H_{p\circ S}=J_S^T(H_p\circ S)J_S
 +(v\cdot\nabla p\circ S)P''\,\ell\ell^T.
\]
Since \(J_S^{-T}\ell=\ell\), the rank-one determinant formula gives
\[
 \det H_{p\circ S}
 =d\left[1+(v\cdot\nabla p\circ S)P''
   (\ell^TH_p^{-1}\ell)\circ S\right]. \tag{14}
\]
The polynomial \(v\cdot\nabla p\) is **nonconstant**, since its gradient is \(H_pv\ne0\). Thus the product correction cannot be a nonzero constant. Because \(P''\ne0\) and the polynomial ring is a domain, the result is a nonzero constant exactly when
\[
 \ell^TH_p^{-1}\ell=0,
\]
in which case that constant is the original \(d\).

Apply (13) to \(p=f\circ T\). Every compatible driver \(\ell\) is supported in \(X\). The shear therefore has a base polynomial automorphism
\[
 X\mapsto X+v_XP(\ell_X\cdot X)
\]
and a dual translation \(Z\mapsto Z+v_ZP(\ell_X\cdot X)\), so its composition with (1) stays in the same family.

If an affine map \(L\) follows \(T\), its inverse-Hessian isotropic plane is \(L^T(\mathbb C^2\oplus0)\). For any compatible shear after \(L\), conjugating it by \(L\) makes its driver base-supported again; the translation part merely shifts the scalar argument of \(P\). Thus it too remains in the family up to affine postchange.

Induction proves that every finite word of affine changes and individually constant-Hessian-preserving one-linear-form nonlinear shears is
\[
 f\circ T_{\rm new}\circ L_{\rm final}.
\]
This is an exact all-degree orbit containment, not a statement that every polynomial source automorphism has this form.

## 6. No constant scalar pivot anywhere in that orbit

The dual-quadratic part of \(f\circ T\) is
\[
 (\overline g(X)\cdot Z)^2.
\]
Let a constant direction have components \(v=(v_X,v_Z)\), and suppose \(D_v^2(f\circ T)\) is constant. Its dual-degree2 coefficients give
\[
 D_{v_X}^2(\overline g_i^2)=0\quad\text{for every }i.
\]
A polynomial square with zero second derivative in a constant direction must be independent of that direction: put the direction along a coordinate and compare its highest polynomial degree. Thus \(D_{v_X}\overline g=0\). Constant invertible \(D\) gives \(D_{v_X}g(\phi(X))=0\). The component \(-\phi_1^3\) forces \(D_{v_X}\phi_1=0\); the component \((1+\phi_1\phi_2)^3\) then forces \(D_{v_X}\phi_2=0\). Since \(\phi\) is an automorphism, \(v_X=0\).

The remaining second derivative is
\[
 D_v^2(f\circ T)=2(\overline g\cdot v_Z)^2.
\]
No nonzero constant linear combination of \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) is constant: compare successively the \(y^3,y^2\), then \(x^3\) coefficients in
\[
 c_1(1+xy)^3+3c_2x(1+xy)^2-c_3x^3.
\]
Base reparametrization and constant \(D\) preserve this fact. Hence \(v_Z=0\).

There is therefore **no nonzero constant direction with constant second derivative**, including zero. Arbitrary affine source postchanges preserve this obstruction. Adding any quadratic polynomial also cannot remove it, because it adds only a constant to each second directional derivative; such additions are not claimed to preserve full determinant or collision.

By the separately proved univariate-composition lemma from cycle15, a critical section polynomial jointly in its level and residual variables would force a constant scalar pivot. Therefore no affine pivot in this orbit admits such a joint polynomial all-level critical inverse. The following two arguments give the stronger selected-level exclusion, without requiring a constant corner on the section.

### Pure-dual pivots: even a selected polynomial section is impossible

Before the final affine postchange, choose any nonzero constant pivot direction confined to the dual variables and complete it to a constant dual frame. In that frame,
\[
 A_{\rm new}=\alpha(X)\tau+\beta_1(X)r_1+\beta_2(X)r_2+\theta(X).
\]
The coefficient triple \((\alpha,\beta_1,\beta_2)\) is unimodular because it is a constant invertible transform of \(\overline g\). Also \(\alpha\ne0\), since no nonzero constant combination of \(g\) vanishes. For every fixed level \(\sigma\), the unique fraction-field critical solution is
\[
 \tau=\frac{\sigma-13\alpha-2\overline h_v-2\alpha\theta}{2\alpha^2}
 -\frac{\beta_1}{\alpha}r_1-\frac{\beta_2}{\alpha}r_2.
\]
If it were polynomial in \(X,r_1,r_2\), then \(\alpha\) would divide both \(\beta_j\). Unimodularity of the triple would make \(\alpha\) a constant unit. That contradicts the no-constant-combination property of \(g\). Thus **no pure-dual affine pivot has a global polynomial critical section at any level**, including zero. This does not assert that every rational critical value is nonpolynomial.

### Mixed pivots: every selected derivative fibre is irreducible

This strengthening was first derived by hessian_check12 in cycle16 and then independently reconstructed here from the literal published formulas. Put \(\overline h=D^Th\circ\phi\), \(\theta=H\cdot g\circ\phi\) and \(C=H\cdot h\circ\phi\). Then
\[
 f\circ T=(\overline g\cdot Z+\theta)^2
 +13(\overline g\cdot Z+\theta)+2\overline h\cdot Z+2C.
\]
Choose a constant direction \(v=(p,r)\) with \(p\in\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\}\). Write \(D_p=p\cdot\nabla_X\). Over \(K=\mathbb C(X)\), define the dual-linear forms
\[
 L=\overline g\cdot Z,\qquad
 M=(D_p\overline g)\cdot Z,\qquad
 N=(D_p\overline h)\cdot Z,
\]
and \(d_0=\overline g\cdot r+D_p\theta\). The derivative \(P=D_v(f\circ T)\) satisfies, at every fixed level \(\sigma\),
\[
 P-\sigma=2LM+(2\theta+13)M+2d_0L+2N+C_0-\sigma, \tag{14a}
\]
where \(C_0=(2\theta+13)d_0+2\overline h\cdot r+2D_pC\). The checker verifies the full free-jet expansion.

The three forms are independent over \(K\). Indeed, for an arbitrary polynomial base vector \((a,b)\), the literal source formulas give
\[
 \det(g,D_{(a,b)}g,D_{(a,b)}h)
 =6(1+xy)(a-bx^2)^2. \tag{14b}
\]
The pushed derivation \(\delta=\phi_*D_p\) is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of \(\mathbb C[x,y]\); its components \(a=\delta x,b=\delta y\) are polynomials. It cannot satisfy \(a=bx^2\): that would make \(\delta x\in(x)\). For a locally nilpotent derivation on a characteristic-zero domain, its degree function is additive on products and satisfies \(\deg_\delta(\delta x)=\deg_\delta(x)-1\) when \(\delta x\ne0\). Hence divisibility by \(x\) is impossible unless \(\delta x=0\). The relation then gives \(\delta y=0\), contradicting \(\delta\ne0\). Thus (14b), composed with \(\phi\) and multiplied by \(\det D\), is generically nonzero.

After an invertible \(K\)-linear change of the three dual variables to \((L,M,N)\), expression (14a) is linear in \(N\) with unit coefficient2. Its quotient ring is \(K[L,M]\); it is therefore irreducible in \(K[Z]\), for **every** \(\sigma\in\mathbb C\).

There are no vertical factors when returning to \(\mathbb C[X,Z]\). In fact the coefficients of all positive-degree dual monomials have no common base zero. At a putative zero, \(\overline g\ne0\) by unimodularity. The quadratic dual part \(2LM\) would vanish, forcing \(D_p\overline g=0\) at that point. The linear part would then give
\[
 D_p\overline h=-d_0\overline g.
\]
For the everywhere invertible Keller Jacobian of \(\overline F=\overline h+t\overline g\), this means
\[
 J\overline F\,(p,d_0)^T=0
\]
at that base point and any \(t\), contradicting \(p\ne0\). In particular the positive-degree coefficients generate the unit ideal over \(\mathbb C[X]\); primitivity is independent of the selected level. Gauss's lemma now proves that \(P-\sigma\) is globally irreducible in \(\mathbb C[X,Z]\) for every \(\sigma\).

In any constant affine frame with pivot direction \(v\), a global polynomial critical section \(\tau=s(u)\) would give the monic divisor \(\tau-s(u)\mid P-\sigma\). Global irreducibility forces \(P-\sigma\) to be a constant multiple of that divisor. Consequently \(P_\tau=D_v^2(f\circ T)\) would be a nonzero constant, contradicting the preceding no-pivot theorem. Affine translations and postchanges transport the same argument. Together with the pure-dual gate, this proves
\[
 \boxed{\text{No global polynomial critical section at any selected level
 for any affine pivot in the entire stated orbit.}} \tag{14c}
\]
This conclusion does not require a unit-corner assumption. It does not assert that every rational critical envelope is nonpolynomial, or exclude a genuinely nonlinear pivot chart outside this orbit.

### Scalar quadratic repairs and arbitrary base-only potential additions

The obstruction also applies to
\[
 p=f\circ T+\Gamma(X),\qquad\Gamma\in\mathbb C[X],
\]
followed by an affine source change. This is an additive-potential extension, not a claim that \(\Gamma\) comes from a source automorphism. Add \(\Gamma\) to the lift (3); it changes only the free upper-left block \(E\). Hence the full determinant, zero inverse base block and off-plane no-LND argument all survive. The no-pivot proof still uses exactly the same dual-degree2 part. In (14a), only the scalar \(C_0\) acquires \(D_p\Gamma\); irreducibility and the global coefficient-primitivity proof are unaffected. Every affine pivot still has no polynomial selected-level section.

For a scalar quadratic repair \(\kappa(\ell\cdot U)^2/2\), with constant \(\kappa\ne0\), the determinant lemma gives
\[
 \det H_{p+\kappa(\ell\cdot U)^2/2}
 =\det H_p\,[1+\kappa\ell^TH_p^{-1}\ell].
\]
If the repaired full determinant is a nonzero constant, the contraction must be constant. The strengthened inverse-contraction result forces \(\ell\) into the transformed base plane and its contraction to zero. Thus every compatible scalar quadratic repair is base-driven, leaves the full determinant unchanged and falls in the additive extension just excluded. Affine-linear repair terms do not affect this conclusion or the selected-section obstruction.

Actual collision is an additional obligation: adding \(\Gamma\) requires its base gradients to agree at the two transformed witness points, or, together with \(H\), the sum of the translation and added gradients to agree. No unconditional collision preservation is asserted. Higher-rank quadratic repairs may have cancellations and are not covered by this scalar-repair theorem.

### General selected-level component gate and its quantifier control

For a general affine pivot \(\tau\), put \(P=\partial_\tau p\), where \(p\) is any potential in the orbit. Suppose a global polynomial section \(\tau=s(u)\) exists at the actual selected level \(\sigma\), and suppose the corner on that graph is a nonzero constant unit \(\kappa=P_\tau|_{\tau=s}\). Monic division gives
\[
 P-\sigma=(\tau-s(u))R(\tau,u),\qquad R|_{\tau=s}=\kappa.
\]
If \(R\) were constant, then \(P_\tau\) would be a globally constant pivot, already excluded. Hence \(R\) is nonconstant and has a nonempty zero set over \(\mathbb C\). Its zero set is disjoint from the section graph because \(R|_{\rm graph}=\kappa\ne0\).

The constant full Hessian determinant implies \(\nabla P\) is nowhere zero: it is a row of an everywhere invertible Hessian. Therefore this critical fibre is reduced, and any two distinct irreducible components are disjoint; an intersection or a repeated factor would make its gradient zero there. The selected-level route would necessarily use a disconnected fibre containing the section graph and at least one additional component.

Consequently, connectedness or irreducibility of the **actual selected collision-level** fibre of \(P\) obstructs this type of section. Generic fibre irreducibility alone is not enough. The mixed-pivot proof above now supplies irreducibility of every actual fibre for this orbit; the general component argument remains useful for classes outside it.

The scalar control \(P(\tau,u)=\tau+u\tau^2\) makes the quantifier issue explicit: its gradient \((1+2u\tau,\tau^2)\) never vanishes, \(\tau=0\) is a polynomial section at level0 with corner1, yet that fibre is the disjoint union \(\tau=0\) and \(1+u\tau=0\). This is only a scalar control; it is not claimed to possess a constant-Hessian gradient completion or to belong to the orbit.

## 7. Actual collision compatibility and a global nonlinear control

For (1), let \(X_\pm=\phi^{-1}(\pm1,\mp3/2)\), \(Z_\pm=-D^{-1}H(X_\pm)\), and \(w=(-1/2,0,0)\). The pulled-back gradients are
\[
 ((DH(X_\pm))^Tw,\ D^Tw).
\]
Thus the actual pair is retained exactly when
\[
 (DH(X_+))^Tw=(DH(X_-))^Tw. \tag{15}
\]
Taking \(H=0\) always works. For a one-linear-form shear applied to a known collision with common gradient \(w_{\rm full}\), the corresponding criterion is
\[
 (v\cdot w_{\rm full})
 [P'(\ell\cdot q_+)-P'(\ell\cdot q_-)]=0.
\]
These are exact pullback conditions, not assumed collision invariance.

The checker supplies a genuinely nonlinear control:
\[
 \phi(X,Y)=(X+Y^2,Y),\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2,1),
\]
\[
 H=\bigl(V,YV,0\bigr),\qquad
 V=((X+Y^2)^2-1)^2.
\]
It has global polynomial inverse, Jacobian1, full Hessian determinant128 by (3)–(4), and retains the actual collision at
\[
 (-5/4,-3/2,0,0,0),\quad (-13/4,3/2,0,0,0)
\]
with common gradient \((0,0,-1,0,0)\). It has no global polynomial critical section for any affine pivot at any level. This control demonstrates compatibility of source constancy, actual collision and nonlinear base movement, while sharply identifying the missing descent interface.

## 8. Scope, prior and next step

Meng–Yang AppendixA's bordered determinant/Schur bridge and its original-coordinate assumptions are prior. Equations (2), (5), (14), coordinate conjugation, Gauss's lemma and polynomial-flow algebra are standard. The inverse cubic was derived in cycle12. The new programme deductions combine its no-LND consequence, the exact inverse-isotropic plane for arbitrary base/translation charts, the closed individually-preserving elementary-shear orbit, and the all-level selected-section obstruction. The determinant identity (14b), mixed-fibre irreducibility and its selected-section consequence are credited to hessian_check12's cycle16 derivation with independent reconstruction here.

No exhaustive novelty certification is claimed. All decisive deductions have independent hostile internal reconstruction by hessian_check12; this is not external validation.

The next route must leave this orbit and its base-additive extension in a specified way: use a variable dual-linear coefficient, a genuinely multivariate elementary driver, a joint word whose intermediate determinants fail but whose final determinant is restored, or a higher-rank potential repair with cancellations. It must then recheck the complete Hessian chain, full constant determinant, actual collision and the required polynomial critical section. Rational envelopes and genuinely nonlinear pivot charts also require their own hypotheses. The theorem does not justify a blind degree sweep inside the closed orbit.
