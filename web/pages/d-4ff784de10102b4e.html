| Statement | Parameter access | Error | Operational use |

The first two give a parameter-independent, two-way comparison of the whole

The stopped diffusion does not improve the unknown-parameter experiment
comparison. It constructs a different mixture for each supplied `A,b`, uses a

## 2. Consequences for critical metrology

Let `Theta` be the fixed compact set of symmetric `A` and vector `b`, and let
`P_theta` be the quartic law. Combining the two parameter-independent maps gives
two deficiencies bounded by `delta_N=C_(M,B)N^(-1/2)`. For `R` independent
blocks at one fixed unknown parameter, product channels and telescoping give

\[
\Delta(\{\rho_{N,\theta}^{\otimes R}\},\{P_\theta^{\otimes R}\})
 \le R\delta_N.
\]

Here `T(ρ,σ)=||ρ-σ||_1/2` and classical `TV` use the same convention.

Consequently every bounded-loss Bayes or minimax decision risk differs by at
most `R delta_N`. For `R=o(sqrt(N))` this tends to zero, including for a joint
POVM on all blocks. This statement is stronger than a comparison of selected
moments: it covers all bounded-loss decisions on the admitted experiment.

For the classical model, the natural sufficient statistic is
`T(x)=(x,xx^T)`. For fixed known `A`, the field score is `x-E_b X` and
`I_b=Cov_b(X)`. For joint unknown `(A,b)`, the Fisher matrix is the covariance
of these sufficient statistics pulled back to independent coordinates of
symmetric `A`; a coordinate `A_ij`, `i<j`, uses score `2x_i x_j`.
Quartic tails give finite moments of every order. The covariance is positive
definite: if a linear combination of the coordinates of `T(x)` had zero
variance, its linear-plus-quadratic polynomial would be constant on an open
set, forcing every coefficient to vanish. Continuity and compactness give a
uniform positive minimum eigenvalue on a fixed compact parameter set.
Thus the limiting classical experiment has ordinary `R^(-1/2)` parameter
estimation scale. At `A=b=0`, the field information per observation is the
exact value in my `INITIAL.txt`, `0.213523984564 I_3`.

For bounded squared loss on the compact parameter set, the transfer error is
additive `O(RN^(-1/2))`. To show this is negligible relative to the classical
`1/R` mean-square scale using only the uniform channel bound, it suffices to
take `R=o(N^(1/4))`. The weaker `R=o(sqrt(N))` still gives equivalence of
bounded risks in absolute value, but does not by itself prove a relative
`1/R` MSE approximation. This distinction is easy to miss.

I also derive a quantum lower bound on parameter acquisition that does not use
the many-copy channel approximation. Write the state as
`rho_theta=exp(K_0+G_theta)/Z_theta`, where a direction in parameter space
changes the exponent by

\[
G_{\Delta\theta}
 = Q_{\Delta A}(J)/N^{3/2}+\Delta b\cdot J/N^{3/4}.
\]

For a line segment that stays inside the admitted compact parameter set, the
log-partition Hessian in a direction `G` is the Bogoliubov--Kubo--Mori variance

\[
\psi''[G,G]=\int_0^1\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^s\widetilde G\rho^{1-s}\widetilde G)\,ds
 \le \mathrm{Tr}(\rho G^2).
\]

The inequality follows by diagonalizing `rho` and using the logarithmic-mean
bound `(lambda_i-lambda_j)/(log lambda_i-log lambda_j) <=
(lambda_i+lambda_j)/2`. The critical sector estimates in the supplied baseline
give, uniformly for fixed `M,B`,
`E[J^2]=O(N^(3/2))` and `E[(J^2)^2]=O(N^3)`. Also
`||Q_D(J)|| <= ||D||_op J^2` in each spin sector. Hence

\[
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho G_{\Delta\theta}^2)
 \le C_{M,B}(\|\Delta A\|_{op}^2+\|\Delta b\|^2),
\qquad
D(\rho_\theta\|\rho_{\theta+\Delta\theta})
 \le \tfrac12 C_{M,B}\|\Delta\theta\|^2.
\]

Relative entropy adds over `R` copies and quantum Pinsker then bounds their
trace distance by `(sqrt(C_(M,B))/2) sqrt(R)||Delta theta||`. A two-point
Le Cam argument therefore gives a minimax squared-error lower bound
`c_(M,B)/R` for estimating any nondegenerate unknown parameter coordinate,
even with a collective measurement on all `R` blocks. The constants are
independent of `N`. For the coefficient `h=b/N^(3/4)` multiplying `J`, the
corresponding lower scale is `N^(-3/2)/R` in mean-square error. This is an
information-theoretic lower bound on acquiring unknown parameters, not a
statement about the stopped-diffusion sampler.

Thus in the regime where the channel comparison also supplies an upper bound,
critical `b` acquisition has the same `Theta(1/R)` squared-error order as the
quartic observation. The theorem does not claim convergence of the infinitesimal
quantum Fisher matrix. The known-parameter exponential mixture cannot be used
as a parameter-free metrology decoder.

## 3. Multi-block state approximation

For fixed supplied `(A,b)`, c07_s02's stopped-diffusion claim gives
`T(rho_N,sigma_N)<=C exp(-cN)` for `N>=N0(M,B)`, with `sigma_N` fully
separable across the `N` qubits. For `R` independent blocks, tensorization gives

\[
T(\rho_N^{\otimes R},\sigma_N^{\otimes R})
 \le R C e^{-cN}.
\]

The surrogate remains fully separable across all `RN` qubits. Every POVM,
including a collective measurement across blocks, changes outcome TV by at
most this amount. Any bounded separability witness with operator norm at most
one has negativity at most `2RCe^(-cN)`. The approximation remains useful for
`R=exp(c'N)` with any fixed `c'<c`; it does not imply anything for `R` near or
above the unknown constant `c`.

If a single unknown parameter is shared across blocks and has prior `pi`,
convexity gives the same bound between
`int rho_(N,theta)^(tensor R) pi(dtheta)` and
`int sigma_(N,theta)^(tensor R) pi(dtheta)`. The latter is fully separable but
has classical correlations through the shared latent parameter. Constructing
it from a known prior requires drawing `theta` and then sampling its
parameter-dependent paths. For a fixed unknown `theta`, no parameter-free
algorithm for this stopped mixture follows. By contrast, the `R=o(sqrt N)`
quartic-experiment equivalence is parameter-independent and directly applies
to unknown-parameter decision tasks.

## 4. Tractable-simulation and acquisition ledger

**Known rational parameters.** The diffusion theorem supplies an existence
representation, not a sampler. Its law requires the heat-seed branch, a
three-dimensional stopped SDE, and size-biasing by
`w=exp(int V_N dt)`. The explicit bound is

\[
h_N=(2M+1)\sqrt N/4+(2M+1)/(2\sqrt N)+(B/2)N^{1/4},
\qquad 0<w\le e^{h_N}.
\]

The proof makes the normalized path weight at least a constant for sufficiently
large `N`, while the crude rejection envelope is `e^(h_N)`. Thus elementary
envelope rejection would have only an `exp(-O_(M,B)(sqrt(N)))` guaranteed
acceptance lower bound, even assuming exact path sampling and arithmetic. This
is a loose upper bound on that naive sampler's expected proposals, not a lower
bound on every possible algorithm. No polynomial-cost SDE path sampler,
weight sampler, interval integration, bit-cost bound, or hardware run is
supplied. The uniform covariance margin `D_C >= 3I/4` inside the stopped ball
does make the local diffusion square root well conditioned; it does not control
weight variance, Brownian discretization, stopping-time error, or normalization.

`A,b` must be supplied as exact rationals or through a charged precision
interface. The constants `M,B` are fixed public bounds, not growing with `N`.
The stopped proof's `O(exp(-cN))` is an accuracy theorem; it is not an
efficient quantum Gibbs preparation algorithm or classical sampler. The old
finite-bit product construction has a different positive local-filter sampler
and only its `O(N^(-3/2))+2 eta+epsilon` state error. Its stated real-arithmetic
work is `O_(M,B)(eta^(-3)+N)` and a separate polynomial bit construction is
audited but unexecuted. Setting `eta=exp(-cN)` there would cost exponential
work and still would not remove its `O(N^(-3/2))` theorem term. The old sampler
does not automatically implement the stopped SDE.

**Unknown parameters.** If `A,b` are learned from `R` copies rather than
provided, the relative-entropy argument above forces `Omega(epsilon^(-2))`
copies to estimate a scaled parameter to mean-square accuracy
`epsilon^2` (for an interior, nondegenerate parameter set). In the physical
field coefficient `h=b/N^(3/4)`, the lower scale is
`Omega(1/(N^(3/2) epsilon_h^2))` copies. In the channel-equivalence regime,
quartic observations give a matching parametric upper order, subject to the
more restrictive `R=o(N^(1/4))` if one wants the additive comparison error
negligible relative to MSE `1/R`.

The target Gibbs state itself is uniformly Lipschitz in the scaled parameters:
the same BKM/moment bound gives `T(rho_N(theta),rho_N(theta+u))<=L||u||`,
uniformly in `N`. Thus if a mathematical sampler at a rational `theta_tilde`
achieves `Ce^(-cN)` relative to `rho_N(theta_tilde)`, the triangle inequality
gives error at most `Ce^(-cN)+L||theta_tilde-theta||` for the target at the
true supplied parameter. No sensitivity bound on the particular mixture
`sigma_N` is needed for this target-error statement. Fixed-dimensional input
rounded to `q` bits per coordinate adds `O(2^-q)` state error; preserving a
target tolerance `e^(-aN)`, `a<c`, needs only `O(N)` supplied bits. This does
not make the SDE a finite-bit sampler: path, weight, stopping, and arithmetic
errors remain uncompiled. If parameters are unknown, learning scaled values
to `e^(-cN)` precision still requires `Omega(e^(2cN))` copies under the stated
measurement interface. Supply cost and acquisition cost must remain distinct.

**Simulation target.** A separable decomposition does not automatically make
arbitrary measurement simulation efficient. One must still sample the
weighted path law, represent the local density matrices at required precision,
and simulate the requested POVM. The forward/reverse maps give an
information-theoretic classical simulation for the specified Gibbs family,
but their coherent/Schur measurement and readout costs are not compiled. No
claim about local-lattice Gibbs states, dynamic protocols, preserved external
references, or arbitrary input states follows.
