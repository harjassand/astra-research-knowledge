## Exact interface and prior quantifiers

Let `G:C²→C²` be polynomial, `det DG=-2`, and `(G1,G2)=(x,y)`. These are precisely the normalized plane coefficient maps attainable by the permitted residual-affine completions. The component-ideal identity implies that the only finite point above0 is the reduced point0. Let `d=[C(x,y):C(G1,G2)]` be the generic degree.

## The exact missing-sheet implication

Define `A_G` to be the finite targets approached by `G(z_j)` along source sequences with `||z_j||→∞`. By Jelonek's results it is empty or an algebraic hypersurface. Because every Keller local multiplicity is1, Proposition 6 gives

\[
0\notin A_G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d=1.
\]

In this class `d=1` also means polynomial invertibility. One elementary proof of the usual birational Keller lemma is useful here. A birational inverse coordinate has the form `A(q)/B(q)` with coprime polynomials. If an irreducible factor `b` divides `B`, then `b(G)` is a nonconstant polynomial. A component of its zero set maps dominantly to the hypersurface `b=0`: the everywhere invertible derivative preserves dimension at a general smooth point. The identity `A(G)=x_i B(G)` forces `A` to vanish generically on `b=0`, contradicting coprimality. Thus all inverse denominators are constant.

Therefore every hypothetical nonautomorphism in the permitted completion class must be nonproper **at the origin itself**, not merely somewhere in the target.

More precisely, let a small target neighbourhood `V` admit the analytic local inverse at0, with inverse image in a neighbourhood `U` of0. For any finite radius `R`, the compact set

\[
\{z:\|z\|\le R\}\setminus U
\]

has no zero of `G`. Its image stays a positive distance from0. Consequently, when targets approach0, every inverse point outside the local inverse branch eventually leaves every fixed ball. At targets outside `A_G` there are exactly `d` distinct points. Exactly one remains on the inverse branch in `U`, and all remaining `d-1` points escape uniformly to infinity.

A generic complex line through0 is not a component of `A_G`, so a sufficiently small punctured disc on that line avoids it. The punctured-disc cover has one distinguished sheet whose monodromy is fixed, and `d-1` escaping sheets. This does not imply that global monodromy fixes that sheet: loops elsewhere can act differently. No global contradiction is inferred from the local fixed sheet alone.

## A discriminating algebraic certificate

Write `qi` for target coordinates, `[X0:X1:X2]` for projective source coordinates, and `Hi(X)` for the homogenization of `Gi` to its own degree `di`. Form the homogeneous graph-closure ideal over the target ring:

\[
J=\bigl(H_1-q_1X_0^{d_1},\ H_2-q_2X_0^{d_2}\bigr):X_0^\infty.
\]

The boundary fibre over0 is the projective variety of

\[
J+(X_0,q_1,q_2).
\]

It is empty exactly when both affine projective charts `X1=1` and `X2=1` give unit ideals after adding these equations. The saturation can be computed by introducing `s` and eliminating it from the generators together with `1-sX0`; it must occur **before** setting the targets to0.

If that boundary fibre is empty, its image under projective projection misses0. The boundary image is closed, so an open target neighbourhood of0 has proper finite graph projection. The one-point reduced fibre forces `d=1`, and the preceding birational lemma proves that `G` is a polynomial automorphism. Conversely an automorphism has no boundary fibre over any finite target. Thus, within this singleton-zero Keller class,

\[
G\text{ is an automorphism}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the saturated graph has no boundary above0}.
\]

This is an exact, candidate-specific acceptance gate. Computing only `(G1,G2)=(x,y)`, then projectively closing that already-specialized finite fibre, discards the missing sheets. Counting boundary **points** is also insufficient: several escaping sheets can approach one projective point, as the diagnostic below shows. No generic-degree or boundary emptiness claim follows from a finite sample of target values.

The alternative prior test from Jelonek's Proposition 7 is to obtain the primitive irreducible equation of each source coordinate over `C[q1,q2]`. Properness at0 is equivalent to nonvanishing at0 of both leading coefficients. An arbitrary resultant with extraneous factors cannot be substituted for those primitive equations without justification.

## Exact diagnostic using the published three-dimensional map

Use the prior Alpöge map displayed by Meng–Yang:

\[
F=(u^3z+y^2u(4+3xy),\ y+3xu^2z+3xy^2(4+3xy),\ 2x-3x^2y-x^3z),
\quad u=1+xy.
\]

The independent exact script checks `det DF=-2` and the stronger component-ideal equality `(F1,F2,F3)=(x,y,z)` by a Gröbner basis. Thus the finite zero fibre is the reduced origin.

For every nonzero `t`, the three inverse points of the target `(0,2t,0)` are exactly

\[
(0,2t,-16t^2),\qquad
(t^{-1},-t,5t^2),\qquad
(-t^{-1},2t,8t^2).
\]

The first tends to0; the other two escape. To prove exhaustiveness without sampling, if `x=0`, the target equations force the first point. If `x≠0`, `F3=0` gives `z=(2-3xy)/x²`, after which

\[
F_1=\frac{(1+xy)(xy+2)}{x^2},\qquad
F_2=\frac{4xy+6}{x}.
\]

Hence `xy=-1` or `-2`, giving the two escaping points. At `t=0`, neither nonzero-`x` branch is a finite point. Both escaping branches approach the same projective source point `[0:1:0:0]`.

The generic degree is3. On the dense chart use `w=x/u`, `r=1/u`, and targets `(a,b,c)`. Direct substitution yields

\[
a=(1+r)/w^2-c/w^3,\qquad
b=(4+2r)/w-3c/w^2,
\]

\[
2aw^3-bw^2+2w-c=0,\qquad
r=aw^2+c/w-1.
\]

The source coordinates recover rationally from `(a,b,c,w)`. Over `C(a,b)`, the expression `c=2aw³-bw²+2w` has degree3 as a polynomial in `w`, proving the field degree3. This diagnostic is algebraic, not numerical. It is a three-dimensional example from a published construction; it gives no plane Keller counterexample.

## Research-selection consequence and missing lemma

The finite zero ideal is guaranteed by the coefficient class and therefore is not a discriminating success signal. A proposed noninvertible member must exhibit the origin boundary or equivalent leading-coefficient degeneration as well as its nonzero constant Jacobian and an actual collision. Conversely, an empty boundary closes that candidate in every degree. This sharpens the search gate without resolving its hard step.

The open load-bearing question remains whether any normalized plane Keller map with component ideal `(x,y)` can have a nonempty graph boundary above0. The correspondence with all permitted completions is our programme deduction, independently audited; the properness criteria and missing-sheet mechanism are prior. Literature-wide priority for the completion converse remains unresolved. This cycle does not classify transformations nonlinear in the residual dual variables and does not assert that the plane singleton-zero subclass is equivalent to unrestricted JC2.
