# Amplitude-independent Gibbs comparison on a saturated region

Root derivation, 7 October 2026. **New deduction, independently reconstructed by pde_spectrum9 in cycle15.** Not a claim of historical novelty. The purpose is to remove perturbation-amplitude dependence from the new noncommuting-island thermal-memory route. This note does not prove generic extensive noncommuting stability.

## Abstract finite-dimensional lemma

Let the system factor as H_X tensor H_R. Let

    H0 = I_X tensor H_out + T,      0 <= T <= M I,
    H  = H0 + V_X tensor I_R,

where V_X is any finite Hermitian operator, with no norm-smallness premise. Let P be a projector such that

    P = I_X tensor P_R,
    [P, I_X tensor H_out] = [P,T] = 0.

Write p_P(K)=Tr[(I-P) exp(-beta K)]/Tr exp(-beta K), beta>=0. Then

    p_P(H) <= exp(2 beta M) p_P(H0).                 (1)

If v is any valid bound on ||V_X-cI|| for any scalar c, there is also the standard common-block comparison exp(2 beta v). Thus the smaller of the two factors is valid. This is a trace statement on invariant blocks, not operator monotonicity of exp.

**Proof.** Put K=I_X tensor H_out+V_X tensor I. Each of P and I-P reduces K,T,K+T. On either block, ordered eigenvalues satisfy lambda_j(K)<=lambda_j(K+T)<=lambda_j(K)+M by the min-max principle. Hence Z_bad(K+T)<=Z_bad(K), while Z(K+T)>=exp(-beta M)Z(K). Therefore p_P(H)<=exp(beta M)p_P(K).

The exact tensor factorization gives

    Z_bad(K)=Tr_X exp(-beta V_X) Tr_R[(I-P_R)exp(-beta H_out)],
    Z(K)=Tr_X exp(-beta V_X) Tr_R exp(-beta H_out).

The V_X partition function cancels: p_P(K)=p_P(I_X tensor H_out). Applying the same block eigenvalue bounds to H0=(I_X tensor H_out)+T in the reverse comparison gives Z_bad(H_out)<=exp(beta M)Z_bad(H0) and Z(H_out)>=Z(H0). Thus p_P(H_out)<=exp(beta M)p_P(H0), proving (1). The zero-rank blocks cause no difficulty. No commutation of T with V_X is required.

## Application to a supplied stabilizer memory

Take H0=sum_a h_a(I-C_a)/2, h_a>0. For a region X, let T sum exactly the checks whose whole supports meet X. Then H_out acts trivially on X, T>=0 and M<=sum_(a: support(C_a) meets X)h_a. If each qubit meets at most g checks of weight <=h_max, M<=g h_max |X|.

Let P1 be the original decoder's one-site-error good syndrome projector. For the complete Pauli group on X, phases ignored, define

    Q = intersection_F F^dagger P1 F.

For bath supports Y_alpha define

    P = intersection_(E in union_alpha Pauli(Y_alpha)) E^dagger Q E,

including identity. All these are intersections of commuting syndrome projectors. Conjugation by a Pauli on X permutes the full F inventory, also after an E conjugation (Pauli phases cancel). Thus P and Q commute with the entire matrix algebra on X and have the required tensor-factor form. They also commute with each original check, and therefore with T and H_out. The abstract lemma applies to both.

If reference single-error mass is p1 and any weight-w Pauli changes reference energy by at most g0 w, then the elementary union bound gives

    p_P(H) <= exp(2 beta M) 4^m M_B exp[beta g0(m+q)] p1,  (2)
    m=|X|, q=max_alpha |Y_alpha|,
    M_B <= 1 + sum_alpha (4^|Y_alpha|-1).

The bound can be capped by1. A sharper order of operations, independently derived by rounding_spectral and checked by root, pde_gate10 and pde_spectrum9, replaces (2) by

    p_P(H) <= 4^m M_B exp[beta(2M+g0 q)] p1.          (3)

Indeed first compare p1,out<=exp(beta M)p1. In rho(H_out), the X factor is maximally mixed, so every F_X conjugation has identical probability and incurs ZERO energy-translation cost. Only bath E costs exp(beta g0 q). Union-bound there, then compare the final saturated P from H_out+V to H with the remaining exp(beta M). The X partition again cancels because P is fully invariant on X. All intermediate projectors for the first comparison are syndrome functions reducing H_out and T. Formula (2) remains correct but is weaker.

Multiplying (2) or (3) by an amplitude factor would double-charge: exp(2beta M) REPLACES exp(2beta||V||). Distinct-support and syndrome-orbit deduplication may improve the inventory, but is not assumed here.

For p1<=C exp(-c Q_k), polynomial bath inventory, and M<=g0 m, the sufficient exponent margin is

    m(log4+2 beta g0)+beta g0 q+log M_B+log C < c Q_k.

Consequently m can be a sufficiently small fixed fraction of Q_k, even for arbitrarily strong noncommuting V_X, PROVIDED the actual bath-rate/coherent-cost envelope required by the dynamics theorem remains controlled. Large V can change these physical rates. The Gibbs-tail theorem alone does not establish storage, state preparation, or microscopic Davies validity.

## Why the logical and dynamical premises still matter

The independent workers' two-projector argument establishes full-frequency protection: the small space P and larger space Q reduce H, the bath maps P into Q, and original logicals commute H on Q. A separately cleaned and patched full logical Pauli algebra can commute H globally when X is a correctable erasure region. The old Chesi protected-encoding theorem can then use (3) without the weighted criterion's square-root tail loss. Root has read the primary Theorem1 and Sections6.1-6.2 prerequisites; the completed island/patch audit is separate from this elementary Gibbs lemma.

Cleaning |X|<d, decoder-good ground compatibility, and economical acquisition are distinct obligations. The original thermal encoder is not automatically the new one. Computing Q/P naively costs up to4^m M_B decoder queries, and base/new thermal preparation remains unacquired. This result makes a supplied-model theorem stronger; it does not infer an efficient experimental protocol.

## A noncommuting analytic fixture

On two qubits take H0=J(I-Z1 Z2)/2+Delta(I-Z2)/2 and V=u X1+w Y1+v Z1. Region X={1}, T=J(I-Z1Z2)/2, M=J, and bad projector=(I-Z2)/2. The bad mass is exactly

    p = [1 + exp(beta Delta) cosh(beta R_plus)/cosh(beta R_minus)]^(-1),
    R_plus=sqrt(u^2+w^2+(v-J/2)^2),
    R_minus=sqrt(u^2+w^2+(v+J/2)^2).

Reference p0=(1+exp(beta Delta))^(-1). Since |R_minus-R_plus|<=J and log(cosh) is1-Lipschitz, p<=exp(beta J)p0, stronger than the general lemma here. Taking u and v arbitrarily large leaves a bounded ratio; [V,H0]!=0 when u or w is nonzero. This is a finite illustration, not a simulation of the recursive memory.

## The orbit entropy cannot be dropped from this abstract argument

Let both X and R have dimension d=2^m, H_out=0, and let I-P1 be the rank-one maximally entangled projector on X tensor R. Pauli conjugation on X generates the d^2 orthogonal Bell states. Hence the saturated good projector Q is zero, although p1,out=1/d^2. The union amplification is exactly d^2=4^m. This can be a syndrome projector of the commuting Bell stabilizers. It shows optimality of the unrestricted saturation-entropy factor, not a no-go for geometrically structured decoder-good events or a counterexample to the recursive-memory theorem.
