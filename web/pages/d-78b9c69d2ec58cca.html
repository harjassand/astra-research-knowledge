# Bizeul–Klartag–Lehec KLS claim: scoped proof audit

## Verdict and scope

The current arXiv record for Bizeul, Klartag, and Lehec, **“Presenting a proof of the Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits conjecture,”** is v1, posted 2026-10-04 19:30:34 UTC. It claims a universal Poincaré bound for every isotropic log-concave probability measure, hence a full KLS proof if correct. The local source archive is byte-for-byte the versioned arXiv e-print (`sha256 c84a67e57d8dce8cea9c4b82b301b4129ac1582081e2d27cd7201b327f031318`). The submission is a source-reported claim, not an externally established theorem.

I reconstructed the decisive chain from the high-order tilt-average criterion through the cumulant induction and suspension. The main inequalities and quantifiers fit together, and I found no decisive counterexample or fatal algebraic error in that chain. The Taylor-doubling lemma suppresses a symmetrization comparison, repairable by an explicit triangle-and-contraction estimate with constants absorbed. The first-pass audit also flagged the integrated-energy endpoint; the follow-up below closes that stochastic-integrability step for each fixed dimension and order under the paper's compact-support reduction. The fixed-n,m constants justify the limit but do not enter the claimed universal induction constant. The correction history is retained in the addendum.

The Astra KLS entry N75 is a distinct covariance-only heat-flow route. It records a spectral plateau and identifies a missing scale-integrated observation mutual-information estimate, so that route remains open (`work/agents/probability_kinetics/N75.pinned.txt`, lines 5–10; `work/astra_prior/01_CORE.txt`, line 57). BKL’s mechanism does not bridge that information gap: it uses the Letwin third-cumulant estimate, all-order cumulant energies along stochastic localization, and the tilt-derivative criterion. This is a separate route to the same KLS target.

The current primary Letwin v1 source states, for isotropic log-concave (X) and symmetric (M),

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X^\top M X)\le 2\,\mathbb E|\nabla(X^\top M X)|^2=8\,\operatorname{tr}(M^2).
\]

The derivation of the third-cumulant estimate from that statement is short and correct: pair \(\kappa_3(u)\) with symmetric \(M\), interpret the pairing as \(\operatorname{Cov}(\langle X,u\rangle,X^\top M X)\), apply Cauchy–Schwarz and the quadratic-form estimate, and take the Hilbert–Schmidt dual supremum. This gives \(R_3\preceq 8I\).

## Exact theorem interfaces

**BKL main claim.** For every dimension \(n\ge1\), every isotropic log-concave probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R^n\) satisfies \(C_P(\mu)\le C\), with one universal \(C\). The formal theorem is `KLS5.tex`, lines 210–229. The proof first handles regular measures with a smooth potential whose Hessian is bounded above and bounded below by a positive measure-dependent constant; it then invokes Gaussian convolution, a strictly convex quadratic tilt, recentering, and covariance normalization to pass to arbitrary full-dimensional isotropic log-concave measures (`KLS5.tex`, lines 406–418). An isotropic measure is full-dimensional.

**Song–Zhang tilt criterion (as restated and reproved by BKL).** Let \(\mu\) be centered, regular, log-concave, with \(\operatorname{Cov}(\mu)\preceq I\). If one number \(R\ge1\) satisfies, for every \(f\in L^2(\mu)\) and every integer \(d\ge1\),

\[
\left|\mathcal T_d f\right|\le R^d\|f\|_2,
\qquad \mathcal T_d f=\frac1{d!}\nabla^d F_f(0),\quad
F_f(z)=\mathbb E_{\mu_z}f,
\]

then \(C_P(\mu)\le C R^2\) for a universal \(C\). The tensor norm is Hilbert–Schmidt; \(R\) must work for all degrees and all functions for the given measure. See `KLS5.tex`, lines 704–750.

**Cumulant bound used to meet the criterion.** For every isotropic log-concave \(\mu\) in every dimension and each integer \(m\ge2\), BKL claims

\[
R_m^\mu\preceq K^{m-1}((m-1)!)^2 I,
\qquad
\langle R_m^\mu u,u\rangle
=|\kappa_m^\mu(u,\cdot,\ldots,\cdot)|_{HS}^2,
\]

with one universal \(K\). This is Theorem `thm_cumulants` at `KLS5.tex`, lines 1310–1331.

**Suspension interface.** If the preceding cumulant bound of order \(d+1\) holds with constant \(b\) uniformly over every isotropic log-concave law and every dimension, then for every isotropic regular \(\mu\), every \(f\in L^2(\mu)\),

\[
|\mathcal T_d f|^2\le \frac{2b}{(d!)^2}\|f\|_2^2.
\]

The proposition is stated for \(d\ge2\) at `KLS5.tex`, lines 2019–2030. The proof handles centered unit functions orthogonal to affine functions first, and then adds the constant and linear subspaces explicitly.

## Reconstructed load-bearing argument

1. **The tilt criterion turns high-order control into a spectral-gap bound.** For a regular measure, write \(L=\Delta-\langle\nabla V,\nabla\cdot\rangle\), choose a normalized first nonconstant eigenfunction \(Lf=-\lambda_1f\), and build tensor-valued iterates
   \[
   h_{i+1}=\alpha_{i+1}(-L)^{-1}\nabla_0 h_i,
   \qquad
   \alpha_{i+1}=\frac{\|\nabla_0h_i\|_2}{\|\nabla(-L)^{-1}\nabla_0h_i\|_2}.
   \]
   Here \(\nabla_0\) centers each gradient component. The normalization gives \(v_i=\|\nabla h_i\|_2^2\) decreasing and \(p_i=v_i-v_{i+1}=|\mathbb E\nabla h_i|^2\), so \(\lambda_1=v_{N+1}+\sum_{i\le N}p_i\). The integrated Bochner identity and \(\nabla^2V\succeq aI\) give
   \[
   \|Lh_i\|_2^2\ge \|Lh_{i+1}\|_2^2+\chi_i+a v_i,
   \qquad \chi_i=\|\nabla^2h_i-\alpha_{i+1}\nabla h_{i+1}\|_2^2,
   \]
   hence \(v_i\to0\) and \(\sum_i\chi_i\le\lambda_1^2\). These definitions and identities are at `KLS5.tex`, lines 755–810 and 941–1000.

2. **Tilt integration by parts doubles Taylor degree while exposing only a controlled symmetry defect.** The exact identity \(F_{-Lu}(z)=\langle z,F_{\nabla u}(z)\rangle\) yields
   \[
   \mathcal S_{d+1}\mathcal T_d(\nabla_0h_{i+1})
   =\alpha_{i+1}\mathcal T_{d+1}(\nabla_0h_i).
   \]
   Stop at the first \(N\) with \(v_{N+1}<\lambda_1/2\). Then \(\alpha_i^2\le2\lambda_1\) for \(2\le i\le N\), and the adjacent-swap estimate from \(\chi_i\) controls the failure of \(\nabla_0h_i\) to be symmetric in its first \(q\) derivative slots. Combining these facts and the two-block tensor recovery lemma gives the Taylor-doubling estimate (equation `eq_taylor_doubling`, `KLS5.tex`, lines 1094–1175):
   \[
   |\mathcal T_d(\nabla_0h_i)|^2
   \le (C\lambda_1)^d|\mathcal T_{2d}(\nabla_0h_{i-d})|^2
   +\frac{C^dR^{2d}}{\lambda_1}
     \sum_{j=i-2d+1}^{i-1}\chi_j.
   \]
   The main term is a repeated use of the tilt recurrence. The error is the componentwise application of the assumed \(R^d\) bound to a tensor symmetrization error.

3. **A dyadic sum contradicts small \(\lambda_1R^2\).** The first Taylor coefficients control the total drop:
   \[
   \sum_{i=1}^Np_i\le\lambda_1^2+2\lambda_1 S_1,
   \qquad S_d=\sum_{i=1}^{N-d}|\mathcal T_d(\nabla_0h_i)|^2.
   \]
   Summing the doubling estimate gives \(S_d\le(C\lambda_1)^dS_{2d}+4dC^d\lambda_1R^{2d}\). If \(\lambda_1R^2\le(100C^2)^{-1}\), telescope over \(d=1,2,4,\ldots\) to obtain \(S_1<1/20\). Then \(\sum p_i<\lambda_1/2\), contrary to the stopping choice, which gives \(\sum p_i>\lambda_1/2\). Therefore \(\lambda_1\gtrsim R^{-2}\). The sum and contradiction are `KLS5.tex`, lines 1176–1288.

4. **Stochastic localization propagates the third-cumulant estimate to every order.** For compactly supported isotropic \(\mu\), the Eldan posterior \(\mu_t\) has mean \(a_t\), covariance \(A_t\), and
   \[
   da_t=A_t^{1/2}dB_t,\qquad
   dA_t=\sum_iH_{i,t}dB_{t,i}-A_tdt,
   \qquad \mathbb E A_t=e^{-t}I.
   \]
   Differentiating its log-Laplace SDE gives
   \[
   d\kappa_m(t)=\kappa_{m+1}(t)(\cdot,\ldots,A_t^{-1/2}dB_t)
       -(m\kappa_m(t)+L_m(t))dt.
   \]
   The product-rule remainder \(L_m\) contains only products of cumulants of orders 3 through \(m-1\). In the covariance-adapted norm \(|T|_t^2=\langle T,(A_t^{-1})^{\otimes r}T\rangle\), Itô’s formula and the third-cumulant matrix bound yield
   \[
   \operatorname{drift}\mathcal E_m(t)
   \ge\tfrac12\mathcal E_{m+1}(t)-Cm^2\mathcal E_m(t)
      -2\langle\kappa_m(t)(u),L_m(t)(u)\rangle_t.
   \]
   The source defines the process and derives these equations at `KLS5.tex`, lines 1385–1600, and proves the drift estimate at lines 1605–1800.

5. **The lower-order convolution closes with factorial cancellation.** Assuming the energy/cumulant estimates for orders below \(m\), each cardinality-\(a\) product in \(L_m\) satisfies
   \[
   \int_0^\infty\mathbb E|P_a(t)|_t^2dt\le2b_a b_{m-a+1},
   \quad b_r=K^{r-1}((r-1)!)^2.
   \]
   Multiplication by \(\binom{m-1}{a-1}\) cancels the two factorials exactly to \(\sqrt{b_m}\); the \(m-3\) cardinalities give \(\|L_m\|_{L^2(dt\,d\mathbb P)}\le\sqrt2(m-3)\sqrt{b_m}\). Integrating the drift then gives the induction coefficient
   \[
   \frac{2C}{K}\frac{m^2}{(m-1)^2}
   +\frac4{\sqrt K}\frac{m-3}{m-1}\le1
   \]
   for \(K\ge\max\{5,9C,64\}\). This establishes \(R_m\preceq b_mI\) uniformly in dimension and order. See `KLS5.tex`, lines 1838–2008.

6. **Suspension converts that factorial tensor bound into the criterion’s geometric growth.** For centered, unit \(f\perp\{1,x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\), take iid \(X_i\sim\mu\), independent Laplace noise \(\eta_\beta\), and
   \[
   S=\frac{N^{-1/2}\sum_i f(X_i)+\eta_\beta}{\sigma_\beta},
   \quad \sigma_\beta^2=1+2/\beta^2.
   \]
   For \(N\) large enough that \(\beta\|\nabla^2f\|_\infty/\sqrt N\le a\), the joint law of \((S,X_1,\ldots,X_N)\) is isotropic and log-concave. Mixed derivatives of its log-Laplace transform give the original \(\mathcal T_df\) in each repeated-copy block. Since the auxiliary cumulant norm includes those entries plus nonnegative entries involving the noise coordinate,
   \[
   b\ge |\kappa_{d+1}(e_0)|^2
       \ge\frac{(d!)^2}{\sigma_\beta^2}|\mathcal T_df|^2.
   \]
   Let \(\beta\to\infty\) (choosing a finite, larger \(N\) for each \(\beta\)), then use density in \(L^2\) and split off affine functions. With \(b=K^d(d!)^2\), this gives \(|\mathcal T_df|^2\le2K^d\|f\|_2^2\); together with the direct \(d=1\) covariance bound, take \(R=\sqrt{2K}\) in the criterion. This is `KLS5.tex`, lines 2009–2205.

## Adversarial checks and unresolved details

**Taylor-doubling symmetrization.** The text defines \(T=\mathcal T_d(G)\), \(\widetilde T=\mathcal T_d(\mathcal S_{2d}G)\), and applies block recovery to \(\widetilde T\). The needed quantity is \(|\mathcal S_{2d}\widetilde T|\), whereas the recurrence directly controls \(|\mathcal S_{2d}T|\). These quantities need not be equal, and contraction alone does not give \(|\mathcal S_{2d}\widetilde T|\le|\mathcal S_{2d}T|\). The valid bridge is

\[
|\mathcal S_{2d}\widetilde T|
\le |\mathcal S_{2d}T|+|\mathcal S_{2d}(\widetilde T-T)|
\le |\mathcal S_{2d}T|+|\widetilde T-T|.
\]

Substituting this into the block-recovery estimate adds a factor at most \(1+4^d\) to the already controlled symmetry error. Its square is exponential in \(d\) and is absorbed by increasing the universal \(C^d\) in `eq_taylor_doubling`. Thus the written transition is compressed and its literal “contraction” reading is insufficient, but the estimate follows with the advertised form after a routine constant change.

**Integrated-energy endpoint (first-pass note; superseded by follow-up below).** Lemma `lem_integrated_energy` integrates the drift to time \(T\) and then says integrability allows \(T\to\infty\). The finite-time formula has a terminal term \(\mathbb E\mathcal E_m(T)\), which cannot simply be dropped. From \(\int_0^\infty\mathbb E\mathcal E_m(t)dt<\infty\), choose \(T_j\to\infty\) with \(\mathbb E\mathcal E_m(T_j)\to0\), then use monotone convergence along \(T_j\). The stochastic integral also needs a localization argument to justify zero expectation and passage from stopped processes. This looks standard under the compact-support process setup, but BKL does not spell out the stopping construction or its uniform-integrability check. The lemma appears at `KLS5.tex`, lines 1803–1835.

**Scope of current reconstruction.** I checked the displayed high-order criterion proof, cumulant dynamics/drift/induction, suspension identities, and theorem interfaces. I did not independently re-prove Letwin’s 21-page quadratic-form theorem, all cited regularity/approximation facts, or all existence and integrability assertions for the Eldan process. The source itself states that most proofs and mathematical ideas were found with ChatGPT and that the authors take responsibility (`KLS5.tex`, lines 396–401); this disclosure is provenance, not external validation.

## Consequences if the proof survives external review

The claimed conclusion is a uniform spectral gap for every isotropic log-concave law. Standard Cheeger–Poincaré comparison converts it to dimension-free isoperimetry; standard concentration consequences give exponential tails for Lipschitz observables. Applying Poincaré to \(x\mapsto|x|^2\), with the usual truncation/domain argument, gives \(\operatorname{Var}|X|^2\le4C_P n=O(n)\). The result would therefore subsume the KLS-level sampling and conductance bounds discussed in the paper’s introduction, but BKL does not provide a new sampler, implementation, or explicit algorithmic cost analysis. The suspension’s auxiliary dimension \(N\) grows with the smoothing/noise parameter and \(\|\nabla^2f\|_\infty/a\); it is a proof construction, not an efficient algorithm.

## Follow-up: fixed-dimension stochastic integrability closure

This follow-up addresses the stopping/infinite-time gate, conditional on the compact-support localization setup explicitly adopted in BKL (KLS5.tex, lines 1378–1430). It does not establish the imported Letwin inequality or the theorem's other regularity and approximation steps.

Let nu be any isotropic log-concave law on R^n. For each coordinate, X_j is mean-zero, variance-one and log-concave. Murawski's Theorem 1.2 gives ||X_j||_Lp <= (p/2)||X_j||_L2 = p/2 for p >= 2; for 1 <= p <= 2, monotonicity gives ||X_j||_Lp <= 1 <= p. Thus ||X_j||_Lp <= p for all p >= 1. This is a primary-source moment comparison: [Murawski, Comparing moments of real log-concave random variables, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05210). By Holder, every coordinate product moment of order r <= m is bounded by m^r. The moment-cumulant partition formula therefore gives the explicit finite choice J_m = m^m sum over partitions pi of [m] of (|pi|-1)!, so every order-m coordinate cumulant has absolute value at most J_m. For a unit vector v, Cauchy-Schwarz over its first coordinate and then summing the remaining indices yields

    |kappa_m^nu(v, ., ..., .)|_HS^2 <= n^m J_m^2 =: C_{n,m}.

Indeed, for each remaining index tuple, |sum_i v_i kappa_{i,j_2,...,j_m}|^2 <= sum_i kappa_{i,j_2,...,j_m}^2, and there are n^m entries in the full order-m tensor.

By homogeneity, R_m^nu <= C_{n,m} I. This is a deliberately crude, fixed-dimension bound; it is not the desired dimension-free b_m estimate and invokes no KLS conclusion.

For BKL's posterior mu_t, write A_t = Cov(mu_t), a_t = E_{mu_t} X, nu_t = (A_t^{-1/2}(X-a_t))_# mu_t, and q_t = <A_t u,u> for the fixed unit vector u. Since nu_t is isotropic log-concave and BKL's affine cumulant identity holds (KLS5.tex, lines 1561–1578), pathwise

    E_m(t) = |kappa_m^{nu_t}(A_t^{1/2}u, ., ..., .)|_HS^2 <= C_{n,m} q_t.

For compactly supported initial mu, let D = diam(supp mu). Every posterior has the same support, so 0 < A_t <= D^2 I. From BKL's matrix inequality sum_i H_{i,t} A_t^{-1} H_{i,t} <= 8 A_t (KLS5.tex, lines 1589–1602), and A_t^{-1} >= D^{-2} I,

    sum_i H_{i,t}^2 <= D^2 sum_i H_{i,t} A_t^{-1} H_{i,t}
                    <= 8 D^2 A_t <= 8 D^4 I.

Hence sum_i ||H_{i,t}||_HS^2 <= 8 n D^4. For an increasing localizing sequence tau_k tending to infinity on every finite interval, the stopped covariance SDE reads

    e^(t wedge tau_k) A_(t wedge tau_k)
        = I + sum_i integral_0^(t wedge tau_k) e^s H_{i,s} dB_(s,i).

For each fixed T, the stopped integral has expected squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm at most 8 n D^4 integral_0^T e^(2s) ds, so it has mean zero. Also e^(T wedge tau_k) A_(T wedge tau_k) is bounded in norm by e^T D^2; dominated convergence as k tends to infinity gives E[e^T A_T]=I. This justifies E A_t = e^{-t} I from the SDE (KLS5.tex, lines 1414–1429), and therefore E q_t = e^{-t}. Consequently, uniformly over all times t >= 0, all compactly supported isotropic log-concave inputs in this fixed dimension satisfy

    E E_m(t) <= C_{n,m} e^{-t},
    integral_0^infinity E E_m(t) dt <= C_{n,m},
    lim_{T -> infinity} E E_m(T) = 0.

The same bounds hold at order m+1. In particular, the limit in the integrated-energy lemma can use every T -> infinity, not only a selected sequence.

It remains to justify the energy martingale. The first-order coefficient in BKL's normalized Ito expansion (KLS5.tex, lines 1723–1750) is

    g_{i,t} = 2<Ttilde_t,Wtilde_{i,t}>
              - <Ttilde_t, Sigma_{Htilde_{i,t}} Ttilde_t>,

where sum_i |Wtilde_{i,t}|^2 = E_{m+1}(t), |Ttilde_t|^2 = E_m(t), and BKL's metric estimate gives sum_i |Sigma_{Htilde_{i,t}} Ttilde_t|^2 <= 8(m-1)^2 E_m(t) (KLS5.tex, lines 1764–1794). Using (x-y)^2 <= 2x^2 + 2y^2,

    d<M>_t/dt = sum_i g_{i,t}^2
              <= 8 E_m(t) E_{m+1}(t) + 16(m-1)^2 E_m(t)^2.

Since q_t <= D^2, the fixed-dimension envelopes imply the explicit global bound

    E[<M>_infinity] <= D^2 (8 C_{n,m} C_{n,m+1}
                            + 16(m-1)^2 C_{n,m}^2) < infinity.

Since E[<M>_infinity] is finite, a localizing sequence sigma_k for this energy martingale satisfies M_(T wedge sigma_k) -> M_T in L^2 for each finite T: the squared error is the expected bracket increment after T wedge sigma_k, which tends to zero by dominated convergence. Thus M is a true square-integrable martingale and its expectation is zero. At finite T, all drift terms are integrable: the energy terms by the exponential envelope, and the mixed term by Cauchy-Schwarz in dt tensor dP, using the induction's integral_0^infinity E|L_m(t)(u)|_t^2 dt < infinity. Taking expectations in the stopped Ito inequality and removing the stops is therefore justified. Letting T -> infinity, the terminal term vanishes by C_{n,m} e^{-T} -> 0, while the nonnegative energy integrals converge monotonically and the mixed integral converges absolutely. This proves the endpoint inequality used at KLS5.tex, lines 1802–1835.

Quantifier boundary: the envelope C_{n,m} is uniform in time and in the input law only after fixing n,m; the martingale bound also depends on the compact support diameter D. This suffices to justify each induction step for every fixed dimension/order and every compactly supported input. It is not a claimed dimension-free, order-uniform integrability estimate. The induction's resulting b_m = K^(m-1)((m-1)!)^2 remains dimension-free because the auxiliary C_{n,m} constants are used only to legitimize expectations and limits, not in the numerical induction coefficient (source lines 1841–2005).
