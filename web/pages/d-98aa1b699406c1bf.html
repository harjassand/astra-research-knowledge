STATUS

The supplied network-specific drift argument is a plausible, direct application of the classical CTMC Foster criterion on a strict, rate-dependent feasible region. The identified general network theorems do not already prove the full claim: the binary and tier hypotheses fail on an explicit reachable catalyst-free tier, and the rank-one / bimolecular results do not apply. Complex-balance product form covers a special equality submanifold only. A published result already contains the single autocatalytic edge A+B <-> 2A+B (the k=1 case), but in a different network where B is conserved. The reported two-species recurrence theorem would cover the r=s=1 case if its proof is formally available and applies as described; the author project page currently lists the manuscript as in preparation. 1. ASSIGNED OBJECT AND DIRECT DRIFT CHECK

Species are A_j (j=1,...,r) and B_i (i=1,...,s), with r,s >= 1; all listed rate constants are positive, k_ji is an integer >= 1, and the stochastic model uses standard falling-factorial mass-action propensities. The reactions and rates are

  0 <-> A_j                 (alpha_j, delta_j)
  A_j <-> A_j+B_i           (p_ji, q_ji)
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i   (u_ji, v_ji).

The candidate has positive ell_j,w_i with

  delta_j ell_j > sum_i p_ji w_i                  for each j,
  q_ji w_i > k_ji u_ji ell_j                     for each (j,i),

and V(a,b)=sum_j ell_j a_j + sum_i w_i b_i + K 1_{a=0}, K>max_j ell_j.

For the linear part V0, direct use of the stochastic mass-action generator gives

  L V0 = sum_j alpha_j ell_j
         - sum_j (delta_j ell_j - sum_i p_ji w_i) a_j
         - sum_{j,i} (q_ji w_i - k_ji u_ji ell_j) a_j b_i
         - sum_{j,i} k_ji v_ji ell_j (a_j)_{k_ji+1} b_i,

where (n)_m=n(n-1)...(n-m+1), zero when n<m. The last term is nonpositive. The indicator contributes -K sum_j alpha_j at a=0, leaving L V = sum_j alpha_j(ell_j-K)<0 there, for every b. Its only positive jump correction is at a=e_j (all other catalysts zero), where A_j->0 adds K delta_j; this correction is uniformly bounded. Thus, under the strict inequalities, states with a != 0 have negative drift tending to -infinity if any active a_j or any b_i tends to infinity; on a=0 the drift is a fixed negative constant. V is positive and norm-like. The same identity gives a global upper bound on L V (the positive boundary correction is bounded); since V has a positive minimum, L V<=C V for some C>=0, which supplies the standard non-explosion Lyapunov condition. The candidate therefore yields drift at most some -c<0 outside a finite set (rescale V if a -1 convention is desired), and the usual physical-time Foster theorem gives positive recurrence of the irreducible class. This is a family-specific sufficient recurrence proof, conditional on the algebra and routine CTMC hypotheses being checked. It says nothing when the inequalities have no feasible positive weights; they are sufficient, not asserted necessary.

The parameter restriction is genuine. For r=s=1, k=1, choose alpha=delta=v=1, p=1/2, q=3, u=1/10, ell=w=1. Both strict inequalities hold. Complex balance would require c_A=alpha/delta=1 and c_A=u/v=1/10, which is impossible. So the strict Foster region is not just the complex-balanced subfamily.

The reaction graph has one linkage class: every edge is reversible and every complex connects through A_j to 0. Its stoichiometric rank is r+s, since the vectors e_Aj and e_Bi occur. There are 1+r+2rs distinct complexes, hence deficiency s(2r-1)>0. The largest source order is max_{j,i}(k_ji+2) >= 3. The full lattice is one communicating class: clear B counts while a catalyst is present using A_j+B_i->A_j, then clear A counts via A_j->0; from 0 create a helper A_1, create any requested B counts via A_1->A_1+B_i, remove the helper, and create requested catalyst counts. Thus the catalyst-free states (0,b) belong to the class and are not closed faces, despite being decisive for tier tests.

2. EXACT PRIOR-THEOREM COMPARISON

Anderson–Kim, Theorem 4.2 (2018) is a rate-independent tier criterion: for every tier sequence x_n, the top stochastic tier T_S,1 must intersect D_{x_n}, the source complexes of deterministic-tier-descending reactions; then each state in each closed irreducible component is positive recurrent, with finite expected entrance time to the union of such components. Corollary 4.3 assumes D_{x_n} nonempty and T_D,1=T_S,1 for every tier sequence. For any fixed i, use the reachable sequence x_n=(a=0,b=n e_i). All sources containing A_j have stochastic propensity zero, so T_S,1={0}. Under the D-tier comparison (x_n vee 1)^y, the top tier consists of complexes containing B_i, including A_j+B_i and (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i; 0 is not in T_D,1. The reaction A_j+B_i -> A_j is D-descending, but its stochastic propensity is zero on x_n. Hence T_S,1 intersect D_{x_n} is empty, while T_D,1 != T_S,1. Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.3 do not cover this family. The same sequence is a proper tier sequence in the exact Definition 5.3 of ACK (reachable states, coordinate limits, at least one coordinate diverging, both tier partitions present).

ACK (Anderson–Cappelletti–Kim), Theorem 6.1 (2020), assumes a weakly reversible single-linkage mass-action system and, for every proper tier sequence, T_S,1 subset T_D,1. The family has the graph hypotheses, but the same sequence has 0 in T_S,1 and not in T_D,1, so the tier premise fails. Their Theorem 4.1 instead assumes weak reversibility, a single linkage class, binary complexes (all complex orders <=2), and {S,2S} intersect C nonempty for every species S. Here the high complex has order k_ji+2>=3, and there is no pure B_i or 2B_i complex. Theorem 4.1 also fails. Their method studies a fixed number of embedded jumps; it should not be conflated with the supplied physical-time, rate-weighted generator drift.

Anderson–Kim, Theorem 3.1, is the generic CTMC Foster conclusion: if a finite K exists and a positive V has AV<=-1 outside K, then every state in each closed irreducible component is positive recurrent and expected time to the closed-class union is finite. This is the general theorem used by the proposed certificate, not a family-specific prior theorem. The same paper's structural theorems (2.1, 2.2) are binary; the single-linkage inflow/outflow result requires all inflows and outflows, absent for each B_i, and the double-full result is likewise binary.

ACKN, Theorem 7.2 (Anderson–Cappelletti–Kim–Nguyen, 2020), gives a positive-recurrence class built from a strongly endotactic core plus, for each species S_i, a nonempty set of pure-source drains a S_i -> sum r'_j S_j with a>=m-1 and product order <=a-1 (m is the maximal source order of the core), along with permitted low-order R'' reactions; it also imposes max product order in R' < min source order in R'. This family is strongly endotactic (indeed, a one-linkage weakly reversible network is strongly endotactic), but contains no pure B_i-source depletion reaction at all, and its A_j->0 drains are unary while the full core has source order >=3. Theorem 7.2 therefore does not match the family. Its binary fully-open corollary also fails: the network is not binary and B_i->0 is absent. ACKN's strongly-endotactic transient/explosive examples are not weakly reversible; they are not counterexamples to the weakly reversible recurrence conjecture and must not be substituted for one.

Complex balance/product form is a special case, not a general-rate solution. Anderson–Craciun–Kurtz, Theorem 4.1 (2010), says that if the deterministic standard mass-action system has a positive complex-balanced equilibrium c, then the stochastic standard mass-action chain has product-Poisson stationary law; on a closed irreducible class its restriction is the stationary law. Their deficiency-zero theorem supplies an all-rate complex-balance guarantee for weakly reversible deficiency-zero networks; this family has positive deficiency. Since this graph is a tree with every edge bidirected, complex balance is equivalent to edgewise balance and requires

  c_Aj = alpha_j/delta_j,
  c_Bi = p_ji/q_ji for every j (so this ratio is independent of j),
  c_Aj^k_ji = u_ji/v_ji for every (j,i).

These equalities are not implied by the strict Foster inequalities (see the non-complex-balanced numerical choice above). The product-Poisson result therefore covers an equality submanifold only; it does not invalidate a non-complex-balanced Foster proof in the strict region. For non-explosion in this special case see ACKK (2018), “Non-explosivity of stochastically modeled reaction networks that are complex balanced,” which proves non-explosivity for complex-balanced stochastic mass-action systems.

Do not conflate this with the broader existence of a stationary measure. Wiuf–Xu, “Any Stochastic Reaction Network has a Stationary Measure” (arXiv:2312.07590v1, posted 11 Dec 2023; the HTML manuscript dateline is 24 Aug 2026), proves a nonzero stationary measure on every closed irreducible component of a general CTMC of the stated type, and observes the result for weakly reversible SRNs. The measure need not be finite/normalizable; the paper explicitly says positive recurrence is still needed to infer a stationary distribution. Thus it does not prove recurrence or furnish the probability law needed here.

Wiuf–Xu, Corollary 4.10 (2023 version), proves positive recurrence for weakly reversible mass-action SRNs under H2–H3 on every positive irreducible component: H2 is stoichiometric rank dim S=1, and H3 requires reactions with positive and negative projection on the one-dimensional reaction direction. This family has rank r+s>=2, so the one-dimensional result does not apply, despite having two species when r=s=1.

Xu, arXiv:2409.05340v2 (metadata version date 2026-05-09; the HTML manuscript dateline says 2026-08-24), Theorem 4.6 proves regularity/non-explosivity for second-order weakly reversible stochastic mass-action systems; Theorem 4.13 says a bimolecular WR system is positive recurrent iff every communicating class has a stationary distribution. Neither proves stationary-distribution existence for this family. Its maximal source order is k+2>=3, so both the second-order and bimolecular hypotheses fail. The version's mixed dates are recorded because the public HTML itself has this inconsistency.
The same v2 gives a termolecular weakly reversible example (Example 4.19) for which no linear Lyapunov function can establish regularity; the text explicitly cautions that third-order WR systems may lack such a linear regularity witness. This is not a transience or non-recurrence example. It is relevant only as a limitation on linear regularity certificates, and does not cover the present rate-weighted, boundary-corrected candidate.

3. CLOSE AUTOCATALYSIS, ENZYME, AND TWO-DIMENSIONAL SOURCES

Cappelletti–Wiuf (2016), Example 5, contains the exact reversible autocatalytic edge A+B <-> 2A+B (our k=1 edge), together with A <-> 2A. B is conserved in that example; on each fixed-B class, it reduces to the one-species A <-> 2A chain with effective rates depending on the fixed B count. It has two linkage classes and no 0<->A immigration/death pair or A<->A+B substrate-birth/death pair. This is a close edge-level antecedent, not the full multi-catalyst/multi-substrate family or its weighted certificate.

Hoessly–Mazza (2019), “Stationary Distributions and Condensation in Autocatalytic Reaction Networks,” gives product-form stationary distributions for a specified autocatalytic class of mostly non-weakly-reversible networks, subject to mass-exchange reversibility, one monomolecular linkage class, matching molecularity/rate constraints across catalyst channels, and a reversible-chain stationary-weight condition. The paper states these CRNs are mass-preserving, so classes have fixed total molecule count. The present family changes total population through 0<->A_j and through A_j+B_i<->(k+1)A_j+B_i, and is an all-edge reversible single linkage class. The stated Hoessly–Mazza class does not include it. Hoessly (2021) later gives a decomposition/join framework for product-form stationary laws when component-chain laws have compatible shared-species factors; this is relevant prior art, but no compatible product-form decomposition of the assigned family for the proposed strict non-complex-balanced rate region has been established here.

The textbook stochastic enzyme-substrate scheme S+E <-> SE -> E+P appears as the standard example in Anderson–Kim (2018, Example 2.1). In that usual form the product-release step is irreversible, so it is not a weakly reversible network. Closed enzyme mechanisms often also have finite conserved-total classes, where classwise positive recurrence is a finite-state fact. Such models are not a substitute for this open, irreducible, unbounded state-space CTMC. This audit located no primary source proving the exact assigned family by an enzyme/autocatalysis theorem; absence from this bounded search is not a priority result.

Agazzi–Anderson–Cappelletti–Laurence–Mattingly 2D result: the current author-hosted ConStRAINeD publications page states that a complete proof exists for every two-species weakly reversible SRN and every positive rate vector, sketches a Wentzell–Freidlin/coarse-graining plus patched-Lyapunov strategy, explicitly says the arbitrary-dimensional conjecture remains open, and lists “A proof of the chemical recurrence conjecture in two dimensions” as In preparation. Cappelletti's Feb. 15, 2023 seminar page and the official INFORMS APS 2023 Book of Abstracts report a two-dimensional proof by adapted Foster–Lyapunov methods; an Andrea Agazzi APS25 abstract makes the same two-dimensional claim. The author-hosted 2022+ slides show an unrestricted-looking theorem line, but later call the result a “first step” and list high-dimensional proof questions. Therefore these are SOURCE-REPORTED presentation/project claims, not a formally inspectable proof manuscript. Do not promote them to independently verified theorem status. If r=s=1, this source-reported result would subsume the entire two-species case (all positive rates); if r+s>=3, it does not apply.

No primary-source weakly reversible transient/explosive counterexample was found in this bounded check. The current ConStRAINeD project page explicitly says the full arbitrary-dimensional WR conjecture remains open. The stochastic strongly-endotactic counterexamples in ACKN are not WR. The 1D result and the source-reported 2D result leave dimensions >=3 unresolved in the general all-rate WR problem. This observation is an audit boundary, not a proof that no counterexample exists.

4. NOVELTY BOUNDARY / WHAT IS AND IS NOT CLAIMED

Already covered by established results:

* The logical implication from a proper norm-like Foster function with strictly negative outside-finite-set physical-time drift to positive recurrence is standard CTMC theory (Anderson–Kim Theorem 3.1; also ACKN Theorem 7.3).
* Every weakly reversible one-linkage graph is strongly endotactic; deterministic endotactic permanence and stochastic CTMC recurrence are distinct properties.
* Complex-balanced rates on this graph have the standard product-Poisson stationary law; the exact complex-balance equalities are given above.
* The k=1 autocatalytic reversible edge is known in a different B-conserving network (Cappelletti–Wiuf Example 5).
* The two-species all-rate WR statement is source-reported by the authors but not available here as a formal proof.
* A nonzero stationary measure on every closed communicating class is a general Markov-chain fact (Wiuf–Xu, arXiv:2312.07590v1); it may have infinite total mass and therefore does not establish positive recurrence.

Not covered by the checked general recurrence theorems:

* The strict weighted region with arbitrary positive rates and non-complex-balanced choices in this full-rank, order>=3, single-linkage network.
* The discontinuous indicator correction K 1_{a=0} used to force negative drift on the catalyst-free high-substrate states while absorbing the bounded positive correction on catalyst-death jumps.
* The generalized r-catalyst/s-substrate family as a dimension>=3 WR recurrence theorem.

The candidate is a **conditional/family-specific rate-region theorem**, not an all-rate WR theorem. Its mathematical content, if fully checked, is the explicit sufficient inequalities and boundary-corrected certificate. The Foster criterion itself is not new. Since the exact family and certificate have not been cleared against all published and unpublished literature, novelty/priority is UNKNOWN. No claim of a breakthrough, universal WR recurrence, or necessity of the inequalities is supported.

The assigned family is standard stochastic mass action with falling factorial propensities. It must not be conflated with prior card N33's separate deterministic ODE candidate interface: the card states dx/dt=sum_e k_e(t)x^{y_e} nu_e with measurable bounded-positive coefficient paths and a classwise common compact convex absorber; real/negative source exponents are admitted there. N50 is an ODE global-certificate recognizer candidate. Neither card is a theorem about CTMC propensities kappa x^{underline y}, non-explosion, or positive recurrence. This note records the model boundary only; it does not revisit ODE permanence novelty.
