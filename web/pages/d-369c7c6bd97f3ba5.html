0. STATUS, EXACT SCOPE, AND INPUTS

This is a reconstruction of a restricted square analogue of the pinned
triangular coupling.tex c:comparison. It does not import that whole theorem.
The restriction is important: the annealed original marginal laws are
zero-pinned hard-Lipschitz laws on finite free vertex sets in the FULL
square lattice, with all exterior sites (and any original internal pins)
fixed to zero. Thus both fields have globally 1-Lipschitz zero extensions.
An adapted
revealing transcript may subsequently freeze coordinates, provided its
conditional marginal kernels are the corresponding exact Gibbs kernels.
The deterministic tested macrobox has a strictly larger common FREE buffer
after revealing. No arbitrary nonzero-boundary/sign-wall sparse law is
assumed or proved here. The finite macro-average theorem is a supplied,
internally reconstructed and separately audited premise, not an external
certification of the square scaling theorem.

Write Q_r=x0+[-rn,rn]^2 intersect Z^2, and fix
  0<r_*<r<s<R<R_+<infinity.
Both replicas have every site and edge of Q_(R_+) free, with no revealed
sites, original pins, or individual walls there. Original ambient finite
graphs may differ and can have arbitrarily large diameter. Each original
free component connects to zero pins. Their uniform Lipschitz polytopes
have positive full-dimensional volume in the free coordinates. Constants
below depend on the displayed fixed radii and local testing parameters,
not on ambient volume, diameter, or the number of components.

For each transcript construct a stationary synchronous NORMAL-REFLECTED
joining of its two conditional uniform laws. Common free coordinates are
driven by the same variance-two Brownian noise. Coordinates free in only
one replica can use additional independent Brownian coordinates. Set
  z_t=h_t-h'_t on the common free buffer,
  c0=the chi^2-weighted average of z_0, for a fixed deterministic local
     cutoff chi supported in Q_R and one on a square Q_* of radius>=r_*,
  F=z_0-c0.
All centers used in this document have deterministic weights of total
mass one, maximum O(n^-2), and support in Q_R. The reference square has
radius bounded below by r_*. Thus |F|<=C n throughout Q_R by Lipschitzness.

1. ACTUAL STATIONARY SYNCHRONOUS REFLECTION

For a fixed transcript the conditional finite polytopes are compact
convex. Normal reflection has a pathwise unique solution. Indeed, for two
solutions driven by the same path, monotonicity of the normal cone makes
d||X-X'||^2 nonpositive; equal initial states give uniqueness. P1,P3
identify the reflected marginal semigroup with the Neumann diffusion.
The latter preserves the normalized Lebesgue law and is reversible.

The synchronous product process is Feller by fixed-body driver/initial
continuity. Start from any joining of the two invariant marginals and
time-average its laws. Compactness supplies a subsequential stationary
joining. Every averaged law has the required two marginals, so the limit
does too. Integrate over transcripts. Conditional parameters need only be
measurably disintegrated; alternatively take a weak limit of joint laws
including the unchanged transcript coordinate. Recompute all good-box
marks on the resulting initial pair. No marks from the unrejoined pair
are carried over. Estimates are annealed, not uniform for every transcript.
Fix this joining ONCE. All later defect levels and step lengths test this
same stationary joint process; their initial pair law is not changed.
It has a two-sided stationary extension with fresh Brownian innovations
independent of the joined forward past (and transcript), obtained from the
strong Markov transition driven by independent Brownian increments.
Conditional polytopes have full dimension a.s. in their remaining free
coordinates: a uniform original sample has strict free constraints a.s.,
and its finitely many queried values therefore admit a strict remaining
filling. Null transcripts may be assigned any regular conditional version.

2. SHORT STEP, GLOBAL TRANSMISSION ANIMALS, AND SPATIAL MOMENTS

P1,P2 give, for each marginal coordinate on a stationary time interval,
  X_i(t)-X_i(0)=1/2 M_i(t)
                 +1/2 [Mhat_i(T-t)-Mhat_i(T)].
In each time direction the vector martingale has bracket 2t I, hence is
a variance-two Brownian vector with independent coordinates by Levy's
criterion. Forward and reverse vectors need NOT be independent. Use the
safe estimate sup|X_i(t)-X_i(0)|<=2(sup|M_i|+sup|Mhat_i|).
For variance-two scalar Brownian motion,
  P(sup_(t<=T)|M(t)|>a)<=4 exp(-a^2/(4T)).
If j distinct coordinates all move by more than d, every coordinate has a
forward or reverse witness above d/4. At least j/2 witnesses use the same
direction. Summing the 2^j assignments and using independence only in
that direction gives
  P(all j move>d)<=[4 exp(-d^2/(128T))]^j.                 (2.1)
A fixed enlargement contains K_L sites. Coloring the block grid into J
classes makes the site sets disjoint in each class. Consequently the
all-finite-set movement bound has base
  beta(T)=[4 K_L exp(-d^2/(128T))]^(1/J),
once the bracket is below one. This tends to zero with T, uniformly in
ambient finite volume. The same argument is valid conditional on the
transcript; its constants do not depend on pinned values.

P4 controls near-extremal original edges on the ENTIRE original graph,
including pin collars. Each block has finitely many possible edge
witnesses; a fixed coloring makes witnesses distinct. Thus the event E_B
that any relevant initial edge has slack<s0 has an all-set base
rho_edge(s0) tending to zero as s0 tends to zero. Random subsequent query
pins cause no problem: the remaining free-free contacts are a subset of
these original edge events, and the initial annealed marginal is the
original zero-pinned law. Different replicas need not be independent.

After fixing S1's L and enlargements, choose s0<=s_initial small enough
for the desired GLOBAL CAP edge-event base. This relaxes the initial
good-box strictness requirement and leaves all S1 marks and long-pair
tests unchanged; it does not enlarge L again. Take d=s0/8 and contact
tolerance tau=s0/4. If an edge with initial slack
at least s0 becomes tau-close to contact, one endpoint moves by more than
d. Every transmission box therefore has one of FOUR witnesses:
initial near-edge in replica 1 or 2, or movement in replica 1 or 2.
For k boxes, one witness type occurs on at least k/4 boxes. Union over the
4^k assignments gives the all-set bound
  P(A subset DynamicBad)<=theta^|A|,
  theta=4 max(rho_edge(s0),beta(T))^(1/4).                 (2.2)
No independence between witnesses is asserted. Choose s0 and then T so
64 theta<1, or as much smaller as any later geometric requirement needs.

An overestimating transmission graph may include original contacts to
subsequently frozen sites. These sites supply inputs, not actual free
averaging paths; overestimating the component only enlarges H. Every
nontrivial transmission component lies in a star-component of DynamicBad
blocks, with H<=C_L times its block size. Singletons have H=1. Hence all
local H moments have uniform exponential tails, including outside the
tested buffer, without an ambient-volume prefactor. A component meeting
Q_s and reaching beyond Q_R has size at least c n(R-s), so its local
crossing probability is at most C n^2 exp(-c n(R-s)).

The spatial concentration proof is reconstructed from the exposed Luna
hole report, SHA22539bb68d483a31a9e6c51f65ed4387caa543057c5638530c6c17ce7d8961af.
For complete disjoint bad components of exact sizes m_i, use the joint
inclusion bound on their union. Rooted star animals have at most 64^m
shapes (depth-first walks of length 2(m-1)). If Z_k counts components
k<=m<2k whose hulls meet a square of side N, then, for k<=A log N,
  P(Z_k>=r)<=[C N^2/r (64 theta)^k]^r.                    (2.3)
The probability uses only DISJOINT component tuples; their count is then
upper-bounded by the count of all tuples. This avoids an independence
assumption or a factor-of-two entropy loss.
Hull diameter is O(m) and incidence area O(m^2). A spatial power r0 of
diameter, cluster size, or largest incident cluster is bounded by a fixed
constant plus C sum_components (1+m)^(r0+2). Put
  r_k=ceil(N^2 k^(-(r0+4))).
For k beyond a fixed cutoff, (2.3) is at most
exp[-c N^2/k^(r0+3)]. On Z_k<r_k, the normalized contribution is bounded
by C/k^2+C k^(r0+2)/N^2. Dyadic summation is finite. Components larger
than A log N cost C N^2 exp(-c A log N); choose A for any desired inverse
power. Smaller fixed sizes have a deterministic spatial bound.
Thus every specified finite spatial moment has a deterministic threshold
outside O(N^-D). The threshold can be chosen independently of D for all
N above a D-dependent cutoff: the main dyadic sum/cutoff depend only on
the moment and theta, and the rounding term tends to zero for every
fixed A. This avoids a circular choice of analytic constants versus the
exceptional exponent in the iteration below.
Union over the polynomially many integer subboxes with radius>=sqrt(n)
gives the simultaneous assertion. A macroscopic eligible buffer suffices;
a fixed-width buffer would not. This step uses all-finite-set inclusion,
not merely one-root exponential tails or constant-probability geometry.

Let I contain the transcript and the rejoined initial pair. For any

because E[X|I]<=M+E[X 1_(X>M)|I] and conditional Markov bounds the latter

All necessary orders are finite and chosen before n. The truncation

3. PROJECTION AVERAGING AND CONSERVATIVE LOSS

Projection onto a finite difference-constraint polyhedron is piecewise
affine. On a fixed active face its derivative averages the input
derivatives within every saturated edge component. A component touching
a fixed input has that input derivative instead. For an insulated common
free component, no such input occurs. Interpolating the two preprojection
inputs, then integrating the a.e. derivative, gives a convex mixture A
of orthogonal averaging projections. Each A is symmetric, positive
semidefinite, entrywise nonnegative, and doubly stochastic on that
component. In particular it is a positive contraction and
  ||v-Av||_2^2<=||v||_2^2-||Av||_2^2.                    (3.1)
This local calculation does not require the two ambient free sets to
coincide. At a fine projection step the active constraints of either
endpoint process stay inside its endpoint near-contact components.
On an insulated common free component C, the projection KKT conditions
then involve only the SAME internal graph constraints in both replicas.
Restricting the two endpoint projections to C is exactly projection onto
that common internal constraint polyhedron: all cross-component
constraints are inactive, and their multipliers vanish. Interpolate these
two LOCAL inputs, rather than variable-dimensional ambient polytopes.
All averaging is consequently on C. Uniform convergence of the finite
projection approximations ensures that every endpoint active edge is
included in the fixed-tolerance continuous transmission graph for fine
enough mesh. P3 passes the estimates to continuous normal reflection.

Compositions have nonnegative doubly stochastic coefficients. Jensen's
variance identity bounds the variance in EVERY intermediate output row
by the total conservative loss
  D_C=sum_(x in C) z_0(x)^2-sum_(x in C) z_T(x)^2>=0.
Every intermediate value is within sqrt(D_C) of an initial level, and
each elementary move is at most sqrt(D_C) by (3.1). A gap>4 sqrt(D_C)
between successive initial levels cannot be crossed. There are H_C
levels. Therefore
  sup_t max_(x in C)|z_t(x)-z_0(x)|<=C H_C sqrt(D_C).       (3.2)
The loss and displacement estimate are invariant under adding a constant.
The mean on C is conserved, and the diameter Lipschitz bound also gives
  |z_t(x)-z_0(x)|<=2 diam(C),
  D_C<=C H_C diam(C)^2.                                  (3.3)
An arbitrary doubly stochastic contraction would NOT suffice: the
two-coordinate swap has D=0 and nonzero displacement. Positive elementary
averaging, not merely double stochasticity of their product, is essential.

4. FIXED-BOX FACETS, BROWNIAN FORCING, AND INSULATION

Work in one eligible enlarged square box, with all its sites free. In
relative coordinates y_i=h_i-h_i(0), original directed edge constraints
are y_j-y_i<=a_ij, with s0<=a_ij<=2-s0. On the outer collar impose
auxiliary |y_i|<=r0<s0/8, only to construct a controlled path. Every site
has an original path to the collar. The resulting fixed-dimensional body
contains a uniform ball around zero and has diameter bounded by C N,
where N is the fixed site count. Delete redundant inequalities. For
difference constraints, implication bounds are shortest directed path
costs: along a path inequalities sum; conversely distance labels attain
the minimal bound. Positivity of all costs removes cycles.
Each original central edge has cost<=2 and hence a facet path of length
at most N+1. It cannot use an auxiliary collar constraint, since reaching
the collar from a central vertex costs>Lambda>2 by S1. Its path consists
entirely of genuine original facet edges. Thus its initial z-jump is a
sum of at most N+1 genuine facet z-jumps. One of those has magnitude at
least its magnitude divided by N+1.

For each limiting parameter body choose a relative-interior point of
the selected genuine facet, strict for all other distinct facets and all
auxiliary collar bounds. A segment from zero to that point is feasible.
Then drive a fixed outward normal displacement. Its reflected path has a
positive impulse I0 on that facet and keeps the collar strict.
Fixed-domain driver continuity P3 makes this persist in a Brownian tube.
For these finite convex bodies its condition (B) is explicit: if B(0,r)
is inside the body and the body lies in B(0,M), then at a boundary point x
the unit vector -x/|x| has dot product>=r/(2M) with inward normals at
points within r/2 of x. Condition (A) holds with infinity by convexity.

Changing-domain continuity is proved separately. For graph constraints
|h_i-h_j|<=1 with moving individual interval endpoints or pins, projection
is monotone in the input and all endpoints. The minimum of two feasible
vectors is feasible for the lower-data body, and their maximum for the
upper-data body. Swapping the two vectors on the set where their order
fails cannot increase the sum of their two quadratic objectives when
the two inputs are ordered. Uniqueness of each projection then forces
the order. Hence changing initial heights and all collar/pin endpoints
by at most delta changes every projected trajectory by at most delta
under the same increments. Pass to the limit via P3. This supplies the
sup-norm parameter stability, without citing a varying-domain theorem.

The admissible initial relative heights and collar parameters form a
compact set with a common interior ball, bounded diameter, and finitely
many possible normals. Persistent limiting facets and finite covering
give a common positive tube probability p0 and impulse I0, for the fixed
testing family and fixed T. Facets which disappear as parameters vary
are not used. Brownian motion has positive probability in every tube of
these finitely many piecewise linear drivers. Constants can be very
small but do not depend on n or ambient volume.

Cut the shell edges of the selected FORCED replica. The other replica
need not keep its microscopic collar quiet; its extra contacts are
included in H and in the conservative-cluster loss. The cut exterior may
have an unanchored constant mode. Its finite-dimensional constraint set
is still a full-dimensional polyhedron, and its finite-time Skorokhod
problem is well-defined: convexity gives (A), and the finitely many
possible face-normal cones give (B) for this fixed finite polyhedron.
No ambient-uniform estimate on its law is used.
For initially good B let A_B be the event that a cut exterior shell
endpoint moves more than s0/4 in the cut exterior process. This event
implies that an endpoint in the ORIGINAL stationary shell moves by more
than s0/4: otherwise the cut and uncut processes agree until any first
cut contact, but such a contact itself would require that much original
endpoint motion. Extend A_B to be false on initially bad boxes. If no such
contact occurs, exterior paths agree all
the way and the implication is immediate. This uses uniqueness, not
stationarity of the cut exterior.
Its all-set probability bound therefore follows from (2.1). For events
A_i with P(intersection over J)<=gamma^|J| and any conditioning I,
  P(all k conditional probabilities>q)
      <=(2/q) [2 gamma^(q/2)]^k.                         (4.1)
To prove it, the conditional expected count exceeds q k. Conditional
probability of a count>=q k/2 is at least q/2. Unconditionally sum the
at most 2^k subsets of size>=q k/2. For q=1/2, absorb the prefactor into
an arbitrarily small base by shortening T.
On the complement, cut exterior quietness has conditional probability
>=1/2 and uses exterior Brownian coordinates. The interior forcing tube
uses independent interior coordinates, stays below r0 on its collar,
and prevents all cut contacts. Thus its actual uncut conditional force
probability is >=p0/2. Initial-good and conditional-quiet-bad marks from
both replicas still obey an arbitrarily small all-set bound by a finite
type pigeonhole, without replica independence.

At a forced upper edge contact in one replica, the oriented difference
z_j-z_i is nonnegative by feasibility of the other replica. Its normal
impulse dissipates the squared difference. Reflection of the other
replica is likewise dissipative. If a selected initial facet jump g has
the opposite sign, or falls below |g|/2 before the forced impulse, some
endpoint has moved by at least c|g|; (3.2) gives D>=c g^2/H^2.
Otherwise the positive impulse gives D>=c I0 |g|>=c' g^2, since a genuine
edge jump satisfies |g|<=2. Therefore D>=c g^2/(1+H^2).
Allocate to box B
  d_B=sum_(C incident to B) D_C/H_C.
Every component meets at most C_L H_C testing boxes, so
  sum_B d_B<=C_L sum_C D_C.
On the force event d_B>=c g^2/(1+H_B^3). Conditional Cauchy gives
  E[d_B|I]>=c p0^2 g^2/[1+E(H_B^3|I)].                   (4.2)
Finite facet-path telescoping transfers this to every nominated central
edge. It is harmless to make the weight smaller and use
  w_B=c1/[1+E(H_B^8|I)],  0<c1<=1.                       (4.3)
The same weight can be assigned to its finitely many tested edges.
Equations (2.4),(2.5) prove all required deterministic inverse-weight
spatial bounds outside arbitrary polynomial exceptions. The weights are
strictly positive a.s.; finite unconditional moments imply finite
conditional moments a.s.

Only insulated clusters are allocated in (4.2). To exclude a cluster
reaching noncommon data outside Q_R, subtract its conditional crossing
probability from p0/2. Initial configurations with that conditional
probability>p0/4 form a GLOBAL exception of probability O(n^2 exp(-c n)).
Keep this as an exceptional event, rather than falsely including it in
an all-set sparse bad law. On its complement use p0/4 in (4.2).
The energy of discarded edges is bounded by C n^2 times that probability;
centering and local martingale bounds below handle the cutoff error.

5. CUTOFF LOSS, STATIONARY CENTERING, AND AMBIENT UNIFORMITY

Choose a deterministic cutoff eta, one on Q_r, zero off Q_s, with lattice
Lipschitz constant C/[n(s-r)]. Let eta_C=max_C eta. Put c_eta(t) equal to
the minimizer of sum eta_x^2(z_t(x)-c)^2. For a conservative C, (3.2)
and contraction imply
  sum_C |u_T^2-u_0^2|<=C H_C^(3/2) sqrt(D_C)||u_0||_2,
where u_t=z_t-c_eta(0). Since
|eta_x^2-eta_C^2|<=C eta_C H_C/[n(s-r)], Young gives
  sum_C eta_x^2(u_T^2-u_0^2)
    <=-1/2 eta_C^2 D_C
       +C [n(s-r)]^-2 H_C^5 sum_C u_0^2.                 (5.1)
No division by a minimum cutoff is used. If C only touches the support,
choose one support site x_C. Lipschitzness gives
sum_C u_0^2<=2H_C u_0(x_C)^2+C H_C^3. These terms are charged to the
local sum of H_x^8(u_0(x)^2+1). Large crossings of Q_R are exceptional.
At local sites the marginal forward/reverse formula gives all moments
of sup_t|z_t-z_0| uniformly in ambient volume. Together with |F|<=C n,
Holder bounds any omitted local square-change term on a crossing by
C n^B exp(-c n(s-r)); there is never an ambient-coordinate factor.

Crucially, stationarity is used for the MINIMUM at each time:
  min_c sum eta^2(z_T-c)^2<=sum eta^2(z_T-c_eta(0))^2.
The minima at times 0,T have equal expectations. Thus the expectation
of the fixed-time0-center difference on the left of (5.1) is NONNEGATIVE.
It is not asserted that the field centered at a random time0 average is
stationary.

The weights defining c_eta have mass one, max O(n^-2), and support Q_s,
uniformly since r>=r_*. Hence S2 gives all moments of c_eta(0)-c0. Holder
with the rooted H moments bounds
  E n^-2 sum_(x in Q_s) H_x^8 |c_eta(0)-c0|^2 <=C.
This permits one fixed center c0 in every subsequent local norm:
  N_n(Q)=n^-2 sum_(x in Q) H_x^8(F(x)^2+1).
Combining (4.2),(5.1), with testing enlargements absorbed by an intermediate
box, yields
  E E_n(Q_r)+E sum_(C insulated, C meets Q_r) D_C
       <=C(s-r)^-2 [E N_n(Q_s)+1].                       (5.2)
This is the actual dynamical dissipation inequality; S2 or constant-
probability hole geometry by itself does not imply it.

6. DETERMINISTIC FILLING AND ABSORPTION USING ONLY FIRST ENERGY MOMENTS

Thicken the initial/conditional bad block set by a fixed layer and fill
its outermost star-component hulls. Every hull lies in its component's
bounding rectangle. Its exterior rim is good: an exterior rim vertex
touches an original bad component, hence cannot be bad in a distinct
star-component. Outer rim paths can be loop-erased, have length
O((1+d)^2), and have bounded overlap among retained outer hulls.
Fill a hole constantly at one deterministic surrounding good vertex.
Let U be the affine interpolation of this filled field on the scaled
square grid (using a fixed triangulation only for interpolation).
For any original fine rim edge, use its assigned positive weight.
Telescoping entering edges along SIMPLE rim paths gives
  sum |grad U|^p<=C sum_(good e) (1+d_e)^(2p)|grad F(e)|^p.
Holder implies
  ||grad U||_Lp<=M_p E_n^(1/2)                            (6.1)
in scaled coordinates when the required finite spatial powers of d_e
and w_e^-1 are bounded. In particular the explicit power in the holder
sum is (1+d_e)^(4p/(2-p)) w_e^(-p/(2-p)); a second Holder inequality
separates its two geometric factors. Every hole point differs from its

7. VANISHING FILL ERROR AND VOLUME-ROOTED TIGHTNESS

For each j repeat ONLY the tests/weights/step choice on the SAME joining
with target defect density rho_j
tending to zero. It gives new fixed parameters, good edges and constants
C_j independent of n. For 0<a<1, deterministic inverse-weight spatial
moments imply the expected density of good edges with w<a is at most
C_j a^q+o_n(1). The discarded good-geometry events have probability
o_n(1), and the full original edge jump is always<=2. Cauchy and (0.1)
give on the remaining good edges
  E n^-2 sum |grad z_0|
    <=C rho_j+C_j a^q+C_j/(n sqrt(a))+o_n(1).             (7.1)
Take n to infinity, then a to zero, then j to infinity. This proves
all-original-edge normalized L1 gradient tends to zero. It does not
change the ORIGINAL fixed hole filling used for U_n.

For those fixed holes, first restrict their diameters to M. Connect every
hole site to its chosen anchor by a full square-lattice path of length
O(M); congestion is bounded by a fixed polynomial in M. Then
  E n^-2 sum |U_n-F|
    <=C M^k E n^-2 sum_(all original edges) |grad z_0|
       +C_q M^-q+o_n(1).                                (7.2)
The large-hole term uses the pointwise O(1+d) error and high hole moments.
Paths stay in the common buffer except on its exponentially small
crossing event. The exceptional zeroing error was charged above. Take
n to infinity and then M to infinity to prove (0.2).

For an independent uniform tested edge, (0.1) gives
  P_vol(n|grad z_0|>M)
       <=C rho_j+C_j a^q+C_j/(a M^2)+o_n(1).             (7.3)
Choose j, then a, then M. This proves tightness. A fixed microscopic
increment is a sum along finitely many edges and follows by a union
bound, with a deterministic interior buffer for all shifted roots.

For displacement choose ONE fixed sufficiently short step T0 from one
construction. On a conservative cluster with H<=K, (3.2) and uniform

8. UNRESOLVED BROADER ENDPOINTS AND NON-IMPLICATIONS

The original triangular theorem permits bounded nonzero boundaries,
sign-wall strings, rough observations, and zero-preserving full-exterior
extensions. Its additional f:conditioned-sparse hypotheses have not been
proved for the square model here. In particular bounded-data average
moments alone do not give edge-slack rarity next to arbitrary observed
pins or shrinking two-sided walls. This proof makes no such assertion.
Interior centering suffices for the stated comparison and does not give
a full-domain zero trace or an ordinary square GFF limit.

Constant-probability macro geometry, one-site hole tails, or the S2
moment theorem alone do not imply the reflected estimate. Sections 2,
4,5 supply respectively spatial/conditional geometry, local contact
forcing, and stationary dissipation; each is essential. No heatbath QIP
or conductance homogenization is substituted for normal reflection.
The parent/tensor reflected bulk/tangent programme still needs its own
response/covariance identification, boundary approximation, and limiting
operator arguments. No novelty, formal verification, or external
validation is claimed.
