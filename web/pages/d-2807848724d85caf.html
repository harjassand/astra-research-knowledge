A02 elliptic solver — frozen research record v2 (strengthens v1)
Date: 2026-10-09
Scope: a principal-angle obstruction for bounded-support FEM energy preconditioners, a sharp local arity threshold, and its charged exact-factor escape; not a completed solver.

## Claim status

- Exact local matrix calculation: internally derived.
- Seven-element conforming FEM embedding: internally derived, finite-dimensional and explicit.
- Lower bound against every factor-width-two positive-semidefinite preconditioner for the embedded 3-by-3 FEM family: internally derived proof below.
- This includes all signed-SDD forms, arbitrary positive diagonal scalings of them, pairwise-local positive-semidefinite energy sums, and the ordinary doubled-graph representation.
- General near-linear solver for arbitrary high-contrast tensor FEM: unresolved. The result does not rule out generators supported on three or more physical unknowns, nonlocal/block preconditioners, or general lifts whose reduced form is outside factor width two.
- No external validation or novelty claim.

## Exact target

For conforming P1 finite-element stiffness matrices of uniformly elliptic divergence-form operators, determine the minimum local factor support needed to represent contrast-dominant tensor modes, and identify what global solve cost remains after that representation is available. The diagnostic includes diagonal scalings, signed graph factors and double-cover reductions.

The result below is a lower bound for the whole factor-width-two preconditioner class on an explicit patch, not just an obstruction to a particular sign choice. The general principal-angle bound shows that a factor support size \(k\) smaller than the support of a stiff rank-one mode forces a contrast-dependent condition number. This isolates a remaining solver requirement: a robust general-tensor method must use preconditioner energy generators whose support or structure can represent the contrast-dominant multi-node mode, or use a different mechanism.

## Explicit seven-element FEM family

Let the inner triangle have vertices

\[
I_0=(0,0),\qquad I_1=(1,0),\qquad I_2=(0,1),
\]

with centroid \(c=(1/3,1/3)\). Let the outer triangle have vertices \(O_i=c+4(I_i-c)\), so \(O_0=(-1,-1), O_1=(3,-1), O_2=(-1,3)\). The domain is the outer triangle. Its ring between inner and outer triangles is triangulated, cyclically, by

\[
(O_i,O_{i+1},I_{i+1}),\qquad (O_i,I_{i+1},I_i),\quad i=0,1,2,
\]

with indices modulo 3. Add the central face \((I_0,I_1,I_2)\). Impose homogeneous Dirichlet data at the three outer vertices. The three inner vertex values \(u=(u_0,u_1,u_2)^T\) are the free unknowns.

Put the constant tensor

\[
K_\kappa=R_{\pi/4}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}R_{\pi/4}^{T}
=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}\kappa+1&\kappa-1\\\kappa-1&\kappa+1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \kappa\ge 1,
\]

on the central face and coefficient \(I\) on all six ring faces. For each finite \(\kappa\), the piecewise coefficient is uniformly elliptic, with global eigenvalue range \([1,\kappa]\); hence the contrast is exactly \(\kappa\). The mesh and number of elements are fixed as \(\kappa\) grows.

On the reference central face, the P1 basis gradients are \(g_0=(-1,-1),g_1=(1,0),g_2=(0,1)\), and the area is \(1/2\). Directly evaluating \(A^T K_\kappa A\) gives the local stiffness

\[
A_T(\kappa)=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}
4\kappa&-2\kappa&-2\kappa\\
-2\kappa&\kappa+1&\kappa-1\\
-2\kappa&\kappa-1&\kappa+1
\end{pmatrix}
=\kappa ww^T+vv^T,
\]

where

\[
w=(1,-1/2,-1/2)^T,\qquad v=(0,1/2,-1/2)^T.
\]

The equality follows either by multiplication or from
\(\int_T (\nabla u)^T K_\kappa\nabla u = \frac{\kappa}{4}(u_1+u_2-2u_0)^2+\frac14(u_1-u_2)^2\).

Let \(S\) be the assembled stiffness of the six ring faces with coefficient \(I\), with outer values fixed to zero and the three inner values free. Then \(S\succ0\): if \(y^TSy=0\), every affine ring-face function has zero gradient; connectivity makes the function constant throughout the ring; its zero outer trace forces the constant and then all inner nodal values to be zero. Therefore the assembled three-by-three matrix is

\[
A_\kappa=\kappa ww^T+C,\qquad C:=vv^T+S\succ0,
\]

where \(C\) is a fixed matrix independent of \(\kappa\).

Exact rational assembly for the listed ring triangulation gives
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}7&-3/2&-7/2\\-3/2&19/2&-4\\-7/2&-4&23/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}7&-3/2&-7/2\\-3/2&39/4&-17/4\\-7/2&-17/4&47/4\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Sylvester's criterion verifies \(S,C\succ0\). Also \(\|C\|_2\le\|C\|_F=\sqrt{1389}/2\), so the bound below implies the concrete weaker estimate \(r\ge\kappa/(5\sqrt{1389})\).

For large enough \(\kappa\), its off-diagonal signs are \(A_{01}<0,A_{02}<0,A_{12}>0\), since the ring contribution is fixed while the central terms are \(-\kappa/2,-\kappa/2,+\kappa/4\). Their product is positive. Under any diagonal sign matrix \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\), the product of the three transformed edge signs is unchanged because each vertex sign appears twice. If all three transformed edges were nonpositive, their product would be negative. Thus no diagonal sign change turns this family into an M-matrix. The absolute diagonal-dominance deficit at rows 1 and 2 is \(\kappa/2+O(1)\), so it is not signed-SDD either for large \(\kappa\).

This also classifies ordinary scalar graph lifts on the three retained physical values. Let \(L\) be any grounded graph Laplacian, partition its vertices into the three physical terminals \(T\) and auxiliary vertices \(H\), and assume every auxiliary component is connected to ground so \(L_{HH}\) is invertible. The terminal energy after exact elimination is the Schur complement
\[
Q=L_{TT}-L_{TH}L_{HH}^{-1}L_{HT}.
\]
The matrix \(L_{HH}\) is a positive-definite M-matrix, so \(L_{HH}^{-1}\ge0\) entrywise; also \(L_{TH},L_{HT}\le0\) entrywise. Hence the product subtracted above is entrywise nonnegative and every off-diagonal of \(Q\) is nonpositive. No number of scalar auxiliary graph vertices changes this sign property. A diagonal sign change preserves the cycle-product obstruction just proved, so this FEM matrix cannot be represented exactly as such a terminal Schur complement, even after arbitrary scalar graph lifting. In the approximate-preconditioner setting, the factor-width-two lower bound below further rules out contrast-independent spectral equivalence for every pairwise-local PSD terminal energy, not only exact graph realizations. Lifts whose terminal map is a non-coordinate linear transformation or whose reduced energy has factors on three or more physical coordinates are outside this classification.

## Principal-angle lower bound for factor width k

For an SPD matrix, say that a preconditioner has factor width at most \(k\) if it has a decomposition
\[
B=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\beta_\ell g_\ell g_\ell^T,\qquad \beta_\ell\ge0,\qquad |\operatorname{supp}g_\ell|\le k.
\]
This includes any sum of positive-semidefinite local blocks on at most \(k\) physical coordinates, because each block has a positive rank-one spectral decomposition. Factor-width-two includes signed-SDD matrices and any positive diagonal congruence of one: their rank-one generators remain supported on at most two coordinates. It also includes signed graph/double-cover reductions; a pair term \((x_i-x_j)^2\) is represented by same-layer edges in two copies, and \((x_i+x_j)^2\) by cross-layer edges, with diagonal slack represented by grounding. Thus the class allows arbitrary pair coefficients, arbitrary pair signs, and arbitrary positive scalings of the factors.

Here is a general algebraic statement. Suppose
\[
A_\kappa=\kappa hh^T+C\succ0,
\]
where \(C\succ0\) is fixed and \(h\in\mathbb R^n\) has support \(J\). For \(1\le k<|J|\), define the relative tail mass
\[
\rho_k(h):=\min_{I\subseteq J,\ |I|\le k}\frac{\|h_{J\setminus I}\|_2^2}{\|h\|_2^2}>0.
\]
Then every factor-width-\(k\) SPD preconditioner \(B\) obeys
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cond}(B^{-1/2}A_\kappa B^{-1/2})\ge
\frac{\kappa\|h\|_2^2\rho_k(h)}{\operatorname{tr}C}.}
\]

Proof. Let \(r\) be the displayed relative condition number, \(\lambda_{\min}=\min_{x\ne0}x^TA_\kappa x/(x^TBx)\), and \(B_0=\lambda_{\min}B\). Then \(B_0\preceq A_\kappa\preceq rB_0\), and \(B_0\) retains the factor-width decomposition with nonnegative weights. Let \(P\) project orthogonally onto \(h^\perp\). If \(g\) is supported in \(I\subseteq J\), \(|I|\le k\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\|Pg\|_2^2=\|g\|_2^2-\frac{(h^Tg)^2}{\|h\|_2^2}
\ge\frac{\|h_{J\setminus I}\|_2^2}{\|h\|_2^2}\|g\|_2^2
\ge\rho_k(h)\|g\|_2^2.
\]
If \(g\) also uses coordinates outside \(J\), those coordinates are orthogonal to \(h\) and only increase \(\|Pg\|\), so the same inequality holds with \(I=\operatorname{supp}(g)\cap J\). Therefore
\[
\rho_k(h)\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\|g_\ell\|_2^2
\le\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\|Pg_\ell\|_2^2
=\operatorname{tr}(PB_0P)
\le\operatorname{tr}(PA_\kappa P)=\operatorname{tr}(PCP)\le\operatorname{tr}C.
\]
The last inequality follows by writing \(\operatorname{tr}(PCP)=\operatorname{tr}(C^{1/2}PC^{1/2})\) and using \(0\preceq P\preceq I\). Also
\[
h^TB_0h=\sum_\ell\beta_\ell(g_\ell^Th)^2
\le\|h\|_2^2\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\|g_\ell\|_2^2
\le\frac{\|h\|_2^2\operatorname{tr}C}{\rho_k(h)}.
\]
Since \(h^TA_\kappa h=\kappa\|h\|_2^4+h^TCh\ge\kappa\|h\|_2^4\), the inequality \(A_\kappa\preceq rB_0\), evaluated on \(h\), gives the bound. \(\square\)

For the explicit triangle patch above, \(h=w\), \(J=\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\operatorname{tr}C=57/2\). At factor width \(k=2\), the omitted coordinate has squared magnitude at least \(1/4\), while \(\|w\|_2^2=3/2\), so \(\rho_2(w)=1/6\). The bound is therefore
\[
\operatorname{cond}(B^{-1/2}A_\kappa B^{-1/2})\ge\frac{\kappa}{114}.
\]
For comparison, the earlier signed-SDD proof in v1 gives a separate bound \(\kappa/(10\|C\|_2)\) for a narrower class. On this matrix the principal-angle factor-width-two estimate \(\kappa/114\) is stronger, and it also covers positive diagonal scalings and arbitrary pair-local PSD blocks.

For a P1 simplex \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\), let \(\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_d\) be its nodal basis and choose a unit high-conductivity direction \(a\) for which every entry of \(h_i=|T|^{1/2}a^T\nabla\phi_i\) is nonzero. Such directions exist because only a finite union of proper hyperplanes is excluded. With \(K_\kappa=\kappa aa^T+(I-aa^T)\), the element stiffness has the exact decomposition
\[
A_T(\kappa)=\kappa hh^T+C_T^0,\qquad
C_T^0=|T|\big((I-aa^T)\nabla\Phi\big)^T\big((I-aa^T)\nabla\Phi\big)\succeq0,
\]
where \(\nabla\Phi\) maps nodal values to the constant element gradient. The stiff vector \(h\) generically has all \(d+1\) vertex entries. For every fixed dimension, this local matrix can be embedded without adding free nodes: take an outer simplex homothetic about the centroid of \(T\), triangulate each frustum between corresponding inner/outer faces with a pulling (staircase) triangulation from one global vertex order, and set the outer vertices to zero. In dimension one, use the two outer intervals. The shell coefficient is \(I\). Its stiffness \(S_T\) on the inner vertices is positive definite: zero shell energy makes the piecewise-affine function constant on each connected component, and each component meets the zero outer boundary. Thus the exterior stiffness adds a fixed positive-definite matrix \(S_T\), giving \(A_\kappa=\kappa hh^T+C\) with \(C=C_T^0+S_T\succ0\). Thus for every fixed simplex and direction with full support, every preconditioner of factor width \(k\le d\) has condition at least
\[
\frac{\kappa\|h\|_2^2\rho_k(h)}{\operatorname{tr}C},
\quad
\rho_k(h)=1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}|h|_{(j)}^2}{\|h\|_2^2}>0,
\]
where \(|h|_{(1)}\ge\cdots\) are the sorted coordinate magnitudes. This gives the dimension/locality threshold: support smaller than the simplex's \(d+1\) vertices cannot robustly represent a generic element's stiff mode. At width \(d+1\), this particular obstruction vanishes because \(hh^T\) itself is an allowed factor. The statement is local and does not prove that width \(d+1\) suffices for a global near-linear solver.

The exact width-\(d+1\) escape is inexpensive to acquire but not to invert automatically. Given each element tensor \(K_T\) and nodal-gradient matrix \(G_T=[\nabla\phi_0\ \cdots\ \nabla\phi_d]\), compute a Cholesky factor \(R_T\) with \(K_T=R_T^TR_T\) and form
\[
F_T=|T|^{1/2}R_TG_T\in\mathbb R^{d\times(d+1)},\qquad A_T=F_T^TF_T=\sum_{r=1}^{d} f_{T,r}f_{T,r}^T,
\]
where each row factor \(f_{T,r}\) is supported on the \(d+1\) element vertices. For \(M\) elements, reading the geometry/tensors and forming these factors costs \(O(Md^3)\) arithmetic and \(O(Md^2)\) storage; applying the assembled operator costs \(O(Md^2)\). These become linear in \(M\) for fixed dimension. Choosing this exact operator as \(B\) gives condition one, but applying \(B^{-1}\) is the original linear solve. Without a separately proved inverse representation, a safe generic dense Cholesky fallback costs \(O(n^3)\) arithmetic and \(O(n^2)\) storage for \(n\) free unknowns. Thus the local arity threshold removes the pair-factor obstruction but gives no near-linear acquisition-to-solution algorithm.

The lower-bound constant depends on the fixed local geometry, the chosen direction through \(h\), and the fixed anchoring stiffness through \(\operatorname{tr}C\). For a family of varying or degenerating simplices these constants may deteriorate; no uniform shape-regularity constant is asserted here. The two-dimensional mesh above is an explicit conforming FEM realization with all constants computed exactly. The matrix and factor-width inequalities are exact-real statements. The finite diagnostic uses rational arithmetic; no finite-precision iterative solver or backward/forward error bound is claimed.

The geometric reason for the bound is precise. The stiff mode \(w\) has nonzero entries on all three inner nodes. Every pair-supported factor misses a nonzero tail of this vector, so its projection onto \(w^\perp\) has a fixed positive angle. Raising the factor weight to capture order-\(\kappa\) energy along \(w\) necessarily creates energy on \(w^\perp\), where the target energy remains bounded by \(C\). Nonnegative factor weights prevent cancellation of that leakage.

## Executable finite check

`check_v1.py` assembles all seven P1 face matrices using exact rational arithmetic, verifies \(A_\kappa=\kappa ww^T+C\), checks positive leading principal minors for \(S\) and \(C\), and confirms the frustrated sign cycle and absolute diagonal-dominance deficits at \(\kappa=100,1000\). Run it with `python3 work/applied/a02_elliptic_solver/check_v1.py`. This is a finite assembly check, not an independent proof of the symbolic factor-width lower bound.

## What the result does and does not imply

The patch has only seven elements and three free degrees of freedom, and its matrix is assembled in O(1) operations and storage. It therefore has no hidden hierarchy, coarse-space, or local generalized-eigensolve construction cost: the lower bound is already present after exact assembly. Any method whose final preconditioner has factor width two pays at least \(\Omega(\kappa)\) in its spectral condition certificate, even if pairwise factors are applied exactly. This includes positive diagonal scalings of signed-SDD matrices. The proof does not imply an unconditional lower bound for arbitrary algorithms, factors with support at least three, nonlocal/block preconditioners, or general lifts whose reduced form is outside factor width two; nor does it rule out tailored solvers exploiting this tiny family.

In particular, the statement that a PCG iteration certificate based on this preconditioner would inherit a \(\sqrt{\kappa}\) factor is a consequence of the usual condition-number-based PCG estimate, not a lower bound on the actual iteration count for every right-hand side. The task's full contrast-robust near-linear solver remains open in this record.

## Primary-source comparison (live, exact sources)

1. Erik G. Boman, Bruce Hendrickson, Stephen Vavasis, “Solving Elliptic Finite Element Systems in Near-Linear Time with Support Preconditioners,” arXiv:cs/0407022v4, https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0407022v4, accessed 2026-10-09, especially Theorem 5 and discussion at HTML lines 267–320 and 509–520. They treat scalar conductivity \(\nabla\cdot(\theta\nabla u)=-f\), factor the stiffness as an incidence/diagonal/bounded-conditioning product, and obtain a weighted graph Laplacian. Their contrast-independent statement assumes the mesh respects large conductivity jumps; their bound also contains mesh-quality, quadrature and intra-element scalar-variation factors. The method requires mesh/PDE information, not just the assembled matrix (lines 512–514). It does not establish robustness for a general anisotropic tensor: the rotated tensor above has a positive-product signed triangle and a contrast-dominant three-node generator.

2. Yin Tat Lee, Richard Peng, Daniel A. Spielman, “Sparsified Cholesky Solvers for SDD linear systems,” arXiv:1506.08204v2, https://arxiv.org/html/1506.08204v2, accessed 2026-10-09, Theorem 5.10 at lines 420–440. It gives a linear-work vertex-sparsifier chain for an SDDM matrix and, once supplied that chain, an \(\epsilon\)-approximate solve in O(m log(1/epsilon)) work. The signed-SDD/double-cover algebra above is explicit; our lower bound says this graph route does not handle this tensor FEM family with contrast-independent same-space spectral quality.

3. Mario Bebendorf, “Low-rank approximation of elliptic boundary value problems with high-contrast coefficients,” arXiv:1410.3717v1, https://arxiv.org/html/1410.3717v1, accessed 2026-10-09. Theorem 1/3 (HTML lines 379–385 and 450–474) proves existence of rank O(|log epsilon|^{d+1}) separated Green-function approximation in a coefficient-dependent flux norm for arbitrary measurable SPD tensor coefficients bounded above/below. The norm is defined through \(-\Delta\phi_v^D=\mathcal L v\), \(\phi_v^D=v\) on \(\partial D\) (lines 216–239). Theorem 4 and proof (lines 477–521) show conversion to ordinary L2 error incurs a contrast-dependent logarithmic rank term, \(k\sim|\log(\epsilon\kappa_A^{-3/2}/\lambda_{\max})|^{d+1}\). The paper gives hierarchical matrix storage/matvec estimates O(kn log n), but the existence/rank theorem is not an energy-norm spectral preconditioner theorem, and no contrast-independent construction cost for its local subspaces is charged there. This is a potentially useful object for future work, not a solver result here.

## Remaining mathematical gap

To meet the original target, one must give an explicit algorithm for a broader preconditioner/factorization class and prove all of the following on a stated mesh and coefficient class: (i) \((1-\varepsilon)A^{-1}\preceq M\preceq(1+\varepsilon)A^{-1}\) or an equivalent energy norm guarantee; (ii) contrast-independent bound on total setup, storage, application, and solve work, charging any local spectral/eigenproblem solves; (iii) stability under adversarial tensor orientations, channels, and the three-node patch above. Neither the SDD route nor the coefficient-dependent low-rank existence theorem supplies those missing steps. No candidate meeting this bar was derived in this finite investigation.
