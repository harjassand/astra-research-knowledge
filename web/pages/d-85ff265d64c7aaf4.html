# Persister reservoir and pharmacodynamics

## Candidate

Bulk kill curves do not identify whether residual cells were already tolerant, entered a tolerant state during treatment, or became stably resistant. In the minimal reversible-state model, the on-drug reservoir clears at an effective rate approximately \(\delta\beta_D/(\delta+\alpha)\): sensitive-cell death \(\delta\), entry into tolerance \(\alpha\), and exit back to sensitivity \(\beta_D\) all matter. The removable bottleneck is to measure state occupancy and transition separately, then predict a held-out, equal-AUC pulse schedule. This is a line-specific model candidate, not a claim about all tumors.

## What the primary evidence does and does not establish

In PC9 EGFR-mutant lung-cancer cells, Sharma et al. reported about 0.3% viable drug-tolerant persisters after nine days of 2 μM erlotinib, refreshed every three days. Acute persisters reacquired EGFR-inhibitor sensitivity within nine drug-free doublings. Drug-tolerant expanded persisters (DTEPs) were a different, proliferating state: sensitivity recovery took about 90 doublings. Single-cell-derived PC9 clones also produced DTPs. These results support reversibility in this assay system and warn against collapsing acute DTPs and DTEPs into one compartment. They do not establish the same rates in tumors or patients. [Sharma et al., *Cell* (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851638/).

The induction-only interpretation is also too strong. In a melanoma system, Shaffer et al. found rare pretreatment transcriptional variability that predicted which cells later resisted treatment, followed by drug-induced reprogramming toward stable resistance. Thus a useful model must allow both a starting tolerant fraction and treatment-driven entry. [Shaffer et al., *Nature* (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28607484/).

Chen et al. reported that BET inhibition delayed relapse in xenograft models, but explicitly noted that no biomarker unambiguously identified DTPs in vivo; the attribution of delayed relapse to DTP elimination relied on their in-vitro evidence. That is a current provenance gap, not evidence against the mechanism. [Chen et al., *Cancer Cell* (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10983104/).

## Minimal model and exact consequences

Let \(S(t)\) be drug-sensitive cells and \(P(t)\) reversibly drug-tolerant persisters. During constant drug exposure, assume:

\[
\dot S=-(\delta+\alpha)S+\beta_D P,\qquad
\dot P=\alpha S-\beta_D P.
\]

Here \(\delta>0\) is the sensitive cells' net death rate at the measured drug concentration, \(\alpha\) is the sensitive-to-persister transition rate, and \(\beta_D\) is persister exit under drug. The model assumes persisters neither divide nor die directly under drug; their only removal route is exit to \(S\), followed by drug killing. Rates are constant over the pulse, concentrations are maintained, and mutation, spatial drug gradients, immune killing, and cell-cell effects are omitted.

The characteristic polynomial is

\[
\lambda^2+(\delta+\alpha+\beta_D)\lambda+\delta\beta_D=0.
\]

For \(\delta,\beta_D>0\), both modes decay. The slow rate is

\[
\lambda_{slow}=-\frac{(\delta+\alpha+\beta_D)-\sqrt{(\delta+\alpha+\beta_D)^2-4\delta\beta_D}}{2}
\simeq-\frac{\delta\beta_D}{\delta+\alpha}\quad (\beta_D\ll\delta+\alpha).
\]

If \(\beta_D=0\), the slow eigenvalue is exactly zero: higher dose can kill the sensitive compartment faster, but it cannot clear a nondividing, drug-insensitive reservoir in this model. When \(\beta_D\) is small, a persister's exit is followed by sensitive-cell death with probability \(\delta/(\delta+\alpha)\), since death and re-entry compete in \(S\). This gives the effective reservoir-clearance rate \(\beta_D\delta/(\delta+\alpha)\).

For a target residual fraction \(\varepsilon=P(T)/P_{eff}\), the small-\(\beta_D\), long-after-fast-transient clearance condition is

\[
\beta_D\gtrsim\frac{\delta+\alpha}{\delta T}\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]

As a calculation example only (not a PC9 estimate), take \(\delta=1\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\), \(\alpha=0.02\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\), and \(\beta_D=0.05\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\). The exact slow eigenvalue is about \(-0.049\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\), a roughly 14-day reservoir half-life. Doubling \(\beta_D\) to \(0.10\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\) gives about \(-0.098\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}\), or a 7-day half-life; the slow-mode survivor fraction after 14 days is about 0.50 versus 0.25. These rates only illustrate the scaling; the proposed held-out assay tests the model after estimating actual rates.

In the limiting initial kill phase where \(\beta_D\) is negligible, a starting sensitive cell enters \(P\) before dying with probability \(\alpha/(\delta+\alpha)\). Thus the post-transient reservoir is approximately

\[
P_{eff}\simeq P_0+\frac{\alpha}{\delta+\alpha}S_0.
\]

An endpoint survivor count therefore mixes pretreatment state occupancy and induced entry. The formula is a model consequence, not a measurement of \(\alpha\) in the cited PC9 experiment.

### Bulk-count non-identifiability

More generally, write \(u=r_S-d_S(C)\) and \(v=r_P-d_P(C)\) for the two states' net rates at one fixed concentration. With \(x=(S,P)^T\), the transition matrix is

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}u-\alpha&\beta_D\\\alpha&v-\beta_D\end{pmatrix},\qquad N=S+P.
\]

The observed total obeys

\[
N'=uS+vP,\qquad N''-\operatorname{tr}(A)N'+\det(A)N=0,
\]

where \(\operatorname{tr}(A)=u+v-\alpha-\beta_D\) and \(\det(A)=uv-u\beta_D-v\alpha\). Even an arbitrarily dense, noiseless total-count trajectory at this concentration supplies only the two eigenvalues plus the initial total slope (three shape quantities when \(N_0\) is fixed). The unknowns \(u,v,\alpha,\beta_D\), and initial persister fraction are five quantities. Generically there is a continuum of kinetically different parameter sets with the same bulk trajectory; collecting more bulk time points cannot resolve this structural ambiguity. State-resolved counts, a washout/rechallenge, or externally fixed rate functions are needed. A dose series alone is insufficient if the state-specific dose-response functions are also unknown.

### Washout regrowth and the holiday boundary

After washout, assume \(P\) exits at rate \(\beta_0\), sensitive cells grow at net rate \(r>0\), and \(P\) itself neither grows nor dies. Then

\[
P(t)=P_Te^{-\beta_0t},\qquad
S(t)=S_Te^{rt}+\frac{\beta_0P_T}{r+\beta_0}\left(e^{rt}-e^{-\beta_0t}\right).
\]

When \(S_T\) is negligible, late regrowth reaches burden \(N_R\) at approximately

\[
t_R\simeq\frac1r\log\!\left(\frac{N_R(r+\beta_0)}{\beta_0P_T}\right),
\]

provided the threshold is reached after the initial reversion transient. A single time-to-regrowth therefore confounds reservoir size \(P_T\) with reversion \(\beta_0\); drug-free cell counts plus state fractions are needed to separate them.

There is a conditional schedule result. Start a holiday with \(P_0\) persisters and negligible \(S_0\); let the holiday last \(h\). During the holiday assume no sensitive-to-persister entry and no persister growth or death. In the following sufficiently long drug pulse set \(\beta_D=0\): each reverted sensitive cell either dies or returns to \(P\). Let \(q=\alpha/(\alpha+\delta)\) be the probability that a sensitive cell re-enters \(P\) before drug killing. Under these assumptions, the reservoir after one holiday-plus-pulse cycle is

\[
\frac{P_{next}}{P_0}=R(h)=\left(1-\frac{q\beta_0}{r+\beta_0}\right)e^{-\beta_0h}
+\frac{q\beta_0}{r+\beta_0}e^{rh}.
\]

For \(0<q<1\) and \(\beta_0>0\), this has a positive reservoir-minimizing holiday

\[
h^*=\frac{1}{r+\beta_0}\log\!\left(\frac{r+\beta_0(1-q)}{qr}\right).
\]

At \(h=0\), \(R'(0)=-\beta_0(1-q)<0\); sufficiently short holidays reduce the persister reservoir. At long holidays the \(e^{rh}\) term wins and the reservoir rises after the next pulse. If \(q=0\), this simplified model favors longer holidays without bound; if \(q=1\), it gives no holiday benefit. This is not a treatment recommendation: total tumor burden during the holiday is

\[
N(h)=P_0e^{-\beta_0h}+\frac{\beta_0P_0}{r+\beta_0}(e^{rh}-e^{-\beta_0h}),
\]

and grows while sensitive descendants expand. Any feasible schedule must additionally respect a prespecified burden ceiling. Persister proliferation, genetic resistance, pharmacokinetic decay, or unequal tumor drug penetration can reverse these conclusions.

## Decisive experiment: equal-AUC interruption placement

**Question.** With equal total drug exposure and equal calendar time, does state-transition history predict residual persisters better than a static sensitive/resistant mixture?

**System and schedule.** Use a single-cell-derived PC9 line and 2 μM erlotinib, matching the published DTP induction condition. Compare (A) 3 days on / 3 off / 6 on / 6 off with (B) 6 days on / 3 off / 3 on / 6 off. Both arms receive 9 total drug days at the same concentration, 9 total drug-free days, the same 18-day duration, and end after the same 6-day washout. Refresh medium every 3 days in both arms. This matches nominal concentration-time AUC, handling cadence, and time since the last dose; it changes the placement of the interruption. Confirm comparable exposure or target engagement. A separate calibration run supplies rate estimates and their uncertainty; the two 18-day schedules are held out from fitting. Calibration consists of a constant-drug PC9 time course plus washout samples through nine doublings, with baseline growth and target-engagement measurements.

**Measurements.** At days 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18, measure live-cell number and the persister-state fraction. In this PC9 setting, CD133 can be used as a candidate state marker only after confirming in the same run that the marker-high fraction is enriched among drug survivors and returns toward parental sensitivity after washout; the 2010 paper found CD133 in all DTPs but only about 2% of parental cells. If this validation fails, use a line-validated live reporter or treat the state fraction as unmeasured and do not fit \(\alpha,\beta\). Measure phospho-EGFR two hours after initial dosing and after each drug restart in each schedule, so a schedule-specific exposure failure is not miscalled as state switching. Both schedules enter their final drug-free interval on day 12. From that point, measure sensitivity at zero, three, six, and nine drug-free doublings; use a 72-hour erlotinib response assay at each point. Genotype survivors for EGFR T790M and MET amplification.

**Controls and fair comparator.** Include vehicle-treated parental cells with the same medium exchanges, an independently genotyped stable EGFR-T790M resistant control under both schedules, and parental cells kept continuously on drug as a kill/reference arm. The primary matched comparison is interruption placement in each genotype: same starting density, drug concentration, cumulative drug days, calendar time, medium changes, state assay, target-engagement assay, and final washout. Fit (i) the reversible two-state model and (ii) a static two-subpopulation model to the same calibration data: both get the same initial mixture, drug-response measurements, and total/state-resolved observations, while the static control fixes the subpopulation identities and sets state transitions to zero. Score both on held-out day-18 live count, state fraction, and post-washout rechallenge curve with a complexity penalty. Do not tune either model on the schedule outcomes.

**Predeclared falsifier.** The proposed state-reservoir account fails for this line if the measured state fraction does not reversibly change with washout, if either schedule's held-out endpoint falls outside the two-state model's calibration-derived 95% predictive interval while the static model predicts it, or if the apparent schedule effect is explained by unequal target suppression. Score the held-out log total live-cell count, persister fraction, and post-washout IC50 jointly; support the reversible model only if its predictive log score exceeds the static-mixture control with a bootstrap 95% interval above zero. It also fails as a *reversible* DTP explanation if the recovered survivors remain resistant after nine doublings or carry a selected resistance alteration. A successful fit would establish only this PC9 assay's kinetics; it would not show that DTPs caused relapse in vivo.

**Time and assay cost.** The schedule comparison takes 18 days, with initial dosing plus five 3-day medium/drug refreshes (days 3, 6, 9, 12, 15). The washout/rechallenge follow-up runs in parallel from day 12 until nine drug-free doublings have elapsed, then each sample needs a 72-hour assay. There are six conditions (two schedules × two genotypes, vehicle parental, and continuous-drug parental reference). With four independent biological cultures per condition, the seven destructive state-count time points require 168 culture-timepoint samples. The four sensitivity-recovery points require another 96 split culture pools before plating dose-response curves; for six erlotinib concentrations plus vehicle, that is 672 dose-response wells before technical replication. Phospho-EGFR checks at first exposure and each restart, plus endpoint targeted genotyping, require separate samples. Calibration separately adds a constant-drug induction time course and washout through nine doublings. Repeated imaging can reduce separate wells for total counts but not flow-cytometry samples. The four-replicate design is a feasibility screen, not a powered confirmatory study; use its variance for a later power calculation. Exact dollar cost depends on local culture, cytometry, and sequencing rates and is not estimated here. A DTEP extension costs substantially more time: the cited PC9-derived DTEPs needed roughly 90 doublings to reacquire sensitivity.

## Status and unresolved points

- **Derived exactly under stated model assumptions:** the slow clearance eigenvalue; the zero-eigenvalue no-clearance case when \(\beta_D=0\); early entry probability; washout growth curve; and the conditional holiday minimizer.
- **Primary-source-supported but model-specific:** PC9 acute DTP prevalence, marker enrichment, drug-free resensitization, and the much slower reversal of expanded persisters.
- **Unresolved:** whether a prospective in-vivo state marker can distinguish DTPs from other residual cells; whether \(\beta_D\) and \(\beta_0\) can be estimated stably in a tumor; whether repeated holidays reduce relapse once genetic resistance, spatial pharmacokinetics, and persister growth are included; and whether any clinically tolerable perturbation selectively changes \(\beta_D\).
- **Next observation that changes the decision:** a held-out, state-resolved equal-AUC schedule comparison with matched target engagement. Bulk viability alone cannot decide between the reversible-reservoir model and a static resistant mixture.
