REGULAR SU(3): ADJOINT MULTIPLICITY AND FIRST-HARMONIC MOMENTS
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: representation/moment audit only. No operational energy or
coercivity conclusion is used or asserted.

CLAIM

For every regular SU(3) highest weight V_(a,b), a,b>0, the adjoint
representation occurs with multiplicity exactly two in End(V_(a,b)) under
conjugation. Thus covariance alone does not imply that an adjoint-valued
operator moment is a scalar multiple of the represented generators. The
correct general moment has two independent operator components.

1. EXACT MULTIPLICITY

Write 3=V_(1,0), 3bar=V_(0,1), and 8=V_(1,1). Since
End(V_(a,b)) ~= V_(a,b) tensor V_(b,a), Frobenius reciprocity and
self-duality of 8 give

 mult(8, End(V_(a,b))) = mult(V_(a,b), 8 tensor V_(a,b)).

The fundamental tensor rules are

  3 tensor V_(a,b) = V_(a+1,b) + V_(a-1,b+1) + V_(a,b-1),
  3bar tensor V_(p,q) = V_(p,q+1) + V_(p+1,q-1) + V_(p-1,q),

with terms having negative labels omitted. Because a,b>0, all three
summands in the first line are present. Tensoring each with 3bar gives one
copy of V_(a,b) from each summand:

  (a+1,b) tensor 3bar contains (a,b),
  (a-1,b+1) tensor 3bar contains (a,b),
  (a,b-1) tensor 3bar contains (a,b).

The identity 3 tensor 3bar = 1 + 8 therefore gives three copies of
V_(a,b) in (1+8) tensor V_(a,b). The singlet term contributes one, so
the adjoint multiplicity is 3-1=2.

Equivalently, omitting every term with a negative label,

  8 tensor (a,b) = (a+1,b+1) + (a+2,b-1) + (a-1,b+2)
                   + 2(a,b) + (a+1,b-2) + (a-2,b+1)
                   + (a-1,b-1).

For (a,b)=(1,1), this is 27 + 10 + 10bar + 2*8 + 1; dimensions
sum to 64. The multiplicity-one statement for Sym^N(C^3)=V_(N,0)
is a boundary case and does not extend to regular weights.

2. TWO EXPLICIT ADJOINT EMBEDDINGS

Let t_a, a=1,...,8, be Hermitian fundamental generators with
Tr(t_a t_b)=delta_ab/2, [t_a,t_b]=i f_abc t_c, and
{t_a,t_b}=delta_ab I/3 + d_abc t_c. In V_(a,b), let T_a be the
represented generators. Define

  Q_a = d_abc T_b T_c = (1/2)d_abc {T_b,T_c}.

The symmetry of d makes Q_a Hermitian. Invariance of d under SU(3)
implies that (T_a)_a and (Q_a)_a each transform as an adjoint-valued
operator family under conjugation. Both families are traceless: the
trace is an invariant linear functional on the nontrivial adjoint.

They are linearly independent for every a,b>0. Here is a direct check,
which also avoids assuming a multiplicity formula to certify the second
embedding. Realize the highest-weight vector as the product highest vector
in Sym^a(3) tensor Sym^b(3bar), and write its Cartan weight in the
(t_3,t_8) coordinates as

  lambda = (a/2, (a+2b)/(2 sqrt(3))).

For one fundamental highest factor, the mean generator vector is
omega_1=(1/2,1/(2 sqrt(3))); for one antifundamental highest factor it
is omega_2=(0,1/sqrt(3)). The identities

  d_abc t_b t_c = (5/6)t_a in 3,
  d_abc tbar_b tbar_c = -(5/6)tbar_a in 3bar,
  d(omega_1,omega_1)=omega_1/3,
  d(omega_2,omega_2)=-omega_2/3

follow from the anticommutator normalization and
d_338=1/sqrt(3), d_888=-1/sqrt(3). On the product highest vector,
different tensor factors factor in expectation. Splitting T_bT_c into
same-factor and distinct-factor terms consequently gives

  <Q> = d(lambda,lambda) + (1/2)(a omega_1 - b omega_2).

In particular, for the two Cartan components,

  lambda_3 = a/2,                  lambda_8 = (a+2b)/(2 sqrt(3)),
  <Q_3> = a(2a+4b+3)/12,
  <Q_8> = (2a^2-4ab-4b^2+3a-6b)/(12 sqrt(3)).

If Q_c=c T_c for some scalar c, the ratios of the corresponding
expectations would agree. They do not:

  <Q_3>/lambda_3 - <Q_8>/lambda_8
      = 2b(a+b+1)/(a+2b) > 0.

Thus Q is not proportional to T in any regular representation.

For explicit Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal embeddings, use the quadratic and
cubic Casimirs in this normalization:

  C2(a,b) = (a^2+b^2+ab+3a+3b)/3,
  C3(a,b) = (a-b)(2a+b+3)(a+2b+3)/18.

They satisfy sum_a T_a^2=C2 I and sum_a T_a Q_a=C3 I. Invariance
then gives

  Tr(T_a T_b) = d_V C2/8 * delta_ab,
  Tr(T_a Q_b) = d_V C3/8 * delta_ab,

where d_V=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2. Set

  alpha=C3/C2,                 S_a=Q_a-alpha T_a.

Then Tr(T_a S_b)=0 for every a,b. The family S is nonzero by the
independence just proved, and Schur invariance gives
Tr(S_a S_b)=gamma_S delta_ab with gamma_S>0. Thus (T_a) and (S_a)
are two explicit orthogonal adjoint embeddings. The contraction
sum_a T_a Q_a is the cubic Casimir; Q is also the covariant quadratic
d-symbol operator family.

At (a,b)=(1,1), C3=0, so S_a=Q_a. The highest-vector checks give
<Q_3>=3/4 and <Q_8>=-3/(4 sqrt(3)), in contrast with
lambda_3=1/2 and lambda_8=sqrt(3)/2; this is a small exact witness of
independence.

3. CORRECT OPERATOR-VALUED MOMENT

Let U_g be the representation on V_(a,b), let R(g) be the real 8 by 8
adjoint matrix, and let tau_g=U_g tau_0 U_g^dagger be a covariant orbit
of trace-one states. Let z(g) be a real adjoint-valued observable,
z(hg)=R(h)z(g), and use Haar probability dg. Define

  M_a = integral z_a(g) tau_g dg.

Changing variables by left translation shows that (M_a)_a is an
adjoint-valued operator family, hence an intertwiner from 8 into
End(V_(a,b)). Since the intertwiner space has dimension two, its exact
general form is

  M_a = kappa_T T_a + kappa_S S_a.                         (1)

The coefficients are the Hilbert-Schmidt projections

  kappa_T = [sum_a Tr(T_a M_a)]/[d_V C2],
  kappa_S = [sum_a Tr(S_a M_a)]/[sum_a Tr(S_a^2)].

There is no representation-theoretic reason for kappa_S to vanish. A
particular thermal family may impose extra identities, but those must be
proved for that family; covariance and a first-harmonic observable alone
give (1), not a one-term formula.

The Haar-averaged state is integral tau_g dg=I/d_V. Thus for any nonzero
POVM effect E>=0, the exact posterior mean under the uniform group prior is

  E[z_a | E] = d_V/Tr(E) Tr(E M_a)
             = d_V/Tr(E) [kappa_T Tr(E T_a) + kappa_S Tr(E S_a)].   (2)

This is the corrected posterior/moment statement. It concerns only the
operator first harmonic and does not assert anything about a risk or an
energy functional.

4. EXACT FAILURE OF A UNIVERSAL SCALAR FORMULA

Take V_(1,1), so d_V=8 and S_a=Q_a is nonzero and orthogonal to T_a.
Choose epsilon>0 with epsilon ||S_8||_op<1/8, and define

  tau_g = I/8 + epsilon U_g S_8 U_g^dagger,
  z_a(g) = R(g)_a8.

Every tau_g is a full-rank density operator; its Haar average is I/8.
Since U_g S_8 U_g^dagger=R(g)_b8 S_b, Schur orthogonality of the
irreducible adjoint matrix coefficients gives

  M_a = integral R(g)_a8 tau_g dg = epsilon S_a/8.          (3)

This is nonzero and Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal to T. It cannot have the
form M_a=kappa T_a.

The failure is visible at the posterior level as well. Let
E=I/8+delta S_8 with delta>0 and delta ||S_8||_op<1/8. Then
0<=E<=I, Tr(E)=1, and

  Tr(E T_8)=0,                 Tr(E S_8)=delta Tr(S_8^2)>0.

Equations (2)-(3) give E[z_8|E]=epsilon delta Tr(S_8^2)>0, whereas
any scalar-generator posterior formula proportional to Tr(E T_8) gives
zero. This is an exact counterexample to deriving the scalar formula
from covariance/multiplicity-one reasoning for arbitrary regular SU(3)
carriers. It does not claim that every specially specified Gibbs orbit
has kappa_S nonzero; such a model requires its own projection calculation.

5. BOUNDARY AND SOURCE NOTE

For nontrivial boundary weights (a=0,b>0) or (b=0,a>0), the missing
boundary terms in the fundamental tensor rules reduce this adjoint
multiplicity to one; at (0,0) it is zero. This explains why the symmetric
power calculation does not detect the regular case. The proof above uses
only the fundamental SU(3) tensor rules and the normalized f,d identities.
For convention background, see Chaturvedi and Mukunda, “The Schwinger
SU(3) construction - I: Multiplicity problem and relation to induced
representations,” arXiv:quant-ph/0204119v1 (submitted 2002-04-20),
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0204119. The multiplicity and moment
conclusions here are derived directly above.
