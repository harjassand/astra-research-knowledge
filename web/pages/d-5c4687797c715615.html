N26 — Finite causal scalar response, higher-derivative loopholes
2026-10-09

Status: internally derived response-class statements plus a source-checked, already-known KGB counterexample to extending the N01 first-derivative high-k closure beyond its stated class. This is not a new discovery or priority claim, not a complete synthesis theorem for generally covariant dark sectors, and not an empirical fit. The question is whether finite, healthy, minimally coupled local fields/media can supply scalar metric responses that look like modified gravity while the tensor sector keeps Einstein propagation. Main finding
============
Two scope boundaries matter.

1. In a stationary/frozen patch, a finite positive-energy quadratic sector has a finite-pole response. For a conjugate source/stress pair, its pole residue matrices are positive semidefinite and their total rank is at most the number of propagating scalar modes. This gives exact exclusions for that response class: a genuine branch cut, infinitely many independent poles, an upper-half-plane instability pole, or a negative diagonal spectral weight cannot be produced by finitely many stable conservative linear modes. Off-diagonal cross-residues need not be positive. A local contact polynomial is not a propagating pole.

2. The O(k^0) metric-to-stress response and O(k^-2) Einstein-constraint correction are not general consequences of “finite, healthy, minimally coupled local fields.” They need derivative-vertex bounds as well as a coercive spatial principal symbol. They hold for the narrow first-derivative P(phi^A,B^AB) setup with uniformly positive scalar speeds and zero hidden retarded initial data, but fail in the broader finite-derivative class: kinetic gravity braiding (KGB) is an explicit one-scalar, second-order, minimally coupled example with a luminal Einstein-Hilbert tensor sector and a nonzero high-k scalar effective gravitational coupling. It can be healthy and subluminal on a finite cosmological interval. This known example prevents upgrading the v2/v3 single-solid closure or a P(phi,B) high-k argument to all local dark sectors.

Finite-pole response lemma
==========================
Freeze coefficients in a stationary local rest frame and fix a spatial Fourier mode p. Let q in R^n be the propagating hidden scalar variables after nondynamical constraints are eliminated. For the conservative quadratic subclass with no derivative source vertices, write

  L_2 = 1/2 qdot^T K qdot - 1/2 q^T C(p)q + q^T B J + 1/2 J^T D J,

where K=K^T>0, C=C^T>=0, J is a vector of external scalar probes, and the measured operators are conjugate to these probes. Set z=omega+i0 and A=K^{-1/2}C K^{-1/2}. The retarded solution gives

  chi_R(z,p) = D + B^T[C(p)-z^2 K]^{-1}B.

Diagonalize A=V diag(Omega_j^2)V^T. Then

  chi_R(z,p) = D + sum_j r_j r_j^T/(Omega_j^2(p)-z^2),
  r_j = B^T K^{-1/2}v_j.

Each residue r_j r_j^T is positive semidefinite; for a degenerate pole its residue is the sum over the eigenspace and remains positive semidefinite. Its rank is at most that eigenspace dimension, and the sum of pole ranks is at most n. This is a direct finite-dimensional spectral decomposition, not a new theorem. It extends to Hermitian matrix-valued spectral weights for any stationary positive-norm linear system when source and observable are the same conjugate operators. If the output and input are different operators, the cross-residue is a product of two different overlap vectors and can have either sign. Do not impose entrywise positivity on cross responses.

For fields with standard isotropic principal dispersion, Omega_j^2(p)=m_j^2+c_j^2 p^2+o(p^2), with 0<c_min^2<=c_j^2<=1 and m_j^2>=0. At fixed low frequency |omega|<=omega_0 and p c_min >> omega_0, the resolvent is O(p^-2). If minimal first-derivative metric couplings give input and output vertices each O(p), then the induced stress response is O(p)*O(p^-2)*O(p)=O(1), plus local contact terms. If its coefficients stay of background size O(M_Pl^2 H^2), the response fed into an Einstein scalar constraint M_Pl^2 p^2 Phi ~ delta-rho changes the potentials at relative order O(H^2/p^2). This is the useful P(phi,B) scaling, not an all-local-action result.

A finite mode count alone does not imply that an arbitrary target is realizable: the target must also have allowed dispersions, the correct source/output vertices, covariant stress Ward identities, background matching, and correct initial data. A rational positive-residue kernel can be synthesized as an externally driven oscillator network, but that is not by itself a covariant minimally coupled matter action. Conversely a finite-field stationary linear system cannot exactly equal a nonzero continuous spectral measure/branch cut on an open frequency domain; rational and non-rational analytic functions cannot agree there and then differ by continuation.

Why zero sound is a real boundary
=================================
For a pressureless barotropic medium in a local flat patch, the linear continuity and Euler equations with externally specified potential Psi are

  dot(delta) = -theta,
  dot(theta) = p^2 Psi,

up to the conventional overall sign of Psi. With zero retarded initial data this gives

  delta(omega,p)/Psi(omega,p) = +/- p^2/(omega+i0)^2.

Thus the density response is O(p^2) at fixed low omega, not O(1). In FRW, Hubble drag changes the double zero-frequency kernel but not the p^2 forcing: for slowly varying Psi over a Hubble time one gets delta=O(p^2 Psi/H^2). This does not contradict the uniformly coercive estimate: the spatial restoring matrix has a null direction and c_min=0. N01 v3's F(X,Y,Z) construction explicitly includes the marginal fluid endpoint mu=0 and c_a^2=0 with positive time kinetic but zero scalar sound speeds; v3/v4 warn that zero-speed limits may be strongly coupled. So this is a classical linear counterexample to any claim that merely nonnegative causal cones suffice. If “healthy” is defined to exclude zero-speed/strong-coupling limits, then the exact dust example is outside that narrower definition, but arbitrarily small positive c_s still clusters like dust on the finite band p c_s <= O(H). A uniform observational suppression requires p c_s >> H across the band, not just p>>H.

Higher-derivative counterexample: kinetic gravity braiding
=========================================================
Take the explicit minimally coupled KGB action

  S = integral sqrt(-g) [ M_Pl^2 R/2 + K(phi,X) - G_3(phi,X) Box(phi) ] + S_known[g],
  X = -g^{mu nu} partial_mu(phi) partial_nu(phi)/2.

For shift-symmetric K(X), G_3(X), the field equations remain second order and there is only one added scalar degree of freedom. The scalar equation and stress tensor contain essential metric-scalar derivative mixing: in the equivalent K+G Box(phi) conventions, Deffayet et al. (2010), arXiv:1008.0048v2, eq. (6) has a curvature term proportional to G_X R^{mu nu} partial_mu(phi) partial_nu(phi), while eq. (10) contains second derivatives in T_{mu nu}. Their discussion around eqs. (12)-(13) explains that the mixing cannot be removed by a conformal transformation. This is the structural reason the source/output vertices can scale with p^2, outside the O(p)-vertex estimate above.

For G_4=M_Pl^2/2 and G_5=0, the tensor coefficients in De Felice, Kobayashi & Tsujikawa (2011), arXiv:1108.4242v2, eqs. (22)-(23), are F_T=G_T=M_Pl^2. The tensor speed is therefore c_T^2=F_T/G_T=1, and the tensor kinetic normalization is constant. Their KGB specialization eq. (63) has exactly these G_4,G_5 choices. Their scalar quasi-static Poisson/slip formula, eq. (64), is

  G_eff/G = [M^2-D_9 p^2] / [M^2-(D_9+2 X^2 G_{3,X}^2/M_Pl^2)p^2],   eta=1,

where p=k/a, and their eq. (65) defines D_9. For shift-symmetric K(X),G_3(X), their scalar mass parameter M^2=0; eq. (66) then gives a k-independent generally non-GR G_eff whenever G_{3,X} is nonzero and the denominator is regular. Thus there is an O(1) high-p change in the scalar growth/Poisson transfer with Einstein tensor propagation, despite finite field count and local finite derivative order.

This is not only a ghostly or superluminal pathology. Deffayet et al. derive the scalar sound speed and no-ghost conditions in eqs. (34)-(38), and give a shift-symmetric example K=-X, G=mu X. Their eqs. (54)-(58),(70)-(78) identify healthy attractor regions with positive kinetic normalization and c_s^2>0; the tensor modes remain unmodified (also stated with their quadratic action in Appendix A, eqs. (96)-(100)). In a late matter-containing interval with small positive Omega_ext, their table gives c_s^2 approximately 5 Omega_ext/12 during matter domination and 5 Omega_ext/6 in the quasi-de-Sitter dust case. Choose a compact interval with Omega_ext bounded away from zero and sufficiently small: c_s has a positive lower bound there and is subluminal. The model's early-time branch can be superluminal, so the counterexample is intentionally limited to that late interval. The cited paper does not supply a strong-coupling cutoff estimate; the formal high-p quasi-static limit is physically usable only if an actual window H/c_s << p << Lambda exists.

This KGB point is stronger than the zero-sound loophole: it shows that even positive scalar sound speed and an Einstein tensor cone do not imply O(p^-2) scalar closure once finite derivative mixing is admitted. It does not contradict the narrower theorem for first-derivative P(phi,B), which excludes the G_3 Box(phi) operator. It also does not provide an arbitrary target-function synthesis or prove exact equality to every f(R)/Horndeski transfer function.

Memory, poles, and causal cones
===============================
For frozen coefficients, a finite number of stable quadratic modes supplies a finite sum of pole memories. A genuinely dissipative continuum response, a branch cut, a fractional kernel, or a non-rational transfer cannot be exactly realized by finitely many such modes. Increasing finite N can approximate a smooth continuum kernel on a compact frequency band but does not give exact equality; the approximation error and mode/coupling costs must be specified. Local higher-derivative terms can add polynomial contact pieces or extra poles; if the higher-time-derivative theory is nondegenerate and has an Ostrogradsky mode, it fails the healthy premise. Degenerate second-order interactions such as KGB evade that simple reduction because they create derivative mixing without extra scalar mode.

A stationary pole claim is not a global FRW theorem. On expanding backgrounds the exact retarded object is a two-time Green kernel G_R(k;t,t'), not a function of omega with fixed poles. A frozen/WKB pole interpretation requires coefficients to vary slowly relative to the mode period. Quasi-static estimates require p c_min >> H and source variation rates, with p below the actual EFT cutoff. Near a resonance omega^2 approximately Omega_j^2(p), the susceptibility is large and the low-frequency resolvent estimate does not apply. Time-dependent coefficients, parametric amplification, and nonzero homogeneous modes need separate treatment.

The zero-hidden-initial-data condition only controls the retarded part. Independent homogeneous scalar modes add transfer columns without being poles in the retarded metric-to-stress kernel. With one adiabatic primordial seed, every field transfer can share its amplitude; with multiple independent modes an unresolved covariance does not identify individual transfer columns. A test of reconstructed cosmological transfer data therefore needs mode identification or an explicit covariance model, plus matter density/momentum subtraction and nuisance profiling as emphasized in N01 v3-v4. A single observed adiabatic column cannot certify the full response matrix.

Strongest supported consequence
===============================
The proposed stress/pole gate should be split by assumptions. For first-derivative P(phi^A,B^AB), uniformly positive kinetic and spatial principal matrices, bounded metric-source vertices of at most first spatial order, low-frequency forcing, and zero retarded hidden initial data, finite N preserves the O(1) stress / O(p^-2) Einstein-feedback scaling; rank or finite-field count does not change the exponent. For causal but degenerate zero-speed modes this bound fails. For healthy second-order finite-derivative KGB the bound also fails through derivative mixing, with c_T=1 and a known subluminal stable window. Therefore a meaningful generalization must state the action derivative order, degeneracy and principal-symbol conditions, vertex order, actual frequency band, and EFT cutoff. Tensor-speed agreement alone does not close scalar-sector ontology.

The pole/residue statement is a standard normal-mode consequence and the KGB counterexample predates this note; neither is claimed novel. The internal contribution here is the scoped comparison that blocks the proposed extension of the N01 single-solid/P(phi,B) logic to all finite local media, together with the explicit zero-speed response derivation and the exact assumptions needed for the finite-pole lemma.

Correction to the late-time sound-speed numbers in “Higher-derivative counterexample: kinetic gravity braiding”: for the cited Deffayet et al. shift-symmetric linear model on its attractor, Eq. (5.20) gives

  c_s^2 ≃ [(5/3 + w_ext)/4] Omega_ext,  as Omega_ext -> 0.

For external dust (w_ext=0), this is 5 Omega_ext/12. The previously written 5 Omega_ext/6 in the quasi-de-Sitter dust clause was a factor-of-two transcription error and should be disregarded. The 5 Omega_ext/12 entry is the late, scalar-dominated limit in the matter-external-fluid row of Table 1; when the scalar is subdominant during matter domination, Eq. (5.19) instead gives c_s^2 -> 4/3, which is superluminal. The intended finite-interval claim remains: take a late compact interval with 0 < Omega_ext < 1 and bounded away from zero, where the attractor has positive kinetic coefficient D*=1+Omega_X and 0 < c_s^2 < 1. Source: arXiv:1008.0048v2, Eqs. (5.12), (5.18)-(5.20), Table 1, especially pp. 25-27.

Additional precision: the exact no-branch-cut statement is about the finite conservative quadratic mode class defined in “Finite-pole response lemma.” Its retarded spectral measure is a finite sum of point masses at the normal-mode frequencies. A continuous spectral density therefore cannot equal it. This conclusion is not asserted for interacting fields, whose loop response can contain continua.
