## Operators, dimensions and orientation

Write d=5 or6, D=d+24, g=binom(n+2,3), t_D=binom(n+D-10,D-9),

    C=binom(n+D-1,D), R=g t_D, N=binom(n+d-1,d), p=R+N.

M:QQ^R -> QQ^C has the Macaulay SOURCES AS COLUMNS. The column indexed by (alpha,beta) is the coefficient vector of P_alpha(v)v^beta, |beta|=D-9. W:QQ^N -> QQ^C has columns f(v)^6 v^gamma, |gamma|=d. Thus J_d=ker(W mod im M). Applying M means a sum of shifted polynomial products; applying M^T is coefficient cross-correlation. No matrix of C R entries is required.

Here and in the code replace P by the integer-scaled remainder 64P. Let F=f(v), a(x)=grad f(v).x and D_v differentiate in x. Direct polarization gives

    64P(v,x)=16 F^2 D_v f(x)-4F a(x) D_v^2 f(x)+2a(x)^3.

Its ordinary cubic-in-x coefficient list is the original legal degree9 generator list multiplied by one common nonzero scalar. This avoids fractional coefficients and does not introduce a saturated or surrogate ideal.

Set m=min(C,p). If C<=p, use the coefficient identity E:QQ^C -> QQ^C, so m=C and no sampling is needed. If C>p, choose m=p independent integer evaluation points xi_j in {1,...,L_eval}^n, and E h=(h(xi_j))_j. Set Mtilde=E M, Wtilde=E W. The point coordinates and every operator application are acquired from f and freshly sampled integers.

Let U=im M+im W, of unknown dimension s<=p. Evaluation is injective on U except on a proper polynomial zero set. Indeed, any basis h1,...,hs of U consists of linearly independent degreeD polynomials. Their evaluation functions span QQ^s: otherwise a fixed nonzero linear combination would vanish at every rational point. Hence an s-by-s minor of evaluations at the first s sampled points is a nonzero polynomial of total degree<=D s. Schwartz-Zippel gives

    Pr[E is not injective on U] <= D p/L_eval.

For the two degrees choose L_eval=16(D29 p29+D30 p30). Joint evaluation failure is <=1/16; identity branches can simply omit their samples. This is not a rank certificate. It is a probability bound for independent coordinate samples. A fixed pseudorandom seed provides reproducibility of a finite run, not this independence theorem.

## An exact ambient coker projector from consistent CG

Define G=Mtilde Mtilde^T, an m-by-m rational symmetric positive-semidefinite Gram operator. Source columns make this orientation essential. We never store G. Its ordinary computational dot product has no relationship to the unknown aligned metric.

For an integer probe a in QQ^m solve Gx=Ga from x0=0. Since b=Ga lies in im G, all Krylov vectors lie in im G, where G is positive definite. Exact conjugate gradients terminates after at most rank G<=m steps without nonzero-residual breakdown. The unique obtained x in im G gives z=a-x in ker G=ker Mtilde^T. Verify Mtilde^T z=0 exactly before using it.

To make direction rescaling and rational heights explicit, maintain x and residual r=b-Gx as vectors of integer numerators with ONE reduced positive common denominator each. Store each search direction as a primitive integer vector p_k. With den=p_k^T Gp_k>0, use

    alpha=(p_k^T r)/den,
    x_new=x+alpha p_k, r_new=r-alpha Gp_k,
    gamma=(p_k^T G r_new)/den,
    p_new=primitive(r_new-gamma p_k).

Stop when r_new=0. Primitive divides by the gcd of the integer numerators after clearing the single common denominator and chooses a fixed sign. It is not legal to rescale p and then blindly use the usual unrescaled beta recurrence. The displayed formula accounts for scaling.

For completeness, K_k=span(b,Gb,...,G^(k-1)b). Inductively x_k is the G-energy minimizer in K_k and r_k is orthogonal to K_k. The next vector r_k is in K_(k+1). For a previous search p_j, j<k-1, Gp_j belongs to K_(j+2) subset K_k, so p_j^T G r_k=0. The gamma correction makes the new direction G-orthogonal to the immediately preceding direction as well. If it vanished with nonzero residual, residual orthogonality and positive definiteness on im G would fail. Thus this rescaled recurrence is ordinary exact CG with the same iterates and finite termination. On termination x is the im-G solution and a-x the orthogonal coker projection. No guessed minimal polynomial, numerical tolerance, rank, spectral gap or latent metric is needed.

## Random dual rows and completeness boundary

Take k=rmax independent probes with each entry in {1,...,L_probe}, L_probe=16rmax. From each verified z retain the row z^T Wtilde in QQ^N, discarding dependent rows by small exact row reduction. Retain at most rmax rows. Denote the resulting full-row-rank matrix K_d.

On an injective evaluation event the linear map a -> z^T Wtilde has rank r. The projector pairs the coker of Mtilde nondegenerately with the image of Wtilde mod im Mtilde; thus its kernel on the target is exactly J_d. A fixed nonzero r-by-r minor in the first r probe outputs has total degree r in their entries, so failure to span has probability<=r/L_probe<=1/16. Union over d=5,6 and evaluation gives a good-event probability>=13/16, in particular >3/4.

There is NO completeness conclusion from rank(K_d)<rmax. The actual r need not equal rmax. On any sample, every true J_d vector is annihilated, because Mtilde^T z=0; the computed kernel can be too large. It equals J_d only on the proved complete event or after a separate exact completeness certificate. Quotient checks below and the final representation verifier are mandatory. A successful final representation does not retroactively certify complete reflection enumeration on an incomplete sample.

## Bit accounting: finite-field slogans do not suffice

Let B be expanded-input encoding length, Sf the sum of absolute integer quartic coefficients after clearing denominators. Fixed derivatives/products give source coefficient l1-norm<=384 Sf^3 and target f^6 l1-norm<=Sf^6. At integer points bounded by L_eval, source/target entries have magnitude bounded by these constants times L_eval^D. Thus initial operator entries have bits b0=O(B+log n), since D<=30 and L_eval is polynomial in n. Gram entries have bits bG=O(b0+log R). Put kappa=1+bG+log m+log L_probe.

The following deliberately conservative exact-height bound is part of the proposed implementation, not a field-operation slogan. Krylov columns b,Gb,...,G^(j-1)b are integer vectors with bits O(j kappa). Before termination they are independent. The j-by-j positive-definite Krylov Gram K_j^T G K_j has entries of bits O(j kappa). Cramer/minor bounds give a common denominator and numerators of the CG minimizer x_j, and of r_j=b-Gx_j, of bits O(j^2 kappa). The unique next G-conjugate ray in K_(j+1) is given by cofactors of K_j^T G K_(j+1); multiplying by the integer Krylov basis gives an integer vector of bits O(j^2 kappa). Its primitive direction cannot have larger height. Canonical common-denominator reductions therefore keep x,r,p at bits O(m^2 kappa). Each dot product, implicit product and common-denominator vector update creates only a constant sum/product of such sizes, so temporary heights have the same asymptotic bound. Krylov columns and Gram minors are proof devices and are NOT stored. Unnormalized rational recurrences or per-coordinate denominator lcms are not covered by this implementation bound.

Projected dual rows, retained small reductions and sections have polynomial height. A coarse safe bound permits O(rmax*m^2*kappa) bits per reduced dual/section entry after their additional minors; downstream polynomial-bit remains, without pretending all rows stay at b0 bits. The code records actual maximum numerator/denominator sizes. This height charge is essential: linear vector slots do not imply linear bit memory.

## Operator representations and full costs

Coefficient branch: store the g degree9 generator templates, at most g*C9 entries, C9=binom(n+8,9), and the degree24 f^6 coefficient template, C24=binom(n+23,24). Sparse shifts apply M/M^T in O(R*C9) arithmetic, with at most R+C vector entries. Generator and f^6 preprocessing by fixed-degree products is polynomial and charged; C24<=C=m in this branch. Monomials can be indexed by multisets of length at most30 with ranking/unranking, rather than n-entry exponent tuples in the asymptotic implementation.

Evaluation branch: store only m*n sampled integer coordinates and vector workspaces. From each xi compute F, grad f(xi), D_xi f(x) and D_xi^2 f(x) from the observed quartic in O(n^4) field operations; combine their coefficients using the displayed64P identity. Stream monomial multipliers of degreeD-9 and evaluate all sources as needed. Each M/M^T application costs O(m(R+n^4)) arithmetic with O(R+m+mn) slots; no coefficient degree30 array, f^6 expansion, evaluation matrix or Gram is stored. Target dual-row formation costs O(m(N+n^4)). Every repeated pass recomputes and is charged. Precomputed source evaluations would be a forbidden m*R dense materialization for this claimed representation.

Let S denote the chosen implicit matvec cost and k=rmax. At most O(k*m) Gram applications per degree and O(k*m^2) auxiliary vector arithmetic suffice, plus k target correlations and small quotient work. Shared-denominator arithmetic costs are evaluated at O(m^2 kappa) bit integers, with gcd reduction included. A conservative workspace bound, before small quotient-output elimination, is

    O((R+m)*m^2*kappa + k*N*rmax*m^2*kappa
      + m*n*log L_eval + (g*C9+C24)*b0) bits,

where absent coefficient/evaluation terms are omitted in the respective branches. The additional rmax in retained-row height is a safe minor-growth charge, so this bound is intentionally weaker than simply counting slots. As n grows, R=O(n^24), m<=R+N=O(n^24), k=O(n^2), N6=O(n^6), and vectors dominate with O(n^72(B+log n)) bits. Evaluation-branch arithmetic has the crude bound O(n^74) field operations; this is still an enormous polynomial, not a feasible universal runtime claim.

The former dense Gaussian implementation has initial C*R=O(n^54) entries and conservative full-elimination storage O(C*p*min(C,p)*(B+log n))=O(n^78(B+log n)) bits. The new method avoids matrix materialization and gives a lower conservative peak upper bound, but O(n^72) can exceed dense INITIAL O(n^54) bit storage. No optimal-memory lower bound, unconditional practical improvement, or dominance over all competent dense/modular implementations is established. For moderate n, C<p and the large ambient coefficient vector remains; the evaluation switch removes it only when C>p. Degree58 can be implemented identically but with its larger operator dimensions. FLOW circuits on their proved classes remain equally informed comparators and may be much cheaper.
