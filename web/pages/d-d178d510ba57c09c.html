CLASSICAL/ENTANGLEMENT-BREAKING FLOOR FOR THE CRITICAL XY EXPERIMENT
Status: internally reconstructed proof. Not external validation or priority.
All errors and constants use FULL trace norm, not half trace distance.

1. A DIMENSION-INDEPENDENT EB WITNESS

For density operators rho,sigma on any separable Hilbert space define

 F(rho,sigma)=||sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)||_1       (root fidelity),
 A(rho,sigma)=Tr sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)         (affinity),
 g=F-A>=0.

Let T be any entanglement-breaking (measure-and-prepare) channel, with
arbitrary classical alphabet and arbitrary prepared output states. Then

                         A(T rho,T sigma)>=F(rho,sigma).            (1.1)

Proof: if p_x=Tr rho M_x, q_x=Tr sigma M_x are the measurement laws, the
polar variational formula and Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz give
F(rho,sigma)<=sum_x sqrt(p_x q_x). For example insert sum M_x=I between
sqrt(rho),sqrt(sigma) in Tr U sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma), and apply Cauchy-Schwarz
to each term; maximize over U. If the preparation states are eta_x,
Lieb's joint concavity of Tr X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}, together with its homogeneity,
gives A(sum p_x eta_x,sum q_x eta_x)>=sum sqrt(p_xq_x)Tr eta_x.
This proves (1.1). Integral alphabets follow by approximation/joint
concavity in trace class. A finite approximation is sufficient if one
prefers to restrict physical classical archives to finite alphabets.
The sole identified trace-function background input here is Lieb's
concavity theorem, DOI 10.1016/0001-8708(73)90011-X.

For positive trace-class rho,rho',

 ||sqrt(rho)-sqrt(rho')||_2^2<=||rho-rho'||_1.                        (1.2)

An elementary proof of the needed Powers-Stormer inequality is as follows.
Write X=sqrt(rho),Y=sqrt(rho'),C=X-Y=C_+-C_-, and S=sign(C). Then
||X^2-Y^2||_1>=Tr S(XC+CY)=Tr |C|(X+Y).
The latter minus Tr C^2 equals 2Tr(C_+Y+C_-X)>=0.
Approximate by finite-rank operators if necessary.

Consequently if delta=max(||T rho-rho||_1,||T sigma-sigma||_1), then
|A(T rho,T sigma)-A(rho,sigma)|<=2sqrt(delta), so

                            delta >=g^2/4.                         (1.3)

No finite-dimensional compactness argument, decoder-family compactness,
or approximate no-broadcast theorem has been assumed. The bound applies
directly to infinite-dimensional output.

Moreover g=0 iff rho and sigma commute. Equality Tr X=||X||_1 for
X=sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma) forces X to be positive by the polar decomposition.
Thus sqrt(rho),sqrt(sigma) commute; the converse is immediate.

A useful deliberately nonsharp fully quantitative comparison is

 g>=||[rho,sigma]||_1^2/128,
 delta>=||[rho,sigma]||_1^4/65536.                                  (1.4)

Proof: with X=U|X| and F<=1,
||X-X^*||_1<=2||(U-I)|X|||_1<=2sqrt(2Fg)<=2sqrt(2g).
Writing R=sqrt(rho),S=sqrt(sigma), [R^2,S^2] is the sum of four products
containing [R,S], and ||R||,||S||<=1. Hence its norm is at most
4||[R,S]||_1<=8sqrt(2g). Combine with (1.3).

2. EXACT LOCAL RESPONSE IN THE CONTINUUM FAMILY

Fix lambda=0 and the J,tau>0 of proof.txt. Let
d_k=2tau J pi^2 k^2, p_k=(1+exp(d_k))^{-1}.
At kappa=0 the continuum density rho_0 is the independent Bernoulli Fock
state with diagonal probabilities

 rho_0(S,S)=p_vac exp(-sum_{k in S}d_k),
 p_vac=prod_{k>=1}(1+exp(-d_k))^{-1}>0.

Let h_kappa=h_0+kappa V, V=[0,K';-K',0], K'=-4tau J partial_x.
For omega_n=(2n+1)pi, the Matsubara expansion is

 f(h)=1/2-sum_{n>=0}[(h-iomega_n)^{-1}+(h+iomega_n)^{-1}].           (2.1)

It converges strongly. Its difference between kappa and 0 converges in
operator norm, since

 ||V(h_0-iomega)^{-1}||<=sqrt(8tau J)/sqrt(2omega).

This follows from ||K'u||^2=8tau J<u,Du> and
sup_{d>=0}sqrt(d)/sqrt(d^2+omega^2)=1/sqrt(2omega).
The exact resolvent expansion, with the unperturbed resolvent on the right,

 R_kappa=R_0-kappa R_0 V R_0+kappa^2 R_kappa V R_0 V R_0,

gives an operator-norm second remainder bounded by
tau J kappa^2 after summing both signs, because
sum_{n>=0}omega_n^{-2}=1/8. Thus

 ||f(h_kappa)-f(h_0)-kappa L_f(V)||<=tau J kappa^2.                  (2.2)

The off-diagonal derivative is the divided difference

 [L_f(V)]_{k+,l-}=K'_kl [p_k-(1-p_l)]/(d_k+d_l).

In particular K'_12=32tau J/3, d_1+d_2=10tau J pi^2. For the pair
anomalous expectation w(kappa)=Tr rho_kappa d_2 d_1 (up to the fixed
covariance sign convention),

 c0=16/(15pi^2)(1-p_1-p_2)>0,
 |w(kappa)-sign*c0 kappa|<=tau J kappa^2.                           (2.3)

Hence for 0<|kappa|<=c0/(2tau J), |w(kappa)|>=c0|kappa|/2.
This quantifies a genuine pair coherence; it does not assume that all
temperature dependence can be replaced by a two-level ground state.

3. FULL-STATE NONCOMMUTATIVITY AND AN EXPLICIT (POOR) FLOOR

If rho_kappa commuted with rho_0, every same-spectator matrix element
between |S> and |S union {1,2}> would vanish: their rho_0 eigenvalues have
ratio exp[-(d_1+d_2)]!=1. The anomalous expectation is the sum of exactly
these matrix elements, with fermionic signs. That would force w(kappa)=0,
contradicting (2.3). This is a full-state argument. Noncommutativity of
arbitrary reduced states by itself would not imply full noncommutativity.

Here is an explicit lower bound, rather than only the word 'positive'.
Choose any 0<kappa_*<=min(K,c0/(2tau J)), for example
min(K/2,c0/(4tau J),1), assuming K>0. Choose M>=2 so that

 t_M:=4pi^2 kappa_*^2/M+sum_{k>M}exp(-2tau J k^2)
                         <=c0^2 kappa_*^2/64.                      (3.1)

Let P_M project onto vacuum above M. Gentle projection gives
|Tr(P_M rho_kappa P_M)d_2d_1|>=c0 kappa_*/4.
On this finite Fock subspace all relevant eigenvalue differences of rho_0
are at least

 alpha_M:=p_vac exp(-sum_{k=1}^M d_k)(1-exp[-d_1-d_2])>0.

Construct an operator with the same pair-shift entries as d_2d_1, divided
entrywise by these eigenvalue differences. The shift has disjoint domain
and range basis vectors, so this operator has norm at most 1/alpha_M.
Pairing it with P_M[rho_0,rho_kappa]P_M recovers the projected anomalous
expectation. Therefore

 ||[rho_0,rho_kappa_*]||_1>=eta:=alpha_M c0 kappa_*/4,
 inf_EB T max_{kappa in {0,kappa_*}}||T rho_kappa-rho_kappa||_1
                             >=eta^4/65536>0.                     (3.2)

All constants are explicit. To avoid an unevaluated infinite product use
p_vac>=exp[-sum_{k>=1}exp(-d_k)], and bound the sum geometrically as in
proof.txt (2.5). Also sum_{k=1}^M d_k=tau J pi^2 M(M+1)(2M+1)/3.
This constant is extremely conservative, generally astronomically small;
it proves a strict operational obstruction and does not claim a practical
experimental margin. The sharper intrinsic bound is g(rho_0,rho_kappa)^2/4.

4. PASSAGE TO THE FINITE-CHAIN EXPERIMENT

Proof.txt establishes uniform trace-norm convergence of the embedded
states. Root fidelity and affinity are continuous in trace norm by (1.2)
and Hilbert-Schmidt product bounds. Thus g_L->g>0 for the selected pair.
Every finite-chain classical encoder/decoder yields an EB channel after
the public isometric Fock embedding. Apply (1.3) before taking the limit:

 liminf_{L->infinity} inf_EB T_L
 max_{kappa in {0,kappa_*}}||T_L rho_L,kappa-rho_L,kappa||_1
                       >=g(rho_0,rho_kappa_*)^2/4>0.                (4.1)

The encoders, alphabet sizes, and output preparations may all depend on L;
the inequality still holds. It excludes vanishing-error classical archives
even with unlimited classical memory for this unknown-parameter subfamily.
It does not exclude a decoder given kappa itself, multiple-copy tomography,
or reconstruction of only restricted observables.

Together with proof.txt §7, this is the operational transition: at kappa=0
the XX family is commuting and has its exact number-sufficient classical
archive; a compact anisotropy family containing 0 and a small nonzero value
has a strict asymptotic EB floor, while public finite quantum archives
achieve every requested error with persistent memory independent of N.

5. EVIDENCE AND LIMITATIONS

The local response, full-state commutator reduction, and EB witness are
analytic mathematical arguments. Small finite matrices separately test the
formula and affinity gap; finite tests do not prove a continuum floor.
The inequality does not prove optimal quantum memory, a large finite
classical advantage, or a thermal-production speedup. Background fidelity,
Lieb concavity, and Bloch-Messiah facts are existing results; the stated
critical experiment/application is a candidate derivation whose external
validation and priority remain open.
