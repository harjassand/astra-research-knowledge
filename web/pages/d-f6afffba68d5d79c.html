EXACT CONTINUOUS-TIME RESULT
---------------------------
In paper III, Condition (CD3), p. 535, requires a positive measurable
norm-like V in the extended-generator domains of the stopped/truncated
processes and constants c>0, d<infinity for which

    A_m V(x) <= -c V(x) + d,     x in O_m, for every m.

The paper's standing state-space setting is a locally compact separable
metric space with a right process; a countable reaction lattice with its
discrete topology fits this topological setting.
“Norm-like” means bounded sublevel sets are precompact (p. 522). Theorem
6.1, p. 535, assumes that Phi is a right process and that all compact sets
are petite for some skeleton chain; together with (CD3), it concludes that
there are beta<1 and B<infinity such that

    ||P^t(x,.) - pi||_f <= B f(x) beta^t,  t>=0, x in X,
    f = V + 1.

The theorem's prose assumption is “all compact sets are petite for some
skeleton chain.” It does not require a separately supplied one-step
minorization on one named set. In the proof, the authors show that (CD3)
implies their nonexplosion condition (CD0), then invoke Theorem 2.1; they
also show that for some delta>0, the delta-skeleton P^delta is irreducible
and aperiodic and all compact sets are P^delta-petite. They derive a
skeleton drift from Dynkin's formula, apply Theorem 6.3 of paper I, and
extend the bound from times n delta to every t=n delta+s. Thus:

* Nonexplosion is a consequence of (CD3) plus the right-process hypothesis,
  not an additional hypothesis of Theorem 6.1. Since (CD3) implies
  A_m V<=d, it implies the paper's upper-growth condition (CD0); Theorem
  2.1 proves nonexplosion.
* Aperiodicity and skeleton irreducibility are obtained in the proof under
  the theorem's hypotheses; they are not omitted from the discrete-time
  step. The proof explicitly needs the aperiodic skeleton to invoke its
  geometric convergence conclusion.
* Petite is weaker than “small at one fixed step.” A set C is petite when
  a randomized sampled kernel K_a(x,.)=integral P^t(x,.)a(dt) has a common
  nonzero lower measure for x in C. A one-step minorization
  P^delta(x,.)>=epsilon nu(.) implies P^delta-smallness and hence
  petiteness, but Theorem 6.1 asks that every compact set be petite for
  some skeleton chain.

For a countable-state reaction network, the same paper gives the more
direct corollary Theorem 7.1, p. 537: for X=Z_+, irreducibility and a
norm-like V satisfying QV(i)<=-cV(i)+d for every state imply the same
V+1 exponential f-norm convergence. Its preceding text notes that a
discrete state space is a T-process. For a multidimensional lattice
N_0^n, Theorem 6.1 applies directly: the state space is countable discrete,
compact sets are finite, and irreducibility plus an aperiodic fixed-step
skeleton makes each finite set petite. Do not silently enlarge Theorem 7.1's
printed X=Z_+ notation to N_0^n; use Theorem 6.1 for that generalization.

DISCRETE-TIME STEP AND SMALLNESS
-------------------------------
Paper III cites Theorem 6.3 of paper I. In paper I, Theorem 6.3 assumes
all compact sets are petite for the discrete chain, the one-step drift
condition (DD4), and V_0 uniformly bounded on compact subsets. In its
aperiodic case it yields R<infinity and rho<1 with

    ||P^n(x,.)-pi||_f <= R (V_0(x)+1) rho^n.

The paper I proof constructs a common precompact drift set from the drift
constants. A small-set minorization is one standard way to verify the
petite/aperiodic condition. The short exact source phrase is “In the
aperiodic case we may improve the upper bound on the rate of convergence”
(Theorem 6.3, p. 564).

RATE-BOX CLAIM: WHAT FOLLOWS AND WHAT DOES NOT
---------------------------------------------
Theorem 6.1 is stated for one process and only asserts existential B and
beta. By itself, it does not assert these constants are uniform over a
parameter family. A common generator drift alone also does not supply
uniform mixing: the skeleton time, minorization measure, or minorization
mass could deteriorate with the rate parameter.

For the compact strictly-positive rate box in
outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle3/leaves/geometric_rate_box.txt,
the missing quantitative condition can be supplied, conditional on the
all-state drift derivation in that leaf's internally-proved-candidate status.
That leaf gives a single norm-like V and common C,c>0 with

    L_theta V <= C - c V     for every state and every theta in the box.

Fix one delta>0, e.g. delta=1. Dynkin's formula gives the common skeleton
drift

    P_theta^delta V <= exp(-c delta) V
                       + (C/c)(1-exp(-c delta)).

A sufficiently large common V-sublevel D is finite and gives the usual
geometric drift outside D. Since every directed reaction rate has a
strictly positive lower endpoint and finite upper endpoint, and the full
lattice is irreducible, choose for each x in D a finite executable
reaction path to one reference state x*. On the finite union of these
paths, every desired jump rate has a positive uniform lower bound and
every total exit rate has a finite uniform upper bound. Requiring just the
chosen path jumps and then no further jump before delta therefore yields
one epsilon>0, independent of theta and x in D, such that

    P_theta^delta(x,.) >= epsilon delta_{x*}(.), x in D, theta in Theta.

The fixed-step skeletons are irreducible (a finite path can occur within
any positive time) and aperiodic (the no-jump event gives
P_theta^delta(x,x)>0). Thus the common skeleton drift, common finite D,
common one-step minorization, and common bound on V over D give common
discrete-time drift/minorization constants. The drift/minorization proof
then yields common R<infinity and rho<1. The semigroup drift bound controls
the remainder s in [0,delta), so for common M,gamma>0,

    sup_theta ||P_theta^t(x,.)-pi_theta||_V
         <= M V(x) exp(-gamma t),     t>=0.

Conditional on that drift proof, this gives a valid uniform-in-rate-box
V-geometric convergence result for the exact compact positive box, provided
the displayed common drift and finite-path minorization argument are
included. It is a deduction from the primary fixed-process theorem plus
uniform quantitative inputs; neither paper states a parameterized rate-box
theorem. The cycle 3 statement “No uniform mixing-time bound is asserted”
is therefore a limitation of that report, not an obstruction in this
setting. If the box allows rates to
approach zero or has no common fixed-step minorization, uniform mixing does
not follow from common drift alone. Also, because V is unbounded, the bound
is V-weighted; it is not a uniform-in-initial-state total-variation bound.
