# CRISPR multiplex resistance: a sharp limit of marginal-rate evidence

## Question

Can single-guide resistance frequencies (p_1,\ldots,p_m), by themselves, justify the claim that a classical (m)-guide homing drive reduces complete target-site escape at the product rate \(\prod_i p_i\)?

## Result: no, without a dependence assumption

For an inherited target-region haplotype, let (X_i=1) mean that guide (i)'s site is unavailable to cleavage (mutated, deleted, or otherwise lost). Let (B=\{X_1=\cdots=X_m=1\}) be complete molecular escape, and suppose only the marginal probabilities \(p_i=\Pr(X_i=1)\) are known. Then the exact sharp bounds are

\[
\max\!\left(0,\sum_{i=1}^m p_i-(m-1)\right)
\;\leq\; \Pr(B) \;\leq\; \min_i p_i.
\]

The upper endpoint is attained by a common-event coupling: for a uniform (U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]), set (X_i=\mathbf 1\{U\le p_i\}). The lower endpoint is attained by arranging the complement events \(\{X_i=0\}\) to cover disjoint portions of a unit interval until their total mass reaches one. Thus the interval cannot be improved using marginals alone. The product \(\prod_i p_i\) is valid only under joint independence (or a separately justified dependence model).

For the equal-rate case (p_i=p>0), the independent prediction is (p^m), while the common-event construction has \(\Pr(B)=p\) for every (m). For example, with four guides and (p=0.05), the independent estimate is (6.25\times10^{-6}), while a perfectly correlated repair event gives (0.05), an 8,000-fold difference with identical per-guide marginals.

This is a molecular escape theorem, not a theorem about functional resistance or population suppression. Let (F\subseteq B) be the event that the allele both evades all guides and preserves enough target function to be selected as functional resistance. Marginals imply only (0\le\Pr(F)\le\min_i p_i). The functional fraction among (B) must be measured or supported by a target-specific functional model. If the sequencing assay can miss large structural variants, add a separately bounded missed-route probability (s): then the conservative ceiling is \(\Pr(F)\le\min(1,\min_i p_i+s)\).

## Biological mechanism and counterexample

The common-event coupling has a concrete repair interpretation: simultaneous breaks can be resolved in one end-joining event that deletes the interval and removes several guide sites at once. It violates per-guide independence even when each single-site marginal is measured without error.

This mechanism has direct, but scope-limited, experimental support. A July 2026 *Anopheles stephensi* study reports that in a classical four-guide construct, 8.5% of all sequenced reads (14% of modified reads) lacked all four target sites; 68% of those all-site-loss reads had one deletion. The authors explicitly state that these were somatic indels, not germline measurements, so the frequency is not a germline escape estimate. Separately, a four-guide *Drosophila hairy* rescue drive produced functional resistance in cage populations; the reported alleles had a large deletion and were thought likely to use an alternate start codon. This is evidence that structural escape and retained function can coincide, not a quantified general rate.

The sharp probability bound itself is standard Fr\u00e9chet coupling mathematics. The research contribution here is a scoped rejection of an inference from guide-level rates to exponential resistance suppression, together with an explicit measurement gate. It should not be presented as a new probability theorem or as evidence that all multiplex drives fail. Current work also demonstrates useful multiplex designs: July 2026 studies report (i) experimental two-/three-site *doublesex* drives plus a mutagenesis/function-screening framework, and (ii) additive *cardinal* targeting that can bypass the single-construct common-deletion route, subject to cumulative fitness costs. Those results are compatible with this bound because they measure or alter additional parts of the system.

## Design gate and falsifier

For a candidate construct, acquire the following on the relevant biological route (sex, germline timing, embryo exposure, and drive background):

1. Single-site cut-resistant marginals (p_i), with counts and uncertainty.
2. A phased, same-haplotype joint count of complete molecular escapes (B), including large deletions and rearrangements spanning the outer target sites. Read-level sequencing must not treat multiple reads from one mosaic germline event as independent observations.
3. Functional evidence on recurrent escape classes: target expression/phenotype or a complementation/fitness readout appropriate to the target gene, including plausible alternate translation starts and isoforms.
4. A separate drive-performance comparison: homing/conversion, maternal or embryo cutting, and construct fitness. Lower resistance frequency does not alone establish stronger suppression or persistence.

The equal-information null comparator is the independence prediction \(\prod_i p_i\); the robust dependence comparator is \(\min_i p_i\). Both use the same marginals. A direct joint-haplotype and functional assay decides between them. An observation of a common multi-site deletion already falsifies the independent repair model for molecular escape. An observed functional resistant allele arising through such a deletion falsifies the claim that guide count alone exponentially suppresses *functional* resistance for that architecture.

If (n) independent germline-origin transmissions yield zero functional escapes, the exact one-sided 95% binomial upper limit is \(1-0.05^{1/n}\). To support a per-transmission risk ceiling \(\epsilon\) with zero observed escapes requires

\[
n\ge\frac{\log(0.05)}{\log(1-\epsilon)}\approx\frac{2.996}{\epsilon}.
\]

For \(\epsilon=10^{-5}\), this is 299,572 independent transmissions. This charge is in biological sampling and functional screening, not model runtime; clustering by parent or early germline mosaicism reduces the effective sample size. The acquisition cost therefore belongs in any claimed deployment-scale guarantee.

## Prior-art boundary and status

Earlier multiplex studies already discuss independence assumptions, simultaneous cutting, deletions, and the tradeoff between guide count and homing. In particular, the 2020 performance model assigns independent per-site resistance formation and assumes simultaneous-cut deletions disrupt target function; it also warns about target spacing and frame restoration. The 2018 experimental paper already describes avoiding large deletions as part of the multiplex design problem. Thus the main biological caveat is not new. The exact coupling inequality is standard. The defensible output is a theorem-backed audit rule and falsifier, not a priority claim.

Status: **internally reconstructed mathematical bound; source-reported mechanism evidence; no independent reproduction of source experiments; no population-level suppression theorem; novelty/priority not claimed.**
