## 1. Independent reconstruction of the global constants

For `a=R^(2m)>b=R^(2n)`, differentiating each log factor separately gives

`d(log(v_m/v_n))/dt = -2a/(1-t²a²)+2b/(1-t²b²)`

`= -2(a-b)(1+t²ab)/[(1-t²a²)(1-t²b²)]`.

Its absolute value is at least `2(a-b)` throughout `[-T,T]`. For positive `v_i,v_i'≥L_i`, `|log v_i-log v_i'|≤|v_i-v_i'|/L_i`. Therefore

`2(a-b)|Δt| ≤ m|Δμ_m|/L_m+n|Δμ_n|/L_n`

and Cauchy–Schwarz gives exactly the displayed `L_t`. In particular the voltage `/n` factors have been included.

Since `log c=log f_m(t)-log v_m`, `|(log f_m)'|≤D_m=2a/(1-T²a²)`. Also `|c-c'|≤c_max|log c-log c'|`, by the mean value theorem for exp between log c and log c'. These give the stated `L_c`. On the parameter rectangle, the exact physical change splits as

`|c(1+t)/(1-t)-c'(1+t')/(1-t')| ≤ K|Δc|+J|Δt|`,

with `K=(1+T)/(1-T)` and `J=2c_max/(1-T)²`. Both intermediate points remain in the rectangle. Taking the Euclidean norm with the second physical coordinate `c` proves exactly `L_phys=sqrt((K L_c+J L_t)²+L_c²)`. Thus this is a global secant bound, stronger than a local-Jacobian diagnostic. Pairwise output amplitudes multiply its lower bound by `min(|g_m|,|g_n|)`, and disjoint-pair energy summation proves the stated κ.

Independent physical differentiation gives

`∂μ_n/∂c1 = -4a_n/[n(c1+c2)²(1+t a_n)²]`,

`∂μ_n/∂c2 = -μ_n/c2+4a_n c1/[n c2(c1+c2)²(1+t a_n)²]`.

These match the implementation exactly.

## 2. Normalized sphere tails and moment range

Uniform `h∈S^(2B-1)` projected onto any chosen B coordinate axes has density

`Γ(B)/[π^(B/2)Γ(B/2)] · (1-||z||²)^(B/2-1)`

on the unit B-ball. For B≥2 this is bounded by `D_B`. If κ(h)<ε, each disjoint pair has at least one coordinate with magnitude less than `δ=ε/c_*`. Choosing such a coordinate in each pair gives 2^B events. Each selected B-coordinate vector lies in a cube of volume `(2δ)^B`. Thus

`P(κ<ε)≤min(1,D_B(4ε/c_*)^B)=min(1,(A_S ε)^B)`.

There is no use of independence after normalization. For `0<p<B`, splitting `p∫_0^∞ z^(p-1)P(κ<1/z) dz` at z=A_S gives

`Eκ^(-p)≤A_S^p+p A_S^p/(B-p)=B A_S^p/(B-p)`.

The same tail integration validates the raw-Gaussian bound. B=1 is appropriately excluded from the bounded-density argument.

For clarity, p<B is sharp for this *particular paired lower bound*: keep the unselected B coordinates away from zero and let the selected B coordinates tend to zero. The projection density is positive near zero, and κ is the weighted Euclidean norm of those small coordinates there. Its small-ball probability is bounded below by a positive constant times ε^B, so its inverse moments diverge for p≥B. This does **not** establish sharpness for the best stability constant of the whole observation map: surviving coordinates from different pairs can still identify the model. The draft correctly separates its proved κ from that best constant.

## 3. Finite reconstruction and noise

For each fixed λ in the admissible interval, `||λw(t)-z||` is L_Y-Lipschitz in t. Infima over the same λ interval preserve that uniform Lipschitz bound. The grid includes endpoints and is an h/2 covering, so its best residual is within η=L_Y h/2 of the continuous minimum. The true parameter has residual ||e||; consequently the retained candidate obeys

`||Y_g(p_hat)-Y_g(p0)||≤||Y_g(p_hat)-z||+||e||≤2||e||+η`.

The script retains the best grid point among its final candidates, so golden-section refinement need not find a global or even local optimum to preserve this guarantee. The displayed MSE inequality follows from `(2||e||+η)²≤8||e||²+2η²` and noise independent of g. For a unit current, `L_Y≤max_i 2x^i/[i c_min(1-Tx^i)²]`, as claimed.

## 4. Fair comparison and exact sufficient statistics

Required interface: known fixed radius; radial scalar conductivities; orthonormal Fourier current/voltage coefficients; fixed nonadaptive currents known to the estimator; independently sampled detector noise with equal variance σ² for each acquired scalar; total energy `Σ_ji α_ji²`; linear ideal detectors without saturation or extra experiment-dependent noise. Detector counts and physical experiment counts are separately charged. Duration/peak constraints or adaptive design require a different comparison.

For mode i let α_i be the vector of applied coefficients and s_i=||α_i||. When s_i>0, rotate its Gaussian data by an orthogonal matrix whose first row is α_i/s_i. The first rotated coordinate is

`z_i=s_i μ_i+N(0,σ²)`.

All remaining coordinates have independent N(0,σ²) laws with zero parameter-dependent mean, because the signal vector is parallel to α_i. Thus z_i is sufficient; ancillary residuals can be generated independently to reconstruct the whole experiment. When s_i=0 every observation of that mode is ancillary. Independence across modes preserves the product experiment. A single current with coefficients s_i has the same informative data and energy `Σ_i s_i²=Σ_ji α_ji²`. It requires one informative detector per active mode, while a physical multi-current experiment can acquire more scalars; those acquisition costs must remain visible.

In particular six pure-mode currents of amplitude 1/√6, one appropriate detector each, give exactly the same six-scalar Gaussian experiment and total energy as the six-mode equal-amplitude current. This is an exact distributional equivalence, not an empirical conjecture. Known signs only rotate the noise and do not change information. Random designs are mixtures of admissible deterministic designs, so their average risk cannot improve on the infimum deterministic risk for the same loss, truth distribution and admissible budget. The audit supports no random superiority claim.

The optimized two-mode comparator is selected using the same declared model and prior range, before trial truths/noises; it is the best of a finite list under a finite-grid Jacobian criterion, rather than a proven globally optimal risk design. It still pays for all six detectors in the simulation. Shared truth/noise draws are a valid paired comparison. The random design is sampled independently of these draws and normalized to exact unit power.
