## General padding lemma

Suppose a finite set of n distinct unit charges has K specified nondegenerate critical points, with disjoint compact witness neighborhoods separated from the charges. For any N>=n, put N-n further unit charges at the sites (R+j,0,0), 1<=j<=N-n. As R tends to infinity, their summed potential gradient and Hessian converge uniformly to zero on those neighborhoods. Quantitative Morse persistence retains all K points. All strengths remain exactly +1 and all sites remain distinct. The new sites leave the near-unit sphere; that boundary must be explicit. This argument is a routine perturbation lemma, with no novelty claim.

## Fully charged bounds for the present compiler

Keep epsilon, rho, eta=epsilon*rho^D from `POLYNOMIAL_BIT_COMPILER.md`, and let n_0 be its original source count. Choose the integer R=N/eta (eta is an inverse power of two), and add the N-n_0 sites above. Work with the entire potential divided by n_0, then rescale by epsilon*rho^D; this common normalization does not change critical points or the fact every physical strength equals one.

On |y|<=2, x=rho*y, every added site is at distance at least R/2. Therefore their rescaled derivative errors are bounded by

    gradient <= 4N / [n_0 epsilon rho^(D-1) R^2]
             = 4 epsilon rho^(D+1)/(n_0 N) <= 4 rho,
    Hessian  <= 16N / [n_0 epsilon rho^(D-2) R^3]
             = 16 epsilon^2 rho^(2D+2)/(n_0 N^2) <= 16 rho.

## Practical algorithmic prior

Chen, Frommer and Lang, *Computational existence proofs for spherical t-designs*, Numerische Mathematik 117(2), 289-305 (2011), DOI 10.1007/s00211-010-0332-5, already describes interval enclosures certifying (t+1)^2-node designs for strengths through 100. The institutional primary abstract and bibliographic record were read; its full proof and software were not read. It establishes that numerical candidate generation followed by rigorous design certification is existing methodology. It does not, from the accessed abstract, supply an all-degree polynomial-time complexity theorem.

The next scientific question is whether these established numerical design methods, or a constructive consequence of the September proof, can acquire the strict Gram/mean interface efficiently. Requiring exact design certification may be unnecessary for our radial inverse, which needs only open inequalities; this is a precise candidate for computational improvement, not an established speedup.
