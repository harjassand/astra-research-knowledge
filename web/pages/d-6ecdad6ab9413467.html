# Independent audit of the r12 four-phase quasifree certificate

Assigned by root after r03's main lead was completed. This proof was reconstructed directly from the supplied formula; r12's forthcoming derivation was not yet present when checked. No source/theorem novelty is inferred from worker agreement.

## Interface and exact statement

Finite fermionic modes i=1,...,m. C is a Hermitian contraction 0<=C<=I. For a real phase function phi on the modes, U_phi=diag(exp(i phi_i)) and

S(phi)=Tr C−Tr(C U_phi^dagger C U_phi).

For phase functions phi_1,...,phi_r define a real symmetric matrix with

M_aa=S(phi_a),
M_ab=[S(phi_a+phi_b)−S(phi_a−phi_b)]/4, a!=b.

Then M is positive semidefinite for any r. Phases may be arbitrary diagonal functions, not only linear momenta on a translation-invariant system. Products of the U's commute. The assertion does not cover arbitrary noncommuting unitaries.

## Independent complete derivation

Let w_ij=|C_ij|²>=0 and b=Tr C−Tr C²=Tr(C(I−C))>=0. Since C is Hermitian,

Tr(C U_phi^dagger C U_phi)=sum_ij w_ij exp[i(phi_i−phi_j)]
                             =sum_ij w_ij cos(phi_i−phi_j).

Consequently S(phi)=b+sum_ij w_ij(1−cos(phi_i−phi_j)). Diagonal i=j terms vanish in this sum. For each pair (i,j), put delta_a=phi_a,i−phi_a,j and v_ij=(sin delta_1,...,sin delta_r)^T. The cosine difference identity gives the off-diagonal entry

M_ab=(1/2)sum_ij w_ij sin delta_a sin delta_b.

Finally 1−cos t=(1/2)sin²t+(1/2)(1−cos t)². Thus

M=b I_r + (1/2)sum_ij w_ij v_ij v_ij^T
  + (1/2)diag_a[sum_ij w_ij(1−cos delta_a)²].

Every term is PSD. This is a universal algebraic proof, not an inference from finite tests. The proposed 2x2 inequality follows by r=2 and entails

|S(phi_x+phi_y)−S(phi_x−phi_y)| <=4 sqrt(S(phi_x)S(phi_y)).

For countably infinite trace-class C, finite-mode truncation or absolute summability (sum_ij w_ij=Tr C²<=Tr C<infinity) also justifies the sums and the same proof. Infinite systems with divergent total density require a separate volume normalization/limit argument.

## Why this is the physical connected structure factor

For a gauge-invariant quasifree fermionic state, let C_ij=<c_i^dagger c_j> and N_phi=sum_i exp(i phi_i)n_i. Wick factorization yields the connected density covariance

Cov(n_i,n_j)=delta_ij C_ii−|C_ij|².

Hence <(N_phi−<N_phi>)^dagger(N_phi−<N_phi>)> equals the supplied S(phi). This holds for mixed quasifree states (eigenvalues of C anywhere in [0,1]) as well as pure Slaters (C projector). Number-conserving/gauge-invariant is load-bearing: anomalous contractions of a general paired Gaussian state change the formula. Experimental use must subtract the mean Fourier density and account for any anomalous contributions instead of applying the displayed formula blindly.

## Decisive convex-mixture counterexample

Consider four modes and the equal classical mixture of Fock/Slater states |1100> and |0011>. Each component is a pure gauge-invariant quasifree state and each has total particle number 2. The mixture has C=(1/2)I but is not quasifree: occupation fluctuations have correlations that fail Wick factorization.

Choose exp(i phi)=(1,−1,i,−i), equivalently phi=(0,pi,pi/2,3pi/2). In either Slater component N_phi=0, so the measured connected S(phi)=0. For doubled phases exp(2i phi)=(1,1,−1,−1), N_2phi is respectively 2 or −2, so S(2phi)=4. Total N_0 is exactly 2 in either component, hence S(0)=0.

Take phi_x=phi_y=phi. The displayed test matrix is [[0,1],[1,0]], whose eigenvalues are +1 and −1. The witness vector (1,−1) has quadratic form −2. This is an exact violation by a convex mixture of Gaussian states, including a fixed-N mixture. A small perturbation of one phase function retains negative eigenvalue by continuity, so the distinction is not an artifact of using identical phase functions.

The Gaussian state with the same C=(1/2)I instead has S(phi)=1 for every phase and satisfies the certificate. This explicitly shows why substituting the one-body covariance of a nonquasifree state into the Gaussian formula does not reproduce its measured connected structure factor.

Therefore a statistically significant violation excludes a single gauge-invariant quasifree state with the claimed covariance/Wick model. It does not certify departure from the convex hull of Gaussian states, a distinction crucial to resource-theoretic use of the word non-Gaussian.

## Validation and downstream extension

work/r03_quantum/check_geometry_mixture.py enumerates the two occupations and exact complex phases in {1,-1,i,-i}; it verifies the connected values, negative quadratic form, and differing Gaussian prediction for identical C. No rounded trigonometric evaluation is used. results/r03_quantum/geometry_mixture_certificate.json records it.

The r-phase matrix extension is immediately usable as a family of observable null constraints: one diagonal phase structure factor per coordinate and two summed/differenced phases per pair. A scalar 2x2 pass does not imply all r-dimensional principal matrices pass. Finite-data application should use simultaneous confidence bounds or a preregistered negative witness vector; optimizing eigenvectors on the same noisy estimates requires uncertainty accounting.

Novelty unresolved: the algebra reduces to a positive Gram representation and standard Wick covariance. Independent derivation establishes validity and scope, not priority.
