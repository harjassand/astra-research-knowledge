## 1. Introduce a hereditary family of fields

First D=I; real invertible diagonal covariance transports the result later. For 1<=N<=n and k=n-N define

    R_N(y)=sum_(i=1)^N y_i^2,
    f_N(y)=a sum_i y_i^4+b R_N(y)^2,
    H_N=Hess f_N,   h_N=det H_N,
    F_(N,k)=adj H_N grad[R_N^k h_N].              (1)

The initial field is F_(n,0). Every f_N corresponds to the PD principal matrix aI_N+bJ_N: for b<0, a+Nb>=a+nb>0; for b>=0 this is immediate. Homogeneous degree of F_(N,k) is4N+2k-3. All fields are sign-equivariant, so every coordinate ideal(y_i) is invariant.

On the face y_N=0 the full Hessian is block diagonal with upper block H_(N-1) and lower scalar4b R_(N-1). Its adjugate upper block is4b R_(N-1) adj H_(N-1), and

    h_N|_(y_N=0)=4b R_(N-1) h_(N-1).

Consequently, for b!=0,

    F_(N,k)|_(y_N=0)
       =(4b)^2 R_(N-1) F_(N-1,k+1).            (2)

This is the key repair: the restriction is not the original lower-dimensional natural field, but belongs to the enlarged family after multiplication by a nonzero polynomial. The extra index k records every lost dimension, keeping N+k=n throughout the induction.

For any derivation D and nonzero polynomial eta, repeated application has

    (eta D)^j y_i=eta^j D^j y_i
                  +sum_(l<j)c_(j,l)D^l y_i.

Thus the N-variable coordinate extactic transforms as

    E_(eta D)=eta^[N(N-1)/2]E_D.                (3)

Equation(3) is row triangularity and makes(2) a valid nonvanishing inheritance statement even though eta is variable.

## 2. Every axis repels F_(N,k) and attracts -F_(N,k)

Assume b!=0 and N>=2. At any axis e_i put A=a+b and

    c=h_N(e_i)=12A(4b)^(N-1),

which is nonzero. The Hessian there is diagonal:12A in coordinate i and4b transversely. Its adjugate has entries c/(12A) and c/(4b). Put g=R_N^k h_N. Euler and sign symmetry give

    partial_i g(e_i)=2(N+k)c,
    partial_j g(e_i)=0 for j!=i,
    partial_jj g(e_i)=partial_jj h_N(e_i)+2kc.

Therefore

    F_i(e_i)=(N+k)c^2/(6A),

and a direct adjugate/Hessian derivative calculation yields the transverse projective slope

    [partial_j F_j(e_i)-F_i(e_i)]/(c^2/2)
      = a[3a+(N+k+4)b]/[b^2(a+b)]
      = a[3a+(n+4)b]/[b^2(a+b)].               (4)

To check(4) independently, the k=0 formula is

    3(a+b)/b^2+(N-2)/b-(N+1)/(a+b)
      =a[3a+(N+4)b]/[b^2(a+b)].

The g factor R_N^k contributes k[1/b-1/(a+b)]=ka/[b(a+b)] to the scaled slope; adding it gives(4). This follows from partial_j adj(H)_ji=-c/(6A), grad_i g=2(N+k)c and partial_jj g=h_jj+2kc, so no unproved projection identity is being invoked.

The quantity in(4) is positive. If b>0, every factor in its numerator is positive. If b<0, the assumption b>-a/n implies

    3a+(n+4)b > a(2-4/n)>=0,

with strict positivity also at n=2. Also A=a+b>0. Hence every transverse ratio has a strictly negative linear coefficient for -F_(N,k).

Writing r_j=y_j/y_i with y_i>0 and changing time by ds/dt=y_i^[4N+2k-4], the ratio ODE is analytic near r=0 with that strictly negative linear part and corrections vanishing at r=0. A sufficiently small cube about zero is forward invariant and all |r_j| decay exponentially. The equation for log y_i has bounded coefficients on the cube, so y_i remains positive and finite for every finite s. An open real cone therefore approaches the i-axis projectively. Distinct decay exponents are not required: all transverse rates here are tied by symmetry.

## 3. Extactic nonvanishing by induction on N

Use D_(N,k)=-F_(N,k) dot grad. For N=1, E=y_1!=0. Suppose E_(N-1,k+1)!=0. Choose any j-axis as the attracting axis from Section2. The polynomial minor W consisting of coordinate columns i!=j and derivative rows0,...,N-2 restricts on y_j=0 to the extactic of the lower-dimensional field times

    [(4b)^2 R_(N-1)]^[(N-1)(N-2)/2].

By(2)-(3) this is nonzero, so W is nonzero as an ambient polynomial.

If the full E_(N,k) vanished, solve its first N-1 rows over the rational-function field to get

    D^l y_j=sum_(i!=j)c_i(y)D^l y_i,
                  0<=l<=N-2.

The final-row dependence gives the same identity at l=N-1. Differentiate each identity and subtract the next; invert W to obtain Dc_i=0. In particular y_j=sum c_i(y)y_i.

Choose an initial point in the attracting cone with W!=0. Its c_i are finite and initially constant along the trajectory. Analytic continuation gives

    y_j(s)=sum_(i!=j)c_i(initial)y_i(s)

for every finite s, even if a rational representative later has a pole. Divide by y_j(s) and let s->infinity: the right side tends to zero, contradicting1. Thus E_(N,k)!=0. The induction proves E_(n,0)!=0. Replacing -F by F changes its determinant by the nonzero constant(-1)^[n(n-1)/2], so the implemented field has the same nonvanishing status.

For b=0, use the separate diagonal Vandermonde-flow proof in the main note. It already handles arbitrary positive diagonal coefficients and every n, so no limit argument through the b!=0 proof is needed.
