## Every affine-dual completion

Let Y have three fiber variables. A fixed invertible polynomial matrix U has
first row g and `(t,z)=UY`, with t=A. If another matrix V has the same first
row, then the first row of V U^{-1} is `(1,0,0)`, so

`V U^{-1}=[[1,0],[h,C]]`, C polynomial invertible.

Adding arbitrary polynomial base translations gives
`t'=A+delta`, `r'=h A+C z+b`. If B=beta_t A+gamma z, substitution gives

`B=(beta_t-gamma C^{-1}h)(t'-delta)+(gamma C^{-1})r'-(gamma C^{-1})b`.

Put G=gamma C^{-1}, beta'=beta_t-Gh. For potential L A²+M A+N B and
constant conjugate level sigma, the critical equation of f-sigma t' solves

`t'=delta+(sigma-M-N beta')/(2L)`.

Completing the square yields the exact critical value

`psi_sigma= -(sigma-M-N beta')²/(4L)-NGb-sigma delta+NG r'`.

Thus residual affine changes, translations and shears alter G by a
polynomial matrix gauge and alter only the base polynomial otherwise.
They do not create a different interface than `h(x,y)+N G(x,y).r`.

## Four-variable gradient iff plane Keller map

The Hessian of psi=h+N G.r has the symmetric block form
`[[*,N DG^T],[N DG,0]]`; hence

`det Hess psi=N^4 (det DG)^2`.

The determinant is a nonzero constant iff det DG is a nonzero constant.
Its gradient is `(grad h+N DG^T r,NG)`. Under this condition:

* an equal-gradient pair must have an equal-G pair of bases, and DG
  invertible then determines each residual r;
* conversely an equal-G pair of distinct bases gives an equal-gradient
  pair by choosing any common first block a and setting
  `r=(N DG^T)^{-1}(a-grad h)` separately at each base;
* a polynomial inverse of G gives a polynomial inverse of the gradient;
* a polynomial inverse of the gradient supplies a polynomial right inverse
  of G by fixing the first target block to zero. A right inverse is
  dominant, and `R o G=id` follows on its dense image, so it is two-sided.

This proves the stated equivalence. The base polynomial h cannot repair a
failure of constant Jacobian or create a collision if G is injective.

## Constructive maximal-ideal converse

The pinned prior toric normalizer identifies all determinant-one residual
gauges with `G=(y,2x) D`, D in SL2(k[x,y]). The forward implications are
immediate:

`(G1,G2)=(x,y)`, `G(0)=0`, `det DG(0)=-2`.

Conversely suppose the component ideal equals (x,y) and det DG(0)=-2.
Write G=u D0, u=(y,2x). This is possible because G1,G2 are in (x,y).
The derivative at the origin gives det D0(0)=1. Therefore det D0-1 lies
in (x,y)=(G1,G2), so choose polynomials a,b with

`det D0-1=a G1+b G2`.

Set n=(-2x,y)^T. Then u n=0, and the exact two-dimensional adjugate
identity is

`adj(D0) n=(-G2,G1)^T`.

For `D=D0+n(b,-a)`, the row G is unchanged. The rank-one determinant
identity gives

`det D=det D0+(b,-a) adj(D0)n`
`     =det D0-bG2-aG1=1`.

Thus every such polynomial G occurs. This works in every degree and needs
no finite-degree search. The maximal-ideal assumption and determinant
normalization at the origin are both used explicitly.

Over C, a Keller map whose geometric zero fiber is the singleton {0} has
a reduced zero fiber: the Jacobian is invertible at that point, so the
fiber scheme is etale and reduced there. Its finite coordinate algebra has
support only at0, hence equals C; its component ideal is (x,y). Conversely
the ideal equality already gives that fiber exactly. A finite etale map in
this class has degree1 from this fiber, hence is an isomorphism. A successful
counterexample in the class would therefore have to be nonproper.

## Missing theorem and primary prior context

The remaining question is precisely whether a complex plane Keller map
with `(G1,G2)=(x,y)` must be a polynomial automorphism. Nothing above answers
it. Calling it a singleton-zero lemma does not make it routine.

Frederico Xavier, [*Geometric and topological aspects of the passage from
local to global invertibility*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40863-024-00482-z),
published 2025-01-16, Section2.1 Example1, records that a cubic-homogeneous
Keller map I+H automatically has singleton zero by Euler's identity:
`DF(x)x=3F(x)-2x`; F(x)=0 at nonzero x would make -2 an eigenvalue of DF,
contradicting the unipotent Jacobian. The same section's neighborhood
criterion requires all nearby fibers to be singletons and expressly notes
that the zero-fiber argument provides no such control.

That primary result concerns arbitrary dimensions and does not close the
plane singleton-zero case here. It explains why a singleton special fiber
is not itself a global inverse certificate. A short literature search found
this relevant context, not an exhaustive historical audit or a theorem
settling the missing plane case. The source's topology/conformal theorems
were not fully read or adopted.

## Independent projective-boundary gate

The peer subsequently proposed the following sharper gate. I independently
reconstructed it rather than inferring properness from a singleton special
fibre. Let G:C²->C² be Keller, let its generic degree be d, and suppose
`G^{-1}(0)={0}`. If d>1, then0 must lie in its nonproper-value locus.

Here is a direct graph argument, identifying the established geometric
prerequisites. Close the graph in `P²_source x A²_target`, and let B be its
intersection with the source hyperplane at infinity. The projective
projection maps the closed B to a closed set A in the target. The graph
has dimension2; B has dimension at most1, so A is proper. Over the open
complement of A there is no graph boundary, and the graph projection is
proper. A Keller map is etale and quasi-finite, so this proper restriction
is finite etale. Its rank is its generic degree d. These are the standard
proper-quasi-finite/finite and finite-etale rank facts; no unresolved
Jacobian statement is being used.

If0 were outside A, its fibre would consequently have length d. The
singleton Keller fibre has length1, a contradiction when d>1. Equivalently,
empty projective boundary fibre over0 certifies properness near0 and forces
d=1. The graph restriction is then a finite etale map of rank1, hence an
isomorphism on that open set. Degree1 also makes G birational. The remaining
birational Keller implication has a short independent argument here. Write
a coordinate of its rational inverse as a(q)/b(q), with a,b coprime.
Substitution gives `a(G)=x_i b(G)`. If an irreducible nonconstant h divides
b, then h(G) is nonconstant by dominance. Choose an irreducible curve in
its zero set. Its image under the quasi-finite G is infinite, hence dense
in the irreducible target curve h=0. On this source curve a(G)=0, so a
vanishes on h=0 and h divides a, contradicting coprimality. Thus b is
constant. Every inverse coordinate is polynomial, proving the global
automorphism conclusion. A singleton special fibre by itself supplies
none of the properness or birationality needed by this argument.

For an analytic form, choose a generic line `q(t)=t v` not contained in A.
An arbitrarily small punctured disc on it avoids A. At each nonzero t the
fibre has d distinct points. The analytic inverse at0 supplies exactly one
bounded point. Every other point escapes uniformly to infinity as t->0:
otherwise a bounded subsequence would limit to the unique zero and
eventually enter its inverse neighbourhood, where the inverse point is
unique. The d-1 escaping points can have monodromy; the proof does not
require single-valued labels on the punctured disc. Transversality at0 is
also unnecessary and could fail if A is singular there.

There is an exact algebraic interface for a candidate. Homogenize each
component G_i to H_i(X0,X1,X2) of its degree m_i, and form in the ring
`C[X0,X1,X2,q1,q2]`

`J=(H1-q1 X0^m1,H2-q2 X0^m2):X0^infinity`.

Then the boundary fibre at zero is the projective scheme with homogeneous
coordinate ring

`C[X0,X1,X2,q1,q2]/(J+(X0,q1,q2))`.

An empty Proj gives the properness certificate above. The saturation is
taken **before** specializing the target to zero: specializing the affine
zero fibre first only records its finite points and can discard limiting
points at infinity. The ideal `(G1,G2)=(x,y)` is not an empty-boundary
certificate. Computing the boundary is a falsifiable next step for an
actual candidate; none has been supplied here.

## Exact escaping fibres of the displayed three-variable prior map

The displayed F in Meng--Yang v1 Section2 (attributed there to Alpoge) gives
a useful completely explicit check of that logical gap in dimension3.
This is a further deduction from a published prior formula; no historical
priority or new Jacobian counterexample claim is made. With u=1+xy,

`F=(u³t+y²u(4+3xy), y+3xu²t+3xy²(4+3xy), 2x-3x²y-x³t)`.

Its independently checked Jacobian is -2. Solve its entire fibre over
`(s,0,0)` by exhausting x=0 and x!=0. If x=0, the second coordinate gives
y=0 and the first gives t=s, so there is precisely `(0,0,s)` on this branch.
If x!=0, its third coordinate gives

`t=(2-3xy)/x²`.

Substituting this into its first two coordinates gives exactly

`F1=(1+xy)(2+xy)/x²`, `F2=(6+4xy)/x`.

Thus F2=0 forces xy=-3/2, then t=13/(2x²) and F1=-1/(4x²).
There is no such point when s=0. Hence `F^{-1}(0)={(0,0,0)}`. For s!=0,
the two additional points are exactly

`x²=-1/(4s), y=-3/(2x), t=-26s`.

They escape as s->0. A single rational parametrization displaying the
projective boundary is

`(x,y,t)=(1/w,-3w/2,13w²/2)`, `F=(-w²/4,0,0)`.

In P³_source it becomes
`[X0:X1:X2:T]=[w:1:-3w²/2:13w³/2]`, tending to `[0:1:0:0]` over target0.
For each nonzero s there are three points in this special target-axis
fibre; that fact alone is not a generic fibre-count proof. It is sufficient
to demonstrate both noninjectivity and why a singleton zero fibre does not
ensure properness. This example concerns dimension3 and does not answer
the missing plane singleton-zero lemma.

There is also a separate independently reconstructed generic-degree proof,
following the peer's message that a dense-chart cubic exists. Denote the
target by (a,b,c) and put `w=y+1/x` on x!=0. Eliminating t via the third
output gives

`b=2/x+4w-3cw²`, `2a=cw³-2w²+bw`.

Define `D=b-4w+3cw²`. The source field is recovered exactly by

`x=2/D`, `y=w-D/2`, `t=5D²/4-3wD/2-cD³/8`.

Thus `C(x,y,t)=C(a,b,c)(w)`, where w obeys
`cw³-2w²+bw-2a=0`. The target coordinates are algebraically independent
because Jac F=-2. The displayed cubic is irreducible in C[a,b,c,w]: its
quotient is the polynomial domain C[b,c,w], by eliminating a, so its
principal ideal is prime. It is primitive as a polynomial in w over
C[a,b,c] because one coefficient is the unit -2. Gauss's lemma therefore
makes it irreducible over C(a,b,c). Its degree in w is3, establishing
function-field degree3, hence three distinct generic points in
characteristic zero. The exact substitutions in the checker give
`F1=a+(cw³-2w²+bw-2a)/2`, `F2=b`, `F3=c`, and recover w itself.

This proof independently verifies the generic-degree claim for the
published F that v1 had taken from the announcement. It does not assert a
generic fibre count for the five-variable gradient of Psi, and it adds no
priority claim to that published result.
