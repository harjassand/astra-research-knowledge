# Finite directional geometry from twists and a Gaussian null test

Status: elementary universal finite-dimensional statements reconstructed here, with numerical checks and independent audit requested. Novel application/estimator status not established; underlying Wick, determinant and trigonometric identities have extensive prior art.

## Bott scalar, exact prior-art relation

For a finite square torus, let P be an orthogonal occupied projector of rank m, Q=I-P, U_v=exp[2pi i(v_x X+v_y Y)/L], with integer v. On the occupied subspace set A_v=P U_v P and E_v=I-A_v^dagger A_v>=0. Define

D_v=-(2pi)^(-1) log det(I-E_v), F_v=(2pi)^(-1) Tr E_v.

D_v=+infinity if singular. Chatterjee et al., arXiv:2604.04447v1 (2026-04-06), Eq (18) and Supplement Lemma III.2 give M_b=D_x+D_y for the adjoint plaquette. This part is an author result, not our invention. It relies on adjoints, not inverse commutators or polar-unitarized Bott matrices.

For the Slater determinant Psi of any occupied orthonormal frame, exterior algebra gives

z_v=<Psi| exp[i(2pi/L) sum_a v.r_a]|Psi>=det(A_v).

Resta-Sorella's finite localization parameter is ell_v=-(L^2/(4pi^2 m)) log |z_v|^2. Therefore D_v=(2pi m/L^2)ell_v and M_b=(2pi m/L^2)(ell_x+ell_y), exactly at finite size. The determinant formula and localization formula appear in Sgiarovello et al., cond-mat/0101440v2, Sec II Eq (7) and Sec III Eq (13), following Resta-Sorella 1998 and Souza-Wilkens-Martin 2000. The new Bott packaging must be compared to this existing observable; directional localization alone is not a credible novelty claim.

## Four-momentum PSD theorem

Consider any number-conserving fermionic Gaussian state, pure or mixed. Let C be its one-particle matrix, 0<=C<=I, and assume anomalous contractions <c_i c_j> vanish. Each mode i is attached to a torus position r_i, with internal orbitals permitted. Define rho_q=sum_i exp(i q.r_i)n_i and the **connected**, unnormalized static density structure factor

S(q)=<rho_q^dagger rho_q>-|<rho_q>|^2.

Wick's theorem yields the exact finite formula

S(q)=Tr C-Tr(C U_q^dagger C U_q).

Set b=Tr C-Tr C^2>=0 and w_ij=|C_ij|^2. Using Hermiticity and interchange i,j,

S(q)=b+sum_ij [1-cos(q.(r_i-r_j))]w_ij.

For any two phase functions q_x.r_i and q_y.r_i (reciprocal coordinate directions are only one choice), define

T=[[S(q_x), (S(q_x+q_y)-S(q_x-q_y))/4], [same,S(q_y)]].

For a=(q_x.(r_i-r_j))/2 and d=(q_y.(r_i-r_j))/2, the summand equals twice w_ij times

B(a,d)=[[sin^2 a, sin a sin d cos a cos d], [same,sin^2 d]].

B is PSD: diagonal entries are nonnegative and det B=sin^2 a sin^2 d (1-cos^2 a cos^2 d)>=0. Summing and adding b I proves T>=0. Hence

**|S(q_x+q_y)-S(q_x-q_y)| <= 4 sqrt[S(q_x)S(q_y)].**

This requires no translation invariance, locality, gap, thermodynamic limit, or Hamiltonian knowledge. It does require one fixed gauge-invariant Gaussian state, not an arbitrary mixture of Gaussian states. Pairing/anomalous Gaussian states require a different Wick expression and are excluded.

For a pure Slater, b=0 and S(q_v)=Tr E_v, so T/(2pi) is a finite PSD estimator of the real integrated metric tensor from four periodic twists. Its finite PSD property is stronger than merely converging to a PSD tensor.

Independent r03_quantum reconstruction: write phi_alpha=2a_alpha. The r-dimensional generalization has

T=b I+(1/2)sum_ij w_ij s s^T+(1/2)diag_alpha sum_ij w_ij(1-cos phi_alpha)^2,

where s_alpha=sin phi_alpha. Diagonal entries are S(q_alpha) and offdiagonals the same polarized difference. This proves PSD in any dimension; it uses r coordinate and 2 per pair diagonal directions. This independent proof is saved by that worker, not treated as external validation.

## Finite error from log determinant to leakage tensor

If eta_v=||E_v||<1, the exact scalar log remainder obeys

0<=R_v=D_v-F_v<=Tr E_v^2/[4pi(1-eta_v)].

This is the source Supplement Lemma III.3 with normalization restored. Polarize D and F as above. Their tensor operator-norm difference is at most

max(R_x,R_y)+(R_plus+R_minus)/4.

Thus any negative eigenvalue of the log-polarized tensor has magnitude bounded by this certificate, because the leakage tensor is PSD. The certificate fails at singular twists; F remains finite and defined there.

For a displacement-averaged projector correlator c_L(r)=L^-2 sum_u Tr P_u,u+r P_u+r,u, c_L>=0. For integer directions,

F_v=L^2/pi sum_r sin^2[pi(v.r)/L]c_L(r).

The finite second-moment tensor G^L_ab=pi sum_r r_a r_b c_L(r) is PSD. With |sin t-t|<=|t|^3/6, the diagonal leakage-tensor error is bounded by

|T^F_xx-G^L_xx| <= pi^3/(3 L^2)sum_r r_x^4 c_L(r),

and its mixed error by

|T^F_xy-G^L_xy| <= 2pi^3/(3 L^2)sum_r (|r_x||r_y|^3+|r_y||r_x|^3)c_L(r).

Here T^F=T/(2pi). The mixed formula uses [F_plus-F_minus]/4=(L^2/(4pi))sum sin(2pi r_x/L)sin(2pi r_y/L)c_L. These are finite inequalities, not assertions that a finite periodic sample uniquely determines an infinite-volume metric. Uniform fourth moments plus source thermodynamic-compatibility assumption imply convergence; the limit rate of c_L itself is separate.

## Exact counterexample: log polarization loses PSD under uniform locality

Give every cell r two internal orbitals A_r,B_r. Occupy psi_r=(|r,A>+|r+(1,1),B>)/sqrt(2). The psi_r are mutually orthogonal, rank m=L^2, and their projector has strictly bounded range independent of L. It is the occupied projector of a uniformly gapped decoupled-dimer Hamiltonian.

Writing t=pi/L, the twist singular value for direction v is |cos[(v_x+v_y)t]|. Hence D_x=D_y=-(L^2/(2pi))log cos^2 t, D_minus=0 and D_plus=-(L^2/(2pi))log cos^2(2t).

The smallest eigenvalue of the polarized log tensor is

D_x-D_plus/4<0 for every L>4.

Proof: c=cos^2 t in (1/2,1), and cos^2(2t)=(2c-1)^2<c^4 since c^2-(2c-1)=(1-c)^2>0. Expanding gives lambda_min=-pi^3/(4L^2)+O(L^-4). The true tensor is (pi/2)[[1,1],[1,1]]; the leakage tensor is PSD and converges to it, with its smallest eigenvalue +pi^3/(2L^2)+O(L^-4). Thus no localization pathology is needed for finite log polarization to fail.

## Gaussian interpretation obstruction

On a 3x3 torus, let A_a occupy the three sites (x,x+a mod 3), a=0,1,2. The coherent equal superposition of these Fock states, and their classical equal mixture, give the same density-diagonal observations. Means are homogeneous, and

S(q_x)=S(q_y)=S(q_x+q_y)=0, S(q_x-q_y)=9.

T=[[0,-9/4],[-9/4,0]] has eigenvalues +/-9/4. This refutes a single gauge-invariant Gaussian description but the classical mixture is explicitly convex-Slater, so it cannot certify mixed-state fermionic entanglement. The test is also satisfied by some non-Gaussian states; passing is not confirmation of Gaussianity.
