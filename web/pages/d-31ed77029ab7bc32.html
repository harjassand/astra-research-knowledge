N593-chebyshev-signed-particle-fast-forwarding-obstruction | Chebyshev fast-forwarding has exponential signed-mass cost on regular-tree walks
status=source_derived_unreviewed; The exact tree calculation is source-reported and was not independently reconstructed by intake. It rules out one stated estimator mechanism only. External review and any general fast-forwarding conclusion remain unresolved.
Symbols and resource contracts LOCAL to this card.

Claim: For simple random walk P=A/d on the infinite d-regular tree, d>=3, the Chebyshev polynomial T_k(P) has signed endpoint mass satisfying sum_y |T_k(P)(x,y)| >= [2(d-1)/d]^(k-1), for k>=1. The same witness applies in a finite d-regular graph when the radius-k neighborhood of x is a tree. Consequently any single signed-particle estimator W delta_Y with E[W 1{Y=y}]=T_k(P)(x,y) has E|W| at least this large and E[W^2] at least its square. This is a source-reported exact obstruction to the proposed signed-particle representation.
Interface: Classical simple random walk on a d-regular tree, Chebyshev first-kind polynomial, and an unbiased one-particle signed representation of the polynomial endpoint measure. The result concerns this estimator form and this local tree witness, not arbitrary access to a finite transition matrix or global algorithms.
Proof spine: At distance k, lower powers vanish and P^k(x,y)=d^-k. The leading coefficient of T_k is 2^(k-1), and there are d(d-1)^(k-1) vertices at distance k. Summing the absolute mass over that sphere gives the exponential lower bound. Unbiasedness forces E|W| to dominate the l1 norm, and Jensen gives E[W^2]>=(E|W|)^2.
Costs: For d>=3 the base 2(d-1)/d exceeds one, so the l1 mass and second-moment requirement grow exponentially with k for this representation. A positive universal-cover/nonbacktracking representation is also given, but its expected reduced path length remains linear in t for d>=3; the d=2 diffusive case is standard.
Failure: This is not a general classical fast-forwarding lower bound, a lower bound for all signed decompositions using multiple particles, or an obstruction to explicit global-state access. The separate bounded-radius locality observation applies only to local-output procedures. The broader proposed capability was stopped at this mechanism gate.
Closest comparator: The source credits established Chebyshev/nonbacktracking representations, including Apers–Sarlette quantum fast-forwarding, Alon–Benjamini–Lubetzky–Sodin nonbacktracking walks, and Biswas–Pyne–Rubinfeld local random-walk access. These works use distinct access models; the present obstruction does not subsume them.
Open verification: Test a different positive or global-access mechanism against this signed-mass obstruction without inferring a general no-fast-forwarding theorem from it.
