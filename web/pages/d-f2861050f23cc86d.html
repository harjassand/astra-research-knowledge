Bounded primary-source priority and assumption audit of the Schur-sector depth candidate
Worker c09_s01; closed 2026-10-08 Australia/Brisbane. Imported manuscripts remain in owned work sources only.

Disposition: the column-wedge upper construction is explicit prior art. Its joint-twirl consequence gives depth(P_lambda/Tr P_lambda) <= ell(lambda) and d-producibility of central Werner states. Priority of the arbitrary k-product row-count support lower bound remains UNKNOWN. No absence of a search hit is evidence of novelty. This audit does not replace the separately assigned mathematical proof reviews.

Candidate interface: N,d >= 1, lambda a partition of N with ell(lambda) <= d, and P_lambda the full Schur-Weyl isotypic orthogonal projector on (C^d)^{tensor N}. Entanglement depth is the least k permitting a convex decomposition into pure products over arbitrary disjoint clusters of size at most k. Central Werner means invariance under both U^{tensor N} for every U in U(d) and every party permutation. The candidate is exact sector depth ell(lambda), not a formula for the depth of an arbitrary mixture of sectors.

The direct antecedent is Robin Reuvers, “Lower bound on entanglement in subspaces defined by Young diagrams,” J. Math. Phys. 60, 012201 (2019), Lemma 13, Eq. (61). For a column-ordered tableau t with column heights c_1 >= ... >= c_r, it places v = tensor_j(u_1 wedge ... wedge u_{c_j}) in Im(P_t). The full lemma and its induction proof were read. Its principal Theorem 1 instead optimizes a Schmidt coefficient for one party versus N-1; Corollary 2 bounds that bipartite entropy. Neither reviewed statement names the arbitrary k-product row-count lower theorem.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1805.00364v2 ; published DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5050904

Our direct implication: v above is a product over column clusters, each of size at most c_1=ell(lambda), and lies wholly in the lambda isotypic sector. Joint U(d) and S_N twirling makes its density operator scalar on V_lambda tensor S_lambda by irreducibility, with trace one; hence the result is P_lambda/Tr P_lambda. Each twirled term preserves cluster size. Finite-dimensional convex closure justifies the integral mixture. A jointly invariant state is a convex mixture of these normalized projectors, so its depth is at most d. This is a prior-derived corollary even if its exact wording is absent from the source.

The closest direct central-family comparison is Rico, Grinko, Krebs and Zaw, published as “Entanglement Structure and Matrix Inequalities from Isotypic Measurements,” PRL 137, 100203 (2026). The arXiv title is “Detection of many-body entanglement partitions in a quantum computer.” Its Definition 1 and symmetry (8) use the same two symmetries and permuted cluster partitions. ArXiv Proposition 3, including Appendix B proof, gives an exact N=3 characterization: full separability iff p_21-4p_111 >=0 and 3p_3-p_21+p_111 >=0; biseparability iff p_21-2p_111 >=0. The three normalized sector vertices therefore already have depths 1,2,3 when present. ArXiv Theorem 4 states the all-N minimum of sum c_lambda P_lambda over [N-1|1]-separable states through interlacing partitions. This theorem interface, and the described grouping extensions, do not directly state the arbitrary k-product row-count lower bound. The published main paper and table, and the arXiv statement, definitions and relevant N=3 proof, were inspected; no claim of complete proof verification of its all-N theorem is made.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13822v1 ; https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/nvk2-h8d5

Moharramipour, Lessa, Wang, Hsieh and Sahu, “Symmetry-Enforced Entanglement in Maximally Mixed States,” PRX Quantum 5, 040336 (2024), treats normalized invariant/singlet projectors. For fundamental SU(d) and N=md, that projector is the rectangular Schur sector lambda=(m^d). Results 2-5, Lemma III.1, the bipartite decomposition and preparation discussion, and Appendix C were reviewed. Its exact E_F=E_D, logarithmic half-cut entanglement and local preparation-depth lower bounds concern bipartite resource quantities and spatial circuits. A mixture of nonlocal d-site wedge clusters can have these properties while remaining d-producible. No arbitrary-sector k-product row-count lower theorem was found among the reviewed results.

Earlier adjacent sources were checked with their exact scope retained. Livine and Terno, PRA 72, 022307 (2005), study normalized zero-angular-momentum mixtures, their bipartite Schur decomposition and E_D=E_F; this supplies singlet-projector context, not a reviewed cluster-depth theorem. A linear span of pair structures is not a positive mixture, so that statement alone does not establish 2-producibility. Assumptions that matter:
- Use the full isotypic projector. A fixed-tableau or multiplicity-resolved projector is a different interface.
- The upper bound requires both twirls. Permutations preserve arbitrary cluster size but need not be local operations on fixed named parties. No efficient or geometrically local preparation claim is supplied.
- An arbitrary vector in the sector need not have depth <= ell(lambda): the symmetric sector contains GHZ_N for N>=2, with depth N, although its normalized full projector is fully separable.
- An arbitrary Werner state need not be central; the proof above supplies no d-producibility claim for that larger family.
- A central mixture does not have depth equal to the largest occupied row count: I/d^N is fully separable and has positive weight in every nonzero isotypic sector.
- An invertible product filter preserves exact depth by applying it and its inverse to decompositions, with normalization reweighting the mixture. A common filter preserves Schur isotypic support but generally not centrality. Singular limits use closedness of k-producibility; singular filtering does not preserve exact depth in general.
- A claim that the sector contains no k-product vector for k<ell(lambda) is stronger than the one-party Schmidt statements inspected. That is the specific lower-bound interface whose priority remains UNKNOWN here.
