STRONGEST CROSS-PHASE RESULT

Assume the c07_s02 parameter-independent forward measurement and reverse
product-state channel both satisfy their claimed uniform
delta_N=C_(M,B)N^(-1/2) bounds on the full compact critical family. Tensoring
and telescoping yields two-way Le Cam distance at most R delta_N between R
quantum blocks at one shared unknown (A,b) and R i.i.d. quartic observations.
Therefore every [0,1]-bounded Bayes or minimax decision risk differs by at most
R delta_N, even for a collective POVM on all blocks. For R=o(sqrt(N)), this
removes any nonvanishing bounded-loss advantage from the quantum realization.
It does not assert finite-N quantum Fisher-information convergence.

The quartic law is a regular finite-dimensional exponential family with
sufficient statistics (x,xx^T), positive-definite Fisher covariance, and
ordinary R^(-1/2) estimation scale. The quantum Gibbs family itself has a
relative-entropy curvature bound: for an admissible parameter segment,
D(rho_theta||rho_(theta+u)) <= C_(M,B)||u||^2/2. This follows by integrating
the log-partition BKM Hessian, bounded above by Tr(rho G_u^2), then using the
critical J^2 and J^4 moment bounds and ||Q_D(J)||<=||D||_op J^2. Tensorization,
Pinsker, and a two-point test give an Omega(1/R) squared-error lower bound for
any interior, nondegenerate scaled parameter, under arbitrary collective
measurement. For physical field coefficient h=b/N^(3/4), this becomes
Omega(N^(-3/2)/R). The lower bound is an acquisition cost, not a sampler claim.

The channel-risk comparison is additive O(RN^(-1/2)). It gives asymptotic
equivalence of bounded risks when R=o(sqrt(N)); using only that bound, an
O(1/R) mean-square estimation claim transfers with negligible relative error
only in the stricter regime R=o(N^(1/4)). The two thresholds answer different
questions and should not be conflated.

STOPPED-DIFFUSION CONSEQUENCES

Conditionally on c07_s02's known-parameter result
T(rho_N(A,b),sigma_N(A,b))<=C exp(-cN), tensorization gives
T(rho_N^tensor R,sigma_N^tensor R)<=RC exp(-cN). The surrogate is fully
separable across all RN qubits. Every POVM's outcome law and every norm-one
bounded separability witness change by at most this error (twice it for a
trace-norm witness expectation). The approximation remains useful for
R=exp(c'N), c'<c. A prior mixture with one shared latent parameter is also
fully separable, with the same integrated error, but its preparation samples
that parameter and then uses parameter-dependent path laws.

This is not an unknown-parameter metrology channel. The stopped diffusion uses
the supplied A,b and is distinct from the parameter-independent c07_s02
forward/reverse maps. Trace-distance approximation transfers bounded outcome
risks but does not, by itself, imply derivative-level QFI convergence or a
parameter-free simulator.

TRACTABLE-SIMULATION AND INPUT COST

The stopped-SDE result is an existence representation. Its path law needs a
stopped three-dimensional SDE and Feynman--Kac size bias w=exp(int V_N dt),
with 0<w<=exp(h_N) and
h_N=(2M+1)sqrt(N)/4+O_(M,B)(N^(1/4)). The proof bounds the normalizing path
weight below by a constant for large N. Thus crude envelope rejection has a
guaranteed acceptance lower bound of order exp(-O_(M,B)(sqrt(N))), even if
exact unweighted paths and arithmetic are free. This implies only a loose
exp(O(sqrt(N))) upper bound on proposals for that particular naive method;
it is not a lower bound for all samplers. No certified polynomial-cost path,
weight, stopping-time, bit-precision, or POVM compiler is provided.

The theorem assumes exact supplied real parameters. An executable interface
must charge rational input bit length or an explicit precision oracle. The
relative-entropy lower bound says estimating scaled parameters to MSE epsilon^2
requires Omega(epsilon^(-2)) blocks, and estimating b to exp(-cN) precision
therefore costs Omega(exp(2cN)) blocks.

The same BKM/moment proof gives uniform target-state continuity
T(rho_N(theta),rho_N(theta+u))<=L||u||. If a mathematical rational-input
sampler achieves Ce^(-cN) error at theta_tilde, its error for the exact target
at theta is at most Ce^(-cN)+L||theta_tilde-theta|| by the triangle inequality;
no sensitivity bound on the sampler's particular mixture is needed. For the
fixed nine-dimensional (A,b), q-bit coordinate rounding adds O(2^-q) trace
error. Preserving a target tolerance e^(-aN), a<c, needs O(N) supplied bits.
This does not provide a finite-bit SDE solver: path discretization, weighting,
stopping, and arithmetic remain uncompiled. Learning b to that precision
still costs exponentially many samples, distinct from supplying O(N) bits.

The earlier finite-bit positive local-filter sampler is a separate
construction, with its own O(N^(-3/2))+2 eta+epsilon approximation and stated
O_(M,B)(eta^(-3)+N) real-arithmetic work; it is not an implementation of the
stopped diffusion. Decreasing eta exponentially costs exponentially many
operations in that sampler and leaves its O(N^(-3/2)) theorem term. Neither
representation currently supplies an efficient implementation of the
c07_s02 collective forward measurement.

The limiting quartic observation can also be reduced to a finite alphabet.
Uniform quartic tails and a uniform W^(1,1) gradient bound imply Le Cam error
at most C eta + C exp(-c r^4) for grid side eta and truncation radius r.
Choosing eta=Theta(epsilon), r=Theta((log(1/epsilon))^(1/4)) uses
3 log2(1/epsilon)+(3/4)log2log(1/epsilon)+O(1) bits per block. Composed with
the spin channels, the R-block error is at most R(C N^(-1/2)+epsilon).
Therefore O(log N) outcome bits per block suffice for bounded-risk equivalence
when R=o(sqrt(N)); this bounds alphabet size, not collective POVM gate cost.
The continuous reverse randomization also remains an ideal kernel. Full
derivations and the supplied/acquired, sample, energy, time, and finite-bit
ledger are in revisions/spin_interface_resources.md.
