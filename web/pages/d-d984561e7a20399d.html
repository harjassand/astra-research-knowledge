# Independent prior-art and assay audit: paired-sister damage trajectories

**Scope.** Primary-source audit of the proposed assay/mechanism question: whether paired sister-cell damage measurements near division can distinguish physical lesion partition at cytokinesis from sister-specific post-division repair or 53BP1-reporter dynamics. Focal paper: Wang et al., *Cell Research* (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41422-026-01221-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-026-01221-z). GEO: [GSE289323](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE289323). Checked 2026-10-08.

## Finding

The broad phenomenon is already reported in the focal paper: 2C-like mouse ESCs undergo divisions with sister-lineage asymmetry in DNA-damage-associated signals, and the lower-signal lineage is more likely to survive and regain pluripotency. A proposal cannot claim novelty for paired sister tracking, live 53BP1-focus asymmetry, or the 2C-like ESC damage/rejuvenation association.

A narrower mechanistic gap remains plausible: **in spontaneous 2C-like mESC divisions, does the physical burden of a defined lesion class already differ between daughters at or immediately after cytokinesis, or do daughter differences in 53BP1/γH2AX arise or change through postmitotic repair, synthesis, signaling, or focus organization?** The focal paper does not directly quantify physical lesion load in the same sister pair at the division boundary. But the generic idea of separating inheritance from repair is not new: Ferrand et al. measured UV photoproducts in paired early-G1 daughters and sister chromatids, and found CPD repair negligible during the first hours. Thus novelty would need to be specific to spontaneous 2C-like ESC lineages, lesion class, temporal resolution, or the lineage-fate link.

## What the focal study already measured

Wang et al. tracked mESC lineages with H2B-mIFP and a MERVL reporter. For a sister-pair endpoint experiment, cells were time-lapse imaged and then fixed and stained for γH2AX; the paper reports more γH2AX asymmetry in sisters from 2C-entering cells than in MERVL-negative controls (Fig. 3a–c; 97 pairs per group). This gives lineage identity plus a fixed sister-pair signal, but γH2AX is a damage-response chromatin mark, not a direct count of unrepaired DNA lesions.

For live damage tracking, the authors used a fusion of the 53BP1 tandem Tudor domain with mVenus. They explicitly report that this reporter co-localized more with γH2AX than with endogenous 53BP1 and interpreted it as reporting a broad range of lesions, rather than only DSBs. In live 2C-entering lineages, 53BP1-mVenus foci became asymmetric before the MERVL reporter diverged between daughter branches; in 54 pairs, the branch with more foci was more often the 2C-death lineage. Imaging was sampled every 15 or 20 minutes; foci were counted manually in S phase. ATM, ATR, CHEK, and PARP inhibition/knockdown reduced the reported asymmetry. These results already cover live paired sisters, reporter asymmetry, temporal precedence over fate-marker divergence, and DDR perturbation.

The paper also used alkaline comet assays to show increased DNA breakage in sorted MERVL::GFP-positive cells. This is an orthogonal physical-damage assay, but it is destructive and was applied to sorted cell populations, not to the tracked paired sisters at defined minutes after cytokinesis.

| [GSM8789193](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSM8789193) | MERVL GFP positive | Sorted MERVL::GFP-positive mESC single-cell RNA-seq; 10x-style barcodes/features/matrix files |
| [GSM8789194](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSM8789194) | MERVL GFP negative | Sorted MERVL::GFP-negative mESC single-cell RNA-seq; 10x-style barcodes/features/matrix files |

The sample metadata labels the groups as “MERVL GFP positive” and “MERVL GFP negative,” lists total RNA and transcriptomic single-cell RNA-seq, and provides per-sample barcode, feature, and count-matrix files. The article’s Methods likewise say GFP-positive and GFP-negative cells were sorted and single-cell RNA-seq libraries made with the Chromium Next GEM kit. The accession contains no parent/sister IDs, division timestamps, image sequences, or time-course annotations. **GSE289323 is a state-sorted transcriptomic snapshot, not a paired-sister lineage dataset or a damage time course.** Single-cell barcodes resolve cells within each library; they do not encode cell ancestry.

The focal article’s supplementary movies are representative lineage examples (including a 53BP1-focus segregation movie), not a GEO-deposited quantitative image/trajectory series.

## Strongest adjacent primary prior art

1. **Direct physical lesion measurements in sister cells — Ferrand et al., 2025.** In human U2OS and RPE-1 cells, the authors inflicted local UV damage before mitosis, stained CPDs (cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers) in spatially identified early-G1 sister pairs, and measured CPDs on sister chromatids in metaphase spreads. Daughter-pair CPD bias averaged about 15%; observed bias was near the maximum predicted by co-segregating the more damaged chromatids and exceeded random chromatid assignment. They state CPD repair was negligible during the first hours, so the early-G1 asymmetry was not explained by unequal repair. This is the closest prior art to the proposed partition-versus-repair contrast. It uses a defined, directly stainable UV lesion in synchronized human cells, not spontaneous lesions in 2C-like mESCs, and it is fixed-cell/timepoint analysis rather than continuous same-pair damage trajectories. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56090-8).

2. **Multigenerational live sister tracking and post-division heterogeneity — Panagopoulos et al., 2025.** The authors tracked PCNA and 53BP1 in single-cell lineages over multiple generations, then combined trajectories with endpoint γH2AX, p53, p21, and pRb staining. Replication stress and acute G1 damage produced sister-cell differences in foci, S-phase timing, and damage-response state; damage responses could persist into daughters/granddaughters. They explicitly discuss lesion segregation alongside alternative explanations such as limiting DDR/replication proteins and aberrant repair. This establishes that sister-specific postmitotic kinetics and response dynamics are substantial confounds and that lineage/time-resolved assays are feasible. It does not directly quantify physical lesions at the cytokinesis boundary. The paper states raw imaging data are available on request; public GEO accessions are transcriptomic datasets. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08986-0).

3. **Reporter-specific postmitotic dynamics — Lezaja et al., 2021.** In cells with endogenously tagged RPA70 plus 53BP1-GFP, live imaging showed RPA foci emerging as cells exited mitosis, before 53BP1 foci, rarely colocalizing with them, and persisting longer. The authors describe asymmetric inheritance of RPA-marked lesions in daughters, in contrast to symmetric 53BP1 distribution. Their results demonstrate that 53BP1 and RPA report distinct lesion classes and have different formation/disappearance kinetics; a 53BP1-only trajectory cannot establish total lesion partition. RPA is still a bound-protein marker of ssDNA, not a universal lesion counter. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23806-5); [primary imaging dataset, BioStudies S-BIAD106](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/studies/S-BIAD106).

4. **53BP1 nuclear bodies as inherited-lesion markers, not direct lesion counts — Spies et al., 2019.** Under-replicated DNA can pass through mitosis, form lesions in daughter G1, and be sequestered in 53BP1 nuclear bodies. These bodies regulate later replication timing and repair, so their number/intensity can evolve after division as part of a repair program. This makes a postmitotic 53BP1 body a useful subset marker of inherited replication lesions, not a universal measure of the physical damage burden at cytokinesis. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-019-0293-6).

5. **Replication-stress nonrandom inheritance and DDR dependence — Xing et al., 2020.** Replication stress triggers ATR/CHK1-dependent nonrandom segregation of old-template DNA, associated with asymmetric DDR and daughter fate. This is close mechanistic prior art for DNA damage-response-dependent biased inheritance, although old-strand segregation and DDR readouts are not a direct lesion-molecule census. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.04.005).

6. **Paired sister genomes and mutation footprints — Ginno et al., 2024.** Microfluidics was used to isolate mitotic sister cells after UV exposure and sequence their descendants, separating sister-specific UV-associated mutational phasing from chronic ROS mutagenesis. This shows paired mammalian sister lineages can be physically separated and analyzed; the readout is post-expansion mutation history, not lesion load in the first minutes after division. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01712-y).

7. **Aging/rejuvenation substrate precedent — Aguilaniu et al., 2003; Shcheprova et al., 2008.** Budding yeast studies established asymmetric inheritance of oxidatively damaged proteins and mother–daughter age resetting, respectively. These are important aging-model precedents but concern damaged proteins/age-associated components, not direct DNA lesion partition in mammalian ESCs. [Aguilaniu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1080418); [Shcheprova et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07212).

## Biological measurability and the remaining gap

- **Defined physical lesion:** CPD immunostaining is direct and quantitative for UV photoproducts; Ferrand et al. show it can be measured in individual sister cells shortly after mitosis and on sister chromatids before division. It is a strong calibration system for a lesion-partition assay, but it does not identify the endogenous lesion mix in spontaneous 2C-like ESCs.
- **Replication-associated ssDNA:** RPA can be imaged live and marks inherited ssDNA intermediates; Lezaja et al. show its postmitotic timing and different behavior from 53BP1. It measures a lesion-associated substrate class, not every damaged base or break.
- **53BP1 / γH2AX:** feasible live or fixed readouts for damage-response structures. They report recruitment, chromatin state, and repair signaling as well as lesion presence. The focal paper itself finds its 53BP1-Tudor reporter aligns better with γH2AX than endogenous 53BP1.
- **Comet assay:** measures DNA migration from breaks/alkali-labile sites at the single-cell level, but is destructive and does not by itself preserve sister identity across repeated timepoints. The focal paper’s comet result is group-level validation, not a paired-sister assay.
- **No universal live physical-damage reporter is established by these sources.** A credible discriminating experiment should name a lesion class and use a direct lesion assay at matched early post-division timepoints, with a live lineage reporter in parallel. A series of fixed, independently tracked sister-pair cohorts can measure the evolution of physical lesion signal; the exact same pair cannot be repeatedly subjected to destructive CPD/comet endpoints.

## Novelty decision for the candidate

**Reject as already-known:** “2C-like ESCs divide asymmetrically and one daughter inherits more DNA-damage signal”; “track paired sisters live with 53BP1”; “53BP1 asymmetry precedes MERVL fate split”; “ATM/ATR/CHEK/PARP affects asymmetry.” Wang et al. already report all of these.

**Potentially defensible narrower contribution:** establish, for a specified spontaneous damage class in 2C-entering mESCs, a measured pre-/peri-/immediate-post-cytokinesis lesion bias and its early postmitotic kinetics, then test whether that physical bias predicts the later 53BP1/γH2AX asymmetry and daughter fate. A controlled lesion-class arm (e.g. UV CPDs) can validate the assay, while the spontaneous 2C state tests biological relevance. The novelty claim should be “mechanism discrimination in the 2C-like ESC context,” not first discovery of sister damage asymmetry or first proof of physical lesion segregation in mammalian cells.

**Uncertainty:** I checked the named focal paper, its linked supplementary materials, GSE289323, and the directly relevant primary studies above. This is a bounded prior-art attack, not a comprehensive search of every asymmetric stem-cell lineage or lesion-segregation paper; avoid a global “first” claim until a broader systematic review is done.
