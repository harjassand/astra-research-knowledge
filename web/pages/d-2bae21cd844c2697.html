# C8 nonadiabatic / spin-dependent chemistry audit

**Finding:** The strongest concrete lead is a conditional sign mismatch between
the 2026 mScarlet–flavin RYDMR fit and the 2025 six-species photocycle it says
it reuses. If the fitted `β` is Xiang et al.'s literal radical-pair separation
fraction and fluorescence tracks fluorescent ground-state mScarlet, the fitted
positive `Δβ/ΔB1` predicts a fluorescence decrease. The experiment reports an
RF-on increase. This is a model-interface countercheck, not a refutation of
the observed resonance or a new chemistry capability. The spin-dynamics
extension I tested also fails the fitted RF response after baseline/linewidth
calibration.

The high-ceiling issue is whether the fitted magnetic-response parameter has
the same physical orientation as the downstream redox-network parameter. That
is testable from equations and product-resolved experiment. Until its source
mapping is resolved, the safe finding is “conditional sign mismatch”; there is
no warranted claim of predictive control, novelty, or biological mechanism.

## Evidence and reconstruction

The Nature version-of-record supplement S8 says the radical-pair partners and
nuclear-spin distributions are unknown, calls the calculation semi-quantitative,
changes a parameter `β` to `β_i + Δβ B1`, describes that parameter as the
magnetic-field-dependent separation fraction, and takes the remaining kinetic
rates from Xiang et al. Its fitted values are `β_i=.68(2)` and
`Δβ=.117(2) mT^-1`. The Nature paper reports positive RYDMR, including a
near-10% increase at the highest reported purified-sample conditions.
References and precise scope are in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

I reconstructed the JACS 2025 source ODE and its reaction meaning from the
authors' code. `β` diverts triplet encounters to separated dark mScarlet/FMN
radicals at rate `β k_red H T`; the complementary fraction restores fluorescent
ground-state mScarlet. For the exact 2026 simulation concentrations (50 μM
mScarlet, 20 μM FMN, 330 μM FMNH2) and JACS default rates, I derived the three
stoichiometric invariants and reduced the stationary state to one scalar `Q`
(FMNH radical). On the physical branch, the ground-state concentration is
strictly decreasing as β rises throughout the range `.68` to `.7151` (the
latter is the Nature fitted law at `B1=.3 mT`). The stationary ground state
falls from `7.7679339` to `7.4666565 μM`, a `3.8785%` drop. More strongly, at
the baseline equilibrium, an RF-on β step changes the immediate derivative by
`ΔGdot(0+)=-Δβ k_red H T=-.32924 μM/s`. The derivation, interval sign proof,
and full ODE residuals are in [MECHANISM_DERIVATION.md](MECHANISM_DERIVATION.md),
[steady_sign.py](steady_sign.py), and
[steady_sign_results.json](steady_sign_results.json).

The sign claim is restricted to the shared-variable mapping. Both sources call
β a separation fraction and the Nature supplement explicitly sources its other
rates from Xiang, so the mapping is plausible and load-bearing. The Nature
simulation is nevertheless called semi-quantitative, its input radical pair is
unknown, and it does not publish an independent conversion from model `G` to
camera counts. A complement convention or different observation map would
remove the mismatch; I found no such conversion in the inspected source
packet. This audit is not a claim that the experiment's fluorescence change is
false.

## Mechanism transfer test

I implemented an exact finite-cutoff Floquet-Liouville integrated first-event
yield solver for the authors' two-electron radical-pair model with an added
transverse monochromatic drive. This is a transparent extension of the JACS
static independent-Gaussian local-field model, not an assumed reconstruction
of the unknown Nature hyperfine Hamiltonian. I charged two calibration choices:
static width `σ=1.3 mT` to approach the observed line and
`η=k_rec/k_sep=20.3228` to match the no-RF separation fraction near `.68` at
`B0=15.9 mT`. With fixed seed 20261008, `n=1200`, and K=3 sidebands, predicted
on-resonance separation-yield changes at 447 MHz for `B1=.07,.1,.2,.3 mT` are
`−.0173,−.0320,−.0872,−.1347`. At 400 MHz they are
`−.0068,−.0129,−.0374,−.0605`. Nature's fitted scalar changes are positive:
`+.00819,+.0117,+.0234,+.0351`. The maximum yield-normalization residual is
`1.72e−14`; the Monte Carlo standard errors and all frozen parameters are in
[floquet_scan.json](floquet_scan.json). A separate fixed-seed K=2/3/4 cutoff
check at B1=.1 and .3 mT agrees between K=3 and K=4 to below `1.3e−10` in
ensemble mean ([floquet_cutoff_check.json](floquet_cutoff_check.json)). The
paired B0 grid gives a roughly interpolated 3.07 mT FWHM centered at 15.85 mT
for B1=.07 mT, close to the cited 2.7(7) mT line; this is an explicit
calibration check used to choose σ, not an independent prediction
([floquet_line_scan.json](floquet_line_scan.json)). The predicted RF response
magnitude and sign fail, so this extension produces no current-system capability
claim.

## Strongest controls and decisive discriminant

The best available published control for explaining the response curve is the
Nature paper's own two-free-parameter phenomenological β fit. It fits the
observed response by design, but it does not independently predict the sign
of product-resolved chemistry. A thermal/photobleaching response is another
control; the supplement measures RF-associated heating and includes
orientation, field/frequency and biological controls, but these do not identify
the radical-pair partner or turn fitted β into an independently measured
separation yield. Generic radical-pair optimal control is established prior
art and assumes a known Hamiltonian; with current unknown hyperfine data, it
does not supply a stronger informed prediction. The comparator scopes and
known-control boundary are logged in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

The decisive test is simultaneous, product-specific measurement of the radical
yield and fluorescence during a B1 sweep through ESR, after independent
transient EPR/ENDOR determination of partner and hyperfine distribution. Freeze
the mechanism and parameter values before held-out B1/frequency runs; compare
against (i) the same-data phenomenological response model and (ii) an
absorbed-power- and temperature-matched detuned/heating control. The sign and
shape of product yield versus fluorescence would directly resolve whether the
Nature β and Xiang β are the same observable. Charge sample preparation,
EPR/ENDOR, temperature sensing, RF/B0 calibration, transient product
measurement, and held-out replicates. No free PES, hyperfine constants, or
DFT were assumed. Detailed stop/restart gates are in
[FAILURE_AND_RESTART_GATES.md](FAILURE_AND_RESTART_GATES.md).

The Astra prior cards were checked selectively. They preserve useful
chemical-stochastic scope distinctions but contain no premise about this
radical-pair spin mechanism. RF resonance itself is already published; bounded
optimal control of supplied radical-pair Hamiltonians is also prior work.
Accordingly no novelty or priority claim is made. Machine-readable statuses
and validation limits are in [CLAIMS.json](CLAIMS.json).
