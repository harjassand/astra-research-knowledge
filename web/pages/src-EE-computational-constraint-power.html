A nonlinear-interface oracle with exact feasibility
The mechanism extends beyond resistor-network damage to a broader computational task: repeatedly solving constrained nonlinear response and continuous-design problems on small, query-selected parts of a large physical model.
The supported construction preprocesses a fixed, linearly constrained quadratic model. Afterwards, a query can select up to \(k\) local variables, impose new hard constraints or convex nonlinear constitutive terms, vary loads within a supplied load space, and optimise additional local design variables.
The resulting small optimisation problem preserves exactly which local responses admit a globally feasible extension. Its energy approximation is uniform over all admissible local responses—not merely over previously observed solutions. That distinction permits optimisation over the compressed model without retraining it at each candidate.
The principal additional results are:
- a uniform guarantee for all load combinations, including nearly cancelling combinations;
- a reduction of explicit convex nonlinear queries to a small convex quadratic program;
- recovery of a near-optimal, globally feasible full state using the original, unchanged system solver, without solving a newly damaged or nearly singular global system.
This is a derived algorithmic candidate with proofs below. Independent correctness review, historical originality, and competitive large-scale performance remain unestablished. The bulk model must remain quadratic; the result is not a general solver for nonlinear mechanics or unrestricted topology optimisation.
1. The precise computational capability
Let \(x\in\mathbb R^N\) describe the full state. The input supplies rational matrices defining
\[
E(x)=x^\top Hx,\qquad H=G^\top WG\succ0,
\]
where \(W\) is positive diagonal, together with
\[
Ax=Fa.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(A\) has full row rank, \(a\in\mathbb R^d\) contains load or prescribed-data coefficients, and the columns of \(F\) are linearly independent. Dependent columns can first be removed by exact elimination.
The input also supplies a dictionary of \(M\) local measurements
\[
y=Cx.
\]
These “local measurements” can be member forces, nodal displacements, fluxes, or specified linear combinations. Taking \(C=I\) permits arbitrary query-time linear constraints involving a small number of state coordinates.
A query selects \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}\), \(|S|\le k\), and specifies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x,a,z}\quad&
x^\top Hx+\psi(C_Sx,a,z),\\
\text{subject to}\quad&
Ax=Fa,\\
&(C_Sx,a,z)\in\mathcal P.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
For the operational theorem, \(\mathcal P\) is an explicitly supplied rational polyhedron, and \(\psi\) has an explicit convex-quadratic-program formulation with nonnegative objective. Auxiliary variables can represent absolute-value costs and nonnegative squared-hinge penalties.
Thus the interface includes coupled capacities, one-sided constraints, complete suppression of selected variables, soft constraints, and local continuous design with linear budgets. It does not assume that an arbitrary nonlinear function comes with a free optimisation oracle.
Derived theorem
For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), preprocessing uses
\[
q=O\!\left(
\frac{k\log(eM/k)+\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}
\right)
\tag{3}
\]
random response vectors and \(d\) reference-load responses.
With probability at least \(1-\delta\), one stored structure works simultaneously for every admissible support and query:
1. The reduced problem is feasible exactly when the full problem is feasible.
2. Its optimal value approximates the full optimal value within relative error \(\varepsilon\).
3. A reduced optimiser specifies local variables and design parameters admitting a globally feasible extension with objective at most \((1+\varepsilon)\) times optimal, after allocating internal tolerances appropriately.
The query requires
\[
\boxed{
O(qk^2+kd+d^2)
\text{ arithmetic operations}
+
\text{one explicit QP in }O(k+d+\dim z)\text{ variables}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The quadratic-program solver’s bit complexity is charged. There is no assertion that arbitrary QPs take \(O(k^3)\) time.
The probability statement is uniform over the entire response geometry. Queries may therefore be chosen adaptively, including after inspecting the stored data.
2. What is—and is not—new relative to specialised methods
Static condensation already eliminates a linear interior before solving nonlinear contact equations. It has been applied to engineering contact and friction problems, and modern component-based reductions also preserve interface relations through structured coupling. Merely writing a Schur complement for a nonlinear interface would not be an advance. KAIST
Randomised model reduction already compresses approximation and residual spaces, including online selection from dictionaries. Randomised sketches of convex programs also have established approximation guarantees based on constraint geometry. arXiv
The candidate contribution here is more specific:
Preprocess before the interface, constitutive constraints, and load combination are selected; retain their exact feasible domain in finite arithmetic; and approximate the entire partially minimised energy uniformly enough to support subsequent optimisation.

There is no assumed low-dimensional solution manifold or training set. There is also no claim that the stored random vectors themselves form an accurate physical reduced-state basis.

3. Compress the minimum extension energy, not a guessed state space
Define
\[
L=AH^{-1}A^\top,
\qquad
X=H^{-1}A^\top L^{-1}F,
\qquad
Q_0=X^\top HX.
\]
Then \(Xa\) is the minimum-energy state satisfying \(Ax=Fa\).
The homogeneous feasible-response operator is
\[
K=
H^{-1}-H^{-1}A^\top L^{-1}AH^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
It satisfies
\[
AK=0,\qquad KHK=K,
\]
and its range is \(\ker A\).
For a selected interface, set
\[
\Gamma_S=C_SKC_S^\top,\qquad D_S=C_SX.
\]
The minimum bulk energy compatible with local values \(y\) is
\[
\mathcal E_S(a,y)=
\min_{\substack{Ax=Fa\\ C_Sx=y}}x^\top Hx.
\]
Its exact formula is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_S(a,y)
=
a^\top Q_0a+
(y-D_Sa)^\top\Gamma_S^+(y-D_Sa),
}
\tag{6}
\]
provided
\[
\boxed{
y-D_Sa\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_S).
}
\tag{7}
\]
Otherwise the value is \(+\infty\).
To verify this, write \(x=Xa+v\), where \(Av=0\). The two terms are \(H\)-orthogonal. Among homogeneous feasible corrections with \(C_Sv=y-D_Sa\), the minimum-energy correction is
\[
v=KC_S^\top\Gamma_S^+(y-D_Sa).
\]
Equation (6) is established quadratic minimisation. The harder obligations are preserving (7) exactly and approximating (6) relatively when its constituent matrices are nearly singular.
4. Acquiring the compressed geometry with exact constraints
Draw a sign matrix \(R\in\mathbb R^{q\times p}\), where \(p\) is the number of rows of \(G\), with entries \(\pm1/\sqrt q\). Choose \(q\) to be a power of four so this normalization is rational.
The ideal sketch is
\[
Y_0=RW^{1/2}GKC^\top.
\tag{8}
\]
For each support \(S\), the vectors being embedded span a space of dimension at most \(k\). A subspace-embedding estimate and a union bound over supports give, with the row count in (3),
\[
(1-\theta)\Gamma_S
\preceq
Y_{0,S}^\top Y_{0,S}
\preceq
(1+\theta)\Gamma_S
\tag{9}
\]
simultaneously for all supports. The dimension count uses
\[
\|W^{1/2}GKC_S^\top v\|^2
=
v^\top\Gamma_Sv.
\]
The use of random projections is standard; the issue is how to acquire their rows without destroying exact dependencies. arXiv
Repair before storing
Each ideal row is obtained from a constrained baseline solve producing
\[
j_i=KG^\top W^{1/2}r_i\in\ker A.
\]
Construct an exact rational right inverse \(T\) with \(AT=I\). Such a matrix can be obtained from a nonsingular column submatrix of \(A\); its construction, storage and application costs are charged.
After approximately computing a row \(v_i\), store
\[
\boxed{\widehat j_i=v_i-TAv_i.}
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore \(A\widehat j_i=0\) exactly. Form the stored sketch as
\[
Y_{i,:}=\widehat j_i^\top C^\top
\]
using exact arithmetic on the stored finite numbers. Independently rounding these final entries would generally invalidate the guarantee.
Let \(e_i=\widehat j_i-j_i\). If
\[
\sum_i\|e_i\|_H^2\le a_0^2,
\]
then, because every \(e_i\) is homogeneous-feasible,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(Y-Y_0)_Sv\|^2
&=\sum_i|e_i^\top C_S^\top v|^2\\
&\le a_0^2\,v^\top\Gamma_Sv.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
This is a relative geometry error, even when \(v^\top\Gamma_Sv\) is tiny. On a true null direction, the error is exactly zero.
Allocating small constant fractions of the tolerance to (9) and (11) gives
\[
\boxed{
(1-\theta)\Gamma_S
\preceq\widehat\Gamma_S:=Y_S^\top Y_S
\preceq(1+\theta)\Gamma_S.
}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
\ker\widehat\Gamma_S=\ker\Gamma_S.
\]
No lower bound on a selected interface eigenvalue appears.
5. Multiple loads require more than individually accurate solves
This continuation exposed an additional failure mode: exact feasibility and small error for each individual load do not suffice for arbitrary load combinations.
For example, take
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H=I,
\qquad
F=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&\rho\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exactly feasible approximate load frame
\[
\widehat X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
0&\rho\\
\eta&-\eta
\end{pmatrix}
\]
has small error in each column. But for \(a=(1,-1)\), the true energy is \(\rho^2\), while the approximate energy is
\[
\rho^2+4\eta^2.
\]
The relative error diverges as \(\rho\to0\).
Uniform load-frame construction
Instead, construct a repaired frame \(\widehat X\) satisfying
\[
A\widehat X=F
\]
and the matrix inequality
\[
\boxed{
(\widehat X-X)^\top H(\widehat X-X)
\preceq\eta^2Q_0.
}
\tag{13}
\]
This can be enforced without knowing future load coefficients. A certified lower bound \(c_-I\preceq Q_0\), followed by sufficiently accurate column solves, is enough: require each repaired column error to be at most \(\eta\sqrt{c_-/d}\) in energy norm.
For rational input, a positive bound \(c_-\) can be constructed using finite matrix bounds and exact elimination. Its acquisition and the additional precision are preprocessing costs. Nearly dependent load bases may require more bits; they are not declared well-conditioned for free.
Store
\[
\widehat D=C\widehat X,\qquad
\widehat Q_0=\widehat X^\top H\widehat X.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
Q_0\preceq\widehat Q_0\preceq(1+\eta^2)Q_0.
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover, for every \(a,S\),
\[
e_S=(\widehat D_S-D_S)a
\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_S),
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
e_S^\top\Gamma_S^+e_S\le\eta^2a^\top Q_0a.
}
\tag{15}
\]
The proof is the minimum-energy extension inequality: \((\widehat X-X)a\) is itself a homogeneous feasible extension of \(e_S\).
Consequently,
\[
y-\widehat D_Sa\in\operatorname{range}(\widehat\Gamma_S)
\iff
y-D_Sa\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_S).
\tag{16}
\]
The full affine feasible domain—not just its dimension—is preserved exactly.
Uniform energy approximation
Define
\[
\widehat{\mathcal E}_S(a,y)
=
a^\top\widehat Q_0a+
(y-\widehat D_Sa)^\top
\widehat\Gamma_S^+
(y-\widehat D_Sa).
\]
Writing \(c=a^\top Q_0a\) and
\(v=(y-D_Sa)^\top\Gamma_S^+(y-D_Sa)\), equation (15) bounds the centering error by
\[
2\eta\sqrt{cv}+\eta^2c
\le\eta(c+v)+\eta^2c.
\]
Combining this with (12)–(14) gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{1-\eta-2\eta^2}{1+\theta}\,
\mathcal E_S
\le
\widehat{\mathcal E}_S
\le
\frac{1+\eta+2\eta^2}{1-\theta}\,
\mathcal E_S.
}
\tag{17}
\]
Choosing \(\eta,\theta\) as sufficiently small constant multiples of the requested \(\varepsilon\) proves the theorem’s energy guarantee.
This bound holds for every feasible \(a,y\), including those selected by a subsequent optimiser.
6. The nonlinear optimisation reduction
There is no need to form a pseudoinverse or insert an artificial regularization floor.
Introduce \(t\in\mathbb R^{|S|}\) and impose
\[
y=\widehat D_Sa+\widehat\Gamma_St.
\]
The online problem is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\min_{a,t,z}\quad&
a^\top\widehat Q_0a+
t^\top\widehat\Gamma_St+
\psi(\widehat D_Sa+\widehat\Gamma_St,a,z),\\
\text{subject to}\quad&
(\widehat D_Sa+\widehat\Gamma_St,a,z)\in\mathcal P.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{18}
\]
It is an explicit convex QP. Singular directions of \(t\) are harmless redundant variables and can also be eliminated exactly.
The full and reduced problems have the same feasible local tuples by (16). Since \(\psi\ge0\), the multiplicative bound (17) survives adding \(\psi\). It therefore survives taking minima. An optimiser of the reduced problem is near-optimal for the true partial-energy problem.
Polynomial-time solvability of rational convex quadratic programs is established prior mathematics, including degenerate cases; the construction does not require a strictly feasible interior or a fixed active set. ScienceDirect
A force-driven corollary
The result also handles a common signed physical objective without incorrectly claiming relative accuracy for arbitrary differences of energies.
For homogeneous constraints \(Ax=0\), let a passive convex local device have potential \(\psi(y)\), with \(\psi(0)=0\) and an origin-feasible convex domain. Under local force \(b\), define
\[
V=\sup_y
\left[
b^\top y-\psi(y)-\tfrac12\mathcal E_S(0,y)
\right].
\tag{19}
\]
This is the negative minimum total potential, measured relative to the undeformed state.
From (12), the conservative energy
\[
E_{\rm safe}(y)=(1+\theta)\widehat{\mathcal E}_S(0,y)
\]
satisfies
\[
\mathcal E_S(0,y)\le E_{\rm safe}(y)
\le\kappa\,\mathcal E_S(0,y),
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}.
\]
Let \(V_{\rm safe}\) be (19) with \(E_{\rm safe}\). Then
\[
\boxed{V/\kappa\le V_{\rm safe}\le V.}
\tag{20}
\]
For the lower bound, scale a true optimiser toward the origin by \(1/\kappa\). Convexity gives
\(\psi(y/\kappa)\le\psi(y)/\kappa\), while the bulk energy scales quadratically.
Thus force-driven nonlinear contact can receive a conservative relative potential-value certificate. This is not a claim that the exact active contact set or every contact force is recovered correctly when the system is arbitrarily close to an active-set transition.
7. Full-state recovery is possible—but not free
A reduced optimiser gives local variables and an energy guarantee. A full physical field has \(N\) entries and must be charged separately.
Retain the repaired homogeneous vectors in
\[
J=[\widehat j_1,\ldots,\widehat j_q],
\qquad C_SJ=Y_S^\top.
\]
For a returned pair \(a,y\), put
\[
h=y-C_S\widehat Xa.
\]
Because \(h\in\operatorname{range}(\widehat\Gamma_S)\),
\[
JY_S\widehat\Gamma_S^+h
\]
is an exactly feasible homogeneous correction. But its energy can be much larger than the minimum. Treating these sketch vectors as an accurate reduced physical basis would be a mistake.
Baseline-solve refinement
Start with \(v_0=0\), residual \(r_j=h-C_Sv_j\), and repeatedly apply
\[
v_{j+1}
=
v_j+
\operatorname{Approx}\!\left(
KC_S^\top\widehat\Gamma_S^+r_j
\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Every approximate correction is repaired to satisfy \(A\Delta v=0\) exactly.
For exact applications, (12) implies
\[
\|r_{j+1}\|_{\Gamma_S^+}
\le
\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}
\|r_j\|_{\Gamma_S^+}.
\]
Energy-relative errors in the baseline solves enter the same residual norm, without a selected-interface condition-number factor. Sufficiently accurate corrections retain a contraction bounded away from one.
After reducing the residual, apply the exact sketch lift to the remaining \(r_j\). Since
\[
\|J\|_{2\to H}\le\sqrt p+1
\]
under the preprocessing tolerances, only
\[
O\!\left(\log\frac{p+2}{\zeta}\right)
\]
baseline solves are needed to obtain an energy-norm recovery error of order \(\zeta\) times the minimum correction norm.
Errors orthogonal to the port-response space are controlled by the sum of the baseline solve errors. The load-frame contribution is bounded separately by (13). Consequently, the recovered full state satisfies both sets of linear constraints exactly and has near-optimal energy.
The additional work is
\[
\boxed{
O\!\left(
\log\frac{p+2}{\zeta}\,
[T_{\rm base}(\zeta)+\operatorname{nnz}(C_S)+k^2]
+Nq+Nd
\right),
}
\tag{22}
\]
plus small rational-system work and output bits.
All large solves use the unchanged baseline operator. No newly damaged global matrix is inverted.
8. Resource accounting and a non-Laplacian physical application
General explicit-matrix implementation
Let \(P_{\rm base}\) be the cost of constructing a solver for the fixed constrained quadratic problem, and \(T_{\rm base}\) the cost of one sufficiently accurate solve. Let \(P_T,T_T\) be the construction and application costs of the exact repair map.
Preprocessing costs
\[
\begin{aligned}
P={}&P_{\rm base}+P_T\\
&+O\!\left(
(q+d)[T_{\rm base}+T_T+\operatorname{nnz}(G)+\operatorname{nnz}(C)]
+p d^2
\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
with finite-precision factors included in the actual subroutine costs.
These are not abstract free oracles. A dense implementation factors the \(N+\operatorname{rows}(A)\) KKT matrix once, giving cubic preprocessing and quadratic work per right-hand side. Faster sparse solvers can replace it when the supplied structure permits.
The local-query data occupy
\[
O(M(q+d)+d^2)
\]
working words. Full-state recovery additionally retains \(O(N(q+d))\) numbers and any baseline factorization.
The random sketch uses \(pq\) fair signs. Online queries use no randomness.
All conservation matrices and local measurement relations are preserved as the exact rational input. Approximate solve outputs are rounded before repair; the products defining \(Y\) and \(\widehat D\) are not independently rounded afterwards. General right inverses can have large rational entries, so their bit lengths are included rather than assumed constant.
If \(b_*\) bounds stored-number bit lengths and \(L_{\rm loc}\) is the query description length, the online bit cost is
\[
O(qk^2+kd+d^2)\operatorname{poly}(b_*)
+
\operatorname{QPbit}\!\left(
k+d+\dim z,\,
L_{\rm loc}+O((k+d)^2b_*)
\right).
\tag{24}
\]
There is no numerical factor inverse in the smallest positive queried-interface eigenvalue. Small eigenvalues still affect the bit lengths of exact local answers and multipliers.
Application: nonlinear members in an elastic truss
Let \(x_e\) be axial member forces, \(A\) the equilibrium matrix, and
\[
H=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\ell_e}{E_ea_e}\right).
\]
Then \(x^\top Hx\) is twice the complementary elastic energy. Complementary-energy optimisation, including nonlinear cable models, is established mechanics; it is not a new physical formulation. ScienceDirect
After preprocessing the bilateral elastic structure, a query may choose arbitrary \(k\) members and impose, for example,
\[
x_e\ge0,\qquad
|x_e|\le u_e,\qquad
x_e=0,
\]
or additional compliance and convex piecewise-linear constitutive response represented by squared-hinge costs.
These respectively model tension-only restrictions, force limits, complete member removal, and specified monotone nonlinear constitutive modifications within the stated static model. The method does not simulate path-dependent plastic history merely because a force cap is present.
The load vector can range over the entire supplied load basis. Capacity parameters can also be optimisation variables when their costs and coupling constraints give an explicit convex query.
For a two-dimensional, bounded-range truss layout with geometric separators, nested dissection gives a baseline factorization cost of \(O(N^{3/2})\) arithmetic and \(O(N\log N)\) work per solve. These are the specialised baseline costs used here, not dense cubic solves. SIAM
There are concrete layouts with an inexpensive exact equilibrium repair: horizontal rows anchored at one boundary and vertical columns anchored at another provide two forest-routing systems. Additional diagonal members need not make the stiffness matrix a scalar graph Laplacian.
For such a layout, with \(M,p=O(N)\), bounded local descriptions and fixed \(d\),
\[
\boxed{
P=\widetilde O(N^{3/2}+Nq),
}
\tag{25}
\]
followed by small QP queries independent of \(N\) in arithmetic dimension. Precision factors remain charged.

A small fixed interface known beforehand should normally use ordinary condensation. The proposed advantage is for many different, adaptively selected interfaces, when \(q\ll M\), and when local outputs rather than full fields are required for most candidates.
High accuracy can make \(q\propto\varepsilon^{-2}\) expensive. No performance claim is made where that preprocessing exceeds the cost of the intended workload.
9. Exact feasibility does not remove every singular-limit obstruction
Two boundaries are decisive.
Changing the quadratic bulk is not automatically covered. Suppose a scalar bulk energy is \(E(y)=y^2\), approximated by \(\widehat E(y)=(1+\theta)y^2\). Subtracting \((1-\rho)y^2\) produces the convex physical energy \(\rho y^2\), but the compressed energy becomes \((\rho+\theta)y^2\). Relative error is \(\theta/\rho\).
Thus even a convex final model can defeat this approximation if a query cancels the baseline energy. Changes expressible as additional nonnegative complementary energy—including selected-member weakening and removal—remain covered. Arbitrary negative quadratic corrections, changing equilibrium geometry, and near-buckling bulk models do not.
Optimisation does not make combinatorial search disappear. The theorem solves continuous convex design on a query-selected support. It can evaluate an explicit candidate list with one uniform guarantee. It does not search all \(\binom Mk\) supports in polynomial time, solve nonconvex robust design automatically, or infer constitutive parameters from measurements.
Likewise, exact feasibility concerns the supplied rational model. Uncertain geometry or an uncertain contact gap cannot be promoted into an exact physical constraint merely through arithmetic.
