0. EXACT SETUP AND CONCLUSION

Let h^{(n)}_v be the actual zero-boundary square height field, extended
by zero to ALL v in Z^2. Its random free set is not being selected:
ordinary deterministic finite domains lie in one box of diameter O(n).
Use the fixed NE diagonal on each square cell to interpolate globally
at mesh 1/n:
  Phi_n(x)=sum_{v in Z^2} h^{(n)}_v I_{n,v}(x).
The nonnegative nodal hats form a partition of unity, including the
ZERO-PIN hats. Each has integral n^-2 and support radius <=sqrt(2)/n.
This formula is on R^2 first, not a discrete Fourier sampling shortcut.
Assume the supplied deterministic global Lipschitz bound <=C_Lip n.

Embed the zero extension in a fixed torus T_L=(R/LZ)^2 with a common
zero collar at its seam; L may be chosen once larger than the fixed
domain bounding box. This is the ordinary global zero-field embedding.
The proof below also accounts for the finite translated copies used
by a periodic convolution. No interaction is introduced across a
physical boundary. Denote the periodic version by f_n.

Supplied full macro theorem: for each fixed spread constant C_* there
are a>0,M<infinity, independent of n, the finite volume, R>=1, and
the deterministic real coefficient array q, such that
  E exp(a |sum_v q_v h^{(n)}_v|)<=M
when
  sum_v q_v=0, max|q_v|<=C_*/R^2,
  sum|q_v|<=C_*, diam(supp q)<=C_* R.
The sum is over ALL coefficients before deleting zero terms. Zero-pin
vertices, including added fixed exterior vertices, are permitted.
The supplied theorem has an additional macroscopic connected-zero-
anchor branch. Our main proof needs only its exact-zero branch, because
the coarse test can be compensated on spread exterior zero vertices.
An anchored coarse estimate is an equivalent optional alternative.

Supplied weak field law: f_n converges to the stated zero-extended
square Dirichlet GFF in H^{-s0}(T_L) for every s0>1. We do not assume
any covariance upper bound or already sharper-topology field theorem.

CONCLUSION:
 (a) For EVERY u>0, sup_n E||f_n||_{H^{-u}(T_L)}^2<infinity.
 (b) For EVERY s>0, laws(f_n) are tight in H^{-s}(T_L).
 (c) For EVERY s>0, f_n converges in that space to the SAME GFF law.
The GFF has an almost-sure realization in all H^{-s}, s>0. There is
no uniform bound as s decreases to zero, and no H^0 conclusion.

1. A COMPACT DYADIC DIFFERENCE HAS AN EXACT LEGAL TEST ARRAY

Choose one nonnegative even chi in C_c^infinity(R^2), supported in the
unit ball, with integral one. Let chi_r(x)=r^-2 chi(x/r). Fix r_0>0
small relative to the torus period and zero collar; put
  r_j=r_0 2^-j,  K_j=chi_{r_j}-chi_{2r_j}, j>=1.
Thus integral K_j=0, ||K_j||_1<=2, support radius <=2r_j,
  ||K_j||_infty <=(5/4)||chi||_infty r_j^-2.

For a deterministic y in R^2 and r=r_j>=1/n, define
  q_v(y)=integral I_{n,v}(x) K_j(y-x) dx.
Partition of unity gives EXACTLY
  sum_{ALL v} q_v(y)=integral K_j=0.                         (1.1)
Nonnegativity of the hats and their integral give
  max|q_v|<=(5/4)||chi||_infty (nr)^-2,
  sum|q_v|<=2,
  diam(supp q)<=4nr+2sqrt(2)<=7nr.                          (1.2)
With R=nr>=1 these are the same fixed spread class for EVERY j,n,y.
The observable equals (K_j*Phi_n)(y), since all removed pin values
are zero. Its array remains admissible by (1.1) even if the sum of
the remaining FREE coefficients is nonzero. Deleting the pin weights
BEFORE checking exact total would be an invalid transfer.

Choose C_* once large enough for (1.2) and the coarse tests below.
The macro exponential bound implies
  E |(K_j*Phi_n)(y)|^2 <=B, B=2M/a^2,  r_j>=1/n,            (1.3)
using t^2<=2 exp(t). It holds at each deterministic y with a constant
independent of y,j,n; no spatial stationarity is required.

For periodic convolution, periodize the kernel. Because its support
radius is less than L/4 and Phi_n is supported in one fundamental box,
  (K_j^per*f_n)(y)=sum_m (K_j*Phi_n)(y+Lm),                 (1.4)
with at most nine possibly nonzero translated terms, after choosing
the fundamental representative of y. Each term is an actual legal
global-grid test above. Squaring costs at most nine times the sum of
their squares; no independence is assumed. In the common zero collar
the crossed-seam terms in fact vanish. Formula (1.4) also prevents an
incorrect support-diameter claim about two wrapped pieces regarded as
one unlifted lattice array.

Use normalized torus measure dx/L^2. Integration and Fubini yield
  E ||K_j^per*f_n||_2^2 <=81B, r_j>=1/n.                    (1.5)
The constant 81 is deliberately loose.

2. SUBGRID SCALES ARE NOT DROPPED

For r_j<1/n the macro theorem at R=nr_j is not invoked. Instead,
the zero integral and deterministic global Lipschitz bound give
  |(K_j*Phi_n)(y)|
   <=C_Lip n integral |K_j(z)| |z| dz
   <=3 C_Lip n r_j integral |z| chi(z) dz
   <=3 C_Lip n r_j.
The finite periodized-copy count thus gives
  ||K_j^per*f_n||_2^2 <=C n^2 r_j^2, r_j<1/n.              (2.1)
This controls every continuum mode above the mesh, including arbitrarily
large aliases. It is a statement about the ACTUAL affine interpolation.
No discrete-frequency identification or Nyquist truncation is used.

3. THE COARSE KERNEL, INCLUDING THE ZERO MODE

The coefficient array for (chi_{r0}*Phi_n)(y) has total ONE, l1<=1,
max<=||chi||_infty r0^-2 n^-2, and support diameter O(n). Consider
only the finitely many lifts y+Lm in (1.4), so their centers remain in
a fixed bounded macroscopic box.

Choose once for each n an n by n lattice square Z_n of exterior zero
vertices, at distance O(n) from that box and entirely outside the free
domain and these kernels. Set q'_v=q_v-n^-2 1_{v in Z_n}. Then
  sum_ALL q'_v=0,  sum|q'_v|<=2,
  max|q'_v|<=C n^-2,  diam(supp q')<=C n.
Thus it is a legal exact-zero test at R=n with one fixed C_*. Its
observable equals the original coarse convolution, since h_v=0 on
Z_n. Adding fixed zero sites to a finite graph changes neither the
zero-extended field nor its law. This uses exactly the supplied
all-coefficient/pin convention, not an assumption of zero free mass.

The macro bound and finite periodization consequently give
  sup_n E||chi_{r0}^per*f_n||_2^2<=C_0.                    (3.1)
Alternatively the supplied connected macroscopic exterior-zero anchor
gives (3.1) directly. Both routes have fixed support/anchor constants;
neither charges a shrinking scale to a fixed coarse test. The coarse
kernel has multiplier one at frequency zero, so the mean is covered.

4. CORE FOURIER COERCIVITY FOR EVERY POSITIVE ORDER

This elementary argument replaces a wavelet or Littlewood--Paley
norm-characterization import. Define
  lambda_k=2pi k/L,
  fhat(k)=L^-2 integral_T f(x) exp(-i lambda_k.x) dx,
  ||f||_{H^{-u}}^2=sum_k (1+|lambda_k|^2)^-u |fhat(k)|^2.
The standard product Fourier basis/Parseval and convolution identity
give multipliers a_j(lambda)=chihat(r_j lambda) and
  m_j(lambda)=a_j(lambda)-a_{j-1}(lambda).

There exist delta>0 and B_*>delta with
  |chihat(xi)-1|<=1/8 for |xi|<=delta,
  |chihat(xi)|<=1/8 for |xi|>=B_*.
One can take delta=1/8 (unit support), and
B_*=max{1,sqrt(8||Delta chi||_1)} by integration by parts.
Choose an integer N>=1 so 2^N delta/2>=B_*.

If r0|lambda|<=delta, |a_0|>=7/8. Otherwise choose the unique
j>=1 for which delta/2<r_j|lambda|<=delta.
If j>=N, the N-term telescope gives
  |sum_{ell=j-N+1}^j m_ell|
    =|a_j-a_{j-N}|>=3/4,
so Cauchy--Schwarz gives sum |m_ell|^2>=9/(16N). For u>0,
  sum_{ell>=1} r_ell^{2u}|m_ell|^2
   >=(9/(16N))(delta/(2|lambda|))^{2u}.                    (4.1)
This charges a FIXED number of scales near each frequency, independent
of |lambda|. It has no logarithmic or dimension-of-grid loss.

If 1<=j<N, telescope instead
  a_j=a_0+sum_{ell=1}^j m_ell,
whose modulus is >=7/8. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
|a_0|^2+sum_{ell=1}^j|m_ell|^2>=49/(64N). All weights
r_ell^{2u} in this finite set are bounded below by a positive constant
depending only on u,r0,N. These low frequencies are therefore covered
by the same scalar inequality. At lambda=0, a_0=1 and all m_j=0.

Combining the cases, for EVERY u>0 and EVERY real lambda,
  (1+|lambda|^2)^-u
   <=C_u[|chihat(r0 lambda)|^2
                  +sum_{j>=1} r_j^{2u}|m_j(lambda)|^2].      (4.2)
Multiply by |fhat(k)|^2, sum, and use Parseval/Tonelli:
  ||f||_{H^{-u}}^2
   <=C_u[||chi_{r0}^per*f||_2^2
                   +sum_{j>=1} r_j^{2u}||K_j^per*f||_2^2].  (4.3)
This is a one-sided norm bound valid for all L2 functions; that is all
the actual prelimit affine fields require. It does not assume a
covariance formula or positivity of a Fourier multiplier.

5. WHOLE-NORM MOMENT AND COMPACT TIGHTNESS

Apply (4.3) to f_n. The macro scales have expected contribution
  <=81B sum_{r_j>=1/n} r_j^{2u}<=C_u.
The subgrid contribution by (2.1) is
  <=C n^2 sum_{r_j<1/n} r_j^{2+2u}<=C_u n^{-2u}.            (5.1)
The bound remains uniform for the finitely many n with n r0<1.
The coarse term is (3.1). Thus
  sup_n E||f_n||_{H^{-u}}^2<=C'_u<infinity, EVERY u>0.       (5.2)
In particular the residual subgrid series vanishes as n grows for
every fixed u>0. Constants may diverge as u decreases to zero.

For a target s>0 choose 0<u<s. Closed H^{-u} balls are compact as
subsets of H^{-s}: their Fourier tails obey
  sum_{|lambda_k|>K}(1+|lambda_k|^2)^-s |fhat(k)|^2
     <=(1+K^2)^-(s-u)||f||_{H^{-u}}^2,
and their finitely many coordinates are bounded. A diagonal subsequence
and the tail estimate give convergence in H^{-s}; Fatou makes the
limiting vector still belong to the same H^{-u} ball.
Markov applied to (5.2) supplies these compact sets with probability
arbitrarily close to one, uniformly in n. This proves tightness in
H^{-s}, for EVERY s>0, with the required actual interpolation and pins.

6. IDENTIFICATION OF THE ALREADY KNOWN GFF LAW

Fix s>0 and choose s0>max{1,s}. The embedding H^{-s}->H^{-s0}
is continuous and injective. Tightness gives subsequential limits in
the stronger space; their weaker pushforwards equal the supplied GFF
law by continuous mapping and known whole-law convergence.

There is at most one stronger Borel probability with that weaker law.
Indeed the countable real/imaginary Fourier coordinates are continuous
in both spaces, and generate the Borel sigma-field of H^{-s}: squared
distances are increasing limits of finite weighted coordinate sums,
and a countable dense family of balls generates the topology. Thus
all stronger subsequential laws coincide, proving full convergence.

No GFF covariance upper bound is imported to infer support. In fact
the extended H^{-u} squared norm is lower semicontinuous on the weaker
space, as the supremum of its finite Fourier sums. The supplied weak
convergence and (5.2) imply E||G||_{H^{-u}}^2<=C'_u. Consequently
G belongs a.s. to all positive negative orders after intersecting the
countably many rational u>0. This is the SAME distributional GFF with
the previously established physical stiffness, not a new normalization.
