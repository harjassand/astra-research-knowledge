Abstract

We exhibit a configuration of five point charges in Euclidean space whose electrostatic potential admits at least 24 critical points all of which are non-degenerate. Maxwell’s conjecture that the field of 
n
nn point charges has at most 
(n-1)^{2}
(n−1)2(n-1)^{2} critical points which are all non-degenerate is therefore false.

1 Introduction

In J. C. Maxwell’s 1873 treatise on electricity and magnetism he discusses the number of equilibria of the electric field generated by 
n
nn point charges [5, §113]. Apparently unaware of this, M. Morse and S. S. Cairns in 1969 posed the problem of finding an upper bound for the number of equilibria [6, p. 293]. The first general bounds were supplied by A. Gabrielov, D. Novikov, and B. Shapiro in [3] who, based on their reading of [5, §113], formulated the ‘Maxwell conjecture’ which states that if the critical points of the electrostatic potential generated by 
n
nn point charges are all non-degenerate then their number cannot exceed 
(n-1)^{2}
(n−1)2(n-1)^{2}. These bounds were later improved by V. Zolotov in 2023 [8] and further improved by H. Edelsbrunner, C. Fillmore, and G. Oliveira in 2026 [2].
Maxwell’s bound is trivially achieved for 
n=2
n=2n=2 but it is not known even for 
n=3
n=3n=3 if 4 is the maximum number, except in the case of equal charges [7].
Further related problems in classical electrostatics are discussed in [1].

In this note we produce a counterexample to Maxwell’s conjecture: a configuration of five point charges with at least 24 critical points all of which are non-degenerate. The counterexample starts with three unit charges placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle. This configuration has four equilibria: one at the centre and three displaced inwards around the edges of the triangle, as shown in Figure 1(a). Two small charges are then added to the centre of the triangle and moved slightly apart along the orthogonal axis to form a shallow triangular bipyramid. The three edge equilibria persist under the addition of the small axial charges whereas the central equilibrium bifurcates into a family of 21 equilibria.
