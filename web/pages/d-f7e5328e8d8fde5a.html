# Radius-uniform experiment compression for generalized Gaussian location noise

Independent derivation, 7 October 2026. This is an internal mathematical
derivation, not formal verification, independent external review, or novelty
clearance. The Gaussian special case reconstructs the missing N30 mechanism;
the non-Gaussian cases are a transfer derived here.

## Statement and interface

Fix an integer k>=1, a>0, and observation dimension s. Let

    p(z)=Z^(-1) exp(-a z^(2k)),   Z=integral_R exp(-a z^(2k)) dz.

Observe ONE vector X with independent coordinate densities p(x_i-theta_i),
where the UNKNOWN means satisfy |theta_i|<=R_i. The radii and noise law are
known. The encoder sees X, never theta. Every retained register depending on
X passes through an algebra direct_sum_j M_(d_j), charged by D=sum_j d_j.
The decoder outputs a fresh real vector Y. Independent shared classical
randomness is free, with dimension <=D at every seed. There is no free
entangled receiver reference or input-dependent side information.

Use TV=half L1, and require sup_theta TV(law_theta(Y),law_theta(X))<=epsilon.
For fixed s,k,a there are c,C,epsilon_0>0, independent of ALL radii, such that

    c product_i psi_epsilon(R_i)
      <= D_quantum(epsilon;R)
      <= D_classical(epsilon;R)
      <= C product_i psi_epsilon(R_i),

    psi_epsilon(R)=max(1,sqrt(R/epsilon),R/sqrt(epsilon)),

simultaneously for R_i>=0 and 0<epsilon<epsilon_0. The constants may depend
on s,k,a. Thus k=1,a=1/2 is precisely Gaussian noise with variance one;
k=2,a=1 is quartic location noise. These are equality of orders, not optimal
constants or a finite-bit execution theorem.

## 1. A general trace-mass lemma

Let mu be ANY source probability law, sigma_(r,x) an encoded trace-one
positive state on a direct sum of total dimension <=D, and M_r(dy) any
decoder POVM. For a measurable relation A(x,y), independently of the seed,

    Pr(A(X,Y)) <= D sup_y mu({x:A(x,y)}).                   (1)

Indeed sigma_(r,x)<=I. Tonelli and positivity therefore give, at every seed,

    integral mu(dx) integral 1_A(x,y) Tr(sigma_(r,x) M_r(dy))
      <= integral Tr(M_r(dy)) mu({x:A(x,y)})
      <= D sup_y mu({x:A(x,y)}).

The total trace of the identity in the memory is sum_j d_j. It is not the
number of matrix entries sum_j d_j^2, nor the largest branch dimension.
Average over the independent seed. This argument permits mixed encodings,
continuous outputs, and arbitrary mixing of coordinates.

## 2. A general posterior/proper-loss conversion

Choose a finite prior over parameters, only for the converse. Let p_i(x)
be any marginal posterior probability vector, or a binary posterior
probability. For a differentiable convex function F, use the proper loss

    L(t,q)=F(e_t)-F(q)-grad F(q) dot (e_t-q),

with scalar e_t=t for binary t in {0,1}. If p is the conditional posterior,
then expected loss at q minus expected loss at p is exactly

    B_F(p,q)=F(p)-F(q)-grad F(q) dot (p-q).                (2)

The actual channel coupling is parameter -> X -> (seed,memory) -> Y.
Consequently the conditional posterior given X and Y is still p_i(X_i),
not p_i(Y_i). Evaluating the original posterior map at Y is a chosen
decision rule. If the coordinate losses have a uniform bound L_* and
their sum is used, rowwise TV preservation implies

    E sum_i B_Fi(p_i(X_i),p_i(Y_i)) <= s L_* epsilon.      (3)

This holds even though Y's true posterior under the altered experiment
need not equal the original posterior function evaluated at Y.

## 3. Weak-coordinate converse, uniformly in 0<R<=1

Use equally likely means -R/2 and +R/2. With V(x)=a x^(2k), the posterior is

    b_R(x)=logistic(H_R(x)),
    H_R(x)=V(x+R/2)-V(x-R/2).

On the fixed interval C=[1,2], there are positive constants depending only
on k,a such that

    predictive_mass(C)>=m,
    |b_R(x)-1/2|<=A R,
    b_R'(x)>=v R,                                      (4)

uniformly in 0<R<=1. To verify this: H_R=O(R) on C by the mean value
theorem; H_R'=integral_(x-R/2)^(x+R/2) V''(t)dt>=cR
since t>=1/2; and logistic'(H_R) has a positive lower bound on the bounded
H_R range. Both shifted noise densities have a positive minimum on C.

Define F_R(t)=sqrt(R^2+(t-1/2)^2). Its derivative has magnitude <=1. The
proper loss above is nonnegative and at most 3, independently of R. Its
curvature is

    F_R''(t)=R^2/(R^2+(t-1/2)^2)^(3/2).

For p satisfying |p-1/2|<=AR, and ANY q in [0,1],

    B_FR(p,q)>=c_A R min(((p-q)/R)^2,1).                 (5)

For |p-q|<=R the segment lies within distance (A+1)R of 1/2, so curvature
is >=c_A/R and integration gives c_A(p-q)^2/(2R). For larger displacement,
integrate the same curvature on the final length-R segment ending at p:
the identity B_F(p,q)=integral_between(q,p) |p-t| F''(t)dt supplies >=c_A R/2.
Thus (5) remains valid when q is far outside the central posterior range.

For any target q and any delta>0, monotonicity in (4) bounds the length of

    {x in C: |b_R(x)-q|<=delta}

by 2delta/(vR). The predictive density is uniformly bounded. Therefore
small posterior displacement of size O(sqrt(R epsilon)) has source
probability at most C sqrt(epsilon/R), provided R is at least a sufficiently
large fixed multiple of epsilon.

Fixed Brier curvature would give R^2 instead of R. The scale-dependent
bounded proper loss is the step that recovers the linear weak coefficient.

## 4. Strong-coordinate converse, uniformly in R>=1

Put M=floor(R), K=2M+1, and use the uniform prior on integer means
j=-M,...,M. Let p_j(x)=p(x-j)/S_M(x), where

    S_M(x)=sum_(j=-M)^M p(x-j).

The full integer alias sum sup_x sum_(j in Z) p(x-j) is finite. Thus the
prior-predictive density S_M(x)/K is <=C/R. On

    C_R=[-M+1/2,M-1/2],

its mass is bounded below by a positive constant: all the K-2 interior
prior atoms contribute the fixed event |noise|<=1/2, and for M=1 the central
atom contributes at least one third of that mass.

For each x in C_R choose two adjacent prior atoms j,j+1 such that
x-j lies in [-1/2,1/2]. At the upper endpoint choose j=M-1. Both corresponding
posterior coordinates are >=b>0 independently of R, because their numerator
arguments lie within [-3/2,3/2] and the denominator is uniformly bounded.
Their log ratio is

    log(p_(j+1)(x)/p_j(x))=V(x-j)-V(x-j-1).

The derivative of this function is >=b_0>0 on the entire real line:

    V'(z)-V'(z-1)
      =a(2k)[z^(2k-1)-(z-1)^(2k-1)]
      >=a(2k) 2^(2-2k).                                (6)

The minimum occurs at z=1/2. For k=1 the derivative is constant.

If another posterior vector is within Euclidean distance b/2, the two
selected coordinates remain >=b/2. The logarithm is then Lipschitz, and
(6) gives |x-x'|<=C ||p(x)-p(x')||_2. Therefore, for any posterior target q,
the set

    {x in C_R:||p(x)-q||_2<=delta}

has diameter <=C delta when delta<=b/4. To see this for arbitrary q, compare
any two points of the preimage; their posterior vectors differ by <=2delta.
Its predictive probability is consequently <=C delta/R.

The ordinary vector Brier loss L(j,q)=||e_j-q||_2^2 is <=2, uniformly in
K, and its regret is ||p-q||_2^2. Thus an O(epsilon) regret threshold produces
source probability at most C sqrt(epsilon)/R. No dimension factor K appears
in the loss bound or the inverse posterior estimate.

## 5. One joint event proves the anisotropic product

Select the preceding priors independently across coordinates. For weak
coordinates with R_i>A_s epsilon use Section 3; for R_i>=1 use Section 4;
coordinates with R_i<=A_s epsilon can be omitted and fixed at zero in the
lower prior. Their psi_epsilon factors are bounded by a constant depending
only on s,k,a, so omitting them affects only the final constant.

The product central event has predictive probability >=m_s>0. By (3) and
Markov, choose lambda_s so that its intersection with

    sum_i posterior_regret_i <=lambda_s epsilon

has probability >=m_s/2. Choose A_s large enough that (5) forces each weak
posterior displacement to be <=C_s sqrt(R_i epsilon). Choose epsilon_0
small enough that each strong displacement <=sqrt(lambda_s epsilon) lies
in the range covered by Section 4.

For each fixed decoder output y, the source probability of this joint
event is at most

    C_s product_(weak active i) sqrt(epsilon/R_i)
        product_(strong i) sqrt(epsilon)/R_i.             (7)

The source factorizes; the actual encoder need not factorize. Combine
the positive actual coupling mass with (1) and (7). This gives exactly the
product lower bound in the theorem. Independent seed averaging in (1)
preserves it. This avoids the invalid inference of a product from separate
one-coordinate lower bounds.

## 6. Strong upper: Cauchy compactification and finite derivative moments

For R>=1 choose L=1+R and public density

    g_L(x)=L/[pi(L^2+x^2)],
    G_L(x)=1/2+atan(x/L)/pi,
    q_theta(G_L(x))=p(x-theta)/g_L(x).

Writing f(x)=p(x-theta), direct differentiation gives

    ||q_theta''||_1
      = pi^2/L^2 integral_R |(L^2+x^2)^2 f''(x)
              +6x(L^2+x^2) f'(x)+(6x^2+2L^2) f(x)| dx.  (8)

All polynomial moments of p,p',p'' are finite. Set x=theta+z, use
|theta|<=R<=L and L>=1 in (8), and obtain

    sup_(|theta|<=R) ||q_theta''||_1<=C_(k,a) L^2.        (9)

Both q and its first derivative vanish at the interval endpoints, because
super-polynomial noise decay dominates Cauchy factors. Its periodic
extension is in W^(2,1).

For J>=5 and h=1/J use periodic triangular hats

    a_j(u)=(1-dist_circle(u,jh)/h)_+.

Encode j with probability a_j(u), and decode a fresh v with density a_j(v)/h.
Their kernel K_h(u,v)=h^(-1)sum_j a_j(u)a_j(v) is symmetric, Markov, <=1/h,
and supported at circle distance <=2h. On the local unwrapping its first
moment about v is zero: each hat has mean its vertex, and the two active
outer weights interpolate that vertex mean to v. Taylor's formula gives

    ||K_h q-q||_1 <=(8/3)h^2 ||q''||_1.

Apply the inverse Cauchy transform at decoding. This supplies TV<=C R^2/J^2.
For J<5 the trivial TV<=1 is covered after enlarging the constant.

## 7. Weak upper: exact reference preservation and weighted Taylor estimates

For |theta|<=1 use L=1, nu(dx)=p(x)dx, and the same hats on u=G_1(x). Define

    alpha_j=integral a_j(G_1(x)) nu(dx),
    Gamma_j(dy)=a_j(G_1(y)) nu(dy)/alpha_j.

Each alpha_j is strictly positive. The encoder uses a_j(G_1(x)); the decoder
uses Gamma_j. The channel preserves nu EXACTLY, is reversible with respect
to nu, and its u-coordinate kernel has support at circle distance <=2h.

Let x(u)=tan(pi(u-1/2)), w(u)=pi(1+x(u)^2)p(x(u)), and

    f_theta(u)=p(x(u)-theta)/p(x(u)).

The output likelihood ratio is

    K f_theta(u)=sum_j a_j(u)
                     [integral a_j(v) f_theta(v) w(v)dv]/alpha_j. (10)

In particular K1=1. The following estimates prove

    ||K f_theta-f_theta||_(L1(nu))<=C_(k,a)|theta|h^2.     (11)

Choose a sufficiently small fixed c>0 and h<=h_0, and put
T=c h^(-1/(2k+1)). Enlarge h_0's reciprocal if needed so T>=2. On the central
region |x(u)|<=T, every active hat and its integration support lie within
distance 2h of u and avoid the seam. The tangent addition formula gives

    |x(v)-x(u)|<=C h(1+x(u)^2).

Moreover

    |(log w)'(v)|<=C(1+|x(u)|^(2k+1)),
    |(log f_theta)'(v)|<=C|theta|(1+|x(u)|^(2k)).

Thus w(v)/w(u) and f_theta(v)/f_theta(u) have uniformly bounded positive
ratios over these supports. Direct derivatives also give

    sup |f_theta'|<=C|theta|(1+|x(u)|^(2k))f_theta(u),
    sup |f_theta''|<=C|theta|(1+|x(u)|^(4k))f_theta(u).      (12)

For a knot u_j, let mu_j=integral a_j(v)(v-u_j)w(v)dv. Its unweighted tent
first moment is zero. Subtracting w(u_j), bounding w' by its local supremum,
and using alpha_j>=c_1 h w(u_j), gives

    |mu_j/alpha_j|<=C h^2(1+|x(u)|^(2k+1)).              (13)

Taylor-expand the weighted conditional average in (10) around u_j. Its
constant term is f_theta(u_j), its linear term is f_theta'(u_j)mu_j/alpha_j,
and the remainder is bounded by C h^2 sup|f_theta''|. The outer hat
interpolation of the knot values cancels its first moment about u. By
(12)-(13), the pointwise central error is therefore at most

    C |theta| h^2 (1+|x(u)|^(4k+1)) f_theta(u).           (14)

Integrating against nu makes the right side a polynomial moment of
p(x-theta), uniformly bounded for |theta|<=1. This proves the central part
of (11).

For A={|x|>T}, use exact K1=1 and reversibility:

    integral_A |K(f_theta-1)| dnu
      <=integral |f_theta-1| K(1_A) dnu
      <=integral_(|x|>T/2) |p(x-theta)-p(x)|dx.           (15)

The last inequality follows from 2h support in Cauchy coordinates: for h
small enough, a point with |x|<=T/2 cannot reach |x|>T. Endpoint wrapping
only connects two far-tail points. The mean value theorem supplies the
uniform tail estimate

    integral_(|x|>T/2) |p(x-theta)-p(x)|dx
      <=C|theta| exp(-c_2 T^(2k))
      <=C'|theta|h^2.                                  (16)

The direct integral of |f_theta-1| on A satisfies the same bound. Combining
(14)-(16) proves (11). For the finitely many J with h>h_0, decode p itself:
TV(p(. -theta),p)<=|theta| ||p'||_1/2, and enlarge the J^-2 constant.
Hence, for ALL J>=1,

    sup_(|theta|<=R) TV(output,p(. -theta))<=C R/J^2,
    0<=R<=1.                                          (17)

No tail cutoff occurs in the actual encoder or decoder. The central/tail
split is only a proof device. The zero-reference identity is exact.

## 8. Tensorization and scalar tolerance allocation

Assign each coordinate tolerance epsilon/s. For weak coordinates choose
J_i of order max(1,sqrt(R_i/(epsilon/s))); for strong coordinates choose
J_i of order R_i/sqrt(epsilon/s). Use an unused one-label exact simulator
when R_i=0. Equations (9) and (17) give a product classical code. The product
TV inequality bounds the total error by the sum of coordinate errors.
The retained alphabet has product_i J_i labels, bounded by the claimed
C_s product_i psi_epsilon(R_i). This finishes the upper and the theorem.

## 9. Resources, limits, and status

These are ideal measurable stochastic channels. Known radii, noise law,
reference normalizations, and continuous-output sampling are supplied model
and decoder inputs. Acquiring the model, evaluating alpha_j, handling rare
conditional branches to finite precision, and physically implementing a
continuous-output decoder are additional costs. Atomic finite-bit outputs
have TV one against these continuous laws unless the output interface is
changed. No efficient compiler, physical quantum advantage, exact constant,
or growing-dimension uniformity follows.

The proof applies to full coordinate boxes and, by containment, comparable
full ellipsoids in a coordinate system where the noise still has the declared
law. It does NOT establish a law for thin curved or finite parameter sets
whose geometry changes with epsilon. It also does not turn an approximate
statistical equivalence into an exact one: a bidirectional bridge with error
eta leaves an additive 2eta in the experiment-compression error comparison.

Posterior quantization, proper scores, information-distortion lower bounds,
and positive approximation operators are established machinery. The exact
radius-uniform generalized-location formulation's historical priority has
not been cleared. The contribution here is a self-contained internal
derivation and a non-Gaussian transfer suitable for independent review.
