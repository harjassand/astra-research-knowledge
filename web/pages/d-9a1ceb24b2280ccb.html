# Conditional kinetic-commitment theorem

## Interface

There are $m\ge2$ competing, independent outer-sphere SET channels. Channel $i$ has a substrate pool held constant over the observation window and a shared reduced catalyst concentration $D$. SET generates a reduced intermediate $R_i$ at flux

\[
u_i=\alpha_iD,\qquad \alpha_i=k_{\rm ET,i}[S_i]>0.
\]

From $R_i$, product formation occurs with a first-order *effective commitment* hazard $c_i>0$, while back electron transfer has pseudo-first-order hazard $b_iA$, where $b_i=k_{\rm BET,i}>0$ and $A\ge0$ is the oxidized-catalyst concentration. There are no other exits in this reduced model. $D,A,[S_i]$, and all rates are constant; the radical intermediates are at quasi-steady state. The model does not assert that a multi-step chemical pathway has one experimentally measured $c_i$; it treats $c_i$ as the acquired first-passage commitment hazard to the selected product channel. Set

\[
q_i=\frac{c_i}{b_i},\qquad
J_i(A)=\frac{v_i(A)}{D}
=\alpha_i\frac{c_i}{c_i+b_iA}
=\alpha_i\frac{q_i}{q_i+A}.
\]

Here $q_i$ has concentration units and is the acceptor concentration at which channel $i$'s BET and commitment hazards are equal. $v_i=D J_i$ is product flux.

## Theorem 1: exact multichannel selectivity and robust monotonicity

The target product fraction is

\[
s_j(A)=\frac{\alpha_jq_j/(q_j+A)}{\sum_{i=1}^m\alpha_iq_i/(q_i+A)}.
\]

As $A\to\infty$,

\[
s_j(A)\longrightarrow
s_j^\infty=\frac{\alpha_jq_j}{\sum_i\alpha_iq_i}.
\]

If $q_j\ge q_i$ for every competitor $i\ne j$, then $s_j(A)$ is nondecreasing in $A$. It is strictly increasing whenever some competitor has $q_i<q_j$ and has nonzero flux.

### Proof

The quasi-steady balance is $0=u_i-(c_i+b_iA)[R_i]$, so $[R_i]=\alpha_iD/(c_i+b_iA)$ and $v_i=c_i[R_i]$, giving the expression above. Divide numerator and denominator by $A$ for the high-acceptor limit.

Write $V_-(A)=\sum_{i\ne j}v_i(A)$ and $w_i(A)=v_i(A)/V_-(A)$. Since $\frac{d}{dA}\log v_i=-1/(A+q_i)$,

\[
\frac{d}{dA}\log\frac{v_j}{V_-}
=\sum_{i\ne j}w_i(A)\left(\frac1{A+q_i}-\frac1{A+q_j}\right)\ge0
\]

under the stated ordering. As $s_j=(v_j/V_-)/(1+v_j/V_-)$, it has the same monotonicity. $\square$

## Theorem 2: interval-robust setpoint compiler

Suppose each channel has independently acquired rectangular intervals

\[
\alpha_i\in[\underline\alpha_i,\overline\alpha_i],\qquad
q_i\in[\underline q_i,\overline q_i],
\]

with positive endpoints. Under every parameter choice in these intervals, the target fraction is bounded below at a fixed $A$ by

\[
\underline s_j(A)=
\frac{\underline\alpha_j\underline q_j/(A+\underline q_j)}
{\underline\alpha_j\underline q_j/(A+\underline q_j)
+\sum_{i\ne j}\overline\alpha_i\overline q_i/(A+\overline q_i)}.
\]

If $\underline q_j\ge\max_{i\ne j}\overline q_i$, this robust lower envelope is nondecreasing. Its high-$A$ ceiling is

\[
\underline s_j^\infty=
\frac{\underline\alpha_j\underline q_j}
{\underline\alpha_j\underline q_j+\sum_{i\ne j}\overline\alpha_i\overline q_i}.
\]

For a selectivity floor $p\in(0,1)$, a finite feasible $A$ exists only if $p<\underline s_j^\infty$, except that if $\underline s_j(0)\ge p$, $A=0$ already meets the floor. If $A$ is restricted to a finite catalyst pool $0\le A\le C_T$, it is feasible exactly when $\underline s_j(C_T)\ge p$. Where feasible and the ordering condition holds, bisection on the rational monotone lower envelope produces a certified bracket for the minimum acceptable $A$. The script kinetic_commitment_gate.py computes this bracket using exact rational arithmetic and returns UNKNOWN if the required commitment ordering is not established.

### Proof

For fixed $A$, $J_i=\alpha_iq_i/(A+q_i)$ is nondecreasing in each positive variable $\alpha_i$ and $q_i$, because

\[
\partial_{\alpha_i}J_i=\frac{q_i}{A+q_i}>0,\qquad
\partial_{q_i}J_i=\frac{\alpha_iA}{(A+q_i)^2}\ge0.
\]

Target flux is minimized at both lower endpoints, and every competitor flux is maximized at its upper endpoints; this gives the lower envelope. Its monotonicity follows from Theorem 1 with target lower commitment at least every competitor's upper commitment. The ceiling and feasibility statements follow from monotonicity and continuity. Exact rational bisection preserves endpoint inequalities; the executable certificate records those comparisons. $\square$

## Two-channel closed form and finite-BET gate

For target 1 and competitor 2, let

\[
g=\frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2},\qquad
u=\frac{c_1}{c_2},\qquad
\beta=\frac{bA}{c_2}
\]

when the BET bimolecular constants are equal, $b_1=b_2=b$. Then exact QSS gives

\[
R(A)=\frac{v_1}{v_2}=g\,u\frac{1+\beta}{u+\beta}.
\]

For required odds $\rho=p/(1-p)$, a finite chemical commitment ratio can meet $R\ge\rho$ only if $g(1+\beta)>\rho$. When that condition holds and the target is not already above threshold at $\beta=0$,

\[
u\ge u_{\min}=\frac{\rho\beta}{g(1+\beta)-\rho}.
\]

The target commitment probability per SET attempt at this gate is $\pi_1=u_{\min}/(u_{\min}+\beta)$, so its expected SET attempts per committed target product in the independent-return model are $1/\pi_1=1+\beta/u_{\min}$. This is an electron-transfer-attempt proxy; it is not photon quantum yield, Faradaic efficiency, or energy per isolated product.

The executable exact check uses the SI Fig. 5c *illustrative* $\beta=560$, not a fitted Ket-1/styrene parameter. At equal capture $g=1$, a 90% target share requires $u\ge210/23\approx9.1304$, while 10:1 odds require $u\ge5600/551\approx10.1633$. With the caption-consistent prose capture ratio $g=2/15$, the gates are $u\ge3600/47\approx76.5957$ and $u\ge7000/81\approx86.4198$, respectively. These numbers expose the effect of the source's disputed capture rate; they do not establish that the chemical rates or $\beta$ attain those values in the reaction.

## Theorem 3: throughput-optimal static setpoint under a finite redox pool

Assume $D+A=C_T$ with $C_T>0$, all $\alpha,c,b$ fixed, and target $j$ has the largest $q_j$ so its selectivity rises monotonically with $A$. Then its flux

\[
v_j(A)=(C_T-A)\alpha_j\frac{q_j}{q_j+A}
\]

is strictly decreasing on $0\le A<C_T$. Therefore the maximum target throughput among static settings meeting $s_j(A)\ge p$ is attained at the **minimum feasible acceptor concentration** $A_{\min}$. Its value is

\[
v_j(A_{\min})=(C_T-A_{\min})\alpha_j\frac{q_j}{q_j+A_{\min}}.
\]

For interval uncertainty, replacing the target $\alpha$ and $q$ by lower endpoints yields a certified lower bound on that flux at the conservative upper endpoint of the bisection bracket. If $A_{\min}=C_T$, the selectivity floor can be reached only with zero donor and zero product flux; the design is chemically nonproductive.

### Proof

Differentiation gives

\[
v_j'(A)=-\alpha_jq_j\frac{C_T+q_j}{(A+q_j)^2}<0.
\]

Thus no larger $A$ can improve target throughput after satisfying the monotone selectivity floor; choosing the smallest feasible $A$ is optimal within this static two-state pool model. $\square$

## Evidence boundary

The two-channel QSS identity is already printed by Edgecomb et al. as SI Eq. S9. The contribution here is its explicit $N$-channel interval-robust static setpoint and throughput extension, together with finite-resource/status logic. It is an internal derivation and exact finite checker, not an externally reviewed theorem or a demonstrated chemical design capability. The parameter intervals, redox-pool constraint, and effective channel commitment hazards must be measured or justified for any real reaction. Temporal schedules, species depletion, cross-channel electron transfer, catalyst-state dynamics, and full optical/electrical costs are outside the theorem.
