1. PRECISE STATEMENT

Let T:M_d->M_d be completely positive, trace-preserving and unital. It
need NOT be self-adjoint in Hilbert-Schmidt inner product. Let T* be its
trace/HS adjoint, and put S=T* composed with T. Let Psi be ANY fixed
bistochastic CPTP map, and let C>0, satisfying on EVERY Hermitian A

  E_Psi(A)<=C E_S(A),                                      (1)
  E_R(A):=Tr[A(A-R(A))].

Here traces are ordinary unnormalized traces. Then simultaneously for
EVERY density matrix rho, including singular rho, with entropy in nats
and HALF trace distances,

  D=D(rho||I/d)=Tr[rho log(d rho)],
  b=(1/2)||rho-T(rho)||_1,
  e=(1/2)||rho-Psi(rho)||_1,

one has

  e^3 <= (27C/2) D b.                                     (2)

BROADCASTING COROLLARY. Suppose an ACTUAL CPTP B:M_d->M_d tensor M_d
has both marginals T. Then S=T*T is itself bistochastic, HS-self-adjoint,
HS-positive and legally self-compatible. The supplied universal C4 theorem

The SAME Psi for EVERY rho obeys

  e^2<=3*2^(4/3)(D+1)b^(2/3)
      <=8(D+1)b^(2/3).                                    (4)

The residual b is measured against the ORIGINAL raw T, not S or a Petz

2. S=T*T IS AN ACTUAL LEGAL BROADCAST MARGINAL

Complete positivity of T implies complete positivity of T* by adjointing
Kraus operators. TP of T implies T*(I)=I. Unitality of T implies that T*
is TP. Thus T* is bistochastic CPTP, and so is S. Directly,
  <A,S(A)>_HS=||T(A)||_2^2>=0,
and S is HS-self-adjoint as T*T. In particular, S has no problematic
nonreal or negative HS spectrum even when raw T is nonself-adjoint.

Given the joint B, define
  B_S=(T* tensor T*) composed with B.
This is CPTP because EACH local T* is CPTP. Its two marginals are
T* composed with T=S, by trace preservation of the opposite local T*.
It is therefore a legal joint broadcaster for S. Output-symmetrizing B
before or after this construction is permitted and preserves S.
This is not an assumption that a general adjoint is a physical channel:
unitality of the RAW T is essential to that conclusion.

3. EXACT DISCRETE ENTROPY-LOSS LEMMA

For any density rho define
  Delta_T(rho)=h(T rho)-h(rho), h(rho)=-Tr[rho log rho].
Claim:

  Delta_T(rho) >= 1-||T(sqrt(rho))||_2^2
               = E_S(sqrt(rho)).                          (5)

The identity at the right is the adjoint relation and Tr rho=1. Its
nonnegativity follows from bistochastic CP Schwarz and trace preservation.
No HS-self-adjointness of T is needed.

First assume rho>0. Choose rank-one eigenprojectors P_j of rho with
eigenvalues r_j>0 and rank-one eigenprojectors Q_i of T rho with
eigenvalues y_i>0. Unitality ensures T rho>=lambda_min(rho) I>0. Set

  p_ij=Tr[Q_i T(P_j)].

The two eigenbases can be different, and T need not map one diagonal
algebra into the other. Positivity, TP and unitality give respectively
  p_ij>=0, sum_i p_ij=1, sum_j p_ij=1.
In general p_ij is NOT symmetric. Nevertheless y_i=sum_j p_ij r_j.

Define two normalized classical distributions on the PAIR (i,j):
  a_ij=p_ij r_j, c_ij=p_ij y_i.
Their sums are one. Their nonzero supports coincide for faithful rho.
Direct expansion gives

  D_cl(a||c)
    =sum_ij p_ij r_j log(r_j/y_i)
    =sum_j r_j log r_j-sum_i y_i log y_i
    =Delta_T(rho).                                        (6)

Let
  beta=sum_ij sqrt(a_ij c_ij)
      =sum_ij p_ij sqrt(r_j y_i)
      =Tr[sqrt(T rho) T(sqrt(rho))].                       (7)

Scalar Jensen for the convex function -log, with weights a_ij, gives
  D_cl(a||c)>=-2 log(beta)>=2(1-beta).                     (8)
Indeed apply Jensen to sqrt(c_ij/a_ij), omit zero-weight entries, and
use -log u>=1-u. These are actual probabilities, so 0<beta<=1.
This proves the classical KL/Hellinger estimate directly, with the factor
two and without a citation-dependent convention about squared fidelity.

Now put w=sqrt(T rho), v=T(sqrt(rho)). Both are positive Hermitian,
||w||_2^2=1, and beta=<w,v>_HS. Although they may NOT commute,

  2(1-beta)-(1-||v||_2^2)=||w-v||_2^2>=0.                (9)

Combining (6)--(9) proves (5). This step retains all off-diagonal entries
of v through its Hilbert-Schmidt norm; it never identifies that norm with
a diagonal classical sum or supposes T is random-unitary.

The somewhat stronger bound
  Delta_T(rho)>=-log(||T(sqrt(rho))||_2^2)
also follows from beta<=||v||_2, but it is not needed below.

For possibly singular rho, mix rho_delta=(1-delta)rho+delta I/d. The
entropy is continuous in finite dimension, as are square root, T, and the
HS norm. Letting delta decrease to zero proves (5) for every rho, without
logs on a singular support or division by y_i=0. Equivalently, in (6) any
y_i=0 implies p_ij r_j=0 for all j, so no positive joint mass is divided
by zero. The faithful-state limit makes every convention explicit.

4. THE SAME C4 COMPARATOR CONVERTS DISCRETE LOSS TO TRACE ERROR

For a bistochastic CPTP Psi and any state rho, the already derived
CP-Schwarz/canonical-purification argument gives

  Tdist(rho,Psi rho)^2<=2 E_Psi(sqrt(rho)).                 (10)

Here Tdist denotes HALF trace distance, to distinguish it from channel T.
For clarity: the positive block [[rho,sqrt(rho)],[sqrt(rho),I]] and
unital CP Psi give Psi(sqrt(rho))^2<=Psi(rho); square-root monotonicity
then gives Psi(sqrt(rho))<=sqrt(Psi(rho)). Positive multiplication inside
the trace gives affinity
  Tr[sqrt(rho) sqrt(Psi rho)]>=1-E_Psi(sqrt(rho)).
Canonical vectorizations purify the two states, with this affinity as
overlap. Partial-trace contraction and the exact HALF trace distance of
two pure states give (10). Also 0<=E_Psi(sqrt(rho))<=1 by CP Schwarz,
TP and HS Cauchy-Schwarz, so the scalar squaring step is legitimate.

Thus for EVERY state rho, using the SAME fixed Psi,

  e(rho)^2<=2C E_S(sqrt(rho))<=2C Delta_T(rho).             (11)

For C4 this is e(rho)^2<=8 Delta_T(rho). Ordinary and normalized trace
conventions introduce no extra dimension factor: applying the supplied
normalized-trace form order to sqrt(d rho) is exactly (1) applied to
sqrt(rho), with both sides scaled equally.

5. RAW NONREVERSIBLE POISSON FLOW AND THE EXACT ENTROPY IDENTITY

Let
  Q_t=exp[t(T-I)]=exp(-t) sum_(n>=0) (t^n/n!) T^n,
  rho_t=Q_t rho.
Every Q_t is bistochastic CPTP, since this is a convex Poisson mixture
of bistochastic CPTP powers. It need NOT be HS-self-adjoint. For rho>0,
rho_t stays uniformly faithful because Q_t is positive and unital.

Let D_t=D(rho_t||I/d). Trace spectral differentiation, without any
commutation of rho_t and its derivative, gives
  D'_t=Tr[(T rho_t-rho_t) log rho_t].
There is an EXACT identity

  -D'_t
    =Delta_T(rho_t)+D(T rho_t||rho_t)
    >=Delta_T(rho_t).                                    (12)

The equality follows by adding and subtracting Tr[(T rho_t)log(T rho_t)].
The last term is nonnegative quantum relative entropy. It can be checked
elementarily by choosing separate eigenbases of its two density matrices:
the overlap squares form a doubly stochastic matrix and express the
quantum relative entropy as a normalized classical joint KL, just as in
(6). Thus (12) is stronger than the proposed convexity inequality and
does not depend on a HS reversibility hypothesis.

Since D_t>=0, integration and (11) give for every horizon H>0,

  integral_0^H e(rho_t)^2 dt
    <=2C integral_0^H Delta_T(rho_t) dt
    <=2C D.                                             (13)

6. ORIGINAL RAW RESIDUAL, ORBIT AVERAGING, AND CONSTANTS

The derivative equation and the fact that Q_t is a function of T give
  rho_t-rho=integral_0^t Q_s(T rho-rho) ds.
Hermitian trace-norm contraction of every Q_s therefore implies
  Tdist(rho_t,rho)<=t b.
Trace contraction of the SAME Psi and the triangle inequality give
  e(rho)<=e(rho_t)+2t b
for EVERY t. Uniform averaging over [0,H], followed by scalar
Cauchy-Schwarz and (13), yields

  e<=b H+sqrt(2C D/H).                                   (14)

For b,D>0 choose H=(C D/(2b^2))^(1/3). Then the square-root term is
2bH, so e<=3bH and e^3<=(27C/2)Db, proving (2).
For C4 this is exactly e^3<=54Db. There is no restriction on H.

The universal cap e<=1 gives (3) without changing Psi. Squaring (3) and
maximizing D^(2/3)/(D+1), whose maximum is 2^(2/3)/3 at D=2, gives
  e^2<=3*2^(4/3)(D+1)b^(2/3).
This coefficient is below 8 exactly because its cube is 432<512=8^3.
No sharpness of the actual broadcasting exponent or coefficient is claimed.
