# Exact stationary-point acquisition baseline and vanishing ideal

2026-10-07. Complete mathematical reduction and runnable rational prototype. **This is an exponential algebraic baseline, not the polynomial acquisition advance.** The newer extactic route developed with proof_linear now has priority. No historical novelty is claimed for exact polynomial-system solving.

## Input and finite complex stationary structure

The sole input is expanded rational p(x)=((Lx)^2-a)^TQ((Lx)^2-a), with hidden rational invertible L, Q positive definite, a>0 and b=Qa>0. Acquire M=-Hess(p2)/4 rational positive definite. The full promise further implies G^-1 1=diag(b)a>0 for G=diag(b)^-1Qdiag(b)^-1. Generic positive-definite G need not satisfy this extra condition.

In hidden orthonormal coordinates, the stationary equations on the complex sphere are

    y_i[(Gm)_i-lambda]=0, m_i=y_i², sum_i m_i=1.

For any nonempty support S, positive definiteness makes G_SS invertible and c_S=1^T G_SS^-1 1 strictly positive. Necessarily

    lambda=1/c_S, m_S=G_SS^-1 1/c_S.

Every support has at most 2^|S| square-root solutions. Components of m_S which vanish invalidate that exact support, and negative components give purely imaginary coordinates rather than real points. Thus the complex stationary set is finite, with at most 3^n-1 distinct points. Multiple algebraic roots may occur; nondegeneracy of all stationary points is not assumed.

The projective charts w_j=1 with equations grad p4(w)=lambda M w are also zero-dimensional. An isotropic projective solution cannot occur: after the hidden normalization, support stationarity gives m_S=c G_SS^-1 1, and sum m=0 forces c=0, hence w=0, contradicting the chart. Every chart solution can therefore be scaled to a sphere stationary point. Its number of distinct complex rays is at most (3^n-1)/2.

## Rational exact acquisition and deflation

At a true axis, x=L^-1 e_i/sqrt(b_i) on the ellipsoid x^TMx=1. Hence Mx=sqrt(b_i)ell_i^T, and Mxx^TM=b_i ell_i^Tell_i is rational. More directly, every projective primal axis L^-1e_i has rational coordinate ratios. It is enough to enumerate rational roots in the projective critical charts, not all algebraic roots.

A global maximum is an axis, and one with G_ii=max_j G_jj has strictly negative tangent Hessian: G_ij<G_ii by positive definiteness. Therefore some rational stationary chart point passes an exact negative-definite tangent-Hessian test. Every point passing this test is an axis by the sphere escape lemma. No latent mu threshold is used.

For rational current embedding B, form f(u)=p4(Bu), M'=B^TMB. Given an acquired rational primal ray w, its original covector is r=(M B w)^T, using the ORIGINAL M. This lifting step is important: B need not be Euclidean orthonormal. Update B by a rational nullspace basis of rB. The hidden remaining row system stays rational invertible and the quartic/metric retain a positive-definite principal-submatrix promise. Approximate deflation errors never arise.

After all rows are acquired, transform the full input polynomial by their rational inverse. Read Q' from fourth-degree coefficients, b' from quadratic coefficients, and solve a'=Q'^-1b'. Verify Q'>0, a'>0 and the full coefficient identity. Rational LDL supplies positive weighted squares. All these quantities are acquired; hidden fixture parameters are unavailable to the learner.

`work/equal_charge_audit/cycle8/exact_axis_acquisition.py` implements exact lexicographic Groebner elimination and recursive rational-root extraction for each chart. It receives only expanded p and variable names. Exact stationary residuals, tangent Hessians, final expansion, rational positive definiteness and retrospective fixture-only ray comparisons are checked. ## Cost boundary

The implemented Groebner routine terminates on the promise by zero-dimensionality. It is not advertised as a polynomial-time method. A source-backed exact alternative uses one-block real quantifier elimination for each coordinate projection, extracts its rational roots and checks Cartesian combinations. Basu's survey Theorem 2.18 gives singly exponential fixed-block arithmetic bounds and corresponding intermediate coefficient-height bounds; this supports a singly exponential-in-a-polynomial-of-n baseline after accounting for the successive rational substitutions. That theoretical variant is not implemented here, and no tight implementation bound is inferred from root counts alone. July 2026 Demin–Rouillier supplies a recent exact rational-univariate implementation comparator for general zero-dimensional ideals, not a new algorithmic primitive of ours.

The acquired final rational G permits an explicit positive lower bound det(G)/trace(G)^(n-1) on its least eigenvalue. This is post-acquisition conditioning, not an input oracle used to make the search run. A polynomial dependence on 1/mu would not itself be polynomial in input bits; G=11^T+2^-B I demonstrates that gap. That example alone is not an intrinsic hardness result, because its observable constant-on-sphere component can be removed and the remainder rescaled.

## Stronger full-polynomial ideal identity

Let J=<ell_i(x)^2-a_i> in Q[x]. Then

    J = <p, partial_1 p, ..., partial_n p>.            (*)

Proof: in latent y, every complex gradient-critical support S solves m_S=Q_SS^-1 b_S. Write T for its complement. If S is nonempty and T is nonempty, substitution gives

    p_critical = a_T^T (Q_TT-Q_TS Q_SS^-1 Q_ST) a_T > 0.

The Schur complement is positive definite and a_T is nonzero. The empty support gives p(0)=a^TQa>0. Thus a simultaneous zero of p and its gradient must have full support and y_i²=a_i. Conversely all those 2^n real points are simultaneous zeros. At each, Hess p=8 L^T diag(y)Q diag(y)L is invertible, so the gradient ideal is locally reduced. The zero-dimensional ideal <p,grad p> is therefore radical and has exactly the same complex zero set as the radical complete-intersection ideal J. Equality follows over C and descends to Q by faithful scalar extension. This proves (*), not just equality of real radicals.

Consequently the hidden rational quadratic factor subspace equals the degree-at-most-two part of this ordinary ideal. It can be acquired by exact elimination without a real-radical oracle. One known quadratic already follows from Euler: p-(x·grad p)/4=p2/2+p0. Whether all factors admit uniformly low-degree membership certificates remains unresolved here. The identity has been shared with proof_linear as a possible separate route to cheaper structure acquisition; the newer extactic method currently provides the stronger efficiency prospect.
