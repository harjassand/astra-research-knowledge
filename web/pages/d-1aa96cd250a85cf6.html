## Hypotheses carried through the deduction

Use the source curve `E: y²=x³−25x`, with rank-one group decomposition
`E(Q)=ZP⊕T`, where `T={O,(0,0),(5,0),(−5,0)}`. Appendix A supplies a descent
argument for rank one and this torsion subgroup. Fix a generator `P` modulo
torsion. For fixed even `m_*`, write

`W(Q)=H(x([m_*]Q))`, with `H(a/d)=max(|a|,d)` for a reduced fraction and
`W(Q)=1` when `[m_*]Q=O`.

The hypotheses used here are precisely:

1. `W(nP+t)=W_|n|`, `W_0=1`, and `W_j` is strictly increasing.
2. `log W_j = a j² + ε_j`, `|ε_j|≤c` for every `j≥1`.
3. The fixed integers and real bounds satisfy `a>2c`,
   `6c<log κ<2a−6c`, with integer `κ>0`.
4. For each `j≥1` there is exactly one representative `C(j)` among
   `(jP+T)∪(−jP+T)`: require nonidentity, positive y, `W>1`, and maximum
   x-coordinate in its torsion coset. The source uses `y≥0`; `W>1` excludes
   torsion, so y is in fact nonzero and positive.
5. Every point has one 13-coordinate natural code: two reduced six-coordinate
   rational codes and an identity flag. Rational arithmetic, point addition,
   fixed scalar multiplication, maxima, comparisons and their complete
   subsidiary tuples have single-fold polynomial implementations. Every
   inactive case-local coordinate is forced to zero, including nested calls.

The height theorem and choice of large fixed `m_*` supply hypotheses 1–3 in
the source. The proof of hypothesis 4 uses only finite torsion translates:
distinct translates in a nontorsion coset have distinct x-coordinates, while
negation pairs the maxima of opposite cosets. None of the indices or logarithms
is an existential variable in a polynomial system.

## Addition

Take valid positive representatives `Q=C(k)`, `R=C(l)`, where `k,l≥1`.
Their actual signed generator indices can have either sign; the representative
convention does not choose their sign in the quotient. Nevertheless,

`max{|ind(Q)+ind(R)|, |ind(Q)−ind(R)|}=k+l`.

Fixed point additions and strict monotonicity therefore give

`A(Q,R)=max{W(Q+R),W(Q−R)}=W_(k+l)`.

Define the addition graph by requiring a representative `S` and the equality
`W(S)=A(Q,R)`. The sequence is injective, so `S=C(k+l)` is the sole output.
There is no existential index and no unbounded scalar-multiplication call.
This can alternatively be evaluated by taking whichever of `Q+R,Q−R` has the
larger W-value, then maximizing x over its four torsion translates and selecting
positive y. Ties are assigned to a fixed branch. All these are fixed finite
operations.

## Multiplication: the six-height polarization window

For a candidate positive representative `S=C(r)`, form

`L=A(Q,R)·W(S)·W_1`,

`D=W(Q)·W(R)·max{W(S+P),W(S−P)}`.

The final maximum is `W_(r+1)`. The exact polynomial identity

`(k+l)²+r²+1−k²−l²−(r+1)² = 2(kl−r)`

then gives

`log(L/D)=2a(kl−r)+E`,

where

`E=ε_(k+l)+ε_r+ε_1−ε_k−ε_l−ε_(r+1)` and `|E|≤6c`.

Repeated indices may cancel error terms; the uniform six-term bound remains
valid. If `r=kl`, then `|log(L/D)|≤6c<log κ`. If `r≠kl`, integrality gives
`|log(L/D)|≥2a−6c>log κ`. Consequently the two natural-number polynomial
inequalities

`L≤κD` and `D≤κL`

hold exactly when `r=kl`. Each inequality introduces one uniquely determined
nonnegative slack. Thus the window selects precisely `C(kl)`.

This deduction strengthens the source's square window locally. It uses the
same amplification and the same error margin, and adds no new elliptic-height
assumption.

## The complete witness and zero

The domain of encoded arithmetic is the set of point codes for `O` or a
positive representative. The bijection is `0↦O`, `j↦C(j)` for `j≥1`.
The identity flag distinguishes O from the affine torsion point `(0,0)`.

For two positive inputs, the multiplication auxiliary tuple includes:
the output point code; its representative-test tuple; point codes and complete
addition tuples for `Q+R`, `Q−R`, `S+P`, `S−P`; all fixed scalar-chain and
height tuples for each queried point; the two maximum tuples; the named
products `L,D`; and their two comparison slacks. The input codes are displayed
arguments, not hidden witnesses. Values reused by more than one expression are
declared once or, if deliberately recomputed, all copies have unique values.
There is a constant number of calls, depending on the fixed scalar chain,
independent of k,l,r. Once the window fixes S, every remaining coordinate is
fixed by the source's complete-witness closure lemma.

For zero operands use a finite partition into the three cases
`Q=O`, `Q≠O and R=O`, and `Q≠O and R≠O`. Multiplication outputs O in the
first two cases. Addition outputs the other operand. Guards and every nested
equation are gated. Every coordinate local to an inactive branch is zero;
in particular, an inactive rational code is not required to have a positive
denominator outside its gated branch. The shared output is always a valid
domain point. The selectors are unique because these cases are disjoint.
This gives a single-fold polynomial operation graph for both operations on
the displayed coded domain. Invalid domain inputs have no solutions.

Taking the sum of squares of the finite defining equations preserves exactly
the same natural-number complete tuple. No proof step assumes that memoization
or reusing a formula permits reusing an unconstrained witness.

## What this enables and what it does not

The useful mechanism is an error-separated arithmetic interpreter: bounded
additive error in a quadratic observable can be converted into an exact
integer relation by polarization, fixed amplification, and a two-sided
multiplicative window. It provides a small interface for further polynomial
encoding proofs and isolates three independently testable obligations:
canonical representatives, an explicit uniform height-error certificate,
and complete auxiliary uniqueness.

This is not a Diophantine model of N over Q. All coordinate normalization and
slack witnesses range over N, and the proof uses their integrality and order.
Nor does this note provide a single-fold graph between an ordinary input index
`j` and its point code `C(j)`. Given a numerical generator P, that code is
computable by ordinary repeated/group addition and finite canonicalization;
that observation does not supply a fixed polynomial representation of the
unbounded scalar call.

The heights satisfy `log W_j=Θ(j²)`. Thus the expanded point representation
is large relative to the binary index. Addition has output height bits of
order `(k+l)²`; multiplication has output height bits of order `k²l²`.
These are representation costs, not a proved runtime for discovering the
window's unique output. Verification of any supplied finite tuple is ordinary
integer-polynomial evaluation, polynomial in its explicit bit length when
the fixed polynomials are given.

The paper does not numerically display P, `m_*`, κ, or its eventual cutoff.
That is an executable-construction gap, not a theorem that no recursive
compiler exists. The arithmetic constants are fixed for all machines; one
valid finite tuple could be hard-coded, after which the displayed finite
compilation operations are recursive. An existence proof of such a tuple
therefore still implies existence of a recursive compiler. A supplied
implementation must additionally acquire and certify a valid numerical tuple.

## Exact search obstruction

Assume the source's Theorem 1.1 for a fixed nonrecursive recursively enumerable
set `S⊆N`. Fix its single-fold polynomial `P(a,w)` with m witness coordinates.
For every total recursive `b:N→N`, there are infinitely many members a such
that their unique witness has some coordinate exceeding `b(a)`.

Proof: if all members' witnesses were bounded by b, enumerate the finite box
`[0,b(a)]^m` and test `P(a,w)=0`. This decides membership in S. If the claim
failed only at finitely many a, hard-code their witnesses into a modified
recursive bound, giving the same contradiction. The identical argument applies
to witness bit lengths and to a recursive bound depending on the encoding
length of the specialized polynomial `P(a,W)`, since specialization and its
encoding length are computable for fixed P. This is a universal obstruction,
not a conclusion from finite timing tests.

The ordinary DPRM representation already has the analogous witness-bound
barrier. The new uniqueness condition does not remove it. No theorem here
claims a speedup against an equally informed ordinary Diophantine encoding
or a proof-search control.
