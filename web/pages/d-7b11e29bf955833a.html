C01_L03 DEEPER ATTACK — PLÜCKER DEFECT PATH VERSUS PAIR-CLOSED ENERGY
7 October 2026, after SCIENCE_RESUME. INITIAL remains immutable.

RESULT

The one-column Plücker inequality cannot be projected termwise to the paired-basis state space, even with any finite multiplicative factor. An exact family shows why, while also showing that retaining split columns can repair connectivity without any weight loss in a special case.

Let n=2q and let F be the cyclic permutation matrix F_{i,i+1 mod n}=1. In V=[I,F^T], write e_i for identity column i and f_i for the column equal to e_{i+1}. A paired basis associated to I⊆[n], |I|=q, is P(I)={e_i,f_i:i∈I}, with determinant equal up to sign to det F[I,I^c]. This determinant is nonzero exactly when I^c=I+1 (cyclic successor), which forces I to alternate. Thus precisely two pair-closed bases are supported: P(E) and P(O), where E and O are even and odd sites, and each determinant has modulus one.

For any proper s-pair replacement of P(E) by s odd pairs, 1≤s<q, the new site set is neither E nor O, so its determinant vanishes. In particular, all one-pair paired-basis exchange destinations have zero product weight (and in fact every bounded-size update with size<q does). Thus a direct SOS inequality whose RHS sums only pair-closed exchange products has LHS 1 and RHS 0. No finite coefficient can fix that projected inequality.

Now apply the Plücker identity to the two supported ordinary column bases A=P(E), B=P(O), choosing a=e_0∈A. The GP list has d=n terms. Exactly one non-endpoint term is nonzero: exchange a=e_0 with b=f_{n-1}∈B, which is the other labelled copy of the same vector e_0. Both exchanged ordinary column sets remain bases and are pair-split. Hence the amplitude product of the endpoints (modulus 1) is carried entirely by a non-pair-closed split term. This is a concrete obstruction to discarding mismatch registers after the Plücker expansion, rather than an issue of a poor Cauchy constant.

The split register is not inherently high energy in this cycle family. There is an explicit path of exactly n=2q symmetric single-column cross-swaps from (A,B) to (B,A):

  Stage 1, for j=0,...,q-1: swap e_{2j} in A with f_{2j-1} in B (indices mod n).
  Stage 2, for j=0,...,q-1: swap f_{2j} in A with e_{2j+1} in B.

Each exchanged pair consists of two labels whose columns are the same coordinate vector. So both determinants stay of modulus one at every step. The split-pair count along the shortest path rises 0,2,...,2q and falls 2q-2,...,0. The n-step lower bound is exact: each of the 2q columns in A must change replica, and a cross-swap moves out only one column of A. Thus an enlarged state space can turn the disconnected pair support into a flat-weight defect path, but it must retain up to all n split labels. This special path does not supply a general construction or bound for arbitrary F.

`plucker_pair_closure_audit.py` uses Bareiss integer determinants. It enumerates all rank-n column bases of V for n=4 and 6, constructs the graph of ordered complementary pairs (A,A^c) when both minors are nonzero, computes its components and shortest route between the paired endpoints, and verifies the Plücker terms and pair-replacement destinations.

`plucker_pair_closure_audit_result.json` and `.txt` record:

- n=6: 20 pair-closed bases, exactly 2 supported; 64 valid complementary ordinary-basis pair states, one component; endpoint distance 6; all path product weights 1; 1 nonzero Plücker exchange term and it is split; all 9 one- and 9 two-pair replacements from the alternating endpoint are zero.

The general support and n-step path claims above are proved directly for every even n; finite code checks only the bookkeeping for n=4,6. These checks are not a mixing theorem.

A CHARACTER-REGISTER FORMULATION AND EXACT RARITY AUDIT

There is a useful exact encoding of the defect register. In the exterior basis, write a rank-n Plücker vector as |Psi>=sum_{|S|=n} p_S |S>. For each pair label i, let c_i(S)=|S intersect {e_i,f_i}| and let the group element gamma_i in {+1,-1} multiply both columns of pair i by gamma_i. The induced representation acts diagonally:

  rho(gamma)|S> = product_i gamma_i^{c_i(S)} |S>.

Thus S lies in character sector chi(S)={i:c_i(S)=1}, exactly the set of split pair labels. The group average 2^(-n) sum_gamma rho(gamma) is the orthogonal projector onto chi=empty, so its squared norm is precisely the pair-closed determinant partition. A one-column cross-swap between replicas toggles the two corresponding split-label bits in each replica; this makes the independent (chi_1,chi_2) registers explicit. Identifying chi_1=chi_2 is a diagonal-character projection, which need not preserve a polynomial fraction of the independent product norm.

The rarity can be computed exactly on q independent two-site blocks with F_block=[[0,1],[1,0]], n=2q, V=[I,F^T]. Cauchy-Binet gives the unrestricted rank-n volume-sampling partition

  sum_{|S|=n} |det V_S|^2 = det(V V*) = det(2 I_n) = 4^q.

A pair-closed basis must choose exactly one of the two site-pairs in each block. There are 2^q such bases, each with determinant modulus one, so the target parity partition is 2^q and the pair-closed event under the unrestricted DPP is exactly 2^(-q). The 2^q nonzero split-character sectors all have mass 2^q. Hence the diagonal-character projection has squared norm

  sum_chi Z_chi^2 = 2^q (2^q)^2 = 2^(3q),

whereas the independent tensor norm squared is (sum_chi Z_chi)^2=2^(4q); their ratio is 2^(-q). This is an exact algebraic analogue of the common-orientation loss. It does not identify every sewing projector with this group projector, but it pinpoints the same danger: collapsing independent mismatch registers can erase an exponential fraction. The full exact sector masses and checks for q=1,2,3 are in `plucker_pair_closure_audit_result.json`.

There is also an exact invariant of the proposed cross-swap walk. A one-column swap between replica bases at labels i and j toggles both character registers chi_1 and chi_2 on {i,j}; hence chi_1 symmetric-difference chi_2 is conserved. From a pair-closed endpoint the walk remains in the diagonal fiber chi_1=chi_2. Under the natural product-determinant stationary weights in that fiber, the target (empty,empty) sector has mass ratio Z_empty^2 / sum_chi Z_chi^2, which is exactly 2^(-q) in the block example. Thus retaining the full diagonal character register restores paths but the unmodified product-weight lift makes the target sector exponentially rare. Any successful exact-marginal lift must change the defect-sector weighting or avoid normalizing over all diagonal sectors.

WHAT THE CALCULATION DOES AND DOES NOT CLOSE

A Metropolis chain on unrestricted complementary ordinary bases has the wrong state space for linear matroid parity. Its cross-swaps preserve the multiset union of the two bases (and their intersection), so the product chain is fibered. The target line-pair law lives on pair-closed bases. Conditioning an unrestricted DPP/volume-sampling law on pair-closure can make that event exponentially rare; a penalty/softening route was separately ruled out in the campaign by exact tensor examples. Conversely, projecting each split term back to a pair-closed endpoint erases the only nonzero term in the cycle example. This pins the gap to an exact-marginal defect-register/repair construction, not to the pointwise Plücker algebra.

The exact path above is encouraging only in a restricted sense: for a permutation matrix the mismatch can be traversed with unit determinant products. For a general rational-complex matrix, the Plücker sum guarantees a nonzero split neighbor and a squared-mass aggregate bound with factor d, but it neither selects a polynomially congested path nor proves connectivity of every fixed-union/intersection fiber. The ordinary matroid basis-pair symmetric-exchange graph is precisely a global issue, not a consequence of nonzero degree. A 2026 binary-matroid counterexample demonstrates the danger for arbitrary matroids; it does not transfer by the tested rational lift. The regular-matroid proof is narrower and does not cover arbitrary rational-complex representations.

Primary literature handles for that scope check:
- Larson, “Counterexamples to two conjectures about matroids,” arXiv:2607.02208v1 (2 July 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02208; Section 1 defines symmetric exchanges and gives a rank-9 binary counterexample (Theorem 1.5, lines 72–101 of the HTML). This is a warning about matroid-level inference, not a counterexample in the present complex-rational class.
- Bérczi, Mátravölgyi, Schwarcz, “Reconfiguration of basis pairs in regular matroids,” arXiv:2311.07130, https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07130; the proven class is regular matroids. It cannot be silently generalized to every rational representation.

NEXT DECISIVE GATE

Construct a joint law on (paired endpoint, split/mismatch register) with exactly the desired pair-basis marginal, computable conditional normalizers, and a polynomial Poincaré/congestion bound. The one-swap lift is too small: its heat-bath endpoint fiber only repairs the two split pairs and reduces to a single paired exchange, which vanishes on the cycle family. A longer-register scheme must permit 2q-scale repairs while avoiding exponentially many endpoint preimages or an unknown aggregate coefficient. No such scheme has been proved here.
