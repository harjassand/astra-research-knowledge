Claim: For the two native backbone carbon indicators, let p=E(X_1+X_2)/2, c=P(X_1=X_2=1), r=P_1/(2P_0) and t=P_2/P_0. The source gives the exact inversion p=(r+t)/(1+2r+t), c=t/(1+2r+t); thus the singly substituted ratio alone does not identify mean heavy-carbon abundance. Under a source-mixture model where molecules form independently within reservoirs with heavy-carbon fraction P, c-p^2=Var(P). If every source lies in [a,b], Var(P)<=(p-a)(b-p), a sharp endpoint bound. The report's equal-marginal correlated benchmark predicts P_2/P_0 23.71 times its independent-backbone alternative for Bennu and 6.18 times for Murchison; these are conditional native-isotope predictions, not observed peaks. For the stated Bennu organic-material interval, the source reports a maximum covariance 134.28 times below its benchmark requirement, excluding that bounded source class under the chosen interface.

The source uses the displayed VPDB-ratio-to-probability interface, but applying that correction to this pair model is unverified. A derivatization reagent adds carbon, so native double-substitution ratios require the stated generating-polynomial deconvolution. Two 13C substitutions and one 18O substitution are separated by only 0.00246468 Da; calibrated line shapes, standards and integration are needed.
Proof spine: Express P0=1-2p+c, P1=2(p-c), P2=c and solve the ratio definitions for p,c. In a reservoir mixture c=E[P^2], p=E[P], so covariance equals Var(P); the pointwise inequality (P-a)(b-P)>=0 gives the sharp interval bound. Independent-position unequal marginals alone move r in the opposite direction. The source reports additional LP and generated-distribution checks, not replayed by intake.
