UNIFORM OPEN-XY CRITICAL GIBBS EXPERIMENT: PROOF
Status: internally reconstructed mathematical proof; not external validation.
Date: 2026-10-08. All trace norms below are the FULL Schatten 1-norm.

1. MODEL, EMBEDDING, AND EXACT FINITE MATRICES

Fix J,tau>0 and B,K<infinity. Let L=N+1, lambda in [-B,B],
kappa in [-K,K], beta=tau L^2, gamma=kappa/L, n_i=(1-Z_i)/2,

 H_L = -J sum_{i=1}^{N-1}[(1+gamma)X_iX_{i+1}
                         +(1-gamma)Y_iY_{i+1}]
       +(4J-lambda/L^2) sum_i n_i.

Jordan-Wigner is a basis identification for an OPEN chain: no parity-dependent
boundary term occurs. Apply the known sine transform
phi_k(i)=sqrt(2/L) sin(pi k i/L), 1<=i,k<L, to fermionic annihilators.
Embed the resulting N-mode Fock space in F(ell^2(N)) with modes k>=L vacuum.
The embedding is independent of lambda and kappa.

The scaled Hamiltonian G_L=beta H_L is

 sum_k A_Lk d_k^* d_k
 + sum_{k<l} K_Lkl(d_k^*d_l^* + d_l d_k),

where
 D_Lk=4 tau J L^2(1-cos(pi k/L)), A_Lk=D_Lk-tau lambda,
 K_L=-2 tau J kappa L B_L,
 B_Lkl=0 if k+l even,
 B_Lkl=-(4/L) sin(pi k/L)sin(pi l/L)
                  /(cos(pi k/L)-cos(pi l/L)) otherwise.

B_L is the sine-basis representation of S-S^*, with S the one-step shift
with Dirichlet endpoints. K_L is real antisymmetric. In the physical/site
spin basis the N=2 even-sector off-diagonal is -2J gamma. The sine-mode
pair coefficient is instead +2J gamma: B_12=-1 and the sine transform has
determinant -1. The factor of two in the XX-YY matrix element is already
included; it must not be inserted a second time.

The self-adjoint 2N by 2N Bogoliubov matrix is

 h_L = [ A_L   K_L ; -K_L  -A_L ].

Its Gibbs generalized covariance is C_L=f(h_L), f(s)=(1+exp(s))^{-1}.
In particular Q_L=(C_L)_{++} satisfies (Q_L)kk=Tr(rho_L d_k^*d_k).
Other covariance conventions conjugate/transposes the anomalous block;
the bounds and nonvanishing statements below do not depend on its sign.

2. THE CRUCIAL UNIFORM OCCUPATION ESTIMATE

For q_k=pi k/L one has exactly

 ||K_L e_k||^2=16 tau^2 J^2 kappa^2 L^2 sin^2(q_k)(1-2/L)
             <=16 tau^2 J^2 kappa^2 pi^2 k^2.                 (2.1)

Indeed (S-S^*)phi_k(i)=2 sin(q_k)sqrt(2/L)cos(q_k i), and
sum_{i=1}^{L-1}cos^2(q_k i)=(L-2)/2. At N=1 the factor is zero.

For any self-adjoint h, unit vector u, a>0, and residual r=(h-a)u,
the spectral measure nu of u obeys

 nu((-infinity,a/2]) <=4||r||^2/a^2.

Since f(s)<=1 everywhere and f(s)<=exp(-a/2) for s>=a/2,

 <u,f(h)u> <=4||r||^2/a^2+exp(-a/2).                     (2.2)

Take u=(e_k,0), a=A_Lk. Its residual is (0,-K_L e_k), exactly.
The elementary inequality 1-cos(pi k/L)>=2k^2/L^2 gives
D_Lk>=8 tau J k^2. Set k0=max(1,ceil(sqrt(B/(4J)))). For k>=k0,
A_Lk>=4 tau J k^2, hence

 (Q_L)kk <=4 pi^2 kappa^2/k^2 + exp(-2 tau J k^2).          (2.3)

Thus for every M>=max(1,k0-1), every finite N, and every allowed parameter,

 Tr rho_L sum_{k>M} n_k <= T_M
 :=4 pi^2 K^2/M + sum_{k>M} exp(-2 tau J k^2),              (2.4)

with the first term omitted when K=0. For M>=1, sum_{k>M}k^{-2}<=1/M.
An explicit upper bound for the second term is

 exp[-2 tau J(M+1)^2]/[1-exp(-2 tau J(2M+3))].              (2.5)

Modes above N in the actual embedded finite state are vacuum. Notice that
(2.2) controls a NORMALIZED state directly. Hilbert-Schmidt pairing alone,
convergence of individual modes, or a scalar energy subtraction would not
have supplied (2.4). No estimate of partition functions was needed here.

3. SELF-ADJOINT CONTINUUM BOGOLIUBOV OPERATOR

Identify ell^2(N) with L^2(0,1) using phi_k(x)=sqrt(2)sin(pi kx). Put

 D=2 tau J(-partial_x^2), domain H^2(0,1) intersect H_0^1(0,1),
 A=D-tau lambda,
 K=-4 tau J kappa partial_x,
 h=[A,K;-K,-A], domain Dom(D) direct-sum Dom(D).

K is skew symmetric on H_0^1. On this domain

 ||K u||^2=8 tau J kappa^2 <u,D u>.

The derivative matrix is <phi_k,partial_x phi_l>=4kl/(k^2-l^2)
for k+l odd and zero otherwise. Thus

 K_kl=-16 tau J kappa kl/(k^2-l^2), k+l odd.               (3.1)

K itself is unbounded and is not Hilbert-Schmidt.
It is infinitesimally D-bounded. Therefore h is self-adjoint by the
relative-bound perturbation theorem. Finite sine spans are a common core.

For finite L extend D_L diagonally to all modes using D_k for k>=L,
and extend K_L by zero outside the first N modes. Call the corresponding
full-space self-adjoint operator h_L^ext. Uniformly in L,

 D_L>=8 tau J diag(k^2),
 ||K_L u||^2<=8 tau J kappa^2 <u,D_L u>.                  (3.2)

For the last inequality write T=2I-S-S^*. Since
S-S^*=(S-I)+(I-S^*),
|| (S-S^*)u ||^2<=4<u,T u>, and D_L=2 tau J L^2 T.
The same infinitesimal relative bound gives self-adjointness of h_L^ext.

4. FINITE-COLUMN AND UNIFORM RESOLVENT CONVERGENCE

For fixed k, D_Lk ->2 tau J pi^2 k^2. Each fixed matrix entry K_Llk
converges to (3.1). The exact norms in (2.1) converge to
||K e_k||^2=16 tau^2 J^2 kappa^2 pi^2 k^2.
Entrywise convergence and bounded norms imply weak column convergence;
equality of the limiting norms upgrades it to strong column convergence.
Consequently h_L^ext v ->h v on every finite sine span. Convergence on a
core for self-adjoint operators implies strong resolvent convergence.

Here and below this standard core implication also follows directly:
for v=(h-i)u with u in the core,
(h_L^ext-i)^{-1}v-u=(h_L^ext-i)^{-1}(h-h_L^ext)u ->0;
these v are dense and all resolvents have norm<=1.

The convergence is uniform on compact lambda,kappa sets for each vector.
Details, rather than an assumption of pointwise-to-uniform promotion:
from (3.2), for b=sqrt(8 tau J)K,

 ||diag(D_L,D_L)u||
 <=||h_L^ext u||+tau B||u||+b||diag(D_L,D_L)^{1/2}u||
 <=||h_L^ext u||+(1/2)||diag(D_L,D_L)u||+C||u||.

The interpolation step uses sqrt(xy)<=eta x+y/(4eta).
Thus the graph norm of diag(D_L,D_L)(h_L^ext-i)^{-1} is uniformly bounded.
The resolvent identity gives

 ||R_L(lambda,kappa)-R_L(lambda',kappa')||
 <=C(|lambda-lambda'|+|kappa-kappa'|),                    (4.1)

because the parameter difference is tau(lambda-lambda') on the diagonal
and (kappa-kappa')K_L^unit off diagonal, and K_L^unit R_L is uniformly
bounded by (3.2) and the graph estimate. The continuum obeys the same bound.
A finite parameter net, pointwise strong convergence, and (4.1) prove the
claimed uniform strong convergence. There is also uniform compactness of
the resolvents from the graph bound and D_L>=c diag(k^2), but it is not
needed for the state theorem.

For f(h_L^ext), handle the distinct limits f(-infinity)=1,f(+infinity)=0
explicitly. Cut f off outside [-R,R] to a C_0 function f_R. Resolvent
convergence yields f_R(h_L^ext) strong convergence, uniformly on compact
parameters. On a fixed finite sine vector v, the spectral tail norm is
<=||h_L^ext v||/R, uniformly bounded. This proves uniform convergence of
all fixed finite-block entries of C_L^ext=f(h_L^ext).

The extension only adds independent free thermal modes k>=L. Their total
occupation is at most exp(tau B)sum_{k>=L}exp(-2 tau J pi^2 k^2).
Replacing this independent tail by vacuum changes the state in trace norm
by at most twice this sum. Equivalently, all fixed physical covariance
blocks agree for L larger than the block. The extension is a proof device,
not an uncharged physical tail.

5. CONSTRUCTION OF THE NORMAL FOCK LIMIT AND TRACE-NORM CONVERGENCE

C=f(h) is a valid particle-hole-symmetric covariance, defining its even
quasi-free CAR state. Applying the same residual estimate to h gives
Tr Q<=infinity and the continuum analogue of (2.4).

One can establish normality directly without assuming implementability:
let rho^(M) be the M-mode state determined by the compressed covariance C,
and embed it with all higher modes vacuum. Finite reductions are consistent
by Wick's rule. If P_M projects onto vacuum above mode M, then for any state
rho with high-mode occupation t,

 Tr rho(1-P_M)<=t,
 ||rho-P_M rho P_M||_1<=2 sqrt(t),
 ||rho-(Tr_{>M}rho) tensor |vac><vac| ||_1<=2 sqrt(t)+t.   (5.1)

For completeness, purify rho and project its purification by P_M: the
rank-two pure-state difference has norm sqrt(t(4-3t))<=2sqrt(t), and partial
trace contracts the norm. The reduced state differs from P_M rho P_M by a
positive operator of trace t, proving the last bound. Ordered Fock modes
give the indicated tensor identification; all states are even, so fermionic
grading produces no ambiguity for this operation.

Using t<=T_M in larger finite reductions shows rho^(M) is Cauchy in trace
norm. Its limit is a density operator rho(lambda,kappa) on the original
vacuum Fock representation with covariance C. Wick moments identify it
uniquely as the continuum quasi-free Gibbs state. Equation (5.1) applies
to this state with the same T_M.

On fixed M modes, the density matrix is a finite linear combination of
Majorana monomials whose coefficients are Pfaffians of covariance subblocks
(Wick's rule). Hence covariance convergence implies finite-state trace-norm
convergence, uniformly in the parameters. Combining this with (5.1),

 sup_{|lambda|<=B,|kappa|<=K}||rho_L-rho||_1
 <=4 sqrt(T_M)+2T_M
    +sup_params ||rho_L^(M)-rho^(M)||_1 ->0              (5.2)

first as L->infinity with M fixed, then as M->infinity. This is convergence
of the WHOLE experiment in a common parameter-blind Fock embedding. It
does not assert a finite-L rate from mere core convergence.

6. UV SCALAR ENERGY AND THE ACTUAL FOCK REPRESENTATION

Let epsilon_Lj>=0 be the positive members of the particle-hole-paired
spectrum of h_L. The exact scaled many-body ground energy is

 E0_L=(Tr A_L-sum_j epsilon_Lj)/2.

After scalar subtraction, G_L-E0_L=sum_j epsilon_Lj b_j^*b_j and
Zren_L=prod_j(1+exp(-epsilon_Lj)). The scalar subtraction leaves rho_L
unchanged. Raw formal pair creation in (3.1) does not act on the bare
vacuum as a Fock vector because sum_{k<l}|K_kl|^2 diverges. Consequently
one must not claim convergence of the unrenormalized Hamiltonian on the
finite-particle vacuum core. The theorem proves state convergence instead.

At lambda=0 an explicit bound shows the UV energy is at most linear:

 -tau J kappa^2 N(1-2/L) <=E0_L<=0.                     (6.1)

To prove it, pinch h_L^2 into its two diagonal blocks A_L^2+K_LK_L^*.
Concavity of Tr sqrt under pinching gives sum epsilon_Lj<=
Tr sqrt(A_L^2+K_LK_L^*). The tangent inequality for this concave trace
function bounds the last quantity by Tr A_L+(1/2)Tr A_L^{-1}K_LK_L^*.
Finally (2.1) and sum_k cos(pi k/L)=0 give
Tr A_L^{-1}K_LK_L^*=4 tau J kappa^2 N(1-2/L).

For fixed kappa !=0 the divergence is genuinely linear, not just an upper
bound: pair disjoint neighboring modes k,k+1 with q_k in [pi/3,2pi/3].
There are cL such pairs. On each pair the variational BCS ground energy is
alpha-sqrt(alpha^2+|K_L,k,k+1|^2), alpha=(A_Lk+A_L,k+1)/2.
In this range |K_L,k,k+1|>=c1 tau J |kappa|L and
alpha<=c2 tau J L^2; hence, for bounded kappa and sufficiently large L,
each pair lowers the energy by at least c3 tau J kappa^2.
The product of these pair states has zero cross-pair quadratic expectations.
Thus E0_L<=-c4 tau J kappa^2 L. These constants may be chosen uniformly
away from kappa=0. Compact lambda changes E0_L by only O(1): a ground-state
occupation uses the spectral set s<=0 in (2.2), giving the improved tail
Qground,kk<=pi^2 K^2/k^2 beyond k0. The variational principle in both
directions bounds the lambda difference by tau B times this uniformly
bounded total ground occupation. This argument includes zero modes by
bounding their covariance by the spectral projection onto s<=0.

The negative-energy projection of the continuum h has finite particle
trace by the same ground bound. Its off-diagonal change of polarization
is therefore Hilbert-Schmidt. This supplies the fermionic implementability
condition in the original vacuum Fock representation. The graph estimate
also gives eigenvalue counting #(|h|<=R)<=C sqrt(R+1): a spectral subspace
larger than the low-D subspace would contain a vector with large D-norm,
contradicting the graph estimate. Thus sum_j exp(-epsilon_j)<infinity.
Diagonalization in the implementable representation gives a positive
renormalized Fock Hamiltonian with these quasiparticle energies, finite
thermal partition function, and the density operator constructed in §5.
If zero energies occur, their finite-dimensional Gibbs block is maximally
mixed. No choice of a discontinuous zero-mode ground polarization affects
the finite-temperature state. Standard CAR implementability is an
identified background theorem; the direct normal-density construction in
§5 does not depend on accepting a formal second-quantized series.

Physical versus scaled energy: beta=tau L^2, so a linear scaled shift is
an O(kappa^2/L) physical ground-energy shift. The low-temperature resource
and detuning/anisotropy precision are not constant-size resources.

7. PARAMETER-BLIND QUANTUM ARCHIVE, WITH AN IMPROVED NUMBER CUTOFF

The state is supplied once, with lambda,kappa UNKNOWN. Public data are
N,J,tau,B,K, the sine transform, and requested norm error epsilon in (0,1).
Keeping the first M modes and preparing vacuum in discarded modes gives
error <=2sqrt(T_M)+T_M. This already proves quantum memory independent of N,
with M=O(K^2/epsilon^2+sqrt(log(1/epsilon))) for fixed public data.

A stronger archive also truncates TOTAL bare particle number. For any
finite-mode fermionic Gaussian state with mu=Tr Q and s>=0,

 Tr rho exp(s Nbare) <=exp[mu(exp(2s)-1)].                (7.1)

Proof: in Majorana convention let Gamma be the real skew covariance. Put
S=sqrt(I+Gamma^2). The doubled covariance [Gamma,S;-S,-Gamma] is real skew
and squares to -I, so it is a pure Gaussian purification. The ancillary
block -Gamma has expected bare number M-mu. Particle-hole conjugate every
ancillary mode, a Bogoliubov operation, to make its expected number mu.
This gives a pure Gaussian purification with expected TOTAL number 2mu.
(A Gaussian thermofield purification gives the same result with suitable
graded signs.) A pure Gaussian
state has, after a particle-number-preserving one-particle rotation, a
product of two-mode BCS blocks and possibly filled blocked modes. A BCS
block with probability p has total-number generating function
1+p(exp(2s)-1)<=exp[p(exp(2s)-1)]; a filled mode obeys
exp(s)<=exp[(exp(2s)-1)/2]. Thus a pure Gaussian state's generating function
is bounded by exp[(expected number)(exp(2s)-1)/2]. Physical number is no
larger than total number, yielding (7.1). Finite approximations and monotone
convergence extend it to the normal continuum state.

A uniform finite mean bound from (2.3) is
mu<=mu_*:=k0+(2/3)pi^4 K^2+sum_{k>=1}exp(-2 tau J k^2).
Chernoff optimization gives, for r>=2mu_*,

 Pr(Nbare>=r)<=exp[-(r/2)log(r/(2e mu_*))-mu_*].           (7.2)

Let P be the fixed subspace: no occupation above M and at most r particles
in the first M modes. Then Tr rho(1-P)<=T_M+Pr(Nbare>r).
Encode P isometrically into a register and send its complement to an
overflow symbol; decode P exactly and overflow to vacuum. The reconstruction
error is <=2sqrt(p_bad)+p_bad<=3sqrt(p_bad).
Choose T_M<=epsilon^2/18 and the bound for Pr(Nbare>r)<=epsilon^2/18.
The memory dimension and qubit count are

 Darchive=1+sum_{j=0}^{min(r,M)} binom(M,j),
 qarchive=ceil(log_2 Darchive).

For fixed nonzero K and fixed B,J,tau as epsilon->0,
M=O(epsilon^{-2}), r=O(log(1/epsilon)/log log(1/epsilon)), and
qarchive=O((log(1/epsilon))^2/log log(1/epsilon)).
This is an upper bound, not an optimal-memory theorem. Small N may simply
be retained exactly. The number cutoff is coherent: measuring the exact
number and throwing away its coherence is not this channel.

Encoding/decoding cost: the sine-mode transformation can be implemented
with O(N^2) adjacent fermionic Givens/matchgates, followed by reversible
particle counting and combinatorial ranking/unranking on M modes. The
binomial table has O(Mr) entries, each O(r log M) bits; straightforward
reversible arithmetic has a polynomial cost in M,r,log M. This is an
explicit finite algorithm, with conservatively O(N^2)+poly(M,r,log M)
gates each direction and at least O(N) output work. A synthesis of g gates
to operator-norm error O(epsilon/g) needs O(log(g/epsilon)) angle bits
and a gate-library-dependent synthesis overhead. No fast Fourier or free
decoder is assumed. Register workspace, the public transform/table,
ancillas, and output N spins are separate from persistent archive memory.

An equally informed decoder supplied lambda,kappa could reprepare its
Gaussian Gibbs state and omit an archive, but acquiring those parameters
from a single unknown copy is not granted by this interface. A classical
covariance description requires sample acquisition if not supplied.
Thermal-state production at beta=tau L^2 is not proved efficient here.

Energy qualification: trace-norm reconstruction controls bounded
observables; it does not preserve the scaled UV energy. A reconstructed
state with all modes above a fixed M vacuum has a bounded expectation of
G_L as L grows, since its finite mode block converges. But E0_L is negative
linear in L for fixed kappa!=0. Its renormalized energy expectation
Tr reconstructed_state (G_L-E0_L) consequently diverges at least linearly
in L. The true Gibbs state's renormalized energy sum_j epsilon_Lj f(epsilon_Lj)
is uniformly bounded by the uniform eigenvalue-counting estimate. Thus this
archive is NOT a bounded-renormalized-energy reconstruction theorem. In
physical units the same O(L) scaled energy discrepancy is O(1/L), because
beta=tau L^2. Nor does the proof assign free computational resources to an
infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian. A parameter-aware Gaussian preparation
baseline has O(N^3) dense diagonalization and O(N^2) Gaussian circuit work,
with O(N^2) numerical storage and charged parameter/angle precision.

8. REUSABLE QUADRATIC-EXPERIMENT THEOREM AND ITS SHARP UV BOUNDARY

The proof is reusable beyond this chain. Suppose finite quasi-free Gibbs
experiments share a parameter-independent one-particle basis and embedding,
their self-adjoint BdG operators converge on a common core uniformly on
compact parameter sets as in §4, and for top-mode vectors u_k

 a_nk>=c omega_k-C,
 ||(h_n-a_nk)u_k||^2<=R_k,
 sum_k R_k/omega_k^2<infinity,   sum_k exp(-c omega_k/2)<infinity.

The residual split proves a summable particle tail; finite covariance
blocks plus (5.1) prove uniform whole-Fock trace-norm convergence to the
normal quasi-free state. The same particle and number cutoffs give public
quantum archives. Scalar UV energy shifts have no effect on this theorem.
The exact mathematical inputs are stated; matrix-element convergence alone
cannot replace common-core/resolvent convergence, and an HS relative kernel
without a state tail cannot replace the displayed summability condition.

For d-dimensional elliptic modes with diagonal order z and pairing order r,
Weyl scaling omega_k~k^(z/d), R_k=O(k^(2r/d)) makes the sufficient threshold

                         2(z-r)>d.

The threshold is sharp for this class: take independent two-mode blocks
H_j=a_j(n_{2j-1}+n_{2j})+b_j(c_{2j-1}^*c_{2j}^*+c_{2j}c_{2j-1}),
a_j=j^(z/d), b_j=eta j^(r/d), 0<=r<z. Their high-energy ground pair
occupation probability is
p_j=[1-a_j/sqrt(a_j^2+b_j^2)]/2~b_j^2/(4a_j^2).
At fixed positive inverse temperature the thermal correction is summable
and exponentially smaller. If 2(z-r)<=d, sum_j p_j diverges. For every
fixed cutoff m the probability that all modes j>m are vacuum tends to zero
as the finite block count tends to infinity. A vacuum-tail projection then
distinguishes this sequence from every fixed-cutoff density with norm
approaching 2. The sequence is not trace-norm Cauchy in the vacuum Fock
representation. Scalar vacuum-energy subtraction cannot repair it.
This is a rigorous boundary example, not a theorem about every 2D lattice.

9. VALIDATION SCOPE AND OPEN GATES

The argument establishes an internal mathematical proof, including the
missing global topology, rather than inferring trace convergence from an
HS heuristic. Finite computations separately check conventions, residual
constants, Gibbs covariance, and archive tails. They do not validate the
infinite theorem. External expert verification and a complete priority
search remain open. No general interacting spin limit, non-Gaussian state
class, constant-time thermal preparation, or optimal archive claim follows.

The EB/classical reconstruction obstruction is a separate proof in
eb_obstruction.txt; noncommuting reduced covariances alone are not enough
to certify that obstruction.
