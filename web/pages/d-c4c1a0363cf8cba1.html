GAMES/HAMILTONIANS — CYCLE 3 HIGH-CEILING ROUTE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the expander-clock identity and factorwise-decoder obstruction; SOURCE-REPORTED for BMVZ, BJSW, Lin, and BNZ statements. No full positive sparse encoding of the augmented BMVZ list was obtained.

DECISION

A constant-gap expander clock has an exact common-isometry construction for the whole finite BMVZ term list. The construction would meet the common positive-encoding equations with a diagonal 1-sparse B and an all-state enforcer C of constant spectral gap if every clock-edge relative decoder had polynomial row support. For the hardness-preserving BMVZ augmentation containing the two BJSW singleton colors, this condition fails throughout the factorwise-across-repetition-copies decoder class. The obstruction permits arbitrary vertex-dependent and non-Clifford decoders inside each copy: it uses only the noncommutation of the two seed projectors. A Poincare argument on the decoder-frame metric forces an expander-clock edge with Omega(R) incompatible copy factors, hence at least 2^Omega(R) nonzeros in a transition row. This differs from the earlier path-kernel bound: it permits arbitrary intermediate clock frames and uses the two forced endpoint frame classes.

This is not a no-go for global decoders that mix all R copies. In fact, the cosine-sine decomposition for two projections gives pairwise diagonalizing bases with at most two nonzeros per transition row, so the factorwise hypothesis is essential. Whether one can choose such bases compatibly and efficiently for the full polynomial BMVZ list is UNKNOWN. Thus no cost-qualified full construction or unrestricted expander-clock impossibility is claimed.

1. THE EXACT EXPANDER-CLOCK CANDIDATE

Let the full augmented output be an explicitly generated positive list

    H = sum_{j=1}^M w_j h_j,       h_j = U_j^* D_j U_j,

where each D_j is a diagonal projection. For BMVZ, flattening an iterated clause gives R=poly(n) fixed-local projection factors; a factorwise decoder U_j is a product of the corresponding constant-local diagonalizers, and D_j is the diagonal OR predicate. The generated list has M=poly(n) terms and rational weights. Replicate label j exactly q_j=K w_j times, where K is a common denominator; for the BMVZ constant-layer generator K can be chosen polynomial because the number of layers is constant and the per-layer path counts and denominators are polynomial. Write j(a) for the term at clock vertex a in [K]. Then every label has q_j copies and the clock law is uniform.

Choose any explicit Delta-regular graph Gamma on K vertices with normalized adjacency P=A/Delta and spectral gap delta_Gamma=1-lambda_2(P)>0 constant. Put G=direct_sum_a U_{j(a)} and define

    V |psi> = K^(-1/2) sum_a |a> U_{j(a)} |psi>,
    B = direct_sum_a D_{j(a)},
    C = (1/2) G ((I-P) tensor I_data) G^*.

These are exact identities:

    V^*V=I,
    0<=B<=I, B is diagonal and 1-row-sparse,
    V^* B V = (1/K) sum_a h_{j(a)} = H,
    ker C = ran(V),
    C >= (delta_Gamma/2)(I-VV^*),  and 0<=C<=I.

The last two equations follow by conjugating C with G: it is one half the normalized graph Laplacian tensored with identity. Thus this candidate has compression constants alpha=beta=1 and invalid-space gap delta_Gamma/2, independent of K. In block form, C_aa=(1/2)I and, for an edge a~b,

    C_ab = -(1/(2 Delta)) U_{j(a)} U_{j(b)}^*;

all other off-diagonal clock blocks vanish. If s_edge is the maximum row support of these relative unitaries, then every C row has at most 1+Delta*s_edge nonzeros. This is the entire row-access bottleneck.

Cost ledger if s_edge were polynomial:

* K and M are polynomial; the clock uses ceil(log_2 K) qubits plus a valid-label check if K is padded to a power of two. Invalid padded labels can receive an identity penalty, preserving the same kernel and taking the minimum gap with 1.
* An explicit regular expander gives a neighbor in O(1) time after reading a vertex and O(log K) random bits. Sampling a uniform clock vertex and a uniform neighbor is polynomial-time; it is an acquired clock graph, unlike a claim inferred from an HDX routing graph.
* A diagonal B entry is evaluated from the BMVZ clause label and its R local violation predicates in O(R poly(n)) time; enumeration is one entry per row. The controlled decoder V has a polynomial circuit when each U_j is given by its factorwise local decoder and the term generator is polynomial. Uniform-state preparation costs O(log K) gates, or a stated polynomial-precision synthesis if the gate set does not allow exact 1/sqrt(K).
* An edge entry of C is the corresponding entry of U_{j(a)}U_{j(b)}^* divided by -2Delta. For a product decoder its value is a product of R local entries, so a specified entry takes O(R) local entry queries and polynomial bit precision. Listing every nonzero in that row costs Omega(s_edge); a fast entry oracle does not replace row enumeration.
* C is not constant-local in general. Its edge blocks act on the clock and the union of the decoder supports, potentially all R data copies. B is diagonal but can depend on all R copies. Constant row sparsity and constant qubit locality are separate conditions.

BMVZ's source gives the layered/path clause and iterated-list interface (Definitions 1.6-1.9 and Theorem 7.3); its terms are readily measurable, but the paper explicitly leaves sparse-matrix conversion open. The construction above changes that issue to an exact relative-transition support problem, rather than asserting that the path-label expander itself solves it.

2. LONG-EDGE SUPPORT WITH ARBITRARY PER-COPY DECODERS

Use the hardness-preserving augmented input from the prior cycle: scale the original QMA-hard layer weights by 1/2 and append two BJSW five-qubit rank-one projectors on a fresh block as singleton colors of weight 1/4 each. The added block has a common orthogonal complement, so the promise is shifted/scaled without losing hardness. BMVZ's singleton rule emits, after R repetitions, the two terms

    Q_phi,R = I - (I-P_phi)^{tensor R},
    Q_psi,R = I - (I-P_psi)^{tensor R},

with clock masses exactly 1/4 each. The seed kets are

    phi_5 = |-> |00> |10>,
    psi_5 = (|000>-|011>-|101>-|110>)/2 tensor |10>,
    |<phi_5,psi_5>| = 1/(2 sqrt(2)).

Their rank-one projections do not commute: two rank-one projections commute only when their ranges are equal or orthogonal, while this overlap is strictly between zero and one.

Define the expander-clock subclass precisely as follows. Every branch/vertex decoder factors across the R original BMVZ copies,

    U_a = tensor_{r=1}^R U_{a,r},

but U_{a,r} may be any unitary on the entire single-copy data space, may depend arbitrarily on the clock vertex a, and need not be Clifford or local. The block D_a is diagonal and U_a h_{j(a)} U_a^*=D_a. For the two seed vertices, factorization and diagonality of Q_v,R imply that each U_{a,r} diagonalizes P_v tensor I on that copy: I-D_a is a tensor product of the conjugated complements, and a tensor product of nonzero projections is diagonal only if every factor is diagonal.

For two vertices a,b define

    d(a,b) = #{r : U_{a,r} U_{b,r}^* is non-monomial},

where monomial means one nonzero per row and column. This is a metric on the product of per-copy monomial-equivalence classes. If the local relative unitary at a copy were monomial, it would map diagonal projections to diagonal projections; the two decoded seed projectors would then be simultaneously diagonal in one basis and commute. Therefore every phi-branch decoder and every psi-branch decoder differ non-monomially in all R copies:

    d(a,b)=R  for every a in Phi and b in Psi.

For any unitary W, non-monomiality implies max_row_nnz(W)>=2. Tensor-product row supports multiply, so

    max_row_nnz(U_a U_b^*) = product_r max_row_nnz(U_{a,r}U_{b,r}^*) >= 2^{d(a,b)}.

This lower bound is decoder-independent within the stated subclass. It applies to vertex-dependent choices and arbitrary non-Clifford changes of basis on a whole copy, not just the source's Clifford frames.

Now let Gamma be any Delta-regular reversible clock graph on these K vertices, with the two seed classes each having stationary mass 1/4. Let q=max_{a~b} d(a,b), and take the 1-Lipschitz function

    F(a)=dist_d(a,Phi),

where Phi is the set of all phi-branch vertices. Then F=0 on Phi and F=R on Psi, so Var(F)>=pi(Phi)pi(Psi)R^2=R^2/16. Every graph edge changes F by at most q, hence its random-walk Dirichlet form is at most q^2/2. The variational formula gives

    gap(P) <= 8 q^2/R^2,
    gap(C) = gap(P)/2 <= 4 q^2/R^2.

If C has maximum row support s, every edge block appears in a distinct clock-column block, hence max_row_nnz(U_a U_b^*)<=s and q<=log_2 s. Consequently

    gap(C) <= 4 (log_2 s)^2/R^2.

A constant invalid-space gap gamma therefore requires

    s >= 2^(R sqrt(gamma)/2).

Since R is polynomial and grows with the BMVZ amplification parameter, a polynomial-row C cannot have a size-independent gap anywhere in this expander-clock subclass, regardless of how the constant-degree expander edges are chosen. The obstruction allows arbitrary intermediate frames: the distance-to-Phi function forces a long edge in the frame metric. It is not the earlier theorem for reversible kernels changing q positions of the BMVZ path label, and it is not Sol's fixed-image Chebyshev bound for every positive sparse operator.

3. EXACT ACCESS FIGURES AND SCOPE OF THE DECODER BOUND

For the source's chosen BJSW Clifford preparation decoders, exact Q(sqrt(2)) multiplication gives 8 nonzeros per row for a one-copy relative matrix, each with squared magnitude 1/8. A long edge differing on h such seed copies has exactly 8^h nonzeros per row, each with squared magnitude 8^{-h}. Its entry oracle is a product of h constant-size exact factors; row listing takes Theta(8^h). In the vertex-independent seed-to-seed edge, h=R and support is exactly 8^R.

For arbitrary per-copy non-Clifford decoders, the exact row count may vary from 2 up to the single-copy dimension, but the rigorous lower bound remains 2^h. An entry is still computable in O(R) arithmetic if each local decoder has a supplied polynomial-time entry oracle and a coefficient-precision bound; row listing costs at least 2^h. A circuit description by itself does not guarantee a polynomial-time amplitude or row-enumeration oracle for a general non-Clifford circuit.

The boundary is real. For two arbitrary projections A and B, the cosine-sine decomposition gives orthonormal bases in which the relative basis-change matrix is a direct sum of 1-by-1 and 2-by-2 blocks. Thus a global decoder of Q_phi,R and a global decoder of Q_psi,R can have at most two nonzeros per row, even though the factorwise Clifford transition has 8^R. This finite-dimensional fact blocks any extension of the preceding support lower bound to global cross-copy decoders. The CS bases' polynomial circuit/entry access and a single compatible choice for every vertex of the full BMVZ graph are not established. The missing theorem is now concrete: either construct those global bases with polynomial circuits and polynomial-row edge transitions for every edge of one explicit expander on the full list, or prove a multi-projection incompatibility theorem that rules them out.

4. NORMALIZED ENERGY, HDX SOUNDNESS, AND THE NAMED GAME ANCHOR

Keep three gaps separate:

* BMVZ's final no-instance promise is an ordinary normalized expectation-energy statement for H=sum_j w_j h_j. The expander-clock identity would preserve it exactly on the encoded image: V^*BV=H. The clock gap is the separate invalid-image penalty gamma in C. Neither gap implies the other without both displayed operator inequalities.
* BNZ's HDX/qLTC result is a combinatorial support guarantee, not an ordinary energy lower bound. If H=sum_j p_j Pi_j, its combinatorial quantity is sum_j p_j 1{Tr(Pi_j rho)>0}, while expectation is sum_j p_j Tr(Pi_j rho); only E<=C holds in general. An exact one-qubit family P=|1><1| and Q_r=|v_r><v_r|, |v_r>=(r|0>+|1>)/sqrt(1+r^2), has combinatorial violation exactly 1/2 for every state but lambda_min((P+Q_r)/2)=(1-1/sqrt(1+r^2))/2<=r^2/4. Taking r=2^-n makes energy exponentially small. Also a normalized bounded-weight qLTC check average has gap at most w/N by a single-site Pauli excitation; constant Hamming soundness gives only the Omega(1/N) code-space gap. Thus neither a constant combinatorial cap nor HDX walk expansion supplies the uniform energy/enforcer inequalities above.
* Lin's named MIPco=coRE verifier can acquire a constant question-resampling gap by one-sided erasure, but that is a game-chain gap and does not create a finite Hamiltonian V,C,B.

For the named verifier, the source-reported interface is a uniform polynomial-time two-prover, one-round commuting verifier with question strings of length x(n), answer strings of length a(n), sampler Q and predicate D, with value 1 on yes instances and at most 1/2 on no instances. Erase Alice with probability 1/4 using two fair bits, keep Bob's y and use the original sampler on the ordinary branch. The transformed law is

    nu(x,y)=3 mu(x,y)/4,
    nu(bottom,y)=mu_Y(y)/4.

On the erased branch accept exactly the bottom answer; otherwise use D. For tensor and commuting models,

    omega(G_one)=1/4+3 omega(G)/4,
    omega_yes=1,  omega_no<=5/8.

Its maximal correlation is rho(nu)=sqrt(3/4) rho(mu), so the support is connected and the pair resampling gap is at least

    sigma >= (2-sqrt(3))/4 > 1/15.

This proof uses no table of the original law and no assumption about its original gap. Per copy the sampler costs s(n)+2 random bits and T_Q(n)+O(1) time, where s,T_Q are its original bit/time costs. Alice's question/answer alphabet grows to 2^x+1 and 2^a+1, Bob's remains 2^x and 2^a; a canonical common-binary-string implementation uses x+1 question bits and a+1 answer bits for both sides. There is no bounded-degree claim for the question-pair support graph; the gap comes from maximal-correlation contraction, not from a constant-degree graph. The one-sided law is asymmetric, and the existing source does not certify that the tagged wrapper remains in Lin's synchronous/conditionally-linear normal form.

Conditional on the inherited L02 commuting threshold theorem, k-fold all-wins soundness on a no instance satisfies

    omega_co(G_one^parallel k) <= exp[-27 k/(7,864,320 (1+ln d_one))],
    d_one=(2^a+1)2^a,

and 1+ln d_one<=2a+2. In the common padded syntax use the conservative bound 2a+3. Target exp(-L) needs k=ceil(7,864,320(2a+3)L/27), which remains polynomial for polynomial a and L. Total random bits are k(s+2); wire lengths and referee time are O(k(x+a)) and O(k[T_Q+T_D+x+a]). The player count and one-round format remain unchanged, but the total test reads k coordinates. Wrapper identities are INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; this repetition consequence is CONDITIONAL on L02, whose proof is not externally/formally validated here. The one-sided anchor is an audited prior design, not a new mechanism.

The anchor transforms the verifier's value and question law. It does not transform the full-list BMVZ Hamiltonian into a sparse positive encoding, and it cannot substitute for the factorwise expander-clock edge oracle. Conversely, the expander-clock formula is a Hamiltonian encoding candidate, not a verifier amplification or a named game theorem. These interfaces should not be conflated.
