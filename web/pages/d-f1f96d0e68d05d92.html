# Reversible two-pulse RNA-control benchmark

## Status and interface

This is an exact theorem for a three-state continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC), not a fitted RNA model. The state sequence is `0 <-> 1 <-> 2`. An input raises the conductance of one edge in both directions:

\[
Q(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}-x&x&0\\x&-x-y&y\\0&y&-y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q_A=Q(k,\epsilon),\quad Q_B=Q(\epsilon,k).
\]

Assume `k, epsilon >= 0`. Each fixed-input generator is symmetric and has exactly the same stationary distribution \(\pi=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\). Start from state 0. In schedule AB, apply A for `u` and then B for `v`; in BA, apply B for `v` and then A for `u`. Read the state distribution immediately at the end of the two pulses. Rates have units of inverse time and `u,v` have units of time.

The shared stationary law is deliberate: the result isolates finite-time path order from a changed endpoint equilibrium. A physical RNA implementation would need two selective transition-state catalysts that accelerate opposite directions of a single local edge equally, without changing state free energies. No such RNA actuator has been identified in the source audit below.

## Exact state-exchange theorem

Let

\[
s=k+\epsilon,\quad \rho=\sqrt{k^2-k\epsilon+\epsilon^2},\quad
\lambda_\pm=s\pm\rho,
\]

and

\[
h(t)=\frac{e^{-\lambda_-t}-e^{-\lambda_+t}}{2\rho}.
\]

For row distributions

\[
p_{AB}=e_0^T e^{Q_Au}e^{Q_Bv},\qquad
p_{BA}=e_0^T e^{Q_Bv}e^{Q_Au},
\]

the exact identity is

\[
p_{AB}-p_{BA}=(0,-\Delta,+\Delta),\qquad
\Delta=(k^2-\epsilon^2)h(u)h(v).
\]

Thus the order swap exchanges probability mass only between states 1 and 2; it leaves state-0 occupancy unchanged. For any common downstream assay whose conditional success probabilities from states 0, 1, 2 are \(r_0,r_1,r_2\), its AB-minus-BA output is exactly

\[
\Delta_{out}=\Delta(r_2-r_1).
\]

This identifies a conditional *competence gap* between states 2 and 1 if the kinetic exchange coefficient \(\Delta\) is separately measured. The downstream assay can include common later dynamics if its conditional success map is stable across the two arms. It cannot include uncontrolled order-dependent post-pulse evolution.

### Derivation

The characteristic polynomial of either generator is \(z(z^2+2sz+3k\epsilon)\), so the eigenvalues are \(0,-\lambda_-,-\lambda_+\). Cayley–Hamilton gives

\[
e^{tQ_A}=I+a(t)Q_A+b(t)Q_A^2,\qquad
e^{tQ_B}=I+a(t)Q_B+b(t)Q_B^2,
\]

where interpolation at the three eigenvalues yields

\[
a(t)-2s b(t)=h(t).
\]

Direct multiplication of the two quadratic polynomials gives

\[
e_0^T\{e^{uQ_A}e^{vQ_B}-e^{vQ_B}e^{uQ_A}\}
=\bigl(0,-(k^2-\epsilon^2)h(u)h(v), +(k^2-\epsilon^2)h(u)h(v)\bigr),
\]

which proves the identity. This is a short matrix-algebra proof, not an external or formal proof-assistant validation. `pulse_order.py` independently checks the identity against uniformization for asymmetric pulse durations, cross-talk below and above the strong edge rate, and the equal-rate zero case.

## Quantitative design consequence

For \(\epsilon>0\), write \(h(t)=e^{-st}\sinh(\rho t)/\rho\). Then

\[
(\log h)''(t)=-\rho^2\,\mathrm{csch}^2(\rho t)<0.
\]

With equal cost per unit time in the two inputs and an *at-most* total pulse-time budget `B`, strict log-concavity makes the optimal durations

\[
u^*=v^*=\min\{B/2,t^*\},\qquad
t^*=\frac{\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-)}{2\rho}.
\]

When `B > 2 t*`, excess actuator-on time should be left unused: both fixed-input CTMCs relax toward the same \(\pi\), erasing the history signal. If `epsilon=0`, `h` is increasing and the at-most budget is used in full. This optimization charges only actuator-on time. It omits the molecular cost of state-0 preparation, catalyst supply, buffer exchange, transcript production, readout, and any work needed to modulate transition barriers.

If actual pulse durations lie independently in \([u_L,u_U]\) and \([v_L,v_U]\), the exact contrast lower bound for `k > epsilon` is

\[
(k^2-\epsilon^2)\min_{[u_L,u_U]}h\;\min_{[v_L,v_U]}h.
\]

Because `h` is unimodal for `epsilon>0`, each interval minimum is attained at an endpoint. For `k=1 s^-1`, `epsilon=0.02 s^-1`, nominal `u=v=1 s`, and independent duration errors at most `0.05 s`, the analytic lower bound is `0.1730300`; the nominal exact contrast is `0.1784119`. These are illustrative parameters, not inferred RNA rates.

For independent paired Bernoulli output runs, `X_i = Y_AB,i - Y_BA,i` lies in `[-1,1]`; Hoeffding gives the one-sided 95% sign-detection sufficient count `n >= 2 log(20)/Delta_min^2`, or 201 independent pairs at the illustrative robust contrast. A two-sided 95% interval with half-width `Delta_min/2` needs 986 pairs. Each pair means two condition-level reactions. Molecules from a shared reaction batch do not automatically count as independent experimental units; batch effects would require a hierarchical model and more data.

## Nuisance bounds and sharp failures

For a row-sum-zero perturbation `E` to a pulse generator, Duhamel's formula and stochastic-semigroup contraction give

\[
\|e_0^T(e^{(Q+E)t}-e^{Qt})\|_1\le t\|E\|_\infty.
\]

Consequently, for the two pulse generators perturbed by \(E_A,E_B\), the state-2 order contrast can move by at most

\[
2\{u\|E_A\|_\infty+v\|E_B\|_\infty\}.
\]

If initialization has total-variation error at most `m` from state 0, the contrast can move by at most `2m`. These bounds leave a positive guaranteed sign only when their total is smaller than the analytic margin.

Pulse-duration error alone is not the whole time-alignment problem. A shared post-pulse CTMC can alter the readout direction. Numerically, with `k=1`, `epsilon=0.02`, `u=v=1`, and a common symmetric post-pulse generator having conductances `(g01,g12,g02)=(0.01,1,0.1) s^-1`, the state-2 contrast changes from `+0.1784119` at zero delay to `-0.000779694` after 10 s. This is a finite numerical counterexample, not a physical RNA fit. It shows that delayed single-state readout needs a separate generator/readout model or an absorbing commitment stage. If the downstream chain is known to be order-preserving nearest-neighbor and its success map is increasing in state, stochastic monotonicity is a sufficient sign-preservation condition; arbitrary hidden shortcuts are not covered.

Other load-bearing cases:

- `k = epsilon`: the inputs are identical and \(\Delta=0\).
- `k < epsilon`: the sign reverses; the labels do not ensure the desired order.
- Uniform initialization: both schedules retain \(\pi\), so no order signal exists.
- Long pulses with positive `epsilon`: \(h(t)\to0\), so the signal vanishes.
- General symmetric edge inputs \(Q(x_A,y_A),Q(x_B,y_B)\): the short-time sign is controlled by \(x_Ay_B-x_By_A\), which may have either sign. The all-time factorization above uses mirror symmetry.
- Adding a direct `0 <-> 2` edge *during* a pulse, unequal forward/reverse rate modulation, ligand-bound states, feedback, or a state-dependent pulse clock changes the exact theorem and needs new analysis.

## Computational evidence and dependencies

`pulse_order.py` uses only Python's standard library. It applies finite CTMC exponentials by uniformization and records a Poisson-tail diagnostic; floating-point calculations are numerical checks, while the closed form above is the mathematical claim. The script checks the exact formula and state-exchange vector to approximately `1e-16` on four fixtures, verifies the small-pulse commutator coefficient, sweeps pulse width, reports a budget-constrained optimum, and reproduces the post-delay sign reversal. No source kinetic dataset is fitted, no RNA sequence is simulated, and no wet-lab experiment was performed.
