## Exact family, source legality, determinant, and collision gate

Put B=(x,y), u=1+xy, and take the primary map

```
F(B,t)=(u^3 t+y^2u(4+3xy),
        y+3xu^2t+3xy^2(4+3xy),
        2x-3x^2y-x^3t)=h(B)+t g(B),
g=(u^3,3xu^2,-x^3), det JF=-2.
```

Its HC5 potential is `f(B,z)=(g dot z)^2+13(g dot z)+2h dot z`.
Let phi be any global polynomial automorphism of the two base variables,
D any constant invertible3-by-3 matrix, and H(B) any polynomial dual
translation. Define

`f'=f(phi(B),Dz+H(B))`.

The source inverse is explicit: B=phi^-1(oldB), then
z=D^-1(oldz-H(B)). Write tildeF=F(phi(B),t), and barF=D^T tildeF.
The six-variable lift

`G=2z dot barF+2H dot tildeF-(t-13/2)^2`

has G_tt=-2 and global polynomial critical section

`t*=13/2+bar g dot z+H dot tilde g`.

The envelope is exactly f'. Its Hessian determinant is

`32(det JbarF)^2=128(detD)^2(Jac phi)^2`,

a nonzero constant. All nonlinear chain-rule terms from phi and H are
retained in the base/pivot block of the lift; no ordinary Hessian
covariance under a nonlinear source change is assumed.

A gradient collision of the original f is not automatically preserved.
For a known pair U+,U- with common gradient k, let V+=Phi^-1(U+),
V-=Phi^-1(U-), where Phi=(phi,Dz+H). The exact acceptance gate is

`JPhi(V+)^T k=JPhi(V-)^T k`.

This first-jet condition is distinct from source legality and constant

## Only base covectors are constant inverse-Hessian-isotropic

For every member of this family, the constant covectors ell with

`ell^T (Hess f')^-1 ell=0` identically

are exactly the two-dimensional base-covector plane. Here inverse-Hessian
isotropy is a scalar identity; it is not the same as a constant direct
Hessian pivot.

To prove it, set X=(B,t), J=2JbarF. The lifted Hessian has the exact block
form and inverse

```
Hess G=[[A,J^T],[J,0]],
(Hess G)^-1=[[0,J^-1],[J^-T,-J^-T A J^-1]],
A=2 sum_i z_i Hess_X barF_i
  +Hess_X(2H dot tildeF)-2e_t e_t^T.
```

The inverse envelope Hessian is the (B,z) submatrix of this inverse,
evaluated at the critical section. Thus every base covector is isotropic.
For a general constant covector ell=(p,q), p in C^2 and q in C^3, put
r=J^-1q. Its isotropic identity is

`2p dot r_B-r^T A r=0`.                           (1)

At fixed B,t the dual variables range over the affine plane

`bar g dot z=t-13/2-H dot tilde g`.

The vector bar g is nowhere zero, and r is fixed on this plane. The
coefficient of the linear z-dependence in (1) must therefore be normal
to the plane:

`Hess barF(r,r) is proportional to bar g`.          (2)

This inference uses every point of the plane, not one selected value of
z. It remains valid under all degenerate ranks of that affine plane
because bar g is unimodular, hence never zero.

Define the polynomial derivation delta=(JbarF)^-1q on C[B,t]. It is
polynomial since the determinant of JbarF is a nonzero constant. A
factor2 in r is harmless. Differentiating JbarF delta=q shows

`delta^2 X=-(JbarF)^-1 Hess barF(delta,delta)`.

Since JbarF e_t=bar g, (2) implies `delta^2 B=0`.

The original g is polynomial-unimodular. One explicit covector is

```
k=(u^2-5uxy+10x^2y^2, 0,
   10u^2y^3-5uxy^4+x^2y^5),  k dot g=1,
```

from `(u-xy)^5=1`. Base automorphism and constant D preserve this
property, giving a polynomial bar k(B) with bar k dot bar g=1.
Therefore

`t=bar k(B) dot [barF-bar h(B)]`.

Each B generator is delta-nilpotent (second derivative zero), as is
each barF generator (delta barF=q). Polynomial products of finitely
delta-nilpotent elements are delta-nilpotent by the Leibniz rule. Hence
t is delta-nilpotent too, making delta a locally nilpotent derivation on
the whole polynomial source ring. This is the load-bearing all-generator
step; delta^2 B=0 alone is not claimed to imply local nilpotence.

## The actual map has no nonzero constant-translation LND lift

Constant target D and the source base automorphism conjugate the above
derivation to `(JF)^-1 q0` for a nonzero constant q0 if q is nonzero.
We now rule out all such q0 without sampling directions.

Let K=C(x,y,t) be the original source fraction field, and denote the
three target functions F by (a,b,c). They are algebraically independent
because detJF=-2 makes F dominant. For `w=y+1/x`, direct substitution
gives

`P0(w)=cw^3-2w^2+bw-2a=0`.

Along a hypothetical polynomial flow of delta_q0, the target functions
become `(a+s q1,b+s q2,c+s q3)`. Thus its rational source expression
w(s) in K(s) must solve

`P0(w)+s Q(w)=0`, `Q=q3w^3+q2w-2q1`.             (3)

For q0!=0, Q is a nonzero polynomial over C. If it is nonconstant,
each of its roots is a constant zeta in C. None is a root of P0:
`c zeta^3+b zeta-2a-2zeta^2` is a nonzero polynomial in the algebraically
independent a,b,c. Hence gcd(P0,Q)=1 over K[w]; a constant Q is
immediate.

The polynomial P0+sQ is primitive in K[w][s], and is irreducible over
K(w) as a polynomial of degree one in s. Gauss's lemma makes it
irreducible in K[w,s], and then in K(s)[w]. Its w-degree is three,
since c+s q3 cannot vanish identically. It has no rational root in K(s).
But local nilpotence makes exp(s delta)x, exp(s delta)y and exp(s delta)t
polynomials in s, so w(s) is such a rational root. This contradiction
rules out every q0!=0.

The original w at s=0 is already a root of P0 in K. This does not give
a generic factor of P0+sQ; the coprime linear-in-s argument above is
the precise reason. No numerical degree or irreducibility assertion is
substituted for it. Consequently q=0 in (1), proving the isotropic-plane
claim.

## Individually preserving elementary shears remain in this family

For a source shear `S(U)=U+v H1(ell dot U)` with nonzero constant v,
nonzero constant ell, ell dot v=0, and degree H1 at least two, the
inverse is obtained by negating H1. The exact chain/determinant formula is

`det Hess(f' composed S)=det Hess f'`
` *[1+H1'' (f'_v composed S) (ell^T(Hess f')^-1 ell composed S)]`.

The Jacobian of S preserves ell, which is why no extra transformed
covector appears. A nonzero constant v makes f'_v nonconstant: otherwise
its gradient `(Hess f')v` would vanish despite the invertible Hessian.
Thus the product in brackets cannot differ from1 by a nonzero constant.
If the new determinant is constant, its correction is zero. Since H1''
and f'_v are nonzero polynomials, the inverse-isotropic identity is
necessary. This also proves that any constant value is the original
determinant; merely saying f'_v is not identically zero would not justify
that extra assertion.

The covector ell must therefore belong to the base plane. The resulting
shear changes B through a polynomial planar elementary automorphism and
changes z through a polynomial base-dependent translation. This stays
within the family. Affine conjugation transports the isotropic plane
by the corresponding constant linear map. Induction covers finite words
whose every nonlinear elementary step separately preserves a constant
Hessian determinant, with affine steps allowed.

**Scope boundary:** no argument excludes a word that has nonconstant

## No constant direct Hessian pivot in the whole family

Suppose a nonzero constant direction v=(p,w) has D_v^2 f' constant.
The degree-two dual-z coefficient is

`D_p^2 (bar g dot z)^2`.

For a polynomial in one coordinate along p, its square cannot have
second derivative zero unless it is independent of that coordinate.
Therefore D_p bar g=0. Since D is constant invertible, this says
D_p g(phi(B))=0. The component `-X^3` gives D_p X=0, and U^3 gives
D_p U=0, where (X,Y)=phi(B), U=1+XY. The polynomial domain then gives
D_p Y=0. Invertibility of Jphi forces p=0.

For the remaining direction (0,w),

`D_v^2 f'=2(bar g dot w)^2`.

A constant value requires bar g dot w constant. In independent rational
coordinates (X,U), g consists of the linearly independent homogeneous
cubics U^3,3XU^2,-X^3. No nonzero constant combination is constant.
Thus w=0, a contradiction. Every member has no constant-direction
quadratic or affine pivot, and this property survives affine changes of
the full source coordinates.
