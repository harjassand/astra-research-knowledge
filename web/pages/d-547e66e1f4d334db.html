## 1. Genuine idempotents and branch ring isomorphisms

The cycle11 selector identity is algebraic, not a statement about final points. For any j≠i, z_i=U_iW_i belongs to C. For clarity, a charged reconstruction is

    E=U_iH_ij+2m_i s_j A_i=z_i e_ij,
    Q=W_i A_i=z_i V_i,
    T=W_j E−z_i H_ji=2m_j s_i z_i V_j,
    R=z_i s_i A_j+s_j T=z_i s_i V_j,
    S=q_i R+q_j s_i Q−s_i V_j Q−s_i(q_ij+s_i s_j)E
      =Δ_ij z_i s_i,
    (s_i S+Δ_ij Q)/(Δ_ij q_i)=z_i.

The maximum degree of coefficient times original A/H generator is10. Only positive nonzero constants q_i and Δ_ij are divided out. This holds at zero q_ij as well.

Define μ_i=−W_i and ρ_i=2U_i in A. Direct polynomial identities give

    μ_i+ρ_i=1, μ_iρ_i=−2z_i,
    μ_i²−μ_i=ρ_i²−ρ_i=2z_i.

Thus their images are orthogonal complementary idempotents. Products of one μ_i or ρ_i for each i split A as a direct product of eight branch rings. On a mass branch μ_i=1, U_i=0 and m_i s_i=1/2. On a root branch ρ_i=1, W_i=0 and m_i s_i=1/4; there U_i=1/2, so A_i=0 forces V_i=0. This last conclusion would not hold from the equation V_i=0 alone.

There is also an explicit global unit identity,

    q_i=A_i+s_i(s_i+2q_i m_i−2m_i s_i²).

Hence every s_i is a unit before branch splitting. Put u_i=s_i^−1. On mass indices u_i=2m_i; on root indices u_i=s_i/q_i and m_i=u_i/4. The branch presentation is exactly

    root i:  q_i u_i²=1,
    mass i:  q_i u_i²+q_ij u_i u_j=2, for every j≠i.       (1)

Indeed, multiply the mass equation H_ij=0 by u_i u_j: since W_i=−1 and 2m_i=u_i, the result is 2−q_i u_i²−q_ij u_i u_j=0. Root H_ij vanishes because W_i=V_i=0. Conversely, in (1) all u_i are units: root inverse q_i u_i, mass inverse (q_i u_i+q_ij u_j)/2. Define m_i=u_i/4 and s_i=q_i u_i on root indices, and m_i=u_i/2 and s_i=(q_i u_i+q_ij u_j)/2 on mass indices. Different choices of j give the same s_i by (1). These formulas satisfy the original branch equations and are inverse ring maps. No uncharged localization or point-count-to-reducedness inference is involved.

## 2. Complete support and compatibility classification

Use A for a mass index and B for a root index. The empty branch is the zero algebra; its unit idempotent is zero in the full product. All assertions are over Q, before choosing square roots.

**BBB.** The ring is the tensor product of q_i u_i²−1, i=1,2,3. Each quadratic has two distinct real roots. The length is8 and the ring is reduced and totally real.

**ABB, with index1 mass.** Subtract the two mass equations to obtain u_1(q_12u_2−q_13u_3)=0. Since u_1 is a unit:

* q_12=q_13=0 gives independent equations q_1u_1²=2, q_2u_2²=q_3u_3²=1; length8, reduced and totally real.
* Exactly one of q_12,q_13 is zero forces a nonzero constant times a unit to vanish; the ring is zero.
* Both are nonzero gives u_3=(q_12/q_13)u_2. The two root equations are compatible exactly when q_3q_12²=q_2q_13². Failure gives the unit ideal. With compatibility, the exact ideal is that linear relation together with q_2u_2²−1 and q_1u_1²+q_12u_1u_2−2. For each real u_2 root, the last quadratic has discriminant q_12²/q_2+8q_1>0 and nonzero constant term. There are four simple real points, so length4. The unused coefficient q_23 imposes no further condition in this branch.

**AAB, with masses1,2.** Write d_i=q_i u_i², κ_i=2−d_i and r_ij=q_ij u_i u_j. Equations (1) give κ_1=κ_2=r_12=r_13=r_23.

* Any zero off-diagonal forces all off-diagonals to be zero in a nonempty branch, because the u_i are units. If all are zero, the independent equations d_1=d_2=2,d_3=1 give length8. Partial zero support gives the zero algebra.
* If all off-diagonals are nonzero, set r=q_13/q_23 and t=q_12/q_23. Equality of the products, followed by unit cancellation, gives u_2=r u_1 and u_3=t u_1. The exact remaining conditions are

      q_1=q_2 r²,  q_1+q_12r=2q_3t².                   (2)

  Failure of either gives the unit ideal, since u_1² is a unit. If both hold, the exact ideal is the two linear relations and q_3t²u_1²−1. Its coefficient is positive and t≠0, so length2 with two simple real points. The strict pair minor and the first condition in (2) give |q_12r|<q_1, consistent with positivity of the mass coefficient.

**AAA.** All κ_i and all r_ij coincide. Zero support is either all-zero, which gives three independent d_i=2 equations and length8, or partial-zero, which is empty. With all off-diagonals nonzero, put r_1=q_23/q_13 and r_3=q_12/q_13. Exact unit cancellation gives u_1=r_1u_2 and u_3=r_3u_2. Compatibility is

    q_1r_1²=q_2=q_3r_3².                              (3)

If (3) fails the branch is empty. If it holds, its exact ideal is the two linear relations and (q_2+q_12r_1)u_2²−2. Since |q_12r_1|<q_2 by the strict pair minor, the coefficient is positive, and this is a reduced totally real length2 algebra.

These are scheme presentations, so reducedness has been proved factor by factor. The direct product is reduced and totally real, with length≤8·8=64. Diagonal q attains64; no positive-definite degeneration omitted above increases the bound.

## 4. B invertibility and the correct positive trace form

At a real branch point use d_i=q_i u_i²>0, r_ij=q_ij u_i u_j. Strict pair minors imply |r_ij|<sqrt(d_i d_j). A root has d_i=1. On an edge incident to a mass index, r_ij=κ_i=2−d_i.

For a mass-root edge, κ_i²<d_i=2−κ_i, so −2<κ_i<1. If all endpoints are mass, the common κ obeys κ²<(2−κ)², so κ<1. A root-root edge has |r_ij|<1. These arguments include zero coefficients. Thus every r_ij<1 and

    β=B u_1u_2u_3=3−r_12−r_13−r_23>0.                (4)

Since all s_i are units, B vanishes at no point of this reduced finite algebra. Equivalently its multiplication matrix over Q is invertible, of size at most64.

For any finite reduced totally real Q-algebra, after tensoring with R it is R^d. The ordinary trace form Tr(L_ab)=Σ_p a(p)b(p) is positive definite. The weighted form Tr(L_βab)=Σ_p β(p)a(p)b(p) is positive definite by (4). **B itself is not a universally positive weight.** Under simultaneous sign reversal u↦−u, B changes sign whereas β does not. Already BBB supplies both signs. The replay code correctly computes the weight B∏u_i, not B.

Conversely, for an exactly constructed finite quotient, positive definiteness of its ordinary trace Gram proves reducedness and total reality for that quotient. A nilpotent n satisfies Tr(L_nb)=0 for every b because nb is nilpotent. A complex residue factor contributes a negative value to the square of its imaginary unit. Either would contradict positive definiteness. This is an elementary exact algebra argument; positivity on finitely many supplied matrices is not a parameter-uniform proof.

## 5. What this gives, and what it does not

The auxiliary algebra now has an explicit fixed-size rational description on every parameter stratum. Exact tests for zero support and compatibility use rational arithmetic. Branch bases can be chosen with degree≤3: squarefree bases for the length8 branches, degree≤2 for ABB length4, and degree≤1 for length2 branches. Original auxiliary variables are linear in u on each branch. B inversion in these bases takes constant matrix size and polynomial bit cost in the supplied q bit length; denominators can depend on q but no hidden numerical conditioning bound is assumed.

Products of the three idempotent selectors have degree≤6. Recombining a branch inverse therefore gives an original-variable representative b of degree≤9 with bB=1 modulo C. The sign involution allows b to be chosen odd. On nonzero ABB length4 branches the odd subspace has a degree1 basis, so that branch's inverse can be represented linearly; the length2 branches also need only degree1. Root-only and isolated-mass length8 branches may require degree3. These are quotient representative bounds.

The polynomial 1−bB can consequently have degree≤12 while its expression as a sum of coefficients times the raw A_i,H_ij may require a larger degree. Membership in C is not membership in its degree12 Macaulay span. The previously charged selector identity has degree10; using it plus already charged branch unit certificates proves degree22, independently of this finite-algebra theorem. No argument here proves raw degree8 completeness, triple saturation power4, a smaller uniform f-power, or practical coefficient-only recovery.

The next decisive step is a tracked inverse-and-reduction calculation with universal rational coefficient identities on every stratum, or an exact dual obstruction to a specified lower degree on a PD matrix. Constant algebra length, reducedness, a positive trace form and finite replay rank do not replace that step. The earlier mock-ideal origin obstruction remains a separate warning against inferring an origin saturation bound from reduced localization; it is not a counterexample to this actual polar ideal.
