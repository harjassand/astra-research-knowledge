# From reachable observations to identifiable counterfactuals

Status: exact general obstruction, independently constructed here. It is standard indistinguishability reasoning, not a claim of novelty. The toy computation is not an actual ULTRADISCOVERY run or counterexample.

## Source-motivated question and interface

ULTRADISCOVERY, arXiv:2610.03092v1 (2 October 2026), has a fixed hidden mechanism behind raw observations, finite paid actions, periodic resets, and an exact unseen intervention target. The relevant question is whether a legal transcript determines its target, conditional on the hypothesis class the learner is actually entitled to use.

Source claims, in compact paraphrase: Appendix A.6 describes hidden label equality as shared identity/state/quota/queue, maintenance verbs as coordinate rewrites, and instrument readings as body-specific monotone transforms. Appendix A.9 derives the exact target from hidden mechanism parameters. Appendix C.3 supplies a 39-action scripted route and an oracle commitment and tests a model on the resulting record. The main experiments diagnose poor representation construction. The limitations promise a later code release. These claims do not themselves give a constructive inference algorithm or an exhaustive identifiability certificate for the generator's parameter family.

Our deduction is the distinction below. It neither rejects the reported finite experiments nor proves the benchmark target unidentifiable. Access to the actual generated parameter class is necessary for that stronger judgment.

## Transcript criterion

Let M be a declared class of deterministic models, I a legal action/observation interface, and y(M) the target counterfactual. For transcript h define V(h)={M: M produces h under the actions in h}. An exact target is identifiable from h if and only if {y(M):M in V(h)} is a singleton.

Proof: if the set is a singleton, output its element. If M0,M1 in V(h) have different targets, any deterministic rule using only h outputs the same answer in the two worlds and therefore fails in one. Any randomized rule has the same output distribution; under a uniform prior over the twin worlds its exact success probability is at most 1/2. This is an information obstruction, independent of model size, prompting, proof search, or representation syntax.

This criterion needs the whole model class. A numerical simulator trace for the true instance, an evidence checklist, and existence of an oracle's calculation do not substitute for it. Conversely, failure by tested language models does not prove non-identifiability.

## Monotone extrapolation twins

For any integer budget q>=1, state s starts at 0. Every legal action costs one. `advance` increments s, `reset` sets s=0, and `observe` leaves s unchanged. After every action the learner receives exact phi(s). Dynamics and action semantics are known. The learner must predict phi(q+1), a counterfactual reading beyond its exploration budget.

Compare two instrument models on nonnegative integers:

    phi_0(s)=s,
    phi_1(s)=s+1{s>=q+1}.

Both are strictly increasing, deterministic, integer-valued, and affine on all reachable calibration states 0,...,q. Every action history of at most q paid actions has s<=q. Its observations are therefore identical in the two worlds. Adaptive policies also produce identical transcripts, by induction on actions. Yet the target values are q+1 and q+2. No learner can universally make the exact prediction at budget q under this class, even when the entire latent dynamical representation is disclosed.

The counterexample remains if passive waiting is permitted without changing s; it fails if a legal cheap action can jump to or otherwise reveal the target state. It also fails under a genuinely supplied global affine-instrument assumption, since calibrated slope/intercept then determine the target. These boundary cases are why the access model and prior class are load-bearing.

More generally, with a finite set of observed states and unrestricted strictly monotone real-valued readout, any unobserved target in a nontrivial gap admits multiple compatible interpolations. For integer readouts, a gap may be forced to a unique value when strict monotonicity and neighboring readings leave only one available integer. The integer construction above deliberately avoids that exception.

## Foundational use and remaining bottleneck

A scientific discovery system should produce one of two reviewable objects: a model/derivation whose target prediction is invariant across all currently compatible models, or two compatible models with different predictions plus an experiment that separates them. The object is target-specific; recovery of a unique named ontology is unnecessary. Label-coordinate renamings can preserve all predictions, while a hidden readout ambiguity can matter despite perfect identification of label identities and dynamics.

For finite supplied M, the recursive optimal worst-case target-acquisition cost is exact:

    C(V)=0 if all targets agree;
    C(V)=min_a [c(a)+max_o C({M in V: observation(M,a)=o})]

with C=+infinity when no legal distinguishing continuation exists. Histories/reset restrictions must be included in the state; experiments are not assumed independent. This is ordinary decision-tree/version-space reasoning, not a new algorithm. It exposes the actual unresolved problem: constructing an economical, expressive candidate model family and distinguishing experiments from the raw scientific interface, rather than supplying the family or its target oracle.
