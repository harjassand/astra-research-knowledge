# Cycle 4 — restricted noise actuation for stationary OU identification

Status: constructive two-diagonal-setting theorem and uniform spanning-probe theorem derived; the one-diagonal-setting pointwise question for arbitrary n remains open in this note. No historical novelty claimed. Shared fixed physical interface: dX=(-AX+b)dt+sqrt(D0)dW with arbitrary positive-stable real A, unknown SPD D0, all state coordinates observed, and calibrated independent additive Brownian noise that leaves A,b unchanged. D0 and Q denote covariance power, so the Fokker–Planck diffusion is half this matrix. Unknown OU structure, full observability, drift invariance and independent stationary ensembles are supplied assumptions; they are not learned from covariance fitting.

Only **diagonal** powers are required here: independent force amplitudes on each physical coordinate, rather than calibrated correlations between every pair. For a positive diagonal Q, the stationary covariance response Γ(Q)=ΣQ−Σ0 solves AΓ+ΓAᵀ=Q and is SPD. The unknown baseline noise cancels. Calibrating Q fixes the physical time scale.

## Theorem: two diagonal settings suffice generically for every fixed drift

Fix any stable A and any strictly positive diagonal Q1. Acquire baseline and two perturbed stationary covariances for Q1 and a second positive diagonal Q2. For each fixed A,Q1, almost every Q2 in the n-dimensional positive diagonal cone makes C=Γ1^(-1/2)Γ2Γ1^(-1/2) have simple spectrum. Hence the two-increment reconstruction of cycle3 recovers A uniquely, without assuming diagonal baseline D0. Recover D0=AΣ0+Σ0Aᵀ and b=Aμ0 afterwards. For n≥2 two settings are minimal when baseline D0 is unrestricted, because any single covariance response Γ1 leaves A+ηΩΓ1⁻¹ indistinguishable for small η and nonzero skew Ω.

### Rank-one response lemma

Whiten Γ1 with W=Γ1^(-1/2) and put B=W A W⁻¹. Then B+Bᵀ=WQ1W≻0. Let Ejj denote the coordinate diagonal unit, Gj=L_A⁻¹(Ejj)⪰0 and Cj=W Gj W⪰0. They satisfy

B Cj+Cj Bᵀ=wj wjᵀ,   wj=W ej,

and ∑j (Q1)jj Cj=I. Every positive eigenvalue of Cj is simple: if P projects onto an r-dimensional positive-eigenvalue eigenspace with eigenvalue c, compressing the equation gives

P wj wjᵀ P=c P(B+Bᵀ)P.

The right side has rank r; the left has rank at most1. Thus r≤1. Zero eigenvalues may repeat because a coordinate actuator need not make the drift controllable.

The same lemma holds after restriction to any common kernel N of previously selected Cj. For x∈ker Cj, its quadratic form in the equation gives wjᵀx=0; the equation applied to x then gives Cj Bᵀx=0. Thus every such kernel, and their intersection N, is Bᵀ-invariant. In an orthogonal range/kernel basis, B has lower-left block zero. Compressing the equation of any further Ck to N therefore gives an accretive Lyapunov equation with a rank-one right-hand side; its positive eigenvalues are again simple.

### Complete existence and genericity argument

Start with C1. Its positive eigenvalues are simple; its zero space is N1=ker C1. Add ε2 C2 with ε2>0 sufficiently small. Existing positive eigenvalues remain distinct by continuity. The new eigenvalues emerging from zero are ε2 times the positive eigenvalues of P_N1 C2 P_N1 plus o(ε2), and are simple by the compressed lemma. The remaining exact zero space is ker C1∩ker C2, because both summands are PSD. Repeat, choosing each additional coefficient sufficiently small after the previous ones are fixed. This standard first-order symmetric perturbation statement follows by splitting the existing positive spectrum from zero and taking the Schur complement; the cross-block correction is O(ε²).

After all n matrices are included, ∑j (Q1)jj Cj=I ensures the common kernel is zero. The resulting positive linear combination has every eigenvalue simple. It is C(Q2) for a strictly positive diagonal Q2. Thus the discriminant of C(Q2), a polynomial in the n independent entries of Q2, is not identically zero. Its zero set has Lebesgue measure zero. Any continuous probe distribution with density in a positive diagonal box succeeds almost surely, for this fixed A. The hierarchy is an existence witness using unknown responses, not an implementable oracle design and not a uniform conditioning bound.

This argument was developed jointly with hostile auditor check_wcrg, who independently identified the rank-one lemma and hierarchical construction. The simpler full-SPD polynomial proof from cycle3 alone would not establish genericity in this lower-dimensional diagonal cone.

## Exact three-dimensional blind selected diagonal probe

One increment with unknown diagonal D0 has a different interface. The general cycle3 iff criterion is: for C=Σ0⁻¹/²Σ1Σ0⁻¹/² with eigenvectors U, require simple C and nonsingular F=diag(c−1)[(UᵀΣ0⁻¹/²)∘(UᵀΣ0⁻¹/²)]. Diagonal forcing does not make F nonsingularity automatic for n≥3.

An exact rational example is

A=½[[1,−5,−7],[5,1,3],[7,−3,1]],  D0=I,  Σ0=I,  Q=diag(1,2,4),

Γ=[[13/6,0,1/6],[0,5/2,−1/6],[1/6,−1/6,7/3]].

Direct multiplication verifies AΓ+ΓAᵀ=Q. The symmetric part of A is I/2, so A is stable. Γ has three distinct positive eigenvalues 7/3 and 7/3±√3/6. Nevertheless

H=[[-1/2,7,13/2],[−7,−1/2,−15/2],[−13/2,15/2,1]]

obeys HΓ+ΓHᵀ=0 and H+Hᵀ=diag(−1,−1,2). Consequently A'=A+ηH and D0'=I+ηdiag(−1,−1,2) give exactly the same baseline and perturbed stationary Gaussian laws. For |η| small, D0' and the symmetric part of A' are positive, so the alternative is physical. The ambiguity occurs despite distinct coordinate ratios Qjj/(D0)jj and a healthy covariance spectral gap. It is not a pointwise genericity counterexample: for this same fixed A,D0, Q'=diag(1,3,2) makes the joint operator invertible.

Construction: let p=(3−√3)/12, v=(√p,√p,√(1−2p)), U=I−2vvᵀ. M=U∘U has rank2 and distinct rows. Set c=M(1,2,4)ᵀ, Γ=Udiag(c)Uᵀ, and choose skew Kij=(UᵀQU)ij/(cj−ci). Then A=I/2+UKUᵀ has the rational form above. This also explains why the singular diagonal recovery system is not merely a numerical threshold accident.

## Uniform spanning-coordinate protocol

A deterministic, universally valid fallback is n calibrated coordinate probes Ejj, or n strictly positive diagonal powers spanning the diagonal matrix space. This recovers A for every stable A, even with arbitrary unknown SPD baseline D0. Rank-one increments are allowed positive semidefinite additions; the total physical baseline remains SPD.

For a homogeneous ambiguity H, all responses obey H Gj+GjHᵀ=0. Whiten G*=∑Gj=L_A⁻¹(I)≻0. The transformed H is skew K and commutes with every transformed Cj. Every positive eigenvalue of each Cj is simple, so a commuting real skew K annihilates its positive eigenvectors: K maps each one-dimensional eigenspace to itself and a real skew scalar is zero. Thus K acts only within every ker Cj. Their intersection is zero because ∑Cj=I. Hence H=0; baseline D0 and b follow.

n distinct coordinate rank-one probes are minimal **within that coordinate-only family** for uniform arbitrary drift: if a coordinate is unprobed, a positive diagonal A leaves its scalar drift/noise pair uncalibrated. This does not assert n settings are minimal among arbitrary positive diagonal powers. The generic two-setting protocol already shows much less pointwise cost. A finite set of positive diagonal powers spanning the diagonal space can be chosen as I and I+Ejj for j<n (n settings).

## Acquisition, conditioning and costs

Two-setting reconstruction uses the observed response covariances, not microscopic graph information. Diagonalize C=Γ1⁻¹/²Γ2Γ1⁻¹/², fix symmetric transformed drift S=Γ1⁻¹/²Q1Γ1⁻¹/²/2, and recover skew K by

(cj−ci) Kij=[Γ1⁻¹/²Q2Γ1⁻¹/²−SC−CS]ij.

This costs O(n³) arithmetic and O(n²) storage. Small gaps can make an exactly identifiable experiment unusable at finite precision. The cycle3 cheap bound applies: with acquired Γhat1≻0, c=||Chat||op, g=minpair|ci−cj|>0 and β=√[(1/2+c/g)²+1/g²], the empirical two-response moment operator has a conservative lower singular value

γhat ≥ λmin(Γhat1)/(√κ(Γhat1) β).

For n1 omit the skew term. If the three estimated covariances have simultaneous errors ||Σhatj−Σj||op≤εj, response errors are bounded by ε0+εj. Thus δP=2√[(ε0+ε1)²+(ε0+ε2)²]. If actuator calibration errors obey ||Qhatj−Qj||F≤ζj, let ζ=√(ζ1²+ζ2²). For any acquired Ahat, residual r in the empirical two-response equations and certified γhat>δP,

||Ahat−A||F≤[r+δP||Ahat||F+ζ]/(γhat−δP).

This is the same elementary perturbation proof as cycle3, with covariance differences and both calibration errors charged. D0 reconstruction has bound 2||Σhat0||op||Ahat−A||F+2ε0(||Ahat||F+||Ahat−A||F), and b requires a separately acquired mean bound. Positivity and stability can be checked from the confidence set; fitting residuals do not establish the OU assumption.

A data-acquisition schedule conditional on access to stationary ensembles can use fresh independent stationary Gaussian ensembles at each of three settings, estimate centered covariance, and attach the fully acquired radius εj=ρj||Σhatj||op/(1−ρj), ρj=√[n(n+1)/((Nj−1)δj)]<1. Allocate summable δj,t over doubling stages; stop only when the displayed target radius is met, otherwise report unresolved conditioning. This conservative finite-data protocol needs no supplied covariance norm. A continuously random diagonal Q2 in a known positive amplitude box supplies genericity; finite-bit actuation must instead rely on its measured certificate or a separate discrete anti-concentration result.

Resources per setting: n independent calibrated force amplitudes rather than n(n+1)/2 correlated-power controls; one baseline and two perturbed ensembles; O(n³) reconstruction. Sample/preparation work is separate. There is no uniform sample cap over unbounded stable drifts: the two-dimensional high-frequency rotation from cycle3 produces shrinking response anisotropy and Θ(ω²/η²) constant-error fixed-hypothesis discrimination cost. Independent stationary preparation, reset/mixing time, sensor noise and drift changes under forcing remain physical assumptions/costs. Generic exact rank is not an acquired uniform rate.

## Sharper acquired covariance confidence and finite simulated acquisition

Let m=N−1 after centering N iid Gaussian observations. Standardize the sample covariance by the unknown Σ for the proof only; for every fixed unit vector its quadratic form is χ²_m/m. For 0<λ<1/2 the centered χ² log-mgf satisfies log E exp[λ(χ²_m−m)]≤mλ²/(1−2λ); for negative λ it is at most mλ². Chernoff gives upper deviation 2√(mx)+2x and lower deviation 2√(mx), with total failure≤2e^(-x). A sphere 1/4-net has at most9^n points, and symmetric operator norm is at most twice the maximum absolute quadratic form on that net. Therefore with

x=n log9+log(2/δ),   ρ=4√(x/m)+4x/m,

we have ||Σhat−Σ||op≤ρ||Σ||op with probability≥1−δ. If ρ<1, acquire ε=ρ||Σhat||op/(1−ρ). This proof uses no supplied covariance scale. Independent auditor reconstructed the Chernoff/net proof. Small-ρ sample cost is O((n+log(1/δ))/ρ²), improving the earlier elementary Markov bound. Parameter cost still depends on the acquired inverse gap.

## Uniform deterministic boundary, independently reconstructed

Audit artifact: `notes/check_wcrg/cycle4/diagonal_forcing_audit.md`, with exact symbolic replay `work/check_wcrg/cycle4/diagonal_checks.py`. For n2, direct elimination gives det[q↦(Γ11,Γ22)]=1/(4det A)>0. With unknown diagonal D0=diag(d), one positive diagonal probe fails exactly on q1d2−q2d1=0. Two nonproportional prescribed positive diagonal probes succeed uniformly; one randomized probe succeeds for every fixed truth. A single coordinate rank-one probe is not uniform because Γ can be singular.

For n≥3, **every prescribed pair** of positive diagonal powers has an admissible stable drift for which baseline and both perturbed complete Gaussian laws are unchanged under a nonzero drift rotation. Baseline power can even be known and fixed. Here is the full construction rather than an inference from a selected example. Normalize Q1 to I by diagonal physical-coordinate congruence, and Q2 to diag(ti). Repeated ratios permit an isotropic drift and rotation within a repeated pair. Otherwise select three ratios a<c<b. Put R=W²=diag(p,1,q), α=c−a, β=b−c, with p,q>0 and αp≠βq. Let

u=(√[βq/(αp+βq)],0,√[αp/(αp+βq)]),  v=(u3,0,−u1),  E=span(e_middle,u).

The compression of W(Q2−cI)W to E is zero. Define τ=(vᵀWQ2Wv)/(vᵀRv)>0; then τ−c=(βq−αp)/(vᵀRv)≠0. Let C=cI on E and τ on span(v). Let B's symmetric part be R/2, its within-E skew block zero, and its skew cross block

K_Ev=[(WQ2W)_Ev−(c+τ)R_Ev/2]/(τ−c).

Block multiplication gives BC+CBᵀ=WQ2W. Set A=W⁻¹BW, Γ1=W⁻², Γ2=W⁻¹CW⁻¹, and D0=I so Σ0=Γ1. Any nonzero skew Ω acting within E commutes with C; H=W⁻¹ΩW preserves Σ0,Γ1,Γ2. A+ηH remains stable for all realη because its whitened symmetric part stays R/2≻0. Independent modes handle extra coordinates. Undo the diagonal normalization. This proves the universal prescribed-pair obstruction, not just a numerical example.

The rational replay uses Q1=I,Q2=diag(1,2,3), D0=I,

A=[[2,0,−5/6],[0,1/2,0],[10/3,0,1/2]], Σ0=Γ1=diag(1/4,1,1),

Γ2=[[11/34,0,3/17],[0,2,0],[3/17,0,31/17]], H=[[0,−1/2,0],[2,0,2],[0,−2,0]].

Generic minimality is different: two are minimal with unknown full baseline at n≥2. One generic diagonal probe with unknown diagonal baseline remains open at n≥3.
