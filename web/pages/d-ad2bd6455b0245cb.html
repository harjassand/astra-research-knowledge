# Independent audit: Gaussian sequence archives over polynomial ellipsoids

Date: 7 October 2026. Auditor: `gaussian_sequence_audit`.

**Finding.** The claimed retained-memory order passes an independent reconstruction in the exact measurable-channel model. Both bounds are uniform in the amplitude `A`, with constants depending only on the fixed smoothness exponent. The lower can use a fixed transformation `tanh X` and an explicit bounded proper loss, giving

\[
 \ln D\ge \frac d2\ln\frac r\epsilon
              -\left(\frac54+\ln 2\right)d,
 \qquad 0<r\le1.
 \tag{1}
\]

Only the `2d` means `+/-r e_i` are needed for this inequality; preserving the null law is not an extra lower-bound premise. The upper is a finite coordinate partition followed by a null-conditional Gaussian decoder. It does not rely on N30, fixed-dimension constants, or a quantum-to-Gaussian bridge.

The result is an information-theoretic theorem for continuous-output random kernels. It is not a finite-running-time theorem for a machine that returns finitely many bits: such output is countably supported and has TV distance one from a continuous Gaussian. Generating the whole countable sequence also requires infinite output and an ideal source of fresh randomness. Those resources must be explicit. This audit is internal mathematical evidence, not external correctness certification or historical novelty clearance.

## 1. Exact interface and theorem

The observation space is the standard Borel product

\[
 \Omega=\mathbb R^{\mathbb N},\qquad
 P_\theta=\bigotimes_{j\ge1}N(\theta_j,1).
\]

The supplied mean class is

\[
 \Theta_{\alpha,A}
  =\left\{\theta:\sum_{j\ge1}j^{2\alpha}\theta_j^2\le A^2\right\},
 \qquad \alpha>0,  A>0.
 \tag{2}
\]

Every such mean is in `ell_2`. The coordinate system, covariance, exponent, amplitude and requested tolerance are supplied public model inputs. The encoder receives one draw `X`, not the unknown `theta`.

The archive is a finite quantum memory, or a hybrid direct sum of total Hilbert dimension

\[
 D=\sum_b d_b.
\]

Every retained input-dependent register and classical branch counts. The decoder is a parameter-independent POVM/random kernel with output `Y in Omega`; it cannot revisit `X`. Input-independent shared classical randomness is allowed. Free entangled receiver references are excluded; equivalently, the counted decoder input must include every quantum register that can carry input-correlated information. Local ancillary states and private randomness prepared independently of the input can be incorporated into the POVM.

Use `TV(P,Q)=sup_C |P(C)-Q(C)|`, and require

\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\alpha,A}}
       \operatorname{TV}(P_\theta,\operatorname{Law}_\theta(Y))
       \le\epsilon.
 \tag{3}
\]

Let `B_q` and `B_cl` be the infima of `log_2 D` over admissible quantum/hybrid and classical archives. There are absolute `epsilon_0>0` and `C_0>1` such that, for each fixed `alpha>0`, positive constants `c_alpha,C_alpha` satisfy

\[
 c_\alpha(A/\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}
 \le B_q\le B_{\rm cl}
 \le C_\alpha(A/\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}
 \tag{4}
\]

for all `A>0`, `0<epsilon<=epsilon_0` and `A/epsilon>=C_0`. The explicit proof below permits `epsilon_0=1/128`, `C_0=128`, and explicit positive constants depending only on `alpha`.

Uniformity is important: `A` can tend to zero, stay fixed, or diverge while `A/epsilon` grows. The theorem does not assert the rate when the ratio stays close to one. For example, a one-label null simulator has error at most `A/sqrt(2pi)`, so its charged bits are zero when `A<=sqrt(2pi)*epsilon`.

## 2. Scalar null-conditional archive

Supply `a>=0`, `0<h<=1`, and assume `|theta|<=a`. Put

\[
 T=a+\sqrt{2\ln(8/h^2)},\qquad M=\lceil2T/h\rceil.
 \tag{5}
\]

Partition `[-T,T]` into `M` equal intervals of width `w=2T/M<=h`, and add two exterior rays. Fix half-open endpoint conventions. All cells have positive standard-normal probability.

The encoder stores the cell containing `X`. On that cell the decoder draws an independent `N(0,1)` conditioned on the cell. This is a legal decoder because the conditioner uses a supplied null law, rather than the unknown mean. Write its output law as `Q_theta`. It preserves the reference exactly: `Q_0=P_0`.

On a cell `C`, the output density is `P_theta(C) P_0(.|C)`. The KL chain rule gives

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta\Vert Q_\theta)
  =\sum_C P_\theta(C)
       \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta(.|C)\Vert P_0(.|C)).
 \tag{6}
\]

For the conditioned null exponential family let

\[
 \psi_C(t)=\ln\mathbb E_{P_0(.|C)}e^{tX}.
\]

It has `psi_C(0)=0`, `psi_C''(t)=Var_{N(t,1)|C}(X)`, and

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta(.|C)\Vert P_0(.|C))
   =\theta\psi_C'(\theta)-\psi_C(\theta)
   =\int_0^\theta t\psi_C''(t)\,dt.
 \tag{7}
\]

The last expression is valid for negative `theta` as well; the integration orientation and the sign of `t` both reverse.

On a bounded cell, the variance is at most `w^2/4<=h^2/4`, so its conditional divergence is at most `theta^2 h^2/8`. On either ray a one-sided truncated unit-variance Gaussian has variance at most one. Explicitly, for truncation above `z`, `lambda=phi(z)/(1-Phi(z))` gives variance `1+z lambda-lambda^2<=1`, because `lambda>=z`; reflection handles the lower ray. Thus a ray contributes conditional divergence at most `theta^2/2`.

The actual shifted tail probability obeys

\[
 P_\theta(|X|>T)
  \le2e^{-(T-a)^2/2}=h^2/4.
 \tag{8}
\]

Combining (6)--(8) yields the dimension-free scalar estimate

\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta\Vert Q_\theta)
 \le \theta^2h^2/8+(\theta^2/2)(h^2/4)
 =\theta^2h^2/4.
 \tag{9}
\]

This is first-order conditional reconstruction; no positive-hat or second-order approximation premise is needed for the sequence rate.

## 3. Infinite-product upper and its exact bit count

Set

\[
 a_j=A j^{-\alpha},\qquad
 m=(A/\epsilon)^{1/\alpha},\qquad k=\lfloor m\rfloor.
 \tag{10}
\]

For `j<=k`, use the scalar construction with `h_j=epsilon/a_j<=1`. If the last radius equals `epsilon`, either retaining or omitting that coordinate is legal; retaining it simplifies this floor convention. For `j>k`, store nothing and output independent standard normals.

The finite vector of cell labels has a finite alphabet. Decode active coordinates independently from their null-conditioners and inactive coordinates independently from `N(0,1)`. This defines a measurable kernel on the countable product, by the usual product-measure construction. The finite-bin encoder is Borel measurable and reads only the first `k` coordinates.

Every finite prefix has divergence bounded by

\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta^{(n)}\Vert Q_\theta^{(n)})
 &\le\frac12\sum_{j\le n}
   \theta_j^2\min\{1,\epsilon^2/a_j^2\}\\
 &\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}
       \sum_{j\ge1}\theta_j^2/a_j^2
 \le\epsilon^2/2.
 \end{split}
 \tag{11}
\]

Pinsker gives TV at most `epsilon/2` for every prefix. This also bounds whole-product TV: for two probability measures on a countable product, the TV distances of increasing finite-coordinate restrictions converge to whole-space TV. One proof approximates an arbitrary event by finite-coordinate events in `L1((P+Q)/2)` using the increasing sigma fields. Hence no hidden accumulation in the infinitely many omitted coordinates remains. Alternatively, relative entropy on the increasing product sigma fields is the supremum of the prefix entropies.

For the bit count, let `q_j=a_j/epsilon=(m/j)^alpha>=1`. Then

\[
 \frac{T_j}{h_j}
 =\epsilon q_j^2+q_j\sqrt{2\ln(8q_j^2)}.
 \tag{12}
\]

For `q>=1`, `ln q<=(q^2-1)/2` gives `sqrt(2ln(8q^2))<=sqrt(2ln8)q`. With `epsilon<=1`, the scalar label count satisfies

\[
 L_j=M_j+2\le10 q_j^2.
 \tag{13}
\]

The archive dimension is `D_cl=product_{j<=k} L_j`. Since

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^k\ln q_j
   =\alpha\{k\ln m-\ln(k!)\}\le\alpha k,
 \tag{14}
\]

we obtain the explicit uniform bound

\[
 \ln D_{\rm cl}\le(\ln10+2\alpha)k.
 \tag{15}
\]

For (14), use `ln(k!)>=k ln k-k+1` and `k ln(m/k)<=1` when `k=floor m`. A mixed-radix index needs `ceil(log_2 D_cl)` binary bits. Separate coordinate fields add at most `k` rounding bits. There is no exponentially sized codebook to acquire: the public cell grids are described by (5), (10), and the coordinate index.

The potentially large `a_1=A` is harmless for uniformity. Its interval count contains `epsilon q_1^2`, which is bounded by `q_1^2` in the asserted precision regime. The sum of logarithmic per-axis costs is linear in `m`, rather than `m log m`.

## 4. Explicit sparse converse with fixed `tanh` geometry

Consider a legal code on the finite family

\[
 \{+r e_i,-r e_i:1\le i\le d\},\qquad 0<r\le1.
 \tag{16}
\]

All additional coordinates of the infinite observation, if present, have the null law under this family. They can be integrated into randomized encoding of the first `d` coordinates. No finite-dimensional source premise is hidden here.

### 4.1 A bounded proper loss

For posterior probabilities `p in (0,1)`, define

\[
 u_r(p)=\tanh\left(\frac{\operatorname{artanh}(2p-1)}r\right),
 \qquad
 F_r(p)=\int_{1/2}^{p}u_r(t)\,dt.
 \tag{17}
\]

Extend `u_r` by `u_r(0)=-1,u_r(1)=1`. Then `F_r` is convex, symmetric about `1/2`, and `c_r=F_r(0)=F_r(1)`. For a true binary label `s in {0,1}` and action `q in [0,1]`, use

\[
 \ell_r(s,q)=c_r-F_r(q)-(s-q)u_r(q).
 \tag{18}
\]

Both losses lie in `[0,1]`. For `s=0`, their derivative is `q F_r''(q)>=0`, and the endpoint values are zero and one. For `s=1`, the derivative is `-(1-q)F_r''(q)<=0`, with endpoint values one and zero. Given posterior `p`, excess Bayes loss is the Bregman divergence

\[
 D_{F_r}(p,q)=F_r(p)-F_r(q)-(p-q)u_r(q).
 \tag{19}
\]

The inverse link is

\[
 p(u)=\frac{1+\tanh(r\operatorname{artanh}u)}2,
 \qquad
 \frac{dp}{du}= \frac r2
  \frac{\operatorname{sech}^2(r\operatorname{artanh}u)}{1-u^2}
  \ge\frac r2.
 \tag{20}
\]

The inequality uses `r<=1` and `sech^2(artanh u)=1-u^2`. In either order of `p,q`, change variables in the Bregman integral; (20) proves

\[
 D_{F_r}(p,q)\ge\frac r4\{u_r(p)-u_r(q)\}^2.
 \tag{21}
\]

This is the load-bearing weak-signal step. A fixed Brier loss would give an `r^2` coefficient and would not prove the claimed uniform amplitude rate. The loss (18) has curvature concentrated on the posterior scale `r` while remaining bounded.

### 4.2 Convert law preservation to simultaneous score distortion

For each coordinate `i` separately, use equal prior on the two means `+/-r e_i`, with binary sign `s`. Its posterior given `X` is

\[
 p_r(X_i)=\frac{1+\tanh(rX_i)}2,
 \qquad u_r(p_r(X_i))=\tanh X_i.
 \tag{22}
\]

Apply the rule `q=p_r(Y_i)` to the decoded observation. Parameterwise TV at most `epsilon`, together with `0<=ell<=1`, changes this rule's average risk by at most `epsilon`. The original rule `q=p_r(X_i)` is Bayes optimal. Since the encoder is blind, `s -> X -> Y` is a Markov chain, including randomized encoding and independent shared seeds. Equations (19)--(22) imply

\[
 \mathbb E_{M_i}(\tanh X_i-\tanh Y_i)^2
       \le4\epsilon/r,
 \tag{23}
\]

where `M_i=(P_{+r e_i}+P_{-r e_i})/2` is the input mixture, and the expectation uses the original-input/decoded-output coupling induced by the code.

Relative to the *same* full null law `P_0`,

\[
 \frac{dM_i}{dP_0}(X)
    =e^{-r^2/2}\cosh(rX_i)\ge e^{-1/2}.
 \tag{24}
\]

Thus all coordinates simultaneously satisfy, under one common null coupling,

\[
 R:=\mathbb E_{P_0}\sum_{i=1}^d
      (\tanh X_i-\tanh Y_i)^2
       \le4e^{1/2}d\epsilon/r.
 \tag{25}
\]

The original null law need not itself meet the reconstruction criterion. It is only the common dominating input measure used to add the independent score directions.

### 4.3 Finite dimension bounds accessible source information

For completeness, a direct trace proof supplies the needed information ceiling without any delicate continuous-ensemble entropy extension. Let `sigma_x` be the retained density matrix on the counted `D`-dimensional space, and let the decoder POVM be `M(dy)`. Because `sigma_x<=I`, its output kernel obeys

\[
 K_x(dy)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_xM(dy))
          \le\mu(dy):=\operatorname{Tr}M(dy),\qquad
 \mu(\Omega)=D.
 \tag{26}
\]

Use the probability reference `nu=mu/D`. Then `dK_x/dnu<=D`, so `KL(K_x||nu)<=ln D`. Averaging and the KL chain rule give

\[
 I(X;Y)+\operatorname{KL}(P_Y\Vert\nu)\le\ln D,
 \qquad I(X;Y)\le\ln D.
 \tag{27}
\]

This applies to hybrid block-diagonal states with the total dimension `sum_b d_b`. Conditional on an independent shared seed `Z`, apply the argument to its own encoder/POVM. Equivalently use the joint reference `nu_z(dy)P_Z(dz)`. Averaging proves `I(X;Y,Z)<=ln D`; no per-seed error guarantee is needed. Additional input-independent local ancillas are absorbed into the POVM on the counted input space.

### 4.4 Entropy and the explicit constant

For `G~N(0,1)`, `U=tanh G` has finite differential entropy

\[
 h(U)=\frac12\ln(2\pi e)-2\mathbb E\ln\cosh G
      \ge\frac12\ln(2\pi e)-1,
 \tag{28}
\]

because `ln cosh x<=x^2/2`. Under the common null, the `U_i=tanh X_i` are independent. With `V_i=tanh Y_i`, the continuous squared-error information bound gives

\[
 I(U;V,Z)\ge h(U)-\frac d2\ln\left(2\pi e R/d\right)
        \ge\frac d2\ln(d/R)-d.
 \tag{29}
\]

This follows from the maximum differential entropy at fixed second moment, or directly from conditional Gaussian KL. If `R=0`, the mutual information is infinite and finite `D` is impossible. The bounded source transform makes all required source entropies finite.

Data processing, (25), (27), and (29) prove exactly (1):

\[
 \boxed{\ln D\ge\frac d2\ln(r/\epsilon)
                   -(5/4+\ln2)d.}
 \tag{30}
\]

No constant grows with dimension. No full box of mean parameters is used. The family has only `2d` possible means, but law reconstruction demands retaining a continuous score in every potential direction before its sign and axis are known.

### 4.5 Stronger converse for a later coordinate query

The same bound remains true if the decoder is weaker than a common full-sequence reconstruction: after storage it can receive an axis index `i`, perform an arbitrary `i`-dependent POVM on the archive, and return only a scalar `Y_i`. It need only approximate the scalar shifted law for the two source parameters `+/-r e_i`. No joint vector of these noncommuting measurement outcomes is assumed.

The proper-loss argument is scalar, so it proves the same averaged distortion bound for each chosen decoder. Consequently

\[
 I(U_i;Y_i,Z)\ge\frac12\ln(r/\epsilon)-(5/4+\ln2).
 \tag{30a}
\]

To bound their sum using one quantum memory, partition each `U_i=tanh X_i` by a finite dyadic grid, with label `L_i^{(n)}`. These labels are independent under the null and remain independent conditional on the input-independent seed `Z`. Finite classical-quantum information identities, strong subadditivity and the Holevo ceiling give

\[
 \sum_i I(L_i^{(n)};Q\mid Z)
 \le I(L_1^{(n)},\ldots,L_d^{(n)};Q\mid Z)
 \le\ln D.
 \tag{30b}
\]

For the first inequality, the chain rule has terms `I(L_i;Q|L_{<i},Z)=I(L_i;Q,L_{<i}|Z)>=I(L_i;Q|Z)` because the labels are independent. The last inequality is `S(E sigma)-E S(sigma)<=ln D` for the finite label ensemble in each seed branch. The measurement chosen for coordinate `i` obeys data processing, so

\[
 \sum_i I(L_i^{(n)};Y_i,Z)\le\ln D.
 \tag{30c}
\]

Let the grids refine. Classical mutual information increases to `I(U_i;Y_i,Z)` on the generated Borel sigma field. The finite sum therefore also has total at most `ln D`. Summing (30a) proves (30), without positing joint outcomes of the different POVMs. This finite-partition argument avoids any need to define or approximate the quantum conditional entropy of a continuous classical register.

This is a continuous random-access corollary of established Holevo/quantum random-access methods combined with the calibrated Gaussian loss. It is not a new information-capacity principle. `sparse_gaussian_converse` independently checked the loss, constant, and query-dependent information-chain step.

## 5. Apply the converse to the ellipsoid

Take

\[
 r=128\epsilon\le1,\qquad
 d=\left\lfloor(A/(128\epsilon))^{1/\alpha}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{31}
\]

Every needle in (16) belongs to (2), since `r^2 i^{2alpha}<=A^2` for `i<=d`. Therefore

\[
 \ln D\ge\left(\frac52\ln2-\frac54\right)d
          =0.48286795\ldots\,d.
 \tag{32}
\]

For `A/epsilon>=128`, the quantity inside the floor is at least one and `floor z>=z/2`. Consequently

\[
 B_q\ge \frac{(5/2)\ln2-5/4}{2\ln2}
             128^{-1/\alpha}(A/\epsilon)^{1/\alpha}.
 \tag{33}
\]

Together with (15), this proves the advertised rate with absolute `C_0=128`, `epsilon_0=1/128` and explicit constants depending only on `alpha`.

## 6. Observation, execution, noise, and output costs

The charged resource in (4) is retained input-dependent archive dimension. It is not a total experimental cost statement.

| Resource | What this construction actually uses |
|---|---|
| Model acquisition | None is proved. The Gaussian experiment, covariance, axes, `alpha`, `A`, and tolerance are supplied. |
| Observed coordinates | Exactly `k=floor((A/epsilon)^(1/alpha))` axis values; no tail axes are read. |
| Observation precision | The mathematical encoder receives real values and compares them with public real thresholds. This is exact-real access, not a finite-bit sensor theorem. Threshold ties have probability zero for every mean. |
| Retained classical information | `log_2 product L_j=O_alpha(k)` bits, with all bin indices counted. Separate fields cost at most another `k` bits. |
| Public decoder description | Cell formulas, null conditioner, and model parameters; it contains no observed-value information. The representation cost of arbitrary real supplied parameters is outside the abstract memory measure. |
| Encoder computation | `O(k)` grid calculations/comparisons in an ideal real-arithmetic model; no bit-running-time or arithmetic-precision claim is proved. Mixed-radix arithmetic acts on `O_alpha(k)`-bit integers and must be charged in a bit implementation. |
| First `n` decoded coordinates | `n` independent samplings, of which `min(n,k)` use truncated null normals and the rest ordinary null normals. An inverse-normal-CDF oracle gives a constant number of ideal operations per coordinate. |
| Entire output | Countably infinitely many coordinates. It is a well-defined random element of the product space, but not a finite materialized output. |
| Fresh randomness | Exact real uniforms or an equivalent infinite random-bit source. An exact normal can be generated lazily from bits; returning a fully specified real cannot finish in finite bits. |
| Quantum storage advantage | The lower covers arbitrary joint mixed-state/hybrid encoders, but only rules out an improvement in order. It does not fix optimal constants. |
| Input-dependent external record | Not preserved. Marginal law reconstruction does not preserve `X` jointly with an uncounted reference record or its purification. |

If observation acquisition is charged as individual axis queries, this order of queried coordinates is also necessary, including for randomized adaptive reading. Let `p_i` be the probability of ever reading coordinate `i` under the null. Until the first read of that coordinate, the read decision depends only on other coordinates and input-independent randomness; hence its probability is the same under both `+/-r e_i` alternatives. If it is never read, the output laws coincide. Conditional on its being read, data processing bounds their output separation by `TV(N(r,1),N(-r,1))`. Therefore

\[
 (1-p_i)\{2\Phi(r)-1\}\le2\epsilon,
 \qquad
 p_i\ge1-\frac{\epsilon}{\phi(1)r}\quad(0<r\le1).
 \tag{33a}
\]

At `r=128 epsilon`, this is at least `1-1/(128 phi(1))>0.96`. The expected number of distinct axis reads is at least `0.96d=Omega_alpha((A/epsilon)^(1/alpha))`. This claim assumes axis-query access; a model that grants arbitrary global functionals of the infinite observation at unit cost has a different acquisition interface.

A concrete null-conditional sampler for interval endpoints `l,u` is

\[
 Y=\Phi^{-1}\left(\Phi(l)+W\{\Phi(u)-\Phi(l)\}\right),
 \qquad W\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1),
 \tag{34}
\]

with `Phi(-infinity)=0,Phi(infinity)=1`. This handles the rays as well. One ideal uniform real can also encode countably many independent uniforms by splitting its random binary digits. That representation does not make its entropy or the full output finite; it identifies the supplied randomness resource.

Under finite-prefix/finite-precision output requirements, one should compare the output with the identically quantized target, or declare a metric compatible with finite precision. Under literal TV against an unquantized continuous target, every terminating digital sampler with finitely many output bits is singular to that target. The theorem itself deliberately uses continuous-output kernels.

## 7. Falsification and boundary checks

1. **Growing-dimension substitution is unnecessary.** Fixed-dimensional N30 constants could conceal a dimension blowup. The direct scalar KL estimate and the constant in (30) are independent of dimension, so the sequence theorem survives that attack.
2. **Uniform small amplitude is real.** Taking `A -> 0` does not refute (4) as long as `A/epsilon>=128`. The lower uses needles of amplitude `128 epsilon`, rather than a fixed positive amplitude. A Brier-only converse would fail this test by losing a factor of the amplitude.
3. **Small ratio is a counterexample to an ungated formula.** If `A<=sqrt(2pi) epsilon`, a one-label null decoder suffices. A claim of positive Theta bits for every nonzero `A/epsilon` would be false.
4. **The endpoint `alpha=0` fails.** The `ell_2` ball contains `+/-r e_i` for arbitrary `d` when `r<=A`. For `r/epsilon` sufficiently large, (30) forces unbounded `D`. Decaying axes, hence `alpha>0`, are essential for a finite archive.
5. **No fresh continuous randomness fails.** A deterministic decoder from a finite classical alphabet has finite support and TV one. Random decoding is essential even at the null parameter.
6. **Uncounted input dependence fails.** Keeping an analog copy of `X`, an input-dependent side register, or a correlated seed outside `D` defeats the intended resource accounting. A free seed must be independent of the observed source, not merely unspecified to the analyst.
7. **Joint reference preservation fails.** A finite archive cannot preserve the exact relation `Y=X` with an externally stored continuous observation, even if its marginal experiment is reproduced well. That is a different fidelity criterion.
8. **Known means are a different problem.** A decoder supplied with `theta` can simply simulate `P_theta` with one label. The blind common-kernel quantifiers prohibit this.

## 8. Prior art and contribution boundary

The exact claimed theorem was not located in this bounded search. This is not historical novelty clearance.

* [Hayashi and Tan, *Minimum Rates of Approximate Sufficient Statistics*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02542) studies finite retained codes, stochastic reconstruction, and KL/variational-distance error in parametric experiments. The full first-page interface and abstract were read from the locally retained source text. Its growing-sample parametric results do not by themselves prove this infinite ellipsoid precision theorem.
* [Zhu and Lafferty, *Quantized Nonparametric Estimation over Sobolev Ellipsoids*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07368) studies the same class of Gaussian sequence/Sobolev mean models under storage constraints, with squared estimation risk. The v3 text dated April 2017 was inspected. Estimating the mean under quadratic loss differs from reproducing every law of the experiment in uniform TV.
* [Koch, *The Shannon Lower Bound is Asymptotically Tight*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.08245) supplies an established information-versus-continuous-distortion framework. The inequality used in (29) is standard and is derived directly here; the exponent from rate-distortion theory is not claimed as new.
* Accessible-information ceilings for finite quantum memories are established Holevo theory. The elementary trace domination in (26)--(27) is sufficient for the common-output decoder, avoiding any appeal to a specialized continuous-ensemble theorem.

The candidate-specific mechanism is the pairing of a coordinate-adapted KL archive with a weak-signal proper loss whose fixed `tanh` coordinate transform has dimension-uniform entropy. The ellipsoid makes both the weighted KL budget and the bit sum scale as `(A/epsilon)^(1/alpha)`. All model structure is supplied. This does not establish a model-free scientific archive, learn a useful coordinate system, or solve unknown-model acquisition.

With Gaussian sequence noise standard deviation `sigma>0`, whitening replaces `A` by `A/sigma`. Therefore the same declared-model result is `B=Theta_alpha((A/(sigma epsilon))^(1/alpha))` in the corresponding precision regime. In a white-noise experiment with `sigma=n^(-1/2)`, this is `Theta_alpha((sqrt(n) A/epsilon)^(1/alpha))`. Any transfer to a different physical or statistical experiment must include its separate comparison error and acquisition costs.
