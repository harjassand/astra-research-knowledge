# The exact diamond-ball radius of depolarizing-floor absorption

16 October 2026. This sharpens a sufficient constant in the supplied modified
memory construction. It is an elementary finite-dimensional consequence of
Choi theory, not a claim of a new memory mechanism or historical novelty.

## Exact radius theorem

Let d>=2, let D(A)=Tr(A) I_d/d, and let E be a complex-linear map on d by d
matrices which is Hermiticity preserving and trace annihilating. Use normalized
Choi matrices

    J(F) = (F tensor Id_d)(|Omega><Omega|),
    |Omega> = d^(-1/2) sum_i |i,i>.

For a fixed floor rate nu>0 define N=D+E/nu. Then:

1. N is CPTP if and only if lambda_min[J(E)] >= -nu/d^2.
2. The sufficient diamond-norm condition is

       ||E||_diamond <= 2 nu/d^2.                              (S1)

3. The radius 2nu/d^2 is optimal among diamond balls containing ALL
   Hermiticity-preserving, trace-annihilating maps E. Every strictly larger
   radius contains an E for which N is not CP.

The producer uses nu/d^2, which is valid but conservative by exactly a factor
of two for this uniform diamond-ball problem. This correction does not change
the poor fixed-patch dimension scaling.

## Proof of sufficiency and the directional criterion

Hermiticity preservation makes J(E) Hermitian. Trace annihilation implies

    Tr_output J(E) = 0,       Tr J(E) = 0.                    (S2)

For any traceless Hermitian matrix X, the positive and negative parts have
equal trace, namely ||X||_1/2. Hence

    -lambda_min(X) <= ||X||_1/2.

The normalized Choi input is a density operator of trace norm one, so directly
from the diamond-norm definition,

    ||J(E)||_1 <= ||E||_diamond.

Consequently

    J(N) = I_(d^2)/d^2 + J(E)/nu
         >= [1/d^2 - ||E||_diamond/(2nu)] I_(d^2).            (S3)

This proves Choi positivity under (S1). The partial trace of J(N) is I_d/d by
(S2), so N is trace preserving. Conversely, since J(D) is a scalar matrix,
its positivity is exactly the minimum-eigenvalue condition in assertion 1.

For completeness, a positive normalized Choi matrix has a decomposition
J(N)=sum_j |v_j><v_j|. Reshape sqrt(d) v_j into matrices B_j. Comparison on
matrix units gives N(A)=sum_j B_j A B_j^dagger, and the partial-trace condition
gives sum_j B_j^dagger B_j=I. Thus the used positivity criterion supplies an
explicit finite Kraus construction. It does not depend on a convention switch
between normalized and unnormalized Choi matrices.

The generator identity is exact on all operators and with every reference:

    nu(D-Id)+E = nu(N-Id).                                    (S4)

As in the producer, the right side is a rate-nu local CPTP proposal generator.
No approximation of a coherent generator by a Pauli twirl is involved.

## Proof that the ball radius is optimal

First establish

    ||Id-D||_diamond = 2(1-1/d^2).                            (S5)

One proof uses the d-dimensional Weyl unitaries W_(a,b). Their uniform twirl is
D, and W_(0,0)=I. The channel triangle inequality gives

    ||Id-D||_diamond
      <= (1-1/d^2)||Id||_diamond
         + (1/d^2) sum_(a,b)!=(0,0) ||Ad_W(a,b)||_diamond
      = 2(1-1/d^2).

The Weyl-twirl equality can be checked on matrix units: averaging the diagonal
phase kills off-diagonal units, then averaging the cyclic shift sends each
diagonal unit to I/d. Applying Id-D to half of |Omega><Omega| gives

    |Omega><Omega| - I_(d^2)/d^2,

whose trace norm is 2(1-1/d^2). This is the matching lower bound, proving (S5).

Now set

    E_a = a(D-Id),       a>=0.

This E_a is Hermiticity preserving and trace annihilating. Its norm is
2a(1-1/d^2). The normalized Choi matrix of D+E_a/nu has eigenvalues

    1/d^2 - (a/nu)(1-1/d^2),       on |Omega>,
    (1+a/nu)/d^2,                 on its orthogonal complement.

At a=nu/(d^2-1) the first eigenvalue is zero, the others are positive, and
||E_a||_diamond=2nu/d^2. At any strictly larger a it is negative. Choosing a
arbitrarily close above that boundary proves that no larger uniform ball is
contained in the CPTP region. For d=1 the trace-annihilating map space is zero;
the nontrivial radius assertion is deliberately stated for d>=2.

This witness proves failure of the fixed-rate representation N=D+E/nu, not
failure of every possible representation or of memory: E_a=a(D-Id) simply
increases a legitimate depolarizing rate. There is also a separate sharp
local-generator statement. Choose a trace-zero unitary W, for example the
cyclic shift on d levels, and E_a=a(D-Ad_W). Its norm is again
2a(1-1/d^2), by composing the difference in (S5) with a unitary channel.
Let |Omega_W>=(W tensor I)|Omega>, which is orthogonal to |Omega>. For
G=nu(D-Id)+E_a,

    <Omega_W|J(G)|Omega_W>
        = nu/d^2 - a(1-1/d^2).

This is negative above the same boundary a=nu/(d^2-1). If exp(tG) were CP
for all sufficiently small t>0, its Choi matrix would have expectation
t<Omega_W|J(G)|Omega_W>+O(t^2)>=0 in that vector, an impossibility. Thus no
larger ball of ALL HP trace-annihilating directions can even guarantee that
the floor-perturbed generator is GKSL. The radius 2nu/d^2 is exact for that
stronger neighborhood question as well. This worst direction need not itself
be a physical positive-rate perturbation.

## Exact Hamiltonian-specific absorption criterion

For a Hermitian local Hamiltonian H, let E_H=-i[H,.], and write

    mu_H = Tr(H)/d,
    sigma_H^2 = Tr(H^2)/d - mu_H^2.

Let |h>=[(H-mu_H I) tensor I]|Omega>. Then <Omega|h>=0 and ||h||=sigma_H.
Directly,

    J(E_H) = -i( |h><Omega| - |Omega><h| ).                  (S6)

If sigma_H>0, in the orthonormal basis |Omega>, |h>/sigma_H the only nonzero
block is

    [ 0          i sigma_H ]
    [-i sigma_H       0    ].

Its eigenvalues are plus and minus sigma_H; all other eigenvalues are zero.
For sigma_H=0 the commutator vanishes. Therefore

    D + E_H/nu is CPTP  iff  nu >= d^2 sigma_H.              (S7)

This is the exact criterion for absorbing this Hamiltonian direction into a
complete depolarizing floor at rate nu. It can be sharper than any bound that
uses only ||H|| or its spectral diameter. Subtracting any scalar from H leaves
both the commutator and sigma_H unchanged.

For comparison, ||E_H||_diamond=diam(spectrum H). The upper bound follows by
subtracting the midpoint of the extreme eigenvalues and using
||[H,A]||_1<=2||H|| ||A||_1, also after adjoining a reference. The matching
lower bound is attained on a superposition of the extreme eigenvectors.
Popoviciu's elementary variance bound gives sigma_H<=diam(H)/2, so (S1) implies
(S7). Equality occurs for balanced two-point spectra, including a qubit.

For a general E the directional condition in assertion 1 is exact and may be
used directly instead of a diamond-norm bound when its local Choi matrix is
supplied. Constructing that matrix, estimating its eigenvalue, acquiring H,
or calibrating a physical generator are separate costs, not furnished here.

## Variable proposal rate: a stronger Hamiltonian tolerance

The fixed-rate condition (S7) is not the best use of a floor when the allowed
perturbations are Hamiltonians. Keep the implemented generator

    G_H = nu(D-Id) - i[H,.],

but seek a channel proposal representation at rate gamma, which need not equal
nu. The unique proposed channel is N_gamma=Id+G_H/gamma. In the span of
|Omega> and |h>/sigma_H its normalized Choi matrix has block

    [ 1-nu/gamma+nu/(gamma d^2)    i sigma_H/gamma ]
    [ -i sigma_H/gamma             nu/(gamma d^2) ].         (S10)

On the orthogonal complement its eigenvalue is nu/(gamma d^2)>0. The lower
diagonal entry in (S10) is positive, so block positivity is equivalent to a
nonnegative determinant. After multiplying by gamma^2, this is

    nu gamma/d^2 - nu^2(1-1/d^2)/d^2 - sigma_H^2 >= 0.

Consequently the exact minimum rate is

    gamma_min(H,nu)
       = nu(1-1/d^2) + d^2 sigma_H^2/nu.                    (S11)

The sigma_H=0 case follows directly from the Choi matrix and satisfies the
same formula. Every gamma>=gamma_min gives an exact CPTP proposal, and every
smaller positive gamma fails CP. In particular, any finite H can be
uniformized after a positive full-patch floor, but the charged proposal rate
may exceed the memory's allowed rare-fault budget.

This minimum is within state-independent finite-rate CPTP channel-proposal
representations of the displayed generator. It is not a physical lower bound
on reservoir operation rates, energy, reset or implementation resources.

Let r be the total proposal rate allotted to this patch type. The condition
gamma_min<=r is

    sigma_H^2 <= (nu/d^2)[r-nu(1-1/d^2)].                  (S12)

Optimizing the implemented floor rate in this sufficient construction gives

    nu_opt = r/[2(1-1/d^2)],
    sigma_H <= r/[2 d sqrt(1-1/d^2)].                      (S13)

The nominal floor itself is deliberately present; its actual rate nu_opt is
less than r for d>=2. At the worst permitted Hamiltonian, the entire floor
plus drift is represented by rate-r proposals, so its full hazard is charged.
This changes the worst balanced-spectrum Hamiltonian scale from d^-2 under
the producer's fixed-rate absorption to d^-1. It is still potentially an
extremely poor dimension dependence for a large patch.

For a spectral-diameter promise, sigma_H<=diam(H)/2 yields the sufficient
condition diam(H)<=r/[d sqrt(1-1/d^2)]. The exact variance promise (S13) can
be less restrictive for unbalanced spectra. Each patch type can be assigned
its own floor nu_a and fixed proposal budget r_a, with sum_a r_a below the
classical per-cell threshold. The same schedule theorem then covers arbitrary
spatial Hamiltonians obeying (S12), or bounded measurable deterministic
time-varying Hamiltonians with that inequality uniform in time. The rate-r_a
proposal law is fixed; the instantaneous channel may vary with H(t).

This improvement concerns a restricted Hamiltonian class. It does not enlarge
the sharp diamond ball for ALL HP trace-annihilating perturbations proved
above. No d^-1 tolerance to arbitrary changes of correction dissipators is
being inferred.

More generally, let E be HP and trace annihilating, put P=|Omega><Omega| and
Q=I-P, and set

    a = nu/d^2 + <Omega|J(E)|Omega>,
    b = Q J(E)|Omega>,
    B = (nu/d^2)Q + Q J(E) Q.

The Choi matrix of Id+[nu(D-Id)+E]/gamma, multiplied by gamma, has block
[[gamma-nu+a,b^dagger],[b,B]]. Hence a finite-rate CPTP representation exists
exactly when B>=0, b is in the range of B, and some gamma>0 satisfies

    gamma >= nu-a + b^dagger B^+ b.                        (S14)

Here B^+ is the inverse on its range. This is the elementary positive-block
Schur-complement criterion: complete a square on the range of B, and test
kernel vectors to obtain the range condition. With B>0 the range condition
is automatic. If the right side is nonpositive, every positive gamma meeting
the displayed condition works. For Hamiltonians, a=nu/d^2, B=(nu/d^2)Q and
||b||=sigma_H, giving (S11). Formula (S14) is a supplied-local-matrix criterion;
it does not acquire a physical generator or bypass the charged gamma budget.

## Conditional memory consequence

In the producer's deliberately completed and depolarized baseline, replace
the sufficient per-patch condition by

    ||E_(x,a)||_diamond <= 2 nu_a/d_a^2.                     (S8)

The total charged per-cell proposal rate sum_a nu_a and all fixed patch radii
remain unchanged. Equations (S3)-(S4) put the perturbed generator in the same
arbitrary-local-CPTP proposal class. The same universal schedule certificate,
if its independent classical tail is correct, then yields the same bound

    ||Dec exp(TG) Enc-Id||_diamond
      <= min(2,A L^4 (1+kappa T) exp(-cL)).                  (S9)

This is a larger sufficient local-norm neighborhood of the MODIFIED baseline.
It says nothing new about robustness of the original active-jump generator or
the completed corrector without a floor.

The local operator step also allows arbitrary bounded measurable deterministic
time dependence E_(x,a)(t), with (S8) holding almost everywhere. At a fixed-rate
proposal at time t apply N_(x,a)(t)=D_a+E_(x,a)(t)/nu_a. Averaging the proposal
circuit gives the time-ordered evolution for G(t). The schedule law and its
good event are unchanged, so the same uniform bound follows for the resulting
propagator. This is tolerance to temporal drift of an autonomous baseline,
not a claim that the perturbed generator is time independent.

For quantum-bath-valued continuous generator perturbations, installing a
memory-only floor does not provide a full Choi interior on memory tensor bath:
J(D_memory tensor Id_bath) is singular for a nontrivial bath. The finite memory
criterion above cannot be silently applied to such joint generators. The
separate persistent-bath theorem applies to sparse CPTP interactions, with a
fresh independent proposal-location law, not all continuous bath couplings.
