# A three-candidate law class: shrinking the output can destroy localization

7 October2026. Independently derived from the definition of offset complexity. Historical novelty UNKNOWN. The recent starting paper is Block–Rakhlin–Sellke (ICML2026); the strongest older control is the star estimator, credited to Audibert2007 and analyzed for general classes by Liang–Rakhlin–Sridharan2015. Audibert's original paper was not read here. No statistical gain or general representation acquisition is claimed.

## Question and supplied interface

Can a symbolic-law discovery method transfer a localized squared-loss certificate through a stable postprocessor by merely symmetrizing its discrete candidate library, or by replacing it with its convex hull at constant complexity cost?

Let X have one point and let F={0,+1,-1}, three constant hypotheses, on any n supplied covariates. Let epsilon_i be independent fair signs and S_n=sum epsilon_i. At parameter eta>0 define

    R_eta(F)=n^{-1} E max_{f in F}[S_n f - eta n f^2].

The operator ell(u)=u/2 is centered and globally1/2-Lipschitz. F is finite, symmetric, contains zero, is bounded by1 and satisfies any ordinary finite-class/Donsker requirement. It is not convex. There is no adaptive sampling, parameter-fitting uncertainty, hidden operator offset, dummy point or complicated syntax.

## O1: exact quantities

Direct maximization over the three candidates gives

    R_eta(F)=E[(|S_n|-eta n)_+]/n,
    R_eta(ell F)=E[(|S_n|/2-eta n/4)_+]/n.

Thus an output contraction lowers both the noise correlation and the quadratic penalty, and the second effect changes the relevant tail threshold from eta n to eta n/2. For small positive eta and large n the latter tail can be exponentially more likely. Standard un-offset Rademacher contraction does hold: R_0(ell F)=R_0(F)/2. It cannot be substituted for localized contraction.

## O2: a universal exponential obstruction, proved without simulation

Take 0<eta<=1/4 and require 3 eta n/4 to be a possible simple-walk endpoint, i.e. k=n/2+3 eta n/8 is an integer. Chernoff's elementary bound gives

    R_eta(F)<=P(|S_n|>eta n)<=2 exp(-eta^2 n/2).

On S_n=3 eta n/4 the contracted integrand divided by n equals eta/8. Write p=k/n=1/2+3eta/8. The binomial probability of this endpoint is at least exp(-n D(p||1/2))/(n+1): under Bernoulli(p), k is a mode, so its mass is at least1/(n+1), and changing back to parameter1/2 multiplies it by exp(-nD).

For x=2p-1=3eta/4,

    D(p||1/2)=sum_{j>=1} x^(2j)/[(2j)(2j-1)]
              <=x^2/2+x^4/[12(1-x^2)]
              <(3/10) eta^2.

The final uniform rational check uses eta^2<=1/16 and

    9/32 + 81/(192*247) < 3/10.

Consequently

    R_eta(ell F)/R_eta(F)
        >= eta exp(eta^2 n/5)/[16(n+1)].

The denominator is strictly positive because the all-positive/all-negative sign strings give positive integrands for eta<1. For eta=1/4 and n divisible by32 this is exp(n/80)/[64(n+1)]. A weaker exp(9n/800) inequality was the first reconstructed version; the displayed uniform bound is stronger.

This is not merely failure at a forbidden large eta. Let h>=4 be an integer, n_h=64h2^h and eta_h=1/(8h). The endpoint integrality condition holds, and log n_h<=4h^2, hence eta_h<=(4 sqrt(log n_h))^{-1}, the recent positive theorem's eta regime. Yet

    ratio >= exp(2^h/(5h))/[128h(n_h+1)],

which grows faster than any power of log n_h (indeed faster than any fixed power of n_h). Only convexity among that theorem's stated class assumptions is missing. This is an independent transparent reconstruction of a necessity boundary; the recent paper already claims lack-of-convexity obstructions, so do not call the boundary historically new.

## O3: convexifying is a real statistical completion cost

The convex symmetric hull is the interval of constant functions [-1,1]. For each sign string its optimizer is

    t_hat=clip(S_n/(2 eta n),-1,1).

Using the feasible point t=S_n/n yields

    (1-eta)/n <= R_eta(conv F) <= 1/(4 eta n),

because E S_n^2=n and the unconstrained real quadratic optimum is S_n^2/(4eta n). Combining with O2 shows

    R_eta(conv F)/R_eta(F)
       >= (1-eta) exp(eta^2 n/2)/(2n).

Thus a one-dimensional convex completion can raise this localized functional by an exponential factor. No large number of new coordinates is needed. Although a convex hull of a finite class has the SAME un-offset Rademacher complexity (linear maximization), its offset complexity is different because of the quadratic term. A covering-dimension or syntax-size statement cannot remove this charge.

The recent convex/symmetric positive theorem can be applied to conv F if its other hypotheses hold, but only with the completed class's complexity. It does not recover a bound in terms of the original tiny R_eta(F).

## O4: competent same-information regression controls

Consider n iid labels Y_i in{-1,+1}, each sign equally likely, at the one covariate. The population regression mean is0. Both learners receive the same labels and the same three-candidate input library; the mean0 is used to evaluate this exact discriminator, not passed to either learner as an oracle.

Proper ERM over F picks +1 if S_n/n>1/2, -1 if S_n/n<-1/2, and0 otherwise, breaking boundary ties toward0. Its expected excess squared risk above the best candidate0 is

    E_proper=P(|S_n|>n/2).

Proper ERM over ell F uses thresholds1/4 and emits +/-1/2, so

    E_shrink=(1/4)P(|S_n|>n/4).

These are actual learning rules on the same iid data. Stable shrinking can worsen this discrete decision at this population because it lowers the separation from0. The exact binomial calculations are distribution integration, not noisy training experiments or a universal worse-risk theorem.

The older2015 star estimator first finds a proper ERM, then minimizes over its star hull with F. Here that star hull is always [-1,1], for ANY initial ERM choice, so the second stage returns the ordinary sample mean S_n/n. Its expected excess risk is exactly1/n. A direct projected sample-mean algorithm returns the same number with O(n) additions and one division, as does one-dimensional quadratic minimization; neither needs our contraction-audit Bellman search. This competent comparator is the appropriate control for a convex/improper repair. At this zero-mean population it forfeits the discrete library's exponential proper-selection benefit. Other populations and minimax objectives can favor improper aggregation; no blanket rejection of the star estimator is intended.

The primary2015 analysis also explicitly charges a larger difference/star class H=F-f*+star(F-F), rather than certifying the estimator with the raw F offset. Its finite-dictionary discussion notes that the star hull is richer and this bound can be crude. The present three-point construction makes that completion-cost distinction exact; it does not discover the estimator or its general assumption boundary.

All exact controls expose the full candidate library in advance. Obtaining scientifically correct candidate laws, a data/noise law or a causal experiment remains unsolved by this discriminator.

## Relation to the first starting paper

Kocabay–Akkuş–Yalçın2606.29331v1 studies iid UN-offset Rademacher complexity. Its Lemma4.6's max<=sum finite-union step requires pointwise nonnegative component suprema, e.g. a common zero function in every component; it is false for arbitrary singleton components. Centering exp/cos separately is not a fixed translation of the whole union. The ordinary finite-union remedy adds an envelope*sqrt(log vocabulary/n) term; Nomura–Hamano–Ono2604.17402v2 Lemma1 independently states/proves that standard remedy. These source audits are retained separately; neither invalidates valid iid contraction nor promotes its empirical finite-batch slopes to adaptive/offset certificates.
