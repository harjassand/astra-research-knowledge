CYCLE10: SQUARE SPARSE HOLES, TRACE AND SIMULTANEOUS COERCIVITY
Internal reconstruction; finite square-grid statement; 2026-10-09.
Premises and source scope: TARGET_AND_PREMISES.txt. No reciprocal weights.

1. HOLE GEOMETRY AND A ONE-SITE TAIL
Let B be a finite random subset of Z^2 with
  P(A subset B) <= rho^|A|                             (1)
for every finite A. Choose rho below a fixed square-grid animal-counting
threshold. No independence, stationarity or conditional version of (1)
is needed. Star adjacency means l-infinity distance one.

Take the star-connected components A of B. Form H(A) by adjoining all
bounded nearest-neighbor components of Z^2 minus A. Retain only the
outermost hulls. They are disjoint, have no star-adjacent sites belonging
to different retained hulls, and each is contained in the bounding
rectangle of its original bad component. If d is its l-infinity diameter,
  |H| <= (d+1)^2,    number of entering edges <=4(d+1)^2.    (2)

Here are the elementary square-grid contour facts used in this construction.
The external nearest-neighbor boundary of a filled hull consists of sites
of its original bad component. A bounded complementary site cannot be
adjacent to the unbounded component without contradicting its definition.
After adjoining the diagonal corner sites outside H, the exterior layer
  R(H)={z outside H: dist_infinity(z,H)<=1}
is made of good sites and connects every exterior endpoint of an entering
edge by nearest-neighbor paths of length <=C(d+1)^2. This is a discrete
contour assertion, not an assumption about Euclidean boundary regularity.
To see it, trace the outer sides of the unit squares centered at H. At a
convex turn two side-adjacent exterior sites are joined through the exterior
corner square; at a concave turn they coincide or are neighbors. At a
diagonal touching vertex, continue around the outer face, thereby resolving
the two possible branches without entering a bounded complementary face.
Each outer side and corner is traversed a bounded number of times. There
are at most 4|H| sides, and all sites so visited are in the displayed layer.
Thus a nearest-neighbor rim walk contains every entering-edge endpoint
and has length <=C|H|. A layer site diagonally adjacent only to an originally
filled complementary site is still adjacent to an original boundary site:
otherwise a two-step free path would connect that bounded complement to
the exterior. Since A is a maximal star-component, every rim site is good.

Two distinct components cannot cross each other's outer contours: crossing
requires bad sites at star distance one, which would join the components.
Consequently overlapping hulls are nested. After retaining outermost hulls,
two hulls cannot touch even diagonally for the same boundary reason. Rims
may overlap, but any rim site is within star distance one of an original
bad site of that hull. At most eight distinct components can therefore
use one rim site; rim-edge multiplicity is bounded by a fixed constant.
These facts also show that the finite construction commutes with grid
translations; choosing a lexicographically first rim site is allowed.

Define d_z as the largest diameter of a retained hull containing z or
having a site within l-infinity distance two of z, and zero if none.
This fixed incidence radius conveniently includes corner and rim edges.
For e={x,y}, put d_e=max(d_x,d_y).

If d_z>=r, an original star-animal of size m>=r/C has a vertex within
C(m+1) of z: hull diameter is at most twice its component's diameter, the
component diameter is at most m, and z is in or next to its bounding box.
For each m there are O((m+1)^2) possible roots. The number of star-animals
of m sites at a root is at most C8^m; for example a depth-first traversal
of a spanning tree gives C8=64 up to an immaterial fixed prefactor.
Using (1) and a union bound gives
  P(d_z>=r) <= sum_{m>=r/C} C(m+1)^2 (C8 rho)^m
             <= C exp(-c r).                         (3)
Constants are uniform in the volume, deterministic pin collars and z.
In particular E(1+d_z)^q <=C_q for every fixed finite q. No claim of
independence of neighboring d_z, or polynomial spatial concentration,
is used below.

2. DETERMINISTIC FILLING WITH LOCALIZED ENERGY
Let u be a globally K-Lipschitz field on the coarse square grid. Outside
the retained hulls leave it unchanged; inside a hull set v equal to u at
the lexicographically first exterior endpoint of an entering edge, on
its good rim. Then
  |u(z)-v(z)| <= C K(1+d_z).                         (4)
Internal hull edges have zero v-gradient. On an entering edge the
difference is telescoped along a good rim path to the selected site.
For 1<p<2, |sum_{i=1}^m t_i|^p<=m^(p-1) sum_i |t_i|^p.
The path bound and entering-edge count in (2), and bounded rim overlap,
therefore give
  sum_{e in S} |grad v(e)|^p
       <= sum_{e good in S+} W_e |grad u(e)|^p,
  W_e <= C_p (1+d_e)^(2p).                          (5)
Here S is a coordinate square, S+ a fixed larger square, and the statement
is used on the event that every hull meeting S (or its neighboring sites)
and its rim are contained in S+. A good edge means both endpoints lie
outside all retained hulls. Indeed a given hull contributes at most
C(d+1)^(2p) to a rim-edge coefficient, and only boundedly many hull rims
use that edge. All required rim edges remain inside S+ on this event.

Put E=sum_{e good in S+}|grad u(e)|^2. Holder with exponents 2/p and
2/(2-p) yields the exact useful form
  ||grad v||_{p,S}
    <= C_p sqrt(E)
       [sum_{e in S+} (1+d_e)^(4p/(2-p))]^((2-p)/(2p)). (6)
For p=3/2 the hole exponent is TWELVE and the outside power is 1/6:
  ||grad v||_{3/2,S}
      <= C sqrt(E) [sum_{e in S+}(1+d_e)^12]^(1/6).   (7)

If S and S+ have side comparable to N and separation at least cN, (3)
implies E sum_{S+}(1+d_e)^12<=CN^2. A Markov bound makes this sum at most
A N^2 except on a fixed arbitrarily small probability. The chance that
a hull meeting S reaches outside S+ is <=C N^2 exp(-cN), by (3) and a
union over O(N^2) deterministic sites. For sufficiently large N it too
has arbitrarily small probability. On the resulting geometry event,
  ||grad v||_{3/2,S} <= C N^(1/3) sqrt(E).            (8)
The event depends ONLY on B, not on u; (4)-(8) hold simultaneously for
every globally K-Lipschitz u. This simultaneity will be essential.

3. TRACE AGAINST A SPREAD MEASURE
Let S have side comparable to N, nu be a positive measure on its vertices
with mass <=C, and for all x and 0<=r<=CN assume
  nu(B_infinity(x,r)) <= C ((r+1)/N)^alpha.           (9)
For 1<p<2 and alpha>2-p,
  |nu(v)-nu(1) mean_S(v)|
       <= C N^(1-2/p) ||grad v||_{p,S}.              (10)

Proof. Telescoping the averages over dyadically growing, boundary-clipped
rectangles around x, using discrete L1 Poincare at each scale, gives
  |v(x)-mean_S(v)|
       <= C sum_{e in S}|grad v(e)|/(1+dist(x,e)).
At scale r=1,2,4,...,O(N), the nu-averaged kernel is bounded by
F_r(e)=C r^(-1) nu(B(e,Cr)). Its supremum is <=C N^(-alpha)r^(alpha-1);
its l1 norm is <=Cr, since each nu-site sees O(r^2) edges. For
p'=p/(p-1), interpolation of l-infinity and l1 gives
  ||F_r||_{p'} <= C N^(-alpha/p) r^(1+(alpha-2)/p).
The dyadic series is bounded by C N^(1-2/p) exactly when its r exponent
is positive, namely alpha>2-p. Holder proves (10). Boundary clipping
preserves comparable areas/aspect ratios, so constants are uniform.

For weights |w_z|<=C N^(-2), sum|w_z|<=C, (9) holds with alpha=2.
A nearest-neighbor connected pin set of diameter >=cN in S carries a
probability measure with alpha=1: one coordinate projection has length
>=c'N, connectivity fills every intermediate integer coordinate, and
choose one pin above each such coordinate with equal weights. A ball of
radius r sees at most 2r+1 selected coordinates. Both alpha=2 and alpha=1
meet alpha>2-p for p=3/2.

4. COERCIVITY, INCLUDING PINS THAT ARE OVERWRITTEN BY FILLING
Let X be any globally K-Lipschitz coarse field with K fixed, and put
u=T_M(X), T_M(t)=sign(t)(|t|-M)_+, with M fixed (M=0 is permitted).
Let deterministic real w be supported in S, with sum|w|<=C and
max|w|<=C N^(-2). Suppose either
 (a) sum w=0, or
 (b) u=0 on a deterministic connected coarse pin set of diameter >=cN
     within S. Let nu be the probability measure constructed above.

There is a B-determined event of probability >=1/2 on which, for EVERY
such X simultaneously,
  |sum_z w_z X_z| <= C0+C1 sqrt(E),                  (11)
with E the good-edge energy of u in the larger square S+.
Proof: Choose the threshold in (7) so its failure probability is <=1/8,
and exclude buffer-crossing hulls with failure <=1/8. Equation (4), the
maximum weight bound and the same moment event imply
  sum_z |w_z| |u_z-v_z| <=constant.
Truncation costs at most M sum|w|. Formula (10) applied to |w| and (8)
has scale N^(-1/3) N^(1/3)=1. In case (a), the mean cancels EXACTLY,
giving |sum wv|<=C sqrt(E).
In case (b), filling can overwrite a pin, but (4) still gives
  |nu(v)| <=C K sum_z nu(z)(1+d_z).
The right side has uniformly bounded expectation by (3). A separate
Markov threshold with failure <=1/8 bounds it by a deterministic constant.
Formula (10) applied to nu gives |mean_S(v)|<=constant+C sqrt(E).
Applying (10) to the signed w, dominated by |w|, then bounds |sum wv|
by the same expression. Total failure is <=3/8, hence (11) holds on an
event of probability at least 1/2. It is independent of X and the chosen
fillings. Fixed small N is handled directly from Lipschitzness and either
exact cancellation or the nearby anchor; the next proof gives that case.

This is an UNRESCALED estimate with a bounded additive pin error. It is
not a vanishing rescaled boundary trace, an inverse-conductance estimate,
or a scaling theorem. Only the one-site exponential hole tail and Markov
are required; the stronger spatial claims of the source f:hole are not
imported or claimed here.
