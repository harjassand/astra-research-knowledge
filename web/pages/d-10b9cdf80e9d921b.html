Abstract
Scientific discovery often begins when scat-
tered clues call for a new way of describing
the world. Such abductive exploration can re-
quire constructing the representation in which
an explanation is stated, when the world isepis-
temically open, and composing evidence scat-
tered across contexts, when it isstructurally
interconnected. Existing benchmarks rarely
separate these two demands or control them
independently. We introduce ULTRADISCOV-
ERY, an interactive world of five domains in
which an agent revises an initially successful
theory and predicts the outcome of an unseen
cross-domain intervention. A 2×2 design
leaves the representation open or discloses it,
and leaves the evidence distributed or aligns
it, with the latent dynamics fixed. With the
representation open, agents across eleven mod-
els often retract the axiom they were taught,
and none introduces the unobserved entity or
rewrites the variables that a replacement re-
quires. Disclosure triples intervention requests
and adds about one of the eighteen findings the
world affords, and alignment adds less. Two
vendor-harness systems carry discovery into
more domains, and one of them rewrites the
variables in Open episodes. No system makes
the exact prediction within 200 paid actions.
At larger budgets one exact prediction appears
with both aids, while every Open episode re-
mains inexact. The results locate the difficulty
in the step from accumulating evidence to com-
posing it into a representation that transfers.

2 Related work
2.1 Interactive scientific discovery
benchmarks
Most interactive discovery benchmarks specify the
entities, variables, or experiment interface before
interaction, or constrain solutions to a predefined
hypothesis family (Wang et al., 2022; Jansen et al.,
2024; Majumder et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2026;
Wiemann et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026). Recent
benchmarks relax parts of this setup, but typically
entangle representation formation with exploration
and planning, leave the organization of evidence
across contexts uncontrolled, or evaluate hypothe-
sis construction outside a shared interactive world
(ARC Prize Foundation, 2026; Warrier et al., 2026;
Chen et al., 2025a; Zhong et al., 2026; Ju et al.,
2026; Mittal et al., 2026). ULTRADISCOVERYin-
stead varies representational openness and evidence
organization independently within the same latent
world.
2.2 Representation formation and abduction
Cognitive accounts of scientific discovery distin-
guish searching over hypotheses and experiments
from choosing how observations are represented
(Klahr and Dunbar, 1988; Schunn and Klahr, 1995).
Work on conceptual change and abduction further
emphasizes that productive explanations can re-
quire introducing new distinctions or explanatory
primitives (Carey, 2009; Chi, 2008; Douven, 2021;
Magnani, 2009). Computational discovery systems,
however, usually search within a fixed representa-
tional substrate (Langley et al., 1987; Kemp and
Tenenbaum, 2008; Ellis et al., 2021; Grand et al.,
2024; Murphy, 2026). ULTRADISCOVERYoper-
ationalizes representation formation as an experi-
mentally controlled cost by comparing open and
disclosed representations.
2.3 Scaffolds for discovery agents
Long-horizon agents are commonly strengthened
through self-reflection, falsification-oriented ex-
perimentation, or search over candidate solutions
(Shinn et al., 2023; Madaan et al., 2023; Huang
et al., 2025; Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov
et al., 2025; Lange et al., 2026). Their effectiveness
can depend strongly on how the candidate space
is constructed and evaluated (Gideoni et al., 2026).
We therefore treat scaffolding as an experimental
factor: holding the world and interaction protocol
fixed, ULTRADISCOVERYcompares self-critique,
falsification, and evolutionary scaffolds in the two
Open conditions.
3 The ULTRADISCOVERYuniverse
3.1 A world that must be redescribed
ULTRADISCOVERYis a formation of five derelict
seed-ships whose caretaker machinery still runs
five domains of work: a
 grazing fen, a
 repair
trellis, a
 ladder of beacons, a
 freight floor, and
a
 registry (Figure 3). Every twenty-four ticks the
world resets, and the agent’s
 notes are all that
persists. Appendix A tells the story in full, and
Appendix G the game that inspired its design.
The agent is asked to explain a stall that ends ev-
ery pass across the whole formation and to predict
the outcome of one intervention that has never been
attempted. A free prologue teaches the caretakers’
doctrine, which is numerically exact on its own
cases. The explanation of the stall lies beyond the
doctrine’s vocabulary, and the representation that
can state it appears in no action, no reading and no
feedback. Constructing that representation is the
discovery.
Any site is one action away, so the budget
is spent on observations and interventions. The
time loop gives a repeatable baseline to perturb, a
clean boundary between experiments and a termi-
nal anomaly that recurs in every pass. The interface
is raw on both sides: interventions are physical acts
on named objects, observations are readings in the
instrument’s own words, and the world returns read-
ings alone, with no score and no hint.

3.2 Episodes and the terminal prediction
An episode spends the contract budget of 200 paid
actions on one instance of the world, during which
the agent keeps a free-text workspace that it alone
writes. With ten actions remaining the caretakers
post a standing order, an intervention at two sites
in the same instant, and the agent commits once, in
advance, three integer readings that instruments in
three domains will show afterwards. The prediction
is a transfer test: it asks whether the agent can use
its cross-domain account to forecast an intervention
it has not observed.
3.3 Epistemic structure
What there is to discover is fixed in advance as
eighteenepistemic blocks, the clues and inferences
the world affords. Fifteen belong to one domain
each and three are cross-domain bridges. A hyper-
graph over the blocks records how knowledge of
the world depends on evidence (Figure 2). Its or-
dinary edges are the design’s canonical evidential
dependencies, the support relations from which the
reference route of Section 3.5 is derived, and each
block is scored on its own evidence and statement
(Section 4.3). Threehyperedges, H1 to H3, each
join three sources from three domains in a joint
claim that licenses onerepresentation change, RC1
to RC3: the retraction of an axiom, the postula-
tion of an entity the interface never renders, and a
change of the variables the laws are written over.
A fourth, HC, gathers the five sources from which
the design computes the readings of the terminal
prediction.
Structural interconnection thus concerns the ev-
idence needed to identify a mechanism, which
is distinct from the arity of its physical interac-
tions: a mechanism of pairwise interactions can
still need observations from three domains to be
known. Read alone, each source of a hyperedge
is an oddity in one domain that a patch in that do-
main’s vocabulary absorbs, and two such patches
can be held together. The three sources share one
regularity that one change of representation ex-
plains at once, so observations from three domains
jointly support one explanation outside the doc-
trine. The prologue gives the doctrine its initial
support, which makes local repair the natural first
response and composition across domains the step
that reveals its limit.

3.5 Validation
We validate evidence reachability and resistance to
shortcuts (Appendix C). A scripted reference route
takes39 paid actionsin every variant to acquire
the readings from which the terminal prediction is
computed and to commit. Agents reach the world’s
evidence too: the Open episodes of the main com-
parison together hold the evidence ofall 18 blocks,
and at the contract budget no episode assembles
more than 3 of the five sources of HC. On reserved
instances, a model given what an agent holds before
its first observation commits no exact prediction,
one given the surface vocabulary states none of the
mechanism’s laws, and one given the complete ref-
erence record in a single prompt commits no exact
prediction either: holding the readings and building
the account that composes them are separate steps.
The mechanism, the criteria and the materials
are printed in full (Appendices A, B and F), and the
results here were measured before this disclosure
and stand as the benchmark’s reference results.

6 Discussion and conclusion
ULTRADISCOVERYseparates the construction of
an explanatory representation from the composition
of evidence across domains inside one world, and
its results show why progress on evidence falls
short of a transferable explanation. Because each
source admits a local patch, an agent gains evidence
and grounded inferences while its representation
keeps the doctrine’s terms. Doubt is voiced often,
while a replacement has to be built from sources
held together and read as one claim. What the
prediction measures is an account that transfers, by
the designed route or by one of the agent’s own.
A representation also organises inquiry. Sup-
plied with one, agents turn to intervention, and
without one, retraction and experiment go together.
A representation names the kinds of mechanism to
test for and leaves the location of their evidence
to inquiry, which is why disclosure moves inter-
vention more than discovery and why Disclosed
agents state changes whose sources they seldom
hold. The scaffolds show the same separation: fal-
sification obtains tests, and grounded discovery
barely moves.
The vendor-harness systems and the larger bud-
gets carry inquiry further along these steps, and
every Open episode still leaves the unobserved en-
tity unstated. The step that limits discovery in such
a world is the one a hyperedge encodes: holding
evidence from several domains at once and build-
ing from it an entity or a set of variables beyond
the taught theory’s vocabulary. Here the entity is a
hidden label that makes bodies on several hulks one
individual, and the variables are its coordinates and
each body’s place in the rotation (Appendix A.8).

Limitations
ULTRADISCOVERYinstantiates one authored
world, and its findings are measured on language-
model agents. Worlds with the two separable prop-
erties built by others would show how far the find-
ings extend, and runs by human volunteers would
calibrate the world’s difficulty for people. Its open-
ness is operational: the representation is absent
from the interface and the initial materials, and
what an agent brings from prior knowledge is its
own. The two contrasts are effects of declared
grants. Disclosure supplies structural knowledge
together with vocabulary, and alignment changes
both the access to evidence and the budget left for
experiments, so each contrast is read as the value
of its grant. The metrics read an agent’s workspace
and its event log, and an inference that an agent
made and left unwritten lies outside their view.
The results were measured before the mecha-
nism, the criteria and the instances were disclosed;
this paper prints them in full, so the results stand
as the benchmark’s reference results. We will re-
lease the complete code of the world with its four
compiled variants, the evaluation and validation
instances, the reference route with its oracle, the
event log and notes of every episode, the judge’s
verdicts and the scoring. The contribution we in-
tend to last is the separation of the two burdens, con-
structing an explanatory representation and com-
posing distributed evidence, and the design that
prices each with a declared grant inside one latent
world.

All five domains are generated by a shared latent

and wherever they stand. The 36 objects begin a

D.4 Evolution
The first mutation call of
 evolution seeds four
candidate mechanisms in free prose. After that,
one batched call before each turn proposes two new
candidates, plus one for each place left empty. A
proposal may cite any current candidates as parents,
so that lineage forms a graph, and must change
something the log could decide. A fitness call then
scores every candidate by retrodiction: the share of
a digest excerpt it would have called correctly in
advance, from 0 (contradicted outright) to 1 (every
line accounted for), whatever the length, detail or
plausibility of the account. The fitness excerpt is
a longer tail of the digest than the other calls read.
Selection keeps the four best and skips a candidate
whose charactern-grams nearly duplicate one al-
ready kept. The population forms the region, and
a note appended to the turn names the leader and
observes that a step telling the leading candidates
apart is worth more than one agreeing with the
leader. The agent’s ordinary turn is the selector.
The population and the mutation loop follow
AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) and ShinkaE-
volve (Lange et al., 2026), and treating the paid
actions as a fixed evaluation budget follows Sim-
pleTES (Ye et al., 2026). Those systems score
candidates with an external evaluator. Here the one
external verdict is the final commitment, so fitness
is the model’s own retrodiction against readings
already in the digest, and the evaluator stays fixed
for the episode, where Hyra (Hyra Team, 2026)
evolves it as well.

G.1 The game
Outer Wilds, developed by Mobius Digital and pub-
lished by Annapurna Interactive in 2019 (Mobius
Digital, 2019), places the player in a small solar
system whose sun goes supernova twenty-two min-
utes after the player wakes beside a campfire on the
home planet. Then the same twenty-two minutes
begin again. The player has a wooden spaceship, a
spacesuit with a jetpack, a signalscope that picks
up signals from across the system, a launcher for a
camera probe called the scout, and a translator for
the writing of the Nomai, an extinct species whose
ruins cover the planets. Each loop restores every
planet and every object. The ship’s log carries over:
it records what the player has found, sorted by place
in one view and linked into a web of rumours in the
other. Game facts below follow the in-game texts as
the community wiki documents them (Outer Wilds
Wiki, 2026). The game began as a master’s thesis
on curiosity-driven exploration (Beachum, 2013),
and Eurogamer and Polygon each named it their
game of the year for 2019 (Donlan, 2019; Stark,
2019).
We read the game as a worked instance of
the two properties of this paper (Appendices G.2
and G.3), and its players’ accounts as a record
of what those properties ask of a person (Ap-
pendix G.5). Table 15 sets the game’s demands
beside those of ULTRADISCOVERY, and a critic’s
account of its progression could describe one of
our episodes: “When you start a new loop, you
don’t bring anything from the previous one with
you except knowledge, much of which is conve-
niently tracked in your spaceship’s computer” (My-
ers, 2019).
G.2 Clues that interlock across planets
The game is structurally interconnected by design.
The thesis that began it ties each central mystery to
clues on several worlds: “Each Curiosity is linked
to three Points-of-Interests, each of which is lo-
cated on a different planet” (Beachum, 2013). The
released game keeps this shape. The Quantum
Moon wanders between five orbits in the system
and has a sixth location beside the Eye of the uni-
verse, the object whose signal brought the Nomai to
the system. A shrine on the moon takes the player
there. Its inscriptions ask the visitor to recall three
rules, the last of them, “Recall the rule of the sixth
location”, beside a mural of a tower hanging above
a black hole. The rule of quantum imaging is taught
in a tower on Giant’s Deep, the rule of quantum
entanglement by a wandering shard in the caves of
Ember Twin, and the rule of the sixth location in
a tower hanging from the crust of Brittle Hollow.
Each rule answers a local curiosity. Held together,
the three form a procedure.
Smaller facts travel between planets in the same
way. The anglerfish of Dark Bramble hunt by
sound, and the player learns that they are blind
on Ember Twin. A rare cyclone that pushes ob-
jects below the ocean current of Giant’s Deep is
explained beside working models of cyclones on
Brittle Hollow, and the insulation of the jellyfish
that carry the player through the electrified barrier
around the core of Giant’s Deep is recorded in a
traveller’s notes in Dark Bramble.
The ending composes the whole system. The
player removes the advanced warp core that powers
the loop from a Nomai facility inside Ash Twin,
carries it to the Vessel on which the Nomai arrived,
now lost deep inside Dark Bramble, and enters the
coordinates of the Eye that a probe tracking module
has recorded at the core of Giant’s Deep. Each
of the three goals is reached through knowledge
gathered on other planets. Polygon’s citation for its
game of the year describes the experience plainly:
“It’s only by piecing together the game’s mechanics,
the Nomai’s findings, and the Hearthians’ current
observations that I’m able to truly grasp what is
going on” (Stark, 2019).
G.3 A representation the player builds
The game is also epistemically open. It supplies
facts in the words of whoever recorded them and
leaves their bearing to the player. The thesis states
the principle: “One approach that seems to suc-
cessfully encourage curiosity is to populate the
world with things that reveal affordances elsewhere
in the world without telling players what to do”
(Beachum, 2013). The ship’s log follows it. In
the writer’s words its entries are “only what we’re
100% certain the player knows from reading that
piece of found text”, with “NO leaps of logic, no
inferences” (Beachum, 2021). The log links clues,
and every conclusion stays with the player.

G.4 Progress stored in the player
The loop makes exploration persistent and keeps
all of its progress in the player. The game’s writer
takes up the question whether a first-time player
could finish in a single loop, and answers that
it is possible in principle and extremely unlikely,
since it would take some advance knowledge of
the game (Beachum, 2021). Removing the warp
core switches the loop off, so the last run is one
life in which a death ends the game. The coordi-
nates of the Eye stay the same in every loop, so a
player who has read them once can enter them, and
everything that run needs is already in the player.
