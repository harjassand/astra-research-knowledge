PRIMARY SOURCE CHECK — Hudson

Publisher PDF: https://www.kybernetika.cz/content/2010/6/1061/paper.pdf
Published: Kybernetika 46(6), 2010, pp.1061–1068. A search-indexed draft is dated March 12, 2009; use the verified published version for citations.

Theorem 3.1, printed p.1064 (publisher PDF page index 3), for an irreducible canonical pair: rho -> f(z)=Tr[rho W(z)] bijects density operators and normalized continuous omega-positive-definite functions on R^2.

Conventions, p.1063 eqs.(4)–(5): W(z)W(z')=exp(i sigma(z,z')/2)W(z+z'), sigma((x,y),(x',y'))=xy'-yx'. Quantum positivity requires [exp(-i sigma(z_j,z_k)/2)f(z_k-z_j)] >= 0. Ordinary positive definiteness instead requires [f(z_k-z_j)] >= 0.

Page1064 immediately before Theorem3.1 states f is necessarily L2, so its inverse Fourier–Plancherel transform defines the L2 Wigner function. Theorem3.1 imposes no L1 assumption on f. Section4, opening paragraph p.1064, identifies true probabilities with ordinary-positive-definite normalized continuous Fourier transforms and quantum Wigner distributions with omega-positive ones; their intersection gives nonnegative Wigner states.

Inference making the density step explicit: ordinary Bochner gives a finite positive measure; because f is L2, distributional Fourier uniqueness/Plancherel forces that measure to have the same L2 Wigner density. Hence the density is nonnegative a.e. and belongs to L1 with integral1. Do not silently replace Fourier–Plancherel inversion by an absolutely convergent integral.
