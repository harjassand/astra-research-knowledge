# Cycle 2: reversible persisters in the robust extinction certificate

## Result

A common positive weight can certify uniform decay of the reversible sensitive/persister block over a finite rate box, and the global certificate then gives an explicit residual-risk bound plus a successful-stable-resistance source term. If stable resistance has nonnegative net growth during the pulse, no positive weight can make the full sensitive/persister/resistant system strictly decay: the resistant type's own column prevents it. In that case the valid result is a source-risk floor (or UNKNOWN if its source/success bound is missing), not global extinction.

The drift inequalities and risk formulas below are exact conditional deductions from the stated branching/mean-generator model. The numerical rate boxes and schedule calculations are illustrative fixtures; they are not fitted to PC9 or any tumor and do not establish treatment benefit.

## Robust drift with reversible state switching

Let $x=(S,P)^T$, where $S$ is drug-sensitive and $P$ is a reversibly drug-tolerant state. Write $g_S=b_S-d_S$ and $g_P=b_P-d_P$ for net birth/death rates before phenotype switching. During a fixed-dose pulse, let $\alpha$ be the $S\to P$ rate and $\beta$ the $P\to S$ rate. The mean generator is the Metzler matrix

\[
M_{SP}(\theta)=
\begin{pmatrix}g_S-\alpha&\beta\\\alpha&g_P-\beta\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E[x]=M_{SP}(\theta(t))\mathbb E[x].
\]

Assume a **simultaneous** rectangular rate set
\[
g_S\in[\underline g_S,\bar g_S],\quad
g_P\in[\underline g_P,\bar g_P],\quad
\alpha\in[\underline\alpha,\bar\alpha],\quad
\beta\in[\underline\beta,\bar\beta].
\]
For one vector $w=(w_S,w_P)\gg0$, a common exponential certificate is
\[
w^TM_{SP}(\theta)\le -\delta w^T
\quad\text{componentwise for every allowed }\theta,
\qquad \delta>0. \tag{1}
\]
For $V=w^Tx$, this gives $\mathbb E[V(t)]\le V_0e^{-\delta t}$ under arbitrary time variation or switching inside the set. With $\rho=w_P/w_S$, (1) is equivalent to
\[
\bar g_S+
\begin{cases}
\bar\alpha(\rho-1),&\rho\ge1,\\
\underline\alpha(\rho-1),&\rho<1
\end{cases}
\le-\delta,
\]
\[
\bar g_P+
\begin{cases}
\bar\beta(1/\rho-1),&\rho\le1,\\
\underline\beta(1/\rho-1),&\rho>1
\end{cases}
\le-\delta. \tag{2}
\]
Thus a persister fraction alone is insufficient: the interval bounds on entry and reversion, and on both types' net rates, determine whether the common drift exists. For correlated uncertainty, optimize each column over the joint confidence polytope (or check its vertices); independent unadjusted intervals do not automatically give simultaneous coverage after selecting $w$ and dose.

### What changes when stable resistance is a third tracked type

If $G$ is a stable-resistant type and an event converts one $S/P$ cell into one $G$ cell at rates $m_S,m_P$, the three-type mean generator is
\[
A_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
g_S-\alpha-m_S&\beta&0\\
\alpha&g_P-\beta-m_P&0\\
m_S&m_P&g_G
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With $\rho=w_P/w_S$ and $\sigma=w_G/w_S$, a strict common drift requires
\[
\bar g_S+\alpha_*(\rho-1)+m_{S,*}(\sigma-1)\le-\delta,
\]
\[
\bar g_P+\beta_*(1/\rho-1)+m_{P,*}(\sigma/\rho-1)\le-\delta,
\qquad \bar g_G\le-\delta. \tag{3}
\]
Each starred endpoint is the upper endpoint when its displayed coefficient is nonnegative and the lower endpoint otherwise. If $G$ is instead a mutant **daughter** and the parent remains, the first two inequalities contain $+\bar\mu_S\sigma$ and $+\bar\mu_P\sigma/\rho$, rather than the one-for-one conversion terms; the controlled $S/P$ birth rates must exclude those mutant daughters if they are separately counted. This distinction prevents double-counting mutation births.

The last inequality in (3) is the obstruction: if the upper confidence bound on $g_G$ is nonnegative, no $w_G>0$ and no choice of $w_S,w_P$ can give strict all-type decay. Infeasibility of this common-weight test is not proof that a pulse fails; it means the proposed global extinction certificate cannot cover $G$. Stable $G$ must then be bounded as a successful escape source, or modeled with its own pulse-phase dynamics and a valid probability of surviving the remainder of the pulse. A post-pulse establishment probability by itself is not enough if $G$ can expand or die materially before pulse end.

## Risk bound and fixed-dose minimum duration

Use (1) for the controlled $S/P$ population, and let $\bar q_S,\bar q_P$ upper-bound the post-pulse establishment probabilities of residual $S/P$ founders. Let $\eta_S(t),\eta_P(t)$ upper-bound **successful-$G$-founder intensity per cell**. For example, $\eta_i=\bar\mu_i\bar q_G^{\rm pulse}$, where $\bar\mu_i$ bounds the rate of producing a stable-resistant founder and $\bar q_G^{\rm pulse}$ bounds that founder's probability of causing relapse after accounting for the rest of the pulse and maintenance. Set
\[
C_R=\max(\bar q_S/w_S,\bar q_P/w_P),\qquad
C_G=\max(\bar\eta_S/w_S,\bar\eta_P/w_P).
\]
Assume no immigration into $S/P$, and let $R_0$ upper-bound risk from any already-present stable-resistant founders. Markov's inequality for residual cells and a union bound over successful-$G$ events give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(\text{relapse by the declared endpoint})\le{}&
R_0+C_RV_0e^{-\delta T}
+C_GV_0\int_0^T e^{-\delta t}\,dt\\
={}&R_0+\frac{C_GV_0}{\delta}
+\left(C_RV_0-\frac{C_GV_0}{\delta}\right)e^{-\delta T}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Other bounded external-source terms from the global certificate add to this expression. It is an upper bound, not an exact probability. Its asymptote is
\[
F_{\rm cert}=R_0+C_GV_0/\delta.
\]
That asymptote is a floor of this **constant-source upper bound**, not a lower bound on true or posterior relapse risk. If a G founder produced at time $t$ is killed by continued treatment, use a duration-aware successful-source bound $C_G(t,T)$ and replace the source term by $V_0\int_0^T C_G(t,T)e^{-\delta t}\,dt$. A tight bound can then fall below the constant-source asymptote as $T$ grows. A positive exact risk floor requires an explicit protected-source mechanism, such as a pulse-insensitive G type with a duration-independent chance of establishing.
For fixed pulse concentration, put $A=C_RV_0$, $D=C_GV_0/\delta$, and $F=R_0+D$. If $A>D$ and $F<\epsilon<R_0+A$, the shortest duration certified to reach target risk $\epsilon$ is
\[
T_{\min}(\epsilon)=\frac1\delta
\log\!\left(\frac{A-D}{\epsilon-F}\right). \tag{5}
\]
No duration is certified for $\epsilon\le F$; if $\epsilon\ge R_0+A$, duration zero already meets this bound. If $A\le D$, longer treatment does not lower (4). This is a **fixed-dose duration**. Optimizing dose or concentration-time exposure needs measured functions $g_i(u),\alpha(u),\beta(u),\mu_i(u),q_i(u)$, feasible-dose constraints, and a declared cost $c(u)$; those data are absent here.

Illustrative interval calculation only: take the pulse box
\[
g_S\in[-1.1,-0.9],\quad
g_P\in[-0.002,0.002],\quad
\alpha\in[0.015,0.025],\quad
\beta\in[0.04,0.06]\quad(\text{per day}).
\]
The common weight $w=(1,5)$ satisfies (2) with $\delta=0.03\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}$: the worst normalized $S,P$ columns are $-0.80,-0.03$ per day. Let $V_0=1$, $\bar q_S=0.2,\bar q_P=0.8$, $\bar\mu_S=0.004,\bar\mu_P=0.020$ per day, and $\bar q_G^{\rm pulse}=0.25$. Then $C_R=0.2$, $C_G=0.001\,\mathrm{day}^{-1}$, and (4), with $R_0=0$, is
\[
\bar R(T)=0.03333+0.16667e^{-0.03T}.
\]
At target $\epsilon=0.05$, (5) gives $T_{\min}=76.75$ days. A target at or below $0.03333$ is not certified at any duration. These are invented values to show the calculation, not biological rate estimates; the long duration illustrates how conservative a worst-case source bound can be.

## Exact source-risk floor in a minimal reversible-switching model

The certificate floor in (4) is not a lower bound on nature's relapse probability. A separate, exactly solvable model shows when the *true* asymptotic risk is positive. During treatment, an $S$ cell dies at rate $\delta$, switches to $P$ at rate $\alpha$, or generates a successful stable-$G$ founder at rate $\eta_S$. A $P$ cell reverts to $S$ at rate $\beta$ or generates a successful $G$ founder at rate $\eta_P$. There are no births or direct $P$ deaths in this deliberately minimal competing-risk model; a successful $G$ founder is assumed to guarantee the declared relapse endpoint. Every $S/P$ lineage otherwise eventually clears when $\delta,\beta>0$.

For pulse length $T$, let $u_S(T),u_P(T)$ be the probability of no relapse from one founder, including the post-pulse establishment probabilities $q_S,q_P$ of any residual cells. The exact backward equations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_S'&=\delta(1-u_S)+\alpha(u_P-u_S)-\eta_Su_S,\\
u_P'&=\beta(u_S-u_P)-\eta_Pu_P,
\end{aligned}
\]
with $u_S(0)=1-q_S,\ u_P(0)=1-q_P$. For $n_S,n_P$ independent founders the exact risk is $1-u_S(T)^{n_S}u_P(T)^{n_P}$. As $T\to\infty$, residual $S/P$ cells clear and the one-founder successful-source probabilities $h_i=1-u_i(\infty)$ solve
\[
h_S=\frac{\eta_S+\alpha h_P}{\delta+\alpha+\eta_S},\qquad
h_P=\frac{\eta_P+\beta h_S}{\beta+\eta_P}.
\]
Writing
\[
\Delta=(\delta+\alpha+\eta_S)(\beta+\eta_P)-\alpha\beta,
\]
this gives
\[
h_S=\frac{\eta_S(\beta+\eta_P)+\alpha\eta_P}{\Delta},\qquad
h_P=\frac{\beta\eta_S+(\delta+\alpha+\eta_S)\eta_P}{\Delta}. \tag{6}
\]
For $\delta=1,\alpha=0.02,\beta=0.05,\eta_S=0.001,\eta_P=0.005$ per day, $h_S=0.0028103$, $h_P=0.0934639$; ten independent starting $S$ founders have exact limiting risk $1-(1-h_S)^{10}=0.02775$. The large contrast between $h_S$ and $h_P$ is the reservoir effect: repeated $P\to S$ return creates substantial future source opportunity even though $P$ itself does not divide in this submodel.

This positive floor is exact **only for the specified competing-risk model**. Branching births, density dependence, spatial refuges, imperfect state calls, and pulse effects on $G$ change it and require the corresponding probability-generating-function or validated likelihood model. In a natural system a missing upper bound on $\eta_P$, initial $P$, or the pulse-time success of $G$ leaves relapse risk UNKNOWN.

## Equal-AUC interruption model: point calculation versus robust certificate

Use the reversible-state mean model from the prior persister note. During on-drug time, take
\[
M_{\rm on}=\begin{pmatrix}-1.02&0.05\\0.02&-0.05\end{pmatrix};
\]
off-drug, take
\[
M_{\rm off}=\begin{pmatrix}0.7&0.2\\0&-0.2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The point values mean $\delta=1,\alpha=0.02,\beta_D=0.05$ on drug and $g_S=0.7,\alpha_0=0,\beta_0=0.2,g_P=0$ off drug, in $\mathrm{day}^{-1}$. Start with $x_0=(1,0)$. Let successful-$G$ source intensities be $\eta_S=0.001,\eta_P=0.005$ per cell-day. Compare equal nominal concentration-time exposure and equal 18-day calendar time:

| Arm | Schedule | On days | Off days | Final washout |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 3 on / 3 off / 6 on / 6 off | 9 | 9 | 6 days |
| B | 6 on / 3 off / 3 on / 6 off | 9 | 9 | 6 days |

Piecewise RK4 integration of the linear ODE (step 0.0005 day; doubling and halving the step agree to the shown precision) predicts:

| Arm | $S(18)$ | $P(18)$ | $S+P$ | $\int S\,dt$ | $\int P\,dt$ | $H_G=\int(\eta_SS+\eta_PP)\,dt$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.301618 | 0.003990 | 0.305608 | 2.279760 | 0.206520 | 0.003312 |
| B | 0.301964 | 0.002434 | 0.304398 | 1.506410 | 0.174906 | 0.002381 |

This point model therefore predicts 39% fewer endpoint persisters and 28% less successful-$G$ source exposure in B, while total endpoint burden differs by only about 0.4%. $H_G$ is an expected number of successful-source events under the stated mean path and the corresponding first-moment union-bound quantity; it is **not** an exact branching-process relapse probability. The result is a model comparison, not an observed effect.

For a finite-interval certificate, use the pulse box above and an illustrative off-drug box $g_S\in[0.6,0.8],g_P=0,\alpha_0=0,\beta_0\in[0.15,0.25]$. The same $w=(1,5)$ gives
\[
w^TM_{\rm on}\le-0.03w^T,\qquad
w^TM_{\rm off}\le0.8w^T.
\]
An all-phase strict negative drift is impossible because the off-drug $S$ column is positive. For A and B, the common-weight terminal envelope is therefore the same,
\[
V(18)\le V_0e^{-0.03(9)+0.8(9)}=1022.49V_0.
\]
The prefix envelope integrals are 1337.24 for A and 1309.97 for B; with $C_G=0.001$, the source term already exceeds one, so the probability bound clipped at one is uninformative. Thus the robust certificate cannot guarantee B's point-model advantage. The split-pulse state-resolved holdout can test that advantage; interval bounds alone cannot.

## Primary evidence, matched test, and resource cost

The biological mechanism and mathematical tools have direct prior art. Sharma et al. reported about 0.3% viable, largely quiescent PC9 cells after nine days of EGFR-TKI exposure and described transiently acquired/relinquished tolerance; their result supports a candidate state marker and timescale, not these rate values or a tumor-level risk estimate ([primary paper, *Cell* 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851638/)). Gunnarsson et al. already analyze phenotype switching, multitype branching, extinction, and stable resistance in a cancer model ([primary paper, *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/)). Foo and Michor analyze resistance under continuous and pulsed administration with a multitype stochastic model ([primary paper, *PLoS Computational Biology* 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/)). Common linear copositive Lyapunov functions for continuous-time positive switched systems are established control-theory machinery ([Fornasini & Valcher, *IEEE TAC* 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2010.2049918)). The candidate contribution here is only the explicit persister-weighted embedding and its failure/floor calculation; neither switching nor pulse therapy nor common-weight stability is new.

A decisive assay would use single-cell-derived PC9 cells at the published 2 μM erlotinib exposure as a starting system, then verify concentration and target engagement rather than treating nominal AUC as delivered dose. Run A/B plus two schedule controls: C (3 off / 9 on / 6 off), a contiguous 9-day block with the same 18-day duration, total 9 on + 9 off days, and final 6-day washout; D (9 on / 9 off from day 0), an immediate-start block with the same total exposure and calendar time but a 9-day final washout. C necessarily delays first exposure; D necessarily changes time since last dose. Keeping both makes those timing tradeoffs visible rather than attributing them to switching. Include parental and isogenic stable EGFR-T790M cells under all four schedules, vehicle-treated parental cells, and continuously treated parental reference cells. Fit the reversible model and a static sensitive/resistant mixture to the same calibration data, then freeze both before the held-out schedule run.

Measure absolute live-cell counts and a state-resolved fraction on days 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18; validate CD133 or another live reporter in this line, since a marker call is not automatically a persister call. Record target engagement after initial dosing and each restart, verify medium drug concentration at refresh/restart, and genotype or barcode endpoint survivors. From each schedule's washout start, follow resensitization through 0, 3, 6, and 9 drug-free doublings, then run a 72-hour rechallenge curve. Predeclare the falsifier: reject this reversible-state explanation if the measured state does not reversibly change, a held-out state/count/rechallenge outcome lies outside its frozen predictive interval, stable resistance explains rebound, or the apparent schedule ranking vanishes after target-engagement matching. The useful-result comparison is whether the switching model predicts held-out outcomes better than the equally informed static-mixture model and whether the schedule ordering survives the AUC/time-matched controls.

For four independent biological replicates, the ten conditions (four schedules × two genotypes, plus vehicle and continuous-drug parental controls) require 280 destructive culture-timepoint samples for seven state/count measurements. The four schedule arms × two genotypes × four washout points add 128 recovery pools; a seven-concentration rechallenge curve uses 896 wells before technical replication. Initial-dose and restart target-engagement checks require 48 samples across the four schedule arms and two genotypes, before technical replicates; concentration checks and survivor sequencing add further samples. The culture schedule takes 18 days, with additional time for nine drug-free doublings and the 72-hour assay; calibration separately needs event-resolved division/death/switch measurements. Repeated imaging can reduce count-only wells but not destructive state/genotype assays. Four replicates are a feasibility screen, not a powered confirmatory study. Monetary costs are lab-specific and not imputed. No assay, fitting, animal work, or validation was performed for this note.

## Evidence boundary

- **Derived from the model:** the interval endpoint inequalities; the impossibility of all-type strict drift when $\bar g_G\ge0$; the risk bound and fixed-dose duration rule; the exact competing-risk floor; and the point-model A/B schedule outputs.
- **Primary-source reported:** PC9 persister prevalence/reversibility and published cancer branching/pulse models listed above.
- **Illustrative only:** interval boxes, mutation/source rates, establishment probabilities, fixed-dose duration, and equal-AUC schedule effect sizes.
- **Unresolved:** whether the required simultaneous rate bounds and pulse-time $G$-success probability are measurable with useful precision, whether PC9 results transport to a tumor, and whether any schedule beats a genuinely optimized equally informed control after toxicity and assay latency are priced.
