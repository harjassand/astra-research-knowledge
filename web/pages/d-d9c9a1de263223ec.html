# Cycle 9 deeper attack — the coupled skull-wave model and what one speed can identify

## Finding

The Fog/receptor/contact-age source package did not identify a dynamic propagation threshold. I redirected to Dang et al. (2025), whose skull morphogenesis paper releases a coupled density–phenotype–force-balance model, its finite-difference notebook, model output arrays, tracked-cell summaries, and explant-interface data.

I independently transcribed the notebook’s centered-space, forward-Euler scheme and its tridiagonal force-balance solve. At the published grid, the replay reproduces all three released arrays to numerical roundoff: `φ` RMSE `1.81×10⁻¹⁵`, density RMSE `1.27×10⁻¹⁷`, and velocity RMSE `4.81×10⁻¹⁴`. Refining the source initial condition (`aρ=aφ=4`) from `Nx=200, Nt=100` through `Nx=3200, Nt=25600` stabilizes the 0–8 h `φ=0.5` OLS slope at `21.73416 μm/h`; the end-to-end secant stabilizes at `21.77502 μm/h`. This is a verified finite replay of the **published model**, not evidence that the full PDE describes living tissue.

The sharper model result is a **conditional identifiability failure**. In the full nonlinear solver, changing the assumed matched initial profile from `aρ=aφ=4` to `aρ=aφ=8` while reducing `α` from `3.5×10⁻⁶` to `1.934×10⁻⁶` leaves the 8 h front secant almost unchanged (`21.77502` versus `21.77423 μm/h`, difference `0.00079 μm/h`). The fitted `α` differs by about 45%. The alternative predicts a substantially different Eulerian tissue velocity at the `φ=0.5` line (`10.923` versus `17.998 μm/h`), so paired velocity and initial-profile measurements can disambiguate it. The result blocks **speed-only** inversion of `α` across this model family; it does not refute the paper’s joint fit using its other data and fixed initial profile.

The source’s scalar Fisher–KPP reduction is a useful comparator when estimands are aligned. For the public `aφ=4` seed, the far-tail exponent is `q₀=0.005333 μm⁻¹`, only `0.0865 q*` in the homogeneous-leading-edge reduction. The shallow-tail scalar prediction plus sampled model velocity and density drift is `21.6851 μm/h`, within `0.0899 μm/h` of the replayed secant. Across matched-profile sweeps through `q₀≈q*`, the comparator remains close to the full finite-time solver. At very steep profiles, the solver’s measured density-gradient drift reduces the relative front speed and the comparator overpredicts by roughly `1.3–1.9 μm/h` in the tested cases. No new Fisher–KPP law is claimed; this is source-specific model validation and a parameter-boundary audit.

An apparent finite-model zero of front motion relative to local tissue flow occurs only at extreme profile steepness, and its location changes with the chosen time window. In the `Nx=3200, Nt=25600` run, the 0–8 h average relative front speed changes from `+0.147 μm/h` at `aφ=110` to `−0.132 μm/h` at `aφ=120`, with a near-zero value at 115–116. The 6–8 h estimate is already slightly negative at `aφ=110`. At `aφ≈115`, the initial profile e-fold width is about `6.5 μm`, below the density-derived characteristic cell spacing `1/√ρ≈11–12 μm`; the paper’s `15 μm` grid also cannot resolve it. This is a finite solver diagnostic, not a biological or engineering threshold. It removes the proposed use of continuum tail steepening as an intervention rule from this evidence package.

## Source reconstruction and numerical method

The primary source is Dang et al. (2025), [*Self-propagating wave drives morphogenesis of skull bones in vivo*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59164-9). Its public [SkullWave repository](https://github.com/YitengDang/SkullWave/tree/b2cbb61097cdc25bfff1e0a89ba690a31c2a4adc) is pinned to commit `b2cbb61097cdc25bfff1e0a89ba690a31c2a4adc`; selected CSV Git blobs and local SHA-256 hashes are recorded in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

The SI equations balance total cell density `ρ`, osteoblast fraction `φ`, and viscous force. The notebook closes `E(φ)=E_M+(E_O−E_M)φ`, `k_D=α(E−E_M)`, and solves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\rho+\partial_x(\rho v)&=[k_A(1-\phi)+k_B\phi]\rho,\\
\partial_t\phi+v\partial_x\phi&=D\left(\partial_{xx}\phi+\frac{\partial_x\rho}{\rho}\partial_x\phi\right)
 +(k_B-k_A)\phi(1-\phi)+k_D(1-\phi),\\
\eta\partial_{xx}v-\gamma v&=E\partial_x\log(\rho/\rho_h)+\partial_xE\log(\rho/\rho_h).
\end{aligned}
\]

The [independent replay](replay_coupled_fd.py) uses the published `L=1500 μm`, `tmax=8 h`, `Nx=200`, `T=100`, Dirichlet endpoints, tanh initial conditions, central differences, forward Euler, and a zero-Dirichlet tridiagonal solve for `v` each time step. The optional DST-I path diagonalizes the same discrete force operator; it agrees with the Thomas solve to floating-point roundoff in cross-checks. Source-grid agreement is checked against the pinned output CSVs. Base-profile refinement uses `dx={15,7.5,3.75,1.875,0.9375} μm` and `dt={0.08,0.02,0.005,0.00125,0.0003125} h`.

The source notebook sets `D=15 μm²/h`, `τ=10 h`, `ρ_A^c=0.0067`, `ρ_B^c=0.0077`, `E_A=388.8`, `E_B=12960` in its converted model units, `α=3.5×10⁻⁶`, `ρ_h=0.001`, and `aρ=aφ=4`. The tanh seed has `q₀=2aφ/L=0.005333 μm⁻¹`. Freezing density at A-homeostasis and removing uniform advection gives scalar logistic growth `r=0.056986 h⁻¹`, `q*=√(r/D)=0.061637 μm⁻¹`, and shallow-tail speed `Dq₀+r/q₀=10.7649 μm/h`. The pulled minimum is `1.8491 μm/h`; it is not the seed’s scalar branch.

The model front estimand matters. On the released output, the continuous `φ=0.5` 0–8 h OLS slope is `21.7353 μm/h`, while the end-to-end secant is `21.7736 μm/h`. The companion Rmd’s strict-threshold/origin fit is `20.5881 μm/h`; the notebook’s two-window profile-shift estimator is `18.75 μm/h`. Its objective applies `Abs` after a signed spatial sum, so its `0.03728` residual is not pointwise/L1 shape error; the L1 argmin happens to select the same shift for this output. Steep matched profiles accelerate strongly over the first two hours, so the OLS and secant differ by several μm/h. A finite 8 h fit should not be called a time-asymptotic wave speed.

## Conditional front-speed comparison and full-model alias

For the released profile, the matched-time decomposition uses the end-to-end front secant and integrates the Eulerian `V` and `D∂x logρ` sampled along the same moving level. It gives a tissue-frame front rate `10.8517 μm/h` and density-adjusted scalar target `10.8484 μm/h`; the source scalar shallow-tail prediction is `10.7649 μm/h`. The scalar-plus-drift total is `21.6851 μm/h`, `0.0899 μm/h` below the secant. This validates the scalar reduction as a finite model comparator for this seed, not as a theorem for the coupled PDE or a claim about the in-vivo front.

The matched steepness sweep changes `aρ` and `aφ` together, as in the source initial condition, with all other notebook parameters fixed. Representative `Nx=1600, Nt=6400` results are:

These are finite model runs, not intervention measurements. The full solver and matched scalar comparator agree within about 3% through the tested steep-tail regime, with growing discrepancy at `aφ=80`. The numerical result shows how a lab-frame front rate can be dominated by tissue flow while the rate relative to local flow falls; it does not prove a pulled/pushed classification for nature. At the very steep end, refined-grid values vary more, and the continuum scale is below a cell.

Within the full PDE family, the following two parameter sets give a constructive speed-only alias at `Nx=3200, Nt=25600`:

| `aρ=aφ` | `α` | 0–8 h `φ=0.5` secant | Mean local `V` | `q₀/q*` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | `3.5×10⁻⁶` | 21.77502 μm/h | 10.92332 μm/h | 0.0865 |
| 8 | `1.934×10⁻⁶` | 21.77423 μm/h | 17.99760 μm/h | 0.2139 |

Both profile exponents are shallow in their respective scalar reductions. The speed difference is `0.00079 μm/h`; `α` differs by `44.7%`. The velocity field and the initial profile differ, so joint profile and same-observable velocity data can identify these cases. The source’s notebook fixes `aρ=aφ=4`; it does not report a biological calibration of that seed exponent in Table S1. This is a constructive **single-front-speed** non-identifiability result, not evidence that the paper’s joint parameter fit is false.

The scalar model alone has the same non-injectivity: for adjusted target speed `c=10.8484 μm/h`, any admissible shallow exponent `q` defines `α(q)=[q(c−Dq)−Δk]/ΔE`. The replay gives three positive pairs at `q={0.005333,0.01,0.02} μm⁻¹` with `α={3.535×10⁻⁶,7.477×10⁻⁶,1.575×10⁻⁵}` and identical `c` up to floating-point rounding. This is an elementary conditional inversion failure, not new KPP theory.

## Finite-window stall boundary and why it does not transfer

Define the mean front rate relative to local Eulerian flow over `[t₀,t₁]` as

\[
\bar c_{\rm rel}=\frac{x_{1/2}(t_1)-x_{1/2}(t_0)}{t_1-t_0}
 -\frac{1}{t_1-t_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_1}v(x_{1/2}(t),t)\,dt.
\]

For `Nx=3200, Nt=25600`, matched initial steepness `aφ=110` gives `+0.147 μm/h` over 0–8 h, `aφ=115` gives `+0.0017`, `aφ=116` gives `−0.0258`, and `aφ=120` gives `−0.132`. The solution of the finite-grid model depends continuously on the seed steepness while the profile remains regular and has a unique crossing, so this sign change brackets a finite-model zero in `[115,116]`. But the 6–8 h rate is already `−0.015 μm/h` at `aφ=110`, demonstrating strong finite-window dependence. The model therefore supplies no robust, time-independent critical steepness from these 8 h runs.

At `aφ=115`, the tanh far-tail e-fold length is `L/(2aφ)≈6.52 μm`, compared with area-derived cell spacings `1/√ρ_A≈12.2 μm` and `1/√ρ_B≈11.4 μm`. The source grid is `15 μm` and the scalar pulled-threshold e-fold length is `16.2 μm`, about one source grid cell. The reported zero is both unresolved by the paper’s mesh and below a cell-scale continuum interpretation. It is useful as a stop rule: do not propose a steepness-based biological switch from this PDE or its movie.

The source Supplement Table S1 prints `η=10⁴ Pa·s`, while the pinned notebook parameter cell sets `η=10³ Pa·s` before unit conversion; both give the same `γ=0.8×10³ Pa·s/μm²`. The notebook value exactly reproduces the released output. Substituting the table viscosity at the source grid gives `φ` RMSE `2.37×10⁻⁶`, density RMSE `1.28×10⁻⁷`, and velocity RMSE `6.36×10⁻⁴` against the released arrays, with less than `0.003 μm/h` change in the front secant. This documents a source inconsistency and its small effect for this baseline; it does not decide which viscosity value the authors intended or its effect elsewhere.

## Limits, costs, and status

The replay uses selected pinned model files and Python/NumPy only. Source-grid execution takes under a second. The finest runs use an exact DST-I solve of the notebook’s discrete force operator and take tens of seconds; no Mathematica executable, wet-lab work, or new biological data were used. The finite-difference replay and parameter sweeps are reproducible in [replay_coupled_fd.py](replay_coupled_fd.py); released-array scalar diagnostics are in [replay_tail_selection.py](replay_tail_selection.py) and [replay_tail_selection.json](replay_tail_selection.json). Derivations, provenance, failures, and restart gates are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md), [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md), and [FAILURES_AND_GATES.md](FAILURES_AND_GATES.md).

The 2025 authors report that migration and bulk tissue displacement remain difficult to disentangle. The model’s initial steepness is not a directly measured control input, the alternative α/profile pair changes the Eulerian velocity field, and the Fickian relative flux and force closure remain assumptions. Ex-vivo interface slopes and tracked-cell speeds are different observables and were not used as held-out validation here. The 2026 Fog/receptor relay remains a separate biological system; no numerical cross-tissue law is inferred.

**Status:** exact reproduction of one released coupled-model run; base-profile grid/time convergence; source-specific conditional parameter non-identifiability; finite diagnostic sweeps and estimator audit. No new biological propagation principle, regeneration capability, intervention dose law, nature-level `α`, external expert review, or novelty priority is established.
