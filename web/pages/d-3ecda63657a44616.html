N20 — PHASE II PRIOR-ART / PREEMPTION AUDIT
Persistent heredity in protocells before templated genomes
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: v2 is an append-only correction to the frozen v1 record. It supersedes any implication in v1 that the seed-family architecture is an original foundational mechanism. Do not silently edit v1; interpret its equations only under the conditional assumptions below.

DECISION
No original principal mechanism or new physical capability is established here. The core–periphery seed principle, multiple heritable autocatalytic cores, compartmental partition loss, alternative chemical states, and selection without template replication were all proposed and analyzed before this record. A paper published days before this task also reports a close model of selectable pre-template state variation in dividing compartments. The valid result left in v1 is an internally derived exact finite-copy fission calculation for a specific, idealized interchangeable-seed model. It is a conditional mathematical extension, not a chemical discovery, priority claim, or solution to how the replication function first arises.

1. DIRECT PREEMPTION BY CORE–PERIPHERY HEREDITY
Vasas, Fernando, Santos, Kauffman and Szathmáry, “Evolution before genes,” Biology Direct 7, 1 (2012), DOI 10.1186/1745-6150-7-1, gives the key idea directly. The article divides an autocatalytic network into autocatalytic cores and dependent peripheries; says one molecule of a core species can seed production of all core and peripheral species; describes multiple distinct cores and their alternative stable network states; and discusses stochastic core loss at compartment division as mutation/heredity (primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284417/). The relevant text is especially explicit at article sections around the core definition and its Figure 2, and the genotype/phenotype discussion: lines 151–170 and 253–256 in the PMC text.

This predates and preempts the broad regenerative-core heredity principle used in v1, though the exact graph motif is not identical. V1 assumes each complete k-member core can rebuild one shared state and that there are r such disjoint interchangeable seeds. The 2012 core–periphery model treats core molecules as regenerative seeds of their corresponding peripheries and treats multiple cores as selectable hereditary units. Its seed condition can be weaker than v1's: one core molecule is described as sufficient. Thus, the most defensible distinction in v1 is the exact finite-occupancy arithmetic for a chosen k-member interchangeable-seed family and its sibling-correlated fission outcomes, plus a particular synthesis-turnover ledger. That distinction has not been searched exhaustively for mathematical priority and should not be described as novel.

A second direct antecedent is Matsubara et al., “Robustness of compositional heredity to the growth and division dynamics of prebiotic compartments,” later published as “Conditions for Darwinian evolution in compartmentalized autocatalytic reaction networks,” arXiv:2211.03155 (2022). It studies multiple autocatalytic compositional growth states, gives dilution and growth–division stability bounds, and incorporates stochastic variation and differential reproduction. The article explicitly identifies earlier multiple-core proposals and concludes that autocatalytic states can support heredity, variation and selection in parameter regimes (https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03155). This means v1's general claims about exact growth/selection equations are baseline modeling, not a new answer to whether pre-template chemical heredity is possible.

2. NEAR-CONTEMPORARY RESULT
Iván, Szilágyi and Szathmáry, “Bistability of compartmentalized autocatalytic systems at the dawn of hereditary variation,” PNAS 123(39), e2621400123 (article date 2026-09-21; publication date 2026-09-29), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2621400123, was published before the campaign date. Its PubMed-indexed abstract reports two mutually interacting autocatalytic networks with alternative stable chemical states maintained by weak cross-catalysis, environmental switching in reproducing compartments, stochastic partitioning that creates compartments dominated by the previously disfavored state, and amplification by selection (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42766742/). The PNAS full text redirected to a cookie/login page in this inspection; therefore this is an abstract-level comparison only. Do not infer its detailed reaction scheme, molecular identity, thermodynamic ledger, or finite-copy assumptions from the abstract alone.

This publication preempts the broad proposed outcome—multiple pregenetic chemical states carried through compartment division and selected—more directly than the 2012 paper. The abstract describes a theoretical result and a dynamical signature for future experiments, not demonstration of a prebiotic chemical implementation. It does not settle whether a physically identified, locally detailed-balanced network can pay its fuel costs and reproduce in a membrane compartment. Those remain empirical and mechanistic questions, but v1 did not answer them either.

3. WHAT THE EXACT CALCULATION DOES ESTABLISH
Under v1's explicit artificial partition model, there are r disjoint k-molecule cores, each is sufficient to regenerate the same specified chemical state, and each of the kr molecules is assigned independently to either daughter with probability 1/2. Write a=2^-k. Then the exact per-daughter retention probability and sibling offspring probabilities are

    q = 1-(1-a)^r,
    P0 = (1-2a)^r,
    P2 = 1-2(1-a)^r+(1-2a)^r,
    P1 = 2[(1-a)^r-(1-2a)^r].

The mean active offspring number is 2q. For the non-degenerate supercritical case, the Galton–Watson survival probability is (2q-1)/P2. For k=2,r=3 this gives q=37/64, P0=1/8, P1=19/32, P2=9/32, and survival 5/9. Exhaustive enumeration in mechanism.py verifies the closed forms for the script's small cases. Because a parent’s finite inventory is split between its sisters, their states are correlated; treating them as independent Bernoulli(q) births would be wrong.

The expected ideal seed-restoration turnover count in the same model is kr[1-(1-2^-k)^(r-1)], with k=2,r=3 giving 21/8. It assumes one unit-fuel turnover per missing catalyst molecule, no reverse synthesis turnover and no catalyst degradation. A detailed-balanced stoichiometric placeholder can represent each net step as P+F ⇌ X+W with a catalyst complex returned unchanged, but no actual molecular reaction set, rate constants, chemical potentials, membrane chemistry, or observed fuel yield has been specified. Therefore v1's local-detailed-balance paragraph shows how a model could be thermodynamically driven; it does not establish a thermodynamically parameterized physical realization.

These formulas are exact bookkeeping conditional on seed sufficiency. They do not derive that sufficiency. V1 writes the central step as C_a + P_j + F_j ⇌ C_a + X_j + W_j for each state-defining species, explicitly granting the core-catalysed rebuilding ability the task asked us to explain. Naming a reaction for every output makes the assumption visible, but does not derive a chemically plausible catalyst, selectivity, rate, or regenerative pathway. The candidate therefore does not meet the task's stronger “without assuming the replication function to be explained” gate.

4. BOUNDARIES AND VALID CLAIMS
The following claims may be retained as model-internal facts, with the assumptions stated: (i) finite dilution can destroy even an autocatalytic state; (ii) a complete seed set can mathematically lower inheritance burden relative to requiring every peripheral component; (iii) correlated sibling fission changes persistence probabilities; (iv) reconstruction consumes matter and free energy, and a fuel-free equilibrium cannot support ongoing net synthesis; and (v) state distinction and selection require finite observable reporters and differential viable growth. The code checks algebra, not chemical feasibility or empirical truth.

The following claims should not be retained: that the seed-family idea is a new heredity principle; that the arithmetic removes Eigen's error threshold in a general chemical sense; that the proposed placeholder reactions constitute a realized autocatalytic system; that derived branching survival establishes long-lived individual lineages; or that population-supercriticality proves stable coexistence under shared resources. Multiple-core and core–periphery inheritance are known, exact reliability needs to be scoped to this model family, and the stochastic formulas assume independent molecular partition and complete seed sufficiency.

5. DECISIVE OPEN PROBLEM AFTER PREEMPTION
A meaningful new result would need to replace the assumed seed-regeneration map with a chemically grounded finite reaction network whose catalyst specificity and rates can be calculated or measured. For each proposed state, one must provide atom- and charge-balanced reactions, a finite fuel/waste cycle satisfying local detailed balance, stochastic seed establishment under finite copy number, reverse reactions and leakage, state-specific reporter outcomes, and coupling from measured chemical flux to compartment growth and fission. Then compute the finite chemical master equation's daughter-state transition kernel and show a nonempty regime with both (a) supercritical persistence of more than one state-bearing lineage and (b) differential growth under a changed environment. A counterexample should be sought where ideal partition predicts persistence but finite-rate reconstruction, reverse turnover, or leakage makes the true mean viable offspring at most one.

The present work does not pass that gate. The most useful contribution to the larger campaign is the prior-art correction plus an exact reference calculation that can be reused as a baseline for any later chemically instantiated model.
