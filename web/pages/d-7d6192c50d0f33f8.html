# Independent reconstruction of the canonical SOS metric and residual space

Analytic PASS for the exact optimizer theorem and rational metric-confidence

complexity remain OPEN. SOS/determinant optimization and symmetry are prior
methods; historical novelty of this characterization has not been established.

Input is only the expanded rational quartic f, with the unknown rational
invertible L, rational Q>0 promise f(x)=m(Lx)^T Q m(Lx), m(y)=(y_i^2).
The proposed learner is not supplied L, Q, a metric or a conditioning bound.
Let z be all ordinary quadratic monomials. The coefficient-only program is

    maximize log det M, M>0,
    A(G)=f, [[G,m(M)],[m(M)^T,1]] >=0,

where m(M) now means the coefficient vector of x^T M x and A(G)=z^T G z.
These are two explicitly different uses of m, not interchangeable interfaces.
The PSD block has order n(n+1)/2+1.

A positive definite Gram for f exists: in hidden coordinates add small positive
mixed-monomial diagonal entries and subtract half those coefficients from
the corresponding mass-block offdiagonals of Q. For sufficiently small
entries the mass block remains PD. The invertible quadratic action of L
preserves PD. A sufficiently small M>0 supplies strict feasibility.
Feasible M are bounded by evaluating f at coordinate vectors and applying
|M_ij|^2<=M_ii M_jj. PSD Gram matrices representing a fixed homogeneous f
are bounded: the sphere moment matrix E[z z^T] is PD and Tr(G E[z z^T])
is the fixed sphere integral of f. Thus compactness of the closure and a
positive feasible determinant give an attained positive optimum. Strict
concavity gives uniqueness of M_*.

Hidden coordinate sign flips preserve f, SOS and determinant under congruence.
Uniqueness forces M_*=L^T diag(d) L with d_i>0. This does not mean the hidden
sign flips or their frame have been computationally acquired.

## An alternative proof avoiding an assumed contact support

Minimize on p>0 the strictly convex coercive function

    phi(p)=p^T Qp/2 -(1/n) sum_i log p_i.

The unique minimizer is interior and satisfies Qp=d, d_i=1/(n p_i)>0.
Consequently p^T Qp=d^T p=1 and d^T Q^-1 d=1. Cauchy in the Q metric gives
Q-dd^T >=0, with rank n-1, so diag(d) is SOS feasible.
For any OTHER feasible aligned d'>0, evaluate its pointwise inequality at
squared coordinates p. It yields d'^T p<=1. Arithmetic-geometric mean gives

    product_i(d'_i p_i) <= (1/n)^n = product_i(d_i p_i).

Thus product d'<=product d, proving optimality and the full-support contact
formula without assuming KKT positivity of an unknown support. This also
shows equality points include all 2^n sign choices of y_i=sqrt(p_i), and
Cauchy shows these are exactly the contact rays up to common scale.

## Unique residual Gram

Every square q(y)^2 in any quadratic SOS of the optimal residual must vanish
at every contact. On the signed cube, mixed monomials y_i y_j have distinct
nonconstant Walsh characters, whereas diagonal monomials are constants p_i.
Therefore q has no mixed coefficient and its diagonal coefficient a obeys
a^T p=0. Hence each PSD residual Gram has range in the same (n-1)-dimensional
quadratic subspace D_0. On the diagonal-monomial space the map from a symmetric
Gram to quartic coefficients is injective: y_i^4 and y_i^2 y_j^2 identify all
entries. Thus that Gram is uniquely Q-dd^T. Congruence through L preserves
uniqueness and rank. Adding the metric quadratic supplies the missing
independent direction, since d^T p=1, and recovers EXACTLY the full hidden
space span{ell_i^2} as a mathematical property of the optimizer.

A floating-point Gram need not have this exact range. No threshold on its
singular values, rational-space rounding or bit-cost follows from uniqueness
alone. Generalized simultaneous diagonalization becomes legitimate only after
the rational space, an SPD member and a separating member have been acquired.

## Rational posterior metric certificate

For rational primal Mc>0, Gc with A(Gc)=f and Gc-mc mc^T>=0, take a rational
quartic functional lambda, K=A*lambda, and w satisfying
2w^T m(M)=Tr(Mc^-1 M). If [[K,-w],[-w^T,alpha]]>=0, every feasible M obeys

    Tr(Mc^-1 M) <= m(M)^T K m(M)+alpha <= lambda(f)+alpha=:c.

The second inequality uses K>=0 and G-m m^T>=0. At M=Mc this gives c>=n.
AM-GM of positive generalized eigenvalues then gives
log(det M*/det Mc)<=n log(c/n)<=c-n=:delta.
Optimality of log det at M* on the convex feasible set gives
Tr(M*^-1 Mc)<=n. Therefore the eigenvalues lambda_i of M*^-1/2 Mc M*^-1/2
satisfy sum_i(lambda_i-1-log lambda_i)<=delta.
For rational 0<a<1/2 and 2delta<=a^2, integration of (t-1)/t gives

    (1-a)M* <= Mc <= M*/(1-a).

Equivalently (1-zeta)Mc<=M*<=(1+zeta)Mc is safe with zeta=a/(1-a).
All supplied posterior checks are rational equality and PSD/PD checks. Finding
the certificate at useful accuracy remains a computational task, and must
return UNKNOWN if it has not done so. It is not an independently supplied
true metric, a noisy-source guarantee, or a speed advantage over ordinary SOS.
