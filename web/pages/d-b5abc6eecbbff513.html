Cycle06 literature memo — KMS p=1 regularity and root Dirichlet energy
Status as checked 2026-10-09; primary-source PDFs and SHA-256 records are in sources/ and SOURCES.txt.

Decision
For a faithful-state KMS-symmetric semigroup, the requested universal pointwise comparison between entropy production J and the square-root/KMS Dirichlet energy E is not established by the checked literature. It is exactly the p=1 endpoint of quantum Lp-regularity. Kastoryano–Temme explicitly conjecture weak regularity for every primitive generator and strong regularity for reversible generators; their “reversible” convention is KMS symmetry. Gao–Wang v2 (5 Oct 2026) explicitly says strong Lp-regularity is not known to follow from KMS symmetry, while proving an MLSI by another route. No counterexample to this J/E comparison appears in the checked sources. Thus a positive universal constant is conjectural here, not proved or refuted by these sources.

For GNS-symmetric generators the comparison is a theorem: Beigi–Datta–Rouzé, Theorem 14, gives J >= 4 E, including the tracial/HS-self-adjoint case. KMS symmetry alone is weaker: it is self-adjointness in the s=1/2 sandwich inner product, while GNS symmetry is s=1 (and implies modular commutation). This distinction is the load-bearing gap, not a change of notation.

Exact interface and constants
Use Heisenberg convention: P_t=e^{-tL}, L is positive, P_t is unital CP, and sigma is faithful and invariant. Write Gamma_sigma(X)=sigma^{1/2} X sigma^{1/2}; KMS symmetry means Gamma_sigma P_t=P_{t*} Gamma_sigma, where * is the trace dual. For a faithful state rho, set f=Gamma_sigma^{-1}(rho) and I_{2,1}(f)=Gamma_sigma^{-1/2}(sqrt(rho)). Let L_* be the trace-dual positive generator and T_L=Gamma_sigma^{-1/2} L_* Gamma_sigma^{1/2}. Then

  J(rho) = Tr[L_*(rho)(log rho-log sigma)] = 2 E_1(f),
  E(rho) = Tr[sqrt(rho) T_L(sqrt(rho))] = E_2(I_{2,1}(f)).

The second identity follows from KMS balance: T_L=Gamma_sigma^{1/2} L Gamma_sigma^{-1/2}, so the Hilbert–Schmidt form in sqrt(rho) is the KMS L2 Dirichlet form of I_{2,1}(f). Kastoryano–Temme Definition 9 gives weak regularity at p=1 as E_1(f)>=E_2(I_{2,1}f), hence J>=2E. Strong regularity at p=1 is E_1(f)>=2E_2(I_{2,1}f), hence J>=4E. The p=1 inequalities are the exact requested comparison; all-p weak/strong regularity is stronger.

For a KMS-symmetric channel P, its Poissonization P_t=exp[-t(I-P)] is a KMS-symmetric QMS, so the same generator comparison applies to L=I-P. In the nonprimitive case, if needed, P_epsilon=(1-epsilon)P+epsilon E_sigma with E_sigma(X)=Tr(sigma X)I is primitive and KMS-symmetric; taking epsilon down to zero would transfer any already-proved primitive pointwise inequality to the channel. This regularization does not prove the missing primitive inequality.

Strongest matching prior and the boundary
1. Kastoryano–Temme (2013), arXiv:1207.3261v2, Definitions 6–9 and Conjecture 19. Definition 6 sets detailed balance to Gamma_sigma L=L* Gamma_sigma (KMS symmetry); Definition 9 gives the exact weak/strong Lp comparisons above. Conjecture 19 states every primitive Liouvillian is weakly Lp-regular, and that if reversible it is strongly Lp-regular. In their convention reversible is the KMS detailed balance condition. This is a conjecture, not their theorem. They prove sufficient criteria and examples (including unital, projection, and thermal families), not the arbitrary-KMS assertion. See HTML lines 231–236, 238–276, 279–289, 458–497.

2. Beigi–Datta–Rouzé (2020), arXiv:1804.10100v2, Theorem 14. Their hypothesis is self-adjointness of L in the GNS inner product Tr(sigma X*Y), called strongly sigma-reversible; it implies KMS symmetry and modular commutation. The theorem proves E_p(I_{p,2}X)>=E_q(I_{q,2}X) for 0<p<=q<=2. Taking p=1,q=2 gives J>=4E under the interface above. Their Remark 2 identifies the maximally mixed case with earlier tracial work. This covers all tracial Hilbert–Schmidt-self-adjoint Poisson generators and is the decisive established comparator, but its GNS hypothesis cannot be dropped by renaming KMS symmetry. See HTML lines 481–492, 564–572. Their proof uses modular-eigenoperator Kraus operators and a positive exponential-sum representation (lines 586–610), which KMS self-adjointness alone does not supply.

3. Gao–Wang (latest v2, 5 Oct 2026), arXiv:2609.20753v2. They define alpha_1 MLSI and alpha_2 LSI, state alpha_1>=alpha_2 under strong Lp-regularity and explicitly say strong regularity is satisfied for GNS-symmetric semigroups but “not known to follow from KMS symmetry” (Section 3.1, lines 303–319). Their Theorem B / Theorem 3.2 proves alpha_1 >= lambda/(2+log||sigma^{-1}||_infty) from primitive coercivity/hyperboundedness without Lp-regularity (lines 346–398). This is an entropy-vs-relative-entropy estimate with a state-spectrum loss; it does not establish J>=cE. It does confirm the open conversion remains after the newer MLSI theorem.

4. Liu–Wan–Wu (arXiv v1, 12 Sep 2026), arXiv:2609.13726v1. Theorem 3.1 proves every primitive finite-dimensional KMS-symmetric QMS has some MLSI constant beta>0 (lines 161–176). The proof uses q=inf Q(X)>0 over a compact centered sphere and then gives alpha_MLSI >= q^2/[14 ||L||_{BKM,rho}(kappa-1)], with kappa at least 1/lambda_min(rho) (lines 177–197, 232–241). This is system/generator dependent and compares Fisher information to relative entropy, not to the root Dirichlet energy.

The paper’s Proposition 5.1 gives a primitive KMS-but-not-GNS qubit example: V=[[0,1],[2,0]], rho=diag(1,4)/5, L(X)=1/2{V*V,X}-V*XV (lines 310–353). Theorem 5.2 says a non-scalar commutant of the Lindblad noise makes CMLSI constant zero; the M2 ancilla witnesses it (lines 356–373, proof 396–427). The actual diagnostic quotient tends to a GNS s=1 Rayleigh quotient as an ancilla weight r tends to zero (lines 402–425). This is not the target J/E ratio: J is the BKM entropy-production form, and E is the square-root KMS L2 form. Their example is therefore not an obstruction to the proposed comparison without an additional conversion; it also is not evidence for the comparison.

5. Vernooij–Wirth (2023), arXiv:2303.15949v1, Theorem 4.2 and Section 5. For uniformly continuous KMS-symmetric QMSs on a von Neumann algebra, they represent the KMS L2 generator as delta*delta for a derivation into a correspondence (lines 713–739); quantum Dirichlet forms and KMS-symmetric QMSs correspond (lines 982–1002), and Theorem 5.4 represents general quantum Dirichlet forms by a derivation (lines 1022–1035). This validates the quadratic KMS energy structure but contains no comparison to the BKM/p=1 form. The paper explicitly distinguishes GNS symmetry via modular commutation (lines 160–173).

6. Carlen–Maas (2017 journal; arXiv:1609.01254v1), Theorem 7.6. Their sigma-detailed-balance class has a state-dependent transport metric for which the QMS is gradient flow of relative entropy (lines 831–841), and their inner-product definitions distinguish GNS s=1 from KMS s=1/2 (lines 159–179). This produces entropy-dissipation inequalities in their gradient geometry, but it does not identify the target square-root KMS Dirichlet form or supply the p=1 regularity comparison. It is not a conversion of the desired J/E statement.

Liu–Wan–Wu scope check
- The primitive-KMS MLSI theorem is positive but not a universal J/E theorem; its proof constant depends on the BKM coercivity quotient, generator norm, and inverse smallest stationary eigenvalue.
- The primitive KMS/non-GNS CMLSI failure is an amplified example. Its limiting zero quotient is GNS-weighted and compares Fisher information with relative entropy. It does not show J/E tends to zero because the target denominator is the KMS root energy. CMLSI failure therefore neither proves nor refutes the requested inequality.
- Their nonprimitive KMS example without MLSI is also a statement about relative entropy to the ergodic projection. It is not a root-energy counterexample.

Concrete missing lemma
For all faithful sigma and all KMS-symmetric primitive finite-dimensional QMS generators L, prove either
  E_1(f) >= c E_2(I_{2,1}f) for every positive f,
with one universal c>0 (which yields J>=2cE), or give a sequence of KMS examples and states for which E_1/E_2(I_{2,1}f)->0. The primary literature checked gives c=1 conjecturally from weak regularity, c=2 conjecturally from strong regularity, and c=2 as a theorem under GNS symmetry. No exact KMS-only proof or counterexample was found in this narrow audit; absence is not a novelty claim.
