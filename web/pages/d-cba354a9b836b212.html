    u=1+xy,

For independent target variables (a,b,c), define

    V={P in C[a,b,c]: partial_t^2[P(F)]=0}.

The condition is an identity on the whole original source; the target degree is unrestricted. Put

## Independent source-field and operator reconstruction

Take w=y+1/x, v=1/x, c=F3 in the generic source field. Their rational inverse is

    x=1/v, y=w-v, t=5v^2-3wv-cv^3.

They are algebraically independent. Direct substitution in (A1) gives the POLYNOMIAL map to C[w,v,c]

    rho(a)=w^2+wv-cw^3,
    rho(b)=4w+2v-3cw^2, rho(c)=c.                 (A3)

At fixed w,v, the original derivative is partial_t=-v^(-3)partial_c. Consequently P belongs to V exactly when rho(P) has c-degree at most one. This is an equivalence of fraction-field expressions and hence of polynomial identities. The generic computation does not discard x=0, c=0, or other source loci.

The exact all-degree divisibility operator also follows directly. The independent target variables remain algebraically independent under F because its Jacobian is nonzero. The reciprocal relation is

    P0(W)=cW^3-2W^2+bW-2a, P0(w)=0.              (A4)

P0 is irreducible in C[a,b,c,W]: viewed as a polynomial in a it is linear with unit coefficient -2 and primitive, so Gauss gives irreducibility. Viewed as a polynomial in W it is primitive because its W^2 coefficient is the unit -2. Gauss therefore makes it irreducible over C(a,b,c). It is the minimal polynomial of w over that target field.

The original velocity is

    partial_t F=v^(-3)(w^3,3w^2,-1).

Since F is affine in t, the chain rule gives, for ANY target polynomial P,

    partial_t^2[P(F)]=v^(-6) S_P(w),
    S_P(W)=W^6 Paa+6W^5 Pab+9W^4 Pbb
             -2W^3 Pac-6W^2 Pbc+Pcc.             (A5)

All derivatives in (A5) are evaluated at the target outputs when pulled back. Thus

    P in V iff P0 divides S_P in C(a,b,c)[W].     (A6)

## Uniform homogeneous-cone proof, reconstructed without bounded interpolation

Give v,c degree one and w degree zero. Ordinary target-homogeneous P_D of degree D has top (v,c)-homogeneous pullback

    rho0(P_D),
    rho0(a)=wv-cw^3, rho0(b)=2v-3cw^2, rho0(c)=c. (A7)

The omitted parts w^2 and 4w in (A3) have degree zero. Lower target degree cannot contribute to degree D in this filtration. The Jacobian of the three expressions in (A7) with respect to (w,v,c) is 2v, nonzero. Thus rho0 is injective, so a nonzero top P_D cannot disappear after substitution. One elementary justification of the Jacobian-to-injectivity implication in characteristic zero is to differentiate a nonzero algebraic relation of minimal degree: invertibility of the Jacobian in the fraction field makes all its first partial derivatives vanish under substitution, contradicting minimality until a nonzero constant relation is reached.

If rho(P) has c-degree at most one, each of its (v,c)-homogeneous parts has c-degree at most one. In particular

    deg_c rho0(P_D)<=1.                          (A8)

This implication is uniform in D and prevents cancellation between high c powers of different target degrees: they live in different filtration degrees.

The cone polynomial R0 is irreducible. As a polynomial in c it is linear over C(a,b), and its coefficients 27a^2 and b^3 are coprime in C[a,b]. Gauss makes it irreducible and hence prime in C[a,b,c]. Its direction-cone parametrization is

    (a,b,c)=lambda(-w^3,-3w^2,1).

It covers the dense open subset of R0=0 with b,c nonzero: set lambda=c and w=3a/b. The cone relation then reconstructs both a and b. In particular the open set is nonempty, and prime irreducibility prevents its complement from being a hidden component.

Therefore, for a positive-degree homogeneous Q,

    Q(-w^3,-3w^2,1)=0  iff  R0 divides Q.         (A9)

The forward implication extends the vanishing by homogeneity to every lambda and then to the prime cone. This does not just assert that a one-parameter curve is dense in a two-dimensional cone; the scaling parameter is explicitly used. No rational map is being accepted as a global polynomial source chart.

The decisive exact factor is

    rho0(R0)=v^2(8v-9w^2c), deg_c rho0(R0)=1.    (A10)

Suppose a nonzero homogeneous Q of degree m>0 has rho0(Q) independent of c. Because rho0(Q) is (v,c)-homogeneous of degree m, it must equal v^m A(w). At v=0 this gives

    c^m Q(-w^3,-3w^2,1)=0.

By (A9), Q=R0 Q1. Injectivity ensures rho0(Q1) is nonzero. The polynomial domain makes c-degrees additive, so (A10) yields

    deg_c rho0(Q)=1+deg_c rho0(Q1)>=1,

contradicting independence of c. Hence the ONLY nonzero homogeneous Q with c-independent rho0(Q) has degree zero. This is an all-degree lemma, not a recursive experiment or a guessed leading-coefficient pattern.

Now let P in V have target degree D>=2. The coefficient of c^D in rho0(P_D) is P_D(-w^3,-3w^2,1), which must vanish by (A8). Equation (A9) forces P_D=R0 Q with Q homogeneous of degree D-3. Degree2 is thus impossible. Equations (A8),(A10) force rho0(Q) to be c-independent. The lemma makes Q constant, so D=3. No polynomial in V has degree greater than three.

The corrected cubic is in V by the literal identity

    rho(R)=4(v-w)(2w+v)(w+2v)+9c w^2(w^2-v^2).  (A11)

Subtracting its scalar multiple removes the degree-three top part of any P in V. The remainder cannot have degree two and is therefore affine. The affine functions and R all satisfy the condition. This proves (A2) in ALL degrees, independently of the cycle17 113-by20 matrix certificate.

## Entire filtered Keller-completion consequence
