Abstract

Perturbative partial-wave amplitudes diverge in cases with a massless exchanged particle in the 
t
tt-channel. We argue that the divergence is an artifact of perturbation theory and give a prescription for the all-orders correction factor that renders the partial waves finite. As an example, we apply this to longitudinal 
W^{+}W^{-}
W+​W−W^{+}W^{-} elastic scattering, for which there is a photon exchange 
t
tt-channel contribution, and derive improved quasi-perturbative unitarity bounds on the mass of the Higgs. The method is also useful for 
t
tt-channel exchange of very light particles.

The partial-wave decomposition plays an important role in many advances in particle physics. It is used in analysis of experimental data to uncover the quantum numbers of resonances seen in scattering. On the theory side, it is used extensively in exploiting the consequences of unitarity on scattering amplitudes. In this vein, the conditions that unitarity imposes on partial waves were central to establishing upper bounds on scattering amplitudes at asymptotically large center of mass (CM) energies [1, 2]. Famously, partial-wave unitarity was used to establish the strength of Higgs self interactions, or, equivalently, the Higgs mass, for which perturbation theory remains applicable [3, 4, 5]. Among many other examples, an extension of that idea has been more recently used to obtain bounds on di-boson scalar resonances [6], on multi-Higgs extensions of the Standard Model (SM) [7, 8], on the strength of ALP interactions [9] and on dimension-6 and -8 operators in an effective field theory extension of the SM (SMEFT) [10, 11, 12, 13].

Unitarity bounds at the electroweak scale play a key role in defining the Higgs Effective Field Theory (HEFT) which displays non-decoupling new physics [14, 15], in contrast to SMEFT in which the new physics decouples.

However, when a massless particle is exchanged in the 
t
tt-channel, the resulting scattering amplitude displays a singularity in the forward direction, a non-integrable divergence that renders the partial-wave amplitudes ill-defined. This is of course well known: it is precisely the same divergence displayed by the Rutherford scattering amplitude. In this letter we give a prescription that renders the partial waves finite. The clue to the resolution is to include in the computation non-perturbative effects. Short of proving this assertion, we will present evidence that we think is both suggestive and persuasive, and hope to come back to providing a proof in the future.
