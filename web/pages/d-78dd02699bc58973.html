# BKL localization-gate audit (v1)

**Superseded verdict for the induction gate.** The bracket algebra and compact-support estimate reconstructed in this audit are correct conditional on pointwise order-(m) and order-((m+1)) tensor bounds. This file originally instantiated those bounds with BKL's target `b_r=K^(r-1)((r-1)!)^2`, making that particular integrability argument post-theorem and circular if used during the induction. A separate, noncircular fixed-(n,r) envelope `C_{n,r}` from one-dimensional isotropic log-concave moments and the moment-cumulant partition formula has since closed the stochastic expectation gate; see `../../kls_proof_audit/FINAL_REPORT.md` lines 165–206 and the independent reconstruction in `../FINAL_REPORT.md`. The old b_r-only gate is retained as a superseded failed route, not as the current closure proof. Neither version is a separate lemma stated in BKL. The random quantity must be set to

\[
q_t:=\langle A_tu,u\rangle,\qquad \mathcal E_m(t):=|\kappa_m(t)(u)|_t^2,
\]

for fixed deterministic unit (u). Then (\mathbb E q_t=e^{-t}), but (q_t) is not asserted to decay pathwise. Compact support gives a pathwise bound on (q_t), which is the additional ingredient needed to integrate the *quadratic* bracket bound.

## Source-reported statements

1. **Cumulant tensor normalization and pointwise bound.** In §4, BKL define
   \[
   \langle R_m^\mu v,v\rangle=|\kappa_m^\mu(v,\cdot,\ldots,\cdot)|^2
   \]
   using the ordinary Euclidean Hilbert–Schmidt norm on the remaining (m-1) slots (TeX `eq_cumulant_matrix`; PDF p.18, eq. (65)). Theorem 4.1 says, for every dimension (n\), isotropic log-concave \(\mu\), and integer (m\ge2\),
   \[
   R_m^\mu\preceq b_m I,\qquad b_m=K^{m-1}((m-1)!)^2,
   \]
   where (K\) is universal (PDF p.18, eq. (66); TeX `eq_cumulant_bound`, with the induction constants at `eq_induction_constants`). Thus one may set (C_{n,m}=b_m); the source's bound is stronger than an (n)-dependent bound.

2. **Localization and mean covariance.** For compactly supported isotropic \(\mu\), BKL use Eldan localization (PDF pp.19–20, eqs. (67)–(69); TeX `eq_localization`, `eq_covariance_dynamics`). They state (e^tA_t) is a martingale and
   \[
   \mathbb EA_t=e^{-t}I
   \]
   (PDF p.20, eq. (70); TeX `eq_mean_covariance`). Compact support is introduced by standard approximation for proving Theorem 4.1 (PDF p.19, immediately before (67); TeX near `eq_localization`).

3. **Time-dependent tensor norm and posterior whitening.** BKL set
   \[
   |T|_t^2=\langle T,(A_t^{-1})^{\otimes r}T\rangle,
   \qquad \mathcal E_m(t)=|\kappa_m(t)(u)|_t^2
   \]
   (PDF p.20, eqs. (75)–(76); TeX `eq_covariance_norm`, `eq_cumulant_energy`). The push-forward \(\nu_t\) of \(\mu_t\) by \(x\mapsto A_t^{-1/2}(x-a_t)\) is isotropic, compactly supported, and log-concave. The affine cumulant identity gives
   \[
   \mathcal E_m(t)=|\kappa_m^{\nu_t}(A_t^{1/2}u)|^2
   \le b_m\langle A_tu,u\rangle
   \tag{*}
   \]
   whenever the order-(m) pointwise bound is available for all compactly supported isotropic log-concave laws (PDF p.21, eq. (77); TeX `eq_affine_cumulant`). This is a pathwise statement for each finite (t), not merely an expectation bound.

4. **Noise covariance contraction.** From the order-three estimate (R_3^\nu\preceq8I), BKL state
   \[
   \sum_i H_{i,t}A_t^{-1}H_{i,t}\preceq8A_t
   \tag{**}
   \]
   (PDF p.21, eq. (79); TeX `eq_covariance_noise_bound`). Their displayed derivation contracts the two covariance-normalized output slots of \(\kappa_3\), so the (A_t^{-1}\) in the middle is required and the constant is (8), not (8n).

5. **Energy diffusion coefficients.** For (T_t=\kappa_m(t)(u)), (W_{i,t}=\kappa_{m+1}(t)(u,\cdot^{m-1},A_t^{-1/2}e_i)), the source gives the paired dynamics (PDF p.22, eq. (81); TeX `eq_energy_pair`). At each time it normalizes with (P=A_t^{-1/2}):
   \[
   \widetilde T=P^{\otimes(m-1)}T,\quad
   \widetilde H_i=PH_iP,\quad
   \widetilde W_i=P^{\otimes(m-1)}W_i.
   \]
   (PDF p.22, eqs. (83)–(86); TeX `eq_energy_invariance`, `eq_normalized_coefficients`, `eq_normalized_ito`). The first-order coefficient of the Taylor expansion in PDF pp.22–23, eq. (88) (TeX `eq_joint_energy_expansion`) is therefore the scalar Brownian coefficient
   \[
   g_i=2\langle\widetilde T,\widetilde W_i\rangle
       -\langle\widetilde T,\Sigma_{\widetilde H_i}\widetilde T\rangle,
   \qquad
   \Sigma_H=\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}H^{(s)}.
   \]
   The source uses this expansion for the drift rather than naming this martingale coefficient separately.

## Reconstruction of the proposed bracket bound

For the local martingale part \(M\) of \(\mathcal E_m\), define
\[
M_t=\sum_i\int_0^t g_i(s)\,dB_{s,i}.
\]
The identities in PDF p.23, eq. (90) (TeX `eq_energy_identifications`) are
\[
|\widetilde T|^2=\mathcal E_m,\qquad
\sum_i|\widetilde W_i|^2=\mathcal E_{m+1}.
\]
The second identity contracts the last slot against \(\{A_t^{-1/2}e_i\}\); it is precisely the covariance-weighted norm on that slot, not an unweighted coordinate sum.

For each (i), Cauchy–Schwarz and \((a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2\) give
\[
g_i^2\le 8|\widetilde T|^2|\widetilde W_i|^2
 +2|\widetilde T|^2|\Sigma_{\widetilde H_i}\widetilde T|^2.
\]
By (**), in normalized coordinates \(\sum_i\widetilde H_i^2\preceq8I\). Applying this to each of the (m-1) output slots and using Cauchy–Schwarz across slots gives the exact source estimate
\[
\sum_i|\Sigma_{\widetilde H_i}\widetilde T|^2
\le8(m-1)^2|\widetilde T|^2
\tag{PDF p.23, eq. (91); TeX `eq_metric_error_bound`}.
\]
Consequently, pathwise,
\[
\frac{d\langle M\rangle_t}{dt}=\sum_i g_i(t)^2
\le8\mathcal E_m(t)\mathcal E_{m+1}(t)
 +16(m-1)^2\mathcal E_m(t)^2.
\]
Thus the two constants in the proposed bound follow exactly; no dimension factor or additional factor of two is missing.

Now let \(D=\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)\). For finite (t), the Radon–Nikodym derivative in (67) is strictly positive on the original support, so \(\mu_t\) has the same essential support. For unit (u), the range width of \(\langle u,X\rangle\) is at most (D); hence
\[
0\le q_t=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}\langle u,X\rangle\le D^2
\]
(in fact Popoviciu gives (D^2/4)). The upper bound is pathwise. In contrast,
\[
\mathbb E q_t=\langle (\mathbb EA_t)u,u\rangle=e^{-t},
\]
is only a mean-decay identity; no pathwise monotonicity or decay of (q_t) is used. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E q_t^2\le D^2\mathbb E q_t=D^2e^{-t}.
\]
Assuming the order-(m) and order-(m+1) bounds (*) with constants (C_{n,m}) and (C_{n,m+1}), respectively, Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative bracket integrand gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\langle M\rangle_\infty
&=\int_0^\infty\mathbb E\sum_i g_i(t)^2\,dt\\
&\le D^2\left[8C_{n,m}C_{n,m+1}
 +16(m-1)^2C_{n,m}^2\right]\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\,dt\\
&=D^2\left[8C_{n,m}C_{n,m+1}
 +16(m-1)^2C_{n,m}^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking (C_{n,r}=b_r) gives the version directly supported by Theorem 4.1. This finite expected bracket implies that (M) is an (L^2)-bounded martingale and converges in (L^2); expectation exchange is justified by Tonelli and the resulting finite integral. The Popoviciu refinement improves the right-hand side by a factor (1/4) if (D) is the support diameter.

## Limits and proof-use restriction

- The bracket inequality is reconstructed from BKL's equations (81), (85), (88), (90), and (91); BKL v1 does not state a process named (M), define (q_t), or state the integrated bracket estimate.
- This artifact's original choice (C_{n,r}=b_r) depends on Theorem 4.1. That choice is a **superseded failure as an induction-integrability justification** because it presupposes orders (m,m+1). The fixed-((n,r)) crude moment envelope in `kls_proof_audit/FINAL_REPORT.md` removes that circularity for the compact-support induction; it is not dimension/order-uniform and is not a replacement for the theorem's (b_r) conclusion.
- The compact-support reduction in the BKL theorem proof is by approximation (p.19 and p.25, eqs. (95)–(97), followed by the last paragraph of the proof around p.26). The new bracket estimate carries the factor (D^2), which may diverge along approximations. It does not by itself extend to a noncompact initial law or supply a uniform-in-approximation bracket bound.
- The source's original induction instead uses the drift inequality (80), the integrated-energy lemma (92)–(93), and bounds the lower-order (L_m) terms in (98) onward (PDF pp.23–26). This audit does not claim that the auxiliary gate is a missing or necessary step in that proof.
- All checks here concern source identities and the stated conditional reconstruction. No external mathematical validation is claimed.
