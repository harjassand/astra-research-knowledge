## Complete normalized quadratic-fibre classification

Every T in (Q2) has T=R+H, with H fibre-homogeneous quadratic. Its residual determinant is identically1: the determinant of a polynomial automorphism is a unit in A, and its value at R=0 is1. Hence tr J_R H=0 and det J_R H=0 separately by fibre degree.

Over K=C(x,y), divergence zero makes H=(P_eta,-P_rho) for a homogeneous fibre cubic P. This can also be obtained directly by integrating its six quadratic coefficients, with characteristic-zero divisions. For

    P=a rho^3+b rho^2eta+c rho eta^2+d eta^3,

the zero-Hessian equations are

    3ac=b^2, 9ad=bc, 3bd=c^2.

If a!=0, P=a(rho+b eta/(3a))^3; if a=0, b=c=0 and P=d eta^3. Thus, including scalar factors, every nonzero H has the form

    H=m(b) v(b) z^2,
    q=(q1,q2) primitive in A^2, v=(q2,-q1),
    z=q dot R.                                    (Q7)

To descend, clear denominators of the linear form and remove the gcd of q1,q2. The resulting q is primitive in the UFD A. A denominator in m would divide every coefficient of H, including m q1^3 and m q2^3; primitiveness excludes it. Hence m belongs to A. This uses the fraction field only in a proof; every resulting source map is polynomial. Conversely (Q7) is automatically a global polynomial automorphism with inverse R-mv(q dot R)^2, since q dot v=0.

If G dot v=0, the added fibre term is invisible and psi=h+G dot R, whose determinant is (Jac G)^2, nonconstant. Assume m and A0=G dot v are nonzero. Put

    a=m A0, e=max(deg q1,deg q2).

The maximum coefficient degree of a z^2 is exactly deg a+2e: the square of an entry of q of degree e has nonzero leading coefficient. The degree-seven bound therefore gives

    deg a+2e<=5.                                  (Q8)

Since A0 belongs to (x,y), deg A0>=1. Consequently e<=2. This is the complete degree reduction; larger coefficient degrees are not silently truncated.

## e=1: all affine moving directions and arbitrary scalar coefficients

The degree-four leading part of G dot v is G1_top(v1_top-2v2_top). Since deg(G dot v)<=3 from (Q8), cancellation forces v1_top=2v2_top. Hence

    v=(2L+k,L), q=(-L,2L+k),
    L=ax+by+c nonconstant affine, k!=0.             (Q11)

The shift in L absorbs the constant part of v2. Primitiveness forces k!=0; otherwise q has common factor L. The scalar m remains arbitrary subject to (Q8).

For a general polynomial a(b) in psi=h+G dot R+a z^2, (Q11) gives the exact fibre-degree-four part of its Hessian determinant

    (det H4)_R4=8 a^2 k^2 z^4
      [2 J(a,L)^2-a grad L^T adj(Hess_b a)grad L].   (Q12)

Here J(a,L)=a_x L_y-a_y L_x. Derivation: C=Hess_R psi=2a qq^T has rank1. Let n=(-q2,q1). The leading mixed contraction is Lmix n=2a z k grad L. The leading base block is

    B2=(Hess a)z^2
      +2z w(grad a grad L^T+grad L grad a^T)
      +2a w^2 grad L grad L^T,
    w=(-1,2) dot R.

The determinant of the leading mixed block is4ak z^2 J(a,L). In the exact block formula det H4=-tr(adj B Lmix adj C Lmix^T)+(det Lmix)^2, contraction against grad L kills the last two B2 summands. This yields (Q12). The fixed h and fibre-affine G terms are of lower fibre degree and cannot alter it.

Constancy forces the bracket in (Q12) to vanish. The nonzero constant derivation D=L_y partial_x-L_x partial_y differentiates along the L-level lines, in a complementary affine coordinate N, and the bracket is2(Da)^2-a D^2a. A polynomial of positive N degree r has nonzero leading factor r(r+1). Thus Da=0 and a belongs to C[L]. Since a=m A0, every divisor A0 must also belong to C[L]; factor a into parallel L-level lines to justify this UFD conclusion.

But

    A0=(2G1+G2)L+kG1                              (Q13)

never belongs to C[L] when L is nonconstant affine and k!=0. If b!=0, A0 lacks y^3, so any polynomial H(L) equalling it must have degree<=2, whereas its xy^2 coefficient is4b!=0. If b=0, a!=0, the coefficient of y is

Vanishing would successively force k=4a, c=-6a, then4a=0, contradicting a!=0. This contradiction includes every scalar m; one cannot clear it by polynomial multiplication. Therefore e=1 fails.

## e=2: a complete degree-budget descent to raw constant directions

Equation (Q8) forces m to be a nonzero constant and G dot v=alpha x+beta y. Let w=D2v. Solving

    2y w1+4x w2=alpha x+beta y

in A gives

    w=(beta/2,alpha/4)+H(b)(2x,-y).                 (Q14)

The vector D2^-1(2x,-y) has degree3, with first leading entry x^2(x+y) and second half that entry. For nonzero H its product has degree deg H+3>2 and cannot cancel D2^-1 applied to the constant vector, of degree at most2. Since deg v=2, H=0. Thus v=D2^-1 s for a nonzero constant raw direction s=(s1,s2); q=(-s2,s1)D2 depends only on x.

Its Wronskian W=q'_x dot(-q2,q1) is nonzero. Indeed the row q is unimodular over C[x]. If W=0, its entries are constant proportional; their coprimeness makes the row constant. But no nonzero constant combination of the rows of D2 is constant: cancelling degree2 forces row1-2row2=(x-6,8-2x), still nonconstant. This argument includes a zero row entry and does not assume both entries have positive degree.

For a=m(2s1y+4s2x), the exact fibre-degree-four term is

    (det H4)_R4=8 a^2 W^2 z^4(2a_y^2-a a_yy).      (Q15)

It is the same rank-one block derivation with q_y=0; terms involving q_xx vanish from the yy contraction. If s1!=0, a_yy=0 and a_y=2ms1!=0, so (Q15) is nonzero.

For s1=0, s2!=0, use the actual raw coordinates z0=row1(D2) dot R. The tail is4kappa x z0^2, kappa=m s2^3!=0. With

    det H4=J^2(4-8kappa x h_yy).                   (Q16)

To reconstruct: the residual block is8kappa x q0q0^T; the mixed block contracted with n0=(-q02,q01) is (J,0); its determinant is -2J; the yy base entry is h_yy. Apply the exact 2+2 formula.
