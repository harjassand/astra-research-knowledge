# Global singleton-zero plane gate and tractable missing hypotheses

Worker hessian_check12, 2026-10-07. Question: does a polynomial plane
Keller map G with ideal(G1,G2)=(x,y) have to be invertible? This is the
exact remaining interface of the affine-dual coefficient class. The
global plane source and constant Jacobian are essential. No solution of
this interface is proved here. The cycle12 origin-boundary criterion is
rechecked against primary sources and sharpened into practical filters.

## The missing boundary statement is exact

For a Keller map, every finite fibre is reduced and finite. Hence the
ideal identity says G^-1(0) is exactly one smooth point with local degree1.
It does not fix the generic field degree d=[C(x,y):C(G1,G2)]. If d>1,
choose a generic target line through0 and a punctured disc avoiding the
nonproper set. There are d distinct inverse points over each nonzero
disc point. The inverse-function neighbourhood at the unique zero
contains exactly one bounded sheet. Every other sheet escapes to infinity:
any bounded subsequence would limit to the unique zero and enter that
same one-to-one neighbourhood. Thus0 belongs to the nonproper locus.

This is consistent with Jelonek1993 Proposition6 (properness iff the
finite fibre's total local multiplicity equals d) and Proposition14
(nonproper locus is the projection of the boundary of the projective
graph closure). The proof is dimension-independent; it does not furnish
the missing plane-only contradiction.

For homogeneous coordinate X0 in the source compactification, first
form the graph closure by saturating the homogenized graph ideal by
X0. Then set target0 and X0=0. An empty projective fibre is equivalent
to properness at0; for a singleton Keller fibre it forces d=1 and the
established birational Keller theorem gives an automorphism. Saturation
must precede specialization. The affine ideal(G1,G2)=(x,y) alone does
not certify that this projective fibre is empty.

An alternative exact computational certificate is Jelonek's
Proposition7. For each source coordinate take its primitive irreducible
minimal equation over C[G1,G2], cleared to polynomial coefficients.
The nonproper set is the union of the zero sets of the leading
coefficients. If all these leading coefficients are nonzero at target0,
G is proper there and hence invertible. This is a discriminating test
on a specific candidate, not a universal proof or a cheap complexity claim.

## A concrete extra hypothesis: an A1 fibre component

Suppose an irreducible component C of some coordinate fibre G1=c has
smooth projective normalization of genus0 and exactly one omitted point
at infinity. The Keller condition makes every affine coordinate fibre
smooth, so C equals its affine normalization. Thus C is isomorphic to
A1. On C, the restriction G2 has derivative nowhere zero: its
differential is the nonzero companion to dG1 in the invertible Jacobian.
In a polynomial parameter of C, G2 is a univariate polynomial with no
critical point, hence has degree1. The whole G is injective on C.

By the established Abhyankar--Moh embedding theorem, a polynomial source
automorphism straightens C to an affine line. Gwoździewicz's
alg-geom/9305008v1 Theorem1.1 then makes G a polynomial automorphism.
This is a tractable missing hypothesis: genus and the number of points
removed from the normalization are exact algebraic-curve invariants.
It is stronger than mere rationality and is not implied by the
singleton-zero component ideal.

The primary two-page Gwoździewicz proof was fully read and rebuilt with
its two named prerequisites: Abhyankar--Moh and the Newton-polygon
homothety theorem for a Jacobian pair. Straighten the image of an
injective source line so that H(x,0)=(x,0). If both components have
degree>1, the homothety sends the point(1,0) of the first Newton polygon
to a positive point on the x-axis of the second. That requires a pure
x monomial in the second component, contradicting H2(x,0)=0. If one
component has degree<=1, the constant Jacobian gives a triangular
polynomial inverse. The primary TeX has a harmless final-proof
composition typo: use H1^-1∘H, as its Jacobian and recovery lines
already indicate, rather than the printed H1∘H. The older named
embedding and polygon theorems are not reproved here.

A cheap special case is any affine-line component of a coordinate
fibre. In the programme row form `G=(y,2x)D`, D in SL2(C[x,y]),
this immediately excludes each of

`x|D11`, `y|D21`, `x|D12`, `y|D22`,

because respectively x|G1, y|G1, x|G2, y|G2. More generally, a
nontrivial constant target combination lambda G1+mu G2 with any affine
linear source factor supplies the same filter. These prior-based
criteria do not classify all SL2 coefficient matrices or establish
properness from a singleton fibre.

## Which nonproper geometry genuinely constrains the plane problem

The primary Jelonek1993 paper was read in full. Its Theorem15 says the
nonproper locus is a uniruled hypersurface with a degree bound; it does
not impose the Keller condition. Example23 constructs a polynomial
map with finite fibres realizing every polynomially parametrized
rational plane curve as its nonproper locus. Thus rationality and
uniruledness alone cannot close our gate.

Chau math/0305088v1, Theorem1 and Corollary2, impose the plane Keller
degree-ratio parametrizations and one projective point at infinity.
The displayed leading-binomial coefficients in Corollary1 appear
reversed: substituting u=A t^(md), v=B t^(me) gives the leading
relation B^d u^e-A^e v^d=0, not the printed A^e u^e-B^d v^d.
This normalization typo is not used in the current theorem. The full
older Newton--Puiseux proof is not independently reproved here.

Chau math/0408048v1 is a different paper. Theorem1 links exceptional
values of a component with target-line tangencies to the nonproper
curve. Theorem2 excludes a nonproper curve whose entire set of
components has parametrizations (t^k,q(t)) in a suitable target
coordinate system. Corollary3 excludes a simply connected nonproper
curve. Its proof uses Suzuki's Euler formula to produce an A1 fibre
component and then the line-injectivity result above. The singleton
fibre hypothesis does not imply its target-projection or Euler
hypothesis. Corollary3 concerns the whole nonproper curve, not an
arbitrarily selected smooth local branch.

Here is an exact curve model demonstrating the remaining geometric
freedom, not a Keller nonproper curve construction:

`p=t³-t`, `q=t⁴-t²`, `q³-p²q-p⁴=0`.

Its normalization is A1, because t=q/p generically. It has one
projective point at infinity [0:1:0], but the affine origin has three
distinct normalization preimages t=0,±1 and three tangent directions.
It is not simply connected: its normalization identifies those three
points and is otherwise injective. No nonzero affine linear projection
ap+bq is a shifted pure power. For b=0, the missing quadratic term
forces a putative cubic centre0, contradicting the linear term. For
b!=0, comparison with b(t-r)^4+delta forces r²=-1/6 from the quadratic
coefficient and r²=-1 from the linear coefficient. Nonlinear target
coordinate equivalence to a power-parametrized curve is UNKNOWN, and
no claim is made that this model survives every Keller restriction.
It shows precisely why A1 normalization/one-infinity does not itself
give the simple-connectivity or target-projection hypothesis.

## Recent finite cusp-germ theorem: useful, but not the global bridge

Orevkov's *On germs of mappings C²→C²* was first submitted as
arXiv2512.07986v1 on2025-12-08 19:16:41UTC. The primary MathNet body
records publication2026-06-19, Sb.Math217(4),572--582,
DOI10.4213/sm10389e. Thus it is a recent published prerequisite with
a2025 preprint, not an originally2026 theorem inferred from a snippet.
The full primary preprint source and author PDF are saved. Only §1,
Theorem2 and its part(I) formulas/proof were read; the full classification
proof is UNADOPTED in this cycle.

Its finite germ classification assumes a target cusp u^d1=v^d2 with
coprime d1,d2>1, a smooth source domain in C² (A1), and a bijective
restriction on every irreducible divisor over the cusp (A2). The
ramification divisor is a single irreducible curve. A singleton affine
Keller fibre does not establish these conditions at infinity. A finite
normalization of the graph closure can be singular; resolving it can
lose finiteness. The nonproper target can have several local branches
or several characteristic pairs, and A2 also requires proof.

Even if A1/A2 were established for one boundary germ, the classification
permits nontrivial covers. One member of part(I)(a), l1=1,k1=1,k2=2,l2=0,
is exactly

`F_local(x,t)=((t²+2x)/3,x t)`.

It is finite of degree3, since x=(3u-t²)/2 and
`t³-3ut+2v=0`. Its Jacobian is 2(x-t²)/3, and

`u³-v²=(t²-x)²(t²+8x)/27`.

The critical divisor x=t² maps as(t²,t³), and the other divisor
x=-t²/8 maps as(t²/4,-t³/8); both are bijective onto the cusp.
The zero fibre has a singleton support but scheme length3. This is
ramified and is not a Keller map or a plane counterexample. It is a
permitted exact local completion model: adding one disjoint bounded
identity sheet and deleting the cusp divisors from the other sheet
leaves one inverse point at0 and four generic inverse points nearby.
That local disconnected open-source model fails the global affine-plane
and constant-Jacobian conditions, so it cannot settle or refute our
global problem. It pinpoints the global gluing/source conditions still
required rather than treating all local cusp ramification as impossible.

Orevkov's earlier author snapshot, *On three-sheeted polynomial mappings
of C²*, Theorem1.1, rules out generic degree2 or3 for a polynomial plane
Keller map. The author homepage distinguishes the Russian1986 article
from its English1987 translation. I read the statement and introductory
scope, not the full older10-page proof; it is a named established
prerequisite. On the checked sources alone, a surviving singleton-zero
plane obstruction has generic degree at least4. No stronger degree
bound is adopted merely from secondary snippets.
