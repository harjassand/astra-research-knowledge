CYCLE 4 ROOT AUDIT — SPARSE SUPPORT-SWITCH GEOMETRIC DRIFT
==========================================================

Status
------
INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE after independent in-programme reconstruction
and adversarial review. The proof is for the paired sparse bipartite family
below, uniformly on a supplied compact positive rate box. It proves a common
physical-time geometric Foster inequality on the active-coordinate quotient.
It does not prove a theorem for arbitrary weakly reversible networks, and
novelty/priority remain UNKNOWN pending external review.

Network and class interface
---------------------------
Let J,I be finite and E subset J x I. For every j in J include

    0 <-> A_j

with rates alpha_j,delta_j. For every (j,i) in E include

    A_j <-> A_j+B_i,              rates p_ji,q_ji,
    A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i,  rates u_ji,v_ji,

where k_ji>=1 is a fixed integer. Propensities use stochastic mass action;
in particular the two autocatalysis channels have rates
u_ji a_j b_i and v_ji (a_j)_{underline{k_ji+1}} b_i. All six rate types
range independently in a fixed rational rectangular box with finite upper
endpoints and strictly positive lower endpoints.

Write N(i)={j:(j,i) in E}, I_+={i:N(i) nonempty}, and
eta_i(a)=1{sum_{j in N(i)}a_j=0}. Every B_i with i outside I_+ is frozen
under all reactions. Fixing these isolated-substrate counts gives the
communicating classes; after deleting frozen coordinates, each such class
has the same active-coordinate generator. Isolated catalysts are not labels:
their immigration/death channels remain active.

The quotient is irreducible. To clear a state, for each i in I_+ choose a
neighbor catalyst; immigrate it if absent, remove each B_i using
A_j+B_i->A_j, then drain all catalysts. To build a target, immigrate needed
catalysts, create B_i using A_j->A_j+B_i, and use a temporary neighbor
catalyst if a target has no active neighbor. Every prescribed finite path
has positive probability. The graph is weakly reversible because every
reaction is paired with its reverse. No ODE permanence premise is used.

Exact generator
---------------
Use

    V(a,b)=1 + A + sum_(i in I_+) (1+eta_i(a)) R_i^(b_i),
    A=sum_j a_j,

with R_i>1 rational. Put c_i=R_i-1, d_i=1-R_i^(-1),

    g_ji(m)=p_ji c_i R_i^m - q_ji d_i m R_i^m + k_ji u_ji m,

and

    D_ji(a)=1{a_j=1 and a_l=0 for all l in N(i)\{j}}.

Let H=sum_j alpha_j and alpha_i=sum_(j in N(i))alpha_j. Directly summing
the reaction increments gives

    LV = H - sum_j delta_j a_j
       + sum_(j,i in E) [a_j g_ji(b_i)
                          - k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i]
       - sum_(i in I_+) alpha_i eta_i(a) R_i^(b_i)
       + sum_(i in I_+) sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j D_ji(a) R_i^(b_i).

The only changes to eta_i are catalyst immigration and catalyst death.
Immigration of j decreases the i-weight by R_i^(b_i) exactly when eta_i=1.
Death of j increases it only when j is the sole active neighbor with a_j=1,
which is D_ji. Both autocatalysis channels leave a_j positive and do not
toggle eta_i. The reverse factorial term and its multiplicity k_ji are as
displayed.

Common compact-box drift proof
-----------------------------
For each j choose R_i sufficiently close to one that

    chi_j=delta_j^- - sum_(i:(j,i) in E) p_ji^+(R_i-1) > 0.

There are finitely many j and all delta_j^->0, so common rational R_i>1
exist. For each edge, uniformly in the rate box,

    g_ji(m)/R_i^m
       <= p_ji^+(R_i-1)-q_ji^- d_i m+k_ji u_ji^+ m/R_i^m
       -> -infinity.

Also G=max(0,sup_(j,i),m,theta g_ji(m)) is finite: g_ji(m) itself tends
to minus infinity because the negative m R_i^m term dominates the linear
term. Choose L>2 D G+2, where D=max_j |N(j)|, and choose one finite M so
that for all edges and m>=M,

    g_ji(m)+delta_j^+ R_i^m <= -L R_i^m.                 (1)

Existence is uniform over the compact box and finite edge set. Let
H_b={i:b_i>=M}. For i in H_b:

* If eta_i=1, all its edge terms and last-neighbor death terms vanish;
  the immigration switch is at most -alpha_i^- R_i^(b_i), where
  alpha_i^-=sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j^->0.
* If eta_i=0, set A_i=sum_(j in N(i))a_j>=1. Since D_ji<=a_j,
  inequality (1) gives the sum of incident edge terms and all positive
  death switches at most -L A_i R_i^(b_i). The elementary inequality
  A_i R_i^(b_i)>=(A_i+R_i^(b_i))/2 allocates a negative half to this
  substrate exponential and a half to every active neighboring catalyst.

Thus high inactive substrates have direct negative immigration drift; high
active substrates control both their own exponential weight and their
active neighboring catalyst counts. Each catalyst with a high neighbor is
active at that neighbor (otherwise that substrate would be inactive), so
the catalyst half absorbs all positive low-edge terms: these are at most
D G a_j, and L/2>D G+1.

It remains to control a catalyst j with no high adjacent substrate. Its
neighboring b_i all lie in the finite set {0,...,M-1}. If all are zero,
its coefficient of a_j is at most -chi_j. If any neighbor i has
1<=b_i<M, the reverse term on that edge is at most

    -v_ji^- (a_j)_(underline{k_ji+1}),

because k_ji b_i>=1. For a_j>=k_ji+1 and k_ji>=1,
(a_j)_(k_ji+1)>=(a_j)_2=a_j(a_j-1). The finite low-neighbor patterns
and compact rate bounds make all positive edge terms at most a fixed
constant times a_j. The reverse quadratic tail therefore gives constants
C_j<infinity and c_j>0 with this catalyst's terms <=C_j-c_j a_j.
The last-neighbor death switches on low substrates are bounded constants:
their indicators force a_j=1 and their exponentials have b_i<M.

There are finitely many catalysts and substrates. Combining the cases gives
uniform constants C0<infinity and c0>0 such that

    LV <= C0 - c0 [A + sum_(i in H_b) R_i^(b_i)].          (2)

Every low substrate weight is at most 2 R_i^(M-1), a fixed constant;
every high substrate multiplier is at most 2. Therefore V is bounded by
A+2 sum_(i in H_b)R_i^(b_i)+C_low, for a common finite C_low. Decreasing
c0 and increasing C0 in (2) yields the common inequality

    L_theta V <= C-c V,   V>=1, all states, all theta in the box. (3)

If E is empty, the active substrates are absent and the result reduces to
the immigration-death network with V=1+A. If an isolated substrate exists,
V omits that constant class label; (3) is a quotient/classwise statement,
not a global norm-like drift on the full lattice.

Nonexplosion, recurrence, and effective certificate status
----------------------------------------------------------
On every fixed label class, V is norm-like and finite sublevel sets have
bounded total exit rates. From (3), LV<=C. Stopped Dynkin at the first hit
of level n gives P_x(tau_n<=t)<=(V(x)+Ct)/n. Explosion while remaining in
a finite sublevel set is impossible; taking n to infinity proves
nonexplosion in physical time. Irreducibility plus (3) gives positive
recurrence and a unique invariant law on each class.

An explicit rational construction for the box is independently written in
`../sparse_certificate_effectivity/REPORT.txt`. It supplies thresholds and
rational V,c,C directly from E,k and the positive rational box, rather
than searching states. Its claimed polynomial bit complexity uses binary
encoding and exact integer/rational arithmetic; thresholds may be
numerically large and the certificate is sufficient, not necessary. The
fixture script checks the exact formula/threshold arithmetic on 625 states;
that finite test is not the all-state proof.

Uniform mixing consequence on the quotient
-------------------------------------------
Fix any T>0. Localized Dynkin and Gronwall applied to (3) give the common
skeleton drift

    P_T^theta V <= e^(-cT)V +(C/c)(1-e^(-cT)).

It defines one common finite V-sublevel set F on the active quotient. For
each x in F choose a path to the dynamic origin: if a neighbor catalyst is
absent, immigrate it; clear each active substrate count with
A_j+B_i->A_j; then drain all catalysts. The finite union of paths has a
common finite upper bound on total exit rates over the rate box and a
common strictly positive lower bound on every prescribed channel rate.
Placing the jumps in ordered subintervals of (0,T) and requiring no other
jumps gives

    P_T^theta(x,.) >= eta delta_0(.), x in F, theta in Theta,

for one eta>0. The finite-step skeleton is irreducible; its no-jump
self-loop makes it aperiodic. The discrete drift/minorization theorem
therefore gives common M<infinity and rho<1 such that

    ||P_(nT)^theta(x,.)-pi_theta||_V <= M V(x) rho^n.

The drift bound on 0<=s<=T controls the remainder in t=nT+s, giving
common M',gamma>0 with

    ||P_t^theta(x,.)-pi_theta||_V <= M' V(x)e^(-gamma t).

This is V-weighted convergence, not total-variation convergence uniformly
over all initial states. For isolated-substrate labels, interpret P and pi
on the common active quotient and lift the result separately to each label;
the full-state target atom depends on that label.

Primary theorem gate: Meyn and Tweedie, “Stability of Markovian Processes
III: Foster-Lyapunov Criteria for Continuous-Time Processes,” Adv. Appl.
Prob. 25(3), 518-548 (1993), Theorem 6.1 and CD3, pp. 535-537,
https://doi.org/10.2307/1427522. It is a fixed-process theorem. Parameter
uniformity here comes from the common drift, explicit common finite-set
minorization, and common bounds, not from Theorem 6.1 alone. The detailed
primary-source reconstruction is in
`../geometric_ergodicity_prior/REPORT.txt`.

Uniformity and falsifier boundaries
-----------------------------------
* If a rate lower endpoint approaches zero, the common path-rate lower
  bound and/or inactive-face immigration coefficient can vanish; this
  proof gives no uniform mixing/moment constants across such a family.
* If any isolated substrate label ranges without bound, full-state
  stationary laws over those classes are not tight: each class law puts
  mass one on its own frozen label. Active-coordinate marginals remain
  identical across labels at fixed rates.
* The chosen R must stay fixed above one. An exact one-edge sequence with
  R_n=1+n^-4 has bounded V_n at (a,b)=(1,n^2) but LV_n->+infinity, even
  with a positive catalyst margin. Thus the proof is not uniform as
  R downarrow1; see `../sparse_switch_adversarial/AUDIT.txt`.
* Removing a reverse channel, allowing zero rate lower endpoints, or
  changing stochastic mass-action propensities invalidates the proof
  interface. Failure of this certificate outside the interface says
  nothing about recurrence.

Prior-art boundary
------------------
The product-form complex-balanced stationary theorem covers only its
complex-balanced rate constraints; its sparse/complete support coverage
was checked in the Cycle 4 primary audit. Published tier criteria leave a
reachable catalyst-free high-substrate ray outside their hypotheses for
this family. That failure does not imply nonrecurrence. A two-species
all-rate announcement remains source-reported in checked materials. The
all-rate sparse paired-edge result here and its compact-box strengthening
are internal proofs; no external or novelty validation is claimed.
