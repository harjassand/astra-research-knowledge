1. Exact V306 statistical interface

The V306 model (Definition 2.2, printed pp.4–5) has X~N(0,I_d), known twice-differentiable link f, unit-norm beta*, and Y=f(X^T beta*)+zeta. It assumes E[zeta|X]=0, E[zeta^2|X]=sigma^2 exactly (homoscedastic), and E[zeta^4|X]=K_4^4 exactly. Theorem 4.1 also takes a bound K_4≤K. Its contamination definition (Definition 2.1, p.4) is strong: the adversary sees the whole clean sample and changes at most epsilon n entire (x,y) pairs arbitrarily. This matches the global/noncausal replacement interface, but the noise and unit-norm assumptions are stronger than Sol V1/V2's E[zeta^2|a]≤sigma^2 and unknown amplitude q=||x*||.

The link assumptions are Assumptions 2.1–2.2 (printed pp.5–6): f is twice continuously differentiable; the population squared-loss Hessian at beta* has positive minimum eigenvalue mu; on a fixed-radius ball the fourth-moment envelopes of f' and f'' are finite and satisfy 6 sqrt(15) C_lip(R) R≤mu, with C_lip=M_1 M_2; and the Expected Squared Convexity

  c=ESC(beta*,f)=E[(f'(X^T beta*))^2+f(X^T beta*)f''(X^T beta*)]

is positive. For f(z)=z^2 and ||beta*||=1, these conditions do include phase retrieval: c=6; the envelopes are finite on every bounded ball, and the curvature condition holds for a sufficiently small positive constant radius. At beta*, the population squared-loss Hessian is 4I+8 beta* beta*^T, with eigenvalues 4 off-signal and 12 on-signal. Thus one may take mu=4 and mu_1=12. This is a genuine, direct quadratic-link inclusion, not an analogy.

2. Exact quadratic moment and representation comparison

Lemma 4.1 and Appendix D (printed pp.7, 18–19) use second-order Gaussian Stein to show

  E[Y^2 XX^T] = (sigma^2+E[f(Z)^2]) I + 2 ESC(beta*,f) beta* beta*^T,
  Z~N(0,1).

For f(z)=z^2 this is

  E[Y^2 XX^T]=(sigma^2+3)I+12 beta* beta*^T,

so the eigengap is 12 and the top eigenvalue is sigma^2+15. V306 uses the squared-response matrix E[Y^2XX^T], whereas Sol V1's un-clipped scalar-response identity is E[YXX^T]=uI+2x*x^T and its shifted/clipped target has gap 2u. These are different PSD representations: they share the Gaussian Stein mechanism and signal direction, but not the coefficient or isotropic term. V306's quadratic matrix is the exact second-order feature matrix in the older phase-retrieval line; Sol V1 changes the representation to make only a conditional second moment sufficient.

V306's Algorithm 2 forms vectors YX, and robust 1-ePCA sees PSD outer products Y^2 XX^T. It does not truncate Y or randomize its sign. Lemma 4.2 (p.7) asserts (4,C_4)-hypercontractivity with

  C_4 = 3( (E[f(Z)^8])^(1/8) + K_4 )/sigma.

For the quadratic link, E[Z^16]=15!!=2,027,025 and (E[f(Z)^8])^(1/8)=(15!!)^(1/8); this is finite. The displayed C_4 nevertheless degenerates as sigma→0 (and is undefined at sigma=0), so the stated robust initializer is not a noiseless-endpoint theorem. V306's robust initializer uses the higher-moment hypercontractive robust-PCA condition; it has no claim of stability for the AISTATS paper's specific norm-truncated feature.

Sol V1 instead clips only the scalar response through g_L(y)=L+clip_L(y) in [0,2L], forms a learner-signed vector X_L=xi sqrt(g_L(y))a, and targets S_L=(L+u)I+2x*x*^T. It establishes uniform empirical fractional-deletion stability with respect to S_L from only the conditional second-moment upper bound, acquiring h≈q² from the same globally corrupted sample and choosing L=A h. V2 reuses that initializer and adds a uniform finite-query gradient-refinement argument. Thus V306 has the same broad positive-PSD spectral theme, but not the same feature vector, clipping, stability claim, noise interface, or unknown-scale support.

3. V306 sample and runtime statements as printed

Theorem 4.2 (p.7) sets initializer sample count

  m = Theta(C_4^2 (d log d + log(1/delta))/epsilon^(3/2)),

and gives phase-invariant initializer error

  dist(beta_0,beta*) = O(C_4 epsilon^(1/4) sqrt((sigma^2+E[f(Z)^2]+c)/c)).

Its stated sufficient initializer contamination is O(min{C_4^-4, c^2/[C_4^4(sigma^2+E f^2+c)^2]}). The full Theorem 4.1 (p.7) further restricts epsilon by the convex-basin and gradient terms, takes tilde-m=tilde-O(d/epsilon), and states total sample count tilde-O(m+P tilde-m), P=O(1), with error O(sigma sqrt(epsilon)). For fixed link/noise constants and constant epsilon this is linear in d up to logarithms. The exact epsilon and link/noise constants are not negligible; in particular C_4 depends on sigma and K_4 and becomes singular in the low-noise limit.

There is a printed runtime mismatch that should be preserved as unresolved rather than silently reconciled. Lemma 2.3 (printed p.5) specifies robust 1-ePCA runtime O(n d/rho^2 polylog(...)) and rho=Theta(C_4^2 sqrt(epsilon)). Substituting rho^2=Theta(C_4^4 epsilon) and n=m yields O(m d/(C_4^4 epsilon) polylog). Theorem 4.2 (p.7) instead prints O(m d/C_4^4 polylog), omitting a factor 1/epsilon; Theorem 4.1 inherits the latter initializer term. The source provides no explanation for that algebraic change. This is a parameterized-runtime inconsistency, though both expressions are near-linear in the number of input entries when C_4, epsilon are held fixed.

There is also an optimization-precision mismatch. Detailed Theorem 4.3 (printed p.8; proof Eq.22, p.25) gives

  ||beta_P-beta*|| ≤ R exp(-P gamma/(alpha+gamma)) + O(sigma sqrt(phi_2 epsilon)/gamma)

before normalization. By contrast, main Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 (p.9) state O(sigma sqrt(epsilon)) while saying P=O(1). For fixed positive link parameters, suppressing the optimization term below the noise/corruption floor requires P at least on the order of (alpha+gamma)/gamma times log(R/(sigma sqrt(epsilon))) when the ratio is large. In particular, sigma=0 with finite P leaves a nonzero displayed optimization term, so the exact zero-error conclusion does not follow. P can plausibly be increased logarithmically and absorbed into tilde notation, but the theorem must then state that dependence; P=O(1) and the displayed exact error do not jointly establish it. V306's appendix proves only the detailed bound with the decaying term.

4. Two correctness/interface defects in the phase-retrieval claim

First, V306 explicitly lists Phase Retrieval f(z)=z² among the links in Corollary 4.1 (printed p.9), but Definitions 2.3 and Corollary 4.1 state a signed Euclidean error ||beta_hat-beta*||. Under f(z)=z², the clean-data law under beta* equals the law under -beta*. For any estimator, the output distribution is therefore the same in these two parameter worlds. If its signed error is <1 for one unit vector, it cannot also have error <1 for the opposite vector; for epsilon=sigma=0 the asserted signed error is exactly zero. This is an information-theoretic contradiction unless a sign convention/orientation is supplied as part of the model. The initializer theorem (Theorem 4.2) correctly uses dist(beta_0,beta*)=min(||beta_0-beta*||,||beta_0+beta*||), and Appendix D's induction handles both signs; those facts support a repair to phase distance, not the signed corollary as printed. This flaw is specific to even links; it does not refute the asymmetric-link applications.

Second, although the input adversary is global, Algorithm 1 randomly partitions the already corrupted full sample into buckets and then claims fresh-sample robust-gradient guarantees at beta_t. Appendix D's Lemma D.2 bounds gradient covariance for each fixed beta; the proof around Eqs. (18)–(20), printed pp.24–25, conditions on beta_t and says a fresh sample is used. Under a global adversary who saw every clean pair, corrupted values in an earlier bucket can encode the clean observations assigned to a later bucket. Randomly balancing the number of corruptions in buckets (Remark C.1, p.18) does not restore conditional independence of later clean data from beta_t. A pointwise fixed-beta robust-mean guarantee therefore cannot be conditioned on the adaptive iterate as done there without a uniform-in-beta event or a causal adversary assumption. The covariance upper-bound formula itself is uniform over beta in the ball, but the mean-estimation concentration event is only asserted for fixed beta. This is the same gap Sol V1 explicitly left open; V2's finite-grid event is designed to repair it.

These findings make the source a meaningful, close comparator, but do not validate its phase-retrieval signed guarantee, its fixed-P endpoint, or the fresh-batch composition under the stated global adversary. They are internal proof-interface audits, not an external erratum or novelty claim.

5. Independent blind review of Sol V2: model and source interfaces

The V2 theorem's stated model is coherent: d≥1, x*≠0, q=||x*||, clean iid Gaussian pairs a~N(0,I_d), y=(a^Tx*)²+zeta, E[zeta|a]=0, E[zeta²|a]≤sigma²; one adversary sees the entire clean m-sample and replaces up to epsilon m pairs before any learner randomness. Inputs include epsilon, R≥sigma/q², confidence delta, and target p>0; neither q nor sigma nor a fourth-moment bound is supplied. Output is phase distance. This is strictly broader in conditional noise and unknown amplitude than V306's stated phase-retrieval specialization.

The principal imported interface is Depersin–Lecue (Annals of Statistics 50(1), 2022), Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 1, local primary PDF/text work/sol/robust_recovery/research/dl22.{pdf,txt}; author-hosted primary copy https://lecueguillaume.github.io/assets/AOS2118.pdf. Assumption 1 explicitly permits the adversary to inspect all N iid clean vectors and replace arbitrary values; it also notes surviving inliers may be dependent after adaptive selection. Proposition 1 supplies the stronger event needed for composition: simultaneously for every PSD M with tr(M)=1, at least 9K/10 block means obey ||M^(1/2)(barX_k-mu)||≤8r, with failure exp(-K/180000), provided K≥300|O|. On this event the remaining solver proof is deterministic except for its covering-SDP random seeds. V2 uses K≥600 epsilon m, so for every fixed score-query and every corruption of at most epsilon m original rows, K≥600|O|. The transformed outliers at a fixed query are arbitrary, and the clean block event can be made uniform across queries by a union bound. This source supports the strong-adversary interface; the candidate does not infer it from a theorem's unconditional success probability alone.

The finite grid is a legitimate proof device if its stated definitions are implemented precisely. For fixed true q, h in [26q²/27,28q²/27] implies the dyadic s=2^floor(log2 sqrt(h)) can take at most two values. Each scale has a finite lattice of size at most (3+6sqrt(d)/rho)^d. The learner selects one scale from h and rounds every iterate to its grid; the proof unions clean events over both deterministic-at-fixed-q grids and every grid point in the q/9 phase basin. No grid enumeration is performed. Since each fixed clean score sample is iid and the DL22 event is uniform over all replacement sets, the union event covers an iterate selected using all clean observations and the global corruption. This is the central valid repair of the fresh-batch dependence issue, assuming the DL22 deterministic-event extraction and the event union are retained exactly.

6. Independent algebra audit of Sol V2

The V2 initializer proof reuses V1's bounded positive response shift. Its scalar MOM scale estimate has Var(y)≤(2+R²)q⁴; Chebyshev at threshold sqrt(8v/b) gives a bad-block probability at most 1/8, Hoeffding gives exp(-K/32) for at least K/4 bad blocks, and at most epsilon m≤K/32 blocks are corrupted under K=ceil(32epsilon m+32log(2/delta_s)). The resulting h relative error and sample condition in V1 Sections 3 and 6 check algebraically. The response clipping bias bound, Gaussian fractional-deletion bound, uniform L-mesh concentration, and normalization of the weighted second moment in V1 Sections 2–6 also check: for a retained weight mass 1-alpha, Q_w-S_L=[(Qhat_L-S_L)-D+alpha S_L]/(1-alpha). With the displayed sufficient inequality this yields operator error at most gamma lambda_min(S_L), hence the Loewner stability relation. Removing the original epsilon fraction and a further 20epsilon fraction of survivors costs at most epsilon+(1-epsilon)20epsilon≤21epsilon of full clean mass.

The low-dimensional and zero-contamination repairs in V2 are sound under the phase metric. For d=1, h alone gives |sqrt(h)-q|≤q/27 when nu=1/27; the sign is not identifiable and is appropriately quotiented out. For d=2, adding an independent learner-generated Gaussian sensing coordinate after corruption embeds x* as (x*,0); conditional mean/variance assumptions persist because the coordinate is independent, the d=3 source theorem applies, and projection cannot increase distance to either embedded sign. Do not renormalize the projected initializer. For epsilon=0, epsilon_alg=max(epsilon,1/m)=1/m lets the strict-positive-epsilon stable-PCA theorem be used by mathematically padding one point's mixture mass; the full-clean deletion budget beta=21/m remains sufficient. The exact initializer sample inequality must use epsilon_alg in its logarithm. Any simplified formula with log(1/epsilon) is undefined at epsilon=0 and should be avoided or explicitly replaced by epsilon_alg / an implicit m bound.

The direct gradient derivation checks. For z*=±x*, e=x-z*, U=a^Tz*, V=a^Te, W=U+V, the score is P_x=[2U²V+3UV²+V³]a-zeta W a and

  g(x)=E P_x=(3||x||²-q²)x-2<x,x*>x*,
  H(x)=(3||x||²-q²)I+6xx^T-2x*x*^T.

For ||e||≤q/9, completing the square 6(t+z)^2-2t²=4(t+3z/2)^2-3z² yields (4/3)q²I≤H≤11q²I. With eta=1/(12h) and h∈[26q²/27,28q²/27], eta H has spectrum in [3/28,99/104], so the population update contracts by at most 25/28. Holder/Gaussian eighth moments give the signal covariance bound below 1300q^4e²; the conditional-variance noise term is at most sigma²(||x||²I+2xx^T)≤4sigma²q²I. Cross covariance vanishes from E[zeta|a]=0. No fourth noise moment is used.

The robust-mean substitution then gives an error at a grid point at most B_m sqrt(1300q^4e²+4sigma²q²), where

  B_m=808[1200 sqrt(d/n)+sqrt(1200K/n)].

Combining eta≤9/(104q²), sqrt(1300), and the noise coefficient yields the recurrence

  e_{t+1}≤(53/56)e_t+(9/52)B_m sigma/q+rho q,

under B_m≤1/[100(1+R)]. At e=q/9 the additive term is at most (9/5200+1/1000)q<q/168, exactly below the contraction slack. Summing gives noise coefficient (56/3)(9/52)=42/13 and rounding coefficient (56/3)rho=(14/75)p for rho=p/100. The chosen T makes the initial term at most pq/5. Thus the stated looser finite-precision accuracy target dist(x_T,x*)≤pq+(42/13)B_m sigma/q follows from the recurrence. The sample relation K=O(epsilon m+D_p), n≥m/2 implies B_m≤C[sqrt(epsilon)+sqrt(D_p/m)], with D_p=d log(Csqrt(d)/p)+log(C/delta); the displayed sufficient conditions make the basin invariant.

Degenerate regimes: (i) sigma=0: the scale argument remains valid; the refinement bound becomes pq, not exact finite-iteration recovery. This is the right endpoint statement for finite p, though a bit-complexity result remains absent. (ii) epsilon=0: use epsilon_alg=1/m in stable PCA; the robust mean block count remains logarithmic in the finite query-grid cardinality and is compatible with K≤m/2 when the explicit m conditions hold. (iii) q→0: q=0 is excluded; for fixed sigma>0 the supplied bound R≥sigma/q² diverges and so does the stated sample requirement. If sigma=O(q²) and R remains bounded, the dimensionless formulas remain applicable for each q>0 in exact real arithmetic. No uniform q=0 guarantee is claimed. (iv) p→0: the theorem gives arbitrary positive optimization tolerance with logarithmic p costs, never p=0 exactness.

No fatal V2 algebra or imported-interface defect was found in this blind pass. Exact theorem review still requires preserving several boundaries: allocate a total δ/4 across the initializer's scalar, covariance, deletion, norm and PCA events (not δ/4 separately to each); use epsilon_alg in every stable-PCA log/rate when padding; keep the source's real-arithmetic solver and input-encoding caveat; and state the sample/runtime conditions in exact implicit form when epsilon=0. These are presentation/control requirements, not a counterexample to the finite-grid composition. The V2 candidate has not been implemented, finite-tested, externally reviewed, or novelty-validated.

7. Scoped comparison and status

V306 demonstrates that a robust PSD spectral initializer plus robust-gradient refinement is a real and close prior-art architecture for SIMs, and quadratic f is explicitly within its link class. It neither proves the exact AISTATS truncated stability proposition nor, as printed, a valid signed phase-retrieval rate or fixed-iteration zero-noise endpoint. Its fixed-query fresh-batch composition also needs a uniformity/causality repair under its own global adversary. Sol V2's claimed differentiator is a same-sample, finite-grid uniform robust-mean event under only conditional second moments, with unknown q acquired and an explicit pq approximation term; if the imported source conditioning and all exact inequalities survive independent review, this is a materially stronger interface for quadratic recovery. It is not thereby a validated breakthrough or proven novel result. The original AISTATS Question 4.8 remains unanswered as a statement about its particular truncated X stability. No lower bound or maximal epsilon/moment theorem is established in this record.
