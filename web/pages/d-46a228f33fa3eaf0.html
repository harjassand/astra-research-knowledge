# Workstream 8 — stochastic hybrid safety across guards and resets

## Finding

The concrete failure mode is omission of the reset transition from a safety argument. A barrier that decreases along every continuous flow segment does not, by itself, bound safety risk for a hybrid process: repeated guard-triggered resets can increase its value and make failure probability approach one. A two-reset example gives an exact contradiction to the flow-only (1/2) claim. The correct safety interface must include the reset kernel on each guard and the guard-event count (or a compensator for it).

This is a scoped counterexample and a useful quantitative repair, not a novelty claim. Guard-crossing/reset conditions already appear in deterministic hybrid barrier work, and stochastic barrier papers treat Markov mode switches in the generator. The audit therefore rejects the route “reuse a flow certificate and infer whole-hybrid safety” while leaving open a stronger, novel result for controlled continuous-time state-triggered stochastic resets.

## Exact model and decision interface

Let (Q) be a finite set of modes and (D_q\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_q}) the continuous state domain in mode (q). In mode (q), between discrete events, the state follows

\[
dX_t=b_q(X_t,u_t)\,dt+\sigma_q(X_t,u_t)\,dW_t+
\int_Z \xi_q(X_{t-},u_t,z)\,N(dt,dz),
\]

where (W) is Brownian motion and (N) is a marked Poisson random measure. A directed edge (e=(q,q')) has a supplied Borel guard (G_e\subseteq D_q), an invariant/flow convention specifying when the flow stops, and a reset kernel

\[
K_e( dq',dx'\mid q,x^-,u,a),
\]

where (x^-) is the pre-reset state, (u) is the flow input, and (a\in A_e) is the reset action. If guards overlap, a priority rule or an adversarial edge choice is part of the model. The first guard hit is a stopping time; the post-reset state and mode are right-continuous. This specification must also state whether a Poisson jump can itself cross a guard and which transition takes priority.

For the benchmark below, the controller observes the exact pair ((q_t,X_t)) continuously and observes the active edge and (X_{\tau-}) at a guard. The flow action (u_t\) is predictable, bounded, and held between controller sample times; the reset action (a_k) is measurable with respect to the observed pre-reset history. Partial observation would require replacing this with an observation kernel and restricting policies to the observation filtration; no such claim is made here.

For target sets (S_q\subseteq D_q) and horizon (T), safety means

\[
\mathbb P^\pi\{(q_t,X_t)\in\cup_q\{q\}\times S_q\text{ for every }t\in[0,T]\}\ge 1-\delta,
\]

including both sides of each jump and every point along each stochastic flow excursion. Well-posedness and a no-Zeno condition are required. The quantitative lemma below assumes a pathwise event-count cap, which is stronger and easier to audit than merely saying “non-Zeno.”

Costs must be split into supplied and acquired resources: guard/invariant geometry; flow drift, diffusion, and Poisson mark law; each guard reset kernel and its action dependence; state/edge sensing rate and precision; controller sample count and computation; reset/actuation energy, latency, and wear; and physical calibration data. Certification from a supplied exact kernel is distinct from learning that kernel from experiments. The analytical example is dimension one, fully observed, has one flow law and one reset coin, and makes no claim of physical validation.

## Strongest comparator reconstructed

The strongest mathematical comparator found is exact finite-horizon stochastic reachability dynamic programming, not a barrier relaxation. Abate, Prandini, Lygeros, and Sastry model a controlled discrete-time stochastic hybrid system by a Borel transition kernel over a hybrid state ((q,x)), with separate transition and reset controls. They allow forced mode changes through invariant exits encoded in the transition kernel. Their value recursion optimizes the probability of staying in the safe set at every step, with a Markov policy attaining the optimum when the stated measurable-selector conditions hold. In shorthand,

\[
V_N(s)=\mathbf1_S(s),\qquad
V_k(s)=\mathbf1_S(s)\sup_{a\in A(s)}\int V_{k+1}(s')\,T(ds'\mid s,a).
\]

This is exact for the supplied discrete-time kernel and is the equal-information benchmark. For a continuous-time guard model, the analogous exact kernel must include the whole flow excursion, first-hit time/edge, whether the excursion stayed safe, and the post-reset law. Its state is continuous, so the recursion is an ideal comparator rather than a generally cheap implementation.

Prajna, Jadbabaie, and Pappas (2007) already make the deterministic guard/reset obligation explicit: each location has its own barrier, its derivative is constrained during flow, and the post-reset barrier must remain on the safe side at every guard. Their stochastic theorem treats switching diffusions whose continuous state is held fixed at a Markov mode switch, charging switching in the infinitesimal generator. Thus the result here does not claim that guard conditions or mode-transition terms are new.

Lavaei, Soudjani, and Frazzoli (2022) give a closer stochastic barrier comparator for sampled-data systems with Brownian and Poisson flow noise and stochastic reset equations. They augment the state with an elapsed-time counter, impose an expected barrier inequality on each augmented transition, and derive a finite-horizon failure bound. Zaker et al. (2026, Automatica; arXiv:2409.10018) extend this style to interconnected stochastic hybrid subsystems and small-gain composition. Their jump times remain constrained to a window ε₁τ through ε₂τ and are represented by a counter. The full-text model therefore addresses scheduled resets and network coupling, while the state-triggered guard interface above remains distinct. No priority claim against other stochastic hybrid reset literature is made.

The pinned Astra snapshot (commit `8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83`) contains a relevant but separate result, N35. Its certificate concerns actual factorial mass-action jumps with bounded predictable rates, a supplied buffered initial set, and one strictly inward polytope shared by all allowed rate modes; finite-horizon exit probability is bounded even over an exponential horizon under the stated margins. Modes may switch off reactions only when each mode retains the collar. It does not model arbitrary state-triggered resets. ## Quantitative result and decisive counterexample

**Guard-budget bound (internal derivation).** Suppose every continuous excursion stays within the safe set almost surely. At each guard event, conditional on the complete pre-reset history and on the chosen reset action, the next hybrid state is safe with probability at least (1-p). If at most (M) guard events can occur by time (T), then

\[
\mathbb P(\text{unsafe by }T)\le 1-(1-p)^M.
\]

For edge-specific conditional reset risks (p_e), if edge (e) can fire at most (M_e) times, the corresponding bound is (1-\prod_e(1-p_e)^{M_e}). The proof is repeated conditioning on survival through guard (k); pad paths with fewer than (M) events by safe no-op events. The bound is sharp: the loop example below attains equality. For a requested total risk δ and a uniform cap (M), the exact per-guard requirement is

\[
p\le 1-(1-\delta)^{1/M}.
\]

This exposes a charged quantity a certificate must supply: either an event-count cap or a sharper model-specific compensator. A non-Zeno assumption alone does not supply a finite pathwise (M_T).

**Loop counterexample.** There is one mode and one scalar (x). From (x=1), the flow is (\dot x=-1) until it hits the guard (G=\{0\}), exactly one time unit later. The safe set is (S=[0,1]). At each guard hit, the reset is (x^+=1) with probability (1-p), and (x^+=2) with probability (p); (x=2) is unsafe and absorbing. Reset coins are conditionally independent. There are exactly (M) opportunities by horizon (T=M), with unit dwell time, so there is no Zeno behavior and

\[
\mathbb P(\text{unsafe by }M)=1-(1-p)^M.
\]

The scalar (B(x)=x\) is nonnegative, satisfies (B(1)=1) on the initial state, (B(2)=2) on the unsafe state, and has strictly negative flow derivative (\dot B=-1). A flow-only calculation using initial level 1 and unsafe level 2 would suggest the usual supermartingale exit bound (1/2). At the guard, however,

\[
\mathbb E[B(X^+)\mid X^-=0]=1+p>B(0)=0,
\]

so the hybrid process is not a supermartingale. At (p=1/2), (M=2), the actual failure probability is (3/4), contradicting that flow-only bound. As (M\to\infty), the failure probability tends to one for every fixed (p>0). This does not contradict reset-aware barrier theorems; it falsifies only the transfer that drops the guard/reset condition.

An implementable certificate route would first prove a uniform conditional reset-risk bound over every reachable guard state and admissible reset action, then prove a pathwise visit cap (for example from a uniform dwell time) or a valid event compensator, and finally combine them with a separately valid flow-excursion guarantee. Exact DP remains stronger when the full kernels are available; the product bound is useful when only per-guard tail certificates and visit counts can be acquired.

The residual research gate is to audit stronger stochastic hybrid models with genuinely state-triggered random resets and compare their guard measurability, event-count assumptions, and reset-kernel conditions against the interface above.
