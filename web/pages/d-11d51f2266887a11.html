# CTMC path discrimination with reset and leakage

## Model and claim

Let (X_t) be a non-explosive CTMC on a finite state space (S). Under hypothesis (H_j), (j\in\{0,1\}), a specified collection of transitions has rates

\[
q_j(x,y)=\theta_j a(x,y),\qquad 0<\theta_0<\theta_1,
\]

and every other transition (including any reset or leakage transitions) has the same rate under both hypotheses. The initial law is also the same. Assume common support for the two generators. Observe the complete labelled path on ([0,T]).

Write (K_T) for the number of signal-sensitive transitions and

\[
A_T=\int_0^T a(X_t)\,dt,\qquad a(x)=\sum_{y:(x,y)\text{ sensitive}}a(x,y),\qquad a_{\max}=\max_x a(x).
\]

Define the Poisson-rate divergence

\[
d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)=\theta_1\log(\theta_1/\theta_0)-\theta_1+\theta_0.
\]

**Theorem.** The full-path log-likelihood ratio and relative entropy are

\[
\log\frac{dP_1^T}{dP_0^T}=K_T\log(\theta_1/\theta_0)-(\theta_1-\theta_0)A_T,
\qquad
D(P_1^T\Vert P_0^T)=\mathbb E_1[A_T]d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)\le a_{\max}T d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0).
\]

Thus all full-path evidence about the scalar signal parameter is contained in the two statistics ((K_T,A_T)); reset/leakage transitions affect the law of (A_T) but add no direct likelihood term when their rates are signal-independent.

If a test has false-alarm probability at most \(\alpha\) under (H_0) and miss probability at most \(\beta\) under (H_1), then necessarily

\[
d_{\rm B}(1-\beta\Vert\alpha)\le a_{\max}T d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0),
\]

where (d_{\rm B}(p\Vert q)=p\log(p/q)+(1-p)\log((1-p)/(1-q))). In particular, the required duration is at least

\[
T\ge \frac{d_{\rm B}(1-\beta\Vert\alpha)}{a_{\max}d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)}.
\]

This lower bound does not improve with the number of internal states. It charges the complete observation duration and the maximum signal-sensitive hazard. For a bounded stopping time \(\tau\), the same argument gives (d_{\rm B}(1-\beta\Vert\alpha)\le d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)\mathbb E_1[A_\tau]\le a_{\max}d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)\mathbb E_1[\tau]).

## Proof

For a path with jumps (x_{t^-}\to x_t), its density is the initial probability times the product of the jump rates times the exponential of minus the integrated total exit rate. All signal-independent factors cancel between (P_1^T) and (P_0^T). Each sensitive jump contributes \(\log(\theta_1/\theta_0)\); the change in total exit hazard contributes \(-\int_0^T(\theta_1-\theta_0)a(X_t)dt\). This gives the displayed path likelihood ratio.

Under (H_1), the predictable compensator of (K_T) is (\theta_1 A_T), so (\mathbb E_1[K_T]=\theta_1\mathbb E_1[A_T]). Taking the (P_1)-expectation of the log likelihood gives

\[
\mathbb E_1[A_T]\{\theta_1\log(\theta_1/\theta_0)-\theta_1+\theta_0\}.
\]

Since (A_T\le a_{\max}T), the entropy bound follows. For any test, mapping the path to its binary decision cannot increase relative entropy. Applying this data-processing inequality yields

\[
D(P_1^T\Vert P_0^T)\ge d_{\rm B}(P_1(\text{alarm})\Vert P_0(\text{alarm}))\ge d_{\rm B}(1-\beta\Vert\alpha),
\]

where the last inequality uses monotonicity in the first argument and antitonicity in the second on the regime (1-\beta>\alpha). Combining inequalities proves the result. The stopping-time version follows from the same likelihood expression stopped at \(\tau\) and the counting-process compensator, under the stated boundedness/integrability condition.

## Exact reset/leakage example and memory tradeoff

Take (S=\{0,1,\ldots,N\}). For (i<N), let (i\to i+1) occur at rate \(\theta\); for (i>0), let (i\to0) occur at rate \(\rho>0\), independent of \(\theta\). Start from state 0. Here (a_{\max}=1), (K_T) counts upward jumps, and (A_T=\int_0^T\mathbf 1\{X_t<N\}dt).

At fixed \(\theta\), set (r=\theta/(\theta+\rho)). The stationary probabilities are \(\pi_i=(1-r)r^i\) for (0\le i<N) and \(\pi_N=r^N\). Therefore, for the binary hypotheses, the stationary full-path relative-entropy rate is

\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T D(P_1^T\Vert P_0^T)=(1-r_1^N)d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0),\qquad r_1=\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_1+\rho}.
\]

The factor (1-r_1^N) is the active exposure fraction under (H_1). Adding states increases this factor monotonically, but its (N\)-to-(N+1) increment is (r_1^N(1-r_1)), and the information rate is capped by (d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)), the always-active Poisson-counter benchmark with the same per-active-time hazard. For example, (\theta_0=1,\theta_1=2,\rho=1) gives (d=2\log 2-1\approx0.3863) nats/time; the rates are (d/3\approx0.1288) at (N=1), (19d/27\approx0.2718) at (N=3), and approach (d) as (N\to\infty). So the theorem does not claim that state count never helps: in this model it helps only by reducing time spent in the terminal inactive state, with geometric diminishing returns.

The sensor has (N+1) internal states, requiring \(\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil\) bits to encode its current state. A full-path assay over duration (T) records (J_T) transition labels and dwell times; here \(\mathbb E_1J_T\le(\theta_1+\rho)T\). With timestamp resolution \(\Delta\), a straightforward event log costs (O(\mathbb E J_T[\log_2(N+1)+\log_2(T/\Delta)])\) bits. The exact sufficient path summary is only (K_T\) plus (A_T), but storing (A_T) to resolution \(\varepsilon_A\) still costs \(O(\log(T/\varepsilon_A))\) bits. The theorem uses ideal continuous timestamps; it does not claim a free finite-precision implementation.

## Why a terminal threshold can fail

In the reset example, a detector that sees only (X_T) has, as (T\to\infty), one draw from the stationary categorical distribution \(\pi_\theta\). These distributions have full support for finite (N), so their total variation is strictly below 1; no decision based only on the terminal state can drive both error probabilities to zero by increasing (T\), and the equal-prior Bayes error stays positive. By contrast, the path likelihood test can: (K_T/A_T\to\theta\) almost surely as (T\to\infty), because (A_T/T\to1-r^N>0) and (K_T-\theta A_T) is a counting-process martingale with quadratic variation \(\theta A_T\). Thresholding (K_T/A_T) between (\theta_0\) and (\theta_1\) is therefore consistent. This is a counterexample specifically to terminal-state-only thresholds, not to temporal or downstream accumulators.

## Structural identifiability boundary

With known (a(x,y)) and fully observed time-stamped paths, the likelihood in \(\theta\) is proportional to \(\theta^{K_T}e^{-\theta A_T}\), so the path MLE is (\widehat\theta=K_T/A_T) when (A_T>0). If the rates instead depend on an unknown gain (g) and signal (s) only through the product (\theta=gs), then no full-path observation can identify (g) and (s) separately: any two pairs with equal product generate the same generator and exactly the same path law. Calibration or another signal-dependent transition channel is a necessary additional premise.

If only a stationary endpoint from the (N=1) reset chain is observed, its activation probability is (\theta/(\theta+\rho)), so multiplying both \(\theta\) and \(\rho\) by a common constant leaves the endpoint law unchanged. Time-resolved transition counts and state sojourns break this common-clock ambiguity. These are exact model-identifiability statements, not empirical claims.
