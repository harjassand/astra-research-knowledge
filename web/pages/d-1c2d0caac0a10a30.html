## Primary sources reconstructed

1. Mundhe et al., “Spatial patterning of contractility by a self-organized mechanogen activity gradient underlies Drosophila gastrulation,” *Nature Communications* 17, 1721 (published 23 Jan 2026), [DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z), full primary article [Nature Communications page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68418-z).
   - Introduction/Results, “GPCR signaling is required in the propagation zone”: primordium-only Gα rescue preserves initial invagination but gives 2.5±0.06 versus 7.1±0.3 rows of MyoII propagation (article Fig. 1; Results around lines 98–104 in web extraction). This isolates a propagation-zone requirement from initiation.
   - Results, Fog source/rescue and tethered-Fog test: source-confined diffusible Fog rescues a mean 5.3±0.2-cell propagation while membrane-tethered source Fog yields 1.7±0.1 cells despite similar primordium invagination (Fig. 2; Results around lines 108–129). Thus source Fog dispersal is required in this experimental setup; this is not proof that its extracellular concentration profile is spatially graded.
   - Results, “Fog production and uptake tune an exponential gradient of MyoII”: WT MyoII exponential length 3.18±0.29 μm; two extra Fog copies, 5.26±0.58 μm; gprk2 RNAi, 5.48±0.58 μm. WT source amplitude and range: 11.55±0.10 A.U. and 27.08±5.72 μm; two extra Fog copies: 32.59±0.32 A.U. and >38.01±1.92 μm; gprk2 RNAi amplitude/range: 14.25±0.14 A.U. and >33.59±2.5 μm (Results around lines 130–149, Fig. 3). Threshold ranges are censored where marked `>`; they are not treated as point estimates.
   - Results/Fig. 4: measured extracellular Fog::YFP is uniform across the propagation zone; interpolated FCS diffusion is 55 μm²/s for Fog::YFP and 87 μm²/s for secreted GFP (Results around lines 151–171). This is direct evidence against an extracellular Fog concentration gradient at the tested resolution/conditions.
   - Results/Fig. 5–6: apical FCS separates a fast Fog pool (84%, D=51±2 μm²/s) and minor slow pool (16%, D=1.6±0.2 μm²/s), with receptor Smog D=0.54±0.04 μm²/s. The slow component is interpreted as membrane-bound/interacting ligand, not chemically proven to be receptor-bound. Gprk2 RNAi raises slow-pool fraction from 16±1% to 30±15% and flattens the within-embryo slow-fraction slope (WT −1.9×10⁻³±1.7×10⁻³ μm⁻¹; RNAi 6.5×10⁻⁴±2.6×10⁻³ μm⁻¹; Results around lines 172–194).
   - Results/Fig. 7 and Discussion: integrin loss suppresses the expanded/high MyoII gradient under Gprk2 depletion; GPCR clustering can remain high without functional MyoII output, consistent with integrins as signaling cofactors rather than direct clustering agents. Increasing Fog activity range/amplitude slows the invagination wave through increased adhesion/reduced de-adhesion. Exact integrin–GPCR coupling remains unresolved and post-translational Fog regulation is explicitly retained as an alternative (Results around lines 217–239; Discussion around lines 241–267).
   - Limitation for our candidate: this study does not fit a transport model linking slow-pool D, material-frame speed, clearance, and MyoII length; it does not directly measure an effective first-order clearance constant; source figure summaries alone do not provide a matched same-embryo parameter set. The authors state the molecular mechanism is not fully elucidated.

2. Bailles et al., “Genetic induction and mechano-chemical propagation of a morphogenetic wave,” *Nature* 572, 467–473 (2019), [DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9), [primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1492-9).
   - Results/Fig. 1 report 2.2±0.2 μm/min constant MyoII activation-wave speed, approximately one cell per three minutes. The paper also provides an explicit 1D model where strong nonlinear local mechanochemical feedback plus finite spatial coupling suffices for a constant-speed wave, and experiments show Rok inhibition arrests Rho1/Myosin propagation. This is a serious mechanical-relay baseline; our transport candidate cannot replace it.
   - Results/Figs. 4–5 report a 3D contact/deformation cycle and integrin-mediated vitelline-membrane adhesion. Source interpretation: local invagination pushes neighboring cells, apical adhesion provides resistance/anchorage, contact activates MyoII, and detachment relays the cycle.

3. Collinet et al., “Mechanical regulation of substrate adhesion and de-adhesion drives a cell-contractile wave during Drosophila tissue morphogenesis,” *Developmental Cell* 59, 156–172.e7 (2024), [DOI 10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022), [primary article record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1534580723006202).
   - Abstract/figures report integrin clusters anchor cortex and promote MyoII, MyoII enhances adhesion, contraction after detachment amplifies MyoII, prolonged vitelline-membrane contact strengthens adhesion and slows detachment/wave propagation, and detachment angle changes tensile force. This supports a nonmonotone speed/reach engineering objective rather than maximizing signal amplitude. It predates the 2026 surface-Fog-gradient study.

4. “Mechanochemical patterning localizes the organizer of a luminal epithelium,” *Science Advances* (2025), [DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adu2286](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu2286), [primary open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12190006/).
   - Hydra regenerating spheroids couple Wnt-dependent softening to strain-dependent Wnt production under periodic osmotic inflation. The model uses quasi-static shell stress balance, a strain-production/turnover/diffusion equation, and pressure-volume dynamics. Pipette/agarose-window constraints bias the region of maximal stretch and organizer site. The authors explicitly say the model is qualitative because chemical/mechanical parameters are uncertain and use a one-dimensional reduced model for onset stability. This is a stronger baseline for mechanical axis steering; the present candidate targets a traveling Fog/integrin front and does not claim the Hydra intervention as new.

5. Classic morphogen-transport prior art, which bounds any novelty claim for the reduced transport equation:
   - “Analysis of dynamic morphogen scale invariance,” *Development* (2010), [open primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817156/), treats diffusion, receptor binding and endocytosis as determinants of morphogen length/time scales. Its scale analysis already covers the broad physical ingredients behind binding/clearance-controlled gradient lengths.
   - “Formation of the Long Range Dpp Morphogen Gradient,” *PLOS Biology* (2011), [DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1001111](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001111), [primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001111), analyzes free, receptor-bound and internalized ligand pools with transport, binding, internalization and degradation. It is a particularly direct prior-art comparator for multi-pool ligand transport and restricted extracellular mobility.
   - These sources make clear that neither receptor-mediated clearance nor a slow/fast ligand-pool reaction-transport model is novel by itself. The present conditional calculation adds no general morphogen-transport theorem; any candidate value would have to come from a new, validated connection between same-condition Fog-slow mobility/clearance, a cell-relative moving front, and an experimentally useful range-speed objective. Priority is unverified.
