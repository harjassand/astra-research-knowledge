1. The global theorem
Let
\[
P_1,\ldots,P_m\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}
\]
be distinct, nonidentity Pauli strings, with canonical signs. Write \(S_a=\operatorname{supp}P_a\), and assume
\[
|S_a|\le k,
\qquad
\max_i\#\{a:i\in S_a\}\le\Delta.
\]
The second condition bounds the number of candidate interactions touching each site, including candidates whose current coefficient is zero.
Consider
\[
H(h)=\sum_{a=1}^m h_aP_a,
\qquad
\max_i\sum_{a:i\in S_a}|h_a|\le g,
\]
and its unrestricted Gibbs state
\[
\rho_h=\frac{e^{-\beta H(h)}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H(h)}}.
\]
For a Hermitian observable \(A\), let
\[
A_c=A-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_hA)I,
\]
and define
\[
\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho_h}(A,A)
=
\int_0^1
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\rho_h^tA_c\rho_h^{1-t}A_c\right)\,dt.
\]
Theorem — Uniform thermal conditioning
For every fixed \(k,\Delta\), there is a finite constant \(C_{k,\Delta}\) such that, for every system satisfying these assumptions and every \(u\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho_h}
\left(\sum_a u_aP_a,\sum_a u_aP_a\right)
\ge
e^{-C_{k,\Delta}(1+\beta g)^2}
\sum_a u_a^2.
}
\tag{1}
\]
In fact, the proof establishes the stronger intermediate inequality
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
\rho_h^{1/2}A_c\rho_h^{1/2}A_c
\right)
\ge
e^{-C_{k,\Delta}(1+\beta g)^2}\|u\|_2^2.
}
\tag{2}
\]
The constant is independent of \(n\), \(m\), the coefficient signs, spectral gaps, and mixing times. It does not require commutativity, absence of frustration, high temperature, or decay of correlations.
This includes arbitrary bounded-degree interaction graphs, not just fixed-dimensional lattices. For the familiar assumption \(|h_a|\le J\), one may take \(g=\Delta J\).
The constant is constructive but very conservative. No practical value for it, or optimal temperature dependence, is claimed.
2. What this improves—and what was already known
There are two particularly direct comparisons.
The 2021 theorem of Anshu, Arunachalam, Kuwahara and Soleimanifar gives a log-partition Hessian lower bound with an explicit \(1/m\) loss for geometrically local quantum Hamiltonians. Theorem (1) removes that system-size loss under the stated bounded-incidence assumptions. arXiv
The bounded-degree appendix of the October 2026 paper Learning SYK Hamiltonians treats the same basic canonical Pauli setting and states
\[
\nabla^2\log Z(h)\succeq
\frac{c_{\beta,J,k,\Delta}}{m(1+\log(2m))}I.
\]
The bound derived here replaces that denominator by a constant depending only on the fixed physical parameters. This comparison concerns its bounded-degree appendix, not its dense-SYK results. arXiv
Several important positive regimes and learning capabilities predate this argument. Haah–Kothari–Tang obtained optimal high-temperature learning, while Rouzé–França established system-size-independent lower Hessian bounds for commuting Gibbs families. The intended extension here is arbitrary finite temperature with noncommuting bounded-degree interactions. arXiv
Nor is this the first arbitrary-temperature Hamiltonian-learning result. Bakshi–Liu–Moitra–Tang already established polynomial-time learning at fixed temperature; Chen–Anshu–Nguyen subsequently obtained strong local-learning guarantees, including near-optimal sample scaling and near-linear classical processing on lattices. arXiv
Bergamaschi’s October 2026 stationarity framework is especially relevant methodologically. Its “Fisher information” measures entropy production under a candidate Gibbs sampler. That is not the equilibrium BKM covariance matrix bounded in (1). Its local soundness estimates therefore should not be silently identified with the global theorem sought here. arXiv
3. The mechanism that closes the cancellation gap
The proof has two genuinely global components: an \(\ell_2\) bound on frequency-truncation errors and a bound on the image of the local interaction space under an auxiliary operator.
The crucial change in representation is:
Instead of demanding a local Gibbs sampler that mixes rapidly, construct localizable operators that are exactly self-adjoint in the thermal inner product. Move those adjoints before estimating spatially separated terms.

The resulting argument does not need ordinary correlation decay.
3.1 Local commutators detect all coefficients
Rescale
\[
\widehat H=H/g,
\qquad b=\beta g,
\]
so that the incident interaction strength of \(\widehat H\) is at most one.
Use the KMS inner product
\[
\langle X,Y\rangle_\rho
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{1/2}X^\dagger\rho^{1/2}Y),
\qquad
\|X\|_K^2=\langle X,X\rangle_\rho.
\]
Let \(Q_{i,\alpha}\) be a one-site Pauli, with \(\alpha=X,Y,Z\). For
\[
A=\sum_a u_aP_a,
\]
the commutator \([Q_{i,\alpha},A]\) is supported on the union of candidate interactions touching \(i\). That union has at most
\[
s=1+\Delta(k-1)
\]
sites.
The previous spectral-multiplicity theorem therefore gives
\[
\|[Q_{i,\alpha},A]\|_K^2
\ge
\kappa_b\,
\tau\!\left([Q_{i,\alpha},A]^\dagger[Q_{i,\alpha},A]\right),
\]
where
\[
\kappa_b=\frac{4}{9\,2^s}e^{-4bs},
\qquad
\tau(X)=2^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}X.
\]
Pauli orthogonality gives an exact identity:
\[
\sum_{i,\alpha}
\tau\!\left([Q_{i,\alpha},A]^\dagger[Q_{i,\alpha},A]\right)
=
8\sum_a |S_a|u_a^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
S(A):=\sum_{i,\alpha}\|[Q_{i,\alpha},A]\|_K^2
\ge8\kappa_b\|u\|_2^2.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The manuscript reconstructs the local spectral-multiplicity proof rather than importing it as an unreviewed premise.
Equation (3) is still only a gradient bound. Bounding every commutator separately by a global norm would reintroduce a system-size loss. The next steps avoid that.
3.2 Gaussian smearing gives exact, localizable thermal adjoints
Write \(j=(i,\alpha)\) and define
\[
B_{j,\omega}
=
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_{\mathbb R}
e^{-t^2/2}e^{-i\omega t}
e^{it\widehat H}Q_j e^{-it\widehat H}\,dt.
\]
These operators satisfy
\[
\|B_{j,\omega}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1,
\qquad
B_{j,\omega}^\dagger=B_{j,-\omega},
\]
and the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
e^{z\widehat H}B_{j,\omega}e^{-z\widehat H}
=
e^{z\omega+z^2/2}B_{j,\omega+z}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
To verify (4), in an energy eigenbasis the matrix element is
\[
(B_{j,\omega})_{rs}
=
(Q_j)_{rs}
e^{-(\omega-(E_r-E_s))^2/2};
\]
the identity is completion of the square.
Now put
\[
\partial_{j,\omega}(X)=[B_{j,\omega},X].
\]
Its exact KMS adjoint is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{j,\omega}^{\sharp}(Y)
={}&
e^{-b\omega/2+b^2/8}
B_{j,-\omega+b/2}Y\\
&-
e^{b\omega/2+b^2/8}
YB_{j,-\omega-b/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the potentially troublesome imaginary-time conjugation becomes a real frequency shift and an explicit scalar factor.
Define
\[
w_b(\omega)=e^{-(1+b)|\omega|},
\]
\[
\mathcal G_j
=
\int_{\mathbb R}
w_b(\omega)\,
\partial_{j,\omega}^{\sharp}\partial_{j,\omega}\,d\omega,
\qquad
\mathcal G=\sum_j\mathcal G_j.
\]
Every \(\mathcal G_j\) is exactly KMS-self-adjoint, positive in that inner product, and annihilates \(I\). Also,
\[
\|\mathcal G_j\|_{\infty\to\infty}
\le8e^{b^2/8}.
\]
The associated quadratic form is
\[
\mathcal D(A)
=
\langle A,\mathcal GA\rangle_\rho
=
\sum_j\int_{\mathbb R}
w_b(\omega)\|[B_{j,\omega},A]\|_K^2\,d\omega.
\tag{6}
\]
Gaussian operator Fourier methods and exact thermal adjoints have established antecedents in Gibbs sampling and local learning. The argument does not claim those ingredients individually as new. arXiv
The important freedom here is that \(-\mathcal G\) need not be a physical sampling generator. Complete positivity and rapid mixing are unnecessary for the covariance argument.
3.3 Locality and frequency tails can be summed without losing \(m\)
Connect sites that share a candidate interaction. This graph has degree at most \(\Delta(k-1)\), and its balls grow at most exponentially.
For every fixed spatial exponent \(\mu>0\), the Gaussian-smoothed operators admit approximations supported on a radius-\(r\) ball:
\[
\sup_\omega
\|B_{j,\omega}-B_{j,\omega}^{(r)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C_\mu e^{-\mu r}.
\tag{7}
\]
The exponent can be chosen arbitrarily large; its prefactor then becomes large but remains independent of system size. This follows by integrating a finite-range Lieb–Robinson estimate against the Gaussian. The proof uses the propagation estimate, not a ground-state or thermal clustering conclusion. arXiv
Using (5), the corresponding local approximations to \(\mathcal G_j\) satisfy
\[
\|\mathcal G_j-\mathcal G_j^{(r)}\|_{\infty\to\infty}
\le C_\mu e^{b^2/8}e^{-\mu r}.
\tag{8}
\]
For frequency truncation, nested commutators obey
\[
\|\operatorname{ad}_{\widehat H}^{\ell}(Q_j)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le(2k)^\ell\ell!.
\]
Combining the resulting short-imaginary-time bound with Gaussian smearing gives exponentially decaying frequency tails. Combining that bound with (7) gives joint shells of the form
\[
\|E_{j,r,\omega}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C e^{-\mu r}e^{-a|\omega|},
\qquad a=\frac1{8k}.
\]
Consequently, the residual
\[
R_{j,\Omega}
=
Q_j-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_{-\Omega}^{\Omega}B_{j,\omega}\,d\omega
\]
satisfies the global coefficient-space bound
\[
\boxed{
\sum_j\|[R_{j,\Omega},A]\|_K^2
\le T^2e^{-2a\Omega}\|u\|_2^2,
}
\tag{9}
\]
where \(T\) depends only on \(k,\Delta\).
The reason there is no system-size factor is explicit. The relevant kernel is
\[
K_{ia}=e^{-\mu\,\operatorname{dist}(i,S_a)}.
\]
For sufficiently large \(\mu\), its row and column sums are uniformly bounded:
\[
\sup_i\sum_a K_{ia}\le\Delta V_\mu,
\qquad
\sup_a\sum_i K_{ia}\le kV_\mu.
\]
The Schur test therefore bounds its \(\ell_2\)-operator norm independently of \(n,m\).
Minkowski and Cauchy–Schwarz now give
\[
\sqrt{S(A)}
\le
L_{b,\Omega}\sqrt{\mathcal D(A)}
+
Te^{-a\Omega}\|u\|_2,
\]
where
\[
L_{b,\Omega}^2
=
\frac{e^{(1+b)\Omega}-1}{\pi(1+b)}.
\]
Choose
\[
\Omega_b=
\max\left\{
1,\,
\frac1a\log\frac{2T}{\sqrt{8\kappa_b}}
\right\}.
\]
The residual is then at most half the lower bound from (3), so
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D(A)\ge\eta_b\|u\|_2^2,
\qquad
\eta_b=\frac{2\kappa_b}{L_{b,\Omega_b}^2}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Because \(\Omega_b=O_{k,\Delta}(1+b)\),
\[
\eta_b\ge e^{-C_{k,\Delta}(1+b)^2}.
\]
3.4 The decisive anti-cancellation lemma
What remains is to bound \(\|\mathcal GA\|_K\) without assuming thermal correlation decay.
The required lemma is:
A family of exactly thermal-self-adjoint maps with sufficiently fast local approximations has a uniformly bounded image on a bounded-incidence local interaction space.

For the maps above, it yields
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal GA\|_K^2
\le
B_0e^{b^2/4}\|u\|_2^2,
}
\tag{11}
\]
with \(B_0\) independent of \(n,m,b\).
Here is the essential proof.
Consider the Gram matrix
\[
M_{ac}
=
\langle\mathcal GP_a,\mathcal GP_c\rangle_\rho
=
\sum_{i,\alpha,j,\gamma}
\langle\mathcal G_{i,\alpha}P_a,
\mathcal G_{j,\gamma}P_c\rangle_\rho.
\]
Let \(R=\operatorname{dist}(S_a,S_c)\) and choose
\[
L=\lfloor R/6\rfloor.
\]
Terms whose centers lie far from their respective supports are exponentially small by (8), because a local approximation acting disjointly from \(P_a\) annihilates it.
For the remaining terms, move the exact thermal adjoint first:
\[
\boxed{
\langle\mathcal G_{i,\alpha}P_a,
\mathcal G_{j,\gamma}P_c\rangle_\rho
=
\langle P_a,
\mathcal G_{i,\alpha}\mathcal G_{j,\gamma}P_c\rangle_\rho.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Replace both maps on the right by radius-\(L\) approximations. Their relevant supports are disjoint, and therefore
\[
\mathcal G_{i,\alpha}^{(L)}
\mathcal G_{j,\gamma}^{(L)}P_c=0.
\]
The original term is consequently controlled by approximation errors—not by an estimate of thermal correlations.
If ball volume grows as \(e^{\nu r}\), summing close-center pairs contributes at most \(e^{2\nu L}\). Choosing the spatial decay exponent sufficiently larger than \(\nu\) produces
\[
|M_{ac}|
\le
C e^{b^2/4}
e^{-\sigma\operatorname{dist}(S_a,S_c)},
\qquad \sigma>\nu.
\]
The candidate incidence bound makes the rows of this Gram kernel summable. A second Schur bound proves (11).
This is the step that controls cancellation between arbitrary coefficients. It does not assert that the original covariance entries
\(\operatorname{Cov}_\rho(P_a,P_c)\) decay.
3.5 Closing the theorem
Since \(\mathcal GI=0\) and \(\mathcal G\) is KMS-self-adjoint,
\[
\mathcal D(A)=\langle A_c,\mathcal GA\rangle_\rho.
\]
Combining (10), Cauchy–Schwarz, and (11),
\[
\eta_b^2\|u\|_2^4
\le
\|A_c\|_K^2\,
B_0e^{b^2/4}\|u\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\|A_c\|_K^2
\ge
\frac{\eta_b^2}{B_0e^{b^2/4}}\|u\|_2^2
\ge
e^{-C_{k,\Delta}(1+b)^2}\|u\|_2^2.
}
\]
Finally, in the energy eigenbasis, BKM weights matrix elements by the logarithmic mean of two Gibbs probabilities, while KMS uses their geometric mean. The former is at least the latter. This proves (1).
There is no additional unproved tensorization assumption in this argument. The full manuscript supplies the local input, tail estimates, image lemma, and a constant-construction recipe.
4. A decisive counterexample to the weaker assumption
The distinction between bounded overlap and bounded weighted strength is necessary.
Fix \(\beta,h,\lambda>0\). On \(N+1\) spins, consider
\[
H_N
=
-h\sum_{i=0}^{N}Z_i
-\frac{\lambda}{N}Z_0\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_j.
\]
Every interaction is at most two-local. Every displayed Hamiltonian coefficient is nonzero. The total incident interaction strength is bounded by \(h+\lambda\), independently of \(N\).
Set
\[
t=\tanh(\beta h),
\qquad
A_N=
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_0Z_j-tZ_0.
\]
Its coefficient norm satisfies
\[
\|u^{(N)}\|_2^2=t^2+\frac1N,
\]
which stays bounded away from zero.
Conditioned on \(Z_0=s\), the leaves are independent with mean
\[
t_s=\tanh\!\left(\beta\left(h+\frac{\lambda s}{N}\right)\right),
\qquad
|t_s-t|\le\frac{\beta\lambda}{N}.
\]
Since \(H_N\) and \(A_N\) commute,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho_N}(A_N,A_N)
&=\operatorname{Var}_{\rho_N}(A_N)\\
&\le
\frac1N+\frac{(\beta\lambda)^2}{N^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_{\rho_N}(A_N,A_N)}
{\|u^{(N)}\|_2^2}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{13}
\]
The obstruction is already classical: an average over many leaves becomes nearly deterministic and cancels a local term.
The center participates in \(N\) pair interactions. Therefore this disproves the desired inequality under bounded locality and bounded weighted interaction strength alone, while leaving the bounded-incidence theorem intact.
5. Consequences for physical inference
Let
\[
F(h)=\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H(h)},
\qquad
m_a(h)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_hP_a),
\]
and abbreviate
\[
c=e^{-C_{k,\Delta}(1+\beta g)^2}.
\]
On any convex coefficient domain satisfying the common strength bound,
\[
\nabla F(h)=-\beta m(h),
\qquad
\nabla^2F(h)\succeq\beta^2cI.
\]
Two consequences follow immediately.
Uniform inversion of the original local moment map
For any two Hamiltonians in that domain,
\[
\boxed{
\|h-h'\|_2
\le
\frac1{\beta c}\|m(h)-m(h')\|_2.
}
\tag{14}
\]
This identifies all coefficients jointly from the original local expectation values. There is no additional system-size deterioration in the inverse Lipschitz constant.
Relative entropy detects coefficient displacement
The relative entropy is the corresponding free-energy Bregman divergence, so
\[
\boxed{
D(\rho_h\|\rho_{h'})
\ge
\frac{\beta^2c}{2}\|h-h'\|_2^2.
}
\tag{15}
\]
These are global statements about the coefficient space. They do not require a nearby-product-state approximation.
Measurement and computational resources
The overlap graph of the candidate terms can be colored with
\[
\chi\le k(\Delta-1)+1
\]
colors. Terms of one color have disjoint supports and can be measured simultaneously using single-qubit Pauli-basis measurements.
Estimating each local moment to error \(\delta\), with joint failure probability at most \(p\), requires
\[
N=
O\!\left(
\chi\delta^{-2}\log\frac{2m}{p}
\right)
\]
independent Gibbs-state copies.
Using a constrained maximum-entropy estimator, total coefficient error
\(\|\widehat h-h\|_2\le\varepsilon\) is therefore guaranteed with
\[
\boxed{
N=
O\!\left(
\frac{\chi m}{\beta^2c^2\varepsilon^2}
\log\frac{2m}{p}
\right).
}
\tag{16}
\]
For root-mean-square coefficient error
\[
m^{-1/2}\|\widehat h-h\|_2\le\varepsilon,
\]
the factor \(m\) disappears. That is not an \(\ell_\infty\) guarantee for every coefficient.
The measurement layer needs no entangling gates. However, strong convexity does not make the maximum-entropy objective inexpensive to evaluate. Direct dense evaluation still involves matrices of dimension \(2^n\); independent equilibrium-state preparation is also an input cost, not a free consequence.
Accordingly, this establishes improved conditioning and statistical guarantees for the minimal local moment map. It does not establish an end-to-end computational advantage over the existing arbitrary-temperature learning algorithms.
6. Low temperature, frustration, phases, and validation
The proof allows arbitrary coefficient signs and noncommuting Pauli terms. Frustration does not enter as an excluded case.
Temperature deterioration is unavoidable. For one qubit with \(H=hZ\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_\rho(Z,Z)
=
\operatorname{sech}^2(\beta h)
\sim4e^{-2\beta h}.
\]
Thus no temperature-independent or merely polynomial-in-\(\beta\) universal lower bound is possible. The quadratic exponent obtained here is not claimed optimal.
The theorem also does not bound the largest covariance eigenvalue. It is therefore compatible with phase-dependent enhancement of response; it neither proves clustering nor excludes phase transitions.
Hard constraints require separate treatment. In a state restricted to a sector where \(\sum_iZ_i\) is fixed, that observable has zero variance. Such a state is outside the unrestricted full-rank Gibbs assumption, and its null coefficient directions must first be removed.
The accompanying code performed six finite algebraic checks and 24 small-system covariance checks, including noncommuting chains and antiferromagnetic cycles. The declared numerical tolerances passed. Those computations are falsification checks, not proof or independent validation.
The substantive outcome is a resolution of the cancellation step for bounded-incidence Pauli families, together with a rigorous impossibility result when only weighted strength is bounded. The outstanding issues are external proof reconstruction and priority assessment, sharper temperature dependence, extensions to growing-degree or constrained systems, and computationally efficient exploitation of the new conditioning theorem.
