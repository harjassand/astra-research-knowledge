## Quantum: structural information theorem

Let rho_N be any permutation-invariant N-qubit state. Write M=2j for its total-spin-sector label. Suppose fixed constants A>=1,B,C>=0 satisfy

    E exp(t M/sqrt(N)) <= A exp(Bt^2+Ct), all t>=0,

uniformly in N. No purity, coherent-state form or commutativity is imposed inside the sectors. Let tau_N=rho_{N,1}. Then

    Petz chi-square(rho_{N,r} || tau_N tensor r) = O((r/N)^2),
    D(rho_{N,r} || tau_N tensor r) = O((r/N)^2),

for every subextensive r_N=o(N); r=1 is exactly zero. Constants and finite ranges are explicit in subgaussian_spin_tail_FROZEN.md. Exact white Schur weights are one sufficient instance.

Dependency spine: unitary Schur realization for complex two-by-two matrices; collective-spin MGF control; a centered Hermitian one-site basis with its complex Gram matrix; exact weight-one cancellation; support-wise Gram conditioning, multivariate Cauchy coefficient bounds and all-weight summation; spectral-overlap Jensen for noncommuting quantum entropy. Paying Gram conditioning per support is essential; multiplying one global inverse-density norm would lose the growing-r result.

## Quantum: ALLSEP matched operational theorem

The null class consists of all fully separable N-site states without a Schur law or Casimir promise. Each trial uses an independent fresh target preparation, and at most r distinct sites of that copy can be touched. Site and axis random choices follow preparation. Classical adaptive labels, quantum memory and arbitrary joint measurements on the accessed registers are allowed for the lower bound. Coherent addressing that physically touches all N sites is not an r-site protocol.

The matching product tau_N tensor N is a legal separable null, so the centered theorem forces m=Omega(N^2/r^2) preparations for any fixed testing gap. At r=1 certification is impossible for every m through this interface. The direct lower concerns independent copies of the target and this particular independent null; it already obstructs every uniformly sound ALLSEP test.

For targets in the tail class with a strictly positive collective spin-variance violation,

    liminf [2 - 4 sum_a Var(J_a)/N] > 0,

the bounded common-axis heat effect supplies a sound test with m=O(N^2/r^2 log(1/eta)). For exact white weights this extra condition is liminf N|b_N|^2>1. Its bounded outcome, post-preparation random subset, all-weight target control and uniform optimization over heterogeneous product arrays yield a gap of order r/N. Convexity handles mixed separable nulls. The separate two-site tangent witness covers every bounded r>=2. The result is asymptotic with enormous constants; no practical moderate-N experiment has been demonstrated.

## Promise distinction and falsifier

A trusted null Casimir promise <J^2>=3N/4 excludes the matching product marginal in the polarized example. Fully separable states satisfying that promise have |b|<=1/sqrt(N), while the target has asymptotic norm sqrt(8/pi)/sqrt(N). Local mean sampling then admits O(N/r) preparations, and one-body access is sufficient. The quadratic ALLSEP theorem must not be described as a universal barrier across those different null classes. Acquiring a global Casimir promise experimentally is a charged task, not an automatic gift to the certifier.

The independent rare-sector counterexample satisfies the exact same Casimir mean but violates the centered quadratic order: a weight 3/(N+2) all-up component mixed with a permutation-twirled singlet state gives D=Omega(r/N) for log N << r=o(N). Thus some quantitative spin-sector tail condition is mathematically necessary for the claimed general structural bound. A mean-only extension is refuted.
