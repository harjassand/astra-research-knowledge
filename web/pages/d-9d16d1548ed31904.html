## Exact failed implication, with no hidden ensemble convention

Source Eq32 defines a state-dependent tensor

K_ijkl(rho)=<c_i c_j (c_k c_l)^dagger>_rho
 −G_ik(rho)G_jl(rho)+G_il(rho)G_jk(rho),

where G_ik(rho)=<c_i c_k^dagger>_rho.

In Section III.D / III.4, Eq68 replaces K(sum_tau p_tau rho_tau) by sum_tau p_tau K(rho_tau), and Eq69 applies maximum-eigenvalue convexity to that presumed affine tensor. The surrounding text identifies the left side as the same mixed state's 2CRDM; it does not introduce a decomposition-dependent or convex-roof definition. The substitution is invalid for state-only Eq32 because its disconnected products are nonlinear in rho.

Our independently derived correction is exact:

K(rho)=E_tau K(rho_tau)
 +Cov_tau(G_ik,G_jl)−Cov_tau(G_il,G_jk).

Here covariance means E(XY)−E(X)E(Y), without complex conjugation, with indices as displayed. It follows simply by expanding Eq32 and the affine four-point expectation. If all component G matrices coincide, this correction vanishes; the paper's unrestricted mixed-state ensemble does not impose that promise.

## Small exact counterexample

Let rho=.5|1100><1100|+.5|0011><0011|. Each pure component is a fixed-two-particle Slater determinant with K=0, hence an allowed separable Gaussian component. In the mixture G=.5 I. For i=1,j=2,k=1,l=2,

<c1 c2(c1 c2)^dagger>=<(1−n1)(1−n2)>=.5,

G11 G22=.25, G12 G21=0, so K1212=.25.

Thus Eq68's left side is .25 while its right side is zero. All states in this counterexample have fixed total particle number, so number-sector fluctuations and anomalous pairing are irrelevant. It directly invalidates the advertised mixed-state extension under Eq32's operational definition. It does not refute their pure-state threshold derivations or their measured numerical pure-state examples.

## Analytic arbitrary-size false-positive family

For N>=2, use 2N modes split into blocks A and B of N modes, and mix their complementary Slaters equally. Write s_i=+1 in A, −1 in B. Hole occupations have covariance s_i s_j/4. Since the ensemble is occupation-diagonal, its full cumulant is

K_ijkl=(delta_ik delta_jl−delta_il delta_jk) s_i s_j/4 for i!=j,

and K=0 for i=j. In the realignment R_(ik),(jl), the diagonal-index sector (i,i) has the matrix

(ss^T−I_(2N))/4,

whose largest eigenvalue is (2N−1)/4, with eigenvector s; its other eigenvalues are −1/4. Every off-diagonal pair {(i,j),(j,i)} is a 2x2 flip block with eigenvalues +/-1/4. Therefore the largest eigenvalue of the entire realigned cumulant is exactly (2N−1)/4. The components have zero cumulants while the mixture's supposed entanglement witness grows without bound with N.

This reproduces r12's finite CAR result by a separate full analytic tensor and spectral derivation. Worker agreement is not external validation; the independently checked publisher definition and exact counterexample are the decisive evidence.

## Stronger observational obstruction

For N>=3 the coherent state (|A>+|B>)/sqrt(2) has the same 1RDM and 2RDM as the classical mixture. A one- or two-body fermionic monomial can move at most two particles and therefore cannot connect the two occupation configurations, which differ by moving all N particles. Thus all cross terms vanish in those reduced expectations. Their state-only cumulants and all its flattenings are identical.

Consequently no function of only the complete 1RDM and 2RDM can distinguish these coherent GHZ-type states from the corresponding separable Slater mixture. Increasing precision or altering a scalar threshold of this same statistic cannot repair that particular indistinguishability. A mixed-state entanglement witness needs genuinely different information, a valid compatibility/convex-roof characterization, or explicit physical promises such as a verified pure-state setting.
