MINIMAL THREE-GAIN REGULARIZATION FOR AXIAL COLD PARTNERS
Derived in this run, 7 October 2026; independent differential check by
/root/pde_physics/kinetic_fluctuation. Status: self-contained local lemma,
not a new global kinetic/PDE theorem or a priority-cleared historical advance.

The October release uses FIVE gains in 10-nonconcentration.tex,
lem:five-gain-rank. The same a.e. rank and robust pullback conclusion holds
with THREE gains, and two gains cannot suffice for this seven-coordinate
space-time-velocity pullback.

Exact interface:
Work in R^3. Fix t0>0, x0, v0 not parallel to e3; fix three positive scales
and signs. Let p_i(t,x)=a_i(t,x_3)e3, where a_i is the analytic axial
transport profile of the source (the witness below only needs C1 profiles).
For t_i>0 and L_i in R^3 define
  x_i=x_{i-1}-(t_{i-1}-t_i)v_{i-1},
  n_i=v_{i-1}-p_i(t_i,x_i),
  k_i=n_i cross L_i,
  v_i=v_{i-1}+k_i,
for i=1,2,3. Then Phi=(t3,x3,v3) is analytic on the connected open
domain (0,infinity)^3 x R^9 and rank D Phi=7 a.e., in particular on
0<t3<t2<t1<t0. For finite-temperature core maps converging C1 locally,
the source's uniform pullback lemma consequently applies to three gains.

Proof of one full-rank witness (clean version):
Rotate about e3 so v0=(R,0,z), R>0. Choose 0<h<tau<t0 and set
  t1=t2=tau, t3=tau-h,
  k1=0, L1=0; k2=e2=(0,1,0); k3=0, L3=0.
At the first two collisions positions coincide at x_tau=x0-(t0-tau)v0.
Write n1=(R,0,b1), n2=(R,0,b2). Both are nonzero and k2 perpendicular n2,
so choose L2=-(n2 cross k2)/|n2|^2. The final normal is
  n3=(R,1,b3),
where b3 depends on the third profile at its actual location. In particular
n3 dot k2=1, independently of all three axial profile values or derivatives.

Step 1. First-two-gain angular variations can produce every delta v2 in R^3.
Since t1=t2, varying L1,L2 leaves x1,x2 and hence p1,p2 unchanged.
With [a]_cross denoting the matrix for a cross product,
  delta v2=(I-[L2]_cross)[n1]_cross delta L1
                     +[n2]_cross delta L2.
An annihilator of the second range must be a scalar multiple of n2.
But
  (I-[L2]_cross)^T n2=n2+L2 cross n2=n2-k2=(R,-1,b2),
which is not parallel to n1=(R,0,b1). Thus no nonzero annihilator remains:
the displayed angular map is onto R^3.

Step 2. Obtain all final-velocity directions and two position-only directions.
For a variation giving delta v2=d and holding L3 fixed at zero,
  (delta x3,delta v3)=(-h d,d).
The position dependence of p3 contributes no term because L3=0.
For any d in n3-perp, an additional variation
  delta L3=(n3 cross d)/|n3|^2
gives n3 cross delta L3=-d. Thus the combined variation is
  (delta x3,delta v3)=(-h d,0).
Taking a basis of n3-perp gives two independent position-only directions.
Taking arbitrary d in Step 1 gives three independent final-velocity directions.

Step 3. Obtain the remaining position direction from the second collision time.
At the witness,
  x3=x0-v0(t0-t1)-v1(t1-t2)-v2(t2-t3), v3=v2.
Holding the other parameters fixed, the t2 column is
  (partial_t2 x3,partial_t2 v3)
    =(-k2-h partial_t2 v2, partial_t2 v2).
Subtract the Step 1 angular variation with d=partial_t2 v2. The result is
  (-k2,0).
This is independent of the two position-only directions from n3-perp because
n3 dot k2=1. Hence six parameter directions have full rank for (x3,v3)
while keeping t3 fixed. Appending the t3 parameter and the output t3 gives
rank seven for Phi. The seven selected directions need not be coordinate
columns: their independence implies some coordinate 7x7 minor is nonzero.

Step 4. Ordered histories and robust measurable pullback.
The equal first two times are allowed in the connected analytic extension
used by the source. The full-rank witness persists under a small perturbation
making t1>t2>t3. A nonzero analytic minor has null zero set on the connected
parameter domain. Thus full rank holds a.e. in the ordered region. The source
nc:uniform-pullback then applies, with arbitrary bounded measurable masks.
For convergent finite-temperature profiles on compact sets, the source's
implicit derivative bound >=1 gives the same C1 convergence with spectator
thermal variables. This step introduces no new quantitative modulus.

Minimality:
With two gains, there are two times and two plane coordinates per gain,
so at most six effective parameters for a seven-coordinate output. In the
redundant L parameterization, variation of each L_i along n_i changes no
increment and no later state, giving independent kernel directions when
n_i!=0. At n_i=0 the corresponding angular block vanishes and rank cannot
improve. Therefore the two-gain map never has rank seven. Three is minimal
for this particular full spacetime/velocity pullback task.

What this actually buys:
The source's five-gain nonconcentration argument away from the velocity axis
can use a third iterated gain for the full-rank smoothing input, instead of a
fifth. A separate downstream audit checked every fixed-count truncation:
endpoint tails bound two other gains instead of four; three successive
off-axis input deletions retain the three normal lower bounds; spatial-axis
deletions still cost C_a rho/b; there are finitely many three-tuples of
scales/signs; the U=1 upper-endpoint deletion costs
  2(C/a)^3 delta^3/3!;
the ordered-time boundary has four time strips instead of six, each costing
C_a kappa; the compact parameter domain contains 3 times, 9 L coordinates
and 9 cold spectator coordinates. The rank/pullback proof above applies.
Near-axis suppression still uses the source's separate two-gain estimate,
with one bounded subsequent gain. The new residual inequality is
  f_n <= (a+l_n+epsilon_n)[1+C log(1/a)+(C log(1/a))^2/2]
                                          +V_n^3 f_n.
At cap endpoints the third term tends to zero using axial/nonaxial cases,
so 1/2 <= a[1+C log(1/a)+(C log(1/a))^2/2], contradicted as a->0.
These replacements use exactly the source's kernel/tail/axis assumptions;
the new rank lemma does not independently establish those assumptions.
The local rank mechanism is independent
of fine spatial masks and of the three axial profile values. It may support
generalization to other cold partner profiles/collision geometries, but such
generalizations need actual gain estimates and cannot be inferred from rank.

Consequence boundary:
This sharpening neither creates a uniform grazing limit for the positive
hot generator nor solves Landau nonuniqueness. No global uniqueness class,
hydrodynamic limit, Boltzmann-Grad selection theorem, or original-release
correctness is established merely by this rank improvement.
