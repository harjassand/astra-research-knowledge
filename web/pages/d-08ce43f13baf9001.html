1. EXACT INTERFACE

The audited equation is the q=2 Saint-Raymond scaling
  eps partial_t f_eps + v dot grad_x f_eps = eps^(-2) Q(f_eps,f_eps),
with isotropic normalized scattering kernel B(u,omega)=|u|/(4 pi) and
homogeneous kinetic time tau=t/eps^3. The family uses R=K eps^-3,
delta=R^-3, b=-R^-2/(1-R^-3), and
  s^2=1-1/[3R(1-R^-3)]-1/[3R^4(1-R^-3)^2].
The bulk-plus-tail Gaussian mixture has exactly mass 1, momentum 0, energy 3.
Its tail mass, energy, and cubic flux are respectively R^-3, R^-1, and
order one; convexity bounds the relative entropy by 1/(2R)+O(R^-2).
Thus at fixed K, H/eps^2 ->0. The alternative exponent-one scaling would map
c/R to c eps^5/K; the recorded eps^6/K is correct specifically for q=2.

2. POSITIVE-TIME WEIGHTED-L2 LOWER BOUND

For the mild gain-loss equation, gain positivity and the conserved mass and
energy imply
  F(tau,v)>=F(0,v) exp[-tau nu_max(v)],
  nu(tau,v)<=|v|+int |v_*|F(tau,v_*)dv_*<=|v|+sqrt(3).
On A=B(Re_1,R^-1), the tail Gaussian gives F(0,v)>=cR^-3; the reference
Maxwellian inverse satisfies M(v)^-1>=c exp((R-R^-1)^2/2). Since
f_eps(t)=F(t/eps^3), for 0<=t<=T this yields, uniformly on A,
  f_eps(t,v)/M(v)>=cR^-3 exp((R-R^-1)^2/2
                              -eps^-3 t(R+R^-1+sqrt(3))).
For R=K eps^-3, the leading exponent is
  (K^2/2-Kt) eps^-6.
K>4T is more than enough to make this positive uniformly on [0,T]. Squaring,
using f_eps>=2M for small eps, and multiplying by vol(A)~R^-3 yields
  int (f_eps-M)^2/M >= cR^-9 exp((R-R^-1)^2/2
                         -2eps^-3 T(R+R^-1+sqrt(3))) -> infinity,
uniformly on [0,T]. The prefactor R^-9 correctly combines the squared tail
amplitude R^-6 and ball volume R^-3. This proves failure of Saint-Raymond
(2009) condition (2.3) at every positive time in any preassigned finite
horizon after fixing K>4T. It does not imply a flux defect: the reciprocal
Maxwellian weight magnifies a velocity ball whose ordinary mass is tiny.

The existence interface is appropriate for the datum: it is nonnegative,
L-infinity, and has finite mass/energy (indeed every polynomial moment) for
each fixed eps. Mischler--Wennberg Theorem 1.1 gives the global conservative
homogeneous cutoff solution in this class; its mild gain-loss formula is the
input to the lower bound. The angular kernel is fixed and bounded. The proof
does not assume the tail is collision-free; the upper collision-frequency
bound is integrated for every time in the interval.

3. SHRINKING SIGNED-FLUX LAYER

The fourth-moment initial cost is
  M4(0)= (1-delta)(b^4+10b^2s^2+15s^4)
        +delta(R^4+10R^2+15) <= C0 R.
For uniform post-collision direction sigma on S^2, write V=(v+v_*)/2,
u=v-v_*. Then
  v'=V+|u|sigma/2, v_*'=V-|u|sigma/2,
  average_sigma(|v'|^4+|v_*'|^4)-(|v|^4+|v_*|^4)
    =2|u|^2(|V|^2/3-(V dot uhat)^2)
    <=(2/3)|V cross u|^2
    =(2/3)|v cross v_*|^2
    <=(2/3)|v|^2|v_*|^2.
This directly verifies the fourth-moment Povzner upper bound for the chosen
isotropic angular kernel. The weak collision identity therefore gives
  M4'(tau)<=C int F F_* |u||v|^2|v_*|^2
            <=C M3 M2<=C sqrt(M4),
using mass 1 and M2=3. Hence M4(tau)<=C1 R for tau<=c0/R.

The exact cubic pair sum is
  |v|^2v+|v_*|^2v_*=2|V|^2V+(|u|^2/2)V+(V dot u)u.
An elastic collision keeps V and |u| fixed, so the pair-sum increment is at
most 2|V||u|^2. Including the hard-sphere rate |u| and integrating gives
  |q'(tau)|<=C int F F_* |V||u|^3<=C2 M4(tau).
Thus |q(tau)-q(0)|<=C3 R tau on [0,c0/R]. Direct substitution gives
q_1(0)->1. Choosing c_*<=c0 with C3 c_*<=1/4 proves q_1>=1/2 on
0<=tau<=c_*/R. Under q=2 scaling, the layer is
0<=t<=c_* eps^6/K.

Saint-Raymond's flux test is Psi(v)=v(|v|^2-5)/2; with zero momentum,
int Psi f=q/2. Therefore the first component of its scaled-fluctuation
integral over this layer is at least c_* eps^5/(4K), and its absolute value
is O(eps^5/K). The lower bound is positive but tends to zero. The homogeneous
solution has zero spatial divergence of this flux. No fixed-positive-time
flux persistence or hydrodynamic defect follows.

4. PRIOR-THEOREM SCOPE AND DECISIVE BOTTLENECK

Saint-Raymond (2009), Theorem 2.1, uses scaling
  eps partial_t f_eps+v dot grad_x f_eps=eps^-q Q, q>1,
assumes initial H/eps^2 bounded and entropic convergence, and additionally
requires (2.3), exactly int (f_eps-M)^2/M dv<=C a.e. in time and space.
The q=2 family has H/eps^2->0 and its scaled initial fluctuation tends to zero
in L1(Mdv); the homogeneous mild solution violates (2.3) uniformly on each
chosen finite horizon. This demonstrates that entropy preparation alone
does not propagate/produce the theorem's stronger weighted-L2 tail input
for this actual solution. It does not refute the theorem because (2.3) is a
hypothesis and it is violated.

The same family is well-prepared with zero limiting velocity for the
Saint-Raymond 2003 route. Its entropy and fluctuation limit meet the stated
well-prepared data interface. That baseline uses refined dissipation/momentum
flux arguments and does not acquire (2.3) from entropy. Accordingly there is
no contradiction between (i) persistent weighted-L2 divergence, (ii) a
vanishing integrated signed flux on the proven initial layer, and (iii) the
well-prepared Euler convergence result.

Decisive unresolved gate: bound the signed cubic q(tau) for tau comparable
to eps^-3 (fixed positive macroscopic time), or control its full later-time
integral. The high-tail Duhamel estimate is only a lower bound on F and
cannot prevent angular redistribution or signed cancellation in q. The
Povzner estimate proves q persistence only through tau=O(1/R), whose scaled
flux integral vanishes. No extension to unforced Navier--Stokes or any
Navier--Stokes singularity claim is made.
