BLIND VERDICT
The proposed e^3 <= (27C/4) D b is true under exactly the supplied
hypotheses, with the stronger bound

 e^3 <= (27C/8) D b.                                    (T)

Psi need NOT be Hilbert-Schmidt self-adjoint. Complete positivity,
bistochasticity, and the specified full real-Hermitian quadratic order
suffice. For C=4 this gives

 e <= 3*2^(-1/3) (Db)^(1/3) <= 3(Db)^(1/3),
 e^2 <= 3(D+1)b^(2/3) <= 5(D+1)b^(2/3).                 (C4)

The SAME fixed Psi works for every density matrix rho. State-dependent
averaging below is only a proof device, not a new/adaptive comparator.

1. EXACT HYPOTHESES AND CONVENTIONS
Let d<infinity, tau=Tr/d. Phi and Psi are fixed linear completely positive
maps on M_d, TP and unital. Phi is HS-self-adjoint. Assume fixed C>0 and
for EVERY Hermitian X,

 tau[X(I-Psi)(X)] <= C tau[X(I-Phi)(X)].                 (H)

When Psi is not self-adjoint, this is quadratic-form order, equivalently
order for the symmetric HS part of I-Psi. No nonsymmetric linear-operator
Loewner order is being asserted. Hermitian quadratic values are real.
No assumption that Psi commutes with Phi, is covariant, is canonical or
is EB is required for this theorem. An EB comparator can of course supply
Psi when (H) is available, and is then kept unchanged throughout.

For density rho, define
 D=Tr[rho log(d rho)]=D(rho||I/d),
 b=||Phi(rho)-rho||_1/2,
 e=||Psi(rho)-rho||_1/2.
All logarithms are NATURAL; 0 log 0=0. Since I/d is faithful, D is finite
for every finite-dimensional density, including rank-deficient ones.
If D is instead in bits, D_nat=(ln2)D_bits, so the stated weaker constants
also remain valid with D_bits. For other log bases include the conversion.

Put h=d rho, tau(h)=1, and define for Hermitian U
 E_L(U)=tau[U(I-L)(U)].
Bistochastic CP maps contract HS norm, so these forms are nonnegative;
the proof below also verifies positivity on the square roots it uses.

2. DIMENSION-FREE KRAUS ESTIMATE, NO PSI SELF-ADJOINTNESS
For ANY bistochastic CP map L and density sigma, take Kraus operators K_j
with sum K_j* K_j=sum K_j K_j*=I. Let rectangular block-row matrices
 A=[K_1 sqrt(sigma),K_2 sqrt(sigma),...],
 B=[sqrt(sigma)K_1,sqrt(sigma)K_2,...].
Then AA*=L(sigma), BB*=sigma, ||A||_2=||B||_2=1, and

 ||A-B||_2^2
   =2-2Tr[sqrt(sigma)L(sqrt(sigma))]
   =2 E_L(sqrt(d sigma)).

The cross trace is real and nonnegative because sqrt(sigma) and its
positive image have nonnegative real trace pairing. Schatten Holder gives
 ||AA*-BB*||_1
 <=||A-B||_2 (||A||_2+||B||_2)
 =2 sqrt(2 E_L(sqrt(d sigma))).
Thus, with half trace distance,

 e_L(sigma)^2 <= 2 E_L(sqrt(d sigma)).                   (K)

The factor d cancels exactly: tau[sqrt(d sigma)(I-L)sqrt(d sigma)] equals
Tr[sqrt(sigma)(I-L)sqrt(sigma)]. All rectangular dimensions disappear
from the Holder bound. Neither L nor its Kraus operators must be HS
self-adjoint. Applying (H) to sqrt(d sigma) therefore gives

 e_Psi(sigma)^2 <=2C E_Phi(sqrt(d sigma)).               (KPhi)

3. CP POISSON SEMIGROUP AND ENTROPY DISSIPATION
For t>=0 define

 P_t=exp[t(Phi-I)]=e^(-t) sum_(k>=0) t^k Phi^k/k!.

This is CPTP and unital, commutes with Phi, and has generator Phi-I.
Negative eigenvalues of Phi cause no difficulty. P_t is used analytically;
no efficient simulation, Choi acquisition or apparatus is inferred.
First assume rho is faithful and put rho_t=P_t(rho), h_t=d rho_t.
Unital positivity preserves its positive lower spectral bound for finite
times. Standard finite matrix trace differentiation, which does not require
commutation of h_t and its derivative, gives

 -d/dt D(rho_t||I/d)=tau[(h_t-Phi(h_t)) log h_t].         (D1)

Diagonalize h_t ONLY for this calculation, with rank-one spectral basis
P_i and positive eigenvalues a_i. Define
 k_ij=Tr[P_i Phi(P_j)].
Complete positivity gives k_ij>=0; unitality and TP make all row and column
sums one. HS-self-adjointness of Phi gives k_ij=k_ji. The full channel need
not preserve the diagonal algebra; trace pairings with functions of h_t
depend only on these coefficients. Consequently

 tau[(h_t-Phi(h_t))log h_t]
   =(1/(2d)) sum_ij k_ij (a_i-a_j)(log a_i-log a_j),
 E_Phi(sqrt(h_t))
   =(1/(2d)) sum_ij k_ij (sqrt(a_i)-sqrt(a_j))^2.

For positive a,b,
 (a-b)(log a-log b) >=4(sqrt(a)-sqrt(b))^2.              (SV)
A self-contained check: by symmetry take x=sqrt(a/b)>=1; the claim is
(x+1)log x>=2(x-1). Its difference divided by x+1 is
log x-2(x-1)/(x+1), whose derivative is
(x-1)^2/[x(x+1)^2]>=0 and whose value at one is zero.

Equations (D1) and (SV) give

 -D'(rho_t) >=4 E_Phi(sqrt(h_t)).                       (D2)

Integrating, using D(rho_T||I/d)>=0, and then (KPhi), yields for ANY T>0

 integral_0^T E_Phi(sqrt(h_t)) dt <=D/4,
 integral_0^T e_Psi(rho_t)^2 dt <=CD/2.                 (D3)

No spectral gap, lower eigenvalue promise, curvature estimate, monotonicity
of square-root energy or commutation of Psi with P_t is used. Only the
initial faithful proof step uses a lower eigenvalue, removed in Section 6.

4. TIME AVERAGING AND THE EXACT RESIDUAL COEFFICIENT
Since P_s is trace-norm contractive on Hermitian matrices,

 ||rho_t-rho||_1
 <= integral_0^t ||P_s(Phi-I)(rho)||_1 ds <=2bt.

Let bar_rho_T=(1/T) integral_0^T rho_t dt. This is a density matrix.
Linearity of the SAME Psi and convexity of its residual norm, followed by
Cauchy-Schwarz and (D3), give

 e_Psi(bar_rho_T)
 <=(1/T) integral_0^T e_Psi(rho_t)dt <=sqrt(CD/(2T)).

Moreover ||rho-bar_rho_T||_1<=bT. CPTP contractivity of Psi and the triangle
inequality therefore give

 e_Psi(rho)
 <=e_Psi(bar_rho_T)+||rho-bar_rho_T||_1
 <=bT+sqrt(CD/(2T)), for every T>0.                     (A)

The correction is bT, not 2bT: the factor 2 in ||rho_t-rho||_1 integrates
to (1/T) integral_0^T 2bt dt=bT. The channel comparison's two half-distance
terms sum to one full ||rho-bar_rho_T||_1. All uses of Psi remain fixed.

5. OPTIMIZATION AND THE REQUESTED CONSEQUENCES
For D>0 and b>0, minimize (A) at
 T=(CD/(8b^2))^(1/3).
The two terms are (CDb)^(1/3)/2 and (CDb)^(1/3), respectively. Thus

 e<=(3/2)(CDb)^(1/3), e^3<=(27C/8)Db,

which proves (T) and the proposed looser 27C/4 bound.
For any D>=0, the function D^(2/3)/(D+1) attains its maximum at D=2,
equal to 2^(2/3)/3. Therefore

 e^2 <=(27C/8)^(2/3) D^(2/3)b^(2/3)
      <=(3/4)(2C)^(2/3)(D+1)b^(2/3).

For C=4 this is exactly e^2<=3(D+1)b^(2/3), stronger than the requested
coefficient five. Also e<=3*2^(-1/3)(Db)^(1/3)<=3(Db)^(1/3).
These constants are independent of d. Dependence on d through the value
of D itself, such as D=log d for pure rho, is explicit rather than hidden.

6. ENDPOINTS AND RANK-DEFICIENT STATES
If b=0, (A) and T->infinity imply e=0. If D=0, rho=I/d and unitality gives
e=b=0; alternatively (A) with T->0 gives e=0. d=1 is also trivial. The
optimizing expression is used only when b,D>0; no division by zero occurs.

For general rho use rho_eta=(1-eta)rho+eta I/d, 0<eta<1. It is faithful.
Because Phi and Psi fix I/d,
 b_eta=(1-eta)b, e_eta=(1-eta)e.
Apply the faithful theorem and pass eta->0. D(rho_eta||I/d)->D(rho||I/d)
by continuity of x log x on [0,1], including x=0. Hence (T) and all its
consequences hold for every density. There is no minimum eigenvalue premise
and no dimension-dependent regularization constant. Psi is not changed.

7. DESIGNED SYMBOLIC CONTROL AND CLAIM BOUNDARY
For d=2, take Phi=Psi=conjugation by the Pauli X matrix, C=1, and
rho_x=diag((1+x)/2,(1-x)/2), 0<=x<=1. Then e=b=x and
D(x)=[(1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x)]/2>=x^2/2 (differentiate twice).
The theorem holds, including x=1 rank deficiency and the negative Phi

uniformity. This checks the Kraus normalization and rules out casually
replacing its factor two by one under the supplied hypotheses. It does
not establish optimality of the final cubic constant.
