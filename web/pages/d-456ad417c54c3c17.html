## 1. Exact question and output

Acquire an increasing sequence of finite sets X_J of distinct rational exact-unit points on S², without moving any point after its insertion, such that every equal-weight rule on X_J has

    E_s(X_J)<=C_s^nest |X_J|^-s/2

simultaneously for every fixed real 1<s<2. The constants may depend on s. The output is only at the explicit refinement cardinalities below, not at every integer N; the members are not exact polynomial designs. Given J, charge deterministic polynomial bit time in |X_J| and O(log |X_J|) coordinate bits. Increasing refinement should reuse the previous function evaluations.

The input acquisition theorem is the corrected-initializer Euclidean theorem just independently gated in this cycle, or the earlier accepted LP theorem. Either returns degree-T nodes of count a(T+1)², a=720000, mean <=beta_T and Gram >=13/19, in polynomial bit time with O(log(T+1)) coordinate height. No arbitrary-accuracy extension is needed for this subcritical corollary.

The cycle11 root and independent QMC gates supply the following exact consequence of that mean/Gram interface, in the spectral Sobolev norm with weights [1+l(l+1)]^s:

    E_s(B_T)<=C_s T^-s+sqrt(3/2) P_s beta_T,
    P_s²<=4+3/[2(s-1)], beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].      (N1)

Here P_s denotes the explicit point-evaluation constant and C_s the published positive-cubature error constant. Formula (N1) was proved using the exact normalized positive auxiliary weights H=S-bb^t and a discrete covariance estimate; the final rule has equal weights. The auxiliary weights are not inserted into the new sets or into a physical equal-charge interface. Its assumptions require no separation or nesting.

For 1<s<2, beta_T<=T^-s/2048. Thus every acquired block satisfies

    E_s(B_T)<=[C_s+sqrt(3/2)P_s/2048]T^-s,
    P_s²<=4+3/[2(s-1)].                              (N2)

This holds for all these fixed s on the same degree family, with no change to the acquisition accuracy schedule.

## 2. A finite rational rotation search with exact disjointness

Let B have m>=1 distinct rational unit points and F have n>=0 distinct rational unit points already retained. A rotation preserves area probability measure and the spectral Sobolev norm, so E_s(RB)=E_s(B). We now select R in SO(3,Q) deterministically so RB and F are disjoint, and charge that selection.

First choose a rational unit axis. For u_j=j/(2m+1), j=0,...,2m, define

    a_j=((1-u_j²)/(1+u_j²), 2u_j/(1+u_j²), 0).

These are 2m+1 distinct rational unit vectors, each of height O(log(m+1)). Each new point excludes at most two axis directions, its own direction and its antipode. Consequently at least one candidate a is not parallel to any point of B. Scan and check this exactly with cross products. This takes at most 2m+1 trials and O(m²) elementary rational comparisons.

For a chosen axis a and tau in [0,1), define the exact Rodrigues rotation

    c=(1-tau²)/(1+tau²), s=2tau/(1+tau²),
    R_tau x=c x+s(a cross x)+(1-c)(a dot x)a.         (N3)

All entries are rational. The axis has eigenvalue one and the transverse plane has rotation coefficients c,s with c²+s²=1; hence R_tau^tR_tau=I and determinant one. Because no point of B lies on the axis and the angle 2atan tau is injective on [0,1), for each pair x in B, y in F the equality R_tau x=y holds for at most one tau in this interval. Thus at most mn parameter values are forbidden. Try

    tau_k=k/(mn+1), k=0,...,mn.

One of these mn+1 distinct candidates avoids all pairs. Compute the moved block and compare its rational coordinates with F exactly. Its internal distinctness and exact unit norm follow from invertibility and orthogonality. It is not necessary to approximate a Haar-generic real rotation, compare irrational roots, or assume positive separation from old points.

The axis and rotation coefficients have O(log(m+n+1)) height. Each moved coordinate is a sum of a fixed number of products of an input coordinate and these coefficients, so its height is O(B+log(m+n+1)) if the block coordinates have B bits. With naive equality scanning, at most mn+1 rotations and mn comparisons per rotation cost O((mn+1)^2), plus their node evaluation costs. This is polynomial in m+n and the input bit heights. Any use of an exact set/dictionary in the finite implementation is convenience, not an uncharged expected-time primitive in this worst-case bound.

The new block is rotated exactly once. Previously inserted points are untouched, so their coordinate heights do not accumulate across refinements.

## 3. Explicit family, spectral bound and all costs

For j=1,2,... acquire the degree T_j=2^j-1 block B_j. Its count is exactly

    m_j=a4^j.

After acquiring B_j, apply Section2 against the cumulative preceding set to obtain a disjoint rotated block A_j. Set X_J=union_j=1^J A_j. Then

    N_J=|X_J|=a sum_j=1^J 4^j
              =960000(4^J-1),
    X_J subset X_(J+1), N_(J+1)/N_J ->4.             (N4)

By T_j>=2^(j-1), (N2) and rotation invariance give

    E_s(A_j)<=D_s m_j^-s/2,
    D_s=2^s a^(s/2)[C_s+sqrt(3/2)P_s/2048].

For a normalized Sobolev test, the equal-weight union error is exactly the cardinality-weighted sum of its block errors. Put alpha=1-s/2>0. Therefore

    E_s(X_J)<=D_s/N_J sum_j=1^J m_j^alpha
       <=D_s m_J^alpha/[N_J(1-4^-alpha)]
       <=D_s/[1-4^-(1-s/2)] N_J^-s/2.              (N5)

The same sets satisfy this estimate for every fixed 1<s<2. The constant deteriorates as s approaches2; no uniform endpoint estimate is claimed. The exact low-frequency moments of one block need not vanish for this argument.

Acquisition of each B_j has polynomial bit time in T_j and output scale m_j. The largest block is O(N_J), and there are J=O(log N_J) blocks. Summing any fixed polynomial cost over this geometric progression is polynomial in N_J; even a looser O(log N_J) times the largest polynomial cost suffices. The rotations are polynomial by Section2. Each fresh block has O(log m_j) coordinate bits and receives one rotation of height O(log N_j), so every point in X_J has O(log N_J) bits. Scanning old points uses their already bounded heights. Only a finite prefix is output in a finite computation; no infinite construction is charged as one run.

To refine a cubature computation for a fixed integrand f, evaluate f only on A_(J+1). The update is exactly

    Q_(J+1)f=(N_J/N_(J+1))Q_J f
                   +(1/N_(J+1))sum_(x in A_(J+1)) f(x).

All old evaluations are reused. Equal weights at a level remain exactly 1/N_J. The node-generation theorem charges exact rational geometry and arithmetic; the integrand-evaluation cost is external to it and is explicitly the count of newly added evaluations. This is a concrete multilevel integration interface, not merely a proposed use.

## 4. Decisive limits, prior and finite evidence

The proof stops strictly below s=2. At s=2 the same triangle estimate is O(J/N_J)=O(log N_J/N_J); above2 it gives only O(1/N_J). This is a limitation of the proved block bound, not a universal impossibility theorem for nested QMC. A fixed early block can retain a nonzero low-degree discrepancy whose normalization decreases only as 1/N_J unless later blocks compensate it. Increasing the precision of later almost-design blocks does not itself perform that compensation.

The recent Wu–Zhuang Theorem3.1 does establish an existence completion using low-frequency cancellation and equal-weight cubature for a comparable density. Its cited selection does not furnish a finite-bit implementation in the inspected proof. To acquire analogous rational completions at or above the critical index, the missing interface is an acquired comparable-density equal-weight rule with uniform bit/error control and old-node retention. That is not implied by (N1) or by unequal positive-weight repair. It remains a separate open construction here.
