# Exact resolvent row lower for the complement-LCU incidence enforcer

**Status.** The exact incidence formula and the row lower below are internally proved for an explicit BMVZ-admissible, hardness-preserving augmentation. The lower applies to every real orthogonal PREPARE completion in the specified $K_0=I-H$ incidence construction, and is not a $C_0=I-VV^*$ argument. It rules out a polynomial row-list oracle for this incidence enforcer on that family. A Neumann polynomial gives a polynomial-circuit norm approximation but no row-list bound. A block-expander construction shows that the seed-projector row lower is not robust to operator-norm approximation by itself; whether the full BMVZ incidence enforcer has a polynomial-row approximation or a different sparse exact enforcer remains **UNKNOWN**.

## Interface and legal family

Take a QMA-hard equitable 5-local starting family $G_{0,n}$, normalized as an expectation of projections and decomposed into $O(1)$ commuting layers of weights bounded below by a constant. Append five fresh qubits and the BJSW Clifford-Hamiltonian projection

\[
p_\phi=|\phi_5\rangle\langle\phi_5|,\qquad
|\phi_5\rangle=(|00010\rangle-|10010\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\]
as one new singleton layer, and assign weight $1/2$ to this layer and weight $1/2$ to the old family. The resulting starting Hamiltonian is

\[
G_{+,n}=\tfrac12 G_{0,n}\otimes I+\tfrac12 p_\phi.
\]
This preserves the low-versus-inverse-polynomial-high promise: in a YES case use an old low-energy witness tensored with any vector in \(\phi^\perp\); in a NO case positivity gives \(\lambda_{\min}(G_{+,n})\ge\lambda_{\min}$G_{0,n}$/2\). The number of layers remains constant and their minimum weight stays constant. Thus BMVZ's equitable-layer hypotheses still apply. This is an explicit legal augmentation of their input class; it is **not** a claim that the unmodified ABN-to-BMVZ canonical reduction output already contains this seed.

BMVZ's small-layer rule trivially repeats a singleton clause. Iterating it therefore sends the seed layer exactly to

\[
P_R=I-Q_R,\qquad Q_R=(I-p_\phi)^{\otimes R},
\]
where $R=(2t)^\ell$ is the number of copies after the chosen amplification rounds. All generated seed clauses are identical and can be coalesced into one label. Let $G_n$ be the normalized BMVZ output from the old layers alone, so (0\le G_n\le I\). The full amplified output has the exact tensor form

\[
H_{\rm out}=\tfrac12G_n\otimes I+\tfrac12I\otimes P_R.
\]
For the complement-LCU construction add a silent zero projector of weight $1/4$, scaling the active output by $3/4$. Thus

\[
H=\tfrac38G_n\otimes I+\tfrac38I\otimes P_R,\qquad
Y=I-H\ge\tfrac14I.
\tag{1}
\]
The seed term has weight \(\pi_\phi=3/8\). The identity-reflection LCU label has amplitude \(\chi_{\rm id}=1/\sqrt2\), and \(\chi_\phi=\sqrt{\pi_\phi/2}=\sqrt3/4\). With any real orthogonal PREPARE matrix (A) whose first column is the LCU vector \(\chi\), the physical branch operators are

\[
K_a=\sum_j A_{ja}\chi_jR_j,\qquad K_0=Y,
\quad R_\phi=I-2P_R.
\tag{2}
\]
The number (m) of output labels and the BMVZ term-generator description are polynomial in the original input length.

## Resolvent separates the seed layer exactly

Write $G=G_n$, $P=P_R$, $Q=Q_R=I-P$, and set

\[
g_0(z)=(1-3z/8)^{-1},\qquad
g_1(z)=(1-3z/8-3/8)^{-1}=(5/8-3z/8)^{-1}.
\]
Since $G$ and $P$ act on disjoint registers, they commute. Functional calculus applied to (1) gives the exact identity

\[
Y^{-1}=g_0(G)\otimes Q+g_1(G)\otimes P.
\tag{3}
\]
Both functions are positive definite on $[0,1]$: $g_0\ge1$ and $g_1\ge 8/5$, with \(\|g_1(G)\|\le4\). This is a layer-conditional resolvent formula; it removes all mixing between the two seed energy sectors but leaves a nontrivial resolvent on the hard register.

For every computational basis string (b) of the hard register,

\[
\langle b|g_i(G)|b\rangle>0\quad(i=0,1),
\tag{4}
\]
because each $g_i(G)$ is positive definite. Let $u=00010$, $v=10010$. On their two-dimensional span, $I-p_\phi$ has every entry in row $u$ equal to $1/2$. Therefore, for $x=u^{\otimes R}$ and every $y\in\{u,v\}^{\otimes R}$,

\[
\langle x|Q_R|y\rangle=2^{-R};
\quad
\langle x|P_R|y\rangle=
\begin{cases}1-2^{-R},&y=x,\\-2^{-R},&y\ne x.\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
There are exactly $2^R$ nonzero entries in this row of $P_R$. An exact rational-arithmetic check at $R=2$ gives the row \((3/4,-1/4,-1/4,-1/4)\) on columns $uu,uv,vu,vv$; the general support count is the product calculation in (5), not an extrapolation from that check.

## Every PREPARE completion has a dense incidence row

For $a>0$, define the incidence block $D_a=K_aY^{-1}$, with $D_0=K_0Y^{-1}=I$. Orthogonality of the columns of (A) gives the exact branch-recovery identity

\[
\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}A_{\phi a}K_a=\chi_\phi R_\phi,
\qquad
\sum_{a>0}A_{\phi a}D_a
=\chi_\phi(R_\phi Y^{-1}-I).
\tag{6}
\]
Take the row labelled by $(b,x)$ and, for each $y\in\{u,v\}^{\otimes R}\setminus\{x\}$, the column $(b,y)$. Using (3)–(5), the corresponding entry of the right-hand side of (6) is

\[
\chi_\phi\,2^{-R}
\big(\langle b|g_0(G)|b\rangle+\langle b|g_1(G)|b\rangle\big)>0.
\tag{7}
\]
The identity in (6) contributes nothing because (y\ne x). Thus the union of the supports of these $m-1$ rows of $D_a$ contains at least (2^R-1) distinct columns. At least one branch $a>0$ consequently has

\[
\operatorname{supp}\big(D_a[(b,x),\cdot]\big)
\;\ge\;
\left\lceil\frac{2^R-1}{m-1}\right\rceil.
\tag{8}
\]
This proof holds for **every** real orthogonal completion (A) with the prescribed first column; it does not assume that PREPARE isolates the seed term.

The bound transfers to the actual positive graph-incidence enforcer. Define

\[
Tz=(z_a-D_az_0)_{a>0},\qquad C_{\rm inc}=T^*T/16.
\tag{9}
\]
Its $(a,0)$ block is exactly \(-D_a/16\). Hence (8) is a row lower for $C_{\rm inc}$, not just for a canonical projector. For a particular legal completion, choose an extra column supported only on the id and \(\phi\) term coordinates:

\[
A_{{\rm id},a}=\frac{\chi_\phi}{\sqrt{\chi_{\rm id}^2+\chi_\phi^2}},\qquad
A_{\phi,a}=-\frac{\chi_{\rm id}}{\sqrt{\chi_{\rm id}^2+\chi_\phi^2}}.
\]
Then (K_a=2\sqrt{3/22}\,P_R\), so (3) gives (D_a=2\sqrt{3/22}\,g_1(G)\otimes P_R). Its row $(b,x)$, restricted to columns $(b,y)$, has all $2^R$ entries from (5) nonzero. This sharper witness is optional; the all-completions bound (8) is the robust statement.

For completeness, the enforcer has the advertised exact spectrum control. Since $V$ is an isometry and $K_0=Y$,

\[
\sum_{a>0}D_a^*D_a=Y^{-2}-I\le15I.
\]
Thus $TT^*=I+DD^*$ lies between $I$ and $16I$, 
\(\ker T=\operatorname{ran}V\), and

\[
0\le C_{\rm inc}\le I,
\qquad C_{\rm inc}\ge\tfrac1{16}(I-VV^*).
\tag{10}
\]
The lower bound is therefore a row-access obstruction for an exact, positive, constant-gap enforcer, not a failure of its algebraic kernel or gap.

If $R=n^{\Omega(1)}$ and $m=\operatorname{poly}(n)$, as in the polynomial-copy BMVZ amplification regime, (8) is superpolynomial. A complete row-list oracle must output at least that many columns and cannot run in polynomial time. This obstruction is specific to (9), the fixed computational data basis, and the explicitly augmented BMVZ family; it is not a lower bound for every positive matrix with kernel \(\operatorname{ran}V\).

**Architecture consequence.** The pipeline “BMVZ term generator → complement-LCU isometry → exact graph-incidence image penalty → sparse-Hamiltonian row oracle” fails at the final access interface on this legal family, despite polynomial term generation, a polynomial circuit for \(V\), a one-sparse diagonal \(B\), and the exact constant gap (10). Term-sampling verification and circuit access to \(V\) are not ruled out; the obstruction is specifically to claiming a sparse-matrix verifier from this incidence conversion.

## What the resolvent algorithms do and do not provide

The Neumann series is valid because \(\|H\|\le3/4\):

\[
Y^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}H^k,\qquad
\left\|Y^{-1}-\sum_{k=0}^{L}H^k\right\|
\le4(3/4)^{L+1}.
\tag{11}
\]
It is not a finite exact formula in general. Taking $L=O(\log(1/\eta))$ yields a circuit/block-encoding approximation to the inverse, and substituting it into (9) gives a positive Gram operator $C_L=T_L^*T_L/16$ with

\[
\|C_L-C_{\rm inc}\|
\le\frac{(8+\sigma_L)\sigma_L}{16},
\qquad \sigma_L=4(3/4)^{L+1}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is enough for a norm-robust promise-gap transfer after charging the perturbation, as detailed in `10_approximate_consequence.md`. It generally loses exact annihilation of (V).

Neither (11) nor a commutator/layer identity gives a polynomial row list for $C_L$. A BMVZ term entry is easy to evaluate: for a specified pair it is a product of $R$ constant-size local entries plus a Kronecker delta, and summing over the $M=\operatorname{poly}(n)$ generated terms costs $O(MR)$ exact local operations. But a worst-case row of a 5-local-factor OR term can have $2^{5R}$ entries. Naively expanding a degree-$L$ power has a row-count upper bound of order \((M2^{5R})^L\); this is only a crude upper bound, not a lower bound. Polynomial circuit/block-encoding cost and polynomial-time specified-entry evaluation do not supply a complete row list. Conversely, (8) proves that no rearrangement of the exact inverse computation can make the exact incidence matrix row-sparse: the matrix itself has too many nonzeros.

## Operator-norm approximation: a seed escape, but not a full enforcer

The exact lower (8) is not robust to operator-norm approximation by itself. There is a concrete sparse positive approximation to the isolated seed projector (P_R) that preserves its kernel and constant gap.

Partition the computational basis of the $R$ five-qubit seed blocks into invariant blocks as follows. At a position, either the five-bit string lies in $S=\{u,v\}$, giving a free binary coordinate, or it is one of the 30 strings outside (S), in which case its value is fixed. On a block with $k$ free binary coordinates, $Q_R=(I-p_\phi)^{\otimes R}$ is the rank-one uniform projector on $2^k$ vertices; hence $P_R$ on that block is $I-|\mathbf1_k\rangle\langle\mathbf1_k|$, with \(|\mathbf1_k\rangle\) normalized uniform. For $k=0$, $P_R=0$.

Choose a uniform explicit $d$-regular graph family $G_k$ on $2^k$ vertices whose normalized adjacency $A_k/d$ has uniform eigenvector and all other eigenvalues of magnitude at most \(\rho<1\). On each $k>0$ block replace $P_R$ by the normalized graph Laplacian $L_k=I-A_k/d$, and put zero on $k=0$ blocks. The direct sum \(\widetilde P_R\) satisfies

\[
\widetilde P_R\ge0,\quad
\ker\widetilde P_R=\ker P_R,\quad
\|\widetilde P_R-P_R\|\le\rho,
\]
with row degree at most $d+1$. A row oracle reads the outside/free mask in $O(R)$ time and applies the explicit expander neighbor map to its $k$-bit vertex name. For constant error, $d$ is constant; for target error $\rho=1/\operatorname{poly}(n)$, graph powering gives degree polynomial in $(1/\rho)$ and a polynomial-time neighbor oracle. Thus even a positive same-kernel constant-gap approximation can erase the $2^R$ seed row while staying sparse.

This construction only sparsifies the seed factor. In the selected incidence block the full operator is (g_1(G)\otimes P_R); replacing (P_R) by \(\widetilde P_R) leaves the hard-register resolvent (g_1(G)) untouched, and other BMVZ term branches also remain. It is therefore **not** a sparse approximation to the full $C_{\rm inc}$, and it does not defeat the all-completions exact result. It does show why (8) alone cannot be upgraded to a norm-approximate-enforcer lower bound. The general question whether the complete BMVZ incidence penalty, or another exact/approximate image enforcer, admits a uniform polynomial row oracle is **UNKNOWN**.

The seed expander also explains the distinction from positive local syndrome-budget bounds. $P_R=I-\bigotimes_r(I-p_{\phi,r})$ is a global OR projector; it is not a normalized sum of positive tests each supported on fewer than $R$ copies. A local average such as $R^{-1}\sum_r p_{\phi,r}$ has the same kernel but gap $1/R$, while the graph construction is globally generated and is not a bounded-support positive-term decomposition. Neither object falls under the positive local budget theorem's full hypotheses.

## Access and cost ledger

* **Term generation / exact membership.** The added seed is an explicit singleton color. BMVZ's stated small-layer convention repeats it; the all-equal output clause is exactly (P_R). The augmentation preserves QMA hardness and the equitable-layer conditions, but membership in the particular unaugmented published reduction output is not asserted.
* **Term entries and circuits.** A specified $P_R$ entry uses the product formula \(\delta_{xy}-\prod_r(I-p_\phi)_{x_r,y_r}\), $O(R)$ rational operations, and $O(R)$ bits for the seed-only dyadic factors. A specified entry of $H$ or $K_a$ is a sum over polynomially many generated terms, costing polynomially many such local operations plus PREPARE coefficient precision. SELECT implements each $R_j=2Q_j-I$ by the term-generator's $R$ constant-size factors and a reversible AND; V and the diagonal one-sparse $B=I-|0\rangle\langle0|$ have polynomial circuit descriptions.
* **Exact inverse and rows.** The exact inverse matrix element has no established polynomial-time exact evaluator; the hard-register functions $g_i(G)$ are resolvents. No such evaluator is needed for the support proof, which uses only their strict positivity. The row-list lower for the exact incidence matrix is \(\lceil(2^R-1)/(m-1)\rceil\) for every PREPARE completion, and $2^R$ for the displayed special completion. This is an output-length lower bound even if a chosen entry were cheaply computable.
  A generic dense exact inversion on dimension $D=2^N$ takes $D^{O(1)}$ arithmetic operations; for rational input entries of bit length at most $B$, the adjugate gives the crude output-bit upper bound $O(D(B+\log D))$. These are generic upper bounds, not lower bounds for this structured instance.
* **Approximation.** Neumann degree $L=O(\log(1/\eta))$ yields the operator error (12) and polynomial circuit cost in (M,R,L,\log(1/\eta)), but no row-list algorithm is known. The seed block-expander oracle has row cost (O(R+d)); it does not include the $g_i(G)$ factor or the other branches.
  With a polynomial-cost block encoding of $H$, polynomially many gates in $M,R,L$ suffice to apply the truncation, and coefficient precision $O(\log(1/\eta))$ bits controls the requested norm accuracy. A naive classical entry expansion has row-work upper bound $(M2^{5R})^{O(L)}$; this is only an upper bound. The seed block-expander oracle uses exact rational edge weights of $O(\log d)$ bits and row cost $O(R+d)$.
