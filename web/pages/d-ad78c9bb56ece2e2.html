# N02 addendum v3 — equal-growth roots obstruct generic seed universality

This append-only continuation preserves v1 and v2. It supplies an explicit five-beam counterexample in which the same ELN has two distinct, equally fast homogeneous instabilities. Each root has an exact one-soliton pulse, but the peak conversion envelopes differ. A generic small seed excites both modes; their relative phase rotates on the logarithmic onset time, so even the shifted total-conversion observable has no single seed-universal limit. This establishes a precise boundary on the v1/v2 seed-matching statement.

## Exact five-beam spectrum with a degenerate fastest pair

Choose five distinct nonzero velocities
\[
 v_i=(-1,-1/2,1/4,3/5,1)
\]
and the quartic
\[
 P(u)=\bigl((u+3/10)^2+1/25\bigr)
       \bigl((u-2/5)^2+1/25\bigr),\qquad
 Q(u)=\prod_{i=1}^5(u-v_i).
\]
Set \(G_i=P(v_i)/(v_iQ'(v_i))\), so the partial-fraction identity gives
\[
 \ell(u)=\sum_i\frac{v_iG_i}{u-v_i}=\frac{P(u)}{Q(u)}.
\]
The beam ELN weights and their first moments are exactly
\[
 (G_i)=\left(-\frac{53}{100},\frac{544}{2475},
 \frac{548}{1575},-\frac{425}{924},\frac{173}{225}\right),
 \quad G_0=\frac{26}{75},\quad G_1=1,\quad G_2=\frac3{20}.
\]
There are three angular crossings. The nonreal Lax zeros are
\[
 u_1=-3/10+i/5,\quad u_1^*,\quad
 u_2=2/5+i/5,\quad u_2^*.
\]
Both growing modes have \(\Gamma=|G_1\operatorname{Im}u_j|=1/5\), but their pendulum spin frequencies are different:
\[
 \omega_1=-G_1\operatorname{Re}u_1=3/10,
 \qquad \omega_2=-G_1\operatorname{Re}u_2=-2/5.
\]
For \(q_i=D_i^x+iD_i^y\) in the frame corotating with \(D_0^z\), their exponents are \(s_1=1/5+3i/10\) and \(s_2=1/5-2i/5\). The full-frame exponents add the same \(iG_0\); their frequency difference remains \(\Delta\nu=7/10\). The eigenvectors may be taken as
\[
 q_i^{(j)}=\frac{v_iG_i}{v_i-u_j}.
\]
Direct substitution in the linearized equation verifies \(\sum_iq_i^{(j)}=0\), \(\sum_i v_iq_i^{(j)}=G_1\), and \(\dot q^{(j)}=s_jq^{(j)}\). These roots are simple and have identical maximal real parts.

## Different exact pulse maxima from the same ELN

For each root, the v2 Lax-root formula yields a valid exact homogeneous temporal soliton in the same five-beam EOM. Its first-pulse angular fractions are
\[
 \chi_j(v)=\frac{b^2v^2}{|u_j|^2[(v-\operatorname{Re}u_j)^2+b^2]},
 \qquad b=1/5.
\]
The total peak charge conversions, using the same initial \(G_i\), are exactly
\[
 C_{*,1}=\sum_i|G_i|\chi_1(v_i)=\frac{736}{1001}
       \simeq0.7352647353,
 \qquad
 C_{*,2}=\sum_i|G_i|\chi_2(v_i)=\frac{8989}{9625}
       \simeq0.9339220779.
\]
The profiles are different as well. Explicitly,
\[
 (\chi_1(v_i))=\left(\frac{400}{689},\frac{25}{26},
 \frac{100}{1781},\frac{144}{1105},\frac{400}{2249}\right),
 \qquad
 (\chi_2(v_i))=\left(\frac1{10},\frac1{17},\frac15,\frac9{10},\frac12\right).
\]
Thus the ELN plus the largest linear growth rate does not select one nonlinear first-pulse conversion envelope when the fastest Lax root is degenerate. A seed aligned with one unstable eigendirection selects that root's pulse in the \(\epsilon\to0\) first-swing limit; seeds aligned with the other select the other pulse. Their maxima remain distinct. The global charge budget is \(C\le2A_m\), where \(A_-=5717/5775\) is the minority ELN charge and \(A_+=2573/1925\); both pulses lie strictly below this bound.

## Generic mixed seeds: no logarithmically shifted limit

Take an initial seed with both growing projections nonzero,
\[
 q(0)=\epsilon(\alpha_1q^{(1)}+\alpha_2q^{(2)})+O(\epsilon^2),
 \qquad \alpha_1\alpha_2\ne0.
\]
With \(t_\epsilon=\Gamma^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)=5\log(1/\epsilon)\), the linear solution at fixed shifted time \(t=t_\epsilon+\tau\) is
\[
 q_i(t)=e^{\Gamma\tau}\left[
 \alpha_1e^{i\nu_1(t_\epsilon+\tau)}q_i^{(1)}+
 \alpha_2e^{i\nu_2(t_\epsilon+\tau)}q_i^{(2)}
 \right]+o(1),
\]
where a common frequency shift between lab and corotating frames cancels in the relative phase. For \(\tau\) fixed and sufficiently negative, this is a controlled small-amplitude expansion on the unstable manifold. With fixed beam lengths,
\[
 C(t)=\frac14\sum_i\frac{|q_i(t)|^2}{|G_i|}+O(\|q(t)\|^4).
\]
The cross coefficient is nonzero. In the displayed (unnormalized) eigenvector convention, exact rational arithmetic gives
\[
 \sum_i\frac{q_i^{(1)}\overline{q_i^{(2)}}}{|G_i|}
 =\frac{366}{175}-\frac{226}{275}i\ne0.
\]
Therefore \(C(t_\epsilon+\tau)\) contains a nonzero term proportional to
\[
 e^{2\Gamma\tau}\operatorname{Re}\!\left[
 \alpha_1\overline{\alpha_2}
 \left(\frac{366}{175}-\frac{226}{275}i\right)
 e^{i(\nu_1-\nu_2)(t_\epsilon+\tau)}\right].
\]
Its onset phase is \((\nu_1-\nu_2)t_\epsilon=(7/10)5\log(1/\epsilon)=\tfrac72\log(1/\epsilon)\), which has no limit modulo \(2\pi\). Choosing two sequences \(\epsilon_n\downarrow0\) with this phase converging to different values gives distinct subsequential limits. Since the remainder is order \(e^{4\Gamma\tau}\) for sufficiently negative fixed \(\tau\), the phase-dependent quadratic term cannot be canceled by nonlinear corrections. A global flavor azimuth multiplies both modes by the same phase and does not remove this relative phase. A time translation also leaves \(|\alpha_2/\alpha_1|\) invariant because the two growth rates agree. Hence for generic mixed seeds there is no single first-onset conversion curve selected by \(G\) alone, even after the common logarithmic delay and global azimuth are removed.

This is a precise counterexample to unconditional seed-universal saturation. It does not contradict the one-root theorem: v1's seed-universal pulse applies after imposing a unique isolated fastest eigenvalue. With degenerate fastest roots, the correct nonlinear data include at least their complex seed projections; the Lax curve identifies possible one-root pulses but does not choose the mixture.

## Consequence and remaining boundary

The sharp conditional statement supported by v1–v3 is:

- If the collisionless homogeneous linearized operator has one isolated simple fastest unstable root, a generic seed with nonzero projection onto it has a seed-independent first-pulse shape after removing the log delay, azimuth, and fixed normalization. The first pulse can be calculated from the root and full ELN distribution; it does not generically saturate the absolute lepton-charge budget for a nonatomic angular spectrum.
- If the maximal growth rate is degenerate, the full ELN and growth rate do not select one pulse. The explicit five-beam construction shows distinct exact pulse heights for one ELN, and generic mixed seeds have no logarithmically shifted conversion limit due to a surviving relative phase.

Both claims are collisionless homogeneous mean-field statements. Inhomogeneous propagation loses the homogeneous Lax curve; collisions break the beam-length/Lax invariants unless specially constrained. No universal statement is made for realistic supernova/merger evolution with multiple waves, transport, collisions, or late-time mode competition.
