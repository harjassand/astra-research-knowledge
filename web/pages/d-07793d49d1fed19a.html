# Cycle 2: photon-count geometry removes the quadratic-channel inference

## Verdict

The deterministic raw-intensity row bound is real, but it is not a photon-count lower bound. It applies to the unnormalised Euclidean error in the *squared* outputs. A one-photon instrument produces categorical outcomes (or Poisson counts after flux randomisation), whose exact statistical distance is Hellinger distance between probabilities. Its square-root coordinates are precisely the amplitude phase-retrieval map. A Parseval random frame therefore gives a concrete counterexample to any claim that near-best physical phase discrimination requires (d^2) channels: with (m=O(d)) outcomes it has a uniform Hellinger lower modulus and a global estimator with squared phase-error risk (O(d/N)) from (N) incident photons. The (d/N) rate matches the Haar-average lower bound for *all* measurements, including collective quantum measurements, up to constants.

This is a conditional theorem about a calibrated, lossless pure-state instrument. The best existing ingredients already appear in primary literature: Xia–Xu–Xu (2024) prove the random complex-Gaussian magnitude stability, and Bruß–Macchiavello (1999) prove the optimal pure-state fidelity bound. The whitening-to-POVM and count-risk composition below is internally derived and not priority-cleared. The estimator is a global nonconvex minimum-distance fit; the explicit finite-net implementation is exponential in (d). No apparatus, specimen, or biological measurement was run.

## Interface and the raw-intensity obstruction

Let (d\ge2), let (A\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}) have nonzero rows (a_j^*\), and define the source-style intensity map and quotient metric
\[
 \Psi_A(x)=(|a_j^*x|^2)_{j=1}^m,\qquad
 d_*(x,y)=\|xx^*-yy^*\|_*.
\]
The output metric is the ordinary, unnormalised \(\ell_2\) norm on raw squared intensities. Write (L_A,U_A\) for its lower and upper constants and \(\beta_A=U_A/L_A\). This model contains no count distribution, photon dose, per-channel variance, calibration error, or detector background.

There is an elementary quantitative row-count consequence of the lower-constant formula in Peng–Han–Huang (2026). Set (m_+=\#\{j:a_j\ne0\}\) and (S=\sum_j\|a_j\|^4\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad \beta_A^2\ge\max\!\left\{4,\frac{2d(d+1)}{m_+}\right\}. \quad} \tag{1}
\]
For the second term, (U_A^2=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\sum_j|a_j^*x|^4\ge\max_j\|a_j\|^4\ge S/m_+\), by taking (x) along a largest row. The paper's formula expresses (L_A^2) as the infimum of
\[
 \sum_j\{\operatorname{Re}(u^*a_ja_j^*v)\}^2
\]
over unit pairs with \(\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb R\). Restrict to a Haar-random ordered orthonormal complex pair. For each fixed (a),
\[
 \mathbb E\{\operatorname{Re}(u^*aa^*v)\}^2
 =\frac{\|a\|^4}{2d(d+1)}.
\]
Indeed, for \(w=a/\|a\|\), conditional isotropy of \(v\in u^\perp\) gives half of \(|u^*w|^2(1-|u^*w|^2)/(d-1)\); \(|u^*w|^2\) has Beta\((1,d-1)\) moments (1/d\) and (2/[d(d+1)]\). Thus (L_A^2\le S/[2d(d+1)]\) and (1) follows. The term 4 is the source's universal complex lower bound. Consequently a constant \(\beta_A\) under this exact raw-output convention forces \(m_+=\Omega(d^2)\); exact \(\beta_A=2\) in fact forces a weighted projective 2-design and at least (d^2) rows, as in Cycle 1.

This is a deterministic distortion statement for adversarial additive \(\ell_2\) errors in squared intensities. It says nothing by itself about statistical efficiency under shot noise. Scaling every row changes (L,U) but leaves \(\beta\) unchanged, while photon numbers and variances change. Therefore (1) cannot be read as a lower bound on photons or on physical detector count without a noise model.

## A linear-outcome one-photon instrument

Let (A\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}\) have independent standard circular complex-Gaussian rows with \(\mathbb E aa^*=I\), and put
\[
 G=A/\sqrt m,\qquad S=G^*G=A^*A/m,\qquad B=G S^{-1/2}=A(A^*A)^{-1/2}.
\]
For a fixed \(0<\zeta<0.05\), Xia–Xu–Xu's Theorem 4.3 gives
\[
 L_G\ge \frac1{\beta_0}-\zeta,
 \qquad \beta_0=\sqrt{\frac4{4-\pi}}\simeq2.159,
\]
with probability at least \(1-4e^{-c_2\zeta^2m}\), provided (m\ge C\zeta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)d\). Their Lemma 4.2 also gives \(\lambda_{\max}(S)\le1+\zeta\) except with probability at most (2e^{-c_1\zeta^2m}) under the same order of sample size. On the intersection event, (B^*B=I), and
\[
 \||Bx|-|By|\|_2
 =\||G S^{-1/2}x|-|G S^{-1/2}y|\|_2
 \ge \ell_\zeta d_\phi(x,y),\qquad
 \ell_\zeta=\frac{1/\beta_0-\zeta}{\sqrt{1+\zeta}},
\]
where (d_\phi(x,y)=\min_{|c|=1}\|x-cy\|_2). This follows by applying the source lower inequality to (S^{-1/2}x,S^{-1/2}y) and using \(d_\phi(S^{-1/2}x,S^{-1/2}y)\ge d_\phi(x,y)/\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(S)}\). The combined event probability is at least (1-6e^{-c\zeta^2m}) for a universal (c>0).

Because (B^*B=I), its row vectors (b_j^*\) define a complete rank-one POVM (E_j=b_jb_j^*\), (j=1,\ldots,m). For a unit pure input \(x\), the one-photon outcome law is
\[
 p_j(x)=\langle x,E_jx\rangle=|b_j^*x|^2,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^m p_j(x)=1.
\]
The isometry (x\mapsto Bx) can be extended to a mode unitary and followed by one detector per output channel. Every photon produces one of these (m) outcomes in the ideal lossless model; no setting is sampled after the fact and no outcome is discarded. This is (O(d)) channels for fixed \(\zeta\), although the theorem hides a potentially large constant.

The amplitude condition number of (B) is also near best possible. Since (B) is an isometry, 
\[
 \||Bx|-|By|\|_2\le d_\phi(x,y),\qquad U_B\le1,
 \quad\text{and hence}\quad \beta_B\le1/\ell_\zeta.
\]
As \(\zeta\downarrow0\), the right side tends to \(\beta_0\), which is a universal lower bound for every complex amplitude frame. Thus this O(d)-outcome instrument is asymptotically optimal for magnitude distortion, not for the different raw-squared \(\ell_2\) objective.

## Exact shot-noise law and estimators

For (N) independent photons measured by this single POVM, the full count vector is \(Y\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N,p(x))\), with exactly (N) incident photons and every detector channel represented. The empirical frequencies \(\hat p=Y/N\) satisfy, for every distribution on (m) channels,
\[
 \mathbb E_x H^2(\hat p,p(x))
 =\mathbb E\sum_j(\sqrt{\hat p_j}-\sqrt{p_j(x)})^2
 \le\frac{m-1}{N}. \tag{2}
\]
For each positive (p_j), use \((\sqrt{q}-\sqrt p)^2\le(q-p)^2/p\), then \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat p_j)=p_j(1-p_j)/N\); zero-probability channels contribute zero. Define the global minimum-Hellinger-distance estimator
\[
 \hat x\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\|z\|=1\;/\;\text{global phase}}H(\hat p,p(z)).
\]
It exists because projective state space is compact. The triangle inequality, optimality, and the uniform frame lower bound imply
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathbb E_x d_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
 \le \min\!\left\{2,\frac{4(m-1)}{N\ell_\zeta^2}\right\}.
 \quad} \tag{3}
\]
In detail, \(\ell_\zeta d_\phi(\hat x,x)\le H(p(\hat x),p(x))\le2H(\hat p,p(x))\), and (2) gives (3). Thus if (m\le C_\zeta d), expected squared phase error is at most (4C_\zeta d/(N\ell_\zeta^2)). Equivalently, (N=O_\zeta(d/\epsilon^2)) incident photons suffice for RMS quotient error \(\epsilon\), with explicit constants in the displayed risk bound.

This risk theorem is statistical, not a runtime claim. A direct finite algorithm is to make a \(\gamma\)-net of projective state space, evaluate \(H(\hat p,p(z))\) at each point, and return the minimizer. A sphere net gives at most \((C/\gamma)^{2d}\) candidates and \(O(md)\) arithmetic per candidate. Since \(U_B\le1\), the resulting risk bound is \(8(m-1)/(N\ell_\zeta^2)+2\gamma^2/\ell_\zeta^2\). The total search is exponential in \(d\). An efficient algorithm achieving (3) is not established here.

If the number of incident photons is Poisson with mean \(\tau\), independent thinning yields the full observed count law
\[
 Y_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\tau p_j(x))\quad\text{independently},\qquad \sum_j\mathbb E Y_j=\tau.
\]
For two candidate states (x,y), its exact Bhattacharyya coefficient is
\[
 \operatorname{BC}(P_x,P_y)
 =\exp\!\left[-\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_j(\sqrt{p_j(x)}-\sqrt{p_j(y)})^2\right]
 \le \exp[-\tau\ell_\zeta^2d_\phi(x,y)^2/2]. \tag{4}
\]
This is a direct product-Poisson calculation and remains exact when some probabilities vanish. The likelihood-ratio test between two specified candidate states has sum of type-I and type-II errors at most this coefficient, since \(\min(P,Q)\le\sqrt{PQ}\). Therefore each error is at most \(\alpha\) whenever
\[
 \tau\ge \frac{2\log(1/\alpha)}{\ell_\zeta^2\epsilon^2}
 \quad\text{for }d_\phi(x,y)\ge\epsilon.
\]
For a known finite hypothesis set of (K\) states with minimum quotient separation \(\epsilon\), maximum likelihood plus a union bound gives error at most ((K-1)e^{-\tau\ell_\zeta^2\epsilon^2/2}). All (\tau) expected photons are charged; this is not a parameter-independent estimate of the full continuous state. The separate MDE risk theorem (3) is the continuous-state result.

For a uniform known efficiency \(\eta\in(0,1]\), the observed Poisson means become \(\eta\tau p_j(x)\), so the exponent in (4) is multiplied by \(\eta\); incident photons, not just detected photons, cost \(\tau\). With fixed (N\) launches, record a no-click category of probability (1-\eta\); it is common to all states, so the Hellinger distance is multiplied by \(\sqrt\eta\), and the risk bound becomes at most (4m/(N\eta\ell_\zeta^2)). This assumes known uniform loss and a launch clock. Dark counts, unknown channel gains, mode mismatch, saturation, and state-dependent loss are outside the theorem; arbitrary nonuniform loss may destroy the lower modulus.

## Rate comparison and strongest informed baseline

For a Haar-uniform unknown pure state in \(\mathbb C^d\), Bruß–Macchiavello's optimal state-estimation theorem gives average fidelity at most
\[
 F_{\rm opt}(N)=\frac{N+1}{N+d}
\]
for any measurement/estimator on (N\) copies, including collective measurements. Since for (c=|\langle x,\hat x\rangle|\),
\[
 d_\phi(x,\hat x)^2=2(1-c)\ge 1-c^2=1-F(x,\hat x),
\]
every design has Haar-average mean squared phase error at least ((d-1)/(N+d)). Our uniform upper bound (3) is (O(d/N)) whenever (m=O(d)) and \(N\gtrsim d\); this matches the strongest quantum lower-bound scaling. It does not match the exact optimal fidelity constant.

The same comparison can charge uniform detector loss. Suppose exactly $N$ photons are launched, each is independently detected with known state-independent efficiency $\eta$, and a no-click is recorded as a state-independent erasure. Conditional on the detected count $K$, the strongest possible estimator has Haar mean fidelity at most $(K+1)/(K+d)$, even if it can make an arbitrary collective measurement on those $K$ copies. Since $f(k)=(k+1)/(k+d)$ is concave and $\mathbb E K=N\eta$,
\[
\mathbb E[1-F]\ge 1-f(N\eta)=\frac{d-1}{N\eta+d}.
\]
Pointwise $d_\phi^2\ge1-F$, so this is also a lower bound on expected squared quotient error for every instrument under the erasure model. The Parseval-frame MDE upper bound with the no-click category is $4m/(N\eta\ell_\zeta^2)$, as derived in the loss paragraph above. Thus the $O(d/(N\eta))$ order remains minimax-rate optimal up to constants for $N\eta\gtrsim d$. This does not account for unknown or mode-dependent losses, dark counts, or the energy cost of producing an incident photon.

The equal-information comparison has the same known frame, state norm, photon count, and all outcomes. The strongest hypothesis test is the exact likelihood-ratio test; no estimator that first forces the data into unweighted squared-intensity \(\ell_2\) can improve its simple-vs-simple error. The continuous estimator uses the same (N\) photon outcomes and known (B\). In contrast, exact \(\beta_{\Psi_A}^{\ell_2}=2\) in the 2026 squared-intensity metric requires a weighted projective 2-design and (m\ge d^2\). Thus exact raw-intensity optimality buys a different norm guarantee at a quadratic channel count, while shot-noise phase recovery has a linear-channel, minimax-rate-optimal construction.

Two existing Poisson phase-retrieval results are important controls, not evidence of novelty. Li–Lange–Fessler (2023) give low-count Poisson maximum-likelihood algorithms and simulated recovery; they do not prove this uniform Parseval-frame risk bound. Dirksen–Krahmer–Römer–Salanevich (2025) prove a real-Gaussian spectral recovery guarantee for independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(|\langle a_i,x\rangle|^2)\) outputs with (m\ge n\log n\), but their norm \(\|x\|^2=\alpha\) acts as per-measurement dose and their theorem is not a normalized one-photon POVM or a fixed-total-photon comparison. Their result is more algorithmically direct (top eigenvector); ours is complex, uses a normalized instrument, and the certified global estimator may be exponential-time. A decisive practical comparison still needs a polynomial-time solver with an equally explicit low-dose theorem.

## Resource and precision ledger

- **Design/acquisition:** sample/store (md) complex Gaussian coefficients and compute a (d\times d) Gram matrix and inverse square root. Dense preprocessing is (O(md^2+d^3)) arithmetic and (O(md)) storage. The good-frame theorem is probability over this design draw; checking only \(B^*B=I\) does not certify the global lower constant.
- **Instrument:** one (d\)-mode input, an isometry to (m) output modes, and (m) counters. An isometry can be embedded in an (m\times m) unitary using (O(md)) two-mode rotations, but the optical depth, insertion loss, and calibration protocol are platform-dependent and not charged by the theorem. Each ideal photon gives one registered channel label. There are no (d^2) repeated basis settings.
- **Data/exposure:** fixed-(N) risk uses exactly (N) incident photon copies; Poisson risk uses expected incident flux \(\tau\), with total expected counts exactly \(\tau\). Uniform efficiency \(\eta\) costs a factor (1/\eta\) in required incident flux. No background or discarded channels are permitted in the stated model.
- **Precision/calibration:** the theorem assumes the actual Parseval matrix and all (p_j(x)\) are known exactly. It does not give a finite-bit perturbation threshold, a calibration sample count, or robustness to unknown gains. These are material open costs. Storing an exact real Gaussian matrix is an ideal-model representation; a finite-precision optical compiler needs a new robustness lemma.
- **Estimator:** the exact global minimum-Hellinger fit exists; finite-net search costs (O(md(C/\gamma)^{2d})) arithmetic and memory to store the design/net. No polynomial-time guarantee is claimed.
- **Nature:** the model is a pure, fixed-norm state with identical independent copies (or a Poissonized coherent-state count model). It does not model mixed states, specimen evolution/damage, unknown illumination norm, detector dead time, or heterogeneity across copies.

## Finite check, falsifiers, and next gate

`poisson_frame_check.py` uses one fixed (d=4,m=24\) Gaussian draw. It checks (B^*B=I\), total outcome probability one, and the product-Poisson affinity by multiplying truncated one-coordinate series; discrepancies were (3.3\times10^{-15}), (2.9\times10^{-15}), and (1.7\times10^{-16}). A (4{,}000)-trial empirical Hellinger risk check gave (0.099963\) against the proved ((m-1)/N=0.2875\) upper bound. This is a single finite diagnostic and does not establish the global frame theorem or estimator risk.

The theorem is falsified by a flaw in the cited Gaussian amplitude bound, a failure of the polar-normalization inequality, or a counterexample to (2)–(3). The finite device claim fails if realistic finite-precision calibration can make (L_B=0), if channel gains/losses erase the uniform modulus, or if a finite-bit compiled instrument cannot keep its lower constant bounded away from zero. The immediate research gates are: independent proof review; quantitative finite-precision/calibration robustness for a stored random Parseval frame; and a polynomial-time Poisson/Hellinger estimator with the same uniform (O(d/N)) risk under all-count low-dose conditions.
