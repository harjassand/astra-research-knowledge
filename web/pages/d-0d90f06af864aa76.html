# Temporal design repairs identification, but not rare-rate acquisition

Status 2026-10-06: independently derived within this worker and replayed with exact symbolic algebra; r08's separate reconstruction PASS is in ../r08_information/cycle5/endpoint_audit.md. No external validation or historical priority established. A scoped theorem, not a general theory of nonequilibrium emergence. Timestamp and general-graph extensions have separate notes.

## Question and exact interface

Can changing observation times resolve continuous-time thermodynamic ambiguity, and what does it cost to acquire entropy production afterwards?

Assume a finite irreducible time-homogeneous CTMC with all state labels directly observed, row generator Q, no hidden variables, and passive measurement. The experimental clock is independent. At each step the learner chooses a calibrated rate λ>0, then observes the next endpoint after an independent Exp(λ) interval. **The interval itself is not retained as data.** Adaptive rate choice can depend on past endpoint labels, past chosen rates, and private randomness, but not on unrevealed times. Rate calibration and full state observation are supplied; Q is acquired. Actual physical waiting time and number of endpoint readings are separate costs. The lower bound below does not cover timestamp-retaining or jump-resolved records.

## Prior art and author claims

Recent starting points: Das–Manikandan, Communications Physics 9,237 (2026-05-08), especially equations 5–7 and Figure 9, learns an effective dissipative force at the observation resolution. The authors explicitly restrict the analytical coarse-graining benchmark to linear models. This is not a claim that arbitrary microscopic dissipation is identifiable from finite-resolution data. Seifert's 2026-07-20 review supplies broad context; only its subscription preview/abstract was accessible.

Bauer–Seifert–van der Meer, arXiv:2412.15642v2 (2025-03-14), Section 3, already proves generator reconstruction below an escape-rate threshold and enumerates matrix-logarithm branches beyond it. Their equations 30–33 give an exact reversible/nonreversible pair with the same fixed-lag propagator. The rate threshold and alias construction are established, not our discovery.

Duffie–Glynn, Econometrica 72(6),1773–1808 (November 2004), sections 2–4 and Proposition 4, already establishes identification from known-intensity random observation without timestamps using resolvents. Poisson sampling as an alias repair is also established. Our substantive question is a finite-information acquisition boundary for thermodynamic functionals after this classical repair. Prior-art status of the exact three-cycle cost theorem remains unverified.

## Classical resolvent repair, independently reconstructed

For constant λ, integrating the semigroup gives

P_λ = ∫_0^∞ λe^(−λt)e^(tQ)dt = λ(λI−Q)^−1,

therefore Q=λ(I−P_λ^−1). The integral converges because a finite Markov semigroup is bounded. Unlike a matrix exponential, this map is injective on all finite generators without an escape-rate promise. If Π=diag(π) for the stationary law,

ΠQ−QᵀΠ = λP_λ^(−T)(ΠP_λ−P_λᵀΠ)P_λ^−1.

Thus reversibility of P_λ is equivalent to reversibility of Q. No unobserved temporal circulation can make the exact Poisson endpoint chain reversible unless Q itself is reversible. This does not survive arbitrary state lumping or unknown clock calibration; scaling Q and λ together leaves P unchanged.

There is an operational posterior stability certificate. If ||P̂−P||∞≤ε, B=P̂^−1 exists, M=||B||∞, and Mε<1, then

||λ(I−B)−Q||∞ ≤ λ M² ε/(1−Mε).

Proof: P=P̂+E=P̂(I+BE); the Neumann bound gives ||P^−1||∞≤M/(1−Mε), and the inverse difference identity gives ||P^−1−B||∞≤M²ε/(1−Mε). No unknown rate bound appears in this posterior certificate. The reconstructed matrix need not be a legal generator at finite sample size; the interval certificate, rather than silently clipping rates, is the supported output.

For comparison, if R=max_i(−Q_ii) is supplied, ||P^−1||∞=1+2R/λ. The leading norm sensitivity λ(1+2R/λ)² is minimized at λ=2R, with value 8R. This is a norm-bound optimization, not an optimal statistical design.

Acquiring a rowwise ε certificate still costs data. For independent prepared-state row samples, Hoeffding plus a union bound gives ε=n sqrt(log(2n²/δ)/(2m)) from m samples per state, total nm endpoints and expected waiting nm/λ, plus preparation costs. Prepared-state access is an additional assumption. A single recurrent endpoint trajectory can collect first m departures per row, using the independent outgoing-stack construction, but the total cover time is extra and can be arbitrarily bad if no stationary-mass/hitting-time promise is supplied.

## Exact acquisition lower bound: a three-cycle

Fix clockwise jump rate 1 and anticlockwise jump rate b>0 on Z/3Z. Let S be the clockwise cyclic shift and

Q_b=S+bSᵀ−(1+b)I.

The stationary law is uniform, every reverse edge is positive, and

σ(b)=(1−b)log(1/b).

The endpoint increments under Poisson sampling have probabilities p_0,p_+,p_− with common denominator

D=3b²+3bλ+3b+λ²+3λ+3,

and numerators

n_0=b²+2bλ+b+λ²+2λ+1,

n_+=b²+b+λ+1,

n_−=b²+bλ+b+1.

These follow by exact inversion of λI−Q_b. Translation invariance makes successive displacements independent for a fixed λ; for adaptive λ their conditional laws retain this form.

**Lemma.** For every b≥0 and λ>0,

I_λ(b)=Σ_y [∂_b p_y(b,λ)]²/p_y(b,λ) ≤1.

The proof is an exact polynomial positivity certificate: reduce I=A/B to the factored rational expression generated by the replay; B>0, and B−A is a polynomial in b,λ whose 60 nonzero coefficients are positive integers, minimum 1. Every coefficient is stored in results/r17_nonequilibrium/fisher_positive_polynomial.json. The replay reconstructs the row law from the generator, differentiates it, reconstructs the rational expression and independently checks all coefficients. This verifies a universal algebraic inequality, unlike a finite parameter-grid test.

At b=0 it simplifies to

I_λ(0)=λ²(λ+2)(λ³+5λ²+6λ+3)/[(λ+1)²(λ²+3λ+3)²],

which tends to 1 as λ→∞ and to 0 as λ→0. This limit is finite despite the diverging sensitivity of σ(b) as b→0.

**Theorem.** Let an endpoint-only learner make at most N such observations, with arbitrary adaptive λ choices. Let L_b denote its full retained transcript distribution. Then

TV(L_b,L_c) ≤ sqrt(N)|b−c|/2.

Proof: define H²(p,q)=Σ(√p−√q)². Since ||∂_b√p||₂²=I_λ(b)/4≤1/4, integrating this vector derivative gives H²(p_b,p_c)≤(b−c)²/4 uniformly in λ. Therefore conditional Hellinger affinity is at least 1−(b−c)²/8 under every shared history. Recursing on histories, the transcript affinity is at least (1−(b−c)²/8)^N when the right base is nonnegative, hence H²(L_b,L_c)≤N(b−c)²/4. If the base is negative the asserted total-variation bound is trivial. Finally TV≤H. Randomized rate selection shares the same conditional kernel under the two hypotheses; adaptive stopping at N can be padded with ignored samples. Both points preserve the bound.

Choose c=b/2, 0<b≤1/2. Exactly,

σ(b/2)−σ(b)=log2+(b/2)log[1/(2b)]≥log2.

Any estimator of σ with additive error ε<log2/2 and success probability at least 0.9 under both hypotheses yields a test with each error ≤0.1. Such a test requires TV≥0.8. Consequently

N≥10.24/b².

This holds even though the clockwise rate, state space, stationary law, and one-parameter form are supplied, and even though the chosen rates can adapt. General unknown generators cannot be easier under the same observation interface.

## Decisive obstruction and comparator

Exact alias-free identification does not imply uniform finite-cost thermodynamic estimation. Take c=b² and b→0. The transcript TV at any fixed N tends to zero, while |σ(c)−σ(b)| diverges. Thus no uniform fixed-N finite additive-accuracy guarantee exists over this family without an affinity/reverse-rate promise, even with escape rate ≤1.5.

The comparison with event-resolved access explains the cost, but it is a different interface: homogeneous clockwise and anticlockwise events are independent Poisson clocks with rates 1 and b. Recording every physical jump reveals its direction; T=C/b gives an O(1) expected number of reverse jumps and Θ(1/b) total jumps. Standard Poisson concentration with a larger C identifies b to fixed relative accuracy and σ to fixed additive accuracy. Endpoint-only Poisson reading costs Ω(1/b²) readings. This is an observation-resolution resource separation, not a same-data estimator advantage or a claim that exponential scheduling is always better than a competent fixed-lag estimator.

Timestamps are cheap to retain in many experiments and were deliberately excluded from the theorem. The bound cannot be advertised against an algorithm that uses them. Hidden-state, non-Markovian, nonstationary, many-body, quantum, and continuous-space extensions remain open. No universal upper bound on hidden dissipation follows.

## Downstream use and falsifiable next steps

For biochemical cycle measurement, a reversible-looking fixed-grid record is insufficient; independently randomized observation can test detailed balance at the exact-law level. Before promoting reconstructed entropy production, require a confidence interval that stays away from zero reverse rates. The inverse certificate addresses generator acquisition; a thermodynamic certificate additionally needs lower reverse fluxes and stationary conditioning.

1. Independent proof gate: reconstruct the rational law and the 60-term certificate without trusting this replay, then check the adaptive Hellinger argument and constants.
2. Retain timestamps: either prove a uniform Fisher-information bound for terminal observations conditional on a known interval, or exhibit a timing scheme escaping quadratic endpoint-count dependence. Do not assume the endpoint-only lower bound automatically extends.
3. Same-interface comparison: compare Poisson resolver MLE with fixed-lag branch-aware MLE at equal state-reading and physical-time budgets; on rare reverse rates, quantify rather than infer an advantage.
4. Practical clock constraint: a positive minimum inter-reading delay produces exp(t_min Q)λ(λI−Q)^−1. Its injectivity/stability requires a separate argument; ideal exponential support down to zero is not a free hardware assumption.

Unexplored alternatives: multirate deterministic schedules with incommensurate lags; path-feature variational EP estimators; transition event sensors; minimum-cost joint acquisition of a generator and microscopic dissipation. The classical resolver is not a new foundational discovery; the scoped information obstruction is the candidate contribution needing a gate and novelty audit.
