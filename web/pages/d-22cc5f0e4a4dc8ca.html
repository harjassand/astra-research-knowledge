CYCLE 3 — FINITE-PRECISION BARRIER FOR RAW-HMM PREDICTIVE TRUNCATION
8 October 2026

STATUS

This is a self-contained, internally proved two-point lower bound and
matching-order estimator on a known two-state symmetric HMM subfamily. It
separates (i) statistical indistinguishability from (ii) deterministic
finite-bit aliasing. The lower bound is for every raw-path learner, including
exact-arithmetic full-path likelihood. It is not a general minimax theorem for
arbitrary HMMs or a theorem about a particular implementation of spectral
learning. Exact rational checks are in
work/programmes/acquisition_learning/cycle3/predictive_precision/verify_exact.py.
No novelty or priority claim is made.

MAIN RESULT

For each integer M >= 4, let epsilon_M = 1/(64 M^4). There is a stationary
binary-output HMM family with a fixed two-state transition matrix, constant
mixing, and emissions bounded away from zero, containing a rank-one process
P_0 and a rank-two process P_M, such that:

  * the smallest nonzero singular value of a complete two-by-two predictive
    Hankel block is gamma_M = 1/(4 M^2) = 2 sqrt(epsilon_M);
  * the one-step predictive laws after history + differ in TV by gamma_M and
    in KL by between 8 epsilon_M and 16 epsilon_M;
  * for 0 < eta <= 1/3, from one raw stationary trajectory of T symbols,
    any learner that
    returns an epsilon_M-KL-accurate forecast at that history with
    probability at least 1-eta under both processes must use

      T >= 1 + kl(1-eta || eta)/(144 epsilon_M),

    where kl(a||b) is binary relative entropy. This applies even if the
    learner knows the family, M, and transition matrix and sees the entire
    path with exact arithmetic;
  * a disjoint-pair count test, and therefore a full-path likelihood learner
    that is at least as informed, achieves the same order
    T = O(epsilon_M^(-1) log(1/eta)).

Thus the gap-free obstruction is statistical, not an SVD artifact. Separately,
rounding the population Hankel entries to a dyadic grid coarser than gamma_M
maps P_0 and P_M to the identical matrix, even with infinitely many
observations. That is a representation-specific numerical alias, avoidable
with enough bits or exact counts; it does not remove the raw-data lower bound.

1. RAW OBSERVATION MODEL AND TASK

The hidden state Z_t is a sign in {-1,+1}. It is stationary uniform and has
transition probabilities

  P(Z_(t+1)=Z_t)=3/4,   P(Z_(t+1)=-Z_t)=1/4.

Equivalently, Z_(t+1)=Z_t F_t for iid signs F_t with mean lambda=1/2.
Emissions are Y_t=Z_t E_t, where E_t are iid signs independent of the chain
and E[E_t]=theta. The learner sees only the raw uninterrupted string
Y_1,...,Y_T. There are no hidden labels, conditional-sampling queries,
dictionary, matrix oracle, or supplied singular-gap promise. To make the
lower bound stronger, grant the learner the family form, lambda=1/2, and the
two candidates below.

The null has theta=0, denoted P_0. Its observations are iid fair signs even
though the latent chain still has two states. The alternative has theta=1/M,
denoted P_M. In either model every emission probability is at least 3/8,
the hidden chain is irreducible with fixed nontrivial eigenvalue 1/2, and its
mixing time is constant under usual total-variation conventions. The latent
state cap is exactly two throughout; the minimal observable/predictive rank
is one at the null and two at the alternative.

The task is the conditional law of Y_(t+1) after the observed one-symbol
history Y_t=+1. The prediction is required in KL at tolerance epsilon_M, with
success probability at least 1-eta for each candidate. This is a local
predictive task, not recovery of the entire HMM or every conditional law.

2. COMPLETE HANKEL WITNESS AND TASK SEPARATION

Let mu=E[Y_t Y_(t+1)]=lambda theta^2=theta^2/2. With rows and columns ordered
as +1,-1, the population two-symbol joint-probability Hankel block is

  H_theta = 1/4 [[1+mu, 1-mu],
                 [1-mu, 1+mu]].

It is a complete block for this two-dimensional observable family: its
eigenvectors are (1,1) and (1,-1), with eigenvalues 1/2 and mu/2. It is
symmetric positive semidefinite, so these are also its singular values.
At theta=0 the matrix has rank one. For theta=1/M,

  gamma_M = sigma_2(H_(1/M)) = mu/2 = theta^2/4 = 1/(4 M^2),
  det(H_(1/M)) = mu/4 = gamma_M/2 = 1/(8 M^2).

Hence the exact rank changes while the least nonzero singular value tends to
zero. The state count, transition matrix, transition mixing, and emission
floor remain fixed.

The history has stationary probability 1/2. Therefore

  q_0(+ | +) = 1/2,
  q_M(+ | +) = P(Y_t=+,Y_(t+1)=+)/P(Y_t=+)
             = (1+mu)/2 = 1/2 + gamma_M.

Their TV distance is gamma_M. For Bernoulli distributions with parameters
1/2 and 1/2+gamma, Pinsker gives KL >= 2 gamma^2; the chi-square upper bound
gives KL <= 4 gamma^2. Since gamma_M^2=4 epsilon_M,

  8 epsilon_M <= KL(q_M || q_0) <= 16 epsilon_M.

The same lower bound holds with the KL arguments reversed by Pinsker. Thus
the two laws are task-relevant at the stated tolerance: a forecast within
epsilon_M KL of either one cannot also be within epsilon_M KL of the other.
More explicitly, Pinsker puts each success set inside a TV ball of radius
sqrt(epsilon_M/2), while

  2 sqrt(epsilon_M/2) < gamma_M = 2 sqrt(epsilon_M).

This makes the two success sets disjoint and turns any successful predictor
into a test between P_0 and P_M.

3. RAW-PATH KL LOWER BOUND

The following calculation uses all T observed symbols and no hidden labels.
Write F_t for the observations through time t, and let

  r_t = E_theta[Z_t | Y_1,...,Y_(t-1)],
  m_t = E_theta[Z_t | Y_1,...,Y_t].

Stationarity gives r_1=0. For y in {-1,+1}, Bayes' rule and the transition
matrix give

  P_theta(Y_t=y | F_(t-1)) = (1 + theta y r_t)/2,
  m_t = (r_t + theta y)/(1 + theta y r_t),
  r_(t+1) = lambda m_t.

For theta <= 1/4, induction gives |r_t| <= 3 theta/2. Indeed, if this holds,
then

  |1 + theta y r_t| >= 1 - 3 theta^2/2 >= 29/32,
  |r_t + theta y| <= 5 theta/2,
  |r_(t+1)| <= (1/2)(80/29)theta = 40 theta/29 < 3 theta/2.

The base r_1=0 is immediate. Under P_0 every conditional observation is
fair. The KL chain rule, followed by KL <= chi-square for each conditional
Bernoulli law, therefore yields

  KL(P_theta^(T) || P_0^(T))
    = sum_(t=1)^T E_theta[
        KL(P_theta(Y_t | F_(t-1)) || Ber(1/2))]
    <= (9/4)(T-1) theta^4.

The first summand is zero because r_1=0. For each later summand the
conditional mean shift is theta r_t, whose squared value is at most
9 theta^4/4. With theta=1/M and theta^4=64 epsilon_M,

  KL(P_M^(T) || P_0^(T)) <= 144 (T-1) epsilon_M.            (1)

Suppose a forecast estimator succeeds with probability at least 1-eta under
each candidate. The disjoint prediction success sets above induce a test
with both errors at most eta. By data processing from the raw string to the
test bit,

  KL(P_M^(T) || P_0^(T)) >= kl(1-eta || eta),

for 0<eta<1/2. Combining this with (1) proves

  T >= 1 + kl(1-eta || eta)/(144 epsilon_M).                (2)

For any fixed eta<1/2, this is Omega(epsilon_M^(-1)); uniformly for
0<eta<=1/3, the numerator is Theta(log(1/eta)). A second finite-sample form
follows directly from Pinsker:

  TV(P_M^(T),P_0^(T))
    <= sqrt((9/8)(T-1)/M^4).

When T-1 <= M^4/18, this TV is at most 1/4, so every test has worst-case
error at least (1-TV)/2 >= 3/8. Exact real arithmetic cannot evade either
bound. The only information supplied to the proof is the entire raw path;
processing it into a Hankel matrix, likelihood ratio, neural summary, or
randomized decision is post-processing.

4. MATCHED RAW-COUNT AND FULL-PATH BASELINES

For n=floor(T/2), form disjoint adjacent products

  W_i = Y_(2i-1) Y_(2i)
      = (Z_(2i-1) Z_(2i))(E_(2i-1) E_(2i)),  i=1,...,n.

The transition flips and emission signs are independent, so the W_i are iid
signs with mean 0 under P_0 and mean

  mu_M = theta^2/2 = 1/(2 M^2) = 4 sqrt(epsilon_M)

under P_M. The threshold test selecting P_M when
Wbar > mu_M/2 has, under either hypothesis, error at most

  exp(-n mu_M^2/8) = exp(-2 n epsilon_M).

Thus n >= (1/(2 epsilon_M)) log(1/eta) suffices. Output q_0 if the test
selects the null, and q_M if it selects the alternative. On a correct
decision the forecast KL error is zero; on an incorrect decision it exceeds
epsilon_M. This establishes the matching-order upper bound
T=O(epsilon_M^(-1) log(1/eta)) using only an exact integer count of W_i.

The strongest matched baseline is the likelihood-ratio test between the
known full-path laws P_0^(T) and P_M^(T). It knows at least as much as the
pair-count estimator and sees every raw symbol. A two-state forward
recursion evaluates either candidate likelihood in O(T) arithmetic
operations and O(1) state slots; a direct exact-rational implementation
uses integers whose bit lengths grow at most linearly in T log M, with
bit-runtime depending on the chosen big-integer multiplication routine.
The optimal likelihood test cannot beat the lower bound (2), and it cannot
have worse testing error than the explicit pair-count test. Thus, for
0<eta<=1/3, the full-path likelihood baseline also has the same
Theta(epsilon_M^(-1) log(1/eta)) order up to constants. The gap-free
statistical failure is therefore not peculiar to SVD, operator fitting, or
discarding path data.

5. RANK/SVD, OPERATOR CONDITIONING, AND FINITE PRECISION

Statistical estimation of the weak direction. On this family the weakest
singular value is gamma_M and the pair-product mean is mu_M=2 gamma_M.
The disjoint count estimates mu_M with standard deviation of order n^(-1/2).
A Weyl-safe rank cutoff needs operator error below a constant multiple of
gamma_M, so this structured two-by-two pilot needs n=Theta(gamma_M^(-2)
log(1/eta))=Theta(epsilon_M^(-1) log(1/eta)) samples for reliable
classification. This agrees with the all-algorithm lower bound. It does not
prove that every useful predictive truncation should identify exact rank:
the task-risk estimator above directly predicts the requested law.

Population rounding alias. Define Q_b(x) by rounding x to the nearest
multiple of h=2^(-b), with ties irrelevant below. For b>=2 the null entries
1/4 lie on the grid, while the alternative entries are 1/4 plus or minus
gamma_M/2. If h>gamma_M, each displacement is less than h/2. Thus

  Q_b(H_(1/M)) = Q_b(H_0).

Even an infinite-data matrix-first workflow cannot distinguish these
rounded inputs. Avoiding this particular alias requires h<gamma_M, hence
b >= log_2(1/gamma_M) up to an additive constant. A simple robust SVD
certificate uses a stronger sufficient setting: nearest-grid coordinate
error at most h/2 gives Frobenius and operator error at most h for this
two-by-two matrix, so h<=gamma_M/4 guarantees perturbation at most
gamma_M/4. This asks

  b >= ceil(log_2(4/gamma_M))
    = (1/2) log_2(1/epsilon_M) + O(1)

fractional bits. These are precision requirements for this matrix-rounding
interface, not lower bounds on arbitrary encodings. Keeping integer counts
and rational denominators exact avoids this intermediate rounding.

Prediction-output and exact-rational encodings. The two predictive
probabilities differ by gamma_M=2 sqrt(epsilon_M). Since q_0=1/2 lies on
the dyadic grid, robustly separating their rounded values requires a step
at most a constant multiple of gamma_M, hence
(1/2)log_2(1/epsilon_M)+O(1) fractional bits. For the stronger task of
returning a numerically accurate forecast, a convenient sufficient setting
is step h<=sqrt(epsilon_M)/4: rounding adds at most
sqrt(epsilon_M)/8 in probability error and at most epsilon_M/12 in KL when
the output stays in [1/4,3/4]. This also uses
(1/2)log_2(1/epsilon_M)+O(1) fractional bits. The
exact parameter theta=1/M has denominator length Theta(log M) =
(1/4)log_2(1/epsilon_M)+O(1). The reduced predictive rational

  q_M(+ | +) = (2 M^2 + 1)/(4 M^2)

and each alternative Hankel entry have numerator and denominator lengths
Theta(log M), equivalently Theta(log(1/epsilon_M)) total bits per rational.
An exact pair count C in {0,...,n} costs ceil(log_2(n+1)) bits; at the
matching sample size this is Theta(log(1/epsilon_M)+log log(1/eta)).
These are representation costs. In the known binary subproblem, selecting
one of two candidates can be communicated by one decision bit, and no
universal memory lower follows from the rational code lengths alone. The
unavoidable result here is the raw-symbol lower bound (2), not an
unqualified claim that every learner must store a particular parameter
encoding.

Operator inversion. The block has ||H^(-1)||=1/gamma_M and condition number
1/(2 gamma_M). If an operator reconstruction explicitly inverts H, and
||Hhat-H|| <= gamma_M/2, then

  ||Hhat^(-1)-H^(-1)||
    <= ||Hhat^(-1)|| ||Hhat-H|| ||H^(-1)||
    <= 2 ||Hhat-H||/gamma_M^2.

This is a generic sufficient perturbation bound, not a matching numerical
lower bound for every realization method. The prediction task here can be
estimated from one scalar pair moment without any inverse; imposing the
generic inverse precision can be wasteful. A rank-free risk certificate
should therefore account for its actual output map and conditioning, not
merely the exact algebraic rank.

6. COST LEDGER AND INTERFACE COMPARISON

Raw information: one stationary trajectory of T symbols; no reset queries
or matrix outputs. The lower bound charges all T symbols, not just the
disjoint pairs used by the upper bound. A known two-hypothesis full-path
likelihood baseline is granted the same T symbols and candidate family.

Count/prediction estimator: one pass over T symbols, O(T) sign operations,
one exact count with ceil(log_2(n+1)) bits, and O(1) additional state. A
dyadic output uses b=Theta(log(1/epsilon_M)) fractional bits. Counting in
the usual bit model costs O(T log T) bit operations conservatively; the
arithmetic-operation count is O(T).

Hankel/SVD pilot: the witness matrix is only 2 by 2, so storage and
arithmetic after the pair count are constant-sized. Obtaining the count
still costs the same O(T) raw pass. A robust rank certificate needs
sampling error plus numerical error below a constant times gamma_M; the
sample term costs Theta(gamma_M^(-2) log(1/eta)) and the stated direct
dyadic matrix format needs b=Theta(log(1/gamma_M)) fractional bits.
Numerically rounded population matrices can alias exactly as above.

Operator route: an inverse/pseudoinverse adds sensitivity proportional to
gamma_M^(-2) for perturbations, with coefficient-dependent precision beyond
the rank-cutoff precision if a fixed operator error is requested. This
conditioning statement is only for algorithms using such an inverse.

Full-path likelihood: two candidate likelihoods can be propagated by the
two-state forward algorithm in O(T) arithmetic operations and constant
state dimension. Exact rational numerators/denominators can grow to
O(T log M) bits. Its best achievable error has the same lower bound (2);
the pair-count decision proves an upper bound of the same order. Thus the
matrix and likelihood approaches face the same statistical frontier even
though their numeric representations and computation differ.

7. COUNTEREXAMPLE, LITERATURE, AND UNKNOWN

Counterexample to an overbroad claim. The family above refutes: “a fixed
hidden-state cap, positive emission floor, and constant hidden-chain mixing
time suffice for uniform exact predictive-rank recovery or for a
gap-independent finite-sample certificate.” For any finite T, choose M
large enough that T is far below M^4. Equation (1) then makes the full raw
path laws close, although the exact rank differs. Adding exact arithmetic
does not help. It also refutes “the obstruction is just a weak SVD
implementation”: the strongest full-path likelihood test obeys the same
Le Cam lower bound. Conversely, the count estimator shows that low rank
need not prevent useful task prediction when enough data are paid.

This does not prove that every gap-free, rank-free predictive truncation
fails at fixed target tolerance. In the constructed family the appropriate
sample cost is expressed in task tolerance, and the direct predictor
achieves it without matrix inversion. A task-risk method may legitimately
discard a weak direction when its effect is below tolerance.

UNKNOWN:

1. A matching minimax frontier for arbitrary fixed-state HMMs, especially
   task-risk truncation uniformly over all histories and longer futures.
2. Whether a non-inverse, finite-precision algorithm can give useful
   rank-free certificates for broad HMM classes under only task-risk
   assumptions.
3. General memory lower bounds beyond lossless count/parameter encodings
   and the explicit fixed-grid alias. The known two-point test needs only a
   one-bit decision output.
4. Necessary precision for full observable-operator reconstruction. The
   inverse perturbation estimate above is sufficient and algorithm-specific.
5. Novelty and external validation. This is an elementary tailored
   construction and Le Cam argument; no priority claim is made.
