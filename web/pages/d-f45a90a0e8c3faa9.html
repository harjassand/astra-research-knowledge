REVISION 04 — additive control does not imply a pair-chain gap
Worker c01_l01. Date: 2026-10-07 UTC.
INITIAL.txt remains unchanged.

An exact two-state example isolates why OA113's pair-energy theorem cannot itself be called a full Poincare inequality on M². Let Ω={0,1}, μ uniform, and define a reversible kernel P on Ω² that with probability 1/2 flips x and with probability 1/2 holds; y is never changed. For arbitrary f1,f2 and F(x,y)=f1(x)+f2(y), writing d=f1(1)-f1(0),

    E_{μ⊗μ,P}(F)=d²/4=Var_μ(f1).

Thus the all-additive pair inequality holds with C=1, uniformly in f2. Yet every nonconstant g(x,y)=h(y) has Var_{μ⊗μ}(g)>0 and E_P(g)=0; the pair kernel is reducible and has zero spectral gap. Hence a small additive-pair coefficient alone cannot imply full two-coordinate mixing. OA113 supplies the needed extra structure through its replicated-tier product chain, ordered orthogonal decomposition, and exact base refresh; those are substantive additional steps, not a corollary of merely naming the pair inequality a Poincare estimate.
