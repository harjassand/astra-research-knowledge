# Corrected sourcewise-cone criterion for Langevin reaction-network identifiability

## Exact scope

For a mass-action reaction (y\to y'\) with displacement ν = (y'-y\),
the Langevin approximation has drift contribution ν κ x^y and diffusion
covariance contribution νν^ᵀ κ x^y. Package those together as the
lifted signature

\[
s(\nu)=(\nu,\nu\nu^{\mathsf T}).
\]

For each source complex (y\), define its **strict positive cone** as all
positive linear combinations of the lifted signatures of reactions leaving
(y\). The generator coefficients are polynomials in (x\); equality on a
nonempty open subset therefore forces coefficientwise equality for each
source monomial (x^y\). Thus two networks with the same source set have equal
Langevin generators for some strictly positive rate vectors iff the two
strict positive cones intersect at **every** source. The negation is that at
least one source has disjoint cones.

Consequently, the corrected unconfoundability criterion is:

* if source sets differ, the generators cannot agree for strictly positive
  rates; or
* if source sets agree, the networks are unconfoundable iff there **exists**
  a source (y\) for which the lifted positive cones are disjoint.

The published Theorem 3.3(2) of Faul, Hoessly, and Xia (published online 23
April 2026, DOI [10.1017/S0956792526100382](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382))
instead says that unconfoundability requires disjoint cones for **every**
common source. Its Theorem 5.15 states the correct confoundability direction,
and the proof in §7.3 explicitly derives cone intersection at every source
from confoundability, then constructs positive rate vectors from the
intersection at every source. Negating that equivalence yields an existential
source condition, not a universal one. The published online article retains
the mismatch found in arXiv v3.

## Exact counterexample

Take one species (A\) and networks

\[
\mathcal N: \quad \varnothing\to A,\quad A\to\varnothing;
\qquad
\mathcal N': \quad \varnothing\to 2A,\quad A\to\varnothing.
\]

Both source sets are \(\{\varnothing,A\}\). At source \(\varnothing\),
the signatures are \((1,1)\) and \((2,4)\); their positive rays are disjoint,
since \((\alpha,\alpha)=(2\beta,4\beta)\) would force \(\beta=0\), excluded
by strict positivity. At source \(A\), both networks have signature
\((-1,1)\), so those cones coincide.

Writing the source rates of \(\mathcal N\) as \((a,b)\) and those of
\(\mathcal N'\) as \((c,d)\), the generator drift and diffusion coefficients
are

\[
A_{\mathcal N}(x)=a-bx,\quad B_{\mathcal N}(x)=a+bx;
\qquad
A_{\mathcal N'}(x)=2c-dx,\quad B_{\mathcal N'}(x)=4c+dx.
\]

Equality of generators would require \(a=2c\) from constant drift and
\(a=4c\) from constant diffusion, impossible for \(c>0\). Hence these
networks are unconfoundable, although their cones are not disjoint at every
source. The bounded exact-arithmetic witness is in
[`verify_faul_cone_counterexample.py`](verify_faul_cone_counterexample.py).

This finite fixture corroborates the derivation; the general criterion follows from coefficientwise polynomial identity and Theorem 5.15.
* **Assumptions:** same species set; mass-action Langevin approximation; complete, known candidate reaction graphs; all species observed in the model; strictly positive rate constants; structural generator/SDE identifiability on positive open subsets. This is not finite-data identifiability, partial-observation identifiability, CTMC identification, or an assay validation claim.
