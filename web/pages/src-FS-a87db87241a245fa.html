# Astra: direction-preserving convexification and a Picone certificate for compatible gradients

Research date: 15 October 2026.

## Status and principal distinction

The full implication from rank-one convexity to quasiconvexity for planar homogeneous quartics, including the complete nine-dimensional left-SO(2)-invariant cone, is **not established here**. No rigorously negative compatible closed or Dirichlet test is constructed.

This investigation changes the operation rather than extending a profile potential. For an arbitrary fixed spatial direction field n(x), the full compatible energy of u=r n is convex in rho=r^4. It admits an exact direction-preserving Picone identity, a quantitative remainder, and scalar flux lower certificates. Under a positive rank-one value bound, minimizing over amplitudes is an attained convex variational problem. Applied to an arbitrary smooth affine-boundary test after a constant target translation, this gives a global compatible energy-decreasing replacement. It does not minimize over directions and does not assume that minimization over directions is convex.

An explicit coupled two-dimensional phase is certified for every amplitude, using rational Fourier coefficients and outward-rounded interval arithmetic over the complete torus. This is a demonstration of the general certificate mechanism, not a universal phase theorem. An exact second-variation example disproves joint phase-density convexity even for the convex energy |A|^4.

Hidden convexity, convex perspectives and Picone identities have established predecessors. The contribution claimed within this investigation is the precise rank-one-convex vectorial application and its implementation, not invention of those general tools. Historical novelty and external correctness remain unestablished.

Ten groups of exact algebraic checks passed. A separate continuum interval certificate passed; it depends on the stated IEEE-arithmetic model and is not a rational-only or proof-assistant verification. The previous ten-group checker was replayed, but none of the earlier long contact classifications is a premise of the new principal theorems.

## 1. Audit of the inherited critical-trace representation

The inherited smooth local storage argument was reconstructed. For P(U)=integral p(U,U_y), its first variation contains -p_qq(U,U_y)U_yy. Substituting U=z0+w(Ny)/N and velocities of both signs leaves the variational action bounded while this term is of order N. Arbitrary periodic jets force p_qq=0. Therefore p=A(U).U_y+c(U). Constant profiles fix grad c, and freezing the skew curvature at a profile value followed by a small-support, zero-mean derivative test gives

    F(B+X)-F(B)-DF(B):X >= kappa det X.

Existence at all B yields the determinant-affine convex support representation and hence polyconvexity. This reasoning does exclude smooth first-jet local storage; it does not exclude nonlocal or nonsmooth functionals. It is not contradicted by the operation developed below, which does not assert a dominating functional on transverse traces.

The critical trace calculation also survives reconstruction. For

    F_DM(A)=|A|^4-(4/sqrt(3))|A|^2 det A,

and Phi(x,y)=(x/2)(cos y,sin y), 0<=x<=2, exact integration gives

    action=103/120-sqrt(3)/2 < 0.

The sign is certified by 3/4-(103/120)^2=191/14400>0. Reversing both variables gives the oppositely oriented negative return. Traces N^(-3/4)e^(+/- iNy) with widths 2/N retain these actions. Their W^(3/4,4) norms stay bounded while they converge weakly to zero. Thus the prior restrictions on weak/subcritical trace storage do not vanish by changing terminology.

The replay is in audit/prior_replay.log. This replay checks the finite expressions, not independently the analytic localization proof; the preceding two paragraphs state the analytic reconstruction used here.

### A primary-source obligation not resolved by a claimed complete proof

Pedregal's arXiv:1905.06571v4 claims the complete planar implication. In Step 2 of the proof of Proposition 6.1, the positivity-feasible set mathscr(P)_l is shown to contain a neighborhood; the text then passes from its full linear span to its equality with the entire space P_l. That passage needs an additional argument: a positivity-constrained set need not be a vector subspace, and full span alone does not supply admissible nonnegative weights for every vector. The elementary set [-1,1] in R illustrates the logical distinction. This identifies an unfulfilled proof obligation in the displayed argument, not a counterexample to Proposition 6.1 or a disproof of Morrey's assertion. The claimed theorem is not used as a premise.

## 2. The general direction-preserving convexification

Let Omega be a bounded Lipschitz domain, or a flat torus. For the initial calculations all fields are smooth. Let

    F : R^(m x n) -> R

be C^1, positively p-homogeneous for p>1, and rank-one convex. Let n(x) be a fixed C^1 unit vector field in R^m. No rotational invariance of F is assumed.

For r>0 define

    u=r n,      rho=r^p,
    A_r=Dn+n tensor grad(log r).

The chain rule and homogeneity give exactly

    Du=r A_r,
    F(Du)=rho F(Dn+n tensor (grad rho/(p rho))).             (2.1)

### Theorem 2.1: convexity in the density

For the fixed direction field n, the functional

    E_n(rho)=integral rho F(Dn+n tensor (grad rho/(p rho)))   (2.2)

is convex in rho>0.

Equivalently, for positive amplitudes r0,r1 and 0<=t<=1,

    rt=[(1-t)r0^p+t r1^p]^(1/p)

satisfies the pointwise inequality

    F(D(rt n)) <= (1-t)F(D(r0 n))+t F(D(r1 n)).              (2.3)

Proof. At each point x, the function

    a -> F(Dn(x)+n(x) tensor a)

is convex. Differences of any two matrices in this affine set have rank at most one. Its perspective (rho,z)->rho f_x(z/(p rho)) is jointly convex. Substituting z=grad rho proves the result.

For a direct check, set rhoi=ri^p and weights w0=(1-t)rho0/rhot, w1=t rho1/rhot. They sum to one, and

    grad rhot/(p rhot)=w0 grad rho0/(p rho0)+w1 grad rho1/(p rho1).

The two normalized full gradients differ by a rank-one matrix n tensor a. Apply rank-one convexity and multiply by rhot. QED.

The proof does not require F to be globally convex, nonnegative, or quasiconvex. It allows arbitrary spatial variation of n and r; no carrier or frequency separation is imposed.

When F is a homogeneous quartic, the theorem extends to nonnegative W^(1,4) amplitudes by approximation. The fourth-root mean has a W^(1,4) gradient controlled by the gradients of the two amplitudes. One can regularize each amplitude by (r_i^4+epsilon^4)^(1/4), prove the inequality for the regularized amplitudes, and pass strongly in W^(1,4). The density domain is precisely the set of nonnegative rho with rho^(1/4) in W^(1,4), rather than an unqualified Sobolev space for rho.

### An explicit Hessian of the perspective

For planar left-invariant F, later write f_k(a)=F([a;k]) with a,k rows. At fixed k, h(rho,z)=rho f_k(z/(4rho)) satisfies

    D^2 h[(d rho,d z),(d rho,d z)]
       = (d z-4a d rho)^T D^2_aa f_k(a)(d z-4a d rho)/(16rho),
    a=z/(4rho).                                            (2.4)

The right side is nonnegative by rank-one convexity. The exact checker verifies this identity for every homogeneous quartic monomial; positivity is the analytic rank-one argument.

## 3. An exact vectorial Picone identity

### Theorem 3.1: direction-preserving relative energy

Let r,s>0 and keep the same direction field n. Put

    Q=n tensor grad(log(s/r)),
    R_F(C,Q)=F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C):Q.

Then pointwise

    F(D(sn))-(1/p)DF(D(rn)):D(n s^p/r^(p-1))
       =s^p R_F(A_r,Q) >=0.                                (3.1)

Proof. The derivative of the test field is exactly

    D(n s^p/r^(p-1))=(s^p/r^(p-1))(A_r+pQ).

Also DF(D(rn))=r^(p-1)DF(A_r), and Euler's identity gives DF(A_r):A_r=pF(A_r). Substitution proves the equality. The difference Q is rank one, so the remainder is nonnegative. QED.

This is a vectorial use of the established Picone/hidden-convexity mechanism, but only rank-one convexity is needed because the direction n is retained throughout the comparison.

### Quantitative quartic version

For a rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic, let

    mu_F=min{F(R):rank R=1, |R|=1}.

The one-dimensional convex quartic calculation gives

    R_F(C,Q) >= F(Q)/3

for every rank-one Q. Indeed, for f(t)=At^4+Lt^3+C2t^2+Dt+E, convexity implies C2>=3L^2/(8A) if A>0, and

    A+L+C2-A/3=3(L+4A/3)^2/(8A)+C2-3L^2/(8A)>=0.

For A=0, global nonnegativity of f'' forces L=0 and C2>=0.

If n is unit, (3.1) therefore strengthens to

    F(D(sn))-(1/4)DF(D(rn)):D(n s^4/r^3)
       >= (mu_F/3) r^4 |grad(s/r)|^4.                      (3.2)

The identity and bound extend to real W^(1,4) competitors s when the reference r is smooth and bounded away from zero. Approximation at the zeros of s is legitimate; the expression D(n s^4/r^3) contains no division by s. For a real homogeneous quartic, replacing s by |s| preserves F(D(sn)) almost everywhere.

### Ground-state consequence

On a periodic cell, suppose a positive reference r solves the scalar radial equation

    -n . div DF(D(rn)) = 4 lambda r^3.                     (3.3)

Pair (3.3) with s^4/r^3 and integrate (3.2). Then

    E_n(s^4)-lambda integral s^4
       >= (mu_F/3) integral r^4 |grad(s/r)|^4.             (3.4)

Testing (3.3) with r also gives E_n(r^4)=lambda integral r^4. Hence every sufficiently regular positive radial critical point at fixed fourth mass is a global amplitude minimizer. If mu_F>0 on a connected domain, equality forces s/r constant, giving uniqueness after mass normalization. No theorem that all minimizing amplitudes are strictly positive is assumed here.

For nonzero common boundary values, apply (3.2) to the difference of test fields n(s^4/r^3-r), which has zero boundary trace. The same mass-normalized comparison holds with the corresponding boundary term canceled.

## 4. A global compatible replacement for arbitrary affine-boundary tests

This section is not restricted to a special phase family.

Assume F is a rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic with mu_F>0, and n is smooth with bounded Dn. For a fixed fourth mass M and an admissible nonnegative boundary amplitude g, consider

    minimize integral F(D(rn))
    subject to r>=0, r|boundary=g, integral r^4=M.           (4.1)

### Theorem 4.1: existence and global amplitude minimization

When the admissible set is nonempty, (4.1) has a minimizer. Its density formulation is convex.

Proof. By polynomial growth and Young's inequality,

    F(n tensor grad r+r Dn)
       >= (mu_F/2)|grad r|^4-C_F ||Dn||_infinity^4 r^4.     (4.2)

At fixed M, a minimizing sequence is bounded in W^(1,4). Pass to a subsequence weakly there and strongly in L4. Nonnegativity, trace and the fourth-mass constraint pass to the limit. The scalar integrand is convex in grad r, since changing grad r changes the matrix by n tensor a. Its continuity and fourth-order growth, together with (4.2), give weak lower semicontinuity under weak gradients and strong amplitudes. This proves attainment.

The admissible densities form a convex set: boundary values and mass are affine in rho, and the fourth-root interpolation of Section 2 preserves W^(1,4). Thus no non-global amplitude minimum is hidden by the transformation. This is an existence statement and an exact convex formulation, not a universal polynomial-time numerical complexity bound. QED.

### Application to a completely general smooth test

Let u(x)=Bx+phi(x), with phi smooth and zero on the boundary. Choose a constant target translation c large enough that u+c is nonzero everywhere and, in two target dimensions, lies in a fixed open half-plane. Translation does not change Du. Set

    r=|u+c|,       n=(u+c)/|u+c|,
    M=integral |u+c|^4,       g=|Bx+c| on boundary.

Use (4.1) to replace r by a global amplitude minimizer r_*. The field

    u_*=r_* n

is still an actual Sobolev map and has boundary Bx+c. Moreover,

    integral F(Du_*) <= integral F(Du).                    (4.3)

Thus any negative quasiconvexity test at B has an equally negative or lower-energy replacement with globally minimized amplitude at its direction field. Complete compatibility and the affine boundary are preserved. The operation does not preserve the fourth gradient norm, and no such claim is made. If B was an optimizer for the old test, optimizing again for the new zero-boundary perturbation can only lower its gap further.

This applies to every smooth planar test, not just maps already presented in polar form. In a bounded planar domain, W^(1,4) maps are continuous; the smooth reduction is enough because strict negative gaps persist under strong smooth approximation. Fixed-mass amplitude minimizers may vanish, but n remains a prescribed smooth field, so u_*=r_*n is still well defined.

For the unresolved non-polyconvex members of the full left-SO(2) quartic cone, mu_F>0. A short reconstruction is as follows. If F vanishes at a nonzero rank-one matrix, its convex binary rank-one restriction has a nonzero null direction. Its fourth root is a seminorm. After a real input-coordinate change the restriction is a single fourth power. The complete invariant polynomial is then a u^2+gamma q^2+q(Lu+Mv+Nw); PSD Hessians at the rank-one matrices e12 and e11 force L=M=N=0 and gamma>=0. This is polyconvex. By compactness, every non-polyconvex member has strictly positive minimum rank-one value. The new method can alternatively be used with the explicit hypothesis mu_F>0, without relying on this classification observation.

### What is not eliminated

The direction field n is not optimized in (4.3). No general amplitude positivity statement, regularity theory for every minimizer, or sign of its minimum is inferred. These are genuine limits of the result.

## 5. Planar phase coordinates and a constructive scalar dual

For left-SO(2)-invariant F, let

    n=(cos theta,sin theta),      k=grad theta,
    f_k(a)=F([[a1,a2],[k1,k2]]).

Output invariance removes theta itself and gives

    E_theta(rho)=integral rho f_k(grad rho/(4rho)).          (5.1)

A phase with integer winding is permitted on a torus: theta need not be a real periodic function, provided n and its derivatives are periodic.

For a positive smooth reference density sigma, define

    a_sigma=grad sigma/(4sigma),
    j_sigma=partial_a f_k(a_sigma),
    g_sigma=f_k(a_sigma)-a_sigma.j_sigma-(1/4)div j_sigma.  (5.2)

### Theorem 5.1: scalar lower certificate with a positive remainder

On the torus, for every real W^(1,4) amplitude s,

    integral F(D(s n))
      >= integral g_sigma s^4
        +(mu_F/3) integral |grad s-(s/4)grad(log sigma)|^4. (5.3)

In particular,

    inf_{integral s^4=1} E_theta(s^4) >= ess inf g_sigma.  (5.4)

Proof. The derivative of h(rho,z)=rho f_k(z/(4rho)) is

    partial_rho h=f_k-a.partial_a f_k,
    partial_z h=(1/4)partial_a f_k.

Integrating its tangent inequality gives (5.4), since the functional is homogeneous of degree one in rho. Keeping the pointwise rank-one Bregman remainder and applying (3.2) gives (5.3). Equivalently, apply the Picone identity with r=sigma^(1/4). All derivatives of the reference are bounded, so density extends the inequality to the asserted competitors. QED.

For a periodic positive radial critical point, g_sigma is constant and (5.4) is exact. This is a checkable scalar Euler residual, not an assumed dominating functional on all transverse profiles.

### Flux formulation and affine-boundary data

Let f_k^*(j)=sup_a(j.a-f_k(a)). For any smooth flux j,

    E_theta(rho)
       >= integral [-div j/4-f_k^*(j)]rho
          +(1/4)integral_boundary rho j.nu.                (5.5)

This follows directly from Fenchel's inequality and integration by parts. If the interior bracket is at least lambda, the right side is at least lambda M plus the fixed boundary flux contribution. At j=j_sigma, differentiable convexity gives f_k^*(j_sigma)=a_sigma.j_sigma-f_k(a_sigma), recovering (5.2).

No unqualified infinite-dimensional strong-duality/attainment theorem is needed for this a posteriori bound. Any supplied reference sigma or flux can be tested independently. The missing global theorem would have to give adequate bounds for all admissible direction fields and boundary data, not merely define those bounds through unknown quasiconvexity.

## 6. A continuum certificate for a genuinely coupled phase

Use the retained integrand

    G(A)=3(u+v-2q)^2+(35/24)u^2+q^2-(5/2)qu,              (6.1)

where u=a^2+c^2, v=b^2+d^2, q=ad-bc. The exact checker independently constructs its acoustic Gram matrix; see Section 9. It has mu_G=3 and G(I)=-1/24. The latter together with the centered determinant-balanced scaling law on +/-36I and +/-diag(1,-1) proves non-polyconvexity; its exact expected energy is -1350/1297. This is only a non-polyconvexity witness, not a gradient law.

Consider the fully two-dimensional phase

    theta(x,y)=y+(3/25)sin x+(1/10)sin y+(2/25)sin(x+y).    (6.2)

Let sigma be the mean-one real trigonometric polynomial specified by the 80 rational coefficients in phase_density_certificate.json:

    sigma=1+sum_(i,j) [A_ij cos(ix+jy)+B_ij sin(ix+jy)],

with 0<=i<=4, -4<=j<=4, and either i>0 or j>0. There is no constant mode in this sum. The rational data, not exploratory optimizer values, are the certificate input.

The outward-rounded adaptive interval computation proves on the complete torus

    sigma>1/10,            g_sigma>=1.                    (6.3)

The certified minimum lower bounds retained by the computation were approximately 0.10197036 for sigma and 1.000000791 for g; only the simpler rational inequalities (6.3) are used in the theorem.

Theorem 5.1 therefore gives, for every real periodic W^(1,4) amplitude r,

    integral G(D(r e^(i theta)))
      >= integral r^4
         +integral |grad r-(r/4)grad(log sigma)|^4.        (6.4)

The coefficient of the remainder is one because mu_G/3=1. All amplitudes are covered, including arbitrary oscillations and concentration at this fixed smooth phase. No quasiconvexity of G is assumed. This is a proof demonstration of the scalar-certificate method, not a theorem for arbitrary phases or arbitrary affine additions to this particular periodic family.

A separate full-cell interval integration gives

    E_theta(sigma) < 25/8.

Since integral sigma=1 exactly, the normalized amplitude minimum for (6.2) is bracketed between 1 and 25/8. The numerical optimizer's value around 2.69928454 is merely a search diagnostic; it is not a certified exact minimum.

### Interval proof details and limits

The certificate uses no floating sine or cosine as a mathematical oracle. A rational Machin formula brackets pi; degree-33 sine Taylor polynomials with explicit degree-35 remainder brackets supply values at cell centers; sine's one-Lipschitz property encloses each complete cell. Polynomial coefficients are rational and are enclosed on conversion to binary64. Basic interval operations are rounded outward with numpy.nextafter.

Starting from 256x256 cells, unresolved cells are subdivided in four. The terminal grid denominator is 8192; 6,550,288 cells were evaluated across all refinement levels. Every terminal cell certifies (6.3). This is a finite covering proof, not collocation. The status, counts and lower bounds are in adaptive_continuum_certificate.json. The proof need not store every cell because the deterministic subdivision is reproducible from the coefficient file and code.

The independent 512x512 integral enclosure is [2.360128314081601,3.12047551995183], which implies the rational upper bound 25/8. Its pointwise dual bounds are inconclusive; the adaptive calculation, not that coarse grid, supplies (6.3).

This is a computer-assisted interval proof under the stated IEEE arithmetic assumptions. It is not a rational-only certificate and is not formal verification of Python, NumPy or the hardware. The exact algebra verifier is separate. No claim that interval numerics certify all the preceding analytic arguments is made.

## 7. An exact obstruction to inferring joint convexity

For fixed density, (5.1) is convex in the phase gradient as well: varying k changes only one matrix row, a rank-one difference. It is tempting to infer a globally convex two-field problem. That inference is false.

Take the explicitly convex F(A)=|A|^4 and, on the 2pi torus,

    rho=1+a cos(2y),        theta=(2+b)sin y.

For |a|<1 the density is positive and has mean one. The energy is

    E=E_y[rho theta_y^4
          +rho_y^2 theta_y^2/(8rho)
          +rho_y^4/(256rho^3)].                            (7.1)

At (a,b)=(0,0), exact trigonometric averaging gives

    Hessian_(a,b) E = [[1,8],[8,18]],     determinant=-46. (7.2)

Thus the two blocks are individually convex but the joint functional is not. This is a representation obstruction, not a quasiconvexity counterexample: the original F is convex.

A further warning concerns target translations. Any bounded smooth test u can be replaced by u+Re1 without changing Du. As R grows, its phase and phase gradient are O(1/R), while its amplitude is O(R). Therefore small phase alone, with unrestricted amplitude and background scaling, is not a valid new near-line hypothesis. A general negative test could also be placed in that apparently small-phase chart. The small parameter does not remove the quartic coupling.

## 8. Competing counterexample experiments

The experiments were changed from profile-cycle or fixed-Poisson searches to the phase-density variables of the actual map with affine boundary.

On the unit square take the exactly correct reference map u0=c+Bx, with a target translation that keeps it in a half-plane. Let rho0=|u0|^4 and theta0=arg(u0). The trial fields are

    rho=rho0[1+sum a_ij sin(pi i x)sin(pi j y)],
    theta=theta0+sum t_ij sin(pi i x)sin(pi j y),
    u=rho^(1/4)(cos theta,sin theta),

with i,j=1,2,3. They satisfy u=c+Bx on the entire boundary, not just at sampled points. The constraint sum|a_ij|<=0.95 guarantees rho>=0.05rho0 everywhere. It is a convex constraint in the amplitude-density block. At fixed phase the numerical amplitude problem is convex; at fixed amplitude the numerical phase problem is convex. The integral was approximated by 28x28 Gauss quadrature. Therefore search energies and optimizer success flags are exploratory rather than continuum certificates.

Six completed initializations used B=I (two seeds), diag(1,3/2), [[1,1/2],[-3/10,1]], the zero matrix, and diag(2,1/2). Initial phase perturbations varied, and each block was updated to numerical stationarity. No materially negative energy was obtained. All runs returned to or numerically very near the affine baseline. The reported final gaps ranged from approximately 7.7e-13 to 7.3e-10. Such values are numerical zero for this purpose, not certified positive margins and not evidence that the full energy is globally convex. Full coefficients, translations, iteration records and quadrature settings are in dirichlet_search.json.

This is a changed compatibility-preserving operation, but the finite family and alternating algorithm are limited. The l1 bound restricts concentration; three sine modes in each direction restrict geometry; separate convexity does not ensure global joint optimization. The result does not warrant more identical runs. No Fourier random state from an earlier investigation was reused as a purported new negative witness.

The coupled-phase amplitude search used separate Fourier densities with only sampled positivity during discovery. Its numerical output was rounded, and the independent interval calculation in Section 6 then certified the selected positive density and all-amplitude lower bound. Positivity between points was not inferred from the collocation optimization.

The first script uses only SymPy for exact algebra and writes directional_density_verification.json.     107/2, 642, 1605/8, 8445/16, 2815/8, 26865/4.

## 10. Precise remaining obligation and research assessment

The amplitude part of the variational problem is now a convex, attained subproblem with an explicit lower-certificate mechanism, for every prescribed spatial direction. This is a global compatible replacement, not only a theorem for selected carriers. It also supplies a different search operation: a negative test can be sought after globally minimizing its amplitude rather than exploring all vectorial degrees of freedom equally.

What is still missing is a uniform comparison over directions. For affine-boundary data after a target translation, the unresolved inequality is

    min_{rho >=0, rho^(1/4)n|boundary=Bx+c,
         integral rho=M} E_n(rho) >= |Omega| F(B)

for every admissible n and every mass M arising in a test. Equation (5.5) gives an explicit way to certify individual direction problems, including boundary contributions, but no uniform family of fluxes is supplied. Calling the left side a ground-state energy does not establish its sign. Likewise, blockwise convexity plus an affine stationary point is not global positivity, as (7.2) demonstrates.

The profile-storage architecture should no longer receive unconstrained concentrated inference: its local and fixed-Poisson versions have exact obstructions, and repeated finite graphs do not address the continuum requirements. The phase-density replacement is a different, concrete mathematical operation and deserves preservation, but this run does not establish that its unresolved phase optimization is easier in a complexity or discovery-probability sense. Six returns to the affine baseline are not evidence of convergence to a proof.

A productive continuation would need either a universal bound on the scalar dual residual/ground-state energy for admissible phases with affine boundary, a phase operation lowering energy while preserving its constraints, or a negative compatible phase after certified amplitude minimization. Without such a candidate, repeating alternating searches should be stopped and the branch parked for external mathematical review. No unrelated foundational problem is declared superior solely to create a positive ending; this investigation contains no calibrated comparison supporting that claim.
