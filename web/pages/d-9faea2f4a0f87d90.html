## N3. Heisenberg support gives an occupancy criterion

    ||C_j||<=g_j:=||[V,O_j]||+2b_R+3w_R

This implication is stronger than merely putting a commutator on the right: the complete Heisenberg derivative is Hermitian. It does not require J_a to preserve P or P to reduce H. In particular, protection does NOT imply the initial p persists.

## N4. What approximate stationarity really supplies for occupancy

    p_u<=min{1, p+u(r+Lambda delta)/2,

## N5. Initial protection cannot replace occupancy: exact delayed-error example

The generic implication "initial product defect is tiny, hence stationary estimates persist" is false. Consider a logical qubit and a three-level auxiliary g,b,c with

    P=I_L tensor |g><g|,
    J_1=sqrt(epsilon) I_L tensor |b><g|,
    J_2=Z_L tensor |c><b|, 0<epsilon<=1/10,
    rho=(I_L/2) tensor diag(1-2p,p,p), 0<p<epsilon/100.

This rho is full rank and Gibbs for a purely auxiliary Hamiltonian. All N4 protections hold, H commutes every logical Pauli, and delta=0. Initially p_bad=2p, while ||L rho||_1=2epsilon(1-2p). Initial product defects are O(p); the large first jump is logical blind.

For input |+><+|, the exact probability that the second jump has applied a logical Z by time t is

    f(t)=p(1-e^(-t))+(1-2p)
              [1-(e^(-epsilon t)-epsilon e^(-t))/(1-epsilon)].

The decoded state is (1-f(t))|+><+|+f(t)|-><-|, so its trace error is2f(t). At t=1/epsilon, e^(-1)<3/8 and1-epsilon>=9/10 imply the bracket is at least7/12. Also1-2p>499/500. Hence2f(1/epsilon)>3493/3000>1. Thus small initial Gibbs bad mass and initial logical product defect are insufficient when a slow auxiliary escape is followed by a fast logical error. The actual baseline occupancy grows, exactly as N11 requires. This is an analytic counterexample to a general criterion shortcut, not an actual local CSS bath or a KMS-reservoir no-go.
