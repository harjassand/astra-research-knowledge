\[ \boxed{\text{measured covariance}-\text{thermal response covariance}.} \]
Neither covariance needs to have decaying correlations or bounded largest eigenvalue. Their difference can nevertheless be bounded uniformly. That is the cancellation mechanism used here.
Let
\[ H(h)=H_{\mathrm{bg}}+\sum_{a=1}^{m}h_aP_a, \qquad \rho_h=\frac{e^{-\beta H(h)}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H(h)}}. \]
The \(P_a\) are distinct, canonical-sign, nonidentity Pauli strings satisfying
\[ |\operatorname{supp}P_a|\le k, \qquad \max_i\#\{a:i\in\operatorname{supp}P_a\}\le\Delta. \]
As before, candidate incidence includes zero-valued coefficients.
The background is fixed with respect to \(h\). At the operating point, assume the full Hamiltonian has a decomposition
\[ H=\sum_X H_X, \qquad |X|\le\ell, \qquad \max_i\sum_{X\ni i}\|H_X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le g. \]
This permits a background outside the parameter dictionary. It also permits many weak background interactions: bounded combinatorial degree of the background is unnecessary.
The state must be the unrestricted, full-rank Gibbs state at known \(\beta>0\). No commutativity, absence of frustration, high-temperature condition, spectral gap, or correlation-decay assumption is imposed.
On every copy, independently choose \(X\), \(Y\), or \(Z\) uniformly at each site, measure that single-qubit observable, and retain both the basis and outcome.
Allow calibrated independent sign errors with site visibility
\[ \eta_i\in[\eta,1],\qquad \eta>0. \]
A visibility \(\eta_i\) means a sign-flip probability \((1-\eta_i)/2\).
The corresponding unbiased feature for \(P_a\) is
\[ Y_a= \prod_{i\in S_a} \left[ \frac{3s_i}{\eta_i} \,\mathbf1\{b_i=(P_a)_i\} \right], \qquad S_a=\operatorname{supp}P_a. \]
Thus
\[ \mathbb E_hY_a=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_hP_a). \]
This is a local-Pauli classical-shadow measurement, with the noise correction explicit; that measurement primitive is established prior work, not the invention claimed here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08953)

\[ u^TCu= \int_0^1 \operatorname{Tr} \left(\rho^t(A_u)_c\rho^{1-t}(A_u)_c\right)\,dt, \qquad A_u=\sum_a u_aP_a. \]
Let \(F_{\mathrm{loc}}\) be the classical Fisher matrix of the complete recorded measurement outcome, and let \(F_Q\) be the symmetric-logarithmic-derivative quantum Fisher matrix for the equilibrium parameters \(h\).
These concern changes in the Gibbs state as the Hamiltonian parameters change—not unitary phase encoding. Thermal Hamiltonian-parameter quantum Fisher information has been studied previously; the new target here is the information retained by a specified, parameter-independent local measurement. More strongly,
\[ \boxed{ 0\preceq\beta^2C-F_{\mathrm{loc}} \preceq\beta^2\nu I, } \tag{2} \]
and therefore
\[ \boxed{ 0\preceq F_Q-F_{\mathrm{loc}} \preceq\beta^2\nu I. } \tag{3} \]
The constant \(\nu\) is independent of \(n,m\) at fixed physical parameters.
For an extensive collective direction with \(\|u\|_2^2=O(n)\), equation (3) says that even optimal quantum readout can gain at most \(O(n)\) additional Fisher information over this fixed local measurement.
The theorem does not require a lower bound on \(\lambda_{\min}(C)\). It remains valid independently of whether N711 ultimately survives review.

\[ N:=V-V_q\succeq0. \]
This follows directly from positivity of measurement noise. For \(f=u\cdot Y\), \(A=A_u\), and measurement effects \(E_y\), unbiasedness gives
\[ \sum_y f(y)E_y=A. \]
Consequently,
\[ \sum_y(f(y)I-A)E_y(f(y)I-A) = \sum_yf(y)^2E_y-A^2 \succeq0. \]
Taking the state expectation proves \(u^TNu\ge0\).
The crucial cancellation is exact:
\[ \boxed{N_{ac}=0\quad\text{when }S_a\cap S_c=\varnothing.} \tag{5} \]
For disjoint supports, the product measurement reproduces the quantum second moment, including any long-range correlation. It adds no cross-covariance error.
For overlapping supports, direct evaluation gives
\[ |N_{ac}| \le \left(\frac3{\eta^2}\right)^k-1. \]
Each candidate overlaps at most \(1+k(\Delta-1)\) candidates, counting itself. Hence
\[ 0\preceq N\preceq\nu_{\mathrm{meas}}I, \]

This step applies to arbitrary states. It does not assume Gibbs structure or independent quantum outcomes.
For any Hermitian \(A\), the Brooks–Harris inequality gives

This is an established thermal correlation inequality. The variance-minus-susceptibility quantity is also an established object, often called quantum variance. Neither is being claimed as new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.2882)

With \(x=(\log p_i-\log p_j)/2\),

The double-commutator weight is \(4x^2L_{ij}/\beta\), giving (7).
