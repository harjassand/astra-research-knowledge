1. OWN-BAND AVERAGING IS EXACT; NO GLOBAL LABEL UNIFORMITY IS NEEDED.
Let I_n be disjoint complete round-degree bands with cardinal N_n. At the base,
the return permutation of band-n's adjacent-swap program is one N_n-cycle C_n;
all other bands are fixed. Thus one complete stage-m program has return
C_1...C_m, with commuting disjoint supports. For a fixed phase s inside that
program, the prefix maps I_n bijectively to I_n, even if s is inside another
band's loop. Across Q_m repetitions, where Q_m is a multiple of every N_n,
the incoming label visits each position in I_n exactly Q_m/N_n times.
Therefore at every fixed phase its repetition average is precisely the ordered
frequency sum over I_n divided by N_n. Integrating in phase preserves this identity.
The average is bounded by the source min-max estimate on complete round blocks:
 beta_n=(sqrt(C*)/a) [F(M_n)-F(M_(n-1))]/[B_(M_n)-B_(M_(n-1))],

With M_(n-1)/M_n->0, beta_n/M_n->2sqrt(C*)/(3a). This is compatible with
choosing q between that number and 1/sqrt(c), and k_n=ceil(q M_n).

The local adjacent-swap cycle sends its first label through every other label,
and repetitions let every label take that role. Every within-band pair hence
crosses repeatedly with alternating order. Labels from different bands never
coincide when the band boundaries are protected. Frame sign holonomy does not
affect the rank averaging; sign/permutation changes have only finitely many
possibilities for a fixed cutoff and therefore do not require uniformity in n.
