# Cycle 2: source-free odd elasticity and a no-pumping theorem

## Verdict

Under equilibrium passivity, a closed quasistatic cycle cannot deliver net work once every controlled mechanical coordinate is included. This remains true with arbitrary internal coordinates and ideal unilateral contact. For reversible behavior the all-port work is exactly zero; for passive hysteretic/viscoelastic behavior, the work supplied to the complete system is nonnegative and is dissipated. This is a standard consequence of a single-valued free energy and passivity, not a new frontier result.

Shaat and Park's 2023 paper explicitly assumes reversible isothermal behavior and states the pointwise balance \(\delta W=d\psi\) in Eq. (31), p. 14. It then reports a closed-cycle boundary-work output of about \(16.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}\) with zero change in \(\psi\). Those claims cannot both hold for the same complete system. The paper's cycle also changes the circle profile with a rigid slider at two intermediate states. It says an actuating mechanism is required, but excludes that port based on unchanged endpoint stress; it gives no slider force-versus-travel record. That slider is a physically valid omitted work port, but the paper does not establish whether its net work is zero or whether it supplies the claimed output. The alternative is that the assumed constitutive law is non-equilibrium/active and does not obey Eq. (31).

The source itself identifies its odd contribution \(\delta w_{cn}\) as not derivable from potential energy. That is exactly the obstruction: a non-exact work one-form cannot at the same time be the differential of the equilibrium Helmholtz free energy. Unilateral contact can switch active sets and modulate tangent stiffness, but it cannot remove this integrability requirement. If the active contact geometry is externally moved, its generalized actuator work must be counted.

## Theorem and proof

Let the complete configuration be \(q=(x,z,a)\). Here \(x\) denotes externally controlled sample-boundary coordinates (translations, strains, rotations, and spins); \(z\) contains all material internal coordinates; and \(a\) contains any modulated contact geometry, sliders, fixtures, or other moving mechanical parts. The admissible set is specified by unilateral, frictionless constraints
\[
  g_j(q)\ge 0,\qquad \lambda_j\ge 0,\qquad \lambda_jg_j(q)=0.
\]
Assume the complete material-plus-mechanism has a single-valued stored Helmholtz free energy \(\Psi(q)\), is quasistatically equilibrated at every point, has no uncounted energy reservoir or actuator, and all contact is ideal (no friction, impact, or adhesion). Assume a closed cycle returns the complete state, including \(z\), \(a\), contact geometry, and any branch label, to its initial value.

Let \(P\) collect the generalized forces/torques exerted by all external mechanical ports, with positive work defined as work **into** the complete system. Static equilibrium with ideal contact can be written, with a consistent multiplier sign convention, as
\[
  \nabla_q\Psi=P+\sum_j\lambda_j\nabla_q g_j.
\]
Taking the inner product with an admissible quasistatic displacement \(dq\) gives
\[
  d\Psi=P\mathbin{\cdot}dq+\sum_j\lambda_j,dg_j.
\]
For an active contact, \(g_j=0\) and a smooth sustained-contact motion has \(dg_j=0\). For an inactive contact, complementarity gives \(\lambda_j=0\). Thus \(\lambda_jdg_j=0\) almost everywhere on each smooth segment. At ideal quasistatic contact transitions, isolated endpoints contribute no finite work; if a transition has an impulse, finite gap, adhesion, or frictional loss, it is an additional event/port and is outside this reversible statement. Therefore
\[
  \delta W_{\rm all,on}=P\mathbin{\cdot}dq=d\Psi.
\]
Integrating over a closed complete-state cycle yields
\[
  W_{\rm all,on}=\oint\delta W_{\rm all,on}
  =\oint d\Psi=\Psi(q_T)-\Psi(q_0)=0.
\]
The material's net output is the negative of this quantity, so it is also zero. Contact-state changes do not alter the proof: apply it on each smooth active-set segment and telescope the endpoint free energies. A slider is included in \(q\); its conjugate force and travel belong in \(P\mathbin{\cdot}dq\).

An equivalent reduction makes the role of equilibrated internal coordinates explicit. If \(V(x,a)=\min_{z\in K(x,a)}\Psi(x,z,a)\), then on every differentiable equilibrium branch the envelope theorem gives the measured boundary and slider tractions as the derivatives of the same scalar \(V\). The branchwise tangent stiffness is a Hessian and is symmetric. With unilateral contact, \(V\) can be nonsmooth when the active set changes, but it remains a state value and its integral around a closed path is still zero. If local metastable branches, hysteresis, or a changing hidden state invalidate a single-valued equilibrium reduction, that state must be included in \(z\); if it is not restored, the cycle is not closed.

For passive irreversible materials, replace equality with the isothermal passivity balance
\[
  \delta W_{\rm all,on}=d\Psi+\delta D,\qquad \delta D\ge0.
\]
For a cycle returning the complete state, \(W_{\rm all,on}=D\ge0\), so the system cannot deliver positive net work. Viscoelasticity and friction fit this inequality when passive; they add dissipation rather than a work source. If the material stores a non-equilibrium chemical, thermal, electrical, or other resource, its free energy is another state variable/port and must be included.

### Exact scope and exclusions

- **Included:** arbitrary conservative internal coordinates, local elastic/contact energy, multiple contact patches, frictionless unilateral contact, fixed contact geometry, and reversible changes of active contact set, provided the complete state returns.
- **Powered modulation:** a driven slider or time-varying geometry is admissible only when its force-displacement work is counted. Omitting \(\int Q_a\,da\) invalidates a source-free conclusion.
- **Active elements:** motors, batteries, feedback, chemical fuel, biological activity, and externally powered gears violate the passive-material premise unless their reservoir and power are included.
- **Viscoelasticity/friction:** these violate the reversible equality but remain covered by the passive inequality. A periodically driven passive viscoelastic body can dissipate input work; it cannot be a cyclic net-work source without an active/free-energy port.
- **Non-equilibrium:** a thermal or chemical gradient, non-equilibrium internal state, aging reservoir, or maintained odd-elastic constitutive law is outside the equilibrium theorem. Its supply term must be identified before calling output source-free.
- **Inertial/impact effects:** excluded from the quasistatic theorem. Inertia can store and return energy; impacts can dissipate it. A repeated cycle still requires the full kinetic/contact energy balance.

This theorem is a model-level consequence of thermodynamic passivity, not proof that every fabricated contact system has been measured or is free of hidden reservoirs. It does not rule out a driven odd response or transient output from initially stored free energy.

## Audit of Shaat–Park (2023)

Primary source: Mohamed Shaat and Harold S. Park, “Chiral nonreciprocal elasticity and mechanical activity,” *Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids* 171 (2023), 105163, [DOI 10.1016/j.jmps.2022.105163](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2022.105163), [publisher-hosted manuscript PDF](https://people.bu.edu/parkhs/Papers/shaatJMPS2023.pdf). A local source copy and text extraction are in [`sources/`](sources/).

The energy-accounting sequence is explicit in the manuscript:

1. Eq. (31), p. 14, derives \(\delta W=d\psi\) for isothermal reversible deformations and says its proposed passive elastic solids “always satisfy” that relation during reversible interactions.
2. Eq. (32), p. 15, defines local mechanical work from stress and couple-stress power, then decomposes it as \(\delta w=\delta w_p+\delta w_{cn}\). The text says \(\delta w_{cn}\) cannot be obtained from potential energy.
3. The closed-cycle section, pp. 14–17, sets the state-function change to zero but then defines the residual between cumulative work and \(\oint d\psi\) as available mechanical work. For a cycle satisfying Eq. (31) pointwise, that residual is identically zero. The inference is a direct contradiction, not a matter of sign convention.
4. The example uses a 4×4 lattice, \(\alpha_0=30^\circ\), lattice length 20 mm, aluminum ligaments with \(E=70\) GPa, 1.5 mm thickness, 30 mm width, and geometric asymmetry \(\theta_0=60^\circ\). Four cycles use dilation and rotation magnitudes 0.0125. The favorable cycle is reported to yield about \(16.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}\); the opposite-sign contact-switching cycle reports about \(-16\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}\). Appendix A.1 says 40,000 increments are used and computes boundary work from boundary nodal force/moment and displacement/spin histories.
5. To construct the favorable path, the circle profile is changed at states 3 and 4 so that the contact angle changes from 19.7° to 60°. The paper says this is practically done by a rigid slider that moves up and down and “would require a mechanism actuating it.” It excludes its energy because the solid's stress state is unchanged at the endpoints. It provides no slider reaction force, actuator torque, or force–travel integral. Endpoint stress alone is not an energy balance for the actuator path.
6. Appendix A checks ABAQUS's internal-energy/external-work balance for each **deformation process**, while the slider geometry switch is described separately. The reported check therefore does not establish the all-port work balance across those switches. The available manuscript reports FEA and plotted traces, not a raw all-port dataset with slider work.

Two logically distinct repairs are possible, neither retaining the stated source-free equilibrium conclusion. If the complete slider/actuator does net positive work on the specimen, it is an omitted energy source for the reported boundary output. If its net work is zero, the pointwise constitutive work law used to generate nonzero \(\oint\delta W\) cannot also equal the reversible free-energy differential of Eq. (31). More generally, if an unmodeled internal reservoir supplies work, the material is not passive equilibrium in the theorem's sense. The manuscript establishes none of these repairs by reporting the slider work.

## Closest prior art and novelty boundary

- Scheibner et al., “Odd elasticity,” *Nature Physics* 16 (2020), [arXiv:1902.07760](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07760), explicitly treats odd elasticity as active/non-conservative. It states that odd moduli are forbidden by energy conservation, derives cycle work proportional to the enclosed strain-space area, and describes active bond forces. Thus a nonzero odd-elastic cycle integral is already understood as requiring activity or another non-equilibrium work source.
- Rahimi and Park, “Driven Odd Elasticity in Passive Mechanical Metamaterials,” [arXiv:2607.13997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13997), submitted 15 July 2026, is especially close mechanistically. The authors explicitly call their effect **driven**: chiral gears are rotated periodically (reported amplitude 0.01 rad and frequency \(12\pi\)), gear-wall friction is set to 0.1, and tooth-to-tooth recontact after detachment transfers driven shear to a square-lattice sample. The paper states that external periodic mechanical loading is the input energy source. It is a preprint and simulation result, not evidence of source-free pumping. It confirms that passive components can yield odd response when a powered drive is present.
- Shaat and Park's source itself calls the behavior “static thermodynamic non-equilibrium” in §4.1 and its abstract advertises activity. Those descriptions are consistent with a non-equilibrium constitutive mechanism, but inconsistent with the stronger claim that an equilibrated passive body supplies work without an energy source.

The no-pumping theorem above is therefore a correctness boundary and an audit tool, not a novelty claim. A genuine new contribution would require a fully specified non-equilibrium or driven mechanism plus all-port energy closure and measured reproducible performance.

## Decisive raw-data falsifier and costs

No physical sample or raw all-port time series was available in the cited Shaat–Park paper; the reported demonstration is finite-element analysis. The decisive test is a synchronized, cycle-resolved work ledger on the exact favorable cycle, including every slider transition.

Use the on-system sign convention and record, at every increment and each of the two profile changes:

\[
W_{\rm bdy,on}=\oint\sum_b(F_b\cdot dx_b+M_b\,d\theta_b),\qquad
W_{\rm slider,on}=\sum_s\int F_{s,\rm specimen}\,da_s.
\]

Also record contact gaps/angles and reactions, full-field lattice displacement/rotation (DIC or equivalent), internal energy/stress, temperature, and any motor/electrical/thermal input. Compute
\[
R=W_{\rm bdy,on}+W_{\rm slider,on}+W_{\rm other,on}-\Delta\Psi-D.
\]
For a reversible equilibrium cycle, \(\Delta\Psi=0\), \(D=0\), and \(R=0\) within propagated measurement/model uncertainty. For a passive dissipative cycle, \(D\ge0\) and the all-port input must equal \(D\). If the reported \(16.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}\) is boundary work delivered by the sample, then \(W_{\rm bdy,on}\approx-16.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}\); a passive full apparatus requires \(W_{\rm slider,on}+W_{\rm other,on}=16.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,m^{-3}}+D\). Observing the output with all input ports bounded below that amount falsifies the source-free passive account; observing the balancing slider work identifies the hidden drive and closes the contradiction. A nonzero residual larger than uncertainty falsifies the proposed physical model or the measurement/calibration, rather than establishing free energy.

For a computational falsifier, rerun the four-step 4×4 ABAQUS setup with the two slider motions represented as explicit degrees of freedom. Export every-increment actuator reaction×travel, all boundary force/moment×displacement/spin, internal energy, friction/contact dissipation, and residual. The source uses 40,000 increments, so the reported computational scale is about that many recorded increments per cycle, multiplied by the explicit slider dofs and the four sign choices. The source's published FEA does not include publicly supplied slider-work traces.

For a physical falsifier, costs include fabricating or machining the 4×4 aluminum lattice and profile sliders, a two-mode displacement/rotation loading stage, specimen-side slider force sensors (or calibrated actuator torque and transmission ratio), boundary load/torque sensing, DIC, contact-state observation, calibration coupons, repeated closed cycles in both orientations, and uncertainty propagation. The minimum useful physical test must account for all these ports; stress-only endpoint comparison is insufficient. No dollar estimate is given because prices, fabrication route, and instrumentation are unspecified.
