Continuous-time rare-failure estimation without rare-trajectory rejection
The strongest construction from this investigation is a classical randomized algorithm for estimating when a multivariate Gaussian trajectory enters a polyhedral danger region.
For the explicitly supplied finite-spectrum model below, it provides a relative-error probability estimate in
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{poly}\!\left(L,\varepsilon^{-1},\log\delta^{-1}\right)}
\]
bit operations, where \(L\) is the complete input length. There is no multiplicative \(1/p\) factor, where \(p\) is the failure probability, and no time-discretization grid. The construction also produces approximate samples of complete trajectories conditioned on failure.
The central operation is:
Sample a boundary crossing that already satisfies the other failure conditions; reconstruct the corresponding trajectory; correct for the number of ways that trajectory could have been selected.

For \(m\) facets and Fourier degree \(d\), that number is at most \(md+1\). The difficult part is acquiring the boundary-crossing distribution without performing an exponentially unlikely rejection. A convex lifting of the probability flux supplies that operation.
This is a derived, unreviewed algorithmic theorem. Historical originality and practical superiority remain unestablished. Scalar Gaussian rare-event simulation already has strong algorithms; the proposed contribution is the uniform multivariate, polyhedral, continuous-time, finite-bit construction—not the general idea of importance sampling or Rice’s formula.

2. Exact input and output contract
Supplied process
On one period \(0\le t<2\pi\), let
\[
X(t)=\mu+Z_0+
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(A_j\cos jt+B_j\sin jt\bigr)
\in\mathbb R^r.
\tag{1}
\]
The input supplies rational \(\mu\), rational covariance matrices, and independent Gaussian blocks
\[
Z_0\sim N(0,S_0),
\]
\[
\begin{pmatrix}A_j\\B_j\end{pmatrix}
\sim
N\!\left(
0,
\begin{pmatrix}
S_j&T_j\\
-T_j&S_j
\end{pmatrix}
\right),
\qquad
S_j=S_j^\top,\quad T_j=-T_j^\top.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume \(S_0\) and every covariance block in (2) are positive definite. This can be checked by exact rational elimination.
These covariance blocks are invariant under phase rotations, making \(X\) stationary. The skew matrices \(T_j\) permit cross-component phase correlations; the construction does not require independent coordinates or time-reversal symmetry.
The Fourier degree is dense input size. The theorem is polynomial in \(d\), not in the binary logarithm of an arbitrarily large maximum frequency.
Supplied danger region
The input also supplies
\[
\mathcal K=\{x\in\mathbb R^r:a_i^\top x>b_i,\ i=1,\ldots,m\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Exact linear programming detects empty interior and removes redundant inequalities. The interesting case is a nonempty, full-dimensional region with genuine facets. The entire-space case is trivial.
Define
\[
E=\{\exists t\in[0,2\pi):X(t)\in\mathcal K\},
\qquad p=\Pr(E).
\tag{4}
\]
The strict-interior event is deliberate. Hitting a lower-dimensional closed set is not silently identified with entering an open region.
Derived theorem
For \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1/2\), there is a randomized finite-bit algorithm producing a relative approximation to \(p\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\), in time polynomial in
\[
L,\quad \varepsilon^{-1},\quad \log\delta^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
If the region has empty interior, it returns \(p=0\) exactly. Otherwise \(p>0\).
Very small probabilities are returned as a logarithm with certified additive accuracy, or as a binary mantissa and binary-encoded exponent. The algorithm does not write exponentially many leading zeroes.
There is also a conditional trajectory sampler. For requested waveform accuracy \(\zeta\) and distributional error \(\eta\), it returns finite coefficients that can be coupled to a trajectory drawn from \(X\mid E\) so that
\[
\sup_t\|\widehat X(t)-X(t)\|\le\zeta
\]
except with probability \(\eta\). Its expected cost is polynomial in the input, \(\eta^{-1}\), and \(\log\zeta^{-1}\); a polynomial cutoff gives a bounded-time version with an explicit failure outcome.
This is a finite-output approximation guarantee. A distribution on rational coefficients cannot literally have small total-variation distance from a continuous Gaussian law.
3. A bounded number of witnesses replaces inverse failure probability
Let \(N(X)\) count entries into \(\mathcal K\): a facet is crossed inward while the remaining inequalities hold strictly.
Define
\[
W(X)=\mathbf 1_{\{X(0)\in\mathcal K\}}+N(X).
\tag{6}
\]
Almost surely,
\[
E\iff W>0.
\]
Each scalar trigonometric polynomial \(a_i^\top X(t)-b_i\) has at most \(2d\) roots and hence at most \(d\) upcrossings. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
1\le W(X)\le K_*:=md+1
\quad\text{on }E.
}
\tag{7}
\]
The initial-state witness is necessary: a trajectory can remain inside the region throughout the period without crossing a boundary.
Let
\[
\mu_0=\Pr[X(0)\in\mathcal K],
\]
and let \(\mu_i\) be the expected number of entries through facet \(i\). Put
\[
M=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu_i=\mathbb E W.
\tag{8}
\]
Construct a proposal law \(Q\) by mixing:
- the trajectory law conditional on \(X(0)\in\mathcal K\), with weight \(\mu_0/M\);
- the trajectory law seen from an inward crossing of facet \(i\), with weight \(\mu_i/M\), placed at a uniform phase.
The weighted Rice identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{dQ}{dP}(X)=\frac{W(X)}{M}.
}
\tag{9}
\]
One way to derive it is to integrate a narrow strip around each facet, weighted by positive normal velocity, and let the strip width tend to zero. Each simple crossing contributes once. Applying the same calculation with an arbitrary bounded trajectory functional proves the change-of-measure identity, not merely its normalization.
It follows that
\[
\boxed{
p=M\,\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\frac1W\right].
}
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{M}{K_*}\le p\le M.
\]
For the ideal estimator \(Y=M/W\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_QY^2
&=M\,\mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1_E}{W}\right]\\
&\le Mp\\
&\le K_*p^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_QY}{p^2}\le K_*-1=md.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Only
\[
O\!\left(
K_*\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1}
\right)
\tag{12}
\]
independent proposal trajectories are needed.
There is also an exact ideal conditional sampler: propose from \(Q\), then accept with probability \(1/W\). Its accepted law is \(P(\,\cdot\mid E)\), and its acceptance probability is
\[
p/M\ge1/K_*.
\tag{13}
\]
These identities are useful only if \(Q\) and \(M\) can actually be acquired. That is the next step.
4. Acquiring an inward crossing without rare rejection
Fix facet \(i\), and examine time zero. Define
\[
U=a_i^\top X(0),\qquad
V=a_i^\top X'(0).
\]
Delete one coordinate of \(X(0)\) to obtain \(Y\in\mathbb R^{r-1}\). On \(U=b_i\), the missing coordinate is recovered by a rational affine map
\[
x=x_i(Y).
\]
The conditional law
\[
(Y,V)\mid U=b_i
\]
is an explicitly computable, nondegenerate Gaussian with rational mean and covariance. Write its density as \(g_i(y,v)\).
Rice’s formula gives
\[
\mu_i
=
2\pi\,\varphi_U(b_i)
\int
v_+\,
\mathbf1_{\{a_j^\top x_i(y)>b_j,\ j\ne i\}}
g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(\varphi_U\) is the univariate Gaussian density of \(U\).
Why a naive crossing proposal fails
Conditioning only on \(U=b_i\) and \(V>0\), then rejecting until the other inequalities hold, is insufficient.
For independent coordinates and a region requiring all \(r\) coordinates to exceed \(u\), that rejection can cost a factor
\[
\overline\Phi(u)^{-(r-1)}.
\]
The rare-event difficulty has merely moved into proposal generation.
Convex lifting of the flux
Introduce one auxiliary height \(s\), and define
\[
D_i=
\left\{
(y,v,s):
\begin{array}{l}
a_j^\top x_i(y)\ge b_j,\quad j\ne i,\\
v\ge0,\quad0\le s\le v
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then
\[
\int_{D_i}g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv\,ds
=
\int v_+\mathbf1_{\{\text{other inequalities}\}}g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv.
\tag{16}
\]
The set \(D_i\) is convex. The density is the exponential of a negative convex quadratic. Therefore its integral and its normalized distribution are log-concave integration and sampling problems in dimension \(r+1\).
Sampling this distribution and discarding \(s\) yields exactly the required inward-flux law. The other failure inequalities are built into the sampling distribution, rather than tested by rare rejection.
General log-concave integration and sampling are established algorithmic capabilities. Modern work by Kook and Vempala includes acquisition of starting distributions and geometric rounding; it does not require treating a stationary-distribution sampler as free. The construction here must supply the geometric and arithmetic inputs to those algorithms. arXiv
Position and velocity must remain correlated
Stationarity implies that \(U\) and its own derivative \(V\) are uncorrelated. It does not imply that the remaining position coordinates \(Y\) are independent of \(V\).
The covariance matrices \(T_j\) can create precisely those correlations. Replacing \(g_i(y,v)\) by a product of its marginals would generally change the crossing intensity and the proposed trajectory law. The lifted sampler retains the full joint Gaussian.
5. A polynomial-bit implementation of the rare convex integrals
It is not enough to write “sample a truncated Gaussian.” A remote or narrow feasible region may have an extraordinarily small Gaussian mass.
Here is a conservative explicit reduction that establishes polynomial complexity without assuming a favourable initial rejection probability.
Both the initial-region distribution and a lifted facet distribution have the form
\[
Z=\int_D e^{-q(x)}\,dx,
\tag{17}
\]
where \(D\) is a rational polyhedron, \(q\) is a rational convex quadratic, and its sublevel sets on \(D\) are bounded. In the facet case, the height coordinate is bounded by \(0\le s\le v\).
Compute
\[
q_*=\min_{x\in D}q(x),\qquad f=q-q_*.
\]
The minimization is a rational convex quadratic program; its cost is included. The factor \(e^{-q_*}\) is retained in logarithmic form:
\[
Z=e^{-q_*}J,\qquad
J=\int_D e^{-f(x)}dx.
\tag{18}
\]
Uniform energy-tail bound
Let \(x_*\) be a minimizer and \(n_D=\dim D\). Convexity implies, for \(0<a<1\),
\[
\int_D e^{-af(x)}dx
\le
a^{-n_D}\int_D e^{-f(x)}dx.
\]
Indeed, contract \(D\) toward \(x_*\) by factor \(a\), and use
\[
f(x_*+a(x-x_*))\le af(x).
\]
Taking \(a=1/2\) yields
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{e^{-f}/J}[f>t]\le2^{n_D}e^{-t/2}.
}
\tag{19}
\]
The enormous rarity factor \(e^{-q_*}\) has disappeared.
Reduction to a bounded convex body
For an integer \(k_{\mathrm{lift}}\), define
\[
\mathcal B_k=
\left\{
(x,z)\in D\times\mathbb R^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}:
f(x)+k_{\mathrm{lift}}|z_\ell|
\le k_{\mathrm{lift}}
\text{ for every }\ell
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
This is convex, and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal B_k)}{2^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}}
=
\int_D
\left(1-\frac{f(x)}{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}\right)_+^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}dx.
\tag{21}
\]
Choose
\[
t=2\left(n_D\log2+\log\frac8\eta\right),
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{lift}}\ge
\max\left\{2t,\frac{8t^2}{\eta}\right\}.
\]
On \(f\le t\),
\[
e^{-f-t^2/k_{\mathrm{lift}}}
\le
\left(1-\frac f{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}\right)^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}
\le e^{-f}.
\]
Together with (19), this proves a relative \(O(\eta)\) approximation to \(J\). Projecting an approximately uniform sample from \(\mathcal B_k\) onto \(x\) also approximates the desired normalized density.
All geometric inputs are acquired
A strictly feasible rational point is obtained by linear programming. Moving a sufficiently small rational distance from \(x_*\) toward that point gives a centre \(x_c\) with \(f(x_c)<1/8\) and positive constraint slacks.
Those slacks and the quadratic coefficients give an explicit inscribed-ball radius at least
\[
2^{-\operatorname{poly}(L,\log k_{\mathrm{lift}})}.
\]
Coercivity of the original Gaussian quadratic, the bound \(s\le v\), and \(|z_\ell|\le1\) give an explicit containing radius at most
\[
2^{\operatorname{poly}(L,\log k_{\mathrm{lift}})}.
\]
Membership and separation use only rational linear and quadratic evaluations. Established convex-body volume and sampling algorithms therefore apply with polynomial-bit geometric parameters. Their preprocessing, rounding, sampling and confidence amplification are part of the cost. Department of Mathematical Sciences
This conservative implementation has lifted dimension
\[
O\!\left(
\frac{(r+1+\log\eta^{-1})^2}{\eta}
\right).
\tag{22}
\]
It is a complexity proof, not a claim of an efficient production implementation. Direct modern log-concave algorithms operate in the original lifted dimension \(r+1\) and are the stronger practical starting point. The polynomial guarantee does not rely on an uncharged warm start.
6. Reconstructing the trajectory and counting entries without a time grid
Once the local position and inward velocity have been sampled, the rest of the trajectory is acquired by Gaussian regression.
Let \(c\) be the complete Fourier-coefficient vector, with covariance \(\Sigma\), and let \(H_{\mathrm{obs}}c=b_{\mathrm{obs}}\) encode the sampled position and normal velocity. Draw an independent original Gaussian coefficient vector \(c_0\), and set
\[
\boxed{
c=
c_0+
\Sigma H_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top
(H_{\mathrm{obs}}\Sigma H_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top)^{-1}
(b_{\mathrm{obs}}-H_{\mathrm{obs}}c_0).
}
\tag{23}
\]
This gives the exact conditional Gaussian law in the ideal-real construction. All regression matrices are computed from the input; their inversion and multiplication costs are charged.
For a facet proposal, rotate the trajectory by an independent uniform phase. Stationarity makes this the required unanchored crossing law. The initial-region component is not randomly rotated.
A finite implementation can preserve the planted facet equality exactly: round latent inputs first, then apply the rational affine parameterization and regression. Uniform phase can be generated from two independent Gaussian variables \(g,h\), using
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{h^2-g^2}{h^2+g^2},
\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{2gh}{h^2+g^2}.
\tag{24}
\]
For rational \(g,h\), these identities preserve the unit-circle relation exactly.
Exact crossing count for finite coefficients
Put \(z=\tan(t/2)\). For each facet,
\[
P_i(z)
=
(1+z^2)^d
\left[a_i^\top X(2\arctan z)-b_i\right]
\tag{25}
\]
is a rational polynomial of degree at most \(2d\).
At a simple real root, the signs of the other \(P_j\) determine whether the point lies on the actual polyhedral boundary, and the derivative sign determines whether the crossing is inward.
Sturm sequences and exact sign determination therefore compute \(W\) in polynomial bit complexity. The point \(t=\pi\), corresponding to infinity in this parameterization, is handled separately. Exact real-root isolation is established algebraic computation, not another assumed oracle. arXiv
In an anchored facet proposal, one root is planted at \(z=0\). That factor is removed before discriminant tests; it is not mistakenly treated as a numerical degeneracy.
Why arbitrary coefficient rounding is unsafe
A path can make an extremely narrow excursion:
\[
f(t)=(1+2^{-b})\cos(t-\phi)-1.
\]
Its positive interval has width of order \(2^{-b/2}\). A fixed grid can miss it, and careless rounding can remove it.
The finite-bit proof must therefore control changes in crossing topology under the proposal distribution, not merely assert that tangencies have probability zero.
7. Finite precision without an inverse-probability penalty
The relevant bad events are algebraic: a multiple root, simultaneous crossings of two facets, a root at an endpoint used by the counter, or degeneration of the phase parameterization.
After eliminating the deliberately planted equality, these events are zeros of nonzero polynomials in the sampled latent variables. Their degrees and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in \(L,m,d\). Full positive-definite Fourier blocks and genuine facets ensure that no additional crossing degeneracy is forced.
Two estimates make finite precision sufficient.
First: the proposal density is not exponentially large in the rarity exponent
For each local distribution, subtracting \(q_*\) gives density proportional to \(e^{-f}\). The constructed interior ball has polynomial-bit radius and \(f\le1\), so its normalizer is at least
\[
2^{-\operatorname{poly}(L)}.
\]
Hence the normalized density is at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\). Gaussian regression variables obey analogous bounds.
Equation (19) and Gaussian tails confine the latent variables to a box of polynomial-bit radius except with any prescribed small probability.
Crucially, this bound does not divide an unconditional estimate by the original tiny facet probability.
Second: a polynomial is unlikely to be extremely close to zero
For a nonzero univariate polynomial with leading coefficient \(c\) and degree \(h\),
\[
\operatorname{length}\{x:|P(x)|\le u\}
\le
2h(u/|c|)^{1/h}.
\]
To see this, factor over the complex numbers. If the product of the \(h\) root distances is at most \(u/|c|\), at least one distance is at most \((u/|c|)^{1/h}\).
Conditioning successively on variables gives a corresponding multivariate bound. Comparing the bounded joint density on the latent box with uniform measure produces, for each nonzero bad-event polynomial \(P\),
\[
\Pr(|P|\le u)
\le
2^{\operatorname{poly}(L,m,d)}
u^{1/\operatorname{poly}(m,d)}
\tag{26}
\]
after incorporating its polynomial-bit coefficient lower bound.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
b_{\mathrm{work}}
=
\operatorname{poly}\!\left(
L,m,d,\log(K_*/\varepsilon)
\right)
}
\tag{27}
\]
bits suffice to preserve the required crossing count except with probability \(O(\varepsilon/K_*)\).
Polynomial derivative bounds on the same box turn the small-ball estimate into a rounding-stability bound. No minimum crossing separation is supplied as a promise.
For a conditional waveform output, adding precision polynomial in \(\log\zeta^{-1}\) controls the requested uniform waveform error.
8. Complete algorithm, error allocation and resource costs
The finite algorithm is:
Check covariance positivity and the danger-region interior.
Remove redundant facets.

Compute Gaussian regression data for the initial-region and facet components.
Approximate each component mass μ_i to relative error O(ε).
Construct its log-concave sampler.

Repeat O((md+1) ε⁻² log(1/δ)) times:
    choose a component using its normalized estimated mass;
    sample its local position/velocity variables;
    reconstruct a conditional Fourier trajectory;
    apply a uniform phase only for a facet component;
    count its entries algebraically;
    accumulate 1/W, treating a flagged numerical failure as score zero.

Return log(sum μ_i) + log(mean score).

Component masses and mixture probabilities are computed in the logarithmic domain. Terms too small to affect the error budget may be omitted with a charged total-mass bound; extremely small exponentials need not be written in fixed-point form.
Relative mass errors of \(O(\varepsilon)\) cause only relative \(O(\varepsilon)\) error in the ideal mixture. Component sampling and crossing-count errors are set to \(O(\varepsilon/K_*)\), because
\[
\mathbb E_Q(1/W)\ge1/K_*.
\]
This distinction prevents an unnecessarily severe precision requirement.
Let \(D_c=r(2d+1)\) be the coefficient dimension. Let \(I_{\mathrm{conv}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{conv}}\) denote the paid costs of the explicit convex integration and sampling constructions above—not free oracle calls.
A conservative bound is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{total}}
\le{}&
\operatorname{poly}(L)
+(m+1)I_{\mathrm{conv}}
\\
&+
O\!\left(
K_*\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1}
\right)
\left[
S_{\mathrm{conv}}
+\operatorname{poly}(D_c,m,d,b_{\mathrm{work}})
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{28}
\]
Every term is polynomial in the stated input and accuracy parameters. The conservative convex-body construction can have a high polynomial overhead; no near-linear or low-degree runtime is claimed.
For a strict worst-case polynomial execution bound, expected-time sampling subroutines are capped using their proved expectation bounds. Their small timeout probability is included in the bounded-score error budget. Confidence in the integration stage is amplified by independent repetitions, giving logarithmic dependence on \(\delta^{-1}\), rather than paying \(1/\delta\).
Memory. Dense regression preprocessing can conservatively store \(O((m+1)D_c^2)\) rational numbers, plus the convex-sampler workspace. Proposal trajectories are processed one at a time. No table of all time points or all Monte Carlo trajectories is required.
Randomness. All draws are implemented with finite fair-bit procedures at the precision in (27). There is no exact-real random-number oracle in the bit-complexity claim.
Outputs. A probability estimate occupies polynomially many bits in logarithmic or scientific notation. One conditioned trajectory has \(O(rd)\) coefficients. Requesting every entry time or a densely sampled waveform additionally pays for that output.
Acquisition. The covariance matrices, mean and facets are explicit inputs. The algorithm does not infer them from measurements or certify a physical Gaussian approximation for free.
9. Strongest consequence: probability and conditioned failure histories
The construction supports two distinct scientific outputs.
First, it estimates continuous-time entry probabilities for a supplied multivariate spectral model. This includes simultaneous threshold failures such as
\[
X_1(t)>u_1,\ldots,X_r(t)>u_r
\]
and general linear combinations representing prescribed danger modes.
Second, inverse-multiplicity acceptance produces trajectories conditioned on entering the danger region. In the ideal construction,
\[
\Pr(\text{accept})=p/M\ge1/K_*,
\]
so conditioned sampling costs at most \(K_*\) proposals on average—not \(1/p\).
The sampled crossing is not necessarily the first crossing. The complete waveform must be examined to obtain a first-entry record or a sequence of recrossings.
A polynomially supplied union of polyhedral danger regions is also covered. Count initial-state and entry witnesses separately for each region, even if the regions overlap. The same correction works with
\[
K_*
=
\sum_{\ell}
(1+m_\ell d).
\tag{29}
\]
This permits an explicit list of failure modes without inclusion–exclusion over their overlaps. It does not permit an exponentially expanded Boolean description to be treated as a short list.

11. Scope and remaining uncertainty
The guarantee concerns the supplied stationary finite Fourier model. It does not automatically transfer to nonstationary dynamics, arbitrary infinite-bandwidth processes, singular spectral covariances, nonlinear failure boundaries, or microscopic physical models that have not been reduced to the stated input.
Spectral truncation deserves particular caution. A small absolute model error need not give a small relative error in an extremely rare probability. This algorithm does not make that certification free.
Likewise, the polynomial dependence on dense degree \(d\) is essential. A frequency of size \(2^b\) supplied succinctly does not receive a polynomial-in-\(b\) crossing-count guarantee here.
The construction evaluates a supplied model or an explicitly supplied list of designs. It does not globally optimise arbitrary design spaces, learn unknown covariances, or give relative accuracy for every subsequent rare condition imposed on an accepted trajectory.
Established outcome
The surviving mechanism is
\[
\boxed{
\text{convex acquisition of inward boundary flux}
\;+\;
\text{bounded entry multiplicity}
\;+\;
\text{algebraic continuous-time counting}.
}
\]
Together, these give a polynomial-time construction for multivariate continuous-time polyhedral failure probabilities and failure-conditioned trajectories, with acquisition and precision charged rather than assumed.
The unresolved questions are whether this complete theorem is historically new, how its best implementation compares with specialized reliability methods, and how far the finite-spectrum model can be certified for demanding physical workloads. The mathematical construction does not currently depend on an unresolved committor, an unprovided rare-event sampler, or an uncharged fine time grid.
