## Question and exact interface

Fix integers n,m >= 1 and a characteristic-zero field F. A polynomial f in F<x1,...,xn> is supplied only through exact full-matrix evaluations. Its coefficients admit

\[
 f_w=uB_wv,
 \qquad B_{i_1\cdots i_k}=B_{i_1}\cdots B_{i_k},
\]

where u is a constant 1-by-m row, v is a constant m-by-1 column, and every B_i is strictly upper triangular. This is the acyclic coefficient-representation convention of the source's Proposition 5.1. An acyclic ABP with affine-linear edge labels and m vertices satisfies this convention after eliminating scalar edges; **m counts vertices, not edges or coefficient bits**. No cyclic representation, arbitrary shared circuit, inverse gate, numerical oracle, or unknown-model learning guarantee is claimed.

The generator may use only n,m. It does not read u,B_i,v, target coefficients, support, or a formula tree. The response is the **complete d-by-d evaluated matrix**. The first column alone has a different lower bound and does not support the result below.

## Conservative theorem

Set d=2nm and, with indices 0 <= r,q < d, define

\[
 (T_i)_{r,q}=
 \begin{cases}
 (-1)^{r-q-1}/(r i^{r-q}),&q<r,\\
 0,&q\ge r.
 \end{cases}
\]

**Claim.** Every nonzero f with the acyclic m-state representation above satisfies f(T_1,...,T_n) != 0. The matrices are the source's equation (13), at the smaller dimension d. Their deterministic construction is polynomial in n,m in bit operations.

For the source's formula model (ordered fan-in-two + and multiplication trees with all leaves and gates counted), Lemma 3.1 supplies m <= 2s. Thus d=4ns is sufficient uniformly for size at most s, using the full response. The source's d=2ns^2 guarantee uses only one column.

The hostile audit supplies the optional refinement d=2n(m-1) for m>=2: A(t)v lies in the invariant subspace spanned by the first m-1 basis vectors, and the same kernel argument runs on that subspace. The final rational vector still has denominator q and numerator degree at most n-1. Constants when m=1 need dimension one. This improves a constant term in the state count, not the order; the frozen experiments and the cyclic theorem retain the conservative d=2nm.

## Complete proof

### 1. The input-dependent Volterra kernel

Put

\[
 A(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n B_i/(z+i)=P(z)/q(z),
 \quad q(z)=\prod_{i=1}^n(z+i),
 \quad \deg P\le n-1.
\]

In characteristic zero q(0)=n! is nonzero. Because B_i are strictly upper triangular, products of length at least m vanish. Consequently

\[
 G(z)=\sum_{|w|<m}B_wh_w(z)
\]

is a finite sum with G(0)=I, G'=AG, and G invertible in Mat_m(F[[z]]).

If f's constant coefficient f_empty=uv is nonzero, all nonempty products of the strictly lower triangular T_i are strictly lower triangular, and the diagonal of f(T) is f_empty. Detection is immediate. Hence assume f_empty=0.

For any a in F[[z]], define

\[
 H_a(z)=\sum_{1\le |w|<m}B_wJ_wa(z).
\]

Termwise differentiation, in the correct left-letter order, gives

\[
 H_a'=A H_a+A aI,\qquad H_a(0)=0.
\]

Variation of constants over formal power series therefore gives

\[
 f(J)a=uH_av
 =\int_0^z K(z,t)a(t)\,dt,
 \qquad K(z,t)=uG(z)G(t)^{-1}A(t)v.
 \tag{1}
\]

All coefficients of K lie in F[[z,t]]: A is regular at zero and G(t) is formally invertible there. Since f is nonzero, the finite faithfulness lemma gives f(J)1=h_f !=0. Equation (1) then implies **K !=0**.

### 2. A short diagonal derivative detects K

Define rational rows

\[
 R_0=u,\qquad R_{j+1}=R_j'+R_jA.
\]

Induction gives (uG)^(j)=R_jG and, writing R_j=Z_j/q^j,

\[
 Z_0=u,\qquad
 Z_{j+1}=qZ_j'-j q'Z_j+Z_jP,
 \qquad \deg Z_j\le j(n-1).
 \tag{2}
\]

The degree statement includes n=1: the Z_j are constant. All R_j are regular at zero.

Since u !=0, choose k <= m so that R_0,...,R_(k-1) are independent over F(z) and R_k is their first dependent row. Let V be their rational span. It is stable under the covariant derivative D_A(R)=R'+RA. Indeed D_A(R_j)=R_(j+1), and the Leibniz rule

\[
 D_A(cR)=c'R+cD_A(R)
\]

makes the derivative of every rational combination lie in V. Inductively every R_j lies in V.

For fixed t, differentiate (1) in z and then restrict to z=t:

\[
 \left.\partial_z^jK(z,t)\right|_{z=t}
 =R_j(t)A(t)v=:b_j(t).
 \tag{3}
\]

If b_j were zero for every j<k, stability would make every b_j zero. Then the formal Taylor expansion

\[
 K(t+x,t)=\sum_{j\ge0}b_j(t)x^j/j!
\]

would vanish identically. The substitution (z,t)=(t+x,t) is an invertible linear substitution in the formal power-series ring, so this would contradict K !=0. Thus some j<k<=m has b_j !=0.

By (2),

\[
 b_j(t)=Z_j(t)P(t)v/q(t)^{j+1}.
\]

Its nonzero polynomial numerator has degree at most (j+1)(n-1), while its denominator is a unit at zero. Hence ord_0 b_j <= (j+1)(n-1). The expansion in (x,t) contains a nonzero term of total degree at most

\[
 j+(j+1)(n-1)=n(j+1)-1\le nm-1.
 \tag{4}
\]

Invertible linear changes of variables preserve the first nonzero total homogeneous degree. Therefore the first nonzero homogeneous part of K(z,t) has some degree ell-1 <= nm-1, i.e. 1 <= ell <= nm.

### 3. Full matrix entries recover that homogeneous kernel part

Write that part as

\[
 K_{\ell-1}(z,t)=\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-1}c_b z^{\ell-1-b}t^b,
 \qquad (c_0,...,c_{\ell-1})\ne0.
\]

Apply (1) to a(t)=t^q. The coefficient at output degree q+ell is

\[
 [z^{q+\ell}]f(J)z^q
 =\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{c_b}{q+b+1}.
 \tag{5}
\]

No higher homogeneous part contributes at that degree. Since J_i preserves the quotient modulo z^d, (5) equals the matrix entry f(T)_(q+ell,q) whenever q+ell<d.

For q=0,...,ell-1 these entries apply the ell-by-ell Cauchy matrix C_(q,b)=1/(q+b+1) to c. It is invertible in every characteristic-zero field. A self-contained proof: if g(X)=sum_b c_b/(X+b+1) vanishes at X=0,...,ell-1, its numerator has degree at most ell-1 and ell distinct roots, so it is zero. Evaluation at X=-b-1 then gives c_b times a nonzero product of distinct integer differences, so every c_b is zero, a contradiction.

Thus some entry is nonzero. Its largest possible row is 2ell-1<=2nm-1=d-1. This proves the candidate theorem.

## Construction and response costs

The matrices have n*d*(d-1)/2 nonzero rational entries, each with numerator +/-1 and denominator bit length at most O(log d+d log(n+1)). The source's direct integer-power construction gives polynomial bit time and output length at the smaller d. The theorem concerns generator construction and exact response semantics; coefficient encoding and oracle computation are still charged separately.

The old proof retains one column of dimension Theta(nm^2), whereas the new proof retains a complete matrix with Theta(n^2 m^2) field elements. At fixed n both retain order m^2 information. The proved gain is the dimension of each evaluation matrix and its dense query description: the number of input entries changes from order m^4 to order m^2. Under the original complete-matrix oracle both input and output dimensions shrink; under a redesigned selective-column oracle, the old and new response counts are different and no response-order improvement follows automatically. No learner/sample-count reduction follows from the dimension theorem alone.

For downstream residual extraction, one seed is insufficient. A full-matrix residual of width d can be exposed by prefix layers of width d with identity edges, costing dimension (k+1)d for a prefix of length k, or by d seeded scalar-chain queries of dimension d+k. Thus an old count of one query per prefix at dimension D+k cannot be transferred unchanged to dimension d+k merely by substituting the new d. This boundary was independently reconstructed by frontier_analysis.

A predetermined similarity also gives integer matrices: let L=lcm(1,...,n), D_rr=r! L^r. Then

\[
 (DT_iD^{-1})_{r,q}
 =(-1)^{r-q-1}\frac{(r-1)!}{q!}(L/i)^{r-q}
\]

is integral and has bit length O(d log d+d log L). All complete matrix values are conjugated by D, preserving detection. This is a standard similarity corollary, not a floating-point stability result.

Each original T_i applies to a vector via y_0=0, y_r=(v_(r-1)-(r-1)y_(r-1))/(ir); the integer version has y_r=(L/i)(v_(r-1)-(r-1)y_(r-1)). A supplied formula tree can use these recurrences in O(sd) arithmetic operations to compute **one** f(T)v without expanding words. Computing all d seed responses this way costs O(sd^2) arithmetic. Exact bit growth, scalar leaf heights, and memory must still be charged. This is a structured-operator implementation interface; it is not the original dense black-box query interface. White-box identity testing already has polynomial-time prior methods.

## Why the earlier first-column bound cannot simply be improved

For t>=1, every binary homogeneous polynomial of degree 2t has an acyclic affine-edge ABP with m_t=3*2^t-2 vertices. Take a binary prefix trie of depth t-1 (2^t-1 states), a reversed suffix trie of depth t (2^(t+1)-1 states), and connect each prefix leaf u to each suffix leaf v with the affine-linear edge label a_(u1v)x1+a_(u2v)x2. Every word coefficient is independently selectable. Thus the entire 4^t-dimensional coefficient space belongs to the m_t-state class.

For any fixed matrix tuple and seed e, the map f -> f(T)e is linear into F^d. Universal detection on this vector space requires d>=4^t=(m_t+2)^2/9. A complete matrix response only implies d^2>=4^t, hence d>=2^t. The candidate d=2nm is therefore **order-optimal in m at fixed n for full matrix responses**, while the source's quadratic first-column dimension is also order-optimal for that different response interface. This is not an arbitrary shared-circuit lower bound.
