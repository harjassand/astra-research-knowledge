# Weighted multiplicity-gap extension: independent reconstruction

Status: **the stated functional inequality passes a complete derivation**, with exact repairs to finite coefficient counting and Hasse top-degree handling. This is a consequence of the Kumar–Mon mechanism, proposed by r14 and reconstructed here. External novelty is UNKNOWN; weighted finite-field maximal inequalities have substantial older prior art.

## Exact functional statement

Let q be a prime power, 1<=r<=n, and f:F_q^n->R_(>=0). Suppose a fraction at least delta>0 of the r-dimensional linear subspaces V admit an affine shift b+V with average f at least beta>0. With uniform counting probability on F_q^n, define

\[
c=\|f\|_{L_n}=\left(q^{-n}\sum_xf(x)^n\right)^{1/n}.
\]

Then

\[
c\ge\frac{\delta q^r}{\delta q^r+q-1}\,\beta.
\]

The exponent is the ambient dimension n. It cannot silently be replaced by a fixed low moment or an efficiently acquired source statistic.

## Polynomial upper degree bound with strict coefficient surplus

For integer M tending to infinity, impose Hasse multiplicity m_x=ceil(M f(x)) at every point. There are at most

\[
B_M=\sum_x\binom{m_x+n-1}{n}
\]

linear coefficient constraints. Choose the smallest D_M for which binom(D_M+n,n)>B_M, strictly. Then a nonzero polynomial P_M exists with degree d_M<=D_M and all required multiplicities. Since the finite set of f values is fixed,

\[
B_M=\frac{M^n}{n!}\sum_xf(x)^n+O(M^{n-1}),\qquad
\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{D_M}{M}=\left(\sum_x f(x)^n\right)^{1/n}=qc.
\]

Here f is nonzero because beta,delta>0. Zero multiplicity imposes zero constraints. Strict inequality is essential for finite M; equality of constraints and variables does not guarantee a nonzero solution.

## Extend the good-direction fraction to all matrix ranks

For a random n-by-r matrix V of full column rank, its span is a uniform r-subspace, so a delta fraction admit a good shift. Conditional on rank s<r, complete its uniform s-dimensional span Lambda to a uniform r-subspace Lambda'. Uniformity follows by the same Gaussian-binomial flag argument as in the linear-disperser audit. At least delta of the rank-s matrices therefore admit some good completion.

Tile a good affine r-space into q^(r-s) cosets of Lambda. At least one tile has average f>=beta. Each point of that tile has q^(r-s) preimages under t->b+Vt. Thus for a delta fraction of all matrices, at every rank, some b satisfies

\[
\sum_{t\in F_q^r}f(b+Vt)\ge q^r\beta.
\]

This establishes the needed statement for all q^(nr) matrices without paying a full-rank conditioning factor.

## Degree lower bound over a rational function field

Fix P=P_M of actual degree d and one such good V,b. Its Hasse derivative of order vector i, w=|i|, has degree at most d-w and multiplicity at least m_x-w at x; when that lower bound is negative it remains a valid lower bound. Pulling back by the affine map cannot decrease multiplicity. Hence Q_i(t)=P^(i)(b+Vt) has total multiplicity at least

\[
q^r(M\beta-w).
\]

Multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel forces Q_i to be the zero polynomial whenever q(M beta-w)>d-w. If d>=qM beta the desired degree lower bound below is already trivial. Otherwise put

\[
h=\left\lceil\frac{qM\beta-d}{q-1}\right\rceil>0.
\]

Every w<h satisfies the strict vanishing condition. Let P_top be P's degree-d homogeneous part. The degree-(d-w) homogeneous component of Q_i is (P_top)^(i)(Vt), so this is identically zero. This formulation remains valid if a leading Hasse derivative vanishes identically in positive characteristic; it does not assert the generally false equality between the highest nonzero part of the whole derivative and the derivative of the highest part.

View P_top as a nonzero polynomial over the function field F_q(t_1,...,t_r). The set

\[
T=\{u\cdot t:u\in F_q^r\}
\]

has q^r distinct elements. Its n-fold product corresponds bijectively to all n-by-r matrices V. On at least a delta fraction of T^n, every Hasse derivative of order less than h vanishes, so P_top has multiplicity at least h there. A second multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel bound gives

\[
d\ge\delta h q^r\ge
\delta q^r\frac{qM\beta-d}{q-1},\qquad
\frac dM\ge\frac{q\delta q^r\beta}{\delta q^r+q-1}.
\]

Combining this with d<=D_M, taking M to infinity and dividing by q proves the functional inequality.

## A useful tail form and the old comparator

Define the affine r-plane maximal average M_r f(V)=max_b average_(x in b+V) f(x). For beta>c the inequality rearranges to

\[
\Pr_V[M_r f(V)\ge\beta]\le
\min\left(1,\frac{(q-1)c}{q^r(\beta-c)}\right).
\]

In particular, Pr[M_r f>=(1+tau)c]<=(q-1)/(tau q^r). This supplies explicit constants for nonnegative weighted coset loads. A uniform linear hash kernel is a uniform subspace, so the tail statement can be used in weighted linear-hashing analyses. Acquiring or upper-bounding c may itself require reading a large support; no acquisition advantage follows from the theorem alone.

Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao arXiv:0903.1879v2, Theorem1.3 already gives a weighted strong L_n Kakeya maximal inequality for **lines** with dimension-dependent constants C_n. In normalized form it gives a line-tail lower bound of order C_n^(-1) delta^(1/n) beta (up to the explicit finite-field projective-direction normalization). That dependence can be stronger in a small-delta regime. The current multiplicity consequence has an explicit rational constant, treats r-planes, and is particularly suggestive when delta q^r is large. It is not established here as an improvement over all known weighted higher-plane maximal estimates.

For f=1_S+(1-epsilon)1_(S complement), a good r-plane has beta=alpha+(1-epsilon)(1-alpha), and c^n=|S|/q^n+(1-|S|/q^n)(1-epsilon)^n. Substitution recovers Kumar–Mon Theorem2.8 exactly. Thus the functional statement is a faithful generalization of the read mechanism, not a different unexplained theorem.

## Source repairs and novelty boundary

Kumar–Mon arXiv:2609.17020v1,15September2026, Section2.1 and Section2.2, Lemma2.9, Proposition2.11 and proof of Theorem2.8 were read. Lemma2.9's finite existence statement as literally printed uses non-strict coefficient comparison and is false in that form: q=2,n=1,S=F_2,M=1 selects d=1 because there are two constraints and two variables, but no nonzero polynomial of degree at most1 vanishes at both0 and1. Choosing strict surplus repairs this and changes the asymptotic degree ratio by o(1). The displayed Hasse degree equality and highest-part identity likewise need the degree-at-most/fixed-degree-component formulations above. These issues do not invalidate the repaired limiting argument.

The authors' multiplicity-gap mechanism is the primary contribution. General nonnegative multiplicity assignments and weighted maximal estimates have older roots; novelty of this exact rational functional form requires further primary-literature comparison. No numerical experiment establishes its novelty or optimality. A concrete next step is to compare higher-plane weighted maximal bounds in the large delta q^r regime, or construct examples showing whether the displayed constant can be attained for genuine weights beyond the two-level sources.
