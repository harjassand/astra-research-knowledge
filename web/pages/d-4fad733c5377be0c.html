# Why a second dominant wave does not automatically remove folds

Let a>0 and 1/2<lambda<1 be constant. The leading frozen field

    F0(r,t,u)=exp(-ar)[lambda cos(at)+(1-lambda)cos(au)]

is harmonic in (r,t,u). The plane u=0 is gradient-invariant. On it put zeta=at and c=(1-lambda)/lambda<1. Along a gradient trajectory with sin zeta>0,

    dr/dzeta=[cos zeta+c]/[a sin zeta],
    ar=log(sin zeta)+c log(tan(zeta/2))+constant.       (1)

The radial turn is zeta*=arccos(-c), where F0=0 and F0_t is nonzero. Both branches tend to r=-infinity: near zeta=0 the right side is (1+c)log zeta+O(1), and near zeta=pi it is (1-c)log(pi-zeta)+O(1). At an inner radial section a fold of height H therefore has endpoint phase sensitivities of order

    exp[-aH/(1+c)] and exp[-aH/(1-c)].                 (2)

Thus dominant second-wave mixing still has a folded return on its exact leading symmetry plane. It changes the two contraction rates, rather than making the leading geometry radial-throughgoing. Since F0 is harmonic, its conductivity cocycle is identically zero; (1)-(2) alone are not an actual h obstruction.

The inactive u derivative has a further distinction: in the radial equation u'=F0_u/F0_r its linear coefficient at u=0 is

    a(1-lambda)/[lambda cos zeta+(1-lambda)].

This is positive at the positive crest and negative at the negative crest. Hence backward radial integration contracts u on one branch and expands it on the other.
