## Question, interface and sources

Given copies of an unknown faithful state rho on C^d and known Hermitian generators A_a, define S_a(rho) by -i[A_a,rho]={rho,S_a(rho)}/2. Does matching these operator scores identify rho? If not, what precise supplementary information repairs the loss, and at what cost?

The interface supplies copies, known generators and candidate Hamiltonian descriptions. It does not supply the target density matrix/eigenbasis. Explicit finite-dimensional certificates below can use a supplied candidate tau. Such matrix computations are exponential in the number of qubits unless separately structured.

Dong–Leng, [2609.28391v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28391v1), 2026-09-23, Definition2/Lemma6 and AppendixA.1/Theorem15 establish the SLD definition, copy-expectation objective, and sufficient dressed-frame commutant condition. Theorems9–11 guarantee local X/Z identification and efficient learning at high temperature. Lemma16 gives the ordinary-irreducibility sufficient condition for endpoint scores. These are author claims, correctly scoped; the collisions below do not contradict them. Our full relevant-section HTML snapshot is `sources/team_b_quantum/2609.28391v1.html`.

The modular SLD tanh formula predates the seed: Jiang 1310.2687v3 §II Eq2.20, following Knysh–Durkin. Commutant-versus-gap methods are standard in quantum Markov theory (Carlen–Maas 1609.01254v3 Theorem5.3). See `leads/b_prior.md` for verified versions/locations and metadata. Exact fiber geometry reverses the seed proof rather than creating a new score definition.

## Exact fiber, not just a sufficient condition

For a faithful candidate tau, let

K_a = tau^(-1/2)(A_a-i S_a(tau)/2)tau^(1/2).

In an eigenbasis tau=diag(t_i), K_a,ij=2sqrt(t_i t_j)/(t_i+t_j) A_a,ij, hence K_a is Hermitian. Let C={K_a}' be its complex *-commutant and m=dim_C C=dim_R C_Herm.

**Theorem.** The entire faithful equal-score fiber is

F_tau={tau^(1/2) X tau^(1/2): X in C, X>0, tr(tau X)=1}.

Proof: fixing S=S_a(tau), its equation for rho rearranges to (A-iS/2)rho=rho(A+iS/2). Substitute rho=tau^.5 X tau^.5 and use the defining Lyapunov equation for tau to obtain [K_a,X]=0. Conversely that commutation reverses the algebra; positivity of rho makes the Lyapunov solution unique. Trace/positivity are exactly the displayed constraints. Thus the fiber has real dimension m-1 locally at X=I, and unrestricted identification holds iff C=CI. If C is nonscalar, any B=B* in C with tr(tau B)=0 yields rho_t=tau^.5(I+tB)tau^.5 for sufficiently small real t. In a restricted Hamiltonian model, this fiber must intersect the model before nonidentification follows.

For nonsymmetric lambda scores, the exact faithful fiber uses the *-commutant {K_lambda,a,K_lambda,a^*}', because X is Hermitian. The seed's scalar commutant for K_lambda alone is sufficient; the *-commutant condition is necessary and sufficient. Do not discard this distinction at endpoints.

## Repair 1: optimally many scalar moments

Choose a Frobenius-orthonormal Hermitian basis E_0=I/sqrt(d),E_1,...,E_{m-1} of C. Adding the m-1 residuals tr[(rho-tau)E_j] to the score loss removes exactly the ambiguity.

Proof: on the fiber put X=I+Y, Y in C_Herm. Define Phi_C(Y)=P_C(tau^.5 Y tau^.5), where P_C is Frobenius orthogonal conditional expectation. Then

<Y,Phi_C(Y)>=tr(Y tau^.5 Y tau^.5)=||tau^.25 Y tau^.25||_F^2 >= t_min ||Y||_F^2.

Thus Phi_C is positive definite/invertible. Equality of all moments, including the known normalization moment E_0, implies Phi_C(Y)=0 and Y=0. Conversely fewer than m-1 scalar linear moments cannot identify a neighborhood in the fiber: their restrictions have a nonzero kernel on {Y in C_Herm:tr(tau Y)=0}; positivity is open at I, so a small nonzero kernel perturbation is a genuine faithful competing state. This proves minimality for linear scalar anchors, not for additional operator scores.

**Cost obstruction.** This is a supplied-candidate tomography/diagnostic repair. Choosing E_j requires computing the dressed commutant, and predicted moments tr(tau E_j) generally require model thermal moments/partition information. It can destroy the seed's partition-free advantage. Do not sell this as an efficient many-qubit learner.

## Quantitative repair certificate

Let J=1/2 sum_a tr[rho(S_a(rho)-S_a(tau))^2], and let g>0 be the smallest positive eigenvalue of sum_a ad_Ka^* ad_Ka on C^perp. Let a^2=sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|tr[(rho-tau)E_j]|^2. Put t_min=lambda_min(tau), t_max=lambda_max(tau), z=sqrt(2J)/(t_min sqrt(g)). Then

||rho-tau||_1 <= sqrt(d)t_max [a/t_min+(1+t_max/t_min)z].

Proof: X=tau^-1/2 rho tau^-1/2, X-I=Y+Z with Y=P_C(X-I), Z in C^perp. For D_a=S_a(rho)-S_a(tau), the residual L_a=-i[A_a,rho]-{rho,S_a(tau)}/2={rho,D_a}/2 obeys tau^-1/2 L_a tau^-1/2=-i[K_a,X]. In a rho eigenbasis, ||{rho,D}/2||_F^2 <= lambda_max(rho)tr(rho D^2) <=tr(rho D^2), hence g||Z||_F^2<=2J/t_min^2. The moment vector equals P_C[tau^.5(Y+Z)tau^.5]. The invertibility bound above gives ||Y||_F<=(a+t_max||Z||_F)/t_min. Finally ||tau^.5(Y+Z)tau^.5||_1<=sqrt(d)t_max(||Y||_F+||Z||_F). If C is the full matrix algebra, Z=0 and only the anchor term is needed. This is conservative and may be exponentially ill-conditioned in n; algebraic uniqueness is not a useful temperature-uniform guarantee.

## Repair 2: one generic operator, with exact quantifiers

Suppose at least one existing A is nonscalar. For every fixed faithful target rho, adding one independent Hermitian generator drawn from a continuous full-support density (e.g. GUE, then norm-normalized) makes the dressed frame irreducible with probability1.

Proof: T_rho(A)=K is nonscalar because T is invertible and fixes I. For any fixed nonscalar Hermitian K, there exists an orthonormal basis with every off-diagonal entry of K nonzero; each forbidden-zero entry is a nonzero real-analytic condition on U(d), hence almost every basis works. A generic Hermitian B has simple spectrum and such an eigenbasis, so commuting with B forces a diagonal matrix in that basis, and commuting with K forces its diagonal entries all equal. Equivalently the relevant commutator-rank minor is a nonzero polynomial, so exceptional B are Lebesgue-null. The invertible real-linear map T_rho transports a full-support continuous draw of A to such B. Norm scaling leaves commutants invariant.

The quantifier is **for every fixed rho, probability1 over A**; it is not probability1 that one A works simultaneously for every rho. It provides no useful lower bound on the dressed gap. A dense generic generator generally has exponentially many Pauli coefficients/nonlocal measurement cost. Its SLD still uses partition-free spectral ratios, but computational efficiency needs a separate representation/measurement theorem. See `leads/b_math.md` for the expanded proof.

## Diagonal subclass: exact classical ratio-matching bridge

Let p,q be positive distributions on {0,1}^n and states diag(p),diag(q). For bit flip x^i and generator X_i,

r_i^q(x)=(q(x)-q(x^i))/(q(x)+q(x^i))=2q(x_i|x_-i)-1,

S_i(q)_{x,x^i}=2i r_i^q(x).

Direct multiplication gives diagonal Hyvarinen observable 2(r_i^q)^2-4r_i^q. Binary two-outcome Brier loss for the observed x_i is B_i(q;x)=(1-r_i^q)^2/2. Therefore the sampleable QSM loss is exactly 4 sum_i E_p B_i(q;X)-2n, and J=4 times excess conditional Brier risk. Candidate conditional probabilities for diagonal Gibbs H are local logistic energy differences and do not need the partition function. All Z_i scores vanish. This is an exact algebraic bridge, verified by `work/team_b_quantum/check_brier_bridge.py` on 12 deterministic random n=1,2,3 fixtures (maximum discrepancy 1.8e-15); its proof above applies universally.

**Prior art:** conditional Brier/ratio matching is already explicit in Dawid–Lauritzen–Parry 1104.2224v3 Example4.4 Eq14, following Hyvarinen2007 Eq13. Their convention uses one-outcome Brier, so QSM is 8 times that convention's risk up to a constant. This is not a new diagonal estimator. The bridge can transfer existing discrete local-scoring theory and identifies an exact classical control for future quantum-learning comparisons.

For diagonal tau, K_Zi=Z_i and K_Xi has nonzero hypercube-edge weights w_i(x)=2sqrt(tau_x tau_xi)/(tau_x+tau_xi). Thus irreducibility holds at every finite temperature. More strongly, for all tracefree matrices M,

sum_i (||[M,K_Xi]||_F^2+||[M,Z_i]||_F^2) >= 4w_min^2 ||M||_F^2.

Proof: split M into diagonal D and offdiagonal O. The [D,K_Xi] support is only bit-i edges; [O,K_Xi] vanishes on those edges, so their Frobenius cross term is zero. Z energy is at least4||O||_F^2. Hypercube Poincare gives X energy on tracefree D at least4w_min^2||D||_F^2. If max single-flip energy difference is L, w_min>=sech(beta L/2); for r incident unit-bounded diagonal terms L<=2r. This is a dimension-free algebraic gap, but conversion to state loss can still involve tiny Gibbs eigenvalues and parameter statistics remain thermally hidden.

For known distinct nonempty diagonal Pauli terms H_theta=sum_j theta_j Z_Sj, |theta_j|<=1, site incidence<=r and neighborhood size<=m<=r(k-1), worker b_math also derives the global parameter-growth bound

J_Q(theta)>=2 beta^2 sech^4(beta r) exp(-2 beta r m) sum_j |S_j|(theta_j-theta_j*)^2.

Proof mechanism: each conditional field h_i has absolute value<=r; tanh(beta h) has derivative at least beta sech^2(beta r). Each neighborhood configuration has marginal probability at least 2^(-|N_i|)exp(-2 beta r |N_i|), using sequential conditional probabilities. Uniform Walsh orthogonality separates the distinct coefficients in h_i. Sum over sites counts each term |S_j| times. This is finite-temperature quadratic growth with no n in its constant, but no claim of global convexity, efficient optimizer or novel rate follows; the loss is the older ratio-matching estimator. See `notes/b_math/SLD_IDENTIFIABILITY.md` for assumptions and full proof.
