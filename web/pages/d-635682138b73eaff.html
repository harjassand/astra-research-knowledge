## Exact source theorem and interface match

The source works over C. Proposition 3.5, PDF pp6–7, states a necessary and sufficient criterion for a point outside a smooth quartic surface in P3 to be quasi-Galois: its linear third polar divides its cubic first polar. Proposition 3.1, PDF pp4–5, identifies the corresponding even quartic normal form and involution. Corollary 2.6, PDF p3, proves finiteness using the finite projective linear automorphism group. Theorem 5.4, PDF p16, enumerates 28 quasi-Galois points of the Fermat quartic surface.

This matches the parent workers' **metric-free quartic reflection certificate**. It is not merely an analogy to Euclidean reflection detection. The source begins with homogeneous coefficients and uses polar derivatives, not a supplied inner product. It supplies a pointwise certificate and special enumerations; it does not give an algorithm with polynomial bit complexity as the number of variables grows.

For every promised four-variable input \(f(x)=m^TQm\), \(m_i=(Lx)_i^2\), \(Q\succ0\), the smoothness premise holds over C. In hidden coordinates, any nonzero critical point would have active set \(I=\{i:y_i\ne0\}\) with

\[
 0=\partial_if=4y_i(Qm)_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q_{II}m_I=0.
\]

The real PD principal matrix \(Q_{II}\) is nonsingular over C. Thus all active squared coordinates vanish, a contradiction. In particular, the source covers **every promised n=4 input**, not only generic smooth fixtures.

The elementary linear-reflection criterion below extends in all dimensions and over any characteristic-zero field. That extension is our independently reconstructed algebra; the paper's stated quasi-Galois geometric theorem remains specifically for quartic surfaces over C. Do not silently attribute the all-dimension/rational-algorithm theorem to the paper.

## Complete independent reflection equivalence

Let \(f\) be any homogeneous quartic over a characteristic-zero field and \(v\) satisfy \(f(v)\ne0\). Define

\[
 w=\nabla f(v),\qquad c=w^Tv=4f(v),\qquad
 \ell(x)=\frac{w^Tx}{c},\qquad R_v=I-2v\ell.
\]

Then \(\ell(v)=1\), \(R_v^2=I\), and its minus-one eigenspace is \(\langle v\rangle\). Its fixed hyperplane is \(\ker\ell\). The third polar is

\[
 D_v^3 f(x)=6\nabla f(v)^Tx=6c\ell(x).
\]

Write \(x=z+tv\), where \(\ell(z)=0\). Taylor expansion gives

\[
 f(z+tv)=f(z)+tD_vf(z)+\frac{t^2}{2}D_v^2f(z)
       +\frac{t^3}{6}D_v^3f(z)+t^4f(v).
\]

The cubic odd term vanishes by definition of \(\ell\). The linear odd term vanishes for every \(z\in\ker\ell\) exactly when \(\ell\mid D_vf\). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{f(R_vx)=f(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_v^3f\mid D_vf.}
\]

This proves sufficiency and necessity for \(R_v\), without numerical geometry or Hessian inversion.

It also characterizes **every** linear reflection preserving \(f\) whose minus-one direction \(v\) has \(f(v)\ne0\). If \(R=I-2v\eta\), \(\eta(v)=1\), preserves \(f\), parity in \(t\) forces \(D_v^3f\) to vanish on \(\ker\eta\), so \(w\) is proportional to \(\eta\). Euler's identity normalizes the proportionality to \(\eta=\ell\).

For real QPD inputs, every nonzero real direction has \(f(v)>0\), so the nonzero-value condition is automatic. For a rational direction the reflection and hyperplane coefficients are rational. These are exactly the reflections relevant to a rational acquired metric. Additional complex quasi-Galois points can be nonrational or nonreal and must not be silently averaged into a rational real SPD construction.

Given a candidate hyperplane instead of a direction, solve the linear conditions \(D_vf|_{\ell=0}=0\) for \(v\), normalize \(\ell(v)=1\), and check \(D_v^3f\) is a multiple of \(\ell\) (or directly check \(f\circ R=f\)). The first-polar restriction alone is not a complete certificate for an arbitrary preselected \(\ell\); the third-polar alignment supplies the other odd coefficient.

## An all-input exact, dimension-dependent acquisition baseline

The criterion converts the promised reflection problem into a finite polynomial system, without a supplied metric. This is a substantive exact fallback, although it is not a variable-n polynomial algorithm.

Choose a projective direction chart \(v_k=1\), and a polar chart \(w_j\ne0\). Substitute

\[
 x_j=-\sum_{a\ne j}\frac{w_a}{w_j}x_a
\]

into the cubic \(D_vf\). Multiply by \(w_j^3\), equate all remaining monomial coefficients to zero, and saturate/exclude \(w_j f(v)=0\). Since \(w_a\) has degree3 in \(v\), each coefficient has degree at most10. There are \(O(n^3)\) coefficient equations. The projective direction charts have \(n-1\) main unknowns; inversion variables used for saturation can add a constant number. There are at most \(n^2\) charts. These equations are built directly from the expanded coefficients.

Why is the solution set finite on the promised class? The infinitesimal stabilizer of a QPD biquadratic quartic is zero. In hidden coordinates, the \(y_i^4\) coefficients force a stabilizer matrix's diagonal to vanish. The two odd coefficients \(y_i^3y_j\), \(y_iy_j^3\) give the invertible principal 2x2 system with entries \(Q_{ii},Q_{ij},Q_{jj}\); every off-diagonal entry vanishes. The complex algebraic automorphism group is zero dimensional, hence finite. Each polar-system solution yields a distinct reflection by the boxed equivalence, so the saturated projective direction system is zero dimensional.

Exact zero-dimensional solving and rational-point selection therefore recover all rational reflection directions, including every hidden axis. At fixed n the degree/number of variables are constant, giving a polynomial-in-coefficient-bits baseline using standard exact elimination and rational factorization. In variable n, a naive Bézout bound grows exponentially, approximately \(10^{O(n)}\), and standard elimination can have substantial intermediate complexity. **This is no proof of polynomial variable-n bit cost**, nor a lower bound that no faster solver exists. It explains why discovering the finite points efficiently is still a load-bearing issue after the recent certificate.

Later structural refinement: `COMPLEX_MONOMIAL_AUTOMORPHISMS.md` proves that all complex automorphisms of every QPD quartic permute its original hidden axes. Thus there are at most \(2n^2-n\) distinct complex reflection points and at most \(n^2\) real ones. This improves the bound on distinct final solutions, not the algebraic multiplicity or intermediate elimination complexity of the polar presentation. Do not convert this count alone into a polynomial-time claim.

One can then form all corresponding rational reflection matrices. Classical finite-group invariant-inner-product averaging gives a rational SPD form because these rational automorphisms generate a finite group. Averaging the entire group may take exponential enumeration. The parent workers' exact projector from a short generator list is a separate computational improvement to audit; the existence of an invariant SPD form is established representation theory.

The resulting metric is aligned with the original hidden axes: invariance under every hidden flip forces the metric to be diagonal in hidden coordinates. A supplied-metric exact axis procedure may then be used. Alternatively, at fixed n enumerate candidate independent direction tuples, invert their direction matrix, and verify evenness and PD by substitution. The latter can be exponentially expensive in variable n; completeness at fixed dimension does not imply scalable tractability.

## Independent Fermat enumeration and rational boundary

For \(f=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^4\), the criterion is

\[
 \left(\sum_i v_i^3x_i\right)\mid\left(\sum_i v_i x_i^3\right),
 \qquad\sum_i v_i^4\ne0.
\]

A direction with support at least three cannot pass: restrict to any three nonzero coordinates. The resulting diagonal cubic defines a smooth projective plane cubic and has no linear factor. With support one, every coordinate axis passes. With support two, normalize to \((1,a)\); division gives \(a^8=1\), while the nonzero-value condition excludes \(a^4=-1\). Thus \(a\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}\).

There are therefore \(n+4\binom n2\) complex directions and \(n+2\binom n2=n^2\) rational/real directions. For n=4 these are exactly 28 and16, respectively, independently reconstructing the source's complex count and the arithmetic distinction needed by this task.

## Novelty and next decisions

The following broad claims are rejected as discoveries: quartic reflection certification without a metric; existence of a rational SPD invariant form for a finite rational group; acquisition of an algebraic metric by tensor norm minimization; or coefficient-only binary quartic axes via the Hessian sextic.

A still distinguishable candidate contribution is an algorithm with **variable-dimension polynomial bit complexity** that acquires enough rational reflections/axes from a promised expanded quartic, without a supplied metric or algebraic-number output overhead. A generic nonzero-extactic guarantee is a generic theorem; it remains weaker than all promised QPD inputs. The parent construction's vector field and extactic machinery each have older prior, so novelty requires precise composition/interface/cost claims and a wider literature search. Its full-class nonvanishing lemma remains the decisive next falsifiable question.

Scientific use is concrete for exact quartic K3 projection symmetries and for coordinate normalization of rational coupled quartic physical potentials. Most standard applications supply their Euclidean/kinetic metric or display the even normal form already. The stronger missing-metric task becomes relevant under rational coordinate mixing/calibration or symbolic equations in an arbitrary frame; that extra assumption should be explicit in any downstream claim.
