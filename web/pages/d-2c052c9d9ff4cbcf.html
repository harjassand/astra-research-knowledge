> For every integer m ≥ 1, the specified rational harmonic polynomial P_m on R³ has degree at most 256m and at least 8m³ distinct, real, nondegenerate critical points, each of Morse index 2.

## Reconstruction

Set k = 4m and let U be the cyclic sum of `cos(kx) cos(ky) cosh(sqrt(2)kz)`. Every summand is harmonic because its three second derivatives contribute coefficients −k², −k², and +2k².

Each product expands into eight exponentials with coefficient 1/8. Their vectors have two coordinates ±ik and one coordinate ±sqrt(2)k, hence bilinear square zero. For j ≥ 2,

`Delta (w.X)^j = j(j−1) (w.w) (w.X)^(j−2) = 0`.

Degrees 0 and 1 are harmonic directly. Thus the degree-D Taylor truncation is harmonic term by term. Real product expansions show that only monomials `x^(2a)y^(2b)z^(2c)` occur. For the summand with hyperbolic z coordinate, their coefficients are

`(-1)^(a+b) 2^c k^(2a+2b+2c) / [(2a)!(2b)!(2c)!]`.

This proves rationality independently of complex cancellation. The finite check directly constructs these coefficients and confirms vanishing Laplacian; its construction does not use the null-wave representation.

Let the selected coordinates be `t_j = (j+1/2) pi/k`, for j = −m,...,m−1. There are 2m coordinates, all of absolute value less than pi/4 < 1. Their Cartesian product has exactly 8m³ points. At any selected point, all three cosines vanish. Every first derivative retains a zero cosine factor. All diagonal second derivatives vanish as well.

Write `s_x = sin(kx)`, and similarly for y,z; each s is ±1. Let `A = cosh(sqrt(2)kx)`, `B = cosh(sqrt(2)ky)`, `C = cosh(sqrt(2)kz)`. The Hessian is

```
k² [       0  s_x s_y C  s_x s_z B
     s_x s_y C       0  s_y s_z A
     s_x s_z B s_y s_z A       0 ].
```

Its determinant is exactly `2 k^6 A B C > 0`, and its trace is zero. Since it is real symmetric, these two facts force one positive and two negative eigenvalues. In particular, every grid critical point is nondegenerate and has index 2. Sign choices do not change this conclusion: conjugation by `diag(s_x,s_y,s_z)` removes them.

All A,B,C lie in `[1, exp(sqrt(2)k)]`. The maximum absolute row sum gives `||H|| ≤ 2k² exp(sqrt(2)k)`. The determinant divided by the square of this norm lower-bounds the smallest singular value:

`sigma_min(H) ≥ (k²/2) exp(−2sqrt(2)k) ≥ (k²/2) exp(−3k)`.

Thus `||H^(-1)|| ≤ 2k^(−2)exp(3k)` uniformly over all selected centers. This argument uses singular values and remains valid for the indefinite Hessian.

## Tail and variation estimates

Take D = 64k. On `[-2,2]^3`, every null wave obeys `|w.X| ≤ 2(2+sqrt(2))k < 7k`. A coordinate derivative of order s ≤ 2 contributes a factor at most `2k²`; this single bound also covers s = 0 and s = 1 when k ≥ 1. Differentiating the tail starting at degree D+1 leaves scalar powers beginning at D+1−s, so beginning all three estimates at N = D−1 is conservative. Summing the 24 coefficients 1/8 gives

`|partial^alpha(P−U)| ≤ 6k² sum_{j≥N}(7k)^j/j!`, for `|alpha| ≤ 2`.

Here N ≥ 63k and the consecutive-term ratio is at most 7/64 < 1/2. Using `N! ≥ (N/e)^N` and e < 3 gives

`sum_{j≥N}(7k)^j/j! ≤ 2(7ek/N)^N < 2(1/3)^N ≤ 2exp(−63k)`.

The last inequality follows from log 3 > 1, and the proposed relaxed bound `12k² exp(−60k)` is therefore valid entrywise for every derivative of order ≤ 2.

For any third coordinate derivative of U, the absolute product of frequency factors is at most `(sqrt(2)k)^3`, cosine and sine factors have absolute value ≤ 1, and the single hyperbolic factor has absolute value at most `exp(2sqrt(2)k)`. Three summands give an entry bound strictly below `9k³ exp(3k)`. The induced trilinear norm is at most 27 times the largest entry, because each coordinate of a Euclidean unit vector has absolute value at most 1. Hence it is less than `243k³ exp(3k)`, which verifies the proposed bound `300k³ exp(3k)` without needing a tight tensor-norm conversion.

## Quantitative persistence

For each center p, take the closed Euclidean ball of radius `r = exp(−7k)/(10000k)`, and set `T(X) = X − H^(-1) grad P(X)`, where H is the Hessian of U at p. These balls lie inside the estimate cube: every center coordinate has absolute value < 1 and r < 1. They are pairwise disjoint since distinct centers have Euclidean separation at least pi/k, while 2r < pi/k.

Along a line segment contained in one such ball, the trilinear derivative estimate implies `||D²U(X)−H|| ≤ 300k³ exp(3k)r`. A 3-by-3 matrix with entries bounded by a has operator norm at most 3a. Consequently,

`||DT(X)|| ≤ 0.06 exp(−k) + 72 exp(−57k) < 1/4`.

This strict inequality holds already for k ≥ 1: replacing e by 2 upper-bounds the expression by `3/100 + 72/2^57 < 1/4`.

At the center, `grad U(p)=0`. The gradient tail has Euclidean norm at most sqrt(3) times its entry bound, so

`||T(p)−p|| ≤ 24sqrt(3) exp(−57k) < 42 exp(−57k)`.

Dividing this last bound by r/2 gives `840000k exp(−50k)`. It is less than 1 for all integer k ≥ 1: it is bounded by `840000k 2^(−50k)`, whose ratio at k+1 versus k is at most `2^(−49)`, and whose value at 1 is below 1. Therefore T maps the ball into itself, indeed into the concentric radius-3r/4 ball. Banach's fixed-point theorem supplies a unique critical point of P in each ball.

At that critical point, and throughout its ball, `||H^(-1)(D²P−H)|| < 1/4`. For every s in [0,1],

`H+s(D²P−H) = H[I+s H^(-1)(D²P−H)]`

is invertible by the Neumann criterion. The path is a path of real symmetric matrices, so no eigenvalue crosses zero. It preserves inertia and gives index 2 to each retained critical point. Disjointness yields at least 8m³ distinct critical points. No assertion that these are all critical points is needed or established.
