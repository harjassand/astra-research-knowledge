## 1. Statement and quantifiers

Let
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathbb C^2,\qquad
A_n=c_nI+a_n\sigma_z,\qquad B_n=b_n\sigma_x,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(a_n\ge1\), \(a_n\to\infty\), \(c_n\in\mathbb R\) are arbitrary, and \(b_n\in\mathbb R\) form a bounded sequence. Put \(A=\bigoplus A_n\), with its maximal self-adjoint direct-sum domain, and \(B=\bigoplus B_n\), which is bounded. All real combinations \(sA+tB\) are unambiguously self-adjoint direct sums. Define
\[
r_*=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log |b_n|}{a_n}\in[0,\infty],
\qquad-\log0=+\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(R\in(0,\infty]\), write \(D_R=\{g\in\mathbb R^2:|g|<R\}\), with \(D_\infty=\mathbb R^2\). The full finite Laplace domain of a scalar probability is
\[
\mathcal D(\nu)=\{g:\int e^{g\cdot z}\nu(dz)<\infty\}.
\]

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent.

1. \(R\le r_*\).
2. There are one scalar probability law \(\nu\), with \(D_R\subseteq\mathcal D(\nu)\), and a normalized POVM \(M\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), on the whole \(\mathcal H\), such that
   \[
   \boxed{\widehat M(s,t)=\widehat\nu(s,t)e^{i(sA+tB)}
   \quad\text{for every }(s,t)\in\mathbb R^2.}
   \tag{3}
   \]
3. Such a law can be chosen with a smooth, everywhere positive, radial, strictly log-concave density \(p\), satisfying
   \[
   \mathcal D(p)=D_R,
   \tag{4}
   \]
   and \(M\) can have an everywhere positive-semidefinite bounded operator-valued smooth density.

Normalization and countable additivity are in the weak/strong POVM sense, not a claimed integral of the density in operator norm. The characteristic is strongly continuous; no norm continuity at zero is claimed. Every derivative of \(p\) has an exponentially weighted absolute integral at every \(g\in D_R\).

Thus some nonzero exponential neighborhood is possible exactly when \(r_*>0\), and all-exponential scalar regularization is possible exactly when \(r_*=\infty\). If \(0<r_*<\infty\), the maximal **open centered moment ball is attained**, with exact domain \(D_{r_*}\). Individual boundary moments of arbitrary noises are not classified.

This is an infinite-family result. The law \(p\) is fixed once and works simultaneously in every block.

## 2. Established sphere kernel, reconstructed with the needed constants

Let \(\mu\) be the projection of normalized surface measure on \(S^2\) onto its first two coordinates:
\[
\mu(du\,dv)=\frac{\mathbf1_{u^2+v^2<1}}{2\pi\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2}}\,du\,dv.
\tag{5}
\]
It is a probability, invariant under both coordinate reflections; its \(u\)-marginal is uniform on \([-1,1]\). Rotating a vector in \(\mathbb R^3\) and integrating a uniform sphere coordinate gives
\[
\int e^{i(\xi u+\eta v)}\,d\mu
=\frac{\sin\sqrt{\xi^2+\eta^2}}{\sqrt{\xi^2+\eta^2}}.
\tag{6}
\]
The value at zero is one. Differentiation in a common dilation gives the cosine transform.

For \(a\ge1,b\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
\begin{split}
T_{a,b}(h)=\int\big[&
h(au,bv)I
+(au\,h_1(au,bv)+bv\,h_2(au,bv))I\\
&+a h_1(au,bv)\sigma_z+b h_2(au,bv)\sigma_x\big]\,d\mu.
\end{split}
\tag{7}
\]
This compact matrix-valued distribution has support in \(\{|y|\le a+|b|\}\), order at most one, and \(T_{a,b}(1)=I\). Equation (6) gives
\[
T_{a,b}(e^{i(sy_1+ty_2)})
=\cos\omega\,I+i\frac{\sin\omega}{\omega}
(as\sigma_z+bt\sigma_x)
=e^{i(sa\sigma_z+tb\sigma_x)},\quad
\omega^2=a^2s^2+b^2t^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Real exponentials give
\[
H_{a,b}(g):=T_{a,b}(e^{g\cdot y})
=e^{g_1a\sigma_z+g_2b\sigma_x}
\succeq e^{-(a+|b|)|g|}I.
\tag{9}
\]

For a smooth density \(p\), its convolution \(K_{a,b}=T_{a,b}*p\) is
\[
\begin{split}
K_{a,b}(z)=\int\big[&
(p-au p_1-bv p_2)(z-(au,bv))I\\
&-a p_1(z-(au,bv))\sigma_z
-b p_2(z-(au,bv))\sigma_x\big]\,d\mu .
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
At \(b=0\), integration by parts in the uniform \(u\)-marginal gives exactly
\[
K_{a,0}(z)=D_a(z):=
\begin{pmatrix}p(z-ae_1)&0\\0&p(z+ae_1)\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{11}
\]
For the upper entry, for example,
\(\frac12\int_{-1}^1[p-a(u+1)p_1](z-au e_1)\,du
=[(u+1)p(z-au e_1)]_{-1}^1/2=p(z-ae_1)\).

The sphere kernel is classical. Ricker (1997), pp. 322-324, credits Anderson's 1969 sphere formula and derives the projected-disk derivative representation. The new use here is a uniform high-gap estimate combined with a low-gap construction.

## 3. Necessity: tilted transverse variance

Assume (3), and compress to one block. The compressed POVM has characteristic function
\(\widehat\nu(s,t)e^{i(sc_nI+sa_n\sigma_z+tb_n\sigma_x)}\).
The same \(\nu\) occurs in every compression.

Real moments must first be justified. For a finite block and an interior point \(g\) of \(\mathcal D(\nu)\), the distribution \(T_{a,b}*\nu\), shifted by \(c_ne_1\), equals the compressed POVM by Fourier uniqueness for tempered distributions. Apply (7) to \(e^{g\cdot x}\chi_N(x)\), where smooth nonnegative cutoffs increase to one and have uniformly bounded first derivatives. On the fixed compact \(y\)-support, integrands and their first derivatives are bounded by a block-dependent constant times \(e^{g\cdot z}\) under \(\nu\). Dominated convergence gives \(m_\nu(g)e^{g_1A_n+g_2B_n}\). Positivity and monotone convergence identify this as the actual POVM Laplace integral. Polynomial factors \(x_2,x_2^2\) are also legitimate because an exponential neighborhood of \(g\) makes them integrable. This is a genuine moment identity, not a formal imaginary Fourier substitution.

Fix \(0<s<R\), and let
\[
v_s=\partial_t^2\log m_\nu(s,0)<\infty.
\]
It is the transverse variance after scalar exponential tilting. The shift \(c_n\) cancels from the following Schur complement. Set \(u=sa\),
\[
H(t)=e^{u\sigma_z+tb\sigma_x},\qquad F(t)=m_\nu(s,t)H(t).
\]
Positivity of the tilted first/second moment matrix gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}F(0)&F'(0)\\F'(0)&F''(0)\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]
Its Schur complement, after canceling the scalar first derivative, is
\[
v_sH(0)+H''(0)-H'(0)H(0)^{-1}H'(0)\succeq0.
\tag{12}
\]
Direct differentiation yields
\[
H(0)=e^{u\sigma_z},\quad H'(0)=b\frac{\sinh u}{u}\sigma_x,
\]
\[
H''(0)=b^2\left[
\frac{\sinh u}{u}I+
\frac{u\cosh u-\sinh u}{u^2}\sigma_z\right].
\tag{13}
\]
Conjugating \(H'H(0)^{-1}H'-H''\) by \(H(0)^{-1/2}\) gives eigenvalues
\[
\pm\frac{b^2}{2u^2}(\sinh(2u)-2u).
\]
Therefore every block obeys
\[
v_s\ge
\frac{b_n^2}{2a_n^2s^2}
\big[\sinh(2a_ns)-2a_ns\big].
\tag{14}
\]
For fixed \(s>0\), the right side is asymptotic to
\(b_n^2e^{2sa_n}/(4a_n^2s^2)\). Boundedness forces
\[
\sup_n\frac{|b_n|e^{sa_n}}{a_n}<\infty,\qquad r_*\ge s.
\tag{15}
\]
Allow every \(s<R\) to obtain \(R\le r_*\).
Equivalently,
\[
R\le r_*
\iff
\sup_n|b_n|e^{\lambda a_n}<\infty
\quad\text{for every }0<\lambda<R.
\tag{16}
\]
Polynomial factors can be absorbed by increasing \(\lambda\) slightly inside \(R\).

## 4. High-gap positivity estimate

Let \(p=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\) be smooth and positive, with globally
\(\|D^2V\|\le H_2,\|D^3V\|\le H_3\).
Fix a block, choose \(B_0\ge\max(1,|b|)\), and put
\[
Q=\sup_{|w-z|\le a+B_0}|\nabla V(w)|.
\]
Derivative identities imply, in that ball,
\[
|p_2|\le Qp,\quad |p_{22}|\le(Q^2+H_2)p,\quad
|p_{122}|\le(Q^3+3H_2Q+H_3)p.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(C_p=2+4H_2+H_3\) and \(D=2C_p\). These coefficients are bounded by \(C_p(1+Q)^3\).

Reflect \(v\) in (10). The diagonal difference from (11) is even in \(b\). Taylor's theorem, including the term \(-bv p_2\), gives
\[
|K_{\pm\pm}(z)-D_{a,\pm\pm}(z)|
\le b^2\left[\frac32\sup|p_{22}|+a\sup|p_{122}|\right].
\tag{18}
\]
The suprema run over the same ball. Also
\[
|K_{+-}(z)|\le |b|\sup|p_2|.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(w=z-(au,bv)\), Lipschitz control gives
\[
\frac{p(w)}{p(z\pm ae_1)}\le e^{Q(2a+B_0)},\quad
\frac{p(w)}{\sqrt{p(z-ae_1)p(z+ae_1)}}\le e^{Q(a+B_0)}.
\tag{20}
\]
The latter follows because the average of the two endpoint distances is at most \(a+B_0\). Both comparisons apply to the intermediate points in (18).

Set
\[
\eta=|b|(1+a)^2(1+Q)^3e^{Q(a+B_0)}.
\]
The diagonal entries of
\(D_a^{-1/2}(K_{a,b}-D_a)D_a^{-1/2}\) have absolute value at most \(D\eta^2\), and its off-diagonal entry has absolute value at most \(\eta\). Hence
\[
\eta\le\eta_0:=\frac1{4(1+D)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{K_{a,b}(z)\succeq\frac12D_a(z)\succ0.}
\tag{21}
\]
The normalized perturbation norm is at most \(D\eta^2+\eta<1/2\).

Keeping the geometric mean of the endpoint densities retains the decisive factor \(b e^{Qa}\); replacing it by a worst endpoint would lose the sharp radius.

## 5. Low-gap local Laplace estimate

Suppose \(|\nabla V(z)|\le t\), \(a+|b|\le K\), and the local Hessian norm is at most \(h\) on \(|w-z|\le K\). With \(g=\nabla V(z)\), convexity gives
\[
F_z(y)=V(z-y)-V(z)+g\cdot y\ge0,\quad
|F_z(y)|\le hK^2/2,\quad |\nabla_yF_z(y)|\le hK.
\]
Since \(p(z-y)/p(z)=e^{g\cdot y}e^{-F_z(y)}\), the order-one bound in (7) implies
\[
\left\|\frac{K_{a,b}(z)}{p(z)}-H_{a,b}(g)\right\|
\le(1+2K)e^{tK}h
\left[K+\frac{(1+t)K^2}{2}\right].
\tag{22}
\]
This uses \(0\le1-e^{-F_z}\le F_z\), \(e^{-F_z}\le1\), and the product rule. No prior smallness condition on \(F_z\) is hidden. By (9),
\[
(1+2K)e^{2tK}h\left[K+\frac{(1+t)K^2}{2}\right]\le\frac12
\ \Longrightarrow\
K_{a,b}(z)\succeq\frac12p(z)H_{a,b}(g)\succ0.
\tag{23}
\]

## 6. One radial law for the full infinite family

Assume \(R\le r_*\). Fix \(B_0=\max(1,\sup_n|b_n|)\). Choose strictly increasing slopes \(\lambda_j\uparrow R\): use \(\lambda_j=j\) for \(R=\infty\), and \(\lambda_j=R(1-2^{-j})\) for finite \(R\). Choose \(M_0<\infty\) dominating their increments; \(M_0=1\) or \(M_0=\max(1,R)\), respectively, suffices.

Use the smooth step
\[
\theta(s)=
\frac{\int_0^s\mathbf1_{0<u<1}e^{-1/[u(1-u)]}\,du}
{\int_0^1e^{-1/[u(1-u)]}\,du},
\]
extended by zero and one. It obeys
\[
\|\theta'\|_\infty<8,\qquad\|\theta''\|_\infty<128.
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed the denominator is at least \(\tfrac12e^{-16/3}\), the numerator for \(\theta'\) is at most \(e^{-4}\), and the derivative numerator for \(\theta''\) is at most \(u^2e^{-u}\le16e^{-4}\), with \(u=1/[s(1-s)]\ge4\).

Fix constants before choosing thresholds:
\[
Q_1=8M_0,\quad H_2=2\lambda_1+10M_0,\quad
H_3=6\lambda_1+190M_0,
\]
\[
C_p=2+4H_2+H_3,\quad D=2C_p,\quad
\eta_0=[4(1+D)]^{-1},\quad c_0=\log(B_0+3).
\tag{25}
\]
By (16), choose nondecreasing integer cutoffs \(A_j\ge1\) satisfying
\[
a_n\ge A_j
\ \Longrightarrow\
|b_n|(1+a_n)^2(1+\lambda_j)^3
e^{\lambda_j(a_n+B_0)}\le\eta_0
\quad\text{for all }n.
\tag{26}
\]
Existence follows by inserting an exponent between \(\lambda_j\) and \(R\). These are certified global cutoffs, not empirical finite maxima.

For integers \(k\ge0\), put
\[
K_k=A_{k+2}+B_0,\quad t_k=\lambda_{k+1},
\]
\[
G_k=\max\left\{1,\,
(1+2K_k)\left[K_k+\frac{(1+t_k)K_k^2}{2}\right]
e^{2K_kt_k}(1+K_k)
[2t_k+Q_1(2K_k+1)]\right\}.
\tag{27}
\]
These are nondecreasing. Choose thresholds for \(k\ge1\) so that
\[
S_k\ge\max\{k+2,\log(1+A_{k+2}),\log G_k\},
\qquad S_{k+1}\ge S_k+c_0+3.
\tag{28}
\]
Define
\[
q(s)=\lambda_1+
\sum_{j\ge1}(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_j)\theta(s-S_j),
\quad s\ge0,\quad
k(s)=\#\{j:S_j\le s\}.
\tag{29}
\]
The transitions are disjoint. We have
\[
q(s)\le\lambda_{k(s)+1},\quad q(s)\uparrow R,\quad
q(s)\le\lambda_1+M_0s,\quad
0\le q'\le Q_1,\quad|q''|\le128M_0,
\]
\[
k(s+c_0)\le k(s)+1.
\tag{30}
\]
All higher derivatives are bounded as well. Let
\[
\rho(w)=\sqrt{1+|w|^2},\quad
H(u)=\int_1^u q(\log v)\,dv,\quad f(w)=H(\rho(w)).
\]
Choose \(L\ge\max(2,2G_0)\), and set
\[
V_L(z)=Lf(z/L),\qquad p(z)=Z^{-1}e^{-V_L(z)}.
\tag{31}
\]
This is the single common scalar noise.

### 6.1 Geometry, derivatives and exact moment domain

For \(s=\log\rho(w)\),
\[
\nabla f(w)=q(s)w/\rho,\quad
D^2f(w)=\frac q\rho I+\frac{q'-q}{\rho^3}ww^\top.
\tag{32}
\]
Its tangential eigenvalue is \(q/\rho>0\) and radial eigenvalue is \((q+q'|w|^2)/\rho^3>0\). Thus \(V_L\) is strictly convex. Direct differentiation gives
\[
\|D^2f\|\le(2q+|q'|)/\rho,\quad
\|D^3f\|\le(6q+7|q'|+|q''|)/\rho^2.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (30) imply the global bounds
\[
\|D^2V_L\|\le H_2/L\le H_2,\qquad
\|D^3V_L\|\le H_3/L^2\le H_3.
\tag{34}
\]
Since \(q\ge\lambda_1>0\), \(0<Z<\infty\). Also
\[
\lim_{|z|\to\infty}V_L(z)/|z|=R
\tag{35}
\]
with infinite limit for \(R=\infty\). All moments at \(|g|<R\) are finite. For finite \(R\), \(q<R\) gives \(V_L(z)\le R|z|\). On a fixed-width tube in a boundary-tilt direction, \(e^{g\cdot z-V_L(z)}\) is bounded below by a positive constant as the longitudinal coordinate tends to infinity. Its integral diverges. The same holds outside the ball. Thus \(\mathcal D(p)=D_R\), excluding all boundary points.

Each derivative of \(p\) is bounded by \(p\) times a fixed power of \(1+\log\rho(z/L)\), up to an order-dependent constant, by the radial chain rule and bounded step derivatives. This proves the weighted derivative integrability. The gradient is a diffeomorphism onto \(D_R\), although that fact is not needed for positivity.

### 6.2 Positivity at an arbitrary point and block

Fix \(z,a,b\). Put \(\rho_z=\rho(z/L)\), \(s_z=\log\rho_z\), \(k=k(s_z)\), \(s_a=\log(1+a)\), and \(\ell=k(s_a)\).

**High gap: \(a\ge A_{k+2}\).** We also have
\[
a\ge A_{\ell+2}.
\tag{36}
\]
If \(\ell=0\), use \(A_{k+2}\ge A_2\). If \(\ell\ge1\), use
\(\log(1+a)\ge S_\ell\ge\log(1+A_{\ell+2})\).
For \(|w-z|\le a+B_0\),
\[
\rho(w/L)\le\rho_z+a+B_0
\le(B_0+3)\max(\rho_z,1+a).
\]
By (30),
\[
Q=\sup_{|w-z|\le a+B_0}|\nabla V_L(w)|
\le\lambda_{\max(k,\ell)+2}.
\tag{37}
\]
Also \(a\ge A_{\max(k,\ell)+2}\). Equation (26) bounds the high-gap parameter \(\eta\) by \(\eta_0\). Equation (21) proves \(K_{a,b}(z)\succ0\).

**Low gap: \(a<A_{k+2}\).** Then \(a+|b|\le K_k\), and \(|\nabla V_L(z)|\le t_k\). For \(|w-z|\le K_k\),
\[
\rho_z/(1+K_k/L)\le\rho(w/L)\le\rho_z(1+K_k/L).
\]
Hence \(q(\log\rho(w/L))\le t_k+Q_1K_k\), and the local Hessian satisfies
\[
h\le\frac{(1+K_k)[2t_k+Q_1(2K_k+1)]}{L\rho_z}.
\tag{38}
\]
The left side of (23) is at most \(G_k/(L\rho_z)\). For \(k=0\), this is at most \(G_0/L\le1/2\). For \(k\ge1\), \(s_z\ge S_k\ge\log G_k\), so it is at most \(1/L\le1/2\). Equation (23) again proves positivity.

The cases are exhaustive and hold for every block, every point, and the same fixed density.

## 7. Normalize on the whole infinite-dimensional space

Define \(M_n(z)=K_{a_n,b_n}(z-c_ne_1)\). Integrability of \(p\) and its derivatives in (10) gives
\[
\int M_n(z)\,dz=I_2,\quad
\widehat M_n(s,t)=\widehat p(s,t)
e^{i(sc_nI+sa_n\sigma_z+tb_n\sigma_x)}.
\tag{39}
\]
The absolute version of (18)-(19), using global bounded derivatives, gives
\[
\sup_z\|M_n(z)\|
\le\sup p+|b_n|\sup|p_2|
+b_n^2\left[\frac32\sup|p_{22}|+a_n\sup|p_{122}|\right].
\tag{40}
\]
Condition (16) at one positive exponent implies \(\sup_n a_nb_n^2<\infty\). Thus \(M(z)=\bigoplus_nM_n(z)\) is bounded, uniformly in \(z\), including arbitrary shifts \(c_n\). Apply the same argument after each fixed derivative to get uniform derivative bounds; one further derivative proves operator-norm smoothness.

Set
\[
M(E)=\bigoplus_n\int_E M_n(z)\,dz.
\tag{41}
\]
Every block is between zero and identity. Dominated convergence on the squared components of a vector proves strong countable additivity. Equation (39) proves \(M(\mathbb R^2)=I\) and the Fourier identity (3) in the strong operator sense. This completes sufficiency.

No global operator-norm Laplace identity is asserted: the tuple is unbounded, and the output can have infinite exponential moments in states with insufficient energy tails. The block-compressed real moments used in necessity remain valid. The density need not be operator-norm integrable: its translated spectral peaks can occupy infinitely many separated energy locations.

## 8. Analytic interpretation and decisive examples

For this family,
\[
e^{izA_n}B_ne^{-izA_n}
=b_n\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{2ia_nz}\\e^{-2ia_nz}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\|e^{izA_n}B_ne^{-izA_n}\|=|b_n|e^{2a_n|\operatorname{Im}z|}.
\tag{42}
\]
The maximal open strip of norm-holomorphic orbit continuation has half-width \(r_*/2\). Uniform convergence on smaller strips uses exponent slack in (16); the displayed block norms give the converse. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\text{common all-exponential scalar regularization}
\iff B\text{ has a norm-entire orbit under }A.}
\tag{43}
\]
Equivalently, \(B\) is an entire analytic vector for \(i[A,\cdot]\).
Indeed, \(\|\operatorname{ad}_A^jB\|=\sup_n(2a_n)^j|b_n|\). An exponential bound at exponent \(q>2\rho\) sums
\(\sum_j\rho^j\|\operatorname{ad}_A^jB\|/j!\), using
\((2a)^j\le j!(2/q)^je^{qa}\). Conversely that series dominates
\(\sup_n|b_n|e^{2\rho a_n}\).
This equivalence is asserted for (1), not arbitrary analytic interactions.

Take \(a_n=n,c_n=2n\). Then \(A_n=\operatorname{diag}(3n,n)\): \(A\) is positive with compact resolvent and finite thermal trace at every positive inverse temperature.

| Coupling | \(r_*\) | Exact conclusion |
|---|---:|---|
| \(e^{-\sqrt n}\) | \(0\) | No exponential-neighborhood regularizer |
| \(e^{-\alpha n}\) | \(\alpha>0\) | Exact open ball \(D_\alpha\) attainable; all-exponential impossible |
| \(e^{-\alpha n+\sqrt n}\) | \(\alpha\) | Same exact open ball attainable despite unbounded \(b_ne^{\alpha n}\) |
| \(e^{-n\log(n+1)}\) | \(\infty\) | One all-exponential regularizer exists |
| \(e^{-n^2}\) | \(\infty\) | One all-exponential regularizer exists |

All these \(B\) are trace class, map into every \(D(A^j)\), and have every iterated commutator bounded and trace class. This crosses the incoming obstruction on its same positive compact-resolvent architecture.

For the critical example \(b_n=e^{-Rn+\sqrt n}\), (14), with
\(s=R-\delta\) and \(n=\lceil1/(4\delta^2)\rceil\), forces
\[
\log v_{R-\delta}\ \ge\ \frac1{2\delta}+4\log\delta+O_R(1)
\quad(\delta\downarrow0).
\]
Thus the necessary tilted transverse variance diverges near the boundary; the theorem does not hide a uniform variance bound there. For \(R=1\), a completely explicit family of admissible cutoffs is
\[
A_j=\max\{100,\ 16\cdot4^j,\ 48\cdot2^j\}.
\]
To verify (26), put \(\delta_j=2^{-j}=1-\lambda_j\). For
\(a\ge A_j\), both \(\sqrt a\) and \(2\log(1+a)\) are at most
\(\delta_ja/4\). Also
\(3\log(1+\lambda_j)+\lambda_j+\log(1/\eta_0)<12\), and
\(\delta_ja/2\ge24\). These bounds prove the required inequality for every tail index, including the critical radius.

For \(b_n=e^{-n^2}\), take \(\lambda_j=j,B_0=1\) and explicit monotone cutoffs
\[
A_j=\left\lceil4(j+1)+
2\sqrt{3\log(1+j)+j+\log(1/\eta_0)}\right\rceil+3.
\tag{44}
\]
For \(a\ge A_j\), \(2\log(1+a)\le a\) and \((j+1)a\le a^2/4\) verify (26). Thus the positive example has a specified infinite construction.

Dependence is essential for the all-exponential contract. If \(\nu=\nu_1\otimes\nu_2\), then \(v_s=\operatorname{Var}(\nu_2)\) is constant. For even one block with \(a>0,b\ne0\), (14) diverges as \(s\to\infty\). Therefore independent-coordinate all-exponential noise cannot work. The radial construction has dependent coordinates.

## 9. Supplied information, costs and boundaries

Required inputs are the exact separated-block architecture, real gaps/couplings, bounded \(B\), and certified global exponential bounds sufficient to choose every cutoff in (26).
