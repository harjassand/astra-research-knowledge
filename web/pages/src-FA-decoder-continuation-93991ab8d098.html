LUNA01 — ALL-PREFIX FRACTIONAL COMPLETION AND SELECTOR CLOSURE
13 October 2026. Exact local counterexample; no general complexity result.

Question

Can a polynomial-time separation oracle for a compact, convex, all-prefix
completion body supply causal signs when each scalar drift is chosen after the
sign? The inherited game has a supplied fixed direction schedule v_1,...,v_N,
||v_t||_2<=1, action epsilon_t in {-1,+1}, then adversarial zeta_t in
[-gamma,gamma], gamma<1, with s_t=s_(t-1)+(epsilon_t+zeta_t)v_t. Exact past
state is available before each choice. The source-derived theorem is an
existence result with radius O((1-gamma)^-3 sqrt(d)); its supplied compiler is
not polynomial in dimension.

A precise sufficient oracle condition

For a safe convex body B_R and each time t, suppose a controller has convex
sets Q_t and selector regions C_t^+, C_t^- with:
  (i) s_0 in Q_1 and every Q_t is contained in B_R;
  (ii) Q_t is covered by C_t^+ union C_t^-;
  (iii) for every s in C_t^epsilon, both endpoint states
       s+(epsilon-gamma)v_t and s+(epsilon+gamma)v_t lie in Q_(t+1).
If Q_(N+1) is contained in B_R, then choose the sign whose selector region
contains the observed state. Convexity of Q_(t+1) puts the whole scalar segment
s+(epsilon+[-gamma,gamma])v_t inside Q_(t+1), so induction keeps every prefix
inside B_R. With separation/membership oracles for these sets and a dispatch
rule for the two selector regions, execution uses a constant number of oracle
queries per round. This is a sufficient, independently checkable format for a
causal policy. It does not construct the sets or prove their coverage cheaply.

The tempting all-prefix fractional relaxation is
  P_(N+1)=B_R,
  A_t = B_R intersect union_(epsilon=+-1)
        {s: s+(epsilon-gamma)v_t and s+(epsilon+gamma)v_t are in P_(t+1)},
  P_t=conv(A_t).
Thus each step admits a sign into the next fractional body, then convexifies
the resulting predecessor states. Exact viability instead obeys
  W_(N+1)=B_R,
  W_t = B_R intersect union_(epsilon=+-1)
        {s: s+(epsilon+zeta)v_t is in W_(t+1) for every zeta in [-gamma,gamma]}.
A natural relaxed selector tests only whether the two endpoint successors lie
in P_(t+1); convexity then includes intermediate disturbances. The examples
below show that this test can accept an action even though one endpoint state
has no safe continuation. Pointwise convexification loses causal recourse:
the needed closure is P_t subseteq A_t, and convexification need not preserve it.

Exact Euclidean counterexamples

Both fixtures use gamma=1/2, radius R=11/10, two rounds, and all-prefix
round-boundary safety. Every vector is rational and has norm at most one.
The checker uses Fraction arithmetic and directly enumerates every sign and
endpoint disturbance. At the final step, checking both endpoints is also exact
for the full continuous interval because the Euclidean ball is convex.

Dimension two: v_1=(1/2,1/2), v_2=(1,0). At the two disturbance endpoints,
the first-round states are either (1/4,1/4) and (3/4,3/4), or their negatives;
the full continuous successor segment lies between them. The high state
(3/4,3/4) is in W_2: choosing epsilon=-1 makes the final endpoints
(-1/4,3/4) and (-3/4,3/4), whose squared norms are 10/16 and 18/16, both below
R^2=121/100. The low state (1/4,1/4) is not in W_2. For epsilon=-1, one final
endpoint is (-5/4,1/4), squared norm 13/8; for epsilon=+1, one is
(7/4,1/4), squared norm 50/16. Both exceed 121/100.

Yet the low state is in conv(W_2): it is the midpoint of (1,1/4) and
(-1/2,1/4). The first point is in W_2 using epsilon=-1; the second is in W_2
using epsilon=+1. Their negatives similarly witness the negative low state.
Thus both endpoint successors of either first sign lie in conv(W_2): one is a
winning high state and the other is a fractional mixture of two winning states.
The convex-hull selector accepts either first sign, while the exact game loses
because the adversary can choose the low endpoint and force failure next round.

Dimension three: v_1=(1/2,1/2,1/10), v_2=(1,0,0). The low state is
(1/4,1/4,1/20), and the high state is (3/4,3/4,3/20). The high state is in
W_2 with epsilon=-1; its endpoint squared norms are 259/400 and 459/400,
both below 121/100. The low state is not in W_2: epsilon=-1 has a failing
endpoint (-5/4,1/4,1/20), squared norm 651/400; epsilon=+1 has a failing
endpoint (7/4,1/4,1/20), squared norm 1251/400. The low state is the midpoint
of (1,1/4,1/20) and (-1/2,1/4,1/20), both in W_2 by direct endpoint checks.
Again, the negative branch follows by symmetry, so the convex-hull selector
accepts either first sign but the exact continuous-noise game loses.

What this establishes

The exact two-step counterexamples reject the natural statewise convex-hull
completion relaxation as a causal selector, even in dimensions two and three,
with Euclidean safety and continuous scalar postdecision noise. They show that
convex membership of each possible successor does not ensure that the actual
successor has a deterministic future continuation.

This is not a hardness result and does not rule out a different compact
polytope with an explicit selector-closure property. In particular, if a
polynomially separable family Q_t and selector regions C_t^epsilon satisfying
the sufficient condition above can be constructed, the policy follows. The
existence of such a family for arbitrary supplied schedules, a polynomial-time
separation/selection oracle for it, and an end-to-end high-dimensional
compiler remain UNKNOWN. No mission-level capability or novelty claim follows.
