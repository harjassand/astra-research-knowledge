# c_nonlinear: certified local thermal repair in interacting Ising models

Cycle 2 addition, 2026-10-06: [value-only shared-direction Gaussian cloud correction](../notes/c_nonlinear/cycle2/central_secant_cloud.md). A separate scoped theorem preserves a derivative-energy covariance budget through central secants in expectation, with explicit finite-step bias, value-precision and rejected-attempt costs. Integrable bridge heat smoothing removes a higher derivative assumption. It uses a stronger contraction scale and dimension-dependent precision; it does not modify or replace the Ising result below.

Status, 2026-10-06: rigorous reconstructed certificate and exact finite numerical checks. **No novelty or multiscale-sampler advantage established.** The positive result is a concrete nonlinear interacting baseline; the obstruction identifies what extra source certificate an advantage claim needs.

## Question and legal interface

Given a finite graph G=(V,E), target couplings K_ij and fields h_i, target

    μ(x) ∝ exp(Σ_{ij∈E} K_ij x_i x_j + Σ_i h_i x_i),   x_i∈{−1,+1},

an arbitrary starting law ν, and a bounded observable f depending on S⊂V, can local target Gibbs updates repair Eνf to Eμf with error and work independent of |V|? Thermal change is K_ij=βJ_ij, h_i=βH_i, with β changed from a supplied old value. No unknown-target inference is hidden in the correction.

Interface requires the adjacency and target parameters in and incident to the queried patch. It does **not** require ν's density, partition function, or conditional law. A sample from ν can be used, but an arbitrary deterministic initial state is also legal. Only f's expectation is certified; the global distribution may remain very far from μ.

Let δ_i(f)=sup{|f(x)−f(y)|: x_j=y_j for j≠i}, and D_f=Σ_{i∈S}δ_i(f). Let B={i:dist(i,S)≤R}, m=|B|. Assume an influence matrix C has C_ij=0 off graph edges and

    TV(μ_i(·|x_{−i}), μ_i(·|y_{−i})) ≤ Σ_j C_ij 1[x_j≠y_j],
    max_{i∈B} Σ_j C_ij ≤ q<1.                     (A)

The simple computable choice is C_ij=tanh|K_ij|. Condition (A) is needed only at sites inside B, including their influences from exterior neighbors. Thus the arbitrary-start certificate can be acquired locally. For q=0 the independent-site case follows directly; formulas below use 0<q<1. If B=V, the spatial boundary term is zero.

## The certificate and algorithm

Freeze exterior spins from ν, or fix them arbitrarily. Make M updates: select a site uniformly from B, redraw it from its **target** conditional Bernoulli law

    Pr(x_i=+1|x_{−i})=[1+tanh(h_i+Σ_j K_ij x_j)]/2.

Let ν_{B,M} be the output law. Then

    |Eν_{B,M} f−Eμ f| ≤ D_f [q^(R+1) + (1−(1−q)/m)^M].       (1)

Exact conditional resampling of the whole B gives the stronger

    |EνT_B f−Eμ f| ≤ D_f q^(R+1).                            (2)

There is no factor |V|. These are standard Dobrushin/spatial-mixing consequences, reconstructed below, not proposed new theorems. They do not cover critical regimes, generic nonconvex continuous φ⁴, or all observables simultaneously at fixed cost.

For error ε<D_f, choose R+1≥log(2D_f/ε)/log(1/q) and

    M ≥ m/(1−q) log(2D_f/ε).

An update costs O(Δ) local arithmetic and one Bernoulli draw. Acquiring incident target parameters costs O(mΔ); working memory is O(mΔ) unless adjacency is queried on demand. On a fixed-dimensional lattice, m=O(|S|(R+1)^d); on a bounded-degree tree it can be exponential in R. Volume independence does not imply dimension independence, critical-temperature efficiency, or small constants. Exact conditional enumeration costs O(2^m mΔ), so efficient approximate heat bath is the main algorithm.

For a bounded f with range width W, independent algorithm runs give Hoeffding sampling error ≤W sqrt(log(2/α)/(2N)) with probability ≥1−α. This must be added to the bias in (1). N runs cost N times patch work. Reusing correlated source samples does not justify this confidence statement; fixed initial state with independent update randomness does.

## Complete proof reconstructed from coupling

1. **One-site influence.** Write a=h_i+Σ_{k≠j}K_ikx_k. Flipping x_j changes the probability of +1 by

       |tanh(a+K_ij)−tanh(a−K_ij)|/2 ≤ tanh|K_ij|.

   This follows by maximizing the even function of a at a=0. Telescope changes at several sites. The exact field-sensitive C_ij can instead be computed by maximizing this expression over the 2^(deg(i)−1) configurations of other neighbors; this is potentially costly but purely local.

2. **Coupled dynamics.** Couple initial X∼ν and Y∼μ arbitrarily. Freeze both exteriors, choose the same random site in B, and maximally couple their target conditional Bernoulli draws. Y remains distributed as μ since every single-site target heat bath preserves μ. Let p_i^k=Pr(X_i^k≠Y_i^k). For i∈B,

       p_i^(k+1) ≤ (1−1/m)p_i^k + (1/m)Σ_{j∈B}C_ij p_j^k
                  + (1/m)Σ_{j∉B}C_ij p_j^0.

   Exterior p_j^k=p_j^0≤1. Let A=(1−1/m)I+C_BB/m, c=C_{B,V\B}1, and H=(I−C_BB)^−1 c. All matrices are nonnegative and the inverse exists because ||C_BB||∞≤q<1. Iteration gives

       p_B^M ≤ H + A^M(p_B^0−H) ≤ H+A^M1.

   Since A1≤λ1 for λ=1−(1−q)/m, A^M1≤λ^M1.

3. **Spatial barrier.** Set d_i=dist(i,V\B), w_i=q^d_i inside B and w_j=1 outside. Adjacent vertices obey d_j≥d_i−1. Therefore

       Σ_{j∈B}C_ij w_j + c_i ≤ Σ_j C_ij q^(d_i−1) ≤ q^d_i=w_i.

   Multiplying the nonnegative inverse shows H≤w. Every i∈S has d_i≥R+1. If a connected component of B never meets the exterior, H=0 there. Consequently p_i^M≤q^(R+1)+λ^M on S.

4. **Observable.** Telescope coordinates to get |f(X)−f(Y)|≤Σ_iδ_i(f)1[X_i≠Y_i]. Take expectations. This proves (1). Let M→∞; the finite strictly positive heat-bath chain on B converges to its unique target conditional Gibbs law, proving (2).

No finite experiment is used in this proof. The simultaneous coupling also yields the sharper matrix certificate

    bias ≤ Σ_{i∈S}δ_i(f)[H_i+(A^M1)_i],

which may be much better than the scalar q bound. One can compute H and A^M1 from the acquired patch, with all linear algebra charged. Bound H by min(H,1) componentwise when helpful; the full bias can also be capped by W.

## Certified thermal warm start: what actually yields a gain

Suppose ν is **exactly** another Gibbs law μ₀ with known K⁰,h⁰. This is a substantive source guarantee, not inferred from a learned sampler's name or training loss. Now assume the target row-sum bound q<1 holds globally, or supply a global bound on the source/target coupling below. Define uniform local conditional discrepancies

    b_i=sup_x TV(μ_i(·|x),μ⁰_i(·|x))
       ≤ min{1, (|h_i−h⁰_i|+Σ_j|K_ij−K⁰_ij|)/2}.

The derivative of the +1 conditional probability in its scalar effective field is at most 1/2, proving this bound. Set η=min{1, ||b||∞/(1−q)}.

To prove an initial coupling with p_i^0≤η, use a joint random-site chain updating one marginal under μ₀ and the other under μ with maximal Bernoulli coupling. A stationary joint law exists in the finite space; each marginal is the unique appropriate Gibbs law. Stationarity implies p≤Cp+b. Iterating gives p≤Σ_{k≥0}C^kb≤||b||∞/(1−q), and trivially p≤1. This constructs one simultaneous coupling, rather than separate pairwise marginal couplings.

Run the target patch dynamics under that coupling. Exterior mismatch is at most η, initial interior mismatch is at most η, so the same calculation yields

    |Eμ₀, B,M f−Eμ f| ≤ η D_f [q^(R+1)+λ^M],
    |Eμ₀ T_B f−Eμ f| ≤ η D_f q^(R+1).                       (3)

For thermal change, b_i≤|β−β₀|(|H_i|+Σ_j|J_ij|)/2. A sufficiently small certified drift can shrink both radius and relaxation time. If ηD_f≤ε, simply retaining the old sample is already a competent zero-repair baseline. Small drift alone is not an algorithmic advantage over that baseline.

Acquisition caveat: verifying global q and ||b||∞ for an arbitrary inhomogeneous graph generally costs O(|V|Δ). A uniform supplied structural bound avoids that cost; a scan of one patch does not establish a global warm-start certificate. For an arbitrary proposal ν, uniform conditional residual bounds could substitute for b, but obtaining them is an additional task. Empirical conditional tests on sampled configurations do not establish their suprema. The arbitrary-start result remains valid with η=1.

## Strong baselines and exact discriminating experiment

The equally informed fresh baseline initializes B and its exterior arbitrarily and runs the same target patch chain. It has (1) with η=1. For exact patch sampling, retaining ν contributes only its boundary law; it contributes no interior information at all. Hence a universal proposal advantage cannot be inferred from (2).

`work/c_nonlinear/ising_repair.py` implements exact conditional enumeration, exact finite heat-bath distribution evolution, and analytic zero-field cycle transfer formulas. `results/c_nonlinear/ising_repair_checks.json` records every value. Tests use β₀=.20, β=.35, n∈{12,32,128,1024}, R∈{0,1,2,3}, M∈{0,m,5m,20m}; all finite inequalities passed. Full-ring enumeration at n=10 independently crosschecks the transfer formulas and reduced boundary law. A distinct full-state implementation additionally tests 30 random seven-site graphs with signed edges and nonzero fields, both all-plus arbitrary starts and exact old-Gibbs thermal starts, two radii, and all M=0,...,50: all 6,120 inequalities passed. Computations use float64, not interval arithmetic.

For f=x₀x₁ at n=1024, the target mean is .3363755443363322, and the uncorrected old mean is .197375320224904. Exact repair absolute errors at R=0,1,2,3 are respectively about .00305205, .0000454955, .0000006110, .0000000079482. Fresh independent fair boundary spins give .00382475, .0000488966, .0000006260, .0000000080143. The certified warm-start η=.458367 is conservative; the scalar bounds are substantially looser than the actual errors.

This distinguishes correction from advantage: repair works, but old boundary reuse buys little against this competent fresh local baseline. Moreover, for the 1D cycle a transfer-matrix/closed-form method computes the observable directly; this is an even stronger baseline. The experiment checks a mechanism, not computational superiority. A 2D experiment would remove the trivial 1D baseline but would still need exact or independently certified comparison data.

## Decisive obstruction to a temperature-uniform local claim

On a zero-field cycle the target central magnetization is exactly zero by spin-flip symmetry. Resample a radius-R interval conditional on exterior spins all +. Let t=tanh β and d=R+1. The exact central mean is

    m_R(β)=2 t^d/(1+t^(2d)).

Derivation: the zero-field chain is a binary Markov chain with d-step transition Pr(y|x)=(1+xy t^d)/2. Conditioning on plus endpoints distance d to either side gives weights (1+x t^d)^2/4, whose normalized mean is the displayed expression. For every fixed R, m_R(β)→1 as β→∞. Therefore no temperature-independent finite patch radius can achieve small error uniformly over all interacting targets and arbitrary source phase/boundary choices. Taking cycles larger than the patch avoids wraparound. The finite code tabulates this exact formula; the limit argument supplies the obstruction.

This does not prove impossibility of all local algorithms: a zero-field magnetization query can exploit symmetry and return zero, and independent fair boundary randomization cancels this particular odd observable. It obstructs **arbitrary-boundary patch repair** with radius independent of temperature. It also does not say Dobrushin is necessary: the 1D model has spatial decay at every finite β even when 2 tanh β≥1.

## Prior art, author claims, and novelty status

Verified prerequisite: Rebeschini and van Handel, *Comparison Theorems for Gibbs Measures*, arXiv:1308.4117v1, submitted 2013-08-19; journal J. Stat. Phys. 157 (2014), 234–281. Read excerpts: §2.3 Corollary 2.6 and §2.4 Corollary 2.8; preserved v1 PDF and HTML. Their classical comparison result controls observable error by influence-resolvent times conditional-law discrepancies. Their block generalization is broader. Our elementary coupling specializes that known mechanism to patch repair and explicitly accounts for finite update work. No new foundational theorem is claimed.

Feng, Guo, Yin, *Perfect sampling from spatial mixing*, published online 2022-02-18, issue December 2022, Random Structures & Algorithms 61(4), 678–709; verified metadata and introductory excerpts. Their sampler is substantially more ambitious: perfect global sampling under spatial-mixing versus neighborhood-growth assumptions; the introduction also identifies dynamic sampling when Gibbs parameters change. This is relevant prior art against calling parameter repair novel. Full paper was not read. A later web attempt to read §7 triggered a browser challenge; the initial successful HTML snapshot is preserved.

Recent window entry: Yi Han, *Glauber dynamics for random field Ising models on bounded degree graphs and MLSI*, arXiv:2605.02002v1, 2026-05-03. Read introductory excerpts through Theorems 1.2/1.5/1.10 and the warm-start condition in Lemma 5.6. The author supplies probabilistic-over-disorder polynomial mixing and MLSI under anti-concentration/boundedness conditions, and a warm-start sampling result under averaged weak spatial mixing plus growth restrictions. These are not pointwise arbitrary-boundary local q<1 certificates. No full-proof validation or adoption of these claims has been performed here.

## Downstream use and next falsifiable step

Use (1) as a mandatory nonlinear local baseline when auditing multiscale field samplers: perturb β, choose a physical local observable, charge all target conditional evaluations, parameter acquisition, source sample generation, and confidence runs. A learned scheme must improve on fresh patch sampling and on retaining an already-certified nearby source, rather than comparing only against full-volume global re-equilibration.

The promising unresolved direction is an **acquired local certificate beyond pointwise Dobrushin**, perhaps using block influences or validated field-disorder rare-region bounds. The load-bearing missing lemma is a source/data-access-costed, target-local mixing/boundary certificate that does not assume its acquisition. Han's disorder-average theorem does not fill that gap automatically.

Next test: at equal charged target-update budget on 2D inhomogeneous Ising, compare repaired old Gibbs samples, fixed and randomized fresh boundaries, and an exact conditional/block baseline; independently bound relevant boundary sensitivities rather than assuming them from training. Reject an advantage if differences fall below sampling uncertainty or if computing the certificate costs the claimed gain. Block comparison and critical-regime extensions remain unexplored here.
