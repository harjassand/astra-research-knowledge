# A relative radial derivative condition excludes all-exponential decay

7 October 2026. **Root derivation using the standard positive-commutator method.** Independently reconstructed by r07 and r15. No novelty claim: locating the closest classical statement is a separate prior task. This identifies a sufficient hypothesis missing from the real counterexamples; it is not a new general resolution of Landis.

## Statement and interface

Let V be real, bounded and C^1 on R^n. Suppose that for some beta>0, C and R0,

    partial_r V(x) <= C |x|^(-1-beta), |x|>=R0.

Only an upper bound is needed; tangential derivatives and the negative part of the radial derivative are unrestricted by this condition. Let u solve (-Delta+V)u=0 weakly and assume exp(A|x|)u belongs to L^2 for every A>0. Then u=0. Real and complex solutions are covered because the operator is real. The proof below works for n>=2, the dimensions relevant here; the exterior-component version also works in one dimension.

Thus the customary two-sided relative bound |grad V|<=C r^(-1-beta) implies this conclusion. Mere D^mV tending to zero for every fixed order does not imply it. A bound with beta=0 is not proved by this particular argument, but is ALREADY covered by the stronger established result below.

## An asymptotically linear convex weight

Put gamma=min(beta,1/2), f(r)=r-r^(1-gamma), phi=tau f. Increase R0 so r>=1 and

    1/2 <= f'(r)=1-(1-gamma)r^(-gamma) <=1,
    f''(r)=gamma(1-gamma)r^(-1-gamma)>0.

The radial and tangential eigenvalues of Hess f are f'' and f'/r, so Hess f is positive. The radial Laplacian identities give

    Delta^2 r=(n-1)(3-n)r^(-3),
    Delta^2 r^(1-gamma)=O_(n,gamma)(r^(-3-gamma)).

In particular |Delta^2 f|<=C r^(-3). All calculations are confined to r>R0, away from the origin.

## Exact commutator and coercivity

For a smooth function w compactly supported in that exterior region, put z=exp(phi)w and H=-Delta+V. Then

    exp(phi) H exp(-phi) = S+A,
    S=-Delta-|grad phi|^2+V,
    A=2 grad phi dot grad + Delta phi.

On compactly supported functions S is symmetric and A is skew symmetric. Therefore

    ||exp(phi)Hw||_2^2
      =||Sz||_2^2+||Az||_2^2+ <z,[S,A]z>.

Integration by parts, or separately real and imaginary parts, gives

    <z,[S,A]z>
      =4 integral Hess phi(grad z,grad conjugate(z))
       +integral [4 Hess phi(grad phi,grad phi)
                  -Delta^2 phi-2 grad phi dot grad V] |z|^2.

The first integral is nonnegative. The cubic term is

    4 tau^3 f''(f')^2 >= gamma(1-gamma) tau^3 r^(-1-gamma).

The potential term is bounded below by -2C tau r^(-1-beta), using f'>0 and the ONE-SIDED hypothesis. Negative radial derivatives only improve this estimate. Since beta>=gamma and r>=1, this error is at most C tau r^(-1-gamma) in magnitude. The biharmonic weight error O(tau r^-3) obeys the same upper bound. Choose tau>=tau0 large, independently of the support of w. Then

    c tau^3 integral r^(-1-gamma) exp(2tau f)|w|^2
      <= ||exp(tau f)Hw||_2^2.                         (1)

The norm and the sign of the commutator are important: including V in the symmetric part avoids a squared-amplitude error. This is a positive-commutator estimate for real V, not the complex-potential endpoint estimate in Cruz-Sampedro.

## Cutoffs and the order of limits

Bounded V and the equation imply local H^2 regularity. The usual unit-ball Caccioppoli bound, summed with exponential weights whose ratio on each doubled ball is bounded, shows exp(A r)grad u belongs to L^2 for every fixed A>0. This uses only the assumed exponential L^2 bounds on u and boundedness of V.

Choose a fixed inner cutoff h, zero on r<=R0+1/2 and one on r>=R0+1. Multiply h u by an outer cutoff chi_T equal to one on r<=T and zero on r>=2T, with first/second derivatives O(1/T), O(1/T^2). Apply (1) by H^2 approximation to w=h chi_T u. Since Hu=0, the forcing consists only of cutoff commutators.

First keep tau fixed and let T tend to infinity. The outer forcing tends to zero in weighted L^2: f(r)<=r and exp(tau r)u, exp(tau r)grad u are in L^2. This does not assume a decay rate uniform in tau. One obtains

    c tau^3 integral r^(-1-gamma) exp(2tau f)|h u|^2
      <= integral_(R0+1/2<r<R0+1) exp(2tau f)|[H,h]u|^2.

For any fixed b>R0+1, f(b)>f(R0+1). Restrict the left integral to r>=b, divide by exp(2tau f(b)), and send tau to infinity. The finite unweighted annular forcing norm on the right is multiplied by a strictly decaying exponential. Hence the weighted L^2 mass of u on r>=b is zero. Thus u has compact support. Standard local unique continuation for bounded V yields u=0 on R^n.

## Consequence for selection

The real arbitrary-loss construction can have every absolute derivative tending to zero while violating this outward radial bound. The relative first-derivative direction is therefore not an unproved target at this strength: this standard commutator already excludes all-exponential decay. The next useful targets must weaken this precise condition, identify its necessity for the constructed examples, or address the still unresolved scalar Euclidean finite-index question.

## Stronger exact prior subsequently recovered

Escauriaza–Kenig–Ponce–Vega, arXiv1010.1906v1, Theorem5 equations1.27–1.31, already proves exponential uniqueness under (partial_r V)_+<=C/r for real bounded V, after a smooth exterior cutoff and the sign conversion V_tilde=-V. The compact remainder satisfies its decaying V1 bound; the cutoff derivative is absorbed into C/r on its compact annulus. Root directly read the primary theorem and Theorem6; r04 and r07 independently checked the specialization. Its linear-weight proof uses additional coercivity from S squared. Thus this note reconstructs a strictly weaker covered case. See notes/r04_statphys/cycle6/ONE_SIDED_RADIAL_PRIOR.md. The positive radial dilation r(partial_r V)_+ must be unbounded along our nonzero all-exponential examples; otherwise that established theorem would exclude them. This implication is a useful diagnostic, not a new uniqueness theorem.
