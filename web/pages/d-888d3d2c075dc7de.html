1. Exact space, transports, and generators
-----------------------------------------
Let X=CP^2 with invariant probability dv. For a normal line v, the fiber
Y_v=CP(v^perp) is CP^1, identified metrically with the unit two-sphere
whose positive Laplacian C_v has eigenvalues ell(ell+1). Use its invariant
probability du_v. The full flag Y={(v,u):u a line in v^perp} has measure
dnu(v,u)=dv du_v. Scalar Lp norms throughout refer to this probability.

For almost every nonorthogonal v,w let T_{w<-v}:Y_v->Y_w be the actual
unitary plane transport along the canonical horizontal geodesic. It is
a CP^1 isometry, T_{v<-w}=T_{w<-v}^{-1}, and it preserves du. Phases
of determinant/cold lines do not affect the transported CP^1 point.
Make any measurable choice on the zero-measure orthogonal locus. Only
the inverse/isometry properties are used; NO flatness is assumed.

For integer M>=1 put d_M=(M+1)(M+2)/2 and
 K_M(v,w)=d_M |<v,w>|^{2M},
 (H_M g)(v,u)=int_X K_M(v,w)g(w,T_{w<-v}u)dw.

K_M is a symmetric probability kernel, since x=|<v,w>|^2 has law
2(1-x)dx and int_0^1 2x^M(1-x)dx=1/d_M. H_M is positive as a map
of nonnegative functions, preserves constants, and is an Lp contraction.
Changing variables (v,w,u)->(w,v,T_{w<-v}u) proves self-adjointness
on L2(Y). Its spectral values may be negative, but lie in[-1,1].

C_V acts as C_v on each fiber. It is the nonnegative self-adjoint
direct-integral spherical Laplacian; E_V(g)=<g,C_V g>. Its heat
V_s=exp(-s C_V) is a probability Markov operator on every fiber.
Each T is a spherical isometry, hence intertwines all vertical harmonic
projections and vertical heat operators. H_M consequently COMMUTES
STRONGLY with C_V and V_s. The products along a base path may have
nontrivial holonomy; strong commutation does not remove that holonomy.

For integer k>=1 and t>=1 define the discrete smoothing operator
 B_t=H_M^t exp[-(t/k^2)C_V].
Equivalently B_t=P^t for P=H_M exp[-C_V/k^2]. This is not the continuous
semigroup exp[-t((I-H_M)+C_V/k^2)].

2. General isometric-bundle path lemma
--------------------------------------
The following argument applies to any base probability Markov kernel K,
any measured compact fibers with compatible isometries T_{w<-v}, and
any fiber Markov heat invariant under those isometries. Reversibility is
needed for the later Nash step, not for the path kernel bound itself.

For a base path v=v_0,v_1,...,v_t=w write
 T_path=T_{v_t<-v_{t-1}} ... T_{v_1<-v_0}.
Let p_s^w(z,u') denote the vertical probability heat density on Y_w.
The kernel of H^t V_s on the total space is exactly

 b_{t,s}((v,u),(w,u'))
  =int_{X^{t-1}} product_{j=1}^t K(v_{j-1},v_j)
                         p_s^w(T_path u,u') dv_1...dv_{t-1}.       (P)

This follows by iterating the definition of H and then applying heat at
the final fiber. Commutation is an alternative justification for placing
the heat at the final fiber. Every term is nonnegative. It integrates
to one in the endpoint and, under reversibility, in the start point.
Taking the uniform vertical heat bound INSIDE the path integral gives

 b_{t,s}((v,u),(w,u'))<=h_V(s)K^t(v,w),
 ||H^t V_s||_{1->infinity}<=h_X(t)h_V(s).                         (2.1)

The same bound applies to the essential kernel supremum. For nonnegative
kernels it gives the operator norm; approximation to point masses gives
the converse equality between that norm and the essential supremum.
Here all fibers have the same vertical heat bound. The transport still
appears in(P); the estimate takes its supremum, not a product-model
identification or a change of the physical horizontal walk.

3. Exact finite CP2 spectrum and an explicit heat constant
----------------------------------------------------------
For completeness the scalar Berezin computation is reconstructed. The
CP2 harmonic of bidegree(ell,ell), with its traces removed, is the SU3
type(ell,ell), dimension h_ell=(ell+1)^3. This also follows by subtracting
the trace subspace dimensions:
 binom(ell+2,2)^2-binom(ell+1,2)^2=(ell+1)^3.
These harmonic spaces are complete: phase-invariant coordinate
polynomials are dense in C(CP2), and harmonic decomposition removes
the factors |w|^2 successively. Their irreducibility is the standard
Cartan highest-weight decomposition, whose Weyl dimension is precisely
the difference just displayed. Invariant K_M is scalar on each type.

Take the highest harmonic phi_ell(w)=w_1^ell conjugate(w_3)^ell and
v=(e_1+e_3)/sqrt(2). In expanding |<v,w>|^{2M}, balance phases so the
bar-w_1 degree exceeds the w_1 degree by ell. For M>=ell the integrated
sum is
 d_M 2^{-M} [2/(M+ell+2)!]
       sum_{j=0}^{M-ell} binom(M,j)binom(M,j+ell)(j+ell)!(M-j)!.
The sum equals (M!)^2 2^{M-ell}/(M-ell)!. Divide by
phi_ell(v)=2^{-ell}. The exact eigenvalues are therefore

 beta_ell=M!(M+2)!/[(M-ell)!(M+ell+2)!], 0<=ell<=M,
 beta_ell=0 for ell>M.                                      (3.1)

This calculation uses the elementary Dirichlet moment
int |w_1|^{2a}|w_3|^{2b}dw=2 a!b!/(a+b+2)!. All beta are nonnegative.
By the addition theorem and Cauchy--Schwarz for positive spectral kernels,
their supremum is their constant diagonal value:

 h_X(t)=||K_M^t||_{1->infinity}
            =sum_{ell=0}^M (ell+1)^3 beta_ell^t.             (3.2)

Here is a deliberately crude explicit estimate independent of the owner
packet's heat constant. For ell>=1,
 beta_ell=product_{j=0}^{ell-1}(M-j)/(M+3+j),
 log beta_ell<=-sum(3+2j)/(M+3+j)
              <=-ell(ell+2)/[2(M+1)]<=-ell^2/[2(M+1)].
Set z=t/[2(M+1)]. For1<=t<=M+1,0<z<=1/2. Comparing the ell>=1 sum
termwise to int_0^infinity(x+2)^3 exp(-z x^2)dx gives

 h_X(t)<=1+1/(2z^2)+3sqrt(pi)/(2z^{3/2})+6/z+4sqrt(pi)/sqrt(z).

Since sqrt(pi)<2 and z<=1/2, the coefficient after multiplying by z^2
is at most 1/4+1/2+3+3+4=43/4. Hence

 h_X(t)<=43(M+1)^2/t^2<=86 d_M/t^2, 1<=t<=M+1.             (3.3)

In particular the owner constant H_2=128 is safe. No large-M expansion
or fixed-degree error is used; the complete finite spectrum and zero
complement are included. The beta_0=1 floor forbids extending a pure
t^-2 estimate indefinitely without a time window.

4. Explicit CP1 vertical heat bound
-----------------------------------
On the unit sphere with probability measure, the vertical heat kernel
is the sum of spherical harmonic projectors. Its constant diagonal is

 h_V(s)=sum_{ell>=0}(2ell+1)exp[-s ell(ell+1)].

The addition theorem bounds every off-diagonal value in absolute value
by this diagonal. Nonnegativity and mass conservation follow from the
spherical heat equation maximum principle and constant eigenfunction.
For0<s<=1, replace ell(ell+1) by ell^2 and compare the ell>=1 terms
on[x=ell-1,ell] with(2x+3)exp(-s x^2). Then

 h_V(s)<=1+1/s+3sqrt(pi)/(2sqrt(s))<=5/s.                  (4.1)

The last numerical inequality uses sqrt(pi)<2: s+1+3sqrt(s)<=5 for
s<=1. The generator's ell(ell+1) normalization is essential; a different
metric normalization rescales s and the explicit constant.

5. Full-flag heat and Nash theorem
-----------------------------------
Combining(2.1),(3.3),(4.1) with s=t/k^2 proves

 ||B_t||_{1->infinity}<=430 d_M k^2/t^3,
             1<=t<=min(M+1,k^2), t integer.                (5.1)

Using the owner's H_2=128 instead gives5H_2 d_M k^2/t^3, exactly their
stated bound. In particular, for m~k^2 and M=m+k this is
O(m^2 k^2/t^3), with the six real dimensions of the full scalar flag.

For every g in L2(Y) with finite E_V(g), define
 E_H(g)=<g,(I-H_M)g>.
Joint spectral calculus is justified by strong commutation. On joint
spectral values b in[-1,1], c>=0,

 1-b^t exp(-tc/k^2)
 =1-b^t+b^t[1-exp(-tc/k^2)]
 <=1-b^t+1-exp(-tc/k^2)
 <=t(1-b)+tc/k^2.                                        (5.2)

The first inequality uses b^t<=1 and a nonnegative bracket. For the
second, 1-b^t=(1-b)sum_{j=0}^{t-1}b^j<=t(1-b), because b<=1;
all powers b^j<=1 even when b is negative. Positivity of H_M as an
L2 operator is not needed, and no unjustified spectral lazification is
made. Therefore

 ||g||_2^2<=t E_H(g)+(t/k^2)E_V(g)+<g,B_t g>
          <=t[E_H(g)+E_V(g)/k^2]+h_B(t)||g||_1^2.         (5.3)

The last bound is valid even for complex g by taking absolute values
inside the nonnegative bounded kernel; the carrier symbol is positive.
Equations(5.1),(5.3) are the claimed rigorous heat/Nash module, with no
horizontal differential comparator and no uncharged spectral cutoff.

8. Exact failure of the tempting continuous-time full heat estimate
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Let A=(I-H_M)+C_V/k^2. Strong commutation gives

 exp(-tA)=e^{-t} sum_{j>=0}(t^j/j!) H_M^j V_{t/k^2}.

All summands preserve positivity. The j=0 term has weight e^{-t}>0
and diffuses only inside the original fiber, leaving the CP2 base fixed.
For a base set B of arbitrarily small positive measure, take
g_B(v,u)=1_B(v)/dv(B). It is constant in the fiber, has L1 norm1, and
V_s g_B=g_B. Therefore

 ||exp(-tA)g_B||_infinity>=e^{-t}/dv(B) ->infinity.

Thus ||exp(-tA)||_{1->infinity}=infinity for EVERY finite t>0 on the
full scalar flag. No tiny e^{-t} coefficient repairs this operator norm.
A finite bandlimited input subspace could absorb that coefficient with
a dimension-dependent estimate, but that would be a different, charged
cutoff theorem. The discrete B_t has at least one true horizontal jump
and has the bounded kernel proved above. Mixing the two time conventions
would invalidate the heat proof; the owner uses the valid discrete one.
