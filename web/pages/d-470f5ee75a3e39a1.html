GAMES AND HAMILTONIANS — CYCLE 3 HIGH-CEILING ROUTE
Rational filters, local detectability sweeps, and the BMVZ history image
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS

INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the operator identities, contraction costs,
rational-filter support lower bound, and sparse Schur-lift calculation below.
The imported detectability-lemma estimate is EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED (AAV,
Lemma 2/Corollary 3). This audits amplification on the particular full-list
history isometry built in Cycle 2. It is not a no-go for all common
isometries, coherent active workspace, or arbitrary positive sparse
enforcers. It does not compose with L02: L02 supplies no finite projector
family or common encoding to which these calculations apply.

DECISIVE RESULT

The full augmented BMVZ list has a Cycle-2 history isometry V with

    ker C = ran V,            C >= gamma_K (I - VV*),
    gamma_K = (1-cos(pi/K))/2 = Theta(K^-2),
    maximum row-support(C) <= 9,
    V* B V = (Q/K) H,         maximum row-support(B) = 1,

where K is polynomial. The BMVZ-admissible augmentation has two singleton
colors whose T-copy terms are the repeated BJSW seeds phi and psi. In the
chosen Clifford decoder convention, every row of U_phi U_psi* has exactly
8 nonzero entries, so every row of U_phi^tensor T U_psi*^tensor T has 8^T.
T is the polynomially growing BMVZ repetition count.

Three routes have different exact losses:

1. A Chebyshev polynomial gives a positive constant-gap operator with
   degree Theta(K log(1/epsilon)); its direct row-support upper bound is
   10^Theta(K log(1/epsilon)). Coefficientwise nonnegative polynomials in C
   cannot amplify the normalized gap at all.
2. A two-layer detectability sweep on the clock-edge projectors needs
   q=Theta(K^2) sweeps. Its positive output F=I-(D^q)*D^q has a constant
   all-state gap, but on this actual history image it has a row with at
   least 8^T nonzeros. This uses strict positivity of the clock transition
   matrix and the exact seed-relative Clifford support; it does not repeat
   Sol's fixed-image Chebyshev row-mass theorem.
3. The rational filter R_tau(C)=C(C+tau I)^(-1), tau=K^-2, keeps the exact
   image and has gap at least 1/2, but its transported resolvent has a
   cross-tail block with 8^T entries per row. A sparse positive Schur lift
   retains exact logical compression and row support at most 10, but its
   normalized all-state gap remains Theta(K^-2). Scaling it for constant
   absolute gap costs norm Theta(K^2), so normalization erases the gain.

No route supplies both a constant normalized image gap and polynomial row
support. The rational Schur lift makes the workspace/norm loss explicit
while preserving the common logical test.

The source interface is the BMVZ weighted layered Hamiltonian and its
path-term generator. The two BJSW projectors are appended on a disjoint
five-qubit block as singleton colors of weight 1/4 each, while the original
hard family is scaled by 1/2. This preserves the promise gap up to a
constant and forces the repeated seed terms without a self-loop assumption.
This augmentation and the history isometry are Cycle-2 constructions, not
claims made by the cited papers.

The fixed history clock has K positions, decoder U_s at position s, and
successive local transition gates G_s satisfying U_(s+1)=G_s U_s. Define

    V|psi> = K^(-1/2) sum_s |s> U_s|psi>,
    W       = sum_s |s><s| tensor U_s.

For each edge s write

    e_s = (1/4) [ |s><s| + |s+1><s+1|
                  - |s+1><s| G_s - |s><s+1| G_s* ],
    C = sum_(s=0)^(K-2) e_s.

Then W* C W = (L_path/4) tensor I. The path Laplacian eigenvalues are
2-2 cos(j pi/K), j=0,...,K-1, so C has first positive eigenvalue
gamma_K=(1-cos(pi/K)) / 2 and maximum eigenvalue 1-gamma_K. The kernel is
exactly ran V. Each e_s is positive with norm 1/2 and row support at most
9. The diagonal positive contraction B from Cycle 2 obeys the exact identity
V*BV=(Q/K)H, with Q/K>1/4. It is 1-row-sparse. Its diagonal predicate
may inspect all data registers, so B is not asserted to have constant
qubit-locality.

The selected phi and psi tails have decoders U_phi^tensor T and
U_psi^tensor T. In the exact BJSW Clifford fixture,
U_phi U_psi*=c_phi* c_psi and each row has 8 nonzeros (squared modulus
1/8), as checked over Q(sqrt(2)) in the Cycle-2 exact test. Tensoring over T
blocks gives exactly 8^T nonzeros per row. A row of a transformed history
operator restricted to one clock column has the support of its relative
decoder whenever the scalar clock coefficient is nonzero.

1. POLYNOMIAL FILTER COSTS AND A POSITIVE-COEFFICIENT OBSTRUCTION

The bound gamma_K=sin^2(pi/(2K)) >= K^-2 for K>=2 follows from
sin(x)>=2x/pi on [0,pi/2]. Use the rational lower bound gamma_0=K^-2, so
the filter coefficients need no algebraic approximation to the gap.

Let

    a=(1+gamma_0)/(1-gamma_0),  b=2/(1-gamma_0),
    p_d(x)=T_d(a-bx)/T_d(a).

Here T_d is the Chebyshev polynomial. We have a=cosh(theta) for
theta=2 atanh(1/K), and p_d(0)=1. On [0,gamma_0], its argument lies in
[1,a], hence 0<p_d(x)<=1. On [gamma_0,1], its argument lies in [-1,1],
so

    |p_d(x)| <= 1/T_d(a) <= 2 exp(-d theta).

For fixed 0<epsilon<1, choose

    d >= log(2/epsilon) / (2 atanh(1/K))
      = Theta(K log(1/epsilon)).

Then the positive contraction

    R_d = (I-p_d(C))/2

has exact kernel ran V and gap at least (1-epsilon)/2 on its orthogonal
complement. V and B are unchanged, so V*BV=(Q/K)H remains exact.

This does not certify a sparse enforcer. If C has row support at most s,
then C^j has row support at most s^j, so a degree-d polynomial in C has row
support at most

    1+s+...+s^d <= (s+1)^d.

For s=9, the direct filter representation has at most 10^d entries per
row. Fixed epsilon gives d=Theta(K), and K is polynomial in input size.
This is a potentially exponential row-support bound. No claim is made that
all Chebyshev cancellations are absent; the DL and rational constructions
below have exact row lower bounds.

Coefficient and precision costs are explicit. For K>=2, gamma_0<=1/4,
a<=5/3, b<=8/3. If ||q||_1 is the sum of absolute monomial coefficients,
the Chebyshev recurrence gives

    ||T_(j+1)(a-bx)||_1
      <= 2(a+b)||T_j(a-bx)||_1 + ||T_(j-1)(a-bx)||_1
      <= 15 ||T_j(a-bx)||_1.

Thus ||p_d||_1<=15^d and ||R_d||_1<=15^d. The coefficients are rational
with bit height O(d log K): the affine substitution denominator divides
(K^2-1)^d and the integer Chebyshev coefficients have O(d) bits. For
exact algebraic C from Cycle 2, exact output coefficients and entries can
therefore be represented with polynomial bit length when K,d are
polynomial.

If C entries are supplied only to operator error delta and both matrices
are contractions, telescoping powers gives
||C^j-C_tilde^j||<=j delta, hence
||R_d(C)-R_d(C_tilde)||<=d 15^d delta. Since each row of C has at most
9 entries, per-entry error rho implies delta<=9 rho by the Hermitian
row/column-sum bound. Error at most eta in the filter is guaranteed by

    rho <= eta/(9 d 15^d),

or O(d+log(d/eta)) bits. This is a numerical stability cost, not an
exact-kernel guarantee: approximate local matrices can move the kernel.
The exact filter uses the exact algebraic C and rational coefficients.

There is a sharper degree-independent obstruction for positive-coefficient
power filters. Suppose p(x)=sum_(j=1)^d a_j x^j with a_j>=0. Then p(C) is
positive and is a positive sum of PSD matrices C^j. Writing
Lambda=||C||=1-gamma_K>=1/2, we have, for every j>=1,

    gamma_K^j <= (gamma_K/Lambda) Lambda^j,

and therefore

    p(gamma_K) <= (gamma_K/Lambda) p(Lambda)
                <= 2 gamma_K ||p(C)||.

After normalizing p(C) to norm at most one, its first positive eigenvalue
is at most 2 gamma_K. No degree can turn a nonnegative linear combination
of powers of this C into a constant normalized-gap enforcer. Signed
coefficients (as in Chebyshev) or a non-polynomial functional calculus are
necessary for this single-matrix route. This is about coefficientwise
nonnegative p, not every polynomial nonnegative on the spectrum.

2. LOCAL DETECTABILITY SWEEPS: CONSTANT GAP, EXACT ROW DENSITY

Set Q_s=2e_s. Each Q_s is a projector and sum_s Q_s=2C. In the W gauge,

    Q_s^0=(1/2)(|s>-|s+1>)(<s|-<s+1|) tensor I.

Non-neighboring edge projectors have orthogonal clock supports and commute;
adjacent ones do not. Thus each projector fails to commute with at most
g=2 others. The gap of the projector sum is Gamma=2 gamma_K.

Let P_s=I-Q_s. Even-edge projectors commute with one another, as do the
odd-edge projectors. Define

    Pi_even = product_(s even) P_s,
    Pi_odd  = product_(s odd) P_s,
    D       = Pi_odd Pi_even.

AAV Corollary 3 gives for every unit psi perpendicular to ran V,

    ||D psi||^2 <= 1/(1+Gamma/4).

Thus for q sweeps

    F_q = I - (D^q)*D^q

is a positive contraction with exact kernel ran V and gap at least
1-(1+Gamma/4)^(-q). Choosing
q >= log(2)/log(1+Gamma/4)=Theta(K^2) makes the gap at least 1/2.
The logical test is unchanged, hence V*BV=(Q/K)H exactly.

For this history image F_q is exactly row-dense on the seed pair, not just
uncontrolled by a generic sparsity estimate. In the W gauge each P_s^0
averages clock amplitudes at s,s+1: its edge block is
(1/2)[[1,1],[1,1]], and it is identity elsewhere. Hence Pi_even and
Pi_odd are nonnegative doubly-stochastic matrices. Their product D_0 has
positive diagonal entries and positive entries at every neighboring pair:
move across the desired edge in its matching and stay in the other; staying
in both matchings also has positive probability. A nearest-neighbor path
from s to t, padded by these self-loops, gives

    (D_0^q)_(s,t)>0 for every s,t whenever q>=K-1.

The constant-gap choice q=Theta(K^2) satisfies this. Therefore every entry
of (D_0^q)*D_0^q is strictly positive, and every off-diagonal entry of

    F_q^0=I-(D_0^q)*D_0^q

is strictly negative. Returning to the history basis, for s!=t,

    (F_q)_(s,t)=(F_q^0)_(s,t) U_s U_t*.

Choose s in the phi tail and t in the psi tail. The scalar is nonzero and
every selected row of U_s U_t* has exactly 8^T nonzeros. Those columns at
clock t alone give a row of F_q with at least 8^T entries. Other clock
blocks cannot cancel these entries.

This is specific to the transported path history and the stated
two-matching DL sweep. The proof uses strict positivity of its clock
transition matrix; it does not use polynomial approximation to VV* or
projection-row mass. It does not imply that every enforcer for every
redesigned image is dense.

Costs:
* There are K-1 edge projectors per sweep, two commuting operator layers,
  q=Theta(K^2) sweeps, and Theta(K^3) projector factors. Each factor has
  row support at most 9, exact coefficient 1/2, and acts on log_2 K
  binary-clock qubits plus at most two data qubits. Its qubit locality is
  log_2 K+2, not constant.
* The algebraic layer count is two. A circuit compilation with shared
  binary-clock controls can require polynomial depth within a layer; the
  guaranteed description uses O(K^3) edge-projector applications, each
  controlled on O(log K) bits and a constant-size algebraic gate. This is
  polynomial description cost, not a sparse matrix representation of F_q.
* For one ordered sweep R_j=P_j...P_1, the exact positive telescoping
  identity is I-D*D=sum_j R_(j-1)* Q_j R_(j-1). The q-sweep identity has
  prefix-conditioned positive terms. Such summands can involve the full
  history/data register; the combined F_q already has the exact 8^T row
  lower bound.
* Listing a selected nonzero row needs at least 8^T outputs. Entries in
  the chosen BJSW Clifford cross-tail block are exactly computable in
  polynomial time; that does not make the row polynomially enumerable.
  For general long relative circuits, no polynomial entry algorithm follows
  from the term generator alone.

N26 is not used: this is an all-output-state row-support result for a named
history enforcer, not a positive budget-one test of independent syndrome
bits.

3. RATIONAL FILTER AND A SPARSE WORKSPACE LIFT

Set tau=K^-2 and

    R_tau(C)=C(C+tau I)^(-1)=I-tau(C+tau I)^(-1).

This positive contraction has ker R_tau(C)=ker C. Since gamma_K>=K^-2,
its first positive eigenvalue is at least 1/2. If used in place of C with V
and B unchanged, logical compression remains exact.

The loss is exact row density. In the W gauge,

    (C+tau I)^(-1)
       = W [(L_path/4+tau I)^(-1) tensor I] W*.

The scalar matrix S=L_path/4+tau I is a positive-definite irreducible
tridiagonal M-matrix, so S^(-1) has strictly positive entries. Directly,
factor its positive diagonal: the remaining nonnegative nearest-neighbor
matrix has spectral radius below one, and for every s,t some power has a
positive (s,t) entry. Thus for every s!=t,

    (R_tau(C))_(s,t)=-tau (S^(-1))_(s,t) U_s U_t*

has nonzero scalar multiplier. Choosing phi and psi tails again gives at
least 8^T nonzeros in one row. The rational function avoids the
polynomial-degree cost by creating dense transported blocks.

This is an exact row-support statement, not merely an access-complexity
warning. The scalar inverse S^(-1) is a K-by-K rational matrix for
tau=K^-2; a fraction-free tridiagonal recurrence gives polynomial bit
length (O(K log K) bits suffice). A selected BJSW Clifford relative entry
is also computable exactly in polynomial time. Nevertheless the selected
row contains 8^T nonzeros, so enumerating it costs at least 8^T output
operations. Entry access and polynomial-row-sparse representation are
different interfaces.

For the Chebyshev polynomial, 10^d is only a worst-case row-support upper
bound from multiplying a 9-row-sparse matrix. In this history construction,
C^j is gauge-equivalent to a scalar path-walk matrix tensor I, so a
specified entry can factor into a path coefficient and an entry of
U_s U_t*. Such entries may be computable without enumerating the row. I
have not proved that every cross-tail scalar coefficient of the selected
Chebyshev polynomial is nonzero; I therefore make no exact row lower-bound
claim for that filter. For the DL sweep and rational resolvent, strict
positivity proves the needed coefficients are nonzero and yields 8^T
actual row support.

There is a sparse positive parent with Schur complement R_tau(C). For k>0
define

    A_(tau,k) =
      [[ I,             sqrt(k tau) I ],
       [ sqrt(k tau) I, k(C+tau I)    ]].

Schur complement over the lower block is
I-tau(C+tau I)^(-1)=R_tau(C); over the upper-left I it is kC. Equivalently,
the termwise-positive decomposition is

    A_(tau,k) =
      [[1,sqrt(k tau)],[sqrt(k tau),k tau]] tensor I
      + |1><1| tensor kC.

The 2-by-2 matrix in the first term is rank-one PSD, and each edge term k e_s
in the second is positive. The kernel is

    ker A_(tau,k)
      = { (-sqrt(k tau) V|psi>, V|psi>) : |psi> logical }.

An isometry onto it is

    V_(tau,k)|psi>
      = (-sqrt(k tau) V|psi> tensor |0>
          + V|psi> tensor |1>) / sqrt(1+k tau),

up to flag/system register ordering. With B'=B on both diagonal flag blocks,

    V_(tau,k)* B' V_(tau,k) = V*BV = (Q/K)H.

Thus the lift preserves common logical compression exactly. It adds one
flag and a second copy of the K-clock/data output. Its row support is at
most 10: a flag-0 row has two entries; a flag-1 row has the at-most-nine
entries of kC and one off-diagonal identity entry. The flag-only term has
locality one; each positive C edge has locality log_2 K+3. B' is
1-row-sparse but may act on all data, as before. For tau=K^-2 and k=1,
the amplitudes and coefficients have O(log K) bit descriptions; V_(tau,1)
is polynomial-size, built from V and a flag rotation. The row algorithm for
A is the Cycle-2 row algorithm for C plus O(log K)-bit arithmetic. All
construction and access costs are polynomial.

The Schur-complement gap is not the parent gap. In the history gauge,
A_(tau,k) acts on a path eigenvector of C with eigenvalue lambda as

    M_lambda =
      [[1,             sqrt(k tau)],
       [sqrt(k tau),   k(lambda+tau)]].

Its determinant is k lambda and trace is 1+k(lambda+tau). Since M_lambda
increases in PSD order with lambda, the first positive eigenvalue of A is

    mu_k = [1+k(gamma_K+tau)
             - sqrt((1+k(gamma_K+tau))^2-4k gamma_K)]/2.

For k=1 and tau=K^-2, gamma_K=Theta(K^-2), so mu_1=Theta(K^-2) while
||A_(tau,1)||=Theta(1). The normalized gap remains Theta(K^-2), despite
the Schur complement gap >=1/2. The full-space low mode includes the
workspace direction, so a Schur-energy bound after minimizing workspace
does not imply a full-space spectral gap.

For k=K^2=1/tau, sqrt(k tau)=1 and alpha=K^2 gamma_K lies in
[1,pi^2/4]. At the first positive mode,

    mu_(K^2)=(alpha+2-sqrt(alpha^2+4))/2
             >= (3-sqrt(5))/2 > 0.

This gives constant absolute gap and exact compression with maximum row
support 10. But ||A_(tau,K^2)||=Theta(K^2), since its lower-right block
contains K^2 C and ||C||>=1/2. Normalizing to a contraction reduces the
gap to Theta(K^-2). The edge coefficients are K^2/4 (O(log K) bits) and
have polynomial norm. This is an absolute-gap, norm-scaled lift, not
constant normalized-gap amplification.

This diagnoses the standard one-block Schur realization only. It does not
prove that every coherent workspace encoding has the same tradeoff.

Rational-filter barrier, scoped theorem. For the Cycle-2 full-list history
matrix C on the augmented BMVZ family, tau=K^-2, and the specified
factorwise BJSW endpoint decoders:

(i) R_tau(C) is a positive contraction, ker R_tau(C)=ran V, and
R_tau(C)>=(1/2)(I-VV*), but its maximum computational-basis row support is
at least 8^T.

(ii) A_(tau,1) is a sparse positive parent with maximum row support at most
10, exact kernel equal to the image of V_(tau,1), and
V_(tau,1)* (B tensor I_flag) V_(tau,1)=(Q/K)H. Its norm is Theta(1), and
its first positive eigenvalue is

    mu_1 = [1+gamma_K+tau
             - sqrt((1+gamma_K+tau)^2-4 gamma_K)]/2
          = Theta(K^-2).

(iii) Taking k=K^2 gives a constant absolute gap and norm Theta(K^2), so
the normalized gap remains Theta(K^-2).

For (i), strict positivity of every entry of S^(-1) fixes a nonzero clock
coefficient between the selected tails, so the 8^T relative-unitary row
survives in that block. For (ii), Schur complements prove positivity and
the exact kernel. The block on a C-eigenvector of eigenvalue lambda has
determinant lambda and trace 1+lambda+tau; the smaller eigenvalue at
lambda=gamma_K is

    mu_1 = 2 gamma_K /
           [1+gamma_K+tau
             + sqrt((1+gamma_K+tau)^2-4 gamma_K)].

For K>=2, gamma_K<=1/2 and tau<=1/4, so this denominator is bounded above
and below by positive constants. The full norm is bounded above by a
constant and below by 1, proving the claimed Theta(K^-2) normalized gap.
For k=K^2, alpha=K^2 gamma_K lies in [1,pi^2/4], and the smaller
eigenvalue is
(alpha+2-sqrt(alpha^2+4))/2 >= (3-sqrt(5))/2; the largest eigenvalue is
Theta(K^2).

This separates two effects: functional calculus amplifies the Schur
complement without amplifying the sparse parent's normalized gap, and the
resolvent's sparse scalar clock formula does not make its transported
matrix row-sparse. The theorem rules out this rational filter and its
displayed Schur lift as solutions to the normalized-gap gate. It is not a
universal lower bound on other workspace dilations or common isometries.

4. COMPOSITION AND DECISION

For this full BMVZ family, the Cycle-2 history construction already gives
positive row-sparse C and B and exact constant logical comparison. The
missing property is a size-independent normalized all-state gap of C.
On that same image:
* Chebyshev reaches a constant gap at degree Theta(K), with row-support
  upper bound 10^Theta(K); exact cancellations are not resolved.
* The two-layer DL filter reaches a constant gap with q=Theta(K^2) and has
  actual row support at least 8^T.
* The rational resolvent has actual row support at least 8^T; its sparse
  Schur parent retains only Theta(K^-2) normalized gap, or costs norm
  Theta(K^2) for constant absolute gap.

This is not an L02 composition. L02 controls a repeated-game threshold tail
for arbitrary commuting-operator strategies; it supplies no finite exact
frustration-free projectors, overlap bound, clock image, or common positive
logical test. N26 has a separate interface (positive budget-one effects
reading bounded subsets of independent syndrome bits) and is neither
invoked nor strengthened here.

Strongest equally-informed baseline: the full-list path-history isometry
with gamma_K=Theta(K^-2) and exact V*BV=(Q/K)H, compared against Chebyshev,
two-layer detectability, and a rational resolvent on the same C. The DL
argument is stronger than a generic row-growth bound because it proves
nonzero support across the actual phi/psi tails. The sparse Schur lift is
the strongest extra positive construction: it preserves positivity,
common compression, polynomial row access, and termwise positive
decomposition while quantifying why normalized gap does not improve.

FALSIFIERS / NEXT GATE

The exact DL row lower bound on this fixed V is falsified if either (i) some
entry of D_0^q between clock positions is zero for q>=K-1, or (ii) the
claimed 8^T entry support of the chosen relative Clifford row is wrong.
The first has a direct positive-path proof; the second is exactly checkable
with the Cycle-2 Q(sqrt(2)) test.

The scoped negative conclusion is falsified by an explicit replacement
(V',C',B') for the full augmented BMVZ family with

    V'*V'=I,
    C'=C'^* >= 0,       ker C'=ran V',
    C' >= c (I-V'V'^*) for c>0 independent of input size,
    0<=B'<=I,
    alpha H <= V'^* B' V' <= beta H for constants alpha,beta>0,

and polynomial output qubits, maximum row supports, exact row/entry
access, coefficient precision, construction time, plus a complete
locality/norm ledger. The present results do not rule out such an active
workspace construction.
