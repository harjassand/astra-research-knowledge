## W1. Same Hamiltonian, same coupling, explicit CP filter

On a logical and a gauge qubit put

    B=Z_L tensor X_G, H=delta B, A=I_L tensor Z_G,
    O=X_L tensor I_G, beta,delta,T,sigma,Gamma>0.

The unchanged A commutes with the entire original logical Pauli frame.
Let omega=2delta and A(t)=exp(itH)A exp(-itH). With the Fourier convention
used below positive omega is energy released by the corresponding jump.
Define the L2-normalized Gaussian and a bounded smooth KMS rate by

    f_T(t)=(pi T^2)^(-1/4) exp[-t^2/(2T^2)],
    gamma(nu)=Gamma exp[-(nu-beta sigma^2/2)^2/(2sigma^2)],
    L_nu=sqrt(gamma(nu)/(2pi)) int_R f_T(t) exp(i nu t)A(t)dt,
    D_T=sum_integral_nu D[L_nu].                       (W1)

Here gamma(-nu)=exp(-beta nu)gamma(nu). This is a completely positive
Markov dissipator by construction, but its stationarity at the intended
temperature remains an obligation. The Gaussian has infinite time support;
T is a width, not a literal compact cutoff or an acquired physical averaging
time. No replacement of the actual Davies bath is silently adopted.

## W2. Independent Gaussian convolution

The spectral jumps are J_s=(A+sBA)/2, s=+,-, with J_s*=J_-s and J_s^2=0.
They have opposite energies, and J_s*J_s=P_-s where P_s=(I+sB)/2.
Plancherel gives |Fourier(f_T)(x)|^2/(2pi)=(T/sqrt(pi))exp(-T^2x^2).
Writing c=beta sigma^2/2, completing the square gives

    r_T(x)=int gamma(nu)(T/sqrt(pi))exp[-T^2(nu-x)^2]dnu
      =Gamma sqrt[2sigma^2 T^2/(1+2sigma^2 T^2)]
          exp[-T^2(x-c)^2/(1+2sigma^2 T^2)].           (W2)

The two-frequency cross coefficient is real and nonnegative:

    r_cross=exp(-T^2omega^2)r_T(0).                    (W3)

Indeed the product of the two real Gaussian Fourier amplitudes has
exponent -T^2(nu^2+omega^2). The rate ratio is EXACTLY

    r_T(-x)/r_T(x)=exp(-beta_eff x),
    beta_eff=beta·2sigma^2 T^2/(1+2sigma^2 T^2)<beta.    (W4)

The unfiltered KMS property of gamma does not survive this convolution at
the same beta. Finite bandwidth and finite temporal width therefore have
an actual equilibrium cost.

## W3. Nonzero actual Gibbs stationarity defect

The actual Gibbs state is rho_beta=(I-mB)/4, m=tanh(beta delta). Cross
terms J_+rho J_-* and their cross anticommutators vanish exactly because
J_+^2=J_-^2=0 and rho is B-diagonal. Thus

    D_T(rho_beta)=[r_T(omega)+r_T(-omega)]
       [tanh(beta delta)-tanh(beta_eff delta)]B/4,
    ||D_T(rho_beta)||_1=[r_T(omega)+r_T(-omega)]
       |tanh(beta delta)-tanh(beta_eff delta)|>0.       (W5)

The norm follows from B*B=I in dimension4. The sign is positive for the
specified parameters. Every commutator [K,rho_beta], even for unrestricted
Hermitian K, has zero H-diagonal blocks because rho_beta is scalar on each
H eigenspace. The nonzero diagonal defect W5 cannot be canceled by adding
a coherent Hamiltonian correction while retaining this dissipator.

## W4. Nevertheless original-frame weighted leakage is small

O commutes with A and anticommutes with B. Its commutators with J_+ and
J_- are opposite unitary matrices (the overall sign is immaterial).
The frequency-filtered thermal leakage is consequently

    k_T=int Tr rho_beta[O,L_nu]*[O,L_nu]dnu
       =r_T(omega)+r_T(-omega)-2exp(-T^2omega^2)r_T(0). (W6)

Independently in the time representation,

    [O,A(t)]* [O,A(t)]=4sin^2(omega t) I.

Using gamma<=Gamma and Plancherel, the Gaussian f_T^2 integral yields

    k_T<=2Gamma[1-exp(-omega^2T^2)]
        <=2Gamma omega^2T^2=8Gamma delta^2T^2.          (W7)

Thus the CP filter indeed removes the seed's discontinuous order-one
original-logical leakage when delta T is small. This gain coexists with
W5. The general stationary weighted theorem cannot be applied by simply
relabeling this generator as an actual-Gibbs stationary bath. Any proposed
nonstationary criterion must pay the defect, or independently acquire an
invariant reference/decoded-memory mechanism. Harmless auxiliary drift
can occur in other systems, so W5 alone does not exclude every possible
decoded-memory proof for a finite-resolution model.

## W6. Earlier bounds in the complete candidate also reconstruct

For any real L2-normalized f, apply scalar Plancherel to the vector
f(t)A(t)u for arbitrary u, and drop gamma<=Gamma. This proves
int L_nu*L_nu dnu<=Gamma||A||^2 I. The CP gain map's diamond norm is
the norm of its adjoint at I; the anticommutator has at most the same
norm. Hence ||D_f||_diamond<=2Gamma||A||^2, with no frequency count.
Apply Hilbert-space Plancherel to [O,A(t)]sqrt(rho) to obtain F2c.
For the other orientation apply it to sqrt(rho)[O,A(t)]; the two
time-domain integrands agree because this commutator is anti-Hermitian.
Their weighted frequency integrands need not agree; only their common
upper bound was asserted. This distinction is correct.

For the conditional repair define m_A=||rho^(-1/2)A rho^(1/2)||. Directly
A rho A*<=m_A^2rho, so ||X A||_(rho,2)<=m_A||X||_(rho,2). Left
multiplication by A costs ||A||. Hamiltonian conjugation is an isometry
in the rho norm. Integrating O(-t) and using these two different costs
gives F3a; Gaussian L2 Minkowski and int t^2|f_T|^2=T^2/2 give F3b.
This verifies a sufficient CONDITIONAL premise, not its uniform acquisition.
