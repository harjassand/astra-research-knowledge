# Cycle 3: a two-source catalytic CTMC separates rates hidden by the full CLE

**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**Status:** internally reconstructed finite-state witness; exact arithmetic checked; chemical plausibility, noisy assay performance, and novelty remain unverified.

Two positive rate vectors on the same state-dependent mass-action graph give exactly the same full Langevin drift and covariance at every state, but different finite-count product-path laws. Competing first- and second-order source complexes make the CTMC output neither a one-source compound-Poisson process nor an iid mark sequence. With exact product-yield jump measurements, the optimal test separates the two fixed rate hypotheses in five independent runs at total type-I-plus-type-II error below 5%; endpoint product counts alone need sixteen runs for the same criterion. This is a model-level assay design, not evidence about an observed enzyme.

## Source theorem and comparison

For reactions with displacement \(\nu_r\), source \(y_r\), and mass-action rate \(\kappa_r x^{y_r}\), the Langevin drift and covariance are
\[
A(x)=\sum_y x^y\sum_{r:y_r=y}\kappa_r\nu_r,\qquad
B(x)=\sum_y x^y\sum_{r:y_r=y}\kappa_r\nu_r\nu_r^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Faul, Hoessly, and Xia, Theorem 3.3(1), state that rate identifiability from the full SDE law is equivalent to linear independence, separately at each source \(y\), of the lifted vectors \(s(\nu)=(\nu,\nu\nu^{\mathsf T})\). Assumptions are a fixed known reaction graph and species set, positive rate constants, complete state observation, positive initial states, and law comparison stopped at the boundary of bounded open subsets of the positive orthant. The proof equates generator coefficients on an open set and uses linear independence of distinct monomials \(x^y\). A nonzero source-wise dependence can be scaled around a positive rate vector to give a distinct positive vector with the same generator. This is structural identifiability; it gives no noisy-data or finite-sample bound. The authors name partial observation and discrete-time inference as open practical directions.

The published Theorem 3.3(2) has a cone-quantifier problem: it prints sourcewise disjointness for unconfoundability, while Theorem 5.15 and §7.3 require sourcewise nonempty intersection for confoundability. Negation yields an existential disjoint source. The separate correction is in [the parent audit](../chemistry_catalysis/observability_correction.md); I rely only on Theorem 3.3(1).

Duval, *When is it no longer possible to estimate a compound Poisson process?*, *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 8(1), 274–301 (2014), [DOI 10.1214/14-EJS885](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-EJS885), studies a different interface. Theorem 2 concerns a centered compound-Poisson process with unknown intensity \(\lambda\) and jump density \(f\), observed through \(n_T=\lfloor T/\Delta_T\rfloor\) independent increments. With loss
\[
\|r\|_{2,P}=\|(\lambda,f)\|_{2,P}:=\|\lambda f\|_{L^2},
\]
if \(\Delta_T\to\infty\), \(T/\Delta_T\to\infty\), and
\[
T/\Delta_T^2=o((\log(T/\Delta_T))^{-1/4}),
\]
then for every \(r_0\in P\) of finite loss norm and every \(\delta>0\), a neighborhood \(V_\delta(r_0)\) of diameter at most \(\delta\) exists with
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\inf_{\widehat r}\sup_{r\in V_\delta(r_0)}
\mathbb E_r^{\lfloor T/\Delta_T\rfloor}\|\widehat r-r\|_{2,P}>0.
\]
The infimum is over all estimators. Thus for \(\Delta_T\asymp T^\alpha\), \(1/2<\alpha<1\), consistent estimation of intensity and unknown jump density is impossible under that experiment. Theorem 3 also gives coarse-mesh asymptotic equivalence to Gaussian experiments under its smoothness and moment assumptions. These are stationary independent increments and asymptotic coarsening results, not a finite-volume state-dependent CRN or our finite assay.

## Fixed-graph catalytic pair

Use species order \((C,E,S,P)\), with \(C=E:S_4\), free enzyme \(E\), residual substrate \(S\), product \(P\). For \(k=1,2,3,4\), include all reactions
\[
C\to E+(4-k)S+kP,\qquad 2C\to2E+(8-k)S+kP.
\]
The displacements are \(b_1+ku\), \(b_2+ku\), where \(b_1=(-1,1,4,0)\), \(b_2=(-2,2,8,0)\), and \(u=(0,0,-1,1)\).

| Source | A rates for yields \(1,2,3,4\) | B rates for yields \(1,2,3,4\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(C\) | \((2,1,4,1)\) | \((1,4,1,2)\) |
| \(2C\) | \((4,1,10,1)\) | \((1,10,1,4)\) |

All rates are strictly positive; graph, species, and source complexes match. Conservation laws are \(C+E=2\) and \(4C+S+P=8\). Starting at \((C,E,S,P)=(2,0,0,0)\), each finite CTMC has 13 reachable states: one initial, four with \(C=1\), and eight absorbing \(C=0\) states indexed by terminal \(P=1,\ldots,8\).

At each source, \(\nu_k=b+ku\), so first and second lifted signatures depend only on \(M_j=\sum_k k^j\kappa_k\), \(j=0,1,2\):

| Source | Model | \(M_0\) | \(M_1\) | \(M_2\) | \(M_3\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(C\) | A | 8 | 20 | 58 | 182 |
| \(C\) | B | 8 | 20 | 58 | 188 |
| \(2C\) | A | 16 | 40 | 114 | 346 |
| \(2C\) | B | 16 | 40 | 114 | 364 |

The rate differences are \(\alpha=(1,-3,3,-1)\) and \(3\alpha\). Here \(\sum_k k^j\alpha_k=0\) for \(j=0,1,2\), and \(\sum_k k^3\alpha_k=-6\). Four yields are minimal within this one-dimensional affine-yield construction because the Vandermonde matrix of \(1,k,k^2\) at three distinct yields is nonsingular.

With factorial-normalized source monomials \(x_C\) and \(x_C^2/2\) (finite-count propensities \(C\) and \(\binom C2\)), both CLEs have
\[
A(x)=x_C(-8,8,12,20)+\frac{x_C^2}{2}(-32,32,88,40),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(x)={}&x_C
\begin{pmatrix}
8&-8&-12&-20\\-8&8&12&20\\-12&12&26&22\\-20&20&22&58
\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\frac{x_C^2}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
64&-64&-176&-80\\-64&64&176&80\\-176&176&498&206\\-80&80&206&114
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exact polynomial identities for every \(x\). \(B\) denotes covariance; where the local martingale problems are well posed, equal coefficients give equal stopped CLE laws. No global boundary solution claim is needed for the finite CTMC assay.

## Hidden-source law, third moments, and rate recovery

At \(C=2\), first-order total hazard is \(2\cdot8=16\), second-order total hazard is \(\binom22\cdot16=16\). The first wait is Exp(32), and each source is selected with probability \(1/2\). A first-order event leaves \(C=1\), where the next event has total rate 8; a \(2C\) event terminates immediately. Thus one- and two-event paths each occur with probability \(1/2\). Holding times are Exp(32), then Exp(8) if a second event occurs; timing alone carries no A/B information.

Measure only ordered product-count jump sizes, not source label or internal state. Let \(r\) be the \(C\)-source vector and \(q\) the \(2C\)-source vector. A complete assay produces either \(H_k=(k)\) or \(H_{ij}=(i,j)\), with
\[
\Pr(H_k)=q_k/32,\qquad \Pr(H_{ij})=r_i r_j/128.
\]
Their integer weights over denominator 128 are in <code>multisource_audit.py</code>. The exact likelihood ratio over \(N\) independent runs is
\[
\Lambda=\prod_{\ell:H_{k_\ell}}\frac{q^A_{k_\ell}}{q^B_{k_\ell}}
\prod_{\ell:H_{i_\ell j_\ell}}\frac{r^A_{i_\ell}r^A_{j_\ell}}{r^B_{i_\ell}r^B_{j_\ell}}.
\]
This is optimal for the two supplied simple hypotheses and does not observe the hidden source.

The ideal marked-time law also identifies the eight rates when unknown. For general positive \(r,q\), set \(R=\sum r_k\), \(Q=\sum q_k\), \(H=2R+Q\). One-event density for mark \(k\) at \(t\) is \(q_k e^{-Ht}\); two-event density for ordered marks \(i,j\), \(0<t_1<t_2\), is
\[
2r_i r_j e^{-Ht_1}e^{-R(t_2-t_1)}.
\]
The first wait identifies \(H\), second gaps identify \(R\), then \(Q=H-2R\). Conditional marks identify \(r_k/R,q_k/Q\), hence all rates. Without event times, common scaling of all rates preserves the mark-path law, leaving absolute scale confounded. This is structural, not a finite-sample nuisance-rate result.

Terminal \(P\)-only probabilities for \(z=1,\ldots,8\) are
\[
P_A(z)=(16,8,44,21,12,18,8,1)_z/128,\quad
P_B(z)=(4,41,12,34,12,17,4,4)_z/128.
\]
Both have \(\mathbb E[P]=15/4\), \(\mathbb E[P^2]=273/16\), and variance 3; third cumulants are \(93/64\) and \(177/64\), respectively. The third raw-moment gap is \(21/16\). At the initial state,
\[
(\mathcal Q_A-\mathcal Q_B)P^3=2(182-188)+(346-364)=-30.
\]
The CTMC distinction is above the first two jump moments and remains visible after hiding the chemical state and source label.

## Exact finite-count cost and baseline

Assume supplied simple hypotheses, independent exact initializations at \(C=2\), the stated finite-count propensities, complete paths, exact product-yield resolution, and no background reactions. No simulation was used; all risks are rationally enumerated.

| Full marked paths | Sum of type-I and type-II errors | Error under either model |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 runs | \(8233/65536\approx0.125626\) | \(\approx0.062813\) |
| 4 runs | \(663613/8388608\approx0.079109\) | \(\approx0.039554\) |
| 5 runs | \(13413363/268435456\approx0.049969\) | \(\approx0.024984\) |

Thus five runs are minimal for total error sum at most 5%; four are minimal for equal-prior Bayes error at most 5% (and give each symmetric conditional error below 5%). Endpoint-only optimal CTMC testing needs 16 runs for total error sum at most 5% (15: 0.052103; 16: 0.044913), or 11 runs for equal-prior Bayes error at most 5% (10: 0.112259; 11: 0.095981). At five runs, endpoint-only total error is 0.259162. Each likelihood-ratio test is optimal for its supplied observation.

Five full-path runs require ten preformed \(E:S_4\) complexes, at most ten product-jump records (expected 7.5), and one completion/residual-\(C\) check per run if completion is not otherwise observed. Each run's expected completion time is \(3/32\) time units. At a fixed horizon \(T\), the exact per-run censoring probability is
\[
\frac23e^{-8T}+\frac13e^{-32T}\le e^{-8T}.
\]
Taking \(T\ge\log(500)/8\approx0.777\) makes the union-bound censoring probability at most 1% for five runs. Endpoint-only assays need one final \(P\in\{1,\ldots,8\}\) measurement but 16 preparations for the same sum-error target: event-resolved product measurements trade for 3.2 times fewer preparations.

The strongest equally informed baseline is the exact finite-state CTMC likelihood-ratio test on the same observations; it is the reported optimum. ODE and full-CLE fitting have zero power between the rate vectors because their laws coincide. The endpoint-only CTMC likelihood is the lower-resolution comparator. The ideal readout must report each ΔP exactly as an integer in {1,2,3,4}; for a calibrated analog readout followed by nearest-integer rounding, absolute error below 0.5 molecule-equivalent is sufficient. This is a mathematical precision requirement, not a claim that an available instrument meets it. Detector error beyond that idealization, rate calibration, preparation yield, nuisance-rate fitting, and instrument runtime are unpriced; no noisy-readout guarantee is asserted.

## Assay, falsifiers, and novelty boundary

Prepare independent wells with exactly two \(E:S_4\) intermediates, record ordered product-count jumps until \(C=0\) (or verify terminal depletion), then apply the preregistered CTMC likelihood ratio. The readout needs product-yield increments, not reaction identity or intermediate trajectories. A controlled \(C=1\) arm could check the first-order branch but is unnecessary for the fixed-rate test.

A yield outside 1–4, more than two events from initial \(C=2\), persistent nonabsorbing \(C\), or violation of \(C+E=2\) / \(4C+S+P=8\) falsifies the closed pair. Poor likelihood under both models in a larger preregistered batch rejects the pair rather than selecting the less-bad model. Timing and source-choice probabilities alone cannot discriminate.

This strengthens the earlier one-source branch construction retained in <code>FINAL_REPORT.md</code>: there are two source monomials, competing state-dependent hazards, hidden source identity, and an endpoint-versus-event-resolved acquisition gap. The algebra still uses a known degree-3 finite-difference null vector; it is not a new general theorem. No literature-priority claim is made. The \(2C\) concerted-release chemistry is engineered, and no biochemical precedent or actual dataset was validated. The empirical gate is chemistry review, rate calibration, completion readout, and a product-jump confusion matrix.
