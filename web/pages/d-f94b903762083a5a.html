# Genetics and programmable biology: candidate audit

## Verdict

No line in this branch currently supports a defensible novelty or priority claim. Four concrete model results survived mathematical attack, but each is either a standard probability argument or overlaps established primary work. I am handing them off as bounded design gates and principled rejections of overstrong inferences. The most useful surviving lesson is to state the resource or observation interface exactly: peak translation capacity differs from average expression cost; guide-level escape marginals do not determine joint resistance; a nonlinear assay link can erase latent-scale epistasis; and a multicellular relay cannot create information absent from its receptor-count input.

## 1. Circuit reliability under finite translation capacity

Let a circuit’s measured protein count be (N_t\in\{0,\ldots,M\}). Births add one molecule with causal predictable intensity (\lambda_t\le B), regardless of hidden promoter, mRNA, controller, or resource state. Each extant molecule is independently removed at rate (\gamma>0). There are no burst jumps or alternate upward routes, and feedback does not reduce the loss rate. Couple this process to the capped immigration–death chain (X_t) that accepts every rate-(B) candidate birth below (M) and loses each molecule at rate \(\gamma\). Shared births and moleculewise deaths preserve (N_t\le X_t) pathwise. The comparator has stationary truncated-Poisson law

\[
\pi_B(n)=\frac{q^n/n!}{Z_M(q)},\qquad q=B/\gamma,\qquad Z_M(q)=\sum_{j=0}^M q^j/j!.
\]

Therefore, from any common initial count,

\[
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\Pr[N_t<h]\ge
\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{h-1}q^n/n!}{Z_M(q)}.
\]

This is a **peak-throughput** ceiling. It does not say feedback cannot help at matched average synthesis cost. An exact finite counterexample in [the circuit report](circuit_reliability/FINAL_REPORT.md) compares (M=10,\gamma=1): feedback birth rate \(10-n\) has stationary \(\mathrm{Binomial}(10,1/2)\), mean 5, dropout (1/1024\); a constant-rate truncated-Poisson circuit tuned to the same mean has attempted rate \(\approx5.10412\), dropout \(\approx0.006168\). The feedback policy has the larger peak but same average accepted production. This resolves an easy but consequential comparator error: peak-matched and average-cost-matched claims are different.

The coupling is not a new theorem. Hemberg and Barahona’s 2007 perfect-sampling method already bounds Hill and Monod gene-expression birth propensities by a first-order immigration–death process and uses it for the toggle switch and repressilator (\S2–3, \S7; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.106.099390)). Extending their Markov coupling to predictable history-dependent rates by Poisson thinning is elementary. The model result remains a useful design screen if (B,\gamma,M) are measured, but peak translation, controller cost, transcript dynamics, growth-mediated dilution, and cell-to-cell variability must be charged separately. In particular, a proteome-level implementation fails the theorem’s interface if multi-protein bursts or state-dependent loss are omitted.

Current primary work confirms why this boundary matters. Chakravarty et al. compare antithetic controllers in resource-coupled multi-module circuits and explicitly rescale transcription/translation rates to match mean mRNA/protein counts; they report noise reduction by stochastic simulation and fluctuation-dissipation analysis ([2024 CDC paper, PMC manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11987709/)). Sechkar et al. give a resource-aware bacterial model with growth-mediated feedback and show that resource competition can produce qualitative circuit changes ([Nature Communications 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46410-9)). Neither is contradicted by the peak-cap coupling, and neither validates a universal noise or reliability guarantee.

## 2. CRISPR multiplex escape and gene-drive resistance

For guide-site escape events \(X_i\) with only marginal probabilities \(p_i=\Pr(X_i)\), the sharp Fréchet bounds are

\[
\max\!\left(0,\sum_i p_i-(m-1)\right)\le\Pr\!\left(\bigcap_i X_i\right)\le\min_i p_i.
\]

The upper endpoint is attained when one shared repair/deletion event drives all sites; the lower endpoint follows by arranging the complements to cover the sample space. The product \(\prod_i p_i\) requires independence or another measured joint-repair model. Thus marginal guide rates alone cannot certify exponential suppression as guide count grows. Molecular escape also differs from *functional* resistance: function-preserving alternative starts and large deletions require direct target-function assays.

This is a standard probability inequality, and the biological mechanism is established. Hou et al. report functional resistance from a large deletion in a multiplexed *Drosophila hairy* rescue drive ([2024 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jgg.2024.04.001)). A July 2026 *Anopheles stephensi* study reports same-read loss of all four target sites in somatic sequencing, with many such reads carrying a single deletion; the authors explicitly do not call these germline transmission rates ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13388953/)). Recent *doublesex* and *cardinal* studies report workable multiplex designs, so the result is an inference limit, not a claim that multiplexing fails ([Morianou et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3003879)).

The decisive measurement is phased, same-haplotype joint escape plus functional screening on independent germline-origin transmissions. If zero events are observed, certifying risk below \(10^{-5}\) at one-sided 95% confidence needs 299,572 independent transmissions; clustering and assay dropout increase that cost. The full guide-by-guide audit and its structural-variant caveats are in [the CRISPR report](crispr_drive/FINAL_REPORT.md).

## 3. Epistasis under an unknown assay link

On genotypes \(x\in\{0,1\}^2\), an unknown strictly increasing assay link can make an additive latent phenotype and an epistatic latent phenotype observationally identical for every adaptive genotype-query strategy. One explicit pair is \(f_0=x_1+2x_2\), \(g_0(u)=u+1\), versus \(f_1=x_1+2x_2+7x_1x_2\) and a strictly increasing piecewise-linear \(g_1\) through \((0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(10,4)\). Both observed mean tables are \((1,2,3,4)\); with identical Gaussian noise, every transcript law agrees, giving total variation zero and minimax error at least (1/2), regardless of query count or adaptivity.

This formalizes a known scale problem, not a new biological effect: Sailer and Harms show that nonlinear phenotype scales induce apparent higher-order epistasis and study power-transform corrections ([Genetics 2017](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/205/3/1079/6066378)). The repair is independent calibration over the assay’s relevant range. On that declared calibrated scale, the four-cell factorial contrast has variance \(4\sigma^2/r\) with (r) independent replicates per genotype; its Gaussian power condition and excluded calibration costs are recorded in [the epistasis report](epistasis_identifiability/FINAL_REPORT.md). A finite set of standards cannot identify an unrestricted monotone link between standards, so a parametric/smoothness assumption or full-range calibration is a charged premise.

## 4. Spatial pattern programming as a Poisson test

Under the idealized model of independent receptor event counts \(Y_i\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\mu_i)\) versus \(Y_i\sim\mathrm{Pois}(q\mu_i)\), where \(q=e^{\delta/\lambda}\), let \(M=\sum_i\mu_i\) be the total expected events across the full cell array and measurement window. If every downstream gene circuit and cell-to-cell relay is only stochastic postprocessing of those counts, any binary decoder with worst-case error at most \(\varepsilon<1/2\) requires

\[
M\ge\frac{-\log(4\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon))}{(e^{\delta/(2\lambda)}-1)^2}.
\]

For small \(\delta/\lambda\), this scales as (4\log(1/[4\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)])(\lambda/\delta)^2). This is a valid finite-error model theorem, but the physical question is already close to established positional-information limits (Tostevin et al. 2007; Gregor et al. 2007), multicellular communication bounds (Mugler et al. 2016; Ellison et al. 2016), and robust boundary work (Bojer et al. 2022; Vetter and Iber 2022). The theorem should remain a total-event-count baseline until it is matched quantitatively to those field-specific models. Its assumptions—known gradient, no saturation/background/heterogeneity, independent Poisson counts—are not validated for any organism. See [the spatial report](spatial_computation/REPORT.md) and its full source comparison.
