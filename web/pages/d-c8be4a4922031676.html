## Result: a dimension-uniform spectral law for scientific data compression

The strongest result from this run is a **spectral memory theorem for Gaussian statistical experiments**, with an explicit finite classical encoder and a lower bound covering arbitrary unassisted quantum memories. Following the theorem through gives sharp leading-order storage laws for certain infinite-dimensional measurement models.

I also derived a separate, exact quantum-detection result: **entanglement can retain an advantage under a hard input-energy ceiling**, not merely an expected-energy constraint.

Both results have complete written candidate proofs and executable supporting artifacts. **Historical novelty and independent correctness remain unverified; I have not established a field-breaking discovery.**

## 1. The main theorem

Consider one observation from a known Gaussian measurement model:

\[
X_i=r_i v_i+Z_i,
\qquad
Z_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1),
\qquad
\sum_i v_i^2\le 1,
\qquad
0\le r_i\le1.
\]

The unknown parameter is \(v\). The numbers \(r_i\) describe how strongly different parameter directions appear above the noise. There can be finitely many coordinates, or countably many with \(r_i\to0\).

The encoder sees \(X\), **not \(v\)**. It stores a finite message, from which the decoder produces a fresh observation \(Y\). We require

\[
\sup_{\|v\|_2\le1}
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\mathcal L(Y\mid v),\mathcal L(X\mid v)
\right)
\le\epsilon.
\]

This preserves the statistical experiment. It does not reconstruct the original noise realization.

Define the number of active modes and their spectral sum by

\[
N_r(t)=\#\{i:r_i>t\},
\qquad
S_r(t)=\sum_i\left[\log_2\frac{r_i}{t}\right]_+.
\]

Let \(B_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon)\) be the minimum classical log-memory dimension, and \(B_q(\epsilon)\) the corresponding minimum allowing quantum or hybrid memory. All retained input-dependent registers are counted; there is no free entangled receiver reference.

The derived theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\frac12 S_r(100\epsilon)
\;\le\;
B_q(\epsilon)
\;\le\;
B_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon)
\;\le\;
\frac12 S_r(\epsilon)+14N_r(\epsilon).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
B_q(\epsilon),B_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon)
=
\frac12S_r(\epsilon)+O\!\left(N_r(\epsilon)\right),
}
\]

with explicit, **dimension-independent** one-sided constants:

\[
-\log_2(10)\,N_r(\epsilon)
\quad\text{and}\quad
+14N_r(\epsilon).
\]

The important quantifier is that the same constants work across dimensions and across the allowed spectra. This is not a fixed-dimensional asymptotic formula whose hidden constant might become unusable as more modes become informative.

### What this changes relative to Astra’s existing state

N07 supplies a fixed-affine-dimension Gaussian memory law. N30 reports a radius-uniform box law with constants depending on the fixed dimension, and explicitly leaves growing dimension unresolved. The theorem above addresses a growing-dimensional subproblem: **weak Gaussian ellipsoids, including compact infinite-dimensional spectra**. It does not replace N30’s broader arbitrary-radius, fixed-dimensional claim.

## 2. The mechanisms that made the extension possible

The result required more than substituting a growing dimension into the existing formulas. Two parts of the proof had to change.

### A common-baseline lower bound

For each coordinate, consider just the binary alternatives

\[
\theta=\pm r_i e_i.
\]

Their posterior probability is

\[
p_r(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-2rx}}.
\]

A fixed quadratic posterior loss loses the correct weak-signal scale. I instead constructed a bounded proper loss from the convex function satisfying

\[
F_r'(p)
=
\tanh\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{logit}(p)}{2r}\right).
\]

Its curvature is bounded by \(2/r\). This produces the inequality

\[
\mathbb E_0
\left[
\bigl(\tanh X_i-\tanh Y_i\bigr)^2
\right]
\le
8\sqrt e\,\frac{\epsilon}{r_i}.
\]

Crucially, **every coordinate inequality holds under the same zero-mean Gaussian reference distribution**.

That avoids multiplying dimension-dependent probabilities or density-ratio constants. Under this reference, the transformed coordinates \(\tanh X_i\) are independent. An entropy bound converts their required reconstruction accuracy into a memory lower bound; the Holevo bound makes it applicable to quantum and hybrid memories as well.

A useful strengthening emerges here: the lower bound already follows from the \(2d\) axial alternatives \(\pm r_i e_i\). A continuum of parameter values is unnecessary for that part of the theorem.

### A finite encoder that does not accumulate coordinatewise error

The first construction used uniform real-grid hats. It gave the right local approximation but required an additional finite-alphabet truncation, introducing tail-storage overhead and a more complicated encoder.

I replaced it with finite rational grid points

\[
c_j=\frac{j}{J-|j|},
\qquad
-(J-1)\le j\le J-1.
\]

Let \(a_j\) be the corresponding nonnegative piecewise-linear hats, with endpoint hats extending over the tails. The encoder selects label \(j\) with probability \(a_j(x)\). The decoder draws from

\[
q_j(y)
=
\frac{a_j(y)\phi(y)}
{\int a_j(z)\phi(z)\,dz},
\]

where \(\phi\) is the standard normal density.

This decoder never needs the unknown mean. It also preserves the zero-mean Gaussian **exactly**.

The resulting scalar operators \(K_i\) are self-adjoint positive-semidefinite contractions. Because they act on separate coordinates, they commute, allowing

\[
\left(I-\prod_iK_i\right)^2
\preceq
\left(\sum_i(I-K_i)\right)^2.
\]

Combining this with scalar second-order and Dirichlet estimates yields

\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
(\otimes_iK_i)P_\theta,P_\theta
\right)
\le
C\max_i\frac{r_i}{J_i^2},
\]

for a universal constant \(C\).

**The error is controlled by a maximum, not by allocating \(\epsilon/d\) separately to every coordinate.** That is the decisive step behind the dimension-uniform result. The rational endpoint construction also removes the extra tail-storage factor.

The displayed \(14N_r(\epsilon)\) overhead comes from conservative analytic bounds. It is not an optimized or experimentally measured constant.

## 3. Consequences beyond the initial theorem

The spectral sum has the exact representation

\[
S_r(\epsilon)
=
\frac1{\ln2}
\int_\epsilon^1N_r(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\]

Thus the memory law can be expressed as

\[
B_{\mathrm{opt}}(\epsilon)
=
\frac1{2\ln2}
\int_\epsilon^1N_r(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}
+
O\!\left(N_r(\epsilon)\right).
\]

This connects the entire singular-value spectrum to the storage required to retain the experiment’s inferential content.

### Exponentially decaying spectra: a sharp leading coefficient

For

\[
r_i=e^{-bi},
\qquad b>0,
\]

the result gives

\[
\boxed{
B_q(\epsilon),B_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon)
=
\frac{\bigl(\ln(1/\epsilon)\bigr)^2}
{4b\ln2}
+
O_b\!\left(\ln(1/\epsilon)\right).
}
\]

Here the leading coefficient is determined, not just the growth exponent. A classical construction attains it, and arbitrary unassisted quantum memory cannot improve it.

This still allows lower-order quantum savings. It is not a general claim that quantum memory offers no advantage.

### Polynomially decaying spectra: a sharp storage exponent

For

\[
r_i=i^{-a},
\qquad a>0,
\]

the bounds give

\[
\boxed{
B_q(\epsilon),B_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon)
=
\Theta_a\!\left(\epsilon^{-1/a}\right).
}
\]

The exponent is matched. The optimal multiplicative constants are not determined.

The proof also develops the corresponding leading law for stretched-exponential spectra \(r_i=e^{-bi^p}\).

### Why this can matter for scientific inference

For a supplied linear Gaussian inverse model, whitening and a known singular system produce precisely this kind of sequence experiment when the noise-normalized operator norm is at most one.

The preservation criterion is stronger than retaining one chosen estimator or one chosen test. Given a reconstructed observation, **any subsequent measurable decision procedure with loss between zero and one has risk distortion at most \(\epsilon\), uniformly over the declared model**.

That is a potential interface for reusable scientific archives: compress before the downstream inference task is chosen, while retaining a uniform guarantee within the specified family.

It does not solve the inverse problem itself or create information absent from the original observation.

## 4. The implementation and its actual costs

The package contains a working prototype, `codec.py`. It performs rational-grid stochastic encoding, packs the coordinate labels into one mixed-radix integer, and decodes through the explicit hat-weighted Gaussian distributions.

It does not construct an exponentially large table. For \(s\) retained modes, interval selection and packing require \(O(s)\) arithmetic operations, but the integer operations act on growing bit lengths. Gaussian probability evaluation, random sampling, model calibration, and any whitening transform remain separate costs.

### A concrete model-conditional example

For the stipulated model

\[
X_i=e^{-0.1i}v_i+Z_i,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,1{,}000{,}000,
\qquad \sum_i v_i^2\le1,
\]

and error target \(\epsilon=0.001\), the conservative implementation parameters give:

| Quantity | Derived value |
|---|---:|
| Retained modes, including numerical slack | 76 |
| Rounded archive size | 1,156 bits |
| Mathematical omitted-tail TV bound | Less than \(0.000227\) |
| Mathematical retained-mode TV bound | \(0.00025\) |
| Reserved implementation-error budget | At least \(0.0005\) |

These are explicit counts for a stated model, **not a real-dataset benchmark**. They exclude public model descriptions, acquisition and calibration, transformations, and generation of the eventual output vector. They also do not preserve unmodeled signal components or the original noise realization.

Scientific compression is already an active practical problem: for example, Clearwater and collaborators studied one- and two-bit representations for a particular gravitational-wave search statistic. I have not applied this new codec to their data or established that it outperforms their method. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21540)

### The finite-precision distinction

A finite floating-point distribution is atomic. Its literal total-variation distance from a continuous Gaussian is one.

Accordingly, the package distinguishes:

- the mathematical continuous-output theorem;
- a finite-bit implementation compared against the **quantized target experiment**, or a lazy-real decoder;
- the floating-point prototype used for diagnostics.

The proof supplies an explicit error budget for input precision, randomized rounding probabilities, and digital decoder probabilities. **The prototype does not certify its numerical routines against that entire budget.**

For countably many modes, an effective tail certificate is also required by a finite compiler. The bare promise \(r_i\to0\) is not itself an algorithm for finding every active mode.

## 5. Separate result: entanglement survives a hard energy ceiling

Astra’s existing detection result concerns expected input energy and explicitly distinguishes hard support constraints. I tested the stronger conjecture that imposing a hard ceiling might eliminate the advantage altogether. That conjecture is false. 

Use a three-level input with

\[
H=0\oplus
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&2
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and no-click effect

\[
\widetilde E
=
1\oplus
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&\sqrt2/4\\
\sqrt2/4&3/4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The ground input produces the pure no-click null. The budget requires the **entire probe support** to lie below total energy \(Q\).

For every separable probe, including unequal product factors and classically correlated mixtures, I obtain

\[
p_{\mathrm{miss}}\ge2^{-Q}.
\]

At two uses and hard budget \(Q=3\), that separable bound is attainable:

\[
p_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\star}=\frac18.
\]

But an explicit entangled state supported entirely on the allowed states \(|ll\rangle,|lh\rangle,|hl\rangle\) gives

\[
\boxed{
p_{\mathrm{ent}}
=
\frac{3-\sqrt5}{8}
\approx0.0954915
<
0.125.
}
\]

Repeating this block produces a per-hard-budget error-exponent advantage of

\[
\frac{-\ln((3-\sqrt5)/8)}{3\ln2}
\approx1.12949.
\]

There is also a nonzero calibrated-effect perturbation margin: the two-use separation survives operator-norm error

\[
\delta<\frac{\sqrt5-2}{32}\approx0.007377,
\]

provided the pure-null condition remains intact.

This does **not** establish the optimal general hard-budget exponent, nor a total thermodynamic-work advantage. The effect does not commute with the Hamiltonian; phase references, control work, and measurement-apparatus costs are not included in the input-energy budget.

## 6. What was checked, and what remains unverified

The package preserves the actual outputs from **560 numerical Bregman fixtures, 2,196 exact rational mesh fixtures, 105 exact mixed-radix round trips, 56 scalar quadratures, ten tensor-product diagnostics through dimension 512, and 1,500 end-to-end prototype draws**. The hard-energy eigenvalue and eigenvector identities were checked symbolically.

The scalar quadratures included weak signals, zero signal, both signs of the mean, and grid sizes through \(J=512\). Their maximum normalization residual was approximately \(10^{-15}\). Those finite checks are debugging evidence, not a substitute for the general proof or an independent review.

The literature boundary is important. Approximate-sufficient-statistic compression and quantum population compression have substantial established antecedents in Hayashi–Tan and Yang and collaborators. The package credits those mechanisms rather than claiming the entire framework as new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.02542v2)

I also checked the 5 October 2026 preprint *Quantum minimum description of density matrices*. Its main theorem explicitly fixes dimension and spectrum and disclaims a uniform claim as eigenvalues collide or approach zero. That distinguishes its stated scope from the varying-mode problem here, but it does not establish this result’s novelty. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06742v1)

The OpenAI release and Astra’s accumulated release map informed the search. I did not re-audit all 722 manuscripts, and neither new proof depends on an unverified release theorem. The release itself describes differing levels of verification. 

No independent subagents, external reviewers, formal proof kernel, or physical experiments were used. The remaining decisive checks are independent reconstruction—especially of the compact-hat estimates—and historical-priority review.
