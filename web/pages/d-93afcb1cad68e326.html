# Conditional moving-frame transport lemma

## Interface

Let `x` be distance into the propagation zone, measured in the material-cell coordinate relative to the moving invagination front. Let `v_rel > 0` be the rate at which the front sweeps through cell rows in that coordinate. Let `b(x)` be the *nonuniform excess* of a surface-associated ligand pool over its spatially uniform background. Assume (only for this lemma) that the excess pool has constant effective lateral diffusivity `D > 0`, first-order clearance `k >= 0`, and no local amplification in the source-free region ahead of the front.

The steady profile in the front frame obeys

```text
0 = D b'' + v_rel b' - k b.
```

The sign of the drift follows from a coordinate moving anteriorly with the front: in the cell-material coordinate a point ahead of the front is swept toward the front at relative speed `v_rel`.

## Exact solution and design boundary

Set `b(x) = b_0 exp(-x/lambda)` and write `q=1/lambda`. The equation becomes

```text
D q^2 - v_rel q - k = 0.
```

The positive root is

```text
q = (v_rel + sqrt(v_rel^2 + 4 D k)) / (2 D),
lambda = 2 D / (v_rel + sqrt(v_rel^2 + 4 D k)).
```

Thus `lambda` is decreasing in both front-relative speed and clearance. Since `k >= 0`,

```text
lambda <= D / v_rel,
k = D/lambda^2 - v_rel/lambda >= 0  iff  v_rel * lambda <= D.
```

This gives a conditional design rule: for a target passive excess-pool footprint `lambda_target`, the measured `D` and `v_rel` must satisfy `v_rel lambda_target <= D`; then the required effective clearance is `k = D/lambda_target^2 - v_rel/lambda_target`. If the inequality fails, this passive transport model cannot produce the target range. A local positive source/gain, an incorrectly measured material-frame speed, a larger effective mobility, or a different field must be added.

## Dimensional substitution (cross-paper, not a matched experiment)

Bailles et al. report a Drosophila posterior-endoderm MyoII wave speed `v=2.2 +/- 0.2 um/min`, corresponding to roughly one cell row every three minutes (Nature 2019, DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9). Mundhe et al. report `D_slow=1.6 +/- 0.2 um^2/s` for a minor slow Fog::YFP population at the apical surface; they describe its identification with receptor-bound or membrane-interacting ligand as a presumption, not a direct molecular identification (Nature Communications 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z). Using the two means gives `D_slow/v = 43.64 um`; cornering the reported `+/-` values gives 35–54 um, not a confidence interval.

The corresponding free Fog estimate is `D_fast=55 um^2/s`, so `D_fast/v = 1500 um`. That scale is much larger than the tens-of-microns propagation zone and is consistent with the paper's direct finding that extracellular Fog is spatially uniform. The slow-pool scale is in the same order as the observed range of the moving activity/contact zone. This is a plausibility calculation only: the speed is imported from the 2019 paper, the molecular diffusivity from the 2026 paper, and the measured MyoII profile need not equal the ligand excess profile.

If, more restrictively, the observed MyoII exponential length were set by the same scalar pool, the inferred `k` is about 0.147 s^-1 for WT (`lambda=3.18 um`), 0.051 s^-1 for two extra Fog copies (`5.26 um`), and 0.047 s^-1 for gprk2 RNAi (`5.48 um`). This conditional inversion is consistent in sign with slower GPCR clearance broadening the profile but is not a measurement of endocytosis kinetics. Integrin signaling is a cofactor in the primary study, so `MyoII proportional to b` is not established.

## Strongest alternative: cell-local wave-timed kinetics with no lateral ligand transport

If each cell's signaling response is a local kinetic waveform `M(s)` indexed by time-to/after its VM-contact and detachment events, then a front moving through rows at `v_rel` produces the spatial snapshot `M(x)=M(s(x))`. An exponential per-cell segment with `s=x/v_rel` exactly produces an exponential spatial profile with `lambda=v_rel tau`; no lateral ligand transport is needed. This is an adversarial model class, not a claim that the embryo uses this exact waveform. Its strength is that the primary studies observe a repeated cell contact/adhesion/activation/detachment cycle, and the 2026 paper explicitly notes that the spatial profile is associated with temporal signaling maturation in a cell's frame. Matching the WT MyoII length and 2019 speed requires a kinetic time constant of `86.7 s`; matching `5.48 um` requires `149.5 s`. These times are not ruled out by the cited data.

The non-identifiability is not special to exponentials. For any single observed profile `P(x)` over a monotone moving front, define a cell-local response waveform `M(a)=P(v_rel a)` on the observed age interval. Then the no-transport model yields `M(x/v_rel)=P(x)` exactly. Thus a spatial snapshot alone cannot distinguish lateral transport from a cell-age response unless the waveform is independently constrained by Lagrangian time-course data or by held-out perturbations. The exponential calculation above gives one parsimonious member of this much larger alias class, not evidence that its time constant is the true cellular kinetics.

Therefore the scale match does not identify a surface-transport mechanism. The decisive discriminator is a same-condition Lagrangian measurement of cell-relative front speed, bound-pool mobility/clearance, and each cell's MyoII trajectory from first Vitelline-membrane contact through detachment. Predict the full spatial profile from each model, hold out one Fog-clearance dose and one integrin condition, and compare embryo-level profile likelihood/prediction error.

## Falsifiers and scope

1. If a same-condition surface-pool profile has `v_rel lambda > D_slow` while its background-subtracted field has no local gain, the passive moving-pool model is false.
2. If the measured `MyoII(x)` profile is predicted by the independently measured contact-age waveform with `lambda=v_rel tau`, and does not change when the bound-pool mobility or clearance is changed at matched contact geometry, the transport explanation adds no predictive value.
3. If changing Gprk2 changes MyoII range after matching Fog source level and cell-relative speed but measured surface-pool clearance does not change, the interpretation of Gprk2 solely as `k` is false.
4. If the measured surface-slow pool is not receptor-bound/membrane-associated, or its diffusion is measured in a different frame than the assumed material coordinate, the numerical substitution is invalid.

This is an exact lemma for a specified linear advection-diffusion-clearance model, not a theorem about the biological tissue. It omits contact-dependent source terms, receptor saturation, nonlinear GPCR/integrin amplification, tissue deformation feedback on advection, stochastic cell-to-cell response, and cell-row discreteness. Those are measured/modelled separately in the cited primary work. No real tissue is claimed to have been controlled by this equation.
