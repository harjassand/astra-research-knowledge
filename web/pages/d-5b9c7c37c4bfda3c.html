CYCLE 3 LEAF: SINGULAR-BOUNDARY HMM PREDICTION
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: exact scoped theorem proved; broader arbitrary-HMM theorem is prior art, not claimed as new

CLAIM AND SCOPE
===============

This leaf gives an explicit raw-stream Krichevsky–Trofimov (KT) predictor for
the observable-Markov subfamily of HMMs. Its cumulative expected stationary-
history next-symbol KL risk matches the minimax Markov-source redundancy to
leading order, even when hidden realizations are nonidentifiable and the
observable Hankel rank can collapse. The predictor uses one stationary raw
trajectory, no hidden-state labels, no reset, and no mixing or singular-value
margin. Its main guarantee is a cumulative/random-time risk guarantee; it does
not prove a terminal-time bound for this estimator.

The requested broader terminal-time result for arbitrary finite-state HMMs is
already proved in primary literature: Han, Jiang, and Wu (COLT 2024) give a
matching minimax terminal next-symbol KL rate, with no mixing, rank, or
identifiability assumptions, using a raw-observation universal-compression
predictor and dynamic programming. That theorem is the controlling prior-art
comparison here. This leaf does not claim to have independently re-proved
their full theorem or to have discovered a new general-HMM rate.

1. LEGAL INTERFACE AND WHAT IS ACQUIRED
=======================================

Alphabet: Sigma=[A], A>=2. Hidden-state cap: D>=A+1, supplied. The learner is
given only the structural promise that the *observable law* is a stationary
order-one Markov chain. Its transition matrix P is unknown. The underlying
HMM state count, transition matrix, emission matrix, state labels, and any
state-splitting weights are not supplied. Only one raw trajectory
X_1,...,X_T is available; there are no hidden-state observations, scalar
queries, resets, or independent episodes.

For transparent mixing accounting one may further restrict P to the supplied
Doeblin subclass P_ij>=eta/A for a known 0<eta<1. Then the observable
Dobrushin coefficient is at most 1-eta and beta(r)<= (1-eta)^r. The theorem
below does not use eta: the same upper bound holds for every stationary
observable Markov chain, including reducible chains with a chosen stationary
law. Thus the mixing-time multiplier in this online cumulative-risk bound is
exactly absent. On the Doeblin subclass it remains absent, and the lower
bound below still holds for every fixed eta<1 because a full-dimensional open
neighborhood of the uniform transition matrix lies inside the subclass.

The target at time t is the true full-history conditional law. Under the
observable-Markov promise,

  P(X_(t+1) in . | X_1,...,X_t) = P_(X_t,.)
  and also P(X_(t+1) in . | X_s, s<=t) = P_(X_t,.) .

For an online predictor p_t(.|X_1:t), define the cumulative stationary-history
next-symbol KL risk

  R_T(P,p) = E_P sum_(t=1)^(T-1)
               KL(P_(X_t,.) || p_t(.|X_1:t)).                      (1)

Equivalently, choose U uniformly from {1,...,T-1}, independently of the
stationary path, and evaluate the expected one-step KL of the predictor that
has seen X_1:U. Its random-time risk is R_T/(T-1). This is not the risk of a
single frozen model evaluated after the final training time.

Acquired: an observable transition predictor, sufficient for the stated
order-one task. Given: the alphabet, state cap, order-one observable-law
promise, and (optionally) eta. Not acquired: hidden states, hidden transition
or emission parameters, a minimal HMM, or a predictor for HMMs whose output is
not order one.

2. THEOREM: EXACT ONLINE KT RISK AND MINIMAX RATE
================================================

For every t and pair i,j, let

  N_ij(t) = sum_(s<t) 1{X_s=i, X_(s+1)=j},
  N_i(t)  = sum_j N_ij(t),
  p_t(j|i) = (N_ij(t)+1/2)/(N_i(t)+A/2).                          (2)

The predictor outputs p_t(.|X_t). It is a rational function of integer
transition counts, and is defined even when a context or transition has not
yet been seen.

Theorem 1 (one-stream observable-Markov acquisition). There is a universal
constant C such that, for every A>=2, every T>=2, every stationary A-symbol
Markov chain P, and the predictor (2),

  sup_P R_T(P,p)
    <= [A(A-1)/2] log(T+1) + C A^2 log(A+1).                      (3)

All logs in this report are natural unless a source explicitly uses bits.
For fixed A, the minimax cumulative risk over stationary order-one Markov
sources satisfies

  inf_(all sequential predictors q) sup_P R_T(P,q)
     = [A(A-1)/2] log T + O_A(1),                                 (4)

so (2) matches the leading minimax rate. The lower bound also holds on the
known-eta Doeblin subclass for any fixed eta<1. In particular, the random-time
average risk is O(A^2 log T/T), and its fixed-A minimax order is
Theta(log T/T).

The rate has no dependence on D once D>=A+1, because the task's observable
transition law has A(A-1) free coordinates. D only enters the condition that
the singular hidden realization described below is allowed. There is no
mixing or filter-stability multiplier. The statement is cumulative/average
online risk, not terminal-time risk.

Proof of the upper bound. Conditional on X_1, the predictor induces a proper
probability assignment Q_KT on X_2:T:

  Q_KT(x_2:T | x_1) = product_(t=1)^(T-1) p_t(x_(t+1)|x_t).

For each current symbol i, collect in order the successor symbols seen after
visits to i. The row factors of Q_KT are independent Dirichlet-(1/2,...,1/2)
mixtures. If that row has counts n_1,...,n_A and m=sum_j n_j, its mixture
probability is

  Q_i = Gamma(A/2)/Gamma(m+A/2) * product_j Gamma(n_j+1/2)/Gamma(1/2).

The standard KT/multinomial regret inequality, obtained by applying Stirling
bounds to these Gamma factors, gives uniformly over every row distribution
P_i and every row sequence

  log [product_j P_ij^(n_j) / Q_i]
       <= (A-1)/2 log(m+1) + C_0 A log(A+1),                      (5)

for a universal C_0. This inequality is pointwise in the row sequence; it
uses no independence assumption about the order in which the context rows are
visited. Summing (5) over A rows and using m<=T-1 proves the deterministic
sequence bound

  log [product_(t=1)^(T-1) P_(x_t,x_(t+1)) /
       Q_KT(x_2:T|x_1)]
       <= [A(A-1)/2] log(T+1) + C A^2 log(A+1).                   (6)

For the stochastic risk, condition on the observed prefix at each time. The
Markov property gives

  E[ log(P_(X_t,X_(t+1))/p_t(X_(t+1)|X_1:t)) | X_1:t ]
      = KL(P_(X_t,.) || p_t(.|X_1:t)).

Therefore

  R_T(P,p) = D(P_(X_2:T|X_1) || Q_KT(.|X_1))
            = E_P log [P(X_2:T|X_1)/Q_KT(X_2:T|X_1)].             (7)

Taking expectations in (6) proves (3). Equation (6) is a *pathwise code
regret* bound. Equation (7) is the separate expected conditional-KL identity;
(6) is not a pathwise bound on the random sum of conditional KL values.

Proof status of the lower bound. The minimax cumulative risk in (4) is the
minimax redundancy of order-one Markov sources, up to the O_A(1) initial-
symbol convention. Jacquet and Szpankowski prove the worst-case minimax
redundancy leading term [A(A-1)/2] log_2 T for an A-symbol order-one Markov
source. Converting bits to nats gives the coefficient in (4). The same
leading lower bound holds when P_ij>=eta/A for fixed eta<1: the row-simplex
parameter space then contains an open neighborhood of P_ij=1/A, the
stationary distribution is smooth there, and the per-transition Fisher
information is positive definite in the A(A-1) free row coordinates.
Standard smooth-family minimax redundancy lower bounds apply on that open
subfamily. This is an observable-law lower bound; hidden-state recovery is
not part of the loss.

Failure probability. The theorem is an expected-risk minimax statement and
has no confidence parameter delta. The deterministic code-regret inequality
(6) fails with probability zero. If a high-probability statement about the
random conditional-KL sum K_T=sum_t KL(P_(X_t,.)||p_t) is desired, (3) and
Markov's inequality give only the valid but crude bound

  Pr{K_T > ([A(A-1)/2]log(T+1)+C A^2 log(A+1))/delta} <= delta.    (8)

No sharp high-probability bound for K_T is claimed here.

3. SINGULAR HMM REALIZATIONS AND IDENTIFIABILITY TEST
=====================================================

For any A-symbol transition matrix P and any hidden cap D>=A+1, partition
the D hidden states into nonempty groups G_1,...,G_A, with at least one group
having two states. Give every state s in G_i deterministic emission i. Let
w_(j,s')>0 for s' in G_j sum to one within each group, and define

  P_hidden(s,s') = P_ij w_(j,s')  when s in G_i, s' in G_j.       (9)

The output process is exactly the Markov chain P. The hidden states within a
group have identical emissions and identical transition rows. Their split
weights and labels are invisible in the output. The emission operator has
duplicate rows and is rank-deficient as a D-row hidden-state map. If P is
positive and each w is positive, this realization is irreducible; if P has a
Doeblin floor, the hidden chain inherits the same common-overlap contraction
because each P row is passed through the same within-group weights.

Concrete identifiable/nonidentifiable pair. Let

  P = [[3/4, 1/4], [1/4, 3/4]],   pi=(1/2,1/2).

Model I has two hidden states, identity emissions, and transition matrix P.
Since its stationary symbol masses are positive, its observable pair law
recovers each row P_(i,.) as P(X_(t+1)|X_t=i). Model II has four states
(i,b), i,b in {0,1}, emits i,
and transitions from (i,b) to (j,b') with probability P_ij/2. Both models
give every output string x_1:T exactly probability

  (1/2) product_(t=1)^(T-1) P_(x_t,x_(t+1)).

Model II's four hidden states are not identifiable: b and its transition
splitting are unobservable, and its emission rows are duplicated. The KT
predictor acquires the same observable transition law from either source; it
does not claim to reconstruct which hidden realization generated it. The
exact rational checker in work/programmes/acquisition_learning/cycle3/
singular_boundary_prediction/verify_exact.py verified this identity for all
126 binary strings of lengths 1 through 6. The analytic identity follows by
summing the independent split-bit choices in (9).

There is also an exact Hankel-collapse point inside the same Doeblin family:
P_ij=1/A gives an iid uniform output process with Hankel rank one. For
A=2, the matrices

  P_rho = [[(1+rho)/2, (1-rho)/2],
           [(1-rho)/2, (1+rho)/2]],  0<=rho<1-eta,

all obey P_ij>=eta/2, while the nonstationary eigenvalue/singular direction
has magnitude rho and tends to zero. Thus the theorem does not hide a
positive observable Hankel gap behind its mixing statement. The hidden
duplicated realization also has exact null directions.

4. SHARP SCOPE FAILURE FOR THE ORDER-ONE CONSTRUCTION
=====================================================

The order-one KT guarantee relies on the observable-Markov promise. It is
not a universal HMM predictor. Here is a concrete mixing, singular-emission
counterexample.

Let the hidden state be S_t=(X_(t-1),X_t) in {0,1}^2, with deterministic
emission X_t equal to the second coordinate. From (a,b), transition to (b,c)
with probability

  Pr(c|a,b) = [1 + lambda (-1)^(a+b+c)]/2,   0<lambda<1.          (10)

The uniform distribution on four hidden pairs is stationary: for each target
(b,c), the probabilities arriving from (0,b) and (1,b) sum to one. Hence
every observed pair (a,b) has mass 1/4, so the one-step observable table is
identical to iid fair bits. But the triple law is

  Pr(X_(t-1:t+1)=(a,b,c))
      = [1+lambda(-1)^(a+b+c)]/8,

and conditioning on the full two-symbol history gives (10), not 1/2. Its
full-history KL from the one-symbol/iid prediction is the positive constant

  d(lambda) = ((1+lambda)/2) log(1+lambda)
            + ((1-lambda)/2) log(1-lambda) > 0.                   (11)

The hidden chain is mixing: every specified two-step transition has
probability at least ((1-lambda)/2)^2, so its two-step kernel has a Doeblin
minorization. Its emission rows are duplicated (rank 2 rather than 4).
Consequently pair-law acquisition alone cannot solve full-history HMM
prediction, even in a positive/mixing example with no emission-rank gap.
The finite exact checker verified stationarity, pair marginals, and the triple
formula for lambda=1/3. This is a limitation of the order-one KT leaf, not an
impossibility for learners using the complete path; Han–Jiang–Wu's HMM result
below covers terminal full-history prediction in expectation.

5. STRONGER PRIMARY-SOURCE CONTROL: ARBITRARY FINITE-STATE HMMS
==============================================================

Han, Jiang, and Wu define the target from one stationary observed path
X_1,...,X_n over an alphabet of size A and predict X_(n+1) given the full
observed history. Their HMM has k hidden states and unknown transition and
emission matrices. They explicitly impose no full-rank, identifiability,
mixing, emission-floor, or filter-stability condition; parameter
nonidentifiability is noted in their model definition.

Their Theorem 1.1 proves, for n>=C k(k+A),

  Risk_HMM(n,k,A) <= C [ (kA/n) log(n/(kA))
                         + (k^2/n) log(n/k^2) ],                (12)

and proves the matching lower bound with C^{-1} in the regimes either
A>=k and n>=kA, or k,A>=2 and n>=k^C. In particular for fixed k,A>=2 the
minimax terminal risk is Theta(log n/n). The upper estimator runs in
n^{O(k^2+kA)} time. There is no mixing/filter term and no positive singular
gap. The two terms separately charge emission and transition complexity.

The paper's construction is raw-data-only. It puts an add-one KT mixture on
each hidden transition row and each hidden emission row, starts the hidden
sequence uniformly, and sums out the latent path to form an observable
probability assignment Q_X. A pointwise joint-path regret bound gives
observable redundancy O(k^2 log(n/k^2)+kA log(n/(kA))). A suffix-average
predictor converts this sequence redundancy to terminal prediction risk;
its memory term is at most log k/n because
sum_t I(X_(n-t);X_(n+1)|X_(n-t+1:n)) <= I(Z_1;X_(n+1)) <= log k.
For the lower bound the authors reduce prediction risk to redundancy of
subclasses and use mutual-information priors; the overcomplete binary case
uses an explicit transition-estimation reduction. These are the paper's
proof mechanisms, not a new proof claim by this leaf.

At most D states, unknown actual state count. The fixed-k result adapts by
forming Q_mix=(1/D) sum_(k=1)^D Q_k. For a true k-state source,
Q_mix(x_1:n+1)>=Q_k(x_1:n+1)/D, so sequence redundancy adds log D. In the
suffix-average conversion the hidden-memory term is at most log D. Thus the
source theorem yields its oracle upper bound plus 2 log D/n for unknown
k<=D, with the computational cost sum_(k<=D)n^{O(k^2+kA)}. The exact-k=D
subclass gives the corresponding lower bound whenever its stated regime
holds. This is a simple count-mixture adaptation, not an inferred state
rank.

Pathwise versus expected risk. The paper first bounds pointwise sequence
redundancy for a universal code, then uses a separate expected-risk
reduction and a suffix-memory term to obtain terminal risk. A pointwise code
ratio bound is not itself a pathwise bound on conditional KL. The order-one
proof above instead has an exact chain-rule identity for *cumulative online*
risk; it does not convert that result to risk of the final frozen predictor.

6. SAME-DATA FULL-LIKELIHOOD CONTROL
===================================

Order-one class. The strongest equal-information baseline receives the same
entire raw string. The row-wise full likelihood is

  L_ML(P;x_1:T)=product_(i,j) P_ij^(N_ij).

Its MLE rows are N_ij/N_i, but unsmoothed MLE can assign zero to an unseen
symbol and have infinite test KL. The KT mixture (2) is a proper, smoothed
full-likelihood code on exactly the same transition counts. The normalized
maximum-likelihood (NML) distribution is minimax for pathwise regret; the
minimax-redundancy results cited below show that no sequential predictor can
improve the leading [A(A-1)/2] log T cumulative KL term. A raw-path retainer
can reproduce every count and the same predictor. The KT representation
provides no additional information advantage; it may trade storage and
computation against retaining the full string.

Arbitrary HMM class. Han–Jiang–Wu's DP mixture also sees the full raw path and
matches the same-data minimax lower bound. A full-HMM maximum-likelihood
routine is not an information-stronger baseline: it receives the same path,
and the universal code is a regularized full-sequence likelihood. No strict
advantage over a learner retaining the full trajectory is established.

7. PRECISION, FAILURE, AND RESOURCE LEDGER
==========================================

For (2), each output coordinate is the exact rational
(2N_ij+1)/(2N_i+A). Numerator and denominator need O(log(T+A)) bits. Store A^2
counts using O(A^2 log(T+1)) bits; update one transition in O(1) integer
operations and produce a row in O(A) arithmetic operations. Input storage if
retained is T ceil(log2 A) bits. A final full transition table takes
O(A^2 log(T+A)) bits. Emitting a full A-entry row at each time costs
O(T A log(T+A)) output bits. Exact arithmetic has no quantization failure.

If each emitted coordinate is rounded to a common dyadic grid with
coordinatewise error at most 2^-b and the row is renormalized by largest
remainders, then the KT floor is at least 1/(2T+A). For
2^-b<=1/[2(2T+A)], the one-step KL change is at most
2(2T+A)2^-b. Thus b>=ceil(log_2(2(2T+A)/epsilon)) makes the per-step added
KL at most epsilon; to keep total cumulative added KL <=epsilon, use
b>=ceil(log_2(2T(2T+A)/epsilon)). This assumes exact counts and a
simplex-preserving rounding rule.

The arbitrary-HMM DP from Han–Jiang–Wu uses rational add-one probabilities.
For a suffix of length at most n, each emitted-symbol predictive coordinate
is at least 1/(A+n), since each KT emission row has that floor and marginal-
izing hidden states preserves it. If the exact rational output is rounded
coordinatewise by at most 2^-b, then 2^-b<=1/[2(A+n)] gives at most
2(A+n)2^-b extra one-step KL. A b=O(log((A+n)/epsilon)) dyadic precision
therefore suffices for epsilon additive terminal risk. Their paper states
arithmetic-time n^{O(k^2+kA)}; a conservative exact-bit implementation can
use sufficient-statistic arrays with entries <=n and common rising-factorial
denominators of O(k n log(n+k+A)) bits, plus integer path counts of
O(n log(k+1)) bits. This bit-width accounting is derived here; it is not a
reported implementation benchmark.

No statistical confidence failure delta is needed for these expected-risk
claims. The order-one pathwise code regret holds for every finite string. For
the random conditional-KL sum, only the expectation bound and the explicitly
crude Markov tail (8) are claimed. The Han–Jiang–Wu minimax theorem is also an
expected risk theorem, not a high-probability guarantee.

   infinite memory, with applications to hidden Markov and renewal

Novelty boundary. The general bounded-state HMM task in the parent prompt is
already solved in expected terminal KL up to universal constants by Han et
al. under a strictly broader class than this leaf's observable-Markov
subclass. The order-one leaf adds an elementary exact row-code proof, a
singular/nonidentifiable HMM embedding, and a scope counterexample; it is not
a new all-HMM theorem or a priority claim.
