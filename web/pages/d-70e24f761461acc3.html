Question
--------
Check whether primary literature already proves an all-N thermal collective
bath result from a full maximally mixed N-qubit input, especially the
large-N/fixed finite bath-occupation entanglement claim for

    L rho = Gamma [(nu+1) D[J_-] + nu D[J_+]] rho.

Closest all-N finite-temperature primary result: collective thermal steady
states are already explicit
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Camille L. Latune, Ilya Sinayskiy, and Francesco Petruccione, “Collective heat
capacity for quantum thermometry and quantum engine enhancements,” New J.
Phys. 22, 083049 (2020), arXiv:2004.00032:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00032

This is the strongest match to the thermal generator and all-N sector
structure. Its Eq. (1) is the collective-spin thermal Lindbladian from a
Born–Markov–secular bath coupling, with raising/lowering jumps J^+ and J^- and
thermal rates Gamma(±omega). Eqs. (3)–(4) give the stationary state for an
ensemble of arbitrary spin dimension and arbitrary size as a mixture over
total-spin irreducible sectors and multiplicities:

    rho_ss = sum_{J,i} p_{J,i} rho^th_{J,i}(beta),
    rho^th_{J,i}(beta) = Z_J^{-1} sum_{m=-J}^J exp(-beta hbar omega m)
                                  |J,m>_i <J,m|.

The sector weights p_{J,i} are the initial state's weights in those sectors
and are conserved. The authors explicitly state that this is a multiple-
steady-state dynamics. They flag a caveat for initial coherences between
multiplicity labels; the maximally mixed input I/2^N has no such coherences,
so the displayed formula applies directly to it. For spin-1/2, detailed
balance turns the within-sector weights into the truncated geometric law
q^m, with q = exp(-beta hbar omega) = nu/(nu+1), up to the choice of which
ladder direction is called excitation. Thus the all-N sector-stationary
mechanism and the q-dependent stationary state used in the present analysis
are not new. The paper does not analyze separability, partial transpose, or
the entanglement witness in the present work; it does not state the
fixed-finite-nu / sufficiently-large-N entanglement conclusion. Its
high-temperature heat-capacity scaling is principally reported for the
maximal-spin sector, so do not conflate that with the full-space maximally
mixed input, whose conserved sector weights are different.

An independent exact derivation for all spin sectors appears in Christopher
J. Wood, Troy W. Borneman, and David G. Cory, “Cavity Cooling of an Ensemble
Spin System,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 112, 050501 (2014), arXiv:1305.1029:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1029

Their Eqs. (12), (15), and (24) give the cavity-mediated effective thermal
jump generator and exact per-J stationary Dicke probabilities

    P_{J,m}(infinity) =
      nbar^(J+m) (1+nbar)^(J-m) /
      ((1+nbar)^(2J+1) - nbar^(2J+1)).

They state that the derivation holds for all J because the Tavis–Cummings
Hamiltonian does not couple the total-spin subspaces. This is an exact
all-J/all-N stationary-population precedent for the same truncated geometric
law, in a cavity-cooling implementation. The paper's application is
polarization/cooling rather than entanglement; its large-N simulations focus
on the symmetric Dicke subspace and zero cavity occupation, not the full
maximally mixed ensemble at fixed nonzero nbar. It therefore preempts a claim
to the sector stationary formula, but not by itself the entanglement witness
or its all-N thermal scaling consequence.

Finite-N full-maximally-mixed thermal-like common-bath precedent
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fabio Benatti and Adam Nagy, “Three qubits in a symmetric environment:
dissipatively generated asymptotic entanglement,” arXiv:1009.1284 (2010),
Ann. Phys. 326, 740–753 (2011): https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.1284

Their symmetric three-qubit Markovian generator (Eqs. (5)–(8)) is built from
global spin operators. It has collective raising and lowering channels with
rate imbalance parameter b/a, plus collective S_3 dephasing c>0; |b|<a gives
a full-rank stationary product state and c>0 ensures convergence to the
stationary projection. This is a common symmetric environment, but the paper
does not identify its three-qubit model with the pure bosonic thermal
amplitude-damping generator above, and the extra collective dephasing should
be retained in any comparison.

Their Eq. (38) takes rho_123(alpha) = rho(alpha) tensor I/2. At alpha=1/4,
rho(alpha)=I_4/4, so this is exactly I_8/8. They give the asymptotic reduced
two-qubit concurrence in Eq. (59). Substituting alpha=1/4 into that equation
shows it is strictly positive exactly when

    4 r^6 + 9 r^4 - 9 > 0,   r = b/a,

equivalently |r| > 0.92288... (the positive root has r^2=0.85170813...).
This is a full-rank-parameter, N=3, full-maximally-mixed-input example whose
asymptotic two-qubit reduction is entangled; hence the three-qubit whole state
is entangled. Strict positivity and continuity also imply entanglement at
some finite time. This is an important finite-N common-reservoir precedent,
but its generator includes collective dephasing and it supplies no growing-N
or fixed-bosonic-occupation threshold.

The already-recorded Li–Xu result remains the cleanest exact match to the
pure thermal amplitude-damping model at N=2: at zero bath occupation,
I_4/4 tends to (3/4)|00><00| + (1/4)|singlet><singlet|; their concurrence
formula also gives a finite-temperature threshold. See the earlier
COLLECTIVE_DARK_SECTOR_PRIORITY_AUDIT.txt for the derivation and scope.

Nearby arbitrary-N entanglement work and boundaries
---------------------------------------------------
Laleh Memarzadeh and Stefano Mancini, “Entanglement dynamics for qubits
dissipating into a common environment,” Phys. Rev. A 87, 032303 (2013),
arXiv:1210.5340: https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.5340

This treats arbitrary N under zero-temperature collective decay, but its
analytic entanglement results assume initial states with a small number of
excitations and study selected pair reductions. It is not the full-maximally-
mixed input or finite-temperature scaling problem.

Priority boundary
-----------------
The all-N finite-temperature stationary state, conserved total-spin-sector
weights, and per-sector truncated geometric distribution have clear primary
precedents (Latune et al. 2020; independently Wood et al. 2014). These
sources substantially narrow any novelty claim for deriving or presenting
that stationary ensemble. I did not find, in this targeted primary-source
pass, a paper deriving its entanglement for the full maximally mixed input,
proving the quoted separable-state witness, or establishing that every fixed
finite bosonic occupation nu eventually yields entanglement as N grows.
Benatti–Nagy gives a distinct N=3 full-I/8 common-environment example with
extra collective dephasing; Li–Xu gives the pure thermal collective-jump
example at N=2. This is a bounded search result, not a comprehensive priority
or novelty determination.
