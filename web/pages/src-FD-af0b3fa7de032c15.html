Investigate several exceptionally important scientific obstructions.

For each serious candidate, identify the capability that would become possible, the strongest existing method, and the actual mathematical, computational or physical limitation preventing it.

Do not choose primarily by the probability of completing a restricted theorem.

Once selected, preserve the consequential objective through different failed mechanisms rather than restarting the scientific search whenever a proposed approach fails.

## Invent beyond existing methods

Challenge the objects being computed, the representations used, the information assumed necessary and the physical interventions available.

Investigate genuinely different mechanisms, including possibilities outside established algorithms and scientific control frameworks.

Explore both backward from an impossible capability and forward from unfamiliar mathematical or physical structures.

When credible exceptional leverage emerges, develop it deeply.

A substantive unfinished mechanism can be a legitimate research outcome. A narrowly completed theorem is not a substitute for progress on the decisive objective.

If the gate is exponentially depleted, its resistance can reappear explicitly in the transport cost.

That does not prove exponential computational complexity: a large resistance can sometimes be reconstructed cheaply from known geometry. It does show exactly why the translated-bath result does not solve arbitrary hidden gates.

The retained research problem is therefore:

Acquire and integrate finite-width gate currents without waiting for the original rare population transfers—and without assuming those currents or their thermal weights are already known.

8. Scientific advantage: what remains to be earned
There is substantial nearby prior art. Conditional-expectation coarse-graining already distinguishes equilibrium free-energy information from effective dynamics. Functional a posteriori methods already learn auxiliary fields to produce rigorous elliptic error bounds. Committor-based methods already optimize rare-event information acquisition. 

Accordingly, the candidate novelty is not any of those ingredients individually. It is the proposed source-conserving transport construction, its lifetime-specific learning objective, and the possibility of acquiring its information without equilibrium sampling. An exhaustive priority comparison has not been completed.

A meaningful advantage must survive the following accounting:

Requirement	Current status
Obtain the potential, mobility, boundaries and initial law	Explicit model inputs; no real molecular model calibrated here
Discover a useful map	Unresolved for genuinely gated, coupled systems
Obtain equilibrium information	Eliminated in the exact translated-bath sector; replaced by potentially difficult integration in the general construction
Control numerical uncertainty	Exact formulas and finite algebra tests established; certified high-dimensional integration unimplemented
Beat strong specialized methods	Not demonstrated; comparison must include optimized WE and current committor/path-sampling methods
Reduce physical or computational energy cost	No measured claim; this is a computational proposal, not a physical barrier-removal device
The decisive next experiment is a genuinely gated, multimodal high-dimensional model, not a product model supplied in favorable coordinates. Gate barriers and spectator dimension must vary independently. Every comparator should receive identical potential-and-force access, and all map search, sampling, training and validation costs must count.

The construction must then produce cheaper, correctly covered lifetime intervals—or reliable kinetic rankings—than the strongest matched baselines. A multichart or branching current representation may be necessary; its interface source conservation must be exact or explicitly bounded.

Bottom line
The most useful principle derived in this investigation is:

For a specified reversible diffusion, a mean lifetime can be bounded by the cost of transporting its conditional equilibrium ensembles, without assuming that those ensembles equilibrate rapidly during the physical process.

In an explicit non-Gaussian transported-bath class, this removes equilibrium-data acquisition entirely. For general systems, cumulative-mass transport provides an exact current construction and a concrete optimization target.

The hard scientific problem remains gate acquisition: learning the finite-width transport geometry and controlling its conditional mass overlap at a cost that does not reproduce the original rare-event difficulty. That—not another certificate on a supplied easy representation—is the consequential objective to carry forward.
