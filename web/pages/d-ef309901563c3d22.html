EXACT RATIONAL MATRIX-RESPONSE PACKING
2026-10-07. New deduction in this run. Internal algebraic proof; priority unassessed.

Theorem (one diagonal readout simulates a full nilpotent matrix response).
Let f be a recognizable nc rational formal series over Q,
 f_w = u B_w v,
with at most m states, n letters, and every entry of u,v,B_i of rational height at most H: numerator and denominator absolute values at most 2^H. Suppose A_1,...,A_n are supplied rational q-by-q matrices, every word product A_w is zero for |w| >= ell, and the matrices have entry height at most h. There is an explicitly computable rational tuple Z of dimension q+2 and polynomial bit length in (n,m,q,ell,H,h), and a polynomial-time exact decoder, such that ONE returned entry f(Z)_(1,1) recovers the ENTIRE f(A). The tuple is jointly nilpotent with the same index ell, so every proper-at-zero rational realization is legally evaluable. No coefficients of the hidden realization are supplied to the encoder.

This result assumes H is supplied. It does not imply coefficient-free single-scalar learning or replace noncommuting matrix input access with passive samples. Exact output is essential.

Proof.
1. Uniform response bounds without knowing a common denominator.
Let P=n m^2+2m, C=2^H. The product Q of all hidden realization denominators is at most 2^(PH). Let q_A be the product of all denominators appearing in A, computable from the query, and let M_A=max(1,max|A_i(a,b)|). Every f(A) entry and constant c=uv has denominator dividing
 R_den = Q^(ell+1) q_A^(ell-1).
Thus every digit below has denominator <= D=2^(PH(ell+1)) q_A^(ell-1), and log D is polynomial. This uses the finite truncation |w|<ell; Q need never be computed.

Put R=max(1,n m q M_A C), M_f=ell m C^2 R^ell, M=2 M_f. The coefficient estimate
 |u B_w v| <= m^(|w|+1) C^(|w|+2)
and the matrix product entry estimate
 |(A_w)_(a,b)| <= q^(|w|-1) M_A^|w|, |w|>=1,
show |f(A)_(a,b)|,|c| <= M_f. Therefore c and every entry of G=f(A)-cI have magnitude <=M, and denominator <=D. Rational bounds can be rounded upward to integer M. log M is polynomial.

2. Construct a scalar encoding by similarity-covariant block evaluation.
Choose any integer K > 4 M D^2+2. Index a,b from 0 to q-1 and define row alpha_a=K^(qa+1), column beta_b=K^b. Set
 X_i = [[0, alpha A_i, alpha A_i beta],
        [0, A_i,       A_i beta],
        [0, 0,         0]].
For every nonempty word w, straightforward block multiplication gives
 (X_w)_(1,last)=alpha A_w beta,
with middle block A_w, top-middle alpha A_w and middle-right A_w beta. Consequently f(X)_(1,last)=alpha G beta and f(X)_(1,1)=c. Let S=I+E_(last,1), with S^-1=I-E_(last,1), and put Z_i=S^-1 X_i S. Similarity covariance gives
 y=f(Z)_(1,1)=c+alpha G beta
   =c+ sum_(a,b) G_(a,b) K^(qa+b+1).
The constant is digit 0; all q^2 matrix entries have distinct positive exponents. X_w=0 whenever A_w=0, so legality is retained. Input sizes are polynomial because the largest power is K^(q^2) and log K is polynomial.

3. Polynomial exact rational digit decoding.
More generally, suppose y=sum_(j=0)^L c_j K^j, every |c_j|<=M and denominator(c_j)<=D. Let e=L. Then
 |y/K^e-c_e| <= M/(K-1) < 1/(2D^2).
Distinct rationals of denominator <=D are at least 1/D^2 apart. Hence c_e is the unique rational of denominator <=D within this distance; continued fractions recover it in polynomial bit time (equivalently exact nearest-rational-with-bounded-denominator). Subtract c_e K^e, decrement e and repeat. The same bound holds at every step. Recover G and c, then f(A)=G+cI.
This addresses an easy pitfall: the denominator of reduced y can be smaller than the denominator of its individual digits, so extracting integer base-K digits after multiplying only by denominator(y) is invalid. The continued-fraction decoder requires only the bound D, not an actual common denominator and not lcm(1,...,2^H), whose size would be exponential in H.

The scalar response numerator/denominator height is polynomial: a common denominator <=D exists, |y|<=2 M K^(q^2), so its bit length is O(logD+logM+q^2 logK). Gaussian elimination over polynomially many decoded rational entries has polynomial bit cost. This proves the theorem.

Higher-order consequences.
A. Every exact full-output nilpotent matrix-oracle algorithm for H-bounded rational realizations can be simulated by an oracle returning only its fixed first DIAGONAL entry, one scalar call per full-output call, with polynomial dimension and bit overhead. In particular, if the source L01 polynomial matrix-oracle realization learner is correct, its full-output requirement is removable under this height contract. This is a query-interface reduction, not acquisition of a physical matrix-input oracle.
B. Conditional on the source L01 universal nilpotent detector at state bound 2m, there is one explicit polynomial-bit matrix query whose one diagonal response is injective on ALL rational recognizable series with at most m states and height <=H. Use the detector's direct-sum tuple for 2m states as A; f-g has <=2m states and height <=H by a block-diagonal realization. Equality of scalar responses implies equality of all base outputs by rational digit uniqueness, and the detector then gives f=g. Thus one exact scalar response is an information-theoretically complete fingerprint. Efficient reconstruction from that ONE response is not proved; enumerating all bounded-height realizations is finite but exponential. Polynomial adaptive acquisition follows only by simulating the actual full-output learner.
C. Off-diagonal-only oracle access cannot detect an arbitrary constant term, even at arbitrarily many matrix queries; f=cI always has zero off-diagonal entries. A diagonal readout, supplied constant term, or zero-constant restriction is necessary. Trace readouts remain blind to commutators under every matrix query, so this reduction does not extend to trace-only access.

Prior-art/novelty limits.
Base/radix packing, rational reconstruction, and similarity covariance are standard. The specific polynomial-bit rational packing lemma and its use to remove L01's full-response assumption were derived in this run; historical novelty not established. This is an interface/cost consequence, not an important standalone PIT breakthrough. H supplies exactly the scale contract absent in coefficient-free exact-arithmetic claims. An integer common-denominator supplied interface makes the decoder simpler but is not needed.
