A. SYMMETRIC FULL-SUPPORT FAMILY WITH NORM RATIO >3 AND TV >3/5

Use four sites 0,1,2,3, k=2, delta=2^(-L), L>=4. Every diagonal entry is
zero; set F[1,2]=F[1,3]=F[2,3]=1 and their symmetric counterparts to one.
Specify the remaining three symmetric edges by (x,y,z)=(F[0,1],F[0,2],F[0,3]).
Let

 F_v: x=1+delta^2,
      y=1+delta+2delta^2,
      z=1-delta+3delta^2;
 F_u: x=1+delta+delta^2,
      y=1+2delta^2,
      z=1+3delta^2.

Every off-diagonal entry is strictly positive. The three complementary
minor groups {01,23}, {02,13}, {03,12}, each with two configurations,
have respective squared amplitudes

 (y-z)^2, (x-z)^2, (x-y)^2.

This follows by writing the actual 2-by-2 minors; complementing a row set
transposes its complementary minor because F is symmetric. Thus

 Z_v=c_2(F_v)=2[(2delta-delta^2)^2+(delta-2delta^2)^2
                              +(-delta-delta^2)^2]
             =12delta^2(1-delta+delta^2),
 Z_u=c_2(F_u)=2[delta^4+(delta-2delta^2)^2+(delta-delta^2)^2]
             =4delta^2(1-3delta+3delta^2).

All three amplitudes in both matrices are nonzero for 0<delta<=1/16.
Therefore ALL SIX fixed-half-filling hard configurations have positive
weight in both matrices, with exactly the same support.

The projected norm ratio is

 Z_v/Z_u=3(1-delta+delta^2)/(1-3delta+3delta^2)>3,

since the numerator minus denominator inside the ratio is
2delta(1-delta)>0, and the denominator is positive. It is also below four
throughout delta<=1/16, although this upper bound is not needed for the
obstruction. Both norms tend to zero like delta^2.

Let A denote the first complementary-minor group {01,23}. Its normalized
probabilities are

 pi_v(A)=(2-delta)^2/[6(1-delta+delta^2)],
 pi_u(A)=delta^2/[2(1-3delta+3delta^2)].

Hence TV(pi_v,pi_u)>=pi_v(A)-pi_u(A). The difference tends to 2/3 as
delta goes to zero and is greater than 3/5 uniformly for delta<=1/16.
A deliberately loose rational verification of the latter is

 pi_v(A)>= (31/16)^2 / [6(1+1/256)] =961/1542,
 pi_u(A)<= (1/256)/[2(1-3/16)] =1/416,
 961/1542-1/416=398234/641472>3/5.

The matrices are nevertheless close and well conditioned. Their difference
is a symmetric star centered at 0, with edge differences (-delta,+delta,
-delta), up to the choice of subtraction order. A symmetric star with edge
vector d has eigenvalues +||d||_2,-||d||_2,0,0. Therefore

 ||F_v-F_u||_2=sqrt(3)delta.

Let F0=J-I. It has singular values 3,1,1,1. Both perturbations from F0 are
symmetric stars. For F_v the sum of edge perturbation magnitudes is
delta^2+(delta+2delta^2)+(delta-3delta^2)=2delta.
For F_u it is delta+6delta^2<=2delta. Thus

 ||F_v-F0||_2, ||F_u-F0||_2<=2delta,
 sigma_min(F_v),sigma_min(F_u)>=1-2delta,
 sigma_max(F_v),sigma_max(F_u)<=3+2delta,
 kappa_2(F_v),kappa_2(F_u)<=(3+2delta)/(1-2delta)<=25/7<4.

Their shared zero diagonal causes no entrywise relative ambiguity. Every
off-diagonal entry is at least 1-delta, and its change is at most delta;
therefore the entrywise relative difference is at most
delta/(1-delta)<=2delta.

Consequences with exact scopes:
1. No modulus depending only on n, kappa(F), and an entrywise relative or
   operator-norm perturbation can tend to zero uniformly for normalized
   hard-parity laws: n=4 and kappa<4 stay fixed while perturbations vanish
   but TV>3/5.
2. A constant-relative projected norm estimate cannot be justified by
   condition-number-only input rounding: the norm ratio exceeds three
   for matrices whose distance tends to zero. For example epsilon=1/4
   relative-estimation intervals are disjoint.
3. This does not contradict polynomial BIT complexity. Each entry has
   O(L) bits and a legal exact-input algorithm may spend polynomial work
   reading them and preserving the determinant cancellation. Nor does it
   refute multiplicative approximation of the actual projected positive
   coefficients or an exact rarity-independent counting theorem.

B. NONSYMMETRIC FULL-SUPPORT FAMILY WITH TV TENDING TO ONE

This complements A with the stronger TV separation available outside the
spin-singlet restriction. Let F0=J-I on four sites, C[i,j]=(i+1)^j for i!=j,
C[i,i]=0, H=4! 65^4, delta=2^(-L), L>=4, epsilon=delta^2/H. Define

 F_+=F0+delta E_(0,2)+epsilon C,
 F_-=F0+delta E_(2,0)+epsilon C.

For epsilon=0, F_+ has exactly two positive complementary row minors,
I={0,1},{0,3}, with amplitudes +delta,-delta. F_- has exactly the two
disjoint complementary row minors I={1,2},{2,3}, again with amplitudes
of absolute value delta. Both norms are exactly 2delta^2.

Adding epsilon C changes each amplitude by at most epsilon H=delta^2.
To see this directly, all unperturbed minor entries have magnitude at most
two, and the coefficient sum of a 2-by-2 determinant in epsilon is at most
2!(2+64)^2=8712<=H. Every former positive amplitude remains nonzero.
Every former zero amplitude also becomes nonzero: on a disjoint 2-by-2
minor not containing the distinguished delta entry, the entries before
epsilon are all one. If row values are s<t and column exponents a<b,

 det(ones+epsilon C)=
 epsilon[(t^b-t^a)-(s^b-s^a)]
 +epsilon^2 s^a t^a(t^(b-a)-s^(b-a))>0.

Here t^b-t^a is strictly increasing for t>=1 and a<b. Hence all six hard
weights are strictly positive in both matrices. The same bound gives
nonendpoint squared amplitudes <=delta^4 and endpoint squared amplitudes
>=delta^2(1-delta)^2. For A={{0,1},{0,3}},

 pi_+(A)>=1-2delta^2/(1-delta)^2,
 pi_-(A)<=delta^2/(1-delta)^2,
 TV(pi_+,pi_-)>=1-3delta^2/(1-delta)^2 ->1.

The common perturbation cancels, so ||F_+-F_-||_2=delta exactly, and their
entrywise relative difference is at most delta. Since ||epsilon C||_2
<=epsilon 4*64<=delta^2, both matrices are within delta+delta^2 of F0.
Their condition numbers are bounded by
(3+delta+delta^2)/(1-delta-delta^2)<4.

Again, positive entries, unchanged full support, rank and conditioning
cannot replace the actual determinant/bit precision contract.

C. NATURAL #BIS HARDNESS ENCODING HAS A REAL-ROOTEDNESS BARRIER

This is a restriction on one proposed reduction format, not a general
AP-reduction impossibility theorem. The exact hard-BCS charge polynomial

 C_F(z)=sum_k c_k(F)z^k

has only negative real zeros, or is the constant one, by the baseline clone
proof. A short direct t=0 reconstruction suffices here. Put
H=[[0,2F],[2F*,0]], and retain exactly one chiral mode at each original site,
up at sites A and down elsewhere, obtaining H[R_A] of size n. Then

 Q(x)=2^(-n)sum_A det(xI-H[R_A])=x^n C_F(-1/x^2).

A balanced 2k chiral principal minor contributes
(-1)^k 2^(2k)|det F[I,J]|^2. It can be retained only if I and J are
disjoint, in which case the retaining probability is 2^(-2k). This proves
the identity coefficientwise. The multivariate characteristic determinant
det(diag(u,v)-H) is real stable: a strictly positive imaginary quadratic
form precludes a kernel. Apply product_i (partial_u_i+partial_v_i), divide
by 2^n and identify all remaining variables with x. Positive directional
derivatives preserve stability by one-variable Gauss-Lucas and boundary
specialization; the leading coefficient is one. Q is thus a real-rooted
real univariate polynomial. The displayed substitution, together with
C_F(0)=1 and positive coefficients, forces negative real zeros of C_F.
This is a reconstruction of an existing baseline theorem, not a new claim.

The bipartite star K_(1,3) has independent-set size polynomial

 I_star(z)=(1+z)^3+z=1+4z+3z^2+z^3.

Its cubic discriminant is
3^2*4^2-4*4^3-4*3^3-27+18*3*4=-31<0.
Thus it has two nonreal roots. No exact size-preserving positive-fugacity
encoding can satisfy C_F(z)=alpha I_star(beta z), alpha,beta>0. Nor can
adding a monomial padding factor or a nonzero polynomial dummy factor
remove these nonreal zeros. This blocks a naive direct coefficient-profile
embedding of #BIS into the hard-BCS charge law.

It does NOT exclude a fixed-sector embedding, an encoding whose size map is
nonlinear, signed interpolation, multiple oracle queries, approximation-
preserving transformations with other information, or #BIS hardness by a
different route. In particular it is not evidence that a generic FPRAS
exists. The attempted generic AP-hardness gate remains UNRESOLVED.
