## G1. Exact form interface

## G3. The inverse branch and anti-Pick function

Define B(f(x)²)=b(x). Direct differentiation on 0<s<1 gives

\[
-B'(s)=\tfrac12[1/s+R(s)],\qquad
R(s)=\frac{1-s}{s(B(s)-1)}.
\tag{G2}
\]

To extend B, parameterize s=(u/sin u)² and B=u cot u. Let p_*(q) be the unique point in (π/2,π) satisfying p tan p=−q tanh q. Its existence, uniqueness and continuity follow from strict monotonicity of p tan p on that interval. For D={p+iq:q>0,0<p<p_*(q)}, a(u)=sin u/u maps into the lower-right quadrant. I checked the two signs directly:

* Real positivity for p>π/2 is equivalent to p tan p<−q tanh q; for p≤π/2 it is immediate.
* Imaginary negativity for p<π/2 is tan p/p>tanh q/q; for p≥π/2 it is immediate.

All finite boundary pieces map to the coordinate axes, including the corners a(0)=1 and a(π)=0. Uniformly in p∈[0,π], |a(p+iq)|≥sinh q/√(π²+q²)→∞. Hence compact interior sets have compact inverse images. The strict sign

\[
\operatorname{Im}(u\cot u)
=\frac{q\sin2p-p\sinh2q}{\cosh2q-\cos2p}<0

excludes critical points of a, since a'=0 would imply u cot u=1. A proper local biholomorphism from the connected domain D onto the connected quadrant is a covering: its image is open/closed, fibers are finite, and compactness excludes extra nearby inverse points. The quadrant is simply connected, so there is one sheet. This establishes the claimed holomorphic inverse; univalence is proved rather than assumed from a drawing.

For Im s>0, s^(−1/2) is in that quadrant, so the inverse defines a holomorphic anti-Pick B (Im B<0). ## G4. Why the derivative really is Stieltjes

Apply the Herglotz representation to −B, whose representing measure is supported on (−∞,0] because it is real and analytic across the positive axis. Subtracting its value at 1 yields

\[
G(s)=\frac{B(s)-1}{1-s}
=\beta+\int_{(-\infty,0]}\frac{d\mu(r)}{(1-r)(s-r)},\quad\beta\ge0.
\]

Then H(s)=sG(s) is Pick: Im[s/(s−r)]≥0 for r≤0. It is positive on the positive real axis and has no zero in the upper half-plane; an interior zero would force its nonnegative imaginary harmonic part to vanish identically and hence make H a real constant, which cannot be zero. Thus R=1/H is anti-Pick, positive on the positive axis, and analytic across it.

Applying Herglotz to −R gives for s>1

\[
0\le R(1)-R(s)=\widetilde\beta(s-1)
+\int\left[(1-r)^{-1}-(s-r)^{-1}\right]d\widetilde\mu(r)
\le R(1).
\]

Positivity of R eliminates the affine slope; monotone convergence gives ∫(1−r)^(-1)dμ̃≤R(1). Rearrangement yields a nonnegative constant plus a positive Stieltjes integral for R. Continuing G2 therefore gives

\[
-B'(s)=c+\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{d\nu(r)}{s+r},
\quad c\ge0,\quad\int(1+r)^{-1}d\nu(r)<\infty.
\tag{G3}
\]

The explicit 1/(2s) in G2 contributes an atom of mass 1/2 at zero, ensuring strict positivity. This derivation is essential: anti-Pick B alone would not automatically make its derivative Stieltjes.
