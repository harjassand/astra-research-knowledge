# Quantum-free counting and sampling audit: general-graph matchings

## Verdict

The pinned release contains a strong classical counting mechanism: its item #113 manuscript claims an FPRAS for perfect matchings of every finite simple undirected graph, with certain zero detection and worst-case polynomial bit time. From that theorem, a feasible-on-every-run sampler within total variation distance \(\tau\) of uniform follows by a deletion self-reduction. The manuscript itself already proves that sampler, and already transfers it to fixed-cardinality matchings and prescribed-degree edge subsets. This is an internally reconstructed source-reported consequence, not a new result.

One important scope limit: the sampler is not exact uniform sampling. Its guarantee is \(d_{TV}\leq\tau\); the source explicitly says it does not claim pointwise almost-uniformity. Arbitrary input edge weights also are not supported. I therefore reject this as a distinct novelty target while preserving the fully charged classical corollary for the parent synthesis.

The manuscript's Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 1–2) takes an explicitly represented finite simple, unweighted, undirected graph \(G\) and rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), \(0<\delta<1/2\). It returns a nonnegative rational \(\widehat Z\) with
\[
  \Pr[(1-\varepsilon)Z(G)\leq\widehat Z\leq(1+\varepsilon)Z(G)]\geq1-\delta,
\]
where \(Z(G)\) is the number of perfect matchings. If \(Z(G)=0\), it returns zero with certainty. Its claimed worst-case bit time is polynomial in the full input length, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log\delta^{-1}\). I classify this as **source-reported / not independently proof-validated**: the pinned 45-page manuscript was read and its sampling corollary reconstructed below, but this subtask did not independently verify every lemma of the main FPRAS proof.

At a high level, the paper starts from the complete graph and suppresses forbidden edges by an activity schedule. Estimated two-hole partition ratios update positive vertex scales so row maxima stay balanced. A subdivision and a tree of weak clique bags turn these ratios into marked events under an auxiliary weighted matching law. For sampling that law, the paper builds a product chain with many copies at adjacent activity tiers. The two-coordinate energy estimate alone only controls additive pair functions; an ordered orthogonal decomposition and replication bound the pairwise residuals and yield a spectral gap for arbitrary functions of the product state (Lemma 7.2, pp. 30–31). The unit-activity bottom tier has an exact tree dynamic program and exact rational categorical sampling (Lemma 7.1, pp. 28–29). The marked-event estimates then recover the partition-function ratios (pp. 33–37), and Section 9 bounds rational bit lengths, random draws, and every-execution runtime (pp. 38–40). This is the manuscript's classical algorithmic mechanism; it uses no quantum data or quantum oracle.

## Load-bearing sampler lemma

The manuscript's Lemma 10.2 (PDF pp. 42–44) is the exact interface needed for a sampling corollary. Given an FPRAS for \(Z(H)\) on every simple graph \(H\), together with exact polynomial-time perfect-matching feasibility, it produces a sampler that is always supported on perfect matchings and is within \(\tau\) in total variation of the uniform law.

Here is the error argument, with the charged parameters. Let \(m_0=|E(H_0)|\), \(m_* = \max(1,m_0)\), and set
\[
  \eta=\gamma=\frac{\tau}{8m_*},\qquad
  t=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8m_*}{\tau}\right\rceil.
\]
At each step choose a fixed first residual edge \(e=uv\). The exclude child has count \(z_-=Z(H-e)\); the include child has count \(z_+=Z(H-u-v)\). Test child feasibility exactly. If only one child is feasible, take it. If both are feasible, make fresh FPRAS calls for \(z_+,z_-\), each with relative error \(\eta\) and failure probability \(\gamma\), obtaining \(A,B\). Use \(\widehat p=A/(A+B)\) and exactly \(t\) fair bits to choose the include branch with probability \(2^{-t}\lfloor 2^t\widehat p\rfloor\).

The ideal rule uses \(p=z_+/(z_++z_-)\). For successful estimates \(A=z_+(1+a)\), \(B=z_-(1+b)\), \(|a|,|b|\leq\eta\), direct algebra and \(z_+z_-\leq(z_++z_-)^2/4\) give
\[
  |\widehat p-p|\leq\frac{\eta}{2(1-\eta)}.
\]
The fixed-bit floor contributes at most \(2^{-t}\leq\eta\). The two estimate failures contribute at most \(2\gamma\) to the conditional branch-law TV error. Thus every branch contributes at most
\[
  \beta=\frac{\eta}{2(1-\eta)}+2\gamma+2^{-t}\leq4\eta,
\]
since \(\eta\leq1/8\). Each decision removes at least one edge, so at most \(m_0\) decisions occur. Coupling to the ideal process gives output TV at most \(m_0\beta\leq4m_*\eta=\tau/2<\tau\). In the ideal process, the exact branch ratios telescope along each root-to-leaf path to \(1/Z(H_0)\), proving uniformity of the comparison law.

Feasibility is independent of estimate quality: impossible branches are never selected, and when both are feasible either can be taken. Hence every run returns a perfect matching when one exists. The per-call failure guarantee applies conditionally at every adaptive history, so a union bound over at most \(2m_0\) count calls is valid. This is a genuine **worst-case bit-time** sampler: it makes at most \(2m_0\) FPRAS calls, at most \(2m_0+1\) exact feasibility tests, at most \(m_0\) branch choices, and uses exactly \(t\) fresh fair bits per non-forced decision. Arithmetic on estimates, ratios, and floors is exact. The FPRAS's every-execution bit-time bound also bounds output bit lengths on failed calls. Substituting \(\eta=\gamma=\tau/(8m_*)\) makes the total runtime polynomial in graph input length and \(\tau^{-1}\); its confidence overhead is logarithmic in \(m_*/\tau\). No rejection loop or random stopping time is used.

The \(\tau\) guarantee is a distributional TV guarantee, not a per-run success event. The output's feasibility is exact on every execution; its law is only approximately uniform. In particular, it does not establish exact sampling or pointwise relative error for each matching's probability.

## Distinct downstream target: fixed-cardinality matchings

For \(M_k(G)=\{M\subseteq E: M\text{ is a matching and }|M|=k\}\), the release manuscript's §10 already gives a constant-fibre reduction. Inputs are explicit simple unweighted graphs; absent edges are structural zeros. For \(0\leq2k\leq n=|V|\), add \(q=n-2k\) labeled dummy vertices, each adjacent to every original vertex, with no edges between dummies. Every perfect matching of this graph uses each dummy on a different original vertex and therefore uses exactly \(k\) original edges. Conversely, every \(k\)-matching lifts by assigning its \(q\) exposed vertices bijectively to the labeled dummies. The fibre size is the same \(C_k=q!\) for every target, so
\[
  Z(H_k)=q!\,|M_k(G)|.
\]
The gadget has at most \(2n\) vertices and at most \(m+n^2\) edges; \(\log(q!)=O(n\log(n+1))\). Binary-encoded invalid \(k\) values are rejected before construction. The counting algorithm applies Theorem 1.1 to \(H_k\) and divides by \(q!\) using exact rational arithmetic; the sample algorithm applies Lemma 10.2 and discards dummy-incident edges. Constant fibres make the projected exact uniform law uniform, and deterministic projection cannot increase TV. Thus counts have the same relative error / confidence / certain-zero guarantee, and sampling has TV at most \(\tau\), exact feasibility, and worst-case polynomial bit time.

The same paper gives the more general prescribed-degree (\(f\)-factor) reduction. It uses one tagged port per incidence and \(d_v-f_v\) labeled private dummies at vertex \(v\), with constant fibre \(C_f=\prod_v(d_v-f_v)!\). It validates \(0\leq f_v\leq d_v\) before gadget expansion; the resulting graph has at most \(4m\) vertices and \(m+2m(n-1)\) edges, and \(\log C_f=O(m\log(n+1))\). This is an unweighted structural-zero model, not an arbitrary weighted matching model.

## Matched baseline and novelty boundary

Jerrum, Valiant, and Vazirani's 1986 paper already proves that for self-reducible problems, randomized approximate counting and almost-uniform generation are inter-reducible. Perfect matchings have precisely the branch decomposition used above. Therefore the reduction from the new general-graph FPRAS to a TV sampler is a standard consequence of a new counting premise, not a new sampling principle.

The strongest adjacent baseline before this release is the Jerrum–Sinclair–Vigoda FPRAS for the permanent, which covers bipartite perfect matchings (including nonnegative edge weights) but not arbitrary nonbipartite simple graphs. Štefankovič, Vigoda, and Wilmes (2017) documented the then-open general-graph FPRAS question and proved torpid mixing for the JSV-style perfect/near-perfect chain on some nonbipartite graphs, while obtaining an FPRAS for graphs sufficiently close to bipartite. Against that baseline, the release's **general-graph counting theorem** is the substantive claim. Its TV-sampling and fixed-\(k\)/\(f\)-factor corollaries follow once that theorem is granted and are already included in the pinned manuscript.

No edge weights, compressed multiplicities, or quotient-by-isomorphism inputs are handled by the §10 corollaries. A weighted extension would need a separate theorem or a bit-efficient reduction preserving exact fibres; internal rational activities in the main algorithm do not by themselves supply that extension. The main theorem and its proof remain externally unreviewed here.

## Falsifier and next gate

The reconstructed sampler lemma would fail if any adaptive feasible residual could produce a branch-law discrepancy above \(\beta\), if a chosen branch could be infeasible, or if a path probability failed to telescope to \(1/Z\). The exact feasibility branching, ratio calculation, fixed-bit rounding bound, and path telescoping above rule out those failure modes conditional on Theorem 1.1. The novelty rejection would change only if a distinct consequence beyond §10 and the JVV reduction were found and proved under an explicitly stated input model. No such consequence is claimed here.
