## Exact polynomial parametrization and the original source condition

In the generic source field, put `w=y+1/x`, `v=1/x`, `c=F3`. The verified rational inverse is

`x=1/v`, `y=w−v`, `t=5v²−3wv−cv³`.

`rho(a)=−cw³+w²+wv`,
`rho(b)=−3cw²+4w+2v`,

The substitution(2) lands in C[w,v,c] even though its inverse to the original source is rational.

## Homogeneous leading map and its injectivity

Give v,c weight1 and w weight0. For an ordinary target-homogeneous polynomial P_D of degreeD, the highest weight part of rho(P_D) is

`rho0(P_D)`, where

`rho0(a)=wv−cw³`, `rho0(b)=2v−3cw²`, `rho0(c)=c`. (3)

Terms w² and4w in(2) have weight0. Hence, if P has ordinary total target degreeD and top homogeneous part P_D, its highest weight part after substitution is exactly rho0(P_D); terms of lower target degree cannot contribute weightD. The map(3) is dominant: its Jacobian in(w,v,c) is2v, a nonzero polynomial. Therefore rho0 is injective, so rho0(P_D) is nonzero for P_D!=0. This prevents accidental loss of the top degree.

If rho(P) has c-degree<=1, each of its weight-homogeneous parts also has c-degree<=1. In particular

`deg_c rho0(P_D)<=1`.                          (4)

This filtration argument is uniform in D, rather than a collection of bounded coefficient calculations.

## The irreducible direction cone

Set `R0=b³+27a²c`. It is irreducible in C[a,b,c]: as a polynomial in c it is linear and primitive, because27a² andb³ are coprime; Gauss then gives irreducibility. Hence(R0) is prime.

## No positive-degree homogeneous invariant of rho0

**Lemma.** If Q is a nonzero homogeneous target polynomial of degree m and rho0(Q) is independent of c, then m=0.

For contradiction suppose m>0. The polynomial rho0(Q) is homogeneous of weightm in v,c. Independence of c therefore makes it `v^m A(w)` for some polynomialA(w). Setting v=0 gives

`c^m Q(−w³,−3w²,1)=0`.

The cone lemma implies `Q=R0 Q1`, with nonzero Q1. Injectivity of rho0 ensures rho0(Q1)!=0. But(5) then gives

`deg_c rho0(Q)=1+deg_c rho0(Q1)>=1`,

contradicting c-independence. This proves the lemma for every degree, without recursion or a degree bound.

## All-degree degree bound and complete basis

Take a nonzero P in V with target degreeD and top homogeneous part P_D. If D>=2, condition(4) makes the coefficient of c^D in rho0(P_D) vanish. That coefficient is exactly

`P_D(−w³,−3w²,1)`.

The cone lemma gives `P_D=R0 Q` with Q nonzero homogeneous of degreeD−3. In particular D>=3, so degree2 is impossible. By(4) and(5),

`1+deg_c rho0(Q)=deg_c rho0(P_D)<=1`.

Thus rho0(Q) is c-independent, and the preceding lemma forces Q to be a scalar. Therefore D=3 and P_D is a scalar multiple of R0. No polynomial in V can have degree greater than3.

The corrected cubic R is genuinely in V. Direct substitution gives

`rho(R)=4(v−w)(2w+v)(w+2v)+9c w²(w²−v²)`.      (6)

Subtract the appropriate scalar multiple of R from P. Its target degree is then at most2, and it remains in V; degree2 was just excluded. The difference is affine. Conversely all affine target functions and R pull back affine in oldt. This proves(1) without relying on the cycle17 bounded matrix result.
