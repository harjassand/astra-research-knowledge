# Astra Meta-Genesis: global modulation calibrations and resonance-block assembly

Research date: 14 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane.

## Status and scope

The full implication from planar rank-one convexity to quasiconvexity is **not established here**. No compatible negative quasiconvexity gap is constructed.

This investigation proves a global Jensen inequality for an infinite-dimensional family of internally resonant gradients: arbitrary real scalar amplitudes multiplying a periodically rotating target vector, and more generally a convex-loop carrier. The main positive identity holds for every C² rank-one-convex integrand on R^(m x n), without homogeneity or rotational symmetry. For planar left-SO(2)-invariant quartics it yields an explicit fourth-order coercivity bound, uniform over all affine backgrounds. Neither a near-line condition nor a bounded-background condition is imposed.

A second theorem gives an exact assembly rule, after complete affine optimization, for Fourier packets independent through degree four. Its condition preserves all internal packet resonances. A separate compatible example proves that unrestricted assembly fails: even a polyconvex energy has a strictly negative interaction increment. A variable-phase identity identifies the signed term absent from the successful modulation theorem.

These statements have analytic proofs below. `verify_global_compatibility.py` checks nine groups of finite algebraic identities and exact examples. It does not certify the analytic proofs, establish historical priority, or formally verify them in a proof assistant. The earlier tangent verifier was replayed (12 groups passed). The new main theorem does **not** use the earlier contact classification, Hodge estimate, or near-line theorem as premises.

The techniques used here—rank-one supporting inequalities, integration by parts, convexity, Fourier moment matching, and quadratic minimization—are established mathematical tools. Their use here supplies the stated constructions; no claim of historical invention of those tools, or of priority for the complete combination, is made.

## 1. Audit and change of representation

The preceding near-line theorem concerns a compatible gradient X satisfying two relative smallness conditions about a *single fixed matrix line*. Its proof relies on a covariance-fitted line, a weighted positive Schur expansion, and an L²/L⁴ mixed-derivative estimate. The replayed symbolic checks verify its contact algebra and weighted polynomial coefficients, not its analytic interpolation, uniform-remainder argument, or exhaustion of all contact types.

A spatial partition by the locally dominant matrix direction does not preserve gradients: if X=Dphi, then cutting it by an indicator generally creates curl at the interfaces. Likewise, the constant background selected by complete optimization cannot be minimized independently on arbitrary spatial pieces without accounting for interfaces and cross terms. Critical concentration does not remove this difficulty.

The new route does not attempt such a partition. It builds a *potential-level family* for which a rank-one tangent remainder and a positive acoustic integral exactly represent the global gap. It then investigates which different families may be superposed without changing the sign of the fully optimized quartic interaction.

Throughout, matrix inner products are Frobenius. Periodic cells carry normalized Lebesgue measure when expectations are used. Integral identities are valid with normalized or unnormalized measure.

## 2. A global convex-loop modulation theorem

Let F:R^(m x n)->R be C² and rank-one convex. Let V:R->R^m be a C² periodic curve satisfying

    V''(theta) = -a(theta) V(theta),     a(theta)>=0.          (2.1)

The circle V(theta)=(cos theta,sin theta) and every fixed linear image of it have a=1. The curve is allowed to lie in a two-dimensional subspace of a larger target.

Let k be a constant vector. In a periodic problem require theta(x)=k.x modulo the period of V to be well defined on the spatial torus. On the standard 2pi torus with V of period 2pi, it suffices that k is an integer vector. Let r be an arbitrary smooth, real, periodic scalar function of **all n spatial variables**. Define

    phi(x)=r(x)V(k.x).                                      (2.2)

No sign, smallness, frequency, or separation condition is placed on r.

### Theorem 2.1: an exact positive integral representation

For every constant B in R^(m x n),

    integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] >= 0.                           (2.3)

More precisely, put theta=k.x, W=V',

    P=r W tensor k,     Q=V tensor grad r,

and define the rank-one Bregman remainder

    R_F(C,Q)=F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C)[Q].

Then

    integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
      = integral R_F(B+P,Q)
        + integral a(theta) r^2 integral_0^1
            t D²F(B+tP)[V tensor k,V tensor k] dt.            (2.4)

Both integrands on the right are nonnegative. In particular, this is a positive construction of the original variational gap, not an assumed moment completion.

### Proof

The gradient identity Dphi=P+Q follows by the chain rule. Q is rank one at every point. Rank-one convexity implies R_F(B+P,Q)>=0.

On the variables (theta,r), define the vector-valued primitive with components

    A_i(theta,r)=integral_0^r
        DF(B+sW(theta) tensor k)[V(theta) tensor e_i] ds.     (2.5)

Differentiating its pullback to the spatial domain gives

    div A(theta(x),r(x))
      = DF(B+P)[Q]
        + sum_i k_i integral_0^r {
            D²F(B+sW tensor k)[sW' tensor k,V tensor e_i]
            + DF(B+sW tensor k)[V' tensor e_i]} ds.

Use W'=-aV and V'=W. The second part of the integral is the derivative of F(B+sW tensor k) in s. Therefore

    div A = DF(B+P)[Q] + F(B+P)-F(B)
      - a(theta) integral_0^r
           s D²F(B+sW tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k] ds.    (2.6)

The periodic divergence has zero mean. The last integral equals

    r² integral_0^1 t D²F(B+trW tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k] dt,

including when r is negative. Its integrand is nonnegative by rank-one convexity. Substitution into the Bregman identity proves (2.4).

The proof works in any input and target dimension. It uses the actual potential, not the distribution of its values or determinant moments. QED.

### Boundary, regularity and geometric extensions

If r is smooth with zero boundary trace on a bounded domain, the primitive (2.5) vanishes on its boundary, so the same proof holds. For quartic F, approximation extends the theorem to periodic W^(1,4) amplitudes, or r in W_0^(1,4) on a bounded Lipschitz domain: multiplication by the smooth bounded carrier preserves W^(1,4), and quartic integrals are continuous under strong W^(1,4) convergence. The primitive has at most fourth-order growth, so the positive representation also passes by approximation. For a general C² integrand, the smooth or bounded-gradient version is asserted without an unstated growth hypothesis.

Every smooth strictly convex closed planar curve with positive curvature and containing the origin can, be put in form (2.1) by parametrizing at constant areal speed. Indeed, start with positive orientation and choose theta so that det(V,V') is a positive constant. Differentiation gives det(V,V'')=0, hence V''=-aV. Positive curvature gives det(V',V'')=a det(V,V')>0, hence a>0. This proves the geometric assertion rather than assuming an external curve classification. The circle or ellipse suffices for all the applications below.

For bounded gradients, convolution of any continuous rank-one-convex test integrand with a smooth approximate identity preserves rank-one convexity. Passing to the limit in (2.3) extends Jensen to all such tests. In the usual terminology, these compactly supported gradient distributions are laminates. The Jensen property is proved here, not assumed from their being gradients.

### Relation to older constructions

Astala, Iwaniec, Prause and Saksman, Proposition 5.1 of [1], prove Jensen-type bounds for radially linear maps f(x)=Lambda(|x|)x and every continuous rank-one-concave integrand. Their proof also uses a rank-one tangent inequality followed by integration. The present amplitude r depends arbitrarily on all spatial coordinates and multiplies a prescribed loop V(k.x), rather than being a matrix function of radius. This identifies a different sufficient class, but is not a complete priority comparison with all laminate constructions in the literature.

## 3. Uniform quartic coercivity in the complete invariant cone

Let F:R^(2x2)->R be a left-SO(2)-invariant rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic. Define

    mu_F=min {F(R): rank R=1, |R|=1}.                        (3.1)

This is nonnegative: the restriction t->F(tR)=t^4F(R) is convex. Let

    V(theta)=(cos theta,sin theta),     phi=r(x)V(k.x).

### Theorem 3.1

For every constant matrix B,

    integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
        >= (mu_F/12) integral |Dphi|^4.                     (3.2)

Consequently the same bound holds after minimizing over *all* B. It imposes no smallness on the amplitude, no near-line assumption, and no boundedness condition on the affine optimizer.

### Proof: two scalar completions

First consider the polynomial f(t)=F(C+tQ), with rank Q<=1. Write

    f(t)=A t^4+L t^3+C2 t²+D t+E,    A=F(Q)>=0.

Its second derivative is nonnegative for all real t. For A>0 this implies C2>=3L²/(8A). Thus

    f(1)-f(0)-f'(0)-A/3
      = 3(L+4A/3)^2/(8A) + C2-3L²/(8A) >=0.                (3.3)

If A=0, global nonnegativity of f'' forces L=0 and C2>=0, and the same inequality follows. Hence the first term in (2.4) is at least F(Q)/3, or mu_F |grad r|^4/3.

For the second term, let

    g(s)=D²F(B+sW tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k]
         =A2 s²+L2 s+C2'.

This is a nonnegative quadratic. Output rotational invariance and orthonormality of V,W give

    A2=D²F(W tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k]
       =4F(W tensor k).

For A2>0, C2'>=L2²/(4A2), and

    integral_0^r s g(s) ds - A2 r^4/36
      = r²(L2+4A2 r/3)^2/(8A2)
        + (r²/2)(C2'-L2²/(4A2)) >=0.                        (3.4)

The degenerate case A2=0 is again immediate. The second term of (2.4) is therefore at least mu_F r^4|k|^4/9.

Finally V and W are orthonormal, so

    |Dphi|²=|grad r|²+r²|k|²,

and for nonnegative x,y,

    x²/3+y²/9 >= (x+y)²/12.

Equations (2.4), (3.3), (3.4) prove (3.2). The case k=0 uses only the first term and is included. QED.

The constant is independent of k and of the amplitude. A critically concentrated zero-boundary template phi_rho(x)=rho^(1/2)phi(x/rho) remains in the class, with amplitude rho^(1/2)r(x/rho) and carrier k/rho. In two dimensions its fourth gradient integral is unchanged, so (3.2) controls this concentration without an equiintegrability assumption. This does not express arbitrary concentrated profiles in the template.

No claim of optimality is made for 1/12. Nonnegative Jensen holds even when mu_F=0. For the non-polyconvex invariant quartics considered in the earlier program, positivity on nonzero rank-one matrices gives mu_F>0. The coercive theorem itself states mu_F explicitly and does not need the long contact classification to be applicable.

### A field far from every rank-one matrix line

On the standard two-dimensional torus take

    r(x,y)=1+(1/2)cos x,
    phi=r(x,y)(cos y,sin y).                                (3.5)

Its covariance in row-major matrix coordinates is exactly

    C=diag(1/16,9/16,1/16,9/16).

The maximum variance captured by a unit rank-one direction is 9/16 (attained by e12), while the total is 20/16. Thus no rank-one line captures more than 9/20 of its second moment. The smallest possible relative transverse L² ratio is sqrt(11/9)>1. This is a globally multidirectional field, not a near-line perturbation in disguised coordinates.

For the prior exactly certified integrand

    G=3(u+v-2q)^2+(35/24)u²+q²-(5/2)qu,                     (3.6)

mu_G=3. The verifier independently reconstructs its global positive acoustic Gram. For (3.5), b_G=0,

    E|Dphi|^4=33/16,
    Delta_G(Dphi)=8139/1024.

A second explicit example uses the genuinely two-dimensional amplitude

    r=1+(1/3)cos x+(1/4)cos y+(1/5)cos(x+y),
    phi=r(cos y,sin y).                                     (3.7)

It has exact mean gradient and determinant zero, positive-definite covariance and H, and nonzero cubic vector b. Its support has six frequency classes (0,1), (0,2), (1,-1), (1,0), (1,1), (1,2), forming one connected component through 12 primitive triad and 18 primitive quartet relations (unordered signed tuples, including conjugate tuples). These exact combinatorial counts are checked in the verifier. Thus its internal resonances are not removed by the packet decomposition of Section 5. Complete rational affine minimization gives

    E|Dphi|^4=27691873/12960000,
    Delta_G(Dphi)=3646241010554033/507189555936000.

The latter is approximately 7.189109018274296. Its significance is not a small numerical gap, but an exact internally resonant, full-rank example in a class proved positive for every amplitude and every affine background. The theorem is not obtained by these finite tests.

## 4. A companion global class: complex one-coordinate envelopes

A related direct averaging argument covers a different class, useful for testing the limits of the numerical representation.

Let z(x) be a complex-valued periodic W^(1,4) function of one coordinate, let n be a nonzero integer, and identify

    phi(x,y)=z(x)exp(i n y)

with a real two-component potential. For an invariant quartic as in Section 3,

    inf_B E[F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] >= (mu_F/24) E|Dphi|^4.          (4.1)

Proof. Begin with smooth z having no zeros and write z=r exp(i vartheta), r>0. Remove the output rotation by invariance. The gradient becomes

    r [[r'/r,0],[vartheta',n]].

Let M=integral r^4 dx and average using the probability weight r^4/M. The weighted mean of r'/r is zero. Put tbar=integral r^4 vartheta'/M. Convexity in the first column and the rank-one Bregman estimate (3.3) give

    EF(Dphi) >= mu_F M(tbar²+n²)²
      +(mu_F/3) integral r^4 |(r'/r,vartheta')-(0,tbar)|^4.

The fourth power of the full gradient norm is at most eight times the sum of the two fourth-order expressions in this display (without coefficients), by |U+V|^4<=8(|U|^4+|V|^4). This gives the factor 1/24.

Averaging over y makes b=E DF(Dphi)=0: output rotation covariance of DF gives a full circle average of a vector. Also H>=0 by reciprocity and the Fourier/Legendre-Hadamard argument in Section 5. Thus every affine gap is at least EF(Dphi), proving (4.1).

For zeros of z, approximate by smooth curves with a small constant complex translation avoiding zero. A smooth curve has zero planar area, so such translations exist arbitrarily close to zero. Strong W^(1,4) continuity completes the proof. No winding-number restriction on z is imposed. QED.

This covers arbitrary complex envelopes of one coordinate, whereas Section 3 permits arbitrary real amplitudes of both coordinates. Neither theorem asserts positivity for general sums of differently phased carriers.

## 5. Exact quartic assembly after all affine directions are optimized

This section does not assume that spatially overlapping fields are independent merely because they have different names. A precise degree-four condition is required.

### 5.1 Moment-level assembly identity

Let X_1,...,X_N be centered random matrices with finite fourth moments. Suppose all their joint polynomial moments of total degree at most four agree with those of independent variables with the same marginal laws. Define

    C_i=E X_i X_i^T, H_i=E D²F(X_i), b_i=E DF(X_i), c_i=E F(X_i).

For any homogeneous quartic F, polarization gives

    H_sum=sum_i H_i,        b_sum=sum_i b_i,
    c_sum=sum_i c_i + (1/2)sum_(i<j) tr(H_i C_j).             (5.1)

The expression is symmetric because tr(H_i C_j)=tr(H_j C_i). These are exact polynomial identities; they do not require a gradient hypothesis.

If F is rank-one convex and each X_i is a compatible periodic gradient, then H_i>=0 in every matrix dimension. Indeed,

    A:H_i A=E D²F(A)[X_i,X_i]

by quartic reciprocity. Parseval splits the right side into quadratic forms at rank-one real and imaginary Fourier coefficients. Each is nonnegative by rank-one convexity. Approximation covers W^(1,4). This proof does not import a higher-dimensional determinant S-lemma.

Suppose the individual affine quadratic problems have finite attained minima. Put

    B_i=-H_i^dagger b_i,   B_* = -(sum H_i)^dagger(sum b_i),
    Delta_i=c_i-(1/2)b_i^T H_i^dagger b_i.

The range conditions are automatic if each block already satisfies all-affine Jensen, since otherwise its affine quadratic would be unbounded below. They are also automatic when H_i is positive definite. Then

    Delta(sum_i X_i)
      = sum_i Delta_i + (1/2)sum_(i<j) tr(H_i C_j)
        + (1/2)sum_i (B_i-B_*)^T H_i(B_i-B_*).               (5.2)

Proof. Write each individual affine gap as

    Delta_i+(1/2)(B-B_i)^T H_i(B-B_i),

sum these expressions and use (5.1). Minimization gives (5.2). Every additional term is nonnegative when H_i>=0. This is elementary quadratic algebra; its role here is preserving the *complete optimized* interaction, rather than choosing one background direction prematurely.

Consequently positive blocks cannot become a negative profile under degree-four-independent assembly. This is spatially overlapping assembly, different from the earlier disjoint-support inequality.

### 5.2 Deterministic compatible realization: resonance blocks

Let each X_i=Dphi_i be a finite Fourier gradient. Partition its nonzero frequency support into packets. A signed zero-sum tuple of frequencies is called primitive if it has no proper nonempty zero-sum subtuple; multiplicities are allowed. Treat k and -k as one frequency class.

Assume every primitive zero-sum tuple of length at most four lies wholly inside one packet. Then all joint polynomial moments through degree four factor across packets.

Proof. Expand a monomial of total degree at most four into Fourier characters. A term survives spatial averaging precisely when its frequencies sum to zero. Decompose that tuple into primitive zero-sum subtuples. By assumption each such subtuple lies in one packet; hence each packet's total frequency sum is separately zero. The surviving terms are exactly those from independent phase averaging. Conversely, separately zero packet sums always survive. QED.

No restriction is placed on the number or complexity of internal resonances. Applying (5.2) gives global Jensen for arbitrary finite assemblies of packets from Sections 2–4 satisfying this cross-packet condition.

Such assemblies exist exactly, not just asymptotically. Let phi_i have maximum frequency infinity norm K_i. Replace

    X_i(x) by X_i(N_i x)=D[phi_i(N_i x)/N_i],

where N_1=1 and successive positive integers satisfy

    N_j > 4 sum_(i<j) N_i K_i.                              (5.3)

In any relation of length at most four, a nonzero net frequency from the largest-index packet has norm at least N_j, exceeding all lower-packet contributions. Thus it must vanish; induction treats the remaining packets. This retains each packet's internal law and internal resonances exactly. It changes the configuration rather than pretending to preserve the energy of an arbitrary pre-existing field.

### 5.3 A canonical obstruction reduction

For any finite Fourier potential, form the hypergraph whose vertices are frequency classes and whose hyperedges are primitive zero-sum tuples of length three or four. Its connected components satisfy the condition above. Each component is still a compatible gradient, since keeping Fourier modes of a potential preserves curl compatibility.

Therefore, if a finite Fourier gradient has a negative gap for a rank-one-convex quartic, at least one of these connected resonance components has a negative all-affine gap. To see this without assuming every individual minimum is attained, use the fixed-background form of (5.1): if every component were nonnegative for every B, their sum plus the nonnegative covariance interactions would be nonnegative for every B.

This reduces a counterexample to a primitive-resonance-connected component. It does not prove positivity inside such a component; components can be very large. A single finite Fourier counterexample would still suffice, since a smooth negative-gap potential can be approximated in W^(1,4) by finite Fourier sums at a fixed offending background.

## 6. A compatible obstruction to unrestricted assembly

The cross-packet condition cannot be removed. Even the polyconvex quartic F(A)=(det A)^2 has negative interaction increments.

Let

    r(x)=1+a cos x,     s(x)=1-a cos x,
    phi_1=r(x)(cos y,sin y),
    phi_2=s(x)(cos 2y,sin 2y).

Both are in the class of Theorem 2.1. For their sum,

    det D(phi_1+phi_2)=r r'+2s s'+(2r's+r s')cos y.

Exact averaging gives

    E(det D(phi_1+phi_2))²
       -E(det Dphi_1)²-E(det Dphi_2)²
        =-(7/4)a²+(17/16)a^4.                               (6.1)

At a=1/2 the individual values and the total are

    c_1=17/128,    c_2=17/32,    c_sum=75/256,
    c_sum-c_1-c_2=-95/256.                                  (6.2)

Each individual cubic vector vanishes by averaging its output rotation, and H_i>=0, so Delta_i=c_i. The total optimized value is at most its zero-background value c_sum, strictly smaller than Delta_1+Delta_2. It is nonetheless nonnegative, because determinant-square Jensen proves quasiconvexity directly.

This is an actual compatible obstruction to a proposed decomposition/assembly inequality, **not** a Morrey counterexample. It shows that even individually globally positive resonant packets cannot be assembled by simply adding their optimized positive margins.

## 7. Variable phase: the exact missing signed term

The successful operation fixes an affine phase. It is important to show, rather than conceal, what changes for an arbitrary phase.

Let theta(x) be smooth and let k(x)=grad theta. Keep phi=rV(theta), W=V', V''=-aV, and now set P=rW tensor k, Q=V tensor grad r. Define the same primitive as in (2.5), but with k as an additional variable. Differentiating it gives the extra term

    E_F(x)=sum_i integral_0^r s
      D²F(B+sW tensor k)[W tensor partial_i k,V tensor e_i] ds. (7.1)

The exact identity becomes

    integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
      = integral R_F(B+P,Q)
        + integral a(theta) integral_0^r s
            D²F(B+sW tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k] ds
        - integral E_F.                                    (7.2)

The first two terms remain nonnegative. The last involves two different rank-one directions in a mixed Hessian pairing; rank-one convexity does not sign it. It vanishes for affine theta, yielding Theorem 2.1.

At B=0 in the planar left-invariant cone, the expression is particularly explicit. Write the orientation-odd part as

    q(Lu+Mv+Nw),     M_2(k)=Lk_1²+Mk_2²+Nk_1k_2,
    Xi(k)=M_2(k)(k_2,-k_1).

All reflection-even terms have zero first derivative in the orthogonal output direction at W tensor k. Direct differentiation gives

    E_F = (r^4/4) div Xi(grad theta).                        (7.3)

Thus phase curvature and amplitude are correlated in precisely the signed term that the affine-phase identity avoids.

The sign failure occurs even for an explicitly convex invariant quartic. Take

    F_c(A)=(u+v-2q)^2=|x_complex+i y_complex|^4,
    theta(x,y)=x+sin y,
    r(y)^4=1-(1/2)sin(2y).

This is a smooth periodic potential, since V is 2pi-periodic in theta. Here L=M=-4,N=0, and exact normalized averaging gives

    E E_(F_c)=1/4>0.                                       (7.4)

Changing the sign of the sine in r^4 changes the sign of this mean. Thus discarding the signed term, or asserting it nonpositive, is invalid even for convex F. The total Jensen gap is still nonnegative in this example; (7.4) is not a counterexample to quasiconvexity.

A nonlinear phase-straightening diffeomorphism would introduce variable input coefficients and their correlation with the amplitude. As with the prior shear conjugacy, no unrestricted reduction follows merely from changing coordinates. A global theorem would need to control (7.1) together with the positive terms, not rename it or average its coefficients independently.

## 8. Changed compatible counterexample search

The numerical representation was changed to complex carrier-envelope potentials

    phi(x,y)=sum_(n in N) z_n(x) exp(i n y),

where each z_n is a continuous periodic piecewise-linear complex envelope. This preserves genuine gradient compatibility. Uniform and strongly nonuniform x meshes allow localized gradients; multiple carriers produce interactions not covered by the one-carrier theorems. A constant invertible input transformation permits deformed periodic cells.

On each x interval the gradient is linear in the affine local coordinate. Three-point Gauss integration therefore integrates the quartic exactly at the representation level. The y grid has more than four times the largest carrier magnitude, avoiding Fourier aliasing for all moments used. Floating arithmetic and optimization errors remain.

The exactly certified G in (3.6) was used throughout. E|Dphi|^4 was normalized to one. All four affine directions were eliminated; regularization was used during the solve, while retained states with a positive Hessian were reevaluated using the unregularized quadratic form.

## 9. What is now established and what is not

The new main result reaches beyond rank-one neighborhoods: arbitrary scalar amplitudes depending on all spatial coordinates, including internally resonant and concentrating amplitudes, admit a positive integral representation. Its quartic corollary controls every affine background uniformly. Explicit full-rank fields demonstrate that it is not a consequence of small transverse mass.

The assembly theorem preserves complete quartic optimization for exactly specified resonance-separated packets. It admits arbitrarily many overlapping packets, all with their internal nonlinear correlations retained. Its canonical resonance-component reduction restricts where a counterexample must occur. It is not a decomposition theorem for all gradients into proved-positive pieces.

Two exact compatible tests mark the obstruction: cross-packet energy can decrease even for determinant square, and the variable-phase curvature correction can have either sign even for a convex quartic. Neither gives a negative quasiconvexity gap. They prohibit two tempting but invalid global assemblies.

The complete nine-coefficient implication remains unresolved in this investigation. The missing operation would control connected cross-packet resonances, or the signed phase-curvature term and its positive Bregman/acoustic budget, without discarding their correlation. Arbitrary planar potentials cannot be assumed to have affine phase or a resonance-separated positive decomposition. Zeros and winding add further issues to any attempt to use a global polar representation.

This direction now has an analytic global exclusion rather than only contact asymptotics. Repeating contact classification or one-carrier optimization is not productive. Concentrated effort is warranted only if directed at the signed cross-carrier interaction or an independently checkable compatible negative profile. No compute-calibrated expected probability of success is asserted.
