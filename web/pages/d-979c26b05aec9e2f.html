1. Primary source and applicable theorem

Anari, Huang, Liu, Vuong, Xu, and Yu, “Parallel Discrete Sampling via Continuous Walks,” STOC 2023, pp. 103–116, DOI 10.1145/3564246.3585207 (full paper copy in the NSF Public Access Repository; URL in SOURCES.json). The paper's Theorem 1 (full-paper p. 3) gives polylogarithmic parallel sampling from a Boolean-cube law if (i) its external-field tilts are W1-O(1)ℓ1 transport-stable and (ii) there is an approximate Laplace-transform oracle. Theorem 3 (p. 4) instead assumes O(1) semi-log-concavity plus an exact Laplace-transform oracle and permits n^{O(log n)} processors. Corollary 2 gives its polylogarithmic-time sampler for DPPs and partition-constrained DPPs with O(1) parts. The paper notes that generic laws have transport parameter O(n), yielding the quasi-polynomial processor loss. Its existence theorem for O(1)-site local chains requires W∞-O(1)ℓ0 transport stability (Theorem 36, p. 24).

These conditions do not automatically transfer to the site-pair parity-volume law. On a doubled-frame DPP, the event is “each site contributes either zero or both columns,” with a fixed total number of pairs. This is not an exact-count-per-partition-cell constraint: it has one local 0-or-2 constraint per site. Writing it as a mixture of fixed occupancy patterns gives a number of parts growing with n, outside Corollary 2's O(1)-part hypothesis.

2. Exact constant-term identity and a randomized-determinant failure

Let V be any n-by-2n frame, L=V*V, and let sites pair columns (e_i,f_i). For positive site activities y_i, define

  Z_k(y) = sum_{S subset [n], |S|=k} det L[P(S)] prod_{i in S} y_i,

where P(S) selects both columns at every i in S. Set

  D(theta,y) = diag(sqrt(y_1)e^{i theta_1}, sqrt(y_1)e^{-i theta_1}, ..., sqrt(y_n)e^{i theta_n}, sqrt(y_n)e^{-i theta_n}),
  X(theta,y) = [z^{2k}] det(I_n + z V D(theta,y) V*).

The Cauchy–Binet / principal-minor expansion is

  det(I + z V D V*) = sum_{T subset [2n]} z^{|T|} det L[T,T] prod_{j in T} D_jj.

For each site i, the phase exponent in a term is 1_{e_i in T}-1_{f_i in T}. Averaging independent uniform phases kills every term with a nonzero exponent. Since a site has only two columns, zero exponent means selecting either both columns or neither. The remaining degree-2k terms are exactly the target pair terms, and each selected pair contributes y_i. Thus E_theta X(theta,y)=Z_k(y). This is a valid unbiased determinant-projection estimator for the partition function / Laplace transform numerator.

Now take n=2m and F=direct_sum_{j=1}^m [[0,1],[1,0]], with V=[I,F^T], and k=m. In a 2-site block, the two site-pair choices each have Gram determinant 1, while selecting both sites duplicates columns and has determinant 0. Therefore the valid global supports select exactly one site from every block, each with weight 1, and Z_m(1)=2^m. The target law is simply a product of m fair binary choices.

For one block, write t_i=e^{i theta_i} and u=t_1 t_2. Directly,

  det(I_2 + z V_block D_block V_block*)
    = (1+z(t_1+t_2^{-1}))(1+z(t_2+t_1^{-1})),
  X_block = [z^2](...) = 2+u+u^{-1}.

Since u is uniform on the unit circle, E X_block=2 and E|X_block|^2=6. The blocks are independent and the full z^{2m} coefficient is their product. Consequently

  E X = 2^m,   E|X|^2 = 6^m,
  E|X|^2/(E X)^2 = (3/2)^m,
  Var(X)/(E X)^2 = (3/2)^m-1.

For N independent phase samples, the relative mean-square error of their average is exactly (((3/2)^m)-1)/N. Thus this direct unbiased phase-averaging route needs exponentially many samples for fixed relative RMS error on a linear-bit input family, despite the target itself being trivial to sample. It is a failure of this estimator, not an impossibility result for randomized determinants generally. With activities y_1,y_2 in one block, the estimator factor is y_1+y_2+sqrt(y_1 y_2)(u+u^{-1}); its relative second moment is 1+2y_1y_2/(y_1+y_2)^2, maximized at balanced fields. Exponential growth therefore persists at the balanced target tilt.

This is an idealized lower bound on sample count: it grants exact determinant evaluation and perfect continuous phases, so it omits bit-precision, random-bit, and determinant-computation costs. Those costs cannot improve the stated variance of this estimator.

3. Exact sensitivity obstruction to the localization theorem

Use the cyclic-shift instance from INITIAL, with n=2q and target law uniform on the two alternating site sets. Encode a site set S as x(S) in {±1}^n, with +1 on S and -1 off S. The two supported vectors are x and -x and their Hamming distance is n.

For any coordinate i and t>0, tilt the uniform law by w'=t e_i. The two mode probabilities become e^{t x_i}/(2 cosh t) and e^{-t x_i}/(2 cosh t). The total mass moved between the two modes is (1/2)tanh(t); every unit of that mass travels Hamming distance n. Hence

  W1(mu, tau_{t e_i} mu) = (n/2)tanh(t),    ||t e_i||_1=t,

so the best W1-ℓ1 transport-stability parameter is at least (n/2)tanh(t)/t, tending to n/2 as t→0. The O(1) hypothesis of Theorem 1 fails on this family.

For the semi-log-concavity condition, take w'=s x/n. Then ||w'||_2=s/sqrt(n), while the tilted mean is tanh(s)x. Therefore

  ||mean(tau_{w'}mu)-mean(mu)||_2 / ||w'||_2
    = n tanh(s)/s → n as s→0.

The O(1) hypothesis of Theorem 3 also fails. The theorem's generic O(n) stability fallback allows quasi-polynomially many processors only if there is an exact Laplace-transform oracle; that oracle is the very pair-volume partition function at arbitrary fields, not supplied by the ordinary determinant. For this family the oracle is easy because the support has two modes, but that does not furnish an oracle for arbitrary F.

The same support geometry rules out any irreducible stationary Markov chain whose transitions change fewer than n coordinates: stationarity prohibits transitions to zero-mass states, and the only two positive-mass states are distance n apart. Thus the paper's constant-site local-chain corollary cannot transfer either.

4. Strengthened exact stability-polynomial fact

For p_q(z)=prod_{i even} z_i + prod_{i odd} z_i (the cyclic support polynomial, with q sites in each parity class), its exact fractional-log-concavity threshold is alpha=1/q. Let A=prod_even z_i^alpha, B=prod_odd z_i^alpha and r=A/(A+B). The Hessian of log(A+B) is

  -D + alpha^2 r(1-r) v v^T,

where D_ii=alpha r/z_i^2 on even sites, D_ii=alpha(1-r)/z_i^2 on odd sites, and v_i=+1/z_i on even, -1/z_i on odd. Since

  v^T D^{-1}v = q/(alpha r(1-r)),

the rank-one PSD criterion says this Hessian is negative semidefinite exactly when alpha q<=1. Therefore p_q(z^alpha) is log-concave on the positive orthant iff alpha<=1/q; the previous INITIAL Hessian bound is tight globally, rather than merely necessary at the all-ones point. (The theorem transfer still fails because this parameter decays with q.)

5. Decision and next gate

The outward transfer has been tested at the exact algorithmic interfaces: stochastic localization lacks uniform stability on an explicit valid target family; a natural randomized determinant constant-term oracle has exact exponential relative variance on a different valid family. Neither rules out a pair-aware localization process, a structured Laplace oracle, importance sampling, or a different estimator. The next decisive gate is a computable relative-accurate oracle for Z_k(y) on arbitrary rational F, or a new pair-aware stability theorem that avoids the two-mode sensitivity witness. No such oracle/theorem is supplied here; the original sampler remains open.
