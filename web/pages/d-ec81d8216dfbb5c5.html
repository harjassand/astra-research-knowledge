# Spectral threshold repetition in the commuting-operator model

Status: COMPLETE_INTERNAL_DERIVATION with an independently reconstructed
operator-transport gate by frontier_quantum_bridge. No external validation,
formal verification, or historical novelty certificate is claimed.
This changes the source's strategy interface; it is not an automatic
corollary obtained by identifying commuting and finite tensor models.

## Question, assumptions and theorem

Let G be a finite two-player one-round game, with classical finite question
sets X,Y, finite nonempty answer sets A,B, known law mu on X times Y and
Boolean acceptance V. A commuting strategy consists of a Hilbert space H,
a unit vector psi, and POVMs M_x^a,N_y^b on H with

    [M_x^a,N_y^b]=0 for EVERY x,y,a,b.

H may be infinite dimensional; it need not carry a tensor factorization
or a trace. Define omega^co(G) as the supremum of
sum mu(x,y)V(x,y,a,b)<psi,M_x^a N_y^b psi> over these strategies.
The corresponding repeated strategy has arbitrary joint local POVMs for
the complete question/answer strings, subject to the same cross
commutation condition.

On the positive-mass pair graph H_mu, adjacent pairs share a local input.
For every non-singleton component j put mu_j=mu(.|j),
P_j=(P_X+P_Y)/2 on L2(mu_j), and

    sigma_j=gap(I-P_j on the orthogonal complement of constants).

Take sigma=min_j sigma_j, with sigma=1 if all components are singletons.
Connectedness and finiteness ensure sigma>0. All logarithms are natural.

**Internally derived theorem.** For v=omega^co(G)<1, 0<delta<1-v,
k>=1, and every commuting strategy for the independent k-fold questions,

    Pr[W_k >= ceil((v+delta)k)]
       <= exp[-sigma delta^3 k/(2048(1+log(|A||B|)))].       (T)

The bound passes to the supremum. If the support is connected, 2048 can
be replaced by 1024. Taking delta upward to 1-v at fixed k gives the
all-wins consequence

    omega^co(G^tensor k)
       <= exp[-sigma(1-v)^3 k/(2048(1+log(|A||B|)))].       (A)

In particular, since every finite question law has finitely many connected
components with positive gaps, this yields qualitative exponential
threshold and all-wins repetition for every finite game with commuting
value below one. The rate depends on its given question law; this is not
a universal gap-free cubic rate or an efficient gap-acquisition theorem.

## 1. Resolvent maps on an arbitrary Hilbert space

For bounded T>=0 define F_T:H -> L2((0,infinity),ds) tensor H by

    (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^(-1)h.

The spectral theorem and scalar integral int t^2/(t+s)^2 ds=t give
F_T*F_T=T in every Hilbert space. For an effect this is a contraction.
Define a bounded partial isometry WITHOUT an unbounded inverse by

    (V_T h)(s)=sqrt(T)(T+sI)^(-1)h.

Then V_T*V_T=Pi_T by int t/(t+s)^2 ds=1 for t>0, zero for t=0,
and F_T=V_T sqrt(T). Strong measurability follows from norm continuity
in s for each h. The vector integrals are well-defined; no finite rank
or positive lower spectral bound is required.

The Jensen bound also extends in operator norm. First use A_l+epsilon I
and B+epsilon I, with B=sum q_l A_l. These operators are bounded below.
The exact inverse second-difference integral and its positive integrand
domination from the finite proof remain norm-convergent for bounded
operators. Consequently

    sum q_l(F_(A_l+epsilon)-F_(B+epsilon))*
                   (F_(A_l+epsilon)-F_(B+epsilon))
       <=sum q_l f(A_l+epsilon I)-f(B+epsilon I),             (1)

where f(t)=t log t, f(0)=0. The argument applies to bounded positive
operators, so their upper bound 1+epsilon causes no problem.

For a scalar t>=0,

    ||F_(t+epsilon)-F_t||_L2^2
      =epsilon^2 int_0^infinity s^2/
         [(t+epsilon+s)^2(t+s)^2] ds
      <=epsilon^2 int_0^infinity (epsilon+s)^(-2) ds
      =epsilon.

Functional calculus gives ||F_(T+epsilon I)-F_T||<=sqrt(epsilon).
All difference Grams on the left of (1) therefore converge in norm, and
continuity of f on [0,2] gives norm convergence on the right. Letting
epsilon go to zero proves

    sum q_l(F_A_l-F_B)*(F_A_l-F_B)
       <=sum q_l f(A_l)-f(B).                               (2)

Thus a continuous spectrum accumulating at zero is covered. Restricting
to supp B and treating its inverse as bounded would have been invalid
in this infinite-dimensional step.

## 2. Bounded transport in one von Neumann algebra

Let mathcal A be a von Neumann algebra on H, T in mathcal A an effect,
and G_a in mathcal A positive with sum_a G_a=T. Define a positive form
on ran sqrt(T) by

    q_a(sqrt(T)u,sqrt(T)v)=<u,G_a v>.

It is well-defined since ker T is contained in ker G_a. Positivity and
G_a<=T give |q_a(u',v')|<=||u'|| ||v'|| and
0<=q_a(u',u')<=||u'||^2. It extends to the closure supp T and determines
a unique contraction C_a supported there, satisfying

    G_a=sqrt(T) C_a sqrt(T),       sum_a C_a=Pi_T.            (3)

The bounded regularizations belong to mathcal A and converge strongly:

    (T+epsilon I)^(-1/2)G_a(T+epsilon I)^(-1/2)
       =R_epsilon C_a R_epsilon  -> C_a,
    R_epsilon=sqrt(T)(T+epsilon I)^(-1/2) -> Pi_T.

Von Neumann strong closure implies C_a belongs to mathcal A. This proves
both existence and algebra membership instead of assuming an unbounded
T^(-1/2)G_aT^(-1/2) formula is meaningful.

Every D in mathcal A' intertwines V_T and I tensor D, since each
sqrt(T)(T+s)^(-1) commutes with D. Therefore

    Q_a=V_T C_a V_T* + 1_(a=a_*)(I-V_T V_T*)                 (4)

commutes with I tensor mathcal A'. The commutant identity gives
Q_a in B(L2_s) bar-tensor mathcal A. It is positive, sums to I, and

    F_T* Q_a F_T=sqrt(T) C_a sqrt(T)=G_a.                   (5)

This includes T=0, where V_T=C_a=0 and the completion always outputs a_*.
The construction depends only on T,G_a and retains membership in the
amplification of the ORIGINAL local algebra.

## 3. Simultaneous transport preserves commutation and the joint law

Take commuting von Neumann algebras mathcal A,mathcal B on H, T,G_a
in mathcal A, S,K_b in mathcal B, and sum_a G_a=T, sum_b K_b=S.
The common target is

    Htilde=L2_s tensor L2_t tensor H.

Alice's transported Q_a acts on (s,H) and belongs to
B(L2_s) bar-tensor I_t bar-tensor mathcal A. Bob's R_b acts on (t,H)
and belongs to I_s bar-tensor B(L2_t) bar-tensor mathcal B. These two
amplified algebras commute: their auxiliary actions use distinct factors,
and every original Alice coefficient commutes with every Bob coefficient.

Define the unnormalized vector

    Psi_TS(s,t)=T(T+s)^(-1) S(S+t)^(-1)psi.                  (6)

It is the sequential application of F_T in the s register and the lift
of F_S in the t register. Its squared norm is <psi,TS psi> by separate
spectral integration, since T and S commute.

For the exact joint probability, Alice's Q_a commutes with S and K_b,
and intertwines the t-purification of S. Consequently

    <Psi_TS,Q_a R_b Psi_TS>
       =<F_T psi,Q_a (I_s tensor K_b)F_T psi>
       =<psi,G_a K_b psi>.                                (7)

The first equality is Bob's pullback (5); the second is Alice's pullback,
using that F_T intertwines all Bob operators. This is an operator identity
on H, not an approximation of commuting correlations by tensor ones.
All inputs' transported Alice POVMs belong to the same amplified Alice
algebra, and all Bob POVMs belong to the same amplified Bob algebra, so
cross commutation holds for ALL choices of inputs, not merely at a chosen
question pair.

## 4. The exposure lemma survives, with exact costs

Fix any repeated commuting strategy and let mathcal A,mathcal B be the
von Neumann algebras generated by its entire local POVM families. They
commute. Coarse effects and all conditional averages still belong to the
corresponding original algebras.

Use C,p,m,L,tau, mixed flags and transcript r from the finite gate. The
unconditioned probability argument giving independence of unfixed local
question strings is unchanged. Replace the old tensor-product
purification by (6), with T=F_x^alpha and S=H_y^gamma. A single
Htilde contains all conditional and local-approximation vectors. There
are finitely many questions/effects/transcripts at this k, but Htilde is
permitted to be infinite dimensional.

Write chi(A,B)=<psi,AB psi>. For B in the positive Bob algebra, the
functional A->chi(A,B) on the Alice algebra is positive and normal:

    chi(A,B)=<sqrt(B)psi,A sqrt(B)psi>.

Its mass is g=chi(I,B)<=1. Scalar spectral-measure Jensen gives exactly
-chi(f(F),B)<=h log(g/h)<=-h log h, where h=chi(F,B).
Thus U_j,Z_j still lie in [-p log(d_C/p),0]. No von Neumann entropy,
density matrix, finite trace or tracial embeddability is assumed.

For the squared distance to the y-description, integration gives

    ||Psi_(F_x,H_y)-Psi_(F_y,H_y)||^2
       =chi((F_(F_x)-F_(F_y))*(F_(F_x)-F_(F_y)),H_y).

The Gram on the right belongs to mathcal A and commutes with H_y.
Applying (2) therefore bounds it by the same Jensen increment U_i-U_(i-1).
The Bob-side statement uses Z_(i-1)-Z_i. Telescoping proves the two
separate budgets pL and the total 2pL.

Using (7) for finer answer effects proves the exact conditional
answer law and the same norm-weighted transcript law. Hence the finite
exposure identities hold with vectors in Htilde and commuting transported
POVMs:

    sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)||Psi_ir(z)||^2=p,
    sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)c_ir(z)<=2p tau,
    sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)<Psi_ir(z),W_ir(z)Psi_ir(z)>=p sbar.    (8)

W_ir(z)=sum V Q_(r,x)^a R_(r,y)^b is an effect because its POVM
products are commuting positive operators and their sum is I.

## 5. Barycenter rounding gives a legal commuting strategy

The scalar graph Poincare bound applies to Hilbert-valued functions in
Htilde. For a fixed finite graph, their vector span is finite dimensional,
so this can be seen by decomposing that finite span into an orthonormal
basis; no operator or tensor compression is performed. With
beta_ir,j=E_mu_j Psi_ir(z), the exact identity remains

    P_hat_j=P_j-E_j,        E=sum lambda_j E_j<=p tau/sigma. (9)

For P_hat_j>0, take shared Hilbert space ell2(ir) tensor Htilde and state

    P_hat_j^(-1/2) sum_ir sqrt(w_ir)|ir> tensor beta_ir,j.

Both players' POVMs are block diagonal on the SAME finite classical flag
register. Within every block their transported operators commute by
Section 3, so all Alice and Bob controlled POVMs commute. Their state
is independent of the new questions. Its component success is
T_hat_j/P_hat_j, therefore T_hat_j<=v_j P_hat_j with
v_j=omega^co(G_j). If P_hat_j=0, T_hat_j=0.

The norm/effect Cauchy-Schwarz bound gives

    T_j<=v_j P_j+2sqrt(P_j E_j).

Every local question identifies its support component. Tensoring the
finitely many component COMMUTING representations gives a legal
commuting strategy for the full game, so sum lambda_j v_j<=v.
This tensoring of different shared representations does not assert that
the original Alice and Bob algebras factor within any representation.

The exact conditional component weights are
lambda_tilde_j=lambda_j P_j/p=(1/m)sum_i Pr[J_i=j|E_C]. Their joint
conditioned likelihood ratio is at most 1/p relative to lambda^tensor m.
Product relative entropy, Pinsker and Cauchy-Schwarz give

    TV(lambda_tilde,lambda)<=sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m)).

Combining these facts with (9) proves

    sbar<=v+sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m))+2sqrt(tau/sigma).             (10)

The drift term vanishes for connected support. All normalizations and
zero-vector cases are accounted for; neither local acquisition nor
efficient preparation of beta is needed for a value upper bound.

## 6. Threshold transfer and short repetition lengths

At T=sigma delta^2/128, (10) is at most v+delta/4. The purely binary
sampling transfer in the finite gate uses only this conditioning bound
and closure of the strategy class under independent tensor powers. The
commuting strategy class has that closure: tensor the shared Hilbert
representations and the local POVMs across copies. All cross operators
still commute and block outcomes are independent.

Marginal success in every coordinate is at most v by fixing all other
question pairs and retaining the chosen local answers, a valid single-game
commuting strategy. Thus the same zero-value case and baseline hold.
The selected-set contradiction gives rate delta T/(16(1+log d)) for large
lengths. Taking tensor powers of a short fixed strategy and then roots
gives every k. This proves (T) without any finite-dimensional argument
about the commuting representation. The endpoint limit proves (A).

## Decisive obstructions and downstream use

The new state may be infinite dimensional and may lie outside the original
algebra's representation. It is nevertheless permitted by omega^co.
Discarding this strategy-model allowance would leave a gap: we have no
claim that the rounded strategy remains in a fixed factor, a prescribed
type-II_1 representation, a finite tensor model, or a hardware-limited
class. There is likewise no universal cubic constant with sigma removed.
The almost-disconnected 2x2 question law has sigma approaching zero.

For efficiently specified verifier families with a supplied or certified
sigma>=1/poly(n), completeness c and commuting soundness s with
Delta=c-s>=1/poly(n), midpoint threshold gives

    completeness >=1-exp(-Delta^2 k/8),
    commuting soundness <=exp[-sigma(Delta/2)^3 k/
                                  (2048(1+log d))].

Hence any target error exp(-L) is reached with

    k>=max{8L/Delta^2, 16384(1+log d)L/(sigma Delta^3)}.

This directly amplifies the original question law and keeps two players
and one round. Question and answer strings grow by factor k. The referee's
score predicate spans k tests; no constant-query, sparse-isometry,
constant-locality Hamiltonian or quantum PCP theorem is produced.
The theorem also applies when finite tensor soundness is not known but
a commuting soundness upper bound is supplied; conversely it cannot
replace v^co by a smaller v* when they differ.
