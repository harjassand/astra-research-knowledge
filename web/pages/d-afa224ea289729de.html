# A universal weighted curvature certificate for uniform reflections

2026-10-07. Scoped theorem; historical priority unverified. This eliminates an entire structured stationary candidate family for every positive definite weight, not just finitely sampled weights. It does not settle the weighted OJD landscape or acquire hidden axes from quartic coefficients.

Let F_G(W)=sum_k (W[:,k]^{circ2})^T G(W[:,k]^{circ2}), G symmetric PD. For n>=3 set W=I−2J/n, P=I−J/n. Put x_i=W^T e_i and

  A_i=x_i e_i^T−e_i x_i^T=(2/n)(e_i1^T−1e_i^T).

Then the EXACT aggregate second-variation identity is

  sum_i d²F_G(W exp(tA_i))/dt² at0
    =64(3n−8)/n³ tr(PG)>0.

No stationarity assumption is required for this aggregate. Consequently at least one mixed rank-two direction has positive second variation. W cannot be a local maximum for any G PD. This also explains why tuning a PD weight by SDP cannot turn this family into a full-Hessian trap.

## Complete proof

For arbitrary orthogonal W and skew A, differentiating each column quadratic gives

  F''_G(W;A)=tr(G K_A),
  K_A=2 D D^T + M D_2^T + D_2 M^T,
  D=2W circ WA, D_2=2(WA)^{circ2}+2W circ WA².

The coefficient matrix K_A is rational, symmetric and kills1. The last assertion follows independently from the fact that adding1v^T+v1^T to G changes F by the constant2v^T1 on O(n), so all first/second derivatives vanish for these weights.

For uniform-reflection W, conjugating by any coordinate permutation fixes W and permutes the A_i. Thus K=sum_i K_Ai is invariant under every permutation and has the form alpha I+beta J. Since K1=0, K=cP. It remains to compute c from G=I.

Let a=1−2/n, b=−2/n, p=a² and s=a⁴+(n−1)b⁴. The entrywise-square matrix M=(1−4/n)I+(4/n²)J has eigenvalue1 on1 and1−4/n on1-perp. Hence

  (M³)_ii=1/n+(1−1/n)(1−4/n)³.

At G=I, W is stationary: W^T W^{circ3} is symmetric because both matrices are symmetric combinations of I,J. The Jin v2 Eq8 pivot identity, independently recoverable by the displayed derivative formula, gives

  F''_I(W;A_i)=4 N,
  N=3(M³)_ii+3p−4p²−2s
   =16(n−1)(3n−8)/n⁴.

Therefore tr(K)=n4N. As tr(P)=n−1,

  c=4nN/(n−1)=64(3n−8)/n³.

For n>=3, c>0. PD G implies tr(PG)>0 because P is a nonzero PSD projector. This proves the theorem. At n2 the uniform reflection is a signed permutation and the coefficient is negative, consistent with a maximum; no extrapolation to n2 is made.
