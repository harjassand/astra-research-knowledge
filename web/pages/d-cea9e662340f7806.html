## Exact gated interface

For each n>=3 and each positive loss L(r) tending to infinity, choose the admissible staircase a from `notes/r15_geometric_analysis/cycle6/ADMISSIBLE_SLOW_ENVELOPES.md`. There exist one fixed K, one legal partition and tangent-frequency assignment, one fixed sparse threshold tau, and one fixed positive radial smoothing parameter sigma, such that the actual global Dirichlet-repaired pair (F,V) satisfies

    (-Delta+V)F=0, F real smooth and nonzero,
    exp(M|x|)F in L2 for every fixed M>0,
    |D^m V| <= C_m a(R)^(m+3/2)/sqrt(R), every fixed m,
    |V(x)| <= C |x|^(-1/2)L(|x|) eventually,

and -Delta+V has infinitely many negative eigenvalues accumulating at zero. The loss bound is eventual; positivity of an arbitrary L on compact sets does not give a positive compact-set lower bound. Constants need not be effective or uniform in L, sigma, or m.

The spectral conclusion is existential among legal construction choices. It neither classifies an already fixed arbitrary cover/mollifier nor proves that every possible real potential with such a threshold state has infinite Morse index. The zero state and potential are constructed together.

The inherited proof provides the sparse weighted inverse and smooth zero-safe quotient construction. I checked its exact hypotheses against the reserved cover below; this is where the inherited result is used. Its mathematical interface, rather than agreement labels in earlier audits, is the input.

## 1. Plateaus, partition freedom, and parameter order

Choose a fixed smooth increasing step s_0, zero on (-infinity,0] and one on [1,infinity). The loss construction chooses T_j with T_(j+1)>=T_j+2, T_j>=exp(j), and the appropriate tail-infimum bound for L. Then

    a(R)=1+sum_j s_0(log R-T_j)

is constant with value A_j=j+1 on [exp(T_j+1),exp(T_(j+1))]. In particular, every plateau has logarithmic width at least one. Its derivative estimates and subpower growth are independent of the positions of the steps. Choosing R_j=exp(T_j+3/2) places R_j a fixed logarithmic distance from either edge. After K is fixed, set r_j=sqrt(R_j^2-K^2) and x_j=r_j e_1, dropping the finite prefix for which this is undefined or too close to an edge.

Fix B=4*pi*sqrt(n). The macro radius ell_j=sqrt(R_j/A_j) satisfies ell_j/R_j=eta_j=(A_j R_j)^(-1/2)->0. Thus the cubes |x-x_j|_infinity<=4B ell_j, and any prescribed fixed enlargements, lie entirely in the same plateau eventually. The successive R_j have ratios at least exp(2); their macro cubes and fixed enlargements are disjoint eventually. Removing finitely many cubes changes no infinite-index conclusion.

Take any ordinary slow-radius partition chi_i^0 with the proved bounded overlap, support size O(ell_i), and all-order bounds |D^m chi_i^0|<=C_m ell_i^(-m). Choose disjoint b_j in [0,1], equal to one on the 3B cubes and zero outside the 4B cubes. Set

    chi_j^res=b_j,
    chi_i^other=(1-sum_j b_j)chi_i^0.

These functions are nonnegative and sum to one. They have the required support size and derivative bounds: whenever b_j meets chi_i^0, slow-radius comparability gives ell_j comparable to ell_i; disjointness means at most one b_j contributes locally. The product rule supplies every derivative bound with a fixed m-dependent constant.

The degree estimate needs a minor explicit clarification: the union of the original and reserved centers need not have disjoint cores across the two types. Instead use packing separately in each type. The old centers have disjoint small cores; the reserved cubes have disjoint cores and enlargements. All radii meeting a given enlarged support are comparable, and their centers lie in one fixed enlarged macro ball. Each family consequently contributes only O_(n,B)(1) neighbors. The union has bounded degree and every micro ball sees a bounded number of indices. These are precisely the geometric properties used in the PDE/sparsity proof; no normalization-of-bumps formula is separately required there.

Set all reserved t_j=e_2. They are tangent to x_j and no two reserved vertices are neighbors. Assign the other directions greedily, avoiding both signs of every neighboring reserved direction and every earlier assigned neighbor. At most finitely many caps are forbidden on S^(n-2). Since n>=3, the standard cap measure estimate gives a uniform positive separation depending only on n and B. Preassigning infinitely many reserved directions is harmless because each remaining vertex has only finitely many such neighbors.

The frozen cosine family therefore still has bounded cardinality, positive nonnegative weights summing to one, comparable frequencies, and separation modulo sign. It is compact and no member is identically zero. The compact-family analytic sublevel argument gives sparsity exactly as in the inherited proof. All the constants can be fixed uniformly for sufficiently large K. A noncircular order is:

1. Choose a from L, n, B, the partition templates, and the uniform geometric/coloring constants.
2. Choose theta for the weighted sparse inverse, then rho for its small origin contribution.
3. Choose tau from compact-family sparsity, decreasing it so delta=arcsin(tau)<=1/10.
4. Choose one sufficiently large K for scale comparability, coefficient freezing, and C rho^2 eta(K)<=tau/40. Realize the reserved centers/cover at this K.
5. Fix one radial normalized nonnegative smooth kernel psi, and choose sigma small enough for the original zero-safe smoothing bound, sigma<delta/4, and the two strict mean inequalities proved in Section 4 below.
6. Hold everything fixed and take j to infinity.

The cover and centers can be specified as functions of K before selecting its final value; the compact family and all constants in steps 1-3 are uniform in K. Thus no circular dependence of tau on a subsequently altered geometric constant is needed. Later derivative estimates do not select K_m or sigma_m.

On every reserved 3B cube, all other partition weights vanish and

    q=cos(A_j x_2), Omega={|cos(A_j x_2)|<tau}.

No approximation of the actual stripe geometry has been introduced.

## 2. Independent local expansion with an explicit second coefficient

Suppress j and write A=A_j, R=R_j, ell=sqrt(R/A), eta=1/(A ell), mu=A/R=A^2 eta^2. Let s=x_1-r_j, t=x_2, z=(x_3,...,x_n), S=s/ell, T=t/ell, Z=z/ell, zeta=A t, d=n-1, U=T^2+|Z|^2, b_0=exp(-U/2), c=cos(zeta).

Since a is exactly constant in the local cube, Phi(R(x))=A R(x)+constant. Factor E(x)=E(x_j) exp(-A s)b_eta. If K=0 formally, direct Taylor expansion gives

    A(R(x)-R-s)
       = U/2 - eta S U/2 + eta^2(S^2 U/2-U^2/8)+O_B(eta^3),

and hence

    b_eta=b_0[1+eta S U/2+eta^2 b_2+O_B(eta^3)],
    b_2=U^2/8-S^2 U/2+S^2 U^2/8.                    (G1)

For the fixed actual K put q_K=K^2/R^2. The change caused by r_j/R=sqrt(1-q_K) is O_B(q_K/eta) in the exponent and in the finitely many macro derivatives used here. Now

    (q_K/eta)/eta^2 = K^2 A^(3/2)/sqrt(R) -> 0.

The final limit follows from subpower growth. Thus (G1) with a macro C^2 remainder o(eta^2) is valid for the actual regularized origin. When applying the operator, its error is O_B(eta^3+q_K): L_0 annihilates every slow coefficient times c, and eta D_1 acting on O(q_K/eta)c costs O(q_K). This explicitly justifies the forcing scale later used in the barrier.

For a slow/fast function, removal of exp(-A s) gives the exact operator

    A^(-2)exp(A s)Delta exp(-A s)
        =L_0+eta D_1+eta^2 D_2,
    L_0=partial_zeta^2+1,
    D_1=2(partial_zeta partial_T-partial_S), D_2=Delta_Y.  (G2)

Applying it to b_eta c gives

    eta b_0 2T sin(zeta)
      +eta^2 b_0[-d c-2ST(1-U/2)sin(zeta)]
      +O_B(eta^3+q_K).                                (G3)

In particular D_2(b_0 c)=(U-d)b_0 c, and -2 partial_S(b_0 S U c/2)=-U b_0 c. The radial slow correction is indispensable; freezing it would alter the order-mu mean.

## 3. Cell equations and distributional endpoint signs

On the stripe centered at pi/2 let h=zeta-pi/2. The zero-Dirichlet solutions are

    p=h sin h-delta tan(delta)cos h,
    r=(h cos h-delta cot(delta)sin h)/2,
    k=C_delta h cos h-(h^2/2)sin h
        +(delta^2/2-C_delta delta cot(delta))sin h,
    C_delta=(1+2delta tan(delta))/2.

Extend by zero off the stripes and antiperiodically by pi. Direct differentiation gives L_0 p=2 sin(zeta), L_0 r=c, and L_0 k=-2p' in the open stripes, and all three have zero trace. Their first derivatives have endpoint jumps; these jumps belong to the distributional L_0 and must be retained.

Let K_c=L_0 p-2sin(zeta), A_c=L_0 r-c, B_c=L_0 k+2p'. The regular parts outside the stripes are respectively -2sin(zeta), -c, 0; the regular parts inside are zero. Define

    W_app=E(x_j)exp(-A s)b_0[-eta T p
         +eta^2(d r+(T^2-1)k+ST(1-U/2)p)].            (G4)

Substitution in (G2) gives the full distributional numerator, through its second order,

    Delta(f+W_app)/[A^2 E(x_j)exp(-A s)]
      = -eta T b_0 K_c
        +eta^2 b_0[d A_c+(T^2-1)B_c+ST(1-U/2)K_c]
        +O_B(eta^3+q_K).                             (G5)

The remainder is regular locally; no endpoint measure has been absorbed in it. In the open stripes all displayed cell residuals vanish, so (G5) gives the forcing estimate O(A^2(eta^3+q_K)E(x_j)exp(-A s)).

The endpoint sign can be checked without choosing a normal convention. For an odd zero-Dirichlet cell u(h), zero extension has second derivative measures u'(-delta)delta_(-delta)-u'(delta)delta_(delta). Since c=-sin h, both quotient contributions are u'(delta)/sin(delta). Therefore

    M_r=mean(A_c/c)
        =[-pi+2delta+cot(delta)-delta csc(delta)^2]/pi,
    M_k=mean(B_c/c)=2k'(delta)/(pi sin(delta)),
    r'(delta)=[cos(delta)-delta csc(delta)]/2,
    k'(delta)=C_delta[cos(delta)-delta csc(delta)]-delta sin(delta).

Here mean is the normalized integral over a pi period. The quotients are defined to be zero near c=0, where the residual distributions have no support. K_c/c is odd and pi-periodic, so its mean is zero.

Let D=cos(delta)-delta/sin(delta). Since sin(delta)cos(delta)<delta, D<0 and M_k<0. On 0<delta<=1/10, elementary sine bounds give

    M_r<=-1+2delta/pi, |M_k|<=8delta/pi,
    d M_r-M_k<=-2+12delta/pi<-3/2.                    (G6)

For example delta/sin(delta)-cos(delta)<=delta^2 follows from
delta-sin(delta)cos(delta)=2 integral_0^delta sin^2 h dh and sin(delta)>=2delta/3. Also C_delta<=1 and sin(delta)>=delta/2, giving the stated bound on |M_k|.

## 4. The fixed finite smoothing moments

On the plateau epsilon=sigma/A is exactly constant. Put

    S_sigma h(zeta)=integral psi(v)exp(sigma v_1)h(zeta-sigma v_2)dv.

The original radial harmonic averaging property, applied to exp(-A s)cos(A t) and exp(-A s)sin(A t), gives S_sigma c=c and S_sigma sin=sin exactly. An ordinary one-dimensional convolution without the exponential tilt would not have this property.

Let

    C_sigma=integral psi(v)exp(sigma v_1)v_2 c(zeta-sigma v_2)dv,
    K_sigma=integral psi(v)exp(sigma v_1)v_2 K_c(zeta-sigma v_2)dv,
    P_sigma=S_sigma p-sigma C_sigma.

A physical kernel translation sends Y to Y-sigma eta v and zeta to zeta-sigma v_2. At first order

    b_0(Y-sigma eta v)
       =b_0(Y)[1+sigma eta(Tv_2+Z dot v_z)]+O_B(eta^2).

The v_z moments vanish by reflection symmetry. Translating the first numerator -T b_0 K_c therefore gives the additional second coefficient

    -sigma(T^2-1)b_0 K_sigma.

Translating the leading denominator b_0 c gives the additional first coefficient sigma T b_0 C_sigma. Thus the denominator is

    F/[E(x_j)exp(-A s)b_0]
       =c+eta[SU c/2-T P_sigma]+O_B(eta^2).           (G7)

Dividing (G5) after smoothing by (G7), away from its safe collar, gives

    V=A^2[-eta T v_sigma
       +eta^2(d alpha_sigma+(T^2-1)beta_sigma
                      +ST v_sigma-T^2 n_sigma)]+o(mu),  (G8)

where

    v_sigma=(S_sigma K_c)/c,
    alpha_sigma=(S_sigma A_c)/c,
    beta_sigma=(S_sigma B_c-sigma K_sigma)/c,
    n_sigma=(P_sigma/c)v_sigma.

In particular the ST U/2 denominator correction cancels the -ST U/2 numerator term, leaving exactly ST v_sigma. Both finite moment corrections in the candidate are correct.

For sigma<delta/4, all numerator cell supports are separated from c=0 by at least 3delta/4 in the fast coordinate. Every displayed quotient is smooth and extended by zero on the remaining inner collar. The actual smoothed F is exactly harmonic on that collar, so its actual V is identically zero there. On the complementary region |c| has a fixed positive lower bound; (G7) bounds the actual denominator below for all sufficiently large j. Thus this quotient expansion never divides through an unresolved zero.

Reflection in v_2 shows S_sigma preserves the appropriate parity. Since K_c is odd and c is even, mean(v_sigma)=0 exactly. The residual distributions are finite signed measures plus bounded regular densities over each cell. Their smoothed total variations are uniformly bounded for 0<sigma<delta/4, because the tilted kernel mass is at most exp(sigma). Approximate-identity convergence, with denominators safely bounded, gives

    mean(alpha_sigma)->M_r,
    mean(beta_sigma)->M_k.                            (G9)

The sigma K_sigma term tends to zero in L1 using the same uniform total-variation bound.

For the potentially dangerous product, p is continuous and Lipschitz as a zero extension, so P_sigma->p uniformly. Choose a fixed smooth periodic reciprocal-c-squared cutoff equal to 1/c^2 on every smoothed residual support and zero near the zeros. Then

    pi mean(n_sigma)=pairing(S_sigma K_c, P_sigma/c^2)
        -> pairing(K_c,p/c^2)=0.                     (G10)

Indeed uniform convergence of P_sigma times the uniform variation bound handles the moving test function, and weak convergence handles its fixed limit. The regular part of K_c is supported where p=0, and p vanishes at each endpoint measure. Thus there is no leftover boundary-layer contribution. No product of two singular distributions occurs.

By (G6), (G9), and (G10), one sufficiently small positive sigma satisfies, with m_r,m_k,m_n its three fixed means,

    d m_r-m_k<-1, m_k-m_n<0.                          (G11)

Choose that sigma once, also imposing the inherited zero-safe bound. Divergent constants as sigma tends to zero do not matter: sigma is fixed before any j limit. This proves the quantifier needed in the candidate; a sequence sigma_j is unnecessary.

## 5. Global Dirichlet localization, including data outside the core

The actual zero extension W obeys |W|<=C_K eta E, solves Delta W=-Delta f in Omega, and has zero trace on the literal stripe walls inside the 3B cube. W_app has the same trace. Their difference e=W-W_app therefore solves

    -Delta e=Delta(f+W_app)

in each bounded cube/stripe segment. On the artificial macro faces |e|<=C_(B,K) eta E(x_j)exp(-A s). This follows from the inherited global bound and envelope comparability on a fixed macro cube; it does not assume that the repair is a collection of independent local solutions.

For a stripe node t_0 use h=A(t-t_0), lambda=pi/(4delta), and b(h)=cos(lambda h). On the whole stripe b>=1/sqrt(2) and |b'/b|<=lambda. Let H=E(x_j)exp(-A s)b(h). Then

    -Delta H=A^2(lambda^2-1)H>0.

For each macro face let d_f(x) be its nonnegative distance inside the cube and set H_f=H exp(-A d_f). The exact lower bounds for (-Delta H_f)/(A^2 H_f) are

    s faces: lambda^2 or lambda^2-4,
    z faces: lambda^2-2,
    t faces: at least lambda^2-2-2lambda.

All are strictly positive because delta<=1/10. Each face barrier equals H on its own face and is nonnegative on the stripe walls. Thus

    P=C_(B,K)[(eta^3+q_K)H+eta sum_f H_f]

dominates the forcing and both signs of the artificial boundary data. The error has zero trace on the stripe walls. The weak maximum principle on every bounded Lipschitz stripe segment gives |e|<=P. The estimate is uniform in the number of stripes; no sum over stripe components is taken.

Inside the 2B cube every macro-face distance is at least B ell. Therefore

    |W-W_app|<=C_(B,K)E(x_j)exp(-A s)
        [eta^3+K^2/R^2+eta exp(-B/eta)].              (G12)

Both zero extensions vanish outside Omega, so this bound also holds there. Its constant may include the fixed K and the inherited repair constant; this dependence is harmless and should not be mistaken for a constant uniform in the origin regularization.

To compare the actual smoothed function with the smoothed local approximation on the B cube, differentiate only the constant local kernel. Its m-th derivative has size O_(m,sigma)(A^(n+m)) on volume O(A^(-n)). Local micro-envelope comparison gives an error O_(m,sigma,B,K)(A^m) times the bracket in (G12). No derivative or pointwise normal derivative of the global W is required. At order two the potential error is

    O(A^2 eta^3)+O(A^2 K^2/R^2)
                  +O(A^2 eta exp(-B/eta))=o(mu).     (G13)

The ratios to mu are eta, K^2 A/R, and exp(-B/eta)/eta, all tending to zero for the same fixed construction. The denominator is safe on the residual support, and the exact harmonic collar treats the rest. This proves that (G8) is an expansion of the actual global repaired potential, rather than a potential-only surrogate.

## 6. Trial localization and infinite negative spectrum

On |Y_i|<B let chi(Y)=product_i cos(pi Y_i/(2B)), with zero extension. This compact H1 trial satisfies

    ||grad_Y chi||_2^2/||chi||_2^2=n*pi^2/(4B^2)=1/64.

Its squared weight is C1 on the line with integrable second weak derivative. Its value and first derivative vanish at every endpoint. For a smooth pi-periodic zero-mean v, choose bounded periodic primitives Q_1'=v and Q_2'=Q_1, with the first primitive normalized to zero mean. Two integrations by parts give

    integral g(T)v(T/eta)dT
          =eta^2 integral g''(T)Q_2(T/eta)dT=O(eta^2)

for g(T)=T cos^2(pi T/(2B)), zero extended. Applied to v_sigma this shows that the first term A^2 eta T v_sigma in (G8) contributes O(A^2 eta^3)=o(mu), not merely o(A^2 eta). The second-order cell coefficients converge to their means against the compact slow weights. The ST v_sigma term also vanishes directly by symmetry in S for this trial.

For chi_j(x)=chi((x-x_j)/ell_j), (G8) and (G13) consequently give

    q_H(chi_j)/||chi_j||_2^2
       =mu_j[1/64+d m_r-m_k
                    +(m_k-m_n)<T^2>_(chi^2)+o(1)]<0  (G14)

eventually, by the strict fixed inequalities (G11). The trials have disjoint escaping compact supports. The quadratic form is diagonal and strictly negative on each finite span. The min-max principle gives arbitrarily many negative eigenvalues.

The inherited all-order bound makes actual V bounded and V(x)->0. Multiplication by V is relatively compact with respect to the free Laplacian (split into a compactly supported bounded part and a uniformly small tail; use local Rellich compactness). Hence the essential spectrum is [0,infinity), negative spectrum is discrete with finite multiplicity, and its only possible accumulation is zero. The disjoint negative spans therefore give infinitely many actual negative eigenvalues. Approximation of each H1 trial by smooth compact trials preserves its strict negative form if desired.
