REGULAR SU(3): STABLE LOW-CASIMIR PROJECTOR PROFILE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally proved stable-range count; no novelty or priority claim.

SCOPE

This is an independent Weyl-character / lattice-count audit of the low-
Casimir part of End(V_(a,b)) for arbitrary positive integers a,b. It does
not repeat the full Littlewood-Richardson decomposition recorded in
`spectrum_exact.txt`. The result below is exact up to an explicit cutoff,
uniform for comparable a,b, and gives the leading projector-dimension
constant. All multiplicities are counted.

NORMALIZATION AND DEFINITION

Take V_(a,b) to be the SU(3) irrep with Dynkin labels a,b>0 and

  d_(a,b)=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2.

Use Hermitian generators with Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2. The Casimir on
the output irrep V_(r,s) in the conjugation action is

  C2(r,s)=(r^2+rs+s^2+3r+3s)/3,
  dim V_(r,s)=(r+1)(s+1)(r+s+2)/2.

Write m_(r,s)(a,b) for its multiplicity in End(V_(a,b)), and define the
cumulative spectral-projector dimension, with multiplicity, by

  D_(a,b)(T)=sum_(r,s>=0) m_(r,s)(a,b) dim(V_(r,s)) 1{C2(r,s)<=T}.

The central character of End(V_(a,b)) is trivial, so only r congruent to s
modulo 3 can occur. This congruence is necessary, but it does not make the
decomposition multiplicity-free.

STABLE-RANGE MULTIPLICITY LEMMA

Put Delta=min(a+1,b+1). For every r,s>=0 with C2(r,s)<Delta^2,

  m_(r,s)(a,b) = 1{r congruent s (mod 3)} (min(r,s)+1).       (1)

In particular, for every real T with 0<=T<Delta^2, the exact projector
profile is

  D_(a,b)(T)=sum_((r,s)>=0, r congruent s mod 3, C2(r,s)<=T)
                 (min(r,s)+1)(r+1)(s+1)(r+s+2)/2.             (2)

Proof. Frobenius reciprocity gives

  m_(r,s)(a,b)=dim Hom_G(V_(r,s) tensor V_(a,b), V_(a,b)).

Apply the Klimyk character formula, in its finite-dimensional form that
follows from the Weyl character formula. A contribution to V_(a,b) from a
weight nu of V_(r,s) and Weyl element w requires

  nu=w(lambda+rho)-(lambda+rho),   lambda=a omega_1+b omega_2.

Equivalently, the coefficient is

  m_(r,s)(a,b)=sum_(w in W) det(w) mult_(r,s)(w(lambda+rho)-(lambda+rho)),

where mult_(r,s)(nu) is the weight multiplicity in V_(r,s). This is the
form used in the displacement argument below.

Use the A2 realization as the plane x_1+x_2+x_3=0 with roots of squared
length 2. The coordinates of x=lambda+rho have successive gaps
A=a+1 and B=b+1. The A2 Weyl-orbit displacement bound is

  ||w x-x|| >= sqrt(2) Delta   for every w != 1.                (3)

Indeed, the three reflections (coordinate transpositions) have displacement
lengths sqrt(2)A, sqrt(2)B, and sqrt(2)(A+B); each of the two 3-cycles has
squared displacement 2(A^2+AB+B^2). On the other hand every weight nu of
V_(r,s) lies in the convex hull of the Weyl orbit of its highest weight,
so its norm is at most the highest-weight norm. Here

  ||(r,s)||^2=2(r^2+rs+s^2)/3,
  C2(r,s)=||(r,s)||^2/2+(r+s),
  ||nu||^2 <= 2 C2(r,s).

If C2(r,s)<Delta^2, then ||nu||<sqrt(2)Delta, excluding all nonidentity
w by (3). For w=1 the only contributing weight is nu=0, and its coefficient
is the zero-weight multiplicity of V_(r,s).

For completeness, that multiplicity can be counted with U(3) Gelfand-
Tsetlin patterns. Lift (r,s) to the partition (r+s,s,0). A zero SU(3)
weight is the U(3) weight (q,q,q), where q=(r+2s)/3. Thus there are no
such weights unless r congruent s mod 3. When q is integral, the patterns
have top row (r+s,s,0), middle row (u,v), and bottom entry q, with
u+v=2q and interlacing. Their number is s+1 if r>=s and r+1 if r<=s;
equivalently it is min(r,s)+1. This proves (1) and (2).

The strict inequality in the cutoff matters. At C2=Delta^2, a nonidentity
Weyl displacement can no longer be excluded by this argument. A convenient
finite convention is T<=Delta^2/2, which is safely inside the exact range.

UNIFORM ASYMPTOTIC AND EXPLICIT TWO-SIDED BOUNDS

As T tends to infinity within 0<=T<Delta^2, (2) has the uniform asymptotic

  D_(a,b)(T)=T^3/12+O(T^(5/2)).                                  (4)

The error constant is absolute and independent of a,b. In particular,
D_(a,b)(T)~T^3/12 uniformly whenever T tends to infinity while remaining
in the stable range. If cN<=a,b<=CN for fixed 0<c<C, the explicit window

  576<=T<=c^2 N^2/2                                               (5)

lies in that range and gives (4) uniformly; the asymptotic statement is
understood as N and T tending to infinity subject to (5).

To compute the constant, set R=sqrt(3T), r=Ru, s=Rv. The limiting region
is Omega={(u,v)>=0:u^2+uv+v^2<=1}. The sublattice r congruent s mod 3 has
density 1/3. The leading summand in (2) is
f(r,s)=min(r,s) r s (r+s)/2, homogeneous of degree four. Hence

  D(T)= (R^6/3) integral_Omega
          [min(u,v) u v (u+v)/2] du dv + O(R^5).

Splitting Omega along u=v and setting v=ut on u>=v gives

  integral_Omega [min(u,v)uv(u+v)/2] du dv
    = (1/6) integral_0^1 t^2(1+t)/(1+t+t^2)^3 dt
    = 1/108.

Since R^6/3=9T^3, the coefficient is 1/12. The O(R^5) error includes
the boundary-cell discrepancy and all lower-degree terms in the exact
polynomial summand. The triality density is essential: omitting it would
give an incorrect leading coefficient.

For a fully finite, deliberately loose pair of bounds, for every
1<=T<Delta^2,

  D_(a,b)(T) <= 729 T^3,

and for every 576<=T<Delta^2,

  D_(a,b)(T) >= T^3/442368.                                       (6)

For the upper bound, C2(r,s)>= (r+s)^2/4 gives r+s<=2 sqrt(T), so there
are at most 9T lattice points; each has multiplicity at most 3 sqrt(T)
and dimension at most 27 T^(3/2). For the lower bound, let R=sqrt(T)>=24
and restrict both r,s to the integers in [R/4,R/3]. Every such pair has
C2(r,s)<=T; there are at least R/24 choices per coordinate. Among these,
at least R^2/1728 pairs satisfy r congruent s mod 3, by the three-residue
count and Cauchy-Schwarz. Each summand in (2) is at least R^4/256. This
proves (6). The constants are only used to make the finite uniform claim
explicit; (4) is much sharper asymptotically.

If cN<=a,b<=CN and T<=c^2N^2/2, the statements above are uniform over
all such (a,b). No a=b specialization is used.

CONSEQUENCE FOR RANK-ENERGY FILTERS

The general partial-trace/Schmidt-rank theorem gives, for every unit
Hilbert-Schmidt matrix X of rank at most Q,

  E(X)=sum_A ||[t_A,X]||_HS^2 >= Phi_(a,b)(Q),
  Phi_(a,b)(Q)=integral_0^infinity [1-QD_(a,b)(T)/d_(a,b)]_+ dT.  (7)

Combining (2), (4), (6), and d_(a,b) comparable to N^3 yields, for fixed
0<c<C, all sufficiently large N, and 1<=Q<=d_(a,b)/2,

  Phi_(a,b)(Q) is comparable, with constants depending only on c,C,
  to (d_(a,b)/Q)^(1/3), hence to N Q^(-1/3).                    (8)

The relevant positive-profile cutoff is O((d/Q)^(1/3))=O(N), which is
inside the O(N^2) stable window (5). The finite bounds (6) control the
large-T tail; D(0)=1 controls bounded T. This is a rigorous lower bound
for the rank-constrained energy, not a matching all-matrix extremizer
claim and not an assertion that (7) is the exact infimum.

One explicit coarse comparison verifies (8). Put H=(d/Q)^(1/3), so
H>=2^(1/3). On [0,3), D=1, hence Phi>=3(1-1/H^3)>=3/2. For H>=12,
let alpha=1458^(-1/3). On 0<=T<=alpha H, the upper bound in (6), and
D(T)=1 below T=1, give D(T)<=H^3/2; thus Phi>=alpha H/2. Combining
these cases gives Phi>=alpha H/2 for all H>=2^(1/3). For the upper
comparison take T0=max(576,77H). Then (6) gives D(T0)>=H^3, so the
integrand vanishes for T>=T0 and Phi<=T0<=460H. Since H<=d^(1/3)=O_C(N)
and Delta^2 is bounded below by a constant times c^2N^2, T0 is inside the
stable range for sufficiently large N depending only on c,C. Finally
d_(a,b) is comparable to N^3 under cN<=a,b<=CN, so H is comparable to
N Q^(-1/3). These loose explicit constants establish the uniform order
claim without assuming the asymptotic (4) at bounded T.

The first nontrivial stable constituent is (1,1): C2(1,1)=3, its zero-
weight multiplicity is two, and its dimension is eight. Thus D(T)=1 for
0<=T<3 and D(3)=17 for every a,b>0. Consequently, for d_(a,b)/2<=Q<=d_(a,b),
the projector lower profile is exactly

  Phi_(a,b)(Q)=3(1-Q/d_(a,b)).                                    (9)

For Q=d-k this profile gives 3k/d. It is weak near full rank; no stronger
near-full-rank all-matrix conclusion follows from this projector argument.

PITFALLS AND CLAIM BOUNDARY

- Count dimensions with the full multiplicity m_(r,s). In particular the
  adjoint occurs twice for every regular (a,b), so D(3)=17, not 9.
- Triality is r congruent s mod 3, equivalently r+2s=0 mod 3. Its index-3
  lattice density is part of the 1/12 coefficient.
- The cutoff in the stable formula is strict: T<min(a+1,b+1)^2. The
  formulas are uniform on the explicit half-cutoff window, not at or above
  its boundary.
- The Casimir and energy constants here use Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2.
  A generator convention scaled by a different factor rescales every
  Casimir threshold and the integrated profile.
- These estimates concern low-Casimir projectors. They do not prove a full
  asymptotic for D(T) up to the top spectrum, nor do they establish a
  matching upper bound for the minimum energy over rank-Q matrices.

As a finite diagnostic only, (1) was cross-checked against the local exact
Littlewood-Richardson multiplicity formula for 4,623 output labels across
all 100 pairs 1<=a,b<=10 lying below the strict cutoff. This check is not
used in the proof.
