## A. The common-witness selector and fractional-cover comparison

**Author statement.** In *Integral and fractional expectation thresholds are equivalent*, Theorem 2.1 takes a non-p-small family F of nonempty subsets and an arbitrary probability mass λ_H supported on each H. Independent colors have probabilities π_i=256^i p for 1≤i≤s, with the remaining mass on s+1 and p∑256^i≤1/2. With probability at least .9, one common H satisfies λ_H(A_i)≥1−2^(−i) for every cumulative color set A_i. Theorem 1.1 then proves q_f≤25·512^4 q, preserving cover budget 1/2.

**Independent reconstruction.** The following four points are the central content, rather than an appeal to the theorem label.

1. Fix a coloring a and an original target H_0. Among colorings z≤a whose changed coordinates lie in H_0, minimize the total number of color levels moved, subject to existence of some unrestricted witness H and truncated inequalities. A feasible move exists by moving H_0 to color 1 and using zero cutoffs. Each resulting changed set lies in H_0, so these sets cover F. For a bad coloring every changed set is nonempty. Non-smallness forces their total p-cost above 1/2.
2. Group pairs (a,z) by the complete final coloring z and all transition counts n_{ih}=|{x:z(x)=i,a(x)=h}|. These counts determine m_i=|A_i(z)\A_i(a)|. Thus one common witness H and its largest admissible cutoffs can be chosen for the whole group, independent of which H_0 selected a particular z. For cutoff ε_i, maximality implies |R_i|≤2^i t, t=total changed coordinates. The right derivative of the truncated inequality is 2^(−i)|R_i|−m_i; a positive derivative would allow a larger admissible cutoff.
3. Every coordinate moved into color i lies in R_i. If not, delaying it one level changes only A_i, lowers its truncated left side by at most ε_i and lowers its charged right side by exactly ε_i. It preserves feasibility and changed-set containment but lowers the minimum objective by one. The complete transition profile is essential: per-scale witnesses alone cannot justify this argument.
4. For row totals t_i, t=∑t_i, the candidate-vertex choices are bounded by ∏binom(2^i t,t_i)≤∏(e4^i)^(t_i). Weighted AM–GM with α_i=t_i/t and ∑2^(−i)≤1 proves this bound. The exact likelihood identity p^t P(a)=P(z)∏(p/π_i)^(t_i)∏π_h^(n_ih) cancels the density price. Summing original-color counts uses the multinomial theorem, then ∑_zP(z)=1. The remaining tuple sum is bounded by ρ/(1−ρ), ρ=∑e/64^i<1/21. This gives bad probability below .1.

The rounding step assigns λ_H by distributing each nonempty fractional coefficient g(S) uniformly across S, then normalizing the mass M_H≥1/2. On the selector event the average color under λ_H is ≤2. At least half of the g-mass in that H is carried by sets of mean color ≤4. Put Y_x=512^(4−a(x)) and Z=∑_{S≠∅}g(S)∏_{x∈S}Y_x. Such sets have product at least one, so E Z≥9/40. Taking p=r/(25·512^4), choosing maximal s, and summing the geometric color probabilities gives E Y_x≤r/5. Independence and nonemptiness of S give E Z≤(1/5)∑g(S)r^|S|≤1/10. The contradiction proves existence of an integral cover. The empty-set fractional coefficient is handled by g(∅)≤1/2 and does not enter Z.

No gap was found in these reconstructed finite combinatorial/probability steps. There is no source claim here of a polynomial-time cover-finding algorithm, efficient exact-marginal rounding, or an algorithm from membership access to F. The proof assumes an arbitrary full family and all its assigned masses are available as mathematical data; minimal moves may require extensive searches.

**Our consequence.** The separate note `HETEROGENEOUS_DISCRETE_CONVEXITY.md` adapts the discrete-convexity proof to different coordinate probabilities. It is a direct reuse of the source mechanism and has not received novelty clearance. A second potential direction is a common-member selector for several assigned masses; adding m masses by averaging incurs a linear m factor in geometric color densities when the truncation error is reduced by m. That extension is not needed by, and is not claimed as a checked theorem in, this cycle's main result.

## B. Discrete convexity and heterogeneous product costs

**Author statement.** *Talagrand's discrete-convexity conjecture*, Theorem 1.1, uses common Bernoulli p, k=2^75, arbitrary D, repetitions allowed, and original p-cost ≤1/2. The proof uses a 32-row signed product identity, a degree-regularized pair-sampling covering lemma, and exact per-row Bernoulli coupling.

The full independent adaptation in the heterogeneous note proves the same conclusion for any vector 0<p_x<1, with costs ∑_I∏_{x∈I}p_x. The signed kernel and global Fourier estimate were reconstructed without calling the companion threshold theorem. Saturated coordinates q_x=min(Lp_x,1)=1 are handled by fixing their section, not by applying an interior-probability Fourier formula at q_x=1. This is the principal new technical consequence from this worker.

**Obstruction.** The source cover uses potentially all Boolean subsets and enormous residual tuples. A cover exists with at most 2^N distinct generators, but its acquisition under sparse/oracle input is not charged by this theorem. The current exact checks exercise identities and a few couplings, not the universal theorem by enumeration.

## C. Requirement compression before Hall selection

**Author statement.** *Graph Decompositions at the Integral Expectation Threshold*, OpenAI, October 5, 2026, Theorem 1.1, partitions the edges of every finite graph into an absolute number of fixed pieces, each with ordinary containment threshold at most L times the integral expectation threshold of the original graph. Pieces may share vertices, and their embeddings need not agree. This theorem depends on the companion discrete-convexity result.

The especially reusable new proof device is Lemma 3.1. For requirements R⊆{T⊆Y:1≤|T|≤s}, greedily select a T when the probability of satisfying current Q conditional on Z⊇T is below 1/2. The union probability increases by more than r^s/2 per addition, so fewer than 2r^(−s) additions occur. At termination every original requirement is satisfied by Q after a fresh Bernoulli-r extension with probability at least 1/2. This is a smoothed implication; it is not a deterministic subfamily covering every original satisfying set.

Proposition 3.2 establishes concentration for every small Y and every compressed Q before choosing an actual graph J or Hall subset U. It then applies the compression lemma to the neighborhood requirements of U. Source test sums imply |U|≤4xm. Target counts give at least (3/4)x_*m candidates, with x_*≥1−(1−x)^16. When x≤1/16 this is ≥6xm; otherwise it is >n/8, the cap on positive-degree outside vertices. Hence Hall holds even though Q was chosen after J and U. Repeated neighborhoods disappear from the requirement family but distinct vertices still contribute separately to the source sum. Those two points prevent a tempting but invalid test-selection shortcut.

The remainder of the paper's layered embedding, star-forest estimates, and deterministic edge splitting was read and checked at the displayed-inequality level. No external independent validation or algorithm for acquiring the typical-family cover is asserted here. The huge absolute n_0 chosen from uniform concentration gives no practical graph-size guarantee in this worker's report.

## D. Thin and cheap trees: conditional outer reduction only

**Author statement.** The constructive thin-tree paper claims deterministic polynomial bit time on binary multiplicities. Corollary 8.1 assumes x_e,c_e≥0 rational and x(δ(S))≥1 for every nontrivial cut, and produces a positive-support tree with cut loads and cost bounded by 24B times their fractional values, B any fixed integer ≥max(1,C) from the thin-tree theorem. No metric assumption on c is needed.

**Checked deduction conditional on the core algorithm.** Compress parallel records to their cheapest edge with positive x, cap each pair's total x-mass at 1, and create floor(2n² y_pair) copies. Every cut retains at least n² edges; total cost is at most 2n² c·x; fewer than n⁴ edge copies occur. Repeatedly extract and delete floor(n²/(6B)) trees using connectivity parameter floor(n²/2). Each cut retains at least half its original size, so each call remains legal. Every extracted tree is 6B-thin relative to x, while the cheapest has cost at most 24B c·x. The small n²<12B case uses any tree. Zero cost and tiny rational x values are handled without division by c·x or expansion of a common denominator.

The principal finite-bit hierarchy/signing construction was not fully read or independently verified by this worker. This outer reduction cannot be adopted as an unconditional algorithm here. It supplies one feasible tree for one nonnegative cost, rather than a distribution with prescribed exact marginals. A colleague is independently examining the dominated-marginal consequence and the exact-marginal obstruction.
