# Finite ellipsoid archive with a conditional Gaussian reference decoder

Status: complete mathematical specification, finite implementation and completed bounded experiment. The asymptotic upper bound below has an elementary analytic proof. Numerical enclosures check listed finite cases and the public compiler; they are not an external proof audit, a lower bound, or novelty clearance.

## Interface and exact archive

Public supplied inputs are a dimension `d`, `A>0`, `alpha>0`, `0<eps<=1`, the coordinate axes, and the known identity covariance. The parameter class is

\[
\Theta_d=\{\theta\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d(\theta_j/a_j)^2\le1\},
\qquad a_j=A j^{-\alpha}.
\]

The encoder observes one draw `X~N_d(theta,I_d)`. It never sees `theta`. Its sole retained input-dependent information is an integer message, or equivalently a vector of finite bin labels. Original observations, posterior values, adaptive choices, hidden real registers, and an additional copy of that vector are not permitted as uncharged retained data.

Retain exactly coordinates `j<=k` where `a_j>eps`. Set

\[
h_j=\epsilon/a_j<1,\quad
T_j=a_j+\sqrt{2\log(8/h_j^2)},\quad
M_j=\lceil2T_j/h_j\rceil,\quad w_j=2T_j/M_j\le h_j.
\]

Partition each active coordinate into `M_j` equal central intervals covering `[-T_j,T_j)` and two rays. Endpoint assignments have probability zero under every source law; the code chooses a left closed convention. The encoder retains the interval index. The decoder independently outputs `N(0,1)` conditioned on that interval. For `j>k` it outputs a fresh unconditioned `N(0,1)`.

The exact classical alphabet and archive sizes are

\[
D=\prod_{j=1}^k(M_j+2),\quad
B_{\rm packed}=\lceil\log_2 D\rceil,\quad
B_{\rm vector}=\sum_{j=1}^k\lceil\log_2(M_j+2)\rceil.
\]

The empty product gives `D=1`, zero bits. Storing both packed and vector forms requires their sum; the public implementation has `encode_packed` to avoid that duplicate. There is no acquired axis list: `k` and all coordinate identities depend only on public design inputs. The implementation supports rational `A,eps` and positive integer `alpha`; the analytic argument supports all `alpha>0`.

## Analytic guarantee and scalar formulas

Write `phi` for the standard Gaussian density and `p_t(B)=N(t,1)(B)`. The fresh scalar output density is

\[
q_\theta(y)=\phi(y)\frac{p_\theta(B(y))}{p_0(B(y))}.
\]

In particular, this decoder preserves the reference law exactly: `q_0=phi`, including the tail bins. It resamples within a bin and is not a pointwise estimate of the input observation.

For any fixed interval `B`, define

\[
F_B(t)=\log\int_B\phi(x)e^{tx}\,dx.
\]

Then `F_B''(t)=Var[N(t,1)|B]` and

\[
D_{\rm KL}(N(\theta,1)|B\Vert N(0,1)|B)
=\theta F'_B(\theta)-F_B(\theta)+F_B(0)
=\int_0^\theta tF_B''(t)\,dt.
\]

For a bounded bin of width `w`, variance is at most `w^2/4`, so this is at most `theta^2 w^2/8`. For either ray it is at most `theta^2/2`. The latter needs no unproved normal-truncation assumption: for a right truncated standard Gaussian at `z`, its variance is `1+z lambda(z)-lambda(z)^2`, where `lambda=phi(z)/Pr[Z>=z]`; `lambda(z)>=z` by the elementary Mills inequality when `z>0` and trivially when `z<=0`. Left rays follow by reflection. Thus the variance is at most one.

Uniformly for `|theta|<=a_j`, the chosen outer cutoff gives

\[
\Pr_\theta(|X|>T_j)\le2e^{-(T_j-a_j)^2/2}=h_j^2/4.
\]

Weighting conditional divergences by bin probabilities yields

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_{\theta,j}\Vert Q_{\theta,j})
\le\theta_j^2h_j^2/8+\theta_j^2\Pr_\theta(|X|>T_j)/2
\le\theta_j^2h_j^2/4.
\]

An inactive axis has exact divergence `theta_j^2/2`. Product additivity and the ellipsoid constraint therefore imply

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_\theta\Vert Q_\theta)
\le\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^d\theta_j^2\min\{1,\epsilon^2/a_j^2\}
\le\epsilon^2/2,
\qquad \|P_\theta-Q_\theta\|_{\rm TV}\le\epsilon/2.
\]

The constants leave a margin for implementation. This is a fresh-output distribution guarantee, not preservation of samplewise squared error. A dropped zero-mean coordinate, for example, has `E[(Y-X)^2]=2` although its output law is exact.

The scalar code evaluates two equivalent exact formulas. First,

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_\theta\Vert Q_\theta)
=\theta^2/2-\sum_Bp_\theta(B)\log\frac{p_\theta(B)}{p_0(B)}.
\]

Second, with `l,u` the bin endpoints, its weighted conditional divergence is

\[
p_\theta(B)D_B
=\theta\{\theta p_\theta(B)+\phi(l-\theta)-\phi(u-\theta)\}
-\tfrac12\theta^2p_\theta(B)
-p_\theta(B)\log\frac{p_\theta(B)}{p_0(B)}.
\]

Infinite endpoint density terms are zero. The experiment checks enclosure agreement, every listed conditional-bin bound, Gaussian masses, tail probabilities, and normalization. It evaluates scalar TV by splitting each bin at the exact likelihood-ratio crossing, with certified enclosures for that crossing.

## Memory order and strongest supported consequence

Set `K=(A/eps)^(1/alpha)`. For active axes `r_j=a_j/eps=(K/j)^alpha>1`. Since `eps<=1`,

\[
M_j+2\le11r_j^2
\]

for exact cutoffs: use `a_j^2/eps=eps r_j^2<=r_j^2` and `sqrt(2log(8r_j^2))<=3r_j`. A certified rational cutoff chosen in `[T_j,T_j+1]` changes 11 to 13. Consequently

\[
\log D\le k\log13+2\alpha[k\log(K/k)+k]
\le[\log13+2\alpha(1+1/e)]K.
\]

This removes the extra `log(1/eps)` incurred by resolving all retained coordinates at the largest-axis precision. It gives a finite blind classical archive with `O_alpha((A/eps)^(1/alpha))` retained bits, uniformly in finite `d`. No matching lower bound is proved in this worker report. In fixed dimension this bin construction does not attain the sharper positive-hat decoder's `eps^(-d/2)` alphabet behavior; that separate construction has a different interface and proof.

The alphabet order is not a whole-system runtime claim. Producing all `d` outputs costs at least `d` coordinate operations. The infinite-sequence statement, if used by the parent, concerns a product-law kernel or finite output queries, rather than generating an infinite physical vector in finite time.

## Deterministic numerical enclosure and public compiler

`certified.py` uses only Python integers and fractions for the enclosing arithmetic. Every arithmetic operation rounds outward to dyadics with denominator `2^1024`. Gaussian normalization uses Machin's identity for pi, alternating arctangent remainders, and integer square-root bounds. The logarithm uses binary range reduction and the convergent `2 atanh(z)` series with a geometric remainder. Gaussian probabilities use the integrated exponential Taylor polynomial

\[
\int_0^z e^{-x^2/2}dx
=\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{(-1)^nz^{2n+1}}{2^nn!(2n+1)}+R_N,
\quad |R_N|\le\frac{z^{2N+3}}{2^{N+1}(N+1)!(2N+3)}.
\]

This remainder is valid even before terms decrease; it follows by integrating Taylor's Lagrange remainder. Roundoff and cancellation are included in the enclosing interval. No adaptive quadrature's error heuristic is accepted as a certificate. The integrator has a 20,000-term guard and fails if a positive probability lacks a positive lower enclosure, rather than silently logging zero.

The code chooses a dyadic `T_j^+>=T_j` at 50 fractional bits and recomputes `M_j=ceil(2T_j^+/h_j)` exactly. Therefore central widths and the conservative tail proof survive endpoint approximation. The experiment cross-checks the enclosing answers against 400-digit mpmath evaluations, which are additional diagnostics rather than the source of the certificates. The elementary interval library has not been formally verified or externally audited.

## Finite output sampler and costs

The ideal continuous conditional decoder need not perform rare-event rejection from `N(0,1)` or store conditional CDF tables. For a central interval `[l,u]`, propose a uniform point and accept with probability `exp(-(x^2-min_{z in [l,u]}z^2)/2)`. This yields exactly the Gaussian conditional law in the real-randomness model. With `r=a_j/eps>=1`,

\[
T_jh_j=\epsilon+\sqrt{2\log(8r^2)}/r
\le1+\sqrt{2\log8},
\]

so acceptance is at least `exp(-T_j h_j-h_j^2/2)` and expected proposals are below 35. The chosen 50-bit upward endpoint rounding changes this by less than the slack to 35. For a right ray `[T,infinity)`, propose `X=T+E` with `E~Exp(rate T)` and accept with probability `exp(-E^2/2)`. Jensen gives acceptance at least `exp(-1/T^2)` and expected proposals at most `exp(1/(2log8))<1.273`; reflect for the left ray. Difference-of-squares evaluation avoids evaluating the extremely small raw Gaussian tail mass. These are exact ideal-real mechanisms, with fresh continuous uniforms and charged arithmetic/scratch registers; they are not literal finite-bit samplers of a continuous law. This table-free preparation observation was supplied by the coordinating `information_statistics` worker and independently checked here.

A bounded finite-bit sampler that outputs literal real values has a finite support. Even an almost-surely terminating unbounded finite-string sampler has countable support. Its TV distance from a non-atomic Gaussian is one. Accordingly, this executable sampler does **not** claim a continuous-output TV guarantee. It compiles the common finite clipping/rounding map applied to both the exact output and target, and proves the guarantee for that finite output alphabet.

The demonstration has `d=8, A=1, alpha=1, eps=1/4`. It uses a common output grid from -6 to 6 with step 1/8 and two clipping cells, giving 98 output labels per coordinate. Each conditional-reference row's Gaussian masses are deterministically enclosed. Its cumulative probabilities are then floored to multiples of `2^-40`. A categorical output uses 40 decoder-fresh fair bits. If a row's cumulative errors are `e_i`, telescoping gives TV at most `sum_i e_i`; these errors, including compiler arithmetic, are explicitly saved. The global kernel error is the sum of the worst row errors for active axes and the reference row errors for the other axes. The experiment also independently verifies the compiled cell-mass L1 discrepancy and exact dyadic normalization.

Let `eta_dec` denote that saved bound. By data processing and mixture contraction, the rounded target's output-law TV is at most `eps/2+eta_dec`. This bound holds uniformly in theta, independently of the displayed finite theta fixtures. The fixtures add deterministic marginal-law comparisons and product upper bounds; no Monte Carlo confidence interval supplies this result.

Compiler tables depend only on supplied public design inputs. A dense implementation stores `(1+sum_j(M_j+2))*(98-1)` cumulative cutoffs, each at most `2^40`, so each needs at most 41 bits. It can select a row and sample with a binary search in 97 entries. Public tables, source code, dependency runtime, table construction, random bits and output registers are real implementation costs despite not increasing the input-dependent archive alphabet. A retained table pointer that encodes a bin index must count as that input-dependent index; it is not an extra free register.

For an actual finite input interface, take a clipped observation grid of spacing `delta_in`, with clipping beyond all outer cutoffs. It differs from the ideal encoder only when rounding crosses a bin boundary. Gaussian density is at most `1/sqrt(2pi)` and each active axis has `M_j+1` boundaries, so

\[
\eta_{\rm enc}\le\delta_{\rm in}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^k(M_j+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]

Thus the finite rounded-output guarantee becomes `eps/2+eta_enc+eta_dec`. For this example, input range [-8,8] and spacing `2^-24` require 29 bits per streamed input coordinate; there are 57 active boundaries. That grid error is below `1.36e-6`. The archive still holds 13 packed bits after input buffers are released. Streaming encoder scratch, exact rational arithmetic intermediates, any duplicate labels, random generator state and decoder output registers must be charged in runtime memory; this Python prototype is not a 13-bit RAM implementation. Code constants use roughly 50 fractional endpoint bits plus integer endpoint and interval-count bits. General table compilation requires probability precision resolving the smallest reference bin mass; for large `A` that can require precision of order `T_max^2+log(1/eta_dec)`. Nothing here establishes acquisition of an unknown basis/covariance or a uniform low-precision compiler for arbitrarily large radii.

## Comparator and falsification boundary

`memory_comparison.csv` compares exact message counts at the same supplied ellipsoid and error budget. The principal control truncates at the same `k` and uses the same conditional reference decoder, but gives every active coordinate the common largest-axis width `eps/A`, with its own valid outer cutoff. This is an equally informed legal archive with the same proved error budget. A second legal comparator retains all 256 coordinates at that common precision. The float64-prefix column is only a raw-register storage count: literal float outputs have continuous TV one, so it is not a competing continuous-law guarantee. Exact raw prefix registers would require infinitely many bits.

Decisive possible falsifications include a conditional KL exceeding the analytic range/one-sided-variance bounds, an underestimated Gaussian tail, retained observation data outside the declared message, non-monotone compiled CDFs, or a compiled table exceeding its saved TV budget. The executable has assertions for the corresponding listed numerical cases. The scalar tests do not certify all untested radii/dimensions; the uniform theorem uses the separate analytic proof.

## Prior-art and scope checks

Conditional-reference reconstruction, exponential-family information loss under quantization, Gaussian truncation and variance bounds are standard ingredients. No originality claim is made for them. The convex-support Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality supplies a more general route to the ray bound; I inspected the relevant inequality and convex-support passage in Barthe and Cordero-Erausquin, *Invariances in variance estimates*, pp. 5–6, [arXiv:1106.5985](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5985). A primary copy is retained here as `primary_variance_1106.5985.pdf`. The explicit truncated-normal API boundary and Gaussian conditioning convention agree with the [SciPy documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.truncnorm.html), but SciPy is not installed or used by the prototype. I inspected the NIST note returned by a truncated-normal search and rejected it as a load-bearing source: it addresses circular complex Gaussian truncation, a different interface.

The prior Astra fixed-dimensional Gaussian card is only a scoped internally reconstructed research record. Its memory exponent and positive-hat construction do not certify this new sequence-law result or its novelty. Likewise the project memory was used for the discipline of separating a reproducibility check from an externally validated theorem, not as proof of any new claim. The parent prior-art worker owns broader priority comparison.

## Completed finite evidence

All assertions passed for 18 scalar configurations and 2,364 conditional bins. The scalar radii were `a=0.3,0.5,1,2,4,8`, with `theta=a/4,a/2,a`. The accuracy was `eps=1/4` for the first five radii and `eps=1/2` for radius 8. The elementary arithmetic, pi, normalization, Gaussian CDF/PDF, exponential and logarithm cross-checks contributed 111 assertions. Every scalar bin's Gaussian probabilities, conditional-KL bound, every scalar tail bound and the two KL formulas were checked. Scalar TV was additionally certified for the first three radii. The numerically tiny reference tail bins remained strictly positive in the enclosing arithmetic.
