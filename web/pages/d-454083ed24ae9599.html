TRANSPORT / ENTROPY BRIDGE AUDIT
Leaf 04 | research date 2026-10-08

STATUS

The candidate implication “bounded entropy / compact targets / a small W2 perturbation imply a source-independent one-third Brenier-map modulus” is false as stated. The evidence below separates four logically different failures:

1. A centered isotropic log-concave law with a fixed finite relative entropy has only exponential tails, not subgaussian tails.
2. For one fixed absolutely-continuous source of density <8, the squared map error divided by target TV is at least 4n along a sequence. Thus no atom-count-independent estimate E <= C TV holds even for a fixed source. This refutes the proposed TV leg, not the W2^(1/3) endpoint.
3. For one fixed compact non-concentrated source, potential stability can fail at every Hölder exponent; and another fixed uniform compact source rules out every map exponent alpha>1/3 (for W2). Neither example rules out alpha=1/3.
4. A different thin-source family shows that no source-density-independent one-third constant can hold, even among log-concave sources on the same square. This is a source-uniform failure; it is not a fixed-source failure. The potentials remain Lipschitz-stable in this family.

The strongest positive statements must be kept in separate evidence classes. The primary family-374 manuscript states the sharp one-third map modulus for a uniform source on a compact convex body. A broader compact-log-concave-source theorem with explicit M_rho dependence is a reconstruction from that source plus the L08 source material, not a theorem attributed to those authors. The full arbitrary bounded-source W2^(1/3) endpoint remains UNKNOWN here. Global novelty and priority remain UNKNOWN.

CONVENTIONS AND TARGET-METRIC FACTS

Let rho be an absolutely continuous probability on source X, let mu,nu be probability measures supported on the same compact target set Y, and write D=diam(Y), T_mu,T_nu for the quadratic Brenier maps,

    E(mu,nu) = integral |T_mu-T_nu|^2 d rho,
    TV(mu,nu) = sup_{Borel A} |mu(A)-nu(A)|.

Thus TV is half the signed-measure variation norm. For every such pair,

    W1 <= W2,       W1 <= D TV,       W2^2 <= D^2 TV,
    W2^2 <= D W1.

For the TV bounds, couple the common part of mass 1-TV identically and couple the residual mass TV arbitrarily inside Y. Conversely, no small-Wasserstein-to-small-TV bound exists: on Y=[0,1], mu=delta_0 and nu_epsilon=delta_epsilon have TV=1 but W1=W2=epsilon. Hence a TV reweighting estimate cannot be inferred from W1/W2 closeness alone. The direction W2^2 <= D^2 TV is a one-way upper bound and does not make TV a small quantity when W2 is small.

COUNTEREXAMPLE A: FINITE ENTROPY DOES NOT GIVE SUBGAUSSIAN TAILS

Let X have density f(x)=2^(-1/2) exp(-sqrt(2)|x|) on R. It is centered, has variance one, and is log-concave. Direct integration gives

    P(|X|>t)=exp(-sqrt(2)t),
    h(X)=1+log(sqrt(2)),
    D(Law(X)||N(0,1)) = (log(pi)-1)/2 < infinity.

The relative entropy is a fixed numerical constant (about 0.0724 nats), not a quantity diverging along a sequence. If a subgaussian tail estimate P(|X|>t) <= 2 exp(-t^2/(2 sigma^2)) held for any finite sigma, then exp(-sqrt(2)t) <= 2 exp(-t^2/(2sigma^2)) for all t; taking logarithms contradicts the quadratic term as t -> infinity. Thus finite/bounded entropy, even with isotropy and log-concavity, does not imply a subgaussian tail estimate. This does not contradict the weaker concentration consequences known for log-concave laws; it blocks the specific entropy-to-psi_2 shortcut.

COUNTEREXAMPLE B: FIXED-SOURCE, UNRESTRICTED-TARGET LINEAR-TV REPAIR FAILS

This reconstructs the exact N60 zigzag construction. It has one fixed source and an increasing finite target cardinality. All target laws are supported on the same square.

Source. For n>=2 set s_n=2^(-n), h_n=s_n/16, w_n=s_n/(4096 n^2),

    R_n=(-w_n,w_n) x (s_n-h_n,s_n+h_n),
    B=(-1/8,1/8) x (1/2,1),
    U=B union (union_{n>=2} R_n), Z=|U|, rho=Z^(-1) 1_U dx.

The vertical intervals of successive R_n are disjoint since the upper endpoint of R_(n+1) is (17/32)s_n and the lower endpoint of R_n is (15/16)s_n; all lie below B. Therefore U is a disjoint union up to boundaries, |B|=1/8, Z>1/8, and f=Z^(-1)<8. The fixed compact source container is X=closure(U) subset [-1,1]^2. This source does not depend on n. It is disconnected and not log-concave.

Targets and maps. Fix n and abbreviate s=s_n,h=h_n,w=w_n,v=x_2-s. Let sigma_i=(-1)^i and z_i=-1/4+i/(2n), 0<=i<=n. Define sites

    a_i=(sigma_i,z_i), 0<=i<=n;     c_-=(0,-1), c_+=(0,1),

all in Y=[-1,1]^2. Set q=h/(100n) and define convex finite affine maxima

    u_n(x)=max( sigma_i x_1+z_i v-h z_i^2 for 0<=i<=n,
                -v-5h/8, v-5h/8 ),
    u'_n(x_1,x_2)=u_n(x_1,x_2-q),
    mu_n=(grad u_n)#rho,  nu_n=(grad u'_n)#rho.

A finite maximum of affine functions is convex. Its gradient selects an active slope almost everywhere; ties lie on finitely many lines and have rho-mass zero. The supporting-plane inequality proves that each selected gradient is the unique quadratic Brenier map to its own pushforward.

Cell geometry on R_n. For |x_1|<=w, the interface between consecutive internal planes i and i+1 has vertical coordinate

    b_i(x_1)=h(z_i+z_(i+1)) - (sigma_(i+1)-sigma_i)x_1/(z_(i+1)-z_i), 0<=i<n.

Since |sigma_(i+1)-sigma_i|=2 and z_(i+1)-z_i=1/(2n), its deviation from h(z_i+z_(i+1)) is at most 4nw=h/(64n). The unperturbed centers are h/n apart, so every consecutive internal cell interval has length at least h/n-2h/(64n)=31h/(32n). The lower and upper cap interfaces are

    b_-(x_1)=-3h/4-(4/3)x_1,
    b_+(x_1)= 3h/4+(4/3)sigma_n x_1.

The internal interfaces lie between -h/2-h/(64n) and h/2+h/(64n), while each cap remains outside them and inside (-h,h), with positive margin because w=h/(256 n^2). Thus every listed cell occurs on R_n. Replacing x_2 by x_2-q shifts every interface by exactly q. Since q=h/(100n)<(31h/(32n))/2, the changed strips are disjoint and remain inside R_n.

No changes elsewhere. On R_m with m<n, v>=7s_m/16 and |x_1|<=s_m/(4096m^2), while h_n<=s_m/32. For the shifted potential the upper cap minus any internal score is bounded below by

    (3/4)(v-q)-5h_n/8-|x_1| > 0.

On R_m with m>n, v<=-15s_n/32 and |x_1|<=s_n/32768; the lower-cap gap is at least

    (3/4)(-v+q)-5h_n/8-|x_1| > 0.

On B, v>=1/4, |x_1|<=1/8 and h_n<=1/64, so the upper-cap gap is at least

    3/16-5/512-1/8-(3/4)q >0.

These strict gaps hold before and after the shift; all other source pieces remain assigned to a cap, so their map values and masses do not change.

Exact target and map costs. Write A=Z^(-1). Each strip has source mass

    delta_n=2 A w_n q_n = A 4^(-n)/(3,276,800 n^3).

Each of the n internal interfaces switches mass delta_n from a_i to a_(i+1); each of the two cap interfaces switches delta_n between its adjacent internal site and cap. Every internal site loses and gains one such strip, so its total mass is unchanged. The bottom cap gains delta_n and the top cap loses delta_n, hence exactly

    nu_n-mu_n = delta_n(delta_(c_-)-delta_(c_+)),
    TV(mu_n,nu_n)=delta_n.

For an internal switch, |a_(i+1)-a_i|^2=4+1/(4n^2). For either cap switch, the squared distance is 1+(3/4)^2=25/16. The strips are disjoint and all have mass delta_n, so

    E(mu_n,nu_n)
      =delta_n [ n(4+1/(4n^2)) + 25/8 ]
      >=4n delta_n.

Consequently E/TV>=4n -> infinity, for the same fixed rho,X,Y and density bound A<8. This is a fixed-source counterexample to every finite constant C in E<=C TV when the target atom count is unrestricted. It has n+3 sites. A direct endpoint coupling also gives W2^2<=4delta_n and W1<=2delta_n; because delta_n decays exponentially, this construction does NOT refute a W2^(1/3) map bound.

Finite-atom comparator (equal interface). If both targets use a fixed set of N common sites of diameter D, write their affine intercepts c_i,d_i and q_i=c_i-d_i. A positive-mass switch i->j forces q_i>q_j except on null tie hyperplanes. The directed switch flow is therefore acyclic. Its divergence is the signed target mass difference; decomposing into deficit-to-surplus paths shows total path weight TV and at most N-1 edges per path. Thus switched off-diagonal mass <=(N-1)TV and

    E<=D^2(N-1)TV.

The N dependence is real: the fixed-source construction transports a delta_n-scale perturbation through n consecutive internal switches while the endpoint target difference remains only delta_n. The equally informed comparison holds the source, containers, and targets' affine-support interface fixed and changes only the number of available sites. There is no charged computation in this analytic counterexample; the source and targets are explicit inputs.

COUNTEREXAMPLE C: POTENTIAL STABILITY CAN FAIL ON A FIXED NON-CONCENTRATED SOURCE

Here is an explicit room-and-passage source, instantiating the obstruction in Letrouit–Mérigot, arXiv:2411.04908v3, Section 5. Put r=1/4 and for n>=1 set

    b_n=1-2^(-n),  c_n=1-(3/4)2^(-n),
    Delta_n=c_n-b_n=2^(-(n+2)),  epsilon_n=Delta_n/8,
    h_n=2^(-(n^3+6)).

Define rooms and passages

    R_1=(0,b_1) x (-r,r),
    R_(n+1)=(c_n,b_(n+1)) x (-r,r),
    P_n=(b_n-epsilon_n,c_n+epsilon_n) x (-h_n,h_n),
    U=(union_n R_n) union (union_n P_n),
    Z=|U|, rho=Z^(-1) 1_U dx.

Here b_(n+1)-c_n=Delta_n, so each next room has area 2r Delta_n. Since epsilon_n+epsilon_(n+1)<Delta_n, passages do not meet one another. Each P_n overlaps R_n on its left and R_(n+1) on its right, so U is open and path-connected. It lies in (0,1) x (-1/4,1/4); its area is finite and positive, hence rho is one fixed compactly supported uniform probability with density 1/Z on U. The rectangle core (b_n,c_n)x(-h_n,h_n) lies in P_n and in no room; it is the only part of U where the two threshold maps below differ. Its rho-mass is exactly

    p_n=rho(P_n intersect ((b_n,c_n)x(-h_n,h_n)))=2 h_n Delta_n/Z.

For each n define convex potentials and target laws

    phi_n(x)=|x_1-b_n|,  phi'_n(x)=|x_1-c_n|,
    mu_n=(grad phi_n)#rho,  nu_n=(grad phi'_n)#rho.

Their maps take values +/-e_1 and are Brenier maps by the convex-gradient optimality criterion. The maps agree except on the passage core, where grad phi_n=+e_1 and grad phi'_n=-e_1. Thus mu_n,nu_n differ by moving exactly p_n mass between two sites at distance 2, so for every finite p>=1,

    W_p(mu_n,nu_n)=2 p_n^(1/p).

Let F_n=phi'_n-phi_n. It equals +Delta_n to the left of b_n, -Delta_n to the right of c_n, and lies in [-Delta_n,Delta_n] in between. The fixed room R_1 is entirely to the left of b_n for all n>=2; R_(n+1) lies to the right of c_n. The pairwise-variance identity gives

    Var_rho(F_n)=(1/2) integral integral |F_n(x)-F_n(y)|^2 d rho(x)d rho(y)
       >=4 Delta_n^2 rho(R_1)rho(R_(n+1))
       =8 r^2 Delta_n^3/Z^2.

This is the squared L2 distance between the centered potentials. Also, the source Poincare constant is infinite: |grad F_n|=2 on the core and 0 elsewhere, so integral |grad F_n|^2 d rho=4p_n=8h_n Delta_n/Z, while Var_rho(F_n)/(integral |grad F_n|^2 d rho)>=r^2 Delta_n^2/(h_n Z)->infinity. For any q>0 and finite p>=1,

    Var_rho(F_n)/W_p(mu_n,nu_n)^(2q)
      >= [8r^2 Delta_n^3/Z^2] / [2^(2q) (2h_n Delta_n/Z)^(2q/p)] -> infinity,

because Delta_n=2^(-(n+2)) while h_n=2^(-(n^3+6)); the cubic exponential in h_n dominates every power of Delta_n. Thus one fixed, bounded, path-connected, uniform source has no W_p-Hölder potential stability estimate at any positive exponent q. The narrow passages make the source fail the concentration/Poincare geometry required by positive stability results. The construction is fully explicit and its room-and-passage mechanism is already present in the primary Letrouit–Mérigot source; no novelty claim is made. For d>2, take the product with the uniform law on [-r,r]^(d-2) and keep potentials dependent only on x_1; all W_p and variance identities remain unchanged.

COUNTEREXAMPLE D: A FIXED UNIFORM SOURCE RULES OUT MAP EXPONENTS ABOVE 1/3

A second primary fixed-source example is Letrouit, arXiv:2510.13265v1, Theorem 1.2 and Sections 3.2–3.4. For d>=2, it constructs one bounded open source X (a countable union of separated paired rectangular cells), rho=uniform(X), and one compact target container Y such that for every p>=1 and alpha>p/[2(p+1)], no C can satisfy

    for all mu,nu in P(Y),  ||T_mu-T_nu||_(L^2(rho)) <= C W_p(mu,nu)^alpha.

For the W2 metric this threshold is alpha>1/3. Construction details: let Q(ell,r)=(0,ell) x (-r/2,r/2) x (-1/2,1/2)^(d-2), T^+(A,ell,r)=A+Q and T^-(A,ell,r)=A-Q. Set A_i^+=(u_i+w_i,0,...,0), A_i^-=(u_i-w_i,0,...,0), S_i=T^+(A_i^+,ell_i,r_i) union T^-(A_i^-,ell_i,r_i), X=union_i S_i. The cells are disjoint and ordered, with

    ell_i=w_i=c0 c1 i^(-2), r_i=c0 2^(-i),
    u_(i+1)-u_i=c0 c2 i^(-2),
    w_i>=100r_i,
    min(u_i-u_(i-1),u_(i+1)-u_i)>=100 max(ell_i,r_i,w_i).

Choose c1,c2 large enough for the two inequalities and c0 so sum_i ell_i r_i=1/2; since |S_i|=2 ell_i r_i, |X|=1 and rho=1_X dx is uniform. All coordinates are bounded, so X is bounded and the target sites below lie in a fixed compact Y.

Put sigma_i=ell_i r_i, B_i^+=(u_i,w_i,0,...), B_i^-=(u_i,-w_i,0,...), C_i^+=(u_i+r_i,w_i,0,...), C_i^-=(u_i-r_i,-w_i,0,...),

    mu=sum_i sigma_i(delta_(B_i^+)+delta_(B_i^-)),
    nu_i=mu+sigma_i(delta_(C_i^+)+delta_(C_i^-)-delta_(B_i^+)-delta_(B_i^-)).

The nearest-site assignment is optimal. Under mu, each of the two rectangles in S_i is split by the sign of x_2: its upper half maps to B_i^+ and its lower half to B_i^-, giving mass sigma_i to each atom. Under nu_i, the rectangle centered at A_i^+ maps to C_i^+ and the rectangle centered at A_i^- maps to C_i^-. The separation condition keeps all other target sites farther, and w_i>=100r_i makes the perturbed corresponding site the closer of C_i^+,C_i^- on its box. Thus each rectangle has half its mass switching between the vertical target levels, and the map error is exactly

    E_i=||T_mu-T_nu_i||_2^2 = sigma_i(2r_i^2+4w_i^2) >=4sigma_i w_i^2.

The natural target coupling moves only the two i-th atoms horizontally by r_i, so the exact upper bound needed here is W_p(mu,nu_i)<=2^(1/p) r_i sigma_i^(1/p). (The source HTML prints the same expression without 2^(1/p); counting both atoms in its own definition gives the factor 2^(1/p). This harmless factor does not affect the theorem's exponent.) Therefore

    E_i/W_p(mu,nu_i)^(2alpha)
      >=4*2^(-2alpha/p) w_i^2 r_i^(-2alpha) sigma_i^(1-2alpha/p)
      = const * i^(-6+4alpha/p) * 2^(-i[1-2alpha/p-2alpha]).

When alpha>p/[2(p+1)], the bracket is negative, and the ratio diverges exponentially. For p=2, every alpha>1/3 fails for one fixed uniform source and compact target set. The endpoint alpha=1/3 is not refuted. This matches, but does not prove, a one-third endpoint for general sources. The source has infinitely many connected components and lacks the log-concave/John concentration structure used by positive results.

COUNTEREXAMPLE E: NO SOURCE-UNIFORM MAP CONSTANT, EVEN FOR LOG-CONCAVE SOURCES

This is the separate L08 thin-source family. Fix K=Y=[-1,1]^2. For n>=1, let

    I_n = sqrt(pi) erf(n)/n,
    f_n(x) = exp(-n^2 x_1^2)/(2 I_n) 1_K(x).

Each rho_n=f_n dx is a probability with log-concave density on the convex square. Let

    mu=1/2(delta_(-e_1)+delta_(e_1)),
    nu_n=1/2(delta_(-(e_1+n^(-1)e_2))+delta_(e_1+n^(-1)e_2)).

The convex potentials are |x_1| and |x_1+x_2/n|; their gradients are the maps by Fenchel equality and symmetry. The straight matching has site displacement 1/n and the crossed matching costs sqrt(4+n^(-2)), so W2(mu,nu_n)=1/n exactly. For uniform x_2, a sign mismatch occurs on a slice of x_1 of length (1-|x_2|)/n; integrating the Gaussian x_1 density yields

    p_n=J/(sqrt(pi) erf(n)),
    J=integral_0^1 (1-r)e^(-r^2) dr
     =sqrt(pi) erf(1)/2-(1-e^(-1))/2 >0.

Equal-sign map differences have squared norm n^(-2); opposite-sign differences have squared norm 4+n^(-2). Therefore

    ||T_mu-T_nu_n||_(L^2(rho_n))^2 = 4 p_n+n^(-2) >=4J/sqrt(pi)>0.

So no constant uniform in rho can satisfy map error <=C W2^alpha for any alpha>0 on these fixed containers, even with two-atom targets. This is a source-uniform failure: the source changes with n. It is not a fixed-source discontinuity. In the same example, |u_mu-u_nu_n|<=1/n pointwise, so potential W2-Lipschitz stability survives. The source concentration profile

    M_rho_n = 1/I_n +1/2 = n/(sqrt(pi) erf(n))+1/2 ~ n/sqrt(pi)

blows up; the one-third constant in the L08 reconstruction scales as M_rho^(1/3), and this order is necessary along the family.

PROPOSED BRIDGE AND EXACT FAILURE SCOPE

Suppose one had both (i) squared map error E<=C W_infinity for a displacement-only target perturbation, and (ii) E<=C TV for a mass reweighting on common sites, with constants independent of target cardinality. Truncating an optimal W2 target coupling at displacement threshold r would leave tail mass at most W2^2/r^2. A D^2 cost for the tail and the two assumed bounds would yield schematically

    E <= C(r + W2^2/r^2).

If the two premises yielded E<=C_1 r+C_2 W2^2/r^2, the exact minimizing choice is r=(2C_2/C_1)^(1/3)W2^(2/3), giving E<=3*2^(-2/3) C_1^(2/3) C_2^(1/3) W2^(2/3), hence map error <=sqrt(3)*2^(-1/3) C_1^(1/3)C_2^(1/6)W2^(1/3). Counterexample B refutes premise (ii) for unrestricted target support cardinality, even for one fixed source. Therefore that proof route is invalid. This does NOT refute the endpoint: neither B nor D produces a fixed-source sequence with E/W2^(2/3)->infinity. The W_infinity premise is also unproved in this audit.

STRONGEST POSITIVE STATEMENTS AND ATTRIBUTION BOUNDARY

A. Direct primary-source statement on a log-concave subclass. The family-374 manuscript “Sharp one-third stability of optimal transport maps” (OpenAI math repository snapshot adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a, 25 Sep 2026) states: if rho is uniform on a compact convex body K with nonempty interior in dimension d>=2, and mu,nu are arbitrary probabilities supported in a fixed compact Y, then

    ||T_mu-T_nu||_(L^2(rho)) <= C_(K,Y) W2(mu,nu)^(1/3).

Its fixed-cube three-atom example proves the exponent cannot be increased: for K=Y=[-1,1]^2 and uniform rho, the source constructs u_a=max(-x_1,x_1,a), u_tilde_a=max(-x_1,x_1,a+(a/2)x_2), with W2=a^(3/2)/2 and squared map distance a/4+a^3/4. Thus every exponent above 1/3 fails. This source is a manuscript/release, not external peer review. Uniform-on-convex-body rho is log-concave; the theorem does not by itself cover every compact log-concave density. Letrouit–Mérigot arXiv:2411.04908v3, Theorem 2.1, provides a compact-source variance inequality when the source density is log-concave (or comparable to a log-concave density); Theorem 1.4 gives potential/map estimates for strongly log-concave full-support laws under its Hessian hypotheses. These are different interfaces and are not an arbitrary-source W2 one-third theorem.

B. Broader compact-log-concave result, internally reconstructed from primary ingredients. For a full-dimensional compactly supported log-concave probability rho=f dx with K=supp(rho) subset B_R, compact target Y subset B_L, and centered convex Brenier potentials (integral u_mu d rho=0), the L08 derivation gives

    ||u_mu-u_nu||_(L^2(rho)) <= C_p W2(mu,nu),  C_p=(2+sqrt(5))R,
    ||T_mu-T_nu||_(L^2(rho)) <= C_m W2(mu,nu)^(1/3),

    C_m=(sqrt(3)/2^(1/3)) (12 d C_p^2)^(1/6) (28 L^2 M_rho)^(1/3),
    M_rho=sum_(j=1)^d integral_(e_j^perp) sup_t f(xi+t e_j) dxi < infinity.

This is not stated as an author theorem in family 374. The potential endpoint was reconstructed from an L08 midpoint/convex-cell argument; the map step layers the family-374 coordinate-slice estimate over log-concave superlevel sets. The density-dependence M_rho is real, as Counterexample E shows. This result should be labeled internally reconstructed, conditional on the source midpoint/coordinate-slice ingredients, with external proof audit and priority UNKNOWN.

C. General fixed-source boundary. The fixed-source family-374/Letrouit example rules out alpha>1/3 for some uniform source, and the TV zigzag breaks one tempting proof. No result here proves or refutes the endpoint

    for every fixed compact absolutely continuous rho: exists C_(rho,Y) such that
    ||T_mu-T_nu||_(L^2(rho)) <= C_(rho,Y) W2(mu,nu)^(1/3)
    for all mu,nu in P(Y).

In particular, bounded entropy of rho or finite target TV relations cannot replace a concentration/geometry hypothesis. This endpoint remains UNKNOWN.

COSTS, BASELINE, FALSIFIER

Charged inputs for all constructions: the explicitly defined source law, its geometric container, and target laws/potentials. Target support cardinality is stated; in B it grows as n+3. No samples, oracle queries, source reconstruction algorithm, numerical solver, or optimization runtime are claimed. The proof cost is analytic and its constants/scales are shown above; there is no hidden acquisition budget.

Equally informed baseline: each comparison uses the same source and target container (for B and LM room-and-passage, one fixed source; for E, same K,Y and target atom count two), with only the target pair changing. In B, the exact TV endpoint move is small while the map switches through n internal sites. In E, the source family is varied and must not be described as fixed-source failure. In Letrouit’s bounded-uniform construction, the source itself is fixed while a single atom pair is perturbed at a time.

Falsifier for the unresolved arbitrary-source endpoint: exhibit one fixed compact absolutely continuous rho and a sequence mu_n,nu_n supported in one fixed compact Y with

    ||T_mu_n-T_nu_n||_(L^2(rho)) / W2(mu_n,nu_n)^(1/3) -> infinity.

Equivalently, E/W2^(2/3)->infinity. The fixed-source TV zigzag does not satisfy this criterion. A valid proof of endpoint would need a replacement for the failed atom-independent TV estimate or a different target-displacement-sensitive mechanism. External review of the exact switches, non-John source theorem, and primary family-374 source is the appropriate next validation step.
