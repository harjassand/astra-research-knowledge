\section{Executable certificates and their scope}
The evidence package includes the dependency-free Python checker
\texttt{reaction\_certificate.py}, a rational example input, and exact
positive and negative controls. The checker verifies a supplied finite
concave piecewise-affine function using exact linear algebra, Farkas
witnesses for containment, rational interval monomials, and subdivision.
Its outcomes are \texttt{CERTIFIED}, \texttt{REFUTED}, and
\texttt{UNKNOWN}; exhausting a subdivision budget does not disprove a
network theorem. Certificate scope explicitly separates invariance,
entry conditional on staying in a supplied box, and global absorption
when the entire positive class is separately certified bounded.

For the two reactions $2A\to A+B$ and $2B\to A+B$, let $x=A$,
$B=1-x$, and let both rates lie in $[1,4]$. Then
\[
 \dot x=k_B(1-x)^2-k_Ax^2,\qquad
 F(x)=\min\{0,x-1/4,3/4-x\}.
\]
For $x\le1/4$, $\dot x\ge(3/4)^2-4(1/4)^2=5/16$.
For $x\ge3/4$, the symmetric inequality is $-\dot x\ge5/16$.
Consequently $[1/4,3/4]$ is a common invariant interval, and every
positive initial state in this conserved class enters it in at most
$(1/4)/(5/16)=4/5$ time units. These inequalities are exactly certified
by the program. Widening the claimed rate box to $[1,16]$ produces
an exact counterexample to this same interval certificate:
$\dot x=-7/16$ at $x=1/4$, $k_A=16$, $k_B=1$.
This refutes the candidate interval, not the network's permanence.

Strict margins allow fixed-scale rationalization and exhaustive
certificate search for safety of a supplied compact initial range.
A finite chain can certify entry from a supplied compact range.
No efficient all-network synthesis bound is proved. In particular,
a finite inward-facet certificate alone does not verify a global basin
on an unbounded class; the classwide absorption theorem above, or a
separate global certificate, is still required. Fixed-scale rational
approximation does not by itself yield a rational all-scale compiler.
The source affine lemma, full theorem and finite examples have been
reviewed internally; neither successful code execution nor document
compilation is a substitute for the general mathematical proof.
