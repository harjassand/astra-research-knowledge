PRIMARY RESULT: ALL-ENDOTACTIC PERMANENCE

Let a finite exponent-arrow diagram have sources y and increments nu_e, with actual dynamics

where 0 < kappa <= k_e(t) <= K < infinity are arbitrary measurable functions. Real source exponents are allowed on the positive orthant; conventional molecular mass action is the integer nonnegative special case.

Endotacticity applies to the actual diagram: for every w, among essential reactions with w.nu_e != 0, every essential reaction at a maximal w-source has w.nu_e < 0. Tangent edges need not point strictly inward. For S=span{nu_e}, each positive class P=(c+S) intersect R^d_{>0} has ONE compact convex positive invariant absorber, independent of initial point and rate schedule. Every positive initial point gives a unique global positive trajectory and enters this absorber in finite time, uniformly over rate schedules for that initial point. This is not a single entry time uniform over an unbounded class.

Mechanism. Release family149 supplies a finite concave affine-minimum construction with arbitrary positive direction-dependent tolerance, including discontinuous tolerance. The manuscript reconstructs it fully. If a nonorthogonal slope r is active at x=h^p, active comparisons force x^u <= h^H x^w whenever r.(u-w)>0, and nonzero projected increments have magnitude at least 2H. Select a lowest r-essential source w. Endotacticity provides a positive projected reaction from that tier; every negative projected reaction starts strictly higher. For L=sum_e ||nu_e||_1 and M=max_e ||y_e||_1,
  r.f >= x^w (2 kappa H - K L h^H) >= kappa H h^M,
once K L h^H <= kappa H. This weighted argument transfers the source mechanism beyond fixed-rate weak reversibility.

The finite affine minimum obeys the almost-everywhere chain rule along absolutely continuous solutions. Orthogonal slopes cannot be active below the plateau in P. Compact positive plateaux and capped square-root scale transitions prove absorption and continuation without assuming nested plateaux. Negative source exponents are handled on compact positive boxes.
