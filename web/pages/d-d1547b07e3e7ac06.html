# Randomized single-current obstruction in physical conductance error

2026-10-06. Inverse-team lead reconstruction combining r02's spherical-prior experiment with r09's complete-network realization. The posterior-variance metric transfer below is an additional deduction. This is a universal finite passive-network theorem; it is not a continuum PDE theorem, and novelty is unverified.

## Physical interface

Fix n≥2 current coordinates, N=n+1 exposed terminals and D=n(n+1)/2 edges. A known isometry U:R^n→1⊥ fixes physical mean-zero current and voltage coordinates. The admitted unknown A lies in the Frobenius ball in Sym(n), ||A||_F≤R, where R>0. Choose τ>0 with τR≤1/(4N²), and define

    b=1/N,  F_A=bI+τA,  Λ_A=U F_A⁻¹ Uᵀ,
    c_ij(A)=−(Λ_A)_ij,  i<j.

These are complete-graph conductances with all vertices exposed. They form a continuous D-dimensional nonlinear parameter image. At A=0, Λ₀=NP=NI−11ᵀ, so each edge conductance is one. The resolvent identity yields

    ||Λ_A−Λ₀||_op ≤ τN²R/(1−τNR) < 1/3.

Thus each c_ij(A)>2/3. Symmetry and zero row sums make Λ_A exactly the graph Laplacian. The current-to-voltage map in U coordinates is precisely F_A. Currents Ug and detector vectors Uh preserve Euclidean norm and total-current zero.

A single current g may be randomized independently of A, with its realized value revealed, and ||g||²≤E. All detectors may depend on the known model and g. Any finite collection of noisy scalar readings from that current is a statistical garbling of the exact vector Ag after subtraction of the known baseline. This remains true with arbitrary repeated or nonorthogonal unit detectors and any independent noise level. No adaptive change of source current is permitted. Loss is Euclidean squared error in the physical edge table c(A), not merely response error.

## Conductance metric constants

For A,B in the ball, inverse congruence and singular-value bounds give

    τ||A−B||_F/(b+τR)² ≤ ||Λ_A−Λ_B||_F
                          ≤ τ||A−B||_F/(b−τR)².

For a complete-graph edge contrast δc, each off-diagonal occurs twice and each diagonal is a sum of incident edges. Therefore

    √2||δc||₂ ≤ ||δΛ||_F ≤ √(2N)||δc||₂.

The upper inequality follows from Cauchy–Schwarz on the N−1 incident changes in each diagonal. Consequently

    l||A−B||_F ≤ ||c(A)−c(B)||₂ ≤ L||A−B||_F,
    l=τ/[√(2N)(b+τR)²],  L=τ/[√2(b−τR)²].

Dimension, signal scale and contrast range appear explicitly in l,L. No dimension-independent equivalence is claimed.

## Randomized minimax lower bound

For every admitted single-current design and every estimator of the edge table,

    sup_{||A||_F≤R} E ||c_hat−c(A)||₂²
        ≥ l² R²(D−n)/D.                              (1)

This is independent of noise and finite detector count. In contrast to a worst pair chosen after a random current, the same prior proves (1) for all randomized designs.

Proof. Use the uniform spherical prior ||A||_F=R, independent of the design. Condition on all realized design randomness and grant the exact vector Ag. If g≠0, the linear map A↦Ag has rank n on Sym(n). Isotropy and reflection symmetry of the sphere imply that its Bayes squared Frobenius risk is R²(D−n)/D. If g=0, risk is R² and the same lower bound holds.

Let A,A' be conditionally independent draws from the posterior given Ag. For any Euclidean-valued unknown X, posterior squared-error Bayes risk equals half the posterior expectation of ||X−X'||². Hence the conductance Bayes risk given Ag is

    ½ E[||c(A)−c(A')||₂² | Ag]
       ≥ l² · ½ E[||A−A'||_F² | Ag].

Average over Ag and then the known random design. The original finite data are a garbling of Ag, so cannot have lower Bayes risk. The spherical prior is supported on the fixed admitted network class, and Bayes risk bounds frequentist minimax risk from below. This proves (1), without projecting an arbitrary edge estimate back into the model image and without a factor-four projection loss. QED.

## Matched multiple-current upper bound

Apply n deterministic currents g_j=√(E/n)e_j and n unit orthonormal voltage detectors to each. Total current norm budget is E and exactly n² independent Gaussian scalar readings are acquired. Let their noise standard deviation be σ and s=√(E/n). The matrix observation is

    Y=s(bI+τA)+σZ.

The estimator A_raw=[sym(Y)/s−bI]/τ has risk σ²nD/(τ²E). Project it in Frobenius norm onto the radius-R ball to obtain A_hat, then output the explicit conductance table c(A_hat). Projection cannot increase A error and the Lipschitz upper bound yields

    sup_A E||c(A_hat)−c(A)||₂²
        ≤ L² σ² nD/(τ²E).                            (2)

Inversion and table extraction cost O(n³) arithmetic; the known isometry and admitted family are supplied. Source settings are n versus one. Both arms may use the same n² scalar budget and total squared current-amplitude budget E. This is not model-dependent Joule dissipation. The single arm may repeat detectors; even its exact noiseless Ag is insufficient.

For any fixed n,R,τ,E satisfying the physical condition, (1) is positive because D>n. The multiple-current bound (2) tends to zero as σ→0. Therefore this fixed finite physical family has arbitrarily large noisy recovery separation under the matched budgets. Unlike the weighted infinite-block construction, no family spectrum needs to change as noise decreases: a finite single source has an exact invisible subspace.

## Scope, provenance and falsifiable extension

The topology is supplied, generally nonplanar and fully accessible. The edge parameter class is the nonlinear conductance image c(A), rather than an independent rectangular edge box. The theorem does not involve hidden interior nodes, circular-planar constraints, unknown topology, continuum conductivity, adaptive source selection or excitation-switching cost equalization.
