# Exact CL law cannot equal the displayed answer-reduction law

Status: elementary exact arithmetic obstruction reconstructed here. It concerns the equality of two specified sampler laws, not the validity of Lin's complexity-class theorem.

## Finite dyadic law lemma

A (k,m,p)-CL sampler in Lin Definition 5.5 draws one seed uniformly from F_(2^p)^m and returns deterministic conditionally linear maps L^A(seed), L^B(seed). For any recognizable output event E,

    Pr[E] = |{seed : output(seed) in E}| / 2^(p m).

Every such probability is dyadic. The CL levels and the conditional linear maps do not alter this counting fact.

For all integers N>=0 and K, K/2^N cannot equal 1/3: equality would imply 3K=2^N, contradicting unique prime factorization. Thus no exact finite CL sampler has a recognizable event of probability 1/3.

The proved comparison survives dyadic repairs. Example: a four-label role selector gives each role mass 1/4; if a separately detyped low-degree subtest has a private invalid-prefix probability at least 1-q_LD, and its invalid branch erases all low-degree shared outputs while retaining no extra common register, then
