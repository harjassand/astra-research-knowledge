Deterministic statement

Work on the nearest-neighbor square lattice. Let H_i be the retained
outermost filled hulls of the bad star-components after a fixed finite
thickening. A hull contains its bad component and all bounded complementary
components. Merge touching hulls, and discard nested non-outermost hulls.
Choose the fixed thickening large enough that each retained hull has a
nearest-neighbor connected exterior good rim and the rim neighborhoods of
distinct hulls have bounded overlap. No nearest-neighbor edge joins two
different filled hulls. These are the standard square-cell contour facts:
the union of the bad unit cells in a star-component is connected; its
outermost rectilinear contour has at most 4|H_i| sides; a corner pinch is
resolved by a one-layer exterior detour. The detour stays good because of the
fixed thickening. Walking the contour lists its exterior rim sites; whenever
two consecutive sites meet only diagonally at a corner, insert the exterior
corner site to turn that step into two nearest-neighbor steps. The resulting
path remains in the fixed exterior layer. Since the hull lies in a box of
side d_i+O(1),

\[
 |H_i|+|\partial H_i|+|\partial_{\rm ext}H_i|
       \le C(d_i+1)^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{length}(\gamma)\le C(d_i+1)^2
\]

for a rim path \(\gamma\) between any two rim sites. All constants depend
only on the fixed thickening and the square lattice. The same construction
gives uniformly bounded rim overlap. If a selected thickening causes two
obstacles to touch, regard them as one hull; the frozen sparse attack gives
the same animal estimate for that fixed thickening.

Let \(G\) be the available good sites outside the filled hulls, and let
\(G_E\) be nearest-neighbor edges with both endpoints in \(G\). For a field
\(u\) on \(G\), choose one surrounding good rim site \(a_i\) per hull and
set \(v=u\) outside the hulls and \(v\equiv u(a_i)\) on \(H_i\). Define

\[
 d_z=\max\{d_i:z\in H_i\text{ or }z\text{ is a nearest-neighbor
                 neighbor of }H_i\},\quad d_z=0\text{ if none},
 \qquad d_e=\max(d_x,d_y).
\]

Fix 1<p<2 and positive edge weights a_e>0 on G_E. Let
\(E=\sum_{e\in G_E}a_e|\nabla u(e)|^2\). There are nonnegative coefficients
\(W_e\) such that

\[
 \sum_{e\text{ in the target box}}|\nabla v(e)|^p
       \le \sum_{e\in G_E}W_e|\nabla u(e)|^p,
 \qquad W_e\le C_p(1+d_e)^{2p},
\]

and hence

\[
 \|\nabla v\|_{\ell^p}
 \le E^{1/2}
   \left(\sum_{e\in G_E}
       W_e^{2/(2-p)}a_e^{-p/(2-p)}\right)^{(2-p)/(2p)}.
\]

The norm on the left is over target edges; (G_E) and its energy include the
larger buffer needed to contain every rim path. The constant C_p is
uniform in the box size, hole sizes, field, and weights. It depends only on
p, the fixed contour enlargement, and its bounded-overlap constant.
If the normalized spatial sum of
\((1+d_e)^{4p/(2-p)}a_e^{-p/(2-p)}\) is bounded by M, this gives
\(\|\nabla v\|_{\ell^p}\le C_p M^{(2-p)/(2p)}N^{2/p-1}\sqrt E\)
on a target square of side N. For a_e=1, the needed hole moment order is
4p/(2-p). The exponential tail below supplies every fixed
one-site moment; it does not by itself assert a deterministic uniform bound
on spatial averages of those moments.

If, in addition, u is the restriction of a globally K-Lipschitz
field, then

\[
 |u(z)-v(z)|\le C K(1+d_z)
\]

at every site where u is defined. This is a pointwise filling-error
bound, not an exact boundary-value assertion.

Proof of the weighted Dirichlet comparison

Good-good edges outside hulls keep their original difference; edges inside
one hull have zero difference. Consider a crossing edge e={x,y} with
x outside H_i and y in H_i. Then
\(\nabla v(e)=u(x)-u(a_i)\). Both x and a_i lie on the exterior
good rim. Follow a rim path \(\gamma_{e,i}\) between them. The path uses only
good-good edges and has at most C(d_i+1)^2 edges, so

\[
 |\nabla v(e)|^p
 \le |\gamma_{e,i}|^{p-1}
       \sum_{f\in\gamma_{e,i}}|\nabla u(f)|^p.
\]

There are at most 4|H_i| <= C(d_i+1)^2 crossing edges. For a fixed
rim edge f, its coefficient from this hull is at most

\[
 C(d_i+1)^{2(p-1)}\,C(d_i+1)^2
       \le C_p(d_i+1)^{2p}.
\]

The fixed rim-overlap bound lets us sum over hulls. Every hull whose chosen
rim uses f is incident to an endpoint of f, after the fixed
one-layer contour convention, so d_i<=d_f. Put the displayed
coefficient into W_f, and put coefficient one on unchanged good edges.
This proves the first inequality. Hölder with exponents 2/p and
\(2/(2-p)\) gives

\[
 \sum W_e|\nabla u(e)|^p
 \le E^{p/2}
       \left(\sum W_e^{2/(2-p)}a_e^{-p/(2-p)}\right)^{(2-p)/2};
\]

taking the p-th root gives the stated formula. No energy on bad or
filled-hull edges is assumed; all new jumps are charged to original good
rim edges. The pointwise error follows because the anchor is at distance at
most (C(d_i+1)) from any site in its hull.

On a filled target square Q_N, the usual row-and-column path argument also
gives

\[
 \|v-\overline v_{Q_N}\|_{\ell^p(Q_N)}
       \le C N\|\nabla v\|_{\ell^p(E(Q_N))}.
\]

If v vanishes on the outer one-site boundary of a larger square, the same
inequality holds without subtracting the mean, by telescoping each row from
its zero boundary value. Combining either form with the weighted bound above
is the discrete Poincare/Dirichlet comparison. For the piecewise-affine
rescaling V_N(x/N)=v(x), norm equivalence on the fixed square triangulation
gives \(\|\nabla V_N\|_{L^p}\asymp N^{1-2/p}\|\nabla v\|_{\ell^p}\).
This exactly cancels the factor \(N^{2/p-1}\) in the scaled Dirichlet bound.

The square sparse attack supplies the required hole-tail input. For a fixed
thickening, it proves Pr(A subset B+) <= rho^|A| for every finite
set of eligible coarse blocks, with ρ as small as desired. The number of
star-connected animals of size m containing a fixed root is at most
\(8^{2(m-1)}\le64^m\), by encoding a deterministic depth-first traversal.
Thus a rooted bad-component size tail is at most \((64\rho)^m\). If a
filled hull of diameter at least r is incident to z, its enclosing
bad component has at least c r sites: its filled hull lies in the component's
coordinate bounding box. Since z is in or adjacent to that hull, a root lies
within distance C m of z. There are at most C(m+1)^2 such roots. Consequently,
when the per-set parameter is retuned so 64 rho < 1,

\[
 \Pr(d_z\ge r)
       \le \sum_{m\ge cr}C(m+1)^2(64\rho)^m
       \le C'e^{-c'r}.
\]

This is uniform for eligible sites whose fixed enlarged patches lie inside
the random part of the volume. It does not cover deterministic pin-collar
blocks as random bad sites. Fixed thickenings are permitted because the
frozen sparse attack supplies their per-set bound after choosing a smaller
starting parameter. No independence between blocks is used.

Exact boundary and pinning exclusions

The ordinary single-anchor fill is not required to preserve pins. If an
internal pin lies in a filled hull, its value is replaced by the chosen rim
value. The 3-by-3 bad-ring check in `finite_checks.py` is an exact example:
the center is a pin of value 1, the surrounding good anchor has value 0,
and the globally 1-Lipschitz field remains feasible before filling, but the
ordinary fill changes the pin to 0. Therefore the base lemma applies to an
interior target square with a larger buffer and with no requirement that
arbitrary pins inside filled hulls be preserved. For an exact-pinning use,
either require every filled hull to avoid all pinned vertices, or use a
boundary-compatible construction under stronger assumptions.

The zero-exterior variant has the following precise scope. Let D be a finite
simply connected square-grid polygon whose boundary vertices form one
nearest-neighbor cycle and whose exterior is nearest-neighbor connected to
infinity. Include in the boundary vertex set every interior endpoint of an
edge crossing from D to its exterior. Set E_D to be these boundary vertices
and all vertices outside the interior of D. Assume u=0 on E_D, its zero
extension is globally K-Lipschitz, and the good-edge energy and inverse-
weight bounds include every edge incident to the fixed boundary. For a hull
meeting E_D, choose a fixed deterministic E_D-path from a site of the hull
to infinity, take its first exterior rim vertex, and fill that hull with
zero. Fill hulls disjoint from E_D by a good
rim value. The same path-counting proof gives the same weighted gradient
bound and preserves v=0 on E_D; edges crossing the polygon boundary are
included in the rim-energy accounting. For fields with identical boundary
data, apply this construction to their difference, which is zero on the
boundary. Affine interpolation on a conforming square triangulation then
vanishes outside D.
This variant does not cover arbitrary disconnected internal observed sets,
nonzero pins inside a hull, or polygons with a disconnected zero exterior.
It also requires a buffer for the random bad blocks; hulls that reach the
outer buffer boundary are excluded from the deterministic application.
Under the frozen exponential diameter tail, for an interior region of area
O(N^2) and buffer width b, the probability that some incident hole reaches
the buffer is at most C N^2 exp(-c b); choosing b proportional to N makes
this smaller than any fixed inverse power of N. A fixed pin collar remains
deterministic and is handled by the zero-exterior rule or excluded from the
random-hole estimate.

For square polygons, the boundary-preserving statement is derived from the
source's `c:exterior-zero` construction with the triangular diagonal
detour removed. It is an internal geometric reconstruction; the pinned
source's stated lemma itself is triangular-lattice. The prior comparison
audit explicitly did not establish a square GFF theorem.

Status, verification, and limits

The weighted path inequality and Hölder step are proved above for the stated
square contour interface with uniform constants. The square bad-hull
diameter tail follows from the frozen sparse per-set animal estimate. The
claim is conditional on the fixed-thickening contour construction and on
separate inverse-weight moments; sparsity alone does not bound
\(a_e^{-1}\). No spatial macromoment concentration is claimed. The exact
internal-pin counterexample confirms why the ordinary fill cannot be used
as a boundary-preserving operation. No simulation, formal replay, external
review, or whole-field theorem verification was performed.
