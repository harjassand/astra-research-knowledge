# A one-setting repair for an exact quantum-response observation fibre

## Question and scope

The selected decision task is a targeted screen at the N768 operating point
`V0 = 1109/512`: decide whether the certified infinite-chain transport
coefficient `D(V0)` is above or below `0.603`, under the promise that the
device is one of two exact parameter points from the N768 dossier. This is a
binary response decision, not reconstruction of the many-body state, the
Hamiltonian, or the response function.

N768 already supplies the local six-spin Ramsey access contract and an
independent source-reported global-regret policy for choosing a near-optimal
`V` without estimating `U` or `d`. That policy is sufficient if the only goal
is to act. The present result addresses a different question: whether the
absolute certified response at `V0` clears a specified threshold. For this
question the original single-quadrature data are exactly insufficient, while
one additional local readout axis is sufficient.

All statements about the macroscopic `D(V0)` intervals below are conditional
on the N768 source's internally certified enclosures. N768 remains
`source_derived_unreviewed`; this work does not independently validate the
infinite-chain response theorem or establish laboratory performance.

## Exact two-model family and accessible operation

Let `a` be either `-1/100` or `+1/100`, and set

\[
 U_a=4+a,\qquad
 d_a=2+2\log\!\left(\cos(a/2)+\frac{255}{32}\sin(a/2)\right).
\]

The common local assay is the N768 six-spin preparation: freeze the incident
hopping on the block, prepare the central pair in
`(|10>+|01>)/sqrt(2)`, set the four neighbours to `(1,1,0,0)`, evolve for
`tau=1/2`, and measure the logical coherence. The supplied model has `t=1`,
`U` in `[3.9,4.1]`, physical dephasing `d` in `[1.9,2.1]`, and `V` in
`[0,3]`; the probe phase is independent of `V` and the two-site coherence
decays at rate `d`.

Write

\[
 \psi=\operatorname{atan2}(-255,32),\quad
 \cos\psi=32/257,\quad \sin\psi=-255/257,
\]

and `x=a/2`. The original analyzer measures logical `X` and has mean

\[
 q_a=e^{-d_a/2}\cos(\psi+x).
\]

The added analyzer measures logical `Y` on a fresh, identically prepared
specimen at the same interrogation time, with mean

\[
 y_a=e^{-d_a/2}\sin(\psi+x).
\]

Implementing this change requires a `pi/2` readout-axis rotation relative to
the existing Ramsey analyzer. It changes one local measurement setting; it
does not expose the full many-body state or a channel Choi matrix.

## Exact non-identifiability with the original setting

Define

\[
 g(x)=\cos x+\frac{255}{32}\sin x.
\]

Since `d_a=2+2 log g(x)`, `e^{-d_a/2}=e^{-1}/g(x)`. Also,

\[
 \cos(\psi+x)=\frac{32\cos x+255\sin x}{257}
 =\frac{32}{257}g(x).
\]

Therefore, for either sign of `a`,

\[
 \boxed{q_a=e^{-1}\frac{32}{257}.}
\]

Each original `X` outcome is in `{−1,+1}` with probability
`P(X=+1)=(1+q_a)/2`, so the entire single-shot law is identical at the two
models. Every finite product law is then identical. More generally, a
randomized or adaptive decision rule whose only informative query is this
same `X` setting has the same transcript distribution under both hypotheses:
condition on each past transcript and the next `X` outcome law is still the
same. For any binary classifier, its two conditional error probabilities sum
to one. Its worst-case error is at least `1/2`, regardless of shot count.

The N768 response enclosures put the two models on opposite sides of the
chosen threshold:

\[
 D_{U_-,d_-}(V0)\in[0.6134535792,0.6137844323],\qquad
 D_{U_+,d_+}(V0)\in[0.5928974757,0.5931093049].
\]

Thus `D(V0)>0.603` is exactly the `a<0` hypothesis within this two-point
promise. The indistinguishability result is a no-go for this threshold under
the one-contrast information interface, not a claim that the two physical
models are indistinguishable by all possible experiments.

## The minimum added setting and its exact signal gap

For the `Y` readout, direct substitution gives

\[
 y_a=\frac{e^{-1}}{257}
 \frac{-255\cos x+32\sin x}
      {\cos x+(255/32)\sin x}.
\]

Let `t=tan(1/200)>0`. Dividing numerator and denominator by `cos x` and
subtracting the two signs yields

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_Y:=y_{+}-y_{-}
 =e^{-1}\frac{32\,132098\,t}
 {257(1024-65025t^2)} >0.}
\]

The numerical values are

\[
 y_-\approx-0.3804023817586985,\quad
 y_+\approx-0.3508098382579005,\quad
 \Delta_Y\approx0.0295925435007980.
\]

For a rational lower bound, alternating Taylor bounds give
`sin(x) > x-x^3/6`, `cos(x) < 1-x^2/2+x^4/24`, and therefore
`tan(x) > (x-x^3/6)/(1-x^2/2+x^4/24)`. Also `sin(x)<x` and
`cos(x)>1-x^2/2` give `tan(x)<x/(1-x^2/2)<1/100`, so the denominator in
the gap formula is positive. The exponential series through `1/6!`, with
the remaining tail bounded by `1/4410`, gives `e<2.7183`. Since
`t/(1024-65025 t^2)` is increasing on this interval, these rational bounds
prove `Delta_Y > 0.02959` without using the displayed floating-point values.

The midpoint threshold is approximately `-0.3656061100082995`. Because
`Delta_Y` is nonzero, one new observable setting suffices. Zero new settings
cannot suffice by the preceding identical-law argument. Hence one additional
setting is minimal among experiments that retain the supplied `X` assay and
add a finite number of distinct single-copy readout axes.

This minimum is specific to the two-model promise. It does not say that one
additional scalar measurement identifies an arbitrary point in the full
`(U,d)` box. For that broader two-parameter identification problem, the
orthogonal pair of means

\[
 (q,y)=r(\cos\phi,\sin\phi),\quad
 r=e^{-d/2},\quad \phi=(U-4)/2+\psi
\]

is injective on the stated box: `d=-2 log(sqrt(q^2+y^2))` and
`U=4+2(atan2(y,q)-psi)`, with the continuous phase branch selected by
`U in [3.9,4.1]`. For general `tau`, the Jacobian columns of `(q,y)` with
respect to `(U,d)` are `tau*r*(-sin(phi),cos(phi))` and
`-tau*r*(cos(phi),sin(phi))`; they are orthogonal and both have length
`tau*r`. At `tau=1/2` and `d in [1.9,2.1]`, the smallest singular value is
at least `e^-1.05/2 > 0.1749`, so the local inverse sensitivity is below
`5.72` in Euclidean norm. For an exact rational verification, use `e<2.7183`
and bound the tail of `exp(1/20)` after its quadratic term by its cubic term
times `1/(1-1/80)`; their product is less than `1/0.3498`. By contrast, the
single-`X` Jacobian has rank one
and infinite condition number for the two-parameter inverse problem. This
reconstruction is not needed by the threshold test; its pairwise finite-shot
gap is the relevant conditioning certificate for the one-bit decision.

## Finite-shot certificate and acquired resources

Use `n=50,000` independent `Y` shots from the six-spin preparation and
classify as the high-response model when the empirical mean is below the
midpoint of the two ideal `Y` means (the `a<0` model has the smaller `Y`
mean). Assume the total systematic displacement
of either model's `Y` expectation is at most `b=10^-4`, including readout-axis,
preparation, switching, timing and readout errors. Then the actual means remain
separated by more than `0.02959-0.0002 > 0.02939`. Each outcome is bounded by
one in magnitude, so Hoeffding's inequality gives

\[
 P(\text{misclassification})
 \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{n(0.02939)^2}{8}\right)
 < 2e^{-5.3}<0.01.
\]

The last inequality follows from the positive rational Taylor partial sum
`sum_{k=0}^{13} 5.3^k/k! > 200`. The exact rational checks are in
`assay_cost.py`; its floating-point outputs are only readable numerical
diagnostics.

At `tau=1/2`, the lower-bound interrogation cost is `25,000` native time
units. The total operation also needs 50,000 fresh local preparations,
resets, block switching/freeze operations and `Y`-axis readouts. Their time,
energy, yield and calibration costs are unknown. The original N768 `10^-4`
mean-bias allowance was for its `X` assay; it does not certify the new `Y`
axis. As a standalone axis-error budget, `|delta_phi| <= 2.5e-4` radians
contributes at most `e^-0.95 |delta_phi| < 10^-4` to the mean; other error
sources share the same total bias allowance. Independence and the physical
validity of the reduced local probe remain acquisition requirements.

This is a targeted threshold decision, not a cost advantage over N768's
near-optimal-`V` policy. N768's one-contrast policy uses 262,144 shots and
reports global regret below `8.7804e-5` under its assumptions; that policy is
already the right access route if action quality is the only objective. The
50,000-shot `Y` test answers the different, narrower question of which side of
the `D(V0)=0.603` threshold the promised model occupies. The shot counts have
different outputs and do not establish a speedup.

## SLD and channel-order relation

For the local logical qubit, the output state is

\[
 \rho_{U,d}=\tfrac12(I+r(\cos\phi\,X+\sin\phi\,Y)),\quad
 r=e^{-d/2},\quad \phi=(U-4)/2+\psi.
\]

At a general `tau`, replace `r` by `e^{-d tau}` and the `U` derivative of
`phi` by `tau`. The one-qubit SLDs for `U,d` are

\[
 L_U=\tau r(-\sin\phi\,X+\cos\phi\,Y),\qquad
 L_d=\tau\frac{r^2I-r(\cos\phi\,X+\sin\phi\,Y)}{1-r^2}.
\]

They give

\[
 F^{SLD}(U,d)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \tau^2r^2 & 0\\
  0 & \tau^2r^2/(1-r^2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is the ordinary SLD information matrix for the reduced qubit state with
no reference system. A single `X` measurement has classical Fisher matrix
`grad(q) grad(q)^T/(1-q^2)`, hence rank one; it discards one local direction.
The `Y` analyzer restores the component needed for this exact pair. At the
two-model level it is also the one-copy Helstrom measurement: the states
satisfy `rho_+ - rho_- = (Delta_Y/2) Y`, so the positive/negative eigenspaces
of the difference are exactly the `Y` outcomes. This establishes
single-copy measurement optimality for equal-prior discrimination, not
collective multi-copy sample optimality.

Do not identify this calculation with N661's source-reported *complete SLD
channel order*. N661 quantifies every finite untouched reference and all
input state pairs, and its finite sufficient lift certificate assumes the
unnormalized Choi matrices are supplied. It does not provide an
acquisition-efficient way to obtain those matrices from the six-spin assay.
The general complete-order reconstruction gate remains open. Here a direct
two-dimensional state calculation is sufficient; no Choi oracle, complete
channel reconstruction or unavailable encoder is assumed.

## Prior art and remaining boundary

An orthogonal Ramsey quadrature and the one-qubit SLD calculation are standard
quantum estimation tools. N768 itself notes that a second fine quadrature can
distinguish its exact one-contrast pair; its source also cites semiparametric
quantum estimation and active Hamiltonian learning. The present contribution
is the exact task-specific `Y` gap, a rigorous finite-shot threshold test with
an explicit added-axis bias budget, and the proof that this single added
setting repairs the stated two-point decision fibre. No general novelty or
capability claim is made.

The primary literature already includes quantum semiparametric bounds for
functionals with nuisance parameters and efficient local Hamiltonian learning.
Those works are not matched sample-cost baselines for this two-hypothesis
screen. No head-to-head superiority claim is warranted. The measured
response intervals remain source-reported; the new calculation does not
validate them experimentally or extend them to a continuous unknown model
family.
