PRODUCT ISOTOPE CODES AS A PHYSICAL TIMING CORRELATOR
2026-10-12. Candidate, not a demonstrated technology or a priority claim.

Result within the stated model: a reaction joining independently labelled
one-atom precursors records a two-time feed-code correlation in the isotope
distribution of its stable product. Its time average survives arbitrarily slow
unit-mass downstream linear transport. For one common feed and a two-atom
product, programmable isotope pulses measure the law of the absolute difference
between the two precursor ages. A complete single-step isotope transient does
not generally identify that law. Full arbitrary programmed isotope experiments
can implement this operation; there is no claim of beating their optimum.

What is new is UNKNOWN. Isotope crossover, full two-atom isotopologue analysis,
periodic isotope modulation, and DC nonlinear frequency response all predate
this investigation. The potentially useful specific object is a nonparametric
precursor-age GAP distribution measured using one common isotope feed and slow
product analysis. Neither physical validation nor consequential discovery has
been achieved.

1. EXACT PHYSICAL CONTRACT

Hold chemical flow, pressure, temperature, and electrochemical operating point
fixed. Program only the isotope fraction p(t) of one molecular feed. Each feed
molecule contributes exactly one retained tagged atom; a two-atom product joins
two such independently labelled molecules. A possible application is a C2
product assembled from two one-carbon CO-derived precursors. This is a proposed
application, not a verified description of every CO/CO2 reduction pathway.

At a product-formation event at time t, let (A,B) be the ages of the two tagged
atoms measured back to the ACTUAL isotope-programming interface. Its joint law
K is stationary and independent of isotope code. Conditional on (A,B,t), the
two heavy-label indicators L1,L2 are independent Bernoulli variables with
probabilities p(t-A), p(t-B). Their atom roles may be indistinguishable.
Set q(t)=2p(t)-1 and product isotope parity Y=(2L1-1)(2L2-1).

The sensor returns counts of product isotope classes 0,1,2 heavy atoms, e.g.
separated intact-product GC-MS channels. Correct backgrounds, fragmentation,
cross-talk, isotope-dependent sensitivity, and count-rate effects. Pure code
complementation on ONE common feed does NOT reverse C2 parity and does NOT
remove these calibration requirements.

Supplied structure: known atom provenance/stoichiometry and isotope retention;
isotope-neutral stationary chemistry; controllable actual feed waveform;
calibrated product isotope response. Acquired quantity: F(w)=E min(|A-B|,w), or
samples of a transform of the age-gap law. K itself is never supplied to the
proposed physical procedure. It is supplied only in the explicit simulation.

Costs: actuator rise time and jitter; gas/liquid delivery bandwidth; local-code
calibration and branch transport; isotope purchase and consumption; guard and
startup time; reactor time and energy; product collection; sensor calibration;
independent detected ions and effective sample count; code computation. These
costs cannot be replaced by the mathematical existence of a waveform.

2. COMMON-FEED IDENTITY AND DOWNSTREAM INVARIANCE

For a bounded periodic delivered code q of period P, define
  R_q(d) = (1/P) integral_0^P q(t) q(t-d) dt.
Conditional independence gives E[Y|A,B,t]=q(t-A)q(t-B). Fubini and periodic
translation invariance imply exactly
  M_q := time-average E Y = E R_q(A-B) = E R_q(D), D=|A-B|,
because real autocorrelation R_q is even. This is a physical multiplication of
two historical labels in product assembly, followed by slow histogram analysis.

Writing mu=(1/P) integral q, the complete averaged three-class histogram is
  P0=(1-2 mu+M_q)/4,
  P1=(1-M_q)/2,
  P2=(1+2 mu+M_q)/4.
Thus a single common periodic code measures one scalar linear functional of the
gap law. It does not recover a full joint age law in one run.

Suppose each intact product isotope class j is subsequently transported through
a causal linear time-invariant kernel h_j with integral 1. In periodic steady
state, its concentration becomes h_j*f_j. Integrating over an integer number
of periods and exchanging integrals gives
  mean(h_j*f_j)=integral h_j * mean(f_j)=mean(f_j).
The kernels need not be fast or identical. Fixed isotope-specific loss changes
their masses and must instead be calibrated as an efficiency. Nonlinear
transport, isotope exchange, reaction, or time-dependent gain is not covered.

An infinite/pre-established periodic regime is a real assumption. A finite
experiment started with an unlabelled reactor must reach it. A gap bound D<=L
does NOT bound max(A,B): arbitrarily long common storage can preserve D while
forcing arbitrarily long isotope startup. Downstream residence tails likewise
cost guard time. Give a measured tail bound, charge burn-in/flush, or collect all
material assigned to an input block. There is no uniform finite wall-time bound
from D<=L alone.

3. RECTANGULAR PULSE-WIDTH OPERATION

This particularly simple version was independently derived by the root agent.
Feed pure heavy isotope for a window of width w in every period P and pure light
isotope otherwise. If 0<=D<=L, 0<=w<=L, P>2L, the two shifted heavy windows have
symmetric-difference length 2 min(D,w). Therefore
  P1(w)=2 E min(D,w)/P,
  M(w)=1-4 E min(D,w)/P.
Only two switches per period are required. Slow product collection suffices.
The heavy isotope duty fraction is w/P, which must be charged.

F(w)=E min(D,w)=integral_0^w Pr(D>u) du. At atoms use one-sided derivatives:
F'_+(w)=Pr(D>w), F'_-(w)=Pr(D>=w). For h>0,
  Pr(D>w+h) <= [F(w+h)-F(w)]/h <= Pr(D>w).
Consequently pulse widths on a grid yield certified bin-averaged survival
probabilities, without a parametric exponential residence-time fit.

If independently detected product counts are binomial with N ions per width,
  Shat=(P/(2h)) [p1hat(w+h)-p1hat(w)]
has variance <=P^2/(8 h^2 N). A conservative sufficient count for absolute error
epsilon with failure probability alpha is
  N >= (P^2/(2 h^2 epsilon^2)) log(4/alpha)
at EACH of the two widths, by two Hoeffding bounds. Calibration and code errors
are additional. Fine resolution costs (P/h)^2 counts. Generic density recovery
requires a smoothness/regularization assumption; finite differences alone
cannot certify pointwise density or arbitrarily sharp features.

4. A SINGLE-STEP IDENTIFIABILITY SEPARATION

Let tau>0. Under H0, unordered precursor ages are (0,2tau) or (tau,3tau), each
with probability 1/2. Under H1 they are (0,3tau) or (tau,2tau), each with
probability 1/2. Randomly exchange the two atom roles. Both models have the same
atomic age marginal: uniform on {0,tau,2tau,3tau}. Both have identical min-age
law (uniform on {0,tau}) and max-age law (uniform on {2tau,3tau}).

For a common pure isotope STEP at t=0,
  P2(t)=Pr(max(A,B)<=t),
  P0(t)=Pr(min(A,B)>t),
  P1(t)=1-P0(t)-P2(t).
The entire ideal three-class time trace is therefore identical for H0 and H1.
Identical downstream transport and noise preserve indistinguishability. No
estimator, including an optimally informed fit to this exact one-step data,
can distinguish the two hypotheses. This is stronger than comparing only
single-label marginals.

But H0 has D=2tau exactly, whereas H1 has D=tau or 3tau with equal probability.
With P=8tau and w=2tau, pulse-width operation yields P1(H0)=1/2 and
P1(H1)=3/8. This separation comes from the changed physical input experiment,
not a better analysis of unchanged observations.

For this toy discrimination task, the pulse-width inversion design is NOT the
strongest common-feed waveform. A pure binary code alternating every tau
(period 2tau) yields M(H0)=+1 and M(H1)=-1. It aliases other age gaps and is not
full-law tomography. It is also available to an equally informed programmed
isotope baseline. A full multi-switch baseline matches the candidate exactly.

The step separation is not restricted to point masses: add iid U,V uniform on
[0,sigma] to the two ages with sigma<tau. Ordering is preserved, so H0 and H1
still have the same min/max marginals and full step trace. The alternating code
has parities +/-[1-2 sigma/(3tau)]. This is a mathematical event-age model;
realizability by a particular catalytic reaction network has not been proved.

5. RANDOM-TELEGRAPH VERSION AND ITS NON-SHOT-NOISE COST

For a stationary balanced pure binary isotope code S(t), flipping at Poisson
rate lambda independently of chemistry,
  E S(t)S(t-d)=exp(-2 lambda |d|),
so isotope parity averaged over codes and product events is
  M_lambda=E exp(-2 lambda D).
A sweep of lambda determines the gap law in exact infinite data by uniqueness
of the Laplace transform. It does not provide a stable finite-data inversion
without explicit range, resolution, tail, and precision assumptions.

For the H0/H1 example,
  M0=exp(-4 lambda tau),
  M1=[exp(-2 lambda tau)+exp(-6 lambda tau)]/2.
The maximum contrast is only 2/27, attained at lambda tau=(log 3)/2.
Thus random switching is inferior to an informed binary code for that example.

Many products share one random code realization. Counting N ions does NOT
produce N independent code samples. In the ideal stationary Poisson event model
with rate r and iid age pairs, a raw parity average over physical duration T
has variance bounded by
  Var <=1/(rT)+2 E D/T.
Here r is detected independent-product rate, not feed-molecule throughput.

Proof of the code term: for fixed ages parity is (-1)^N(I), where N counts
Poisson switches in the interval I between the two precursor sampling times.
For intervals I,J its covariance is
  exp[-2lambda |I symmetric-difference J|]
    -exp[-2lambda(|I|+|J|)],
which is nonnegative and zero for disjoint intervals. For intervals of lengths
d1,d2, translating one across the other produces nonzero covariance over a
set of shifts of length d1+d2, and the covariance is at most 1. Integrate over
the age law: integrated covariance <=2 E D. The time-average variance bound
then follows from the standard double-integral formula. Poisson shot variance
is at most 1/(rT). Site dynamics, ageing, or correlated product events require
their own covariance term/effective sample-size estimate.

A deterministic repeated pseudorandom sequence instead measures its ACTUAL
known cyclic autocorrelation E R_code(D). It is incorrect to replace a finite
code by ideal exp(-2lambda D) without bounding code-correlation error. Code
period also introduces modulo-period aliasing.

6. UPSTREAM TRANSPORT AND THE COMMON-DELAY GAUGE

If intrinsic precursor ages are (a,b), but independently dispersed delivery
adds (U,V), the actual measured signed difference is Delta=a-b+U-V. In Fourier
coordinates, assuming independence,
  chi_observed(omega)=chi_intrinsic(omega) phi_U(omega) phi_V(-omega).
Independent upstream dispersion therefore ATTENUATES contrast and may make
deconvolution ill-conditioned. It does not cancel like post-product transport.
A shared delay added equally to both atoms cancels algebraically, but this must
be physically justified rather than assumed for independent reactant molecules.

Equivalently, if delivered isotope fraction is a filtered external code,
q_local=h_up*q_external, product parity measures E R_local(Delta_intrinsic).
For telegraph input this is
  E_{a,b,U,V} exp[-2lambda |a-b+U-V|].
For harmonic input the upstream attenuation is |H_up(omega)|^2 for identical
branches. Faster switching cannot bypass an unmeasured slow delivery interface.

For any nonnegative common delay Z (possibly correlated with the ages), the
transformation (A,B)->(A+Z,B+Z) leaves every stationary common-code result
unchanged. Thus even infinite pulse-width data do not identify absolute
precursor lifetime, the common post-coupling residence, a unique reaction
mechanism, active-site count, or the full joint K. A gap-law change is evidence
of changed within-product timing, not by itself evidence of an elementary path.

7. GENERAL m-LABEL DC SELECTION RULE

This is a generalization for independently controlled labelled feed branches;
it is NOT the one-feed implementation above. Let age vector A in R_+^m have
law K. Label probabilities u_i(t-A_i) are conditionally independent. Let
u_i(t)=sum_{nu in Lambda_i} c_i(nu) exp(i nu t) be finite Fourier series.
Define Phi_K(xi)=E exp(i xi dot A). Then
  mean E product_i L_i
   =sum_{nu_1+...+nu_m=0} [product_i c_i(nu_i)] Phi_K(-nu).
Proof: expand the finite product, take expectation, and average exp(i sum nu_i t).
Only zero-sum frequency vectors survive. Exact integer-period integration is
enough for commensurate frequencies in periodic steady state.

For centred parity product_i(2L_i-1), and q_i(t)=a_i cos(omega_i t+phi_i),
  M=(product a_i)/2^m *
       sum_{s in {+/-1}^m:sum s_i omega_i=0}
       exp(i sum s_i phi_i) Phi_K(-s_1 omega_1,...,-s_m omega_m).
If exactly one conjugate pair resonates, its amplitude is
  2^(1-m) product a_i.
Estimating Phi at fixed precision from independent product counts therefore
costs O(4^(m-1)/(product a_i^2 epsilon^2)); high-order coincidence is costly.

The hyperplane sum xi_i=0 identifies exactly the relative-age law
  (A1-Am,...,A_{m-1}-Am),
since its characteristic function is Phi_K(eta_1,...,eta_{m-1},-sum eta_i).
It loses the common absolute-delay coordinate. Access to all such modes still
requires separate measurements and sufficient branch bandwidth, rather than
one scalar measurement returning an entire law.

With m independently complemented feeds, a full Walsh cycle over s_i=+/-1
can cancel unknown FIXED DIAGONAL detection efficiencies: weight each count by
output parity times product_i s_i, and divide the pooled weighted counts by
pooled total counts. Orthogonality eliminates every lower isotope moment;
the same sum of channel efficiencies occurs in numerator and denominator.
Cross-talk, background, unequal exposure/flux, and gain drift do not cancel.
For one common C2 feed only s1=s2 is accessible; product_i s_i=1, so this
gain-cancellation argument is unavailable. Calibrate the three-class sensor.

8. STRONGEST BASELINE BOUNDARY

An independent m-feed delayed step has all-heavy response
  C_s(t)=Pr(t>=max_i(A_i+s_i)).
Its integrated difference from a reference step equals the corresponding
difference of E max_i(A_i+s_i). The derivative in s_m gives
  Pr(A_i-Am<=s_m-s_i for every i<m),
the joint relative-age CDF. Unit-mass downstream transport preserves integrated
differences. Therefore DC Fourier mixing is not an information capability
beyond independently delayed m-feed crossover. That baseline requires
independent feed roles, unavailable in the ONE common-feed C2 contract.

One-feed full single-step isotope data can be nonidentifying, as proved above.
One-feed arbitrary programmed isotope data already includes the proposed pulse,
sine, random, and optimized binary experiments. It must tie the candidate when
it executes the same waveform and isotope analysis. A meaningful comparison is
with demonstrated common-feed step/pulse methods using the same isotope budget,
bandwidth, calibration, reactor time, and hypothesis information; superiority
to an oracle optimal experiment policy is neither claimed nor possible.

10. FALSIFICATION AND ADVANCEMENT GATE

First validate actual delivered isotope code, atom retention, isotope neutrality,
and the calibrated 0/1/2 product response. A slow inert tracer alone may not
identify spatially varying delivery correlations at the active site. Nonlinear
isotope effects or scrambling can produce an apparent gap signal independently
of the intended K and invalidate the interpretation.

Then compare full standard step analysis and common-feed pulse-width analysis
on the same stable sample, with equally informed mechanism fits and every
material/time/calibration cost. An informative change is reproducible separation
of candidate age-gap laws that remain observationally identical under the
complete step protocol, together with independent checks of delivery/exchange.
No such physical experiment has been run. A successful demonstration would
establish a metrology capability within the atom-lineage model, not a major
scientific discovery or a unique catalytic mechanism.
