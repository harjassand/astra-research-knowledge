# Independent persistent-bath theorem audit

Date: 16 October 2026. Auditor: `fz_memory_bath_theorem_audit`.

**Verdict: the finite-dimensional operator theorem and its joint-input diamond
bound are correct under the written universal schedule certificate.** The bath
may be shared across future faults, spatially nonlocal within the bath, initially
entangled with the logical input and reference, and reused indefinitely during
the finite circuit. No Markov assumption for its quantum state is needed. The
stochastic lifetime remains conditional on the receiving classical envelope,
forcing, LSS/HT, stopped-torus, whole-slab and accumulated-homology arguments.
This audit does not establish those external imports, historical originality,
physical implementation, or useful threshold constants.

This result is established correctable-operator-span mathematics applied to a
model-specific schedule certificate. It is not a new general QEC theorem.

## 1. Exact theorem that was checked

Let `V: L -> M` be an isometry, with finite-dimensional logical and qubit-memory
spaces. Let `Dec: T(M) -> T(L)` be one fixed full CPTP channel with Kraus operators
`D_s`. Fix an externally specified finite schedule `h` of fault and ideal
correction proposals. Every fault is supported on its fixed memory patch `A_i`
and the same finite-dimensional persistent bath `B`. Identity acts on all other
memory qubits and on an untouched reference `R`.

At an ideal correction proposal, the memory Kraus operators are `Q_j Pi_j`,
where `Pi_j` is a syndrome projector or sum of syndrome projectors and `Q_j`
is its prescribed legal Pauli correction. The channel is full CPTP. On every
Pauli orbit `E V`, `Pi_j E V` is exactly `0` or `E V`. In particular, the
selection cannot depend on the encoded logical vector.

The required certificate is the following simultaneous statement:

    for this same h, for every choice P_i in Pauli(A_i) at every fault,
    and for every surviving correction record r and decoder label s,
    D_s H_(h,r,P) V = c_(h,r,P,s) I_L.                         (1)

`H` includes all the correction projectors before they are collapsed, all
ordered fault Paulis, and their phases. A killed summand has coefficient zero.
One and the same `Dec` occurs in (1). Correctness for each error with a separately
chosen inverse, a certificate for one sampled Pauli distribution, or small
terminal syndrome alone would not be (1).

Given this certificate, allow arbitrary full CPTP maps on `A_i B`, arbitrary
bath-only CPTP maps between proposals, and causal changes of those maps based
on the preceding external classical schedule. Feedforward from quantum
measurement outcomes is included by retaining the usable records in `B` or an
explicitly included controller, and averaging their complete instrument tree.
The proposed physical maps must remain linear CPTP maps on the same joint input
space. An assignment depending on the unknown input density operator is not
such a map.

Then, after encoding and recovery, the joint channel has the factorization

    Gamma_h = Id_L tensor Lambda_(B,h),                       (2)

where `Lambda_(B,h)` is CPTP on `B`. Equality holds on every operator on `L B`,
including every logical-bath cross term. Therefore it also holds after
tensoring with `Id_R`, for arbitrary initial `rho_LBR`.

## 2. Independent operator reconstruction

For a fault Kraus operator `F_(i,a)` on `A_i B`, the finite local Pauli basis
gives the exact expansion

    F_(i,a) = sum_(P in Pauli(A_i)) P tensor B_(i,a,P),
    B_(i,a,P) = d_i^(-1) Tr_Ai[(P^dagger tensor I_B) F_(i,a)]. (3)

These coefficients are operators on the persistent bath. They are neither
probabilities nor independent random variables. In particular, coefficients
at different locations and times may fail to commute.

Fix a complete circuit Kraus record `alpha`, including any branch-dependent
measurement/feedforward labels and the final decoder label. Its decoded
operator is

    A_alpha = (D_s tensor I_B) W_N ... W_1 (V tensor I_B).    (4)

Expand each fault factor using (3). Bath-only factors are already identity on
memory. Each summand starts with a code state and subsequently has a definite
Pauli memory factor `E V`; it need not have a definite bath state. At an ideal
correction, the memory projector therefore kills the entire operator summand
or acts as identity on its full logical domain. If it survives, the correction
Pauli is precisely the legal deterministic correction for that summand.

Every surviving term in (4) is consequently one of the histories in (1).
Its logical factor is scalar identity. Keeping bath factors in their exact
chronological order gives

    A_alpha = I_L tensor B_alpha,
    B_alpha = sum_histories c_history
              B_last ... B_second B_first.                  (5)

All coherent interference, Pauli phases, bath noncommutativity, and cancellations
remain inside the actual sum defining `B_alpha`. There is no absolute-coefficient
bound, Pauli twirl, sign-to-probability conversion, or assumption of fresh baths.

The full encoded proposal circuit followed by `Dec` is CPTP. Thus

    sum_alpha A_alpha^dagger A_alpha = I_L tensor I_B.

Substituting (5) and taking any nonzero logical matrix element yields

    sum_alpha B_alpha^dagger B_alpha = I_B.                   (6)

Hence `Lambda_(B,h)(X) = sum_alpha B_alpha X B_alpha^dagger` is CPTP. To check
the all-input claim directly, expand a joint operator as
`X = sum_(u,v) |u><v| tensor X_uv`. Equations (5)-(6) send it to
`sum_(u,v) |u><v| tensor Lambda_(B,h)(X_uv)`, proving (2) on the full operator
space. No initial product-state assumption entered the proof.

For a causal instrument tree, the individual Kraus factors at a node may
depend on earlier labels. Completeness at each node still telescopes to
completeness of the full tree, so (4)-(6) apply unchanged. Conditioning on one
leaf of that tree is generally trace decreasing and is not the channel in (2).

The fixed-decoder hypothesis also implies the cross-error Knill-Laflamme
identities. For two physical history operators `H_p,H_q`, decoder completeness
and (1) give

    V^dagger H_p^dagger H_q V
      = sum_s (D_s H_p V)^dagger (D_s H_q V)
      = [sum_s conjugate(c_(s,p)) c_(s,q)] I_L.              (7)

This explicitly excludes the invalid inference from individually invertible
errors to correction of their coherent span.

## 3. Averaging schedules: the precise norm statement

Sample `h` using an external law `mu`, independent of the entire initial joint
state on `L B R` and of every quantum outcome. In the application, it is the
fresh independent Poisson proposal law. The schedule includes correction and
fault locations, types, and times, but no quantum Kraus outcomes.

Assume there is one location-only measurable event `Good_T` such that every
`h` in it satisfies (1). Set `p_bad=mu(Good_T^c)`. Fault actions may depend
causally on the past external schedule or retained records; their quantum
correlations do not change `mu` or this event. For any fixed admissible policy,
`Gamma_T = integral Gamma_h dmu(h)` is a CPTP map on `L B`.

For good `h`, bath trace preservation in (2) implies

    Tr_B composed with Gamma_h = Tr_B                       (8)

as a map on all `L B` operators. For bad `h`, both maps in their difference
are channels `L B -> L`, whose diamond-norm distance is at most two. Convexity
therefore gives

    || Tr_B composed with Gamma_T - Tr_B ||_diamond
       <= integral_bad 2 dmu(h) = 2 p_bad.                  (9)

This diamond norm has the **joint** input domain `L B`. It controls every
initial `rho_LBR`, including initially correlated baths:

    || Tr_B[(Gamma_T tensor Id_R)(rho_LBR)] - rho_LR ||_1
       <= 2 p_bad.                                         (10)

For an independently prepared fixed bath state `sigma_B`, precompose (9) with
the CPTP preparation `rho_L -> rho_L tensor sigma_B` to obtain the usual
logical-channel diamond bound. With an arbitrary correlated bath there is
generally no state-independent map from `L` alone; (9) is the correct claim.

If the conditional classical support theorem supplies
`p_bad <= C L^4(1+kappa T) exp(-cL)`, the explicit inherited bound here is
`min(2, 2 C L^4(1+kappa T) exp(-cL))`. Absorbing the factor two into a newly
named constant is legitimate. The quantum argument introduces no bath-
dimension, Kraus-rank, or Pauli-coefficient factor.

## 4. Shared future bath and record rates

A bath that interacts at one patch, retains coherent information, and later
interacts at an arbitrarily distant scheduled patch is included. Its retained
information changes the `B_alpha` products, while the physical memory Pauli
histories in (1) already quantify over all local choices at all those locations.
No extra unscheduled memory interaction is included.

For a good schedule and a particular complete outcome record, the probability
is `Tr[(I_L tensor B_alpha^dagger B_alpha) rho_LB]`. This can depend on the bath
input and on correlations represented there. Its normalized postselected
logical output need not equal the original logical marginal: measuring one
half of an entangled pair steers the other half. Summing all records and using
(6) is what yields (8). Thus outcome-adaptive **actions** are allowed, whereas
outcome-adaptive charged **locations or rates** do not inherit the fresh-law
probability theorem merely by retaining the same average rate.

The fixed finite `B` must actually contain any retained quantum or classical
controller used by the admitted process. The theorem is uniform over every
chosen finite dimension; it does not silently furnish an unbounded classical
measurement log for all Poisson event counts. Such a log needs a specified
larger common space or an infinite-dimensional formulation. Past external
schedule histories may be classical arguments of the policy as explicitly
allowed in the candidate. Every `Gamma_h` averaged in (9) must have the same
joint input space.

## 5. Infinite baths and continuous coupling

The candidate expressly proves the elementary theorem for finite `B`; that
restriction is mathematically safe. A dimension-free finite-dimensional
estimate alone does not construct an infinite-bath dynamics, justify a domain
for an unbounded Hamiltonian, or prove convergence of truncations in diamond
norm. These are separate obligations. This audit makes no such promotion.

There is a useful analytic route beyond mere truncation: for a specified finite
schedule of normal CPTP maps on trace-class operators of a Hilbert-space bath,
use bounded Stinespring isometries for the maps and the finite Pauli expansion
of their memory matrix blocks. Each block coefficient is a bounded map from
the bath to the bath plus dilation environment. The finite Pauli-history sum
then gives a total recovered isometry `I_L tensor J_B`. Isometry completeness
gives `J_B^dagger J_B=I_B`, and tracing the dilation environment gives a normal
bath channel. This avoids infinite Kraus-product rearrangements. To turn that
route into a theorem for a stochastic infinite-bath process requires specified
normal maps, common spaces, measurability and existence of the schedule average.
None of it permits unscheduled continuous memory-bath interactions.

In particular, this theorem does not transfer the receiving memory-only Choi
floor to an arbitrary continuous joint memory-bath Hamiltonian. A floor
`D_A tensor Id_B` has a singular joint Choi matrix, and arbitrarily small joint
Hamiltonian directions can leave the CP cone immediately. The exact principal-
minor countermodel is in `COUNTERMODELS_AND_SCOPE.md`.

generators `XXXX, ZZZZ`, logical dimension four, and its full fixed CPTP

## 7. Historical credit and remaining gates

The fixed-recovery scalar condition and linear closure appear explicitly in
Knill and Laflamme, Theorem 3.1; Theorem 3.2 gives the cross-error criterion and
Theorem 3.5 the tensor syndrome form. They are primary antecedents of the
operator step used here. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9604034v1)

Aharonov and Ben-Or already distinguish location fault paths from arbitrary
operations at those locations, including a reused environment. Their framework
and general-noise expansion supply relevant fault-path background; their
specific circuit theorem is not the present sweep theorem.
[Primary paper, Definition 7 and noise-model discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9906129v1)

Terhal and Burkard explicitly use Pauli operators with bath-operator coefficients,
long bath memory, and good/bad fault paths in a non-Markovian threshold analysis.
Their locality and Hamiltonian-strength assumptions differ from the externally
scheduled sparse-interaction model here.
[Primary paper, Sections 1.1 and 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0402104v2)

stated tail, its whole-slab control of every transient/pre-jump syndrome, the
fixed toric-code recovery and homology induction, compatible lattice sizes,
and exact initialization/correction/readout. The probability imports and
internal model deductions remain named conditions. The classical stochastic
location law is not a physical reservoir acquisition or a continuous-bath
