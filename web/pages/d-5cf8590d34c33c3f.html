# Functional multiplicity-gap reconstruction

6 October 2026. A second recent anchor is Vinayak M. Kumar and Geoffrey Mon, *List Decoding, Linear Hashing, and Furstenberg over F_q*, arXiv:2609.17020v1, verified 15 September 2026. Read the full Section 2.2 derivation (Theorem 2.8, Lemma 2.9, Proposition 2.11 and Claims 2.12/2.13), Section 2.4's dimension reduction and Theorem 2.20, and the hashing translation in Section 3.1. Recoverable HTML is `sources/r14_computation/2609.17020v1.html`.

Author contribution: vanishing multiplicities outside a set, slightly lower than inside, improve Furstenberg bounds and hence hashing max-load/list-decoding estimates. Our deduction below replaces their two-level multiplicity profile by an arbitrary nonnegative function. Its novelty is **UNKNOWN**, and maximal inequalities over finite fields have substantial prior art, notably Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao (arXiv:0903.1879). This is an exact functional reconstruction/corollary candidate, not a claimed new resolution of a Kakeya problem.

## Interface and theorem

Let q be a prime power, n>=r>=1, and f:F_q^n->R_>=0 an arbitrary supplied function. Define the plane maximal average

    M_r f(V) = max_b q^(-r) sum_(x in b+V) f(x),

where V ranges uniformly over r-dimensional linear subspaces. If a fraction at least delta>0 of directions satisfy M_r f(V)>=beta, then

    (q^(-n) sum_x f(x)^n)^(1/n)
       >= [delta*q^r / (delta*q^r+q-1)] * beta.                 (W)

Every quantity is finite and explicit. This is a geometric implication, not an efficient algorithm for evaluating exponentially many f(x), nor a learnable promise on an unknown source. The exponent is the ambient dimension n; that may make (W) weak for concentrated functions and must remain charged.

## Complete derivation

Fix a large integer M and impose vanishing order R_x=ceil(M f(x)) at every point x. There are C_M=sum_x binom(R_x+n-1,n) homogeneous linear constraints on a polynomial's coefficients: for each point, all Hasse derivatives of total order below R_x vanish. Choose the smallest d with **binom(d+n,n)>C_M**, using a strict inequality. There is then a nonzero degree-at-most-d polynomial P satisfying those constraints. Equality between constraint count and number of variables is insufficient in general; the strict choice increases the degree by at most a bounded amount and does not change the limit. Choose P of actual degree D<=d, with nonzero top homogeneous part P_H.

Counting asymptotics give

    lim_(M->infinity) d/M = (sum_x f(x)^n)^(1/n).

This follows by dividing C_M and binom(d+n,n) by M^n/n!, with finitely many x; if f=0 identically, (W) is immediate. No independence of the derivative constraints is required for the existence bound.

For every full-rank n×r matrix V having a good direction, choose a good shift b. For any derivative multi-index i of total order w, the restricted polynomial Q_i(t)=P^(i)(b+Vt) has total multiplicity at least

    sum_(t in F_q^r) mult(Q_i,t) >= M beta q^r - w q^r.

Hasse differentiation loses at most w multiplicity; restriction cannot lower it. Negative lower bounds for individual points are harmless, because multiplicities are nonnegative. A uniformly chosen full-rank matrix represents a uniform r-subspace.

The same good-fraction assertion holds for arbitrary matrices of each rank s<r: complete their image uniformly to an r-subspace. Its marginal distribution is uniform, so at least a delta fraction of rank-s images admit some good completion. Tile a good r-dimensional affine shift by cosets of the s-dimensional image. At least one coset has f-average>=beta. Each point of this coset has q^(r-s) preimages under V, recovering the same total-multiplicity inequality. Thus a fraction at least delta of **all** n×r matrices has the displayed property, simultaneously for every derivative order.

Multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel over F_q^r says a nonzero degree-at-most-(D-w) polynomial has total multiplicity at most (D-w)q^(r-1). Therefore Q_i is identically zero whenever

    q(M beta-w)>D-w,

equivalently w<t=ceil((q M beta-D)/(q-1)). For such derivatives, the degree-(D-w) homogeneous component of Q_i is (P_H)^(i)(Vt). It too is zero; lower degree pieces cannot cancel it. This remains valid if (P_H)^(i) itself is zero.

Work now over the rational function field K=F_q(t_1,...,t_r). The product set

    T^n = {Vt : V in F_q^(n×r)},
    T = {u dot t : u in F_q^r}

has |T|=q^r, with distinct matrices giving distinct K-valued vectors. Hence P_H, a nonzero polynomial over K, has multiplicity at least t on at least a delta fraction of T^n. When t>0, multiplicity Schwartz–Zippel over K yields

    D >= delta*q^r*t
      >= delta*q^r*(q M beta-D)/(q-1).

When t<=0, D>=q M beta already implies the eventual lower bound. Rearranging in either case gives

    D/M >= q*delta*q^r*beta/(delta*q^r+q-1).

Combine D<=d with the constraint-counting limit, and divide the resulting norm by q. This proves (W).

## Recovering the source theorem and weighted application

For f=1 on S and 1-epsilon on its complement, a plane hitting S on an alpha fraction has average beta=alpha+(1-epsilon)(1-alpha). The left side raised to n is [|S|+(1-epsilon)^n|S^c|]/q^n. Rearranging (W) reproduces Kumar–Mon Theorem 2.8. Thus its two-level estimate is genuinely a special case of the reconstructed profile argument.

For an arbitrary supplied likelihood/weight function, put rho=(E f^n)^(1/n). If beta>rho, (W) rearranges to

    Pr_V[M_r f(V)>=beta]
      <= min(1, (q-1)*rho/[q^r*(beta-rho)]).                 (WT)

For a uniform surjective hash whose kernel has dimension r, M_r f is the largest normalized weighted fiber sum. Formula (WT) therefore controls weighted fiber overload without pretending f is a uniform set. Kernel choice is uniform, the weights are supplied explicitly, and evaluation cost still includes reading them. This is potentially useful for likelihood-weighted coding lists or uncertain-source hash analyses, but no concrete improvement over competent weighted maximal-function prior art has yet been established.

Adding a baseline b>=0 to f yields an optional family of bounds replacing rho by (E(f+b)^n)^(1/n) and beta by beta+b. This includes the source's multiplicity-gap optimization. A proof of all-fiber **coverage** does not follow from an upper bound on maximum load alone; an empty fiber can coexist with a moderate maximum load. The linear support theorem in the other note remains the relevant coverage result.

## Competent weighted baseline: Ellenberg–Oberlin–Tao

Read the saved 12 May 2009 v2, Theorem 1.3/Remarks 1.4–1.5, Proposition 2.3 and the complete arbitrary-weight polynomial stage of Proposition 2.4 (pp.8–9), and Section 4.12's higher-plane discussion. Their polynomial proof already assigns vanishing multiplicity f(v) at every point and counts sum f(v)^n constraints. Thus arbitrary weighted multiplicities are established prior art; our weighted rewrite must not be called the first functional multiplicity theorem.

For lines (r=1), their strong l_n maximal inequality implies, writing D=(q^n-1)/(q-1) for the number of projective directions and rho for normalized ||f||_n,

    delta <= C_n^n * q^(n-1)/D * (rho/beta)^n.

This has stronger high-threshold decay than (WT)'s first-power tail, with a dimension-dependent unspecified constant. Our explicit coefficient is useful in a different near-baseline/dimension-accounted regime and applies to arbitrary r, but is not a replacement for the established line theorem. Their Section 4.12 also supplies mixed-plane norm inequalities using the sharper input exponent n/r and records a full plane-maximal conjecture in that 2009 version. No statement about the conjecture's current status is made without a present literature audit. Our derivation uses input exponent n and cannot be sold as that conjecture's solution.

The strengthened r-plane indicator bound (q^r/(q^r+q-1))^n is also already linked to the older Kopparty–Lev–Saraf–Sudan line of work in the recent paper. The functional derivation here therefore has a substantial prior-art burden. It remains a transparent reusable derivation until that burden is resolved.

## Decisive product-source discriminator: why this is not a general hashing improvement

Let X consist of 100 independent Bernoulli(2/5) bits, let mu be its probability mass, and f(x)=2^100 mu(x). Hash to m=20 output bits, so kernel dimension r=80. The normalized ambient L_100 norm is exactly

    rho = 2^99 * ((2/5)^100+(3/5)^100)
        = ((4/5)^100+(6/5)^100)/2
        > 2^20.

For every surjective hash, every coset-average of f is 2^20 times that fiber's probability, so it is at most 2^20. Therefore **no possible threshold beta exceeds rho**: (WT) is completely vacuous for this product source. Its norm can be calculated from 100 supplied biases without enumerating 2^100 masses; lack of an acquisition oracle is not the reason for failure.

A competent elementary comparison is the collision calculation for the same uniform binary linear family. Distinct x,x' collide with probability2^-m. Writing p_h(y)=Pr[h(X)=y], independence is unnecessary:

    E_h sum_y(p_h(y)-2^-m)^2
      = (1-2^-m) sum_x mu(x)^2
      <= (13/25)^100.

Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen thus give

    E_h TV(p_h, uniform) <= (1/2)*sqrt(2^20*(13/25)^100)
                         < 3.3e-12.

This controls statistical deviation, rather than giving the same all-fiber maximum guarantee, so the comparison is explicitly scoped. It nevertheless disproves any claim that the weighted L_n rewrite generally improves useful random hashing for nonuniform product sources. The exact norm inequality and numerical bound are recorded in `results/r14_computation/weighted_product_discriminator.json`. Reducing the exponent n or using a different supplied source statistic is a genuine missing advance; the derivation above does neither.

## Falsifiable checks and unresolved scope

`work/r14_computation/weighted_furstenberg.py` uses exact rational powers to check (W) for every function F2^3->{0,1,2}, every tail threshold attained by its maximal averages, and r=1,2. It also checks 400 seeded functions F2^4->{0,1,2,3,4} for each r=1,2,3. No numerical roots or tolerances are used. These are adversarial finite checks, not the proof.
