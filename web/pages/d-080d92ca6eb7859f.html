# Fixed-potential-budget approximation barrier: prior and full quantifiers

## Result

For the pinned target density \(n_*\), conditionally on the source's Theorem 1.1,
the exact local-potential ensemble sets
\[
\mathcal R_C=\{\rho_\Gamma:\ \Gamma\text{ is a ground-state ensemble of }H_s(v),\
v\in L^{3/2}+L^\infty,\ \|[v]\|_{L^{3/2}+L^\infty}\le C\}
\]
are closed in \(X=L^1\cap L^3\) for each finite \(C\), under the fixed-
particle-number, spinful, scalar spin-independent, ensemble interface. Since
\(n_*\notin\bigcup_C\mathcal R_C\),
\[
\delta_C:=\operatorname{dist}_X(n_*,\mathcal R_C)>0
\]
whenever \(\mathcal R_C\neq\varnothing\). This separation is strictly
positive but not effective: no lower-bound expression or computable value for
\(\delta_C\) follows.

There is also a standard complementary fact that completes the quantifiers.
Apply the Brøndsted–Rockafellar density theorem to the **ensemble
noninteracting kinetic functional** \(F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}\) on \(X\), not the
determinant-only functional commonly denoted \(T_s\). Its effective domain is
the finite-kinetic-energy \(N\)-representable densities, and its subgradient
domain is the ensemble noninteracting \(v\)-representable densities. The
pinned target belongs to \(\operatorname{dom}F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}\): Proposition 5.1 supplies
an ensemble minimizer of finite kinetic energy at \(n_*\). Therefore
\[
n_*\in\overline{\operatorname{dom}\partial F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}}^{\,X}
\subseteq\overline{\bigcup_{C<\infty}\mathcal R_C}^{\,X}.
\]
In particular, an exact-ground-state ensemble sequence \(n_j\to n_*\) in
\(X\) exists with finite-norm local potentials. It cannot have bounded
quotient norms: otherwise a bounded-budget subsequence and closedness of some
\(\mathcal R_C\) would imply \(n_*\in\mathcal R_C\). Thus every such
approximating sequence has \(\|[v_j]\|\to\infty\).

The complete qualitative statement is consequently:

- every finite budget has a positive strong-\(L^1\cap L^3\) error floor;
- the floors are nonincreasing and \(\delta_C\downarrow0\) as \(C\to\infty\);
- at least one approximating sequence exists, and every such sequence has
  diverging potential norm;
- no rate relating \(\delta_C\) and \(C\) is established.

The phrase “approximating sequence exists” is for exact ensemble ground-state
densities in the dual-potential space, not for practical approximate-DFT
outputs, finite-basis solutions, energies, grids, or other observables.

## Why the closedness argument survives the hostile cases

The direct proof in `BOUNDED_LOCAL_POTENTIAL_BARRIER.md` is an independent
reconstruction of the fixed-budget closed graph, with the following load-bearing
steps. Choose potential representatives modulo constants with bounded
\(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) decompositions. Strong \(X\) convergence bounds the
potential-density pairings. One fixed smooth trial determinant gives a uniform
upper bound on ground energies; their exact identity \(E=T_s[\rho]+\langle
v,\rho\rangle\) then gives a uniform kinetic bound. Strong \(L^1\) convergence
gives spatial tightness. Tightness plus the kinetic bound yields trace-norm
compactness of the ensemble states by cutoff, Rellich, and finite-rank
projection arguments. Weak \(L^{3/2}\) and weak-* \(L^\infty\) limits preserve
the potential budget, while strong \(X\) convergence passes the pairings.
Variational comparison makes the limiting ensemble a ground ensemble.

This avoids assuming a uniform form-bound modulus over an \(L^{3/2}\) ball.
The adversarial critical-scale well
\(v_\epsilon(x)=-\lambda\epsilon^{-2}W(x/\epsilon)\) has constant
\(L^{3/2}\) norm while its ground density's \(L^3\) norm scales as
\(\epsilon^{-2}\); it therefore does not converge strongly in \(X\), and it
does not refute this closure theorem. Weak-* compactness of the \(L^\infty\)
component, additive-constant gauge, degeneracy/ensembles, escaping mass,
collapse, and kinetic-energy control are addressed in the hostile report.

The closure proof is standard convex analysis/DFT, not a new theorem claim.
The subgradient inequality itself gives a strong-density/weak-* potential
closed graph: if \(n_j\to n\) strongly and \(u_j\rightharpoonup^*u\), with
\(u_j\in\partial F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}(n_j)\), testing the inequalities at
\(n\) first gives \(F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}(n_j)\to F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}(n)\), and
then passage to the limit gives \(u\in\partial F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}(n)\).
Weak/weak-* subsequential compactness of bounded decompositions in
\(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) then yields the fixed-budget result. The direct
compactness proof above is a hostile check of this standard route under the
actual ground-state ensemble interface.

## Primary prior audit

Lieb's *Density functionals for Coulomb systems* establishes the convex
ensemble density-functional framework on the \(L^1\cap L^3\) / 
\(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) dual pair, constrained-search attainment, and the
noninteracting specialization by replacing the internal Hamiltonian with the
kinetic operator; see [Lieb (1983), DOI 10.1002/qua.560240302](https://doi.org/10.1002/qua.560240302), especially §§3–4 and Theorem 3.14.
The Brøndsted–Rockafellar theorem gives density of the exact subgradient domain
in the effective domain for a proper convex lower-semicontinuous functional.
Penz et al.'s primary review spells out the exact density/potential interface:
the ensemble constrained-search functional is attained, convex, and lower
semicontinuous; \(v\)-representability is the nonempty subdifferential
condition; and \(\operatorname{dom}\partial F\) is dense in
\(\operatorname{dom}F\) by Brøndsted–Rockafellar. The same Legendre/subgradient
construction applies with the ensemble noninteracting functional
\(F^0_{\mathrm{CS,ens}}\); the determinant-only functional need not have the
same subgradient domain under degeneracy;
see [Penz et al. (2023), DOI 10.1021/acsphyschemau.2c00069](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphyschemau.2c00069), §§VI–VIII.

This primary prior also makes the scope boundary explicit: nearby exact
ensemble local-potential densities can use potentials very far away in the
potential dual norm. It supplies no effective norm/error tradeoff for this
particular target. It does not establish a practical local-DFT error floor.

## Status and limits

- `bounded_potential_ball_closedness`: internally reconstructed and hostile
  checked; standard Lieb/subdifferential consequence.
- `positive_fixed_budget_X_error`: conditional on the pinned nonrepresentability
  theorem; positive but non-effective.
- `dense_approximants_with_diverging_potential_norm`: standard consequence of
  applying Brøndsted–Rockafellar to \(F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}\), plus the source's finite
  constrained-search value and the closedness result. The approximation is in
  \(L^1\cap L^3\) and uses exact ensemble ground states.
- No source charge bound, no explicit \(\delta_C\), no physical-molecule
  generalization, and no claim in weaker metrics or for practical approximate
  DFT.

The pinned source's charge is defined by its Eq. (7.10) through a supremum over
the bad coupling set; the source proves finiteness by excluding a small interval
near zero but explicitly gives no effective numerical upper bound. Turning this
into a modest-parameter counterexample would require quantitative moduli for
the source's spectral perturbation, uniform minimizer convergence, exterior
channel remainder, strip radius, and the all-minimizer margin. None is supplied
by the existence arguments, and no numerical value is inferred here.
