# Cycle 5 — Arithmetic exponent to finite-bit witness and selective-output boundary

**Verdict.** There are two different extraction questions. Conditional on the source-derived all-field (9/4) exponent statement and its prime-subfield form, a single deterministic Turing procedure can *eventually* find and print a finite exact bilinear block decomposition for each encoded characteristic and rational exponent slack: enumerate candidate blocks and verify them exactly. This proves computability, not a useful construction bound. Separately, a supplied exact rational Laurent witness admits a direct finite-bit coefficient-extraction compiler with explicit height, output-size, and runtime bounds below. Neither fact yields a subquadratic arbitrary-mask selective-output algorithm. The missing selective premise is a mask-aware output circuit whose construction, coefficient sizes, output incidence, and evaluation costs are bounded.

**Status.** The coefficient-extraction theorem, the exhaustive-search theorem conditional on L03/leaf04, and the W-tensor counterexample are proved in this report. The W diagnostic is exact and finite. The (9/4) premise remains a source-derived, internally reconstructed claim, not a claim independently reproved here. No complexity bound for the witness search follows; the arbitrary-mask selective target remains `UNKNOWN`, and no general lower bound is claimed.

**Restart.** Keep the witness-search result in the computability column only. To advance the requested-output target, supply a uniform mask-dependent circuit or exact degeneration with (i) an output-sensitive application/decoder, (ii) bounded coefficient heights and field representation, (iii) a charged acquisition algorithm, and (iv) total cost below the desired threshold. The Cycle 4 Cartesian-mask result is a matched positive structured case, not an arbitrary-mask solution.

## 1. Precise interfaces and quantifiers

Write (T_u) for the trilinear matrix-multiplication tensor of (u\times u) matrices: its associated trilinear form is
\[
 (A,B,C)\longmapsto \operatorname{tr}(ABC)
 =\sum_{i,j,k=1}^u A_{ij}B_{jk}C_{ki}.
\]
A rank-(r) witness is an exact identity
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(ABC)=\sum_{s=1}^r a_s(A)b_s(B)c_s(C),
\]
where each (a_s,b_s,c_s) is a linear form in the (u^2) entries of the corresponding input. The data is (3ru^2) field coefficients. The input/output encoding in the computability result is:

* characteristic `c=0`, with coefficients encoded as reduced rationals, or a promised prime `c=p`, with coefficients encoded as residues in `F_p`;
* rational exponent slack (\delta>0), encoded as a numerator/denominator pair;
* output (u,r) and the complete exact coefficient arrays for a block witness.

For an arbitrary extension field, this same prime-subfield program embeds into the field. A *bit-time evaluator* over an arbitrary field additionally needs an exact finite representation of field elements, addition, multiplication, equality, and their costs. “Every abstract field” alone is not a Turing-machine input model.

The requested-output instance considered in the parent programme is different: `X in Z^(N x D)`, `Y in Z^(D x N)`, a supplied arbitrary `W subseteq [N]^2`, and exact output `{(XY)_ij : (i,j) in W}`, with `D=N^(1/4)`, `|W|<=N^(15/8)`. This report does not claim an algorithm or lower bound for that open interface.

## 2. Conditional total-computable search for an arithmetic block

Here is a constructive consequence that is weaker than a complexity theorem but stronger than “a witness is not displayed.” Assume the L03/leaf04 premise in its prime-subfield form: for every rational (\delta>0), some (u\ge2) has a rank-(r) decomposition of (T_u) over (K_c), where (K_0=\mathbb Q), (K_p=\mathbb F_p), and
\[
 r<u^{9/4+\delta}.
\]
The all-field transfer is conditional/source-derived; this section proves only what follows from that premise.

**Search theorem.** There is one deterministic Turing machine `FindBlock(c,delta)` which, on every valid input above, halts and outputs such an exact block witness. In characteristic zero the output coefficients are rational; in characteristic (p), they are in (\mathbb F_p). The program works unchanged over every extension field of the same characteristic.

**Proof.** Put (9/4+\delta=A/B) in lowest terms, with positive integers (A,B). Enumerate stages (s=1,2,\ldots). At stage (s), enumerate every (2\le u\le s), (1\le r\le s) satisfying the exact integer inequality
\[
 r^B<u^A.
\]
For each pair enumerate all coefficient arrays of total encoding length at most (s) over (K_c). For (\mathbb Q), enumerate reduced signed numerator/positive denominator strings; for (\mathbb F_p), enumerate residue arrays. Verify each proposed rank-(r) identity by expanding the (u^3) coefficients of the two trilinear forms and comparing exactly. This verification is finite and decidable. By the premise, at least one qualifying pair has a finite coefficient array; its (u,r), and encoding length are all eventually at most a stage (s). At that stage it is enumerated and accepted. Thus the procedure halts. All output is an ordinary finite bitstring. Its correctness over any extension follows because the prime-subfield identity embeds. \(\square\)

This is a *proof of total computability*, not a practical extractor. It does not compute an a priori height bound from `p,delta`; the witness height is learned only when the search succeeds. If `S=max{u,r,h,2}`, where `h` is the largest coefficient encoding length of the first witness encountered, its full coefficient array has at most `O(r u^2 h)=O(S^4)` bits. The staged search therefore halts by stage `O(S^4)` and uses at most `2^(O(S^4))*poly(S,log p,A log S+B log S)` bit work, where `9/4+delta=A/B` in lowest terms. The power comparison is exact and may itself have bit length proportional to `A log u+B log r`; thus the displayed bound is not polynomial in the binary encoding length of `delta` in general. This bound is in terms of the unknown first-witness size `S` and numeric exponent numerator/denominator `A,B`, not a useful bound in the user inputs. The source supplies no useful upper bound on `S` or on the search time. A stronger resource claim would need effective witness-size/height bounds and a tighter exponent-comparison interface.

This search is for exact rank certificates of growing matrix-multiplication blocks. It is **not** an algorithm that converts an arbitrary supplied border-rank degeneration into an exact same-rank decomposition of its limit; the W counterexample below rules out that stronger one-shot interface.

After the search, the recursive bilinear algorithm has `O(n^(log_u r))` field operations on powers of `u`, with padding changing only a fixed factor; taking `delta<epsilon` gives the advertised `O_(c,epsilon)(n^(9/4+epsilon))` arithmetic exponent. This is exactly where an exponent differs from a bounded-time compiler: `u,r,h`, search time, practical crossover, and the field-element evaluator all remain cost parameters. For integer inputs of `b` bits, once a block is known, the recursive program is executable by exact rational arithmetic. Let `Delta` be the product of all local coefficient denominators, and let `M=max(2, ||a_s||_1, ||b_s||_1, ||c_s||_1 over s)`. At recursion depth `L=ceil(log_u n)`, each input linear form has coefficient `l1` norm at most `M^L` and common denominator dividing `Delta^L`; each output is a sum of at most `r^L` products of two such forms times an output coefficient from a tensor product of `L` local coefficients. A common-denominator implementation therefore bounds every intermediate numerator and denominator by `O(2b + L(3 log_2 Delta + 3 log_2 M + log_2 r + 1))` bits. This is finite and explicit once the block is known. The search time, `Delta`, and `M` have no useful source-derived bound, so no `N`-only bit-time theorem follows.

The first-witness search has a matched baseline: for a given (u,r), exact verification is only a polynomial-time certificate check in the coefficient encoding length (expand (u^3) identities); the expensive part is discovering a coefficient array. Exhaustive search is deliberately the worst possible acquisition baseline. The official arithmetic proof's existential block is not falsely labelled a finite-bit lower bound: the search removes the bare *computability* objection, while leaving the actual resource question unresolved.

## 3. Exact finite-bit compiler from a supplied Laurent witness

This theorem handles a different input contract. Let (F=\mathbb Q), (T\in F^{n_1}\otimes F^{n_2}\otimes F^{n_3}), and suppose the caller supplies exact rational Laurent vectors in
\[
 G(t)=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i(t)\otimes b_i(t)\otimes c_i(t),
 \qquad G(t)=T+O(t),
\]
where every coordinate exponent of every factor lies in ([-d,d]). The latter is a *formal identity*: all negative-degree coefficients cancel exactly and the constant coefficient is (T). Suppose (C) scalar Laurent coefficients are encoded, each numerator and denominator has at most (H) bits.

For every (m\ge1), an exact compiler outputs a rank-one decomposition of (T^{\otimes m}) with at most
\[
 R_m=r^m(2md+1)^2
\]
terms over (\mathbb Q). The proof is coefficient extraction. Expand (G(t)^{\otimes m}); its constant coefficient is (T^{\otimes m}). For each of the (r^m) choices of one summand from each copy, write the three factor coefficient vectors as (A_u,B_v,C_w), with indices in ([-md,md]). Its constant coefficient is
\[
 \sum_{u+v+w=0}A_u\otimes B_v\otimes C_w.
\]
Choose (u,v); then (w=-u-v) is fixed, giving at most ((2md+1)^2) rank-one terms. Summing over the (r^m) choices proves the bound.

**Finite-bit charge.** Let \(\Delta\) be the product of all input coefficient denominators, so \(\log_2\Delta\le CH\). Each coordinate of an (m)-fold coefficient vector is a sum of at most ((2d+1)^m) products of (m) input rationals. A common denominator is (\Delta^m); numerator and denominator bit lengths are therefore
\[
 H_m=O\bigl(mCH+mH+m\log(d+1)\bigr).
\]
This is an explicit finite bound from the supplied record. A dense output writes at most
\[
 R_m(n_1^m+n_2^m+n_3^m)
\]
rational coordinates, plus their indices, so its bit length is at most this count times (O(H_m+m\log(n_1+n_2+n_3)+\log R_m)). The construction can compute each required coefficient vector by successive one-variable polynomial convolution, using (O(m^2d^2)) rational operations per coordinate; across the (r^m) summand words and the ((2md+1)) possible exponents per mode this is bounded by the explicit output scale times a polynomial in (m,d). Construction time is output-sensitive and generally exponential in (m); there is no claim of a polynomial-in-(m) total output algorithm.

The input witness verification also has a direct exact procedure: for every tensor coordinate, convolve the three coordinate Laurent polynomials for each of (r) terms, sum, and check all coefficients in ([-3d,3d]), verifying zero below degree zero and target (T) at degree zero. This uses (O(rn_1n_2n_3d^2)) rational operations. The bit cost is polynomial in the full input encoding length; a simple common-denominator implementation has intermediate coefficient lengths polynomial in (CH+\log(d+1)). This is verification, not witness acquisition.

For a represented extension (E/F) of degree (k), the same coefficient extraction first gives (r^m(2md+1)^2) rank-one terms over (E). Supply an (F)-basis and multiplication table, and an (F)-linear functional \(\ell:E\to F\) with \(\ell(1)=1\). Expanding each of the three vectors in that basis and applying \(\ell\) to tensor entries turns each (E)-simple tensor into at most (k^3) (F)-simple tensors. Thus the descended rank bound is (k^3r^m(2md+1)^2), and the extension basis, multiplication constants, heights, and bit costs must all be charged. This extraction is exact and needs no numerical conditioning parameter.

If the input consists only of approximate coefficients, finite precision is not by itself a witness. If each unknown coefficient is known to be rational with denominator at most (Q=2^H), distinct candidates are separated by at least (Q^{-2}); a certified absolute error below (1/(2Q^2)) permits unique bounded-denominator reconstruction, after which the formal identity must still be verified exactly. Without a denominator/height bound there is no uniform precision threshold: (0) and (1/q) are indistinguishable to any fixed tolerance once (q) is large enough. Exact symbolic Laurent coefficients avoid this numerical step. A height bound on the input coefficients does not control the condition number of a specialized decomposition.

This is a useful supplied-witness exactification lemma, but its explicit output has (r^m\operatorname{poly}(m)) terms and dense vectors of dimension (n_i^m). It establishes an asymptotic rank exponent, not a compact circuit for applying the decomposition to selected coordinates.

## 4. Independent obstruction: the W tensor and finite-field semantics

Let
\[
 W=e_1\otimes e_0\otimes e_0+e_0\otimes e_1\otimes e_0+e_0\otimes e_0\otimes e_1
 \in(\mathbb Q^2)^{\otimes3}.
\]
For nonzero rational (t),
\[
 W(t)=t^{-1}(e_0+te_1)^{\otimes3}-t^{-1}e_0^{\otimes3}
 =W+t(e_1e_1e_0+e_1e_0e_1+e_0e_1e_1)+t^2e_1e_1e_1.
\]
Thus (W(t)) has rank at most two and converges exactly coefficientwise to (W). But (W) has rank three: its two third-mode slices are
\[
 A_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and \(\det(xA_0+yA_1)=-x^2\). A rank-two decomposition would force the pencil determinant to be a nonzero constant times two independent linear factors (all three factor pairs must be independent, since the slices are independent and (A_0) is invertible); those factors have two distinct roots over an algebraic closure. Contradiction. Three displayed simple tensors show rank exactly three. Hence the given border-rank-two witness cannot be compiled into an exact rank-two decomposition of the original tensor, regardless of precision. It *can* be used for the tensor-power extraction theorem above, giving \(\operatorname{rank}(W^{\otimes m})\le2^m(2m+1)^2\).

On the standard specialization (t=2^{-p}), the two summands have norm sum
\[
 2^p\bigl((1+2^{-2p})^{3/2}+1\bigr)=\Theta(2^p),
\]
while \(\|W(2^{-p})\|_F^2=3+3\cdot2^{-2p}+2^{-4p}=\Theta(1)\). Thus this representation's cancellation condition is \(\Theta(2^p)\), and its coefficient encoding needs \(\Theta(p)\) bits. Under termwise floating-point evaluation, the usual error bound is (O(u2^p)) for unit roundoff (u); keeping that evaluation error below the approximation error (2^{-p}) requires (u=O(2^{-2p})), about (2p+O(1)) working bits. This is the cost of this unstable specialization route, not a universal precision lower bound; formal rational coefficient extraction is exact.

A second interface pitfall is finite-field equality. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), (x^2=x) for both input values, but the formal polynomial (x^2-x=x^2+x\) is nonzero in \(\mathbb F_2[x]\). Thus a verifier that substitutes pointwise function equality for formal tensor coefficient identity can accept a false polynomial identity. The family-107 all-fields formalization explicitly separates pointwise `P.Correct` semantics from its stronger coefficient-rank theorem; this report does not allege that its formal rank theorem makes this mistake.

Both examples are concrete failure modes for underspecified extraction: border rank alone does not preserve one-shot exact rank, and pointwise function semantics do not certify formal coefficient identities. They do not imply a lower bound against algorithms that use a different construction.

## 5. Selective outputs, matched costs, and decisive missing premise

From an exact rank-(r) block decomposition of (T_u), the usual recursion computes a full square product with (O(n^{\log_u r})) field operations. Running that algorithm on zero-padded (N\times N) inputs and discarding unrequested entries gives an exact but nonselective bound (O(N^{9/4+\varepsilon})) under L03. For the thin target, direct selected dot products cost
\[
 O(ND+D|W|)=O(N^{17/8})
\]
at (D=N^{1/4}), (|W|\le N^{15/8}). Dense rectangular multiplication costs (O(N^2D)=O(N^{9/4})). Neither bound is (O(N^{2-\eta})). More importantly, the scalar rank (r) does not bound the number of recursive products needed after pruning an arbitrary mask: that depends on the supports and cancellations of the output factors and on a mask-aware decoder. A supplied full-product algorithm may be postfiltered, but no arbitrary-mask saving follows from its exponent alone.

For `b`-bit integer inputs, the direct method reads `O(ND b)` input bits, uses `O(ND+D|W|)` integer arithmetic operations, and writes `O(|W|(b+log D))` output bits. With `b=O(log N)`, ordinary integer multiplication gives `O(N^(17/8) polylog N)` bit time. The discovered-block full-product route has separate preprocessing `P_search(c,epsilon)` and, after it halts, `O(n^(log_u r) M(2b+L C_block))` bit operations plus output, where `M(q)` is the bit cost of q-bit multiplication, `L=ceil(log_u n)`, and `C_block=3 log_2 Delta+3 log_2 M_1+log_2 r+O(1)` uses the block's exact coefficient denominator product and factor l1 norms as in §2. This parameterization exposes the bit cost; it supplies no bound on `P_search`, `Delta`, or `M_1` as a function of characteristic and exponent slack.

There is a useful matched positive baseline: the existing Cycle 4 proof handles a Cartesian mask (I\times J) by independent (D\times D) Strassen tiles at cost (O(|I||J|D^{\log_2 7-2})), including recombination and the stated bit ledger. This is subquadratic for its selected-column family; its proof explicitly stops before arbitrary masks. It confirms that structure in (W), not just the full multiplication exponent, is load-bearing.

Therefore the new exhaustive search answers one narrow question positively: a field-characteristic-parameterized finite-bit *block finder exists in the computability sense* (conditional on L03), despite not being displayed by the source. It does not answer the requested-output complexity question. The decisive missing premise for an AP/bit transfer is an explicit, uniform, mask-aware exact circuit whose acquisition/preprocessing time and description length are charged, whose coefficients have bounded encodings and a given field evaluator, and whose shared arithmetic plus selected-output decoder is proved below (N^{2-\eta}). No such premise is supplied by L03 or by the border-rank coefficient-extraction lemma.
