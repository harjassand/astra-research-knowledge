## A. Spontaneous magnetization: exact claim

OpenAI manuscript, 24 September 2026. H_Λ=−Σ_{〈x,y〉}S_x·S_y on nearest-neighbor Z^d, fixed d≥3 and positive half-integral S. Main theorem `thm:main`: there is a finite β₀(d,S) such that for each fixed finite β≥β₀ there is a translation-invariant, zero-field β-KMS state with ω(S₀^z)≥S/4. It does not assert nonzero magnetization of the symmetric zero-field finite-volume Gibbs state. The positive-field state selection and thermodynamic limit are explicit.

### A1. Loop representation and uniform mark bound

`01-loops.tex`: ℓ=2S slots at each site, all ℓ² exchange edges per neighboring pair at rate1/2, and sitewise uniform permutation seams. Projection onto symmetric site tensors reproduces the quantum trace. Each loop receives factor2, and continuous boundary pins fix its color to up. Missing-loop weights w_D=2^{n_D} give the exact pinned measure. An inserted exchange increases the missing-loop count by at most one; Mecke therefore gives an n-mark factorial comparison factor≤2^n. This is unconditional in the pinned loop measure and is not a bound conditional on an arbitrary exposure. Dense boundary pin limits are finite-volume bounded-convergence arguments, not uniform-volume rejection estimates.

### A2. The simultaneous pin test

`02-pin-test.tex`: the polynomial ∏_i(x_i+y_{π(i)}) is stable; output transposition averaging preserves complex multiaffine stability. Eliminating an unpinned index by identifying x_q=y_q and extracting its linear coefficient splices that index and pays precisely the factor2 for pin-free cycles. Differentiating at the old pins and specializing yields the stable distribution of images of the new pins. Its strong-Rayleigh negative correlation gives the product bound for simultaneous returns. The old/new loop-weight comparison gives

    P_E(T avoids E)≤(2r)^{|T|},

where r is the average return fraction under the **more-pinned** law P_{E∪T}. The order of the two laws is consequential; interchanging them would break the induction.

### A3. Exact exposure and fresh-turn first moment

`03-exposure.tex`: expose all pin-free loops with their complete histories and endpoint mappings. Two independent clock families realize the unconditioned residual exchanges at fixed exposure; site completions are uniform. Actual conditional residual law is Q_a(.|G_P), with outer density containing g_P(a). The independent-transposition exterior-moment identity and the arbitrary-W determinant proof yield the exact Schur hitting kernel. The fresh-turn walk resamples transitions every period. Its killed one-period U has nonnegative row and column sums≤1, hence is an ℓ² contraction. Minimal hitting f exists by monotone finite-horizon recursion, without certain boundary absorption. Harmonicity outside T yields

    |T|(1−r)≥E_P〈f,(I−U)f〉.

The finite proof and its sampler/physical-flip limitations are independently saved in `EXTERIOR_PIN_TRANSFER_AND_OBSTRUCTIONS.md`.

### A4. The sparse environment induction

`04-sparse-geometry.tex`: admissible pin sets have growth exponent α=1/100. Under the induction assumption, grid blocking of prescribed sparse sites has base p, and a nongrid-time blocking test uses either old blocking or a nearby actual mark. Splitting sites and using factorial moments avoids an independence claim. Large components produce binary multiscale witnesses with distinct leaves and controlled growth. Choosing a fixed large scale factor b with b^α>4 gives a uniform component tail exp(−c h^γ), γ=log_b2>0. It may be extremely small but is fixed before p.

At a regular anchor, local deletion of fewer than2d sites leaves bounded-radius connectivity; the distant component condition supplies polynomially small detours. The regularity failure bound is δ≤CR^d p^{2d}+Cexp(−cR^{νγ}), ν=1/(100d). This is a fixed-time/averaged-time statement, not an all-times event. The local Nash inequality follows by mapping available-slot maxima to the full lattice with bounded short-path energy cost.

### A5. Finite-energy flows and the same-environment pairing

`05-flows.tex`: thickened obstacle components have disjoint or nested filled hulls. Collapse each maximal hull **once** to an exterior representative; do not assume the representative map is idempotent. Routed adjacent hull endpoints require the reversed middle edge. Boundary connectivity is proved by cut parity and a graph argument, with the nearest-neighbor detour version separately credited to Timár/Kesten. An orthant unit flow is pushed through the routes and a bounded connector. A target edge has only polynomially many source preimages, without an extra path-length factor.

The flow envelope is r^{−(d−1−dν)}. Its square is summable in d≥3, and the overlap exponent d−2−2dν exceeds α. Subunit injections at sparse regular anchors therefore admit a deterministic flow of energy≤C|T|R^α. Pairing with the evolving g=f−1_T works even when g, the injection and the environment are dependent. No probabilistic independence is used at this step.

### A6. Leakage and cancellation of the positive energy term

`06-leakage.tex`: U=BMA with disjoint initial and final length2s intervals, and their adjoints reverse the actual forced transports correctly. It gives a lower bound by half the two forward/reverse heat losses. The initial indicator η is localized in radiusR per anchor. Chronological paths shorter than the sampled period allow actual inserted marks to be counted once, alongside independent fresh marks. Localization loss is O(e^{−cs}); regular-time Nash smoothing gives O(s^{−d/2}+δ).

The retained local injection is paired with g by √(C|T|R^α D(g)). The time weights w(u)=2(2s−u)/s² and W(u)=(2s−u)²/s² satisfy ∫w²/W=4/s. Young's inequality cancels the positive accumulated energy rather than paying a divergent endpoint integral. The final error is

    ε(p)≤C[e^{−cs}+R^α(s^{−d/2}+δ+s^{−1})].

For s=p^{−11/10}, R≈s the decisive exponents are 1089/1000 for R^α/s, at least1639/1000 for R^αs^{−d/2}, and at least2689/1000 for R^{α+d}p^{2d}. They exceed1; the stretched-exponential term is o(p). Thus fixed small p_* makes2ε≤p_*, closing downward induction in the **finite grid pin set**. Taking dense grids gives uniform singleton boundary connectivity. The constants exist but no practical β₀ or sampler is acquired.

### A7. Physical state selection and KMS passage

`07-kms.tex`: the probability of all boundary cycles up costs exp(−C_β|∂Λ|), using Jensen and the unconditional boundary-mark mean. Conditioned magnetization has mean≥S(1−p_*); bounded support then gives a positive probability of M≥SV/2. The commuting field tilt h_N=N^{−1/2} beats this surface cost and yields magnetization≥S/4 in the limit. Translation averaging uses one fixed weak-* subsequence. Named finite-range Lieb–Robinson/norm-dynamics convergence supplies uniform deep-box convergence. A Gaussian multiplier and strip maximum principle transfer **both** KMS boundary orders along that same subsequence. This acquires a genuine zero-field KMS state, not only a ground-band state or a single-time magnetization marginal.

Here N is the cube half-side, Λ_N=[−N,N]^d∩Z^d, not the number of spins: the tilt is order N^{d−1/2} and its surface cost is order N^{d−1}. Relabelling N as volume would incorrectly reverse this comparison in d≥3.

The S=1/2 hard-core-boson application is an attractive lattice gas. Rotating the chosen ordered state gives nonzero mean b and an averaged ODLRO lower bound. It is not the repulsive continuum hard-sphere Gibbs theorem.

## B. Positive-temperature exact canonical BEC: exact claim

Main theorem `thm:main` gives a strictly positive fixed T_phys independent of volume and a positive liminf constant-orbital fraction in the exact canonical Gibbs state. Scaled parameters are r=α/D, D=N/K³≫1, α in a fixed window, and0<T≤1/D. There is no asserted sharp critical temperature or efficient Gibbs preparation.
