Improved estimate of local Markovianity for quantum Gibbs states
Tai Hsuan Yang
Affiliation: Department of Physics and Institute for Condensed Matter Theory, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

Abstract
We establish improved locally Markovian properties of quantum Gibbs states at every fixed positive temperature.
For finite-range interactions of bounded interaction degree, the conditional mutual information (CMI) decays exponentially with separation, with a prefactor exponential in the boundary size.
This improves the exponential volume dependence of the local Markov bounds established by Chen and Rouzé [9].
For exponentially and stretched-exponentially decaying interactions, we obtain stretched-exponential CMI decay with a boundary-dependent prefactor.
For power-law interactions, we prove inverse-logarithmic or algebraic decay with polynomial region-size dependence.
Our proof is static, in contrast to the dynamical approach of Chen and Rouzé.
We compare the Gibbs state with a product state obtained by removing boundary interactions, reducing the CMI bound to a relative-entropy loss that we control using locality estimates.

I Introduction
Local interactions constrain thermal correlations most directly through conditional independence.
In a classical Gibbs distribution, conditioning on a region  $B$  that separates  $A$  from  $C$  makes the variables in  $A$  and  $C$  independent.
The Hammersley–Clifford theorem expresses this connection between positive Markov random fields and Gibbs distributions with clique-local interactions [12].
It is distinct from decay of ordinary correlations: a classical Gibbs distribution retains its Markov property even at a thermal phase transition.
The quantum analogue asks how much conditional dependence can survive across a wide shielding region when the interaction terms need not commute.
Let  $\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C$  be a finite set of sites partitioned into three regions, and write  $|X|$  for the number of sites in a region  $X$ . For a quantum state  $\rho_{ABC}$  on this partition, conditional dependence is measured by the conditional mutual information (CMI),
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho}=S(\rho_{AB})+S(\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_{B})-S(\rho_{ABC}),\qquad S(\omega)=-\Tr(\omega\log\omega),$
(1)
where  $\rho_{X}=\Tr_{\Lambda\setminus X}\rho$  denotes the reduced state on  $X$  and logarithms are natural. Vanishing CMI is equivalent to exact recovery of  $A$  from  $B$ : there is a completely positive trace-preserving map  $\mathcal{R}_{B\to AB}$  with  $\rho_{ABC}=(\mathcal{R}_{B\to AB}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{C})(\rho_{BC})$  [13]. The Fawzi–Renner theorem makes this interpretation quantitative, bounding the recovery error in terms of the CMI [11].
Gibbs states of commuting local Hamiltonians have the exact Markov property whenever  $B$  shields  $A$  from  $C$  in the interaction graph [3]. Noncommuting Hamiltonians generally require an approximate statement: for  $\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}=e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}}/\Tr e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}}$ , does the CMI decay as the separation  $r=d(A,C)$  increases? Here  $H_{\Lambda}$  is the Hamiltonian on  $\Lambda$ ,  $\beta>0$  is the inverse temperature, and  $d(A,C)$  is the minimum distance between sites in  $A$  and  $C$ .
We write  $n=|\Lambda|$  for the total number of sites and  $D$  for the spatial dimension. The positive constants  $C_{\beta},c_{\beta}$  may depend on temperature and fixed interaction parameters, but not on the regions or total volume.

The first general results for noncommuting systems were obtained in one dimension. Araki’s analysis established locality and clustering properties of finite-range quantum spin chains at positive temperature [1]. Building on one-dimensional clustering and quantum belief propagation, Kato and Brandão proved a CMI bound decaying exponentially in the square root of the buffer length at every fixed positive temperature [14]. Kuwahara subsequently obtained exponential decay in the buffer length in one dimension, and an arbitrary-temperature pairwise Markov bound in higher dimensions [16]. The latter uses locality estimates for effective Hamiltonians of reduced states, with an exponential dependence on the sizes of both outer regions.
In a major advance, Chen and Rouzé established local Markovianity for finite-range Hamiltonians with bounded interaction degree at every finite inverse temperature  $\beta>0$  and in arbitrary spatial dimension [9].
Their proof constructs a recovery map by time-averaging the evolution generated by the quasi-local Kubo–Martin–Schwinger (KMS) detailed-balanced Gibbs sampler [6, 7, 5, 10].
At the same time, Kato and Kuwahara used quasi-local quantum belief-propagation channels to prove CMI bounds of the form  $\operatorname{poly}(n)e^{-c_{\beta}r^{1/(D+3)}}$  under rapid mixing of the KMS detailed-balanced Gibbs sampler and  $\operatorname{poly}(n)e^{-c_{\beta}r^{1/D}}$  under uniform clustering [15].
With the assumption of high temperature, Bakshi, Liu, Moitra, and Tang later obtain the exponential bound  $C_{\beta}|A||C|e^{-c_{\beta}r}$  for local Hamiltonians with bounded interaction and growth parameters,
using a quantum Dobrushin condition and rapid mixing [2, Theorem 2.3].
More recently, Rosa-Ruiz, Scandi, Capel, and Alhambra proved CMI decay for shielded partitions of fixed points of primitive, frustration-free, rapidly mixing  $k$ -local Lindbladians [19].
Their bounds have polynomial region-size dependence and give exponential decay for short-range interactions and algebraic decay for sufficiently fast power-law interactions.
Separately, they extended the local Markov property of Chen and Rouzé to Gibbs states of  $k$ -local Hamiltonians with power-law decay exponent  $\alpha>D$  at every finite inverse temperature  $\beta>0$ ,
obtaining algebraic CMI decay up to logarithmic factors, with a prefactor exponential in one outer-region size and linear in the other.
Our main quantitative improvement is to replace the exponential volume dependence by exponential boundary dependence. Write  $H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}\Phi(X)$ , where  $\Phi(X)$  is supported on  $X$ , and let  $A^{c}=\Lambda\setminus A$ . With  $\|\cdot\|_{\infty}$  denoting the operator norm, for finite-range interactions we prove
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}e^{C_{\beta}g_{A\mid A^{c}}-c_{\beta}r},\qquad g_{A\mid A^{c}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq\Lambda\\
X\cap A\neq\varnothing,\ X\cap A^{c}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty},$
(2)
Here  $g_{A\mid A^{c}}$  is the total interaction strength across the cut. On the lattice  $\mathbb{Z}^{D}$ , the cut strength  $g_{A\mid A^{c}}$  is bounded by a constant times  $|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|$ , the number of nearest-neighbor edges crossing the boundary. Thus the exponential factor improves from  $e^{O_{\beta}(|A|)}$  to  $e^{O_{\beta}(|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)}$ .
We also extend the argument to quasi-local interactions.
Under the aggregate decay assumption  $\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\leq J_{\mu,\theta}e^{-\mu d(x,y)^{\theta}}$  of Theorem II.2,
where  $\mu>0$  and  $J_{\mu,\theta}<\infty$  are fixed and  $0<\theta\leq 1$ , we obtain stretched-exponential CMI decay in  $D\geq 2$
without a  $k$ -local assumption on the interaction terms.
For power-law interactions satisfying
$\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\leq J_{\zeta}(1+d(x,y))^{-\zeta}$  with fixed  $J_{\zeta}<\infty$  and decay exponent  $\zeta>D$ ,
Theorem II.3 gives inverse-logarithmic CMI decay for  $D<\zeta\leq 2D$  (with a squared iterated-logarithm correction at  $\zeta=2D$ ) and algebraic decay for  $\zeta>2D$ .
This differs in scope from the  $k$ -local power-law Gibbs result in Ref. [19]: our support sizes are unrestricted, while the algebraic theorem uses the stronger sufficient threshold  $\zeta>2D$ .
Table 1 compares these results with previous CMI bounds, listing their assumptions, distance decay, and region-size dependence. It highlights the boundary-dependent prefactors in our finite-range and stretched-exponential bounds and the polynomial region-size dependence in our power-law bounds.
Our approach is static: we compare the Gibbs state with the reference state obtained by removing interactions across the cut  $A\mid BC$ .
The reference state is a product across this cut, and the CMI is bounded by the decrease in relative entropy between the two states when  $C$  is traced out.
A variational bound based on a resolvent formula reduces the estimate of this loss to spatial localization, controlled by Lieb–Robinson bounds [17, 8], and spectral estimates for the relative modular operator.
For finite-range interactions, short-imaginary-time estimates control the prefactor in terms of the interaction strength across the cut.
For the stretched-exponential and algebraic power-law bounds, we first truncate long interactions within a shielding neighborhood around  $A$  and control the resulting change in CMI by Gibbs interpolation.
We then combine spatial localization near  $A$  with spectral bounds derived from the decay of interaction strengths with support size to derive the final CMI estimates.

Sections IV, V, and VI prove the finite-range, stretched-exponential, and power-law bounds, respectively. Section VII discusses their scope and open questions. Appendices A and B provide auxiliary proofs, and Appendix C collects the notation.
II Setting and main results
Let  $\Gamma$  be a set of sites with distance  $d(x,y)$ , and assign a finite-dimensional Hilbert space  $\mathcal{H}_{x}$  to each site  $x$ . On  $\mathbb{Z}^{D}$ , we use the nearest-neighbor graph metric  $d(x,y)=|x-y|_{1}$ , where  $|z|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{D}|z_{j}|$  for  $z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{D})\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}$ . For a finite volume  $\Lambda\Subset\Gamma$ , all operators are understood to act on  $\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{x}$ , with identities on omitted tensor factors. We write  $XY=X\cup Y$  for disjoint regions,  $X^{c}=\Lambda\setminus X$ , and  $d(X,Y)=\min_{x\in X,y\in Y}d(x,y)$  for nonempty finite sets. We use the ordinary, unnormalized trace, natural logarithms, the operator norm  $\|O\|_{\infty}$ , the trace norm  $\|O\|_{1}=\Tr\sqrt{O^{\dagger}O}$ , and the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product  $\langle X,Y\rangle=\Tr(X^{\dagger}Y)$ , with norm  $\|O\|_{2}^{2}=\langle O,O\rangle$ . Entropy and CMI are defined in (1); the relative entropy is
$D(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma).$
(3)
We consider the Gibbs state
$H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}\Phi(X),\qquad\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}}}{Z_{\Lambda}},\qquad Z_{\Lambda}=\Tr e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}},\qquad\beta>0,$
(4)
where  $\Phi(X)=\Phi(X)^{\dagger}$  is supported on  $X$ . An empty-support scalar term can be omitted. All Gibbs states and their marginals are faithful in finite volume.
Throughout the main theorems,
$\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C,\qquad r=d(A,C),$
(5)
is a full partition, with  $A$  and  $C$  nonempty. Thus  $B$  contains every site of  $\Lambda$  outside  $A\cup C$ . This hypothesis is essential to the cut-Hamiltonian argument; a marginal with an arbitrary unobserved exterior is not silently identified with a local Gibbs state.
For each interaction class, define the cut interaction and its total strength by
$V=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq\Lambda\\
X\cap A\neq\varnothing,\ X\cap A^{c}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\Phi(X),\qquad g_{A\mid A^{c}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq\Lambda\\
X\cap A\neq\varnothing,\ X\cap A^{c}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}.$
(6)
In particular,  $\|V\|_{\infty}\leq g_{A\mid A^{c}}$ . When interactions are indexed by terms  $h_{\gamma}$  instead of supports  $X$ , the same definitions are taken term by term.
The quantitative statements below imply this property and, through the recovery theorem, the existence of recovery maps supported on the shielding neighborhood of  $A$ .
Theorem II.1 (Finite-range interactions).
Suppose
$H_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\gamma}h_{\gamma},\qquad\|h_{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq J,\qquad\diam(\supp h_{\gamma})\leq R_{0}.$
(7)
Assume that each term overlaps at most  $\mathfrak{d}$  terms, counting itself. The bounds  $J>0$ ,  $R_{0}$ , and  $\mathfrak{d}$  are uniform in  $\Lambda$ . For every fixed  $\beta>0$ , there exist  $C_{\beta},c_{\beta}>0$ , depending only on these interaction parameters and  $\beta$ , such that
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}\exp\!\left(C_{\beta}g_{A\mid A^{c}}-c_{\beta}r\right).$
(8)
On  $\mathbb{Z}^{D}$  with its nearest-neighbor graph metric, let  $\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A$  denote the set of unoriented nearest-neighbor edges with exactly one endpoint in  $A$ . Then  $g_{A\mid A^{c}}\leq C|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|$ , with  $C$  depending only on the local interaction parameters, and therefore
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}e^{C_{\beta}|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|-c_{\beta}r}.$
(9)
Theorem II.2 (Stretched-exponential interactions of unrestricted body size).
Let  $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{D}$ ,  $D\geq 2$ , with the  $\ell^{1}$  metric and uniformly bounded on-site dimensions. Suppose  $\mu>0$ ,  $0<\theta\leq 1$ , and
$J_{\mu,\theta}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}e^{\mu d(x,y)^{\theta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
x,y\in X\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}<\infty.$
(10)
The supremum includes  $x=y$ . Thus this is the aggregate pair bound
$\sum_{X\ni x,y}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\leq J_{\mu,\theta}e^{-\mu d(x,y)^{\theta}}$ .
No uniform bound on  $|X|$  or on the number of terms meeting a site is assumed.
For every fixed  $\beta>0$ , there are constants  $C_{\beta},c_{\beta}>0$  depending only on
$\beta,\ D,\ \mu,\ \theta,\ J_{\mu,\theta},$
and, for  $C_{\beta}$ , the fixed bound on the on-site dimensions, such that
$\begin{split}I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}\exp\!\bigl[C_{\beta}(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}-c_{\beta}(1+r)^{\theta/(D+1-\theta)}\bigr].\end{split}$
(11)
The boundary  $\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A$  is taken in the ambient lattice  $\mathbb{Z}^{D}$ , including edges leaving  $\Lambda$ . The estimate is uniform over all finite  $\Lambda$  and all full partitions.
Theorem II.3 (Power-law interactions of unrestricted body size).
Let  $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{D}$  with the nearest-neighbor graph metric and uniformly bounded on-site dimensions. Assume
$J_{\zeta}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}(1+d(x,y))^{\zeta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
x,y\in X\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}<\infty,\qquad\zeta>D,$
(12)
with the supremum including  $x=y$ . No bound on  $|X|$  or on the number of terms meeting a site is required. Fix  $\beta>0$  and a full partition  $\Lambda=A\sqcup B\sqcup C$  with nonempty  $A,C$ . Write  $r=d(A,C)$  and
$b_{\zeta}=1+\frac{2\zeta}{D},\qquad\nu_{\zeta}=\frac{b_{\zeta}(\zeta-2D)}{\zeta-D+b_{\zeta}}.$
(13)
For  $D<\zeta<2D$ ,
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}(1+|A|)^{b_{\zeta}+1}[\log(e+r)]^{-b_{\zeta}}.$
(14)
At the endpoint  $\zeta=2D$ ,
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}(1+|A|)^{6}[\log(e+r)]^{-5}[\log(e+\log(e+r))]^{2}.$
(15)
For  $\zeta>2D$ ,
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{\Lambda,\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}(1+|A|)^{b_{\zeta}+1}(1+r)^{-\nu_{\zeta}}.$
(16)
The constants depend only on  $\beta,D,J_{\zeta},\zeta$  and the fixed on-site dimension bound, not on  $\Lambda,A,B,C$  or  $r$ . All estimates hold uniformly over all full partitions of finite volumes.
Unless indicated otherwise, constants denoted by  $C,c,C_{\beta},c_{\beta}$  may be enlarged or decreased from one occurrence to the next.
They never depend on the finite volume or the chosen regions; all region-size dependence is displayed explicitly.
III The entropy-loss energy and its variational upper bound
For faithful states  $\rho,\sigma$  on a tensor product  $R\otimes E$ , put
$\delta_{R}(\rho,\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_{R}\|\sigma_{R}).$
(17)
III.1 Comparison with a cut Gibbs Hamiltonian
Lemma III.1 (Cut-Hamiltonian comparison).
Let  $H_{\Lambda}=H_{0}+V$ , where  $V$  is given by (6). For  $R=A,BC$ , define
$H_{R}=\sum_{X\subseteq R}\Phi(X),\qquad\gamma_{R}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{R}}}{\Tr e^{-\beta H_{R}}},\qquad\gamma_{B}=\Tr_{C}\gamma_{BC}.$
(18)
Set  $Z_{0}=\Tr e^{-\beta H_{0}}$  and  $Z=\Tr e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}}=Z_{\Lambda}$ . Then
$H_{0}=H_{A}+H_{BC},\qquad\sigma=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{0}}}{Z_{0}}=\gamma_{A}\otimes\gamma_{BC},\qquad\rho=\frac{e^{-\beta(H_{0}+V)}}{Z}$
(19)
satisfy
$\delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma)=I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho}+D(\rho_{BC}\|\gamma_{BC})-D(\rho_{B}\|\gamma_{B}),\quad\text{and}\quad I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho}\leq\delta_{AB}(\rho,\sigma).$
(20)
Moreover,
$|\log Z-\log Z_{0}|\leq\beta\|V\|_{\infty}\leq\beta g_{A\mid A^{c}}.$
(21)
Proof.
Expand the two relative entropies, use the tensor-product form of  $\sigma$ , and collect the four entropies appearing in CMI. This gives the first line of (20). Data processing under  $\Tr_{C}$  makes its last difference nonnegative.
For the free-energy estimate, let  $Z(s)=\Tr e^{-\beta(H_{0}+sV)}$ . Differentiation under the trace gives
$\frac{d}{ds}\log Z(s)=-\beta\Tr(\rho_{s}V),\qquad\rho_{s}=Z(s)^{-1}e^{-\beta(H_{0}+sV)}.$
(22)
Its absolute value is at most  $\beta\|V\|_{\infty}$ . Integration from  $0$  to  $1$  proves (21).

III.2 A variational resolvent formula
For the moment,  $\rho,\sigma$  are arbitrary faithful states on  $R\otimes E$ . Let
$\Delta=L_{\sigma}R_{\rho^{-1}},\qquad\Delta(Y)=\sigma Y\rho^{-1},\qquad K=\log\Delta.$
(23)
Here  $L_{X}$  and  $R_{X}$  denote left and right multiplication on Hilbert–Schmidt space, respectively:  $L_{X}(Y)=XY$  and  $R_{X}(Y)=YX$ . The operators  $L_{\sigma}$  and  $R_{\rho^{-1}}$  are self-adjoint and commute, so  $\Delta$  is self-adjoint. Faithfulness of  $\rho$  and  $\sigma$  also gives, for every nonzero  $X$ ,
$\langle X,\Delta(X)\rangle=\Tr(X^{\dagger}\sigma X\rho^{-1})=\|\sigma^{1/2}X\rho^{-1/2}\|_{2}^{2}>0.$
(24)
Thus  $\Delta$  is strictly positive on Hilbert–Schmidt space, and  $K=\log\Delta$  is well defined and self-adjoint. If  $K=\sum_{j}k_{j}P_{j}$  is its spectral decomposition, where  $P_{j}$  projects onto the eigenspace with eigenvalue  $k_{j}$ , then  $f(K)=\sum_{j}f(k_{j})P_{j}$ . V Quasi-local interactions: spatial localization and modular tails
Compared with the finite-range case, two additional difficulties arise: individual interactions can span the shielding region, and their supports can contain arbitrarily many sites. The bounded-degree commutator counting used in Lemma IV.2 therefore no longer applies directly, so we cannot rely on the same short-imaginary-time bound for the negative modular moment.
We first remove interactions of large diameter that touch a neighborhood of  $A$ . A Gibbs-interpolation estimate bounds the resulting change in CMI, while the retained interactions admit a spatial localization estimate inside that neighborhood. Instead of using the negative-moment estimate in Lemma IV.2, we apply the resolvent bound (30) directly. Its small-resolvent contribution is controlled by spectral-tail estimates: spatial decay bounds the strength of interactions with large supports, which in turn controls moments of the relative modular logarithm with boundary-dependent prefactors. Combining this spectral control with spatial localization and balancing the truncation error against the spectral-tail contribution gives the stretched-exponential distance decay in Theorem II.2.

(74)

$\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{J_{\mu,\theta}J_{2a_{\mathrm{supp}}}}\,|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}|z|_{1}e^{-(\mu/2)|z|_{1}^{\theta}}.$
Here  $z=y-x$  groups pairs by their displacement. Since
$\sum_{z}|z|_{1}e^{-(\mu/2)|z|_{1}^{\theta}}<\infty$ , this proves (69).

V.2 A weighted commutator estimate
For an operator in a fixed finite volume, introduce the decomposition norm
$\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}:=\inf_{O=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}O_{X}}\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}e^{s|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_{X}\|_{\infty},\qquad 0\leq s\leq a_{\mathrm{supp}},$
(75)
where  $O_{X}$  is supported on  $X$ . A scalar multiple of the identity is allowed on the empty support. The label  $\mathrm{dec}$  distinguishes this support-weighted decomposition norm from the Schatten norms. In particular,
$\|O\|_{\infty}\leq\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}},\qquad\|\mathbf{1}\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}=1.$
The weights increase with  $s$ , so  $\|O\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}\leq\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}$  for  $0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s\leq a_{\mathrm{supp}}$ . The concavity inequality
$|X\cup Y|^{\theta/D}\leq|X|^{\theta/D}+|Y|^{\theta/D}$  gives
$\|OP\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}\leq\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}\|P\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}.$
(76)
The natural decomposition of the cut interaction and Lemma V.1 imply
$\|V\|_{a_{\mathrm{supp}},\mathrm{dec}}\leq C|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|.$
(77)
Lemma V.2 (Commutator loss of support weight).
For  $0\leq s^{\prime}
$\|[H_{0},O]\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}\leq 2J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\left[\frac{D}{e\theta(s-s^{\prime})}\right]^{D/\theta}\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}},$
(78)
where  $J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\leq J_{2a_{\mathrm{supp}}}$  is defined as in (68) with  $a_{\mathrm{supp}}$  in place of  $2a_{\mathrm{supp}}$ .
Proof.
Fix a decomposition  $O=\sum_{X}O_{X}$ . Only interaction terms meeting  $X$  contribute to  $[H_{0},O_{X}]$ . By subadditivity of the support weight,
$\|[H_{0},O_{X}]\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}\leq 2e^{s^{\prime}|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}\sum_{Y:Y\cap X\neq\varnothing}e^{s^{\prime}|Y|^{\theta/D}}\|\Phi(Y)\|_{\infty}\leq 2J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}|X|e^{s^{\prime}|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}.$
(79)
Removing cut terms from  $H_{0}$  only reduces the sum. To absorb the factor  $|X|$ , use
$\sup_{u\geq 0}ue^{-(s-s^{\prime})u^{\theta/D}}=\left[\frac{D}{e\theta(s-s^{\prime})}\right]^{D/\theta}.$
(80)
Writing  $e^{s^{\prime}|X|^{\theta/D}}=e^{s|X|^{\theta/D}}e^{-(s-s^{\prime})|X|^{\theta/D}}$  and applying this bound with  $u=|X|$  gives
$\displaystyle\|[H_{0},O]\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}$
$\displaystyle\leq\sum_{X}\|[H_{0},O_{X}]\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}$
(81)
$\displaystyle\leq 2J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\sum_{X}|X|e^{-(s-s^{\prime})|X|^{\theta/D}}e^{s|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}$
$\displaystyle\leq 2J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\left[\frac{D}{e\theta(s-s^{\prime})}\right]^{D/\theta}\sum_{X}e^{s|X|^{\theta/D}}\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}.$
This holds for every decomposition of  $O$ .
Taking the infimum of the final sum gives  $\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}$  by (75), proving (78).
∎
V.3 Moments of the relative modular logarithm
Recall  $K=\log\Delta$  from (23). Since left and right multiplication commute,
$K=L_{\log\sigma}-R_{\log\rho},\qquad\log\sigma=-\beta H_{0}-(\log Z_{0})\mathbf{1},\qquad\log\rho=-\beta(H_{0}+V)-(\log Z)\mathbf{1}.$
(82)
Using  $[\rho^{1/2},\log\rho]=0$ , we obtain, for every operator  $O$ ,
$\displaystyle K(O\rho^{1/2})$
$\displaystyle=(\log\sigma)O\rho^{1/2}-O\rho^{1/2}\log\rho$
(83)
$\displaystyle=\bigl((\log\sigma)O-O\log\rho\bigr)\rho^{1/2}$
$\displaystyle=\left(-\beta H_{0}O+\beta O(H_{0}+V)+(\log Z-\log Z_{0})O\right)\rho^{1/2}$
$\displaystyle=\left(\beta[O,H_{0}]+\beta OV+(\log Z-\log Z_{0})O\right)\rho^{1/2}.$
The free-energy estimate also gives
$|\log Z-\log Z_{0}|\leq\beta\|V\|_{\infty}\leq\beta\|V\|_{a_{\mathrm{supp}},\mathrm{dec}}$ .
Combining Lemma V.2 with submultiplicativity and monotonicity of the weighted norms gives, for  $0\leq s^{\prime}
$\displaystyle\left\|\beta[O,H_{0}]+\beta OV+(\log Z-\log Z_{0})O\right\|_{s^{\prime},\mathrm{dec}}\leq\left(2\beta J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\left[\frac{D}{e\theta(s-s^{\prime})}\right]^{D/\theta}+2\beta\|V\|_{a_{\mathrm{supp}},\mathrm{dec}}\right)\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}.$
(84)
The last two terms each contribute at most
$\beta\|V\|_{a_{\mathrm{supp}},\mathrm{dec}}\|O\|_{s,\mathrm{dec}}$ , accounting for the factor  $2$ .
Lemma V.3 (Uniform modular moments and spectral tails).
Under (10), there is a constant  $C_{\beta}>0$ , depending only on  $\beta,D,\mu,\theta,J_{\mu,\theta}$ , such that for every integer  $n\geq 1$ ,
$\|K^{n}\rho^{1/2}\|_{2}\leq\left[C_{\beta}\left(n^{D/\theta}+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|\right)\right]^{n}.$
(85)
Consequently, for every  $\ell\geq 0$ ,
$\langle\rho^{1/2},\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|\geq\ell\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle\leq C_{\beta}\exp\!\left[C_{\beta}(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}-c_{\beta}\ell^{\theta/D}\right].$
(86)
Proof.
To apply (84)  $n$  times, decrease the support weight from  $a_{\mathrm{supp}}$  to  $0$  in equal steps  $a_{\mathrm{supp}}/n$ , starting from the identity. Equation (83) shows inductively that  $K^{n}\rho^{1/2}=O_{n}\rho^{1/2}$ , where
$\|O_{n}\|_{0,\mathrm{dec}}\leq\left[2\beta J_{a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\left(\frac{Dn}{e\theta a_{\mathrm{supp}}}\right)^{D/\theta}+2\beta\|V\|_{a_{\mathrm{supp}},\mathrm{dec}}\right]^{n}.$
(87)
Since  $\|O_{n}\rho^{1/2}\|_{2}\leq\|O_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|O_{n}\|_{0,\mathrm{dec}}$ , use (77) to prove (85).
For  $\ell>0$  and every integer  $n\geq 1$ , we have  $\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|\geq\ell\}}\leq\ell^{-2n}|K|^{2n}.$
Taking its expectation in  $\rho^{1/2}$  and applying (85) gives
$\displaystyle\langle\rho^{1/2},\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|\geq\ell\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle$
$\displaystyle\leq\ell^{-2n}\langle\rho^{1/2},|K|^{2n}\rho^{1/2}\rangle$
(88)
$\displaystyle=\ell^{-2n}\|K^{n}\rho^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}$
$\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{C_{\beta}(n^{D/\theta}+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)}{\ell}\right]^{2n}.$
We first consider  $\ell$  large enough so that the moment order can be chosen proportional to  $\ell^{\theta/D}$  while keeping the ratio in (88) below one.
Specifically, for  $\ell\geq 4C_{\beta}|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|$  and  $\ell\geq 4C_{\beta}2^{D/\theta}$ , choose
$n=\lfloor(\ell/(4C_{\beta}))^{\theta/D}\rfloor$ . The two contributions to the ratio in (88) are each at most  $1/4$ . The second threshold also ensures  $n\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\ell/(4C_{\beta}))^{\theta/D}\geq 1$ . Hence the tail is at most  $2^{-2n}\leq e^{-c_{\beta}\ell^{\theta/D}}$ , with  $c_{\beta}=(\log 2)(4C_{\beta})^{-\theta/D}$ .
If either threshold fails, then
$\ell^{\theta/D}\leq(4C_{\beta})^{\theta/D}\max\{|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|^{\theta/D},2\}\leq 2(4C_{\beta})^{\theta/D}(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}.$
(89)
In this remaining range, the spectral probability is at most one, so (86) is satisfied after enlarging the coefficient of  $(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}$  to make its right-hand side at least one.
Combining the two ranges and enlarging  $C_{\beta}$  proves (86) uniformly in all finite volumes.
∎
Lemma V.4 (Small-resolvent tail).
Under the same hypotheses, for  $\ell>0$ ,
$\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle\leq C_{\beta}\exp\!\left[C_{\beta}(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}-c_{\beta}\ell^{\theta/D}\right].$
(90)
Proof.
For real  $u$ ,
$\log(1+e^{-\ell-u})\leq e^{-\ell/2}+(\log 2+|u|)\mathbf{1}_{\{u<-\ell/2\}}.$
(91)
On  $u\geq-\ell/2$  this follows from  $\log(1+w)\leq w$ ; on the complementary set use  $\log(1+e^{v})\leq\log 2+v_{+}$ . For  $b\geq 0$ , spectral calculus gives
$\displaystyle\langle\rho^{1/2},|K|\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|>b\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle=b\langle\rho^{1/2},\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|>b\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle+\int_{b}^{\infty}\langle\rho^{1/2},\mathbf{1}_{\{|K|>s\}}\rho^{1/2}\rangle\,ds.$
(92)
Combining these inequalities with (86) at  $b=\ell/2$  gives
$\displaystyle\langle\rho^{1/2},\log(1+e^{-\ell-K})\rho^{1/2}\rangle\leq e^{-\ell/2}+C_{\beta}e^{C_{\beta}(1+|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A|)^{\theta/D}}\left[(1+\ell)e^{-c_{\beta}(\ell/2)^{\theta/D}}+\int_{\ell/2}^{\infty}e^{-c_{\beta}s^{\theta/D}}\,ds\right].$
(93)
For the integral, use
$\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}e^{-c_{\beta}s^{\theta/D}}\,ds$
$\displaystyle\leq e^{-(c_{\beta}/2)b^{\theta/D}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(c_{\beta}/2)s^{\theta/D}}\,ds$
(94)
$\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}e^{-(c_{\beta}/2)b^{\theta/D}}.$
The factor  $1+\ell$  is absorbed by decreasing the decay constant. The term  $e^{-\ell/2}$  satisfies the same stretched-exponential bound because  $\theta/D\leq 1$ , with small  $\ell$  covered by enlarging  $C_{\beta}$ . This proves (90).

V.4 A Gibbs-specific continuity estimate
A trace-distance continuity bound for conditional entropy introduces a binary-entropy term and therefore a small-error logarithm [22]. For states connected by a bounded Hamiltonian perturbation at fixed temperature, the following direct estimate avoids that loss. It holds for an arbitrary perturbation, not only a local one.
Lemma V.5 (CMI along a Gibbs interpolation).
Let  $H,W$  be Hermitian operators on  $ABC$ , put  $d_{A}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}$ , and let
$\rho_{s}=\frac{e^{-\beta(H+sW)}}{\Tr e^{-\beta(H+sW)}},\qquad 0\leq s\leq 1.$
(95)
Then
$\bigl|I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{1}}-I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{0}}\bigr|\leq 6\beta(1+\log d_{A})\|W\|_{\infty}.$
(96)
There is no dependence on the Hilbert-space dimensions of  $B$  or  $C$ .
Proof.
We first bound a conditional logarithm for a faithful state  $\omega_{AY}$ . Put
$\tau=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\omega_{Y},\qquad R_{A\mid Y}=\log\omega_{AY}-\log\tau.$
(97)
For any orthonormal basis  $\{|i\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d_{A}}$  of  $A$ , define the pinching map
$\mathcal{P}_{A}(O)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}(P_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{Y})O(P_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{Y}),\qquad P_{i}=|i\rangle\langle i|.$
(98)
The pinching inequality gives  $\omega_{AY}\leq d_{A}\mathcal{P}_{A}(\omega_{AY})$ .
Every diagonal block of  $\mathcal{P}_{A}(\omega_{AY})$  is bounded above by  $\omega_{Y}$ ;
thus  $\omega_{AY}\leq d_{A}\mathbf{1}_{A}\otimes\omega_{Y}=d_{A}^{2}\tau$ .
Inversion reverses the operator order, giving  $\tau^{-1}\leq d_{A}^{2}\omega_{AY}^{-1}$ ; multiplying on both sides by  $\omega_{AY}$  and taking the trace yields  $\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{2}\tau^{-1})\leq d_{A}^{2}\Tr\omega_{AY}=d_{A}^{2}$ .
We claim that
$\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{1+\alpha}\tau^{-\alpha})\leq d_{A}^{2\alpha},\qquad\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{1-\alpha}\tau^{\alpha})\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1,$
(99)
For the first inequality, the function  $\alpha\mapsto\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{1+\alpha}\tau^{-\alpha})$  is log-convex: in eigenbases of  $\omega_{AY}$  and  $\tau$ , it is a positive weighted sum of exponentials in  $\alpha$ . Interpolation between  $\alpha=0$  and  $\alpha=1$  therefore gives
$\displaystyle\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{1+\alpha}\tau^{-\alpha})$
$\displaystyle\leq(\Tr\omega_{AY})^{1-\alpha}[\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{2}\tau^{-1})]^{\alpha}$
(100)
$\displaystyle\leq d_{A}^{2\alpha}.$
For the second inequality, apply Schatten Hölder with conjugate exponents  $1/(1-\alpha)$  and  $1/\alpha$ , for  $0<\alpha<1$ :
$\Tr(\omega_{AY}^{1-\alpha}\tau^{\alpha})\leq(\Tr\omega_{AY})^{1-\alpha}(\Tr\tau)^{\alpha}=1.$
(101)
At  $\alpha=0,1$ , this inequality is an equality by normalization.
To use these estimates without assuming commutativity, diagonalize
$\omega_{AY}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|$  and
$\tau=\sum_{j}q_{j}|j\rangle\langle j|$ .
The probabilities  $p_{i}|\langle i|j\rangle|^{2}$  define a scalar random variable
$u_{ij}=\log p_{i}-\log q_{j}$ . Its second moment equals
$\Tr(\omega_{AY}R_{A\mid Y}^{2})$ , and its exponential moments are exactly the traces in (99).
The scalar inequality
$u^{2}\leq\alpha^{-2}(e^{\alpha u}+e^{-\alpha u}-2)$  gives
$\Tr(\omega_{AY}R_{A\mid Y}^{2})\leq\frac{d_{A}^{2\alpha}-1}{\alpha^{2}}.$
(102)
Choosing  $\alpha=(1+2\log d_{A})^{-1}$  yields
$\Tr(\omega_{AY}R_{A\mid Y}^{2})\leq(e-1)(1+2\log d_{A})^{2}.$
(103)
Define, for a faithful tripartite state, the logarithmic Markov defect
$K_{\mathrm{M}}(\omega)=\log\omega-\log\omega_{AB}-\log\omega_{BC}+\log\omega_{B}.$
(104)
It is the difference of  $R_{A\mid BC}$  and the embedded  $R_{A\mid B}$ . The triangle inequality in the state-weighted Hilbert–Schmidt norm, followed by (103) for the state and its  $AB$  marginal, gives
$\Tr\!\left[\omega K_{\mathrm{M}}(\omega)^{2}\right]\leq 4(e-1)(1+2\log d_{A})^{2}.$
(105)
Here  $K_{\mathrm{M}}$  is an operator on the physical Hilbert space and is distinct from the relative modular superoperator  $K$  in (23).
For Hermitian  $X,Y$ , write the Bogoliubov–Kubo–Mori inner product as
$\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho}=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(\rho^{u}X\rho^{1-u}Y)\,du.$
(106)
In an eigenbasis of  $\rho$ , the logarithmic mean is bounded by the arithmetic mean; hence
$\langle X,X\rangle_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho}\leq\Tr(\rho X^{2})$ .
Differentiating the normalized Gibbs exponential by Duhamel’s formula, (218), gives
$\rho_{s}^{\prime}=-\beta\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{s}^{u}\bigl(W-\Tr(\rho_{s}W)\mathbf{1}\bigr)\rho_{s}^{1-u}\,du.$
(107)
Since every marginal derivative has trace zero, differentiation of the four entropies gives
$\frac{d}{ds}I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{s}}=\Tr\!\left[\rho_{s}^{\prime}\bigl(\log\rho_{s}-\log\rho_{s,AB}-\log\rho_{s,BC}+\log\rho_{s,B}\bigr)\right]=\Tr\!\left[\rho_{s}^{\prime}K_{\mathrm{M}}(\rho_{s})\right],$
(108)
where marginal logarithms are embedded by tensoring with the identity.
Substituting the Gibbs derivative yields
$\frac{d}{ds}I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{s}}=-\beta\left\langle W-\Tr(\rho_{s}W)\mathbf{1},K_{\mathrm{M}}(\rho_{s})\right\rangle_{\mathrm{BKM},\rho_{s}}.$
(109)
Cauchy–Schwarz, (105), and
$\Tr\rho_{s}(W-\Tr(\rho_{s}W)\mathbf{1})^{2}\leq\|W\|_{\infty}^{2}$  imply
$\left|\frac{d}{ds}I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho_{s}}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{e-1}\,\beta(1+2\log d_{A})\|W\|_{\infty}\leq 6\beta(1+\log d_{A})\|W\|_{\infty}.$
(110)
Integration over  $s\in[0,1]$  proves the lemma.
∎
V.5 Modular leakage after local truncation
We use the cut Gibbs pair and the general leakage bounds (51) and (47) from Section IV.
Lemma V.6 (Weighted integrated modular leakage after local truncation).
Let  $\Phi$  be an interaction on  $\mathbb{Z}^{D}$  with
$J_{0}:=\sup_{x}\sum_{X\ni x}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}<\infty,\qquad J_{1}:=\sup_{x}\sum_{X\ni x}|X|\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}<\infty.$
(111)
For  $r\geq 24$ , choose an integer  $1\leq L\leq r/24$  and define
$\Omega=\{x\in\Lambda:d(x,A)\leq\lfloor r/3\rfloor\},\qquad H^{(L)}=H_{\Lambda}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq\Lambda\\
X\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing\\
\diam X>L\end{subarray}}\Phi(X).$
(112)
Let  $V^{(L)}$  be the sum of retained interactions crossing  $A\mid A^{c}$ , and define
$H_{0}^{(L)}=H^{(L)}-V^{(L)},\qquad\rho^{(L)}=\frac{e^{-\beta H^{(L)}}}{\Tr e^{-\beta H^{(L)}}},\qquad\sigma^{(L)}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{0}^{(L)}}}{\Tr e^{-\beta H_{0}^{(L)}}}.$
(113)
Let  $\mathfrak{q}_{AB}^{(L)}$  denote the integrated modular leakage in (29) evaluated with  $R=AB$ ,  $\rho=\rho^{(L)}$ , and  $\sigma=\sigma^{(L)}$ . Suppose  $w>0$  satisfies the weighted interaction bound
$\sup_{x}\sum_{y}e^{wd(x,y)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\ni x,y\\
X\subseteq\Omega\\
\diam X\leq L\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\leq J_{*}$
(114)
for a fixed constant  $J_{*}>0$ . Then
$\mathfrak{q}_{AB}^{(L)}\leq C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}e^{-c_{\beta}wr}.$
(115)
The constants depend only on  $\beta,D,J_{0},J_{1},J_{*}$ , and are independent of  $w,L,\Lambda,A,B,C$ . In particular,  $w$  may depend on  $L$  provided the same  $J_{*}$  bounds the weighted sum. The choice  $w=1/L$  always satisfies the hypothesis with  $J_{*}=eJ_{1}$ .
Proof.
Remove the cut interaction from  $H^{(L)}$  and write
$H^{(L)}=H_{0}^{(L)}+V^{(L)},\qquad H_{0}^{(L)}=H_{\rm in}+H_{\rm out}+W_{\Omega}.$
(116)
The last three sums contain, respectively, interactions inside  $\Omega$ , outside  $\Omega$ , and crossing its boundary. Every retained interaction touching  $\Omega$  has diameter at most  $L$ .
In particular,  $V^{(L)}$  is supported inside the  $L$ -neighborhood of  $A$  and
$\sum_{X\text{ in }V^{(L)}}|X|\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\leq|A|J_{1},\qquad\sum_{Y\text{ in }W_{\Omega}}\|\Phi(Y)\|_{\infty}\leq J_{0}|\Omega|.$
(117)
An individual support  $X$  in  $V^{(L)}$  and every support  $Y$  in  $W_{\Omega}$  satisfy
$d(X,Y)\geq\lfloor r/3\rfloor-2L\geq r/4-1\geq r/6,$
(118)
where we used  $L\leq r/24$  and  $r\geq 24$ .
Indeed, a crossing support  $Y$  has an exterior point at distance greater than  $\lfloor r/3\rfloor$  from  $A$ , and all its points are within  $L$  of that point; a support of  $V^{(L)}$  is within  $L$  of  $A$ .
The assumed weighted bound gives the interaction matrix estimate
$a_{xy}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\ni x,y\\
X\text{ in }H_{\rm in}\end{subarray}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty},\qquad\sup_{x}\sum_{y}e^{wd(x,y)}a_{xy}\leq J_{*}.$
(119)
In the commutator-path expansion, the triangle inequality supplies the factor  $e^{-wd(X,Y)}$ , and each weighted step is bounded by  $J_{*}$ . Summing the time-ordered series gives  $e^{2J_{*}|t|}$ . Thus, for disjoint supports,
$\|[\tau_{t}^{H_{\rm in}}(O_{X}),P_{Y}]\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}\|P_{Y}\|_{\infty}|X|e^{v|t|-wd(X,Y)},\qquad v=2J_{*},$
(120)
where  $\tau_{t}^{G}(O)=e^{itG}Oe^{-itG}$ .
This is the standard Lieb–Robinson iteration [17, 8]; no bound on  $|Y|$  is needed because the row sum already includes all possible endpoints in  $Y$ .
The evolution under  $H_{\rm in}+H_{\rm out}$  of  $V^{(L)}$  is its evolution under  $H_{\rm in}$  alone. To bound the Duhamel integrand, expand both interactions and apply (120) and (118). For  $u\geq 0$ ,
$\displaystyle\|[W_{\Omega},\tau_{\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})]\|_{\infty}$
$\displaystyle\leq\sum_{X\text{ in }V^{(L)}}\sum_{Y\text{ in }W_{\Omega}}\|[\Phi(Y),\tau_{\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{\rm in}}(\Phi(X))]\|_{\infty}$
(121)
$\displaystyle\leq 2e^{vu-wr/6}\left(\sum_{X\text{ in }V^{(L)}}|X|\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\right)\left(\sum_{Y\text{ in }W_{\Omega}}\|\Phi(Y)\|_{\infty}\right).$
The two strength bounds in (117) therefore give
$\|[W_{\Omega},\tau_{\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})]\|_{\infty}\leq 2|A|J_{1}J_{0}|\Omega|e^{vu-wr/6}.$
(122)
Duhamel’s formula and unitarity of the exterior evolution give
$\displaystyle\|\tau_{t}^{H_{0}^{(L)}}(V^{(L)})-\tau_{t}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})\|_{\infty}$
$\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{|t|}\|[W_{\Omega},\tau_{\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})]\|_{\infty}\,du$
$\displaystyle\leq C|A|J_{1}J_{0}|\Omega|\,|t|e^{v|t|-cwr}.$
(123)
Only commutators at the inner–outer interface are estimated. No locality assumption is imposed on  $H_{\rm out}$ .
Up to a scalar phase, the relative modular evolution
$(\sigma^{(L)})^{it}(\rho^{(L)})^{-it}$  equals
$U(t):=e^{-i\beta tH_{0}^{(L)}}e^{i\beta tH^{(L)}}$ , which satisfies
$U^{\prime}(t)=i\beta\tau_{-\beta t}^{H_{0}^{(L)}}(V^{(L)})U(t)$ .
Define the approximating unitary by
$\widetilde{U}^{\prime}(t)=i\beta\tau_{-\beta t}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})\widetilde{U}(t),\qquad\widetilde{U}(0)=\mathbf{1}.$
(124)
Its generator is supported on  $\Omega\subseteq AB$ , so
$\mathsf{E}_{AB}(\widetilde{U}(t))=\widetilde{U}(t)$ .
Duhamel’s formula and (123) give
$\displaystyle\|U(t)-\widetilde{U}(t)\|_{\infty}$
$\displaystyle\leq\beta\int_{0}^{|t|}\bigl\|\tau_{-\beta\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{0}^{(L)}}(V^{(L)})-\tau_{-\beta\operatorname{sgn}(t)u}^{H_{\rm in}}(V^{(L)})\bigr\|_{\infty}\,du$
(125)
$\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{2}|A|J_{1}J_{0}|\Omega|e^{-cwr}\int_{0}^{|t|}ue^{v\beta u}\,du$
$\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}|A||\Omega|t^{2}e^{v\beta|t|-cwr}.$
The last step uses  $\int_{0}^{|t|}ue^{v\beta u}\,du\leq(t^{2}/2)e^{v\beta|t|}$ .
The scalar phase does not affect the leakage norm. Contractivity of  $\mathsf{E}_{AB}$  and  $|\Omega|\leq C|A|(1+r)^{D}$  now yield
$\displaystyle\eta_{AB}^{(L)}(t)$
$\displaystyle=\|(\mathrm{id}-\mathsf{E}_{AB})(U(t)-\widetilde{U}(t))\|_{\infty}$
(126)
$\displaystyle\leq 2\|U(t)-\widetilde{U}(t)\|_{\infty}$
$\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}t^{2}e^{v\beta|t|-cwr}.$
Also  $\eta_{AB}^{(L)}(t)\leq 2$  by unitarity and contractivity, so
$\eta_{AB}^{(L)}(t)\leq\min\{2,C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}t^{2}e^{v\beta|t|-cwr}\}.$
(127)
Split its integral against  $1/(2|\sinh\pi t|)$  at
$T=cwr/(\pi+\beta v)$ .
For  $T\geq 1$ , on  $|t|\leq T$  we have  $v\beta|t|-cwr\leq-\pi T$ , so the second bound and
$\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{2}/|\sinh(\pi t)|\,dt<\infty$  give a contribution at most  $C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}e^{-\pi T}$ .
On  $|t|>T$ , the first bound and the exponential kernel tail give at most  $Ce^{-\pi T}$ .
Hence
$\mathfrak{q}_{AB}^{(L)}\leq C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}e^{-\pi T}=C_{\beta}|A|^{2}(1+r)^{D}\exp\!\left[-\frac{c\pi}{\pi+\beta v}wr\right].$
(128)
For  $T<1$ , the same bound follows after increasing  $C_{\beta}$ , since
$\mathfrak{q}_{AB}^{(L)}\leq\beta g_{A\mid A^{c}}/2\leq\beta J_{0}|A|/2$  by

VI.3 Spatial leakage for summable power-law profiles
Lemma VI.5 (Power-law modular leakage).
Under (12),
$\eta_{AB}(t)\leq\min\left\{2,\ C\beta J_{\zeta}|t|e^{v\beta|t|}\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}(1+d(a,c))^{-\zeta}\right\},\qquad v=C_{D,\zeta}J_{\zeta}.$
(187)
Consequently, for  $r=d(A,C)$ ,
$\mathfrak{q}_{AB}\leq C_{\beta}|A|(1+r)^{-\kappa_{\beta}},\qquad\kappa_{\beta}=\frac{\pi(\zeta-D)}{\pi+\beta v}>0.$
(188)
Here  $C$  and  $C_{D,\zeta}>0$  depend only on  $D,\zeta$ , while  $C_{\beta}$  depends only on  $\beta,D,J_{\zeta},\zeta$ . All constants are independent of the regions and finite volume.
Proof.
Set  $F(r)=(1+r)^{-\zeta}$ . Since  $\zeta>D$ , the lattice sum  $\sum_{z}F(|z|_{1})$  is finite, and
$\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}F(d(x,z))F(d(z,y))\leq 2^{\zeta+1}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}F(|z|_{1})\right)F(d(x,y)).$
(189)
Indeed, for each  $z$ , either  $d(x,z)\geq d(x,y)/2$  or  $d(z,y)\geq d(x,y)/2$ ; bound the corresponding factor by  $2^{\zeta}F(d(x,y))$  and sum the other factor.
Thus the Lieb–Robinson bound [17, Theorem 3.1] applies with the interaction bound  $J_{\zeta}$  from (12). Followed by Haar averaging on  $C$ , it gives for an operator  $O_{X}$  supported on  $X$  disjoint from  $C$ ,
$\|e^{-iuH_{0}}O_{X}e^{iuH_{0}}-\mathsf{E}_{AB}(e^{-iuH_{0}}O_{X}e^{iuH_{0}})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|O_{X}\|_{\infty}e^{v|u|}\sum_{x\in X,c\in C}F(d(x,c)).$
(190)

Removing interactions to form  $H_{0}$  preserves the pair bound with the same  $J_{\zeta}$ .
Since each cut support meets  $A$ , (12) and (189) give
$\displaystyle\sum_{X\text{ crossing }A\mid A^{c}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\sum_{x\in X,c\in C}F(d(x,c))$
$\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{x\in\Lambda,c\in C}F(d(x,c))\sum_{X\ni a,x}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}$
(191)
$\displaystyle\leq J_{\zeta}\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}F(d(a,x))F(d(x,c))$
$\displaystyle\leq C_{D,\zeta}J_{\zeta}\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}F(d(a,c)).$
This treats direct interactions spanning the entire buffer as well as dynamically spreading terms.
Apply the local approximation bound to each term of  $V(u)=e^{-iuH_{0}}Ve^{iuH_{0}}$  and use (191). Then (51) gives

$\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\beta|t|}\|V(\operatorname{sgn}(t)u)-\mathsf{E}_{AB}(V(\operatorname{sgn}(t)u))\|_{\infty}\,du$
(192)
$\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\beta|t|}e^{vu}\,du\right)\sum_{X\text{ crossing }A\mid A^{c}}\|\Phi(X)\|_{\infty}\sum_{x\in X,c\in C}F(d(x,c))$
$\displaystyle\leq C\beta J_{\zeta}|t|e^{v\beta|t|}\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}F(d(a,c)).$
The last step uses  $e^{vu}\leq e^{v\beta|t|}$  throughout the integration interval. Combining this with  $\eta_{AB}(t)\leq 2$  and  $F(r)=(1+r)^{-\zeta}$  proves (187).
For every finite  $A$ ,
$\sum_{a\in A,c\in C}(1+d(a,c))^{-\zeta}\leq C_{D,\zeta}|A|(1+r)^{D-\zeta}.$
(193)

(194)

The last inequality uses  $\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|/|\sinh(\pi t)|\,dt=1/2$  and (54).
Choose
$T=\frac{(\zeta-D)\log(1+r)}{\pi+\beta v}.$
(195)
Then the two decay factors agree:

VII Summary and Discussion
Our results relate approximate conditional independence in Gibbs states to the strength and spatial decay of their interactions, without assuming clustering or rapid mixing. For finite-range interactions, the exponential prefactor is controlled by the interaction strength across the cut and hence by the boundary size. This improves the exponential volume dependence of previous local Markov bounds while retaining exponential decay with separation at every fixed positive temperature. The estimates are uniform in the total volume and the size of the distant region.
For exponentially and stretched-exponentially decaying interactions, the static comparison with a cut Gibbs state remains effective even when interaction supports have unbounded size.
Truncating long interactions near  $A$  and combining spatial localization with spectral estimates gives the distance exponent  $\theta/(D+1-\theta)$  and a boundary-dependent prefactor.
In two dimensions, both the finite-range and exponentially decaying interaction bounds have a useful consequence for coarse-graining. Consider regular full partitions  $\Lambda_{s}=A_{s}\sqcup B_{s}\sqcup C_{s}$  obtained by enlarging a fixed geometry, with
$|\partial_{\mathrm{e}}A_{s}|\leq ps,\qquad d(A_{s},C_{s})\geq qs,$
(212)
where  $s$  is the length scale and  $p,q>0$  are fixed geometric constants. The finite-range bound gives
$I(A_{s}:C_{s}\mid B_{s})_{\rho_{\Lambda_{s},\beta}}\leq C_{\beta}\exp\!\left[-(c_{\beta}q-C_{\beta}p)s\right].$
(213)
At fixed  $\beta$ , choosing a sufficiently wide buffer relative to the size of  $A_{s}$  makes  $c_{\beta}q>C_{\beta}p$ , so the CMI tends to zero exponentially as the entire partition is enlarged with these proportions held fixed.
For exponentially decaying interactions ( $\theta=1$ ), the boundary and distance terms in the exponent instead both scale as  $s^{1/2}$ , yielding stretched-exponential suppression for a sufficiently wide proportional buffer.
The essential point in both cases is that the boundary length and buffer width scale linearly in two dimensions.
This suppression under geometric rescaling has a renormalization-group interpretation and may find applications in approximate entanglement bootstrap for mixed quantum states in two dimensions [21, 23].
For power-law interactions, the bounds have polynomial region-size dependence: Theorem II.3 gives inverse-logarithmic decay for  $D<\zeta\leq 2D$ , with a squared iterated-logarithm correction at  $\zeta=2D$ , and algebraic decay for  $\zeta>2D$ .
The sufficient threshold  $2D$  arises from the truncation error and the requirement that the retained interaction range be smaller than the separation.
Obtaining algebraic decay for unrestricted-support interactions throughout  $D<\zeta\leq 2D$ , improving the distance exponents, and establishing stronger boundary dependence remain open.
There is also a tradeoff between distance decay and region-size dependence. Under the respective hypotheses of Theorems II.1 and II.2, finite-range and stretched-exponentially decaying interactions admit, for every fixed  $p>0$ , a bound of the form
$I(A:C\mid B)_{\rho}\leq C_{\beta,p}(1+|A|)^{q_{p}}(1+r)^{-p},$
(214)
where  $q_{p}<\infty$  and  $C_{\beta,p}$  are independent of the regions and finite volume, but may depend on  $p$  and the fixed interaction parameters. In the finite-range case, choose  $\alpha=\alpha_{0}/(1+\beta g_{A\mid A^{c}})$  in Corollary III.5 and use (59) and Lemma IV.1. This gives a bound proportional to  $(1+\beta g_{A\mid A^{c}})\exp[-c_{\beta}r/(1+\beta g_{A\mid A^{c}})]$ , which implies (214) using  $g_{A\mid A^{c}}\leq\mathfrak{d}J|A|$ . For stretched-exponential interactions, keep the moment order  $n$  fixed in (88): the resulting small-resolvent bound is polynomial in the boundary size and decays as  $\ell^{-(2n-1)}$ . Combining it with the same truncation and leakage estimates, and choosing  $n$  sufficiently large for the prescribed  $p$ , gives (214). Thus polynomial region-size dependence is available at the cost of algebraic distance decay; the polynomial degree may increase with the requested decay exponent.
Two broader directions concern global Markovianity of Gibbs states and extensions to bosonic systems.
For finite-range interactions, the goal is to obtain exponential or stretched-exponential CMI decay with at most polynomial region-size dependence beyond the regimes covered by existing high-temperature, clustering, or rapid-mixing assumptions.
Understanding when the exponential boundary prefactor can be replaced by such a polynomial while retaining these distance-decay rates would strengthen the bounds above.
Bosonic lattices pose a different challenge because their local Hilbert spaces are infinite-dimensional and their interactions are typically unbounded.
A possible route is to combine thermal bounds on local occupation numbers with spatial localization and modular spectral estimates,
while controlling entropy errors through energy-constrained continuity bounds [22]. Establishing estimates that remain uniform as the local occupation cutoff is removed would be a key step toward applying the static approach to interacting bosonic Gibbs states.

Appendix A Duhamel’s formula and Grönwall’s inequality
Lemma A.1 (Duhamel’s formula).
Let  $G_{1}(t),G_{2}(t)$  be continuous Hermitian matrix-valued functions, and let  $U_{j}(t,s)$  solve  $\partial_{t}U_{j}(t,s)=-iG_{j}(t)U_{j}(t,s)$  with  $U_{j}(s,s)=\mathbf{1}$ . For  $t\geq 0$ ,
$U_{1}(t,0)-U_{2}(t,0)=-i\int_{0}^{t}U_{1}(t,s)\bigl(G_{1}(s)-G_{2}(s)\bigr)U_{2}(s,0)\,ds.$
(215)
Consequently, unitarity gives
$\|U_{1}(t,0)-U_{2}(t,0)\|_{\infty}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{1}(s)-G_{2}(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds.$
(216)
For a continuously differentiable matrix-valued function  $M(s)$ , the exponential derivative takes the form
$\frac{d}{ds}e^{M(s)}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{(1-u)M(s)}M^{\prime}(s)e^{uM(s)}\,du.$
(217)
In particular, for  $\rho_{s}=e^{-\beta(H+sW)}/\Tr e^{-\beta(H+sW)}$  with  $H,W$  Hermitian,
$\rho_{s}^{\prime}=-\beta\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{s}^{1-u}\bigl(W-\Tr(\rho_{s}W)\mathbf{1}\bigr)\rho_{s}^{u}\,du.$
(218)
Proof.
Differentiate  $U_{1}(t,s)U_{2}(s,0)$  with respect to  $s$ . Its derivative is  $iU_{1}(t,s)(G_{1}(s)-G_{2}(s))U_{2}(s,0)$ . Integrating from  $0$  to  $t$  proves (215); taking the norm proves (216).
For the exponential derivative, differentiate  $e^{(1-u)M(s+h)}e^{uM(s)}$  with respect to  $u$  and integrate to obtain
$e^{M(s+h)}-e^{M(s)}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{(1-u)M(s+h)}\bigl(M(s+h)-M(s)\bigr)e^{uM(s)}\,du.$
(219)
Divide by  $h$  and let  $h\to 0$  to prove (217). Apply it with  $M(s)=-\beta(H+sW)$  and differentiate the normalization. Cyclicity gives  $\frac{d}{ds}\log\Tr e^{-\beta(H+sW)}=-\beta\Tr(\rho_{s}W)$ , yielding (218).
∎
Lemma A.2 (Grönwall’s inequality).
Let  $T>0$ , let  $f:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)$  be continuous, and let  $a:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)$  be integrable. If, for some  $c\geq 0$ ,
$f(u)\leq c+\int_{0}^{u}a(s)f(s)\,ds\qquad(0\leq u\leq T),$
(220)
then
$f(u)\leq c\exp\!\left(\int_{0}^{u}a(s)\,ds\right)\qquad(0\leq u\leq T).$
(221)
Proof.
Put  $g(u)=c+\int_{0}^{u}a(s)f(s)\,ds$ . Then  $f\leq g$ ,  $g(0)=c$ , and
$g^{\prime}(u)=a(u)f(u)\leq a(u)g(u)$  almost everywhere.
Multiplying by  $\exp[-\int_{0}^{u}a(s)\,ds]$  and integrating proves the assertion.
For  $c=0$ , first replace  $c$  by  $\varepsilon>0$  and then let  $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ .
∎
Appendix B Resolvent and variational representations of the entropy loss
Lemma III.2 (restated). Let  $\rho,\sigma$  be faithful states on the finite-dimensional tensor product  $R\otimes E$ , and set  $\Delta_{R}=L_{\sigma_{R}}R_{\rho_{R}^{-1}}$ . Set  $\Delta=L_{\sigma}R_{\rho^{-1}}$  and  $\delta_{R}(\rho,\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_{R}\|\sigma_{R})$ . Then
$\begin{split}\delta_{R}(\rho,\sigma)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}j(s)\,ds,\\
j(s)&=\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle-\langle\rho_{R}^{1/2},(\Delta_{R}+s)^{-1}\rho_{R}^{1/2}\rangle.\end{split}$
The integral is absolutely convergent.
Proof.
Left multiplication by  $\sigma$  and right multiplication by  $\rho^{-1}$  are commuting positive operators on Hilbert–Schmidt space. Hence
$(\log\Delta)(Y)=(\log\sigma)Y-Y\log\rho,$
(222)
and therefore
$-\langle\rho^{1/2},\log\Delta\,\rho^{1/2}\rangle=\Tr\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)=D(\rho\|\sigma).$
(223)
The same calculation on the reduced space gives
$-\langle\rho_{R}^{1/2},\log\Delta_{R}\,\rho_{R}^{1/2}\rangle=D(\rho_{R}\|\sigma_{R})$ .
For  $\lambda>0$ , direct integration gives
$\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+s}-\frac{1}{1+s}\right)ds=\log\frac{\lambda+T}{1+T}-\log\lambda\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}-\log\lambda.$
(224)
Applying this identity by spectral calculus and using
$\|\rho^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\rho_{R}^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}=1$ , we obtain
$\displaystyle D(\rho\|\sigma)$
$\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle-\frac{1}{1+s}\right)ds,$
(225)
$\displaystyle D(\rho_{R}\|\sigma_{R})$
$\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\langle\rho_{R}^{1/2},(\Delta_{R}+s)^{-1}\rho_{R}^{1/2}\rangle-\frac{1}{1+s}\right)ds.$
Faithfulness makes both integrands bounded near  $s=0$ , and the resolvent expansion at infinity makes them  $O(s^{-2})$ . Thus both integrals converge absolutely, and subtraction cancels the scalar terms and proves (25).
∎
Lemma III.3 (restated). Under the setup of Lemma III.2, define
$W_{R}(Y)=\bigl(Y\rho_{R}^{-1/2}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{E}\bigr)\rho^{1/2}.$
This map is an isometry and satisfies
$W_{R}^{\dagger}(Z)=\Tr_{E}(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_{R}^{-1/2},\qquad W_{R}\rho_{R}^{1/2}=\rho^{1/2},\qquad W_{R}^{\dagger}\Delta W_{R}=\Delta_{R}.$
For every  $s>0$ ,
$j(s)=\inf_{y\in\Ran W_{R}}\bigl\|(\Delta+s)^{1/2}\bigl(y-(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\bigr)\bigr\|_{2}^{2}.$
The infimum is attained uniquely at  $y=W_{R}(\Delta_{R}+s)^{-1}\rho_{R}^{1/2}$ . In particular,  $j(s)\geq 0$ , and every trial vector in  $\Ran W_{R}$  gives an upper bound on  $j(s)$ .
Proof.
With the convention  $\langle X,Z\rangle=\Tr(X^{\dagger}Z)$ , cyclicity of the trace and the defining property of the partial trace give, for arbitrary  $Y$  on  $R$  and  $Z$  on  $R\otimes E$ ,
$\displaystyle\langle W_{R}(Y),Z\rangle$
$\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\rho^{1/2}\bigl(\rho_{R}^{-1/2}Y^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{E}\bigr)Z\right]$
(226)
$\displaystyle=\Tr_{R}\!\left[\rho_{R}^{-1/2}Y^{\dagger}\Tr_{E}(Z\rho^{1/2})\right]$
$\displaystyle=\Tr_{R}\!\left[Y^{\dagger}\Tr_{E}(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_{R}^{-1/2}\right]$
$\displaystyle=\left\langle Y,\Tr_{E}(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_{R}^{-1/2}\right\rangle.$
By the defining identity  $\langle W_{R}(Y),Z\rangle=\langle Y,W_{R}^{\dagger}(Z)\rangle$ , this proves
$W_{R}^{\dagger}(Z)=\Tr_{E}(Z\rho^{1/2})\rho_{R}^{-1/2}.$
Consequently,
$\displaystyle W_{R}^{\dagger}W_{R}(Y)$
$\displaystyle=\Tr_{E}\!\left[\bigl(Y\rho_{R}^{-1/2}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{E}\bigr)\rho\right]\rho_{R}^{-1/2}=Y,$
(227)
$\displaystyle W_{R}^{\dagger}\Delta W_{R}(Y)$
$\displaystyle=\Tr_{E}\!\left[\sigma\bigl(Y\rho_{R}^{-1/2}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{E}\bigr)\right]\rho_{R}^{-1/2}=\sigma_{R}Y\rho_{R}^{-1}=\Delta_{R}(Y).$
Together with  $W_{R}\rho_{R}^{1/2}=\rho^{1/2}$ , which follows directly from the definition, these calculations prove (27).
Write  $y=W_{R}Y$ . Expanding the squared norm and using (27) gives
$\displaystyle\bigl\|(\Delta+s)^{1/2}\bigl(W_{R}Y-(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\bigr)\bigr\|_{2}^{2}$
(228)
$\displaystyle=\langle Y,(\Delta_{R}+s)Y\rangle-2\operatorname{Re}\langle Y,\rho_{R}^{1/2}\rangle+\langle\rho^{1/2},(\Delta+s)^{-1}\rho^{1/2}\rangle$
$\displaystyle=\bigl\|(\Delta_{R}+s)^{1/2}\bigl(Y-(\Delta_{R}+s)^{-1}\rho_{R}^{1/2}\bigr)\bigr\|_{2}^{2}+j(s).$
Since  $\Delta_{R}+s$  is strictly positive, the last squared norm vanishes exactly at  $Y=(\Delta_{R}+s)^{-1}\rho_{R}^{1/2}$ . Taking the infimum proves (28), including uniqueness of the minimizer. The left-hand side is nonnegative for every trial vector, so its minimum is  $j(s)\geq 0$ , and each trial value is at least  $j(s)$ .
∎
B.1 Derivation of the Fourier resolvent identity
For completeness, we derive (32) from the standard integral [18, Eq. (4.40.8)]
$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(at)}{\sinh(\pi t)}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\tan(a/2),\qquad-\pi
(229)
Both sides are holomorphic in the strip  $|\operatorname{Re}a|<\pi$ , so the identity extends to that strip. Substituting  $a=iv$ , with  $v\in\mathbb{R}$ , and cancelling  $i$  gives
$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(vt)}{\sinh(\pi t)}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\tanh(v/2).$
(230)
Set  $v=u-\log s$  and  $w=-\log s$ . Since
$s^{-it}(e^{itu}-1)=e^{ivt}-e^{iwt}$ , pairing the positive and negative halves of the integral yields
$\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{s^{-it}(e^{itu}-1)}{\sinh(\pi t)}\,dt$
$\displaystyle=2i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(vt)-\sin(wt)}{\sinh(\pi t)}\,dt$
(231)
$\displaystyle=i\left[\tanh\!\left(\frac{u-\log s}{2}\right)-\tanh\!\left(\frac{-\log s}{2}\right)\right]$
$\displaystyle=i\left[\frac{e^{u}-s}{e^{u}+s}-\frac{1-s}{1+s}\right].$
Multiplication by  $i/(2s)$  therefore gives
$\frac{i}{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{s^{-it}(e^{itu}-1)}{\sinh(\pi t)}\,dt=\frac{1}{s+e^{u}}-\frac{1}{1+s},$
(232)
which is (32). All integrals in the last two displays are ordinary absolutely convergent integrals: the numerator vanishes to first order at  $t=0$ , and  $1/\sinh(\pi t)$  decays exponentially at infinity.
