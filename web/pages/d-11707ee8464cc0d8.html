## 1. A stronger local contraction transfer

Write c(A,B)=(A->(A->B))->(A->B). Let a=A^t, a'=A^s,
b=B^t, b'=B^s. Suppose the target has a cached ordinary proof

    Z_B: b' => bottom.                              (1)

Use the single-conclusion target LJ_!: it permits ordinary weakening and
contraction as well as the linear-language connectives. This is the SAME
strong intuitionistic target used in the hard-family lower bound; it is
stronger than the affine target used for the original Chu compiler.
Set

    V=(a->b) AND (b'->a'),  V'=a*b',
    U=(a->V) AND (V'->a'), U'=a*V'.

Definition11 gives c(A,B)^t=(U->V) AND(V'->U'). Here is an exact
primitive-rule proof from (1), without any assumption on A:

1. Identity a=>a and b=>b, L->, then L-AND give a,V=>b.
2. Identity a=>a and this endpoint, L->, then L-AND give a,a,U=>b.
3. Apply ONE ordinary contraction to a: a,U=>b. R-> gives U=>a->b.
4. The bottom axiom U,bottom=>a' and cut with (1) give U,b'=>a'.
   R-> gives U=>b'->a'. R-AND and R-> prove =>U->V.
5. The bottom axiom a,bottom=>U' and cut with (1) give a,b'=>U'.
   L* and R-> prove =>V'->U'. R-AND gives the exact translated target.

Only constantly many rows and formula occurrences are added, with at most
four formula occurrences in an intermediate sequent. The cached (1)
endpoint is reused as a theorem; it is not contracted as an antecedent.
The one explicit target contraction is legal in LJ_!. Every ordinary
affine Chu schema remains valid in this stronger target. Thus a source
proof augmented with contraction instances whose B satisfies (1) transfers
at the previous shared-DAG cost plus the cost of acquiring those endpoints.

Fully conservative B, in Definition16's grammar, satisfies (1) by the
negative half of Lemma19. Simultaneously caching that half over a common
syntax DAG costs O(V), rather than expanding each occurrence. This
requires only the named local-prefix construction already independently
reconstructed for the root compiler.

## 2. Typed skeleton compression preserves this grammar

Consider the explicit source proof system: the canonical affine Frege
rules plus c(A,B) as an extra axiom ONLY when B is fully conservative.
This is a syntactically guarded proof system; do not call it a new
substitution-closed logic without a separate argument.

Freeze its actual line/rule skeleton and final target F. The usual term
equations have O(L+|F|) constructor nodes. A supplied finite proof is a
ground solution, excluding constructor clashes and directed cycles.
Annotate each guarded codomain root with the fully-conservative sort.
Propagate Definition16's local grammar constraints through forced
constructors: a fully-conservative implication demands a conservative
antecedent and a fully-conservative consequent; additive conjunction
and disjunction demand two fully-conservative children. Conservative
nodes additionally allow fusion and !, with conservative children.

The original ground solution proves that every forced constructor meets
all requested sorts. If several requests meet in a quotient class, all
their child obligations are retained. Ground every unconstrained class
to one atom, which belongs to BOTH sorts. The same acyclic quotient then
satisfies every schema equation and every guarded grammar condition,
while retaining the literal final F. This is a special type-preservation
argument, not a general unification lemma for arbitrary side conditions.
It fails for languages whose free classes have no common ground term or
whose side conditions inspect arbitrary expanded size/freshness.

Consequently the guarded system has an ordinary LJ_! proof of every
conservative F with O(L+|F|) formula occurrences, using the shared Chu
compiler, (1), the fixed contraction macro and cached conservativity.
On the named prior hard family Theta*_n this yields an exponential source
LINE lower bound, provided the complete previous Chu-template gate and
the exact prior LJ_! lower bound are retained. This candidate theorem is
stronger than the unguarded-affine baseline; it does not add every source
contraction and is not a classical Frege lower bound.

An EF version is valid when each guard is checked on the FULL ACYCLICALLY
UNFOLDED codomain. Unfolding first preserves that guard; replace each
expanded definition axiom by a fixed ordinary theorem and apply the
guarded line transfer. Formula trees may become enormous; the line
bound and syntax DAG remain the relevant measures.

## 3. Syntactic guards are defeated by fresh definitions

If instead the guard only checks the unexpanded displayed B, fresh
acyclic EF permits EVERY contraction at constant overhead. For arbitrary
A,B, introduce a fresh e with definition (e->B) AND(B->e). Its two
directions are closed lines by the fixed additive projections. Since e
is an atom, c(A,e) passes the displayed fully-conservative codomain guard.

For any closed f:X->Y, the canonical pf axiom and modus ponens give

    R_A(f): (A->X)->(A->Y)

at constant cost. Apply R_A twice to B->e and once to e->B:

    (A->(A->B))->(A->(A->e)),
    (A->e)->(A->B).

Compose these with c(A,e), using two fixed pf instances and modus ponens.
The result is c(A,B), with a fixed number of extra rows independent of
the sizes of A and B. The literal final target contains no e. All
definition freshness conditions are met: e did not occur in the earlier
proof, in B, or in the final target. No cyclic extension or illicit
linear antecedent contraction occurs inside this macro.

Replacing every ordinary unrestricted-contraction axiom in a supplied
classical proof by this macro gives a polynomial-size proof in the
display-guarded extension system. In particular the recent source's
polynomial classical proof of Theta*_n defeats an affine-style EF
lower bound for that naive extension interface. More fundamentally,
fresh-definition elimination is not conservative for this guarded
system, because expansion turns c(A,e) into a guard-violating c(A,B).

This does NOT refute root's affine EF theorem: its source has no such
syntactic contraction guard and every rule is uniformly substitutive.
It gives an exact test any proposed controlled-contraction EF extension
must pass. A bang-head guard survives alias expansion; a restriction
that treats all atom names as safe does not.

## 4. Adding target contraction alone cannot repair unchanged Chu

There is an even sharper obstruction than the affine Lukasiewicz model.
Take A=p and B=the linear constant0, with D=N=bounded bottom. In the
ordinary two-element Boolean algebra, interpret fusion as AND, 1/top
as1, and0/bottom as0. The source contraction formula is a tautology.
Nevertheless

    (p->0)^t=0,  (p->0)^s=p,
    (p->(p->0))^t=NOT p,
    (p->(p->0))^s=p AND p,
    c(p,0)^t=p.

At p=0 the translated formula is false even in a FULL classical target.
Thus merely adding positive target contraction cannot make unchanged
Chu simulate arbitrary source contraction. The codomain condition (1)
is substantive: linear0 has s(0)=1 and violates it. This is a method
obstruction, not a theorem asserting classical proof hardness.

The alias conclusion applies more broadly than this particular grammar.
Any displayed-syntax codomain guard which accepts arbitrary fresh
definition atoms, together with the same two-direction definitions and
canonical composition/projection rules, admits the identical18-row
simulation of every unrestricted c(A,B). Hence such a guard cannot
support this affine-style EF lower bound. A guard that examines expanded
definitions can be checked bottom-up on an acyclic syntax DAG in
polynomial time; exponential tree expansion is unnecessary for checking
this particular regular grammar. The checking can be incremental:
freshness forbids a later definition from naming an atom already used in
an earlier line. Hence future definitions cannot change an earlier
guard's expanded meaning. Store the fully/conservative membership bits
for each shared constructor and defined name, then evaluate the new
codomain using the already available definition roots. This costs
polynomial time in explicit certificate size and rejects the alias
fixture at its guarded contraction line.
