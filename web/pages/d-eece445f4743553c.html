STATUS

No counterexample to the bounded-Holevo, vanishing-broadcast-error implication is established. The strongest independently derived result here is an exact growing-dimension benchmark: for a complete traceless Hermitian involution basis (the nonidentity Pauli basis, or any common unitary conjugate), the full family has fixed sandwich and constant Holevo capacity, yet its exact two-output broadcasting error tends to a/4. Its accessible information tends to zero. The lower bound uses an explicit Choi witness and the matching upper bound is the optimal universal 1-to-2 cloner. This closes the random-basis/complete-Clifford candidate route at a positive broadcast error; it does not answer the target because b2 does not vanish.

A second exact boundary is that every random-unitary instrument, after the supplied symmetrized instrument-append and Petz correction, gives a genuinely self-compatible channel whose powers approach depolarization in normalized infinity-to-one norm at rate 4^-m. This excludes random-unitary quantum expanders as counterexamples to the CP-range-to-EB lifting claim, even without a spectral-gap assumption. Standard LOCC data hiding also does not transfer: global EB measurements may use arbitrary POVMs, and orthogonal hiding states are exactly EB-reconstructible and exactly broadcastable. The large symmetric-power ladder in N61 does have b2->0 and eEB->1, but it fails both fixed sandwich and bounded-capacity hypotheses; isotropic noise sufficient for a fixed upper sandwich forces its replacer error to zero.

1. EXACT PAULI-FRAME BROADCASTING BENCHMARK

Let d=2^q and let {P_i}_{i=1}^{d^2-1} be all nonidentity q-qubit Pauli strings. Each P_i is a traceless Hermitian involution and

  tau(P_i P_j)=delta_ij,   tau=Tr/d.

For fixed 0<a<1 define the finite experiment

  rho_(i,s)=(I+s a P_i)/d,  i=1,...,d^2-1, s in {+1,-1},
  sigma=I/d.

It is faithful and satisfies (1-a)sigma <= rho_(i,s) <= (1+a)sigma. All member entropies are log d-c(a), where

  c(a)=((1+a)log(1+a)+(1-a)log(1-a))/2.

For any prior, S(mean)<=log d, so C(E)<=c(a); the equal prior on the two signs of one i has mean sigma and attains c(a). Thus C(E)=c(a), independent of d. Replacing every input by sigma gives eEB<=a/2. Any EB channel can be broadcast by preparing its output twice, hence b2<=eEB and eEB>=b2.

Claim (exact):

  b2(E)=a d/[4(d+1)].

For the upper bound, Werner's optimal universal 1->2 qudit cloner has each marginal

  Lambda(X)=gamma X+(1-gamma)Tr(X)I/d,
  gamma=(d+2)/(2(d+1)).

On every rho_(i,s), its trace-distance error is a(1-gamma)/2=ad/[4(d+1)]. Werner's construction is a full CPTP broadcaster, so this is admissible for the stated state error (quant-ph/9804001, Section 3, eq. (3.8), with N=1,M=2).

For the matching lower bound, let B be any CPTP map from the full input algebra to two outputs, with both marginal errors at most epsilon. Let J be its unnormalized Choi operator, so Tr J=d and J/d is a state. For each i and receiver R, trace-distance duality applied to P_i and the two signs gives

  tau(P_i B_R(P_i)) >= 1-2 epsilon/a.

Indeed the difference of the two output scores is at least 2a-4epsilon, while rho_(i,+)-rho_(i,-)=2aP_i/d. Summing over both receivers yields

  2(d^2-1)(1-2epsilon/a) <= Tr[(J/d) W] <= ||W||,
  W=sum_i P_i^T tensor (P_i tensor I + I tensor P_i).

The Pauli completeness identity is

  sum_(i including identity) P_i^T tensor P_i=d^2 |Omega><Omega|,

where |Omega> is normalized maximally entangled. Therefore

  W=d^2(Q_RB+Q_RC)-2I,

with Q_RB=|Omega><Omega|_RB tensor I_C and Q_RC=|Omega><Omega|_RC tensor I_B. These are rank-d projections. The overlap between their ranges has operator norm 1/d: vectors |Omega>_RB|v>_C and |Omega>_RC|w>_B have inner product <v,w>/d. The largest eigenvalue of Q_RB+Q_RC is consequently 1+1/d; the smallest is 0. Hence ||W||=d^2+d-2. Substitution gives

  1-2epsilon/a <= (d^2+d-2)/(2(d^2-1))=(d+2)/(2(d+1)),

which is epsilon>=ad/[4(d+1)]. No output-flag, covariance, or output-symmetry assumption is used in this lower bound. It is a full Choi optimization bound, not a finite SDP diagnostic.

The same family has vanishing accessible information. For an arbitrary POVM {E_y}, put x_(iy)=tau(E_y P_i)/tau(E_y); the outcome marginal under the uniform signed prior is tau(E_y). Its mutual information is

  I(X;Y)=sum_y tau(E_y)/(d^2-1) sum_i c(a x_(iy)),
  c(t)=((1+t)log(1+t)+(1-t)log(1-t))/2.

For |t|<=a<1, the power series gives c(t)<=t^2/[2(1-a^2)]. The Pauli basis Parseval identity and rank-one refinement of each POVM effect give

  sum_y tau(E_y) sum_i x_(iy)^2 <= d-1.

For the refinement step, v -> ||v||^2/v_0 is convex for v_0>0, with v_0=tau(E_y) and the other coordinates tau(E_yP_i); a rank-one effect w|psi><psi| contributes w(d-1)/d by purity, and all weights sum to d. Therefore

  I_acc(E) <= a^2(d-1)/[2(1-a^2)(d^2-1)]
           = a^2/[2(1-a^2)(d+1)] -> 0.

This gives an explicit capacity-bounded, globally hard-to-measure, robustly non-EB family, but the exact b2 limit a/4 blocks it as a counterexample to b2->0. Applying one random Haar unitary U to every P_i gives a randomly oriented matrix basis with identical formulas; the random orientation changes no witness norm. Independent random matrices without this complete-frame identity are not covered by this calculation.

2. QUANTUM-EXPANDER / RANDOM-UNITARY INSTRUMENTS: CP-RANGE LIFTING IS EASY IN THIS CLASS

Take any finite random-unitary bistochastic channel L(A)=sum_y p_y U_y A U_y*, sum p_y=1, on M_d, with faithful tracial reference sigma=I/d. This includes unitary quantum expanders, but assumes no expander gap. Its supplied Kraus operators are K_y=sqrt(p_y)U_y, so M_y=K_y*K_y=p_y I. The canonical measurement/Petz channel of these effects is exactly D(A)=tau(A)I, the depolarizing EB channel.

Use the actual instrument-append broadcaster: on input state rho it outputs sum_y p_y U_y rho U_y* tensor sigma, then symmetrizes receiver order. Its common marginal is C=(L+D)/2. Apply the tracial Petz adjoint C* to both outputs. The resulting full CPTP broadcaster has common marginal Phi=C*C, so Phi is genuinely self-compatible. This is the supplied bistochastic-instrument construction of work/cycle4/detailed_balance_broadcast_attack.txt, specialized to scalar effects; no candidate marginal is inferred from Choi compatibility alone.

On the traceless subspace, D=0 and C=L/2. Thus

  Phi(A)=L*L(A)/4 for tau(A)=0.

Bistochastic channels contract tracial L2, giving ||Phi||_(2->2, traceless)<=1/4. Since Phi fixes I,

  ||Phi^m-D||_(infinity->1,tau) <= 4^-m.

For ||A||_infinity<=1, write A0=A-tau(A)I; then ||A0||_(2,tau)<=1 and
||Phi^m(A)-D(A)||_(1,tau)<=||Phi^m(A0)||_(2,tau)<=4^-m. The map D is EB. Also every power Phi^m is actually self-compatible: apply Phi to both outputs of a Phi-broadcaster at each iteration. The dimension-free lifting conclusion is therefore true for this actual-broadcasting random-unitary class, even when L has no spectral gap. The result does not cover arbitrary self-compatible positive detailed-balance maps, especially those with slowly varying classical sectors or no supplied instrument representation.

3. CLIFFORD, DATA HIDING, AND THE LARGE-DIMENSION COUNTEREXAMPLE BOUNDARIES

The earlier flagged-Clifford construction gives fixed C=c(a), eEB approaching a/2, pair fidelity-affinity gap tending to zero, and uniform-prior accessible information tending to zero. Its exact Choi SOS nevertheless gives b2 >= (a/2)(1-1/sqrt(2)) for every clique size, including arbitrary output-flag changes and entangled outputs (work/cycle3/large_clique_broadcast.txt; independent local algebra and diagnostics are recorded there). It is a valid obstruction to pairwise-gap and accessible-information shortcuts, not a vanishing-b2 counterexample. The complete Pauli-frame calculation above supplies another exact collective witness and an exact b2 value.

Standard data hiding bounds the power of LOCC or another restricted measurement class. The EB optimization here allows an unrestricted global POVM on its input. In the standard symmetric/antisymmetric example, rho_+=P_sym/Tr(P_sym) and rho_-=P_asym/Tr(P_asym) have orthogonal supports; measuring {P_sym,P_asym} and preparing the corresponding state reproduces both exactly. The same measurement-and-prepare map broadcasts them by preparing two copies conditional on the outcome. Adding common maximally mixed noise makes the states full-rank but keeps them jointly diagonal in the symmetric/antisymmetric decomposition; measuring a common eigenbasis and preparing two copies of each basis state still reproduces both states exactly. Thus LOCC hiding alone supplies neither an EB lower bound nor a broadcasting obstruction (DiVincenzo, Leung, Terhal, quant-ph/0103098; their stated restriction is local operations and classical communication).

N61 is a genuine source-derived symmetric-power ladder: its supplied all-input splitter has marginal error 1/L, while its stated EB error tends to one. This exposes a useful boundary. For k_j=2^j, K=2^(L-1), one-particle dimension d0=LK, and D_j=binom(k_j+d0-1,k_j), the Haar average is sigma=(1/L) direct-sum_j I/D_j. The member states are (1/L) direct-sum_j |psi><psi|^tensor k_j. The capacity is (1/L)sum_j log D_j, since each member entropy is log L and the Haar mean maximizes average entropy. In particular D_(L-1)>= (d0/K)^K=L^K, so C>=K log(L)/L -> infinity. Each member is rank one within every sector, so it fails every fixed lower sandwich.

For isotropic repair rho^eta=(1-eta)rho+eta sigma, the maximum likelihood ratio in the top sector is eta+(1-eta)D_(L-1). Requiring rho^eta <=(1+theta)sigma implies

  1-eta <= theta/(D_(L-1)-1).

Consequently the replacement channel X->Tr(X)sigma has uniform error at most 1-eta<=theta/(D_(L-1)-1)->0. The standard noise repair that supplies a fixed sandwich erases the ladder's EB lower bound. This calculation is independent of the cited N61 lower-bound proof; it only uses its displayed state family and the given sandwich requirement. N61 card status is source-derived/unreviewed, so its b2/eEB asymptotics are not upgraded here.

A relevant recent primary de Finetti source now sharpens this boundary without removing it: Jeronimo, Wu, Xu, arXiv:2608.02590, Theorem 1.1, gives bosonic two-site trace distance at most sqrt(d-1)/(2(N-1)) and states the dimension dependence is optimal. The same paper's dimension-free Hilbert-Schmidt theorem is O(N^-1/2); standard Hilbert-Schmidt distance does not imply dimension-free physical trace distance when local dimension grows. Thus the theorem helps only when N dominates sqrt(d), and it does not yield the required dimension-uniform trace reconstruction of arbitrary growing-dimensional families.
