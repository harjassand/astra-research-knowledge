TAIL-UPPER AUDIT: SECTIONS 6 AND INPUTS (10.4), (11.1)
Scope: outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_tail_precision.txt §6,
and the cited full-trace bound (10.4) and exact Schur upper weight (11.1) in
outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_branch.txt. Assumptions used:
integer n>=1, 0<r<=1/2, nr^2<=c1^2, t=atanh(r), Q>=1. The critical regime
ensures r<=1/2 for sufficiently large n. Audit is analytic; no finite checks.

1. Per-spin bound and its constants
For Q<d=L+1, (10.4) says

  e_j <= (3/(2Q))(beta+beta^2 j/3) exp(beta j),
  beta=2t, j=L/2.

Substitution gives exactly

  e_j <= (3t+t^2 L)/Q exp(tL).

For r<=1/2, t<=4r/3 (integrate t'=1/(1-r^2)<=4/3). Therefore

  3t+t^2L <= 4r+(16/9)r^2L <=4r(1+rL),

so the stated e_j<=(4r/Q)(1+rL)e^(tL) is valid. If L+1<=Q, the
identity branch has zero error. Since L is integer, all potentially
nonzero sectors satisfy L>=Q.

The constants in (10.4) are consistent with (10.3): summing the thermal
series gives e_j<= E(F)/(4s j^2)(h+h^2/3)e^h, h=beta j; inserting
E(F)/s<=6j/Q yields (3/(2Q))(beta+beta^2j/3)e^(beta j). This checks the
half-trace factor and the substitution used above.

2. Schur-tail substitution (6.1)
For L=n,n-2,..., let k=(n-L)/2 and
pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,k). Equation (11.1) gives

  P_L <= [2(L+1)^2/n] pi_0(L) exp(tL).

Multiplying by the per-spin error gives the coefficient
8r/(Qn), polynomial (L+1)^2(1+rL), and exponential exp(2tL). If X is
the sum of n unbiased independent +/-1 variables, Pr(X=L)=pi_0(L) on
exactly this parity lattice. Summing over L>=Q consequently gives exactly

  e <= 8r/(Qn) E[(X+1)^2(1+rX) exp(2tX) 1_{X>=Q}],

with no missing parity or factor-of-two term. The exponential is squared
only in the sense that one factor exp(tL) comes from each of the two
bounds, producing exp(2tL). The half-trace norm of a block-diagonal Schur
state is the probability-weighted sum of its sector half-trace norms, so
this is the correct full-state averaging step.

3. Hoeffding and Cauchy–Schwarz exponents
Set Y=(|X|+1)^2(1+r|X|)exp(2tX). On X>=Q>=1 this is exactly the
nonnegative integrand in (6.1). Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  E[Y 1_{X>=Q}] <= (E Y^2)^(1/2) Pr(X>=Q)^(1/2).

Hoeffding (or the subgaussian mgf Chernoff bound) gives
Pr(X>=Q)<=exp(-Q^2/(2n)); taking the square root produces precisely
exp(-Q^2/(4n)) in (6.2). Thus the nested square-root exponent is correct.

4. Explicit second moment and M(c1)
For a=|X|, convexity gives (a+1)^4<=8(a^4+1) and
(1+ra)^2<=2(1+r^2a^2), hence

  (|X|+1)^4(1+r|X|)^2
    <=16(X^4+1+r^2X^6+r^2X^2).

The squared exponential in Y^2 is exp(4tX). For each polynomial term use
Cauchy–Schwarz and E exp(8tX)<=(exp(32nt^2)):

  E[X^4 exp(4tX)] <= sqrt(E X^8) exp(16nt^2)
                    <= sqrt(105)n^2 exp(16nt^2),
  E[exp(4tX)] <= exp(16nt^2),
  E[X^6 exp(4tX)] <= sqrt(10395)n^3 exp(16nt^2),
  E[X^2 exp(4tX)] <= sqrt(3)n exp(16nt^2).

Here the even-moment bounds are E X^8<=7!! n^4=105n^4,
E X^12<=11!! n^6=10395n^6, and E X^4<=3n^2. They follow by
coefficient comparison cosh(u)^n<=exp(nu^2/2). With nr^2<=c1^2 and n>=1,
the four terms combine to

  E Y^2 <=16 n^2 exp(16nt^2)
       [sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3))].

Taking the square root contributes 4n, a square root of the bracket, and
exp(8nt^2). Since t<=4r/3, 8nt^2<=128c1^2/9. This gives exactly

  sqrt(E Y^2)<=M(c1)n,
  M(c1)=4 sqrt[sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3))]
                 exp(128c1^2/9),

as in (6.3). All nested roots and exponential factors are accounted for.

5. Consequences
Substitution in (6.1) cancels n and gives (6.4):

  e<=8M(c1)(r/Q)exp(-Q^2/(4n)).

If Q>=1/r, then r/Q<=r^2<=c1^2/n, proving (6.5) with
C_U(c1)=8M(c1)c1^2. Thus the tail upper is internally consistent with
the stated assumptions and constants. This audit does not re-prove the
instrument construction or other sections, and does not establish external
validation or priority.
