# Primary-literature attack and boundary

This is a scoped prior-art check, not an exhaustive novelty search.

## Established mathematical mechanisms

- Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard, and Klein (2004), *Metastability in Reversible Diffusion Processes I*, prove sharp capacity/exit-time asymptotics and the Eyring–Kramers formula for a stated reversible diffusion class. The barrier-exponential rate law is established mathematics, not a new result here: [JEMS/EMS primary article, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/14](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/172).
- Freidlin–Wentzell large deviations supply the path action used in the lemma. The first variation of an optimized action under a smooth drift perturbation is a direct envelope-theorem calculation. I found no basis in the bounded search to claim the first-variation lemma itself is novel.

## Cell-fate dose, time, landscape, and noise prior art

- Tian, Zhang, and Xing (2013) modeled TGF-β-induced EMT as a sequential reversible pEMT switch followed by a ZEB/miR-200-dependent switch that can make full EMT persistent. Their model and stochastic simulations already connect stimulus strength and duration to cell fate; they discuss a broad transition-time distribution and explicitly propose pulse-response experiments: [Biophysical Journal 105 (2013), 1079–1089](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3752104/).
- Celià-Terrassa et al. (2018) experimentally compared hysteretic and non-hysteretic TGF-β responses. In their NMuMG assay, 5 minutes of TGF-β pulse was sufficient to induce EMT in the wild-type line, while the non-hysteretic mutant required at least 1 hour. Their work measures pulse strength/duration and persistence after withdrawal, so a generic “dose × time controls fate” claim is not new: [Nature Communications 9 (2018), article 5005](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-07538-7).
- Saez et al. (2022) combine quantitative differentiation data with catastrophe-theory/approximate-Bayesian landscape construction and predict cell-fate outcomes under signal combinations: [Cell Systems 13 (2022), DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2021.08.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2021.08.013).
- Lv et al. (2025) introduce landscape control over gene-network parameters and compare it with optimal least-action control on MISA, EMT, and HESC model networks. Their approach uses landscape/control computations and model noise; their discussion calls out uncertain high-dimensional landscape measurement, empirical validation, and uncertainty models as future work. A generic “compute a barrier and optimize a genetic control” proposal overlaps substantially: [Communications Physics 8 (2025), article 240](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02160-8).
- Liu, Zanca, Stumpf, and Ham (2026, article in press) use transition-path theory to show in illustrative cell-fate SDEs that noise type and strength can shift reactive densities/currents, create alternative routes, and move the dominant transition path away from the deterministic least-action/steepest-descent path. Their toggle-switch example makes this a direct warning against transferring the gradient-barrier theorem to a generic gene network: [npj Systems Biology and Applications (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41540-026-00762-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41540-026-00762-8).
- Zhang, Li, and Zhang (2026) construct a hyper solution landscape over regulatory parameters and report that different routes between the same states yield different fate distributions. This overlaps with route-dependent control, although not with the action-susceptibility perturbation formula: [Cell Systems 17 (2026), article 101562, DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2026.101562](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903533/).
- Deshmukh et al. (2022) measure dose- and time-dependent EMT distributions by single-cell RNA sequencing and find dose-dependent intermediate states. That work means endpoint-only binary fate labels can discard stable intermediate phenotypes: [PubMed, PMID 36159174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36159174/).

## What might remain distinct, and why novelty remains open

The narrow candidate contribution is an explicit link from a *measured time-resolved action-susceptibility kernel* to an (L^2)-dose-optimal pulse, with a stated observation-sample bound, actuator-amplitude/timing-noise debit, and equal-resource experimental comparator. This appears more specific than the landscape-control and EMT pulse-response items above, but it is an elementary sensitivity optimization layered on established Freidlin–Wentzell/Eyring–Kramers machinery. No systematic search was completed for control-functional derivatives, instanton sensitivity, robust stochastic pulse control, or equivalent formulas in physics, chemistry, and control theory. Status: **novelty UNKNOWN**.

The strongest likely prior-art/failure barrier is empirical rather than algebraic: the 2026 TPT article shows the most-probable-path picture can be incomplete under biologically motivated multiplicative noise and non-equilibrium curl forces. In addition, EMT is multi-step and may become self-sustaining after a brief pulse. Any claimed advance must validate its reduced stochastic model against measured lineage paths and reporter currents, not merely fit endpoint fractions or a deterministic bifurcation diagram.
