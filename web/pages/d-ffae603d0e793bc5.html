## Main source correction

Faul, Hoessly, and Xia's published Theorem 3.3(2) in *Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks* states that, for two networks with the same source set, they are unconfoundable exactly when the lifted strict-positive cones are disjoint at **every** common source. Their Theorem 5.15 and the proof in §7.3 instead establish that the networks are confoundable exactly when the cones intersect at **every** source. Taking the logical negation gives an existential disjoint-source condition. Thus the published “every” in Theorem 3.3(2) should be “some” (assuming equal source sets); differing source sets also force nonconfoundability. The published Cambridge version and the inspected arXiv v3 both carry the mismatch. Primary source: [Faul, Hoessly & Xia (2026), DOI 10.1017/S0956792526100382](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382), [arXiv:2505.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07638).

The counterexample uses one species and the networks \(\varnothing\to A, A\to\varnothing\) and \(\varnothing\to2A, A\to\varnothing\). Both have source set \(\{\varnothing,A\}\). At the empty source, signatures \((1,1)\) and \((2,4)\) have disjoint positive rays; at source \(A\), both signatures are \((-1,1)\). Generator equality would force \(a=2c\) from the constant drift coefficient and \(a=4c\) from the constant covariance coefficient, impossible for strictly positive \(c\). The exact counterexample and scope are in [observability_correction.md](observability_correction.md), with a bounded exact check in [verify_faul_cone_counterexample.py](verify_faul_cone_counterexample.py).

This is a logical correction to a source statement, not a new theorem of catalysis or a claim of scientific priority. The assumptions are the paper's structural interface: complete known candidate graphs, equal known species, mass-action Langevin approximation, strictly positive rates, and generator/law comparison on positive open state sets. It says nothing about finite noisy observations, hidden species, or real enzyme chemistry. No author or editor confirmation has been obtained; independent mathematical review is the next gate.

## Strongest finite kinetic discriminator

For the common single source \(C=E:S_4\), four possible release channels with product yields \(k=1,2,3,4\) and changes \(\nu_k=(-1,1,4-k,k)\) can have rates \((1,1,4,0)\) or \((0,4,1,1)\). The networks preserve enzyme and substrate moieties. Both have the same rate-weighted moments \(M_0=6,M_1=15,M_2=41\), hence identical ODE drift and identical full Langevin covariance for every state. Both also have the same \(\mathrm{Exp}(6)\) first-turnover time. Their third product-yield moments differ (117 versus 123), and the conditional yield third cumulants are \(-1/2\) and \(+1/2\).

Starting each assay from one preformed intermediate and recording only the integer product yield, the exact likelihood-ratio test separates these two fixed hypotheses with five independent turnovers: the sum of type-I and type-II error probabilities is \(181/3888\approx0.04655\). Four turnovers cannot reach the 5% combined-error target; the minimum is \(65/648\approx0.10031\). A fixed completion deadline \(T\ge\log(500)/6\approx1.036\) gives a union-bound censoring probability at most 1% across five assays. This is a finite model calculation and a test design, not experimental evidence or a general sample-complexity theorem. Full construction, rate law, readout requirements, and controls are in [the kinetic-observability report](../chemistry_kinetic_observability/FINAL_REPORT.md); exact arithmetic reruns with `python3 work/agents/chemistry_kinetic_observability/witness_audit.py`.

The chief failure mode is chemical instantiation: an enzyme-bound four-unit intermediate with exactly these release channels has not been identified. A real assay would also need measured yield confusion probabilities, background turnover, intermediate preparation yield, and count resolution. Seeing both yield 1 and yield 4 in one batch has probability zero under either fixed candidate and falsifies the two-model family. ODE or diffusion fitting cannot discriminate these hypotheses, even with ideal data; the exact CTMC likelihood test is the equally informed baseline.

## First-cycle evidence across the other catalytic lanes

These results are recorded as separate conditional results, not combined into a grand claim.

- **Thermodynamic current ceiling:** for an autonomous resolved \(N\)-state unicycle with bare rate sum \(K=\sum_i(k_i^++k_i^-)\) and cycle affinity \(A\), the reconstructed sharp bound is \(|J|\le (K/N^2)\tanh(|A|/(2N))\). Its inverse gives a conditional drive/dissipation floor when the bare rate budget is measured. The \(N=3\) rate-sum frontier is source-reported in Mottes et al. (2026), and the traffic-budget ETP is established prior art; arbitrary-\(N\) priority is unknown. See [proof](../chemistry_catalytic_thermodynamics/PROOF.md) and [report](../chemistry_catalytic_thermodynamics/FINAL_REPORT.md).
- **Rare-event kinetics:** untimed reactive state sequences are invariant under uniform rate rescaling, while the absolute transition rate scales. An absolute rate therefore requires physical-time flux information; this is a scoped identifiability no-go, not a new sampler. The report reconstructs forward-flux sampling and its sampling/runtime costs in [chemistry_reaction_rare_events/FINAL_REPORT.md](../chemistry_reaction_rare_events/FINAL_REPORT.md).
- **Inverse molecular design:** a representation that identifies mirror catalysts in an achiral environment cannot predict signed selectivity for both; an orientation bit resolves pair identity but not magnitude. The theorem is standard symmetry reasoning and does not identify a new catalyst. See [the report](../chemistry_inverse_molecular_design/FINAL_REPORT.md).
- **Quantum-chemistry representation:** near-integer natural-orbital occupations do not alone certify a transition-state barrier error. A paired-energy bound requires a discarded-space spectral gap and retained/discarded coupling norm; an exact two-block example refutes occupation-only certification. The cited Ru calculation compares endpoint energies in a small imposed-singlet model, not a correlated transition-state barrier or quantum advantage. See [the report](../chemistry_quantum_chem_representation/FINAL_REPORT.md).
- **Autocatalysis and selectivity:** with a common multiplicative catalytic exposure, common amplification preserves product ratios; differential growth/loss or nonmultiplicative pathways are required to change composition. The finite-batch example amplifies total replicator concentration elevenfold while retaining a 9:1 ratio. See [the report](../chemistry_autocatalysis_selectivity/FINAL_REPORT.md).
- **Electrochemical tradeoffs:** there is no universal intrinsic Faradaic-efficiency/overpotential/turnover law without reaction, transport, and cell assumptions. The report derives a conditional mass-transfer-coupled Tafel ceiling and an exact cell-voltage energy identity, and proposes a segmented-cell discriminator. No measurements were made; see [the report](../chemistry_electrochemical_tradeoffs/FINAL_REPORT.md).
