c07_l08 CROSS-PHASE COST AUDIT OF c07_s02 STOPPED_DIFFUSION
7 October 2026. INITIAL remains immutable. This is a cost/implementation
audit of c07_s02/revisions/STOPPED_DIFFUSION.txt, not an independent proof of
its whole theorem. Origin: stopped-diffusion proposal by c07_s02; the seed
quartic inequality below was already derived in c07_l08/INITIAL.txt.

STATUS

The written construction is a mathematical positive-mixture/separability
candidate. It is not yet a finite-bit state-preparation algorithm. Its own
global Feynman--Kac bound gives an exponential-in-sqrt(N) rejection cost, and
the paper explicitly leaves Brownian generation, weight normalization,
stopping resolution, and local emissions uncompiled.

I derive two cost improvements that are compatible with the stated interface:
(i) a larger certified diffusion ball and sharper heat-seed tail exponent;
(ii) a seed-radius-dependent rejection envelope. For the stopped diffusion,
the latter has a finite N-uniform normalizer, whereas the global envelope is
exp(h_N). A uniform second-moment bound supports truncating the rare bad paths
without paying exp(h_N). This gives a credible polynomial finite-bit route
conditional on a certified weak-error/interval implementation of the stopped
SDE. That final compiler gate is NOT closed here. In particular, no achieved
runtime or practical advantage is claimed.

1. RAW COSTS ACTUALLY PRESENT IN THE ASSIGNED DERIVATION

Write L=2M+1, T=Tr C<=3L, s=N^(3/4). Its path weight obeys

  |log w| <= h_N = (L/4)sqrt(N)+L/(2sqrt(N))+(B/2)N^(1/4).

The obvious exact-rejection implementation samples the reference stopped
diffusion and accepts with w/exp(h_N). Since the represented trace is >=1/2
for sufficiently large N, its acceptance probability is >=(1/2)exp(-h_N),
so its expected proposals are at most 2exp(h_N). This is a valid upper bound,
not an observed runtime; it grows superpolynomially.

Reproducible example M=0.1, B=0.2, N=50,000:

  h_N=68.5801; exp(h_N)=6.0806e29; global rejection bound=1.2161e30
  proposals; the largest raw weight needs 99 binary integer bits (plus the
  chosen fractional precision). At N=1,000,000, h_N=303.163 and the global
  rejection bound is about 9.18e131 proposals.

The large numeric weight does not itself imply exponential bit complexity:
storing log(w) needs O(log N+p) fixed-point bits for p fractional bits, and
storing w=e^(log w) directly uses O(sqrt(N)+p) magnitude bits. The expensive
part of the global-envelope sampler is the acceptance rate. Repeatedly
normalizing a batch of raw weights has the same range problem if one relies
only on max(w)<=exp(h_N); the report gives a pathwise envelope that removes
that dependence.

Other costs present at the interface:

* A local Bloch-vector error d_m changes k_N(m)=rho(m)^(tensor N) in trace
  distance by at most N*d_m/2 (telescoping the N factors). Thus a final
  vector precision d_m<=epsilon/(4N) suffices for epsilon/8 state error.
* Emitting rho(m)^(tensor N) needs N local mixed-qubit preparations. If the
  primitive is pure-state preparation, write rho(m) as a Bernoulli mixture
  of the two pure eigenstates in direction m/|m| and spend N additional
  independent random bits (or use N local purifying ancillas). One common
  random sign for all qubits would give the wrong tensor product.
* For rational/finite-bit A,b, interval evaluation of the displayed 3x3
  coefficients and square root costs polynomially in their input bit length
  and working precision because the diffusion matrix has a stated positive
  margin on the stopped ball. Arbitrary exact-real A,b with no representation
  or evaluation oracle are not a finite-bit input interface. A sufficient
  worst-case coefficient quantization is ||dA||=O(epsilon/sqrt(N)) and
  ||db||=O(epsilon/N^(1/4)); this follows from ||J||<=N/2 and ||Q_dA||<=
  N^2||dA||/4 in the exponent.

2. LARGER INNER BALL; IMPACT ON THE HIDDEN THRESHOLD

The exact lower bound in (S5), with x=|m|^2, is
  g_L(x)=(1-x)^2-Lx=1-(L+2)x+x^2.
Let
  x_* = 2/(L+2+sqrt(L^2+4L)),
the smaller root of g_L. For any fixed 0<theta<1, choosing
  r_0^2=theta*x_*
gives the exact margin
  g_L(theta*x_*)=(1-theta)(1-theta*x_*^2)>0.
Thus the stopped diffusion remains uniformly elliptic. With theta=0.99 and
L>=1, the margin is at least 0.0085; the square-root conditioning is still a
fixed constant. The prescribed radius in STOPPED_DIFFUSION is much smaller:
r_old^2=1/[16(L+1)].

The heat-seed proof in c07_l08/INITIAL also establishes
  f(u)=u^2/2-log(cosh u) >= delta*u^4, 0<=u<=1,
  delta=1/2-log(cosh 1)=0.0662191695... .
This strengthens the assigned text's f(u)>=u^4/48. Consequently its small-r
tail exponent can use delta*a_0^4/16 rather than a_0^4/768, where
a_0=2*atanh(r_0/2). This uses only the same heat seed, and does not change
the proof's supplied/acquired boundary.

For M=0.1, B=0.2, N=50,000, theta=0.99:

  assigned radius r_old=0.16855; assigned kappa_0*N=0.0530;
  larger radius r_0=0.58948; ellipticity lower bound=0.00878;
  a_0=0.60750; strengthened kappa_0*N=28.19;
  assigned hit exponent kappa_1*N^(3/2)=1,033.9; larger-ball value=12,646.7.

At N=50,000 the *published coarse error bound* (S12) is still vacuous: its
seed term contains exp(h_N-kappa_0*N), which remains enormous even after
these changes. With the larger ball and strengthened seed exponent,
kappa_0 approximately 5.64e-4; the leading comparison kappa_0*N versus
(L/4)sqrt(N) crosses only around N~2.8e5, before the N^(3/4) prefactor and
unknown tail constant are paid. N=10^6 gives a large exponent gap, but is
already a million-qubit mathematical regime. So the improvement repairs a
hidden constant materially; it does not establish practical utility at the
50,000-spin illustration.

3. A WEIGHT-MOMENT LEMMA, WITH ITS ASSUMPTIONS MADE EXPLICIT

The global cap exp(h_N) ignores the seed's quartic radial tail. Let R=N^(1/4)r
be the radial heat-field variable (so |m_0|=tanh(r/2)<=r/2), and let
Z=N^(3/4) sup_{t<=1 wedge tau}|M_t| for the stopped martingale. For N large
enough that the retained seed mass is at least 1/2, the exact heat density
and its lower normalizer L_base=(7/3)exp(-Lambda/2-1/12) give

  density(R) <= C_0 R^2 exp(-c R^4-Lambda*R^2/16),
  c=delta/16, C_0=2 exp(a_0/2)/L_base,

on the retained branches (with an atom at R=0 from the replacement). This
uses t_0<2/N, including the extra negative quadratic from Lambda.

For each coordinate of the stopped martingale, quadratic variation is at
most T/(2N^(3/2)). The exponential-martingale maximal inequality and a
three-coordinate union bound give
  P(Z>z | initial branch) <= 6 exp(-z^2/(3T)).
The bound is uniform in that branch. The drift bound (S8) implies

  y := N^(1/4) sup_{t<=1 wedge tau}|m_t|
     <= R/2+d_N+Z/sqrt(N),
  d_N=N^(1/4)*beta_N
     <= c_a*N^(-1/4),
  c_a=T*r_0/2+3T*r_0/4+B/2.

Using the exact potential formula, on every stopped path
  |log w| <= (L/4)y^2+(B/2)y+T/(4sqrt(N)).
Apply (u+v)^2 <= (3/2)u^2+3v^2 to u=R/2 and
v=d_N+Z/sqrt(N), then (d+e)^2<=2d^2+2e^2. This yields the explicit
second-moment domination

  w^2 <= exp(C_N) exp((3L/16)R^2+(B/2)R)
                 exp((3L/N)Z^2+(B/sqrt(N))Z),
  C_N=T/(2sqrt(N))+3L*c_a^2/sqrt(N)+B*c_a/N^(1/4).

Combining it with the heat and martingale tails proves E[w^2]<=C_2(Lambda,L,T,B)
uniformly for N>=max(Lambda^2, 18LT, N_seed), where N_seed ensures retained
mass>=1/2. For example one explicit finite upper bound is

 C_2 = exp(T/2+3L*c_a^2+B*c_a) * C_Z(1/(6T),B;T)
       * [1+C_0*Integral_0^infinity
                       R^2 exp(-cR^4+alpha R^2+(B/2)R)dR],
 alpha=(3L-Lambda)/16,
 C_Z(a,b;T)=1+6*Integral_0^infinity (2az+b)
                           exp(az^2+bz-z^2/(3T)) dz.

The atom at R=0 from the bad-branch replacement is covered by the leading
1. Both integrals converge: the seed integral has a negative quartic leading
term; the martingale integral has a negative quadratic leading term. This is
an explicit N-uniform moment result, but its coarse C_2 can be large and must
be interval evaluated before it can be treated as a numerical runtime
constant.

Since trace(k_N(m))=1, E[w]=Tr(W_1). For the stopped process started from
rho_0' (the radially truncated/replaced heat seed), its un-stopped comparison
G'=e^(M_H)rho_0' also has trace>=1: Tr(H rho_0')>=0 because the seed has zero
mean Bloch vector and the C quadratic part is nonnegative on the Bloch ball;
then scalar Jensen gives Tr(e^H rho_0')>=exp(Tr(H rho_0'))>=1. Equation
(S10) bounds only the stopping residual from G' to W_1, so E[w]>=1/2 once
that residual is <=1/2. This avoids relying on the coarse rho_0-to-rho_0'
term of (S11) for the sampler's normalization, but it does NOT repair that
initial-replacement term in the final target-to-mixture certificate.
The moment lemma permits weighted rare-event truncation by Cauchy--Schwarz:
for any path event E,
  E[w 1_E] <= sqrt(C_2*P(E)).
This is the exact place where typical weighted mass can replace the crude
e^(h_N)P(E) estimate for sampling. It does not on its own improve every
operator-norm Duhamel remainder in (S11).

4. PATHWISE ENVELOPE THAT AVOIDS GLOBAL-WEIGHT REJECTION

Fix epsilon<1/8. Choose cutoffs X,Z_* so that (using the preceding density
and tail bounds)
  E[w 1_{R>X}] <= epsilon/16,
  E[w 1_{Z>Z_*}] <= epsilon/16,
and choose N so the stop/extension remainder has weighted mass <=epsilon/16.
For instance Z_* can be O_T(sqrt(log(C_2/epsilon^2))); X can be
O_{Lambda,L,B}((log(C_2/epsilon))^(1/4)). On the retained event, the
pathwise displacement bound gives

 d_*=N^(1/4)*beta_N+Z_*/sqrt(N),
 log w <= log g_N(R)
   := (L/4)(R/2+d_*)^2+(B/2)(R/2+d_*)+T/(4sqrt(N)).

Therefore a reference proposal with initial radial law proportional to
q_N(R)g_N(R)1_{R<=X}, uniform direction, and the original conditional path
law can accept a path with probability (w/g_N(R))1_{good}. On accepted
paths, the factor g cancels exactly:
  q_N(R)g_N(R) * w/g_N(R) = q_N(R)w.
The accepted path is the desired Feynman--Kac law restricted to the good
event. The discarded weighted mass is <=3epsilon/16. No unknown target
partition function is used in the acceptance probability.

The tilted-seed normalizer has no exp(sqrt(N)) factor. Its replacement atom
contributes at most g_N(0); the continuous radial integrand is bounded by a
constant times

 R^2 exp(-cR^4 + (L-Lambda)R^2/16
          +(L*d_*/4+B/4)R + O_{L,B,T}(d_*^2+N^(-1/2))).

Since L-Lambda=M, its positive quadratic is dominated by the quartic for
every fixed M. Hence G_N=E_q[g_N 1_{R<=X}] is bounded by an explicit finite
constant depending on M,B and (at worst) polynomially on 1/epsilon, not
exp(h_N). Once E[w 1_good]>=1/4, acceptance is >=1/(4G_N); capping the
number of proposals at 4G_N*log(1/delta) makes failure probability <=delta.
This changes the weight-normalization bottleneck from exp(O(sqrt(N))) to a
cost controlled by explicit fixed-parameter quartic integrals and log(1/delta).

For the numerical example above, a 30-digit mpmath quadrature (not an
interval proof) estimates C_2<=2.59e4 at N=50,000. Taking epsilon=0.1,
the displayed Cauchy--Schwarz cutoffs are Z_*=15.45 and X=8.16. The
path-envelope normalizer bound, including the replacement atom and d_*, is
about G_N<=62.5. This gives an expected-proposal upper bound 4G_N about
250; a fixed cap about 750 proposals gives a 95% acceptance-run success
bound. These numbers cover only the ideal continuous/discrete-law
normalization analysis, and the integral values are not interval-certified.

To implement the proposal with finite bits, the initial radial law can be
rescaled to R and tabulated on a grid. On R<=X, a radial grid spacing
O(epsilon/N^(3/4)) gives Bloch error O(epsilon/N), since |m|<=r/2 and the
product-kernel Lipschitz constant is N/2. The number of radial bins is
O(X*N^(3/4)/epsilon); angular uniforms need O(log(N*X/epsilon)) bits. Use
interval quadrature for bin masses and rational cumulative probabilities.
For path increments, a Rademacher Euler scheme uses exactly 3 random bits per
time step instead of 3 inverse-CDF normals. It matches Gaussian mean,
covariance and third moments, so a weak Taylor audit can target first-order
global error. With coefficients on the inner ball,
  |a|=O(N^(-1/2)), |sigma|=O(N^(-3/4)), |V|=O(sqrt(N));
and the product-kernel derivatives through order four are O(N^4). The
leading local weak-defect scales are V^2*k=O(N), a^2*D^2k=O(N), and
sigma^4*D^4k=O(N), suggesting a certified step count K=poly(N,1/epsilon)
(a first-order estimate is K>=C(M,B,X)*N/epsilon). Each step requires
constant-dimension interval arithmetic and a fixed number of random bits.

That last weak-error estimate is a target, not a verified bound in this
artifact. A complete compiler still must: (a) prove the stopped-boundary or
smooth-extension discretization remainder in trace norm; (b) propagate
interval errors through square roots, weights and the tilted-seed table;
(c) specify a finite-bit Bernoulli/angle implementation and accumulate its
total error; and (d) check N against the analytic separability error (S12),
whose constants are not yet interval-certified. Until then, this is a
conditional implementation path, not a certified polynomial sampler.

5. PREPARATION/WEIGHT PRECISION LEDGER

If the preceding compiler gates pass with K time steps and total requested
error epsilon, a conservative per-output ledger is:

* path random bits: 3K Rademacher bits, plus O(log(NX/epsilon)) angular and
  seed-table index bits and O(N) independent local-mixture bits;
* coefficient/state arithmetic: p=O(log(NK/epsilon)+log(1/delta_D)) bits
  after the 3x3 spectral margin delta_D is fixed; table preprocessing uses
  O(X*N^(3/4)/epsilon) radial intervals and certified elementary-function
  quadrature;
* weight arithmetic on accepted candidates: storing raw log w globally costs
  O(log N+p) log-weight bits, while direct exp(h_N) weights require
  O(sqrt(N)+p) magnitude bits. Under the localized envelope, R<=X and Z<=Z_*
  bound log(w/g) on the accepted support by O_{M,B}(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))+1),
  so only O(log(1/epsilon)+log G_N) weight bits are needed;
* proposals: at most O(G_N log(1/delta)) complete paths for failure delta,
  each K steps, instead of the global 2exp(h_N) bound;
* output: N mixed single-qubit preparations and N independent local random
  choices, with per-qubit angle/Bloch precision O(epsilon/N).

The input must be known finite-bit A,b (or supply a certified evaluation
oracle); the construction is not an acquisition algorithm for unknown A,b.
The total cost is polynomial in N, 1/epsilon and input bit length only after
the weak-error and interval compiler gates above are proved. No practical
runtime claim follows from polynomiality alone; the displayed radial table,
step constant, and N-threshold need actual interval evaluation.

6. GATES AND VERIFICATION STATUS

Independent algebra check: the Bloch identities (S2)-(S4) are consistent
with the exact Fraction fixture report supplied in c07_s02, but N<=3 fixtures
do not prove them for all N. The sign of -R_n^2 and the inner-ball PSD bound
remain essential. The cost derivations here use those displayed formulas;
they do not validate (S1) as a whole.

New exact algebra: the larger-ball identity
g_L(theta*x_*)=(1-theta)(1-theta*x_*^2) follows by substituting
g_L(x_*)=0. It preserves a fixed positive margin for theta<1.
New analytic derivation: the uniform E[w^2] bound above follows from the
quartic heat-seed tail, stopped-martingale maximal tail, and explicit
potential envelope. It is an internal derivation; no independent verifier
has checked every constant or boundary condition.

Runnable evidence is limited to the arithmetic scripts and JSON beside this
report. The pure-Python script evaluates displayed constants for
(M,B,N)=(0.1,0.2,50,000) and (0.1,0.2,1,000,000), and numerically checks the
radius identity. The separate mpmath script estimates the one-dimensional
moment/envelope integrals above. Neither script interval-certifies those
integrals, executes an SDE, runs a random-bit sampler, or emits qubits.
