## Primary literature read

### Hu and Liu, 2025: learned PDE boundary safety filter

- Hanjiang Hu and Changliu Liu, “Safe PDE Boundary Control with Neural Operators,” L4DC 2025, PMLR 283:513–526. Primary paper: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/hu25a.html . Full arXiv v2 HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15643v2 (version dated 22 May 2025).
- Read depths: arXiv HTML abstract/introduction, problem definition, Theorems 2 and 3, Sections 3.2–3.3, Algorithm 1, and Appendix C discrete-time implementation.
- Mechanism reconstructed: Definition 1 is eventual finite-horizon boundary-output feasibility; Theorem 2 gives a time-dependent boundary barrier condition; a neural operator is trained from $K$ input/output trajectories sampled at $M$ points to model boundary input-to-output trajectories; Theorem 3 recovers the barrier guarantee only under an exact learned operator; an affine derivative dependence is inserted into a QP filter.
- Limitation recorded from the source: the discrete algorithm updates a predicted output trajectory through the learned operator. Appendix C says the QP is not yet real-time, uses threshold $\eta$ as a workaround for model mismatch, and leaves that mismatch analysis for future work. The theorem itself is conditional; this report does not treat it as refuted.
- Scope comparison: the safety output is a boundary trajectory, not the domain inventory $\int q$. The paper includes a transport benchmark with recirculation, but the theorem in this work assumes zero recirculation.
- Resource boundary: the source trains its neural operator from $K$ trajectories sampled at $M$ points and reports an online QP that is not yet real-time in its discrete implementation. No comparable physical energy or deployment-latency figure was extracted; unreported remains unreported.

### Kiss, Molnár, Ames, and Orosz, 2023: control barrier functionals for delays

- Adam K. Kiss, Tamás G. Molnár, Aaron D. Ames, and Gábor Orosz, “Control Barrier Functionals: Safety-critical Control for Time Delay Systems,” International Journal of Robust and Nonlinear Control (2023), DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/rnc.6751 ; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08409 .
- Read depths: arXiv PDF pp. 1–5 and the time-delay CBF definition and invariance theorem at pp. 9–11 (PDF extracted lines 637–650 and 847–856).
- Mechanism reconstructed: the delay history is the state in a function space; a Fréchet-differentiable control-barrier functional has an affine derivative $\dot H=L_FH+L_GH\,u$; requiring its derivative to satisfy a comparison inequality makes the delay-state safe set invariant, and a QP can enforce the constraint.
- Scope boundary: the paper treats a fixed delay and state history, with continuous-time feedback. Its introduction says it does not address disturbances. This remains the closest structural comparator for the scalar plug-flow identity, while the newer coupled-PDE output-regulation result below is a stronger direct PDE-boundary safety comparator. The transport theorem here is positioned as a possible tractable specialization for uncertain fixed speed, acquired initial cell masses, and sampled/held inputs. Priority is open.

### Bhan, Bian, Krstic, and Shi, 2024: PDE Control Gym

- Luke Bhan, Yuexin Bian, Miroslav Krstic, and Yuanyuan Shi, “PDE Control Gym: A Benchmark for Data-Driven Boundary Control of Partial Differential Equations,” L4DC 2024, PMLR 242:1083–1095. Paper: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v242/bhan24a.html . Transport environment documentation: https://pdecontrolgym.readthedocs.io/en/latest/environments/hyperbolic-1d.html .
- Read depths: paper abstract and transport environment equations/implementation description.
- Mechanism/scope: the benchmark transport equation is $u_t=u_x+\beta(x)u(0,t)$, with boundary actuation at $x=X$; its goal is asymptotic stabilization. With $\beta=0$, reversing the spatial coordinate gives the scalar plug-flow PDE here. The benchmark uses a first-order finite-difference scheme, so its numerical trajectories are not a continuum certificate.
- Limitation: this route supports a testbed for a zero-recirculation subcase, not a transfer to the benchmark’s full recirculating dynamics.

### Koga and Krstic, 2023: continuum PDE safety filter for a Stefan phase-change model

- Shumon Koga and Miroslav Krstic, “Safe PDE Backstepping QP Control with High Relative Degree CBFs: Stefan Model with Actuator Dynamics,” IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 68(12), 7195–7208 (2023), DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2023.3250514; primary source https://arxiv.org/html/2111.01187v1 and author-hosted published PDF https://flyingv.ucsd.edu/papers/PDF/424.pdf .
- Read depths: plant/state constraints (1)–(8), CBF definitions (11)–(14), QP-backstepping-filter derivation (31)–(44), Theorem 1, and upper-state constraint extension. The liquid has a moving interface $s(t)$, heat PDE $T_t=\alpha T_{xx}$, boundary heat flux $q_c$, actuator dynamics $\dot q_c=U$, and Stefan boundary equation. Their CBFs include an energy-deficit functional, nonnegative heat flux, and a backstepping auxiliary barrier that handles relative degree two. The QP projects an arbitrary operator input onto an explicit state-dependent interval $U_*\le U\le U^*$; Theorem 1 states continuum safety of the interface and temperature for all time under its initial, setpoint, gain, and classical-solution assumptions.
- Scope boundary: this is a strong continuum PDE safety-filter precedent, but it is a parabolic free-boundary phase-change model with an actuator integrator and full-state quantities in the filter, not scalar linear transport with uncertain speed, partial cell-mass sensing, ZOH input, and a total-inventory cap. Its additive-manufacturing evidence is numerical simulation, not physical testing.
- Resource boundary: the controller uses the interface, boundary heat flux, and a distributed-temperature energy functional; sensor implementation/cost and measured electrical heater energy are not reported in the source read. The QP is an explicit scalar projection once those state-dependent bounds are available; source execution latency was not reported.

### Koga and Krstic, 2025: safe Stefan stabilization with second-order moving-boundary dynamics

- Shumon Koga and Miroslav Krstic, “Safe Stabilization of the Stefan Problem with a High-Order Moving Boundary Dynamics by PDE Backstepping,” arXiv:2510.06571v1 (8 Oct 2025): https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06571 .
- Read depths: abstract, model/constraints (1)–(10), backstepping boundary law (22)–(37), and main stability theorem. The moving interface has second-order dynamics; the boundary heat-flux control is constrained to remain nonnegative, and the maximum principle plus a backstepping/Lyapunov design yields safety of the temperature and interface with exponential stabilization. Simulations are numerical. This is a current Stefan-specific boundary safety result, not an inventory filter for linear transport.

### Wang and Krstic, 2025/2026: safe adaptive and output regulation for hyperbolic PDE-ODE cascades

- Ji Wang and Miroslav Krstic, “Output-Positive Adaptive Control of Hyperbolic PDE-ODE Cascades,” IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 70(10), 7055–7062 (2025); primary preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05596 and author-hosted published PDF https://jiwebpage.github.io/Output-Positive_Adaptive_Control_of_Hyperbolic_PDE-ODE_Cascades.pdf .
- Read depths: abstract and published-paper introduction/summary via the author-hosted PDF. Mechanism: backstepping with a high-relative-degree adaptive CBF and batch least-squares identification (BaLSI), for positivity of a distal ODE output in a hyperbolic PDE-ODE cascade with parametric uncertainties. Source reports finite-time exact parameter identification and simulation; this is a coupled PDE-ODE output-positivity result, not an inventory-capacity result for scalar plug flow.
- Ji Wang and Miroslav Krstic, “Safe Output Regulation of Coupled Hyperbolic PDE-ODE Systems,” arXiv:2512.05822v4 (21 Aug 2026): https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05822v4 .
- Read depths: problem assumptions, system (1)–(5), high-order barrier construction (44)–(52), Theorems 1 and 2, and observer-error safety modification (130)–(135). The plant is a known-parameter heterodirectional 2x2 hyperbolic PDE coupled to a strict-feedback ODE, with disturbances/reference generated by an observable finite-dimensional neutrally stable exosystem. Boundary measurements feed an extended observer. The controller predicts the ODE output over the transport dead-time $1/q_2$, then uses a high-relative-degree barrier/backstepping design; output feedback adds a signed exponentially decaying term $M_c e^{-sigma_r(t-t_0)}$ whose bound comes from an assumed bound on initial observer error. The theorem assumes classical solutions, smooth barrier with derivative bounded away from zero, specified observability and initial-data bounds. It gives continuous-time output safety and regulation under those assumptions; the no-actuation transit interval requires a predicted-safe initial trajectory for safety from $t_0$, otherwise it provides later recovery claims.
- Scope boundary: this is a closer current boundary-actuated hyperbolic safety comparator than Hu–Liu for continuous-time PDE boundary regulation, but its output is a scalar distal ODE tracking error. Its model, state/observer, exogenous-signal and barrier assumptions differ from uncertain-speed scalar plug-flow inventory. Its analytical controller is not a sampled-data cell-mass robust filter.
- Resource boundary: the source computes backstepping kernels and an extended distributed observer from boundary/ODE measurements; sensor installation, runtime, and physical actuation energy are not quantified in the read source. The disturbance and initial-observer bounds are supplied assumptions, not quantities acquired by an experiment here.

### Block and Stockar, 2025: in-domain spatial CBF for traffic density

- Brian Block and Stephanie Stockar, “Constrained Control of PDE Traffic Flow via Spatial Control Barrier Functions,” arXiv:2512.04823v2: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04823v2 .
- Read depths: model/linearization, CLF/sCBF formulation (6)–(7), (13)–(26), and case-study simulation from both the HTML and official arXiv v2 PDF, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.04823v2 . The source uses linearized LWR traffic dynamics with distributed in-domain variable-speed-limit actuation $u=\partial_z\Delta b$; its spatial barrier is $h(z,t)=\lambda(z,t)-x(z,t)$ for an upper density profile. It combines a CLF and sCBF through an optimization/QP and demonstrates a circular-road simulation. It is a direct density-constraint comparator, but not a boundary-actuated inventory constraint; this read did not reconstruct a discretization-error certificate or independently validate a continuum implementation.
- The displayed CBF set condition (23) is $L_fh+L_ghu\ge -\alpha(h)$, while the QP constraint displayed in (26) is $L_fh+L_ghu\le -\alpha(h)$. I checked the official arXiv v2 PDF (2 Jun 2026), pp. 3–4, and the sign discrepancy is present there as well as in the HTML. No implementation/code audit or author clarification was found, so the report does not use Eq. (26) as a verified safety filter. It records only the model, barrier target, actuation location, and source-reported simulation; whether the discrepancy is typographical or affects the simulated implementation is UNKNOWN.
- Resource boundary: the spatial control is a distributed VSL field coupled to a QP; the paper gives a 1000 m, 200 s circular-road simulation but no wall-clock runtime, energy, or discretization-error bound in the read source.

### Wang and Krstic, 2026: adaptive output positivity for a parabolic PDE-ODE cascade

- Ji Wang and Miroslav Krstic, “Output-Positive Adaptive Control of Parabolic PDE-ODE Cascades,” arXiv:2602.04656v2 (29 Sep 2026): https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04656v2 .
- Read depths: plant/assumptions, backstepping and positivity mechanism, and Theorems 1–2. It treats a reaction-diffusion PDE coupled to a distal ODE, with unknown bounded reaction parameter and input gain in its adaptive result. Backstepping maps to a stable heat target; a decaying target boundary signal plus a spectral-series bound preserves positivity of the distal ODE output. BaLSI identifies unknown parameters in finite time. This is a current adjacent safety result for a different parabolic model/output; it is not transport inventory control.
- No downloads, numerical replay, or physical measurements were performed for these papers. Source claims remain source-reported.
