Result: primary prior-art screen for exact comparator-only nonlinear acquisition

The strongest prior-art match is exact active learning of lexicographic
preferences from one-bit comparisons of chosen outcome pairs. The strongest
structural match is the classical strictly triangular polynomial-permutation
group, with a separate multivariate-quadratic cryptanalysis literature. The
screen did not identify a primary result combining those pieces with recovery
of an unknown affine input basis from only the ordinal comparison bit and an
exact full decoder guarantee. This is a bounded screen; novelty and priority
remain UNKNOWN.

The precise candidate is a permutation on F_2^n whose most-significant-first
bits have hidden-affine triangular form q_i(x)=u_i+g_i(u_<i), u=Lx+c,
L invertible, deg(g_i)<=d. The only observations are chosen-pair answers
C(x,y)=[int Q(x)<int Q(y)]. No coordinate labels, target ranks, coefficients,
affine transform, or prefix fibers are supplied. Exact full decoding and
arbitrary-prefix execution are the intended output. The degree-2 case is the
main nonlinear target. The full contract, retrieved sources, exact page
ranges, and source boundaries are recorded in evidence/PRIOR_ART.txt.

The closest published interface prior is Booth et al.'s active elicitation of
lexicographic preference relations: submit a pair of outcomes, receive one
preference bit, then identify the relation exactly with query complexity
matching the information lower bound. Their model includes conditional local
preferences. If the latent coordinates u and their significance order were
visible, the target's g_i are precisely conditional binary local-preference
rules; a degree bound makes this a succinct subfamily. The preference paper
does not learn a hidden affine change of the outcomes and does not exploit a
low-degree coefficient representation. Its unrestricted conditional tables
are exponentially large. This is a close model and oracle precedent, so any
novelty statement must center on efficient acquisition under hidden affine
features and the low-degree promise, not on lexicographic comparison learning
alone.

The target map family itself is not new: Maubach defines strictly triangular
polynomial maps over finite fields in the same coordinate form and notes that
they are automatically invertible. Quadratic triangular shears are inside
that algebraic family when coordinates are visible. Step-wise triangular
multivariate-quadratic cryptanalysis and polynomial-equivalence algorithms
also recover or exploit hidden structure, but their inputs include public
quadratic equations, coefficient forms, ciphertexts, or related outputs. They
do not use the target's one-bit chosen-pair ordinal oracle. Pairwise-comparison
learning for combinatorial functions is a broader adjacent area, but its
principal guarantees are distributional approximation rather than an exact
finite rank decoder.

Three inherited campaign results narrow the novelty claim further:

* The affine-only subcase is already covered by an exact comparator learner.
  If d<=1, composing the triangular affine map with L leaves an affine rank
  map. The source handoff gives O(n^2) one-bit queries and a matching-order
  information lower bound n^2+n-2. A new claim must isolate d>=2.
* The inherited nonlinear ordinal-bit result already recovers one exact
  low-degree rank bit from a comparison-only oracle for a general bijection,
  using a median anchor and Reed-Muller self-correction. It does not recover
  the remaining rank coordinates or a full decoder.
* The inherited block-shear result is adjacent but not a direct subcase of
  the current significance orientation. Its integer rank places y xor g(z)
  in the high block and z in the low block, so leading significant bits may
  be quadratic in lower-significance coordinates. The candidate triangular
  contract forces q_1 to be affine. The block shear is therefore a distinct
  reverse-block-triangular model unless its leading shear is affine. Its
  exact finite tests and probabilistic first-feature acquisition are useful
  internal controls, not external novelty evidence.

The coordinator reports a stronger tournament-before-interpolation method
with expected Q<=n M_d+5n(n-1)/2 comparisons, where
M_d=sum_{k=0}^d binom(n,k). At each stage it compares candidate translation
bits before interpolating only the winning bit. For distinct actual rank bits
of a permutation, the bits are independent under uniform x, so their values
agree with probability 1/2; a capped search can return UNKNOWN. This count and
the stated cap-failure bound are coordinator-reported claims and were not
independently proved or run in this prior-art lane. The current
root_ordinal/triangular_translation.py in the workspace is an earlier
enumerate-and-interpolate implementation with an O(n^2 M_2) query bound; its
finite checks cannot be cited as verification of the improved count.

For comparison, with the latent basis known, degree-d triangular maps already
have D=sum_{k=0}^d binom(n,k+1) independent Boolean coefficients across their
local rules, each choice inducing a distinct total order. Thus D is a
one-bit-query information lower bound for exact identification of this
subclass. For fixed d>=2, the reported n M_d count is asymptotically about
(d+1)D. This is a direct counting check on the candidate, not a literature
novelty result and not a proof of the learner. The hidden-affine basis can add
requirements beyond this known-basis subclass.
