Cyclic length and chromatic fixed-point loss
OpenAI

Abstract
For a finitep-group G and a subgroupH, we prove that the optimal chromatic fixed-point
loss equals the shortest length of a subnormal chain fromH to G with cyclic quotients. The
equality holds at every prime and every nonnegative height, resolving positively the equality
proposed by Kuhn and Lloyd.
1 Introduction
The chromatic Smith problem asks how much information about fixed points at one subgroup is
forced by chromatic acyclicity at another. Fix a primep, and letK(i) denote MoravaK-theory
at p, withK(0) =HQ. For a finitep-group G, a subgroupH≤G, and an integern≥0, define
rn(G,H ) to be the least nonnegative integerr such that
K(n +r)∗(ΦHX) = 0 = ⇒K(n)∗(ΦGX) = 0 (1.1)
for every finitep-local genuineG-spectrum X. Here ΦHX denotes the underlying spectrum of the
geometric fixed points, and finite spectra include compact retracts.
Let ℓ(G,H ) be the least length of a subnormal chain
H =G0 ⊴G1 ⊴···⊴Gs =G
whose successive quotients are cyclic. We setℓ(G,G ) = 0. Such chains exist in finitep-groups.
The cyclic chromatic Smith theorem, applied along a chain, givesrn(G,H )≤ℓ(G,H ). Our result
is the converse.
Theorem 1.1. For every primep, finitep-groupG, subgroupH≤G, and integern≥0,
rn(G,H ) =ℓ(G,H ).
Thus the optimal loss is independent of the height. For every integer0≤r<ℓ(G,H ), there
is a finite genuineG-spectrum whose geometricH-fixed points areK(n +r)-acyclic and whose
geometric G-fixed points have nonzeroK(n)-homology. The theorem includes rational fixed-point
detection atn = 0.
The embedding ofH matters. For example, in G = Cp2×Cp, take H1 = pCp2×0 and
H2 = 0×Cp. Both subgroups are isomorphic toCp, butG/H1∼= Cp×Cp and G/H2∼= Cp2.
Chains aboveHi correspond to chains in these quotients. The first quotient requires two cyclic
steps, while the second requires one. Thusrn(G,H 1) = 2 andrn(G,H 2) = 1 at every target height.
A cyclic quotient of orderp2 still counts only once.

History and significance
The classical starting point is Smith theory: for a finite complex with an action of a finitep-
group, mod-p acyclicity passes to fixed points. Floyd strengthened this to an inequality between
total mod-p Betti numbers [6, Theorem 4.4]; the finitep-group statement follows by iteration
from prime-order actions. Chromatic Smith theory asks which implications survive when mod-p
homology is replaced by individual Morava theories.
Building on the nonequivariant thick subcategory theorem of Hopkins and Smith [12], Balmer
and Sanders identified all primes of the category of finite genuine equivariant spectra. They reduced
its topology to subgroup–height inclusions and computed the caseCp, crediting Strickland’s earlier
work [2, Introduction, Theorems 4.9 and 4.14, and Section 7]. Barthel, Hausmann, Naumann,
Nikolaus, Noel, and Stapleton determined the finite abelianp-group case: the loss forH≤G is the
minimum number of generators ofG/H [3, Theorem 2.3]. This replaced the original order-based
prediction by a rank bound; alreadyCp2 has loss one rather than two. The sharp elementary abelian
examples use the partition-complex work of Arone, Dwyer, Lesh, and Mahowald [3, Theorems 2.2
and 4.1]. Balderrama and Kuhn later gave an elementary proof of the abelian chromatic Smith
theorem, including target height zero [1, Theorem 1.1 and Section 3.2].
For nonabelian groups, a general lower bound is the rank of the largest elementary abelian
quotient of G in which H maps to the identity. This can be smaller than the cyclic-chain
upper bound. Kuhn and Lloyd proved that chromatic acyclicity implications are equivalent to
corresponding inequalities between Morava homology dimensions, allowing representation-theoretic
constructions to give stronger lower bounds [15, Theorem 2.2]. They obtained equality withℓ
for every subgroup pair inside a central extensionC2→G→E with E elementary abelian [15,
Theorem 2.13]. In particular, a noncentral subgroup of order two inD8 has loss two, although
the elementary-quotient lower bound is one. Their discussion of the general problem expresses
“cautious hope” thatrn(G,H ) = r+(G,H ) = ℓ(G,H ), and separately asks whether the loss is
independent of height [15, Section 2.2]. Theorem 1.1 establishes the proposed equality, and hence
height independence, for all finitep-groups.
The remaining obstacle is sharpness for an arbitrary embedded subgroup. The Hopkins–Kuhn–
Ravenel characters are indexed by maps fromZn
p, whose images are abelian [10, Theorem C].
Thus they cannot directly supply characters with full nonabelian image. General character rings
with other source groups already appear in Kuhn’s framework [13, Section 2]. Here we select a
profinite source from the finite groups detected by telescopic cochains, derive constraints on all its
open subgroups by relative integration, and use those constraints to prove the required fixed-point
vanishing estimates.
Combined with the conjugacy andp-subnormal reductions of Balmer–Sanders, the theorem
determines the remaining finite-height prime inclusions for arbitrary finite groups [2, Proposi-
tion 6.11]; Corollary 7.5 gives the precise test. Their description of the infinite-height inclusions and
of Thomason subsets then determines the thick tensor ideals [2, Corollaries 8.19, 10.5, and 10.6].
The sharpness witnesses in this paper are obtained by separation of primes.
