# r02 causal: identifiability closure must retain nuisance parameters and experimental semantics

Status (2026-10-06): two independently reconstructed continuum obstructions to arXiv:2604.09309v1; exact arithmetic replay completed. A sound moment-based repair is proved below, but it is standard IV/auxiliary-variable reasoning rather than a new foundational breakthrough. A credible next lead is intervention allocation for recursively corrected, overidentified causal moments. No claim of full algorithm completeness or implementation audit.

## Exact interface and verified sources

Given a known acyclic mixed graph, observational covariance Σ, unknown nonzero directed coefficients and positive-definite error covariance Ω with prescribed bidirected zero pattern, identify a particular directed coefficient as a rational function of the permitted data. Optional intervention data must specify the family of actual do-distributions, rather than being converted to known direct coefficients by fiat. Gaussian errors are allowed; equal mean/covariance then gives equal full observational laws.

The in-window seed is Ding and Zhang, *Iterative Identification Closure: Amplifying Causal Identifiability in Linear SEMs*, arXiv 2604.09309v1, 10 April 2026. PDF p4 Definition 4.2/Theorem 4.3 and p12 Appendix A.1 are decisive. The original HTC prerequisite is Foygel, Draisma and Drton, Annals of Statistics 2012, arXiv 1107.5552v2: Definition 4, Theorem 1, proof in Section 9, and Algorithm 1. Relevant prior art is Chen/Pearl/Bareinboim 1511.02995v1 (2015); Chen/Kumor/Bareinboim ICML 2017, sections 3–4, Algorithm 1; Kumor/Cinelli/Bareinboim ICML 2020, Algorithm 2 (ACID). Each already iterates identification using previously known effects. Therefore the seed's broad claim that iterative feedback is absent from all earlier methods is contradicted by primary literature.

A constructive in-window prerequisite is Mareis, Sturma and Drton, *Semiparametric Inference for Half-Trek Estimators in Linear Structural Equation Models*, 2606.26931v2 (v1 25 June, v2 12 August 2026): Lemma 6, Theorem 7, Proposition 10 and Remark 9. It explicitly retains uncertainty from earlier residualization and distinguishes HTC influence functions from efficient influence functions.

## Obstruction 1: the target coefficient has an open covariance fiber

Take graph A→X→Y with A↔X and A↔Y; no X↔Y. Structural equations:

A=eA; X=aA+eX; Y=bX+eY.

Under paper Definition 4.2, target Y has K=∅, R={X}, W={X}. The trivial half-trek X→X has no sided intersection. X is neither a descendant nor sibling of Y. Thus the rule accepts b. This is not merely a disputed interpretation of trivial paths: Theorem B.8(a), PDF p15, explicitly uses the same trivial-parent witness.

For any fixed positive-definite observational Σ, choose a′ near a and set

b′=(ΣXY−a′ΣAY)/(ΣXX−a′ΣAX),

Ω′=(I−B′)Σ(I−B′)ᵀ,

where B′ has entries a′ on A→X and b′ on X→Y. Direct expansion gives Ω′XY=0. Other off-diagonal entries are unrestricted by the graph. Positive definiteness follows by invertible congruence, and all required nonzeros persist locally. The reconstructed SEM has exactly the same Σ. Its derivative is

db′/da′=(ΣXYΣAX−ΣAYΣXX)/(ΣXX−a′ΣAX)².

The numerator is not identically zero; hence b changes along the fiber for generic parameters. This is a generic nonidentification proof, strengthened by exact finite fixtures, not inferred from them.

Exact fixture:

a=1/2, b=7/10,
Ω=[[1,1/5,3/10],[1/5,1,0],[3/10,0,1]],
Σ=[[1,7/10,79/100],[7/10,29/20,233/200],[79/100,233/200,3841/2000]].

At a′=3/5, b′=691/1030 and
Ω′=[[1,1/10,33/103],[1/10,97/100,0],[33/103,0,42859/42436]].

Both Ω and Ω′ have leading principal minors 1,24/25,87/100. The same graph, same nonzero support and same Gaussian law therefore give different target coefficients.

To cover that branch, append an independent Z→Y with any known nonzero c; replace Y by Y−cZ. The same fiber remains, K={Z}, R={X}, W={X}, and c is identified by ΣZY/ΣZZ. ## What fails in the proof and experimental validation

Appendix A.1 proves full rank of the derivative in b directions with nuisance parameters held fixed. In this example that rank is 1. The actual covariance map has seven parameters and six entries: its full Jacobian rank is 6, and removing the b column leaves rank 6. Nuisance changes can therefore cancel changes in b. An exact kernel tangent at the fixture, with parameters ordered (a,b,ωAA,ωXX,ωYY,ωAX,ωAY), is

(1,−3/11,0,−2/5,9/110,−1,21/110).

`python3 work/r02_causal/jacobian_check.py` checks this using exact forward differentiation and rational elimination. It produces `results/r02_causal/jacobian_check.json`.

The correct local scalar test is rank(J)>rank(J without target column), not nonzero target-column rank. Even that establishes only local identification at regular points; a finite-to-one fiber can still have distinct globally compatible coefficients. Generic local identifiability also does not imply rational invertibility of an arbitrary algebraic map (t↦t² suffices as the general obstruction).

IIC Appendix C.4, PDF p18, describes its numerical ground truth using the map Bji↦Σ and target/node rank, not the entire nuisance-sensitive map. The reported 100% precision therefore is not persuasive evidence of global identification. We did not obtain the released implementation: the anonymous repository is inaccessible through web and direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. No claim is made about actual code behavior or graph restrictions inside that code.

The missing condition corresponds to an omission from the real HTC: if a witness lies in htr(Y), earlier incoming coefficients for that witness must be identified and its structural residual used. Here Y↔A→X makes X reachable from Y by a half-trek. Excluding only descendants does not repair this.

## Obstruction 2: a perfect intervention does not supply every outgoing direct effect

The paper's section4.1 lists every v→child edge as an intervention seed. Consider X exogenous and independent of correlated eM,eY:

M=aX+eM; Y=cX+bM+eY,

with graph X→M→Y, X→Y, M↔Y. Set

b′=b+δ; c′=c−aδ; eY′=eY−δeM.

Then Y=c′X+b′M+eY′ equals its original value exactly under observation and under every fixed or stochastic intervention on X. For every do(X=x), (M,Y) is jointly Gaussian with mean slopes (a,c+ab), covariance MY=b Var(eM)+Cov(eM,eY), and Y variance b²Var(eM)+2bCov(eM,eY)+Var(eY). All are invariant under the transformation. Positive-definite error covariance and nonzero edge/bidirected support persist for small δ, but direct coefficient c changes. Thus even complete knowledge of all do(X=x) laws does not identify every outgoing direct effect.

Replay: `python3 work/r02_causal/intervention_obstruction.py`; output `results/r02_causal/intervention_obstruction.json`. This uses four rational fixtures with identical joint response slopes/covariance and nonzero confounding. The continuum derivation, not the four checks, establishes the obstruction.

The practical consequence is an experiment-to-parameter gap: a planned randomized source intervention may supply a total-effect constraint rather than a directed-edge seed. Treating these interfaces interchangeably can make every downstream propagated coefficient unjustified.

## Sound replacement theorem (independent derivation; standard prior art)

Let the known coefficients into target i be κ, unknown ones β, and Y*=Xi−κᵀXK=βᵀXR+ei. Suppose an instrument vector Z=T(Σ,I)X is legally computable from permitted data and identified quantities, has E[Z ei]=0 for every model in the admissible class, and A=E[Z XRᵀ] is square and generically nonsingular. Then

β=A⁻¹E[Z Y*].

All right-side quantities are observable/identified rational functions, so this proves rational identification globally on the denominator-nonzero set. No Jacobian-to-global step is needed. A conservative raw-witness condition for acyclic graphs is Anc(W)∩(sib(i)∪{i})=∅; half-trek/determinant certificates may then prove relevance. Known source coefficients allow residualized instruments and recover stronger HTC/AV rules. Retaining valid certificates while adding knowledge gives sound iterative closure by induction and termination after at most |D| successful edge additions. This theorem is a reconstruction of ordinary IV/AV principles, not a novelty claim.

With coefficient estimates, define m=E[Z(Xi−κᵀXK−βᵀXR)]. If Z=Xw−γᵀXPw is itself residualized, first-order corrected moment influence is

Rw=Zw ei−E[XPw ei]ᵀφγ−E[Zw XKᵀ]φκ.

The two correction terms follow by differentiating the moment in γ and κ. They are essential when seeds/instruments are estimated. For m≥r certified moments, A=E[Z XRᵀ], C=E[RRᵀ] positive definite, the minimum-variance linear influence function subject to LA=I is

φβ=(AᵀC⁻¹A)⁻¹AᵀC⁻¹R.

Proof: set L*=that expression. Every feasible L=L*+D has DA=0, and L*CDᵀ=(AᵀC⁻¹A)⁻¹AᵀDᵀ=0. Therefore LCLᵀ−L*CL*ᵀ=DCDᵀ≽0. This is optimal GMM/GLS within the certified moment span, not generally the efficient estimator in the full SEM model.

For two independent external source instruments with strengths a,c and unit target error variance, the target asymptotic variance is 1/(a²+c²), versus 1/a² or 1/c² for a single witness. This supplies a concrete reason to optimize information rather than just edge count. The June/August 2026 HTC inference paper's Remark9 already highlights precisely this issue, so the elementary example is not new.

## Stronger research direction and falsifiable next step

Exact question: for a supplied mixed graph, specified legal intervention families, pilot moment estimates, total experimental budget and costs, can one efficiently select interventions and jointly estimate their constraints to minimize the corrected asymptotic variance of designated downstream effects? Required interface retains nuisance parameters, intervention total-effect constraints, seed-estimation covariance, validity certificates, and weakest singular-value margin.

Missing theorem: a graph-specific characterization of when the span of all legally generated HTC/AV moment influences attains the full SEM semiparametric efficiency bound, plus a costed intervention algorithm under that characterization. A single witness choice is insufficient; a generic edge-count gain is insufficient. We have not proved this characterization, polynomial optimal design, or new universal identifiability criterion.

A tractable first experiment is to enumerate small mixed graphs and valid auxiliary instruments, compute nuisance-aware identification certificates and all corrected moment covariance matrices, then compare square-witness HTC, overidentified GMM, and complete constrained likelihood in Gaussian fixtures. Find a smallest graph where certified GMM still misses the full covariance-model information bound. A positive equality or an exact separating example changes the research direction; mere random precision tests do not.

Alternatives retained: (1) observational CRL with partial latent sharing (2605.19135v1, May18) has structural sparsity/injective mixing assumptions requiring separate proof audit; (2) POSCM 2605.03268v2 (July15) kernel identification assumes experimental adjacency/context/value readout plus coverage, so acquisition of those readouts remains load-bearing; (3) March26 CRL paper 2603.25796v1 lies before the requested starting window but is a useful prerequisite: logarithmic unknown multi-node interventions use exact zero clamps, two noise-variance regimes with distinct ratios, and strongly separating target patterns. For any positive leakage, all covariance ranges can become full and its literal intersection-of-column-spaces statistic loses the target signature; this is an algorithm-interface obstruction, not a general identifiability impossibility. These alternatives have excerpt-level reading only.

Seven substantive primary excerpts read, five recent submissions/revisions plus two older prerequisites.
