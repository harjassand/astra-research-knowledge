The source's more explicit displayed numerical example uses \(m=9,\varepsilon=10^{-6}\); it also states the fixed-\(m\) directional Schur-loss limit \(5m^2/108\). ## Strong \(L^4\) coupling and compatibility

There is an explicit coupling that verifies the strong \(L^4\) claim. Let \(U\) be uniform on \([0,1]\). Assign, in order, intervals of lengths \((1-p)/2,(1-p)/2,2p/3,p/3\) to \(+B_e,-B_e,T,-2T\). For every fixed \(U<1/2\), the coupled value is eventually \(+B_e\); for every fixed \(U>1/2\), it is eventually \(-B_e\), except on a shrinking rare interval. Thus \(X_e(U)\to X_0(U)=\operatorname{sgn}(1/2-U)R\) almost everywhere. All supports are uniformly bounded (for, e.g., \(0<e<1/10\)), so dominated convergence gives \(\mathbb E|X_e-X_0|^4\to0\). This is strong \(L^4\) convergence of random matrices; it does not assert gradient compatibility.

The plane containing the support has
\[
\det(sB_e+tT)=6(t^2-e^2s^2),
\]
so its only rank-one lines are \(R_+=B_e+eT\) and \(R_-=B_e-eT\). The pairwise determinants of distinct support differences are \(-24e^2\), \(6(1-e^2)\) (twice), \(6(4-e^2)\) (twice), and \(54\). Hence no two of the four atoms have rank-one difference when \(0<e<1/10\). The stronger plane-compatibility reason is the source's Section 8 Fourier argument: any periodic gradient confined to this plane splits into independent one-dimensional phases along the two rank-one lines. In rank-one coordinates,
\[
B_e\mapsto(1/2,1/2),\quad -B_e\mapsto(-1/2,-1/2),\quad
T\mapsto(1/(2e),-1/(2e)),\quad -2T\mapsto(-1/e,1/e).
\]
For \(e<1/10\), both marginals have four distinct values, whereas a product law with both marginals nonconstant has at least sixteen support pairs. This four-atom law therefore cannot be the distribution of such a periodic plane gradient. Pairwise non-rank-one differences alone would not prove this; the plane's two-line Fourier compatibility is the needed argument.

## Acoustic flatness and exact factor check

The normalized directions
\[
\widehat R_\pm=\frac{B_e\pm eT}{\sqrt D},\qquad
D=|B_e|^2+e^2|T|^2=1+13e^2+36e^4,
\]
are rank one and unit. Their squared sine angle is
\[
s_e^2=1-(\widehat R_+\!:\widehat R_-)^2
=\frac{52e^2(1+36e^4)}{D^2}.
\]
Since \(D^2G(I)[B_e\pm eT,B_e\pm eT]=30e^4\), homogeneity gives
\[
\frac{I^T\{D^2G(\widehat R_+)+D^2G(\widehat R_-)\}I}{|I|^2}
=\frac{30e^4}{D}.
\]
With the exact source definition
\[
\mu_G(s)=\min\lambda_{\min}(D^2G(R)+D^2G(S))
\]
over unit rank-one \(R,S\) with \(1-(R:S)^2\ge s^2\), this admissible pair yields
\[
0<\mu_G(s_e)\le\frac{30e^4}{D},\qquad
\frac{30e^4/D}{s_e^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore no fixed constant \(c>0\) can satisfy \(\mu_G(s)\ge cs^2\) for every sufficiently small \(s\). This is consistent with the source's compactness-only assertion \(\mu_G(s)>0\); it adds a specific upper-bound witness and does not claim a complete asymptotic formula for \(\mu_G\).
