# Small-ball carrier components in the random replacement

7 October 2026, rounding_spectral. This proves a geometric discriminator
for the actual folded construction. It does not prove the relative
filling anchor, either CSS sector of TQO-2, or perturbation stability.

## Exact geometric lemma

Let an abstract triangular two-complex be mapped simplexwise affinely
into Euclidean space. Different abstract cells may overlap geometrically
without identification. Assume every triangle side has length at most L,
every triangle has width at least w>0, and every abstract edge has length
at least l>0. Width means the minimum distance from a vertex to its
opposite closed side. For an ambient ball B(x,s), set

    R = (1+2L/w)s.                                      (1)

Build an incidence graph whose nodes are triangles meeting B. Join two
nodes only when an identified common abstract edge or vertex has image
meeting B. If 2R<l, every connected component lies in one of:

* a single triangle;
* the triangles incident to one abstract edge;
* the triangles incident to one abstract vertex v with |I(v)-x|<=R.

Here a star is an incidence statement, not a claim that the physical
ball has a single embedded sheet. Arbitrarily many unrelated stars may
meet the same ambient ball.

Proof. First all triangle angles obey sin(theta)>=w/L. To cover obtuse
triangles as well as acute ones, choose the longest side a. The opposite
perpendicular foot is on that closed side, so its altitude is at least
w. Twice the area is at least aw. Every other line-altitude is at least
that altitude, since its side is no longer than a. Expressing the
altitude as the product of a neighboring side and sin(theta) proves
the bound.

If the ball meets two different sides sharing vertex v, choose points
y,z on those two sides in B. Their distance is at most 2s. The distance
from y to the line of the second side is |y-v|sin(theta), and hence
|y-v|<=2sL/w. Therefore |v-x|<=s+2sL/w=R. Since adjacent vertices are
at distance at least l>2R, at most one vertex of a triangle is within R
of x.

If a component has an adjacency through a common vertex in B, all its
incident triangles contain that near vertex. Otherwise consider a
triangle with incident graph edges labeled by two distinct abstract
sides. These sides share a near vertex v. An adjacent triangle across
either side contains v. If that triangle continues through a side not
containing v, the new pair of sides shares the other endpoint u of the
old side, placing u within R too. This contradicts |u-v|>=l>2R.
Induction along a graph path therefore keeps every triangle incident to
v. The same argument covers a later adjacency through a common vertex.
If no triangle switches between distinct sides and no vertex adjacency
occurs, every edge traversal has the same abstract edge label, so the
component is contained in its edge star. Isolated triangles give the
first case. This proves the classification.

The proof makes no use of a separation distance between nonincident
cells. Accidental geometric crossings create no graph edge.

## Application to the actual source geometry

The source is Balasubramanian--Davydova--Lin, arXiv2605.10943v1,
paper.tex lines1353--1397,1447--1498,2181--2197. Formal diagonals
triangulate each parent square for its piecewise-linear realization.
The source's displayed perturbed-side and width bounds give
L<=ell, w>=gamma lambda and l>=(alpha2-1)lambda. There is a locality
bookkeeping caveat: a Tanner-edge bound1 allows an auxiliary square
diagonal of length2. The source calls the triangulated map (2t,1)-embedded.
Rather than depend on that extra assertion, the following safe bounds
are independently available for the same actual perturbed positions:

    L <= (2alpha+2)lambda,
    l >= (alpha2-2)lambda,
    w >= [alpha(1-cos(alpha2/alpha))-2]lambda.            (2a)

The two endpoints of any auxiliary side are at most two Tanner edges
apart before scaling. The triangle inequality, the source's separated
caps and the radius-alpha-lambda sphere prove the first two bounds.
For a vertex of a triangle and any convex combination of the opposite
vertices, the old-position contribution has length at most2lambda.
Projection of the cap contribution on the vertex's radius vector is at
least alpha lambda[1-cos(alpha2/alpha)], proving the width bound. Thus
no unsupplied extra bound on an auxiliary diagonal is needed here.

At alpha=1000, alpha2=139/2, the Taylor inequality
1-cos(x)>=x^2/2-x^4/24 with x=139/2000 gives

    1000[x^2/2-x^4/24]-2 > 2/5.

Consequently L<=2002lambda, l>=(135/2)lambda and w>=(2/5)lambda
are conservative actual-position bounds. This bookkeeping does not
refute the source's construction or change its geometric realization.

For a fine error supported on qubits in B(x,r), connect supported qubits
when they share a syndrome check. Include that check and the corresponding
fine Tanner edges. Every such edge is contained in B(x,r+1), because
its length is at most1 and its qubit endpoint is in B(x,r). Each edge
has an intrinsic parent-square carrier. Splitting its carrier along the
formal diagonal produces a path through the preceding triangle graph
with s=r+1. At transitions between different parent squares, gluing is
only across their identified parent edges/corners, exactly as in the
source's quotient definition. Collapsed doubling cells stay in the same
parent carrier. Thus a connected fine interaction component is contained
in a parent triangle/edge/vertex carrier star when

    2(1+2L/w)(r+1) < l.                               (2b)

In particular, the independently safe bounds imply the sufficient
inequality 2*10011*(r+1)<(135/2)lambda.

This conclusion is robust to unrelated sheets crossing within B.
Its graph is an over-approximation: two fine pieces in one parent
triangle can still be intrinsically disconnected. It does not identify
those pieces or introduce checks across a crossing.

At the recurrence anchor r=a lambda/(lambda-1)+1, with
a=alpha+2ell/lambda+2, the source alpha=1000 and enormous lambda give
a bounded R<6e7 versus parent edge lengths of order1e26. For example,
lambda>=10^24 and the source's least-odd-integer choice
ell<=(2001lambda)+2 imply r+1<5007; (2b) then follows. The source's
displayed chosen lambda approximately5.4e24 is more than enough. The
asymptotic adjective "small" has been replaced by an explicit inequality.

## What the lemma does and does not buy

An interaction component of a zero-syndrome error is itself zero-syndrome:
otherwise a check with a nonzero contribution would also connect that
component to another contributing component. But a component of a global
stabilizer need not itself be a global stabilizer merely because it is
closed; separate homologous logical pieces can multiply to a boundary.
Consequently carrier classification is not a local-generation proof.

The needed boundary-relative calculation is as follows. For sector X,
let V_A be the span of fine qubits in the small region A and G_B the span
of coefficients of original X checks whose whole support is in B.
It must show

    im(D0) intersect V_A  subseteq  D0 G_B.              (3)

For sector Z it must show

    im((D1)^T) intersect V_A  subseteq  (D1)^T G_B.     (4)

The actual star contains doubled sheets, cylindrical seams and the
shifted local complexes C_q; its correct boundary conditions are imposed
by all gluing/restriction maps to the complement. Replacing it by a free
disk or computing unrestricted H1 of a star is insufficient. Even the
connected parent links used in the paper prove only its full-block H1
statement, unless the relative images in (3)--(4) are controlled.

A promising weaker cochain statement is that all unaffected components
of the doubled-cone one-skeleton that meet outside an enlarged B_R lie
in one component. A small disk can cut off an apex cap in a narrow
wedge, so connectedness outside the original B_r is false. If the weaker
statement is proved for the actual fine mesh, a local X potential can
be set to zero on the unique outside component, localizing a filling.
The dual chain calculation is separate. Neither statement is adopted in
this note; both require actual mesh and seam checks.

## Costs, prior and next falsifiable step

This is an exact Euclidean/incidence proof, not a finite simulation or a
new stability theorem. The paper's source itself supplies the bounded
geometry; our discriminator records how it handles folded overlaps.
Priority for this elementary carrier lemma has not been established.
Constructing the actual relative patch matrices costs their O(ell^2)
size (and a naive elimination O(ell^6)); ell is astronomical in the
random construction. Bounded density does not make that work free.

Next: prove the compact-potential statement on actual doubled parent
stars, or expose a genuine relative boundary requiring a remote check.
An arbitrary synthetic star with a missing check would refute a general
implication, not establish a failure in this recursively reachable code.
