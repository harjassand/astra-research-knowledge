# Primary source scan: AC-driven sequential electroreduction

## Verdict

The strongest fully inspectable 2025 lead is Sreekumar, Beeler, Mal, White, and Luo, “Origin of Selectivity in Alternating Current-Enabled Partial Reduction of (Hetero)Arenes,” *JACS* 147 (2025), 26917–26925, DOI [10.1021/jacs.5c08873](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c08873). This is a driven, open, **sequential** reaction network: substrate 1 is reduced to desired intermediate 2, which can be reduced further to overreduction product 3 or chemically decomposed. It differs materially from c9’s parallel SET/BET product-commitment gate. The 2025 paper supplies the mechanism, its finite-element implementation, and its ΔE_FOW heuristic; those are source results, not new claims here.

The exploitable follow-up is an exact stoichiometric extent balance plus a phase-resolved, charge-aware test of whether one frequency-independent kinetic model predicts product formation and loss. This is not a nontrivial catalytic-cycle invariant: the modeled sequence is irreversible and has no closed catalytic loop. The balance is ordinary stoichiometry, but measuring every term would expose hidden electron sinks and test whether the proposed frequency mechanism actually accounts for selectivity.

## Primary evidence and precise locators

The full primary paper is hosted by the authors at [University of Utah](https://luo.chem.utah.edu/_resources/documents/sree_jacs_2025.pdf); the [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12579538/) and [ACS article page](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jacs.5c08873) provide the primary record. The [ACS Figshare record for the Supporting Information](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Origin_of_Selectivity_in_Alternating_Current-Enabled_Partial_Reduction_of_Hetero_Arenes_A_Case_Study_of_Two_Consecutive_Irreversible_Electrochemical_Steps/29608327) has SI DOI `10.1021/jacs.5c08873.s001` and PDF `ja5c08873_si_001.pdf`.

- **Fig. 1b–e and Results, “Understanding the Poor Performance under DC Electrolysis” (main paper pp. 2–3):** for methyl thiophene-2-carboxylate 1, the paper reports no detectable 2 and about 14% recovered 1 under undivided DC, versus 60% 2 under 10-Hz AC at 3.6 V. Fig. 1d and SI §5 estimate methoxide after 10 minutes at about 0.53 mM under AC and at least 1.6 mM under DC. Fig. 1e and SI §6 support a second product-loss path: after static exposure to 1.6 mM TBAOMe, 2 is fully decomposed within 1 hour; 1 is about 86% recovered at 1 hour and 84% at 3 hours.
- **Fig. 2 and Results, “Fast-Scan Voltammetric Study” (main pp. 3–4; SI §§3–4, Figs. S6–S8):** microelectrode CVs at 20–100 V/s support faster initial reduction of 1 than reduction of 2 (`k1 > k2`). The 1 peak current scales with the square root of scan rate; the 2 reduction is described as electron-transfer-kinetics limited. Fig. 2e plots `i1/i2` at −2.8 V against scan rate and equivalent frequency, with selectivity proxy increasing over about 3–14 Hz. SI §4 maps 10 Hz to approximately 72 V/s by equating one AC cycle to one CV cycle; the authors explicitly call this an approximation.
- **Eqs. (1)–(2), Fig. 3, and SI §8 (main pp. 4–5; SI pp. 17–20):** the COMSOL model simplifies reduction to `1 + 2e → 2` followed by `2 + 2e → 3`, with 3 hypothetical. The model has a 1-D diffusion domain from bulk at x = 5 mm to the electrode, Tafel fluxes, and square-wave potential. Its fitted `k0` and `E0` values are explicitly phenomenological: the source says they are not independently identified and can compensate for each other. Fig. 3d–h and SI Fig. S13 show near-electrode depletion/replenishment and frequency-specific simulated concentrations.
- **Fig. 4 and Conclusions (main pp. 5–6):** source interpretation combines lower methoxide accumulation with frequency-modulated transport, which samples the initial kinetically controlled part of a cathodic pulse. Fig. 4c shows semiquantitative agreement over 1–10 Hz; experiment falls at higher f while the model continues to improve. The paper attributes the high-frequency shortfall to charging/discharging voltage loss; SI Eq. S1 and Fig. S5 estimate an RC constant of 9 ms and effective cell voltage dropping rapidly above 20 Hz.
- **Eqs. (5)–(6), Fig. 5a–f, and Conclusion (main pp. 6–7):** equal-concentration foot-of-wave CVs give the paper’s relation `ΔE_FOW = (RT/αF) ln(k2/k1) ≈ 120 mV log10(k2/k1)` under its setup. The authors infer `k2/k1 < 0.2` when `|ΔE_FOW| > 80 mV`, and predict >30% intermediate-product yield below 100 Hz. Fig. 5f tests phthalimide partial reduction: 42% 2′ under AC at 10 Hz and 50 mA amplitude for 3 h; DC gives no desired product after full substrate conversion at 20 mA for 3.5 h. The paper presents the 80-mV threshold as a “rule of thumb,” not a theorem.

## SI operating details and controls

SI §2.1 uses 0.50 mmol 1, 1.5 mmol iPr₂NH, 0.264 mmol Et₄N·BF₄ in 4.0 mL MeOH, argon, room-temperature stirring, two glassy-carbon plates with about 2 cm² electroactive area and roughly a 1-mm electrode gap. It applies a square-wave 3.6-V output at 1–50 Hz for a fixed 3 h, followed by evaporation, ether dilution, brine wash, drying, filtration, concentration, and ¹H NMR yield measurement against CH₃NO₂. This general procedure reports 72% NMR yield of 2; the main-text Fig. 1b reports 60% for its plotted AC comparison. The relationship between those reported yield values is not resolved in the accessible text and should not be silently merged.

The main 1→2 runs are fixed-voltage/fixed-time comparisons; the paper does not report integrated current, cathodic Faradaic charge, or a conversion-matched frequency series. The SI explicitly says the cell waveform alternates between −3.6 and +3.6 V every 50 ms at 10 Hz, corresponding approximately to electrode potentials −2.8 to +0.8 V. Although it measures solution resistance and estimates effective voltage, it does not close the charge or electrical-energy budget. The 2′ test reports current amplitudes and durations, but the AC and DC arms differ (50 mA × 3 h versus 20 mA × 3.5 h) and are not charge matched.

There **is** a partial product-challenge control already: SI §6 incubates authentic 1 and 2 in 1.6 mM TBAOMe without an applied waveform. Thus a proposed follow-up must not claim that base stability was never tested. What remains untested is an AC/DC replay or phase-resolved challenge of authentic 2 at matched product loading and measured interfacial potential, with overreduction and chemical-loss products quantified separately. SI §5 adds bromothymol blue after 10 min and measures bulk methoxide; it does not provide a phase-resolved methoxide history. No inspected source reports the requested matched-conversion/matched-charge comparison.

## Exact hidden identity and decisive test

For the paper’s simplified irreversible model, let `ξ1` and `ξ2` be integrated molar extents of 1→2 and 2→3, respectively. In the absence of other sinks, `n1=n10−ξ1`, `n2=ξ1−ξ2`, and `n3=ξ2`, hence

```text
n1 + n2 + n3 = n10
Qseq/(2F) = ξ1 + ξ2 = n2 + 2 n3
```

The second equality assumes each step transfers two electrons and `Qseq` excludes capacitive current, methanol reduction, hydrogen evolution, and other faradaic side reactions. If base-mediated destruction removes `D` mol of 2-derived material, the corresponding closed balance is `n1+n2+n3+D=n10` and `Qseq/(2F)=n2+D+2n3`. This is a model accounting identity, not a new theorem or cycle invariant. The source’s overreduction product 3 is hypothetical and its actual product network is not fully identified, so `D` and all carbon-containing products must be measured before using the balance experimentally.

An equivalent kinetic identity in the lumped, well-mixed special case makes the proposed “kinetic window” precise. If `a(t)` and `b(t)` are measured pseudo-first-order hazards for 1→2 and 2→3, then

```text
S′ = −a(t)S;   P′ = a(t)S − b(t)P
P(t) = ∫₀ᵗ a(s)S(s) exp(−∫ₛᵗ b(u)du) ds.
```

With a measured first-order chemical-loss hazard `d(t)`, add it to the survival exponent. This identity separates the flux creating intermediate from its survival against later electrochemical and chemical loss. It is only a lumped reduction of the paper’s spatial model; it does not replace the COMSOL diffusion calculation.

**Experiment:** repeat the named 1→2 reaction at the source’s geometry and chemistry while recording the true electrode potential and current waveform. Run a frequency series at the original fixed 3 h and additional pairs matched on substrate conversion and on cathodic Faradaic charge. Take rapid aliquots at defined AC phases and quantify 1, 2, tetrahydrothiophene-2-carboxylate, and major decomposition products by calibrated quantitative NMR/LC/GC-MS. Measure methoxide over time. Separately replay the measured waveforms on authentic 2 at matched 2 loading, bulk methoxide, and interfacial potential to quantify electroreduction versus base loss. Fit transport, `a(t)`, `b(t)`, and base-loss hazards from independent measurements, then predict held-out frequencies without refitting reaction-specific constants.

**Falsifier:** preregister analytical/calibration uncertainty and compare the held-out phase-resolved product trajectories with the independently parameterized model. The proposed mechanism fails as a sufficient explanation if (i) the AC-relevant interfacial measurements do not establish `k1>k2`, or (ii) the measured product-2 trajectory, overreduction flux, or material/charge balance falls outside the model’s propagated 95% prediction interval at matched charge/conversion, or (iii) a frequency-dependent product split remains after the measured substrate flux, interfacial waveform, and methoxide exposure are held fixed. A failed balance would identify an omitted sink/pathway; it would not by itself rule out every possible AC effect.

## Prior-work overlap screen

- **N76 factorial event-bound:** closest network-control item in the local Astra packet, but its supplied result is an event-chain bound for weakly reversible mass-action networks and does not establish physical-time selectivity. This source’s model is an externally forced irreversible sequence with spatial diffusion and competing side current. N76 does not supply the kinetic-window or selectivity result here.
- **N58 kinetics:** local prior concerns PDE kinetic regularization/minimal gains, not a driven product network or electrolysis waveform; no direct preemption found.
- **Dynamic-catalysis no-pumping/cycle-space reports:** the RHJ/no-pumping result in the prior packet concerns closed cyclic detailed-balanced stochastic pumps under restricted driving. This paper models irreversible electrochemical steps under an imposed voltage, chemostatted reagents, diffusion, and methoxide loss; it is outside that no-pumping premise and does not introduce a new cycle-space theorem.
- **c5 dynamic chemistry:** inspected c5 prior covers Cu dynamic activation, Cu hydrogenation routes, and intervention/topology work. Those mechanisms do not cover this sequential thiophene electroreduction. No cycle invariant is shared because the present model has no catalytic surface loop.
- **c9 BET/selectivity setpoint:** c9’s source-known formula partitions two parallel photoredox channels through capture, commitment, and BET hazards. Here selectivity is intermediate survival in a sequential irreversible network with mass transfer and a chemical-loss branch. Treating these as the same two-channel model would erase the relevant mechanism.
- **Underlying chemistry prior:** the authors cite Baran et al.’s 2021 rapid-alternating-polarity method and 2022 (hetero)arene partial-reduction paper ([2021 JACS DOI 10.1021/jacs.1c06572](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.1c06572); [2022 JACS DOI 10.1021/jacs.2c02102](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c02102)). Thus the 2025 primary contribution is a mechanistic/kinetic analysis and generalized proxy, not discovery of AC-enabled arene partial reduction.
