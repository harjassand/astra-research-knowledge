# Family 115 transfer: fixed-margin randomization for gene-by-cell counts

## Verdict

Family 115 supplies a useful combinatorial engine for conditional randomization on a fixed-margin table, but the standard statistical bridge needs a correction: **independent Poisson counts conditioned on both margins are generally not uniform over tables**. They have Fisher's factorial-weighted conditional law. Uniformity follows exactly from independent geometric counts with multiplicatively separable odds, or from a Poisson model only in special fibers where the product of cell factorials is constant (notably binary tables).

Thus a uniform fixed-margin table is a defensible *microcanonical randomization null* if the scientific null says every feasible allocation with the observed gene totals, cell totals, and structural support is equally likely. It is not automatically the null implied by Poisson independence, and it does not by itself encode biological heterogeneity, donor structure, overdispersion, or capture artifacts.

## Table interface and algorithmic claim

Let G be the set of genes, C the set of cells, and E a subset of G × C of allowed gene-cell positions. With integer bounds ell_gc ≤ x_gc ≤ u_gc and margins r_g,s_c, the feasible fiber is

\[
\mathcal F(r,s,\ell,u)=\left\{x\in\mathbb Z^{G\times C}:\;\sum_c x_{gc}=r_g,\;\sum_g x_{gc}=s_c,\;\ell_{gc}\le x_{gc}\le u_{gc}\right\},
\]

with forbidden positions represented by u_gc = 0. All tables are counted once. A lower-bound shift z_gc = x_gc - ell_gc reduces this to zero lower bounds, residual margins, and capacities u_gc - ell_gc. Empty fibers must be detected; equal margin totals alone do not guarantee feasibility when caps or structural zeros are present.

The same fiber is an integer-flow fiber on the bipartite graph: each gene is a supply vertex, each cell a demand vertex, and an edge from g to c carries x_gc between ell_gc and u_gc. This is the precise route by which the bounded counting result supports a near-uniform sampler for capped or structural-zero tables.

As stated in the pinned source:

- For uncapped complete-support tables, the exact sampler terminates almost surely and has expected bit complexity polynomial in |G|, |C|, and log(N+1), where N is the common total. Its separate 2^-k-TV sampler has worst-case bit cost polynomial in |G|, |C|, log(N+1), and k. The proof includes a rare exhaustive correction branch; exactness is an expected-time guarantee, not a worst-case time guarantee.
- For arbitrary binary-encoded caps, the FPRAS estimates the size of F to relative error epsilon with failure at most delta, with all-execution bit cost polynomial in total input length L, epsilon^-1, and log(delta^-1). It returns zero on every execution if the fiber is empty. Zero caps are permitted.
- The bounded-table self-reduction gives a sampler within TV tau of uniform, reports infeasibility exactly, and has all-execution bit cost polynomial in L and tau^-1. This is approximate sampling; it is not the exact unbounded-table sampler.

The stated polynomials have deliberately large/unspecified exponents. No practical runtime or memory bound follows from “polynomial,” and no source implementation was benchmarked here.

## Exact conditional-law calculation

Let X_gc be independent Poisson variables with means lambda_gc > 0, restricted to allowed positions and conditioned on X belonging to F. For every feasible table x,

\[
\Pr(X=x\mid X\in\mathcal F)
=\frac{\prod_{(g,c)\in E}\lambda_{gc}^{x_{gc}}/x_{gc}!}
 {\sum_{z\in\mathcal F}\prod_{(g,c)\in E}\lambda_{gc}^{z_{gc}}/z_{gc}!}.
\]

If the Poisson mean has the log-linear independence form lambda_gc = a_g b_c, then fixed margins make

\[
\prod_{g,c}(a_g b_c)^{x_{gc}}
=\prod_g a_g^{r_g}\prod_c b_c^{s_c},
\]

constant across the fiber. What remains is

\[
\Pr(X=x\mid\text{both margins},X\in\mathcal F)\propto\prod_{(g,c)\in E}\frac1{x_{gc}!},
\]

not a uniform law in general. Structural zeros and fixed cell bounds only restrict the fiber; they do not remove the factorial weights. The same factorial weights arise if each cell-column is directly modeled as multinomial conditional on its library size, with gene probabilities shared across cells; fixing row totals cancels the probability powers but not the cell factorials. With non-factorized means, residual gene-by-cell interaction factors lambda_gc raised to x_gc remain too. This is the noncentral conditional law; only the row/column-separable part cancels.

A useful exact characterization is: under the factorized-Poisson model, the conditional law is uniform on a particular finite fiber if and only if the product of all x_gc factorials is constant over that fiber. It holds automatically for 0/1 tables because every factorial is one. It can also hold accidentally on a singleton or a specially restricted fiber.

There is a clean *different* generative bridge to uniformity. If counts are independent geometric variables with

\[
\Pr(X_{gc}=k)=(1-q_{gc})q_{gc}^{k},\qquad q_{gc}=a_g b_c<1,
\]

then conditioning on row and column margins makes the product of q_gc raised to x_gc constant, so every feasible table has the same probability. This remains true after imposing arbitrary fixed rectangular cell bounds or removing structural-zero cells: truncation contributes only cellwise normalizers, constant across the conditioned fiber. More generally, if a table model has mass proportional to a base weight h(x) times an exponential of linear sufficient statistics Ax, conditioning on Ax=b fixes the exponential term and leaves only h(x). The conditional law is uniform exactly when h is constant on the fiber. Poisson has h(x) equal to the product of 1/x_gc factorial, while geometric has h(x)=1.

This geometric realization establishes a mathematical bridge, not a claim that gene-by-cell UMI counts are independent geometric. If the intended biological model is independent Poisson, use its factorial-weighted conditional law (e.g. the classical independence-table distribution), not the family 115 uniform sampler.

## Positive application: an overdispersed null and one randomization test

A precise overdispersed gene-by-cell null is independent geometric counts with separable odds. For each gene g and cell c, let X_gc have probability (1-q_gc) q_gc^k at count k=0,1,..., where q_gc=a_g b_c and 0<q_gc<1. This is the negative-binomial distribution with shape 1. Its mean is q_gc/(1-q_gc) and its variance is q_gc/(1-q_gc)^2 = mean + mean^2, so it is overdispersed relative to Poisson. The mean itself need not factor by row and column; the condition is on the geometric odds q_gc. If the counts are independent under this model, conditioning on the observed margins gives exactly the uniform law on the feasible fiber, for every choice of the nuisance factors a_g and b_c. No estimation of these factors is needed for the conditional test.

For a concrete statistic, let C1 be a cell group labeled before looking at the target statistic, let H be a prespecified gene set, let S1 be the sum of the observed cell totals in C1, and let N be the grand total. Define T(x) as the maximum over genes g in H of the absolute difference between the count for gene g in C1 and (S1/N) times the row total r_g. This centering is just part of the chosen statistic; calibration comes from its conditional randomization distribution, not from claiming it is the exact conditional expectation.

Given the observed table x_obs, draw M independent exact-uniform tables X^(1),...,X^(M) from the same uncapped fiber and calculate K = number of draws with T(X^(m)) at least T(x_obs). Under the specified geometric null, the observed table and the M draws are conditionally exchangeable given the margins, so the plus-one p-value (1+K)/(M+1) is finite-sample superuniform (inclusive ties make it conservative). It has resolution 1/(M+1). For estimating the tail probability p itself, Hoeffding gives probability at most 2 exp(-2 M eta^2) of additive error greater than eta; M at least log(2/delta)/(2 eta^2) suffices for confidence 1-delta. When p is rare, relative precision instead costs order log(1/delta)/(p eta^2) samples.

For full-support uncapped tables, the family 115 exact sampler is the relevant interface; it gives the exact null samples with expected polynomial bit cost per sample in the two dimensions and log(N+1). For a predeclared cap or structural-zero mask, use the bounded-fiber result instead: the companion FPRAS estimates the fiber size, and its conditional-count self-reduction gives a tau-almost-uniform feasible table sampler with worst-case polynomial bit cost in L and tau^-1. The bounded result does not give exact samples. With M bounded-fiber draws each within TV tau of uniform, the joint law is within M tau, so set tau at most tau_total/M to keep the total sampling-law error within tau_total. Lower bounds are handled by shifting them to zero; zero upper bounds encode true structural exclusions. Do not use the capped interface for a mask or bound learned from the same observed random zeros without a selection model.

This is a model-scoped capability, not a novelty claim: independent geometric (negative-binomial shape 1) cell counts with row/column-separable odds condition to uniform tables because the base measure is constant. It is useful when overdispersion is scientifically acceptable and gene/cell effects enter only through those separable odds. It does not make the usual independent-Poisson null uniform.


## Small exact counterexample

Take a 2 by 2 table with both row margins and both column margins equal to 2. Its entire fiber consists of

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For factorized Poisson means, the mean-power term is the same for all three. Their remaining weights, one over the product of x_ij factorials, are 1/4, 1, and 1/4, which normalize to 1/6, 2/3, and 1/6. The uniform sampler gives 1/3 to each. The same fiber also separates geometric from other negative-binomial shapes: shape 1 gives 1/3 to each table, while independent NB counts of common shape 2 and factorized odds give weights 9, 16, 9 and probabilities 9/34, 16/34, 9/34. The exact-rational check is saved at [poisson_margin_counterexample.py](poisson_margin_counterexample.py). This falsifies the common inference that independent Poisson, or arbitrary overdispersed negative-binomial, counts plus fixed margins imply uniform tables.

## Single-cell interpretation and failure modes

For a raw UMI matrix, rows can be genes and columns cells. Conditioning on each gene's row total fixes its total count; conditioning on each cell's column total fixes its library size. Uniform sampling then asks whether the observed gene-by-cell pattern is unusual relative to all integer allocations with those margins and the supplied support/bounds. It tests a particular exchangeability/random-allocation null. It is not a generic test of no biology, and it conditions away gene totals and cell depths by design.

The Poisson assumption is plausible for some UMI measurement regimes: a primary analysis found that Poisson models can fit technical variation after modeling latent signal, and another UMI study explicitly assessed independent Poisson count models. Other primary analyses use regularized negative-binomial regression or model cell- and gene-specific capture. These papers establish that model choice is data/protocol dependent, not a universal distributional fact. See [Poisson distribution model for UMI scRNA-seq](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10276395/), [Hafemeister–Satija negative-binomial regression](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1874-1), and [DECENT capture-efficiency model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6954660/).

The concrete mismatches are:

- **Poisson factorial weights:** even correctly independent, log-linear Poisson counts produce weights proportional to one over the product of cell factorials after margins are fixed. Binary occupancy or a fiber with constant factorial product is the stated exception.
- **Overdispersion:** for independent negative-binomial counts with common shape r, conditioning cancels multiplicatively separable odds but leaves weights proportional to the product of (r) rising-factorial x_gc divided by x_gc factorial. These are generally nonconstant; r=1 is the geometric case. Random row/column odds factors preserve the shape-1 conditional uniform law, while nonseparable cell interactions, varying shapes, or a dependence structure that does not reduce to independent conditionally factorized counts can break it. A fitted NB model is not interchangeable with the uniform fiber law.
- **Zero inflation:** a random extra-zero mixture weights tables according to which cells are zero, and margins usually do not fix that zero pattern. This changes the conditional law. A structural zero is different: it is a known impossible position, encoded as a forbidden cell. Observed zeros are not structural zeros. Droplet UMI data are not universally zero-inflated; Svensson's primary analysis argues against extra zero inflation for droplet UMI data, while other protocols and later model comparisons report assay-dependent behavior. Do not add a zero-inflation mechanism without validation.
- **Capture bias:** a purely separable cell-specific factor b_c and gene-specific factor a_g cancel from the Poisson mean-power term after both margins are fixed, but the Poisson factorial weights remain. Under Poisson thinning, observed means are the true transcript means times capture efficiencies; if both are gene-by-cell separable, the product is still of row/column form. Gene-by-cell capture interactions, batch-specific capture, ambient RNA, dropout, or nonseparable efficiencies survive conditioning and can shift the null. Capture-efficiency models explicitly include gene and cell effects; a column total alone does not remove arbitrary gene-specific capture bias.
- **Biological structure and selection:** pooled donors, cell types, conditions, cell-cycle states, or disease states can create gene-by-cell interactions. A single global fiber randomization can destroy these structures. Stratify or condition on a design justified before inspecting the target statistic. Filtering genes/cells using the same counts, imputation, normalized floating counts, or post-selection masks can also invalidate the stated conditional null.

For single-cell use, call a cell bound or structural zero only when it is a pre-specified measurement/design impossibility or a justified scientific constraint. Do not set a bound of zero merely because the observed count is zero. If the question concerns association with a label, preserve donor/batch structure and define whether the label is permuted, fixed, or stratified; the table algorithm supplies no design correction by itself.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

Fixed-margin tables have long served as conditional models for two-way data. Patefield's [Algorithm AS 159 (1981)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2346669) is the classical efficient sampler for the fixed-margin distribution under independence; its conditional law is factorial-weighted, which is exactly the distinction above. Diaconis and Gangolli's [Rectangular Arrays with Fixed Margins (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0801-3_3) survey the model and algorithms. Diaconis and Sturmfels' [Algebraic Algorithms for Sampling from Conditional Distributions (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1030563990) develops Markov-basis sampling on conditional fibers, with target weights supplied by the statistical model rather than presumed uniform.

For uniform fixed-margin sampling, Miller and Harrison's [exact sampling/counting work (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-AOS1131) treats nontrivial classes and motivates the uniform fixed-margin law as a conditional null. Arman, Gao, and Wormald's [sparse-table sampler (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AAP2013) gives linear expected-time uniform generation under a sparsity condition. Earlier general algorithms impose fixed-row, dense-margin, or other restrictions. These are primary antecedents; family 115's claim is broader in margins/dimensions and binary bit accounting, but correctness, historical priority, and practical advantage are **not independently established here**. For bounded binary tables, the permanent-based FPRAS of [Jerrum, Sinclair, and Vigoda (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1008731.1008738) is an important special-case comparator.

The application-side correction (Poisson conditioning is factorial-weighted; geometric conditioning can be uniform) is a direct exponential-family derivation, not a novelty claim. No evidence here establishes that a uniform allocation null is scientifically appropriate for a particular cell dataset.

## Data, runtime, and experimental gate

For an uncapped table, the algorithmic input is the exact integer margins: |G|+|C| integers with O(log(N+1)) bits each. Preparing these margins from the observed matrix costs O(nnz) additions for a sparse count file or O(|G||C|) for dense input. The test statistic still needs the observed table. A dense generated table costs at least |G||C| cell writes; sparse output is an implementation question, not a theorem guarantee.

For cell-bounded data, the input also includes each upper bound, so an explicit dense bound array costs O(|G||C|) entries and roughly O(|G||C| log(U+1) + (|G|+|C|) log(N+1)) bits up to encoding overhead. A zero bound is a structural exclusion. No compressed sparse-mask runtime is claimed by the source. Count approximation costs polynomial in L, epsilon^-1, and log(delta^-1); approximate sampling costs polynomial in L and tau^-1, all on every execution. For exact uncapped samples, expected cost is polynomial in dimensions and log(N); a worst-case polynomial sampler at TV 2^-k costs polynomial in k.

If M independent random tables are used to estimate a tail probability, add M sampler calls and M statistic evaluations. For an indicator tail estimate, additive error eta and confidence 1-delta take M=O(eta^-2 log(1/delta)) by Hoeffding; relative error when the tail probability is p much less than 1 costs O((p eta^2)^-1 log(1/delta)). Exact table sampling does not make a Monte Carlo p-value exact; the plus-one randomization p-value or an exact enumeration/confidence procedure is still needed. With the bounded 2^-k-TV sampler and M draws, coupling gives total deviation at most M times 2^-k, so choose k at least ceil(log2(M/tau_total)) for an overall TV budget tau_total.

A decisive biology-facing check is a blinded negative-control study with raw integer UMIs, donor/batch and cell labels, and (if available) spike-in or technical replicate information. Pre-register a statistic such as the maximum gene-by-condition association, use fixed margins, and compare (i) uniform fiber randomization, (ii) the factorial-weighted Poisson conditional law, and (iii) a fitted NB/capture-aware null. Check type-I calibration across control replicates and power on a positive control while reporting wall time, peak memory, number of generated tables, Monte Carlo interval, and all preprocessing. A failure of uniform-null calibration or a systematic shift in the fitted conditional weights rejects the biological bridge without refuting the combinatorial sampler.
