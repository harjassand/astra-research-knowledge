## Independent algebra and probability reconstruction

Let M have source columns, G=MM^T an implicit integer symmetric PSD operator of ambient size m, and b=Ga. If b=0, a is already its coker projection. Otherwise any integer polynomial q of degree at most m with q(0)!=0 and an **exact integer certificate** q(G)b=0 gives

    z=q(G)a/q(0).

Indeed Gz=0, while q(X)-q(0)=Xh(X) gives a-z in im G. Orthogonality of im G and ker G proves z is the unique projection. Neither q's minimality, a modular rank, cyclicity, a latent metric nor a hidden spectrum is required for acceptance. This remains sound if a modular attempt used a composite modulus: only the integer certificate is decisive.

If every source entry is at most a0 in absolute value, set A=max(2,R a0^2). Every Gram entry has magnitude at most A, and ||G||_2<=mA. The rational relative minimal polynomial of b divides the monic integer characteristic polynomial. Gauss's lemma makes it monic integer; symmetry and b in im G make its roots distinct and positive, bounded by mA. Its coefficients are therefore at most H=(1+mA)^m. It has nonzero constant term. This proof does not store a characteristic polynomial.

If its degree is d, the first d Krylov columns are independent. Some integer d-by-d minor Delta is nonzero. Column j has entries at most (mA)^(j+1)L, where L bounds |a_i|. Hadamard yields the producer's safe

    log2|Delta| <= K
      =2+m ceil(log2 m)+m^2 ceil(log2(mA))
         +m ceil(log2(max(2,L))).

For a prime not dividing Delta, reduction preserves d independent columns. The reduced monic rational relative polynomial is still a degree-d relation, so it is exactly the modular relative minimal polynomial. No characteristic-root separation or guessed discriminant is needed. If |Delta|=1, there are no bad primes; the following bound is trivially valid.

With s=max(16,2H+1,64K,16m), Liu–Nguyen–Yang arXiv2507.02433v1 Section 3.5, Theorem 5 supplies a bounded sampler whose output, conditional on success, is uniform among primes in [s,s^2].
