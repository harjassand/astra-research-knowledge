STRONGEST CONCRETE FINDING

I reconstructed a conditional extension of OpenAI release OA139 from well-conditioned strongly log-concave targets to globally smooth targets satisfying a Poincare inequality, which may be nonconvex. For every fixed theta>0, fixed beta,C,a, and all sufficiently large d:

  pi proportional to exp(-V), V in C^2(R^d), ||Hess V||op<=beta,
  E_pi|X|^2 finite, PI(pi)<=C,
  SUPPLIED initial sampler rho_0 with chi^2(rho_0||pi)<=d^a,

imply a sampler of pi with TV error <=1/10 using at most C_theta d^theta exact gradient queries on EVERY execution. Initial-sampler acquisition and draw cost are separately charged. No target minimizer, normalizer, conditional-mean oracle or restricted-Gaussian sampler is supplied by the statement. It imports OA139's finite-gradient noisy near-quadratic primitive, whose correctness is not externally certified here. Thus this is a conditional consequence with a complete transfer derivation, not an unconditional breakthrough or novel primitive.

The detailed proof is optimization_sampling_proof.txt. Its independent moment lemma is:

  E_pi||grad V||^2 <= beta d,
  E_pi||grad V||^4 <= beta^2 d(d+2).

These hold without convexity, by integration by parts and truncation of ||grad V||^2. They control primitive errors under polynomial chi-squared warmness, avoiding an unproved gradient bound on an approximate trajectory.

LOAD-BEARING MECHANISM AND COSTS

OA139's auxiliary theorem only assumes a small Lipschitz gradient for the perturbation F; F need not be convex. Original-target strong convexity enters its outer W2 proximal contraction. Replacing that step with the established PI proximal chi-squared contraction is legal.

Set delta=d^-e and h=delta/beta. The normalized restricted conditional has F_y(z)=V(y+sqrt(h)z), with Lip(grad F_y)<=delta. The source primitive implements its sample PLUS an independent Gaussian with q_prim<=polylog(d) delta^-t queries and local TV error

  polylog(d) delta^K [sqrt(d)+sqrt(h)|grad V(y)|].

The ideal Y-proximal chain contracts chi-squared divergence by [C/(C+h)]^2 per step. T=O(d^e log d) steps suffice from W<=d^a. By the fourth-moment lemma and Cauchy-Schwarz, average local error under every ideal prefix is <=polylog(d) delta^K d^(1/2+a/4). Ideal-prefix telescoping gives total error

  polylog(d) d^[1/2+a/4-e(K-1)],

which vanishes when K>1+(a+2)/(4e). Source variance-halving and an approximate mode-centered Gaussian remove the remaining auxiliary Gaussian in O(log d) further primitive calls. That construction also only requires a Lipschitz perturbation; its stationary initial gradient moment is supplied by the lemma above. Query cap is d^[e(1+t)+o(1)], and e can be chosen below theta/2. All iteration counts and primitive query caps are prescribed before observing oracle answers.

The quantitative cap is O~((1+beta C)d^[e(1+t)]), so a declared product beta C=d^o(1) also retains exponent zero. This admits smooth isotropic weakly log-concave targets using existing polylogarithmic PI bounds, subject to the same warm-start and representation costs; a new dimension-free KLS theorem is unnecessary.

The apparent large matrix square root in its compiler is rank one:

  P = I-vv^T/[4D^2(1+sqrt(1-||v||^2/(4D^2)))],   ||v||<=D.

It costs O(d times number of seed slots) to apply. No high-order tensor is constructed at runtime: tensor estimates analyze a finite affine circuit in conditional means, and terminal calls evaluate the original gradient. Given the source's effective scalar tables, the circuit count gives near-linear real-arithmetic overhead d^[1+e(1+t)+o(1)], plus gradient evaluation cost and the warm draw. A full uniform BIT implementation has additional unsatisfied gates: effective parameter thresholds, scalar-table generation, radius/encoding bounds, a certified approximate gradient oracle and quantized Gaussian generation. The source's unrestricted real-computation model cannot silently be replaced by ordinary floating point.

EXACT BOUNDARIES / FALSIFICATION

1. Finite-bit output has TV=1 against an absolutely continuous target. A finite-bit theorem must compare against a quantized target Q_b pi, or retain continuous randomness in its output model. The proposed compiler uses final independent Gaussian reserves to turn local roundoff into TV control, then sums kernel errors. Naively multiplying fixed-seed Lipschitz constants across d^e chain steps can give a superpolynomial precision cost; that proof is invalid.

2. A PI certificate does not acquire a warm start. Even pi=N(0,(2/3)I_d), Hessian (3/2)I, has chi^2(N(0,I_d)||pi)+1=(2/sqrt(3))^d. The polynomial-warmness hypothesis is substantive. Nor does this prove a dimension-free PI/KLS bound from gradient access.

3. Explicit nonconvex legal family: V=|x|^2/2+A cos(x_1), A>1 fixed. Its Hessian is negative at some points; beta<=1+A, PI<=exp(2A), and a standard Gaussian warm law has chi^2<=exp(2A)-1. But a fully informed baseline given its explicit value formula can use ordinary Gaussian rejection in constant expected time. This is an interface witness, not evidence of algorithmic advantage.

4. General-matroid ROUND remains distinct. Astra P-round assumes actual-edge support k(P) and exact strong optimization. It gives a reported exact-mean hard-feature bound O(k), not a sampler for an arbitrary law or an operator discrepancy theorem. N24's acquired rational cube/partition-base rounders have weaker sqrt(mu log(rows)) bounds and do not acquire a general matroid exchange cube. No new unrestricted discrepancy result was obtained here.

5. Chen-Liu's new common-base FPRAS already has every-execution polynomial bit guarantees. Its curvature sum threshold eta+kappa>=0 is explicit; weakening it to any fixed negative threshold would imply NP=RP by its Appendix A.3. A third matroid-intersection transfer would additionally face NP-complete three-dimensional matching feasibility. Neither is solved by treating soft constraints or external fields as free.
