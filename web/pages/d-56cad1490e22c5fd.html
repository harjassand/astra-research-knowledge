# Continuous channel acquisition: exact replay, conditional guarantees, and report invariants

2026-10-06. Cycle3 independent derivations, executable exact product-law comparison, and a scoped approximate converter. No real sensor was calibrated; no originality or special-architecture claim. Preserve the distinct interfaces below: their sample bounds are different.

## Interface and scientific question

Two deployment states theta=0,1. Known source F_1=Bern(p), F_0=Bern(1-p), p>.5. Unknown target G_1=Bern(q), G_0=Bern(1-q), with q in a supplied continuous full-support interval. Labelled calibration permits iid target-row observations; complement samples from row0 to obtain Bern(q). State preparation, target calibration requests and source deployment requests are paid. All samples are fresh conditional on q/theta. Source p carries no information about q. This independence is load-bearing: if source and target share an unknown parameter, Bernoulli factories can change the answer.

The stringent target is a fresh entire n-bit G_q transcript, or all reports of it. An algorithm may retain its calibration observations, randomize, and use unlimited computation. A competent baseline receives and retains the same information. We ask separately for unconditional output-law approximation, and for accuracy conditional on the realized retained calibration dataset with high confidence. Known-law Li-Liu conversion applies after laws are supplied; it does not acquire q. [Versioned recent source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19897v2), independently verified in cycle2.

## 1. Exact unconditional reproduction requires n paid target observations

Give the algorithm theta for free; a lower bound in this easier problem is valid for deployment. Fix any two q,r in the allowed interval. Target-calibration actions give Bern(q) or Bern(1-q); their directed KL relative to r is the same number k=KL(Bern(q)||Bern(r)). Source p requests have zero q-versus-r KL. Let M count all target-law requests, including any made during deployment. Adaptive labelled choice is allowed.

For an integrable complete stopped transcript, the likelihood-chain/Wald identity gives KL(input_q||input_r)=E_q M*k. If output is exactly Bern(q)^n, output KL is n*k. Data processing therefore gives E_q M>=n. This holds at every q, choosing any distinct allowed r. No differentiability/Fisher-information assumption or finite deterministic cap is needed. If M is not integrable, it cannot provide a finite cheaper expected budget.

Thus exact unknown-q full-transcript reproduction cannot save target measurement count in this independent-parameter acquisition interface. This does not refute exact known-law source conversion, shared-parameter factories, finite separated model catalogues, or a report whose law is insensitive to q.

### Strong retaining baseline and conditioning distinction

Store n independent labelled Bern(q) calibration bits. Identify deployment theta from the known source with error at most eta; output the stored bits for theta_hat=1 and their complements for theta_hat=0. Calibration is independent of deployment theta/source observations. Unconditionally over calibration data, output TV from the desired product law is at most eta. Cost is n target requests plus source testing. Estimating q to radius eta/sqrt(n) was not necessary. This baseline prevents the unjustified universal claim that all unknown-law simulation needs n/eta^2 calibration.

Conditional on the observed calibration vector D, however, replay is deterministic (apart from orientation error). Grant theta known and even randomize the stored bits' order: conditional on count k, the replay law is uniform on the count-k shell. Its TV from a fresh target product law is exactly 1-Pr_q(Bin(n,q)=k), typically close to one. Unconditional marginal correctness is not a frozen-dataset confidence certificate.

Replaying the same bank twice also fails the fresh joint law. With theta known, replay gives Pr(Y1=Y2)=1, whereas two independent target n-transcripts give (q^2+(1-q)^2)^n. At q=.8,n=1024 this is .68^1024. A subsequent binary report testing equality distinguishes these outputs almost perfectly. Fresh joint use of R n-bit transcripts needs a bank of Rn bits or another valid generator; marginal correctness per query is insufficient.

## 2. Unconditional approximate reproduction has an Omega(n) acquisition lower bound

Suppose the output law is within epsilon TV of Bern(q)^n for every q in an interval containing [.7,.9], where epsilon<.3646. For n>=100 pick q_plus=.8+1/sqrt(n), q_minus=.8-1/sqrt(n). The event that the output average is at least .8 has target probability at least 1-exp(-2) under q_plus and at most exp(-2) under q_minus. Consequently the algorithm's two output laws are separated by at least

    a=1-2exp(-2)-2epsilon >0.

Data processing, Pinsker and the stopped calibration KL identity imply

    E_plus M >= 2a^2/KL(Bern(q_plus)||Bern(q_minus)).

The KL is at most the chi-square divergence 4/[n q_minus(1-q_minus)]<=25/n, since q_minus in [.7,.8]. Thus E_plus M >= (2/25)a^2 n. This universal expected-count lower bound permits retention and arbitrary random generators; it is not an estimator-specific argument. Its constant is conservative. It proves Omega(n) at fixed epsilon, not a sharp impossibility of every constant-factor approximate saving and not Omega(n/epsilon^2).

## 3. Conditional confidence accuracy costs Omega(n/epsilon^2)

Now demand: with probability at least 1-beta over retained calibration D, the conditional fresh-output kernel Q_D is within epsilon TV of Bern(q)^n, uniformly for every true q in the class. Source observations of known p and deployment randomization are part of Q_D. This is the appropriate stronger promise for later reports selected using calibration evidence: conditional all-output TV bounds every such fixed-D report simultaneously.

The following lower bound applies to **every** such generator, not merely plug-in estimators. It was independently reconstructed and cross-checked against r08's same conclusion.

For odd n=2k+1>=3, epsilon<=1/16, choose d=4sqrt(2)epsilon/sqrt(n), q_plus=.5+d, q_minus=.5-d. Define f_n(q)=Pr_q(Bin(n,q)>=k+1). Its derivative is

    f'_n(q)=n binom(2k,k) q^k(1-q)^k.

The central binomial bound binom(2k,k)/4^k>=1/(2sqrt(k)) follows by induction: the coefficient ratio is (2k+1)/(2k+2), whose square is at least k/(k+1). Since d^2<=1/(8n), Bernoulli's inequality gives (1-4d^2)^k>=3/4. Integration over [.5-d,.5+d] yields

    TV(Bern(q_plus)^n,Bern(q_minus)^n)
      >= (3/4)d n/sqrt(k) >= d sqrt(n/2)=4epsilon.

Hence the epsilon-good sets of calibration datasets for the two parameters are disjoint. The hypothetical generator gives a calibration test: choose plus when Q_D is within epsilon of the plus product law, otherwise choose minus. It has both error probabilities at most beta. We may grant arbitrarily costly exact evaluation of Q_D in this information lower bound; computational difficulty cannot invalidate a lower bound in the easier unrestricted-computation problem.

Its certificate-bit KL is at least

    k_beta=kl(1-beta||beta)=(1-2beta)log((1-beta)/beta).

Meanwhile calibration's one-sample KL is

    k_d=2d log((.5+d)/(.5-d))
       <=8d^2/(1-4d^2)<=16d^2.

Stopped KL/data processing gives

    E_plus M >= k_beta/k_d >= n k_beta/(512epsilon^2).

A Pinsker version gives n(1-2beta)^2/(256epsilon^2). Adaptive integrable calibration does not avoid the expected-count bound; a confidence sequence only makes the stopping decision sound. If beta->0 the stronger bound includes log(1/beta).

### Interior extension at the q=.8 benchmark

The .5-centred family is a convenient proof, not an assumption required by the obstruction. Set q0=.8 and take N=n-1 divisible by5, so k=.8N is the Bin(N,q0) mode. Chebyshev puts at least3/4 mass within sqrt(N) of the mean, so the mode probability is at least1/(4sqrt(N)). For |q-q0|<=d<=.1/sqrt(n), the log of its mode density has zero first derivative at q0 and second derivative at least -N/.1^2. Therefore the density at q is at least exp(-1/2) times its q0 density. The event Bin(n,q)>=k+1 has derivative n times that mode density. With d=8epsilon/sqrt(n), epsilon<=1/80,

    TV(product_plus,product_minus) >= (d sqrt(n)/2)exp(-1/2)
                                  =4exp(-1/2)epsilon>2epsilon.

Disjoint confidence-good sets again give a test. Since q_minus in [.7,.8], its calibration KL is at most25d^2. Thus

    E_plus M >= n k_beta/(1600epsilon^2).

This matches the scientifically relevant interior interval, albeit with coarse constants. For n=1026, epsilon=.01,beta=.01, the actual chosen two-point KL yields at least57932 expected target calibration samples; the conservative symbolic bound is28877. This is already far larger than1026 target requests. Exact numerical values are recorded in the executable result; rounded prose is not the certificate.

## 4. Product KL improves the elementary linear coupling upper bound

Clip an empirical scalar estimate q_hat to the supplied interval [b,1-b], 0<b<.5. On a calibration confidence event |q_hat-q|<=e, chi-square domination gives

    KL(Bern(q)||Bern(q_hat)) <= e^2/[b(1-b)].

Product additivity and Pinsker give

    TV(Bern(q)^n,Bern(q_hat)^n) <= e sqrt[n/(2b(1-b))].

Every report of that n-transcript inherits the same bound by data processing. This improves per-step maximal coupling's ne bound to sqrt(n)e for iid full transcripts. Applying an effective one-step fitted-channel generator rather than enumerating a report law preserves computational tractability when feasible, as r08's cycle2 construction shows. Its known source row errors still need separate treatment.

Hoeffding radius e=sqrt(log(2/beta)/(2m)) gives the sufficient plug-in calibration budget

    m >= n log(2/beta)/[4b(1-b)epsilon^2].

This is a method upper bound, and section3 supplies a matching-order general lower bound for the specified conditional confidence interface. It is not the correct universal bound for unconditional retaining samplers.

### Honest adaptive calibration via a confidence sequence

For every m>=1 use e_m=sqrt(log(2m(m+1)/delta)/(2m)). Hoeffding's failure probability at time m is at most delta/[m(m+1)]; summing gives simultaneous coverage for all m with probability at least1-delta. Therefore calibration may stop adaptively and select any future joint horizon

    n_m <= floor[2b(1-b)epsilon^2/e_m^2].

The clipped fitted product law then has conditional TV at most epsilon on the shared coverage event. This elementary confidence sequence costs log(m) overhead; sharper constructions exist and are not reconstructed here. For multiple jointly requested reports, n_m bounds their **total** fresh signal horizon, not each report separately at no additional cost. Nothing here certifies arbitrary integrable target stopping policies without horizon/tail control.

## 5. A target-specific invariant gives a cheap effective approximate converter

Target report: take n=1024 BSC target observations and output the majority estimate of theta. For every q>=q_min>.5, its statewise error is at most exp[-2n(q_min-.5)^2]. Its binary output law is uniformly close to the invariant point mass at theta; preserving the underlying target noise parameter is unnecessary for this report.

Known source p=.9: take m=12 source observations and output their majority. Statewise error is at most exp[-2m(.9-.5)^2]=exp(-3.84)<.021494. This is an effective O(m) algorithm; a same-information retaining baseline can run it unchanged.

We can acquire a **coarse** target certificate rather than full-transcript precision. Take231 labelled target calibration samples. A one-sided Hoeffding bound with delta=.01 gives lower confidence bound q_lower=q_hat-.1. Accept the report conversion when q_hat>=.7, hence on coverage q>=.6. At that lower endpoint the1024-target majority error is at most exp(-20.48)<1.276e-9. Triangle inequality via the point mass at theta gives conditional report TV below .021494, simultaneously in both states and for every event of this binary report. The procedure may return UNKNOWN when the lower confidence test fails. If true q=.8, rejection probability is at most exp[-2*231*.1^2]<.009853. On acceptance, charged requests are231 target calibration plus12 source deployment, total243, versus1024 fresh target requests.

This is a scoped approximately correct report conversion with controlled confidence and abstention. It provides neither all-report preservation nor an architecture advantage. The calibrated lower bound and the target's supplied BSC/symmetry/labelled-state interface are substantive assumptions; source p is still supplied exactly. Reset/preparation costs and physical sample prices may erase the nominal request saving and must be added for an actual sensor. If calibration is shared/sunk, the retaining comparator receives the same coarse certificate.

This construction shows how a scientific report's approximate invariant quotient can avoid full-matrix precision costs: the limiting majority report depends only on theta. It also identifies the limitation: an adversarial report of the raw noise transcript depends on q and reinstates sections1-4. The target-specific guarantee must be declared before presenting cost savings.
