# Turning ambient local filling into actual-graph spectral stability

Root, 7 October 2026. **Conditional mathematical deduction; independent gates in progress.** This construction avoids changing the graph interface of the De Roeck–Khemani–Li–O'Dea–Rakovszky theorem. Every graph edge below comes from actual nonidentity Pauli support. The auxiliary system is used in the proof; the final spectral conclusion concerns the original Hamiltonian and original perturbation. No thermal-lifetime conclusion is claimed.

## Premises

Let H0=sum_a (I-C_a)/2 be a frustration-free Pauli stabilizer Hamiltonian on N qubits in a finite subset of R^D. Assume fixed bounds on check range, check weight, qubit degree and the number of qubits in a unit cube, independent of N. Assume no idle original qubits. Let d be its code distance. Suppose every global stabilizer supported in B(x,r) is generated by original checks with whole supports in B(x,C max(r,1)), uniformly up to a stated radius r_fill. C is fixed but may be enormous.

Assume the embedding lies in an axis-aligned lattice box of polynomial volume in N. More generally it suffices that the number N' of original plus grid qubits obey log N'=O(log N), with constants charged below. A sufficient simple condition is a connected original qubit interaction graph: bounded physical length of its edges gives diameter O(N) and a box of O(N^D) grid sites. For a theorem family the effective distance min(d,r_fill), after the fixed losses below, must exceed a sufficiently large multiple of log N'. Infinite r_fill is allowed. These are explicit premises, not consequences of geometric locality alone.

Let V=sum_X V_X be Hermitian, each X have physical diameter at most a fixed R_V, and let J=sup_q sum_{X containing q} ||V_X||. Scalar terms may be removed since they only shift all energies. The claim is stability for sufficiently small J depending on all fixed geometry/filling/range parameters, not a useful numerical threshold.

## A finite-range Clifford extension with a genuine connected grid

Put one ancillary qubit at each site v of an integer box containing all original qubits and a fixed margin. Attach each original qubit q to one nearest grid site a(q). Bounded original density gives at most b attachments per site, with b independent of N. Let F be the graph with nearest-neighbor grid edges and attachment edges q--a(q). Its maximum degree is at most Delta=2D+b (increase to at least one). Every edge has bounded physical length.

Start from the decoupled Hamiltonian

    Hbar0 = H0 tensor I + sum_v (I-X_v)/2.

Apply U=product_{uv in E(F)} CZ_uv. The gates commute, U=U*=U^{-1}, and a bounded edge coloring realizes U in constant depth. More importantly, the exact Pauli rules give

    U X_u U* = X_u product_{v adjacent u} Z_v,
    U Z_u U* = Z_u.

Thus either conjugation spreads a Pauli's support by at most one F-neighborhood, multiplying its cardinality by at most s=Delta+1. It does not spread iteratively along the whole grid, because the newly added Z factors are unchanged by further CZ gates.

Define C'_a=U C_a U* and

    K_v=U X_v U*=X_v product_{w grid-neighbor v} Z_w
                         product_{q attached to v} Z_q.

Then H'0=U Hbar0 U* is a commuting Pauli Hamiltonian with these actual checks. Adjacent grid checks K_v,K_w share v and w, so their check-graph nodes are adjacent. An original transformed check C'_a retains a nonidentity Pauli at every original qubit in supp C_a: no gate joins two original qubits, and original Z factors cannot cancel original X/Y factors. Hence C'_a shares an original qubit q with K_{a(q)}. Every original check is attached to the connected grid of ancillary checks.

The actual qubit graph similarly contains every grid nearest-neighbor edge and every original attachment edge, because K_v contains both endpoints. Its degree and the actual check graph's degree are bounded. The number and weights of checks are O(N'), with constants depending on original density/range/degree. This is genuine LDPC incidence, not declared identity-site support.

## Distance and local generation survive conjugation

The untransformed augmented code has the original logical dimension: all ancillas are fixed to |+>. Its distance is the original d. Indeed a Pauli in its centralizer can have only I or X on ancillary sites, and those ancillary factors are themselves stabilizers. A nontrivial logical therefore restricts to a nontrivial original logical. Conversely every original logical extends by identity.

For a logical p of the transformed code, U*pU is a logical of the untransformed augmented code and has weight at most s wt(p). Consequently d' >= d/s. This uses the lower bound; it does not assume exact distance preservation.

Now take a transformed global stabilizer p supported in an ambient ball of radius r. Conjugating back gives a product g_original product_{v in A} X_v supported in the ball enlarged by a fixed R_U. The original factor is an original global stabilizer supported there. By the filling premise it is a product of original checks with whole supports within radius C max(r+R_U,1), provided r+R_U<=r_fill. The ancillary X factors are already checks at sites in that enlarged input ball. Conjugating each selected check forward enlarges support by only R_U. Thus p has a representation using actual transformed checks inside a ball of radius C'(r+1), with fixed C'.

Let S be a connected set in the actual transformed check graph. Fixed physical range of every transformed check implies supp(S) lies in a ball of radius R_c(|S|+1), anchored at some qubit of any selected check. Therefore every p in G(S) has a filling by transformed checks in a ball of radius C''(|S|+1), as long as R_c(|S|+1)+R_U<=r_fill.

Every filler check connects to a grid check at a physically neighboring grid site, in one check-graph step. A grid check in that ball connects to a grid check attached to S by a Manhattan path of length at most c_D C''(|S|+1). The integer box is rectangular, so this path remains inside the finite ancillary system; no convexity of the original occupied set is required. Including the endpoint steps gives

    G(S) subseteq G_{B_{ell |S|}(S)}

for a fixed ell and every nonempty connected S below the stated filling scale. With all-radius filling this is exactly the actual-graph TQO-II of arXiv2411.02384v3 Section2.2, whose cutoff is proportional to actual distance. If r_fill is much smaller than actual distance, the printed theorem cannot be quoted verbatim with that equality: its proof can instead be run with a conservative small-word cutoff proportional to min(d,r_fill), since TQO-I remains true below that cutoff and every distance use is a lower bound or truncation. Both independent bridge checkers identified and audited this qualification. The actual quantum-memory application seeks all-radius filling and does not need that cutoff extension. No larger filling window is inferred from code distance alone.

## The original physical perturbation meets the actual-graph norm

Use V'=U(V tensor I)U*. A finite-range input term contains at most q0 original qubits by bounded density. Its Pauli expansion has coefficient l1 norm at most 2^{q0} ||V_X|| (4^{q0} would be a harmless looser bound). Conjugation permutes signed Paulis and adds at most the attached neighboring sites to an original-only term. Every resulting Pauli is contained in a connected actual-qubit-graph set of cardinality at most m0, obtained from the grid box around X plus its original attachments; m0 is a fixed function of density, D and R_V.

For an original qubit u, transformed terms touching u came from terms containing u. For an ancilla v, transformed original-only terms touching v came from terms containing some original qubit attached to v. Therefore the sum of originating term norms is at most max(1,b)J at any site. Triangle inequality under combination of equal Pauli strings gives the precise sufficient estimate

    |||V'|||_mu <= max(1,b) 2^{q0} exp(mu m0) J.

The norm is the primary Section2.3 intensive Pauli norm using minimal connected actual-graph covers; our explicit cover upper bounds that minimum. Thus it is finite for every fixed mu and can meet the prior smallness threshold by taking a sufficiently small J independent of N. The exponential loss in m0 and in the required mu must be charged; the resulting threshold may be extraordinarily small.

## Applying the prior theorem and removing the auxiliary system

Under the above premises and d_* >= c log N', invoking the published Theorem2.1 of De Roeck et al yields a band of exactly 2^K lowest eigenvalues of H'0+V', with width bounded by a constant times N'_c J exp(-c' d_*), separated from the rest by a size-independent positive gap for sufficiently small J. N'_c=O(N') is the number of actual checks. The present deduction supplies the theorem's legal interface. **The team has not independently closed its full KAM proof:** beyond repairable support/norm bookkeeping, the canonical counterexample in CANONICAL_KAM_COLLECTION_OBSTRUCTION.md invalidates the literal collection-level cancellation inference at(4.8). It does not refute the theorem's conclusion, but a norm-controlled rewriting or another prior is needed for a fully independent spectral proof. The implication in this note is explicitly published-theorem-dependent.

But exactly,

    H'0+V' = U[(H0+V) tensor I + I tensor Hanc]U*.

The spectrum is the original perturbed spectrum plus nonnegative integer ancillary excitation energies. Once the low-band width is less than one, all its 2^K states have ancillary ground factor: any ancillary excitation adds at least one to an original energy, whose minimum is already the total minimum. Thus the original H0+V has exactly that low band, its width and a positive gap above it. This concludes spectral stability of the original Hamiltonian. It does not require physically implementing the ancillas or U.

The argument proves the spectral claim only. Transferring all quasi-local-unitary, analyticity or excited-band conclusions would require specifying their original-system form separately. No open-system Davies-generator or thermal-lifetime stability is obtained from this composition.
