EXACT THEOREM AUDITED

Let d>=1, let finitely many reaction vectors nu_e in R^d be fixed, and put
S=span{nu_e}. Zero vectors may be discarded: they affect neither the vector
field nor any essential reaction set. Let a family of rates w_e^f(t,x)>0
be measurable in time and locally uniformly Lipschitz in x on positive
compacts, with the standard Caratheodory local existence/uniqueness meaning
on every finite time interval. Suppose continuous tau_e:[-1,1]^d -> R and
a common epsilon(h)->0 satisfy, for all sufficiently small h, all admitted
f, all times t>=0, all p in [-1,1]^d, and all e,

 |log(w_e^f(t,h^p))/log(h)-tau_e(p)| <= epsilon(h).       (UV)

For every r in the cube, including coordinate and reaction-normal faces,
set E_r={e:r dot nu_e !=0}. If E_r is nonempty, require EVERY minimizer of
tau_e(r) over E_r to have r dot nu_e>0.                  (TE)

Then every positive solution of x'=sum_e nu_e w_e^f(t,x) is global. For
every c>0, P=(c+S) intersection R^d_{>0} admits one nonempty compact convex
K_P subset P, invariant for every admitted f, that absorbs every solution
with initial x0 in P in finite time T(x0), uniformly over f. For S={0},
take K_P={c}. The proof below also gives a time bound uniform over x0 in
any fixed positive compact subset of P.

1. AFFINE LEMMA: COMPLETE LOAD-BEARING CHECK

The exact lemma permits arbitrary positive E:[a,b]^m -> (0,infinity), with
no continuity or positive lower bound. It produces one fixed finite set of
labels (r_lambda,b_lambda(h)), r_lambda in [a,b]^m and b_lambda=o(h^a).
For F_h(x)=min_lambda(r_lambda dot x+b_lambda(h)), every tied minimum is
active. If a FIXED label stays active along h_n->0 and p_n in R^m -> p in
the cube, then ||p-r_lambda||_infinity < E(r_lambda). The approximating
p_n may be outside the cube. The family is chosen before the cutoff in h.

The induction is well founded: in dimension m, the local type-k families
use only dimensions k<m. Fix t=min_i p_i. The initial local label is
(t*1,0). Type k means exactly k slope coordinates exceed t. After choosing
the finitely many type k-1 slopes, choose beta_k with

 t<beta_k<b, beta_k<beta_{k-1} when k>1,
 beta_k-t<E(r) for every already chosen type k-1 slope.

Positivity of the finitely many numbers E(r) suffices for this choice.
For each k-coordinate set U use induction on [beta_k,b]^U with tolerance
E(r(s)), where r(s) equals s on U and t elsewhere. Lift each sublabel
(s,d(h)) to r(s) with offset -h^{beta_k}+d(h), d=o(h^{beta_k}). There are
only types 0,...,m-1 because a limit with minimum t has a coordinate at t.
All resulting local offsets are o(h^t).

Suppose a type-k label is active along a limit with minimum t. Comparison
to the all-t label, when k>0, gives

 sum_{i in U}(r_i-t)h_n^{p_{n,i}} <= (1+o(1))h_n^{beta_k}.

All coefficients are >=beta_k-t>0. Therefore every limiting p_i on U is
>=beta_k. This inference uses a STRICT gap if p_i<beta_k; it makes no
unsupported power comparison when p_i=beta_k. Within the same U, the
off-U contributions and -h^{beta_k} cancel exactly. The sublabel is active
in its induction family, and its limit is in [beta_k,b]^U. Induction gives
strict error <E(r) on U. The outside-cube clause is needed here: p_{n,U}
can approach beta_k or b from outside its lower-dimensional cube.

For k<m-1, an off-U coordinate p_j>beta_{k+1} would allow a type k+1
competitor raised on U union {j}. Every limit exponent there is STRICTLY
above beta_{k+1}, since on U it is at least beta_k>beta_{k+1}. Relative to
the all-t label, this competitor has value

 -h^{beta_{k+1}}+o(h^{beta_{k+1}}).

The active type-k value is 0 for k=0 and at least
-h^{beta_k}+o(h^{beta_k}) otherwise. The competitor is eventually smaller,
because beta_k>beta_{k+1}. Hence off-U exponents are <=beta_{k+1}; their
error is <=beta_{k+1}-t<E(r). For k=m-1 the sole remaining coordinate is
exactly t, since the limit minimum is t and all raised coordinates exceed
t. This covers every local label, including ties. For m=1 or t=b the
singleton proves the fixed-minimum assertion directly.

The extension near t does not assume a regular gauge or regular offsets.
For each selected label define A_lambda to be the cube limits of ACTUAL
active sequences, allowing arbitrary approximants in R^m. It is closed:
from z_j in A_lambda -> z choose an active witness with h_j<1/j and
||p_j-z_j||<1/j. This diagonal sequence witnesses z in A_lambda. The bad
set

 B_t=union_lambda [A_lambda intersection
                  {p:||p-r_lambda||>=E(r_lambda)}]

is compact because the union is finite and each E(r_lambda) is a fixed
positive NUMBER. Its disjointness from {min p_i=t} gives a radius rho_t>0
on which there are no bad limiting active points. Shrink rho_t so every
local offset is o(h^{t+rho_t}); this is possible because all beta_k>t.
Offsets zero in the singleton case require no additional restriction.

For completeness, the interval gluing has no endpoint gap. Choose finitely
many symmetric I_t=(t-rho_t,t+rho_t) covering [a,b], remove contained
intervals, and order the remaining centers t_1<...<t_N. Their endpoints
L_j,R_j are both strictly increasing. Consecutive intervals overlap;
otherwise the gap between them lies between their centers in [a,b] and is
uncovered. The cut interval

 (max(t_j,L_{j+1}), min(t_{j+1},R_j))

is nonempty: all four pairwise lower-versus-upper inequalities follow
from ordered centers, each center lying in its interval, and overlap.
Choose q_j there, q_0=a, q_N=b. Then q_j increases and the CLOSED range
[q_{j-1},q_j] lies inside the OPEN I_{t_j}, including the outer endpoints.

Shift every label in family j by -sum_{ell<j}h^{q_ell}. Each global offset
is o(h^a); local decay is stronger than a, and every shift exponent is >a.
A global active label from family j cannot have min p_i>q_j: comparison
to the next shifted all-t label gives h^{q_j}+o(h^{q_j})>0. It cannot have
min p_i<q_{j-1}: comparison to the previous shifted all-t label has only
positive scalar-product coefficients, one power below q_{j-1}, and value
divided by h^{q_{j-1}} tending to +infinity. Local offsets are negligible
in both comparisons by the strict cut placement. At a cut equality no
power comparison is made; membership in the closed assigned range is
enough to invoke the nearby-minimum guarantee. Thus every global active
label satisfies the strict tolerance. The outer cases and N=1 are covered.

Finally, if no uniform small-h activity cutoff existed on the cube, choose
a violating sequence; finite labels and compactness give one fixed label
and p_n->p. The reverse inequality >=E(r_lambda) survives the limit and
contradicts the sequential lemma. This fixes all finite slopes BEFORE any
uniform flux constants or scale are chosen. There is no circular uniform
margin assumption over the continuum of slopes.

2. FROM THE AFFINE FAMILY TO FINITE UNIFORM DRIFT

For each r not in S^perp choose an essential minimizing edge e_+(r).
TE gives a(r)=r dot nu_{e_+}>0 and a strict order gap between this edge
and every edge whose projection against the FIXED r is negative. By
continuity and finiteness this gap persists with value >g(r)>0 in a
relative cube neighborhood of r of radius R(r). If no negative edge
exists, choose arbitrary positive g,R; the restriction is vacuous.

Choose a positive E(r)<=1/2, E(r)<|r_i|/2 for every nonzero coordinate,
and E(r)<R(r) when r is nonneutral. At neutral r only the coordinate
condition is needed. This is the precise discontinuous gauge allowed by
the affine lemma. Choose the resulting finite family once on [-1,1]^d.

Over its finitely many nonneutral slopes let a_0=min a(r)>0, g_0=min
g(r)>0 and B=max_r sum_e |r dot nu_e|<infinity. For an active nonneutral
r at h^p in B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d, the tolerance puts p in the correct
fixed-normal neighborhood. UV then gives, for every negative edge,

 w_e/w_{e_+} <= h^{g_0/2}

once 2 epsilon(h)<g_0/2. Notice that edges neutral against r drop out
EXACTLY even if they would be nonneutral against the nearby p. The proof
never replaces the fixed normal r by a moving exponent vector p.

Consequently r dot f(t,h^p)>=(a_0-B h^{g_0/2})w_{e_+}.
Bounded continuous profiles on the cube give a finite M>=1 with
tau_e(p)+epsilon(h)<=M at all small scales. Since 0<h<1, w_{e_+}>=h^M.
For all sufficiently small h,

 r dot f >= delta_h=(a_0/2)h^M>0 for active nonneutral r,
 r dot f =0 for neutral r.

These constants and cutoffs are uniform in time and admitted paths. There
is no need for a positive gap for every direction at once. The nonneutral
selected set is nonempty when S!=0 by the compactness argument below.

UV also supplies all-time boundedness of rates on every positive compact
D: choose ONE valid fixed h with D subset B_h and use the bounded profiles
at that h. Rates are also bounded away from zero there. Thus the stated
local Lipschitz hypotheses have the growth bounds needed for Caratheodory
well-posedness; unbounded temporal amplitude at a fixed state is excluded.

3. THE GLOBAL PLATEAU IS REALLY COMPACT AND POSITIVE

Fix c>0. For small h, p_c=log(c)/log(h) lies in the cube and tends to 0.
There are finitely many slopes. For each nonzero slope r choose a nonzero
coordinate i; once |p_{c,i}|<|r_i|/2, its distance from r exceeds |r_i|/2
and hence E(r). Such a slope cannot be active. Some zero label is therefore
active. With M_h=F_h(c), its constant affine value gives F_h(x)<=M_h for
ALL x in R^d, not just for points of the positive box. Indeed M_h is the
minimum offset among all zero labels and is independent of c whenever
zero is active. Thus

 K_h={F_h=M_h}=intersection_lambda
                 {x:r_lambda dot x+b_lambda(h)>=M_h}

is nonempty, closed and convex, containing c.

At every x in K_h intersection B_h a minimum-offset zero label is active.
The uniform activity statement forces its exponent vector to have norm
<E(0)<=1/2, so x is in the STRICT interior of B_{sqrt(h)}. Any purported
point of K_h outside B_h, including a nonpositive point, would have a
segment to c meeting the boundary of B_h in K_h, contradicting that strict
interior conclusion. Hence K_h subset int B_{sqrt(h)}. This proves compact
positivity in the ambient R^d before intersecting a stoichiometric class.

On c+S each neutral affine label has constant value at least F_h(c)=M_h.
At x outside K_h, F_h(x)<M_h. Therefore every active slope at such a class
point is nonneutral. If all selected slopes were neutral and S!=0, K_h
would be invariant under translations by S and could not be compact.
This also justifies the earlier finite positive minima a_0 and g_0.

4. MEASURABLE TIME, TIES, INVARIANCE AND CONTINUATION

For fixed h, the finite affine minimum is globally Lipschitz in x, hence
F_h(x(t)) is absolutely continuous along every absolutely continuous
solution on each compact time interval. At a common differentiability
time for it and all finitely many affine compositions, every active label
has derivative equal to F_h': its difference from the minimum is
nonnegative and vanishes at that time, so its derivative is zero. Thus
all active derivatives actually agree almost everywhere; the usual
minimum-active-derivative formula follows. Intersect with the full-measure
set on which the ODE holds. No differentiability in time of the rates is
used. The stated all-time UV bound avoids null-set quantifier problems.

Inside B_h the derivative is nonnegative. On the class outside K_h it is
>=delta_h. A solution starting in K_h cannot exit it before exiting B_h:
the affine minimum starts at M_h, is nondecreasing, and cannot exceed its
GLOBAL upper bound M_h. But K_h is strictly inside B_h, so an exit from
B_h is impossible as well.

Compact continuation can be proved directly here. If a maximal solution
in K_h had finite terminal time, uniform boundedness of the vector field
on K_h makes the trajectory Lipschitz and gives a limit x_* in K_h.
Local Caratheodory existence at that time and x_* extends it inside the
positive domain. This contradicts maximality. Hence such solutions are
global and remain positive. There is no assumption of prior boundedness
or global positivity at this step.

5. EVERY INITIAL POINT AND FINITE NONNESTED ABSORPTION

For each fixed x0>0, the same finite-slope coordinate argument puts x0 in
K_{h0} for every sufficiently small h0: zero is active at both c and x0,
and BOTH affine minima equal the same minimum zero offset. This first
gives global existence and positivity for every initial point, before
using the absorption argument. Mere inclusion of x0 in B_{h0} would not
have been sufficient; that weaker, circular route is not used.

Choose one small valid h_P depending on c and the common family data.
For x0 choose h0<=h_P with x0 in K_{h0}. Set

 h_{j+1}=min(sqrt(h_j),h_P).

The sequence reaches h_P after finitely many steps because -log h is
halved until its fixed target is reached. The old plateau is invariant
and K_{h_j} subset B_{sqrt(h_j)} subset B_{h_{j+1}}. Therefore, after
entry into K_{h_j}, the trajectory remains in the next box while trying
to enter the next plateau, even if K_{h_j} and K_{h_{j+1}} are not nested.

At the next scale the affine minimum increases at least delta_h outside
the next plateau and has finite available range on B_h. The explicit
bound is

 W_h=(M_h-min_{B_h}F_h)/delta_h.

If no earlier entry occurred, integration up to W_h forces F_h>=M_h;
the global cap and continuity imply F_h=M_h and entry at or before that
time. This also handles possible asymptotic approach without silently
assuming that a closed plateau is hit earlier. Every W_h is finite.
The finite sum of stage bounds is uniform over admitted paths. Finally
K_P=K_{h_P} intersection (c+S) is compact, convex, nonempty and positive,
and every solution in P enters it and remains there. Class invariance
follows by integrating the S-valued vector field.

An immediate strengthening is uniform entry on compact initial sets:
for a fixed positive compact D subset P, ||log x|| is uniformly bounded,
so one h0 works for every x0 in D. The same finite scale sequence and sum
of W_h then bound entry uniformly over both x0 in D and admitted paths.
This is a consequence of the audited mechanism, not a novelty assertion.

6. SPECIALIZATIONS AND COMPANION CHECKS

For tau_e(r)=y_e dot r, TE is ordinary endotacticity under w=-r: relevant
edges are the essential edges, minimizing r-source order is maximizing
w-source order, and positive r-projection is negative w-projection.
Scaling preserves signs and linear source order, so the cube covers every
direction. The condition concerns essential source extrema, not extrema
among all source complexes. The distinction is load-bearing near faces.

For weak reversibility, a negative edge from an essential source of
minimal r-order m has target below m. A directed return path must have a
first nonzero r-projection; all preceding projections are zero, so that
edge has a relevant source below m, a contradiction. Every essential
minimizer is therefore positive. This proves the weakly reversible
specialization without a single-linkage-class assumption.

For bounded measurable kappa_e(t)>0 and c_e(t) in a fixed bounded interval,
the modifier exp(c_e(t)(1+sum_i log(x_i)^2)^(alpha/2)), 0<alpha<1, changes
the valuation uniformly by at most

 [max |log kappa| + C(1+d L^2)^(alpha/2)]/L ->0, L=-log h.

Its x derivatives are uniformly bounded on positive compacts, so it
satisfies the stated local regularity. This validates the exposed rate
example even with independent measurable switching paths.

The companion nonseparability obstruction is also valid. At diagonal
exponents and gauge E(s,t)=|s-t|/100 off the diagonal, every active product
label in a separable sum must have s=t, because its distance from a
diagonal point is >=|s-t|/2. Distinct active slopes in either factor would
then contradict all-ties activity. At fixed h each factor has one active
slope value at every point of a connected interval; finite affine
continuity makes it locally constant, hence constant. The diagonal gauge
eta<(b-a)/2 cannot then approximate both endpoint exponents a,b. Thus no
finite separable sum works. This refutes a tempting compiler, not the
nonseparable lemma just audited.

The companion all-time-uniformity falsifier is exact: w_+=exp(-t), w_-=x
on 0<->X has the pointwise-in-time profiles 0,r and TE, but not UV.
Its solution (x0+t)exp(-t) tends to zero. This agrees with the blind
baseline's observation that UV excludes vanishing time prefactors.

7. FAILURE LEDGER AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

The independent baseline's failed direct entropy route remains a failure:
sum x_i(log x_i-1) is finite at coordinate boundaries, so its sublevels
alone need not be compact in the positive orthant. A moving-normal drift
argument near inactive reaction faces was not established either. Neither
is needed after the exposed affine reconstruction.

Checking only generic directions is false: 0->Y, 2Y->Y, X+Y->Y gives
x'=-xy, y'=1-y^2. Starting at y=1 yields x=x0 exp(-t). TE fails on the
face r=(r_x,0), r_x>0; discarding that face invalidates the criterion.
No numerical scan was used in this audit.

The optional activity-clock extension is not a premise of the main proof.
With a nonnegative locally integrable common multiplier A(t), the same
proof has drift A(t)delta_h; infinite total activity gives finite entry.
Uniform entry across clocks requires uniform divergence of their
cumulative integrals. Finite total activity is insufficient, as the
companion exact x'=exp(-t)(1-x) example shows. This is a routine conditional
time-change consequence, not an enlargement silently assumed by UV.
