## Legal input and acquisition boundary

Input comprises rational symmetric N-by-N S>=0 and W>0; an integer 0<=r<=N; rational gamma>0, 0<=epsilon<=gamma/4; requested rational 0<eta<1. The top r eigenvalues of A=SW must be at least gamma and all others at most epsilon. These are observable matrix inequalities: inertia of S-gamma W^-1 has r nonnegative and N-r negative eigenvalues, while inertia of S-epsilon W^-1 has r positive and N-r nonpositive eigenvalues. Thus threshold equality is admitted. At epsilon=0, the PSD condition supplies the nonpositive tail. Any failed test returns UNKNOWN.

For r=0 or N the empty/complete coordinate basis is direct after the tests. Below 1<=r<N. No eigenvectors, hidden space, source frame, condition bound or exact square root is supplied. ## Filter, exact bit growth and acquired condition bound

The identity W A=A^T W follows from S,W symmetry. Let H=W^1/2 S W^1/2; it is symmetric PSD, and A=W^-1/2 H W^1/2. Proof-only square roots are not computed. Compute

    Q0=A(A+(gamma/2)I)^-1,
    Q_(j+1)=3Q_j^2-2Q_j^3.

The low eigenvalues of Q0 lie in [0,1/3], and the high eigenvalues in [2/3,1]. For p(t)=3t^2-2t^3, p preserves [0,1], p(1-t)=1-p(t), and p is increasing there. The first error is at most 7/27. Since p(t)<=3t^2, subsequent errors satisfy

    delta_j=(1/3)(7/9)^(2^(j-1)), j>=1.

This is computable by delta1=7/27, delta_next=3delta^2. Let Pi be the orthogonal top projector of H and P=W^-1/2 Pi W^1/2. It is the generally oblique **right-eigenspace** projector of SW. Similarity gives ||Q_j-P||<=sqrt(kappa(W)) delta_j. In particular range P has dimension r and is the desired space.

Acquire integer K>=max(1,kappa(W)) from rational input. Both (tr W)^N/det W and tr(W)tr(W^-1) are valid upper bounds; the latter follows from lambda_max<=tr W and 1/lambda_min<=tr W^-1. Their ceiling/minimum is still a bound. Determinant/inverse/cofactor heights make log K polynomial in N and the input bit length. K is computational conditioning, unrelated to a latent aligned metric.

Set tau=2^(-2r)K^(-r)N^(-r) and choose j with

    delta_j<=min(1/(2K),eta tau/K).                    (1)

The iteration count is O(log(2+log(1/delta_target))). To avoid a per-entry denominator assumption, represent Q=Pnum/d with a **single positive denominator**. Then the next numerator is 3d Pnum^2-2Pnum^3 and denominator d^3, followed by common gcd reduction. If b bounds numerator and denominator bits,

    b_next<=3b+2ceil(log2 N)+O(1).                    (2)

Consequently b_j<=3^j[b_0+O(log N)]. Since 3^j is bounded by a fixed power of log(1/delta_target), all filter entries have polynomial bit size. Initial A/shift/inverse also have polynomial height by rational minors. Exact inverse/multiplication/LDL and gcd costs are charged; the iteration count alone is not a bit argument. No characteristic polynomial or algebraic field is expanded in this algorithm.

## Column acquisition and range proof

The nonzero singular values of P are at least 1/sqrt(K), by similarity's least singular values, and ||P||<=sqrt(K). (An idempotent in fact has nonzero singular values at least one; the weaker bound suffices.) With (1), ||Q-P||<=sqrt(K)delta<=1/(2sqrt(K)), so sigma_r(Q)>=1/(2sqrt(K)) and ||Q||<=2sqrt(K).

Choose r columns greedily by largest squared residual under exact rational Euclidean projection onto previously selected columns. A rank-k approximation to Q leaves operator norm at least sigma_(k+1)(Q). Its N columns therefore include a residual of length at least 1/(2sqrt(KN)). This makes every selected column independent and the selected B's volume at least [1/(2sqrt(KN))]^r. All its other singular values are at most 2sqrt(K), so

    sigma_min(B)>=2^(-2r+1)K^(-r+1/2)N^(-r/2)>=tau.   (3)

Let E be the ordinary orthogonal projector onto range P. Then ||(I-E)B||<=||Q-P||<=Kdelta. Dividing by sigma_min(B) bounds the directed range gap by Kdelta/tau<=eta. Equal dimensions make this the ordinary Euclidean projector-norm gap. This proof concerns a space, not a distinguished eigenbasis when high eigenvalues repeat.

For representation, selected columns may be separately reduced to primitive integer multiples before normalization; this does not change their range. The bound (3) is applied to the originally selected columns of Q and is not incorrectly asserted for arbitrarily rescaled columns. Gram/minor bounds keep greedy rational scores and normal-equation arithmetic polynomial in N,r,b_j. No exponentially large collection of minors is searched.

## Exact rational normalization

For any selected full-rank rational B, compute B^T B=L D L^T with exact positive LDL, and C=B L^-T. Its Gram is the positive diagonal D. For each d=D_ii, find integer k such that t=d4^k belongs to [1,4). Bit lengths and one exact comparison determine floor(log2 d), hence k; it does not require a logarithm oracle. Choose

    c=1        when 1<=t<=3/2,
    c=3/4      when 3/2<t<2,
    c=1/2      when 2<=t<4,
    s=2^k c.

Then d s^2 respectively lies in [1,3/2], (27/32,9/8), or [1/2,1). All endpoints and scalings are rational. Thus Y=C diag(s) preserves the acquired range and I/2<=Y^T Y<=3I/2. A pure power-of-two scale would fail, e.g. d=2/5: k=1,t=8/5,s=3/2 gives ds^2=9/10. LDL height and the dyadic exponent/mantissa have polynomial encoding in B's size. Constant relative normalization suffices regardless of eta.
