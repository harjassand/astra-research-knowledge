# Charged deterministic acquisition of the finite prime pools

7 October 2026; `rounding_prior`, verified GPT-6.1 Sol/max, no nested
workers. This is a consequence of the finite-capacity theorem in
`WEIGHTED_RESULTANT_TESTER.md` and the effective uniform Linnik theorem.
The prime acquisition is deterministic; the subsequent identity tester
is randomized. No historical novelty or practical runtime advantage is
asserted.

## Exact acquisition question

For integers k>=1, d>=0 and w>=2, put

    b=ceil(log2 w), B=ceil(log2(kw)), L=k+binom(k,2),
    U=max{k,6d(k^2+1)}, A=6Lb, D=6B.

Acquire a finite set Q of distinct primes q>U with
sum floor(log2 q)>=A. After drawing q, acquire a finite set S_q of
distinct primes p=1 mod q with sum floor(log2 p)>=D, and an element
gamma_p of order q in every used F_p. The main tester receives an exact
oracle supporting all these field parameters. Input bounds, circuit
expansion and oracle evaluation are separate charged obligations.

We prove that acquisition, including primality certification and order
construction, has deterministic bit cost polynomial in the **numerical**
d,k and in b. This is not polynomial in log d, log k or log q, and is
not a polynomial-in-circuit-size claim for succinct circuits whose
expanded degree, width or coefficient height can be exponential.

## An elementary ordinary-prime supply bound

For every integer n>=3,

    binom(2n,n)>=4^n/(2n+1),
    binom(2n,n)<=(2n)^{pi(2n)}.

The first inequality follows because the central coefficient is the
largest of the 2n+1 binomial coefficients summing to 4^n. For the second,
Legendre's formula gives

    v_p binom(2n,n)
      =sum_(j>=1)(floor(2n/p^j)-2floor(n/p^j))
      <=floor(log_p(2n)).

Each summand is 0 or 1 and vanishes for p^j>2n; hence each prime's
contribution p^{v_p} is <=2n. Since 2n+1<=2^n for n>=3 (induction),

    pi(2n)>=n/log2(2n).                                  (1)

For integers U,M>=1, set t=U+M and 2n=16t^2. Then n=8t^2>=3,
and log2(16t^2)=4+2log2 t<=8t. Equation1 gives pi(16t^2)>=t.
At most U primes are <=U, so there are at least M distinct primes in

    (U,16(U+M)^2].                                      (2)

This deliberately loose supply bound is proved here; no asymptotic
prime-number theorem or unspecified effective density estimate is used.

Set l_q=floor(log2(U+1))>=1 and M_q=ceil(A/l_q).
Enumerating integers above U, retaining certified primes until capacity
A is reached, terminates by

    q_max <= 16(U+M_q)^2=:Q_max.                        (3)

Retaining every encountered prime cannot hurt the lower capacity.
Enumeration may stop with fewer than M_q primes. Trial division gives
an explicit deterministic certification algorithm whose work is at most
O(Q_max^{3/2} polylog Q_max) bit operations with schoolbook arithmetic.
The inequality is a safe upper bound, not a claim of optimal complexity.

## Many progression primes from one effective least-prime theorem

The primary prerequisite is Triantafyllos Xylouris,
[On Linnik's constant, arXiv0906.2749v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2749v1),
15 June 2009. In the saved PDF, printed p6 defines P(m) as the maximum
of the least prime in a mod m over **all** gcd(a,m)=1. Theorem1.1,
printed p9, states P(m)<<m^{5.2} with effectively computable implied
constant. Printed p5 specifies effectivity of implicit constants in
Chapters2–4; §1.3 Eq1.5, p7, states the classical theorem with effective
absolute positive constants. The final proof is Chapter4, pp69–74.

We use only this consequence: there are absolute effectively computable
C>=1 and an absolute integer Lambda=6 such that, for every m>=2 and
every gcd(a,m)=1, there is a prime p=a mod m with

    p <= C m^Lambda.                                    (4)

Any finite small-modulus exceptions can be absorbed in C. We have read
the theorem, conventions and final proof reduction, not independently
reproved or numerically audited the complete analytic-number-theory
proof and its source tables. The theorem's C is not numerically
instantiated here. The enumerator below does not need C as an input;
Equation4 proves a polynomial bound on its stopping time.

Fix prime q and an integer M>=1. Acquire an auxiliary prime

    ell>V:=max(q,M),   ell<=16(V+1)^2,                  (5)

using Equation2 with U=V and M=1. In particular ell!=q. For each
t=1,...,M, the Chinese remainder theorem gives a residue a_t modulo
m=q ell with

    a_t=1 mod q,    a_t=t mod ell.

Since 1<=t<=M<ell and q!=ell, gcd(a_t,m)=1. The M residues are distinct.
Equation4 supplies M corresponding primes p_t<=C(q ell)^Lambda.
They are pairwise distinct because they have different residues mod ell;
every p_t is 1 mod q. Thus

    # {prime p<=C[16q(max(q,M)+1)^2]^Lambda : p=1 mod q} >= M. (6)

This is a derived finite multiplicity guarantee. It does **not** invoke
a separate uniform many-primes theorem, average-modulus result or GRH.
Reusing the same residue cannot supply multiplicity, which is why the
extra coprime CRT modulus and distinct t values matter.

For the required capacity D, let l_p=floor(log2(q+1))>=1 and
M_p(q)=ceil(D/l_p). Every prime p=1 mod q is >=q+1, so M_p(q) such
primes have capacity at least D. Deterministically enumerate p=jq+1
for j=1,2,..., certify primality, and stop once capacity D is reached.
Equation6 with M=M_p(q) proves termination at

    p_max(q)<=C[16q(max(q,M_p(q))+1)^2]^Lambda.          (7)

The implementation can use this simple progression stream. It does
not have to explicitly search the CRT classes; the auxiliary construction
proves a uniform bound for the ordinary stream. Trial division including
all rejected candidates is at most O(p_max(q)^{3/2} polylog p_max(q))
bit operations, again an intentionally loose bound.

For a certified p, test h=2,3,...,p-1 until gamma=h^{(p-1)/q}!=1.
Such h exists because the multiplicative group of F_p is cyclic and
the displayed map has image of size q. Gamma^q=1, and q prime with
gamma!=1 certifies order exactly q. At most p-2 modular power tests
are needed, costing polynomial in numerical p and log p. An order
certificate is simply 1<gamma<p and gamma^q=1 mod p. No factorization
of p-1 or supplied primitive generator is needed.

## Complete asymptotic and oracle boundary

Equations3 and7 imply that numerical q,p and all deterministic work
above are polynomial in d,k,b; Lambda and C are absolute constants.
B<=b+ceil(log2 k), L<=k^2, and M_p<=6B ensure this directly.
The maximum field bit width is O(log(d+1)+log(k+1)+log(b+1)), with an
absolute constant contribution from C. This is an asymptotic bound;
we do not turn the uninstantiated C into a concrete useful field limit.

Only the p-pool of the sampled q needs acquisition for one query.
Materializing all Q pools also costs polynomial in d,k,b, but spends
more time and storage. Across repetitions, cached certificates may be
reused; fresh random samples are still required for the independent
soundness statement. The fixed integer target and certified bounds must
be independent of the sampled q,p. An adversarially changing coefficient
lift is outside the theorem.

Add the time to read and validate the actual input descriptions/promises,
perform any condensation/expansion, and evaluate Eval. The w premise
bounds f_j,h_j,D_jt, not the individual g_j coefficients. A supplied
circuit can therefore contain large constants not accounted for by b;
their reads/reductions and all scalar operations must be added. The
theorem is polynomial overhead plus the charged oracle cost, under the
parameterized condensed-sum interface. Testing rational circuit P/P'
also requires source-specific denominator handling. A fixed-field oracle,
exact real oracle or floating-point evaluator cannot silently replace it.

The single-large-prime alternative log p>=6B supplies capacity without
a pool, but its width is Omega(B). The construction here can keep each
field width logarithmic in B by purchasing total capacity across many
distinct primes. It improves this width guarantee, not automatically
total runtime; the acquisition exponent is large and controls with
explicit coefficients may decide the toy input much more cheaply.

## Decisive consequence and next gate

The conditional finite-pool tester can now be composed with a deterministic
acquisition procedure with polynomial numerical-parameter overhead. The
recent paper's good-q/density promise and q>2kw numerical-height threshold
are not required by this composed condensed-sum theorem. The resultant
argument and effective Linnik prerequisite are older standard methods;
historical novelty of the exact interface remains UNVERIFIED.

loose asymptotic bounds as timings. Its field-width comparison with the
