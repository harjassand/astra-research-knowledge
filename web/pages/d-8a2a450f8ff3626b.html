# Cycle 2: attack on the common-weight extinction certificate

**Verdict.** A common positive-weight contraction gives a valid, globally-in-time *upper bound* on mean at-risk burden under arbitrary switching among the covered rate matrices. Combining it with conditional establishment caps and a union bound gives a valid relapse-risk envelope, including bounded sources. It does not imply that true relapse risk decreases with pulse length. A finite assay supports a dose-minimal duration only relative to a stated confidence set, a closed list of risk-relevant states, bounded source rates, a verified uniform contraction, and a monotone certificate on the permitted duration window. Without these, report `UNKNOWN`. The mathematics below is reconstructed; no clinical or experimental result has been generated.

## Uniform switched contraction

Let $m(t)\in\mathbb R^k_{\ge 0}$ be expected counts of all tracked at-risk states in a branching or other expectation-closed model, and let

\[
\dot m(t)=A_{\theta(t),u(t)}m(t)+s(t),
\]

where $A$ is Metzler (nonnegative off-diagonal entries), $\theta(t)$ represents uncertain or evolving rates, $u(t)$ is the treatment input, and $s(t)\ge0$ is immigration into the tracked states. Suppose there is a **single** vector $w\gg0$ and $\delta>0$ such that, for every admissible model, treatment, and time,

\[
w^\top A_{\theta,u}\le -\delta w^\top\quad\text{componentwise},
\qquad w^\top s(t)\le S(t).
\tag{1}
\]

For $V(t)=w^\top m(t)$, positivity and (1) give $\dot V\le-\delta V+S(t)$. Thus

\[
V(T)\le V(0)e^{-\delta T}+\int_0^T e^{-\delta(T-r)}S(r)\,dr.
\tag{2}
\]

This is a direct comparison argument. It remains valid under arbitrarily fast switching because (1) holds pointwise for every active matrix. For a finite family or polytope of rate matrices, checking the vertices suffices; for general uncertainty sets, each column requires the robust optimization $\sup_{A,\theta,u}(w^\top A)_j\le-\delta w_j$. Normalize $\sum_iw_i=1$ and search for $w,\delta$ by linear feasibility/bisection when the uncertainty constraints are linear. An elementwise upper matrix is a sufficient conservative check when it preserves the Metzler comparison.

The matrix must include birth/death and within-list phenotype or genotype transitions. For example, a one-for-one switch contributes a negative diagonal loss and a positive off-diagonal gain. Mutation that creates tracked at-risk cells belongs in $s$ or in the appropriate offspring matrix. A source of cells outside the tracked list is not covered until bounded and added. This common linear copositive Lyapunov condition is standard positive switched-systems machinery, not a cancer-specific novelty ([Wu & Sun, *Journal of the Franklin Institute* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfranklin.2013.12.018)). Failure to find $w$ only means this particular certificate failed; it does not establish that every allowed schedule is unsafe.

The pointwise requirement is material. For

\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&10\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\10&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]

each fixed mode is stable. But alternating $A_1,A_2$ for $0.5$ time units each multiplies the dominant component by approximately $9.92$ per cycle: with $x=10(0.5)=5$, the two-mode product has spectral radius

\[
e^{-1}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{2}+x\sqrt{1+x^2/4}\right)\approx9.92>1.
\]

Consequently, “each measured treatment mode shrinks the mean” is not enough if the rate/state composition can switch. The robust inequality has to cover every permitted active mode or use a separately justified dwell-time/mode-dependent certificate.

## Turning burden into an establishment-risk bound

At pulse end, suppose a type-$i$ cell has conditional probability at most $q_i^U$ of establishing under the continuation regimen. Then the union bound gives a terminal-lineage risk at most

\[
\sum_iq_i^Um_i(T)\le c_TV(T),\qquad c_T=\max_i q_i^U/w_i.
\]

Suppose escape-founder events from tracked state $i$ occur at predictable rate at most $\lambda_i(t)X_i(t)$, and an escape founder has establishment probability at most $q_{E,i}^U$. If $\sum_i\lambda_i(t)q_{E,i}^U X_i\le\kappa V(t)$ for every nonnegative state vector (sufficiently, $\lambda_iq_{E,i}^U\le\kappa w_i$), their total risk is at most $\kappa\int_0^T V(t)dt$. Add $h(t)$ for direct successful-founder hazard from an external population that is *not already counted* in $m$ or $s$. A second union bound yields

\[
P(\text{establishment})\le c_TV(T)+\kappa\int_0^T V(t)dt+\int_0^T h(t)dt.
\tag{3}
\]

No independence between routes is needed for this upper bound. Do not count a transition twice: a mutation entering a tracked state belongs in (2), while a founder outside that state list belongs in $h$ or an explicit additional state. If $S(t)\le\bar S$ and $h(t)\le\bar h$, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(T)&\le V_0e^{-\delta T}+\frac{\bar S}{\delta}(1-e^{-\delta T}),\\
\int_0^TV(t)dt&\le\frac{V_0}{\delta}(1-e^{-\delta T})+\frac{\bar S}{\delta}\left[T-\frac{1-e^{-\delta T}}{\delta}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Equations (3)–(4) are globally valid only relative to their stated process, confidence set, and source/establishment caps. A persistent source generally leaves a nonzero burden floor and can keep creating escape opportunities; a source cap that is merely guessed is not a certificate.

## The sharp monotonicity condition—and its limits

The simplest scalar envelope for a fixed-dose pulse is

\[
U(T)=a e^{-\delta T}+\frac b\delta(1-e^{-\delta T})+cT,
\tag{5}
\]

where $a$ bounds initial terminal-establishment risk, $b$ bounds escape-founder risk flux from an exponentially contracting tracked population, and $c$ bounds a separate persistent successful-source hazard. Here $a$ has units of risk; $b,c$ have units of risk per time. Direct differentiation gives

\[
U'(T)=(b-\delta a)e^{-\delta T}+c.
\]

For the envelope itself, these are sharp conditions:

- On an allowed finite window $[0,T_{\max}]$, $U$ is non-increasing exactly when $\delta a\ge b$ and $c\le(\delta a-b)e^{-\delta T_{\max}}$.
- On all $T\ge0$, it is non-increasing exactly when $c=0$ and $\delta a\ge b$ (strictly decreasing when $\delta a>b$).
- If $c>0$, the envelope eventually turns upward; if $\delta a<b$, it rises from the start. Longer exposure therefore cannot be assigned a universal benefit.

For $c=0$, put $F=b/\delta$. If $F<\epsilon<a$, the shortest duration certified by (5) to meet risk target $\epsilon$ is

\[
T_{\rm cert}=\frac1\delta\log\frac{a-F}{\epsilon-F}.
\tag{6}
\]

Under the monotone case $a\ge F$, if $\epsilon\ge a$, zero duration already passes; if $\epsilon<F$, no finite duration passes; if $\epsilon=F<a$, the bound reaches the floor only asymptotically; and if $F<\epsilon<a$, use (6). For $c>0$, on a finite window satisfying the monotonicity condition, the first crossing can be found by bisection. For example, the *illustrative, unfitted* values $a=.20$, $b=.001\;\mathrm{day}^{-1}$, $\delta=.05\;\mathrm{day}^{-1}$, $c=.001\;\mathrm{day}^{-1}$ make (5) decrease only until $T=\delta^{-1}\log((\delta a-b)/c)\approx43.9$ days; its upper envelope later rises. This is a fixture, not a biological estimate.

For assay-derived confidence limits, a conservative scalar certificate can use $a\le a_U$, $b\le b_U$, $\delta\ge\delta_L>0$, and $c\le c_U$ in (5), giving

\[
U_{\rm assay}(T)=a_Ue^{-\delta_LT}+\frac{b_U}{\delta_L}(1-e^{-\delta_LT})+c_UT.
\]

Its finite-window monotonicity condition is $\delta_La_U\ge b_U$ and $c_U\le(\delta_La_U-b_U)e^{-\delta_LT_{\max}}$. This supports a **minimum certifiable duration** for the specified fixed-dose pulse if its first crossing exists. It is not proof that actual risk is monotone or that no other schedule uses less drug. Calling it a genuinely dose-minimal treatment requires a validated exact/identified risk or lower bounds showing every shorter duration violates the target, plus optimization over the candidate schedules. A one-sided upper bound alone cannot prove that claim. If efficacy/burden and the risk envelope are both monotone, the certified duration is the larger of their first-crossing times; otherwise optimize the whole feasible duration set.

## What a finite assay can and cannot certify

Let $D$ be the assay data and $\Theta_D$ a joint $1-\alpha$ confidence set for initial absolute clone counts, birth/death and switching rates, mutation/immigration supply, post-pulse establishment caps, and the allowed time variation. If (1) and the source caps hold for every $\theta\in\Theta_D$, then the risk statement has at least the assay's stated coverage, subject to the sampling model. Point estimates inserted into (5) do not give this guarantee. The admissible set must include all plausible switching matrices and treatment concentrations; if no uniform $\delta_L>0$ can be supported, the common-weight certificate has no positive contraction claim.

A zero-detection assay only bounds prevalence under its sampling assumptions. With $N_{\rm eff}$ iid effective independent cells, per-cell detection sensitivity $\eta$, and no detections, the one-sided $1-\alpha$ prevalence limit is

\[
q_U=\min\left(1,\frac{1-\alpha^{1/N_{\rm eff}}}{\eta}\right).
\]

At $N_{\rm eff}=10^6$, $\eta=1$, and 95% confidence, $q_U\approx3.0\times10^{-6}$. In a tumor of $10^9$ cells, this still permits roughly 3,000 undetected cells in expectation under uniform sampling. This is not a cap on an unsampled spatial refuge, an unrecognized state, or a newly generated mutant. If hidden clones have no finite count/rate bound or are absent from the state list, risk can be arbitrarily high relative to the certificate; a finite bulk assay alone cannot certify extinction. Sequencing read count is not automatically $N_{\rm eff}$: molecule independence, assay sensitivity, purity, copy number, barcode dropout/collision, and spatial sampling need calibration.

The assay also consumes time. If the treatment or sensitive reservoir continues evolving during turnaround $\tau$, add that interval to source generation and cumulative dose, or measure a post-delay state. Costs to report include assayed cells and independent cultures, sequencing molecules and reads, single-cell/lineage imaging, state/genotype validation, estimation uncertainty, assay latency, exposure while waiting, and pulse dose. These limits matter before any clinical translation.

## Unlinked response marginals versus paired clone-by-drug data

The previous exact two-clone construction provides a clean comparison. Two equally frequent clones have either same-ranked sensitivities

\[
R_{\rm same}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-a&-a\end{pmatrix}
\]

or split sensitivities

\[
R_{\rm split}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-a\\-a&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Each drug has the same unlinked marginal responses $\{0,-a\}$ in both cases. At $a=2$ and total exposure $x_A+x_B=1$, monotherapy leaves burden $B_M=(1+e^{-2})/2\approx0.5677$. Equal exposure split leaves $B_{\rm same}=0.5677$ in the same-ranked world and $B_{\rm split}=e^{-1}\approx0.3679$ in the complementary world. The log-burden advantage in the split world is $g=\log\cosh(1)\approx0.434$.

Under only unlinked marginals, the worst-case coupling is the comonotone pairing. With a fixed combination overhead $\lambda>0$ charged in log-burden units, the minimax action is monotherapy: splitting ties its burden in the same-ranked world and adds $\lambda$. Paired data can support the split in the complementary world precisely when $\lambda<g$. But across both worlds, the *worst-case absolute burden* remains $0.5677$ even after pairing: the same-ranked world still selects monotherapy. Paired data improve the split-world decision and can reduce worst-case regret; they do not reduce this two-world minimax burden. If no prior probability over worlds is justified, do not report an expected value of paired information. Include assay cost and delay before claiming net value.

For finite empirical marginals, the robust objective is

\[
\min_x\;\sup_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_J)}R(x;\pi)+C_{\rm dose}(x)+C_{\rm combo}(x),
\]

where $\Pi$ is the set of all clone pairings compatible with measured drug-wise marginals. This is a finite optimal-transport linear program when the supports are finite. Linkage is unnecessary if the same action wins for every compatible coupling, or if the controller is willing to retain the minimax envelope. Linkage is decision-relevant only when compatible couplings reverse the action ranking and the potential reduction in regret/robust risk exceeds its cells, sequencing, turnaround, and exposure cost. If response transitions or mutation rates also differ across couplings, response-pairing data alone are insufficient; those parameters need clone-linked estimates or adverse bounds.

## Prior-art verdict and discriminating test

The extinction-scheduling mechanism has direct primary prior art. Patil et al. (2026) analytically model and simulate two-strike therapy, study switching near the tumor nadir, high-extinction windows, demographic/evolutionary parameters, and unequal drug efficacy ([*Genetics*](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/232/2/iyaf255/8343559)). Dabi et al. (2026) compare sequential two-strike extinction therapy with combination therapy and examine evolutionary rescue under treatment ([*PLOS Computational Biology*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014803)). Multitype branching under timed/pulsed cancer therapy is older prior art ([Foo & Michor 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/)); cancer-specific branching with phenotype switching and extinction probabilities is also established ([Gunnarsson et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/)). Barcode lineage inference for evolving phenotypic drug resistance is established ([Whiting et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7)). General common copositive certificates for positive switched systems are established as noted above. Accordingly, neither extinction scheduling, clone-response pairing, switching-aware branching, nor the common-weight inequality is a novelty claim. A narrow new contribution would require a source-calibrated, confidence-qualified risk certificate that changes a matched schedule decision and validates on held-out outcomes; novelty/priority for that composite remains `UNKNOWN`.

A discriminating preclinical test would seed known barcoded resistant/tolerant clone counts into an isogenic sensitive background, estimate division/death, state transitions, mutation supply, and continuation establishment caps on training replicates, then freeze the common-weight certificate and pulse duration. In blinded holdouts compare it with (i) the equally informed unlinked-marginal minimax controller and (ii) the best bulk-growth-only schedule, matching information, cumulative exposure, and follow-up; add the clone-paired controller only with measured assay/latency cost. The primary result is the fraction of holdout replicates with barcode-confirmed rebound versus each predeclared upper-risk interval at matched dose. A falsifier is any systematic undercoverage of the bound, failure of the common contraction on held-out rate estimates, or no reduction in dose at the same prespecified false-safe rate relative to the matched baseline. No assay, fit, simulation, or validation was performed for this note.
