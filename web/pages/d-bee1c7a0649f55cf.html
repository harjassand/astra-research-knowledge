INDEPENDENT PROOF AUDIT: COMMUTING ZERO-SPECTRUM EXTENSION
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNAL PASS on the requested operator, exposure-weight, and mixed-component steps; not a proof certificate for the entire theorem.

Scope checked
-------------
I independently reconstructed the zero-spectrum resolvent Jensen limit, bounded POVM transport within each von Neumann algebra, the conditional exposure normalization, and the support-component barycenter/mixture estimate in the L02 proof. I did not repeat the prior-art review. No failure was found in these steps. The key distinction is that two different limits occur: the Jensen comparison passes in operator norm, while the regularized Radon-Nikodym operators used for algebra membership converge only strongly in general.

1. Resolvent Jensen inequality at zero
--------------------------------------
Let H be any Hilbert space; let A_1,...,A_n be bounded positive operators on H; let q_l>=0 with sum q_l=1; set B=sum_l q_l A_l and D_l=A_l-B. No commutation among the A_l is assumed. Define

  F_T : H -> L2((0,infinity),ds) tensor H,
  (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^(-1)h,
  f(t)=t log(t), f(0)=0.

The exact lemma needed by exposure is the operator-order inequality

  sum_l q_l (F_A_l-F_B)^*(F_A_l-F_B)
       <= sum_l q_l f(A_l)-f(B).                         (J)

It holds for bounded positive operators, including kernels and continuous spectrum at zero. The domain of every resolvent in the proof is H and its parameter s is strictly positive; no T^(-1), support inverse, trace, or finite-dimensional compression occurs.

Proof check. First replace A_l and B by A_l^eps=A_l+eps I and B^eps=B+eps I, eps>0. D_l is unchanged, and B^eps and every A_l^eps are bounded below by eps I. Write R_l(s)=(A_l^eps+sI)^(-1) and R_B(s)=(B^eps+sI)^(-1). The second resolvent identity, with the order displayed, is

  R_l=R_B-R_B D_l R_B+R_B D_l R_l D_l R_B.

Using f(t)=int_0^infinity [t/(1+s)-t/(t+s)]ds and
A(A+sI)^(-1)=I-s(A+sI)^(-1), the linear term cancels because sum q_l D_l=0. Thus

  sum_l q_l f(A_l^eps)-f(B^eps)
    = int_0^infinity s R_B(s)[sum_l q_l D_l R_l(s)D_l]R_B(s) ds.    (1)

The integrand is positive. The resolvent difference gives
F_A_l^eps-F_B^eps=s R_A_l(s)D_lR_B(s), so its Gram integrand is

  s^2 R_B D_l R_l^2 D_l R_B.

Since s R_l^2<=R_l by scalar spectral calculus, this is bounded above by the corresponding integrand in (1), by congruence with D_l and R_B. For fixed eps, the integrals converge in operator norm: near s=0, the remainder is O(s) because all resolvents have norm at most 1/eps; at infinity it is O(s^(-2)). This proves (J) for the shifted family. The integral representation has a nonintegrable-looking linear term if considered alone, so the cancellation across l must be made before integrating; (1) is the integrable, positive remainder.

The shift can be removed in norm. For every scalar t>=0,

  ||F_(t+eps)-F_t||_L2^2
    =eps^2 int_0^infinity s^2/[(t+eps+s)^2(t+s)^2] ds
    <=eps^2 int_0^infinity (eps+s)^(-2)ds=eps.

The spectral theorem therefore gives ||F_(T+eps I)-F_T||<=sqrt(eps) as an operator H -> L2 tensor H. Consequently each difference Gram converges in operator norm. Since f is continuous on [0,M+1] for every common finite bound M on the A_l, f(A_l+eps I)->f(A_l) and f(B+eps I)->f(B) in operator norm. Let eps decrease to zero in the shifted inequality. This proves (J) with zero spectrum present.

Convergence boundary: for T=M_x on L2([0,1]), R_eps=sqrt(T)(T+eps I)^(-1/2) converges strongly to I but not in norm: its multiplier sqrt(x/(x+eps)) approaches 0 near x=0 for every eps, while its pointwise limit is 1 for x>0. Thus the proof must not upgrade the separate transport limit below to norm convergence. This does not affect the norm-safe Jensen limit, which concerns F_(T+eps)-F_T and has the explicit sqrt(eps) bound.

2. Bounded transport and strong algebra closure
-----------------------------------------------
Let A be a concrete von Neumann algebra on H, T in A satisfy 0<=T<=I, and G_a in A be positive with sum_a G_a=T. Then G_a<=T. On ran(sqrt(T)) define

  q_a(sqrt(T)u,sqrt(T)v)=<u,G_a v>.

This is well-defined: T u=0 implies <u,G_a u>=0 and hence G_a^(1/2)u=0. Cauchy-Schwarz for the positive form G_a gives |q_a|<=||sqrt(T)u|| ||sqrt(T)v||. It extends to closure(ran(sqrt(T)))=P_T H and defines a positive contraction C_a supported on P_T, with

  G_a=sqrt(T) C_a sqrt(T),   sum_a C_a=P_T.               (2)

For eps>0, put R_eps=sqrt(T)(T+eps I)^(-1/2). Then ||R_eps||<=1 and R_eps converges strongly to P_T by bounded spectral convergence of sqrt(t/(t+eps)). The regularization

  (T+eps I)^(-1/2)G_a(T+eps I)^(-1/2)=R_eps C_a R_eps

belongs to A because it is expressed using T,G_a in A. Since C_a=P_T C_a P_T and R_eps->P_T strongly with a uniform norm bound, R_eps C_a R_eps->C_a strongly. Strong closure of A gives C_a in A. The equality in (2) uses no unbounded T^(-1/2) operator.

Let K=L2((0,infinity),ds) and define V_T:H->K tensor H by (V_T h)(s)=sqrt(T)(T+sI)^(-1)h. It is a bounded partial isometry: V_T^*V_T=P_T because int_0^infinity t/(t+s)^2 ds is 1 for t>0 and 0 at t=0. The pointwise operator norm of the kernel can diverge as s decreases to zero; its vector-valued map is nevertheless well-defined and bounded by this integral identity. Also F_T=V_T sqrt(T). For D in A', V_T D=(I_K tensor D)V_T. Thus

  Q_a=V_T C_a V_T^* + 1_{a=a_*}(I-V_TV_T^*)

is a POVM in B(K) bar-tensor A: it commutes with I_K tensor A', which characterizes that von Neumann tensor product. Its pullback is exact, F_T^*Q_aF_T=G_a. At T=0, P_T=V_T=C_a=0 and the default outcome is I.

For Alice and Bob, use different L2 registers K_s,K_t. If A and B are commuting algebras and their effects are in A and B respectively, the transported algebras B(K_s) bar-tensor A and B(K_t) bar-tensor B on K_s tensor K_t tensor H commute. The joint vector

  Psi_(T,S)(s,t)=T(T+sI)^(-1)S(S+tI)^(-1)psi

has squared norm <psi,TS psi>; two applications of the exact pullback give <Psi,Q_a R_b Psi>=<psi,G_a K_b psi>. This holds because the ancillas are separate and every Alice coefficient commutes with every Bob coefficient. No factorization of A and B on H is asserted.

3. Conditional exposure weights and Jensen telescopes
------------------------------------------------------
Fix a repeated commuting strategy, its generated commuting von Neumann algebras A,B, a tested set C of size |C|<k, and E_C=the event that all tested coordinates are won. Write p=Pr(E_C)>0, m=k-|C|, d=|A_ans||B_ans|, d_C=d^|C|, L=log(d_C/p), and tau=L/m. For live coordinate i, reveal

  r0=(X_C,Y_C,X_<i,Y_>i)

under the original product-pair question law, with distribution nu_i; the fresh pair z=(x,y) is independent with law mu. For answer strings alpha,gamma on C, set theta(r0,alpha,gamma)=1 exactly when those tested answers pass. Define local coarse effects F_x^alpha=E[M_X^alpha | r0,X_i=x] and H_y^gamma=E[N_Y^gamma | r0,Y_i=y], and conditional means F_y^alpha=E_(x|y)F_x^alpha, H_x^gamma=E_(y|x)H_y^gamma. All are finite conditional averages of effects in A or B, hence remain effects in the same algebras.

The correct unnormalized transcript weight is

  w_i(r0,alpha,gamma)=nu_i(r0) theta(r0,alpha,gamma),
  w_ir=w_i(r)/m.

It is independent of the fresh pair, but it is generally not a probability distribution when used alone. Under the two-register transport, let f_ir(z)=Psi_(F_x^alpha,H_y^gamma), a_ir(x)=Psi_(F_x^alpha,H_x^gamma), and b_ir(y)=Psi_(F_y^alpha,H_y^gamma). Then ||f_ir(z)||^2=chi(F_x^alpha,H_y^gamma), where chi(F,H)=<psi,FH psi>. Conditional independence of the two full question strings given r0,x,y and the exact pullback imply the exact identities

  sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)||f_ir(z)||^2=p,
  sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)<f_ir(z),W_ir(z)f_ir(z)>=p sbar,

where W_ir(z)=sum_(a,b) V(z,a,b)Q_(r,x)^aR_(r,y)^b is an effect and sbar is the conditional mean live-coordinate win fraction given E_C. The probability law of the transcript and fresh pair is proportional to w_i(r)mu(z)||f_ir(z)||^2, not w_i(r) alone. Dividing by m and summing i normalizes the total mass exactly to p. This is the normalization needed later; treating answer-mask weights as probabilities would lose the vector-norm likelihood.

For B0 in the Bob algebra, A0->chi(A0,B0)=<sqrt(B0)psi,A0 sqrt(B0)psi> is a normal positive functional on A with mass g=chi(I,B0)<=1. For an effect F in A, let h=chi(F,B0). Scalar spectral-measure Jensen, applied to t log t on [0,1], gives

  -chi(F log F,B0)<=h log(g/h)<=-h log h,

with the value 0 at h=0. The reversed statement holds on B. Summing over the d_C answer-string pairs and using concavity of -t log t gives each exposure potential U_j,Z_j in [-pL,0]. This uses neither a trace nor a density matrix.

At each reveal, conditional Jensen (J), evaluated in the appropriate positive normal functional, controls the squared distance between the fine and conditional-mean descriptions. Explicitly,

  U_i-U_(i-1)=E_(nu_i mu_Y) sum_(alpha,gamma)theta
       chi(E_(x|y)f(F_x^alpha)-f(F_y^alpha),H_y^gamma)>=0,
  Z_(i-1)-Z_i=E_(nu_i mu_X) sum_(alpha,gamma)theta
       chi(F_x^alpha,E_(y|x)f(H_y^gamma)-f(H_x^gamma))>=0,

where here f(t)=t log t. Integrating the resolvent vectors identifies these Jensen gaps as upper bounds for E||f_ir-b_ir||^2 and E||f_ir-a_ir||^2, respectively. Telescoping U and Z across m reveals costs at most pL each. Thus

  sum_ir,z w_ir mu(z)[||f_ir(z)-a_ir(x)||^2+||f_ir(z)-b_ir(y)||^2]
       <=2pL/m=2p tau.                                   (3)

Weights remain unnormalized throughout; both (3) and the mass/success equations are exact. Zero-norm vectors have zero likelihood and need no conditional distribution. Zero-probability x|y or y|x branches are omitted.

4. Mixed-support component barycenter
-------------------------------------
Let the positive-support pair graph have components j with masses lambda_j and conditional laws mu_j. Each local positive-mass question identifies its component uniquely: two positive pairs sharing that question are adjacent. On L2(mu_j), let P_X,P_Y be conditional-expectation projections, K_j=(P_X+P_Y)/2, and sigma_j the least eigenvalue of I-K_j on the mean-zero subspace; use convention sigma_j=1 for a singleton component, and sigma=min_j sigma_j. Since the support is finite and each nonsingleton graph is connected, sigma>0 and sigma<=1.

For every vector-valued f on a component, the scalar spectral gap applies to f by expanding its finite value span in an orthonormal basis. Orthogonal-projection identities give

  sigma_j Var_(mu_j)(f)
    <=[E||f-P_X f||^2+E||f-P_Y f||^2]/2
    <=E[||f-a(x)||^2+||f-b(y)||^2]/2

for any local approximants a(x),b(y); conditional means minimize these squared errors. Apply this to each f_ir, sum with lambda_j w_ir and use (3). If

  beta_ir,j=E_(mu_j) f_ir(z),
  P_j=sum_ir w_ir E_(mu_j)||f_ir||^2,
  E_j=sum_ir w_ir Var_(mu_j)(f_ir),
  P_hat_j=sum_ir w_ir||beta_ir,j||^2,

then P_hat_j=P_j-E_j, sum_j lambda_j P_j=p, and sum_j lambda_j E_j<=p tau/sigma.

The barycenter is implementable as a legal commuting strategy, not merely a vector approximation. For P_hat_j>0, use the state

  P_hat_j^(-1/2) sum_ir sqrt(w_ir)|ir> tensor beta_ir,j

on a finite central flag register times the common transported Hilbert space. Both parties use POVMs block diagonal in the same |ir> basis. Their cross commutators vanish in each block by separate-register transport; the shared flag is central. The state and flag law are independent of the fresh pair sampled from mu_j. Thus its success is T_hat_j/P_hat_j, so T_hat_j<=v_j P_hat_j where v_j is the commuting value of the component game. If P_hat_j=0, positivity implies T_hat_j=0. The norm comparison for an effect W,

  |<f,Wf>-<beta,W beta>|
       <=(||f||+||beta||)||f-beta||,

and weighted Cauchy-Schwarz yield T_j<=v_jP_j+2sqrt(P_jE_j). Summing and using sum lambda_j v_j<=v gives a base value term, but the component law in the conditioned experiment is not lambda: it is

  lambda_tilde_j=lambda_j P_j/p
        =m^(-1)sum_i Pr[J_i=j | E_C].

This distinction is essential. Under the unconditioned law the live component tuple has law lambda^(tensor m). The conditional law of the full question tuple given E_C has likelihood ratio Pr(E_C|questions)/p<=1/p. Data processing therefore gives D(P_(J_1,...,J_m | E_C)||lambda^(tensor m))<=log(1/p). The chain rule implies this joint relative entropy is at least the sum of the marginal divergences; convexity then gives D(lambda_tilde||lambda)<=log(1/p)/m. With natural logarithms and TV=(1/2)||.||_1, Pinsker gives

  TV(lambda_tilde,lambda)<=sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m)).

Since 0<=v_j<=1, changing component weights changes their mean value by at most this TV. Dividing the summed success inequality by p gives the exact conditional estimate

  sbar<=v+sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m))+2sqrt(tau/sigma).          (4)

For connected support, lambda_tilde=lambda and the drift term is exactly zero. For disconnected support it cannot simply be dropped; conditioning on E_C can bias component labels.

5. Constants retained from the requested step
----------------------------------------------
For the disconnected case choose T0=sigma delta^2/128. If tau<=T0, the drift in (4) is at most sqrt(T0/2)<=delta/16 (using sigma<=1), and the exposure term is at most 2sqrt(T0/sigma)=delta/sqrt(32). Their sum is strictly below delta/4. The scalar tested-set selection then uses eta=delta/16, r=log((v+delta)/(v+delta/2)), R=log(d/(v+delta)), and

  c=delta T0/[16(1+log d)]=sigma delta^3/[2048(1+log d)].

For connected support choose T0=sigma delta^2/64. The drift is absent, 2sqrt(T0/sigma)=delta/4, and the same selection gives

  c=delta T0/[16(1+log d)]=sigma delta^3/[1024(1+log d)].

These are the exact constants in the L02 tail claim. The endpoint delta->1-v at fixed k is valid because for delta sufficiently close to 1-v, ceil((v+delta)k)=k; it gives the all-wins event. The requested operator/exposure/barycenter steps do not alter the constants.

Verdict and limitations
-----------------------
No counterexample or proof failure found in the requested steps. The zero-spectrum Jensen argument is valid in operator norm after scalar regularization; the POVM form/Radon-Nikodym repair uses strong closure and correctly does not claim norm convergence. The exposure weights are correct only when their vector-norm masses are retained; their sum is p after the stated 1/m averaging. The disconnected-support barycenter needs the actual conditional component distribution and the Pinsker drift term; the proof includes both. The constructed state proves existence in the unrestricted commuting model, not efficient state preparation, finite-dimensional tensor equivalence, or an acquired spectral gap. This is a bounded internal audit, not external or formal certification of the full L02 theorem.
