# Result: finite classical memory at a qubit spectral collision

The strongest result I obtained is a **finite-memory compression theorem for qubits whose eigenvalues approach one another**. It gives a matching leading-order lower bound—even against arbitrary quantum memory—and resolves a subtle distinction between the vanishing-signal and critical regimes.

Write
\[
\rho_{\delta,v}=\frac{I+\delta\,v\cdot\sigma}{2},
\qquad v\in S^2,
\]
where the spectrum, equivalently \(\delta\), is known, but the direction \(v\) is unknown. For \(n\) copies, define
\[
\kappa=n\delta^2.
\]

The main consequence is:

> **When \(\delta_n\to0\) and \(n\delta_n^2\to\infty\), the optimal leading storage cost, among codes with vanishing global trace-distance error, is**
> \[
> \boxed{\log_2(n\delta_n^2)\,(1+o(1)).}
> \]
> **That leading cost is achievable entirely with classical memory: no retained qubits are necessary.**

This is not just the trivial regime where every input becomes indistinguishable from the maximally mixed state. When \(\kappa\to\infty\), those inputs remain strongly distinguishable.

The handoff already supplied a spin-uniform measure/prepare bound and explicitly left finite-memory compression across spectral collisions open. The new step here is to convert that continuous-outcome channel into a finite, fully counted classical memory, then establish the converse and critical-regime obstruction. Pasted text

The statements below are deductions developed in this continuation. Historical novelty and independent validation have not been established.

**Can the family retain an increasing amount of distinguishable information while losing its need for quantum storage—and what quantity then determines its memory cost?**

The answer below is yes, with \(n\delta^2\) determining the optimal leading cost.

## 2. Precise finite-memory theorem

Let \(D\) be the dimension of the **entire retained memory**, including every classical register. Its cost is \(\log_2 D\). Define the reconstruction error by
\[
e=\sup_{v\in S^2}
\frac12\left\|
\mathcal D\mathcal E(\rho_{\delta,v}^{\otimes n})
-\rho_{\delta,v}^{\otimes n}
\right\|_1.
\]

This task preserves the density operator, **not a purification**.

### Theorem A: finite classical compression

There is a universal constant \(C\) such that, for every
\[
n\ge1,\qquad 0<\delta\le\frac12,\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\frac14,
\]
a purely classical memory with
\[
\boxed{D\le C(1+n\delta^2)\varepsilon^{-4}}
\]
achieves
\[
\boxed{e\le \frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}+\varepsilon.}
\]

The proof actually gives \(\varepsilon^{-7/2}\); I use the simpler, weaker exponent \(4\). Neither exponent is claimed to be optimal.

### Theorem B: lower bound for arbitrary quantum memory

Every classical, quantum, or hybrid code with \(e\le1/8\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{D\ge \frac{1+n\delta^2}{1024}.}
\]

Consequently, if \(\delta_n\to0\) and \(\kappa_n\to\infty\), choosing
\[
\varepsilon_n=\frac1{\log(2+\kappa_n)}
\]
gives a classical-only code with vanishing error and
\[
\log_2D_n
\le
\log_2\kappa_n
+4\log_2\log(2+\kappa_n)+O(1).
\]
Every vanishing-error code, even with quantum memory, obeys
\[
\log_2D_n\ge \log_2\kappa_n-O(1).
\]

Thus the leading coefficient is settled for this regime.

### Theorem C: vanishing and critical signal

There is also a sharp qualitative distinction:

| Regime | Storage needed for vanishing error |
|---|---|
| \(\kappa_n\to0\) | Zero total memory suffices. |
| \(\kappa_n\to\kappa_0\in(0,\infty)\) | Every fixed-dimensional memory fails, but **any diverging classical-memory budget suffices**. |
| \(\kappa_n\to\infty\), with \(\delta_n\to0\) | Optimal leading cost is \(\log_2\kappa_n\), achievable classically. |

The critical case is therefore neither “zero memory” nor “a compulsory logarithmic number of bits.”

## 3. Proof of the finite classical construction

### 3.1 Re-establish the spin-uniform channel inequality

Put
\[
r=\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta},\qquad a=1-r.
\]
In a spin-\(j\) sector, write \(L=2j\). After aligning the axis with \(v\), its thermal state is
\[
\tau_L(r)=\frac1{Z_L}\sum_{k=0}^{L}r^k|k\rangle\langle k|,
\qquad
Z_L=\sum_{k=0}^{L}r^k.
\]

Let \(|L,u\rangle\) be a spin-coherent state, with normalized spherical area measure \(\mu\). The identity resolution
\[
(L+1)\int |L,u\rangle\langle L,u|\,d\mu(u)=I
\]
defines the measure/prepare channel
\[
\mathcal C_L(X)
=(L+1)\int
\langle L,u|X|L,u\rangle
\,|L,u\rangle\langle L,u|\,d\mu(u).
\]

Axial symmetry makes \(\mathcal C_L(\tau_L)\) diagonal in the same basis as \(\tau_L\). Its \(k\)-th eigenvalue is
\[
q_k=\frac1{Z_L}
\mathbb E\!\left[(r+(1-r)T)^L\right],
\qquad
T\sim\operatorname{Beta}(L-k+1,k+1).
\]

Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean gives
\[
r+(1-r)T\ge r^{1-T}.
\]
Jensen’s inequality then yields
\[
\mathbb E[r^{L(1-T)}]
\ge
r^{L\mathbb E(1-T)}
=
r^{L(k+1)/(L+2)}
\ge r^{k+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
q_k\ge r\,\frac{r^k}{Z_L},
\]
and hence the stronger operator inequality
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_L(\tau_L)\succeq r\tau_L.}
\]

Because both normalized states have trace one,
\[
\frac12\|\mathcal C_L(\tau_L)-\tau_L\|_1\le1-r=a.
\]

Crucially, **the bound is independent of \(L\)**.

The qubit Schur decomposition has the form
\[
\rho_{\delta,v}^{\otimes n}
\cong
\bigoplus_L p_L(n,\delta)\,
\tau_{L,v}(r)\otimes\frac{I_{m_L}}{m_L},
\]
where the sector probabilities \(p_L\) do not depend on \(v\). This is also the sector structure used by the existing qubit-compression protocols. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06742v1)

Applying \(\mathcal C_L\) in every sector therefore reconstructs the entire \(n\)-copy state with error at most
\[
a=\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}.
\]

That still leaves a continuous measurement outcome. The next step removes it without paying the wrong memory scale.

### 3.2 The key abstraction: approximate mixing probabilities, not individual states

Suppose a measurement produces a density \(f_\theta\) relative to a known measure \(\nu\), and reconstructs
\[
\widetilde\rho_\theta
=\int f_\theta(x)\omega_x\,d\nu(x).
\]

For a finite partition \(\mathcal P\), retain only the cell \(A\) containing the outcome, and decode it as
\[
\omega_A=\frac1{\nu(A)}\int_A\omega_x\,d\nu(x).
\]
Writing \(\Pi_{\mathcal P}\) for averaging on partition cells, the resulting state satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\widehat\rho_\theta-\widetilde\rho_\theta\|_1
\le
\frac12\|f_\theta-\Pi_{\mathcal P}f_\theta\|_{L^1(\nu)}.
}
\]

This follows directly from the triangle inequality and \(\|\omega_x\|_1=1\).

**No assumption says that \(\omega_x\) and \(\omega_y\) must be close inside a cell.** That is the useful distinction. Quantizing each coherent state separately would demand an unnecessarily fine angular resolution. Quantizing their mixing law can be much cheaper.

### 3.3 The measured likelihood varies on the scale \(1+aL\)

Conditional on spin label \(L\), the coherent-measurement outcome has density
\[
f_{L,v}(u)
=
\frac{(L+1)a}{1-r^{L+1}}
\left[r+a\frac{1+v\cdot u}{2}\right]^L
\]
relative to \(\mu\).

Two estimates control its finite encoding.

**Angular variation.** Set \(t=(1+v\cdot u)/2\). Then
\[
|\nabla t|^2=t(1-t)
\]
and
\[
|\nabla\log f_{L,v}|^2
=
L^2a^2\frac{t(1-t)}{(r+at)^2}.
\]
Under this density, \(y=r+at\) has density proportional to \(y^L\) on \([r,1]\), giving
\[
\mathbb E[1-t]\le\frac1{a(L+2)}.
\]
Since \(\delta\le1/2\) implies \(r\ge1/3\),
\[
\int f_{L,v}|\nabla\log f_{L,v}|^2\,d\mu
\le9aL.
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
\boxed{\|\nabla f_{L,v}\|_{L^1(\mu)}\le3\sqrt{aL}.}
\]

Thus the angular likelihood varies on the scale
\[
(1+aL)^{-1/2},
\]
rather than the coherent-state scale \((L+1)^{-1/2}\).

**Variation with the spin label.** Extend the density formula to real \(L\ge0\) for this estimate. The variable
\[
z=-\log(r+at)
\]
has a truncated exponential distribution with rate \(L+1\). Differentiating the normalized density gives
\[
\frac12\int|\partial_L f_{L,v}|\,d\mu
\le \mathbb E[z]
\le \min\{-\log r,(L+1)^{-1}\}.
\]
For \(r\ge1/3\),
\[
-\log r\le2a,
\qquad
\min\{2a,(L+1)^{-1}\}\le\frac{3a}{1+aL}.
\]
Integration therefore proves
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(f_{L,v},f_{K,v})
\le
3\left|\log\frac{1+aK}{1+aL}\right|.
}
\]

So nearby values of \(1+aL\), on a multiplicative scale, can share a stored spin-bin label.

### 3.4 Control the tail without importing a nonuniform asymptotic approximation

The total-spin identity gives
\[
\mathbb E[L(L+2)]
=
3n+n(n-1)\delta^2.
\]
Indeed, each individual spin contributes \(3/4\) to \(\mathbb E[J^2]\), while each ordered pair contributes \(\delta^2/4\).

Since \(a\le2\delta\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[(aL)^2]
\le4\kappa^2+12\kappa
\le9(1+\kappa)^2.
}
\]

This exact second-moment estimate suffices; no fixed-gap central-limit theorem is being extrapolated.

### 3.5 Construct and count the memory

Define
\[
q_L=1+aL,\qquad
Q=1+\frac{6(1+\kappa)}{\sqrt\varepsilon},
\qquad
h=\frac{\varepsilon}{12}.
\]

Collapse \(q_L>Q\) into one tail label. Its probability is at most \(\varepsilon/4\).

Partition the remaining spin labels into bins
\[
[(1+h)^b,(1+h)^{b+1}).
\]
Any two labels in a bin have conditional angular laws within
\[
3\log(1+h)\le\varepsilon/4
\]
in total variation.

For a bin with upper endpoint \(q_b^+\), partition the sphere into
\[
O(q_b^+/\varepsilon^2)
\]
cells. The angular variation estimate ensures that averaging on those cells costs at most \(\varepsilon/4\).

For completeness, such partitions can be made by subdividing the six faces of a cube and radially projecting them onto the sphere. Their area Jacobians are bounded above and below. The ordinary square \(L^1\) Poincaré inequality then gives
\[
\|f-\Pi_Nf\|_1\le\frac{C_0}{N}\|\nabla f\|_1
\]
for a grid with \(O(N^2)\) cells. Taking \(N\asymp\sqrt{q_b^+}/\varepsilon\) gives the claimed count.

The finite channel is now explicit:

**Encoding:** measure the Schur sector and coherent POVM, then retain only the spin bin and angular cell, or the tail symbol. Equivalently, integrate the measurement effects over each cell to obtain a finite POVM directly.

**Decoding:** draw \(L\) from its known distribution conditioned on the stored bin; draw \(u\) uniformly inside the stored angular cell; prepare the corresponding coherent spin state and maximally mixed multiplicity state, then invert the Schur transform.

The exact spin label is **not** retained for free. It is resampled, and the error caused by that resampling is precisely what the spin-variation estimate controls.

Within a bin, the total-variation error is at most \(\varepsilon/2\); the tail adds at most \(\varepsilon/4\). The probability-coarse-graining inequality transfers this to reconstruction error.

Finally,
\[
\sum_bq_b^+\le C_1\frac Qh
\]
by the geometric-series formula. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
D
&\le1+C_2\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_bq_b^+\\
&\le C_3(1+\kappa)\varepsilon^{-7/2}\\
&\le C_3(1+\kappa)\varepsilon^{-4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding the initial channel error proves Theorem A.

## 4. Why quantum memory cannot improve the leading rate

The lower bound uses distinguishable directions, not a restriction to classical encoders.

Divide the input qubits into three groups and measure the three Pauli coordinates separately. The empirical Bloch-vector estimate obeys
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat b-\delta v\|_2^2\le\frac{18}{n}.
\]

For \(\kappa\ge576\), choose a maximal spherical packing with chordal separation
\[
d=\frac{24}{\sqrt\kappa}.
\]
A covering-area argument gives at least
\[
N\ge\frac{\kappa}{144}
\]
directions. Nearest-point decoding from \(\widehat b/\delta\) fails with probability at most
\[
\frac{18/\kappa}{(d/2)^2}=\frac18.
\]

A compression code with error at most \(1/8\) must therefore preserve discrimination success at least \(3/4\).

Let \(\omega_i\) be the encoded states in a \(D\)-dimensional memory, and let \(F_i\) be the discriminating POVM pulled back through the decoder. Since \(\omega_i\preceq I\),
\[
\frac34
\le
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(F_i\omega_i)
\le
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}F_i
=
\frac DN.
\]
Hence
\[
D\ge\frac{\kappa}{192}.
\]

For smaller \(\kappa\), \(D\ge1\) supplies the uniform bound
\[
D\ge\frac{1+\kappa}{1024}.
\]

This argument includes arbitrary quantum encoders and decoders. It is why the classical construction has the optimal leading storage rate.

## 5. The critical regime: a finite-rank obstruction

The distinction between \(\kappa\to0\) and \(\kappa\to\kappa_0>0\) requires an additional argument.

### Zero memory

Preparing \((I/2)^{\otimes n}\) without retaining input information gives
\[
e\le\sqrt{\kappa/2}.
\]
Conversely, antipodal inputs have root fidelity
\[
F(\rho_{\delta,v}^{\otimes n},\rho_{\delta,-v}^{\otimes n})
=(1-\delta^2)^{n/2}.
\]
If one common zero-memory output approximates both with error \(e\), then
\[
2e\ge1-(1-\delta^2)^{n/2}.
\]
Therefore zero-memory error vanishes **exactly when \(\kappa\to0\)**.

### Positive critical signal

Suppose
\[
\sqrt n\,\delta_n\to c>0.
\]
Restrict the unknown directions to
\[
v(t)=(\sqrt{1-t^2},0,t),\qquad -1\le t\le1.
\]
Consider the bounded observables
\[
U_{n,s}
=
\exp\!\left(is\,n^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell=1}^nZ_\ell\right).
\]
Their exact expectations are
\[
F_n(t,s)
=
\left[
\cos(s/\sqrt n)+i\delta_n t\sin(s/\sqrt n)
\right]^n,
\]
so
\[
F_n(t,s)\longrightarrow e^{-s^2/2+icts}.
\]

Suppose a subsequence of codes uses memory dimension at most \(d\). Set
\[
q=d^2+1,
\]
choose \(q\) distinct \(t_i\), and take \(s_j=jh\), with \(h>0\) small enough that \(e^{icht_i}\) are distinct.

The limiting expectation matrix is
\[
\left[e^{-s_j^2/2+ict_is_j}\right]_{i,j}.
\]
After dividing out its nonzero Gaussian column factors, this is a Vandermonde matrix. Its rank is \(q\).

But every reconstructed expectation matrix factoring through a \(d\)-dimensional memory has rank at most \(d^2\): encoded states lie in a \(d^2\)-dimensional matrix space, and decoding and taking expectations are linear.

Vanishing trace-distance error would force entrywise convergence to the rank-\(q\) matrix, contradicting closedness of the rank-at-most-\(d^2\) set.

Thus **every bounded-capacity subsequence is excluded**: \(D_n\to\infty\) is necessary.

It is also sufficient. When \(\kappa_n\) is bounded, Theorem A fits any diverging capacity by choosing \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) sufficiently slowly. No retained quantum memory is required.

## 6. Scientific meaning, checks, and boundaries

For the concrete family \(\delta_n=n^{-\alpha}\), the conclusions are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
0<\alpha<1/2:
&\displaystyle
m_n=(1-2\alpha)\log_2n+o(\log n),
\quad\text{entirely classical};\\[2mm]
\alpha=1/2:
&\text{any diverging classical bit budget suffices, but fixed memory fails};\\[1mm]
\alpha>1/2:
&\text{zero total memory suffices}.
\end{array}
\]

For example, with \(\delta_n=n^{-1/4}\), the optimal leading cost is
\[
\frac12\log_2n,
\]
not the \(\frac34\log_2n\) suggested by an inadmissible substitution into the fixed-spectrum expression.

The mechanism is not ordinary tomography followed by preparing another noisy product state. That would add a second layer of statistical uncertainty. The decoder instead prepares coherent sector states, with the required uncertainty already present in their measured mixing law.

Moreover, measuring along the true direction and thresholding the sample mean gives
\[
\frac12\left\|
\rho_{\delta,v}^{\otimes n}-(I/2)^{\otimes n}
\right\|_1
\ge1-\frac8\kappa
\]
whenever the bound is positive. Thus the growing-signal family can approach trace distance one from the maximally mixed state while its need for retained qubits disappears.

I checked the local algebra with **198 exact rational channel cases**, encompassing **3,366 eigenvalue inequalities**, **400 exact Schur normalization and moment cases**, **594 exact angular-Fisher checks**, and **140 high-precision likelihood comparisons**. All assertions passed. These are reproducible finite checks, not a substitute for the analytic proof or independent refereeing.

The result does **not** determine the optimal error exponent, an additive memory constant, or optimal storage for a prescribed error much smaller than \(\delta_n\). It also does not establish a higher-dimensional collision theorem or an end-to-end gate-complexity bound.

The substantive conclusion for this qubit family is nevertheless sharp: **approaching a spectral collision can remove the need for quantum storage long before it removes statistical information. The optimal leading memory scale becomes \(n\delta^2\), rather than \(n\delta\).**
