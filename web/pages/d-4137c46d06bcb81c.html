Bounded primary-literature comparison: attainment of best normal EB approximation
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: source comparison only; candidate theorem not independently proved here.

Candidate interface
-------------------
Let H_in and H_out be separable Hilbert spaces, let I be any nonempty index set, and let (rho_i)_{i in I} and (sigma_i)_{i in I} be normal states on B(H_in) and B(H_out), respectively. In Schrödinger notation these are density operators in T(H_in) and T(H_out). Define

  e_EB = inf_{Lambda normal CPTP and EB} sup_{i in I} (1/2)||Lambda(rho_i)-sigma_i||_1.

The candidate says this infimum is attained by one normal EB channel, with no countability, finite-capacity, or common-faithful-state assumption on the family. A separate candidate consequence says finite Holevo capacity permits finite-outcome approximation, but does not promise a finite-outcome optimizer. Neither candidate claim is treated as established by this literature report.

Closest exact antecedents
------------------------

1. Shirokov's compactness criterion is the nearest topological theorem. In the exact v1 source (Corollary 1A, PDF p. 6), a closed set F_0 of quantum operations on separable Hilbert spaces is compact in the strong, pointwise trace-norm topology iff there is a faithful input state sigma for which {Phi(sigma): Phi in F_0} is trace-norm compact. Corollary 2 (PDF pp. 7-8) gives the corresponding relative compactness criterion. Remark 3 (PDF p. 7) shows closedness cannot be dropped: replacement channels to states sigma_n converging to sigma_0 can have compact outputs on a faithful state while the family omits its strong limit. Proposition 2 (PDF pp. 11-13) makes the relevant obstruction explicit as output tightness under finite-rank projections.

This does not imply the candidate. A worst-case error bound puts outputs of a faithful mixture in a trace-norm ball around a target mixture; in infinite-dimensional trace class, that ball need not be relatively compact. The candidate instead needs only pointwise weak-star compactness against compact output observables and must handle the trace that escapes in that weaker topology. For example, replacement channels X -> Tr(X)|n><n| have outputs converging weak-star to zero against compact operators, but no trace-norm convergent subsequence.

2. Shirokov's infinite-dimensional EB closure theorem is exact but uses the strong topology. For separable H,H', Section 5 (PDF p. 10) defines P_k as channels whose outputs, after tensoring with any reference identity, have Schmidt number at most k. Since the corresponding Schmidt class is closed and convex, P_k is a closed convex subset of channels in the strong topology; P_1 is the EB class. This gives strong-closedness of normal EB channels, not closure of the EB class among trace-nonincreasing maps under pointwise sigma(T,K) convergence.

Holevo's structure theorem (Theorem 1, PDF p. 5; measurement-prepare form (2), PDF p. 2) represents an EB channel on separable Hilbert spaces by a general Borel POVM and measurable state-preparation map, with an integral over a complete separable metric outcome space. Thus “normal EB” naturally allows general, including continuous, outcomes. Shirokov's Corollary 2 (PDF p. 13) gives EB channels for which every Kraus representation has only infinite-rank Kraus operators. Since a finite-outcome measure-prepare channel admits a countable rank-one Kraus expansion by decomposing its finitely many prepared states, finite-outcome channels are a strict subclass of all normal EB channels. This channel-level strictness does not show that a particular finite-capacity minimax problem lacks a finite-outcome optimizer.

3. Jenčová's quantum Le Cam theorem is the closest decision-theoretic match. The paper states at the outset that all Hilbert spaces are finite-dimensional (PDF p. 3). Theorem 4 (PDF pp. 18-20) treats experiments S=(K,{sigma_theta}) and T=(H,{rho_theta}) with an arbitrary common parameter set Theta: a uniform decision/guessing comparison is equivalent to existence of one CPTP alpha with sup_theta ||sigma_theta-alpha(rho_theta)||_1 <= 2 epsilon. In the preceding channel-deficiency definition (§3, PDF p. 11), the post-processing infimum is attained because the finite-dimensional channel set is compact. In finite dimensions the same compactness argument applies with the EB channels as the feasible closed compact subset, so the candidate's attainment statement is immediate there. The source excludes the separable infinite-dimensional case and optimizes over all channels in the deficiency theorem; it does not state the normal-EB attainment result.

4. Kuramochi gives an infinite-dimensional order-theoretic result that is close in vocabulary but not in objective. Theorem 2 (PDF p. 12) characterizes EB maps through separability, QC domination, measurement-prepare form, and compatibility with every finite number of copies. Theorem 4 (PDF pp. 19-20) says randomization-equivalence classes of normal EB channels with fixed input von Neumann algebra are upper and lower Dedekind-closed in the randomization preorder. This is closure under order suprema/infima of monotone nets, not compactness for trace-distance loss and not attainment of a uniform minimax distance. The paper distinguishes normal channels (sigma-weak continuous in Heisenberg picture) from general C*-algebra channels; the candidate is specifically about normal channels between trace-class state spaces.

Candidate mechanism and exact missing gate
------------------------------------------
The proposed mechanism is a compactification-plus-repair argument. A minimizing sequence of normal EB channels is extracted to a pointwise sigma(T(H_out),K(H_out)) limit Lambda_0. The desired limit is a normal CP trace-nonincreasing EB map. For each i, positivity and trace escape should give

  liminf_n ||Lambda_n(rho_i)-sigma_i||_1 >= ||Lambda_0(rho_i)-sigma_i||_1 + 1 - Tr(Lambda_0(rho_i)).

Writing D = I - Lambda_0^*(I) >= 0 and fixing any normal output state tau, the trace-preserving repair

  Lambda(X) = Lambda_0(X) + Tr(DX) tau

is a channel. If Lambda_0 is EB, this repair is EB; the triangle inequality gives no larger statewise loss than the escaped-trace lower bound. The same fixed tau works for every i, so the argument is compatible with a supremum over an arbitrary index set.

The decisive source/proof gate is the closure statement for the EB cone under this particular pointwise weak-star limit: limits of normal EB CPTP maps must remain normal CP TNI and EB, with the correct meaning of EB for subchannels. Existing Shirokov closure is only strong-topology closure for channels. Existing Holevo/Kuramochi structure theorems describe normal EB channels but do not state this weak-star compactification-and-repair minimax theorem. The scalar trace-defect inequality is also an explicit proof obligation; it is not supplied by the cited compactness papers.

Finite-outcome side claim
-------------------------
The primary sources establish that general normal EB channels may require general outcomes and need not have countable rank-one Kraus decompositions. They do not establish the candidate's finite-capacity uniform approximation theorem, nor do they provide an example where a finite-capacity best-approximation problem has no finite-outcome optimizer. Keep approximation and attainment separate.

Priority and significance
-------------------------
This bounded source pass found no theorem that directly states the candidate for arbitrary separable input/output systems and arbitrary normal-state families. That is not a priority result and must not be reported as novelty by absence. If the weak-star EB-subchannel closure and trace-repair proof are fully established, the result would extend the finite-dimensional uniform Le Cam/deficiency existence statement to a constrained EB class in separable infinite dimensions, while explaining how mass lost at infinity is completed without worsening any state's error.
