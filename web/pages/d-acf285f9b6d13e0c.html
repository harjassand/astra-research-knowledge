# Mitochondrial turnover: exact neutral threshold accounting, with a prior-art stop

## Verdict

**Do not promote this as a new aging mechanism or scientific advance.** The broad mechanism is mature prior art: controlled mtDNA-population models already show that genotype-blind turnover drives heteroplasmy variance; mitochondrial-network models already connect fusion/fission, mitophagy, copy number and selective clearance; human clone studies already estimate somatic turnover and mtDNA replication error; and a 2026 primary paper already computes exact Moran takeover-time distributions and demonstrates cross-sectional threshold inference on synthetic data.

I reconstructed one useful finite-copy consequence and exposed the quantitative tradeoff: in a neutral Moran model, an allele introduced in one of (N) mtDNA copies reaches a threshold of (m) copies before loss with exact probability (1/m), while (N\rho\mu) new mutation labels arise per cell-year. In the infinite-sites, neutral, homeostatic-turnover model, the expected count of mutation labels born in an interval that eventually reach (m\approx Nh_*) is therefore (N\rho\mu/m\approx\rho\mu/h_*): the mtDNA-copy-number factor cancels in this long-run lineage accounting. This is an accounting corollary of the classical Moran process, not a discovery. The finite-age threshold probability remains rate- and horizon-dependent.

## What the model establishes

For an (N)-copy cell with genotype-blind replication and removal, every replacement attempt chooses a parent and a removed copy uniformly. The count (K) of one neutral allele follows a symmetric birth-death chain. Stopping it at loss (0) or a chosen threshold (m), its exact hitting probability is (K_0/m). Changing the turnover rate ρ multiplies every transition rate by the same factor, so faster turnover cannot lower the chance of having crossed the threshold by a fixed age; it advances the same embedded chain. The ultimate hit-before-loss probability is independent of ρ.

With (N=100), ten initial mutant copies, an 80-copy threshold and a ten-year horizon, the exact uniformized model gives a threshold-crossing probability of (6.41\times10^{-7}) at 0.5 turnover cycles/year and (9.83\times10^{-3}) at 2 cycles/year. Both models have the same eventual hit probability, (10/80=1/8). This is an illustrative finite-chain counterexample to the proposal that *more genotype-blind turnover itself repairs an existing neutral allele*. It is not a prediction for a specific tissue.

There is an equally important sign-changing counterexample. If mutants have replication fitness (1-s), their exact expected count drift per Moran attempt is

\[
-\frac{sK(N-K)}{N(N-sK)}<0.
\]

Then faster turnover accelerates mutant loss. A replication-advantaged deletion instead moves in the other direction. The practical gate is therefore the measured **allele-conditioned birth/removal bias**, not bulk mitophagy flux. A PINK1/Parkin or fusion/fission intervention cannot be evaluated from total turnover alone.

## Translation to source-reported human parameters

An et al. (2024) report a replication-associated mutation rate of (5.0\times10^{-8}) per base-pair replication, a ∼750 mtDNA copy number for their model translation, and homeostatic turnover estimates of 6.5–11.5 cycles per year across the reported cell types. Using their turnover definition (one homeostatic cycle corresponds to (N) replacement replications), this gives a Poisson mean of 0.6213 newly arising sequence alterations per turnover cycle per 750-copy cell. With the **illustrative** threshold (h_*=0.8) ((m=600)), one neutral new label has probability (1/600) of ever reaching threshold. The model’s expected count of labels born per year that eventually reach that threshold is 0.00673–0.01191 per cell-year over the reported ρ range.

This is not a dysfunction rate. Most sequence alterations are not known to be pathogenic; cell/tissue thresholds vary; the source reports functional constraint for truncating mutations; and future threshold crossings may occur well after the mutation was born. The 2026 deletion paper already derives finite-time takeover distributions with the Moran forward equation and demonstrates cross-sectional inference on synthetic data; it emphasizes RNA-versus-DNA abundance bias. Its authors describe direct application to a separate companion manuscript. Do not turn the eventual-label expectation into a present-day fraction of impaired cells.

## The decisive test

The model has a narrow falsifier. In a nondividing isogenic cell preparation with a single neutral mtDNA marker, verify constant copy number and equal allele-specific replication/removal, then alter the measured homeostatic replacement rate. Measure single-cell mtDNA counts at sufficient depth to see one copy (about 0.13% at (N=750)), copy number, pulse-chase turnover, heteroplasmy distribution and threshold-crossing histories. At matched event count, the neutral model predicts the same threshold-hit probability; at matched calendar age, increasing ρ can only move that probability upward. Any systematic mean shift at matched copy number, or any event-count-normalized difference by turnover condition, rejects genotype-blind time-rescaling and signals selection, copy-number regulation, multi-copy compartment effects or another omitted process.

For mutations that can affect function, repeat the readout with allele-specific replication and clearance measured directly. Include a deletion or known deleterious allele only as a separate arm: these variants need not follow neutral dynamics, and 2024 clone data show high-VAF truncating variants are constrained. An assay that measures only bulk mitophagy, mitochondrial mass, membrane potential or RNA VAF does not identify this model’s key parameters.

## Resource and evidence costs

The model’s required inputs are copy number (N), turnover cycles/year ρ, per-replication mutation rate μ, the starting mutant count, threshold (m), and the genotype-conditioned selection/clearance coefficient. The 2024 study reports the first three only through clone-level sequencing plus model inference; it does not furnish the allele-specific clearance coefficient or universal (h_*). A ∼0.3% VAF floor would not robustly observe a single mtDNA copy in a 750-copy cell. The decisive assay needs error-corrected single-molecule mtDNA sequencing, pulse-chase turnover calibration and enough independent lineages. A binomial back-of-envelope for 20% relative error on (p=1/600) requires roughly 15,000 independent lineages before accounting for tracking loss, cell attrition, repeated mutation classes or sequencing error. If the endpoint is within a finite aging window, fit the finite-time CTMC; the eventual (1/m) probability alone is insufficient.

## Prior-art attack and boundary

Johnston & Jones (2016) already derive a feedback-independent neutral heteroplasmy variance rate under their stated equal-rate and steady-copy assumptions. Aryaman et al. (2019) show that network fusion can shield mtDNA from turnover, that an unfused fraction rescales stochastic genetic dynamics, and that the dependence changes with the mode of copy-number control. Hoitzing et al. (2015) simulate coupled selective fusion, selective mitophagy, replication and organelle mixing, including cases where increasing network cycling without enough selectivity worsens mutant burden. An et al. (2024) provide human clone-level turnover/mutation estimates and a lifetime-drift simulation; they also find functional restraint on truncating variants. The 2026 npj Aging paper explicitly uses the Moran process to obtain exact 90%-takeover time distributions and fits mutant-threshold fractions from cross-sectional data.

Thus the only defensible capital here is a clean finite-(N) derivation, source-unit audit and bounded intervention prediction. No biological experiment was run; no aging-causality claim, therapy claim, independent replication of primary data, priority claim or nature-level rejuvenation claim is established. Astra’s pinned local topic tables had no mtDNA/aging entry; this records a bounded retrieval result only, not a literature-priority result.
