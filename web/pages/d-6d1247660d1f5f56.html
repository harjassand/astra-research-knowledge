LITERATURE FINDINGS (three research lanes)

1. General fixed-manifold bounds — Colding–Minicozzi

Colding–Minicozzi, “Weyl type bounds for harmonic functions,” Invent. Math.
131 (1998), 257–298, Theorem 0.26,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050204.
For complete n-manifolds with Ric>=0 they prove a bound of the form

    h_d(M) <= C_1(n) alpha d^(n-1) + C_2(n) f(d^(n-1)),
    alpha = lim_R Vol(B_R)/R^n,
    f(t)=o(t),

with dimension-only constants. This is a fixed-manifold limsup bound of the
right order and proportional to asymptotic volume, but its leading constant
is not the sharp cone coefficient. In dimension 3 with normalized
AVR v, it yields only limsup h_k/(k+1)^2 <= C_1(3) omega_3 v. The cited
statement does not supply a coefficient shown to be below the proposed c;
it therefore does not rule out the target. The source statement is also
quoted in Huang’s primary preprint, Theorem 1.1 and the paragraph following
Problem 1.2: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07534v1.

2. Nonunique tangent cones — cone dimensions and three-circles control

Honda, “Harmonic functions on asymptotic cones with Euclidean volume growth,”
J. Math. Soc. Japan 67 (2015), 69–126, Theorems 1.1–1.2,
https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/06710069.
For every asymptotic cone C(X) of a fixed Ric>=0, Euclidean-volume-growth
manifold, Honda identifies its polynomial-growth harmonic dimension with the
cross-section eigenvalue count and proves two-sided Weyl-order bounds
c(n) V_M d^(n-1) <= dim H_d(C(X)) <= C(n) V_M d^(n-1), uniformly for every
asymptotic cone. These are coarse constants and concern each cone, not the
dimension h_d(M) on the original manifold; they do not imply an exact
fixed-manifold asymptotic coefficient.

Xu, “Three circles theorems for harmonic functions,” Math. Ann. 366 (2016),
1281–1317, arXiv:1601.02066v1, Theorem 3.2 (and Theorem 3.4 in the
maximal-volume-growth case), https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02066v1.
This theorem explicitly allows nonunique tangent cones: assuming every
tangent cone is a metric cone with conic limit measure and the cross-section
has positive one-dimensional Hausdorff measure, it gives a large-scale
three-circles implication at each exponent alpha outside D(M), the union of
the homogeneous-degree spectra of all tangent cones. It is a growth
propagation statement, not a dimension count or an upper bound on
limsup h_k/k^2. When the union D(M) fills or is dense in high degrees, this
exclusion condition may provide no useful high-degree restriction. Thus it
does not presently kill the proposed limsup.

3. Sharp comparison, known excess, and current restrictions

Cai–Lai, “Dimension of polynomial growth harmonic functions on locally
conformally flat manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature,” arXiv:
2608.04553v2 (2026), Theorem A,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04553v2.
For every complete noncompact locally conformally flat n-manifold with
Ric>=0, it proves h_d(M)<=h_d(R^n) for every integer d. Its introduction
states that the unrestricted integer-degree Euclidean comparison remains
open, summarizes Donnelly’s older counterexamples in dimensions n>=5 at
some real 1<d<2, and records Huang’s sharp asymptotic coefficient under
maximal volume growth plus unique tangent cone. Theorem A is a sharp
restricted-class impossibility result; it cannot apply to a metric realizing
the proposed strict excess, which would itself violate its conclusion.

Lin–Wang–Xu, “Eigenvalues on spheres,” arXiv:2607.11544v1 (paper dated
2026-08-24), Theorem 1.12 / Section 17,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11544v1.
For complete 3-manifolds with nonnegative sectional curvature and positive
AVR it proves h_k(M)<=h_k(R^3)=(k+1)^2 for every integer k, with rigidity
in the equality case. The release-361 metrics have negative radial
sectional planes arbitrarily far out (release paper, p. 2), so this
restriction does not apply to them. The theorem does rule out the target in
that stronger curvature class.

Donnelly, “Harmonic functions on manifolds of nonnegative Ricci curvature,”
IMRN 2001(8), 429–434, https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792801000216.
The abstract states that in every dimension n>=5 there are complete
Ric>=0 manifolds with more than n+1 independent subquadratic-growth
harmonic functions, violating Euclidean comparison for some real degree
1<d<2. This is a fixed-manifold excess precedent but neither a 3D result
nor a high-integer-degree limsup result; it does not settle or duplicate the
specific target.

For context only, Huang, arXiv:2109.07534v1, Theorem 1.6 and Corollaries
1.8–1.9 (numbering per cited version), https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07534v1,
proves an upper bound by the unique cone’s spectral counting function and
the sharp 3D coefficient lim h_k/k^2=v under maximal volume growth and a
unique tangent cone at infinity. Uniqueness is essential to that theorem’s
stated scope. The release construction has uncountably many nonisometric
tangent cones at infinity, so this is not a fixed-metric impossibility
result for the proposed nonunique-cone setting.

RANKED NOVELTY KILLER / BOUNDED CONCLUSION

No located primary theorem proves or forbids the proposed fixed-manifold
limsup excess for a general smooth 3D Ric>=0 metric with AVR v and
nonunique tangent cones. The exact limit coefficient v is known when the
tangent cone is unique; broad fixed-manifold and all-cone estimates retain
dimension-dependent constants; nonunique-cone three-circles control does
not count dimensions. The nearest exact impossibility results require
locally conformally flat geometry or nonnegative sectional curvature. The
target is therefore a plausible, genuinely stronger unresolved extension
of release family 361, not an established novel result: priority is not
certified by this bounded search. The source-local obstacle remains the
release proof’s finite p and fixed outer-gap tau_* in Proposition 5.2,
pp. 27–29; the existing construction does not provide uniformity as the
spectral cycle grows.
