# Weighted Wasserstein contraction for heat-bath Glauber dynamics

## Result

For a finite product spin system, the continuous-time heat-bath chain contracts 1-Wasserstein distance in a weighted Hamming metric whenever the weighted total influence *into each changed coordinate* is uniformly below one. If the margin is `κ`, then the contraction is `exp(-κt)` when every site has update rate one. This gives a mixing bound with explicit metric-diameter prefactor. For pairwise Ising models, the usual high-temperature condition `max_j Σ_i tanh(β|J_ij|) ≤ 1-κ` gives the result in ordinary Hamming distance; for symmetric interactions this is the familiar maximum weighted-degree criterion.

The mechanism is classical path coupling / Dobrushin influence control, not a new theorem. The useful repair from this derivation is to keep the influence-matrix orientation explicit: with `c_ij` meaning “changing site j can change the conditional at updated site i,” the weighted contraction condition is a **column** inequality `Σ_i w_i c_ij ≤ (1-κ)w_j`. A three-leaf Ising star below shows why an unweighted same-site coupling cannot be inferred from the opposite row inequality, even when all those row sums are below one.

## Exact setup and theorem

Let `V={1,…,n}`, let each `S_i` be finite, and let `Ω=∏_i S_i`. Let `μ` be a strictly positive probability law on `Ω`; strict positivity makes all single-site conditionals defined and the heat-bath chain irreducible. Define

`c_ij = sup || μ_i(· | x_{-i}) - μ_i(· | y_{-i}) ||_TV`,

where the supremum is over `x,y` that agree except possibly at site `j`; set `c_ii=0`. Write `C=(c_ij)`. For positive weights `w_i`, set

`d_w(x,y)=Σ_i w_i 1{x_i≠y_i}`.

The continuous-time heat-bath generator is

`Lf(x)=Σ_i [ Σ_{s∈S_i} μ_i(s|x_{-i}) f(x^{i←s}) - f(x) ]`,

so each of the `n` sites has an independent rate-one clock.

**Theorem.** If for some `κ∈(0,1]` and every `j`,

`Σ_i w_i c_ij ≤ (1-κ) w_j`,

then for all probability laws `ν,η` on `Ω` and all `t≥0`,

`W_{1,d_w}(νP_t,ηP_t) ≤ exp(-κt) W_{1,d_w}(ν,η)`.

For the discrete random-scan chain `P=(1/n)Σ_i P_i`, the corresponding one-step bound is

`W_{1,d_w}(νP,ηP) ≤ (1-κ/n) W_{1,d_w}(ν,η)`.

Here `P_i` is the heat-bath update at site `i`, and `W_{1,d_w}` is the optimal transport distance with cost `d_w`.

### Proof

For any `x,y`, change their unequal coordinates one at a time and use the triangle inequality for total variation. The definition of `c_ij` gives

`||μ_i(·|x_{-i})-μ_i(·|y_{-i})||_TV ≤ Σ_j c_ij 1{x_j≠y_j}`.

Couple the two chains by using the same rate-one clock at each site and, when site `i` rings, a maximal coupling of their two heat-bath conditionals. Let `I_j=1{x_j≠y_j}`. A site-`i` update removes its old contribution `w_i I_i` and leaves a new expected contribution equal to `w_i` times the conditional total-variation distance. Therefore the coupling generator applied to the distance satisfies

`L̃ d_w(x,y) ≤ -Σ_j w_j I_j + Σ_j (Σ_i w_i c_ij) I_j ≤ -κ d_w(x,y)`.

Dynkin’s formula and Grönwall’s inequality give `E[d_w(X_t,Y_t)]≤e^{-κt}d_w(X_0,Y_0)`. Start the coupling from an optimal coupling of `ν,η` to obtain the Wasserstein claim. For random scan, average the same one-site calculation over the `n` possible sites, giving the factor `1-κ/n`.

This proof charges each update at rate one per site. It assumes the exact influence bounds are valid uniformly over all boundary conditions; a sampled estimate of `C` is not a certificate for the theorem.

## Concrete Ising consequence

For spins `σ_i∈{-1,+1}` with

`μ(σ) ∝ exp(β Σ_{ij∈E} J_ij σ_iσ_j + β Σ_i h_iσ_i)`,

the conditional probability is

`μ_i(+1|σ_{-i}) = [1+tanh(β h_i + β Σ_j J_ij σ_j)]/2`.

Changing only `σ_j` shifts the argument by `2βJ_ij`; maximizing the resulting Bernoulli total-variation difference over the midpoint of the two arguments gives

`c_ij ≤ tanh(β|J_ij|)`.

The bound is uniform in all external fields. Since the interaction is symmetric, if

`max_j Σ_{i:ij∈E} tanh(β|J_ij|) ≤ 1-κ`,

we may take `w_i=1`. Thus `W_1` in Hamming cost contracts by `e^{-κt}` in continuous time. The full configuration space has Hamming diameter `n`, and `d_w≥1` off the diagonal, so

`sup_x ||δ_xP_t-μ||_TV ≤ n e^{-κt}`,

and consequently

`t_mix^TV(ε) ≤ κ^{-1} log(n/ε)`

in rate-one-per-site continuous time. In random-scan steps the bound is `t_mix^TV(ε) ≤ (n/κ) log(n/ε)`. A sufficient bounded-degree condition is `Δ tanh(βJ*)≤1-κ` when the maximum degree is `Δ` and `|J_ij|≤J*`.

More generally the weighted theorem gives, for `D_w=Σ_i w_i` and `w_min=min_i w_i`,

`sup_x ||δ_xP_t-μ||_TV ≤ (D_w/w_min)e^{-κt}`,

so `t_mix^TV(ε)≤κ^{-1}log(D_w/(w_min ε))`. This exposes a limitation: a dimension-uniform contraction margin does not by itself give dimension-free total-variation mixing if `D_w/w_min` grows with system size.

## Orientation counterexample and finite check

Take a four-spin Ising star with center `0`, three leaves, zero leaf fields, dimensionless edge coupling `b=atanh(1/2)`, and dimensionless center field `a=6`. Under the convention above, each leaf is influenced by the center by exactly `1/2`. The center is influenced by a leaf by

`c = sinh(2b) / [cosh(2(a-2b)) + cosh(2b)] = 0.0001474339200697…`.

The influence matrix has center row sum `3c≈0.00044230`, each leaf row sum `1/2`, and maximum row sum `1/2`; its center column sum is `3/2`. For a pair of configurations differing only at the center, the common-site maximal heat-bath coupling has initial expected Hamming-distance drift

`-1 + 3(1/2) = +1/2`.

So the row inequality alone does not prove contraction for this standard unweighted coupling under this orientation. This calculation does **not** rule out every possible coupling in Hamming cost. The weighted choice `w_0=1`, `w_leaf=1/3` satisfies the theorem with `κ=1/2`; its diameter is `2`, its minimum weight is `1/3`, and the resulting certified TV bound is `6e^{-t/2}`. The independent exact-boundary enumeration is in [`star_check.py`](star_check.py); it reports all 16-state influences and checks the inequalities. It has no external package dependencies.

This example also separates influence asymmetry from asymmetric physical couplings: the Hamiltonian is an ordinary undirected Ising star. A strong field at the center makes leaves weakly affect the center conditional, while the center still affects each leaf by `1/2`.

## Prior art and novelty status

Bubley and Dyer introduced path coupling as a method for reducing contraction proofs to adjacent states ([1997 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1997.646111); [author-hosted PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~montanar/TEACHING/Stat375/papers/path_coupling.pdf)). Dyer, Goldberg, and Jerrum systematically developed dependency-matrix norm criteria for random-update and systematic-scan Glauber dynamics, with heat-bath-specific improvements ([Annals of Applied Probability paper, DOI 10.1214/08-AAP532](https://doi.org/10.1214/08-AAP532); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702744)). These sources already establish that this is a standard rapid-mixing mechanism; the present report claims no novelty. Its contribution to the parent audit is the explicit continuous-time weighted-Wasserstein derivation, a charged mixing bound, and a concrete orientation/coupling diagnostic.

## Assumptions, costs, and unresolved scope

- The state space is a finite product, `μ` has full support, and the dynamics resamples the exact one-site conditional at rate one. The bound does not cover approximate conditionals, asynchronous update schedules, block updates, hard constraints without a separate irreducibility argument, or non-product state spaces.
- For general supplied conditionals, computing the worst-case `c_ij` can require enumerating exponentially many boundaries. The theorem is an implication from a verified influence certificate; it is not itself an efficient certificate-finding algorithm. For pairwise Ising, the upper bound `tanh(β|J_ij|)` is directly available from the input interactions.
- The continuous-time rate counts all sites at rate one, so the total event rate is `n`. Simulating to the certified TV threshold takes expected `O((n/κ)log(D_w/(w_min ε)))` single-site update events, with per-update conditional-sampling cost still to be charged by the application.
- If the weighted column condition fails, this proof says nothing about mixing. The condition is sufficient, not necessary; no claim is made about low-temperature systems, phase coexistence, or sharp thresholds.
- The star calculation is an exact finite mechanism check, not evidence that the condition is necessary, nor evidence against alternative couplings or other transport metrics.
