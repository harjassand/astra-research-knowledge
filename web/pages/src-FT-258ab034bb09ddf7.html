\[

## 3. New short-connector modulus: strong continuity without strict coercivity

\[

\[

\[
 A_U(\tau)=2\int_0^\tau\mathbb E|D(EU)|^4\,dt\longrightarrow0.
\]

Let (\delta=W-U\). A base connector on ([0,2\tau]\times\mathbb T) is

\[

\]

It starts and ends at (U), and its derivative energy is exactly (A_U(\tau)). It is continuous across the fold, so it is Sobolev-compatible even if the normal derivative jumps.

To change the final trace by (\delta), set (t=2\tau-x) and add

\[

\]

where (\kappa(0)=1,\kappa(1)=0) is a fixed smooth cutoff. This term has initial trace zero and final trace (\delta). The product rule, the fixed extension bound and its (L^4) slice bound give

\[
 \int|D\Psi_\delta|^4
   \le C\bigl(\|\delta\|_X^4+\tau^{-3}\|\delta\|_4^4\bigr).
\]

Consequently (\Phi=\Phi_U+\Psi_\delta) connects (U) to (W), with

\[
 \int|D\Phi|^4\le K_U(\tau,\delta)

Hölder on the slab of normalized measure (2\tau) bounds its (L^2) derivative energy by ((2\tau)^{1/2}K_U^{1/2}). Domination applied to this connector and to its (x)-reversal gives

\[

 \le C_F\bigl[|B|^2(2\tau)^{1/2}K_U(\tau,\delta)^{1/2}

Reflection invariance of (F) is not assumed: both actions are bounded in absolute value using their unchanged derivative norms.

Taking (\tau=\|\delta\|_X=s\) for sufficiently small (s>0) gives (K_U\le C[A_U(s)+s+s^4]). Therefore (3.1) tends to zero as (W\to U) in (X). This proves strong critical-trace continuity **at every trace**, including rough traces. The argument applies to any finite dominating adjusted storage, not only the extremal incoming value.

At the origin, (2.1) supplies the sharper modulus (O_B(s^2+s^4)). If the fixed base trace (U\in W^{1,4}(\mathbb T)), mollifier differentiation gives (A_U(\tau)\le C\tau\|U_y\|_4^4), so (3.1) yields the **pointwise Lipschitz bound**

Their extension tails need not have a uniform rate. Neither a bounded-critical-ball Lipschitz modulus nor an incoming-gradient bound is asserted here. Other arguments might provide additional bounds for (G); their impossibility is not claimed.

Thus non-strict quasiconvexity suffices for finite strong-continuous storage, while the earlier strict theorem supplies the stronger uniform local modulus by a different mechanism.
