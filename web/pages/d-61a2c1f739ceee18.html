QUESTION / QUANTIFIERS

For n=2j, 1<=ell<=n, put L=ell(ell+1), N=n(n+2), z=L/sqrt(N),

  eta_n,ell = (-1)^n (n+1) { n/2 n/2 ell ; n/2 n/2 ell },
  mu_n,ell  = n!(n+1)!/[(n-ell)!(n+ell+1)!],
  q_n,ell   = (1-eta_n,ell)/(1-mu_n,ell)^2.

Earlier results eliminate bounded z, the explicit region z<=log(N)/10,
fixed fractional bands 0<a<=ell/n<=b<1, and the high endpoint. A vanishing-q
sequence, if one exists, must have n,z->infinity, ell/n->0, z>log(N)/10,
eta->1 and mu->0. The requested residual scale is therefore mesoscopic:
ell=o(n) and L>sqrt(N) log(N)/10. At leading order the latter says
ell^2>n log(N)/10; it is a constant-factor lower cutoff, not a claim that
ell/sqrt(n log n) must diverge.

No proof of eta<=1-epsilon uniformly on this residual scale, and no
countersequence with q->0, emerged here. Status remains UNKNOWN. The finite
interval records below are diagnostics, not a uniform theorem.

EXACT POINTWISE ENCLOSURE

The terminating series and exact ratio are

  eta = sum_(r=0..ell) (-1)^r t_r,  t_0=1,
  t_(r+1)/t_r = R_r
    = [L-r(r+1)]^2 / [(r+1)^2 (N-r(r+2))].

For every r<ell the already-derived Racah inequality gives

  0<=R_r<=z^2/(r+1)^2.                                  (1)

The new evaluator is `mesoscopic_racah_interval.py`; its full output is
`mesoscopic_racah_interval.json`. It uses only integer arithmetic. Fix S=2^P
and enclose each t_r by integer endpoints [a_r,b_r]/S. Starting with
a_0=b_0=S, update exactly by

  a_(r+1)=floor(a_r p_r/q_r),
  b_(r+1)=ceil(b_r p_r/q_r),

where p_r=[L-r(r+1)]^2 and q_r=(r+1)^2[N-r(r+2)]. Alternating interval
addition gives exact dyadic endpoints for eta; the JSON stores both these
integer endpoint numerators and their common denominator 2^P. The coherent
multiplier mu is enclosed by the same outward-rounded recurrence applied to
its positive product factors (n-s)/(n+s+2).

Here is the precision certificate. If w_r=b_r-a_r, outward rounding gives
  w_(r+1)<=R_r w_r+1.
Every consecutive product of the majorants z^2/k^2 is at most exp(2z):
before k crosses z the product is bounded by one term z^(2m)/(m!)^2, and
after the crossing each additional factor is <=1; moreover
  sum_(m>=0) z^(2m)/(m!)^2 <= exp(2z).
Thus w_r<=r exp(2z), and the width of the alternating-sum interval is at
most ell(ell+1) exp(2z)/2^P. The product interval for mu has width at most
ell/2^P because all its factors are <=1.

The script sets

  P = ceil(2000 z_up/693) + ceil(log2(ell(ell+1))) + 100 + 2,
  z_up = L/n,

using n<sqrt(N)<n+1. The rational inequality ln(2)>693/1000 follows already
from the first three terms of ln(2)=2 sum_(k>=0) 1/[(2k+1)3^(2k+1)]; hence
ceil(2000 z_up/693) is an integer upper bound for log2(exp(2z)). This proves
both eta and mu interval widths are <2^-100. No floating point enters the
endpoint construction or precision rule. Display decimals in the JSON are
rounded outward; exact dyadic numerators/denominator are retained.

For eta<1 and mu<1 certified by the intervals, q is enclosed monotonically:

  q_low  = (1-eta_high)/(1-mu_low)^2,
  q_high = (1-eta_low )/(1-mu_high)^2.

The code asserts its claimed interval-width target at every point. Direct
Fraction arithmetic independently checked containment at (n,ell)=(1024,45),
(4096,222), and (16384,1448); all three exact rational sums lie in the
dyadic intervals.

WHICH INTERVAL SCALES FAIL

The earlier positive-term Racah-to-Bessel error is
  |eta-J_0(2z)| <= 2(z^2+3z) exp(2z)/(3 sqrt(N)).
It is effective through z<=log(N)/10, but its right side ceases to be small
in the residual region. This is a proved limitation of that bound, not a
counterexample to the desired statement.

The pointwise fixed-point evaluator also cannot use a fixed P as z grows.
At P=256 bits, its certified eta interval width is already at least 2^460
for (n,ell)=(65536,4096), at least 2^2246 for (16384,3821), and at least
2^6425 for (65536,12416). The widths are generated by outward-rounding each
large positive term before the alternating cancellation. These figures
show failure of that fixed-precision interval enclosure; they do NOT show
that exact rational evaluation fails. The adaptive prescription instead
uses P=866, 2699, and 6920 bits, respectively, and certifies widths below
2^-100 at those exact points.

The adaptive method is pointwise, and its cost grows as
P >= 2z/ln(2) + 2 log2(ell) + 100 + O(1).
It gives no finite uniform precision as z->infinity and no interval over
the unbounded integer parameter set (n,ell). A collection of such finite
pointwise certificates, however large, cannot prove eta<=1-epsilon for all
residual modes or produce a q->0 sequence. The non-effective shifted-column
compactness proof for fixed positive ell/n does not supply a rate as
ell/n->0. These are the exact gaps left by this attack.
