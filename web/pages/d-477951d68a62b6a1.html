## Fresh names can invalidate a syntactic restriction

A displayed-atom guard is not a sound EF interface. For any A,B introduce fresh e<->B and use the apparently admitted c(A,e). Closed implication congruence gives

    (A->(A->B)) -> (A->(A->e)),
    (A->e) -> (A->B).

Composing these with c(A,e) produces c(A,B) at constant Frege-line overhead. Every extension freshness condition holds and the final formula is extension-free. Thus checking guards only on written names recovers unrestricted contraction. A guarded EF lower-bound theorem must check the guard AFTER acyclic definition unfolding, or use another proven representation-stable admission policy. This does not affect the old uniform affine EF theorem.

The boundary is stronger than the old affine countermodel: for c(p,linear0), ordinary Boolean evaluation of its Chu translation is p. Setting p=0 refutes it even in a classical target. Merely adding target contraction cannot make the translation universal.
