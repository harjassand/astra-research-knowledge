Abstract
QMC designs were introduced in a 2014 paper by Brauchart, Saff and the present
authors (Math. Comp. 83:2821–2851). They represent a novel approach to cubature on
the sphere Sd , in which instead of requiring cubature rules to be exact for polynomials
up to a certain degree, a sequence of cubature rules is a QMC design sequence if the
worst-case error for functions in a Sobolev space Hs(Sd) is of order O(N−s/d), where
N is the number of cubature points. The original paper considered only equal cubature
weights, but the present paper allows positive weights that sum to 1. After reviewing
the known results, the present paper presents necessary and sufﬁcient conditions for
QMC design sequences, one of which requires only a ﬁnite sum for each tested value
of N . The paper also gives experimental results that aim to give insight into the design
of QMC designs, and extends the known QMC designs to a 3-dimensional example.

1.1 Conjectured QMC Design Sequences and Strengths for d = 2
The paper [9] demonstrated examples of QMC design sequences with equal weights,
while [ 20] discusses QMC design strength for some random conﬁgurations. The
sequences studied in [ 9] include sequences of spherical designs (where a spherical t-
design is an equal weight cubature rule that integrates exactly all spherical polynomials
of degree ≤ t), and also speciﬁc rules not relying on polynomial exactness. Examples
for S2 include generalized spiral points from Saff and Kuijlaars [16], Rakhmanov, Saff
and Zhou [ 15], and a modiﬁed version Bauer [ 2] introduced for star catalogs, which
appear to be QMC designs of strength 3. Fibonacci points [ 1] have a similar spiral
construction. Other examples are a point set chosen to maximize the sum of Euclidean
distances (apparent strength 4); and a (Fekete) point set chosen to maximize the deter-
minant with respect to a polynomial basis (apparent strength 1.5). Note that point sets
chosen to minimize/maximize a criterion cannot be quickly generated for large N ,
and can be unreliable for estimating strengths. On the other hand fast methods such
as equal area partitions and generalized spiral constructions are currently restricted to
S2, except for Leopardi’s equal area partititon points [ 14].

case of spherical designs little is known theoretically. An exception is the maximum
sum of distances point set, which is proved in [ 9] to be a QMC design sequence for
the limited range s ∈ (1, 2). The minimum energy, maximum determinant, maximum
sum of distance and spherical designs are found via optimization, so do not scale well

6 Conclusion
QMC design sequences achieve a speciﬁed rate of convergence for the worst case error
of numerical integration of functions in a Sobolev space on Sd , d ≥ 2. This paper
focuses on tools to help analyze potential QMC design sequences, especially when
d > 2.
Proving the strength of a QMC design sequence remains elusive, even for d = 2.
New numerical experiments with equal area partition points with equal weight on S3
are discussed. Also the behavior of the strength as the dimension d increases is not
known.
Fast generation of good point sets and weights for cubature over Sd , d > 2 and
large N that achieve a fast rate of convergence remains a challenge.
