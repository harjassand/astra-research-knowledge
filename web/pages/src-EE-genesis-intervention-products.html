I derived an experimental completion theorem for unknown, heterogeneous interventions.

Its central operation is:

Combining controls multiplicatively makes inner products of their unknown microscopic rate changes observable. Those inner products certify whether a data-derived combination of controls can recover the hidden predictive residual.

The experiment does not reconstruct the microscopic generator or estimate each transition’s response coefficient. It can also start from a reproducible nonstationary distribution.

The result is conditional on an explicit access interface. Product composition must be justified, and unobserved internal transitions must either remain unchanged or be covered by an activity counter. Thermodynamic rate ratios alone still cannot supply this information.

Assessment against the mission
The supported advance is an experimentally acquired criterion for whether unknown interventions contain enough information to certify controlled state sufficiency.

It removes the need to calibrate every microscopic kinetic response. Unknown gains can be heterogeneous and substantial; their relevant inner products and the error of the proposed completion are measured through product experiments. The resulting positive certificate applies to nonstationary preparations and supports full-path prediction and control-performance bounds for tested action libraries.

The exact remaining boundary is also clearer. The theorem does not eliminate structural knowledge of the actuator. Product composition and complete accounting of affected events remain indispensable parts of this route. The source-clock counterexample prevents replacing them by thermodynamic ratios alone.

The strongest practical next obligation is therefore not another concealed finite-state example. It is to realize and validate this product/event-access interface at acceptable coefficient, rate and preparation costs. No such laboratory demonstration, optimized sample complexity, or independently major breakthrough status has been established here.
