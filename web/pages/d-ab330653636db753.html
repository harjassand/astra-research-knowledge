N10 — Hidden dissipation under finite-bandwidth observation
Version: v1 (append-only frozen record)
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: candidate theorem, internally derived; one finite construction checked by an executable script. No empirical confirmation, external proof review, or priority claim.

1. Exact target and scope

Question. For a stationary nonequilibrium system observed only through a finite-bandwidth, even-valued trajectory, which dissipation information survives when the observed signal itself is time-reversal symmetric?

Model for the theorem. X_t is an irreducible finite-state continuous-time Markov jump process with time-independent rates k_ij and stationary law pi. All microscopic states are even under time reversal. The physical process obeys local detailed balance, so its steady-state entropy production rate divided by k_B is the stationary path-space relative-entropy rate and is at least the rate computed from the state-transition generator if multiple physical channels have been aggregated. An observation Y_t=g(X_t) is a fixed deterministic map into {0,1}; measurements are instantaneous samples at a finite interval and do not resolve jumps between samples. Logs are natural, so rates below are in nats/time, or k_B nats/time when multiplied by k_B.

The main candidate is a model-independent positive EPR floor from an anti-correlated binary pair, even when every observed path has exactly zero time-reversal KL. A three-state driven ring realizes that wedge. The exact minimum EPR over all compatible CTMC completions remains open.

2. Additive-reversibilization inequality

Write the stationary directed flows on an unordered edge {i,j} as a=pi_i k_ij and b=pi_j k_ji. Let L* be the stationary adjoint of generator L and define R=(L+L*)/2. Then R has stationary law pi and is reversible, with rates r_ij=(a+b)/(2 pi_i). If P_L^[0,T] and P_R^[0,T] are stationary path laws, the stationary path-KL rate is

  h(L||R) = sum_{unordered {i,j}} [ a ln(2a/(a+b)) + b ln(2b/(a+b)) ].

The state-level steady EPR rate is

  sigma = sum_{unordered {i,j}} (a-b) ln(a/b).

For one edge put s=a+b and x=(a-b)/s. Its contribution to h is s F(x), where

  F(x)=1/2 [(1+x)ln(1+x)+(1-x)ln(1-x)],

and its contribution to sigma is 2s x atanh(x). The inequality

  F(x) <= (x/2) atanh(x)

holds for |x|<1. For x>=0, the difference has derivative

  1/2 [x/(1-x^2)-atanh(x)] >= 0,

because x/(1-x^2)-atanh(x) is zero at 0 and has derivative 2x^2/(1-x^2)^2. Both sides are even. Summing edges gives h(L||R)<=sigma/4. Thus, for every T,

  D(P_L^[0,T] || P_R^[0,T]) = T h(L||R) <= T sigma/4.       (1)

For channel-resolved physical jumps, the physical EPR is at least the aggregated state-path EPR by the log-sum inequality, so (1) remains valid with the physical EPR on the right. The factor 1/4 is sharp at the edge level in the small-current limit: F(x)~x^2/2 and (x/2)atanh(x)~x^2/2.

3. Binary two-frame dissipation floor

Let Y_t=g(X_t) be stationary and binary, with p=P(Y_0=1), q=P(Y_0=1,Y_t=1). Stationarity implies P(0,1)=P(1,0)=p-q. If the pair law is compared to the pair law under R, both laws have the same one-time marginal p. Under R,

  q_R-p^2 = <f,exp(tR)f>_pi >= 0,  f=1_{g=1}-p,

since R is self-adjoint in L^2(pi) and exp(tR) is positive semidefinite. Therefore every additive-reversible comparison law Q has q_Q>=p^2.

If the measured law has q<p^2, then

  D(P_(Y0,Yt) || Q_(Y0,Yt)) >= I_P(Y0;Yt)                  (2)

for every such Q. To see this, fix p and parameterize any symmetric binary pair law by r=Q(1,1). Its four cells are r,p-r,p-r,1-2p+r. The KL from the measured law is convex in r and is minimized at r=q; under the constraint r>=p^2>q it is minimized at r=p^2, the independent law. The KL there is exactly the measured mutual information I_P. Data processing in (1), first to the observed pair, and (2) yield

  sigma >= 4 I_P(Y_0;Y_t)/t > 0,  whenever q<p^2.            (3)

This holds for every finite-state CTMC completion of the observed stationary binary pair, regardless of hidden-state count or transition activity. It is strictly stronger than the elementary covariance/Pinsker bound. It is a lower bound, not a proof that 4I/t is the exact infimum over physical completions.

4. Exact reversible-output realization

Take X_t on Z_3. From each state it jumps one step clockwise at rate a>0 and one step counterclockwise at rate b>0, with a!=b. The stationary distribution is uniform. Set equal state energies and couple clockwise jumps to a chemical fuel with free-energy drop Delta_mu/3 per step, where beta Delta_mu=3 ln(a/b). Then local detailed balance holds on each edge, the total cycle affinity is 3 ln(a/b), and

  sigma_ring = (a-b) ln(a/b) nats/time.                       (4)

Observe only Y=1_{X=0}. The generator eigenvalues are 0 and -3(a+b)/2 +/- i sqrt(3)(a-b)/2, giving

  p = 1/3,
  q(t) = P(Y_0=1,Y_t=1)
       = 1/9 + (2/9) exp[-3(a+b)t/2] cos[sqrt(3)(a-b)t/2],
  Cov(Y_0,Y_t) = (2/9) exp[-3(a+b)t/2] cos[sqrt(3)(a-b)t/2].   (5)

For any finite a>b one can choose a finite t with cosine negative, so q(t)<p^2 and (3) gives a strict positive universal floor.

The full binary output is nevertheless time-reversal symmetric. Every visit to state 0 is a regeneration point. Its 1-sojourn is exponential with rate a+b. On leaving 0, the exit direction is independent of the holding time, and the ensuing 0-sojourn (the excursion through states 1,2 until return to 0) has one fixed phase-type law. Strong Markov regeneration makes the successive 1-sojourns iid, the successive 0-sojourns iid, and the two sequences independent. The stationary version of this alternating renewal signal is invariant under reversal: the two iid interval sequences reverse in order without changing law, and the stationary offset within the interval containing the origin is uniform and maps to its complementary offset under reversal. Therefore every finite observed word has the same probability as its reversed word. In particular, for any finite sampling bandwidth, the observed path KL against its reverse is exactly zero; choose the finite sample lag t in (5) to retain the negative pair covariance.

This is a strict separation: the usual observed path-KL lower bound is zero, yet every finite-state locally detailed-balanced CTMC that generates the measured binary pair has sigma>=4I/t>0. The direct sample statistic needs only a single lag. The ring itself is a physical finite-state realization, so this is not just a formal binary process.

Numerical point checked in verify_ring.py: a=10, b=1, t=0.258152904363. Then p=1/3, q=0.109770041844, covariance=-0.00134106926717, I=1.82280412191e-5 nats, and (3) gives sigma>=2.82437902670e-4 nats/time. The ring has sigma=9 ln 10=20.7232658369 nats/time. A five-frame sampled-word enumeration has maximum forward/reverse probability discrepancy 2.8e-17. This is internal arithmetic evidence only, not experimental validation.

5. Finite-data protocol and resource accounting

A finite-rate assay is: prepare N independent replicas in the stationary operating condition; for each replica record the even binary occupancy at times 0 and t; estimate p from the first frame and q from the pair indicator. This does not require resolving any between-frame jump. Independent stationary replicas or independently reset preparations are a real condition; a single serial record does not have this concentration guarantee without a mixing bound.

For confidence level 1-alpha, let epsilon=sqrt(ln(4/alpha)/(2N)). Hoeffding plus a union bound gives |p_hat-p|<=epsilon and |q_hat-q|<=epsilon with probability at least 1-alpha. Since |p^2-p_hat^2|<=2epsilon,

  d_L = max(0, p_hat^2-q_hat-3epsilon)

is a confidence lower bound on p^2-q. The binary pair table differs from the independent table by +/-d in four cells, so its total-variation distance is 2d. Pinsker gives I>=8d^2. Combining with (3), with confidence at least 1-alpha,

  sigma >= 32 d_L^2/t.                                      (6)

Cost is N independently stationary pair records, N*t total observation time, plus the separately recorded cost/time for preparation, reset, and waiting to stationary state. If independent starts are unavailable, the bound (6) is not justified without additional dependence/mixing assumptions. For a=10,b=1 the effect is small: at alpha=.05 the crude Hoeffding guarantee needs about 1.1e7 independent pairs just to make d_L positive at the exact model mean. This demonstrates the conservative bound's practical limitation; better calibrated confidence regions or longer trajectory methods are needed before claiming an efficient assay.

6. Non-identifiability of the upper tail

Given any compatible physical model X, append an independent three-state equal-energy fuel-driven ring Z with rates u clockwise and v counterclockwise; observe/control only X. The complete observed trajectory law under every visible-only intervention is unchanged. The extra EPR is

  delta_sigma=(u-v)ln(u/v).

For any target c>0 choose a fixed ratio r>1 and set v=c/((r-1)ln r), u=rv; then delta_sigma=c. Thus compatible total EPR has no finite upper bound, even with infinite-duration passive records or arbitrary adaptive controls applied only to the visible subsystem. For the binary ring example, the identified set over finite-state LDB completions is bounded below by (3) and above only by infinity; this record does not compute the exact lower endpoint.

7. Broader reflection-positive extension (necessary condition only)

For an equilibrium finite-state CTMC and positive times 0<s_1<...<s_m, let U=(Y_{-s_1},...,Y_{-s_m}) and V=(Y_{s_1},...,Y_{s_m}), with all observed variables even. Conditional independence of past and future given X_0, and detailed balance, give

  H_uv=P(U=u,V=v)=sum_x pi_x g_x(u)g_x(v),
  g_x(u)=P(V=u | X_0=x).

So H is completely positive: it is a sum of nonnegative rank-one matrices. For a nonequilibrium CTMC, comparison to its additive reversibilization and (1), over the window length T=2s_m, imply

  sigma >= (4/T) inf_{H in C} D(P_(U,V)||H),

where C may be any computable relaxation containing every equilibrium reflection table with matching one-frame marginals. This is a candidate route beyond pairwise covariance/PSD tests. Complete positivity is necessary, not sufficient for an equilibrium Markov realization; one must not treat the CP cone as a complete characterization. A claimed CP-vs-PSD witness also needs a physically realizable nonequilibrium output law and exact finite-state approximation bounds before it counts as an example. This extension is not claimed as completed discovery here.

8. Alternatives, counterexamples, and exact remaining gap

- Observed path irreversibility: standard data processing gives D(P_obs||P_obs^R)/T<=sigma. It is zero for the ring output above, so it cannot certify the positive floor (3).
- Current/TUR methods can be silent when the visible current is zero; this example has no visible 1->0 versus 0->1 imbalance.
- Response-only inference does not fix the hidden upper tail: an independent driven cycle is unaffected by any intervention restricted to observed coordinates.
- A negative covariance at one lag is only a sufficient witness. Nonnegative covariance at all measured lags does not imply equilibrium, and no exact model-free EPR value follows.
- Time-reversal parity matters. The theorem is for even observables and purely configurational jump states. Odd variables require the physical parity involution in the reversal law.
- Bandwidth aliasing matters for the covariance witness: a fixed sampler can land at lags with nonnegative cosine. The protocol should choose/tune a finite lag where (5) is negative; no between-frame jump resolution is needed.
- Unknown: is 4I/t sharp as an infimum over all finite-state locally detailed-balanced CTMC completions of a binary pair? Unknown: can higher reflection-word tables yield a strictly stronger general lower bound for actual finite-state outputs? Unknown: the exact identified EPR set for a full sampled hidden-Markov law.
- Empirical confirmation is absent. Simulations and exact finite-state arithmetic cannot establish whether a real molecular signal follows the model.

9. Primary-source comparison (live access, 2026-10-09)

1. É. Roldán and J. M. R. Parrondo, “Entropy production and Kullback-Leibler divergence between stationary trajectories of discrete systems,” Phys. Rev. E 85, 031129 (2012), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.85.031129, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.85.031129; also arXiv:1201.5613. Their stated result is that stationary observed time-series KL against reversal lower-bounds physical EPR. This is the baseline data-processing inference. It does not supply the positive lower bound for a time-reversible observed binary process derived here.
2. C. P. Amann, T. Schmiedl, and U. Seifert, “Can one identify nonequilibrium in a three-state system by analyzing two-state trajectories?”, J. Chem. Phys. 132, 041102 (2010), DOI 10.1063/1.3294567, arXiv:1001.0528 (v1 submitted 2010-01-04), https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0528. The abstract studies a stationary three-state system viewed as an effective on/off trace and reports a phase region where the trace is compatible only with NESS. This establishes qualitative equilibrium-incompatibility as prior art; the present candidate gives an all-finite-state CTMC EPR floor through additive reversibilization and binary pair mutual information.
3. B. Wu and C. Jia, “Parameter inference and nonequilibrium identification for Markovian systems based on coarse-grained observations,” arXiv:2406.19586, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19586 (current version accessed 2026-10-09). It derives general sufficient-statistic criteria for equilibrium feasibility from arbitrarily coarse-grained CTMC trajectories, generalizing Amann et al. This is a close qualitative identification framework. The candidate here is a direct quantitative lower bound on EPR that remains positive even when observed full path KL is zero.
4. P. E. Harunari et al., “What to Learn from a Few Visible Transitions’ Statistics?”, Phys. Rev. X 12, 041026 (2022), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevX.12.041026, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.12.041026. They use visible transition sequences and intertransition-time statistics for EPR lower bounds. This is a different partial-observation channel and motivates not treating state-sampled KL as the only observable statistic.
5. J. Schnakenberg, “Network theory of microscopic and macroscopic behavior of master equation systems,” Rev. Mod. Phys. 48, 571 (1976), DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.48.571, https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.48.571. It is the classic network/cycle-affinity framework underlying the ring’s steady EPR accounting.
