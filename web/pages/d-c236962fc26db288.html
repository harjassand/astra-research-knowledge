Cycle 2 leaf — explicit Hermitian-root audit of the SU(m) occupation-layer taper
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: independent internal verification plus scoped exact finite checks

SCOPE AND RESULT

I independently expanded the Hermitian root generators in the bosonic
occupation basis for H_N=Sym^N(C^m). The two Hermitian generators associated
with an unordered root pair contribute exactly one full oriented ladder-edge
conductance, not twice that conductance and not half of it. This verifies the
root-edge energy sum used for the radial occupation-layer taper.

The exact sum and cutoff inequalities show no failure of the claimed upper
bound in its stated rank regime. Keeping both positive terms in the exact
taper norm improves the displayed finite-cutoff estimate. The linear layer
taper is feasible but is not generally energy-optimal: an exact m=2, N=16,
rank-2 example gives a lower-energy radial taper and the exact minimum in that
two-layer diagonal taper space.

This is a targeted audit of the taper calculation. It does not independently
verify the Fischer decomposition, the full SU(m) Casimir spectrum, or the
rank–spectral lower bound in the cycle result.

1. GENERATORS AND THEIR EDGE CONTRIBUTIONS

Let E_ij denote the standard matrix unit on C^m. For each i<j choose

  F_ij=(E_ij+E_ji)/2,
  G_ij=(E_ij-E_ji)/(2i).

These matrices are Hermitian and satisfy Tr(F_ij^2)=Tr(G_ij^2)=1/2;
different root generators are orthogonal for the trace inner product. Add any
orthonormal basis of the traceless diagonal Hermitian matrices, also with
Tr(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2. This is the normalization in the cycle result.

On Sym^N(C^m), the derived representation is the bosonic action

  rho(E_ij)=a_i^* a_j,

so rho(E_ij)|n_1,...,n_m>
  =sqrt(n_j(n_i+1)) |n+e_i-e_j>

when n_j>0, and is zero otherwise. The induced Hermitian root generators are

  rho(F_ij)=(A+A^*)/2,
  rho(G_ij)=(A-A^*)/(2i),  A=rho(E_ij).

For a diagonal operator X|n>=x_n|n>, the oriented edge n ->
n+e_i-e_j has ladder coefficient squared w_n^2=n_j(n_i+1). The corresponding
matrix element of [A,X] is

  <n+e_i-e_j|[A,X]|n>
       =sqrt(w_n^2) (x_n-x_{n+e_i-e_j}).

The commutators [A,X] and [A^*,X] have disjoint matrix-entry supports and
equal Hilbert–Schmidt norms. Therefore

  ||[rho(F_ij),X]||_HS^2
      = (||[A,X]||_HS^2+||[A^*,X]||_HS^2)/4
      = (1/2)||[A,X]||_HS^2,

and the same calculation gives

  ||[rho(G_ij),X]||_HS^2=(1/2)||[A,X]||_HS^2.

Thus the pair sum is exactly

  ||[rho(F_ij),X]||_HS^2+||[rho(G_ij),X]||_HS^2
       = sum_valid_edges w_n^2 |x_n-x_{n+e_i-e_j}|^2.       (1)

This explicitly settles the possible normalization ambiguity: the 1/2 from
each Hermitian generator is canceled by summing the two generators.

2. SPECIALIZATION TO THE HIGHEST-WEIGHT LAYER TAPER

Use beta=(beta_2,...,beta_m), s=|beta|, beta_1=N-s. There are
g_s=binom(s+d-1,d-1) occupation states in layer s, with d=m-1. Let x_beta
depend only on s, and in particular let

  x_s=c(R+1-s) for s<=R, and x_s=0 for s>R, 0<=R<N.

Diagonal Cartan generators commute with X. If i,j>=2, a_i^*a_j preserves
s, so x_beta is unchanged and both Hermitian root commutators vanish. The
remaining positive-root pairs are (1,i), i=2,...,m. Orient the edge by
A_i=rho(E_i1)=a_i^*a_1. It maps beta to beta+e_i with

  |<beta+e_i|A_i|beta>|^2
       = beta_1(beta_i+1)=(N-s)(beta_i+1).

For every such edge with s<=R, x_s-x_{s+1}=c, including the boundary edge
s=R to s=R+1. Applying (1), then summing the d roots for each beta, gives

  E(X)=c^2 sum_{s=0}^R (N-s)
                 sum_{|beta|=s} sum_{i=2}^m (beta_i+1)
       =c^2 sum_{s=0}^R (N-s)(s+d)g_s
       =c^2 W_{N,R}.                                      (2)

The middle equality uses sum_i(beta_i+1)=s+d and the fact that there are
g_s beta-vectors in layer s. This is the exact root-edge sum, including the
outer boundary of the support. There are no omitted Cartan or within-layer
terms: they vanish identically for this X.

The exact norm is

  ||X||_HS^2=c^2 Z_R,
  Z_R=sum_{s=0}^R g_s(R+1-s)^2
      =binom(R+d+2,d+2)+binom(R+d+1,d+2).                 (3)

For example, the last identity follows by putting k=R-s and using the
generating functions sum_s g_s z^s=(1-z)^(-d) and
sum_k(k+1)^2 z^k=(1+z)(1-z)^(-3).

The root conductance sum in (2) has the closed form

  W_{N,R}=d sum_{s=0}^R (N-s) binom(s+d,d)
         =Nd binom(R+d+1,d+1)
             -d(d+1)binom(R+d+1,d+2).                    (4)

Here (s+d)g_s=d binom(s+d,d),
sum_{s<=R}binom(s+d,d)=binom(R+d+1,d+1), and
s binom(s+d,d)=(d+1)binom(s+d,d+1).

3. UPPER-BOUND AND CUTOFF AUDIT

Since N-s<=N, dropping the nonnegative second term in (4) gives
W_{N,R}<=Nd binom(R+d+1,d+1). Also, from (3), write

  A=binom(R+d+2,d+2),  B=binom(R+d+1,d+2),
  Z_R=A+B=A(2R+d+2)/(R+d+2).

The identity B/A=R/(R+d+2) and
binom(R+d+1,d+1)/A=(d+2)/(R+d+2) yield the sharper finite-cutoff estimate

  E(X_R)=W_{N,R}/Z_R
      <= Nd(d+2)/(2R+d+2).                                (5)

The cycle result's simpler estimate
E(X_R)<=Nd(d+2)/(R+d+2) remains valid; (5) improves it by the factor
(R+d+2)/(2R+d+2), tending to 1/2 when R/d grows. In particular, the
normalization formula used by the cycle result was not underestimating the
energy: retaining the second norm term only makes its upper bound stronger.

For a rank budget 1<=Q<d_N, let R=max{r:binom(r+d,d)<=Q}. Then D_R=binom(R+d,d)<=Q
and R<N. Maximality gives Q<binom(R+d+1,d), while

  binom(R+d+1,d)<=(R+d+1)^d/d!.

Hence (d!Q)^(1/d)<R+d+1<R+d+2. Substitution in the looser estimate gives

  E(X_R)< d(d+2)/(d!)^(1/d) * N Q^(-1/d).

Thus the stated upper bound holds for every 1<=Q<d_N, and in particular
throughout the smaller rank interval in the cycle result. No cutoff/rank
counterexample to that upper bound was found; the proof above checks the
critical inequalities symbolically.

4. BETTER TAPER: AN EXACT TWO-LAYER DIAGNOSTIC

The linear ramp is a convenient feasible taper, not the minimizer even among
diagonal radial tapers at a fixed support cutoff. Take m=2 (d=1), N=16, R=1.
Then Q=D_1=2, and this is inside the stated interval because
floor(alpha_2 N^d)=floor(16/8)=2. For arbitrary real layer values a=x_0,
b=x_1 with x_2=0, the exact edge and norm forms are

  E_num=16(a-b)^2+30b^2,
  ||X||_HS^2=a^2+b^2,

so the Rayleigh matrix is

  M=[[16,-16],[-16,46]].

The cycle's linear taper (a,b)=(2,1) has E=46/5. The rational taper
(a,b)=(7,3) has

  E=(16(7-3)^2+30*3^2)/(7^2+3^2)=526/58=263/29<46/5.

The exact minimum over this two-dimensional diagonal taper space is the
smallest eigenvalue of M,

  lambda_min=31-sqrt(481)<263/29<46/5.

The associated lowest-eigenvalue vector has positive entries, so it is an
admissible rank-2 diagonal taper. This is a finite example of a better taper,
not a counterexample to the claimed upper bound: both energies are far below
the stated budget bound 3N/Q=24. Optimizing the radial profile can improve
constants, but this check does not establish a new asymptotic constant or the
globally optimal rank-Q matrix.

5. TINY HERMITIAN-MATRIX CHECK

For an independent factor check, take m=2,N=2, R=1. In the occupation basis
|2,0>, |1,1>, |0,2>,

  A=a_2^*a_1=[[0,0,0],[sqrt(2),0,0],[0,sqrt(2),0]],
  F=(A+A^*)/2,
  G=(A-A^*)/(2i),
  X=diag(2,1,0)/sqrt(5).

The generator matrices F and G have adjacent off-diagonal entries of
magnitude 1/sqrt(2). For each of the two adjacent occupation edges, each
Hermitian commutator contributes 1/5 to its HS norm squared. The pair thus
contributes 2/5 per edge and

  ||[F,X]||_HS^2+||[G,X]||_HS^2=4/5.

The direct ladder-edge formula gives W/Z=(2+2)/5=4/5, exactly. This is a
3-dimensional matrix check of the normalization only; it is not evidence for
the general decomposition.

CONCLUSION AND LIMITS

The Hermitian root expansion confirms exactly the occupation-layer edge
conductance ∑_s (N-s)(s+d)g_s and the taper energy W/Z. It also exposes a
simple sharpening, equation (5), and confirms by an exact small-rank example
that a linear ramp need not be optimal. The stated coarse rank upper bound
does not fail in the claimed regime; the cutoff proof in Section 3 actually
establishes that side for all Q<dim Sym^N(C^m).

All checks here concern finite-dimensional representation matrices and exact
binomial/algebraic identities. They do not establish novelty, operational
quantum-memory transfer, or the separate cycle-2 lower bound. No cycle-1 file
was edited.
