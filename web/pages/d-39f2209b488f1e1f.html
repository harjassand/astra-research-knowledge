# Independent foundational mechanism investigation

Result: I obtained a rigorous, unconditional representation theorem connecting Diophantine geometry, Hamiltonian dynamics and undecidability. I did not obtain a verified historic-scale breakthrough.

The strongest result is that a fixed-dimensional family of quadratic symplectic polynomial automorphisms can encode the halting problem through its integer fixed points, even though every map has an explicitly constructible rational fixed point and every trajectory is an affine line.

This is a genuine theorem, with a short proof given below. However, its ingredients are classical, and I cannot establish that the resulting formulation is historically new.

## 2. Principal theorem: arithmetic universality inside quadratic symplectic maps

### Theorem

There exist a positive integer \\(D\\), an integer-coefficient polynomial automorphism

\\[ F_0:\mathbb A^{2D}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^{2D} \\]

of degree at most two, and a standard basis vector \\(e\\) in one momentum-coordinate direction such that, for every nonnegative integer \\(n\\), the map

\\[ \boxed{F_n(z)=F_0(z)+ne} \\]

has all the following properties:

1. \\(F_n\\) is an integer-coefficient symplectic polynomial automorphism, with inverse of degree at most two.
2. \\(F_n\\) is the time-one map of a complete autonomous polynomial Hamiltonian flow generated by a Hamiltonian of degree at most three.
3. Every orbit is an affine line, in the precise sense that \\[ F_n^k(z)=z+k(F_n(z)-z) \\] for all integers \\(k\\).
4. Its differential is everywhere unipotent: \\[ (DF_n(z)-I)^2=0. \\]
5. \\(F_n\\) has a rational fixed point which can be computed effectively from \\(n\\).
6. Nevertheless, the set \\[ \boxed{ {n\in\mathbb N: \operatorname{Fix}(F_n)\cap\mathbb Z^{2D} \ne\varnothing} } \\] is computably enumerable-complete, and therefore undecidable.

In particular, there is no algorithm deciding whether such a quadratic symplectic automorphism has an integer fixed point, even when an explicit rational fixed point is supplied.

This is a theorem about exact arithmetic fixed points, not about chaotic trajectories or the computational difficulty of simulating the flow.

## 3. Complete proof

### Step 1: Start with a universal Diophantine equation

By the Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem, there is an integer polynomial

\\[ U(t,x_1,\ldots,x_r) \\]

for which membership in a fixed computably enumerable-complete set \\(K\subseteq\mathbb N\\) is characterized by

\\[ n\in K \iff \exists x\in\mathbb Z^r,\quad U(n,x)=0. \\]

Any nonnegative-integer auxiliary variables can be replaced by sums of four integer squares, so the representation can be taken entirely over \\(\mathbb Z\\).

Universal Diophantine representations are established mathematics, developed notably by James P. Jones in Universal diophantine equation, Journal of Symbolic Logic (1982).&#x20;

Introduce an extra variable \\(s\\) and define

\\[ W(t,x,s)=U(t,x)(2s-1). \\]

The factor \\(2s-1\\) has two deliberately different behaviours:

- For integer \\(s\\), it never vanishes.
- For rational \\(s=\tfrac12\\), it always vanishes.

Consequently,

\\[ \exists(x,s)\in\mathbb Z^{r+1}:W(n,x,s)=0 \iff n\in K, \\]

while

\\[ W(n,x,\tfrac12)=0 \\]

for every rational \\(x\\) and every integer \\(n\\).

Thus every parameter has rational solutions, while integer solvability still encodes \\(K\\).

### Step 2: Replace the polynomial by quadratic gate equations

Choose a fixed arithmetic circuit evaluating \\(W\\), using only integer constants, addition and multiplication.

Introduce one new variable for every gate.

A multiplication gate \\(y=uv\\) becomes

\\[ y-uv=0. \\]

An addition gate \\(y=u+v\\) becomes

\\[ y-u-v=0. \\]

Constant and output constraints are linear.

We obtain a fixed collection of integer polynomials

\\[ Q_1(z),\ldots,Q_m(z) \\]

of degree at most two, whose common zero set represents the equation \\(W(t,x,s)=0\\).

Crucially, this is an exact equivalence over every commutative ring: the gate variables have uniquely determined values once the inputs are given.

For the parameter \\(n\\), add one further equation

\\[ Q_0(z)=t-n. \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \exists z\in\mathbb Z^N: Q_0(z)=\cdots=Q_m(z)=0 \iff n\in K. \\]

Every such system also has a computable rational solution, obtained by taking \\(t=n\\), \\(s=\tfrac12\\) and evaluating the circuit.

### Step 3: Convert quadratic equations into a cubic Hamiltonian

Introduce multiplier variables \\(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_m\\), and collect the configuration coordinates into

\\[ q=(z,\lambda)\in\mathbb A^D. \\]

Define

\\[ \boxed{ H_n(q)=\sum\_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_iQ_i(z). } \\]

Since every \\(Q_i\\) is quadratic or linear,

\\[ \deg H_n\le3. \\]

Now introduce momenta \\(p\in\mathbb A^D\\) and use the canonical symplectic form

\\[ \omega=\sum\_{j=1}^{D}dq_j\wedge dp_j. \\]

Define the polynomial transformation

\\[ \boxed{ F_n(q,p)= (q,p-\nabla H_n(q)). } \\]

Its inverse is explicitly

\\[ F_n^{-1}(q,p)= (q,p+\nabla H_n(q)). \\]

Because \\(\nabla H_n\\) has degree at most two, both maps are quadratic.

Moreover,

\\[ F_n^\*\omega = \sum_jdq_j\wedge \left(dp_j-\sum_k \frac{\partial^2H_n}{\partial q_j\partial q_k}dq_k\right) =\omega, \\]

since the Hessian is symmetric.

The corresponding Hamiltonian equations are

\\[ \dot q=0,\qquad \dot p=-\nabla H_n(q). \\]

Their solutions are

\\[ q(\tau)=q(0),\qquad p(\tau)=p(0)-\tau\nabla H_n(q(0)). \\]

This establishes completeness and the affine-line orbit formula.

The Jacobian has block form

\\[ DF_n= \begin{pmatrix} I&0\\\ -\operatorname{Hess}H_n&I \end{pmatrix}, \\]

so \\((DF_n-I)^2=0\\).

All the geometric and dynamical restrictions have now been verified.

### Step 4: Recover Diophantine solvability exactly

A point \\((q,p)\\) is fixed by \\(F_n\\) precisely when

\\[ \nabla H_n(q)=0. \\]

Differentiating with respect to the multiplier variables yields

\\[ \frac{\partial H_n}{\partial\lambda_i} =Q_i(z). \\]

Thus every fixed point satisfies

\\[ Q_i(z)=0\qquad(0\le i\le m). \\]

Conversely, if every \\(Q_i(z)=0\\), take all multipliers to be zero. Then

\\[ \frac{\partial H_n}{\partial z_j} = \sum_i\lambda_i \frac{\partial Q_i}{\partial z_j}=0, \\]

and hence \\(\nabla H_n=0\\).

We have proved, over both \\(\mathbb Z\\) and \\(\mathbb Q\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Fix}(F_n)\ne\varnothing \iff \exists z:\ Q_0(z)=\cdots=Q_m(z)=0. } \\]

Over integers, this is equivalent to \\(n\in K\\).

Over rationals, a fixed point always exists.

Finally, the parameter appears only through

\\[ H_n=H_0-n\lambda_0. \\]

Therefore

\\[ \nabla H_n=\nabla H_0-ne\_{\lambda_0} \\]

and

\\[ F_n=F_0+ne\_{p\_{\lambda_0}}. \\]

Only one momentum coordinate changes, by an integer translation.

Since \\(K\\) is computably enumerable-complete, the stated fixed-point decision problem is also computably enumerable-complete.

This completes the proof.

## 4. A more general proved representation principle

The construction establishes something beyond the undecidability consequence.

### Affine fixed-locus representation theorem

Every affine scheme of finite presentation over \\(\mathbb Z\\) is isomorphic to a scheme-theoretic retract of the fixed-point scheme of a quadratic integral symplectic polynomial automorphism of some affine space.

Proof. Present the scheme as the common zero locus of finitely many integer polynomials. Replace those equations by quadratic arithmetic-circuit equations. This replacement preserves the represented functor on commutative rings.

For the quadratic system \\(Q(z)=0\\), construct

\\[ H(z,\lambda)=\sum_i\lambda_iQ_i(z) \\]

and the associated symplectic shear \\(F_H\\).

There is a projection

\\[ \rho:\operatorname{Fix}(F_H)\to V(Q), \qquad (z,\lambda,p)\mapsto z, \\]

because the fixed-point equations include \\(Q_i(z)=0\\).

There is also a section

\\[ \sigma:V(Q)\to\operatorname{Fix}(F_H), \qquad z\mapsto(z,0,0). \\]

Both are polynomial morphisms defined over \\(\mathbb Z\\), and

\\[ \rho\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}\_{V(Q)}. \\]

Thus \\(V(Q)\\), and hence the original affine scheme, is a scheme-theoretic retract. \\(\square\\)

This theorem identifies the precise mechanism:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Arbitrary algebraic constraints} \longrightarrow \text{quadratic gates} \longrightarrow \text{cubic Hamiltonian} \longrightarrow \text{quadratic symplectic fixed points}. } \\]

It is valid over arbitrary commutative rings. The undecidability consequence is one application over \\(\mathbb Z\\).

## 5. Premise testing and limits

An elementary fixture checks the construction.

Take the quadratic system

\\[ y-x^2=0,\qquad y-2=0. \\]

Its Hamiltonian is

\\[ H=u(y-x^2)+v(y-2). \\]

Then

\\[ \nabla H=(-2ux,\ u+v,\ y-x^2,\ y-2). \\]

A fixed point forces \\(x^2=2\\). There are real fixed points, but no rational or integer fixed points. Direct symbolic checks confirmed the gradient identities, symplectic condition and inverse composition.&#x20;



The important limitation is that the theorem exploits exact arithmetic. It does not establish undecidability of trajectory simulation, finite-precision physical measurement, or fixed-point existence on compact symplectic manifolds.

Indeed, trajectory simulation for these maps is explicit. The dynamical difficulty is eliminated; all computational hardness is concentrated in deciding whether a polynomially described fixed locus contains an integer point.

There is also no computable function \\(B(n)\\) that always bounds the height of a smallest integer fixed point whenever one exists. Otherwise one could enumerate every integer point of height at most \\(B(n)\\) and decide \\(K\\), contradicting undecidability.

## 6. Source verification and actual novelty

Two distinctions matter.

First, the theorem above does not rely on OpenAI's claimed resolution of Hilbert's tenth problem over \\(\mathbb Q\\). Its undecidability statement follows from the classical, established integer theorem. The October 2026 rational manuscript supplies a related arithmetic-circuit normalization, but the rational decision claim itself is not formalized in the pinned Lean catalogue, and I have not independently verified its long proof.

The same construction gives an exact reduction from rational polynomial solvability to rational fixed-point existence for quadratic symplectic shears. Consequently, if the OpenAI rational-solvability theorem is correct, it transfers to this dynamical formulation. That is a conditional consequence, not an independently certified solution of the rational problem.

Second, the Hamiltonian shear itself is a classical object. Koch and Lomelí study Hamiltonian flows whose trajectories depend linearly on time, including the relationship between cubic Hamiltonians and quadratic symplectic maps.&#x20;
