## 1. Exact weak boundary dual and boundary-zero nonattainment

Reviewed `workers/radial_analysis/PROOF.txt`, lines 285–391, after independently reconstructing positivity in `POSITIVITY_INDEPENDENT_RECONSTRUCTION.md`.

    B_g(j)=1/4 integral (j.grad H+H div j)

is independent of the chosen lift: differences are zero-trace Sobolev maps, approximable by compactly supported smooth maps strongly in `W1,4` and hence uniformly. This permits pairing the gradients with `j` and values with the merely `L1` divergence. A classical normal derivative is unnecessary.

When the minimizing amplitude is globally bounded away from zero, `a=grad log r` is in `L4` and `j*=partial_a f_x(a)` is in `L^(4/3)`. The legal compact density variation divided by `4r^3` gives `f_x(a)-a.j*-div j*/4=lambda` in distributions. Thus `div j*` is `L1`. Fenchel equality then gives exact dual attainment including the boundary term. For nonzero boundary, replacing that objective by `lambda M` would be wrong. The worker retains the term correctly.

For the square example `F(A)=|A|^4`, `n=e1`, `g=x`, `M=1/5`, Jensen for the fixed mean gradient proves the unique minimizer `r=x` and energy one. Fenchel equality in any attaining dual forces `j*=4e1/x^3`, whose `L^(4/3)` norm diverges. No other attaining weak flux can avoid this, because the nonnegative Fenchel gap must vanish wherever `rho=x^4>0`.

The supremum remains exact: `j_epsilon=4e1/(x+epsilon)^3` has `q_j=-div j/4-f*(j)=0`, and its fixed-boundary expression is `1/(1+epsilon)^3 ->1`. Hence this is failure of attainment in the specified unweighted flux class, not a duality gap. The source affine translation with positive boundary avoids this counterexample.

Disposition: no decisive gap identified in this boundary-dual claim, with its stated positivity and function-space scope. A weak attained optimizer is not an acquired smooth residual certificate.

## 4. Repair of the rank-one null classification

Reviewed the fresh radial proof's Section 8. The incoming assertion that two specified Hessians force all orientation coefficients to vanish was incomplete, while the new argument supplies the missing test.

The rank-one restriction has the form `F(a tensor b)=|a|^4 h(b)`, with `h` a nonnegative convex binary quartic. Its fourth root is the gauge of a symmetric convex unit set and hence a seminorm. A null direction makes that seminorm constant on parallel lines, so an invertible input change reduces `h` to `alpha b1^4`, `alpha>=0`.

In the nine-coefficient invariant basis, matching this restriction on `q=0` leaves

    F=alpha u^2+gamma q^2+q(Lu+Mv+Nw).

At `A=e12` along `H=(s,t) tensor(x,y)`, the quadratic coefficient is `gamma x^2 t^2-2M xyst-N x^2 st`. Fixing nonzero x,t and varying s,y forces `M=N=0`, then `gamma>=0`. It does not force L. The additional family `A=diag(1,q)`, `H=e21` has constant determinant and quadratic coefficient `2alpha+Lq`. Arbitrary q forces `L=0`. The remaining convex-in-minors expression is polyconvex, and invertible input changes preserve that property.

Consequently every non-polyconvex member of this invariant rank-one-convex quartic class is strictly positive on all nonzero rank-one matrices; compactness gives `mu>0`. This reconstruction avoids the insufficient two-Hessian inference and does not invoke an inherited long classification as a black box.

## 5. A posteriori residual accuracy for a fixed direction

    J(s)=integral g_s s^4+B_g(j_s),

    integral s^4 |grad(r*/s)|^4 <= 3M(u-ell)/mu.

Poincare/Morrey therefore control the relative Sobolev and uniform error after using the positive lower bound of s.
