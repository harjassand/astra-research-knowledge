## Stronger interface

Keep the known three-state CTMC Q_b=S+bSᵀ−(1+b)I, stationary uniform π, σ(b)=(1−b)log(1/b). The learner observes endpoint labels and **may retain every timestamp**. Before each interval it can adaptively choose a deterministic positive lag or any known distribution of lags, using its full previous transcript. The next lag is independent of the future physical trajectory conditional on that transcript; no observation inside the interval, event-triggered measurement, or physical control is allowed. Internal randomization, initialization in any supplied state, arbitrary estimators and bounded stopping at N readings are allowed. Clock laws may depend on the policy's history, but not directly on the unknown b. This is a stronger interface than the untimestamped Poisson subcase.

## Lemma: uniform Fisher information per terminal observation

At a given lag t, the displacement distribution is

p_b(t)= law[(K_+−K_−) mod 3], K_+~Pois(t), K_−~Pois(bt), independent.

Differentiating the second Poisson clock gives ∂_b p_b=t(Tp_b−p_b), where T is the anticlockwise shift. Therefore

I_t(b)=t² χ²(Tp_b || p_b).

Write p_b=p_0*C_b, where C_b is the reverse-clock convolution. Convolution commutes with T and is a Markov channel, so χ² data processing implies I_t(b)≤I_t(0).

For 0<t≤1, anticlockwise shift equals clockwise shift by 2 modulo3. Before taking residues, compare Pois(t)+2 to Pois(t). The former has likelihood ratio (K)_2/t², where (K)_2=K(K−1). Poisson factorial moments give

E[(K)_2²]=t⁴+4t³+2t²,

hence χ²(Pois(t)+2 || Pois(t))=4/t+2/t². Taking residues is another Markov channel. Thus I_t(0)≤4t+2≤6.

For t≥1, the wrapped Poisson law has Fourier formula

p_{0,k}(t)=[1+2e^(−3t/2)cos(√3 t/2−2πk/3)]/3.

It satisfies min_k p_{0,k}≥(1−2e^(−3t/2))/3>0. Parseval gives Σ_k(Tp_0−p_0)_k²=2e^(−3t). Consequently

I_t(0)≤6t²e^(−3t)/(1−2e^(−3t/2))

≤6e^(−3)/(1−2e^(−3/2))<1<6,

because t²e^(−3t) decreases on [1,∞) and the denominator increases. The last strict bound can be checked without numerical arithmetic: e^(3/2)>4, so the expression is <6/(16−8)=3/4. The inequality e^(3/2)>4 follows from the first four positive exponential-series terms.

Therefore **I_t(b)≤6 for every t>0,b≥0**. Numerically the stronger constant 2 appears true, but it is neither needed nor proved here. This proof uses elementary identities and two applications of χ² data processing; it does not depend on a finite grid or symbolic positive polynomial.

## Adaptive transcript lower bound

Integrating ∂_b√p as before yields H²(p_b(t),p_c(t))≤6(b−c)²/4. Retained random times have a shared conditional law under both parameters, so averaging over that law preserves the bound. Adaptive scheduling uses the same conditional policy under each identical retained history. Hellinger-affinity recursion then gives

TV(L_b,L_c)≤sqrt(6N)|b−c|/2.

For c=b/2, 0<b≤1/2, the EP separation is ≥log2. Estimation to ε<log2/2 with success .9 under both hypotheses implies TV≥.8. Hence

**N≥10.24/(6b²)**.

This is a universal lower bound over the stated family of endpoint timing policies, now including timestamp-retaining deterministic and Poisson schedules. Extra initially supplied information that identifies b, physical interventions, hidden sensors, or event-triggered readings are outside the interface.

r08 independently completed the expected-stopping argument by telescoping affinity over alive prefixes. For almost surely finite stopping with finite E_b N and E_c N, this gives TV≤sqrt(6)|b−c|(E_b N E_c N)^(1/4)/2. Hence sqrt(E_b N E_b/2 N)≥10.24/(6b²). This is a geometric-mean lower bound, not a one-sided bound on E_b N alone. The alive-prefix Cauchy argument and truncation justification are in the independent audit.

## Matching acquisition construction with a supplied rate scale

Promise b∈[β,2β] with known 0<β≤1/4. This supplies a reverse-rate scale, not b itself. Choose iid Exp(λ) intervals with λ=1/β. From N endpoint increments, form r̂=frequency of anticlockwise displacement. Write r(b)=p_−(b,λ), using the exact rational law in POISSON_ENDPOINT_THEOREM.md.

Its derivative is

r′(b)=λ(λ+3)(2b+λ+1)/D²>0.

On the promised interval, D/λ²≤2.453125<2.5 and the numerator is ≥λ³. Thus r′(b)≥(4/25)β>β/7. Also r(b)≤4β²: its numerator ≤4β²+2+2β+1≤3.75 and D≥λ².

Clip r̂ to [r(β),r(2β)], then invert the monotone rational function on [β,2β] to get b̂. Clipping decreases probability estimation error. The mean-value theorem gives |b̂−b|≤7|r̂−r(b)|/β.

For x∈[β,2β]⊂(0,1/2], |σ′(x)|=1/x−1+log(1/x)≤2/β, using log y≤y−1. Therefore

|σ(b̂)−σ(b)|≤14|r̂−r(b)|/β².

Independent-clock translation invariance makes the increments iid, regardless of their common initial label. Bernoulli Bernstein implies failure probability ≤δ if

N≥[2r(b)+(2/3)u]log(2/δ)/u²,

where u=εβ²/14. For 0<ε≤1, the bounds above imply the sufficient reading count

**N=ceil[1600 log(2/δ)/(ε²β²)].**

Then |σ(b̂)−σ(b)|≤ε with probability ≥1−δ. Expected physical wait is N/λ=Nβ=O(log(1/δ)/(ε²β)), while the number of readings is O(log(1/δ)/(ε²β²)). Matrix acquisition is unnecessary for this supplied one-parameter family; direct one-statistic inversion is the competent comparator. Constant 1600 is deliberately conservative; no optimal constant is claimed.

The order in endpoint readings matches the general lower bound at fixed ε,δ. It also matches event-resolved physical-time scaling, while spending an extra factor 1/β in endpoint readings. This is why observing each jump is a consequential sensor capability. Merely choosing faster sampling does not remove the endpoint reading cost.

## Scientific consequence and limits

For exact full-state finite CTMCs, randomized timing repairs an established generator alias. For even a supplied homogeneous three-cycle, no endpoint timing policy can avoid quadratic reading dependence in rare reverse rates when estimating microscopic EP to fixed additive accuracy. A known reverse-rate scale allows a matching count protocol with linear physical-time dependence. These statements separate identification, statistical acquisition, reading cost and time cost.

The result is a constrained mathematical contribution, not a claim that all entropy production requires this cost: coarse-grained lower bounds, large-current systems, richer event sensors, supplied affinities and relative rather than additive accuracy have different interfaces. Nearest prior-art audit for rare-affinity minimax thermodynamic estimation is incomplete.
