GAUSSIAN HERALDING: STRONGER PROBABILITY-FREE ENERGY AND TAIL THEOREM
Date: 2026-10-07. Status: new written analytic derivation; not externally reviewed or formally certified. Import OA113 remains separately unverified. No conclusion rests on absence of source package.

0. FURTHER ESCALATION: arbitrary-phase Gaussian theorem, sharp energy order
The positive-class theorem below is a linear-energy specialization of an independently derived ALL-GAUSSIAN statement. For any physical finite-mode Gaussian input (arbitrary phases, mixedness and displacement), original total mean E, and any feasible specified h-photon herald, put Q(E)=E^2+3E. The remaining conditional state satisfies
  Tr tau N <=3h+6(2h+1)E+3Q(E),
and an explicit cutoff
  T_delta=ceil(24(1+2E)[(h+1)ln2/2+ln(1/delta)]+6Q(E)ln2)
has Pr_tau(N>=T_delta)<=delta. Thus cutoff order is (1+E)(h+log(1/delta))+E^2 with no inverse herald probability or mode-count term. This does NOT claim a classical sampler outside the positive class.
The energy order E^2 is necessary already for a vacuum herald h=0. Start from a two-mode squeezed vacuum with thermal occupation n=1 (q,p variances3/2 and cross-covariance sqrt2). Apply a local squeeze diag(s,s^(-1)) to the retained mode, and give the herald mode q-mean s, retained mean0. In units [q,p]=i, the exact energies and probability are
 E=(5/4)s^2+(3/4)s^(-2)+1/2,
 E_vac=(1/4)s^4+(1/4)(s^2+s^(-2)-2),
 p_vac=(1/2)exp(-s^2/4).
Hence E_vac/E^2 ->4/25 and E_vac/E ->infinity. This is a physical exact counterexample to any universal O(E) vacuum-herald bound; positive-kernel linear-energy improvement genuinely uses its positivity condition.

Proof of universal vacuum-energy anchor:
Use real quadrature covariance V with vacuum I/2, mean d, and split E=E_c+E_d where E_c=(tr V-m)/2, E_d=||d||^2/2. A vacuum projection on H gives
 V_0=V_R-V_RH(V_H+I/2)^(-1)V_HR,
 d_0=d_R-B d_H, B=V_RH(V_H+I/2)^(-1).
The covariance energy E_{c,0}<=E_c, because V_0<=V_R and each marginal covariance has nonnegative energy. Schur complement of [[V_R,V_RH],[V_HR,V_H+I/2]]>=0 and (V_H+I/2)^(-1)<=2I imply B B^T<=2V_R.
For every physical V, lambda_max(V)<=2E_c+1. To prove it, rotate a unit maximal-eigenvalue vector x into q1 by a passive symplectic transformation; its conjugate Omega x gives p1, with zero covariance against x because x is an eigenvector. Robertson gives Var(p1)>=1/[4lambda], and the remaining m-1 mode variances sum to at least m-1. Thus 2E_c+1>=lambda+1/(4lambda)>=lambda. This suffices without solving the sharp quadratic inequality.
Consequently ||B||^2<=4E_c+2, and vector Cauchy-Schwarz yields
 E_{d,0}=||d_R-Bd_H||^2/2
        <=(1+||B||^2)(||d_R||^2+||d_H||^2)/2
        <=(4E_c+3)E_d.
Therefore E_0<=E_c+(4E_c+3)E_d<=E^2+3E (use4E_cE_d<=E^2). For a canonical Gaussian purification of this factor, split its energy into squeezing/covariance and displacement: e_s=E_{c,0}+sum_j n_j<=2E_c, e_d=E_{d,0}<=(4E_c+3)E_d<=Q(E). Ancilla displacement is zero. Sections3-4 apply with these separate bounds. The sharper mean inequality gives
 mean<=3h+3(2h+1)e_s+3e_d<=3h+6(2h+1)E+3Q(E).
For the tail retain the quadratic-ladder bound in separated form
 ||N'P_{<=r}||<=6(r+1)(1+e_s)+3e_d.
Iteration gives kth moments<=[12(1+e_s)]^k (alpha)_k with alpha=(h+1)/2+e_d/[4(1+e_s)]. Consequently
 MGF(t)<=(1-12(1+e_s)t)^(-alpha).
At t=1/[24(1+e_s)] Chernoff gives
 Pr(N>=T)<=exp(-T/[24(1+e_s)]+[(h+1)/2+e_d/[4(1+e_s)]]ln2).
Choosing T=24(1+e_s)[(h+1)ln2/2+ln(1/delta)]+6e_d ln2, then bounding e_s<=2E and e_d<=Q(E), proves the displayed additive cutoff. Positivity improves the same anchor E_0<=E and permits the simpler linearE theorem in section1.
Primary standard formulas: Weedbrook et al., Gaussian Quantum Information, https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.3234 (Gaussian measurement/Schur covariance update and Williamson conventions; units rescaled here to vacuum I/2); Botero-Reznik source in section7 for purification normal form.

1. Precise positive-class theorem
Let rho be a finite-mode physical Gaussian state with real Bargmann kernel
R(z,w)=c exp(1/2 xi^T K xi+ell^T xi), K=[[A,C],[C,A]], ell=(g,g),
where K,ell are entrywise nonnegative. E=Tr rho N is the original total mean photon number. Project any chosen subset of modes onto a specified Fock pattern of total h photons, with nonzero probability, and retain any other modes. Denote the normalized conditional state tau. Then
  Tr tau N <= 3h+6(2h+1)E,
and for all T>=0,
  Pr_tau(N>=T) <= 2^((h+1)/2) exp(-T/[24(1+2E)]).
Consequently T=ceil(24(1+2E)[(h+1)ln2/2+ln(1/delta)]) loses at most delta conditional mass. No inverse herald probability, no mode-count term, and only linear E appear. The bound also holds for photon-count statistics after discarding modes because total retained photons can only decrease.
This strictly improves supplied N43's general positive mixed/displaced bound F=3h+6(2h+1)E+96E(1+2E)^2 and mean-based O(F/delta) cutoff. N43's full all-phase Gaussian statement may retain its larger bound; the improvement uses positive kernel for a new vacuum-energy monotonicity step.

2. Positive vacuum-energy monotonicity (exact proof)
Write X for ket/bra swap and M=XK=[[C,A],[A,C]]. M is real symmetric and entrywise nonnegative. Physical normalizability implies lambda_max(M)<1: restricting Bargmann integration to real coherent amplitudes gives I-A-C positive definite; Perron-Frobenius bounds the absolute spectrum of the nonnegative symmetric M by lambda_max(A+C). Set D_t=diag(t_1,...,t_m,t_1,...,t_m).
The photon probability generating function is
 G(t)=c det(I-D_t M)^(-1/2) exp(1/2 ell^T(I-D_t M)^(-1)D_t ell).
The Gaussian trace identity follows either from Bargmann Gaussian integration or Wick expansion. Since rho(M)<1, its logarithm expands absolutely on [0,1]^m as
 log G(t)=log c +1/2 sum_{j>=1} tr((D_t M)^j)/j
                    +1/2 sum_{j>=0} ell^T(D_t M)^j D_t ell.
Every monomial coefficient in both sums is nonnegative. Thus this is the PGF of independent Poisson clusters consisting of closed walks and open walks; the total cluster intensity is finite. More directly, differentiating t_i at all t=1 expresses the total mean E as a nonnegative sum of closed/open walk photon lengths.
Vacuum projection on H sets its ket and bra variables to zero, so the remaining normalized Gaussian factor sigma has exactly K_RR,ell_R. In its walk expansion only walks avoiding H remain. Therefore E_sigma<=E. Equivalently, vacuum conditioning deletes independent Poisson clusters touching H. This exact monotonicity covers mixedness and displacement and does not invoke matching approximation.

3. Polynomial excitation above a low-energy purification (exact proof)
Take any pure Gaussian purification of rho. Differentiation in the herald variables to total order h and evaluation at zero leaves a pure Bargmann vector P(z)G_0(z), deg(P)<=h. The Gaussian factor G_0 is a purification of the vacuum-conditioned sigma from step2. By Gaussian Schmidt decomposition, a Gaussian unitary on the environment changes G_0 to a canonical purification of sigma. Conjugating creators through a Gaussian unitary is affine linear in creators/annihilators; normal ordering on the new Gaussian factor preserves degree<=h. This unitary changes no retained output.
Canonical purification uses one two-mode squeezed partner for each Williamson thermal mode. Its environment energy is sum_j n_j, bounded by E_sigma, while its retained energy is E_sigma. Hence its total factor energy e<=2E_sigma<=2E. To see sum n_j<=E_sigma, use trace(V)>=2 sum_j nu_j, with nu_j=n_j+1/2, and nonnegative displacement energy (Williamson/trace variational principle).
Undo the Gaussian unitary U of this factor. Normal ordering P of the transformed affine ladder operators on vacuum yields a normalized vector chi supported on total photons <=h. Hence the conditional state's purification is U chi with e=vacuum-factor energy<=2E. Extra environment vacuum modes cause no cost in the following bounds.

4. Uniform quadratic-ladder estimates (exact proof)
Domain clarification: there are finitely many modes, but the Fock space and conditional output state are infinite-dimensional. The auxiliary chi alone has finite excitation support. A physical pure Gaussian factor has Bargmann singular values strictly below1; multiplying it by any finite polynomial gives a vector in every N^k domain. Thus the Gaussian conjugation and repeated quadratic-operator estimates are legitimate. On chi the conjugated N' powers have finite excitation support <=h+2k. Spectral-theorem/Tonelli interchange for the MGF uses nonnegative terms and the derived convergent moment majorant; it does not assume a finite-dimensional output state.
Let U^dagger a U=u a+v a^dagger+alpha, e_s=||v||_F^2, e_d=||alpha||_2^2, e=e_s+e_d. Canonical relations give ||u||_op<=sqrt(1+e_s), ||v||_op<=sqrt(e_s).
For any unit chi supported on photons<=r,
 ||(u a+v a^dagger+alpha)chi|| <= sqrt(r(1+e_s))+sqrt((r+1)e_s)+sqrt(e_d).
Thus its mean output photons are <=3r+3(2r+1)e_s+3e_d<=3r+3(2r+1)e. Putting r=h,e<=2E proves the mean bound.
For the higher moments put N'=U^dagger N U. Its normal-ordered quadratic terms are a^dagger H a + a^dagger Q a^dagger + a^T Q^* a + beta^T a^dagger + beta^dagger a +e,
with ||H||<=1+2e_s, ||Q||_F<=sqrt(e_s(1+e_s)), and ||beta||<=2sqrt((1+e_s)e_d). Pair annihilation has norm <=sqrt(r(r-1))||Q||_F; pair creation has norm <=sqrt((r+1)(r+2))||Q||_F. These dimension-free norms follow by treating all a_i a_j as a column operator, whose squared norm is N(N-1), and taking its adjoint. Linear terms have norm <=(sqrt(r)+sqrt(r+1))||beta||.
Using sqrt(r(r-1))+sqrt((r+1)(r+2))<=2(r+1), sqrt(e_s(1+e_s))<=e_s+1/2, and 4sqrt((r+1)(1+e_s)e_d)<=2(r+1)(1+e_s)+2e_d gives
 ||N' P_{<=r}|| <=4r+3+(6r+5)e_s+3e_d
                   <=4r+3+(6r+5)e <=6(r+1)(1+e).
N' raises total support by at most2. Therefore
 ||(N')^k chi|| <= [6(1+e)]^k product_{j=0}^{k-1}(h+2j+1)
                 =[12(1+e)]^k ((h+1)/2)_k.
Since N' is positive, its kth scalar moment is bounded by this norm. Summing its nonnegative exponential series by Tonelli gives
 Tr tau exp(tN) <=(1-12(1+e)t)^(-(h+1)/2), 0<=t<[12(1+e)]^(-1).
Set t=1/[24(1+e)] and use e<=2E to obtain the displayed tail. This is a proof for all finite modes, not an inference from finite tests.

5. Stronger state-level phase theorem (pure positive reference, displacement allowed)
Let the reference pure Gaussian Bargmann wavefunction be exp(1/2 z^T A z+g^T z), A,g>=0, ||A||<1. Perturb each nonzero entry to B_ij=A_ij exp(i theta_ij), f_i=g_i exp(i phi_i), with |theta_ij|,|phi_i|<=theta after a specified local mode gauge. B is physical because ||B||<=||A||; f is any finite displacement parameter. Condition both states on the same feasible h-photon herald. Let rho_A^H,rho_B^H denote their complete remaining pure states.
For each remaining pattern n the unnormalized perturbed/reference amplitude ratio r_n is a positive-weight average of phases. Every pair-or-single matching has at most h+|n| factors, so |r_n-1|<=theta(h+|n|). If z is the normalized reference coefficient vector and y_n=z_n r_n, then ||y-z||^2<=theta^2 E_A[(h+N)^2|H]. Distance from z to the complex line spanned by y is exactly pure-state trace distance. Thus whenever the RHS bound is <1, y is nonzero and
 (1/2)||rho_B^H-rho_A^H||_1 <=theta sqrt(E_A[(h+N)^2|H])
                           <=theta [h+6(h+1)(1+E)].
For the pure reference the vacuum factor has energy <=E (no doubling); the last bound is the k=2 operator estimate, since sqrt(E[N^2])=||N'chi||<=6(h+1)(1+E).
This controls all later channels and POVMs, by trace-distance contraction, and has no inverse herald probability. It strengthens N44's photon-count-only guarantee and allows displacement. Its theta dependence must be linear for a state metric: a one-mode squeezed state with A=r and B=r exp(i theta) has identical photon-count laws but trace distance theta sqrt(E(1+E)/2)+O(theta^2). The N44 quadratic count-TV scaling is compatible with this distinction.
For final photon counts, |r_n|<=1 and 1-|r_n|^2<=theta^2(h+|n|)^2. Writing the reference conditional law as a mixture of the normalized thinned law and the removed mass gives TV<=theta^2 E_A[(h+N)^2|H] without a reciprocal normalization penalty. For displaced inputs the explicit bound above squares; for undisplaced each matching has N/2 factors and recovers the factor1/4.
No claim is made for arbitrary amplitude changes or mixed-state phase perturbations without additional representation control.

6. Consequences and limits
The conditional optical state has an energy/tail promise depending only on E,h even if the herald is arbitrarily rare. Thus photon truncation in any positive-kernel simulation or certified calculation can use logarithmic delta dependence with no m term; OA113 counting costs still depend polynomially on reciprocal requested relative error. The physics theorem is independent of OA113; the general rare-herald polynomial sampler remains conditional on its imported FPRAS and on correctly proved finite-bit weighted/loop reductions.
The state-level phase result provides an experimental tolerance theta=O(eta/[(h+1)(1+E)]) for every subsequent measurement, rather than only same-basis photon counting. It does not give a fast sampler for arbitrary subsequent measurements, and it does not increase laboratory herald success probability.
