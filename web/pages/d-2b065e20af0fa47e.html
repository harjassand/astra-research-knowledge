## C1. Largest optimizers and reduction

For a finite quiver representation V over a field K, a subrepresentation is a tuple of subspaces closed under EVERY arrow. For any signed real vertex weight sigma, the score sigma·dim is modular on the intersection/sum lattice. If U and W maximize it, their intersection and sum also maximize it. Summing all maximizers stabilizes in finite dimension and produces a unique largest maximizer L_K. Its dimension vector is the coordinatewise largest dimension vector on the exposed face of the convex subrepresentation dimension polytope T_K. This statement concerns the convex polytope; equality of literal dimension-profile sets is unnecessary and generally false.

The sealed cycle16 theorem supplies T_Q=T_C and T_Q subset T_Fp at every prime for an integer arrow representation. Rational subrepresentations reduce through primitive lattices at EVERY vertex, preserving dimensions and arrow closure. At a prime that preserves the whole polytope, the exposed faces for every sigma agree. The coordinatewise largest face vector agrees too. The saturated reduction of L_Q is therefore an Fp maximizer with exactly dim L_Fp. Since it is contained in L_Fp, it equals L_Fp vertex by vertex. This proves, simultaneously for every signed real sigma,

    reduction_p(L_Q(sigma)) = L_Fp(sigma), on the whole-polytope event.

No corresponding assertion holds at an arbitrary prime. Rational arrows must first be cleared by a common nonzero scalar on each ENTIRE arrow, not separately on its columns. The theorem is applied to that integer representation; a prime dividing a clearing scalar is not silently excluded from the proof. The primitive reduction inclusion still holds, while the exceptional-prime proof catches any new convex dimension vector. Expanded dense active dimensions and isolated-vertex preprocessing remain the cycle16 bit-encoding boundary.

For integer sigma and A=sum_v dim V_v, sigma'=(A+1)sigma+1 exposes the unique largest original optimizer: any nonoptimal integral score loses at least A+1, exceeding the possible total-dimension gain A; among original maximizers total dimension is uniquely largest at L_K. Rational weights can be multiplied by a common denominator first. Numeric weight expansion must be charged.

## C2. Exact certification at any prime

Suppose a justified Fp support upper U is available and a rational candidate W has all original arrow closures verified exactly and sigma·dim W=U. Then

    U <= support_Q(sigma) <= support_Fp(sigma) <= U.

Thus the candidate certifies the original optimum at ANY prime. It does not certify that the prime preserves the whole polytope. If the supplied weight is sigma', it additionally certifies the largest original sigma optimizer. This is a certificate interface, not a method to acquire an unprovided support upper.

## C3. Rigidity and the modular pivot chart

For the largest sigma maximizer W, Hom(W,V/W)=0 over any field. Indeed, the nonzero image I of a morphism is a quotient of W, so sigma·dim I=sigma·dim W−sigma·dim ker >=0. Viewed as a nonzero subobject of V/W, its preimage strictly contains W; largest optimality forces sigma·dim I<0. This is a contradiction.

In an identity-pivot Grassmann chart Y_v, arrow closure is

    B_a Y_tail − Y_head (B_a Y_tail)_P_head = 0.

Its linearization is exactly the usual condition defining Hom(W,V/W). Consequently the Jacobian has full column rank at a largest optimizer. This proof includes loops and cycles. Full rank alone does not imply that a modular point lifts to Q.

At a whole-polytope good prime, choose primitive integer basis matrices B_v for L_Q. A modular pivot minor is a p-unit, so the corresponding identity-chart representative B_v(B_v)_P^-1 lies in Z_(p). The Q optimizer is a p-integral solution in the SAME modular chart, even when its ordinary Q-RREF uses a denominator divisible by p. Select n independent Jacobian rows modulo p. The square unit-Jacobian Hensel system has a unique residue-compatible p-adic solution, hence recovers that target as precision grows. Every omitted arrow equation must still be checked; the selected square system alone is insufficient at a bad prime.

## C6. New bounded support acquisition and its unconditional rank upper

The new bounded oracle supplies a genuine upstream upper on its admitted controls. For direct bipartite signed integer weights, form a matrix space B with every copy-to-copy block E_ji tensor B_a, source multiplicities sigma_v>0 and target multiplicities −sigma_v>0. For arbitrary quivers first double vertices: identity out_v→in_v, original arrows out_tail→in_head. Put

    T=sum sigma_v^+ dim V_v,
    M=T+max_v(−sigma_v,0)+1,
    weight(out_v)=sigma_v+M>0, weight(in_v)=−M.

A copied original subrepresentation has source size dim R, with B R contained in its copied target spaces. Its deficiency is at least its original sigma score. For ANY exported d-blowup member A in Mat_d tensor B, elementary dimension/rank gives

    rank A <= d Nplus − d(dim R−dim B R),
    support_original(sigma) <= Nplus − rank(A)/d.

This upper is unconditional on maximal nc-rank, Wong success, random completeness or a square-padding theorem. Membership in the specified matrix space and exact rank must be verified. A recovered original subrepresentation with exact arrow closure and score equal to that upper certifies optimum. The implemented success check does exactly this; an unmatched rank upper is reported UPPER_BOUND_ONLY.
