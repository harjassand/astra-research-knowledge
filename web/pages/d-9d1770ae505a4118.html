## 1. Wavelength-sized negative tests are impossible

Suppose a test v is supported in a rectangular box of width d in one Cartesian coordinate and |V|≤M throughout its support. The one-dimensional Dirichlet Poincare inequality on each parallel slice gives

    q_H(v) ≥ (π²/d²−M)||v||².

This remains true for irregular supports contained in that slab by extending by zero and integrating the slice inequality. For a ball of radius C/a centered sufficiently far out, local scale comparability gives M≤C_0 κ and d≤2C/a. Hence its form is strictly positive once C_0 κ/a² < π²/(4C²), which eventually holds because η→0. More generally a negative test contained in a local box must have every available confining width d>π/sqrt(M).

Thus a local nodal filling plus cutoff confined to one fixed multiple of the wavelength cannot produce negative spectrum for this actual construction. This is stronger than observing failure of one cutoff. Its support must extend beyond the κ^{-1/2} scale; the anticipated second-order attraction κ²/a²=a/r instead competes with a cutoff on the larger ℓ scale. No lower bound on the actual negative spectrum follows from this scale comparison.

## 2. An exact corrector identity that retains the full potential

Let g be any real C² function on a neighborhood of the compact support of a real H¹ cutoff χ. Integration by parts gives

    q_V(e^g χ)
      = ∫ e^{2g} { |∇χ|² + [V−Δg−|∇g|²]χ² }.       (1)

Indeed expand |∇(e^gχ)|². Integrating its cross term as ∫e^{2g}∇g·∇(χ²)=−∫e^{2g}(Δg+2|∇g|²)χ² gives (1), without boundary terms because χ has compact support. Smooth approximation allows piecewise smooth compact H¹ cutoffs. There is no division by the sign-changing state and no missing harmonic-collar contribution.

In particular, if one can acquire a Poisson corrector Δg=V−R, the exact effective potential in this trial is R−|∇g|². It is not enough to identify an attractive |∇g|² and discard R. A sufficient certified test is

    ∫e^{2g}(|∇g|²−R)χ² > ∫e^{2g}|∇χ|².             (2)

If (2) holds on infinitely many mutually disjoint escaping compact supports, min–max gives infinite negative index. Because V→0 and is bounded, the negative spectrum is discrete, so this means infinitely many negative eigenvalues tending to zero. A corrector need only be local to each test. Additive constants in g scale both sides equally.

For the present frequency ledger a high-frequency primitive would have expected sizes g=O(η), |∇g|=O(κ/a), and hence |∇g|²=O(a/r). These sizes have NOT been acquired for the actual repaired V: a low-frequency component of V can make a local Poisson primitive much larger. More importantly, a remainder at the smaller scale a/r can completely reverse the spectral verdict, as the next construction proves.

## 3. Matched potential-only fixtures with opposite spectral answers

Use any admissible a above on a sufficiently large tail and define

    Θ(r)=∫^r a(s) ds, b(r)=(r a(r))^{-1/2},
    g(r)=b(r) sin Θ(r).

Complete g smoothly to a radial function constant near the origin. Define globally smooth real potentials

    V_−=Δg,
    V_+=Δg+|∇g|².                                  (3)

Both potentials are bounded and tend to zero; every fixed Cartesian derivative tends to zero. They have the same leading oscillatory term and the same derivative ledger as the Landis construction:

    V_−=−κ sin Θ + O(κ/(ar)),
    V_+=−κ sin Θ + O(a/r),
    |D^m V_±|≤C_m κ a^m →0,
    V_+−V_−=(a/r) cos²Θ+O(r^{-2})=o(κ).             (4)

Nevertheless **H_+=−Δ+V_+ has no negative spectrum**, whereas **H_−=−Δ+V_− has infinitely many negative eigenvalues**.

### Complete coefficient calculation

From the a derivative assumptions, b^{(j)}=O_j(b/r^j). Since Θ'=a,

    g'=ba cos Θ+b' sin Θ,
    g''=(b''−ba²)sin Θ+(2b'a+ba')cos Θ.

The radial Laplacian adds (n−1)g'/r. Thus its leading coefficient is −ba²=−κ and the error is O(ba/r+b/r²)=O(κ/(ar)). Also

    (g')²=(ba)² cos²Θ+O(b²a/r+b²/r²)
           =(a/r)cos²Θ+O(r^{-2}).

Each extra derivative costs at most C_m a, using ar≥1 and the admissibility bounds. Cartesian radial derivatives add only inverse powers of r. The compensation-to-leading-amplitude ratio is (a/r)/κ=1/sqrt(ar)=η→0. Subpower growth then proves the all-derivative decay exactly as in the existing construction. These statements concern both a=log r and the arbitrarily slow admissible envelopes, including long flat intervals.

### Nonnegative member

The positive function e^g solves (−Δ+V_+)e^g=0. Equation (1), now with V_+, reads

    q_+(e^gχ)=∫e^{2g}|∇χ|²≥0.

Multiplication by the globally smooth positive e^g is invertible on compact supports, so every compact test has nonnegative form. The Friedrichs operator H_+ is nonnegative. No L² ground state is asserted.

### Infinite-index member, first proof by disjoint annuli

For V_−, equation (1) becomes

    q_−(e^gχ)=∫e^{2g}(|∇χ|²−|∇g|²χ²).             (5)

Let χ_s(r) be a fixed radial bump scaled to (s,2s), equal to one on [4s/3,5s/3]. Its cutoff energy is at most C s^{n−2}, since g→0. On that inner interval,

    (g')² ≥ (a/(2r))cos²Θ−C/(a r³).

The oscillatory integral is small: integrate exp(2iΘ) by parts using Θ'=a and a'≥0 to obtain

    |∫_I exp(2iΘ(r))dr| ≤ C/a(s)

for every interval I⊂[s,2s]. Explicitly the two boundary terms have size ≤1/a(s), and the integral of a'/(2a²) is at most 1/(2a(s)). Therefore ∫_I cos²Θ≥|I|/2−C/a(s). On I=[4s/3,5s/3] this is at least c s for all sufficiently large s. Since a is nondecreasing,

    ∫e^{2g}|∇g|²χ_s² dx
      ≥ c a(s)s^{n−1}−C s^{n−3}/a(s).

It dominates C s^{n−2}, as s a(s)→∞. Hence (5) is negative. Taking s_j=3^j after the tail threshold gives mutually disjoint compact supports and proves infinite index. The core completion has no effect on these tests.

### Sharper location of the sign change: the macro scale

Fix B>0, let L=B sqrt(s/a(s)), and use the H¹ cutoff

    χ_s(r)=sin(π(r−s)/L), s<r<s+L, and zero outside.

As s→∞, L/s→0, a(s+L)/a(s)→1, g→0 uniformly on this shell, and a(s)L=B sqrt(sa(s))→∞. With t=(r−s)/L, elementary nonstationary-phase integration gives

    ∫_0^1 cos²Θ(s+Lt) sin²(πt)dt → 1/4.

For completeness, write cos²=(1+cos(2Θ))/2, integrate the oscillatory integral by parts with phase derivative 2La(s+Lt), and use |a'|≤Ca/s and bounded first derivative of sin². The remainder is O(1/(La(s))+L/s), which vanishes. The g' error above contributes o(a(s)/s). Consequently

    q_−(e^gχ_s)/||e^gχ_s||²
      = [π²/B²−1/2+o(1)] a(s)/s,                 (6)

    q_+(e^gχ_s)/||e^gχ_s||²
      = [π²/B²+o(1)] a(s)/s.                     (7)

For every fixed B>π sqrt(2), (6) is eventually strictly negative. Shells of this thickness can be selected disjointly and escaping.
