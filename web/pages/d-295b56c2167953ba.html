EXACT THEOREM

Let E be finite and f:2^E -> [0,infinity). Suppose f vanishes on one parity
class, h(x)=sum_U f(U)x^U is multiaffine, nonzero, and Hurwitz stable (h(z) is
nonzero whenever Re z_i>0 for every i). Then for every S,T of the supported
parity and every a in D=S symmetric-difference T,

  sqrt(f(S) f(T)) <= sum_{j in D, j != a}
       sqrt(f(S symmetric-difference {a,j})
            f(T symmetric-difference {a,j})).                 (1)

If f(S)f(T)=0 the claim is immediate. If S=T there is no a in D. Otherwise
|D| is positive and even. Coefficients may vanish; h may have boundary zeros.
No homogeneity or genericity is assumed. The constant one is sharp.

PROOF INCLUDING STABILITY CLOSURES AND INDICES

Assume f(S),f(T)>0. Twist by T: define

  h^T(x) = x^T h(y),  y_i=1/x_i for i in T and y_i=x_i otherwise.

Its coefficient of x^U is f(T symmetric-difference U). If Re z>0 then
Re(1/z)=Re(z)/|z|^2>0, and the prefactor x^T never vanishes in the domain;
therefore h^T is Hurwitz stable. Since h has constant parity, h^T is even.
Set every coordinate outside D to zero. By the Hurwitz boundary-specialization
theorem this is stable or zero; its constant coefficient is f(T)>0, so it is
not zero. This specialization fixes any common core by leaving its coefficient
values unchanged.

Fix a in D, choose arbitrary w_j>0 for j in D\{a}, set x_a=1 and
x_j=w_j z, and call the result p(z). If Re z>0, all inputs to the specialized
polynomial have positive real part, so p has no zero there. Its degree is
exactly d=|D|-1, which is odd, because its leading coefficient is positive.
With W=product_{j != a} w_j, the endpoint and adjacent coefficients of p are

  a_0     = f(T),
  a_d     = f(S) W,
  a_1     = sum_{j != a} f(T symmetric-difference {a,j}) w_j,
  a_(d-1) = W sum_{j != a} f(S symmetric-difference {a,j})/w_j.   (2)

For degree zero, parity permits only the empty monomial. For degree d, only D
can appear. At degree one, the sets must be {a,j}. At degree d-1 they must be
D\{a,j}; twisting back gives exactly the last coefficient in (2). When d=1
the endpoint and neighbor coefficients overlap, and (2) still holds.

Odd-degree endpoint lemma: if p(z)=sum_{k=0}^d a_k z^k is real, d odd,
a_0,a_d>0, and has no zero in Re z>0, then a_0 a_d <= a_1 a_(d-1).
Indeed all roots rho_l satisfy Re rho_l<=0 and rho_l!=0. Put r_l=-1/rho_l;
then Re r_l>=0 and p(z)=a_0 product_l(1+r_l z). Thus

a_1/a_0=sum_l r_l,        a_(d-1)/a_d=sum_l 1/r_l.

Nonreal r_l occur in conjugate pairs and each pair contributes nonnegative
real amounts to both sums. Since d is odd, there is at least one real r_l;
each real r_l is strictly positive. If R is the nonempty set of real roots
r_l, the product of sums is at least

  (sum_{r in R} r)(sum_{r in R} 1/r) >= |R|^2 >= 1,

by Cauchy-Schwarz. This proves the lemma, including imaginary-axis roots and
multiplicity. For d=1 it is equality. Odd degree is essential: 1+z^2 is
zero-free in Re z>0 but violates the endpoint inequality.

Apply the lemma to (2) and cancel W>0. For every positive weight vector,

 f(S)f(T) <= (sum_j A_j w_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j),
 A_j=f(T symmetric-difference {a,j}),
 B_j=f(S symmetric-difference {a,j}).

For nonnegative A_j,B_j,

 inf_{w_j>0}(sum A_j w_j)(sum B_j/w_j)=(sum sqrt(A_j B_j))^2.   (3)

The lower bound is Cauchy-Schwarz. On coordinates with A_j,B_j>0 choose
w_j=sqrt(B_j/A_j); when just A_j>0 send w_j to zero, and when just B_j>0
send w_j to infinity. Coordinates with both zero are irrelevant. These limits
prove (3) even if no finite minimizer exists. Taking the infimum and square
root proves (1), without dividing by any possibly zero coefficient.

HARD-BCS INTERFACE

For n=|E| even, let f(U)=Z_(E\U)(F), where

  Z_R(F)=sum_{I subset R, |I|=|R|/2} |det F[I,R\I]|^2

for even R and is zero for odd R. The supplied signed ambient-hole polynomial is

  Q_F(x)=sum_{U even} (-1)^((n-|U|)/2) f(U) x^U.

If Q_F is real stable in the upper half-plane, then
h(x)=(-1)^(n/2) Q_F(i x)=sum_{U even} f(U)x^U is Hurwitz stable: Re x_i>0
implies Im(i x_i)>0. The sign identity follows by writing |U|=2m; the total
phase is (-1)^(n/2+(n-2m)/2)(-1)^m=1 for even n. Thus (1) applies to all
even hole sets, including a shared occupied core. This is only an aggregate
coefficient statement.

A normalization/index audit was repaired before this freeze. I first used the
wrong principal-minor complement in the determinant expansion, producing a

identity; independently recomputing that index gives the expected x0x1

2^(-n) 2^|U| cancels 4^k. This false alarm was an index-bookkeeping error,

PRIOR ART AND SCOPE

* Petter Branden, “Polynomials with the half-plane property and matroid
theory,” arXiv:math/0605678, Theorem 3.2 and Corollaries 3.3–3.4:
support of an HPP polynomial is a jump system; for multiaffine polynomials it
is a delta-matroid, and homogeneous support is a matroid basis family.
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0605678
* Petter Branden, “Discrete concavity and the half-plane property,”
arXiv:0904.0363v3, Theorem 4: valuations of constant-parity HPP polynomials
over Puiseux series are M-concave. This controls valuation/support exchange,
not the finite coefficient magnitudes in (1).
https://arxiv.org/html/0904.0363v3
* Nima Anari, Kuikui Liu, Shayan Oveis Gharan, Cynthia Vinzant,
Thuy-Duong Vuong, “Log-Concave Polynomials IV,” arXiv:2004.07220v2,
Lemmas 26 and 28: (1)'s homogeneous fixed-cardinality real-stable case and the
odd-degree endpoint inequality are explicit prior art. Their exchange sum is
restricted to the fixed-size exchange neighborhood. The reciprocal twist above
extends the proof to nonhomogeneous one-parity coefficients, but the cycle-5
baseline already contains this extension. https://arxiv.org/html/2004.07220v2
* Tracy Chin, “Oriented and Valuated Delta Matroids from Stable Polynomials,”
arXiv:2609.13501v1, Theorems 3.1 and 4.1: coefficient signs orient a delta-
matroid and Puiseux valuations give valuated delta-matroids. This is not a
finite real-magnitude inequality. https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13501v1
* Borcea–Branden, arXiv:0809.0401v3, stability-preserving operator symbol
criterion and Hurwitz closure provide imported justification for auxiliary
stability-preserving operators; this does not yield an acquired sampler.
https://arxiv.org/html/0809.0401v3
* Hamada–Iwata, arXiv:2610.03030v1, Theorem 1.1, gives deterministic
unweighted linear matroid parity optimization under a field-operation model.
As detailed in the cycle-5 support audit, finite-field reductions can recover
support/witnesses for rational-complex inputs with polynomial bit overhead.
This is support optimization, not a count or positive coefficient oracle.
https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03030v1

ALGORITHMIC BOUNDARY / PRECISE COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A NATURAL ROUTE

The exchange immediately gives simultaneous support exchange: if f(S)f(T)>0,
then at least one j has both exchanged coefficients positive. With m=|D|-1,
some j also has f(S')f(T') >= f(S)f(T)/m^2. This is only a weak approximate
exchange for log f. It does not select an individual minor pair or provide
conditional transition probabilities.

For the directed cyclic permutation matrix F of size n=2q, the natural
fixed-k polynomial at k=q has exactly two supported q-sets (the alternating
ones), so p_q(z)=product_even(z_i)+product_odd(z_i). The exact support was
checked for q=2,3,4,5 in audit_fixtures.py. Its log-coordinate Hessian for
log p_q(exp(alpha y)) at y=0 is

  (alpha^2/4) vv^T - (alpha/2) I,

where v has q entries +1 and q entries -1. The eigenvalue in direction v is
alpha(alpha q-1)/2, hence fractional log-concavity requires alpha<=1/q.
Also p_q vanishes when z_even=e^(i pi/(2q)) and z_odd=e^(-i pi/(2q));
therefore any sector-stability aperture is at most pi/q. The two supported
states differ in q elements, so fixed-size down-up chains changing fewer than
q elements are disconnected. This refutes a uniform-constant fractional-
log-concavity/sector-stability route for this natural polynomial; it is not a
lower bound against every algorithm or every auxiliary representation.

The hard-BCS f(U) are sums of squared complementary minors. The proof's
balancing weights w_j=sqrt(B_j/A_j) depend on those same unknown sums. The
inequality proves existence of a favorable aggregate exchange but neither
computes these weights nor supplies a coupling of determinant labels across
neighboring hole sets. Computing f by its defining sum may require exponentially
many minors; a finite-algebraic-stability oracle or determinant-amplitude query
is not an oracle for that sum. No FPRAS or microscopic two-replica energy bound
follows. A useful next theorem would have to disaggregate (1) into a sampled
kernel with acquired probabilities and prove an integrated Dirichlet-energy
bound without assuming a global positive matching/windability lift.

DEPENDENCIES, COSTS, AND STATUS

The proof depends only on polynomial factorization, real-coefficient conjugate
root pairing, Cauchy-Schwarz, and the primary-source Hurwitz boundary-special-
ization theorem. It avoids Asner's stronger total-nonnegativity theorem. The
proof's symbolic work is linear in |D| after the coefficients are supplied;
acquiring the coefficients from the defining determinant sums is the unresolved
cost, not charged as polynomial. The unweighted optimization/witness routine is
polynomial under the imported finite-field results, with polynomial bit overhead
for rational-complex entries, but contributes no count or sample.
