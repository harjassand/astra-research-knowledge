# Proof and algorithm

## 1. Characteristics and inventory

Consider
\[
q_t+cq_x=0,\quad 0<x<L,\quad q(0,t)=u(t),\quad q(x,0)=q_0(x),
\]
with a fixed speed \(c>0\), measurable \(0\le q_0\le Q\), and a nonnegative, piecewise-constant boundary input. Let \(\tau_c=L/c\).

Following characteristics, a point \((x,t)\) traces to the initial line when \(x-ct\ge0\), and otherwise to the inlet:
\[
q(x,t)=
\begin{cases}
q_0(x-ct),&x\ge ct,\\
u(t-x/c),&x<ct.
\end{cases}
\]
Integrating over \(x\in[0,L]\) and changing variables gives
\[
M_c(t)=\int_0^Lq(x,t)dx=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\int_0^{L-ct}q_0(x)dx+c\int_0^tu(r)dr,&t\le\tau_c,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle c\int_{t-\tau_c}^tu(r)dr,&t\ge\tau_c.
\end{cases}\tag{1}
\]
The two formulas agree at \(t=\tau_c\).

At sample \(t_k=kh\), let \(u=a\) on \([t_k,t_k+h]\), and let \(s\in[0,h]\). If \(t_k+s\le\tau_c\), equation (1) is \(A_k(c,s)+B(c,s)a\), with
\[
A_k(c,s)=\int_0^{L-c(t_k+s)}q_0(x)dx+c\int_0^{t_k}u(r)dr,\qquad B(c,s)=cs.
\]
If \(t_k+s\ge\tau_c\), split the delay window at \(t_k\):
\[
A_k(c,s)=
\begin{cases}
c\displaystyle\int_{t_k+s-\tau_c}^{t_k}u(r)dr,&t_k+s-\tau_c<t_k,\\
0,&t_k+s-\tau_c\ge t_k,
\end{cases}
\quad
B(c,s)=c\min(s,\tau_c)=\min(cs,L).
\]
Thus \(M_c(t_k+s;a)=A_k(c,s)+B(c,s)a\) in both regions.

## 2. Initial profile known through cell integrals

Partition \([0,L]\) into \(N\) cells of width \(d=L/N\). In cell \(I_i\), acquire \(m_i=\int_{I_i}q_0(x)dx\) and know \(0\le q_0\le Q\). For a prefix ending \(r\) into a cut cell whose measured mass is \(m\), the possible partial mass is exactly
\[
\max(0,m-Q(d-r))\ \le\ \int_{\text{cell prefix}}q_0(x)dx\ \le\ \min(m,Qr).\tag{2}
\]
The upper bound is attainable: place mass \(Q\) first in the prefix, then place any remainder in the rest of the cell. The lower bound is attainable by filling the suffix first. The width of this interval is at most \(Q\min(r,d-r)\le Qd/2\), with equality possible at a half-cell cut. Summing full-cell masses and the upper endpoint from (2) defines \(I^+(b)\), an exact upper envelope for \(\int_0^bq_0\) over all profiles consistent with the acquired data.

If the cell sensor instead reports a certified total-mass interval \([m_-,m_+]\), first intersect it with the physically feasible range \([0,Qd]\) and reject inconsistent data. Write the feasible interval as \([\ell,u]\). The exact possible partial-prefix range is then
\[
\left[\max(0,\ell-Q(d-r)),\;\min(u,Qr)\right].
\]
The endpoints follow by minimizing/maximizing the total mass over the interval and filling suffix-first/prefix-first; both are attainable. Use the upper endpoint for the robust safety filter. The \(Qd/2\) ambiguity bound applies to exact-total-mass cells; with interval-valued measurements, measurement uncertainty adds ambiguity and must be charged separately unless built directly into \(I^+\).

Replace the initial prefix in \(A_k\) by \(I^+\) and call the result \(A_k^+\). At a fixed \((c,s)\), this upper envelope is attainable by some admissible initial profile. Therefore the robust one-step threshold below is the largest command safe for every initial profile in the cell-data class, not just a sufficient threshold for one selected profile.

## 3. Robust one-step theorem

Assume:

1. \(c\) is constant in time and belongs to the known compact interval \(C=[c_-,c_+]\subset(0,\infty)\).
2. \(q_0\) is nonnegative, bounded by \(Q\), and its cell integrals are the acquired values \(m_i\).
3. Every applied command is in \([0,U_{\max}]\) and held for \(h\).
4. At \(t_k\), the actual inventory is at most \(M_{\max}\) for every \(c\in C\) and every profile consistent with the data.

For a proposed command \(a\), robust safety over the next sample interval is equivalent to
\[
A_k^+(c,s)+B(c,s)a\le M_{\max}
\quad\text{for every }c\in C,\ s\in[0,h].\tag{3}
\]
At \(s=0\), (3) is the already-verified current-state condition. For \(s>0\), \(B(c,s)>0\), so the largest certified command is
\[
a_k^*=\min\left\{U_{\max},\inf_{\substack{c\in C\\0<s\le h}}
\frac{M_{\max}-A_k^+(c,s)}{B(c,s)}\right\}.\tag{4}
\]
If the right side is negative, no nonnegative command is certified by this step. Applying \(a=0\) remains safe: with zero inlet value, the inventory derivative is \(M'_c(t)=-c q(L,t)\le0\). For a nominal \(v_k\in[0,U_{\max}]\), the projection \(a_k=\min(v_k,a_k^*)\), when \(a_k^*\ge0\), is the closest safe command in squared distance and is also the largest safe command not exceeding the nominal one.

**Proof.** Equation (1) and the split at \(t_k\) give the affine representation. For every compatible \(q_0\), the actual partial initial mass is at most \(I^+\), so (3) implies \(M_c(t_k+s)\le M_{\max}\). Conversely, if (3) fails at a pair \((c,s)\), the partial-cell construction attaining \(I^+\) at the corresponding cut yields an admissible profile and a speed for which that command violates the cap. This proves one-step necessity and sufficiency over the stated uncertainty set. Starting from a certified safe initial profile and applying (3) at each sample proves safety for all \(t\ge0\), including intersample times. No PDE discretization is used in this argument. \(\square\)

## 4. Certified finite-grid implementation

For any fixed admissible \(q_0,u\), \(M_c(t)\) is continuous and piecewise Lipschitz in \((c,t)\). Let \(\tau_{\max}=L/c_-\). On the initial-profile branch,
\[
|\partial_c M_c(t)|\le t(Q+U_{\max})\le\tau_{\max}(Q+U_{\max}).
\]
On the boundary-history branch, differentiating \(c\int_{t-L/c}^tu(r)dr\) at points of differentiability gives
\[
\partial_cM_c(t)=\int_{t-L/c}^tu(r)dr-\frac{L}{c}u(t-L/c),
\]
whose magnitude is at most \(2LU_{\max}/c_-\). At branch transitions the formulas agree, so a global speed Lipschitz constant is
\[
K_c=\max\{\tau_{\max}(Q+U_{\max}),\,2LU_{\max}/c_-\}.
\]
Likewise, on the initial branch \(M'_c(t)=c[u(t)-q_0(L-ct)]\), and on the history branch \(M'_c(t)=c[u(t)-u(t-\tau_c)]\). Hence a valid elapsed-time constant is \(K_s=c_+(Q+U_{\max})\).

Take endpoint-inclusive grids in \(C\) and \([0,h]\), with maximum spacings \(d_c,d_s\). Every point is within \(d_c/2,d_s/2\) of a grid point. For one fixed admissible profile and candidate command,
\[
M_c(t_k+s;a)\le M_{c_i}(t_k+s_j;a)
+\tfrac12(K_cd_c+K_sd_s).
\]
At each grid point, evaluate the exact upper envelope \(A_k^++B a\). Therefore the test
\[
\max_{i,j}\big(A_k^+(c_i,s_j)+B(c_i,s_j)a\big)
+\tfrac12(K_cd_c+K_sd_s)+e_{\rm num}+e_{\rm meas}\le M_{\max}\tag{5}
\]
certifies (3). Here \(e_{\rm num}\) is arithmetic error and \(e_{\rm meas}\) is the mass-unit allowance for uncertain cell readings. The Lipschitz argument compares the same unknown profile at neighboring points; it does not treat the cell upper envelope itself as the physical profile.

At fixed grids, every term is affine and nondecreasing in \(a\). The largest command certified by (5) is found in one pass by taking the minimum of the grid thresholds \((M_{\max}-e-A_k^+)/B\) over points with \(s_j>0\), with \(e=\tfrac12(K_cd_c+K_sd_s)+e_{\rm num}+e_{\rm meas}\). If the current-state/grid check is too conservative and no positive command passes, return zero; zero inlet is safe by monotonicity of inventory. This finite grid is a conservative approximation to (4), not an exact optimizer of the continuous supremum.

For cell and held-input prefix arrays, a point evaluation uses \(O(\log(N+H))\) operations, where \(H=\lceil\tau_{\max}/h\rceil+1\) commands are retained. A grid with \(n_c n_s\) points therefore costs \(O(n_cn_s\log(N+H))\) arithmetic per sample and \(O(N+H)\) memory. Finer grids lower the explicit mass margin in (5); their cost grows as \(n_cn_s\). Finite-precision interval arithmetic must round upper envelopes upward and thresholds downward.

## 5. Hand calculation

Set \(L=1\), \(q_0(x)=0.4\), \(C=[0.8,1.2]\), \(h=0.8\), and \(M_{\max}=0.6\). All residence times exceed \(h\). Then
\[
M_c(s;a)=0.4(1-cs)+cas=0.4+cs(a-0.4).
\]
For \(a\le0.4\), inventory never rises above \(0.4\) during this interval. For \(a>0.4\), it is maximized at \(c=1.2,s=0.8\); thus
\[
0.016+0.96a\le0.6
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\le0.608333\ldots.
\]
The result is attained by the fastest admitted speed at the end of the hold.

## 6. Sharp intersample statement for pointwise output

After the initial profile leaves the domain, the outflow is a pure delay \(y(t)=u(t-\tau_c)\). A pulse with height \(Y+\epsilon\) and width \(w<\delta\), placed strictly between output samples spaced by \(\delta\), yields \(y(j\delta)=0\) at every sampled instant but violates \(y\le Y\) during the pulse. Therefore pointwise sample checks alone imply no continuous-time safety guarantee over unrestricted inputs.

If the actuator guarantees \(|\dot u|\le R\), then \(y\) is \(R\)-Lipschitz. Every point between adjacent output samples lies within \(\delta/2\) of one of them. Thus sample values at most \(Y-R\delta/2-e_y\) imply \(y(t)\le Y\) continuously when measurement error is at most \(e_y\). A triangular tent with zero endpoints and slopes of magnitude \(R\) reaches \(R\delta/2\), so the margin is sharp under only that regularity information.
