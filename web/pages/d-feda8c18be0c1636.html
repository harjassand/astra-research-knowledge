## Exact next theorem

For each integer `d >= 3`, does there exist `C_d` such that every viscosity infinity-harmonic `u` on `B_1 subset R^d` satisfies

`||Du||_{L-infinity(B_{1/2})} + [Du]_{C^{0,1/3}(B_{1/2})} <= C_d osc_{B_1} u`?

The exponent is optimal: Aronsson's example `u=(|x_1|^{4/3}-|x_2|^{4/3})/2`, extended independently of the other coordinates, has `|Du(te_1)-Du(0)|=(2/3)|t|^{1/3}`. Any exponent above `1/3` fails.

The exceptional significance of the neighboring theorem is clear: before the Oct. 2026 release, Evans--Smart had proved pointwise differentiability in all dimensions, but continuity of `Du` was open for `d>=3`; Evans--Savin had `C^{1,alpha}` in the plane. Family 377 claims uniform `C^{1,alpha_d}` for `d>=3`, with `alpha_d` positive but non-explicit. It explicitly makes no endpoint claim. The remaining endpoint is therefore a strict strengthening of that source claim, and is supported by the 4/3 Aronsson obstruction.

## Source mechanism reconstructed from the proof, not just the card

The release's route has two layers.

1. A three-point viscosity maximum argument upgrades near-affine convergence to a midpoint-defect estimate uniform over all endpoint separations. This establishes only qualitative uniform flatness for normalized lifted solutions `U(y,b)=u(y)+b`.
2. A bad-cylinder sequence is anisotropically rescaled: axial length `r`, transverse length `sqrt(lambda) r`, vertical error `lambda r`. The limit solves
   `F[v] = D^2v[(grad_x v,1)] = 0`.
   To rule out a non-affine limit, the proof takes an extremal horizontal Jensen defect, builds the variance-penalized barycentric envelope
   `C_t v(x,t) = sup_{mean y_i=x} { sum theta_i v(y_i,t) - Var(y)/(2t) }`,
   and subtracts `kappa^2 t/2`. The envelope is a subsolution by a common-time theorem-of-sums argument: along the viscosity direction, the weighted variance penalty is affine in time. The resulting positive-gap contact is propagated to time zero using a discrete forward/backward envelope walk whose drift controls squared optimizer-velocity discrepancies. The final envelope-slope rigidity yields a contradiction.

The actual lift lemma, barycentric subsolution calculation, and contact walk were inspected in source. I found no immediate algebraic sign error in those pieces. The critical *quantitative* issue for the endpoint is different: the final bad-scale selection chooses exponents `gamma_i -> 0` and uses a fixed enlargement factor `Lambda=32`; it proves `b(s) <= B s^gamma` for some non-explicit `gamma>0`, but gives no control at the linear threshold `gamma=1`.

## The exact missing gate in the paper's mechanism

Family 377 defines a cylinder fit at `(z,r)` with parameter `lambda` when some slope `P` gives

`|U(z+h)-U(z)-P.h| <= lambda r` on `Z_lambda(z,r;P)`,

where `Z_lambda` has axial radius `r` and transverse radius `sqrt(lambda) r`. Its modulus is `b(s)`, the supremum of bad parameters over normalized lifts and radii `r <= s dist(z, boundary)`.

A sufficient sharp gate for the desired `C^{1,1/3}` conclusion is

`b(s) <= B s`.

Why the exponent matches exactly: choose `lambda = A r` at physical radius `r`. The cylinder contains a ball of radius `rho = sqrt(A) r^{3/2}` and the affine error is `A r^2`. At dyadic scales `r_j=2^{-j}r_0`, fitted slopes on common balls differ by `O(r_j^{1/2})`, so they converge to a gradient with tail `O(r_j^{1/2})`. For `rho_{j+1}<|h|<=rho_j`, the first-order remainder is `O(r_j^2)=O(|h|^{4/3})`; comparing Taylor estimates at adjacent centers gives `|Du(z)-Du(z')|<=C|z-z'|^{1/3}`. This repeats only the local extraction step, not the unproved global gate.

The gate is sharp on the Aronsson example: it has `b(s) >= c s` at the origin and admits `lambda <= C r` cylinder fits at every center. Thus a proof by improving to `b(s)=o(s)` is impossible.

## Actual scaled test on Aronsson's solution

Let `a=1/2`, `f(s)=a|s|^{4/3}`, `u(x)=f(x_1)-f(x_2)`, and `U(x,b)=u(x)+b`. At the origin the correct cylinder axis is the lifted gradient `P=e_b`.

For every axis direction and every `0<lambda<=1`, the cylinder contains both `+h e_1` and `-h e_1` for `h=sqrt(lambda) r/2`. The two affine residuals sum to `2a h^{4/3}`; if each is at most `lambda r`, then

`lambda r >= a h^{4/3}`, hence `lambda >= a^3 r/16 = r/128`.

Conversely, at `P=e_b` and `lambda=r`, any cylinder point has horizontal radius at most `r^{3/2}`, so

`|u(x)| <= 2a (r^{3/2})^{4/3}=r^2=lambda r`.

So the origin exactly saturates the linear parameter law up to constants; the largest allowed improvement one can hope for is `O(r)`.

There is also an all-center estimate for this exact example. If `s=x_i`, `g_i=f_i'(s)`, and `delta_i` is a coordinate displacement, the one-dimensional cusp has the scale-invariant remainder bound

`|f_i(s+delta_i)-f_i(s)-f_i'(s)delta_i| <= C delta_i^2/(|s|+|delta_i|)^{2/3}`.

For a lift-cylinder with `lambda=A r`, every horizontal coordinate increment satisfies `|delta_i| <= C r |s|^{1/3} + sqrt(A) r^{3/2}`. Splitting at `|s|=sqrt(A) r^{3/2}` bounds the remainder by `C(1+A^{2/3})r^2`; choosing A sufficiently large gives a fit. This confirms the source's linear-cylinder gate is compatible with the sharp model, rather than being disproved by the singular axes.

## A useful scaling reduction for falsification

At a critical point, a profile with remainder order `|x|^{1+alpha}` would require cylinder parameter approximately

`lambda ~ r^{2 alpha/(1-alpha)}`

because `(sqrt(lambda) r)^{1+alpha} ~ lambda r`.

- `alpha=1/3` gives `lambda~r`.
- Any `alpha<1/3` gives `lambda/r -> infinity` as `r->0`.

Thus a counterexample to the endpoint should show up as a non-affine anisotropic blow-up with subcritical `4/3` spatial growth. Conversely, a route to the endpoint could seek a Liouville theorem for the limit equation `F[v]=D^2v[(grad_x v,1)]=0` ruling out all admissible non-affine limits with subcritical `4/3` growth.

I derived the spherical equation for a classical homogeneous ansatz `u(r,omega)=r^q phi(omega)`:

`<D_S^2 phi grad_S phi, grad_S phi> + q(2q-1) phi |grad_S phi|^2 + q^3(q-1) phi^3 = 0`.

At a smooth angular critical point with `phi != 0`, this forces `q=1`; therefore any non-affine homogeneous profile of degree `q>1` must have angular singular structure at its extrema. Balancing a power cusp `M-c|theta|^beta` against the nonzero radial term gives `3 beta - 4 = 0`, hence `beta=4/3`, the Aronsson exponent. This is a formal local balance, not a proof that all viscosity profiles have that cusp.

## Falsification status and next proof task

- The standard separable Aronsson family supports, but does not prove, `b(s)=Theta(s)`.
- I found no higher-dimensional primary-source example with a gradient exponent below `1/3`; the 2018 explicit separated-solution paper does not supply one. This negative search is not a literature certification.
- The strongest concrete next lemma is: **every global solution of the anisotropic limit equation produced by a sequence with `lambda_i/r_i -> infinity` must be affine**. This is more precise than the generic quadratic-fit Liouville, because the latter admits the time-independent Aronsson profile and is too weak. The extra inherited constraint from bounded normalized original solutions must be stated and used explicitly.
- Current mathematical status: the endpoint gate is not proved or falsified. The proof machinery from family 377 supplies qualitative flatness and some power decay, not the linear bound.

## Conditional extraction proved independently

Here is the complete local argument behind the sufficient gate. Suppose a Lipschitz function `U` on a neighborhood of `B_{1/2}` has, at every `z` and every small `r`, a cylinder fit with parameter `lambda=A r` and error `lambda r=A r^2`, with one fixed `A`.

The cylinder contains `B_{rho}(z)`, `rho=sqrt(A) r^{3/2}`. Set `r_j=2^{-j}r_0`, choose any fitted slope `P_j(z)` at each scale, and `rho_j=sqrt(A)r_j^{3/2}`. On `B_{rho_{j+1}}(z)` both affine fits apply, so

`|P_j(z)-P_{j+1}(z)| <= A(r_j^2+r_{j+1}^2)/rho_{j+1} <= C_A r_j^{1/2}`.

The geometric tail converges uniformly, defining `P_infinity(z)` with `|P_j-P_infinity|<=C_A r_j^{1/2}`. At every `|h|<=rho_j`,

`|U(z+h)-U(z)-P_infinity(z).h| <= A r_j^2 + |P_j-P_infinity|rho_j <= C_A r_j^2`.

For `rho_{j+1}<|h|<=rho_j`, one has `r_j^2<=C_A |h|^{4/3}`. Therefore `U` has a uniform `O(|h|^{4/3})` first-order remainder. This implies differentiability with gradient `P_infinity`; comparing the remainders at two centers (and one common evaluation point) gives `[DU]_{C^{0,1/3}}<=C_A`. On the lift `U(y,b)=u(y)+b`, restriction to `b=0` is the desired endpoint estimate for `u`.

This closes the extraction direction. It does not prove the key universal linear cylinder-fit bound.

## Quantitative obstruction in the current proof architecture

The endpoint failure can be fed into the source bad-scale selection with exponent `1`, but that selection no longer gives a uniform enlargement constant. If `s_i` is a running near-maximum for `b(s)/s` and `lambda_i` is comparable to `b(s_i)`, then for every fixed scale factor `M`,

`b(2 M s_i) <= 2 b(s_i) (2M)`,

so the enlargement needed to make nearby fits good is `Lambda(M)=O(M)`. A fit at normalized radius `R` then has error `Lambda(R)R=O(R^2)`. The published Liouville theorem needs one constant times `R` at every radius. This pinpoints why the existing proof does not yield the endpoint.

I tested a natural extension of the novel barycentric-envelope mechanism rather than assuming it transfers. Suppose a horizontal defect obeys `D<=kappa r^q`, `1<q<2`. The same envelope penalty optimizes as

`sup_r {kappa r^q-r^2/(2t)} = (1-q/2) q^{q/(2-q)} kappa^{2/(2-q)} t^{q/(2-q)}`.

For `q=4/3`, the correction is `g(t)=(16/27)kappa^3 t^2`. Although `C_t v` remains a subsolution, `W=C_t v-g(t)` satisfies only `F[W]>=-g''=-32 kappa^3/27`, rather than `F[W]>=0`. In the source's step-`epsilon` walk, the forward midpoint gap then has lower bound `D>=-g'' epsilon^2`; after the identity divides by `4 epsilon`, this allows a drift loss of `g'' epsilon/4` per step. The simple symmetric time walk has order `epsilon^{-2}` steps on a fixed interval, so this direct lower-expectation argument loses order `epsilon^{-1}` and cannot preserve the contact estimate. This is an obstacle to reusing the current walk, not a refutation of the endpoint; a compensated martingale or a new forcing-sensitive contact potential could change it.

The same calculation explains the critical scaling. If a critical-point profile has size `|x|^q`, balance of the transverse-cylinder error gives `lambda ~ r^{2(q-1)/(2-q)}`. The ratio `lambda/r` has exponent `(3q-4)/(2-q)`, so `q<4/3` is exactly the regime `lambda/r -> infinity`; `q=4/3` is the linear-fit threshold. A concrete route to an endpoint proof is therefore a subcritical-profile Liouville theorem for the anisotropic limit equation, or an inhomogeneous contact-walk theorem at the `q=4/3` forcing level.

## Full check of the model fit

For `f(s)=a|s|^{4/3}`, `f'(s)=(4a/3)sgn(s)|s|^{1/3}`. Directly splitting `|delta|<=|s|/2` (use `|f''(s)|<=C|s|^{-2/3}`) and `|delta|>|s|/2` (use the 1/3-Hölder bound on `f'`) gives

`|f(s+delta)-f(s)-f'(s)delta| <= C delta^2/(|s|+|delta|)^{2/3}`.

At a center `(x_1,x_2)`, take `P=(f'(x_1),-f'(x_2),1)` for the lift. A displacement in `Z_{A r}(z,r;P)` has each horizontal coordinate increment satisfying `|delta_i|<=r|f_i'(x_i)|+sqrt(A)r^{3/2}`. Write `S=|x_i|` and `T=sqrt(A)r^{3/2}`.

- If `S>=T` and `A>=1`, then `S>=r^{3/2}`, `rS^{1/3}<=S`, and the remainder quotient is bounded by `C(r^2+A r^3/S^{2/3})<=C(1+A^{2/3})r^2`.
- If `S<T`, then `rS^{1/3}<=A^{1/6}r^{3/2}` and `|delta_i|<=C sqrt(A)r^{3/2}`; the Hölder form of the same remainder bound gives `C A^{2/3}r^2`.

Summing two coordinates gives residual `<=C(1+A^{2/3})r^2`. Choose a fixed `A` large enough that `A>=C(1+A^{2/3})`; then `lambda=A r` is a valid fit at every center for all sufficiently small r. Together with the origin lower bound `lambda>=r/128`, the example has the exact linear order `b(s)~s` up to constants.
