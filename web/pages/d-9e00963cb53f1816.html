# Log-concave midpoint domination and density-independent potential stability

## 1. Exact interface and source boundary

Let K be a full-dimensional compact convex body in R^d, contained in B_R(0). Let f be positive C^1 on a neighborhood of K, log-concave on K, and normalized by integral_K f=1. Write rho=f 1_K dx. Targets are arbitrary probability measures supported in a fixed compact Y contained in B_L(0).

The release manuscript is OpenAI, *Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps*, 25 September 2026, pinned commit adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a. Its source proves a moment bound with constant162 for the UNIFORM source, then W2-Lipschitz potential stability and the sharp 1/3 map exponent. Its Lemma4.2 explicitly identifies the midpoint density lemma with older displacement concavity. The weighted midpoint argument, derivative-limit constant sharpening, and conclusions below are our deductions. We use the ordinary finite-dimensional implicit function theorem, conditional scalar quantiles, convex duality, and elementary convex derivative slicing; no Lean statement is assumed.

The positivity/C^1 assumption is deliberately concrete: it permits weighted face differentiation and bounds log-density shifts. This note does not extend the map bound to an arbitrary singular or boundary-vanishing log-concave measure. The POTENTIAL bound has no dependence on min f, max f, or ||grad log f||.

## 2. Weighted midpoint coupling, fully reconstructed

Take convex compact U,V contained in K of positive rho-masses a,b. Couple their conditional rho laws by the same successive-quantile parameter s in (0,1)^d. For U define

  p_k(x_1,...,x_k)=integral f(x) 1_int(U)(x) dx_{k+1}...dx_d,
  p_0=a, p_d=f 1_int(U).

At prefixes in the projection of int(U), p_k is positive and continuous. Indeed, affine slices meeting int(U) have boundary-slice measure zero, and boundedness of f and U gives dominated convergence. Conditional scalar densities p_k/p_{k-1} have interval support and are continuous and positive in that interval. Their quantiles X_k(s_1,...,s_k) are measurable, strictly increasing C^1 in s_k, and

  partial_{s_k} X_k = p_{k-1}(X_<k)/p_k(X_<=k),
  product_k partial_{s_k}X_k = a/f(X).

Likewise the map Z(s) for V has diagonal product b/f(Z). Neither map needs differentiability in earlier parameters. Set H=(X+Z)/2. Its coordinatewise triangular derivatives are positive, and AM-GM plus log-concavity gives

  J_H := product_k partial_{s_k}H_k
       >= sqrt(ab)/sqrt(f(X)f(Z))
       >= sqrt(ab)/f(H).

The last direction matters: f(H)>=sqrt(f(X)f(Z)), so its reciprocal is smaller. For any nonnegative Borel g, successive one-dimensional substitutions in s_d,...,s_1 give

  integral_cube g(H) f(H) J_H ds <= integral_R^d g(x) f(x) dx.

Here f can be extended measurably by zero off K; H lies in K. The proof is coordinatewise substitution and enlargement of each image interval, not a full differentiable change-of-variables theorem. Earlier diagonal factors depend only on earlier parameters; Tonelli justifies each step. Thus

  sqrt(ab) Law((X+Z)/2) <= rho.                         (M1)

When U=P_i(c-sq), V=P_i(c+sq) are opposite intercept perturbations of a Laguerre cell, averaging the defining affine inequalities gives H in P_i(c), so the sharper support statement is

  sqrt(ab) Law(H) <= rho restricted to P_i(c).          (M2)

This is the only geometric input needed for the moment argument. It retains the full weighted density.

## 3. Weighted cell calculus and the moment bound

For distinct sites y_i and affine intercepts c_i, define cells

  P_i=K intersect {x: x.(y_i-y_j)+c_i-c_j>=0 for all j},
  M_i=integral_{P_i} f, B_i=integral_{P_i} x f.

Assume all M_i>0. Weighted face coefficients are

  g_ij=integral_{F_ij} f/|y_i-y_j| dS,
  b_ij=integral_{F_ij} x f/|y_i-y_j| dS.

Then

  DM_i[r,v]=sum_j[g_ij(r_i-r_j)+b_ij.(v_i-v_j)],
  DB_i[q,0]=sum_j b_ij(q_i-q_j).                       (C1)

These are jointly C^1 on the positive-cell distinct-site locus. To see this rather than import an unweighted formula, telescope perturbed half-space indicators, use normal coordinates to each equality plane, and dominated convergence with integrand f or x f. Triple-tie planes and K-boundary sections have surface measure zero. Continuous f supplies the limit on each face. Nearby-plane graph parametrization and the same dominated convergence prove derivative continuity. Strictly active interior points exist since f>0; generic segments between them cross positive-area faces. Thus the graph g_ij>0 is connected and its Laplacian G has kernel constants.

Apply (M2) cellwise. Write alpha_i=M_i(c-sq), beta_i=M_i(c+sq), gamma_i=sqrt(alpha_i beta_i), delta_i=alpha_i-beta_i, and D_i=E|X_i-Z_i|^2. For Phi=R^2-|x|^2>=0 on K, the exact midpoint quadratic identity and partition identities give

  A/4 <= R^2 B/8 + (R/2)sqrt(A B),
  A=sum_i gamma_i D_i, B=sum_i delta_i^2/gamma_i.

Explicitly, the mass deficit is
  (alpha+beta)/2-gamma = delta^2/[2(sqrt(alpha)+sqrt(beta))^2]
                         <=delta^2/(8gamma),
and the endpoint Phi-expectation difference is at most2R sqrt(D_i). Cauchy-Schwarz then gives the displayed inequality. Young's inequality yields

  A <=5R^2 B.                                         (C2)

The first-moment difference satisfies

  Delta B_i=alpha_i E X_i-beta_i E Z_i,
  |Delta B_i|<=R|delta_i|+alpha_i sqrt(D_i).

For this FIXED finite configuration, let E_s=sum_i delta_i^2/M_i. As s->0 all alpha_i,beta_i,gamma_i approach M_i. Therefore

  B=(1+o(1))E_s,
  max_i alpha_i^2/(M_i gamma_i)=1+o(1).

Minkowski in the weighted finite l2 norm and (C2) imply

  (sum_i |Delta B_i|^2/M_i)^(1/2)
     <= R sqrt(E_s)+(sum_i alpha_i^2 D_i/M_i)^(1/2)
     <=(1+sqrt5+o(1))R sqrt(E_s).

Divide by2s and use (C1). We obtain the sharper uniform derivative statement

  sum_i |DB_i[q,0]|^2/M_i
     <= (1+sqrt5)^2 R^2 sum_i |DM_i[q,0]|^2/M_i.        (C3)

No uniform finite step size is claimed: how small s must be may depend on every cell mass and the direction. Only the limiting constant is independent of atom count, masses, site separation, and density contrast. This sharpening works also in the release's uniform-source case.

## 4. Density-independent W2-Lipschitz convex potentials

For prescribed positive masses m_i summing to1, the convex objective
  F_y(c)=integral max_i(x.y_i+c_i) rho(dx)-sum_i m_i c_i
is coercive on sum_i m_i c_i=0; its gradient is M-m. It has one minimizer there: G is an isomorphism on the normalization hyperplane and local isolated minimizers plus convexity force uniqueness. The implicit function theorem gives C^1 dependence on distinct sites. No inverse-Laplacian norm bound is used.

Couple finite targets optimally as sum_i m_i delta_(y_i^0,y_i^1), merge repeated pairs, and let y_i(t)=(1-t)y_i^0+t y_i^1, v_i=y_i^1-y_i^0. Optimal-coupling monotonicity says
  (y_i^0-y_j^0).(y_i^1-y_j^1)>=0.
Hence distinct pairs have distinct interpolated sites for0<t<1. Let c(t) keep m_i fixed, sum m_i c_i=0, r_i=c_i'(t), and u_t=max_i(x.y_i(t)+c_i(t)).

The symmetry of face coefficients plus fixed masses gives, for every q,
  sum_i r_i DM_i[q,0] = -sum_i v_i.DB_i[q,0].          (P1)
Since sum m_i r_i=0, choose q with Gq=(m_i r_i)_i. By (C3),
  sum_i m_i r_i^2
       <= w (sum_i |DB_i[q,0]|^2/m_i)^(1/2)
       <=(1+sqrt5)R w (sum_i m_i r_i^2)^(1/2),
where w=W2(mu,nu)=(sum_i m_i |v_i|^2)^(1/2). Dividing when nonzero,
  ||r||_m <=(1+sqrt5)R w.
At rho-a.e. x the active cell is unique and
  partial_t u_t(x)=x.v_i+r_i,
so
  ||partial_t u_t||_L2(rho) <=(2+sqrt5)R w.             (P2)

The bound on each r_i also gives finite endpoint intercept limits. Endpoint potentials have gradients transporting rho to the target endpoint laws. Convex supporting inequalities and Fenchel duality give optimality and uniqueness without extra target regularity. Centering each potential by its rho mean is an orthogonal projection in L2, so (P2) yields

  ||u_mu^0-u_nu^0||_L2(rho)
       <= (2+sqrt5)R W2(mu,nu).                       (P3)

Here u_mu^0 denotes the normalized convex Brenier potential with integral u_mu^0 rho=0. General compactly supported targets follow by finite atomic approximation: the potentials have the common L-Lipschitz bound, centered potentials are uniformly bounded on K, and uniform subsequential limits satisfy the convex supporting inequality. At differentiability points the bounded gradients converge to that of the limit, which pushes rho to the desired law and is the unique optimal gradient. Thus (P3) is valid for ALL target probabilities on Y.

The constant depends on the source radius only; no lower-density, upper-density, log-gradient, or minimum-cell-mass factor remains. Translations permit optimizing R over enclosing balls of K; additive affine offsets correspond to translating target coordinates.

## 5. A weighted gradient-interpolation ledger and map exponent

For the map bound we pay additional source regularity honestly. Let
  A_f=max_K f, Q_f=max_K |grad log f|,
  P_K=sum_{j=1}^d |projection_{e_j^perp} K|,
  h0=(1+Q_f)^(-1).

For convex L-Lipschitz u,v, F=u-v, one-dimensional forward differences on K's coordinate interval fibers give, for0<h<=h0,

  ||Du-Dv||_L2(rho)^2
    <=[6d(1+e^{Q_f h})/h^2]||F||_L2(rho)^2
       +28 A_f L^2 P_K h.                            (G1)

Details: on each retained interval [a,b-h], convex derivatives are monotone and |u'|<=L. Set delta_u=(u(x+h)-u(x))/h-u'(x), so0<=delta_u<=u'(x+h)-u'(x)<=2L. The unweighted integral of the derivative increment is at most2Lh; hence integral delta_u^2 f<=4A_f L^2 h per fiber. The same holds for v. The squared difference of forward quotients is at most2/h^2 times the sum of F(x+h)^2 and F(x)^2. Since f(x)<=e^{Q_f h} f(x+h), its weighted integral is at most2(1+e^{Q_f h})||F||_rho^2/h^2. The three-term square bound gives coefficient6. Discarded fiber endpoints cost at most4A_f L^2h, so the two convex errors24 plus endpoint4 give28. Summing coordinate projections proves(G1).

Let C_p=(2+sqrt5)R and
  C_small^2=6d(1+e) C_p^2+28 A_f L^2 P_K,
  C_map^2=max(C_small^2,4L^2/h0).
For w<=h0^(3/2), choose h=w^(2/3) and use(P3) in(G1). For w>=h0^(3/2), the trivial map distance<=2L gives the same result with the second constant. Thus

  ||T_mu-T_nu||_L2(rho) <= C_map W2(mu,nu)^(1/3).      (G2)

This is a sharp class exponent: the uniform cube is within these hypotheses and the release's explicit three-atom targets have
  W2^2=a^3/4,
  ||T_mu-T_nu||_rho^2=a/4+a^3/4.
An exponent greater than1/3 therefore fails uniformly even at exactly three atoms.

## 6. Concrete downstream use and obstruction

(P3) is an atom-count-independent stability certificate for potential continuation and target quantization under truncated smooth log-concave source laws, e.g. a truncated Gaussian. If Q projects Y onto an eta-net, W2(mu,Q#mu)<=eta, so the centered potential error is <=C_p eta and the induced map error <=C_map eta^(1/3). This is an analytic certificate, not a cell-solver runtime theorem: exact mass/intercept acquisition, numerical precision, and target representation still have to be charged.

There is also a deterministic information limit for W2-only map reconstruction. At observation bar_mu=mu_a in the cube example, both mu_a and nu_a lie in the uncertainty ball of radius epsilon=a^(3/2)/2. Any reported map has worst-case L2 error at least one half of ||T_mu_a-T_nu_a||, which is at least2^(-5/3) epsilon^(1/3). The upper bound(G2) gives the matching exponent. This is a deterministic metric-noise statement; it is NOT a statistical sample-size lower bound. Rare atomic supports can convey more information than a W2 radius records.
