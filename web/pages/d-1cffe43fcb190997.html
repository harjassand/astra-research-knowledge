HKZ RAW-TRIPLE SPECTRAL HMM RECONSTRUCTION AND AN UNKNOWN-RANK WRAPPER
Cycle 2 theorem/source ownership | 8 October 2026

STATUS

This record reconstructs the source-reported fixed-rank guarantees of Hsu–Kakade–Zhang (HKZ), derives their observable-operator equations directly from legal length-three observation samples, and proves a split-sample unknown-rank wrapper under the same pair-moment singular margin. The wrapper is an internally proved conditional extension of standard singular-value perturbation; it is not a new general HMM-learning theorem. No result here gives a gap-free, rank-free guarantee. Exact sampled-count bit costs and prediction horizons are charged below; the original paper does not give a certified finite-precision bit-runtime for its SVD/pseudoinverse pipeline.

1. PRIMARY SOURCE AND ASSUMPTIONS

Primary source: Daniel Hsu, Sham M. Kakade, and Tong Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” arXiv:0811.4413v6 (6 July 2012), published in JCSS 78(5), 1460–1480 (2012). Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413 . The reconstruction below uses Sections 2.1–2.4, 3.1–3.2, Figure 1, Theorems 6–7, and Appendix A / Section 5.1 (the sampling and parameter-error lemmas). The 2009 COLT proceedings version is https://www.learningtheory.org/colt2009/papers/011.pdf; formulas below follow the expanded v6 source.

HKZ use m hidden states and n observation symbols, with m≤n. Their convention is column-stochastic T: T_{ij}=Pr[h_{t+1}=i | h_t=j], O_{xj}=Pr[x_t=x | h_t=j], and pi_j=Pr[h_1=j]. Condition 1 is pi>0 coordinatewise and rank(O)=rank(T)=m. For the sample-complexity theorem, each sample is an independently generated triple (X_1,X_2,X_3) from the same initial distribution pi. Thus the theorem does not require a long-run mixing assumption, but it does require independent episodes/triples. HKZ say independence is for the analysis convenience; using overlapping triples from one long trajectory would need an additional dependence/mixing argument not in Theorem 6.

The count m is an input to Algorithm LearnHMM(m,N): its SVD step retains the m largest left singular vectors. Theorem 6 assumes this supplied m is the true hidden-state count and Condition 1 holds. The source notes that in practice m may be tuned by cross-validation and that the model can be misspecified; this practical remark is not a rank-free finite-sample theorem. The exact rank enters both the truncation dimension and all displayed sample/conditioning terms.

2. RAW MOMENTS AND OPERATOR DERIVATION (INTERNALLY PROVED)

For a raw triple (x_1,x_2,x_3), define empirical moments by direct event counts:

  P_1[j]       = Pr[X_1=j],
  P_{2,1}[i,j] = Pr[X_2=i, X_1=j],
  P_{3,x,1}[i,j] = Pr[X_3=i, X_2=x, X_1=j].

The estimates are the corresponding averages of indicators over N independent triples. These are legal observation-only statistics: no hidden state, conditional query, transition table, or emission parameter is accessed. There is a small source-text issue worth preserving: Figure 1 prints b-hat_infty=(P-hat_{2,1}^T U-hat)^+ P_1, with P_1 unhatted, while the parameter-error proof in Section 5.1 compares against the empirical P-hat_1. An unhatted population P_1 is not legally available to the learner. The algorithm reconstructed here uses P-hat_1, consistent with the proof and the claim that all three moments are empirically estimated; this appears to be a typographical omission in Figure 1.

Expanding over hidden states gives

  P_1 = O pi,
  P_{2,1} = O T diag(pi) O^T,
  P_{3,x,1} = O T diag(O_{x,:}) T diag(pi) O^T.

The ordering matters: rows index the later endpoint, columns the earlier endpoint, and the middle symbol x is fixed in P_{3,x,1}. With A_x := T diag(O_{x,:}), the last identity is P_{3,x,1}=O A_x T diag(pi) O^T. It is consistent with HKZ Lemma 1, which states Pr[x_1,...,x_t]=1^T A_{x_t}...A_{x_1} pi.

Under Condition 1, rank(P_{2,1})=m. Indeed, P_{2,1}=O[T diag(pi)O^T], and both factors have rank m. If U is any n×m matrix with U^T O invertible (HKZ Condition 2), then the population observable parameters

  b_1 = U^T P_1,
  b_infty = (P_{2,1}^T U)^+ P_1,
  B_x = (U^T P_{3,x,1})(U^T P_{2,1})^+

obey

  b_1=(U^T O)pi,
  b_infty^T=1^T(U^T O)^(-1),
  B_x=(U^T O)A_x(U^T O)^(-1).

For b_infty, solve (P_{2,1}^T U)b=P_1 in the column space of O; since T^T 1=1, this gives (U^TO)^T b=1. For B_x, let D=U^TP_{2,1}=(U^TO)T diag(pi)O^T, which has full row rank; multiplying U^TP_{3,x,1} by D^+ cancels the common right factor and yields the similarity formula. Consequently the sequence probability telescopes:

  Pr[x_1,...,x_t] = b_infty^T B_{x_t}...B_{x_1} b_1.

HKZ Lemma 2 shows that taking U as the m leading left singular vectors of P_{2,1} gives range(U)=range(O), hence U^TO is invertible. LearnHMM replaces each moment by its empirical estimate, takes Uhat from the top-m left singular vectors of P-hat_{2,1}, and uses the same formulas (Figure 1). This is an observable linear realization, not a recovery of O,T; the output may be an improper model and need not produce probabilities in [0,1] without normalization/clipping.

3. SOURCE-REPORTED GUARANTEES AND CONDITIONING

Theorem 6 (HKZ, joint probabilities). Write s= sigma_m(P_{2,1}), o=sigma_m(O), and define

  e_tail(k) = min { sum_{j in S} Pr[X_2=j] : S subset [n], |S|=n-k },
  n_0(z) = min { k : e_tail(k) <= z }.

For a requested error 0<epsilon<1 and horizon t≥1, set z_t=o s epsilon/(4t sqrt(m)). Under Condition 1, there is a universal constant C such that it suffices to take

  N >= C (t^2/epsilon^2) [ m/(o^2 s^4)
                           + m n_0(z_t)/(o^2 s^2) ] log(1/eta).

With probability at least 1-eta, the learned model has

  sum_{x_1,...,x_t} |Pr[x_1,...,x_t]-Pr_hat[x_1,...,x_t]| <= epsilon.

This is L1 error on the full length-t joint distribution (equivalently TV≤epsilon/2), not a pointwise error for every word. The dependence on n is implicit through o,s and n_0; the tail term can be smaller than n when X_2 has a concentrated marginal.

Theorem 7 (HKZ, conditional prediction). Define the value-of-observation margin

  g_obs = inf_{||v||_1=1} ||O v||_1 > 0

and the positivity margin alpha by [A_x]_{ij}≥alpha>0 for every symbol x and states i,j. Put z=o s epsilon/(4 sqrt(m)). Under Conditions 1 and 3, HKZ give the bound

  N >= C { [ m/(epsilon^2 alpha^2)
              + log^4(2/alpha)/(epsilon^4 alpha^2 g_obs^4) ]
             m/(o^2 s^4)
           + [m n_0(z)/(epsilon^2 o^2 s^2)] } log(1/eta).

With probability at least 1-eta, for every time t the expected conditional KL error under the true history law is at most epsilon:

  E_{X_{1:t}}[ log( Pr[X_t|X_{1:t-1}] /
                          Pr_hat[X_t|X_{1:t-1}] ) ] <= epsilon.

The output conditional distribution uses HKZ's normalization by sum_x b_hat_infty^T B_hat_x b_hat_t. This is stronger in horizon uniformity than the fixed-horizon joint-L1 statement, but it needs the additional all-symbol/all-transition positivity margin alpha and is an expected KL statement, not uniform pointwise control over every low-probability history. HKZ note g_obs≥sigma_m(O)/sqrt(n), though g_obs can be larger.

The exact conditioning path is visible in Section 5.1. A perturbation epsilon_{2,1}=||P-hat_{2,1}-P_{2,1}||_2≤s/3 stabilizes the selected m-dimensional subspace; the source's subspace lemma lower-bounds sigma_m(Uhat^T O) by a constant-factor multiple of o. The pseudoinverse perturbation terms scale as epsilon_{2,1}/s^2. In the parameter-error lemma, delta_infty is O(epsilon_{2,1}/s^2 + epsilon_1/s), while the sum Delta=sum_x Delta_x includes factors sqrt(m)/o times (epsilon_{2,1}/s^2 + sum_x epsilon_{3,x,1}/s). Thus the true model's pair-moment singular margin s controls inversion, and the emission margin o controls the coordinate change. These are singular-value margins, not a Markov-chain mixing/spectral-gap condition.

For completeness, HKZ's Lemma 8 reports that for N independent triples, simultaneously with probability at least 1-eta,

  epsilon_1, epsilon_{2,1}, max_x epsilon_{3,x,1}
       <= sqrt(log(3/eta)/N)+sqrt(1/N),
  sum_x epsilon_{3,x,1}
       <= min_k [sqrt((k/N)log(3/eta))+sqrt(k/N)+2 e_tail(k)]
          +sqrt(log(3/eta)/N)+sqrt(1/N).

Combining this concentration result with the above inverse perturbation and the product/telescoping argument gives Theorem 6. The source's universal constant C is not numerically instantiated; the formulas are theorem bounds, not exact minimal sample counts.

4. SPLIT-SAMPLE UNKNOWN-RANK EXTENSION (INTERNALLY PROVED, CONDITIONAL)

Claim. Let the observation alphabet have size n and suppose the unknown HMM satisfies HKZ Condition 1 with true rank m, but m is not provided. Let lambda=sigma_m(P_{2,1})>0. Use N_r independent raw triples as a rank pilot and an independent N_f triples as a fitting sample. On the pilot, compute P-hat^{(r)}_{2,1}, and a certified singular-value estimate sigma-tilde_j with absolute error at most zeta for every singular value. Define

  q_r = n sqrt(log(2 n^2/delta_r)/(2 N_r)),
  R_r = q_r+zeta,
  m_hat = #{j : sigma-tilde_j(P-hat^{(r)}_{2,1}) > 2 R_r}.

If lambda≥4 R_r, then with probability at least 1-delta_r, m_hat=m. This algorithm does not take m as an input. It does require a finite-precision singular-value routine with certified error zeta; zeta is charged explicitly. On the independent fitting sample, run LearnHMM(m_hat,N_f). If N_f meets HKZ Theorem 6's bound with the true m and eta=delta_f, then with probability at least 1-delta_r-delta_f the rank is selected correctly and the fitted model satisfies the source joint-L1 guarantee. The same transfer applies to Theorem 7 when its extra Conditions and sample bound hold.

Proof. For each pair (i,j), a pilot triple contributes the Bernoulli variable 1{X_2=i,X_1=j} with mean [P_{2,1}]_{ij}. Hoeffding and a union bound over n^2 cells give max-cell error at most sqrt(log(2n^2/delta_r)/(2N_r)) with probability at least 1-delta_r. Hence ||P-hat^{(r)}_{2,1}-P_{2,1}||_2≤||.||_F≤q_r. Weyl's singular-value inequality plus the certified computation error gives |sigma-tilde_j-sigma_j(P_{2,1})|≤R_r for all j. Under Condition 1 the population singular values are positive through j=m and zero afterward. For j≤m, sigma-tilde_j≥lambda-R_r≥3R_r>2R_r; for j>m, sigma-tilde_j≤R_r<2R_r. Counting above 2R_r returns exactly m. The independent fit batch is not selected on its own moments; condition on successful pilot selection, apply the fixed-m source theorem to that independent batch, then add failure probabilities.

A concrete sufficient margin/sample setting is: if a lower bound lambda≥mu>0 is part of the promise, take zeta≤mu/8 and

  N_r >= 32 n^2 mu^(-2) log(2 n^2/delta_r).

Then q_r≤mu/8, R_r≤mu/4≤lambda/4 and the cutoff succeeds. The cutoff itself uses R_r, not mu; mu is only a condition used to state a sample guarantee. Total independent-episode cost is N_r+N_f triples, i.e. 3(N_r+N_f) observed symbols. The added pilot cost is O(n^2 mu^(-2) log(n/delta_r)); this conservative entrywise-Hoeffding bound is not claimed optimal. The extension does not remove the HKZ fitting dependence on o, lambda, alpha, g_obs, or n_0, nor does it choose a data-dependent stopping time without a supplied target sample budget/margin promise.

5. SAMPLE, BIT, COMPUTATION, AND HORIZON LEDGER

Sampling interface and samples. Each data unit is one independent length-three observation triple reset from the same pi. The fitting batch contributes N_f triples; the pilot contributes N_r triples. Therefore the acquisition cost is exactly N_r+N_f independent calls if one call returns one triple, and exactly 3(N_r+N_f) categorical observations. No length-t samples are needed to fit; t appears in the Theorem 6 accuracy/sample target, not in each training call. A long single stream with overlapping windows is outside this iid calculation unless a separate mixing/concentration theorem is added.

Raw and sufficient-statistic bits. Assume symbols have fixed-width labels in [n], so a returned triple costs 3 ceil(log2 n) bits. The raw data costs 3(N_r+N_f)ceil(log2 n) bits if retained. The pilot pair-count table has n^2 integers in {0,...,N_r}, requiring n^2 ceil(log2(N_r+1)) bits plus the denominator N_r (O(log N_r) bits). For fitting, the three empirical statistic families have n, n^2, and n^3 integer counters, respectively, each at most N_f; a dense exact-counter implementation uses (n+n^2+n^3)ceil(log2(N_f+1)) bits plus denominators. If the triples are retained, sparse or projected accumulation can trade this tensor storage for the raw-data buffer; it cannot make the information-acquisition bits disappear. Every empirical probability is exactly a rational count divided by N_f, so its stored count/denominator bit cost is explicit.

Numerical precision boundary. HKZ analyze real-arithmetic SVDs and Moore–Penrose pseudoinverses; neither Theorems 6–7 nor the algorithm state a certified bit-complexity or finite-precision implementation. The rank wrapper exposes this missing cost as zeta, the additive singular-value certification error. For the fitting stage, input moment counts can remain exact rationals, but Uhat and the pseudoinverse-derived Bhat_x are generally non-rational. Their rounding errors must be small enough to fit the parameter-error budgets in Section 5.1 (in particular epsilon_{2,1}<s/3 and errors scaled by s^{-2}, o^{-1}, and for conditional prediction alpha^{-1}, g_obs^{-1}). A complete bit algorithm needs certified matrix-function/error bounds and a chosen arithmetic backend. We do not claim that the source's real-RAM polynomial arithmetic already supplies this. A conservative rational implementation can set explicit operator-norm error tolerances for every moment and intermediate; entrywise rounding of an n×n moment matrix to b fractional bits contributes at most n·2^{-(b+1)} in operator norm, but output SVD/pseudoinverse error still needs its own certification. The model has n matrices B_x of shape m×m and two m-vectors, so once a finite coordinate precision and magnitude bound are proved its storage is O((n m^2+m)b) bits; HKZ does not provide that magnitude/rounding guarantee.

Arithmetic and storage. A direct dense implementation stores O(n^3) triple counters and runs an n×n SVD (O(n^3) arithmetic operations). A streaming alternative stores raw triples or the pair table; after Uhat is computed, one sample (x1,x2,x3) contributes the m-vector Uhat^T e_{x3} times e_{x1}^T to the projected central-symbol accumulator G_{x2}=Uhat^T P-hat_{3,x2,1}, costing O(m) arithmetic per sample and O(m n^2) accumulator entries. Forming all Bhat_x=G_x(Uhat^T P-hat_{2,1})^+ costs O(m^2 n^2) dense arithmetic after the m×n pseudoinverse is available. Learning b1,b_infty has smaller additional matrix costs. The source reports that, aside from sampling, its algorithm is dominated by an n×n SVD; this record separately exposes the straightforward all-symbol matrix-product cost rather than silently treating it as free. The source-reported prediction cost for a length-t joint score is O(t m^2) arithmetic. A full next-symbol vector by dense B_x matrix-vector products is O(nm^2); a specified-symbol update is O(m^2) once the normalizer is available. These are arithmetic counts, not bit operations.

Prediction horizons. For a fixed t, Theorem 6 pays a t^2/epsilon^2 factor and certifies L1 error over the whole length-t word distribution. To certify every horizon 1≤s≤T from the same learned model, apply Theorem 6 at each s and union bound: N using T in the polynomial factor, z_T=o lambda epsilon/(4T sqrt(m)), and log(T/delta) in place of log(1/eta) suffices, since s≤T implies s^2≤T^2 and n_0(z_s)≤n_0(z_T). This all-prefix claim is our union-bound derivation, not a separately stated source theorem. Theorem 7 instead states one event on which the expected next-symbol conditional KL bound holds for every t, with no t factor in N; it requires the strong alpha and g_obs margins and does not give pointwise accuracy for rare histories. Model evaluation has O(t m^2) cost regardless of whether the statistical target is joint-L1 or conditional-KL.

6. SCOPE, BASELINES, AND OPEN GATES

Strongest equal-data control for the wrapper: with the same total M iid triples, reserve a validation fraction and fit the truncated HKZ model for each candidate rank q=1,...,n on the remaining triples; score each candidate on held-out triple probabilities or next-symbol Brier loss, with a fixed clipping/smoothing rule, and select the best validation score. This is the direct cross-validation route HKZ themselves mention. It uses the same 3M observed symbols, up to n fits and their matrix costs, and a validation buffer; a retained empirical lookup table for the three-symbol probabilities is an additional no-compression baseline. Compare these against pilot+fit with the same total triple, bit, and arithmetic budgets. The split threshold targets exact algebraic rank under a margin; validation targets predictive loss and may rationally choose less than the exact rank when a weak component has negligible prediction impact. Therefore the two criteria are not interchangeable.

No uniform rank guarantee without a lower singular margin follows from this source: Condition 1 only says s>0, with no uniform lower bound over a class. The pilot theorem is conditional on lambda≥4R_r. An adversary may let lambda decrease below the noise/rounding floor. This record does not prove a minimax lower bound for the exact HKZ full triple interface; it only isolates the margin debt in a sufficient rank-selection result.

Open gates: (i) prove an improved, dimension-sensitive concentration bound for rank selection from paired observations; (ii) choose a concrete certified SVD/pseudoinverse and derive its bit-runtime and output-rounding budget as a function of n,m,s,o,epsilon; (iii) match the threshold procedure against held-out prediction and retained-data baselines at equal raw-triple/bit budgets; (iv) determine whether the unknown-rank wrapper yields a consequential gain beyond a standard model-selection implementation. No novelty or field-breaking claim is made.
