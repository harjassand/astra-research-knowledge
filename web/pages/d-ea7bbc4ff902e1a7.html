1. ORTHOGONALITY MATRIX AND NORMALIZATION

Let m>=3, V=F_2^m\{0}, N=2^m-1 and d=2^{m-1}-1. The dot product
is the standard nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form; it is not alternating.
For every nonzero x, x-perp has dimension m-1 and exactly d nonzero
members, so A_xy=1_{x dot y=0} is symmetric and d-regular. Its transition
kernel A/d is stochastic and uniform pi is stationary. Thus P=A/(Nd)
is a probability law, not a distribution requiring a separate normalizer.
Self-orthogonal nonzero x occur and create permitted diagonal pairs. They
must NOT be excluded by calling the orthogonality graph simple/loopless.
The stated process correctly allows them.

The instrument E_y(X)=<y|X|y> q_y is CP. Each Kraus
K_{y,z}=d^{-1/2}|z><y| has K†K=|y><y|/d; summing the d orthogonal
nonzero z for fixed y gives |y><y| and summing y gives I. Hence the
instrument is TP after forgetting the observed outcome. Its initial state
I/N is stationary by regularity, and every observed positive-probability
history ends at q_y. The unobserved preparation index z is not a new
observable outcome. Revealing it would change the model and its capacity.

2. ALL-PRIORS CAPACITY AND EXACT COMMON MAPS

Every q_x is uniform on d of the N basis states; D(q_x||I/N)=ln(N/d).
Uniformly mixing q_x yields I/N because every basis coordinate belongs to
d rows. For any finite prior, the center identity
 sum pi_j D(rho_j||I/N)=chi+D(mean rho||I/N)
and convexity of relative entropy upper bound chi by ln(N/d), including
convexly forgotten histories and I/N. Uniform q_x prior attains equality.
Thus C=ln(N/d)=ln(2+1/d)<=ln(7/3)<ln4 and tends to ln2. This is the
correct all-priors quantity for the supplied capacity theorem, not merely
one stationary ensemble's Holevo information.

Basis measurement/preparation fixes all states exactly; basis copying
has both marginal channels equal to dephasing. Both channels are full-domain
CPTP and outputs of copying are correlated. These statements require no
independent-copy or reference-preservation assumption. The quantum physical
stationary memory entropy is lnN, so the example makes no quantum memory
advantage claim.

3. SUPPORT RECTANGLES AND EXACT WYNER INFORMATION

For every positive-weight product component a(x)b(y) in a factorization
of P, its support S times R is contained in the orthogonality relation.
U=span(S) and W=span(R) are orthogonal by bilinearity. Nondegeneracy gives
dim(U)+dim(W)<=m; support cardinalities are <=2^r-1 and <=2^s-1, with
r,s>=1. For fixed r, increasing s to m-r cannot shrink the product, and
the resulting expression is
 2^m+1-2^r-2^{m-r}.
It is maximized when r and m-r differ by at most one. Hence the exact
maximum is B_m=(2^{floor(m/2)}-1)(2^{ceil(m/2)}-1). This remains valid
when U intersects U-perp: the dot product is not assumed positive definite
over F_2, and disjointness is not required for a product rectangle.

Each conditional product has entropy <=ln B_m, while H(P)=ln(Nd).
Therefore every screening latent variable C has
 I(X,Y;C)>=ln(Nd/B_m), H(C)>=ln(Nd/B_m).

For equality of Wyner information, choose uniform r-dimensional U with
r=floor(m/2), then draw X uniform U\0 and Y uniform U-perp\0 independently.
Each rectangle has B_m pairs. For fixed permitted x,y, the allowable U
are precisely the r-dimensional subspaces containing span(x) inside
y-perp. The quotient y-perp/span(x) has dimension m-2, so their number
is the same Gaussian-binomial coefficient [m-2 choose r-1]_2 for EVERY
permitted pair. This includes x=y when x is self-orthogonal; no transitivity
of the orthogonal group or linear independence of x,y is needed. No
subspace contains a forbidden pair. Total incidence counting then gives
uniform mass 1/(Nd) on every permitted pair. Conditional entropy is
ln B_m, proving C_W(P)=ln(Nd/B_m).

This witnesses I(X,Y;U), not H(U), and does not generate the entire
Markov process from a small HMM. The baseline preserves this distinction
correctly. Its proven full-process entropy range remains
 ln(Nd/B_m)<=inf H(C_t)<=lnN.

4. FULL RANK AND EXACT HMM STATE COUNT

For x=z the count of nonzero vectors orthogonal to both is d. For distinct
nonzero x,z over F_2, their two linear constraints are independent, giving
c=2^{m-2}-1 common nonzero orthogonal vectors. Therefore
 A²=(d-c)I+cJ=2^{m-2}I+(2^{m-2}-1)J.
Its two eigenvalues are positive: 2^{m-2} on 1-perp and d² on 1.
Thus real rank(A)=N. A K-state HMM factors the adjacent-output law into
K product components at the postoutput memory cut, so K>=rank(P)=N.
The visible Markov chain has N states and proves exact minimum K=N.

The diagonal q_x also span all N diagonal coordinates, since their row
matrix is A/d. Any EB channel fixing them has linear range dimension
at least N; K prepared outcomes give range dimension <=K, hence K>=N.
This is a exact-rank conclusion, with no rank-stability inference at
positive approximation error.

5. ROBUST PARITY-ENTROPY LEMMA, ALL EDGE CASES

Use bits in this paragraph. Fix eta=1/20 and h=h_2(eta). For one product
component a(x)b(y), define e=Pr(X dot Y=1),
G={y:Pr_a(X dot y=1)<=eta}, tau=Pr_b(Y outside G), r=dim span(G).
Markov gives tau<=e/eta. A basis of G provides r independent linear
coordinates of X, each with one-probability <=eta, and an invertible
completion to m coordinates. Subadditivity gives
 H(X)<=m-(1-h)r.
Since G lies in an r-dimensional space, splitting Y by membership in G
gives
 H(Y)<=h_2(tau)+(1-tau)r+tau m<=r+tau m+h_2(tau),
and separately H(Y)<=m.

The baseline's displayed combined bound is valid for EVERY tau in [0,1].
An explicit derivation, avoiding a case-dependent optimization in r, is
 H(X)+H(Y)=H(X)+(1-h)H(Y)+h H(Y)
 <=m-(1-h)r+(1-h)[r+tau m+h_2(tau)]+hm
 =[1+h+(1-h)tau]m+(1-h)h_2(tau).
This covers tau=0, tau=1, G empty (then r=0,tau=1), and all intermediate
values. The conditional-coordinate entropy argument does not require a
bound on each component's e other than the definition of G.

For Q within TV delta of P, its forbidden-pair mass is <=delta. Thus
weighted mean e<=delta and weighted mean tau<=delta/eta<=1/5. Jensen
holds even if some individual tau>1/2. After averaging, monotonicity of
h_2 on [0,1/2] is applied ONLY to their mean, giving the stated bound
on H_Q(X,Y|C). This order of Jensen and monotonicity is essential.

Continuity on an N²-point alphabet gives
 H_Q(X,Y)>=log_2(Nd)-delta log_2(N²-1)-h_2(delta)
            >=log_2(Nd)-2delta m-h_2(delta).
For m>=3, let a=2^{m-1}>=4. Then Nd=(2a-1)(a-1)>=a² because
a²-3a+1>0; hence log_2(Nd)>=2m-2. Subtracting the conditional entropy
bound yields precisely the baseline's alpha_delta and additive constant.
At delta<=1/100 the bound is minimized at the endpoint: alpha decreases
linearly, h_2(delta) increases, and h_2(delta/eta) increases on [0,1/5].

7. COMPARISON WITH THE FROZEN BALANCED-SUBSET ORIGIN

Both examples refute capacity-only memory cardinality and entropy bounds
and preserve exact all-priors/statewise premises. The finite-field example
improves the observed alphabet from binom(N,N/2)+N+1 to N, removes the
three-phase periodic architecture, and has capacity tending to ln2 with
uniform adjacent/past-future information. In fact its visible chain has
some self-loop and is irreducible for m>=3: distinct x,z have at least one
common orthogonal nonzero neighbor c=2^{m-2}-1>=1. Hence it is aperiodic
and mixing in each fixed finite dimension. This last conclusion follows
directly from its A² counts and self-orthogonal nonzero vectors; it is an
audit consequence, not a new exploratory branch. In fact the same identity
gives the exact two-step decomposition
 T²=gamma I+(1-gamma)Pi,

where Pi has every row uniform. Thus for ANY two initial distributions
the two-step TV distance contracts by exactly gamma. The example even has
a dimension-uniform two-step mixing bound; stationarity is not hiding a
slow long-term memory mechanism.

Its robust K lower bound at delta=1/100 is a positive power of N (the
clean version sqrt(N+1)/16); its exact K is N, and its minimum exact
Shannon entropy is bounded within a constant of lnN rather than determined
exactly. The origin balanced-subset process has a charged exponential
