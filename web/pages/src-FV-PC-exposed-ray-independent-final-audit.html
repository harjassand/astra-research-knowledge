4. General output-matrix diffusion: covariance and averaging

The operator class is

 L v=sum_{i,j=1}^2 C^{ij} partial_i partial_j v,
 C^{ij}=C^{ji} real 2-by-2 matrices,
 sym C(n)>=kappa |n|^2 I for some kappa>0,

Spatial coefficient symmetry is without loss because mixed derivatives
commute. Output matrices are allowed to be nonsymmetric. This is the
explicit uniformly positive symmetric-symbol condition, not an arbitrary
weaker notion of parabolicity.

For A=Dv, B_i=partial_i Dv, integration by parts gives

 d/dt integral F(Dv)=-integral E_{F,L}(A,B),
 E_{F,L}=sum_{i,j}D^2F(A)[B_i,C^{ij}B_j].

The operator acts on the output index of B_j. This verifies the order and
orientation of the matrix multiplication in the proposed density.

If v'(x)=U v(R^T x), then

 A'=U A R^T,
 B'_i=sum_k R_ik U B_k R^T,
 C'^{ij}=sum_{k,l} R_ik R_jl U C^{kl} U^T.

The invariance of F gives E_{F,L'}(A',B')=E_{F,L}(A,B). If T(A)=U A R^T,
the corresponding correction is Omega'=det(R)(T^{-1})^*Omega. Indeed,
T^*Omega'=det(R)Omega, while the two spatial derivative directions contribute
the factor det(R). Thus the correction value also agrees. Using inverse
group elements gives the equivalent det(R)T^* notation for averaging.

Each transformed pair is feasible whenever the original pair is feasible.
Averaging the PAIR is legitimate here because F itself is invariant under
the admitted symmetries: the density is linear in C and Omega. It preserves
closedness, degree, pointwise nonnegativity, and strong parabolicity. No
independent averaging of an arbitrary changing energy and operator is used.

Output SO(2) averaging projects each real matrix C onto alpha I+beta J,
where J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]. For the operator coefficients this projection equals
(C+J C J^T)/2. It leaves all closed corrections left-SO(2) invariant as well.

F is invariant under input column swap, and under paired input/output
reflection D=diag(1,-1). The swap equalizes the two diagonal scalar
coefficients. The paired reflection kills mixed scalar coefficients and
diagonal skew-output coefficients; its two sign reversals preserve the
mixed skew-output coefficient. Averaging over these finite symmetries

The independent checker performs this projection on all 12 arbitrary real

Strong parabolicity gives sym C11>=kappa I and sym C22>=kappa I, hence

7. Lyapunov implication and the determinant-square supplement

for an integrand other than F. At fixed A,n, pure jets make the two-form

norm of a solution of y'=-C(n)y. Thus C(n) is positive stable for n!=0,
and exp(-C(n)^Tt) S exp(-C(n)t) tends to zero for every finite S.
Differentiate this expression and integrate from zero to infinity:

8. Mixed-cone deduction and its exact invariance restriction

Frozen v2 correctly handles arbitrary polyconvex addends. Suppose F is a
finite positive sum of LEFT-SO(2)-INVARIANT heat-certified quartics H_i and
arbitrary homogeneous quartic polyconvex addends P_j. Collect the P_j into
one P. It is polyconvex by closure under positive sums; it is invariant
because P=F-sum H_i. Every H_i is ROC by the Lyapunov argument, and P is ROC
by polyconvexity. Apply ell to these invariant polynomials. Every term is
nonnegative and their sum is zero, so each lies on the ray of F.

A nonzero H_i would be a positive multiple of F and would give F a
certificate by positive rescaling, contradicting Section 6. Thus every
heat piece is zero and P=F. Conversely, if F is polyconvex the decomposition
with zero heat part exists. This establishes the stated equivalence with

Individual noninvariant P_j are not asserted to be proportional to F.
Noninvariant heat pieces are outside this deduction: joint averaging of
their changing integrand and changing operator need not factor into one
certificate for an averaged integrand and one averaged operator. Exposure
