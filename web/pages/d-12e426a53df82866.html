# Nonzero-level polynomial critical sections force an origin pivot normalform

Cycle15; 7 October2026. All-degree deduction by hessian_bridge12. Independently reconstructed by hessian_check12; internal audit PASS under the stated hypotheses. No external validation and no constant-Hessian counterexample.

**Exact question.** Which genuinely nonlinear old-pivot changes can admit a global polynomial new-pivot critical section at a nonzero constant level, before expanding a reduced Hessian determinant?

## Theorem

Let \(b\) be fixed base variables, \(R=(\rho,\eta)\), and
\[
 f_0(b,t,R_0)=Lt^2+a(b)t+c(b)R_0,\qquad L\in\mathbb C^\times.
\]
Let
\[
 \Phi_b(\tau,R)=(T(b,\tau,R),\Theta(b,\tau,R))
\]
be an actual polynomial automorphism of \(\mathbb A^3\) over \(\mathbb C[b]\), so its polynomial inverse is also over that base ring. It may move the old pivot and mix it with the residual coordinates. Suppose there is a global polynomial section \(\tau=s(b,R)\) such that
\[
 \partial_\tau(f_0\circ\Phi)(b,s(b,R),R)=\sigma\in\mathbb C^\times. \tag{1}
\]
At any base point \(b_0\) with \(c(b_0)=0\), write \(a_0=a(b_0)\) and \(s_0(R)=s(b_0,R)\). Then constants \(t_0\in\mathbb C\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times\) exist such that
\[
 T(b_0,\tau,R)=t_0+\lambda(\tau-s_0(R)),\qquad
 (2Lt_0+a_0)\lambda=\sigma. \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the old pivot is affine in the new pivot over this entire base fibre, with constant nonzero coefficient. Genuine nonlinear dependence on \(\tau\) at \(b_0\) is impossible.

## Proof

Specialization of a polynomial automorphism and its inverse at \(b_0\) is a polynomial automorphism of \(\mathbb A^3_\mathbb C\). Set \(T_0=T(b_0,\tau,R)\). Since \(c(b_0)=0\), equation (1) specializes to
\[
 (2L\,T_0(s_0(R),R)+a_0)
 \,(\partial_\tau T_0)(s_0(R),R)=\sigma
\]
in \(\mathbb C[R]\). The product is a nonzero constant. Both factors are units in this polynomial ring, hence nonzero constants. Because \(L\ne0\), \(T_0(s_0(R),R)=t_0\) is a constant, while \((\partial_\tau T_0)|_{\tau=s_0}=\lambda\ne0\).

The polynomial \(T_0-t_0\) is a coordinate polynomial: it is the image of an affine coordinate under a ring automorphism. Consequently it is irreducible. Polynomial division by the monic \(\tau-s_0(R)\) shows that \(T_0-t_0\) is divisible by \(\tau-s_0(R)\), since its evaluation at \(\tau=s_0\) vanishes. The divisor is itself irreducible. Therefore the quotient is a unit of \(\mathbb C[\tau,R]\), i.e. a nonzero constant. Its value is \(\lambda\), as seen by the derivative on the section. This proves (2), including the displayed constant relation.

This proof uses no degree cutoff, determinant expansion, finite root test or conjectural injectivity result.

## Consequences and tightness

The reduced potential at \(b_0\) is exactly
\[
 \psi_\sigma(b_0,R)=Lt_0^2+a_0t_0-\sigma s_0(R). \tag{3}
\]
It need not be constant or have a vanishing residual gradient. Equation (3) matches the arbitrary-repair pivot-translation theorem, rather than recovering the fixed-old-pivot ideal. Every polynomial \(s_0\) is attainable in this origin normalform: compose \(T_0=t_0+\lambda(\tau-s_0(R))\) with any residual polynomial automorphism, choosing \(t_0=(\sigma/\lambda-a_0)/(2L)\).

The restriction of \(\Theta_0\) to the section,
\[
 R\longmapsto\Theta(b_0,s_0(R),R),
\]
is a global polynomial automorphism of \(\mathbb A^2\). Indeed (2) identifies the section with the entire inverse image of the old hyperplane \(t=t_0\); restrict the specialized \(\mathbb A^3\) automorphism and its inverse to those two hyperplanes. This is a further necessary global, rather than local, interface.

For the actual normalized potential, \(c=2(y,2x)\), so \(b_0=(0,0)\). Every admissible general base-fixed pivot-mixing chart must satisfy
\[
 T(x,y,\tau,R)\bmod(x,y)
 =t_0+\lambda(\tau-s_0(R)).
\]
Thus higher new-pivot powers must have coefficients vanishing in \((x,y)\). This does not exclude nonlinear pivot changes supported away from the origin.

## A globally legal class excluded before Hessian computation

For any fixed univariate polynomial \(P\) of degree at least2, the chart
\[
 T=\rho+P(\tau),\qquad
 \Theta_1=\tau,\qquad \Theta_2=\eta
\]
is an actual polynomial automorphism, with inverse
\[
 \tau=R_{0,1},\quad
 \rho=t-P(R_{0,1}),\quad
 \eta=R_{0,2}.
\]
Its determinant is \(-1\). Nevertheless, it cannot admit a global polynomial section at any nonzero constant critical level for the above potential when \(c(b_0)=0\). Its old pivot is nonlinear in \(\tau\) at \(b_0\), contradicting (2).

Equivalently, the unit equation forces \(P'(s_0(R))\) to be a nonzero constant. Since \(P'\) is a nonconstant univariate polynomial, this forces \(s_0\) to be constant; then \(T|_{\tau=s_0}=\rho+P(s_0)\) is nonconstant, contradicting the other unit factor. This independently exposes the failure mechanism.

The degree2/3/4/7 source-inverse fixtures are only exact examples; the exclusion is the all-degree argument just given.

## Essential exclusions

The nonzero level is essential. At \(\sigma=0\), the globally invertible chart
\[
 T=\tau^2+\rho,\qquad \Theta_1=\tau,\qquad\Theta_2=\eta
\]
has \(\tau=0\) as a global polynomial critical section, while \(T|_{\tau=0}=\rho\) is not constant. This counterexample was supplied independently by hessian_check12. Here the critical second derivative can fail to be a constant unit; no nonzero-level conclusion is claimed.

The theorem does not cover base-moving charts, rational or merely local sections, source transformations lacking a polynomial inverse, or nonlinear pivot terms vanishing at all \(c=0\) base points. It does not establish determinant constancy or collision retention for any chart that passes the normalform. These remain independent obligations.

## Prior and next discriminating step

The proof consists of standard polynomial-ring units, monic division and irreducibility of a coordinate. No exact prior normalform statement for this critical-envelope interface has been located; claim this as a programme deduction, not a literature-wide novelty certification.

Downstream use: reject pivot-mixing charts with nonlinear new-pivot dependence surviving on the \(c=0\) fibre before costly determinant expansion. For surviving charts, preserve the section residual automorphism and require either direct target Hessian constancy or the full-source Schur interface separately.

The precise next step is to analyze a globally invertible base-fixed chart whose nonlinear new-pivot terms vanish modulo \((x,y)\), and whose global polynomial critical section exists, then test its actual reduced determinant and collision. The normalform alone does not close that remaining class.

## Separate all-level polynomial-critical-inverse interface

Here no source automorphism or origin hypothesis is needed. Let \(u\) denote any residual variables and \(f(\tau,u)\in\mathbb C[\tau,u]\). Suppose a section is polynomial **jointly** in the variable level and residual coordinates:
\[
 S(\sigma,u)\in\mathbb C[\sigma,u],\qquad
 (\partial_\tau f)(S(\sigma,u),u)=\sigma
\]
as a polynomial identity with \(\sigma\) indeterminate. Then
\[
 f(\tau,u)=\frac{\kappa}{2}\tau^2+A(u)\tau+B(u),
 \qquad\kappa\in\mathbb C^\times.
\]

Proof: put \(P=\partial_\tau f\), work over the integral domain \(\mathbb C[u]\), and take degree in the two univariate indeterminates. Neither \(P\) nor \(S\) is constant in its indeterminate, since their composition is \(\sigma\). The leading coefficient of the composition cannot cancel in a domain, so
\[
 \deg_\tau P\ \deg_\sigma S=1.
\]
Both degrees are1. Write \(P=p_1(u)\tau+p_0(u)\), \(S=s_1(u)\sigma+s_0(u)\); the leading coefficient equation is \(p_1s_1=1\). The units of \(\mathbb C[u]\) are nonzero constants, hence \(p_1=\kappa\), proving the claim by integration. Conversely the displayed quadratic has \(S=(\sigma-A)/\kappa\).

This is a standard univariate composition argument, not a novelty claim. It excludes a genuinely nonquadratic new-pivot potential from the *joint polynomial all-level inverse* subinterface. It does not follow from a section at one selected level, nor does full Hessian determinant constancy by itself assert such a section. A selected-level nonquadratic route and a full-source all-level determinant route must keep these premises separate.

Even individually polynomial sections for **every fixed complex level** are weaker than one joint polynomial section. For \(P(\tau)=\tau^2\), each fixed \(\sigma\) admits a constant-in-\(u\) section after selecting a complex square root, but no polynomial \(S(\sigma,u)\) solves \(S^2=\sigma\). This exact quantifier control was supplied independently by hessian_check12.
