Controlled Sensing for Multihypothesis Testing
Sirin Nitinawarat, Member, IEEE, George Atia, Member, IEEE and Venugopal V . Veeravalli,Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—The problem of multiple hypothesis testing with
observation control is considered in both ﬁxed sample size a nd
sequential settings. In the ﬁxed sample size setting, for bi nary
hypothesis testing, it is shown that the optimal exponent fo r
the maximal error probability corresponds to the maximum
Chernoff information over the choice of controls. It is also shown
that a pure stationary open-loop control policy is asymptot ically
optimal within the larger class of all causal control polici es. For
multihypothesis testing in the ﬁxed sample size setting, lo wer
and upper bounds on the optimal error exponent are derived.
It is also shown through an example with three hypotheses tha t
the optimal causal control policy can be strictly better tha n the
optimal open-loop control policy. In the sequential settin g, a
test based on earlier work by Chernoff for binary hypothesis
testing, is shown to be ﬁrst-order asymptotically optimal f or
multihypothesis testing for vanishing error probabilitie s. The
role of past information and randomization in designing opt imal
control policies is discussed.
Keywords: causal control, Chernoff information, controlled
sensing for inference, detection and estimation theory, de sign
of experiments, error exponent, hypothesis testing, open- loop
control, Markov decision process, Stein’s Lemma
I. I NTRODUCTION
The topic of controlled sensing for inference deals primarily
with adaptively managing and controlling multiple degrees of
freedom in an information-gathering system, ranging from t he
sensing modality to the physical control of sensors, to achi eve
a given inference task. Unlike in traditional control syste ms,
where the control primarily affects the evolution of the sta te,
in controlled sensing control only affects the observation s. In
other words, the goal is not to drive the state to some desired
level, but for the decision-maker to infer the state accurat ely
by shaping the quality of observations.
Some relevant applications of controlled sensing include,
but are by no means limited to, target detection, tracking
and classiﬁcation [1], [2], [3], and other sensor network
applications. Castro et al considered the problem of airborne
laser topographical mapping where the goal is to ﬁnd an
optimal policy for sequential redirection of a laser beam in
order to perform quickest detection of topographical step
changes [4]. Controlled sensing policies were also develop ed
for landmine and underwater mine classiﬁcation in [5]. In

In this paper, we focus on the inference problem of hypoth-
esis testing, and our goal is to ﬁnd an asymptotically optima l
joint-design of a control policy and a decision rule (in addi tion
to a stopping rule for the sequential setting) to decide amon g
the various hypotheses. In particular, we consider a Markovian
model for the simple hypothesis testing of multiple hypotheses
with observation control. Prior to making a decision about t he
hypothesis, the decision-maker can choose among different
actions, which in turn affect the quality of the observation s.
In Section III, we focus on a ﬁxed sample size setting. In
this setting, we further consider two possible control poli cies,
namely, open-loop control and causal control. While in the
former the control is chosen a priori and cannot depend on
the measurements, in the latter, the control can be a functio n
of past measurements and past controls.
For binary hypothesis testing without control, in particular,
with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) obs erva-
tions, the characterization of the optimal exponent of the
maximal error probability (maximized over all hypotheses) in
terms of the Chernoff information is well-known (see, e.g.,
[10]). This exponent is obtained by an optimal equalization
of the two error exponents conditioned on each of the two
hypotheses. The optimal exponent of the maximal error prob-
ability for the general multiple hypothesis testing case wi th
i.i.d. observations is characterized as the minimum of pair wise
Chernoff information across all pairs of hypotheses.
In [11], Tsitsiklis considered multiple hypothesis testin g in
a sensor network based on the decentralized quantization of
independent observations collected at geographically separated
sensors. In the formulation of [11], the choice of a local qua n-
tizer can be regarded as a special type of spatial observation
control at a speciﬁc sensor. Since these spatial control act ions
can affect the observations at the various sensors, but not v ice
versa, the observation control policy is effectively an ope n-
loop control policy. In contrast, our main focus here is on
temporal observation control in which the control at each ti me
can be inﬂuenced by the past observations.
In Section III, we ﬁrst derive a characterization for the
optimal exponent of the maximal error probability for the ca se
of binary hypothesis testing with a ﬁxed sample size. Intere st-
ingly, we show that a pure stationary open-loop control, whe re
the control value at each time is ﬁxed, sufﬁces to achieve the
optimal error exponent among the class of causal controls.
These results are in agreement with the results on asymptoti c
optimality of non-adaptive methods for discrimination of t wo
channels [12]. Then, for general multiple hypothesis testi ng2
with a ﬁxed sample size, we derive a characterization for
the optimal error exponent achievable by open-loop control .
With more than two hypotheses, the characterization of the
optimal error exponent achievable by causal control is a muc h
harder problem. In fact, the structure of the optimal contro l is
not known in general. However, we show through a concrete
example with only three hypotheses that the optimal causal
control policy can be strictly better than the optimal open-
loop control policy. We also derive general lower and upper
bounds for the optimal error exponent achievable by causal
control.
In Section IV, we consider a sequential setup wherein the
controller can adaptively choose to stop taking measuremen ts.
In this case, a sequential test is fully described by a contro l
policy, a stopping rule and a ﬁnal decision rule. In contrast
to the earlier ﬁxed sample size setup in Section III, the
error exponent is no longer a good metric since the expected
stopping time depends on the underlying hypothesis.
In [13], [14], the problem of binary sequential hypothesis
testing without control was considered. Therein, the optimal
expected values of the stopping time are characterized sub-
ject to constraints on the probabilities of error under each
hypothesis. It was shown that the Sequential Probability Ra tio
Test (SPRT) is optimal, i.e., among all tests with the same
power the SPRT requires on average the smallest number of
observations. An extension to the case of multiple hypothes is
testing was considered in [15] where the authors proposed a
Multihypothesis SPRT (or MSPRT) which was later shown
to satisfy certain asymptotic optimality conditions [16], [17],
[18].
The problem of sequential binary hypothesis testing with
observation control was considered in [19] and an asymp-
totically optimal sequential test was presented. While Wal d’s
SPRT is optimal in the sense that it minimizes the values of the
expected stopping times among all tests for which the prob-
abilities of error do not exceed predeﬁned thresholds [14], a
weaker notion of optimality is considered in [19]. Speciﬁca lly,
the proposed test is shown to achieve optimal expected value s
of the stopping time subject to the constraints of vanishing
probabilities of error under each hypothesis. Burnaˇ sev [2 0]
considered the problem of sequential discrimination of mul ti-
ple hypotheses with control of observations under a differe nt
information structure. The latter setting is motivated by a
variable-length coding problem considered by the same auth or
in [21]. There, the encoder (ﬁrst decision-maker) chooses a
channel input (control action) based on the knowledge of the
intended message (equivalently the underlying hypothesis ) to
improve the decoding performance at the decoder (second
decision-maker). It is important to note that the controlle d
sensing problem that we consider is fundamentally differen t
from Burnaˇ sev’s problem. Unlike [20], where the control
actions are functions of the underlying hypothesis, in [19] and
the setting we consider herein the control actions cannot be
function of the unknown hypothesis. In that sense, the varia ble
length channel coding problem and the problem considered
in [20] have a simpler structure since the controller knows
the underlying hypothesis. This knowledge simpliﬁes both t he
optimization of control policies as well as their performan ce
analysis. When the hypothesis is unknown to the controller,
as in the controlled sensing problem considered herein, the
controller has to base its control actions on estimates of th e
unknown hypothesis. Consequently, a different and new set o f
techniques is needed for establishing the optimality of con trol
schemes and analyzing their performance.
A Bayesian version of this sequential problem (with obser-
vation control) was considered by the authors in [22] in the
non-asymptotic regime. Since the optimal policy is general ly
difﬁcult to characterize, certain conditions (Blackwell ordering
[23]) were identiﬁed under which the optimal control is an
open-loop control. The main focus of [22], [24] has been on
trying to solve the underlying dynamic program and ﬁnding
the structure of optimal solutions, a task that is futile exc ept
in some special cases. In contrast, our work mostly focuses
on performance analysis and on establishing asymptotic opt i-
mality of proposed control policies.
In Section IV, we extend the result in [19] to the case
of multiple hypothesis testing. In particular, we present a n
asymptotically optimal sequential test and derive its asym p-
totic performance in terms of the optimal tradeoff between
the maximal error probability and the expected sample size
required for decision making.
At a fundamental level, this paper brings a combination
of tools from stochastic control and information theory to
bear on the controlled sensing problem for hypothesis testi ng.
While stochastic control provides insights into the struct ure
of the optimal decision rules and the corresponding sufﬁcie nt
statistics, the main insight for designing efﬁcient contro l laws
is based on information-theoretic considerations. To clar ify,
note that in traditional inference problems (without observation
control) information-theoretic quantities play a key role in per-
formance. For example, as pointed out earlier, the optimal error
exponent for the hypothesis testing problem is characteriz ed
in terms of Chernoff information for the ﬁxed sample size
setting [10]. Similarly, the minimum Kullback-Leibler (KL )
divergence governs the optimal expected stopping time for s e-
quential hypothesis testing without control [15]. This sug gests
that such information-theoretic measures can be used to design
efﬁcient observation control policies. A key contribution of
this paper is to show that such policies are also asymptotica lly
optimal in many settings.
The main technical contributions of this paper can be
summarized as follows.
First, in the ﬁxed sample size setting,
• we characterize the optimal error exponents achievable by
open-loop and causal control for the binary hypothesis test ing
case. In particular, we show that a pure stationary open-loo p
control policy is asymptotically optimal within the larger class
of all causal control policies, and, hence, the two optimal e rror
exponents coincide.
• For the case of multiple hypothesis testing, we derive a
characterization for the optimal error exponent achievabl e by
open-loop control which also serves as a lower bound for the
optimal error exponent achievable by causal control.
• We derive precise lower and upper bounds for the opti-
mal error exponent achievable by causal control for general
multihypothesis testing and propose a causal control polic y3
which chooses the control value that maximizes the minimum
Chernoff information of the distributions corresponding t o the
most likely hypothesis and all the alternative hypotheses.Then,
we provide an example with three hypotheses in which the
proposed policy strictly outperforms the best open-loop control
policy. Interestingly, for this example, the error exponen t
achieved by our proposed policy coincides with the error
exponent computed from our general lower bound.
Second, in the sequential setting,
• we propose a sequential mutihypothesis test, then we
establish that it is asymptotically optimal and characteri ze
its asymptotic performance. The proposed control policy is
obtained by maximizing the KL divergence of the distributions
corresponding to the most likely hypothesis and the nearest
alternative hypothesis. The proposed sequential test is th en
shown to achieve the asymptotically optimal expected sampl e
sizes under all hypotheses simultaneously. In other words, any
other sequential test with a vanishing maximal probability of
error must incur an asymptotically larger expected sample s ize
under every hypothesis.
Our results clearly emphasize the roles of past information
and randomization (in control) for decision making in the
ﬁxed sample size and the sequential settings. In particular ,
it is shown that past information is useful in the ﬁxed sample
size setting only when there are more than two hypotheses,
while it is indispensable in the sequential setting even wit h
two hypotheses. On the other hand, randomization is always
superﬂuous in attaining the optimal error exponent in the
ﬁxed sample size setting, while it is always essential in the
sequential setting except for the binary hypotheses testing case.

sumptions that will be adopted throughout the paper. Our
problem formulations and results for the ﬁxed sample size
setting and the sequential setting are given in Section III a nd
IV, respectively. Examples for both settings are provided i n
Section V. A discussion is provided in Section VI, and a
conclusion is given in Section VII. All proofs are relegated
to the appendices.

VII. C ONCLUSION
We studied multiple hypothesis testing with observation
control. The structure of the optimal controller under vari ous
asymptotic regimes was studied. We showed that the two-
pronged approach combining tools from stochastic control and
information theory can lead to efﬁcient solutions. First, i n a
setup with a ﬁxed sample size, we showed that the optimal
exponent for the maximal error probability corresponds to t he
maximum Chernoff information over the choice of controls
for binary hypothesis testing. Our result showed that a pure
stationary open-loop control policy is asymptotically opt imal
even among the broader class of causal control policies. For
the case of multiple hypothesis testing, we characterized t he
optimal error exponent achievable by open-loop control and
derived precise lower and upper bounds for the optimal error
exponent achievable by causal control. We also proposed a
causal control policy for multihypothesis testing based on
maximizing the minimum Chernoff information of the dis-
tributions corresponding to the most likely hypothesis and
all the alternative hypotheses. We illustrated in an exampl e
with three hypotheses that the proposed causal control poli cy
strictly outperforms the best open-loop control policy and
achieves the same error exponent obtained from our derived
lower bound. Second, a sequential setup was considered
wherein the controller can also decide when to stop taking
observations. In this case, the objective is to minimize the
expected stopping time subject to the constraints of vanish ing
error probabilities under each hypothesis. We proposed a
sequential test for testing multiple hypotheses and showed that
it is asymptotically optimal. The proposed control policy i s
obtained by maximizing the KL divergence of the distributions
corresponding to the most likely hypothesis and the nearest
alternative hypothesis. In summary, we showed that using10
information-theoretic metrics to design observation cont rol
policies can lead to provably asymptotically optimal solut ions
for the ﬁxed sample size and the sequential settings.
For binary hypothesis testing, our ﬁndings showed that past
information is crucial in achieving the asymptotically opt imal
performance in the sequential setting, while it is superﬂuo us
for achieving the optimal error exponent in the ﬁxed sample
size setting. Our results also showed that for general multi ple
hypothesis testing, randomization in control is always superﬂu-
ous (for any number of hypotheses) in achieving the optimal
error exponent in the ﬁxed sample size setting, while it is
crucial in achieving the asymptotically optimal performance in
the sequential setting except for the case of binary hypothe sis
testing.
In our problem formulation, the criterion used to select
a good control policy is based solely on its efﬁciency for
hypothesis testing. In a future study, we would like to chara c-
terize optimal control policies under non-uniform control cost,
i.e., control policies that optimize a joint criteria combi ning
detection performance and non-uniform costs for the variou s
actions.
Our results rely on the assumption that the probabilis-
tic model of controlled observations is fully known to the
decision-maker. A natural question arises as to how one should
perform controlled sensing for hypothesis testing without such
knowledge. The key challenge in this harder setup is how to
exploit control to learn the characteristics of the model th at
are most crucial for testing multiple hypotheses.
Another avenue for ongoing and future research seeks to
explore whether the two-pronged approach can be extended
to other controlled sensing problems such as quickest chang e
detection, parameter estimation, and learning-based clas siﬁca-
tion problems.
