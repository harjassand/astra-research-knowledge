# All history channel verification for a closed finite controller

16 October 2026. Root construction, independently derived during this campaign. The ingredients are finite dimensional invariant-space reachability and Knill–Laflamme error correction. Historical novelty is unestablished. This is an exact local verification operation for a supplied controller, not a construction of the missing autonomous controller or a threshold theorem.

## The operation

Let Q be the complete finite set of classical controller states, including all live request, commit, recycle and observable flags. Let the quantum working space have dimension D, including every live data, ancilla and boundary port used before the accepting cut. The logical input has dimension k and enters through a specified isometry V. Every transition e:q→r has specified Kraus matrices K_(e,a). Quantum noise may be represented by a matrix basis for its allowed local error space. Quantum controlled predicates must be included as Kraus projectors, not treated as a classical guard unless they are classical registers in Q.

An optional primitive fault budget b∈{0,…,f} augments Q. A primitive fault edge increases b by its declared cost. Persistent control damage creates zero additional fault cost on its subsequent ideal transitions; its whole downstream effect must therefore remain in the graph. Terminals are first-accept states: an execution stops at the named EC or output cut, so accepting vertices have no outgoing edge in this verification problem.

For every augmented vertex x, compute the least linear spaces L_x⊂Mat_(D×k) satisfying V∈L_start and K_(e,a)L_x⊂L_y on every allowed edge x→y. Initialize with span{V}; repeatedly add independent edge images to successor bases. The sum of basis dimensions never exceeds |Q|(f+1)Dk. At a fixed point, every finite path map K_path V lies in its endpoint space. Conversely every basis element is a linear combination of actual finite path maps. The proof is induction on path length in one direction and on basis additions in the other. Thus the fixed point equals the complete path-map span, irrespective of loop length, noncommutativity or repeated register use.

This is exact arithmetic over a computable field containing all gate entries. Dense runtime is polynomial in |Q|, f, D, k and the transition count when field operations have unit cost; bit complexity and algebraic-number degree must be charged separately. D and |Q| generally grow exponentially in the physical block size. No scalable efficiency claim follows from the dimension bound.

## Contract with a prescribed decoder

For each trusted terminal flag z let D_(z,r):C^D→C^k be the Kraus matrices of the actual required CPTP decoder. Let U be the intended logical unitary. If the flag is erased or faulty, terminals sharing the same observable flag must use the same decoder and be combined. Fault budget b is a ghost variable, not an observable flag.

The terminating branches have the reference-uniform contract

    Dec_z(A ρ A†) = c_A UρU†

for every logical ρ and every finite terminal path map A if and only if, for every matrix A in a basis of the combined accepting span and every decoder Kraus matrix,

    D_(z,r) A ∈ span{U}.

Sufficiency follows by writing D_(z,r)A=α_(r,A)U. Necessity follows because the Choi matrix of a scalar unitary subchannel has rank one: every Kraus vector of that subchannel is proportional to vec(U). Linearity extends the check from path maps to their saturated span. Tensoring the identities with an untouched reference proves the full channel assertion. The scalar c_A is independent of the logical state. The terminal map is a trace-nonincreasing subchannel until a separate service or acceptance theorem establishes its weight. If the complete accepting instrument is trace preserving, the sum of the c_A weights is one and the decoded channel equals Ad_U.

General intended nonunitary channels require a Choi-face or superoperator test instead of the rank-one criterion. This note does not silently extend the unitary test to arbitrary channels.

## Contract for existence of a decoder

If a decoder may be chosen rather than being prescribed, one common exact recovery exists precisely when

    A†B = λ_(A,B) I_k

for every pair of accepting-span basis matrices in the same trusted-flag class. This is the Knill–Laflamme condition for rectangular encoded errors. The finite Gram test checks all histories, since it is sesquilinear. A recovery to logical identity can be postcomposed with U. This existence test is weaker than the actual controller contract: it cannot certify a specified EC gadget without the prescribed-decoder check.

An accepting state containing both V and VU_L for a nonscalar logical unitary U_L fails immediately: V†VU_L=U_L is nonscalar. An unrecorded logical skip, repeat or reorder cannot be removed by merely naming it a gauge operation. Keeping a trustworthy distinguishing flag can permit different decoders; erasing that flag combines the spaces and restores the obstruction. The reliability and resource cost of the flag are separate obligations.

## What this can remove

For a closed finite candidate, bounded path enumeration is unnecessary for its local channel-safety claim. Saturation either proves safety for all finite accepted histories, or produces path-map provenance for a failing Gram or prescribed-decoder condition. This can expose a zero-cost persistent-fault loop that a bounded search misses. In a code-correctable output region, all orders and repetitions of local operations can remain correctable even when there is no bounded gate-edit count.

The supplied graph is the receiving assumption. An isolated graph with hidden live ports, omitted exterior-dependent predicates, omitted idle noise or idealized reset is not a certificate for the physical controller. A nonterminating execution contributes no accepting path: service, protected idling, and physical-time completion must still be proved. Infinite-dimensional baths and growing history tapes fall outside the finite D model unless a valid finite interface theorem is supplied.

## Receiving obligations for a hierarchy

1. Derive Q and every Kraus edge from actual finite local registers and transitions, including boundary-crossing operations and the entire recycled lifetime.
2. Specify the real decoder and a bounded quantum output footprint for each fault provenance seed. A primitive fault must not get counted anew every time it later acts.
3. Establish physical-time service and idle exposure with the existing stopped-exposure theorem only when its predictable labels, tilted-law service and whole-certificate conflict bounds hold.
4. Prove multi-seed damage/footprint composition, exRec overlap, seam compatibility, constant alphabet/support and self-simulation at every level. An all-history one-block certificate proves none of these automatically.

## Closest predecessors

- Knill and Laflamme, A Theory of Quantum Error-Correcting Codes, https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9604034 and Phys. Rev. A 55, 900 (1997): owns the exact recovery Gram criterion and reference-uniform correction.
- Feng, Yu and Ying, Model checking quantum Markov chains, https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2187 : finite classical execution locations with quantum superoperator transitions and fixed-point verification precede this construction.
- Quantum reachability algorithms, including QReach, already use finite-dimensional reachable invariant spaces. The present campaign contribution is the explicit receiving test for accepted asynchronous controller histories and the distinction between chosen and prescribed recovery. No foundational novelty claim is made for the linear-algebra ingredients.
