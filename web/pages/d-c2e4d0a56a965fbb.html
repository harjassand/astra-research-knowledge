N81 all-mode additive noise: conditioned positive-P comparison
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: internally proved candidate; independent review and N81 compiler replay pending

Question
========

Does fixed inverse-polynomial isotropic displacement noise on every N81 detector
mode preserve the encoded two-mode conditional count law after the rare binary
herald? The generic global trace-distance argument divides by the rare herald
probability and is useless here. The proof below instead compares the positive-P
latent Gaussian distributions *after herald conditioning*. It shows that, for
this N81 family, the noisy conditional retained law converges to vacuum.

The conclusion concerns the supplied N81 compiler family and the specified
noise placement (after the passive network, before uniform loss). It does not
establish an efficient sampler for arbitrary noisy Gaussian inputs, nor an
unconditional complexity separation. N81/N82 themselves remain inherited
conditional proof candidates.

Setup and exact covariance
==========================

Let d=Theta(R) be the total detector-mode count, B=B^T the rational centered
pure-Gaussian matrix at the detector plane, and ||B||<=g<1/4. N81/N82 give
g=2^(-ell), ell>=ceil((q+10)/2), q>=R ceil(log2 R), so in particular ell>=R
for large R. The exact quadrature covariance V_B of this pure state has ordinary
eigenvalues

    (1/2)(1+beta_j)/(1-beta_j),  (1/2)(1-beta_j)/(1+beta_j),

where beta_j are the Takagi singular values of B (with the usual multiplicities).
Consequently, for Delta=V_B-I/2,

    ||Delta|| <= g/(1-g).

Add independent classical Gaussian displacement noise of per-quadrature variance
nu=R^(-a), for any fixed integer a>=1, on all d detector modes, then uniform pure loss
eta=1/R. Since nu>g/(1-g) for all sufficiently large R, the noisy pre-loss
state has a strictly positive Gaussian P density, with quadrature covariance

    Sigma_P = V_B + nu I - I/2 = nu I + Delta.

Pure loss scales coherent amplitudes by sqrt(eta), so its output P covariance is

    C = eta Sigma_P = t(I+E),
    t=eta nu=R^(-(a+1)),  E=Delta/nu,
    ||E|| <= delta := g/((1-g)nu).

Thus the whole output photon-count law is a classical mixture of independent
Poisson laws, with its latent coherent quadratures distributed as a centered
Gaussian. The reference input B=0 under the same noise and loss has latent law
N(0,t I), i.e. a product thermal state of mean t per mode. This is an exact
covariance comparison, not a perturbation around the ideal herald branch.

Herald and posterior comparison
===============================

Let H be the herald-mode set, with target k_j in {0,1}; write h=#ones and
h0=#zeros. For the noise-only reference, the exact herald mass is

    p_ref = [t/(1+t)^2]^h [1/(1+t)]^h0
          = t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0).

Standardize the reference latent quadratures by y=x/sqrt(t), so y~N(0,I_{2d})
before conditioning. Conditional on the PNR event, the reference latent law mu0
factorizes over modes. For an unheralded mode, ||y_j||^2 is Gamma(shape 1,
scale 2); for a heralded mode with target k_j it is Gamma(shape 1+k_j,
scale 2/(1+t)). Therefore, for 0<=theta<1/2,

    E_mu0 exp(theta ||y||^2)
       = (1-2theta)^(-(d-|H|))
         (1-2theta/(1+t))^-(|H|+h).

In particular E_mu0||y||^2<=2(d+h)<=4d, and for theta<=1/8 the log mgf
is at most C d theta for an absolute constant C, since h<=|H|<=d.

The actual standardized latent *prior* is N(0,I+E). Its likelihood ratio to
the reference prior is

    r(y)=det(I+E)^(-1/2)
         exp( (1/2) y^T [I-(I+E)^(-1)] y ).

For delta=||E||<=1/4, the determinant and quadratic-form bounds give

    |log r(y)| <= C delta (2d + ||y||^2).

The PNR event likelihood is the same nonnegative function of y under both
priors. Hence the actual conditional latent law mu_E has density

    d mu_E / d mu0 = r/Z,  Z=E_mu0 r,

and the actual herald mass is p_act=p_ref Z. The displayed gamma mgf and the
bound on log r give, for sufficiently small delta,

    E_mu0[r^2]
      <= exp(C d delta) E_mu0 exp(C delta ||y||^2)
      <= exp(C' d delta),

because h<=d and the total gamma shape is d+h<=2d. Jensen gives

    Z=E_mu0[r] >= exp(E_mu0 log r) >= exp(-C' d delta),

and Cauchy-Schwarz gives Z<=sqrt(E_mu0[r^2])<=exp(C' d delta). Thus

    exp(-C' d delta) <= Z <= exp(C' d delta),
    E_mu0[r^2] <= exp(C' d delta).

Whenever d delta<=c for a sufficiently small absolute c, this yields

    chi^2(mu_E || mu0) = E_mu0[r^2]/Z^2 - 1
                       <= exp(C d delta)-1,
    TV(mu_E,mu0) <= (1/2) sqrt(chi^2) = O(sqrt(d delta)).

Photon counting on unheralded modes is a common Markov kernel applied to these
conditional latent laws. Data processing therefore gives the same TV bound for
the complete unheralded count record, and in particular for the retained two
readout modes. This is the conditioning step: no factor 1/p_ref appears.

N81 asymptotics and resources
============================

Here d=Theta(R), h,h0<=d, eta=1/R, nu=R^(-a), and g<=2^(-ell) with ell>=R.
Thus

    delta <= (4/3) R^a 2^(-R),
    d delta = O(R^(a+1) 2^(-R)) -> 0.

The retained two-mode law is within

    O(sqrt(R^(a+1) 2^(-R))) + 2t

of two-mode vacuum in TV. The second term is the reference thermal-to-vacuum
distance, since two independent thermal modes of mean t are nonvacuum with
probability 1-(1+t)^(-2)<=2t. For a fixed subset U of s unheralded modes,
E_mu0||Y_U||^4=4s(s+1)=O(s^2), since
its radial variables are independent Gamma(shape 1, scale 2). Cauchy-Schwarz,
E_mu0[r^2]=exp(O(d delta)), and Z=exp(O(d delta)) give
E_muE||Y_U||^2=O(s), hence conditional mean count O(ts). For all O(R)
unheralded modes this is O(Rt)=O(nu), and for the retained two modes it is O(t).

The herald mass is not merely lower-bounded by its vacuum block: it satisfies
p_act=p_ref(1+O(d delta)). Its logarithmic inverse is

    log(1/p_ref)=h log(1/t)+(2h+h0)log(1+t)=O(R log R).

The added input energy is d nu=O(R^(1-a)) (plus the original O(d g^2)); the
noise covariance is rational with O(log R) bits per entry for fixed integer a,
the N81 rational B description is unchanged, and the sampler receives numerical
d,h,1/eta=R, energy and log(1/p_act) all polynomial in R. No physical repeat-
until-herald experiment is invoked. The result is proved for every fixed integer a>=1 (the analytic estimates hold
for any fixed a>0 if the noise covariance is represented exactly); it does not
claim uniformity when a grows with R.

Why trace-to-vacuum does not prove this
=======================================

The exact pure-source distance from vacuum is

    lambda_eff = sqrt(1 - det(I-BB*)^(1/2))
                <= g sqrt(d/[2(1-g^2)]).

Dividing by the reference herald probability gives only

    log2(lambda_eff/p_ref)
      <= -ell + (1/2)log2 d + O(1)
         +(a+1)h log2 R +(2h+h0)log2(1+t).

The final term is O(Rt)=o(1), but if h is linear in R the displayed bounds do
not force this ratio to zero. Thus global trace closeness to vacuum is not the
proof. The positive-P likelihood ratio controls the *conditional posterior*
directly and avoids rare-event normalization.

Interpretation and limits
=========================

N81's ideal conditional readout has an encoded constant acceptance gap. Under
the specified all-mode inverse-polynomial additive noise, the same compiled
family has retained conditional count law TV-o(1) from vacuum, so its encoded
constant-TV amplification is erased; subleading probabilities may still encode
information. This invalidates transfer of N81's PP=BPP implication to this
all-mode-noisy version of its instances. It does not establish PP!=BPP, does
not imply noisy Gaussian conditional sampling is easy on arbitrary inputs, and
does not refute the no-noise N81 conditional reduction. A general sampler may
have other hard noisy inputs. N81/N82 compiler and source claims still need
independent review.
