# An efficient two-level conditionally linear sampler with exponentially small gap

Status: exact internally derived construction. This is an obstruction to
inferring a polynomial question-chain gap from the conditionally linear
sampler interface alone. It is not a lower bound on the true repetition
rate of any particular game, or a counterexample to the threshold theorem.

## Source interface

Junqiao Lin, *MIPco=coRE*, arXiv2510.07162v1, 2025-10-08,
Definitions 5.1 and 5.5: a k-level conditionally linear function chooses
its next register's linear map as a function of its own previous output.
A CL question law draws one uniform seed and gives Alice and Bob the
outputs of two such functions using the same register partition.
The definitions and exact recursive formula were read in the primary
snapshot `sources/frontier_check_repetition/cycle19/prior/lin2510.07162v1.html`.
The whole paper and its compression proof were not audited.

This recent audit's spectral theorem yields polynomial repetition cost
for a verifier family only if its question-chain gap is polynomially
bounded below. The source's efficient CL sampler does not by itself
provide that promise.

## Exact sampler and membership in the source class

Let p be any positive odd integer and q=2^p. Use two canonical field
registers over F_q and a uniform seed (r,u) in F_q times F_q. Output

    Alice: X=(0,u),
    Bob:   Y=(r,u) if r!=0, and Y=(0,0) if r=0.              (1)

Alice's zeroth-level linear map is zero and her next-level map is the
identity. Bob's zeroth-level map is the identity; his next-level map is
the identity when his previous output r is nonzero and the zero linear
map when r=0. This is precisely a two-level CL pair. Selection uses the
previous OUTPUT, as Definition 5.1 requires, not an inaccessible raw
seed. Both the identity and zero choices are linear.

The description has O(p) bits under a standard uniform implementation;
sampling uses 2p uniform bits, a zero test and copies. Question lengths
are O(p). No field multiplication is needed. Its support has q^2 pairs,
all of mass q^(-2). This is a succinct sampler, not an explicit q^2-entry
table of constant total description length.

Discard the fixed zero prefix of Alice's input. Let the special Bob
question be bot=(0,0). The support pairs are (u,bot), for every u, and
(u,(r,u)), for r!=0. All support pairs are connected because bot joins
all Alice questions. The bipartite support graph is a tree: one bot hub,
q Alice vertices and q(q-1) Bob leaves. No component drift occurs.

## Exact spectral gap

Given X=u, Bob reveals u together with a uniform nonzero r with
probability 1-1/q and outputs bot with probability 1/q. Given a
non-special Bob question, X is known exactly; given bot, X is uniform.
Put rho=sqrt(1-1/q).

For any centered unit-norm f on uniform F_q, define local functions
on the question pairs

    a_f(u,r)=f(u),
    b_f(u,r)=1_(r!=0) f(u)/rho.

b_f is a function of Bob's local input: bot receives zero and every
nonzero-r input includes u. These vectors have norm one and inner
product rho. Their conditional means satisfy

    P_X a_f=a_f,       P_Y a_f=rho b_f,
    P_X b_f=rho a_f,   P_Y b_f=b_f.

Thus, for P=(P_X+P_Y)/2,

    P(a_f+b_f)=((1+rho)/2)(a_f+b_f),
    P(a_f-b_f)=((1-rho)/2)(a_f-b_f).                         (2)

Centered Alice-local functions have dimension q-1. Centered Bob-local
functions have dimension q(q-1). On the Bob-local subspace orthogonal
to the b_f vectors, P_X is zero and P_Y is the identity, giving eigenvalue
1/2 with multiplicity (q-1)^2. The two local subspaces span all q^2
pair functions: choose the Alice function to fit the bot edge values and
the Bob leaf values to supply all remaining residuals. Their intersection
is the constants by connectedness. Therefore (2), that 1/2 eigenspace
and the constant eigenvector exhaust the dimension:

    1 + 2(q-1) + (q-1)^2 = q^2.

The largest nonconstant eigenvalue is (1+rho)/2. Hence the exact
normalized gap used by the threshold theorem is

    sigma_q=(1-sqrt(1-1/q))/2
           =1/[2q(1+sqrt(1-1/q))]
           ~1/(4q)=2^(-p)/4.                              (3)

This also exactly identifies the maximal local correlation as rho.
The same eigenvectors attain the Hilbert-valued Poincare ratio if their
anchors are their exact conditional means. A polynomially computable
CL sampler can therefore have exponentially small sigma in its seed and
question bit lengths.

## Consequence, prior art and next test

The uniform-in-dimension spectral rate remains valid for (1), but its
certified amplification cost is exponential in p at fixed gap delta.
It cannot justify removing anchoring efficiently from EVERY CL verifier
family. Specific Pauli/introspection/compression sampler families may
have much better gaps; that requires a separate derivation for their
actual laws. The example does not imply poor true game repetition,
because it supplies no predicate or game-specific lower bound.

Erasure channels, maximal correlation and the spectrum of two conditional
projections are standard mechanisms. Historical novelty of this exact
embedding into the CL interface has not been established. The scientific
contribution here is the explicit obstruction to a tempting downstream
inference from efficient CL sampling to efficient unanchored spectral
amplification. The input, support and spectral cost are all charged.

By contrast, the standard independent 1/2 anchoring transformation has
gap at least 1/4 regardless of mu. Frontier_functional independently
derives the resulting cubic anchored commuting bound, improving the
gap exponent in the exact checked Lin Theorem 9.3/A.1 statement. That
transformed-game consequence is consistent with (3).
