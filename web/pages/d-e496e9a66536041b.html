EXACT STATEMENT

Let rho_N be any permutation-invariant N-qubit density operator with exact
maximally mixed Schur-Weyl weights:

 rho_N=direct_sum_j p_j rho_j tensor I_(m_j)/m_j,
 p_j=(2j+1)m_j/2^N,
 m_j=binomial(N,N/2-j)-binomial(N,N/2-j-1),
 Tr rho_j=1.

Let tau=rho_1=(I+b.sigma)/2, t=|b|, sigma_r=tau^tensorr, q=r/N, and define
the Petz weighted chi-square quantity

 chi_P^2(rho_r||sigma_r)
 =Tr[(rho_r-sigma_r)^2 sigma_r^(-1)]
 =Tr[rho_r^2 sigma_r^(-1)]-1.

The inverse exists in the range below. For 1<=r<=N/128,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||tau^tensorr) <=34992(r/N)^2,              (1)
 D_rel(rho_r||tau^tensorr) <=34992(r/N)^2,                (2)

where D_rel uses natural logarithms. In particular, these uniform estimates
apply eventually to every sublinear sequence r_N=o(N), with one universal
constant. At r=1 both quantities are identically zero.

A more general explicit bound is available. Whenever t<1 and
x=4(r/N)/(1-t)<1/2,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||tau^tensorr)
 <=108x[(1+3x)/(1-2x)^(7/2)-1].                         (3)

The subtraction in (3) is exact: all degree-one coefficients vanish because
tau is the actual one-body state. There is no uncharged trace-to-weighted-
norm conversion or exponential factor in the number of observed qubits.

PROOF

1. Sector weights imply a small one-body Bloch vector.

The white sector weights give <J^2>=3N/4. Permutation invariance gives
b=2<J>/N, so

 t^2<=4<J^2>/N^2=3/N.                                  (4)

If r>=2 and r<=N/128, N>=256. Consequently t<=sqrt(3/256)<1/9 and
1/(1-t)<=9/8. Thus x<=9q/2<=9/256 in that range, and tau is strictly positive.
The r=1 case needs no estimate, since rho_1=tau.

2. The local centered Pauli Gram matrix controls the weighted norm.

Put A_a=sigma_a-b_a I for a in {x,y,z}. In the operator Hilbert space with
inner product <U,V>_tau=Tr(tau U^dagger V), I is orthogonal to all A_a, and
the centered Gram matrix is

 G_(ab)=Tr(tau A_a A_b)
       =delta_ab-b_a b_b+i sum_c epsilon_(abc)b_c.

After a common rotation aligning b with z, its eigenvalues are
1-t, 1+t, 1-t^2. Hence

 lambda_min(G)>=1-t.                                   (5)

This complex Hermitian Gram matrix is used directly; it is not replaced
by an invalid real covariance inverse when rho_r and tau^tensorr fail to commute.

For a support S of size k and axis sequence a=(a_1,...,a_k), define
d_a=Tr[rho_N A_(a_1)^(1)...A_(a_k)^(k)]. Permutation invariance makes this
a real symmetric order-k tensor, independent of the selected k sites. Put
T_k^cent=sum_a d_a^2. One-body centering gives d_a=0 when k=1.

Products of I and A_a form a basis of the r-site operator space. Different
supports are orthogonal under tau^tensorr, and the Gram matrix on a fixed
size-k support is G^tensork. Let delta=rho_r-sigma_r and R=delta sigma_r^(-1).
Then

 <R,R>_(sigma_r)=Tr(delta^2 sigma_r^(-1))=chi_P^2,
 <A_S,R>_(sigma_r)=Tr(A_S^dagger delta).

For nonempty S the reference sigma_r has zero centered coefficient, so
the last coefficients are d_a. The constant coefficient is zero because
Tr delta=0. The exact Gram-matrix Parseval formula and (5) give

 chi_P^2
 =sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) d_k^dagger (G^(-1))^tensork d_k
 <=sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k)(1-t)^(-k) T_k^cent.          (6)

Equation (6) proves the precise weighted-norm cancellation, including
noncommuting states. It does not merely remove a first-order term from a
trace-norm bound and assume that its error has a small weighted norm.

3. Bound the white Hilbert-Schmidt norm of shifted collective polynomials.

Write omega_N=I/2^N. For a real unit vector u put Q_i=u.sigma^(i),
beta=u.b, and

 B_l(u)=binomial(N,l)^(-1) sum_(|S|=l) product_(i in S) Q_i,
 B_k^cent(u)=binomial(N,k)^(-1)
             sum_(|S|=k) product_(i in S)(Q_i-beta I).

The elementary expansion is exactly

 B_k^cent(u)=sum_(l=0)^k binomial(k,l)(-beta)^(k-l) B_l(u). (7)

The unshifted B_l of distinct degrees are orthogonal under omega_N, and
Tr(omega_N B_l^2)=1/binomial(N,l). Therefore

 Tr(omega_N (B_k^cent)^2)
 =sum_(l=0)^k binomial(k,l)^2 beta^(2(k-l))/binomial(N,l). (8)

Set m=k-l. By beta^2<=3/N and
binomial(N,k)/binomial(N,l)<=(N^(k-l) l!)/k!, each term in (8), multiplied
by binomial(N,k), is at most

 binomial(k,m) 3^m/m!.

Its sum is at most sum_m binomial(k,m)3^m=4^k, so

 Tr(omega_N (B_k^cent)^2)<=4^k/binomial(N,k).             (9)

4. Transfer the shifted polynomial norm through exact sector weights.

For E_l=binomial(N,l)B_l and S=sum_i Q_i=2J_u,

 S E_l=(l+1)E_(l+1)+(N-l+1)E_(l-1), E_0=I, E_1=S.

Thus every B_l and B_k^cent is a real polynomial of degree at most k in
J_u. In a spin-j sector its eigenvalues are evaluations on the grid
{-j,-j+1,...,j} of that polynomial.

The following uniform polynomial-grid lemma is the same elementary lemma
proved independently in this agent's earlier chi-square report:

 max_(m in grid) P(m)^2
 <=36k^2 [sum_(m in grid)P(m)^2/(2j+1)].                 (10)

For completeness, if j<4k^2, the factor 2j+1<=9k^2 is enough. Otherwise
Markov's derivative inequality on [-j,j] gives ||P'||_infty<=k^2||P||_infty/j.
On an interval of length at least j/(2k^2) around a maximum, |P| is at least
half its maximum. There are at least j/(4k^2) grid points there, giving (10)
since (2j+1)/j<=9/4.

PRIMARY INGREDIENT: Markov's derivative inequality is checked in Lawrence
A. Harris, "A proof of Markov's theorem for polynomials on Banach spaces,"
Theorem 1, author PDF page 3, restricted to X=Y=R and first derivative.
It assumes a real polynomial of degree <=k on [-1,1] and gives the bound
k^2 times its uniform norm. Source:
https://www.ms.uky.edu/~larry/paper.dir/proof.pdf

With p_j exactly the white weights, (10), weighted Cauchy-Schwarz, and (9)
give, for arbitrary within-sector density operators rho_j,

 |Tr(rho_N B_k^cent(u))|^2
 <=sum_j p_j ||(B_k^cent)_j||^2
 <=36k^2 Tr(omega_N (B_k^cent)^2)
 <=36k^2 4^k/binomial(N,k).                             (11)

This is the only structural use of the exact white Schur weights.

5. Recover the entire centered tensor, then sum degrees >=2.

The homogeneous polynomial f_k(u)=sum_a d_a u_(a_1)...u_(a_k) is exactly
Tr(rho_N B_k^cent(u)). For uniform u on S^2, spherical moment pairings give

 E f_k(u)^2 >= [k!/(2k+1)!!] T_k^cent.

Indeed the k! fully cross pairings contribute the tensor's squared
Frobenius norm. Every other pairing contributes the squared Frobenius norm
of an iterated tensor trace, hence is nonnegative. This remains valid for
the centered tensor, with no tracelessness or isotropy assumption.

By (11),

 T_k^cent<=36k^2 4^k(2k+1)!!/[k! binomial(N,k)].          (12)

Use (12) in (6), binomial(r,k)/binomial(N,k)<=q^k, and
x=4q/(1-t). For x<1/2,

 chi_P^2<=36 sum_(k=2)^infinity k^2 (2k+1)!! x^k/k!.

The full generating function is (1-2x)^(-3/2). Applying (x d/dx)^2 and
subtracting its k=1 term, 3x, proves (3) exactly.

6. A uniform numerical constant without rounding.

Put g(x)=(1+3x)/(1-2x)^(7/2). Its derivative is

 g'(x)=(10+15x)/(1-2x)^(9/2).

For x<=9/256,

 g'(x)<=(10+135/256)(128/119)^5
       =7381975040/487010951 <16.

Since g(0)=1, (3) gives chi_P^2<=1728x^2. In the stated range x<=9q/2,
so chi_P^2<=1728(9/2)^2 q^2=34992q^2. This proves (1).

7. Relative entropy, including the noncommuting case.

Let rho_r have eigenvalues lambda_i and eigenvectors |i>, and sigma_r have
positive eigenvalues mu_j and eigenvectors |j>. The weights
lambda_i |<i|j>|^2 form a probability distribution. Jensen's inequality gives

 D_rel(rho_r||sigma_r)
 =sum_(i,j)lambda_i |<i|j>|^2 log(lambda_i/mu_j)
 <=log sum_(i,j)lambda_i^2 |<i|j>|^2/mu_j
 =log Tr(rho_r^2 sigma_r^(-1))
 =log(1+chi_P^2)<=chi_P^2.

Terms with lambda_i=0 contribute zero. This proves (2) and checks the
noncommutative entropy step explicitly.

FRESH-PREPARATION CONSEQUENCE, WITH ITS NECESSARY NULL-CLASS QUALIFIER

For m independent fresh preparations, each exposing only its r-site
marginal, Petz chi-square multiplicativity gives

 1+chi_P^2(rho_r^tensorm||sigma_r^tensorm)=(1+chi_P^2)^m.

For arbitrary positive reference sigma, weighted Hilbert-Schmidt
Cauchy-Schwarz gives D_trace(rho,sigma)<=(1/2)sqrt(chi_P^2(rho||sigma)).
Thus a test requiring trace distance at least d_*>0 needs

 m>=log(1+4d_*^2)/log(1+34992q^2)
  >=[log(1+4d_*^2)/34992] N^2/r^2.                       (13)

The comparator tau^tensorN is fully separable and matches every one-body
measurement. A certification protocol sound on all fully separable states
must accept it as a null, so (13) applies to such certification. In a
false-positive/false-negative formulation with errors alpha,beta, one may
take d_*=1-alpha-beta. Joint processing across the fresh marginals is allowed.

However, tau^tensorN generally does NOT satisfy the supplied exact sector
promise. In fact if t>0,

 <J^2>_(tau^tensorN)=3N/4+N(N-1)t^2/4>3N/4.

Therefore (13) does not establish a universal lower bound for certification
whose null class is restricted to the trusted white-sector or mean-spin
promise. This is a genuine distinction, not a missing constant.

A concrete reason: every separable N-qubit state obeys

 <J^2> >= N/2+|<J>|^2.                                  (14)

On a product state with Bloch vectors v_i, let V=sum_i v_i. Then
<J^2>=3N/4+(|V|^2-sum_i|v_i|^2)/4>=N/2+|V|^2/4.
Convex mixtures preserve (14) by Jensen. Given <J^2>=3N/4, separability
therefore requires |b|<=1/sqrt(N). The white-sector highest-weight state
has |b|~sqrt(8/pi)/sqrt(N)>1/sqrt(N). A certifier trusting the global spin
constraint can certify this example using one-body polarization, with
O(N) one-qubit preparations for fixed error, rather than O(N^2).

If <J>=0, tau=I/2 and the comparator is itself in the white-sector class;
the null-class problem disappears in that special case.
