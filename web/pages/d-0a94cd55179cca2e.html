# Stable-polynomial lift: reconstructed theorem and strict-dual corollary

**Verdict.** In pinned release `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`, the October 5 paper *An Exact Semidefinite Lift of a Nonspectrahedral Hyperbolicity Cone* gives an explicit coefficient rule for a homogeneous (100\times100) real symmetric pencil with 307 auxiliary variables whose projection is the entire closed 23-dimensional cone, including its singular-(X) boundary. The load-bearing algebra is a degree-two angular matching identity that turns a continuum of slice LMIs into one finite moment/localizing matrix. I found a useful exact consequence: this lift is strictly feasible at (e=(I_4,I_4,0)), so standard SDP duality gives an exact (100\times100) PSD certificate for every functional in the dual cone. This consequence is derived here from the source plus Slater duality; it is not presented as novel mathematics.

The claimed exact lift itself remains **source-reported and internally reconstructed**, not independently formally verified or externally reviewed. The October 5 manuscript states that the dimensions are upper bounds and makes no minimality claim. I found no later primary source specifically extending/correcting this construction in a bounded exact-title and construction-keyword search through October 8; this is not an exhaustive priority check.

## Theorem and mechanism

The target takes (V=S^4\times S^4\times\mathbb R^3\cong\mathbb R^{23}), with coordinates given by upper-triangular entries of (X), then (Z), then (y_1,y_2,y_3), and defines
\[
Q(y)=\begin{pmatrix}y_1^2+y_2^2&-y_1y_2&-y_1y_3\\-y_1y_2&y_2^2+y_3^2&-y_2y_3\\-y_1y_3&-y_2y_3&y_3^2+y_1^2\end{pmatrix},\quad
p(X,Z,y)=\det((\det X)Z-\Phi_y(\operatorname{adj}X)),\quad e=(I_4,I_4,0).
\]
The paper states that (p) is homogeneous of degree 20, (p(e)=1), and its closed hyperbolicity cone (K=\Lambda_+(p,e)) has the exact representation
\[
K=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^{23}:\exists v\in\mathbb R^{307},\ \sum_{\nu=1}^{23}x_\nu A_\nu+\sum_{j=1}^{307}v_jB_j\succeq0\right\},\qquad A_\nu,B_j\in S^{100}.
\]

The source proof proceeds by covering the (y)-space with angular planes (Y_\theta(\eta,\xi)=\eta(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0)+\xi(0,0,1)). A complex Gram factor of (Q(Y_\theta)), realified into four columns (r_j\in\mathbb R^3), defines a (20\times20) slice pencil
\[
L_\theta(X,Z,\eta,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}I_4\otimes X&B_\theta\\B_\theta^T&Z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The block determinant identity yields \(\det L_\theta=p(X,Z,Y_\theta)\), first for invertible (X), then for singular (X) by polynomial identity. Since (L_\theta(e)=I_{20}), the slice condition (L_\theta\succeq0) identifies the closed cone on every slice and proves hyperbolicity.

To remove the continuum of angles, the source takes a linear functional (\Lambda) on (E\), the 330-dimensional space of degree-at-most-7 trigonometric coefficients times the 22 slice coordinates \((X,Z,\eta,\xi)\). Output constraints read (X,Z,y\) from moments; nonnegativity of \(q^TL_\theta q\) for all (q\in(T_2)^{20}\), (T_2=(1,\cos\theta,\sin\theta,\cos2\theta,\sin2\theta)\), is exactly (H(\Lambda)\succeq0) for one size-100 matrix. The source degree-two matching lemma supplies the special test vectors that make the converse exact. Its scalar inequalities yield the range inclusion \(\operatorname{range}B_j\subseteq\operatorname{range}X\) and the generalized Schur bound (Z\succeq\sum_jB_j^TX^\dagger B_j\); completing a square proves slice positivity even when (X\) is singular. The proof does not assume that a feasible (\Lambda\) is a measure and does not close the projection.

## Exact Slater point and all 23 source moments

Use the source basis (f_0=1,f_{2r-1}=\cos(r\theta),f_{2r}=\sin(r\theta)) for (1\le r\le7\). Define
\[
\Lambda_0(g)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}g(\theta,I_4,I_4,0,0)\,d\theta.
\]
For (f\in T_7\), its normalized integral is (\mu_f=1) if (f=1) and zero for every nonconstant basis function. Consequently, for every (i\le j\),
\[
\Lambda_0(fX_{ij})=\mu_f\delta_{ij},\quad \Lambda_0(fZ_{ij})=\mu_f\delta_{ij},\quad \Lambda_0(f\eta)=\Lambda_0(f\xi)=0.
\]
These formulas specify all 330 moments. In the exact 23-output source convention, (X) and (Z) are each listed as \((11,12,13,14,22,23,24,33,34,44)\). Thus
\[
\big(\Lambda_0X_{11},\Lambda_0X_{12},\Lambda_0X_{13},\Lambda_0X_{14},\Lambda_0X_{22},\Lambda_0X_{23},\Lambda_0X_{24},\Lambda_0X_{33},\Lambda_0X_{34},\Lambda_0X_{44}\big)
=(1,0,0,0,1,0,0,1,0,1),
\]
and the (Z)-tuple is identical. The final three outputs are
\[
y_1=\Lambda_0(\eta\cos\theta)=0,\quad y_2=\Lambda_0(\eta\sin\theta)=0,\quad y_3=\Lambda_0(\xi)=0.
\]
Hence (O(\Lambda_0)=e=(I_4,I_4,0)), with all 307 unconstrained coordinates equal to zero under the coefficient section below.

At this point (L_\theta(e)=I_{20}). The normalized Gram matrix of (T_2) is
\[
G_{ab}=\frac1{2\pi}\int f_a(\theta)f_b(\theta)\,d\theta=\operatorname{diag}(1,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2),
\]
so (H(\Lambda_0)=I_{20}\otimes G\succ0), with minimum eigenvalue (1/2). This supplies strict feasibility for the **exact finite lift**.

## Coefficient maps and dual identity

Let (P\) be the 23 moment indices (X_{ij}f_0,Z_{ij}f_0) for (i\le j), (\eta\cos\theta,\eta\sin\theta), and (\xi f_0\), in the above source order. Define (O:E^*\to\mathbb R^{23}) by reading precisely these coordinates, with the last three outputs (y=(\Lambda(\eta\cos\theta),\Lambda(\eta\sin\theta),\Lambda(\xi))\). Let (S:\mathbb R^{23}\to E^*\) insert the 23 output coordinates at (P), setting all others to zero. Let (u_1,\ldots,u_{307}\) be the canonical coordinate basis vectors of the remaining moment coordinates, ordered by the source (T_7) basis and variable basis. Then (O\circ S=I), and these (u_j\) form a basis of \(\ker O\).

Define \(\mathcal H:E^*\to S^{100}\) by the source's coefficient rule
\[
\mathcal H(\Lambda)_{(i,a),(j,b)}=\Lambda(f_af_b(L_\theta)_{ij}),\quad i,j=1,\dots,20,\ a,b=1,\dots,5.
\]
The product has degree at most 7, so belongs to (E). The unambiguous linear coefficient maps are
\[
\mathcal A x=\mathcal H(Sx)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{23}x_\nu A_\nu,\quad A_\nu=\mathcal H(Se_\nu),
\]
\[
\mathcal B t=\mathcal H\left(\sum_{j=1}^{307}t_ju_j\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{307}t_jB_j,\quad B_j=\mathcal H(u_j).
\]
Therefore (K=\{x:\exists t,\mathcal Ax+\mathcal Bt\succeq0\}\). Under the Frobenius trace pairing,
\[
(\mathcal A^*Y)_\nu=\operatorname{tr}(A_\nu Y),\qquad(\mathcal B^*Y)_j=\operatorname{tr}(B_jY).
\]

**Derived standard-duality corollary (no novelty claim):**
\[
K^*=\{\mathcal A^*Y:Y\succeq0,\ \mathcal B^*Y=0\}.
\]
For (c\in K^*\), consider the equality-constrained conic LP
\[
\min_{x,t,W}c^Tx\quad\text{s.t. }\mathcal Ax+\mathcal Bt-W=0,\quad W\in S^{100}_+,
\]
with (x,t) free. Its optimum is zero: (x=t=W=0) is feasible and (c\) is nonnegative on every feasible (x\in K\). It has a Slater point (x=e,t=0,W=H(\Lambda_0)\succ0\). Standard conic strong duality and dual attainment give (Y\succeq0\), (\mathcal A^*Y=c\), (\mathcal B^*Y=0\). Conversely, such a (Y\) certifies (c^Tx=\langle Y,\mathcal Ax+\mathcal Bt\rangle\ge0\). Thus each dual-cone element has an exact size-100 PSD certificate. The source paper does not state this dual formula; it follows from its exact lift and the explicit strict witness.

## Prior art, barriers, and failed route

The lift paper is self-contained for the claimed hyperbolicity and lift. Its direct contextual inputs are: Gårding's convexity theory; the coefficient-one Choi–Lam biquadratic (Choi–Lam, 1977); moment/SOS inspiration (Lasserre); and its companion source for the separate assertion that this cone is nonspectrahedral. The companion gives the relevant weak-extremality lemma directly, although it attributes the extremality background to Choi–Lam and the terminology/characterization to Blekherman–Raiţă–Shankar–Sinn (2022). Helton–Vinnikov and Lewis–Parrilo–Ramana are cited only for the three-variable context, while Netzer–Sanyal and Scheiderer are nearby smoothness results, not premises of this construction. Exact links and scope are recorded in [`sources/NOTES.md`](sources/NOTES.md).

The main scope barrier is the distinction between a direct spectrahedron and a spectrahedral shadow. This cone has no direct original-coordinate pencil by the companion source, yet has the stated finite lift. The pinned October 5 no-lift paper concerns a **different** hyperbolicity cone; it blocks the broad inference that every hyperbolicity cone has a semidefinite lift, but that paper's proof was not audited here. Neither result gives a dimension-minimality statement.

One attempted counterexample to the source's degree-two matching lemma is rejected. PDF text extraction dropped conjugation overbars; the source uses \(-\overline g\), not \(-g\), in the affected matrix. The rendered pp. 5–6 were inspected; with (g=-i\sqrt2,h=-\sqrt2,\kappa=i,\delta=4\), the corrected second row agrees on both sides. This was an extraction artifact, not a flaw in the source. See the full retained correction in [`sources/NOTES.md`](sources/NOTES.md).
