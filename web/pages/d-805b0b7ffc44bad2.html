# Cycle 3: acquire an output law before identifying its hidden rates

Status: conditional universal derivations, exact rational/analytic obstructions, and individually sampled synthetic acquisition code. No physical experiment, established novelty, or special architecture advantage. Earlier cycle artifacts are preserved. The direct retaining control wins the executed cost comparison.

## Exact scientific interface

We remove the supplied lambda, k, delta and sigma values from the acquisition algorithm. An experiment resets a linear dissipative system, prepares the same known unit pulse, waits h elementary time units and measures the same normalized aggregate. Its true mean is

    mu_h = <v,E^h v>,  ||v||=1,  mu_0=1.                 (1)

The structural promise is that E is a self-adjoint positive contraction in the declared measurement/preparation inner product: 0<=E<=I. Dimension and microscopic rates need not be known. The two-species symmetric interconversion/leakage class from cycle 2 satisfies this promise; in normalized m,d coordinates v=e1. Each assay adds independent N(0,sigma²) noise, with unknown finite sigma>0, the same across protocols. The Gaussian/noise-independence family, correct reset, known pulse amplitude, calibrated measurement units, linearity and the structural promise remain supplied scientific assumptions. Finite data below do not acquire those assumptions. In a weighted reversible system the requisite inner product/preparation match must itself be calibrated; it is not silently free.

The target is a report of mean impulse responses for integer h=1,...,H, H>=2, with a declared absolute accuracy tau and joint confidence 1-eta. Reusing the report for balanced positive input sequences of total amplitude at most L gives mean forecast error at most L*tau over this horizon, by linear superposition. This covers a specified mean-response menu, not arbitrary interventions, continuous-time queries between elementary steps, species readouts, or exact future noisy report distributions.

A zero-mass reset/assay costs 2 actions. Each pulse reset/preparation/wait/assay costs 4 actions and h units of physical waiting. No free observations or hidden calibration calls. The simulator exposes only zero() and assay(h); both competing acquisition algorithms receive the same access and Gaussian family, neither lambda,k,delta nor sigma. This studies the balanced protocol submenu. Cycle 2's asymmetric preparations are not proved useless or unavailable in a richer apparatus; no optimality claim over all those calibration actions is made. Their availability cannot rescue the observed negative comparison, because the exhibited retaining control already costs less using only this submenu. Code generation/retention is ordinary Python; there is no special engine evaluated.

## Two assays provide a reusable approximate law

Finite-dimensional spectral decomposition of E supplies a probability measure nu on [0,1] with mu_h=int q^h dnu(q). This is just diagonalizing the symmetric matrix and using squared v projections as weights; no microscopic eigensystem is supplied to the learner. The same argument extends to bounded self-adjoint operators by spectral calculus.

Let a=mu_1, c=mu_2 and r=c-a². Then

    r = int (q-a)² dnu(q) = ||(E-aI)v||² >= 0.           (2)

For integer h>=2, f(q)=q^h has 0<=f''(q)<=h(h-1) on [0,1]. Taylor's formula with integral remainder and int(q-a)dnu=0 yield

    0 <= mu_h-a^h <= h(h-1)*r/2.                       (3)

The lower bound also follows from convexity. For h=0,1 the error is zero. Exact one-rate closure for this output is equivalent to r=0: (2) then gives Ev=av and hence mu_h=a^h for every h. Conversely exact closure at h=1,2 gives r=0. This is an output law, not identification of the unobserved operator.

Suppose two independently acquired means give clipped estimates ahat,chat in [0,1], each within w of the truth. Define aL=max(0,ahat-w), aU=min(1,ahat+w), cL=max(0,chat-w), cU=min(1,chat+w),

    rL=max(0,cL-aU²),  rU=max(0,cU-aL²).                (4)

On the joint confidence event rL<=r<=rU. The single stored scalar ahat yields a report for every h<=H with sound simultaneous error bounds

    |mu_h-ahat^h| <= h*w + h(h-1)*rU/2.                (5)

This acquires a useful approximation without recovering lambda,k or the hidden sign. It returns UNKNOWN if the requested tau is not certified. The event is shared by all report reuse within the declared target menu; there is no new union penalty per input/query. No exact equality is inferred by declaring rU numerically small.

Under the correct event, rU<=r+6w for w<=1: cU<=c+2w, aL>=max(0,a-2w), and cU-aL²<=r+2w+4aw (with the same 6w bound when a<2w). Let C=H(H-1)/2 and choose

    w = tau/[2*(H+6C)].                               (6)

For tau small enough that w<=1, whenever r<=tau/(2C), (5) certifies tau. The sampling effort depends on desired forecast accuracy, not inverse powers of an arbitrarily smaller true r. This is the useful distinction from insisting on hidden rate recovery near exact closure. An equally informed retaining moment/observability control can use the same distinction.

## Unknown Gaussian variance: paid calibration with finite expected cost

Use M fresh pairs of zero-protocol observations and form D_j=(Z_{2j}-Z_{2j-1})/sqrt(2). The constant zero-protocol mean cancels, D_j are independent N(0,sigma²), and S=M^-1 sum D_j² has E S=sigma². These measurements calibrate noise scale, not measurement gain or the global Gaussian family.

The Gaussian integral gives E exp(-t D_j²/sigma²)=(1+2t)^-1/2. Markov at t=1/2 gives

    P(S<=sigma²/2) <= exp[-M*(log(2)/2-1/4)].           (7)

Choose M=ceil(log(1/eta_s)/(log(2)/2-1/4)), so sigmaU²=2S is an upper variance bound except on probability eta_s. This costs 2M observations, 4M actions, no waiting, and uses only the observed differences.

Independently repeat each of the two pulse protocols R times, where

    R=ceil(2*sigmaU²*log(4/eta_m)/w²).                  (8)

Condition on the calibration and its good event. Gaussian mean tails and a union bound for the two means give failure at most eta_m; R depends only on independent calibration data. Therefore (4)-(5) hold with probability at least 1-eta_s-eta_m. Take eta_s=eta_m=eta/2. Total cost is 4M+8R actions, 2M+2R observations and 3R waiting units. Expected charged cost obeys

    E cost <= 4M+8+32*sigma²*log(4/eta_m)/w².           (9)

There is no fixed cost bound uniform over arbitrarily large unknown sigma. The random calibration budget adaptation has finite expected cost for every finite sigma. This is confidence-certified approximation; it does not produce an exactly known Gaussian/channel law after finite samples.

## Which extra assays acquire a richer law?

Two exact means do not identify the next response even in the original two-species kinetic class. Consider

    E_low  = [[4/5,1/50],[1/50,1/5]],
    E_high = [[4/5,1/50],[1/50,3/5]].                   (10)

Both give mu_1=4/5 and mu_2=(4/5)²+(1/50)². They give different mu_3, whose gap is (1/50)²*(3/5-1/5)=1/6250. These are positive contractions with finite physically admissible generators, not artificial inaccessible hidden states.

For that last assertion, set A=log E and compare to log diag(a,e). Both minimum eigenvalues exceed e-b; the resolvent integral for matrix log bounds ||A-log diag(a,e)||<=b/(e-b). Thus lambda=-A11 and |delta|=|A12| obey lambda-|delta|>=-log(a)-2b/(e-b)>0 for both displayed choices. Likewise k=(A11-A22)/2>=log(a/e)/2-b/(e-b)>0. Transforming to species coordinates gives off-diagonal rates k and leakage lambda±delta. The script also computes the generators; numerical positivity is a diagnostic alongside these strict analytic bounds.

If the balanced spectral support is promised to have at most two points, a third mean d=mu_3 identifies its exact recurrence when r>0:

    T=(d-a*c)/r,  D=T*a-c,
    mu_h=T*mu_{h-1}-D*mu_{h-2}.                        (11)

For a two-by-two E these are its trace and determinant; Cayley–Hamilton proves the recurrence. Formula (11) is ordinary Prony/observability reconstruction. Division by r exposes the acquisition instability near one-rate closure. Exact sign and unused internal modes remain unidentified, and finite noisy observations do not justify exact recovered coefficients.

There is a stronger four-assay certificate without a supplied two-mode/dimension bound. In L²(nu), use orthonormal functions 1 and (q-a)/sqrt(r). Compress multiplication by q to this span:

    M2=[[a,sqrt(r)],[sqrt(r),e]],
    e=(mu_3-2*a*mu_2+a³)/r = T-a.                     (12)

Its Rayleigh quotient lies in [0,1], so 0<=M2<=I. Let p(q)=q²-Tq+D, and acquire mu_4. Orthogonality to 1 and q follows directly from (11). The nonnegative residual is

    rho = int p(q)² dnu(q)
        = mu_4-T*mu_3+D*mu_2 >= 0.                    (13)

It is also mu_4-<e1,M2^4 e1>. If rho=0, positivity forces nu to be supported on the two roots of p and (11) is the exact global output law. This conclusion needs no supplied microscopic dimension, but still needs the positive spectral-measure structural promise and exact noiseless moments.

For arbitrary rho, let q1,q2 in [0,1] be the eigenvalues of M2 and define the positive two-node spectral prediction muhat_h=<e1,M2^h e1>. Its moments agree with mu_0,...,mu_3. For r>0 the nodes are distinct. The cubic Hermite interpolant L to f(q)=q^h at q1,q2, matching values and first derivatives, has remainder

    f(q)-L(q)=f''''(xi)*p(q)²/4!.

To reconstruct this standard interpolation fact, subtract L and a multiple of p² chosen to vanish at q; the resulting function has five zeros counted with the double-node zeros, so iterated Rolle gives a zero fourth derivative. For h>=4, 0<=f''''/4!<=binom(h,4). Integrate: degree-three moment matching gives int L dnu=int L dnu_2=muhat_h. Hence

    0 <= mu_h-muhat_h <= binom(h,4)*rho.               (14)

The script verifies (13)-(14) with exact fractions on a three-mode measure, including nonzero rho. This is reconstructed Gaussian quadrature prior art, not a new theorem claim. It provides a precise assay-to-report interface: first two moments support a one-rate tolerance certificate; four moments support a two-rate residual certificate; neither obtains the positive-spectrum promise from data.

## Finite-noise four-moment certificate and its singular cost

The following derivation is not an implemented general optimizer. It specifies an honest extension rather than asserting exact Prony fitting from noisy moments. Acquire four means with common confidence half-width w using (8) with log(8/eta_m); cost 4M+16R actions and 10R waiting units. Clip each estimate to [0,1]. If rL from (4) is positive, rhat=chat-ahat² is positive. We have |r-rhat|<=3w. The numerator of e in (12) has error at most 8w. Set ehat=clip((dhat-2ahat*chat+ahat³)/rhat,0,1) and bhat=sqrt(rhat). Then

    |sqrt(r)-bhat| <= 3w/sqrt(rL),
    |e-ehat| <= 11w/rL.                               (15)

For the second bound write |Nhat-e*rhat|<=|Nhat-N|+|e|*|r-rhat|, then use clipping as a nonexpansive projection. Form the symmetric raw Mhat from ahat,bhat,ehat and clip its eigenvalues into [0,1]. Projection onto the positive-contraction cone is nonexpansive in Frobenius norm, so its difference from M2 has operator norm at most

    kappa = sqrt(w²+18*w²/rL+121*w²/rL²).              (16)

Telescoping powers gives moment error at most h*kappa. Therefore

    rhoU=max(0,mu4hat+w-<e1,Mhat^4 e1>+4*kappa),
    |mu_h-<e1,Mhat^h e1>|
       <= h*kappa + binom(h,4)*rhoU                    (17)

is sound for h>=4, with just h*kappa for h<=3. If rL<=0, (15)-(17) do not apply; return the simpler certificate or UNKNOWN. The inverse-rL dependence shows why recovering a second mode can be expensive near the critical law. A target-specific learner should first try the cheaper adequate report, while a competent retaining observability/moment solver can make the identical choice. No claim is made that these conservative bounds beat direct measurement.

## A physical obstruction: the two checks do not acquire symmetry

The first-two-moment equality test becomes false without the structural promise. Here is an explicit finite-rate three-species mass-action system. Let v=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3), u=(1,-1,0)/sqrt(2), w=(1,1,-2)/sqrt(6), and Q=[v,u,w]. In this basis let C cycle v->u->w->v; its matrix is [[0,0,1],[1,0,0],[0,1,0]]. Set

    N=Q C Q^T, D0=.5I+.1vv^T, E=D0+.01N.             (18)

N is orthogonal. The first two N steps have no v projection; the third returns v. Expanding products, every word with one or two N factors has zero v return; therefore

    mu_1=.6,  mu_2=.36,  mu_3=.216+10^-6.              (19)

Thus apparent r=0 from the first two exact means does not entail a one-rate law. E has positive entries because the off-diagonal base .1/3 exceeds .01*|N_ij| and the diagonal base is larger.

It is the exponential of a physical generator G=log E. Since ||I-D0||=.5 and ||.01N||=.01, the log series converges with ||I-E||<=.51. Expanding products/norms termwise bounds

    ||log E-log D0|| <= log(.5/.49) < .020203.

The baseline generator has off-diagonals log(.6/.5)/3>.06077 and column sums log(.6)<-.5108. Hence all off-diagonal entries of G remain positive, and every column sum is at most log(.6)+sqrt(3)*.020203<0. This is a legitimate interconversion/degradation generator. The own numerical log-series check is supplementary; the strict norm bounds establish physical admissibility. Its non-self-adjoint hidden circulation is exactly the unacquired premise that breaks (2)-(3).

Unknown template validity is therefore an object-level missing capability. More repeated first-two assays cannot certify that premise. Noise adds another exactness boundary: for any finite observation record, Gaussian likelihood is positive under both exact closure and nearby nonclosure. Any closure acceptance event of positive probability after a finite number of assays consequently has positive probability under the alternative. Exact zero-error certification is unavailable without a separated supplied catalogue or stronger access. Confidence bounds and finite-horizon approximation are the appropriate outputs here.

## Executed comparator and result

`work/r01_discovery/cycle3/moment_acquisition.py` runs the two-moment acquisition algorithm and a competent retaining direct impulse-response control on identical access. The control calibrates sigma by the same paid protocol, then measures each h<=H with Gaussian confidence half-width tau and retains all means. Its per-protocol count is ceil(2*sigmaU²*log(2H/eta_m)/tau²). Both reports reuse by linearity over the same target input menu.

For H=4,tau=.02,eta=.05,true hidden sigma=.001, lambda=.3,k=.7 and delta in {0,.02,.1}, 400 trials per algorithm/model produced 400 certified reports with no observed false certificate. Noise calibration uses M=39 pairs, 78 zero observations and 156 actions. Mean cost is 2737.4 actions and 968.025 waiting units for the moment certificate, versus 172 actions and 10 waiting units for the direct control. This is a clear negative acquisition-cost comparison. The finite no-false-certificate diagnostic is not the confidence theorem or external validation. A non-near-critical delta=.2 row records UNKNOWN rather than weakening the target tolerance.

The implementation individually draws every charged observation; it does not simulate a sample mean while hiding a large claimed sample count. Each algorithm uses O(M+P*R+H) ordinary arithmetic and stores O(H) report values (P=2 or H); no speculative hardware speedup is claimed. Physical waits and preparation actions dominate the specified information cost, while Python simulator work is ordinary diagnostic overhead. The confidence derivations use ideal real arithmetic; floating-point code is a fixed-fixture diagnostic, not a rigorously rounded implementation for arbitrary extreme numerical inputs. Exact fraction checks and the analytic physical log bounds are distinguished from that diagnostic.

## Prior art and consequential next step

Positive spectral moments, variance/Jensen/Taylor bounds, Prony reconstruction, Gaussian quadrature and finite-noise calibration are established tools. Mourrain's primary Prony discussion is Section 1.1; the older operator-moment paper distinguishes self-adjoint spectral structure from moment observations. Recent generic/critical lumping remains the starting motivation, not the source of our cost comparison. These deductions were reconstructed here; bibliographic novelty is unestablished.

The useful research consequence is the exact acquisition boundary. Microscopic lambda,k,delta and exact sigma need not be obtained to reuse a certified mean law. Two extra calibrated moment assays can certify an approximate two-mode report under a positive-spectrum promise. That promise cannot be inferred from the two apparent closure checks, even in ordinary positive reaction kinetics. Acquiring a scientifically valid structural class is the load-bearing unresolved step.

The next discriminating task is to acquire or reject the reversibility/preparation-duality promise by additional physically available interventions, with their calibration costs, rather than assume it because a low-degree moment fit passed. A paired asymmetric pulse or several reciprocal preparation/readout combinations may test that premise in a declared finite chemical class; finite absence of violations still does not certify unrestricted networks. Retain the direct impulse-response baseline: it already wins this tested finite horizon, and another invariant certificate should be adopted only if its honest target-menu/precision/setup cost improves a consequential use case.
