# Finite exact transport maps do not identify labeled circle geometry

Status: independently derived theorem using established circular-OT and Borsuk–Ulam theorems. Internal independent audit requested; historical novelty unverified. This is an acquisition boundary, not a revolutionary discovery. 6 October 2026.

## Question and supplied interface

On the known labeled circle M=R/Z, recover an unknown smooth Riemannian metric g=q(x)^2 dx^2, q>0, from transport maps for squared geodesic-distance cost. Fix total length integral_0^1 q=1 to remove constant scale. Marginal probability measures are supplied as coordinate densities alpha(x)dx and beta(x)dx, smooth and strictly positive. The unknown Riemannian volume density q is NOT supplied. The oracle returns the entire exact optimal map, including its labels, for each chosen marginal pair. Arbitrarily many samples from that coupling add no information once its map is known.

Main result: no finite fixed list of such experiments is locally injective on the unrestricted smooth metric class, even with the complete exact maps. This holds near EVERY smooth positive baseline metric. A corresponding statement holds for a fixed finite continuously adaptive protocol under the explicitly stated continuity assumption below. Discontinuous adaptive protocols and higher-dimensional analogues are unresolved here.

## Reconstructed circular-transport calculation

Let Q(x)=integral_0^x q(s)ds, so Q(x+1)=Q(x)+1. The lifted cost

    c_q(x,y)=1/2 [Q(y)-Q(x)]^2

has mixed derivative -q(x)q(y)<0, hence strict Monge inequality: integrate that derivative on any rectangle with ordered x,y endpoints. It is invariant under simultaneous integer shifts and grows quadratically with |x-y| because min q>0. Its projection min_{k in Z} c_q(x,y+k) equals1/2 the squared Riemannian circle distance.

For the chosen marginals let A(x)=integral_0^x alpha and B(y)=integral_0^y beta, extended by A(x+1)=A(x)+1 and likewise B. The lifted quantile maps are

    T_theta(x)=B^{-1}(A(x)+theta),
    S_theta(y)=A^{-1}(B(y)-theta)=T_theta^{-1}(y).

Local swaps lower the cost whenever transported pairs cross, by the strict Monge inequality. Reparametrize both marginals by their cumulative mass. A monotone, periodic, measure-preserving map with unit Lebesgue marginals must be a translation of the mass coordinate; this gives exactly T_theta. The established circular theorem supplies the passage from the minimizing periodic lift to the global circle optimum. It is essential to minimize the LIFTED cost over theta, rather than simply differentiate an arbitrary shortest-distance branch.

Define

    C_q(theta)=1/2 integral_0^1 alpha(x)[Q(T_theta(x))-Q(x)]^2 dx.

Differentiate using dT_theta/dtheta=1/beta(T_theta), then change variables y=T_theta(x). The resulting integrand is1-periodic, so the shifted integration interval can be replaced by[0,1]:

    C'_q(theta)=F_q(theta)
       =integral_0^1 q(y)[Q(y)-Q(S_theta(y))]dy.

Since dS_theta/dtheta=-1/alpha(S_theta),

    C''_q(theta)=integral_0^1 q(y)q(S_theta(y))/alpha(S_theta(y))dy
       >= (min q)^2/(max alpha)>0.

C_q is coercive: T_theta(x)-x grows linearly in theta, while q has a positive lower bound. Thus F has exactly one zero Theta(q), giving the unique optimal circle map T_{Theta(q)} modulo the circle. All infinite-dimensional map dependence on q has collapsed to ONE scalar. This is not an assertion that the unknown map is constant or that its graph has finitely many entries: its shape is already determined by the supplied marginals apart from that shift.

F is smooth in q in any C^r positive neighborhood; in fact it is quadratic in q because S_theta is determined by the marginals. The derivative in theta is positive as above, so the scalar implicit-function theorem gives smooth dependence q -> Theta(q). A direct monotone-root continuity argument would also suffice.

## Universal finite fixed-design obstruction (our deduction)

Fix m arbitrary smooth positive marginal pairs. For any normalized smooth baseline q0>0 choose m+1 linearly independent, zero-mean smooth real functions h_i on the circle. They may be L2-orthonormal. For a on S^m set

    q_a=q0+epsilon sum_{i=1}^{m+1} a_i h_i.

Choose epsilon>0 small enough that all q_a remain positive; since the sphere is compact this is possible. All retain total length1, and epsilon can be chosen to place the whole family in any prescribed C^r neighborhood of q0. The observation map

    O:S^m -> R^m,
    O(a)=(Theta_1(q_a),...,Theta_m(q_a))

is continuous. Borsuk–Ulam gives a with O(a)=O(-a). Therefore q_a and q_{-a} yield exactly the same m complete maps. They are different by linear independence of the h_i. Their common length normalization prohibits a nontrivial constant rescaling. This proves failure of local injectivity in every neighborhood of q0.

With L2-orthonormal h_i, ||q_a-q_{-a}||_2=2epsilon, and

    ||g_a-g_{-a}||_2 =4epsilon ||q0 sum a_i h_i||_2
        >=4epsilon min(q0).

Any estimator using this fixed transcript has worst-case metric L2 error at least2epsilon min(q0) on this indistinguishable pair. Pair construction is nonconstructive; the theorem is exact, not a numerical certificate. No randomized minimax-risk bound follows without a separate argument: the pair can depend on the chosen design/seed.

Coordinate labels and measures are held fixed. All normalized circle metrics are intrinsically isometric via reparametrization; we investigate the labeled tensor q(x)^2, as the inverse-map interface does. If geometry is only required up to arbitrary diffeomorphism, normalized circle geometry is already trivial and this obstruction is not a consequential inverse problem.

## Continuous finite feedback and regular transcripts

Suppose a deterministic protocol makes m experiments, each new pair selected continuously (in a topology controlling smooth positive densities) from the previous observed shifts/maps. The complete transcript of m shifts is continuous in q by induction. The same Borsuk–Ulam argument applies. Equality of all shifts means equality of the chosen marginals and full observed maps at every step. This is not asserted for arbitrary discontinuous feedback.

Separately, for ANY realized finite adaptive transcript, hold all its marginal pairs and observed shifts theta_j fixed. On the mean-zero perturbation space the compatibility functionals are F_j(q)=F_{q,j}(theta_j). Their derivative in direction h, H(x)=integral_0^x h, is

    DF_j(q)[h]=integral_0^1 {h(y)[Q(y)-Q(S_j(y))]
                     +q(y)[H(y)-H(S_j(y))]}dy.

If these m derivatives are linearly independent on the mean-zero space, the Banach implicit-function theorem makes the exact-compatible metrics a local infinite-dimensional manifold of codimension m. Every such metric reproduces the same adaptive transcript regardless of whether the protocol's selection rule is continuous. Rank failure is not silently assumed away; singular transcripts require another argument.

## Explicit exact infinite-dimensional witness

For k=1,...,m select a smooth increasing odd periodic lift

    T_k(x)=x+(t/k)sin(2*pi*k*x),  0<t<1/(2*pi).

Take alpha_k=1 and beta_k=(T_k)#dx, i.e. beta_k(T_k(x))=1/T'_k(x). Both marginals are smooth, positive and known independently of q. For EVERY smooth positive even q normalized to integral1, Q is odd, S_k=T_k^{-1} is odd, and q(y)[Q(y)-Q(S_k(y))] is odd and1-periodic. Hence its integral is0. Strict convexity proves that each T_k is exactly optimal for every such q. The compatible metric family is genuinely infinite-dimensional even after fixing length. The maps are nonidentity and full continuum observations, not atomic support artifacts.

For example q=1, q=1+0.3cos(2*pi*x)+0.2cos(4*pi*x), and q=1-0.4cos(2*pi*x)+0.15cos(8*pi*x) have common length1 and positive lower bounds. They share all these maps. The formulas provide an exact witness; quadrature below is only a replay.

These transcripts are also regular for sufficiently small t and each fixed m. In sine directions h_l=sin(2*pi*l*x), expand S_k(y)=y-(t/k)sin(2*pi*k*y)+O(t^2). At q=1,

    DF_k(1)[h_l]=(t/k)delta_{kl}+O(t^2).

The determinant of the m-by-m derivative matrix has leading term t^m/product_{k=1}^m k, so is nonzero for all sufficiently small positive t. Thus the conditional regular-transcript result is nonvacuous and persists in a neighborhood of this construction. The numerical t=.025,m=3 replay found singular values(.0249871,.0124681,.0083204), not a universal rank certificate.

## Independent checks, consequence and next step

I reconstructed the scalar derivative twice: once differentiating C in source coordinates and changing variables, and once differentiating the inverse map S in the displayed F. The positive second derivative is exact. Reflection parity verifies the explicit witness independently of Borsuk–Ulam. The replay checks nine transport/metric combinations, evaluates neighboring shift costs, and computes the finite derivative matrix. None substitutes for an external proof audit.

The result forbids an unqualified finite-experiment acquisition claim for unrestricted labeled circle metrics, even if complete exact maps or unlimited samples per chosen pair are offered. In a finite-dimensional p-parameter smooth submodel the same dimension argument only obstructs m<p locally; full-rank p measurements may succeed. Higher-dimensional full map fields need not collapse to scalar observations, so no extrapolation is justified.

Nearest mechanisms are classical circular-OT shift reduction, dimension topology, and infinite-dimensional inverse-problem nonidentifiability. Our specific finite-data metric consequence has unverified historical novelty. It is likely a useful boundary theorem rather than a high-priority breakthrough. Concrete use: correct the data contract for metric-learning algorithms before spending effort on finite-noise reconstruction, and expose when claimed identification actually relies on a supplied finite parameterization or restricted metric symmetry.

Best next falsifiable step: independently audit the projected-cost bridge and Borsuk–Ulam contract, then test whether a finite number of nonsymmetric full maps in dimension>=2 can impose a coercive differential system on g without supplying the unknown potential/volume form. This would change the acquisition problem; extending the scalar obstruction to dimensions where it does not apply would not. A cheaper follow-up is a quantitative p-parameter circle design with a matched dimension lower bound; its consequence is modest and it should not displace stronger programme lanes.
