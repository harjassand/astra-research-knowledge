FINITE-BIT THEOREM FOR THE MARGINALIZED POSITIVE-P GAUSSIAN HERALD SAMPLER
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: internally proved candidate; exact-real identities were separately audited by the programme lead; finite-bit construction awaits parent audit

1. Scope and exact-real target
=============================

Let C be a rational, strictly positive definite covariance on d centered
coherent quadrature modes, with the PNR convention

    L_n(x_j)=exp(-||x_j||^2/2)(||x_j||^2/2)^n/n!.

Let H be a herald set, with prescribed counts k_j, and let U be the
unheralded output set. Let L denote the total binary input length of C, the
herald, b, and eps; the herald counts are represented in binary but their
sum kappa is also charged numerically. Define H_+={j in H:k_j>0},
m=|H_+|, and kappa=sum_(j in H) k_j. Put P_H for the quadrature coordinate projection
onto every herald mode, and define

    C°=(C^(-1)+P_H)^(-1),  z_H=det(I+C P_H)^(-1/2),
    S=(C°)_(H_+,H_+),
    K=(C°)_(U,H_+) S^(-1),
    V=(C°)_(U,U)-K S K^T.

The matrices C°,S,K,V are rational and computable from C and the herald in
polynomial bit time. The scalar z_H need not be evaluated. The Gaussian tilt
identity and the PNR likelihood give

    p_H=z_H Z_S,
    Z_S=E_(X~N(0,S))[W(X)],
    W(X)=prod_(j in H_+)(||X_j||^2/2)^(k_j)/k_j!,

and the exact conditional law is obtained by drawing

    X_+ ~ pi_+(x)=phi_S(x)W(x)/Z_S,
    X_U | X_+=x ~ N(Kx,V),
    N_u | X_U ~ independent Poisson(||X_u||^2/2).

The theorem below samples this final count record. It does not physically
produce herald successes. If an optical experiment is required to generate
the herald, the expected number of physical trials remains 1/p_H.

Assume m>0 and a supplied rational certificate

    (1-eps)b I <= S <= (1+eps)b I,
    b>0, 0<=eps<=1/4.

Write c=1+eps, D=S^(-1)-(cb)^(-1)I, and

    q(x)=phi_(cb I)(x)W(x)/(cb)^kappa,
    a(x)=exp(-x^T D x/2),
    alpha=E_q[a(X)].

Then D is positive semidefinite, pi_+=q a/alpha, and

    alpha >= exp(-2c eps(m+kappa)/(1-eps^2))
           >= exp(-6 kappa eps).

For the last inequality, m<=kappa, c<=5/4, and
4c/(1-eps^2)<=16/3<6. Set

    Lambda=2^ceil(12 kappa eps).

Since ln 2>1/2, Lambda>=exp(6 kappa eps)>=1/alpha. This is a computable
dyadic envelope lower bound for success probability; alpha itself, Z_S, and
p_H are never evaluated.

2. Finite-bit sampler theorem
=============================

Theorem. Given rational C>0, the herald pattern, rational b and eps, the
certificate above, and rational accuracy and timeout parameters
0<delta,xi<1, there is a bounded-random-bit algorithm whose output law is
within total variation delta+xi of the exact conditional count law. Its
worst-case bit time and random-bit use are polynomial in

    d, L, kappa (numerical, not just binary), Lambda, M_out,
    delta^(-1), log(xi^(-1)),

and the bit lengths of the supplied rational certificate and exact rational
factorizations. M_out is the explicit rational output-rate cap in Section 6.
The polynomial includes the cost of exact certificate checking and is
uniform over all rational C satisfying the input conditions. In particular,
it has no 1/p_H or log(1/p_H) factor. A polynomial-in-L corollary requires
numeric kappa, Lambda, M_out, delta^(-1), and log(xi^(-1)) to be polynomially
bounded in L. If rational scale or covariance data make M_out exponentially
large in L, the theorem charges polynomial time in that numeric M_out.

If kappa=0, then H_+ is empty and W=1. No rejection is used: the heralded
latent state is N(0,C°), and the output is sampled from its U marginal,
followed by Poisson counts. The same finite Gaussian/Poisson procedure below
gives TV at most delta (and is therefore within delta+xi), with no dependence
on the all-zero herald mass z_H. For this branch set V_0=(C°)_(U,U), factor
V_0=L_0 L_0^T, take rational L_{0,*}>=sqrt(tr V_0), and choose `Rz`, `cr`,
and `gamma_L` as in Section 5 with `r=2|U|`; set
`gamma_Z=delta/[512(1+L_{0,*}+gamma_L)]`. Its rate cap is

    M_out=1/2[(L_{0,*}+gamma_L)cr(Rz+1)]^2.

If U is empty, the empty output record is returned exactly.

3. Exact-real rejection law and acceptance coupling lemma
=========================================================

The proposal q factors over positive-count modes. For each j in H_+, draw
independent unit-rate exponentials E_(j,l), l=1,...,k_j+1, and a uniform
phase theta_j, and set

    R_j^2/2 = cb sum_(l=1)^(k_j+1) E_(j,l),
    X_j = R_j(cos theta_j,sin theta_j).

Thus R_j^2/2 has the Gamma(k_j+1, scale cb) law. The total number of
exponentials is K0=m+kappa, and

    ||X||^2=2cb sum_(j,l) E_(j,l),
    E_q||X||^2=2cb K0.

The eigenvalue interval for S gives

    ||D|| <= 2 eps/[b(1-eps^2)].

Jensen proves the displayed alpha lower bound. Accepting a q proposal with
probability a(x) gives exactly pi_+.

The acceptance function is globally Lipschitz:

    |a(x)-a(y)| <= L_a ||x-y||,
    L_a=sqrt(||D||/e).

Indeed, in an eigenbasis of D, the gradient norm is bounded by
sqrt(||D||) r exp(-r^2/2), whose maximum is sqrt(||D||/e).

We use the following elementary normalization lemma. Suppose (X,Y) are
coupled proposal vectors, P(||X-Y||>tau)<=beta, and exact and implemented
acceptance probabilities a(X), ahat(Y) are coupled by a common uniform
coin. Assume on ||X-Y||<=tau that

    |a(X)-ahat(Y)| <= L_a tau + e_a,

and let alpha=E a(X)>=1/Lambda. If

    eps_try=beta+L_a tau+e_a+e_coin <= 1/(2 Lambda),

where e_coin accounts for finite coin discretization and
P[accept indicators disagree]<=eps_try, then the two one-trial success
probabilities are at least 1/(2Lambda). The exact accepted X law and the
implemented accepted Y law admit a coupling with

    P(||X-Y||>tau) <= 3 Lambda eps_try + 2 Lambda beta.

Proof. Let A and Ahat be their accept indicators. Their disagreement
probability is at most eps_try. The event that both accept has probability
at least alpha-eps_try>=1/(2Lambda). The exact accepted marginal differs in
TV by at most eps_try/alpha from its marginal conditional on both accepting;
the implemented accepted marginal differs by at most eps_try/Pr(Ahat=1).
Under the both-accepted coupling, the probability of a proposal pair with
distance greater than tau is at most beta/Pr(A=Ahat=1). Gluing these three
couplings gives the displayed bound (and a smaller bound when the sharper
alpha is used). This lemma compares accepted latent vectors by a coupling;
it does not claim TV closeness between a continuous law and a discrete grid.

4. Finite-bit positive-count proposal and acceptance
===================================================

For the finite implementation use the rational upper bound

    Lbar_a = 1 + sum_(i,j) |D_ij| >= L_a.

Choose

    J=ceil(log_2(256 Lambda/delta)),
    T=8cb(K0+J),
    B=ceil(sqrt(T)),
    tau=delta/[512 Lambda(1+Lbar_a+K_* )],

where K_* is any rational upper bound on ||K||_2, for example the sum of
absolute values of the entries of K. The exponential-sum moment-generating
function at 1/2 gives

    q(||X||^2>T)
      <= 2^K0 exp(-T/(4cb))
      <= exp(-2J)
      <= 2^(-2J)
      <= delta/(256 Lambda).

Generate every proposal uniform from a fixed dyadic grid of mesh h, coupling
it to a continuous uniform in the same cell. For the K0 exponential
uniforms, declare the coupling bad if any exact U<zeta, where

    zeta=delta/(256 Lambda K0).

This event has probability at most delta/(256Lambda). On its complement,
if h<=zeta/2, then U and the cell midpoint Uhat are both at least zeta/2,
and |(-ln U)-(-ln Uhat)|<=2h/zeta. Rational interval evaluation computes
-ln(Uhat) to a requested absolute error eta_e/2 in polynomial bit time; the
midpoint is positive, so this is a finite calculation even on the bad event.
Clamp the numerical estimate of -ln(Uhat) to [0,infinity) before adding the
exponential variates and taking a square root. Since the exact -ln(Uhat) is
nonnegative, this projection cannot increase its absolute approximation
error; in particular it prevents a slightly negative interval midpoint near
Uhat=1 from causing an invalid gamma radius.

Choose eta_e=tau^2/(128cbK0), require h<=min(zeta/2,zeta eta_e/4), and
require the numerical log interval error to be at most eta_e/2. Then the
total error in each exponential is at most eta_e. For each mode, the error
in squared radius obeys

    |R_j^2-Rhat_j^2| <= 2cb(k_j+1)eta_e,

so the Euclidean norm of the vector of radial errors is at most
sqrt(2cbK0 eta_e)<tau/8. Let c_m=ceil(sqrt(m)); compute the square roots to
absolute error tau/(32c_m), giving total additional radial error at most
tau/32. Approximate phases from dyadic uniforms with angle error at most
tau/[32(B+1)], and evaluate sine and cosine to absolute error at most
tau/[32(B+1)]. On the event ||X||^2<=T, these phase and trigonometric errors
contribute less than tau/8 in Euclidean norm. Choose the dyadic phase mesh
small enough that 2(22/7)h<=tau/[32(B+1)]; it can meet this and the
exponential requirement simultaneously. The
finite proposal vector is then coordinatewise clipped to [-B,B]^(2m).
Clipping is a nonexpansive projection onto a convex box, and the exact X is
inside this box whenever ||X||^2<=T. Hence the clipped dyadic/rational
proposal Xhat satisfies

    P(||X-Xhat||>tau) <= beta,
    beta <= delta/(128 Lambda).

All logarithms, square roots, phases, and trigonometric functions are
computed with certified rational intervals (use 22/7 as a rational upper
bound for pi when setting the phase mesh). The required precision in bits is
polynomial in d,L,log(kappa+2),log(Lambda+1),log(delta^(-1)),log(K_*+1),
log(Lbar_a+1), and the bit lengths of b and eps. The procedure uses
O(K0+m) fixed-grid uniforms per trial and polynomially many rational
operations.

For each Xhat, the exponent w=Xhat^T D Xhat/2 is a nonnegative rational.
Compute a rational approximation ahat in [0,1] to exp(-w) with additive
error e_a=delta/(512Lambda). If w>=ceil(log_2(2/e_a)), returning zero has
error at most e_a/2; otherwise w is O(log(e_a^(-1))) and rational interval
exponentiation has polynomial bit cost. Generate the acceptance coin from a
fixed dyadic uniform grid with mesh e_coin<=delta/(512Lambda), and accept
according to ahat. The one-trial disagreement bound is

    eps_try <= beta + Lbar_a tau + e_a + e_coin
             <= delta/(64 Lambda).

Thus the implemented success probability is at least 1/(2Lambda). Apply the
normalization lemma: up to probability at most delta/16, the exact accepted
positive-count latent X and the implemented accepted Xhat can be coupled
within distance tau. Repeating independent trials and conditioning on the
first acceptance leaves the accepted Xhat law unchanged. The uncapped
expected number of finite proposal trials is at most 2Lambda; the exact-real
sampler's corresponding expectation is at most Lambda.

Cap at

    N=ceil(4 Lambda ceil(log_2(2/xi)))

trials. Since one-trial success probability is at least 1/(2Lambda), the
timeout probability is at most exp(-N/(2Lambda))<=xi/2. Returning a fixed
record on timeout changes TV by at most xi/2. The attempt count and random
bits have a deterministic worst-case bound polynomial in Lambda and
log(xi^(-1)); the uncapped expected proposal count is at most 2Lambda.

5. Conditional output Gaussian without a Gaussian sampling oracle
=================================================================

Let r=2|U|. For r>0, set the following rational parameters and compute an
exact rational LDL factorization:

    Jz=ceil(log_2(512r/delta)), Rz=2Jz, cr=ceil(sqrt(r)),
    L_* = ceil(sqrt(tr V)),
    gamma_L=delta/[512(1+cr Rz)],
    gamma_Z=delta/[512(1+L_*+gamma_L)].

These rational precision parameters are fixed before any sampling. Factor

    V=R diag(v_1,...,v_r) R^T,

with rational unit-lower-triangular R and positive rational pivots v_i. Set
L=R diag(sqrt(v_i)), so L L^T=V. Approximate each square root by a rational
dyadic interval to obtain a rational matrix Lhat with

    ||L-Lhat||_F <= gamma_L.

Integer-square-root interval arithmetic achieves this in polynomial bit time
in d,L,log(gamma_L^(-1)), and the exact LDL bit lengths. It requires no
relative precision in a small pivot. In particular, a tiny conditional
variance does not create a hidden inverse-condition-number oracle cost.

For a core vector x, the target output amplitude is

    X_U=Kx+LZ,  Z~N(0,I_r).

Generate each normal coordinate by the inverse standard-normal CDF applied
to a dyadic-uniform midpoint, using the parameters above.

The Gaussian tail bound gives

    P(max_i |Z_i|>Rz) <= 2r exp(-Rz^2/2) <= delta/256.

Indeed, with Jz>=log_2(512r/delta), Rz=2Jz and
2r exp(-2Jz^2)<=2r 2^(-2Jz^2)<=delta/256.

Set R=Rz+1 and phi_lo=2^(-R^2-2). Since pi<4 and e<4,
phi_lo<=phi(R). On the complementary event, the inverse CDF has
derivative at most 1/phi_lo in the one-unit larger interval. Set

    eta_CDF=min(delta/[4096r], phi_lo gamma_Z/[8cr]).

Put X=R^2/2 and eta_exp=eta_CDF/[8(R+1)]. Choose a dyadic normal-uniform
mesh

    h<=min(phi_lo gamma_Z/(8cr),eta_CDF/8).

The formulas here are rational; in particular, `cr` is an integer upper
bound for sqrt(r). Evaluate each inverse CDF by certified interval bisection
to absolute error gamma_Z/(8cr). The exact Z and finite vector Zhat are
coupled so that ||Z-Zhat||<=gamma_Z, except on the displayed tail and the
endpoint ambiguity event quantified below. Clip each coordinate of Zhat to
[-Rz-1,Rz+1]. This is nonexpansive and leaves the good coupling valid. The
discrete vector Zhat is not claimed TV-close to a continuous Gaussian. On
|Z_i|<=Rz, the uniform is at least phi(R)>=phi_lo away from either endpoint
CDF; the stated mesh and interval widths are less than phi_lo/2, so the
midpoint remains in the invertible interval.

Here is the CDF routine. For 0<=u<=X, approximate exp(-u) by
P_N(u)=sum_(j=0)^N (-u)^j/j!, with

    N=ceil(6X+2 log_2(4/eta_exp)).

Since N+1>=6X and successive absolute Taylor terms after degree N have
ratio at most 1/6, the absolute tail is at most
2(eX/(N+1))^(N+1)<=2(e/6)^(N+1)<=eta_exp/2. The same bound applies to
u=x^2/2 throughout |x|<=R. Integrate the polynomial exactly as a rational
polynomial in x:

    integral_0^x P_N(t^2/2) dt
       = sum_(j=0)^N (-1)^j x^(2j+1)/((2j+1) 2^j j!).

Enclose pi by rational intervals using Machin's identity
pi=16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239), with alternating-series remainder bounds;
then enclose (2pi)^(-1/2) by rational square-root intervals. Taking
the normalization-constant interval width at most eta_CDF/[8(R+1)] gives a
rational interval for Phi(x) of width at most eta_CDF after integration and
interval multiplication. The polynomial degree and all rational bit lengths
are polynomial in R^2+log(1/eta_CDF).

For the two endpoint CDFs, return -R when the target is below the lower
endpoint interval, return R when it is above the upper endpoint interval,
and return the corresponding endpoint if the target lies inside either
endpoint interval. The latter ambiguous event has probability at most
2r(eta_CDF+h) across r coordinates, where h is the normal-uniform mesh;
choose h<=eta_CDF/8 as well as the earlier derivative-based mesh bound.
This probability is less than delta/1000 and is charged to the normal
coupling failure budget. Otherwise the target lies strictly between the true
endpoint CDF values and a rational bisection bracket can be maintained. At a
midpoint, compute a CDF interval of width at most eta_CDF. If it lies strictly
below or above the target, update the corresponding bracket endpoint. If it
contains the target, return that midpoint: the true CDF there differs from
the target by at most eta_CDF<=phi_lo times the requested inverse-coordinate
error, and Phi^(-1) is 1/phi_lo-Lipschitz on [-R,R]. Also return the bracket
midpoint once the bracket width is at most the requested inverse-coordinate
error. This gives a deterministic O(log(R sqrt(r)/gamma_Z)) bisection cap,
and terminates with the stated coordinate accuracy even when the target
coincides with a bisection midpoint. The positive rational phi_lo has bit
length O(R^2). Since Rz=O(log(r/delta)), the required CDF
precision has log(1/eta_CDF)=O(Rz^2+log(sqrt(r)/gamma_Z)+log(r/delta)); the
full normal routine has polynomial bit cost in d,L,log(delta^(-1)), and
log(gamma_Z^(-1)).

Let K_*>=||K||_2 be rational. On the good core and normal events,

    ||(KX+LZ)-(KXhat+Lhat Zhat)||
       <= K_* tau + gamma_L sqrt(r)Rz
                          +(L_*+gamma_L)gamma_Z.

Then the last two terms are each at most delta/512; the definition of tau
already makes K_*tau<=delta/512. These accuracy choices use only
O(log Lambda+log(delta^(-1))+log(K_*+1)+log(L_*+1)+log(r+1)) bits. No
continuous Gaussian TV approximation is invoked.

6. Output-rate cap and finite Poisson sampling
==============================================

Every finite core coordinate has magnitude at most B, and every finite
normal coordinate has magnitude at most Rz+1. Let

    cm=ceil(sqrt(2m)),
    M_out = 1/2 [K_* cm B + (L_*+gamma_L) cr(Rz+1)]^2.

This rational number bounds the total Poisson rate of every finite output
amplitude KXhat+Lhat Zhat. Choose

    Jp=ceil(log_2(64/delta)),
    Q=ceil(16(M_out+Jp)).

For any total rate lambda<=M_out, Q>=8lambda and the Poisson Chernoff bound
gives

    P(Poisson(lambda)>Q) <= (e lambda/Q)^Q <= (e/8)^Q <= 2^(-Q) < delta/32.

The finite sampler forms rational interval approximations to the probabilities
e^(-lambda)lambda^n/n!, n=0,...,Q, each of width at most
delta/[128(Q+1)]. Use a nonnegative rational lower endpoint ptilde_n for each
probability and assign 1-sum_(n<=Q) ptilde_n to overflow. The resulting
rational vector has TV distance at most the total lower-endpoint deficit,
which is at most delta/128. Rational range reduction or the Taylor series for
exp has bit cost polynomial in Q,M_out, the rate bit length, and
log(delta^(-1)). If overflow is selected, return a fixed record. Sample the
Q+2 rational outcomes using a dyadic
uniform with

    B_Pois=ceil(log_2(64(Q+2)/delta))

bits. The grid quantile adds at most delta/64 TV. Thus overflow contributes
at most delta/32, and the interval and finite-uniform approximations add at
most delta/32+delta/64.

Conditional on total n<=Q, distribute n by a multinomial with rational mode
probabilities lambda_j/lambda. A dyadic-uniform cumulative sampler with
B_count=ceil(log_2(64Q(r+1)/delta)) bits per draw has total variation error
at most delta/32 over at most Q draws. When lambda=0 the output is the zero
record. Therefore the finite count stage differs from the exact product-
Poisson law at the supplied rational amplitude by at most delta/8.

For two amplitude vectors x,y, the product-Poisson Hellinger affinity is

    exp(-1/2 sum_u (||x_u||/sqrt(2)-||y_u||/sqrt(2))^2).

Using | ||x_u||-||y_u|| |<=||x_u-y_u|| and the Fuchs--van de Graaf
inequality yields

    TV(Pois_x,Pois_y) <= ||x-y||/sqrt(2).

Thus the Euclidean coupling in Section 5 controls the count-law error even
though the finite normal vector is discrete.

7. Total error and bit cost
===========================

Couple the exact accepted core and finite accepted core using Section 4;
their bad-pair probability is at most delta/16. Independently couple their
conditional output Gaussians as in Section 5. The Gaussian tail and endpoint
ambiguity together contribute at most delta/128, and on the good event the amplitude error is at most
3delta/512, so the Poisson-kernel TV contribution is below delta/128. The
finite Poisson stage contributes at most delta/8. The finite rejection cap
contributes at most xi/2. All other interval and uniform-grid errors were
included in the proposal, acceptance, Gaussian, or count budgets above.
Their sum is less than delta+xi. The all-zero branch omits the core and
rejection terms and satisfies the same bound.

Exact rational inversion, Schur complements, LDL factorization, and PSD
certificate checks have polynomial bit complexity in d and input length L;
the denominators and minors have bit length polynomial in d,L. Very small
positive pivots are represented exactly, and square roots are approximated
to absolute (not relative) accuracy. The proposal uses O(m+kappa) elementary
exponential uniforms per attempt. The hard attempt cap is
O(Lambda log(1/xi)); the Poisson cutoff is O(M_out+log(1/delta)); each
multinomial has at most Q draws. The algorithm does not evaluate p_H, Z_S,
the acceptance normalizer alpha, or a determinant oracle. Numerical kappa,
Lambda, M_out, and delta^(-1) are explicit costs; polynomiality in the
binary input length alone is not asserted unless these quantities are
polynomially bounded.

8. N81 specialization and boundaries
=====================================

For the all-mode-noisy N81 family and all sufficiently large R, so that
nu>g/(1-g), the centered post-loss P covariance is C=t(I+E), t=eta nu,
with ||E||<=eps_bar=g/((1-g)nu), d,h=O(R), binary herald counts,
kappa=h=O(R), eta=1/R, and nu=R^(-a) for fixed a>=1. The
positive-count tilted marginal inherits

    eps_eff <= eps_bar/[1+t(1-eps_bar)] <= eps_bar.

For every sufficiently large R with nu>g/(1-g), eps_bar<=1/4, t<=1, and
kappa eps_eff<1/12, the dyadic envelope obeys Lambda<=2. These conditions
hold eventually for each fixed a; no claim about the finite initial range is
needed. The exact-real acceptance tends to one; this dyadic envelope keeps
the finite attempt cap uniformly bounded up to its log(1/xi) factor.
To check the output cap, C°<=C=t(I+E), hence
tr(V)<=tr((C°)_(U,U))<=2|U|t(1+eps_bar)=O(R^(-a)). For t<=1 and
eps_bar<=1/4, write A:=I+E, and set M=A^(-1)+tP_H and M0=I+tP_H. Then
||M^(-1)||<=1+eps_bar<=5/4, ||M0^(-1)||<=1, and
||A^(-1)-I||<=eps_bar/(1-eps_bar)<=4eps_bar/3. The resolvent identity
therefore gives

    ||C°-t(I+tP_H)^(-1)|| <= (5/3)t eps_bar.

The reference matrix is block diagonal between H and U, so
||C°_(U,H_+)||<=(5/3)t eps_bar. Also
lambda_min(S)>=(1-eps_eff)t/(1+t)>=3t/8, whence
||K||_2<=40eps_bar/9. Since K_* is the entrywise absolute sum, its bound
is at most O(R^2 eps_bar) for d,h=O(R). Also K0=m+kappa=O(R), cb=Theta(t),
and T=O(t(R+log(1/delta))). For fixed or inverse-polynomial accuracy,
B=1 eventually and Rz=O(log R). The stated rational norm bounds then give
M_out=O(R log^2 R); sharper factor norms can reduce this conservative cap.
The actual conditional output mean is smaller. The bit
lengths of rational covariance, eta, nu, and the compiler's herald remain
polynomial in R. The sampler therefore has polynomial worst-case bit time
for this noisy conditional law, independent of its rare physical herald
probability, under the stated supplied rational covariance certificate.

This is an efficient sampler for a mathematically specified conditional
count law, not a physical herald-production procedure. For N81 with fixed
inverse-polynomial all-mode noise, the separate positive-P posterior
comparison shows the conditioned output is TV-o(1) from vacuum. Consequently
this sampler makes that specific noisy law easy and vacuum-like; it does not
remove N81's ideal conditional-hardness statement, prove that all noisy
instances are easy, or rule out subleading information in the noisy output.

9. Falsifiers and unresolved audit
===================================

The finite-bit theorem would fail if (i) a continuous uniform cannot be
coupled to the finite cell midpoint with the stated error, (ii) the proposal
tail or accepted-law normalization loses a factor larger than Lambda, (iii)
the Gaussian output factorization uses an uncharged precision oracle, (iv)
the Poisson kernel bound has a different quadrature normalization, or (v)
the count cutoff or categorical total-variation budget is too small. The
proof deliberately makes no claim that a discrete Gaussian is TV-close to a
continuous Gaussian; all such closeness is routed through latent Euclidean
couplings and the Poisson-channel Hellinger bound.

The exact-real likelihood tilt, Gamma proposal, acceptance normalization,
Jensen estimate, and marginal covariance certificate are independently
checked by the programme lead. The finite-bit construction is internally
derived and now explicit, but still awaits an independent proof audit. No
novelty or priority claim is made.

Exact original
