# Does superexponential threshold decay force infinite Morse index?

7Oct2026. Root-assigned structural follow-up. **Status UNKNOWN.** The results below are exact partial deductions and a checked boundary counterfixture; none proves that our constructed operator has infinitely many negative eigenvalues.

## Exact question

Let n≥3, real smooth bounded V→0, H=−Δ+V, and nonzero u∈H²(R^n) solve Hu=0, with |u(x)|≤C_A exp(−A|x|) for every A>0. Must H have infinitely many negative eigenvalues, counting multiplicity? Our power and log constructions supply this decay, and already force at least one negative eigenvalue. An affirmative answer would identify finite Morse index as a stronger structural UC hypothesis, rather than merely requiring absolute derivative bounds.

## What finite index actually implies

**Our reconstruction:** if the negative spectral subspace has finite dimension M, then for some R0

    ∫|∇v|²+∫V|v|²≥0,  v∈H¹_0(R^n\B_(R0)).    (1)

Otherwise, for every radius choose a smooth compactly supported negative-form test outside that radius. Inductively put the next support beyond all previous supports. Cross form terms vanish. The span of any k of these disjoint tests is k-dimensional and negative definite, contradicting finite M by the spectral variational principle. No decay-rate assumption on u is used in this step.

For bounded smooth V, (1) supplies a positive zero-energy solution ψ on the connected exterior, by the Allegretto–Piepenbrink positive-solution principle. Interior elliptic estimates/Harnack give |∇logψ|≤C away from a fixed inner collar; chaining Harnack balls gives exp(−Cr)≲ψ≲exp(Cr), with fixed-point normalization. The ground-state transform v=u/ψ obeys

    div(ψ²∇v)=0,
    Δv+2∇logψ·∇v=0.

It is still superexponentially small. The missing implication is a decay restriction for this signed exterior solution with arbitrary signed inner boundary trace. Whole-space nonnegativity theorems do not supply it.

## A useful threshold nodal bound

**Our deduction, independently also reconstructed by r09:** a zero-energy H² eigenfunction has at most M+1 nodal domains if H has M negative eigenvalues.

For a nodal domain D_i, its Sobolev restriction u_i=u1_(D_i) belongs to H¹, by truncation/approximation of nodal components. Testing Hu=0 with u_i gives q(u_i)=0; disjoint nodal pieces have zero cross form. If there were at least M+2 domains, take a nonzero linear combination of M+1 pieces orthogonal to every negative eigenfunction, leaving one open nodal domain unused. Its form is0 and the operator is nonnegative on the negative spectral complement, so spectral calculus puts this combination in ker H. Bounded V then gives H² regularity. It vanishes on the unused open domain, contradicting local unique continuation. This argument does not assume discrete isolation of the zero eigenvalue or finite multiplicity of its kernel.

This bound is not enough: finite nodal domains need not imply eventual sign, and a domain may form a narrow unbounded channel. We have not proved that a globally superexponential state must have infinitely many nodal domains.

The general elliptic-cylinder extension of that missing implication is actually false: Davey–Jeznach2607.07639v1 Section4 supplies smooth variable-A superexponential states on T²×R with compact V; our bracketing deduction and r09's complete separate gate give finite Morse index. The same threshold Courant proof applies, so that example also has finitely many GLOBAL nodal domains. See `CYLINDER_FINITE_INDEX_DISCRIMINATOR.md` and r09 `spectral/CYLINDER_FINITE_INDEX_GATE.md`. This does not decide A=I on Euclidean R^n; preserve both domain and principal-part hypotheses.

## Exact finite-index boundary counterfixture

r09 supplied the following n3 example; I independently verified both PDE identities symbolically:

    V(x)=−15/(1+r²)²,
    u(x)=x1/(1+r²)^(3/2),
    ψ(x)=(r²−1)/(1+r²)^(3/2).

Then Hu=0 globally; u∈H²∩L² and has polynomial r^(−2) decay. Hψ=0, and ψ>0 for r>1. Ground-state transform directly proves exterior form nonnegativity there. The state has two unbounded half-space nodal domains and mixed sign on every sphere. Any bounded set enclosing a ball has boundary points with both signs. Thus one cannot obtain a constant-sign artificial inner boundary merely from finite index or exterior positivity.

The exact Morse index is1. Put Ω=2/(1+r²), ω=Ω^(1/2), and write a compactly supported test as ωf under stereographic projection to S³. Integration by parts gives

    q(ωf)=∫_(S³)(|∇_(S³)f|²−3f²).

Indeed the conformal Laplacian contributes scalar-curvature term3/4, while V/Ω²=−15/4. The spherical Laplacian eigenvalues are k(k+2): only constants produce negative form, the first harmonics have zero form, and higher harmonics positive form. Functions supported away from the stereographic pole are dense in H¹(S³) because a point has zero H¹ capacity in dimension3, so the form index is precisely1. This is a classical conformal fixture, not a new discovery. The constant spherical trial maps to ω~r^(−1), which is not L²(R³); to exhibit a legitimate negative L² trial use smooth cutoff approximation before taking the limit. No illegal L² constant test is used.

This fixture is **not** a superexponential counterexample to the main question. It only falsifies the tempting intermediate inference finite index ⇒ eventual sign / constant-sign inner boundary.

## Exact prior and failed transfer

Rossi, *The Landis conjecture with sharp rate of decay*, journal2021, Theorem1.5 and full proofpp17–18: for an exterior domain, λ1≥0 requires the additional boundary condition lim_(x→∂Ω)u(x)≥0. In the whole-space case the ratio −u/positive-eigenfunction achieves an interior maximum, so strong maximum principle controls its sign. In an exterior domain that maximum can sit on the inner boundary; the proof uses the sign condition explicitly. The current threshold state does not supply it.

Das–Pinchover2405.11695v1,19May2024, Section6 Theorem6.1: nonnegativity outside compact K plus UCP is combined with constant sign in a neighborhood of ∂K and a comparison to a positive minimal-growth solution. Remark6.2 explicitly calls the boundary-sign condition nontrivial. Its finite-Morse-index discussion does not remove that condition. Primary theorem/proof excerpts read and snapshots preserved; no full-paper reading claimed.

Sirakov–Souplet2609.15821v1 Theorems2.10/4.5 prove whole-space/ball boundary-integral lower bounds under positivity on a larger ball. Applying them to an annulus introduces an uncontrolled inner boundary term; the ball proof's dual solution has zero boundary, whereas u has arbitrary signed trace on the inner boundary. Likewise extending an exterior ψ through the interior and defining a globally nonnegative modified operator changes the equation of u in the compact core. It only yields a compactly forced equation, to which the homogeneous whole-space theorem does not apply.

## Productive next step

Either derive an exterior signed-solution lower bound from finite index, or build an exterior nonnegative operator admitting a signed superexponential zero solution and attach a finite-index interior. The exact nonnegative form/ground-state-transform interface is narrower than arbitrary real bounded V but broader than prescribed nonnegative potentials. A second route would prove that the FI-style construction has negative form tests arbitrarily far out; that would classify this construction, while not proving the universal finite-index theorem. We have not yet controlled the sign/average of its repaired potential well enough to exhibit such tests.

Do not promote infinite negative spectrum or a finite-index Landis theorem until one of these implications is proved. Strong absolute regularity does not acquire form positivity. The smooth all-derivative-decaying construction is internally gated separately; this new structural question remains open in this programme.
