# Cadence law for two diffusion-equivalent burst mechanisms

**Finding.** In the ideal exact-endpoint count experiment, the specified burst pair has a proved pair-specific chi-square/Hellinger expansion and a regularized binary LAN limit. The optimal equal-prior error is asymptotically
\[
e^*(\lambda)=\Phi\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{3\lambda}{2\,41^3}}\right),
\qquad \lambda=\frac{n}{\mu}\sim\frac{T}{V\Delta^2}.
\]
Thus the nontrivial fixed-horizon cadence scale is \(\Delta\asymp V^{-1/2}\). The broad square-root exponent is prior art; the pair-specific coefficient, one-sided support singularity, and the sharply stated measurement interface are the increment. No biological assay has been identified or validated.

The two channels have marks \(1,2,3,4\) and per-volume rate vectors \(k^{(0)}=(1,1,4,0)\), \(k^{(1)}=(0,4,1,1)\). Both have total event rate 6, product drift 15, and variance rate 41, but third cumulant rates 117 and 123. Hence their centered concentration-scaled processes converge to the same \(\sqrt{41}\)-Brownian limit. Only unmarked exact cumulative product counts at equally spaced endpoints are observed; marks and event times are hidden. In reaction-network terms these are zero-order channels ∅ → jP. The rate contrast \((-1,3,-3,1)\) annihilates the mark moments of orders 0, 1, and 2 but not order 3, giving an explicit scalar CLE-generator collision of the kind characterized by Faul, Hoessly, and Xia (Remark 3.5 and Theorem 3.3). Their result motivates the obstruction; it does not give the finite-cadence test below.

There is an important finite-sample asymmetry: \(P_0^\mu(Y=1)=\mu e^{-6\mu}\), while \(P_1^\mu(Y=1)=0\). Thus \(D(P_0^\mu\Vert P_1^\mu)=\infty\) at every \(\mu>0\). The reverse direction is finite, and the exceptional event has probability at most \(n\mu e^{-6\mu}\), negligible when \(n/\mu\) stays bounded and \(\mu\to\infty\). A detector that can report false counts will remove the exact support hole, so its observation kernel must be calibrated.

For the stipulated pair, the proof establishes
\[
\chi^2(P_1^\mu\Vert P_0^\mu)\sim\frac{6}{41^3\mu},\quad
H^2(P_0^\mu,P_1^\mu)\sim\frac{3}{2\,41^3\mu},\quad
D(P_1^\mu\Vert P_0^\mu)\sim\frac{3}{41^3\mu},
\]
where \(H^2=\sum_m(\sqrt{p_1(m)}-\sqrt{p_0(m)})^2\). The missing step in a central Edgeworth-only argument was control of \(p_1(m)^2/p_0(m)\). The pair-specific proof uses the saddle exponent \(F(x)=I_0(x)-2I_1(x)\), whose unique maximum is 0 at \(x=15\), plus explicit low- and high-count bounds. That tail argument closes this pair’s \(L^2(P_0)\) route; it does not extend to arbitrary bounded mark sets.

Conditioning model 0 on \(Y\ne1\) changes the critical product experiment by \(o(1)\) in total variation. The resulting log likelihood converges to \(N(-I/2,I)\) under model 0 and \(N(I/2,I)\) under model 1, where \(I=6\lambda/41^3\). The exact finite-sample Neyman–Pearson test sums the exact log PMF ratios and selects model 0 immediately if any increment is one. The third-Hermite score has the same critical limit.

At a 5% equal-prior limiting error target, \(I=10.8222\), so \(T/(V\Delta^2)\approx124{,}313\) and
\[
\Delta\approx0.002836\sqrt{T/V}.
\]
That corresponds to about \(353\sqrt{TV}\) intervals for one trajectory. If the target is a 5% sum of type-I and type-II errors, the limiting target is \(T/(V\Delta^2)\approx176{,}505\), or \(\Delta\approx0.002380\sqrt{T/V}\). At the nominal scale \(T/(V\Delta^2)=1\), the limiting equal-prior error is still about 0.4981. The exponent is experimentally falsifiable, but the coefficient warns that useful power requires an aggressive cadence and many endpoint reads.

A bounded positive-integer mark pair whose first \(k\ge2\) positive cumulants agree and whose \((k+1)\)-st cumulants first differ has the conditional Hellinger law
\[
H^2\sim \frac{\delta^2}{4(k+1)!\,m_2^{k+1}}\mu^{-(k-1)}.
\]
Under a common lattice span and finite support, the affinity conclusion follows from the local lattice Edgeworth expansion and Chernoff control of the marginal tails. Equal total event rates are not required because fixed low-count discrepancies are exponentially rare. For \(n\) bins, the affinity scale is \(n/\mu^{k-1}\); at fixed \(T\) this gives \(\Delta\asymp V^{-(k-1)/k}\). At finite critical scale the affinity gives rigorous bounds on optimal error, but a Gaussian limit is not claimed generally: bounded marks do not ensure finite chi-square, as the supplied mark-7 counterexample shows. A parity/lattice-span mismatch can even keep total variation bounded away from zero despite cumulant matching.

The same endpoint sample can estimate one unknown common multiplier on both supplied rate shapes: \(\widehat\mu=\bar Y/15\) has absolute error \(O_P(1)\) when \(n/\mu\) tends to a positive constant, and plugging it into the H3 score changes the score by only \(O_P(1)\). Independent, common, mean-zero endpoint read errors with fixed finite sixth moment become 1-dependent differenced errors; the exact H3 identity bounds their total score perturbation by \(O_P(1)\). In either narrow setting the score test retains its leading limit. This does not handle unknown mark proportions, overlapping composite mechanism classes, errors of order \(\sqrt\mu\), growing quantization, saturation, shared drift, or a general dependent-noise likelihood.

## Prior art and interpretation

Duval and Hoffmann’s 2011 paper proves LAN/Fisher-information transitions across sampling scales for a one-parameter symmetric-Bernoulli compound-Poisson model (Theorems 2.2, 2.4, 2.5). Its §3 discusses extension to arbitrary discrete compound laws, but describes that extension as something that could presumably be done if suitable asymptotic expansions were available. It does not explicitly state the first-differing-cumulant testing exponent.

Duval’s 2014 macroscopic-sampling paper studies centered finite-variance compound-Poisson inference as the horizon and sampling interval grow. Its abstract reports a parametric Fisher-information example, impossibility of consistent estimation when \(\Delta_T\) grows faster than \(\sqrt T\), and Gaussian asymptotic-equivalence regimes. Under effective time \(s=Vt\), that is the same broad square-root cadence exponent. These sources rule out a novelty claim for the qualitative information-loss mechanism and \(k=2\) exponent. Exact overlap with the 2014 parametric example has not been reconstructed theorem-by-theorem. The exact pair, support correction, constant, and k-indexed Hellinger coefficient remain a narrow reconstruction with unverified priority, not a priority claim.

The full saddlepoint, cumulant, cadence, finite-bound, nuisance, and sensor arguments are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md). The exact PMF recurrence and floating-point diagnostics are in [exact_cp_audit.py](exact_cp_audit.py) and [exact_cp_audit.json](exact_cp_audit.json); a separate 50-digit check is in [high_precision_cp_check.py](high_precision_cp_check.py) and its JSON output. These calculations are not interval-certified. At \(\mu=100\), affinity-based finite bounds put the optimal equal-prior error only between about 0.017 and 0.129 for \(n/\mu\approx124{,}288\), far too wide to certify 5%; the finite Chebyshev score bound is also weak there.

The molecular interpretation is a continuously maintained source of independent burst-producing complexes. It requires stable replenishment and no depletion, refractory effects, hidden per-bin activity, or unmodeled product background. A real experiment must price endpoint reads, volume and rate calibration, dynamic range, latency, sample preparation, and source replenishment. At the critical scale, reads grow as \(\sqrt V\), while expected chemical events over fixed \(T\) are \(6VT\). The work identifies the exact rate/volume/cadence quantity that must be measured for a test, but does not supply a physical device or measured resource curve.
