0. STATUS, ATTRIBUTION, AND EXACT INTERFACE

Status: internally derived proof of the theorem below, pending independent
hostile review. Not external validation, novelty certification, or a proof
of a complete parallel-repetition theorem. The smooth labels, finite trials,
and synchronization identity are adapted from Zhao Song, ECCC TR26-199,
Lemma 4.1, primary PDF pp.7--11, equations (3)--(11). The replacements of
Schmidt bases by tracial graph projections, arbitrary polar parts, bounded
finite-spectrum approximation, and the explicit all-input POVM interface
are reconstructed here. No new Luna/candidate proof has been read.

Let (M,tau) be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial
STATE tau. No factoriality, finite dimension, or matrix approximation is
assumed. H=L2(M,tau). For v in M, L(v)xi=v xi and R(v)xi=xi v extend to H.
R(v)R(w)=R(wv); R(v)*=R(v*). Left and right algebras commute for ALL v,w.

Let X,Y be finite nonempty input sets, nu a probability law on X times Y,
and a_x,b_y unit vectors in H. Set
  D=sum_(x,y) nu(x,y)||a_x-b_y||_2^2.
Let {A_x^s}_s and {B_y^t}_t be finite POVMs in M. Bob uses R(B_y^t).
The requested target table is
  p_xy(s,t)=<a_x,L(A_x^s)R(B_y^t)a_x>.
The same statement holds for any additional finite coordinate-indexed
families of these POVMs. Their number does not enter the error constant.

THEOREM. For every zeta>0 there are a finite integer l, a finite N, a
single unit resource Omega_N in
  L2((M_l(C) tensor M)^tensor N, Tr_l tensor tau tensor ...),
and POVMs E_x^s in the LEFT algebra and R_N(F_y^t) in the RIGHT algebra,
where E depends only on Alice's own description/input and F only on Bob's,
such that these fixed two algebras commute on EVERY pair of inputs, and
  sum nu(x,y) TV_half(q_xy,p_xy) <= 2048 D^(1/4)+zeta,
  q_xy(s,t)=<Omega_N,L_N(E_x^s)R_N(F_y^t)Omega_N>.
All resource choices are independent of the realized (x,y). The local
protocol keeps its first successful trial and applies the ORIGINAL POVM
on the original M register of that trial after local label erasure.

The trace used on label matrices is unnormalized for convenience. Using
the normalized label trace and rescaling resource vectors gives the same
representation and probabilities. This is a finite matrix amplification
and a finite number of copies of M, not a claim of finite-dimensional
shared entanglement when M is infinite-dimensional.

No trace-preserving output channel onto the original M is asserted.
The bad branches can use DIFFERENT trial indices; they need not produce
a state realizing the original left/right M correlations. The theorem
instead supplies genuine unconditional commuting POVMs on the amplified
resource. A stronger operational losing-event estimate is in Section 8.

1. TRACIAL SPECTRAL COUPLING AND LEFT-MODULUS TRANSPORT

For finite-spectrum self-adjoint c=sum_i lambda_i E_i and d=sum_j mu_j F_j,
put w_ij=tau(E_i F_j). These numbers are nonnegative because
tau(E_i F_j)=tau(E_i F_j E_i), and their row and column sums are tau(E_i)
and tau(F_j). For scalar coefficients r_ij,
  ||sum_ij r_ij E_i F_j||_2^2=sum_ij |r_ij|^2 w_ij.                 (1)
Proof: left orthogonality kills distinct i; cyclicity of tau kills distinct
j. In particular ||c-d||_2^2=sum(lambda_i-mu_j)^2 w_ij. No joint commuting
assumption on c,d is involved. The corresponding spectral-measure identity
for bounded self-adjoint elements follows by step approximation. Equally,
it is the joint spectral calculus of L(c),R(d) on the vector 1 in L2(M).

For arbitrary bounded a,b in M form the SELF-ADJOINT dilations
  Z_a=[[0,a],[a*,0]], Z_b=[[0,b],[b*,0]]
in M_2 tensor M, with trace Tr_2 tensor tau. Scalar absolute value is
1-Lipschitz on R. Applying the preceding identity gives
  || |Z_a|-|Z_b| ||_2^2 <= ||Z_a-Z_b||_2^2=2||a-b||_2^2.
Since |Z_a|=diag(|a*|,|a|), this implies the transport estimate
  || |a*|-|b*| ||_2^2 <= 2||a-b||_2^2.                           (2)
This replaces Song's Schmidt transport bound. It uses neither tensor
Schmidt decomposition nor an affinity--fidelity inequality.

2. FINITE-SPECTRUM LOCAL APPROXIMATION

For any eta>0 every unit vector a in L2(M,tau) has a unit approximation
abar=c u in M, with ||abar-a||_2<=eta, c=|abar*| positive with FINITE
spectrum, and u a partial isometry with uu*=supp(c). This is local to a.

Indeed M is dense in its L2 completion. Approximate a by a nonzero v in M,
normalize v, and write v=|v*|u. Approximate the bounded positive |v*| by a
nonnegative finite-step spectral function, putting a sufficiently small
interval near zero to zero. Its operator-norm error, hence L2 error, is
arbitrarily small. Multiply by u and normalize again. The resulting left
modulus has finite spectrum, and its polar partial isometry is the
restriction of u to the retained support. No unitary extension is needed.

Apply this to every finite a_x,b_y once, using the same eta. Duplicate
descriptions can be assigned the same approximation. Write their unit
approximations abar_x,bbar_y and let
  Dbar=E_nu ||abar_x-bbar_y||_2^2.
By the triangle inequality in the Hilbert direct sum over nu,
  sqrt(Dbar)<=sqrt(D)+2eta, hence Dbar<=2D+8eta^2.                 (3)
The truncation norm eta is a free internal resource-accuracy parameter.
It will be chosen explicitly in Section 7. It introduces no minimum
spectral value, dimension, cardinality, or central-weight error constant.

3. FINITE SMOOTH LABELS AND COMMON RESOURCE

Fix h in (0,1/2). For lambda>0 set s=-log(lambda)/h, k=floor(s), t=s-k,
  g_lambda=cos(pi t/2)|k>+sin(pi t/2)|k+1>.
Set g_0=|o>, an extra label orthogonal to all integer labels. Take one
COMMON finite label interval covering all positive spectral values in the
finite local family. Negative integers must be allowed: in a normalized
tracial algebra a unit L2 vector can have singular values greater than 1.
Let k_min=min floor(-log(lambda)/h), k_max=max floor(-log(lambda)/h),
over all positive values. Label integers run k_min through k_max+1, with
one extra label o, so l=k_max-k_min+3. Set
  T=sum_(k=k_min)^(k_max+1) t_k e_kk tensor 1, t_k=exp(-kh), T|o>=0,
  S=(Tr_l tensor tau)(T^2)=sum_k t_k^2,
  Omega=T/sqrt(S) in L2(K), K=M_l tensor M.                      (4)
This resource depends on the finite family/global parameters, not its
realized input. Its M component is the tracial vector.

For every positive lambda, each T eigenvalue on g_lambda is between
e^-h lambda and e^h lambda. Consequently
  ||(T-lambda)g_lambda||<=2h lambda.                             (5)
The map s->g(s) has speed pi/2 and is globally Lipschitz. Its overlaps are
real and nonnegative. For all lambda,mu>=0,
  mu |1-<g_lambda,g_mu>| <= pi |lambda-mu|/h,
  lambda mu(1-|<g_lambda,g_mu>|^2)
           <=pi^2(lambda-mu)^2/h^2.                             (6)
At a zero coefficient these inequalities follow immediately. For positive
coefficients put x=|log(lambda/mu)|, M0=max(lambda,mu). If x<=h, then
|lambda-mu|>=M0 x/2 and the speed bound proves (6). If x>h, use
|lambda-mu|>=M0 h/2 and the trivial overlap bounds. These arguments use
h<1/2. Zero-label handling will be explicit in the graph projections.

4. GRAPH PROJECTIONS AND EXACT ONE-TRIAL PROBABILITIES

Fix a unit finite-spectrum pair a,b and write c=|a*|, d=|b*|, b=d u,
q_c=supp(c), q_d=supp(d), uu*=q_d. Define partial isometries in K by
  F_c=sum_(integer k) e_(k,o) tensor g_k(c),
  F_d=sum_(integer k) e_(k,o) tensor g_k(d),
where g_k(0)=0: the zero spectral part is OMITTED. Then
  F_c*F_c=e_oo tensor q_c, P_c=F_cF_c* is a projection,
  F_d*F_d=e_oo tensor q_d, P_d=F_dF_d* is a projection.

Alice's successful instrument factor is K_A=F_c* (acting on the LEFT).
Bob's is K_B=F_d(e_oo tensor u) (acting on the RIGHT). Precisely,
  K_A*K_A=P_c, K_BK_B*=P_d.                                    (7)
The second orientation is essential because R is an anti-representation:
R(K_B)*R(K_B)=R(K_BK_B*). These are contractions, including when c,d have
kernels. Their failure factors are (1-P_c)^(1/2), (1-P_d)^(1/2), in their
respective commuting algebras. Each two-outcome instrument is complete.

With tr=Tr_l tensor tau define the UNNORMALIZED weights
  A=||P_c T||_2^2, B=||T P_d||_2^2, C=||P_c T P_d||_2^2.
The success probabilities are A/S, B/S, C/S. Their exact disagreement is
  m=A+B-2C=||P_c T-T P_d||_2^2.                                (8)
To check the cross term, tr(T P_c T P_d)=||P_c T P_d||_2^2 by cyclicity.
Moreover
  e^-2h<=A,B<=e^2h,
  U=A+B-C>=max(A,B)>=e^-2h, U<=S.                              (9)
The last bound is the probability of the union of two commuting events.

Let C_c=F_c(e_oo tensor c)F_c*, C_d=F_d(e_oo tensor d)F_d*.
Estimate (5) gives
  ||P_cT-C_c||_2^2<=4h^2, ||TP_d-C_d||_2^2<=4h^2.
Using (1) and the graph overlap gives the EXACT identity
  ||C_c-C_d||_2^2
   =sum_ij [(lambda_i-mu_j)^2+
       2 lambda_i mu_j(1-|<g_lambda_i,g_mu_j>|^2)]tau(E_iF_j).
Zero eigenspaces are included in this last sum, with zero multiplier.
Together with (2),(6) and the three-term squared triangle inequality,
for Delta=||a-b||_2^2 and r=h^2+Delta/h^2,
  m<=24h^2+6Delta+12pi^2 Delta/h^2 <=128r.                      (10)
Here pi^2<10 and h^2<1/4 suffice for the final numerical bound.

5. LOCAL LABEL ERASURE AND ACTUAL SYNCHRONIZED OUTPUT

The successful joint factor applied to the resource satisfies
  K_A T K_B=e_oo tensor x,
  x=[sum_k t_k g_k(c)g_k(d)]u.                                 (11)
This equation is the actual output of operators in the fixed left/right
algebras, not a choice of an abstract pair-dependent state-preparation
map. The squared norm of x is C. Multiplication by u preserves the norm
of the bracketed expression: its right support is q_d and uu*=q_d.

In the spectral notation of Section 1,
  x=sum_ij beta_ij E_iF_j u,
  beta_ij=<g_lambda_i,T g_mu_j>,
  b=sum_ij mu_j E_iF_j u.
The mu_j=0 terms vanish. For mu_j>0 (including lambda_i=0), (5),(6) give
  |beta_ij-mu_j|^2
       <=8h^2 mu_j^2+2pi^2(lambda_i-mu_j)^2/h^2.
Hence the exact tracial orthogonality identity (1) proves
  ||x-b||_2^2<=8h^2+4pi^2 Delta/h^2<=40r.                      (12)

If C>0, put v=x/sqrt(C). Since b has norm one,
  ||v-b||_2<=2||x-b||_2<=2sqrt(40r).                           (13)
No small-r restriction is needed for this normalization inequality. If
C=0, the successful joint branch has zero probability. In particular,
for r<1/40 it cannot be zero by (12).

After erasure the ORIGINAL POVMs are I_l tensor A_x^s and
R(I_l tensor B_y^t), evaluated on e_oo tensor v. Their conditional table
is exactly <v,L(A_x^s)R(B_y^t)v>. For any two unit vectors the half total
variation of any POVM's outcome laws is at most their Hilbert distance.
One direct proof restricts the difference of their rank-one projectors
to their two-dimensional span: its half trace norm is
sqrt(1-|<v,w>|^2)<=||v-w||. This bound applies to arbitrary H and to the
joint POVM {L(A_x^s)R(B_y^t)} because its effects are positive and sum to I.

6. FINITE FIRST-SUCCESS PROTOCOL AS EXPLICIT COMMUTING POVMS

Use Omega_N=Omega^tensor N and choose
  N=ceil(e^(2h) S log(2/xi)), 0<xi<1.                          (14)
Each party retains its first locally successful trial. Take fixed default
answers s0,t0 on timeout. The complete effects IN K^tensor N are

 E_x^s=sum_(j=1)^N (1-P_c)^tensor(j-1) tensor
       [K_A*(I_l tensor A_x^s)K_A] tensor 1^tensor(N-j)
       +1_(s=s0)(1-P_c)^tensor N,                              (15)

 F_y^t=sum_(j=1)^N (1-P_d)^tensor(j-1) tensor
       [K_B(I_l tensor B_y^t)K_B*] tensor 1^tensor(N-j)
       +1_(t=t0)(1-P_d)^tensor N.                              (16)

They are positive; summing over outcomes telescopes to I. Alice uses
L_N(E_x^s), Bob uses R_N(F_y^t). EVERY E from EVERY description/input
commutes with EVERY R_N(F), without matching the inputs or conditioning
on synchronized success. Bob's reversed factor order in (16) follows
from R(K_B)*R(B)R(K_B)=R(K_B B K_B*).

Let J_A,J_B be the local first-success indices. Independence of trial
resources gives the exact probability of a common retained index:
  w_same=sum_(j=1)^N (1-U/S)^(j-1) C/S
        =(C/U)[1-(1-U/S)^N].                                  (17)
When that index is j, its conditional output is the SAME v from (13),
and all preceding failed trials and subsequent unused trials disappear
from the original-POVM correlation. Consequently the unconditional table
q is a mixture of w_same times the table on v and a genuine bad-branch
outcome table. No postselected correlation is presented as the final q.

Using U-C=m, (9),(14),
  1-w_same=m/U+(C/U)(1-U/S)^N <=e^(2h)m+xi.                    (18)
Also each individual timeout probability is at most xi/2. Thus the
protocol terminates locally with the requested finite error. Combining
(10),(13),(18) gives for the finite-spectrum pair
  TV_half(q,p_b)<=128e r+2sqrt(40r)+xi,                         (19)
where e^(2h)<e<3. If C=0 use TV<=1 and (18), which covers the case too.

7. ORIGINAL DESCRIPTION ERROR AND UNIVERSAL CONSTANT

Use the approximate pair abar_x,bbar_y of Section 2, and let
  r_xy=h^2+||abar_x-bbar_y||_2^2/h^2,
  Rbar=E_nu r_xy<=h^2+(2D+8eta^2)/h^2.
The target bbar_y differs from original a_x by at most
eta+||b_y-a_x||. Averaging (19), using concavity of square root, yields
  E TV_half(q,p_a)
     <=128e Rbar+2sqrt(40Rbar)+xi+eta+sqrt(D).                  (20)

It suffices to treat 0<zeta<=1; for larger zeta use a smaller accuracy.
If D>=1/16, any fixed deterministic-output strategy has error at most
1<=2D^(1/4), so the theorem holds. For 0<D<1/16 set
  h=D^(1/4), eta=h zeta/256, xi=zeta/4.
Then Rbar<=3sqrt(D)+8eta^2/h^2. Applying sqrt(u+v)<=sqrt(u)+sqrt(v),
(20) is at most
  384e sqrt(D)+2sqrt(120)D^(1/4)+sqrt(D)
  +1024e eta^2/h^2+2sqrt(320)eta/h+eta+xi.
Since e<3, sqrt(120)<11, sqrt(320)<18, and sqrt(D)<=D^(1/4), the first
line is below 1175D^(1/4). The second line is below zeta: specifically
it is at most (3/64+9/64+1/512+1/4)zeta<zeta.

If D=0 set h=zeta/128, eta=h zeta/256, xi=zeta/4. Then (20), with
Rbar<=h^2+8eta^2/h^2, is below zeta; the h contribution is at most
3zeta/128+13zeta/128, and the remaining accuracy contributions are
bounded by the same strict bound above. Thus D=0 is covered by finite
resources and arbitrarily small positive zeta, without a zero-width
or infinite-time protocol. The safely rounded universal constant 2048
proves the stated theorem in every case.

8. STRONGER OPERATIONAL LOSING-EVENT ESTIMATE

For each (x,y), allow arbitrary numbers theta_xy(s,t) in [0,1] and let
  L_xy=sum_(s,t)theta_xy(s,t)L(A_x^s)R(B_y^t), 0<=L_xy<=I,
  alpha_xy=<a_x,L_xy a_x>.
The synchronized normalized v from the approximate pair obeys
  ||v-a_x||_2^2
     <=3[4(40r_xy)+||abar_x-bbar_y||_2^2+eta^2]
     <=512r_xy+3eta^2.
Therefore its losing probability is at most
  (sqrt(alpha_xy)+sqrt(512r_xy+3eta^2))^2.
On other branches charge loss one and use (18). Since 128e<384, put
  F_xy=896r_xy+3eta^2+xi.
The actual UNCONDITIONAL protocol loss obeys
  q_xy(theta)<=alpha_xy+2sqrt(alpha_xy F_xy)+F_xy.               (21)
If C=0 there is no synchronized branch; (18) charges the entire outcome
table, and F_xy>=1 suffices for the same inequality.
After averaging, alpha=E alpha_xy and F=896Rbar+3eta^2+xi give
  E q_xy(theta)<=alpha+2sqrt(alpha F)+F.                       (22)
In particular the operational defect is O(h^2+D/h^2) before optimizing,
apart from arbitrarily small finite-resource approximation costs. This
retains the form of the near-perfect-event comparison in Song's argument
without falsely asserting an output state on the original M on bad
branches. A vanishing-eta/xi formulation is permissible only as an
infimum over finite protocols; each chosen protocol above is finite.

9. RESOURCE ACCOUNTING, CENTER OBSTRUCTION, AND LIN'S EXACT SCOPE

If B0 is the largest positive approximating singular value and delta0
the smallest, the construction uses
  l<=ceil(log(B0/delta0)/h)+4,
  S<=e^(2h)B0^2/(1-e^(-2h)),
  N=ceil(e^(2h)S log(2/xi)).
These are finite but need not be dimension- or family-uniform. Local
descriptions, polar decomposition, finite-step spectral calculus, finite
label precision, and original POVM evaluations are supplied operations;
no efficient algorithm or finite representation of an arbitrary vN
algebra is claimed. The ERROR constant alone is uniform. Nonseparable
M causes no obstruction because only finitely many supplied elements and
the standard L2 representation are used.

The fixed-M TP Kraus sampler obstruction is valid but inapplicable here.
If its Kraus factors lie in M, any central projection z obeys
  sum K_j* z K_j=z sum K_j*K_j=z,
so its expectation cannot change. For M=C^2 with equal trace and target
unit vectors sqrt(2)e_0 or sqrt(2)e_1, a common resource with fixed central
weights cannot be converted by such channels to both targets. By contrast
our first-success selection can inspect independent central samples. In
this example Alice and Bob requesting the SAME target have identical
success predicates on every copy; success probability is 1/2 per trial,
and failure after N copies is exactly 2^-N. The selected trial has the
requested central state. The resource remains input-independent and all
input effects still lie in commuting abelian left/right algebras.

Lin arXiv:2304.01940v3, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2, PDF p.12, states
that the l1 CLOSURE of finite-tracially-embeddable correlations is C_qc
for each FINITE input/output alphabet. The standard model in that source
has a bounded positive sigma in M and vector sigma|tau>; this is a
subclass of the descriptions allowed here. It does NOT assert matrix
approximability, exact realization of every correlation, bounded resource
cost, or preservation of postselected state distances by table density.

If a finite-game upper bound has been proved for EVERY finite-tracial
strategy using this sampler and all its OTHER steps, with the same
numerical bound, Lin's closure transfers it to C_qc because game payoff
is a continuous linear functional of a finite correlation table. For a
fixed finite repeated game the same observation applies independently.
The sampler plus Lin alone does NOT prove the rest of Song's
postselection, conditioning, state alignment, or parallel-repetition
theorem in the commuting model. Those remain separately charged gates.
