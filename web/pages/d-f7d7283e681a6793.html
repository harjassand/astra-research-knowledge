Frontier synthesis: what changed, what survived testing, and where to work next
My strongest recommendation is to pursue output-local tensor algorithms: algebraic methods that compute the requested part of a large computation without paying to reconstruct everything else.

This is not just a thematic connection. Working through the new 3SUM/APSP paper, I derived a candidate strengthening of its partial-matrix-product construction, obtained explicit parameters, and checked the relevant inequalities with exact arithmetic. The improvement is modest; it is not itself a foundational breakthrough. But it provides a concrete starting point for a much larger question: can we develop a general algebraic theory that preserves inexpensive access to selected outputs?

The strongest competing opportunity is quantum locally testable codes combined with encoded computation and gap amplification. Its potential consequence is enormous, but several apparently straightforward combinations fail: some are already in the literature, and others run into precise locality or dimension obstructions.

The connection
Three strands now meet:

Partial matrix multiplication has a long history, including Schönhage’s work and Vrana’s more general treatment of partial and weighted multiplication tensors. It is therefore not new to propose “studying partial tensors.”

Why now?
Recent work supplies an explicit mechanism whose cost can be dissected into separate encoding, preprocessing, and query terms. Older partial-tensor theory supplies meaningful comparison theorems rather than just analogies. The exact-tail calculation shows that careful attention to these terms can already improve a parameter range.

What exact obstacle remains?
Global mixing of outputs.

An algebraically efficient decomposition may force the algorithm to recover a large fraction of its intermediate computations even when the requested output set is small. A successful construction needs to preserve enough coordinate structure for sparse recovery without sacrificing the algebraic compression that created the speedup.

That is the missing ingredient—not merely a lower-rank tensor.

What should be tried next?
First, independently verify the candidate corollary’s complete finite construction and cost accounting. Then search for a new identity or composition rule that improves the full encoding–preprocessing–query trade-off, using the existing construction as the control.

The acceptance criterion should be unforgiving: correctness for arbitrary masks, explicit encoding and decoding circuits, and a complete symbolic cost bound. Reject identities whose attractive rank is cancelled by expensive output reconstruction.

My conclusion: the evidence supports a newly actionable foundational research program, not an announced revolution. The most valuable positive result here is a small, explicit improvement that survives exact checks; the most valuable negative results identify combinations that cannot work as advertised.
