Cycle 3 result: equal-information prediction from one raw HMM trajectory
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally proved theorem and lower-bound construction; no novelty claim.

Scope and interface
-------------------
Let Y_t take values in a finite alphabet A. The unknown data-generating process is a stationary HMM with finite hidden state space of size at most D, unknown transition and emission probabilities, and one observed training path X=Y_1:T. No hidden labels, resets, conditional/Hankel queries, task dictionary, or spectral/mixing-margin promise is supplied. A learner may use private randomness independent of the HMM. The prediction task is to predict a finite future block W=Y_{T+1:T+L} from the full observed history X. Its oracle benchmark is the stationary-history kernel K_theta^{T,L}(.|x)=Law_theta(W|Y_1:T=x).

The core theorem is independent of this particular task: whenever a representation and its predictor are measurable functions/kernels of the same raw observations and the same side information, a full-path learner can simulate them exactly. Thus the representation cannot improve statistical risk over the strongest unrestricted equal-information learner. A separate HMM construction quantifies why an accuracy guarantee uniform over all such models needs a dependence on horizon and available path length.

Theorem 1 (full-path simulation; exact equal-information boundary)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Let theta range over any statistical model, let X have law P_theta, and let Z be any task input available to both procedures (it may be a function of X or an external input with the same conditional law/access). A randomized representation predictor is any Markov kernel that, after seeing X,Z, produces an action Q, possibly by first constructing S=A(X) and then using Q~G(.|S,Z). Let loss ell(theta,Z,Q) be any measurable nonnegative loss for which the expectations exist. A learner that retains X can generate S with the same A, run G with the same conditional randomness, and output the same Q. Therefore, for every theta,

  Risk_theta(full-path simulator) = Risk_theta(representation predictor).

Consequently, if F is the class of all predictors with access to X and R is any subclass factoring through a representation, then

  inf_{f in F} sup_{theta in Theta} Risk_theta(f)
    <= inf_{r in R} sup_{theta in Theta} Risk_theta(r),

and the same pointwise dominance holds for expected KL, TV, proper scoring rules, or any other loss. It also holds for data-dependent certificates emitted by the representation: the full-path learner can reproduce the same certificate. For an interactive sequence of later tasks, the full-path learner can rerun the representation algorithm and reproduce the same transcript, provided both receive the same task inputs and randomization.

Proof. Couple the representation learner's private random tape U with the simulator's tape. Given (X,Z,U), both procedures execute the same measurable map and hence return the same Q almost surely. Equality of loss and expectation follows for every theta. Taking infima and suprema gives the minimax inequality. If the representation randomization is specified as a kernel rather than a fixed random tape, sample from the same kernel. No assumption on D, identifiability, HMM structure, or sample size is used. QED.

Interpretation: under equal information and no resource restriction that excludes simulation, the statistical advantage is exactly zero. A positive claim must identify an actual resource or interface asymmetry (for example, smaller persistent memory, lower serving latency, or inability to revisit raw observations). The theorem is not a claim that retaining raw data is memory-efficient.

Theorem 2 (one-path lower bound for finite-horizon HMM prediction)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix any integers L>=2 and T>=1 and define

  r_0=1/(8L),   r_1=1/(2L),
  lambda_i=1-2r_i,   c=(3/4-exp(-1))/8 > 0.

For i in {0,1}, let Z_t in {-1,+1} be the stationary symmetric two-state Markov chain that flips sign with probability r_i at each step. Independently, let E_t be iid with P(E_t=+1)=3/4 and P(E_t=-1)=1/4, and set Y_t=Z_t E_t. This is a stationary binary-output, two-hidden-state HMM. Its transition and emission parameters are unknown to the learner. Let P_i^T be the law of X=Y_1:T, and define W=Y_{T+1:T+L}. The prediction kernel is

  K_i^{T,L}(.|x)=Law_i(W | Y_1:T=x).

For every predictor q(.|X) over A^L (randomized predictors included), define

  R_i^TV(q)=E_{X~P_i^T} TV(q(.|X), K_i^{T,L}(.|X)),
  R_i^KL(q)=E_{X~P_i^T} KL(K_i^{T,L}(.|X) || q(.|X)),

with natural logarithms. If

  T-1 <= c^2 L / 8,

then

  max_i R_i^TV(q) >= c/4,
  max_i R_i^KL(q) >= c^2/8.

Numerically c is about 0.0477651, so the displayed TV and KL constants are positive absolute constants. The conclusion holds even if the learner is told the two candidate HMMs and only has to infer which generated the path. Therefore it also holds in the requested interface, where no candidate dictionary is supplied. It holds with D=2 supplied; hence for every class allowing at least two hidden states, knowing the state cap alone does not remove the obstruction.

Proof. The emission noise has mean m=1/2, and the hidden correlation is E[Z_t Z_{t+k}]=lambda_i^k. Thus E[Y_t Y_{t+L}]=lambda_i^L/4. For a uniform sign pair with correlation rho, the joint law is p(a,b)=(1+rho ab)/4 and the TV distance between two such laws is half the absolute correlation difference. Hence

  TV(Law_0(Y_T,Y_{T+L}), Law_1(Y_T,Y_{T+L}))
    = (lambda_0^L-lambda_1^L)/8
    >= (3/4-exp(-1))/8 = c,

because (1-1/(4L))^L >= 1-1/4=3/4 and (1-1/L)^L <= exp(-1). Projecting the joint laws of (X,W) to (Y_T,Y_{T+L}) cannot increase TV, so

  TV(Law_0(X,W),Law_1(X,W)) >= c.                            (1)

The initial hidden-state distributions under the two models are both uniform. The KL divergence between hidden paths of length T is (T-1) kl(r_0||r_1). For p=r_0 and r_1=4p,

  kl(p||4p) = p log(1/4)+(1-p)log((1-p)/(1-4p))
             <= 3p/(1-4p)=3/(8L-4) <= 1/(2L),

where the first term is nonpositive and log(1+x)<=x; the last inequality holds for L>=2. Passing the hidden path through the common emission channel cannot increase KL. Pinsker therefore yields

  TV(P_0^T,P_1^T) <= sqrt(KL(P_0^T||P_1^T)/2)
                    <= sqrt((T-1)/(4L))
                    <= c/sqrt(32) < c/4.                    (2)

Write J_i for the joint law of (X,W), and let K_i^{T,L} be its conditional future kernel given X. Disintegration and the triangle inequality for TV give

  TV(J_0,J_1) <= E_{P_0^T} TV(K_0^{T,L}(.|X),K_1^{T,L}(.|X)) + TV(P_0^T,P_1^T).

Using (1) and (2), the mean conditional-kernel separation under P_0^T is at least c-c/4=3c/4. For each realized path x, put f_i(x)=TV(q(.|x),K_i^{T,L}(.|x)). The triangle inequality implies f_0(x)+f_1(x)>=TV(K_0^{T,L}(.|x),K_1^{T,L}(.|x)). Also, for any [0,1]-valued f, |E_{P_0^T}f-E_{P_1^T}f|<=TV(P_0^T,P_1^T). Consequently,

  R_0^TV+R_1^TV
    = E_0 f_0 + E_1 f_1
    >= E_0(f_0+f_1)-TV(P_0^T,P_1^T)
    >= 3c/4-c/4=c/2.

At least one TV risk is at least c/4. Pinsker gives KL(K_i^{T,L}||q)>=2 TV(K_i^{T,L},q)^2 pointwise. Jensen then gives R_i^KL>=2(R_i^TV)^2, proving the KL claim. Randomization is handled by adjoining the same independent random tape to both path laws; it does not increase their TV distance. QED.

What this lower bound does and does not say
-------------------------------------------
The obstruction is slow information acquisition relative to the requested prediction horizon: the hidden-chain relaxation gap is 1-lambda_0=1/(4L), and a path with T-1<=c^2 L/8 transitions does not reliably distinguish the alternatives. The emissions are noisy but linearly independent: the binary emission matrix has singular values 1 and 1/2. Each transition matrix is full rank (its eigenvalues are 1 and lambda_i). Thus the construction has no transition or emission rank degeneracy; the hidden-chain mixing gap shrinks with L. The task predicts the future block conditioned on the entire observed past X. The lower bound does not establish impossibility at fixed L as T grows, nor a rate under a supplied mixing margin.

D analysis. The simulation theorem needs no D and remains true whether the cap is given to both methods or neither. The lower bound uses exactly two hidden states and therefore survives the strongest choice D=2 supplied to the learner; an unknown cap cannot help. Exact hidden-state count is not observable in general: a one-state iid Bernoulli(1/2) model and a two-state HMM with both emissions Bernoulli(1/2), under any stationary hidden transition matrix, induce the same observed iid law. Thus a supplied cap is different from an identifiable minimal-state count. If a guarantee is to scale with a learned state number, model selection cost and assumptions must be stated separately.

Resource ledger: a concrete persistent-memory comparison
-------------------------------------------------------
This example is intentionally a restricted positive case, not a general rank-free HMM theorem. Let Y_1,...,Y_T be iid Bernoulli(p), p unknown in the known interval [1/4,3/4]. This is a stationary HMM with one hidden state, no candidate dictionary, and no reset. The task is to reuse a one-step predictor for K future draws.

  Legal training data: one T-bit path; no other observations or queries.
  Representation: the count N=sum_t Y_t, requiring ceil(log2(T+1)) persistent bits.
  Prediction: qhat=clip(N/T,[1/4,3/4]); the exact rational is determined by N and known T.
  Training working memory: ceil(log2(T+1)) bits; one counter update per observation.
  Validation queries: zero; the statement below is a uniform expected-risk bound.
  Persistent precision: N exactly represents qhat; no floating-point approximation is charged.
  Reuse: the same qhat serves K one-step tasks; no task drift is allowed in this example.

For all p in [1/4,3/4],

  E_p KL(Ber(p)||Ber(qhat)) <= 4/(3T),
  E_p [sum_{k=1}^K KL(Ber(p)||Ber(qhat))] <= 4K/(3T).

Indeed, KL(P||Q)<=chi-square(P||Q), so KL(Ber(p)||Ber(q)) <= (p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]. Here qhat is clipped into [1/4,3/4], making qhat(1-qhat)>=3/16. Projection onto an interval containing p cannot increase distance, and E(N/T-p)^2=p(1-p)/T<=1/(4T). Multiplication gives 4/(3T); linearity gives the K-task bound.

A literal baseline that must preserve the entire binary path for replay needs at least T bits: there are 2^T possible paths, and exact reconstruction requires distinct persistent states. It can refit the same qhat on every later task, with exactly the same statistical risk; its archive costs T bits, compared with ceil(log2(T+1)) bits for the reusable count. This is a real memory saving against exact raw-path retention, not a statistical gain. A competent full-information learner may compute N while reading X and discard the path, reaching the same representation and risk. Theorem 1 says precisely why no stronger statistical advantage follows. The example does not prove that every HMM admits a small predictive statistic.

In particular, for any persistent budget B with ceil(log2(T+1))<=B<T, the counter representation fits while an exact-replay archive cannot. This is a lower bound only for strategies required to preserve the full path losslessly; a learner allowed to summarize during the initial pass can also retain N in the same B bits.

Cost boundary and failures
-------------------------
* Main HMM comparison: both methods receive the same T raw symbols and task input; no setup or validation queries are used. Under unrestricted computation/storage, simulation cost and statistical risk are exactly equal. For an arbitrary representation, its bit size, training time, precision, and serving cost are unspecified and cannot be asserted as savings.
* Resource example: exact archive memory is T bits; learned reusable state is ceil(log2(T+1)) bits. The sample size T is paid once, validation cost is zero, exact counter precision is charged, and the K-task loss is explicit. If the baseline may compress/discard raw data, it ties the representation; if exact replay is a requirement, it pays T bits.
* Failure/counterexample to horizon-uniform certification: Theorem 2 gives a fixed positive expected-KL lower bound whenever T-1<=c^2 L/8, even for a supplied D=2 and a regular, full-rank emission matrix. Therefore no procedure can promise risk below c^2/8 uniformly over this HMM family at those (T,L). No preprocessing of the same path can evade this lower bound.
* Theorem 2 relies on stationary initialization and the explicit slow-switching pair. It is not an impossibility theorem for all HMM prediction, and it does not prohibit useful compression when T is large relative to mixing time or when D=1.

Primary-source comparison (bounded; no priority/novelty claim)
-------------------------------------------------------------
1. Daniel Hsu, Sham M. Kakade, and Tong Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” JCSS 78(5), 1460–1480 (2012), primary paper: https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~djhsu/papers/hmm.pdf ; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413 . Their Condition 1 assumes positive stationary probabilities and full column rank of observation and transition matrices. Their sample complexity depends polynomially on inverse singular values; their algorithm is stated as LEARNHMM(m,N), with m supplied. The main analysis uses independent sampled sequences to avoid Markov mixing concentration; the paper notes that for one long sequence the effective sample size should be discounted by the mixing rate. This is not a rank-free, single-path, no-mixing-margin certificate. Relevant primary-paper locations: §§2.3, 4.2.1–4.2.2, and Appendix B.
2. Elisabeth Gassiat, Alice Cleynen, and Stéphane Robin, “Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general identifiable,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657 . Theorem 2.1 assumes known state count k, full-rank hidden transition matrix, and linearly independent emissions; it identifies parameters from the population law of three consecutive observations up to state permutation. This is population identifiability under conditions, not a finite-one-path risk or memory guarantee.

UNKNOWN
-------
1. A matching general upper bound for stationary-history or full-history HMM prediction from one raw path, uniform over unknown D and unknown parameters without a mixing, observability, or stability margin, is not established here.
2. A constructive compact predictive representation with a high-probability, data-computable risk certificate for the full requested HMM class is unknown. The risk calculations above are either a minimax lower bound or a uniform expectation bound on the one-state Bernoulli subclass; neither is an empirical certificate for arbitrary HMMs.
3. The minimum memory/computation frontier for the full path interface is unknown. The exact simulation theorem only rules out statistical improvement when the full-path baseline's resource budget permits simulation. A strict resource advantage requires a formal budget and an appropriately constrained baseline.
4. State-number adaptation without a supplied D, under the same one-path interface and without additional identifiability/stability assumptions, remains unknown.

No claim of novelty is made. The simulation result is a direct class-inclusion/simulation argument; the HMM lower bound is a self-contained Le Cam-style two-point construction. Literature coverage here is deliberately limited to the primary HMM learning and identifiability sources listed above.
