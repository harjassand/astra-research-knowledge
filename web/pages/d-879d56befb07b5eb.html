1. Xiao-Liang Qi and Daniel Ranard, “Emergent classicality in general
multipartite states and channels,” Quantum 5, 555 (2021),
arXiv:2001.01507v3 (last revised 2021-09-27), DOI
https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2021-09-28-555
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2001.01507
Version record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01507 (v3, lines 8-25).
Used passages in arXiv HTML extraction:
  - Theorem 1 and channel assumptions / exact excluded-set bound / common
    POVM / diamond norm: lines 114-138 (especially 114-126).
  - Theorem 2 state version and one-way LOCC_leftarrow norm: lines 152-175.
  - Proposition 1 entropy-budget chain rule and proof: lines 204-243;
    the greedy output-block measurements and S(A) budget are lines 219-241.
  - State-to-channel Choi conversion, dimension factors, and diamond-LOCC
    alternative: lines 255-290.
The pointwise channel theorem is the best direct prior mechanism here. It
has full-domain diamond control and one measurement common to all retained
outputs, at the stated d_A^6 ln(d_A) output-exclusion cost. Its state theorem
is weaker in norm and has a different information interface.

2. Fernando G.S.L. Brandão, Marco Piani, and Paweł Horodecki, “Generic
emergence of classical features in quantum Darwinism,” Nature Communications
6, 7908 (2015), DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms8908.
Primary article plus theorem text: supplementary/full PDF
https://bpfoundations.org/qw2/2014_Brandao_Generic_Emergence_of_Classical_Features.pdf
Source PDF pages 3-4; extracted theorem text lines 194-213 (Theorem 1),
lines 262-286 (interpretation, common POVM, dimension dependence), and
lines 304-321 (subset-output extension, Theorem 2). Theorem 1 says that for
any channel from an input of dimension d_A to n output fragments and any
0<delta<1, a common POVM approximates all but delta n single-fragment
channels in diamond norm by
  [27 ln(2) d_A^6 log_2(d_A)/(n delta^3)]^(1/3).
Prepared states may depend on the fragment. This is an earlier finite
common-measurement theorem, but it is weaker in n than Qi-Ranard's blanket
form for the present use. It likewise has dimension dependence, assumes a
single many-output channel, and is not controlled by R(E).

3. Marius Lemm and Mark M. Wilde, “Information-theoretic limitations on

https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.012304.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1608.07569
Version record: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07569 (v2, lines 8-25).
Used passages in arXiv HTML extraction:
  - Definition 3, state-specific approximate n-broadcast/clone: lines 70-83.
  - Theorem 4, pairwise relative-entropy loss and trace bound: lines 86-99.
  - Theorem 5, pair-dependent recovery channel and exact recovery of the
    cloned reference state: lines 115-135.
  - Theorems 6-7, universal-cloner/symmetrized-partial-trace special case:

This is the closest general approximate no-broadcasting entropy result in
the reviewed set, but its assumptions require one input to be product
cloned and its conclusion is pairwise recovery, not EB approximation of a
full state family.

Search exclusions that matter to interpretation:
  - Lemm-Wilde's “broadcasting” is for one fixed input state at a time, with
    a second state required to be product-cloned; not one unknown-member-
    independent channel with a common EB decoder for all of E.
  - The Janzing-Steudel and local-discord literature optimize average
    Holevo/mutual information or fidelity for a fixed prior/state; they do
    not bound sup_{rho in E} output trace distance to identity.
  - The state-level Markov-blanket result is one-way LOCC and a fixed
    ensemble on its retained reference. Its ordinary trace upgrade has
    dimension factors. The full channel result removes the norm ambiguity
    by paying explicit dimension powers.
