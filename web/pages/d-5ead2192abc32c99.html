## Exact interface and statement

Let F have characteristic zero and let f be a proper recognizable series with a valid supplied state bound m:

\[
 f_w=uB_wv,\qquad B_i\in\operatorname{Mat}_m(F),\quad 1\le i\le n.
\]

The learner has n,m and an exact oracle returning the **entire final output matrix** f(X) for any rational jointly nilpotent tuple X. It receives no hidden transitions, coefficients, gates or equivalence oracle. Proper-series semantics mean the finite sum \(\sum_w f_w X_w\) at such a tuple. An arbitrary syntactic rational formula may have narrower inverse-gate domains and is not automatically this oracle.

Put d=2nm and form the same Volterra jet matrices

\[
 (T_i)_{r,q}=\frac{(-1)^{r-q-1}}{r i^{r-q}},\qquad 0\le q<r<d.
\]

**Internally reconstructed detector.** If f is nonzero, then f(ell T) is a nonzero full matrix for some ell in the m-element grid {1,...,m}. Every evaluation exists by joint nilpotence. Hence the concatenated feature \((f(T),f(2T),...,f(mT))\) is injective on any linear series space sharing an m-state representation. This is a full-response theorem, not a first-column theorem.

The alternative marker \(S_{m+1}\otimes T_i\) also detects every nonzero series as a full matrix, in dimension 2nm(m+1). The direct sum of the m scalar tuples has dimension 2nm². We account below for the difference between m separate responses and one much larger nominal full response.

## Independent full derivation

Work over F(lambda), let q(z)=prod_i(z+i), and set

\[
 A_\lambda(z)=\sum_i\frac{\lambda B_i}{z+i}=\frac{\lambda P(z)}{q(z)},
 \quad \deg_zP\le n-1,\quad q(0)=n!\ne0.
\]

The formal solution G'=A_lambda G, G(0)=I exists and has coefficients in F[lambda][[z]]. Each z^r coefficient has lambda degree≤r. The same property holds for G(t)^(-1), so the two-variable kernel below can be specialized at any scalar lambda without a hidden denominator in lambda.

On a polynomial input a, the state vector Y=(I−sum_i lambda B_i J_i)^(-1) v a satisfies Y'=v a'+A_lambda Y. Variation of constants followed by formal integration by parts gives

\[
 f(\lambda J)a(z)=uv\,a(z)+\int_0^zK_\lambda(z,t)a(t)\,dt,
 \qquad K_\lambda(z,t)=uG(z)G(t)^{-1}A_\lambda(t)v.
\]

If uv≠0, the matrix diagonal detects f at every grid point, so assume uv=0. Select a nonzero finite homogeneous word-length slice f_h. The coefficient of lambda^h in f(lambda J)1 is its nonzero hyperlog image by the source's finite-word faithfulness. Thus K_lambda is nonzero. This step retains the formal length variable; unmarked infinite hyperlog faithfulness is false.

Define rational rows R_0=u and R_(j+1)=R_j'+R_j A_lambda. Write R_j=Z_j/q^j. Their covariant derivative D(R)=R'+R A_lambda obeys D(cR)=c'R+cD(R). The first dependency among the consecutive rows occurs by index m. It makes their F(lambda,t)-span invariant under D, and every later row belongs to the first m rows. Since

\[
 b_j(\lambda,t):=(\partial_z^jK_\lambda)(t,t)=R_j(t)A_\lambda(t)v,
\]

if all b_j for j<m were zero, every derivative in z at z=t would vanish, contradicting K_lambda≠0. Thus some j<m has b_j≠0.

The numerator recurrence

\[
 Z_{j+1}=qZ_j'-j q' Z_j+Z_j\lambda P
\]

proves deg_t Z_j≤j(n−1) and deg_lambda Z_j≤j. The nonzero numerator N=Z_j lambda P v of b_j=N/q^(j+1) therefore has t degree≤(j+1)(n−1), lambda degree≤j+1≤m, and is divisible by lambda. Choose a nonzero t coefficient of N/lambda; its degree in lambda is≤m−1. It cannot vanish at all m distinct nonzero rational integers 1,...,m. For some ell, b_j(ell,t)≠0 and its t order is≤(j+1)(n−1).

No rational row-dependency coefficient is specialized. Only the actual numerator N and the formal differential equation are specialized, so possible lambda poles in dependency coefficients are irrelevant.

Write z=t+h. The h^j coefficient of K_ell(t+h,t) is b_j(ell,t)/j!, so K_ell has a nonzero term of total degree≤j+(j+1)(n−1)=n(j+1)−1≤nm−1. Its lowest nonzero homogeneous part, of degree p≤nm−1, remains nonzero under the invertible change between (h,t) and (z,t). Write it as

\[
 K_p(z,t)=\sum_{b=0}^p c_bz^{p-b}t^b.
\]

Applying this homogeneous kernel to a(z)=z^a gives the coefficient

\[
 z^{p+a+1}\sum_{b=0}^p\frac{c_b}{b+a+1}.
\]

For a=0,...,p, these are the rows of an invertible rational Cauchy matrix. Equivalently the rational function sum_b c_b/(s+b+1) has a numerator of degree≤p; if it vanished at all p+1 nonnegative integers it would be identically zero, forcing every c_b=0 by distinct poles. Some a≤p therefore gives a nonzero matrix entry. Its input degree a<d and output degree p+a+1≤2nm−1<d both lie inside the d-dimensional jet. Other homogeneous kernel degrees lie on different matrix diagonals and cannot cancel this entry. Hence f(ell T)≠0.

For the shorter marker, select a nonzero lambda^h coefficient of N, with h≤m. Its rational t coefficient has the same order bound. The homogeneous-kernel argument detects that specific lambda^h in the full matrix f(lambda T). It survives reduction modulo lambda^(m+1), so f(S_(m+1) tensor T)≠0. **We do not claim that all entries of f(lambda T) have degree≤m**; the proof finds one sufficiently low marker coefficient.

## Minimal residual acquisition and precise queries

Let r≤m be f's true left-residual/Hankel rank. For f_p(s)=f(ps), define

\[
 \Phi(f_p)=\operatorname{concat}_{\ell=1}^m
 \operatorname{vec}\bigl(f_p(\ell T_1^T,...,\ell T_n^T)\bigr),
 \qquad L=m d^2=4n^2m^3.
\]

Transposition reverses words: f_p(ell T^T)=rev(f_p)(ell T)^T. Every linear combination of residuals has the same hidden B_i and an arbitrary initial row. Every reversed combination consequently has the same B_i^T, initial v^T and an arbitrary final column, with m states. The grid detector is injective on this entire residual span. **A 2m comparison bound is unnecessary here**; it would apply to an unrelated external hypothesis versus target, whereas all these residuals share common transitions.

For full-width extraction of a prefix p of length k, use k prefix blocks of width d with its letter-specific I_d edges, followed by suffix ell T_i^T. The output's source/suffix block is precisely f_p(ell T^T). At k=0 query the suffix directly. All queries are strictly upper triangular. The same breadth-first independent-residual construction as in the original note gives a minimal r-state representation, with at most m(1+nr) output queries, dimension≤d(r+1), and feature length L. The first diagonal entry of the ell=1 feature supplies each residual's constant coefficient.

There is a useful row-batching tradeoff. Let 1≤b≤d. For each group of at most b suffix seeds, use k prefix blocks of that group width with identity edges. The final prefix edge is the b×d row selector P into the suffix. The source/suffix block equals P f_p(ell T^T), providing those rows of the full feature. Group all d rows and all m grid values. The empty residual needs only m full suffix queries. Every nonempty queried prefix needs m ceil(d/b) queries, each of dimension≤d+kb. Therefore

\[
 Q\le m+mn r\lceil d/b\rceil,\qquad
 N_{\max}\le d+rb.
\]

Choosing b=d gives the full-width mode. Choosing b=1 gives scalar prefix chains and m+mdnr queries with dimension≤d+r. Since d=2nm, choose the known batch width **b=2n** to obtain exactly m row groups and

\[
 Q\le m+nm^2r,\qquad N_{\max}\le2n(m+r)\le4nm.
\]

Thus deterministic polynomial acquisition can keep every query dimension linear in the state bound for fixed n, by charging more calls. This uses the stronger full-response oracle; it is not a passive sample count.

## Complete charged costs

Every query returns its full square matrix. For batch width b, the total nominal returned field entries are at most

\[
 m d^2+mn r\lceil d/b\rceil(d+rb)^2.
\]

For b=2n and r≤m this is O(n³m⁵). Dense query descriptions have an additional n-factor; allocation/processing of their zeros remains part of the implementation. The feature dimension is L=4n²m³. Straightforward repeated Gaussian coordinate solves use O(nr³L) field operations, at worst O(n³m⁶), plus query construction and full-response processing. The reconstructed output has nr²+2r field entries. The oracle's execution time and per-call latency are charged separately.

Over Q, processing is polynomial in n,m and the total encoded response length. It cannot be bounded independently of arbitrary hidden coefficient heights. A promised hidden rational height H gives a sufficient polynomial response bound: query entries have polynomial rational bit length, common hidden denominators have polynomial bit length, and the support graph cuts off words after at most d+k letters even though a batch query may have dimension d+kb. Product/sum and determinant bounds then give polynomial response and coordinate heights in n,m,H. Positivity, stochastic normalization and numerical filtering stability do not follow from exact minimal realization.

Compared with our original marked first-column learner, this reduces the worst query dimension from O(n²m⁴) to O(nm) in the b=2n mode, increases query count from O(nr) to O(nm²r), and reduces the nominal returned-payload bound from O(n⁵m⁹) to O(n³m⁵). These are worst-case upper-bound comparisons of two implementations at the same strong interface, not demonstrated speed superiority on small fixtures. A supplied realization already has polynomial white-box reachable-space algorithms. Output-only NC ABP reconstruction is old; deterministic polynomial hitting and this explicit cyclic residual compilation remain the relevant possible improvements. Priority is uncleared.

## Adversarial alias and executable gate

The proper series R=AB−B(I+A)^(-1)A(I+B) is nonzero: its AB and BA coefficients are 1 and −1. Yet R(J_1,J_2)=0 as a full formal operator. To see this, for g=J_2 a put h=(z+2)g/(z+1). Direct differentiation shows (I+J_1)h=J_1(I+J_2)a, with zero initial constants. Then J_2h=J_1J_2a, proving the identity. Nilpotent jet quotients preserve it in every dimension. A five-state representation is implemented in the checker. It need not be minimal.

Because the learner substitutes T^T, its invisible fixture must be **rev(R)**. The first replay mistakenly expected R(T^T)=0 and failed that assertion; the correction transposes the hidden transitions and exchanges boundary vectors. This fixture-orientation failure is preserved in `SCALAR_GRID_INITIAL_FAILURE.json`; no detector theorem or learner extraction changed. It concretely checks the reversal convention that the proof requires.

`work/frontier_crossfield/cycle19/check_scalar_grid_acquisition.py` implements the full-width and batched feature learner using only the supplied callable oracle and n,m. Its hidden oracle is an independently coded triangular block-resolvent solver. Each fixture receives separate exact reachable-space all-word equality and finite Hankel minimality certificates. Results and literal numerical query/response costs are saved under this worker's results directory. Finite tests certify those fixtures; the universal statements rest on the reconstructed derivation above.
