# Finite-memory compression at a qubit spectral collision

**Research derivation, 7 October 2026.** This note supplies an explicit finite-classical-alphabet construction, an all-parameter error bound, a dimension lower bound, and reproducible finite checks. The proof is given here rather than inferred from the checks. It has not received independent mathematical review or an exhaustive priority search. The coherent-state measure/prepare mechanism is established prior art; its finite-spin error bound was already present in the supplied research handoff. The contribution proposed for review is the uniform finite-alphabet conversion and the matching dependence on `n delta^2`.

## 1. Result and operational model

Let

\[
 \rho_{v,\delta}=\frac{I+\delta v\cdot\sigma}{2},\qquad v\in S^2,
 \qquad n\geq1,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq\tfrac12.
\]

The polarization `delta` and copy count `n` are known to the encoder and decoder; the direction `v` is unknown. Write

\[
 T(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1,\qquad s=n\delta^2.
\]

The encoder receives `n` independent, identically prepared qubits. The decoder must reproduce their joint density matrix uniformly over `v`. No correlation with an external purification is required to survive. Every retained, input-dependent register is counted. There is no retained quantum side channel or preshared entanglement. Fresh randomness and fixed ancillas are allowed. Processing workspace and the decoder's `n` output qubits are not counted as retained storage.

### Theorem 1: finite classical memory

For every `0 < epsilon <= 1/4` there is a code storing only one of at most

\[
 M\leq 1+2^{24}(1+s)\varepsilon^{-3}
               \ln(1+72/\varepsilon)                                      \tag{1}
\]

classical labels, and no qubits, such that

\[
 \sup_{v\in S^2}T\!\left(\mathcal D\mathcal E
       (\rho_{v,\delta}^{\otimes n}),\rho_{v,\delta}^{\otimes n}\right)
 \leq\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}+\varepsilon.                           \tag{2}
\]

In particular, its number of stored bits is

\[
 \log_2(1+n\delta^2)
 +3\log_2(1/\varepsilon)
 +\log_2\ln(1+72/\varepsilon)+O(1).                                 \tag{3}
\]

The exact channel constructed below has error at most `2 delta/(1+delta) + 3 epsilon/4`, leaving `epsilon/4` for finite-precision realization. The constant in (1) is intentionally loose. The case `delta=0` needs just one label and has zero error.

### Theorem 2: arbitrary quantum memory cannot improve the signal scaling

Any unassisted code, classical or quantum, with uniform trace-distance error at most `1/8`, whose sole retained memory has Hilbert-space dimension `D`, satisfies

\[
 D\geq\frac{1+n\delta^2}{1024},\qquad
 \log_2D\geq\log_2(1+n\delta^2)-10.                                \tag{4}
\]

The bound also holds with input-independent shared classical randomness. It is a bound on fixed maximum memory dimension, not expected variable-length coding cost.

For any fixed target error `0 < eta <= 1/8`, taking `epsilon=eta/2` and `delta<=eta/4` shows that classical and quantum optimal total-memory costs both equal

\[
 \log_2(1+n\delta^2)+O_\eta(1).                                    \tag{5}
\]

The dependence on `epsilon` is not claimed optimal. Theorems 1–2 do not characterize the minimum quantum memory when a requested error is much smaller than `delta`.

## 2. Why this is a different question from fixed-spectrum compression

The supplied handoff, lines 34–38, already observed that fixed-gap formulas cannot be substituted into moving-gap sequences and supplied a uniform finite-spin coherent-state channel estimate. That estimate leaves a continuous classical outcome, so by itself it gives no finite-memory accounting.

Hayden, Maloney, Wang and Yang's 5 October 2026 preprint [1] explicitly fixes the spectrum, with distinct positive eigenvalues. Its qubit expansion is `log_2(n delta)` in that fixed-spectrum limit. It explicitly makes no uniform assertion as eigenvalues collide. The present family instead permits `delta=delta_n -> 0` and does not contradict that result.

Measure/prepare benchmarks for iid spin models and displaced thermal states are older work [2]. Fixed-family hybrid compression and the obstruction to entirely classical storage for a fixed noncommuting model are also prior art [3]. Consequently neither the channel nor the general idea of a classical limit is claimed new here.

A relevant recent adjacent result is Kazi–Marvian's 15 September 2026 mixed-qubit conversion paper [4], which separates information-geometric contributions through right-logarithmic-derivative Fisher information. It concerns conversion rates, not the finite-memory theorem below. No moving-gap estimate is imported from it.

The useful distinction in this note is between two operational quantities: `n delta^2` determines the number of resolvable orientations at fixed accuracy, while `delta` controls one uniform error bound for discarding quantum coherence during storage. The latter is a sufficient bound, not an optimal lower characterization of quantum incompatibility.

## 3. Exact Schur-block description

Let `t=2j` run through the nonnegative integers with the parity of `n`, up to `n`. Schur–Weyl decomposition gives

\[
 \rho_{v,\delta}^{\otimes n}
 \simeq\bigoplus_t q_t\,\tau_{t,v,\delta}\otimes I_{m_t}/m_t,         \tag{6}
\]

where the spin space has dimension `t+1`, the multiplicity is

\[
 m_t={n\choose(n-t)/2}-{n\choose(n-t)/2-1},                         \tag{7}
\]

and a binomial coefficient with lower index `-1` means zero. Put `p_+=(1+delta)/2`, `p_-=(1-delta)/2`. For `delta>0`,

\[
 q_t=m_t(p_+p_-)^{(n-t)/2}
       \frac{p_+^{t+1}-p_-^{t+1}}{\delta}.                          \tag{8}
\]

For `delta=0`, the continuous extension is `q_t=m_t(t+1)2^{-n}`. In particular `q_t` is known, sums to one, and is independent of the unknown orientation.

For `v=z`, let `|k>` be the symmetric basis with `k` down spins. Then

\[
 \tau_{t,z,\delta}=c_t\sum_{k=0}^t r^k|k\rangle\langle k|,
 \quad r=\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta},
 \quad c_t=\left(\sum_{k=0}^t r^k\right)^{-1}.                     \tag{9}
\]

Other directions are rotations of this state. These decomposition identities are standard; formulas are included to make the proposed code reproducible without any uncharged oracle. Efficient approximate Schur transforms are established [5].

## 4. The retained finite-spin lemma, proved explicitly

Let `d omega` be normalized area measure on the unit sphere and let

\[
 |t,u\rangle=|u\rangle^{\otimes t}\in\operatorname{Sym}^t(\mathbb C^2).
\]

The coherent-state resolution of identity is

\[
 (t+1)\int_{S^2}|t,u\rangle\langle t,u|\,d\omega(u)=I_{t+1}.        \tag{10}
\]

Consider the measure/prepare channel

\[
 \mathcal B_t(X)=(t+1)\int
   \langle t,u|X|t,u\rangle |t,u\rangle\langle t,u|\,d\omega(u).     \tag{11}
\]

For input (9), axial covariance makes the output diagonal. Write its diagonal probabilities as `b_k` and the input probabilities as `a_k=c_t r^k`. Integrating the azimuth and setting `x=(1-u_z)/2` yields

\[
 b_k=c_t\,\mathbb E[\{1-(1-r)U\}^t],
 \qquad U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k+1,t-k+1).                       \tag{12}
\]

Indeed the density of this beta law is `(t+1) binom(t,k) x^k (1-x)^{t-k}`. Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean gives `1-(1-r)U >= r^U`. Convexity of the exponential then gives

\[
 b_k\geq c_t r^{t\mathbb EU}
     =c_t r^{t(k+1)/(t+2)}
     \geq c_t r^{k+1}=r a_k.                                     \tag{13}
\]

For `0<r<=1`, the last inequality uses the decreasing dependence of `r^x` on `x`. Endpoint cases follow by continuity; the `t=0` state is fixed exactly. Since both probability vectors sum to one,

\[
 T(\mathcal B_t(\tau_{t,v,\delta}),\tau_{t,v,\delta})
  =1-\sum_k\min(a_k,b_k)
  \leq1-r=\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}.                               \tag{14}
\]

Covariance handles all directions. Keeping the block weights and regenerating the maximally mixed multiplicities proves the same bound for the whole `n`-qubit state, uniformly in `n`. This is the bound retained from the handoff, not the new finite-alphabet result.

## 5. The classical experiment produced by that channel

Measuring the Schur block `t` and then the coherent-state POVM produces joint density

\[
 P_v(t,u)=q_t p_t(u\mid v)                                        \tag{15}
\]

relative to counting measure times normalized spherical area, where

\[
 p_t(u\mid v)=
 \frac{(t+1)2\delta}{(1+\delta)(1-r^{t+1})}
 \left(\frac{1+\delta v\cdot u}{1+\delta}\right)^t.                \tag{16}
\]

At `delta=0` this is the constant density one. Formula (16) is normalized even for real `t>=0`.

If the exact outcome `(t,u)` were retained, preparation of the corresponding coherent spin state, multiplication by the known maximally mixed multiplicity state, and inverse Schur transform would implement (11). The key is to approximate the **density of these outcomes**, not every pure state appearing in the mixture.

Here is the elementary general principle being used. Suppose a measurement has density `p_theta` with respect to a known reference measure `mu`, and a fixed preparation kernel maps outcome `x` to a state `K_x`. For a finite partition, retain only the cell containing `x`, and regenerate `x'` from `mu` conditioned on that cell. The resulting density is the conditional expectation `E_mu[p_theta | partition]`. Preparation contracts total variation, so its additional trace-distance error is at most

\[
 \frac12\|p_\theta-\mathbb E_\mu[p_\theta\mid\text{partition}]\|_1. \tag{17}
\]

The conditional law used at decoding is known and independent of `theta`. This data-processing observation is standard; the uniform partition bounds proved next do the substantive work here.

## 6. Radial regularity in a logarithmic coordinate

Fix `v`, write `z=v dot u`, and put

\[
 b=\ln\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta},\qquad
 Z=-\ln\frac{1+\delta z}{1+\delta}\in[0,b].                       \tag{18}
\]

Under (16), `Z` has the truncated exponential density

\[
 f_Z(y)=\frac{(t+1)e^{-(t+1)y}}{1-e^{-(t+1)b}},\quad0\leq y\leq b. \tag{19}
\]

Differentiating the normalized exponential family in its real parameter `t` gives

\[
 \partial_t\ln p_t=\mathbb EZ-Z,
 \quad
 \|\partial_t p_t\|_1=\mathbb E|Z-\mathbb EZ|.                    \tag{20}
\]

The variance is

\[
 \operatorname{Var}Z=
 \frac1{(t+1)^2}-\frac{b^2e^{(t+1)b}}{(e^{(t+1)b}-1)^2}
 \leq\frac1{(t+1)^2}.                                           \tag{21}
\]

The support bound also gives `Var Z <= b^2/4`. Thus

\[
 \|\partial_t p_t\|_1
 \leq\min\{b/2,1/(t+1)\}
 \leq\frac{4\delta}{1+\delta t}.                                 \tag{22}
\]

For the final inequality, `delta<=1/2` implies `b<=8 delta/3`; setting `x=delta(t+1)` shows `(1+x) min(4/3,1/x)<=7/3<4`, and `1+delta t<=1+x`. Integrating (22) gives

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(p_t,p_{t'})
 \leq 2\left|\ln\frac{1+\delta t}{1+\delta t'}\right|.             \tag{23}
\]

There is no asymptotic approximation in (19)–(23).

## 7. Spin concentration without storing the exact spin label

Let `J=(1/2) sum_i sigma_i` be total angular momentum. Since `4J^2=t(t+2)` on the spin-`t/2` block, product-state evaluation gives

\[
 \mathbb E[t(t+2)]=3n+n(n-1)\delta^2.                             \tag{24}
\]

The blockwise inequality `J_v<=t/2`, together with `E J_v=n delta/2`, gives

\[
 \mathbb Et\geq n\delta.                                        \tag{25}
\]

Expanding the square and using (24)–(25),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(t-n\delta)^2
 &=3n+(2n^2-n)\delta^2-2(1+n\delta)\mathbb Et\\
 &\leq3n-n\delta^2-2n\delta\leq3n.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{26}
\]

In particular `E t <= n delta + sqrt(3n)`.

Set

\[
 a=\sqrt{12/\varepsilon},\qquad
 \mathcal T=\{t:|t-n\delta|\leq a\sqrt n\}.                       \tag{27}
\]

By Markov's inequality applied to (26), the probability outside `T` is at most `epsilon/4`. All such outcomes will use one tail label.

On `T`, the ratio of largest to smallest `1+delta t` is at most

\[
 1+6a^2=1+72/\varepsilon.                                        \tag{28}
\]

To verify this uniformly, put `x=sqrt(n delta^2)`. The ratio is bounded by

\[
 \frac{1+x^2+ax}{1+\max\{0,x^2-ax\}}.
\]

For `x<=2a`, the numerator is at most `1+6a^2`. For `x>2a`, the denominator is at least `1+x^2/2` and the numerator at most `1+3x^2/2`, so the ratio is at most three.

Let `w_0` be the minimum of `1+delta t` on `T`. Form multiplicative bins with boundaries `w_0 (1+epsilon/8)^b`. These have logarithmic width `ln(1+epsilon/8)<=epsilon/8`. When `delta` and `epsilon` are rational, all bin comparisons are rational, so no exact transcendental-comparison oracle is needed. Let the nonempty radial bins be `B_b`. Since `ln(1+epsilon/8)>=epsilon/(8+epsilon)>=epsilon/9`, their number is at most

\[
 B\leq 2+\frac9\varepsilon\ln(1+72/\varepsilon)
  \leq\frac{10}{\varepsilon}\ln(1+72/\varepsilon).                \tag{29}
\]

Within one bin, (23) bounds the pairwise total-variation distance between angular densities by `epsilon/4`. Replacing a bin's `t` by a fresh draw with probabilities `q_t/sum_{r in B_b} q_r` therefore costs at most `epsilon/4` in joint total variation. Importantly, the decoder **does not replace the bin by a fixed representative spin**, and does not need to receive the exact `t`.

## 8. Angular regularity at the signal scale

With the intrinsic spherical gradient,

\[
 |\nabla\ln p_t(u\mid v)|
  =\frac{t\delta\sqrt{1-z^2}}{1+\delta z}.                        \tag{30}
\]

Equation (19) implies `E Z <= 1/(t+1)`. Moreover,

\[
 1-z=\frac{1+\delta}{\delta}(1-e^{-Z}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(1-z^2)\leq\frac3{\delta(t+1)}.                         \tag{31}
\]

Using `(1+delta z)^2 >= (1-delta)^2 >=1/4`, we get

\[
 \int p_t|\nabla\ln p_t|^2d\omega\leq12t\delta,
 \quad
 \int|\nabla p_t|d\omega\leq\sqrt{12t\delta}.                     \tag{32}
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz and (26) then give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_t q_t\int|\nabla p_t|d\omega
 &\leq\sqrt{12\delta\,\mathbb Et}\\
 &\leq\sqrt{12(s+\sqrt{3s})}
 \leq\sqrt{24(1+s)}<5\sqrt{1+s}.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{33}
\]

For `delta=0`, all gradients vanish and the inequality is immediate. This bound is uniform over orientation and copy count, including the critical and subcritical regimes.

### A concrete spherical partition

Radially project the six faces of a cube onto the sphere. For example,

\[
 \phi(x,y)=\frac{(1,x,y)}{\sqrt{1+x^2+y^2}},\qquad(x,y)\in[-1,1]^2.
\]

Use analogous maps for the other signed axes, and assign face boundaries arbitrarily; boundaries have zero area. Subdivide each face into `L by L` equal squares. This makes exactly `6L^2` angular cells. For a density `f`, let `H_L f` be its spherical-area average in each cell.

The chart's derivative has operator norm at most one, and its area Jacobian is `(1+x^2+y^2)^(-3/2)`, between `3^(-3/2)` and one. The ordinary square `L^1` Poincare estimate followed by comparison of weighted and unweighted means gives

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(f,H_Lf)
 \leq\frac{16}{L}\int|\nabla f|d\omega.                           \tag{34}
\]

For completeness, on a square of side `h`, integrating the fundamental theorem of calculus along the two coordinate directions yields

\[
 \int_Q|g-g_Q|\leq\frac h2\int_Q(|\partial_1g|+|\partial_2g|)
 \leq\frac h{\sqrt2}\int_Q|\nabla g|.
\]

Replacing the unweighted mean by the weighted mean costs a factor at most two. Comparing maximum and minimum chart weights costs `3^(3/2)`, while pulling back gradients costs at most one. With `h=2/L` and the factor one-half in total variation, the resulting constant is less than eight; (34) uses the safer value sixteen. Summing over cells requires no continuity across cell boundaries of the histogram.

Choose

\[
 L=\left\lceil\frac{320\sqrt{1+s}}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.       \tag{35}
\]

Combining (33)–(35), the average angular approximation costs at most `epsilon/4`. Also

\[
 6L^2\leq6\cdot321^2(1+s)\varepsilon^{-2}.                        \tag{36}
\]

## 9. Finite encoder, finite decoder, and error accounting

Here is the exact finite channel; continuous registers are not needed even as part of its definition.

**Encoder.** Perform a Schur transform and measure the block `t`. For `t` outside `T`, retain the tail symbol. Otherwise let `b` be its radial bin and measure on its spin space the finite POVM with effects

\[
 E_{t,c}=(t+1)\int_{C_c}|t,u\rangle\langle t,u|\,d\omega(u).        \tag{37}
\]

These are positive and sum exactly to the identity by (10). Retain just the pair `(b,c)` and discard all quantum registers, the exact block label, and other input-dependent workspace.

**Decoder.** On `(b,c)`, prepare the fixed mixed state

\[
 \Omega_{b,c}=\mathcal S^{-1}\left[
  \bigoplus_{t\in B_b}\frac{q_t}{Q_b}
  \left(\frac1{\omega(C_c)}\int_{C_c}|t,u\rangle\langle t,u|
                \,d\omega(u)\right)\otimes\frac{I_{m_t}}{m_t}
 \right],                                                       \tag{38}
\]

where `Q_b=sum_{t in B_b} q_t` and `S^{-1}` denotes conjugation by the inverse Schur transform. On the tail symbol, prepare any fixed density matrix. Empty or zero-probability bins are omitted. All states in (38) are specified independently of `v`.

An equivalent probabilistic description of (38) is to sample `t` from its known conditional distribution and `u` from uniform spherical area in `C_c`, then prepare the corresponding state. That description does not charge a hidden continuous memory: it only describes a fixed finite-dimensional mixed state at the decoder, which could instead be prepared using its finite spectral decomposition.

Relative to reference measure `q_t d omega`, radial and angular averaging are commuting conditional expectations. Both are `L^1` contractions. Consequently their errors add, not multiply. Replacing the tail branch changes trace distance by at most its probability. The three extra costs are therefore

\[
 \underbrace{\varepsilon/4}_{\text{tail}}
 +\underbrace{\varepsilon/4}_{\text{radial}}
 +\underbrace{\varepsilon/4}_{\text{angular}}.                      \tag{39}
\]

The preparation map contracts total variation into trace distance, so (14) and (39) prove the claimed error. The number of retained labels obeys

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M&\leq1+B\,6L^2\\
  &\leq1+6\cdot321^2\cdot10\,(1+s)\varepsilon^{-3}
                 \ln(1+72/\varepsilon)\\
  &\leq1+2^{24}(1+s)\varepsilon^{-3}\ln(1+72/\varepsilon).
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{40}
\]

This completes the exact finite-channel proof of Theorem 1.

## 10. Finite-precision realization and resource accounting

For rational `delta`, `epsilon`, and requested approximation tolerance `nu>0`, of total input bit length `b`, the construction above can be approximated to further channel error at most `nu` with resources polynomial in `n`, `b`, `epsilon^{-1}`, and `nu^{-1}`. This is an existence/construction argument for circuits and classical arithmetic, not a supplied gate compiler or a hardware benchmark. No nearly linear runtime or optimal polynomial exponent is claimed.

Here are the reasons no exponential table or continuous-information oracle is required.

1. **Known parameters and weights.** Membership in the retained set is the rational comparison `(t-n delta)^2<=12n/epsilon`. The angular integer `L` can be chosen by comparing `L^2 epsilon^2` with `320^2(1+s)`, and the radial bins use the rational boundaries specified above. There are at most `n+1` Schur blocks. Formula (8), or its finite geometric sum before division by `delta`, computes the weights with rational arithmetic of polynomial bit length. Known conditional distributions can be sampled by ordinary rational-probability sampling without rejection on rare bins. Very small `Q_b` therefore need extra bits of precision, not exponentially many sampling trials. Alternatively exact rational weights can be used.
2. **Polynomial spin dimensions.** Every effect in (37) acts on a space of dimension at most `n+1`; the number of cells is `O((1+n delta^2) epsilon^{-2})`. The exponential multiplicity is never stored as a dense matrix.
3. **Positive integration.** The map from a Bloch direction to its coherent rank-one projector is smooth in every cube chart. The telescoping product inequality gives trace-norm Lipschitz constant at most a constant times `t` for these projectors. The charts and their Jacobians have uniformly bounded derivatives. Positive square-grid quadrature therefore approximates all cell integrals with a total operator-norm error bounded by a polynomial in `n` times the quadrature mesh. Taking polynomially small mesh gives the needed precision with polynomially many nodes.
4. **A genuine finite POVM.** If the quadrature effects are `F_c>=0`, set `G=sum_c F_c`. For sufficiently fine quadrature `||G-I||<1/2`, and replace them by `G^{-1/2}F_cG^{-1/2}`. They are positive and sum exactly to the identity. Since `G` is well-conditioned, the inverse square root is stable. The sum of effect errors can be made arbitrarily small with polynomial precision; the loose factor from summing norms is at most polynomial in the spin dimension. This avoids an invalid approximately normalized measurement.
5. **Preparation and synthesis.** Decoder spin-state mixtures are similarly approximated by positive normalized quadrature or matrix decompositions. Cell areas are bounded below by a universal constant times `L^{-2}`, so normalization costs polynomial precision. Dense finite-dimensional POVM dilation and state preparation use polynomial-size matrices. Standard finite-precision circuit synthesis and the efficient Schur transform [5] then give polynomial gate counts.
6. **Multiplicity preparation.** A maximally mixed multiplicity register can be made by choosing a uniform valid Schur path or Young-tableau label and preparing that computational basis state. The needed branching counts are binomial differences such as (7), computable with polynomial-size integers. Sequential conditional sampling uses polynomial time and `O(n)` temporary bits, followed by inverse Schur transformation.

Spending `nu<=epsilon/4` in total across these approximations leaves (2) unchanged. Intermediate quantum workspace may be substantial: the theorem removes quantum storage between encoding and decoding, not quantum computation during either operation. The exact channel theorem also makes sense for real known `delta`, but no algorithmic claim is made for noncomputable parameters.

## 11. Proof of the lower bound

Suppose first `s>=576`. Take a maximal collection `v_1,...,v_K` on the sphere with pairwise chordal distance at least

\[
 \Delta=24/\sqrt s\leq1.                                         \tag{41}
\]

Maximality means their chordal caps of radius `Delta` cover the sphere. Such a cap has area `pi Delta^2`, while the sphere has area `4 pi`, so

\[
 K\geq4/\Delta^2=s/144.                                          \tag{42}
\]

The code need not know this packing: it is only a family of input states used in the converse.

To distinguish those inputs before compression, divide the `n` copies into three groups of `floor(n/3)` and measure Pauli `X`, `Y`, `Z` respectively, ignoring leftover qubits. The vector of sample means estimates the Bloch vector `delta v` and has squared-error expectation at most

\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat r-\delta v\|_2^2
 \leq\frac3{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}\leq\frac{18}{n}.                 \tag{43}
\]

Here `n>=6`, as follows automatically from `s>=576` and `delta<=1/2`. Nearest-packing-point classification succeeds whenever the estimation error is less than `delta Delta/2`. Markov's inequality gives failure probability at most

\[
 \frac{72}{n\delta^2\Delta^2}=\frac18.                            \tag{44}
\]

A reconstruction error at most `1/8` preserves the success probability of this measurement up to `1/8`; hence the compressed memory permits discrimination of the `K` inputs with success at least `3/4` under a uniform prior.

For any states `sigma_i` in a `D`-dimensional memory and any decoding measurement `{N_i}`,

\[
 \frac1K\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(N_i\sigma_i)
 \leq\frac1K\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}N_i=\frac DK,                  \tag{45}
\]

because `0<=sigma_i<=I`. Therefore

\[
 D\geq\tfrac34K\geq s/192.                                      \tag{46}
\]

For `s<576`, use `D>=1`. These two cases imply (4). Shared classical randomness independent of the input does not help: apply (45) conditional on each value of that randomness and average. No Holevo-information approximation, local asymptotic normality, or limiting experiment is needed. This completes Theorem 2.

## 12. Consequences and limitations

### 12.1 Vanishing polarization with an increasing information burden

The general consequence is not restricted to power laws. For **any** sequence with `delta_n -> 0` and `s_n=n delta_n^2 -> infinity`, take `epsilon_n=1/ln(e+s_n)` once it is at most `1/4`. The construction stores

\[
 \log_2 s_n+O(\log\log s_n)
\]

classical bits, no qubits, and has error `O(delta_n+1/ln s_n)->0`. Every code needs `log_2 s_n-O(1)` bits of total memory for eventually small error. This matches the leading memory asymptotics for every such supercritical collision sequence.

For an example, take `delta_n=n^{-alpha}` with `0<alpha<1/2`, and for sufficiently large `n` take `epsilon_n=1/ln n`. Then (3) gives zero stored qubits and

\[
 (1-2\alpha)\log_2 n+O(\log\log n)
\]

classical bits, with reconstruction error `O(n^{-alpha}+1/ln n)->0`. The lower bound gives `(1-2 alpha) log_2 n-O(1)` bits of total memory for any code with eventually small error. Thus the leading logarithmic coefficient is matched.

The source family remains distinguishable: the required total memory diverges. Becoming classically storable is not the same as becoming independent of orientation.

### 12.2 Critical signal and vanishing signal

For `delta_n=n^{-1/2}`, the same construction uses `O(log log n)` bits with vanishing error. This is an upper bound, not a sharp accuracy-dependent characterization.

If `s=n delta^2 ->0`, no memory is needed. Indeed

\[
 D(\rho_{v,\delta}\|I/2)
 =\tfrac{1+\delta}2\ln(1+\delta)
  +\tfrac{1-\delta}2\ln(1-\delta)
 \leq\frac{\delta^2}{2(1-\delta^2)}.
\]

The inequality follows by integrating the second derivative `1/(1-delta^2)` from zero. Tensor additivity and Pinsker's inequality yield

\[
 T(\rho_{v,\delta}^{\otimes n},(I/2)^{\otimes n})
 \leq\frac{\sqrt n\,\delta}{2\sqrt{1-\delta^2}}.                  \tag{47}
\]

This reproduces the subcritical upper bound already recognized in the handoff. There is also an elementary converse, making the zero-memory transition exact. A code retaining no information must output one fixed state `sigma_n`. Taking antipodal directions `v` and `-v`, the two input states commute. For their classical product eigenvalue distributions `P,Q`,

\[
 T(P,Q)=1-\sum_x\min\{P(x),Q(x)\}
 \geq1-\sum_x\sqrt{P(x)Q(x)}
 =1-(1-\delta^2)^{n/2}\geq1-e^{-s/2}.
\]

The triangle inequality then gives

\[
 \sup_v T(\sigma_n,\rho_{v,\delta}^{\otimes n})
 \geq\frac{1-e^{-s/2}}2.                                         \tag{48}
\]

Consequently a sequence of these families admits vanishing-error **zero-memory** reconstruction if and only if `n delta_n^2 ->0`. Input-independent shared randomness does not alter this conclusion: its unconditional output is still one fixed density matrix.

### 12.3 A concrete storage guarantee, not a device demonstration

For the purely mathematical example `n=1,000,000`, `delta=0.005`, `epsilon=0.01`, one has `s=25`. Equation (1) gives `ceil(log_2 M)<=52` classical bits and (2) gives error below `0.02`, with zero retained qubits. This is a conservative bound from the theorem, not an implemented million-qubit protocol or an experimentally measured resource saving. The angular grid and POVM sizes in this conservative prescription are very large; the polynomial implementation argument is not a near-term engineering-efficiency claim.

For any later joint POVM on the decoded `n` qubits, the total-variation error in its outcome distribution is bounded by the same trace-distance error. Thus the reconstruction guarantee preserves all possible later state-only tests, rather than only a preselected observable. Implementing those later quantum tests may still require quantum hardware.

### 12.4 What is not established

The note does not claim a complete quantum/classical memory–error tradeoff when required error is much smaller than polarization; optimal constants or optimal `epsilon` dependence; preservation of entanglement with an external reference; unknown-spectrum adaptation; qudit spectral-collision strata; arbitrary correlated inputs; efficient classical simulation of all later measurements; or a hardware implementation. It does not challenge fixed-spectrum quantum-memory lower bounds. Reusing a stored classical label to prepare several outputs does not create independent copies with the corresponding joint guarantee: those outputs can be correlated through the label.

The foundational result established by the derivation is narrower and precise: in this moving, known-spectrum qubit family, the finite storage cost has signal scale `n delta^2`, and no stored quantum system is necessary as `delta` vanishes if one permits the displayed vanishing error schedule.
