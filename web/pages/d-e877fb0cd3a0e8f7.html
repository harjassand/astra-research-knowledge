Q1. INPUT, GENERATOR, AND EXACT STATIONARY DENSITY
There are N>=1 labeled spin-half sites. Ground |0> has Jz=-1/2, excited
|1> has +1/2; J_-=sum_i |0><1|_i and J_+=J_-^dagger.
Let Gamma>0 and
 L(rho)=Gamma[(nu+1)D[J_-](rho)+nu D[J_+](rho)],
 D[A](rho)=A rho A^dagger - {A^dagger A,rho}/2, nu>=0 finite.  (Q1.1)
Pure collective decay is nu=0. No other noise or drive is included.

The initial density in the assigned interface is
 rho0=exp[(alpha J^2-delta Jz^2)/N+hJz]/Z0,
with ANY finite real alpha,delta,h. It is permutation invariant and full
rank. More generally the representation below works for any permutation-
invariant initial density, whether or not diagonal in Jz.

Schur-Weyl decomposition is
 H_N=direct_sum_{b=0}^{floor(N/2)} V_{j_b} tensor M_b,
 j_b=N/2-b, K_b=2j_b=N-2b,
 d_b=dim M_b=binom(N,b)-binom(N,b-1), binom(N,-1)=0.          (Q1.2)
The spin dimension is K_b+1; the entire sector has dimension (K_b+1)d_b.
Permutation invariance implies rho0 has no inter-sector coherence and
each sector equals q_b R_b tensor I_{M_b}/d_b, with Tr R_b=1 and
q_b=Tr(P_b rho0). A zero-weight sector may be omitted.

The collective generator acts on V_j and trivially on M_b. Thus it
conserves q_b and the identity multiplicity factor. Set
 q=nu/(nu+1) in [0,1), Z_K(q)=sum_{r=0}^K q^r.
Then its limiting density is
 rho_infty=direct_sum_b q_b tau_{K_b}(q) tensor I_{M_b}/d_b,
 tau_K(q)=sum_{r=0}^K q^r |j,-j+r><j,-j+r|/Z_K(q).         (Q1.3)
Here 0^0=1. In particular tau_K(0) is the lowest-weight rank-one
projector. K=0 is a one-dimensional state for every q. For q!=1,
Z_K=(1-q^{K+1})/(1-q). The formal q=1 limit is Z_K=K+1 and
tau_K=I_{V_j}/(K+1); it is an infinite-temperature limit, not finite nu.
There are NO binomial coefficients in these spin thermal probabilities.
The binomial multiplicity d_b is separate from both q_b and pi_r=q^r/Z_K.

Q2. WHY COHERENCES DO NOT ALTER THIS INTERFACE
In the spin-j ladder write r=m+j, 0<=r<=K. Downward rate from r is
Gamma(nu+1) r(K-r+1); upward rate from r is
Gamma nu (r+1)(K-r). Adjacent detailed balance is therefore
 pi_{r+1}/pi_r=nu/(nu+1)=q.                                 (Q2.1)
For the stated Gibbs input spin coherences are absent and this irreducible
finite birth-death chain, or its absorbing pure-death endpoint, already
proves (Q1.3).

For completeness the same limit holds for arbitrary spin coherences in
every permutation-invariant input. If nu>0, tau_K is faithful. The adjoint
Lindblad generator obeys the exact identity
 L^dagger(X^dagger X)-L^dagger(X^dagger)X-X^dagger L^dagger(X)
   =Gamma[(nu+1)[J_-,X]^dagger[J_-,X]
           +nu[J_+,X]^dagger[J_+,X]].                      (Q2.2)
If X is a peripheral eigenoperator L^dagger(X)=i omega X, stationarity
and tracing (Q2.2) against faithful tau_K make both commutators vanish.
The irreducible spin representation has scalar commutant, so X is scalar
and omega=0. The finite-dimensional unital semigroup is bounded, excluding
nontrivial Jordan blocks on the imaginary axis. Thus its only peripheral
mode is the scalar one and all density matrices converge to tau_K.
At nu=0 every population at r>0 is eventually absorbed at r=0. Positivity
|R_rs|^2<=R_rr R_ss makes all coherences involving excited levels vanish.
Thus arbitrary R_b also converges to the lowest-weight projector.

Inter-sector or multiplicity coherences are not included in this input
interface. Pure decay can preserve coherences between dark states for a
non-permutation-invariant input. In particular an arbitrary dark
multiplicity density need not equal I_{M_b}/d_b and can have much greater
entanglement depth. Q7 below gives an exact four-qubit counterexample.

Q3. CANONICAL SINGLETS EMBED EXACTLY ONE SPIN SECTOR
Let |s>=(|01>-|10>)/sqrt(2). Pair the first 2b sites as
(1,2),(3,4),...,(2b-1,2b), and leave K=N-2b sites. Define the isometry
 W_b: Sym^K(C^2) -> H_N,
 W_b v=|s>^{tensor b} tensor v.                             (Q3.1)
Every pair's Jz and J_+/- annihilate its singlet. Therefore on this image
the total collective spin acts exactly as the remaining symmetric K-site
spin-j=K/2 representation:
 J_a W_b=W_b J_a^(K), J^2 W_b=j(j+1)W_b.                   (Q3.2)
In particular W_b has no admixture of any other spin. By irreducibility
and the intertwiner property, in a compatible Schur basis
 W_b v=v tensor |mu_b>, ||mu_b||=1,                         (Q3.3)
for one multiplicity vector. This is a statement about an isometric
embedding, not an assumption that the physical sites coincide with the
Schur tensor factors.

Let T_S be the uniform permutation conjugation twirl. The S_N action
is identity on V_j and irreducible on M_b. Schur's lemma yields
 T_S(W_b R W_b^dagger)=R tensor I_{M_b}/d_b                 (Q3.4)
for EVERY operator R on Sym^K, including off-diagonal spin coherences.
Both sides have trace Tr R. Thus this twirl randomizes the multiplicity
and PRESERVES the spin operator; it does not dephase or thermally alter R.

Let |D_{K,r}> be the normalized symmetric K-qubit state with r excitations.
Set
 sigma_K(q)=sum_{r=0}^K q^r |D_{K,r}><D_{K,r}|/Z_K(q).
Equations (Q3.2)-(Q3.4) identify sigma_K exactly with tau_K in the physical
spin sector and reproduce (Q1.3) with all multiplicity factors correct.

Q4. POSITIVE PRODUCT DECOMPOSITION OF THE THERMAL SYMMETRIC BLOCK
For K>=1 use the coherent-state resolution
 P_sym,K=(K+1) integral |psi(x,theta)><psi(x,theta)|^{tensor K}
                          dx dtheta/(2pi),
 psi(x,theta)=sqrt(1-x)|0>+exp(i theta)sqrt(x)|1>,
 0<=x<=1, 0<=theta<2pi.                                    (Q4.1)
To verify it directly, phase integration kills Dicke off-diagonals, and
the r diagonal coefficient is
 (K+1)binom(K,r) integral_0^1 x^r(1-x)^{K-r}dx
   =(K+1)binom(K,r) r!(K-r)!/(K+1)!=1.

The common one-qubit filter F_q=diag(1,sqrt(q)) gives
 F_q^{tensor K} P_sym,K F_q^{tensor K}
   =sum_r q^r |D_{K,r}><D_{K,r}|.                          (Q4.2)
Put a(x)=1-(1-q)x and
 phi_q(x,theta)=[sqrt(1-x)|0>+exp(i theta)sqrt(q x)|1>]/sqrt(a(x)).
The normalized positive mixing law is
 dmu_{K,q}(x,theta)= [(K+1)/Z_K(q)] a(x)^K dx dtheta/(2pi),
since integral_0^1 a(x)^K dx=Z_K(q)/(K+1). Then
 sigma_K(q)=integral |phi_q><phi_q|^{tensor K} dmu_{K,q}.    (Q4.3)
At q=0 the resulting product is simply |0>^{tensor K}; the x=1
normalization singularity is a measure-zero proposal and has zero weight.
At q=1 this is the unfiltered Haar law. K=0 requires no local state.
Thus sigma_K(q) is fully separable for every endpoint as well.

Conditional on phi, the K remaining sites are identical pure product
factors. Averaging over the common phi correlates their classical labels;
the averaged density is not asserted to be a single tensor power.

An optional exact finite ensemble also follows: use a positive n-point
Gauss-Legendre rule on [0,1], n=ceil((K+1)/2), and the K+1 equally spaced
phases. Phase averaging kills all r!=s, and the remaining x integrands
x^r(1-x)^{K-r} have degree K<=2n-1, so this finite cubature is exact.
If its nodes and weights are x_l,w_l, each phase at x_l has probability
 (K+1) w_l a(x_l)^K /[(K+1)Z_K] = w_l a(x_l)^K/Z_K.
The ensemble has O(K^2) rays with algebraic node/filter descriptions for
rational q. This is a mathematical finite representation; no exact
finite-gate preparation or algebraic probability compiler is claimed run.
The fully charged finite-bit sampler in Q8 avoids those additional inputs.

Q5. EXACT TWO-PRODUCIBLE REPRESENTATION AND RANDOM PAIRINGS
Combining (Q1.3), (Q3.4), and (Q4.3) gives
 rho_infty=sum_b q_b E_{pi uniform, phi~mu_{K_b,q}}
    P_pi (|s><s|^{tensor b} tensor
                      |phi><phi|^{tensor K_b}) P_pi^dagger. (Q5.1)
Every pure term is a product of b two-site Bell singlets and K single-site
states. It is 2-producible, even when its pairing differs from other terms.
Therefore rho_infty has entanglement depth at most 2 for every admitted
finite initial parameter and every finite nu>=0.

A uniform permutation is equivalent here to a uniform unordered set of
b disjoint pairs and K remaining sites. Each pairing has exactly
2^b b! K! preimages among N! permutations. Reversing a singlet changes
only an irrelevant pure-vector sign; reordering identical rest factors
does not change the density. The number of possible pairings can be
exponential, but it need not be listed: Fisher-Yates samples a uniform
permutation in O(N) integer selections.

If an independently proved full-separability violation is available, the
depth is EXACTLY 2 (N>=2). For N>=3 this representation also proves that
the stationary state is not genuinely N-partite entangled. For N=2,
depth 2 is ordinary two-party entanglement and can be called genuine
two-party entanglement; that terminology exception must not be hidden.

Q6. PURE-DECAY DEPTH IS EXACTLY TWO FOR EVERY FULL-RANK GIBBS INPUT
The assigned zero-temperature endpoint admits a direct lower bound.
Let E=Jz+N/2 and R=J_+J_-. For a product density with excitation
probabilities p_i and lowering means c_i, positivity gives
|c_i|^2<=p_i(1-p_i). Therefore
 <R>=sum_i(p_i-|c_i|^2)+|sum_i c_i|^2
     >=sum_i p_i^2 >= (sum_i p_i)^2/N.
Convex averaging and Jensen yield the necessary SEP inequality
 <R> >= <E>^2/N.                                          (Q6.1)
This is the root/c07_s02 witness, independently reconstructed here.

In (Q1.3) at q=0 every block is dark: <R>=0, whereas
<E>=beta=sum_b b q_b. Hence beta>0 violates (Q6.1).
For finite alpha,delta,h the input is full rank. If N>=2, at least one
b>=1 sector exists and has strictly positive q_b. Thus beta>0, and the
pure-decay stationary density has depth EXACTLY 2 for every such input.
For the formal q=1 limit, sector mixtures can instead be separable:
q_b=(K_b+1)d_b/2^N yields I/2^N. Occupancy of b>0 sectors alone does
not prove entanglement for an arbitrary finite-temperature mixture.
The finite-time onset and other thermal entanglement lower bounds are
separate dynamics tasks; no such stronger claim is needed for Q5.

Q7. A SHARP INPUT-SCOPE COUNTEREXAMPLE
Remove permutation invariance. The normalized pure N=4 vector with
amplitudes
 (0011,0101,0110,1001,1010,1100)=(2,1,-3,-3,1,2)/sqrt(28)
is a total-spin-zero singlet, killed by J_- and J_+. It is therefore
stationary for every nu, but its Schmidt ranks are 2 across all 1|3
cuts and 4 across all distinct 2|2 cuts. It is genuinely four-partite
entangled, of depth 4. It is invariant under global SU(2), but not under
all site permutations. This refutes an extension of Q5 to general dark
states or to arbitrary multiplicity coherences. Its uniform permutation
twirl IS the two-producible normalized spin-zero projector.

Q8. CHARGED CLASSICAL SAMPLER: SUPPLIED WEIGHTS AND FINITE PRECISION
The exact identity (Q5.1) is an ideal distributional representation.
An exact-real rejection sampler draws x uniform [0,1] and theta uniform
[0,2pi], and accepts with probability a(x)^K. Acceptance is
Z_K/(K+1)>=1/(K+1); the expected number of proposals is at most K+1.
Drawing b from q_b, a uniform pairing, and then phi gives the stationary
density exactly in that ideal real model. Continuous real randomness
and exact real comparisons are explicitly part of this statement.

The following finite-bit version instead outputs two-site singlet
projectors plus rational pure one-qubit projectors, whose averaged
N-site density is within epsilon in trace norm of rho_infty. It uses
only polynomial-size classical descriptions and polynomial bit work,
for 0<epsilon<=1, conditional on the input below.

Input: N in unary/output-size accounting; nonnegative rational weights
hat(q_b), sum 1, of bit length B, with sum_b|hat(q_b)-q_b|<=epsilon/8;
and rational hat(q) in [0,1] of bit length B, satisfying
|hat(q)-q|<=epsilon/[8N(N+1)]. Exact rational supplied weights fit
this interface with zero weight error. Arbitrary uncomputable real
parameters do not constitute a finite-bit acquisition interface.

1. Sample b exactly from the rational hat(q_b). A common integer
denominator formed from their denominators has O(NB) bits. Uniform
integer rejection has expected fewer than two draws, after which
cumulative integer weights select b. These preprocessing bit costs
are charged. Sample a uniform permutation by unbiased Fisher-Yates
integer choices, using expected O(N log N) random bits. Make the first
2b labels into consecutive pairs; the other K labels receive the same
one-qubit ray. K=0 ends immediately; hat(q)=0 may output ground rest.

2. Choose a power of two M with
 sqrt(M)>=160N(N+1)/epsilon.
Draw independent uniform indices l,s in {0,...,M-1} and set
x=(l+1/2)/M, theta=2pi s/M. Let a=1-(1-hat(q))x.
Choose an integer precision S with
2^{-S}<=epsilon/[64(N+1)].
Compute the rational w=a^K and integer C=floor(2^S w) exactly.
Draw an S-bit integer U uniform on {0,...,2^S-1}; accept iff U<C.
Rational powering uses O(log K) multiplications on integers of
O(K(B+log M)+S) bits. There is no floating threshold or exponential
state enumeration. Cap proposals at
T=ceil(2(N+1) log(16/epsilon)).
On timeout use any fixed product rest state with the chosen singlets.

3. On acceptance compute a rational complex vector z approximating
phi_hat(q)(x,theta) in Euclidean norm by
tau=epsilon/[32N], and output the exact rational PSD projector
zz^dagger/(z^dagger z) as the local pure-state descriptor, repeated
on all K remaining labels. The grid midpoint guarantees a>=1/(2M)
independently of q; square-root and trigonometric evaluations therefore
require only polynomial precision/work in B,log M,log(N/epsilon).
Output b pair labels and singlet projectors, plus the rest labels and
this one-qubit descriptor. No 2^N density matrix is output.

Error accounting:
 (a) Sector-weight error contributes at most epsilon/8 in trace norm.
 (b) Since |q^r-hat(q)^r|<=r|q-hat(q)| and Z_K>=1,
     ||sigma_K(q)-sigma_K(hat(q))||_1
         <=K(K+1)|q-hat(q)|<=epsilon/8.
 (c) Let A(x,theta)=[F_hat(q)|psi><psi|F_hat(q)]^{tensor K}.
     Coupling continuous points to the nearest midpoint/phase grid gives
     one-qubit trace error <=2/sqrt(M)+2pi/M<=10/sqrt(M).
     Filtering contracts that norm, every local filtered factor has
     trace <=1, and tensor telescoping gives
     ||integral A - grid_average A||_1 <=10K/sqrt(M).
     The continuous normalization is Z_K/(K+1)>=1/(K+1).
     For positive A,B, normalization obeys
     ||A/Tr A-B/Tr B||_1<=2||A-B||_1/Tr A.
     Thus grid error is <=20K(K+1)/sqrt(M)<=epsilon/8.
 (d) Before threshold rounding the grid acceptance mass is at least
     [1-epsilon/16]/(K+1). Downward rounding changes unnormalized
     density and mass by at most 2^{-S}. Its normalized error is
     <=3(K+1)2^{-S}<=3epsilon/64<epsilon/8. The actual acceptance
     probability remains >=1/[2(K+1)]>=1/[2(N+1)].
 (e) Timeout probability <=exp[-T/(2(N+1))]<=epsilon/16, so the
     trace error from replacing those samples is <=epsilon/8.
 (f) Normalizing z changes its Euclidean error by at most a factor 2;
     the one-qubit projector error is <=4tau. Tensor telescoping
     contributes <=4K tau<=epsilon/8.
Permutation twirling and adding the fixed singlet factors do not enlarge
trace norm. These six errors sum to less than epsilon.

Resource scope: log M and S are O(log N+log(1/epsilon)); T is
O(N log(1/epsilon)). Storage, output descriptions, preprocessing,
random-bit use in expectation, and integer/elementary-function work
are polynomial in N,B,log(1/epsilon). The proposal cap bounds that
part of the runtime; unbiased discrete draws have their usual
unbounded but geometrically decaying rejection tail. The exact-real
sampler is expected O(N) proposals; the finite-bit version does not
claim exact preparation of generic irrational gates. Actual quantum
preparation would require b Bell pairs and K single-site preparations
to the charged accuracy; no quantum device or physical preparation
was performed here.

The epsilon approximation need not retain an exponentially small
entanglement witness violation. At q=0 the exact witness
W_beta=R-(2beta/N)E+(beta^2/N)I has target expectation -beta^2/N
and operator norm <=3N^2. Thus epsilon<beta^2/(3N^3) suffices
to retain its strict violation. For Gibbs parameters with extremely
small beta, log(1/epsilon) at this requested margin can scale with
their numerical magnitudes and need not be polynomial in their
binary description length. Fixed trace accuracy, exact mathematical
depth, and operational certification of a tiny lower-bound margin
are separate claims. Every sampler output remains 2-producible
regardless of that margin.

Q9. KNOWN GIBBS PARAMETERS: OPTIONAL ACQUISITION OF q_b
For the stated initial Gibbs state the exact scalar sector weights are
 A_b=d_b exp[alpha j_b(j_b+1)/N]
          sum_{r=0}^{K_b} exp[-delta(r-j_b)^2/N+h(r-j_b)],
 q_b=A_b/sum_c A_c.                                       (Q9.1)
The number of scalar terms is L=sum_b(K_b+1)=O(N^2), and d_b is
an exactly computable O(N)-bit integer. No 2^N input state is needed.

If alpha,delta,h are supplied rationals of bit length B, q_b can be
acquired to the Q8 tolerance in polynomial bit work even for very
large parameter magnitudes. Compute all bare exponents e_{b,r}
exactly and their largest Emax. Then evaluate
d_b exp(e_{b,r}-Emax), with every exponent now nonpositive.
The scaled sum is at least 1. Choose a binary elementary-exponential
tolerance zeta<=epsilon/(16L 2^N). Arguments less than -R, with
R=ceil(log_2(1/zeta)), can be clipped to zero since exp(-R)<=2^{-R}.
All remaining exponential arguments lie in [-R,0], where
R=O(N+log L+log(1/epsilon)). Certified downward dyadic approximations
to zeta precision need polynomial time in N,B,log(1/epsilon).
The summed weighted error is <=L 2^N zeta<=epsilon/16; normalization
therefore gives l1 sector-weight error <=epsilon/8. The resulting
dyadic integer term sums, divided by their positive total, give exact
rational nonnegative hat(q_b). This is a specified acquisition
algorithm, not an executed large-N Gibbs sampler.
For known rational nu, q=nu/(nu+1) is rational directly. Computable
real parameter oracles must have their precision costs charged;
unqualified finite real parameters support the analytic theorem,
not automatically this numerical runtime claim.

EXECUTED FINITE-BIT SAMPLER ADDENDUM
charged_qubit_sampler.py implements the Q8 integer/grid sampler and the
Q9 positive scalar Gibbs-weight acquisition, entirely with rational/integer
arithmetic. Local amplitudes use dyadic integer-square-root lower values.
The phase uses the Machin identity
pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239), with alternating-series remainder,
followed by the complex exponential Taylor polynomial with certified
remainder <=8^(L+1)/(L+1)! on the real argument range [0,8].
These errors are chosen below one sixteenth of the Q8 ray tolerance.
Each output local projector is verified exactly as rational Hermitian
rank-one PSD of trace one, and the pair/rest labels cover all sites.

For Q9 the code subtracts the largest exact bare exponent, clips below
-R, and uses the degree-L Taylor polynomial of exp(x) for -R<x<=0.
Taylor's remainder is <=R^(L+1)/(L+1)! because exp(c)<=1 on this
negative interval. Subtracting this remainder gives a certified lower
sum; dyadic downward rounding preserves positivity and the zeta error
budget. The exact maximal term is retained as 1, ensuring positive
normalization. The degree can be found by an integer/rational loop.
After L>=2R its remainder decreases by at least a factor 1/2 per
step; its earlier growth is at most exp(R), so the total degree is
O(R+log(1/zeta)), proving the stated polynomial work without an
uncharged exponential oracle.

The four replayable demonstration transcripts use seeded PRNG bits,
explicitly distinguished from the unbiased-bit mathematical interface.
They cover N=4 at q=1/3 with Gibbs weights acquired from
(alpha,delta,h)=(2,-1/3,1/5), the q=0 endpoint at N=4, and supplied
uniform sector weights at N=32 for q=1/3 and q=1. Proposal counts were
4,0,12,1; requested random-bit counts were 221,9,899,266. All rational
descriptor checks passed, with approximately 0.009 seconds of script
