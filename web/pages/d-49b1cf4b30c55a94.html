## 1. Independent bound on Hamiltonian coefficient separation

The eigenvalues of `(1-1/d)I+P` are `a=1-1/d` and `b=2-1/d`. Congruence gives

\[
a\sigma\preceq\rho\preceq b\sigma.
\]

The logarithm is operator monotone on positive matrices. For completeness, its integral representation is

\[
\log A=\int_0^\infty\left[\frac{I}{1+t}-(A+tI)^{-1}\right]dt.
\]

If `0<A<=B`, conjugation shows `(A+tI)^-1>=(B+tI)^-1`; integration gives `log A<=log B`. Since multiplication by a scalar commutes with sigma,

\[
\log\sigma+(\log a)I\preceq\log\rho
\preceq\log\sigma+(\log b)I.
\]

Therefore the Hermitian difference has

\[
\|\log\rho-\log\sigma\|_{op}
\le\eta_d:=\max\{-\log(1-1/d),\log(2-1/d)\}.
\]

For d=64, `eta_d=log(127/64)=0.6853040030989194`.

For any nonidentity Pauli P_j, the dimensionless Hamiltonian coefficient of a state tau is

\[
\theta_j(\tau)=-\frac{1}{\beta d}\operatorname{Tr}(P_j\log\tau).
\]

Removing the identity coefficient of `-log tau` has no effect on these coefficients. Schatten duality and `||P_j||_1=d` imply

\[
|\theta_j(\rho)-\theta_j(\sigma)|\le\eta_d/\beta.
\]

At the certified beta this is `3.6674873186592205e-8`. The midpoint coefficient vector remains inside the common convex coefficient box and lies within half this bound of both vectors. Tensoring isolated blocks adds the same block terms; **the coordinatewise bound does not increase with the number of blocks**.

Thus state separation approaching its maximum after sufficiently many blocks is compatible with one coefficient vector approximating both Hamiltonians to 1.84e-8. This is not an algorithm for acquiring that midpoint; it proves that the two-point collision itself is no obstruction to coefficient accuracy at coarser tolerance. Exact asymptotic identifiability is still lost by the score objective. If beta were rescaled, both the coefficient precision and the interaction normalization must be reported again; the tiny bound is for the programme's stated common-beta embedding.

## 2. Independent all-measurement lower bound for useful amplification

Both states have the same diagonal in U's eigenbasis. In particular their probability of `|u_0>` is

\[
p_0=\frac{1-s^2}{1-s^{2d}}\ge1-s^2.
\]

Here is a direct purification argument, avoiding any new assumption about the implementation of U. Any state tau with `<u_0|tau|u_0>=p_0` has a purification whose ancillary unitary is chosen so its projection onto system `|u_0>` is `sqrt(p_0)|u_0>|e_0>`. Write those purifications for sigma and rho as

\[
|\psi_\sigma\rangle=\sqrt{p_0}|u_0,e_0\rangle+|r_\sigma\rangle,
\qquad
|\psi_\rho\rangle=\sqrt{p_0}|u_0,e_0\rangle+|r_\rho\rangle.
\]

The residuals have squared norm `1-p_0` and are orthogonal to the first component. Their overlap has modulus at least `p_0-(1-p_0)=2p_0-1`, by Cauchy–Schwarz. For `s<=1/sqrt(2)` this is at least `1-2s^2>=0`.

For k tensor powers, use tensor powers of these purifications. The trace distance of pure states with overlap c is `sqrt(1-|c|^2)`, by diagonalizing their rank-two difference. Partial trace contracts trace distance: equivalently any outcome test after tracing is one allowed outcome test before tracing. Hence, defining `T=||rho-sigma||_1/2`,

\[
T(\rho^{\otimes k},\sigma^{\otimes k})
\le\sqrt{1-(1-2s^2)^{2k}}
\le2s\sqrt{k}.
\]

The final inequality uses `1-(1-x)^r<=rx` for `x in [0,1]`. This proof applies to any faithful collision in the displayed fiber sharing these diagonals, independently of its exact t and independently of whether the physical measurement is local, collective, adaptive, or supplied the witness basis.

With equal prior probabilities, any binary measurement has success probability at most `(1+T)/2`: for its decision effect `0<=E<=I`, the excess success is `Tr[E(rho-sigma)]/2<=T/2`. Therefore error at most delta requires

\[
k\ge\frac{(1-2\delta)^2}{4s^2}.
\]

For delta=.1 this is `k>=4/(25*s^2)`. Reconstructing the exact integer denominator from the certificate gives the ceiling of this lower bound a **296599-bit integer**, with base-two logarithm **296598.0887597404**. The six-qubit coherence observable previously suggested supplies an upper bound with the same `s^-2` order and additional d/t constants. The present result supplies the missing lower bound: no better measurement can remove the catastrophic scale of this conservative witness.

Resource interpretation is essential. k copies means k independent preparations of one six-qubit block. One copy of an amplified 6k-qubit product state contains k independent blocks instead; then the bound charges the physical block count/system size. Tensor amplification is mathematically valid and genuinely reaches maximal state distance, but useful separation is not free in either interpretation.

## 3. Local Hamiltonian moments still identify the Gibbs state

Let `rho_H=exp(-beta H)/Z_H` and `rho_K=exp(-beta K)/Z_K`, with beta>0 and H,K in the same finite known Pauli linear span. Direct substitution gives the symmetrized relative entropy identity

\[
D(\rho_H\|\rho_K)+D(\rho_K\|\rho_H)
=\beta\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho_H-\rho_K)(K-H)].
\]

If all model-term moments agree, `Tr(P_j rho_H)=Tr(P_j rho_K)` for every supplied P_j, the right side vanishes. Nonnegativity and the equality case of relative entropy imply `rho_H=rho_K`. Taking logarithms gives `H-K` a scalar identity; excluding the identity from the linearly independent Pauli model makes H=K.

For completeness, reconstruct the needed equality case directly. Diagonalize positive states A and B with eigenvalues a_i,b_j and eigenvectors u_i,v_j. Put `w_ij=|<u_i,v_j>|^2`, whose row and column sums are one. Then

\[
D(A\|B)=\sum_{ij}w_{ij}\left[a_i\log(a_i/b_j)-a_i+b_j\right]\ge0.
\]

The scalar summands are nonnegative, since `x log x-x+1>=0`, with equality only at x=1. Equality of the sum requires `a_i=b_j` whenever w_ij>0. Consequently `B u_i=a_i u_i` for every i, by expansion in the v_j basis, and B=A. This proves both nonnegativity and the strict equality case used above, without borrowing an uninspected Hamiltonian-learning lemma. It is established mathematics, not a novelty claim.

Thus the two distinct Gibbs states in the programme's model must differ in at least one **six-local model Pauli moment**. No target-dependent U-basis coherence measurement is logically required: measuring all 4095 nonidentity six-qubit Paulis on the block suffices to detect some distinction. This is a fixed finite local measurement catalogue, though its cost and the required precision are substantial. For isolated blocks it scales by the number of blocks, not by global tomography on 6k qubits. This does not claim that moment inversion is computationally efficient for general connected systems, nor that small moment error gives uniformly stable Hamiltonian recovery at low temperature.

Therefore the strongest valid interpretation is a failure of an objective's globally identifying statistic outside its positive regime, rather than impossibility of Hamiltonian acquisition. The score method can exploit copies without reconstructing the full SLD; conversely an algebraic theorem about operator-valued score equality is not a ready-made sampling protocol. Any claimed repair or advantage must state whether it acquires target scores, only estimates candidate gradient observables, or adds ordinary Pauli moments. Candidate evolution and measurement synthesis remain charged resources when comparing algorithms.

There is a further distinction: **equal target scores do not prove identical loss/gradient-oracle transcripts**. If sigma and rho share all target scores `S*_a`, then for another candidate tau,

\[
J_\sigma(\tau)-J_\rho(\tau)
=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\left[(\sigma-\rho)
\sum_a(S_a(\tau)-S_a^*)^2\right].
\]

This expression can retain target-dependent information through the weighting state. The collision establishes common zero-loss minimizers, not equality of the entire functions or all candidate gradient expectations. An adaptive learner that deliberately queries other observables/candidate gradients is not covered by a supposed statistical indistinguishability theorem from score equality alone. Whether the attainable gradient-observable span distinguishes this particular pair is open; no assertion of its equality or separation is made here. Minimization's failed Fisher consistency is still exact.
