## All-dimension ideal theorem

Let n>=1, let L be any real invertible n-by-n matrix, and let Q be any real symmetric positive-definite n-by-n matrix. Define the real homogeneous quartic

    f(x)=((Lx)^2)^T Q ((Lx)^2).

Only for the proof, work in hidden coordinates y=Lx. Let T be the symmetric quartic polarization, F=f(v), g(x)=T(v,v,v,x), and form the intrinsic reflection remainder

    P(v,x)=F²T(v,x,x,x)-3F g(x)T(v,v,x,x)+2g(x)^3.

Let I be the homogeneous degree-nine ideal generated by its cubic-in-x coefficients; let J be the reduced homogeneous ideal, over C, of the complex involutive linear-reflection directions preserving f. Both are defined intrinsically from f. Then

    f^6 J subset I subset J,                            (1)
    J_d={h homogeneous of degree d: f^6h in I_(d+24)}
        for every d, in particular d=5,6.               (2)

The maximum degrees required for the coefficient spaces in (2) are 29 and30. The bound6 is independent of n,L,Q and their condition numbers. Claim(1) is an ideal containment for every input in the promise, not a nonvanishing statement about a global polynomial vector field.

Here is the full composition. For n>=3, every real-PD principal triple Q_II satisfies the local theorem. In its raw identity substitute m_i=v_i²/sqrt(F), s_i=(Qv²)_i/sqrt(F). ## Charged dimensions, bit sizes and storage

Let B be the total binary encoding length of the expanded rational coefficient list, including denominators. Clearing denominators yields integers with O(B) bits. P uses fixed cubic products of those coefficients and fixed derivatives; f^6 uses six fixed products. Their coefficient bits are O(B+log n), since the number of summands per fixed-degree coefficient is polynomial in n. Hidden heights and auxiliary certificate denominators do not enter this construction.

At ambient degree D in {29,30}, the explicit Macaulay coefficient matrix has at most

    C_D=binom(n+D-1,D) columns,
    R_D=binom(n+2,3)*binom(n+D-10,D-9) rows.

The binomial(n+D-10,D-9) factor counts allowed degree-(D-9) monomial multipliers. Preimage multiplication adds N_d=binom(n+d-1,d) coefficient vectors with d=D-24. In particular

    C30=binom(n+29,30),
    R30=binom(n+2,3)*binom(n+20,21).

For fixed degree30, C30=O(n^30), R30=O(n^24), and naive dense initial storage is O(n^54(B+log n)) bits. This is a conservative explicit implementation, not a sparse-lower-bound or an assertion that this entire dense allocation is necessary. Each initial Macaulay row shifts a degree-nine generator with at most binom(n+8,9) supported coefficients; initial nonzero entries therefore have upper bound R30*binom(n+8,9)=O(n^33). Generator templates and monomial shifts can avoid materializing repeated entries. This sparse-initial observation does not bound exact elimination fill-in or by itself produce a small structured solver. Exact elimination adds coefficient growth: with k<=min(C30,R30+N6), determinant bounds give O(k(B+log n+log k)) bits per rational entry. A dense Gaussian implementation uses polynomially many bit operations and can be charged conservatively by O(C30(R30+N6)k(B+log n+log k)) stored bits and polynomial rational arithmetic. Any more economical implementation or quantitative runtime must be measured or separately proved.
