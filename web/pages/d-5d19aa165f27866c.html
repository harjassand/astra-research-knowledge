# Statistical learning / optimization intake and cost ledger

## Selected changed premise

The Oct5 release229 companion provides an explicit preserved posterior class for ferromagnetic four-state Potts experiments, including all positive channel parameters, with uniform cubic information saving under every conditionally independent product. The accompanying capacity paper only translates it to constant lambda<1 and bounded-degree known trees. The source's translation incurs B^3 in necessity and 1/(1-lambda) in sufficiency.

The new direct argument replaces those costs by pairwise subadditivity of m+Km^3, a universal third-moment product estimate, and a flow-ray quadratic occupation identity. This removes uniform degree and channel-contraction bounds, and gives the exact weighted increment potential that survives deterministic edges and arbitrary edge subdivision. Full note: weighted_capacity_proof.md.

The deterministic weighted-capacity theorem does not prove this graph claim.

## Costs and legal input

The positive theorem is mathematical, not a complete learn-and-deploy algorithm. The complete deterministic tree and channel parameters are supplied; boundary spins are the actual statistical observations. Existence of an infinite finite-energy flow is not its efficient discovery. Arbitrary computable infinite-tree positivity need not admit finite recognition. No terminal label, target-specific advice, or optimum is acquired for free by naming this characterization.

For a finite explicitly supplied tree, standard four-state belief propagation evaluates a realized posterior in O(number of edges) arithmetic operations. Large products require stable log representation and a charged precision budget in a bit model. The current proof imposes no robustness promise for unknown channel parameters at criticality.

Research work used local source retrieval and exact CPU-only arithmetic. Source acquisition used gh api at the pinned OpenAI commit. No installs, costly jobs, publication, messages to people, or push occurred. Two delegated audit tasks, including the child's numerical adversarial task, independently checked the release polynomial inequalities and the extension; their outcomes remain scoped evidence.
