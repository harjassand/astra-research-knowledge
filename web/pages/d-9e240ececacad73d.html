# Any finite interior weighted-score list can remain blind

Status: complete algebraic theorem, independently reconstructed from r06's Vandermonde circuit construction. This is a general known Hermitian frame result, not a fixed physical local Pauli result. Direct rational whole-score fixtures are independently supplied by r03 and b_math. Literature priority is not established.

## Exact interface and quantifiers

For each faithful target sigma=diag(p)/sum(p), the weighted unitary-orbit score has an invertible scalar denominator. Its similarity-transformed operator has entries

K_lambda(A)_ij = sqrt(p_i p_j) A_ij / ((1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j).

The equal-score faithful fiber is sqrt(sigma) Q sqrt(sigma), for positive Hermitian Q commuting with K_lambda and its adjoint, normalized by tr(sigma Q)=1. The adjoint corresponds to weight1-lambda for Hermitian A. This is the full weighted-score equation, not only its Hermitian component.

For EVERY finite interior weight set, there exist a dimension, a SAME fixed Hermitian ordinary-irreducible frame, and a SAME distinct faithful state pair whose entire score operators agree at all those weights. Close the set under lambda->1-lambda, remove duplicates, and call its size k. Halfweight occurs once. Take any d>=k+2 and distinct positive numbers p_i. Endpoints are excluded essentially.

## Circuit construction

Let D=diag(p), e=ones, and W=span{e,De,...,D^(k-1)e}. For a (k+1)-subset S define z supported on S by

z_i = 1/product_{j in S, j!=i}(p_i-p_j).

Lagrange interpolation's leading coefficient gives sum_i z_i p_i^r=0 for r=0,...,k-1. Put Z=z z^T and define the commuting left/right polynomial operator

H = product_{lambda in Lambda}[(1-lambda)L_D+lambda R_D](Z),
A = D^(-1/2) H D^(-1/2).

Complement closure makes H and A real symmetric. Its entries are

A_ij = z_i z_j /sqrt(p_i p_j) * product_lambda[(1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j].

Removing one factor gives exactly K_lambda(A). Expand its remaining degree k-1 polynomial: every term is D^a Z D^b with a+b=k-1. Applied to e it vanishes because ZD^b e=0. Thus K_lambda e=0. The complement weight ensures K_lambda^T e=0 as well, so ALL specified operators commute with P=e e^T/d. No imaginary/adjoint constraint is discarded.

## Ordinary irreducibility is exact

The support-S block of A is invertible. Write

P(x,y)=product_lambda[(1-lambda)x+lambda y]=sum_{r=0}^k c_r x^r y^(k-r),

where EVERY c_r>0 since all weights are strictly interior. The matrix P(p_i,p_j) on S is V antiDiag(c_0,...,c_k) V^T, with V the invertible (k+1)-square Vandermonde matrix. Multiplication by the invertible diagonal z_i/sqrt(p_i) preserves invertibility. All offdiagonal entries within S are nonzero.

Using every S, a common commutant preserves each coordinate support S: the corresponding support projector is the spectral projector onto the nonzero spectrum of A_S. These projectors separate every coordinate, forcing the commutant to be diagonal. Every pair lies in some S, and nonzero entries force equal diagonal values. Therefore the ordinary commutant is scalar.

An O(d)-observable frame suffices: take the d cyclic consecutive supports of size k+1 (indices modulo d). Distinct coordinates have distinct membership patterns in those supports, so their projectors still separate coordinates. The support graph contains the coordinate cycle, hence is connected; diagonality plus its nonzero edges again forces a scalar. At minimal d=k+2 these are precisely all omit-one supports.

Endpoints have a vanishing endpoint polynomial coefficient. The support-block invertibility proof then fails, consistent with their known identification theorem. For a continuum of weights, analyticity and the endpoint equations also change the conclusion. Neither statement is refuted here.

## Faithful macroscopically separated pair without extreme target spectrum

Choose distinct p_i in[1,2]. Define psi_i=sqrt(p_i)/sqrt(sum(p)) and

rho=.5 sigma+.5 |psi><psi| = sqrt(sigma)[.5 I+(d/2)P]sqrt(sigma).

The bracket is positive and has sigma-weighted trace1. It commutes with all K_lambda and adjoints, so the same rho matches every score of every frame observable. Both states are faithful. lambda_min(sigma)>=1/(2d), lambda_min(rho)>=1/(4d), kappa(sigma)<=2, and kappa(rho)<=4d. Measuring the projector onto psi proves

T(rho,sigma) >= .5[1-sum(p_i^2)/(sum p_i)^2] >= .5(1-2/d) >=1/6.

For rational weights choose rational q_i in[1,sqrt(2)], p_i=q_i^2: the pair and frame are rational. Each observable can be rescaled independently to norm<=1 while preserving the obstruction and ordinary irreducibility.

This does not bound the ordinary-frame commutator gap, support-block condition numbers, model preparation or observable acquisition cost. Vandermonde circuits may be severely conditioned. Macroscopic separation and moderate target spectrum are meaningful, but do not establish an efficiently learnable or efficiently prepared Hamiltonian model.

## Only three frame observables are needed at the minimum construction dimension

At d=k+2, the Vandermonde nullspace W^perp has dimension2. Each omit-one circuit vector is a line in this space. No two such lines coincide: otherwise a nonzero vector supported on their k-coordinate intersection would annihilate k Vandermonde moments, impossible by invertibility. Any three distinct circuit lines have linearly independent rank-one outer products in Sym(W^perp): this is the real degree-two Veronese curve, and a nonzero quadratic cannot vanish at three distinct projective points. Thus three selected circuits span all symmetric Z annihilating W. Their images span every circuit observable used in the irreducibility argument. Those SAME THREE Hermitian observables therefore already have scalar ordinary commutant while remaining blind at all prescribed weights. Independent r06 derivation; this reduction uses no numerical claim that two suffice.

## A finite dimension-dependent positive repair

The negative theorem lets dimension grow with the finite list. It does NOT say finite lists always fail in a known fixed dimension. b_math independently proved this sufficient upper bound: d(d-1) distinct values h=lambda(1-lambda) identify every faithful state under any supplied ordinary-irreducible Hermitian frame. Complementary weights add no information since their scores are adjoints.

Let R=d(d-1)/2. In the eigenbasis of one state, the Hermitian part of its weighted score has offdiagonal entries proportional to

i(p_i-p_j)(p_i+p_j) A_ij/[2(p_i p_j+h(p_i-p_j)^2)].

Every physical-basis matrix entry is a rational function of h with denominator degree at most R and numerator degree at most R-1. The difference between two states has numerator degree at most2R-1. Equal score operators at2R distinct h imply that numerator is identically zero, so their Hermitian parts coincide at every interior weight. Integrating the symmetrized score over lambda from0 to1 gives the unitary derivative of the logarithm, -i[A,log rho]. Hence [A,log rho-log sigma]=0 for all frame observables. Ordinary irreducibility makes the log difference scalar, and normalization makes the states equal. Degenerate eigenvalues lower the rational degree and cause no problem.

This is an exact identification interface, not a sample bound. For n qubits the bound uses d(d-1)=4^n-2^n distinct channels, and interpolating a noisy rational function can be ill conditioned. Endpoint scores give the known stronger simpler exact repair when legally available. Finite-list failure and dimension-dependent sufficiency are therefore compatible.
