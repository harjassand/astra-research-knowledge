# Cycle 4 distinct falsification route: rare outliers and topological almost-commuting frames

Date: 2026-10-08

## Status

**No counterexample from this route; global target remains UNKNOWN.** The standard clock–shift/Bott pair gives a sharp warning about the norm: its operator-norm obstruction does not obstruct full-algebra EB reconstruction in normalized trace distance for bounded tracial perturbation states. In fact a Weyl-covariant rank-one POVM/prepare channel is a common full-domain EB decoder and, by measuring then preparing two copies of each outcome state, also an actual full-domain CPTP broadcaster. For fixed clock–shift coordinates its error is \(O(1/d)\). More generally, the same decoder handles rectangular Weyl-mode families whenever their phase-space area satisfies \(RS/d\to0\).

This is a candidate-mechanism no-go, not a theorem that every almost-commuting family or every bounded-Holevo experiment classicalizes. A broader high-frequency structured family with a proved EB lower bound and a common compatible broadcaster was not found.

## Interface and state families

On \(\mathcal H_d=\mathbb C^d\), use \(\tau(X)=\mathrm{Tr}(X)/d\) and normalized trace distance for states as usual. Let \(X,Z\) be the finite Weyl shift/clock unitaries,

\[
 X|k\rangle=|k+1\pmod d\rangle,\qquad
 Z|k\rangle=e^{2\pi i k/d}|k\rangle,
\]

so \(ZX=e^{2\pi i/d}XZ\). For \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\) define \(W_{a,b}=X^aZ^b\) and Hermitian quadratures

\[
 H_{a,b,\phi}=\tfrac12(e^{i\phi}W_{a,b}+e^{-i\phi}W_{a,b}^*),
 \qquad \phi\in\{0,\pi/2\}.
\]

For odd \(d\), these are traceless Hermitian contractions. Fix \(0<\theta<1\) and take the state family

\[
 E_d(R,S)=\{\rho_{a,b,\phi,\pm}=d^{-1}(I\pm\theta H_{a,b,\phi}):
 0<\max(|a|,|b|),\ |a|\le R,\ |b|\le S\}.
\]

Every member is a density operator. It is full rank for \(\theta<1\). The family includes the Hermitian coordinate parts of the standard clock–shift pair when \(R=S=1\).

### Holevo capacity, all priors

For any \(H=H^*\), \(\tau(H)=0\), \(\|H\|_\infty\le1\),

\[
D(d^{-1}(I+\theta H)\|I/d)
 =\tau[(I+\theta H)\ln(I+\theta H)]
 \le \frac{\theta^2}{2(1-\theta)}\tau(H^2)
 \le \frac{\theta^2}{2(1-\theta)}.
\]

Indeed \(g(x)=(1+x)\ln(1+x)-x\) has \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\) and \(g''(x)=1/(1+x)\le1/(1-\theta)\) for \(|x|\le\theta\); the linear term vanishes under \(\tau(H)=0\). For any prior \(p\),

\[
\chi(p,E_d) = \sum_xp_xD(\rho_x\|I/d)-D(\bar\rho\|I/d)
 \le \frac{\theta^2}{2(1-\theta)}.
\]

Thus \(C(E_d(R,S))\le\theta^2/[2(1-\theta)]\), independently of \(d,R,S\), and of the number of modes. This is an actual all-prior entropy-radius bound, not an accessible-information or rank estimate.

## One common full-algebra EB decoder

Let \(|\psi_w\rangle\) be a normalized periodized discrete Gaussian on \(\mathbb Z_d\), centered at zero with width \(w\), where \(1\ll w\ll d\). Its elementary discrete-gradient/second-moment estimates are

\[
\|(X^a-I)\psi_w\|_2\le C|a|/w,\qquad
\|(Z^b-I)\psi_w\|_2\le C|b|w/d.
\]

The first follows by telescoping shifts and the one-step Gaussian gradient bound; the second follows from \(|e^{iu}-1|\le|u|\) and \(\sum_k k^2|\psi_w(k)|^2\le Cw^2\), using centered representatives. Therefore

\[
1-|\langle\psi_w|W_{a,b}|\psi_w\rangle|^2
 \le \|W_{a,b}\psi_w-\psi_w\|_2^2
 \le C'\left(\frac{a^2}{w^2}+\frac{b^2w^2}{d^2}\right).
\]

The first inequality is the identity \(\|W\psi-\psi\|^2=2(1-\mathrm{Re}\langle\psi,W\psi\rangle)\) together with \(1-|z|^2\le2(1-\mathrm{Re}z)\). The second uses \(W=X^aZ^b\) and the triangle inequality. Constants are absolute for the regime \(w\to\infty\), \(w/d\to0\).

Use the Weyl orbit \(|\psi_{p,q}\rangle=W_{p,q}|\psi_w\rangle\) to define effects and preparations

\[
 M_{p,q}=d^{-1}|\psi_{p,q}\rangle\langle\psi_{p,q}|,
 \qquad \sigma_{p,q}=|\psi_{p,q}\rangle\langle\psi_{p,q}|.
\]

The Weyl twirl identity gives \(\sum_{p,q}M_{p,q}=I\). Hence

\[
 \Psi_{d,w}(A)=\sum_{p,q}\mathrm{Tr}(M_{p,q}A)\,\sigma_{p,q}
\]

is a full-domain entanglement-breaking channel. Weyl covariance makes each \(W_{a,b}\) an eigenoperator, with multiplier

\[
 \mu_{a,b}=|\langle\psi_w|W_{a,b}|\psi_w\rangle|^2.
\]

This follows by expanding the rank-one orbit projectors in the Weyl basis and summing their characters. The same real multiplier acts on both Hermitian quadratures. Therefore, for every state in \(E_d(R,S)\),

\[
T(\Psi_{d,w}(\rho_{a,b,\phi,\pm}),\rho_{a,b,\phi,\pm})
 =\frac{\theta}{2}(1-\mu_{a,b})\tau(|H_{a,b,\phi}|)
 \le \frac{\theta}{2}(1-\mu_{a,b}).
\]

When \(R,S\ge1\) and \(RS/d\to0\), choose \(w^2=dR/S\) (integer rounding is harmless). Then \(R/w\to0\), \(Sw/d\to0\), \(w\to\infty\), and \(w/d\to0\); uniformly over the rectangle,

\[
\sup_{|a|\le R,|b|\le S}(1-\mu_{a,b})\le C''RS/d\longrightarrow0.
\]

It follows that

\[
e_{\rm EB}(E_d(R,S))\le C''\theta RS/(2d)\to0.
\]

This is one common global POVM and one preparation rule for the entire family; it does not select a mode-dependent basis. In the standard fixed-coordinate topological/Bott test, \(R=S=1\), so \(C(E_d)\le\theta^2/[2(1-\theta)]\) while \(e_{\rm EB}(E_d)=O(\theta/d)\). Thus no compatible channel lower bound is needed to reject it as a counterexample: its EB error already vanishes.

The same POVM gives a legal symmetric CPTP broadcaster on every input,

\[
 \mathcal B_{d,w}(A)=\sum_{p,q}\mathrm{Tr}(M_{p,q}A)\,
       \sigma_{p,q}\otimes\sigma_{p,q}.
\]

It is trace preserving, completely positive, defined on all \(B(\mathbb C^d)\), and both marginals equal \(\Psi_{d,w}\). Hence \(b_2(E_d(R,S))\le e_{\rm EB}(E_d(R,S))=O(\theta RS/d)\) in the low-area regime. This verifies the two small-error quantities for the standard candidate, while proving the opposite of its desired EB lower bound.

## What this says about the topological obstruction

The clock–shift commutation phase is \(e^{2\pi i/d}\), and operator-norm almost-commuting-unitary results can have a nonzero Bott obstruction to replacing the pair by commuting unitaries in operator norm. The target metric here is trace distance of density operators. For the bounded perturbation states above, a Weyl-covariant EB map retains the low Weyl modes with multipliers \(1-O(1/d)\). EB reconstruction of these states does not require a multiplicative approximation of \(X,Z\), nor a pair of commuting operator approximants. The operator-norm Bott obstruction is therefore aimed at the wrong metric and wrong output object for this state family.

For a growing rectangle, the discrete coherent-state width balances the shift and phase errors. The scale condition \(RS=o(d)\) is exactly what makes both \(R/w=o(1)\) and \(Sw/d=o(1)\) possible. If one takes \(RS\) of order \(d\) or larger, this EB estimate no longer tends to zero. But no full-domain broadcaster with \(b_2\to0\) and no EB lower bound were obtained in that regime. The failed estimate is not evidence that \(e_{\rm EB}\) stays positive.

### Fuzzy-sphere coordinate triple: exact covariant EB control

The same issue appears for the spin-\(j\) fuzzy sphere. Let \(d=2j+1\), \(H_i=J_i/j\) for \(i=x,y,z\), and

\[
 \rho_{i,\pm}=d^{-1}(I\pm\theta H_i),\qquad 0<\theta<1.
\]

The three Hermitian coordinates have \(\tau(H_i)=0\), \(\|H_i\|_\infty=1\), and \(\|H_i\|_2^2=(j+1)/(3j)\). The same relative-entropy-radius estimate above proves \(C(E_j)\le\theta^2/[2(1-\theta)]\), for every prior on these six states.

Use the spin coherent-state POVM \(M(dn)=d|n,j\rangle\langle n,j|\,d\Omega/(4\pi)\) and prepare \(|n,j\rangle\langle n,j|\). Its EB channel is

\[
 \Psi_j(A)=d\int \langle n,j|A|n,j\rangle |n,j\rangle\langle n,j|\,\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}.
\]

The resolution identity makes this a full-domain channel. Rotational covariance makes \(J_i\) an eigenoperator. For \(i=z\),

\[
 \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(J_z\Psi_j(J_z))}{\mathrm{Tr}(J_z^2)}
 =\frac{d j^2\int n_z^2\,d\Omega/(4\pi)}{d j(j+1)/3}
 =\frac{j}{j+1},
\]

and covariance gives the same eigenvalue for \(x,y\). Therefore

\[
 \sup_{i,\pm}T(\Psi_j(\rho_{i,\pm}),\rho_{i,\pm})
 =\frac{\theta}{2(j+1)}\,\tau(|H_i|)
 \le\frac{\theta}{2(j+1)}\to0.
\]

The measure-and-prepare two-output map

\[
 \mathcal B_j(A)=d\int \mathrm{Tr}(|n,j\rangle\langle n,j|A)
 |n,j\rangle\langle n,j|^{\otimes2}\,\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}
\]

is a symmetric CPTP channel on all of \(B(\mathbb C^{2j+1})\) with both marginals \(\Psi_j\). Hence this bounded-Holevo, topologically motivated coordinate family has both \(b_2\to0\) and \(e_{\rm EB}\to0\), with explicit rate \(O(1/j)\). This directly shows why operator-norm non-approximability of the fuzzy-sphere coordinates cannot be used as a normalized-trace EB lower bound.

## Rare-outlier checks

1. **A small convex signal has a direct full-algebra EB decoder.** If every state is \(\rho_x=(1-\delta_d)\tau_d+\delta_d\sigma_x\), then the replacer \(A\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(A)\tau_d\) gives \(e_{\rm EB}\le\delta_d\). So making an outlier signal vanish in trace weight cannot leave a positive worst-case EB error.

2. **Calling a label rare under one prior does not reduce capacity.** The capacity is a supremum over priors, so the outlier can be reweighted. A two-state prior alone contributes at most \(\ln2\), which shows that a single exceptional state is not ruled out by a constant capacity bound; it does not provide a broadcaster or an EB lower bound. For a growing outlier class, one still needs an all-prior Holevo proof for the full set.

3. **An orthogonal classical outlier flag is readable by EB.** If the outlier class occupies a perfectly distinguishable input block, the EB decoder can measure that block flag and invoke its blockwise full-algebra decoder. A matching full-domain broadcaster can also condition on the block. Merely assigning small prior mass to a flagged block does not hide it from the worst-case decoder; if its state weight is small, the replacer bound above removes its EB-error contribution.

4. **Bott modes are not yet an outlier ensemble.** Adding a collection of modes still inside a rectangle with \(RS=o(d)\) leaves the same common EB decoder effective. Modes outside that window might defeat this particular decoder, but require a new all-prior capacity calculation, one common compatible-channel construction, and a lower bound against every global POVM. None was supplied here.

## Exact failed gate, candidate controls, and limits

The requested topological counterexample would need states for which a full-domain compatible marginal has vanishing maximum trace displacement while every full-algebra EB channel has a positive minimum maximum displacement. For the standard almost-commuting clock–shift coordinates, the common Weyl-covariant EB decoder is already \(O(1/d)\)-accurate, so the third condition fails. Enlarging to \(RS=o(d)\) still makes the third condition fail by the explicit \(O(RS/d)\) bound. Enlarging beyond that scale only removes this upper bound; it does not certify a positive EB lower bound or any broadcaster.

Strong controls remain: (i) N61's splitter ladder has a legal full-domain broadcaster and a global EB lower bound but unbounded Holevo capacity; (ii) N93's fixed-spectrum Clifford/Pauli family has bounded Holevo capacity and a global EB lower bound but nonvanishing pair-broadcast error; (iii) the flagged copy ladders admit global EB decoders. This route is distinct: it uses bounded-entropy Weyl and fuzzy-sphere perturbations and explicitly constructs global EB channels that defeat the operator-norm topological obstruction.

Costs: the Weyl POVM has \(d^2\) rank-one outcomes and the broadcaster prepares \(d^2\) product states; the fuzzy-sphere POVM has a continuous sphere outcome and its standard finite-dimensional covariant integral. These are mathematical full-domain channels, not an efficiency claim. The Gaussian and spin-1 multiplier estimates are analytic; no finite SDP was run. The capacity and EB formulas above are direct derivations. Topological language is used only to describe the motivating operator-norm obstruction; no priority claim is made. Overall bounded-Holevo plus \(b_2\to0\Rightarrow e_{\rm EB}\to0\) remains **UNKNOWN**.
