1 Introduction
Horn and Jackson [15] considered the class of weakly reversible mass-action systems and they showed that complex balanced equilibria are unique and asymptotically stable relative to their positive stoichiometric class by providing a global Lyapunov function. However, existence of complex balanced equilibria is a rare situation. On the other hand, every weakly reversible mass-action system has a positive equilibrium in each positive stoichiometric class [4]. In general, little can be said about the number of equilibria and their stability. It is natural to ask whether other (more global) dynamical properties hold for weakly reversible mass-action systems. For example whether all species coexist for all times and their concentrations remain bounded. These ideas have been formalized as the concepts of persistence, boundedness, and permanence, and several conjectures have been formulated for weakly reversible (and even more general classes, like endotactic) mass-action systems, see e.g. [7], [2], [1], [8], [16], [12], [13], and [3]. Many of these papers use the classical, entropy-like Lyapunov function by Horn and Jackson. In particular, Anderson shows persistence and boundedness for weakly reversible mass-action systems with a single linkage class (see [2] and [1]), Gopalkrishnan, Miller, and Shiu extend this to show permanence for the even larger class of strongly endotactic mass-action systems (see [12] and [13]), and Anderson, Cappelletti, Kim, and Nguyen give further insight (see [3]).
In the present paper, we provide a new proof for the permanence of weakly reversible mass-action systems with a single linkage class. We simplify the previous approaches, and give a more concise and elementary proof of the fact that a variant of the classical Horn-Jackson function is a Lyapunov function near the boundary of the state space. We crucially use ideas from [9] and [4]. On the other hand, we demonstrate in section 7 that the method breaks down for weakly reversible networks with multiple linkage classes.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. After introducing some notations and the necessary notions from chemical reaction network theory (CRNT) in sections 2 and 3, respectively, we state the main result of this paper in section 4. We collect a series of lemmata in section 5 and prove the main result in section 6. We illustrate the limitations of the method in section 7. Finally, we display in appendix A the dependency of the statements in sections 4 and 5 via an acyclic digraph.

3 Mass-action systems
We give a very brief introduction to the basic notions of CRNT. For more details, the reader is advised to consult e.g. [10], [11], and [14].
A reaction network is a triple (𝒳,𝒞,ℛ)(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R}), where 𝒳\mathcal{X}, 𝒞\mathcal{C}, and ℛ\mathcal{R} are the set of species, complexes, and reactions, respectively. Throughout the paper, we use n=|𝒳|n=|\mathcal{X}| and m=|𝒞|m=|\mathcal{C}|. The complexes are formal linear combinations of the species, the coefficients are stored in the matrix Y=[y1,…,ym]∈ℝn×mY=[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}. The iith complex is then (yi)1​𝖷1+⋯+(yi)n​𝖷n(y_{i})_{1}\mathsf{X}_{1}+\cdots+(y_{i})_{n}\mathsf{X}_{n}, where 𝖷1,…,𝖷n\mathsf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{X}_{n} denote the species. The set ℛ\mathcal{R} consists of ordered pairs of complexes, the first and the second element of the pair are called reactant complex and product complex, respectively.
The weak components of the digraph (𝒞,ℛ)(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R}) are called linkage classes. The reaction network is said to be weakly reversible if all the weak components of the digraph (𝒞,ℛ)(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R}) are strongly connected, i.e., for every pair (i,j)(i,j) of complexes, the existence of a directed path from ii to jj implies the existence of a directed path from jj to ii. Most of the results in this paper are about weakly reversible networks with a single linkage class, i.e., the digraph (𝒞,ℛ)(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R}) is strongly connected. When having a single linkage class network, usually it is required that there are at least two complexes and all the complexes are distinct. However, we assume only that there exist at least two complexes that are distinct (thus, there might be i,j∈{1,…,m}i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\} such that i≠ji\neq j and yi=yjy_{i}=y_{j}). We make this weakening for convenience.
Denoting by x⁡(τ)∈ℝ+nx(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+} the concentration vector of the species at time τ\tau, assuming mass-action kinetics, the time evolution of the species concentration vector is described by the autonomous ordinary differential equation (ODE)

The linear subspace 𝒮\mathcal{S} of ℝ+n\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}, spanned by the set {yi−yj|(i,j)∈ℛ}\{y_{i}-y_{j}~|~(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}\}, is called the stoichiometric subspace. The relevant approach is to study the dynamics within a fixed positive stoichometric class.
4 Main result
The formal definition of permanence is the following.
Definition 1.
A mass-action system with bounded kinetics is said to be permanent on the positive stoichiometric class 𝒫\mathcal{P} if there exists a compact set K⊆𝒫K\subseteq\mathcal{P} with the property that for each solution τ↦x⁡(τ)\tau\mapsto x(\tau) with x⁡(0)∈𝒫x(0)\in\mathcal{P}, there exists a τ0≥0\tau_{0}\geq 0 such that for all τ≥τ0\tau\geq\tau_{0} we have x⁡(τ)∈Kx(\tau)\in K.
Results on permanence of planar systems include [17], [8], [6], and [5], while in the papers [1] and [13] there is no restriction on the number of species. The Permanence Conjecture states that all weakly reversible mass-action systems with bounded kinetics are permanent in each positive stoichiometric class. In [12] and [13], the conjecture is proven in the single linkage class case, see [13, Theorem 1.3], and the same result follows from [3, Theorem 5.5] in case the rate constants do not depend on time. In this paper, we give a more concise proof of the single linkage class case of the conjecture.
Next, we state our main result as Theorem 2. Its proof is carried out in section 6, after collecting a series of lemmata in section 5.
Theorem 2.
Let (𝒳,𝒞,ℛ,κ⁡(τ))(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\kappa(\tau)) be a weakly reversible, single linkage class mass-action system with bounded kinetics. Fix a positive stoichiometric class 𝒫\mathcal{P}. Then
(i)
there exists a compact and forward invariant set K⊆𝒫K\subseteq\mathcal{P} with the property that each solution starting in 𝒫\mathcal{P} enters KK in finite time and
(ii)
the system, restricted to 𝒫\mathcal{P}, is permanent.
By forward invariance in (i)(i) of the above theorem, we mean that for all τ0≥0\tau_{0}\geq 0, x⁡(τ0)∈Kx(\tau_{0})\in K implies x⁡(τ)∈Kx(\tau)\in K for all τ≥τ0\tau\geq\tau_{0}. Note that (i)(i) immediately implies (i​i)(ii). Furthermore, for autonomous systems, (i)(i) and (i​i)(ii) are equivalent, because a compact set that all solutions enter can be enlarged to a compact and forward invariant set. However, for non-autonomous systems, (i)(i) is a much stronger property than (i​i)(ii).
