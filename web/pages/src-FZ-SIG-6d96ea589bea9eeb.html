# Minimum acquisition: write first, tag new feed, recover the cohort

**Not performed.** This is a protocol specification for a qualified catalysis/electrochemistry laboratory. It is not an acquired response, apparatus, or control capability.

## 1. Minimum pair that can reject direct old-product purge

Use the productive Pd/AC mixed-feed electrode class, membrane, geometric contact and ambient regime from the source experiment. The supplied preprint transcription specifies 10 mg 1 wt% Pd/AC, 5 cm2 geometric area, and A:B:H2:N2 volumetric flow 0.5:10:30:9.5 mL/min. Verify gas reference conditions, actual Pd loading, impurities, humidity, flows, and final-paper/SI identity. Do not substitute the foil spectroscopy cell.

1. **Restore and establish initial equivalence.** Hold the specimen at the acquired static-positive reference. Establish a recovery procedure experimentally, with steady molecular fluxes, actual potential/current, temperature, pressure, and relevant state proxies. Randomize pulse/sham within independent preparations. Reject trials with drift/carryover; repeated cycles from one electrode are not independent specimens.
2. **Write without the fresh tag.** Arm z=1 gets the source-preprint -0.1 V, 0.1-second write; z=0 gets the time-matched sham. Both then receive the common +1.5 V read command. Record the actual interfacial and terminal potentials; these source values are not assumed interchangeable with cell voltage.
3. **Define the earliest common read.** Measure electrical and gas responses. Admit enriched carbon only after the write has actually ended and after the declared potential/exposure tolerance is met. The isotope arrival at the reacting electrode, not the valve trigger, defines the read onset. If adequate exposure matching takes longer than the response persists, the proposed retained-control interface has failed at that delay. Do not extend a lifetime from the source's 7.76 suppression-equivalent seconds.
4. **Tag both C2 reactants.** For a finite read interval, switch A and B to enriched 13C2 analogues at the same total partial pressures, H2/N2 flows, humidity and actual read potential. The first experiment needs only fresh/old distinction. Isotope substitution is a chemical intervention too: check isotope-effect and pressure/flow switching controls. Natural isotope and residual previous tags must be measured and bounded.
5. **Chase under a common condition.** Restore the unlabelled mixed feed at the same read condition. Collect all labelled A, B, C and carbon-bearing side products until an acquired tail/inventory bound permits a conclusion. Integrated collection or calibrated fraction collection is sufficient for a final-cohort fate claim; millisecond product detection is unnecessary for that claim.
6. **Measure the two useful outcomes.** Certify a fresh ethane deficit using `D_C,lower>0` and a fresh net ethylene gain using `D_U,lower>0`, while retaining acetylene removal at a declared bound. The engineering threshold can be chosen for a consequential effect; it must be preregistered from pilot noise and process goals. A statistically detectable but trivial difference is not a technological advance.

The design uses one treatment/sham comparison. Replication, blank/purity/isotope controls, calibration and recovery are necessary acquisition costs, not extra independent discoveries. The narrow null excludes direct manipulation of old product alone. To attribute the response to retained **catalyst** state rather than environment, the common-exposure requirement and remaining state measurements in `CAUSAL_IDENTIFICATION.md` must also be met.

## 2. Branch source extension

If a meaningful fresh-fate response is acquired, run reciprocal source-labelled read packets in new validated preparations:

| Read campaign | Labelled species | Direct final-fate quantities |
|---|---|---|
| A-origin | 13C2 acetylene; unlabelled ethylene | A remaining, A-derived B, A-derived C, side products/inventory |
| B-origin | unlabelled acetylene; 13C2 ethylene | cofeed B surviving, B-derived C, side products/inventory |

Both campaigns require pulse and sham. A negative B-origin result cannot rule out a change in A-origin overhydrogenation; test both if the initial all-carbon response passes. Do not infer A-derived ethane is a single elementary A-to-C step: A-to-B-to-C is observationally compatible.

Simultaneous different carbon isotopomers could encode source and age in one run, but that adds isotope procurement, background and scrambling obligations. Reciprocal 13C2 campaigns are the cleaner first acquisition. Source-origin tracer attribution is established prior art, including acetylene hydrogenation [P3, P4 in `SOURCES.json`].

## 3. Required readout, balance and control interface

| Acquired quantity | Requirement and purpose | Failure consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Species/isotopomer amount | Calibrated GC/MS or validated species separation with standards for A/B/C and relevant isotopomers | Mass-28 alone confounds N2, labelled acetylene and hydrocarbon fragments; isotope counts cannot be assigned |
| Molar flow | Measure total outlet flow or a conserved inert-flow balance with measured accumulation/leak correction | A concentration change remains compatible with a flow change |
| Carbon fate | Measure side products, condensates/green oil, support/membrane uptake or a conservative unassigned-tag bound | Missing tag can all be assigned to a confounding fate |
| Product transport | Species-specific calibration and complete chase/tail bounds | A nonadsorbing tracer alone does not bound hydrocarbon delay |
| Reactive exposure | Near-specimen composition/pressure/humidity measurements or validated bounded dynamics; sham perturbations over the mismatch envelope | Fresh-feed fate may differ solely because it receives a different H2/A/B exposure |
| Electrical response | Actual working/reference potential, cell voltage, full signed current including transients, sufficient range/bandwidth, no saturation | Command equality does not imply actual common read; work and H-interface interpretations remain open |
| State and heat | Temperature at the reacting region, membrane condition and selected state probes | Thermal or material preparation may explain the response |
| Preparation and reset | Independent specimen preparations, randomized order, tag-free carryover check, measured recovery time | Conditioning, depletion and specimen drift are not separated from the write |

A conserved inert tracer can give `F_out = F_in,inert/y_out,inert` at steady conditions only when that tracer is not reacting, stored, leaking, or accumulating. In a transient, its own balance is `F_out,inert = F_in,inert - dN_inert/dt`; the accumulation term cannot silently be set to zero. Direct flow measurement plus a tracer cross-check is preferable. The near-specimen exposure requirement concerns chemistry **before/during reaction**; complete outlet integration only removes transport **after the final reaction**.

## 4. Sensitivity and uncertainty scale from the supplied aggregate package

These are conditional arithmetic scales, not predictions for a single pulse or instrument error bars. The absolute normalization combines preprint endpoints with final-SI activity and must be reconciled before experimental power is set.

At the inferred supplied flow scale, a 10-second read admits approximately 3.401 micromol A and 68.025 micromol B. The source endpoint calculation gives 2.339 micromol avoided ethane per cycle beyond a zero-ethane negative phase. **If all that deficit came from cofeed B**, it would alter B's fate by only 3.439 percentage points. Source labelling is needed before assuming that origin.

A 1.4% total-carbon gap over the same notional 10 seconds is approximately 1.000 micromol C2 equivalents. The reconstructed dynamic net B gain over 10 seconds is approximately 2.088 micromol: the gap is 47.9% of that useful-output scale. The reported closure deficit is not a statistical uncertainty interval, and equal/opposite species calibration errors can preserve total closure. Calibrate species separately and report uncertainty against the useful change, not merely total cofeed carbon.

Pilot paired/independent-preparation variance sets the repetition count. Predeclare the endpoint, finite-tail rule, allowable exposure mismatch, simultaneous confidence treatment and minimum engineering benefit before held-out delays/widths are tested. Serial time samples and repeated periods do not multiply the number of independent electrodes.

## 5. Complete energy and material cost

Acquire `W_terminal = integral V_cell(t) I_cell(t) dt` over write, common read, reset and conditioning. Record drawn and returned work separately. Credit returned work only if the apparatus actually recovers it; signed cancellation is not wall-plug recovery. Include power electronics, thermal management, membrane maintenance, gas handling, electrode manufacture/degradation and replacement. A working-electrode potential relative to a reference is not the cell voltage in this integral.

Record full `Q+`, `Q-` and `integral |I|` including charging and overshoot; the source's 4.41 mA steady value excludes transients and cannot supply whole-cycle work. Neither charge nor a current-to-turnover ratio identifies individual H/proton fluxes, hydrogen uptake, Faradaic fraction, or counterelectrode chemistry. Acquire H2 inlet/outlet, hydrogen/water inventories and the counterreaction before interpreting a chemically stored state or assigning its preparation cost.

The isotope experiments are an acquisition cost. Process performance is eventually evaluated with ordinary feed, at the same outlet acetylene specification, net ethylene recovery, throughput, durability and total resources as the comparator.

## 6. Sequential decision gates

1. **Current access gate: STOP capability promotion.** No laboratory or same-system transient records are available in this tranche; it cannot acquire the indispensable response. Preserve this exact test, without building a controller or predicting a new device.
2. **Fresh-fate gate:** proceed only if calibrated recovery and common-read bounds support a meaningful future-feed effect. If the effect is fully explained by old inventory or downstream lag, reject retained turnover control. If recovery/exposure cannot be bounded, retain UNKNOWN and stop that claim.
3. **Mechanism gate:** choose the next selective state intervention from the surviving countermodels. If no such intervention is obtainable, retain a system-level empirical response only.
4. **Operation/novelty gate:** distinguish the specific measured control from known dynamic catalysis, electrochemical promotion and isotope transients. A large retained response by itself is not new physics.
5. **Technology gate:** meet a measured useful outlet specification and beat strong same-output methods on an acquisition-inclusive cost/throughput/lifetime frontier. An 81% conversion point from 1% A leaves roughly 1,900 ppm before correction, so it does not meet ppm purification by itself. No arithmetic cascade is authorized.

Failure at a decisive gate ends this branch as a new-capability candidate; a well-measured negative result remains useful scientific evidence.
