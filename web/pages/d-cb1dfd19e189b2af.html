# Vanishing of every lowest-axis matrix-Krylov coefficient inside QPD

7 October2026, cycle11. Our exact obstruction to one universal proof mechanism. **Not a counterexample to K nonvanishing.** Historical novelty UNKNOWN. This note records a positive-dimensional PD exceptional family that is larger than the fully exchangeable collision case.

## 1. The coefficient that an axis argument would need

For a homogeneous sign-equivariant matrix C in three variables, fix an axis e_i and write its diagonal entries there as lambda_i,lambda_j,lambda_k. Put c_ji=partial_j C_ji(e_i) and c_ki=partial_k C_ki(e_i). To first order in the two transverse coordinates, the three Krylov columns y,Cy,C^2y have rows

    i: [1,lambda_i,lambda_i^2],
    j: y_j[1,lambda_j+c_ji,lambda_j^2+(lambda_j+lambda_i)c_ji],
    k: y_k[1,lambda_k+c_ki,lambda_k^2+(lambda_k+lambda_i)c_ki].

Thus the coefficient of y_i^25 y_j y_k in our degree27 K is

    (lambda_k-lambda_j)
    (lambda_j+c_ji-lambda_i)(lambda_k+c_ki-lambda_i).       (1)

The last two factors equal the transverse projective slopes of F=24Cy divided by24. Simple diagonal spectrum at the axis alone is insufficient, because y at the axis is not cyclic and its first off-diagonal deformation changes the projection onto each normal eigenvector.

## 2. Exact PD family with all three coefficients zero

Normalize Q's diagonal entries to1 and take

    Q(t)=[[1,b,t],[b,1,t],[t,t,1]],
    b=t^2/(4t^2-3),   0<t^2<3/8.

Its determinant is (1-b)(1+b-2t^2). Writing z=t^2,

    1+b-2z=(8z-3)(1-z)/(4z-3)>0,

and |b|<1, so every Q(t)>0. Rational t gives an exact rational input.

At e_1, the canonical field's scaled transverse slope towards coordinate3 is

    3/t^2+1/b-4=0.

The same is true at e_2 by symmetry. At e_3, C_11=C_22 by that symmetry, so the first factor of(1) is zero. Every axis's lowest coefficient therefore vanishes identically along this PD family. A proof relying solely on finding one nonzero lowest-axis coefficient would fail here.
