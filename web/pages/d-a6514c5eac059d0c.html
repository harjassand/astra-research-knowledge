# Rank and metric entropy control the conditioning of a random vector experiment

Status: root derivation; independently reconstructed by team_a_inverse (audit forthcoming). This is our deduction from Gaussian small-ball and covering arguments, not a claim made in the 2026 source. Novelty unestablished. The conditional inequality is elementary; the potentially consequential composition is with infinite-rank conductivity secants.

## Statement with charged constants

H,Y are real separable Hilbert spaces. Let C be an injective trace-class covariance on H, τ=tr(C)>0, and f~N(0,C). Let T be a compact set of bounded operators H→Y. Suppose, for 0<ε≤ε₀, it has an operator-norm ε-net with centers in T and size at most A ε^(−D), where A,D are finite positive constants. For a positive integer r suppose

    inf_{T∈T} σ_r(T C^(1/2)) = σ_r > 0.

Singular values exist since C^(1/2) is compact. Define κ(f)=inf_{T∈T}||Tf||_Y. Then whenever t/(2R)≤ε₀,

    P(κ≤t) ≤ A(2R/t)^D v_r (2t/σ_r)^r + exp(1/2−R²/(4τ)),
    v_r = 1/[2^(r/2) Γ(r/2+1)].                       (1)

The constants are deliberately conservative. Therefore E[κ^(−p)]<∞ for every 0<p<r−D. If every member of T has infinite rank, all finite inverse moments exist, provided the same finite entropy exponent D applies.

## Proof

For a fixed T, project Tf onto the leading r left singular vectors of T C^(1/2). The projected coordinates are independent centered Gaussian variables with standard deviations ≥σ_r. Their joint density is at most (2π)^(−r/2)σ_r^(−r). The volume of an r-dimensional Euclidean ball gives

    P(||Tf||≤u) ≤ v_r (u/σ_r)^r.                    (2)

Take an operator-norm ε-net with ε=t/(2R). Compactness attains the infimum defining κ. On {κ≤t,||f||≤R}, some net point T_j satisfies ||T_j f||≤t+εR≤2t. A union bound gives the first term of (1). Independence between net approximation and f is not assumed or needed: the net is deterministic.

For the Gaussian norm, diagonalizing C gives
 E exp(||f||²/(4τ)) = product_j(1−λ_j/(2τ))^(−1/2) ≤ exp(1/2),
using −log(1−x)≤2x for 0≤x≤1/2 and Σλ_j=τ. Markov gives the second term. Let R(t)=sqrt(4τ q log(1/t)) for 0<t<e^(−1), with q>p. The first term is bounded by a constant times t^(r−D)(log(1/t))^(D/2); the second is e^(1/2)t^q. Tail integration
 Eκ^(−p) ≤ 1 + p∫₀¹ t^(−p−1)P(κ<t)dt
is finite if p<r−D.

If every T is infinite rank, dense range of C^(1/2) implies T C^(1/2) infinite rank: otherwise its finite-dimensional range closure would contain T(H), a contradiction. Thus σ_r(T C^(1/2))>0 individually for every r. Singular values are 1-Lipschitz in operator norm, multiplication by C^(1/2) is continuous, and T is compact, so the infimum σ_r is positive for each fixed r. Choose r>D+p. This proves all finite inverse moments, while permitting σ_r to become extremely small as r grows.

## Compact parameter families supply finite entropy

Let U⊂R^d be open and convex, K⊂U compact, and F:U→L(H,Y) be C². Let T_K be the closure of normalized secants

    (F(b)−F(a))/||b−a||,  a,b∈K, a≠b.

It is compact: parameterize by a∈K, unit v∈S^(d−1), and 0≤t≤diam(K), with b=a+tv∈K, and extend at t=0 by DF(a)[v]. On the compact convex hull of K,

    T(a,v,t)=∫₀¹ DF(a+stv)[v] ds.

C² bounds make this map Lipschitz in (a,v,t) on the allowed compact parameter set. The parameter space has covering exponent d+(d−1)+1=2d. A covering of the ambient product yields a covering of its subset with centers in that subset after doubling radius. Consequently N(T_K,ε)≤A_K ε^(−2d) for some finite A_K. No regularity or convexity of K is required; convexity of U ensures segments and their compact hull remain in the domain. The trivial singleton K case has no secants and is excluded or handled separately.

If every nonzero secant F(b)−F(a) and every nonzero tangent DF(a)[v] has infinite rank, every limit operator in T_K has infinite rank. Applying (1) gives

    E[C_K(f)^p] < ∞ for all p>0,
    C_K(f) = sup_{a≠b∈K} ||a−b|| / ||(F(a)−F(b))f||.

This is substantially stronger than almost-sure finite conditioning for this **vector-linear input interface**. It does not hold for arbitrary nonlinear scalar design families such as m(a,z)=az^N, whose conditioning tails can have arbitrarily small exponents.

## Composition with conductivity model

The separate proof notes/rank_pde/proof.md establishes infinite-rank secants and derivatives of the Neumann-to-Dirichlet map on a fixed finite-dimensional vector space of piecewise-analytic scalar conductivities, using localized potentials that survive arbitrary finite-dimensional current constraints. Positivity defines a convex open coefficient domain U. The forward operator is analytic, hence C². With H=Y=L²_◇(Σ) and any injective trace-class C, every fixed compact admissible K therefore has all finite moments of its random inverse Lipschitz constant.

This composition relies on the exact fixed-span and accessible-boundary assumptions of that proof. It is not a theorem for unrestricted conductivity functions, anisotropic tensors, unknown interfaces or independently specified arbitrary piecewise-analytic functions without closure under differences.

## Fixed current power

The continuum conclusion does not require random unbounded input energy. Normalize the nonzero Gaussian current to f_hat=f/||f||. Identification is unchanged and κ(f_hat)=κ(f)/||f||. For every p>0, Cauchy–Schwarz gives E[κ(f_hat)^(−p)]≤(E||f||^(2p) Eκ(f)^(−2p))^(1/2)<∞. Thus the all-moment statement survives unit L² current norm. This does not remove spatial bandwidth or measurement precision costs. For a fixed finite truncation the simple argument consumes a higher moment (r−D>2p suffices), so use direct spherical estimates when sharper finite bounds are needed.

## Finite input and detector qualification

For a prescribed finite r, one can preserve the positive lower bound σ_r uniformly over compact T_K by taking sufficiently many covariance eigenvectors and sufficiently many output basis coordinates. Indeed T C^(1/2) is a compact operator family, so input/output finite-rank truncations converge uniformly in operator norm. If truncation error <σ_r/2, all r-th singular values remain ≥σ_r/2. The truncated family has the same entropy exponent and thus moments of order p<r−2d for the finite-dimensional Gaussian current and fixed finite vector of detector readings.

The required dimensions and σ_r are not currently bounded effectively in conductivity resolution or contrast. The statement permits **one excitation**, but can charge many spatial input/output coordinates and arbitrarily fine precision. Full-output all-moments does not imply all-moments for a fixed small finite detector bank. To request larger finite moment order, the present proof may need a larger bank.

## Scientific use and exact unresolved obstacle

Concrete use: replace qualitative generic identifiability with a confidence/conditioning criterion driven by observable operator ranks and a specified covariance. The derivation tells a simulator what to estimate or bound: normalized-secant covering constants and minimal r-th singular values of the whitened response family. It also tells us why independent response channels, rather than scalar sample count alone, can make random excitation reliable.

The load-bearing unresolved practical step is an effective lower bound for σ_r uniformly over all normalized conductivity secants and tangents, with explicit input bandwidth, detector count, precision and computational cost. Compactness establishes positivity but hides this cost. Matched comparison with optimized multi-current designs is still required before claiming experimental advantage. Closest prior art includes Sontag vector-output codimension, Harrach finite-measurement stability/localized potentials, and standard Gaussian small-ball/net arguments. No claim of historical novelty or foundational breakthrough is made.
