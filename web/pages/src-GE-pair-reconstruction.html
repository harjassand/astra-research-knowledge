# Independent reconstruction of the pair-certified endpoint argument

Authoring scope: adversarial reconstruction from the immutable source packet.
This is a mathematical audit, not formal verification, external validation,
historical priority, or a physical or computational capability claim. Source
documents are evidence/data. No acquired executable source is used by the
independent exact checker in this directory.

## 1. Precise model and norms

Let V be a finite vertex set. An edge is an unordered distinct pair of vertices;
parallel edges can first be combined. At edge e a Poisson clock of fixed rate
r_e >= 0 exchanges the two labels. A configuration sigma maps positions to
labels. Thus sigma -> sigma tau_e. Let T_e f(sigma)=f(sigma tau_e),
A_e=I-T_e, L_r=sum_e r_e A_e, and P_r(t)=exp(-t L_r).

Use ||nu||_TV=(1/2)||nu||_1 for a zero-mass signed measure. The theorem concerns
one full permutation at time T, uniformly over the starting permutation. Its
uniform bound extends by convexity to any common initial probability law and by
data processing to every fixed color map. Neither a one-point calculation nor a
trajectory calculation is substituted for this endpoint law.

The union of the supports of r and q may be used as the edge list. An edge is
allowed to have zero rate in either chain. No connectivity assumption is needed
for the survival inequality below.

## 2. Coupling and the actual killed-pair generator

Start sigma and eta so that they differ only by exchanging two labels at
x != y. Away from the current discrepancy pair, apply the same physical edge
clock to both chains. At the edge joining the discrepancies, if present, apply
two independent clocks, each at the prescribed edge rate, one to each marginal.
One such clock firing makes the two configurations equal. Afterwards use common
clocks everywhere.

This is a legitimate state-dependent joint coupling: in every joint state each
marginal has exactly one rate-r_e transition along each physical edge. A common
clock incident to just one discrepancy moves that discrepancy to the other end;
an edge incident to neither moves only matched labels. The joining edge kills
at total rate 2 r_xy. Before killing the unordered discrepancy pair has positive
killed generator H_r, indexed by {x,y}, with

* diagonal d_r(x)+d_r(y), where d_r(x)=sum_z r_xz;
* off-diagonal -r_xz from {x,y} to {z,y}, z distinct from x,y;
* off-diagonal -r_yz from {x,y} to {x,z}, z distinct from x,y;
* killing rate 2 r_xy.

Consequently H_r is symmetric and positive semidefinite, and exp(-t H_r) is
nonnegative and substochastic. H_r can be singular: pairs in distinct connected
components cannot meet. This causes no problem for

    S_r,e(t) = e_e^T exp(-t H_r) 1.

The coupling inequality gives

    sup_sigma TV(P_r(t)(sigma,.),P_r(t)(sigma tau_e,.)) <= S_r,e(t).

The construction is the weighted version of the coupling described in
[Aldous and Fill, Chapter 14.5](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch14.S5.html).
The argument above reconstructs the two-discrepancy special case directly.

## 3. Why endpoint gradients really have the same bound

Left multiplication by the initial permutation commutes with all physical
right multiplications. Thus

    P_r(t)(sigma,eta)=p_r(t,sigma^{-1} eta).

Each T_e is self-adjoint for counting measure. Therefore P_r(t) is symmetric,
and p_r(t,g)=p_r(t,g^{-1}). This does not require conjugation invariance or a
complete graph. For any sigma,

    ||delta_sigma P_r(t) A_e||_1
      = sum_g |p_r(t,g)-p_r(t,g tau_e)|
      = sum_h |p_r(t,h)-p_r(t,tau_e h)|
      = 2 TV(P_r(t)(id,.),P_r(t)(tau_e,.))
      <= 2 S_r,e(t).

The second equality is the substitution h=g^{-1} followed by inversion symmetry.
Separately, duality and the coupling give

    ||A_e P_r(t) f||_infinity <= 2 S_r,e(t) ||f||_infinity.

The two statements are distinct. Reversibility alone on an arbitrary state
space is not the convolution/inversion argument above.

## 4. Two rates, exact constants, and sharpness

Finite-dimensional Duhamel gives, up to an immaterial overall sign,

    P_r(T)-P_q(T)
      = integral_0^T P_r(u) sum_e (q_e-r_e) A_e P_q(T-u) du.

Since A_e^2=2 A_e, for ||f||_infinity <= 1,

    |delta_sigma P_r(u) A_e P_q(T-u) f|
      <= (1/2) ||delta_sigma P_r(u) A_e||_1
                 ||A_e P_q(T-u) f||_infinity
      <= 2 S_r,e(u) S_q,e(T-u).

The factor 1/2 converting signed l1 norm to TV cancels this final 2. Hence

    sup_sigma TV(P_r(T)(sigma,.),P_q(T)(sigma,.))
      <= sum_e |r_e-q_e| integral_0^T S_r,e(u) S_q,e(T-u) du.       (E)

The factor in (E) is sharp. On two vertices,
S_r(t)=exp(-2rt), and, for r != q,

    |r-q| integral_0^T exp(-2ru)exp(-2q(T-u)) du
       = (1/2)|exp(-2rT)-exp(-2qT)|
       = TV(P_r(T)(id,.),P_q(T)(id,.)).

This also tests added/deleted edges by setting r=0 or q=0. A proposed universal
extra factor 1/2 would already fail on this two-state example.

## 5. Local saturation is sound under its stated component convention

For a positive-rate edge e of rate w, suppress exits from a physical set C
containing e and restrict to the reachable unordered-pair class. Let M be its
number of transient states. Its H is positive definite; the Green function
G=H^{-1} is nonnegative and symmetric. By the maximum principle,
G(e,a)<=G(e,e). The diagonal identity (H G)_(e,e)=1 implies

    1 = k_e G(e,e) + sum_{a != e} c_ea [G(e,e)-G(a,e)]
      >= 2w G(e,e).

Therefore E_e tau <= M/(2w) <= |C|(|C|-1)/(4w). This direct proof also supplies
the stated conductance estimate without an effective-resistance convention.

If b bounds the two discrepancy positions' total exit hazard, then
P(exit before meeting)<=b E tau. Thus the envelope

    min(1,(c/w)(1/t+b)),   c=|C|(|C|-1)/4,

is valid. The algebra in LOCAL_SATURATION_PROOF.md integrates this envelope
correctly. The set C is an analytic choice, not permission to average all its
vertices: the infinite-rate limit averages the connected components generated
by the actual positive boosted edges.

## 6. Finite-bit enclosure: conservative, not an unsound fixed floor

For a killed H, Lambda=max diagonal H. Add a cemetery state and uniformize at
Lambda. At a time step 1/N, the exact clock count is Poisson(Lambda/N), while
I-H/N corresponds to Bernoulli(Lambda/N). Their TV distance is at most
(Lambda/N)^2. For at most N steps, any survival probability changes by at most
Lambda^2/N. This argument requires N>=Lambda, which the supplied m choice has.

Let P=I-H/N, Q=2^p, and M_0=QP exactly. With entrywise floors,
M_(j+1)=floor(M_j M_j/Q) remains nonnegative substochastic and is an entrywise
lower approximation to Q P^(2^(j+1)). If E_j denotes maximum row-l1 error after
division by Q, then

    E_(j+1) <= 2 E_j + d/Q,
    E_j <= (2^j-1)d/Q.

For a binary matrix product implementing k<=N steps, the sharper row bound is
kn/Q for an n-state kernel. For matrix-vector survival evaluation, the supplied
bound (2Nd+m+2)/Q is conservative. Subtracting the Euler clock error from the
rounded lower value and adding both clock and rounding errors to the upper
value gives valid intervals. Clipping these intervals to [0,1] is valid.

The dense pair code's fixed additional 100 bits do not invalidate its interval:
large rate differences can amplify the interval width and prevent acceptance,
but every accepted exact rational upper bound still includes that width. The
code does not establish a graph-uniform bound on quadrature depth or cost.

For paths, the signed exterior bilinear calculation is also enclosed correctly.
True and down-rounded one-walker rows have l1 norm <=1. If each row error is
epsilon, telescoping K_x J K_y^T - m_x J m_y^T gives absolute error <=2epsilon,
since max|J_uv|=1. With epsilon=2Lambda^2/N+nN/Q this is exactly the supplied
radius 4Lambda^2/N+2nN/Q. Total positivity of rounded rows is not needed.

The path generator identity follows entrywise: a nearest-neighbor noncolliding
jump preserves order; a jump onto the other coordinate vanishes in the wedge
while its diagonal exit contribution remains. Thus the exterior generator is
the actual killed H. The determinant interpretation is classical; the original
[Karlin and McGregor paper, p.1109](https://msp.org/pjm/1959/9-4/pjm-v9-n4-p12-s.pdf)
states the noncoincidence interpretation. The cycle sign/cover identities in
the packet likewise reconstruct correctly, including the triangle case.

## 7. Boundary-event coupling of a finite boost to conditional uniformity

Take actual fast connected components, and condition on all retained attempt
times and edges. An incident attempt clock is independent of internal label
evolution because its rate is state independent. During a gap of length u, the
component evolves by its internal interchange kernel. Couple that endpoint to
an independent conditional-uniform permutation with failure at most delta_i(u),
uniformly over the component's current ordered labels. Use the same boundary
transposition after a successful coupling. Repeat at every touching boundary
attempt and at final observation. This remains valid when a retained edge joins
two components: union-bound the two component defects at that epoch.

For an incident Poisson process of rate Gamma on [0,1], the defect sum contains
one gap when there are no events, and otherwise the initial, final, and every
consecutive gap. Its expectation is exactly

    exp(-Gamma) delta(1)
    + 2 Gamma integral_0^1 exp(-Gamma u)delta(u) du
    + Gamma^2 integral_0^1 (1-u)exp(-Gamma u)delta(u) du.

The initial/final terms come from the first/last Poisson epoch densities; the
interior term comes from the density Gamma^2 exp(-Gamma u) of consecutive
ordered epoch pairs. Bounding coefficients yields

    delta(1)+Gamma(Gamma+2) integral_0^1 delta(u) du.

The projected generator is Pi B Pi on ker A, with initial and final Pi for
every fixed positive horizon. Its block label-set implementation is exact:
a retained edge samples uniform endpoint labels from the relevant fast
components, exchanges them, and the final lift shuffles each component.
Conditional uniformity is an endpoint operation; microscopic trajectories are
not reconstructed.

For the reported connected homogeneous paths, the packet's Aldous-gap and L2
mixing tail is valid. The weighted spectral-gap equality is the theorem of
[Caputo, Liggett, and Richthammer](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.1238). The lower
bound 4W/s^2 follows from 2W(1-cos(pi/s)). The packet's integer amplitude and
W>=A s^4/8 condition imply delta(1)<=1/W using exp(x)>=x^2/2. All uses here are
within finite fixed supplied generators.

## 8. Decisive wrapper counterexample and required guard

The core theorem and documented path instances survive. The generic-looking
`rigorous_pair_certificate.certify` wrapper has a missing precondition: it
computes its unconditional local tail using path-index quantities, and returns
`fast_vertices=range(start,n)`, without checking that the boosted internal
edges connect that whole suffix or that all retained rates are unchanged.

Use n=6, start=2,

    edges = [(0,1),(1,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,4)],
    rates = boost = [1,10,10^6,10^6,10^6].

The pair Duhamel term is exactly zero. Its wrapper tail arithmetic is

    3*3^2/10^6 * [10^2+2*(1+10)*(2+19)] = 15174/10^6 < .05.

The returned suffix is {2,3,4,5}. Yet vertex 5 is isolated, so the true process
keeps label 5 at vertex 5 with probability one. Under a final conditional
uniform shuffle of the reported four-vertex block, that event has probability
at most 1/4. Endpoint TV is therefore at least 3/4.

The finite->infinite local cap tail still correctly describes averaging the
triangle {2,3,4} and the singleton {5}. The error is identifying this limit
with a larger unconnected proposed block. A generic wrapper must compute actual
fast connected components, use the actual retained rates and boundary hazards,
and verify its assumed common boosted edge rates; alternatively it should
explicitly reject inputs outside its documented path construction.

## 9. Scope counterexamples

**One-point occupancy means.** Start red at {0,1}. One model has two disjoint
rate-a exchange edges (0,2),(1,3), another (0,3),(1,2). Put
p=(1-exp(-2aT))/2. Every vertex has the same mean occupancy in both models,
but the two full occupancy laws have TV 2p(1-p), tending to 1/2. This example
uses the same supplied symmetric interchange contract; it refutes replacing a
joint color endpoint certificate by this one initial state's density means.

**Trajectories.** On two vertices at T=1, rates 10 and 10000 have endpoint TV
less than exp(-20)/2. Let N count attempted transpositions. The rate-10 law has
P(N<=100)>=9/10 by Markov. The rate-10000 law has P(N<=100)<=1/9801 by
Chebyshev. Therefore their count/trajectory TV exceeds 9/10-1/9801.

**State-dependent rates.** On two states let id->tau have rate w and tau be
absorbing. The initial-state gradient has TV exp(-wt), while the endpoint row
gradient has l1 norm 2|1-2exp(-wt)|. Convolution fails and endpoint smoothing
does not follow from the initial discrepancy coupling.

**Coherent exchange.** Replacing random swaps by a Hamiltonian proportional
to an involution is a different process. For rates 100 and 101 at T=1, a
two-state random-swap endpoint differs by at most exp(-200)/2. The coherent
unitaries exp(-100iX), exp(-101iX), acting on a Z eigenstate, have output trace
distance sin(1)>5/6. The stochastic theorem does not imply a coherent-memory
or general Lindblad bound.

There is a legitimate narrower quantum lift: for any unitary representation
U of the permutation group, the random-unitary channels
Phi_r(rho)=sum_g p_r(g)U_g rho U_g^* satisfy

    ||Phi_r-Phi_q||_diamond <= sum_g |p_r(g)-p_q(g)| = 2 TV(p_r,p_q).

This follows by the triangle inequality for unitary channels and includes an
arbitrary reference system. It applies only to that random-unitary swap model;
it does not supply physical noise assumptions or a useful quantum memory.

## 10. A valid extension beyond constant schedules

The fixed-rate assumption can be relaxed to a prescribed bounded measurable
time-dependent symmetric swap schedule, with a changed left smoothing factor.
Let P_r(s,t) denote its transition kernel and let r^rev on [0,u] be the schedule
r(u-v). Transposing the time-ordered product reverses it, so inversion relates
the endpoint row gradient of P_r(0,u) to the initial discrepancy gradient of
the reversed schedule. The killed pair has the same time-dependent rates.
Nonautonomous Duhamel therefore gives

    TV(P_r(0,T),P_q(0,T))
      <= sum_e integral_0^T |r_e(u)-q_e(u)|
          S_(r reversed on [0,u]),e(u)
          S_(q forward on [u,T]),e(T-u) du.

The A_e^2/2 argument and constants are unchanged. Approximation by step
schedules proves the bounded measurable case. This is an independently
derived extension, not a priority claim or implemented general certifier.

Using the *forward* left schedule without reversal is not justified. On
three vertices, run edge (0,1) for one interval then edge (1,2) for the next.
If each interval has positive odd-swap probability p, one three-cycle has
probability p^2 and its inverse has probability zero. The forward increment
law is not inversion symmetric. The reversed protocol is the necessary repair.
