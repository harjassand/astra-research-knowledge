REVISION 02 — THE BETA=0 COMPACTNESS BOUNDARY

The symbolic identities for the left-invariant frame remain valid at beta=0,
but the compact quotient argument changes. The beta=0 Lie algebra is solvable
(Sol), not sl2(R); an arbitrary positive rational a does not automatically
give a compact lattice. A mapping torus needs a return time T for which the
linear action diag(exp(aT),exp(-aT)) is conjugate to an integer hyperbolic
matrix in SL(2,Z).

The exact prior example uses A=[[2,1],[1,1]], whose expanding eigenvalue is
lambda=(3+sqrt(5))/2, and chooses aT=log(lambda). At T=1, the metric
  g=exp(2at) dx^2 + exp(-2at) dy^2 + dt^2
descends under (v,t)->(Av,t-1) when the transverse lattice is the original
integer lattice expressed in the eigenbasis. Its volume density is dx dy dt.
This is the separate Sol sharpness realization. The sl2 rational examples in
the main report do not claim a continuous compact deformation through beta=0.

Thus the algebraic equality formulas extend across beta=0 locally, while
compactness on the solvable side has an arithmetic lattice gate. This is why
the checkpoint restricts its freely varying compact parameters to beta!=0.
