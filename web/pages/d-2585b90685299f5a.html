N04 CELLULAR SENSING — EXACT FRONTIER AND PRIOR-ART AUDIT v3
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: exact model-family derivation and finite-tested construction. This record appends to v1/v2; it does not replace or rewrite them. The literature audit substantially narrows novelty: the relation is a compact specialization of established autonomous-sensing and prediction-power frameworks, not a claim of a new general law.

Question answered at this checkpoint
For a fixed binary telegraph environment and fixed sensor response rate, is there an exact finite-horizon prediction-error/chemical-work curve that an autonomous chemically consistent sensor attains, without a timing controller or signal holding? Yes, as a sharp lower envelope over thermodynamic realizations of a specified observed Markov generator. Parallel chemical routes can dissipate more while preserving every observable trajectory, so there is no exact work equality for all such networks and no upper work function from error alone.

Autonomous binary sensing network
Let E_t∈{+1,-1} be an exogenous symmetric telegraph signal. It changes in either direction at rate lambda>0, with equal chemical free energies and no work on E jumps. Let M_t∈{0,1} be one reporter/memory bit. For a fixed sensor response rate R>0 and bias 0≤a<1, assign the conditional reporter rates

    u_+ = R(1+a)/2,  d_+ = R(1-a)/2,
    u_- = R(1-a)/2,  d_- = R(1+a)/2,

where u_e is M:0→1 and d_e is M:1→0. In each input state, u_e+d_e=R, so the response relaxation time at fixed E is exactly 1/R. The environment switching is independent of M, so it is genuinely exogenous. Rates are fixed in time; there is no external clock, reset protocol, or controller.

A channel-resolved chemical implementation uses, conditional on the catalyst conformation E,

    E+ + M0 + ATP <-> E+ + M1 + ADP + Pi,
    E- + M0 + ADP + Pi <-> E- + M1 + ATP,
    E+ <-> E-.

The first M route hydrolyses fuel on a forward M transition; the second synthesizes the same fuel on a forward M transition. Choose the fuel affinity A=beta*Delta_mu=ln[(1+a)/(1-a)] and choose reversible kinetic prefactors to realize the rates above. Each E state is a catalyst that is unchanged by an M transition. The E+↔E− rates are lambda in both directions and independent of M. Thus the only sustained chemical work in this model is the fuel work on the two M routes. This idealized finite-state CRN is locally detailed-balanced and autonomous; it is not asserted to be a known natural pathway.

Stationary law and finite-horizon prediction
Write q_e=P(M=1|E=e). The stationary master equations are

    (lambda+R)q_+ - lambda q_- = u_+,
    -lambda q_+ + (lambda+R)q_- = u_-.

They yield

    c := q_+ - q_- = R a/(R+2 lambda),
    p(+0,+1,-0,-1)=((1-c)/4,(1+c)/4,(1+c)/4,(1-c)/4).

Both E and M have uniform marginals, so H(M)=ln 2 (one bit), and the present-state mutual information is I(M_t;E_t)=ln 2-h((1-c)/2). The symmetric telegraph semigroup gives E[E_(t+tau)|E_t]=e^(-2 lambda tau) E_t. Since the future is conditionally independent of the past given E_t, the M-to-future correlation is c_tau=c e^(-2 lambda tau). The best binary decoder therefore has

    error epsilon_tau = [1-c e^(-2 lambda tau)]/2,
    accuracy A_tau = [1+c e^(-2 lambda tau)]/2,
    I(M_t;E_(t+tau)) = ln 2 - h(epsilon_tau).

Here tau is an analysis horizon used to score the predictive memory; the sensor need not measure time or execute a tau-long protocol. No reset is performed between forecasts.

Exact work and sharp lower envelope
Set A=ln[(1+a)/(1-a)] and let J_+,J_- be net forward M currents in the E=+ and E=− routes. From the stationary law,

    J_+ = p(+0)u_+ - p(+1)d_+ = lambda*c/2,
    J_- = p(-0)u_- - p(-1)d_- = -lambda*c/2.

The two route affinities are +A and -A, respectively. Consequently

    beta P_chem = J_+ A + J_-(-A)
                = lambda*c*ln[(1+a)/(1-a)].

This equals the observable four-state path irreversibility rate sigma_obs/kB. One way to see this is that the square cycle has stationary current lambda*c/2 and cycle affinity 2A, so sigma_obs/kB=(lambda*c/2)(2A)=lambda*c*A. It is also an instance of the general log-sum inequality: full channel-resolved entropy production is at least the irreversibility rate of the lumped observed state trajectories. The one-route-per-E-edge CRN above attains equality, so the bound is sharp for this fixed generator and declared boundary.

At fixed lambda, R and horizon tau, the predictor error determines c and then a:

    c = e^(2 lambda tau)*(1-2 epsilon_tau),
    a = ((R+2 lambda)/R)c.

The sharp minimum work can therefore be written directly as

    beta P_min(epsilon_tau;lambda,R,tau)
      = lambda*c*ln[(1+((R+2 lambda)/R)c)/(1-((R+2 lambda)/R)c)],

for 0<=c<R/(R+2 lambda). This is a lower envelope, not an equality for every chemical sensor with those observable rates. It is strictly increasing in c: if k=(R+2 lambda)/R, then d[c ln((1+kc)/(1-kc))]/dc = ln((1+kc)/(1-kc)) + 2kc/(1-k^2c^2)>0. At fixed environmental rate and response speed, more positive finite-horizon forecast correlation therefore requires at least this much ongoing chemical power. The attainable current-state correlation has ceiling c_max=R/(R+2 lambda), approached as a→1; at that limit A diverges and so does P_min. The forecast error floor at horizon tau is [1-c_max e^(-2 lambda tau)]/2.

The curve is sharp because a single reversible route for each observed M edge, with affinity exactly equal to the aggregate rate ratio u_e/d_e, realizes it. It is not a universal exact function of predictive accuracy and work across all physical networks. The three-channel networks A/B in v1 have this same E,M generator and prediction scores but greater chemical power. For the base rates R=3, lambda=1, a=1/3, network C (the two-route construction above) has beta P=(1/5)ln 2. Networks A and B have beta P=(61/120)ln 4 and (79/120)ln 4, respectively. All three have

    A_tau = [1+(1/5)e^(-2 tau)]/2,
    H(M)=ln 2, and response rate R=3.

The fixed-affinity-set LP interval for A/B remains the corrected v2 interval; its lower endpoint exceeds this unrestricted coarse-grained minimum because the ratios {4,1,1/4} do not include the one-channel ratios {2,1/2} that attain sigma_obs.

No observation-only estimator can recover total channel work
Let O_[0,T]=(E_t,M_t)_(0<=t<=T). A and B have the same generator and same stationary law, hence exactly the same probability measure on O_[0,T] for every T, including the infinite-record limit. For any estimator F_T based only on O_[0,T], E_A[F_T]=E_B[F_T] whenever these expectations exist. Since P_A≠P_B, F_T cannot be unbiased for total chemical power in both networks; no statistical test based only on O can distinguish them, even with arbitrarily long observations. The observable path irreversibility remains a valid lower bound, but identifying the excess requires channel labels, fuel turnover measurements, or independently established reaction-network constraints.

This gives a concrete limitation for trajectory-only energy adaptation: any strategy fed only the reporter and exogenous signal paths receives statistically identical data under A and B and therefore cannot know which ongoing ATP cost it is balancing. The recent trajectory-statistics adaptation model of Nicoletti et al. is not contradicted; its estimates apply to the chosen model and observed degrees of freedom. The obstruction says that, in a chemical system with hidden parallel routes, those statistics cannot identify total reaction-resolved work without additional measurements. A discriminating experiment needs pathway-specific turnover or ATP/ADP flux in addition to matched reporter trajectories. Such a test is a model prediction, not evidence that natural chemotaxis uses the proposed routes.

Unbounded hidden excess when chemical affinities are not bounded
The same fixed observable rates admit an arbitrarily large excess work family if no upper bound on pathway affinity is declared. For any K>2, take channel ratios (K,1,1/K), with fuel stoichiometries (+1,0,-1). At E=+ use down rates

    d_(K,+)=1/(K-1),  d_(1,+)=(K-2)/(K-1),  d_(1/K,+)=0;

at E=− use

    d_(K,-)=0,  d_(1,-)=(K-2)/(K-1),  d_(1/K,-)=K/(K-1).

With u_(nu,e)=r_nu*d_(nu,e), these positive active routes sum exactly to (u_+,d_+)=(2,1) and (u_-,d_-)=(1,2) for every K. Since p is unchanged, the hydrolysis current is

    J_fuel(K) = (2K-3)/(5(K-1)) -> 2/5 as K->infinity,
    beta P_chem(K) = J_fuel(K) ln K -> infinity.

This is reaction-resolved work inside the sensing transitions, not a disconnected futile cycle. Zero rates denote catalyst-state-gated routes; if the chemistry requires every channel to be strictly positive, add arbitrarily small cross-rates and adjust the remaining positive prefactors to preserve the two aggregate constraints. The divergence persists. This family shows why a finite upper bound requires explicit limits on chemical affinities/resources or channel fluxes. It does not say that a single cell can vary ATP chemical potential freely; K indexes different physical resource boundaries.

Preparation, observation, reset, and environmental work
All formulas are stationary. The environmental process can start in its equilibrium uniform law. Reaching the joint stationary law from an arbitrary preparation requires a burn-in whose work depends on the initial distribution and transient channel currents; that finite startup work is not included in steady power and must be counted for a finite experiment. No sensor reset occurs during the ongoing prediction task. A physical fluorescence detector, external data store, or reset routine adds energy terms not included here. The external signal is not held or timed; its symmetric E+↔E− flips have zero affinity. Forcing a different signal law in a laboratory would add the generator's work to the declared boundary.

Primary literature and novelty audit
- Barato, Hartich & Seifert, “Information-theoretic vs. thermodynamic entropy production in autonomous sensory networks,” arXiv:1212.3186v3, https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3186; Sections II–IV, especially Eq. 21 and Eqs. 22–23. Their framework already has an exogenous Markov environment unaffected by the internal biochemical network and explicitly says that entropy production must sum over distinct channels when the same internal-state pair has multiple transition types. Their thermodynamically consistent four-state sensor includes ATP-dependent phosphorylation/dephosphorylation. This is a direct precedent for the physical architecture and hidden-channel accounting.
- Marzen & Crutchfield, “Prediction and Power in Molecular Sensors: Uncertainty and Dissipation When Conditionally Markovian Channels Are Driven by Semi-Markov Environments,” arXiv:1707.03962, https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03962; primary text §§II–IV, especially definitions of I_mem and I_fut and Eqs. 4–11. They derive predictive-memory, sensor-to-future information, and power formulas for conditionally Markovian sensors under broad semi-Markov inputs. The current finite-horizon binary classification formula here is a simple telegraph specialization; their sensor-state energy model does not resolve hidden parallel channel work.
- Freitas & Esposito, “Emergent second law for non-equilibrium steady states,” Nature Communications 13, 5084 (2022), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32700-7; primary publisher text, Eqs. 25–26. They state that different thermodynamic channel decompositions can produce the same master equation and different EPR, and show the coarse-grained EPR is a lower bound. Thus the sharp lower-envelope principle is established thermodynamic inference, not claimed as a new theorem.
- Nicoletti, Di Terlizzi & Busiello, “Balancing Information and Dissipation with Partially Observed Fluctuating Signals,” Physical Review Letters 137, 127101 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1103/8gx2-rbns; primary APS article, pp. 1–4 and Eqs. 1–8. They use a Gaussian linear-noise model and an abstract sensor/optimizer to tune readout coupling using finite-window trajectory statistics, mutual information, and a traffic-based dissipation estimate. This recent work makes the observation-only limitation practically relevant; the current construction shows why its trajectory statistics cannot determine hidden route work outside the selected coarse model.
- Chen & Limmer, “Entropy Production Bounds the Accuracy of Computation in Markov Networks,” arXiv:2608.23764v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23764; primary text Eqs. 1–18. Their error is lag from an input-dependent frozen equilibrium state in reversible state-resolved Markov networks; the present metric is future-environment prediction under an autonomous exogenous telegraph process, and the work includes reaction-channel affinities. The results are distinct in hypotheses and observable.

Disposition
The original aspiration for a new universal cost–memory–prediction law is not achieved. The defensible result is an exact autonomous, finite-horizon, chemically realizable lower-envelope construction plus an observational no-go for identifying total reaction-resolved power from state trajectories. Both sit within established autonomous sensing, predictive-information, and coarse-grained thermodynamic frameworks. Any later work should seek a genuinely new result beyond this exact specialization, such as a pathway-constrained biological theorem with a nontrivial experimentally accessible equality condition; do not promote the current record as a foundational discovery.
