## Exact model and observation interface

Known finite irreducible row generator Q0 on n≥3 states; all its positive rates are known. Its edge u→v is absent. Let L=d0(u,v)≥2 be the shortest number of baseline directed jumps from u to v. Unknown rate b∈(0,1] adds exactly this edge:

Qb=Q0+bB, B=e_u(e_v−e_u)^T.

Every state label is visible; passive endpoint observations and timestamps are retained. The next lag is chosen from prior retained observations and independent randomization, with no information from inside the coming interval and no parameter-dependent clock kernel. Arbitrary initial state is fixed independently of b. Baseline rates, graph and scale units are supplied. No unknown-baseline acquisition is claimed.

Set R=max baseline escape rate, C=2(R+1), a_min=min positive baseline rate, and d_xy=d0(x,y), with d_xx=0. Define

m_xy=d0(x,u)+1+min{d0(v,y),d0(u,y)}.

Define lower path constants h_xy=e^(−(R+1)) a_min^(d_xy)/d_xy! and upper derivative constants A_xy=2e^C C^(m_xy−1)/(m_xy−1)!. These are finite, positive and computable from supplied structure.

## Uniform Fisher bound for every starting state and endpoint

For 0<t≤1, select a baseline shortest path from x to y and require exactly its d_xy jumps and no others. Integrating ordered jump times and bounding every survival factor gives

P_b(t)xy≥h_xy t^(d_xy).

The derivative series is ∂b e^(tQb)=Σ_{k≥1}t^k ∂b(Qb^k)/k!, with ∂b(Qb^k)=Σ_{j=0}^{k−1}Qb^j B Qb^(k−1−j).

**Support lemma:** its (x,y) entry vanishes for k<m_xy. A nonzero matrix-product monomial uses a walk in the baseline graph plus u→v, possibly with diagonal stays, and exactly one marked B factor. Before the marked factor it must reach u. Any earlier rare jump requires a prior visit to u and thus forms a removable loop; a shortest such prefix is baseline and has length at least d0(x,u). If B uses u→v, a suffix starting at v can remove each future rare jump's closed v→...→u→v excursion, giving a baseline suffix of length at least d0(v,y). If B uses its u→u diagonal entry, a suffix from u either uses no rare jump, with length at least d0(u,y), or uses the rare jump, with length at least 1+d0(v,y). Taking the minimum of the two B possibilities yields exactly the stated m_xy. Diagonal stays cannot shorten these bounds. The argument holds for b=0 as well, where some potential monomials simply vanish.

Since ||Qb||∞≤C and ||B||∞=2,

|∂b P_b(t)xy|≤2Σ_{k≥m_xy}t^k C^(k−1)/(k−1)!≤A_xy t^(m_xy).

For m selected through the off-diagonal B option, writing a=d0(x,u), c=d0(v,y), we have d_xy≤a+L+c, so 2m_xy−d_xy≥a+c+2−L≥2−L. For the diagonal option, d_xy≤d0(x,u)+d0(u,y), so 2m_xy−d_xy≥2. Consequently, for every initial x,

I_t,x(b)=Σ_y (∂b P_b(t)xy)²/P_b(t)xy≤C_s t^(2−L),

where C_s=max_x Σ_y A_xy²/h_xy. This is an all-endpoint bound; it does not merely inspect the rare-edge endpoint.

Full-path Fisher information is E_b[occupation time of u]/b≤t/b. Indeed the rare-edge counting-process score is N_uv(t)/b−∫_0^t1{X_s=u}ds, the corresponding intensity-martingale variance is E occupation/b. Endpoint observation is a measurable function of that path. Therefore

I_t,x(b)≤min{t/b,C_s t^(2−L)}≤C_s^(1/(L−1)) b^(−(L−2)/(L−1)), 0<t≤1.

## Arbitrarily large observation lags

Let η=min{1/(2n),min_xy h_xy}>0. Every P_b(1) has entries ≥η. By semigroup composition, every P_b(t) for t≥1 has entries ≥η. Its time-one Dobrushin contraction is at most ρ=1−nη<1. Because B1=0 and its only nonzero row is the difference of rows v and u,

||B P_b(s)||∞≤2ρ^floor(s).

Duhamel then gives ||∂b P_b(t)||∞≤∫_0^t2ρ^floor(s)ds≤2/(nη). Thus I_t,x(b)≤4/(n²η³) for t≥1. No reversible baseline or diagonalizability assumption is used; constants may be severe.

With a=(L−2)/(L−1) and K=max{C_s^(1/(L−1)),4/(n²η³)},

**I_t,x(b)≤K b^(−a)** for every starting state x, every t>0, and every b∈(0,1].

## Adaptive minimax reading lower bound

For comparison b versus b/2, on the intervening parameter interval I≤K2^a b^(−a). The square-root likelihood derivative and conditional Hellinger-affinity recursion give

TV(L_b,L_b/2)≤sqrt[N K 2^a b^(2−a)/16].

Any test with each error ≤.1 consequently requires

N≥10.24/(K2^a) b^(−L/(L−1)).

This proves the exponent for relative-rate identification. It automatically permits timestamps and adaptive deterministic or random lags, arbitrary endpoints/initial states, and bounded stopping. Extra parameter-dependent initial data must be charged separately. An expected-stopping version can use r08's affinity telescope, but is not claimed here without its final gate.

## When this is an entropy-production lower bound

To have finite EP for b>0, require the baseline reverse edge v→u of rate a0>0 and reciprocal support for every other baseline edge. Since Q0 is irreducible, stationary π(b) is analytic near b=0, π_v(0)>0. The stationary EP decomposes as

σ(b)=J(b)log(1/b)+G(b), J(b)=π_v(b)a0−π_u(b)b,

where J and G are analytic near zero; all other pair rate ratios are fixed and finite. Therefore σ(b/2)−σ(b)→π_v(0)a0 log2>0. Fixed additive EP accuracy below half this limiting separation inherits the reading exponent for all sufficiently small b. If other reciprocal-support requirements fail, EP may already be infinite and this implication is invalid. If the parameter affects the baseline or clock, the theorem does not apply.

## Matching protocol and explicit source-label acquisition

Supply a local rate bracket b∈[β,2β], β≤1/2. Choose κ≤min{1,log(5/4)/C} and fixed lag t=κ β^(1/(L−1)). Let r_b=P_b(t)uv.

The derivative tail bound ||∂b P_b(t)−tB||∞≤2t(e^(Ct)−1)≤t/2 implies r′_b≥t/2. Coupling baseline motion with an added rare Poisson clock gives r_b≤bt+P0(t)uv. Baseline support forces the latter exponential series to start at order L, so P0(t)uv≤T t^L with T=C^L e^C/L!. Hence r_b≤C_p βt, C_p=2+Tκ^(L−1).

Collect m transitions whose initial observed label is u; record whether the next label is v. Their outcomes are iid Bernoulli(r_b), by the independent outgoing-stack construction for the sampled Markov chain. Clip the empirical proportion to the known monotone interval [r_β,r_2β] and invert. The inverse has Lipschitz constant ≤2/t. Bernstein gives |b̂−b|≤ε_rel β with probability ≥1−δ from

m≥[8C_p+(4/3)ε_rel]log(2/δ)/(ε_rel² βt).

This is m=O(β^(−L/(L−1))) useful departures. It is not automatically the total reading count; now charge source acquisition.

Let k=ceil(1/t). The sampled chain observed every k steps has propagator at kt∈[1,1+t], whose entries are ≥η. From any initial label, expected observations to reach u are at most k/η. Starting at u, the expected return after an observed departure is at most

1+[1−P_b(t)uu] k/η ≤1+(R+1)t k/η≤1+2(R+1)/η.

Thus expected total endpoint readings to collect m departures are at most

k/η+m[1+2(R+1)/η].

No resets or stationary-state preparation are used. This is O(β^(−L/(L−1))) readings and O(β^−1) expected physical time at fixed baseline, accuracy and confidence. A fixed maximum-reading protocol caps collection at its uniform expected-count bound divided by an additional failure tolerance; Markov's inequality gives the corresponding cap failure probability. For fixed confidence this preserves the exponent and fits the bounded-count lower-bound interface.

Elapsed time also has a matching lower bound. For full jump-resolved records stopped at an integrable time T, b versus b/2 has likelihood score N_uv(T)log2−(b/2)A_u(T), with A_u the occupation time of u. Compensator accounting gives KL=b(log2−1/2)E_b A_u(T)≤b(log2−1/2)E_b T. Parameter-independent observation clocks add no likelihood. Any retained-transcript test with errors≤.1 has KL≥kl(.9||.1)=.8log9 by binary data processing. Thus E_b T≥[.8log9/(log2−1/2)]/b. The upper protocol's elapsed-time exponent1 is optimal even against full jump-resolved access. This does not remove the extra endpoint reading cost.

For EP accuracy, compute σ(Q_b̂) from the supplied baseline and b̂. A certified bound on sup_{0<b≤b0} b|σ′(b)| converts relative-rate precision to additive EP precision. Such a finite bound exists from the analytic decomposition, but its numerical acquisition and stationary-solve conditioning must be included in an actual implementation. The cycle case supplies an explicit bound 2 and an executed replay.

## Consequence and limits

The topology-based candidate predicts exponent L/(L−1): a shortest false reverse path of length2 gives quadratic readings; length3 gives exponent3/2; longer baseline detours approach the event-sensor linear exponent. Expected elapsed time stays of order 1/b. The graph, baseline rates, and a local rare-rate bracket are supplied for the matching upper protocol. This is not a theorem for unknown generators, hidden variables, arbitrary controls, or model-free thermodynamic inference. Strongly varying baseline rate constants, graph size, precision, preparation and sensor costs can dominate the asymptotic b exponent.
