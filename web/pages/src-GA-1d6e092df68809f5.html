## Residual convergence and the monomial estimate

Write (E_n=\|T\phi_n\|_p), (M_n=\|D\phi_n\|_s), and (1/t=(1/p+1/s)/2). The mixed estimate

\[
\|a_n\|_t\le C(E_nM_n)^{1/2}
\]

implies (a_n\to0) in measure since (M_n) is bounded and (E_n\to0). Also (c_n,q_n\to0) in measure by their (L^p) bounds. The pointwise domination

\[
|a_n|^s+|c_n|^s+|q_n|^s\le C_\sigma|D\phi_n|^s
\]

transfers uniform integrability. Vitali therefore gives

\[
R_n=\|(a_n,c_n,q_n)\|_s\longrightarrow0.
\]

This uses exactly the uniform integrability assumed in the theorem and finite (s). It does not infer strong (L^s) convergence from the weaker defect norm alone.

For (a^u b^v c^w q^z), set (\beta=u/2+w+z>\alpha) and (\theta=\alpha/\beta\in(0,1)). Interpolate every (a) between its mixed (L^t) bound and its own (L^s) bound (R_n), and every (c,q) between its (L^p) bound (E_n) and its (L^s) bound (R_n). Hölder is applicable because the reciprocal exponents sum to the stipulated balance and all factor exponents are greater than one. The precise result is

\[
\|a^u b^v c^w q^z\|_1
\le C E_n^\alpha
 M_n^{v+\theta u/2}
 R_n^{(1-\theta)(u+w+z)}.
\]

The exponent of (R_n) is positive, the exponent of (M_n) is nonnegative, and the last two exponents add to (m-\alpha). This verifies the root's formula exactly, including the cases (p>s) and (s<2). Thus strict weight gives the absolute little-o statement, and hence the signed one.

At (\alpha=m), no nonzero degree-(m) polynomial has weight strictly greater than (2m). This is consistent with the theorem: there is no nonzero polynomial having the universal little-o property in that case.

## Necessity and the equality boundary

The same compatible family audited in `REPORT.md` has uniformly bounded full gradients in (L^\infty), hence uniformly integrable (s)-th powers for every finite (s). For a first weighted component of weight (j), the null-polynomial lemma gives a fixed smooth leading profile with nonzero signed mean (I) when (m\ge3). Its means are

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle=N^{-j}I+O(N^{-j-1}),\qquad E_N=O(N^{-2}).
\]

If (j>0), a nonzero (I) forces at least one of the leading (psi_{xx}) or (k) fields to be nonzero. Thus (N^2E_N) converges to a positive finite number, and (E_N>0) for all sufficiently large (N). If (j=0), the leading component is a multiple of (b^m); adding any fixed nonzero smooth mean-zero (k) creates positive defect and leaves that leading component unchanged. The root's zero-defect guard is therefore valid.

For (j\le2\alpha), the signed quotient is bounded below by a positive constant times (N^{2\alpha-j}), so it cannot tend to zero. This proves necessity even at equality. Absolute necessity follows from the same signed witness.

A completely explicit equality witness is already present in this worker's Fourier data. Take the packet potential

\[
\psi=-\cos(x+y)-\cos y-\tfrac12\cos(x+2y),
\]

the family with (k=0), and the cubic (P=b\det D\phi), of least weight two. At (\alpha=1,p=2,s=4),

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle=\frac{3\sigma}{4N^2},\qquad
E_N=\frac\sigma{N^2}\sqrt{\frac58},
\]

so

\[
\frac{|\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle|}{E_N}
=\frac3{\sqrt{10}}>0
\]

for every (N), despite uniform (L^\infty) bounds. This directly verifies that uniform integrability cannot generally upgrade the finite estimate at the boundary to little-o.

## Comparison with the construction worker's core theorem

`CORE_THEOREM_v1.md` agrees with the independently audited finite-bound theorem. Its degree-two signed criterion correctly subtracts a scalar multiple of the **full** gradient determinant

\[
aq-\sigma a^2-bc.
\]

It does not incorrectly subtract only the lowest weighted piece (-\sigma a^2-bc). The distinction is necessary: that lowest piece has signed integral (-\langle aq\rangle), and its residual weight-three term can obstruct a larger defect exponent. The worker's successive leading-weight argument handles this exactly. The absolute degree-two criterion has no determinant quotient. No discrepancy was found.

The root compactness theorem deliberately restricts to (m\ge3), so it does not accidentally assert strict ordinary weight for a signed quadratic determinant. A degree-two signed compactness extension would have to formulate strict weight modulo the full determinant, rather than reuse the raw least weight.

For (m=3,\alpha=1), the strict criterion excludes exactly five monomials (b^3,ab^2,a^2b,cb^2,qb^2), leaving dimension fifteen. For degree (m\ge3) at (\alpha=1), the excluded monomials remain exactly five, leaving dimension (\binom{m+3}{3}-5). The target conformal cubic has least weight three, so the stated (L^2/L^4) near-kernel little-o conclusion follows under uniform integrability. It does not supply the sharp all-gradient estimate.
