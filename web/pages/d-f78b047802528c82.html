ONE-MODE CIRCLE FAMILY

Fix q in (0,1) and take normalized pure states in ell^2(N)

  |psi_theta> = sqrt(1-q) sum_(n>=0) q^(n/2) exp(i n theta)|n>,
  theta in [0,2 pi].

This is a trace-norm compact pure family. Its overlaps satisfy

  |<psi_0|psi_theta>| = (1-q)/|1-q exp(i theta)|
                      >= (1-q)/(1+q) >0.

So every member has a direct path to the anchor and
kappa <= (1+q)/(1-q), independent of theta.

The uniform phase prior has covariance

  sigma = integral |psi_theta><psi_theta| dtheta/(2 pi)
         = (1-q) sum_(n>=0) q^n |n><n|.

Its eigenvalues are already sorted. The top-Q tail is exactly q^Q, so the
width dual gives omega_Q>=q^Q. Conversely, the fixed subspace
span{|0>,...,|Q-1>} captures mass 1-q^Q for every theta, which gives
omega_Q<=q^Q. Therefore

  omega_Q=q^Q exactly.

The pure-hybrid theorem implies

  q^Q/[148 ((1+q)/(1-q))^2] <= e_Q
      <= 2 q^(Q/2).

The upper is implemented by the Q-level Fock projection and a fixed
failure fallback, with one branch and no classical label. Thus the minimum
maximum branch dimension for worst-case half-trace error epsilon is

  Q_min(epsilon)=Theta_q(log(1/epsilon)).

This is a compact-domain result. It does not assume a growing phase-space
radius; the family is pure and the decoder reconstructs the same state.
Unlimited labels are allowed in the converse. Gate complexity and physical
implementation of the Fock cutoff are not charged by the branch-dimension
theorem.

FIXED p-TORUS PRODUCT EXTENSION

For fixed p, take tensor products of p independent circle states. The
uniform torus covariance is diagonal in |n_1,...,n_p> with eigenvalues

  (1-q)^p q^(n_1+...+n_p).

There are binom(s+p-1,p-1) modes at total occupation s. The number of modes
through shell N is binom(N+p,p), and the covariance tail after that shell
is Theta_(p,q)(N^(p-1)q^N). For any Q, a top-Q eigenmode projection
captures the same mass for every phase vector: each selected basis
coefficient has squared magnitude depending only on total occupation, not
on the phase vector. The uniform-prior lower and this deterministic upper
therefore show that the convex width is exactly the sum of covariance
eigenvalues after the top Q, including a partial boundary shell. The direct
product anchor overlap is at least
((1-q)/(1+q))^p, so kappa is finite and independent of the phases.

Applying the width lower and the Fock-cutoff upper yields

  Q_min(epsilon)=Theta_(p,q)((log(1/epsilon))^p)

for the p-torus pure-state family. This connects a Hardy/Szego reproducing-
kernel phase orbit and its exact covariance spectrum to hybrid quantum
memory capacity. It is a mathematical corollary, not a novelty claim.
