Claim: Under x'(t)=A x(t) with Metzler A, known positive initial population p, neutral inherited binary tags whose initial fractions W_ji are known, a sufficient declared-state model, and at most s off-diagonal incoming rates per state, the source derives f'_ji(0)=sum_{k!=i} lambda_ik(W_jk-W_ji), where f_ji=x_ji/x_0i and lambda_ik=A_ik p_k/p_i. With an (s+1)-disjunct code, lambda_ik is the minimum eligible derivative over rows W_ji=0,W_jk=1. Separately, for a time-homogeneous A and occupation-integrated tagged counts U_j=integral_0^T exp(-lambda t)x_j(t)dt, endpoint quantity H_j=exp(-lambda T)x_j(T)-x_j(0) satisfies (A-lambda I)U_j=H_j; the source derives a row equation Z_i a_i=D_i for the off-diagonal rates. If every at-most-2s-column submatrix of Z_i has full column rank, the s-sparse row is unique. The source reports exact rational finite examples, including recovery of 16,256 rates.
Interface: Requires known/observed initial abundances and tag fractions, tag neutrality and inheritance, all declared-state classification, calibrated measurements, and no tag-dependent dynamics. The finite-interval identity additionally requires one common time-independent A and occupation-integrated measurements, not just endpoints. Hidden heterogeneous subtypes can invalidate the mean model.
Proof spine: Apply the quotient rule to tagged and total population equations; the diagonal A_ii terms cancel. Nonnegative incoming rates plus disjunctness make the min decoder exact. For finite intervals, integrate the linear dynamics with exponential weight, integrate by parts, then subtract F_ji times the untagged coordinate equation so diagonal and lambda terms cancel. Uniqueness follows because the difference of two s-sparse solutions is a null vector on at most 2s columns.
Costs: The reported 128-state derivative example uses 49 tags and O(n^2 q^2) comparisons for the stated polynomial code. Finite-interval support enumeration is combinatorial in s; conditioning, population balance, discretization/assay error and independent replicate-population variation require separate accounting. Exact rational code tests are source-reported and were not rerun.
Failure: The derivation does not construct a physical state-dependent tag writer or establish neutral tags, lumpability, feasible destructive-cohort sampling, or acquisition advantage over ordinary barcoding/tracer tomography. Exact-real identifiability is not a finite-precision stability guarantee. No new coding theorem or transformative capability is claimed.
Closest comparator: The source explicitly places the construction alongside known prospective lineage barcoding, group testing, compressed sensing and tracer tomography and credits the polynomial disjunct-code construction to Kautz–Singleton. Those prior-art claims were not independently audited during intake.
Open verification: Independently derive both identities and verify the exact-rank assumptions; assess whether the required tag/occupation interface is physically available and compare charged resources with ordinary prospective barcoding and tracer methods.
