CYCLE09 — Full weak-Lp interface for KMS-symmetric QMS
Date: 2026-10-09
Scope: bounded primary-source comparison; no proof-worker material consulted.

Finding

The checked primary literature does not provide a theorem settling the corrected full weak-Lp profile for arbitrary finite-dimensional KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. The strongest explicit general statement pinned here remains Kastoryano–Temme’s Conjecture 19 (2013): weak Lp regularity for every primitive Liouvillian, and strong Lp regularity in addition when reversible. Their printed high-p weak coefficient is internally inconsistent, however, and a later regularity paper repeats the same printed coefficient. Preserve that equation as source text; do not silently repair it or treat its literal high-p inequality as established. For KMS-symmetric generators, a direct p-q duality calculation shows that the coherent high-p coefficient corresponding to the low-p weak bound is 1/(p−1).

The actual remaining interface for the supplied endpoint result is therefore the positive-input interval 1<p<2. If, for every positive f, one proves

  E_p(f) >= E_2(I_{2,p}(f)),   1 <= p <= 2,

then KMS self-adjointness and the power-map composition law give

  E_p(f) >= (1/(p−1)) E_2(I_{2,p}(f)),   p >= 2.

The endpoint p=1 alone does not yield the interval 1<p<2 by any checked theorem. Gao–Wang’s 2026 paper bypasses an Lp-regularity assumption for an MLSI comparison, but does not prove this pointwise Dirichlet-form profile.

KT conventions and the literal source statement

Kastoryano–Temme work in finite dimension with Hermitian observables A_d={f:f=f*}, positive definite A_d^+, a faithful stationary state sigma, Gamma_sigma(f)=sigma^(1/2) f sigma^(1/2), and weighted pairing <f,g>_sigma=tr[Gamma_sigma(f)g]. Their generator L is in the Heisenberg picture and T_t=exp(tL). Reversibility is KMS symmetry: Gamma_sigma L=L* Gamma_sigma, equivalently self-adjointness in this weighted pairing.

Their power map is

  I_{p,q}(f)=Gamma_sigma^(-1/p)( |Gamma_sigma^(1/q)(f)|^(q/p) ),

with I_{p,r} composed with I_{r,q} equal to I_{p,q}. For p>1 and q=p/(p−1),

  E_p(f)=−p/[2(p−1)] <I_{q,p}(f), L(f)>_sigma,
  E_2(f)=−<f,L(f)>_sigma,
  I_{2,p}(f)=Gamma_sigma^(-1/2)(|Gamma_sigma^(1/p)(f)|^(p/2)).

Definition 9, equation (31), literally says for every f in A_d:

  E_p(f) >= E_2(I_{2,p}(f)),             1 <= p <= 2;
  E_p(f) >= (p−1) E_2(I_{2,p}(f)),       p >= 2.

The strong condition in the same definition, equation (32), is E_p(f)>= (2/p)E_2(I_{2,p}(f)) for every p>=1. The journal PDF and arXiv v2 print the same coefficients. The high-p coefficient p−1 cannot be right as written: it is stronger than the source’s strong coefficient for p>2 and is falsified by the following elementary positive-input check.

Take the primitive tracial qubit depolarizing generator L(f)=tr(f)I/2−f, sigma=I/2, and f_epsilon=I+epsilon Z. For sufficiently small epsilon, f_epsilon is positive definite. Direct expansion of the source definitions gives

  E_p(f_epsilon) = (p/2) epsilon^2 + O(epsilon^3),
  E_2(I_{2,p}(f_epsilon)) = (p^2/4) epsilon^2 + O(epsilon^3),

so the ratio tends to 2/p. At p=3, equation (31) would require that ratio to be at least 2, whereas it tends to 2/3. This is a source-level formula inconsistency, not evidence against the coherent weak profile. The same issue is printed in Bardet’s equation (4.3), despite that paper’s strong-regularity theorem in its stated detailed-balance class.

For p=1, Proposition 8 displays E_1(f)=−(1/2)tr[Gamma_sigma(L(f))(log(Gamma_sigma(f))−log sigma)]. This logarithmic expression is directly defined for positive definite f. Thus the safe endpoint domain is positive f (and positive states under the standard normalization). Although Definition 9 formally quantifies over Hermitian f and I uses an absolute value, do not infer an unspecified p=1 logarithmic extension to sign-indefinite f. The useful entropy and hypercontractivity statements later in KT also use positive f.

KMS p-q duality and the corrected high-p interface

For a KMS-self-adjoint L, the following identity follows from KT’s definitions and composition law; it is an algebraic derivation here, not a claimed erratum. Let p>=2, r=p/(p−1), and g=I_{r,p}(f) for positive f. Then I_{p,r}(g)=f and I_{2,r}(g)=I_{2,p}(f). KMS self-adjointness gives

  E_r(g)=(p−1)E_p(f).

Consequently, the low-p inequality E_r(g)>=E_2(I_{2,r}(g)) implies the high-p inequality with coefficient 1/(p−1), not p−1. Conversely the same duality carries the coherent high-p branch back to r in [1,2]. Therefore, for KMS-symmetric maps, it suffices to establish the low branch on [1,2]; p=1 alone still supplies only one endpoint.

The local depolarizing calculation also shows that no universal coefficient multiplying E_2(I_{2,p}f) can exceed 2/p at each fixed p, because the ratio tends to 2/p near the invariant state. Strong GNS-symmetric results attain that coefficient. This is only a sharp local upper bound on a universal profile; it does not show what profile holds for the full KMS class.

What existing primary theorems do cover

1. Beigi–Datta–Rouzé, Theorem 14, prove a quantum Stroock–Varopoulos inequality for a strongly sigma-reversible Lindblad generator. In their terminology, “strongly sigma-reversible” means self-adjoint with respect to the GNS inner product <X,Y>_(1,sigma)=tr(sigma X*Y); they explicitly distinguish this from KMS self-adjointness in the symmetric-weight inner product. Their theorem assumes X>0 and gives E_p(I_{p,2}X)>=E_q(I_{q,2}X) for 0<p<=q<=2 in their normalization. Proposition 8 supplies the p-versus-conjugate-p identity. With the stated normalization factor, this yields the strong 2/p profile in the KT convention. This is a GNS theorem, so it does not cover arbitrary KMS symmetry.

2. Bardet, Theorem 4.1, proves the strong 2/p profile for finite-dimensional QMS satisfying sigma_Tr-DBC, meaning self-adjointness for (X,Y)->Tr[sigma_Tr X*Y]. The theorem’s inequality is stated for positive semidefinite X and all p>=1. This condition is GNS-type and strictly stronger than KMS symmetry. It does not establish the arbitrary-KMS weak profile.

3. Cubitt–Kastoryano–Montanaro–Temme’s quantum Stroock–Varopoulos lemma is in the unital, reversible, primitive setting; it is a tracial specialization and cannot settle nontracial KMS symmetry.

4. Gao–Wang, arXiv:2609.20753v2, Section 3.1, states that the direct comparison alpha_1>=alpha_2 uses strong Lp regularity, which is satisfied by GNS-symmetric semigroups but is not known to follow from KMS symmetry. Their MLSI lower bound from spectral gap/hyperboundedness avoids needing that regularity for the entropy inequality. It is not a theorem of pointwise weak Lp regularity and gives no p in (1,2) conversion.

Relation to the supplied pi endpoint

The supplied candidate J>=pi E on positive states is stronger than the weak p=1 endpoint J>=2E after the conventional identities E_1=J/2 and E_2(I_{2,1}f)=E. It is weaker than the strong p=1 endpoint J>=4E. It supplies no checked bound for E_p(f) with 1<p<2, and no standard noncommutative interpolation result in the pinned literature transfers it to that interval. KMS p-q duality would then transport the entire low-p interval to p>2 with the reciprocal coefficient; it does not create the missing interval from its p=1 endpoint.

Status discipline

The corrected arbitrary-KMS weak profile for 1<p<2 remains UNKNOWN in this bounded primary-source audit. KT’s 2013 Conjecture 19 is a source-reported conjecture, not a theorem. The printed p−1 high-p term is preserved as written and flagged as inconsistent. The reciprocal 1/(p−1) statement above is an explicitly derived consequence of the low-p branch under KMS self-adjointness, not a claim that the 2013 authors or a later paper issued a correction.
