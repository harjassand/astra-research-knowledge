DISCRETE PRUFER / ACTION PROOF ON THE RESIDUAL RACAH SCALE
8 October 2026

Status: internally derived proof candidate for the residual-scale estimate;
no external proof review or historical-priority claim. This is an exact
transfer-matrix argument, not a Ponzano--Regge approximation. It uses the
exact Racah recurrence and normalization recorded in
outputs/research/sol_classicality/RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt, and replaces
the cutoff/IMS normalization step of ALL_MODE_RACAH_PROOF.txt by a
macroscopic Prüfer phase count.

THEOREM (residual scale)

Let n=2j, d=n+1, L=l(l+1), N=n(n+2), and

  u_k=sqrt((2k+1)(2l+1)) {j j k; j j l},  sum_(k=0..n)u_k^2=1,
  eta_l(l)=(-1)^n d {j j l; j j l}.

Along every sequence satisfying

  n->infinity, l/n->0, l^2/n->infinity,                 (1)

eventually

  |u_l|^2 <= 10,485,760/n,
  |eta_l(l)|^2 <= 10,485,760 n/l^2 -> 0.                 (2)

The numerical constants are intentionally crude. In particular, (2)
rules out eta_l(l)->1 in the only residual scale left by the exact
fixed-ratio, bounded-z, and endpoint arguments. It gives the desired
O(z^(-1/2)) bound for |eta|, since z=L/sqrt(N) and n/l^2 is asymptotic
to 1/z under (1).

1. EXACT RECURRENCE AND ACTION TRANSPORT

Use the exact Jacobi recurrence from RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt:

  L u_k=A_k u_k+B_k u_(k+1)+B_(k-1)u_(k-1),  1<=k<=n-1,
  A_k=[N-k(k+1)]/2,
  B_k=(k+1)[d^2-(k+1)^2]/[2 sqrt((2k+1)(2k+3))].      (3)

Set v_k=(-1)^k u_k and p_k=B_k. For 1<=k<=n-1 the exact divergence
form is

  p_k(v_(k+1)-v_k)-p_(k-1)(v_k-v_(k-1))=-q_k v_k,    (4)

where, for s>=1,

  g(s)=(1-1/(4s^2))^(-1/2),
  e(s)=(d^2-s^2)(g(s)-1)/4,
  p_k=[d^2-(k+1)^2]g(k+1)/4,
  q_k=L+1/4+e(k)+e(k+1).                              (5)

The exact facts used below are: p_k and q_k decrease; q_k>=L;
0<=e(s)<=d^2/(16s^2); and sum v_k^2=1. Put

  h_k=p_(k-1)(v_k-v_(k-1)),
  E_k=H(p_k,q_k;v_k,h_k),
  H(p,q;f,h)=q f^2+h^2/p-(q/p)fh.                    (6)

The recurrence gives h_(k+1)=h_k-q_kv_k and
v_(k+1)=v_k+h_(k+1)/p_k. At frozen positive p,q this update preserves
H exactly. If q/p<=1, then

  (qf^2+h^2/p)/2 <= H(p,q;f,h)
                         <= 3(qf^2+h^2/p)/2.          (7)

At fixed state, each of |partial_(log p)H| and
|partial_(log q)H| is at most 3H. Consequently a parameter change
inside q/p<=1 changes log H by at most
3(|delta log p|+|delta log q|).

Eventually (1) implies the finite admission conditions

  n>=64, l^2>=n, 8l<=n.                                (8)

Let K=floor(5n/8). On l<=k<=K the exact coefficient bounds are

  n^2/8<=p_k<=n^2/2,  L<=q_k<=2L,
  q_k/p_k<=16L/n^2<=1/4+2/n<1.                         (9)

For the q upper bound, use (5) and e(s)<=d^2/(16s^2); the p bounds
follow directly from (5) and k+1<=5n/8+1. Since p,q decrease, their
total log variations from l to K are at most log 4 and log 2. Applying
the frozen invariance followed by the parameter derivative bound gives
the exact action transport

  E_l/512 <= E_k <= 512 E_l,  l<=k<=K.                (10)

The state cannot vanish: (4) would then propagate the zero state in
both directions and contradict sum v_k^2=1. Thus the logarithms in this
argument are defined.

2. EXACT ROTATION COORDINATES AND PHASE ERROR

Take the macroscopic core

  I={a,...,b},  a=ceil(n/4)+1,  b=floor(n/2).          (11)

For n>=64, I lies in [l,K], and |I|-1>=n/5. On I, (5),(8) imply

  n^2/8<=p_k<=n^2/2,  L<=q_k<=2L,
  sqrt(2)*sqrt(L)/n <= omega_k:=sqrt(q_k/p_k)
                              <=4*sqrt(L)/n<=6l/n.    (12)

The last inequality uses sqrt(L)<=sqrt(2)l. In particular omega_k<=1
eventually, and

  theta_k:=2 asin(omega_k/2) satisfies
      omega_k<=theta_k<=2 omega_k.                     (13)

Here is the exact one-step map in coordinates adapted to the frozen
action. Let

  X_k=sqrt(q_k)v_k,  Y_k=h_k/sqrt(p_k),
  s_k=(X_k,Y_k)^T,
  M(omega)=[[1-omega^2, omega],[-omega,1]],
  D_k=diag(sqrt(q_(k+1)/q_k),sqrt(p_k/p_(k+1))).        (14)

Direct substitution in (4) gives s_(k+1)=D_k M(omega_k)s_k. Define

  S(omega)=[[1,-omega/2],[0,sqrt(1-omega^2/4)]],
  z_k=S(omega_k)s_k.                                   (15)

Then |z_k|^2=E_k, and direct matrix multiplication gives

  S(omega)M(omega)S(omega)^(-1)=R(-theta),
  R(-theta)=[[cos(theta),sin(theta)],
              [-sin(theta),cos(theta)]].               (16)

We bound the nonconstant-coefficient perturbation explicitly. For
k,k+1 in I, write F(s)=[d^2-s^2]g(s)/4, so p_k=F(k+1). On
n/4<=s<=n/2+1 and n>=64,

  |(log F)'(s)|
   =2s/(d^2-s^2)+1/[4s^3(1-1/(4s^2))] <=4/n.

Also q_k-q_(k+1)=e(k)-e(k+2), so e(s)<=d^2/(16s^2),
k>=n/4, and d<=2n give

  |log(p_(k+1)/p_k)|<=4/n,
  |log(q_(k+1)/q_k)|<=4/L<=4/n.                        (17)

Thus |log(omega_(k+1)/omega_k)|<=4/n, and, for n>=64,

  |omega_(k+1)-omega_k|<=8 omega_k/n<=48l/n^2.        (18)

Furthermore ||D_k-I||<=4/n and ||D_k||<=2. For omega<=1,
a=sqrt(1-omega^2/4)>=sqrt(3)/2. Frobenius bounds give
||S||<=sqrt(2)<2, ||S^(-1)||^2<=8/3<4, and ||M||<=sqrt(2)<2;
also ||dS/domega||<=sqrt(1/3)<1. Hence
||S(omega_(k+1))-S(omega_k)||<=|omega_(k+1)-omega_k|.
Therefore, with

  T_k=S(omega_(k+1))D_kM(omega_k)S(omega_k)^(-1),

the exact conjugation (16) and (17)-(18) yield

  ||T_k-R(-theta_k)||
     <=8|omega_(k+1)-omega_k|+32/n
     <=416/n < 500/n.                                  (19)

When n>=2000, the right side is at most 1/4. A perturbation of operator
norm epsilon<=1/4 from a rotation changes the argument of any nonzero
vector by at most 2epsilon. Write z_k=sqrt(E_k)(cos(phi_k),sin(phi_k)).
Equations (19) and (18) then give

  phi_(k+1)-phi_k=-theta_k+delta_k,
  |delta_k|<=1000/n.                                    (20)

To connect this phase to v_k, invert S. If
gamma_k=omega_k/[2sqrt(1-omega_k^2/4)] and
beta_k=atan(gamma_k), then

  X_k=sqrt(E_k)*sqrt(1+gamma_k^2)*cos(psi_k),
  psi_k=phi_k-beta_k.                                   (21)

On omega<=1, beta'=gamma'/(1+gamma^2),
gamma'=1/(2a^3)<=4/(3sqrt(3))<1, so (18),(20) imply

  psi_(k+1)-psi_k=-theta_k+epsilon_k,
  |epsilon_k|<=1100/n.                                  (22)

Along (1), eventually n>=2000, l>=2000, 8l<=n,
l/n<=pi/128. Equations (12)-(13),(22) then imply that psi is strictly
decreasing on the core, with every step satisfying

  l/(2n) <= |psi_(k+1)-psi_k| <= 32l/n <= pi/4.         (23)

For the lower bound use theta_k>=sqrt(2)l/n and
1100/n<=1100l/(2000n); for the upper bound use theta_k<=12l/n and
1100/n<=0.55l/n. These thresholds are only convenient sufficient
conditions, not optimized.

Turning-point scope: the exact frozen transfer matrix is elliptic whenever
0<omega_k<2; throughout the full action path l<=k<=K, (9) gives the
stronger omega_k^2<=1. Thus this proof crosses no transfer-matrix turning
point and needs no turning-point connection formula. The only nearby
small-index crossover suggested by the bound e(k)<=d^2/(16k^2), namely
e(k) comparable to L at k of order n/l, is below the starting index l
because l^2/n->infinity. The phase estimate itself is used only on I,
where k is a fixed linear fraction of n and q_k=L+O(1).

3. PHASE COUNT AND NORMALIZATION

The total phase variation T=psi_a-psi_b obeys, by |I|-1>=n/5 and
(23),

  T>=l/10.                                               (24)

For l>=2000 this exceeds 4pi. The phase interval therefore contains at
least T/(2pi) complete windows of the form
[m pi-pi/4,m pi+pi/4]. On each such window cos^2(psi)>=1/2. Because the
phase is monotone and its mesh is at most delta_max=32l/n<=pi/4, each
window contains at least pi/(4 delta_max) sampled phase values. The
windows are disjoint, so

  sum_(k in I) cos^2(psi_k)
      >= T/(16 delta_max) >= n/5120.                    (25)

By (10), E_k>=E_l/512 on I. From (12), q_k<=2L; (21) gives
X_k^2>=E_k cos^2(psi_k). Hence

  1 >= sum_(k in I)v_k^2
    >= E_l/(1024L) sum_(k in I)cos^2(psi_k)
    >= E_l n/(5,242,880 L).                             (26)

At k=l, (7) gives E_l>=L v_l^2/2. Combining with (26),

  v_l^2=u_l^2<=10,485,760/n.                            (27)

Since u_l=(2l+1){j j l; j j l},

  |eta_l(l)|^2=[d/(2l+1)]^2u_l^2
             <=10,485,760 n/l^2 ->0.                   (28)

This proves the theorem and excludes any residual sequence with
eta_l(l)->1. No endpoint recurrence was used: the entire phase and
normalization argument is supported on I, while the action transport
starts at l and ends at K.

4. SOURCE BOUNDARIES, COSTS, AND FALSIFIER

Exact source boundary: (3)-(5), column normalization, and the relation
between eta and u are the exact 6j recurrence/orthogonality identities
already substituted in RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt (DLMF 34.5.8 and
34.5.11-14 are its standard identity sources). This note imports no
uniform 6j asymptotic, no stationary-phase remainder, and no finite-grid
absence-of-counterexamples claim. The action transport (10) is derived
from (4)-(9); the new normalization mechanism is the controlled Prüfer
phase count (14)-(25), not the previous cutoff identity.

The result is asymptotic and explicit on an eventual sequence; it is not
a useful finite-spin compiler threshold. It establishes only the
residual Racah estimate needed to contradict eta->1. The already stated
finite strictness and other scale reductions are separate dependencies
if one uses it to re-prove the global scalar infimum.

Index/endpoint accounting: (4) is used only for 1<=k<=n-1. The action
transport uses updates k=l,...,K-1 and coefficients through K; the phase
updates use k=a,...,b-1. Under (8), l>=1 and b<K<n, so every recurrence
row invoked is interior. No k=0 or k=n recurrence, summation-by-parts
boundary term, or endpoint asymptotic is used.

Decisive falsifier: an algebraic error in the exact divergence
coefficients, frozen invariant, conjugation (16), step variation
bounds (17)-(19), phase-to-coordinate relation (21), or the phase-window
count (25); alternatively, any exact sequence satisfying (1) but
violating (28). No such falsifier was found in this derivation. External
correctness and historical priority remain UNKNOWN.
