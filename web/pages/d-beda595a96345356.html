# Executable finite one-current construction for two-layer radial EIT

2026-10-06. Scope: the known-interface radial two-layer disk model only. This is elementary and no novelty is claimed. This construction makes the full-response theorem finite in a solvable model, with explicit conservative constants and matched controls. It does not solve quantitative design for general known-partition EIT.

## Forward map and exact reconstruction from two scalars

Use real orthonormal cosine modes e_n(θ)=cos(nθ)/√π on the unit circle. The known interface radius is0<R<1. Physical unknowns p=(c₁,c₂), c_i>0. Write c=c₂, t=(c₁−c₂)/(c₁+c₂)∈(−1,1), x=R² and

μ_n(c,t)=(1−t x^n)/[n c(1+t x^n)],

v_n=nμ_n=f_n(t)/c, f_n(t)=(1−t x^n)/(1+t x^n).

For one finite current g=Σg_i e_i the measured voltage coefficient is y_i=g_iμ_i. Select distinct m<n with g_mg_n≠0. With exact data obtain v_m=m y_m/g_m and v_n=n y_n/g_n. Set a=x^m>b=x^n and r=v_m/v_n. Then

r(t)=(1−ta)(1+tb)/[(1+ta)(1−tb)],

d log r/dt=−2(a−b)(1+t²ab)/[(1−t²a²)(1−t²b²)]<0.

Thus r uniquely gives t via monotone bisection; afterward

c=(1−ta)/[v_m(1+ta)], c₁=c(1+t)/(1−t).

This requires only two input harmonics and two scalar voltage coefficients. Every deterministic current with both selected coefficients nonzero works; Gaussian current is one possible design. It corrects the earlier radial reconstruction by replacing its infinite-mode limit with a finite algorithm. One pure harmonic fails, as shown in radial_derivation.md.

## Explicit inverse Lipschitz bound

Restrict c∈[c_min,c_max], |t|≤T<1. This compact rectangle corresponds to a compact set of positive physical conductivities. For i=m,n set a_i=x^i and

L_i=(1−T a_i)/[c_max(1+T a_i)]>0,

so v_i≥L_i. For two states, mean value bounds for the logarithm and the derivative of log r yield

|Δt|≤L_t ‖Δ(μ_m,μ_n)‖₂,

L_t=√[(m/L_m)²+(n/L_n)²]/[2(a−b)].

Proof: |log r(t)−log r(t′)|≥2(a−b)|t−t′|; also its change is at most |Δv_m|/L_m+|Δv_n|/L_n. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz and Δv_i=iΔμ_i.

Let D_m=2a/(1−T²a²). Since log c=log f_m(t)−log v_m and c,c′≤c_max,

|Δc|≤L_c ‖Δ(μ_m,μ_n)‖₂,

L_c=c_max(D_m L_t+m/L_m).

Physical c₁=c(1+t)/(1−t) has |∂c₁/∂c|≤K=(1+T)/(1−T) and |∂c₁/∂t|≤J=2c_max/(1−T)² on the rectangle. Integrating over a two-segment path in the rectangle gives

‖Δ(c₁,c₂)‖₂≤L_phys ‖Δ(μ_m,μ_n)‖₂,

L_phys=√[(K L_c+J L_t)²+L_c²].

Define c_pair=1/L_phys. These are global bounds for every pair of states in the rectangle; they are conservative and fully explicit. They include the voltage /n scaling. Applied to actual measured y,

‖Δ(y_m,y_n)‖₂≥c_pair min(|g_m|,|g_n|)‖Δp‖₂.

## B disjoint pairs, one finite current, 2B detectors

Choose B disjoint pairs (m_b,n_b), 2B input harmonics and 2B scalar voltage coefficients. Let c_b be each proved pair bound and c_*=min_b c_b. Summing disjoint output energies gives the stronger deterministic lower bound

κ(g):=√[Σ_b c_b² min(|g_m_b|,|g_n_b|)²],

‖Y_g(p)−Y_g(p′)‖₂≥κ(g)‖p−p′‖₂.

In particular κ(g)≥c_* max_b min(|g_m_b|,|g_n_b|).

For independent centered Gaussian coefficients with positive standard deviations s_i, the pairs are independent. Since P(|N(0,s²)|<z)≤√(2/π)z/s,

P(κ(g)<ε)≤∏_b min{1, √(2/π)(s_m_b^(−1)+s_n_b^(−1))ε/c_b}.

With s_*=min_i s_i and A_G=2√(2/π)/(s_*c_*), this implies

P(κ(g)<ε)≤min{1,(A_G ε)^B}.

The Gaussian current has finite input dimension and expected power Σs_i²; choosing s_i=1/√(2B) makes that expectation1. No white noise in an infinite Hilbert space is used.

For every0<p<B, integrate the tail of κ^(-p):

E[κ(g)^(-p)]≤[B/(B−p)] A_G^p.

Indeed Eκ^(−p)=p∫₀∞z^(p−1)P(κ<1/z)dz, split the integral at A_G, and use the bound1 below it and (A_G/z)^B above it. These moments concern a proved lower stability constant; the actual best inverse Lipschitz constant is at least as good.

## Exact unit-power randomization

For fair experiments normalize Gaussian coefficients to h=Z/‖Z‖₂, with Z∈R^(2B) standard independent normal. Then h is uniform on the sphere and ‖h‖₂²=1 exactly. The pairs become dependent, so the preceding independence proof must not be reused unchanged.

For B≥2, any selected B coordinates of h have density on the unit B-ball

D_B(1−‖z‖₂²)^(B/2−1), D_B=Γ(B)/[π^(B/2)Γ(B/2)]≤sup density=D_B.

This density follows by integrating surface measure over the remaining B coordinates (normalization is the beta integral). If max_b min(|h_m_b|,|h_n_b|)<δ, choose one small coordinate from each pair; there are2^B choices. Each event lies in a B-dimensional cube of side2δ and has probability≤D_B(2δ)^B. Therefore

P(κ(h)<ε)≤min{1,D_B(4ε/c_*)^B}=min{1,(A_S ε)^B},

A_S=4D_B^(1/B)/c_*.

Consequently Eκ(h)^(-p)≤[B/(B−p)]A_S^p for0<p<B. This establishes the same finite exponent with exact current-power matching. The proof deliberately assumes B≥2; the B=1 sphere density requires a separate elementary angular bound.

## Noise and a certified finite algorithm

Data z=Y_g(p₀)+e, e∈R^(2B). Any exact constrained least-squares minimizer p̂ in the compact rectangle obeys

‖p̂−p₀‖₂≤2‖e‖₂/κ(g).

To make computation finite without claiming an optimizer certifies itself, profile λ=1/c∈[1/c_max,1/c_min]. Let w_i(t)=g_i f_i(t)/i, so Y_g=λw(t). For each t,

λ_hat(t)=clip[(w(t)·z)/‖w(t)‖₂²,1/c_max,1/c_min].

Compute r(t)=‖λ_hat(t)w(t)−z‖₂ on a uniform t-grid of step h, then optionally improve the best candidate locally. On the rectangle the forward t-Lipschitz bound is

L_Y=√Σ_i[2|g_i|x^i/(i c_min(1−T x^i)²)]².

Taking the infimum over admissible λ preserves this Lipschitz bound for r(t). A full covering grid includes endpoints, so the selected residual is at most the global optimum plus η=L_Y h/2. Hence the finite reconstruction certificate is

‖p̂−p₀‖₂≤(2‖e‖₂+η)/κ(g).

The grid certificate is rigorous regardless of the optional local solver. It charges O(N_t·2B) arithmetic and retained2B scalar data. With e∼N(0,σ²I) independent of g, B>2 gives

E‖p̂−p₀‖₂²≤8·(2B)σ² Eκ^(-2)+2 E[η²κ^(-2)].

For exact unit power, η≤(h/2)·max_i[2x^i/(i c_min(1−T x^i)²)], making the second expectation explicitly bounded by the same inverse moment. This inequality follows (2‖e‖+η)²≤8‖e‖²+2η² and independence; the constants are conservative. Without a distribution, the deterministic error certificate already applies.

## Radius, bandwidth, and computation cost

For adjacent m,n=m+1, a−b=R^(2m)(1−R²). Thus L_t and L_phys grow at least at the scale set by m/[R^(2m)(1−R²)] in the displayed conservative construction. At highest pair(2B−1,2B), the tail constant A has an exponential-in-bandwidth factor approximately R^(−4B+2), and its pth inverse-moment bound has the factor R^(−p(4B−2)), as well as mode indices, conductivity-range and T factors. More pairs raise the finite moment exponent B but can make the c_* used by this bound extremely small. This is a conservative tail-bound cost: the stronger summed κ can still benefit from its first pair, so no universal deterioration of actual design quality with bandwidth is asserted. Small inclusions(R→0), near-boundary interfaces(R→1), wide contrast ranges(T→1), and tiny input amplitudes must all be charged. This theorem does not imply cheap robust sensing simply from almost-sure identifiability.

## Fair deterministic and multiple-current controls

In this radial model the same Fourier basis diagonalizes every forward operator. For a fixed current, the signs of its g_i have no information effect under symmetric independent Gaussian detector noise: replacing it by deterministic amplitudes |g_i| gives the identical experiment after known sign changes of outputs. Once a random current is drawn, a deterministic current can copy that design exactly. Averaging random design risk cannot beat the minimum risk among deterministic designs with the same admissible budget.

More generally, take any nonadaptive collection of currents α_ji and independent noise with equal per-scalar varianceσ². The data are y_ji=α_jiμ_i+e_ji. For each mode, its sufficient statistic is

z_i=[Σ_jα_ji y_ji]/√Σ_jα_ji²=√Σ_jα_ji² μ_i+N(0,σ²).

Orthogonal residual combinations are parameter-independent noise. Thus one deterministic current with coefficient √Σ_jα_ji² gives the same informative Gaussian experiment with the same total current energy and one retained scalar per used mode. Splitting current power across physical experiments has no intrinsic statistical advantage in this exact radial idealization. If every multi-current experiment measures all modes, charge those additional scalars; inactive outputs carry pure noise.

A concrete matched baseline: N=2B pure-mode currents each amplitude1/√N, one relevant voltage detector per current, has total current energy1 and N scalar observations. It is statistically identical to a single deterministic current with all N amplitudes1/√N measured at those N modes. This exact equality is stronger than a claim based on a numerical comparison. Optimizing mode powers is a deterministic design problem; our experiment also uses the best of a declared finite list of two-mode power splits on a declared parameter grid, without calling it a universal optimum.
