# Bosonic Gaussian states: representation versus statistical access

Primary source: [Chen, Mele, Mele, Preskill, 2609.26705v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26705v1), verified submitted 2026-09-22. Read technical excerpts: sections 1, 2, and 4's reduction interface. Snapshot saved. We have not independently reconstructed their Bayesian anti-concentration proof.

Author theorem summary: Theorems 1–3 give single-copy adaptive tomography lower bounds, bounded-block lower bounds, and the temperature crossover for trace-distance learning. With Sigma >=(1/2+nu)I, the single-copy copy cost is Theta(n² epsilon^-2 min(n,1+nu^-1)); heterodyne achieves it. In the cold passive subfamily, k-copy blocks require Omega(n³/[epsilon² min(sqrt(k),n)]) without retained quantum memory. Thus O(n² epsilon^-2) requires k=Omega(n²). Positive P representations do not remove this restriction. Section 2.3 emphasizes that covariance estimation and density-operator estimation have different losses.

## Independent exact classical experiment behind a thermal mode

Question: why can a state described by a Gaussian covariance remain statistically expensive under a simple Gaussian measurement? Here is a complete one-mode calculation. It explains one ingredient; it does not prove the n³ adaptive lower bound.

Let M be a photon count from a thermal mode of mean occupation nu>0:

p_nu(m)=nu^m/(1+nu)^(m+1), m=0,1,... .

Define a parameter-free classical channel: conditional on M=m, output T with Gamma(m+1,1) density

k(t|m)=exp(-t)t^m/m!, t>=0.

Summing the geometric series in exponential-generating form gives

q_nu(t)=sum_m p_nu(m)k(t|m)
       =exp[-t/(1+nu)]/(1+nu).

The phase of the complex heterodyne outcome is uniform and carries no parameter information; its squared radius has this exponential law. Thus this thermal submodel's photon-count versus heterodyne comparison is **exactly a classical statistical experiment followed by a fixed noisy channel**. No appeal to generic quantum hardness is needed for this calculation.

The input score is (M-nu)/(nu(1+nu)); using Var(M)=nu(1+nu), its Fisher information is

I_count(nu)=1/[nu(1+nu)].

The output score is [T-(1+nu)]/(1+nu)², giving

I_heterodyne(nu)=1/(1+nu)².

Therefore the retained fraction is nu/(1+nu). A small perturbation of nu requires about (1+nu)/nu as many heterodyne samples for the same local statistical precision as direct photon counts. At nu=c/n this factor is Theta(n); at nu constant it is constant. This is an exact local information identity, not a nonasymptotic minimax trace-distance theorem.

The hidden posterior is particularly transparent:

M | T=t ~ Poisson(nu t/(1+nu)).

The missing-information identity now follows directly:

I_count-I_heterodyne
 = E[Var(score_count | T)]
 = 1/[nu(1+nu)²].

All quantities can be verified by summing a geometric law and the Gamma density. The same information loss is reproducible in an entirely classical latent-count model.

## Decisive limitation and transfer

For a known thermal eigenbasis, photon counting measures all occupation numbers simultaneously, so this calculation does not show every single-copy protocol pays the heterodyne loss. The n³ lower bound requires an unknown many-mode basis and incompatibility among directions; treating the scalar calculation as its proof would be false. Nor does positive-P classical simulability grant access to samples from the hidden P distribution: physical heterodyne outcomes include irreducible observation noise.

Transferable mechanism: an easy low-dimensional representation does not supply an efficient observation channel for the scientific target metric. Gaussian covariance error, state trace distance, latent-coordinate error, and prediction error can yield different conditioning even when the same empirical covariance estimates all of them. A research system that compiles an accessible surrogate must charge the inverse observation map and target-loss calibration.

Concrete use: retain this exact latent-count channel as a control when attributing a purported quantum-learning advantage to computation. It separates ordinary loss of information under noise from genuinely quantum incompatibility of unknown-basis measurements.

Next falsifiable step: independently reconstruct the passive Gaussian-to-qudit reduction, including random photon-sector simulation, copy budget, and preservation of bounded-block/no-memory constraints; then ask which task observables avoid full trace-distance tomography. Predictive or energy targets may have far cheaper access than full state learning. Novelty status: the Gamma/geometric identity is elementary and should be treated as a explanatory reconstruction, not a new discovery.
