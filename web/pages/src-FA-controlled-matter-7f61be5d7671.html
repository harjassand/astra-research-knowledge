Material verdict: the pumping construction survives, but the manufacturing case does not
I did not establish a physically obtainable lattice-control operation that produces useful, retained atomic rearrangement. The investigation materially narrows what remains credible.
K β-alumina is still a possible specimen for a specific microscopic coupling experiment, but it is not presently a justified manufacturing platform. Three findings change the assessment: THz-driven directional ionic current is already demonstrated prior art; the relevant potassium transport involves cooperative defect configurations rather than the original independent-ion model; and maintaining a composition change requires more than switching off the excitation.
I also investigated an independent route—electronically assisted defect annihilation in silicon carbide—because its endpoint could retain the transformation intrinsically. That route exposes a more consequential processing task, but I did not identify a new, validated mechanism that outperforms existing methods.
1. The material audit changes the target
Directional THz-driven transport is already known
An important omission from the previous investigation was Minami and colleagues’ 2020 demonstration of THz-driven macroscopic ionic current in sodium β-alumina. They reported an approximately 50 kV/cm onset and picoampere current under a 1 kHz pulse train, with current direction changing with field polarity. Their illustrative activated-transport fit required an adjustable enhancement factor of 62, rather than supplying a predictive microscopic explanation. This is sodium, not potassium, but it establishes the direct-field baseline that a new experiment must exceed. APS Journals
Consequently, detecting a phase-sensitive current, a net displacement, or increased mobility under THz illumination would not by itself establish the proposed new mechanism. The distinctive claim must concern independently characterised structural control of the transition pathways—not simply a different demonstration of field-driven ion motion.
K β-alumina cannot be modelled by borrowing the fastest results from neighbouring compounds
The phase distinction is consequential. Calculations for K β-alumina use the hexagonal, centrosymmetric structure with space group 194. β″-alumina has different stacking and symmetry. For K β-alumina, the calculated vacancy-migration barrier is approximately 0.72 eV, whereas a cooperative interstitial route has a much smaller calculated barrier, approximately 0.08 eV. These are static, relaxed calculations presented as lower bounds, not measured driven transition rates. The still smaller vacancy barriers reported for K β″-alumina do not transfer to this specimen. arXiv
The implication is not that pumping is impossible. It is that the microscopic state must include the relevant collective configurations and their orientations. The original single occupation variable \(p(t)\) does not describe that information.
The spectroscopy supports investigation, not the required two-control operation
Raman and infrared work on stoichiometric β-aluminas, including potassium-containing crystals, identifies in-plane cation vibrations below \(100\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}\), approximately \(3\) THz, and a prominent Raman \(E_{2g}\) feature. However, the narrow and broad linewidths quoted for ordered and nonstoichiometric specimens in the accessible account concern framework modes; they cannot be substituted for the damping of an identified potassium control-mode pair. AIP Publishing
I did not recover a K β-alumina specimen-level pair with all of the required eigenvectors, damping constants, attainable coherent amplitudes, and mode-constrained transition responses. Therefore, I cannot specify a measured-frequency structural loop and honestly predict its displacement.
That is an unresolved material question—not evidence that suitable modes do not exist.
2. Stronger symmetry predictions—and an additional local obstruction
The full crystal symmetry gives more information than the earlier even/odd parity argument.
For an ideal bulk \(D_{6h}\) specimen in the long-wavelength limit, an in-plane polar current transforms as \(E_{1u}\). Represent an in-plane infrared coordinate by
\[
P=(P_x,P_y)
\]
and an \(E_{2g}\) Raman coordinate by
\[
R=
\begin{pmatrix}
r_1&r_2\\
r_2&-r_1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(RP\) transforms as an in-plane polar vector. Thus,
\[
E_{2g}\otimes E_{1u}
\]
contains an allowed current channel. By contrast,
\[
\boxed{E_{2g}\otimes A_{2u}=E_{2u}}
\]
does not contain a polar-vector channel. An \(E_{2g}\) Raman mode combined with a c-axis infrared mode therefore cannot generate this bilinear bulk current under the stated assumptions.
For a calibrated \(E_{2g}\) excitation selected by Raman polarisation angle \(\theta_R\), and an in-plane infrared coordinate oriented at \(\theta_I\), the corresponding mixed-current contribution has direction
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf J_{\times}\ \parallel\
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(2\theta_R-\theta_I)\\
\sin(2\theta_R-\theta_I)
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]
This supplies an experimentally discriminating angular prediction: rotating the Raman polarisation by \(90^\circ\) reverses this contribution; rotating it by \(45^\circ\) rotates the contribution by \(90^\circ\).
These are my symmetry deductions from the stated representation model, not measurements of a potassium response.
Globally allowed current does not establish locally available control
There is a further restriction that the original construction did not address.
Suppose an individually defined occupied basin retains threefold rotational symmetry. Its scalar free energy cannot have a linear \(E_{2g}\) dependence: an invariant gradient would have to be a fixed vector of the \(E_{2g}\) threefold rotation, and no nonzero such vector exists.
Therefore, assigning
“the observed \(E_{2g}\) Raman mode changes the site preference”

is unjustified without examining the symmetry of the actual occupied many-ion configuration.
An oriented split interstitial or defect environment can break that local symmetry, allowing responses absent for an idealised symmetric site. But those configurations must then be included explicitly. The bulk selection rule cannot substitute for that calculation.
Also, ordinary electrical transport whose mobility is Raman-modulated can have the same allowed tensor symmetry. The angular signature is necessary for the proposed channel, not sufficient to identify its mechanism.
3. The decisive calculation is a constrained transition response—not another phonon spectrum
The relevant quantities should be defined on one common physical control manifold:
\[
g_j=
\frac{\partial(F_B-F_A)}{\partial Q_j},
\qquad
h_j=
\frac12
\frac{\partial(F_{\ddagger,+}-F_{\ddagger,-})}{\partial Q_j}.
\]
Here \(Q_j\) are the physically excited coordinates; \(F_A,F_B\) are constrained basin free energies; and the transition quantities refer to the relevant competing collective pathways.
The calculation must hold the same control coordinates fixed while relaxing or sampling the remaining degrees of freedom. Fully relaxing the control mode away at every transition image would answer a different question.
This also avoids double-counting: a mode dominated by motion of the conducting potassium ions cannot automatically be treated as an independent host actuator acting on those same ions.
Why the existing barriers cannot determine the answer
I constructed an explicit counterexample showing that two potential-energy landscapes can have:
- identical harmonic Hessians at their stable wells;
- identical fully relaxed migration barriers;
- but different ranks of the site/pathway control matrix—one controllable in only one direction, the other in two.
The construction and proof are in the attached report. It is a mathematical diagnostic, not a proposed material.
Its consequence is specific: a small equilibrium migration barrier plus suitable spectral peaks cannot establish the required independent control. Mode-constrained transition forces or equivalent pathway-response information are indispensable.
Static control is still not dynamically attainable control
Even after obtaining \(g\) and \(h\), the optical drive must produce the required trajectory. A linear starting point, using mass-weighted coordinates, is
\[
Q(\omega)=
\left[K-\omega^2I-i\omega\Gamma(\omega)\right]^{-1}
\left[Z^*E(\omega)+F_{\rm Raman}(\omega)\right].
\]
The stiffness, damping, effective charges, Raman forces and transition responses must come from the same physical system.
Two independently observed modes need not maintain a useful relative phase. For a simple pair of damped, unequal-frequency ringdowns, I derived the exact enclosed-area expression and checked it numerically. It shows cancellation when detuning substantially exceeds the linewidth. Pulse shaping can change that result, but its field and energy requirements must be included.
More importantly, at these timescales, enclosed structural area alone need not determine transport. The nonlinear-optical evidence for persistent ionic memory is a reason to validate correlated trajectories or an appropriate memory description—not to replace that memory with a conveniently chosen Markov relaxation rate. arXiv
The first material calculation should therefore be a mode-constrained, collective-path response calculation, followed by driven dynamics validated against independent equilibrium observations. Another assumed-amplitude simulation of the original two-state model would not resolve this issue.
4. Retention imposes a stronger constraint than previously calculated
Uniform transport does not write a structure
Continuity gives
\[
\partial_t c=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf J.
\]
A spatially uniform current starting from uniform composition does not create a composition pattern. A cyclic permutation of identical ions can produce nonzero unwrapped displacement while leaving the useful material structure unchanged.
Thus, even a successful microscopic pump still needs a specified destination: a composition boundary, a trap, a chemical phase, or a reaction that consumes the mobile defect.
An uncompensated ionic pattern also undergoes electrical relaxation
The earlier estimate considered neutral Fickian diffusion. For a one-mobile-species conductor with a fixed compensating background, a linearised continuum model instead gives
\[
\mathbf J_n=-D_{\rm chem}\nabla\delta n+\frac{\sigma}{q}\mathbf E,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{q\delta n}{\epsilon}.
\]
For a nonzero Fourier mode,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\delta n_k}{dt}
=
-\left(D_{\rm chem}k^2+\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\right)\delta n_k.
}
\]
This derivation does not identify tracer diffusion with conductivity. \(D_{\rm chem}\), \(\sigma\) and the relevant background permittivity must be independently appropriate to the material.
For the previously stipulated target—a 10 nm sinusoidal pattern retaining 90% amplitude for one hour—this model requires
\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm chem}
\left(\frac{2\pi}{10\ {\rm nm}}\right)^2+
\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}
\le 2.93\times10^{-5}\ {\rm s^{-1}}.
}
\]
Even with the diffusion contribution set to zero, the dielectric relaxation time would have to exceed approximately 9.5 hours. With neutral compensation eliminating the electrical term, the earlier diffusion limit remains
\[
D_{\rm chem}\le 7.4\times10^{-23}\ {\rm m^2\,s^{-1}}.
\]
These are target-derived constraints, not measured potassium coefficients. They apply to the specified homogeneous linear model—not to every trapped, reacting or phase-separated ionic material.
The strategic consequence is that retention must be built into the transformation, rather than appended to a fast conductor. Charge compensation, trapping, phase capture and defect annihilation are different mechanisms, with different costs and failure modes.
The technological accounting is currently incomplete
No credible energy-per-retained-atom or throughput estimate follows from the present potassium evidence. The relevant denominator is
\[
N_{\rm useful,retained},
\]
not the number of vibrational excitations or attempted hops:
\[
E_{\rm process}
=
\frac{\text{total electrical and optical input, including auxiliaries}}
{N_{\rm useful,retained}}.
\]
Required measurements include source conversion, useful and parasitic absorption, heating during ringdown, cooling, field delivery, pulse-to-pulse accumulation, optical addressing, transport depth and damage.
An atomic displacement length is not spatial addressing resolution. Similarly, a picosecond event is not wafer-scale throughput: repetition rate, useful yield per pulse and thermal recovery determine the processing rate.
5. Pivots tested against real controlled-matter tasks
Driven superionicity in AgCrSe₂: more material-specific, but not the missing manufacturing operation
A 5 October 2026 preprint reports a field-responsive atomistic model for AgCrSe₂. An out-of-plane \(0.03\ \mathrm{V/\mathring A}\) oscillating field, with a separate in-plane \(0.0025\ \mathrm{V/\mathring A}\) probe, produces simulated conductivity near \(1.7\ \mathrm{S/cm}\) at thermostatted 300 K and \(0.05\) THz. The reported \(37\) meV-per-Ag transition is collective; partial local rearrangements incur much larger penalties. This is computational evidence for driven mobility, not demonstrated unbiased pumping or retained writing. arXiv
The reported probe values imply
\[
\sigma_xE_x^2
=
170(2.5\times10^7)^2
\approx 1.06\times10^{17}\ {\rm W/m^3}.
\]
That is the probe-work density inferred from the reported transport definition—not a calculation of the out-of-plane drive’s heating. The full account needs the actual drive work, including \(\int E_z\,dP_z\). A Langevin thermostat holding a simulated temperature fixed does not supply that engineering account. arXiv
This alternative does not solve the retention and local-addressing problem merely by having a more realistic interatomic potential.
A more consequential endpoint: repair carbon vacancies in fabricated SiC devices
I examined a different operation:
Remove harmful carbon vacancies from 4H-SiC power-device material after fabrication, without a destructive bulk thermal treatment.

Carbon vacancies are an important carrier-lifetime limitation in this material. The desired chemical endpoint is
\[
C_i+V_C\longrightarrow C_C:
\]
a carbon interstitial fills a carbon vacancy and becomes an ordinary lattice atom. Unlike a stored mobile-ion concentration, this endpoint can retain the transformation by eliminating the mobile defect itself. Carbon injection is already an established approach to vacancy reduction; the new challenge would be selective, low-temperature delivery after fabrication. arXiv
The comparison must include existing low-temperature methods, not only a furnace:

Carbon-rich annealing at \(1250^\circ\mathrm C\)	Reduction of the carbon-vacancy electrical signature below detection, alongside formation of other carbon-related traps.	A low-temperature, post-fabrication process without side products. arXiv

The last result is a particularly important practical comparator. A complicated optical repair proposal must demonstrate a capability that existing electrical treatment cannot provide—not merely claim low-temperature operation.
The apparent low-barrier route fails its microscopic test
The carbon-interstitial/M-centre literature initially appears promising because it contains barriers as low as 0.11–0.24 eV. But those describe rotations, not long-range translation. The reported \(C_i^{-}\) migration barriers are approximately 1.8 eV basally and 2.2 eV axially. The displayed conversion between two configurations is only the lower of two inequivalent axial steps. Repeatedly switching those configurations therefore does not establish repeated cell-to-cell motion. arXiv
This defeats a tempting but incorrect construction: use a soft internal rearrangement as though it were a low-energy transport cycle.
Nor can the reported electronic trap depths simply be used as laser excitation thresholds. A physically supported optical cycle would need its actual excited-state transitions, branching, lifetimes and damage channels.
Accelerating diffusion alone does not improve repair selectivity
I also examined whether a general mobility increase would be enough. In a simple capture model, suppose the desired vacancy and competing sinks have hazards
\[
h_V(t)=a(t)\kappa_VN_V,\qquad
h_j(t)=a(t)\kappa_jN_j,
\]
where the excitation multiplies all mobilities by the same factor \(a(t)\).
Then
\[
\boxed{
P(V\mid\text{capture})
=
\frac{\kappa_VN_V}
{\kappa_VN_V+\sum_j\kappa_jN_j},
}
\]
independent of that mobility multiplier. A finite pulse changes the fraction completing capture, but not the conditional destination fraction under these assumptions.
A successful repair mechanism must therefore alter capture selectivity or pathway branching, not merely make all motion faster. Charge-state control could in principle do that, but I did not establish a real charge/excitation cycle that simultaneously enables full translation, favours vacancy capture and avoids competing defects.
The SiC pivot was not promoted to a qualified discovery candidate. Its retained endpoint is better defined, but the enabling selective transport operation remains unestablished.
6. The strongest remaining experiment
The immediate potassium experiment should determine whether the missing coupling exists before attempting to optimise a pump.
First: acquire the transition response on one specimen
Use a phase- and composition-verified K β-alumina crystal. Measure its polarised complex spectra and actual coherent structural trajectories. On a matching microscopic structure, calculate the constrained collective transition response at the measured positive and negative displacements.
The required result is not just two nonzero spectral peaks. It is an independently supported change in both occupation preference and competing transition pathways—or a different explicitly demonstrated many-body mechanism accomplishing the same transport objective.
The model must reproduce independent equilibrium observations before being fitted to a driven current.
Second: isolate the allowed mixed response
For an observable \(X\), compare four calibrated protocols:
\[
X_{\times}=
\frac{
X(R,P)-X(-R,P)-X(R,-P)+X(-R,-P)
}{4}.
\]
The Raman sign change can be implemented through the calibrated \(E_{2g}\) polarisation operation, while infrared polarity supplies the other sign change.
Test the predicted angular dependence, together with the c-axis-infrared forbidden-channel control. Use measured trajectories to predict the delay dependence; reversing a delay is not automatically reversing a damped structural loop.
This comparison cancels many single-pump contributions, but does not by itself exclude Raman-modulated direct electromigration. The complete model must retain the external field, internal polarisation fields, heating and ordinary mobility changes.
A convincing result would predict multiple field, polarisation and delay protocols from independently acquired parameters—not explain one observed current by fitting an effective enhancement factor.
Third: distinguish translation from reversible response
Pair electrical readout with an independent transport assay. A stable potassium-isotope profile is a possible design: genuine drift changes the profile’s first moment, whereas symmetric diffusion primarily broadens it.
That assay still needs a sensitivity budget. Diffusion during preparation and readout, finite spatial resolution, temperature gradients, contacts and correlated counts cannot be assumed negligible. I have not established that the unknown potassium mixed displacement exceeds such an assay’s detection floor.
Only after transport is established should a separate retained endpoint be tested. Electrical or optical signals that vanish after the pulse do not pass that test; nor does a lasting signal necessarily identify a useful chemical change.
