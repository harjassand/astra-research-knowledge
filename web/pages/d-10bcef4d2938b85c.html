  Abstract.

    
 We give partial affirmative answers to Landis conjecture in all dimensions for two different types of linear, second order, elliptic operators in a domain     Ω  ⊂   ℝ  N    \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}   . In particular, we provide a sharp decay criterion that ensures when a solution of a nonnegative Schrödinger equation in     ℝ  N   \mathbb{R}^{N}    with a potential     V  ≤  1   V\leq 1    is trivial. Moreover, we address the analogue of Landis conjecture for quasilinear problems. Our approach relies on the application of Liouville comparison principles and criticality theory.

In     ℝ  2   {\mathbb{R}}^{2}   , for bounded potential     V  ≥  0   V\geq 0    (which of course implies that       −  Δ   +  V   ≥  0   -\Delta+V\geq 0   ), Kenig-Silvestre-Wang  [ 25 ]  improved the quantitative estimate (  1.2  ) with     e   −   c  ​  R  ​   log  ⁡  R      \mathrm{e}^{-cR\log R}    in the right-hand side of (  1.2  ), and hence, proved the Landis conjecture with a further slower decay rate than that of Bourgain-Kenig. In order to derive this improved quantitative estimate, instead of Carleman-type inequality, they exploited the nonnegativity of    H  H   , and in particular, they used a  three-ball inequality  derived from the  Hadamard three-circle theorem . Note that they considered a more general operator than    H  H    by allowing a drift term. Utilizing the core idea in  [ 25 ]  with appropriate modifications, a series of results on the weak Landis conjecture in     ℝ  2   {\mathbb{R}}^{2}    have been obtained for more general second-order elliptic operators with a bounded potential    V  V    which is allowed to be sign-changing, for instance, see  [ 8 ,  10 ,  11 ,  26 ]  and the references therein. Another approach can be found in  [ 6 ,  9 ,  30 ] , where the authors improved the quantitative estimate (  1.2  ) when     Ω  =   ℝ  2    \Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{2}    using quasiconformal mappings and the nodal structure of the solution    u  u   . However, both of the above two techniques are difficult to extend in higher dimensions. In fact, there are only a handful of results that address the Landis conjecture in its original form in higher dimensions (cf.  [ 5 ,  45 ,  47 ] ).



 
 More precisely, in  [ 45 ] , Rossi used ODE techniques to treat the one-dimensional case and used it to prove the Landis conjecture for radially symmetric, second order, linear, uniformly elliptic operator    ℒ  \mathcal{L}    in nondivergence form with bounded coefficients, and with a bounded potential      V  ​   (  x  )    =    V  ~   ​   (   |  x  |   )     V(x)=\tilde{V}(|x|)    in an exterior domain in     ℝ  N   {\mathbb{R}}^{N}   . In addition, using two different approaches: one by probabilistic tools  [ 5 ]  and another by the comparison principle  [ 47 ] , the Landis conjecture in higher dimensions is proved for a general second-order uniformly elliptic operator, where in  [ 5 ]  it is assumed that the coefficients are bounded, while in  [ 47 ]  this assumption is relaxed. The obtained decay rates under which the Landis conjecture is proved in these articles depend on the coefficients of the operator and the uniform ellipticity constant, and they are not sharp in general. In this context, it is important to mention that the assumptions in both articles  [ 5 ,  47 ]  ensure that the generalized principal eigenvalue of the associated operator    ℒ  \mathcal{L}    is nonnegative, namely,     ℒ  ≥  0   \mathcal{L}\geq 0    (see Definition   2.4  ).



 
 We also refer the reader to Landis-type results for the time-dependent Schrödinger equation and the heat equation  [ 16 ,  17 ] . For developments in the discrete setting see  [ 12 ,  19 ,  18 ,  23 ,  31 ]  and the references therein.



 
 In this article, we obtain new partial affirmative answers to Landis conjecture in all dimensions for two different types of linear elliptic operators in a domain     Ω  ⊂   ℝ  N    \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}   . Moreover, we address the analogue of Landis conjecture for quasilinear problem as well, see Section   5  . The novelty of our approach to proving the Landis conjecture relies on the application of Liouville comparison principles (Theorem   2.9  ) for  nonnegative  Schrödinger operators, and criticality theory for general  nonnegative  second order elliptic operators of the divergence form (  1.6  ).
