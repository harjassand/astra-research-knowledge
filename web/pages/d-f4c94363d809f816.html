# Reduced localization does not bound the origin saturation power

Worker `metric_geometry10`, 2026-10-07, cycle11. **Proved obstruction to a generic proof strategy**, not a counterexample to the intrinsic polar ideal or to cycle10's universal theorem.

## Question and interface

Can fixed generator degree, finite reduced projective zeros, differential injectivity at those zeros, and constant regularity of the saturated point ideal imply a dimension-independent f-saturation exponent? No. This remains false even when f is a rational QPD quartic and the projective point set is its exact reflection-direction set.

The intrinsic polar ideal has degree-nine generators. Local reducedness away f=0 is valuable: it identifies its saturation with the reduced point ideal J. It does not alone charge the scheme supported at the homogeneous origin. The construction below isolates that distinction exactly.

## 1. Explicit degree-nine family

For n≥3, in R=Q[x_1,…,x_n] put

\[
\mathcal I_n=(x_i^8x_j:i\ne j),\qquad J_n=(x_ix_j:i<j).
\]

There are n(n−1) generators, all homogeneous of degree nine. The saturated ideal is J_n, the n reduced coordinate points.

Proof: localizing at x_i makes x_i^8x_j generate every x_j for j≠i, so the projective scheme is reduced on every coordinate chart. Its Jacobian has rank n−1 at the corresponding point. A monomial in J_n survives modulo I_n precisely when it has support at least two and every exponent is at most seven. Therefore J_n/I_n is finite-dimensional, vanishes in degrees above 7n, and has a surviving top monomial ∏x_i^7. This proves I_n^sat=J_n directly. The point ideal has reg(J_n)=2 and Hilbert function n from degree one onward, whereas reg(R/I_n)=7n and reg(I_n)=7n+1. The latter follows from the finite-length saturation defect and R/J_n's regularity one, or directly from its local-cohomology exact sequence.

## 2. Choose a promised quartic with exactly these reflection points

Set q_ii=i and q_ij=ε=1/(10n) for i≠j, and

\[
f_n(x)=(x^2)^TQ_n(x^2).
\]

Q_n is rational symmetric strictly diagonally dominant with positive diagonal, hence positive definite. It has positive coefficients for every degree-two monomial in the variables m_i=x_i².

Under the separately gated complex-monomial theorem, its reflection directions are exactly the coordinate points. Indeed a possible pair direction i,j must satisfy

\[
q_{ii}x_i^4-q_{jj}x_j^4=0,\qquad q_{ik}x_i^2-q_{jk}x_j^2=0\quad(k\ne i,j).
\]

An outside k exists for n≥3, and both outside coefficients equal the nonzero ε. Thus x_i²=x_j²; the first equation would require i=j, a contradiction. All axes are reflection directions and have f_n≠0. Consequently J_n is also the exact reduced reflection-point ideal for this f_n, not merely an unrelated point set.

## 3. Exact saturation exponent

The least integer k with

\[
f_n^kJ_n\subset\mathcal I_n
\]

is

\[
\boxed{k=\lfloor3n/2\rfloor+1.}
\]

Proof: test a generator x_i x_j. Each monomial of f_n^k x_i x_j has form x_i x_j∏x_l^(2a_l), with ∑a_l=2k. It survives modulo I_n iff each a_l≤3: the selected coordinates have exponent 1+2a_l≤7, and the other coordinates have exponent 2a_l≤7. If 2k>3n this is impossible, proving containment for every generator.

For k=⌊3n/2⌋ choose integers 0≤a_l≤3 summing to 2k: all a_l=3 if n is even; one a_l=2 and all others three if n is odd. Every degree-2k monomial in the m_l has a strictly positive coefficient in f_n(m)^k. To see this, partition its multiset of 2k variable labels into k pairs; Q_n supplies every possible degree-two pair with a positive coefficient. Hence the selected monomial has a nonzero positive coefficient and survives. There is no cancellation. This proves failure at k=⌊3n/2⌋, and failure at every smaller k follows by ideal closure under multiplication by f_n.

In particular (I_n)_(f_n)=(J_n)_(f_n), so f-localization is reduced, yet the necessary f-power grows linearly with n. No singular projective point or excluded f=0 point accounts for that growth; it resides entirely at the affine origin.

For comparison, the diagonal quartic f=∑x_i^4 gives the exact smaller threshold n+1: f^n x_i x_j contains the surviving monomial x_i^5x_j^5∏_(l≠i,j)x_l^4, while the pigeonhole principle kills every term for k=n+1. Its extra complex pair reflections mean that J_n is then only a subset of its reflection point set; the positive-coupling family above avoids that mismatch.

## Consequence and limitation

These I_n are deliberately constructed ideals, **not the polar coefficient ideals of f_n**. The example does not weaken f^2053J⊂I, nor the proposed specialized f^39 improvement. It proves that a fixed-degree argument cannot be based solely on the following facts: degree-nine generators; polynomially many reduced reflection points; full local Jacobian rank; no f=0 projective zeros; and constant regularity of the saturated point ideal. Actual identities of the polar coefficients must control the origin defect.

This is an elementary monomial-ideal obstruction with a complete universal derivation. No finite experiment is needed to establish it, and no historical novelty is claimed. Its downstream use is to reject an uncharged inference from finite étale stabilizers to low-degree acquisition. The next falsifiable step is an explicit specialized polar certificate or support-sector relation that bounds that origin defect independently of n.
