1. EXACT ENVIRONMENT AND CONTROLLED CLASS

The lattice is Z^2 with Euclidean positions and density ONE. Fix T>0.
Let mu be a probability law on a STANDARD BOREL two-sided environment E,
invariant under spatial translations tau_x (x in Z^2), step-time shifts,
and square rotations/reflections D4. Spatial ergodicity of mu is needed
for the extraction/disintegration part, not for zero-slope uniqueness.

On step k, E specifies a translation-equivariant partition into finite
nearest-neighbor connected clusters C_k(x), H_k(x)=|C_k(x)|. Assume
  E_mu H_0(0)^a < infinity for every finite a>=0.                  (E1)
On every cluster, its update is an EXACT FINITE ORDERED product
  J_{k,C}=P_{e_l} ... P_{e_1},
  P_{ij} v: replaces v_i,v_j by (v_i+v_j)/2,
with all other coordinates fixed. Empty products are allowed. Each P is
an orthogonal projection and is positive-entry doubly stochastic. The
length l is finite almost surely but has NO assumed moment or bound.
The word retains all genuine contact edges of the physical step; an
artificial near-contact edge not visited need not appear.

The graph consisting of the genuine edges occurring in the words for
ALL k>=0 is almost surely connected. More precisely, every two nearest
neighbors are joined by a finite path of such edges in that future union.
This is an assertion under mu alone, valid without conditioning on a
joined tangent field or on a temporal invariant component. Forward-past
forcing and recurrence, when independently supplied, imply this property
as detailed in Section 2. Local tangent regularity alone does not imply it.

A controlled solution of deterministic slope q is an invariant joining
nu of E and real relative heights w_x(k), modulo one spatial additive
constant at each k, with environment marginal mu. After root anchoring
w_0(k)=0 it is a spatial cocycle, and its update is J_k modulo constants.
For each delta>0, there are random spatial constants c_R such that
  (1/|B_R cap Z^2|) #{x in B_R: |w_x(0)-q.x-c_R|>delta R}
      ->0 in probability as integer R->infinity.                (V)
The constants need not be integrable; their existence can equivalently
be made measurable using finite-volume medians. Write on a step
  u_x=(Jw-w)_x,
  D_C=||w_C||_2^2-||J_C w_C||_2^2,
  d_x=D_{C(x)}/H(x).
The additive gauge cancels in u and D_C. Require
  E_nu d_0 < infinity.                                        (E2)
The source additionally asks E[H_0^a |u_0|]<infinity for every a.
For the exact ordered endpoint-averaging products and (E1), these first
moments follow from (E2) by Section 3, so retaining them is harmless.
No first moment of w_x, no integrable absolute potential, and no adapted

2. RECURRENCE: A SUFFICIENT PRECISE CONTACT-GRAPH PREMISE

Here is the source recurrence implication, reconstructed separately.
Fix an edge. For j>=1 suppose G_j is measurable with respect to the
forward past at an attempt time and P_mu(G_j)->1. A FINITE measurable
cover of G_j assigns a fixed finite path of genuine facets joining the
edge endpoints to each class. At a visit to a class, each assigned facet
can be attempted within a bounded time slot, with conditional success
probability at least p_j>0 GIVEN THE ENTIRE FORWARD HISTORY at the slot's
start. Slots must be disjoint, and the attempt choice past measurable.

Use a stationary grid with spacing accommodating the attempts. Resolve
overlaps measurably. Cycle through the finite facet list separately for
each class. On infinitely many visits to a class, each facet gets
infinitely many attempts. For its successive attempts, conditional
failure probability is <=1-p_j, so probability of m consecutive failures
is <=(1-p_j)^m. The same argument after every finite time gives infinitely
many successes. Poincare recurrence for the grid transformation makes
infinitely many visits to G_j have probability at least P(G_j). Since
the cover is finite, some class is visited infinitely often. Its whole
path occurs in the future contact graph. Sending j->infinity gives
probability one; intersect over countably many lattice edges.

This needs neither temporal ergodicity nor independence of force events.
It does need a positive conditional lower bound after every past history
where the chosen opportunity applies, not an annealed success probability.
Future-dependent insulation markings cannot simply be treated as past
tests. Passing such finite-volume markings to a bulk forward filtration
is an additional source b:force-transfer gate NOT proved here. We may
instead assume the final connected-graph statement of Section 1 directly.

3. DISPLACEMENT WITHOUT A CONTACT-COUNT MOMENT

For an exact endpoint-averaging product on m vertices, telescope its
orthogonal losses. Each individual loss is (v_i-v_j)^2/2 and their sum
is D=||v||^2-||Jv||^2. If D>0, sort the ORIGINAL m values and divide them
into bands at every gap larger than sqrt(2D). Before a first averaging
between bands, all values stay in their original band's convex hull.
The two values of that first crossing would therefore differ by more
than sqrt(2D), and this single averaging would lose more than D. This
contradicts the total loss. No crossing occurs. Every band's range is
at most (m-1)sqrt(2D). Every intermediate coordinate is a convex mixture
of values in its original band. Consequently
  max_x |(Jv-v)_x| <= (m-1)sqrt(2D).                            (3.1)
For D=0, each telescoping loss is zero and every factor fixes its input;
(3.1) remains valid. There is no assumption on the number of factors.

With d=D/m, (3.1) yields
  |u_x| <= sqrt(2) H^(3/2) sqrt(d_x),
  |F_x| <= sqrt(2) T^{-1} H^(5/2) sqrt(d_x),                    (3.2)
where F_x is the cluster flux in Section 6 and diam C<=H-1. Hence
  E H^a|u_0| <= sqrt(2)(E d_0)^(1/2)(E H^(2a+3))^(1/2),
and for 1<r<2, with any fixed c>=0,
  E[H^c sqrt(d_0)]^r
    <= (E d_0)^(r/2)
       (E H^(2cr/(2-r)))^((2-r)/2).                            (3.3)
All the requested displacement moments and an L^r flux moment follow
from ONE energy moment and sufficiently high cluster moments. No
independence of H,d, or the solution is used.

For the FINITE-n comparison, exact tangent convergence is insufficient
to deduce uniform integrability. The following more general algebra
implements the source c:cluster input when its physical projection-mesh
representation is supplied. Let each elementary matrix A be selfadjoint,
0<=A<=I, have nonnegative entries and row/column sums <=1. Products have
these entry/sum bounds, although the product need not be selfadjoint.
Add zero to the list of initial levels to represent deficient row mass.
For an intermediate product B and values v, Jensen gives
  sum_i [sum_j B_ij v_j^2-(Bv)_i^2]
      <= ||v||^2-||Bv||^2 <= D_total.
Here the missing mass at level zero is included; every summand is
nonnegative. Each coordinate is within sqrt(D_total) of some initial
level. Since A has spectrum in [0,1],
  ||z-Az||^2 <= ||z||^2-||Az||^2 <= D_total.
Thus every elementary coordinate jump is <=sqrt(D_total). The closed
sqrt(D_total) neighborhoods of successive initial levels separated by
more than 4sqrt(D_total) cannot be crossed by such a jump. A coordinate
therefore remains in one connected band of at most m+1 levels, giving
  sup_steps max_i |v_i(step)-v_i(0)|
       <= 6(m+1)sqrt(D_total).                                (3.4)
The constant is deliberately loose. The bound is independent of the
projection-mesh length and passes to a convergent mesh limit.

Geometrically, a Euclidean projection onto the free height polytope has,
on each fixed active-face region, derivative equal to the orthogonal
projection onto its tangent space. Active edge equations identify the
derivatives on each connected active component; the derivative averages
those components. An active common pin identifies that component with
zero input. Integration along the segment between two inputs gives a
convex mixture of these positive selfadjoint (sub)bistochastic matrices.
That algebra is square-lattice compatible. Identifying the ACTUAL finite
diffusion comparison with the convergent common-drive projection mesh,
and charging the O(n^-2) drift perturbation and localization errors,
are physical premises in Section 7, not consequences of this matrix
calculation. General doubly stochastic matrices cannot replace A: a
permutation has D=0 and can have nonzero displacement.

4. HUBER DISSIPATION WITH NONINTEGRABLE POTENTIALS

Take a nonnegative compactly supported Lipschitz a with a=1 on B_1.
Choose M=epsilon R, epsilon>0, and define
 Phi_M(s)=s^2 if |s|<=M, otherwise 2M|s|-M^2,
 F_R(w)=min_c sum_x a(x/R) Phi_M(w_x-c).
There are finitely many nonzero weights, positive total weight, and all
values at those sites are finite. The function of c is continuous,
convex, and coercive. A measurable minimizing center exists: choose the
leftmost zero of its continuous monotone derivative. F_R is nonnegative,
finite almost surely, and invariant under adding a spatial constant.
It need not be integrable. Changing its inputs changes F_R by at most
  2M sum_x a(x/R)|u_x|.
This is integrable by (3.2)-(3.3). Time stationarity modulo constants
therefore makes F_R(w) and F_R(Jw) identically distributed, with integrable
difference. For every K their scalar truncations have equal expectation;
|(s cap K)-(t cap K)|<=|s-t|. Dominated convergence yields
  E[F_R(Jw)-F_R(w)]=0.                                       (4.1)
No expectation of either untruncated minimum was used.

At a minimizing center c_R, put a_C=min_{x in C}a(x/R), and
 D_C^Phi=sum_{x in C}[Phi_M(w_x-c_R)-Phi_M((Jw)_x-c_R)].
Doubly stochastic Jensen gives D_C^Phi>=0. Evaluating the new minimum
at the old center and separating the minimum cluster weight gives
 F_R(Jw)-F_R(w)
 <= -sum_C a_C D_C^Phi
     +sum_x [a(x/R)-a_{C(x)}]
              |Phi_M((Jw)_x-c_R)-Phi_M(w_x-c_R)|
 <= -sum_C a_C D_C^Phi
     +2epsilon Lip(a) sum_{x:a(x/R)>0} H(x)|u_x|.               (4.2)
For a zero-weight site its bracket is zero; the last error sum only
needs sites in the deterministic support. Stationarity and (3.3) bound
the expected error by C epsilon R^2, C independent of R,epsilon.
Each side of (4.2) has an integrable difference, and the nonnegative
loss sum is also integrable: it is bounded by that error minus the
integrable difference. Equations (4.1)-(4.2) give
  E sum_C a_C D_C^Phi <= C epsilon R^2.                        (4.3)

For fixed epsilon, condition (V) at slope zero applies to a ball
containing the support of a. Its unweighted exceptional fraction controls
the weighted exceptional fraction because total weight is comparable to
R^2 and a is bounded. Suppose weighted fraction at least 1-delta has
|w_x-kappa_R|<=eta R. At a center c>kappa_R+eta R the derivative from
these sites is at least
  2(total weight)(1-delta) min(c-kappa_R-eta R,M),
while the adverse derivative from the other sites is <=2(total weight)M
delta. For delta<1/2 a minimizing center must satisfy
  |c_R-kappa_R| <= eta R+M delta/(1-delta).                    (4.4)
Use (V), then eta->0, to conclude (c_R-kappa_R)/R->0 in probability.
Thus, for fixed epsilon, all but a fraction tending to zero in probability
of sites have |w_x-c_R|<=M/2. A cluster of bounded size K fails to have
all its inputs in [-M,M] about c_R only if it contains an exceptional
site; each such cluster contributes at most K exceptional-root counts.
The expected bounded exceptional fraction tends to zero. An additional
O(K/R) boundary fraction removes clusters crossing B_R's plateau edge.

On a remaining cluster, all its inputs lie in the quadratic interval;
stochastic averaging keeps the outputs there. Conservation of the sum
then gives D_C^Phi=D_C, independently of c_R. More explicitly fix K,L:
restrict roots to B_{R-K}, clusters H<=K, and d_x<=L. Bad-root counts
cost at most L times their fraction. For the stationary integrable d_0,
the omitted expectation is bounded by
  E[d_0 1_{H>K}] + E[d_0 1_{d_0>L}] + o_R(1).
Both displayed tails tend to zero by dominated convergence. On good
clusters a_C=1, and sum_C a_C D_C^Phi=sum_x a_{C(x)}D_C^Phi/H(x).
Combining with (4.3), normalizing by |B_R cap Z^2|, and sending first
R->infinity then K,L->infinity gives E d_0<=C'epsilon. Send epsilon->0.
This uses loss truncation to discard exceptional sites; a vanishing
site fraction alone does NOT justify discarding an unbounded loss.

5. ZERO LOSS, TIME CONSTANCY, AND UNIQUENESS

Nonnegative d_x and stationarity imply d_x(k)=0 for every x,k on one
full-measure event. Orthogonal telescoping then makes every factor of
every cluster word fix its input. Thus J_k w=w and relative heights are
time constant. Every genuine contact word equates its endpoints. The
environment-only connected future graph forces every two neighboring
sites to have equal relative values. The field is spatially constant.
No conditioning on w or its future dependence was performed.

For two controlled solutions of the SAME deterministic slope q, form a
relatively independent joining over the FULL two-sided E. On standard
Borel spaces regular conditional kernels exist. Invariance of the two
laws makes the independent product of those kernels invariant under
every spatial/time shift: the conditional kernels transform equivariantly
almost surely, and the group is countable. This can also be checked
directly on finite cylinder tests and then extended by monotone class.

The two common affine-volume centers subtract, so the difference has
slope zero in probability: the bad fraction is bounded by the sum of
the two original bad fractions. For a fixed common cluster put
Q_C=I-J_C^T J_C>=0. Its difference loss satisfies
  (v-vtilde)^T Q_C(v-vtilde)
      <=2 v^T Q_C v+2 vtilde^T Q_C vtilde.
Polynomial displacement moments also add, or follow from Section 3.
Hence the difference is controlled. Section 4 makes it spatially
constant. After anchoring it is identically zero at all step times.

In particular, two conditionally independent copies of one solution
coincide. Its conditional distribution, given E, is therefore a Dirac
mass on the root-anchored full relative field. The same deterministic
functional of E works for any solution at that attained q. This is the
conditional uniqueness needed by the cell flux, not a claim that a
solution is adapted to forward Brownian drivers.

6. FINITE MEAN FLUX, LINEARITY, AND D4

  F_x(w)=-(T H(x))^{-1} sum_{y in C(x)}(y-bar C(x))u_y,
  bar C=H^{-1}sum_{y in C}y.

Join solutions at slopes q,q' over E. Their sum is a cocycle updated
by the same linear J, and its affine growth and energy moments satisfy
the same union/positive-quadratic bounds used in Section 5. It is a
controlled solution at q+q'. Scalar multiples, including negative ones,
are controlled at scalar-multiplied slope. The zero solution is attained.
Thus S is a vector subspace and j is additive and homogeneous; all its
values are finite. In a finite-dimensional subspace a linear map is
continuous, with no extra quantitative bound on its norm asserted.

Under an orthogonal lattice symmetry R, the transformed field and
environment have the same permitted law, slope Rq, and flux RF. Thus
j(Rq)=Rj(q). If q!=0 is attained, q and its ninety-degree rotation span
R^2. Consequently S=R^2 and j is a real 2 by 2 matrix commuting with
the quarter-turn and a reflection. The first condition gives A I+B R90;
the reflection makes B=0. Therefore j(q)=Aq with finite real A.
Symmetry alone does not exclude A=0 or A<0.

8. EXTRACTION, ALMOST-SURE FILLED GROWTH, AND DISINTEGRATION

Take a joint subsequential limit in (C1). Exact finite additivity gives
a cocycle w. Volume-root shifts give spatial invariance conditional on
Y; finite comparison stationarity gives time invariance. The bounded
cluster tangent premise gives Jw modulo constants. From (C2), the root
variable D_n=n^2 e_{n,C}^2/H has bounded first expectation: distributing
the loss among its vertices charges at most n^2/|nQ| times the total
cluster loss meeting nQ. Finite-cluster continuity and lower
semicontinuity give E d_0<infinity for the tangent limit. Displacements
are integrable as in (3.2). Nothing here yields integrable potentials.

Include Y and the filling marks in the invariant spatial law nu, and
let I be its spatial invariant sigma-field. Write
  g_i=hat w_{e_i}-hat w_0,  q_i=E[g_i|I].
Then q is spatially invariant and in L^p. The pointwise spatial ergodic
theorem along Z^2 squares, applied to g_i, |g_i|^p and countably many
fixed-ratio translated rational rectangles, gives an almost-sure event
on which their rescaled block averages converge to the conditional
means. These rectangles are tempered Folner sequences. Their weak
gradient limit is q; a fixed square-cell interpolation has this same
mean since cell derivatives are averages of shifted nearest-edge
gradients. There is no interpolation-dependent factor of two.

For each sample on that event, scale the filled potential by R^-1 on
one fixed box and subtract its spatial mean. The averaged L^p gradient
is bounded for all sufficiently large integer R. Poincare bounds its
W^{1,p} norm. Rellich gives L^p precompactness. Every limit has gradient
q and mean zero and hence is the SAME affine function q.y (with its
box mean subtracted). Thus the ENTIRE integer-radius sequence converges
strongly in L^p, almost surely. This proves almost-sure filled affine
volume growth, not just an unconditional probabilistic conclusion.

For the unfilled correction, stationarity gives
  E[volume fraction {|r_x^fill|>delta R}]
      =P(|r_0^fill|>delta R)->0.
Finiteness alone suffices. Common-gauge constants are absorbed in the
growth center. Therefore w has affine volume growth with this q.
The step difference u_x is also finite and spatially stationary; applying
the same fraction argument to u_x shows its cocycle has slope zero.
Consequently the affine slopes of w at successive times coincide.
Uniqueness of an affine volume slope follows since a nonzero affine
linear function cannot be within delta R of one spatial constant on
asymptotically almost every site of a ball for all delta>0. Hence q is
time invariant as well as spatially invariant.

For any Borel event of (Y,q), the conditional probability of that event
given E is spatially invariant. Spatial ergodicity of mu makes it
constant. Thus E is independent of (Y,q), and almost every law conditioned
on (Y,q) has the ORIGINAL environment marginal mu. Regular conditional
laws exist and are spatial/time invariant because (Y,q) is invariant.
The full-measure almost-sure filled-growth event disintegrates. In each
conditioned law its filling correction is finite and stationary, so
the displayed bad-fraction argument applies DIRECTLY in that law.
The nonnegative loss expectation and countably many weighted moments
disintegrate as well. Each such conditional law is a controlled solution
at its deterministic q. The slope is therefore attained almost surely.

Important quantifier: convergence in probability before conditioning
does NOT in general imply convergence in probability along all radii
in almost every conditioned law. The preceding almost-sure filled
growth event and direct stationary correction argument supply precisely
what the source's short disintegration sentence needs. One may not
remove (C5) and retain the same reasoning.

If E z_n converges locally in L^1 to m, (C4) gives the same limit for
E U_n. Uniform L^p control of rooted filled gradients permits their
expectations to pass through the local limit. Summation by parts (or
weak interpolation derivatives) against compact smooth vector tests
identifies their conditional expected limit with grad m. Since Y is
spatially invariant, tower conditioning gives
  E[q|Y]=E[E(g|I)|Y]=E[g|Y]=grad m(Y)                           (8.1)
distributionally, and almost everywhere once its L^p representatives
are chosen. In particular grad m lies in the finite-dimensional closed
attained subspace S. No slope-conditioned environment change or
assumption of ergodicity of the full tangent joining was used.

 F_{n,x}=-(T H)^{-1}sum_{y in C}(y-bar C)r_{n,y}.
For 1<r<2, Holder gives
 E|H^a sqrt(D_n)|^r
 <= (E D_n)^(r/2)
    (E H^(2ar/(2-r)))^((2-r)/2).                              (9.1)
This is uniformly bounded. The additive drift term vanishes in L^r.
This calculation needs only the FIRST energy moment, and the same
estimate with larger a controls every fixed polynomial cluster weight.
Errors on exceptional localization events must already be included in
(C2)-(C3); no good-event-only estimate can be substituted into (9.1).

On a finite cluster truncated to bounded size, the rooted local tangent
and correction convergence make F_n converge to the flux in Section 6.
Remove the truncation using (9.1). The conditional law given (Y,q) is a
controlled solution on mu, so conditional uniqueness identifies its
mean flux with j(q). Thus the expected limiting flux given Y is
  E[j(q)|Y]=j(E[q|Y])=j(grad m(Y)),
where linearity on finite-dimensional S justifies the interchange.

Now test exact stationarity against sum_x f(x/n)z_n(x) for a smooth
compact f in U. On complete free clusters subtract the explicit source
and use sum_C r=0. Taylor-expand f at bar C/n. The resulting identity is
  0=T n^-2 E sum_x b(x/n)f(x/n)
     -T n^-2 E sum_C H F_{n,C}.grad f(bar C/n) + error_n.
The second-order error is bounded by
  C n^-3 E sum_{C meeting an enlarged test support}
                    sum_{x in C} H^a |r_{n,x}| = O(n^-1).
The cluster sum is restricted to clusters meeting the closed support of
f, which also contains the support of grad f. Clusters with no vertex in
that support have zero original test contribution and zero root flux
test contribution, even if their barycenter happens to lie inside it.
Clusters intersecting the support from outside are covered by one
further H power: charge each such cluster to a site inside the deterministic
support and use the maximum displacement bound over its whole cluster. The
moment estimate above and (C2) bound the resulting expected sum by
O(n^2). Replacing grad f(bar C/n) by grad f(x/n) costs O(n^-1) for
the same reason. Clusters reaching pins/free-buffer boundaries are
removed by the charged (C3) localization estimate. All source errors
in a cluster's first moment are covered by its O(n^-1 H^a) scaled
drift term. There is no reversed or symmetrized projection product.

Both normalized sums have square vertex density one. Passing to their
limits and dividing by T proves
  integral_U j(grad m).grad f=integral_U b f.                   (9.2)
If S={0}, its flux is zero and the argument remains valid. When S is
nonzero, (9.2) is A integral grad m.grad f=integral b f.

10. BOUNDARY PREMISE AND POSITIVE RESPONSE

To infer Dirichlet response require the comparison/filling clauses up
to the IDENTICAL ordinary fixed boundaries of polygons D_n converging
to one bounded smooth D. In addition to interior (C1)-(C5), require a
filled extension into R^2, zero outside D_n, such that
  sup_n E||U_n||_{W^{1,p}(R^2)}^p < infinity,
  E||U_n-z_n||_{L^1(D_n)} ->0,  p>1.                          (B1)
Retain energy on edges incident to the common fixed boundary. A
hole meeting the connected zero exterior must be filled from a ZERO
rim anchor, with the same rim-path energy and error bounds. An arbitrary
hole anchor need not preserve this property. The source c:exterior-zero
argument describes exactly this additional geometric/quantitative gate;
the current interior comparison alone does not establish it on square
polygons. Arbitrary disconnected internal observed sets are excluded.

Jensen gives a uniform deterministic W^{1,p} bound for E U_n. Rellich
and weak compactness supply a subsequential global mean limit, and
the expected L^1 error identifies it with E z_n's limit m. Since all
supports eventually lie in arbitrarily small neighborhoods of closure D,
the limit is zero outside D. A global W^{1,p} function zero outside a
smooth domain has zero inside trace: flatten the boundary and use its
absolutely continuous normal-line representatives, which are zero on
the exterior side. Therefore m belongs to W^{1,p}_0(D).

Assume (B1) and (C1)-(C5) apply to at least ONE nonzero smooth source
b compactly supported in D, and to the ACTUAL finite-volume exponential
tilt: if mu_{n,0} is the untilted equilibrium, then
  dmu_{n,t}/dmu_{n,0} = Z_n(t)^{-1} exp(t B_n),
  B_n=n^-2 sum_x b(x/n)h_x,  t>0 fixed sufficiently small.      (B2)
The drift difference is t n^-2 b. This density/drift matching is part
of the actual experiment; a formal source symbol is insufficient.
Each finite polytope is compact, so psi_n(t)=log E_0 exp(t B_n) is smooth
and convex. Consequently
  psi_n'(t)-psi_n'(0)
   =n^-2 sum_x b(x/n)[E_t h_x-E_0 h_x] >=0.                    (10.1)
This is a first-principles sign inequality; it needs neither FKG nor
an unproved monotone synchronous coupling, and b may be signed.

If S={0}, (9.2) with source t b would force t b=0 distributionally,
a contradiction. Hence S=R^2 and j=A I. Equation (9.2) also excludes
A=0. Thus m solves -A Delta m=t b with zero trace.

The zero-trace solution is unique even in W^{1,p}_0, p>1. If u is a
zero-trace distributionally harmonic difference, for any g in C_c^infinity
D solve -Delta phi=g with zero Dirichlet data. On smooth bounded D,
phi is smooth up to the boundary. Approximate phi in W^{1,p'}_0 by
compact smooth functions in the weak equation. Then
  0=integral grad u.grad phi=integral u g,
where the last integration by parts uses u's zero-trace approximation.
Thus u=0. Standard Dirichlet solvability gives
  m=(t/A)(-Delta_D)^{-1}b.
By the L^1 mean convergence, (10.1) passes to
  integral_D b m >=0.
For v=(-Delta_D)^{-1}b, its Dirichlet energy satisfies
  integral b v=integral |grad v|^2>0
since b is nonzero. Therefore t/A>0, and A>0. Its finiteness was
already proved in Section 6. No pointwise positivity assumption on m
is needed for this conclusion.

Once these premises hold for every sufficiently small smooth source,
the same unique weak Dirichlet problem identifies every subsequence.
The deterministic expected response therefore converges to
  A^{-1}(-Delta_D)^{-1}(actual source).
The arbitrary identical-boundary case needs (B1) for its DIFFERENCE
extension and the same common mu; the absolute boundary mean is not
silently supplied. The source's two-arc harmonic trace statement needs
additional local trace and boundedness inputs near those arcs; it is
outside this packet. The Gaussian moment-generating-function step is

11. EARLY FALSIFIERS / SCOPED LIMITATIONS

Orthogonality/positive elementary contraction is essential. A swap of
two values preserves their quadratic norm and is doubly stochastic,
yet moves them. Its zero loss cannot force endpoint equality. A heatbath
ancestry operator or an arbitrary row-stochastic contact description
therefore cannot discharge the tangent premise.

D4 is essential for the scalar conclusion. Anisotropic reflection/contact
laws may yield a matrix flux even when the rest of the argument works.
Full ordered products are essential: for three sites on an L, the two
contact orders have the same energy loss but different first-moment flux.

Environment ergodicity is essential for the asserted conditioning-on-q
environment law. Stationary symmetry or a mixture of ergodic laws does
not make P(q in B|E) constant. A joined tangent law itself may be
nonergodic; that is permitted and handled by its invariant sigma-field.

Interior compactness does not supply a boundary trace. On a smooth D,
u_n(x)=min(1,n dist(x,D^c)), extended by zero, has exact zero exterior
and bounded W^{1,p} on every fixed compact subset, but converges locally
to 1, whose inside trace is 1. Its GLOBAL gradient p-moment grows like
n^{p-1}. It falsifies any inference of (B1) from interior estimates.

First energy moment DOES suffice for flux UI once the full displacement
bound and cluster moments hold, by (9.1). A first moment of the Sobolev
norm DOES NOT suffice for rooted filled-gradient UI. Small probability
of a bad event without its moment charge DOES NOT control its flux.

No rate of convergence, quantitative lower bound for A, square GFF
fluctuation theorem, logarithmic covariance, arbitrary observation-law
comparison, or priority claim is obtained. The attained-slope/flux/PDE
transfer is complete conditional mathematics under the stated inputs.
The outstanding mathematical work for the actual square model is the
physical comparison/filling/common-bulk/recurrence/boundary interface,
each with the exact quantifiers above.
