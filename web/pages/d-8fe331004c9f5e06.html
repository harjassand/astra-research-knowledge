1. FULL DERIVATION

1.1 Fixed-scale entropy comparison.
Write A=A_T(Y) and M=EA. Since I<=A<=(1+L)I, all Gaussian mixture entropies
are well-defined and the mixture covariance is M. The nonnegativity of
D(Law(Z_T)||N(0,M)), or Gaussian maximum entropy, yields

 I(Y;Z_T) <= 1/2 [log det M-E log det A].              (1)

For F(C)=-log det C,
 D^2F(C)[H,H]=tr(C^-1 H C^-1 H)<=||H||_F^2
whenever C>=I. Integrating the second derivative on the line segment joining
M and A gives
 F(A)<=F(M)+DF(M)[A-M]+1/2||A-M||_F^2.
Take expectations; the linear term vanishes. Thus

 I(Y;Z_T)<=1/4 E||A-EA||_F^2
           =1/8 E||f_L(TB)-f_L(TB')||_F^2,           (2)

where B' is an independent copy. This uses an entropy upper bound directly,
not the inadequate replacement of entropy by bounded Hellinger distance.

1.2 Exact matrix scale integral.
For positive definite B,C choose eigenvalues b_i,c_j and orthonormal eigenbases
u_i,v_j. Put w_ij=|<u_i,v_j>|^2. Its row and column sums are one. Functional
calculus and expansion of the Frobenius square give

 ||f_L(TB)-f_L(TC)||_F^2
 =sum_ij w_ij [f_L(Tb_i)-f_L(Tc_j)]^2.                (3)

For b,c>0, a direct substitution T=L u gives
 integral [f_L(Tb)-f_L(Tc)]^2 dT/T = L^2 kappa(b/c),
 kappa(b/c)= (b+c)/(b-c) log(b/c)-2, b!=c,
 kappa(1)=0.                                        (4)

For an explicit integration certificate, writing d=b-c, the integrand after
removing L^2 is
 u d^2/[(1+bu)^2(1+cu)^2]
 = (b+c)b/[d(1+bu)]-b/(1+bu)^2
   -(b+c)c/[d(1+cu)]-c/(1+cu)^2.
The logarithmic terms integrate to (b+c)/d log(b/c); the two squared terms
integrate to -2. The boundary integrals are finite for positive b,c.
The script verifies this rational partial-fraction identity in 60 exact cases.

For t=log(b/c), kappa=t coth(t/2)-2. It is nonnegative by its integral form,
and
  kappa<=t^2/6.                                     (5)
To prove (5), for x>=0 define
 H(x)=(1+x^2/3)sinh x-x cosh x.
Then H(0)=0 and H'(x)=x/3 (x cosh x-sinh x)>=0 because the last bracket has
zero initial value and derivative x sinh x>=0. Hence x coth x<=1+x^2/3.
Take x=|t|/2. Also kappa<=|t| follows from
 2|t|/(exp(|t|)-1)<=2.

From (3)-(5),
 integral ||f_L(TB)-f_L(TC)||_F^2 dT/T
 <= L^2/6 sum_ij w_ij (log b_i-log c_j)^2
 = L^2/6 ||log B-log C||_F^2.                        (6)

The last identity expands both squared traces and the mixed trace. It does
not require B and C to commute. Integrate (2), use Tonelli, (6), and
 E||log B-log B'||_F^2=2 E||log B-E log B||_F^2.
This proves (A), including all dimension constants. Poincare applied to the
real scalar entries of log B and summed proves (B). Measurable fields with
finite log second moment are covered directly; no smooth approximation is
needed for (A). The gradient statement needs its stated Sobolev domain.

2. WHAT IS ACTUALLY ACQUIRED, AND COSTS

Implemented input: an explicit table p_1,...,p_K, with p_k>=0, sum p_k=1,
and b_ki>0 rational, i=1,...,m. Y is the finite reference state and
B(Y=k)=diag(b_k1,...,b_km). All K*m+K scalar inputs are read. No density,
posterior, matrix spectrum, state probabilities, or calibration is acquired
for free. The reference law is supplied by the table, rather than inferred
from one archival observation.

For every rational x>0, normalize x=2^k z, 1<=z<2. Write t=(z-1)/(z+1),
0<=t<1/3, and use
 log z=2 sum_n>=0 t^(2n+1)/(2n+1).
After N terms the omitted nonnegative tail is at most
 2 t^(2N+1)/[(2N+1)(1-t^2)].
The same series at t=1/3 bounds log 2. Sign-aware rational interval arithmetic
then bounds log x to absolute width eta. No floating logarithm is trusted.

Compute V=1/2 sum_ab p_a p_b sum_i (log b_ai-log b_bi)^2 with interval
arithmetic and return L^2 V_upper/24. The implemented transparent pair loop
uses O(K^2 m) rational operations, O(Km) stored log intervals, and
O(Km(q+log H)) series terms when eta=2^-q and H bounds the absolute binary
exponents of input rationals. The numerator/denominator bit sizes grow
polynomially in full table encoding length and q. It can be rewritten as
centered first/second moments in O(Km), but that optimization was not needed
for the fixture. m and K are charged, and L is numerical in the bound.
Matrix logs for general noncommuting supplied matrices are not implemented.
The proof does not imply a free matrix-log oracle.

An optional genuinely data-acquired variant is also elementary. Suppose one
can evaluate supplied diagonal B(Y) on fresh iid Y and has certified ranges
log b_i(Y) in [a_i,c_i]. Put W=sum_i(c_i-a_i)^2. From 2n draws form independent
pairs Z_j=1/2||log B(Y_(2j-1))-log B(Y_(2j))||_F^2 in [0,W/2]. Then EZ_j=V,
and Hoeffding gives, with probability >=1-delta,
 V<=mean Z_j+(W/2)sqrt(log(1/delta)/(2n)).
If each scalar log is computed within eta, add
 2 eta sum_i(c_i-a_i)+2m eta^2
for a deterministic rounding bound. Multiply the result by L^2/24.
This charges 2n source draws, O(nm) evaluations and logs, the range certificate,
and precision eta. To make the sampling part contribute <=tau/2 to the final
information upper, n>=L^4 W^2 log(1/delta)/(1152 tau^2) suffices.
This sample-bound variant was derived but not implemented/tested here. Without
known log ranges or a tail certificate, arbitrary rare covariance states can
escape finite-sample bounds; no distribution-free acquisition claim is made.

The naive gradient route can fail even in dimension one: take Y uniform on
[-1,1], B(Y)=Y^2, and the admitted f_L. Away from the zero,
 partial_y f_L(Ty^2)=2Ty/(1+Ty^2/L)^2,
 integral_0^infinity |partial_y f_L(Ty^2)|^2 dT/T
 =2L^2/(3y^2).
Its expectation is infinite. Thus integrating the absolute-field Poincare
energy cannot replace the required scale entropy bound, despite a bounded

to that proposed bound, not a counterexample to KLS or to the source strategy.
Here log(Y^2) nevertheless has finite variance, so (A) extends by truncation
and gives a finite bound. This difference is the useful second mechanism.

Positive definiteness/common support is essential. If B=0 on one positive-mass
reference state and B=b>0 on another, A_T converges to different covariances

for every L>0. Hence integral I dT/T diverges at infinity. A COMMON fixed
nullspace can be removed and the theorem applied on its positive complement;
mismatched nullspaces are not silently regularized for free.
