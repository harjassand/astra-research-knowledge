## 1. Tangency preserves a small radial derivative

For r≥K,

    t_i·e_r=t_i·(x−x_i)/r,
    |partial_r q|≤C(ell^(−1)+a ell/r)≤C kappa/a.

The last inequality uses R/r≤sqrt(2), ell⁻¹=sqrt(a/R), and a ell/r=sqrt(aR)/r. Partition gradients and the wave terms are both controlled with bounded overlap. Hence

    partial_r f=−a(r/R)qE+O(kappa/a)E.                 (1)

Using only |Dq|≤Ca would lose this estimate and would not prove the claim.

## 2. Fixed-sigma smoothing really preserves (1)

For h∈C²(B(x,s)), define its spherical mean M_h(t). The divergence theorem gives

    M_h'(t)=t^(1−n)/|S^(n−1)| ∫_(B(x,t))Delta h.

Therefore |M_h(t)−h(x)|≤t² sup|Delta h|/(2n). A normalized nonnegative radial kernel average is a convex mixture of these means, so

    |T_s h(x)−h(x)|≤C_psi s² sup_(B(x,s))|Delta h|.   (2)

This estimate depends on the Laplacian rather than the full Hessian and is the decisive improvement. Differentiate G by holding epsilon(x) fixed in T_epsilon first:

    partial_j G=T_epsilon(partial_j f)
       −(partial_j epsilon)∫psi(z)z·Df(x−epsilon z)dz.

Apply (2) to h=partial_j f. The gated DDelta f estimate and local comparability imply

    |T_epsilon(partial_j f)−partial_j f(x)|
       ≤C_K epsilon² kappa a E≤C_(K,sigma) kappa/a E.

The variable-radius term is at most C|D epsilon|aE≤C_sigma E/R≤C_sigma kappa/a E, since Ra≥1. Thus

    |DG−Df|≤C_(K,sigma) kappa/a E.                   (3)

For H, differentiating the kernel itself costs at most C_sigma a^(n+1), with support volume O_sigma(a⁻ⁿ). Multiplication by the undifferentiated W bound yields

    |DH|≤C_(K,sigma) kappa/a E.                      (4)

No differentiated weak solution, nodal-boundary trace, or higher-order parameter smallness has been smuggled in. The constants may be large but finite for this already fixed construction. Combining (1), (3), (4),

    partial_r F=−a(r/R)qE+O_(K,sigma)(kappa/a)E.

Since eta=kappa/a²→0, one sufficiently large tail threshold R1 makes the error divided by aE smaller than tau/8. For r≥R1 and |q|≥tau/2,

    |partial_r F|≥tau aE/8>0.                        (5)

The threshold is selected after the construction; it changes no K, sigma, tau or frequency envelope. The assertion is about one fixed F. Radial symmetry of the kernel is load-bearing for the improved error: a generic kernel is not a mixture of spherical means.

## 3. Harmonic components cannot hide exterior extrema

Increase the tail threshold to R2=R1+2rho. Suppose F has any local maximum or minimum at x with r>R2. Outside O, (5) is impossible at a critical point. Inside O, the harmonic strong maximum principle gives a constant on a small neighborhood, and harmonic unique continuation propagates that constant throughout the connected component C of O containing x.

Sparsity supplies y∈B(x,rho/a(x)) outside Omega. Follow the straight segment from x to y. Its first exit point z from O is approached through C, satisfies |q(z)|=tau/2, and has |z|>R1. Smoothness of F and constancy in C imply DF(z)=0. Equation (5) contradicts this.

The component C need not be bounded, and its boundary need not be smooth. A nearby exit point supplied by microball sparsity is enough. One must use first exit from O, rather than assuming that the endpoint y belongs to the boundary of C. The same proof even rules out a zero-valued tail local extremum.

## 4. All nodal domains intersect one compact ball

Suppose a positive global nodal domain D is disjoint from closed B_(R2). Fix x0∈D with F(x0)=b>0. Uniform decay gives S with |F|<b/2 outside B_S. On closure(D)∩closed(B_S), F attains a maximum at least b. Every finite boundary point of D has F=0: a positive value would provide a connected positive neighborhood meeting D and hence lying in the same global component. The maximizer is therefore an interior point of D outside B_(R2), contradicting Section 3. Apply the same argument to −F for negative domains.

Every global nodal domain consequently meets closed B_(R2) at a point where F is nonzero. This fact alone would be insufficient for arbitrary smooth sign sets, which can have infinitely many components accumulating at a flat zero.

## 5. Harmonic zero neighborhoods make the compact count finite

At a zero, F is harmonic on an open neighborhood and thus real analytic there. Each sign set is locally defined by one analytic inequality. At a nonzero point its sign is locally constant, so each sign set is locally either the full neighborhood or empty, also an analytic description. Hence both global sets {F>0} and {F<0} are semianalytic in the real analytic manifold Rⁿ. F itself need not be globally analytic, and no uniform analytic radius at all tail zeros is needed.

[Bierstone–Milman, *Semianalytic and subanalytic sets*](https://www.numdam.org/item/?id=PMIHES_1988__67__5_0), published 28 January 1988, Corollary 2.7, source page 13, establishes local finiteness of the connected-component family of a semianalytic set. Its short proof reduces each local sign description to finitely many connected pieces. Cover closed B_(R2) by finitely many neighborhoods each meeting only finitely many global components of either sign set. Thus only finitely many such components meet the ball. Section 4 says that these include every global nodal domain. **F has finitely many global nodal domains.**
