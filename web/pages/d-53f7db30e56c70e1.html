# Independent adversarial audit: Cycle 3 Poisson/Parseval tomography

## Verdict

The Cycle 3 transfer is mathematically sound under an ideal calibrated instrument and the exact observation model in Huang–Li–Needell (HLN). A scaled iid sensing block can be completed to a Parseval rank-one POVM, and Poisson thinning gives exactly the independent Poisson observations required by HLN. With a fixed number of launched photons, the full outcome law is multinomial and can be related to the Poisson experiment by conditioning on total launched count. Normalization gives the claimed O(sqrt(d/(N eta))) phase error with conservative constants.

This is a composition of existing optimal-rate tomography/phase-retrieval ingredients, not a new photon sample-complexity rate. Nonadaptive single-copy pure-state tomography already has a matching Omega(d/epsilon^2) lower bound, and established algorithms attain the same order. The potentially specific contribution is one fixed finite rank-one POVM with O(d) outcomes plus a polynomial convex estimator and an exact Poisson-to-multinomial interface. I did not find a primary source that already states precisely that combined interface, but this narrow novelty claim is UNKNOWN pending a focused literature search. It is not a demonstrated hardware advantage: a generic optical Naimark unitary uses O(d^2) two-mode elements, and calibration, losses, background, and solver precision remain uncharged.

## Imported theorem and quantifiers

HLN, [Stable Phase Retrieval: Optimal Rates in Poisson and Heavy-tailed Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00551v1), Assumptions 1–2 and Theorem 1, use independent sensing vectors whose entries are iid complex sub-Gaussian scalars phi satisfying

\[
\mathbb E\phi=\mathbb E\phi^2=0,\quad
\mathbb E|\phi|^2=1,\quad \mathbb E|\phi|^4=1+\mu,\quad \mu>0,
\]

and conditionally independent Y_j ~ Pois(|g_j^* w|^2). If m >= Ld, the source states probability at least 1-O(exp(-cd)), “simultaneously for all signals,” and the leading-eigenvector output of its PSD least-squares program obeys

\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le C_3\max\{K,\|w\|^{-1}\}\sqrt{d/m}.
\]

Here L,c,C_3 are hidden constants depending on K,mu; the complex phase quotient is defined at source Eq. (7). The Gaussian choice has mu=1. The finite alphabet phi=0 with probability 3/4 and phi in {2,-2,2i,-2i}, each with probability 1/16, has the required moments and mu=3, with K=O(1).

The theorem's literal “simultaneously for all signals” wording is stronger than separate fixed-signal bounds. Since the conditional Poisson law changes with w, do not describe it as one physical count record that works for every possible unknown state. The operational guarantee needed here is the uniform tail statement

\[
\sup_{\|x\|=1}\Pr_{G,Y|x}\{\text{phase error exceeds the bound}\}\le C e^{-cd},
\]

where probability is over randomized design and counts generated by that state. This follows from the theorem as stated and suffices for both transfers below. If the intended guarantee is adversarial state selection after a realized device draw, then the stronger common-event interpretation must be checked against the paper's coupled, state-dependent noise process; that post-selection claim is unnecessary for a state fixed before the random measurement is drawn.

HLN's CVX-LS program is the convex problem

\[
\min_{Z\succeq0}\| (g_j^*Zg_j)_{j=1}^m-Y\|_2,
\]

followed by z_* = sqrt(lambda_1(Z_*)) u_1(Z_*). The paper calls convex programming efficient, but gives no bit complexity, solver tolerance/runtime, or conditioning guarantee for a finite-precision SDP/eigensolve. “Polynomial-time” is justified only in the conventional finite-dimensional convex-optimization model with suitable finite-precision inputs, not as an end-to-end complexity theorem with explicit numerical constants.

## Exact instrument reduction

Draw G in C^(m x d) with iid standard complex Gaussian entries (or use the bounded finite alphabet above). For m >= C_G d, standard rectangular sub-Gaussian singular-value concentration gives the good event

\[
\mathcal G=\{m/4\le\lambda_{\min}(G^*G)\le\lambda_{\max}(G^*G)\le4m\}
\]

with probability at least 1-2 exp(-c_G m), after choosing a sufficiently large constant C_G. Let a=1/(4m), R=(I-aG^*G)^(1/2), and

\[
B=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt a\,G\\R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb C^{(m+d)\times d}.
\]

On G, R is defined and B*B=aG*G+R*R=I. The m+d rows of B define a complete rank-at-most-one POVM. For a unit input x, the first-block probability is

\[
q_G(x)=a\|Gx\|^2\in[1/16,1].
\]

This keeps the selected block iid Gaussian; it does not replace it by the correlated polar-whitened block G(G*G)^(-1/2). With uniform known detector efficiency eta, multiply all click effects by eta and add loss effect (1-eta)I.

For a Poissonized source with mean N launched copies, Poisson thinning makes detected port counts independent Poisson variables. In the first block,

\[
\lambda_j=N\eta a|g_j^*x|^2=|g_j^*w|^2,\qquad
w=\sqrt{N\eta a}\,x.
\]

This matches HLN exactly: w is deterministic given N,eta,a,x and independent of random G. Residual and loss channels need not be used by CVX-LS; their probability mass is still included in the instrument and launched-photon cost.

For exactly fixed N, the full channel vector, including the latent loss/no-click category, is multinomial. The identity with all-category independent-Poisson counts conditioned on total T=N is exact:

\[
\Pr(Y=y\mid T=N)=\frac{N!}{\prod_i y_i!}\prod_i q_i(x)^{y_i},\qquad
\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}=e^{-N}N^N/N!\asymp(2\pi N)^{-1/2}.
\]

For a failure event F determined by Gaussian-block counts, the fixed-N failure contribution on good designs satisfies

\[
\Pr_N(F,\mathcal G)
=\frac{\Pr_{\rm Pois}(F,T=N,\mathcal G)}{\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}}
\le C\sqrt N\,\Pr_{\rm Pois}(F).
\]

Adding fallback failure on the bad-frame event gives 2 exp(-c_G m)+C sqrt(N) exp(-cd). This remains exponentially small in d for polynomial N, including N=O(d/epsilon^2) when epsilon inverse-polynomial. This is a distribution identity, not a protocol that postselects on an unobserved Poisson total. It proves the separate fixed-N protocol. The latent no-click count is inferable from known fixed N and all detected counts; under a Poissonized source with unknown realized launch count it is generally not directly observed and is unnecessary for the Poissonized estimator.

## Normalization and photon requirement

Let s=||w||=sqrt(N eta/(4m)). The high-energy HLN branch applies if s>=1/K, namely N eta>=4m/K^2. Then

\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_*,sx)\le C_3K\sqrt{d/m}=:\Delta.
\]

If Delta<s, the normalized estimate is nonzero. Aligning phase to realize Delta and using

\[
\left\|\frac z{\|z\|}-e^{i\theta}x\right\|
\le\left|1-\frac{\|z\|}{s}\right|
+\frac{\|z-se^{i\theta}x\|}{s}
\le\frac{2\Delta}{s}
\]

gives

\[
d_\phi(\widehat x,x)\le4C_3K\sqrt{\frac d{N\eta}}.
\]

Thus for 0<epsilon<=1 it suffices that

\[
N\eta\ge\max\left\{\frac{4m}{K^2},
\frac{16C_3^2K^2d}{\epsilon^2}\right\}.
\]

The factor 4 is safe and not optimized. For m=Cd, the rate is N eta=O(d/epsilon^2). At least N eta/16 expected detected photons fall in the useful block; residuals, missed detections, and launched photons are not free. The estimate is in phase-quotient Euclidean distance. For pure-state density matrices, || |x><x|-|y><y| ||_1=2 sqrt(1-|<x,y>|^2)<=2 d_phi(x,y), so the order also implies trace-norm tomography.

## Prior-art attack and novelty boundary

Lowe and Nayak, [Lower bounds for learning quantum states with single-copy measurements](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.14438v3), Theorem 4.11, prove that nonadaptive single-copy tomography of rank-r states in trace distance epsilon requires Omega(r^2 d/epsilon^2) copies for d>=3, r<=d/3, and epsilon<1/8. Theorem 4.12 gives Omega(r^2 d^2/(ell epsilon^2)) if each measurement has at most ell outcomes. For pure states r=1. Thus an O(d)-outcome POVM cannot beat O(d/epsilon^2) order here, and Cycle 3's rate is the known optimal single-copy rate. The same paper calls the rank-dependent lower bound optimal relative to Kueng–Rauhut–Terstiege and Guta–Kahn–Kueng–Tropp upper bounds; those protocols include nonadaptive random-basis measurements or a continuous uniform POVM and need not be one fixed finite O(d)-outcome device.

As a separate identifiability prior, Edidin, Gonzalez, and Tamo, [arXiv:2511.09505](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09505), review that generic complex frames with at least 4d-4 vectors permit phase retrieval and state that canonical whitening preserves pure-state informational completeness. This is noiseless injectivity, not a statistical stability or polynomial Poisson-estimator theorem. It does show that O(d)-outcome pure-state identification is already standard phase-retrieval territory.

The novelty boundary is narrow: Cycle 3 appears to give an explicit randomized single fixed finite POVM with O(d) output channels and a polynomial convex estimator under Poisson counts, plus a fixed-count tail transfer. The photon order is known-optimal; rank-one POVMs, Naimark completion, Gaussian phase retrieval, and convex lifting are prior art. Without a focused search for fixed finite POVMs with the same uniform noisy risk and computational guarantee, priority remains UNKNOWN. Hidden constants mean the construction is not shown outcome-minimal.

## Physical implementation and conditional robustness

The isometry B:C^d -> C^M, M=m+d, can be extended to U in U(M) by completing columns. A single photon enters the first d modes and the remaining m modes are vacuum; after U, number-resolving measurement of each output mode realizes the POVM. Reck et al.'s optical decomposition [Phys. Rev. Lett. 73, 58 (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.58) implements any finite M x M unitary with O(M^2) two-mode beam-splitter transformations and phase controls. Thus a generic mesh has O(d^2) components and O(d^2) calibrated complex degrees of freedom. This is polynomial in Hilbert-space dimension d but exponential in qubit count log_2 d; no efficient-in-qubits implementation is shown.

A direct conditional robustness bound is available. Suppose ideal and realized mode unitaries obey ||U_tilde-U||_op<=delta. For every unit input x, output vectors differ by at most delta. Trace distance of the output states, and therefore total variation distance of the measured labels, is at most delta. For N independent photon records, TV is at most N delta by the product/coupling inequality; histogram postprocessing cannot increase it. Hence ideal success probability 1-beta becomes at least 1-beta-N delta uniformly over x. For Poissonized flux of mean N, averaging the T delta bound over T~Pois(N) gives the same N delta. For normalized outputs with squared phase loss at most 4, expected squared phase risk changes by at most 4N delta.

If the M-mode optical mesh has L_mesh=O(M^2) ideal two-mode gates and every realized gate is within delta_g in operator norm, telescoping gives delta<=L_mesh delta_g. To limit success-probability loss to alpha, it is sufficient that

\[
\delta_g\le\frac{\alpha}{N L_{\rm mesh}}.
\]

If each gate's control-to-unitary map is Lipschitz and b-bit quantization guarantees delta_g<=C 2^(-b), then b>=log_2(C N L_mesh/alpha). With M=O(d) and N=O(d/epsilon^2), this is O(log(d^3/(alpha epsilon^2))) bits per control up to hidden constants. This is a formal tolerance target, not an experimentally validated calibration protocol. It does not cover optical loss, mode-dependent efficiencies, dark counts, saturation, input preparation error, drift, or approximate-SDP/eigensolver error.

Additional interface costs:
- CVX-LS needs the calibrated first-block matrix G. The theorem hides m/d; the design stores O(d^2) coefficients.
- If residual ports are aggregated, photon counts must be preserved (PNR or a sum of PNR outcomes). A threshold detector can saturate for a Poissonized multi-photon input and change the count law.
- A Poisson source's realized undetected-photon total is latent unless input number is heralded. Poissonized estimation needs only observed first-block counts and known mean dose. Fixed-N transfer concerns the separate known-N input experiment.
- Nonuniform known channel efficiencies yield different row scales and no longer meet HLN's iid-identical assumption as written. Unknown calibration/background also needs a new theorem.
- Finite-alphabet entries avoid arbitrary Gaussian coefficient precision; R and U still require finite-precision synthesis. The robustness result above covers coherent unitary error only.

## Counterexamples and failure boundaries

1. If one conditions on only the clicks entering the Gaussian block, then for K_G=k the row labels have probabilities
   \[
   \Pr(J=j\mid J\text{ in block},x)=|g_j^*x|^2/\|Gx\|^2,
   \]
   not the independent Poisson means N eta a |g_j^*x|^2. Since ||Gx||^2 varies with x, discarding the flux scale changes the model.
2. With mode-dependent efficiency eta_j, the means are N a eta_j |g_j^*x|^2; the row law becomes heteroscedastic and HLN does not apply verbatim.
3. If lambda_max(G*G)>4m, I-G*G/(4m) may fail to be PSD. Reject/redraw or use a valid fallback and charge the bad-frame event.
4. This is a Poisson/multinomial risk result. It does not establish beta_2=2 under raw squared-intensity l2 conditioning, nor does that raw-intensity constant yield a photon lower bound.
