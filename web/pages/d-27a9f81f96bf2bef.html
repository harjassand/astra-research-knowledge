Kinetic Fokker–Planck: the weak-confinement gap closes
=====================================================

Status: PROVED for the stated harmonic family; REFUTED for a positive decay rate uniform as the confinement parameter tends to zero. No novelty claim is made. This is an exact calculation and a parameter comparison with established hypocoercivity theorems.

Question and quantifiers
------------------------

Consider the one-dimensional kinetic Fokker–Planck equation on (x,v) in R^2,

  ∂_t f + v ∂_x f − κ x ∂_v f = ∂_v(vf) + ∂_v^2 f,
  κ > 0,  t ≥ 0,

with unit friction and unit temperature. Each fixed κ gives a confining potential V_κ(x)=κx^2/2. The claim being tested is whether there are constants C<∞ and λ>0, independent of 0<κ≤κ_0, such that either (i) every mean-zero g in the equilibrium-weighted L^2 space satisfies ||P_t^κ g||_2 ≤ C e^(−λt)||g||_2, or (ii) every probability density f_0 of finite relative entropy satisfies H(f_t|F_κ) ≤ C e^(−λt) H(f_0|F_κ), for every t≥0. Both uniform claims are false. For each fixed κ, exponential convergence holds and its sharp L^2 exponent is α_κ below. The sharp relative-entropy exponent is 2α_κ.

Generator, equilibrium, and exact gap
--------------------------------------

The equation in divergence form is

  ∂_t f = G_κ^* f := −∂_x(vf) + ∂_v((κx+v)f) + ∂_v^2 f.

It is the forward equation of

  dX_t = V_t dt,
  dV_t = (−κX_t−V_t)dt + √2 dB_t.

The backward Markov generator, initially on C_c^∞(R^2) and closed in the invariant L^2 space, is

  A_κ φ = v∂_x φ − (κx+v)∂_v φ + ∂_v^2 φ.

Writing z=(x,v)^T, its drift matrix and diffusion matrix are

  M_κ = [[0,1], [−κ,−1]],       D = [[0,0],[0,1]],

where the second-order term is tr(D∇^2). The covariance Σ_κ=diag(κ^(−1),1) solves M_κΣ_κ+Σ_κM_κ^T+2D=0. Hence the normalized invariant density is

  F_κ(x,v) = √κ/(2π) exp(−(κx^2+v^2)/2),

and μ_κ(dxdv)=F_κ(x,v)dxdv. The space variance is κ^(−1), so its standard deviation is κ^(−1/2). The position Gaussian has the exact Poincaré inequality

  ∫|u−∫u dρ_κ|^2 dρ_κ ≤ κ^(−1)∫|u'|^2 dρ_κ,

where ρ_κ=N(0,κ^(−1)); thus its spectral-gap constant is κ.

Define the L^2 spectral gap by

  gap(A_κ) = inf{−Re z : z ∈ σ(A_κ|_{L^2_0(μ_κ)})},

where L^2_0(μ_κ) consists of functions with μ_κ-mean zero. For 0<κ<1/4 set

  α_κ = (1−√(1−4κ))/2,      β_κ = (1+√(1−4κ))/2 = 1−α_κ.

The two eigenvalues of M_κ are −α_κ and −β_κ. The exact L^2 spectrum is

  σ(A_κ|_{L^2_0}) = {−jα_κ−kβ_κ : j,k∈N_0, j+k≥1},

so gap(A_κ)=α_κ. The spectral gap of the Lebesgue-forward generator acting on relative densities has the same real-part gap, since it is the invariant-measure adjoint.

Proof of the spectrum and sharp L^2 exponent
--------------------------------------------

Put S_κ=diag(√κ,1) and y=S_κz. This is an isometry from L^2(μ_κ) to L^2 of the standard two-dimensional Gaussian. In y-coordinates the drift is

  B_κ=S_κM_κS_κ^(−1)=[[0,√κ],[−√κ,−1]],

and B_κ+B_κ^T+2D=0, so e^(tB_κ) is a contraction. The Gaussian Wiener-chaos decomposition is an orthogonal decomposition into invariant subspaces H_n, n≥0. On H_n the OU semigroup is the n-th symmetric tensor power of its action on H_1, whose coefficient matrix is e^(tB_κ^T). When κ<1/4, B_κ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues −α_κ,−β_κ. Therefore the generator on H_n has eigenvalues −jα_κ−kβ_κ for j+k=n. Constants form H_0; all remaining real parts are at most −α_κ, and H_1 contains an eigenvector at −α_κ. This proves the exact spectrum and gap.

It also gives the sharp semigroup exponent. Diagonalization gives ||e^(tB_κ)|| ≤ K_κ e^(−α_κt) for some finite K_κ. Because e^(tB_κ) is a contraction, its n-th symmetric tensor power has norm ||e^(tB_κ)||^n ≤ ||e^(tB_κ)|| for n≥1. Thus ||P_t^κ||_{L^2_0→L^2_0}=||e^(tB_κ^T)||≤K_κe^(−α_κt). The slow eigenfunction gives the reverse exponential obstruction, so no exponent greater than α_κ is possible.

Exact entropy trajectory and counterexample
-------------------------------------------

For a probability density f, let H(f|F_κ)=∫f log(f/F_κ) dx dv. The slow eigenvector of M_κ is w_κ=(1,−α_κ)^T. Start from the Gaussian with covariance Σ_κ and mean m_0=s w_κ. Its covariance stays Σ_κ and its mean is m_t=e^(−α_κt)m_0. The Gaussian relative-entropy formula therefore gives, exactly,

  H(f_t|F_κ) = (1/2)m_t^TΣ_κ^(−1)m_t
              = e^(−2α_κt) H(f_0|F_κ).

Taking s=√(2/(κ+α_κ^2)) makes H(f_0|F_κ)=1, so this is also a normalized-entropy falsifier. Since

  α_κ = 2κ/(1+√(1−4κ)) = κ+κ^2+2κ^3+5κ^4+O(κ^5),

we have α_κ→0. If a common C<∞ and λ>0 worked for all 0<κ≤κ_0, choose κ with 2α_κ<λ and apply the asserted bound to this f_0. It would require exp((λ−2α_κ)t)≤C for every t, which fails as t→∞. The same argument on the slow L^2 eigenfunction rules out a common positive L^2 rate. This falsifies the uniform claim even though every member κ>0 is confining and hypoelliptic.

The scale costs are exact: the spatial variance costs κ^(−1), the macroscopic Poincaré gap is κ, the L^2 e-folding time is α_κ^(−1)~κ^(−1), and the relative-entropy e-folding time is (2α_κ)^(−1)~(2κ)^(−1). At κ=0 the invariant density ceases to be normalizable, so the endpoint is excluded rather than a stationary member of the family.

Primary-source comparison at the same parameters
------------------------------------------------

The sharp matching theorem is Arnold and Erb, “Sharp entropy decay for hypocoercive and non-symmetric Fokker–Planck equations with linear drift,” arXiv:1409.5425v2 (version dated 24 October 2014; first submitted 18 September 2014), https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5425 and full text https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5425. Their kinetic equation (1.1) has friction ν and diffusion σ. Setting ν=σ=1 and V(x)=κx^2/2 gives exactly the equation above and exactly F_κ. In their matrix form (1.3), D=diag(0,1), C=[[0,−1],[κ,1]], and condition (A) holds: C is positively stable for κ>0, and C^T sends ker(D)=span(e_x) outside ker(D). The eigenvalues of C are α_κ and β_κ.

Their Theorem 4.9 states that for every admissible relative entropy generated by ψ, every normalized nonnegative initial density with finite entropy, and every t≥0, the entropy is at most c_κ exp(−2μt) times its initial value when the eigenvalues at the slow real part are nondefective. Here μ=min Re σ(C)=α_κ, and the eigenvalues are distinct for 0<κ<1/4. Thus the theorem gives the same exponent 2α_κ, not a parameter-uniform positive exponent. Their Theorem 6.1 proves sharpness for the logarithmic relative entropy; the shifted Gaussian above is its explicit slow-mode realization. Their Theorem 4.6 gives the same rate for initially ψ-compatible data with an explicit modified-entropy coefficient. Theorem 4.9 extends the claim to every finite-entropy initial datum by regularization.

The prefactor also records a scale cost for the particular modified-entropy bridge in that paper. In its kinetic section, the paper chooses, when 4κ<1,

  P_κ=[[2,1],[1,1−2κ]].

The largest λ_P satisfying Σ_κ^(−1)≥λ_P P_κ^(−1) is

  λ_P=(1−√(1−4κ+16κ^2))/2 = κ+O(κ^2) as κ↓0.

Consequently the convex-Sobolev step e_ψ≤S_ψ/(2λ_P) in Proposition 4.4 has a coefficient of order κ^(−1). This is the cost of that certificate, not a loss in the sharp decay exponent. The full finite-entropy theorem has a parameter-dependent constant c_κ; the exact slow Gaussian shows why its exponent cannot be made uniform. For comparison, the Dolbeault–Mouhot–Schmeiser micro–macro framework uses a macroscopic Poincaré constant Λ=κ here, while the velocity collision gap is λ_m=1. Their abstract Theorem 2 computes a positive rate from λ_m, Λ, and the auxiliary-operator bound, but its assumptions do not give a rate uniform as Λ↓0. Primary source: “Hypocoercivity for linear kinetic equations conserving mass,” arXiv:1005.1495 (10 May 2010), https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1495; published in Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 367 (2015), 3807–3828, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2015-06012-7. Its Fokker–Planck section identifies microscopic coercivity with the unit Gaussian velocity Poincaré inequality; the position marginal above supplies the vanishing macroscopic constant.

Assumptions restoring uniformity
--------------------------------

For a rigorous common L^2 estimate, restrict to any compact interval 0<κ_*≤κ≤κ^*<1/4. The eigenvalues remain separated and the normalized eigenvectors of B_κ can be chosen as (√κ,−α_κ) and (√κ,−β_κ). Their matrix and inverse are uniformly bounded on this interval. Therefore a finite K(κ_*,κ^*) exists with

  ||P_t^κ g||_{L^2(μ_κ)} ≤ K(κ_*,κ^*) e^(−α_(κ_*)t)||g||_{L^2(μ_κ)},

for every κ in the interval, every mean-zero g, and every t≥0. This is the quantitative version of requiring a uniform confining/Poincaré gap. The fixed-friction assumption is also meaningful: if friction γ is allowed to grow, the exact slow rate becomes (γ−√(γ^2−4κ))/2~κ/γ in the overdamped regime, so an upper bound on γ is needed for a uniform rate when κ is fixed. If γ is allowed to vanish, velocity dissipation itself degenerates. A compact range of positive friction and a uniform lower curvature bound are natural sufficient controls; for a uniform prefactor at an exact endpoint rate, also avoid defective drift matrices (here κ=1/4).

On this compact interval, the exact shifted-Gaussian entropy formula itself gives the common bound H(f_t|F_κ)≤exp(−2α_(κ_*)t)H(f_0|F_κ) for that slow-mode Gaussian subclass. Arnold–Erb gives the exact rate for every finite-entropy datum at each fixed κ. Their displayed entropy bridge has λ_P→0 as κ↓0; this note does not claim a common finite-entropy prefactor merely from the pointwise theorem. A full all-data entropy uniformization would require a uniform bound through their regularization step. The unbounded family κ↓0 violates precisely the lower macro-gap condition.

Falsifier and scope
-------------------

Falsifier: the exact family f_0^κ=N(s_κ(1,−α_κ),Σ_κ), s_κ=√(2/(κ+α_κ^2)), has unit initial entropy and H(f_t^κ|F_κ)=exp(−2α_κt). For any proposed uniform λ>0, choose κ with 2α_κ<λ and let t→∞. This rejects the claim without numerics, an asymptotic approximation, or an assumption about generic initial data.

Scope limits: the calculation proves the failure for this concrete family and says nothing by itself about every weakly confining nonquadratic potential, a bounded spatial domain, or parameter families with a uniform macro-Poincaré inequality. Source theorems establish pointwise-in-parameter convergence; uniformity requires tracking the parameter dependence of their constants.
