INDEPENDENT CRITICAL CRN AUDIT
Frozen independent derivation, 2026-10-08. No other worker argument or repository candidate proof was read before this report was written. Exact rates, including the factor b(b-1), are essential.

RESULT
Let lambda,kappa>0 and let (A_t,B_t) have transitions
 (a,b)->(a,b+1) at rate lambda,
 (a,b)->(a+1,b-1) at rate lambda*b,
 (a,b)->(a-1,b-2) at rate kappa*a*b*(b-1).
The chain on N_0^2 is nonexplosive and irreducible. Put r=lambda/kappa.

1. If 0<r<=1/2 (equivalently kappa>=2 lambda), it is null recurrent.
2. If r>1/2 (equivalently kappa<2 lambda), it is transient.
3. It is never positive recurrent on this equality of birth and conversion coefficients. Therefore there is no stationary probability distribution and no stationary probability tail to report. A sigma-finite invariant measure in the null recurrent cases is not a stationary probability law.

The embedded full jump chain and the return chain on b=0 have the same null-recurrent/transient classification here. The continuous-time conclusion is proved below using the actual clock; it is not inferred from recurrence of an arbitrary time change.

Status: internally reconstructed proof. No external validation or novelty/priority claim. A finite exact recursion is an independent diagnostic of the calculated coefficients, not the proof.

PROOF

A. Nonexplosion and irreducibility.
Realize immigration by a rate-lambda Poisson clock. Let I(t),C(t),D(t) be the numbers of immigration, conversion, and annihilation jumps up to t. Conservation of B gives
 B_t=B_0+I(t)-C(t)-2D(t)>=0,
so C(t)+D(t)<=B_0+I(t). Consequently total jumps up to t are at most B_0+2I(t), which is finite almost surely on every finite interval. This establishes nonexplosion without any bound on the potentially large annihilation rate.

From (a,b), perform b conversions to reach (a+b,0), then repeatedly perform two immigrations followed by one annihilation, until (0,0). Every specified finite path has positive probability. From (0,0), an immigration followed by a conversion adds one A and returns to b=0; repeat a times, then perform b immigrations. Thus every state communicates with every other state.

B. The exact return chain and uniform moment bounds.
At each arrival at b=0, wait for the next immigration and then stop at the next return to b=0. Let Y_k be A at successive such arrivals. Its transition from n is exactly the distribution of A at the first hit of b=0 starting from (n,1). In particular Y is an irreducible chain on N_0: from n>=1 there are positive-probability direct transitions to n-1 and n+1, and from 0 there is a direct transition to 1.

The B process is dominated by an immigration-death process Z with immigration rate lambda and death rate lambda*z, under the standard common-birth/common-death coupling; annihilations only decrease B further. Starting B=Z=j, the B excursion ends no later than Z first hits 0. This gives bounds uniform in initial A for each fixed j.

For completeness, the number of jumps of Z before hitting 0 has an exponential moment when it starts at any fixed j. Its embedded chain has up probability 1/(z+1) and down probability z/(z+1). At z>=2 these are bounded by 1/3 and bounded below by 2/3, respectively. The passage from 2 to 1 is dominated by the passage of a nearest-neighbor walk with probabilities 1/3 up and 2/3 down; that passage has an exponential moment (for small theta>0, (e^theta+2e^-theta)/3<1). At each visit to 1, probability 1/2 of the next jump is absorption at 0; the number of unsuccessful trials is geometric. For a sufficiently small exponential parameter, combining these passages proves the assertion. For j>2, finitely many corresponding passages give the same assertion.

The Z holding rates before absorption are lambda*(z+1)>=2lambda. Conditional on the jump path, its holding times can be dominated by independent Exp(2lambda) variables. Its absorption time therefore has a positive exponential moment as well.

The number of B-excursion immigrations is bounded by the number of Z immigrations before its absorption. The number of non-immigration jumps is at most j plus the number of immigrations. The A increment in that excursion has absolute value at most j plus the number of immigrations. It follows that, from (n,j), excursion duration, jump count, and absolute A increment have exponential moments bounded uniformly in n for every fixed j. In particular, for the return chain, there are constants eta>0 and K<infinity with
 sup_n E_n exp(eta*|Y_1-n|)<=K.
The full cycle adds an Exp(lambda) hold at b=0, so its mean is bounded uniformly above, and is at least 1/lambda.

C. Return-chain drift and variance.
An exceptional event means an immigration or conversion when b>=2, as opposed to the usual fast annihilation. Enumerate paths with at most one exceptional event. Starting at (n,1), the next event is conversion or immigration, each with probability 1/2. Conversion terminates with increment +1. Immigration leads to (n,2), where the three probabilities are proportional to
 lambda, 2lambda, 2kappa*n.
Thus an exceptional event at this first b=2 visit has probability O(1/n).

If this exceptional event is conversion, the state is (n+1,1). Its two ordinary outcomes give increments +2 or 0. If it is immigration, the state is (n,3), whose ordinary annihilation gives (n-1,1); the two subsequent ordinary outcomes give increments 0 or -2.

Before a second exceptional event occurs, only a bounded number of states with b<=3 and A within a bounded distance of n are visited. At those states the exceptional probability is O(1/n). The probability of a second exceptional event is therefore O(1/n^2). Just after that second event, b<=4 and A is still within a bounded distance of n. Part B bounds all subsequent absolute-increment moments uniformly. Consequently, for every fixed p, the contribution of all paths with at least two exceptional events to E|Y_1-n|^p is O(1/n^2). This justifies the weighted-moment remainder, not merely a remainder in total probability.

The paths with at most one exceptional event give the following probabilities to first order, with moment-weighted error O(n^-2):
 increment +1: 1/2+O(n^-2);
 increment -1: 1/2-3r/(4n)+O(n^-2);
 increment +2: r/(4n)+O(n^-2);
 increment  0: 3r/(8n)+O(n^-2);
 increment -2: r/(8n)+O(n^-2).
The probability for increment +1 is exactly 1/2 only for the direct path; other paths contributing +1 are contained in the O(n^-2) remainder. The table's entries mean the listed ordinary paths, with all other paths assigned to that remainder.

Hence, with m_j(n)=E_n[(Y_1-n)^j],
 m_1(n)=r/n+O(n^-2),
 m_2(n)=1+3r/(4n)+O(n^-2).
Uniform exponential moments hold by Part B. The coefficient r/n is positive, rather than a negative restoring drift. Rare conversions at b=2 are its source.

D. A useful exact positive-drift identity.
Set F(a,b)=a-floor(b/2). Annihilation leaves F unchanged. Direct calculation gives
 LF(a,b)=0                    for b=0,1;
 LF(a,b)=2lambda*b            for even b>=2;
 LF(a,b)=lambda*(b-1)         for odd b>=3.
All these values are nonnegative, and the b=2 value is strictly positive.

Apply the stopped Dynkin identity to the b excursion. Part B justifies integrability and passage to the stopping time, since F increments and the integral of LF are controlled by excursion jump count, B, and duration, all with finite moments. F equals A at both endpoints of the full cycle. Therefore
 m_1(n)=E_(n,1) integral_0^tau LF(A_t,B_t) dt >0
for every n>=0. Strictness follows from the positive-probability initial immigration from b=1 to b=2 and the positive holding time there. Its leading contribution is (1/2)*4lambda/(2kappa*n)=r/n, consistent with Part C.

E. Recurrence for r<1/2 and at r=1/2; transience for r>1/2.
For a smooth regularization of f(n)=log n, Taylor expansion with Part B gives
 E_n[f(Y_1)-f(n)]=(r-1/2)/n^2+O(n^-3).
It is negative for all sufficiently large n if r<1/2. Since f is nonnegative and tends to infinity, the stopped supermartingale criterion yields recurrence.

At the exact threshold r=1/2, use a smooth regularization of f(n)=log log n. Its drift is
 E_n[f(Y_1)-f(n)]
 = -1/(2n^2 log^2 n)+O(1/(n^3 log n)),
which is negative for all sufficiently large n. This proves recurrence at the threshold as well. The second term in f'' is necessary here; the leading log-n cancellation alone does not classify the threshold.

For r>1/2, choose 0<alpha<2r-1 and use f(n)=(n+d)^(-alpha), with d>0. Then
 E_n[f(Y_1)-f(n)]
 =alpha*((alpha+1)/2-r)*n^(-alpha-2)+O(n^(-alpha-3))<0
for sufficiently large n. The function is positive and decreases to 0. Optional stopping at the first hit of [0,N], for sufficiently large N, gives
 P_n(hit [0,N])<=f(n)/f(N)<1
for n>N. Irreducibility then gives transience.

The usual Lyapunov criteria just used can be seen directly. For increasing unbounded f with nonpositive drift outside [0,N], stopping on [0,N] or [H,infinity) bounds the probability of first reaching the upper set by f(n)/f(H), which tends to 0 as H grows. Irreducibility ensures that a trajectory avoiding the lower set cannot remain in a bounded set forever. Thus it hits [0,N] almost surely. For positive decreasing f tending to 0, stopping at the lower set yields the displayed strict upper bound on its hit probability. Uniform exponential increments make the Taylor estimates valid: the region |Y_1-n|>n/2 contributes an exponentially small remainder, and the ordinary third-order remainder has the displayed order.

F. No positive recurrence of the return chain: an elementary stationarity argument.
Suppose pi were a stationary probability distribution for Y. Under stationarity, X and X'=Y_1 have identical marginal distributions. Uniform exponential increments imply E|X'-X|<infinity. For each K,
 E[min(X',K)-min(X,K)]=0.
The absolute difference is at most |X'-X|. Dominated convergence as K tends to infinity gives E(X'-X)=0. But by Part D,
 E(X'-X)=sum_n pi(n)*m_1(n)>0,
a contradiction. Thus Y has no stationary probability, so the recurrent cases in Part E are null recurrent.

This argument does not assume that the stationary mean of X is finite; truncation is what avoids that potentially invalid assumption. It also shows why a formal heavy-tail stationary ansatz on this critical line cannot be normalized.

G. Transfer to the actual CTMC clock and to the full jump chain.
From every state, B hits 0 almost surely, by the comparison in Part B. Each excursion away from b=0 ends almost surely. Thus the event of returning to a state (n,0) is precisely the event that Y returns to n. The recurrent/transient classification of the full process therefore agrees with that of Y.

In a recurrent case, let N be Y's first return index to n after leaving its current b=0 visit. Since Y is null recurrent, E_n N=infinity. Every cycle contains a hold H_k at b=0 with mean 1/lambda. These holds can be chosen independent of the transition path of Y; their duration has no effect on the state after departure, and the immigration rate is constant. Consequently, by Tonelli,
 E_(n,0)[calendar return time] >= E[sum_(k<N) H_k]
 = (1/lambda)*E_n N=infinity.
The CTMC is therefore null recurrent, even though annihilation can be arbitrarily fast at some states. In a transient case it is transient by the pathwise hit-event correspondence.

Every cycle has at least two full jumps: the departure immigration and an eventual return event. Hence the embedded full jump chain also has infinite expected return jump count in the recurrent cases, and is null recurrent. This distinguishes and then verifies all three classifications instead of conflating them.

An irreducible, nonexplosive countable-state CTMC has a stationary probability distribution exactly when it is positive recurrent. The classifications above exclude it for all lambda,kappa>0 on the equality line. Therefore a stationary probability tail does not exist.

The present no-stationary-probability argument in F is more direct for this chain and does not require invoking a nonpositivity theorem.
