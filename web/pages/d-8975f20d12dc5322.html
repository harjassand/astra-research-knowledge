AN EXPLICIT 2D QUARTIC-DISPERSION POSITIVE-REPULSION TRACE COUNTEREXAMPLE
8 October 2026. Internally proved candidate. No generic-physics novelty claim.

1. THE FULL GIBBS STATES AND THE PRECISE FAILURE

On Lambda_L={1,...,L-1}^2, L>=4, let h_D have diagonal 8 and nearest-
neighbor entries -2, the J=1 quadratic Dirichlet matrix in PROOF.txt.
Define the quartic kinetic matrix and its entire boson Fock lift by

 h_Q=h_D^2/128,  K_Q=dGamma(h_Q),  beta=L^4,  lambda=0.

All coefficients are fixed rational numbers. Let
 W=sum_{x in Lambda} n_x(n_x-1)/2,
the standard onsite repulsion of FIXED strength 1. It is a nonnegative
closed multiplication form, and its dense closed sum with K_Q is well
defined. It annihilates every no-double configuration subspace, and hence
every fixed-range no-close subspace P_R from PROOF.txt. It conserves number.
Let
 rho0=exp(-beta K_Q)/Z0,
 rho1=exp(-beta(K_Q+W))/Z1.
Both are full grand-canonical Fock Gibbs states, with vacuum and every
particle-number sector included. They live on exactly the same Hilbert
space. They have finite partitions and uniform exponential number moments.

For comparison also let P0 denote the no-double-occupation projection
on the full Fock space, and let rho_hc be the Gibbs state of P0 K_Q P0
on ran P0, embedded by zero into that same full Fock space. This physical
hard-core allowed space is finite dimensional (dimension 2^S), S=(L-1)^2.

The following explicit floor holds:

 ||rho1-rho0||_1 >= 2^{-19} exp(-27)>0,     L>=128,     (Q.1)
 ||rho_hc-rho0||_1 >= 2^{-19} exp(-27)>0,   L>=4.       (Q.2)

Here the critical inverse-time parameter is the fixed finite value tau=1
for the quartic scale beta=tau L^4. These are finite-temperature Gibbs
claims on the full Fock space, not a substitution by ground states or
by a fixed-number model. The constants are deliberately conservative.

This is NOT a counterexample to PROOF.txt: that theorem fixes QUADRATIC
dispersion and beta=tau L^2. It refutes an extension based only on being
two dimensional, having rare free collisions and finite free number
moments, after changing the dispersion and its critical time scale.
The nonstoquastic h_Q invalidates the earlier positivity argument, but
(Q.1)-(Q.2) establish actual state separation, not just a failure of a
particular proof method.

2. UNIFORM FREE PARTITION AND MOMENT BOUNDS

Let phi_k be the same real orthonormal product-sine modes as PROOF.txt,
k=(k1,k2), 1<=k_a<=L-1. Let omega_k=4 sum_a(1-cos(pi k_a/L)) be the
eigenvalue of h_D and put
 a_k=L^4 omega_k^2/128.
The elementary sine estimates give

 (1/2)|k|^4<=a_k<=(pi^4/32)|k|^4.                      (Q.3)

In particular a_k>=2 and the single-mode free geometric distributions
are stable. All bounds below are uniform in L; positive-mode sums can
be enlarged to k in N^2.

Define X=sum_k exp(-a_k/4). By (Q.3),
 X<=sum_{k in N^2}exp(-|k|^4/8)<1.
Here is an explicit elementary bound, avoiding a numerical test. Group
k by r=max(k1,k2). The r=1 term is exp(-1/2)<2/3. For r=2 there are
three modes, all with |k|^2>=5, giving at most 3exp(-25/8)<3/16.
For r>=3 the number of modes is 2r-1<=2r and r^4/8>=2r, so the tail is
at most sum_{r>=3}2r exp(-2r)<sum_{r>=3}2r/7^r=19/882<1/8.
Thus X<2/3+3/16+1/8=47/48<1. The inequalities follow, for example,
from exp(1)>8/3 and the first few positive terms in the exponential series.

The symmetric two-particle free partition at quarter inverse time is
at most the full labeled tensor-product partition:

 Z_{0,2}(1/4)=Tr_{M=2} exp(-(beta/4)K_Q)<=X^2<1.        (Q.4)

For the full Fock state,
 log Z0<=sum_k (exp(a_k)-1)^{-1}
       <=2 sum_k exp(-a_k)<2X<2,
so 1<=Z0<exp(2). Likewise the theta=1 fugacity tilt is stable, since
a_k-1>=a_k/2>=1. Its log partition is bounded by
 2sum_k exp(-a_k/2)<2X<2.
Consequently E0 exp(M)<=exp(2), and E0 M^r<=r! exp(2) for every fixed
moment order r. Since W is nonnegative and commutes with M, block minmax
gives the same exponential-number upper bound for rho1, with Z1>=1.
The hard-core compression has that bound as well. Finite half-temperature
partitions and thermal energies follow by the same minmax comparison.
The separation below is not caused by a divergent boson population.

3. A LEGAL TWO-PARTICLE TRIAL WITH EXACT COINCIDENCE ZERO

Write g=(1,1), a=(2,1), b=(3,1). These modes are present for L>=4.
In the physical normalized symmetric two-particle mode basis define

 psi=sqrt(2/5)|g,g> -sqrt(2/5)|a,a> +sqrt(1/5)|g,b>.

This is a normalized boson vector. In labeled coordinates X,Y it is
proportional to

 [cos(pi X1/L)-cos(pi Y1/L)]^2 phi_g(X)phi_g(Y).          (Q.5)

Indeed cos(theta)sin(theta)=sin(2theta)/2 and
cos(theta)^2 sin(theta)=[sin(theta)+sin(3theta)]/4. Expanding the right
of (Q.5) gives (1/2)phi_g tensor phi_g -(1/2)phi_a tensor phi_a
+(1/4)(phi_b tensor phi_g+phi_g tensor phi_b), whose squared norm is
5/8. Normalizing gives exactly the stated coefficients. It vanishes at
X=Y (in fact whenever X1=Y1), so Wpsi=0 and P0psi=psi.

Its dimensionless kinetic energy is

 <psi,beta K_Q psi>=a_g+(4/5)a_a+(1/5)a_b
 <=(pi^4/8)+(4/5)(25pi^4/32)+(1/5)(100pi^4/32)
 =(11/8)pi^4<138,                                      (Q.6)

using pi^4<100. The repulsion energy is zero. Thus the lowest dimensionless
two-particle eigenvalue is at most C=138 both for finite strength 1 and
for the hard compression. Their canonical two-particle partitions are
therefore at least exp(-C), with no ground-state approximation.

4. A THERMAL TWO-PARTICLE ENERGY BOUND, DERIVED RATHER THAN ASSUMED

Let B be either beta(K_Q+W) restricted to M=2 or the dimensionless
hard-core compression beta P0 K_Q P0 on that sector. Let sigma_B be its
canonical Gibbs state at inverse time 1. Eigenvalues E_j>=0 satisfy
Z_B>=exp(-C), and by minmax Z_B(1/4)<=Z_{0,2}(1/4)<1.
Split the thermal mean energy at Q=2C+2=278. The low part is at most Q.
For E>Q,
 E exp(-E)=(E exp(-E/4))exp(-3E/4)
          <=(4/e)exp(-Q/2)exp(-E/4).
Hence the high part, after division by Z_B, is at most
 (4/e)exp(C-Q/2)Z_B(1/4)<4/e^2<1.
Therefore

 Tr sigma_B B<279,  and Tr sigma_B (beta K_Q)<279.       (Q.7)

For finite onsite strength, W in the M=2 sector is exactly the projection
Q_D onto |x,x> configurations, so

 Tr sigma_B Q_D<=279/L^4.                              (Q.8)

For hard core it is zero. These are proved Gibbs estimates from the trial
and partition comparison. No finite-energy condition on an arbitrary
decoder or reconstructed state is imposed anywhere.

5. THE COINCIDENCE IDENTITY FOR A PURIFICATION AND ITS QUARTIC TAIL

Purify sigma_B with an arbitrary ancilla. Let c_{k,l} be its ancilla-vector
coefficients in the normalized symmetric mode basis; use c_k=c_{k,k}
for diagonal mode pairs. Their squared norms sum (together with the
off-diagonal pair coefficients) to 1. Put
 u=||c_g||^2=Tr sigma_B |g,g><g,g|.

Orthogonality of the real one-particle modes gives the exact identity

 sum_{x in Lambda} (the purified wavefunction at x,x)=sum_k c_k.

Off-diagonal symmetric pairs contribute sqrt(2)sum_x phi_k(x)phi_l(x)=0,
and diagonal pairs contribute 1. Therefore for hard core sum_k c_k=0.
For finite repulsion, Cauchy-Schwarz in the sum over S sites and (Q.8)
give

 ||sum_k c_k||<=sqrt(S Tr sigma_B Q_D)<=sqrt(279)/L.     (Q.9)

This uses actual symmetric occupation normalization, including coincident
sites. There is no sector factorial or continuum contact-quadrature claim.

Let N=128. For modes with max(k1,k2)>N, (Q.3) and shell counting imply

 sum_{max k>N}1/(2a_k)
 <=sum_{r>N}(2r-1)/r^4
 <=2 integral_N^infinity r^{-3}dr=1/N^2.                (Q.10)

The high diagonal ancilla-vector sum is thus bounded by
 sqrt[sum_{max k>N}1/(2a_k)]
       sqrt[sum_{max k>N}2a_k ||c_k||^2]
 <=sqrt(279)/N<1/4,                                    (Q.11)

by the kinetic part of (Q.7). There are at most N^2-1 other low diagonal
modes, so their sum has norm at most sqrt(N^2-1)sqrt(1-u).

For finite strength 1 and L>=128 the residual in (Q.9) is also <1/4.
For hard core that residual is zero. The identity (Q.9) therefore implies

 sqrt(u)<=sqrt(N^2-1)sqrt(1-u)+1/2.                    (Q.12)

The last 1/2 is a convenient common upper bound for both models. Set
 epsilon=1/(16N^2)=2^{-18}.
If u>1-epsilon, the first term in (Q.12) is <1/4, so its right side
is <3/4, whereas sqrt(u)>=sqrt(1-epsilon)>3/4. This contradiction proves

 Tr sigma_B |g,g><g,g|<=1-epsilon.                      (Q.13)

The convergence of the inverse QUARTIC diagonal energy in (Q.10) is the
relevance mechanism. For the quadratic 2D scale the analogous diagonal
inverse sum diverges logarithmically, so this finite-tail argument does
not yield a fixed separation. It is the same logarithm that permits the
filter theorem of PROOF.txt.

6. THE FREE CANONICAL GROUND WEIGHT AT THE SAME FINITE CRITICAL TIME

The free two-particle ground vector is |g,g>, unique. Let
 r=sum_{k!=g}exp(-(a_k-a_g)).
Then the free two-particle canonical ground probability obeys

 u0>=1/(1+r+r^2)>=1-2r when r<1.                        (Q.14)

To see this, factor exp(-2a_g) out of the symmetric two-particle partition;
the remaining ground, one-ground, and two-excited occupation terms sum
to at most 1+r+r^2.

We give a uniform rigorous small bound on r for L>=4. For the modes
(1,2),(2,1), the angles are at most pi/2. On this interval
1-cos x>=4x^2/pi^2, which follows from
sin(x/2)/(x/2)>=2sqrt(2)/pi on [0,pi/4]. Thus L^2 omega_(1,2)>=80,
a_(1,2)>=50, while a_g<25/2. Each of these two terms is at most exp(-75/2).
The remaining r=2 mode is (2,2), with a_(2,2)>=32 and hence difference
at least 39/2. For shells r>=3, (Q.3) gives
 a_k-a_g >=(r^4/2)-25/2 >=4r+16.
There are at most 2r modes per shell. Consequently

 r<=2exp(-75/2)+exp(-39/2)
                       +exp(-16)sum_{r>=3}2r exp(-4r)
   <10^{-7}.                                          (Q.15)

For example exp(4)>50, exp(16)>10^6, exp(39/2)>10^8, and
sum_{r>=3}2r/50^r<10^{-4}; these certify the last inequality with a
large margin. All follow from positive rational lower bounds on exp(1).
There is no floating-point eigenvalue or asymptotic spectral assumption.
Since 2*10^{-7}<epsilon/2, (Q.14)-(Q.15) prove

 Tr sigma_{0,2}|g,g><g,g|>=1-epsilon/2.                 (Q.16)

Combining (Q.13) with (Q.16), the two normalized M=2 states have a bounded
observable difference >=epsilon/2, hence trace distance at least epsilon.
Explicitly their difference is traceless; its positive and negative parts
have equal trace. For any effect E, |Tr E(sigma_B-sigma_0)| is at most
half the full trace norm. This supplies the stated factor of 2.

7. LIFT TO THE WHOLE FOCK STATE -- NO CONDITIONAL-MODE SUBSTITUTION

The free whole-Fock probability p2 of M=2 is uniformly positive:

 p2=Z_{0,2}/Z0>=exp(-2a_g)/Z0>=exp(-27),                (Q.17)

because a_g<25/2 and Z0<exp(2). Both interacting two-particle block
probabilities are positive; their partitions have the lower bound from
(Q.6). The whole states commute with M, so their M=2 normalized blocks
are exactly the canonical states analyzed above. Their other sectors
are not discarded or presumed equal.

For any two states rho,sigma with positive probability p,q of a fixed
projection P, write their normalized P blocks as rho_P,sigma_P. Then

 p||rho_P-sigma_P||_1
 <=||p rho_P-q sigma_P||_1+|p-q|
 <=2||rho-sigma||_1.                                   (Q.18)

The first inequality is the triangle inequality; the second follows from
projection compression and its trace. Apply (Q.18) with the FREE state's
p=p2, the repulsive or hard state's normalized M=2 block, and the
conditional trace gap at least epsilon. Equations (Q.17)-(Q.18) give

 ||rho1-rho0||_1, ||rho_hc-rho0||_1
                   >=epsilon exp(-27)/2=2^{-19}exp(-27),

which proves (Q.1)-(Q.2). The test can be implemented by a bounded
projection/effect in the M=2 block; no energy-constrained measurement or
decoder class is built into the lower bound.

The specific free canonical measure-M/prepare-free-M decoder from
PROOF Section 8 fails here as well. Its M=2 output is p_{2,1}sigma_{0,2},
whereas the true block is p_{2,1}sigma_{1,2}; the whole reconstruction error
is at least p_{2,1}epsilon. Since p_{2,1}>=exp(-138)/Z1>=exp(-140), this
decoder has floor epsilon exp(-140), without an output-energy assumption.
The same assertion holds for the exact hard-state input.

This is a refutation of THAT free-canonical reconstruction mechanism under
changed dispersion. A floor for every possible EB map for the common
unknown-interaction quartic family is a separate gate; state separation
alone does not establish it. A different positive-coupling pair and a
quantitative affinity proof are now banked in NONCOMMUTING_QUARTIC_FAMILY
and QUARTIC_EB_OBSTRUCTION; they do not follow from (Q.1) alone.

8. NONSTOQUASTICITY, COSTS, AND PRIOR ART BOUNDARY

h_Q has positive off-diagonal entries between sites at graph distance 2:
each two-step path contributes (-2)(-2)/128>0. Hence the entrywise positive
heat-kernel step of the quadratic proof cannot be reused. However the
counterexample above proves a trace floor independently of that fact.
The inverse diagonal spectral sum, not an unexamined sign heuristic, is
the load-bearing obstruction.

Inputs are public open geometry, fixed rational h_Q, fixed strength1
onsite W (or physical exact hard core), and beta=L^4. No exponentially
precise coefficient, hidden dense row, interacting eigensolver, numerical
ground-state test or assumed convergence theorem is used. h_Q is a fixed-
range sparse matrix. W is diagonal and explicitly computable from the
occupation list. The mathematical Gibbs input is supplied; its efficient
physical generation is not proved. The fulltrace separation and decoder
failure are mathematical information statements, with no finite-energy
restriction on a proposed decoder. The numerical floor is conservative
and small; it is not an optimized experimental-effect prediction.
