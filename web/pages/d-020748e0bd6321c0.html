P17 — Exact PGF for the ideal rare-herald branch
Date: 2026-10-08

Under exact-one photon-number-resolving (PNR) heralding and independent signal/idler loss eta=1/R, the higher-pair posterior changes the retained signal by only O(a), rather than the O(Ra) raw posterior-tail bound. Here a=lambda^2(1-eta)=2^(-2R^2)(1-1/R), R=4^k, and the result concerns only the ideal branch.

For N=R, the balanced Fock expansion of the populated Walsh output mode has amplitude sqrt(R!/R^R) on the all-distinct pattern. Hence the boson-sampling probability is R!/R^R; requiring all R idler photons to survive multiplies it by eta^R. Thus q_R=P(H|N=R)=R!/R^(2R). For N=R+j, the multinomial probability is q_R binom(R+j,R)(1-eta)^j. Combining this with the TMSV geometric prior (z=lambda^2) gives

    P(H)=(1-z)z^R q_R(1-a)^(-(R+1)),
    J=N-R | H ~ NB(r=R+1, fugacity a),
    G_J(s)=((1-a)/(1-as))^r.

The raw higher-pair mass is T=P(J>0)=1-(1-a)^r <= r a=O(Ra). Its input-pair first moment is

    E[N 1_{J>0}|H] = R T + r a/(1-a) = O(R^2 a),

an extra factor R over the raw tail mass. After signal loss,
E[K 1_{J>0}|H]=eta[R T+r a/(1-a)]=O(Ra). This is still distinct from the retained signal’s genuinely additional photons.

Conditional on J, split signal thinning into K=X+Y, with X~Bin(R,eta) independent of (J,Y), and Y|J~Bin(J,eta). The exact bivariate posterior PGF and its signal marginal are

    E[u^J t^K|H]=(1-eta+eta t)^R
       ((1-a)/(1-a u(1-eta+eta t)))^r,
    G_K(t)=(1-eta+eta t)^R
       ((1-a)/(1-a(1-eta+eta t)))^r.

Set y=a eta/[1-a(1-eta)] and v=y/(1-y)=a eta/(1-a). Then Y~NB(r,y), so K is exactly the independent convolution Bin(R,eta)+NB(r,y), and

    E[K|H]=R eta+r v,
    Var(K|H)=R eta(1-eta)+r v(1+v),
    E[Y|H]=r a/[R(1-a)] = O(a)       (eta=1/R).

More sharply, the probability that any excess signal photon survives is

    Delta=P(Y>0|H)=1-(1-y)^r <= r y
      = (R+1)a/[R(1-a(1-1/R))] = (1+O(1/R))a.

The coupling K=X+Y gives d_TV(Law(K|H),Bin(R,1/R))<=Delta. This improves the raw-tail mixture error O(Ra) by an asymptotic factor R.

For X~Bin(R,1/R), the point probabilities converge to e^(-1)/m!, and Le Cam’s bound gives d_TV(X,Poisson(1))<=1/R. A likelihood-ratio expansion sharpens this to

    d_TV(Bin(R,1/R),Poisson(1))=3/(4eR)+O(R^(-2)).

Indeed, for Q~Poisson(1), P(X=m)/P(Q=m)=e(R)_m R^(-m)(1-1/R)^(R-m), and for m<=M=ceil(8 log R/loglog R), its log is b(m)/R+O((m+1)^3/R^2), b(m)=(-m^2+3m-1)/2. The factorial-moment bound makes both tails beyond M at most 1/M!=o(R^-2). Since E[b(Q)]=0 and E|b(Q)|=3/(2e), the stated TV coefficient follows. Combining with the thinning coupling yields

    d_TV(Law(K|H),Poisson(1))=3/(4eR)+O(R^(-2)+a).

Thus the super-exponentially small herald correction is below the leading 1/R binomial-to-Poisson error. For diagonal states, trace norm is twice this TV distance.

Falsifiers and costs. The signal loss is essential: without it K=N and the Poisson(1) conclusion fails (the conditional count concentrates at R). Threshold all-click heralding also differs from exact-one PNR heralding because collisions are admitted in sectors N>R. The success mass above implies log2(1/P(H))=2R^3+log2(R^(2R)/R!)+o(1), so physical rejection needs mean 1/P(H) preparations; the PGF does not remove that cost. Walsh entries are rational for R=4^k, while an explicit dense matrix costs O(R^2 log R) entry bits. No sampler lower bound, noise claim, or optical implementation was established.
