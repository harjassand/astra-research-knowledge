PRECISE PRINCIPAL RESULT

There exists an explicitly specified irreducible stationary continuous-time Markov chain with ten states, rational transition rates, uniform stationary distribution, and a deterministic observation map with six output letters, such that:

(1) Its entire stationary observed law is time-reversal invariant. Its observable reflection Hilbert space has dimension eight, with a selfadjoint contraction semigroup.

(2) For every finite collection of finite future cylinder events, at arbitrary nonnegative times, the matrix pairing their time-reflected past events with their future events is classically completely positive. All such matrices have one common ten-atom nonnegative factorization. Here complete positivity means a matrix factorization BB^T with B entrywise nonnegative, not complete positivity of a quantum channel.

(3) Nevertheless its law lies outside the finite-dimensional-distribution closure of the stationary observations of reversible hidden Markov processes with deterministic state observations and the usual regular conditional Markov interface (in particular, standard-Borel hidden state spaces). In particular no sequence of finite equilibrium hidden CTMCs can approximate all its rational-time cylinder probabilities, even with unbounded hidden dimension, rates, activity and arbitrarily small hidden stationary masses.

(4) The separation is witnessed by one explicitly specified rational, bounded function w of seven observed letters at times
    -t-h, -t, -h, 0, h, t, t+h,
    t=1/1000, h=1/10^12.
It satisfies |w|<=1 and E_equilibrium w>=0 for every stationary reversible hidden Markov realization under that observation interface, while for the constructed process
    E_target w < -1/(6*10^45).
The rational coefficient file and a word evaluator are included. Hidden states are used to construct and prove the certificate; evaluating the fixed statistic uses only the seven observed letters.

(5) Consequently EVERY stationary finite all-even hidden CTMC producing this observed process has entropy production rate
    sigma > 6*10^(-44) nats per model time unit,
with the usual stationary CTMC path-relative-entropy convention. This includes completions with arbitrarily many hidden states and arbitrarily fast rates. It is not a claim about the entropy production of just the supplied ten-state model.

The observed path-reversal relative entropy is zero and every CP reflection test passes. The indispensable obstruction is consistency with a reversible evolution of the common hidden factors.

WHY THE PROOF WORKS

The ten states consist of five internal points on a strictly convex cyclic pentagon and five individually observed marker states. Internal affine functions and marker indicators form an eight-dimensional invariant observable space. The restriction of the nonreversible generator to this space is selfadjoint. Projecting reversed-past features onto this space yields the corresponding nonnegative future features, which proves all-word complete positivity without asserting that actual hidden past and future conditional laws coincide.

Marker-event positivity forces every admissible internal static feature point into the pentagon. A saturated unit-circle second moment forces those points onto its vertices; a nonsingular five-moment matrix fixes their weights. A rational monotone vector field on these points has a strictly negative pairing with the required drift. Reversible evolution would produce symmetric two-time couplings, for which that pairing must be nonnegative.

The closure proof uses uniformly bounded conditional expectations of one fixed finite future basis. It passes to symmetric couplings at fixed positive lag and never differentiates a sequence of hidden generators. An exact Taylor remainder certifies the contradiction already at t=1/1000.

The constructive finite-observation proof replaces exact feature identities by residuals whose reflected squared norms are observable. Polygon projection, circle-defect rounding and Young inequalities absorb these errors into a single linear inequality in a seven-time probability table. Degree-four and degree-sixteen matrix polynomials make every coefficient rational. The resulting raw statistic has target expectation at most -1.173761546566416e-6, with rigorously certified pointwise magnitude below 6e39. These displayed decimals summarize exact rational comparisons; the proof does not depend on floating-point signs.

The final entropy-production consequence uses path-affinity domination. Let p and q be stationary hidden path densities for any compatible finite generator Q and its stationary adjoint Q*, and let s be the path density of S=(Q+Q*)/2. Stationarity makes their initial and holding-time factors agree. Edgewise arithmetic-geometric mean gives sqrt(pq)<=s pointwise. Also sqrt(pq)<=(p+q)/2=:m. Consequently
    TV(m,s)<=1-integral sqrt(pq)
             <=1-exp[-D(p||q)/2]=1-exp(-T sigma/2).
The second inequality is Jensen's inequality, and the last identity is the usual stationary all-even CTMC entropy-production formula. The witness is pointwise invariant under reversing its seven letters, so its expectation under Q equals its expectation under m. Its range is at most two. Its equilibrium expectation is nonnegative and its target expectation is below -delta. Thus
    delta<=2[1-exp(-T sigma/2)],
    sigma>=-(2/T)log(1-delta/2)>=delta/T>6e-44,
    T=2(t+h), delta=1/(6*10^45).
One-way positive stationary flux has infinite entropy production and satisfies the bound automatically. The earlier Pinsker-based bound >2e-89 remains valid but is quantitatively superseded. The complete affinity argument is appended in observable_separation/v3.txt. Its historical priority is unestablished; additive reversibilization and the path-KL identity are known ingredients.

SIGNIFICANCE AND ACTUAL LIMITS

The mathematical distinction is between a static common nonnegative representation of every past/future reflection pairing and a dynamically compatible equilibrium realization. The construction separates them even at finite observation resolution. The companion finite-support theorem characterizes equilibrium closure by a finite symmetric-flow feasibility problem under its precise finite-support hypothesis; it is not a universal finite algorithm for all hidden processes.

The explicit numerical certificate is extremely inefficient. A direct independent-window Hoeffding test at the stated conservative margin and 95 percent confidence requires approximately 1.1e93 independent stationary windows, with temporal resolution 1e-12. No practical experiment, improved hardware capability, or useful numerical dissipation estimate follows from those costs. A single-trajectory sampling guarantee would need separately acquired dependence assumptions.

All-even hidden variables, stationarity and the specified observation interface are essential to the entropy statement. Odd time-reversal variables such as momenta, memory-bearing observation devices and nonstationary experiments are not silently included. The process is an explicit mathematical model; no natural system was empirically shown to obey it.

Prior reflection-positive/Markov reconstruction theory already distinguishes positivity from the Markov property. Existing hidden-dissipation inference and positive-realization theory are indispensable context. In particular the additive-reversibilization path bound and basic negative-correlation dissipation floors appear in the earlier Oct3 candidate pinned in the source ledger. The possible new contribution here is the explicit all-word-CP counterexample, unrestricted closure obstruction, and finite raw-observation witness. The bounded search did not establish historical priority or historic significance.
