# Exact hidden-coordinate acquisition: prior and bit audit

2026-10-07. Question: does an existing primary result already acquire the rational hidden axes and positive weighted-square certificate from the original expanded quartic coefficients, with arbitrary coupled positive-definite Q, unknown L,a,Q, no supplied target matrix family, and polynomial bit cost in variable count and coefficient length? **Historical novelty remains UNKNOWN.** The consulted primary results establish substantial ingredients and some low-dimensional special cases; none read here states the full matched interface. This is a bounded audit, not a novelty certificate.

## Closest prior and what transfers

1. Voss–Belkin–Rademacher, *The Hidden Convexity of Spectral Clustering*, arXiv1403.0667v3 (2016-05-04; v1 2014-03-04), §2 Definition1, Theorem4, Corollary5, Algorithm1 and proof. The exact sphere-to-simplex change of variables, local maxima lying among vertices, and abstract orthogonal deflation are established prior. Their function is separable in hidden coordinates. The proof's underlying convex-maximization fact is broader than that separable presentation. Read PDF pp3–5; not the whole paper.

2. Belkin–Rademacher–Voss, *Basis Learning as an Algorithmic Primitive*, COLT2016, PMLR49,446–487, §2 A1–A4 and Theorems2.1–2.4; §3 proof portions. Their robust gradient iteration and approximate deflation learn separable hidden-coordinate functions with complexity depending on conditioning parameters. The theorem cannot simply be applied to arbitrary coupled H or to polynomial bit complexity independent of hidden conditioning. Existing connections to ICA, tensor decomposition, spectral clustering and mixture learning must be credited. The exact algebraic route changes the method, not the fact that basis acquisition is a research primitive.

3. Pereira, *Vector Fields, Invariant Varieties and Linear Systems*, arXivmath/0011205v1,2000-11-24; AIF51(5),1385–1405(2001). Extactic divisibility and the relation with first integrals are prior. Part1's degree-bounded first-integral result is planar. The arbitrary-dimensional Theorem3 gives a Grassmannian/meromorphic first integral, not a fixed ratio of linear forms. Full decisive theorem/proof sections read. See `EXTACTIC_PRIOR_AND_RECONSTRUCTION.md` for exact scope and independently reconstructed universal nonvanishing argument.

4. Chèze, *Computation of Darboux polynomials and rational first integrals with bounded degree in polynomial time*, arXiv1009.2876v1,2010-09-15; J.Complexity27(2),246–262(2011). Theorem1 and §§3–4 already give polynomial-bit bounded-degree Darboux extraction by extactic factorization in two variables. Page4 explicitly extends the core factor-divisibility algorithm to multivariate derivations; its charged height and size analysis is bivariate and explicitly expands the determinant. It does not establish nonzero extactic for our PD family or a polynomial-in-n compact implementation. Hence the new composition should not be advertised as inventing efficient Darboux extraction. The author credits Lagutinskii and Pereira for the older determinant method.

5. Bostan–Chèze–Cluzeau–Weil, *Efficient Algorithms for Computing Rational First Integrals and Darboux Polynomials of Planar Polynomial Vector Fields*, arXiv1310.2778v1,2013-10-10. Introduction, §5 complexity and §7 algorithm statements read. Formal ODE series plus linear reconstruction already accelerate planar invariant extraction, with arithmetic complexity stated in the degree bound and field-operation model. This supplies a direct method comparator; it is not the arbitrary-dimensional coupled hidden-axis theorem.

Sinha's2016 thesis AppendixC explicitly spells out extracting linear forms from black-box factors using interpolation and identity tests; it was read as secondary implementation navigation to the primary theorem.

7. García-Marco–Koiran–Pecatte, *Polynomial Equivalence Problems for Sums of Affine Powers*, ISSAC2018. Sections1,3–5 and theorem statements read. Algorithms recover affine power sums/separable decompositions; their fourth-order Hessian factor strategy for up to binomial(n+1,2) forms assumes relevant exponents at least5 and genericity. It does not immediately apply to arbitrary coupled degree4 squares. Its computation model permits solving univariate equations as primitive field operations and must not be silently read as the same rational-bit model. Generic Hessian and Lie symmetry shortcuts are separately excluded in `ALGEBRA_OBSTRUCTIONS.md`.

8. Kayal–Nair–Saha–Tavenas, *Reconstruction of Full Rank Algebraic Branching Programs*, CCC2017, publication2017-08-01. Theorems5–7, computational-model discussion and Lie-algebra overview read. Its polynomial-bit reconstruction applies to full-rank iterated-matrix-multiplication structure with independent linear entries, not arbitrary weighted biquadratic forms. The direct polynomial stabilizer Lie algebra of our PD quartic is identically zero, so that specific interface has no hidden axis information.

9. Abramian–Desmorat–Desmorat–Kolev–Olive, *Recovering the normal form of an elasticity tensor*, arXiv1912.07430v2,2020-06-26 (v1 2019-12-16), §4 Theorems4.8–4.9 and §6.5 read. This is a close fixed-dimension comparator: 3D tensor coefficients yield orthotropic axes through polynomial covariants; cubic symmetry is handled by a linear system and a generic symmetric solution. Thus coefficient-only recovery of a coupled quartic frame is already known as a concept, including symmetric cases in dimension3. It does not state an arbitrary-n rational polynomial-bit weighted-SOS theorem. A fully symmetric quartic tensor is a special elasticity tensor, so this prior deserves more weight than merely analogous ICA.

10. Görlach–Hubert–Papadopoulo, *Rational invariants of even ternary forms under the orthogonal group*, arXiv1707.09979v3,2018-11-13. Introduction and algorithm overview read. “Even” means even total degree, not coordinatewise sign invariance. The work fixes dimension3 and constructs rational invariant generators and reconstruction procedures; it is not a same-interface arbitrary-variable acquisition theorem.

## Independent polynomial-height calculation

Write the rational homogeneous vector field as F=P/q with q a positive integer and P integer polynomial. Let degree F=r, and A=max(1,max_i ||P_i||_1). Let Delta=P dot grad. For any homogeneous g of degree s,

    ||Delta g||_1 <= s A ||g||_1.

Thus

    ||Delta^k x_i||_1 <= A^k product_(a=0)^(k-1)(1+(r-1)a).

For line coordinates |u_i|,|v_i|<=S, substitution x=u+tv increases the one-norm by at most (2 max(1,S))^[1+(r-1)k]. Since D^k x_i=q^(-k) Delta^k x_i, the extactic denominator divides q^[n(n-1)/2]. The determinant numerator one-norm is at most n! times the product of these row bounds. Its logarithm is

    O(n²[log A+log q+log(nr)+r log(2 max(1,S))]),

a deliberately loose polynomial bound. Each ambient derivative adds at most the total-degree factor. This explicitly bounds coefficient growth even though the multivariate expansion may have exponentially many monomials. Formal ODE jets on a line and differentiated jets have only polynomially many univariate coefficients; polynomially many truncated products suffice when the numerical degree r is charged. Rational univariate factorization, root heights, multiplicity derivatives, candidate scoring, exact orthogonality and basis inversion then have polynomial bit cost. No hidden L-height or eigenvalue gap is smuggled into this argument.

This supports the bit claim but is not a runtime benchmark. The full proof also needs good-line coverage and an appropriate stopping verifier; the broad-H generalization has a separate gate.
