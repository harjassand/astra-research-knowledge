## Strongest claim

The supplied N81 rare-herald reduction has a valid *conditional implication*
at its stated interface: a uniform polynomial-bit sampler with conditional
total-variation error at most 1/100 on every constructed rational Gaussian
instance would give PP = BPP (and its quantum-sampler counterpart gives
PP = BQP). The reduction uses a positive, explicitly lower-bounded herald and
a constant-gap output event, so neither a zero-probability input nor a
relative estimate of an exponentially small output event is needed. This is
not a complexity-class separation and is not a hardness theorem for the
determinant-weighted parity partition.

For the parity target, the exact zero question is a represented linear-matroid
parity optimization question: for rational-complex F,

    c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J = empty} |det F[I,J]|^2

is positive iff the paired columns {e_i, f_i : i in P} in [I,F^T] contain an
independent set of k pairs. The imported deterministic linear-matroid parity
algorithm, combined with the stated finite-field bit reduction, decides that
support in polynomial bit complexity. It does not estimate c_k. Exact support
therefore removes a zero-promise obstacle, but it supplies no counting
normalizer, relative approximation, or sampler. A normalized sampler alone
cannot be treated as a relative counting algorithm.

## Derivation and interface audit

### N81: why constant TV is enough here

Take a PostBQP circuit for a PP input x with positive postselection
probability p_post >= 2^(-q_post) and conditioned acceptance probability
p_x >= 2/3 on YES, <= 1/3 on NO. The supplied construction uses a known
lower-bound exponent q_post, not the unknown exact postselection probability.
It maps single-photon KLM inputs to TMSV/idler heralds; includes every KLM,
postselection and buncher monitor in a binary photon herald; copies the
computational-basis decision into R dual-rail readouts; and bunches each rail
set into one of two count modes. For R a power of four, both branches acquire
the same bunching probability R!/R^R >= R^(-R), so their ratio remains p_x.
Every source and gate-success factor has a known positive lower bound, giving
the pre-loss herald bound p_* = 2^(-q) > 0.

After uniform loss eta = 1/R, the ideal two-mode decoder reports the occupied
rail when any photon remains and a fair bit otherwise. Its probability of
reporting one is

    r_x = 1/2 + a_R (p_x - 1/2),   a_R = 1 - (1 - 1/R)^R.

Since a_R > 1 - e^(-1) > 3/5, the YES and NO distributions lie more than
1/10 on opposite sides of 1/2. This is a bounded event probability, not a
rare-event probability. A sampler within TV delta changes the decoded event
probability by at most delta, by data processing.

The supplied filtering lemma bounds loss-created false heralds by 2g^2/p_0.
After source rounding the pre-loss herald probability is at least p_*/2; taking
ell >= ceil((q+10)/2), g=2^(-ell), makes this term at most
4g^2/p_* <= 1/256. The pre-filter dyadic source rounding contributes at most
1/200 to the conditioned law, and the hypothetical sampler adds 1/100.
Thus total error is at most

    1/256 + 1/200 + 1/100 = 121/6400 < 0.019.

The remaining one-sided threshold margin is greater than
1/10 - 121/6400 > 3/40. Repetition and majority vote give a bounded-error
classical PP decider. I independently checked this finite arithmetic at R=4
in interface_audit.py; the all-R inequality above is analytic.

The final herald remains positive: the supplied lower bound is
w_* = R^(-h) g^(2h) 2^(-q-1), with
log2(1/w_*) = h log2 R + 2 ell h + q + 1 = O(R^2 log R), since
h=O(R), R is a power of four, q=O(R log R), and ell=O(R log R).
The input matrix dimension and entry bit lengths are polynomial in the PP
circuit size. The assumed sampler must run polynomially in its description
length (or in the listed polynomially bounded parameters such as log(1/w_*));
a runtime polynomial in 1/w_* is not excluded by this reduction. No inverse
success physical preparation is claimed.

This audit found no promise-gap, normalization, or TV/relative-error defect in
the N81 decision transfer. Its endpoint is PP=BPP *if the specified sampler
exists*, not PP != BPP. It assumes pure centered Gaussian input with rational
complex symmetric B, ||B||<1, pure vacuum power loss, ideal photon-number
monitoring, the specified positive binary herald and two retained counts.
The full general compiler, external proof review and priority check remain
unavailable; the supplied 1,596 finite assertions are source-reported rather
than replayed here.

### Why this does not transfer to hard BCS parity counting

For the hard norm, each summand is nonnegative, and Cauchy--Binet gives

    c_k(F) = sum_{|P|=k} det( [I,F^T]_{P}^* [I,F^T]_{P} ),

where P selects both columns e_i,f_i at each selected site. A Gram determinant
is positive exactly when those 2k columns are independent. Hence c_k>0 iff
the represented linear matroid has an independent parity set of size k.
Hamada--Iwata Theorem 1.1 is a deterministic unweighted optimization theorem
over a field; its cost is field operations, not a weighted partition sum.
Clearing rational-complex denominators to Gaussian integers and trying
polynomially many finite fields preserves the exact optimum: every modular
witness lifts (a zero minor cannot become nonzero), and one tested prime
avoids the positive integer norm of a fixed optimal minor. For forced/excluded
prefixes, quotient by the forced independent span, remove excluded pairs and
ask whether the residual maximum reaches the needed cardinality. All of this
is support acquisition only.

An exact example shows why the graph support of F is not a positivity
certificate. Let F=J_4-I_4 and k=2. Its off-diagonal support graph is K4 and
has three perfect matchings. Yet every ordered disjoint I,J of size two gives
an all-ones 2-by-2 submatrix, so det F[I,J]=0 and c_2=0. The script
interface_audit.py enumerates the six such ordered pairs and verifies all
six determinants vanish while the graph has three perfect matchings.

Even on positive instances, a sampler for the normalized fixed-k law does not
return its normalizer. For n=2, k=1, F=[[0,1],[1,0]] has two unit-weight
configurations and c_1=2. Replacing F by F/8 multiplies c_1 by 1/64, to 1/32,
but leaves the normalized law exactly (1/2,1/2). In general
c_k(lambda F)=|lambda|^(2k)c_k(F), while every normalized configuration
probability is unchanged. Also, a TV guarantee controls an event only
additively: if a target event has exponentially small normalized mass, fixed
TV accuracy says nothing about its relative probability. Recovering c_k needs
a normalizer or a separately charged chain of ratios; the two-replica energy,
balancing, coefficient-oracle and sample costs remain the unresolved gate.

Accordingly, neither exact support nor normalized TV sampling yields a
relative-counting oracle. A general #P/approximation-hardness transfer for
c_k has not been established. OA113's graph-matching FPRAS also cannot be
imported by identifying support graphs: its product edge weights do not
represent determinant-squared weights with cancellations inside each minor.
The exact F=J-I example already defeats the simplest support-only shortcut.
