# Independent reconstruction of the FL ancestry claims

This proof reconstruction uses the supplied temporal note and ancestry report as claims to audit. It does not use an ancestry producer script: the intake manifest lists no such script. `independent_checks.py` and `simulate_shared_code.py` are newly written checks from the stated mathematics, not replays.

## 1. Isotope-count estimator with unequal purities

Let the isotope at an entry time be (X_i\in\{+1,-1\}), where (+1) means heavy. If the prescribed heavy fraction is
\[
p(t)=\frac{1+b+a\sigma(t)}2,
\qquad a=p_{\rm high}-p_{\rm low}>0,
\qquad b=p_{\rm high}+p_{\rm low}-1,
\]
then (\mathbb E[X_i\mid \sigma,S_i]=b+a\sigma(S_i)). Conditional independence of the entering labels therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\left[\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{X_i-b}{a}\,\middle|\,\sigma,S_1,\ldots,S_n\right]
=\prod_{i=1}^n\sigma(S_i).
\]
For (K=\#\{i:X_i=+1\}), this product is
\[
W_n(K)=\frac{(1-b)^K(-1-b)^{n-K}}{a^n}.
\]
Thus a whole-product count histogram with (n+1) bins is sufficient for this particular statistic. It does not make the measurement low-noise: since
\[
\left|\frac{X_i-b}{a}\right|\le \frac{1+|b|}{a},
\]
the product weight satisfies ( |W_n(K)|\le B_n=[(1+|b|)/a]^n), and the crude per-draw variance bound is (B_n^2). If (b=0), (W_n(K)=(-1)^K/a^n) for even or odd (n); for even (n), the sign is just the parity of (K).

The calibration must use the actual heavy fraction at the declared entry boundary. A nominal mixer command is not enough if transport, mixing or reservoir exchange changes (p(t)).

## 2. Poisson parity and the target functional

For a stationary fair telegraph sign with homogeneous Poisson flips of rate (r),
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{2m}\sigma(S_i)
=(-1)^{N(A)},\qquad
A=\bigcup_{j=1}^m[S_{2j-1},S_{2j}],\qquad |A|=L_{2m}.
\]
The intervals are disjoint up to endpoints because the entry times are ordered. (N(A)\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(rL_{2m})), so its probability generating function gives
\[
\mathbb E_\sigma(-1)^{N(A)}=\exp(rL_{2m}(-1-1))=e^{-2rL_{2m}}.
\]
This proves the acquisition identity after averaging over histories **only if** the history law and product-selection rule are independent of the isotope code. A stationary two-sided telegraph process, or a recorded code that covers every relevant entry time, is required for the exact stationary statement.

## 3. Geometry and code-only variance

Fix a finite union (A\subset\mathbb R) of at most (m) disjoint intervals, length (\ell), and define
\[
H(s)=|A\cap(A+s)|,\qquad \delta(s)=\ell-H(s)=|A\setminus(A+s)|.
\]
Write (Z_A(t)=(-1)^{N(t+A)}). Since (N((t+A)\triangle(t+s+A))) is Poisson with mean (2r\delta(s)),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(t),Z_A(t+s))
=e^{-4r\delta(s)}-e^{-4r\ell}\ge0.
\]
It is zero when (H(s)=0).

For (s>0), project (A) onto the circle (\mathbb R/(s\mathbb Z)). For almost every residue (y\in[0,s)), the fiber (B_y=\{k:y+ks\in A\}\subset\mathbb Z) is finite. The fiber of (A+s) is (B_y+1), and every nonempty finite integer set satisfies \(|B_y\setminus(B_y+1)|\ge1\). Integrating the fiber counts yields
\[
\delta(s)\ge |\pi_s(A)|.
\]
If a component interval has length at least (s), its projection is the whole circle, of measure (s). Otherwise each component projects to an arc whose measure is its length, and the union has measure at least the longest component length, at least (\ell/m). Therefore
\[
\delta(s)\ge\min(s,\ell/m).
\]

If (H(s)>0), then (s\in A-A). For component lengths (l_1,\ldots,l_q), (q\le m), the sum of lengths of the (q^2) interval differences is
\[
\sum_{i,j}(l_i+l_j)=2q\ell\le2m\ell.
\]
The positive half of the symmetric difference set consequently has measure at most (m\ell). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(0),Z_A(s))\,ds
&\le \int_0^{\ell/m}e^{-4rs}\,ds
   +m\ell e^{-4r\ell/m}\\
&\le \frac1{4r}+\frac{m^2}{4er}
=\frac{1+m^2/e}{4r},
\end{aligned}
\]
using (y e^{-y}\le e^{-1}). For the stationary time average, evenness and nonnegativity of the covariance give
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1T\int_0^T Z_A(t)\,dt\right)
\le \frac{2}{T}\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(0),Z_A(s))\,ds
\le\frac{1+m^2/e}{2rT}.
\]
If (\ell=0), (Z_A\equiv1), so the variance is zero.

For a fixed code-independent stationary mixture (\mu(dA)), set (q_A=e^{-2r|A|}) and (G(t)=\int Z_A(t)\,\mu(dA)). Then
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T G(t)dt-\int q_A\mu(dA)
=\int\left[\frac1T\int_0^T Z_A(t)dt-q_A\right]\mu(dA).
\]
Jensen's inequality followed by the uniform fixed-(A) bound proves the same code variance bound for this *ensemble conditional-mean signal*. The mixture need not have bounded age span or a finite hidden state-space. This conclusion concerns the variance over the code of the mixture mean; it is not a bound on chemical or detector fluctuations.

## 4. What the finite-product term actually assumes

Suppose exactly (N) product observations are iid conditional on the complete code: each output time is uniform on ([0,T]), each history is an independent draw from the same (\mu), and atom labels are conditionally independent. Given the code, the expectation of one weighted count is (G(U)), with (U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]), and ( |W_n|\le B_n\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat Q\mid\text{code})\le B_n^2/N.
\]
The conditional expectation is the code time average of (G), so the law of total variance yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat Q)\le\frac{1+m^2/e}{2rT}+\frac{B_n^2}{N}.
\]
Conditioning on (N), a constant-rate Poisson product stream with iid histories gives the stated uniform output-time model.

The iid assumption is material. If a latent catalyst regime (\Theta\) is selected once per campaign and all product histories in that campaign share that regime, then an additional campaign term remains:
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat Q)\le \frac{1+m^2/e}{2rT}+\frac{B_n^2}{N}+\operatorname{Var}_\Theta(Q_\Theta)
\]
when observations are iid conditional on (\Theta\). More general serial dependence adds the covariance sum of product weights. For example, a campaign-level 50/50 choice between the note's E and O histories has persistent large-(N,T) variance \((Q_E-Q_O)^2/4\), not a variance tending to zero. The code-only theorem remains valid for the fixed population mixture mean, while the finite-sample theorem needs independent history draws or an added dependence term.

## 5. E/O age laws and single-pulse separation

Let (A_j=C+(j-1)\tau+\tau X_j), (j=1,\ldots,4). These ages are ordered because (A_{j+1}-A_j=\tau(1+X_{j+1}-X_j)\ge0). Reversing the ages gives the ordered entry times, and
\[
L_4=(A_2-A_1)+(A_4-A_3)
=\tau[2+X_2-X_1+X_4-X_3].
\]
The eight even strings yield lengths (0,2\tau,4\tau) with multiplicities (1,6,1); the eight odd strings yield (\tau,3\tau) with multiplicities (4,4). Every proper coordinate marginal agrees because every assignment on at most three bits has exactly the same number of even and odd completions.

For a step or rectangular pulse, let (m_k\in\{0,1\}) record whether the age node (C+k\tau), (k=0,\ldots,4), lies inside the pulse's translated age interval. A rectangular interval makes the node sequence (m_0,\ldots,m_4) have at most two transitions. Atom (j) samples node (j-1+X_j), so its pulse membership is (m_{j-1+X_j}), which varies with (X_j) only if (m_{j-1}\ne m_j). At most two coordinates affect the entire vector of atom-label probabilities. The complete isotope-count law is therefore a function of at most two (X_j)'s and agrees under E and O. This argument includes pulse boundaries coincident with age nodes: the convention for whether a boundary point is inside still yields one contiguous node block and at most two transitions.

For a general normalized deterministic isotope mean (u), set (u_k=u(t-C-k\tau)). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(t,C)
&=\mathbb E_E\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+X_{j+1}}-\mathbb E_O\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+X_{j+1}}\\
&=\frac18\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^4}(-1)^{|x|}\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+x_j}\\
&=\frac18\prod_{j=0}^3(u_j-u_{j+1}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus for an (M)-Lipschitz waveform, ( |\Delta|\le(M\tau)^4/8\). Smooth waveforms can distinguish E/O; the exact equality result is only for the stated step and one-pulse family.

## 6. Telegraph numbers and the square-wave competitor

With (x=e^{-2r\tau}), the length multiplicities give
\[
Q_E=(1+6x^2+x^4)/8,\quad Q_O=(x+x^3)/2,\quad Q_E-Q_O=(1-x)^4/8.
\]
At (r\tau=2), (x=e^{-4}), giving (Q_E=0.12525161103782373), (Q_O=0.009160891550543754), and difference (0.11609071948727998).

Now use a balanced square wave satisfying (\sigma(s-\tau)=-\sigma(s)). Each age choice contributes
\[
\sigma(t-C-j\tau-\tau X_{j+1})
=(-1)^{j+X_{j+1}}\sigma(t-C),
\]
so the four-sign product is ( (-1)^{6+\sum_jX_j}=(-1)^{\sum_jX_j}). The corrected product parity is exactly (+1) on E and (-1) on O, for any common delay, except at code discontinuities. Its period is (2\tau), its switch rate is (1/\tau), and its duty cycle is one half. The telegraph setting (r\tau=2) switches at mean rate (2/\tau) and is also balanced. With pure isotope levels, the count laws are
\[
P_E(K=0,2,4)=(1/8,6/8,1/8),\qquad P_O(K=1,3)=(1/2,1/2).
\]
With symmetric imperfect purities (a<1), the raw parity means become (+a^4) and (-a^4), so correction by (a^{-4}) still gives (+1) and (-1). The square wave strictly wins this known-τ synthetic signal contrast and has half the expected switch rate at the comparison setting. A continuous randomized phase or endpoint-excluding acquisition avoids discrete-time aliasing at ideal jumps; finite ramps and measured mixing require a new waveform kernel.

For C2, the rectangular-pulse comparator is exact: for a fixed pair separation (D), the output times at which both atom entries lie in a pulse of width (h) form the overlap of two length-(h) intervals, of measure ((h-D)_+). Averaging over histories gives integrated doubly-labelled flux divided by constant total flux (R) equal to (\mathbb E(h-D)_+=h\,\mathbb E(1-D/h)_+\). Therefore the C2 telegraph functional is not itself a new acquisition capability over a fully collected single pulse.
