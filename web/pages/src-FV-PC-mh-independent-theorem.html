# Sharp independent-noise costs for dichotomic Margenau–Hill regularization

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: internally derived analytic proof; no formal verification or external review. The quantum positivity criterion and geometric-noise machinery have close prior art. Historical priority of this combined statement is unresolved, and no extra quantum information or device advantage is claimed.

The main theorem below does **not** use the inherited difficult positive-convolution existence theorem. The latter is used only in Section 8, explicitly identified as a specialization of established internal capital.

## 1. Exact contract and result

Let \(U,V\) be bounded self-adjoint involutions on an arbitrary complex Hilbert space: \(U^2=V^2=I\). Let \(a,b>0\), \(A=aU\), and \(B=bV\). Scalar offsets in the observables only translate the reports and may be added without changing any variance statement. Assume \([U,V]\ne0\).

Write
\[
J(s,t)=\tfrac12(e^{isA}e^{itB}+e^{itB}e^{isA}),
\qquad \widehat\mu(s,t)=\int e^{i(sx+ty)}\mu(dx,dy).
\]
We seek a normalized POVM \(M\) and **independent positive scalar probability noises** \(\nu_1,\nu_2\) satisfying the equality at every real frequency
\[
\widehat M(s,t)=\widehat\nu_1(s)\widehat\nu_2(t)J(s,t).
\tag{1}
\]
Both noise variances are finite. Means may be subtracted from the reports; they play no role in the result. Dependence between the two scalar noises is not admitted by this theorem.

**Theorem 1 (complete independent-noise variance region).** The attainable pairs \((v_1,v_2)=(\operatorname{Var}\nu_1,\operatorname{Var}\nu_2)\) are exactly
\[
v_1>0,\quad v_2>0,\qquad
\boxed{v_1v_2\ge\|[A,B]\|^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
Every pair in this region is attained by explicit centered, two-sided geometric laws on \(2a\mathbb Z\) and \(2b\mathbb Z\). In particular, for \(w_1,w_2>0\),
\[
\boxed{\min(w_1v_1+w_2v_2)
=2\sqrt{w_1w_2}\,\|[A,B]\|.}
\tag{3}
\]
For the Pauli pair \(A=a\sigma_z\), \(B=b\sigma_x\), the region is \(v_1v_2\ge4a^2b^2\), and the least unweighted sum is \(4ab\).

The optimizing POVM is statistically equivalent to a four-outcome unsharp joint measurement. Its unbounded geometric magnitudes are independent classical randomness. Conditioning the reports on the four signs halves both added variances while retaining unbiasedness. Thus (2) identifies an exact cost of the scalar-convolution reporting contract, not an improvement in information or measurement precision over the finite joint measurement.

## 2. An operator reduction valid without a matrix decomposition

Set
\[
C=\tfrac12(UV+VU),\qquad
\delta=\tfrac12\|[U,V]\|\in(0,1].
\]
The operator \(C\) commutes with \(U,V\), is self-adjoint, and has norm at most one. Moreover
\[
\tfrac14[U,V]^*[U,V]=I-C^2,
\qquad \|I-C^2\|=\delta^2.
\tag{4}
\]
For real \(m,n\in[-1,1]\), define
\[
Q(m,n)=I+mU+nV+mnC.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{Q(m,n)\succeq0
\iff 1-m^2-n^2+(1-\delta^2)m^2n^2\ge0.}
\tag{5}
\]

Here is a proof retaining the infinite-dimensional case. Let \(D=I+mnC\succeq0\), \(X=mU+nV\). These commute and
\[
D^2-X^2=(1-m^2-n^2)I+m^2n^2C^2.
\tag{6}
\]
On the reducing subspace \(\mathcal H_1=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(I-C^2)}\), the polar sign \(R\) of the self-adjoint operator \(i[U,V]\) is a self-adjoint unitary. Anticommutation of \(i[U,V]\) with each of \(U,V\), and commutation of its absolute value with them, give
\[
RUR=-U,\quad RVR=-V,\quad RCR=C
\quad\text{on }\mathcal H_1.
\]
These statements follow, for example, first on the spectral subspaces \(I-C^2\ge\epsilon I\) by the bounded formula for the polar sign, then by their strong limit. Thus positivity of \(D+X\) implies positivity of \(D-X\) on \(\mathcal H_1\). On the complementary subspace \(\ker(I-C^2)\), equation (4) gives \([U,V]=0\), and
\[
D\pm X=(I\pm mU)(I\pm nV)\succeq0.
\]
Consequently \(D+X\succeq0\) implies \(D^2-X^2\succeq0\) everywhere. Conversely, simultaneous spectral calculus for the commuting self-adjoint \(D,X\), with \(D\succeq0\), shows that \(D^2-X^2\succeq0\) implies \(D\pm X\succeq0\). Finally, the bottom of the spectrum of the right side of (6) is exactly the scalar expression in (5), since
\(\inf\operatorname{Spec}(C^2)=1-\delta^2\).
This proves (5), including endpoints and continuous spectrum.

The strict inequality in (5) is equivalent to bounded invertibility and strict positivity of \(Q\) whenever \(|m|,|n|<1\). Indeed, (6) has a positive uniform lower bound exactly in that case, and \(D+X\) is bounded. At equality, (6) has zero in its spectrum and \(D-X\) is bounded, so \(D+X\) cannot be boundedly invertible on the noncommuting subspace; the polar symmetry treats the possible choice of sign.

## 3. Every independent convolution reduces to two scalar likelihood contrasts

The sharp MH operator measure has the four masses
\[
T\{(\epsilon a,\tau b)\}
=\tfrac14(I+\epsilon U+\tau V+\epsilon\tau C),
\qquad \epsilon,\tau\in\{\pm1\}.
\tag{7}
\]
For a scalar noise \(\nu_j\), let \(\nu_{j,+}\) and \(\nu_{j,-}\) be its translates by \(+a_j\) and \(-a_j\), where \(a_1=a,a_2=b\). Put
\[
\gamma_j=\tfrac12(\nu_{j,+}+\nu_{j,-}),\qquad
\xi_j=\tfrac12(\nu_{j,+}-\nu_{j,-}),\qquad
m_j=\frac{d\xi_j}{d\gamma_j},\quad
\eta_j=\|m_j\|_{L^\infty(\gamma_j)}\in[0,1].
\tag{8}
\]
No density or absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue measure is assumed. In particular, singular and lattice laws are covered. Fourier uniqueness and the finite sum (7) show that (1) is equivalent to \(M=T*(\nu_1\otimes\nu_2)\), whose operator density with respect to \(\gamma_1\otimes\gamma_2\) is exactly
\[
Q(m_1(x),m_2(y)).
\tag{9}
\]
The function
\(1-u^2-v^2+(1-\delta^2)u^2v^2\)
is nonincreasing in each of \(|u|,|v|\) on the square. The product measure allows both essential suprema to be approached simultaneously. Equations (5) and (9) therefore give the exact criterion
\[
\boxed{M\succeq0
\iff 1-\eta_1^2-\eta_2^2+(1-\delta^2)\eta_1^2\eta_2^2\ge0.}
\tag{10}
\]
A probability law cannot have \(\eta_j=0\): that would make it invariant under the nonzero translation \(2a_j\). If \(\eta_1=1\), (10) would force \(\eta_2=0\), since \(\delta>0\). Hence every successful independent regularizer has \(0<\eta_1,\eta_2<1\).

For \(\eta\in(0,1)\), the condition in (8) with \(\|m\|_\infty\le\eta\) is equivalent to the two measure inequalities
\[
r\nu(E)\le\nu(E+L)\le r^{-1}\nu(E)
\quad\text{for every Borel }E,
\qquad L=2a_j,
\quad r=\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta}.
\tag{11}
\]
This is a constraint at one fixed shift \(L\), not differential privacy for all shifts of length at most \(L\). Confusing those constraints would incorrectly import the continuous staircase optimization problem.

## 4. A sharp classical variance lemma, with a full proof

**Lemma 2.** Suppose a probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R\) satisfies (11), with \(L>0\), \(0<r<1\). Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}\nu\ge\frac{2L^2r}{(1-r)^2}.}
\tag{12}
\]
If the variance is finite, equality holds exactly for a translated two-sided geometric law
\[
\nu\{c+kL\}=\frac{1-r}{1+r}r^{|k|},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{13}
\]

If the variance is infinite there is nothing to prove. Otherwise set \(c=\mathbb E\nu\) and decompose each real point uniquely as
\(x=c+f+kL\), \(f\in[-L/2,L/2)\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\).
Let \(\nu_k\) be the measure of \(f\) coming from the \(k\)-th interval, and \(\sigma=\sum_k\nu_k\). This is a finite probability measure. Write \(p_k=d\nu_k/d\sigma\). For \(\sigma\)-almost every \(f\), the inequalities (11) hold simultaneously for all adjacent \(p_k\).

For each such fiber, let
\[
e_j^+=p_j-rp_{j-1}\ge0,\qquad
e_j^-=p_{-j}-rp_{-j+1}\ge0,\qquad j\ge1.
\]
Iteration gives the nonnegative decomposition
\[
p_k=p_0r^{|k|}
+\begin{cases}
\sum_{j=1}^k e_j^+r^{k-j},&k\ge1,\\
0,&k=0,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{-k}e_j^-r^{-k-j},&k\le-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the fiber is a positive mixture of a two-sided geometric kernel centered at \(f\), and one-sided geometric kernels starting at \(f\pm jL\), \(j\ge1\). Normalize each component separately and put \(V_*=2L^2r/(1-r)^2\).

The two-sided component has second moment about zero
\(V_*+f^2\ge V_*\).
A positive one-sided component is the law of \(f+jL+LG\), where
\(\Pr(G=k)=(1-r)r^k\), \(k\ge0\). Its second moment is
\[
(f+jL+Lr/(1-r))^2+L^2r/(1-r)^2
\ge V_*+L^2/4.
\tag{15}
\]
The inequality uses \(f+jL\ge L/2\), and its algebra is
\((1+r)^2/4+r-2r=(1-r)^2/4\).
The negative one-sided components satisfy the same bound. Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative mixture and its squared costs. Integrating over \(f\) proves (12).

Equality excludes every one-sided residual and forces \(f=0\) on the remaining mass. Normalization of the two-sided component then gives (13). Conversely, (13) satisfies (11), has mean \(c\), and has variance (12). This proves the lemma for arbitrary real-valued probability measures, not only a fixed lattice.

In the parameters of (8), equation (12) becomes
\[
v_j\ge2a_j^2(\eta_j^{-2}-1).
\tag{16}
\]

## 5. Proof and equality cases of the variance theorem

Put \(x_j=\eta_j^{-2}-1>0\). Dividing (10) by \(\eta_1^2\eta_2^2\) gives
\[
x_1x_2\ge\delta^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (16), (17), and \(\|[A,B]\|=2ab\delta\) proves (2).

Conversely, let any positive finite \(v_1,v_2\) satisfy (2), and set
\[
\eta_1=(1+v_1/(2a^2))^{-1/2},\quad
\eta_2=(1+v_2/(2b^2))^{-1/2},\quad
r_j=(1-\eta_j)/(1+\eta_j).
\tag{18}
\]
Choose
\[
\nu_j\{2a_jk\}=\frac{1-r_j}{1+r_j}r_j^{|k|}.
\tag{19}
\]
Its contrast has magnitude exactly \(\eta_j\), its variance is \(v_j\), and (17) ensures positivity by (10). Thus every feasible pair is attained. Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean proves (3), with
\(v_1=\|[A,B]\|\sqrt{w_2/w_1}\),
\(v_2=\|[A,B]\|\sqrt{w_1/w_2}\).

On the boundary of (2), equality is required in both classical lemmas and in (17). Consequently the two scalar noises are geometric laws (19), up to their arbitrary means. This is a complete equality classification within the independent-noise contract.

## 6. Finite measurement, unbounded report, and exact statistical equivalence

For the law (19), the \(j\)-th output coordinate lies at
\(X_j=\epsilon_ja_j(2K_j+1)\), where \(K_j\ge0\). The scalar measure \(\gamma_j\) gives mass
\[
\gamma_j\{\epsilon_ja_j(2k+1)\}
=\tfrac12(1-r_j)r_j^k,
\qquad m_j=\epsilon_j\eta_j.
\]
Therefore the complete POVM factors as
\[
M(\epsilon,\tau,k,\ell)
=(1-r_1)r_1^k(1-r_2)r_2^\ell F_{\epsilon\tau},
\tag{20}
\]
where
\[
F_{\epsilon\tau}
=\tfrac14(I+\epsilon\eta_1U+\tau\eta_2V
+\epsilon\tau\eta_1\eta_2C).
\tag{21}
\]
The four effects (21) are positive by (10), sum to \(I\), and have the usual unsharp marginals. From \(M\) one recovers \(F\) by taking the two signs. From \(F\) one recovers \(M\) by sampling independent geometric magnitudes \(K_j\), independent of the state and signs. These are two exact state-independent Markov kernels, so the statistical experiments are equivalent.

In particular, all state discrimination, hypothesis testing, and estimation tasks with arbitrary processing have the same attainable risks from the two experiments. No data-processing advantage can come from the geometric magnitudes.

For the specific unbiased coordinate-mean decoder, conditioning on the signs gives
\[
\widetilde X_1=\epsilon a/\eta_1,
\qquad \widetilde X_2=\tau b/\eta_2,
\]
because \(\mathbb E(2K_j+1)=(1+r_j)/(1-r_j)=\eta_j^{-1}\).
For every quantum state \(\rho\),
\[
\mathbb E_\rho X_1=\mathbb E_\rho\widetilde X_1
=\operatorname{tr}(\rho A),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\rho X_1
=a^2+v_1-[\operatorname{tr}(\rho A)]^2,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\rho\widetilde X_1
=a^2+v_1/2-[\operatorname{tr}(\rho A)]^2,
\tag{22}
\]
and likewise for coordinate two. The reduction by \(v_j/2\) is exactly the discarded classical conditional variance. For \(N\) independent copies the sample-mean variances divide by \(N\). This is an operational precision comparison under the same quantum measurement, with no claim that these two mean decoders solve every optimal estimation problem.

For the unit orthogonal Pauli pair at the equal optimum, \(v_1=v_2=2\), \(\eta_1=\eta_2=1/\sqrt2\), and \(r_1=r_2=3-2\sqrt2\). The full reports have coordinate second moment 3; the conditioned reports have coordinate second moment 2. At the maximally mixed state, using the exact scalar-convolution reports therefore needs 3/2 as many copies for the same coordinate sample-mean MSE as the conditioned reports.

## 7. Exact exponential rectangles and a sharp cost at their boundary

From (11), each nonzero finite interval of length smaller than \(L\) has translated masses bounded below by a positive multiple of \(r^{|k|}\). Hence its positive and negative exponential tails force
\[
\mathcal D(\nu_j)\subset
(-\alpha_j,\alpha_j),\qquad
\alpha_j=\frac{-\log r_j}{2a_j}
=\frac{\operatorname{artanh}\eta_j}{a_j}.
\tag{23}
\]
The endpoints themselves have divergent moments. For the geometric law (19), equality holds in (23); its moment generating function is
\[
\mathbb E e^{sZ_j}
=\frac{(1-r_j)^2}
{1-2r_j\cosh(2a_js)+r_j^2}
\quad (|s|<\alpha_j).
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently a symmetric open rectangle
\((-\alpha,\alpha)\times(-\beta,\beta)\)
can be the exact moment domain of a positive independent scalar regularizer if and only if
\[
\boxed{\delta\sinh(a\alpha)\sinh(b\beta)\le1.}
\tag{25}
\]
Sufficiency uses (19) with \(\eta_1=\tanh(a\alpha)\), \(\eta_2=\tanh(b\beta)\). Necessity follows either from (23) and (10), or from the direct Laplace argument in Section 8. Equality in (25) is permitted: all points of the *open* rectangle satisfy the strict Laplace criterion, while its corners lie on the forbidden boundary and have divergent scalar moments.

There is a sharp cost statement when the requested rectangle saturates (25). Suppose only that an independent regularizer has finite moments throughout that rectangle. Equation (23) gives \(\eta_1\ge\tanh(a\alpha)\), \(\eta_2\ge\tanh(b\beta)\). Strict monotonicity in (10) forces both inequalities to be equalities. Thus
\[
\boxed{v_1\ge2a^2\operatorname{csch}^2(a\alpha),\qquad
v_2\ge2b^2\operatorname{csch}^2(b\beta).}
\tag{26}
\]
The two geometric laws attain both bounds simultaneously, and their product is exactly \(\|[A,B]\|^2\). This is a variance-versus-tilt-domain result, not just a positivity-region observation.

## 8. Arbitrarily dependent noises: an explicit specialization of existing FU capital

For completeness, allow a general positive scalar law \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), with arbitrary dependence. The real MH Laplace transform is
\[
H(s,t)=\cosh(as)\cosh(bt)
Q(\tanh(as),\tanh(bt)).
\]
Equation (5) gives its exact uniform positivity region
\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_\delta
=\{(s,t):\delta|\sinh(as)\sinh(bt)|<1\}.}
\tag{27}
\]
In particular this extends the elementary orthogonal-Pauli observation to any two involutions, including continuous spectrum, but the calculation alone is not promoted as a new significant theorem.

If \(T*\nu=M\succeq0\) and the scalar moment at \(g\) is finite, the finite sum (7) directly gives
\[
\int e^{g\cdot x}M(dx)=m_\nu(g)H(g).
\tag{28}
\]
No analytic-continuation argument is needed here: all four translated scalar integrals are absolutely finite. Also \(M(B_R)\to I\) in operator norm, since the finite sum of scalar tail measures goes to zero in norm. For some \(R\), \(M(B_R)\succeq I/2\), and (28) is bounded below by \(e^{-|g|R}I/2\). Thus \(g\) must lie in (27), with strict uniform positivity even in infinite dimension.

The established internal FU arbitrary-open-convex-domain theorem, whose actual proof was read and pinned in `PROVENANCE.json`, supplies the converse: for an open convex \(\Omega\ni0\), there is a positive scalar regularizer with exact moment domain \(\Omega\) iff
\[
\Omega\subset\mathcal P_\delta.
\tag{29}
\]
This is a transferred theorem, not an independent new result. Its finite-order Banach-cone setting applies here to the finite signed operator measure (7), the Banach space of self-adjoint bounded operators, the positive cone, and normalization \(I\). The proof constructs a convex barrier on \(\Omega\) with Hessian prescribed by the local cone margin, takes its Legendre transform, mollifies to control the finite distributional jet, and uses \(p=e^{-W}/Z\). The gradient remains in \(\Omega\), local flatness controls the cone remainder, and separation gives divergence at every boundary tilt on an infinite cylinder. No effective scale bound is transferred.

There is no single greatest attainable open convex moment domain for a noncommuting dichotomic pair. The region (27) contains both complete axes but is not convex. Every point of (27) belongs to a symmetric open rectangle satisfying (25); a greatest convex attainable domain would contain their union and hence both axes, whose convex hull is all \(\mathbb R^2\), contradicting (27).

The largest centered open ball in the scaled coordinates \((as,bt)\) has radius
\[
r_*=\sqrt2\operatorname{arsinh}(\delta^{-1/2}).
\tag{30}
\]
Indeed \(u\mapsto\log\sinh\sqrt u\) is strictly concave for \(u>0\), since its second derivative is
\(-[\sqrt u\,\operatorname{csch}^2\sqrt u+\coth\sqrt u]/(4u^{3/2})<0\).
Thus at a fixed scaled radius \(r\), the largest product \(|\sinh(as)\sinh(bt)|\) occurs at equal absolute scaled coordinates \(r/\sqrt2\). Equation (30) follows. This radius is attainable by (29), and any larger one contains a negative Laplace value. It has no accompanying variance upper bound from the FU existence proof.

No claim is made that (2) remains sharp for dependent scalar noises. The exact dependent-noise variance region is unresolved here.

## 9. Finite output approximation with uniform state and tilt bounds

The exact geometric reports in Section 6 have unbounded support. To obtain a finite report alphabet, retain the signs and clip \(K_j\) to \(\min(K_j,N_j)\), where \(N_j\) are nonnegative integers. Let \(M_N\) be this finite postprocessing of \(M\). The alphabet has at most \(4(N_1+1)(N_2+1)\) points; the underlying quantum measurement still has four effects.

Since magnitudes are independent of the state and signs, coupling the exact and clipped outputs gives
\[
\sup_\rho\|\Pr_\rho(M)-\Pr_\rho(M_N)\|_{\rm TV}
\le r_1^{N_1+1}+r_2^{N_2+1}.
\tag{31}
\]
Here TV is \(\sup_E|P(E)-Q(E)|\). The bound concerns distributions on their common real output space. It does not claim preservation of (1) after clipping.

For a compact tilt box \(|s|\le\beta_1<\alpha_1\), \(|t|\le\beta_2<\alpha_2\), put
\[
q_j=r_je^{2a_j\beta_j}<1,\qquad
D_j=\frac{(1-r_j)e^{a_j\beta_j}}{1-q_j}.
\]
The independent magnitude law has
\(\mathbb E e^{\beta_j a_j(2K_j+1)}=D_j\),
and the same expectation restricted to \(K_j>N_j\) is \(D_jq_j^{N_j+1}\).
For the exact and clipped Laplace operators, positivity of the effects (21), the pointwise exponential difference bound, and the union bound therefore give
\[
\boxed{\sup_{|s|\le\beta_1,|t|\le\beta_2}
\left\|\int e^{sx+ty}(M-M_N)(dx,dy)\right\|
\le D_1D_2(q_1^{N_1+1}+q_2^{N_2+1}).}
\tag{32}
\]
More explicitly, on a fixed sign pair the difference is zero unless clipping occurs, and its absolute value is at most \(e^{\beta_1|X_1|+\beta_2|X_2|}\). Summing the effects over signs gives \(I\), which yields the norm bound without a dimension factor.

For a target error \(\varepsilon>0\), it suffices to choose
\[
N_j+1\ge
\frac{\log(2D_1D_2/\varepsilon)}
{2a_j(\alpha_j-\beta_j)}
\tag{33}
\]
whenever the numerator is positive; otherwise \(N_j=0\) already satisfies the displayed sufficient inequality after direct checking. The required output range is \(a_j(2N_j+1)\). Thus finite distributional accuracy has a geometric cost, while uniform exponential-moment accuracy worsens as the requested tilt approaches the actual moment boundary. Equations (31) and (32) are constructive accuracy statements, not lower bounds for all finite-output approximations.

These formulas charge classical magnitude sampling and output range separately from the four-outcome measurement. They do not validate a physical instrument, state preparation, calibration, finite-bit arithmetic, or an exact sampling circuit.

## 10. What has and has not been established

The substantive new internal result is the complete independent-noise variance region (2), with its equality classification, boundary rectangle cost (26), and an explicit finite-output error budget. It is proved directly rather than inferred from a qualitative existence theorem or finite numerics.

The finite effects (21), their compatibility criterion, and the geometric noise law have substantial primary prior art. The strongest defensible consequence is that this fixed scalar-convolution contract imposes a precisely quantified classical reporting overhead while adding no information to the finite measurement. An exact combined priority comparison remains open. The dependent-noise variance optimization and unrestricted bounded-observable cost problem remain open, as do useful acquisition-inclusive advantages.
