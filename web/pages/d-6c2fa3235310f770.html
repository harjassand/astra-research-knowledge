## Exact question and interface

Given integers `1 <= a < b`, produce a positive Egyptian-fraction expansion of `a/b`. The source principal theorem requires distinct integer denominators and `O(log log b)` terms. It imposes no denominator or runtime bound.

New deduction in this investigation: the source's **quantitative prescribed-denominator** mechanism already applies to every unmarked `a/b`. It implies the explicit eventual uniform bound

`length <= (257/log 2 + epsilon) log log b`

for every fixed epsilon > 0 and all sufficiently large b, uniformly in a. This route bypasses the main density/descent/divisor-moment argument. Before removing repetitions, it also gives denominators `<= exp(O(log b log log b))` and total denominator bit volume `O(log b (log log b)^2)`.

The point is a shorter proof route and a stronger cost interface for the repeated representation. It is an implicit consequence of the source's lemmas, not a separately priority-checked mathematical discovery.

## 1. Rational-divisor supply, reconstructed

Let `ell(v) = ceil(4 log(v)/log 2)` and `P(v)` be the product of the first ell(v) rational primes. For sufficiently large b, put

`K_b = P(b^4)^2 * floor(log log b)!`.

PNT gives

`log K_b <= (32/log 2 + o(1)) log b log log b`.

We now prove that every integer `1 <= s <= b^4` is a sum of at most 16 positive rationals `e_i/t_i` with `e_i | K_b`. No unit fractions are assumed at this stage, and different t_i are allowed.

Fix a sufficiently large odd u and let A be the positive divisors of P(u). Then `N=|A|=2^ell(u) >= u^4`. Call p <= u exceptional if the image A_p in F_p has size at most p^(3/4). For such p, the number of ordered distinct pairs in A congruent mod p is at least

`N^2/|A_p| - N >= N^2/(2u^(3/4))`.

Every nonzero difference between two members of A has absolute value below P(u), and therefore has at most log(P(u))/log 2 distinct prime divisors. Counting pair-prime incidences both ways bounds the number B(u) of exceptional primes by

`B(u) <= 2u^(3/4) log(P(u))/log 2 = O(u^(3/4) log u log log u)`.

Quantitative Vinogradov supplies at least `c u^2/(log u)^3` ordered prime triples with sum u, for every sufficiently large odd u and one absolute c>0. Its singular series is uniformly positive on odd u. At most `3u B(u)` triples contain an exceptional prime. This is `o(u^2/(log u)^3)`, so one triple consists of three nonexceptional primes for the same A.

For such a prime p, put H=A_p and h=|H|>p^(3/4). If U is the product of two independent uniform members of H, then for every nonzero r mod p, additive orthogonality and Cauchy-Schwarz yield

`|E exp(2 pi i r U/p)| <= sqrt(p)/h < p^(-1/4)`.

For five independent U_i, Fourier inversion gives

`Pr(sum U_i = 0 mod p) >= [1-(p-1)p^(-5/4)]/p > 0`.

Choose witnessing residues and lift each of their ten factors to a divisor of P(u). Their five integer products e_i divide P(u)^2 and have positive sum divisible by p. Thus with `t=(sum e_i)/p`,

`p = sum_{i=1}^5 e_i/t`.

Applying this to the three good primes represents odd u using 15 rational divisors. For even s use u=s-1 and append 1. All e_i divide P(b^4)^2 because ell is nondecreasing. The bounded initial exceptional integers s divide floor(log log b)! once b is sufficiently large. They therefore have one-term representations s=s/1. This proves the simultaneous 16-term supply for every s <= b^4.

Extra checked size fact: the construction can always choose `t_i <= 5 K_b`, since each of the five e_i is at most K_b; the appended and small-integer terms have t_i=1. This is a deduction from the constructed witnesses, not a general statement about all supply representations.

The only non-elementary inputs in this supply proof are PNT and quantitative Vinogradov. The latter was checked in the author-hosted Kumchev-Tolev survey, Theorem 1, printed p.3, equations (1.3)-(1.4). The full 74-page survey was not read. Its recoverable PDF is stored under our sources directory. The Vinogradov theorem itself is used as standard prior mathematics; it is not reproved here.

## 2. Ordinary unreduced greedy prefix

Start with `(R,q)=(a,b)` and let `T=b K_b`. While R>0 and q<T, set

`n=ceil(q/R),  R'=n R-q,  q'=n q`.

Do not cancel common factors. Then `0 <= R' < R`, `n <= q`, and q'<=q^2. If the remainder reaches zero, we already have a distinct greedy expansion. Otherwise, at the first crossing of T,

`0 < R < b`, `b K_b <= q < (b K_b)^2`.

The greedy denominators are distinct: every positive remainder after removing 1/n is smaller than 1/n. The first positive remainder is below 1/2 and subsequent remainders decrease at least by squaring. Consequently the prefix has

`j <= 1 + ceil(log_2(log T/log 2)) = (1/log 2 + o(1)) log log b`

terms. Its final positive remainder R/q is smaller than every prefix reciprocal.

## 3. Divisor density of q

Any integer q obtained from b by replacements `q -> n q` with `n <= q+1` has a divisor in `[w/b,w]` for every real `1 <= w <= q`.

Proof by induction. At q=b the divisor 1 works. For q'=nq, use the old divisor for w<=q, and multiply the old divisor chosen at w/n by n when w>=n. The only possible uncovered gap is q<w<n. There n<=q+1, so q itself is a divisor of q' in `[w/b,w]`, because `w<n<=q+1<=bq`. This also covers real w, and no coprimality of multiplier and q is required.

Our greedy q satisfies exactly this hypothesis.

## 4. Grouping and exact tail conversion

Set `X=R K_b`. The stopping rule and R<b give `0<X<bK_b<=q`. As long as a current integer X_i exceeds b^4, select a divisor d_i of q in

`[X_i/b^3, X_i/b^2]`

using the divisor-density statement at w=X_i/b^2. Put `s_i=floor(X_i/d_i)` and remove the group d_i s_i. Then

`b^2 <= s_i <= b^3`, `0 <= X_{i+1}<d_i<=X_i/b^2`.

When `0<X_i<=b^4`, use the last group d_i=1, s_i=X_i. There are at most

`G <= log X/(2log b)+2 <= (16/log 2 + o(1)) log log b`

groups. Every s_i is within the 16-term supply range. Write `s_i=sum_a e_{i,a}/t_{i,a}` with e_{i,a}|K_b. Then

`R/q = X/(q K_b) = sum_{i,a} 1/[(q/d_i)(K_b/e_{i,a})t_{i,a}]`.

These denominators are integers. The tail uses at most 16G terms, and repeated denominators are allowed at this stage. The exact divisibilities used are d_i|q and e_{i,a}|K_b; t_i need not divide anything.

Combining prefix and tail yields

`j+16G <= (257/log 2 + o(1)) log log b`.

## 5. Distinctness and size accounting

For any rational x in (0,1), a positive k-term unit-fraction representation can be made distinct without increasing k (indeed the source Takenouchi replacements retain exactly k). If m is duplicated, replace its two reciprocals by

`2/(2p) = 1/(p+1) + 1/[p(p+1)]`,

`2/(2p+1) = 1/(p+1) + 1/[(p+1)(2p+1)]`.

The total below one excludes duplicated 2 and denominator 1. Each replacement strictly increases the sum of denominators. There are finitely many ordered k-tuples representing fixed positive rational x: the first denominator is at most k/x, and induction applies after choosing it. Termination follows. This is the source's classical argument, not a new normalization theorem.

One may normalize just the tail. Every positive term in any tail expansion is at most its sum R/q, which is smaller than every prefix reciprocal, so a normalized tail cannot collide with the prefix.

Before normalization, every tail denominator is at most

`5 q K_b^2 < 5 b^2 K_b^4`.

The prefix denominators are below bK_b. Hence the repeated expansion has O(log log b) terms, maximum denominator exp(O(log b log log b)), and total denominator bit volume O(log b (log log b)^2). Distinctness cleanup is not claimed to preserve this sharper bound.

For any sorted positive k-term expansion of a/b, even with repetitions, the i-th denominator obeys

`n_i <= (k b)^(2^(i-1))`.

Indeed the positive residual after i-1 terms has a positive integer numerator over `b product_{j<i} n_j`, so it is at least its reciprocal, while the remaining unit fractions sum to at most k/n_i. Induction gives the displayed bound. Therefore total denominator bit volume is at most

`(2^k-1) log_2(kb)+k`.

Using k <= (257/log 2 + epsilon) log log b gives a polynomial bit-size bound of order `(log b)^(258+delta)` for any fixed delta>0 after adjusting epsilon and the threshold. This is extremely large, but it prevents unrestricted denominators from being misreported as necessarily unbounded bit size at fixed short length.

## Acquisition obstruction and costs

No polynomial-time construction is proved. The supply proof searches divisors of primorials and good three-prime representations. Enumerating all divisors of P(b^4) costs `2^ell(b^4) = b^16` up to a constant factor: exponential in the input bit length. Enumerating candidate prime triples up to b^4 is also exponential in that length. These are concrete sufficient exhaustive searches, not lower bounds against better algorithms.

The source's main residue-list route has a different gap: the guaranteed useful fraction of choices is rho/2 with rho=exp(-10^-4 floor(log b/log log b)). Inverting that guarantee produces an upper expected-search bound exponential in log b/log log b; it does not prove a lower bound on actual search time. No polynomial-time claim follows from this success guarantee.

Nearest prior art checked directly: Tenenbaum-Yokota (1990), printed p.151, gives length `(1+epsilon) log b/log log b` and maximum denominator `4b(log b)^2 log log b`. That older result already provides polynomial-size distinct certificates, with stronger denominator control and more terms. It is not displaced in applications where the largest denominator matters.

## Downstream use and next falsifiable step

The reusable tool is a supply of positive rational divisors under one auxiliary integer, paired with divisor density of an unreduced greedy denominator. It converts a small universal integer supply into sparse exact positive reciprocal representations. Its interface may be useful for exact probability mass decomposition or constructions requiring few reciprocal pieces, provided acquisition and denominator costs are charged.
