PRIME-CHARACTERISTIC IDEMPOTENT CONFLICT: COMPLETE CITED-ROUTE RECONSTRUCTION
Selective Sol audit; 2026-10-08; task /root/sol_idempotent_conflict; no descendants.

Outcome and scientific status

The printed Bartels-Lueck-Reich (BLR) prime-characteristic assertion is genuinely broader than the proof supplied by its advertised companion reference. The companion's Formanek/Burger-Valette route proves class-trace vanishing and tau(e) in {0,1} in characteristic p; its final passage to e in {0,1} is a characteristic-zero faithful-trace step. The companion's separate K0 route explicitly requires stable finiteness. Neither cited route, reconstructed below, establishes idempotent triviality for an arbitrary torsion-free hyperbolic group over F_p without that extra detector.

This is a precise missing-premise finding in the cited proof chain. It is NOT a counterexample to the BLR printed assertion, NOT a proof that no other argument could establish it, and NOT a validation of N129. The current truth of the isolated unconditional characteristic-p assertion remains UNKNOWN in this audit. The main hyperbolic Farrell-Jones theorem is not challenged by these calculations.

A stronger algebraic control is proved here: the bicyclic algebra T=k<a,b | ab=1> has augmentation to k and K0(k)->K0(T) an isomorphism, yet e=1-ba is a proper nonzero idempotent with augmentation zero and [eT]=0. Thus even the exact integer K0 isomorphism, together with augmentation, does not supply projective cancellation. T is not being asserted to be a group algebra.

1. Literal scope and the advertised dependency chain

BLR, arXiv math/0701434v2 (29 October 2007), Invent. Math. 172 (2008), 29-70, states its hyperbolic all-coefficient Farrell-Jones theorem at local source lines 298-309. Its Applications preamble at lines 475-487 refers the proofs of its application assertions to BLR's companion. Under all-ring Farrell-Jones, the prime-characteristic bullet at lines 522-529 asserts idempotent triviality for a commutative field of characteristic p, or for a skew-field of that characteristic when G is sofic, assuming every finite subgroup is a p-group. A torsion-free hyperbolic G and F_p satisfy the literal first branch.

The companion, arXiv math/0703548, J. Topol. 1 (2008), 57-86, has two relevant routes. Theorem 0.12 (journal Theorem 1.12) at local source lines 893-911 uses rational K0 induction plus stable finiteness, a characteristic-zero field, or a sofic group with skew-field coefficients. The discussion at lines 925-936 retains soficity in its characteristic-p K0 application. The subsequent attribution at lines 975-986 asserts a broader characteristic-p Formanek criterion and then trivial idempotents for all fields and torsion-free hyperbolic groups.

The original sources cited for that attribution do not contain its characteristic-p final conclusion. Formanek's Theorems 1 and 9 explicitly have characteristic zero. Burger-Valette's positive-characteristic Lemma 4.1 concludes tau(e)=0 or1; Proposition 4.2 concludes trivial idempotents in C[G]. Its proof invokes Kaplansky's theorem on complex traces. Remark 4.3 applies that conclusion to C[G] for torsion-free hyperbolic G.

2. Independent reconstruction of the positive-characteristic trace argument

Let k be a commutative field of characteristic p and G a group. For x in G, write C_x for its conjugacy class and define

    T_x(sum_g r_g g) = sum_{g in C_x} r_g,
    tau(r) = r_1,
    epsilon(r) = sum_g r_g.

These are well-defined because each group-ring element has finite support. T_x is cyclic: gh and hg are conjugate for any g,h in G, and bilinearity gives T_x(uv)=T_x(vu). Consequently every T_x annihilates additive commutators.

For any cyclic k-linear trace T on a k-algebra, expansion and cyclic rotation of length-p words give

    T((u_1+...+u_s)^p) = sum_i T(u_i^p).

Indeed, rotation orbits have length one or p; the latter contribute p equal trace values, hence zero. Length-one words are u_i^p. Iterating this identity gives, for r=sum_g r_g g and n>=1,

    T_x(r^{p^n}) = sum_g r_g^{p^n} T_x(g^{p^n}).                 (A)

Assume that no nonidentity element y is conjugate to y^{p^m} for any m>=1. For a fixed x!=1 and a fixed g, there is at most one n>=1 with g^{p^n} in C_x. Otherwise n<q would imply that y=g^{p^n} is a nonidentity element conjugate to y^{p^{q-n}}, contrary to the hypothesis. There are only finitely many g in the support of e, so there is an n for which none of their p^n powers belongs to C_x. For an idempotent e, e^{p^n}=e, and (A) then gives T_x(e)=0.

If epsilon(e) is an idempotent in the field k, it belongs to {0,1}. Summing the finitely many nonzero conjugacy contributions yields

    epsilon(e) = tau(e) + sum_{C_x != C_1} T_x(e) = tau(e).

Thus tau(e) is 0 or1. This exhausts the trace proof. It gives no implication that e itself equals tau(e).

For torsion-free hyperbolic G, the conjugate-power hypothesis is valid: an infinite-order element has positive stable word translation length, stable translation length is invariant under conjugacy, and it scales by m under the m-th power. Conjugacy of y and y^{p^n} would force l(y)=p^n l(y), impossible when l(y)>0. This geometric fact supports the hypothesis of the trace lemma; it does not add trace faithfulness.

3. The characteristic-zero final step, and why it cannot be imported

For completeness, here is the missing detector in the field where it is valid. A finite set of characteristic-zero coefficients generates a finitely generated field extension of Q, which embeds into C. A group-ring idempotent then acts by left convolution in the group von Neumann algebra. This algebra has the normalized faithful cyclic trace tau.

Let q be the orthogonal projection onto the closed range of an idempotent e. The range is closed because e is bounded and idempotent. The projection q belongs to the von Neumann algebra generated by e. Since qe=e and eq=q, cyclicity gives tau(e)=tau(qe)=tau(eq)=tau(q). If e is proper and nonzero, q is proper and nonzero, so faithfulness gives 0<tau(q)<1. Hence tau(e) in {0,1} forces e=0 or1.

This step uses an ordered real projection dimension and characteristic-zero embedding. A unital field embedding F_p->C cannot exist. In characteristic p an ordinary trace records dimensions modulo p: for example diag(I_p,0) in M_{p+1}(F_p) is a proper nonzero idempotent with trace zero. Even in a group algebra, F_2[C_3] has the proper idempotent g+g^2 with identity coefficient zero. These controls do not have the torsion-free/conjugate-power hypotheses and do not refute the group-specific statement; they identify the unsupported universal trace-faithfulness inference.

4. Exact reconstruction of the K0 route

Let A=kG and let i:k->A and epsilon:A->k be the coefficient inclusion and augmentation. They satisfy epsilon o i=id. Assume i_*:K0(k) tensor Q -> K0(A) tensor Q is an isomorphism. For an idempotent e in A set P=eA, a right projective module.

If epsilon(e)=0, then epsilon_*(P)=0. Since i_* is an isomorphism whose left inverse is epsilon_*, it follows that [P] tensor1=0. Therefore there is an integer m>=1 with m[P]=0 in K0(A). By definition of group completion, there is a finitely generated projective Q with

    P^{direct sum m} direct sum Q ~= Q.

Choose a projective complement Q' with Q direct sum Q' ~= A^n. Adding Q' gives

    P^{direct sum m} direct sum A^n ~= A^n.                   (B)

If A is stably finite, A^n cannot absorb a nonzero module, so (B) forces P^{direct sum m}=0 and P=0. If epsilon(e)=1, apply the same argument to 1-e. This proves BLR's lemma without assuming a priori cancellation.

The role of stable finiteness can also be characterized exactly. If [P]=0 for a finitely generated projective, the preceding completion/complement construction gives P direct sum A^n ~= A^n. Stable finiteness implies P=0. Conversely, if every zero-class projective is zero, a one-sided inverse in any matrix ring has an idempotent defect whose image is a zero-class projective; its disappearance proves stable finiteness. This is the companion's Remark 5.2, reconstructed at the precise needed strength.

The companion supplies stable finiteness for characteristic-zero fields and for sofic groups with skew-field coefficients. It does not supply it for arbitrary characteristic-p fields and arbitrary hyperbolic G. In the N129 setting, stable finiteness would already contradict the proposed one-sided inverse. It cannot be introduced as an innocent consequence of K0 induction.

5. What the finite-subgroup/Farrell-Jones computation actually supplies

For a finite p-group H and a field k of characteristic p, kH is local. One elementary proof uses induction on |H|. Choose a central element z of order p. The central element z-1 satisfies (z-1)^p=0, and quotienting by it gives k[H/<z>]. By induction the quotient augmentation ideal is nilpotent; pulling back and then using (z-1)^p=0 proves nilpotence of the augmentation ideal of kH. Thus every element outside that ideal is invertible, and finite projectives over kH are free. Consequently K0(kH)=Z, generated by [kH]. Induction/conjugation between finite subgroups sends this generator to the generator. The trivial subgroup maps to every finite subgroup, so the relevant colimit is Z.

Combining this with the characteristic-p finite-subgroup induction theorem gives K0(kG)[1/p]=Z[1/p]. This permits p-primary zero classes; for a zero-augmentation projective P it implies p^r[P]=0 for some r. It does not force P=0.

For torsion-free G and the regular coefficient field k, the stronger integral statement applies: K0(k)->K0(kG) is an isomorphism, so K0(kG)=Z. Augmentation identifies the integer class of eA with the dimension of its scalar extension epsilon_*(eA), hence [eA]=0 or[A]. The complement has class zero in the second case. This is still a statement in a group completion.

The Hattori-Stallings map is also group-completed information. Under the integral K0 isomorphism it takes eA to epsilon(e) times the identity conjugacy class, reproducing T_x(e)=0 for x!=1 and tau(e)=epsilon(e). In characteristic p its scalar integer information is reduced modulo p. In the p-local statement one cannot even deduce trace vanishing merely from p^r[P]=0, since multiplication by p already annihilates the characteristic-p trace target.

For the actual proposed N129 witnesses, no assembly theorem is needed to see the invisibility. From ab=1, ac=0, c!=0 one gets ba!=1 and

    e=1-ba=ab-ba,
    e^2=e,
    ec=c,
    e!=0, e!=1,
    R=eR direct sum baR,
    baR=bR ~= R  (maps r->br and x->ax),
    R ~= R direct sum eR,
    [eR]=0,
    T_x(e)=0 for every conjugacy class x,
    epsilon(e)=0.

Here ba cannot be zero since (ab)^2=a(ba)b=1. Every statement about vanishing class or trace is automatically satisfied by this defect. A different detector is required to exclude it. Thus the proposed witness is compatible with the computed K0 and class-trace data, while it conflicts with the literal separate idempotent assertion.

6. A complete algebraic countermodel to K0-plus-augmentation cancellation

This control addresses the strongest generic repair directly. Let k be any commutative field and

    T=k<a,b | ab=1>.

The single rewrite ab->1 terminates and has no overlapping critical pairs. The normal monomials b^i a^j, i,j>=0, form a k-basis. Consequently e=1-ba is nonzero and proper. It satisfies e^2=e, ae=0, eb=0. Sending a,b to1 defines an augmentation T->k with epsilon(e)=0. As in the preceding calculation, T ~= T direct sum eT, so [eT]=0.

We now prove that K0(k)->K0(T) is nevertheless an isomorphism.

Represent T on V=direct sum_{n>=0} kv_n by b(v_n)=v_{n+1}, a(v_0)=0, a(v_{n+1})=v_n. This representation is faithful: for a fixed diagonal displacement i-j, the monomial b^i a^j acts by the corresponding shift on indices n>=j and is zero below j. A finite linear combination can therefore be recovered from successive differences of its values along each diagonal. Define

    E_ij=b^i e a^j.

These act as matrix units, E_ij(v_n)=delta_{jn}v_i. Their span I is the ideal M_infinity(k) of finite matrices. The quotient has both ab=1 and ba=1, so

    T/I ~= k[t,t^{-1}] = B,    b->t, a->t^{-1}.

Equivalently, the kernel is exactly the ideal generated by e; the relations E_ijE_kl=delta_jk E_il show that ideal is I.

Let P be any finitely generated projective right T-module and represent it by an idempotent F in M_N(T). Its image bar(F) over B has free image and free complement because B is a PID. Thus some U in GL_N(B) conjugates bar(F) to D=diag(I_r,0). Stabilize F by one zero coordinate, and U by det(U)^{-1}; the enlarged conjugating matrix has determinant one and still diagonalizes bar(F) direct sum0. The Euclidean algorithm over B expresses it as a product of elementary matrices. Each elementary matrix lifts to an invertible elementary matrix over T because T->B is surjective. Conjugating the stabilized F by this lifted product gives

    F'=D+K,    K in M_{N+1}(I).

Choose m so large that every entry of K is supported within the first m rows and columns of V, and put

    q_m=sum_{j=0}^{m-1} E_jj = 1-b^m a^m,
    Q=diag(q_m,...,q_m).

Then QK=KQ=K and DQ=QD. Hence F' commutes with Q and decomposes as two orthogonal idempotents,

    F'=QF'Q + (1-Q)D.

The first summand belongs to the finite matrix corner Q M_{N+1}(T) Q ~= M_{(N+1)m}(k). If its ordinary finite-dimensional rank is d, its T-projective image is isomorphic to a direct sum of d copies of eT; this follows by diagonalizing within that corner and using its matrix units.

The second summand has r copies of (1-q_m)T. The identities a^m b^m=1 and b^m a^m=1-q_m give (1-q_m)T ~= T. Therefore

    [P]=d[eT]+r[T]=r[T] in K0(T).

All of K0(T) is generated by [T]. Augmentation sends [T] to1 in K0(k)=Z, so this generator has infinite order and induces K0(T)~=Z, with coefficient inclusion and augmentation inverse. This proves the announced control.

This algebra has precisely the projective absorption that a faithful detector would need to exclude. It is not a counterexample with group-ring structure or to the Farrell-Jones theorem. The proof demonstrates why the K0-and-augmentation premises, taken alone, cannot make that exclusion. No assertion of novelty is made; the algebraic Toeplitz/bicyclic example is standard, and the argument is reconstructed here for the exact mechanism.

7. Finite coefficient reduction and remaining possible gates

A nontrivial idempotent over an arbitrary characteristic-p field can be specialized to one over a finite field without losing nontriviality, provided only that the original coefficients are supplied. Form the finitely generated F_p-domain A of its finitely many coefficients. Choose a nonzero coefficient of e and of1-e, with product s!=0. A maximal ideal of A[1/s] has finite residue field by the weak Nullstellensatz. The specialized idempotent preserves those two nonzero coefficients. The same construction preserves ab=1, ac=0 and a chosen nonzero coefficient of c. There is no uniform computational degree/bit-cost claim for arbitrary coefficient input in this existence proof. For N129 the coefficients are already in F_2.

This reduction means that moving to finite coefficient fields does not itself create faithfulness: the defect persists, and its commutator traces remain zero. Nor can scalar extension into characteristic zero be silently assumed; any such unital map would send p*1=0 to p*1!=0. Lifting an idempotent to a p-adic completion is a different statement and does not yield a finite-support characteristic-zero group-ring lift. No such lift is acquired here.

Verified sufficient routes are: the companion's stable-finiteness/sofic hypotheses, characteristic-zero positivity, or an additional group-specific positive rank/cancellation theorem. No independent positive-characteristic detector for arbitrary hyperbolic groups was found in the advertised primary dependency chain. This audit does not prove that such a detector cannot exist elsewhere.

To settle the remaining mathematical conflict requires either an actual independent proof of that unconditional detector, a correction/clarification of the published assertion, or an independently checked N129 counterexample package. N129 still has separate graph, probability, fixed-boundary disk and witness-acquisition gates. Merely observing the published statement's literal scope cannot select which mathematical side fails.
