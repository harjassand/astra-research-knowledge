## What is gained

For fixed rational subspaces S_i of Q^r, an explicitly sized random prime
with polynomial bit length preserves the entire nonnegative subspace-packing
cone with probability at least1-delta. This is simultaneous over all real
weights, not a finite test set or a statement about just input matroid ranks.
No enormous algebraic certificate is computed by the acquisition procedure.
The procedure has expected polynomial bit time with exact primality testing.

The scope is a probabilistic reduction, not a deterministic certificate that
a chosen prime is good. With Hirai's bounded-objective additive algorithm it
gives original-field optimal VALUE / gap-membership approximation; it does
not lift finite-field optimizing flags or decide exact membership in time
polynomial in binary denominators.

## Independent reconstruction

Clear rational denominators columnwise, charging their bit growth. Assume
each integer r-by-d_i matrix B_i has full column rank and height <=2^H.
Remove d_i=0 inputs temporarily. A k-plane W belongs to one of binomial(r,k)
standard Grassmann charts Y with pivot rows equal to I_k. Over EVERY field,
the remaining k(r-k) entries give actual independent k-planes, so this chart
requires neither division by unknown minors nor extra inequations.

The conditions dim(im B_i intersect W)>=a_i are the vanishing of minors of
[B_i Y] of size d_i+k-a_i+1, provided B_i retains rank. There are at most
m8^r equations, degree<=r, coefficient height log<=rH log2+log(r!),
and <=r^2 variables. The latter height includes ALL determinant summands;
minor expansion never creates coefficients with super-polynomial bit height.
There are <=2^r(r+1)^m chart/profile pairs, including k=0,r boundaries.

For each pair EMPTY over C, arithmetic Nullstellensatz gives a nonzero
integer A in the ideal of its chart equations. Directly read primary
Krick--Pardo--Sombra math/9911094v1, IntroTheorem1: its logarithmic height is
at most4n(n+1)d^n[h+log s+(n+7)d log(n+1)]. The number of prime divisors
is at most log_2|A|. For charts with no variables, emptiness means at least
one nonzero constant equation; that constant itself is a certificate.
Input rank failures are bounded using one nonzero d_i-minor per input.

Using the candidate's integer definitions N=r^2,d=2r,h*,l*,C,G,B, the
inequalities log s<=l*, h<=h* and log(n+1)<=N+1+d show B dominates the
TOTAL count of rank primes and exceptional primes for every empty pair.
The factor2 safely changes natural logarithms to base2. Computing B needs
neither finding empty pairs nor obtaining any A. Its bit length, unlike its
numerical value, is polynomial in input size.

Let p avoid that exceptional union. Previously gated lattice specialization
gives P_Fp subset P_Q, and characteristic-zero Galois descent gives
P_Q=P_C. If an x>=0 in P_Q is excluded by a finite-field k-plane W, take
its exact intersection profile a. The corresponding complex incidence
locus must be empty: a complex W' with all intersections>=a would also
violate the SAME nonnegative x inequality. But W lies in one standard
chart and solves its equations modulo p, forcing p|A, contradiction.
This proves the reverse inclusion. Nonnegativity of x is essential.
No rational realization of arbitrary complex incidence profiles is asserted.

## Probability and computational interface

The primary Rosser--Schoenfeld1962 Corollary1, (3.5)--(3.6), read from the
preserved printed-page69 excerpt, bounds the number of primes in the L-bit
interval below by2^(L-1)/(4L), for L>=6. Subtracting the two stated prime
count inequalities verifies that conservative bound directly. Exact uniform
odd-candidate rejection with deterministic polynomial-time primality testing
therefore takes at most2L trials on average and returns a uniform prime.

For q=ceil(1/delta), b=bit_length(B), and L=4[b+bit_length(q)+20],
the bad-prime probability is at most4BL/2^(L-1). The definition gives
Bq<2^(L/4-20), making this probability <1/q<=delta. All giant integer
bound calculations have polynomial bit length. Deterministic AKS testing is
an existence/complexity primitive here; no practical fast implementation
or empirical runtime is claimed. A Miller--Rabin implementation must add
its own failure budget and must not be described as the same exact sampler.

One sampled good prime protects any number of adaptive queries with the
SAME input subspaces, since equality is of cones. Changing B_i requires a
fresh guarantee. A finite-field dual flag remains a finite-field object.
The additive-optimum consequence requires bounded numerical c_max, as in
the BL choice c_i=d_i<=r; it does not turn a pseudopolynomial general
objective algorithm into a binary-polynomial one.
