The useful operational question is: if a fixed effective zero-free boundary θ<1 for all Dirichlet characters is valid, what concrete deterministic arithmetic procedure follows, and what is its charged cost? The answer below uses prime input p; it does not solve factoring composite inputs.

## 1. The prime-row extraction is algebraically sound, conditionally

Let A_u(D) be a character family at coefficient scale D with one target A_1(D). Suppose probe rows indexed by distinct prime ideals have

    u=p^m,  N(p)∈(Y/2,Y],   Y=H^(1/m),
    |A_{p^m}(D)−A_1(D)|≤C D/Y,
    J ≍ Y/log Y,
    Σ_{N(u)≤H}|A_u(D)|² ≤ C D H D^ε.

The row equality is exact for characters whose order divides m, except for their zero values at nonunits. The displayed error counts coefficient ideals divisible by p. Averaging |A_1|²≤2|A_{p^m}|²+2C²D²/Y² gives

    |A_1(D)|² ≪ D H log(Y)/Y + D²/Y².

For H=D^(1+v), absorb the logarithm into D^ε. The first term gives

    |A_1(D)| ≪ D^{1−1/(2m)+v(1/2−1/(2m))+ε},

and the second gives D^{1−(1+v)/m}. For the sextic rows m=6, the leading exponent is exactly 11/12+5v/12+ε. Letting the fixed positive v and exponent loss be arbitrarily small gives the stated 11/12+ε cancellation. This does not verify the mean-square bound itself.

**Interface obstruction.** A bare L2 family estimate and J duplicate probes cannot force a pointwise bound stronger than (energy/J)^(1/2). Artificial rows with value T on those J probes and zero elsewhere have energy J T² and exact probe agreement. With J≈D^(1/m), the energy D² allows T≈D^{1−1/(2m)}. This is a sharpness statement for the information in this abstract extraction interface, not a counterexample using actual arithmetic coefficients. Improvement to 7/8 needs an additional arithmetic or moment input; optimizing the final Cauchy–Schwarz step alone cannot supply it.

## 2. Mellin continuation does not need conductor-uniform constants

Suppose for every smooth compactly supported W and each fixed target Hecke character ν,

    A_1(D) ≪_{ν,W,ε} D^{θ+ε}.

At a hypothetical zero ρ with Re(ρ)>θ choose W(y)=y^{−ρ}φ(y), where φ≥0 is nonzero and supported in (1,2). Then the Mellin transform of W is nonzero at ρ. The scale transform

    M_W(s)=∫_0^∞ A_1(D) D^{−s} dD/D

is holomorphic in Re(s)>θ. At Re(s)>1 termwise integration gives

    M_W(s)=W_hat(s)/L^S(s,ν),

where S is the fixed omitted prime set. Continue the identity L^S M_W=W_hat and evaluate at ρ. The omitted Euler factors are nonzero in Re(s)>0, so this gives the contradiction 0=W_hat(ρ). Thus fixed-character implied constants are enough to prove a common zero-free boundary if the power exponent is common.

This distinction matters: a family theorem can have thresholds depending badly on ν and still exclude every zero of every fixed ν. It does not automatically yield an explicit finite computation of all those thresholds.

## 3. A fixed zero-free boundary gives a quantified nonresidue search

**Conditional hypothesis Z_θ.** Every primitive Dirichlet L-function has no nontrivial zero with Re(ρ)>θ, where 1/2≤θ<1 is a known constant. Put δ=1−θ. Functional equations for χ and its conjugate then place every nontrivial zero in δ≤Re(ρ)≤θ.

For a nonprincipal primitive character χ of conductor q define

    S_χ(x)=Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n)χ(n)(1−n/x).

The weighted Perron kernel is x^s/[s(s+1)]. Shifting the contour of −L'/L(s,χ) gives the zero terms

    −Σ_ρ x^ρ/[ρ(ρ+1)]

plus the residues at zero, at −1, and the trivial zeros. The standard character functional equation and zero count give, under Z_θ,

    |S_χ(x)| ≤ C_δ x^θ log(q+2) + C_δ log(qx).       (S)

Here is the dependency accounting for this familiar explicit-formula step. The ordinary zero-count bound N_χ(T+1)−N_χ(T)≪log(q(T+3)) makes Σ_ρ(1+|Imρ|²)^−1≪log(q+2). Since Reρ≥δ, |ρ(ρ+1)|^−1≤C_δ/(1+|Imρ|²), proving the bound for the zero sum. The finite part of L'/L at zero is O_δ(log q): subtract the partial-fraction expansions at 1 and2, whose difference is absolutely bounded by C_δΣ_ρ(1+|Imρ|²)^−1; the Euler product bounds L'/L at2, and the functional equation transports the bound from1 to0. A possible trivial zero at0 contributes an additional log x; the −1 residue and the remaining trivial-zero series contribute O(log(qx)). No Landau–Siegel denominator remains because δ is fixed. This proves(S) from the classical analytic continuation, functional equation, zero count, and Z_θ. It does not prove Z_θ.

Take χ to be the Legendre character modulo an odd prime p. If every integer up to x<p were a quadratic residue, then all primes up to x have χ=1, as do their powers, giving S_χ(x)=S_1(x). A classical effective Chebyshev lower bound, integrated against 1−n/x, gives S_1(x)≥c x for all sufficiently large x. Equation(S) then forces x^δ≪_δ log p. Therefore

    least quadratic nonresidue modulo p ≤ C_δ (log p)^{1/δ}.

The finitely many primes for which this threshold is not smaller than p can be absorbed into C_δ. It is enough that the bound hold for the least nonresidue prime; scanning all positive integers also finds one.

For θ=7/8 this yields a conditional O((log p)^8) search; for θ=11/12 it yields O((log p)^12). The alternate manuscript already states a fixed polylogarithmic nonresidue consequence, citing the Rodosskii method and Bhargava–Ivanyos–Mittal–Saxena. The explicit exponents and cost account above are a reconstruction of that bridge, not a novelty claim.

## 4. Complete cost and downstream use

Input: a prime p and a promised quadratic residue a modulo p. Let k=ceil(log2 p), and let M(k) be the bit cost of k-bit multiplication/reduction. Scan integers z=2,3,… and compute z^{(p−1)/2} modulo p until the value is −1. Under Z_θ, O(k^{1/δ}) trials suffice. Binary exponentiation costs O(k) modular multiplications per trial. Tonelli–Shanks then uses the found nonresidue to compute a square root in O(k²) further modular multiplications in the worst case. Total conservative bit cost:

    O_δ(k^{1+1/δ} M(k) + k² M(k)),    workspace O(k) bits

for a simple sequential implementation. Thus 7/8 yields O(k^9 M(k)); 11/12 yields O(k^13 M(k)). Faster Jacobi-symbol searches can improve the implementation but are not needed for polynomiality. No table of nonresidues, advice depending on p, or uncharged factorization is supplied; the factorization of p−1 used by Tonelli–Shanks is only its 2-adic valuation, computable by repeated division by2.

This conditional utility is directly relevant to exact modular arithmetic, algebraic computation, and proof tooling. Square roots modulo primes are a reproducible concrete subroutine, unlike an unspecified broad consequence of zero-freeness. Deterministic polynomial-time primality testing already exists independently (AKS); no new primality novelty is claimed here. No unconditional cost theorem follows until the zero-free input is independently validated.
