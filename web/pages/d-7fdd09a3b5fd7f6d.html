# Exact fibers of unitary-orbit SLD scores

Worker: `b_math`. Date: 2026-10-06. Scope: finite-dimensional faithful density operators, exact operator-valued scores. This is a mathematical audit and extension, not an efficient quantum measurement protocol.

## Source and interface

Dong and Leng, *Quantum score matching with applications to learning thermal states*, arXiv:2609.28391v1, submitted 2026-09-23 17:01:36 UTC. Title, authors, version and submission date were verified on the live arXiv abstract page on 2026-10-06. Definition 1, Eq. (6), uses `∂_a X = -i[A_a,X]`. Definition 2, Eq. (9), defines the symmetric score by

\[
-i[A_a,\rho]=\frac12(\rho L_a^\rho+L_a^\rho\rho).
\]

This is the conventional symmetric logarithmic derivative for the one-parameter unitary orbit `ρ(t)=e^{-itA_a}ρe^{itA_a}`. It is not the parameter SLD of an arbitrary statistical model, and it is not `∂_a log ρ` (the BKM score discussed in Appendix A.2).

The paper's Lemma 6 and Theorem 15 already use a **dressed frame**. They do not claim that irreducibility of the ordinary `A_a` alone implies SLD identifiability. Lemma 16 establishes that ordinary irreducibility suffices for the left/right endpoint scores. Theorem 9 supplies a separate uniform high-temperature result for local Pauli frames. None of the examples below refute these stated results.

## Exact fiber theorem (our reconstruction and converse)

Fix `σ>0`, `Tr σ=1`, and a Hermitian frame `{A_a}`. Write `D=√σ`, `L_a=L_a^σ`, and

\[
K_a=D(A_a+iL_a/2)D^{-1}
=\operatorname{sech}(\tfrac12\log\Delta_\sigma)(A_a),\qquad
\Delta_\sigma(X)=\sigma X\sigma^{-1}.
\]

In an eigenbasis `σ=diag(p_i)`,

\[
(L_a)_{ij}=2i\frac{p_i-p_j}{p_i+p_j}(A_a)_{ij},\qquad
(K_a)_{ij}=\frac{2\sqrt{p_i p_j}}{p_i+p_j}(A_a)_{ij}.
\]

Consequently each `K_a` is Hermitian, and the linear map `T_σ:A↦K` is invertible on Hermitian matrices. Let `C={K_a}'` be the joint commutant, a unital finite-dimensional *-algebra. Then the full exact score fiber is

\[
\{\rho>0:\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1,\ L_a^\rho=L_a^\sigma\ \forall a\}
=\{D Q D: Q\in C,\ Q>0,\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma Q)=1\}.
\tag{F}
\]

**Proof.** Put `B_a=A_a-iL_a/2`, `C_a=A_a+iL_a/2=B_a†`. The target score equation is `B_a σ=σ C_a`, which implies `D^{-1}B_aD=DC_aD^{-1}=K_a`. A candidate has the same scores precisely when `B_aρ=ρC_a`. Substituting `ρ=DQD` gives `K_aQ=QK_a`. Positivity and normalization transform as stated. Conversely, any such `Q` solves every target score equation; uniqueness of the faithful SLD then implies equality of all scores. □

This proves the paper's sufficient condition and its converse over the entire faithful state space:

\[
\text{SLD fiber at }\sigma\text{ is a singleton}\iff C=\mathbb CI.
\]

If `C` is nonscalar, choose a nonzero Hermitian `Y∈C` with `Tr(σY)=0`; `Q=I+tY` is positive for sufficiently small nonzero real `t` and gives an unequal state in the fiber. If `m=dim_C C=dim_R C_sa`, the fiber has real dimension `m−1`. For a restricted Gibbs model, the exact criterion is that its intersection with (F) is a singleton; nonscalar `C` alone does not prove ambiguity inside that restricted model.

## Minimal dimensional obstruction to ordinary irreducibility

Take a qutrit,

\[
\sigma=\frac1{14}\operatorname{diag}(1,4,9),\quad
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&5/4&5/3\\5/4&1&13/12\\5/3&13/12&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&-5/4&0\\-5/4&1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Their dressed frame is `K_1=J_3` and `K_2=vvᵀ`, `v=(1,−1,0)ᵀ`. Since `v⊥(1,1,1)`, the two `K` commute; their common eigenvalue pairs are all distinct, so `dim C=3`. Set `P=J_3/3`. It commutes with both `K`. The fiber member from `Q=(I+P)/(1+Tr σP)` is

\[
\rho=\frac1{56}\begin{pmatrix}4&2&3\\2&16&6\\3&6&36\end{pmatrix}\ne\sigma.
\]

Both are full-rank trace-one states and have identical scores

\[
L_1=i\begin{pmatrix}0&-3/2&-8/3\\3/2&0&-5/6\\8/3&5/6&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
L_2=i\begin{pmatrix}0&3/2&0\\-3/2&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**Ordinary irreducibility proof.** The eigenvalues of `A_2` are `9/4`, `−1/4`, `0`, with eigenvectors proportional to `(1,−1,0)`, `(1,1,0)`, and `e_3`. An operator commuting with `A_2` is diagonal in this basis. The first two eigenvectors are each coupled to `e_3` by `A_1`: the unnormalized couplings are `5/3−13/12=7/12` and `5/3+13/12=11/4`, both nonzero. Commutation with `A_1` forces all three diagonal entries equal. Hence `{A_1,A_2}'=CI`, even though the score fiber has dimension two. □

**Why dimension two cannot do this.** In a qubit eigenbasis of `σ`, `T_σ` acts on traceless Hermitian Bloch vectors by the invertible real diagonal map `diag(t,t,1)`, with `t=2√(p_1p_2)/(p_1+p_2)>0`. A Hermitian qubit frame is reducible precisely when all its traceless Bloch vectors are collinear. An invertible linear map preserves that property. Thus ordinary and dressed irreducibility are equivalent for qubits. The qutrit is minimal among arbitrary finite dimensions. A separate exact `d=4` example in the code/result meets the paper's qubit Hilbert-space convention; it is a dense two-qubit frame, not the specific local `{X_x,Z_x}` frame.

## Repairs and their limits

1. **Exact algebraic certificate.** From exact observed scores `L_a`, solve the real linear system `−i[A_a,X]−{X,L_a}/2=0` on Hermitian `X`, then impose `Tr X=1`, `X>0`. Its homogeneous kernel is congruent to `C_sa`. If the kernel has real dimension one and contains a faithful normalized state, reconstruction is unique. This checks identifiability without pre-supposing the unknown target or replacing it with ordinary-frame irreducibility. It still requires matrix-size score access; it is not an acquisition or sample-complexity result.

2. **A finite condition-number certificate.** Let
\[
\gamma_A=\min_{X=X^\dagger,\operatorname{Tr}X=0,\|X\|_F=1}\Big(\sum_a\|[X,A_a]\|_F^2\Big)^{1/2}>0,
\quad M_A=(\sum_a\|A_a\|_F^2)^{1/2}.
\]
If `κ=λmax(σ)/λmin(σ)` and
\[
2\,[1-\operatorname{sech}(\tfrac12\log\kappa)]M_A<\gamma_A,
\tag{G}
\]
then the dressed commutant is scalar. Indeed `||K_a−A_a||_F≤δ||A_a||_F` with `δ=1−sech(logκ/2)`, and the reverse triangle inequality plus `||[X,E]||_F≤2||E||_2||X||_F` bounds the dressed gap below by `γ_A−2δM_A`. This is a rigorous finite-dimensional sufficient condition. Its Frobenius factors may scale poorly with system size; the paper's Theorem 9 is stronger for its local setting.

3. **One generic extra generator for each fixed target.** Suppose the existing frame has one nonscalar Hermitian `A_0`. For every fixed faithful `σ`, an additional Hermitian generator `A` drawn from a distribution absolutely continuous on the Hermitian matrices produces scalar dressed commutant with probability one. The statement remains true for an absolutely continuous distribution on the traceless Hermitian subspace.

**Proof of repair 3.** The existing `K_0=T_σ(A_0)` is nonscalar, since `T_σ` is invertible and fixes `I`. For every pair `i≠j`, the function `U↦(U†K_0U)_{ij}` is real analytic on `U(d)` and is not identically zero: place distinct-eigenvalue eigenvectors of `K_0` into columns `i,j` and rotate them by `π/4`. Its zero set therefore has Haar measure zero. A finite union gives some `U` for which every off-diagonal entry is nonzero. Choose `B=U diag(b_1,…,b_d)U†` with distinct real `b_i` (and sum zero if desired). Any operator commuting with `B` is diagonal in this basis, and commutation with `K_0` forces every diagonal entry equal. Hence `{K_0,B}'=CI`. In a fixed real Hermitian basis, the commutator map for `{K_0,B}` has polynomial entries in the coordinates of `B`. The existence example makes one maximal minor a nonzero polynomial. Its zero set has Lebesgue measure zero. Finally `T_σ` is an invertible real linear map preserving tracelessness, so absolute continuity of `A` transfers to `B=T_σ(A)`. □

The quantifier is `∀ fixed σ, Pr_A[unique fiber at σ]=1`, and hence the same for any finite/countable list of fixed targets. It is **not** a proved simultaneous statement `Pr_A[∀ faithful σ, unique fiber at σ]=1`. Dense random generators need exponentially many coordinates on `n` qubits, and probability-one uniqueness supplies no useful lower bound on conditioning or measurement cost. The proposal repairs exact identifiability, not scalable learning.

4. **Minimal scalar anchors for a known fiber.** Let `{E_0=I/√d,E_1,…,E_{m−1}}` be a Frobenius-orthonormal Hermitian basis of `C`. Knowledge of `Tr(ρ E_j)` for `j=1,…,m−1`, together with `Trρ=1`, distinguishes every state of (F), and this number of scalar linear anchors is minimal. Let `E_C` be Frobenius projection onto `C`, and define `R_σ(Y)=E_C(DYD)` on `C_sa`. Then
\[
\langle Y,R_\sigma(Y)\rangle_F=\|D^{1/2}YD^{1/2}\|_F^2\ge\lambda_{\min}(\sigma)\|Y\|_F^2.
\]
Thus `R_σ` is invertible and the measured coordinates recover `R_σ(Q)` and then `Q`. Fewer than `m−1` scalar linear measurements have a nontrivial kernel on the normalized affine tangent; perturbing an interior fiber state gives ambiguity. This is a target-dependent repair certificate because constructing the dressed commutant requires `σ` (or a faithful candidate reconstructed from the exact score equations). It is not an a priori measurement protocol for an unknown state.

## Prior art, novelty and use

The decisive source is the paper's Theorem 15 proof, Eq. (74)–(80), which already obtains the commutation condition. The exact converse and whole cone-slice fiber are elementary completions of that derivation. The minimal-dimensional explicit counterexample, qubit preservation observation, and generic-generator proof were independently constructed here; novelty beyond this paper has not been established by a comprehensive literature search. Treat them as audit lemmas/candidate extensions, not a claimed new foundational discovery.

Concrete use: reject an unjustified replacement of the dressed commutant by the original one; classify the entire ambiguity rather than returning a binary failure; compute an exact reconstruction certificate; count the independent scalar anchors needed to eliminate a known ambiguity. These depend on ideal exact score access and do not answer how many copies or gates are needed to acquire those scores.

## An all-temperature diagonal subclass with a uniform algebraic gap

This subsection was prompted by a lead exchanged with `team_b_quantum`; the derivation below is reconstructed directly from the computational-basis entries, independently of the paper's high-temperature perturbation argument.

**Theorem.** Let `σ=diag(p_x)>0`, indexed by `x∈{0,1}^n`, `n≥1`, and use the local frame `{X_i,Z_i}`. Put
\[
w_i(x)=\frac{2\sqrt{p_xp_{x^i}}}{p_x+p_{x^i}}>0,\qquad
w_{\min}=\min_{i,x}w_i(x).
\]
The dressed frame is `K_Zi=Z_i` and the weighted-flip operator `(K_Xi)_{x,x^i}=w_i(x)`, with all other entries zero. Its joint commutant is scalar. More quantitatively, for every complex matrix `M` with `Tr M=0`,
\[
\sum_i\big(\|[M,K_{X_i}]\|_F^2+\|[M,Z_i]\|_F^2\big)
\ge4w_{\min}^2\|M\|_F^2.
\tag{D}
\]

**Proof.** Write `M=D_0+O`, with `D_0=diag(d_x)` and `O_xx=0`. The `Z` commutators have no contribution from `D_0`, and
\[
\sum_i\|[O,Z_i]\|_F^2
=4\sum_{x\ne y}\operatorname{Hamming}(x,y)|O_{xy}|^2
\ge4\|O\|_F^2.
\]
For each `i`, `[D_0,K_Xi]` is supported solely on the ordered pairs `(x,x^i)`. On these pairs `[O,K_Xi]` is exactly zero, since its two potentially contributing entries are `O_xx` and `O_{x^i,x^i}`. Therefore the two commutators are Frobenius orthogonal, and
\[
\|[M,K_{X_i}]\|_F^2
=\|[D_0,K_{X_i}]\|_F^2+\|[O,K_{X_i}]\|_F^2
\ge w_{\min}^2\sum_x|d_x-d_{x^i}|^2.
\]
Because `Σ_x d_x=TrM=0`, the hypercube Poincare inequality gives `Σ_iΣ_x|d_x−d_{x^i}|²≥4Σ_x|d_x|²`. For completeness, expand `d` in the Walsh basis; the directed-edge quadratic form has eigenvalue `4|S|` on character `χ_S`, and the zero-mean condition removes `S=∅`. Combining the two bounds and `w_min≤1` yields (D). The gap forces any commuting matrix to be scalar after subtracting its trace multiple of `I`. □

For a diagonal Gibbs state `p_x∝e^{−βE_x}`,
\[
w_i(x)=\operatorname{sech}\big(\beta(E_x-E_{x^i})/2\big).
\]
If each single-spin flip changes energy by at most `L`, the dressed commutator gap is at least `2sech(βL/2)`. For `H=Σ_j θ_j Z_{S_j}`, `|θ_j|≤1`, and each site lying in at most `r` terms, `L≤2r`, yielding `γ_K≥2sech(βr)`, independently of `n`. Under the paper's low-intersection condition each nonempty term intersects at most `D` others, one can take `r≤D+1`.

The result identifies a diagonal target even against **arbitrary faithful competitor states**, not merely diagonal competitors. It is valid at every finite inverse temperature. Exact identifiability does not extend automatically to the zero-temperature singular limit. The gap can be exponentially small in `βr`, and conversion of the transformed unknown `Q` to physical-state or statistical error can incur spectrum-dependent factors. Thus this theorem neither removes low-temperature sample lower bounds nor establishes an all-temperature optimization/sample-complexity guarantee.

### Conditional Brier reduction and global parameter growth

For a specified diagonal Pauli model
\[
H_\theta=\sum_j\theta_j Z_{S_j},\qquad
H_\theta=Z_i h_{i,\theta}(Z_{-i})+H_{0,i,\theta},\qquad
h_{i,\theta}=\sum_{j:i\in S_j}\theta_j Z_{S_j\setminus\{i\}},
\]
the SLDs are exactly `S_Xi=2Y_i tanh(βh_i,θ)` and `S_Zi=0`. Indeed the offdiagonal score at `(x,x^i)` is `−2i z_i tanh(βh_i(x_-i))`, and `Z_iX_i=iY_i`. Set `t_i,θ=tanh(βh_i,θ)`. With the paper's normalization **`J_Q=(1/2)Σ_a||S_a−S_a*||²_σ`**, the discrepancy and sampleable candidate objective are
\[
J_Q(\theta)=2\sum_i\mathbb E_\sigma[(t_{i,\theta}-t_{i,*})^2],\qquad
J_Q(\theta)=2\sum_i\mathbb E_\sigma[t_{i,\theta}^2+2z_i t_{i,\theta}]+C.
\tag{B}
\]
Here `∂_Xi S_Xi=4Z_i t_i,θ`, and the paper's Eq. (17) is `(1/2)S²+∂S`; this fixes all factors. The target's conditional spin mean is `Eσ[z_i|z_-i]=−t_i,*`. Equation (B) is therefore conditional-mean Brier regression. One computational-basis measurement produces a full spin sample from which every local loss/gradient term can be evaluated classically. This diagonal special case needs no quantum circuit to estimate the candidate objective.

Assume known distinct nonempty interaction sets `S_j`, `|S_j|≤k`, `|θ_j|,|θ_j*|≤1`, and every site in at most `r` interactions. Let `N_i=∪_{j:i∈S_j}(S_j\{i})`, `m=max_i|N_i|≤r(k−1)`, and `β>0`. A nonperturbative **global quadratic growth** bound is
\[
J_Q(\theta)\ge
2\beta^2\operatorname{sech}^4(\beta r)e^{-2\beta rm}
\sum_j |S_j|(\theta_j-\theta_j^*)^2.
\tag{P}
\]

**Proof.** Each field lies in `[−r,r]`, so the real mean-value theorem yields `|t_i,θ−t_i,*|≥βsech²(βr)|h_i,θ−h_i,*|`. Conditional on all spins outside `N_i`, at most `r|N_i|` target interaction terms vary. The energy range over configurations of `N_i` is at most `2r|N_i|`, so each configuration has conditional, and hence marginal, probability at least `2^{−|N_i|}e^{−2βr|N_i|}`. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_\sigma[(h_{i,\theta}-h_{i,*})^2]
\ge e^{-2\beta r|N_i|}\mathbb E_{\mathrm{uniform}(N_i)}[(h_{i,\theta}-h_{i,*})^2]
=e^{-2\beta r|N_i|}\sum_{j:i\in S_j}(\theta_j-\theta_j^*)^2,
\]
where distinct Walsh characters are orthonormal. Sum in (B); each coefficient occurs `|S_j|` times. □

The constant in (P) is independent of `n` for fixed `β,k,r` and can be exponentially small in inverse temperature/local degree. It proves global parameter identification and quantitative growth for this known diagonal class. It does **not** imply global convexity or that gradient descent reaches the target; a generic quadratic-growth function can have other stationary points. No finite-sample or optimizer theorem is claimed here. Classical discrete score matching, conditional-mean regression, and pseudolikelihood provide the necessary comparator/prior-art families; the Brier identity is an exact reduction, not evidence of quantum advantage.

**Prior-art status for this subclass.** The paper's explicitly read Theorem 9 and Lemma 21 treat general local Hamiltonians and a high-temperature perturbation of the unweighted Pauli gap. The diagonal all-temperature calculation above is not asserted in those locations. The irreducibility conclusion is immediate from weighted hypercube connectivity; the sharper orthogonality-based uniform gap is also elementary. Broader literature novelty has not been checked. Candidate downstream use: a nonperturbative identifiability and conditioning baseline for learning classical diagonal Hamiltonians through the quantum SLD interface, and a clean boundary case for testing noncommuting extensions.

## Verification and next falsifiable step

`work/b_math/exact_sld_fiber.py` computes both examples with exact SymPy rational algebra. It solves every SLD equation, checks faithful positivity through Sylvester principal minors, compares scores, and computes exact original/dressed commutant dimensions and score-kernel dimensions. Results: `d=3`, dimensions `1/3/3`; `d=4`, dimensions `1/4/4`; equal scores in both. This verifies the displayed examples, while (F), qubit minimality, and generic repair are supported by the proofs above rather than by finite checks.

Most discriminating next step: determine whether a fixed **local Pauli frame** can lose SLD identifiability outside the paper's high-temperature domain inside a specified local-Hamiltonian model. The dense-frame examples do not settle that question. Separately, a useful scalable repair would need a distribution over efficiently implementable local generators plus a uniform lower bound for the dressed commutator gap, rather than merely a probability-one result for dense operators. Both remain unresolved here.
