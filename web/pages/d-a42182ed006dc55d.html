## Exact interface and claim

For integer T>=1, normalized area sigma on S^2, and any orthonormal real basis e of the nonconstant spherical harmonics of degrees 1,...,T, define

    b_X=(1/N)sum_i e(x_i),
    S_X=(1/N)sum_i e(x_i)e(x_i)^T,
    beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].

The proposed algorithm deterministically produces N=720000(T+1)^2 **distinct rational unit vectors** with

    ||b_X|| < beta_T,       S_X > (1/2) I.             (A1)

Its Turing bit complexity is polynomial in T. The final coordinates have O(log(T+1)) bits. The algorithm uses an exact polynomial-time rational LP feasibility implementation, a usual general-purpose algorithmic primitive; it does not assume a design oracle, a Brouwer-zero oracle, a uniform MZ constant, or an uncharged real-arithmetic solver. There are O(log(T+1)) LP calls. The constants imply huge output even at small T; no feasible high-degree runtime or practical noise tolerance is claimed.

Take

    q=600(T+1), N=2q^2,
    h=12/q=1/[50(T+1)],
    delta=2^(-24)(T+1)^(-8),
    I=10+4 ceil(log_2(T+1)),
    epsilon_0=5T h^2+2 delta,
    epsilon_j=4^(-j)epsilon_0+(8/3)delta.              (A2)

The slightly loose epsilon_0 formula is also a valid starting bound. The j=0 member epsilon_j is larger than this bound by (8/3)delta, harmless; for cleaner movement accounting below use the sharper sequence

    E_j=4^(-j)epsilon_0+(8/3)(1-4^(-j))delta.          (A3)

It satisfies E_0=epsilon_0 and E_(j+1)=E_j/4+2delta. The actual algorithm uses E_j and the box radius 2E_j. All these numbers are rational with O(log T) bits.

## 1. An explicit equal-area initializer

For each northern index j=1,...,q put

    a_j=1-j^2/q^2, b_j=1-(j-1)^2/q^2,
    m_j=2j-1.

Divide the ring a_j<=z<=b_j into m_j longitude wedges of width 2pi/m_j. Reflect these cells in z=0 to obtain the southern half. Since sigma=dz dphi/(4pi), every cell has area

    (b_j-a_j)(2pi/m_j)/(4pi)=1/(2q^2)=1/N.

The cells cover the sphere, with boundary overlaps of measure zero. There are exactly 2 sum_j(2j-1)=2q^2 cells. All ring endpoints are rational.

Their geodesic diameter is at most h. To see this, the upper and lower polar angles obey

    theta_j=arccos(1-j^2/q^2)=2arcsin[j/(sqrt(2)q)].

As j runs from 0 to q, dtheta_j/dj<=2/q. The first ring is a polar cap of radius theta_1<=2/q, hence diameter<=4/q. For j>=2, a path first along a meridian and then along a latitude inside the cell has length at most

    2/q + sin(theta_j) 2pi/(2j-1)
      <=2/q + (sqrt(2)j/q) 2pi/(2j-1)
      <=(2+4pi sqrt(2)/3)/q <12/q.

Let the normalized ambient centroid of cell R_i be

    m_i=N integral_(R_i) x d sigma(x), c_i=m_i/|m_i|.

It exists and is well conditioned. For every x in R_i, the whole cell lies in the circular cone of angle h about x. This cone is convex for h<pi/2; averaging and normalizing leave it invariant. Therefore R_i is contained in B_h(c_i), and |m_i|>=cos h>1/2. The exact cancellation is

    integral_(R_i) (I-c_ic_i^T)x d sigma(x)=0.          (A4)

These are ambient centroids, not Karcher centers. No nonlinear optimization is needed to acquire them. For a northern cell centered at longitude phi_0,

    m_z=(a_j+b_j)/2,
    (m_x,m_y)=s_j sinc(pi/m_j)(cos phi_0,sin phi_0),
    s_j=[F(b_j)-F(a_j)]/(b_j-a_j),
    F(z)=[z sqrt(1-z^2)+arcsin z]/2.

The first ring has m_x=m_y=0, so c_i is exactly a pole. Section 7 supplies a certified rational approximation algorithm and its charged bit cost.

## 2. Self-contained rotation-word sampling estimate

Write L_a Q(x)=(a cross x) dot grad Q(x), for a constant axis a. Let L_1,L_2,L_3 be the coordinate-axis rotations. They preserve the degree of a spherical polynomial. The following L1 Bernstein bound has constant exactly n:

    ||L_k Q||_1 <= n ||Q||_1, degree Q<=n.            (A5)

An elementary proof avoids a hidden MZ constant. For a trigonometric polynomial f of degree n>=1, put theta_j=(2j+1)pi/(2n), j=0,...,2n-1. Fourier interpolation gives

    f'(t)=sum_j c_j f(t+theta_j),
    c_j=(-1)^j/[4n sin^2(theta_j/2)],
    sum_j |c_j|=n.

To verify the formula, the discrete interpolation kernel is D(theta)=1+2sum_(k=1)^(n-1)cos(k theta)+cos(n theta)=sin(n theta)cot(theta/2). At theta_j, -D'(theta_j)/(2n) is the displayed c_j. Its action on each Fourier mode -n,...,n is multiplication by ik; the two endpoint modes agree with the same interpolation rule at these shifted nodes. The identity sum_j csc^2[(2j+1)pi/(4n)]=4n^2 follows by differentiating sum_j cot(t+jpi/(2n))=2n cot(2nt), at t=pi/(4n). Thus the sum of the absolute coefficients is n. Apply this formula along the rotation about coordinate axis k and integrate sphere area; rotations preserve area, proving (A5). Repeating gives ||L_(a_1)...L_(a_k)Q||_1<=n^k||Q||_1 for every coordinate word.

Let {R_i} be any equal-area partition. Suppose z_i is within geodesic distance D of **every** y in R_i. For each pair y,z_i, choose the fixed unit axis a which rotates y to z_i through the shorter angle theta<=D. A coincident or antipodal degeneracy can be resolved by any admissible axis; only D<pi is used here. Pointwise Taylor expansion along this rotation yields

    |Q(z_i)-Q(y)|
      <=sum_(k>=1) D^k/k! sum_(w in {1,2,3}^k)|L_w Q(y)|.

The axis can depend on y and i. Its coordinate coefficients have magnitude<=1, so this inequality is valid pointwise even with that dependence; we do not differentiate the axis or exchange differently shifted integrals. Integrating the cells and using (A5),

    sum_i integral_(R_i)|Q(z_i)-Q(y)|d sigma(y)
      <=(exp(3Dn)-1)||Q||_1.                         (A6)

Absolute values also satisfy this error bound. Consequently

    (1/N)sum_i |Q(z_i)| <= exp(3Dn)||Q||_1.           (A7)

For a local supremum, choose z_i attaining that supremum in the specified compact neighborhood and apply (A7). A different maximizing point may be used for each scalar polynomial. The infinite series is legitimate: the integrated nonnegative upper series sums to exp(3Dn)||Q||_1, so Tonelli and the exact entire rotation Taylor expansion apply.

This derivation replaces, rather than assumes, the BRV MZ theorem. BRV's earlier singleton-extension argument is consistent with the neighborhood formulation, but its unspecified r_d is not load bearing here.

## 3. Gradient conditioning, local Hessians, and Gram margin

For a degree-T polynomial P on S^2 let

    G(P)=integral |grad_S P| d sigma,
    F_k=L_kP.

Then F=x cross grad_S P, so |F|=|grad_S P|. All F_k have degree<=T. If x_i lies in B_h(c_i), it is within 2h of every y in its cell. From (A6) and sum_k||F_k||_1<=sqrt(3)G(P),

    |(1/N)sum_i |grad P(x_i)|-G(P)|
      <=sqrt(3)(exp(6hT)-1)G(P) <G(P)/4.             (A8)

The final strict inequality follows from hT<=1/50. Use sqrt(3)<7/4 and, for 0<=u<=.24,

    exp(u)<=1+u+u^2/[2(1-u/3)],                     (A9)

since n!>=2*3^(n-2) for n>=2. At u=.12, the right side is 1.1275. Thus (7/4)(exp(.12)-1)<1/4. The same scalar upper bound gives

    (1/N)sum_i sup_(B_h(c_i)) |grad P| <2 G(P).      (A10)

For a unit tangent vector u at x, choose a=x cross u. The rotation curve about a is a unit-speed great circle through x, and

    Hess_S P(x)[u,u]=L_a^2 P(x).

The Hessian is a real symmetric bilinear form, hence its operator norm equals max_|u|=1 |Hess[u,u]|. Expanding L_a^2 gives

    ||Hess_S P(x)||op <=sum_(j,k=1)^3 |L_j L_kP(x)|.

Apply (A7) to each local supremum and then (A5):

    (1/N)sum_i sup_(B_h(c_i))||Hess_S P||op
      <=3 sqrt(3) exp(6hT) T G(P) <6T G(P).         (A11)

The last numerical bound follows from (A9): (21/4)*1.1275<6. The gradient and Hessian estimates hold uniformly at all nodes and all paths that remain in these fixed anchor balls.

For the Gram matrix, apply (A6) directly to Q=P^2 of degree<=2T. If every x_i lies in its anchor ball,

    |(1/N)sum_i P(x_i)^2-integral P^2|
       <=(exp(12hT)-1)integral P^2 <.28 integral P^2.

Here (A9) at u=.24 gives exp(.24)<1.272<1.28. Therefore

    S_X >.72 I.                                     (A12)

This is stronger than the accepted >I/2 condition and does not require separately solving a covariance problem.

## 4. The centroid residual is second order

For x in R_i put theta=dist(c_i,x)<=h and x=cos(theta)c_i+sin(theta)u. Along the unit-speed great circle,

    P(x)-P(c_i)=theta grad P(c_i) dot u + R,
    |R|<=theta^2/2 sup_(B_h(c_i))||Hess_S P||.

Replace theta by sin(theta). The discrepancy is <=theta^3|grad P(c_i)|/6. The averaged sin(theta) term is exactly grad P(c_i) dot (I-c_ic_i^T) integral_Ri x, which vanishes by (A4). Sum the cells and use (A10)-(A11). For mean-zero P,

    |B_C(P)|=|(1/N)sum_i P(c_i)|
      <=[3T h^2+h^3/3]G(P)<5T h^2 G(P).             (A13)

Rationally approximating every c_i to geodesic accuracy delta changes this residual by at most 2delta G(P), by (A10). Hence the starting rational nodes satisfy

    |B_(X_0)(P)|<=epsilon_0 G(P),                   (A14)

with epsilon_0 from (A2). This first-order cancellation is essential: arbitrary equal-area samples give O(h) residual, which would not enter the contraction basin at this source count.

## 5. Box-LP correction and rational retraction

At a current rational configuration X, define A_X on tangent displacements by

    A_X(v)(P)=(1/N)sum_i grad P(x_i) dot v_i.

If |B_X(P)|<=epsilon G(P) for every mean-zero degree-T P, then a displacement with every |v_i|<=2epsilon and A_X(v)=-B_X exists. Indeed the image C of the product of tangent balls of radius 2epsilon is compact convex, and its support function is

    h_C(P)=2epsilon (1/N)sum_i |grad P(x_i)|
           >(3/2)epsilon G(P)>=|B_X(P)|.

The elementary separating-hyperplane characterization of C therefore contains -B_X. This is a quantitative surjectivity assertion about the derivative; no nonlinear degree theorem is invoked.

For computation, use a rational harmonic basis q_j with integral q_j=0, and solve the bounded rational LP feasibility problem

    (1/N)sum_i grad_S q_j(x_i) dot w_i
        =-(1/N)sum_i q_j(x_i), every j,
    -2E_j <= w_(i,a) <=2E_j, a=1,2,3.              (A15)

The previously proved product-ball solution is feasible for this coordinate box. Set v_i=(I-x_ix_i^T)w_i after solving. Projection is exact rational, does not change any derivative equation, and gives |v_i|<=sqrt(3)|w_i|infinity<4E_j. This formulation needs no tangency constraints and no minimum-L-infinity optimum; minimizing the box radius is also legal, but feasibility suffices.

Use the rational sphere retraction

    R_x(v)=[(1-|v|^2/4)x+v]/[1+|v|^2/4].           (A16)

Because v is tangent and |x|=1, the numerator squared norm is exactly (1+|v|^2/4)^2. Thus the updated point is rational and exactly on S^2. For gamma(t)=R_x(tv), its geodesic angle is alpha(t)=2atan(t|v|/2), so

    |alpha'|<=|v|, |alpha''|<=|v|^3/2.

The second derivative of P along gamma is bounded by |v|^2 sup Hess+(|v|^3/2)sup grad. If s=max_i|v_i|<=1, (A10)-(A11) and second-order Taylor give

    |B_(R_X(v))(P)|
      <=[3T s^2+s^3/2]G(P) <=4T s^2 G(P)
      <=64T E_j^2 G(P).                            (A17)

The constant and linear parts cancel by (A15). Reset every exact rational updated point to a rational unit point within geodesic distance delta, using Section 7. The new residual is bounded by

    64T E_j^2+2delta <= E_j/4+2delta=E_(j+1),        (A18)

provided E_j<=1/(256T). This is an actual Newton-type contraction in a dual gradient norm, not a transfer from Brouwer positivity or a presumed convex-gradient potential.

## 6. Basin, motion budget, final acceptance

For all T>=1, epsilon_0<1/(256T). Indeed 5T h^2=T/[500(T+1)^2], and multiplying by 256T gives at most .512, with a negligible positive delta term; for example 512Tdelta<2^-14. Also (8/3)delta<1/(256T). The sequence E_j lies between these endpoints and stays in that basin.

Let every correction curve and reset be charged by its geodesic length. Retraction distance is 2atan(|v|/2)<=|v|<=4E_j. The total distance from c_i through I iterations and one final perturbation is at most

    2delta +4sum_(j=0)^(I-1)E_j+I delta
      <=(80/3)T h^2+(32/3)delta+(35/3)I delta+2delta.

Since I<=22(T+1), the delta terms are <2^-14(T+1)^-7. The principal term divided by h is at most (80/3)Th<=8/15. The delta terms divided by h are less than 1/1000. Thus this entire budget is <h. Induction justifies every preceding local estimate: each intermediate retraction path and reset remains in B_h(c_i). There is no bootstrap from an estimate valid only at the initial nodes.

For any orthonormal coefficient vector of P, sphere integration by parts gives

    ||grad P||_2^2=sum_(l=1)^T l(l+1)||P_l||_2^2
      <=T(T+1)||P||_2^2.

Hence ||b_(X_j)||<=sqrt(T(T+1))E_j<(T+1)E_j. Since I>=10+4log_2(T+1),

    4^(-I)<=2^-20(T+1)^-8,
    (T+1)E_I < beta_T/8.                            (A19)

For the last inequality, the first term is at most 2^-28 T^-1 (T+1)^-7, while the reset floor contributes at most (8/3)2^-24(T+1)^-7; both together are smaller than 2^-14(T+1)^-5=beta_T/8.

Distinctness need not be assumed during the iteration. After it, deterministically make all nodes distinct by the local rational-chart procedure in Section 7, moving each by at most delta. The added mean change is at most 2delta(T+1), so the final norm remains <beta_T. The full motion remains inside the anchor balls, hence (A12) still proves S>.72I>I/2. Every output site is a rational unit direction. No degeneracy of the optimal LP or collision in the iteration invalidates the proof.

## 7. Precision and complete cost accounting

**Rational resets.** For a rational unit point x, choose the stereographic chart of its hemisphere and set u=x_1/(1+|x_3|), v=x_2/(1+|x_3|). The inverse map is

    F_+(u,v)=(2u,2v,1-u^2-v^2)/(1+u^2+v^2),
    F_-(u,v)=(2u,2v,-1+u^2+v^2)/(1+u^2+v^2).

Its operator Lipschitz constant is at most 2. Round u,v to a dyadic mesh a<=delta/16 (take B=ceil(log_2(16/delta)), a=2^-B), using exact integer division. The chord distance is at most sqrt(2)a and the geodesic distance at most pi sqrt(2)a/2<delta. Every reset has O(B)=O(log(T+1)) coordinate bits. There is no division near a chart pole since 1+|x_3|>=1.

**Initializer acquisition.** Compute the displayed centroid integrals using rational intervals. The nonpolar m_i satisfy |m_i|>1/2, so u=m_x/(|m|+|m_z|), v=m_y/(|m|+|m_z|) is well conditioned. The smallest ring width is q^-2. Computing F(a),F(b), sine, and cosine to O(B+log q) bits therefore suffices to approximate u,v to less than a/4. Pick a dyadic representative from each resulting short interval; even an a error in each coordinate gives geodesic error <pi sqrt(2)a<delta. This avoids requiring exact rounding decisions at an irrational tie.

A fully elementary polynomial algorithm is available for each function: rational square roots by interval bisection; pi from 16atan(1/5)-4atan(1/239); atan by its alternating series after the identity atan t=2atan[t/(1+sqrt(1+t^2))] and reciprocal reduction for t>1; arcsin z=atan[z/sqrt(1-z^2)] with endpoint values treated exactly; sine/cosine by their factorial Taylor series on bounded arguments. The transformations put the atan arguments below 1/2. At nonpolar endpoints, 1-z^2>=q^-2, so at most O(log q) extra precision bits are lost. Every truncation has a geometric or factorial rational error bound. All operations and term counts are polynomial in B+log q. One can use slower certified Riemann sums instead: for j>=2, sqrt(1-z^2) is q-Lipschitz on the ring and q(b-a)<=2, so K=O(delta^-1) midpoint rectangles give O(delta) mean error, with O(log(delta^-1)) square-root bisection per rectangle. Even that fallback is polynomial in T; no transcendental real oracle is hidden.

**Final duplicate removal.** At node i use its rational hemisphere chart (u_i,v_i). Consider N+1 candidates F_(sign)(u_i+j delta/[4(N+1)],v_i), j=0,...,N. Each lies within chord distance delta/2, hence geodesic distance <delta. The chart is injective. At most i-1 candidates coincide exactly with previously selected sites, so an unused candidate exists. Select the first one by rational comparisons. There are O(N^2) candidates and a naive scan of all prior sites uses O(N^3) exact equality comparisons; this cost is charged and remains polynomial. Rational unit coordinates use O(log T) bits, since delta^-1 and N have polynomial magnitude. An ordered dictionary can reduce comparison cost, but is not assumed here. The first-ring poles and any LP-created collisions cause no special case.

**Rational basis and LP input.** Use the established rational solid-harmonic basis from the charge compiler: degree<=T, O(T^2) elements, rational coefficients/norms with polynomial bit size. An explicit alternative is the real/imaginary solid associated-Legendre basis generated by Rodrigues' formula; its factorial coefficients have O(T log T) bits and can be generated with polynomially many arithmetic operations. Constants are omitted from the moment equations. At reset nodes of O(log T) height, each basis value/gradient has polynomial bit size; O(T log T) is sufficient for these factorial formulas. A sum of N rational values may need their product denominator, so the residual vector's bit size is conservatively O(N T log T)=O(T^3 log T), **not** O(T log T). This cost is charged.

There are M=T(T+2)=O(T^2) equations, 3N=O(T^2) variables, and 6N bounds in (A15). Standard exact rational linear programming has polynomial bit complexity in this encoded input and returns a rational feasible point of polynomial height; a basic feasible point has the same bound by determinant estimates. Exact projection and (A16) may temporarily give polynomially large coordinate height. The fixed precision reset brings every node back to O(log T) bits before the next LP. Thus O(log T) iterations do not recursively multiply the bit size into a quasipolynomial bound. These resets are load bearing.

Final acceptance may be verified in the rational basis by sum_j b_qj^2/nu_j<beta_T^2 and the positive definiteness of H-(1/2)diag(nu_j), with H the rational empirical Gram. Exact Gaussian/LDL arithmetic is polynomial in the matrix dimension and encoded bit length. This verification is optional for the mathematical guarantee but makes any implementation reviewable.

The algorithm's total output is O(T^2 log T) bits. Its charged runtime is polynomial, with a very large constant and an unspecified polynomial degree if a generic exact LP implementation is used. This is a theoretical acquisition improvement from exhaustive search; it is not a demonstrated implementation speedup over numerical design codes.
