# Independent physical audit: detector probabilities and the hard proxy

6 October 2026. Assigned by root; no children. The cycle4 Mellin proof is not retested. Verdict: its real-axis leading-kernel repair remains a scoped result. The literal claim that a sharp hard-mode cutoff preserves microcausality is false, including for gauge-invariant Maxwell observables. This does not disprove stripped-amplitude analyticity, Hermitian hard-mode evolution, or the paper's entire detector scaling limit. A positive coherent wavepacket observable exists in the exact external shock fixture, but it is a quadratic probability, not the linear Mellin functional.

## Gauge-invariant free-Maxwell counterexample

Use the free transverse photon Fock space with only modes |k|>E, exactly the free limit of the hard-mode prescription. Its Hamiltonian is positive and Hermitian; time evolution is unitary. This establishes no commutation theorem for spatially separated observables.

As a calculational intermediary, a similarly projected free massless scalar obeys, at r=|x|>|t|,

    C_E(t,r)=[φ_E(t,x),φ_E(0,0)]
       =i/(4π²r){sin[E(r−t)]/(r−t)−sin[E(r+t)]/(r+t)}.

Derivation: the full massless commutator vanishes at spacelike separation. Subtract its missing0<k<E modes; their angular integral yields i/(2π²r)∫_0^E sin(kt)sin(kr)dk. The elementary product-to-sum integral gives the displayed expression. The sign convention is [φ(t,x),φ(0,0)].

Using the two transverse Maxwell polarizations, the magnetic-field commutator has Fourier tensor (k²δ_ij−k_i k_j) times the scalar kernel. Taking its spatial-index trace gives the gauge-invariant result

    Σ_i[B_i,E(t,x),B_i,E(0,0)]
       =−2∇² C_E(t,r)
       =i/(π²r)∫_0^E k² sin(kt)sin(kr)dk.                (M)

This is not a Coulomb-gauge potential artifact: B is a gauge-invariant field strength. Choose E=1,t=1,r=2. Both sine factors are strictly positive for0<k<1, so(M) is strictly nonzero at a spacelike point. Continuity away from the lightcone allows smooth compact smears supported in small spacelike-separated neighborhoods with nonzero summed commutator; consequently at least one component fails exact microcausality. The operator is the hard projection of the magnetic field, not the full parent-theory field: adding the omitted soft modes restores the full commutator cancellation.

At fixed r,t, C_E=i tE³/(6π²)+O(E⁵), while(M)=i tE⁵/(5π²)+O(E⁷). These small fixed-coordinate tails do not establish uniformity over an expanding apparatus. Set t=T/E,r=R/E with R>|T| and define the dimensionless scaled field E^(-2)B_E(T/E,R/E). Its trace commutator is

    i/(π²R)∫_0^1 u² sin(uT)sin(uR)du,

independent of E and nonzero at T=1,R=2. Equivalently one can use dilated smooth spacetime smears with the corresponding field-dimension normalization. This is a specific failure of uniform microcausality over scaled probes; it does not show that a particular dispersive functional has a nonvanishing correction.

Largest-time identities and cutting unitarity can survive a spatially nonlocal but temporally well-defined Hermitian theory. Thus this counterexample targets interpreting the hard proxy as a local relativistic detector theory. It does not invalidate Bellazzini's separate analytic definition from the full amplitude. Nor does it prove failure of their scaling-limit matching for prescribed fixed scattering data. A detector matching argument must state its admissible probes and prove whatever uniform estimate its use of expanding apparatuses requires.

## Amplitude, channel and probability are distinct interfaces

Let V be the full scattering isometry from hard inputs into hard outputs plus unresolved radiation. A physical effect0<=F<=I gives raw probability

    p_F(ψ)=<ψ|V†(F⊗I_soft)V|ψ>.

This preserves a single coherent ψ; no ensemble average is needed. Tracing radiation yields a CP map, which in general has many Kraus operators. Matching basis-to-basis diagonal inclusive probabilities is weaker than reproducing this map for every coherent input. The two-path isometry V|j>=|j>|e_j> leaves the two basis probabilities unchanged but changes a plus-input interference fringe by the overlap<e_1|e_0>. This is a counterexample to the stronger implication, not a claim that Bellazzini asserts all-input channel equality in(2.6).

With a fixed reference isometry C and V=C+iT, isometry algebra gives

    2 Im_H(C†T)=T†T.

The resolved scattering effect Q_F=T†(F⊗I)T is PSD and bounded above by this inclusive effect. Raw counts coincide with Q_F only if (F⊗I)C=0; otherwise reference interference and the reference counts must be retained. A reduced CP map alone cannot reconstruct C†T: already S=1 and S=−1 define identical one-dimensional channels, while T=(S−I)/i gives2Im_H T=0 and4, respectively. Thus real-energy CP positivity cannot supply the linear amplitude's imaginary part, its phase reference, analytic continuation or crossing. This independently supports r03's operator reconstruction without making its missing analytic theorem automatic.

## A legal coherent observable in the exact shock fixture

At a fixed positive lightfront energy, the source's shock propagation is the unitary L²(R²) multiplication operator

    (U_μψ)(x)=exp[−iκ log(μ|x|)]ψ(x), κ=4Gp_+P_−.

For normalized incoming ψ, any finite-response momentum effect0<=w(q)<=1 gives a valid measurement probability ||sqrt(w) Fourier(U_μψ)||². If ψ's momentum support is below E/2 and w is supported on E<=|q|<=Q, w annihilates the free reference ψ, so the same count is exactly ||sqrt(w) Fourier((U_μ−I)ψ)||². This is a positive quadratic scattering effect with the reference condition checked, and it allows a single coherent input. Its expansion is bilinear in impact coordinates, not the cycle4 linear integral of Im M_eik. An ideal asymptotic momentum POVM is being specified; no claim is made that its sharp support comes from a compact local apparatus.

There is an explicit phase-sensitive version that avoids plane waves. Choose disjoint annular normalized packets u_1,u_2 and ψ=(u_1+u_2)/sqrt2. The output effect onto f_θ=(u_1+exp(iθ)u_2)/sqrt2 has

    p_θ=|a_1+exp(−iθ)a_2|²/4,
    a_j=<u_j|U_μ|u_j>.

For uniform annuli with radii L_j,H_j,

    a_j=μ^(−iκ) 2[H_j^(2−iκ)−L_j^(2−iκ)]/
                       [(2−iκ)(H_j²−L_j²)].

This exact finite-packet fringe is positive and μ-independent. Thin annuli approach the familiar relative phase κlog(r_2/r_1). Both arms undergo the same shock; no arbitrary coherent-state mixture is introduced. The numerical fixture κ=1.2, annuli[1,1.03] and[2,2.03] gives p_0≈.8400143 and p_π≈.1599200. Changing μ across.3,1,4 leaves the probabilities unchanged. Source recoil, radiation, and physical preparation/control costs remain outside this external-background idealization.

## Why phase-scale relabeling does not perform detector matching

For μ'=cμ at fixed lightfront energy, U_μ'=exp(−iκlog c)U_μ. The full channel and all same-shock output probabilities are identical. Yet with the reference held at I,

    T_μ' = exp(−iκlog c) T_μ
                       +[exp(−iκlog c)−1]I/i.

The identity term is a forward delta in momentum space. A nonforward amplitude changes by a phase even though its channel does not; removing q=0 hides the compensating distribution. Therefore its smeared imaginary part is not automatically a reference-independent detector count. Identifying the arbitrary μ with E/2 algebraically does not prove that E is an apparatus resolution. Bellazzini's E has a separately defined soft-radiation prescription; that matching must be derived. For coherent superpositions of different lightfront energies, μ-dependent translations also require a common spacetime/reference convention; the fixed-energy invariance above is not extended to an arbitrary energy-coherent input without that convention.

## Remaining dynamical and analytic gates

The shock operator is exactly unitary on its fixed external metric. That exactness does not include two-body recoil, emitted gravitons or strong-impact gravity. The eikonal saddle b_*~Gs/q lies at weak deflection when q/sqrt(s)<<1, but a saddle calculation alone is not a bound on the complete omitted region after smearing. A crude unitarity/area estimate for modifying phases on a disk of radius R_s~Gsqrt(s) allows an amplitude error of order sR_s²~G²s²; its s^(-3) upper envelope is nonintegrable. This only shows that the crude estimate is insufficient. It does not show that the actual remainder or dispersive integral diverges.

The repaired Mellin kernel supplies a real-axis weighted bound for its specified leading sector. Exporting it still requires a weighted dynamical remainder, a justified exact partial-wave smearing, detector matching and analytic/crossing compatibility. r07 additionally reports superexponential upper-half-plane growth of the exact gamma phase; this is a separate pending analytic audit, not established by our probability calculations. Real-axis gamma modulus one and channel phase invisibility cannot imply a Regge contour bound.

## Consequence, novelty and falsifiable next step

The sharp-cutoff failure is a standard Fourier projection phenomenon, independently reconstructed here in an observable Maxwell field; no novelty claim is made. The useful downstream correction is to formulate the detector through a physical POVM on a causal parent theory, retaining the reference and unresolved radiation, and then derive rather than assume the corresponding scalar dispersive interface. The next discriminating calculation is an actual hard-plus-soft instrument at finite E on two coherent incoming packets: compare its resolved effect and interference fringe against the proposed scalar stripped construction, with a common phase reference and explicit weighted UV error. Matching diagonal cross sections alone is insufficient for the stronger interface.
