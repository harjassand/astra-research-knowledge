# Cycle 2: a driven-ring current and work law

**Verdict:** I replaced the first-wave barrier-leakage identity with an explicit, solvable design family. An N-state ring can carry directed cycle current even though every frozen phase is detailed-balanced. The high-frequency limit gives closed expressions for current, effective cycle affinity, directional traffic selectivity, and state-well work. An exact three-state Floquet calculation and an independent RK4 integration agree at finite cadence. This is a conditional model construction and design-family tradeoff, not a global optimality theorem, new general stochastic-pump theorem, or validated catalytic process. Literature on stochastic pumps and dynamic catalysis is substantial; priority is unknown.

## Interface and protocol

Let states be \(i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), \(N\ge3\), with oriented edges \(i\to i+1\). Energies and barriers are in \(k_BT\) units. Let \(A>0\) be a well-energy contrast, \(d>0\) a barrier modulation, \(B_0\ge d\), and \(\nu>0\) an attempt rate. In phase \(r\), set

\[
E_i^{(r)}=\begin{cases}0&i=r,\\-A&i\ne r,\end{cases}\qquad
B_i^{(r)}=\begin{cases}B_0-d&i=r,\\B_0+d&i=r-1,\\B_0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

Each phase lasts \(\tau\); phases rotate in the order \(r=0,1,\ldots,N-1\), for period \(N\tau\). For both directions on edge \(i\leftrightarrow i+1\), use Arrhenius rates

\[
k_{i\to i+1}^{(r)}=\nu e^{-(B_i^{(r)}-E_i^{(r)})},\qquad
k_{i+1\to i}^{(r)}=\nu e^{-(B_i^{(r)}-E_{i+1}^{(r)})}.
\]

Thus \(k_{i\to i+1}^{(r)}/k_{i+1\to i}^{(r)}=e^{E_i^{(r)}-E_{i+1}^{(r)}}\), and the frozen phase has equilibrium \(\pi_i^{(r)}\propto e^{-E_i^{(r)}}\). The driven periodic process is irreducible and has a unique periodic stationary state. The condition \(B_0\ge d\) keeps the barrier-control values nonnegative relative to the high well; it does not itself prove the Arrhenius model is a valid transition-state description.

## Exact barrier leakage and an approximately optimal small-gate rule

The finite-cycle identity is established RHJ prior art, so this section states its useful control consequence without claiming a new no-pumping theorem. More generally, orient a connected graph with incidence matrix \(B\) (source entry +1, target entry −1), fixed reference conductances \(C_0=\mathrm{diag}(c_e^0)\), and \(L_0=BC_0B^\top\). Let \(q_i(t)=p_i(t)e^{\beta E_i(t)}\). For time-varying conductances \(c_e(t)=c_e^0[1+\epsilon_e(t)]\), the current is \(j=C_0B^\top q+\mathrm{diag}(c^0\epsilon)B^\top q\). Integrating node conservation gives the exact Hodge/Laplacian identity

\[
\Phi=C_0B^\top L_0^\dagger[p(0)-p(T)]+P r,\quad
P=I-C_0B^\top L_0^\dagger B,\quad
r_e=\int_0^T c_e^0\epsilon_e(t)(q_i-q_j)dt.
\]

Here \(P\) is a projection onto graph cycles. For a periodic simple unicycle, all edge-integrated currents equal a common \(J_C\). With cycle resistance \(R_C=\sum_e1/c_e^0\), the exact relation reduces to

\[
J_C=\frac1{R_C}\int_0^T\sum_{e=(i,j)}\epsilon_e(t)(q_i(t)-q_j(t))dt.
\]

Consequently, if \(|\epsilon_e(t)|\le\eta<1\) and \(g_{\max}=\sup_{i,t}e^{\beta E_i(t)}\), then

\[
|J_C|\le\frac{\eta}{R_C}\int_0^T\sum_e|q_i-q_j|dt
\le\frac{2\eta g_{\max}T}{R_C}.
\]

For a prescribed periodic well waveform, let \(p_0(t)\) be its periodic state with the reference barriers fixed, and \(D^0_e(t)=p_{0,i}e^{\beta E_i}-p_{0,j}e^{\beta E_j}\). Linear response predicts

\[
J_{\rm lin}(\epsilon)=R_C^{-1}\int_0^T\sum_e\epsilon_e(t)D_e^0(t)dt.
\]

If each edge can be driven independently with symmetric signed relative-conductance budget \(|\epsilon_e|\le\eta\) and no slew/bandwidth restriction, the maximizing first-order feed-forward schedule is \(\epsilon_e(t)=\eta\,\mathrm{sign}(D_e^0(t))\). Its linear value is exactly \(\eta R_C^{-1}\int\sum_e|D_e^0|dt\). This is a usable amplitude-allocation rule after learning the baseline edge imbalances; it is not the nonlinear optimum and assumes an ideal actuator that can change sign at each zero crossing.

There is an explicit error certificate if the fixed-barrier baseline dynamics contract zero-sum perturbations as \(\|U_0(t,s)x\|_1\le M e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|x\|_1\), with \(\Lambda_0\) the maximum baseline exit rate. Duhamel's formula gives \(\sup_t\|p_\epsilon(t)-p_0(t)\|_1\le2M\eta\Lambda_0/\lambda\), and, on a simple ring of degree two,

\[
|J_C-J_{\rm lin}(\epsilon)|\le
\frac{4M g_{\max}\Lambda_0T}{R_C\lambda}\eta^2.
\]

This is a conditional small-gate certificate, not evidence of a new result beyond the explored pump-control literature. Its useful interface is explicit: the baseline waveform, conductance/rate bounds, measured or certified \((M,\lambda)\), gate amplitude, and time resolution are all charged inputs. It fails as a useful guarantee when the baseline loses its uniform mixing gap, or when an actuator's bandwidth/energy cost is material. For the three-state example below, the bound is valid but loose: at \(d=0.1\), the relative conductance deviation is \(\eta=e^{0.1}-1\), and the bound is about 3.2 times the computed finite-period current rate.

The state-independent ceiling is not shown attainable by an admissible periodic controller. Its pointwise equality would require concentrating \(q\) on one node while keeping it zero on another; a periodic master equation couples that concentration to transition rates and re-preparation. The rotating-well construction below approaches one third of this ceiling in the fast limit as \(A\) grows. The global periodic-control capacity remains unknown.

## High-frequency theorem for this family

Put \(q=e^{-A}\) and \(c=\nu e^{-B_0}\). Averaging the phase generators as \(\tau\to0\) gives a homogeneous circulant CTMC with the same forward rate \(u\) and reverse rate \(w\) on every edge:

\[
u=\frac cN\left[e^d+q\left(e^{-d}+N-2\right)\right],\qquad
w=\frac cN\left[q e^d+e^{-d}+(N-2)q\right].
\]

The equal-rate ring has uniform stationary probabilities. Its directed edge current, total jump traffic, and effective cycle affinity are

\[
J_\infty=\frac{u-w}{N}
=\frac{2c(1-q)}{N^2}\sinh d,\qquad
T_\infty=u+w,\qquad
A_{\rm eff}=N\log(u/w).
\]

Consequently its directional-current fraction saturates the unicycle traffic/affinity relation:

\[
\frac{J_\infty}{T_\infty/N}
=\tanh\!\left(\frac{A_{\rm eff}}{2N}\right)
=\tanh\!\left(\frac12\log\frac uw\right).
\]

This is an identity for the averaged, uniform ring, not a new thermodynamic uncertainty theorem. It is a directed-cycle traffic fraction; it is not a chemical product selectivity unless each net ring turn is tightly coupled to one net product.

Define well work as work due to jumps in the state energies, with barrier and controller work omitted. At periodic stationarity the system's Shannon entropy and mean state energy return to their initial values each period. Local detailed balance therefore makes the period-averaged well-work input equal the mean entropy production generated by state-energy changes. In the high-frequency limit the phase distribution tends to uniform, and only the two edges adjacent to the high well contribute to the leading entropy-production rate. Their forward/reverse net currents have magnitudes \(c(1-q)e^d/N\) and \(c(1-q)e^{-d}/N\), respectively; the corresponding energy differences have magnitude \(A\). It follows that

\[
\dot W_{\rm well,\infty}
=\frac{2Ac(1-q)}{N}\cosh d
=NAJ_\infty\coth d.
\]

Along the one-parameter gate family \(d\ge0\), eliminating \(d\) yields

\[
\dot W_{\rm well,\infty}
=NA\sqrt{J_\infty^2+\left(\frac{2c(1-e^{-A})}{N^2}\right)^2}.
\]

This curve is exact and achievable in the high-frequency limit for the stated family. It is not a lower bound over all driving protocols. It shows that, within this design, longer ring length suppresses fixed-parameter current as \(N^{-2}\) and raises state-well work per net turn as \(N\); the barrier-actuator and clock costs can change the full work-per-product conclusion. At \(d=0\), the family has zero net current but positive well-work dissipation, so work can be spent without producing directed output.

For each fixed \(N,A,d,B_0,\nu\), convergence follows from finite-state averaging: the one-period propagator is \(I+N\tau\bar Q+O(\tau^2)\), where \(\bar Q\) is the irreducible averaged generator above. Its stationary state is unique, with real spectral gap \((u+w)(1-\cos(2\pi/N))>0\), so the periodic state converges to uniform and the time-normalized current and entropy production converge to the displayed formulas. This argument gives a limit, not a numerical error constant uniform in \(N\) or parameters.

The independent finite algebra check [cycle_geometry_check.py](cycle_geometry_check.py) directly enumerates phase-specific Arrhenius rates for \(N=3,4,5,8\), then checks the analytic rates, current, effective-traffic equality, and work identities across three amplitude settings. It is a numerical consistency check, not the proof of the general formulas.

## Finite-period witness and adversarial controls

For \(N=3\), \(A=4\), \(d=0.1\), \(B_0=0.5\), \(\nu=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), and \(\tau=0.02\,\mathrm{s}\), each phase's exit rate is bounded by \(2\nu e^{-(B_0-d)}<1.341\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\); the full period is \(0.06\,\mathrm{s}\). Exact matrix exponentials give a periodic closure residual below \(2\times10^{-15}\), net current \(J=0.000795498856018\) turns per pump per period, or \(0.013258314266966\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), and state-well input \(0.095777318558159\,k_BT\) per pump per period, or \(1.596288642636\,k_BT\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). The high-frequency values are \(0.013253656198131\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) and \(1.595736675312\,k_BT\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\); finite-to-limit differences are about 0.035%. The predicted high-frequency effective values are \(u=0.2304936261\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(w=0.1907326575\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(T=0.4212262836\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), and \(A_{\rm eff}=0.568051383\,k_BT\).

Independent RK4 integrations with 200–2000 steps per phase match the exact matrix-exponential flux and well work to below \(1.5\times10^{-14}\) absolute in the chosen units; all three integrated edge currents agree within \(3\times10^{-15}\). This is a finite numerical cross-check, not a proof of experimental validity. Runnable files: [sharp_ring.py](../chemistry_dynamic_catalysis/sharp_ring.py), [crosscheck_sharp_rk4.py](../chemistry_dynamic_catalysis/crosscheck_sharp_rk4.py), and [crosscheck_rk4.py](../chemistry_dynamic_catalysis/crosscheck_rk4.py).

At the same finite parameters, the rotating wells with fixed barriers give net integrated current about \(1.2\times10^{-18}\), in line with the no-pumping theorem; barrier modulation with flat wells gives about \(-4.8\times10^{-18}\). The matched well-plus-barrier schedule gives positive directed current. Mirroring the barrier wave relative to the rotating wells (\(d=-0.1\)) reverses the current to \(-0.013247628703639\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), with a small finite-frequency magnitude mismatch. Reversing the order of the well schedule alone does not reverse the high-frequency current, because the averaged generator is insensitive to phase order; the relative well/barrier pairing is the relevant control. These controls isolate joint modulation and its phase relation, rather than energy modulation alone.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The fixed-barrier state-energy no-pumping theorem is established by [Rahav, Horowitz & Jarzynski (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0015) and the broader [Maes, Netočný & Thomas (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.3811); the latter also makes clear that zero net current need not mean zero instantaneous flux or zero dissipation. [Raz, Subaşı & Jarzynski (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.6.021022) show a mapping between periodically driven pumps and nonequilibrium steady-state currents. [Bogod & Rahav (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.023204) solve a constrained two-site molecular-machine power problem whose optimum approaches arbitrarily fast switching. Dynamic heterogeneous catalysis already uses periodic changes of intermediate and transition-state energies to affect pathway selectivity; see [Foley & Razdan (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2024.109543) and [catalytic-resonance parallel-pathway work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8152411/).

The traffic/affinity equality used above is the established Equal Traffic Principle for unicycles, applied to the uniform averaged CTMC; see [Yang & Dill (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71239-9). It is not a work equality for the periodically driven controller.

Accordingly, the no-pumping theorem, time-periodic production of current, high-frequency averaging, and dynamic selectivity are established phenomena. The N-ring formulas are an exact construction and geometry/work calculation for one equal-dwell protocol family. Whether this specialization is absent from prior work has not been determined; no priority claim is made. The reported barrier work is not priced, and no chemically coupled network or experimental catalyst was built.

## Discriminating experiment and costs

The model predicts a directional cycle current only when the moving well and moving barrier profiles have the stated relative phase. A useful experiment needs a real three-or-more-conformation catalyst or molecular machine with a mapped ring of metastable states. Apply a calibrated periodic field, light, or strain drive; measure state occupancy and direction-resolved transitions (for example with a state-sensitive single-molecule signal), and quantify product formation. Sweep from quasistatic operation through frequencies faster than the measured relaxation rate. At each frequency compare (i) paired well/barrier modulation, (ii) wells moved with barriers fixed, (iii) barriers moved with flat wells, and (iv) a mirrored relative phase. Match the time-average of the controlled parameters across arms. Test the current reversal and the full finite-period Markov prediction, not only the high-frequency formula.

To interpret current as catalytic output, verify tight coupling: each clockwise completed cycle must produce exactly one target molecule, with reverse turns consuming or undoing the product in a measurable way. Otherwise report mechanical/conformational current and product flux separately. The data needed before assay design are the state graph, state and transition-state response to the actuator, attempt rates, relaxation/mixing gap, controller waveform bandwidth, product-coupling stoichiometry, and a calibration of detector errors. Total actuation work, including barrier control, waveform generation, and any feedback, must be measured or modeled from a physical actuator; this Markov construction supplies only state-well work. A failure of the matched phase arm to produce current, or failure of the mirrored schedule to reverse it after model parameters and detection are calibrated, rejects this mechanism for that system.
