## E1. Exact input/output interface

Let `n` be a positive finite integer, with two independent Fock-space states `rho_A tensor rho_B`, each having finite total number expectation. Neither input is required to be Gaussian, diagonal, separable or independent across its own modes. After the same passive beam splitter of transmission `eta in [0,1]` on every paired mode, keep one port. Write

\[
g(x)=(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log x,\qquad
N_S(\rho)=g^{-1}(S(\rho)/n).
\]

The manuscript's Theorem 1.1 is

\[
N_S(\rho_C)\ge\eta N_S(\rho_A)+(1-\eta)N_S(\rho_B).
\tag{E1}
\]

It does not assert this for correlated ports, infinite mode number, amplifier mixing, or conditional entropy with quantum memory. Finite energy both bounds entropy and gives the compactness needed in the proof. Product thermal inputs attain (E1), also at unequal temperatures.

## E2. Finite-energy compactness and the strict witness

For total number `N`, the projection `P_K=1_{N<=K}` is finite rank, and `Tr rho(1-P_K)<=E/(K+1)`. Hilbert–Schmidt factorization bounds the two cross blocks in trace norm by `sqrt(q)` each. Closedness of the energy upper bound gives compactness. Pinching into the two blocks changes entropy by at most `H_2(q)`; the high block contributes at most

\[
q n g(E/(qn))\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus entropy is uniformly continuous on every fixed finite-mode energy sublevel. This implies that any strict violation has a strict violating finite-number-support approximation. It also justifies every later entropy limit; trace-norm convergence alone would not suffice.

## E3. Parameter order and auxiliary services

Fix the strict witness. Its entropy photon numbers are `a_i`. Choose positive thermal references `r_i` equal or close to `a_i`; if `a_i=0`, `g(r_i)/h(r_i)->0`, where `h(r)=log(1+1/r)`. At least one `a_i>0`, so the output reference denominator has a positive finite limit.

Add an independent fixed thermal port `D`, with `r_D>0`, and weights

\[
\lambda_1=(1-\lambda_D)\eta,
\quad\lambda_2=(1-\lambda_D)(1-\eta),
\quad r_0=\lambda_1r_1+\lambda_2r_2+\lambda_Dr_D.
\]

Choose `lambda_D>0` small enough to retain the negative witness. Then choose `epsilon>0` small enough to retain negativity and ensure both

\[
(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^2\lambda_i r_i\le r_0,
\qquad
(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^2\lambda_i(r_i+1)\le r_0+1.
\tag{E2}
\]

The strict gaps at epsilon zero are `lambda_D r_D` and `lambda_D(r_D+1)`. Let raw output be `gamma`, replace it by `rho_0=(1-kappa)gamma+kappa tau_{r_0}`, and set `w_i=(1-kappa)lambda_i`. Choose fixed positive kappa last, small enough for E8. Only the quartic penalty parameter zeta tends to zero. No radius-dependent smoothing or hidden repeated auxiliary limits enter the closing contradiction.

## E4. Attainment and a genuine Gibbs minimum

For `W_i=1+N_i`, minimize over finite-fourth-moment inputs

\[
J_\zeta=\frac{S(\rho_0)-ng(r_0)}{h_0}
-\sum_i\frac{w_i(S(\rho_i)-ng(r_i))}{h_i}
+\epsilon\sum_i\frac{w_iD(\rho_i\Vert\tau_{r_i})}{h_i}
+\zeta\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}\rho_iW_i^4.
\tag{E3}
\]

After expansion, negative input entropy grows only as `log(1+E_i)`, whereas the positive term `epsilon w_i E_i` grows linearly. Consequently negative sublevels have a common energy bound independent of zeta. For fixed positive zeta they have a fourth-moment bound. Compactness, entropy continuity and lower semicontinuity of both positive moments give an attained minimum, uniformly bounded above by a fixed negative number.

The output replacement gives `rho_0>=kappa tau_{r_0}`, hence the resolvent formula and inverse order imply `0<=H_0=-log rho_0<=C W_0` as forms. The positive unital input slice therefore obeys `0<=T_i H_0<=C_i W_i`.

Replace the varied output entropy by its cross entropy against H_0. The resulting functional majorizes J and equals it at the chosen minimum. Thus each input really minimizes an admitted Gibbs free energy, with

\[
\frac{(1+\epsilon)w_i}{h_i}H_i
=\frac{1-\kappa}{h_0}T_iH_0+\epsilon w_iN_i+\zeta W_i^4+c_iI.
\tag{E4}
\]

This is a global one-input majorant argument, not an assumed Euler equation at a possibly singular density matrix.

## E5. Required regularity is acquired

For `K=kW^4+W^{1/2}BW^{1/2}`, `k>0`, bounded positive B, set `C=kI+W^{-3/2}BW^{-3/2}`. The closed form on `D(W^2)` has compact inverse `U=W^{-2}C^{-1}W^{-2}`. Its eigenvectors satisfy the number-coordinate identity

\[
kW^{7/2}\psi_j=u_jW^{-1/2}\psi_j-BW^{1/2}\psi_j.
\]

The right side is in Hilbert space; hence `psi_j in D(W^{7/2})`. Since `U<=k^{-1}W^{-4}`, the eigenvalue counting function is polynomial. Exponential Gibbs weights then yield `Tr rho W^7<infinity`. This argument applies to E4. Passive mixing preserves total number, so the raw/replaced output also has the seventh moment. Logarithms are bounded above as forms by constants times W^4. Constants may diverge as zeta tends to zero; the proof never requires their uniformity.

## E6. Component Hessian estimate, with all normalization factors

At a minimum, use the eigenbasis of each faithful state. Let

\[
\ell(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{\log a-\log b},
\quad \|Z\|_s^2=\sum_{l,r}h_s\ell(p_{s,l},p_{s,r})|Z_{lr}|^2,
\quad v_s(l,r)=\frac{\log(p_{s,l}/p_{s,r})}{h_s},
\quad L_iZ=\frac{T_iZ-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_iT_iZ)I}{\sqrt{\lambda_i}}.
\]

The exact one-input objective difference is

\[
J_\zeta(\widetilde\rho_i)-J_\zeta(\rho_i)
=\frac{(1+\epsilon)w_i}{h_i}D(\widetilde\rho_i\Vert\rho_i)
-\frac1{h_0}D(\widetilde\rho_0\Vert\rho_0)\ge0.
\]

Use an input finite-eigenblock traceless Hermitian variation A. Its relative-entropy Hessian is `Q_i(A)=sum |A_lr|^2/ell(p_l,p_r)`. An output finite-eigenblock test centered by a scalar identity gives the variational lower bound for the actual output perturbation; its partition-function second derivative is `||Z||_0^2/h_0`. Crucially the comparison first multiplies the objective by h_0, giving

\[
2(1-\kappa)\operatorname{Tr}A T_iZ
-\frac{(1+\epsilon)w_i h_0}{h_i}Q_i(A)
\le\frac{\|Z\|_0^2}{h_0}.
\]

The constrained dual of Q_i subtracts the rho_i expectation. Optimize over the completed trace-zero space, using finite-block density and continuity of trace, to obtain

\[
\|L_iZ\|_i^2\le\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\kappa}\|Z\|_0^2.
\tag{E5}
\]

The scalar identity tail has frequency zero and preserves the finite-frequency test class. CP slice Schwarz and `rho_0>=(1-kappa)gamma` identify the continuous extension with the actual bounded slice. No inverse lower bound for the infinite-dimensional input state is assumed.

## E7. Four-weight interpolation, independently reconstructed

For fixed positive coordinate measures, say a diagonal family X satisfies comparison if `Q_{X,0}(Z)>=sum_i w_i Q_{X,i}(L_iZ)`. Assume source tests have finitely many parameter tuples and are closed under functions of those tuples. Put `f(x)=x/sinh x`, `b(x)=x coth x` with continuous values at zero. Four comparisons with weights

\[
mf(x)e^{\pm x},\qquad mf(y)e^{\pm y}
\]

imply comparison with

\[
F=m\frac{f(x)f(y)}{f(x+y)}.
\tag{E6}
\]

Independent derivation of the load-bearing steps:

1. Positive sums, pointwise limits (finite source tuples plus target Fatou), and parallel sums preserve comparison. For the latter, split source Z coordinatewise at its minimizing split and apply linearity of every L_i. No commutation of L_i with the multipliers is required.
2. The logarithmic mean and `XY/ell(X,Y)` follow from reciprocal positive quadratures and parallel sums. Applied to the opposite-exponent pairs they produce the three families `m, ms, mt`, with `s=f(x)^2,t=f(y)^2`.
3. Define `B(f(x)^2)=b(x)`. The analytic continuation follows from `a(u)=sin(u)/u` on `D={p+iq:q>0,0<p<p_*(q)}`, where `p_* tan p_*=-q tanh q`. Its real and imaginary parts place its image in the lower-right quadrant. The boundary maps to the axes; growth at infinity and finite boundary limits give properness. `Im(u cot u)<0` rules out a critical point. A connected proper unbranched cover of a simply connected quadrant has one sheet. Therefore the inverse branch gives an anti-Pick B, real across the positive axis.
4. Herglotz representation for `-B` gives `G(s)=(B(s)-1)/(1-s)` with `sG(s)` Pick and positive on the positive axis. Its reciprocal `R(s)=1/(sG(s))` is anti-Pick, positive there. A second Herglotz representation and monotone convergence remove the affine slope and give a Stieltjes representation for R. Differentiating on `(0,1)` and analytically continuing gives

\[
-B'(s)=\tfrac12(1/s+R(s))
=c+\int_0^\infty\frac{d\nu(r)}{s+r},\quad c\ge0,\quad\int\frac{d\nu(r)}{1+r}<\infty.
\tag{E7}
\]

5. Hence `1/E=(2/m) integral_0^1[-B'(theta s+(1-theta)t)]dtheta` is an inverse of a limit of positive sums of reciprocal weights built from `m,ms,mt`. It follows that `E=m(t-s)/(2(B(s)-B(t)))`, with diagonal value `-m/(2B'(s))`, preserves comparison.
6. Minimize

\[
\tfrac14\sum_\pm mf(x)e^{\pm x}|\xi\mp\phi|^2
+\tfrac14\sum_\pm mf(y)e^{\pm y}|\xi\pm\phi|^2+E|\phi|^2.
\]

Its minimum is E6 times `|xi|^2`, by the identity `b(z)^2=f(z)^2+z^2` and the hyperbolic addition formula. At `x=y`, the value is `mb(x)`; at `x=-y`, it is `mf(x)^2`; at both zero it is m. Applying the comparisons to the source minimizing split, then minimizing separately on each target, proves E6. Positivity and equal-parameter limits are included, not discarded.

In the physical application take `m=1/h_s,x=h_s v/2,y=-h_s/2`. The first two weights are the two arithmetic variances; product-port orthogonal projections prove their joint comparison. The last two are `r_s,r_s+1`, supplied by E2 and E5. The target is

\[
F_s(v)=\frac{b_s(v)-(r_s+1/2)v}{1-v},
\qquad \ell(p_l,p_r)F_s(v)=\ell(r_sp_l,(r_s+1)p_r).
\]

It is continuous, strictly positive, and has positive finite limits r_s and r_s+1 at the two infinities. Thus it is bounded above and below by fixed reference-dependent constants. The constants are independent of the minimizing state and zeta.

## E8. Defects and the replacement cost

Center the annihilator by its mean `d'_j=d_j-mu_j`. Define

\[
q_{s,j}(Z)=(r_s+1)\operatorname{Tr}\rho_s(d'_{s,j})^\dagger Z
-r_s\operatorname{Tr}\rho_s Z(d'_{s,j})^\dagger.
\]

Finite energy makes all one-annihilator/state products trace class by Hilbert–Schmidt factorization. Seventh moments and `H_s<=C_sW^4` control the crossed logarithmic sums by `Tr rho W^5`. The uncrossed sums use `E(W^4)E(W)<=E(W^5)` in each eigenvector. Therefore the exact dual norm is an absolutely summable series

\[
\|q_s\|_{F_s,*}^2
=\sum_{j,l,r}(1-v_s(l,r))[(r_s+1)p_r-r_sp_l]|(d'_j)_{lr}|^2.
\tag{E8}
\]

Weak convolution gives `q_0=sum_i w_i q_i circ L_i` plus the explicit replacement branch-mean functional. A discarded ladder operator is first cut off in its own number basis, commutes with retained bounded observables there, and converges in trace class. This avoids assuming an arbitrary bounded observable preserves a ladder domain.

Let `A_*^2=sum_i w_i||q_i||_*^2`, `M_*^2=sum_i w_i|mu_i|^2`. The branch variance, followed by the two-weight logarithmic-mean operation, bounds the replacement term by `sqrt(2kappa) C_0 M_*`, where `C_0=(h_0 inf F_0)^(-1/2)`. Choosing the already reserved kappa at most `epsilon^2/[4(epsilon+2)C_0^2]` gives

\[
\|q_0\|_*^2\le A_*^2+\tfrac\epsilon2(A_*^2+M_*^2).
\tag{E9}
\]

## E9. Generator and logarithm pairings

At these minima use the thermal generator

\[
\delta_s=\sum_j[(r_s+1)D[d_j]+r_sD[d_j^\dagger]]\rho_s.
\]

`W^2 delta W^2` is trace class. For example the gain term factors through `T=W^3 rho W^3` with bounded outer factors `W^2 d W^{-3}` and its adjoint; loss terms also do. Number cutoffs converge in this weighted trace norm. Passive rotation preserves total number. Partial trace is continuous after insertion of the bounded factor `(W_0^k tensor I)W_tot^{-k}`, k=2 or3. On arbitrary cutoff joint operators the coefficient-one loss dissipators rotate covariantly, and every discarded commutator vanishes on partial trace. This proves

\[
\delta_0=(1-\kappa)\sum_i\Phi_i(\delta_i),
\quad\operatorname{Tr}\delta_0H_0=(1-\kappa)\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}\delta_iT_iH_0.
\tag{E10}
\]

The thermal D port and replacement port have zero generator. Pairing H_s or W^4 is legitimate through `W^{-2}H_sW^{-2}` and the trace-class sandwich. This is an algebraic generator expression: the proof neither differentiates entropy along an unbounded semigroup nor assumes two-sided positivity of `rho+t delta`.

## E10. Exact cancellation

Let `sigma_s=Tr delta_s H_s/h_s` and `e'_s=Tr rho_sN_s-|mu_s|^2`. Absolute summability gives

\[
\sigma_s=\|q_s\|_*^2-(e'_s-nr_s),
\quad\operatorname{Tr}\delta_iN_i=-(e'_i-nr_i)-|\mu_i|^2.
\]

The modified output has centered energy

\[
e'_0-nr_0=\sum_iw_i(e'_i-nr_i)+\kappa(1-\kappa)|\mu_\gamma|^2.
\]

Pair E4 with delta_i and sum. The centered energies cancel exactly:

\[
0=\|q_0\|_*^2-(1+\epsilon)A_*^2-\epsilon M_*^2
-\kappa(1-\kappa)|\mu_\gamma|^2+\zeta P.
\tag{E11}
\]

The quartic number drift is a polynomial with leading term `-4N^4`; its supremum is finite independently of zeta. Combining E9 with E11 forces `A_*^2+M_*^2=O(zeta)`.

## E11. Thermal limit without hidden moment convergence

Take a trace-norm subsequence using the common energy bound. For each fixed finite number-basis matrix test Z, its centered F norm is uniformly bounded. Thus `q_i(Z)->0`; the difference between centered and uncentered ladders tends to zero because the acquired means tend to zero. Both `d_j^dagger Z` and `Z d_j^dagger` are bounded finite rank. Consequently the limit satisfies, as number-basis matrices,

\[
(r_i+1)d_j\widehat\rho_i=r_i\widehat\rho_i d_j.
\tag{E12}
\]

Iterate its entries. Unequal coordinates force zero off-diagonal entries; equal coordinates have geometric ratio `r_i/(r_i+1)`. Self-adjointness covers the other inequality of indices. Trace one fixes the whole n-mode product thermal state. The raw and replaced outputs tend to their reference thermal states. Energy-bounded entropy continuity makes the entropy portion of E3 tend to zero; both other penalties are nonnegative. This contradicts the uniform negative minimum. Energy or first-moment convergence was not assumed.

## E13. Our additional conditional deduction: sharp multimode relative-entropy envelope

Fix r>0 and an arbitrary finite-energy n-mode state rho. Let `a=g^{-1}(S(rho)/n)` and let `Phi_t=E_{exp(-t),r}^{tensor n}`. Finite energy and independent zero-mean thermal environment give the exact energy identity

\[
E_t=e^{-t}E+(1-e^{-t})nr.
\]

Using `D(rho||tau_r)=h(r)(E-nr)-S(rho)+ng(r)` and E1 therefore yields, with `eta=e^{-t}`,

\[
D(\Phi_t\rho\Vert\tau_r)
\le\eta D(\rho\Vert\tau_r)-n\,\mathcal J_g(a,r;\eta),
\quad
\mathcal J_g=g(\eta a+(1-\eta)r)-\eta g(a)-(1-\eta)g(r)\ge0.
\tag{E13}
\]

This finite-time inequality needs no entropy derivative. Its constant is independent of n and the internal entanglement. It is sharp as a bound conditioned on input entropy and energy: displace a product thermal state of mean a by a coherent amplitude of squared norm `E-na`. The attainable domain is `E>=na`, by the maximum-entropy bound. The attenuator outputs the displaced thermal state of mean `eta a+(1-eta)r`, and its displacement squared norm is eta times the input one. Every step in E13 is equality for this state.

In particular, taking a=r gives `D(Phi_t rho||tau_r)<=eta D(rho||tau_r)`. Nonzero displacements of tau_r attain equality. Therefore no larger universal exponential contraction rate is possible, even for one mode. This is a genuine structural obstruction to trying to extract a faster arbitrary-state thermalization rate from EPnI.

## E14. Exact normalization, competent control and rejected novelty inference

Beigi–Rahimi-Keshari, arXiv:2311.09572v2 (28 September 2024), §4.1 defines the multimode generator with coefficients `exp(beta/2)` for loss and `exp(-beta/2)` for gain. With `r=(exp(beta)-1)^(-1)`, this is our thermal generator rescaled by

\[
\gamma=2\sinh(\beta/2).
\]

Hence E13 gives finite-time relative-entropy contraction `exp(-gamma t)` for identical temperatures, all finite n and arbitrary finite-energy rho. At states for which their p=1 Dirichlet/entropy-production pairing is defined, division of the finite-time inequality by t yields `E_1(rho)>=gamma D(rho||tau_r)/4`; their convention has `E_1=entropy production/4`. Coherent displaced thermal states show equality. Thus, conditional on the EPnI theorem and the named derivative-domain service, the sharp identical-temperature multimode p=1 constant is

\[
\widehat\alpha_1=\tfrac12\sinh(\beta/2).
\tag{E14}
\]

The finite-time statement E13 is the preferred reusable interface, avoiding derivative-domain qualifications. However its ordinary contraction after dropping the Jensen gap is **not a new EPnI-dependent theorem**. The older linear quantum EPI already gives

\[
S(\Phi_t\rho)\ge\eta S(\rho)+(1-\eta)ng(r),
\]

which, with the very same exact energy identity, yields `D(Phi_t rho||tau_r)<=eta D(rho||tau_r)`. The coherent displaced-thermal example already certifies sharpness. De Palma–Mari–Giovannetti, arXiv:1402.0404v2 (7 May2015; originally submitted 3 February2014, Nature Photonics8,958–964 in2014), §II Eq(5), supplies the stronger exponential qEPI; taking a logarithm and applying scalar log concavity gives the displayed linear inequality. Its introduction and Eq(5) were directly read and the primary PDF retained; its older proof was not independently reproved here.

In a progress message I initially presented the p=1 tensorization consequence as an increment over the nearest meta-logarithmic-Sobolev source's Gaussian evidence. The same-information linear-EPI control rejects that novelty inference; the correction was sent promptly to root and checker. The meta-logarithmic-Sobolev paper's all-p discussion must not be read as absence of this independent p=1 route.

The EPnI-dependent increment in E13 is the positive entropy-dependent `g` Jensen gap and the exactly attainable envelope conditioned simultaneously on entropy and energy. That envelope is a reformulation of the author's constrained attenuator entropy optimum, not a historical priority claim. It does not settle p>1, different temperatures, or a complete quantum-memory inequality. E14 records the correct p=1 normalization and existing comparator; it is not an independently novel tensorization result.
