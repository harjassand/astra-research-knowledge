# Gene-regulatory dynamics: conditional feedback/enhancer decomposition

## Verdict

**No genuine biological breakthrough or novelty-cleared theorem survives this pass.** The strongest defensible advance is an internally derived, explicitly conditional *experimental identifiability program*: separate a locus-specific enhancer effect from a shared TF negative-feedback effect using matched upstream/TF measurements and orthogonal enhancer perturbations, then test whether a measured TF trajectory's temporal shape contributes to target transcription through a finite-memory enhancer state. This is a concrete mechanism-level hypothesis and test, not a review or an assertion that a cell state can be programmed. Both derivations are simple enough that novelty/priority remains unknown; primary literature already covers much of the surrounding biology.

No Astra biology theorem was used as a premise, and no local Astra source currently supports the biology. No nature-level claim is made. No agents were spawned.

## Why this is the load-bearing question

Recent experiments show that signaling history and chromatin history both affect responses. Wray et al. measure NEMO/complex-I and RelA in the same cells and show that equal-total-dose IL-1 pulse trains can yield disproportionately larger nuclear RelA AUC; their D2FC2 model already introduces DNA binding and NF-κB-dependent chromatin remodeling to reproduce the transition. Wang et al. jointly measure NF-κB dynamics, accessibility and gene expression during macrophage memory, and identify other relevant TF families. Luo et al. already perform dynamic enhancer CRISPRi across a cell-state transition. These results make a generic “chromatin creates memory,” “pulse timing controls transcription,” or “screen enhancers during differentiation” claim untenable. See the detailed source ledger in [NOTES.md](sources/NOTES.md).

The remaining mechanistic ambiguity is causal: at one target, apparent priming may arise because prior history increased that target's effective enhancer gain, because it altered a shared NF-κB feedback loop and therefore the TF trajectory, or because target transcription decodes TF *shape* rather than its total area. Accessibility marks alone do not identify which route is operative. The proposed interface uses calibrated effective gains and same-cell upstream/TF trajectories to separate these alternatives.

## Result A: exact sign law for a restricted feedback/enhancer interface

For the stable local model

\[
\dot x=bu-\lambda x-\gamma z,\quad
\dot m=k_m a_f x-\delta_m m,\quad
\dot z=k_p m-\delta_p z,\quad
\dot y_g=k_g a_gx-\delta_g y_g,
\]

with fully observed response tails and fixed effective gains, the ratio of *positive, nonzero integrated deviations* is

\[
R_g=r_u r_g\frac{1+L}{1+Lr_f},\qquad
L=\frac{\gamma k_pk_ma_f}{\lambda\delta_m\delta_p}.
\]

Here `r_u` is measured upstream-input AUC ratio, `r_f` is the feedback-region gain ratio, and `r_g` is the target-region gain ratio. For input-matched conditions, the exact priming boundary is `r_g=(1+L r_f)/(1+L)`. Thus a target-enhancer gain and an NFKBIA feedback-enhancer gain enter differently: one scales the output numerator; the other changes the shared signal denominator. Example values `L=4, r_g=1.5, r_f=2` yield `R_g=5/6`, while holding the feedback gain fixed yields `1.5`. The values are synthetic.

The derivation and assumptions are in [feedback_chromatin_sign_law.md](proof/feedback_chromatin_sign_law.md). If only target AUC is acquired, `L`, `r_f`, and `r_g` are structurally aliased. With measured input and same-cell RelA AUC and calibrated `r_f ≠ 1`, the restricted model gives `q=(AUC(x)'/AUC(x))/r_u=(1+L)/(1+Lr_f)` and `L=(1-q)/(q r_f-1)`. It is badly conditioned near the singular/high-feedback boundary: at synthetic `L=4, r_f=2`, `|dL/dq|=81`. Intervals should propagate this into `UNKNOWN` unless every feasible parameter corner clears a prespecified sign margin.

This is not a schedule decoder. In the LTI model, full-response AUC is schedule-independent at fixed input area. The finite RK4 replay verifies that the four-spaced-pulse/one-pulse AUC ratio is about 1, while the same synthetic gain perturbation gives target AUC ratio about 0.833 in each schedule. This correctly rejects the LTI model as an explanation of Wray et al.'s pulse-train enhancement. Code and output are [sign_law.py](code/sign_law.py) and [sign_law_run.json](code/sign_law_run.json); these are synthetic checks, not data fits.

## Result B: a finite-memory enhancer kernel that can decode TF shape

To extend beyond a fixed-gain/LTI interface, add a low-opening effective enhancer state `c`:

\[
\dot c=\alpha x-\beta c,\qquad
\dot y=\kappa(a_0+c)x-\delta y.
\]

For a nonnegative measured TF signal `x(t)`, baseline state `c(0)=0`, finite input, and complete RNA tail, direct integration yields

\[
\operatorname{AUC}(y)=\frac{\kappa}{\delta}\left[a_0\operatorname{AUC}(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int\!\int e^{-\beta|t-s|}x(t)x(s)\,ds\,dt\right].
\]

The second component depends on the TF autocorrelation, not merely TF AUC. For a rectangular TF trajectory of fixed area `A` and duration `T`, it equals `αA² f(βT)`, where `f(z)=(z-1+e^{-z})/z²=∫_0^1(1-r)e^{-zr}dr`; hence it varies strictly with duration and enhancer closure rate. This is a minimal schedule-dependent alternative for *gene-specific decoding conditional on the measured TF path*. It does not explain how upstream pulses alter RelA itself, which is already addressed by Wray et al.'s richer model.

The exact derivation and conditional identifiability boundary are in [enhancer_memory_kernel.md](proof/enhancer_memory_kernel.md). At least three waveform conditions are needed even for local identification of the baseline component, memory amplitude, and closure rate; the proposed experiment checks the sensitivity matrix's rank/conditioning before acquisition and returns `UNKNOWN` if the selected waveforms are uninformative. A closed-form synthetic check at `beta=0.02/min` gives a 1.3454-fold ratio in the opening component for equal-area 10- versus 60-minute rectangles ([script](code/kernel_check.py), [output](code/kernel_check_run.json)); no plausible biological closure-rate range or statistical power is yet known.

## Decisive test and strongest controls

In one cell context, select an NF-κB-dominant target plus an IRF/AP-1-dominant control. Randomize at least three microfluidic stimulus histories to produce distinct same-cell RelA trajectories with matched RelA AUC; include target-enhancer and NFKBIA-feedback-enhancer perturbations separately and jointly. Record NEMO/IKK and nuclear RelA in the same living cells, then measure nascent NFKBIA and target transcription across the full response tail. Calibrate effective gains and RNA decay; use ATAC/CUT&Tag as a separate association measurement, not as a substitute for effective transcriptional gain.

Fit the feedback sign law and enhancer kernel on training waveforms, then predict held-out enhancer levels and waveform histories. Compare against equally informed controls: RelA-AUC-only prediction; target-only/no-RelA regression; RelA-only/no-enhancer prediction; unconstrained per-condition coefficients; and a dynamic model with explicit co-TF covariates. Reject the decomposition if RelA/input ratios fail the shared-feedback law, target/RelA ratios depend on the feedback edit, the kernel closure parameter shifts across waveform subsets, enhancer perturbation fails to attenuate the shape effect, or held-out transcription misses preregistered noise bounds.

This work charges for two enhancer perturbations and controls, proximal/TF reporter construction, microfluidics, same-cell imaging, nascent RNA through the tail, transcript-decay/effective-gain calibration, targeted chromatin assays, independent biological preparations and a pilot for variance and sample size. Currency, sample size and throughput remain `UNKNOWN` until perturbation efficiency and pilot noise are acquired. No raw data were downloaded.

## Falsifications, scope, and next gate

- The fixed-gain sign law cannot account for schedule-dependent AUC under matched full-area inputs; that limitation was reproduced synthetically and is not hidden.
- `a_f`, `a_g`, and `c` are effective transcriptional states; ATAC, histone marks, and enhancer contacts are not identified with them.
- Co-TFs, cell-state mixtures, nonlinear saturation, receptor depletion, delays, incomplete tails, and cell growth/death may invalidate the model.
- The kernel's `beta>0` forces the modeled enhancer memory to decay after stimulus removal. Neither derivation contains a stable fate attractor, lineage readout, or proof of cell-state controllability.
- Dynamic enhancer screens, joint chromatin/TF memory predictors, NF-κB/chromatin pulse-train mechanisms, and in-silico landscape controllers already exist. The bounded source check does not establish priority or novelty.

**Next gate:** do not call this an Astra breakthrough or biological discovery. First complete a broader primary-prior-art/identifiability scan, then obtain pilot estimates for effective perturbation, same-cell noise, RNA tails, and plausible enhancer closure times. Proceed to the factorial/waveform experiment only if those estimates show that the sensitivity matrix is conditioned well enough to distinguish the feedback and locus routes. A successful result would still be an experimentally tested transient transcriptional-memory mechanism, not yet a programmable lineage transition.
