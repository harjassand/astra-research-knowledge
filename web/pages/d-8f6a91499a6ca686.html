SU(m) spectral-growth gate: primary-source audit
Date checked: 2026-10-08
Scope: tensor multiplicity in End(V_lambda), zero-weight growth, and the interface with a rank-constrained Casimir profile. This memo records prior art and a short consequence; it makes no novelty claim.

1. The stated tensor-multiplicity upper bound is already an exact consequence of Fronsdal–Hirai (and they prove a stronger singular-weight form).

Primary source: C. Fronsdal and T. Hirai, “A remark on the tensor product of irreducible representations of a compact Lie group,” Japan. J. Math. 7(1) (1981), 201–211, received 1980-07-02, DOI 10.4099/math1924.7.201. Primary publisher scan: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/math1924/7/1/7_1_201/_pdf

Theorem 1, p. 202 (notation cleaned up): for connected compact G, irreducibles U_1,U_2 with highest weights lambda_1,lambda_2, and longest Weyl element w_0, U_1 tensor U_2 embeds as a G-representation in Ind_T^G chi_{lambda_1+w_0 lambda_2}. The character is the corresponding torus character. Setting U_2=U_1^* (highest weight -w_0 lambda_1) makes this character trivial, so

  End(U_1) = U_1 tensor U_1^*  <=  Ind_T^G 1 = L^2(G/T).

Thus, by Frobenius reciprocity / Peter–Weyl, for every irreducible V_nu,

  mult(V_nu, End(V_lambda)) <= dim(V_nu^T) = dim V_nu[0].                 (1)

No regularity assumption on lambda is needed for (1); only G connected compact and finite-dimensional irreducibles. The paper’s Theorem 2 and following corollary improve this: with G(lambda) the coadjoint stabilizer (centralizer) of lambda, End(V_lambda) embeds in Ind_{G(lambda)}^G 1. Therefore

  mult(V_nu, End(V_lambda)) <= dim(V_nu^{G(lambda)}) <= dim(V_nu^T).       (2)

For regular lambda, G(lambda)=T, and (1) is the specialized form of (2). For singular SU(m) weights, (2) is strictly the more informative statement when the larger Levi centralizer has fewer fixed vectors. The paper’s proof reduces to semisimple simply connected G in its proof section, which covers SU(m); the theorem is stated for connected compact G. Haar measure in the induced representation is normalized invariant measure. The inequality is dimensionless and independent of Casimir conventions.

A later primary source states the same mechanism as cyclicity. Matthew Dawson and Yessica Hernández-Eliseo, “A Representation-Theoretic Approach to Toeplitz Quantization on Flag Manifolds,” arXiv:2406.10804v2 [math.RT], 2025-02-12, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10804 and https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10804v2. Theorem 3.10: for compact Lie G and irreducible unitary pi,rho, a highest-weight vector v in pi tensor a lowest-weight vector w in rho is cyclic for pi tensor rho. Apply rho=bar(pi), w=bar(v): v_lambda tensor bar(v_lambda) is cyclic in End(V_lambda). They cite Fronsdal–Hirai (1981) and Tatsuma (1982). This gives the same multiplicity cap because a cyclic vector fixed by the stabilizer can generate at most dim(V_nu^{G(lambda)}) copies of V_nu.

There is also a useful sharpness result in Dawson–Hernández-Eliseo, Corollary 7.4. Fix S subset of simple roots and its Levi L. For a sequence lambda_n supported on Pi\S, with min_{Pi\S} lambda_{n,i} growing faster than every C log n, max_{Pi\S} lambda_{n,i} <= n^D for some fixed D, and V_{lambda_n} self-conjugate, each fixed irreducible pi with V_pi^L nonzero eventually satisfies

  mult(pi, V_{lambda_n} tensor bar(V_{lambda_n})) = dim(V_pi^L).

For regular SU(m) carriers take S empty and L=T. For example lambda_n=n rho is self-conjugate in type A and obeys these growth conditions. This is fixed-target eventual equality, not a uniform-in-pi cutoff or a statement about the whole spectral profile.

2. PRV is a component-existence theorem, not a total-multiplicity upper bound.

Primary source: K. R. Parthasarathy, R. Ranga Rao, V. S. Varadarajan, “Representations of complex semi-simple Lie groups and Lie algebras,” Ann. Math. 85(3) (1967), 383–429, DOI 10.2307/1970351, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1967/85-3/p03. The standard PRV component associated with the longest Weyl element occurs in a tensor product. The all-Weyl version is the PRV conjecture proved by Shrawan Kumar, “Proof of the Parthasarathy-Ranga Rao-Varadarajan conjecture,” Invent. Math. 93 (1988), 117–130, DOI 10.1007/BF01393689, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01393689.

For dominant lambda,mu and w in W, write overline(lambda+w mu) for the dominant Weyl conjugate. The PRV conclusion is that V_{overline(lambda+w mu)} occurs at least once in V_lambda tensor V_mu. Kumar’s refinement says this distinguished component occurs exactly once in the cyclic submodule U(g)(v_lambda tensor v_{w mu}) generated by the selected extremal tensor. “Exactly once in that cyclic submodule” does not upper-bound how many copies occur in the full tensor product: additional copies may be generated from other vectors. Thus PRV alone cannot prove (1) or any upper bound on total multiplicity. The Fronsdal–Hirai induced-representation inclusion does prove a whole-representation upper bound and is the direct antecedent for (1).

3. Zero-weight multiplicity and its growth in type A.

Use roots alpha_ij=e_i-e_j (1<=i<j<=m), rank r=m-1, and N=|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2. With K(x)=#{(n_alpha) in Z_{>=0}^N: sum_alpha n_alpha alpha=x}, Kostant’s primary formula is

  dim V_nu[0] = sum_{w in S_m} (-1)^ell(w) K(w(nu+rho)-rho).             (3)

See Bertram Kostant, “A formula for the multiplicity of a weight,” Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 44(6) (1958), 588–589, DOI 10.1073/pnas.44.6.588, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528626/ . Formula (3) is an alternating sum: replacing it by one raw partition count as an equality is wrong. Independently, PBW gives the safe upper dim V_nu[0] <= K(nu): the zero-weight space of the irreducible highest-weight module is a quotient of the zero-weight space of the Verma module, whose PBW basis is indexed by the nonnegative positive-root decompositions of nu.

For A_{m-1}, the positive-root matrix is the incidence matrix of the complete directed graph after deleting one row; it has rank r and N columns. Its Kostant partition function has piecewise-polynomial degree N-r=(m-1)(m-2)/2. More directly for the needed representation-weight growth, Billey–Guillemin–Rassart prove an exact type-A result:

Sara Billey, Victor Guillemin, Etienne Rassart, “A vector partition function for the multiplicities of sl_k C,” J. Algebra 278(1) (2004), 251–293, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2003.12.005. The primary 2003 version is arXiv:math/0307227v1 (2003-07-16), https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307227 and https://arxiv.org/html/math/0307227.

Their Theorem 5.1 states that for type A_{k-1}, on a finite chamber complex the weight multiplicity pieces are polynomials of degree binom(k-1,2) in the weight beta coordinates, with coefficients of degree binom(k-1,2) in highest-weight lambda coordinates. This is exactly N-r for k=m. For a singular highest weight with coordinate-equality block sizes k_1,...,k_l, their discussion at (41) gives the sharper degree bound

  (m^2 - sum_j k_j^2)/2 - m + 1 = dim(O_lambda)/2 - rank(SU(m)).       (4)

The regular case k_j=1 gives binom(m-1,2); singular strata have smaller degree, e.g. highest-weight pattern (1,m-1) gives degree zero. The source also identifies the leading term with the Duistermaat–Heckman density and gives a pointwise scaling result (Corollary 5.2); no normalization of that density is needed for the upper bound used here. A concrete sharp-degree check from its A_2 chamber polynomials is SU(3): for lambda=n rho=(n,0,-n), dim V_lambda[0]=n+1, so the generic exponent N-r=1 is attained.

Consequently, for fixed m and any dominant nu,

  dim V_nu[0] <= C_m (1+||nu||)^{N-r},                                  (5)

with a sharper exponent on singular Weyl strata. Here ||nu|| can be any fixed norm in the weight space. The constant depends on m, not nu. The chamber-polynomial statement gives this uniform bound because there are finitely many chambers; the PBW/Kostant argument is an independent coarse proof of the exponent. Do not infer a positive leading coefficient on every ray merely from the PBW upper bound; root-lattice admissibility and chamber position matter for lower asymptotics.

4. What this implies for a rank-constrained Casimir profile, and what literature covers.

Fix an irreducible carrier H=V_lambda of dimension d_lambda. Let L be a positive self-adjoint operator on HS(H) commuting with conjugation by SU(m), and let Pi_t=1_[0,t](L), D_lambda(t)=Tr Pi_t. The elementary partial-trace / Schmidt-rank estimate is

  <vec(X), Pi_t vec(X)> <= min(1, Q D_lambda(t)/d_lambda),
  <X,LX> >= integral_0^infinity [1-Q D_lambda(t)/d_lambda]_+ dt,       (6)

for ||X||_HS=1 and rank(X)<=Q. Proof: Gamma_t=Tr_2(Pi_t) commutes with the irreducible carrier, so Gamma_t=(D_lambda(t)/d_lambda)I; if P is the range projection of X, |vec X><vec X|<=P tensor I, whence low-band mass <=Tr(P Gamma_t)<=Q D_lambda(t)/d_lambda. Integrate the complementary low-band probability by layer cake. No log-Sobolev/Poincare/KLS hypothesis is used.

For the quadratic Casimir, make the metric normalization explicit: take Hermitian generators T_a with Tr_fund(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2, long roots squared length 2, and L(X)=sum_a[T_a,[T_a,X]]. Then L acts on the V_nu-isotypic conjugation sector by c_2(nu)=(nu,nu+2rho)/2, and

  D_lambda(t)=sum_{nu: c_2(nu)<=t} mult(V_nu,End(V_lambda)) dim(V_nu).

Combining (1) and (5) gives

  D_lambda(t) <= sum_{nu: c_2(nu)<=t} dim(V_nu[0])dim(V_nu)
               <= C_m(1+t)^N.                                           (7)

Indeed ||nu||=O_m(sqrt(t)); Weyl’s dimension formula gives dim(V_nu)=O_m((1+||nu||)^N); the number of dominant weights in the rank-r Casimir ball is O_m((1+t)^(r/2)); and the zero-weight multiplicity contributes O_m((1+||nu||)^(N-r)). The exponents combine to N. Equations (6)–(7) therefore yield the coarse general lower rate

  inf_{||X||_HS=1, rank X<=Q} <X,LX> >= c_m (d_lambda/Q)^(1/N)

whenever d_lambda/Q is larger than a fixed m-dependent threshold. This is a consequence of existing multiplicity/dimension estimates plus the elementary profile argument; it is not a matching upper bound, an exact finite spectral profile, or an independently sourced theorem about low-rank Casimir minimizers.

The exact rank-profile mechanism itself is close in structure to Gorjan Alagic and Alexander Russell, “Uncertainty Principles for Compact Groups,” arXiv:math/0608702v2 (2008-08-29), Illinois J. Math. 52(4) (2008), 1315–1324, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608702 and https://arxiv.org/html/math/0608702v2. Their Theorem 1 is an exact Hilbert–Schmidt operator identity on L^2(G) for a multiplication-local operator and a left-equivariant operator; Theorem 2 gives mu(supp f) sum_rho d_rho rank(fhat(rho))>=1. It yields support-volume versus spectral-band profiles by layer cake, but its constrained quantity is spatial support (or Fourier matrix ranks), not the matrix rank of one X in End(V_lambda). It does not state (6) as a rank-Q Casimir theorem on a single carrier. Standard Faber–Krahn theorems likewise constrain function support volume, not Schmidt/matrix rank. In this bounded primary-source audit, the closest exact total-multiplicity source is Fronsdal–Hirai (1981), the closest type-A growth source is Billey–Guillemin–Rassart (2004), and the closest profile-identity source is Alagic–Russell (2008). I did not identify a primary theorem that already states the full rank-Q Casimir profile (6) for arbitrary irreducible carriers. This bounded search is not an absence or priority proof.

5. Source inventory / scope cautions.

* Fronsdal–Hirai, 1981, J-stage primary PDF above: Theorems 1–2 and corollary; connected compact groups; exact induced-representation upper, stronger stabilizer form.
* Parthasarathy–Ranga Rao–Varadarajan, 1967, Annals page/DOI above; Kumar, 1988, DOI above: PRV component occurrence; no upper bound on all multiplicity copies.
* Kostant, 1958, PNAS/PMC above: exact alternating weight formula.
* Billey–Guillemin–Rassart, arXiv v1 dated 2003-07-16 and J. Algebra version 2004: exact type-A chamber polynomial and zero-weight degree; root/weight lattice condition lambda-beta in root lattice is explicit.
* Dawson–Hernández-Eliseo, arXiv v2 dated 2025-02-12: cyclicity and fixed-target eventual multiplicity equality under stated sequence assumptions.
* Alagic–Russell, arXiv v2 dated 2008-08-29: compact-group operator/support-Fourier uncertainty, a related but differently constrained profile.

No external theorem in this memo uses the candidate Casimir generator normalization unless explicitly stated in section 4; representation multiplicities and induced-representation inclusions are metric-independent. No KLS-equivalent premise is needed for (6) or the coarse combination (7). Novelty, priority, and independent validation of any broader rank-constrained Casimir profile remain unassessed.
