1. POINTWISE COHERENT VARIANCE AND WORD ESTIMATES

Let T_i be an orthonormal Hermitian Lie basis in the physics metric, with
sum_i T_i^2=C_Lambda I, and let v_x be a coherent highest-weight orbit
vector. Its generator mean vector is Ad_x Lambda, so its squared norm is
||Lambda||^2. Consequently

  sum_i Var_x(T_i)=C_Lambda-||Lambda||^2=2(Lambda,rho).

For B_i=T_i/(L_G N), this gives the source's exact expression

  sum_i Var_x(B_i)=2(Lambda,rho)/(L_G^2 N^2).

Writing Lambda=sum n_j omega_j and N=sum n_j, Cauchy-Schwarz yields
2(Lambda,rho)<=2N(max_j||omega_j||)||rho||, which is precisely
v_G/N for the stated v_G. Each B_i is a contraction because
||Lambda||<=sum n_j||omega_j||<=L_G N. Thus each individual variance
is at most v_G/N, uniformly pointwise. This is not an averaged-in-x
claim.

For a contraction word W=B_(i1)...B_(il), the displayed telescoping
identity is exact: it replaces one factor at a time, with matrix prefixes
and scalar symbol suffixes. Applying it to v_x and using
||(B_i-a_i)v_x||^2=Var_x(B_i) gives

  ||(W-w(x)I)v_x||<=l sqrt(v_G/N).

For the expectation bound, the j-th telescoping term is
<v,L(B_ij-a_ij)v> times a scalar suffix, where L is the prefix. The
mean-zero centered factor permits replacing L^*v by its component
orthogonal to v. That centered vector has norm no larger than
||(L^*-w_prefix^* I)v||, since the expectation is the least-squares
scalar approximation. Applying the same word telescoping estimate to
the reversed adjoint prefix bounds it by (j-1)sqrt(v_G/N). Multiplying
by the centered-factor norm gives (j-1)v_G/N. Summing j=1,...,l gives

  |Tr(P_x W)-w(x)|<=l(l-1)v_G/(2N).

This check uses neither commutation nor an assumed scalar symbol
calculus. The noncommutative order and the real scalar suffixes are
preserved exactly.

2. HAMILTONIAN SYMBOL, SECOND MOMENT, AND EXPONENTIAL TAYLOR

Rewriting a degree<=D polynomial in B_i costs at most
A_eff=A L_G^D because each natural T_i/N contributes L_G B_i and
L_G>=1. Replacing a Hermitian operator expression by half the sum of
itself and its formal adjoint preserves the represented P, degree and
does not increase the coefficient l1 sum. Since the B_i symbols are
real and adjoint reverses words/conjugates coefficients, the resulting
commutative symbol f is real with |f|<=A_eff.

Coefficient triangle inequalities and the word estimates give

  |Tr(P_x P)-f(x)|
      <= A_eff D(D-1)v_G/(2N),
  Tr(P_x(P-f(x)I)^2)
      =||(P-f(x)I)v_x||^2
      <= A_eff^2 D^2 v_G/N.

The second line is valid because P is Hermitian and f(x) is real; the
vector bound sums the centered word vectors before squaring, so it
retains cancellations and does not assume a classical product law.

For Y in the spectral measure of P at v_x, both Y and f(x) lie in
[-H_star,H_star], where H_star=max(h0,A_eff). Taylor's theorem with
second derivative bounded by exp(H_star) gives

  |<e^Y>-e^f|
   <= e^f |<Y>-f| +(e^(H_star)/2)<(Y-f)^2>.

Dividing by e^f>=e^(-A_eff) produces exactly

  |Tr(P_x e^P)/e^(f(x))-1|<=kappa/N,
  kappa=(v_G/2){A_eff D(D-1)
                   +e^(H_star+A_eff)A_eff^2D^2}.

The first-order and second-order constants, including the factor 1/2
in the Taylor remainder, match the source. This is a pointwise relative
raw Husimi estimate, not yet a comparison between normalized laws.

3. NORMALIZED HUSIMI LAW, KL, AND PRODUCT TV

Let z=tau(e^P), and let mu be Haar probability on the coherent orbit.
The coherent POVM density for rho=e^P/Tr(e^P)=e^P/(d z) is

  q(x)=Tr(P_x e^P)/z.

The classical symbol density p(x)=e^(f(x))/Z has
q/p=r/E_p r, where r=Tr(P_xe^P)/e^f and E_p r=z/Z follows from
integral P_x dmu=I/d. Thus normalization on both sides is correctly
included. If u=kappa/N<=1/2 and |r-1|<=u, then E_p r>=1-u and

  |q/p-1|<=2u/(1-u)<=4u.

It follows that chi^2(q||p)<=16u^2. The inequality
D(q||p)<=chi^2(q||p) follows directly from log t<=t-1. For n
independent copies of the same parameter, KL adds, giving at most
16 n kappa^2/N^2; Pinsker with natural logarithms yields

  TV(q^tensor n,p^tensor n)<=sqrt(8) kappa sqrt(n)/N.

All normalization and Pinsker factors match. In particular, using only
the unnormalized relative estimate |r-1|<=u without centering by
E_p r would not justify this quadratic KL rate; the source correctly
performs that normalization step.
