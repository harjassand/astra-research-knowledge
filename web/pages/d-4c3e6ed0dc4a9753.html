1. FINITE-FIELD / BINARY CORNER TRANSFER: LINES 31-53

The calculation is valid. Let F=F_(2^d), M=2^d-1, and alpha generate F^*. Then F2[alpha]=F, so the minimal polynomial f has degree d. Since alpha^M=1, f divides t^M-1. M is odd, so the derivative is t^(M-1), which has no common factor with t^M-1. Thus f and h=(t^M-1)/f are coprime. CRT gives F2[C_M] ~= F2[t]/(f) x F2[t]/(h). Identifying the first factor with F through evaluation at alpha produces a central idempotent e and a nonunital map into the full ring iota:F->eA with iota(1)=e. This is precisely the corner structure needed; silently treating iota as unital into A would fail, but the candidate does not do so.

Coefficientwise extension is injective because the F2-vector space decomposes over distinct G0 coordinates; multiplying group terms introduces no extra twist since C_M commutes with G0. Its image has identity e, and (1-e) annihilates that image on both sides. Therefore
  a'b'=(1-e)+iota(ab)=1,
  b'a'-1=iota(ba-1)!=0,
and the defect is r=iota(1-ba). The full target ring has a scalar F2 witness, not merely a matrix witness or a Bernoulli tensor-power identity.

The d=1 edge case is correct: M=1, f=t-1, h=1, the second CRT factor is the zero ring, e=1, and the construction is simply the original F2 witness. The same ring calculation works for F_(p^d) into F_p[C_(p^d-1)]. The binary theorem correctly assumes characteristic two at lines 8-9. No corresponding characteristic-changing field embedding is proved, and this result must not be quoted as a binary consequence from an arbitrary odd-characteristic witness.

Finite presentation claim at line 16 is correct: a direct product of a finitely presented group with a finite cyclic group is finitely presented, and adjoining the free factor Z preserves finite presentation. Torsion is retained, as lines 20-21 explicitly acknowledge.

Cost, including candidate lines 276-279: if S0=supp(1-ba) in the original field group ring, then S=supp r is contained in S0 x C_M. Hence SS^(-1) is contained in (S0 S0^(-1)) x C_M, giving the sharper bound m<=M |S0 S0^(-1)|+1<=M |S0|^2+1. This is a finite algebraic support bound, not a word-problem or Borel-color computation bound. The elementary Rokhlin upper bound in lines 17-19 is also correct: the binary one-coordinate partition is generating and has entropy log2. Infinite weak Pinsker entropy and this finite Rokhlin upper bound are different statements about different invariants.

2. EFFECTIVE ACTION KERNEL: LINES 57-98

The lemma is correct for the stated countably infinite L. H_r is a subgroup, and each t in H_r permutes the finite nonempty support S. For fixed s in S the map t->ts is injective, so |H_r|<=|S|. Equality of the two h-row forms occurs precisely when h^(-1)g^(-1)h r=r. Since H_r is a subgroup this is equivalent to h^(-1)gh in H_r, and thus the pointwise action kernel is its normal core N_r.

For g outside N_r, J is nonempty. If C_L(g) is infinite, a whole infinite left centralizer coset through any h0 in J remains in J. If the centralizer is finite, each conjugacy preimage of an element of the finite H_r is one finite centralizer coset, so J's complement is finite. Thus J is infinite in either case. Nonzero row differences are uniform F_q linear forms. Their windows hS union g^(-1)hS admit arbitrarily large disjoint subfamilies: a collision with a fixed finite set excludes only h=z s^(-1) or h=g z s^(-1). Independence then gives q^(-n), proving that each g outside the core fixes a null set. Countability gives almost surely stabilizer exactly N_r.

This is a valid generalization of my narrower block proof and is independently understandable from its rows. It does not rely on mixing to dispose of torsion. The finite-normal-kernel caveat in the independent report is exactly represented by N_r.

Primary source comparison confirms the stated prior-art boundary: Tucker-Drob, arXiv:1208.0655v1 (2012), Theorem 1.4 and Corollaries 1.5-1.6, says nontrivial totally ergodic actions have almost surely a fixed finite normal stabilizer, and faithful mixing actions are free. The original proof uses a substantial finite-group-theoretic input; the candidate's linear-row special case avoids it. Neither the general phenomenon nor a novelty claim is established by this special-case reconstruction.

3. FREE-PRODUCT STEP AND ALGEBRAIC ABSORPTION: LINES 102-124

These steps are correct. The original G1-group ring injects into that of G1*Z. Since S is contained in G1, tS=S forces t in G1, so H_r is contained in G1. If n is in its normal core, both n and z^(-1)nz lie in G1; free-product reduced words force n=e. Hence K is essentially free even with arbitrary original torsion and the finite cyclic corner factor.

4. BOREL COLOR AND RIGHT INVERSE: LINES 128-160

The degree is |SS^(-1)|-1 and d0 colors suffice. The supplied countable-algebra construction gives countably many Borel independent color classes. Greedy selection yields a Borel maximal independent set; each remaining vertex loses at least one neighbor. Repeating until the remaining graph is empty gives D+1 classes for maximum degree D. This establishes the needed coloring without relying on an unavailable original KST full text.

The orbit coloring chi_eta(g)=f(g^(-1)eta) has both required properties. A support overlap makes g^(-1)h an element of SS^(-1), and freeness makes the corresponding orbit vertices distinct. Its covariance identity is exact. For fixed seed, distinct same-color sites read disjoint binary input windows; different colors use independent reservoirs. Conditional finite-coordinate laws are therefore exactly Bernoulli.

The binary support coefficients are all one, so w_g=P delta_(g s0) has w_g(g)=1. It vanishes at other selected sites of that color. At fixed h, only g=h s s0^(-1) with s in S can contribute to the right-inverse sum. Coordinatewise partial sums eventually stabilize, and K is closed; hence the sum lies in K. This local finiteness also proves fixed-seed continuity and joint Borel measurability. The candidate's extension at lines 182-184 correctly inverts r_(s0) for an arbitrary finite field.

5. COMPLETE RESIDUAL INDEPENDENCE: LINES 162-180

This is the load-bearing distinction between an ordinary factor and a direct product, and it is handled correctly. For fixed eta, Q_eta=I-S_eta T_eta is a continuous idempotent onto ker T_eta. The map kappa->(T_eta kappa,Q_eta kappa) has inverse (y,r0)->r0+S_eta y, so it is a compact-group isomorphism. Its pushforward of Haar is therefore the product of Haar on X and Haar on the kernel. The X law is the same for every seed, so integrating the conditional laws gives independence from the full pair (eta,r0), not only from eta.

For a polished version, make two routine measurable details explicit: R is the countable intersection of the Borel coordinate equations T_eta(r0)(g)=0, and all seed measures/formulas are restricted to the conull invariant K0. Then R is a standard Borel probability space, and its law is exactly the stated pushforward. Covariance of T_eta and S_eta makes R invariant and that measure G-invariant. These details follow directly from the formulas and do not constitute a missing theorem.

6. BINARY ENTROPY AND COUNTABLE STABILIZATION: LINES 188-203

The finite identities correctly yield one fixed residual R and X ~= X^n x R^(n-1) for all n. The finite simplex construction supplies each h>0 and its positive complement n log2-h. Seward's Theorem 1.1 then identifies X^n with the product of those Bernoulli shifts, so every finite h appears as a direct Bernoulli factor.

The infinity statement can be shortened: X^n itself is a direct Bernoulli factor with entropy n log2, and these entropies diverge. Seward is not needed for that supremum, but is valid for the stronger prescribed-h statement. The checked definition in Bowen-Tucker-Drob, arXiv:1805.03552, Section 0.B.iv, is exactly the supremum of base entropies of direct Bernoulli factors.

The countable-stabilization argument is also correct: regroup countably many K coordinates into blocks of size m, apply the finite isomorphism coordinatewise, and note that the nonatomic standard base F2^N absorbs every standard probability base under measure-space isomorphism. This yields K^N absorption. It does not turn the canonical infinite defect factor map from X into a conjugacy.
