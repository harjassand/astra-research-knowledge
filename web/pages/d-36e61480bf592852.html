Abstract
    

The rational identity testing problem, RIT, asks whether an input rational circuit, i.e. a noncommutative arithmetic circuit that has inversion gates, computes the zero element of the free skew field. For rational formulas the problem is known to be in deterministic polynomial time [GGOW20], [IQS18]. For rational circuits of size ss the associated linear pencil has dimension 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}, and the known algorithms use exponential time and exponential space.
    

We show in this note that RIT for rational circuits over ℚ\mathbb{Q} and finite fields, including the question whether the circuit is a correct computation at every inversion gate, is decidable in deterministic space O⁡(sO⁡(1))O(s^{O(1)}), and hence is in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE}. As a consequence, noncommutative PIT for degree unrestricted input noncommutative circuits is also in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE}. The proof depends on algorithms that take succinct representations of their input.
It has the following three observations that we put together to obtain the
𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE} upper bound.
    


• 



Analyzing the Hrubes-Wigderson construction of a linear pencil for an input rational formula, we obtain a succinctly represented linear pencil AA of size 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)} for the input rational circuit of size ss. More precisely, given indices ii and jj for the linear pencil we can compute Ai,jA_{i,j} in space polynomial in ss and length of i,ji,j. This algorithm essentially gives a succinct presentation of the linear pencil AA obtained by the reduction.


• 



By the recent theorem of Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar, Raj and Thierauf that deciding whether or not a symbolic matrix ∑iAi​xi\sum_{i}A_{i}x_{i} has full noncommutative rank is in 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC}. We note that their algorithm is
logspace-uniform 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC} and we use it as a black-box for computing rank of succinctly represented pencil AA.


• 



Finally, we note a folklore simulation which we state and prove in the form we need. If a problem is solved by a logspace-uniform family of deterministic Boolean circuits of polylogarithmic depth, and its input is not written down but is presented by a polylogspace subroutine that returns any requested input bit, then the circuit can be evaluated in polylogarithmic space. Hence, simulating a depth O⁡(logi⁡M)O(\log^{i}M) circuit for succinctly presented inputs of length M=2Θ⁡(s)M=2^{\Theta(s)} yields a polynomial space algorithm.


  




1 Introduction




Let ℚ⁡⟨X⟩=ℚ⁡⟨x1,…,xn⟩\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle=\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle be the free associative algebra over ℚ\mathbb{Q} in noncommuting variables and let ℚ⁡(⟨X⟩)\mathbb{Q}(\!\langle X\rangle\!) be its universal field of fractions, the free skew field of Amitsur and Cohn [Coh95, Coh06]. A noncommutative rational circuit is an arithmetic circuit with gates {+,×,}−1\{+,\times,{}^{-1}\} over ℚ\mathbb{Q} and inputs from X∪ℚX\cup\mathbb{Q}. An inversion gate is legal precisely when its argument is a nonzero element of the skew field. A circuit all of whose inversion gates are legal is called correct, and it computes an element Φ^\widehat{\Phi} of ℚ⁡(⟨X⟩)\mathbb{Q}(\!\langle X\rangle\!). The rational identity testing problem, RIT, is to decide, given a circuit Φ\Phi, whether Φ\Phi is correct, and if it is, whether Φ^=0\widehat{\Phi}=0.



Prior work.




The algorithmic study of RIT was initiated by Hrubeš and Wigderson [HW15]. They reduced RIT for rational formulas to the question whether an associated linear pencil




L=Q0+∑k=1nQk​xk,Qk∈ℚN×N,L\;=\;Q_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k}x_{k},\qquad Q_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}^{N\times N},



of dimension NN linear in the formula size, is full, that is, invertible over the free skew field. Through this reduction, RIT for formulas is known to be in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE} by the Cohn–Reutenauer criterion [CR99], which expresses non-fullness of a polynomial-size pencil as the feasibility of a system of commutative polynomial equations in polynomially many unknowns. It is in deterministic polynomial time in the white-box model, by the operator scaling algorithm of Garg, Gurvits, Oliveira and Wigderson [GGOW16, GGOW20] over ℚ\mathbb{Q}, and by the constructive rank algorithm of Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam [IQS18] over ℚ\mathbb{Q} and also over finite fields. We also know a deterministic 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC}-Turing reduction from RIT for formulas to the linear matrix rank problem [AJ24]. In the black-box model there is a deterministic quasipolynomial-time algorithm [ACM24].
For rational circuits the situation is very different. A circuit of size ss unrolls into a formula of size 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}, so its pencil has dimension 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}. The polynomial-time rank algorithms run on that pencil in time and space 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}, and the Cohn–Reutenauer route meets a polynomial system with 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)} unknowns, giving only 𝖤𝖷𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{EXPSPACE}. To the best of our knowledge, no polynomial-space upper bound for RIT on circuits was known.




Proof Overview.





1. 



The first step is some well-known complexity theory. A deterministic (logspace) uniform family ofbounded fanin Boolean circuits of depth D⁡(M)D(M) can be evaluated in deterministic workspace O⁡(D⁡(M))O(D(M)), provided D⁡(M)D(M) is at least logarithmic in the circuit size [Bor77, Ruz81]. For uniform 𝖭𝖢i\mathsf{NC}^{i} this is the containment 𝖭𝖢i⊆𝖣𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤⁡(logi⁡M)\mathsf{NC}^{i}\subseteq\mathsf{DSPACE}(\log^{i}M). It is known that presenting an input succinctly, by a small Boolean circuit that returns the bit at a given position, raises the complexity of a problem by one exponential [GW83, PY86, BLT92] given that the succinct presentation
circuit of size ss is for inputs of length 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}.


2. 



Putting the two together, suppose the input string has length M=2Θ⁡(s)M=2^{\Theta(s)} but is presented by a succinct circuit description of size ss, then a 𝖣𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤⁡(logi⁡M)\mathsf{DSPACE}(\log^{i}M) algorithm essentially becomes a 𝖣𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤⁡(si)\mathsf{DSPACE}(s^{i}) algorithm, because logi⁡(2Θ⁡(s))=Θ⁡(si)\log^{i}(2^{\Theta(s)})=\Theta(s^{i}). A space-bounded machine never needs its input written down. It holds a position index, which is only Θ⁡(s)\Theta(s) bits, and calls the succinct description circuit whenever it wants a bit. In other words, the succinct version of any problem in deterministic uniform 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC} is in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE}. This is folklore. Nevertheless, we state it as Theorem 2.5 and prove it, because the exact hypotheses matter for the main result.


3. 



Now, coming to the RIT problem for rational circuits, based on Hrubes-Wigderson construction [HW15], we observe that a rational circuit of size ss linearizes into a pencil LL of dimension N=2O⁡(s)N=2^{O(s)} whose fullness of rank decides the identity testing question. Although the pencil LL is too large to write down, we argue that each entry L⁡[i,j]L[i,j] is computable from the indices i,ji,j in space O⁡(sO⁡(1))O(s^{O(1)}). This algorithm therefore is a succinct presentation of the linear pencil LL as input to the noncommutative rank problem, where the succinct representation’s size is O⁡(sO⁡(1))O(s^{O(1)}).


4. 



Now, we will crucially need the result of Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar, Raj and Thierauf [CGGRT26] who proved checking if a homogeneous noncommutative linear pencil ∑iAi​xi\sum_{i}A_{i}x_{i} has full noncommutative rank is in 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC}. We will apply the polylogspace simulation, described above, of the logspace-uniform 𝖭𝖢2\mathsf{NC}^{2} algorithm of [CGGRT26] with input as the succinctly presented linear pencil LL. This entire simulation can be carried out in deterministic space O⁡(log2⁡M)+O⁡(sO⁡(1))=O⁡(sO⁡(1))O(\log^{2}M)+O(s^{O(1)})=O(s^{O(1)}) which is essentially
polynomial in ss.





Theorem 1.1 (Theorem 6.2).



There is a deterministic Turing machine which, given a noncommutative rational circuit Φ\Phi of size ss over ℚ\mathbb{Q} in variables x1,…,xnx_{1},\dots,x_{n}, decides in space O⁡(sO⁡(1))O(s^{O(1)}):


(a) 



whether Φ\Phi is a correct circuit, identifying an inversion gate whose argument evaluates to 00 if it is not; and


(b) 



if Φ\Phi is correct, whether Φ^=0\widehat{\Phi}=0 in the free skew field.



In particular noncommutative PIT for degree-unrestricted noncommutative circuits is in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE}.







2 Uniform parallel algorithms on succinct inputs




Throughout this section “workspace” counts worktape cells of a deterministic Turing machine. A region of workspace is called reusable if it may be overwritten between calls, so that its size is added and not multiplied. Logarithms are base 22.




2.1 Succinct inputs



Definition 2.1 (succinct presentation; bit oracle).



Let M∈ℕM\in\mathbb{N} and x∈{0,1}Mx\in\{0,1\}^{M}. A succinct presentation of xx with parameters (b,σ)(b,\sigma) consists of


(i) 



the number MM, written in binary, with M≤2bM\leq 2^{b}; and


(ii) 



a deterministic subroutine, the bit oracle, which on input j∈[M]j\in[M] written in binary outputs the bit xjx_{j}, using reusable workspace at most σ\sigma.



An algorithm decides LL on succinctly presented inputs if, given bb and access to the bit oracle as a subroutine, it decides whether x∈Lx\in L.





The case described in the introduction is the special case in which the bit oracle is a Boolean circuit.



Example 2.2 (the circuit presentation).



Let CC be a Boolean circuit with nn inputs, one output and ss gates, and let




xC:=(C⁡(⟨1⟩),C⁡(⟨2⟩),…,C⁡(⟨2n⟩))∈{0,1}2n,x_{C}\;:=\;\bigl(C(\langle 1\rangle),\,C(\langle 2\rangle),\,\dots,\,C(\langle 2^{n}\rangle)\bigr)\;\in\;\{0,1\}^{2^{n}},



where ⟨j⟩\langle j\rangle is the nn-bit binary encoding of j−1j-1. Then CC is a succinct presentation of xCx_{C} with M=2nM=2^{n}, b=nb=n and σ=O⁡(s)\sigma=O(s). Indeed, evaluating CC at a given input is an instance of the circuit value problem, and a circuit of size ss is evaluated in workspace O⁡(s)O(s) by computing the gate values in topological order and storing all ss of them. Here n≤sn\leq s, so b≤sb\leq s.





We will also use a presentation which is not a Boolean circuit. In Section 6 the bit oracle is the entry oracle of a linear pencil. Definition 2.1 is deliberately agnostic about how the oracle is implemented, and only its workspace σ\sigma enters the bounds.





2.2 Uniform circuit families



Definition 2.3 (uniform family).



Let 𝒞={CM}M≥1\mathcal{C}=\{C_{M}\}_{M\geq 1} be a family of Boolean circuits where CMC_{M} has MM inputs, one output and fan-in at most 22. The family has size S⁡(M)S(M) and depth D⁡(M)D(M) if CMC_{M} has at most S⁡(M)S(M) gates and depth at most D⁡(M)D(M). Gates of CMC_{M} are named by binary strings of length λ⁡(M)=O⁡(log⁡S⁡(M))\lambda(M)=O(\log S(M)).




The family is uniform with uniformity space u⁡(M)u(M) if there is a single deterministic procedure which, given MM in binary and using reusable workspace at most u⁡(M)u(M),


(i) 



returns the name of the output gate of CMC_{M};


(ii) 



on a gate name gg, returns the type of gg, which is one of ∧\wedge, ∨\vee, ¬\neg, a Boolean constant, or “input gate number jj” together with j∈[M]j\in[M] in binary; and


(iii) 



on a gate name gg and a child index c∈{1,2}c\in\{1,2\}, returns the name of the cc-th child of gg.







Items (i) to (iii) are the usual direct connection presentation of a circuit family. Both of the standard uniformity conditions supply them within the space we need. 𝖣𝖫𝖮𝖦𝖳𝖨𝖬𝖤\mathsf{DLOGTIME}-uniformity in the sense of Ruzzo [Ruz81], which is the default for 𝖭𝖢\mathsf{NC}, decides the direct connection language in time O⁡(log⁡M)O(\log M) on a random-access machine and hence in space O⁡(log⁡M)O(\log M). Logspace-uniformity gives u⁡(M)=O⁡(log⁡M)u(M)=O(\log M) as well.11

            Logspace-uniformity is usually defined by a transducer that maps 1M1^{M} to a description of CMC_{M} in space O⁡(log⁡M)O(\log M). Such a transducer is simulated without 1M1^{M} physically present: store its input-head position, an O⁡(log⁡M)O(\log M)-bit counter, and answer every read with 11 except at the two ends. Definition 2.4 (uniform 𝖭𝖢i\mathsf{NC}^{i}).



For i≥1i\geq 1, a language LL is in uniform 𝖭𝖢i\mathsf{NC}^{i} if it is decided by a uniform family as in Definition 2.3 with S⁡(M)=MO⁡(1)S(M)=M^{O(1)}, D⁡(M)=O⁡(logi⁡M)D(M)=O(\log^{i}M) and u⁡(M)=O⁡(log⁡M)u(M)=O(\log M).






2.3 The simulation



Theorem 2.5 (succinct simulation).



Let LL be decided by a uniform family {CM}\{C_{M}\} of Boolean circuits of fan-in at most 22, of size S⁡(M)S(M), depth D⁡(M)D(M) and uniformity space u⁡(M)u(M). Suppose x∈{0,1}Mx\in\{0,1\}^{M} is given by a succinct presentation with parameters (b,σ)(b,\sigma). Then whether x∈Lx\in L is decidable in deterministic workspace




O⁡(D⁡(M)+log⁡S⁡(M)+b+u⁡(M))+σ,O\bigl(D(M)\;+\;\log S(M)\;+\;b\;+\;u(M)\bigr)\;+\;\sigma,



and in time 2O⁡(D⁡(M))⋅poly⁡(D⁡(M)+log⁡S⁡(M)+b)⋅Tor2^{O(D(M))}\cdot\mathrm{poly}\bigl(D(M)+\log S(M)+b\bigr)\cdot T_{\mathrm{or}}, where TorT_{\mathrm{or}} bounds the running time of the bit oracle and of the uniformity procedure.




Proof.



The algorithm is a depth-first evaluation of CMC_{M} which stores no gate values except along the current root-path, and which stores no gate names at all.




Data. We maintain a path register π∈{1,2}≤D⁡(M)\pi\in\{1,2\}^{\leq D(M)}, which is the sequence of child-choices taken from the output gate down to the gate currently under evaluation, and alongside each symbol of π\pi one bit st\mathrm{st} recording the value of the first child at that level once that value has been computed. We also maintain a reusable scratch region of O⁡(λ⁡(M)+b+u⁡(M))=O⁡(log⁡S⁡(M)+b+u⁡(M))O(\lambda(M)+b+u(M))=O(\log S(M)+b+u(M)) bits holding one gate name, one input index, and the workspace of the uniformity procedure, together with the reusable oracle region of σ\sigma bits. The path register and its attached bits occupy 2​D​(M)2D(M) bits. No further storage is used, and in particular no recursion stack of gate names is kept.




Recovering the current gate. Define a subroutine NAME⁡(π)\mathrm{NAME}(\pi) as follows. Obtain the name of the output gate from Definition 2.3(i), and then for t=1,…,|π|t=1,\dots,|\pi| replace the name currently held by the name of its πt\pi_{t}-th child, using Definition 2.3(iii). This is a loop and not a recursion: only one gate name is ever held, and the workspace of the uniformity procedure is reused at every step. Its cost is O⁡(λ⁡(M)+u⁡(M))O(\lambda(M)+u(M)) workspace and |π|≤D⁡(M)|\pi|\leq D(M) calls. This is the step at which uniformity is used, and it is what allows the path to be stored as O⁡(1)O(1) bits per level rather than as a list of gate names.




Main loop. Start with π\pi empty, in the descending phase.




Descend. Compute g:=NAME⁡(π)g:=\mathrm{NAME}(\pi) and its type.


• 



If gg is a Boolean constant, set val\mathrm{val} to that constant and ascend.


• 



If gg is the input gate number jj, call the bit oracle on jj, set val:=xj\mathrm{val}:=x_{j}, and ascend. The index jj has at most bb bits and is held in the scratch region.


• 



Otherwise gg is a ¬\neg, ∧\wedge or ∨\vee gate. Append the symbol 11 to π\pi, with its attached bit unset, and descend again.






Ascend with the value val\mathrm{val} of the gate at the end of π\pi.


• 



If π\pi is empty, output val\mathrm{val} and halt.


• 



Otherwise let cc be the last symbol of π\pi and st\mathrm{st} its attached bit. Delete that symbol and recompute g:=NAME⁡(π)g:=\mathrm{NAME}(\pi) and its type, so that gg is the parent. If gg is ¬\neg, replace val\mathrm{val} by ¬val\neg\,\mathrm{val} and ascend. If gg is ∧\wedge or ∨\vee and c=1c=1, append the symbol 22 to π\pi with attached bit val\mathrm{val}, and descend. If gg is ∧\wedge or ∨\vee and c=2c=2, replace val\mathrm{val} by st∧val\mathrm{st}\wedge\mathrm{val} or st∨val\mathrm{st}\vee\mathrm{val} as appropriate, and ascend.






Correctness. By induction on D⁡(M)−|π|D(M)-|\pi|, the procedure ascends out of a path π\pi carrying in val\mathrm{val} exactly the value that the gate at the end of π\pi takes when CMC_{M} is evaluated on xx. The base cases are constants and input gates, and the inductive step is the definition of ¬\neg, ∧\wedge and ∨\vee. This is the only place where determinism is used, and it is used silently: a gate with several root-paths leading to it is evaluated once for each of them, and the induction is valid because all of these evaluations return the same value. The output is CM​(x)C_{M}(x), which is 11 if and only if x∈Lx\in L.




Resources. The workspace is 2​D​(M)2D(M) for the path register, O⁡(log⁡S⁡(M)+b+u⁡(M))O(\log S(M)+b+u(M)) for the scratch region, and σ\sigma for the oracle region. The last two are reused rather than accumulated, which gives the stated bound. For the running time, note that every configuration of the algorithm is determined by π\pi together with the attached bits and a phase, so the number of steps is at most 2O⁡(D⁡(M))2^{O(D(M))}, and each step costs one NAME\mathrm{NAME} computation, that is O⁡(D⁡(M))O(D(M)) calls to the uniformity procedure, plus at most one oracle call.
∎




Corollary 2.6 (succinct 𝖭𝖢i\mathsf{NC}^{i} is in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤\mathsf{PSPACE}).



Let i≥1i\geq 1 and let LL be in uniform 𝖭𝖢i\mathsf{NC}^{i}. Suppose x∈{0,1}Mx\in\{0,1\}^{M} is given by a succinct presentation with parameters (b,σ)(b,\sigma) where b=O⁡(s)b=O(s), so that M≤2O⁡(s)M\leq 2^{O(s)}. Then whether x∈Lx\in L is decidable in deterministic workspace




O⁡(si)+σO(s^{i})\;+\;\sigma

Remark 2.8 (relation to the succinct-input literature).



That succinct presentation raises complexity by one exponential is a standard theme. It goes back to Galperin and Wigderson [GW83] and to Papadimitriou and Yannakakis [PY86], and was systematised as a family of upgrade theorems by Balcázar, Lozano and Torán [BLT92]: a problem complete for a class 𝒞\mathcal{C} under suitably restricted reductions has a succinct version complete for the exponentially larger class. Corollary 2.6 is the space-bounded instance of that theme, and we do not claim it as new. We have stated it with an explicit bit-oracle interface rather than for a fixed complete problem, because that is the form in which Section 6 needs it. Our succinct object is a linear pencil presented by an entry oracle, and not a Boolean circuit.







3 Rational circuits and the free skew field




In this section we recall the basic definitions and fix the notation.




3.1 Inner rank and fullness




Throughout, ℚ⁡⟨X⟩=ℚ⁡⟨x1,…,xn⟩\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle=\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle is the free associative ℚ\mathbb{Q}-algebra and ℚ⁡(⟨X⟩)\mathbb{Q}(\!\langle X\rangle\!) is the free skew field, the universal field of fractions of ℚ​⟨X⟩\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle [Coh95, Coh06]. The canonical map ℚ​⟨X⟩↪ℚ⁡(⟨X⟩)\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\!\langle X\rangle\!) is injective.



Definition 3.1 (inner rank; noncommutative rank).



Let AA be a matrix over ℚ​⟨X⟩\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle, not necessarily square. The inner rank of AA is the least ρ\rho for which there is a factorization A=B​CA=BC with BB having ρ\rho columns and CC having ρ\rho rows, both over ℚ​⟨X⟩\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle. We write nc​-​rank⁡(A)\operatorname{nc\text{-}rank}(A) for the inner rank and also call it the noncommutative rank of AA. A square matrix A∈ℚ​⟨X⟩N×NA\in\mathbb{Q}\langle X\rangle^{N\times N} is full if nc​-​rank⁡(A)=N\operatorname{nc\text{-}rank}(A)=N.




Proposition 3.2 (Cohn).

4 From circuits to succinct pencils




As stated in the abstract and introduction, Hrubes-Wigderson [HW15] present an algorithm that takes as input a rational formula 𝔯\mathfrak{r} of size ss, in noncommuting variables x1,x2,…,xnx_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n} (over a field FF
that is either ℚ\mathbb{Q} or a finite field), and in polynomial-time computes a linear pencil LL of dimension O⁡(s)O(s) such that 𝔯\mathfrak{r} is correct and

Based on [HW15], in this section we present an algorithm that takes as input a rational circuit CC of size ss and computes a succinct representation for a linear pencil of dimension 2O⁡(s)2^{O(s)}. Proposition 6.1.



Let ZERO⁡(⋅)\mathrm{ZERO}(\cdot) be any procedure which, on every correct rational circuit Ψ\Psi of size at most ss, decides whether Ψ^=0\widehat{\Psi}=0 in workspace Z⁡(s)Z(s).
