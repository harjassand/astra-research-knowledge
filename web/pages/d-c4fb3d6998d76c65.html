## Exact admissible interface

Let M be a unital C*-algebra or von Neumann algebra acting on H, or a specified
domain closed under the polar clipping used below. Let ξ be a unit vector and
**φ(x)=<xξ,ξ>**. This identity is necessary, rather than an unrelated positive
state with the same notation. Supply operators Z_i, probabilities p_i,
`||T||≤L`, γ>0, C>0, and certificates

`Σ_i p_i ||TZ_iξ||²≥γ`,

`φ(A²)+Σ_i p_i φ(Z_i* B Z_i)≤C`,

where `A=Σ_i p_i Z_i*Z_i`, `B=Σ_i p_i Z_iZ_i*`.
Supply a bounded linear map F on M with algebra norm at most C1, and a Hilbert
contraction Q satisfying `F(x)ξ=Qxξ` and `QZ_iξ=Z_iξ` for every i. No tracial
state, commutativity or modular averaging budget is assumed. A spectral-range
conclusion requires its own supplied range certificate for Q.

The C*-closure qualification repairs a literal ambiguity in “unital operator
algebra”: a bare polynomial *-algebra need not contain a clipped element.
Continuous functional calculus suffices: `z^[K]=z f(|z|)` with
`f(r)=min(r,K)/r` for r>0 and f(0)=1. No polar partial isometry needs to be
an element of M separately.

## Exact moment identities

Choose an odd prime p≥max(3,m), distinct t_i in F_p, and average uniformly over
`(a,b)∈F_p²`, with

`ε_i(a,b)=exp(2πi(a t_i+b t_i²)/p)`.

Finite character sums give `E conjugate(ε_i)ε_j=δ_ij`. They also say a balanced
fourth product `conjugate(ε_i)ε_j conjugate(ε_k)ε_l` has mean one precisely
when

`t_i+t_k=t_j+t_l`, `t_i²+t_k²=t_j²+t_l²`.

Because 2 is invertible, the equations imply `t_i t_k=t_j t_l`. Thus both
unordered pairs are the root multiset of the same monic quadratic over F_p,
so `{i,k}={j,l}` as multisets. All other means are zero. This includes
repeated labels in a pair. Individual first moments are unnecessary; t_i=0
may have constant phase.

For `z=Σ_i sqrt(p_i)ε_i Z_i`, expand `(z*z)²` without rearranging operators.
The pairing i=j,k=l contributes A². The pairing i=l,k=j contributes
`Σ_i p_i Z_i* B Z_i`. Their overlap is i=j=k=l and contributes
`Σ_i p_i²(Z_i*Z_i)²`, so it must be subtracted once. Hence the exact identity

`E(z*z)²=A²+Σ_i p_i Z_i*BZ_i−Σ_i p_i²(Z_i*Z_i)²`.

The subtracted operator is positive. Applying φ gives `Eφ(|z|⁴)≤C`, and the
second moment gives `E||Tzξ||²≥γ`. Clipping is nonlinear, but it is applied
after these two estimates; no moment identity for the clipped phases is needed.

## Clipping and numerical acceptance

The scalar inequality `(r−K)_+²≤r⁴/(16K²)` holds because the maximum of
`(r−K)/r²` for r>K is 1/(4K), attained at r=2K. Functional calculus and the
vector-state identity give

`E||(z−z^[K])ξ||²≤C/(16K²)`.

Let v=F(z^[K]). Since Q fixes zξ,
`vξ−zξ=Q(z^[K]ξ−zξ)`. Thus the same mean squared error bound holds for v.
Minkowski in L² of the finite phase space gives

`(E||Tvξ||²)^(1/2)≥sqrtγ−L sqrtC/(4K)`.

For `K≥L sqrt(C/γ)`, the right side is at least `3sqrtγ/4`; some assignment
has squared response at least `9γ/16`. Also `||v||≤C1K`, so every squared
score is at most `L²C1²K²`. A uniform assignment therefore meets score γ/4
with probability at least `5γ/(16 L²C1²K²)`, by the elementary bounded-score
mean estimate. The stated specialization at the exact threshold for K follows.

If the supplied score evaluator has absolute error ≤γ/16, accept only a
**certified lower score** ≥γ/4. An equivalent simple rule is estimated score
≥5γ/16. A plain estimated-score threshold γ/4 does not imply the claimed
actual response. Some estimate is at least `9γ/16−γ/16=γ/2`, so the corrected
scan certainly terminates. The norm/filter/clip/score and any localization
precision services are charged; this proof supplies no uniform algorithm for
acquiring them from modular dynamics.

## Exact support lower bound and costs

For any positive-weight finite family of unit phases satisfying the required
balanced fourth moments, the functions `ε_i ε_k`, `i≤k`, are mutually
orthonormal in L² of the family. A family with N support points has an N
dimensional complex L² space, so **N≥m(m+1)/2**. This statement is about
universal exact moment matching, not input-specific or approximate search.

The prime can be found by trial division. Bertrand gives p≤2m for m≥2
(m=1 uses p=3). Hence p²≤4m² assignments, at most O(m³) modular arithmetic
operations for their direct construction, and p² evaluations of the supplied
operator services. Trial-division prime acquisition costs
O(m^(3/2) poly(log m)) bit operations under this simple implementation.
The root of unity has a short exact cyclotomic specification; numerical
evaluation and score accuracy still carry their ordinary bit costs. This
achieves the optimal support **order** and improves the initial four-wise
O(m⁴) family within the acquired finite-certificate interface.
