# PDE bridge: infinite rank for sign-changing piecewise analytic conductivities

Status (2026-10-06): independently reconstructed proof candidate. External inputs are named below; finite-codimension upgrade, rank conclusions, and Gaussian consequence are our deductions. check_vector_measurements, team_a_inverse, and r07_analysis independently checked the finite-codimension construction and monotonicity directions. team_a_inverse also verified the original outer-hull definition and partial-access extension (audit: notes/team_a_inverse/vector_rank_audit.md). Novelty remains unresolved. No claim of novelty.

## Exact model and question

Let Ω⊂R^n be bounded, connected, with smooth boundary, n≥2. Let Σ⊂∂Ω be nonempty relatively open. H=L²_◊(Σ), the real Hilbert space of mean-zero currents. For scalar σ∈L∞(Ω), ess inf σ>0, solve div(σ∇u)=0 with flux g on Σ and zero elsewhere, normalized by ∫Σu=0. Define Λ(σ)g=u|Σ∈H. This map is compact self-adjoint on H.

Fix a finite-dimensional vector space V⊂L∞(Ω) whose every element is piecewise analytic in the precise Harrach2018 Definition 2.1 sense. A safe sufficient example is the span of piecewise polynomials on one fixed finite partition into piecewise smooth subdomains. Choose independent φ₁,…,φ_d∈V and σ₀∈V. Let U={a∈R^d: ess inf σ_a>0}, σ_a=σ₀+Σa_jφ_j. Parameterization is injective; U is open. F(a)=Λ(σ_a). The inverse problem is nonlinear in a despite linear parameterization of conductivity.

Question: does rank(F(a)−F(b))=∞ for a≠b, and rank(DF(a)[v])=∞ for v≠0? If yes, the generic vector-input principle makes a single full-support Gaussian current simultaneously distinguish every parameter in U, with inverse Lipschitz stability on each compact K⊂U, using the entire continuum output F(a)g.

## Verified external mathematical inputs

B. Harrach, arXiv:1808.04737v3 (3 Jan 2019), full PDF recovered at sources/rank_pde/harrach2018.pdf and HTML recovered at harrach2018v3.html. Lemma 2.5 gives differentiability and quadratic identity:

⟨Λ′(σ)[κ]g,g⟩=−∫Ωκ|∇u_σ^g|².

Lemma 2.6, also with σ₁/σ₂ interchanged, gives:

∫Ω(σ₂−σ₁)|∇u_σ₂^g|² ≤ ⟨(Λ(σ₁)−Λ(σ₂))g,g⟩ ≤ ∫Ω(σ₂−σ₁)|∇u_σ₁^g|². (M)

Lemma 2.7: for piecewise analytic σ and measurable target A,bad set D, if int A is not contained in out_Σ D, currents exist with target gradient energy tending to infinity and bad gradient energy tending to zero. Here out_Σ D is the complement of all relatively open connected paths to Σ that avoid D. (The displayed hull formula omits the avoidance clause in BOTH recovered PDF and HTML; its literal reading would be independent of D and false. We use the intended definition, made explicit in Harrach–Ullrich2013 Definition3(d) via χ_D vanishing on accessible open sets, and Section4.3 for partial Σ.)

Lemma 2.8: every nonzero piecewise analytic κ has a visible sign patch. There are D₁ open and D₂=out_ΣD₂, D₁ not contained in D₂, such that either κ≥0 on Ω\D₂ and κ≥c>0 on D₁, or the same inequalities hold for −κ. D₂ is relatively closed; the accessible component of Ω\D₂ meets Σ. No global ordering κ≥0 is assumed.

These external results derive from localized potentials and piecewise analytic unique continuation. They are not new results here. Harrach Theorems 2.3–2.4 already give Lipschitz recovery from the full ND map or a sufficiently large finite Galerkin compression; they do not assert recovery from one current.

## Our finite-codimension localization construction

Take the positive case of the visible sign patch. Fix any finite-dimensional E⊂H with dim E=N. We construct g∈E⊥ with ∫Ωκ|∇u_σ^g|²>0. For N=0 the following construction uses one ball.

Choose a small open ball B₀ with closure compactly contained in D₁\D₂ and in a component U₀ of Ω\D₂ connected to Σ. Choose m=N+1 mutually disjoint closed balls B₁,…,B_m strictly inside B₀. Removing any finite collection of these balls from U₀ preserves its connectivity to Σ in dimension n≥2: a path can be detoured around each ball in a thin annulus contained in B₀. Hence each B_j is accessible from Σ avoiding

D^(j)=D₂∪⋃_{i≠j}B_i.

Apply ordinary localized potentials to target B_j and bad set D^(j). Divide the chosen current by its target gradient norm. For any ε>0 this produces g_j with

‖∇u_σ^{g_j}‖L²(B_j)=1,
‖∇u_σ^{g_j}‖L²(D₂∪⋃_{i≠j}B_i)≤ε. (L)

The ratio tends to zero because the unnormalized target norm tends to infinity and bad norm tends to zero. Each chosen g_j is a legitimate L² current; no upper bound on its current norm is claimed.

Solve the N linear equations Σ_j a_j⟨g_j,e_l⟩=0 for a∈R^m, where e_l is a basis of E. Choose a nonzero solution and normalize ‖a‖₂=1. Set g=Σ_j a_jg_j∈E⊥. Let A=⋃_jB_j. On L²(A;R^n), the block diagonal part of a↦Σ_j a_j∇u_j has norm exactly ‖a‖₂: the j-th diagonal block is a_j∇u_j|B_j with norm |a_j| and disjoint support. By (L), the off-diagonal map has norm ≤√m ε (using a column norm bound ≤ε and then the Hilbert–Schmidt bound). Consequently

‖∇u_σ^g‖L²(A)≥1−√m ε,
‖∇u_σ^g‖L²(D₂)≤√m ε.

Take ε<1/(2√m). Since A⊂D₁ and κ is nonnegative outside D₂,

∫Ωκ|∇u_σ^g|²≥c(1−√mε)²−‖κ‖∞mε²>0

for sufficiently small ε. In the negative case apply this construction to −κ, producing a strictly negative integral. This works for every finite-codimension E⊥ and every piecewise analytic positive background σ.

Stronger conclusion: the quadratic form of Λ′(σ)[κ] has the same visible strict sign on some vector in every finite-codimension subspace. Compact self-adjoint spectral theory therefore implies infinitely many eigenvalues of that sign. The construction only needs strict sign, not an unbounded normalized current response.

## Infinite rank of derivative

If T=Λ′(σ)[κ] had finite rank, E=ran T is finite dimensional and E⊥=ker T because T is self-adjoint. The construction gives g∈ker T with −⟨Tg,g⟩ strictly positive or negative, a contradiction. Therefore every nonzero piecewise analytic κ gives infinite rank. This is stronger than the known injectivity of κ↦Λ′(σ)[κ] (Harrach Remark2.12), which alone would not suffice for the Gaussian bridge.

## Infinite rank of nonlinear differences

Let σ₁≠σ₂, κ=σ₂−σ₁, with both σ_i and κ piecewise analytic. Suppose T=Λ(σ₁)−Λ(σ₂) has finite rank. Use E=ran T, so g∈E⊥ implies ⟨Tg,g⟩=0.

If κ has a visible positive patch, run the finite-codimension construction with background σ₂. The lower inequality in (M) gives ⟨Tg,g⟩≥∫κ|∇u_σ₂^g|²>0, contradiction.

If κ has a visible negative patch, use background σ₁. The upper inequality in (M) gives ⟨Tg,g⟩≤∫κ|∇u_σ₁^g|²<0, contradiction.

Thus T has infinite rank for every pair, including contrasts that change sign arbitrarily across the fixed partition. The appropriate one-sided inequality matters: positivity cannot be transferred through an upper bound.

## Analyticity and composed one-input conclusion

Let W={u∈H¹(Ω):∫Σu=0}. The weak operator A_a:W→W* defined by ∫σ_a∇u·∇w is coercive and affine in a. On a small neighborhood of each a₀∈U,

A_a⁻¹=[I+A_a₀⁻¹(A_a−A_a₀)]⁻¹A_a₀⁻¹

has a norm-convergent Neumann series. B:H→W* is the boundary current functional, C:W→H is the normalized trace. Then F(a)=CA_a⁻¹B is real analytic. DF(a)[v]=Λ′(σ_a)[Σv_jφ_j] has infinite rank by independent basis and the derivative theorem.

Apply the independently checked generic vector-input theorem: for any centered Gaussian g on H with trace-class injective covariance, with probability one a↦Λ(σ_a)g is injective on U and an immersion. On every compact K⊂U there is a finite sample-dependent C(g,K) with

‖a−b‖≤C(g,K)‖Λ(σ_a)g−Λ(σ_b)g‖H, a,b∈K.

This is an exact continuum theorem conditional only on the abstract generic-input theorem's independent audit and the named localized-potential/sign-patch facts. It is not a theorem for unrestricted L∞ conductivities, unknown discontinuity interfaces, or anisotropic tensors. It does not provide reconstruction algorithms or a uniform/noise-calibrated constant.

## Independent PDE cross-check and sign typo

Harrach, Pohjola, Salo, Analysis & PDE12(7),2019,1741–1771, doi:10.2140/apde.2019.12.1741; full PDF sources/rank_pde/helmholtz2019.pdf. For (Δ+k²q)u=0 with real bounded q and nonresonant k>0, Theorem4.1 gives finite-codimension localized potentials. Theorem5.1 asserts infinitely many positive eigenvalues of Λ(q₂)−Λ(q₁) when q₂≥q₁ on a boundary-accessible open set and nontrivially there; Theorem1.1 states the same. Combined with the visible-sign theorem for piecewise analytic contrasts (Corollary5.2), every nonzero difference has infinite rank. The last line of the proof of Theorem5.1 says 'negative', contradicting the theorem and its proof: the preceding contradiction assumes A≤fin0 and demonstrates positive quadratic values in every finite-codim complement. Treat this as a sign typo; infinite rank is unaffected. For derivatives, differentiating the variational problem gives ⟨Λ′(q)[κ]g,h⟩=k²∫κu_q^g u_q^h, so Theorem4.1 plus the sign patch proves infinite rank in the same manner.

The Helmholtz route avoids conductivity-specific gradient localization but requires excluding Neumann resonances throughout the parameter domain. A simple coercive admissible class is q≤−c<0, or any compact coefficient class uniformly avoiding resonances. It is stationary Schrödinger/modified Helmholtz rather than radiating far-field scattering. No far-field statement is claimed.

## Prior art, cost, obstruction, next step

Nearest prior art: Harrach2018/2019 handles arbitrary finite-dimensional piecewise analytic conductivity spaces and finite Galerkin/full electrode-map recovery. Alberti–Santacesaria2019 handles finitely many prescribed full boundary responses under other finite-dimensional assumptions. The 2026 analytic-random-measurements paper motivates reducing scalar measurements to >2d; this bridge instead exploits infinitely ranked response operators to use one input with a full response. Targeted primary-literature searches have not established whether the exact one-Gaussian-current theorem is already known. Novelty is unresolved; single-measurement shape/convex inclusion and fractional/nonlocal uniqueness results have different interfaces.

One excitation is not one scalar measurement. g is an infinite-dimensional random field and its complete voltage function is acquired. An arbitrary finite electrode array or Fourier truncation cannot inherit the infinite-rank hypotheses without a new visibility check; recovering d parameters still needs enough retained output coordinates, input complexity, spatial resolution, precision, and conditioning. Separately, r07_analysis reconstructs a nonconstructive compact finite-minor-cover argument that can select finite current and voltage-testing spaces preserving a sufficient finite rank threshold. This gives an existential finite realization, with no explicit counts or conditioning constants. Full-support Gaussians can be smooth via rapidly decaying covariance, but this may give tiny informative amplitudes. C(g,K) can be arbitrarily bad across draws, families, or resolutions. No noisy/reliable finite-cost gain is proved.

Decisive mathematical obstruction beyond this class: visible-sign patch and accessible localization can fail for contrasts with infinite sign oscillations; anisotropic boundary-fixing diffeomorphism gauge gives rank-zero distinct parameters unless quotienting; unknown interfaces need different derivative analysis.

Concrete downstream use: a rigorous nonadaptive single-excitation design principle for known-partition scalar EIT finite parameter spaces. It suggests testing how many voltage coordinates and which Gaussian covariance retain good conditioning, while preserving classical multi-current controls.

Next falsifiable steps: independent audit of the N+1-ball argument and monotonicity directions; exhaustive prior-art search for generic one-input finite-dimensional EIT; a fixed-partition numerical experiment comparing one rich random current against equally costly deterministic/multiple-current measurements under matched scalar-observation and noise budgets. Such an experiment cannot prove the universal theorem.
