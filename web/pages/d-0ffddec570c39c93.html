# Energy-aware feedback control: deterministic sensing degeneracy

## Verdict

The strongest result from this pass is a model-audit theorem, not a new sensing scheduler. In a deterministic plant with known parameters and known initial state, a sensor that only reports the state gives no new information: a controller can propagate the state internally and replay every action with the sensor disabled. If sensing costs (s) units per unit time and actuation costs (r u^2), replay preserves the state and actuation costs exactly and saves (s\int z(t)\,dt). Thus, positive sensing intervals cannot be optimal in that information model when the actuator is independently available.

This directly limits a recent scalar sensing-cost derivation. Its 2026 model assumes deterministic known-state LTI dynamics, but its switching variable also disables control when sensing is off. The resulting policy is therefore a joint sensor/actuator gate. For a physical system with independent sensing and actuation, a stronger equally informed comparator can do better. This work does not establish novelty or external validation; it records an internally reconstructed counterexample and a route to a nondegenerate stochastic problem.

## Exact interface and theorem

Take a known deterministic plant

\[
\dot x(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\qquad x(0)=x_0,
\]

with known (A,B,x_0), no process disturbance, and a sensor that, when enabled by (z(t)\in\{0,1\}), returns (y(t)=Cx(t)) without affecting the plant. An admissible sensing/controller policy may choose future (z,u) causally from prior sensor outputs. Let its cost be

\[
J(z,u)=\int_0^\infty \bigl(x^TQx+u^TRu+s z\bigr)\,dt,
\quad Q\succeq0,\ R\succ0,\ s>0.
\]

**Replay theorem (internally reconstructed).** For every finite-cost policy π=(z,u), an equally informed policy π̃ exists with sensor always disabled, the same physical state and actuator-input paths, and

\[
J(\tilde\pi)=J(\pi)-s\int_0^\infty z_\pi(t)\,dt.
\]

Consequently, the infimum over policies with an independently available actuator is attained or approached with zero sensing; every policy that spends positive sensing time is strictly dominated by its replay.

**Proof.** Initialize a controller-side copy \hat x(0)=x_0. Given its previous action, integrate \hat ẋ=A\hat x+Bu. Whenever the original policy would observe (Cx), feed the virtual observation (C\hat x) to its simulated policy logic. Uniqueness of solutions to the linear ODE gives \hat x(t)=x(t) on each interval; the virtual and physical policies therefore produce identical sensor decisions and actuator actions by induction. The simulated controller can implement the resulting action schedule without turning on the physical sensor. State and actuation integrals are identical; only the nonnegative sensor term is removed. □

The equality has a sharp interface boundary. If the hardware imposes (z=0\Rightarrow u=0), then (z) is a combined sensor/actuator enable command and the replay may be inadmissible. The conclusion in that case is not that the restricted switching calculation is false; it is that its cost and policy concern a coupled device, not sensing cost in a plant with independently addressable actuation.

## Quantitative fixture against the 2026 fixed-gain switching law

Tran, Ngo, and Das, *Cost of Sensing in Optimal Control* (arXiv:2607.08119v1, 9 July 2026), Section 4.1 / Proposition 2 studies

\[
\dot x=ax-bkzx,\qquad
J=\int_0^\infty[(q_1+q_2z)x^2+sz]dt,
\]

with (x_0>0), (a<0), (a-bk<0), fixed (k>0), and (z=1) during sensing/control and (z=0\) with (u=0). The paper identifies (q_2=rk^2) as the actuator-effort term. For (q_1=1,a=-1,b=1,k=1/2,r=1,s=0.1,x_0=1), its Proposition 2 conditions hold and its one-switch schedule has

\[
g=q_1bk+q_2a=0.25,\qquad
\rho=\frac{-as}{g x_0^2}=0.4,\qquad
t_{sw}=\frac{\ln(0.4)}{2(a-bk)}=0.30543024.
\]

Since (e^{-3t_{sw}}=0.4), the policy's state-plus-actuator cost while on is

\[
(q_1+q_2)\int_0^{t_{sw}}e^{-3t}dt=1.25\frac{1-0.4}{3}=0.25,
\]

and its later open-loop state cost is (0.4/2=0.2). Its sensing cost is (s t_{sw}=0.0305430), so

\[
J_{\rm fixed\ gain}=0.4805430.
\]

With independent actuation, a no-sensor internal-model controller can reproduce that exact (u(t)) path and obtain (J=0.45), saving exactly (0.0305430) while preserving actuator energy. Optimizing the controller as well gives the continuous-time LQR solution for the scalar plant `xdot = -x + u` with cost `integral (x^2 + u^2) dt`: (P=\sqrt2-1), (u=-(\sqrt2-1)x), (x(t)=e^{-\sqrt2t}), and

\[
J_{\rm LQR}=P x_0^2=\sqrt2-1\approx0.41421356,
\]

with zero sensing energy. This is (0.0663294) lower than the fixed-gain switching policy. The first comparison is same actuator trajectory; the second is the strongest full-information comparator and also changes the fixed gain, as a global controller comparison should.

The exact energy accounting is explicit: the source schedule pays (s t_{sw}) for sensing and (\int u^2dt=0.25) for actuation; its sensor-free replay keeps (\int u^2dt=0.25) unchanged. The LQR comparator pays (\int u^2dt=(3\sqrt2/4)-1\approx0.0606602), state cost (\sqrt2/4\approx0.3535534), and zero sensing cost.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

The broad claim in the 2026 preprint that direct sensing-cost integration “has not been reported in the literature” is contradicted by primary sources located in this bounded search:

- Molin and Hirche, “Optimal Event-triggered Control under Costly Observations,” MTNS 2010, model a stochastic Itô plant. Their objective explicitly charges (\int(x^TQx+u^TRu)dt+\lambda k_T); the optimal controller is LQG on the conditional state estimate and the observation scheduler is a threshold policy on estimation error (primary manuscript, pp. 1–4, especially equations (3), (11)–(12)). Brownian process noise makes observations informative, unlike the deterministic model above. [Primary PDF](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1081989/972955.pdf)
- Tzoumas, Carlone, Pappas, and Jadbabaie, “LQG Control and Sensing Co-Design,” IEEE TAC 2020 (arXiv:1802.08376v7), charge sensor activation costs such as power and optimize a finite-horizon LQG objective containing both state and actuator-input quadratic costs. Their model includes process and measurement noise and a finite set of sensors (primary manuscript, pp. 1–3). [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08376)
- The 2026 paper itself says at pp. 3–4 that adding the control-input cost complicates convergence of its two-point boundary-value solver and remains future work. Its one-switch formula instead fixes (k) and optimizes (z) within that restricted interface (Proposition 2, pp. 6–7). [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08119)

The exact fixed-gain deterministic switching family may still be a narrow formulation. The sources above do not certify its novelty or settle every deterministic hybrid sensor/actuator model. The defensible finding is that the broad literature-gap wording is unsupported by these primary counterexamples, and the source model's information assumptions make pure sensing energy degenerate unless actuation is physically gated together with sensing.

## Measurable assumptions, falsifier, and next gate

The replay theorem requires: (i) calibrated deterministic (A,B); (ii) initial state known to the controller; (iii) no unmeasured process input, noise, or parameter drift; (iv) sensor output is a passive function of state; (v) computation and internal prediction consume no cost; (vi) the actuator can be commanded while the physical sensor is off. The paper's continuous sensing charge (s\int zdt) is an energy only after (s) is calibrated in joules per second; (u^TRu) is actuator energy only after (R), (u), and time units are calibrated.

For a bench falsification, log the sensor-off predictor residual (r_t=x_{meas}(t)-\hat x(t)) after replaying the recorded control sequence. Under the theorem's assumptions it is identically zero up to numerical and sensor measurement tolerance, and the measured objective difference must equal (s\int zdt) while ∫(u^TRu) remains equal. A reproducible residual beyond calibrated tolerance, caused by process noise, unknown initial state, drift, or disturbances, falsifies the assumptions and is precisely where sensing can have value. A physical interlock enforcing (z=0\Rightarrow u=0) also falsifies assumption (vi) and changes the object to sensor/actuator co-scheduling.

The next meaningful line is a disturbance-driven stochastic model with separate sensing and actuator controls. The strongest direct comparator is event-triggered LQG with the same process-noise law, observation charge, and actuator energy, rather than a free-sensing LQR or an estimator-only baseline. A genuine advance would need to improve an explicit remaining bottleneck there, such as nonconstant measurement-energy rates, a hard battery budget, or a provable robustness/service guarantee; none is established in this pass.
