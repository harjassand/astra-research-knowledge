# Subcritical weighted Riesz integral systems: complete analytic proof candidate

Research record: 9 October 2026.

## What is established in the supplied argument

The manuscript gives a complete analytic nonexistence argument for the full strict subcritical parameter region of the equal-order weighted Riesz integral system. The stated solution class is positive, continuous, globally finite, and satisfies the integral equations pointwise everywhere.

For every n >= 1, every real Riesz order 0 < s < n, every p,q > 0 and real weights A,B, consider

u(x) = c_s integral |x-y|^(s-n) |y|^A v(y)^p dy,
v(x) = c_s integral |x-y|^(s-n) |y|^B u(y)^q dy,

with c_s > 0. There are no such solutions if

(n+A)/(p+1) + (n+B)/(q+1) > n-s.

No radial symmetry, stability, global boundedness, decay, finite energy, or other global integrability condition is assumed. Local boundedness follows from the explicitly assumed continuity. This is not a theorem excluding singular measurable almost-everywhere solutions: those can exist on the subcritical side and are addressed in the scope assessment.

## Fractional PDE consequence

A separate representation proof also closes the standard full-space fractional PDE transfer for n >= 2 and 0 < s < 2. It assumes positive continuous functions in the natural weighted tail class, A,B > -n, and equations globally in distributions, including through the origin. It does not assume radiality, global boundedness, or finite energy. Green comparison, harmonic-remainder classification, and positivity yield the exact pointwise integral representation, so the same full subcritical nonexistence conclusion follows. See FRACTIONAL_PDE_COROLLARY.md and the complete representation proof and audit. This is a scoped PDE theorem, not a claim about every possible PDE formulation.

## Why this is consequential

The unweighted version is the regular-solution Liouville problem historically called the HLS integral-system conjecture. The full weighted and arbitrary-order statement reaches beyond the radial, decaying/integrable, and restricted-parameter results found in the primary-literature comparison.

For example n=8, s=1, A=B=0, p=3/2, q=11/10 lies in the asserted nonexistence region, since the hyperbola sum is 736/105 > 7. It is outside both the cited scaling-exponent region and the cited scaling-spheres upper rectangle.
