## Exact interface and consequence

Input is the expanded rational coefficients of

    f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2),

with unknown rational invertible L and rational symmetric positive-definite Q. Promise that an unknown permutation makes Q block diagonal, with every block of size at most3. Q, L, blocks, hidden axes, an aligned metric, condition bounds and witness heights are not supplied. Zero couplings, negative triangles, repeated blocks and nontypical parameter equalities are permitted.

Use the normalized symmetric four-linear tensor T with f(x)=T(x,x,x,x). Set

    H=Hess f=12T(x,x,.,.), h=det H,
    F=adj(H) grad h, V=F/h²=H^−1 grad log h,
    L_V=12H^−1 T(x,V,.,.), N_τ=Jac(V)+τ L_V.

Define the polynomial matrix and determinant

    P_τ=h³N_τ=h Jac(F)−2F(grad h)^T
                    +12τ adj(H) T(x,F,.,.),
    K_τ=det[x,P_τx,...,P_τ^(n−1)x].                      (1)

Every entry of P_τ has degree6n−4. The homogeneous degree of K_τ is

    D=n+(6n−4)n(n−1)/2.                                  (2)

For all but at most2n scalar τ, K_τ is nonzero. Each hidden coordinate hyperplane divides every K_τ. This permits the already gated rational-line, multiplicity-aware normal/reflection/metric/terminal-verification composition to acquire the hidden frame with expected polynomial bit cost throughout this promised family. The learner does not find blocks first. A degree-O(n³) implicit detector and its line circuit give a complexity theorem; full multivariate expansion would be inappropriate and is not charged as polynomial.

## 1. Universal invertibility of tensor multiplication

This primitive holds for every positive homogeneous real quartic, without the block or hidden-even promise. Positivity here means f(x)>0 for x≠0.

First H has a positive-definite point. Minimize f on the Euclidean unit sphere, a compact set. At a minimizer u, Euler and Lagrange multipliers give grad f(u)=4f(u)u. The constrained second variation yields H(u)≥4f(u)I on the tangent space. Euler gives H(u)u=3grad f(u)=12f(u)u. Hence radial/tangent cross terms vanish, the radial eigenvalue is12f(u)>0, and H(u)>0. Rescaling u puts this point on f=1 while preserving Hessian positivity.

The level surface M={f=1} is compact, and grad f is nonzero there by Euler. Choose any connected component Ω of {x∈M:H(x)>0}. Such components are open in the locally path-connected manifold M. Its closure in M is compact. A boundary point of Ω cannot remain H-positive: any sufficiently small H-positive connected neighborhood would join it to Ω. A limit of H-positive matrices is positive semidefinite, so every boundary Hessian is singular and h=0 there. Since h>0 on Ω, h attains a positive maximum at an interior point p. This does not require an isolated maximum or full coordinate support.

At p, grad h=λ grad f. Contracting with p uses degrees2n and4 to give λ=nh(p)/2. Since H(p)^−1 grad f(p)=p/3,

    V(p)=(n/6)p,
    L_V(p)=12H(p)^−1T(p,(n/6)p,.,.)=(n/6)I.             (3)

Thus det L_V is not the zero rational function. The minimizer, component and maximizer are existence devices only. No numerical optimization, Euclidean metric acquisition or assumption about the speed of approaching p is part of the algorithm.

The product u⋆v=12H^−1T(x,u,v,.) is commutative, GL-natural and has unit x. It need not be associative or Jordan. In particular L_Vx=V. Because V has degree−3, Euler also gives Jac(V)x=−3V. Hence

    N_τx=(τ−3)V.                                         (4)

The first new matrix vector is still proportional to the existing normalized field. The invertibility fact does not by itself prove cyclicity in dimension≥4.

## 2. The independently gated low-dimensional input

For a single block of size b≤3 put A_b=Jac(V_b). Euler and ordinary differentiation yield

    A_bx=−3V_b, A_b²x=−3A_bV_b=−3D_(V_b)V_b.

Consequently the normalized-field extactic and Jacobian Krylov determinant satisfy

    det[x,A_bx]=−3det[x,V_b]                              (b=2),
    det[x,A_bx,A_b²x]=9det[x,V_b,D_(V_b)V_b]               (b=3).

For b=1 the coordinate vector alone is cyclic. Multiplication of a derivation by h_b² changes its successive coordinate-derivative rows by a triangular transformation with diagonal1,h_b²,h_b^4,...; thus

    E_V=h_b^(−b(b−1))E_F.

The all-QPD binary result follows from the explicit classical Hessian/Jacobian formula. The all-QPD ternary result is already independently root-gated at `notes/root_cycle10/TERNARY_EXTACTIC_GATE.md`, and its full worker proof was read here. Its projective-pencil reduction includes the decoupled-row case; no nonzero-coupling premise is silently imported. Therefore A_b has a generically cyclic initial vector on every permitted block.

For clarity, the binary ingredient needs only a,c>0, Δ=ac−b²>0. With p=a u^4+2b u²v²+c v^4,

    h_2=48[bp+3Δu²v²],
    Jac(p,h_2)=1152uvΔ(a u^4−c v^4)≠0,
    det[(u,v)^T,adj(H_2)grad h_2]=3Jac(p,h_2).

The ternary proof uses that the four roots of p are distinct with nonreal squared-coordinate ratios, so p is coprime to h_2 and every nonzero real diagonal quadratic. Zero three-coordinate extactic would produce a projective pencil of invariant lines; sign symmetry forces a coordinate center. Restricting to the opposite binary face contradicts the coprime Jacobian when the normal row is nonzero. If that row is identically zero, direct block splitting reduces the same pair identity to Jac(p,h_2)=0. The pencil implication is special to projective dimension2 and is not reused on larger blocks.

## 3. Polynomial parameter exclusion, not output counting

For a block of size b, det(A_b+τL_(V_b)) is a polynomial in τ over Q(x), of degree≤b with nonzero leading coefficient det L_(V_b). Thus at most b scalar τ can make it identically zero as a function of x. Its initial-vector Krylov determinant is a polynomial of τ degree≤b(b−1)/2; the value at τ=0 is nonzero by Section2. Thus at most that many scalar τ can make cyclicity fail identically. This is a genuine polynomial-degree argument over a function field, not a reflection-count or finite-point inference.

The total number of parameters bad for at least one hidden block is at most

    Σ_blocks [b+b(b−1)/2]=Σ_blocks b(b+1)/2≤2n.           (5)

## 4. Hidden block composition and denominator clearing

When f is the sum of independent block quartics, H is block diagonal and h is the product of the block determinants. Therefore V, Jac(V) and L_V are exactly block diagonal/vector-split in their normalized versions. Using Jac(F) without the h² normalization would introduce derivatives of other blocks' h factors and would not justify this argument.

Fix a τ good for every block. In each block, h_b, det N_(b,τ) and its Krylov determinant are nonzero rational functions. Their product is nonzero, so their common open set contains rational real points. Select one such block point for the existence proof. These witnesses need not be found and their heights need not be bounded for the circuit algorithm.

At each point the block matrix is invertible and the initial vector is cyclic. Its minimal polynomial has degree equal to its block size. Rescaling the input point by a positive scalar s_b gives

    N_(b,τ)(s_bx_b)=s_b^−4 N_(b,τ)(x_b).

All its eigenvalues are nonzero. Each collision with a fixed eigenvalue in an earlier block excludes only finitely many positive scales. Rational scales avoiding all collisions exist. Thus the scaled block minimal polynomials are pairwise coprime, even if a block is not diagonalizable. The Chinese remainder theorem says that the direct-sum initial vector is cyclic: its annihilating polynomial is the least common multiple, now the product of total degree n. This proves a nonzero full normalized Krylov determinant.

At the chosen direct-sum point h≠0. Replacing N_τ by P_τ=h³N_τ multiplies column k by h^(3k), so the normalized determinant is multiplied by h^(3n(n−1)/2), a nonzero factor. Hence K_τ in (1) is a nonzero polynomial. Hidden blocks, separating scales and spectra are used only in this proof; the actual circuit is built from observed coefficients.

## 5. Covariance and divisibility

For g(z)=f(Az), with A any invertible real or complex matrix,

    H_g=A^TH_f(Az)A, h_g=det(A)²h_f(Az),
    F_g=det(A)^4A^−1F_f(Az), V_g=A^−1V_f(Az).

Consequently Jac(V) and L_V transform by similarity. Formula (1) gives

    P_(g,τ)(z)=det(A)^6A^−1P_(f,τ)(Az)A,
    K_(g,τ)(z)=det(A)^[3n(n−1)−1]K_(f,τ)(Az).

The exponent follows from6 times the sum0+...+(n−1), and det(A)^−1 from the column matrix. This independently verifies the determinant character.

In hidden coordinates, every coordinate sign matrix S preserves f. Similarity covariance gives P(Sy)=SP(y)S. For i≠j its ij entry is odd in both y_i,y_j; a diagonal entry is even. Thus P preserves y_i=0 as a polynomial identity, including singular Hessian points. Each Krylov column lies on that hyperplane when the initial vector does, and y_i divides K_τ.
