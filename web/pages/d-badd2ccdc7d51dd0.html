## Legal interface and exact scope

Nonzero A,B in K[x1,...,xn] are explicitly sparse. K is a supplied finite field of size Q with a supplied arithmetic representation. Numeric D=max(1,degA,degB), input term counts tA,tB, supplied integer T>=number of GCD terms, and error budget delta in(0,1). Output is the lex-leading-coefficient-normalized GCD, or FAILURE; outside good randomness it may also be wrong. No target support, reference monomial, derivative values, primitive element or discrete logarithm oracle is supplied.

T is load-bearing and is not inferred. D is numeric, not logarithmic. The stated Q-size admission is load-bearing; extension construction/conversion is not free and is outside this theorem. The prototype's GF(2^16), fixed seeds and24 rounds are diagnostic, not automatically admitted to the declared field/error budget. Fast dense multiplication and univariate GCD are explicitly charged primitives; schoolbook prototype timing proves no quasi-linear bound.

Set M=16T, W=M+1, R=max(8,ceil(32 ln(16(n+1)T^2/delta))), m=R(n+1), L=(m+1)T+1. Let

    C=m(2WD^2+4D)+D[L(L+1)/2+1].

Require Q−1>=2C/delta. The mathematical algorithm uses R independent base weights uniform in{1,...,M}^n and their n coordinate increments, and one common independent uniform b in(K*)^n. Ideal independence, not a fixed pseudorandom replay seed, is used by the probability proof.

## Gate A: generic actual GCD images, including monomial factors

The input coordinate valuations acquire v_i=min(val_xi A,val_xi B). This is precisely the coordinate valuation of gcd(A,B), since x_i is a prime. Divide both inputs by x^v. Their new GCD F has min_e e_i=0 for every coordinate, though it can have positive minimum weighted degree. Write reduced A=FU, B=FV with gcd(U,V)=1. U,V are analysis witnesses; no algorithm is allowed to obtain them for free.

For a positive integer weight w, tag with independent X variables. U(X y^w),V(X y^w) are coprime in the Laurent ring K[X_1^+/-1,...,X_n^+/-1,y^+/-1]: the substitution X_i->X_i y^wi is an invertible monomial map and localization of a UFD cannot create a common nonunit factor. Localizing X further to K(X) preserves this property. Removing their formal lowest y powers gives P,Q in K[X][y] with nonzero constant coefficient, coprime in K(X)[y]. No separability, squarefreeness, ordinary derivative or large-characteristic hypothesis is used.

If at least one y degree is positive, Res_y(P,Q) is nonzero. Its total X degree is bounded by deg_y(P)deg_X(Q)+deg_y(Q)deg_X(P)<=2WD^2. If both y degrees are zero, they are units in K(X); use resultant1 and exclude both coefficient zeros separately. Their leading and lowest coefficients are nonzero polynomials of total degree at mostD; excluding the four zeros costs at most4D. Repeated identical endpoint polynomials only make this bound conservative.

At a b avoiding those zeros, P_b,Q_b have the correct degrees/valuations and are coprime. The separately observed valuations of A(by^w),B(by^w) equal the sum of their F and cofactor valuations when all required minimum buckets are nonzero. Therefore stripping EACH input's observed valuation before univariate GCD leaves a scalar multiple of

    y^(-k_w) F(by^w),       k_w=min_e w dot e.

The scalar is arbitrary because the primitive returns a monic polynomial. At y=1 the monomial unit equals1, so division by the ACTUAL computed GCD's value at1 removes that scalar and gives

    H_w=y^(-k_w)F(by^w)/F(b).                         (1)

The algorithm never evaluates unknown F(b): it evaluates its observed univariate GCD at1. The common normalization makes (1)'s singleton coefficients identical across weights. The field-grid polynomial bound gives gate-A failure at most m(2WD^2+4D)/(Q−1), conditional on all weights. The remaining F minimum-bucket and F(b) conditions are guaranteed by gate B.

## Gate B: diversification without a supplied dictionary or 2^T union bound

Condition on all weights BEFORE b. Every encountered F bucket is a subset S of its unknown true support, with coefficient polynomial P_S(X)=sum_(e in S) a_e X^e. At most mT buckets occur. Add all at mostT singletons and F itself: at most L formal polynomials. Distinct subsets give distinct nonzero polynomials in every characteristic because the monomial supports differ and all a_e are nonzero. The difference of two different bucket polynomials also remains nonzero in characteristic2; signs changing to plus cannot remove a monomial appearing on only one support.

Every polynomial and difference has total degree at mostD. Exclude all zeros and all pairwise-difference zeros. The failure bound D[L(L+1)/2+1]/(Q−1) is conservative (F's nonzero condition is already in the L list). Thus all encountered buckets are nonzero and different formal buckets have different evaluations. This guarantees minimum and maximum buckets, excludes accidental singleton/cluster aliases, and preserves distinctions after common division by F(b). The finite list is analysis-only and unknown computationally; it depends on the weights but not on b. There is no hidden exhaustive subset oracle.

## Gate C: acquire the reference and handle its adaptive choice

For distinct integer exponent vectors e,f, fix all but a coordinate with e_i!=f_i. The equation s dot(e−f)=0 has at most one integer solution for the remaining s_i. Hence a pair collision probability is at most1/M. The same argument works for s+e_k: adding e_k contributes a fixed integer constant. These equalities concern integer degree labels, not field residues.

Each target term collides in a base round with probability at most(T−1)/M<=1/16. Independent rounds and Hoeffding give

    Pr[collisions>=R/2] <=exp(−49R/128)<=exp(−R/4).

Except with probability T exp(−R/4), every true term is singleton in STRICTLY more than half the base images. Under gate B, its distinct signature consequently occurs more than R/2 times. Conversely a nonsingleton signature retained at frequency>=R/2 must denote the SAME formal subset in each occurrence. Every term of that subset would collide in at least R/2 rounds, contradicting the event just established. Repeated coefficients within one shadow may be marked unusable; under gate B none are repeated. The majority filter therefore acquires EXACTLY the true singleton signatures, including some actual reference, without knowing F.

For every ordered true pair(t,r), coordinate i, and one round, four collision unions (target/reference in base/shifted images) fail with probability at most4(T−1)/M<=1/4. Probability of no simultaneous-isolation round is at most4^(-R). Union over all nT^2 possible pair-coordinate choices gives at most nT^2 4^(-R). This union is taken BEFORE any adaptive reference selection, so choosing an arbitrary retained coefficient as reference is legitimate. No assumed isolated reference is supplied.

The hash error is T exp(−R/4)+nT^2 4^(-R)<=delta/2 for the declared conservative R. The ideal independence of the base rounds is essential; shift/base images within a round need not be independent.

## Gate D: both monomial offsets cancel, and absolute exponents are acquired

On all good events, a genuine isolated coefficient and label are

    c_t=a_t b^e_t/F(b),      d_w(t)=w dot e_t−k_w.

Match using c_t, not the unknown e_t. Pairwise subtraction of degree labels cancels k_w. Then

    [d_(s+e_i)(t)−d_(s+e_i)(r)]−[d_s(t)−d_s(r)]
       =e_ti−e_ri.                                  (2)

Gate C provides a valid round for each such computation, and gate B ensures any observed matching singleton has the intended identity. All available votes agree on the good event. Because all true signatures have been acquired and min_t e_ti=0 after removing v,

    min_t(e_ti−e_ri)=−e_ri.

Subtracting that acquired minimum restores each absolute exponent. Divide c_t by known b^e_t to recover a_t/F(b), restore x^v, and normalize lex-leading coefficient. Only a field unit remained. A one-term GCD becomes a constant after content removal, so the same argument covers T=1 and constants; all relative exponents are zero.

Combining A/B failure bounds gives C/(Q−1)<=delta/2. Gate C supplies the remaining delta/2. Thus the claimed Monte Carlo correctness proof closes. No derivative-based characteristic requirement appears.

## Costs and the adopted totalization repair

Every image has degree<=WD=O(TD); m=R(n+1)=O(nR). Fast dense univariate GCD, image materialization, valuation scan and normalization cost soft-O(nRTD) field operations/comparisons. Evaluate sparse coefficients a_e b^e once, in O(n(tA+tB)log(D+1)) field operations. Each round computes base exponent dot products once in O(n(tA+tB)) integer operations; shifted labels add e_i rather than recomputing n-dimensional dot products. Sort/combine labels per image and use field bit strings as coefficient keys. Coordinate recovery requires O(nRT) dictionary lookups/vote-label operations; final coefficient reconstruction O(nT log(D+1)) is absorbed by the field bound.

Original defect in the overly tight separate cost statement: on bad randomness, a computed GCD can have O(TD) nonzero terms rather than T. A +T-only integer-label/dictionary estimate would then undercharge bad-event processing. This is a repairable cost issue, not a correctness counterexample. r14 adopted:

- Stop with FAILURE whenever an acquired shadow has more thanT terms, before coefficient matching. Good shadows never trigger this cap.
- Bound frequent signatures by T; reject inconsistent/missing votes, duplicate exponents and recovered degree>D.
- Charge dense index/label work at soft-O(nR(tA+tB+TD)log(TD+1)), rather than only +T.

The field arithmetic remains soft-O(nRTD+n(tA+tB)log(D+1)); the stated looser nR input-evaluation term also holds. Matching/sorting has additional representation-dependent key costs, e.g. soft-linear in key count times logQ for conventional coefficient bit strings. Uniform field sampling, exponent/label reading and field arithmetic/representation are charged; an unprovided field extension is not inherited for free. The algorithm stops on bad events rather than restarting with an uncharged expected-time claim. This remains Monte Carlo, not Las Vegas.

## Separate exact failure found in the retaining recursive control

During the audit r14 reconstructed an older recursive known-projected-support baseline using geometric rows r=0,...,t−1. That variant has a deterministic bad row: r=0 sets all previously retained coordinates to1, independently of random alpha. For A=x1−1,B=x2−1, true GCD1, n=2, and c2!=1, the previous projected GCD is1. At the next stage t=1,

    gcd(A(1,u),B(1,u))=gcd(0,u−1)=u−1.

Normalizing at c2 remains nonconstant, not the true projected GCD1. A zero-input guard may reject, but either way this invalidates a generic-success proof for the r=0 baseline. The counterexample works over arbitrarily large fields; it is unrelated to the shadow theorem's field admission.

Repair: use r=1,...,t, matching the actual primary source's indices. For every r>=1, polynomial substitution X_i->X_i^r is injective in EVERY characteristic, so nonzero cofactor resultants stay nonzero; their degrees grow by r and must be charged. Known-node Vandermonde rows1..t differ from rows0..t−1 by an invertible diagonal of nonzero nodes. No additional oracle or discrete logarithm is needed. r14 adopted this repair and retained the failure. This does not restore a novelty claim to normalized shadows.

A further probability gate for that baseline: correct projected GCDs and nonzero values at the normalization point do NOT alone imply correct acquired univariate GCDs. If U=x1−a,V=u−d,G=1, a sampled alpha^r=a gives gcd(0,u−d)=u−d even when the preceding projected gcd and its value are1. A separately charged resultant-specialization event is necessary. The source's Theorem3.3 proof is not adopted here as a proof of that conditional implication or of the reconstructed baseline's shorter probability budget. r14 agreed to charge every geometric cofactor specialization conservatively. This is not a disproof of the source theorem's eventual bound or of the repaired baseline.
