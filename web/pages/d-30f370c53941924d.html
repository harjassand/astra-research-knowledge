\section{Finite-volume safety under predictable reaction control}
\label{app:jump-safety}

An acquired strict inward-polytope certificate has a direct stochastic
consequence. The result below gives explicit finite-volume constants and
allows predictable control of reaction rates. It asserts confinement for
an exponentially long finite horizon; it does not assert stochastic
permanence. Exponential Lyapunov methods and chemical large-deviation
theory are established antecedents.\footnote{Agazzi--Dembo--Eckmann,
\emph{Large deviations theory for Markov jump models of chemical reaction
networks}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126}; their geometric companion,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240}. No general historical novelty is
claimed for the exponential-martingale method.}

\paragraph{Exact input.}
Let $y_r\to y'_r$, $1\le r\le R$, be integer-complex reactions in
$\mathbb N^d$, with $\nu_r=y'_r-y_r$ and
$S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\nu_r\}$. Supply a compact polytope
in the positive orthant,
\[
 Q=\{x\in c+S:g_i(x)=a_i\cdot x+b_i\ge0,
                         \ 1\le i\le m\},
\]
with its proper relative facets and coordinate bounds $0<x_j\le U_j$.
Constant conservation inequalities are removed. Supply
$\ell_i,\gamma_i>0$ such that every admissible rate vector satisfies
\begin{equation}
 a_i\cdot f(x,k)\ge\gamma_i\quad
 (x\in Q,\ 0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i),\qquad
 f(x,k)=\sum_r k_r x^{y_r}\nu_r.
 \label{eq:jump-collar}
\end{equation}
A strict facet margin extends to some positive collar by compactness;
the numerical collar and margin in \eqref{eq:jump-collar} must actually
be certified. Active rates satisfy $0\le k_r\le K$. Switching modes
may set inactive rates to zero, provided \eqref{eq:jump-collar} holds
for every allowed mode.

For integer volume $V\ge1$, counts $Z^V\in\mathbb N^d$ have
concentrations $X^V=Z^V/V$, jumps $\nu_r/V$, and predictable intensities
\begin{equation}
 V k_r(t,\text{past})\phi^V_{y_r}(X^V_{t-}),\qquad
 \phi^V_y(x)=V^{-|y|}\prod_j(Vx_j)_{y_j}.
 \label{eq:jump-intensity}
\end{equation}
Here $(n)_q=n(n-1)\cdots(n-q+1)$ and an insufficient count gives zero.
The convention absorbs binomial-factorial divisions into $k_r$.
Control can depend on time, the full past and independent randomness,
but must be predictable; no Markov assumption is needed. Supply an
initial count $Z^V_0$ with $X^V_0\in Q\cap(c+S)$ and
$g_i(X^V_0)\ge d_i>0$, uniformly in the volumes under consideration.
This is a lattice-feasibility requirement: coordinate rounding may
violate conserved totals or the reaction lattice
$Z^V_0+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{\nu_r\}$.
For rational data, a rational $x_*\in\operatorname{relint}Q$ gives one
compatible sequence by taking denominator multiples $V$ and
$Z^V_0=Vx_*$; prescribed count totals or communicating cosets require
their own feasibility check.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform factorial correction]
For every count-lattice point with $0\le x_j\le U_j$, put
\[
 E_y(U)=\sum_{j:y_j\ge2}\binom{y_j}{2}
              U_j^{y_j-1}\prod_{l\ne j}U_l^{y_l}.
\]
Then $0\le x^y-\phi^V_y(x)\le E_y(U)/V$, including points with
insufficient reactants.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the count lattice the falling-factorial product equals
$\prod_{j}\prod_{q=0}^{y_j-1}\max\{x_j-q/V,0\}$: if a reactant is
insufficient, both products contain a zero factor. Each nonnegative
factor is at most $x_j$, and its deficit from $x_j$ is at most $q/V$.
Telescoping the products, bounding the other factors by their coordinate
maxima, and summing $q$ gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit controlled exit certificate]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_i&=K\sum_r|a_i\cdot\nu_r|E_{y_r}(U),&
 J_i&=\max_r|a_i\cdot\nu_r|,\\
 B_i&=K\sum_r(a_i\cdot\nu_r)^2U^{y_r},&
 \Lambda&=K\sum_r U^{y_r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Proper relative facets have $J_i,B_i>0$. Set
\[
 V_0=\max\left\{1,\max_i\left\lceil\frac{2D_i}{\gamma_i}
                         \right\rceil\right\},\quad
 \mu_i=\frac{\gamma_i}{2},\quad
 \theta_i=\min\left\{\frac1{J_i},\frac{\mu_i}{3B_i}\right\},
 \quad c_* =\min_i\theta_i\ell_i>0.
\]
For $V\ge V_0$, let $\tau_Q$ be the first jump outside the closed
polytope $Q$. Uniformly over all admissible predictable controls and
every finite $T$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P(\tau_Q\le T)
 \le\sum_{i=1}^m e^{-\theta_i Vg_i(X^V_0)}
                 +2mV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V}.
 \label{eq:jump-exit-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, with $c_0=\min_i\theta_i d_i>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P(\tau_Q\le e^{c_*V/2})
       \le m e^{-c_0V}+2mV\Lambda e^{-c_*V/2}.
\]
The same bound holds for a random initial count satisfying these gap
bounds almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The factorial lemma implies that the actual inward drift in collar $i$
is at least $\gamma_i-D_i/V\ge\mu_i$. Before exit the total hazard is
at most $V\Lambda$, and
$\sum_r k_r\phi^V_{y_r}(x)(a_i\cdot\nu_r)^2\le B_i$.
Write $\Phi_i(x)=e^{-\theta_i Vg_i(x)}$ and
$\Phi=\sum_i\Phi_i$. Its predictable generator inside $Q$ is
\[
 \frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_i(x)}{\Phi_i(x)}
  =V\sum_r k_r\phi^V_{y_r}(x)
                        (e^{-\theta_i a_i\cdot\nu_r}-1).
\]
The inequality $e^z-1\le z+z^2 e^{|z|}/2$, together with
$\theta_iJ_i\le1$ and $e<3$, bounds this expression in collar $i$ by
\[
 V\left(-\theta_i\mu_i+\frac32\theta_i^2B_i\right)
          \le-\frac12V\theta_i\mu_i\le0.
\]
Outside that collar, $\Phi_i\le e^{-\theta_i V\ell_i}$ and the
positive generator contribution is at most
$V\Lambda(e^{\theta_iJ_i}-1)\Phi_i
 \le2V\Lambda e^{-\theta_i V\ell_i}$.
Therefore $\mathcal L_t\Phi\le2mV\Lambda e^{-c_*V}$ everywhere in $Q$.

At exit a facet gap is negative, so $\Phi(X^V_{\tau_Q})\ge1$.
Each projected exit overshoot is at most $J_i/V$, and hence
$\Phi(X^V_{t\wedge\tau_Q})\le\sum_i e^{\theta_iJ_i}\le3m$.
The bounded pre-exit hazard gives a nonexplosive stopped process.
The compensated counting-process formula, valid for predictable
intensities, therefore gives for every finite $T$
\[
 \mathbb E\Phi(X^V_{T\wedge\tau_Q})
 \le\Phi(X^V_0)+2mV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V}.
\]
Nonnegativity and the exit lower bound prove
\eqref{eq:jump-exit-bound}. Conditioning on the initial history gives
the random-initial-state statement. This argument handles corners and
overshoots directly and needs no traversal count, equilibrium law,
irreducibility, or global nonexplosion claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A complete rational two-species instance.}
Consider $2A\to A+B$ and $2B\to A+B$, with arbitrary predictable
$1\le k_A,k_B\le4$. Fix total count $A+B=V$ and even $V$, start from
$A=B=V/2$, and write $x=A/V$, $y=1-x$. Use
\[
 Q=\{x+y=1,\ 1/4\le x\le3/4\},\quad
 g_L=x-1/4,\quad g_R=3/4-x,\quad\ell=1/32.
\]
The deterministic facet margin is $5/16$; on the left collar
$x\le9/32$, the worst inward drift is
$(1-x)^2-4x^2\ge205/1024$, and the right collar is symmetric.
Stronger stochastic constants follow directly, without a large-$V$
correction threshold. The scaled birth and death intensities are
$b=k_B y(y-1/V)$ and $d=k_A x(x-1/V)$.
In the left collar either $d=0$ or
\[
 \frac bd\ge\frac{y(y-1/V)}{4x(x-1/V)}
        \ge\frac{y^2}{4x^2}\ge\frac{529}{324}.
\]
The middle inequality uses $y>x$ and $x>1/V$; zero death only helps.
Choose $\theta=12/25$. Since $j!\ge2\cdot3^{j-2}$ for $j\ge2$,
\[
 e^\theta\le1+\theta+
       \frac{\theta^2}{2(1-\theta/3)}
       =\frac{283}{175}<\frac{529}{324}.
\]
Thus the exact left-facet generator is nonpositive:
$b(e^{-\theta}-1)+d(e^\theta-1)
 =(e^\theta-1)(d-b/e^\theta)\le0$.
The right facet is identical with birth and death exchanged.
On $Q$ the total scaled activity is at most
$4\max[x^2+(1-x)^2]=5/2$. Applying the same stopped-sum proof, with
the sharper bound $e^\theta-1\le108/175$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P(\tau_Q\le T)
    \le 2e^{-3V/25}+\frac{108}{35}VT e^{-3V/200}.
 \label{eq:jump-two-species}
\end{equation}
At $T=e^{3V/400}$ the second term is
$(108/35)V e^{-3V/400}$. For example, $V=3000$ gives
$T\simeq5.91\times10^9$ and an exit bound below $1.57\times10^{-6}$,
in the time units fixed by the rate bounds. This numerical evaluation
is not a simulation of that horizon.

\paragraph{Finite horizon, extinction, and resource limits.}
A fixed concentration buffer is essential: $g_i(X^V_0)=O(1/V)$ does
not give an exponentially small initial term. The theorem supplies no
stochastic entrance protocol from an arbitrary exterior initial state.
At fixed positive constant rates the two-species instance eventually
exits $Q$ almost surely for every even $V\ge6$: counts
$A=1,\ldots,V-1$ form a finite irreducible birth--death class, containing
$A=1$ outside $Q$. Its extinction states are inaccessible.
More generally an inward polytope does not exclude inevitable
extinction. For $A+B\to2A$ at rate $2$ and $A\to B$ at rate $1$,
with the same conserved total, the deterministic equation is
$x'=x(1-2x)$. The same $Q$ has strict inward facet margins $1/8$ and
$3/8$, but the finite count chain reaches the accessible absorbing
state $A=0$ almost surely. This last network is not endotactic; it
shows the limit of the inward-polytope stochastic conclusion.

Ordinary molecular mass action requires integer complexes. Rational
source exponents with integer jumps instead define a different
density-dependent model if one uses intensities $Vk_rx^{y_r}$;
rational jumps require a separately specified commensurate lattice.
If the artificial model retains nonnegative counts, it also needs
$V\min_Qx_j\ge\max_r(-\nu_{rj})_+$ or a charged feasibility cutoff.
Neither replacement is silently covered by molecular propensity
\eqref{eq:jump-intensity}. Acquiring $Q$ and its collars remains a
separate geometric/certification cost; exhaustive rational search and
real quantifier elimination establish no efficient acquisition bound.
Once supplied, the displayed constants require $O(mdR)$ scalar
operations plus monomial evaluation, with input bit lengths charged.
A stopped simulator can use rate-$V\Lambda$ thinning, with at most
$V\Lambda T$ expected candidate events and $O(d\log V)$ count storage
for fixed $Q$. Full-horizon simulation is exponential work, and each
event also needs a supplied controller evaluator. Predictability is
not a computability assumption. In open systems, maintaining the
rates for this horizon can require cumulative feed of order $VT$.
The closed examples conserve molecules and require no material inflow;
the energetic and control costs of realizing their assumed rates
are still supplied physical requirements. No sharp quasipotential,
optimal exit exponent, stationary law, or stochastic permanence is
claimed.
