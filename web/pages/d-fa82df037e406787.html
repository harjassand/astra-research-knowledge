1. Exact statement and conventions

Let sigma>0 be a density matrix, s=sqrt(sigma), and let exp(-t L) be a
finite-dimensional unital completely positive Heisenberg semigroup whose
Schrodinger dual fixes sigma. Assume L is selfadjoint for
  <X,Y>_sigma = Tr[s X* s Y].
L is the POSITIVE generator. No primitivity or modular covariance is needed.
For positive f, p>1, p'=p/(p-1), use
  I_{r,p}(f) = sigma^(-1/(2r))
      (sigma^(1/(2p)) f sigma^(1/(2p)))^(p/r) sigma^(-1/(2r)),
  E_p(f) = p/[2(p-1)] <I_{p',p}(f), L f>_sigma.
Then
  E_p(f) >= [p sin(pi/p)/(2(p-1))] E_2(I_{2,p}(f)).             (T)
This holds also for positive semidefinite f and every finite amplification
id_R tensor L, with reference tau_R tensor sigma for any faithful tau_R.
The same proof covers arbitrary strictly positive, unnormalized sigma.

2. Previously independently audited representation used as a premise

Put Gamma_s(X)=s^(1/2) X s^(1/2) and H=Gamma_s L Gamma_s^(-1).
The finite KMS Hermitian-noise representation already audited in Cycle06/08 is
  H(X) = {V,X}/2 - sum_j B_j X B_j,  B_j=B_j*, V=V*,
  s V + V s = 2 sum_j B_j s B_j.                              (2.1)
Its scope is all finite KMS QMS, including their finite amplifications. The
conditional Choi block of -H is positive and invariant under the adjoint
antiunitary, giving Hermitian noise matrices. The remaining Choi block gives
an anticommutator; HS selfadjointness removes an imaginary commutator except
an irrelevant scalar. H(s)=0 fixes V through the invertible Lyapunov map.
In physical coordinates the drift s^(1/2) V s^(-1/2) can be non-Hermitian;
that drift is retained throughout this proof. Thus no physical Hamiltonian
term is silently discarded. The claimed inequality is linear in the generator,
so it suffices to treat one Hermitian noise B and
  V=2 Lyap_s^(-1)(B s B), Lyap_s(X)=sX+Xs.

3. Remove the KT prefactor exactly

Set mu=1-2/p, so -1<mu<1, and define
  Y=s^(1/p) f s^(1/p), q=Y^(p/2).
Thus Y=q^(1-mu), Y^(p-1)=q^(1+mu), and
  I_{2,p}(f)=Gamma_s^(-1)(q),
  E_2(I_{2,p}(f))=E(q):=Tr[q H(q)].                           (3.1)
The raw KT pairing F_mu(q):=<I_{p',p}(f),L f>_sigma equals
  Tr[q^(1+mu) s^(-mu/2)
        H(s^(mu/2) q^(1-mu) s^(mu/2)) s^(-mu/2)].             (3.2)
For one noise this is
  F_mu(q)=Tr[q^2 cosh((mu/2) ad_U)(V)]
      -Tr[q^(1+mu) s^(-mu/2) B s^(mu/2)
                      q^(1-mu) s^(mu/2) B s^(-mu/2)],        (3.3)
where U=log s. Cyclicity of trace gives F_mu=F_{-mu} exactly.
Also p/[2(p-1)]=1/(1+mu) and sin(pi/p)=cos(pi mu/2).
Consequently (T) is equivalent to
  F_mu(q) >= cos(pi mu/2) E(q).                              (3.4)
There is no missing prefactor 2 in this reduction.

4. Exact modular-frequency/state-pair reduction

Decompose B=sum_omega B_omega for ad_U, with
  [U,B_omega]=omega B_omega, B_{-omega}=B_omega*.
All sums are finite; coincident frequencies are grouped. Lyapunov inversion
gives the full ordered-product identity
  V=sum_{omega,nu} exp((nu-omega)/2)
       sech((omega+nu)/2) B_omega B_nu.                       (4.1)
No commutation of q with s is used. In an arbitrary eigenbasis of positive q,
put q=diag(q_i), beta_ij=log(q_i/q_j),
  x_omega^{ij}=<i|B_omega|j>.
For a real constant k, expansion of (3.3) and E(q), followed by replacing the
first frequency with its negative, yields F_mu-kE as a sum of
  q_i q_j conj(x_omega^{ij}) x_nu^{ij} D_{mu,k}(x,y),
where x=beta_ij-omega, y=beta_ij-nu, m=(x+y)/2, h=(x-y)/2, and
  D_{mu,k}(x,y)=exp(-m)[cosh(mu h)-k]/cosh(h)-exp(-mu m)+k.
For clarity the three unsymmetrized coefficients are respectively
  q_j^2 exp((omega+nu)/2) sech((nu-omega)/2)
       [cosh(mu(nu-omega)/2)-k],
  -q_j^(1+mu) q_i^(1-mu) exp(mu(omega+nu)/2),
  +k q_i q_j.                                               (4.2)
The involution (i,j,omega,nu)->(j,i,-nu,-omega), together with
  x_{-omega}^{ji}=conj(x_omega^{ij}),
leaves the full scalar sum unchanged and replaces (x,y) by (-y,-x).
D is real and symmetric in x,y. Averaging the two equal sums gives the EXACT
identity
  F_mu(q)-kE(q)
   =sum_{i,j} q_i q_j sum_{omega,nu}
       conj(x_omega^{ij}) K_{mu,k}(beta_ij-omega,beta_ij-nu)
                            x_nu^{ij},                     (4.3)
where
  K_{mu,k}(x,y)=cosh(m)[cosh(mu h)-k]/cosh(h)-cosh(mu m)+k.
The ordered state-pair coefficient is q_i q_j, without an extra factor 2.
The averaging includes i=j; no diagonal-pair exception or real-amplitude
assumption is needed. Degenerate eigenvalues of q are harmless (beta=0).

5. A contractive analytic row proves the required kernel PSD

Fix -1<mu<1 and put c=cos(pi mu/2)>0. On the open unit disk define
  A(z)=cosh(mu atanh z),
  D(z)=sinh(mu atanh z)/z, with D(0)=mu,
  h(z)=D(z)/A(z), g(z)=1/A(z).
The principal atanh maps the unit disk to |Im w|<pi/4. Hence A has no zeros
and |A(z)|>=cos(pi |mu|/4)>0. In particular g is bounded and analytic.
The map tanh(mu atanh z) maps the disk into itself and vanishes at zero;
Schwarz's lemma gives |h(z)|<=1. Thus h is bounded and analytic as well.

For z on the unit circle apart from +/-1, Im(atanh z)=+/-pi/4. Therefore
  |A(z)|^2-|D(z)|^2
    =|cosh(mu atanh z)|^2-|sinh(mu atanh z)|^2
    =cos(pi mu/2)=c.                                       (5.1)
Here |z|=1 is what replaces D by sinh in the modulus. It follows that
  |h(z)|^2+c|g(z)|^2=1 a.e. on the unit circle.               (5.2)
The bounded analytic row theta=(h,sqrt(c) g) is thus a contractive multiplier
H^2 direct-sum H^2 -> H^2: for u,v in H^2,
  ||h u+sqrt(c) g v||_2^2 <= ||u||_2^2+||v||_2^2.
Consequently
  Q=I-M_h M_h*-c M_g M_g* >=0.
For real t,u in (-1,1), the Szego vectors k_t=(1,t,t^2,...) give the PSD
kernel
  <k_t,Q k_u>=[1-h(t)h(u)-c g(t)g(u)]/(1-tu).                (5.3)

Now take t=tanh(x/2), u=tanh(y/2). Direct hyperbolic identities give
  K_{mu,c}(x,y)
    =2tu [A(t)A(u)-D(t)D(u)-c]/(1-tu)
    =(sqrt(2) t A(t)) <k_t,Q k_u> (sqrt(2) u A(u)).           (5.4)
Thus K_{mu,c} is positive semidefinite on every finite collection of REAL
x-values, with arbitrary complex coefficient vectors. Signed diagonal
congruence is valid. At t=0 the congruence factor vanishes and the original
kernel also vanishes; no division by t is required. At mu=0, A=1,D=0,c=1,
Q=0, so (3.4) is equality, as required at p=2.

Combining (4.3) with (5.4) proves (3.4), hence (T), for every positive q,
every Hermitian noise, and therefore every generator in (2.1).
This is an actual Gram construction, rather than pairwise kernel positivity
or a finite numerical spectrum. No matrix operator-Jensen assertion is used.

6. Supports, amplification, reciprocity, and the entropy endpoint

For p>1, q^(1+mu) and q^(1-mu) have strictly positive exponents. If f>=0 is
singular, apply the proved inequality to f+epsilon I and let epsilon down to
zero. All powers and both finite-dimensional quadratic forms are continuous.
There is no dimension, minimum-eigenvalue, rank, or amplification-size constant
in (T). Faithfulness of sigma is used to define the weighted coordinates.

For an arbitrary finite reference with faithful tau_R, id_R tensor L is KMS
selfadjoint for tau_R tensor sigma. Its HS implementation is id_R tensor H,
with Hermitian noises I_R tensor B_j and V'=I_R tensor V. It annihilates
sqrt(tau_R) tensor s. Thus the exact same proof applies to every joint positive
f, without a product-state or fixed-reference-marginal restriction.

The reciprocal branch p'=p/(p-1) is not an additional conjecture: it sends
mu to -mu and keeps q fixed after the appropriate power substitution. The
even raw pairing F_mu proves both branches with their exact KT prefactors.

For strictly positive fixed f, p down to 1 has
  rho=s f s, I_{2,1}(f)=Gamma_s^(-1)(sqrt(rho)),
  E_1(f)=1/2 Tr[L*(rho)(log rho-log sigma)],
because KMS symmetry gives Gamma_sigma L=L* Gamma_sigma. This agrees with
the directly checked positive-generator KT endpoint convention. Since
  lim_{p down to 1} p sin(pi/p)/[2(p-1)]=pi/2,
the p=1 limit is precisely the previously audited J>=pi E statement.
No singular-state entropy differentiability is needed for the p>1 theorem.
