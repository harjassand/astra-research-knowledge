## 1. A bounded-ψ₂ smooth entropy bottleneck without log-concavity

Let `q_sigma=(N(1,sigma²)+N(-1,sigma²))/2`, for 0<σ<=1/2. It is centered and smooth. Its MGF is `cosh(t)exp(σ²t²/2)<=exp((1+σ²)t²/2)`. Integrating against a Gaussian proves the exact Orlicz certificate

\[
\mathbb E_{q_\sigma}e^{X^2/a_\sigma^2}\le2,
\qquad a_\sigma^2=\frac83(1+\sigma^2)\le\frac{10}3.
\]

The negative log density has second derivative `σ^{-2}−σ^{-4}sech²(x/σ²)`, negative at zero. Thus the excluded hypothesis is exactly log-concavity.

Take the odd clipped-linear function f equal to x/ε for |x|<=ε and sign(x) outside, with ε=σ². Its variance is at least 1/2 because each mixture component puts at least half its mass beyond the adjacent transition edge. Its energy is
`ε^{-2} P(|X|<=ε)`, bounded above by

\[
\frac{2}{\sigma^3\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\left[-\frac{(1-\sigma^2)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right].
\]

Consequently the Poincaré constant, and hence R, obeys

\[
R(q_\sigma)\ge\frac{\sigma^3\sqrt{2\pi}}4
\exp\left[\frac{(1-\sigma^2)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right].
\]

The clipped test is an admissible Sobolev test; smooth approximants and outer cutoffs pass the entropy/variance and energy to it. The lower bound grows exponentially while a² stays bounded. At σ=1/8 it already gives R>3.58×10¹⁰ with a²<=65/24. This is an analytic family, not a finite numerical refutation.

## 2. The same family explicitly breaks entropy-loss when base log-concavity is removed

Set p=N(1,σ²) and q=q_σ. Both meet all smoothness, finiteness, and exponential-square assumptions of Lemma 4.2 except q log-concavity. The original relative score is

\[
w(x)=\frac{2/\sigma^2}{1+e^{2x/\sigma^2}}.
\]

Substitute x=σ²u in its p-energy. Removing the extra factor `exp(-σ²u²/2)<=1` gives

\[
I\le\frac{4e^{-1/(2\sigma^2)}}{\sigma^3\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^u}{(1+e^{2u})^2}\,du
=\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\sigma^{-3}e^{-1/(2\sigma^2)},
\]

since the last integral becomes `∫_0^∞(1+t²)^{-2}dt=π/4`. Conditional Jensen implies the entire prediction integral on 0<r<1 is at most I.

In contrast, the sign test gives `H0>=log2−h_b(Φ(-1/σ))`. After convolution variance 1, write v=1+σ². The exact density identity gives

\[
H_1=1/v-\mathbb E_{N(1,v)}\log\cosh(Y/v)
\le1/v-\log\cosh(1/v).
\]

At σ=1/4 these closed bounds yield `2(H0−H1)>=0.2826814`, while `∫_0^1 E|wbar_r|² dr<=I<=0.0269082`. The gap is >0.25577. There is also an entirely coarse analytic separation: the Gaussian tail at four is <1/20000, binary entropy there is <11/20000, `log(1+e^{-2})>28/225`, and the score bound is <0.048; hence twice entropy loss exceeds 0.247 while the prediction integral is below 0.048. Here the entropy lower bound uses data processing through the sign map: q has sign probability 1/2 and p has probability 1−δ, with δ=Φ(−4). For the coarse separation, put u=16/17<1. Then `H0−H1>=log(1+e^{-2u})−h_b(δ)>28/225−11/20000`. ## 3. A whole positive control family for the entropy-loss constants

For p=N(m,v), q=N(0,τ), put `k=1/τ−1/v`. Given U_r=Y+√rG,

\[
\bar w_r=\frac m\tau+k\frac v{v+r}(U_r-m),
\quad\mathbb E|\bar w_r|^2=\frac{m^2}{\tau^2}
+\frac{(v-\tau)^2}{\tau^2(v+r)}.
\]

The actual heat relative Fisher energy is the same numerator with `(τ+r)²` in place of τ², so it is pointwise smaller. Integrating exact heat entropy dissipation proves Lemma 4.2 for every m∈R and v,τ,z>0 in this family. The predicted-score integral is

\[
\frac{m^2 z}{\tau^2}+\frac{(v-\tau)^2}{\tau^2}\log(1+z/v).

The independent closed KL formula `H_r=[(v+r+m²)/(τ+r)−1+log((τ+r)/(v+r))]/2` verifies the half-Fisher derivative. The replay checks 81 rational parameter tuples at 70 digits; the universal Gaussian-family statement follows from the displayed pointwise comparison, not from those tuples.

## 4. Linear subgaussianity does not supply uniformly good tilted posteriors

Let μ_R have density proportional to e^{-Rx} on [0,1], R>=4. It is log-concave and has mean `m_R=1/R−1/(e^R−1)`. Since x²<=x on this interval,

\[
\mathbb E e^{RX^2/2}\le\frac2{1+e^{-R/2}}<2.
\]

The Orlicz triangle inequality and m_R<=1/R imply
`||X−m_R||_ψ₂<=sqrt(2/R)+1/(R sqrt(log2))<=sqrt(5/R)` for R>=4. An external field +R makes its density uniform on [0,1], whose variance is 1/12. Thus tilted covariance divided by the supplied original a² is at least R/60, unbounded. A subsequent sufficiently weak quadratic observation preserves a positive fraction of this variance.

Therefore applying 093 at the initial measure does not produce strong tilt stability or a uniform easy-posterior oracle. Reapplying it to each posterior requires checking that posterior's own centered ψ₂ scale. Conditional on 093, the initial entropy coefficient is at most 5C/R, whereas the uniformly tilted law requires coefficient at least 2/π² by its Poincaré test; thus that original LSI bound cannot transfer unchanged to every linear tilt. The centered ψ₂ norm in this construction has order R^{-1/2}, as can also be seen from the rare far endpoint; do not replace it by a covariance standard deviation of order R^{-1}.

Explicitly, `P(X>=1/2)>=e^{-R/2}/2` and m_R<=1/4. Any valid centered parameter b must therefore obey `2>=E exp((X−m_R)²/b²)>=(e^{-R/2}/2)exp(1/(16b²))`, so `b²>=1/(8R+16log4)`. Together with the 5/R upper bound this proves the asserted order.

## 5. Diffuse spectrum defeats any fixed-scale replacement of the proof

Consider an exact commuting block algebra in dimension m with `ρ=I/m` and `H_i=e_i e_i*`. It obeys S=∑H_i²=I, exact three-index symmetry, and `||H[v]||_HS=|v|`. Its frozen observation is a product of m independent copies of the scalar variance mixture with scale x=T/m. If J denotes the full scalar scale integral, its full matrix integral is

\[
\int_0^\infty m I_{\rm scalar}(T/m)\frac{dT}{T^2}=J.
\]

This is exact for every m. At a fixed finite T window, however, Gaussian relative entropy to variance one and `h−log(1+h)<=h²/2` give

\[
I_{\rm scalar}(x)\le\frac14x^2\mathbb E\ell^2
=\frac14x^2e^{8t},
\qquad
\int_0^A mI_{\rm scalar}(T/m)\frac{dT}{T^2}
\le\frac{Ae^{8t}}{4m}\to0.
\]

This analytic discriminator shows why a fixed-scale or compact-scale information check loses all the diffuse mass. It independently verifies the need for the full-axis Jacobian in the manuscript. This static block algebra is not a construction of the manuscript's critical stationary law and is not a counterexample to it. An integral over all scales does not by itself give an affordable finite scale-search algorithm when useful eigenvalues are unknown.

## 6. Weak limits and elementary robustness, conditional on 093

If centered log-concave μ_j in one fixed Euclidean dimension satisfy the same ψ₂ bound a and converge weakly, their means converge by uniform subgaussian integrability, and the limit retains the ψ₂ bound by Portmanteau. For compact smooth f, f², f²log f², and |∇f|² are bounded continuous; every term of the LSI passes to the limit. The resulting inequality is valid even on an affine support. Alternatively, add Gaussian variance s to a singular log-concave law; the Orlicz parameter is at most `a+sqrt(8s/3)`, apply 093, and let s→0. Tangential gradients are obtained by an extension constant in normal directions near the support. No dimension-changing weak-limit assertion is made.

For a bounded perturbation ν with density ratio between m>0 and M<∞, the entropy formula
`Ent(f²)=inf_c ∫[f²log(f²/c)−f²+c]dμ` has a nonnegative integrand. Thus `Ent_ν(f²)<=M Ent_μ(f²)` and `∫|∇f|²dμ<=m^{-1}∫|∇f|²dν`, proving coefficient inflation at most M/m. In particular a potential perturbation of oscillation D costs e^D. This is the classical bounded-perturbation mechanism, not a new theorem.

If A is convex and μ(A)=p>0, Hölder gives, with `λ=log(2/p)/log2`,
`E[exp(Z²/(λa²))|A]<=(2/p)^{1/λ}=2` for each original centered linear marginal Z. Its conditional mean has magnitude at most `a sqrt(λ log2)`. The Orlicz triangle inequality therefore gives centered conditional marginal parameter at most `2a sqrtλ`. Applying 093 to the log-concave conditional law yields entropy coefficient `4C a² log(2/p)/log2`. The mass p must be known or separately bounded; finite observations do not certify it for free.

## 7. Strongest supported sampling and research consequence

Conditional on 093, set L=C a² and assume a well-defined stationary Dirichlet diffusion for the target. For an initial law with finite KL H0, its standard generator gives

\[
H_t\le e^{-4t/L}H_0,\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(\nu_t,\mu)\le\sqrt{H_0/2}\,e^{-2t/L}.
\]

Thus the *continuous diffusion time* to TV ε is at most `(L/4)log(H0/(2ε²))` when the logarithm is positive. This needs no positive curvature bound. Uniform convex-body targets require the appropriate reflected/Dirichlet process; an unconstrained smooth Langevin update is not an implementation of that process.

Herbst and tensorization also imply that N independent target samples estimate any fixed K-Lipschitz observable with absolute error ε and failure probability δ using `N>=C a² K² ε^{-2}log(2/δ)`. The concentration scale applies to nonlinear observables such as distance to a set, norms, and suprema of unit-slope affine fields. This is the actual new research capital if 093 passes: a certificate for linear tails becomes a certificate for all such nonlinear observables. Target sample acquisition, data-dependent observable selection, or uniform control over an infinite function class requires additional costs/assumptions.

There is no query or bit-complexity theorem in 093. To see a concrete obstruction to the common Euler inference, take the completely legal Gaussian target `N(0,diag(ε²,1))`. It has exact R=1 and a²=8/3 for every 0<ε<=1. Its Euler Langevin update in the stiff coordinate is `X←(1−h/ε²)X+sqrt(2h)G`, which has a stationary distribution only when h<2ε². Its stationary variance is `ε²/(1−h/(2ε²))`. Taking h=ε²/10 fixes that bias at 20/19 but makes the slow coordinate require Θ(ε^{-2}) updates to reduce a mean by a fixed factor. At ε=10^{-3}, the exact count to reduce its mean tenfold is 23,025,850, while the continuous time is log10. Preconditioning or another sampler can avoid this; it is a limitation of that inference, not a lower bound against all algorithms.

Finally 093 is not an unrestricted KLS reduction. A centered exponential has finite variance but infinite ψ₂ norm, and Gaussian convolution retains its exponential tail. Even an isotropic simplex has a ψ₂ marginal growing with dimension: its standardized barycentric marginal is `Z=((n+1)T−1)sqrt((n+2)/n)` for T~Beta(1,n). Since P(T>=1/2)=2^{-n}, the Orlicz bound forces

\[
a^2\ge\frac{(n-1)^2(n+2)}{4n(n+1)\log2}=\Omega(n).
\]

The statement follows solely from a concrete marginal and moment test. It says the theorem's hypothesis is materially stronger than bounded covariance, irrespective of the separate current KLS proof claim.
