1. EXACT FRAME SUPPORT

Let G be a simple vertex-transitive graph on d vertices, with degree r>0
and clique number omega>=2. For every unordered edge uv take

 A_uv=E_uv+E_vu,   B_uv=i(E_uv-E_vu).

These are traceless Hermitian. For any normalized pure psi, writing
p_u=|psi_u|^2 gives the phase-independent score

 f(psi)=sum_edges(<A_uv>^2+<B_uv>^2)
       =4 sum_edges p_u p_v.                                    (G1)

Put mu=1-1/omega. Exactly

 k=max_{common pure ensemble with barycenter I/d} average f=2mu,
 L=sum_edges(A_uv^2+B_uv^2)=2r I,  V=tau(L)=2r.                  (G2)

For completeness the Motzkin--Straus bound used here has an elementary
proof. If two positive-mass vertices are nonadjacent, transferring their
combined mass to the one with the larger weighted neighbor sum does not
decrease sum_edges p_u p_v. Repetition leaves a clique-supported vector.
On a clique of size q<=omega the value is
(1-sum p_u^2)/2<=(1-1/q)/2<=(1-1/omega)/2.
Uniform mass on a maximum clique attains it. Thus f<=2mu for ALL pure
states, and 2mu I is a global envelope. Average that maximizing state
over independent coordinate signs to remove density off-diagonal entries,
then over graph automorphisms. Vertex transitivity makes its barycenter
I/d and preserves f. This gives a finite common pure ensemble attaining
the upper bound, so k in (G2) is unrestricted, not a seed-hull value.

2. EXACT STAR REDUCTION

The Choi transpose matters: A_uv^T=A_uv and B_uv^T=-B_uv. Consequently

 A_uv^T tensor A_uv+B_uv^T tensor B_uv
   =2(E_uv tensor E_uv+E_vu tensor E_vu).

Hence the two-output star is

 W=2 sum_edges[E_uv(A)(E_uv(B)+E_uv(C))
                  +E_vu(A)(E_vu(B)+E_vu(C))].                   (G3)

For each residual label k, it leaves invariant the orthogonal subspace

 S_k=span{|i,i,k>, |i,k,i>: i=1,...,d},                          (G4)

where the two vectors coincide at i=k, so dim(S_k)=2d-1. The S_k are
mutually orthogonal. All remaining basis vectors have A unequal to both
B and C and are annihilated. Restricted to S_k, W is exactly twice the
adjacency of H_k, the graph formed from two copies of G with vertex k
identified. BC swap exchanges the copies. Let lambda_k=rho(H_k).
Therefore max eigenvalue(W)=2 max_k lambda_k. Vertex transitivity makes
lambda_k=lambda independent of k. No tracial constraint was used here.

3. A GENERAL REGULAR-GRAPH COALESCENCE BOUND

The following bound needs r-regularity but not vertex transitivity.
For ANY r-regular simple graph of clique number at most omega, define
t>0 by

 t(t+r/mu)=r,   or
 t=[sqrt((r/mu)^2+4r)-r/mu]/2.                                  (G5)

Then every root-coalescence graph H_k satisfies

 rho(H_k)<=r+t<r+mu.                                            (G6)

To prove it, first observe t<mu<1 by inserting mu in the increasing
quadratic t(t+r/mu). Let N=N_G(k), with |N|=r, and let Q be its induced
adjacency. Its clique number is at most omega-1. Set
nu=1-1/(omega-1), taking nu=0 when omega=2.
For a nonnegative Perron vector y of Q, the preceding simplex bound and
Cauchy--Schwarz give

 y^T Q y<=nu (sum y)^2<=nu r ||y||^2,
 rho(Q)<=nu r.                                                  (G7)

Put Lambda=r+t and choose the positive vector

 z=(1-t)(Lambda I-Q)^(-1)1,   Z=sum z.

The inverse is entrywise nonnegative by its convergent Neumann series.
Since Q is real symmetric, its resolvent norm is 1/(Lambda-rho(Q)), so

 Z<=(1-t)r/(Lambda-rho(Q))
  <=(1-t)r/(Lambda-nu r)=t.                                     (G8)

The last equality is exactly (G5) rearranged. On H_k assign value 2 to
the glued root, value 1+z_i to each neighbor i in either copy, and value
1 to all other vertices. At a root-neighbor, the adjacency row equals
r+1+(Qz)_i, which is Lambda(1+z_i) because
(Lambda I-Q)z=(1-t)1. At any non-neighbor its row is at most r+Z<=Lambda.
At the glued root its row is 2(r+Z)<=2Lambda. This positive supersolution
implies rho(H_k)<=Lambda. This proves (G6), including disconnected graphs.

4. THE ENTIRE VERTEX-TRANSITIVE COUNTEREXAMPLE MECHANISM CLOSES

Combining (G2), (G3), and (G6) gives the OPERATOR inequality

 W<=2(r+t) I < (V+k) I.                                        (G9)

Thus every normalized tripartite state satisfies the star bound. In
particular every TP self-compatible marginal channel Phi obeys

 2 s_Phi<=2(r+t)<V+k,
 V-k<=C_{r,omega}(V-s_Phi),
 C_{r,omega}=2(r-mu)/(r-t)<2.                                   (G10)

Unitality and HS self-adjointness are not needed for this upper bound.
They are verified in the exact optimal broadcaster construction below.
There can be no ratio above 2 or divergence within the given mechanism.
The empty graph r=0 has V=k=s=0 and is treated separately as trivial.

This is an isotropic paired-edge FRAME result. It does not cover arbitrary
PSD weights mixing these observables, unequal weights on the two edge
quadratures, arbitrary irregular graphs, or the whole observable span.
It cannot by itself be promoted to a simultaneous common-EB theorem
by cone separation, since the required all-frame inequalities are absent.

5. LEGAL BROADCASTER AT THE TRUE STAR OPTIMUM

For a vertex-transitive G, let T be its adjacency and lambda=rho(H_k)>r.
The last strict inequality follows, for example, from the uniform-vector
Rayleigh quotient 2rd/(2d-1)>r on H_k. Define

 F=(lambda I-T)^(-1).

The symmetric-copy Perron vector on H_k has amplitudes x_i in each copy,
with the root counted once. Its non-root equations are lambda x_i=(Tx)_i;
its root equation is lambda x_k=2(Tx)_k. Therefore

 x=(lambda x_k/2) F e_k,  lambda F_kk=2.                         (G11)

Conversely (G11) supplies the Perron vector. The common root lambda is
the unique number >r solving it: lambda F_kk=1+sum_{n>=1}(T^n)_kk/lambda^n
decreases strictly from infinity to 1, since T has a nonzero edge at k.
The diagonal entries of F and F^2 are constant by vertex transitivity.
Put n0=2(F^2)_kk-F_kk^2>0 and X=F/sqrt(n0). Then X is real symmetric,
X_kk=a is independent of k, and

 2 sum_i X_ik^2-a^2=1.                                         (G12)

Define d explicit Kraus operators K_k:C^d -> C^d tensor C^d:

 K_k|k>=a|k,k>,
 K_k|i>=X_ik(|i,k>+|k,i>) for i!=k.                             (G13)

Here K_k^dagger K_k is diagonal, and (G12) plus X_ik=X_ki gives
sum_k K_k^dagger K_k=I. Thus B(Y)=sum_k K_k Y K_k^dagger is CPTP.
Every image lies in the BC-symmetric subspace, so its marginals coincide.

Its normalized Choi state is

 R=(1/d)sum_k |psi_k><psi_k|,
 psi_k=a|k,k,k>+sum_{i!=k}X_ik(|i,i,k>+|i,k,i>).                 (G14)

Each psi_k is normalized, lies in S_k and satisfies W psi_k=2lambda psi_k.
By (G12) and X symmetry, each single marginal of R is exactly I/d.
The off-diagonal entries of those marginals vanish directly in (G14).
This proves marginal unitality, not just input trace preservation.

HS self-adjointness also needs an independent check. The AB Choi pair has
the exact real form

 J_AB=(1/d)[sum_ij (X^2)_ij |i,i><j,j|
                   +sum_{i!=k} X_ik^2 |i,k><i,k|].              (G15)

Since X is symmetric, X_ik^2=X_ki^2. Thus F_swap J_AB F_swap=J_AB,
and its reality gives J_AB=F_swap J_AB^T F_swap. This is precisely the
HS-self-adjoint channel condition. Equivalently the channel has a real
symmetric classical population-transfer matrix and acts on E_ij, i!=j,
as multiplication by the real number (X^2)_ij. Therefore the marginal
Phi is CP, TP, unital, HS-self-adjoint and self-compatible.

Its star value is Tr(RW)=2lambda, so s_Phi=lambda. Hence the true optimum
over fully legal channels is EXACTLY lambda, not only an unconstrained
eigenvalue or a numerically symmetrized candidate. Transitivity alone
would not prove pair-swap symmetry; the symmetric-resolvent calculation
in (G15) is the essential extra legality check.

d=m omega, r=m(omega-1), mu=(omega-1)/omega, r/mu=d.
The neighbor graph Q is balanced complete (omega-1)-partite and regular
with degree nu r. Thus z above is constant, Z=t, and every remaining

 lambda=r+t,

 max_legal (V-k)/(V-s_Phi)=2(r-mu)/(r-t)<2.                      (G16)

The legal broadcaster (G13)--(G15) attains this value. For fixed omega

this paired-graph family, although no finite member exceeds it.
