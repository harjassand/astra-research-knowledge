## Rational direction acquisition

I reconstructed the net argument without a separation assumption. A real orthonormal harmonic evaluation vector through degree D has constant norm sqrt(M), M=D(D+2). The sum of squared tangential gradients is D(D+1)²(D+2)/2<(D+1)^4. Integrating along a geodesic of length at most twice the chord distance bounds evaluation displacement by 2(D+1)² delta.

For delta=2^(-14)(D+1)^(-7), perturbing each exact design node by at most delta gives mean norm at most r/32, where r=1/[256(D+1)^5]. The Gram error is at most 4(D+1)³ delta<1/4. This uses the identity `u'u'^T−uu^T=(u'−u)u'^T+u(u'−u)^T`, with both evaluation norms below D+1. Thus the perturbed set satisfies the actual acceptance tests S>1/2 I and ||b||≤r/8 with slack.

Both stereographic charts are 2-Lipschitz as maps R² to the sphere. Every sphere point has coordinates in one chart with parameter norm at most 1. With Q=ceil[16(N+2)/delta], a nearest parameter-grid point followed by one of N horizontal shifts j/Q, j=0,...,N−1, gives N distinct physical candidate points within delta of the target. All parameters remain in [-2,2]². At step i, only i−1<N previous physical points are forbidden. Hence distinct approximants exist even if the original nodes have no supplied separation bound. Each chart is injective; cross-chart duplications are removed by exact rational comparison.

The complete two-chart grid has O(Q²)=poly(N,D) points with O(log(ND))-bit coordinates. Exhaustively checking its N-element subsets takes exp(O(N log(ND))) candidates. Constructing and deduplicating the grid, and all per-candidate algebra below, take only polynomial additional work. This proves finite acquisition within the stated promise and cost; it is not an appeal to an exact-design oracle.

## Rational harmonic algebra and inverse

The stated solid harmonics are indeed polynomials: in `r^(l−m) P_l^(m)(z/r)`, the remaining r powers are even and nonnegative. Taking real and imaginary parts after multiplication by `(x+iy)^m` gives rational coefficients. Their normalized-sphere norms are

    nu_l0=1/(2l+1),
    nu_lm=(l+m)!/[2(2l+1)(l−m)!], m≥1,

with the same norm for the sine and cosine components. These are mutually orthogonal, and nu_j≥1/(2D+1). The independent fixture checks these statements exactly through degree 4 using monomial integrals over S².

Writing Q for the rational evaluation matrix and V=diag(sqrt(nu)), E=V^(-1)Q, H=QQ^T/N and S=V^(-1)HV^(-1). Thus Gram acceptance is exactly the positive-definiteness of H−diag(nu)/2, and mean acceptance is exactly sum_j b_q,j²/nu_j≤(r/8)². Rational elimination decides these in polynomial bit complexity. Rational matrix entries, determinants and inverses have polynomial bit lengths; no undecidable exact-real oracle or square-root comparison is required.

The rational-coordinate change g=Vh correctly gives

    B_q=−Q^T H^(-1) Lambda^(-1),  t=B_qg.

The order of H^(-1) and Lambda^(-1) is important and is correct. Since the derivative of C at zero is −Lambda Q/N, its product with B_q is the identity. The target coefficient conversion is also correct: if P=sum c_jq_j, then the orthonormal coefficient is p_j=c_j sqrt(nu_j), and z_j=(2l_j+1)epsilon rho^(D−l_j)p_j. Multiplying by sqrt(nu_j) produces the stated rational target zeta_j=(2l_j+1)epsilon rho^(D−l_j)c_j nu_j.

For S>1/2 I, the radial right inverse has norm at most D+1 from Euclidean h-norm to maximum radial displacement. The second-derivative estimate gives a valid Lipschitz constant 1/4 on the radius-r ball. Because ||z||≤r/4 and ||b||≤r/8, the exact iteration maps this ball into radius at most r/2. This retains enough room for every rounded iteration.

Rounding each g-coordinate on a dyadic mesh no coarser than gamma/[(D+1)(2D+1)] creates weighted norm error at most gamma: there are M<(D+1)² coordinates and 1/nu_j≤2D+1. The recurrence gives error at most 4^(−n)r+4gamma/3. With 4^(−n)r≤gamma and gamma=eta/[100(D+1)], radial error is at most 7eta/300<eta/4. Final radius rounding within eta/4 leaves total physical source error below eta/2. The dyadic resets are essential to the bit bound. At a fixed mesh, g's size is bounded by the weighted ball and the factorial-size nu_j, so its coordinates have polynomial bit length.
