# Boolean witness extraction: a quadratic-support escape and a normalization barrier

Status, 2026-10-06: a scoped randomized construction and conditional barrier are derived below. The construction is elementary; novelty is **not established**. No breakthrough or universal proof-search advance is claimed. It is useful as a precise interface test for algebraic approaches to scientific inference and proof search.

## Exact question and interface

Input is an arithmetic circuit C of size s over F2, interpreted as a **formal polynomial** f in F2[x1,...,xn], rather than merely a Boolean function. It is promised that total degree is at most a supplied D, every individual degree is at most two, and f has a Boolean nonroot. Output is a Boolean vector a with C(a)=1, or UNKNOWN after the declared randomized work budget. Output verification is deterministic circuit evaluation. No expanded coefficient list, satisfying assignment, or multilinear reduction oracle is supplied.

For the recent CNSS hardness interface, D=n and the coefficient of x1...xn is 1. This implies Boolean nonroot existence. The top-coefficient promise is supplied, not cheaply verified. Let S={i:deg_xi(f)=2}, and k=|S|. This is semantic degree of the formal polynomial after cancellation; syntactic degrees of intermediate gates need not match it.

## Complete bounded-support construction and proof

Choose q=2^m≥4 with q>(n+1)max(D,1)/δ. Arithmetic in Fq has polynomial bit cost in log q; construction of an irreducible polynomial can use standard randomized finite-field methods. For the finite implementation q=256 is fixed, so its failure probability is larger than a requested arbitrary δ would permit. That implementation is a demonstration, not the general bit-complexity implementation.

For each i write f=c0+c1 xi+c2 xi², with cj formal polynomials in the other variables. Fix α∈Fq\{0,1}. Evaluate the circuit at a uniform random assignment z to the other variables and at xi=0,1,α. Compute

    R_i(z)=f(z,α)+f(z,0)+α(f(z,1)+f(z,0)).
           =(α²+α)c2(z).

Thus nonzero R_i proves i∈S. There are no false positives. If i∈S, c2 is nonzero of total degree at most D−2, so Schwartz–Zippel bounds the false-negative probability by D/q. Define detected support S_hat by these tests. A union bound gives Pr[S_hat≠S]≤nD/q. Repeating each test r times independently improves this to at most n(D/q)^r without adding false positives. No Boolean reduction is used here; extension-field circuit access is essential.

Condition on S_hat=S. Enumerate b∈{0,1}^S. For each b choose a uniform assignment z∈Fq^{[n]\S}, and evaluate f_b(z). Each f_b is now formally multilinear. Because a Boolean nonroot exists, at least one b*=a|S gives a nonzero polynomial f_b*. For that one restriction, Pr[f_b*(z)=0]≤D/q. Consequently the probability that **all** evaluations miss is ≤D/q, without a 2^k union factor: one fixed good restriction suffices. Assignments used for this stage are independent of support detection.

When f_b(z)≠0, round every coordinate outside S to 0 or 1, preserving nonzeroness. With the other coordinates fixed, the univariate polynomial is A+B xi. A nonzero affine polynomial cannot vanish at both 0 and 1. Test 0; if it vanishes, take 1. After n−k steps all variables are Boolean and f(a)≠0. Circuit evaluation over Fq agrees with F2 evaluation on Boolean points, so f(a)=1. Reevaluate at the end and accept only a verified witness.

The success probability is at least 1−(n+1)D/q>1−δ. The algorithm makes at most 3n+2^k+2n+1 circuit evaluations (or 3rn+2^k+2n+1 for repeated support tests), each costing O(s) field operations; random coordinates add O(n2^k) sampling work. Overall bit cost is 2^k poly(n,s,log(D/δ)). If k=O(log(n+s)), this is randomized polynomial time. This is a promise-search theorem; it neither verifies CNSS promises nor decides unsatisfiability when it returns UNKNOWN.

Adversarial proof check: conditioning on correctly detecting S is valid because later randomness is independent. Testing all restrictions does not require all nonzero restrictions to be detected. Rounding is used only after fixing every formally nonlinear variable. No witness is accepted based on a PIT result alone.

## Decisive obstruction and conditional reduction

The polynomial f=x1 x2+x1²+x1 has n=2, total degree 2, individual degree ≤2, and top coefficient 1. Over any extension Fq and α∉{0,1}, f(α,0)=α²+α≠0. Yet f(0,0)=f(1,0)=0. Hence the naive multilinear step “find an extension-field nonroot and round each coordinate” fails even under the full CNSS promise. Characteristic-two ordinary differentiation also misses the x1² term; interpolation is required.

**Conditional normal-form compiler barrier.** Suppose a uniform polynomial-time procedure, given every promised CNSS circuit C in Theorem 3, outputs a polynomial-size circuit G formally computing a multilinear g agreeing with f on {0,1}^n. Then g is nonzero. Extension-field PIT and affine rounding find its Boolean nonroot in randomized polynomial time. Since f and g agree on the Boolean cube, that vector is a nonroot of f. Theorem 3 therefore implies RP=NP. The same conclusion holds for a randomized compiler that produces a valid G with constant probability on every promised input: verify its eventual Boolean witness using C, and truncate runtime on off-promise inputs in the underlying reduction.

This rules out efficient uniform **acquisition** of such a usable representation under RP≠NP; it does not rule out nonuniform existence of small normal forms, every particular family, or a representation supplied by the user. Mathematical existence of the unique multilinear Boolean reduction does not supply a small circuit or a compiler.

The k parameter is representation-sensitive and can be poor: adding Σ_i(xi²+xi) to a simple multilinear polynomial preserves every Boolean value but makes all n variables formally quadratic. Conversely, syntactically quadratic terms can cancel. Formal nonlinearity alone is not proof of hardness, and the algorithm is not generally optimal.
