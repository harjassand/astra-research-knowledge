## 1. Exact statement accepted

Let a,b>0 and let U,V be bounded self-adjoint involutions on a complex Hilbert space, with nonzero commutator. Put A=aU, B=bV and

\[
\delta=\frac12\|[U,V]\|\in(0,1],\qquad C=\frac12(UV+VU).
\]

For **independent positive scalar probability laws** ν₁,ν₂ with finite variances v₁,v₂, require a normalized POVM M satisfying, for all real s,t,

\[
\widehat M(s,t)=\widehat\nu_1(s)\widehat\nu_2(t)
\frac{e^{isA}e^{itB}+e^{itB}e^{isA}}2.
\]

The set of variance pairs for which some such ν₁,ν₂,M exist is exactly

\[
v_1>0,\quad v_2>0,\qquad v_1v_2\ge4a^2b^2\delta^2=\|[A,B]\|^2.
\]

Every positive finite pair in this region is attained by centered two-sided geometric laws on 2aℤ and 2bℤ. On the product boundary those laws, up to arbitrary coordinate translations, are the only attaining laws. The weighted minimum is 2√(w₁w₂)‖[A,B]‖ for positive finite weights.

This statement concerns positive scalar multiplier factors and a specified characteristic operator. It does not assume or construct latent simultaneous sharp outcomes of A and B, and independence of the multiplier laws does not imply independence of the final reports.

## 2. Operator positivity, including continuous spectrum

The identities

\[
C U=U C,\quad C V=V C,\quad
\frac14[U,V]^*[U,V]=I-C^2
\]

are direct algebra with U²=V²=I. For m,n∈[−1,1], write

\[
Q(m,n)=D+X,
\quad D=I+mnC\succeq0,
\quad X=mU+nV.
\]

D and X commute and

\[
D^2-X^2=(1-m^2-n^2)I+m^2n^2 C^2. \tag{A1}
\]

On the support of I−C², the polar sign R of i[U,V] is a self-adjoint unitary, commutes with C, and anticommutes with U and V. These relations follow first where I−C²≥εI and then by strong limits. Thus R(D+X)R=D−X there. On ker(I−C²), U and V commute and

\[
D\pm X=(I\pm mU)(I\pm nV)\succeq0.
\]

Consequently Q≥0 implies D±X≥0 everywhere and hence (A1)≥0. Conversely, simultaneous spectral calculus and D≥0 show that (A1)≥0 implies D≥|X| and Q≥0. Since

\[
\inf\operatorname{Spec}(C^2)=1-\delta^2,
\]

the exact criterion is

\[
Q(m,n)\succeq0
\iff f_\delta(m,n):=1-m^2-n^2+(1-\delta^2)m^2n^2\ge0. \tag{A2}
\]

No eigenvector attaining the norm or discrete block decomposition is used. If the scalar expression is negative, spectral calculus gives a negative quadratic form even when the infimum is not an eigenvalue. If it is zero, positivity is allowed; for |m|,|n|<1 the operator fails bounded invertibility. The polar symmetry ensures that a zero in the spectrum of (D+X)(D−X) cannot be assigned exclusively to D−X while D+X stays invertible.

An explicit continuous-spectrum stress case is H=L²((0,1);ℂ²),

\[
U(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
V(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x&\sqrt{1-x^2}\\\sqrt{1-x^2}&-x\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here C(x)=xI and δ=1, with the commutator norm unattained. At m=n=1/√2, Q(x) is positive definite for every x>0, while its least eigenvalue tends to zero as x↓0. The theorem correctly permits positive effects at the variance boundary. The real Laplace criterion correctly excludes that same noninvertible boundary when a scalar exponential moment is finite.

## 3. Reduction for arbitrary scalar laws

The sharp MH operator measure has four atoms

\[
T_{\epsilon\tau}=\frac14(I+\epsilon U+\tau V+\epsilon\tau C)
\quad\text{at }(\epsilon a,\tau b).
\]

For a coordinate noise ν with scale aⱼ, let ν₊,ν₋ be its translates by ±aⱼ, and set

\[
\gamma=\frac{\nu_++\nu_-}2,\quad
\xi=\frac{\nu_+-\nu_-}2,\quad
m=\frac{d\xi}{d\gamma},\quad \eta=\|m\|_\infty.
\]

These Radon–Nikodym derivatives exist for every Borel probability law, with |m|≤1. No Lebesgue density, lattice support, centering, continuity or nonsingularity is assumed. Fourier uniqueness applied to scalar matrix elements and polarization identifies the required M with T*(ν₁⊗ν₂). Its density relative to γ₁⊗γ₂ is Q(m₁(x),m₂(y)).

Density positivity has no hidden separability assumption on H: all densities lie in the finite-dimensional real span of I,U,V,C. Its cone is closed and its complement has a countable open cover by fixed-vector negative witnesses. A nonpositive density on a positive-measure set would therefore give a nonpositive integral on a measurable subset. Thus M≥0 is equivalent to Q≥0 almost everywhere even for nonseparable H.

The function fδ is nonincreasing in |m|,|n| on the square. Independence gives a product measure, so sets approaching both essential suprema have positive joint measure. Therefore

\[
M\succeq0\iff
1-\eta_1^2-\eta_2^2+(1-\delta^2)\eta_1^2\eta_2^2\ge0. \tag{A3}
\]

This is the criterion for **specified laws**. A probability measure cannot have η=0, because it would be invariant under a nonzero translation. For δ>0, (A3) excludes ηⱼ=1, so every successful independent regularizer has 0<ηⱼ<1.

For L=2aⱼ and r=(1−η)/(1+η), the contrast bound is equivalent to

\[
r\nu(E)\le\nu(E+L)\le r^{-1}\nu(E)
\quad\text{for every Borel }E. \tag{A4}
\]

The inequalities are two-sided domination at the **one fixed shift** L. They are not the usual continuous sensitivity constraint at every shift of magnitude at most L.

## 4. Full variance lemma and common-residue audit

Suppose ν satisfies (A4), with 0<r<1 and L>0. If its second moment is infinite the lower bound is automatic. Otherwise take c=EνX and write every x uniquely as

\[
x=c+f+kL,\qquad -L/2\le f<L/2,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Let σ be the induced probability measure of the residue f and let pₖ(f) be the conditional sequence, obtained by Radon–Nikodym derivatives of the interval measures. Countably many null sets can be removed so that, at each remaining f, all adjacent inequalities hold and ∑ₖpₖ=1. Define

\[
e_j^+=p_j-rp_{j-1}\ge0,\quad
e_j^-=p_{-j}-rp_{-j+1}\ge0\quad(j\ge1).
\]

For k≥1,

\[
p_k=p_0r^k+\sum_{j=1}^k e_j^+r^{k-j},
\]

and the analogous identity holds on the negative side. This decomposes the fiber into a nonnegative sum of a two-sided geometric kernel centered at f and one-sided geometric kernels starting at f±jL. Their unnormalized masses sum to one, so normalization gives a genuine mixture. Tonelli permits exchanging all sums and second-moment integrals without subtracting divergent terms.

Put V*=2L²r/(1−r)². The normalized two-sided component has second moment about the global mean c equal to V*+f². A positive one-sided component is f+jL+LG, with P(G=k)=(1−r)rᵏ for k≥0. If t=f+jL≥L/2, then

\[
E(t+LG)^2
=\left(t+\frac{Lr}{1-r}\right)^2+\frac{L^2r}{(1-r)^2}
\ge V_*+\frac{L^2}4. \tag{A5}
\]

The negative-side components obey the same inequality because f−jL≤−L/2. It follows that

\[
\operatorname{Var}\nu\ge\frac{2L^2r}{(1-r)^2}. \tag{A6}
\]

Equality eliminates every one-sided residual and forces f=0 almost surely. The remaining fiber is exactly

\[
\nu\{c+kL\}=\frac{1-r}{1+r}r^{|k|}.
\]

This addresses the common-residue problem correctly. One cannot silently center each conditional lattice law and then ignore the variation of conditional means; the proof measures every component about the **same global mean**. Singular, absolutely continuous and mixed residue measures are included. An explicit singular stress case is geometric lattice noise plus an independent centered non-atomic Cantor law supported in a sufficiently short interval: convolution preserves (A4) and adds exactly the Cantor variance. An absolutely continuous law cannot attain equality, because equality forces one lattice coset. Continuous laws can approach the optimum by smoothing a geometric lattice law with an independent centered density of arbitrarily small compact support; convolution preserves (A4), and the variance increases by the smoothing variance. There is therefore an infimum but no minimum under a density-only restriction.

## 5. Variance theorem and equality

With L=2aⱼ and r=(1−ηⱼ)/(1+ηⱼ), (A6) becomes

\[
v_j\ge2a_j^2(\eta_j^{-2}-1).
\]

Writing xⱼ=ηⱼ⁻²−1>0, criterion (A3) is exactly x₁x₂≥δ². Multiplication proves

\[
v_1v_2\ge4a^2b^2\delta^2.
\]

Conversely, given any positive finite v₁,v₂ satisfying that bound, choose

\[
\eta_j=(1+v_j/(2a_j^2))^{-1/2},\quad
r_j=(1-\eta_j)/(1+\eta_j),
\]

and choose the two independent laws

\[
\nu_j\{2a_jk\}=\frac{1-r_j}{1+r_j}r_j^{|k|}.
\]

Their contrasts have magnitude ηⱼ, their variances are exactly vⱼ, and (A3) holds. This proves all feasible pairs are attained without compactness, a finite LP, or limiting attainment. On the product boundary, all positive factors in both inequalities must be equalities; (A6) classifies each law up to translation. The weighted minimum then follows from arithmetic–geometric mean, with v₁=‖[A,B]‖√(w₂/w₁) and v₂=‖[A,B]‖√(w₁/w₂).

The assumption [U,V]≠0 must be retained. If [U,V]=0, T is already a positive joint spectral measure, every positive noise law works, and the variance region is all v₁,v₂≥0. Zero variances are attained by point masses. The same degenerate conclusion applies if a=0 or b=0. Formula v₁v₂≥0 alone is compatible with this extension, but the positive-coordinate and unique-geometric-boundary assertions are not.

## 6. Explicit countermodel to a variance-only law criterion

Take A=σz, B=σx, so ‖[A,B]‖²=4. Let ν₁ and ν₂ be independent centered Gaussians, each of variance 3. Their variance product is 9>4, but

\[
m_j(x)=\tanh(x/3),\qquad \eta_j=1.
\]

At x=y=(3/2)log7, both contrasts equal 3/4 and

\[
Q=I+\tfrac34\sigma_z+\tfrac34\sigma_x
=\begin{pmatrix}7/4&3/4\\3/4&1/4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det Q=-1/8.
\]

Both scalar dominating densities are strictly positive, and the negative determinant persists on an open neighborhood. The resulting operator measure is not positive. This is an analytic countermodel, not a floating-point failure. More generally, any two nondegenerate Gaussians have η₁=η₂=1 and fail for every δ>0, at any widths. Compactly supported noises also have η=1: otherwise mutual domination of the translates would force nonzero mass arbitrarily far along every occupied translation orbit.

The proof's use of independence is equally specific. Under a general dependent ν on ℝ², the translated measures need not yield a product reference measure with contrasts m₁(x),m₂(y). Neither the simultaneous-essential-supremum step nor the product variance lower bound has been proved for that setting. This audit does not infer either an improvement or a counterexample in the dependent setting.

## 7. Exponential domains and strict boundary

A nonzero bounded interval E of length smaller than L exists with ν(E)>0. The sets E+kL are disjoint and (A4) gives ν(E+kL)≥r^{|k|}ν(E). Thus at s≥−logr/L the positive-tail moment diverges, and at s≤logr/L the negative-tail moment diverges. Consequently

\[
\mathcal D(\nu_j)\subset(-\alpha_j,\alpha_j),\quad
\alpha_j=\operatorname{artanh}(\eta_j)/a_j,
\]

including divergence at both displayed endpoints. Heavier tails can give a smaller domain; (A4) supplies an upper bound on the domain, not a lower bound. A variance-optimal geometric law has exactly the displayed open domain and

\[
E e^{sZ_j}=
\frac{(1-r_j)^2}{1-2r_j\cosh(2a_js)+r_j^2}.
\]

For **finite positive** target half-widths α,β, exact independent rectangular moment-domain attainability is therefore equivalent to

\[
\delta\sinh(a\alpha)\sinh(b\beta)\le1.
\]

Sufficiency uses η₁=tanh(aα), η₂=tanh(bβ) and the geometric laws. Necessity follows because finite moments throughout the requested rectangle force η₁≥tanh(aα) and η₂≥tanh(bβ), which must satisfy (A3). At a saturated rectangle strict monotonicity in ηⱼ forces equality in these two contrast bounds, and then

\[
v_1\ge2a^2\operatorname{csch}^2(a\alpha),\quad
v_2\ge2b^2\operatorname{csch}^2(b\beta).
\]

Those coordinate bounds are simultaneously attained. The open rectangle can touch the Laplace obstruction at its excluded corners; there is no claim of finite corner moments. An infinite half-width and a positive other half-width cannot occur for δ>0.

For arbitrary dependent scalar ν, direct integration of the finite four-atom convolution gives

\[
\int e^{sx+ty}M(dx,dy)=m_\nu(s,t)H(s,t)
\]

whenever that scalar moment is finite. There is no analytic-continuation issue. Also M(BR)→I in operator norm, because each of the four scalar translated tail masses vanishes. For a finite R with M(BR)≥I/2, the Laplace integral is at least e^{−R|(s,t)|}I/2. Hence H(s,t) must be boundedly invertible positive, not merely positive on every vector. By (A2), its exact region is

\[
\mathcal P_\delta=\{(s,t):\delta|\sinh(as)\sinh(bt)|<1\}.
\]

## 9. Measurement information and charged reporting cost

For the geometric construction the reports can be represented as

\[
X_1=\epsilon a(2K_1+1),\quad X_2=\tau b(2K_2+1),
\]

where the sign pair is measured with the four known effects

\[
F_{\epsilon\tau}=\frac14(I+\epsilon\eta_1U+\tau\eta_2V+
\epsilon\tau\eta_1\eta_2C)
\]

and the two Kⱼ are independent geometric variables with probabilities (1−rⱼ)rⱼᵏ, independent of the state and sign pair. Taking signs is a deterministic kernel M→F. Adding those state-independent magnitudes is a kernel F→M. The two experiments are therefore exactly equivalent for all states and all decision problems allowing arbitrary classical processing. The magnitudes contain no extra quantum information.

Conditioning the coordinate report on signs yields εa/η₁ and τb/η₂. Both coordinate estimators are unbiased for the corresponding quantum expectation. Their variances are respectively

\[
a_j^2+v_j-\langle A_j\rangle_\rho^2,
\qquad a_j^2+v_j/2-\langle A_j\rangle_\rho^2.
\]

Thus the added reporting variance halves; the whole state-dependent variance need not halve. At the unit orthogonal Pauli equal optimum, second moments are 3 and 2. At the maximally mixed state the ratio of copy counts needed by these particular coordinate sample means for the same MSE is 3/2. This is not a universal ratio for optimal state estimation or all tasks. In fact full reports can be processed to recover the better decoder immediately.

An exact realization of this reporting contract has unbounded output support and two classical draws per quantum measurement. E Kⱼ=rⱼ/(1−rⱼ); the entropy of Kⱼ in nats is −log(1−rⱼ)−[rⱼ/(1−rⱼ)]logrⱼ. These quantities distinguish expected classical resources from the infinite worst-case output range. They do not supply a finite-bit exact sampler for arbitrary real parameters. Exact optimal parameter selection also assumes access to a,b and the commutator norm; the theorem gives no algorithm for acquiring or certifying an arbitrary infinite-dimensional norm. The four-effect POVM exists mathematically; no calibrated physical realization, preparation cost, readout error, reset cost or energy accounting has been acquired.

Clipping Kⱼ at Nⱼ gives at most 4(N₁+1)(N₂+1) reports and maximum coordinate ranges aⱼ(2Nⱼ+1). A same-randomness coupling gives the author's uniform TV upper bound \(r_1^{N_1+1}+r_2^{N_2+1}\). For |s|≤β₁<α₁ and |t|≤β₂<α₂, the exponential difference vanishes off the clipping event and is bounded on that event by e^{β₁|X₁|+β₂|X₂|}. Summing positive F over signs yields I, with no dimension factor, and independent geometric tails give exactly the displayed \(D_1D_2(q_1^{N_1+1}+q_2^{N_2+1})\) bound. The choice of Nⱼ follows by taking logarithms. These are constructive upper bounds for a selected approximation, not optimality bounds on all approximations. Clipping preserves the signs and hence the quantum information, but destroys the exact scalar-convolution characteristic identity.
