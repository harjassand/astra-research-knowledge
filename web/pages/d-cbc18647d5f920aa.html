## Matched binary-norm control on the same oracle

Keep exactly the input and parameterized oracle of our theorem. Keep
the same weighted inner-q pool and the same bad-characteristic and
degree accounting. For a fixed good q,v, use the identical nonzero
integer coefficient witness C_v, and R=Res(Phi_q,C_v) with

    1<=|R|<=(kw)^(q-1).

The traditional binary treatment declares every p|R wholly bad,
irrespective of how many primitive roots actually vanish. For distinct
primes and l_p=floor(log2 p),

    sum_(p|R) l_p <= log2|R| <= (q-1)B,
    Pr[wholly bad p] <= (q-1)B/T_p,  B=ceil(log2(kw)).

Thus a same-oracle one-query control has false acceptance bounded by

    Lb/T_q + dk^2/q_min + d/p_min + (q-1)B/T_p(q),

with the last term maximized over used q. Buy capacity T_p(q)>=6(q-1)B
and keep our other admissions. It achieves the same <1/2 soundness.
The acquisition lemma still costs polynomial in numerical d,k,log w:
q is already polynomial in those parameters, so multiplying capacity
by q-1 does not change polynomiality. Both constructions consequently
have the same logarithmic field-width asymptotic. The weighted modular
root theorem saves a **factor q-1 in sufficient outer capacity**; it is
not the first polynomial-parameter or logarithmic-height-width tester.

For the frozen prototype, selected q=7919 and B=258. Our required
capacity is1548; this binary control requires12,257,064. This is an
exact capacity comparison, not an executed old-control timing, prime
count or GPU speedup. The capacity translates into differing acquisition
requirements but cannot alone prove an end-to-end benefit.

The June good-q test is another strong control and has vanishing error
under its actual assumptions. On our synthetic w=2^256,k=4 input, its
displayed q>2kw condition requires at least260 q bits. That field-width
comparison is valid but does not subsume the stronger matched binary
control above. The original Li–Wu fixed-field test provides a further
control when switching characteristics is unavailable.

## Explicit coefficients and actual application costs

When the condensed f_j are supplied explicitly and distinct fractions
are promised, Lindemann–Weierstrass already says P=0 iff every f_j=0.
Checking their coefficient lists decides the problem deterministically.
Our toy has explicit linear coefficient pairs in its provider; its
first constant coefficient is1. This structural control immediately
certifies nonidentity on that supplied toy and dominates prime acquisition.
The toy demonstrates oracle/tester conformance and acquisition costs;
it is not evidence of advantage over the best available information.

For arbitrary sparse-CIT input, the older deterministic NC vanishing-space
method is even stronger than our randomized reduction. It needs supplied
coefficients; it cannot be relabelled as an Eval-only black-box algorithm.
Likewise we cannot hide coefficients from a control if the actual
application input exposes a usable condensed description.

Mirage supplies a real downstream task: rejecting inequivalent candidate
one-exponential-layer tensor fragments during superoptimization. To
demonstrate benefit there, a frozen actual fragment must admit certified
width/degree/height and field-parameter evaluation; source quotient
denominators must be handled; all scalar operations and input constants
must be charged. Fragment partitioning, sparse expansion and mixed-field
compilation are not free. Ordinary small-coefficient softmax fragments
may already satisfy the source's height condition with small fields, so
the present large-height toy should not stand in for those workloads.
