Given a proposed physical model, what additional experiments suffice to certify its long-time predictions without identifying the complete microscopic generator?
There is already substantial work showing that Hamiltonian certification can be cheaper than Hamiltonian learning, principally using real-time evolution access. The theorem below addresses a different interface: short, programmable imaginary-time filtering, with its failures charged.
Let \(H\) be an unknown Hamiltonian and \(K\) a known proposed Hamiltonian. Assume a supplied common spectral bound
\[ 0\preceq H,K\preceq MI. \]
Let \(\psi\) be a known, preparable pure initial state. Define the predictive error
\[ \mathcal E_T(H,K;\psi) = \sup_{0\le t\le T} D\!\left( e^{-itH}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|e^{itH}, e^{-itK}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|e^{itK} \right), \]
where \(D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1\).
The experimental device supplies a heralded filter whose success operator is
\[ B_h=e^{-hH}. \]
It acts on a submitted system state, including a state entangled with an auxiliary qubit. A success returns the normalized filtered state. Every failure counts as an experimental attempt.
The upper bound needs only the single duration
\[ \boxed{h=\frac{1}{4M}.} \]
The experimenter must also implement the candidate-dependent preparations and comparison measurements specified below. No matrix entries of \(H\), its eigenvectors, or its real-time evolution are supplied.
There is an explicit experiment with mean rejection probability \(\bar p\) satisfying
\[ \boxed{ \mathcal E_T(H,K;\psi) \le \min\!\left\{1,\;28MT\sqrt{\bar p}\right\}. } \tag{1} \]
Consequently, after \(N\) independent successful validation shots, zero rejections give the confidence certificate
\[ \boxed{ \mathcal E_T \le 28MT\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{N}}, } \tag{2} \]
with soundness error at most \(\alpha\).
The statement is simultaneous for the entire interval \(0\le t\le T\). A trace-distance certificate of \(\varepsilon\) therefore bounds the error in every norm-one observable by \(2\varepsilon\), on this initial state.
At constant confidence, the optimal worst-case resource orders are
\[ \boxed{ Q(M,T,\varepsilon) = \Theta\!\left(\frac{M^2T^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right) } \tag{3} \]
for raw filter attempts, and
\[ \boxed{ S(M,T,\varepsilon) = \Theta\!\left(\frac{MT^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right) } \tag{4} \]
for total duration of the unknown-system filters.
These are not total computational-runtime bounds. They apply to the promise problem of accepting exact predictive agreement and rejecting trajectory error at least \(\varepsilon\). The lower bounds hold already for a two-level system.
No locality, spectral gap, low entanglement, or commutation assumption is needed.
Write
\[ B=e^{-hH},\qquad C=e^{-hK},\qquad a=e^{-1/4},\qquad b=a^2. \]
Thus
\[ aI\preceq B,C\preceq I. \]
For \(r\in[0,1]\), construct the known positive operator
\[ G_r= \left[(1-r)I+\frac{r}{b}C\right]^{-1}. \tag{5} \]
Its eigenvalues lie in \([b,1]\). The inverse is therefore uniformly well-conditioned.
Choose \(s\) uniformly from \([0,T]\) and \(r\) uniformly from \([0,1]\). Prepare
\[ v_{s,r} = \frac{G_r e^{-isK}\psi} {\|G_r e^{-isK}\psi\|}. \tag{6} \]
This is a state from the proposed trajectory, modified by a bounded positive function of the proposed model.
Now use one auxiliary qubit to prepare
\[ |\xi_{s,r}\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle|v_{s,r}\rangle+       |1\rangle|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \tag{7} \]
Apply the same unknown filter \(B\) to the system in both branches. Upon success, the output is
\[ |\Xi_H(v)\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle Bv+|1\rangle B\psi} {\sqrt{\|Bv\|^2+\|B\psi\|^2}}. \tag{8} \]
Compare this with the known candidate state
\[ |\Xi_K(v)\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle Cv+|1\rangle C\psi} {\sqrt{\|Cv\|^2+\|C\psi\|^2}}, \tag{9} \]
by measuring the projector onto \(|\Xi_K(v)\rangle\). The orthogonal outcome is a rejection.
Neither branch is an inert reference. Both experience the unknown Hamiltonian’s filter. The auxiliary qubit preserves coherence between two prepared inputs; it does not bypass \(H\).
The success probability of each filter attempt is at least
\[ a^2=e^{-1/2}>0.60. \]
Therefore, this procedure does not conceal an exponentially rare postselection event.
For the stated sampling law, one samples \((s,r)\) and retries that same setting until success. Resampling after every failure would bias the distribution of successful settings.
The proof has three substantive steps.
Let \(p(v)\) be the rejection probability in the comparison above. Define the positive scalar
\[ c=\frac{\langle\psi,B\psi\rangle}         {\langle\psi,C\psi\rangle}, \qquad a\le c\le a^{-1}. \]
This scalar is used in the proof; it need not be estimated.
I obtain
\[ \boxed{ \|(B/c-C)v\| \le L\sqrt{p(v)}, \qquad L=\frac{\sqrt2(1+a^{-1})}{a}. } \tag{10} \]
To see this, let
\[ X=(Bv,B\psi),\qquad Y=(Cv,C\psi), \]
and let \(z\) be the least-squares scalar minimizing \(\|X-zY\|\). The pure-state projection formula gives
\[ \|X-zY\|^2=\|X\|^2p(v)\le2p(v). \]
In particular,
\[ \|B\psi-zC\psi\|\le\sqrt{2p(v)}. \]
Taking its inner product with \(\psi\), and using positivity,
\[ |z-c| \le \frac{\sqrt{2p(v)}}{a}. \]
Combining this with the other component’s residual and dividing by \(c\ge a\) proves (10).
This is the reason for the paired probe: a different unknown scalar cannot independently explain every filtered output.
For positive definite matrices \(P,Q\),
\[ \log P-\log Q = \int_0^\infty (P+uI)^{-1}(P-Q)(Q+uI)^{-1}\,du. \tag{11} \]
This follows from the scalar integral representation of the logarithm and the resolvent identity. The order of the factors matters; no commutation is assumed.
Apply it with \(P=B/c\) and \(Q=C\), and substitute
\[ u=\frac{b(1-r)}r. \]
Then
\[ \log(B/c)-\log C = \frac1b\int_0^1 F_r(B/c-C)G_r\,dr, \tag{12} \]
where
\[ F_r= \left[(1-r)I+\frac rb(B/c)\right]^{-1}, \qquad \|F_r\|\le1. \]
The unknown \(F_r\) is never implemented. Only its norm is bounded.
Define
\[ \widetilde H=H+\frac{\log c}{h}I. \]
This differs from \(H\) only by an unobservable common energy shift. Equations (10)–(12) imply
\[ \|(\widetilde H-K)e^{-isK}\psi\| \le \frac{L}{hb}\int_0^1\sqrt{p(s,r)}\,dr. \tag{13} \]
This is the interface bridge: short filtering experiments bound the generator’s action on the proposed trajectory, without reconstructing the generator.
Duhamel’s identity gives
\[ (e^{-it\widetilde H}-e^{-itK})\psi = -i\int_0^t e^{-i(t-s)\widetilde H} (\widetilde H-K)e^{-isK}\psi\,ds. \]
Using (13), extending a nonnegative integral to \(T\), and applying Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[ \sup_{0\le t\le T} \|(e^{-it\widetilde H}-e^{-itK})\psi\| \le \frac{LT}{hb}\sqrt{\bar p}. \]
For the chosen constants,
\[ \frac{L}{hb} = 27.35248\ldots\,M < 28M. \]
Pure-state trace distance is bounded by this vector distance, and the scalar shift from \(H\) to \(\widetilde H\) does not change density matrices. This proves (1).
Finally, a model with rejection probability \(\bar p\) passes \(N\) independent shots with probability
\[ (1-\bar p)^N\le e^{-N\bar p}. \]
That proves the confidence statement and the quadratic upper bound.

For every \(T>0\), the following statements are equivalent:
