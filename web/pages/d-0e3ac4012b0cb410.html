## Existing moving-front proof

For g=Gs>0 and positive b,E<Q, define

    I_b=8 pi s g integral_E^Q J0(bq) sin(2g log(q/E)) dq/q,
    z=clip(g log(1/(bE)),0,g log(Q/E)),
    F_b=4 pi s[1-cos(2z)]>=0.

Independently reconstructing the angular integral gives |J0|<=1 and |J0(x)-1|<=x²/4. Splitting the angular integral at delta=x^-1/2 and integrating the central part by parts gives |J0(x)|<=3/sqrt(x) for x>=1. Thus

    integral_0^infinity |J0(x)-1_{x<1}| dx/x<=1/8+6=49/8,
    |I_b-F_b|<=49 pi s g=49 pi Gs².

The clipping of z handles 1/b<=E, E<1/b<Q and 1/b>=Q correctly; all positive parameters are admitted. There is no unspoken fixed-b assumption. The exponentially moving bQ=exp(a/g) front follows directly. Its export to finite-l Legendre partial waves remains a separate problem. Multiplying by s^-3 and integrating this envelope alone produces a logarithmically divergent high-energy upper bound; r13 correctly distinguished that fact from divergence of the actual integral.

## New complete bound for every truncated Mellin interval

**Theorem.** For every g>=1 and 0<A<B<infinity,

    g |integral_A^B J0(x) x^{2ig} dx/x|<=100.       (M)

No Mellin integral extending to zero is taken: x^{2ig} has no convergent endpoint primitive there. Every interval in (M) is finite, and its bound is uniform. The standard Bessel equation is checked against NIST DLMF10.2.1. All additional estimates below are reconstructed explicitly, so no unquantified large-x asymptotic is required.

Put t=2g>=2 and y=log x, f(y)=J0(e^y). The Bessel equation gives f''=-x²f. Let

    N(y)=exp(ity)[f'(y)-it f(y)], R(y)=t²-x².

The exact identity N'=R exp(ity)f implies on either nonresonant region

    integral exp(ity)f dy=[N/R]-integral N (1/R)' dy. (W)

### Low region x<=t/2

The angular integral and its derivative give |J0|<=1, |J0'|<=1; therefore |N|<=x+t<=3t/2. Also R>=3t²/4. Each endpoint ratio is at most 2/t. The total variation of 1/R over this region is at most 1/(3t²), so (W) bounds any low subinterval by

    4/t+(3t/2)/(3t²)=9/(2t).

Multiplication by g=t/2 gives at most 9/4. This bound includes arbitrarily small A without replacing the truncated integral by an invalid full Mellin transform.

### Quantified oscillatory representation for x>=1

Set H(x)=sqrt(x)J0(x). Direct substitution gives

    H''+[1+1/(4x²)]H=0.

Let alpha=H cos x-H' sin x and beta=H sin x+H' cos x. Then

    alpha'=H sin x/(4x²), beta'=-H cos x/(4x²),
    sqrt(alpha²+beta²)=sqrt(H²+H'²).

At x=1, |H|<=1 and |H'|<=3/2. Gronwall applied to the coefficient-vector norm gives

    ||(alpha,beta)||<=sqrt(13/4)exp(1/4)<3,
    |H|,|H'|<=3.

The derivatives are absolutely integrable, so coefficients alpha_infinity,beta_infinity exist and have norm<=3. Their difference from the finite-x coefficients is at most 3/(4x). Hence, for x>=1,

    J0(x)=x^-1/2[alpha_infinity cos x+beta_infinity sin x]+r(x),
    |r(x)|<=3/(4x^{3/2}).                         (B)

This proves the required remainder, including its constant, instead of importing an asymptotic formula outside its range of validity.

### High region x>=2t

The same H bounds give |f|<=3/sqrt(x) and |f'|=|sqrt(x)H'-H/(2sqrt(x))|<=9sqrt(x)/2. Thus |N|<=6sqrt(x) for x>=2t. Here |R|>=3x²/4 and |(1/R)'|<=32/(9x²), where the derivative is with respect to y. Formula(W) bounds any high subinterval by

    16(2t)^-3/2+(64/3) integral_(log 2t)^infinity exp(-3y/2)dy
      <31(2t)^-3/2.

After multiplying by g=t/2 this is less than 4 for t>=2.

### Resonant slab t/2<=x<=2t

For phi_plus/minus(x)=t log x +/- x, |phi''|=t/x²>=1/(4t), with one sign throughout. An elementary second-derivative estimate is enough: split any interval where |phi'|<=sqrt(lambda) and its at most two outer pieces. The central piece has length<=2/sqrt(lambda). Integration by parts on each monotone outer piece costs at most 3/sqrt(lambda), giving

    |integral exp(i phi)dx|<=8/sqrt(lambda)<=16sqrt(t)

for every subinterval. This also handles intervals clipped on either side of the stationary point. Abel integration with the decreasing amplitude x^-3/2 then gives

    |integral_alpha^beta x^-3/2 exp(i phi)dx|
       <=16sqrt(t) alpha^-3/2<=16*2^{3/2}/t.

The two complex coefficients of the cosine/sine part in(B) have total modulus<=3. Its contribution, multiplied by g, is therefore <=24*2^{3/2}<68. The remainder contributes at most

    (t/2)(3/4) integral_(t/2)^infinity x^-5/2 dx

Summing low, middle and high pieces gives <9/4+68+1/2+4<75<100. Empty pieces are omitted, and any original interval is partitioned into at most these three pieces. This proves(M) for **every** positive A<B, with no relation among the physical cuts and g.

## Consequence: the leading-model twice-subtracted UV budget closes

Use A=bE,B=bQ; the extra phase (bE)^-2ig has unit modulus. Equation(M) gives |I_b|<=800 pi s at g>=1. Since 0<=F_b<=8 pi s,

    |I_b-F_b|<=808 pi s                 for g>=1,
    |I_b-F_b|<=pi s min(49Gs,808)       for all g>0.

Consequently the mathematical leading-sector difference has an absolutely integrable s^-3 envelope over s>=M². A simple matched bound is

    integral_(M²)^infinity |I_b(s)-F_b(s)| ds/s³
      <=49 pi G log_+(1/(GM²))+808 pi min(G,1/M²). (UV)

The front and detector parameters may depend measurably on s: uniformity of (M) preserves this conclusion. For fixed M, (UV) is O(G log(1/G)) as G->0. Matching the minimum envelope instead at s=808/(49G) gives the slightly smaller explicit bound 49 pi G[1+log(808/(49GM²))] when GM²<=808/49, and 808 pi/M² otherwise. No optimal constant is claimed.

In particular 808 pi/s² dominates the weighted front error for every G>0, while the pointwise error tends to zero as G->0 by the old49 pi Gs² estimate. Dominated convergence of this specified leading-model difference is therefore now justified, even with detector cuts and impact parameters varying with G and s. This repairs the earlier failed envelope argument at precisely its model interface.

The physical paper's detector amplitude, real radiation, recoil, full crossing and Bessel-to-Legendre replacement are not supplied by this audit.
