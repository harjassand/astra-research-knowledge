# Finite-resolution gravity positivity: reconstructed eikonal control and exact soft-channel obstruction

Status 2026-10-06: proved mathematical lemmas for explicitly defined kernels and finite-dimensional scattering maps. No universal QFT/gravity theorem, new UV-completion bound, or novelty claim. Eight primary papers (ten recoverable versions) have been read at the locations recorded in sources/metadata.json and literature.md.

## Exact scientific interface

The consequential question is whether the detector-limit positivity construction of Bellazzini et al., arXiv:2512.13780v2, can be extended to a **certified bound at finite coupling and finite detector resolution** in four-dimensional gravity. Such a result could constrain higher-derivative EFT coefficients without hiding the remainder in O(G), and would improve subsequent amplitude-bootstrap research.

Input boundary: supplied leading eikonal kernel, detector cut E>0, momentum-transfer upper scale Q>E, center-of-mass s>0, G>0, and a specified smearing/impact parameter. The proofs below do not acquire a full UV amplitude from this information. A full theorem also needs a uniform estimate for the difference between the exact stripped amplitude and this leading kernel, and sufficient high-energy behavior to justify the dispersion contour. Those are missing.

### Normalization audit

Use natural units. Physical four-scalar gravitational tree amplitudes scale as 8 pi G s^2/q^2, which is dimensionless. The phase variable is g=Gs. Eq. (3.11) of 2512.13780v2 has this s^2 normalization. Its displayed Eq. (4.12) instead has 8 pi Gs/q^2, which has mass dimension -2 unless a further 1/s normalization is intended; no such change is explicit in the surrounding text. Our kernel uses the dimensionally consistent normalization read from Eq. (3.11). This is a scope declaration, not a claim to have repaired the full paper.

## Lemma 1: fixed smooth smearing and the forward defect

Let g>0, L=log(Q/E), kappa=gL, h:[0,Q]->R, and

F_g[h] = 8 pi s g integral_E^Q [h(q)/q] sin(2g log(q/E)) dq.

For h=1 direct integration gives

F_g[1] = 4 pi s [1-cos(2kappa)].

If |h(q)-h(0)| <= H q^nu, nu>0, then

|F_g[h] - 4 pi s h(0)[1-cos(2kappa)]|
 <= 8 pi s g H (Q^nu-E^nu)/nu.

Proof: subtract the constant test function, take absolute values, use |sin|<=1, and integrate q^(nu-1). This is uniform in E, including E=Q exp(-kappa/g), but H must be independent of g.

Thus the pointwise kernel tends to zero at every fixed q>0 as g->0 at fixed kappa, while its smearing has a nonzero endpoint contribution. Interchanging the limit and the integral loses it. The noncommutation is already identified in the source; the displayed bound is our independently reconstructed quantitative version.

For q^p smearing, q^p times the physical absorptive kernel has integrand q^(p-2). The logarithmically marginal case is p=1. For p>1 the integral is O(g) and tends to zero. For p<1 there is generally exponential enhancement from q~E; writing q=E exp(v) gives an E^(p-1) integral of exp((p-1)v)sin(2gv), which has leading size g^2 E^(p-1) for fixed p<1, not a finite universal endpoint mass. Special parameter cancellations do not establish a general limit.

## Lemma 2: a uniform moving impact-parameter front

Define the mathematical eikonal/Bessel model

I_b = 8 pi s g integral_E^Q (dq/q) J_0(bq) sin(2g log(q/E)), b>0.

This is the leading small-angle impact-parameter kernel. It is not an exact identification with finite-l Legendre partial waves, nor an exact full gravitational amplitude. Let

z = clip(g log(1/(bE)), 0, kappa).

Then, for all positive g,s,b,E,Q with E<Q,

|I_b - 4 pi s [1-cos(2z)]| <= 49 pi s g.                    (T2)

In particular, I_b >= -49 pi G s^2. The constant is deliberately loose; its uniformity is the useful part.

### Complete proof

We first establish a Bessel bound rather than importing an asymptotic assumption. From the angular integral

J_0(x)=(1/pi) integral_0^pi cos(x cos theta) dtheta,

|J_0(x)|<=1 for all real x. Also

|J_0(x)-1| <= x^2/4

by 1-cos y <= y^2/2 and integral cos^2 theta dtheta=pi/2.

For x>=1 choose delta=x^(-1/2)<=1. Bound the two endpoint pieces of the complex exponential angular integral by 2delta. On [delta,pi-delta], integrate by parts using phase derivative -x sin theta. The boundary terms have total magnitude at most 2/(x sin delta). The derivative of 1/sin theta contributes at most 2/(x sin delta)-2/x. Therefore

|J_0(x)| <= [2delta+4/(x sin delta)]/pi
 <= [2+4/sin(1)]/(pi sqrt(x)) < 3/sqrt(x).

Here sin delta >= delta sin(1) follows from concavity of sin on [0,1]. This yields an integrable logarithmic comparison:

C_J = integral_0^infinity |J_0(x)-1_{x<1}| dx/x
 <= integral_0^1 x/4 dx + integral_1^infinity 3x^(-3/2) dx
 = 1/8+6=49/8.

Replace J_0(bq) in I_b by the sharp step 1_{bq<1}. Its integral is exactly 4 pi s[1-cos(2z)] because the integration endpoint is min(Q,1/b), unless 1/b<E when it vanishes. The absolute replacement error is

8 pi s g integral_E^Q |J_0(bq)-1_{bq<1}| dq/q
 <=8 pi s g C_J <=49 pi s g.

This proves (T2), with no ordering assumption on the infrared cut and impact parameter.

### Nonuniformity that matters

Take E_g=Q exp(-kappa/g) and b_g Q=exp(a/g), a fixed real number. Then z=clip(kappa-a,0,kappa) and (T2) proves

I_b/s -> 4 pi [1-cos(2 clip(kappa-a,0,kappa))].

For every fixed b, a_g=glog(bQ)->0, giving the ordinary endpoint result. Exponentially large b retains a different front. This is a precise failure of uniform use of the fixed-smearing limit, rather than a counterexample to the authors' exact detector-limit statement. The Bessel-to-Legendre error must be bounded separately before exporting (T2) to all actual partial waves.

## What this does and does not buy for dispersion bounds

On a finite energy interval [M^2,S], an s^-3-weighted error integral from (T2) is at most

49 pi G integral_(M^2)^S ds/s = 49 pi G log(S/M^2).

It is finite and explicit. On [M^2,infinity) this envelope is not integrable. **A divergent envelope is not evidence that the actual error diverges**, and it does not refute the paper's dispersion result. It says exactly that (T2), by itself, cannot be the dominated-convergence certificate for a twice-subtracted all-energy finite-resolution bound. For an s^(-3-n) weight with n>0, the envelope integrates to 49 pi G/[n(M^2)^n]; extra subtractions make this particular obstruction disappear, but they probe different Wilson coefficients and do not settle real-radiation errors.

A load-bearing possible repair is a uniform incomplete-Mellin estimate

sup_(b,E,Q) |g integral_E^Q J_0(bq) exp(2iglog(q/E)) dq/q| <= C for g>=1,

or a physical high-energy estimate that implies an O(s) envelope. Combined with (T2) below s~1/G, this would give a finite matched error budget of order G log(1/G) for this leading model. This estimate is **not proved here**. Full Mellin transform identities do not automatically control every truncated interval. A second repair would use a physical UV assumption, such as a quantitatively controlled tree-level meromorphic completion, rather than assert universality.

## Lemma 3: Coulomb-relative matrix contractivity, with the correct sign

Assume a flux-normalized elastic channel block S on C^N satisfies S^dagger S<=I, and a unitary long-range reference matrix C. Define A=(S-C)/i and B=C^dagger A. Then

S=C(I+iB),
I-S^dagger S = 2 Im_H(B)-B^dagger B,
Im_H(B)=(B-B^dagger)/(2i).

Consequently contractivity is equivalent to

2 Im_H(C^dagger A)-A^dagger A >=0,

and also to the Schur constraint [[I,I+iB],[(I+iB)^dagger,I]]>=0. The latter is convex in the entries of B; it is the standard contraction SDP written in the Coulomb reference convention, not a newly discovered principle. The proof is direct multiplication followed by the Schur complement.

For one channel |C|=1 this is |A|^2<=2 Im(C* A). Equation (27) of 2606.19432v1, in both the PDF page 5 and HTML, instead displays Im(A* C). Taking S=C exp(2i delta) gives A=C[sin(2delta)+i(1-cos(2delta))]. The correct inequality is saturated, whereas the printed one has a negative right-hand side for nonzero delta. This is an algebraic error in that display, not evidence against its forward subtraction construction.

If S and C are rephased together as S_new=D_out^dagger S D_in and C_new=D_out^dagger C D_in, then B_new=D_in^dagger B D_in. The relative inequality is convention covariant. Rephasing only S while retaining the old C changes the model comparison.

Choosing T0=1/(2 beta p_CM) is explicitly called an ansatz away from static/nonrelativistic limits in the June paper. For example multiplying T0 by exp(c beta^2 m1m2/(m1+m2)^2) retains symmetry and both limiting matches. Thus those matches alone do not prove uniqueness. Nothing here contradicts the authors' explicit ansatz qualification. Relative contractivity can handle that convention freedom; it supplies no new crossing theorem. The August Lippstreu paper explains that Coulomb and real-radiative terms must combine under analytic continuation.

## Lemma 4: an exact hard-only amplitude cannot reproduce a generic inclusive detector channel

This statement addresses an interface stronger than a basis-to-basis inclusive cross section. Let V:H_hard->H_hard,out tensor H_soft be a scattering isometry. The apparatus does not observe H_soft. Its exact reduced process is

Phi(rho)=Tr_soft[V rho V^dagger]=sum_mu K_mu rho K_mu^dagger.

There is a single hard-space amplitude K that satisfies Phi(rho)=K rho K^dagger for **all input density matrices and all hard observables** iff the Choi matrix J(Phi) has rank one. Proof: vectorization gives J=sum_mu |K_mu>><<K_mu|; it is rank one iff all nonzero K_mu are proportional. The converse reconstructs K from the rank-one Choi factor. This is standard quantum-channel algebra, independently reconstructed here.

Explicit counterexample: V|0>=|0>|e0>, V|1>=|1>|e1>, with r=<e1|e0>, 0<=r<1. The channel fixes both computational basis states, but multiplies their coherence by r. Its Choi eigenvalues are 1+r,1-r,0,0. The input |+> becomes a mixed state of purity (1+r^2)/2. Any single-K image of a pure input has rank at most one, so no such K exists. A still stronger elementary proof: fixing both basis states forces K to be diagonal with unit-modulus entries, which preserves the magnitude of off-diagonal coherence; Phi reduces it.

Coherent soft states give |r|=exp(-||f0-f1||^2/2), so any distinct unresolved soft profile has the obstruction at a finite regulator. The limiting QED realization and decoherence calculation are already in Carney et al. 1710.02531, Sec. II Eq. (11)-(13). Our use is to identify the exact interface that a finite-detector bootstrap must preserve. This is **not** a no-go theorem against Faddeev-Kulish S-matrices on the full dressed Hilbert space, nor against the April paper's scaling-limit amplitudes, nor against reproducing only diagonal inclusive cross sections.

The real downstream opportunity is a dispersive theory of the Gram/Choi object of a resolved scattering instrument, retaining unobserved soft channels through PSD constraints. However, a completely positive real-energy map alone does not supply analytic continuation or crossing. Analyticity of the constituent dressed amplitudes, their joint detector dependence, and cross-channel compatibility are the missing physics. Calling the Choi positivity itself a gravity positivity theorem would be false.
