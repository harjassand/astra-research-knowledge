# Sharp current and dissipation bounds for a unicyclic CTMC

## Theorem and exact interface

Let a finite, autonomous, irreducible continuous-time Markov chain be a directed ring with $N >= 3$ states (or $N=2$ with two separately resolved transition channels). On edge $i$, the forward rate is $u_i>0$, from state $i$ to $i+1$, and the backward rate is $w_i>0$, with indices modulo $N$. Let $p_i>0$ be its stationary distribution. Stationarity makes the edge current independent of $i$:

\[
J=p_i u_i-p_{i+1}w_i.
\]

Define the cycle affinity and the **bare rate-constant sum** by

\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^N\log\frac{u_i}{w_i},\qquad
K=\sum_{i=1}^N(u_i+w_i).
\]

The sharp bound is

\[
\boxed{|J|\leq \frac{K}{N^2}\tanh\!\left(\frac{|A|}{2N}\right).}
\tag{1}
\]

For fixed $A != 0$ and $K$, equality holds exactly when $p_i=1/N$ and the stationary edge affinities

\[
a_i=\log\frac{p_i u_i}{p_{i+1}w_i}
\]

are all $A/N$, after orienting the ring so $A,J>0$. A realizing rate vector is

\[
u_i=\frac{K}{2N}\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{A}{2N}\right)\right],\qquad
w_i=\frac{K}{2N}\left[1-\tanh\!\left(\frac{A}{2N}\right)\right].
\]

Here $K$ has units of inverse time and is a cap on a sum of rate parameters. It is not the stationary transition traffic, not an energy expenditure, and not automatically the kinetic activity measured along trajectories. The algebraic inequality requires no local-detailed-balance (LDB) assumption. To interpret $k_BT A$ as net work supplied per cycle and

\[
\dot S_{\rm tot}=k_B A J
\tag{2}
\]

as the steady entropy-production rate, impose LDB for the resolved cycle and count every chemical, mechanical, and other reservoir coupled to it.

## Proof

If $J=0$, then every edge has zero net flow, so $a_i=0$ and $A=0$; (1) is then the identity $0=0$. Otherwise orient the ring so $J>0$. Each stationary edge affinity is then positive, and the product of stationary probability ratios telescopes, so $sum_i a_i=A$. Write $z_i=tanh(a_i/2)$, which lies in $(0,1)$. Solving the two equations $x_i-y_i=J$ and $x_i/y_i=exp(a_i)$, where $x_i=p_i*u_i$ and $y_i=p_(i+1)*w_i$, gives

\[
x_i=\frac J2\bigl(\coth(a_i/2)+1\bigr),\quad
y_i=\frac J2\bigl(\coth(a_i/2)-1\bigr).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac KJ
&=\sum_i\left(\frac{x_i}{Jp_i}+\frac{y_i}{Jp_{i+1}}\right)\\
&=\sum_i q_i\coth(a_i/2)
 +\frac12\sum_i\left(p_i^{-1}-p_{i+1}^{-1}\right)\\
&=\sum_i\frac{q_i}{z_i},\qquad
q_i=\frac12\left(p_i^{-1}+p_{i+1}^{-1}\right),
\end{aligned}
\]

because the last difference telescopes around the ring. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\[
\sum_i\frac{q_i}{z_i}\geq
\frac{(\sum_i\sqrt{q_i})^2}{\sum_i z_i}.
\tag{3}
\]

AM-GM gives

\[
\sqrt{q_i}\geq(p_i p_{i+1})^{-1/4},\qquad
\sum_i(p_i p_{i+1})^{-1/4}
\geq N\left(\prod_i p_i\right)^{-1/(2N)}
\geq N\sqrt N,
\]

where the last step uses $\prod_i p_i\leq N^{-N}$. Concavity of $\tanh$ on the positive half-line gives

\[
\sum_i z_i\leq N\tanh\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\frac{a_i}{2}\right)
=N\tanh\!\left(\frac A{2N}\right).
\]

Substitution in (3) yields $K/J >= N^2*coth(A/(2N))$, which is (1). For the displayed uniform rates, $p_i=1/N$, $K=sum_i(u_i+w_i)$, and $J=(u_i-w_i)/N=(K/N^2)*tanh(A/(2N))$, proving sharpness. For $A != 0$, equality conditions in AM-GM and Jensen require uniform $p_i$ and uniform $a_i$; Cauchy is then also an equality. For $A=0$, all detailed-balance unicycles have $J=0$, so (1) is trivially an equality.

Under LDB, the edge entropy flow is $k_B*(p_i*u_i-p_(i+1)*w_i)*log((p_i*u_i)/(p_(i+1)*w_i))$. Summing over edges gives $k_B*J*sum_i(a_i)=k_B*A*J$, which is (2).

## Sharp inverse and dissipation consequence

If the design or measurement interface supplies only $K\leq K_{\max}$, a target current $0<|J|<K_{\max}/N^2$ requires

\[
\boxed{|A|\geq 2N\operatorname{artanh}\!\left(\frac{N^2|J|}{K_{\max}}\right).}
\tag{4}
\]

Under LDB, this implies the sharp conditional dissipation lower bound

\[
\boxed{\dot S_{\rm tot}\geq
2N k_B |J|\operatorname{artanh}\!\left(\frac{N^2|J|}{K_{\max}}\right).}
\tag{5}
\]

The uniform-rate construction attains equality. As $N^2*|J|/K_max$ approaches 1, the required affinity diverges: bounded $K$ gives a speed cap and fast operation near that cap requires unbounded drive. For small $J/K_max$, (5) is $Sdot_tot >= 2*N^3*k_B*J^2/K_max + O(J^4/K_max^3)$. These statements concern steady unicyclic turnover only.

At fixed $K,A$, (1) also gives the sharp current-dissipation frontier

\[
J_{\max}(A,K)=\frac K{N^2}\tanh\!\left(\frac A{2N}\right),\quad
\sigma_{\max}(A,K)=\frac{KA}{N^2}\tanh\!\left(\frac A{2N}\right),
\]

where $sigma=A*J$ is in units of $k_B$ per unit time. Near equilibrium $J_max approximately K*A/(2*N^3)$ and, after eliminating $A$, $J_max approximately sqrt(K*sigma/(2*N^3))$. These are a general-$N$ extension of a source-reported $N=3$ formula, not a claim of priority.

## Do not conflate bare rate sum with physical traffic

Define edge traffic $tau_i=p_i*u_i+p_(i+1)*w_i$ and total traffic $T=sum_i tau_i$. Then $J/tau_i=tanh(a_i/2)$, so

\[
A=2\sum_i\operatorname{artanh}(J/\tau_i).
\]

Jensen's inequality yields the distinct bound

\[
\boxed{|J|\leq \frac{T}{N}\tanh\!\left(\frac{|A|}{2N}\right),}
\tag{6}
\]

with equality for equal edge traffic. This is the established Equal Traffic Principle. $T$ is directly tied to observed transition counts per time; $K$ is the unweighted sum of rate constants. For the uniform saturator $T=K/N$, so (1) and (6) agree. For general stationary probabilities, there is no identity $T=K/N$, and substituting $T$ for $K$ in (1) or vice versa is wrong.

## Exact obstructions to broader readings

1. **Affinity without a kinetic cap does not bound current.** Replacing every $u_i,w_i$ by $c u_i,c w_i$ leaves $A$ unchanged, leaves $p$ unchanged, and sends $J\mapsto cJ$. A barrier-height or affinity measurement alone cannot determine turnover.
2. **A barrier is not a drive.** Under transition-state/Arrhenius parametrization, raising one edge barrier by $deltaB$ multiplies both directional rates by $exp(-beta*deltaB)$ and preserves that edge rate ratio. It can alter $K,T,J$ while leaving $A$ (hence LDB work per completed net cycle) unchanged. The 2026 Yang-Dill motor analysis uses barriers as traffic-routing controls while keeping the cycle force fixed.
3. **A product current does not identify total dissipation or thermodynamic efficiency in a multicycle device.** Take the product of two independent finite rings as one irreducible CTMC. Let the first ring be product-coupled, with fixed $J_p,A_p$. Let the second be a futile fuel-consuming ring, with $A_f,J_f>0$, that has no product output. Product turnover remains $J_p$ while total entropy production is $k_B*(A_p*J_p+A_f*J_f)$; varying the second ring changes fuel use and total dissipation without changing product current. The general expression is a cycle sum $k_B*sum_c(A_c*J_c)$. Count every fuel-consuming/slippage cycle before quoting efficiency.
4. **A pointwise upper envelope does not determine efficiency at maximum power.** Under the extra assumptions of one fuel molecule and one work output per net product cycle, tight coupling, load $l\in[0,A_f]$ in units of $k_BT$, and a rate cap $K$ maintained at each load, the theorem implies

   \[
   P_{\rm out}(l)\leq k_BT\frac K{N^2}l\tanh\!\left(\frac{A_f-l}{2N}\right).
   \]

   This bounds the attainable power at each load and yields an upper-envelope maximizer. A fixed catalyst's actual efficiency at maximum power additionally depends on how its transition rates change with load; the envelope maximizer is not that device's predicted EMP unless it can retune to the saturating rates at the optimizing load. Prior EMP results already show constitutive/load-sharing dependence.
5. **Periodic driving is outside the theorem.** A periodically modulated catalyst has no time-independent stationary generator during a period. Cyclic protocols can pump at zero instantaneous chemical cycle affinity when the allowed controls include the required combination of state-energy and barrier changes; no-pumping results constrain special classes such as changing only well energies with fixed barriers. Apply (1) only to an autonomous phase or after a justified lifted-process reduction, and include protocol work, chemical work, switching/control energy, and the modulation clock in the accounting.
