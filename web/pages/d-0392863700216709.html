1. EXACT THEOREM AND DOMAIN

For d>=3, let tau=Tr/d and let
 Phi_ab(X)=tau(X)I+a(X-X^T)/2
              +b[(X+X^T)/2-tau(X)I]
with real a,b be a CPTP channel supplied with a CPTP broadcaster whose
two marginals are Phi_ab. This is the defining real O(d) representation.
It is unital, TP and HS-self-adjoint. There exists ONE finite canonical
PURE-ensemble EB channel Gamma_ab on M_d such that
 Gamma_ab>=2|Phi_ab|-I                             (A1)
on the ENTIRE real Hermitian HS space H_d. The modulus is only HS
functional calculus, not assumed to be a positive physical channel.

Write
 m_A=d(d-1)/2, m_S=(d-1)(d+2)/2,
 alpha=(2a-1)_+, beta=(2-d alpha)/(d+2).
Gamma_ab has scalar/antisymmetric/symmetric-traceless eigenvalues
(1,alpha,beta). Its actual pure-state orbit and finite support are proved
below. This is not a claim that an abstract PSD covariance is realizable.

2. COMPLETE-POSITIVITY TRIANGLE AND SIGNED LOWER BOUNDS

The real Hermitian decomposition is
 H_d=R I direct_sum A direct_sum S,
where A consists of imaginary antisymmetric matrices and S of real
symmetric traceless matrices. Their dimensions are 1,m_A,m_S and Phi
eigenvalues 1,a,b. Identity/traceless cross terms vanish.

With Omega=|Omega_d><Omega_d| and swap F, the normalized Choi matrix is
 J(Phi)=(1-b)I/d^2+(a+b)Omega/2+(b-a)F/(2d).
Its eigenvalues on Omega, antisymmetric vectors, and symmetric vectors
orthogonal to Omega are respectively
 r_0=[2+d(d-1)a+(d-1)(d+2)b]/(2d^2),
 r_A=[2+d a-(d+2)b]/(2d^2),
 r_S=[2-d a+(d-2)b]/(2d^2).
Multiplicities are 1,m_A,m_S; their weighted sum is one.

The CP region is the convex triangle with vertices
 (1,1),
 (1/(d-1),-1/(d-1)),
 (-1/(d-1),(d-2)/[(d-1)(d+2)]).
The barycentric probabilities are r_0,m_A r_A,m_S r_S.
Consequently
 a,b>=-1/(d-1)>=-1/2,
 2+d a-(d+2)b>=0.                              (CP)
For d>=3 this means (2|a|-1)_+=(2a-1)_+ and similarly for b.

3. EXACT ANTISYMMETRIC BROADCASTER STAR CAP

Let J_ij=-i(E_ij-E_ji), i<j. Then Tr(J_ij^2)=2 and J_ij^T=-J_ij.
An entrywise expansion gives
 sum_{i<j}J_ij^T tensor J_ij=d Omega-F.
On three d-dimensional registers define
 P=Omega_AB tensor I_C, Q=Omega_AC tensor I_B,
 W_A=sum_{i<j}J_ij^T_A tensor(J_ij^B+J_ij^C)
     =d(P+Q)-(F_AB+F_AC).                       (STAR)
Its exact largest eigenvalue is d for every d>=2.

Here is a representation-independent elementary proof. For each basis
index i, put
 u_i=|Omega_d>_AB tensor |i>_C,
 v_i=|Omega_d>_AC tensor |i>_B,
 w_i=|i>_A tensor |Omega_d>_BC.
Vectors belonging to distinct indices are orthogonal. For fixed i the
three Gram diagonal entries are one and offdiagonal entries are 1/d,
so their Gram eigenvalues are 1+2/d,1-1/d,1-1/d>0 for d>=2.
Their span S has dimension 3d. Direct action gives
 W_A u_i=(d-1)u_i+v_i-w_i,
 W_A v_i=u_i+(d-1)v_i-w_i,
 W_A w_i=0.
Thus u_i+v_i-(2/d)w_i, u_i-v_i, w_i are its eigenvectors with
eigenvalues d,d-2,0. S is invariant. Its orthogonal complement is also
invariant, P and Q vanish there, and W_A=-(F_AB+F_AC) there has norm
at most two. Since d>=2, the global maximum is exactly d.

For the supplied broadcaster B let R=(Id tensor B)(Omega). Each marginal
Choi contribution has expectation a Tr(J_ij^2)/d=2a/d. Therefore
 Tr(R W_A)=2a(d-1)<=d,
or
 a<=d/[2(d-1)].                                (ANTI)
This uses a single positive normalized joint state, not independent
optimizers for the marginals. In particular
 0<=alpha=(2a-1)_+<=1/(d-1).

4. EXACT FULL CHOI-SCORE CAP

On span{u_i,v_i}, P+Q has eigenvectors u_i+v_i,u_i-v_i and eigenvalues
1+1/d,1-1/d; it vanishes on the orthogonal complement of that span.
Thus ||P+Q||=1+1/d. In the same joint Choi state R its expectation is
2r_0, so r_0<=(d+1)/(2d). Equivalently
 m_A a+m_S b<=m_S,
or
 d a+(d+2)b<=d+2.                              (CHOI)
No Hilbert-Schmidt-positive-spectrum condition has entered either cap.

5. ACTUAL PURE O(d)-ORBIT COMPARATOR

For any 0<=alpha<=1/(d-1), put
 q=(d-1)alpha in [0,1],
 u=(1+sqrt(1-q))/2,
 psi=sqrt(u)e_1+i sqrt(1-u)e_2, pi=|psi><psi|.
This is a genuine pure state, not a mixed covariance witness.
Average its real orthogonal orbit pi_O=O pi O^T with normalized Haar
measure on O(d), and define
 Gamma_alpha(X)=d integral pi_O Tr(pi_O X) dO.    (ORBIT)
The average state is I/d: independent coordinate sign changes kill
offdiagonal entries, and permutations make all diagonal entries equal.
Hence (ORBIT) is a unital, TP, self-adjoint, positive-HS canonical EB map.
Its CP/EB status follows directly from its pure measure-and-prepare form.

The orbit contains the complex conjugate pi^T: reflect the second real
coordinate. Haar invariance therefore makes Gamma commute with transpose,
so its antisymmetric and symmetric sectors have no cross block.
It also commutes with every real orthogonal conjugation.

For completeness, these symmetries force scalar action on A and S
without an unstated SO(4) irreducibility assumption. Independent
coordinate sign flips distinguish the characters attached to each
unordered offdiagonal pair. Thus the antisymmetric pair basis is
diagonalized, and coordinate permutations make all its eigenvalues equal.
For symmetric matrices, sign changes separate diagonal and offdiagonal
blocks. Permutations make the map scalar on traceless diagonal matrices
and constant on all offdiagonal symmetric pairs. A pi/4 rotation in each
coordinate plane takes E_ii-E_jj to a signed E_ij+E_ji, equating these
two scalars. This proves scalar action on the full S. The same argument
retains the identity direction separately.

Let V=(pi-pi^T)/2. Its ordinary HS norm satisfies
 Tr(V^2)=2u(1-u)=q/2.
The HS trace of Gamma restricted to A is d Tr(V^2), because its
measure-and-prepare form is d times the orbit average of |pi><pi|.
Thus its common eigenvalue on A is
 d(q/2)/m_A=q/(d-1)=alpha.
The full HS trace of Gamma is d Tr(pi^2)=d, while its identity eigenvalue
is one. Therefore its eigenvalue beta on S satisfies
 1+m_A alpha+m_S beta=d,
giving exactly
 beta=(2-d alpha)/(d+2).
In particular beta>= (d-2)/[(d-1)(d+2)]>0 for d>=3.

The pure support may be made FINITE with identical canonical channel.
Choose an ordinary-HS orthonormal traceless basis and let x(pi) in R^N
be its expectation vector, N=d^2-1. The joint moment map
 pi -> (x(pi),x(pi)x(pi)^T)
has target dimension N+N(N+1)/2=N(N+3)/2. The compact orbit's Haar
average belongs to its compact convex hull. Caratheodory gives at most
 1+N(N+3)/2=d^2(d^2+1)/2
pure orbit states with exactly the same first and second moments.
Their barycenter is I/d and their canonical map equals (ORBIT) on
ALL H_d. This is an existence/construction fact, not an efficient
ensemble-extraction algorithm. The d=3 baseline gives a smaller explicit
18-state construction for that dimension.

6. BOTH ABSOLUTE-SPECTRUM INEQUALITIES

Set alpha=(2a-1)_+ and use the actual pure orbit comparator above.
(CP) and (ANTI) ensure its allowed interval. Since a>=-1/2,
alpha>=2|a|-1. The identity inequality is exact, with eigenvalue one.

If b<=1/2, (CP) also gives |b|<=1/2, so beta>=0>=2|b|-1.
For b>1/2, only positive b matters. If a<=1/2, alpha=0 and beta=2/(d+2).
The following exact identity shows the remaining inequality:
 beta-(2b-1)
  =[2(2+d a-(d+2)b)+d(1-2a)]/(d+2)>=0.
If a>1/2, alpha=2a-1 and
 beta-(2b-1)
  =2[(d+2)-d a-(d+2)b]/(d+2)>=0
by (CHOI). Hence beta>=2|b|-1 in every case.

All three orthogonal sectors are controlled simultaneously, proving
Gamma_ab>=2|Phi_ab|-I on the entire Hermitian matrix space. No
direction-dependent measurement or spectrum sign removal is used.

 I-(tensor Gamma_j)<=2[I-(tensor |Phi_j|)].
Self-adjoint product spectra give tensor |Phi_j|=|tensor Phi_j|>=tensor

The comparator is a finite pure product ensemble, with barycenter I/D;

8. DIMENSION TWO AND A PRESERVED FAILED EXTENSION

The d>=3 lower-spectrum step uses -1/(d-1)>=-1/2. It fails at d=2.
Indeed a=-1,b=0 is the legal qubit EB channel that measures the imaginary
Pauli Y axis and prepares the opposite eigenstate on both outputs.
The naive formula alpha=(2a-1)_+=0 would fail alpha>=2|a|-1=1.
This is a counterexample to that naive formula in d=2, not to C2.

Instead the independently established signed-qubit theorem gives a pure
canonical full-domain comparator. In this repeated-eigenvalue family,
put h_a=(2|a|-1)_+, h_b=(2|b|-1)_+, r=1-h_a-2h_b>=0. Its six-axis
pure ensemble has alpha=h_a+r/3 and the two symmetric Pauli eigenvalues
beta=h_b+r/3. It obeys the required modulus comparison. For the displayed
failure witness it selects dephasing on Y, alpha=1,beta=0.

Thus factors of dimension two may be combined with the d>=3 theorem,
retaining the full signed tensor-product C2 result.
