# Independent gate: a polynomial-bit random prime for rational subspace packing

7 October 2026. Worker stat_exec15, verified GPT-6.1 Sol / max; no nested workers. **PASS, with the interfaces below.** The new deduction is independently reconstructed from the primary arithmetic certificate theorem and elementary subspace geometry. ## Exact result and input

Let B_i be explicitly supplied integer r-by-d_i matrices of full column rank, with r,m,H >= 1, 1 <= d_i <= r and absolute entries at most 2^H. Let S_i be their rational column spans. For a geometric field K, define

    P_K = {x in R^m_{≥0}: sum_i x_i dim_K(S_i intersect W) <= dim_K W for all W <= K^r}.

The weights remain ordinary real scalars. They are not reduced modulo p. P_K is a bounded packing polytope; references to a cone mean its corresponding homogeneous dimension inequalities, not that the displayed P_K is itself a cone.

Given rational 0 < delta < 1, the explicit sampler below produces a polynomial-bit prime p such that all input ranks and the entire polytope are preserved with probability at least 1-delta. The same event protects every nonnegative real weight vector, including subsequent adaptive queries. An expected polynomial-time version always produces a prime. A bounded polynomial-time version allows UNKNOWN and charges its timeout separately.

Rational matrices are first cleared **columnwise** to integer matrices, preserving each characteristic-zero span. The bit lengths of the cleared entries are polynomial in the supplied rational encoding, because denominator products have bit length at most the sum of their factor bit lengths. H is charged after this conversion. Zero subspaces may be removed and their unconstrained coordinates restored. Rank is checked for the original integer presentation at the selected prime; neither denominator clearing nor ground-set rank agreement supplies the full-polytope event.

## Field comparison reconstructed independently

For fixed x >= 0 define D(W)=sum_i x_i dim(S_i intersect W)-dim W. For any U,V,

    (S_i intersect U)+(S_i intersect V) subset S_i intersect(U+V),

while the meet is S_i intersect(U intersect V). Dimension additivity gives supermodularity of each intersection dimension and modularity of dim W. Thus D(U)+D(V) <= D(U intersect V)+D(U+V). The finite number of integer dimension profiles gives an attained maximum. Two maximizers have both their meet and join maximizing. A maximizer of largest dimension consequently contains every maximizer and is unique.

Over C, fix the dimension and exact profile of this largest maximizer. In a Grassmann chart, exact ranks are rational polynomial equalities and finitely many nonvanishing minors. Their nonempty constructible locus has an algebraic point: adjoining inverses for those minors reduces this to the weak Nullstellensatz over Qbar. That point has the same score and largest dimension, so uniqueness makes it the original maximizer. Every automorphism of Qbar/Q preserves all profile dimensions and this largest maximizer. Its canonical echelon coordinates are therefore rational. This proves equality of maximal defect over Q, R and C, hence

    P_Q = P_R = P_C,

for all nonnegative real x. Rationality of the weights is unnecessary. This argument does **not** say that every complex intersection profile has a rational realization. Frobenius invariance of the unique largest maximizer similarly gives P_Fp=P_Fpbar; that finite-field descent is not needed to turn an Fp point into a point over its algebraic closure.

At any prime retaining all supplied column ranks, P_Fp is contained in P_Q. For a rational W, the lattices S_i intersect Z^r, W intersect Z^r and (S_i intersect W) intersect Z^r are saturated in Z^r. A saturated lattice is a direct summand, so reduction preserves its dimension and injects into the ambient Fp space. The last lattice reduces into the intersection of the first two reductions. Intersection dimensions can therefore increase, and cannot decrease. Rank preservation for the supplied columns excludes p from their index in the saturated lattice (Smith normal form), so their reduced span is exactly that saturated reduction. Nonnegative weights give the inclusion. In particular, finite-field positive feasibility is sound at every admissible prime; an uncertified rejection can be false for the original field.

These arguments reconstruct the load-bearing ingredients of the cycle15 baseline rather than assuming that all ground-matroid ranks determine ambient intersections.

## Uniform incidence encoding and certificate count

For each k=0,...,r and each integer lower profile 0 <= a_i <= min(d_i,k), use the closed incidence condition

    Z_(k,a) = {W in Gr(k,r)_C : dim(S_i intersect W) >= a_i for all i}.

On a standard affine chart choose k pivot rows and an r-by-k basis Y with the pivot block equal to the identity. Its other n=k(r-k) entries are variables. Every k-plane over every field has such a basis on some chart. The identity block guarantees full rank without localization or an omitted nonvanishing equation.

If B_i has rank d_i, the condition is exactly rank[B_i Y] <= d_i+k-a_i. Impose all minors of size d_i+k-a_i+1 if this size is at most r; larger sizes impose no equations. Uniformly:

    n <= r^2;
    degree <= r;
    log(max coefficient modulus) <= r H log 2 + log(r!);
    number of generators s <= m 8^r;
    total chart/profile pairs G <= 2^r (r+1)^m.

Each determinant has at most r! products, each with coefficient modulus at most 2^(rH), even after grouping identical monomials. Row choices contribute at most 2^r and column choices at most 2^(d_i+k) <= 2^(2r). Summing the chart counts over k gives 2^r. These are proof bounds; the algorithm never expands or enumerates the systems.

The primary [Krick–Pardo–Sombra v1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/9911094v1), Introduction Theorem 1, says that a complex-empty integer polynomial system in n variables has

    A = sum_j g_j f_j,  A nonzero integer, g_j integer polynomials,
    log |A| <= 4n(n+1)d^n [h+log s+(n+7)d log(n+1)].

Here h is the logarithmic maximum coefficient height. I read this exact bound in the preserved primary HTML; the proof of their theorem is imported, not re-proved. If a reduction has a common zero over Fpbar, evaluating the identity forces p to divide A. Thus at most log_2 |A| primes can make this empty system nonempty. This implication and the bad-prime counting principle already appear explicitly in [Hägele–Morais–Pardo–Sombra](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/83447), author manuscript Introduction pp.1–2, published in 2000. They are not a new mechanism here.

The following integers are sufficient and computable without any hidden certificate:

    N = r^2, d = 2r,
    h* = r [H + ceil(log_2(2r)) + 2],
    ell* = ceil(log_2(m+1)) + 3r,
    C = 4N(N+1)d^N [h*+ell*+(N+7)d(N+1+d)],
    G = 2^r(r+1)^m,
    B = 2GC + 2mh*.

Since natural logarithms are smaller than the corresponding base-two logarithms, h<=h* and log s<=ell*. Also log(n+1)<=N+1+d, and n<=N and the actual degree<=d. Hence the certificate has log|A|<=C. The conversion 1/log 2 < 2 gives at most 2C exceptional primes per empty chart. If n=0 or all nonzero generators are constant, choose a nonzero constant as A; its height is at most h*, which is also covered. If every generator is zero, the affine chart is nonempty and needs no emptiness certificate.

Selecting one nonzero maximal minor from each input matrix gives at most 2mh* rank-failure primes. A prime at which input rank drops must divide the selected minor. This does not require the sampler to find those minors; they provide a bound on a finite set. The total exceptional-prime count is at most B, including all rank failures and all characteristic-zero-empty chart systems. No classification of empty systems or construction of A is performed.

Suppose p avoids this union and x in P_Q fails an Fp constraint. Its violating W has dimension k and exact profile a. If Z_(k,a) had a complex point W', nonnegativity would give

    sum_i x_i dim(S_i intersect W') >= sum_i x_i a_i > k,

contradicting x in P_C. Thus that entire profile locus, and the chart containing W, is complex-empty. Its certificate forbids the observed finite-field point. This contradiction gives P_Q subset P_Fp, and the admissible-prime inclusion gives equality. The argument even prohibits new forbidden points over Fpbar. It never infers that a nonempty complex locus has an Fp-rational point. This is one event for the entire polytope, not a union bound over requested weights.

## Exact sampler and charged bit cost

For the expected-time sampler let q=ceil(1/delta), b=bit_length(B), and

    L = 4 [b + bit_length(q) + 20].

An unbiased L-2 bit integer chooses uniformly among the 2^(L-2) odd L-bit candidates. Apply an exact deterministic polynomial-time primality algorithm to successive independent candidates, accepting the first prime. [Agrawal–Kayal–Saxena](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12) supplies such an algorithm without an uncharged probable-prime error. Conditional on acceptance, each prime in [2^(L-1),2^L) has the same geometric-series probability, so this distribution is exactly uniform.

[Rosser–Schoenfeld](https://doi.org/10.1215/ijm/1255631807), Corollary 1, printed p.69 / PDF p.6, gives pi(x)>x/log x for x>=17 and pi(x)<1.25506 x/log x for x>1. I visually inspected both inequalities in the publisher PDF. Publisher page access was blocked; the preserved PDF and separately recovered Crossref metadata verify the source and its 1 March 1962 publication date.

For L>=6 the resulting difference is at least 2^(L-1)/(4L). One direct rational check is

    1/L - 63/[100(L-1)] >= 1/(8L),

equivalent to 196L>=700, which holds for L>=6. Replacing 1.25506 by 1.26 and log 2 by 1 weakens the true bound to this inequality. The interval therefore contains at least 2^(L-1)/(4L) primes and the expected odd-candidate count is at most 2L.

The bad-prime probability is at most 4BL / 2^(L-1). Because B<2^b and q<2^bit_length(q),

    Bq < 2^(L/4-20),
    4BLq / 2^(L-1) < 4L * 2^(-3L/4-19) < 1.

The last inequality holds for L>=80, for example using 4L<=2^(L/4) there and induction on integer L. Thus the probability is below 1/q<=delta. Test ranks after acceptance and return UNKNOWN on rank loss. Repeatedly conditioning the sample on retained rank is a different sampling rule; its probability must be recharged if implemented.

For a bounded procedure take q=ceil(2/delta) and the same L formula. Stop after T=2L*bit_length(q) odd trials. A trial succeeds with probability at least 1/(2L), so timeout is at most exp(-bit_length(q))<1/q<=delta/2. Conditional on finding a prime by T, the equal first-acceptance sums still give exactly uniform primes. Its bad-prime probability is at most 1/q<=delta/2. Return UNKNOWN on timeout or input rank loss. The probability of producing a good prime is at least 1-delta with a deterministic polynomial bound on the number of bit operations, using the named AKS algorithm. No practical AKS runtime is inferred.

The exact integers C,G,B are exponential in dimensions but have polynomial **bit length**:

    log_2 B = O(r^2 log(r+1) + m log(r+1) + log(H+m+2)).

Integer exponentiation, L computation, random-bit generation, primality testing and reduced-matrix rank testing consequently cost polynomially many bits/operations in the dense input and log(delta^-1). The field cardinality is not claimed polynomial; its logarithm is polynomial. The hidden certificates may have exponentially many bits and remain unacquired.

## Hirai composition: scalar value and precise relaxed membership

The primary [Hirai v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13972v1#S3), submitted 14 August 2026, Theorem 3.5 gives an arbitrary-field dual upper approximation v with tau_p<=v<=tau_p+epsilon. Its iterations cost O(c_max^2 r^2 m^2 epsilon^-2), each with O(r^3 m) field operations and polynomial rational-coordinate bit length. I read the algorithm and theorem statement plus Section 4.2; the convergence and building-theoretic proofs remain named source prerequisites.

For the BL objective c_i=d_i<=r, caps u in [0,1]^m and rational epsilon>0, the field-operation count is O(r^7 m^3 epsilon^-2). Over Fp each operation has polynomial bit cost in L, and the external rational coordinates retain the source's bit bound. On the single good-prime event, tau_p=tau_Q for all caps/objectives. The returned rational scalar is therefore an epsilon **upper** approximation to the original optimal value. Generic binary-encoded enormous objective weights retain the source's numerical dependence on c_max; an unqualified polynomial-bit claim for those weights is not adopted. This is an additive FPTAS with polynomial dependence on epsilon^-1, not log(epsilon^-1).

For BL membership, first check u in [0,1]^m and sum_i d_i u_i=r exactly. The source's relevant threshold lemma is **Lemma 4.4**, not Lemma 3.6. If v>=r, then tau_p>=r-epsilon and admissible-prime inclusion gives tau_Q>=r-epsilon. A feasible capped packing x with value at least r-epsilon yields, by the annihilator identity,

    sum_i x_i dim(B_i^T V) >= dim V-epsilon.

Since u>=x, balanced u obeys the same inequality. Thus this affirmative answer to Hirai's relaxed set is sound at every admissible prime. If v<r, then tau_p<r. On the good-prime event tau_Q<r, so u is outside the original exact BL polytope. A bad admissible prime can make this negative branch wrong. The relaxation means additive slack epsilon in every dimension inequality and in balance; it is not an unstated Euclidean-distance promise.

No original rational packing vector, dual flag, violating rational subspace, analytic BL constant or deterministic certificate that the sampled prime is good is output by this composition. Finite-field flags remain finite-field objects. Common-denominator exact threshold tests from cycle15 still cost O(D^2 r^(r+7)m^3) field operations: this is exponential in log D and variable r. The new sampler resolves this particular scalar approximate bit-cost reduction; it does not resolve general exact binary-weight BL membership.

Primitive plane normals have determinant 21; actual cross products have determinant 168. Over Q no line lies in all three planes. The weights (1/2,1/2,1/2) satisfy all line, plane and full-space constraints: line load<=1, plane load<=2 and full-space load=3. The full-space bound proves unweighted optimum 3/2. Over F7 the common line span(e2) forces sum_i x_i<=1; this condition is sufficient, giving optimum 1.

    F1=2x-2z, F2=16x-4z, F3=75x-7-6z,
