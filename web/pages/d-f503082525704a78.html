A. Daniel Hsu, Sham M. Kakade, Tong Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” Journal of Computer and System Sciences 78(5), 1460–1480 (2012), arXiv:0811.4413 v6.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413
Read: arXiv abstract and HTML full text, https://arxiv.org/html/0811.4413 (live checked 2026-10-08).
Relevant exact spans in source HTML: lines 104–108 state the rank condition (positive initial distribution; full column rank observation and transition matrices); lines 113–117 describe the observation-only sample setting, independent sampled triples in the analyzed proof, and note long sequences can be used with effective sample size discounted by mixing; lines 285–306 state that conditional estimation is delicate on unlikely events and give sample complexity dependence on sigma_m(O), sigma_m(P_{2,1}); lines 288–303 explicitly place these singular values in the sample bound.
Use here: primary comparison only. The lower is consistent with separation-dependent sample guarantees: the rare stationary state makes P_{2,1}=O T diag(pi) O^T have a small singular value, even though O and T are full rank for each fixed alpha. In our binary model, the 2-by-2 matrix of adjacent-symbol joint probabilities P_{2,1} has determinant equal to the lag-one observable covariance computed in RESULT.txt, hence O(alpha), while its largest singular value is bounded; therefore its second singular value vanishes with alpha, exactly the kind of margin entering their sample bound. This paper does not establish our lower bound.

B. Elisabeth Gassiat, Alice Cleynen, Stéphane Robin, “Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general identifiable,” arXiv:1306.4657 (2013).
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657
Read: arXiv abstract (live checked 2026-10-08), lines 8–20 of the abstract page.
Exact abstract claim used: finite-state nonparametric HMMs are identifiable when the latent transition matrix has full rank and emission probability distributions are linearly independent.
Use here: each fixed-alpha model satisfies the stated structural conditions (nonzero transition determinant and distinct binary emission laws), so the construction is not a pair of population-equivalent models. This paper does not establish finite-sample uniform conditional prediction or our lower bound.
