3. Main proof campaign: Gaussian noise stability
Let \(\gamma_2\) be standard Gaussian measure on \(\mathbb R^2\). For a partition \(\mathcal A=(A_1,A_2,A_3)\), define
\[
S_\rho(\mathcal A)
=\sum_{i=1}^3\Pr(X\in A_i,\;Y\in A_i),
\]

where \(X,Y\) are standard Gaussian vectors with \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\rho I\).
The three-cell standard-simplex conjecture predicts that, if \(\gamma_2(A_i)=1/3\), three sectors of angle \(120^\circ\) maximize \(S_\rho\) for every \(0<\rho<1\). Existing work establishes restricted correlation ranges and a dimensional reduction for optimizers.

A complete restricted theorem
Theorem (shellwise-balanced Gaussian partitions). Let \(k\ge2\), and let \((A_1,\ldots,A_k)\) partition \(\mathbb R^2\). Suppose that, for almost every radius \(r>0\), each \(A_i\) occupies exactly \(1/k\) of the circumference of the circle of radius \(r\). Then, for every \(0<\rho<1\),
\[
\boxed{
S_\rho(A_1,\ldots,A_k)
\le S_\rho(P_1,\ldots,P_k)
}
\]

where \(P_i\) are \(k\) consecutive equal-angle sectors through the origin.
Proof. Write \(x=r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) and \(y=s(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\). Apart from a positive factor depending only on \(r,s\), the joint density is
\[
\exp\left(
\frac{\rho rs}{1-\rho^2}\cos(\theta-\phi)
\right).
\]

For \(r,s>0\), this angular kernel is symmetric and strictly decreasing with circular distance \(|\theta-\phi|\in[0,\pi]\).
Fix a label \(i\) and radii \(r,s\). The two angular slices of \(A_i\) both have length \(2\pi/k\). The circular Riesz rearrangement inequality therefore gives
\[
\int_{\Theta_i(r)}\int_{\Theta_i(s)}
e^{\kappa\cos(\theta-\phi)}
\,d\theta\,d\phi
\le
\int_I\int_I
e^{\kappa\cos(\theta-\phi)}
\,d\theta\,d\phi,
\]

where \(\kappa=\rho rs/(1-\rho^2)>0\) and \(I\) is any arc of length \(2\pi/k\). The circular rearrangement theorem is classical; its relevant kernel hypotheses are satisfied here. 


researchgate.net



Sum this inequality over labels and integrate against the positive radial part of the joint Gaussian density. The upper bounds for all labels are attained simultaneously by \(k\) fixed, consecutive, equally sized sectors: each label has the same angular arc at every radius. Hence the asserted global inequality follows. \(\square\)
This proves an exact all-correlation result, not merely an asymptotic or numerical one. It also reveals the missing difficulty: global Gaussian balance does not imply balance separately on every radius.
Accordingly, the theorem is not a resolution of the standard-simplex conjecture. Its use of classical circular rearrangement also means that historical novelty has not been established.
4. A second complete deduction: parallel slabs are unstable
A natural competitor to the three-sector partition divides the plane into three parallel Gaussian slabs of equal measure.
This competitor can be ruled out for every positive correlation, without assuming the standard-simplex conjecture.
Proposition. For every fixed \(0<\rho<1\), the equal-measure three-slab partition of \(\mathbb R^2\) admits arbitrarily small measure-preserving perturbations that strictly increase its Gaussian noise stability.
Proof
Set \(a=\Phi^{-1}(2/3)\), and begin with
\[
A_1^0=\{x_1<-a\},\quad
A_2^0=\{-a\le x_1\le a\},\quad
A_3^0=\{x_1>a\}.
\]

For small \(\varepsilon>0\), rotate the outward normals of the two extreme halfspaces toward the same side:
\[
u_\varepsilon=(-\cos\varepsilon,\sin\varepsilon),
\qquad
v_\varepsilon=(\cos\varepsilon,\sin\varepsilon).
\]

Let \(H_1=\{u_\varepsilon\cdot x>a\}\) and \(H_3=\{v_\varepsilon\cdot x>a\}\). Both retain Gaussian measure \(1/3\).
Temporarily treat \(1-1_{H_1}-1_{H_3}\) as the middle-cell indicator. Expanding the corresponding quadratic noise-stability functional gives
\[
\widetilde S_\rho(\varepsilon)
=-\frac13+4S_\rho(H_1)
+2\Pr(u_\varepsilon\cdot X>a,\;
       v_\varepsilon\cdot Y>a).
\]

The halfspace stability \(S_\rho(H_1)\) is rotation-invariant. The correlation of the two scalar variables in the final probability is
\[
t_\varepsilon=\rho\,u_\varepsilon\cdot v_\varepsilon
=-\rho\cos(2\varepsilon).
\]

For a bivariate standard Gaussian, differentiation of the upper-tail probability with respect to its correlation gives the positive joint density \(\varphi_2(a,a;t)\). Since
\[
t_\varepsilon=-\rho+2\rho\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4),
\]

we obtain
\[
\widetilde S_\rho(\varepsilon)
-\widetilde S_\rho(0)
=
4\rho\,\varphi_2(a,a;-\rho)\varepsilon^2
+O(\varepsilon^4)>0
\]

for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).
There is one issue: \(H_1,H_3\) overlap. However, any point in their intersection satisfies
\[
x_2>\frac a{\sin\varepsilon},
\]

so its Gaussian measure is at most
\[
\exp\left(-\frac{a^2}{2\sin^2\varepsilon}\right).
\]

Remove the overlap from \(H_3\) and replace it with an equal-measure subset of the remaining middle region. This restores an exact three-cell partition with all probabilities equal to \(1/3\).
The correction changes the noise-stability functional by at most a constant times the overlap measure, which is smaller than every positive power of \(\varepsilon\). It therefore cannot cancel the positive quadratic improvement.
The resulting exact balanced partition strictly improves the slabs. \(\square\)
This eliminates an entire natural one-dimensional configuration class for every \(0<\rho<1\). It does not eliminate general curved or nonconical competitors. Historical novelty of this perturbation argument has not been established.
5. Adversarial falsification of the main mechanisms
First-Hermite dominance does not extend coefficient by coefficient
For any balanced three-cell partition, the Hermite expansion takes the form
\[
S_\rho(\mathcal A)=\frac13+
\sum_{m\ge1}\rho^m A_m(\mathcal A),
\qquad A_m(\mathcal A)\ge0.
\]

For the three-sector propeller \(P\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
A_1(P)=\frac9{8\pi},\qquad
A_2(P)=\frac9{16\pi^2}.
\]

For the equal-measure slabs \(L\), with \(a=\Phi^{-1}(2/3)\) and standard Gaussian density \(\varphi\),
\[
A_1(L)=2\varphi(a)^2,\qquad
A_2(L)=3a^2\varphi(a)^2.
\]

In particular,
\[
\boxed{A_2(L)>A_2(P).}
\]

Thus the propeller does not maximize every nonconstant Hermite level individually. Its advantage at one level can be offset at another.
Any proof that attempts to promote OpenAI's first-moment propeller inequality into full noise-stability optimality by comparing each Hermite coefficient separately is invalid.
A tempting radial concavity principle is false
Let \(B[p]\) be the centered angular-arc region whose fraction of each radius-\(r\) circle is \(p(r)\). One might hope that
\[
p\longmapsto S_\rho(B[p])
\]

is concave on radial profiles having fixed Gaussian measure \(1/3\). This would make the remaining problem amenable to Jensen's inequality.
It is false for correlations sufficiently close to one.
To see this, choose \(r_1=\sqrt{2\log(3/2)}\) and \(r_2=\sqrt{2\log3}\). Take
\[
p(r)=\mathbf1_{\{r\le r_1\}},\qquad
q(r)=\mathbf1_{\{r\ge r_2\}}.
\]

Both \(B[p]\) and \(B[q]\) have Gaussian measure \(1/3\). The midpoint profile \(h=(p+q)/2\) describes the union of a right half-disk and the right half of an exterior annulus.
Its Gaussian perimeter is
\[
P_\gamma(B[h])
=
\frac{P_\gamma(B[p])+P_\gamma(B[q])}{2}
+
\frac1\pi
\left(
\int_0^{r_1}e^{-t^2/2}\,dt+
\int_{r_2}^\infty e^{-t^2/2}\,dt
\right).
\]

The additional term is strictly positive. Because near-perfect-correlation noise stability has leading deficit proportional to Gaussian perimeter, the midpoint profile has strictly less stability than the mean of the endpoints for all sufficiently large \(\rho<1\).

Therefore the single-profile concavity approach fails. This counterexample does not refute the more structured, coupled three-profile inequality below.
6. The remaining exact proof gate
The polar reduction yields a sharply specified one-dimensional problem.
Define
\[
H_\kappa(a,b)=\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\int_{-\pi a}^{\pi a}\int_{-\pi b}^{\pi b}
e^{\kappa\cos(\theta-\phi)}
\,d\theta\,d\phi,
\]

and
\[
W_\rho(r,s)=\frac{rs}{1-\rho^2}
\exp\left(
-\frac{r^2+s^2}{2(1-\rho^2)}
\right).
\]

For measurable radial profiles \(p_i:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\), let
\[
\mathcal R_\rho(p)=
\sum_{i=1}^3
\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
W_\rho(r,s)
H_{\rho rs/(1-\rho^2)}(p_i(r),p_i(s))
\,dr\,ds.
\]

Impose the exact constraints
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3p_i(r)=1
\quad\text{almost everywhere},
\qquad
\int_0^\infty re^{-r^2/2}p_i(r)\,dr=\frac13.
\]

A sufficient decisive inequality is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_\rho(p_1,p_2,p_3)
\le
\mathcal R_\rho(1/3,1/3,1/3)
\quad\text{for every }0<\rho<1.
}
\tag{L}
\]

If (L) holds, circular Riesz rearrangement and the established dimension-reduction theorem would prove the full positive-correlation three-cell standard-simplex conjecture.
Crucially, (L) is not a reformulation of the original conjecture. It is a stronger one-dimensional inequality: its three individually rearranged cells need not form a genuine partition. It could fail even if the standard-simplex conjecture is true.
An exploratory numerical search over profiles constant on three equal-Gaussian-mass radial shells did not find an improving configuration at \(\rho=0.5,0.85,0.95\). In each run, the optimizer returned profiles numerically close to \(1/3\). This is only finite-dimensional numerical evidence, not a proof or exhaustive falsification test. 



Two limiting mechanisms support further investigation. As \(\rho\downarrow0\), the new propeller bound controls the leading-order term. As \(\rho\uparrow1\), Gaussian perimeter controls the leading deficit. The missing mathematics must establish a uniform comparison between these endpoints, including profiles that reach zero or one and the intermediate-correlation regime.
