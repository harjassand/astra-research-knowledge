# Addendum 2: the universal sharp constant offset is attained

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: complete proof candidate, submitted for a separate independent audit. This strengthens the frozen `ENDPOINT_PROOF_v1.md`; it does not modify that file. Read its Duhamel and analytic-series lemmas and `ADDENDUM_1_MOMENT_JUSTIFICATION.md` as prerequisites. The constants below are conservative existence bounds, not practical costs.

## Exact statement

Fix \(m\ge2\), \(R>0\), and put
\[
 a=2R,\qquad n=m-1,\qquad
 \boxed{b_* = \log\frac4{a n}-1.}
 \tag{S1}
\]
Among scalar radial all-exponential probability densities that positively regularize **every** bounded self-adjoint \(m\)-tuple satisfying \(\|A(u)\|\le R|u|\), the largest possible fixed constant \(b\) in an eventual upper envelope
\[
 p(x)\le C\exp\left[-\frac{|x|}{a}
  \left(\log|x|+\log\log|x|+b\right)\right]
 \tag{S2}
\]
is precisely \(b_*\), and this largest value is attained.

More explicitly, for sufficiently large \(L\), set
\[
 q_L(r)=\sqrt{L^2+r^2},\quad
 h_b(q)=q(\log q+\log\log q+b),
\]
\[
 V_L(x)=\frac{h_{b_*}(q_L(|x|))-h_{b_*}(L)}a,
 \qquad p_L(x)=Z_L^{-1}e^{-V_L(x)}.
 \tag{S3}
\]
This is an everywhere positive, smooth, radial, strictly log-concave density with every exponential moment. For every allowed tuple and Hilbert space,
\[
 M_A=W_A*p_L\succeq0,\qquad \int M_A(x)\,dx=I,
 \qquad \widehat M_A(t)=\widehat p_L(t)e^{iA(t)}.
 \tag{S4}
\]
Positivity holds pointwise on all of \(\mathbb R^m\). The construction has the sharper, multiplicative tail asymptotic
\[
 \boxed{p_L(x)=C_L\exp\left[-\frac r a
  (\log r+\log\log r+b_*)\right](1+o(1)),\quad r\to\infty,}
 \tag{S5}
\]
where \(0<C_L<\infty\). Thus it obeys (S2) at \(b=b_*\), not just at every smaller constant. The constant \(C\) in the envelope is arbitrary finite and does not affect the threshold for \(b\).

For \(m=2\), the obstruction uses the qubit Pauli pair and \(b_* =\log(2/R)-1\). For larger \(m\), a finite-dimensional Clifford tuple gives the additional factor \(m-1\). Nothing here asserts a universal fixed relative margin \(\varepsilon<1\) in (S4): the available lower relative margin tends to zero in the remote tail.

## 1. Necessity: the transverse Clifford moment block

Choose self-adjoint Clifford matrices \(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_m\) with
\[
 \Gamma_j\Gamma_k+\Gamma_k\Gamma_j=2\delta_{jk}I,
 \qquad A_j=R\Gamma_j.
\]
They exist in a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space for every finite \(m\), and \(\|A(u)\|=R|u|\). Suppose a radial all-exponential \(\nu\) makes the convolution positive. Write
\[
 K(s)=\log m_\nu(se_1),\quad z=Rs,\quad u_s=\sinh(z)/s.
\]
All tilted operator moments below are justified by Addendum 1. Under the normalized positive tilt in direction one, the transverse first moments, indexed by \(j=2,\ldots,m\), are
\[
 B_j=u_s\Gamma_j.
\]
The signal's mixed transverse second derivatives vanish for distinct indices. All diagonal transverse second moments have the same signal contribution \(D_s\), diagonal relative to the eigenspaces of \(\Gamma_1\). Its eigenvalue on the \(+1\) eigenspace is
\[
 D_{s,+}=\frac Rs-\frac{e^{-z}\sinh z}{s^2}.
\]
The scalar noise contributes \(v_s=K'(s)/s\) to every diagonal transverse covariance and zero off the diagonal. Positivity of the full transverse moment block and its Schur complement therefore gives
\[
 v_s I_{\mathbb C^n\otimes\mathcal H}
 +I_n\otimes D_s-u_s^2\mathcal V\mathcal V^*\succeq0,
 \qquad \mathcal V\psi=(\Gamma_2\psi,\ldots,\Gamma_m\psi).
 \tag{1.1}
\]
Here \(\mathcal V^*\mathcal V=nI\). If \(\Gamma_1\psi=-\psi\), all components of \(\mathcal V\psi\) belong to the \(+1\) eigenspace of \(\Gamma_1\). Applying (1.1) to such a nonzero vector yields
\[
 K'(s)\ge \frac{n\sinh^2(Rs)+e^{-Rs}\sinh(Rs)-Rs}{s}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \liminf_{s\to\infty}s e^{-as}K(s)\ge\frac{n}{4a}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]
The tail-envelope Legendre bound in Lemma 6.1 of the main proof says that (S2) implies
\[
 \limsup_{s\to\infty}s e^{-as}K(s)\le\frac{e^{-b-1}}{a^2}.
\]
Comparison forces \(b\le\log(4/(an))-1=b_*\). This necessity applies to any radial density obeying the envelope, without log-concavity or derivative assumptions. The tuple used to force it is finite dimensional.

## 2. Why keeping the exact second-order term matters

Let \(V\) be a radial analytic potential. At a point of radius \(r>0\), let
\[
 g=V'(r),\quad e_r=x/r,\quad H=g A(e_r).
\]
Complete \(e_r\) to an orthonormal frame \(e_r,e_2,\ldots,e_m\). Set \(A_r=A(e_r)\) and \(A_j'=A(e_j)\). All these operators have norm at most \(R\).

In the exact local-tilt identity, write
\[
 F_x(y)=V(x-y)-V(x)+\nabla V(x)\cdot y,
\]
\[
 Q_x(y)=\tfrac12 y^TD^2V(x)y,
 \qquad e^{-F_x(y)}-1=-Q_x(y)+J_x(y).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
The analytic remainder \(J_x\) vanishes through degree two. By exact polynomial evaluation in the Weyl distribution, the quadratic part of the sandwiched error is
\[
 -\frac12 V''(r)A_r^2
 -\frac12\frac gr\sum_{j=2}^m
 e^{-H/2}D^2\exp(H)[A_j',A_j']e^{-H/2}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
The first term is exactly as written: \(A_r\) commutes with \(H\), so its second exponential derivative is \(e^H A_r^2\). It must not be bounded by the exponentially amplified general transverse estimate.

Recall the nonnegative simplex integral
\[
 I(z)=\int_{\substack{u,v\ge0\\u+v\le1}}
 e^{z(|u-1/2|+|v-1/2|+1-u-v)}\,du\,dv.
\]
It is nondecreasing in \(z\ge0\). The unsimplified second-order Duhamel estimate is
\[
 \|e^{-H/2}D^2\exp(H)[B,B]e^{-H/2}\|
 \le2\|B\|^2 I(\|H\|)\le2R^2 I(Rg).
 \tag{2.3}
\]
Thus (2.2) has norm at most
\[
 \frac12R^2 V''(r)+nR^2\frac gr I(Rg).
 \tag{2.4}
\]
No sign assertion about a general second derivative of the exponential is being used; (2.3)-(2.4) are operator norm bounds, sufficient for the lower Loewner bound.

Two elementary bounds will retain the sharp leading constant:
\[
 I(z)\le\tfrac12 e^{2z},\qquad
 I(z)\le\frac{e^{2z}}{4z^2}\bigl(1+z^2e^{-z}\bigr)\quad(z>0).
 \tag{2.5}
\]
The first uses the area \(1/2\) and the maximum exponent. For the second, the square \(0\le u,v\le1/2\) contributes at most \(e^{2z}/(4z^2)\), and the other two triangles together at most \(e^z/4\). In particular \(I(z)\sim e^{2z}/(4z^2)\).

## 3. Potential derivatives and explicit constants

In the remainder of the proof set \(b=b_*\). Write
\[
 q=\sqrt{L^2+r^2},\quad \ell=\log q,\quad \rho=r/q\in[0,1),
\]
\[
 B=\ell+\log\ell+b+1+1/\ell,
 \qquad A=1+1/\ell-1/\ell^2.
\]
Then
\[
 g=\rho B/a,\quad g/r=B/(a q),\quad
 V''(r)=\frac{\rho^2A+(1-\rho^2)B}{a q}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
At \(r=0\), use the continuous value \(g/r=B/(aL)\).

Here are explicit constants sufficient for all subsequent estimates. They can be made much smaller, but optimization is unnecessary for existence:
\[
 S_m=m(m+1)2^m,\qquad
 C_H=(50+4|b|)/a,
\]
\[
 D_0=C_H m a^2/2,\qquad
 J_0=(8/3)C_H m^3a^3,
\]
\[
 C_J=(e/2)D_0^2+J_0,
 \qquad C_E=2S_m C_J e^{b+2},
\]
\[
 C_T=C_E+3a/8,
 \qquad C_S=(na/4)e^{(b+2)/2}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]
Choose
\[
 \boxed{\begin{split}
 \ell_0=\max\{&64,\ e^{2|b|+4},\ \log(32a\sqrt m+e^2),\\
 &2\log(D_0+e),\ 2\log(16C_T+e),\ 4\log(4C_S+e)\},
 \qquad L\ge e^{\ell_0}.
 \end{split}}
 \tag{3.3}
\]
This is a finite, fully specified sufficient scale for every \(m,R\), independent of the operator tuple and Hilbert-space dimension. It is extremely large; no practical implementation conclusion is intended.

For every \(q\ge L\) the above choices ensure
\[
 \ell\ge64,\quad \log\ell\ge2|b|+4,
 \quad \ell\le B\le2\ell,
 \quad 0<A\le2.
 \tag{3.4}
\]
The bound on \(B\) follows directly: \(\log\ell+b>0\), while \(\log\ell\le\ell/2\) and \(|b|\le(\log\ell-4)/2\) imply \(\log\ell+|b|+1+1/\ell\le\ell\).

## 4. Uniform analytic Taylor remainder

Use the complex polydisc \(|y_j|\le a=2R\). Its Euclidean radius is at most \(a\sqrt m\le q/32\). Extend the potential holomorphically by
\[
 q(x-y)=\sqrt{L^2+\sum_j(x_j-y_j)^2}.
\]
On the larger complex Euclidean ball \(|y|\le q/8\), the estimates (4.3)-(4.6) of the main proof apply with \(e^2\) replaced by \(L^2\), real comparison radius \(q\), and \(h_1\) replaced by \(h_b\). In detail,
\[
 |q(x-y)^2-q^2|\le(17/64)q^2,
\]
so the square root, logarithm, and iterated logarithm have separated holomorphic branches. The previous Hessian estimate, with the additional \(bq\) term, is
\[
 \|D^2V(x-y)\|\le C_H\ell/q.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
The coefficient of \(|b|\) can be bounded by 4: the complex Hessian of the square root is bounded by less than \(4/q\) on this ball. The remaining coefficient is less than \(50/q\) times \(\ell\), exactly as in the main proof.

For \(|y|\le a\sqrt m\), each Hessian entry at \(x-ty\) has a complex coordinate circle of radius \(q/16\) inside the preceding ball: the center is displaced by at most \(q/32\), and \(q/32+q/16<q/8\). Cauchy's estimate gives
\[
 |\partial_k\partial_i\partial_jV(x-ty)|
 \le16 C_H\ell/q^2.
\]
Consequently, with the notation (2.1),
\[
 \sup|F_x|\le D_0\ell/q,
 \qquad \sup|F_x-Q_x|\le J_0\ell/q^2.
 \tag{4.2}
\]
The second bound uses Taylor's factor \(1/6\) and
\(\sum_j|y_j|\le ma\). Condition (3.3) makes \(D_0\ell/q\le1\): for \(\ell\ge64\), \(\ell\le e^{\ell/2}\), and \(D_0e^{-\ell/2}\le1\).

Since \(|e^{-z}-1+z|\le(e/2)|z|^2\) for \(|z|\le1\),
\[
 \sup_{|y_j|\le a}|J_x(y)|\le C_J\ell^2/q^2.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
Its Taylor coefficients of degrees zero, one, and two vanish exactly. The termwise distribution evaluation and norm convergence of Lemma 3.1 of the main proof therefore apply. Applying the same coefficient sum (allowing the harmless degree-two bound) gives the sandwiched remainder norm at most
\[
 2S_m C_J\frac{\ell^2}{q^2}
       \frac{e^{ag}}{(1+Rg)^2}
 \le C_E\frac{\ell^3}{q}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]
For the last inequality use \(ag=\rho B\le B\) and
\(e^B=q\ell e^{b+1+1/\ell}\le q\ell e^{b+2}\).

The commuting radial quadratic term in (2.4), by (3.1) and (3.4), is at most
\[
 \tfrac12 R^2V''(r)
 \le \frac{3a}{8}\frac\ell q.
\]
Thus the total of this radial term and the analytic remainder is bounded by
\[
 C_T\ell^3e^{-\ell}\le\frac{\log\ell}{16\ell}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]
Indeed \(\ell^4\le e^{\ell/2}\) for \(\ell\ge64\), and (3.3) gives \(16C_Te^{-\ell/2}\le1\le\log\ell\). This estimate holds at every point and is independent of \(r,L,A,\mathcal H\) once (3.3) holds.

## 5. The transverse term stays below one, including the critical tail

The remaining transverse term from (2.4) is
\[
 T(r)=\frac{naB}{4q}I(\rho B/2).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

If \(0\le\rho\le1/2\), the first inequality in (2.5) and \(B\le2\ell\) give
\[
 T(r)\le C_S\ell^{3/2}e^{-\ell/2}\le\tfrac14.
 \tag{5.2}
\]
Here \(\ell^{3/2}\le e^{\ell/4}\) for \(\ell\ge64\), and (3.3) gives \(C_Se^{-\ell/4}\le1/4\). With (4.5), the entire relative error is less than \(1/2\) in this region. This also covers \(r=0\) by continuity; no frame singularity is being used at the origin.

If \(1/2\le\rho<1\), the second inequality in (2.5) gives
\[
 T(r)\le \frac{na e^{b+1}}4
 \frac{e^{1/\ell}\ell}{B}(1+D_B)
 \rho^{-2}e^{-(1-\rho)B},
 \qquad D_B=(B^2/4)e^{-B/4}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]
Because \(b=b_*\), the first factor is exactly one. The function
\(\rho^{-2}e^{-(1-\rho)B}\) is increasing on \([1/2,1]\) when \(B\ge4\), as its logarithmic derivative is \(B-2/\rho\ge0\). It is therefore at most one.

For \(\ell\ge64\), the elementary inequality \(e^{1/\ell}\le1+1/\ell+1/\ell^2\) implies
\[
 B-\ell e^{1/\ell}\ge\log\ell+b\ge\tfrac12\log\ell.
\]
Since \(B\le2\ell\), it follows that
\[
 \frac{e^{1/\ell}\ell}{B}
 \le1-\frac{\log\ell}{4\ell}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]
Also \(\ell\le B\le2\ell\), and
\[
 D_B\le\ell^2e^{-\ell/4}\le\frac{\log\ell}{16\ell}
 \quad(\ell\ge64).
 \tag{5.5}
\]
For the last inequality, \(16\ell^3e^{-\ell/4}/\log\ell\) is decreasing above 64 and already less than one there.

Put \(d_\ell=\log\ell/(4\ell)\). Equations (5.3)-(5.5) give
\[
 T(r)\le(1-d_\ell)(1+d_\ell/4)
 \le1-3d_\ell/4.
\]
Adding the radial and analytic error bound (4.5), which is \(d_\ell/4\), proves
\[
 \boxed{\|e^{-H/2}E_xe^{-H/2}\|
 \le1-\frac{\log\ell}{8\ell}<1
 \quad\text{when }\rho\ge1/2.}
 \tag{5.6}
\]
Together with the inner-region bound this proves all-space positivity. In particular,
\[
 M_A(x)\succeq \eta_L(x)p_L(x)e^{A(\nabla V_L(x))}\succ0,
\]
where one may take \(\eta_L(x)=1/2\) in the inner region and \(\eta_L(x)=\log\ell/(8\ell)\) in the outer region. The strict inequality denotes a positive lower spectral bound at each finite \(x\), not a lower bound uniform in \(x\).

## 6. Density properties, normalization, and the exact envelope

For \(q\ge L\), \(h_b'(q)=B>0\) and \(h_b''(q)=A/q>0\). Composing with \(\sqrt{L^2+r^2}\) gives positive radial and angular Hessian eigenvalues; (3.1) makes them explicit. Thus \(V_L\) is smooth and strictly convex everywhere. It is superlinear, and every derivative of \(p_L\) decays faster than every exponential, exactly as in the main proof.

The all-space relative error is at most one, so
\[
 \|M_A(x)\|\le2p_L(x)e^{R|\nabla V_L(x)|}.
\]
This is integrable because \(|\nabla V_L(x)|=O(\log r+\log\log r)\) and \(V_L\) is superlinear. Norm continuity, the convolution Fourier identity, and evaluation at zero prove normalization, including arbitrary Hilbert dimension, just as in Section 5 of the main proof.

Finally, for fixed \(L\),
\[
 q_L(r)-r=O(L^2/r),\qquad h_b'(r)=O(\log r),
\]
so
\[
 h_b(q_L(r))-h_b(r)=O(L^2\log r/r)=o(1).
\]
It follows that
\[
 V_L(r)=\frac r a(\log r+\log\log r+b)-h_b(L)/a+o(1).
\]
Exponentiating gives (S5) with \(C_L=Z_L^{-1}e^{h_b(L)/a}\). The multiplicative \(1+o(1)\) guarantees an actual finite upper-envelope constant at \(b=b_*\). This closes the distinction between an asymptotic constant inside \(o(r)\) and the exact upper-envelope contract.

## 7. What this closes and what it does not

The leading critical coefficient \(1/(2R)\), the next coefficient one on \(\log\log r\), and the maximal fixed constant \(b_*\) are all attained within smooth radial strict log-concavity and all-exponential moments. These assertions are universal over the specified operator norm ball and all Hilbert dimensions. They use analytic all-space inequalities, not finite grids or a necessary moment condition alone.

This does not optimize the remaining multiplicative/sublinear tail corrections. For example, the theorem does not classify the smallest allowable polynomial prefactor multiplying the critical exponential, nor a vanishing correction to \(b_*\) whose product with \(r\) still diverges. It does not claim an efficient regularization scale or an efficient measurement synthesis. Exact historical priority has not been cleared.
