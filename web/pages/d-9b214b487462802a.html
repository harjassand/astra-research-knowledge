# Origin growth certificates: a sharp threshold and a residual escape control

Worker metric_geometry10, cycle14, 7 October2026. Exact global construction and failed-route boundary. No JC2/HC4 solution or counterexample. This note separates a usable sufficient test from an invariant that the residual class actually supplies.

## Interface and the missing global property

Let G:C²→C² be polynomial, G(0)=0, det JG a nonzero constant, and (G1,G2)=(x,y). The last identity is equivalent to a singleton reduced zero fibre. The residual-affine coefficient class is exactly G=(y,2x)D with D in SL2(C[x,y]) after normalizing det JG(0)=-2. There is no additional restriction on the completion D.

Write d=[C(x,y):C(G1,G2)]. If G is proper at0, its fibre degree there equals d. The unique zero is unramified of local degree1, so d=1. The standard birational Keller conclusion then gives a polynomial inverse. Equivalently, if d>1, every generic punctured target line through0 has d-1 escaping inverse sheets in addition to the single local inverse sheet. This is prior global geometry, reconstructed in hessian_check12/cycle13/GLOBAL_BOUNDARY_GATE.md and directly supported by Jelonek1993 Proposition6.

A short algebraic reconstruction of the final birational step is useful. A rational inverse coordinate is p/q with p,q coprime. If an irreducible nonconstant factor q0 divides q, then q0(G) is a nonconstant polynomial and has an irreducible divisor C. The identity p(G)=source_coordinate·q(G) makes p vanish on G(C). An everywhere nonsingular Jacobian preserves the local dimension of C, so its image is dense in the irreducible target divisor q0=0. Thus q0 divides p, a contradiction. Every inverse coordinate therefore has no denominator. The Jacobian and full affine polynomial source are essential to this argument.

## The inverse-row growth condition is sufficient

Suppose a polynomial matrix B satisfies

    z=B(z)G(z),  z=(x,y)^T,
    ||B(z)|| <= C(1+||z||) for all z.                     (1)

For ||z||>=1, equation (1) gives

    ||G(z)|| >= ||z||/[C(1+||z||)] >= 1/(2C).

Hence no unbounded sequence can have G(z)→0. A sufficiently small closed ball around0 has bounded, closed preimage, giving properness at0. Under the Keller/singleton assumptions above, G is invertible.

For rational B of degree<=1 an explicit rational C is available from sums of absolute coefficient values in the maximum norm. Finding a degree-one B is a finite linear system with12 unknown coefficients and O(d²) equations; alternatively solve6 unknowns for each of x and y. No hidden metric or condition bound is supplied.

### Decisive limitation: this is only a quadratic-degree filter

A global radial bound of order1 for a polynomial B forces degree B<=1. A nonzero highest homogeneous piece of degree>=2 grows too fast on a suitable complex ray.

Differentiating z=B(z)G(z) at0 gives

    B(0) JG(0)=I,

so det B is not the zero polynomial. Multiply by adj B:

    (det B)G = (adj B)z.

The right-hand side has total degree<=2. Polynomial degree in a domain is additive on nonzero products, so every component of G has degree<=2. Therefore (1) adds no degree class beyond the established quadratic Keller case. It cannot be advertised as a new all-degree properness mechanism. This exact degree obstruction changed the investigation's direction before adoption.

One could require linear growth only along arcs with G→0, but that condition is not supplied by D and is close to the desired origin-properness statement itself. No universal arc bound is claimed.

## Exact residual control showing the higher-degree threshold fails

Set

    G=(y+xy²,2x),  D=[[1,0],[y²/2,1]].

Then det D=1 and (y,2x)D=G. Its component ideal is (x,y), because x=G2/2 and y=G1-y²G2/2. At0 its Jacobian is -2 and its zero fibre is a reduced singleton. Nevertheless

    det JG=-2(1+2xy),
    (x,y)=(t,-1/t) gives G=(0,2t)→0 as t→0.

The Jacobian along the escaping arc is exactly2. There is a stronger local control:

    1+2 G1 G2=(1+2xy)².

Its critical-value curve is exactly 1+2uv=0, disjoint from a sufficiently small target neighbourhood U of0. Hence JG is nonzero at every point of G^{-1}(U), while the map is still nonproper at0 and has a singleton zero fibre. Thus even an everywhere unramified map over the entire small target neighbourhood, on the actual open subset of a global affine-plane source, does not force properness at0. The missing force must use the global Keller identity outside U as well. It vanishes elsewhere on the full source. This is an exact non-Keller control, not a counterexample to the global plane conjecture.

The residual matrix has degree2, and its inverse-row certificate contains y². Thus the global growth threshold in (1) is sharp for arbitrary residual completions once the full Keller assumption is dropped. The SL2 row form, maximal ideal and local invertibility alone supply no exclusion of an origin boundary point.

Its normalized map H=(x,y+xy²) even has the nonzero linear symmetry W=diag(1,-1). Thus the acquired-symmetry theorem in the companion note also needs the full global Keller identity: symmetry together with singleton zero and unramified geometry over a neighbourhood U does not suffice. The exact coefficient kernel has dimension1, while the Jacobian polynomial is nonconstant. A concrete candidate must pass the polynomial constant-Jacobian check before that theorem can be applied.

The actual Keller automorphism G=(y+(x+y²)^3,2(x+y²)) from the companion note has no degree-one Bezout matrix either. Exact coefficient ranks prove inconsistency for both source coordinates; its displayed polynomial inverse proves properness. Thus failure of (1) must never be used as a diagnosis of nonproperness.

## Saturation before specialization, reconstructed on the control

Use source projective coordinates [X:Y:Z] and target coordinates (u,v). Homogenizing the graph of the escaping control gives

    YZ²+XY²-uZ³=0,  2X-vZ=0.

Saturating by Z removes the component introduced at infinity. The graph closure has ideal

    2X-vZ,
    vY²+2YZ-2uZ².                                    (2)

This is verified independently by eliminating T from the homogenized graph together with TZ-1. At Z=0, equation (2) has X=0 and vY²=0. Since [X:Y:Z] is projective, Y!=0; hence the graph boundary projects exactly to the target line v=0. At the target origin it contains [0:1:0]. The explicit escaping arc limits to that point.

If the target zero ideal is specialized first, its affine ideal is (x,y). Its projective closure is simply [0:0:1], whose intersection with Z=0 is empty. That operation loses the escaping sheet. This universal order-of-operations issue is prior; our full exact control makes it concrete within the programme's residual row interface.

## An accessible resultant invariant, with the correct logical use

For any dominant polynomial plane map define

    R_x(x;u,v)=Res_y(G1-u,G2-v),
    R_y(y;u,v)=Res_x(G1-u,G2-v).

Take each actual nonzero generic resultant, with its actual generic degree in the remaining source variable; let A_x(u,v),A_y(u,v) be its leading coefficients. If

    A_x(0,0) A_y(0,0) !=0,                             (3)

then G is proper at0. Indeed, near0 these two leading coefficients are bounded away from zero while all other coefficients are bounded. The ordinary univariate Cauchy root bound bounds every source coordinate of every inverse point. This is a directly reconstructed sufficient certificate. A vanishing leading coefficient by itself is not used here to assert an escaping branch without a separate graph or minimal-equation check.

Jelonek1993 Proposition7 gives the exact nonproperness set from primitive irreducible minimal equations of all source coordinates. El Hilany–Tsigaridas2101.05245v3, Section2 Theorems2.3/2.5 and Algorithm2, supplies the corresponding complex plane resultant construction and a stated bit bound of soft-O(d^6 tau) for integer expanded input. We read these statements and the Section7 real/complex output comparison, not the entire sparse real algorithm. The manuscript says these complexity proofs are in an appendix, but no such appendix appears in our recovered32-page PDF; that sharp cost is retained as an attributed author theorem, without an independent complexity reconstruction. The real subset requires separate real branch tests; a complex boundary calculation must not be promoted to a real properness failure. Our sufficient direction (3) is proved without depending on the converse or on an unreviewed implementation.

For rational expanded coefficients, clearing denominators may increase the input bit length by O(d² tau) if tau bounds each individual numerator/denominator; that preprocessing charge precedes any integer-input complexity theorem. No fast execution of the generic graph saturation is claimed. Exact finite-bit charges for unspecified complex coefficients require an explicit representation.

There is also a fully elementary, deliberately loose polynomial-cost construction for (3). Each Sylvester matrix has at most2d rows and entries in Z[x,u,v] of total degree at most d. Its determinant has total degree at most2d², hence at most O(d^6) coefficients in three variables. Evaluate the determinant on an integer tensor grid with 2d²+1 values per variable and interpolate exactly. At each point matrix-entry bit length is O(B+d log d), where B is the cleared integer input bit length; the determinant and intermediate integer-minor bit lengths are O(d B+d² log d) by the permutation/Hadamard bounds. There are O(d^6) grid points, each determinant uses polynomial-size integer linear algebra, and tensor interpolation has polynomial degree/bit growth. Thus this direct sufficient test has a proved polynomial bit implementation, with a very conservative O(d^6) coefficient/value-slot storage bound, multiplied by the charged polynomial bit length of a slot. It does not establish the sharper soft-O(d^6 tau) bound and is not claimed to be competitive with the primary algorithms. No numerical root isolation or hidden branch bound is needed for the sufficient direction.

For the escaping control the exact equations are

    R_x=(2x-v)²,
    R_y=2u-vy²-2y,
    A_x=4, A_y=-v.

The product vanishes at0, and the graph/arc separately establishes genuine nonproperness there. The coordinate y's minimal equation has leading coefficient v, matching Jelonek's invariant.

Taking the leading coefficient must also precede target specialization. At u=v=0, the displayed R_y drops from degree2 to -2y of degree1. Recomputing a leading coefficient after that specialization would return -2 and falsely erase the escaping sheet. Use the leading coefficient of the generic equation, then evaluate it at0. This is the resultant version of the graph-saturation order gate.

For the degree-six residual Keller automorphism, the equations are

    R_x=64u²-16uv³+v^6-32v+64x,
    R_y=8u-v³-8y,
    A_x=64, A_y=-8.

Certificate (3) passes despite the vanishing linear-symmetry kernel and absent linear-growth Bezout certificate. These controls keep the three tests logically separate.

## Consequence, prior and next falsifiable step

The residual family supplies a polynomial inverse-row identity but no growth threshold, grading, or empty graph boundary. A proposed universal low-growth argument reduces to quadratic maps; an inherited-symmetry argument is refuted by an exact proper residual Keller member with kernel0. Those are precise obstructions, not reformulations claimed as discoveries.

Use the companion four-column acquired-symmetry test as a cheap one-sided filter. For any surviving concrete rational map with constant Jacobian, compute the two generic resultants and inspect (3) before investing in a full compactification. If they pass, the candidate is invertible. If they fail, a saturated graph or primitive minimal equation must retain an actual origin boundary point, and an actual collision must still be exhibited separately. For an arbitrary symbolic residual family, proving that (3) always passes would already be a substantive plane singleton-Keller theorem and is not established by these computations.
