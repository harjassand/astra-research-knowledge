# A strong star forces the log-mass proof to leave Qm>=0

7 October 2026, cycle15, metric_acquisition. Our deduction. Exact proof-route obstruction; peer reconstruction requested. It is not a counterexample to E_F, C-matrix, L_V or pencil nonvanishing, and no such detector was tested here.

## The tempting extension and its precise failure

For every Stieltjes Q=I-B>0, B symmetric off-diagonal nonnegative, Section1 of WEAK_STIELTJES_FLOW_NONVANISHING.md proves log h(e^z) concave on H>0. Section2 proves log f(e^z) convex modulo scale where Qm>0. In fact its two-Cauchy proof extends to Qm>=0: diag(c) can be semidefinite, f=sum c>0, and equality in the strictly positive C=PQP inner-product Cauchy still forces a constant direction.

The domain

    C_Q={z: sum_j B_ij exp(z_j-z_i)<=1 for every i}

is convex because each defining exponential sum is convex. On it Qm>=0, K=8Q+4diag(Qm/m)>0, and

    G(z)=log h(e^z)-(n/2)log f(e^z)

is strictly concave transverse to constant shifts. This does not suffice to make its maximum an unconstrained stationary point. The following exact graph has a unique constrained maximum on the boundary of C_Q.

## Full star calculation

Take k>=5 leaves and n=k+1, with

    Q=I-t A_star,  t>0, q=k t^2<1.

Here A_star joins the center0 to every leaf and has no other edges. The Schur complement makes Q>0 exactly when q<1. At the positive masses

    m_0=k t,  m_i=1 for i>=1,

we have

    (Qm)_0=0, (Qm)_i=1-q>0,
    f=k(1-q), c_0=0, c_i=1-q.

The rescaled Hessian K=Y^-1 H Y^-1 has center diagonal8, leaf diagonal4(3-q), and center-leaf entry-8t. Its center Schur complement is

    8-k(8t)^2/[4(3-q)] =24(1-q)/(3-q)>0.

Thus H>0 although the center Qm constraint is active. To compute the exact logdet gradient, put A=YHY. Its center diagonal is8kq, each leaf diagonal4(3-q), and center-leaf entry-8q. Block inversion gives

    xi_0=(A^-1)_00=(3-q)/[24kq(1-q)],
    xi_leaf=1/[4(3-q)]+q/[6k(3-q)(1-q)].

The already derived identity alpha_i=partial_(z_i)logh=1+4 sum_j B_ij m_i m_j(xi_i-xi_j) therefore yields

    alpha_0=[9-(2k+1)q]/[2(3-q)].                       (1)

Choose q>9/(2k+1), still less than1. Then alpha_0<0. Shift homogeneity and leaf symmetry give sum alpha=n and all alpha_leaf=(n-alpha_0)/k. Consequently

    grad G=(alpha_0,-alpha_0/k,...,-alpha_0/k).

The active constraint phi_0=sum_i t m_i/m_0<=1 has value1 and gradient

    grad phi_0=(-1,1/k,...,1/k).

Thus grad G=(-alpha_0)grad phi_0, a positive outward multiplier. For every feasible z, convexity of phi_0 gives grad phi_0(z_*) dot(z-z_*)<=0. Concavity of G then gives G(z)<=G(z_*)+gradG(z_*) dot(z-z_*)<=G(z_*). Strict transverse concavity proves uniqueness modulo the radial constant shift. This proves a boundary maximum by a global supporting-hyperplane argument, not merely a negative derivative at one fixture.

## What remains open

This star violates the small normalized row bound in the new theorem. It blocks extending that theorem by merely replacing the ratio box with the whole Qm>=0 log-convex domain and declaring its maximum interior. It does not say that the unrestricted H>0 maximum is degenerate, that a full-support attracting ray is absent, or that any acquisition detector vanishes. Indeed a connected nonpositive graph forces every nonzero point of the closure of a positive-Hessian orthant component to have full support: a zero coordinate adjacent to a positive mass would make H_ii=4(Qm)_i<0. Compactness can therefore furnish a full-support maximum, but its nondegeneracy or a common open attracting basin still requires a new argument. No continuum-of-maxima assumption is supplied.
