# Root gate of the quiver-wide random-prime corollary

For an integer representation of any finite quiver, write D_K for the set of
dimension vectors of K-subrepresentations and T_K=conv(D_K). Vertexwise sum
and intersection preserve subrepresentations. For ANY signed real objective
sigma, its value on dimension vectors is modular. Two maximizers therefore
have maximizing sum/intersection. A unique largest maximizer exists because
dimensions are bounded. An algebraic point of its fixed-dimension rational
Grassmann chart locus gives the same maximizer; uniqueness makes its RREF
Galois invariant and hence rational. Thus T_Q=T_C. This does not assert
D_Q=D_C, which is false already for a rational rotation matrix.

For a rational subrepresentation, its saturated integral lattices at each
vertex retain rank modulo EVERY prime. Integer arrow maps preserve these
lattices. This gives D_Q subset D_Fp without a separate arrow-rank condition.
The possible rank drops are covered by the later empty-locus argument.

A product of standard Grassmann charts has identity pivot rows. Arrow closure
is exactly M_a Y_tail=Y_head (M_a Y_tail)_pivot. It uses degree<=2 equations,
<=A^2 variables and <=E A^2 entries, where A is total vertex dimension and
E is the arrow count. Coefficient sums increase bit height by at most
O(log(A+1)). Summing chart counts over ALL dimension vectors gives exactly
2^A. There are no hidden inverse matrices, rank constraints on Y, or
extension-field point assumptions.

Apply the same effective arithmetic Nullstellensatz to every C-empty chart.
The worker's C_q and B_q dominate the total count of its exceptional prime
divisors; their bit lengths are polynomial in A,H,log(E+1). For a prime
outside this union, every Fp-subrepresentation dimension vector occurs over
C, so D_Fp subset D_C. Combining the three inclusions proves

    T_Q subset T_Fp subset T_C = T_Q.

The sampler therefore preserves all signed supporting-function values and
all King stability inequalities simultaneously. It does not preserve every
individual dimension vector, recover a rational invariant subspace from a
modular one, or supply a new optimizer. Binary-large weight values do not
enlarge the bad-prime set, but may still enlarge a downstream optimizer's cost.

An exact input-encoding caveat was sent to the producer: when isolated vertex
dimensions are encoded in binary, total A can exceed the size of the dense
arrow data. Remove isolated vertices before computing the bound; their
polytope factors [0,alpha_v] are known and field independent. For all remaining
positive-dimensional active vertices, A is bounded by twice the dense arrow
entry count. Zero-dimensional vertices are removed first. This makes the
polynomial-bit assertion an assertion about actual expanded input length,
not an uncharged numerical dimension. Alternatively one may explicitly
parameterize complexity by expanded A. No mathematical polytope statement
changes under this bookkeeping repair.

The reusable method is to preserve ALL excluded geometric profiles with one
random prime, while using a separately proved specialization inclusion to
recover equality of convex invariants. Preserving nonempty geometric profiles
pointwise would be a stronger and generally false inference. The loop matrix
[[0,-1],[1,0]] has complex/mod5 invariant lines but no rational/mod3 line;
every corresponding dimension polytope is still [0,2].

Prior arithmetic-nullstellensatz, canonical-maximizer descent and stability
theory are named dependencies. The generality is a useful new deduction in

The companion rank-three coordinate-star degree obstruction does not defeat
