# Finite-cut transfer for real L1 targets

**Status:** internally reconstructed candidate deduction from the pinned family-332 proof. The release theorem is source-reported and has not received external proof validation here. Historical priority of the consequences below is `UNKNOWN`.

## Claim A: ambient L1 has metric Markov cotype two

Let \((\Omega,\mu)\) be any measure space and let \(X=L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)\). For every finite family \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X\), every integer \(t\geq1\), and every row-stochastic matrix \(A\) with stationary probability vector \(\pi\), there are \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\in X\) such that

\[
\sum_i\pi_i\|x_i-y_i\|_1^2+t\sum_{i,j}\pi_i a_{ij}\|y_i-y_j\|_1^2
\leq 3024\sum_{i,j}\pi_i\left(\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^t A^s\right)_{ij}\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2.
\]

Thus \(N_2(L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R))\leq\sqrt{3024}=12\sqrt{21}\). The release definition asks for reversibility; the pinned proof's final remark shows stationarity alone suffices, and the argument below preserves that scope.

### Ambient cut reconstruction

Choose measurable representatives of the finite family. For every nonempty proper subset \(B\subsetneq[n]\), set

\[
v(\omega)=\min_i x_i(\omega),\qquad
b_B(\omega)=\left(\min_{i\in B}x_i(\omega)-\max_{j\notin B}x_j(\omega)\right)_+.
\]

These functions are measurable. Pointwise,
\(|v|\leq\sum_i|x_i|\) and \(0\leq b_B\leq2\sum_i|x_i|\), so \(v,b_B\in L_1\). Put \(w_B=\|b_B\|_1\), discard zero weights, and define the binary cut code \(z_i(B)=\mathbf1_{i\in B}\). Give \(\mathbb R^{\mathcal B}\) the weighted norms

\[
\|u\|_{1,w}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}w_B|u_B|,
\qquad
\|u\|_H^2=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}w_Bu_B^2.
\]

At each \(\omega\), list the distinct values among \(x_1(\omega),\ldots,x_n(\omega)\) as \(\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_m\). The only positive \(b_B(\omega)\) are the upper level cuts \(B_r=\{i:x_i(\omega)\geq\alpha_{r+1}\}\), and their weights are \(\alpha_{r+1}-\alpha_r\). They telescope, giving

\[
x_i=v+\sum_B z_i(B)b_B,
\qquad
|x_i-x_j|=\sum_B|z_i(B)-z_j(B)|b_B
\quad\text{pointwise.}
\]

Define the affine recovery map

\[
T(u)=v+\sum_Bu_Bb_B.
\]

Then \(T(z_i)=x_i\), and

\[
\|T(u)-T(u')\|_1
\leq\sum_Bw_B|u_B-u'_B|=\|u-u'\|_{1,w}.
\]

After integrating the pointwise cut identity,

\[
\|x_i-x_j\|_1=\|z_i-z_j\|_{1,w}=\|z_i-z_j\|_H^2,
\]

where the last equality uses that the cut coordinates are binary. This is the key transfer: it is an exact representation of the finite metric together with a contractive map back to the *given ambient* L1 space.

### Reuse of the released energy proof

The remaining proof depends only on the finite weighted cut data, the identities above, and contractivity of \(T\). Let \(\varphi(r)=3r^2-2r^3\), applied coordinatewise as \(\Phi\) on \([0,1]^{\mathcal B}\). Since \(\varphi'(0)=\varphi'(1)=0\), the released quartic-potential estimate bounds squared weighted-L1 increments by 108 times the corresponding fourth-power Hilbert-potential increment. Along the stopped-walk martingale, the linear term vanishes conditionally and the potential telescopes. With killing probability \(1/(t+1)\), the expected jump sum is exactly \(B+tE_A\), the two left-hand terms before applying \(T\). The terminal cost is at most \(108\,\mathbb E\|x_{X_S}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2\).

For a stationary chain and independent geometric \(S\) with \(\Pr[S=s]=(t+1)^{-1}(t/(t+1))^s\), the released endpoint comparison gives

\[
\mathbb E\|x_{X_S}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2
\leq 28\,\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^t\mathbb E\|x_{X_s}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2.
\]

Contractivity of \(T\) therefore gives the displayed inequality with \(108\cdot28=3024\). Reversibility is not used after stationarity.

## Claim B: finite-range maps from Markov-type-2 sources extend into any real L1

Let \((X,d_X)\) be any metric space with Markov type two constant \(M_2(X)<\infty\), let \(S\subseteq X\), and let \(f:S\to L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)\) be Lipschitz with finite range \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_r\}\). Then there exists an extension \(F:X\to L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)\) with

\[
F|_S=f,\qquad \operatorname{Lip}(F)\leq C_{MN}\,M_2(X)\,12\sqrt{21}\,\operatorname{Lip}(f),
\]

where \(C_{MN}<\infty\) is the unspecified universal factor in Mendel–Naor's Corollary 1.13. For a real Hilbert source, \(M_2(H)=1\), so this recovers a universal Lipschitz extension bound. Neither source states a numerical value for the universal extension factor.

**Proof.** Apply the ambient cut construction to the \(r\) distinct range values. It gives a finite weighted space \(E=\ell_1(\mathcal B,w)\), with \(|\mathcal B|\leq2^r-2\), cut codes \(z_i\in E\), and a 1-Lipschitz affine map \(T:E\to L_1(\Omega,\mu)\) satisfying \(T(z_i)=x_i\) and \(\|z_i-z_j\|_E=\|x_i-x_j\|_1\). Rescale coordinates to identify \(E\) isometrically with \(\ell_1^d\), then embed \(\ell_1^d\) as the first \(d\) coordinates of \(\ell_1\). Apply Mendel–Naor's Corollary 1.13 to the coded map, using that the pinned release proves \(N_2(\ell_1)\leq12\sqrt{21}\) and \(\ell_1=c_0^*\). It gives a global extension \(G:X\to\ell_1\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(C_{MN}M_2(X)12\sqrt{21}\operatorname{Lip}(f)\). Project \(G\) onto its first \(d\) coordinates; this projection is contractive and fixes all coded values. Undo the coordinate rescaling and compose with \(T\). The resulting map extends \(f\), and neither the projection nor \(T\) increases the Lipschitz constant. The range size controls the auxiliary dimension, while the extension constant remains independent of \(r\).

This is a global extension theorem for finite-range maps. It does not prove a global extension theorem for arbitrary infinite-range maps into non-dual L1 targets.

## Prior-art boundary and possible falsifier

The 2013 Mendel–Naor extension theorem gives finite partial extensions for general metric-Markov-cotype targets; its global Corollary 1.13 requires a dual Banach target. The pinned release uses \(\ell_1=c_0^*\) to obtain a global result. The factorization above transfers that global result to finite-range maps into any real L1 target without asserting that the whole L1 space is a dual.

The cut mechanism is ambient. Its recovery map may leave a proper subspace \(Y\subset L_1\); therefore the proof does not establish metric Markov cotype for all subspaces of L1. Mendel–Naor's Kalton example is a known obstruction: a closed subspace of L1 fails metric Markov cotype at every finite exponent.

A direct falsifier for the reconstruction lemma would be a finite real-valued family and indices \(i,j\) for which the integrated cut sum differs from \(\|x_i-x_j\|_1\), or for which \(T(z_i)\ne x_i\). The sorted-level proof rules these out for every finite family. The exact three-value fixture \((0,1,2)\) on a unit-measure atom gives weights 1 on the upper cuts \(\{3\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), and recovers pairwise distances 1, 1, and 2. This is only a sanity check, not evidence for the universal claim.
