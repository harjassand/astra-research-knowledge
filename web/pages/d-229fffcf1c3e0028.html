# Recovery costs in budget-aligned proof-search training

Status, 7 October 2026: the recent paper's common deterministic-cost theorem reconstructs. Its supplementary interpretation in terms of bounded stochastic excursions, global restarts, or a mean excursion cost does not follow. Two exact two-step examples reject the heterogeneous-cost gradient bracket. A common iid, policy-independent miss-cost law admits a correct posterior-mixture extension. These are mathematical interface results, not evidence of a trained prover improvement or a historical novelty claim.

## Exact interface

There are (L\) demonstrated steps. Step (t\) independently succeeds with probability (p_t\in(0,1)\), costs one unit on success, and advances to step (t+1\). A miss costs a positive integer and then returns to the **same step**, retaining the already completed prefix. No alternate proof, absorbing branch, selection derivative, or root restart is included. The initial budget is the integer (N\ge L\). Write (q_t=1-p_t\) and

\[
w_t(J)=p_t\partial_{p_t}\log J.
\]

All other success probabilities and all miss-cost laws are held fixed. This is the source's derivative convention, not a theorem about parameter-space training gradients.

For deterministic step-dependent costs (k_t\ge1\), the complete outcome has (m_t\) misses before the success at step (t\), with probability (\prod_t p_tq_t^{m_t}\). Thus

\[
J_N=\Big(\prod_t p_t\Big)
 \sum_{\sum_t k_tm_t\le N-L}\prod_tq_t^{m_t},\qquad
w_t(J_N)=1-\frac{p_t}{q_t}
 E[m_t\mid\text{completion by }N].                    \tag{1}
\]

The cost constraint does not depend on (p\), so (1) follows by differentiating the finite sum. Monotone coupling gives (w_t\ge0\); (1) gives (w_t\le1\). The narrower comparison with unit-cost or all-maximum-cost *gradients* is the issue, not these elementary bounds.

## Two counterexamples to a heterogeneous bounded-cost extension

All probabilities below are (p_1=p_2=1/2\). The costs lie in ([1,2]\), return to the same state, are deterministic, and have no bypass or absorption.

1. (N=4\), (k=(2,1)\). The admissible miss-count pairs are ((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(0,2)\). Hence

\[
J=p_1p_2(1+q_1+q_2+q_2^2)=9/16,
\qquad w_1=7/9.
\]

The all-maximum-cost comparator has effective unit budget (L+(N-L)/2=3\) and weight (3/4\). Thus the proposed bounded-cost upper bracket fails by (7/9-3/4=1/36\). Yet the **probability** sandwich is sound: (1/2\le9/16\le11/16\), where the endpoints are the all-cost-two and all-unit processes.

2. (N=5\), (k=(1,2)\). The admissible polynomial is

\[
J=p_1p_2(1+q_1+q_1^2+q_1^3+q_2+q_1q_2)=21/32,
\qquad w_1=8/21.
\]

The all-unit process at budget five has weight (5/13\), so the proposed lower bracket fails by (5/13-8/21=1/273\). Again (1/2\le21/32\le13/16\) is a valid success-probability sandwich.

Ordering positive objectives does not order logarithmic derivatives. This remains a load-bearing obstruction if one attempts to transfer the source's gradient statement merely from a pathwise cost bound. `logic_gate14` independently enumerated these exact finite models; its result is `results/logic_gate14/cycle17/HETEROGENEOUS_TRAP_GRADIENT_AUDIT.json`. That peer check explicitly did not read or adjudicate the source theorem.

## A mean cost does not determine the gradient

Even with one step and a common iid cost independent of (p\), the mean is insufficient. At (N=3,p=1/2\), let each miss cost one or three with probability (1/2\) each. The mean is two. Completion can use zero misses, one cost-one miss, or two cost-one misses, giving

\[
J_{\rm random}=p(1+q/2+q^2/4)=21/32,
\qquad w_{\rm random}=5/7.
\]

A deterministic miss cost two instead gives (J_{\rm fixed}=p(1+q)=3/4\) and (w_{\rm fixed}=2/3\). Thus the source Appendix I's mean-cost substitution is a distinct heuristic objective. It is not the exact gradient of the random-cost deployment from the mean alone.

## Correct common-iid extension: renewal capacity and posterior mixing

**Lemma.** Suppose every miss, regardless of its step, draws an iid cost (K\in\{1,\ldots,k_{\max}\}\) from a common distribution independent of all tactic-success draws and all (p_t\). It returns to the same step. Put (B=N-L\). Draw an infinite independent cost sequence (K_1,K_2,\ldots\), and define

\[
M=\max\{m\ge0:K_1+\cdots+K_m\le B\},\qquad
\alpha_m=P(M=m).
\]

Then (\lfloor B/k_{\max}\rfloor\le M\le B\), the law (\alpha\) is independent of (p\), and with the paper's unit-cost retry objective (\widehat J_n\),

\[
J_N=\sum_m\alpha_m\widehat J_{L+m},\qquad
w_t(J_N)=\sum_m
 \frac{\alpha_m\widehat J_{L+m}}{J_N}
 \widehat w_t(L+m).                                  \tag{2}
\]

Consequently the bounded-cost gradient bracket is valid for this common-iid interface:

\[
\widehat w_t(N)\le w_t(J_N)
 \le\widehat w_t\big(L+\lfloor(N-L)/k_{\max}\rfloor\big). \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Let (G_t\) be the independent geometric number of trials needed at each step. The total number of misses is (U=\sum_t(G_t-1)\). It is independent of the pre-drawn cost sequence. The total cost is (L+\sum_{j=1}^U K_j\), so completion is precisely (U\le M\). Conditional on (M=m\), its probability is (\widehat J_{L+m}\). This proves the first identity. Differentiate the finite mixture, since (\alpha\) is independent of (p\), and divide by (J_N\) to obtain the second. These are *success-posterior* weights, not the prior probabilities (\alpha_m\) alone. The monotonicity of the unit retry weights then yields (3). □

For completeness, that monotonicity also reconstructs independently. If (S_L=\sum_tG_t\) and (G_t'\) is an independent copy, direct differentiation of the geometric pmf gives

\[
\widehat w_t(n)=1-
 \frac{P(S_L+G_t'\le n)}{P(S_L\le n)}.                \tag{4}
\]

The pmf of each positive geometric variable is log-concave with no internal zeros. Convolution preserves this property: a sequence's Toeplitz matrix has nonnegative (2\times2\) minors exactly when its adjacent positive ratios decrease, and the Cauchy–Binet formula preserves these minors under multiplying Toeplitz matrices. At each fixed index only finitely many summands contribute here. Convolution with the unit step sequence similarly shows that the CDF (F(n)=P(S_L\le n)\) is log-concave. Thus (F(n-k)/F(n)\) is nondecreasing in (n\) for each (k\ge1\) (a product of nondecreasing adjacent inverse ratios). Averaging over (G_t'\) in (4) shows that (\widehat w_t(n)\) is nonincreasing. This is also the source's Appendix C “CAT weight as criticality” lemma, lines 890–909.

The common deterministic theorem is recovered by taking (K\equiv\kappa\). The two heterogeneous counterexamples show that “common” is consequential. The same-mean example shows that knowledge of (E[K]\) does not acquire (\alpha\). The lemma and its synthesis have not been surveyed exhaustively in renewal/search-control literature; historical novelty is **UNKNOWN**.

## Exact supplied-law algorithm and costs

For finite step-specific miss laws (a_{t,r}\) independent of (p\), the correct recurrence is

\[
F_t(n)=p_tF_{t+1}(n-1)+q_t\sum_r a_{t,r}F_t(n-r),     \tag{5}
\]

with (F_{L+1}(n)=1\) for (n\ge0\) and (F_t(n)=0\) for (n<0\). Every positive cost decreases the budget, so (5) is an acyclic dynamic program. With at most (s\) cost atoms per step, values and a reverse derivative pass cost (O(LNs)\) rational arithmetic operations and (O(LN)\) cells; direct forward differentiation of all (L\) parameters costs (O(L^2Ns)\). The executable uses the latter simple version and charges it. Budget (N\) is numeric in these costs, not its bit length.

For rational probabilities, let (D\) be a common multiple of all supplied probability denominators. Each miss transition has denominator dividing (D^2\); every path has at most (N\) transitions. A common denominator (D^{2N}\) therefore suffices for the value sums and derivative polynomials, with polynomial intermediate bit lengths (O(N\log D+\log(LN))\). Computing (D\), reading all atoms, and rational arithmetic are charged; no unit-cost arbitrary real input oracle is silently substituted. Absorbing branches can be added explicitly. A root restart requires the appropriate prefix/configuration state, not only a scalar mean cost.

## Why a global restart is a different interface

A cap bounds the length of one search block; it does not uniformly bound the time needed to re-acquire an already completed prefix. For a two-step proof and cap two, each independent block can succeed only by two successive successes, with probability (a=p_1p_2\). At total budget four, two blocks give

\[
J_{\rm restart}=1-(1-a)^2=7/16,
\qquad w_1=w_2=6/7
\]

at (p_1=p_2=1/2\). Same-state retained-prefix retry at budget four instead has (J=11/16\) and (w_1=w_2=6/11\). If step two misses and the process restarts, any number (h\) of additional blocks can fail to reacquire step two through root misses; the event has positive probability (q_1^{2h}\), with arbitrarily large wasted cost. Its law depends on other demonstrated probabilities, violating the derivative-independent common-cost assumption. A cap smaller than the proof length can prevent trace completion outright. When a block has positive success probability, infinitely many independent restarts still give vanishing weights at large budget; this note does **not** assert the contrary.

## Consequence, comparator and falsifiable next step

The tractable scientific result is an assumption boundary and an exact renewal correction to compute-aligned trace training. It can improve future proof-search experiments by distinguishing supplied trace likelihoods from acquired recovery laws. The strongest same-information comparator is the source's general configuration Bellman recurrence plus its unit-retry and UA objectives. A scalar hand-tuned recovery prior remains a permissible heuristic; the exact theorem needs the acquired return law or the stated deterministic/common-iid assumptions.

Next discriminating experiment: before any expensive fine-tuning, measure kernel-checked return-to-prefix and bypass outcomes at the demonstrated states, record complete miss-cost distributions and restart losses, and compare gradients from (5) with unit retry, common-iid renewal mixing, UA, CE and the same deterministic-mean prior on held-out deviations. Kernel/sample/oracle costs and law-estimation uncertainty must be paid. A finite renewal check does not establish that real best-first configurations possess this reduced interface. No training or deployment advantage is claimed from the present result.
