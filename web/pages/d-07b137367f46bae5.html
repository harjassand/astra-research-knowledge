PROVENANCE AND SCOPE

The card explicitly says the linked technical note, code, result card, verification output, and ZIP were not supplied. I did not assume that packet exists or claim its diagnostics were replayed. My derivation below starts from the formula and identities in the supplied summary and checks the established heat-flow monotonicity against its primary journal source.

The source interface is a centered compactly supported log-concave probability μ on R^d with a Lebesgue density. Write
  P0 = optimal Poincare coefficient of μ,
  σ² = ||Cov(μ)||op,
  μ_s = law(X + sqrt(s) G), X~μ, G~N(0,I) independent.
The reconstructed claim is, for every 0 < s < P0,
  P0 <= P(μ_s) <= P0 + σ² s/(P0-s).                    (1)
All dimensions and all measures meeting the stated interface are quantified. The Gaussian noise covariance is sI. P denotes the variance coefficient, not its square root.

DERIVATION OF (1)

1. The lower bound and the zero-time boundary.

Klartag–Putterman prove that for log-concave μ, its Poincare coefficient is nondecreasing under convolution with a centered Gaussian; hence P(μ_s) >= P0. This is the heat-flow monotonicity theorem, not the new covariance estimate.

For the derivative argument at s>0 the smoothed density is positive and smooth. The endpoint at s=0 can be handled without assuming derivative regularity at zero. If f is a smooth test function, conditional variance and the Gaussian Poincare inequality give
  Var f(X+sqrt(s)G)
   = E_G Var_X f(X+sqrt(s)G)
      + Var_G E_X f(X+sqrt(s)G)
   <= P0 E|grad f(X+sqrt(s)G)|²
      + s E|E_X grad f(X+sqrt(s)G)|²
   <= (P0+s) E|grad f(X+sqrt(s)G)|².
Thus P(μ_s) <= P0+s. Together with monotonicity this proves P(μ_s) -> P0 as s down to zero and justifies integrating a bound first obtained for positive times.

2. Contact inequality for a first eigenfunction.

Fix a regular time s>0. Let ρ_s be the density, V_s=-log ρ_s, K_s=Hess V_s, and
  L_s = Δ - grad V_s . grad,
the reversible generator in L2(μ_s). Set P_s=P(μ_s), λ=1/P_s, and choose a normalized mean-zero first eigenfunction φ:
  -L_s φ = λ φ,     E φ²=1.
Put w=grad φ. Log-concavity gives K_s >= 0. The integrated Bochner identity and the eigenfunction equation yield
  λ²
   = E ||Hess φ||HS² + E<w,K_s w>.
Apply the Poincare inequality for μ_s, with coefficient P_s=1/λ, to each scalar component w_i:
  E ||Hess φ||HS²
   = sum_i E|grad w_i|²
   >= λ sum_i Var(w_i)
   = λ(E|w|²-|Ew|²).
The eigenfunction energy identity gives E|w|²=λ. Substitution proves
  E<w,K_s w> <= λ |Ew|².                                 (2)
This is the gradient-eigenfunction contact inequality used by N75. It is stronger than merely dropping the Hessian term from Bochner.

3. Covariance bounds the mean gradient.

Let Y=X+sqrt(s)G and b_s(Y)=E[X|Y]. Differentiating the Gaussian convolution gives
  grad V_s(Y)=(Y-b_s(Y))/s.
Integration by parts and the eigenfunction equation, tested against each coordinate of Y, give
  Ew = λ E[φ Y],
  Ew = (E[φ Y]-E[φ b_s])/s,
and therefore
  (1-λ s) E[φ Y] = E[φ b_s].
Because P_s>=P0>s, 1-λs=(P_s-s)/P_s>0, so
  Ew = E[φ b_s]/(P_s-s).                                  (3)
The conditional expectation is centered and is an L2 contraction:
  E[b_s b_s^T] <= E[XX^T] = Cov(μ) <= σ² I.
For any unit vector u, Cauchy–Schwarz and Eφ²=1 imply
  |u^T E[φ b_s]|²
    <= E[(u^T b_s)²] <= σ².
Taking the supremum over u in (3),
  |Ew|² <= σ²/(P_s-s)².                                    (4)
This is dimension-free; it uses the full conditional-mean vector, not d coordinatewise bounds.

4. Heat derivative and integration.

For a simple first eigenvalue, the heat-flow eigenvalue variation formula is
  λ'(s) = -E<w,K_s w>.
At eigenvalue crossings the same bound applies to the one-sided derivatives through the first eigenspace; the contact estimate (2) holds for every normalized first eigenfunction. Consequently, wherever P_s is differentiable,
  P_s' = E<w,K_s w>/λ²
       <= |Ew|²/λ
       <= σ² P_s/(P_s-s)².                                 (5)
The same inequality for the upper derivative is enough to integrate the locally Lipschitz spectral curve. The function x/(x-s)² is decreasing for x>s, and P_s>=P0, so
  P_s' <= σ² P0/(P0-s)².
With t as the integration variable, for 0<s<P0,
  P_s-P0 <= σ² P0 integral_0^s (P0-t)^(-2) dt
           = σ² s/(P0-s),
which proves (1).

The regular-time calculation uses the usual eigenfunction-domain, differentiation, and spectral-perturbation justifications for a smooth confining density. Gaussian convolution of compactly supported μ has Gaussian tails. The displayed conditional-variance estimate supplies the s down to zero boundary. A fully formal treatment of arbitrary first-eigenvalue multiplicities and all approximation limits should still be independently checked against the complete technical note, which was not supplied; the derivation above makes the exact finite-dimensional inequalities and the required analytic gate explicit.

PLATEAU CONSEQUENCE AND CURRENT STATUS

If a sequence of admissible measures had P0=1 and σ² -> 0, then for every fixed 0<s<1,
  1 <= P(μ_s) <= 1 + σ² s/(1-s) -> 1.                    (6)
The covariance of μ_s is Cov(μ)+sI, so its operator norm is σ²+s. Equation (6) is only a conditional implication: it does not construct such a KLS-counterexample sequence.

Current-source check changes the status recorded in N75. ArXiv:2610.05474v1, submitted 4 Oct 2026, now claims the full KLS theorem for every isotropic log-concave measure (Theorem 1.1, equivalently P(ν)<=C||Cov(ν)||op for general log-concave ν). The authors disclose that most proofs and mathematical ideas were found by ChatGPT, with the suspension idea attributed to the authors. I have not audited this 32-page proof, its imported Song–Zhang criterion, correctness, or priority; it is an unreviewed primary preprint claim, not a verified theorem in this report. If its theorem is correct, (6) rules out the hypothetical sequence: applying KLS to μ_s gives P_s<=C(σ²+s); choose fixed s<1/(2 max(C,1)) and let σ²->0, contradicting P_s->1. Thus the old “KLS remains open” label in the N75 summary is temporally stale, while the conditional heat-flow estimate remains a coherent lemma.

THE DEEPER INFORMATION ATTACK

N75's requested downstream step is not supplied by (1). The reported construction has Gaussian observations
  Z_T | Y ~ N(0, I + f_L(T R(Y)^2))
and needs a scale-integrated upper bound on mutual information. Its missing premise is a log-Sobolev or otherwise entropy-controlling inequality for the observation likelihoods. A Poincare inequality controls variance. Applied to square-root likelihoods it can control an affinity/Hellinger quantity, but KL mutual information can be larger by an unbounded factor without a likelihood-ratio/tail envelope.

A compact log-concave witness rules out the generic shortcut “uniform Poincare plus covariance gives a uniform log-Sobolev constant.” Let
  dμ_L(x) = e^{-x} 1_[0,L](x) dx/(1-e^{-L}).
Translation centers it without changing either constant. Its one-dimensional log-concave isoperimetric ratio is at least 1, so Cheeger's inequality gives P(μ_L)<=4 for all L; its variance tends to 1. Yet with a=1/2-1/L and f_L(x)=c_L exp(ax), normalized by E_{μ_L} f_L²=1, the tilted density f_L² dμ_L is proportional to exp(-2x/L) on [0,L]. Hence
  E_{f²μ_L} x = (L/2)(1-3e^-2)/(1-e^-2),
  Ent_{μ_L}(f_L²)
    = log((1-e^-L)/[(L/2)(1-e^-2)])
      +(1-2/L) E_{f²μ_L} x,
  E_{μ_L}|f_L'|² = a².
Thus the optimal LSI parameter in the convention Ent(f²)<=2 rho_LS² E|grad f|² satisfies rho_LS² >= Ent(f_L²)/(2a²) = Omega(L), despite uniformly bounded Poincare coefficient and covariance. The measures are compactly supported and log-concave but do not have a uniform subgaussian parameter. This is a precise obstruction to deriving LSI from Poincare/covariance alone; it does not refute a bound specialized to N75's actual likelihood family or a uniformly subgaussian subclass.

A separate channel calculation identifies why average Hellinger by itself cannot replace mutual information. Let B~Bernoulli(p), Z|B=0~N(0,1), and Z|B=1~N(0,M²). A threshold at |Z|=sqrt(M) classifies B with error tending to zero as M grows, so I(B;Z)->h_2(p). For Q=(1-p)Law(Z|0)+p Law(Z|1), squared Hellinger H²(P,Q)=2(1-affinity) obeys
  H²(P_0,Q)<=2(1-sqrt(1-p))=O(p),
  H²(P_1,Q)<=2(1-sqrt(p))<=2.
The prior-average squared Hellinger is O(p), while mutual information tends to h_2(p)~p log(1/p); their ratio diverges. This is an exact heteroscedastic-Gaussian channel obstruction to a generic affinity-to-information conversion. It uses a binary prior, so it is not a counterexample to N75's continuous log-concave matrix-field model. It shows what an admissible proof must add: control of rare likelihood ratios, a subgaussian/tail premise, or a direct channel-specific entropy estimate.
