# Joint generator estimation preserves the minimum-lag resource law

7 October2026. Root's next technical assignment. Complete proof below; independent gate PASS at notes/check_wcrg/cycle6/minimum_lag_joint_gate.md. No children. This replaces the biased P/t nuisance calibration at a sensor-imposed lag by a matrix logarithm of the entire empirical endpoint transition matrix. No ordinary numerical rate is supplied to the estimator. The statistical theorem uses exact calibrated lags; an effective finite-bit implementation additionally requires computable supplied lag/bracket/envelope values.

## Exact finite-state interface and result

Use the known irreducible baseline graph G0, supplied positive compact ordinary-rate interval [qmin,qmax], missing u->v, shortest baseline u-v length L>=2, fixed visible labels and parameter-independent initial state from unknown_baseline_rare_edge.md. Every ordinary numerical rate is unknown. One unknown rare rate b lies in a supplied bracket [beta,2beta], beta<=1/2. The sensor permits only calibrated lags >=tau. There is no reset or jump-resolved observation.

There is an explicit envelope-dependent t0>0 such that, for every tau in(0,t0], a joint rate estimator at one admitted lag obtains |bhat-b|<=epsilon beta with probability>=1-delta and

    E reads <=C [beta^(-L/(L-1))+beta^-2 tau^(L-2)],
    E time  <=C [beta^-1+beta^-2 tau^(L-1)].

Here C depends on the fixed graph, compact rate bounds, fixed epsilon/delta, but not beta,tau or numerical ordinary rates. All state acquisition, return cycles and full-row estimation are charged. At nontrivial fixed accuracy/confidence (for example epsilon<1/4,delta<=0.1) the supplied-baseline lower-bound subfamily gives matching orders for this enlarged nuisance family. Constants can be extremely poor; no practical efficiency claim is made. Arbitrary large tau is not covered by this near-identity proof.

## Supplied constants and lag

Let dmax be the maximum baseline outdegree, R=dmax qmax and lambda=R+1. The actual generator Q=A+bB has escape rates<=lambda, infinity norm<=2lambda, B=e_u(e_v-e_u)^T. Choose

    t0=min{1,1/(8lambda)}, kappa=t0,
    t=max{tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))},
    H=2 kappa^(-(L-1)).

Then t<=t0, b<=H t^(L-1) and ||P-I||_infinity<=2lambda t<=1/4 for P=exp(tQ). The local bracket is the only b input to the lag. Compute shortest baseline distances d_xy, d_xx=0, and Dg=max_xy d_xy<=n-1.

Choose a supplied constant Cp at least max(lambda,1) and at least

    max_(1<=d<=Dg) {
      sum_(k=1)^(d-1) [n max(H,lambda)]^k/k!
        +exp(lambda)lambda^d/d! }.

All constants can be rounded upward to computable rational enclosures. In an effective implementation, compute the sufficient sample-count expression and ell through upward rational enclosures, then choose an integer above that rational bound. Do not require an exact ceiling/equality decision for a general computable real at an integer boundary. Input enclosure acquisition costs remain separate from the physical endpoint-read resource law.

## Rare rates become a length-L weight at the chosen lag

Uniformly over every admitted unknown baseline,

    P_xy<=Cp t^(d_xy), x!=y,
    |(P-I)_xx|<=lambda t.                     (W)

To prove(W), uniformize at rate lambda:

    P=exp(-lambda t) sum_(k>=0)(lambda t)^k/k! M^k,
    M=I+Q/lambda, a stochastic matrix.

For k<d=d_xy, expand a matrix-product path using j rare transitions. Replacing each rare u->v by a baseline length-L path produces a baseline walk; diagonal stays cannot help. Hence k+j(L-1)>=d. Each rare factor contributes at most (H/lambda)t^(L-1). Bounding the remaining M entries by1 and the number of products by n^k gives a bound [n max(H,lambda)]^k t^d/k! for the k-th uniformization term. For k>=d use M^k_xy<=1 and the ordinary exponential tail <=exp(lambda)lambda^d t^d/d!. The diagonal bound follows from P_xx>=exp(-lambda t).

This is a uniform weighted support statement, not an approximation that deletes rare paths. It is false with unknown shortcuts or without the local b-to-t comparison.

## Weighted resolvent controls matrix-log influence

Write K=P-I and Z=|K| entrywise. Its infinity norm is <=1/4. Set

    Rz=(I-Z)^-1=sum_(j>=0) Z^j.

There is an explicit Cr such that

    (Rz)_xy<=Cr t^(d_xy), all x,y.            (R)

For diagonal pairs use Cr>=4/3. For off-diagonal distance d, each entry of Z obeys Cp t^w_xy, with w_xx=1 and w_xy=d_xy otherwise. In any product along an x-y sequence, concatenating baseline shortest paths implies sum w>=d. Thus each j<d contributes at most(n Cp)^j t^d. For j>=d, the operator-norm bound contributes a geometric tail at most(4/3)(2lambda)^d t^d. A legal choice is

    Cr=max{4/3, max_(1<=d<=Dg)
        [sum_(j=1)^(d-1)(nCp)^j+(4/3)(2lambda)^d]}.

The analytic log series converges near identity. For a tangent E,

    Dlog_P[E]=sum_(m>=1)(-1)^(m+1)/m
                         sum_(j=0)^(m-1) K^j E K^(m-1-j).

Let the row-simplex tangent associated with one endpoint event be E^(xy)=e_x(e_y-e_x)^T, y!=x; define

    a_xy=[Dlog_P[E^(xy)]]_uv/t, a_xx=0.

Taking absolute values termwise and using1/m<=1 gives

    |a_xy| <=(Rz)_ux [(Rz)_yv+(Rz)_xv]/t
            <=Cr² t^(d_ux-1)[t^(d_yv)+t^(d_xv)].    (I)

In particular |a_xy|<=2Cr²/t. Define M0=4Cr², which bounds the centered row-event coefficient times t.

The variance of the linear log fluctuation has the needed topology-sensitive order:

    sum_x Var_(Y~P_x)(a_xY)
       <=sum_(x,y!=x) P_xy a_xy²
       <=V0 t^(L-2),
    V0=4n(n-1)Cp Cr^4.                       (V)

For the first term in the squared(I) bound, the power is

    d_xy+2d_ux+2d_yv-2>=L-2,

since L<=d_ux+d_xy+d_yv. For the diagonal-subtraction term, L<=d_ux+d_xv, so d_xy+2d_ux+2d_xv-2>=L-2 as well. The factor2 from squaring a sum yields the stated V0. Rows need not be reversible or normal. A global norm bound on Dlog would lose this exponent; the weighted influence is the load-bearing gain.

## Estimate all rows from legal passive return cycles

For each state x, collect m complete return cycles of the sampled chain at lag t; retain the endpoint of the first transition of each cycle. Complete the last cycle before starting the next state block. The retained endpoints have iid row law P_x by strong Markov. Conditional on any preceding block history, the next row block has this same fixed law; alternatively its scalar concentration can be proved by conditional moment-generating-function iteration. No likelihood assumes stationary initialization.

Let Phat be the empirical row-stochastic transition matrix, E=Phat-P. The linear fluctuation

    S=[Dlog_P[E]]_uv/t
      =sum_x {m^-1 sum_(j=1)^m a_(x,Yxj)-E_Px a_xY}

is analyzed using the unknown true P only as a proof object. It is not computed by the learner. From(V) and the centered coefficient bound M0/t, Bernstein gives

    |S|<=sqrt(2V0 t^(L-2)ell/m)+2M0 ell/(3mt)

except with probability2exp(-ell).

For every off-diagonal empirical entry, P_xy<=lambda t. Bernstein plus a union bound gives

    max_(x,y!=x)|E_xy|
      <=zeta=sqrt(2lambda t ell/m)+2ell/(3m).

Because E1=0, E_xx=-sum_(y!=x)E_xy. Hence ||E||_infinity<=A0 zeta, A0=2(n-1). If m>=ell/t,

    ||E||_infinity²/t <=G0 ell/m,
    G0=A0²(4lambda+8/9).                     (N)

These are actual multinomial row observations; columns are not assumed independent.

## Nonlinear log remainder has no uncredited P/t bias

On ||E||_infinity<=1/4, every K+theta E has norm<=1/2. The log series gives

    ||D²log_(I+K+theta E)[E,E]||_infinity
       <=sum_(m>=2)(m-1)(1/2)^(m-2)||E||²
       <=4||E||².

Taylor's integral remainder therefore yields

    ||log Phat-log P-Dlog_P[E]||_infinity/t
       <=2||E||²/t<=2G0 ell/m.               (REM)

Since ||tQ||<=1/4, the absolutely convergent analytic power identity gives log(exp(tQ))=tQ. Thus the population uv entry is **exactly b**, not b plus a discretization remainder. Phat need not be embeddable; its near-identity log is a valid real statistic even when an empirical off-diagonal log entry is negative.

Choose ell=log(6n²/delta) and

    m=ceil{ell [32V0 t^(L-2)/(epsilon² beta²)
               +(8M0+8G0+2)/(epsilon beta t)]}.

For epsilon<=1,beta<=1/2,t<=1, this implies m>=ell/t and m>=16G0 ell. Equation(N) then gives ||E||²<=1/16, so the log domain event holds. The linear fluctuation is at most epsilon beta/2, and(REM) is at most epsilon beta/4. Define

    braw=[log Phat]_uv/t,
    bhat=clip_[beta,2beta](braw).

Clipping shrinks error from the true b. With a certified numerical log error at most epsilon beta/4, the complete accuracy target |bhat-b|<=epsilon beta holds. Row and linear concentration consume at most2delta/3. The remaining delta/3 is reserved for the physical read cap.

The learner can also obtain a legal entire generator estimate by taking ordinary off-diagonal log entries divided by t, clipping each to[qmin,qmax], retaining absent baseline edges as zero, using bhat at u,v, and rebuilding diagonal row sums. The rare-rate proof does not need this optional projection to be unbiased and does not use its ordinary entries in the inversion.

Indeed the same row event also controls ordinary-rate accuracy. The first log derivative has operator bound ||Dlog_P[E]||<=||E||/(1-||P-I||)<=4||E||/3. With the nonlinear remainder, every ordinary log/t entry has error at most C||E||/t on that event. Therefore the entire acquired ordinary-rate model has maximum edge error

    O[sqrt(ell/(m t))+ell/(m t)].

Since the chosen m has m t>=C/ beta, this is O(sqrt(beta)) at fixed graph/accuracy/confidence, uniformly in the admitted minimum lag; clipping into the true compact interval shrinks the error. This confirms the original small-nuisance-error intuition using the same joint data. A fixed-additive EP export still requires its separate stationary/EP modulus and explicit precision allocation, rather than assuming that this consistency statement itself is an EP certificate.

## Entire cost, sensor floor and matching exponents

Use uniform eta from unknown_baseline_rare_edge.md, based only on qmin/qmax, support and b<=1. For s>=1, P_Q(s)=P_Q(s-1)P_Q(1); row stochasticity and the time-one lower floor therefore give P_Q(s)[x,y]>=eta. Let k=ceil(1/t). Each of n state blocks has initial acquisition cost<=k/eta expected reads, and each completed return cycle costs at most1/pi_x<=1/eta. Thus

    E N_total<=Hcost=1+[nk+nm]/eta,
    E physicaltime<=t Hcost,
    E actual jumps<=lambda t Hcost.

The rare edge is not the only sampled row; all n m row endpoints, returns, initial hits, and the initial state read appear in Hcost. Since m>=c/(beta t), the k terms are lower order. Cap reads at ceil(3Hcost/delta), declaring failure if reached, to allocate failure<=delta/3. This gives a deterministic protocol resource cap as well as the displayed expected bounds.

Before substituting t, the joint nuisance upper is

    reads=O[t^(L-2)/beta²+1/(beta t)],
    time =O[t^(L-1)/beta²+1/beta].

At t=max(tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))) these are exactly the two resource orders in the statement. The same sensor-floor lower bounds hold because a fixed known baseline is a subfamily of the unknown-baseline experiment. The minimax matching claim is for nontrivial fixed epsilon/confidence; a loose tolerance already met by the supplied bracket has no such lower bound.

## Effective arithmetic and retained precision

Phat has rational entries with denominator m. On the successful event, ||Phat-I||<=1/2. The convergent logarithm series

    log Phat=sum_(j>=1)(-1)^(j+1)(Phat-I)^j/j

has an infinity-norm tail at most2^(-K) after K terms. Choose K>=ceil(log2[8/(epsilon beta t)]) to give a rare-rate truncation error<=epsilon beta/8. Reserve another epsilon beta/8 for certified division by a computable lag, so the total numerical allowance remains epsilon beta/4. Exact rational arithmetic provides a directly certifiable implementation; counts require O(log m) bits and the straightforward common-denominator powers require O(K log m+K log K) intermediate bits. There are O(n³K) rational arithmetic operations. More efficient interval arithmetic is permitted only with its rounding ledger. No floating-point matrix logarithm test is a proof of the numerical statement.

Before evaluating, check the empirical norm ||Phat-I||<=1/2 using rational entries. If it fails, declare a counted failure; the row event implies this check passes. If a rational lag is desired under the sensor floor, choose t in[max(tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))),2max(tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1)))] and restrict tau and ideal lag to<=t0/2; this changes constants only, preserves the minimum lag, and keeps the same proved near-identity domain. Alternatively retain an explicitly computable calibrated lag and enclose the final division. Clock jitter, sensor-label noise and dead-time uncertainty are changed physical interfaces, separate from this arithmetic certificate.
