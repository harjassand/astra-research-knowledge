N13 — Replication fidelity: sequence-level work and error clusters
Version 1; 2026-10-09

Status: internally derived; finite-tested by the attached Python script; not
externally reviewed. The results below are conditional register-thermodynamic
statements and stochastic-channel constructions. They are not a demonstrated
polymerase mechanism, a claim of biological evasion of a speed–energy tradeoff,
or a claim of novelty or priority.

1. Exact target and current answer
---------------------------------

Target: determine whether single-site accuracy q alone, or q plus a finite
average cost for writing the copy, forces a universal suppression law for
clusters of replication errors. In parallel, specify what a physical
copying/repair model must include before comparing sequence context,
backtracking, kinetic proofreading (KPR), and mismatch repair (MMR) as one
accuracy–time–fuel system.

Current answer: q alone does not constrain cluster length. With an explicit
thermodynamic boundary—initially independent, uniform, equal-energy 4-letter
output registers—excess sequence-correlation work does constrain the
probability of an all-error window through a relative-entropy inequality. That
inequality is universal for stationary error processes. It does not force the
all-error-window probability to decay with window length at a fixed positive
per-site work overhead: a finite-state, persistent latent-mode block source
can keep a small, length-independent cluster probability while its excess
register-write work is arbitrarily small. This is an information-theoretic
counterexample, not yet a local-detailed-balance chemical implementation.

Consequently, an average q and average work budget do not determine the exact
conditional distribution of error-run lengths. At least one more observable,
such as run-start density, plus physical bounds on mode memory, switching time,
detector noise, and repair resources is needed for a sharp operational
frontier. A coarse universal conditional-tail envelope can still be derived
from q and the error entropy rate; it is given below.

2. Model boundary and work units
--------------------------------

Fix a supplied template x=(x_1,…,x_n) in {A,C,G,T}^n. It is held fixed and its
preparation cost is outside this register calculation. The output is a
4-letter sequence Y. The reference initial output state is U_4^n: every
sequence has probability 4^{-n}, all sequence states have equal energy, and
the registers begin decorrelated from the template and the copying machine.
The machine/controller is required to be returned to its initial state and
uncorrelated with the final copy for the usual quasistatic register bound to
apply. The work W is total external work into this register-writing operation;
the bound below is a lower bound on that operation, not a claim that dNTP/ATP
chemistry has been paid for. In an autonomous chemical copier, all chemical
work, standard-state changes, and controller reset work must be included in W
or added as separate terms.

For a final conditional output distribution Q_x, the nonequilibrium free
energy difference of the equal-energy output registers is

    W_in/(k_B T) >= D(Q_x || U_4^n)   (nats),

or, equivalently,

    W_in/(k_B T ln 2) >= D_2(Q_x || U_4^n)   (bits).

This is the standard free-energy inequality ΔF=kBT D(P||π_eq) for this stated
initial equilibrium ensemble. If the output starts in another state R, the
register-only change is kBT[D(Q_x||U)-D(R||U)]; a blank, pure register can
therefore provide nonequilibrium free energy and invalidate an unqualified
use of the uniform-register bound. A blank monomer, a primer terminus, the
dNTP pool, and a supplied template must be represented with their actual
initial distributions and chemical potentials in a full copying ledger.

Define errors E_i=1[Y_i != x_i]. Conditional on an error, let W_i denote which
of the three wrong bases was written. For each fixed x, the mapping
Y <-> (E,W) is one-to-one. Under U_4^n, E is iid Bernoulli(3/4), and, conditional
on E, wrong labels are iid uniform over the three alternatives. The chain
rule for relative entropy gives the exact decomposition

    D(Q_x || U_4^n)
      = D(P_E || Bernoulli(3/4)^n)
        + E_E[D(P(W | E,x) || U_3^{|E|})].

If wrong-base labels are uniform and independent conditional on E, the second
term vanishes. If labels have bias or additional correlations, that term is a
nonnegative extra cost. For a stationary error process with marginal
q=P(E_i=1) and entropy rate h_E bits/site, the uniform-label case has

    D_2(Q_x || U_4^n)/n -> 2 - q log_2(3) - h_E.

For iid errors at the same q, h_E=h_2(q), so

    w_iid(q) = 2 - h_2(q) - q log_2(3)       bits/site,
    c(q)     = h_2(q) - h_E                 bits/site,
    w(q)     = w_iid(q) + c(q).

Here c is the excess sequence-register write bound over an iid error mask.
It is nonnegative because h_E <= h_2(q). Convert any bit expression to
W/(kBT) by multiplying by ln 2. This is not the number of ATP molecules,
dNTP molecules, or hydrolysis events used by a biological copier.

For iid uniform template letters X, if E is independent of X and each wrong
label is chosen symmetrically among the other three letters, Y remains iid
uniform after averaging over X, even when the error mask E is correlated.
Thus H(Y)=2n bits and, exactly at finite n,

    I(X;Y)=2n-H(E_1:n)-n q log_2(3).

The per-site mutual information tends to 2-h_E-q log_2(3) bits/site. This is
the same asymptotic expression as the fixed-template relative entropy above.
The iid-template statement is an ensemble identity, not a claim that a
particular biological template is random or free to create.

3. Universal cluster-event inequality
-------------------------------------

Let P_m be the distribution of (E_1,…,E_m), and let p_m=P(E_1=…=E_m=1).
For a stationary process, the block entropy H_m satisfies H_m/m >= h_E.
Therefore its divergence from the iid Bernoulli(q) reference obeys

    D_2(P_m || Bernoulli(q)^m)
      = m h_2(q) - H_m
      <= m [h_2(q)-h_E] = m c.

Coarse-graining the block to the event A_m={1^m} cannot increase relative
entropy. Hence, with d_2 denoting binary relative entropy in bits,

    d_2(p_m || q^m) <= m c.                         (1)

This is valid for every stationary error process with the stated marginal and
entropy rate; it assumes no Markov structure. It is a cluster-event bound
under the equilibrium-register boundary, not a bound on chemical dissipation
unless the copying apparatus returns all internal resources to their initial
state and no other nonequilibrium resource is consumed.

For run starts, define s=P(E_{i-1}=0,E_i=1),
r_m=P(E_{i-1}=0,E_i=…=E_{i+m-1}=1). The reference event probability under iid
errors is (1-q)q^m. The same argument on an (m+1)-block gives

    d_2(r_m || (1-q)q^m) <= (m+1)c,                 (2)
    P(run length >= m | a run starts) = r_m/s.

Thus a measured run-start density s plus c gives a direct route to a bound on
the conditional run tail. Formula (1) constrains how much of the sequence can
be occupied by long error windows; it does not specify the number of such
windows or their exact conditional lengths. The rate s is not identified by
q and h_E, but it cannot be arbitrarily small when h_E is positive. By
conditioning reduces entropy,

    h_E <= H(E_i | E_{i-1})
        = G_q(s)
        = (1-q)h_2(s/(1-q)) + q h_2(1-s/q).

For 0<q<=1/2, G_q increases from zero to h_2(q) on
0<=s<=q(1-q). Let s_min(q,h_E) be the root G_q(s_min)=h_E on that interval
(s_min=0 if h_E=0). Every stationary process with these q,h_E has s>=s_min.
Also, the error mass is at least the finite-run mass s E[L], so Markov's
inequality gives the non-sharp but universal envelope

    P(L>=m | a finite run starts) <= min(1, q/(m s_min)).       (3)

This decays only as 1/m and can be loose. Combining it with (2) can tighten
some finite-m cases. An exact entropy/run-length frontier remains open here.

4. Explicit Markov diagnostics and counterexample to q-only claims
-----------------------------------------------------------------

For a stationary first-order binary Markov error process, set

    alpha=P(E_{i+1}=1 | E_i=1),
    beta =P(E_{i+1}=1 | E_i=0).

Stationarity with marginal q imposes

    beta = q(1-alpha)/(1-q).

The start density is (1-q)beta=q(1-alpha). Given a start, run length L is
geometric:

    P(L=ell)=(1-alpha) alpha^(ell-1),
    P(L>=m)=alpha^(m-1),
    E[L]=1/(1-alpha).

The error entropy rate is h_E=(1-q)h_2(beta)+q h_2(alpha), and the uniform
register bound is w=2-q log_2(3)-h_E bits/site. At fixed q, alpha=q gives iid
errors. At q=.01, iid alpha=.01 has P(L>=10)=10^-18. The same q with alpha=.99
has beta=.0001010101, mean L=100, and P(L>=10)=.913517. The error fraction alone
therefore cannot diagnose clusters. As alpha tends to 1, the start density
tends to zero while error occupancy remains q.

Within this restricted first-order Markov family, w increases monotonically
with alpha for alpha>=q. Bisection in the script gives the sharp family
frontier. At q=.01 and a work allowance of 0.01 additional bits/site above the
iid register floor, alpha<=.26685216 and P(L>=10)<=6.8618e-6. This is a
Markov-class result only; it is not a universal error-cluster law.

5. Persistent latent-mode block construction
--------------------------------------------

The Markov frontier might suggest that modest mean write overhead always
forces an exponential reduction in long windows. That inference fails for
non-Markov stationary processes. Fix B>=m and 0<lambda<q<1. Partition an
infinite sequence into blocks of B positions. Independently for each block,
draw Z~Bernoulli(lambda). In a bad block set E_i=1 at all positions. In a good
block draw E_i iid Bernoulli(q_0), where

    q_0=(q-lambda)/(1-lambda),

so the site marginal is exactly q. Draw each wrong-base label independently
and uniformly among the three alternatives. Choose the block phase uniformly
from {0,…,B-1}; this makes the bi-infinite sequence stationary. The phase has
finite entropy log_2(B), so it changes H(E_1:n)/n by at most log_2(B)/n and
does not change the entropy rate. The block source has finite internal memory
(phase and current block mode). Its controller reset and clock resources have
not been priced here.

For the error word e in one block, with k ones, its probability is

    P_B(1^B) = lambda + (1-lambda) q_0^B,
    P_B(e)   = (1-lambda) q_0^k (1-q_0)^(B-k), e != 1^B.

This gives exact block entropy H_B and entropy rate h_E=H_B/B. A random
position is the start of an m-window wholly contained in its block with
probability (B-m+1)/B; if that block is bad the window is all errors. Therefore

    p_m >= lambda (B-m+1)/B.                         (4)

The exact stationary window probability is also simple. For 1<=k<=B, let
g_k=lambda+(1-lambda)q_0^k be the probability that k consecutive sites at a
specified block edge are all errors. Of the B possible start phases, B-m+1
windows lie inside one block and m-1 cross one boundary. Independence of
successive block modes gives

    p_m = [(B-m+1)(lambda+(1-lambda)q_0^m)
           + sum_{k=1}^{m-1} g_k g_{m-k}]/B.          (5)

The attached script computes H_B by summing Hamming-weight layers, without
sampling. For q=.01, lambda=.001, B=1000, and m=10, it returns

    q_0 = .0090090090,
    h_E = .07408674185 bits/site,
    c   = .00670639404 bits/site,
    w_iid = 1.9033572391 bits/site,
    c/w_iid = .00352346,
    p_10 >= .000991,
    p_10 = .00099102716,
    q^10 = 1e-20.

The excess c is 8.3% of the iid error entropy h_2(q), but only about 0.35% of
the stated uniform-register copy-work floor w_iid. The cluster-event probability
is about 9.91e16 times the iid value for this particular 10-window. This is
a stationary, finite-memory stochastic-channel counterexample. It is not an
observed polymerase process.

For fixed lambda and m with B tending to infinity, the all-one block becomes
identifiable from its contents, H_B/B tends to (1-lambda)h_2(q_0), and the
lower bound in (4) tends to lambda. For lambda tending to zero,

    c -> h_2(q) - (1-lambda)h_2((q-lambda)/(1-lambda))
      = lambda log_2(1/q) + O(lambda^2).

So arbitrarily small positive excess write cost can coexist with a
length-independent, small probability of arbitrarily long error windows:
choose lambda small and B much larger than the window. This construction
matches the linear-in-lambda term of (1), because
d_2(lambda || q^m) = lambda m log_2(1/q) - h_2(lambda) + o(m), while
m c = lambda m log_2(1/q)+O(m lambda^2). In the joint regime lambda->0,
m/log(1/lambda)->infinity, and B/m->infinity, the relative slack tends to
zero. This establishes leading-order sharpness in that regime, not exact
attainment for a fixed lambda. The universal inequality cannot be replaced by
a theorem that forces exponential-in-m decay of this window-occupancy metric
from a fixed positive per-site work budget alone.

The process uses a latent mode that persists for B positions. The conditional
run-tail metric is different: among all finite error-run starts, the fraction
whose run reaches m is bounded by the coarse 1/m envelope (3); it need not
stay near lambda. The block construction keeps the probability that a
randomly placed m-window lies wholly in an error run near lambda as B grows,
because rare long runs occupy a fixed fraction of the sequence. A local chemical
machine could in principle represent a mode and a finite phase counter, but
that sentence is not a local-detailed-balance construction. The controller's
initial state, switching reactions, phase reset, mode discrimination, ATP/dNTP
fluxes, and completion time must be charged before describing this as a
physically realizable copying protocol. A clocked block boundary is a real
resource; randomizing phase only makes the output process stationary and does
not make the clock free.

6. Chemical copying model required for a fair physical comparison
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The effective process-level model to build is a finite continuous-time Markov
network for each fixed finite template x of length n. A transient state must
record at least position j, the written prefix y_1:j, polymerase conformation
(including any good/bad or fidelity state), candidate-bound/intermediate
state, proofreading/backtrack state, and any MMR detection/scan/repair state.
For a finite design and fixed n this is finite, though large. The output error
mask is scored only after full completion; false starts, excised bases, and
MMR patching all count.

For each reversible reaction edge e, impose local detailed balance

    ln(k_e/k_reverse(e))
      = beta_T [sum_r nu_{e,r} mu_r - (G(target(e))-G(source(e)))],
    beta_T=1/(k_B T),

where nu_{e,r}>0 is the number of reservoir molecules consumed. A polymerase
extension edge consumes the specific dNTP and releases PP_i; any PP_i
hydrolysis edge and its chemical potential are represented separately.
Proofreading removes the terminal product through explicit strand transfer
and exonuclease states, then re-incorporates through the same dNTP ledger.
KPR adds explicit driven branches and ATP/ADP/P_i (or the actual species)
stoichiometry. An MMR branch includes mismatch recognition, strand
discrimination/nicking, excision/helicase steps, patch synthesis, and ligation;
the organism-specific ATP/NAD chemistry is specified rather than assigned a
generic unit cost. Recognition is a stochastic molecular signal, never an
oracle query of E_i.

Let Q be the transient generator, e_0 the initial-state row vector, and K_{r,+}
and K_{r,-} the transition-rate matrices for forward and reverse reactions
of reservoir species r. For absorption in the completed-copy states,

    E[t_completion] = e_0^T (-Q)^(-1) 1,
    E[N_{r,+/-}]    = e_0^T (-Q)^(-1) K_{r,+/-} 1,
    W_chem          = sum_r mu_r (E[N_{r,+}] - E[N_{r,-}]).

Expected numbers of incorporations, exonuclease removals, futile attempts,
ATP turnovers, MMR patch bases, and resets are obtained by adding their event
matrices. For a repeated-copy steady state, also report throughput, power,
enzyme occupancy, and template-service time. Scaling both directions of every
edge by the same factor preserves local detailed balance and affinities while
changing time; therefore no speed–energy statement follows from affinities
alone without bounds on rate prefactors, enzyme concentration, power, or
allowed completion time.

The input ledger for one copy explicitly contains one supplied n-base template
molecule, the initial primer/template state, an enzyme service slot, the
initial controller state, and the nucleotide/repair-factor pools. Template
synthesis is excluded only because the template is stipulated as supplied;
the exclusion is not generalized to new templates. For a statistical ensemble
of copied templates, its distribution and any template–controller correlations
must be specified. If the controller is not restored, its final nonequilibrium
free energy and its correlations with the copy are charged as resources.

No numerical rate set, chemically complete CTMC, or optimal physical protocol
has yet been constructed. The equations in this section define a required
accounting interface, not a completed mechanism.

7. Oracle repair diagnostic and why it is not a mechanism
--------------------------------------------------------

For the Markov chain only, consider an unrealistically strong policy that
detects a run of m errors perfectly, removes the last m bases, resets to a
clean state, and retries. Let

    R = beta alpha^(m-1),
    P_0 = 1-beta,
    P_l = beta(1-alpha)alpha^(l-1), 1<=l<m.

P_0 and P_l are the terminal non-reset episode probabilities; R is the
per-episode retry probability. Put

    T_0 = P_0 + sum_{l=1}^{m-1} P_l(l+1),
    R_0 = sum_{l=1}^{m-1} l P_l,
    K_0 = sum_{l=1}^{m-1} P_l.

Renewal accounting gives post-reset error fraction R_0/T_0, surviving error
cluster rate K_0/T_0, resets per output base R/T_0, extra failed insertions
per output base mR/T_0, and total incorporations per output base
1+mR/T_0. For q=.01, alpha=.99, and m=3, the script gives post-reset errors
3.01039e-6/base, resets 9.90095e-5/base, and extra dNTP incorporations
2.97029e-4/base. These are exact for the oracle renewal policy.

The associated illustrative repair-cost comparison uses arbitrary unit costs:
perfect reset, perfect mismatch repair, a 25-base flank, and no overlaps. It
prints 1.0008534 candidate units/base against 1.06 sitewise units/base. This is
not a valid advantage claim. It assumes free access to the true run sequence,
has no false alarms, and does not compare optimized controllers with equal
detector information. It is retained only as evidence that burst clipping can
look artificially favorable under oracle access. A fair continuation must
derive actual noisy signals, finite posterior memory, false-positive and
false-negative rates, reset efficacy, reset work, and equal-resource sitewise
and run-triggered controls. A parallel N27 finite-controller diagnostic now
supplies a noisy signal and finite cue memory at the stochastic-channel level;
its scripts are `work/natural/n27_correlated_repair/finite_controller.py`,
`finite_frontier.py`, and `exact_check.py`. A latent G/B polymerase mode has
per-incorporation switching probabilities a=.005 and b=.02 and initial error
probabilities e_G=.0001 and e_B=.1. A cue Z has sensitivity s=.8 and false
positive rate f=.2. These cue probabilities are compatible with a stipulated
equilibrium binary receptor with mismatch-dependent binding free-energy
differences ±kBT ln 4, but its complete binding/unbinding cycle is not modeled.
Every positive cue attempts patch/resynthesis with efficacy eta=.98, expected
extra dNTP eta per positive cue, one stipulated ATP-equivalent per attempt,
and time .4; each reset attempt similarly costs one stipulated
ATP-equivalent and time 1. The three-cue shift register triggers a reset only
when the four-cue history first crosses 3-of-4 positive cues. The reset channel
has both B-to-G=.6 and G-to-B=.2 branches, so false alarms, missed errors,
ineffective resets, and reverse resets are present. The triggering truth table,
with memory bits oldest to newest and index 2*memory+current cue, is
`0000000100010100`, corresponding to `-+++`, `+-++`, and `++-+`.

Exact rational solution of its 16-state stationary chain gives final error
.00816143045/site, reset .02258907044/site, dNTP 1.20628064892/site, time
1.10678525367/site, and ATP-event ledger .233079528519/site. It strictly
improves all five metrics over the exact same-signal sitewise, 1-plus one-shot,
repeated 2-plus, and one-shot 2-plus policies in the stipulated model. It does
not dominate 3-plus controls. `finite_frontier.py` also matches reset rates for
sitewise, 1-plus, repeated 2-plus, and one-shot 2-plus controls and finds lower
error for the candidate; the 3-plus controls use fewer resets and remain
separate lower-spend/higher-error points. It reports H(Z|E)=.721928 bits/site
for the stipulated cue and a .742404-bit/site conditional erasure bound for
the popped cue given the retained shift register. These are information
accounting diagnostics, not additive to one another without a joint sensor and
memory cycle model.

The reset transition kernel alone can be embedded over a unit time by a
two-state continuous-time generator with total relaxation rate ln(5),
k_GB=(ln 5)/4 and k_BG=3(ln 5)/4; exponentiating this generator gives the
stated .2 and .6 transition probabilities. This does not close its chemical
thermodynamics with the separate background G/B switching channel. The
one-ATP-equivalent-per-attempt score is a stipulated gross event budget, not a
net ATP flux or a derived affinity. These results show that cue history can
help control a persistent latent mode in a finite stochastic model; they do
not establish an accuracy-speed-energy advantage in biology. No full
local-detailed-balance copying/sensor/controller network or empirical fit is
supplied. The exact state-chain check proves only the model's arithmetic. The
run detector uses noisy cues rather than ground-truth mismatch labels, but
sensor coupling, joint cue storage/erasure, and molecular reset implementation
remain physical gates.

A simpler batching identity also does not establish an advantage. If a fixed
pre-repair Markov error stream has one repair-setup cost a per cluster, a cost
c_b per patched base, and a flank of g bases per cluster, its idealized
per-site MMR cost is

    q c_b + q(1-alpha)(a+c_b g).

Independent sitewise repair costs q(a+c_b), so batching is cheaper exactly
when alpha a > (1-alpha)c_b g. This omits the cost of generating the correlated
error stream, detector and repair failures, patch overlap, and the actual ATP
kinetics. It is an accounting identity, not an optimized biological protocol.

polymerase conformational partitioning/exonuclease proofreading and ATP-driven
MMR are real molecular processes. The particular q=.01 Markov chain, block
mode fraction lambda=.001, and long-run statistics above are synthetic
examples only. No literature claim was found here that establishes the joint
per-copy error-mask entropy rate, run-start density, and repair-cost relation
needed to calibrate the proposed frontier in a named biological system.

9. Strongest consequences actually supported
---------------------------------------------

1. Given only q, error-run lengths can be arbitrarily large in a stationary
   first-order Markov family by taking alpha toward 1 and adjusting beta to
   preserve q.
2. Under the stated randomized equal-energy output-register boundary and
   symmetric wrong-label channel, the excess sequence-write bound is exactly
   c=h_2(q)-h_E bits/site; in work units it is kBT ln2 times c per site.
3. For every stationary error process, the m-window event satisfies (1), and
   a run-start event satisfies (2). These are universal sequence-level
   constraints under that boundary.
4. A finite-memory persistent-mode process attains length-independent
   all-error-window occupancy at fixed positive average write overhead. It
   matches (1) to leading order in the joint small-lambda, large-m,
   B/m-to-infinity regime specified in section 5. Thus no universal
   exponential decay in window length follows for this occupancy metric from
   q and a finite average sequence-write budget alone. For the conditional
   run-start tail, (3) gives a universal but non-sharp 1/m envelope; its exact
   frontier is unresolved.
5. The independent work required to turn this obstruction into a biochemical
   result is substantial: the synthetic source omits the local chemical
   pathway, discrimination fuel, phase controller, time, and resource reset.
   No claim that context + backtracking + KPR + MMR evade a physical tradeoff is
   supported yet.

10. Exact remaining gates
-------------------------

* State and solve the resource boundary for a chemically complete finite CTMC:
  initial output/monomer distribution, supplied template, enzyme and controller
  state, all dNTP/PP_i and ATP/ADP/P_i or NAD stoichiometries, standard chemical
  potentials, and final reset/correlation state.
* Replace the latent block oracle with a noisy molecular state/signal and a
  finite-memory posterior controller. Derive likelihoods from actual rates;
  include false alarms, missed errors, mode dwell time, phase-reset work, and
  finite reset efficacy. Compare sitewise and run/tract policies under the
  same observations and same total time, enzyme, fuel, and template budget.
* Use a specific sequence-dependent polymerase/exonuclease/MMR CTMC with
  local detailed balance and all reverse edges. Compute q, entropy rate,
  p_m, run-start density, completion-time distribution, and expected net fuel
  counts from its transient generator. Check that every state needed to
  recognize an error is physically accessible and that no true-mismatch label
  is supplied directly.
* Establish a fair optimized KPR baseline under the same discrimination
  energies, kinetic prefactors, copy rate, and total resource budget. A simple
  sitewise editor or arbitrary unit-cost comparison is not sufficient.
* Bound or measure sequence-context dependence and error autocorrelation on
  the same individual newly synthesized molecules. Context-stratified
  mutation spectra alone do not estimate per-copy run persistence.
* Prove whether the information-theoretic block construction can be approached
  by a finite autonomous local-detailed-balance network with returned memory,
  or derive the extra work/time/space term that prevents it. The canonical
  copying-network result of Ouldridge et al. cautions that the register bound
  may be unattainable for autonomous steady-state architectures.
* Resolve whether a universal frontier should use all-error occupancy p_m,
  run-start density s plus conditional tail r_m/s, or a complete run-length
  law. q and entropy rate alone are insufficient to identify the latter.
* Obtain independent mathematical/physical review; no novelty, priority, or
  biological validation claim has been made.
