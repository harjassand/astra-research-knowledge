# Conditional action-sensitivity lemma for pulse timing

This is a model-level lemma supporting the first-pass dose/timing candidate. It
does not establish that a cell-fate system follows the assumed diffusion or
that its biological transition probability is given by the resulting action.

## Setup

Let the small-noise controlled diffusion be

\[
dX_t = \{b(X_t)+u(t)G(X_t)\}\,dt+\sqrt{2\varepsilon A(X_t)}\,dW_t,
\]

where \(A(x)\) is symmetric positive definite. For an absolutely continuous
path \(x\), use the Freidlin--Wentzell action

\[
I_u[x]=\frac14\int
(\dot x-b(x)-uG(x))^\top A(x)^{-1}
(\dot x-b(x)-uG(x))\,dt.
\]

Assume at \(u=0\) the source-to-target minimizer \(x_*\) is unique up to
time-translation, is nondegenerate after fixing that phase, and varies
differentiably under a small control perturbation. Write \(S(u)=\inf_x I_u[x]\).
The envelope theorem gives

\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\theta}S(\theta v)\right|_{\theta=0}
=-\int v(t)\kappa(t)\,dt,\qquad
\kappa(t)=\frac12G(x_*(t))^\top A(x_*(t))^{-1}
(\dot x_*(t)-b(x_*(t))).
\]

Thus, for a bounded pulse \(v\),

\[
S(\theta v)=S(0)-\theta\langle v,\kappa\rangle+O(\theta^2).
\]

The statement is only a first variation of the minimizing action. It does not
include the Eyring--Kramers prefactor, finite-noise corrections, switching to a
different minimizing path, model-estimation error, or biological assay error.

## Dose allocation consequence

Under an \(L^2\) budget \(\|u\|_2^2\le E\), and without amplitude or sign
constraints, Cauchy--Schwarz gives the first-order optimum

\[
u^*(t)=\sqrt{E}\,\frac{\kappa(t)}{\|\kappa\|_2},
\qquad \Delta S_{\rm lin}=\sqrt{E}\|\kappa\|_2.
\]

For a nonnegative dose, replace \(\kappa\) by its positive part
\(\kappa_+(t)=\max\{\kappa(t),0\}\). With a pointwise cap, the optimizer is
the clipped matched kernel with its multiplier selected to use at most the
budget. With an \(L^1\) budget, allocate dose to the bins with the largest
positive \(\kappa\) first. These are local, linearized results; a final
prediction must re-solve or simulate the nonlinear controlled model.

In the reversible gradient case \(b=-\nabla V\), \(A=I\), the uphill
minimizer satisfies \(\dot x_*=\nabla V(x_*)\), so
\(\kappa=G\cdot\nabla V\). If the applied control tilts the potential to
\(V_u=V-u g\), then \(G=\nabla g\) and
\(\kappa(t)=d g(x_*(t))/dt\). For a constant small dose,
\[
\left.\frac{dS}{du}\right|_{0}=-(g(s)-g(a)),
\]
where \(a\) is the source minimum and \(s\) the saddle. This recovers the
barrier-height derivative in the special gradient setting.

## Explicit timing-noise debit

For a pulse shifted by independent mean-zero timing error \(\delta\), the
first-order action decrease is proportional to the autocorrelation
\[
R(\delta)=\langle \kappa(\cdot),\kappa(\cdot-\delta)\rangle.
\]
If \(\kappa\) is differentiable and decays at the window boundaries, then
\[
\mathbb E R(\delta)
=\|\kappa\|_2^2-\frac{\mathbb E[\delta^2]}2\|\dot\kappa\|_2^2
+o(\mathbb E[\delta^2]).
\]
This is a local expected-gain correction, not a lower confidence bound. A
decision rule must use the measured jitter distribution and a one-sided
quantile or a worst-case support bound if it promises a confidence guarantee.
Likewise, multiplicative actuator gain noise \(\eta\) with a calibrated
one-sided lower bound \(1-\eta_{\max}\) multiplies a nonnegative linearized
gain by at least that factor.

## Double-well finite calculation used in the report

For \(V_q(x)=(x^2-1)^2/4-qx\), the zero-dose uphill instanton is
\[
x_*(t)=-\{1+\exp(2t)\}^{-1/2},
\]
and for unit additive drift control \(\kappa(t)=x_*^3-x_*\).
Direct integration gives
\[
\|\kappa\|_2^2=\frac14,\qquad
\|\dot\kappa\|_2^2=\frac12,\qquad
\max_t\kappa(t)=\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}.
\]
These identities and the separate finite-grid calculation in
model_demo.py are arithmetic checks for this toy model. They are not
experimental evidence and do not certify the asymptotic regime at
\(\varepsilon=0.05\).

## What would falsify the proposed use

The kernel-based timing prediction is rejected for the intended biological
system if its measured path is nonunique or changes under dose, if a
non-equilibrium current or multiplicative noise moves the reactive path away
from the fitted path, if the pulse is shorter than within-basin relaxation,
or if a matched pulse fails against equal-resource constant and uniform-dose
controls. In any such case, report the reduced model as unsupported and use
the finite-state or path-resolved observation rule in
CYCLE2_REPORT.md; do not reinterpret a fitted landscape as a biological
theorem.
