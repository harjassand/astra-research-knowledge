C04_S02 FRESH SCHUR-CENTRAL DEPTH DERIVATION

STATUS AND ORIGIN

Root proposed the column-wedge construction and, subsequently, the exact
row-count lower bound. This artifact reconstructs both arguments independently
of c04_s03 and without reading another worker's new Schur proof. Earlier own
files, including INITIAL and the spin proof package, are preserved.

The main claims below are proved as elementary consequences of the standard
Schur-Weyl decomposition and highest-weight structure of its U(d) modules.
They have an explicit continuous product-cluster ensemble. This is internal
mathematical verification, not an external validation or novelty certificate.
A directly relevant 2026 isotypic-entanglement paper is being read separately;
the upper construction should be described as an elementary Schur-Weyl
corollary unless priority is established by more than a search.

1. DEFINITIONS AND EXACT QUANTIFIERS

Let N>=1, d>=1 and H=(C^d)^tensor N, with the defining local representation.
Write U_N=U^tensor N and W_pi for site permutations pi in S_N.
A pure state is k-producible if it is a tensor product over a partition of
the N sites into clusters of size at most k. A mixed state is k-producible if
it is a convex mixture of such pure states; partitions may differ between
mixture terms. Its entanglement depth is the least such k, in [1,N].

Theorem A. If a density matrix rho satisfies
  U_N rho U_N*=rho for every U in U(d),
  W_pi rho W_pi*=rho for every pi in S_N,
then rho is min(d,N)-producible. More precisely, for every partition lambda
of N with r=ell(lambda)<=d, its normalized isotypic projector
  omega_lambda=P_lambda/Tr(P_lambda)
has entanglement depth EXACTLY r. Every density matrix whose support is
contained in that single isotypic subspace has depth at least r.

Theorem B. Any product of invertible local filters preserves entanglement
depth exactly. In particular, for one invertible g on C^d,
  omega_lambda,g = g_N P_lambda g_N*/Tr(g_N P_lambda g_N*)
has exact depth r, and g_N rho g_N*/Tr(g_N rho g_N*) is min(d,N)-producible
for rho in Theorem A. Here g_N=g^tensor N.

Theorem C. Let Z be any Hermitian operator scalar on each Schur-Weyl
isotypic subspace: Z=sum_lambda z_lambda P_lambda, z_lambda real. Let B be
any Hermitian d by d matrix and J_B=sum_i B_i. Then
  rho=exp(Z+J_B)/Tr exp(Z+J_B)
is min(d,N)-producible, at every finite N and all finite real parameters.
This includes arbitrary real functions of the central collective U(d) or
SU(d) Casimirs, with arbitrary common one-body field. B need not be Cartan;
one may diagonalize it by a common unitary. There is no sign, small-coupling,
large-N, temperature or mean-field normalization restriction.

These are depth bounds, not claims of full separability, exact iid product
membership, or entanglement depth equal to the largest row count appearing
in a mixed-block state. The latter claim is false for the maximally mixed
state, which has all blocks present and depth one.

2. STANDARD REPRESENTATION INPUT AND NORMALIZERS

Schur-Weyl duality supplies a unitary decomposition
  H = direct_sum_{lambda |- N, ell(lambda)<=d} V_lambda tensor M_lambda,
  U_N = direct_sum pi_lambda(U) tensor I_{M_lambda},
  W_pi = direct_sum I_{V_lambda} tensor s_lambda(pi).
V_lambda is the polynomial U(d) irreducible module of highest weight lambda,
and M_lambda the irreducible S_N module with the same partition. Each pair
occurs once. Put D_lambda=dim V_lambda and f_lambda=dim M_lambda. With lambda
padded by zeros to d coordinates,
  D_lambda=product_{1<=i<j<=d}(lambda_i-lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i),
  f_lambda=N!/product_{boxes a of lambda} hook(a),
  Tr P_lambda=D_lambda f_lambda.
The standard highest-weight facts used are: highest-weight vectors in
V_lambda have weight lambda and form a one-dimensional space; all other
weights are lambda minus a nonzero sum of positive simple roots. Thus a
strictly dominant Cartan element has a unique maximizing weight in this
module. No numerical character or dimension claim replaces this input.

If rho commutes with all U_N, it has the form
direct_sum I_{V_lambda} tensor A_lambda. If it also commutes with every
W_pi, Schur's lemma gives A_lambda=a_lambda I_{M_lambda}. Positivity and
trace one give
  rho=sum_lambda q_lambda omega_lambda,
  q_lambda=a_lambda D_lambda f_lambda>=0, sum_lambda q_lambda=1.

SU(d) versus U(d): write U=e^{i theta} S, S in SU(d). Its tensor action
differs from S_N only by the scalar e^{iN theta}, so invariance of a density
matrix under SU(d) is equivalent to invariance under U(d) here. Two U(d)
partitions whose SU(d) highest weights coincide differ by an equal shift of
all d rows. At fixed N their sums agree, so that shift is zero. There are no
extra mergers of different lambda labels within this fixed tensor power.

3. COLUMN PRODUCT AND ISOTYPIC SUPPORT

Let the Young columns of lambda have heights h_c, 1<=c<=lambda_1, so
h_c<=r<=d and sum_c h_c=N. Give each column a consecutive cluster of h_c
sites. In an orthonormal local basis e_1,...,e_d define the normalized wedge
  a_h=(h!)^{-1/2} sum_{sigma in S_h} sign(sigma)
           e_{sigma(1)} tensor ... tensor e_{sigma(h)},
and the normalized column product v_lambda=tensor_c a_{h_c}.
It is r-producible.

For a diagonal local matrix diag(t_1,...,t_d), each a_h has character
product_{i<=h} t_i. The total weight of v_lambda is therefore
  (# columns of height >=1,...,# columns of height >=d)=lambda.
For every simple raising matrix E_{i,i+1}, the collective raising operator
annihilates a_h: either e_{i+1} does not occur, or replacing it by e_i gives
a wedge with a duplicate basis vector. It consequently annihilates
v_lambda. Thus v_lambda is a nonzero highest-weight vector of weight lambda
in the full tensor power. In the Schur-Weyl decomposition it can have
components only in V_lambda tensor M_lambda: a component in a different
irreducible module killed by all raising operators would have that module's
distinct highest weight. Hence P_lambda v_lambda=v_lambda.

Height d columns are retained. Their wedges are determinant vectors:
U^tensor d a_d=det(U) a_d. They are SU(d) singlets, but are entangled
d-site clusters, not zero-height columns and not separable scalars. Dropping
determinant columns would destroy the entanglement-depth conclusion.

4. JOINT TWIRL AND THE UPPER BOUND

Let T_U be normalized Haar conjugation by U_N and
T_S(X)=(1/N!) sum_pi W_pi X W_pi*. They commute. Since v_lambda lies in one
block and the joint U(d) x S_N representation of that block is irreducible,
the joint twirl is scalar there, vanishes elsewhere, and has trace one:
  T_S T_U(|v_lambda><v_lambda|)=P_lambda/(D_lambda f_lambda).
Equivalently v_lambda=u_lambda^highest tensor m_lambda under the Schur
decomposition, with normalized m_lambda; the U twirl gives I_V/D tensor
|m><m| and the permutation twirl gives I_V/D tensor I_M/f.

Every ensemble term W_pi U_N v_lambda is a product of the column clusters,
with their sites permuted. Cluster sizes remain <=r. The continuous mixture
therefore lies in the compact convex set of r-producible density matrices.
Alternatively ordinary finite-dimensional Caratheodory converts it to a
finite positive mixture with at most (D_lambda f_lambda)^2 terms inside its
block; no efficient atom-finding claim follows from this existence bound.
Mixing the blocks proves the upper half of Theorem A.

5. LOWER BOUND FOR ANY STATE WITH SINGLE-BLOCK SUPPORT

First show: a nonzero k-producible pure vector v in ran P_lambda requires
k>=r. Fix its cluster product partition, v=tensor_c v_c, with n_c<=k.

5a. Make the highest-weight component nonzero by a common unitary.
Let u be a normalized highest vector in V_lambda. The linear span of the
orbit pi_lambda(U)*u is all V_lambda by irreducibility. If
  ( <u| pi_lambda(U) tensor I_M ) v
were zero for every U, v would be zero. Thus some U has a nonzero highest
component. No supplied oracle for finding this U is used in the existence
proof. For almost every U the component is nonzero, since the coefficient is
a nonzero real-analytic function on the compact group. Replace v by U_N v;
its cluster partition and k-producibility are unchanged.

5b. A common positive Cartan limit selects the highest component.
Choose H=diag(h_1,...,h_d) with h_1>...>h_d, sum_i h_i=0. All weights in
V_lambda other than lambda have strictly smaller H weight. Since e^{tH}_N
preserves the lambda block,
  e^{tH}_N v / ||e^{tH}_N v|| --> w
as t->infinity, where w is a normalized vector of total weight lambda
annihilated by all simple raising operators. It need not have any selected
vector in multiplicity space; that space is irrelevant to the argument.

For every finite t the vector is a product over the same clusters. Each
cluster separately has a normalized limit under the positive Hermitian
exponential: retain its top nonzero eigenspace component. Thus
w=tensor_c w_c for the same cluster partition. One can also use closedness
of pure product vectors for this fixed partition. This is an existence
argument involving an infinite filter parameter, not a bounded-cost protocol.

5c. Total highest weight forces each factor to be highest weight.
For a simple raising operator let E_c be its collective action on cluster c.
The relation sum_c E_c w=0, projected onto w in every other cluster and onto
the orthogonal complement of w_c in cluster c, gives
  (I-|w_c><w_c|) E_c w_c=0.
Hence E_c w_c=a_c w_c. E_c is nilpotent on the n_c-fold tensor power
(each application increases an occupation weight), so a_c=0. Every cluster
is annihilated by every simple raising operator.

For each occupation observable N_i=sum_sites |e_i><e_i| the total vector w
has the definite eigenvalue lambda_i. Variances add on a cluster product,
  0=Var_w(N_i)=sum_c Var_{w_c}(N_{i,c}),
so every cluster has a definite occupation weight nu^{(c)}_i, consisting of
nonnegative integers with sum_i nu^{(c)}_i=n_c. They sum to lambda.
Moreover the su(2) raising/lowering identity yields
  ||E_{i+1,i;c} w_c||^2
    = <w_c|[E_{i,i+1;c},E_{i+1,i;c}]|w_c>
    = nu^{(c)}_i-nu^{(c)}_{i+1} >=0.
Thus each nu^{(c)} is a partition of n_c. It has at most n_c<=k nonzero
rows. Since lambda=sum_c nu^{(c)}, all lambda_i with i>k vanish. This proves
r<=k, including determinant columns and local multiplicity spaces.

Finally let a positive density matrix rho have support contained in ran
P_lambda. In any positive pure-state ensemble, every pure vector lies in
that support: Tr[(I-P_lambda)rho]=sum_a p_a ||(I-P_lambda)psi_a||^2=0.
If rho were k-producible with k<r, its k-product pure ensemble would violate
the result just proved. Therefore depth(rho)>=r. Combined with Section 4,
depth(omega_lambda)=r exactly. General single-block densities can have
larger depth; the upper bound was proved for normalized twirled blocks.

6. FILTERING AND CENTRAL GIBBS STATES

For any pure cluster product, a product of local linear maps acts separately
on each cluster. Normalizing its nonzero image preserves that partition and
its cluster-size bound. The image of a positive mixture is a positive mixture
with weights reweighted by the squared image norms. Thus local filtering
preserves every k-producible cone. With invertible filters the inverse maps
have the same property, so entanglement depth is preserved exactly. Positive
or identical filters are sufficient here but not necessary for this fact.
For noninvertible maps only the forward bound holds, provided the total
output trace is nonzero; zero-norm terms may be discarded.

Take g=exp(B/2), g_N=exp(J_B/2). Since Z commutes with all common local
unitaries, it commutes with J_B and hence
  exp(Z+J_B)=g_N exp(Z) g_N.
exp(Z) is a positive central operator, so Theorem B applies. This proves C.
The argument also applies to any positive central X, even with zero block
weights, via g_N X g_N*/Tr(...). Infinite-parameter Gibbs limits remain
min(d,N)-producible by compactness, if their normalized limits exist.

For a block, the filter normalization is exactly
  Tr[g_N P_lambda g_N*] = f_lambda chi_lambda(g*g),
where chi_lambda is the U(d)/GL(d) Schur character. Consequently the central
Gibbs partition function and filtered block probabilities are
  Z_total=sum_lambda e^{z_lambda} f_lambda chi_lambda(e^B),
  q'_lambda=e^{z_lambda} f_lambda chi_lambda(e^B)/Z_total.
At B=0, chi_lambda(I)=D_lambda, giving the original block probabilities.
No multiplicity f_lambda or Weyl dimension D_lambda may be omitted.

An explicit ensemble for a filtered block is also available. For U Haar let
  t_lambda(U)=||g_N U_N v_lambda||^2
             = product_columns det[(U*g*gU)_{1:h_c,1:h_c}],
  A_lambda=int t_lambda(U)dU=chi_lambda(g*g)/D_lambda.
The determinants are positive when g is invertible. Sample U with density
t_lambda(U)/A_lambda, sample pi uniformly, and emit the normalized pure
cluster product W_pi g_N U_N v_lambda/sqrt(t_lambda(U)). This gives exactly
omega_lambda,g. Permutations have no additional norm reweighting because
the common filter commutes with them. Different heights need different
wedge clusters; they all use the same common U, not independent rotations.

7. QUBIT FORM AND THERMAL-BATH SCOPE

For d=2 write lambda=(N-b,b), K=N-2b=2j. The column product consists of
b singlet pairs a_2=(|01>-|10>)/sqrt(2) and K identical qubits |0>.
  D_lambda=2j+1=K+1,
  f_lambda=binom(N,b)-binom(N,b-1),
  Tr P_j=(2j+1) f_lambda.
All j blocks are 2-producible; when b>0 the normalized single-block state
has exact depth two, while b=0 has exact depth one.

For J_z=sum_i diag(1/2,-1/2)_i and exp(theta J_z), use the SINGLE local
filter g=exp[theta diag(1/2,-1/2)/2]. The singlet pair is fixed because
det(g)=1. The K unpaired qubits retain a common filtered orientation.
  z_j(theta)=sum_{m=-j}^j exp(theta m)
            =sinh[(2j+1)theta/2]/sinh(theta/2),
with z_j(0)=2j+1. The filtered block is P_j exp(theta J_z)/(f_lambda z_j).
For a uniform Bloch orientation u, its norm reweight is
  [cosh(theta/2)+u_z sinh(theta/2)]^K,
whose Haar mean is z_j(theta)/(K+1). These factors give the exact normalized
singlet-pair-plus-common-qubit ensemble, including odd N and half-integer j.

Any PI collective thermal-bath stationary state of the stated normal form
  rho_ss=sum_j p_j [ exp(theta J_z) P_j/(f_j z_j(theta)) ]
is 2-producible, for arbitrary probabilities p_j and finite theta. This
conclusion also holds for sector weights giving a central positive X before
the common filter. If a separate witness rules out full separability, its
depth is exactly two. The row lower bound does NOT prove depth two merely
because a nonsymmetric j block has a positive weight.

One must specify the bath/dynamics yielding this normal form. PI alone,
PI plus commutation with J_z, or an unspecified collective bath does not
suffice. A symmetric W_N state is PI, commutes as a density with J_z, and
has genuine N-partite entanglement: across every nonempty proper bipartition
its Schmidt decomposition has two nonzero terms. It is stationary for the
collective pure-dephasing Lindbladian D[J_z], which can satisfy thermal
zero-frequency detailed balance yet does not thermalize each j block.
Thus a blanket statement about every collective-bath stationary state is
false. For the usual finite-temperature J_- and J_+ primitive spin-block
thermalizer, the normal-form gate can be proved separately or imported from
an exact stated fixed-point theorem; this artifact does not use an unnamed
thermalization assertion.

8. PROPER GROUP REPRESENTATIONS ARE A DIFFERENT INTERFACE

The defining full U(d) or SU(d) is essential. Replacing it by any represented
compact group is invalid. For a concrete counterexample use d=3, N=4 and the
spin-one real Cartesian representation of SU(2), whose image is SO(3).
Define a symmetric tensor
  T_abcd=delta_ab delta_cd+delta_ac delta_bd+delta_ad delta_bc,
and normalize its pure state by ||T||^2=3d(d+2)=45. It is invariant under
SO(3)^tensor4 and all site permutations. It is nevertheless genuinely
four-partite entangled: the 1|3 flattening has rank 3; the 2|2 flattening is
|I><I|+I+swap, of rank 6, with eigenvalues 5 on the trace vector and 2 on
the five-dimensional symmetric traceless subspace. By site symmetry these
are all bipartition types. Its depth is four, exceeding local dimension
three. This does not contradict A: the tensor is not globally SU(3)-invariant.
It also does not by itself refute a separately specified physical-spin
Casimir-only Gibbs subclass. That subclass needs its own representation
argument; one cannot obtain it by relabeling d as 2S+1 in Theorem C.

9. ALGORITHMIC INTERFACE AND COST BOUNDARIES

For supplied exact block probabilities q_lambda, the unfiltered ensemble
requires: sample lambda; form its columns; sample one common Haar U; sample
a uniform site permutation; prepare each height-h normalized wedge, apply
U locally, and assign its sites. For fixed d each cluster has at most d
sites and at most d! wedge terms. This description does not require a full
Schur-transform quantum circuit, an exponential state vector, or computing
all N! permutation terms. Sampling one permutation takes O(N) random-index
steps. There are at most (N+1)^{d-1} allowed partitions for fixed d.

Finite gate precision, exact real Haar sampling and access to q_lambda are
separate interfaces. Rounding a common U to operator error a gives tensor
vector error <=N a and pure-state trace-distance error <=N a. Cluster
preparation errors add over the at most N clusters. This supports an
epsilon/N local accuracy choice but is not by itself a finite-bit runtime
certificate. No physical state preparation or sampler is executed here.

For a filtered block, naive Haar rejection with envelope ||g||^(2N) has
success probability A_lambda/||g||^(2N)>=cond(g)^(-2N). This only provides
an exponential worst-case upper bound on expected trials; no polynomial
filtered-sampling assertion follows. In the Gibbs mixture the block
probabilities q'_lambda above must also be acquired to controlled precision.
Efficient exact/approximate sampling from the determinant-reweighted Haar
law is an additional problem, even for fixed d. Root has separately assigned
that algorithmic task; its result should retain its own author and proof.
