# Supplied-metric homogeneous extension: implementation and limits

7 October 2026, cycle 8. **Independent internal implementation gate PASS.** The underlying general theorem is root's `notes/root_cycle8/HIDDEN_CONVEX_POLYNOMIAL_ACQUISITION.md`. The original coefficient-only quartic acquisition has independently passed `notes/equal_charge_audit/cycle8/EXTACTIC_FULL_GATE.md`. Root's separate implementation checks pass at `results/root_cycle8/homogeneous_independent_checks.json`: direct multivariate extactic and every gradient match the ODE jets for a flat strictly convex h=4 contrast and a genuinely nonseparable h=3 contrast; full recoveries match their hidden axes, and a non-strict radial contrast returns UNKNOWN. This is internal validation, not independent external validation or a novelty determination.

## Exact interface

`work/proof_linear/homogeneous_acquisition.py` receives only an expanded rational homogeneous polynomial f of degree 2h, variable names, h>=2, and a **supplied rational positive-definite metric M**. It does not receive hidden L, B, H, axes or condition numbers. Its promise is f=H((B^(1/2)Lx)^2), M=L^TBL, with H homogeneous and strictly convex on the probability simplex. Root also proves the wider quartic alternative of positive pairwise contrasts, which permits indefinite G.

The output is a rational frame with `PASS_UNDER_PROMISE`, or UNKNOWN within the declared trial budget. This is not an unconditional certificate of hidden convexity, axes outside the promise, or nonnegativity. The original full-p quartic API separately acquires M from p_2 and verifies an unconditional positive weighted-square identity.

## Implementation

The cubic-specialized implementation remains unchanged as an independently checked baseline. The new implementation constructs F=(1/2)M^-1 grad f and evaluates the extactic plus its gradient on one rational line by truncated ODE jets. An arbitrary-degree monomial is evaluated by sequential truncated power-series convolution, with first-order dual coefficients for initial-point differentiation. It never enumerates all weak compositions of an ODE coefficient index among the monomial's factors. The work is polynomial in n, numerical degree h and expanded input size, with standard polynomial-time arithmetic primitives.

Each candidate from a rational line root is scored by f(v)/(v^TMv)^h. Before accepting it, exact rational arithmetic checks constrained stationarity and a strictly negative tangent Hessian in the complement of previously accepted axes. After selection the original pool is filtered by exact M-orthogonality. These checks distinguish an actual promised axis from an arbitrary frame making f even.

The API returns the frame and stage diagnostics only. It deliberately avoids densely expanding f(Vz) for variable h: the transformed monomial count could exceed a polynomial bound in n,h and the original sparse input size. Frame invertibility and M-orthogonality are checked. Fixed-degree quartic certificate expansion remains polynomial and is performed by the original API.

Default sampling uses system random draws. Reproducible fixture seeds and explicitly forced lines are labelled diagnostics; they carry no adversarial-input probability guarantee. As before, SymPy backend worst-case complexity is not certified by observed timings.

## Executed checks

`results/proof_linear/homogeneous_replay.json` records six checks:

- Dense hidden rational n=2 and n=3 raw-coupled but **simplex-separable** sextic controls, H=(sum m)m^T(2I+J)m+(1/7)sum m_i^3. On the simplex this is 2 sum m_i^2+1+(1/7)sum m_i^3, so these controls alone do not demonstrate the new coupled scope. The extactic degrees are 6 and 15; recovered frames match the hidden generator retrospectively. All exact stage tests pass.
- A genuinely nonseparable n=3 sextic H=(sum m)sum m_i^2+(1/10)m_1^2 m_2, with dense hidden rational L and hidden B=diag(1,2,3). The API receives f and M=L^TBL only. On the simplex the Hessian of m_1^2m_2 has operator norm at most 2 by its row-sum bound, so the contrast is strongly convex with lower bound 9/5. In affine simplex coordinates m_3=1-m_1-m_2, the mixed third derivatives partial_1^2 partial_2 and partial_1 partial_2^2 differ by 1/5. They would agree for every sum_i g_i(m_i), so this example is genuinely nonseparable. Exact acquisition and all per-stage checks pass.
- The 45-degree false frame for f=(x^2-y^2)^2 is stationary and makes f even, but its tangent Hessian is positive. The negative-Hessian acceptance test rejects it.
- A line through an intersection of hidden factors yields no accepted frame and returns UNKNOWN within one trial.
- The root's indefinite G with pairwise contrasts 1,1,9 has a negative quadratic direction on the simplex. Its dense-hidden rational axes are still acquired under the broader pairwise-contrast theorem, with no nonnegativity claim.

The four saved successful acquisitions took about 2.04 seconds combined in the final diagnostic replay; fixture generation is excluded and earlier development runs are additional work. The genuinely nonseparable example took about 0.861 seconds. This is not a scaling benchmark or a measured speed advantage over another algorithm.

## Next research question

The acquisition bottleneck has moved. Under the exact rational metric and hidden contrast promises, unknown conditioning and approximate deflation are avoidable. The more consequential open interface is acquiring a suitable metric when no quadratic part supplies it, or retaining a comparable acquisition guarantee for perturbed/noisy coefficients and broader certificate families. No result on either extension is claimed here. Historical novelty of the present exact method remains unresolved.
