## Frequency-free estimates and the modular boundary

For f with L2 norm1, continuous filtered jumps L_nu=sqrt(gamma(nu)/(2pi)) integral f(t)e^(i nu t)A(t)dt are a positive integral of GKLS channels. Plancherel applied to vectors gives integral L_nu^*L_nu<=Gamma||A||^2 I, hence dissipator diamond norm<=2Gamma||A||^2. Applied to [O,A(t)]sqrt(rho), it gives the claimed weighted leakage bound. The opposite orientation obeys the same bound because the time-domain commutator is anti-Hermitian; the frequency-domain jumps need not share that property individually.

Right multiplication in Gibbs norm requires an acquired bound m_A=||rho^-1/2 A rho^1/2||. It implies A rho A^*<=m_A^2rho, and consequently ||CA||_(rho,2)<=m_A||C||_(rho,2). Conjugation by the full H preserves this norm. Integrating O(-t)-O then proves

    ||[O,A(t)]||_(rho,2)
       <=c_A+(||A||+m_A)|t|c_H.

For the normalized Gaussian, the second time moment is T^2/2, so Minkowski yields the candidate square-root leakage estimate. No intensive m_A bound is acquired by c_H alone. The proposed counterexample with H=diag(0,E) and A=[[E^2-1,2E],[2E,1-E^2]]/(E^2+1) is a Hermitian unitary with ||AHA-H||=2E^2/(E^2+1)<2, while its modular norm is at least2E exp(beta E/2)/(E^2+1). Thus bounded energy-change norm cannot supply the missing noncommuting exponential order. It is expressly not a uniform-local-intensity lattice counterexample.

## Gaussian convolution and exact temperature shift

For f_T=(pi T^2)^-1/4 exp[-t^2/(2T^2)], its squared Fourier density is(T/sqrt(pi))exp[-T^2(nu-omega)^2]. The fixed physical gamma(nu)=Gamma exp[-(nu-beta sigma^2/2)^2/(2sigma^2)] obeys beta-KMS in the lowering convention, and its correlation is the displayed Gaussian times exp(-i beta sigma^2 t/2), with integrable moments.

Completing the square in the convolution yields exactly

    r_T(omega)=Gamma sqrt(2sigma^2T^2/(1+2sigma^2T^2))
               exp[-T^2(omega-beta sigma^2/2)^2/(1+2sigma^2T^2)].

Dividing reflected rates gives beta_eff=beta·2sigma^2T^2/(1+2sigma^2T^2), strictly below beta at finite T. A KMS input reservoir is therefore insufficient to retain the original Gibbs target after this prescribed finite filter. T is a Gaussian width, not compact time support; truncating it incurs a separate error.

## Exact logical/gauge control and orders of limits

For B=Z_L X_G,H=delta B,A=Z_G, let J_+=P_-A,J_-=P_+A and omega=2delta. The two filtered amplitudes have Gram matrix with diagonal r_T(±omega) and real cross c_T=exp(-T^2omega^2)r_T(0). Positivity is inherited from the integral, not assumed. On rho_beta=(I-tanh(beta delta)B)/4 the cross terms vanish because J_±^2=0. Direct multiplication gives

    D_T(rho_beta)
       =(r_T(omega)+r_T(-omega))
          [tanh(beta delta)-tanh(beta_eff delta)] B/4.

Its trace norm is strictly positive. In every full-H eigenbasis all diagonal entries of [K,rho_beta] vanish for any Hermitian K. Therefore a coherent correction cannot cancel this diagonal stationary-state defect while holding the transition part fixed. This does not prove logical information is lost: irrelevant auxiliary motion can survive recovery.

For O=X_L, [O,J_+] and [O,J_-] are opposite unitaries, giving

    k_T=r_T(omega)+r_T(-omega)-2c_T
        <=2Gamma[1-exp(-omega^2T^2)]<=8Gamma delta^2T^2.

At fixed T it vanishes as delta->0. At fixed nonzero delta, T->infinity produces exact resolved rates; sending delta->0 afterward leaves2gamma(0)>0. This checks the intended noncommuting limit without using arbitrary chosen spectral jumps. It complements, but does not replace, our actual source-seed all-frequency theorem.
