## 2. Exact one-qubit obstruction to applying the classical factorization quantumly

Let H0=aZ, G={I,Z}, V=bX, with a,b nonzero and beta>0. H0 is exactly G-invariant. Twirling the true Gibbs exponential gives

    E_G exp[-beta(aZ+bX)]
       =cosh(beta r)I-(a/r)sinh(beta r)Z, r=sqrt(a²+b²).

But the classical factorization would give

    exp(-beta aZ) E_G exp(-beta bX)
       =cosh(beta b)exp(-beta aZ).

After trace normalization their Z expectations are respectively -(a/r)tanh(beta r) and -tanh(beta a), and differ. For a=b=1 their Taylor difference is beta³/3+O(beta^5). This is an exact minimal failure of the transfer step, not a counterexample to the classical primary theorem or to every possible quantum gauge-averaging theorem. Defining -beta^-1 log(twirled state)-H0 remains formal; acquiring its local interaction expansion with useful constants is the missing quantum theorem.

## 3. May2026 quadratic cancellation is a genuine nonsecular exception

Shiraishi, Nakagawa, Mori, Davies equation without the secular approximation: Reconciling locality with quantum thermodynamics for open quadratic systems, arXiv2507.10080v1, 14 July 2025; Phys.Rev.A113,052212, published 12 May 2026. The publisher verifies the May2026 date; the recoverable July2025 v1 scientific main text and end matter were read in full. The publisher mathematical version is not separately audited. This is a recent publication of an older prerequisite, not a new preprint date.

Exact interface: number-conserving quadratic fermion/boson Hamiltonian H=sum h_jk a_j* a_k, linear particle-exchange couplings to independent, IDENTICAL baths on EVERY site with the SAME strength, common temperature and chemical potential. In a unitary mode matrix V, the Redfield cross coefficient contains sum_j V_mj V_nj*=delta_mn. Hence cross-frequency coherences cancel before any secular approximation, and the Born–Markov Redfield equation is exactly the Davies equation, with its Lamb shift and Gibbs detailed balance. Degeneracies cause no problem for that identity. The Born–Markov approximation itself is still an approximation; no exponentially long, size-uniform microscopic error theorem is proved by the cancellation algebra alone.

The nonlinear/generic many-body discussion is explicitly numerical/heuristic, invokes ETH-style random cancellation, and is left for future study. It is not a rigorous nonsecular CSS thermal-memory bridge. Conversely one must not claim that Davies and locality are categorically incompatible: this exact quadratic, uniformly coupled exception is primary prior.

## 4. Necessary covariance interface and a false sparse-bath example

Within that quadratic setting, put D=diag(J_j²) and W=V D V*. Structural cancellation of all unequal-mode terms for arbitrary allowed bath kernels requires W_mn=0 whenever omega_m!=omega_n. Equivalently [W,diag(omega)]=0, or [D,h]=0 in the site basis. Uniform D is sufficient; on a connected hopping graph with nonzero edges, a diagonal D commuting with h must be constant along that graph. This is the actual covariance/interface condition behind the cancellation mechanism.

The v1 text proposes a periodic chain coupled only at j=0 mod p and claims sum_j J_j² V_mj V_nj*=(1/p)delta_mn. Even using the correct unitary DFT phases, the sum is instead

    W_mn=(1/p) 1_(m-n=0 mod N/p).

Aliased unequal modes survive. More generally rank(W)=N/p, whereas (1/p)I would have rank N, so the displayed claim is impossible for p>1. For N=4,p=2, W_02=1/2; nearest-neighbor hopping gives omega_0=2,omega_2=-2. Thus this side example does not enjoy the proved equal-all-site cancellation. It does not invalidate the central uniform-bath theorem.

The source also has an adjoint-label typo in its microscopic-reversibility statement; its KMS rates and central unitarity mechanism remain clear.
