## 1. The positive-Hessian cone in mass coordinates

First consider one block of dimension n>=2 with t>0, D=I. Write

    m_i=y_i^2, S=sum_i m_i, A=sum_i(1/a_i),
    f(m)=sum_i a_i m_i^2-t S^2,
    d_i=12a_i m_i-4t S,
    H=diag(d_i)-8t yy^T.                       (4)

Positive definiteness of Q is exactly tA<1. Since H>0 implies diag(d)=H+8t yy^T>0, and a negative rank-one update of a positive diagonal matrix is SPD precisely when its scalar Schur complement is positive, H>0 is equivalent to

    d_i>0 for all i,
    u=1-8t sum_i(m_i/d_i)>0.                  (5)

Use m_i=(d_i+4tS)/(12a_i) to obtain

    u=c_0-c_1 S sum_i[1/(a_i d_i)],
    c_0=1-2tA/3>1/3, c_1=8t^2/3,
    h=(product_i d_i) u.                      (6)

The mass cone C defined by (5) is convex. On S=1, the d_i are affine functions of m, all inequalities d_i>0 are linear, and sum_i 1/(a_i d_i)<c_0/c_1 is a convex reciprocal sublevel set. The positive homogeneous cone over that convex slice is convex. Nonemptiness follows at m_i=1/a_i: H=d_0 I-8t yy^T, where d_0=4(3-tA)>0 and its radial eigenvalue is 12(1-tA)>0. These masses are an existence witness only.

Every nonzero point in the closure of C has all masses strictly positive. If m_i=0 and S>0, then H_ii=-4tS<0, inconsistent with positive semidefiniteness. At every nonzero boundary point H is singular, so h=0. This boundary statement is special to the negative contrast and is not being transferred to arbitrary signed QPD.

## 2. A strict root-concavity inequality

Set phi=h^(1/n)>0 on C. Fix S and take an arbitrary mass direction delta m satisfying delta S=0. Put

    w_i=delta d_i/d_i,
    z_i=c_1 S/(a_i d_i u)>0,
    B_z=diag(1+2z_i)+zz^T,
    v=1+z.

Differentiating the explicit expression (6), without differentiating any square roots, gives

    delta log h=v^T w,
    -delta^2 log h=w^T B_z w.                 (7)

Consequently

    delta^2 phi=-(phi/n) w^T[B_z-vv^T/n]w.    (8)

Here B_z-vv^T/n is positive definite for n>=2 and every z_i>0. A full elementary proof is as follows. Let

    p_i=z_i/(1+2z_i) in (0,1/2),
    P=sum_i p_i,
    W=sum_i z_i^2/(1+2z_i)
     =sum_i p_i^2/(1-2p_i).

Sherman-Morrison gives

    v^T B_z^-1 v=n+W-(P+W)^2/(1+W).

This is strictly less than n exactly when P^2+(2P-1)W>0. For P>=1/2 this is immediate. For P<1/2, since n>=2 and every p_i>0,

    W<sum_i p_i^2/(1-2P)<P^2/(1-2P).

This proves the strict inequality, and the rank-one Schur-complement criterion proves B_z-vv^T/n>0.

Equivalently, direct expansion cancels every one-index term and leaves the manifestly positive identity

    P^2+(2P-1)W
       =2 sum_(i<j) z_i z_j(1+z_i+z_j)
                      /[(1+2z_i)(1+2z_j)].    (8a)

This shorter form was independently reconstructed by `metric_geometry10` and is consistent with the free binary symbolic replay.

The linear map delta m -> delta d is invertible: it is 12diag(a)-4t11^T, with determinant proportional to 1-tA/3>0. Thus (8) is strictly negative for every nonzero S-tangent direction. Homogeneity of phi gives Hess(phi)m=0. Subtracting (delta S/S)m from any direction preserves its Hessian quadratic form and makes it S-tangent. It follows that phi is concave on the convex cone C, with a strictly negative Hessian transverse to its radial direction.

For t=0, H=12diag(a_i m_i), C is the positive mass orthant, and phi=12(product_i a_i m_i)^(1/n), the concave geometric mean. It vanishes on the nonzero cone boundary. In dimension1, phi is linear and the projective problem is trivial. No strict t->0 limiting argument is needed.

## 3. The maximum is interior, unique and nondegenerate

Maximize phi on the compact set closure(C) intersect {m^TQm<=1}. The maximum is positive because C is nonempty, and cannot lie on the cone boundary because phi vanishes there. Homogeneity forces m^TQm=1 at the maximum. Call the maximizer m_*; it has full support.

It is unique. If two distinct normalized maximizers existed, their midpoint would remain in C and concavity would give its phi at least the maximal value. Strict convexity of the Q norm gives midpoint f<1; positive radial rescaling to f=1 would then increase phi, a contradiction. The two points cannot be distinct positive multiples because both have f=1.

At m_*, the Lagrange multiplier relation is

    grad phi=(phi/2) grad f,

by Euler homogeneity. Since Hess phi<=0 and Hess f=2Q>0,

    Hess phi-(phi/2) Hess f <0.                (9)

On the f-tangent space, this gives a negative-definite constrained Hessian of log h. The positive-orthant change y_i=sqrt(m_i) is a smooth local diffeomorphism, so it preserves nondegeneracy and full support. The resulting positive ray y_* is therefore a strict nondegenerate projective maximum of h/f^(n/2) in H>0.
