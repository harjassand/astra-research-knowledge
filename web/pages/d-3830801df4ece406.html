## Interface and normalization

Source2609.28391v1 defines proper regret with1/2: F_lambda(rho||sigma)=1/2 sum_a ||S_lambda,a(rho)-S_lambda,a(sigma)||²_sigma,lambda. Its convex potential is G_lambda(rho)=1/2 sum_a Tr[(partial_a rho)^dagger I_rho,lambda^-1(partial_a rho)]. We integrate lambda uniformly over[0,1], keeping known Hermitian directions fixed, and use partial_a=-i[A_a,.]. The root's Fbar without1/2 equals2Gbar. For Hermitian involutions A_a²=I,

Gbar(rho)=sum_a D(rho||A_a rho A_a),
Fbar(rho)=2 sum_a D(rho||A_a rho A_a).

Proof. In a rho eigenbasis integral_0^1 I_rho,lambda^-1 dlambda=Dlog_rho, the Frechet derivative of matrix logarithm. Differentiating a unitary orbit gives Dlog_rho(partial rho)=partial log rho. Thus the full integrated Fisher term is

Tr(partial rho Dlog_rho(partial rho))=−Tr[A,rho][A,log rho]
=2Tr rho(log rho−A log rho A)=2D(rho||A rho A).

Every step holds on the faithful cone. Ordinary relative entropy notation also applies to unnormalized positive matrices with equal trace, so this potential is positively homogeneous of degree1 when both arguments scale together.

## Gradient and copy observable

For one A, self-adjointness of Dlog_rho under Hilbert–Schmidt pairing and Tr rho Dlog_rho(delta)=Tr delta give

grad Gbar_A(rho)=log rho+I−A log rho A−Dlog_rho(A rho A).

Hence the proper source loss observable is Obar_A=−grad Gbar_A. The root's no-half potential and corresponding loss observable double both expressions. Euler homogeneity gives Tr rho Obar_A=−Gbar_A. The expected regret is exactly

Tr sigma[Obar(rho)−Obar(sigma)]=integral_0^1 F_lambda(rho||sigma)dlambda.

This is the convex-potential/Bregman proper-score construction already covered abstractly by Frongillo2203.07469v1 Theorem3.5. It is not the quadratic BKM score discrepancy in source AppendixA.2: the integrated proper loss is linear in sigma, while that separate objective contains Omega_sigma nonlinearly.

Set dimensionless K=beta H, q=e^-K, rho=q/Tr q. Scaling Dlog_{cq}(cX)=Dlog_q(X) and cancellation of the scalar log partition function give the exact candidate expression

Obar_A(K)=K−AKA−I+Dlog_q(AqA).

The target is used only through expectation of this known candidate-dependent Hermitian observable. Neither sigma's matrix/eigenbasis/modular operator nor a candidate Gibbs sample is supplied or used.

## Positive filter factorization

Let B=e^{K/2}Ae^{-K/2}, W=BB^dagger>=0 and k(nu)=nu/[2sinh(nu/2)], with k(0)=1. In a K eigenbasis,

(Dlog_q(AqA))_ij=[(E_j−E_i)/(e^-E_i−e^-E_j)](AqA)_ij
=k(E_i−E_j)W_ij.

Consequently

Dlog_q(AqA)=k(ad_K)(W)
=integral_R (pi/2)sech²(pi t)e^{itK}W e^{-itK}dt.

For completeness, the Fourier identity has an elementary contour proof. For nu>0 integrate f(z)=sech²(pi z)e^{inu z} along a rectangle of heighti and send its horizontal extent to infinity. Its upper edge is e^-nu times its lower edge with reversed orientation. The only pole is the double pole at i/2, with residue−inu e^-nu/2/pi². Thus (1−e^-nu) integral_R sech²(pi t)e^{inu t}dt=2nu e^-nu/2/pi. Multiplication by pi/2 gives k(nu); evenness handles nu<0 and continuity handles nu0. The map is a unital completely positive mixture of unitary conjugations, so the filtered observable is positive and its norm is at most||B||². The exact two-sided tail probability is1−tanh(pi T)<=2e^-2piT.

This makes the cost transparent. Real-time averaging is benign; candidate half-imaginary-time B remains load-bearing. A generic bound||B||<=e^{osc(K)/2} gives no useful volume-independent acquisition. The next theorem supplies a local construction for1D finite range.

## Primary locality theorem and explicit constants

Pérez-García/Pérez-Hernández, arXiv2004.10516v1, verified22Apr2020 12:12:13UTC. Primary PDFpp10–12: Theorem2.3 Eqs16–19 and Theorem2.4. Journal publication was3Jan2023, CMP399:929–970; do not confuse it with the2020 arXiv version. The theorem is for bounded interactions onZ; translation invariance is not required for these locality estimates. The exponential-tail strip theorem does not give arbitrary temperature for a fixed decay exponent; here we specifically assume finite range.

Let H_phys=sum_X Phi_X on a finite interval Lambda of the spin chain, with local dimension d0, interaction diameter at most integer R>=1 and

J=sup_x sum_{X contains x}||Phi_X||<infinity.

Suppose A is a Hermitian involution of norm1, supported in an interval J_A of length q_A. The locality estimate itself holds for arbitrary norm1 observables, but the integrated loss formula used here requires A²=I. Extend all interactions by zero outside the OPEN finite chain Lambda, so the source theorem applies with the same constants and handles endpoints by intersecting intervals with Lambda. A periodic ring requires separate treatment of wrap interactions and is not asserted here. Let J_A,ell enlarge the interval by ell sites on each side, and H_ell be the sum of terms wholly inside that enlargement.

For any mu>=0 define

M_mu=exp[beta J q_A+2beta J R exp((2beta J+mu)R)],
C=M_0.

Then for B=e^{beta H_phys/2}Ae^{-beta H_phys/2} and B_ell=e^{beta H_ell/2}Ae^{-beta H_ell/2},

||B||,||B_ell||<=C,
||B−B_ell||<=M_mu e^-mu(ell+1) for mu>0.

Derivation directly from source, including constants. Write Omega_j=sup_x sum_{X contains x,diam(X)>=j}||Phi_X||, so Omega_j<=J and Omega_j=0 forj>R. At complex time s=−i beta/2 the source bound has prefactor exp(beta J q_A), coefficients Omega*_k(2beta), and factor exp(2beta J k). Its Eq19 generating function is

sum_k Omega*_k(x)z^k=exp[x sum_{j>=1}Omega_j z^j].

Choosing z=exp(2beta J) bounds the norm by M_0. For the tail k>ell, insert z=exp(2beta J+mu) and multiply by e^-mu(ell+1), bounding the finite sum sum_{j=1}^R Omega_j e^{(2betaJ+mu)j} byJR e^{(2betaJ+mu)R}. This proves the displayed locality estimates. No unproved imaginary-time simulation claim is used.

The constants are intentionally loose: log M_mu is exponential in betaJR, and M_mu is double exponential. Fixed beta,J,R,q_A are essential when saying polynomial in accuracy. An onsite-only interaction is trivially local; R>=1 is a harmless conservative convention.

## Charged computation and actual target-copy measurements

For fixed admitted beta,J,R,q_A,d0, the local dimension D=d0^s is polynomial in1/epsilon, with a beta-dependent exponent and a huge beta-dependent prefactor. Dense local construction costs polynomial(D, bit precision), with ordinary matrix arithmetic O(D³) as the leading dense operation; memoryO(D²). Input coefficient precision must control the local matrices to the stated errors. Finite-region exponentials may require O(beta J s+log(1/eta)) working bits beyond input precision; this is charged, not exact-real free computation. A rigorous implementation can use certified matrix exponential/divided-difference approximations; no spectral-gap assumption is required for the operator function k.

The exact positive local filter obeys||That||<=4C²; after numerical error use

M=2beta q_A J+1+4C²+epsilon.

Two physically explicit acquisition options are available.

* Local projective measurement of known Ohat_A has outcome range[-M,M]. After charging construction/synthesis of its D-dimensional basis rotation, N=O(M² epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) independent target copies suffice for expectation errorO(epsilon), by Hoeffding. Arbitrary basis measurement is a capability requirement, not free experimental access.
* For qubits, avoid that requirement by expanding the computed local Hermitian Ohat_A in Pauli strings. Its coefficient mass is at mostD M, by Frobenius orthogonality and Cauchy–Schwarz. Draw one string proportional to coefficient magnitude, measure its local single-qubit factors on a fresh target copy, and return the signed product times that mass. N=O(D²M² epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) copies suffice. Classical coefficient storage/processingO(D²) and local product measurement on s sites are charged. This uses only target copies and ordinary local Pauli measurements; no candidate Gibbs preparation, target tomography or global evolution is needed.

These are per-generator/per-candidate guarantees. For m directions summing local losses, estimating each toepsilon/m with failuredelta/m gives a valid conservative total-loss estimator; a claimed shared-copy or improved total scaling needs a separate argument. Optimization over many candidates, uniform empirical fitting and parameter recovery require further coverage/stability/optimization analysis. Nothing in this construction makes the nonconvex objective easy to optimize.

An elementary shared-data variant supports fitting many candidates without new measurements per candidate. Let the m required factor windows have at most s qubits each. Every admitted approximate factor has norm<=M, hence Pauli coefficient mass<=D M,D=2^s. On every target copy measure each qubit independently in a uniformly chosen X,Y,Z basis. For a Pauli string P of weightk, form X_P=3^k times its measured outcome product if all its nonidentity bases match P, and0 otherwise. The Born rule and matching probability3^-k give E X_P=Tr(sigma P), while|X_P|<=3^s. Hoeffding and a union bound over at mostm4^s required strings show

N=O(9^s D²M² epsilon^-2 log(m4^s/delta))

copies suffice to estimate every required moment to errornu=epsilon/(D M). For every candidate factor, the expectation error is then at most(sum_P|c_P|)nu<=epsilon. This uniform statement does not require a statistical union bound over a parameter net, because the same moment-event controls all candidate coefficient vectors. Each copy uses n local basis measurements; collecting/storing raw outcomes and computing the moment table are charged polynomial costs in n,N andm4^s. This is ordinary local Pauli/shadow estimation, not a new measurement principle or free target information. Primary prior is Huang–Kueng–Preskill2002.08953v1 (verified18Feb2020 19:00:02UTC), SectionI.2 Eqs2–3 and SectionI.3 Theorem1, with random Pauli bounds in AppendixA.2 Example2. Total loss errors still sum over factors; root's averaged-loss convention and stability determine the per-factor precision needed for recovery.

## Uniform coefficient windows and a charged continuity bound

All constants depend only on a uniform admitted interaction-strength bound J, beta,R,q_A. The truncation guarantee is therefore uniform over every candidate in a coefficient box satisfying that bound, not merely one candidate. The exact finite-window observable depends only on terms wholly inside L, and the local difference H−AHA depends only on terms intersecting A. This gives an actual factor oracle for finite coefficient-grid methods.

For qubit Pauli coefficients in a common known support list, let delta be the maximum change in any coefficient, and let m_A,m_w,m_L count intersect-A terms and terms wholly inside the two windows. Along a straight interpolation that stays in the admitted strength bound, operator Duhamel estimates give

||B_ell(theta)−B_ell(theta')||<=beta e^{beta Jw}m_w delta.

Both B_ell norms are<=C by the locality theorem; hence their W norms differ by at most2C beta e^{beta Jw}m_w delta. For the real-time filtered W, unitary Duhamel gives a further difference bounded by2beta E|t| C² m_L delta. The logistic mean absolute time islog2/pi, by integrating its exact tail. Thus the exact finite-window loss factor is Lipschitz in coefficient max norm with constant

L_window=2beta m_A+2C beta e^{beta Jw}m_w+(2beta log2/pi)C²m_L.

This is independent of total chain length when the supplied support list has bounded per-site term count. It grows with the approximation window; at w=O(1)+O(log1/epsilon) it is polynomial in1/epsilon for fixed admitted constants. Numeric coefficient rounding and matrix evaluation add their separately budgeted operator errors. This continuity bound does not establish an efficient global optimizer; it makes the factor-oracle/grid premise explicit.
