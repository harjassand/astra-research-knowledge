# A lower-order obstruction at gamma = 1

Worker d_prior, 2026-10-06. This is an independently reconstructed universal argument. The rational script checks supporting finite identities; those checks are not the proof. No claim of historical originality for the lattice/Jensen method is made.

## Interface and result

There are three known states i in {0,1,2}. Source action u in {0,1,2} costs one and returns an independent Bernoulli observation with success probability 4/5 when u=i and 1/5 otherwise. Actions may be selected adaptively; auxiliary randomization is state independent. The source stops almost surely and reports a finite output.

The target iid ternary experiment returns i with probability 1/2 and each other symbol with probability 1/4 under state i. Each target observation costs beta=5/24. The target report is the entire length-n word, or just its histogram. Either report has the same pairwise likelihood ratio and KL divergence.

For every exact source reproduction, every state i, and n>=1,

`E_i sourcecost >= (5/24)n + (5/36)[1-(-1/2)^n]`.

Thus all statewise first-order gauge factors are gamma_i=1, but exact same-budget large-cost stopping dominance fails: length-n target reports have cost B_n=(5/24)n, and no source policy can reproduce them with cost at most B_n. The failure holds for arbitrarily large B_n. The excess lower bound tends to 5/36 source observations. This does not invalidate a bound with an additive or square-root remainder, nor strict gamma<1 sufficiency.

## Gauge coefficient

For a fixed pair i,j, a source observation has KL D=(3/5)log4=(6/5)log2 at actions i or j, and zero at the remaining action. Target pairwise KL is g=(1/4)log2. For state i, the information-cost LP must satisfy `D(w_i+w_j)>=g` for both j!=i. Each inequality implies total cost sum_u w_u>=g/D. Setting w_i=g/D and others zero satisfies both. Hence phi_i(g,g)=g/D=5/24, and dividing by beta gives gamma_i=1 for all i.

## Stopped transcript likelihood lattice

Fix i!=j. Let X be the complete source transcript, including all state-independent randomization needed to make the report Y a deterministic function. Action-selection, stopping, and randomization factors cancel between states. Each observation contributes likelihood ratio 4, 1/4, or 1, so

`L(X)=dP_i^X/dP_j^X=4^K`, with K an integer almost surely.

This remains true with random stopping and arbitrary adaptive choices. If expected source length is infinite, the stated cost lower bound is immediate. Otherwise predictable summation gives

`D(P_i^X || P_j^X)=D E_i[N_i+N_j]`.

Finite alphabets, bounded log increments, and integrable length justify the identity. Exact output reproduction requires the target output law H. For output y, put `r=H_i(y)/H_j(y)`. Conditional on Y=y under state j, `E_j[L|y]=r`. The conditional likelihood ratio between the two transcript laws is L/r. KL chain rule therefore gives

`D(P_i^X||P_j^X) - D(H_i||H_j) = sum_y H_i(y) D(P_i^X(.|y)||P_j^X(.|y))`.

## A quantitative Jensen gap

Suppose r is an odd power of 2. Then t=L/r ranges over the lattice `{...,1/8,1/2,2,8,...}`. Its conditional mean under state j is one. Let h(t)=t log t. The line through h at t=1/2 and t=2 is

`ell(t)=[(5t-4)/3]log2`.

Convexity of h makes ell(t)<=h(t) at every lattice point: there are no permitted points in the open interval (1/2,2), and the secant extension lies below a convex function outside that interval. Taking conditional expectations yields

`D(P_i^X(.|y)||P_j^X(.|y)) = E_j[h(t)|y] >= ell(1)=(1/3)log2`.

The coefficient is optimal among arbitrary lattice-valued conditional laws: t=1/2 with probability 2/3 and t=2 with probability 1/3 under state j has mean one and attains it. Actual source implementability can only raise the cost. For outputs with even likelihood exponent, the ordinary nonnegative KL bound suffices.

For the target histogram with counts c, r=2^(c_i-c_j). A draw changes the parity of c_i-c_j precisely when its symbol is i or j; under state i this has probability 3/4. Consequently

`P_i(c_i-c_j odd)=[1-(-1/2)^n]/2`.

The target report KL is n g. Combining the preceding identities gives

`D E_i[N_i+N_j] >= n g + (log2/6)[1-(-1/2)^n]`.

Since total source observations dominate N_i+N_j, division by D=(6/5)log2 proves the announced lower bound. All three states obey it simultaneously.

## General form and interpretation

For any positive output ratio r between consecutive source lattice points a=4^k and b=4a, the minimal conditional loss allowed by the likelihood lattice is

`J(r)=((b-r)/(b-a))*(a/r)*log(a/r) + ((r-a)/(b-a))*(b/r)*log(b/r)`.

It is strictly positive for a<r<b and zero at the endpoints. Therefore one may strengthen ordinary transcript data processing by `sum_y H_i(y) J(H_i(y)/H_j(y))`. This is a convex-order obstruction to exactness, not a testing-error approximation or renewal heuristic.

Equality can also permit dominance: taking source and target to be identical experiments with identical costs gives gamma=1 and exact copying with no overhead. Thus gamma=1 alone cannot decide the budget order; lower-order likelihood structure matters. Li–Liu explicitly raises this boundary question in the conclusion, PDF p15, but the read passage does not supply the present quantitative example.
