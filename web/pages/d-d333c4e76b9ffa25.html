## Interface, quantitative theorem, and comparator

Input: a SUPPLIED open rectangular cluster graph with w rows, even n columns, N=wn sites; its known nearest-neighbour CZ circuit U; rational t in [0,1) with b-bit numerator/denominator. State rho=U[(I+tX)/2]^(tensor N)U†. This is the Gibbs state of H=-J sum_v X_v product_(u neighbouring v)Z_u, with t=tanh(beta J). The exact rational sampler's input is t. Approximating an arbitrary real beta is separately charged and does not supply an exact transcendental probability primitive.

Put m=2w, d=2^m, a=(1-t)^m, q=[(1+t)/(1-t)]^m=exp(4 beta Jw), s=1-1/q. Choose any integer B>=lnq; the bitlength difference plus1 of q's rational numerator and denominator is sufficient. The closed choice

L=1+ceil[q(1+B)]

gives an exact positive mixture of pure stabilizer states, each a product over contiguous complete-column blocks containing at most8wL sites. This is O(w(1+beta Jw)exp(4 beta Jw)). A sharper sufficient L is the smallest integer with

z s^(L-1)<=1, z=[(1+t)^m+(1-t)^m-2]/(1-t)^m.

The ordinary known-graph spectral mixture samples independent X eigenstates then applies U; it already computes Z=[2cosh(beta J)]^N and Pauli observables efficiently. It is the competent equally informed control. Our construction has no trace-estimation, thermodynamic, or general computational advantage. Its additional output is an exact finite spatial-block certificate and an explicit positive stabilizer ensemble realizing it. This is stronger than merely decaying endpoint correlations or a protocol-specific entanglement length.

## Independent proof

Each elementary block consists of two complete columns. On it let G=[(I+tX)/2]^(tensor m), g_min=[(1-t)/2]^m, G'=g_min I, Delta=G-G'. Since the spectrum of G consists of

g_x=[(1+t)/2]^(m-|x|)[(1-t)/2]^|x|,

we have G'<=G<=qG', Delta>=0, trG'=a, trDelta=alpha=1-a. The explicit X-product eigenstates |x_X> diagonalize both G and Delta. Whitened residual eigenvalues are lambda_x=1-g_min/g_x, all rational, and max lambda_x=s. There is no eigensolve.

Group elementary blocks into superblocks of length ell in[L,2L-1]. For M=n/2>=L take floor(M/L) groups and distribute the remaining fewer than L blocks; for M<L use the original graph ensemble on the whole strip, already shorter than the claimed block bound. Thus the work never depends on constructing an enormous group longer than the input.

On one superblock set

Q0=G'_0 tensor G_1 tensor...tensor G_(ell-1)+Delta_0 tensor...tensor Delta_(ell-1),

Qb=Delta_0 tensor...tensor Delta_(b-1) tensor G'_b tensor G_(b+1) tensor...tensor G_(ell-1), 1<=b<ell.

By the telescoping product identity sum_b Qb=G^(tensor ell). Let E_b be the w CZ gates crossing the midpoint of elementary blockb. All graph CZ gates commute; U E_b is exactly the circuit with those w crossing gates deleted, hence factorizes across this complete-column cut. G' commutes with E_b and is a scalar PRODUCT operator. For b>=1, sample X-product eigenstates for Delta/G and computational product states for G'. Their central E_b† transform differs only by a global phase. Applying U realizes pure states with this cut deleted.

For Q0, the X labels T of the other ell-1 blocks leave central operator G'+r_T Delta, with r_T=product lambda_(x_j). After conjugating central by E_0 this is

g_min[I+r_T K], K=E_0 G E_0/g_min-I.

Whitening is scalar. The crossing graph is a matching of w edges. Its stabilizers are X_left Z_right and Z_left X_right. Their same-edge product is YY, with positive sign. Therefore K's identity coefficient is c0=1/a-1; its other d-1 nonzero tensor-Pauli coefficients are t^|A|/a, A a nonempty subset of the m X generators. In tensor-Pauli notation their signs are positive for this matching. The sum S of their magnitudes is [(1+t)^m-1]/a. Hence S-c0=z>=0 and

I+r_T K=(1-r_T z)I+sum_(A nonempty) r_T t^|A|/a (I+P_A).

Every I+P_A is positive and diagonal in a product Pauli eigenbasis. This is an explicit positive PRODUCT decomposition whenever r_T z<=1. Since r_T<=s^(ell-1), the sharper L suffices. For the closed L, z<=q and s^k<=exp(-k/q), so

z s^(L-1)<=q exp[-(1+B)]<=exp(-1)<1.

For each central product vector |p>, input representation uses E_0†|p>. Applying U then equals applying the graph circuit with that midpoint crossing edge set deleted to |p>. Other sites are X eigenstates. Each superblock thus supplies one full-column cut. Different superblock choices remain independent because the input operator is a tensor product and U is unitary. Two adjacent chosen cuts are separated by fewer than8L columns; end segments are shorter. The pure output therefore factorizes into contiguous blocks of at most8wL sites. These outputs are stabilizer states: Pauli product inputs followed by CZ gates. Across a vertical cut their Schmidt rank is at most2^w (one crossing CZ per row); deleting the designated crossing edges gives rank1 there. No dense2^(8wL) block vector is constructed.

## Complete exact sampler and bit charges

The superblock trace weights are w0=a+alpha^ell, wb=a alpha^b for b1,...,ell-1; they sum to1. Draw b. If b>=1, draw the labels of preceding factors with probabilities (g_x-g_min)/alpha, following factors with g_x, and central computational bits uniformly.

For b0, an other-block tuple T has unnormalized weight

(a+alpha r_T) product_j g_(x_j)

=a product_j g_(x_j)+alpha product_j (g_(x_j)-g_min).

Thus choose a two-way branch with masses a and alpha^ell, then independently draw each label from g_x or (g_x-g_min)/alpha. Compute r_T. The central Pauli-term probabilities have unnormalized rational masses

v_identity=a(1-r_T z), v_A=r_T t^|A|.

Their total is a+alpha r_T. For the identity term choose computational bits uniformly. For P_A, choose its product Pauli eigenstates uniformly subject to product eigenvalue+1: choose all but one nonidentity eigenvalue using fair bits, fix the last by parity, and choose identity-axis sites in the computational basis. This exactly samples (I+P_A)/d. Output these central product axes/signs and all other X signs, and retain every graph CZ gate except the designated w crossing gates. No exponential tuple enumeration occurs; no probability is clipped; no local spectral operator is assumed.

Local tables have d=4^w rational eigenvalues and d Pauli terms. Precomputation O(w4^w) rational operations; output and sampling use O(N4^w) rational operations with naive table scans. The output is O(N) Pauli labels and CZ gate bits; Clifford simulators receive the same structure as our sampler. Rational products, cumulative weights and integer totals have O(C_w N b) bitlength for C_w=2^{O(w)}. Using schoolbook integer/rational operations yields the deliberately loose bound O(C_w N³ b²) bit work and O(C_w N² b) retained-table/intermediate storage. Group sizes are at mostN/w, so this bound does not contain an unexecuted factor L when L>N/w. A streaming implementation can use less memory.

To draw rational weights, take the LCM of their denominators, multiply into integer weights, and draw an exact uniform integer below their total using fair bits and rejection. Acceptance is at least1/2. Each draw costs expected at most2(bitlength(total)+1) random bits. With O(C_w N) discrete draws this gives a conservative O(C_w N² b) total expected random-bit bound. LCM computation and the integer comparisons are inside the charged rational work. These are algorithmic upper bounds, not a claim that fractions with N-bit numerators have constant cost. A PRNG is used only to replay the specified exact fair-bit algorithm in code.

The state identity holds for all real t<1. For a real-temperature input, compute rational t' with |t-t'|<=epsilon/N and sample its exact ensemble: tensor telescoping gives ||rho_t-rho_t'||_1<=N|t-t'|<=epsilon. This is an approximation to the original state, explicitly distinct from the exact rational theorem. The geometry/temperature bound and exact zero-entanglement conclusion for real t follow directly from the operator proof; they are not certified by the rational approximation alone.

## Measurement consequence, prior art, novelty gate

Under single-copy LOCC among complete columns, product ancillas, and any nonzero-probability postselection, two column regions at separation greater than8L yield a separable conditional output. Every pure component has some separating full-column cut; LOCC preserves separability across it; tracing helpers leaves a product on the same two ends. Conditioning then gives a positive mixture of such products even when component cuts differ. Nonzero localized entanglement at distance R therefore requires R=O((1+beta Jw)exp(4beta Jw)), and at fixed beta J>0,

w>=lnR/(4beta J)-O_beta(loglogR).

This consequence is NOT promoted as historically new. [Raussendorf–Bravyi–Harrington quant-ph/0407255v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0407255v2), revised17June2005, already studies single-copy thermal end entanglement, a high-temperature zero-LE bound from VBS separability (SectionIII), and logarithmic transverse-size sufficiency in 3D error-corrected geometry (footnote1/remarks to SectionII). [Kay–Pachos–Dür–Briegel quant-ph/0606177](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0606177), submitted21June2006, analyzes MULTI-copy optimal purification; its threshold cannot substitute for this single-copy interface.
