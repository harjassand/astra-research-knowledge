2. Fractional continuum resolvent regularity

Let V be the direct sum of sqrt(2) times the pair traces. On the full
unsymmetrized cube, a pair-trace heat kernel is bounded by the line kernel

 [1/(4pi Jt)] exp[-(s-s')^2/(4Jt)].

Its integral in either variable is 1/(2sqrt(pi Jt)). Remaining coordinates
are heat contractions, and restriction to symmetry cannot increase norm.
Writing ||V exp(-tT/2)||^2 as a direct-sum square gives a SUM over the p
pair norms, not a p^2 factor. Therefore

 ||V exp(-tT)V*||<=p/(2sqrt(pi Jt)).

For 1<s<3/2, use the positive Laplace integral for (T+1)^(s-2):

 ||(T+1)^(s/2-1)V*||^2
 =||V(T+1)^(s-2)V*||
 <=p Gamma(3/2-s)/[2sqrt(pi J)Gamma(2-s)]=c_(m,s)^2.

The power in the integral at zero is t^(1/2-s); this is integrable exactly
in the stated range. The bounded extension justifies the distributional
resolvent identity. For u=(A_g+1)^(-1)f, spectral calculus gives
q_g(u)<=||f||^2/4 and ||Vu||<=||f||/(2sqrt(g)). Hence

 ||(T+1)^(s/2)u|| <=[1+sqrt(g_+)c_(m,s)/2]||f||.

No second-derivative regularity across a contact cusp is used. At s=5/4,
let the constant be K_m. The zero-extension trace inequality gives
B_c(F)<=[4p/sqrt(2J)](<F,TF>+||F||^2); thus with
D_m=1+4g_+p/sqrt(2J),

 q_g(F)<=D_m||(T+1)^(1/2)F||^2.

The tensor sine cutoff P_M commutes with T. If E_M=2Jpi^2(M+1)^2,
then for ||f||<=1 the omitted tail v=u-P_Mu obeys

 ||v||<=K_m(1+E_M)^(-5/8)=z_m,
 sqrt(q_g(v))<=sqrt(D_m)K_m(1+E_M)^(-1/8)=a_m.

These constants have only polynomial m dependence before the sampling
stage. In particular the kinetic tail does not silently lose a Sobolev
power at the diagonals.

3. Low-sine Gram and exact cell comparison

For symmetric F with all sine labels <=M<=n, define F_d(z)=h^(m/2)F(hz).
The one-particle discrete sine identity makes this an exact isometry.
The canonical inverse sampler is JF(y)=sqrt(m!)F_d(y); it is not an
isometry. Its Gram identity is

 ||JF||^2-||F||^2=sum_z[t(z)-1]|F_d(z)|^2.

A one-variable low-mode evaluation at any interior node has norm square
at most 2Mh. Conditional on all other coordinates, a pair tie fixes one
such variable. Sum over the other coordinates. The tie probability is
therefore <=2Mh||F||^2 for each pair. Since t-1 vanishes on distinct
tuples and is <=m!-1, a union bound yields exactly

 0<=||JF||^2-||F||^2<=eta_m||F||^2,
 eta_m=2(m!-1)pMh=(m!-1)m(m-1)Mh.

Let S_hF be the centered ordinary step function with amplitudes F_d.
I JF has amplitudes sqrt(t)F_d. Consequently

 ||I JF-S_hF||^2=sum_z(sqrt(t)-1)^2|F_d(z)|^2
                  <=eta_m||F||^2.

The inequality is valid at ALL coincidence multiplicities, because
(sqrt(t)-1)^2<=t-1 for t>=1.

Integrating a sine over its centered cell gives
<phi_k,S_h phi_l>=delta_kl sinc(pi k h/2). Both maps are isometries.
For a product label the overlap is the product of these sinc factors;
their arguments are below pi/2, so the factors lie in [0,1]. Using
1-product a_i<=sum(1-a_i) and 1-sinc x<=x^2/6 gives

 ||S_hF-F||^2<=pi^2 m M^2 h^2||F||^2/12.

This proves the displayed recovery norm estimate (3.3), including the
outer strips where S_h is zero. Those strips are already accounted for
by the exact overlap calculation.

4. Exact contact quadrature

At x_i=x_j=s, F contains two retained sine factors in s; each product is
a cosine polynomial of frequencies at most 2M. In |F|^2 there are four
such factors, so the collapsed-variable frequencies are at most 4M.
For every other coordinate the frequencies are at most 2M. Complex
coefficients only change the coefficients of the finite cosine expansion.

For N=n+1, the trapezoid functional

 (1/N)[f(0)/2+sum_{z=1}^{N-1}f(z/N)+f(1)/2]

equals the integral for every cos(pi k s) with 0<=k<2N. For odd k the
sum cancels by reflection. For nonzero even k below 2N it vanishes by
the finite geometric sum; k=0 is integrated exactly. Thus N>2M rules
out every possible diagonal alias at frequency 4M.

The collapsed integrand vanishes at every outer endpoint: in s both
coincident sine factors vanish, and in another coordinate its sine factor
vanishes. Therefore the trapezoid rule reduces to the interior nodal sum.
Tensorizing proves exactly

 h^(m-1)sum_{z_i=z_j}|F(hz)|^2=||Tr_ij F||^2.

The boundary N=2M does fail in general: with m=2,M=1,N=2 and
F(x,y)=2sin(pi x)sin(pi y), the collapsed integral is 3/2 while its
interior nodal sum is 2. The proof deliberately uses strict N>2M.
This exact lemma eliminates an
M-dependent, dimension-dependent supremum/Riemann error term.

5. Canonical kinetic excess and all ghost faces

For a chamber edge incrementing v to v+1 the number of labeled preimages
is q_e=w(y)r_v=m!/[(r_v-1)!r_(v+1)!prod_other r_u!]. Ghost-normal faces
have the corresponding endpoint multiplicity w(y)r_1 or w(y)r_n. These
are positive integers at most m!. If both endpoint tuples are distinct,
q_e=m!; otherwise a source or destination pair tie occurs. Thus the
canonical-minus-labeled energy is supported on those bad edges and
(m!-q_e)<=(m!-1)q_e.

For an edge in direction a and a source/destination pair tie, choose an
implicated coordinate b DIFFERENT FROM a. Conditional on every other
coordinate, the edge difference remains low-sine in b. The tie fixes one
value of b, possibly shifted by one from the moved coordinate. Evaluation
has norm square <=2Mh. There are at most 2p endpoint/pair tests, so
the bad labeled energy is <=4pMh times all labeled energy.

The argument applies to ghost-normal edges because the unmoved coordinate
is still a retained sine coordinate; its ghost value is zero. Tangential
ghost edges have identically zero difference. It does not require applying
an evaluation inequality to the moved coordinate after differencing.

The low-mode labeled finite kinetic eigenvalues are no greater than their
continuum counterparts. Hence, exactly with the stated constants,

 q_L,kin(JF)<=(Lh)^2[1+4(m!-1)pMh]<F,TF>.

No extra labeled-to-ordered factorial is needed: q_e already counts the
edge lifts, and the factor m!-1 explicitly charges the excess.

6. Multiple-tie contact excess

At a labeled tuple z define P(z)=sum_v r_v(r_v-1)/2 and W=sum_v(r_v-1).
The canonical contact contribution, expressed as a LABELED sum, has
coefficient gL tW. The labeled pair contribution has coefficient 2gL P.
Thus the exact excess is gL(tW-2P)|F_d(z)|^2. It is nonnegative, at most
gL m!m |F_d(z)|^2, and zero for a tuple with just one pair tie.

Every remaining collision pattern enforces at least two distinct pair
equalities. Any two distinct edges in the equality graph form a forest:
either a three-vertex path or two independent edges. Choose two leaves,
one from each edge in the latter case. Conditional on the roots and all
remaining coordinates, BOTH leaves can be evaluated together in the
original two-variable tensor low-mode space. Its evaluation norm square
is at most (2Mh)^2, even if the two roots have the same value.

Union-bounding at most p^2 such edge choices gives multiple-tie mass
<=4p^2M^2h^2||F||^2. This does not assume that a post-evaluation root
function remains low-sine. Exact quadrature from Section 4 gives the
labeled pair term (Lh)q_g,contact(F). Consequently

 q_L,contact(JF)<=(Lh)q_g,contact(F)
                   +4g_+L m!m p^2M^2h^2||F||^2.

For L>=2m, h<=2/L, (Lh)^2<=4 and (Lh)^2-1<=8m/L.
Also <F,TF><=2Jpi^2 mM^2||F||^2. Combining the two excess bounds for
||F||<=1 gives precisely

 q_L(JF)<=q_g(F)+(8m/L)q_g(F)
       +64Jpi^2(m!-1)pmM^3/L+16g_+m!m p^2M^2/L.

The M powers are 3 and 2, independent of m. All growth in m is exposed
through the displayed factorial and polynomial factors.

7. Q1 interpolation transfer and its cross term

For arbitrary source psi, interpolate u_z=h^(-m/2)Kpsi(z), with zero
ghost faces, by S_Lpsi. On each Q1 cell a partial derivative is an edge
difference divided by h. Convexity, integrated tensor weights and the
2^(m-1) sharing of an interior edge give the sharp inequality

 ||grad S_Lpsi||^2<=E_h(u).

Within a centered nodal cell, every interpolation vertex is within one
coordinate step of the node. A path to it uses at most m edges. Convexity
and path Cauchy--Schwarz bound the interpolation error by m times the
sum of squared differences in that node's radius-one star. A fixed edge
can lie in at most 2*3^(m-1)<=3^m such stars. Integrating the cell volumes
gives the bulk estimate m3^m h^2 E_h and, for a collapsed diagonal cell,
the trace estimate m3^m h E_h. Ghost cells have at most the same volumes
and stencil incidence. These local grid counts are independent of m!.

With q_L(psi)<=1, the canonical free comparison and Lh>=1 give
E_h<=1/(2J). The exact polar/K-step difference has norm <=sqrt(<W>)
<=(g_-L)^(-1/2). It follows that

 ||Ipsi-S_Lpsi||<=d=(g_-L)^(-1/2)+h sqrt(m3^m/(2J)).

For a sharper tracking of the contact error, let D_ij be the collapsed
nodal step and e_ij=Tr_ij S_Lpsi-D_ij. Set
b=sqrt(m3^m h/(2J)). Then

 sum||D_ij||^2<=q_L,contact/(2g),
 sum||e_ij||^2<=p b^2 q_L,kin.

The cross term is therefore bounded by

 4g sqrt(sum||D_ij||^2 sum||e_ij||^2)
 <=2sqrt(2gp)b sqrt(q_L,contact q_L,kin)
 <=2sqrt(2gp)b q_L.

The last inequality is deliberately loose. The squared-error term is
<=2gp b^2 q_L. Along with the sharp kinetic direction, this proves

 q_g(S_Lpsi)<=(1+e)q_L(psi),
 e=2sqrt(2g_+p)b+2g_+p b^2.

There is no missing p or sqrt(p) factor in the candidate's e_m.

9. Heat and positive trace upgrade

For R=(A+1)^(-1), the function
B_k(R)=(k/tau)R[I+(k/tau-1)R]^(-1) vanishes at R=0. For k>=tau, its
resolvent difference has norm <=(k/tau)||R_L-R||. Telescoping the k-th
powers multiplies this by at most k. The scalar backward-Euler error is
<=2/k on [0,infinity): for x<=k, use x-klog(1+x/k)<=x^2/(2k) and
exp[-klog(1+x/k)]<=exp(-x/2); for x>=k, use 2^(-k)<=1/k.
Thus heat error is <=4/k+k^2 eta/tau. The public choice in Section 6
gives omega_m<=min(2,H_tau F_m^(1/3)L^(-1/96)).

Choose public R_energy>=0 and let Q=floor(sqrt(R_energy/(2Jpi^2))).
The count of free symmetric energies <=R_energy is <=
K=binom(Q+m-1,m), or zero at Q=0. Min-max gives continuum heat
eigenvalue t_(K+1)<=exp(-tau R_energy). Embedded finite eigenvalues,
with zero padding, differ labelwise by <=omega. The half-heat trace
B_m=[e C_tau]^m therefore bounds their respective eigenvalue tails by

 B_m exp(-tau R_energy/2),
 B_m[exp(-tau R_energy/2)+sqrt(omega)].

The total trace difference is <=Komega+B_m[2r+sqrt(omega)], where
r=exp(-tau R_energy/2). Let P be any analytical rank-K projection onto
the first continuum heat eigenvectors. Its continuum out-of-block trace
is <=B_m r. The finite one is at most

 2Komega+B_m[3r+sqrt(omega)].

Indeed subtract the rank-K block from the total trace, using the previous
trace difference and the additional Komega block trace bound.

For a positive trace-class operator T, purification and projection give
||T-PTP||_1<=2sqrt(Tr T * Tr((I-P)T)). Applying this to both operators
and using sqrt(a+b+c)<=sqrt(a)+sqrt(b)+sqrt(c) gives

 ||heat_L-heat||_1<=Komega+2sqrt(2B_m Komega)
                    +6B_m exp(-tau R_energy/4)+2B_m omega^(1/4).

The coefficient 6 is valid since 2(sqrt(3)+1)<6. This also covers K=0
and finite ranks smaller than K. Degeneracies cause no difficulty: the
projection is analytical and any eigenbasis within a degenerate space
works. No interacting eigenvectors or cluster gaps are supplied to an
archive, and only the public free spectral rank enters the certificate.
