# r03_quantum: score accessibility is not identifiability

Status: an exact obstruction and independently reconstructed fiber theorem; novelty unconfirmed. Not a refutation of the seed paper. Team B is independently checking and extending this lead.

## Question and interface

Given copies of an unknown full-rank finite-dimensional quantum state sigma, select known Hermitian derivation operators A_a. Define partial_a(O)=-i[A_a,O] and the symmetric score S_a(rho) by partial_a(rho)={rho,S_a(rho)}/2. Can a small ordinary irreducible frame guarantee that matching these scores identifies sigma, at all finite temperatures?

The interface supplies the frame, copies of sigma, and the candidate state/model description. It does not supply tomography of sigma, its eigenbasis, or its modular evolution. State identifiability, coefficient identifiability modulo additive identity, statistical conditioning, and computational accessibility are separate questions.

## Primary-source starting point

[Dong and Leng, 2609.28391v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28391v1), submitted 2026-09-23, gives a copy-expectation score loss (Lemma 7), a sufficient transformed-commutant identifiability condition (Lemma 6 / Appendix A.1 Theorem 15), and a high-temperature guarantee for bounded-locality bounded-degree Pauli Hamiltonians using local X/Z (Theorems 9–11). Endpoint scores keep ordinary irreducibility sufficient (Lemma 16). Appendix A.2 explains why the linear BKM score has a target-dependent nonlinear quadratic metric. Their sample-optimal high-temperature rate matches earlier Hamiltonian learning rather than improving its minimax rate.

[Shukla, Jayakumar, Lokhov, 2609.25246v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25246v1), submitted 2026-09-21, independently studies the endpoint operator score, numerical Hamiltonian recovery, and local optimization conditions (Appendices B–D). Its global identification argument uses conjugation rather than the SLD filter.

These are author results. Everything below is our reconstruction/deduction. Recoverable HTML snapshots and metadata are in sources/r03_quantum/.

## Exact score fiber theorem

Let sigma>0 have trace 1. Define

K_a = sigma^(-1/2) (A_a-i S_a(sigma)/2) sigma^(1/2).

In an eigenbasis sigma=diag(p_i), the defining Lyapunov equation gives

S_a(sigma)_ij = 2i (p_i-p_j)/(p_i+p_j) (A_a)_ij,

K_a,ij = 2 sqrt(p_i p_j)/(p_i+p_j) (A_a)_ij.

Thus K_a is Hermitian. For every rho>0, **all its SLD scores equal sigma's scores iff**

rho=sigma^(1/2) Y sigma^(1/2), Y>0, tr(sigma Y)=1, [Y,K_a]=0 for every a.

Proof: the score equation for an unknown rho with fixed S=S_a(sigma) rearranges exactly to

(A-iS/2) rho = rho (A+iS/2).

The two coefficients equal sigma^(1/2)K sigma^(-1/2) and sigma^(-1/2)K sigma^(1/2), respectively. Multiply on left and right by sigma^(-1/2). The equation becomes [K,Y]=0. Conversely, that commutation implies the score equation; uniqueness of its Hermitian solution follows because all eigenvalue sums of rho are positive. Positivity and trace constraints are precisely those stated. QED.

Over the unrestricted full-rank state class, the scalar-commutant condition is therefore necessary as well as sufficient. If the Hermitian commutant contains non-scalar B, put B0=B-tr(sigma B)I. Then Y=I+tB0 is positive for sufficiently small nonzero t, normalized, and distinct. The fiber is a convex section of a positive cone and can contain an entire flat line of exact population-loss minimizers. In a restricted Hamiltonian family, a non-scalar commutant alone does not establish a competing feasible state; the fiber must intersect that family.

## Explicit two-qubit obstruction

Take d=(1,2,3,4)^T, D=diag(1,4,9,16), z=30. Let

sigma=D/30, rho=(D+dd^T)/60.

Both states have trace 1, both are strictly positive, and they differ: e.g. rho_12=1/30 while sigma_12=0. Set u=(1,1,1,1)^T, v=(1,-1,0,0)^T, w=(0,0,1,-1)^T and

K1=uu^T, K2=vv^T+ww^T,

A_l,ij=(d_i^2+d_j^2)/(2 d_i d_j) K_l,ij.

Explicitly,

A1 = [[1,5/4,5/3,17/8], [5/4,1,13/12,5/4], [5/3,13/12,1,25/24], [17/8,5/4,25/24,1]],

A2 = [[1,-5/4,0,0], [-5/4,1,0,0], [0,0,1,-25/24], [0,0,-25/24,1]].

**Ordinary frame is irreducible.** A2 has four distinct eigenvalues -1/4,9/4,-1/24,49/24, with the symmetric and antisymmetric vectors in each coordinate pair as eigenvectors. Any matrix commuting with A2 is diagonal in this basis. In that basis A1 couples each vector in the first pair to each vector in the second pair: the four entries are 49/16,-5/16,35/48,-7/48. All are nonzero. Commuting with A1 forces all four diagonal entries equal. Thus {A1,A2}'=C I. Multiplying either frame member by a nonzero scalar preserves this, so norm-bounded generators are available.

**State-filtered frame is reducible.** For rho, the whitened Y=(I+uu^T)/2 commutes with K1 and K2: v and w are orthogonal to u. By the fiber theorem rho and sigma have identical scores. Their common scores have entries i(d_i^2-d_j^2)/(d_i d_j) K_l,ij. Their squared score discrepancy is exactly zero, although their states are different.

The exact rational script work/r03_quantum/check_collision.py directly checks both Lyapunov equations and computes joint-commutant nullities without numerical tolerances. Results: original commutant dimension 1, filtered dimension 6. The direct equations, analytic positivity, and analytic irreducibility proof together certify the obstruction; this is not a universal conclusion extrapolated from a finite experiment.

The construction is genuinely within a two-qubit Hilbert space. Its frame is **not** the paper's particular local X/Z frame, and its state/model regime is not the paper's uniformly small-beta bounded-degree guarantee. Every full-rank state is Gibbs for some finite Hamiltonian; that fact does not force prescribed beta, coefficient bounds, or model locality assumptions. No seed theorem has been falsified.

## Minimal dimension and quantitative failure

Dimension 2 cannot exhibit this particular gap. If the filtered Hermitian frame has non-scalar commutant, all K_a belong to span{I,B} for one non-scalar Hermitian B. The inverse SLD filter is linear and fixes I, so all A_a belong to span{I,T_sigma^(-1)(B)}. They commute and are reducible. Thus dimension 3 is minimal; the script also verifies a 3-dimensional example obtained by d=(1,2,3), K1=uu^T, K2=(1,-1,0)(1,-1,0)^T. Its commutant changes from dimension 1 to 3.

For the two-qubit example, trace distance is bounded away from zero without diagonalizing: sigma and rho have equal diagonals and rho-sigma has off-diagonal entries d_i d_j/60. Testing with the unit-norm Hermitian operator |1><2|+|2><1| gives |tr(O(rho-sigma))|=1/15, hence trace distance >=1/30. Consequently no bound of the form trace-distance <= C sqrt(J_SLD), for finite C, can hold for all ordinarily irreducible frames. This is a calibration obstruction, not merely a slow optimization example.

## Nearest prior art and novelty status

The commutant framework and sufficient implication are already in Dong–Leng Appendix A.1; the converse fiber characterization follows by reversing their rearrangement. Therefore claim no novelty for that algebra alone. The explicit minimal qutrit / two-qubit witnesses and the resulting universal-calibration counterexample were independently constructed here; a targeted primary-source search did not locate these witnesses, but absence in a short search is not evidence of priority. We have not fully searched older SLD sufficiency/statistical-equivalence literature.

Endpoint identification is already in both contemporary score papers. Merely switching to it is not a discovery: the endpoint score contains conjugation by rho or rho^-1, with matrix elements amplified by eigenvalue ratios. A fixed two-level Gibbs gap produces factors exp(beta Delta), so algebraic identifiability alone gives no temperature-uniform resource guarantee. The SLD score uses bounded tanh spectral factors but can collapse algebraic access as above. BKM linearizes the Hamiltonian score while making the quadratic metric nonlinear in the unknown target. These are three distinct costs, not interchangeable score definitions.
