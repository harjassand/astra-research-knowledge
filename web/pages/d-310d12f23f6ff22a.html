# Source and priority audit: stopped-diffusion exp(-cN) claim

**Worker:** c07_l01 (gpt-6-luna requested; observed model UNKNOWN)  
**Date:** 2026-10-07 UTC  
**Scope:** Authorized cross-phase review of `work/cycle6/c07_s02/revisions/STOPPED_DIFFUSION.txt`; primary-source priority of its claim S1. This is an addendum; `INITIAL.txt` is unchanged.

## Claim under review

`STOPPED_DIFFUSION.txt` claims that for fixed finite `M,B`, all large `N`, and known `A,b` with `||A||<=M`, `|b|<=B`, the whole N-qubit Gibbs density

`rho_N(A,b) ∝ exp(2 J^2/N + Q_A(J)/N^(3/2) + b·J/N^(3/4))`

is within trace distance `C exp(-cN)` of a positive mixture of identical physical one-qubit product states. The proposed mixture is generated by a stopped real-Bloch-ball diffusion. The source itself explicitly says this is not an executed sampler or a finite-bit preparation theorem; the worst-case path-weight accounting is exponential in `sqrt(N)`. This audit addresses prior art and scope, not the full independent proof verification of S1.

## Exact 1991 FKV scope that the primary record supports

The primary publisher page for Fannes, Kossakowski, and Verbeure, *Critical fluctuations for quantum mean-field models*, J. Stat. Phys. 65, 801–811 (1991), DOI 10.1007/BF01053756, gives this abstract-level scope: a Ginzburg–Landau-type approximation for **local Gibbs states** that yields exact thermodynamics; spin fluctuations for some spin-1/2 models; and an explicitly calculated critical-temperature distribution with abnormal fluctuations. The page identifies the item as subscription content and the PDF route redirects to the abstract page. Its visible bibliography includes Størmer's quantum de Finetti theorem, Fannes–Spohn–Verbeure's mean-field equilibrium-state paper, and the Petz–Raggio–Verbeure Gibbs variational paper. Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01053756

This primary evidence establishes substantial overlap in critical quantum mean-field scaling and fluctuation laws. It does **not** establish that FKV prove a finite-N, whole-density trace-norm approximation by a positive product mixture, an `exp(-cN)` rate, exact finite-N separability, or a preparation/compiler bound. Because the complete article was not available in the inspected source, that is an unresolved access gap, not evidence of omission. No author request, institutional-login attempt, or purchase was made.

## Later primary work narrows what can be inferred from fluctuation or local-state results

Verbeure–Zagrebnov's 1994 primary article, *Gaussian, non-Gaussian critical fluctuations in the Curie-Weiss model*, J. Stat. Phys. 75, 1137–1152, is available as author-uploaded full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225435472_Gaussian_non-Gaussian_critical_fluctuations_in_the_Curie-Weiss_model . It explicitly says that the calculations in this article are worked out for the **classical** Curie–Weiss model, with spins as random variables and a scalar q-mode magnetization fluctuation. The introduction cites FKV 1991 for the quantum mean-field statement that critical fluctuations are likewise abnormal. The conclusion cautions that models may have different rate functions and fluctuation distributions even when the Ginzburg–Landau criticality agrees. It therefore corroborates the old fluctuation lineage but cannot establish the current quantum whole-state approximation or identify its `p_(A,b)` law with FKV's result. Its fading-field parameterization is not equated here to the vector coefficient `b·J/N^(3/4)`; that conversion should be checked from the source's exact field normalization before asserting an exponent match.

A particularly useful later finite-size boundary is Fannes–Vandenplas, *Finite size mean-field models*, arXiv:quant-ph/0605216v1 (2006): https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0605216 . Their Theorem 1 says that **infinitely exchangeable** states are mixtures of identical product states. Their Theorem 4 gives, for a finite permutation-invariant N-spin state, trace-norm distance at most `d(d+1)/N` between its **two-site marginal** and an exchangeable two-site marginal. The theorem is specifically about the two-site reduced state. They also emphasize that finite symmetric states need not be exchangeable. These results do not cover the whole N-body density and give no critical Gibbs-specific full-state rate. For qubits their bound is `6/N` in the paper's unhalved trace norm. This is an important reason not to promote the FKV local/thermodynamic statement or ordinary finite de Finetti into S1.

An exact witness already recorded in INITIAL makes the gap concrete: GHZ+ and its fully separable incoherent mixture have the same every-proper-subsystem reductions and the same full `J_z` law, but trace distance `1/2`. This rules out deriving whole-state closeness solely from those local data and one fluctuation observable; it does not refute a proof using the full matrix semigroup and all components as S1 attempts.

## Stochastic spin phase-space is antecedent, but its positive law is not a positive product-state decomposition

Barry–Drummond, *Qubit phase-space: SU(n) coherent state P-representations*, arXiv:0808.2226 (2008), https://arxiv.org/html/0808.2226 , is a close method antecedent. It gives differential identities for spin coherent-state kernels and stochastic Fokker–Planck/SDE methods. The paper explicitly distinguishes diagonal coherent-state projectors from the off-diagonal kernels needed for positive diffusion: diagonal product-projector representations are tied to separability, while the off-diagonal positive-P representation has doubled complex variables and can represent entangled Bell states. In particular, a positive stochastic distribution in that enlarged representation is **not** a convex mixture of physical product density matrices. The paper's Ising thermal example is a separate commuting model, not the critical isotropic/anisotropic all-to-all Gibbs family here.

Ng–Sorensen, *Exact Real Time Dynamics of Quantum Spin Systems Using the Positive-P Representation*, arXiv:1307.6267 (2013), https://arxiv.org/html/1307.6267 , similarly maps Schwinger-boson spin dynamics to complex Ito variables with off-diagonal coherent projectors and calculates observables under real-time quenches. It demonstrates that stochastic spin phase-space dynamics and operator-to-differential correspondences are not new in themselves; it gives no positive physical-product mixture or trace-distance theorem for the target Gibbs states. These are close methodological precedents, not theorem-level anticipation of S1.

## Modern broad separability bounds do not subsume the target

Putterman–Zlokapa–Cotler, *When quantum thermal states look classical*, arXiv:2607.28536v1 (30 July 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28536v1 , proves that every `(s,k)` long-range Pauli Gibbs state is exactly separable for `beta <= 1/(72 s k)` and gives polynomial classical preparation within a constant-factor subwindow. The target's isotropic term expands as a constant plus pair-Pauli terms of coefficient `1/N`, so its baseline local absolute strength is `s=3(N-1)/N` and `k=2`; the anisotropic `N^(-3/2)` pair coefficients and `N^(-3/4)` one-body field add only `o(1)` to this fixed-`M,B` local-strength estimate. At `beta=1`, this generic sufficient condition (`about 1/432`) does not apply. The theorem's converse exhibits other Hamiltonians that are entangled beyond a scale `Omega(1/(sk))`; it does not imply this target is entangled there. This result is therefore a relevant broad comparator, neither an anticipation nor a contradiction. It concerns exact separability/samplability in a generic high-temperature regime; S1 is model-specific and claims exponentially small distance to separability near a critical point.

## Priority verdict

The strongest overlap securely established is old: non-Gaussian/abnormal critical fluctuation scaling for quantum mean-field spin systems and critical sensitivity to finite-size fields. The retrieved later literature also contains exact quantum de Finetti structure in the infinite-exchangeability limit, finite-size `O(1/N)` two-site marginal approximation, and stochastic coherent-state phase-space SDE methods.

No inspected primary source states the combined S1 result: uniform fixed-`M,B` exponential whole-N trace-distance approximation by a positive mixture of identical **physical** qubit density matrices for this perturbed critical all-to-all Gibbs family. The inaccessible full FKV article is the priority-critical gap. Accordingly the correct status is **candidate extension; historical priority UNKNOWN**, not “new beyond FKV” and not “already known in 1991.” A full-text audit should compare FKV's exact Hamiltonian, local-state topology/norm, finite-volume limit, fluctuation test functions, field scaling, and any positive coherent-state representation against S1 line by line.
