# Guarded mode-specific certificates for finite-volume reaction safety

**Status.** Internally derived conditional theorem plus an exact rational example. This is an extension of the source-reconstructed finite-volume polytope argument, not an externally reviewed or priority-checked result. The proof is a stopped-Dynkin restart argument; existing switched-system/multiple-Lyapunov work may already contain a close analogue.

## Contract

Let the concentration process be a finite-volume molecular jump process on a supplied stoichiometric class. In mode `j`, reaction `r` has concentration jump `nu_r/V` and predictable intensity

\[
  V k_{jr}(t,\mathcal F_{t-})\,\phi^V_{y_r}(X_{t-}),\qquad
  \phi^V_y(x)=V^{-|y|}\prod_s(Vx_s)_{y_s},
\]

with `0 <= k_{jr} <= K_j`. Modes may disable reactions. For each mode supply a compact rational polytope `Q_j` in the same stoichiometric class, contained in a prescribed safe set `S`, and facet functions `g_{ji}(x)=a_{ji}.x+b_{ji}`. Supply coordinate upper bounds `U_{js}` on `Q_j` and, for every facet, a layer width `ell_{ji}>0` and a strict mode-uniform deterministic margin

\[
 a_{ji}\cdot\sum_r k_{jr}x^{y_r}\nu_r\ge\gamma_{ji}>0
 \quad\text{for }x\in Q_j,\quad 0\le g_{ji}(x)\le\ell_{ji}.
\]

The network mode is piecewise constant and predictable. Every time the controller enters a mode `j`, its state must lie in the supplied guard core

\[
  Q_j^{d_j}=\{x\in Q_j:g_{ji}(x)\ge d_{ji}>0\text{ for every facet }i\}.
\]

The initial state obeys the same condition for the initial mode. A switch that fails the target-mode guard is disallowed. Let `N_T` be the number of mode segments the controller can start by horizon `T`; it may be path-dependent, but has finite expectation. This is a zero-reset switch: switching changes reaction rates but not the molecular state. A post-jump state outside the current `Q_j` is counted as a safety failure before any mode update.

## Conditional theorem

For each mode/facet define

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_y(U_j)&=\sum_{s:y_s\ge2}{y_s\choose2}U_{js}^{y_s-1}\prod_{q\ne s}U_{jq}^{y_q},\\
D_{ji}&=K_j\sum_r|a_{ji}\cdot\nu_r|E_{y_r}(U_j),\\
J_{ji}&=\max_r|a_{ji}\cdot\nu_r|,\qquad
B_{ji}=K_j\sum_r(a_{ji}\cdot\nu_r)^2U_j^{y_r},\\
\Lambda_j&=K_j\sum_rU_j^{y_r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Assume `J_{ji}, B_{ji}>0` on essential facets, and let

\[
V_0=\max_{j,i}\left\lceil\frac{2D_{ji}}{\gamma_{ji}}\right\rceil,
\quad \mu_{ji}=\gamma_{ji}/2,
\quad \theta_{ji}=\min\{J_{ji}^{-1},\mu_{ji}/(3B_{ji})\},
\quad c=\min_{j,i}\theta_{ji}\ell_{ji},
\quad A_V=\max_j\sum_i e^{-\theta_{ji}Vd_{ji}},
\quad C=\max_j2m_j\Lambda_j.
\]

For every lattice-compatible `V >= V_0`, the probability that the process exits the currently active certificate before `T` is bounded by

\[
\boxed{\Pr(\text{some active }Q_j\text{ is exited by }T)
 \le A_V\,\mathbb E[N_T]+C VT e^{-cV}.}
\]

In particular, if each facet has `d_{ji} >= d>0`, `m_j<=m`, `theta_{ji}>=theta`, and `Lambda_j<=Lambda`, this becomes

\[
  \Pr(\text{exit by }T)\le m\,\mathbb E[N_T]e^{-\theta dV}
                      +2m\Lambda VT e^{-cV}.
\]

Thus a deterministic segment cap `N` gives the same inequality with `E[N_T]` replaced by `N`. For `T=e^{\alpha V}` and `E[N_T]<=e^{\beta V}`, the certified risk tends to zero whenever `alpha<c` and `beta<theta d` (up to fixed prefactors).

### Proof

The factorial correction is uniform, including insufficient-reactant lattice points:

\[
0\le x^y-\phi_y^V(x)\le E_y(U_j)/V.
\]

Hence for `V>=V_0` each finite-volume generator has inward drift at least `mu_{ji}` in the corresponding facet collar. For a fixed mode `j` define

\[
\Phi_j(x)=\sum_i e^{-\theta_{ji}Vg_{ji}(x)}.
\]

The generator calculation from the single-certificate argument applies mode by mode. In a facet collar, use `e^z-1 <= z+z^2 e^{|z|}/2`, `theta_{ji}J_{ji}<=1`, and `theta_{ji}B_{ji}<=mu_{ji}/3` to get nonpositive generator for that facet term. Outside its collar, its positive contribution is bounded by `2V Lambda_j exp(-theta_{ji}V ell_{ji})`. Therefore `L_t^j Phi_j <= 2m_j V Lambda_j exp(-c_j V) <= C V exp(-cV)` throughout `Q_j`.

At exit from `Q_j`, at least one facet gap is negative, so `Phi_j>=1`. At the beginning of each segment in mode `j`, the guard gives `Phi_j<=sum_i exp(-theta_{ji}Vd_{ji})<=A_V`. Stop that segment at its first exit, its next switch, or `T`. Conditional Dynkin's formula for the bounded stopped jump process gives

\[
\Pr(\text{exit in this segment}\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma})
 \le \Phi_j(X_\sigma)+CV e^{-cV}\,\mathbb E[\text{segment duration}\mid\mathcal F_\sigma].
\]

Sum only over segments whose starts occur before any earlier certificate exit. The sum of their start indicators is at most `N_T`, and their stopped durations add to at most `T`. Taking expectations yields the boxed inequality. The argument uses predictable feedback only through the conditional intensity; it does not require a Markov controller or a fixed switching schedule.

The bound is conservative and pays one initial-buffer term per mode segment. It gives neither infinite-horizon survival nor a stochastic-permanence conclusion. It also assumes the state is observed well enough to enforce the guard; measurement error must be absorbed into a smaller certified core.

## Exact two-mode mass-action example

Take a closed two-species class `A+B=V`, `x=A/V`, and prescribed safe interval `S=[1/10,9/10]`. Use the four integer-complex reactions

\[
3B\to A+2B,\quad A+2B\to3B,\quad2A+B\to3A,\quad3A\to2A+B.
\]

Their concentration jumps in `x` are `+1,-1,+1,-1`, respectively. Define two rate vectors, in that order,

\[
 k_1=(741/4000,\;2167/4000,\;1043/4000,\;49/4000),
\quad
 k_2=(7/500,\;591/2000,\;567/1000,\;247/2000).
\]

All rates are positive and below `K=3/5`. The deterministic mode drifts are exactly

\[
 f_1(x)=-(x-3/10)(x-13/20)(x-19/20),\\
 f_2(x)=-(x-1/20)(x-7/20)(x-4/5).
\]

Choose

\[
Q_1=[1/5,51/100],\qquad Q_2=[49/100,9/10],\qquad G=[499/1000,501/1000].
\]

Each set is a strict inward certificate for its own mode with `ell=1/100` and `gamma=1/100`. Exact lower bounds on the four collar drift magnitudes are, respectively,

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&\text{left collar}&\text{right collar}\\
Q_1& (9/100)(44/100)(74/100)=0.029304 & (1/5)(7/50)(11/25)=0.01232\\
Q_2& (11/25)(7/50)(3/10)=0.01848 & (21/25)(27/50)(9/100)=0.040824.
\end{array}
\]

The guard `G` lies at least `9/1000` inside both certificates. The initial count may be `A=B=V/2` for even `V`. In fact, no common interval contained in `S` can even contain the guard `G`: its right endpoint `b` would satisfy `b>=501/1000`, while the signs are `f_1<=0,f_2>0` on `[501/1000,13/20]`, `f_1>=0,f_2>0` on `[13/20,4/5]`, and `f_1>0,f_2<=0` on `[4/5,9/10]`. At each shared root a strict inward margin fails as well. So the common-polytope premise cannot certify even the switching region, while each local certificate can.

This example is a model witness for the geometric gap only. The generic probability bound has very conservative constants: `D=24/5`, `B=12/5`, `Lambda=12/5`, `V_0=960`, `theta=1/1440`, and `c=1/144000`. With guard gap `d=9/1000`, its specialization is

\[
  \Pr(\text{exit by }T)\le 2N e^{-V/160000}+(48/5)VT e^{-V/144000}
\]

for at most `N` mode segments. The theorem therefore proves a vanishing risk tradeoff asymptotically, not a useful small-volume guarantee. A sharper example-specific generator ratio is a separate gate.

## Relation to source theorem, costs, and falsifiers

The source-reconstructed one-polytope theorem uses the same falling-factorial propensities and stopped exponential facet potential. Its exact formula is `sum_i exp(-theta_i V g_i(X_0)) + 2m V Lambda T exp(-cV)` and it allows arbitrary predictable time/history-dependent rates when every mode has one common strict inward collar. The extension above restarts the potential at each guarded mode change. It changes the certificate geometry, not the molecular interface.

The acquired input is the complete mode list, rational reaction complexes/rates, safe set, every `Q_j`, collars, margins, guard, lattice-compatible initial counts, and switch budget. Certificate acquisition and proof search are not free. Once acquired, constant calculation costs `O(sum_j m_j |R_j| d)` arithmetic operations plus rational monomial evaluation; actual control evaluation, molecular turnover, sensing/actuation energy, and simulation of the horizon `T` are separately charged. If `T` is exponential in `V`, direct trajectory simulation can remain exponentially expensive despite the probability certificate.

The theorem fails if a mode lacks any facet margin, the controller can switch outside its new guard core, jumps alter the supposedly conserved class, a controller rate exceeds its charged envelope, or the finite-volume propensity is silently replaced by a deterministic power law. The need for a positive target guard is witnessed by this same example: start mode 2 exactly at its lower certificate boundary `x=49/100` (with `V` a multiple of 100) and allow the switch with no positive margin. The first death jump exits `Q_2`. Its probability as `V→∞` is exactly

\[
 \frac{d_2(49/100)}{b_2(49/100)+d_2(49/100)}
 =\frac{677803}{1603606}\approx0.422674,
\]

and the first event occurs by any fixed `T>0` with probability tending to one because total intensity is `Theta(V)`. Thus a zero-margin switch has order-one exit risk; the initial-buffer term cannot be dropped.

## Prior-art boundary

- Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann, [Large deviations theory for Markov jump models of chemical reaction networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126), establish sample-path LDP and Wentzell–Freidlin transition asymptotics under their stated hypotheses. This is the strongest source comparator for long-time chemistry but is not the same finite, controller-uniform certificate interface.
- Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann, [On the geometry of chemical reaction networks: Lyapunov function and large deviations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240), provide geometric network/Lyapunov antecedents.
- The source repository's N35 result records the single-common-polytope stochastic theorem, its proof, examples, and known limits. Its own status is source-derived/internal and externally unreviewed.
- No full search of switched jump-process, multiple stochastic Lyapunov, or safe hybrid-control literature has yet established novelty. Treat the extension as an internally reconstructed conditional candidate only.
