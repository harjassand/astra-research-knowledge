# Generic immersion and compact Lipschitz recovery from analytic measurements

Independent reconstruction, 2026-10-06. All results below are our deductions unless explicitly identified as source claims.

## Exact interface and theorem

Let X be a second-countable real-analytic d-dimensional manifold, d≥1. Let A be a connected second-countable real-analytic m-dimensional manifold, m≥1, with smooth positive volume. Let F:X×A→R be jointly real analytic. For a=(a₁,…,a_q), define E_a(x)=(F(x,a₁),…,F(x,a_q)). Assume:

(P) For every x≠y, F(x,·)−F(y,·) is not identically zero.

(T) For every x and nonzero v∈T_xX, a↦D_xF(x,a)[v] is not identically zero.

Then, outside a volume-null subset of A^q, E_a is an immersion if q≥2d. Outside a volume-null subset E_a is injective if q≥2d+1 and (P) holds. Under both assumptions q≥2d+1 gives an injective immersion simultaneously. Any joint sampling law absolutely continuous relative to volume on A^q gives probability one; independence is sufficient but unnecessary. This is a uniform statement for every x and tangent, not a separate probability-one statement for each fixed x.

If K⊂X is compact and X is embedded C¹ in a Banach space B, then such E_a restricted to K is bi-Lipschitz in the ambient B distance. A connected compact smooth manifold also admits the conclusion in any fixed smooth Riemannian distance. No deterministic lower constant uniform over measurement tuples is claimed.

Complex scalar measurements obey the same conservative counts by selecting at each zero a real or imaginary component with nonzero analytic germ.

## Complete elementary incidence proof

We first establish a lemma. Let B be a second-countable smooth b-dimensional manifold and h:B×A→R smooth jointly, analytic in A, with h(p,·) nonzero for every p. Assume all sampling-coordinate derivatives of h are jointly C¹ (joint analyticity is more than sufficient). For integer q form
I={ (p,a₁,…,a_q): h(p,a_i)=0 for all i }.
If q>b, projection of I into A^q lies in a volume-null Borel set.

Choose countable coordinate atlases of B and A. For a point (p,a) with h(p,a)=0, analytic connectedness implies h(p,·) cannot have the zero germ at a: a zero germ propagates across connected A by the real-analytic identity theorem. Thus in sampling coordinates some finite derivative is nonzero. Choose minimum total degree k≥1 of a nonzero derivative, α with |α|=k, and j with α_j>0. Write β=α−e_j. All derivatives of degree less than k vanish at this point, so g(p,a)=∂_a^βh(p,a)=0, while ∂_{a_j}g(p,a)≠0.

At any point of I make these choices separately in each sampling block. In the corresponding product coordinate domain define g_i(p,a_i)=∂_{a_i}^{β_i}h(p,a_i), and restrict to the open set where ∂_{(a_i)_{j_i}}g_i≠0 for every i. The equations g_i=0 have rank q: their Jacobian restricted to the selected q sampling coordinates is diagonal with nonzero diagonal. The implicit-function theorem says their common zero set S is a C¹ submanifold of dimension b+qm−q. The original incidence point lies in S. It is not necessary that a whole neighborhood of I lies in this particular S.

There are countably many choices of charts, multiindices and coordinate j_i. Consequently I is contained in a countable union of such S. Each S has a countable C¹ atlas; subdivide each chart into countably many compact cubes lying inside its domain. On each cube the sampling projection composed with its chart is Lipschitz, mapping a subset of R^{b+qm−q} into R^{qm}. If b+qm−q<qm this image has qm-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero: a bounded n-dimensional cube can be covered by O(ε^{-n}) cubes of side ε, whose Lipschitz images have diameter O(ε), thus total N-dimensional covering volume O(ε^{N−n})→0 for N>n. These compact images are Borel, and their countable union is a Borel null superset of the projected incidence. Smooth chart volumes are locally equivalent to Lebesgue volume. This proves the lemma, including measurability sufficient for probability statements.

For pair separation use B=(X×X)\Δ, b=2d, h((x,y),a)=F(x,a)−F(y,a). The bad tuples are exactly the projection of I. Assumption (P) applies and q>2d proves injectivity.

For immersion use B=P(TX), the real projective tangent bundle, dimension d+(d−1)=2d−1. On each countable local projective chart choose an analytic nonzero representative v(x,ℓ); define h((x,ℓ),a)=D_xF(x,a)[v(x,ℓ)]. Different representatives multiply h by a nowhere-zero scalar, so the zero incidence is well-defined. Apply the lemma on each base chart using (T). A failure of immersion means a nonzero v lies in the kernel of every component derivative, and hence is exactly the projection of this incidence. q>2d−1, that is q≥2d, excludes it almost surely. Intersect the full-measure sets for pairs and tangents to finish.

## Complete compact bi-Lipschitz argument in ambient parameter norm

Fix a good tuple and write E=E_a. Suppose K⊂X compact, with embedded C¹ charts φ:U⊂R^d→B, and E a C¹ injective immersion on a neighborhood of K.

At z₀ in a chart, J=D(E∘φ)(z₀) is injective, so s=min_{|w|=1}|Jw|>0. Restrict to a convex ball around z₀ where ||D(E∘φ)(z)−J||≤s/2 and ||Dφ(z)||≤L. Integrating along a segment gives
|E(φ(z))−E(φ(w))|≥s|z−w|−(s/2)|z−w|≥(s/2)|z−w|,
||φ(z)−φ(w)||_B≤L|z−w|.
Thus a local lower ratio s/(2L)>0 holds.

For the upper ratio, embeddedness and injectivity of Dφ(z₀) provide a bounded linear left inverse R:B→R^d of Dφ(z₀): coordinate functionals on its finite-dimensional range extend by Hahn–Banach. After reducing the ball, ||RDφ(z)−I||≤1/2, so |z−w|≤2||R||·||φ(z)−φ(w)||_B. Bounded D(E∘φ) then supplies a local upper ratio.

Finitely many smaller chart neighborhoods cover K. Their pairwise same-neighborhood union is an open neighborhood of the diagonal in K×K. The complementary pair set C is compact and misses the diagonal. On C, injectivity makes |E(x)−E(y)| positive and ambient distinctness makes ||x−y|| positive. Their continuous ratio therefore has a positive minimum and a finite maximum. Combining these with the finite local constants gives 0<c≤C<∞ with
c||x−y||_B≤|E(x)−E(y)|≤C||x−y||_B for all x,y∈K.
The same local argument with coordinate comparability of a smooth Riemannian metric, and compactness away from the diagonal, proves the connected compact Riemannian version.

## Adversarial checks and precise obstructions

1. Pair uniqueness does not imply tangent visibility. X=R, A=R, F(x,a)=x³ is analytic and separates every pair. Every E_a is injective, but all derivatives vanish at x=0. On K=[−1,1], |E_a(t)−E_a(0)|/|t|=√q t²→0. Thus source uniqueness alone cannot imply Lipschitz inverse.

2. Connectedness cannot be dropped without componentwise visibility. Take A a disjoint union of two open intervals; set F(x,a)=0 on one component and x on the other. This is jointly analytic, globally pair- and tangent-visible. A sampling law placing positive mass on the silent component has positive probability that all q samples fail. Repair: require every relevant h nonzero on each sampling component with positive probability.

3. Smoothness alone fails. On X=R, A=R use F(x,a)=x ψ(a), where ψ is smooth nonzero but vanishes on an open interval. Pair and tangent visibility hold globally, yet draws all in that interval have positive failure probability. Analyticity supplies the finite nonzero jet at every zero, which the proof uses essentially.

4. Absolute continuity of the joint law matters. F(x,a)=ax on R×R satisfies both assumptions but an atomic law at a=0 fails. Merely absolutely continuous marginals with arbitrary dependence do not imply the joint condition required by the proof.

5. Compactness matters for a global lower constant. F(x,a)=arctan(x) is real analytic on R², and visible in both senses. A good tuple is an injective immersion, but derivative tends to zero at infinity; no global inverse Lipschitz constant exists on R.

6. No universal noise constant follows even on fixed compact K. F(x,a)=ax, samples uniform in (0,1), K=[−1,1]. Good tuples have lower constant ||a||₂, arbitrarily near zero with positive probability. Quantitative probability bounds require information beyond generic dimension arguments.

7. A bad tuple with q=2d can remain globally noninjective. Our immersion conclusion at q=2d does not eliminate the 2d-dimensional pair-incidence projection; the proof strictly requires q>2d for separation. We do not assert sharpness for each particular analytic family.

## Downstream use and next falsifiable step

For a physical scalar sampling family already known to separate both states and tangent directions, 2d+1 generic physical measurements give compact-set qualitative Lipschitz recovery, without arbitrary observables. This is a valid extension from exact recovery, subject to the additional differential hypothesis. The decisive application bottleneck is proving tangent visibility for the chosen inverse-PDE model and fixed admissible physical measurement family. Next: independently derive the linearized PDE measurement map and test its kernel on the selected model tangent spaces. If any nonzero tangent is invisible, stop the Lipschitz claim. If all are visible, derive explicit lower-tail bounds on minimum singular values and secant separation; the present theorem gives no useful certified numerical noise tolerance by itself.

# Appendix: full sparse improvement and cross-support stability

## Source-exact sparse interface and finite sampling

Use the source's real Banach ambient X, open parameter domain U⊂X (renamed from source A to avoid confusion with sampling manifold), finite-dimensional real subspace V with basis e₁,…,e_D, and S_s=⋃_{|I|≤s}E_I where E_I=span{e_j:j∈I}. Measurement architecture is source §2.1 or §2.2 with the stated separation hypotheses. Theorem 2.5 states M≥2min{D,2s}+1; our following deduction improves this to M≥2s+1 (s≤D).

For each ordered pair of supports I,J, form B_IJ=((U∩E_I)×(U∩E_J))\Δ. This is an open subset of E_I×E_J, hence a second-countable analytic manifold of dimension b_IJ=|I|+|J|≤2s. The subtraction map h((x,y),a)=m(x,a)−m(y,a) is analytic and nonzero as a function of sample a by the source separation hypothesis, which in fact only needs to hold for sparse pairs. The elementary lemma above gives a null bad sampling set if M>b_IJ. There are finitely many I,J. Union these null sets. Every distinct pair of sparse parameters belongs to some B_IJ. Thus M≥2s+1 independent samples separate every sparse pair almost surely. This counts the true pair manifold rather than enlarging both endpoints to E_{I∪J} and then counting all pairs there (dimension up to 4s), which explains the source's sufficient count. Overlapping supports and the removed diagonal do not increase dimensions.

## Gaussian multilinear proof in full

Let Z be source's separable real Hilbert sampling space, γ Gaussian with injective covariance, ψ:Z→H bounded real linear with dense K-linear span, and analytic forward map G:U→L^r(H;K). Measurements m(x,z)=G(x)(ψz¹,…,ψz^r), z∈Z^r. Assume G injective on U∩S_s. Then for every sparse pair p=(x,y), the continuous real multilinear form T_p(z)=m(x,z)−m(y,z) is nonzero: if it vanished on ψ(Z)^r, K-multilinearity and the dense span would make G(x)−G(y)=0.

Fix I,J and exhaust B_IJ by countably many compact sets K_n contained in its coordinate domain. This requires no semianalytic property: finite-dimensional open sets have a compact exhaustion. Let f₁,f₂,… be a covariance eigenbasis of Z, all eigenvalues positive, and Z_N=span(f₁,…,f_N). For each p∈K_n, there is some N_p and some tuple z_p∈Z_{N_p}^r with T_p(z_p)≠0: truncate any tuple with nonzero value and use continuity. By continuity in p the same tuple stays nonzero on a neighborhood of p. A finite subcover of K_n gives a common N such that T_p|_{Z_N^r} is nonzero for every p∈K_n. Increasing N preserves this property. Furthermore the union of these neighborhoods is open in B_IJ and contains K_n; call it O. All p∈O have the nonzero finite restriction, which is the open-base condition needed in the incidence lemma.

For each sample i split its r coordinates as z_i=u_i+w_i, u_i∈Z_N^r, w_i∈(Z_N^⊥)^r. Covariance eigenbasis splitting gives independent finite and tail components, even for a nonzero Gaussian mean. Finite components have a smooth positive Lebesgue density on R^{Nr}. Fix arbitrary tails w₁,…,w_M. Set h_i(p,u_i)=T_p(u_i+w_i). This is analytic in the finite variables and p. Multilinearity shows its top total degree-r homogeneous part in u_i is exactly T_p(u_i), nonzero for every p∈O. Thus h_i(p,·) is nonzero for each p, irrespective of tails.

The jet-cover lemma applies equally to different h_i in different sample blocks: the diagonal rank argument uses only their individual nontriviality. Base dimension remains b_IJ≤2s and sample dimension Nr; for M>b_IJ it makes the projection of the simultaneous zero incidence over O Lebesgue-null. In particular, conditional on any tails, the probability of a pair collision in K_n is zero.

For Fubini there is no hidden measurability problem. The event C_n={ (z₁,…,z_M): ∃p∈K_n, T_p(z_i)=0 for all i } is closed in (Z^r)^M: for a convergent sample sequence with witnesses p_k∈K_n choose a convergent witness subsequence and use continuity. Thus C_n is Borel. Integrating its zero conditional probability over the independent tails gives γ^{rM}(C_n)=0. Countable union over n and finite union over I,J prove uniform sparse injectivity almost surely for M≥2s+1. This is a proof under the exact Gaussian architecture, not a reduction to arbitrary finite-dimensional Gaussian measurements.

## Why ordinary supportwise immersion is inadequate

Take V=R², s=1, and vector observable E(x,y)=(x+y,y²). On each axis E is injective and immersive: derivatives at the origin are respectively (1,0) and (1,0). E is also injective on the entire union of the two axes: an x-axis image has second coordinate zero, while a nonzero y-axis image has positive second coordinate, and the first coordinate distinguishes points within either axis. Nevertheless compare (t,0) and (0,t): their parameter distance is √2|t| and output distance is t². The ratio tends to zero. DE(0)(1,−1)=0. Thus even global sparse injectivity plus supportwise immersion does not give compact sparse inverse Lipschitz stability.

This example can be encoded into a connected analytic scalar family m((x,y),a)=(x+y)+a y², a∈R. Every finite tuple separates the sparse union if at least two sampled a-values differ; it immerses each support for every tuple. Yet the ratio for the same cross-support pairs is |t|·||(a₁,…,a_M)||/√2→0, so almost-sure sparse injectivity and supportwise immersion still do not imply stability.

Define (CT): for all support pairs I,J, all x∈U∩E_I∩E_J and all nonzero v∈E_I+E_J, a↦D_xm(x,a)[v] is nonzero. For Gaussian architecture replace this by DG(x)[v]≠0; dense ψ span then gives measurement tangent visibility. CT is a sufficient condition, and for full neighborhoods within the sparse model it is necessary for any differentiable measurement map to have an inverse Lipschitz constant there: if DE(x)v=0 write v=u−w with u∈E_I,w∈E_J; compare x+tu and x+tw, whose separation is |t|·||v|| but output difference is o(|t|).

For each I,J use the analytic base
C_IJ=(U∩E_I∩E_J)×P(E_I+E_J).
Its dimension is |I∩J|+|I∪J|−1=|I|+|J|−1≤2s−1. The derivative incidence proof above therefore shows that M≥2s generic finite analytic samples have no invisible support-sum tangent at any support intersection. The same Gaussian proof applies with T_p(z)=DG(x)[v](ψz¹,…,ψz^r) on local projective charts and compact exhaustions; it remains analytic in p, continuous multilinear in z, and nonzero by CT. Thus M≥2s+1 gives both sparse pair separation and all cross-support differential conditions almost surely.

## Compact sparse bi-Lipschitz proof

Fix a good tuple E and compact K⊂U∩S_s. All norms on V are equivalent, so choose a Euclidean norm. The upper Lipschitz ratio follows locally from bounded DE on neighborhoods of compact K; pairs outside these neighborhoods have finite upper ratio by compactness.

For the lower ratio suppose contrary that there exist distinct x_n,y_n∈K with ||E(x_n)−E(y_n)||/||x_n−y_n||→0. Take convergent subsequences x_n→x, y_n→y, and fixed supports x_n∈E_I,y_n∈E_J (finite possibilities). If x≠y, continuity and injectivity make the ratio tend to a strictly positive number, a contradiction. Thus x=y belongs to E_I∩E_J. After another subsequence v_n=(x_n−y_n)/||x_n−y_n||→v∈E_I+E_J with ||v||=1. For large n the segment connecting x_n,y_n lies in a small convex ball inside U. The fundamental theorem of calculus and continuity of DE yield
(E(x_n)−E(y_n))/||x_n−y_n||=DE(x)v_n+o(1)→DE(x)v.
The assumed ratio tends to zero, so DE(x)v=0, contradicting the cross-support condition. Therefore a positive lower constant exists. Norm equivalence gives the ambient Banach distance version. This proof handles singular support intersections directly and does not falsely treat S_s as a smooth s-dimensional manifold.
