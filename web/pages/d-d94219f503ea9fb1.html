## Strongest claim

For the already admitted rational easy-plane family

\[
H=\sum_{uv}\bigl[\alpha_{uv}(X_uX_v+Y_uY_v)+\gamma_{uv}Z_uZ_v\bigr]
 +\sum_v(b_vX_v+c_vZ_v),\qquad
\alpha_{uv}\ge |\gamma_{uv}|,\quad b_v\ge0,
\]
on any loopless graph, the local homogeneous counting gates can be replaced by rational **quadratic Taylor gates** while retaining the exact homogeneous log-concave coefficient reduction. A palindromic product of these gates gives a one-step log-generator error of order \((\beta D/m)^3\), and total error \(O((\beta D)^3/m^2)\), rather than the previous \(O((\beta D)^2/m)\). Thus the number of local gate layers needed for trace-relative error \(\varepsilon\) falls from \(O(\tau^2/\varepsilon)\) to \(O(\tau+\tau^{3/2}/\sqrt\varepsilon)\), where \(\tau=\beta D\) and \(D=2C\) is the rational norm bound. This preserves the existing conditional FPRAS consequences and their numerical-temperature dependence; it does not expand the XXZ coupling cone or implement the imported FPRAS.

The result is a gate-decomposition improvement, not a new proof of the Chen--Liu counting theorem, a practical counting program, a quantum Gibbs-state preparation circuit, or a priority claim. Generic second-order symmetric splitting is established background; the candidate increment is the exact rational, local log-concavity verification needed to make that order useful inside this homogeneous counter.

## Exact Hamiltonian shift and local gates

Write \(r_v=b_v+|c_v|\),
\[
A_{uv}=3\alpha I+\alpha(XX+YY)+\gamma ZZ,\qquad
A_v=rI+bX+cZ,
\]
and \(K=\sum_iA_i=H+CI\), where
\[
C=3\sum_{uv}\alpha_{uv}+\sum_v(b_v+|c_v|),\qquad
D:=2C,\qquad \sum_i\|A_i\|\le D.
\]
The edge eigenvalues are \(3\alpha+\gamma\) (twice), \(5\alpha-\gamma\), and \(\alpha-\gamma\); the field eigenvalues are \(r\pm\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\). The stated cone makes all \(A_i\) positive semidefinite, and their computational-basis matrices are entrywise nonnegative.

For rational \(t\ge0\), use
\[
G_i(t)=I+tA_i+\frac{t^2}{2}A_i^2.
\]
These matrices are positive definite because each eigenvalue is \(1+t\lambda+t^2\lambda^2/2>0\) for \(\lambda\ge0\). Their entries are nonnegative rationals.

For an edge put \(\chi=3\alpha\). In basis \(00,01,10,11\), \(G_e(t)\) has six-vertex form with weights
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=1+t(\chi+\gamma)+\tfrac{t^2}{2}(\chi+\gamma)^2,\\
b&=1+t(\chi-\gamma)+\tfrac{t^2}{2}\bigl((\chi-\gamma)^2+4\alpha^2\bigr),\\
c'&=2t\alpha+2t^2\alpha(\chi-\gamma),
\end{aligned}
\]
so its table is \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0&0&0\\0&b&c'&0\\0&c'&b&0\\0&0&0&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\). After keeping row bits and complementing column bits, its homogeneous quadratic has Hessian eigenvalues \(a+b+c',a-b-c',-a+b-c',-a-b+c'\). The three potentially positive eigenvalues are nonpositive because
\[
\begin{aligned}
c'-(a-b)&=2t(\alpha-\gamma)(1+t(\chi+\alpha)),\\
c'+(a-b)&=2t(\alpha+\gamma)(1+t(\chi-\alpha)),\\
a+b-c'&=2+2t(\chi-\alpha)+t^2\bigl((\chi-\alpha)^2+(\alpha+\gamma)^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
All right-hand sides are nonnegative when \(|\gamma|\le\alpha\), so the required triangle inequalities hold, including their boundary cases. A homogeneous nonnegative quadratic with at most one positive Hessian eigenvalue is log-concave.

For a field put \(q=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\le r\),
\[
U=1+tr+\tfrac{t^2}{2}(r^2+q^2),\qquad w=t+t^2r.
\]
Then \(G_v(t)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}U+wc&wb\\wb&U-wc\end{smallmatrix}\right]\). Its row/column-complement polynomial is \(d(1+xy)+u x+v y\), with \(d=wb\), \(u=U-wc\), and \(v=U+wc\). We need \(d^2\le uv\). Let \(T=tr\) and \(X=tq\), so \(0\le X\le T\). The function
\[
U-wq=1+T+\tfrac12(T^2+X^2)-X(1+T)
\]
is decreasing in \(X\in[0,T]\) and equals \(1\) at \(X=T\). Hence \(U-wq\ge1\), so \(uv-d^2=U^2-w^2q^2\ge0\). The existing rational two-dummy lift therefore applies unchanged:
\[
\widetilde q_v=d z_1z_2+\tfrac{u}{2}x(z_1+z_2)+\tfrac{v}{2}y(z_1+z_2)+dxy.
\]
It is homogeneous degree two, has rational nonnegative coefficients, specializes to the original local polynomial at \(z_1=z_2=1\), and is log-concave. For \(d>0\), its Hessian has an \((x,y)\) block with inertia \((1,1)\); the Schur complement is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-uv/(2d)&d-uv/(2d)\\d-uv/(2d)&-uv/(2d)\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose eigenvalues are \(-d\) and \((d^2-uv)/d\), both nonpositive. For \(d=0\), the lift factors as \((z_1+z_2)(ux+vy)/2\).

This Taylor gate does **not** widen the local coupling cone: the first-order term in \(t\) still requires \(|\gamma|\le\alpha\) in this six-vertex orientation. The formulas above prove preservation of, not extension beyond, that interface.

## Homogeneous coefficient reduction

Take any ordered list of \(p\) local edge/field terms and set \(s=\beta/m\). Form
\[
V=G_1(s/2)G_2(s/2)\cdots G_p(s/2),\qquad B=VV^*.
\]
The second half of each step is the reverse order. The exact coefficient reduction from the existing XXZ audit applies gate by gate. Each edge contributes four row/column half-edges; each field contributes two physical half-edges and the two dummy variables above. If there are e edge-gate occurrences and f field-gate occurrences in the circuit, there are 2e+f physical wires. Let P be the product of local homogeneous signatures, and Q the product of one (x_i+x_j) per physical wire and the uniform-matroid polynomial e_f on the 2f dummy variables. Every local signature is homogeneous log-concave by the calculations above, so P is also log-concave; Q is homogeneous log-concave by the partition- and uniform-matroid constructions. Both have degree 2e+2f. The full squarefree coefficient [x_U](P Q) equals Tr(B^m): every physical wire is selected once, the remaining f selected dummy variables sum the two-dummy lifts back to the field gates, and idle qubits contribute a known factor of two.

Thus Theorem 5.1 of Chen--Liu applies directly, with both homogeneous weight functions explicitly evaluable as products of rational local entries. If \(p\) is the number of Hamiltonian terms, \(B^m\) has \(2mp\) local gates and the lifted ground set has \(N=8mp\) variables. A positive initial support point is obtained locally for every gate; the wire factor chooses one endpoint per wire and \(e_f\) chooses any \(f\) dummy variables. The coefficient range has \(\log R=O(mp(L+\log m))\) for input bit length \(L\); the wire/uniform factor has range one. The imported arithmetic bound is \(O(N^{10}(N+\log R)^2\eta^{-2}\log^2(2(N+\log R)/\eta))\) per estimate, with polynomial-bit implementation conditional on Chen--Liu Remark A.4. This is still a very large polynomial and is not a practical run.

## Second-order error bound with explicit constants

Let \(h=\beta/m\), \(\tau=\beta D\), and write the ordered factors of \(B\) as
\[
B=\prod_{j=1}^{2p}\left(I+h B_j+\tfrac{h^2}{2}B_j^2\right),
\]
where \((B_1,\ldots,B_{2p})=(A_1/2,\ldots,A_p/2,A_p/2,\ldots,A_1/2)\). This is a palindrome and \(\sum_jB_j=K\), \(\sum_j\|B_j\|=\sum_i\|A_i\|\le D\). The degree-zero and degree-one coefficients match \(e^{hK}\). The degree-two coefficient is
\[
\frac12\sum_jB_j^2+\sum_{j<k}B_jB_k
=\frac12K^2+\frac12\sum_{j<k}[B_j,B_k]
=\frac12K^2,
\]
because reversal sends each commutator to its negative (a middle mirrored pair has equal factors and zero commutator).

For \(x=hD\), the norm sum of all terms of degree at least three in this finite product is at most the corresponding scalar-coefficient tail of \(\prod_j(1+z h\|B_j\|+z^2h^2\|B_j\|^2/2)\) evaluated at \(z=1\). Coefficientwise, the whole product is bounded by \(e^{z h\sum_j\|B_j\|}=e^{zx}\), so its tail is at most \(e^x x^3/6\). The same Taylor-tail bound applies to \(e^{hK}\), since \(\|K\|\le D\). Therefore
\[
\|B-e^{hK}\|\le \delta,\qquad \delta:=e^x x^3/3.
\]
Choose the least integer \(m\) satisfying
\[
m\ge1,\qquad m\ge4\tau,\qquad m^2\ge4\tau^3/\varepsilon.
\]
These comparisons are exact rational comparisons after squaring; \(m=O(1+\tau+\tau^{3/2}/\sqrt\varepsilon)\). Then \(x\le1/4\), \(\delta<1/100\), and \(e^{hK}\succeq I\). Congruencing the absolute error by \(e^{-hK/2}\) gives
\[
(1-\delta)e^{hK}\preceq B\preceq(1+\delta)e^{hK}.
\]
The matrix logarithm is operator monotone, hence
\[
\|\log B-hK\|\le -\log(1-\delta)\le2\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
\|m\log B-\beta K\|\le\frac{2e^{1/4}}{3}\frac{\tau^3}{m^2}
<\frac{\tau^3}{m^2}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.
\]
Min--max eigenvalue comparison and scalar exponentiation yield
\[
e^{-\varepsilon/4}\operatorname{Tr}e^{\beta K}
\le\operatorname{Tr}(B^m)
\le e^{\varepsilon/4}\operatorname{Tr}e^{\beta K}.
\]
This avoids an additive trace error divided by the partition function. Approximate this rational positive trace by the imported FPRAS and multiply by \(e^{-\beta C}\) with a separately charged relative-precision scalar evaluation to recover \(\operatorname{Tr}e^{\beta H}\).

The previous audited first-order gate count chose \(m=O(\max\{\tau,\tau^2/\varepsilon\})\). Here it is \(O(\tau+\tau^{3/2}/\sqrt\varepsilon)\). For the inherited largest-eigenvalue reduction, \(\beta=20(n+1)/(\varepsilon C)\) gives \(\tau=40(n+1)/\varepsilon\), so the gate count improves from \(O(n^2\varepsilon^{-3})\) to \(O(n^{3/2}\varepsilon^{-2})\), up to constants. The Chen--Liu coefficient counter's high polynomial exponent remains, so this asymptotic gate reduction does not establish practical speed.
