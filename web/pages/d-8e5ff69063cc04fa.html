BLIND NORMAL EB OPTIMIZER LEMMA
2026-10-09. /root/covariant_review_sol. No cycle04 origin proof exposure.

CLAIM. On separable input/output Hilbert spaces, any nonempty normal-state
family with prescribed normal output targets has an attaining normal EB
channel for the infimum of the worst half-trace-distance error. In the
assigned reconstruction problem the target of rho is rho itself. Capacity
and trace compactness of the family are unnecessary for this lemma.

This is an internal derivation requiring hostile review; not a novelty or
external validation claim. It is isolated because ordinary compactness of
normal CPTP maps fails and cannot justify the conclusion by itself.

1. EXTRACT A NORMAL CP TRACE-NONINCREASING SUBCHANNEL

Take a minimizing sequence Lambda_n of normal CPTP EB maps with worst
error <=e+1/n. Trace-class output is the dual of the compact operators K(H).
Because the output H is separable, K(H) is separable, so bounded balls in
T(H) are weak-star sequentially compact in sigma(T(H),K(H)). The trace-norm
one ball is weak-star closed.

Use a countable basis of finite-rank input matrix units. Their Lambda_n
outputs have bounded trace norms. A diagonal subsequence converges weak-star
on every such unit. Linearity and weak-star lower semicontinuity of the norm
give a linear contraction Gamma on their finite linear span. Extend it by
norm continuity to all trace class. Uniform contraction of Lambda_n shows
pointwise weak-star convergence on EVERY trace-class input, by finite-rank
norm approximation, not merely on the chosen countable set.

Gamma is CP. At every finite matrix amplification, positivity is tested by
finite-rank compact output compressions, which converge in finite-dimensional
norm from positive Lambda_n outputs. Input finite-rank compressions and
norm continuity then extend positivity to all amplified positive inputs.
For positive rho,
 Tr Gamma(rho)=sup_P finite_rank Tr[P Gamma(rho)]<=Tr rho.
Thus Gamma is trace-nonincreasing. It is an actual bounded map T_in->T_out,
so its Heisenberg adjoint is normal. This avoids taking a possibly singular
cluster point on B(H) with no predual.

2. EB PROPERTY SURVIVES IN THE SUBNORMALIZED CONE

For a finite ancilla and positive input omega, the outputs
(Lambda_n tensor id)(omega) converge weak-star to (Gamma tensor id)(omega),
by the entrywise trace-class convergence above. Compress the output H by
any finite-rank P. Each compressed output is separable and subnormalized,
because local postselection preserves the separable cone. The finite-
dimensional cone of positive separable operators is closed, so its norm
limit P(Gamma tensor id)(omega)P is separable.

Let P increase to I. Since (Gamma tensor id)(omega) is positive trace class,
these compressions converge to it in trace norm; the subnormalized separable
cone is trace-norm closed. Hence Gamma is EB at finite ancillas. For an
arbitrary ancilla, compress its normal marginal to its first finitely many
positive eigenvectors and use trace-norm continuity of the normal CP
trace-nonincreasing amplified map. Every normal marginal has separable
support even if the ambient ancilla is nonseparable. Thus the same property
holds against ALL ancillas. No countable measure/prepare representation or
rank-one Kraus representation of the original Lambda_n is assumed.

3. LOST TRACE IS PART OF THE LIMITING TRACE-DISTANCE COST

Let y_n be normalized positive output states converging to positive
subnormalized y weak-star against compact operators, and let tau be any
normal target state. For finite-rank P and Q=I-P, pinching gives
 ||y_n-tau||_1
 >=||P(y_n-tau)P||_1+||Q(y_n-tau)Q||_1
 >=||P(y_n-tau)P||_1+Tr(P tau)-Tr(P y_n).
The second inequality uses ||Z||_1>=Tr Z for the Q corner and Tr y_n=Tr tau=1.
For fixed P, finite-dimensional compression converges in norm. Therefore
 liminf_n ||y_n-tau||_1
 >=||P(y-tau)P||_1+Tr(P tau)-Tr(P y).
Let P increase to I. This proves the stronger lower-semicontinuity bound
 liminf_n ||y_n-tau||_1>=||y-tau||_1+1-Tr y.                 (*)
The final lost-mass term is essential. Ordinary trace-norm lower
semicontinuity alone would not justify trace completion at unchanged error.

4. COMPLETE THE SUBCHANNEL WITH ONE NORMAL PREPARATION

The defect d(X)=Tr X-Tr Gamma(X) is a bounded positive functional on input
trace class. There is an effect M=I-Gamma*(I), 0<=M<=I, such that
d(X)=Tr(MX). Fix ANY normal output state sigma0 and define
 Lambda(X)=Gamma(X)+Tr(MX)sigma0.
This is normal CP and TP. The added subchannel is measure/prepare and EB.
Together with Section 2, the sum is EB against arbitrary ancillas.

For every input rho with target tau_rho, put y=Gamma(rho) and l=1-Tr y.
Triangle inequality and (*) give
 ||Lambda(rho)-tau_rho||_1
 <=||y-tau_rho||_1+l
 <=liminf_n ||Lambda_n(rho)-tau_rho||_1<=2e.
The same sigma0 and the same Lambda work for all rho, even for uncountably
many inputs. Pointwise convergence on all trace class was established in
Section 1, so no countability assumption on the family or diagonalization
over its labels occurs. Taking its supremum proves that Lambda attains e.

The lemma permits general normal EB optimizers, potentially with non-atomic
measurement representations. It gives neither finite-outcome attainment nor
efficient construction. For trace-compact input families, finite input and
output compression separately proves equality of the finite-outcome infimum
with this attained general optimum; a finite-outcome optimizer need not exist.
