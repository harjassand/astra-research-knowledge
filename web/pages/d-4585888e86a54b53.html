## Interface and result

Supply the eighteen fixed physical observables \(A\in\{X_i,Y_i,Z_i:1\le i\le6\}\), in the ordinary computational tensor basis. For a faithful state, its Hermitian SLD score is uniquely defined by
\[
 -i[A,\sigma]=\tfrac12\{\sigma,S_A^\sigma\}.
\]
There exist distinct faithful six-qubit density matrices \(\sigma,\rho\) with **the entire operator score identical for all eighteen observables**. The observables obey the exact Pauli tensor relations and their ordinary joint commutant is scalar. The target Hamiltonian is generally dense and the spectrum extremely spread; locality or high-temperature model restrictions are not preserved.

For \(\sigma=U\operatorname{diag}(p_i)U^T\), the state-dependent Hermitian filtered observable is
\[
 K_{A,ij}=\frac{2\sqrt{p_ip_j}}{p_i+p_j}(U^TAU)_{ij}.
\]
The exact fiber criterion is
\[
 \rho=\sqrt\sigma Y\sqrt\sigma,\quad Y>0,\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma Y)=1,\quad[Y,UK_AU^T]=0\quad\forall A.
\]
It follows by rearranging the fixed-score equation as \((A-iS/2)\rho=\rho(A+iS/2)\) and conjugating by \(\sigma^{-1/2}\); see the complete earlier independent derivation in `notes/r03_quantum/cycle2/real_ift_counterexample_audit.md`. Thus a common reducing projection of the filtered frame supplies an exact score collision.

## Real Bell base, Gray ordering and the six Y constraints

Let \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=iY\). Enumerate \(P_i\in\{I,X,Z,J\}^{\otimes3}\) in lexicographic order. Their real vectorizations divided by \(\sqrt8\) form an orthonormal Bell basis across a three-qubit/three-qubit split. Order these columns by
\(g_k=k\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(k\!\gg\!1)\), \(k=0,\ldots,63\).

Two consecutive labels differ in exactly one bit. In the two-bit index of one real Pauli, a bit flip changes the relative product by a single X or Z, up to sign. Therefore
\(P_{g_k}P_{g_{k+1}}^T\) and \(P_{g_k}^TP_{g_{k+1}}\) are real symmetric. The independent replay verifies this with exact integer matrices, not modular arithmetic.

Use the explicit rational Cayley half rotations from the retained input snapshot,
\[
 V=(I-S_V)(I+S_V)^{-1},\quad W=(I-S_W)(I+S_W)^{-1},\quad
 U_0=(V\otimes W)\operatorname{Bell}_{g}.
\]
Every Bell column remains maximally entangled. Hence the diagonal of \(U_0^TAU_0\) vanishes for every local Pauli A. In particular:

* For X and Z, \(B_A=U_0^TAU_0\) is real symmetric with zero diagonal.
* For Y, write \(A=-iJ_i\), so \(D_i=U_0^TJ_iU_0\) is real antisymmetric. Its nearest-neighbor entries \(D_{i,k,k+1}\) vanish exactly. To see this on the left half, the relevant matrix element is \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{g_k}^TV^TJ_iVP_{g_{k+1}})/8\); the rotated J is antisymmetric while the adjacent relative product is symmetric, so the trace is zero. The right-half proof is identical with \(P_{g_k}^TP_{g_{k+1}}\).

This adjacent-entry property holds for arbitrary real orthogonal half rotations, so it is compatible with generic non-Clifford rotations and a full-rank Jacobian.

## Complete analytic equations

\]
For real \(x\), Q is orthogonal. Define the analytic scalar filter
\[
 C_{ii}(s)=1,\qquad C_{ij}(s)=\frac{2s^{|i-j|}}{1+s^{2|i-j|}}\quad(i\ne j).
\]
For all twelve X/Z observables use the 63 centered row sums
\[
 F_{A,k}(x,s)=\sum_j(C(s)\circ B_A(x))_{kj}
 -\frac1{64}\sum_{l,j}(C(s)\circ B_A(x))_{lj},\quad k=0,\ldots,62,
\]
where \(B_A(x)=U(x)^TAU(x)\). The missing centered row follows because all 64 sum to zero. These equations make \(\mathbf1\) a common eigenvector of every real symmetric X/Z filtered observable.

For each of the six Y generators use the 63 prefix cut flows
\[
 G_{i,k}(x,s)=\frac1s\sum_{a\le k<b}C_{ab}(s)D_{i,ab}(x)
 =\sum_{a\le k<b}\frac{2s^{b-a-1}}{1+s^{2(b-a)}}D_{i,ab}(x),
\]
where \(D_i(x)=U(x)^TJ_iU(x)\). This expression is analytic at zero, with
\(G_{i,k}(x,0)=2D_{i,k,k+1}(x)\).
Internal terms cancel when summing the first k+1 row sums of an antisymmetric matrix, so these are precisely the prefix row sums divided by s. For every nonzero s, all G zero if and only if \((C\circ D_i)\mathbf1=0\): successive differences recover rows 0 through62, and the total row sum is zero. Therefore the physical Y filtered observables \(-i(C\circ D_i)\) annihilate \(\mathbf1\).

There are **756 real X/Z equations plus378 real Y equations**. The other components vanish identically under the real orthogonal chart: X/Z matrices have no imaginary part, and Y matrices have no real part. Thus the construction has not discarded the imaginary Y conditions; it has analytically desingularized all of them. At \((x,s)=(0,0)\), every F and G vanishes.

## Explicit positive existence interval

All Jacobian entries have absolute value at most4: X/Z diagonal derivatives are at most2, and Y derivatives have the prefactor2 multiplying an entry of a commutator of norm at most2. The nonzero rational determinant satisfies \(|\det\mathcal J|\ge D^{-m}\). Cofactor expansion and row sums imply
\[
 \|\mathcal J^{-1}\|_\infty\le L,
 \qquad L=m!\,4^{m-1}D^m.
\]

For \(R=(I-\Omega/2)^{-1}\), \(\|R\|_{\rm op}\le1\). The Cayley derivatives are
\(\partial_iQ=RE_iR\) and
\(\partial_{ij}Q=(RE_jRE_iR+RE_iRE_jR)/2\), both norm at most1. Entries of first/second derivatives of a conjugated physical observable are at most2/4.

For \(|s|\le1/2\), the X/Z bounds from the previous audit hold. For a Y cut, put \(r_s=|s|\). At most ℓ cross edges have length ℓ. Therefore the sum of absolute cut weights is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{a\le k<b}\left|\frac{2s^{b-a-1}}{1+s^{2(b-a)}}\right|
 \le2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell r_s^{\ell-1}\le8.
\]
The sum of absolute derivatives of these weights is at most
\[
 2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell(\ell-1)r_s^{\ell-2}
 +4\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell^2r_s^{3\ell-2}<36.
\]
Consequently \(|G_s|\le36\), \(|\partial_iG|\le16\), and \(|\partial_{ij}G|\le32\). The variation of each first x derivative from the base is at most \(32\sum_j|x_j|+72|s|\). Since \(d=64\), these are dominated by the same combined bounds as the X/Z equations:
\[
 \|\mathcal F_s\|_\infty\le4d,
 \quad
 \|D_x\mathcal F(x,s)-\mathcal J\|_\infty
 \le8dm(m\|x\|_\infty+|s|),
\]
where \(\mathcal F=(F,G)\). Let
\[
 r=\frac1{32dLm^2},\qquad
 \epsilon=\frac1{256d^2L^2m^2}.
\]
For \(|s|\le\epsilon\), the map \(x\mapsto x-\mathcal J^{-1}\mathcal F(x,s)\) contracts the cube \(\|x\|_\infty\le r\) with factor at most1/2, and its center displacement is at most \(4dL\epsilon=r/2\). It maps the cube into itself. Thus every such s has a unique solution x(s) there. The inverse function theorem also gives analytic dependence on s.

Fix the explicit rational \(s_*=\epsilon/2\). The equations are rational functions of x,s with rational coefficients: Bell normalization cancels in conjugation. Clearing the nonzero denominators gives a rational polynomial system with an isolated nonsingular root x(s*), so its coordinates are real algebraic. The rational parameter, polynomial equations and isolating cube define the state pair exactly. This is not a feasible-temperature or small-bit-cost guarantee.

## Faithful distinct states with identical full local scores

For any \(0<s<\epsilon\), define
\[
 p_i=\frac{s^{2i}}{\sum_{j=0}^{63}s^{2j}},\quad
 \sigma_s=U(x(s))\operatorname{diag}(p_i)U(x(s))^T.
\]
Every eigenvalue is positive and its genuine SLD filter is exactly C(s). Every filtered X/Z observable has common eigenvector \(\mathbf1\); every filtered Y observable annihilates it. All eighteen are Hermitian, so they reduce the projection
\[
 P_s=U(x(s))\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^T}{64}U(x(s))^T.
\]
The projection has target expectation1/64. Hence
\[
 Y_s=\frac{63}{64}I+P_s,\qquad
 \rho_s=\sqrt{\sigma_s}Y_s\sqrt{\sigma_s}
\]
is normalized and faithful: Y has eigenvalues127/64 and63/64. It differs from \(\sigma_s\), and the exact fiber criterion proves equality of all eighteen full physical SLD operators.

## Consequence and remaining scope

Complete physical single-site X/Y/Z scores do not identify arbitrary faithful states at arbitrary temperature. Exact Pauli tensor relations, local Lie-algebra closure and ordinary frame irreducibility alone cannot rescue SLD identification. Extra restrictions must constrain the target, the filtered commutant or the supplied observation interface.

Nearest source is Dong–Leng, arXiv:2609.28391v1, AppendixA.1–A.2 (state-dependent filtered commutant) and their high-temperature local X/Z theorem. Our earlier exact stabilizer-target coercivity result remains positive, because the generic eigenbasis here is not Clifford diagonal. This full-X/Y/Z theorem does not refute a high-temperature/sparse-Hamiltonian theorem, and does not establish an obstruction under asymmetric weighted scores for this same full physical frame.

Concrete downstream use: an exact regression obstruction for purported full-local SLD calibration and universal inverse-Hamiltonian claims. Next falsifiable steps are a rigorously certified continuation at a moderate spectrum, a lower-dimensional exact witness, or a collision within a specified bounded-range noncommuting Hamiltonian class. Each requires new evidence; none follows from the present extreme-spectrum dense target.

## Bounded local Gibbs consequence

This finite six-qubit block admits a shared known Gibbs family: expand both centered negative logarithms in the full 4095 nonidentity physical six-qubit Pauli strings, and choose a common finite inverse temperature large enough that every coefficient lies in [-1,1]. Repeating independent six-qubit blocks gives locality at most six and interaction intersection degree at most4094 independent of system size. Entire local scores tensor with identity. This is a bounded-locality obstruction at a possibly enormous fixed temperature parameter; it does not establish failure for two-local, sparse, connected or moderate-temperature models.

For repeated blocks the two states can approach trace distance one while all these scores remain identical. In the common eigenbasis their (0,1) matrix-entry difference is sqrt(p0 p1)/64, so the observable U(E01+E10)U^T has nonzero expectation difference 2sqrt(p0 p1)/64. Repeated-block bounded-observable measurements and Hoeffding's inequality separate the products with error tending to zero. The repetition cost scales at least inversely with the square of this tiny gap; this is a consistency obstruction, not an efficient experiment.
