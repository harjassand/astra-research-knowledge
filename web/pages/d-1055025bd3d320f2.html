# Cycle 2 — finite-data extinction certification with sanctuary flux

## Result and novelty verdict

A bulk resistant-clone decline certificate is invalid if a viable sanctuary can keep sending resistant founders into the treated region. The failure has an explicit flux threshold. A spatial first-moment certificate can cover a sampled domain if its worst-case growth operator is uniformly contracting and all source flux is bounded; persistent unmeasured input creates a nonzero floor, and unbounded or unobserved refugia leave relapse risk `UNKNOWN`.

This is a useful audit boundary, not a new spatial mechanism or theorem. Fu, Nowak and Bonhoeffer already model cancer drug sanctuaries as source compartments, coupled by migration to drug-exposed compartments, using multitype branching processes; their routes explicitly include mutation in the sanctuary followed by migration and migration followed by mutation. The weighted-PDE inequality below is a standard Lyapunov/first-moment bound. The only plausible contribution is a finite-sampling interface that translates spatial cell coverage and measured flux into a confidence-bound on relapse risk. Novelty/priority for that data-to-certificate workflow remains `UNKNOWN`.

## A sharp two-compartment failure boundary

Let `H(t)` be resistant cells in a sanctuary and let `m` be their per-cell rate of exiting the sanctuary into a treated region. Assume the treated region is otherwise clear, finite measurements support an upper envelope on expected sanctuary burden, and `q` upper-bounds the conditional probability that descendants of one arrival establish under the **remaining pulse plus subsequent regimen**. This includes survival through the rest of the pulse; a post-pulse establishment probability alone is not enough.

\[
H(t)\le H_0^U e^{\rho_U t}.
\]

Then the expected number of arriving founders over an interval of length `T` is bounded by

\[
\Lambda_U(T)=m_UH_0^U G(\rho_U,T),\qquad
G(\rho,T)=\begin{cases}(e^{\rho T}-1)/\rho,&\rho\ne0,\\T,&\rho=0.\end{cases}
\]

Without assuming independent arrivals or independent outcomes, a union bound gives

\[
P(\text{at least one successful reseed by }T)\le q_U\Lambda_U(T).
\]

If the arrival process is Poisson with deterministic intensity and each arriving lineage independently establishes with probability `q`, thinning gives the exact probability `1-exp(-q Lambda(T))`. This is a source-path bound; a full global-eradication claim must also bound any resistant population that remains inside the sanctuary at `T`. If its expected burden at `T` is at most `H0^U exp(rho_U T)`, and each such cell has future establishment probability at most `q_H`, add `q_H H0^U exp(rho_U T)` to the union bound (use `q_H=1` if any residual viable sanctuary cell counts as failure). Include this term for every possible sanctuary stratum.

For a risk target `epsilon` and no other risk terms, the conservative certificate requires

\[
q_Um_UH_0^UG(\rho_U,T)\le\epsilon.
\]

At `rho_U=0`, it fails for every `T` when `q_U m_U H_0^U T>epsilon`; the exact-Poisson boundary is `q m H_0 T <= -log(1-epsilon)`. If `rho_U>0`, the maximum certifiable horizon is

\[
T\le {1\over\rho_U}\log\!\left(1+{\rho_U\epsilon\over q_Um_UH_0^U}\right).
\]

For `rho_U<0`, the lifetime source integral is finite, `G(rho_U,infinity)=1/|rho_U|`; if `q_Um_UH_0^U/|rho_U| >= epsilon`, extending the pulse cannot meet the risk target. Any persistent positive flux over an infinite horizon yields establishment probability tending to one when `q>0` under the Poisson model. Thus a finite window can certify only a finite-horizon outcome unless the refuge and its future flux are also eliminated or bounded over the remaining horizon.

**Illustrative counterexample, not a fit:** take `H0=1,000`, `m=10^-4` exits per sanctuary cell-day, `rho=0`, `q=0.1`, and `T=30` days. The treated compartment starts empty and may show complete local clearance, yet the sanctuary supplies an expected three founders. Exact Poisson risk is `1-exp(-0.3)=0.259`; the robust union bound is `0.30`. To certify risk below 5% at this `H0,q,T`, the exact-Poisson boundary is `m<=1.71e-5` per cell-day (the union-bound limit is `1.67e-5`). At fixed `m=10^-4`, the sanctuary upper bound must instead be `H0<=171` viable resistant cells. With `rho=0.01/day`, that burden threshold tightens to about 147 cells. A scalar decrease in the treated compartment says nothing about any of these quantities.

## Spatial branching/PDE certificate

On a sampled domain `Omega`, let `u(x,t)` be the expected density of a rare resistant lineage under a linear branching-diffusion approximation:

\[
\partial_tu=D\Delta u+r(x,t)u+s(x,t),\qquad D\partial_nu=j(x,t)\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
\]

Here `r` is local net growth in the measured background, `s` is internal input such as de-novo resistant founders, and `j>=0` is inward boundary flux from a reservoir outside the sampled domain. Assume there is a positive weight `w(x)>=1`, with `partial_n w=0`, and a uniform contraction margin `delta>0` such that the **worst-case** growth envelope satisfies

\[
D\Delta w+r_U(x,t)w\le-\delta w
\quad\text{for every }x\in\Omega,\ t\in[0,T].
\]

Define `W(t)=integral_Omega w(x)u(x,t) dx`. Integration by parts gives

\[
{dW\over dt}\le-\delta W+F_w(t),\quad
F_w(t)=\int_\Omega w s\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega} wj\,dA.
\]

If simultaneous upper confidence bounds establish `F_w(t)<=Fbar`, then

\[
W(T)\le W_0e^{-\delta T}+{Fbar\over\delta}(1-e^{-\delta T}).
\]

If any cell present at `T` has probability at most `qbar` of establishing under the specified future regimen, the union bound yields

\[
P(\text{establishment from cells present at }T)
\le qbar\left[W_0e^{-\delta T}+{Fbar\over\delta}(1-e^{-\delta T})\right].
\]

The certificate has a nonzero floor `qbar Fbar/delta`. If this is at least the allowed `epsilon`, no choice of longer `T` certifies the target under this bound. If `Fbar/delta < epsilon/qbar` and `W0>Fbar/delta`, the sufficient duration is

\[
T\ge{1\over\delta}\log\!\left({W_0-Fbar/\delta\over\epsilon/qbar-Fbar/\delta}\right).
\]

This bounds establishment from lineages present at `T`. If a refuge can continue sending cells **after** `T`, its future integrated successful-founder flux must be added separately; a constant positive future source has infinite-horizon risk one for `q>0` under independent Poisson arrivals. The alternative is to include the sanctuary in `Omega`, in which case its growth and migration belong in the operator and the source boundary must disappear. A sanctuary with positive growth may make `delta>0` impossible; this is a genuine failure of the certificate, not a parameter-fitting inconvenience.

For a finite spatial discretization, the mean vector obeys `m'(t)=A m(t)+g(t)`, where `A` is a Metzler matrix containing clone growth and interpatch migration. A positive left vector `w` satisfying `w^T A_U <= -delta w^T` gives the same result. Here `A_U` must use simultaneous one-sided upper bounds on local growth and migration, not point estimates. If no such vector exists for the upper-envelope matrix, this particular extinction certificate is unavailable. If the estimated principal growth rate is positive, report failure/unknown; do not force a negative mean by averaging away a refuge.

The proof is conditional on the branching-diffusion first-moment model, measured upper parameter bounds, correct domain coverage, and a valid post-`T` establishment bound. It does not establish that real tumor cells follow independent diffusion, nor that a deterministic mean predicts probability without the union-bound/establishment step.

## What finite spatial sampling must cover

To make the bound auditable, finite data need more than a few biopsies and a total ctDNA decline.

1. **Domain and absolute burden:** enumerate the anatomical compartments and viable malignant-cell burden `N_i` in each, including plausible low-drug, hypoxic, necrotic-edge, marrow/CNS, and metastatic reservoirs. VAF alone is not a resistant-cell count; account for purity, copy number, allele multiplicity and viable fraction.
2. **Clone abundance by stratum:** predefine strata from anatomy and drug-access/oxygen measurements, then measure resistant and compound-escape genotypes within each by a validated single-cell or error-corrected assay. Record detection sensitivity `s_i`; a negative call is not zero cells.
3. **Local growth envelope:** estimate clone-specific division/death or, at minimum, net growth `r_i` under the actual regimen in each stratum. Drug concentration alone is not the net growth field because target response, hypoxia, competition and phenotype state intervene.
4. **Routes and flux:** map adjacency and measure per-cell migration/reseeding rates `m_ij` across all sampled interfaces, plus inward flux across the sampled-domain boundary. A sampled sanctuary without its outward flux bound, or a bounded local flux with an unbounded external refuge, is insufficient.
5. **Post-entry establishment:** estimate an upper bound `q_i` for a single founder entering each treatment-exposed stratum under the future regimen, for example by single-founder limiting-dilution/regrowth with the same environment. The assumed value `q=0.1` in the numerical fixture is not evidence.
6. **Spatial resolution and time:** preregister a minimum sanctuary size/width that the model claims to cover and sample with a covering radius no larger than half that scale; collect repeated positions on a timescale that can resolve migration. If arbitrarily small or anatomically hidden sources are allowed, finite spatial sampling cannot guarantee their absence.

For a known stratum with `N_i` viable cells, suppose `n_i` cells are sampled uniformly without replacement (the binomial approximation is accurate when `n_i` is small relative to `N_i`) and each resistant cell is detected independently with sensitivity `s_i`. If no resistant cells are found, a one-sided upper prevalence bound at error `alpha_i` is

\[
p_i^U={1-\alpha_i^{1/n_i}\over s_i}\quad(\text{cap at }1),\qquad H_i^U=N_i p_i^U.
\]

For simultaneous coverage of `K` strata, one conservative choice is `alpha_i=alpha/K`. At sensitivity `s=0.95`, familywise confidence 95%, and `K=20`, zero calls among 628 usable cells per stratum only bounds each prevalence below 1% (about 12,560 cells total); 6,304 per stratum are needed to bound each below 0.1% (about 126,080 total). These limits bound prevalence, not a small relapse probability in a huge cell population.

For the numeric sanctuary fixture above, take one known refuge stratum with `N_H=10^6` cells. The 5% risk target requires `H0^U<=171`, or prevalence at most `1.71e-4`. With 95% detection sensitivity, zero resistant calls require 18,442 independent randomly sampled cells for a 95% one-stratum upper bound. If there are 20 possible refuge strata and the total 5% risk budget is split equally, each stratum must be bounded by `H_i^U<=8.55` cells; with `N_i=10^6`, sensitivity 95%, and Bonferroni familywise correction, this requires about 737,732 cells per stratum (14.75 million total) after zero calls. These idealized counts rise with clustering, assay failures and uncertainty in `N_i`.

Flux requires its own exposure cost. If `k=0` sanctuary exits are observed across `E` tracked cell-days and exits are Poisson, the one-sided 95% rate upper bound is `m_U=-log(0.05)/E`. To establish `m_U<=1.71e-5/day` therefore requires at least 175,212 sanctuary cell-days: about 1,000 fully tracked cells for 175 days, or 5,834 cells for 30 days, with zero observed exits. The assay also needs enough independent single-founder regrowth trials to bound `q`; with zero establishments in `n` independent trials, `q_U=1-0.05^(1/n)`, so 29 zero-outgrowth trials are needed just to put the one-sided 95% upper bound below 0.1. All numbers are idealized and assume correctly classified viable cells and independent exposures.

The fundamental finite-data limit is clear: without a known coverage domain, an upper bound on refuge burden, and an upper bound on future flux, the risk bound is not finite. Even complete sampling of the treated core does not constrain a reservoir outside it. For large tumors, ruling out a few clonogenic founders by random biopsy can demand more cells than exist in the accessible specimen; the right output is then a non-certification, not “eradicated.”

## Prior-art attack and contribution boundary

- **Direct cancer overlap:** Fu, Nowak and Bonhoeffer, “Spatial Heterogeneity in Drug Concentrations Can Facilitate the Emergence of Resistance to Cancer Therapy,” *PLOS Computational Biology* 11 (2015), model sanctuary/drug compartments, limited migration, branching, mutation and both migration–mutation orders. They derive how spatial heterogeneity and migration jointly alter escape probability and timing. The present two-compartment source term is a coarse reduction of that mechanism: [primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004142), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4366398/).
- **Direct cancer spatial-therapy overlap:** Strobl et al. compare an on-lattice spatial adaptive-therapy model with its mean-field ODE, vary resistant-cell nest layout, and fit longitudinal PSA data in 65 prostate cancer patients: [Communications Medicine (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-022-00110-x).
- **Spatial drug-pattern/eigenvalue overlap:** Freire et al. use principal-eigenvalue mutant fitness under spatially varying drug gradients and vary periodic pattern spacing while conserving total drug amount (Fig. 8): [PLOS Computational Biology (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012098). This is bacterial rather than cancer biology, but blocks novelty for the general math principle.
- The risk union bound, positive weighted Lyapunov function, principal-eigenvalue test and zero-event Poisson upper limit are standard probability/positive-systems tools. No new proof technique is claimed.

**Strongest defensible next gate:** prospectively measure refuge burden and migration flux in a spatially resolved cancer model, propagate simultaneous confidence bounds into the weighted certificate, and compare blinded recurrence-risk calibration against (i) the original well-mixed net-growth model and (ii) an equally informed spatial branching model without the certificate. The certificate adds practical value only if it is calibrated and changes a decision under matched measurements; that has not been tested.

## Evidence status and open items

- **Internally derived:** source-flux failure threshold; weighted PDE/finite-patch Lyapunov bound; finite-sampling and zero-exit limits; illustrative numerical fixtures above.
- **Externally reported:** sanctuary migration/mutation pathways and spatial resistance models in the cited papers.
- **Not established:** cancer parameter values for `H0`, `rho`, `m`, `delta`, `q`; whether all relevant refuge compartments can be enumerated; whether the bound predicts clinical relapse; novelty of the proposed calibration workflow.
- **Not performed:** no cancer-cell experiment, tumor fit, trial design validation, or empirical risk calibration.
