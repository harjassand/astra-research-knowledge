## Concrete question

The parent's implicit converter uses the classical symmetric three-state targeted Bernoulli source, an iid ternary target of length n, and a proposal/verifier accepted subexperiment Q. After rejection it classifies afresh with confusion matrix A, and samples from a row of `W=A^-1(H-Q)` normalized through rejection rather than computing a common residual mass delta. Target reports are represented by histograms with uniform ordering reconstructed afterward. Can this avoid exponential report-law enumeration while preserving the sharp source-observation coefficient? Does the sampler itself constitute a new primitive?

## Primary source priority threat

The complete algorithm and discussion on that page were read and visually inspected. It describes exact rejection from a signed mixture: sample from the normalized positive-part envelope and test the original signed density pointwise against that envelope. It attributes the algorithm to Bignami and de Matteis (1971). Its discussion also separates rejection count from density-evaluation work and allows partial evaluation when rigorous tail bounds are available. Thus signed coefficients, pointwise implicit evaluation, and avoiding support-wide enumeration are classical.

## Independent reconstruction: common delta need not be computed

Let H_l be known probability mass functions; Q_l accepted subprobability masses obey `0<=Q_l(z)<=H_l(z)`. Set `R_l=H_l-Q_l>=0`. Let b be one row of A^-1, and suppose the chosen accuracy ensures `W(z)=sum_l b_l R_l(z)>=0`. Define

`e(z)=sum_{l:b_l>0} b_l H_l(z)`, `B=sum_{l:b_l>0} b_l`.

Then `W(z)<=sum_{b_l>0} b_l R_l(z)<=e(z)`, and the envelope has mass B. Select l with probability b_l/B among positive coefficients, draw z from H_l, and accept with probability W(z)/e(z); zero envelope points are never proposed. Per-trial accepted mass at z is W(z)/B. If the mass of W is positive, eventual output law is W normalized by its own mass, with geometric trial count of mean B/sum_z W(z). No support enumeration or residual normalization constant is needed. This is exactly ordinary unnormalized rejection using the classical signed-mixture envelope principle.

If every R_i has the same mass delta, then `A1=1` implies `A^-1 1=1`, so every W_k also has mass delta. A fresh classifier followed by the implicit row sampler gives `A(W/delta)=R/delta`, precisely the conditional residual experiment required after a branch of probability delta. The complete law is Q+R=H.

**Load-bearing condition:** not computing delta does not remove the need to prove its common statewise value. If residual masses differ by state, independent normalization of inverse rows generally destroys the matrix identity required by the outer branch. Positivity of W and pointwise computability are additional obligations; a signed expression alone is not a sampling algorithm.

**Cost condition:** conditional on the residual branch, expected proposal count is B/delta. Multiplication by its statewise entry probability delta cancels this factor in unconditional average count. This only controls total evaluator work if each trial's sampling/evaluation work is itself bounded in expectation under the proposal and restart state. Rare-branch cancellation does not bound the maximum runtime, preprocessing, arbitrary bigint lengths, or an expensive W evaluator. Devroye already warns that rejection constants do not account for evaluation cost.

## Histogram law and exact output reconstruction

For iid ternary probabilities p_i, the histogram h=(h0,h1,h2) has mass

`H_i(h)=n!/(h0!h1!h2!) * product_a p_i(a)^h_a`.

Conditional on h, all words of that histogram have equal probability under every state. Sample uniformly by repeatedly drawing the next symbol proportional to its remaining count. The probability of any particular ordering is `h0!h1!h2!/n!`, so the reconstructed word has exactly its iid target probability. This proof is state independent and needs no 3^n law table. It does not itself bound the work of computing Q_i(h) or W_k(h). Polynomial-size histograms do not imply a polynomial evaluator unless the verifier dynamics and arithmetic are explicitly controlled.
