# Cycle 2: a value-only shared-direction cloud correction

2026-10-06, c_nonlinear. Status: reconstructed scoped theorem; independent external validation and novelty audit remain open. This is an alternative to the source's Appendix B action implementation, not a validation or dimension improvement of its complete sampler.

## Exact interface and result

Supply value queries to a globally Lipschitz Φ:R^m→R^n, constants L,r with r≥1, 0<L²≤1/4, and almost-everywhere derivative bounds

    ||DΦ(x)||op≤L, ||DΦ(x)||F²≤rL².             (I)

The theorem uses exact value queries and exact real arithmetic. It does not query Jacobian-vector products, transpose actions, or any intrinsic projector. Standard Gaussians and a consistent finitely revealed m-dimensional Brownian bridge are supplied. Certifying (I), constructing the value oracle, and finite precision are separate acquisition costs. No higher derivative bound is supplied: heat smoothing from bridge averaging provides the required derivative variation bound. The paper's composed maps still need a verified differential-energy bound before this applies to them.

For secant step h>0 define

    F_h(x,v)=[Φ(x+hv)−Φ(x−hv)]/(2h).

For each covariance estimator, draw fresh T uniform on [0,1], G₁,G₂,V independent standard m-dimensional Gaussians, reveal B_T from the same proposal's Brownian path, and set

    X_j=B_T+sqrt(1−T)G_j,
    a_j=F_h(X_j,V), S_h=a₁ a₂ᵀ.                       (O)

The direction V is **shared** between the two secants. S_h is a realized linear rank-one matrix action although its coefficients are nonlinear secants. It costs four Φ values and O(m+n) vector arithmetic; S_h z=a₁(a₂ᵀz). Conditional independence of fresh S_h draws is with respect to the full Brownian path.

Let p≥2 be an even integer, K≥1, clipping B∈(0,1], and

    α_p=L²(√r+2√p)² <1,
    e_p=2p α_p/(1−α_p),
    Δ_h=2sqrt(2/π) h L²√r sqrt(m(m+2)).

Use the source's finite likelihood product estimator

    M_k=S_{h,k}⋯S_{h,1},
    W_K=½Σ_{k=1}^K[Z′ᵀM_kZ′/k−ZᵀM_kZ],

where Z,Z′ are independent standard n-dimensional Gaussians under the proposal and the same realized matrices act on both vectors. Clip W_K to [−B,B] and apply Poisson-product first-order rejection sampling (FORS). Its output Y=Φ(B₁)+Z obeys, for universal constants C,c,

    TV(law Y,N(EΦ(G),I_n))
      ≤ C[ rL^(2K+2) + B(e_p/B)^p + Δ_h ],            (T)

provided CrL^(2K+2)≤c. All failed attempts and estimator calls are charged:

    expected attempts ≤exp(2B),
    expected W_K calls ≤2B exp(2B),
    expected Φ calls ≤exp(2B)[1+8BK].                 (Q)

The last count uses one endpoint Φ value per proposal and four values per covariance factor. Deferring the endpoint value until acceptance can save queries, but (Q) does not rely on that optimization. Expected vector work is O(exp(2B)(m+n)[1+BK]) plus time-index management; a balanced bridge map adds O(exp(2B) BK log(1+K)) expected comparison work for B≤1. Each output already costs Ω(n). A realized proposal with J Poisson estimator calls stores O(m[1+JK]+n) coordinates if bridge points are retained and other workspaces are reused.

The comparison bound must account for rare blocks of K insertions. For J~Poisson(2B), JK log(1+JK)≤JK[log(1+K)+log(1+J)] and E[J log(1+J)]≤CB for B≤1. Multiplying by the expected reached attempts gives the displayed bound. Using log(1+BK) instead would incorrectly substitute the mean insertion count inside the logarithm; the J=1 event disproves it when B=K^−2. The independent c_gaussian gate identified this bookkeeping correction; it leaves (T) and (Q) unchanged.

For δ accuracy choose K and p logarithmic in (r+1)/δ, e_p≤cB, and h≤cδ/[L²√r sqrt(m(m+2))]. The admitted L² scale is O(B/[p(r+p)]). This is dimension-free in the **query-count/contraction-scale** sense for fixed supplied r. It is weaker in r than the exact-Jacobian gadget's O(r^−1/2) scale for L². The finite-step choice contains m and the resulting arithmetic precision depends on m. There is no dimension-free total computational claim.

### Quantified value precision extension

Suppose each query instead uses a deterministic approximate value function Φhat with **uniform Euclidean output error** ||Φhat(x)−Φ(x)||≤τ. This must be a supplied precision guarantee, not inferred from observed numerical residuals. Its central secant error is at most γ=τ/h. Conditional independence of bridge points still implies a PSD mean covariance Σhat_h, because averaging over those points produces an outer product of a mean secant with itself. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

    ||Σhat_h−Σ_h||_*≤2L√r γ+γ²,
    ||Σhat_h||op≤(L+γ)²,
    trΣhat_h≤(L√r+γ)²≤r(L+γ)².

Also ||||Shat_h||op||p≤[L(√r+2√p)+γ]². Thus replacing L by Lhat=L+γ everywhere in α_p, e_p and the truncation term gives a conservative version of (T), provided Lhat²≤1/4. Replace Δ_h by

    Δ_h+2L√r γ+γ²+τ.

The final τ term accounts for using Φhat(B₁) in the returned center: under the ideal Gaussian tilt its conditional covariance has eigenvalues ≥3/4, so a mean shift of norm τ contributes at most Cτ in TV. The covariance discrepancy terms are controlled by the same conditional Gaussian KL calculation as (4). This is a quantitative oracle-precision extension, not a proof for arbitrary floating-point roundoff in the bridge, dot products, or acceptance comparisons.

For example, in the small-L regime choose γ≤cδ/(1+L√r) and τ=hγ, in addition to the finite-secant step condition. Hence the value error requirement contains h and therefore input dimension m. Producing each of n output coordinates with Euclidean accuracy τ generally requires coordinate accuracy τ/√n and correspondingly more bits. The value-oracle cost at this requested precision, its output magnitude/offset, Gaussian generation, and arithmetic errors must all be charged before claiming runtime superiority.

## 1. Central secants preserve differential energy in expectation

For fixed x, F_h(x,v) is odd in v and L-Lipschitz. Its derivative in v is

    D_vF_h(x,v)=½[DΦ(x+hv)+DΦ(x−hv)].

Consequently its op norm is ≤L and Frobenius norm is ≤√rL. Gaussian Poincaré, summed over the n output components, gives

    E F_h(x,V)=0, E||F_h(x,V)||²≤rL².                 (1)

This is uniform in x,h,m,n. Scalar Gaussian concentration for the L-Lipschitz norm gives

    || ||F_h(x,V)|| ||_q ≤ L(√r+√(2q)), q≥2.          (2)

For the explicit constants used here, q is even. Integrating the positive tail exp(−s²/(2L²)) bounds its q-th moment by (2L²)^(q/2)Γ(q/2+1); Γ(q/2+1)≤(q/2)^(q/2) for integer q/2, and the displayed √(2q) is conservative. Jensen gives E norm≤L√r. Thus (2) follows by the triangle inequality. Uniform conditional versions hold when x is independently randomized before V.

These facts can be reconstructed using the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup P_t. For scalar f,

    Varγ(f)=2∫₀∞E||∇P_tf||²dt,
    ∇P_tf=e^(−t)P_t∇f,

so Jensen and invariance give Varγ(f)≤E||∇f||². Gaussian concentration follows from the Gaussian log-Sobolev entropy identity and the Herbst argument, or from the heat-flow proof in Ledoux's note, Theorem 1 and §4. Neither concentration fact is new.

One-sided increments do not share this property. The smooth scalar map Φ(x)=Lsqrt(1+||x||²) satisfies ||DΦ||op=||DΦ||F≤L and H≤L. At x=0 its one-sided secant is L(sqrt(1+h²||V||²)−1)/h, whose second moment approaches mL² as h→∞, while the central secant is identically zero. Oddness is a load-bearing condition, not cosmetic centering.

## 2. The averaged covariance is PSD and has the original spectral budget

Given B,T let

    g_{B,T}(v)=E_G F_h(B_T+sqrt(1−T)G,v).

It is again odd, L-Lipschitz, and has Frobenius derivative budget rL², by conditional Jensen. Independence of X₁,X₂ conditional on B,T,V gives

    E[S_h|B,T]=E_V g_{B,T}(V)g_{B,T}(V)ᵀ.

Therefore Σ_h=E[S_h|B] is symmetric PSD. For any u, scalar Poincaré yields

    uᵀE[g(V)g(V)ᵀ]u=E(uᵀg(V))²≤E||Dg(V)ᵀu||²≤L²||u||².

Vector Poincaré gives tr E[g(V)g(V)ᵀ]≤rL². Averaging T proves **pathwise**

    0≤Σ_h≤L²I, trΣ_h≤rL².                           (3)

This does not claim that Σ_h has rank r. It can be full rank when derivative directions rotate.

Independent V₁,V₂ in (O) would give E S_h=0 because both secants are odd with zero direction mean. Using one bridge point X₁=X₂ would instead estimate E[F_hF_hᵀ] and fail to average the Jacobian correctly. Both independence of bridge endpoints and sharing of the direction are necessary.

## 3. Quantified transfer to the exact martingale covariance

Let u_t(x)=E Φ(x+sqrt(1−t)G) and A_t=Du_t(B_t)=E[DΦ(B_t+sqrt(1−t)G)|B]. The exact cloud covariance correction is Σ=∫₀¹A_tA_tᵀdt. First average the bridge endpoints: g_{B,t}(v)=F_h[u_t](B_t,v). This identity is the reason no higher derivative of Φ is needed.

For t<1 Gaussian integration by parts applied to its bounded weak Jacobian gives, for any direction a,

    D(Du_t)(x)[a]=(1/sqrt(1−t)) E[DΦ(x+sqrt(1−t)G)(Gᵀa)],
    ||D(Du_t)(x)[a]||op≤sqrt(2/π) L||a||/sqrt(1−t).

Thus the heat-smoothed derivative has globally Lipschitz op norm with H_t=sqrt(2/π)L/sqrt(1−t). Its operator and Frobenius budgets are still L and √rL by Jensen. Taylor's integral formula now gives

    ||g_{B,t}(V)−A_tV||≤(H_t h/2)||V||²,
    (E||g_{B,t}(V)−A_tV||²)^(1/2)
       ≤(H_t h/2)sqrt(m(m+2)).

Put a=A_tV and g=g_{B,t}(V). Both have Gaussian energy at most rL². Since the nuclear norm of uvᵀ is ||u||||v||, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to

    ggᵀ−aaᵀ=(g−a)gᵀ+a(g−a)ᵀ

shows, uniformly over B for t<1,

    ||E[S_h|B,T=t]−A_tA_tᵀ||_*
       ≤H_t h L√r sqrt(m(m+2)).

The singular bound is integrable: ∫₀¹H_tdt=2sqrt(2/π)L. Therefore ||Σ_h−Σ||_*≤Δ_h, hence the same Frobenius bound. The endpoint t=1 has zero probability under the uniform time draw. Both I−Σ and I−Σ_h have eigenvalues in [3/4,1]. The exact Gaussian KL formula and x−1−log x≤C(x−1)² for x∈[3/4,4/3] imply

    TV(N(0,I−Σ_h),N(0,I−Σ))≤C||Σ_h−Σ||F≤CΔ_h.       (4)

Conditioning on the whole Brownian path, adding the same Φ(B₁), and integrating preserve this bound. The exact martingale completion yields Φ(B₁)+N(0,I−Σ)∼N(EΦ(G),I_n); thus (4) controls finite-secant target bias.

This proof relies on averaging the independent bridge endpoints before estimating finite-secant bias. A direct Taylor bound on the raw Φ secants would require an extra supplied derivative modulus and would miss this implication. That initially explored restriction is unnecessary for this construction.

## 4. Realized random moments replace deterministic matrix budgets

S_h is rank one and its op, Frobenius and nuclear norms all equal ||a₁||||a₂||. From (2) at q=2p and Hölder, even though the directions are shared,

    || ||S_h||op ||_Lp(B)≤L²(√r+2√p)²=α_p.          (5)

Conditionally independent factors imply

    || ||M_k||op ||_Lp(B)≤α_p^k,

and M_k remains rank at most one. If M=uvᵀ and Z is Gaussian with covariance C≤I, scalar Gaussian moments and Hölder give

    ||ZᵀMZ||p≤2p||u||||v||=2p||M||op.

Condition on B and every matrix; apply this to Z′ and Z. Triangle inequality, then (5), gives

    ||W_K||Lp(Q)≤2pΣ_{k≤K}α_p^k≤e_p.                (6)

For the exact truncated tilt P_K based on Σ_h, Z|B is Gaussian with covariance

    C_K=(I+Σ_h+⋯+Σ_h^K)^−1≤I.

The auxiliary matrices and Z′ retain their conditional laws: the tilt depends only on B,Z. Hence the same rank-one Gaussian bound proves ||W_K||Lp(P_K)≤e_p. This handles the tilted moments directly and does not assume a realized L² op bound. Products across levels are dependent, but the proof uses only the independence of the individual fresh factors conditional on B.

By construction E[M_k|B]=Σ_h^k; averaging Z′ yields E[W_K|B,Z]=w_K(Σ_h), the source's truncated logarithmic tilt. These are genuine unbiased matrix products, rather than nonlinear nested direction actions.

## 5. Truncation, clipping, FORS and all attempts

Using (3), the scalar eigenvalue calculation for λ∈[0,L²] gives

    C_K(λ)=(1−λ)/(1−λ^(K+1)),
    |log Z_K(B)|≤CΣ_iλ_i^(K+1)≤CrL^(2K+2).

Conditional Gaussian KL and the Brownian marginal normalizer then yield TV(P_K,P_∞(Σ_h))≤CrL^(2K+2). This is the same trace-budget truncation calculation as the exact derivative audit; it does not need the individual random matrices to have bounded op norm.

For clipped mean tilde w=E clip_B(W_K)|B,Z, (6) gives under both Q and P_K

    E|w_K−tilde w|≤E(|W_K|−B)_+≤B(e_p/B)^p.

Let A=E_Qe^w_K. The normalizer bound keeps A away from zero. The inequality |e^u−e^v|≤|u−v|(e^u+e^v), integrated under Q, bounds the unnormalized density discrepancy by A times the P_K error plus e^B times the Q error. Normalizing gives clipping TV≤CB(e_p/B)^p. Add (4) and truncation to prove (T).

FORS uses J~Poisson(2B) and a fresh uniform acceptance draw with probability Π_{j=1}^J(B+clip_B(W_j))/(2B). Conditional acceptance expectation is exp(tilde w−B), in [e^(−2B),1]. Thus expected attempts≤e^(2B). The number J in each reached attempt is independent of previous attempts, so the expected total estimators is at most 2Be^(2B). This remains correct when J=0, and includes all rejected attempts. Multiplying by four Φ values per S and K per estimator gives (Q). Every estimator must use fresh seeds conditional on the same full Brownian path; replacing them by independent newly resampled paths would change the acceptance law.

## 6. Exact penalty and strong controls

For the linear map Φ(x)=Ax with r singular values equal to L, S_h=(AV)(AV)ᵀ exactly, independent of h, and

    E||S_h||F²=L⁴(r²+2r).

For K=1, W=½(Z′ᵀS_hZ′−ZᵀS_hZ), so E W²=E||S_h||F². The standard deviation is ≍rL², compared with ≍√rL² for a deterministic exact covariance action. Thus this single-direction operator cannot simply inherit the exact gadget's rank-optimal moment scale, even on a linear fixture.

Averaging N independent shared-direction operators on this fixture gives

    E||Sbar||F²=L⁴[r+(r²+r)/N].

N≳r recovers the desired second-moment order at N-fold query/vector cost. This does not yet prove the needed product/high-moment bound for nonlinear rotating derivative ranges. Such a proof is an unexplored next step; its cost must remain in the comparison. A known linear A also admits a direct analytic Gaussian baseline and automatic differentiation, so this fixture validates moments rather than demonstrating an algorithmic advantage.

Ordinary Monte Carlo mean estimation is another informed control: Gaussian Poincaré gives E||sample_mean−EΦ||²≤rL²/N, and convolving with a standard Gaussian gives TV≤½E mean-error norm≤Lsqrt(r/N)/2. It reaches δ with N=O(rL²/δ²) values without clipping. Our theorem gives logarithmic-in-accuracy query count only in its admitted small-L regime and with a sufficiently precise value oracle. A claim of benefit must compare these complete costs.

Gaussian Stein outer-product Jacobian estimators F_h(x,V)Vᵀ do not solve the problem automatically: in a linear rank-r model E||AVVᵀ||F²=L²r(m+2), regaining input dimension m. The shared-direction covariance construction avoids this cost by estimating JJᵀ directly.

## Bounded-region oracle transfer and actual source gradient costs

The source's Appendix B does not supply our uniform-accuracy oracle on all of R^m. Lemma B.3 gives value accuracy on prescribed input regions and a gradient-query cost at most C S³ ℒ_* for a composition with S local steps. Proposition B.4 and Lemma B.7 then cap operation counts/input radii and account for tail/stopping contributions. Merely multiplying (Q) by the source's nominal per-value cost would omit this transfer. The following explicit cap argument can bridge the **abstract value oracle** to a bounded-region implementation, conditional on its computability and accuracy on the enlarged region.

Give the caps a separate TV budget δ_cap. Let

    Amax=ceil(e^(2B) log(16/δ_cap)),
    Jmax=ceil([2B+log(16Amax/δ_cap)]/log 2),
    N_g=3Amax Jmax K, N_z=Amax(1+Jmax),
    R_path=sqrt(2m log(64mAmax/δ_cap)),
    R_g=sqrt(m)+sqrt(2log(16max(1,N_g)/δ_cap)),
    R_z=sqrt(n)+sqrt(2log(16max(1,N_z)/δ_cap)).

Stop and return a fixed fallback if an attempt count exceeds Amax, a Poisson count exceeds Jmax, a revealed bridge input exceeds R_path, a fresh input Gaussian exceeds R_g, or an output Gaussian/direction exceeds R_z. Check these caps before querying any out-of-region value. Use prescribed value accuracy τ on the ball

    ||x||≤R_path+(1+h)R_g.                           (Region)

The accepted ideal run and capped run can be coupled until a cap fires. The geometric acceptance bound gives P(attempt overflow)≤exp(−Amax e^(−2B))≤δ_cap/16. For J~Poisson(2B), Markov at parameter log2 gives P(J≥j)≤exp(2B)2^(−j); a union bound over Amax attempts controls count overflow. Gaussian norm concentration controls the N_g input and N_z output Gaussian slots. For the Brownian paths, reflection and a coordinate union bound give

    P(sup_{0≤t≤1}||B_t||>R)≤4m exp(−R²/(2m)).

Union over Amax paths controls all adaptively revealed bridge points without asserting their independence. With the conservative constants above, the total cap probability is at most δ_cap. These bounds concern all reached **proposal** attempts under Q, so rejected attempts are included and no unjustified transfer of Q probabilities to the accepted tilt is needed. The stopped sampler differs by at most δ_cap in TV.

To make the bounded oracle comparison formal, extend the deterministic bounded-region value function by exact Φ outside (Region) in the proof only. The extension satisfies the uniform τ premise. The implemented sampler stops before querying outside the region, so its path agrees with that hypothetical uniform-oracle sampler until a cap fires. This yields the previous theorem plus δ_cap without estimating unbounded polynomial estimator tails. There is no conditioning on successful caps that would silently alter the target law.

Under a **proved** Lemma B.3 value implementation on this enlarged region, each Φ call costs C S³ ℒ_*(Region,τ) gradient queries, with ℒ_* including log(1/τ), region/offset sizes, composition order and dimensions. Thus a conservative expected gradient cost is

    C S³ ℒ_*(Region,τ) e^(2B)(1+8BK),                (Gradient cost)

and the cap supplies a deterministic upper count Amax(1+4JmaxK). A blockwise source Φ may require summing the value costs over its blocks; a four-value secant is not automatically four single-block gradients. Because τ=hγ and h shrinks with m, log m and the requested total accuracy enter ℒ_* even though the covariance moment bound has no ambient dimension. Source hidden-center/surrogate computability, reference radii and all enclosing recursive errors must also be verified for these perturbed inputs. We have not done that full Appendix B/forest assembly.
