T05 NONLINEAR PDE — FROZEN RECORD v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived identities and counterexamples; no regularity theorem or breakthrough.

TARGET

For unforced 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes on R^3,

  ∂_t u + (u·∇)u = −∇p + νΔu,     ∇·u=0,     ν>0,
  ω=∇×u,

seek a scale-critical mechanism generated by the actual evolution that depletes
vortex stretching strongly enough to exclude finite-time singularity for smooth
finite-energy initial data. This record derives (i) the exact evolution of a
pairwise vorticity-direction defect, (ii) an exact affine-compensated
inter-scale representation, and (iii) a pressure-transport identity. The first
does not close; the second removes affine strain exactly but transfers the
uncontrolled quantity into deformation and residual strain; the third is an
identity with an unclosed pressure source. No unconditional regularity claim
follows.

1. VORTICITY MAGNITUDE AND DIRECTION

Write ρ=|ω|, ξ=ω/ρ where ρ>0, A_{ij}=∂_j u_i,
S=(A+A^T)/2, and α=ξ·Sξ. Let D=∂_t+u·∇. Curling Navier–Stokes gives

  Dω = Aω + νΔω.

Since Ω=(A−A^T)/2 acts as Ωv=(ω×v)/2, Ωξ=0. Define

  L_ξ = Δξ + 2∇logρ·∇ξ + |∇ξ|² ξ.

The product rule and |ξ|²=1 give the exact identities

  Dρ = αρ + ν(Δρ − ρ|∇ξ|²),                         (1)
  Dξ = (I−ξξ^T)Sξ + νL_ξ.                           (2)

For (1), ξ·Δ(ρξ)=Δρ−ρ|∇ξ|² because ξ·∂_kξ=0 and
ξ·Δξ=−|∇ξ|². For (2), expand Δ(ρξ), subtract ξDρ, and divide by ρ.
Also ξ·L_ξ=0, as required by the unit-length constraint. Equations (1)–(2)
are invariant under the Navier–Stokes scaling
u_λ(x,t)=λu(λx,λ²t), ω_λ=λ²ω(λx,λ²t): α and every term in (1) scale
like λ²ρ, while every term in (2) scales like λ².

Integrating (1) against ρ gives the standard exact enstrophy budget, now with
its direction contribution exposed:

  (1/2) d/dt ∫ρ² dx
      = ∫αρ² dx − ν∫(|∇ρ|²+ρ²|∇ξ|²) dx
      = ∫αρ² dx − ν∫|∇ω|² dx.                       (3)

The directional diffusion term is nonnegative dissipation. It does not by
itself bound the signed stretching integral.

2. NONLOCAL DIRECTION KERNEL AND ITS LIMIT

For decaying smooth vorticity, Biot–Savart is

  u(x)=(1/4π) p.v.∫ ω(y)×(x−y)/|x−y|³ dy.

Differentiating and symmetrizing the gradient gives, with e=(x−y)/|x−y|,

  S(x)=(3/4π) p.v.∫ M(e,ω(y))/|x−y|³ dy,
  M(e,z)=[e⊗(e×z)+(e×z)⊗e]/2.

Indeed, ∂_j u_i has kernel ε_{ikl}ω_k(δ_{lj}|r|^{-3}−3r_lr_j|r|^{-5})/(4π);
the delta terms cancel in the symmetric part and the remaining two terms are
exactly the displayed M. Consequently, for ω(y)=ρ(y)ξ(y),

  α(x)=(3/4π) p.v.∫ ρ(y) D(e,ξ(y),ξ(x)) |x−y|^{-3}dy,
  D(e,η,ξ)=(e·ξ) det(e,η,ξ),
  |D(e,η,ξ)|≤|η×ξ|=sin(angle(η,ξ)).               (4)

Thus a direction defect is genuinely present in the exact stretching kernel,
but the source is nonlocal and the integral is principal value. Under scaling,
the integral in (4) has the units of α, namely λ². An angular defect alone
does not control the contribution from low-vorticity or remote regions.

The primary conditional comparison is Constantin–Fefferman (1993), which
assumes that for pairs x,y where both vorticity magnitudes exceed a fixed
threshold, the angle obeys |sin φ(x,y,t)|≤|x−y|/r_* for a fixed positive
length r_*, and proves regularity. That is a conditional geometric criterion;
it does not derive this coherence from the Navier–Stokes evolution. The article
also notes viscous mechanisms that produce some direction regularity; this
record does not derive the required pairwise hypothesis or claim to improve
or contradict their theorem.

3. EXACT EVOLUTION OF A PAIRWISE ANGULAR DEFECT

Let X(t),Y(t) be two fluid trajectories with nonzero vorticity along them,
and put c(t)=ξ(X(t),t)·ξ(Y(t),t), E(t)=1−c(t)². Use subscripts x,y for
evaluation at the two points. Directly applying (2) at both trajectories
gives

  ċ = ξ_y·S_xξ_x + ξ_x·S_yξ_y − (α_x+α_y)c
       + ν(L_{ξ,x}·ξ_y + ξ_x·L_{ξ,y}),               (5)
  Ė = −2c ċ.                                         (6)

Every term in (5) has scale λ²; E is dimensionless, so ∫ċ dt is scale
invariant. This is an exact evolution law, including viscosity. It has no
one-sided sign: the two cross-strain terms can have either sign, and the
viscous terms contain both direction curvature and gradients of log ρ.
Substituting (4)'s Biot–Savart tensor for S_x and S_y makes (5) a nonlocal
integral identity, not a closed differential inequality for E. In particular,
the longitudinal terms α_x c and α_y c remain in the equation.

The absence of a sign is already visible algebraically. Set
ξ_x=e_1, ξ_y=c e_1+s e_2 with c,s>0 and c²+s²=1, take
S_x=b(e_1⊗e_2+e_2⊗e_1), and S_y=0. These are trace-free symmetric
strain jets. Then α_x=α_y=0, ċ=bs, and Ė=−2cbs, which has either sign as
b changes sign.

Here is a finite-energy test that rules out a local direction-only estimate
for α, even if the material derivative of ξ is included. Fix a>0 and γ≠0,
and take the constant trace-free matrix

  A = [[−a/2, −γ, 0], [γ, −a/2, 0], [0, 0, a]].       (7)

Then S=diag(−a/2,−a/2,a), and curl(Ax)=2γe_3. To make a smooth compactly
supported divergence-free datum with this exact jet on a ball, let v(x)=Ax,
let b(x)=−(1/3)x×v(x), and choose a smooth cutoff χ equal to 1 on that
ball and compactly supported. The identity ∇×(x×v)=−3v for this linear,
trace-free v gives

  u_0=∇×(χb) ∈ C_c^∞(R³),   ∇·u_0=0,
  u_0=Ax on the ball.

At every point in the ball, ρ=2|γ|, ξ=sgn(γ)e_3, all spatial derivatives of
ρ and ξ vanish, but α=a. Equations (1)–(2) therefore give, at t=0,

  Dρ=aρ>0,      Dξ=0.                                (8)

This holds for the local smooth Navier–Stokes solution from u_0, for every
ν>0: the local Laplacian terms vanish. Varying a leaves the vorticity
direction and all its spatial jets in the ball unchanged while making the
instantaneous stretch arbitrarily large. Thus no pointwise bound for α by
local direction data (or by Dξ at that instant) can work. This does not
refute (4) or the global Constantin–Fefferman criterion: the affine strain
is supported nonlocally by the vorticity outside the affine patch, and the
global direction field there is not constant.

4. AFFINE-COMPENSATED INTER-SCALE REPRESENTATION

The preceding obstruction identifies what a geometric mechanism must see:
uniform affine strain can stretch magnitude while leaving local direction
constant. A different representation removes that affine part exactly.

Suppose on the region under study that u(x,t)=B(t)x+v(x,t), where
tr B=0. Let F solve

  F'(t)=B(t)F(t),   F(0)=I,

so detF(t)=1. Set y=F(t)^{-1}x, w(y,t)=F(t)^{-1}ω(F(t)y,t),
ṽ(y,t)=F(t)^{-1}v(F(t)y,t), and G=F^{-1}F^{-T}. The vorticity equation
and the chain rule give

  ∂_t w + ṽ·∇_y w = (w·∇_y)ṽ + νG^{ij}(t)∂_{y_i y_j}w.    (9)

Proof: at fixed y, (F^{-1})'ω=−F^{-1}Bω and x_t=BFy=Bx. Thus

  ∂_t w = −F^{-1}Bω + F^{-1}[∂_tω+(Bx)·∇_xω].

The equation for ω has the matching Bω term from
(ω·∇)(Bx), so these cancel. The residual advection and stretching transform
as F^{-1}(v·∇_xω)=ṽ·∇_y w and
F^{-1}(ω·∇_x v)=(w·∇_y)ṽ. Finally
Δ_x(Fw)=F G^{ij}∂_{y_i y_j}w. No pressure term is omitted: (9) is obtained
from the curl equation, in which pressure has already canceled.

For a fixed compactly supported material cutoff χ(y), multiplying (9) by
χ²w and integrating gives the complete localized budget

  (1/2)d/dt∫χ²|w|²dy + ν∫χ² G^{ij}(∂_i w)·(∂_j w)dy
    = ∫χ² w·((w·∇)ṽ)dy
      +∫χ|w|² ṽ·∇χ dy
      −2ν∫χ G^{ij}(∂_iχ) w·(∂_j w)dy.              (10)

If χ=χ(y,t), add ∫χ(∂_tχ)|w|² to the right side. The first term is
residual vortex stretching; the next two are the transport and viscous
boundary fluxes. For χ≡1 with sufficient decay, only the residual stretching
and nonnegative anisotropic diffusion remain.

The same cancellation can be stated in frequency space. For a scalar q
transported by Bx, the Fourier equation is

  ∂_t q̂ − (B^Tk)·∇_k q̂=0.

The material wavevector κ=F^Tk is constant along k'=−B^Tk. Therefore the
ellipsoidal multiplier Λ_{ℓ,F} with symbol φ(ℓF^Tk) obeys

  ∂_tΛ_{ℓ,F} = [Λ_{ℓ,F}, Bx·∇]                 (11)

for fixed material scale ℓ. This cancels the affine shell-crossing term in
the filtered transport equation. Applying F^{-1} to vorticity cancels the
matching affine amplitude stretch. A spherical, fixed physical shell can
register apparent transfer under Bx; the co-deforming shell has zero such
transfer. If ℓ=ℓ(t), (11) gains the explicit scale-motion term
φ'(ℓF^Tk)·(ℓ'F^Tk), with relative cost |ℓ'|/ℓ.

This is a compensated inter-scale identity, not a regularity proof. Its
unclosed terms are identifiable:

  (a) F can be highly distorted. Since F'=BF,
      cond(F(t)) ≤ exp(2∫_0^t ||B(s)||ds).
      The residual gradient is ∇_y ṽ=F^{-1}(∇_x u−B)F, so comparison with
      physical strain costs cond(F). The metric G is positive and detG=1;
      its anisotropic Dirichlet form has a coordinate-invariant scalar Sobolev
      constant, but this does not control the F factors in residual vector
      stretching or compare |w| with physical |ω|=|Fw|.

  (b) In an application, B would be the coarse strain
      ∇u_{≤N(t)}(x_0(t),t). Bernstein gives
      ||B|| ≤ C N(t)^{3/2}||∇u(t)||_2, hence
      ∫_0^T||B||dt ≤ C(∫_0^T N(t)^3dt)^{1/2}
                        (∫_0^T||∇u||_2²dt)^{1/2}.
      The energy inequality controls the second factor only. It gives no
      bound on ∫N³ when N must follow a concentration scale.

  (c) The low-frequency field is not affine across a whole concentration
      region. On a patch of radius ℓ, the Taylor remainder in its gradient is
      at most ℓ||∇²u_{≤N}||_∞. At the natural ℓ=N^{-1}, Bernstein makes this
      of the same worst-case order as ||∇u_{≤N}||_∞; taking ℓ≪N^{-1} shrinks
      the patch and increases the cutoff/boundary costs in (10).

  (d) The high-frequency residual stretch in (10) is exactly the remaining
      nonlinear difficulty. No sign or estimate absorbs it for arbitrary
      finite-energy data. Letting ℓ(t) follow a shrinking physical scale also
      introduces |ℓ'|/ℓ; a scale tending to zero has unbounded total log
      variation.

For an exact affine NS/Euler solution u=Bx, take
B'=−skew(B²) and p(x,t)=−(1/2)x·sym(B²)x. Then
B'+B²=sym(B²), so the momentum equation holds and Δu=0. In this model v=0,
(9) makes w constant in material coordinates and (10) has zero production.
In physical coordinates ω=Fw can grow. This confirms that the compensation
captures affine stretch exactly, but also that the compensated norm by itself
is not a physical regularity bound. This affine solution has infinite energy.
In the compactly supported example (7), the affine transform applies only in
the affine ball; the cutoff boundary and the non-affine exterior reappear in
(10).

5. PRESSURE-TRANSPORT ALTERNATIVE, FULL COMMUTATOR

The scalar q=ω·∇p appears to couple vorticity to the nonlocal pressure. Its
material derivative has an exact cancellation. Since

  D(∇p)=∇(Dp)−(∇u)^T∇p,

the two terms involving A cancel in D(ω·∇p), yielding

  D(ω·∇p)=ω·∇(Dp)+νΔω·∇p.                         (12)

This is exact for Navier–Stokes; for Euler the last term vanishes. It is not
a closure because Dp is another nonlocal pressure quantity. The full Poisson
commutator makes the missing terms explicit. With f=A_{ij}A_{ji},
Δp=−f and

  DA_{ij}=−p_{ij}−A_{ik}A_{kj}+νΔA_{ij},
  Df=2A_{ji}DA_{ij},
  [Δ,D]p=(Δu_k)p_k+2A_{ki}p_{ki}.

Therefore

  Δ(Dp)=−Df+[Δ,D]p
        =4A_{ij}p_{ij}+2 tr(A³)−2νA_{ij}ΔA_{ij}
          +(Δu)·∇p.                                (13)

The factor 4 consists of 2A_{ji}p_{ij} from −Df and 2A_{ki}p_{ki}
from the Laplacian commutator; symmetry of the Hessian makes them equal.
The remaining right side contains a pressure-Hessian contraction whose sign
is not supplied by Δp, a cubic velocity-gradient term, viscous commutator,
and Δu·∇p. Poisson inversion does not give a sign. Also q=∇·(pω), so its
whole-space integral is zero when the boundary term vanishes and cannot serve
as a positive coercive quantity without a new localized weighting. No such
weighting was found here.

6. TESTS, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND CLAIM STATUS

Local affine strain (7) is an exact jet of smooth compactly supported
finite-energy data, not an unforced global solution. It falsifies an
instantaneous local estimate of longitudinal stretch by the direction field
or its material derivative. It leaves the global nonlocal geometric
criterion untouched.

A second stress test is the Burgers-type strained vortex, used only as a
model (it has an infinite-energy affine far field). Let
u_r=−ar/2, u_θ=(Γ/(2πr))(1−exp(−ar²/(4ν))), u_z=az. Then
ω=ω_z(r)e_z with ω_z=(Γa/(4πν))exp(−ar²/(4ν)), so ξ=e_z and α=a.
Direct differentiation gives

  u·∇ω_z = (a²r²/(4ν))ω_z,
  νΔω_z = (a²r²/(4ν)−a)ω_z,
  aω_z+νΔω_z = u·∇ω_z.

Thus positive stretching is exactly balanced in the magnitude equation by
radial transport and diffusion while the direction is constant. This is a
steady model maintained by imposed affine flow at infinity, not a finite-energy
counterexample to the regularity target; it tests the sign and diffusion terms
in (1). It satisfies the full steady momentum equation with

  p(r,z)=−a²r²/8 + ∫_0^r u_θ(s)²/s ds − a²z²/2 + constant.

Indeed, the radial and axial components give
p_r=−a²r/4+u_θ²/r and p_z=−a²z; direct differentiation of u_θ gives the
azimuthal balance
u_r(∂_r u_θ+u_θ/r)=ν(∂_{rr}u_θ+r^{-1}∂_r u_θ−r^{-2}u_θ).

Claim ledger:

  • Internally derived: (1)–(6), (9)–(13), including all displayed
    commutators and boundary terms.
  • Internally constructed: compactly supported datum with the local jet
    (7)–(8).
  • Model calculation: Burgers-type balance above; not a finite-energy
    unforced solution.
  • Source-reported: Constantin–Fefferman conditional direction criterion;
  • Unresolved: any unconditional bound on residual stretch, on the
    concentration-scale deformation cost, or on pressure-Hessian source.
  • Not established: new regularity criterion, global smoothness, singularity
    exclusion, novelty, or priority.

The exact remaining inference for the target is an a priori estimate, derived
from the unforced evolution and finite-energy data, that controls the
residual production in (10) (or the signed integral in (3)) uniformly up to
any finite time while also controlling the deformation/scale-selection cost.
No such estimate is proved here.
