Primary-source prior-art screen (retrieved 2026-10-13)

Scope of the claim being screened
  Target interface: chosen pairs of n-bit raw inputs; oracle reveals only one
  strict comparison bit for their hidden lexicographic ranks. The proposed
  output is all n hidden quadratic coordinate ANFs (and, with the complete
  toggle flag, an exact inverse), under a supplied bijective quadratic-map
  promise. This differs from learning a total order over an explicitly listed
  finite set, from learning a known-cue permutation, from comparator learning
  of scalar-valued combinatorial functions, and from full-vector cryptanalysis
  oracles. These differences narrow the comparison; they do not establish
  novelty.

1. Goldman, Rivest, Schapire (1993), “Learning Binary Relations and Total
   Orders,” SIAM Journal on Computing 22(5), 1006–1034,
   https://doi.org/10.1137/0222062 ; primary full text:
   https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/GRS93.pdf

   Reviewed abstract and article §4, especially pp. 1026–1032. It explicitly
   studies learning an unknown total order from pair comparisons and gives
   mistake-bounded prediction algorithms. This is a direct historical
   comparison-learning neighbor. Its objects are an enumerated finite set of
   size N and its guarantee is in terms of N; setting N=2^n makes those bounds
   exponential in the bit length n. It does not output a compact quadratic
   map or exploit an affine triangular promise. The referenced 1990 Goldman
   MIT thesis, “Learning Binary Relations, Total Orders, and Read-once
   Formulas,” was not independently audited here and remains a follow-up. A
   targeted search located the primary thesis PDF at
   https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/student-theses/phd-1990-goldman.pdf .
   The 30.6 MB file has 175 pages but no extractable text in the local PDF
   reader, so its contents are not represented as reviewed evidence here.

2. Dombi, Imreh, Vincze (2007), “Learning lexicographic orders,” European
   Journal of Operational Research 183(2), 748–756,
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2006.10.029 ; author manuscript:
   https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=2353e136a5b4d44a2160a498cbb547d319599397&repid=rep1&type=pdf

   Reviewed the primary author manuscript’s model and abstract (pp. 1–3 and
   9–10). It learns the priority order of known criteria from exchange-value
   vectors in {-1,0,+1}, including an oracle model. This is close in its
   lexicographic priority learning and pairwise feedback. Its criterion
   vectors are supplied; it does not recover hidden quadratic coordinate
   functions of raw binary inputs. The paper also studies sample-distance
   restrictions that are absent from the present exact chosen-query model.

3. Schmitt and Martignon (2006), “On the Complexity of Learning Lexicographic
   Strategies,” Journal of Machine Learning Research 7, 55–83,
   https://jmlr.org/papers/volume7/schmitt06a.html ; primary PDF:
   https://jmlr.org/papers/volume7/schmitt06a/schmitt06a.pdf

   Reviewed §2 and the abstract. A comparison is lexicographic over known
   Boolean cue profiles, and the paper studies finding/learning cue
   permutations from comparison examples, with agnostic error and optimization
   hardness. This directly warns that lexicographic comparison learning is an
   established topic. The hidden cue-generation map is not the unknown object;
   the cues are supplied as profiles, and the objective is approximate cue
   order fitting rather than exact recovery of a promised quadratic bijection.

4. Balcan, Vitercik, White (2016), “Learning Combinatorial Functions from
   Pairwise Comparisons,” COLT 2016, PMLR 49, 310–335,
   https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/balcan16b.html ; primary PDF:
   https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/balcan16b.pdf

   Reviewed the abstract and §§3–5, especially §5. It explicitly gives
   comparator-learning algorithms from pairwise comparisons for broad
   classes of set functions, including functions with limited nonlinear
   interactions and Fourier-sparse functions. This is a substantial nearby
   comparison-only learning result. Its target is a distributional
   comparator approximation for those scalar valuation-function classes; it
   does not recover an exact ordered tuple of hidden Boolean quadratic
   coordinates or an inverse of a bijection. The overlap should be discussed,
   not dismissed because the vocabulary differs.

5. Biryukov, Lambin, Udovenko (2023), “Cryptanalysis of ARX-based White-box
   Implementations,” IACR Transactions on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded
   Systems 2023(3), 97–135, DOI 10.46586/tches.v2023.i3.97-135,
   https://doi.org/10.46586/tches.v2023.i3.97-135 ; primary PDF:
   https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/55609/1/Cryptanalysis_Whitebox_ARX.pdf

   Reviewed §§6–7, especially pp. 110 and 124–127. The attack treats the
   white-box round as a vector-valued black-box oracle and recovers structured
   affine/quadratic encodings using output-vector linear combinations,
   cube-sum tests, black-box interpolation, and known ARX/addition structure.
   It is a strong cryptanalytic neighbor for learning structured quadratic
   encodings from oracle access. Its oracle gives full vectors, not one scalar
   order bit per selected raw-input pair; its target is a restricted
   cryptographic encoding with known structural relations. It therefore does
   not directly prove or refute the exact comparison-only result here, but
   prevents presenting “quadratic encodings from an oracle” as an unoccupied
   area.

6. Bhattacharyya, Kopparty, Schoenebeck, Sudan, Zuckerman (2010), “Optimal
   Testing of Reed-Muller Codes,” FOCS 2010; arXiv:0910.0641,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0641 ; primary full text:
   https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2009/bkssz-full.pdf

   This is a method-source check, not a direct model match. The Reed–Muller
   cube/Gowers test is an established component for degree-d function tests;
   the 7-vertex degree-2 parity identity used by the Sol03 correction is
   standard. It does not supply the comparison-median labels, prefix-fiber
   sampler, or the sequential acquisition construction.

Audit conclusion
  The papers above establish that total-order comparison learning,
  lexicographic priority inference, comparator learning, and structured
  quadratic-encoding cryptanalysis all have meaningful primary literature.
  They differ in important interfaces or targets, but this bounded screen is
  not an exhaustive survey and does not clear novelty or priority for either
  the partial-flag median/RM decoder or complete-flag translation decoder.
  The MIT thesis and exact work on comparator learning for unknown quadratic
  triangular Boolean coordinate maps remain explicit unresolved searches.
  The thesis PDF was located, but its scanned/non-extractable text prevented
  an audit in this pass.
