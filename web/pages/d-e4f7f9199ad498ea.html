# Frozen second discriminator: rank-sensitive potential

This protocol is fixed before running the additional test. Family 102's latent
gadget compares a nonlinear failure probability contracting by 1-theta at each
level with linear-character rank loss. The generic rank-loss law is at most one
rank, with probability at most a*r*theta. The harmonic potential cancels r.

Test exactly the pure-death control chain with states 0,...,R, one-rank loss
probability r/256, for R=4,8,16 and horizon floor(128*H_R). This chain has no
nongeneric states and a=1. Compare the harmonic survival lower bound
1-T/(256*H_R) with the raw-rank lower bound max(0,1-T/256) and the exact
finite-chain survival probability. Both bounds receive the same full transition
law; the exact dynamic-programming calculation is the strong numerical control.
No simulated independence or solver status is used.

Also check all finite-field quadratic-block identities in the source for F2 and
F4 by exact enumeration. Count actual nonlinear differences for each update
subspace, then compare with 1-1/(q^2+q+1). These finite checks cannot prove
genericity at arbitrarily high rank, the concatenation theorem, or Unique Games.

The falsifiable methodological claim is narrow: a harmonic potential supplies a
log(R) horizon under the stated conditional hazard assumptions, whereas the
raw-rank bound gives only constant horizon in units of theta^-1. Neither bound
is expected to outperform the exact same-information finite-chain calculation.
