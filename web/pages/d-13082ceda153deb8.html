# Conditional sign audit of the mScarlet–flavin photocycle

## Interface reconstructed from the primary source

The supplied `MFE_kinetic_model.m` in Xiang et al. (JACS 2025) evolves
`(G,T,R,F,H,Q)`: fluorescent mScarlet ground state, mScarlet triplet, dark
mScarlet radical, FMN, FMNH2, and FMNH radical. With

\[
a=\frac{k_{isc}l_{is}}{k_{sr}+k_{isc}},\qquad
u=\beta k_{red}HT,\quad v=k_{ox}FR,\quad w=k_{dis}Q^2,
\]

the six source-code equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot G&=-aG+[k_{tr}+(1-\beta)k_{red}H]T+v,\\
\dot T&=aG-(k_{tr}+k_{red}H)T,\\
\dot R&=u-v,\\
\dot F&=-v+w,\quad \dot H=-u+w,\\
\dot Q&=u-2w+v.
\end{aligned}
\]

These equations have three linear invariants, found directly by summing the
source reaction vectors:

\[
M=G+T+R,\qquad L=F+H+Q,\qquad I=G+T+2F+Q.
\]

For the Nature supplement's simulation concentrations `G0=50`, `F0=20`,
`H0=330` μM and all other initial species zero, these are `M=50`, `L=350`,
and `I=90` μM. The six-state ODE's own source comments call `G` mSc3 ground
state. Interpreting fluorescence as proportional to `G` is therefore the
model-observable mapping being tested; the current Nature supplement does
not state an independently measured calibration from `G` to camera counts.

At any positive steady state, `u=v=w`, so

\[
aG=(k_{tr}+k_{red}H)T,\qquad
\beta k_{red}HT=k_{ox}FR=k_{dis}Q^2.
\]

Subtracting `M` from `I` yields `R=2F+Q-40`, and `L` gives `H=350-F-Q`.
Writing `c=k_dis/k_ox`, the remaining invariant and radical balance give
`F(2F+Q-40)=cQ²`; hence the positive branch is

\[
F(Q)=\frac{40-Q+\sqrt{(Q-40)^2+8cQ^2}}{4},\quad
R(Q)=2F(Q)+Q-40,\quad H(Q)=350-F(Q)-Q.
\]

Set `C=k_tr+k_red H`, `A_m=M-R`. Then

\[
G(Q)=A_m\frac C{a+C},\quad T(Q)=A_m\frac a{a+C},\quad
\beta(Q)=\frac{k_{dis}Q^2(a+C)}{a k_{red}H A_m}.
\]

This is a one-dimensional exact reduction of the stationary equations, not a
fit or a numerical integration. Its derivatives are

\[
G'=-R'\frac C{a+C}+A_m\frac{a k_{red}H'}{(a+C)^2},
\]

and

\[
\frac{\beta'}\beta=\frac2Q+
\frac{k_{red}H'}{a+C}-\frac{H'}H+\frac{R'}{M-R}.
\]

The whole parameter excursion needed below can be certified on
`Q∈[.0357,.036] μM`. Write `D=sqrt((Q−40)^2+8cQ²)` and
`N=Q−40+8cQ`. Direct expansion gives
`N²−D²=8cQ((8c+1)Q−80)>0` on this interval, so
`F'=(-1+N/D)/4>0`. Therefore `R'=2F'+1>0` and `H'=-F'-1<0`.
Endpoint evaluation of the explicit increasing `F` gives `F<42`, hence
`H>300`; endpoint evaluation of increasing `R` gives `R<43`, so
`A_m=50−R>7`. Also `F'<1000` follows from `D≥sqrt(8c)Q` and
`N<(8c+1)Q`. The only negative term in `β'/β` then has magnitude less than
`50.5406·1001/(224+50.5406·300)<3.3`, while `2/Q>55.5` and the last two
terms are positive. Thus `β'>0` throughout the interval. The displayed
formula gives `G'<0` there as both terms are negative, so the stationary
response is strictly decreasing in β across the full fitted change.

At β=.68, the evaluated derivatives are
`F'=780.7554`, `R'=1562.5109`, `H'=-781.7554`,
`G'=-1562.3915 μM/μM`, and `β'=174.8725 /μM`. Thus `dG/dβ=-8.93446 μM`
per unit β there.

There is also an immediate-time check that does not depend on reaching the
new steady state. Hold any pre-step concentrations with `H,T>0` fixed and
step β upward. In the source equation for `G`, β appears in the
`(1−β)k_red H T` contribution, so

\[
\Delta\dot G(0^+)=-\Delta\beta\,k_{red}HT.
\]

Thus the sign is negative for every such pre-step state. At the baseline root,
`k_red H T=9.38002 μM/s`; the Nature fit's `.3 mT` change `Δβ=.0351`
therefore changes the initial G derivative by `−.32924 μM/s`. The source
model predicts an immediate decrease at RF-on as well as the lower new steady
state. `steady_sign.py` records this value.

The fitted RF law in Nature S8, if directly substituted as the same Xiang β,
sets `β=.68+.117 B1` with `B1` in mT. At `.3 mT`, β becomes `.7151`; the
stationary branch gives `G=7.4666565 μM`, versus `7.7679339 μM` at β=.68,
a `-0.3012774 μM` or `-3.8785%` change. The published experiment reports
positive RF-on fluorescence (up to nearly 10% in purified RFP samples).
Therefore the literal same-parameter model transfer has the wrong sign.

The conclusion is conditional at the interface: Nature S8 says it varies the
magnetic-field-dependent separation fraction and obtains other rate constants
from Xiang et al.; Xiang's source code labels β as the SCRP separation fraction
and uses it in the separated-radical production term. That is strong evidence
for direct semantic identity, but the Nature simulation is described as
semi-quantitative and the radical identities/spin distributions are admitted
unknown. An unreported complement convention or different fluorescence
observation map could remove the mismatch. Neither is documented in the
current source packet. Do not call this a refutation of the observed RYDMR.

## Floquet diagnostic and its limit

`rp_floquet.py` extends the JACS two-electron source model's static independent
Gaussian local-field ensemble with a transverse monochromatic Zeeman drive.
For each draw it solves the finite Floquet-Liouville integrated first-event
resolvent, with triplet-born initial density `P_T/3`, singlet sink
`k_rec P_S`, and spin-blind separation sink `k_sep I`; it integrates the
separation and recombination probabilities and checks they sum to one. RF
birth phase is averaged. The calculation is exact for its finite sideband
cutoff and its stated source-adjacent model, not for the unknown 2026 radical
pair.

The source's σ=2 mT width did not match the reported ≈2.7 mT response line,
so the diagnostic uses σ=1.3 mT and η=`k_rec/k_sep=20.3228`, the latter set to
match the no-RF separation yield near .68 at B0=15.9 mT. These are explicit
calibrations, not measured radical parameters. With 1,200 fixed-seed Monte
Carlo draws and K=3 sidebands, the on-resonance separation-yield changes at
447 MHz for B1=.07,.1,.2,.3 mT were approximately
`-.0173,-.0320,-.0872,-.1347`; at 400 MHz they were
`-.0068,-.0129,-.0374,-.0605`. The largest per-sample yield-normalization
residual in the scan was `1.72e-14`. A separate paired B0 scan gives a grid
interpolated FWHM of about `3.07 mT` centered at `15.85 mT` for B1=.07 mT;
this is the empirical reason for selecting σ≈1.3 mT and is a calibration,
not validation. Its data are in `floquet_line_scan.json`. The signs oppose the fitted
`+.00819,+.0117,+.0234,+.0351`
RF parameter changes. This is a negative transfer result: no predictive
capability was established.

Uniform drive-birth phase does imply an analytic even response in B1 for a
finite sink-bounded model: changing `(B1, phase)` to `(-B1, phase+π)` leaves
the phase-averaged yield invariant, so its low-amplitude expansion begins at
order B1². This is a standard symmetry observation, not claimed as new
chemistry, and does not challenge a linear fit over the measured finite B1
range.
