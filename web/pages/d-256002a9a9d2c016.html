SOURCE020 SECTION 3 — AFFINE COUNTING TRANSFER RECONSTRUCTION
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally reconstructed theorem module, conditional on its uniform patch and Hilbert hypotheses. This is not validation of Proposition 1.4.

Theorem interface
-----------------
Fix ambient dimension N, an upper dimension r≤N, degree bound e_0, positive coordinate weights ω_1,...,ω_N, and a box constant C. For each X≥2 let S⊂Z^N satisfy |z_l|≤CX^{ω_l} and lie on an algebraic set of degree at most e_0 and dimension at most r. For every fixed degree bound e and every irreducible Q-defined subvariety A of that set, degree≤e, dimension h>0, meeting S, assume uniformly in X,A,S either

  (small-count) |S∩A| ≪_{e,α} X^α for every fixed α>0,

or

  (Hilbert-rich) H_A(T) ≥ (δ_h^h/h!) T^h − C_e T^(h−1), T≥1,

for fixed δ_1,...,δ_r>0 and uniform C_e. Let E_h=max_{h≤s≤r}δ_s. The source concludes |S|≪_ε X^(E_1+...+E_r+ε) for every ε>0. I independently checked the mechanism that converts these moving-subvariety hypotheses into the bound.

Patch lemma and height control (Lemma 3.2)
------------------------------------------
For an algebraic set Z' of bounded degree, first decompose into boundedly many irreducible components. If a component A is not defined over Q, choose σ∈Aut(C/Q) with σ(A)≠A. Every rational point of A is fixed by σ, hence lies in A∩σ(A), a proper bounded-degree intersection. Recurse on its irreducible components. Dimension drops at every such step, and Bézout bounds the number and degrees of all branches. If every automorphism fixes A, the unique reduced-row-echelon basis of each bounded-degree equation space is fixed, so its coefficients are rational; thus A is Q-defined.

For a Q-defined irreducible component A of dimension h and degree e', its ideal equations of ordinary degree at most e' have generic Jacobian rank N−h: choose h algebraically independent coordinate functions, then for each remaining coordinate use the equation of the image hypersurface in those h coordinates plus that coordinate. Characteristic zero makes its derivative in the extra coordinate nonzero; together these equations give a diagonal generic minor. Evaluate all degree-≤e' monomials on S∩A. There are a fixed number of columns and entries of size X^{O(1)}. Select independent rows and solve for a rational nullspace basis by bounded-size cofactors; after clearing determinants, this gives integer spanning polynomials of height X^{O(1)}. If this nullspace strictly exceeds the degree-≤e' ideal, one spanning polynomial does not vanish on A and cuts it properly; recurse. If equal, choose N−h bounded-height equations with a nonzero Jacobian minor. These define a smooth patch wherever that minor is nonzero; recurse on its zero locus for uncovered points. A rational zero-dimensional point z is handled by equations x_l−z_l and minor 1. All recursive degrees, patch counts, coefficient-height exponents, and recursion depths are bounded in terms of fixed input data. This gives boundedly many Q-patches covering S∩Z' with minors nonzero at covered points and height at most X^{C'}.

I found no coefficient-height issue: the evaluation matrix may have many rows, but its rank is bounded by the fixed number of monomial columns, so all cofactors used have bounded size. The non-Q descent also preserves rational points because σ fixes each point of Z^N.

Weighted determinant cut (Lemma 3.3)
------------------------------------
Take one h-dimensional patch (A,G,Δ), a point z∈S∩A with Δ(z)≠0, and distinct primes q with q∤Δ(z). If Q is their product and Q≥X^{δ_h+ν}, the points of S∩A congruent to z modulo all q lie on a bounded-degree polynomial not identically zero on A.

Proof. The Hilbert lower bound lets one select L independent coordinate monomials on A, ordered by weighted degree, with j-th weight at most δ_h(h!j)^(1/h)+O(1). Their total weight is at most δ_h s_h(L)+O(L), where s_h(L) is the minimum total degree of L distinct monomials in h variables and

  s_h(L)= [h/(h+1)](h!)^(1/h) L^(1+1/h)+O_h(L).

For a prime q∤Δ(z), use the h free coordinates outside the chosen Jacobian minor. The implicit-function recursion solves the other coordinates as convergent power series over Z_q in free-coordinate differences u∈qZ_q^h. Every monomial evaluation on the entire residue class therefore has such an expansion. For any L distinct points in the class, expand the L×L evaluation determinant in these series. Repeated expansion monomials give determinant zero; otherwise the L selected multi-indices are distinct and their total degree is at least s_h(L), so the determinant has q-adic valuation ≥s_h(L). Multiplying over distinct q shows divisibility by Q^{s_h(L)}.

On the other hand the integer determinant has absolute value at most L! C_L X^{δ_h s_h(L)+C_1L}. Choose L so ν s_h(L)>C_1L (possible since s_h(L) grows as L^(1+1/h)); after fixing L, take X large enough to absorb the fixed determinant factor L!. The upper bound is then strictly below Q^{s_h(L)}≤|det| if the determinant were nonzero. Hence every L-point determinant vanishes. The evaluation rank on the whole finite set is <L, giving a nontrivial linear combination of the selected independent monomials that vanishes on the residue class but not identically on A. Its weighted degree, hence ordinary degree (all ω_l have a fixed positive minimum), is bounded independently of X. Further congruence restrictions only shrink the set.

The adaptive-prime coupling (Lemma 3.4) is independently proved in `outputs/research/geometry_number/source020_leaf_cat0/REPORT.txt`. It uses the deterministic whole-kernel path convention, a mixed-radix record R∈[0,B_0), averaging over T_0 consecutive subset sizes, and the fact that a bounded-height product of nonzero minors has O(1/ζ) prime divisors ≥X^ζ. The result is expected bad-prime fraction ≤B_0/T_0+o(1), uniformly in the point and other blocks.

Counting tree and exponent
--------------------------
Set h_i=r+1−i. Put E_h=max_{s≥h}δ_s, E_{r+1}=0, and a_i=E_{h_i}−E_{h_i+1}≥0. Choose a small γ>0. At step i use a disjoint pool of primes in [X^ζ,C_rX^ζ] and a block size t_i in a fixed interval beginning at ceil((a_i+γ)/ζ), with T_0 fixed and ζ(T_0+1)≤γ. Thus a_i+γ≤ζt_i≤a_i+2γ. Prime pools exist by standard prime-counting bounds for fixed ζ,C_r and have cardinality tending to infinity. From Lemma 3.4, Markov and a union bound, choose the fixed blocks so at least half of S are regular: in each block at most a fraction ξ of its primes divides any full-path minor, where ξ(E_1+rγ)<γ/2.

At a parent patch of dimension h≤h_i, a prime q not dividing its minor yields O(q^h) residue classes meeting the patch with nonzero minor. Fix the h free coordinates; the other N−h equations have invertible Jacobian, so their solutions are isolated and Bézout bounds them even if other components of the reduction have positive dimension. At a bad prime use q^N. For a regular point, the number of simultaneous classes introduced by block i is at most

  O(X^((a_i+2γ)(h_i+Nξ))).

If h=h_i, the product of good primes in the first i blocks is at least

  X^((1−ξ)ζ Σ_{j≤i}t_j) ≥ X^(E_{h_i}+iγ−ξ(E_1+rγ)) ≥ X^(δ_{h_i}+γ/2).

Apply the weighted determinant cut to the current parent patch. Its cutting polynomial lies in the current bounded-degree kernel because it vanishes on the entire residue class, not merely its regular points; the child patch dimension drops by one. If h<h_i, dimension already satisfies the next-stage bound. Thus dimension decreases at every stage; after r stages all surviving leaves are zero-dimensional and have O(1) points each. If a positive-dimensional node satisfies the small-count alternative, stop and charge O(X^α) points there. Its prefix branch exponent is no larger than the full branch exponent because all a_i and error terms are nonnegative.

Summing branching exponents gives

  Σ_i h_i a_i + 2γΣ_i h_i + NξΣ_i(a_i+2γ)
  = Σ_{h=1}^r E_h + 2γΣ_i h_i + Nξ(E_1+2rγ).

First choose γ small, then choose ξ small subject to the product condition, then α small. The last two terms and α can be made <ε. At least half of S was regular, so multiplying the tree bound by two proves the stated theorem.

Audit conclusion and limits
---------------------------
I find the architecture of Theorem 3.1 internally consistent, including its uniform moving-variety step: the determinant cut applies to every bounded-degree Q-subvariety created by subsequent cuts because the source assumes its Hilbert/small-count alternative uniformly for all such descendants, and Lemma 3.2 gives the required bounded-height patch for each moving set. The tree does not require the whole reduction mod q to have good reduction; only the chosen patch minor is used.

This reconstructs an abstract counting theorem, not its application. Source020's Proposition 1.4 still requires verifying that the paired arithmetic boxes satisfy its box hypotheses and that every surface/curve descendant satisfies the appropriate Hilbert-rich or small-count alternative with the exact parameter thresholds in Sections 4–11. The result also relies on the source's fixed-dimension, fixed-degree, bounded-height patch construction; it makes no uniform claim over varying degrees, fields, or f.
