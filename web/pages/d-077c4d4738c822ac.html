CYCLE05 PRIMARY-LITERATURE TRIAGE: ROBUST UNILOCAL BROADCASTING
Date: 2026-10-09
Scope: bounded primary-source pass; no Cycle05 Sol proof material read.

INTERFACE UNDER REVIEW

For a bipartite state rho_AB with I(A:B)_rho <= C, let Gamma_{B->B1B2} be one local CPTP channel and omega_AB1B2=(id_A tensor Gamma)(rho_AB). The premise is Delta(omega_ABj,rho_AB)<=b for j=1,2, where Delta(x,y)=||x-y||_1/2 is the joint-state trace distance. The target is one EB channel Lambda_{B->B}, acting only on B, with Delta((id_A tensor Lambda)(rho_AB),rho_AB)<=g(C,b), where g(C,b)->0 as b->0 uniformly over local dimensions.

The source results below do not state this implication. The closest result gives a channel-level EB approximation when a channel has many outputs supported on the fully symmetric subspace, with an explicit local-dimension penalty. The exact one-sided no-local-broadcasting theorem identifies the zero-error boundary. The recoverability and discord theorems quantify nearby information quantities, but do not turn the two-output, one-state premise into an EB reconstruction estimate.

STRONGEST DIRECT QUANTITATIVE ANTECEDENT

Piani, “Hierarchy of efficiently computable and faithful lower bounds to quantum discord,” arXiv:1501.06855v1 (2015), PRL 117, 080401 (2016), is the closest channel-level EB approximation theorem in this pass. A k-Bose-symmetric-extendible channel is specifically a channel Lambda_B^{Sym+(k)} obtained by tracing all but one output of a channel B -> B_+^k whose output lies in the fully symmetric subspace. Equation (4), p. 3, states that there is an EB channel Lambda_B^EB such that

  sup_{rho_AB} Delta((id_A tensor Lambda_B^{Sym+(k)})(rho_AB),
                     (id_A tensor Lambda_B^EB)(rho_AB)) <= d_B/k.

Here Delta is half trace distance and d_B=dim(H_B); the supremum over reference-system inputs makes this a diamond-norm channel guarantee. Theorem 1, p. 7, gives the corresponding state-fidelity consequence

  sup_{Lambda^EB} F(rho_AB,(id_A tensor Lambda^EB)(rho_AB))
    >= sup_{Lambda^{Sym+(k)}} F(rho_AB,(id_A tensor Lambda^{Sym+(k)})(rho_AB))
       - sqrt(2 d_B/k).

F is root fidelity. This is a genuine quantitative theorem connecting symmetric local redistribution to EB reconstruction. Its hypotheses and cost do not match the candidate: k=2 gives no dimension-free channel estimate; the candidate only supplies two approximate marginals on one input state; and a swap-invariant two-output state need not be supported on the fully symmetric subspace. Symmetrizing Gamma can make its output swap invariant without placing its output in that subspace. Also, an arbitrary 2-extendible state is merely a state extension; it need not be generated from rho_AB by a channel acting on B. The candidate's channel-generation premise is stronger and should be retained explicitly.

The exact boundary is known. Piani’s “Local Broadcasting of Quantum Correlations,” arXiv:1608.02650v1 (2016), Theorem 5, p. 5, says that a state rho_AB is exactly locally broadcastable by a channel B -> B1B2, with A untouched and both ABj marginals equal rho_AB, iff it is quantum-classical on B:

  rho_AB = sum_j p_j rho_A^j tensor |b_j><b_j|_B

for an orthonormal family {|b_j>}. Theorem 7, p. 7, is the corresponding mutual-information equality version: both I(A:Bj) after the channel equal I(A:B)_rho iff rho_AB is classical on B. This is qualitative at zero error; it provides no stability modulus in joint trace distance.

Piani’s same note, Eqs. (31)–(35), gives the closest state-specific fidelity relation. With D(A|B)_rho the one-sided discord and F_B^{EB}(rho)=max_{Lambda^EB} F(rho,(id_A tensor Lambda^EB)(rho)), Eq. (34) is

  D(A|B)_rho >= -2 log_2 F_B^{EB}(rho).

For F^{max}_{B,1->2}(rho), the maximum root fidelity between rho_AB and either ABj marginal of a symmetric-output channel B -> B1B2 applied to rho_AB, the note shows F^{max}_{B,1->2} >= F_B^{EB} and hence D(A|B)_rho >= -2 log_2 F^{max}_{B,1->2}(rho). This is the wrong direction for the candidate’s desired conclusion: a near-perfect broadcast makes this lower bound small, but does not upper-bound discord or produce a nearby EB output. It is a fixed-state optimization, not a uniform channel approximation.

RECOVERABILITY, CONTINUITY, AND MANY-FRAGMENT RESULTS

Fawzi–Renner, “Quantum conditional mutual information and approximate Markov chains,” arXiv:1410.0664v3 (2015), Theorem 5.1, p. 15, applies to separable Hilbert spaces. For any rho_ABC there is a CPTP recovery map R_{B->BC} such that

  F(rho_ABC,(id_A tensor R)(rho_AB)) >= 2^{-I(A:C|B)_rho/2}.

This gives recovery from small conditional mutual information, not an EB map. Applied to omega_AB1B2 from the candidate, data processing gives I(A:B1B2)_omega <= I(A:B)_rho and

  I(A:B2|B1)_omega = I(A:B1B2)_omega - I(A:B1)_omega.

If one had a dimension-free C-only continuity modulus controlling I(A:B1)_omega from Delta(omega_AB1,rho_AB)<=b, this would make the conditional mutual information small and invoke Fawzi–Renner. The cited recoverability theorem then yields a recovery channel, not an EB reconstruction or a quantum-classical state. The stability step from approximate channel-generated broadcasting to EB reconstruction remains separate.

The continuity literature does not supply that C-only modulus in the candidate’s generality. Shirokov, “Continuity of characteristics of composite quantum systems,” arXiv:2201.11477v4 (2022), Proposition 10, p. 47, gives a QCMI continuity bound with the factor 2 ln(min{dim A,dim B}); Proposition 23, p. 62, gives, when d_B is finite,

  |D_B(rho)-D_B(sigma)| <= epsilon ln(d_B)+2 g(epsilon),

for epsilon=Delta(rho,sigma); with rho_B=sigma_B the last term improves to g(epsilon). Proposition 26B, p. 67, gives sequential continuity of classical correlation and discord when rho_n -> rho_0 and I(A:B)_{rho_n}->I(A:B)_{rho_0}<infinity. These are dimension-dependent bounds or local sequential criteria requiring convergence of the information values, not a uniform modulus from I<=C alone. The paper explicitly notes that the discord and classical-correlation functions need not be continuous when both local dimensions are infinite (p. 66). Lami–Shirokov, “Continuity of the relative entropy of resource,” arXiv:2308.00696v1 (2023), Proposition 2 and its discussion apply total-correlation convergence to continuity of relative-entropy entanglement. They do not state a local-broadcasting or EB reconstruction theorem.

Brandao–Piani–Horodecki, “Generic emergence of classical features in quantum Darwinism,” arXiv:1310.8640v2 (2015), Theorem 1, pp. 5–6, is a strong many-fragment result. For any CPTP channel Lambda:A -> B1...Bn and any delta>0, at least (1-delta)n effective channels Lambda_j:A -> Bj are within diamond distance

  (27 ln(2) (d_A)^6 log(d_A)/(n delta^3))^(1/3)

of measure-and-prepare channels E_j(X)=sum_k Tr(M_k X) sigma_{j,k}, with one common POVM {M_k}. The output preparations may vary with j. The result concerns many fragments, is channel-level and therefore controls all inputs, but carries d_A dependence (the input system’s dimension) and requires n to grow. In the candidate’s variable assignment the broadcast input is B, so the factor is d_B. It does not turn a two-output, fixed-state marginal condition plus I(A:B)<=C into a dimension-free trace-distance estimate. The theorem concerns diamond norm, stronger than state-specific trace distance; no LOCC norm is substituted in this comparison.

Xie–Fang–Wang–Duan, “Approximate broadcasting of quantum correlations,” arXiv:1705.06071v2 (2017), Theorem 5, p. 4, gives an SDP for the optimal n-output unilocal broadcasting fidelity of one fixed bipartite state, with symmetric output and an average over output marginals (equivalently any one marginal after symmetrization). Their Proposition 1, p. 3, states the one-sided n->infinity average mutual-information-loss characterization of one-sided discord; Theorem 2, p. 3, establishes the bilocal two-sided counterpart. The proof uses dimension-dependent continuity estimates, including Eq. (4). Their ensemble problem, Eqs. (26)–(29), optimizes sum_i p_i F(rho_i,output_i) for a supplied fixed prior eta. It is average-prior performance, not max_i or a guarantee uniform over all priors. These are optimization characterizations and asymptotic average-loss identities, not the candidate’s finite two-output worst-marginal trace-to-EB implication.

MISSING MECHANISM / STATUS

The decisive source-level gap is a dimension-free stability theorem for the exact one-sided no-local-broadcasting characterization, in the channel-generated, state-specific joint trace metric: from I(A:B)_rho<=C and one B-local two-output CPTP channel whose two ABj marginals are within b of rho_AB, produce one B-local EB channel whose AB output is within g(C,b) of rho_AB. Existing direct approximate-broadcast results give fidelity optimization, a dimension-dependent many-output channel de Finetti bound, or a recovery map; none of the checked statements provides this implication. The bounded-MI hypothesis is not a local-dimension bound, so factors in log d_A, log d_B, or d_B/k cannot be replaced by C without an additional theorem. This pass found no exact counterexample to the candidate, but absence from this bounded search is not evidence of novelty or priority. Candidate validity remains a mathematical-proof question outside this literature audit.
