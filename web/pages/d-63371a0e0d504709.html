## Exact Y collision criterion

For a target σ and Hermitian generator H, the score is defined by −i[H,σ]={σ,L_H}/2. In its eigenbasis put D=diag√p and K_H=D(H+iL_H/2)D⁻¹. Its entries are (K_H)ᵢⱼ=CᵢⱼHᵢⱼ, with Cᵢⱼ=2√pᵢpⱼ/(pᵢ+pⱼ). To verify the fiber implication independently, set B=H−iL_H/2 and E=H+iL_H/2. The target equation is Bσ=σE, hence D⁻¹BD=DED⁻¹=K_H. The candidate has the same target score exactly when Bρ=ρE, equivalent to K_HQ=QK_H. Faithfulness makes the SLD solution unique.

For Yᵢ define Jᵢ=iYᵢ; this is a real skew-symmetric physical tensor matrix, with one-qubit J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]. Since U(s) is real, Bᵢ(s)=U(s)ᵀJᵢU(s) is real skew-symmetric and K_Yᵢ=−i(C(s)⊙Bᵢ(s)) is imaginary skew-symmetric Hermitian. Commutation with Q is equivalent to commutation with P_w, which is equivalent to K_Yᵢw=λw. The scalar must be zero: w†K_Yᵢw=0 by skew symmetry. Therefore the exact necessary-and-sufficient collision criterion for this pair is

    (C(s)⊙Bᵢ(s))·1 = 0.

No multiplication identity of ordinary Pauli matrices can substitute for this criterion: the target-dependent Schur dressing is not an algebra homomorphism.

## Explicit remainder bound: the prescribed s_* is small enough

Write gᵢ(s)=(C(s)⊙Bᵢ(s))1 and use zero-based coordinate 1. The diagonal of Bᵢ(s) vanishes. The two neighboring terms have equal weight 2s/(1+s²), so

    gᵢ(s)₁ = [2s/(1+s²)] [Bᵢ(s)₁₀+Bᵢ(s)₁₂]
                + Σⱼ≥₃ [2sʲ⁻¹/(1+s²ʲ⁻²)] Bᵢ(s)₁ⱼ.

The contraction argument in the supplied audit gives ||y(s)||₂≤2KAs for 0<s≤s_*. This follows directly from ||G_s(0)||≤KAs and contraction constant at most 1/2. Independently, Ω(y) is real skew with ||Ω(y)||₂≤||Ω(y)||F=√2||y||₂. Real orthogonal conjugation and integration of its derivative give

    ||Bᵢ(s)−Bᵢ⁰||₂ ≤2||Ω(y)||₂ ≤3||y||₂.

Thus the error in B₁₀+B₁₂ relative to κᵢ is ≤6||y||≤12KAs. Every |Bᵢ(s)₁ⱼ|≤1 because Jᵢ has operator norm one. For s≤1/2 the remaining geometric tail is ≤2s²/(1−s)≤4s². Consequently

    |gᵢ(s)₁ − [2s/(1+s²)]κᵢ| ≤(24KA+4)s² ≤28KAs².

Since 2/(1+s²)≥1, for 0<s≤s_*,

    |gᵢ(s)₁| ≥s[|κᵢ|−28KAs]
              ≥s[|κᵢ|−7/(4KL)]
              ≥s[|κᵢ|−7/(4·756·12288)] > s/256 >0.

Here K≥m=756, and the last bound is an exact rational comparison. Therefore the collision criterion fails for each Yᵢ at the *same explicit* s_*, and throughout this positive branch interval. This is stronger than unspecified leading-order nonvanishing.

## Useful scope, costs and unresolved claim

Yᵢ is a physical single-qubit direction implementable by a local rotation. Any one-qubit Hermitian generator aI+bXᵢ+cYᵢ+dZᵢ with c≠0 also distinguishes this pair: dressing is linear and Q already commutes with the dressed I,Xᵢ,Zᵢ, so the commutator is c[Q,K_Yᵢ]≠0. A redundant added direction in span{I,Xᵢ,Zᵢ} does not help. Thus “any added local score” needs this nonzero-Y qualification.

This is a repair of the displayed rank-one fiber member, not a complete identifiability certificate. Other positive commutant perturbations might survive the expanded frame; neither ordinary XYZ irreducibility nor XY=iZ rules them out after dressing. Score acquisition still requires the paper's score interface; a simple generator does not make its exact full score matrix cheap to learn. In fact ordinary single-site Y measurements do not distinguish these real states: Tr(ρYᵢ)=Tr(σYᵢ)=0, so their ±1 outcome probabilities are both 1/2. The result concerns the unitary-orbit SLD matrix, not that ordinary Y measurement. The witness's extremely small s_* gives no practical discrimination or sample-cost bound.

For tensor products of the displayed six-qubit states, a Y score on one block distinguishes that tensor-product pair, by the same faithful SLD tensor identity used in the original audit. It does not repair arbitrary multi-block ambiguities using one measurement direction.
