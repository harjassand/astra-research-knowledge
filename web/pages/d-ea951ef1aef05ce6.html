## Primary literature consulted

1. Stefan Kiefer and A. Prasad Sistla, “Distinguishing Hidden Markov Chains,” LICS 2016; arXiv:1507.02314. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02314). Read abstract and paper text through the arXiv HTML result. It studies two given finite-state hidden Markov chains and observation-string distributions, establishes polynomial-time distinguishability decision, and gives two-sided/one-sided likelihood monitors with exponential error decay in the known-model setting. This is the closest comparator for the exact transcript-equivalence lemma. It is not biological evidence or a result about continuous-time intervention safety.
2. Bart Vanluyten, Jan C. Willems, and Bart De Moor, “Equivalence of state representations for hidden Markov models,” Systems & Control Letters 57(5), 2008, 410–419. [Primary article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167691107001429). The abstract/interface distinguishes Mealy/Moore and quasi/positive HMMs, treating equivalent state representations and realization. It supports the prior-art boundary that hidden parameter vectors can differ while output laws agree.
3. Mojmir Mutny, Tadeusz Janik, and Andreas Krause, “Active Exploration via Experiment Design in Markov Chains,” AISTATS 2023, PMLR 206, 7349–7374. [Primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/mutny23a.html). Its input is a Markov chain with policy-controlled experiments/measurements; it develops sequential policy selection for optimal measurement allocation. This is an active-design baseline, but it does not remove exact IO equivalence in a hidden model.
4. N. Suhas Jagannathan et al., “Transcompp: understanding phenotypic plasticity by estimating Markov transition rates for cell state transitions,” Bioinformatics 36(9), 2020, 2813–2820, DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021. [Primary article](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/36/9/2813/5714733). The full article page describes time-series single-cell measurements or bulk fractional compositions; a discrete-time Markov fit to multi-time composition data; optional known/bounded/fit phenotype-specific proliferation; and bootstrap/resampling uncertainty. It does not provide the finite controlled-word equivalence/no-go result or a hidden-state actionwise-CLE collision. We do not claim superiority in biological rate fitting.
5. Einar Bjarki Gunnarsson, Jasmine Foo, and Kevin Leder, “Statistical inference of the rates of cell proliferation and phenotypic switching in cancer,” Journal of Theoretical Biology 568 (2023) 111497, DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497. [Primary article / full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10372878/); [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08096). The source describes a CTMC branching model with division, death and phenotype switching, likelihood-based confidence intervals, and inputs from phenotype fractions or absolute counts at one or multiple time points; fractions can leave only switching reliably identified while count data support net growth and can allow division/death separation. This is the closest cell-rate inference baseline. It does not imply the present moment-collision model describes a biological system.
6. Yoshua Bengio and Paolo Frasconi, “Input-output HMMs for sequence processing,” IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks 7(5), 1996, 1231–1249, DOI 10.1109/72.536317. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18263517/). IOHMMs model input sequences to output sequences with latent states and EM/GEM training. This is modeling/estimation prior art, not an identifiability certificate.
7. Ivan Markovsky, Eduardo Prieto-Araujo, and Florian Dörfler, “On the persistency of excitation,” Automatica 147 (2023) 110657, DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2022.110657. [Primary article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109822005210). It concerns LTI finite-horizon identification/data-driven behavior and persistent excitation, including special initial-condition caveats. The assumptions differ from the hidden CTMC interface here; excitation cannot separate IO-equivalent models.
