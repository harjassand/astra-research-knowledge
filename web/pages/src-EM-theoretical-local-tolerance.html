1. The distinction that matters
The previous active-filter theorem supplied a sound, one-sided certificate: passing established predictive accuracy, but failing did not establish inaccuracy. In particular, it could reject a candidate whose entire requested trajectory was already approximately correct. Its coherent preparations and programmable filter were also substantive experimental resources.    research_note    research_note
The new result shows that the absence of a tolerant converse is not necessarily a technical defect in a particular certificate.
Under restricted physical access, these can be fundamentally different tasks:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Exact-agreement testing:}&\quad
E_T=0\ \text{versus}\ E_T\ge\varepsilon,\\
\text{Tolerant testing:}&\quad
E_T\le\eta\ \text{versus}\ E_T\ge 3/4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(E_T\) measures the error in the requested prediction—not a norm distance between the complete Hamiltonians.
The construction makes the first task polynomial and the second superpolynomial under the same experimental interface.
2. The experimental model
Consider a qubit chain. A set \(S\) of sites is accessible for preparation and control; call these the ports.
Each experiment starts with the remaining qubits in the known vacuum state. Within \(S\), the experimenter may prepare arbitrary states, use arbitrary quantum memory, and apply arbitrarily strong controls, measurements, resets and feedback.
The unknown system undergoes native real-time evolution for at most \(\tau\) during that experiment. Splitting the evolution into many controlled segments does not evade this cap. Starting another experiment requires a fresh vacuum outside \(S\).
For the lower bound, I grant an additional capability: the entire chain may be returned as a quantum output at the end, with arbitrary collective processing across experiments. Thus the theorem is stronger than a lower bound restricted to local terminal readout.
All attempted, discarded and postselected experiments count. Bath resets, control operations and computation are granted free in the lower bound; charging them cannot make the task easier.
Two limitations are essential: the experimenter cannot prepare the inaccessible bath arbitrarily, and cannot retain one evolving physical sample beyond the per-experiment duration cap.
The prediction target
Let
\[
\mathsf N=\sum_x n_x,\qquad n_x=|1\rangle\langle1|_x.
\]
The proposed model is simply
\[
K=\mathsf N.
\]
Prepare
\[
|\psi\rangle=|+\rangle_a\otimes|0\rangle_{\text{other sites}},
\]
where \(a\in S\). The candidate predicts a known phase rotation of qubit \(a\).
Define
\[
E_T(H)=
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
D\!\left(\rho_H^a(t),\rho_K^a(t)\right),
\qquad
D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1.
\]
Only this one-qubit reduced trajectory must be certified.
3. Main theorem
Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(0<\eta\le1/8\). Set
\[
T=\frac{256d^2}{\eta^2}.
\]
There are two nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians \(H_g,H_b\) on \(2(d+1)\) qubits such that
\[
\boxed{E_T(H_g)<\eta,\qquad E_T(H_b)>3/4.}
\tag{1}
\]
Both systems have bounded interaction terms, nonzero hopping on every edge, positive density–density interactions, and a unique product vacuum ground state with gap greater than \(0.9\).
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{H_b=P H_gP^\dagger}
\]
for a reflection \(P\) of the chain. They are therefore exactly isospectral as full many-body Hamiltonians, not merely in a selected sector.
Their coefficients differ only by exchanging two distant endpoint onsite terms. The complete pair of alternatives may be supplied in advance. The only unknown information is which alternative is present.
Label the two arms from their adjacent central sites as
\[
L_0,\ldots,L_d,\qquad R_0,\ldots,R_d.
\]
Allow control of both prefixes through site \(r<d\), and write
\[
R=d-r
\]
for the distance from the controlled region to either differing endpoint.
Lower bound
For \(0<\tau\le R/2\), any procedure that accepts every \(E_T\le\eta\) system and rejects every \(E_T\ge3/4\) system, each with probability at least \(2/3\), requires
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
\ge
\frac{(2R+1)(R!)^2}{144\,\tau^{\,2R+1}}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
At fixed positive \(\eta,\tau\), with \(r\le d/2\),
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
\ge
\exp\!\bigl(\Omega(d\log d)\bigr)
=
\exp\!\bigl(\Omega(\sqrt T\log T)\bigr).
}
\tag{3}
\]
This remains true even when roughly half the chain is controllable and complete terminal quantum readout is available.
Equation (2) is a lower bound, not a matching minimax law. No optimal tolerant upper bound is claimed.
For durations chosen at the start of each experiment, the average expected total native evolution time under the two hypotheses also satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\overline S
\ge
\frac{(2R+1)(R!)^2}{144\,\tau^{\,2R}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The duration statement allows adaptation between experiments, but does not assert an optional-stopping theorem for arbitrary outcome-dependent termination within a run.
4. Exact agreement has a polynomial local certificate
The contrast is explicit.
Consider the common ambient class of excitation-preserving Hamiltonians that fix the vacuum and have one-excitation restriction
\[
H\big|_{\text{one excitation}}=I+C,\qquad \|C\|\le2.
\]
The hard chains belong to this class. It also contains nontrivial exact-null models differing from \(K\) outside the predictive sector.
Choose
\[
h=\min\{\tau,1/2\}\le T.
\]
Prepare \(\psi\), evolve for time \(h\), undo the known candidate phase on qubit \(a\), and measure \(X_a\). Let \(p\) be the probability of the minus outcome.
Then
\[
\boxed{
E_T(H)\le \min\left\{1,\frac{2T}{h}\sqrt p\right\}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Proof
Let \(\nu\) be the spectral measure of \(C\) at the initially excited site. Write
\[
F(t)=\langle a|e^{-itC}|a\rangle
=\int e^{-itx}\,d\nu(x).
\]
The experiment gives
\[
p=\frac12\int[1-\cos(hx)]\,d\nu(x).
\]
Because \(|hx|\le1\),
\[
p\ge \frac{h^2}{8}\int x^2\,d\nu(x).
\]
Meanwhile, the squared distance between the full state vectors obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U_H(t)\psi-U_K(t)\psi\|^2
&=1-\Re F(t)\\
&\le\frac{t^2}{2}\int x^2\,d\nu(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking reduced states cannot increase trace distance, so
\[
E_T^2\le \frac{4T^2p}{h^2}.
\]
This proves (5).
Consequently, zero rejections in
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{4T^2}{h^2\varepsilon^2}
\log\frac1\alpha
\right\rceil
}
\tag{6}
\]
shots give a sound \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy certificate with error probability \(\alpha\). Exact agreement gives \(p=0\).
The procedure uses one-qubit preparation, a one-qubit phase correction and one-qubit measurement. It does not learn the Hamiltonian or simulate a many-body candidate.
But it is not genuinely tolerant: it may reject an already accurate model. The lower bound shows that repairing this defect can be fundamentally expensive.
The ambient class permits zero hopping, so exact agreement is a nonempty promise. The lower-bound alternatives themselves have nonzero hopping throughout. I am not comparing against a vacuous exact-null problem.
5. Construction: a small harmless component conceals a consequential phase shift
The missing mathematical ingredient was a positive spectral construction satisfying two requirements simultaneously:
- one system remains uniformly close to the simple candidate;
- the other has nearly identical local short-time structure but develops a large later phase error.
Positive moment matching
Put
\[
m=2d,\qquad \delta=\frac{\eta^2}{64d^2},
\]
and use the shifted Chebyshev nodes
\[
x_j=
\delta+(1-\delta)\frac{1-\cos(j\pi/m)}2,
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
Let
\[
w_j=\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{-x_k}{x_j-x_k}.
\]
Lagrange interpolation gives
\[
p(0)=\sum_jw_jp(x_j)
\]
for every polynomial of degree at most \(m\). The weights alternate in sign.
Their total absolute mass is exactly
\[
\Lambda
=
T_m\!\left(\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right)
=
\cosh\!\left(2m\,\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt\delta\right).
\]
Set \(A=(\Lambda+1)/2\), and define probability measures
\[
\nu_g
=
\frac{\delta_0+\sum_{j\text{ odd}}|w_j|\delta_{x_j}}{A},
\qquad
\nu_b
=
\frac{\sum_{j\text{ even}}w_j\delta_{x_j}}{A}.
\tag{7}
\]
Their moments agree through degree \(2d\).
The good measure has mass \(1-\rho\) at zero, where
\[
\boxed{
\rho
=
\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}
=
\tanh^2\!\left(m\,\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt\delta\right)
\le\frac{\eta^2}{9}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
The bad measure has mass at least \(1-\rho\) at \(\delta\).
Therefore, for every time,
\[
1-\Re F_g(t)\le2\rho,
\]
whereas at
\[
t_*=\frac{\pi}{\delta}<T,
\]
\[
\Re F_b(t_*)\le-1+2\rho.
\tag{9}
\]
The first response has only a small persistent deviation. The second accumulates an almost opposite phase.
The contamination in (8) is not chosen arbitrarily. It is optimal for this moment-matching problem: applying the same Chebyshev polynomial to any competing measures gives
\[
(1-\rho)\Lambda-\rho\le1,
\]
hence \(\rho\ge(\Lambda-1)/(\Lambda+1)\). The construction attains equality. This is an application of established Chebyshev extremality, not a claim to have invented that approximation theorem.
Converting the measures into local Hamiltonians
Orthonormalizing the monomials produces Jacobi matrices \(A_g,A_b\), with first-site spectral measures \(\nu_g,\nu_b\).
Matching moments through \(2d\) makes every hopping coefficient and all but the final diagonal coefficient identical. A trace calculation gives
\[
\boxed{
A_b-A_g
=
\frac{1+\delta}{2}|d\rangle\langle d|.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus a substantial change at one distant boundary implements the spectral distinction.
Place \(A_g\) on the left arm and \(A_b\) on the right, joined by a hopping
\[
\gamma=\frac{\eta}{16T}.
\]
Reflecting the arms defines the other hypothesis. Promote these matrices to qubit Hamiltonians with hopping terms and add
\[
\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}n_xn_y.
\]
These are connected interacting chains. Their full spectra coincide by reflection.
The hopping-plus-onsite part has one-particle matrix bounded below by \((1-\gamma)I\). On a path, its fermionic diagonalization makes the many-body operator at least \((1-\gamma)\mathsf N\). The added density interactions are nonnegative. Hence the vacuum gap is at least \(1-\gamma>0.9\).
For the target input, the dynamics remain in vacuum plus one excitation. Equations (8)–(9) give good-case error below \(\eta/2\) and bad-case local error at least \(1-\rho\) before adding the joining bond. Duhamel’s identity bounds the bond’s effect through \(T\) by
\[
\gamma T=\eta/16,
\]
preserving (1).
The target trajectory is low-excitation, although the validation protocol is allowed to inject many excitations. I am not claiming a hard finite-density many-body prediction theorem.
6. Why arbitrary active port controls do not defeat the construction
Scalar moment matching is not enough for this step. The proof bounds the entire controlled quantum experiment.
Let \(B_{xy}=|J_{xy}|\) be the hopping adjacency matrix. Its row sums are at most one.
For a purified state, define
\[
p_x(t)=\langle n_x\rangle,\qquad q_x(t)=\sqrt{p_x(t)}.
\]
Outside the controlled ports, the control Hamiltonians and all density interactions commute with \(n_x\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
|\dot p_x|
\le
2\sum_yB_{xy}\sqrt{p_xp_y},
\]
and therefore, with the standard limiting interpretation at \(p_x=0\),
\[
\dot q_x\le\sum_yB_{xy}q_y.
\]
Since the outside starts empty and \(q_x\le1\) on controlled sites,
\[
q_x(t)
\le
(e^{tB}\mathbf1_S)_x
\le
Q_R(t):=\sum_{k\ge R}\frac{t^k}{k!}
\]
at an endpoint distance \(R\) away. For \(t\le R/2\),
\[
\boxed{Q_R(t)\le\frac{2t^R}{R!}.}
\tag{11}
\]
This holds uniformly over all port inputs, entangled memories, injected excitations and port-control strengths.
Let \(V_g(t),V_b(t)\) be the forward isometries describing the complete controlled experiments, retaining all measurement records. The two generators differ only by
\[
H_b-H_g=c_\delta(n_{L_d}-n_{R_d}),
\qquad c_\delta=(1+\delta)/2.
\]
Common control operations preserve cross overlaps. Between controls,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(V_g^\dagger V_b)
=
iV_g^\dagger(H_g-H_b)V_b.
\]
At either endpoint,
\[
\|V_g^\dagger n_xV_b\|
\le
\|n_xV_g\|\,\|n_xV_b\|
\le Q_R(t)^2.
\]
Consequently, a complete experiment of duration \(u\le\tau\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|I-V_g(u)^\dagger V_b(u)\|
\le
\frac{8u^{2R+1}}{(2R+1)(R!)^2}
=:\zeta(u).
}
\tag{12}
\]
The squared arrival amplitude is the important information bound. It includes the full quantum output, not just the observed local correlator.
After \(N_{\mathrm{exp}}\) adaptive fresh experiments, purified overlap bookkeeping gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{final}}^2
\le
\min\{1,\,2N_{\mathrm{exp}}\zeta(\tau)\}.
\]
Distinguishing the hypotheses with success probability \(2/3\) requires \(D_{\mathrm{final}}\ge1/3\). Substitution proves (2).
The duration bound follows by using
\[
\zeta(u)
\le
\frac{8\tau^{2R}}{(2R+1)(R!)^2}\,u
\]
in each adaptive history block. The complete bookkeeping is in the note.
This proves the active observation bound independently of the earlier Genesis or quantum-snapshot arguments.
7. Physical costs, calibration and escape routes
No powerful filter is hidden
The positive certificate uses ordinary waiting under \(H\), one-qubit operations and a supplied fresh vacuum bath. There is no imaginary-time implementation, filter normalization or uncharged heralding failure.
If vacuum reset costs \(C_{\rm reset}\) and local preparation/readout costs \(C_{\rm local}\), its resource bill includes
\[
N_{\mathrm{exp}}(C_{\rm reset}+C_{\rm local})
\]
plus native evolution time \(N_{\mathrm{exp}}h\). Vacuum preparation is an explicit source assumption, not a universal free laboratory operation.
The lower bound grants these resources free and still applies.
Finite coefficients suffice—but not constant noise
The note proves an inverse-polynomial lower bound on every arm hopping,
\[
|J_j|\ge\frac{\eta}{300d^3}.
\]
Matched dyadic rounding with step
\[
q\le
\min\left\{
\frac{\eta}{96T},
\frac{\eta}{1200d^3}
\right\}
\]
preserves the predictive separation, the gap, the distant support of the difference and exact reflection isospectrality.
Thus only
\[
O(\log(d/\eta))
\]
bits per bounded coefficient are required.
However, the joining bond is inverse-polynomially weak, and the system is deliberately inhomogeneous. This is not a theorem for uniformly bounded-below hopping, fixed-strength disorder, finite-temperature initial states or persistent decoherence.
For the elementary positive certificate, an implementation bias of at most \(\beta\) in rejection probability gives
\[
E_T\le\frac{2T}{h}\sqrt{p_U+\beta},
\]
where \(p_U\) is a statistical upper confidence bound. Certifying error \(\varepsilon\) therefore requires \(\beta=O(h^2\varepsilon^2/T^2)\) by this route.
The tolerant lower bound already holds with ideal noiseless experiments.
One additional preparation location defeats the pair
Allow preparation of \(|+\rangle\) at the distant endpoint and one-qubit readout there. The onsite difference is at least \(1/2\).
A short-time expansion shows a difference in rotating-frame \(Y\) expectations of at least
\[
\frac t2-4t^2\ge\frac t4,
\qquad 0<t\le1/16.
\]
For \(t_0=\min\{\tau,1/32\}\),
\[
O\!\left(t_0^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)\right)
\]
shots identify the supplied binary alternative, independently of \(d\) and \(T\).
Alternatively, allowing one coherent run of duration \(t_*=\Theta(T)\) reveals the bad prediction directly.
These are exact boundaries: the theorem concerns limited spatial intervention and short fresh experiments—not an intrinsic inability to learn the system by any means.
8. Comparison with current certification and learning algorithms
The comparison changes when “local access” is unpacked.
Normalized-Frobenius certification. Bluhm and coauthors give \(O(1/\varepsilon)\)-total-time tolerant certification for fixed-locality Hamiltonians, including ancilla-free variants. Their target is normalized Frobenius distance between generators, with general evolution access and candidate-dependent processing. That is not the promise of accepting every accurate one-qubit trajectory under a short local-port interface. The distinction is not simply the presence of an ancilla. arXiv
Single-qubit-operation certification. Shin and Tong’s eigenphase-engineering work supplies learning and tolerant certification using single-qubit operations and forward dynamics. Those operations are not confined to the protected ports here. Small evolution steps also do not imply a small total coherent duration for one controlled experiment. arXiv
A single readout qubit can already suffice under stronger control. Liu and coauthors’ 1 October 2026 work learns many-body Hamiltonians through one measurable qubit using controls throughout the register and a connected reference backbone. They explicitly construct coherent information routing. This is a particularly important comparison: restricting readout alone is not the obstruction established here. Their systemwide controls are absent from my lower-bound interface. arXiv
Very restricted settings can still provide broad information. Gong and coauthors’ 5 October 2026 work reconstructs generic two-local Hamiltonians using uniform preparation and measurement settings across the system, with separate computational-basis results for specified interaction families. Such settings are experimentally restricted, but spatially global; they do not leave the inaccessible bath fixed in vacuum. arXiv
Structured local-probe reconstruction. Quantum Probe Tomography provides positive results using local probes, structured Hamiltonian families and thermal-state access. Those assumptions differ from the inhomogeneous worst-case family and fixed reset interface here. The new theorem does not exclude those positive guarantees. arXiv
Accordingly, the result is not “local measurements cannot certify quantum dynamics.” It is a statement about the joint costs of where one may intervene, how long each system may evolve, which bath states can be prepared, and whether approximately correct predictions must be accepted.
9. What has actually advanced
The new contribution is a self-contained separation between an exact predictive certificate and genuine tolerant forecasting under ordinary active real-time access.
It is stronger than the previous fixed-input imaginary-time obstruction in several specific respects: the experiments may be actively controlled; the target is only one qubit; the complete Hamiltonian alternatives are already known; full spectra coincide; and the proof bounds whole controlled quantum experiments.
The package contains 2,502 finite numerical assertions, including 690 multiprecision checks, controlled interacting-chain checks, the exact-null certificate and the far-end escape. It also supplies an explicit dyadic ten-qubit instance. These are diagnostics, not independent verification or a laboratory demonstration.
The broad positive objective remains unestablished: I have not produced a universally tolerant, efficient certifier from short local-port experiments. Instead, the theorem proves that such a guarantee is impossible over the stated class.
The resulting principle is precise: a sound certificate that accepts exact agreement need not be convertible into a procedure that reliably accepts approximately correct long-time predictions. Under restricted physical access, acquiring that tolerance can itself require superpolynomial information—even when there is only one unknown microscopic bit.
