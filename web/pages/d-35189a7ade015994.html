## Exact author claims and interface

Theorem 1.1 (`thm:primitive-root`) claims that for every integer a other than -1 and the integer squares, at least `c_a x/(log x)^2` primes in each sufficiently large interval (x,2x) have primitive root a. Both c_a>0 and the onset may depend on a. The introduction explicitly distinguishes this scale from the conjectured x/log x.

The separate Theorem 1.2 (`thm:hecke-zero-free`) claims that **for every cyclotomic number field F containing mu_12, and every finite-order Hecke character eta of F**, L_F(s,eta) has no zero in `Re(s)>1-10^-6`. The principal character and its pole at 1 are allowed. There is no conductor or height cutoff. F and eta are fixed before auxiliary data, and constants/onsets can depend on them, but the strip width is absolute.

Worker metric_use10 independently reported that family 142's factorization theorem uses this result as its only analytic input and derives an auxiliary prime bound `ell_q <= c B^20,000,000`. Our exact reading confirms that 029's theorem has the wide universal scope the factorization application requires.

## Independently reconstructed conditional Mellin lemma

Let delta>0 and lambda=8/15. Suppose L has meromorphic continuation on `Re(s)>1-delta`, and H is holomorphic and nowhere zero there. Suppose

`A(s)=exp((s-5/6)^2) H(s)/L(s)`

is holomorphic on Re(s)>1 and is sufficiently rapidly decreasing on each vertical line to the right of 1 for the following inverse integral and line shifts. Define

`f(Z) = (1/(2 pi i)) integral_(2) Z^(s-lambda) A(s) ds`.

Assume also `f(Z)=O(Z^(1-lambda-delta))` as Z tends to infinity and `f(Z)=O_A(Z^A)` as Z tends to zero for every A>0. Constants and the large-Z onset may depend on fixed L,H.

Then

`T(s)=integral_0^infinity f(Z) Z^(-(s-lambda)) dZ/Z`

converges locally uniformly and is holomorphic for Re(s)>1-delta. At infinity the exponent is 1-delta-Re(s)<0; at zero choose A larger than Re(s)-lambda on each compact set. Powers of log Z are covered by the same strict margins, so derivatives are justified.

Put t=log Z. On the original line,

`exp(-(2-lambda)t) f(exp(t)) = (1/(2pi)) integral_R exp(i tau t) A(2+i tau) d tau`.

The endpoint assumptions make the left side integrable. Fourier inversion gives `T(2+i tau)=A(2+i tau)`. The identity theorem first identifies them for Re(s)>1 and then as meromorphic functions on the larger connected half-plane. T is holomorphic and exp((s-5/6)^2)H(s) never vanishes there; therefore L cannot have a zero there. Poles of L cause zeros, rather than singularities, in A and are harmless.

The release's Section 6, `eq:principal-Mellin-integral` and `eq:f-large-bound`, has delta=1/20000, with large exponent `7/15-1/20000`. This yields the stronger candidate exclusion Re(s)>1-1/20000, and thus includes the theorem's narrower strip. The omitted finite Euler factors have zeros only on real part zero because eta is finite order. The conditional Mellin implication checks directly. A field-dependent onset changes only a finite part of the Mellin integral and does **not** by itself invalidate this strip conclusion.

## The load-bearing unverified inputs

Section 6's final step is conditional on substantial earlier inputs:

- `prop:probe-low` and the reflection scales must give the stated additive probe bound with saving 1/6000 before losses, uniformly in the required fixed-field arithmetic setup.
- `prop:euler-product` must factor the same probe correctly, and its specialization H_eta must be holomorphic **and nowhere zero** throughout Re(s)>1-1/20000. Holomorphy alone would permit H to cancel zeros of L and is insufficient.
- The cubic theta coefficient and sextic Kummer twist normalizations, relative-norm Gauss phase, reciprocal target Euler factor, and Whittaker correction must apply over every cyclotomic F in the theorem's scope.
- The conductor large sieve, good-row reciprocal bounds, contour regions and nonprincipal row estimates must hold for the precise family, not just an idealized primitive character model.

The numerical margins in Section 6 can be recomputed, but they do not establish these analytic/arithmetic hypotheses. No decisive flaw or unconditional counterexample was found here. Our conclusion is neither acceptance nor rejection; the source theorem is unvalidated at exactly these interfaces. The independently checked downstream Mellin step should not be used to label the upstream theorem proved.

## Prior art and consequences

The closest standard comparison read is Thorner-Zaman, *A unified and improved Chebotarev density theorem*, arXiv:1803.02823v3, 27 October 2018, Theorem 3.1 on printed p.11. It gives a logarithmic zero-free region for the product of relevant Hecke L-functions of an abelian extension, with at most one exceptional real simple zero. Its width depends on discriminant/conductor, degree and height. It does not supply 029's universal constant strip. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02823v3).

The claimed constant strip is therefore the high-consequence analytic dependency: if genuinely established, it would strengthen many splitting and norm-equation arguments, including the family 142 route reported by metric_use10. Our Egyptian-fraction construction uses only classical quantitative Vinogradov and is independent of this new claim. The immediate allocation should audit the reciprocal Euler factor and nonvanishing correction, followed by the reflection/probe estimate over arbitrary F, before importing 029 into downstream algorithms.
