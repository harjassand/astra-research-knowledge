# Phase nucleation and metastability: branch report

## Finding

I did not establish a new physical law or a novel general theorem for material nucleation. The strongest defensible contribution is a quantitative, primary-literature-grounded **kinetic-model discrimination design**: two reversible Ising dynamics share the same equilibrium Gibbs distribution and critical droplet but have different low-temperature nucleation exponents. It gives a clean falsifier for the assumption that a static barrier alone determines switching time. The dynamic contrast is established prior art; the proposed sampling calculation is conditional and internally derived.

For the square-lattice Ising model with \(J=1\), all-minus metastable phase, and field \(H=3/2\), the critical droplet is a 2-by-1 rectangle plus one protuberance. The standard Glauber (“hard”) model predicts \(\Gamma=7\); the factorized (“soft”) detailed-balance model predicts \(\Gamma=10\). The source prefactors give a mean-lifetime ratio asymptotic to \((1/3)e^{3\beta}\) in their clock convention. This is large enough to make the kinetic rule experimentally or computationally discriminable in principle.

The central practical implication is limited but useful: fit **rates and nucleus morphologies**, not only a static free-energy profile, before selecting a kinetic Monte Carlo rule. No inference about an unmeasured real material follows until its order parameter, field coupling, saddle morphology, and transition rates are calibrated.

## Primary reconstruction

Neves and Schonmann establish the low-temperature critical-size and metastable-escape picture for finite 2D Ising systems under a positive field; Park et al. compare two detailed-balance dynamics and report the exponent shift \(\Gamma_{soft}-\Gamma_{hard}=2H\) for \(0<H<4J\). In the nondegenerate regime,

\[
\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2J}{H}\right\rfloor+1,\qquad
\Gamma_{hard}=8J\ell-2H(\ell^2-\ell+1).
\]

The shape is an \(\ell\times(\ell-1)\) rectangle with one added spin. The barrier formula follows from \(\Delta E(C)=2J|\partial C|-2H|C|\). Full assumptions, derivation, and evidence level are in [MECHANISM_RECONSTRUCTION.md](MECHANISM_RECONSTRUCTION.md).

Park et al. report an analytical one-step calculation, computer-assisted absorbing-chain calculations, and simulations at \(L=24\) with 2,000 escapes (6,000 in the strongest-field cases); the parameter fits agreed within two standard errors for the tested cases. The one-step calculation is exact for the reduced chain, while the full-lattice soft-rate result is treated here as a source-reported result with finite numerical corroboration. I did not independently replay code or re-prove the full-lattice result. The hard-dynamics saddle and exponential barrier are established external results. The material interpretation remains an inference requiring measurement.

## Measurement design

At fixed field, run repeated independent metastable preparations across several temperatures, track the first supercritical nucleus with time-resolved imaging, and fit the low-temperature slope of \(\log\mathbb E\tau\) versus \(\beta\). At \(J=1,H=1.5\), the hard/soft slope predictions are 7 and 10. Verify the assumed nucleus shape, single-droplet regime, waiting-time law, box-size sufficiency, and clock convention. The precise hard/soft rates can be compared in simulation while holding the Hamiltonian fixed; an experiment can only be said to match a rate family after independent kinetic calibration.

Under independent exponential waits and a temperature-independent prefactor, \(M\) replicates per temperature give approximate slope standard error \(\sqrt{2/M}/|\Delta\beta|\). Resolving the predicted gap \(2H\) at \(z\) standard errors therefore needs approximately \(M\ge z^2/[2H^2(\Delta\beta)^2]\) per temperature. The exact conditional variance uses the trigamma function. This does not include finite-temperature corrections, pilot runs, multiple-comparison control, or material-to-model calibration.

The pre-registered falsifier is straightforward: after confirming single-droplet behavior and correct time units, a measured slope outside both predicted values rejects both candidate kinetic rules for that system/parameter range. If morphology or waiting times violate the model assumptions, the test rejects the Ising mapping rather than its mathematical result.

## Costs and unresolved gates

The empirical design charges independent sample preparations and waiting-time exposure; their laboratory cost and energy per sample are unknown. Direct kinetic Monte Carlo has cost proportional to \(L^2\) attempted site updates per sweep and an expected time that grows exponentially in \(\beta\Gamma\); the source did not report wall time or joule usage. Accelerated rare-event sampling requires separate accounting for interface placement, event counts, and estimator bias. Model energies \(J,H,\Gamma\) are not compute energy or physical work.

The open gate for an actual materials advance is a named material system with independently calibrated order parameter, local transition state barriers, defect distribution, and time-resolved nucleus images. Without those data, the route is a model audit and experiment design, not a materials prediction. The broad novelty search was not exhaustive, and the established Ising result must not be presented as new.
