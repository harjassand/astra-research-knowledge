## 1. Exact promise and metric transformation

The input is expanded rational p=((Lx)^2-a)^TQ((Lx)^2-a), with rational invertible L, rational symmetric Q>0, a>0 and b=Qa>0. It yields the rational positive metric M=-Hess(p2)/4=L^T diag(b)L. Set F=(1/4)M^-1 grad p4 and D=F·grad. With T=diag(sqrt(b))L and y=Tx, the transformed field is exactly

    Dy_i=y_i(Gm)_i, m_i=y_i²,
    G=diag(b)^-1 Q diag(b)^-1>0.

This verifies every factor of 2 and 4 in the coordinate transformation. T is used in the proof only. The actual algorithm performs rational inversion of M, not algebraic whitening. The additional original-promise relation G^-1 1=diag(b)a>0 is valid but is not needed for the universal nonvanishing lemma.

Every hidden rational row form ell_i is D-invariant in the sense D ell_i=ell_i h_i for a polynomial h_i. Induction gives ell_i | D^k ell_i. Changing the coordinate column basis in the determinant therefore proves ell_i divides E=det[D^k x_i]_(0<=k<n). This requires no supplied factor family. A linear coordinate change multiplies E by a nonzero determinant, so the nonvanishing question can be checked in hidden y coordinates.

## 2. Independent proof of universal nonvanishing

I reconstructed the argument independently before reading the written proof. It is sound and handles the important rational-pole issue.

Induct on n. Choose j maximizing G_jj. For i!=j, positive-definite principal 2 by 2 minors give G_ij<G_jj, even when diagonal maxima or transverse rates tie. The (n-1)-square minor B from columns i!=j and derivative orders 0 through n-2 restricts at y_j=0 to the smaller extactic determinant: the ideal (y_j) is D-invariant, so restriction commutes with D. The induction hypothesis makes B a nonzero polynomial.

If E vanished identically, invert B over R(y) to express D^k y_j=sum_i c_i D^k y_i for k=0,...,n-2. The zero full determinant extends the relation to k=n-1. Differentiating each relation and subtracting the next gives B·(Dc)=0, hence each rational c_i satisfies Dc_i=0. In particular y_j=sum_i c_i y_i.

Now take y_j>0 and small ratios r_i=y_i/y_j. With ds/dt=y_j², the ratio equation is

    r_i'=r_i[G_ij-G_jj+sum_(k!=j)(G_ik-G_jk)r_k²].

Choose a small cube so that every quadratic correction is at most half the least positive G_jj-G_ij. It is forward invariant, and all ratios tend exponentially to zero in s. The equation for log y_j has bounded coefficients on that cube, so the rescaled solution exists at every finite s and y_j stays positive.

Some initial point in this open cone avoids all rational-function poles, because det B is not the zero polynomial. On a small initial interval the coefficients c_i are their finite initial constants. The resulting fixed-coefficient relation between analytic coordinate trajectories extends to every finite s by the identity theorem. No rational representative is evaluated at later poles. Dividing by y_j and letting s increase gives 1=sum_i c_i(initial)r_i(s)->0, a contradiction.

Only the polynomial minor B was restricted to y_j=0. Restricting rational first integrals there would be invalid and is correctly avoided. There is no hidden rate or G oracle in the algorithm; the flow is solely a proof that E is nonzero.

## 3. One-line factor recovery, including multiplicity

Let E=ell^e H with ell a hidden rational linear form, ell not dividing H. Its total degree is d=n². For a line u+t v satisfying ell(v)!=0, the intersection parameter r=-ell(u)/ell(v) is rational. If H(u+rv)!=0, its univariate multiplicity is exactly e. At z=u+rv,

    grad[D_v^(e-1) E](z)
      =e! ell(v)^(e-1) H(z) grad ell.

This follows from the product rule: all e derivatives must hit the e linear factors for a nonzero value at ell=0. Terms differentiating H leave an undifferentiated ell and vanish. The resulting vector is nonzero. The code computes exactly this mixed derivative by differentiating each ambient gradient polynomial along the line e-1 times. Repeated factors are therefore retained; plain gradients would fail.

The bad-line conditions for this factor are ell(v)=0 or H(ell(v)u-ell(u)v)=0. Homogeneity gives degree 1 and 2(d-e), respectively. The second polynomial is nonzero because the displayed argument reaches every point of ker ell after choosing ell(v)!=0, and H does not vanish identically there. Taking the product for all n hidden factors gives degree at most n+2n(d-1)<2nd. Uniform independent sampling from a grid with more than 8nd elements per coordinate therefore succeeds with probability greater than 3/4. The implemented grid is more than large enough.

The theorem's probability statement requires genuinely independent uniform samples. The current API uses system randomness by default and explicitly labels fixed-seed runs as diagnostics. A fixed public pseudorandom seed has no adversarial-input Schwartz–Zippel guarantee. The audit caught a test-harness mismatch during that API update, recorded below; it was not an algorithmic counterexample.

## 4. Extraneous candidates and exact one-pool selection

Every nonzero recovered normal h gives the primal ray w=M^-1h, not h itself. Its observable normalized score is p4(w)/(w^TMw)^2. In hidden normalized coordinates this is m^TGm on the probability simplex. Direct expansion gives

    sum_i m_i G_ii-m^TGm
      =sum_(i<j)m_i m_j(e_i-e_j)^TG(e_i-e_j)>0

for every m with at least two positive coordinates. Thus a candidate pool containing all true axes has a true axis at its highest score. Extraneous normals from nonlinear extactic factors need not be recognized or factored further; they cannot beat a genuine global maximizing axis.

After selecting w, exact M-orthogonality retains every remaining true axis and confines all other surviving candidates to the corresponding hidden coordinate face. The same strict inequality applies to the positive-definite principal submatrix there. Induction therefore selects all axes from ONE original candidate pool. There is no approximate deflation and no recursion that accumulates coordinate-reconstruction height. Positive definiteness makes pairwise M-orthogonal nonzero selected rays independent, so their matrix V is invertible.

On an unlucky line, the method may select wrong candidates or too few of them. The final exact verifier protects soundness; coverage follows only on good lines. The two roles are correctly separated.

## 5. Final certificate soundness, including false promises

Substitution x=Vz is entirely rational and uses acquired V. Reading the even fourth-degree terms yields a candidate Q'; reading the quadratic terms yields b'. Solving a'=Q'^-1 b' and checking Q'>0, a'>0 is necessary but is not enough: the implementation also compares the FULL expanded polynomial, including constant, odd-degree and mixed-coordinate terms. This rejects an incorrectly guessed frame or an input with the same leading geometry but a wrong lower-degree part.

Rational LDL of Q' yields strictly positive diagonal weights and explicit rational quadratic weighted-square factors in the original x coordinates. The code independently re-expands that final weighted sum. Thus every returned PASS is a valid exact positive certificate even outside the input promise. This does not make UNKNOWN a proof of nonexistence. On promised input every good line reaches this successful verifier, so independent repetitions have expected trial count at most 4/3 and budget-T failure probability at most 4^-T.

## 6. Polynomial bit accounting reconstructed explicitly

There are polynomially many input coefficients because the degree is fixed at four. Rational metric inversion, cubic-field construction and common-denominator clearing all have polynomial bit cost. To see that no hidden enormous coefficient appears later, clear a common denominator A in F and use the integer field Ftilde=A F. Let C=max_i ||Ftilde_i||_1. For a homogeneous polynomial g of degree r,

    ||Dtilde g||_1<=r C||g||_1.

Consequently ||Dtilde^k x_i||_1<=C^k product_(j<k)(2j+1). Summing logarithms across the determinant columns gives

    log2 ||Etilde||_1 <=log2(n!)
      +[n(n-1)/2]log2 max(1,C)+sum_(k<n)k log2 max(1,2k),

which is polynomial in n and input coefficient bits. The integer-field extactic differs from E only by the nonzero factor A^[n(n-1)/2]. Substituting a sampled line with coordinates bounded by S multiplies its coefficient l1 bound by at most (2S)^d. Here log S=O(log n) and d=n². Gradient coefficients add a factor at most d. Thus the univariate polynomial and all gradient polynomials have polynomial degree and coefficient bit length.

Their construction need not expand E in n variables. The formal cubic ODE jets through order n-1 have polynomially many univariate coefficients. Each cubic monomial uses a convolution of three truncated series; first-order dual components give all derivatives in the initial coordinates. The recurrence divides only by integers up to n; factorial denominators have polynomial bit length, and multiplication by k! recovers D^k x. Polynomial-ring determinants and cofactors have polynomial-time exact algorithms and the height bounds above. The explicit prototype loops implement this polynomial-size construction.

Polynomial-time univariate rational factorization is a standard prerequisite, not a new result of this programme. Retaining linear factors gives roots whose numerator and denominator bit lengths are bounded by the univariate coefficient height. Mixed derivatives, evaluation at those roots, normalized rational scores, Gram orthogonality checks, final substitution and LDL are then polynomial bit operations on polynomial-size rationals. No bound on the scale of a latent representation L is supplied or needed: canonical acquired directions have their own polynomial height bound through this extraction.

The mathematical algorithm may use standard polynomial-time polynomial-ring linear algebra and univariate factorization. SymPy timings alone would not establish those asymptotics; the theorem does not rely on such an inference. The proof supplies expected polynomial bit time and polynomial time per declared trial, not an unbounded worst-case deterministic success time.
