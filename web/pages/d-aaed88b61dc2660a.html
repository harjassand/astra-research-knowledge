# Cancer evolution: branching-risk control audit

## Candidate bottleneck

Adaptive and evolutionary therapy models often switch on bulk growth or net clone growth. A net rate does not, by itself, determine the probability that a rare lineage leaves any descendants: a linear birth–death process with division rate (b) and death rate (d) has mean (e^{(b-d)t}), while its finite-time distribution and establishment probability depend on (b) and (d) separately. This matters when a proposed collateral drug lowers a resistant intermediate but may also induce a more dangerous state or genotype.

The current concrete biological anchor is the V299L intermediate in BCR–ABL1-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Zhao et al. found that the dasatinib-resistant V299L mutant was collaterally sensitive to foretinib/vandetanib through on-target ABL1 inhibition; a one-week course in mice reduced measured burden by about three logs and lengthened median survival, with some long-term survivors. Certain V299L compound mutations weakened the vulnerability, while others retained it. This supports a *candidate* resistance branch, but the paper does not measure per-cell birth/death/switch rates or prove clonal extinction. [Zhao et al., Cell (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5152932/)

## Exact two-type decision equation

Consider types (R) and (E). Under a constant collateral pulse, an (R) cell produces one additional (R) daughter at rate (b_R), dies at rate (d_R), and switches one-for-one to (E) at rate \(\nu\). An (E) cell births a same-type daughter at rate (b_E) and dies at rate (d_E). At the end of the pulse a fixed maintenance drug is continued. Assume independent lineages, constant rates within each phase, and (b_i^A>d_i^A) under maintenance, so the future establishment probabilities are (q_i=1-d_i^A/b_i^A).

Let (p_i(T)) be the probability that a lineage started as type (i), exposed to the collateral drug for duration (T), produces no lineage that establishes under the post-pulse maintenance drug. Then (p_i(0)=1-q_i), and the probability-generating-function/branching property gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dp_R}{dT}&=b_R(p_R^2-p_R)+d_R(1-p_R)+\nu(p_E-p_R),\\
\frac{dp_E}{dT}&=b_E(p_E^2-p_E)+d_E(1-p_E).
\end{aligned}
\]

For (n_R,n_E) independent founders, the no-establishment probability is (p_R(T)^{n_R}p_E(T)^{n_E}). The ODE is exact for this model and can be composed across piecewise-constant treatment phases. Its assumptions exclude density dependence, spatial reseeding, unmodeled resistance types, and drug-dependent switching rates; those must be modeled or treated as adverse uncertainty.

At zero pulse duration,

\[
p_R'(0)=q_R\big[(d_R-b_R)+b_Rq_R\big]-\nu(q_E-q_R).
\]

Thus the collateral pulse initially lowers relapse risk exactly when (p_R'(0)>0): extinction pressure on (R) exceeds the penalty for sending (R) into the more relapse-prone state (E). If only the net rate (r_R=b_R-d_R) is measured, this sign is not identified because the term (b_Rq_R^2) still depends on turnover.

### Exact sign-flip counterexample

Use the same post-pulse probabilities (q_R=0.2), (q_E=0.8), same switching rate \(\nu=0.005\) day\(^{-1}\), and same collateral net growth rate (r_R=-0.01\) day\(^{-1}\). Both collateral models are subcritical:

| Model | (b_R) | (d_R) | (b_R-d_R) | (p_R'(0)) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| low turnover | 0.01 | 0.02 | −0.01 | −0.0006 day\(^{-1}\) |
| high turnover | 0.10 | 0.11 | −0.01 | +0.003 day\(^{-1}\) |

Indeed, the formula gives (0.2(0.01+0.2b_R)-0.005(0.6)). The low-turnover model predicts that a very short collateral pulse increases relapse probability; the high-turnover model predicts that it decreases it. Bulk expected clone size is identical in both models, (E[R_t]=R_0e^{-0.01t}), for every (t). This refutes any universal sign prediction based on that net trajectory alone within the specified branching/switching model. It does not refute inference using sufficiently many replicate stochastic trajectories, cell-lineage trees, or direct division/death measurements.

For one initial (R) cell, the short-pulse risk change is approximately 

\[
\Pr(\text{establishment by }T)-q_R=-p_R'(0)T+O(T^2).
\]

The concrete separating observation is therefore the event-level turnover (b_R,d_R), along with the transition rate \(\nu\) and post-pulse establishment probabilities (q_R,q_E). A controlled assay can estimate division and death hazards by live-cell lineage tracking, state transitions by barcodes plus validated phenotype calls, and (q_i) by limiting-dilution regrowth under maintenance. A lineage-inference method may replace direct reporters only after its identifiability is verified for the rare-transition regime and its errors are propagated to the risk interval.

## Conservative risk bound from mean clone trajectories

If event-level birth/death rates cannot be identified, there is a weaker bound that needs only clone-specific mean trajectories. Let (m_R(t)=E[R_t]). Suppose each (R) cell makes a transition/immigration attempt into an escape type at predictable rate at most \(\nu(t)\); a resulting escape founder establishes with probability at most \(\bar q_E(t)\). Suppose each residual (R) cell at pulse end establishes with probability at most \(\bar q_R\). By applying Markov's inequality to the number of successful founder events and a union bound,

\[
\Pr(\text{relapse})\leq \bar q_Rm_R(T)+\int_0^T\bar q_E(t)\nu(t)m_R(t)\,dt.
\]

Independent external escape immigration of intensity \(I_E(t)\) contributes the additional term \(\int_0^T\bar q_E(t)I_E(t)dt\). No branching independence is required for the union-bound statement if the conditional success bounds hold; the formula can be conservative because it counts overlapping routes multiple times. If (m_R(t)\leq n e^{-\delta t}), with constants (\delta>0,\nu,\bar q_E,\bar q_R), the bound is

\[
n\bar q_R e^{-\delta T}+n\frac{\nu\bar q_E}{\delta}(1-e^{-\delta T}).
\]

This tends to a nonzero mutation/escape floor (n\nu\bar q_E/\delta). Given a tolerated risk \(\epsilon\), the bound certifies a pulse only if the floor is below \(\epsilon\); otherwise longer pulse duration alone cannot establish the target. For instance, the unfitted illustrative values (n=1,\delta=.06,\bar q_R=.25,\nu\bar q_E=.0001\) day\(^{-1}\), (T=30) days give an upper bound of about (0.043). This is an algebraic fixture, not a fit to the Zhao experiments.

The bound requires absolute (R)-lineage abundance (or a valid upper confidence envelope), not just total tumor burden or a variant allele fraction without copy-number, shedding, and purity correction. If a sensitive population seeds new resistance, add its transition terms too. If the risks \(q_i\), mutation supply, or abundance are unknown, keep the result as `UNKNOWN`; no extinction certification follows from net shrinkage alone.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

The mechanisms and mathematical tools substantially overlap published work:

- Zhao et al. already combine a stochastic branching model, temporal collateral sensitivity, resistant-intermediate selection, and in-vivo V299L responses; the paper establishes a vulnerability, not lineage extinction or calibrated extinction probabilities. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5152932/)
- Cancer resistance under nonhomogeneous drug schedules has already been modeled with multi-type birth–death processes and generating functions. [Foo & Michor (2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/)
- A two-phenotype switching cancer model already derives extinction probabilities and mutation/phenotype-switch thresholds. [Gunnarsson et al. (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/)
- Drug-induced mutation and stochastic rescue have already been coupled to optimized cancer treatment schedules. [Kuosmanen et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009418)
- Barcode-based inference of phenotype switching in cancer resistance is recent prior art. [Whiting et al. (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12181405/)

Therefore neither the branching-process equations nor the biological claim that collateral sensitivity might steer resistance are new. The defensible candidate is a narrow data-to-decision bridge: validate whether a mutation-triggered collateral pulse's *lineage-resolved relapse probability* can be predicted on blinded held-out experiments, and show that the exact turnover/switch-aware forecast changes a decision relative to a net-growth-only controller at matched information and treatment exposure. No such test is performed here, and novelty/priority remain `UNKNOWN`.

## Prospective falsifier and costs

Use isogenic BCR–ABL1 WT, V299L, and selected compound-mutant cells, each tagged with neutral lineage barcodes. On training replicates, measure (b_i,d_i,\nu), post-pulse (q_i), and clone counts under dasatinib, foretinib, and vandetanib. Freeze the model and choose a pulse duration before running held-out replicate cultures (then a suitable syngeneic validation if the culture gate passes). Compare against continued dasatinib, best available mutation-specific TKI, fixed-duration collateral pulse, and an exposure-matched concurrent combination. Outcomes are barcode-resolved recurrence after washout and the calibration of predicted risk; a prespecified failure is a held-out rate outside the predictive interval or failure to outperform the strongest matched control. The data must separately price cell counts, independent cultures, single-cell imaging, barcode sequencing, compound-mutant sequencing, drug exposure, and follow-up. Report in-vitro dose attainability; mouse results do not certify a safe human regimen.

## Evidence status

- **Externally reported:** Zhao et al.'s mutation-specific collateral sensitivity, in-vivo burden/survival outcomes, and compound-mutant escape variation; published work on phenotype switching and resistance under time-varying treatment.
- **Internally derived exact model result:** the two-type backward equation, local sign threshold, same-net opposite-decision counterexample, and the expectation/union-bound risk certificate under their stated assumptions.
- **Unverified:** novelty, whether the needed parameters are estimable at clinically relevant clone counts, whether their estimates transport from organoids to marrow/tumors, and whether a pulse improves any in-vivo or clinical outcome.
- **Not performed:** no biological assay, simulation campaign, parameter fit, or empirical prediction test.
