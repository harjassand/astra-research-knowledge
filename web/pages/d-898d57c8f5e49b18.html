Abstract
For a pair of quantum channels with the same input space, we show that the possibility of approximation of one channel by post-processings of the other channel can be characterized by comparing the success probabilities for the two ensembles obtained as outputs for any ensemble on the input space coupled with an ancilla. This provides an operational interpretation to a natural extension of Le Cam’s deficiency to quantum channels. In particular, we obtain a version of the randomization criterion for quantum statistical experiments. The proofs are based on some properties of the diamond norm and its dual, which are of independent interest.
I Introduction
The theory of comparison of statistical experiments started in the works of Blackwell [1], who introduced a natural ordering of experiments in terms of the risks of optimal decision rules. This ordering was extended by Le Cam [2] into a deficiency measure on statistical experiments, expressing how well an experiment 𝒮\mathcal{S} can be approximated by randomizations of another experiment 𝒯\mathcal{T}.
Le Cam’s randomization criterion shows that deficiency also gives the maximal loss in the average payoffs of decision procedures, experienced when the experiment 𝒮\mathcal{S} is replaced by 𝒯\mathcal{T}. For an account on comparison of statistical experiments, see e.g. [3, 4].
An extension of Blackwell’s results for quantum experiments was first obtained by
Shmaya [5] in the framework of quantum information structures. In [6], a theory of comparison for both classical and quantum experiments is developed in terms of statistical morphisms. In both works, either additional entanglement or composition of the experiment with a complete set of states is required. Quantum versions of Le Cam’s randomization criterion were studied in [7, 8]. In particular, Matsumoto in [8] introduced a natural generalization of classical decision problems to quantum ones and proved a quantum randomization criterion in this setting. The main drawback of this approach is the lack of operational interpretation for quantum decision problems.
Comparison of channels can be obtained as an extension of the theory of comparison of experiments. A natural idea is the following: given two channels with the same input space, compare the two experiments emerging as outputs for a single input experiment. If the output experiment of the channel Ψ\Psi is always more informative than the output of the channel Φ\Phi, we say that Ψ\Psi is less noisy than Φ\Phi. Similar orderings were studied in e.g. [9, 10, 11]. For some more recent works and possible extensions to deficiency measures see e.g. [12, 13].
In the quantum setting, it is possible to use a stronger ordering, namely to compare experiments on the input space coupled with an ancilla. As it turns out, for quantum channels, Ψ\Psi is less noisy in this stronger sense if and only if Φ\Phi is a post-processing of Ψ\Psi. In fact, it is enough to compare guessing probabilities for ensembles of states. This remarkable result was first obtained by Chefles in [14], based on [5]. It was extended and refined in [6], in particular it was proved that no entanglement in the input ensemble is needed. Some applications were already found in [15, 16, 17, 13].
The aim of the present work is to establish an approximate version of these results, which may be called the randomization criterion for quantum channels. More precisely, we study an extension of Le Cam’s deficiency for quantum channels, based on the diamond norm. It is shown that deficiency can be characterized by comparing success probabilities for output ensembles, with respect to the success probability of the input ensemble. These results are then applied to statistical experiments and a quantum randomization criterion is proved in terms of success probabilities.
The diamond norm naturally appears as a distinguishability norm for quantum channels [18]. As it was observed in [19], this norm can be defined using only the order structure given by the cone of completely positive maps and has a similar relation to the set of channels as the trace norm has to the set of states. We also show that the dual norm on positive elements can be expressed as the optimal success probability for a certain ensemble. These properties provide a very convenient framework for proving our results and are of independent interest.
II Notations and preliminaries
If not stated otherwise, the full proofs of all results can be found in [20].
Throughout the paper, all Hilbert spaces are finite dimensional.
If ℋ\mathcal{H} is a Hilbert space, we fix an orthonormal basis {|ei⟩,i=1,…,dim(ℋ)}\{|e_{i}\rangle,i=1,\dots,\dim(\mathcal{H})\} in ℋ\mathcal{H}.
We will denote the algebra of linear operators on ℋ\mathcal{H} by B⁡(ℋ)B(\mathcal{H}), the set of positive operators by B​(ℋ)+B(\mathcal{H})^{+} and
the real vector space of self-adjoint elements by Bh​(ℋ)B_{h}(\mathcal{H}).
The set of states, or density operators, on ℋ\mathcal{H} will be denoted by
𝔖⁡(ℋ):={σ∈B​(ℋ)+,Tr​σ=1}.\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):=\{\sigma\in B(\mathcal{H})^{+},\ \mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma=1\}.
Let ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}) denote the real vector space of Hermitian linear maps B⁡(ℋ)→B⁡(𝒦)B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{K}). The set ℒ​(ℋ,𝒦)+\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})^{+} of completely positive maps forms a closed convex cone in ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}) which is pointed and generating. With this cone, ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}) becomes an ordered vector space. We will denote the corresponding order by ≤\leq. An element of ℒ​(ℋ,𝒦)+\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})^{+} that preserves trace is usually called a channel. We will denote the set of all channels by 𝒞⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}).
For ϕ∈ℒ⁡(ℋ,ℋ)\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}), we define
s⁡(ϕ)=∑i,j⟨ei,ϕ⁡(|ei⟩​⟨ej|)​ej⟩,s(\phi)=\sum_{i,j}\langle e_{i},\phi(|e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{j}|)e_{j}\rangle,
for some orthonormal basis |ei⟩|e_{i}\rangle of ℋ\mathcal{H}. It is easy to see that ss defines a linear functional s:ℒ⁡(ℋ,ℋ)→ℝs:\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R} and for all ϕ∈ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}), ψ∈ℒ⁡(𝒦,ℋ)\psi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H}), s⁡(ψ∘ϕ)=s⁡(ϕ∘ψ)s(\psi\circ\phi)=s(\phi\circ\psi).
We now identify the dual space of ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}) with ℒ⁡(𝒦,ℋ)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H}), where duality is given by
⟨ψ,ϕ⟩=s⁡(ψ∘ϕ),ϕ∈ℒ⁡(ℋ,𝒦),ψ∈ℒ⁡(𝒦,ℋ).\langle\psi,\phi\rangle=s(\psi\circ\phi),\qquad\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}),\ \psi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H}).
Note that the tracelike property of ss implies that we have ⟨ϕ,ψ⟩=⟨ψ,ϕ⟩\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,\phi\rangle and
⟨ϕ,ξ∘ψ⟩=⟨ϕ∘ξ,ψ⟩=⟨ψ∘ϕ,ξ⟩.\langle\phi,\xi\circ\psi\rangle=\langle\phi\circ\xi,\psi\rangle=\langle\psi\circ\phi,\xi\rangle.
The dual cone of positive functionals satisfies
(ℒ​(ℋ,𝒦)+)∗\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})^{+})^{*}
:={ψ∈ℒ(𝒦,ℋ),⟨ψ,ϕ⟩≥0,∀ϕ∈ℒ(ℋ,𝒦)+}\displaystyle:=\{\psi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H}),\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\geq 0,\forall\phi\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})^{+}\}

so that, in this sense, the cone of completely positive maps is self-dual.

Let us denote

It is of course possible to use the Choi representation with this duality, but for our purposes it is mostly more convenient to work with the spaces of mappings.

IV The main result
Let Φ∈𝒞⁡(ℋ,𝒦)\Phi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}) and Ψ∈𝒞⁡(ℋ,𝒦′)\Psi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}). Similarly to Le Cam’s deficiency for statistical experiments, we may define

δ⁡(Φ,Ψ)=infα∈𝒞⁡(𝒦′,𝒦)‖Φ−α∘Ψ‖⋄.\delta(\Phi,\Psi)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\mathcal{K})}\|\Phi-\alpha\circ\Psi\|_{\diamond}.
Since 𝒞⁡(𝒦′,𝒦)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\mathcal{K}) is convex and compact, the infimum is attained, in particular, δ⁡(Φ,Ψ)=0\delta(\Phi,\Psi)=0 if and only if Φ=α∘Ψ\Phi=\alpha\circ\Psi for some α∈𝒞⁡(𝒦′,𝒦)\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\mathcal{K}). V The randomization criterion for quantum experiments
A quantum statistical experiment is a pair 𝒯=(ℋ,{ρθ,θ∈Θ})\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{H},\{\rho_{\theta},\ \theta\in\Theta\}), where ρθ∈𝔖⁡(ℋ)\rho_{\theta}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}) for all θ∈Θ\theta\in\Theta and Θ\Theta is an arbitrary set of parameters. Any experiment can be
viewed as the set of possible states of some physical system, determined by some prior information on the true state. Note that this definition contains also classical statistical experiments on finite sample spaces, which can be identified with diagonal density matrices.
Based on the outcome of a measurement on the system, a decision jj is chosen from a (finite) set DD of decisions. This procedure, or a decision rule, is represented by a POVM on ℋ\mathcal{H} with outcomes in DD. The performance of a decision rule is assessed by a payoff function, which in our case is a map g:Θ×D→ℝ+g:\Theta\times D\to\mathbb{R}^{+}, representing the payoff
obtained if j∈Dj\in D is chosen while the true state is ρθ\rho_{\theta}. The average payoff of the decision rule MM at θ∈Θ\theta\in\Theta is computed as
P𝒯​(θ,M,g)=∑j∈Dgθ,j​Tr​ρθ​Mj.P_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta,M,g)=\sum_{j\in D}g_{\theta,j}\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_{\theta}M_{j}.
The next theorem is the celebrated Le Cam’s randomization criterion for classical statistical experiments.
Theorem 3.
[2] Let 𝒯\mathcal{T} and 𝒮\mathcal{S} be classical statistical experiments. Then the following are equivalent.
(i)
Tor any decision space (D,g)(D,g) and any decision rule MM for 𝒮\mathcal{S}, there is some decision rule NN for 𝒯\mathcal{T} such that
supθ∈Θ[P𝒮​(θ,M,g)−P𝒯​(θ,N,g)−ϵ​maxd​|g⁡(θ,d)|]≤0.\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left[P_{\mathcal{S}}(\theta,M,g)-P_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta,N,g)-\epsilon\max_{d}|g(\theta,d)|\right]\leq 0.
(ii)
There is some channel α\alpha such that
supθ‖σθ−α⁡(ρθ)‖1≤2​ϵ\sup_{\theta}\|\sigma_{\theta}-\alpha(\rho_{\theta})\|_{1}\leq 2\epsilon
As it was proved in [21], Theorem 3 does not hold for quantum experiments, even if we assume that one experiment is classical. The quantum randomization criterion proved in [8] is based on an extension of the classical decision spaces to quantum ones. These are defined as pairs (𝒟,G)(\mathcal{D},G), where 𝒟\mathcal{D} is a Hilbert space and G:Θ→B⁡(𝒟)G:\Theta\to B(\mathcal{D}) a function. Decision procedures are given by channels
ϕ∈𝒞⁡(ℋ,𝒟)\phi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D}) and the average payoff is computed as
P𝒯​(θ,ϕ,G)=Tr​ϕ​(ρθ)​G​(θ).P_{\mathcal{T}}(\theta,\phi,G)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\phi(\rho_{\theta})G(\theta).
With this extension, Theorem 3 holds also for quantum experiments, but an operational interpretation of the quantum decision problems is not clear.
The aim of the present section is to apply Theorem 1 to prove a quantum randomization criterion, formulated in terms of optimal guessing probabilities of some ensembles. In the case ϵ=0\epsilon=0, a similar result was obtained in [6].

VI Concluding remarks
We proposed and proved a version of the randomization criterion for quantum channels. For this, we used some properties of the diamond norm and its dual that can be obtained from the order structure given by completely positive maps. This suggests the possibility to apply similar methods to more general situations.
For example, one may assume some structure in the channels, obtaining similar results for more specific quantum protocols, such as quantum combs or testers, [22, 23]. Another interesting possibility is to consider more general cones of positive maps, see [24] for some results in this direction. But it is also possible to study more general kinds of processings: instead of post-processings where only the output is processed, we may define deficiency in terms of pre-processings. In the special case of POVMs regarded as a special kind of channels, this leads to an approximate version of the ordering of POVMs by cleanness, introduced in [25]. The corresponding randomization criterion will be investigated in a forthcoming paper, see also [24].
More generally, the processing can consist of a combination of pre- and post-processing, also allowing some correlations between input and output systems, either classical or quantum. It seems that all these situations can be treated within the suggested framework.
Following the work of Raginsky [12], one can also define a deficiency measure based on more general distances, such as some generalized relative entropies.
Another challenging problem is the extension of these results to infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces, or to general von Neumann algebras.
Although the methods used in [19, 24] rely on finite dimensions, it seems plausible that the useful properties of the norms can be extended also to this case. All these problems are left for future work.
