1. Complete ansatz and its exact nullspace

Let u: R^2 -> R^2, A=Du with row-major coordinates (a,b,c,d), and write the
six compatible second derivatives as

 z=(u1_xx,u1_xy,u1_yy,u2_xx,u2_xy,u2_yy),
 B1=(z0,z1,z3,z4), B2=(z1,z2,z4,z5).

The vector space of scalar polynomials of bidegree (2,2) in (A,z) has
dimension dim Sym^2(R^4) dim Sym^2(R^6)=10*21=210. There are no coefficient
symmetry assumptions in this count. In particular, it includes every local
homogeneous A^2 H^2 polynomial correction on compatible planar second jets.

For component i, the Euler operator is

 Ei(L)=-Dx L_(ui_x)-Dy L_(ui_y)+Dx^2 L_(ui_xx)
                  +DxDy L_(ui_xy)+Dy^2 L_(ui_yy).

There is no factor two on the mixed term, because ui_xy is one independent
coordinate of the symmetric Hessian. Product-rule total derivatives use all
compatible third and fourth derivatives. They produce an integer matrix E
with 696 rows and 210 columns.

This formal Euler kernel is the relevant integral nullspace. If Ei(L)=0,
differentiate the integral along A0+D(t phi), D^2(t phi); integration by parts
shows it is constant, and the t=0 value vanishes. Conversely, integral
invariance under all compactly supported perturbations implies the Euler
identity, and arbitrary finite compatible jets are realized by local smooth
polynomials multiplied by a cutoff. The same local argument works inside
a periodic cell. Thus the finite coefficient calculation has the required
variational meaning; it is not a sampled-field test.

There are 80 homogeneous cubic polynomial one-form coefficients on R^4.
Their derivatives are closed two-forms with homogeneous quadratic
coefficients. The kernel consists exactly of gradients of homogeneous
quartic scalar polynomials, of dimension C(7,3)=35. Polynomial radial
homotopy proves both assertions: for a closed homogeneous quadratic two-form
Omega, alpha=(1/4)i_Euler Omega satisfies d alpha=Omega; a closed cubic
one-form is similarly the differential of a quartic scalar. Therefore the
closed quadratic two-form space has dimension 80-35=45.

The pullback Omega_A(B1,B2) is injective, even without closedness. Its six
Hessian minors are

 z0 z2-z1^2,       z0 z4-z1 z3,       z0 z5-z1 z4,
 z1 z4-z2 z3,      z1 z5-z2 z4,       z3 z5-z4^2.

Unique monomials z0z2,z0z4,z0z5,z2z3,z1z5,z3z5 show linear independence.
Consequently Omega is unique for a given pullback density. Its primitive
alpha is not unique: it is determined modulo a quartic scalar differential.

2. Complete symmetry reduction for the isotropic heat section

For F=alpha |A|^4+beta (det A)^2, define

 D_F(A,z)=D^2F(A)[B1,B1]+D^2F(A)[B2,B2].

The audited certificate is D_F+Omega_A(B1,B2)>=0 for all A,z, with Omega a
closed homogeneous quadratic two-form. This permits arbitrary pointwise
nonnegativity at first; it does not assume an SOS Gram.

Under T(A)=L A R^T, with L,R orthogonal, compatible Hessians transform to
H'_i=sum_j L_ij R H_j R^T. Accordingly B'_i=sum_k R_ik T(B_k), and the
correction transforms by the TWISTED action

 Omega -> det(R) T^*Omega.

The determinant sign is essential. Averaging over O(2)xO(2) preserves
compatible jets, pointwise positivity, homogeneous degree, and closedness.
The averaged form is left O(2) invariant, right SO(2) invariant, and odd
under a right reflection. Rotation Lie equations plus one reflection in
each O(2) factor characterize the whole group, not a finite subgroup.

In the full 60-coefficient form space, these conditions and closure have
rank 58. The independent checker verifies this rank by exact modular
elimination and checks two independent exact kernel vectors. Thus the
complete averaged space, over the real numbers, is precisely

 Omega=d(k s zeta+l j theta),
 s=|A|^2, j=det A,
 theta=-c da-d db+a dc+b dd,
 zeta=-b da+a db-d dc+c dd.

If the initial certificate instead uses a constant symmetric positive

Its average is gamma I, gamma=tr G/2>0. Dividing the averaged certificate by
gamma reduces to the ordinary-heat case above. This extension is valid for

5. Primary theorem comparisons and priority

Ball, Currie and Olver (1981), Theorem 3.1 (printed page 140), treats general
finite-order null Lagrangians as divergences. Theorem 4.1 (page 149) gives
the Jacobian characterization for L depending exclusively on kth-order
derivatives. Our L(Du,D^2u) depends on two orders, so invoking Theorem 4.1
alone does not establish its 45-dimensional classification. A further
reduction would need justification. The theorem pages were visually read
from the original author-hosted scan.

Olver and Sivaloganathan (1988), Lemmas 5-6 and Theorem 7 (pages 393-394),
give the closed-form/divergence characterization for unconstrained first-
order fields. Their introduction mentions a higher-order extension. Treating
Du as four independent fields would change the admissible class, so it
does not automatically prove completeness under compatible Hessian jets.
These classical results motivate the construction; the independent exact
kernel calculation establishes this specialization. Historical novelty of
that specialization has not been cleared.

Calderon (1973) proves that every nonnegative biquadratic form with one
binary variable group is a sum of squares of bilinear forms. The original
publisher abstract states the result; its full article was not obtained.
The author's 8-by-8 rank-one-convex lift for all nine left-SO(2) quartic
coefficients is an application of this established theorem. It was already
credited and used in capital FE. Hanselka and Sinn (2017) provides a primary
univariate matrix-positivity formulation. None of this implies the arbitrary
four-by-six heat biquadratic is SOS.

Bruno and Pasqualotto, arXiv:2608.06367v1 (2026), Sections 2-3, already uses
heat flow and exact quadratic two-forms. Appendix A handles the three-
coefficient left-and-right SO(2) class by splitting Q=a W_t+mu(det A)^2,
using heat for W_t and polyconvexity for the determinant term. It does not
assert heat-only monotonicity for the whole class. Its scope is smaller than
the nine-coefficient left-SO(2) target. The targeted search did not establish
priority for 12+8sqrt(3), the three-evaluation dual, or the 45-dimensional
specialization; it is not a comprehensive historical clearance.
