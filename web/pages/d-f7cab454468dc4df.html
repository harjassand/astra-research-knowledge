# Controlled-menu exact conversion: independent derivation and obstruction

Status (2026-10-06): finite-output lemma, fixed-length target corollary, and arbitrary integrable fixed-target stopping-policy extension independently reconstructed below. The extension is for a FIXED target G, not an adaptively selected target menu. All models are supplied/known. ## Exact interface

States i=0,...,m-1 are finite. A source action a belongs to finite A. Its observation law P_i^a has a finite alphabet (which may depend on a), and is strictly positive on that alphabet in every state. Conditional on the past and chosen action, the observation is independent of the past with law P_i^a. Actions are chosen without knowledge of the state. The menu collectively distinguishes every ordered pair. Write D_ij^a = KL(P_i^a||P_j^a).

For a fixed randomized design q, reveal a and then x; E(q)_i(a,x)=q(a)P_i^a(x) on q's support. Its directed KL is d_ij(q)=sum_a q(a)D_ij^a. Choose a common identifying q_0, e.g. uniform. For every candidate k choose a fixed q_k satisfying d_kj(q_k)>0 for all j != k. q_k need not distinguish two states both different from k. Mutual absolute continuity implies d_jk(q_k)>0 whenever d_kj(q_k)>0.

The target is any finite common-support experiment H_i(y)>0. Set lambda_ij(y)=log(H_i(y)/H_j(y)), Delta=max_ijy |lambda_ij(y)|, and

B_i(H;q_i)=sum_y H_i(y) max_{j != i} [lambda_ij(y)]_+ / d_ij(q_i).

## Finite-output exact conversion lemma

For the fixed source menu, q_0, and q_k, there are C<infinity and eta_0>0 such that for every H and 0<eta<=eta_0 an exact controlled-source simulator exists with, simultaneously in every state,

E_i N <= B_i(H;q_i) + C[sqrt(1+B_i(H;q_i)) + log(1/eta) + eta(1+Delta)].

C is independent of the number of target outputs and H. It depends on all fixed candidate designs, especially reverse d_ik(q_k), and may be large. This is not a constant uniform over designs approaching loss of identification.

### Proof primitives reconstructed

1. Classification: under q_0, ML classification after n observations has error at most (m-1) exp(-c n). For each i,j, the event likelihood_j>=likelihood_i has probability <=E_i exp(-S_ij/2)=(sum_{a,x}sqrt(E(q_0)_i(a,x)E(q_0)_j(a,x)))^n. Each coefficient is <1. A union bound gives c>0.

2. Passage: under true k and fixed q_k, bounded iid increments of S_kj have mean d_kj>0. For thresholds b_j, put t=max_j [b_j]_+/d_kj. Let tau be first simultaneous passage of all thresholds. For integer n>=t,
P_k(tau>n)<=sum_j P_k(S_kj(n)<b_j)<=sum_j exp(-c_j(n-t)^2/n),
by Hoeffding. Summing n from ceil(t) gives E_k tau<=t+C sqrt(1+t)+C: for n-t<=t the Gaussian sum is O(sqrt(t)); beyond t it is geometric. A one-dimensional version also holds under true i for S_ik under q_k.

3. Bounded-diameter repair under q_0: let A_n be the confusion matrix of the classifier. Its row norm satisfies ||A_n-I||_infinity<=C exp(-cn). For n large it is invertible and ||A_n^-1-I||<=C' exp(-cn). For any common-support R, choose n=O(1+Delta(R)) so exp(Delta(R))||A_n^-1-I||<=1. Then K=A_n^-1 R has row sums one and entries
K_i(y)>=R_i(y)[1-exp(Delta(R))||A_n^-1-I||]>=0.
Taking n q_0 observations, classifying as k, and drawing y from K_k reproduces R exactly, since A_n K=R. This is a finite matrix operation given exact probabilities.

### Construction, errors, exactness, cost

Classify using q_0 for ell=O(log(1/eta)) observations so P_i(K!=i)<=eta. Given K=k, propose y~H_k using independent randomization. On fresh observations from q_k define

success: all S_kj >= [lambda_kj(y)]_+ + g, j!=k;
reject: ANY S_jk >= g, j!=k;
g=log(1/eta).

Stop at first success or rejection, rejecting ties. The positive-part success thresholds only strengthen the likelihood condition and avoid negative initial boundaries. Under true k, success passage is integrable by primitive 2. Under true i!=k, rejection is no later than the one-dimensional S_ik passage, so E_i V_{k,y}<=C(1+g), uniformly in y and Delta. This direct rejection is essential: Li–Liu's original rejection asks some alternate state to beat every other state and need not terminate under q_k when two alternatives have identical q_k laws.

Under true k, exp(S_jk) is a nonnegative mean-one martingale. Ville plus a union bound bounds false rejection by (m-1)eta; thus p_k(k,y)>=1-(m-1)eta. On successful verification S_ki>=lambda_ki(y)+g. Stopped likelihood change of measure gives
p_i(k,y)<=eta H_i(y)/H_k(y) p_k(k,y), i!=k.
This identity is valid although q_k depends on the candidate: conditional on the candidate, the verifier design is the same under both states; action randomization factors cancel.

Let A_i(k)=P_i(K=k), r_i(k,y)=A_i(k)H_k(y)p_i(k,y). Thin successful outputs by the independent probability a_eta=(1-eta)/(1+(m-1)eta), giving accepted mass Q_i(y)=a_eta sum_k r_i(k,y). Then
Q_i(y)<=(1-eta)H_i(y),
Q_i(y)>=a_eta(1-eta)(1-(m-1)eta)H_i(y).
Consequently the rejection/thinning flag has mass delta_i=1-sum_y Q_i(y) satisfying eta<=delta_i<=2m eta (for eta small).

Define R_i(y)=(H_i(y)-Q_i(y))/delta_i. It is a common-support probability law; eta H_i(y)<=H_i(y)-Q_i(y)<=H_i(y) gives
Delta(R)<=Delta(H)+log(2m/eta).
Run primitive 3 on a fresh q_0 tail only on the flag. The final law is exactly Q_i(y)+delta_i R_i(y)=H_i(y).

Correct-candidate cost: primitive 2 with b_j=[lambda_ij(y)]_++g gives t<=max_j [lambda_ij(y)]_+/d_ij(q_i)+C g. Averaging over y and using Jensen gives B_i+C[sqrt(1+B_i)+g]. Wrong-candidate cost is <=C(1+g), and occurs with probability <=eta. Classification cost is O(g). Repair costs delta_i O(1+Delta(R))<=C eta(1+Delta+g). Absorb eta g into a constant to obtain the displayed inequality. All components are integrable.

For formal fresh tails, sample an iid row array containing one observation from every source action and independent randomizers; a chosen action reveals only its component of that row. Unused rows after a stopping time remain conditionally iid under each state. This supplies pasting without assuming the candidate's design remains fixed across the whole simulator.

## Sharp first-order conversion of fixed target samples

Let G be one finite full-support pairwise identified target experiment. Define

rho_i^*=min_{q in simplex(A)} max_{j!=i} I_ij(G)/d_ij(q).

Equivalently, solve the LP minimizing sum_a x_a subject to sum_a x_a D_ij^a >= I_ij(G) for every j!=i, x_a>=0. A minimizer exists; q_i=x/sum x has all required d_ij>0. There is ONE controlled source policy reproducing n iid G samples exactly and satisfying E_i N<=rho_i^* n+C sqrt(n), simultaneously in all states.

Proof of upper bound: choose these q_i once. For H=G^n, the output log ratio is S_ij^G(n)=n I_ij(G)+M_ij(n), with E_i|M_ij(n)|<=sqrt(n Var_i(log G_i/G_j)). Therefore B_i(H;q_i)<=rho_i^* n+C sqrt(n); Delta(H)<=n L_G. Apply the lemma with eta=eta_0/n; log n and eta Delta are absorbed into C sqrt(n).

Lower bound: any exact controlled-source reproduction of G^n has stopped-record KL
sum_a E_i N_a D_ij^a >= n I_ij(G)
for every j, by likelihood cancellation, optional expectation of bounded increments, and data processing. Taking x_a=E_i N_a/n in the LP gives E_i N>=rho_i^* n exactly. This proves optimality of the coefficient, including simultaneous attainability. The full arbitrary stopping-policy bound follows from the dyadic construction recorded below.

Computable certificate: give source laws, target laws, a feasible x and LP dual weights z_j>=0 such that sum_j z_j D_ij^a<=1 for every a. Primal and dual objective equality sum_a x_a=sum_j z_j I_ij(G) verifies rho_i^*. No simulator execution is needed to validate this information lower bound.

## Uniform extension to all integrable fixed-G stopping policies

**Theorem.** For a fixed target G as above, every finite-output stopping G-policy (T,Z), integrable under every state, has an exact controlled-source reproduction with

E_i N <= rho_i^* E_i T + C sqrt(E_i T), all i,

where C depends only on the source menu, fixed optimizing q_i, common identifying q_0, and G. It is uniform over the target policy and its finite output alphabet. The coefficient is optimal by the fixed-n lower bound already proved.

**Bounded-policy derivation.** If T<=M, its terminal experiment H has common support. State-independent behavioral decision kernels at each finite target signal history make this explicit: every positive terminal-output probability contains a positive signal-history summand under every state. Let S_ij(T)=I_ij(G)T+M_ij(T). At the stopped sigma-field, likelihood change of measure gives H_j(Z)/H_i(Z)=E_i[exp(-S_ij(T))|Z]. Conditional Jensen implies lambda_ij(Z)<=E_i[S_ij(T)|Z]. Convexity of positive part followed by a finite maximum bounds
B_i(H;q_i)<=E_i max_j [S_ij(T)]_+/d_ij(q_i)
<=rho_i^* E_i T + sum_j E_i|M_ij(T)|/d_ij(q_i).
For bounded T, martingale differences are orthogonal and their predictable inclusion indicator is 1{T>=t}, so E_i M_ij(T)^2=Var_i(log G_i/G_j)E_i T. Cauchy–Schwarz yields B_i<=rho_i^* E_i T+C sqrt(E_i T). The stopped log ratio is bounded by M L_G, whence Delta(H)<=M L_G. Apply the finite-H lemma with eta=eta_0/M, absorb sqrt(1+B), log M, and eta Delta into C sqrt M. Thus every bounded policy has cost rho_i^* E_i T+C sqrt M. Requiring at least one source observation can be imposed by appending one ignored sample, absorbed in C sqrt M.

**Arbitrary integrable-policy construction.** Replace the target's auxiliary randomness by state-independent behavioral kernels q_h at each finite signal history h, each deciding CONTINUE or STOP with output z. This representation preserves the joint laws in all states: for a given signal history the state likelihood factor depends only on that history, so conditional auxiliary decision probabilities are state-independent. Copy its time-zero decision exactly by free auxiliary randomization; if it stops, output z with N=0.

For continued paths use blocks of target lengths L_k=2^k and checkpoints s_{k-1}=2^k-1. At each checkpoint history h, the finite block continuation stops at tau<=L_k and reports either (STOP,tau,new signal string,z), or (CONTINUE,new signal string of length L_k). The report includes the complete signal history needed to resume. Its support is common, and its bounded conversion uses the SAME q_i and source-dependent constants for every h and k. Fix one such simulator for each of the countably many finite (k,h). Their report laws exactly equal the target block-report laws in each state, and their costs obey E_i N_{k,h}<=rho_i^* E_i tau_{k,h}+C sqrt L_k. They use at least one observation.

The source runs the matching (k,h) simulator on a fresh row-array tail, records its report, and either stops or advances to the next history and block. Selection of h is measurable at the previous source stopping time. The stopped-array fresh-tail law supplies the target unconditional continuation law under each fixed true state; the source does not need to know that state. Induction in k proves equality of every reach-history probability and every complete stopped report with the target process. In particular, the probability of entering stage k is exactly P_i(T>s_{k-1}). It tends to zero, so infinitely many stages occur with probability zero in every state. Summing the disjoint stopped report probabilities proves exact terminal output equality.

Write D_k=(T wedge s_k)-(T wedge s_{k-1}); these nonnegative target block lengths telescope to T. Conditioning on exact reach histories, and then summing by Tonelli, gives
E_i N<=rho_i^* E_i T+C sum_{k>=0}2^(k/2)P_i(T>2^k-1).
Since T is integer, T>2^k-1 iff T>=2^k. Pathwise,
sum_{k:2^k<=T}2^(k/2)<=sqrt(2)/(sqrt(2)-1) sqrt T.
Tonelli and Jensen therefore bound the remainder by C' sqrt(E_i T), even when T has no higher moments. No additive constant is needed: immediate stops cost zero and every entered block is paid by its reach probability. Source stopping times increase stage by stage; their limit is a stopping time since {N<=n}=intersection_k{sigma_k<=n}. The positive-duration convention ensures nontermination means N=infinity. Every terminal report is measurable at the stopping time, and the bound gives integrability in every state. This completes the uniform theorem.

**Boundary of the extension.** A controlled TARGET menu does not automatically inherit the same coefficient by replacing KL with expected action occupancies: expectation of a maximum of action-dependent drift can differ linearly from maximum expected drift. The safe sufficient factor max_{j,a} D_ij(G^a)/d_ij(q_i) follows from a pathwise drift bound, but optimal universal controlled-to-controlled conversion is not established here.

## Exact counterexample to pairwise-max menu dominance

States 0,1,2. Source binary sensors A and B have P(X=1):
A=(4/5,1/5,4/5), B=(4/5,4/5,1/5).
The target has 3 outputs; row i assigns 2/3 to output i and 1/6 to each other output. Every observation law has full support. Put D=(3/5)log 4 and g=(1/2)log 4=5D/6.

Every directed pair i!=j has max_a D_ij^a=D>g=I_ij(G). Nonetheless any exact reproduction of n target observations must obey in state 0:
D E_0 N_A>=ng, D E_0 N_B>=ng,
so E_0 N>=5n/3. Thus strict pairwise maxima fail to imply even same-budget reproduction of these particular finite target outputs.

The LP gives rho=(5/3,5/6,5/6). Witnesses: state0 x_A=x_B=5/6, dual z_1=z_2=1/D. State1 x_A=5/6,x_B=0 and dual z_0=1/D,z_2=0. State2 exchanges A and B. Candidate designs q_0=(1/2,1/2), q_1=(1,0), q_2=(0,1) certify attainable coefficients via the lemma.

For one fixed iid randomized design q=(u,1-u), rho_0=(5/6)/min(u,1-u), rho_1=(5/6)/u, rho_2=(5/6)/(1-u). Under the uniform state prior the average coefficient is
(5/18)[1/min(u,1-u)+1/u+1/(1-u)],
whose unique minimum is 5/3 at u=1/2. The adaptive-state coefficient average is 10/9, a factor 3/2 improvement over the best single iid mixture. Worst-state adaptive and best fixed-mixture coefficients are both 5/3: the gain here is statewise and Bayesian average, not minimax. Arbitrary time-varying or randomized open-loop schedules are a larger comparator class and are NOT covered by this fixed-mixture comparison.

## Classical testing certificate and complete short proof

For δ-correct identification, max_i P_i(hat_i!=i)<=δ, set C_i=max_q min_j d_ij(q)>0. Every adaptive test has E_iT>=kl(1-δ,δ)/C_i: stopped KL gives sum_a E_iN_aD_ij^a>=kl(P_i(hat_i=i),P_j(hat_i=i))>=kl(1-δ,δ), and normalize expected occupancies to a design q.

A simultaneous matching policy can be reconstructed without Chernoff's continuous re-estimation: put L=log(1/δ). Classify under uniform design for ceil(L^(2/3)) observations. If classified as k, take ceil((L+L^(4/5))/C_k) fresh samples from a maximizing design q_k. Check the cumulative all-rival likelihood boundary A=log(2(m-1)/δ) once at the end; accept a winning state if one exists. Otherwise discard the old data and run a fresh uniform-design MSPRT at the same boundary. First-stage wrong acceptance and fallback errors are each <=δ/2 by stopped likelihood change of measure. Classification failure is exp(-cL^(2/3)); conditional on correct classification the initial data contribute at worst -O(L^(2/3)), while main-phase mean has slack L^(4/5), so Hoeffding bounds fallback probability by exp(-cL^(3/5)). The fresh fallback expected cost is O(L), by summing its Hoeffding passage tail. Hence E_i T=L/C_i+o(L), simultaneously. This is a proof of a known coefficient, not a new discovery.

For the explicit two-sensor family, C=(D/2,D,D). Uniform-prior adaptive testing coefficient averages 4/(3D), best fixed iid mixture averages 2/D: again factor 3/2. Computing the exact-output LP rather than only pairwise maximal KL exposes the shared-budget obstruction.
