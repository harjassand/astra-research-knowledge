# Detectability amplification on the bounded-degree BMVZ family

Status: **scoped obstruction; no end-to-end sparse positive encoding.** The
detectability lemma gives the expected contraction and constant-energy
amplification for the bounded-degree 5-local input. On a valid BMVZ hard
subfamily, the canonical global positive terms acquire exponentially many
matrix entries in one row. A branch encoding makes the logical test 1-sparse,
but its exact image-enforcer has an exponential row lower bound in the
seedwise direct-branch architecture, unchanged by any number of clean
spectator ancillas. Active workspace or a different common isometry remains
open.

## Source and instance interface

Primary DL source used here: Anshu--Arad--Vidick (AAV), *A simple proof of the
detectability lemma and spectral gap amplification*, arXiv:1602.01210v3,
Lemma 2, Corollary 3, and Lemma 4 (Gao's sequential-projector bound).
BMVZ, arXiv:2510.01333v1, supplies the
bounded-degree input in Claim 8.1 and its equitable coloring in Theorem 8.3 /
Corollary 8.4, the layer amplifier in Appendix A (A.1--A.3), and the iterated
amplifier in Theorem 7.3. The 5-local Clifford seed vectors are from BJSW,
arXiv:1604.02804v1, Theorem 2, equations (4)--(7). The inherited interfaces
are N11 and N26; this note does not re-prove either card.

BMVZ Claim 8.1 starts with m unweighted 5-local projections on n0=poly(n)
qubits, with at most 7 terms on each qubit. The term-overlap graph therefore
has maximum degree at most 35 (the paper uses the conservative 5*7 bound),
and BMVZ obtains g<=36 balanced disjoint layers. Each projector has norm one.
Write H0=sum_i P_i and H=H0/m. If the unnormalized NO lower bound is b, then
the normalized minimum eigenvalue is gamma=b/m. For projectors with norm
bound Lambda=1, the DL parameter below is the unnormalized energy m*gamma=b.
If instead 0<=h_i<=Lambda I, replace h_i by its support projector Q_i; the
projector-sum gap is at least the h-sum gap divided by Lambda. This is the
only norm conversion used here.

## Contraction and gap costs

For projectors Q_1,...,Q_M, let each Q_i fail to commute with at most d_nc
other terms and let D_DL=(I-Q_M)...(I-Q_1), in any order. For d_nc>=1,
AAV Lemma 2 gives, for
phi=D_DL psi and nonzero phi,

    ||phi||^2 <= 1 / (1 + epsilon_phi/d_nc^2),
    epsilon_phi = <phi|sum_i Q_i|phi> / ||phi||^2.

If d_nc=0, one sweep is the exact common-kernel projector. If d_nc>=1, the
common kernel G is nonzero, and the unnormalized projector sum has gap Gamma
above G, Corollary 3 therefore gives

    ||D_DL psi_perp||^2 <= 1/(1+Gamma/d_nc^2).

For the BMVZ 5-local input, use d_nc<=35 conservatively. For an average
Hamiltonian with normalized gap gamma=Gamma/M, the bound is
`1/(1+M*gamma/35^2)`, not `1/(1+gamma/35^2)`. The factor M is the normalization
cost. For a positive local sum H=sum h_i with 0<=h_i<=Lambda Q_i and a common
kernel, replace Gamma by Gamma_H/Lambda.

The BMVZ NO case need not be frustration free. If `lambda_min(H0)>=b>0`,
AAV Lemma 2 applied directly says `||D_DL psi||^2 <=
(1+b/d_nc^2)^(-1)` for every unit psi: the normalized filtered state, when
nonzero, still has energy at least b. Hence on NO instances
`F_q>= [1-(1+b/d_nc^2)^(-q)] I`. This is the relevant instance-level
contraction; the common-kernel statement is the stronger AGSP interface.

In the frustration-free case, after q sweeps the positive global filter

    F_q = I - (D_DL^q)^* D_DL^q

has exact kernel G and absolute gap at least
`1-(1+Gamma/d_nc^2)^(-q)`. Thus q=Theta(d_nc^2/Gamma) yields constant absolute
gap when Gamma/d_nc^2 is small; in terms of a normalized average gap this is
q=Theta(d_nc^2/(M*gamma)). A sweep uses M local projector applications and
depth at most 36 layers on this input, so q sweeps use O(qM) applications and
depth O(36q). The filter is positive and has norm at most one, but it is a
global operator. Its positive telescoping decomposition contains conditioned
prefixes of the earlier projectors, so it does not preserve constant support.
For a YES state of unnormalized energy at most a, AAV Lemma 4 applied to the
projector sequence repeated q times gives `<F_q><=4qa`; polynomial q
therefore preserves negligible completeness when a=2^-poly(n).

For BMVZ's one-shot layer amplifier, let `A_chi=product_{i in layer chi}
(I-P_i)` and `w_chi=m_chi/m`. On t copies it outputs

    H_DL(t) = I - sum_chi w_chi A_chi^(tensor t)
            = sum_chi w_chi (I-A_chi^(tensor t)).

Each summand is a positive projection. Appendix A.1--A.3 and the DL converse
give the source bound

    lambda_min(H_DL(t)) >= (w_min/(4g))
        * [1 - (1 + m*gamma/g^2)^(-t)].

For balanced layers with m>=2g and w_min>=1/(2g), this is at least
`(1/(8g^2))*[1-(1+b/g^2)^(-t)]` on a NO instance with unnormalized energy
b. It reaches a constant depending only on g when t=Theta(g^2/b). Here g<=36,
so inverse-polynomial b costs polynomial t. If a YES witness has unnormalized
energy at most a, its t-fold product witness has output energy at most
`t*w_max*a` by the commuting-layer union bound. With BMVZ's a=2^-poly(n) and
polynomial t, completeness remains negligible. The output has nt physical
qubits and g=O(1) global positive terms; each layer term touches at most
`min(n0,5*m_chi)*t` qubits, hence at most nt. The DL gap estimate itself is
independent of encoding size; the actual circuit description costs O(mt)
local projector applications.

This is the expected amplification trade: bounded overlap controls the
contraction, while the amplified positive tests act on a growing part of the
system. Constant colors do not make those tests local or row-sparse.

## Exact row-support test on the actual 5-local seed form

To make a concrete BMVZ-valid hard subfamily, start with its bounded-degree
5-local QMA-hard instance and add five isolated qubits with two terms
`P_phi=|phi><phi|` and `P_psi=|psi><psi|`. Take the BJSW five-qubit Clifford
stabilizer seeds

    phi = |-> |00> |10>,
    psi = (|000>-|011>-|101>-|110>)/2 |10>.

The first three factors in each displayed seed are the BJSW data vector and
the last two are the same clock pattern. Their overlap is
`<phi|psi>=1/(2*sqrt(2))`, so their rank-one projectors do not commute. They
have a common orthogonal complement of dimension at least 30, hence
`lambda_min(P_phi+P_psi)=0`. Adding them on a disjoint register leaves the
unnormalized ground energy and promise thresholds exactly unchanged, preserves
5-locality, and keeps the maximum per-qubit term incidence at max(7,2)=7.
They are separated into different colors by any proper coloring. This is a
polynomial-time, identity-preserving augmentation of the actual bounded-degree
hard family; it avoids assuming that a particular unmodified output circuit
already contains both gate types.

The vector phi has exactly two computational-basis support elements. For a
row z in that support, `I-P_phi` has exactly two nonzero entries: a nonzero
diagonal entry 1/2 and one off-diagonal entry of magnitude 1/2. Across t
disjoint copies, the row of `(I-P_phi)^(tensor t)` has 2^t entries, so the
positive layer projection `I-A_chi^(tensor t)` has at least `2^t-1`
off-diagonal entries in that row. Other disjoint projector factors cannot
reduce the count: choose any nonzero row for each; tensor supports multiply.
Thus the canonical positive terms of the BMVZ DL amplifier already fail
polynomial matrix-row sparsity for t=Theta(1/b). Exact entry evaluation still
takes polynomial time by multiplying the O(mt) constant-size local factors;
their polynomial-bit input coefficients give polynomial-bit products.
Enumerating all nonzeros takes exponential time. This separates succinct term
measurement from sparse row access.

The same calculation applies to BMVZ's derandomized tensor-product clauses.
For any listed walk f, put

    P_f = I - tensor_{j=1}^t (I-P_{f(j)}).

If the walk visits the phi seed L times, then an appropriate row has at least
`2^L-1` nonzeros. The expander path family contains every walk; from the seed
vertex and any neighbor, the back-and-forth walk visits it at least
`ceil(t/2)` times. Therefore an actual listed amplified clause has row support
at least `2^ceil(t/2)-1` (or more if other factors are non-diagonal).

There is also a row-support recurrence for the iterated BMVZ Theorem 7.3 hard
family, not just a one-round witness. Use its fixed amplification parameter s
(the source denotes it by t; the round uses 2s tensor factors). Let r_i be the
support of a distinguished row of a projection term at round i. The added
seed has r_0=2. At each round choose a walk alternating between that term and
one neighbor in its same-color expander; it contains s copies of the term.
For a projection P with a row of support r>=2, the same row of I-P has support
r: from P=P^2, `P_zz=sum_y |P_zy|^2`, and a nonzero off-diagonal entry forces
0<P_zz<1. The tensor product therefore has at least r^s entries, and
subtracting it from I cancels at most the diagonal. Consequently

    r_(i+1) >= r_i^s - 1,
    r_1 >= 2^s-1,
    log2(r_i) >= (s-1)^i  for i>=1.

The theorem chooses fixed s>2 and
`ell=ceil(6 log p(n)/log s)` rounds for inverse-polynomial input soundness
`1/p(n)`. Hence the distinguished term has row support at least
`2^(p(n)^kappa)` for `kappa=6 log_s(s-1)>0`, while the BMVZ output still has
polynomially many qubits, terms, and a polynomial-time term generator. This
proves that the **canonical positive term list** is not polynomial-row sparse
on this valid augmented hard family. It does not prove that the sum of all
terms has no cancellations or that no different sparse encoding exists.

## Common positive encoding and extra workspace

For the one-shot DL layer output, local seed decoders give an explicit common
isometry and a sparse positive logical test. Let U_chi diagonalize each
five-qubit projection in layer chi, extended over all t copies, and let D_chi
be the resulting diagonal projector A_chi^(tensor t). The added seed uses the
BJSW Clifford decoders; arbitrary fixed-size projectors can be diagonalized
in their constant-dimensional support, with gate precision charged at the
requested operator-norm accuracy.
With a g-dimensional orthogonal branch register, set

    V|v> = sum_chi sqrt(w_chi) |chi> tensor U_chi|v>,
    B = sum_chi |chi><chi| tensor (I-D_chi).

Then V*V=I, `0<=B<=I`, B is diagonal and 1-row-sparse, and
`V*BV=H_DL(t)` exactly. V has a polynomial-size product circuit of O(mt)
constant-size local decoders plus preparation of the rational weight state.
Entries of B are
0/1; the weight amplitudes require O(log m) input bits and a stated synthesis
precision if a circuit rather than exact algebraic amplitudes is required.

The missing operator is C. The natural `C=I-VV*` has gap one and the exact
kernel but is dense. More strongly, for the direct-branch isometry using the
BJSW seedwise Clifford decoders, every exact enforcer has exponentially many
entries per nonzero row. The added noncommuting pair lies in different colors.
On its isolated five-qubit register, the relative Clifford decoder W is
non-monomial: otherwise it would
map a computational basis state to a basis state and its overlap with the
other seed would be zero or one, contradicting `1/(2*sqrt(2))`. A nonmonomial
Clifford has maximum matrix entry at most `1/sqrt(2)`. Since the gadget is
disjoint from all other input terms, the layer decoder can be chosen
factorwise, and the relative decoder on the full one-copy register has
maximum entry mu<=1/sqrt(2). Across t copies its maximum entry is at most
`2^(-t/2)`.

For completeness, the row argument is architecture-specific but exact. A row
of V in branch chi, after normalization by sqrt(w_chi), is a row of U_chi.
Rows in the same branch are orthonormal; rows in distinct branches have inner
product at most `2^(-t/2)`. If a row of any C with CV=0 had support s and
`(s-1)*2^(-t/2)<1`, Gershgorin makes the Gram matrix of those V rows positive
definite, so they cannot have the linear dependence required by CV=0.
Therefore every nonzero row of C has support at least
`1+2^(t/2)`. The N11 gap condition `C>=gamma_C(I-VV*)`, gamma_C>0, ensures C
is nonzero. The bound does not use positivity; it applies to every exact
linear enforcer for this branch architecture.

Appending any number a of **clean spectator** qubits cannot help. If
`V'=V tensor |0^a>` and C' is positive with kernel ran(V') and
`C'>=gamma_C(I-V'V'^*)`, compress it to the spectator-zero principal block:

    C_0=(I tensor <0^a|) C' (I tensor |0^a>).

Then C_0 is positive, `ker(C_0)=ran(V)`, and
`C_0>=gamma_C(I-VV*)`. A principal compression cannot increase any row's
nonzero count, so a polynomial-row C' would contradict the lower bound for
C_0. This handles arbitrary clean spectator-workspace size, including
polynomially many qubits. Branch-dependent or actively computed workspace
changes V and is not covered; neither is a different non-branch common
isometry. Those remain live escape routes.

## L02 and N26 boundary

There is no direct composition with L02. Its repeated-game theorem quantifies
over arbitrary commuting-operator strategies and controls a threshold tail
using the question-law parameter sigma. DL instead needs a supplied finite
projector family, an overlap bound, a spectral-energy premise (and, for the
AGSP interpretation, a common exact kernel). The data do not identify sigma
with an operator gap, and L02 supplies no finite local verifier Hamiltonian,
common isometry, or positive sparse C. If a separate compiler supplied those
objects, the contraction estimates above could be applied, but the global
positive filter and the encoding obstruction would remain.

This is distinct from N26's normalized check-average bound
`c<=eta+(r/t)(1-eta)`. N26 assumes a positive termwise budget-one mixture of
effects each reading at most r independent syndrome bits, on the same
auxiliary space. The present obstruction is instead exponential **matrix-row
support** for named global tensor-product projectors and a direct-branch image
enforcer. It neither derives nor strengthens the N26 energy inequality, and it
does not cover a general positive sparse matrix or active sector-coupling
workspace.

## Decision and exact open gate

The DL route improves the absolute NO energy at polynomial cost, with
degree/overlap d_nc<=35 and norm-one 5-local projections. It preserves positive
global terms and negligible completeness, but its canonical terms are
exponentially row-dense. The branch construction makes B sparse, while C
cannot be polynomial-row sparse for the stated seedwise direct branches even
after arbitrary clean spectator padding. Therefore this route does not meet
N11's common positive C,B interface and supplies no verifier-level
composition with L02.

The obstruction is falsified by an explicit active-workspace or alternative
common isometry for this same amplified hard family, with exact completeness,
`ker C=ran V`, a size-independent positive gap on every auxiliary state,
two-sided compression inequalities, and polynomial row, entry, precision,
and normalization costs. No such object is constructed here. The unresolved
issue is not the DL contraction formula; it is converting its global positive
filter (or the BMVZ tensor clauses) into the all-auxiliary-state sparse image
enforcer while preserving the logical compression.
