Quantum Fisher Information as the Convex Roof of Variance
Sixia Yu
Affiliation: Centre for quantum technologies, National University of Singapore, 3 Science Drive 2, Singapore 117543, Singapore
Affiliation:
Hefei National Laboratory for Physical Sciences at Microscale and Department of Modern Physics, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei, Anhui 230026, China
Abstract
Quantum Fisher information places the fundamental limit to the accuracy of estimating an unknown parameter. Here we shall provide the quantum Fisher information an operational meaning: a mixed state can be so prepared that a given observable has the minimal averaged variance, which equals exactly to the quantum Fisher information for estimating an unknown parameter generated by the unitary dynamics with the given observable as Hamiltonian.
In particular we shall prove that the quantum Fisher information is the convex roof of the variance, as conjectured by Tóth and Petz based on numerical and analytical evidences, by constructing explicitly a pure-state ensemble of the given mixed state in which the averaged variance of a given observable equals to the quantum Fisher information.
Quantum Fisher information [1] places the fundamental limit to the accuracy of estimating an unknown parameter, playing a paramount role in quantum metrology [2]. One fourth of the quantum Fisher information Iϱ​(H)I_{\varrho}(H) is in fact a metric adjusted skew information [3], generalizing the skew information introduced initially by Wigner and Yanase [5]. Here we shall refer both quantities Fσ​(H)F_{\sigma}(H) and Iϱ​(H)I_{\varrho}(H) to as the quantum Fisher information.

In this note we shall establish this fundamental connection rigorously by constructing explicitly an ensemble in which the averaged variance attains the quantum Fisher information, i.e.,

Any convex function of ϱ\varrho that reduces to the standard variance in the case of pure states, e.g., various metric adjusted skew informations [3] as well as generalized Fisher information [4] proper scaled, is a lower bound of the averaged variance. From above discussions we know that the quantum Fisher information Iϱ​(H)I_{\varrho}(H) equals to the averaged variance in the minimal ensemble 𝒰{\mathcal{U}}. And thus all the other convex functions of ϱ\varrho should be no larger than the quantum Fisher information, i.e., the quantum Fisher information is the maximal convex function of ϱ\varrho that is the lower bound of all the possible averaged variances. Simply put, the convex roof of variance equals to the quantum Fisher information Iϱ​(H)I_{\varrho}(H).
In certain sense the minimal ensemble 𝒰{\mathcal{U}} for a given observable HH and mixed state ϱ\varrho is unique as long as the unitary matrix that diagonalizes YHY_{H} is unique, or the observable YHY_{H} is non degenerated. This is because for any unitary UU pure states Eq.(8) define an ensemble for ϱ\varrho and the observables Γk\Gamma_{k} defined in Eq.(10) are still orthogonal to each other. However for Eq.(15) to hold it is necessary for ZHZ_{H} to have an expansion using only orthogonal observables Γk\Gamma_{k}, regarded as a part of a complete set of orthogonal operator basis. This determines UU to be the unitary matrix that diagonalizes YHY_{H}. As such we obtain an operational interpretation of the quantum Fisher information and observable HH in a given state ϱ\varrho: it is the minimal averaged variance for the given observable HH among all possible preparations of the given mixed state ϱ\varrho.
For completeness we also construct an alternative pure-state ensemble 𝒱{\mathcal{V}} in which the averaged variance is maximized, i.e., equals to the standard variance.
We note that for every traceless observable XX there is a basis under which all diagonal elements vanish. By an argument of continuity there exists a state |ϕ⟩|\phi\rangle such that ⟨ϕ|X|ϕ⟩=0\langle\phi|X|\phi\rangle=0. If it is not true the observable XX is either positive or negative semidefinite and thus has a zero trace if and only if it equals to zero. By taking |ϕ⟩|\phi\rangle as a basis and the observable XX in the orthogonal complement subspace is also Hermitian with zero trace and so on.

As a final remark, in a general parameter estimation task where the state is given by ϱ=∑kλk​|ψk⟩​⟨ψk|\varrho=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}| with eigenvalues λk\lambda_{k} and eigenstates |ψk⟩|\psi_{k}\rangle depending on the unknown parameter θ\theta, the quantum Fisher information is given by Fϱ​(θ)=Tr​ϱ​Lθ2F_{\varrho}(\theta)={\rm Tr}\varrho L_{\theta}^{2} with LθL_{\theta} being the symmetric logarithmic derivative satisfying 2​ϱ˙=Lθ​ϱ+ϱ​Lθ2\dot{\varrho}=L_{\theta}\varrho+\varrho L_{\theta}. The quantum Fisher information has a suggestive decomposition Fϱ​(θ)=Fϱc​(θ)+Fϱ​(Hθ)F_{\varrho}(\theta)=F_{\varrho}^{c}(\theta)+F_{\varrho}(H_{\theta}) [7] into a classical Fisher information Fϱc​(θ)=∑kλ˙k2/λkF^{c}_{\varrho}(\theta)=\sum_{k}\dot{\lambda}_{k}^{2}/\lambda_{k} and a quantum part Fϱ​(Hθ)F_{\varrho}(H_{\theta}) as defined in Eq.(1) for the following Hamiltonian, which may be θ\theta-dependent,
Hθ=i​∑k|ψ˙k⟩​⟨ψk|.H_{\theta}=i\sum_{k}|\dot{\psi}_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|.
(18)
Our results shows that the quantum Fisher information in general, apart from a classical Fisher information arising from the θ\theta-dependence of the eigenvalues, equals to 4 times the convex roof of the variance of the Hamiltonian HθH_{\theta} defined above, arising from the θ\theta-dependence of the eigenstates.
In sum, we have constructed a special ensemble for a given mixed state in which the averaged variance of a given observable attains its minimal value, which equals exactly to the quantum Fisher information, proving that the the convex roof of variance equals to the quantum Fisher information. Also we provide an alternative ensemble in which the averaged variance attains its maximal value, showing that the variance is its own concave roof as proved already in [6].
