## Two independently proved sharpness delimiters

### A. No dimension-uniform coefficient above4

On hyperbolic `H^n` with sectional curvature `−1`, scalar curvature is `−n(n−1)` and

\[
\lambda_0(-\Delta)=\frac{(n-1)^2}{4}.
\]

Here is a direct reconstruction. A Busemann function has `|db|=1`, `Δb=n−1`. Integrating `div(φ²∇b)` and applying Cauchy-Schwarz gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, use radial compactly supported annular tests `e^{-(n-1)r/2}χ((r−R)/L)`. As `R→∞`, their weighted radial density becomes constant; then as `L→∞`, the derivative-cutoff energy tends to zero and the quotient tends to `(n−1)²/4`.

Consequently

\[
\lambda_0(-a\Delta+R)
=(n-1)\left[\frac a4(n-1)-n\right]>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a>\frac{4n}{n-1}.
\]

For each fixed `a>4`, choose `n≥4` with `n>a/(a−4)`. This gives positive spectral curvature at coefficient `a`, but infinite width to any target of dimension below `n`. Thus replacing4 by any larger **single coefficient for all dimensions** is impossible. For a fixed dimension, this only rules out `a>4n/(n−1)`; it does not decide the interval `(4,4n/(n−1)]`.

### B. Codimension two cannot uniformly become three

On `S²(√2)×R^{n−2}`, scalar curvature is1, so `−4Δ+R≥1`. Its Urysohn `(n−3)`-width is infinite because restricting any proposed map to the Euclidean `R^{n−2}` slice still bounds all fibers. The cube lemma below then contradicts a finite fiber bound.

### Cube lemma and its complete elementary proof

Let a compact set `Q` be homeomorphic to `[-L,L]^m` and carry a metric in which each pair of opposite coordinate faces is at distance at least `2L`. Any continuous map from `Q` to a simplicial complex of dimension at most `m−1` has a fiber of diameter at least `2L`.

Suppose every fiber has diameter `<2L`. By compactness, each target point has an open neighborhood whose entire preimage in `Q` has diameter `<2L`: otherwise pairs in increasingly small preimages have a limiting pair in one fiber at distance `≥2L`. The compact image lies in a finite subcomplex. To see the latter assertion, if a compact subset met infinitely many open cells, choose one point in each; every subset of these chosen points is closed by the closure-finite/weak-topology property, giving an impossible infinite closed discrete compact subset. The finite subcomplex has an open refinement of order at most `m`, obtained from sufficiently fine barycentric subdivision. Pulling back and taking a finite subcover gives open sets `U_j` with multiplicity at most `m` and no set meeting opposite faces.

Choose a subordinate partition of unity `α_j`. Choose `a_j∈[-L,L]^m` by setting its `i`th coordinate to `+L` if `U_j` meets the positive `i`th face, to `−L` if it meets the negative face, and to0 otherwise. There is no conflicting assignment. The map `F=Σα_ja_j` sends each face into itself. The straight homotopy from the identity to `F` preserves every face, so `F` has relative degree1 and is surjective onto the cube. Yet multiplicity at most `m` puts its image in the finite union of convex hulls of at most `m` vertices, each of dimension at most `m−1`. That finite union has zero `m`-dimensional volume and cannot contain the cube. Contradiction.

Euclidean cubes satisfy the hypothesis. Hyperbolic cubes do too: the inverse exponential map at a point in a Hadamard manifold is1-Lipschitz. This follows directly from the polar metric comparison `dr²+sinh²r dΩ²≥dr²+r²dΩ²` in hyperbolic space, applied to the inverse image of any connecting path. The exponential image of `[-L,L]^n` therefore has opposite faces at distance at least `2L`. Taking `L→∞` proves the infinite-width claim used above.

These delimiters use classical ingredients and are not claimed as new foundational discoveries. The closest source for the cover/large-fiber mechanism is Larry Guth's *Metaphors in systolic geometry*, ICM2010, Section6, PDF pp.7–9; the argument here was reconstructed explicitly for simplicial targets and expanding hyperbolic cubes.

## Family335: precise secondary status

The scalar integral theorem claims a dimension-only positive `a_n` with `∫(R^-)^{n/2}≥a_n||M||` for every closed connected oriented smooth `n≥3` manifold and metric. Theorem2.1 imports qualitative simplicial-volume vanishing for a metric of nonnegative scalar curvature. The Section2 Yamabe reduction is correct conditional on that input: a positive-simplicial-volume manifold has negative Yamabe constant in every conformal class; its minimizing metric normalized to scalar `−1` has volume `(-Y)^{n/2}`, bounded above by `∫(R^-)^{n/2}` through Hölder. Therefore a topology-uniform scalar-minus-one volume estimate suffices.

The quantitative input remains unaudited: Theorem4.1 produces measured entropy metrics with a large energy coefficient exceeding a prescribed fixed-height exponential tower in label-graph complexity; Theorem5.2 is a measured degree estimate with high-dimensional singular descent; Section6 realizes rounded dual chains and compares degree. The original volume factor is retained through a logarithmic density term. These are attractive reusable interfaces, but their proof must be reconstructed before claiming the universal scalar inequality.
