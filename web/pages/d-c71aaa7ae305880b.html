# Full coefficient acquisition for weighted, irregular graph supports

Date 2026-10-06. r06 universal conditional proof, independently implemented coefficient-only exact replay. Extends r09's direct diagonal-cluster construction; not a claim of historical novelty or a new polynomial-equivalence complexity separation. ## Exact question, input and assumptions

Input: all rational coefficients of an n-variable quartic p and the following normal-form promise:

    p(x)=q(Lx)^T Q q(Lx),        q_i(y)=y_i^2−1,
    L in GL_n(Q), Q symmetric rational,
    Q_ij>=0 for i!=j, Q1=b1,
    r_i=sum_{j!=i}Q_ij < b/2 for every i.

No hidden L, graph support, Q, b, zeros or diagonal algebra is supplied. The graph may be irregular, have unequal edge weights, false twins, triangles, arbitrary degree and isolated vertices. The diagonal coefficients Q_ii=b−r_i may differ. The constant-row-sum assumption is retained; arbitrary shifted forms and absence of spectral separation are not covered. Strict diagonal dominance implies Q positive definite, since Q_ii−r_i=b−2r_i>0.

Output: rational hidden coordinate lines, a factor-space basis, a complete exact polynomial identity and a positive rational weighted-SOS certificate, in polynomial bit complexity. This is a promised-class theorem. A total implementation must return UNKNOWN or a class rejection when the algebraic tests fail; it must not infer arbitrary nonnegativity or nonmembership from an exhausted heuristic search. The replay script uses assertions on promised fixtures, not a total arbitrary-input classifier.

## Coefficient-derived metric and catalecticant

Write p0 and p2 for constant and degree-two parts. Constant row sums give

    p0=nb,       b=p0/n,
    p2(x)=−2b x^T L^T L x,
    M=−Hess(p2)/4=b L^T L.

Thus the exact expanded input supplies b and a compatible metric without supplying coordinates. Let T be the symmetric fourth tensor of p4: each degree-four coefficient is divided by its multinomial multiplicity. On symmetric matrices define the rational linear operator

    C(S)_ab=sum_cd T_abcd (M^{-1} S M^{-1})_cd.

Under S=L^T S' L, contracting all four indices gives

    C(S)=L^T C'(S') L.

In hidden coordinates T_iiii=Q_ii and T_iijj=Q_ij/3 for i!=j. Therefore C' preserves the diagonal and every offdiagonal elementary symmetric direction. On these subspaces:

    diagonal block: B=[diag(Q_ii)+off(Q)/3]/b^2,
    edge {i,j}:    alpha_ij=2Q_ij/(3b^2),
    nonedge:       0.

The offdiagonal factor two follows from contracting both (i,j) and (j,i) entries. No unknown-axis oracle is used to build C; this canonical description only proves its spectrum.

## A rational threshold separates the entire diagonal space

Set tau=1/(3b), known from p0. Every offdiagonal eigenvalue satisfies

    0<=alpha_ij<=2r_i/(3b^2)<tau.

Gershgorin for the real symmetric diagonal block gives

    lambda_min(B)>=min_i [Q_ii−r_i/3]/b^2
                  =min_i [b−4r_i/3]/b^2 > tau.

All inequalities are strict. Thus the diagonal cluster lies wholly above a known rational threshold, even though its eigenvalues may be repeated or irrational. All smaller eigenvalues are rational: they are explicit rational edge weights, and possibly zero.

Compute the degree-N rational characteristic polynomial of C, where N=n(n+1)/2, and factor over Q. Collect every distinct rational root alpha<tau, from its linear irreducible factors, and form

    h(t)=product_{alpha<tau}(t−alpha).

If the collection is empty, h=1. No root isolation or assignment of irrational diagonal eigenvalues is needed: all irrational factors belong to the large diagonal cluster, while rational diagonal roots are above tau. The operator is similar to a direct sum of a real symmetric block and scalar blocks, so it is diagonalizable. Consequently

    Dspace=im h(C)=L^T Diag_n L.                         (1)

Its dimension is exactly n. Polynomial evaluation, factorization and rational columnspace extraction implement (1). This avoids any supplied edge eigenvalue or regular degree; unlike the unweighted cycle shortcut, it generally factors a degree-N characteristic polynomial. That cost is charged.

For an acquired rational basis S_1,...,S_n of Dspace, M^{-1}S_j span L^{-1}Diag_n L (the factor 1/b does not affect the span). These operators commute. A deterministic simple element is acquired from

    A(t)=sum_{j=0}^{n−1} t^j M^{-1}S_{j+1}.

Each pairwise hidden eigenvalue difference is a nonzero degree-at-most-(n−1) polynomial, because the basis spans the whole diagonal space. Among t=0,...,(n−1)binom(n,2), one has distinct eigenvalues. They are rational since the canonical diagonal coefficients and L are rational. Rational characteristic factorization and one-dimensional nullspaces give an axis matrix V with LV a scaled permutation. This step uses no generic simple-covariant premise.

## Full certificate, factor space and Gram uniqueness

Substitute x=Vz in the supplied polynomial. The acquired axes imply

    p(Vz)=q'(z)^T Q' q'(z),       q'_i=z_i^2−a_i,
    Q'>0 rational, a_i>0 rational.

Read Q'_ii from z_i^4 and Q'_ij from half the z_i^2 z_j^2 coefficient. Let c be the degree-two coefficient vector and solve a=−(Q')^{-1}c/2. Verify ALL coefficients of this identity, including the constant and the absence of odd/mixed monomials. A rational LDL decomposition of Q' has positive rational pivots and supplies positive rational weighted squares. It is not necessary to take irrational square roots in the returned certificate.

For completeness the acquired factor space is also the entire supporting factor space of every PSD Gram: Q'>0 makes the real zeros precisely z_i=+/-sqrt(a_i). A degree-at-most-two polynomial vanishing at all sign choices has no linear or cross terms, by independence of sign-cube characters, and its constant is minus the sum of its diagonal coefficients times a_i. Thus every squared factor in any PSD Gram lies in span{z_i^2−a_i}. Quartic coefficient comparison then uniquely fixes its compressed Gram to Q'. The feasible PSD Gram is a singleton of rank n; the factor space dimension is n; the minimal supporting face of the PSD cone has geometric dimension n(n+1)/2. These three dimensions must not be conflated. The proof uses the sign cube, but the algorithm never enumerates its 2^n zeros.

## Costs, comparator and novelty

The coefficient representation has O(n^4) entries, including charged rational heights. Building C uses polynomially many rational operations on an N-dimensional matrix. Determinants/minors bound the heights generated by metric inversion, polynomial evaluation of h(C), rational elimination and characteristic polynomials. Rational univariate factorization has polynomial bit algorithms. The separation search has O(n^3) trials with O(n log n)-bit weights. Degree-four substitution, coefficient verification and positive LDL are polynomial. This establishes a standard exact polynomial-bit algorithm, not a runtime bound for every internal SymPy heuristic and not robustness to finite precision or ill-conditioned transforms.
