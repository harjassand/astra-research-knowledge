# Failure and boundary ledger

- **Probe invisibility (exact):** for isotropic rotational OU, a shifted isotropic Gaussian initial condition has the same KL relaxation curvature under the driven and equilibrium processes even though \(\sigma=2\omega^2\). A zero signal cannot imply zero dissipation. The 2026 Guo–Huang preprint independently notes this isotropic model has \(\xi_Q\equiv0\). Need probe selection/alignment; a random probe family may still miss current directions.
- **No coarse-grained guarantee:** the candidate denominator uses the complete density ratio, its spatial gradient and a diffusion-generator term (thus second derivatives of \(f\)); coarse-grained observations need not determine \(Q_f\), and arbitrary hidden currents remain possible.
- **Conditioning:** if \(f\) approaches zero, \(G_f\) contains inverse powers of \(f\); if the measured curvature difference is a small difference of two second derivatives, the numerator is noise-sensitive. No finite-sample or discretization error theorem has been derived.
- **Reference access:** the equilibrium comparator requires knowing \(\rho\), temperature/mobility, and its corresponding conservative drift, or a valid way to compute its predicted short-time curvature. No material or experiment was calibrated.
- **Mathematical domain:** strict positivity, smoothness and decay/integrability sufficient for integration by parts and two time derivatives are assumptions. Boundary fluxes, degenerate diffusion, nonconstant diffusion tensors, non-Markovian dynamics, and quantum finite-amplitude perturbations are not covered.
- **Novelty unresolved:** the classical finite-amplitude inequality is an internal direct derivation from Auconi's local theorem. It appears to fill an explicit future-work direction in Guo–Huang (2026), but no exhaustive literature/priority review was performed and no external review of this derivation occurred.
