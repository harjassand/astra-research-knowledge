CYCLE 2 PASSIVE RAW-TRAJECTORY MINIMAX
8 October 2026

VERDICT

A fixed-margin two-state HMM family has matching passive raw-stream minimax risk Theta(T^(-1/2)) for uniform one-step forecasting over every finite observed history. Thus target expected-TV risk epsilon requires Theta(epsilon^(-2)) raw symbols, even though the hidden chain mixes geometrically at rate 1/2, every transition and emission is positive, emissions have fixed singular value, the predictive Hankel rank is exactly 2 with a fixed gap, and a complete rank witness uses only one-symbol prefixes and suffixes.

The lower bound is for a full uninterrupted observation trajectory and is proved by KL against the complete hidden-labeled path. It is not a reset/truncation or rare-switch obstruction. An explicit compact filter estimator using disjoint adjacent products matches the T^(-1/2) rate uniformly over all finite histories.

MODEL AND PROMISES

Hidden and observed alphabets are {−1,+1}, the initial hidden state is stationary uniform, and for theta in [2/5,3/5]:

  P(Z_(t+1)=z'|Z_t=z)=(1+(1/2)zz')/2,
  P_theta(Y_t=y|Z_t=z)=(1+theta yz)/2.

The transition matrix has entries 3/4 and 1/4, nontrivial eigenvalue 1/2, and hidden-state TV mixing contraction 1/2 per step. Emission probabilities are at least 1/5; the 2x2 emission matrix has singular values 1 and theta, so sigma_min>=2/5.

Let mu=(1/2)theta^2. The pair Hankel witness for one-symbol prefixes and suffixes is

  H_(1,1)(a,b)=P(Y1=a,Y2=b)=(1+mu ab)/4,

with singular values 1/2 and mu/2. Therefore sigma_2>=1/25 and the full predictive rank is exactly 2; the witness horizon (1,1) is complete because the hidden-state factorization bounds full Hankel rank by 2.

OBJECTIVE

Training receives one raw stationary stream S=Y1:T. A compact learner outputs a one-step predictive rule q_S(.|h) for every finite binary history h, with risk

  R_T(q)=sup_theta E_theta[ sup_(h in {±1}*) TV(q_S(.|h), Law_theta(Y_(|h|+1)|h)) ].

This is uniform over histories, not an average over a random test context. A query at history length m consumes/given m context symbols and returns one Bernoulli probability.

MATCHING THEOREM

For every T>=25,

  1/(128 sqrt(T)) <= inf_q R_T(q)
                   <= (335/56)/sqrt(floor(T/2)).

Hence the minimax risk is Theta(T^(-1/2)) and the symbol complexity for risk epsilon is Theta(epsilon^(-2)). The constants are explicit but not optimized.

LOWER PROOF

For k=ceil(sqrt(T)), set delta=1/(2k), theta0=1/2 and theta1=1/2+delta. For T>=25 both lie in the promised parameter interval and have O(log T)-bit descriptions. At history h=(+1), the forecast is P(Y2=+1|Y1=+1)=(1+theta^2/2)/2, so the two forecasts have TV separation

  D=(theta1^2-theta0^2)/4=(delta+delta^2)/4
    >= 1/(16 sqrt(T)).

For a fixed hidden state, changing theta0 to theta1 changes an emission Bernoulli parameter by delta/2. Since the alternative's Bernoulli probabilities stay in [1/5,4/5], KL<=chi-square gives per-symbol KL <=25 delta^2/16 <2 delta^2. The hidden path law is identical under the two models; hence KL of the complete path (Z1:T,Y1:T) is at most 2T delta^2<=1/2. Data processing gives this same upper bound for the entire raw observation stream. Pinsker yields TV(P0^T,P1^T)<=1/2.

Classify a forecast at h=(+1) by whichever of the two candidate forecast laws is nearer. A mistaken classification means forecast loss at least D/2. Le Cam gives summed testing errors at least 1-TV>=1/2, so the larger model risk is at least D/8>=1/(128 sqrt(T)). This remains true even if the learner is given all hidden labels in addition to the observations. A local two-point Le Cam argument is the sharp tool for this one-dimensional parametric rate; no Fano packing is needed.

UPPER PROOF

Take n=floor(T/2) disjoint products W_j=Y_(2j-1)Y_(2j). Writing Y_t=Z_t E_t, each W_j uses two distinct emission noises and one transition flip; these underlying variables are disjoint across j. Thus W_j are iid with mean mu=(1/2)theta^2. Project their sample mean onto [2/25,9/50], yielding mu_hat. Then E|mu_hat-mu|<=1/sqrt(n). Put theta_hat=sqrt(2 mu_hat), and filter each supplied history with theta_hat:

  m_0=0,
  m_j=(m_(j-1)/2 + theta_hat y_j)/(1+theta_hat y_j m_(j-1)/2),
  q_hat(+1|h)=(1+theta_hat m_m/2)/2.

For theta in [2/5,3/5], the filter map contracts in its state argument by at most 6/7 and has parameter derivative at most 100/49. Therefore for every history length, |m(theta)-m(theta_hat)| <= (100/7)|theta-theta_hat|. Also |theta-theta_hat|<= (5/2)|mu-mu_hat|. The resulting uniform one-step TV error is at most (335/56)|mu-mu_hat|; expectation proves the upper bound.

COST ACCOUNTING

The lower and upper use T raw binary observations in one stream start, not independent length-three snippets. The learner's training time is O(T), memory O(1), and its sufficient statistic needs O(log T) bits. The local hard pair itself has O(log T)-bit descriptions and one unknown index bit. For epsilon-risk, quantize the compact filter parameter to O(log(1/epsilon)) bits; a forecast after a length-m history takes O(m) arithmetic steps, and filter contraction controls rounding error. If the stream is split into stationary segments totaling N symbols, the lower KL is still at most 2N delta^2; resets do not reduce the symbol complexity. A reset charge rho adds rho for the one-stream upper or R rho for R segments.

MARGIN DEPENDENCE AND SCOPE

All structural margins are constants here: transition minimum 1/4, hidden mixing contraction 1/2, emission minimum 1/5, emission singular value at least 2/5, and predictive Hankel second singular value at least 1/25. With those fixed, the remaining rate is exactly parametric epsilon^(-2), not a horizon or rank-gap blowup. The upper's explicit stability constant comes from the contraction 6/7 and emission lower bound 2/5; no optimal dependence as these margins vary is claimed. This is a fixed-margin subclass theorem, not an arbitrary-HMM minimax result.
