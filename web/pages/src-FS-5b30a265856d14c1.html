2. The global convexification theorem
Let
\[
F:\mathbb R^{m\times d}\to\mathbb R
\]
be \(C^1\), positively homogeneous of degree \(p>1\), and rank-one convex. Let
\[
\omega(x)\in S^{m-1}
\]
be an arbitrary fixed smooth spatial direction field.
Write a map as
\[
u=r\omega,\qquad r>0,
\]
and set
\[
\rho=r^p.
\]
The chain rule gives
\[
D(r\omega)
=
r\left(D\omega+\omega\otimes\frac{\nabla\rho}{p\rho}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\omega(\rho)
=
\int_\Omega
\rho\,F\left(D\omega+
\omega\otimes\frac{\nabla\rho}{p\rho}\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Theorem A — Convexity in the amplitude density
For every fixed \(\omega\), the functional \(\mathcal E_\omega\) is convex in \(\rho\).
Equivalently, if
\[
r_t=\bigl((1-t)r_0^p+t r_1^p\bigr)^{1/p},
\]
then pointwise
\[
\boxed{
F(D(r_t\omega))
\le
(1-t)F(D(r_0\omega))
+tF(D(r_1\omega)).
}
\tag{2}
\]
There is no rotational-invariance assumption. The field \(\omega\) may vary in every spatial coordinate, and neither its phase nor its Fourier structure is restricted.
Proof
At each point \(x\), the function
\[
a\longmapsto F(D\omega(x)+\omega(x)\otimes a)
\]
is convex: the difference between any two of its matrix arguments is rank one.
Its perspective
\[
(\rho,z)\longmapsto
\rho F\left(D\omega+\omega\otimes\frac{z}{p\rho}\right)
\]
is therefore jointly convex. Substituting \(z=\nabla\rho\) proves the theorem.
More explicitly, the normalized gradients associated with \(r_0,r_1\) differ by a rank-one matrix. Their weights are
\[
w_0=\frac{(1-t)r_0^p}{r_t^p},
\qquad
w_1=\frac{tr_1^p}{r_t^p},
\]
which sum to one. Rank-one convexity applied with those weights gives (2).
For homogeneous quartics, approximation extends the inequality to nonnegative \(W^{1,4}\) amplitudes. The admissible density space is
\[
\{\rho\ge0:\rho^{1/4}\in W^{1,4}\},
\]
not an unspecified Sobolev space for \(\rho\).
This uses established hidden convexity and convex-perspective mathematics. Brasco and Franzina develop the scalar hidden-convexity/Picone framework and its equivalences. The point established here is why rank-one convexity suffices for a vectorial energy along a prescribed, spatially varying direction, without requiring \(F\) itself to be convex or nonnegative. I am not claiming invention of the underlying machinery. arXiv
3. An exact relative-energy identity
The convexity statement has a stronger, usable form.
For positive amplitudes \(r,s\), define
\[
A_r=D\omega+\omega\otimes\nabla\log r,
\qquad
Q=\omega\otimes\nabla\log(s/r).
\]
The increment \(Q\) is rank one. Let
\[
\mathcal R_F(C,Q)=F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C):Q.
\]
Theorem B — Direction-preserving Picone identity
\[
\boxed{
F(D(s\omega))
-\frac1pDF(D(r\omega)):
D\left(\omega\frac{s^p}{r^{p-1}}\right)
=
s^p\mathcal R_F(A_r,Q)\ge0.
}
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed,
\[
D\left(\omega\frac{s^p}{r^{p-1}}\right)
=
\frac{s^p}{r^{p-1}}(A_r+pQ),
\]
while
\[
DF(D(r\omega))=r^{p-1}DF(A_r),
\qquad
DF(A_r):A_r=pF(A_r).
\]
Substitution proves the identity. Nonnegativity follows from the rank-one supporting inequality.
A quantitative quartic remainder
For a homogeneous rank-one-convex quartic, put
\[
\mu_F=
\min_{\substack{|R|=1\\\operatorname{rank}R=1}}F(R)\ge0.
\]
A convex univariate quartic gives
\[
\mathcal R_F(C,Q)\ge\frac13F(Q)
\]
for every rank-one \(Q\). To see the constant, write the restriction as
\[
At^4+Lt^3+C_2t^2+Dt+E.
\]
Convexity gives \(C_2\ge3L^2/(8A)\) when \(A>0\), and
\[
A+L+C_2-\frac A3
=
\frac{3}{8A}\left(L+\frac{4A}{3}\right)^2
+C_2-\frac{3L^2}{8A}\ge0.
\]
The degenerate case follows directly.
Thus (3) strengthens to
\[
\boxed{
F(D(s\omega))
-\frac14DF(D(r\omega)):
D\left(\omega\frac{s^4}{r^3}\right)
\ge
\frac{\mu_F}{3}\,r^4
\left|\nabla\frac sr\right|^4.
}
\tag{4}
\]
This controls the complete variation of the amplitude, not its linearization. With a positive smooth reference \(r\), it extends to real \(W^{1,4}\) competitors \(s\), including zeros, by approximation.
One consequence is an exact global-minimum certificate. If a positive periodic reference solves
\[
-\omega\cdot\operatorname{div}DF(D(r\omega))
=4\lambda r^3,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\int F(D(s\omega))-\lambda\int s^4
\ge
\frac{\mu_F}{3}\int r^4
\left|\nabla\frac sr\right|^4.
}
\tag{5}
\]
A positive radial critical point is therefore a global amplitude minimizer at fixed fourth mass. If \(\mu_F>0\), equality forces proportional amplitudes on a connected domain.
This does not assert that every minimizing amplitude is strictly positive.
4. A compatible replacement for every smooth affine-boundary test
The operation is not confined to maps already presented in a convenient phase form.
Assume \(\mu_F>0\), and fix a smooth direction field \(\omega\). Consider
\[
\min_r\int_\Omega F(D(r\omega))
\]
subject to
\[
r\ge0,\qquad r|_{\partial\Omega}=g,\qquad
\int_\Omega r^4=M.
\tag{6}
\]
Theorem C — Attained convex amplitude replacement
Whenever the admissible set is nonempty, (6) has a minimizer, and its density formulation is convex.
The required coercivity follows from the quartic expansion:
\[
\boxed{
F(\omega\otimes\nabla r+rD\omega)
\ge
\frac{\mu_F}{2}|\nabla r|^4
-C_F\|D\omega\|_\infty^4r^4.
}
\tag{7}
\]
At fixed \(M\), a minimizing sequence is bounded in \(W^{1,4}\). Weak compactness, strong \(L^4\) convergence, and convexity in the scalar gradient \(\nabla r\) give attainment. Boundary data, nonnegativity and the mass constraint pass to the limit.
Now take an arbitrary smooth test
\[
u(x)=Bx+\varphi(x),\qquad \varphi|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
\]
Choose a constant target translation \(c\) large enough that \(u+c\) never vanishes and, in two target dimensions, lies in an open half-plane. Translation leaves the gradient unchanged.
Set
\[
r=|u+c|,
\qquad
\omega=\frac{u+c}{|u+c|},
\qquad
M=\int|u+c|^4,
\qquad
g=|Bx+c|\ \text{on }\partial\Omega.
\]
Replace \(r\) by a global minimizer \(r_*\) of (6). Then
\[
u_*=r_*\omega
\]
is an actual Sobolev map with the same affine boundary, and
\[
\boxed{
\int_\Omega F(Du_*)\le\int_\Omega F(Du).
}
\tag{8}
\]
Any negative quasiconvexity test would therefore have an equally negative, or lower-energy, replacement whose amplitude has already been globally minimized.
This preserves compatibility and the specified affine boundary. It does not preserve the fourth gradient norm, and it does not minimize over \(\omega\).
The positive rank-one value hypothesis covers the unresolved non-polyconvex members of the invariant quartic cone. The short algebraic reason is that a zero value at a nonzero rank-one matrix degenerates the convex binary restriction; the remaining invariant quartic then reduces, after input normalization and rank-one Hessian tests, to a real-column fourth power plus a nonnegative determinant square. That is polyconvex. The new method can alternatively be used with \(\mu_F>0\) as an explicit hypothesis, without importing the longer contact classifications.
5. A scalar certificate that can actually be checked
For planar left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant \(F\), write
\[
\omega=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\qquad k=\nabla\theta,
\]
and define
\[
f_k(a)=F
\begin{pmatrix}
a_1&a_2\\
k_1&k_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Output invariance gives
\[
\mathcal E_\theta(\rho)
=
\int\rho\,f_k\left(\frac{\nabla\rho}{4\rho}\right).
\tag{9}
\]
For a positive smooth reference density \(\sigma\), put
\[
a_\sigma=\frac{\nabla\sigma}{4\sigma},
\qquad
j_\sigma=\partial_a f_k(a_\sigma),
\]
\[
\boxed{
g_\sigma=
f_k(a_\sigma)-a_\sigma\cdot j_\sigma
-\frac14\operatorname{div}j_\sigma.
}
\tag{10}
\]
The Picone identity gives, on a periodic cell,
\[
\boxed{
\int F(D(s\omega))
\ge
\int g_\sigma s^4
+\frac{\mu_F}{3}
\int\left|
\nabla s-\frac s4\nabla\log\sigma
\right|^4.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Hence
\[
\inf_{\int s^4=1}\mathcal E_\theta(s^4)
\ge\operatorname*{ess\,inf}g_\sigma.
\tag{12}
\]
This is an a posteriori certificate: a proposed reference density can be checked independently. It does not assume an unknown quasiconvex envelope or dominating profile functional.
There is also a flux version. For any smooth \(j\), Fenchel’s inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\theta(\rho)
\ge
\int\left[-\frac14\operatorname{div}j-f_k^*(j)\right]\rho
+\frac14\int_{\partial\Omega}\rho\,j\cdot\nu.
}
\tag{13}
\]
The boundary term is fixed when the amplitude boundary data are fixed. Choosing \(j=j_\sigma\) recovers (10).
A universal proof would need adequate such bounds for every admissible direction field and affine boundary. That obligation remains; the advantage is that the amplitude problem now has independently checkable primal and dual tests.

6. The exact obstruction that remains
Convexity in each field separately does not imply joint convexity.
For fixed \(\rho\), expression (9) is convex in \(\nabla\theta\): changing that gradient changes one matrix row, hence a rank-one direction. Nevertheless, the pair \((\rho,\theta)\) is not jointly convex.
Take the explicitly convex integrand \(F(A)=|A|^4\), and
\[
\rho=1+a\cos2y,\qquad
\theta=(2+b)\sin y.
\]
The density is positive for \(|a|<1\) and has mean one. Exact averaging gives
\[
\boxed{
D^2_{(a,b)}\mathcal E\big|_{(0,0)}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&8\\
8&18
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det=-46.
}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus even a convex original energy has an indefinite joint Hessian in these variables. Alternating globally solved amplitude and phase blocks is not a proof of global joint minimization.
There is another important limitation. Adding a large target translation \(Re_1\) makes the phase and its gradient of any fixed bounded smooth test \(O(R^{-1})\), while its amplitude is \(O(R)\). The original gradient and variational gap are unchanged. Therefore “small phase” alone cannot supply the missing theorem when amplitudes and affine backgrounds are unrestricted.
The precise unresolved statement is now the sign of
\[
\min_{\substack{\rho\ge0,\ \int\rho=M\\
\rho^{1/4}\omega|_{\partial\Omega}=Bx+c}}
\mathcal E_\omega(\rho)
-
|\Omega|F(B)
\tag{18}
\]
as \(\omega\) ranges over all admissible direction fields.
The inner minimization is convex and attained under the stated hypotheses. The outer direction problem remains genuinely nonlinear. Calling the inner minimum a ground-state energy does not establish its sign.

What this run establishes
The amplitude can be eliminated by a global, compatibility-preserving convex replacement, not by a local contact approximation. The associated identity works in arbitrary matrix dimensions for prescribed direction fields. Its scalar dual can certify all amplitudes at a genuinely coupled phase.
What has not been established is a uniform phase-to-dual construction, a globally decreasing direction operation, or a negative compatible test. The critical-trace restrictions remain true; the new method avoids postulating a trace-storage functional rather than defeating those restrictions.
