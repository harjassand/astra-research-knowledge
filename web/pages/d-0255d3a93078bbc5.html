# Independent mathematical audit of r06 two-dictionary simplex cover

Auditor: r09_algebra. Date: 2026-10-06. Verdict: **proof passes within its stated real-vector, independent-generator interface.** This is independently reconstructed mathematics and a separate executable, not independent external scholarly validation. Novelty is unverified.

The audited statement: S is a basis of R^n, n≥2; k≥1; kS allows repetition. A,B are arbitrary finite real-vector sets, |A|=2, kS⊆A+B. Then

min |B|=[z^k](1−z)^{−(n−1)}(1−z²)^{−1}.

I read `results/r06_combinatorics/two_dictionary_theorem.md`, inspected the primary Xu theorem and source date/author metadata, and independently reconstructed the following argument.

## Load-bearing steps checked

1. Apply an invertible linear change of coordinates and translate A,B oppositely, so A={0,δ} and the target is T={x∈Z^n_≥0:Σx_i=k}. If any b covers two targets, their difference is δ. Thus δ must be nonzero integral with coordinate sum zero. Nonintegral or nonzero-sum steps permit only singleton coverage and cannot improve the answer. No assumption that B is on-grid is being introduced.

2. Edges joining x to x+δ form finite paths because every vertex has at most one successor and predecessor, and a linear functional nonzero on δ is strictly monotone. A path with ℓ vertices has cover number ceil(ℓ/2), even if b outside T is permitted: a choice of b covers at most two adjacent vertices on one path. Conversely alternate path vertices attain the lower bound. Equivalently min |B|=|T|−ν, where ν is maximum matching size; use of the matching formula is valid for paths.

3. Put L=Σ_i max(δ_i,0)=Σ_i max(−δ_i,0)≥1. The number I_j of starts of j consecutive edges is N_n(k−jL), N_n(u)=binom(n+u−1,u) for u≥0 and zero otherwise. To see this without a graph shortcut: all endpoints x,…,x+jδ are nonnegative iff x_i≥j max(−δ_i,0); all intermediate inequalities follow from endpoint convexity, and the shift consumes exactly jL units of total coordinate mass. Every ℓ-vertex path contributes max(ℓ−j,0) to I_j. Directly summing the alternating sequence yields floor(ℓ/2). Hence

min |B| for fixed δ = Σ_{j≥0}(−1)^j N_n(k−jL)=[z^k](1−z)^{−n}/(1+z^L).

4. The minimization over L is the delicate step and it is valid. For n=2, all T points lie on an interval with k+1 vertices; any matching has size ≤floor((k+1)/2), and δ=e1−e2 attains this. This establishes the nonnegativity of every coefficient of

(1−z)^{−2}[(1+z^L)^{−1}−(1+z)^{−1}]

for each fixed integer L≥1, including k=0. Multiplication by (1−z)^{−(n−2)}, whose coefficients are nonnegative, proves the fixed-L inequality in every n≥2. No alternating-series monotonicity is being incorrectly assumed.

5. The construction is actual coverage: at each root x with x−δ∉T, list x,x+δ,… and place alternating vertices in B. A+B can include additional off-target points; containment rather than equality is exactly the authorized interface.

## Progression boundary checked

For A a translate of an r-term arithmetic progression, an integral zero-sum step gives path blocks of at most r consecutive vertices. Their exact cover counts are ceil(ℓ/r), and the consecutive-start expansion gives

Q_L(n,k,r)=[z^k](1−z)^{−n}(1−z^L)/(1−z^{rL}).

The same n=2 ceiling-count lower bound and positive-coefficient convolution prove Q_L≥Q_1. The real-step extension needs the denominator argument, and r06 supplies it: if some jδ with 1≤j≤r−1 is integral of sum zero, δ is rational, sum zero, and the least q with qδ integral divides every successful j. Targets covered by a fixed b can use only one residue class of progression indices modulo q. At most h=ceil(r/q) target indices fit, consecutively along Δ=qδ paths. Thus the effective block size is h≤r; selecting the longest residue class actually attains Q_L(n,k,h). If no such j exists, each translate covers at most one target.

I found no mathematical gap in this extension. It remains a statement about progression dictionaries; all r-element dictionaries are treated only when r=2. For n=1 the advertised coefficient formula may fail and n≥2 is essential. Torsion groups and dependent S require different arguments. Exact covers say nothing about approximate representation.

## Independent executable evidence

`work/r09_algebra/audit_simplex_cover.py` does not construct paths or use r06's fixed-step formula. For each finite target T and dictionary A, it enumerates every relevant b=t−a, records which target subset b+A covers, and solves the exact set-cover problem by bitmask recursion. Candidate completeness follows because any useful b covers some t and therefore equals t−a for some a.

It checks all progression steps causing an overlap: δ=(x−y)/j with x,y∈T, 1≤j≤r−1, modulo the sign/translation equivalence. Scope: n=2..3,k=1..4,r=2..4, 374 steps, integral and fractional, zero failures. A direction causing no overlap has cover number |T| and cannot improve on the formula. Output: `results/r09_algebra/simplex_cover_audit_checks.json`. This finite computation catches real-step and B-outside-T mistakes but is not the universal proof.

The non-progression obstruction also checks independently: for n=4,k=2,A={e1,e2,e3}, exact set cover gives |B|=5, whereas every 3-term progression needs six. This blocks any extrapolation of the progression formula to arbitrary r≥3.

## Later supplement: finite three-point cases

r06 subsequently supplied a simple n=3,k=3 construction and exact n=4,k=3 search. I checked both the real-vector normalization argument and the finite minimizations independently.

For n=3,k=3, take A={2e1,2e2,2e3} and B={e1,e2,e3,−e1+e2+e3}. Every degree-three composition is either 3e_i, 2e_i+e_j, or e1+e2+e3; the displayed sets cover all three types. There are ten targets and each b covers at most three, so |B|≥ceil(10/3)=4. The construction proves the exact global optimum four. Every progression requires five, so this is an analytically certified higher-order separation.

To normalize general three-point dictionaries in a finite instance, choose one representation of every target and graph the three A labels by shared B values. If an A label is isolated, every B assigned to it covers a single target, and one may replace each such B by target−a0 for a fixed label a0 in another component. Thus a disconnected representation graph can be converted to a two-label cover with no larger |B|. Any cover beating the exact two-point bound therefore has a connected three-vertex graph. Choose the central vertex of a spanning path; both differences from that vertex to the other A labels belong to T−T. Opposite translations of A,B then place A={0,d1,d2}, with distinct nonzero d1,d2∈T−T. This handles arbitrary real A: the global translation may be nonintegral, but the normalized relative differences are forced integral.

The auditor's unpruned translate-candidate DP reran every unordered pair of nonzero differences for n=3,k=3 (630 dictionaries) and n=4,k=3 (10,585 dictionaries). It obtained minima four and nine, respectively, agreeing with r06's separately written, pruned solver. In n=4,k=3 the two-point bound is thirteen, so covers below thirteen must be among the enumerated connected normalized dictionaries. This certifies the finite arbitrary-real-A optimum nine within the exact implementation and normalization argument. It does not imply a general formula. Every progression has size eleven in this case.
