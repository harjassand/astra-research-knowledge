## 1. Critical trees separate nonlinear inference from additive squared-loss estimation

The strongest first-pass consequence is a four-state tree construction at the exponential Kesten–Stigum boundary. Assuming the released family-229 capacity theorem, a bounded-degree deterministic tree can permit a fixed nonlinear reconstruction advantage while the best additive estimator's squared-loss advantage tends to zero. No efficient algorithm with sublinear observation cost follows.

### Exact interface

The root spin is uniform in four states; an edge uses

\[
P_\lambda(j\mid i)=\lambda\mathbf1_{i=j}+(1-\lambda)/4,
\quad 0<\lambda<1.
\]

The supplied deterministic tree is spherically symmetric, with `N_k` vertices at depth `k` and a fixed upper bound on children. The observations are **all** depth-`n` spins from one broadcast realization. All vertices and their labels count as acquired data. Reconstruction means a positive limiting expected posterior TV distance from the uniform root law.

The source's new criterion is positive nonlinear `L^3` capacity with path potential

\[
\sum_{e\in\xi}(\lambda^{-2|e|}\theta(e))^2.
\]

For a spherically symmetric tree its capacity is exactly

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_{3,\lambda}
 =\left[\sum_{k\ge1}(\lambda^{-2k}/N_k)^2\right]^{-1/2},
\]

with value zero when the series diverges. The uniform flow of mass `M` has each depth-`k` edge flow `M/N_k`, attaining this formula. Conversely average the path potential under the uniform ray law, then apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the flows at each level: the maximum path potential is at least `M²` times the displayed series. This proves the formula without importing another capacity theorem.

Consequently, for `N_k` comparable to `λ^(-2k) k^α`, the source criterion gives reconstruction **exactly when `α>1/2`**, including failure at `α=1/2`.

### Independent theorem for every additive estimator

Let `φ` be a mean-zero, variance-one root contrast. All three orthonormal contrasts have edge eigenvalue `λ`, and

\[
\mathbb E[\phi_a(\sigma_u)\phi_b(\sigma_v)]
 =\mathbf1_{a=b}\lambda^{d(u,v)}.
\]

For an estimator `F=Σ_v w_v φ(σ_v)` on depth `n`, normalize `Σ_v w_v=λ^(-n)` so that `Cov(F,φ(root))=1`. Put `θ(e)=λ^n Σ_{v\text{ below }e}w_v`. The innovations

\[
\epsilon_e=\phi(\sigma_{\operatorname{child}(e)})
       -\lambda\phi(\sigma_{\operatorname{parent}(e)})
\]

are pairwise orthogonal, orthogonal to the root contrast, and have variance `1−λ²`. Thus

\[
F=\phi(\sigma_\rho)+\sum_{|e|\le n}\lambda^{-|e|}\theta(e)\epsilon_e,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}F
 =1+(1-\lambda^2)\sum_{k=1}^n\lambda^{-2k}
                             \sum_{|e|=k}\theta(e)^2.
\]

At each level the signed flows sum to one. Cauchy–Schwarz gives `Σθ(e)²≥1/N_k`, with equality for uniform weights. Hence

\[
\sup_{F\text{ additive}}\operatorname{Corr}(F,\phi(\sigma_\rho))^2
 =\left[1+(1-\lambda^2)\sum_{k=1}^n\lambda^{-2k}/N_k\right]^{-1}.
\]

This also covers arbitrary scalar functions separately applied to each leaf: expand those functions in the three orthonormal contrasts. Components orthogonal to the target contrast add variance and no covariance. Optimizing the overall scalar coefficient gives the same quantity as the maximal squared-loss improvement over the zero estimator. It says nothing about arbitrary nonlinear thresholding of an additive statistic.

For `N_k≈λ^(-2k)k^α`, the additive improvement is `Θ(n^(-(1−α)))` for `α<1`, `Θ(1/log n)` at `α=1`, and stays positive for `α>1`.

### Explicit bounded-degree example

Take `λ=1/√2` and `α=3/4`. Set

\[
q_k=\left\lfloor\frac{3\log(k+2)}{4\log(3/2)}\right\rfloor,
\quad q_0=1,\quad
b_k=2(3/2)^{q_k-q_{k-1}}\in\{2,3\}.
\]

Every depth-`k−1` vertex has `b_k` children. The increment is zero or one because `(3/4)log((k+2)/(k+1))<log(3/2)`. Then

\[
N_k=2^k(3/2)^{q_k-1}\asymp2^k(k+2)^{3/4}.
\]

Its nonlinear capacity is positive, whereas its additive squared-loss advantage is `Θ(n^(-1/4))`. The source's symmetric posterior has three equally sized contrast components; positive reconstruction implies a fixed positive Bayes squared-loss advantage. Thus belief propagation separates from **every additive estimator in squared loss** on this explicit tree.

An explicit admissible uniform-flow mass is

\[
M_0=\frac{4}{9\,2^{1/4}},
\]

because `Σ(2^k/N_k)²≤(81/16)√2`. The source sufficiency proof provides a positive reconstruction bound from such a flow; its constants are not optimized for experimental usefulness.

Belief propagation on the supplied finite tree uses four posterior entries per vertex and `O(V_n)` real arithmetic, where `V_n=Σ_{k≤n}N_k=Θ(N_n)`. It reads `Θ(N_n)` observed spins. `N_n` is exponential in depth. Fixed `λ<1` bounds channel factors away from zero; ordinary log-domain or interval evaluation with `O(log V_n+log(1/ε))` bits suffices for a polynomial-bit implementation with certified posterior error `ε` via worst-case error propagation. This is a structural inference separation, not a computational lower bound against all algorithms.

## 2. Critical SK has a finite-amplitude remanence prediction

### Source interface and dependency

For Gaussian or Rademacher iid couplings, zero-field critical SK at inverse temperature one has

\[
\pi_J(\sigma)\propto\exp\{n^{-1/2}\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j\},
\quad C_{n,J}(t)=\frac1n\sum_i\langle\sigma_i,P_t^J\sigma_i\rangle_\pi.
\]

Clocks refresh at rate one **per spin**. The [October 5 functional source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Functional-universality-of-critical-SK-autocorrelations-October-5-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex) claims

\[
A_n(s)=n^{1/3}C_n(sn^{2/3})\Rightarrow A(s)
\quad\text{in }C_{\rm loc}((0,\infty)),
\]

with the same nondegenerate random law for both coupling distributions. Thermal expectations are already averaged; remaining randomness is the disorder. `A` is positive, decreases to zero, and has an essentially unbounded value at every fixed positive time.

This is an unformalized input. The current [Lean scope document](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/227.md) does not list this functional theorem and gives only a subexponential critical mixing upper bound, not the separate manuscript's `n^(2/3+o(1))` matching upper bound. I did not replay Lean or audit the full dependency closure.

### Exact finite-field theorem, independent of the critical source

Any zero-field spin-flip-symmetric Ising law satisfies `Eπ σ_i=0`. Adding a dimensionless equilibrium field `h` to one selected site gives exactly

\[
\pi_{i,h}(\sigma)=\pi(\sigma)[1+\sigma_i\tanh h].
\]

Remove that field at time zero and then use the original zero-field semigroup. Its same-site remanence is

\[
M_{i,h}(t)=\mathbb E_{\pi_{i,h}}\sigma_i(t)
 =\tanh(h)\langle\sigma_i,P_t\sigma_i\rangle_\pi.
\]

The first term vanishes by stationarity and symmetry. Therefore the average over selected sites obeys `M_h(t)=tanh(h) C_n(t)` for **every** finite `n`, every finite `h`, and all times. No small-field expansion or unknown uniform remainder is used.

The conditional prediction is consequently

\[
n^{1/3}M_h(sn^{2/3})\Rightarrow\tanh(h)A(s).
\]

It is a local-field preparation/same-site readout experiment averaged over independently selected sites, not a uniform-field susceptibility. No nearest-neighbor lattice inference follows.

A tilted initial configuration can be prepared from zero-field equilibrium samples by accepting a favored spin with probability one and the unfavored spin with probability `e^(-2|h|)`. The acceptance probability is exactly `(1+e^(-2|h|))/2≥1/2`, so at most two equilibrium samples are needed on average. Acquiring those equilibrium samples still has to be charged.

### Reconstructed spectral lemma and derivative deduction

Section 9 of the source represents

\[
B_n(z)=\int(z+\lambda)^{-1}d\nu_n(\lambda),
\qquad A_n(s)=\int e^{-s\lambda}d\nu_n(\lambda).
\]

Joint convergence of all positive Stieltjes tests, including `z=1`, makes the finite measures `dη_n=(1+λ)^(-1)dν_n` tight on `[0,∞]`. The tests `(1+λ)/(z+λ)` have a dense separating linear span: partial fractions make the reciprocal functions an algebra, and the constant test is present. They identify the limiting random measure, including absence of escaped mass at infinity. This reconstructs the stated continuity lemma.

The same proof gives a stronger conclusion: for every integer `k≥0`, `(1+λ)λ^k e^(-sλ)` is continuous, vanishes at infinity, and varies continuously in sup norm for `s` in a compact positive interval. Finite nets plus bounded measure mass give uniform convergence of every derivative. Thus the source's resolvent proof gives joint **`C^∞_loc`** convergence, with no control at `s=0`.

For an equilibrium infinitesimal local field, differentiating the finite-state stationarity identity `π_h L_h=0` and applying Duhamel gives the diagonal response kernel

\[
R_n(t)=-C_n'(t)\ge0.
\]

Therefore `n R_n(sn^(2/3))⇒−A'(s)` on positive compact windows. More generally `n^(1+2k/3)R_n^(k)(sn^(2/3))⇒−A^(k+1)(s)`.

The slow response in a window `[a n^(2/3),b n^(2/3)]` has mass `n^(-1/3)[A(a)−A(b)+o_law(1)]`. Full zero-frequency integrals, disorder expectations, quench response, or uniformly finite fields applied dynamically need additional estimates. This is a standard spectral/FDT consequence, not a new response mechanism.

### Resources, validation, and prior art

With a fixed grid of `K` positive scaled times, an iid trajectory estimate uses at most

\[
B=\left\lceil2n^{2/3}\epsilon^{-2}\log(2K/\delta)\right\rceil
\]

paths for absolute `ε` accuracy of the scaled remanence, with failure probability at most `δ` by Hoeffding. For Gaussian disorder, conditional on the separate unformalized critical mixing upper bound, each equilibrium preparation and horizon uses `n^(5/3+o(1))` uniform-site attempts, with logarithmic amplification for the required initial TV precision. Updating cached dense fields costs `O(n)` arithmetic per attempt, so this elementary acquisition protocol costs `n^(10/3+o(1)) ε^(-2) log(K/δ)` arithmetic, plus `Θ(n²)` coupling input. No matching Rademacher preparation bound is supplied by the functional theorem. Storage is `O(n² b)` for `b`-bit couplings plus `O(nb+K log B)` scratch; precision and arithmetic cannot be treated as free. Efficient observation alone does not validate the source theorem.
