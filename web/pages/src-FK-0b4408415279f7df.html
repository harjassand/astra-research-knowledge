# Pawlowski–Rothkopf direct imaginary-frequency sampler: equation-level audit

**Disposition: scoped negative result for the published local-replacement stochastic update.** The equations in Pawlowski–Rothkopf (PR) match the incoming finite-thermal-anchor process after the action and Fourier measures are normalized consistently. The incoming theorem therefore applies to that specified process under its stated mass, bounded-moment, and fixed-anchor assumptions. An independent weak-coupling calculation finds that the process approaches the vacuum tadpole coefficient, while the correct finite-temperature response contains the thermal tadpole. This does not reproduce the authors' code or their reported λ=24 correlator, does not refute an exact Schwinger–Keldysh contour formulation that retains its full initial-state constraint, and does not establish that every implementation called “direct imaginary frequency” is wrong.

The most temperature-faithful obtainable repair identified here is output-specific certification from ordinary compact thermal Euclidean data, following Mutzel–Tilloy (M–T). For a direct sampler, the repair would instead require deriving and retaining the full KMS/density-matrix boundary coupling or another nonlocal term that demonstrably produces the thermal weights. Neither can be obtained by a Fourier normalization change alone.

## 1. What was audited

The target is the PR prescription in Pawlowski and Rothkopf, “Thermal dynamics on the lattice with exponentially improved accuracy,” [arXiv:1610.09531v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.09531v2), published in *Physics Letters B* 778 (2018), DOI [10.1016/j.physletb.2018.01.037](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2018.01.037), together with their earlier proceedings version “Improved real-time dynamics from imaginary frequency lattice simulations,” [arXiv:1710.02672v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02672v1), DOI [10.1051/epjconf/201817507001](https://doi.org/10.1051/epjconf/201817507001). References below identify the precise sections/equations in the local source copies in `primary_sources/`.

The incoming thermal note and its checks were copied unmodified into `inputs/`. I read its finite-anchor comparison theorem and the first-order covariance test. I also read the original pasted current-capital sources N724 and N725, the N728 bridge note, and N732 Section 3. Those are not premises for this calculation: N724 is a noisy scalar-response information model; N725 is a fixed-input imaginary-time quantum-snapshot model; N728 treats candidate-generator verification; N732 treats calibrated conductivity/DN measurements. Matching phrases such as “imaginary time,” “boundary,” or “anchor” do not transfer their theorems across these distinct access contracts.

The primary arXiv bundles contain source TeX, bibliography, and figures, but no simulation source code or raw correlator tables. P7 reports recording after Δs=1 for 10⁶ configurations and relative statistical error around 10⁻³; its comparison “QM computation” uses a 32-state oscillator Hilbert-space truncation. Those are paper-reported settings, not independently reproduced costs or raw results. The source packages and plotted PDFs support an equation/figure audit only. A bounded search of the arXiv code-links metadata and repositories keyed to the paper title/arXiv IDs found no public implementation. This is not proof that no private or unindexed code exists. In particular, I did not reproduce PR's λ=24 run or quantify its plotted ω=0 offset from raw values.

## 2. Reconstructing the actual update

### Contour and thermal information

PR start from the thermal Schwinger–Keldysh path integral: a compact Euclidean field φ_E on τ̄ ∈ [0,β] samples the density matrix, and its endpoints sew to the forward/backward real-time branches (P7, contour construction before Eq. (1)). They then move the real-time endpoint to infinity, argue that finite-time correlations decouple from the endpoint, rotate the forward branch into the upper complex half-plane, and use a new, noncompact imaginary-time coordinate τ for the dynamical branch (P7, contour discussion preceding Eq. (2)). That τ is distinct from the compact thermal τ̄.

The observable is $G^{++}(\omega)=\langle\phi^+(\omega)\phi^+(-\omega)\rangle$, which P7 says can be obtained from the forward branch in equilibrium (P7 Eq. (3)). The initial condition would impose a Fourier-space relation between the noncompact field and φ_E(τ̄0). P7 explicitly writes the relation schematically as Σ_l φ+(ω_l)=φ_E(τ̄0), then argues that its highest-frequency quadratic penalty makes that constraint irrelevant in the continuum limit and that one mode has vanishing weight at infinite spatial volume. On this basis the authors lift the Matsubara/initial constraint from the frequency-local quadratic action S0. They state that temperature then enters solely through the interaction term (P7, paragraphs before Eq. (4)).

This is the decisive scope point: the exact sewn contour motivates the construction, but the implemented frequency-space S0 is *unconstrained*. The infinite-spatial-volume limb of that rationale is absent in the 0+1-dimensional toy model; the temporal-anchor comparison here instead analyzes the update they implement. The paper's practical temperature channel is not a full sampled KMS boundary condition on the noncompact path. It is a local substitution during the interaction drift.

### Drift and single-slice replacement

For φ⁴ theory, P7 Eq. (5) gives the frequency-space Langevin update

\[
\partial_s\phi^+(\omega_l)=-\frac{\delta S_0}{\delta\phi^+(\omega_l)}-
\sum_j\frac{\delta S_E^{\rm int}}{\delta\phi^+(\tau_j)}
\frac{\delta\phi^+(\tau_j)}{\delta\phi^+(\omega_l)}+\eta(\omega_l),
\]

with \({\cal P}(\omega_l)=(2-2\cos(2\pi l/N_\omega))/a^2\) and

\[
S_0=\sum_l\Delta\omega\,\tfrac12[\mathcal P(\omega_l)+m^2]
|\phi^+(\omega_l)|^2.
\]

The source is independently Langevin-updated on the compact lattice, with noise covariance $2\delta_{ij}/a$. At the distinguished initial-condition slice \(\tau_0\), PR replace \(\phi^+(\tau_0)\) by \(\phi_E(\bar\tau_0)\) **when evaluating the local \(\phi^3\) interaction drift** (P7 Eq. (5) and following text). P8 says the same thing directly: the compact and noncompact fields are updated concurrently with independent Gaussian noises, and the value of \(\phi_E(0)\) is imprinted by replacing \(\phi^+(0)\) in the interaction drift. P8 calls this “an additional constraint on the drift” supplied by the thermal density matrix.

For \(0+1\) dimensions there is one such temporal site. With the periodic inverse DFT, and after changing from the paper's frequency coordinates to the real path coordinates, the specified update is the anchored SDE

\[
dX=-[K_aX+g_a(I-P)X^3+g_a P Y^3]ds+\sqrt2\,dW,
\quad K_a=a(-\Delta_a+m^2),\quad g_a=a\lambda/6,
\]

where $P$ selects the initial slice, $Y$ is the compact thermal source, and the source and target noises are independent. In multiple spatial dimensions $P$ selects that time slice at each spatial site. The DFT is periodic on its finite auxiliary grid, so this is a one-slice anchor on a cycle; the paper itself identifies the resulting finite-volume/convolution artifact and says it vanishes as $N_\omega\to\infty$ (P7 around Eqs. (4)–(5)).

### Fourier normalization and what is intensive

P7 does not spell out a finite-box forward/inverse DFT normalization independently of its action equation. Its Eq. (6) permits an action-consistent convention. Let $F_l=N_\omega^{-1/2}\sum_j e^{-2\pi i lj/N_\omega}x_j$ be the unitary DFT and $\Delta\omega=2\pi/(N_\omega a)$. The convention

\[
\phi_l=\sqrt{a/\Delta\omega}\,F_l
=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_j e^{-2\pi i lj/N_\omega}x_j
\]

gives the position-space quadratic action $a\sum_j[(\nabla_a x_j)^2+m^2x_j^2]/2$ exactly as P7's frequency action $\sum_l\Delta\omega[\mathcal P_l+m^2]|\phi_l|^2/2$. Thus the intensive finite-box spectral coefficient is

\[
\Delta\omega\,\langle|\phi_l|^2\rangle
=a\,\langle|F_l|^2\rangle.
\]

An unnormalized mode product contains a box/Dirac-delta factor and must not be compared across box sizes without this measure. A harmless (2\pi) convention factor can be moved between the transform and frequency measure; it cannot change a thermal-to-vacuum ratio or the drift's invariant law. This action-derived normalization does not establish the authors' exact internal code convention, because no code was available.

## 3. Does the incoming finite-anchor theorem apply?

**Yes, to the finite-replacement SDE above, under the theorem's hypotheses.** The map is exact at the equation level: $P$ is the one-slice indicator; the free drift is $K_aX$; all unanchored quartic coordinates use $X^3$; the selected slice uses $Y^3$; and the compact source has finite, dimension-independent moments for the massive convex oscillator. PR's independent source/target noises give the particularly simple independent-source case. For fixed $a>0$, $m>0$, and fixed $\lambda$, the theorem's $\alpha=am^2$ is positive and its $B$ is finite. It concludes that for a fixed local spatial coordinate and a normalized temporal Fourier mode, the difference from the unanchored noncompact process is at most $O(N_\omega^{-1/2})$ at fixed lattice spacing. Hence the thermal correction from one fixed slice vanishes in the long auxiliary-time limit.

In the continuum with fixed physical auxiliary length $L=N_\omega a$, the physical action gives $g_a=O(a)$, $\alpha=O(a)$, and a bounded-field source discrepancy $B=O(a)$. The comparison bound then scales as $O(\sqrt a)$ for one fixed slice. These are distinct limits: $N_\omega\to\infty$ at fixed $a$ sends $L\to\infty$; $a\to0$ at fixed $L$ refines a fixed physical interval. Neither is a theorem about an exact contour with its boundary condition kept.

The theorem does not automatically cover: a boundary term that couples a macroscopic fraction of the path to the compact thermal path; a hard constraint changing the free covariance; an anchor count or strength growing outside the theorem's regime; a zero-mass/critical limit α→0; or a nonlinearly changed stochastic generator. Those are changed processes, to be analyzed separately.

## 4. Independent weak-coupling countertest

The nonperturbative comparison theorem gives a qualitative large-domain limit. A separate first-order calculation identifies *which temperature factor is lost*.

Let $C=K_a^{-1}$, $c_0=C_{ii}$, and $Q=I-P$. At $\lambda=0$, $X$ and $Y$ are independent centered Gaussians. Differentiate the stationary covariance equation of the target SDE at λ=0. Itô's formula and Wick contraction \(\mathbb E[X_i^3X_j]=3c_0C_{ij}\) give

\[
K_a C_1+C_1K_a=-\frac{a c_0}{2}(QC+CQ),
\qquad C_1=\left.\partial_\lambda\operatorname{Cov}(X)\right|_{0^+}.
\]

The source term is absent at this order because \(\mathbb E[Y_i^3X_j]=0\). The fact that $Y$'s law depends on $\lambda$ does not alter this first derivative: the source force is already multiplied by λ, and the zeroth-order target is independent and centered. For a Fourier mode $q$, the DFT-intensive derivative is

\[
\left.\partial_\lambda G^{\rm anchor}_q\right|_{0^+}
=-\frac{c_0(1-1/N_\omega)}{2[m^2+\mathcal P(\omega_q)]^2}.
\]

By contrast, the standard compact finite-temperature path has

\[
c_\beta=\frac1\beta\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{\omega_n^2+m^2}
=\frac{1}{2m}\coth(\beta m/2),
\quad
\left.\partial_\lambda G^{\rm thermal}(i\eta)\right|_{0^+}
=-\frac{c_\beta}{2(m^2+\eta^2)^2}.
\]

This is the standard one-quartic-insertion/tadpole result, continued to $i\eta$. The noncompact target has $c_0\to\frac1\beta\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}(\omega^2+m^2)^{-1}=1/(2m)$. Thus the PR local-replacement derivative tends to the vacuum coefficient, not the finite-temperature one. The missing fraction tends to $1-\tanh(\beta m/2)>0$ for every finite $\beta$.

The independent code `reconstruction/anchor_fourier_check.py` solves this finite-dimensional Sylvester equation directly and compares it with the closed form. For $\beta=m=1$, $N_\tau=16$, and $a=1/16$, the compact-source free variance is 1.08159852 (continuum 1.08197671), while the long noncompact target variance is 0.49975604 (continuum 0.5). At $\eta=0.5$ the continuum first derivatives are −0.34623255 (thermal) and −0.16 (one anchor), leaving 53.7883% of the thermal first-order correction absent. At $N_\omega=512$, mode $q=3$ has $\omega=0.58905$; direct matrix derivative −0.1374621331770815 agrees with the closed form to $4.1\times10^{-15}$, and the Sylvester residual is $1.04\times10^{-12}$. Its thermal-lattice comparison is −0.29808503602665015, so 53.8849% of the thermal first-order magnitude is missing. This is an independent, finite-matrix equation check—not a replay of PR's sampler, not a finite-$\lambda$ simulation, and not a raw-data reproduction.

The sign agrees with the direction described in PR: a less-negative interaction correction leaves the direct-update zero-frequency correlator above the standard thermal Matsubara value. Their P7 text and figures report this offset and say its meaning needs further clarification. Agreement of direction is suggestive, not proof that this calculation explains their full λ=24 difference; finite-volume effects and the standard simulation's finite $N_\tau$ also matter.

## 5. Could a boundary term or integration variable fix it?

### Retaining the boundary condition is a genuine escape from this theorem

The exact Keldysh construction begins with density-matrix sewing. The finite-anchor theorem applies only after replacing that global condition by the one-slice interaction-force substitution and dropping the condition in $S_0$. If the boundary constraint is retained in a way that changes the target's free covariance or makes an $O(1)$ contribution to bulk response, the local-anchor comparison assumptions fail. That would be a distinct method and could restore temperature dependence. The source itself warns that simply imposing periodic or Dirichlet endpoints on a finite noncompact path gives the wrong free $G^{++}$ and proposes extending the path; it does not supply a derived finite-volume boundary correction that restores the thermal interacting measure (P8, paragraphs before Eq. (4)).

The right repair is therefore not “add an arbitrary boundary term.” It is to derive the finite-β KMS/density-matrix influence on the noncompact branch, include its normalization and correlations, and verify that the resulting process has the target thermal correlator. A hard point constraint can change even the temperature-independent free theory. A nonlocal boundary functional could be adequate, but its kernel and cost must follow from the contour and be tested against the standard compact path. The current evidence does not show such a term in the published local-replacement update.

### A correct Fourier change does not repair temperature

The time/frequency transform is an invertible linear coordinate change. If the action, Jacobian (constant for this transform), Langevin mobility, and noise covariance are transformed consistently, the stationary law is unchanged. The factor $a$ from the action and the frequency quadrature $\Delta\omega$ are essential for matching the continuum operator, but a missing factor that merely rescales plotted mode amplitudes cannot insert the Bose factor \(\coth(\beta m/2)\). Conversely, choosing a different preconditioner or noise after changing variables may change the generator, but then it is a new stochastic process; its invariant law needs proof. There is no coordinate convention that turns a single $O(1)$ boundary-slice force into an extensive thermal bulk contribution while preserving the same action and fluctuation-dissipation balance.

## 6. Temperature-correct observable route: M–T certification

Mutzel and Tilloy, “Certified spectral functions from lattice Monte Carlo data,” [arXiv:2606.09791v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09791v1), provide the strongest practical comparator found for this output. Their exact finite-lattice Källén–Lehmann relation is

\[
C_\beta(\tau)=\int_0^\infty \rho_E(d\Delta)
[e^{-\Delta\tau}+e^{-\Delta(\beta-\tau)}],
\quad \rho_E\ge0,
\]

for reflection-positive actions (their Eqs. (5)–(6)); the finite-temperature response measure is $d\mu=(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})d\rho_E$. Therefore the requested regular response is the **linear functional**

\[
\chi_{\beta,A}(i\eta)=\int_0^\infty
\underbrace{\frac{2\Delta(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})}{\Delta^2+\eta^2}}_{G_\eta(\Delta)}
\,\rho_E(d\Delta),\qquad \eta>0.
\]

M–T's dual SDP bounds linear functionals over all nonnegative spectra consistent with the measured compact-Euclidean correlator and its covariance. Their hierarchy replaces the infinite pointwise kernel inequality by finite piecewise-affine envelopes; each feasible dual solution gives an outer bound even before the envelope hierarchy converges. For fixed $\beta$ and $\eta>0$, $G_\eta$ is bounded and can be enclosed by those envelopes. This returns an interval for the requested observable without claiming to recover a unique spectrum. A lattice-specific kernel should be used at finite $a$; continuum response then requires a separately controlled extrapolation.

This is temperature-correct in the sense that its input is a conventional finite-$\beta$ Euclidean correlator and its target is the thermal spectral functional. It is not a direct-frequency sampler, an acquisition shortcut, or a universal sign-problem solution. The paper assumes access to reliable configurations and approximately Gaussian errors with known covariance; its example uses $10^2$–$10^6$ configurations, 129 time-separation constraints, and $N_v=700$ envelope intervals in one reported convergence analysis. Its 2D \(\phi^4\) example reports integrated autocorrelation small compared with its measurement separation. Sampling/equilibration, autocorrelation analysis, covariance/bootstrap, the SDP, precision checks, and continuum extrapolation remain paid costs. The arXiv source archive contains paper source/figures but no runnable solver or Monte Carlo code, so this is a source-derived algorithmic comparator rather than an executed certificate.

Other primary alternatives do different jobs:

- **Tupitsyn et al., [arXiv:2102.11418v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11418v2), “Real-frequency response functions at finite temperature.”** Diagrammatic Monte Carlo analytically sums internal Matsubara frequencies using spectral representations of dressed propagators/effective interactions, then samples the remaining momentum/spectral integrals on the real-frequency axis. It computes a jellium plasmon linewidth, but needs those auxiliary spectral inputs and a controlled diagrammatic expansion/order limit; this is not a black-box general thermal solver.
- **Jeckelmann, DDMRG, PRB 66, 045114 (2002), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.66.045114.** Correction-vector DMRG obtains broadened spectra for ground-state, low-dimensional lattice systems. Broadening, finite-size scaling, and tensor-network resources are part of the method; this is not automatically a finite-temperature Gibbs-response algorithm.
- **PR itself.** Its rational imaginary-frequency spectral kernel can improve a reconstruction *if the input correlator is the correct thermal one*. A better-conditioned post-processing kernel does not validate the sampled thermal measure.

## 7. Exact conclusion and limits

1. The paper's **implemented local replacement** is a one-slice thermal force on an otherwise noncompact, unconstrained path. This equation-level prescription lies inside the incoming finite-anchor theorem's scope for fixed positive mass, fixed anchor count, and bounded massive-source moments.
2. The theorem and independent first-order calculation both say that one fixed thermal slice does not carry a finite bulk thermal response as the auxiliary domain grows. The derivative tends to the vacuum tadpole; the finite-β target has the Bose-enhanced tadpole.
3. This does **not** refute the initial-state Schwinger–Keldysh contour, nor a future implementation that keeps its exact KMS constraint or derives an $O(1)$ nonlocal correction. Such a model is outside the finite-anchor theorem and remains to be checked.
4. A Fourier normalization or correctly transformed integration variable cannot repair the missing thermal factor. An altered mobility/noise could change the sampler, but then correctness must be re-established.
5. The PR authors' plotted ω=0 discrepancy has the sign predicted by the weak-coupling test, but no source code/raw data reproduction was available. The finite-λ numerical explanation remains **unknown**.
6. For a real temperature-correct response output available today, M–T's positivity-constrained direct functional bounds from standard compact thermal Euclidean data are a concrete alternative, conditional on a reliable sampling/error model and reflection positivity. They provide an interval, not a recovered exact spectrum or a free data source.

No new efficient many-body algorithm, generic thermal solver, physical-device result, or priority claim is established here.
