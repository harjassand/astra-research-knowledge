# Independent check: IIC reduced-HTC soundness fails generically

Status: independently reconstructed theorem-level counterexample, exact replay passed. This is a correction/audit result; novelty as an original scientific observation has not been established. It invalidates the stated sufficient criterion, not every use of coefficient substitution or iterative causal identification. The released implementation could not be accessed (HTTP 401), so no implementation-level false-positive count is claimed.

## Question and interface

Given an acyclic mixed graph, exact observational covariance, and sound knowledge of some directed coefficients, does IIC v1 Definition 4.2 imply the rational generic identifiability conclusion of Theorem 4.3? Sources: [IIC v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09309v1), submitted 10 April 2026, by Ziyi Ding and Xiao-Ping Zhang. Exact statement: main text PDF p.4; proof Appendix A.1 p.12; Algorithm 1 p.5. This graph has a bow, which the stated interface permits. Errors can be jointly Gaussian; no distributional side information is supplied.

I independently read the paper and constructed code before consulting r02_causal's files. The colleague's interpretation was not used as a premise. Agreement is a second reconstruction, not external validation.

## Author claims and our deductions

Theorem 4.3 claims that known target-parent coefficients K plus a sibling-free system of half-treks from non-descendant W to remaining parents R suffices for identification. Appendix A.1 acknowledges nonzero Cov(X_w,epsilon_i), differentiates while other parameters stay fixed, then uses a target-only full-rank derivative to conclude identification. Our deduction: this cannot rule out a covariance-preserving curve that changes target and nuisance parameters jointly. The generic counterexample below satisfies the premises and changes the target coefficient.

The paper's broad claim that iterative propagation is absent from previous graphical methods also fails against directly read qID 2017 and ACID 2020 algorithms. This is separate from its soundness failure.

## Three-variable construction and exact witness

Order variables A,X,Y, equations A=epsilon_A, X=aA+epsilon_X, Y=bX+epsilon_Y. Directed edges are A->X and X->Y. Bidirected edges are A<->X and A<->Y; epsilon_X and epsilon_Y are uncorrelated. Let T(a,b)=I-B(a,b), with B[target,source], and Sigma=T^{-1} Omega T^{-T}.

For fixed positive-definite Sigma, the sole forbidden error correlation is

    Omega_XY = Sigma_XY - b Sigma_XX - a Sigma_AY + ab Sigma_AX = 0.

Thus, for free t replacing a, set

    b(t) = (Sigma_XY - t Sigma_AY)/(Sigma_XX - t Sigma_AX),
    Omega(t) = T(t,b(t)) Sigma T(t,b(t))^T.

This enforces Omega_XY=0 identically and preserves Sigma identically. T is unit triangular and invertible. Therefore Omega(t) is positive definite for every finite t with nonzero denominator, by invertible congruence; positivity is not an assumption left to numerical tolerance.

Exact base model: a=1/2, b=2/3,

    Omega0 = [[1,1/5,1/4],[1/5,1,0],[1/4,0,1]],
    Sigma  = [[1,7/10,43/60],[7/10,29/20,131/120],[43/60,131/120,163/90]].

Exact alternative: a=3/5, b=397/618,

    Omega1 = [[1,1/10,55/206],[1/10,97/100,0],[55/206,0,170919/169744]].

Both error matrices have leading principal minors 1,24/25,359/400, strictly positive. Every graph-required directed coefficient and error covariance is nonzero in both models. The target b differs, while Sigma is exactly identical.

Theorem 4.3 permits K=empty and is refuted already on this graph: R={X}, W={X}, the trivial length-zero half-trek is allowed, X is neither Y nor a descendant nor a sibling of Y. The paper itself uses trivial half-treks in B.5-B.8. Algorithm 1 line 12 separately requires nonempty K; hence the three-variable example alone does not isolate that line's propagation failure.

## Four-variable construction satisfying nonempty K

Add Z=epsilon_Z, independent of every other error, and Z->Y with c=3/7. Now

    Y=bX+cZ+epsilon_Y.

Z is independent of X, so c=Sigma_ZY/Sigma_ZZ is identifiable from observations. The two models above extend with the same c and Var(epsilon_Z)=1. In order A,X,Z,Y their common covariance is

    [[1,7/10,0,43/60],
     [7/10,29/20,0,131/120],
     [0,0,1,3/7],
     [43/60,131/120,3/7,8797/4410]].

The known-parent set K={Z} is sound even without an experiment or externally supplied number. Remaining parent R={X}, source W={X}; sib(Y)={A}; desc(Y)=empty. All Definition 4.2 conditions hold. Subtracting cZ reproduces the three-variable fiber. Thus Theorem 4.3 is false with nonempty K, and Algorithm 1's propagation predicate accepts a generically nonidentified coefficient after a sound seed. If the paper's own weakened 'standard HTC' initializer accepts b earlier, that adds another false positive and does not rescue the reduced rule. An actual released-code output remains unverified.

## Genericity, not an exceptional witness

Write the original noise parameters u=Omega_AA, v=Omega_XX, w=Omega_YY, p=Omega_AX, q=Omega_AY. The derivative of the fixed-Sigma fiber is

    b'(t) = (Sigma_AX Sigma_XY - Sigma_AY Sigma_XX)/(Sigma_XX - t Sigma_AX)^2,
    b'(a) = -q/(v+a p).

q is nonzero for this graph's exact support, and v+a p is a nonzero polynomial except on a proper algebraic subset. At every admissible generic point b changes along the fiber near a. All required nonzero coefficients/error correlations persist in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Hence b is generically locally nonidentifiable, and therefore cannot be rationally generically identifiable. The four-variable extension preserves the fiber and known c. This generic proof, rather than the two numeric points, is the decisive result.

At the rational base point the full covariance Jacobian has rank 6 on seven parameters (a,b,u,v,w,p,q). Its b column has rank 1, but deleting b's column still leaves rank 6. A kernel vector is (44/7,-10/7,0,-88/35,5/14,-44/7,1). Its b component is nonzero. This directly distinguishes a nonzero target derivative from genuine local target identification.

## Exact lost condition in original HTC

[Foygel, Draisma, Drton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5552v2), Theorem 1, requires a total ordering with every source w in W intersect htr(i) already solved before i. Its Section 9 proof uses residualized source rows when w belongs to htr(i), rather than always using raw Sigma_w,r. Its Algorithm 1 repeatedly expands SolvedNodes.

Here X belongs to htr(Y) via Y<->A->X, despite not being a descendant or sibling of Y. The source X is contaminated:

    Cov(X,epsilon_Y) = a q != 0 generically.

Its incoming a is not identified, so epsilon_X=X-aA cannot be formed from known quantities. With a genuinely known, the valid formula would be

    b = [Cov(X-aA,Y-cZ)]/[Cov(X-aA,X)].

Reduced HTC's no-directed-descendant restriction fails to enforce this source dependency. Source non-sibling does not imply source exogeneity. In the original HTC proof, residualization and source solvability are load-bearing assumptions. IIC's Step 6 also wrongly conflates generic local identifiability with global rational recovery; finite polynomial maps can have several branches. Our counterexample fails even local identification, so that second issue is unnecessary for the rejection.

## Prior art and novelty limits

[Chen, Kumor, Bareinboim 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/chen17f.html), Section 4 pp.761-762 and Algorithm 1 qID, accepts IDEdges, uses identified coefficients to form auxiliary variables, revisits previously unresolved edge sets, and repeats until no new coefficients appear. It also incorporates limited experimental information. Section 3 provides a multi-stage worked example. This directly predates the advertised iteration mechanism.

[Kumor, Cinelli, Bareinboim 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/kumor20a.html), Section 3.4 and Algorithm 2 ACID p.5507, iteratively enlarges identified coefficients and recomputes auxiliary variables/cutsets until no change. It explicitly tracks unremoved ancestors of candidate probes. Section 2.1 derives the residual-covariance formula used above. Theorem 3.5 proves subsumption of several earlier criteria, as claimed by those authors; I have not independently reconstructed that full theorem.

The original HTC also has recursive propagation across solved source nodes. These observations refute the blanket absence-of-propagation claim. They do not by themselves prove that every possible corrected IIC implementation is subsumed by ACID, nor that the exact four-node counterexample has never been recorded elsewhere.

## Concrete downstream use and next falsifiable step

Future identification software should preserve a coefficient's recovery formula and source dependencies alongside its graph label. Before solving a target linear system, ensure the chosen observable or residualized probe has zero covariance with target noise, or explicitly known contamination. Then check that the coefficient matrix is generically nonsingular. Parent-count reduction alone is unsafe.

A sound conditional repair follows immediately from the structural equation: let Z_w be a rationally constructible linear combination of observations with Cov(Z_w,epsilon_i)=0, and let K's coefficients be rationally known. If M_wr=Cov(Z_w,X_r) is square and generically invertible, then remaining coefficients equal M^{-1} times Cov(Z_w,X_i-sum_K beta_ki X_k), hence are rationally generically identifiable. For raw probes, exclude every node having an ancestor in {i} union sib(i); for residualized epsilon_w probes, know every incoming coefficient of w and require w not sibling/target. This is an established IV/residualization idea, not a new method claimed here. General graphical rank conditions still need proof or existing auxiliary-variable criteria.

Use the three- and four-node exact models as rejection fixtures. For local target diagnostics, require full covariance Jacobian rank to increase on adding the target column to nuisance columns; a rank test alone cannot certify global rational identification. Next falsifiable step: obtain the released code once accessible and replay both graph fixtures, record its source revision, accepted source set, status and alleged formula; then trace which eligibility restriction is missing. The theorem counterexample is already settled independently of that replay.
