# Autocatalysis and selectivity: shared-environment no-go and test rule

## Result

For positive seeded replicators whose per-capita growth is a species-specific constant times one shared, time-varying catalytic exposure, selectivity is exactly the integrated difference in per-capita rates. A common autocatalytic drive can increase total product by an arbitrarily large factor while leaving every product ratio unchanged. Equal-rate competitors do not select between themselves merely because they replicate autocatalytically or deplete a common substrate.

This is an exact kinetic identity, not a claim of a new general theorem. It gives a useful falsification boundary: composition changes require differential per-capita gain or loss, a nonmultiplicative pathway such as spontaneous initiation or cross-inhibition, or measurement/model error. The result is limited to positive seeded populations and a common-field deterministic interface.

## Exact interface and quantitative statement

For $i=1,\ldots,m$, assume $x_i(t)>0$ is absolutely continuous on $[0,T]$ and

\[
\dot x_i(t)=\bigl(a_i u(t)-d_i(t)\bigr)x_i(t)+r_i(t),
\]

where $u(t)\ge 0$ is a shared measurable exposure with finite integral, $a_i$ is the species-specific autocatalytic efficiency, $d_i(t)\ge0$ is an integrable loss rate, and $r_i(t)$ is the net residual flux outside this multiplicative family. Require $r_i/x_i$ to be integrable. Define

\[
U_T=\int_0^T u(t)\,dt,\quad
L_{ij}(T)=\log\frac{x_i(T)/x_j(T)}{x_i(0)/x_j(0)},\quad
R_{ij}(T)=\int_0^T\left(\frac{|r_i|}{x_i}+\frac{|r_j|}{x_j}\right)dt.
\]

Then

\[
L_{ij}(T)=(a_i-a_j)U_T-\int_0^T(d_i-d_j)dt
+\int_0^T\left(\frac{r_i}{x_i}-\frac{r_j}{x_j}\right)dt
\]

and therefore

\[
\left|L_{ij}(T)-\left((a_i-a_j)U_T-\int_0^T(d_i-d_j)dt\right)\right|\le R_{ij}(T).
\]

In the equal-efficiency, equal-loss, residual-free case, $x_i/x_j$ is a first integral. For $m$ species with common $a_i=a$, common loss $d_i=d$, and $r_i=0$, the entire composition vector $p_i=x_i/\sum_jx_j$ is constant, for any shared feedback law that changes $u(t)$.

### Proof

Divide each equation by its positive state and subtract the $j$ equation from the $i$ equation:

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{x_i}{x_j}
=(a_i-a_j)u-(d_i-d_j)+\frac{r_i}{x_i}-\frac{r_j}{x_j}.
\]

Integration gives the identity. The residual bound follows from the triangle inequality. The proof does not require $u$ to be constant or independent of the $x_i$; it can encode shared substrate depletion, a common free-catalyst concentration, light-driven active-species abundance, dilution, or feedback, provided those effects enter every per-capita replication rate through the same scalar factor.

## Finite-batch mass-action counterexample

Take a closed batch with species $S,X,Y$ and irreversible reactions

\[
S+X\xrightarrow{k}2X,\qquad S+Y\xrightarrow{k}2Y.
\]

Under deterministic mass action,

\[
\dot x=ksx,\quad \dot y=ksy,\quad \dot s=-ks(x+y).
\]

Start at $x(0)=9$, $y(0)=1$, and $s(0)=100$ in common concentration units. Then $d\log(x/y)/dt=0$, while $x+y+s=110$. As substrate is exhausted, total replicator concentration increases from 10 to 110 (11-fold); the limiting final concentrations are $x=99,y=11$. Thus the selectivity multiplier is exactly

\[
\frac{(x/y)_{\rm final}}{(x/y)_{\rm initial}}=1.
\]

The limit is reached because $s(t)$ decreases to zero: since $x+y\ge10$, $\dot s\le-10ks$ for $k>0$. This is a direct finite-batch counterexample to inferring compositional amplification from total autocatalytic yield. It is not a counterexample to models with unequal autocatalytic efficiencies or mutual inhibition.

If the two rate constants differ, then

\[
\log\frac{x(T)/y(T)}{x(0)/y(0)}=(k_X-k_Y)\int_0^T s(t)dt.
\]

Differential exposure changes the ratio only when a differential kinetic parameter is present. With losses $d_X,d_Y$, subtract $\int_0^T(d_X-d_Y)dt$. A target log-selectivity $q$ requires signed differential drive at least $q-R_{XY}$; no amount of common exposure meets a positive target when both parameter differences and the residual budget are zero.

## Chemical interpretation and limits

The mass-action specialization is $S+X_i\to2X_i$, with common substrate concentration $s(t)$, $u(t)=s(t)$, and $a_i=k_i$. A common externally controlled catalyst or photochemical activity can be folded into $u(t)$ only when it multiplies all per-capita replication rates by the same factor. This does not cover saturable species-specific enzyme complexes, unequal binding modes, cross-catalysis, spontaneous production, product-dependent degradation, stereochemical inhibition, or small-copy-number stochastic fixation.

An endpoint distribution alone does not isolate autocatalytic selection when a network also has seed-independent formation $b_i(t)$, sequence-specific decay, or template-dependent catalyst occupancy. In the generalized equation, $r_i$ must be measured or bounded in the same mixture. No-template data can estimate spontaneous synthesis, but are a valid subtraction only if they reproduce catalyst occupancy and light/substrate history of the seeded mixture.

## Primary-literature boundary

Bartus et al. (first published 1 February 2026) report a UVA-driven foldamer replicator library with 78 dimers, calmodulin (CaM) catalysis, both non-autocatalytic and templated autocatalytic formation, and replicator breakdown. The authors report that replication rates exceed non-autocatalytic formation by about an order of magnitude, observe seed-dependent rate increases, and find sequence-dependent light/CaM effects consistent with competition for limited CaM binding sites. At 6 micromolar CaM, reported total steady-state dimer concentrations at 2.55, 3.8, and 5.1 mW cm$^{-2}$ are 1.8, 5.5, and 32.5 micromolar. They also compare 6 versus 12 micromolar CaM and an equilibrium dynamic-covalent-library control. These are source-reported results, not reproduced here. Because the source network has mixed pathways, the shared-field identity is a discriminator for a reduced mechanism, not a reanalysis or refutation of that experiment.

Primary source: [Bartus et al., “Light-Driven Competitive Selection in a Protein-Catalyzed Dissipative Peptide Replication,” Angewandte Chemie International Edition (2026), DOI 10.1002/ange.202518911](https://doi.org/10.1002/ange.202518911). The full text describes both spontaneous/non-autocatalytic synthesis and templated replication, along with degradation; its reported mechanism motivates route-resolved flux measurements. The figures report three independent repeats; that count alone does not establish power for the proposed test.

## Experimental design rule

**Measure differential per-capita flux before turning up the shared drive.** In a two-replicator assay, a common light pulse, substrate feed, or catalyst pool changes integrated exposure. It creates compositional selection only through a measured difference in autocatalytic efficiency/loss or through a nonmultiplicative interaction. If calibrated $a_i$ and $d_i$ are equal within uncertainty, the reduced model predicts retention of the initial composition despite shared resource depletion.

To test both the neutral prediction and the rate-difference prediction, use two pairs from a fixed library: (1) a matched-efficiency pair with $a_i\approx a_j$ and $d_i\approx d_j$, and (2) a positive-control pair with independently measured $a_i-a_j\ne0$ and closely matched binding/degradation. Run reciprocal seed ratios (1:1 and 9:1) at fixed total seed concentration. A compact dose matrix can reuse demonstrated conditions from the primary paper: CaM at 6 and 12 micromolar with UVA at 5.1 mW cm$^{-2}$, plus 6 micromolar CaM at 3.8 mW cm$^{-2}$. Keep monomer concentration, buffer, temperature, vessel geometry, mixing, and total seed fixed within each contrast. Record a time course through the 4-hour window used in the primary study rather than comparing only endpoints.

Acquire for each run:

- calibrated $x_i(t)$ concentrations and degradation products by quantitative LC-MS/HPLC with sequence-specific standards;
- total precursor/substrate concentration, free and bound CaM (for example, by fluorescence binding or validated native mass spectrometry), and incident UVA fluence at the sample;
- autocatalytic $a_i$ from single-template seed titrations, and losses $d_i$ from a pulse-chase/degradation assay under the same light and buffer conditions;
- spontaneous/non-autocatalytic formation fluxes from no-seed controls, with occupancy-matched nonreplicating template analogs where possible.

Compute $u(t)$ from measured shared substrate and active catalyst, or use a validated common activity reporter; integrate it numerically and compare observed log-ratio change with $\Delta a\,U_T-\int_0^T\Delta d(t)dt$. The matched-efficiency pair tests ratio invariance; the positive-control pair tests the predicted slope and sign. If ratio changes remain after exposure/loss correction, the common-field model is falsified.

Matched controls are:

1. Protein-free seeded reactions with the same light and precursors.
2. No-seed reactions with the same protein, precursor, light, and buffer to quantify initiation.
3. A binding-competent, nonreplicating analog at matched total template occupancy to separate binding competition from templating.
4. The equilibrium exchange control with identical initial monomers and protein to separate dissipative selection from equilibrium affinity enrichment.

A no-seed control without occupancy matching is not a sufficient subtraction for a seed-dependent shared catalyst.

Use independent vials as experimental units and randomize positions across the illumination field. Choose vial count from a pilot estimate of standard deviation $\sigma$ for the replicate log-ratio statistic. Under a normal approximation, detecting a predicted between-group contrast $|q|$ at two-sided 5% size and 80% power requires about

\[
n\ge2(1.96+0.84)^2\sigma^2/q^2\approx15.7\sigma^2/q^2
\]

independent vials per group. This is a planning approximation, not a guarantee for non-Gaussian or censored LC-MS data. Pre-register contrasts and assay detection limits.

## Costs, outcomes, and evidence status

The assay consumes the two replicators, precursor/monomer pool, CaM, buffer, calibration standards, and nonreplicating analog; requires UVA illumination with measured energy/fluence; and uses LC-MS/HPLC, occupancy measurement, and pulse-chase instrument time. Acquisition also includes $a_i$, $d_i$, $u(t)$, detection-limit, and variance calibrations. Analysis requires time-course quantification, uncertainty propagation, and numerical exposure integration. Report photon energy, reaction volume, mole amounts, elapsed time, vial count, instrument time, and standards rather than only concentration selectivity.

- **Shared-field model survives:** reciprocal seed ratios follow the exposure/loss prediction within propagated uncertainty. The neutral pair retains its initial ratios. This supports common-field amplification but not selection by scarcity alone.
- **Shared-field model fails:** after route, degradation, exposure, and assay corrections, a reproducible residual depends on competitor identity or catalyst occupancy, or changes sign across the dose matrix. This separates sequence-specific interaction from simple shared-field autocatalysis but does not uniquely identify the responsible pathway.
- **Unresolved:** source flux, active-catalyst exposure, or degradation cannot be measured under matched occupancy. An endpoint PIAF/LIAF or product ratio then remains descriptive, not a causal estimate of autocatalytic selectivity.

The bounded internal check uses the common-rate model and the alternative differential-rate model with the same initial substrate and seed amounts. Before calculation, the falsifier was fixed as any change in $X:Y$ under equal rates, or any claim that a positive selectivity factor follows from total yield alone. The common-rate calculation yields an 11-fold total increase and unit selectivity multiplier; the differential-rate comparator changes the ratio by exactly $(k_X-k_Y)\int s(t)dt$. The identity proves this for every positive initial seed pair and every finite substrate amount, while the numeric fixture provides a concrete check. Arithmetic cost is zero beyond the derivation; a physical replay would incur the assay costs listed above. The ratio identity and fixture are proved directly under their displayed deterministic assumptions. Experimental values are source-reported. Transfer to the CaM/foldamer mixture is untested, and novelty/priority are unassessed. No stochastic or nature-wide claim is made.
