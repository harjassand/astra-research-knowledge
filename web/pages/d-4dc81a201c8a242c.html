# RNA sequence-to-ensemble control: prior-art attack and scoped rejection

**Assessment (8 October 2026).** I did not find a defensible new RNA ensemble-design algorithm after reconstructing the closest existing methods. The natural candidate—choose an RNA sequence whose target structure has high Boltzmann probability (or low ensemble defect), perhaps while controlling a broader distribution of folds—is already the explicit objective of established inverse-folding software and is directly covered by the 2026 SamplingDesign framework for arbitrary per-sequence objectives. Cotranscriptional population/pathway models also exist. A new claim would need a concrete interface beyond those methods, together with a fair computational or experimental result. None is present in the supplied work, and I do not manufacture one.

This is a **principled rejection of the generic novelty claim**, not a claim that the biological problem is solved. An integrated optimizer for measured, multi-condition cotranscriptional response remains a possible research direction, but its algorithmic novelty and performance are `UNKNOWN` until a precise objective, implementation, and matched test distinguish it from the existing methods below.

## Candidate and interface

The candidate family considered was: given a target secondary structure `y`, allowed sequence positions/bases, temperature and solution condition, return sequence `x` that controls the folding ensemble. For a pseudoknot-free thermodynamic model, write

`p_x(σ | θ) = exp[-E_θ(x,σ)/(RT)] / Z_θ(x)`, where `Z_θ(x) = Σ_σ exp[-E_θ(x,σ)/(RT)]`.

The simplest output is target occupancy `p_x(y | θ)`. A common alternative is normalized ensemble defect, the expected fraction of nucleotides whose paired/unpaired status differs from `y`. A true distribution-control objective would instead specify a desired distribution `q_θ` over structures or biologically defined macrostates and score a quantity such as `D_KL(q_θ || p_x(·|θ))`, or a list of observable expectations. SamplingDesign's arbitrary-objective framework could in principle optimize a computable KL or observable score as well, though the paper does not report that evaluation; merely changing the scalar score is therefore a weak novelty boundary. The distinction matters: with three structures `(target, A, B)`, distributions `(0.9,0.1,0)` and `(0.9,0,0.1)` have identical target occupancy but predict different outputs whenever the assay distinguishes A from B. Thus the old target-vs-off-target mass certificate is not itself a full-distribution control method.

**Subsumption argument.** SamplingDesign defines a distribution over sequences compatible with the target and optimizes the expected value of an arbitrary sequence objective by Monte Carlo estimates of both objective and score-function gradient; it returns the best sampled sequence. Set that objective to `f(x)=p_x(y|θ)` or `f(x)=NED(x,y)`. This is exactly the standard target-probability/ensemble-defect design problem, expressed with a different search procedure. Its paper already evaluates both objectives and reports improvements over SAMFEO and NEMO on Eterna100. Consequently, proposing a new local search, sequence sampler, or heuristic that only optimizes one of these same objectives does not clear the novelty bar.

The exact equilibrium occupancy certificate is also already present in the supplied work: if `A` is the target basin, `B` its complement, and `ΔF=F_B-F_A`, then `P(A)=1/(1+exp(-ΔF/RT))`; hence `P(A)≥1-δ` iff `ΔF≥RT log((1-δ)/δ)`. At 298 K, a 95% target-occupancy claim needs `ΔF≥1.74 kcal/mol`. This is a useful certificate, but extending it to a search over sequences is an optimizer choice, not a distinct theorem. See [the prior folding-landscape result](../../../biomolecular_design/children/folding_landscape/RESULT.md) and the older [biomolecular report](../../../biomolecular_design/FINAL_REPORT.md).

## Primary-literature boundary

The most decisive prior is Tang et al., **SamplingDesign** (Nature Communications, 20 February 2026; [DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67901-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67901-3)). It formulates target-structure RNA design as continuous optimization over coupled distributions on valid sequences, samples to estimate arbitrary objectives and gradients, and returns the best observed sequence. Its paper explicitly uses target Boltzmann probability and ensemble defect, uses Turner 2004 parameters/ViennaRNA, and evaluates Eterna100 and other pseudoknot-free sets. The reported configuration uses 2,500 samples and ViennaRNA v2.6.4 for final reevaluation. It reports stronger in-silico target probability and defect than SAMFEO/NEMO on the benchmark, including longer puzzles. The authors state that wet-lab/chemical-probing validation is future work. Code and data are available at [SamplingDesign](https://github.com/weiyutang1010/SamplingDesign) and [the archived software DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17674021). This is direct prior art for the proposed generic equilibrium objective, not evidence that its predictions are experimentally correct.

The closest earlier objective-level prior is Zhou et al., **SAMFEO** (Bioinformatics, 2023; [DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btad252](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad252); [code](https://github.com/shanry/SAMFEO)). It explicitly minimizes `1 - p(target | sequence)` or ensemble defect, with a multifrontier mutation search, and benchmarks against RNAinverse-pf, NUPACK, NEMO, MODENA, and other methods. It reports Eterna100 and long-16S structure design. So neither using ensemble probability instead of MFE nor retaining multiple candidate sequences is a new contribution by itself.

For multi-target or intentionally complex energy landscapes, Höner zu Siederdissen et al.'s **RNAdesign** (Biopolymers, 2013; [DOI 10.1002/bip.22337](https://doi.org/10.1002/bip.22337); [software description](https://www.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de/~choener/software/RNAdesign.html)) takes multiple structural targets and offers ensemble-defect, constrained partition-function, and relative-energy objectives. NUPACK and Frnakenstein are additional multi-target precedents. The recently published **Probabilistic RNA Designability** work (Bioinformatics, 2026; [DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btag246](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag246)) adds an interpretable approximation to target Boltzmann probability and a linear-time dynamic program for optimizing its decomposition bound; it is about an upper bound/designability analysis, not a sequence optimizer, but closes another easy “ensemble probability is unexplored” argument.

Equilibrium models are not interchangeable with cotranscriptional control. Xu et al.'s **landscape-zooming** method ([J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2022; DOI 10.1021/acs.jctc.1c01233](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.1c01233)) builds coarse-grained helix partitions and predicts time-dependent populations/pathways; it was applied to SRP and HIV-1 TAR and compared with experiments. Earlier helix-based kinetic methods and the 2025 review of cotranscriptional folding models ([Jin & Chen, DOI 10.1016/j.csbj.2025.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2025.06.005)) cover the surrounding predictor family. A 2025 RBM paper also targets metabolite-responsive RNA switches ([DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-66265-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66265-y)). In the supplied work, the reversible U↔C↔B model already gives an exact response surface and an all-time/all-concentration partial-order certificate; see [the prior kinetic report](../../../biomolecular_design/children/rna_kinetics/CYCLE2_REVERSIBLE_CTMC.md). This makes “the sequences control folding kinetics” too broad a candidate claim.

## Model limits, costs, and what remains open

The above equilibrium methods operate on a stipulated sequence, target structure, energy model, and solution condition. The cited SamplingDesign and SAMFEO benchmark is computational: a target secondary structure (pseudoknot-free), sequence design constraints, Turner energy parameters, and ViennaRNA-derived objectives are supplied. It does not estimate salt/Mg/temperature corrections from new measurements, acquire cotranscriptional transition rates, validate kinetic commitment, or establish cellular expression. SamplingDesign's own discussion identifies wet-lab structure probing/ribozyme validation as future work. Its optimization has no global-optimality guarantee; its runtime is dominated by evaluating sampled sequences. Its reported 2,500 samples per optimization step and final exact reevaluation are finite compute counts, not synthesis/assay counts. Each exact pseudoknot-free partition-function evaluation scales cubically in sequence length in the standard dynamic program ([McCaskill 1990](https://doi.org/10.1002/bip.360290621)); sampled candidate evaluations and conditions multiply that cost. The asymptotic cost of the full stochastic optimization also depends on the number of updates and is not a theorem-backed bound.

The kinetic interface needs more than sequence and equilibrium free energies: it needs an explicitly measured or modeled transition graph, transition-rate calibration, nascent-chain elongation/pause schedule, ligand concentration schedule, and a readout definition. Its acquisition cost includes the sequence panel, state-resolved trajectories or cotranscriptional probing, rate confidence intervals, repeated conditions, and validation reporters. Prior reports charged those costs and did not synthesize or assay a new sequence. No work here ran SamplingDesign, SAMFEO, ViennaRNA, CRKR, or a cotranscriptional experiment; their published source-reported performances are not replayed in this workspace.

The Astra local router was also checked: [its topic index](../../../../astra_prior/topics.txt) lists mathematical routes but no RNA/biomolecular topic, and its topic TSVs contain no RNA, riboswitch, or folding entry. Therefore no Astra card was treated as biological evidence.

## Decision and decisive next gate

**Decision:** stop the generic “new RNA sequence-to-ensemble controller” claim. The strongest version tested is subsumed at its equilibrium objective interface; the kinetic variant is too underspecified to distinguish from existing prediction-and-search pipelines. Report the result only as a scoped prior-art rejection.

A distinct next study would need to freeze a measurable kinetic distribution or response surface before sequence search. For example: hold an aptamer fixed; define expression-platform edit positions; specify ligand concentrations and RNAP pause regimes; define the desired state-occupancy/readthrough vector over transcription time; and report how uncertainty in rates and energy parameters enters the score. Compare (i) SamplingDesign with the same kinetic objective whenever its valid-sequence parameterization applies, (ii) a prespecified local/constraint-based search using the same kinetic evaluator, and (iii) a baseline fitted only from the same trajectory data. Match candidate count, model inputs, evaluation calls, synthesis, and single-molecule trajectory budgets. Freeze top candidates before held-out cotranscriptional SHAPE/SMFRET and readthrough assays. The decisive falsifier is that the proposed method does not improve calibrated held-out output per equal total budget, or that its apparent gain vanishes when competitors receive the same objective and information. Until that comparison exists, the integrated kinetic optimizer is `UNKNOWN`, not new.
