# All-degree classification of a monomial fibre completion

Worker hessian_check12, cycle14, 7 October 2026. The completed quartic
class and its first leading obstruction were proposed by hessian_bridge12
after our independent degree-nine analysis. The stronger classification
below was derived here, then independently reconstructed by the bridge.

general HC4 claim is used.

## Exact question, scope and result

Let B,C,D,E be arbitrary polynomials in C[x,y], let k be a nonzero
constant, and let d be an integer at least three. Assume E is not the
zero polynomial and set

`q=x eta-k rho`,
`psi=B(x,y)+C(x,y)rho+D(x,y)eta+E(x,y)q^d`.

**Theorem.** If det Hess psi is a nonzero constant, then

```
E=e in C*,
K=-C/k=alpha x+beta,          alpha in C*,
H=D+xC/k=hx x+b y+h0,       b in C*,
B=Bx x+By y+B0.
```

Conversely every such potential has constant determinant
`k² b² alpha²` and a direct global polynomial gradient inverse.
Thus this entire class has no HC4 counterexample. The theorem is
all-degree in d and places no prior degree bound on B,C,D,E. It is
not a classification of arbitrary additional nonlinear lower fibre
terms. The exhaustive argument does not cover d=2 or the coordinate
specialization k=0. For E=0 the class returns to the plane Keller
lift interface and cannot be silently included in this theorem.

In particular the completed quartic suggested by the degree-nine
pattern, with E=-xy/8 and d=4, can never have constant determinant,
regardless of B,C,D. This is an all-degree obstruction to that
completion class, not an obstruction to all degree-nine repairs.

## Legal coordinate change and the Hessian correction

Use new coordinates (x,y,q,v), v=eta. The inverse source map is

`Phi=(x,y,(xv-q)/k,v)`.

Both directions are polynomial globally, and det J_Phi=-1/k.
In these coordinates write

`P=B+K q+E q^d`, `H=D+xC/k`, `psi o Phi=P+vH`.

There is a required nonlinear Hessian term. The only nonzero second
derivatives of Phi are in its rho coordinate, at the mixed x,v
entries, with value1/k. Since partial_rho psi=-k P_q,

`J_Phi^T (Hess_old psi) J_Phi`
`    =Hess_new(P+vH)+P_q (E_xv+E_vx)`.

Consequently det Hess_old psi is k² times the determinant on the
right. The last covector of that bordered matrix is

`a=H_x+P_q`, `b=H_y`,

not merely grad H. Direct determinant expansion gives

```
det Hess_old psi / k²
 = (a P_yq-b P_xq)²
   -P_qq [ b²(P_xx+vH_xx)
           -2ab(P_xy+vH_xy)+a²(P_yy+vH_yy) ].       (1)
```

The chain identity and the universal bordered determinant are checked
independently with arbitrary base functions and free Hessian entries.
No nonlinear source congruence is presumed without its second-jet
correction. All divisions in the coordinate map use the constant k.

## First leading coefficient: E cannot depend on y

The q-degree4d-4 coefficient of (1) is

`d³ E² [d E_y²-(d-1)E E_yy]`.                     (2)

Indeed the square term contributes d⁴E²E_y². The only contraction
at that q degree is -P_qq a² P_yy, contributing
-d³(d-1)E³E_yy. All other terms have lower q degree; d>=2 is enough
for this separation. Since determinant constancy forces (2)=0 and
E is not zero, `d E_y²-(d-1)E E_yy=0`.

If m=deg_y E is positive, its leading y coefficient is a nonzero
multiple of

`d m²-(d-1)m(m-1)=m(m+d-1)`.

This is nonzero in characteristic zero. Hence E=E(x). This is a
degree argument on every polynomial E, not a coefficient search.
For E=-xy/8,d=4 the q12 coefficient of the original determinant is
`k² x^4 y²/16`, already an impossible nonconstant term. No lower
choice of B,C,D can cancel it: the remaining contraction has q
degree at most9 for this particular coefficient.

## Eta coefficient and the next q degrees

The coefficient of v in (1), with nonzero P_qq, forces

`b²H_xx-2abH_xy+a²H_yy=0`.                        (3)

As a has nonzero q^(d-1) coefficient dE, its highest q term gives
H_yy=0. The next one gives bH_xy=0; the constant term then gives
b²H_xx=0. There are two exhaustive branches: b=H_y=0, so H=H(x),
or b is not identically zero and H is affine in x,y. In the latter
case b is a nonzero constant. No pointwise division by b is used
before this branch distinction.

With E_y=0, the q^(3d-3) coefficient of (1) is
`-d³(d-1)E³K_yy`; hence K_yy=0. After this, its q^(3d-4)
coefficient is `-d³(d-1)E³B_yy`, so B_yy=0. This second exponent
strictly exceeds the other terms' maximal q degree2d-2 precisely
when d>=3. This is why d=2 is outside the exhaustive theorem.

Write

`K=k1(x)y+k0(x)`, `B=b1(x)y+b0(x)`.

If b=0, (1) now becomes `(a k1)²` because P_yy=0. A nonzero
constant determinant forces both polynomial factors to be units,
but a contains dE q^(d-1). This is impossible. Thus H is affine
with b!=0, and no omitted branch remains.

## Highest x degree forces k1=0 and E constant

The q^(2d-2) coefficient in the remaining branch is

```
d²(E k1-bE')²
  -d(d-1)E [b²E''-2d E b k1'].                    (4)
```

Suppose k1 is a nonzero polynomial, with n=deg_x k1>=0, and
let m=deg_x E>=0. In (4), the term d²E²k1² has degree2m+2n.
The mixed and k1' terms have degree at most2m+n-1; the E derivative
terms have degree at most2m-2. Zero derivatives only lower these
bounds. The leading term is therefore unique and nonzero, a
contradiction. It follows that k1=0.

Equation(4) reduces to
`d b² [d(E')²-(d-1)E E'']=0`. The same leading-degree argument
as for y, with m(m+d-1), forces E=e in C*. Thus K=K(x).

After these conclusions, (1) is

```
b²(K')²
 -d(d-1)e q^(d-2)
   [b²(B_xx+K''q)-2b(H_x+K+d e q^(d-1))B_xy].     (5)
```

Its q^(2d-3) coefficient forces B_xy=0. Since B_yy=0,
this makes b1 constant and B_xx=b0''. The q^(d-1) and q^(d-2)
coefficients in (5) then give K''=0 and b0''=0. Thus B is affine,
K=alpha x+beta, and the surviving determinant is k²b²alpha².
Nonzero determinant forces alpha!=0.

This proof exhausts every branch with k!=0,d>=3,E!=0. It uses
only exact determinant identities and elementary polynomial degree
arguments, with no JC2 or HC4 conjecture assumption.

## Direct inverse and parameter exceptions

The normal form can be written

`psi=B+K(x)q+H(x,y)eta+F(q)`,

where F(q)=e q^d. In fact the following sufficiency calculation
works for every univariate polynomial F. For target gradient outputs
(Ux,Uy,Urho,Ueta), recover in this order:

```
eta=(Uy-By)/b,
S=-Urho/k,
q=(Ux-Bx-eta(S+hx))/alpha,
x=(S-beta-F'(q))/alpha,
rho=(x eta-q)/k,
y=(Ueta-xS-hx x-h0)/b.
```

These are global polynomials; denominators are only the nonzero
constants b,k,alpha. Both inverse compositions and the determinant
k²b²alpha² are independently checked with an arbitrary derivative
F'(q), so they do not rely on testing finitely many d. Conversely
these formulas verify every normal form in the theorem. The parameter
exceptions k=0,alpha=0,b=0 are separated rather than specialized
through a denominator.

For real nonzero normal-form parameters the determinant is positive,
and the Hessian has the nonzero isotropic direction y. Invertibility
therefore makes its signature(2,2). This independent observation
prevents importing Liu's real index1 results into this class.
