1. EXACT SUPPLIED-PROFILE COMPILER LEMMA

Interface. Let N>=1. Supply a continuous B:T->C with absolutely convergent
Fourier series B(t)=sum_k b_k exp(2*pi*i*k*t), and real b_k (equivalently
B(-t)=conj(B(t))). Supply nonnegative alpha,beta,K such that

  1 <= |B(t)| <= 1+alpha                  for every t,
  sqrt(N)|b_k| <= 1+beta                  for 0<=k<N,
  sum_{k<0 or k>=N}|b_k| <= K/N.

Supply evaluations of B(j/N), j=0,...,N-1, with absolute error <=tau;
symmetrize the evaluations under j<->N-j. Charge their cost. The profile and
constants are supplied input, not structure learned from unknown signals.

Compute the normalized N-point DFT c_k of these samples and
Q(t)=sum_{k=0}^{N-1} c_k exp(2*pi*i*k*t). Then c_k are real and

  ||Q-B||_infty <= xi := 2K/N + N*tau,
  sqrt(N)|c_k| <= S := 1+beta+K/sqrt(N)+sqrt(N)*tau.

Proof. With exact samples, orthogonality of Nth roots gives
c_k=sum_h b_{k+hN}. The exterior terms partition across the N residue classes.
Hence sum_k|c_k-b_k| <= K/N, while discarding exterior Fourier coefficients
costs another K/N. An evaluation perturbation changes each DFT coefficient by
at most tau and changes Q uniformly by at most N*tau. The individual bound
uses |c_k-b_k|<=K/N. Absolute convergence justifies interchanging all sums.

For xi<1 normalize Y_k=sqrt(N)c_k/S in [-1,1] and put U=Q/S.
Parseval and the lower pointwise bound give

  (1/N)sum_k Y_k^2 >= ((1-xi)/S)^2,
  mu/N := (1/(2N))sum_k(1-|Y_k|)
       <= q := [1-((1-xi)/S)^2]/2 <= 1/2.

The defect-sensitive rounding lemma produces real signs a_k with

  ||N^(-1/2)sum_k a_k exp(2*pi*i*k*t)-U||_infty
      <= E := C/sqrt(N)+C*sqrt(q*log(80/q)).

At q=0 the second term is zero. Therefore the compiled binary polynomial has
the explicit uniform bounds

  (1-xi)/S-E <= |P(exp(2*pi*i*t))|/sqrt(N)
               <= (1+alpha+xi)/S+E.

These are finite-N, fully quantified bounds. In particular tau=N^-2 suffices:
for fixed supplied K, alpha and beta, all sampling errors vanish with N;
the residual signing error is O(sqrt(beta*log(1/beta))). For every fixed target
epsilon, sufficiently small alpha,beta and sufficiently large N give the
desired epsilon bound. Source Proposition waves has precisely this interface,
with alpha=O(delta), beta=O(sqrt(delta)), K=K_delta, and an unspecified N0(delta).

This alias compiler is internally proved here. It is not a novel DFT identity;
the useful deduction is its combination with this exact l1-tail/defect
interface. It also works for any independently supplied analytic wave profile.

2. FINITE CONSTRUCTIVE ROUNDING REPAIRS THE COMPACTNESS GAP

The source obtains full signs for a real R-by-s matrix A in [-1,1] by applying
Lovett-Meka partial coloring with approximation eta->0 and taking a compactness
subsequence. That passage is not algorithmic. Its required finite conclusion
can instead be acquired without the limit.

At a partial-coloring stage with u<=s free coordinates, choose thresholds
c_i=4*sqrt(log(16R/u)), so sum_i exp(-c_i^2/16)=u/16. Apply Lovett-Meka Theorem 4
with eta=1/(100s) and the current free values as its starting point. With
probability at least 0.1 it changes each row by at most
4*sqrt(u*log(16R/u)) and puts at least u/2 coordinates within eta of a sign.
Round and freeze those coordinates. Across the complete sequence of stages,
this extra rounding changes each row sum by at most s*eta<=1/100. The remaining
free counts halve. Thus the total discrepancy is

  <= 4 sum_{h>=0} sqrt(2^-h*s*log(16R/(2^-h*s))) + 1/100
  <= C0 sqrt(s*log(2R/s)).

Success at a finite stage can be checked; repeat unsuccessful edge walks.
The expected arithmetic cost of one matrix signing is
O((R+s)^3*s^2*log(Rs)*log(s+1)); constants absorb 100^2 and the positive
success probability. This is deliberately coarse.

Run that signer in the source's dyadic defect rounding. Start from downward
dyadic approximations of p_k=(1-|Y_k|)/2 at J=ceil(log2 N), losing <=1 in each
grid row. At scale h the active set has s_h<=min(N,2^h*mu). Reverse a whole
signing if necessary so its sign sum is nonpositive; then the defect mass
cannot increase. The Fourier-row cost is
sum_h 2^-h*C0*sqrt(s_h*log(80N/s_h)), bounded by
C*(1+sqrt(mu*log(80N/mu))). Real and imaginary rows on 20N equally spaced
points suffice; the source's Bernstein/Cauchy derivative bound transfers the
grid estimate to the complete circle. R=40N gives total expected arithmetic
rounding cost soft-O(N^5), including dyadic and partial-coloring logarithms.

This repair preserves the source's defect order. It is a routine algorithmic
adaptation of an established constructive discrepancy theorem. It was proved
at the real-arithmetic level; the random walk and its numerical robustness
were not implemented here. A worst-case finite-bit running-time theorem still
needs a specified finite-precision Lovett-Meka implementation and all profile
evaluation costs. N sample evaluations plus DFT cost O(N log N) arithmetic
before that rounding. N output sign bits must be stored. No claim of polynomial
dependence on 1/epsilon, practical onset, or complete filter acquisition follows.

3. CONDITIONAL FULL-CONVOLUTION CONSEQUENCE AND ITS EXACT BOUNDARY

Let a=(a_0,...,a_{N-1}) be any supplied real signing whose P satisfies
(1-epsilon)sqrt(N)<=|P(z)|<=(1+epsilon)sqrt(N) on the full unit circle,
0<epsilon<1. For EVERY L>=1 and x in C^L define all N+L-1 outputs

  (T_{a,L}x)_t = N^-1/2 sum_{j=0}^{L-1} a_{t-j}x_j,
  a_k=0 outside 0,...,N-1.

Multiplication of Fourier transforms and Parseval prove

  (1-epsilon)^2||x||_2^2 <= ||T_{a,L}x||_2^2
                        <= (1+epsilon)^2||x||_2^2.

Thus EVERY finite Toeplitz Gram G_{a,L}=T^*T has all eigenvalues in that same
interval, and condition(T)<= (1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon), independently of L.
Its entries are C_{|j-k|}/N, where C_k=sum_j a_j*a_{j+k}, zero for k>=N.
The bound also holds for every subset of columns, with all output coordinates
retained. Least-squares recovery from y=T x+e has deterministic error at most
||e||_2/(1-epsilon). FFT convolution costs O((N+L)log(N+L)) per application;
conjugate gradient on G needs O(log(1/eta)) iterations for fixed epsilon to
reach relative solution tolerance eta in the standard arithmetic model.

The reverse implication is equally exact. If the same two-sided Gram bound
holds for EVERY L, test x_j=exp(i*j*theta)/sqrt(L). Its Rayleigh quotient tends
as L->infinity to |P(exp(i*theta))|^2/N: the autocorrelation sum has weights
(1-k/L). Hence this universal finite-section property is equivalent to the
continuous spectral bound, not stronger mathematical content than ultraflatness.

Consequently no sparsity, acquisition from signal samples, dimension reduction,
or extension beyond the source's binary spectral theorem has been solved.
Two dangerous overtransfers have exact counterexamples:

  (a) Checking only the N-point DFT is insufficient. For a=(1,1,1,-1), every
      fourth-root sample has squared magnitude 4, so its binary 4-by-4
      circulant is exactly orthogonal after normalization. On the full circle,
      |P|^2=4+8x-8x^3, x=cos(theta), and extrema are 4 +/- 16/(3 sqrt(3)).
      The normalized minimum is 0.479791 and maximum 1.330338. Long linear
      convolution condition numbers approach 2.772745 rather than one.

  (b) Keeping only the first L causal outputs is insufficient. The resulting
      square lower-triangular Toeplitz matrix has diagonal a_0/sqrt(N), hence
      determinant magnitude N^(-L/2). Its smallest singular value is at most
      N^-1/2. No full-circle source theorem repairs this omitted-boundary loss.

Equal numbers of +1 and -1 coefficients also force P(1)=0; any balanced binary
modulation constraint would invalidate the lower bound immediately.

4. EXACT FINITE ACCEPTANCE CERTIFICATE

For supplied a and rational epsilon, form the integer autocorrelations C_k and
the integer Chebyshev polynomial Q(X)=N+2 sum_{k=1}^{N-1} C_k*T_k(X).
Its value at X=cos(theta) is exactly |P(exp(i theta))|^2. The desired STRICT
spectral interval is equivalent to positivity on [-1,1] of

  Q-N(1-epsilon)^2,       N(1+epsilon)^2-Q.

For each rational polynomial, exact positive endpoint values and zero real
roots on [-1,1] are a complete strict-positivity certificate, by continuity.
Exact Sturm root counts implement this finite acceptance test. With a margin
(e.g. source signs for epsilon/2 tested against epsilon), it recognizes every
successful source candidate. Enumerating 2^(N-1) signings and applying this
test is an exponential exhaustive acquisition fallback. The released theorem
would guarantee success for every sufficiently large N; it supplies neither
a useful numerical threshold nor a polynomial exhaustive-search bound.
