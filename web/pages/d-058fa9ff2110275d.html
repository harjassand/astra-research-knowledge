# Result card: neutral mtDNA turnover and threshold-risk accounting

**Question.** Does increasing bulk mtDNA turnover necessarily clear damaged mtDNA or reduce the chance that a neutral variant crosses a functional threshold?

**Verdict.** No. Under a fixed-copy, genotype-blind Moran model, turnover only changes the event clock: it preserves mean heteroplasmy, increases finite-age threshold-crossing risk, and leaves eventual threshold-hit probability unchanged. If mutants are selectively cleared, faster turnover can instead lower their load. Bulk turnover alone is not enough to determine the sign.

**Exact model result.** For N copies, threshold m = ceil(N h*), and initial neutral-variant count k, the probability to hit m before loss is exactly k/m. In an infinite-sites mutation model with mu new labels per replication and rho homeostatic turnover cycles/year, the expected number of labels born during an interval of length T that eventually hit m is N times rho times mu times T divided by m, approaching rho times mu times T divided by h*. Mutation supply and one-origin establishment probability cancel the leading N factor. This counts eventual allele-lineage outcomes, not disease incidence or labels already over threshold at the interval endpoint.

**Illustrative source translation.** An et al. (2024) report an approximate copy number of 750, a mutation rate of 5.0 × 10^-8 alterations per bp replication, and 6.5–11.5 inferred homeostatic turnover cycles/year for the named cell types. With 16,569 bp, the model gives 0.621 new labels per turnover cycle and 0.00673–0.01191 eventual 80%-threshold lineages per cell-year if every label is neutral. This is only a conditional accounting illustration; it is not a dysfunction rate.

**Finite-horizon demonstration.** For N=100, k=10, m=80, and T=10 years, exact Poisson-uniformized chain propagation gives a 6.41 × 10^-7 threshold-hit probability at 0.5 turnover cycles/year and 9.83 × 10^-3 at 2 cycles/year. Both eventual hit probabilities remain 1/8. Code/output: `code/mito_turnover_model.py` and `code/model_run.json`.

**Load-bearing assumptions.** Constant N; exchangeable copies; equal allele-specific replication and removal; no selection; no cell division; no focal-site recurrence after single-origin introduction; threshold measured as copy count. These assumptions are falsifiable and biologically restrictive.

**Prior-art boundary.** Neutral mtDNA drift/variance under feedback (Johnston & Jones 2016), network-state/time-rescaling effects (Aryaman et al. 2019), coupled fusion/fission/mitophagy-selection models (Hoitzing et al. 2015), human clone-based turnover/mutation estimates (An et al. 2024), and exact Moran takeover-time distributions (npj Aging 2026) already cover the core mechanism. This result is an internally reconstructed classical-process corollary, not a novelty or priority claim.

**Decisive measurement.** In nondividing cells, measure single-cell copy number, error-corrected allele counts down to one copy, calibrated homeostatic turnover, and allele-conditioned replication/removal. At fixed copy number and neutral event-count dynamics, changing only the turnover clock must not change the embedded threshold-hit probability; faster turnover can only increase probability by a fixed age. A mean shift or event-count-normalized difference rejects the genotype-blind model.

**Falsifier of the proposed generic repair story.** If a turnover increase without measured genotype selectivity leaves neutral mean heteroplasmy unchanged but broadens the single-cell distribution and increases fixed-age threshold hits, “more turnover is intrinsically rejuvenating” fails for this allele class. Selective clearance or advantageous deletions can reverse the sign, so the result does not generalize across mtDNA lesions.

**Scientific status.** Conditional exact model derivation plus finite computation; source parameters are source-reported; no biological validation, clinical implication, or externally cleared novelty claim.
