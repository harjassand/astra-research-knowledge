INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF COLLECTIVE COUNT REFERENCE AND LOCAL REJECTION
15 October 2026. Internal mathematical audit; historical novelty and physical
validation remain unestablished. Source agreement is not the audit criterion.

Verdict
-------
The restricted probability theorem is correct. A supplied count-dependent
collective reference plus residual rate factors between 1 and 1+beta/n gives
a full stopped likelihood cap exp(beta), including macroscopic k<=n. A local
Poisson-mark rejection construction also avoids summing all residual hazards.
It produces the exact target event-conditioned law in an ideal continuous
randomness model, with expected at most exp(beta) proposals. A finite count
truncation is approximate and must be charged. No general bit-complexity,
physical acquisition, novelty, or superiority claim follows.

The saved finite-error worker implementation was reviewed in its narrower
n=k=400, T=1/5, beta=2 rational fixture. Its tail, hazard update, clock coupling,
and quantization budgets are mathematically consistent. Independent small-state
reconstruction and counterexample diagnostics are in independent_checks.py
and independent_results.json. These are checks of a proof, not substitutes for
the proof or external review.

1. Exact theorem and the missing assumptions that matter
------------------------------------------------------
Let 1<=k<=n, T>0, b_s>0 for s=0,...,k-1, and c=beta/n with beta>=0. On a
set x of size s<k, an intact component i has target rate

  a_i(x) = b_s/(n-s) * (1+eta_i(x)),   0<=eta_i(x)<=c.

All rates and residuals are supplied mathematical inputs. They may be arbitrary
functions of x, but must be exactly queryable or come with an explicit numerical
error contract. There are no repairs or unbounded extra real jumps. Let A be
{tau_k<=T}. Under P0 each next label is uniform among intact components and the
count holding time at s is Exp(b_s), independently of all labels. Put h=P0(A)>0
and Q0=P0(.|A), on paths stopped at tau_k.

For a successful stopped path with prefix states x_s, failed labels i_s and
holding times Delta_s, write bar_eta(x_s) for the alive-label average. Then

  L = product_s(1+eta_i_s(x_s))
      * exp[-sum_s b_s bar_eta(x_s) Delta_s].

This is the complete dP/dP0 on stopped paths. No survival factor after tau_k is
appropriate. Let M=(1+c)^k<=exp(beta). The holding exponent is nonnegative,
so 0<L<=M.

The bound p=P(A)>=h does NOT require monotone b_s or an attractive set coupling.
Draw unit exponentials E_s independently. At each target prefix choose its next
label using the target mark law and use holding E_s/A(x_s), where
A(x_s)=b_s(1+bar_eta(x_s))>=b_s. The reference count holding times E_s/b_s are
independent exponentials with the required reference count law. Thus the target
sum of k holdings is pathwise no larger. This works even when b_s decrease or
oscillate sharply.

Consequently Y=hL is unbiased for p. With mu=E_Q0 L=p/h>=1 and L^2<=ML,

  E[Y^2]/p^2 = E[L^2]/mu^2 <= M/mu <= M <= exp(beta).

The same inequalities provide h<=p<=Mh. Thus a constant-factor deterministic
probability approximation already accompanies the strong-efficiency theorem.
Refining that interval remains meaningful; the interval itself is not a new
precision capability.

2. Local rejection without an aggregate-rate oracle
---------------------------------------------------
For each proposal from Q0:

  (a) Independently accept every real jump with probability
      (1+eta_i_s(x_s))/(1+c).
  (b) In holding interval s draw N_s~Pois(c*b_s*Delta_s). For each mark choose
      a uniform intact label i and reject the proposal with probability
      eta_i(x_s)/c. The beta=0 case accepts everything without these marks.

For a fixed path, the probability that no mark rejects in interval s is

  exp[-c*b_s*Delta_s * average_alive(eta_i/c)]
  = exp[-b_s*bar_eta(x_s)*Delta_s].

The overall acceptance probability given the path is L/M. Therefore the
unconditional proposal acceptance is a=p/(Mh)>=1/M, and an accepted path has
law P(.|A). Repeating independent proposals needs mean 1/a<=M trials.
The scalar estimator Mh*I_accept is unbiased and has relative second moment
1/a<=M. It has larger variance than hL whenever L is not effectively binary;
its attraction is local-query access and target conditional path production.

Per proposal, there are at most k real-jump residual queries and expected at
most c*sum_s b_s*Delta_s<=c*B*T mark queries, where B=max_s b_s. Early rejection
can only reduce this. Thus expected local queries are <=k+beta*B*T/n. Each
eta query's own runtime/precision is charged separately. If eta is supplied by
a dense pair matrix, its read/preprocessing or local summation cost is not a
unit-cost physical oracle. No claim of unconditional sublinear acquisition of
an arbitrary residual law is justified.

3. Positive uniformization: a stronger conditioned tail bound
------------------------------------------------------------
Uniformize the absorbing count chain at B>=max_s b_s. Its discrete transition
matrix has R_s,s=1-b_s/B, R_s,s+1=b_s/B and R_k,k=1. Define

  v_0(s)=1{s=k},
  v_m(s)=(1-b_s/B)v_(m-1)(s)+(b_s/B)v_(m-1)(s+1).

The conditional augmented event count has law

  P(N=m|A) = exp(-BT)*(BT)^m/m! * v_m(0) / h.

Given N, the discrete count bridge uses ordinary conditional probabilities
from v, and the N Poisson epochs are uniform order statistics. Assign uniform
distinct labels only at real upward steps. This is the standard endpoint-
conditioned uniformization construction specialized to a bidiagonal count
chain, not a new bridge principle.

There is a sharper tail certificate than the unconditional bound divided by h.
Conditional on a real stopped path that satisfies A, virtual events before
tau_k have intensity B-b_s on stage s, and those after absorption have rate B.
Hence

  N | real stopped path = k + Pois(BT - sum_s b_s*Delta_s).

The Poisson parameter lies in [0,BT]. It follows that

  N | A <=st k+Pois(BT),     E[N|A]<=k+BT.

For J=k+j, the removed conditioned mass is <=P(Pois(BT)>j). Choose this last
tail <=delta. The bridge conditioned additionally on N<=J has TV distance
at most delta from Q0. Moreover its positive partial h_J obeys

  h_J <= h <= h_J/(1-delta).

This is a relative enclosure without knowing h beforehand. Conservative
j=O(BT+log(1/delta)) suffices; dependence on log(1/h) is not necessary for
this pure-birth absorbing structure. The recurrence costs O(kJ) arithmetic
and storage. The discrete bridge is O(J), and ordered uniform epochs can be
drawn in O(J) real arithmetic using normalized independent exponential
spacings. The worker's actual implementation sorts its epochs and therefore
uses O(J log J) time for that step.

A finite J cannot be called exact: the conditional Poisson tail is positive.
An exact adaptive/lazy construction has unbounded support and needs a separate
expected random-bit/precision analysis. Ideal real arithmetic is a legitimate
mathematical statement, but it is not a finite-bit implementation theorem.
For rational b_s/B and BT, the finite categorical weights have a common
exp(-BT) factor that cancels; exact rational categorical sampling is available,
with the rational bitlength and random integer generation charged.

4. Conditioned approximation and finite clocks
---------------------------------------------
Suppose a genuinely continuous/lazy bridge approximation Qhat0 has TV distance
<=delta from Q0. Apply the same exact rejection kernel to both. Their joint
proposal/acceptance laws have TV<=delta, and |ahat-a|<=delta. If delta<1/M,
then ahat>0 and expected approximate-proposal repetitions are at most

  M/(1-M*delta).

The accepted output laws have TV<=delta/max(a,ahat)<=M*delta. One proof uses
the Jordan positive/negative masses of the two success-restricted measures;
each is <=delta, and renormalization divides by at least max(a,ahat).
The estimate Mh*I has relative expectation error <=M*delta. If h is replaced
by hhat with relative error <=rho, the total relative bias is at most

  rho + (1+rho)*M*delta.

Any further rejection-kernel approximation error can be added to delta if it
is controlled in joint-law TV uniformly over the proposal path.

Finite-grid times are singular relative to continuous times, so their full
path TV is one. They require a functional coupling or a lazy real output. In
the worker's constant-total-rate fixture the count-tail TV is valid, while the
64-bit midpoint clocks are handled by a weight coupling. Sorting is a
contraction in maximum coordinate error. Since labels are sampled independently
of ordered times, no label-order mismatch is introduced by close epochs.

For a fixed label order and matched count, if all k times move by <=epsilon,
and extra total hazards are between 0 and D, the holding exponent changes by
at most 2kD*epsilon and the likelihood by at most M*2kD*epsilon. This supplies
a scalar expectation-bias budget, not a full continuous-path TV guarantee.

5. A runtime counterexample for arbitrary supplied b_s
-----------------------------------------------------
For k=2, T=1, b_0=epsilon and b_1=1/epsilon, with epsilon->0,

  h = P(Exp(epsilon)+Exp(1/epsilon)<=1) ~ epsilon,
  B*T=1/epsilon ~ 1/h.

The input rates have O(log(1/epsilon)) bitlength when epsilon=2^-m, but
uniformization uses Theta(BT) augmented steps at the proposed cost scale.
Thus the statement has no explicit rare-event rejection factor; it is NOT
uniformly polynomial in input bitlength and log(1/h) over arbitrary rates.
Stiffness can itself reproduce inverse-rarity cost. Constant b_s=n in the
worker fixture avoids this obstruction. A claim about arbitrary b_s must
retain the charged BT dependence or use a different stiff-aware bridge.

6. Strong matched baseline: permutation conditional Monte Carlo
--------------------------------------------------------------
For any fixed label prefix pi, let x_s be its preceding sets, let
A_s=sum_alive a_i(x_s), and let H(A_0,...,A_(k-1);T) be the probability that
independent exponential holdings with these rates sum to at most T. With pi
uniform among ordered distinct k-label prefixes, define

  Z(pi) = product_s [(1+eta_pi_s(x_s))/(1+bar_eta(x_s))]
          * H(A_0,...,A_(k-1);T).

Then E Z=p. More specifically Z=E[hL|pi] under Q0, because the reference count
bridge is independent of its uniform labels. Therefore

  Var(Z) <= Var(hL),   0<=Z/h<=M.

This is a genuine equally informed conditional Monte Carlo / permutation
baseline whenever aggregate hazards are available at comparable cost. For
dense pair residuals, the worker already computes aggregate hazards along each
prefix; a positive k-state holding-time calculation is O(kJ_A). At k=Theta(n)
and reference total rates Theta(n), this is O(n^2), the same arithmetic order
as the worker's dense per-path likelihood calculation. Constants and precision
still matter, and no measured superiority in either direction is established.
For arbitrary local residual-query access, CMC must pay its aggregate queries;
it must not be supplied an unearned aggregate oracle.

The accessible count-reference Doob transform has rates
b_s*h_(s+1)(T-t)/h_s(T-t), where h_s is its count continuation probability.
Uniformization and this transform describe the same conditioned reference.
The exact TARGET Doob transform requires h(x,T-t) for the interacting set
model; granting that unavailable function to a baseline for free is unfair.
Conversely, rebranding the count-reference Doob/conditioning construction as
an unprovided primitive would understate existing methods.

8. Supplied rates and the acquisition gate
------------------------------------------
Strong relative efficiency estimates a supplied nominal model. It does not
acquire b_s or certify the residual bound on all physically reachable states.
An arbitrary table of eta_i(x) has exponential input; a local oracle is an
explicit access-model assumption. A dense pair model has n^2 supplied values.

In the Poisson-tail reference h(mu)=P(Pois(mu)>=k),

  d log h / d log mu = mu*P(Pois(mu)=k-1)/h(mu).

For k much larger than mu this is approximately k-mu. A small common relative
rate error can therefore produce O(k) times larger relative probability error.
At k=400, mu=80, a one-percent relative probability target calls for common-rate
calibration roughly at the few-times-10^-5 scale in the local sensitivity
approximation, in addition to residual/model-discrepancy and channel coverage.
This is a sensitivity observation, not a bound on an acquired material model.

9. Primary prior-art anchors and status
--------------------------------------
Hobolth and Stone (2009), Simulation from endpoint-conditioned, continuous-time
Markov chains on a finite state space, Sections 2.3 and 4, explicitly give the
conditional Poisson count, conditional discrete bridge and uniform epoch
algorithm, and discuss costs/instances where no one sampler dominates:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.1683

Cancela, L'Ecuyer, Rubino and Tuffin (2010), Combination of Conditional Monte
Carlo and Approximate Zero-Variance Importance Sampling for Network Reliability
Estimation, gives the relevant conditioning/approximate-zero-variance tradition:
https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc10papers/116.pdf

Rao and Teh (2013), Fast MCMC Sampling for Markov Jump Processes and Extensions,
uses uniformization and hidden-state forward/backward sampling for a broader
posterior setting; it is an MCMC method, not an iid guarantee for this candidate:
https://jmlr.org/papers/volume14/rao13a/rao13a.pdf

The exact macroscopic residual scaling and local marked rejection theorem can
be recorded as a proved restricted construction after correcting the access
and finite-error statements. Its historical novelty and practical advantage
remain UNKNOWN. No physical or mission-level achievement is established by
this audit.
