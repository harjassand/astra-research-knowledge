# Two-channel BET law and selectivity gates

## Interface and exact law

Assume channel `i` receives reduced-substrate flux `J_i = kSET_i [S_i][D]`, where substrate and reductant concentrations are held constant. Its radical anion has two exits: productive commitment at first-order rate `k_i`, and back electron transfer at pseudo-first-order rate `x_i = kBET_i[A]`. Under the quasi-steady-state approximation,

`[S_i•−] = J_i / (k_i + x_i)` and `v_i = J_i k_i / (k_i + x_i)`.

Thus, at common observation time with these rates fixed,

`R := v_1/v_2 = g (k_1/k_2) (x_2+k_2)/(x_1+k_1)`,

where `g = kSET_1[S_1]/(kSET_2[S_2])`. This is the source SI's Eq. S9 in hazard notation. If `x_1=x_2=x`, write `u=k_1/k_2`, `β=x/k_2`, giving

`R = g u (β+1)/(β+u)`.

Target product fraction is `R/(1+R)`. Therefore 90% requires `R≥9`; the reported 70:7 annulation:hydrogenation ratio corresponds to `R=10` (90.91% among these two products).

For any required odds `R*`, the exact common-BET commitment-ratio gate is

`u ≥ R* β / (g(β+1) − R*)`,

provided `g(β+1)>R*`; if not, no finite `u` can pass. The no-finite-commitment ceiling is `lim_(u→∞) R = g(β+1)`. More generally, with unequal BET hazards, rearranging the exact law gives

`k_1 ≥ R* k_2 x_1 / (g(x_2+k_2) − R* k_2)`,

provided the denominator is positive.

## Caption/prose rate discrepancy and numerical gate

At equal substrate concentrations, the strict reading of SI captions assigns `kSET_1(Ket-1)=3×10^10 M^-1s^-1` (S2.18) and `kSET_2(styrene)=3×10^10 M^-1s^-1` (S2.3), so `g=1`. The S30 prose instead reports `kSET_1(Ket-1)=4×10^9 M^-1s^-1`, while the styrene value remains `3×10^10`, giving `g=2/15`. These cannot both be the Ket-1 rate. The figure/table text for S2.18 is also internally suspicious: its paragraph calls the fitted slope capture “by pyrene” even though the experiment is headed Ket-1 as quencher. I preserve the two interpretations rather than selecting one.

For an illustrative `β=560` (`kBET=10^10 M^-1s^-1`, `[A]=56 μM`, and `k_2=10^3 s^-1`, the separate Figure 5c model settings in SI S97), the exact gates are:

| Capture ratio `g` | Target odds `R*` | Minimum `u=k1/k2` | Target commitment `k1/(x+k1)` at gate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (caption-literal) | 9 (90%) | 9.1304 | 1.60% |
| 1 (caption-literal) | 10 (70:7) | 10.1633 | 1.78% |
| 2/15 (S30 prose) | 9 (90%) | 76.5957 | 12.03% |
| 2/15 (S30 prose) | 10 (70:7) | 86.4198 | 13.36% |

The last column exposes a selectivity/throughput cost that product ratio alone hides. Under the caption-literal rate ratio, `g(β+1)=561`, and under the prose ratio it is `74.8`; both exceed 10, so these odds are mathematically attainable in this illustrative regime. The target commitment fraction and total productive fraction per electron capture still depend on the flux weights and hazards; neither column is an experimental Faradaic efficiency.

## Evidence boundary and decisive gate

Primary source: Edgecomb et al., *Nature* 656, 630–637 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7), with official SI [41586_2026_10897_MOESM1_ESM.pdf](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10897-7/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10897_MOESM1_ESM.pdf): S25/S30/S80 for the conflicting capture values; S81–S86 for the kinetic assumptions and Eqs. S9–S13; S97 for the illustrative Figure 5c settings; S7–S8 for the 70%/7% GC outcomes. The numerical gates above are model algebra plus SI settings, not fits to those GC yields.

The deciding experiment is an independently replicated, same-condition determination of both channel-specific capture constants and BET/commitment hazards in the actual reaction mixture, paired with product rates and charge balance. This specifically resolves the S2.18 caption/S30 prose conflict. The paper's mixed reductant states, reduced substrate/radical concentration constancy, and unmeasured wall-plug/photon efficiencies remain outside this two-channel gate.
