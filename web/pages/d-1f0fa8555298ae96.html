## Question, equal information, and exact scope

Can a predetermined list of m full-matrix queries expose a nonzero proper cyclic series that vanishes at the unscaled Volterra tuple, while correctly rejecting a nontrivial cyclic zero?

The unmarked control receives n,m and an exact complete-matrix oracle and queries T at dimension d=2nm. The grid protocol receives the same information and queries ell*T for every ell=1,...,m at the same dimension. Neither constructor reads hidden coefficients, support, state matrices or an expression tree. Hidden realizations are supplied ground truth inside the experimental oracle. The grid pays for m queries; it is not a same-query-budget speed comparison.

The oracle semantics are those of a proper recognizable series

    f_w=u B_w v,
    f(X)=(u tensor I)(I-sum_i B_i tensor X_i)^(-1)(v tensor I).

Here B_i may have cycles and m counts states. All queried X_i are jointly strictly lower triangular. Thus the inverse is a finite nilpotent sum and every evaluation is defined, irrespective of the hidden cycles. Arbitrary rational-expression syntax may have a different domain and is outside this experiment and theorem. The field is characteristic zero; computations use exact rational arithmetic.

## Universal repair and proof dependency

Root's conservative theorem states that m predetermined tuples ell*T_i^(2nm), ell=1,...,m, detect every nonzero proper m-state series. One direct-sum tuple has dimension 2nm^2. The alternative length marker S_(m+1) tensor T has dimension 2nm(m+1). Complete matrix responses are required.

The proof starts with the same operators J_i a(z)=integral_0^z a(t)/(t+i) dt. Insert an indeterminate lambda to separate word lengths. Finite-word hyperlogarithm independence, source Lemma 2.1, makes f(lambda J)1 nonzero even though f(J)1 can vanish for cyclic series. With q(z)=product_i(z+i), A_lambda=lambda P/q and G_lambda'=A_lambda G_lambda, the nonconstant part has kernel

    K_lambda(z,t)=u G_lambda(z) G_lambda(t)^(-1) A_lambda(t)v.

The rational covariant rows R_0=u, R_(j+1)=R_j'+R_j A_lambda have form Z_j/q^j with deg_z Z_j<=j(n-1) and deg_lambda Z_j<=j. Their first rational dependence yields a nonzero diagonal kernel derivative b_j=R_j A_lambda v for some j<m. Its numerator has an explicit factor lambda; after removing it the remaining lambda-degree is at most m-1. It cannot vanish at all m distinct nonzero integers. At some scale ell, the actual numerator and formal differential equation specialize without poles, producing a kernel term of total degree at most nm-1. The acyclic proof's invertible Cauchy moments then expose an entry inside d=2nm. Rational row-dependence coefficients themselves are never specialized.

This bounds a selected nonzero derivative numerator, not the degree of the whole evaluated polynomial f(lambda T), whose degree can be larger. The proof, rather than the finite experiment, supports the universal claim.

## Independent implementation checks and complete ledger

The main evaluator solves the proper pencil by physical output rows: for every seed q,

    Y_r=v*delta_(r,q)+sum_i B_i sum_(t<r) X_i[r,t] Y_t;
    response[r,q]=u Y_r.

It never expands into free words. Identity diagonal state blocks establish determinant one and domain membership. A separate finite Neumann matrix evaluator agrees on ell=1 for both dense cases. The alias also has the independent rational-expression comparison at all five scales. Coefficient truth is checked on all words of length below m. For a supplied m-state realization this is a decisive zero test because the reachable row space stabilizes by length m-1; no state bound is inferred from finite samples.

The original run passed in 1.516 seconds. All 14 queries described 11,776 complete input field elements and returned 5,888 field elements. The explicit entrywise reduced-rational format uses numerator magnitude bits (at least one), one sign bit, and denominator magnitude bits for every entry, including zeros. Under that format, inputs used 75,264 bits and responses 42,359 bits. This excludes matrix indices, container overhead, and hidden representation storage; it is a defined serialization ledger, not an entropy lower bound or measured process memory.

The main solver charged 237,248 input scalar products, 75,312 transition scalar products, and 4,884 output scalar products. Separate dense cross-checks charged 486,662 scalar products. Maximum response denominator height was 42 bits and maximum numerator height 47 bits in this bounded run. These are fixture costs, not an m-only coefficient-height bound.

## Inference, prior boundary, and next falsifiable step

The experiment distinguishes a mathematically invalid unmarked cyclic extrapolation from its length-graded repair on a deliberately hostile witness. The paired dense zero checks rejection of internal cancellation, while the dense nonzero avoids relying only on the hand-designed alias. These finite cases check evaluators and provide an adversarial discriminator; they do not prove the universal grid theorem.

The source provides the explicit T_i and finite-word independence. Root supplies the cyclic scalar-grid composition of our bivariate-kernel construction. For a full-width residual learner with hidden rank r<=m, the grid would use at most m(1+nr) queries of dimension at most (r+1)*2nm and features of length 4n^2m^3. Recovering a full response through scalar prefixes instead uses separate seeds, changing the query count. These constructions still require an acquired or supplied exact full-matrix oracle; they do not supply passive-sample learning, positive realizations, floating-point stability, or response-height bounds.
