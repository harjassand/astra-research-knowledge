# INITIAL — c10_l04 (fixed130), 2026-10-07

## Claim and status

The strongest exact statement I can currently support is a scoped counterexample to **uniform stationary tightness across stochastic components of one deterministic stoichiometric class**. A single weakly reversible, one-linkage, deficiency-zero mass-action network has one positive stoichiometric class

\[
S=\{(A,B,C,D)\in\mathbb R_{>0}^4:B+C=2\}
\]

whose deterministic ODE trajectories all converge to the same equilibrium \((1,1,1,1)\). Its stochastic mass-action chain on the integer points of the same class has infinitely many distinct closed irreducible positive-recurrent classes \(\Gamma_n\), each with an explicit invariant probability \(\pi_n\), and \(\pi_n(A\ge n)=1\). Hence the family \(\{\pi_n:n\ge0\}\) is not tight. In fact, \(\pi_n(B=0)\to1\) as \(n\to\infty\).

This refutes the implication “one deterministic compatibility class has a common globally attracting equilibrium, therefore the stationary laws of all stochastic irreducible components in that class are uniformly tight.” It does **not** refute componentwise recurrence: every \(\Gamma_n\) in this construction is positive recurrent. The original N77 packet is unavailable; this is an independent reconstruction from its supplied summary, not external validation or a novelty claim.

## Network and deterministic dynamics

Use unit rate constants and stochastic mass-action propensities \(\prod_i (x_i)_{y_i}\), where \((x)_k=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)\). The reactions are

\[
2C\to A+B+C,\qquad A+B+C\to3A+2B,\qquad 3A+2B\to2C,
\qquad 2C\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]

The four complexes form one strongly connected linkage class. The first three reaction vectors in \((A,B,C)\) coordinates are \((1,1,-1),(2,1,-1),(-3,-2,2)\), with the third equal to minus the first two; the D reaction adds an independent direction. Thus the stoichiometric rank is 3 and the deficiency is \(4-1-3=0\). The only linear conservation law is \(B+C\). At \((1,1,1,1)\), every complex monomial equals 1, so this point is complex balanced. Complex balance in \(B+C=2\) gives \(C^2=ABC=A^3B^2=C^2D\); positivity then forces \(A=D=1\) and \(B=C=1\). The one-linkage-class global-attractor theorem therefore gives convergence to this equilibrium from every positive initial point in \(S\) [Anderson, arXiv:1101.0761, Corollary 28].

## Exact stochastic invariant and recurrent classes

The integer chain conserves \(B+C\). On its slice \(B+C=2\), the only possible \((B,C)\) pairs are \((0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\). Define

\[
N(A,B)=A-\binom{B+1}{2}.
\]

When \((B,C)=(0,2)\), the first reaction is enabled and changes \((A,B)\) by \((1,1)\), so \(\Delta N=1-1=0\). At \((1,1)\), the second reaction changes \((A,B)\) by \((2,1)\), so \(\Delta N=2-2=0\). At \((2,0)\), the third reaction changes \((A,B)\) by \((-3,-2)\), so \(\Delta N=-3-(-3)=0\). The D reactions leave N unchanged. For each integer \(n\ge0\), the resulting closed class is

\[
\Gamma_n=\{(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d):d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\}.
\]

Writing these three positions as phases 0, 1, 2, their cycle rates are respectively

\[
r_0=2,\qquad r_1=n+1,\qquad r_2=2P_n,
\quad P_n=(n+1)(n+2)(n+3).
\]

At phase 0 the D immigration rate is 2 and the D death rate at count d is \(2d\); at phases 1 and 2, D is frozen. The phase cycle plus the D births/deaths make \(\Gamma_n\) irreducible. For fixed n, phase rates are bounded; D births are dominated by a rate-2 Poisson process, and the number of deaths by time T is at most the initial D count plus the number of births. Thus there are finitely many jumps on bounded time intervals and the chain is nonexplosive.

An unnormalized stationary weight at state x in \(\Gamma_n\) is the restriction of \(1/(A!B!C!D!)\). After removing the common factor \(1/n!\), the phase weights are

\[
w_0=\tfrac12,\qquad w_1=\frac1{n+1},\qquad w_2=\frac1{2P_n},
\]

and the D weight is \(1/d!\). Direct balance verifies this product law: the three directed phase fluxes are \(w_0r_0=w_1r_1=w_2r_2=1\), while \((1/d!)2=(1/(d+1)!)2(d+1)\) balances D birth and death at phase 0. Since the weights sum to a finite number, normalization gives a stationary probability. Irreducibility and nonexplosion imply positive recurrence and uniqueness of this invariant probability. This is also the complex-balanced product-form mechanism of Anderson–Craciun–Kurtz [arXiv:0803.3042], but the balance was checked directly here.

Let \(Z_n=P_n+2(n+2)(n+3)+1=n^3+8n^2+21n+19\). The stationary phase probabilities are

\[
\pi_n(0)=\frac{P_n}{Z_n},\qquad
\pi_n(1)=\frac{2(n+2)(n+3)}{Z_n},\qquad
\pi_n(2)=\frac1{Z_n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\pi_n(A\ge n)=1,\qquad \pi_n(B=0)=\frac{P_n}{Z_n}\longrightarrow1,
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_n}A=n+\frac{2n^2+10n+15}{Z_n}.
\]

Every compact subset of \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4\) has bounded A-coordinate, so for any compact K, \(\pi_n(K)=0\) for all sufficiently large n. This is genuine escape of stationary laws, not merely divergence of a moment.

## Boundary of the counterexample

N is not a global stochastic invariant: from \((2,1,2,0)\), the enabled \(2C\to A+B+C\) reaction changes N by \(-1\). Its continuous extension is not a deterministic first integral either. With

\[
\dot A=C^2+2ABC-3A^3B^2,\qquad
\dot B=C^2+ABC-2A^3B^2,
\]

one has

\[
\frac{dN}{dt}=\dot A-(B+\tfrac12)\dot B
=(\tfrac12-B)C^2+(\tfrac32-B)ABC+(2B-2)A^3B^2.
\]

At the positive point \((A,B,C)=(2,1/2,3/2)\) on \(B+C=2\), this derivative is \(-1/2\). The invariant is a lattice-and-boundary phenomenon: the integer slice enables different reactions at its three boundary phases, whereas the deterministic interior has several reactions active at once.

The surviving statements are exact and useful: each fixed \(\Gamma_n\) has one tight invariant law; any family with n bounded is uniformly tight (A,B,C then range over a finite set and D has the same Poisson(1) tail); and the non-tightness across n is carried by A, while D remains Poisson(1) and B+C=2. The counterexample says nothing against componentwise positive recurrence. It also does not preclude stationary mixtures on the whole plane; it shows that the collection of all component stationary laws is not uniformly tight.
