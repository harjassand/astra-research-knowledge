Cycle 3 follow-up: coercive exponential Foster drift on compact positive rate boxes
==================================================================================

Status
------
INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. This strengthens the pointwise complete-pair
recurrence result in exponential_foster_outside_gain/RESULT.txt. It does not
cover arbitrary weakly reversible networks and makes no novelty or priority
claim. The proof below is for the exact complete catalyst-substrate family
defined in that report, with fixed finite r,s and k_ji>=1.

The additional conclusion is uniform stationary tightness and uniform
exponential moments of substrate counts over any compact box of strictly
positive rates. Uniform mixing is not claimed.

The rate-box theorem
--------------------
Fix r,s>=1 and finite integers k_ji>=1. Put every parameter in a compact
box with positive lower and finite upper endpoints:

  0<alpha_j^-<=alpha_j<=alpha_j^+<infinity, and similarly for
  delta_j,p_ji,q_ji,u_ji,v_ji.

All reactions and stochastic mass-action propensities are as in
exponential_foster_outside_gain/RESULT.txt. There are constants c,C>0 and a
single function V, independent of the rate vector in the box, such that

  L_theta V(x) <= C-c V(x)       for every state x and every rate vector theta.

One may choose V(a,b)=A+(1+kappa*1_{A=0}) Psi(b), where
  A=sum_j a_j, Psi(b)=sum_i R^{b_i},
with kappa=1 and a common rational R>1 chosen sufficiently close to one.
Consequently every chain in the box is nonexplosive and irreducible, has a
unique invariant law pi_theta, and the invariant laws have a common finite
V-moment bound. In particular, the family {pi_theta} is uniformly tight;
each substrate coordinate has a uniformly bounded R-exponential moment,
and total catalyst count has a uniformly bounded first moment.

Exact generator identity
------------------------
Write c_R=R-1, d_R=1-R^{-1}, H=sum_j alpha_j, and

  g_ji(m)=p_ji c_R R^m-q_ji d_R m R^m+k_ji u_ji m.

For A=0, immigration is the only enabled reaction. Its jump in V is
1-Psi (since the multiplier on Psi changes from 2 to 1), and therefore

  LV(0,b)=H-alpha_sum Psi(b),    V(0,b)=2 Psi(b),
  alpha_sum=sum_j alpha_j.                                      (1)

For A>=1, the multiplier on Psi is one. The only jump that changes this
multiplier is a death from a=e_j to A=0; its extra jump is +Psi, beyond the
ordinary catalyst decrement -1. Direct summation of all mass-action rates
gives

  LV=H-sum_j delta_j a_j
       +sum_j a_j sum_i g_ji(b_i)
       -sum_{j,i} k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i
       +sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j} Psi(b).                         (2)

The final term is the one-catalyst boundary switch. Formula (2) uses the
falling factorial exactly, including its zero value below the reaction
threshold. The checker geometric_rate_box_check.py compares (1)-(2) with
the explicit reaction list on a boundary-inclusive rational fixture grid.

Proof of a uniform geometric drift
----------------------------------
Let delta_-=min_j delta_j^-, p_+=max_{j,i}p_ji^+,
q_-=min_{j,i}q_ji^-, v_-=min_{j,i}v_ji^+, and
delta_+=max_j delta_j^+. Choose rational R>1 so that

  chi_*:=delta_- - s p_+(R-1)>0.                                 (3)

Then for every rate vector in the box and every j,
  delta_j-sum_i p_ji(R-1)>=chi_*.

Set kappa=1. Since q_-d_R>0 and k_ji,u_ji,p_ji are bounded above, for
every finite L>delta_+ there is an integer m0>=1, common to all pairs and
all rates in the box, such that

  g_ji(m)<=-L R^m,                 m>=m0.                       (4)

Indeed, after division by R^m the upper bound is
p_+(R-1)-q_-d_R m+U m/R^m, where U=max_ji k_ji u_ji^+; this tends to
-infinity uniformly in (j,i,theta). For m<m0 there is a common finite
upper bound G>=0 on all g_ji(m).

Define S(b)=sum_{i:b_i>=m0}R^{b_i} and B_*=sR^{m0}. Then
  Psi(b)<=S(b)+B_*.
For A>=1, (2), (4), nonpositivity of the factorial terms, and the boundary
switch bound give

  LV <= H_+ + sG A - L A S + delta_+ 1_{A=1}(S+B_*)
     <= H_+ + delta_+ B_* + sG A -(L-delta_+) A S.             (5)

In the second line we used A>=1, hence delta_+ S 1_{A=1}<=delta_+ A S.
Choose S_*>=1 large enough that sG <=(L-delta_+)S_*/2. Whenever
S>=S_*, (5) yields

  LV <= C_1 - (L-delta_+) A S/2 <= C_2-c_2 V,                 (6)

with positive c_2 independent of theta. The last inequality follows from
A>=1, S>=1, AS>=A, AS>=S, and Psi<=S+B_*; explicitly
AS>=(A+S)/2 and V=A+Psi<=A+S+B_*.

If S<S_*, every b_i is bounded by a common integer B_1: coordinates below
m0 are already bounded, and any coordinate at least m0 contributes R^{b_i}
to S. Thus only finitely many substrate vectors remain. Handle them as
follows, with constants uniform over the rate box.

* If b=0, (2) has no factorial term and
  -delta_j+sum_i g_ji(0)=-delta_j+sum_i p_ji(R-1)<=-chi_*.
  The boundary-switch term is bounded because it is supported on A=1.
  Hence LV<=C_3-chi_* A, which implies LV<=C_4-c_4 V since Psi is
  bounded on this case.

* Fix a nonzero b with b_i0>=1. For each j, the reverse reaction on edge
  (j,i0) contributes at most
  -v_- (a_j)_(k_ji0+1), since k_ji0 b_i0>=1. All positive linear-in-a_j
  terms in (2) have a common bound M a_j on this finite b-box. For
  a_j>=k_ji0+1, (a_j)_(k_ji0+1)>=a_j(a_j-1); for smaller a_j the range is
  finite. Therefore there are uniform constants C_j and c_j>0 such that
  M a_j-v_-(a_j)_(k_ji0+1)<=C_j-c_j a_j for every a_j. The switch term
  is bounded on A=1. Summing over j gives LV<=C_5-c_5 A. Since Psi is
  bounded on b<=B_1, this implies LV<=C_6-c_6 V.

There are only finitely many b vectors in the S<S_* case, so take the
minimum c and maximum C over them and combine with (1) and (6). For A=0,
(1) directly gives

  LV <= H_+ - (alpha_sum^- /2) V,                              (7)

where alpha_sum^-=sum_j alpha_j^->0. This completes the common drift
inequality.

Physical time and stationary consequences
------------------------------------------
V is norm-like on the full lattice: A is controlled by V, and each
R^{b_i} is controlled by V. Its sublevel sets are finite. The geometric
drift implies LV<=C, so localized stopped Dynkin at
tau_n=inf{t:V(X_t)>=n} yields

  P_x(tau_n<=t) <= (V(x)+Ct)/n.

On every finite sublevel set all reaction rates are bounded; a finite set
cannot contain infinitely many jumps in a finite time. Since V is
norm-like, finite-time explosion would require exiting all V-sublevel
sets. Letting n increase rules this out in physical time. For the compact
rate box, C and V are common and all exit rates on any fixed finite set
have a common finite upper bound, so the localization estimate is also
uniform over rate vectors for each fixed initial state and time interval.

Each chain is irreducible by the explicit clearing/building paths in the
main report. The continuous-time Foster criterion applied after
nonexplosion gives positive recurrence and a unique invariant law. The
geometric drift is stronger: the standard extended-generator drift theorem
with f=V gives finite invariant V moment. For the common numerical bound,
stop at tau_n=inf{t:V(X_t)>=n}; the drift inequality gives

  E_x V(X_(t wedge tau_n)) + c E_x int_0^(t wedge tau_n) V(X_s) ds
       <= V(x)+Ct.

Let n increase. Nonexplosion and monotone/Fatou convergence yield
1/T int_0^T E_x V(X_s) ds <= V(x)/(cT)+C/c. The time-averaged occupation
laws are tight by this same estimate; every limit is invariant, and
irreducibility makes it pi_theta. Lower semicontinuity of V then gives
pi_theta(V)<=C/c. This argument uses the same constants for every theta.
Thus, for every N,

  sup_theta pi_theta(A>N) <= (C/c)/N,
  sup_theta pi_theta(b_i>N) <= (C/c) R^{-N}.

This is uniform stationary tightness and a uniform exponential substrate
moment over the compact strictly positive box. It says nothing about boxes
approaching zero rates or unbounded rates. It does not imply a common
mixing-time bound; none is claimed.

Relation to pointwise theorem and known results
-----------------------------------------------
The pointwise all-rate recurrence theorem in
exponential_foster_outside_gain/RESULT.txt needs only an additive
all-catalysts-zero correction and proves LV<=-1 off a finite set. Its drift
on A=0 is constant negative in b, so that theorem alone does not yield an
exponential stationary moment. The multiplicative switch used here gives
the stronger A=0 drift (1), proportional to Psi, while the q-term absorbs
the catalyst-death switch on the A=1 face.

The complete-pair family is a proper subfamily of weakly reversible mass
action networks. This result therefore does not close the general
d>=3 weakly reversible recurrence problem. The literature audit in
foster_multicatalyst_primary_audit.txt remains the applicable source gate;
the two-species all-rate result cited there is source-reported rather than
used here. For the abstract implication from a nonexplosive irreducible
process and an extended-generator drift DV<=-delta f+b 1_C to finite
f-moment recurrence, see Kontoyiannis and Meyn, *On the f-Norm Ergodicity
of Markov Processes in Continuous Time*, Theorem 1.1, arXiv:1512.00523:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00523 . This paper is a tool citation, not a
claim of priority for the network-specific construction.
