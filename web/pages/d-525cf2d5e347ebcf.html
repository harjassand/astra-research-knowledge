LEMMA
Let 0<C<1,S=1-C,0<c<=2S,0<=t<=1 and0<=z<=c t. Define

 Q(t,z)=[(1+t^2)cosh z-2t sinh z+(1-t^2)(2C-1)]/2.

Then Q>0 and

 F(t,z):=log[Q(t,z)/C]+t z-z^2/(2c)>=0.                     (1)

COROLLARY FOR THE AXIAL CONTOUR
Put C=cos^2(a/2),S=sin^2(a/2),lambda=(N+2)/N,c=delta lambda,b=1/(4delta).
Along either vertical contour edge, t=tanh[(s+v)/2] after reflecting s>=0,
v=c t,z=v-y in[0,c t]. If c<=2sin^2(a/2), the EXACT edge amplitude relative
to exp[Nv^2/(4delta)]w0(s+v) has exponent

 -Nb a^2+N b(z^2-2c t z)-(N+2)log Q/2
 =-Nb a^2+(N+2)logsec(a/2)-(N+2)F(t,z)/2
 <=-Nb a^2+(N+2)logsec(a/2).                               (2)

This eliminates the older independently maximized term delta(N+2)^2/(4N).
The two-edge length bound is unchanged. The old bound remains valid and all
old proofs are independent of this refinement. At t0 the length is zero;
a quantitative F>=k t^2 could exploit that correlation to improve thresholds.

PROOF OF THE LEMMA
First take0<=t<1. Let A=atanh t and u=A-z/2. Since c<=2 and z<=c t,
u>=A-t>=0. The hyperbolic identity is

 Q=(1-t^2)[C+sinh^2 u]>0.

Set w=tanh u in[0,1]. Differentiating F with respect to z gives

 F_z=-w/(C+S w^2)+t-z/c.

The tanh function is1-Lipschitz, so w>=t-z/2. Also C+S w^2<=1, hence
w/(C+S w^2)>=w. Since c<=2, t-z/c<=t-z/2. Therefore F_z<=0.
For fixed t, F(t,z) is smallest at z=c t.

Write G(t)=F(t,c t), and now u=atanh t-c t/2,w=tanh u. Direct differentiation
gives the FACTORIZED derivative

 G'(t)=[1/(1-t^2)-c/2]*[2w/(C+S w^2)-2t].                 (3)

The first factor is nonnegative because c<=2. For t>0 let r be the smaller
root of

 r=t(C+S r^2).

The roots are real since4CS t^2<=1; the quadratic is positive at0 and
negative at1 (value t-1), so its smaller root lies in(0,1), its larger root
is>=1. The relation gives r<=t and

 t-r=S t(1-r^2).

Since1/(1-x^2) increases for nonnegative x,

 atanh t-atanh r=integral_r^t dx/(1-x^2)
             >=(t-r)/(1-r^2)=S t.

Our hypothesis c/2<=S therefore implies
u=atanh t-c t/2>=atanh r, and hence w>=r. Because w<=1 and1 is below the
larger root, tS w^2-w+tC<=0. This is exactly w/(C+S w^2)>=t. Thus the
second factor in(3) is nonnegative. G'(t)>=0 and G(0)=0, so G(t)>=0.
Since F(t,z)>=G(t), (1) holds for all0<=t<1.

For t1 the formula is Q=e^-z, and
F(1,z)=-log C-z^2/(2c). Since0<=z<=c<=2S and-log C>=1-C=S,
F(1,z)>=S-c/2>=0. This also agrees with the continuous t→1 limit.
Thus all boundary cases are covered. The c0 limit corresponds to delta0
and needs no contour deformation.

EXACT RELATION TO THE EDGE DENOMINATOR
The normalized denominator squared is
 cos^2(a/2)[cosh(z/2)-t sinh(z/2)]^2
 +sin^2(a/2)[t cosh(z/2)-sinh(z/2)]^2.
Expanding gives exactly Q(t,z), not a bound. Completing the Gaussian factor
uses v=c t and z=v-y, so y^2-v^2=z^2-2c t z. Substituting delta=c/lambda
then gives(2) exactly. The lemma retains the same t in both the saddle shift
and strip amplitude; it does not combine incompatible maximizers.

moderate. Central |u|<=1 has a stable Gaussian coefficient near b-1/8, while
