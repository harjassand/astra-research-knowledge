## Independent identity and global proof

For orthogonal R, Wki=Rki² has every row and column sum equal to1. Expanding one column and summing proves

    tr Q − sum_i hQ(R e_i)
       =sum_i sum_(k<l)(Qkk+Qll−2Qkl) Rki² Rli².

Thus the axes globally maximize the contrast whenever all pair gaps are nonnegative. Define

    d=n−sum_(k,i)Rki⁴=2sum_i sum_(k<l)Rki²Rli².

The candidate contrast deficit at a true frame is at least qc d. If a certified true half-gap qt is available, its opposite deficit is at least qt d; otherwise use qt=0. Write Sc and St for the sums of the fourth tensor powers of the candidate and true unit axes. Orthogonality gives ||Sc−St||²=2d. Adding the two deficits and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives

    (qc+qt)d ≤ <fc−ft,Sc−St> ≤ e sqrt(2d),
    sqrt(d) ≤ sqrt(2)e/(qc+qt).

No local Taylor expansion, nearby-branch premise, hidden coefficient height or dimension factor enters. At d=0 the conclusion holds without division. The tensor bound is ||Sc−St||≤2e/(qc+qt).

If D=2e²/qc²<1/2, each row of W has maximum greater than1/2: its squared sum is at least1−D and cannot exceed its maximum. Column sums prevent two maxima in the same column, yielding a unique permutation. Each matched axis has squared sine distance at most D. This proves matching from the posterior cap; it does not assert the cap contains only realizable models. If it is large or qc≤0, the algorithm must retain ambiguity.

For n2, fc=a(x⁴+y⁴), ft=a[(cx+sy)⁴+(-sx+cy)⁴], c²+s²=1, both qc=qt=a>0. Direct tensor contraction gives d=4c²s² and e²=2a²d. Equality holds in the stronger bound, so its constant is sharp even in the SPD subclass. The rational rotation(3/5,4/5) permits an exact check.
