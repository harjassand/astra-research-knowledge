## 1. Interface, parameters, and exact legal nodes

Input is an integer T>=1. Let sigma be normalized area on S², and let `V_T` be the real mean-zero harmonics of degrees 1 through T. Write `lambda_l=l(l+1)`, `M=T(T+2)`, and

    ||P||_(H1,0)=||grad_S P||_2,
    zeta_X=sup_(P!=0) |N^-1 sum_i P(x_i)|/||P||_(H1,0).

Equivalently, in an L2(sigma)-orthonormal harmonic basis e,

    zeta_X²=sum_(l,m) |N^-1 sum_i e_(l,m)(x_i)|²/lambda_l.  (P1)

Use

    q=600(T+1),      p=1/q,           N=2q²,
    h=12/q,         delta=2^-24(T+1)^-8,
    I=20+10 ceil(log_2(T+1)),
    beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].                              (P2)

The cells and their true anchors c_i are unchanged: northern ring j has z interval `[1-j²/q²,1-(j-1)²/q²]`, split into `m_j=2j-1` equal longitude wedges; reflect across the equator. Each cell has area 1/N and lies in `B_h(c_i)`, where c_i is its normalized ambient centroid. Put

    r_j=(j-1/2)p,    z_j=1-r_j²,
    phi_(j,k)=(2k+1)pi/m_j,
    kappa=(pi²-1)/12,       gamma=kappa+1/4=(pi²+2)/12.

Both kappa and gamma are in `(0,1)`. If the northern true centroid has latitude coordinate `z_(C,j)>0`, define the **new real initializer** by

    z_(Y,j)=z_(C,j)-kappa p²,
    y_(j,k)=(sqrt(1-z_(Y,j)²)cos phi_(j,k),
             sqrt(1-z_(Y,j)²)sin phi_(j,k), z_(Y,j)).     (P3)

The southern nodes have exactly the same first two coordinates and the negative third coordinate. All weights are 1/N. All longitudes and all latitudes in (P3) are specified, including the first ring: `z_(C,1)=1`, `phi_(1,0)=pi`, so the northern first node is

    (-sqrt(2kappa p²-kappa²p^4), 0, 1-kappa p²).

Its southern counterpart has third coordinate `-1+kappa p²`. The poles are thus moved, rather than silently retained or removed. No azimuth is left unspecified.

The last northern centroid satisfies `z_(C,q)>=p-p²/2`, so every shifted northern latitude remains positive. Subtracting kappa p² decreases a positive latitude. The meridian angle moved is at most the value at z=1,

    arccos(1-kappa p²)<2p.                              (P4)

For example `1-cos(2p)=2sin²p>p²>kappa p²` proves the inequality. Thus the new initializer is inside `B_(h/6)(c_i)`, with known slack for all later corrections.

The algorithm acquires rational unit approximations to (P3), resets them to distinct rational unit nodes within delta, then executes I exact minimum-Euclidean tangent corrections, rational Cayley retractions and the same bounded-height distinct resets. The output target is

with O(log(T+1)) coordinate bits, acquired in deterministic polynomial bit time in T/output scale.

## 3. Sharpened midpoint formula for the shifted surrogate

The finite half-integer Abel–Plana identity is

    p sum_j f((j-1/2)p)-integral_0^1 f(r)dr
      =-i integral_0^infinity
         [f(iy)-f(-iy)-f(1+iy)+f(1-iy)]
                            /[exp(2pi q y)+1]dy.        (P8)

It applies to f_l: its possible poles at `r=+-sqrt(2)` lie outside the closed strip, and its vertical growth is polynomial. Cycle11 gives a residue derivation with the sign and rescaling explicit. We now extract the first term and bound the remainder, rather than keeping only an O(p²) estimate.

The scalar integral is exactly

    integral_0^infinity y/[exp(2pi q y)+1]dy=p²/48.

We claim for every even 2<=l<=T,

    p sum_j f_l(r_j)-integral_0^1 f_l(r)dr
       =(p²/12)[1-P_l(0)]+E_quad,
    |E_quad|<=14l²p^4.                                (P9)

The following bounds prove the claimed constant.

### 3.1 Pole boundary error: at most 2l²p^4

At r=iy, eta is real and

    eta(iy)=1+y²+p²[a(1+y²)-gamma],
    |eta(iy)-1|<=4(y²+p²)exp(2y),
    eta(iy)<=cosh[4(y+p)].

Here `a(1+y²)<=(1+y²)²` and `gamma<1`. The last inequality follows by comparing `cosh(4u)-1` with `u²+u^4+u^6`, `u=y+p`. The Legendre/Gegenbauer endpoint formula and its positive integral outside 1 give, throughout the real segment between 1 and eta,

    |P'_l|<=l² exp[4l(y+p)].

Because f_l is odd in r,

    |f_l(iy)-f_l(-iy)-4iy|
       <=16l² y(y²+p²) exp[4lp+(4l+2)y].

Use `4l+2<=6l`, `exp(4lp)<2`, and `2pi q-6l>5q`. Integrating this error in (P8) is at most

    32l²[6/(625q^4)+1/(25q^4)]<2l²p^4.                (P10)

### 3.2 Equator boundary error: at most 12l²p^4

At r=1+iy, let `z=y²-2iy` and `xi=z+p²[a(z)-gamma]`. Since `|1+z|>=1`,

    |a(z)|<=|z|+|z|²,
    asinh|xi|<=p²+8y,
    |xi|<=(p²+4y)exp(3y),
    |Re xi|<=(y²+p²)exp(4y).                           (P11)

For the asinh bound, `|z|<=y²+2y`, so
`|xi|<=p²+(1+y)^4-1<=p²+sinh(8y)`. The remaining two bounds follow by the mean value formula for `(1+y)^4` and direct expansion of `1+|z|+|z|²`.

The complex Laplace representation gives

    |P_l(w)|<=exp[l asinh|w|].

Cauchy's formula on radius `2/l` then gives

    |P''_l(w)|<=5l² exp[l asinh|w|].

For even l, `P'_l(0)=0`. Integrating the last bound from zero gives

    |P_l(w)-P_l(0)|<=(5/2)l²|w|²exp[l asinh|w|],
    |P'_l(w)|<=5l²|w|exp[l asinh|w|].

Also `P_l(conjugate xi)=P_l(-conjugate xi)`. The segment between xi and `-conjugate xi` stays in the disk of radius |xi|. Consequently

    |f_l(1+iy)-f_l(1-iy)-4iy P_l(0)|
      <=l² exp[lp²+(8l+7)y]
               [20p^4+90p²y+100p²y²+240y³].           (P12)

To check the coefficients, the two errors before (P11) are
`20l²|Re xi||xi|` and `10l²y|xi|²`, times `exp[l asinh|xi|]`. Expansion gives
`20p^4+(80p²+10p^4)y+100p²y²+240y³`; `p^4<=p²` gives (P12).

Now `8l+7<=15l`, `exp(lp²)<2`, and `2pi q-15l>5q`. Its contribution to (P8) is at most

    2l²[20/(5q^5)+90/(25q^4)+200/(125q^5)+1440/(625q^4)]
                                                       <12l²p^4. (P13)

Indeed the coefficient is `2[5.904+5.6/q]<12` for q>=600. Together with (P10), and with `-i*4iy` and the scalar integral, this proves (P9).

## 4. Shifted integral: the constant defect cancels exactly

Changing variables z=1-r² gives

    integral_0^1 f_l(r)dr=integral_0^1 P_l(phi(z))dz.

The increasing map phi has endpoints `-gamma p²` and `1-p²/12`, and derivative `1+p²a'(z)>=1`. Let psi be its inverse. Its Jacobian is

    W(t)=1/[1+p²a'(psi(t))].

Compare the integral over this interval to the integral over `[0,1]`. The exact first-order expression is

    integral_0^1 P_l(phi(z))dz-integral_0^1 P_l(z)dz
      =p²[-1/12+gamma P_l(0)-integral_0^1 a'(z)P_l(z)dz]
           +E_int,
    |E_int|<=7l²p^4.                                  (P14)

Here are direct bounds, requiring no formal asymptotic expansion. The added interval `[-gamma p²,0]` differs from its leading `gamma p²P_l(0)` by at most `(l²+3/2)p^4`: use `|P'_l|<=l²`, its length at most p², and `|W-1|<=3p²/2`. The removed interval `[1-p²/12,1]` differs from its leading `-p²/12` by at most `l²p^4/144`. On the interior,

    |W(t)-1+p²a'(t)|
       <=p²|a'(psi(t))-a'(t)|+p^4 |a'(psi(t))|²
       <=(15/4)p^4,

since `|psi(t)-t|<=p²`, `|a''|<3/2` and `|a'|<3/2`. The extra last interval in the leading interior integral contributes at most p^4/8. Summing is less than `7l²p^4`.

Combining (P7), (P9), and (P14), the ordinary Legendre moment at the actual shifted nodes obeys

    B_Y(P_l)=p²[a(1)P_l(0)-integral_0^1 a'(z)P_l(z)dz]+E_l,
    |E_l|<=[147+18log(2q/l)]l²p^4.                     (P15)

Indeed `gamma-1/12=a(1)`, and the two constants `+1/12` and `-1/12` cancel. For unshifted centroids the uncancelled constant is `(pi²-1)/12`; this explains the prescribed correction. Odd zonal degrees vanish by the exact southern reflection.

## 8. Rational acquisition and total bit costs

All real operations in (P3) are acquired to the declared precision. The centroid integrals have the existing binomial-series expansion with geometric tail ratio at most 1/2; Machin's arctangent formula certifies pi, and Taylor sine/cosine tails certify the longitudes. The new kappa requires one additional squaring of the certified pi interval. On all shifted northern nodes `z_(Y,j)>=p-3p²/2>p/2`. Their transverse radius is smallest in the first ring, where it is `sqrt(2kappa p²-kappa²p^4)>p`; the other rings also have a uniform constant times p lower bound. Rational square-root intervals therefore require only O(log q) extra guard bits beyond the output precision. The initial hemisphere stereographic chart has denominator `1+z>=1`. There is no sign oracle or root acquisition hidden in the shift. The exact real initializer is (P3); finite approximation errors are covered by delta, not inserted into its exact moment identities.

Use the rational orthogonal harmonic basis q_j and its known rational squared norms nu_j. Both the derivative equations and the H1-dual exact certificate are rational:

    zeta_X²=sum_j B_X(q_j)²/[nu_j lambda_(degree j)].

With columns of D equal to `grad q_j(x_i)`, form `H=DD^t` and solve

    H alpha=-sum_i q(x_i),       v_i=D_i^t alpha.

H is positive definite by (P22). This is exactly the minimum-Euclidean correction: `Dv=-sum_i q(x_i)` and `v=D^t alpha` are the exact primal and quadratic-objective KKT identities. Everything is rational; no orthonormalizing square root, numerical proposal or exact-LP primitive is needed.

The rational Cayley identity preserves unit length. The established stereographic grid with spacing `delta/(16N)` and at most N+1 deterministic candidate shifts makes each reset distinct within distance delta. Naive equality scanning costs O(N³) comparisons; an ordered dictionary can improve it, but neither is hidden. Each reset coordinate has O(log(N/delta))=O(log(T+1)) bits.

Basis coefficients have O(T log(T+1)) bits. Summing evaluations with differing denominators is charged up to O(NT log(T+1)) input height; it is not charged as one summand. Forming the normal matrix takes O(NM²) rational arithmetic operations and Gaussian elimination O(M³). Denominator clearing and determinant bounds keep intermediate height polynomial in T and the charged input height. Retraction outputs can temporarily be large, then the reset limits the next solve's node input height again. There are I=O(log(T+1)) steps. The complete deterministic bit cost is polynomial, and final coordinate height is O(log(T+1)). Large constants and large transient exact matrix heights remain; a practical runtime improvement requires a separate equally informed measurement.
