# Constant-pivot HC4 has exactly the plane Keller obstruction

Root, 7 October 2026. Programme deduction; historical priority unresolved. Independent reconstruction: `notes/hessian_check12/cycle13/ZERO_PIVOT_GATE.md`. The recent cylindrical theorem and the low-dimensional Hessian theorems below are **prior results**, not discoveries of this programme. Their use retains the named established prerequisites; no external or formal proof-assistant validation is claimed.

## Precise theorem

Let f be a complex polynomial in four variables, with det Hess f a nonzero constant. Suppose some nonzero constant vector v has D_v^2 f constant (possibly zero). If that constant is nonzero, grad f is a polynomial automorphism. If it is zero, a constant linear coordinate change puts f into one of two cases:

1. f = B(x,y) + z A(x,y) + w Q(x,y), where G=(A,Q) is a plane Keller map. Polynomial invertibility and noninjectivity of grad f are respectively equivalent to those of G.
2. Q is affine-linear in the representation f=P(x,y,z)+w Q(x,y); grad f is a polynomial automorphism, by the established two-dimensional Hessian theorem.

The alternatives can overlap; they are an exhaustive useful classification, not a uniqueness assertion. Consequently, the assertion that **every constant-pivot HC4 map is invertible is equivalent to the plane Jacobian conjecture**. The reverse implication uses f=z G1(x,y)+w G2(x,y) for an arbitrary plane Keller map G. This does not establish JC2, unrestricted HC4, or a descent of any known five-dimensional counterexample.

Here a pivot is along a constant linear direction. Nonlinear coordinate changes add second-derivative chain-rule terms and need independent legality/determinant checks. A pointwise Schur complement is not automatically a global polynomial reduction.

## Zero pivot: recent prior reduces ternary Q to binary Q

Choose w along v. Zero pivot gives f=P(x,y,z)+w Q(x,y,z). Put q=grad Q. Bordered determinant expansion yields

    det Hess f = -q^T adj(Hess P + w Hess Q) q.

Its coefficient of w^2 is -U(Q), where U(Q)=q^T adj(Hess Q)q. The highest possible w degree is TWO, not three. Thus U(Q)=0. Also q never vanishes, since otherwise the last Hessian row vanishes.

[Lin, arXiv2609.06465v1, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06465v1) proves that a nonconstant complex polynomial in three variables with U=0 has a nonzero constant translation direction. A constant linear change among x,y,z therefore makes Q independent of z. Root read the complete main proof; the independent worker reconstructed it using its named Bertini--Krull and Piontkowski rank-one cylinder prerequisites. Piontkowski's exact scope was checked in a recoverable primary author version. Those classical geometric theorems are not reproved here.

## Binary determinant and all-degree dichotomy

Now Q=Q(x,y). Define

    K_Q = Q_y^2 Q_xx - 2 Q_x Q_y Q_xy + Q_x^2 Q_yy.

Free-jet determinant expansion gives

    det Hess f = (Q_x P_yz - Q_y P_xz)^2
      - P_zz [Q_y^2 P_xx - 2Q_x Q_y P_xy + Q_x^2 P_yy + w K_Q].

The w coefficient implies P_zz K_Q=0 in the polynomial domain.

If P_zz=0, P=B+z A, so det Hess f=(Jac(A,Q))^2. A polynomial whose square is a nonzero constant is itself a nonzero constant. Thus G=(A,Q) is Keller. With base b=(x,y) and dual d=(z,w),

    grad f = (grad B(b) + DG(b)^T d, G(b)).

If G has an inverse, recover b first and then d using the polynomial inverse Jacobian. If distinct b1,b2 have the same G value, independently choose d1,d2 to give the same first gradient block; this constructs a gradient collision. Conversely, a polynomial inverse of the gradient, restricted to first target block zero, gives a polynomial right inverse of G. Its image is dominant, so this right inverse is two-sided. Hence the exact equivalences.

If P_zz is nonzero, K_Q=0. Here is the elementary global polynomial argument, separate from Lin's ternary result. On any smooth irreducible fibre component of Q, the tangent derivation D=Q_y partial_x-Q_x partial_y satisfies

    D(Q_x/Q_y)=K_Q/Q_y^2=0

whenever Q_y does not vanish identically. A nonzero derivation of a complex function field of transcendence degree one has constant field C: a nonconstant constant-of-derivation would give a finite separable extension on which the derivation vanishes identically. Thus the slope is constant, and so is the corresponding affine normal coordinate. Components with Q_y identically zero are vertical by the same argument. Every fibre component is therefore an entire affine line. Components from different fibre values cannot intersect, forcing their directions to agree. Their union is dense, so D_u Q=0 for one constant nonzero u. Hence Q=H(ell) for an affine-linear ell. Since grad Q is nowhere zero over C, H' has no root and is constant. Thus Q is affine-linear.

Global polynomiality matters: Q=x/y on the punctured chart y!=0 has K_Q=0 but its fibre lines can meet at the deleted point. It is not a counterexample to this lemma.

Normalize Q=x+gamma. Then det Hess_(y,z) P=-c. The prior HC2 theorem over C(x) gives a polynomial inverse H of (P_y,P_z) in y,z. It descends to C[x] by the coefficient lemma below. The full inverse is

    x=Y_w-gamma, (y,z)=H(x,Y_y,Y_z), w=Y_x-P_x(x,y,z).

## Nonzero pivot: the established HC3 branch

Write f=a w^2+w Q(x,y,z)+P(x,y,z), a nonzero constant. For the target Y_w=sigma, the polynomial critical section is w=(sigma-Q)/(2a). The reduced gradient is grad R_sigma, where

    R_sigma=P-(Q-sigma)^2/(4a).

The Schur identity gives det Hess R_sigma=c/(2a). The established all-degree HC3 theorem over C(sigma) gives a polynomial inverse in the three base variables. Coefficient descent puts it over C[sigma], yielding a polynomial four-variable inverse after recovering w. This nonzero-pivot reasoning is already present in Ni's prior common-form proof and is not new here.

The precise prior used for HC2 and HC3 is de Bondt, arXiv1203.6605v3, Theorem 2.1, over any characteristic-zero field. Its primary scope and Ni2608.14217v1 Proposition 6.1 were verified in cycle12. Ni's stated zero-pivot common form imposes degree Q<=2; the binary argument above removes that degree restriction after Lin's reduction.

## Coefficient descent lemma

Let R be a characteristic-zero domain, F in R[X]^m, and det JF a unit of R. Suppose F is a polynomial automorphism over Frac(R). Translate F(0) to zero. Its linear coefficient matrix is invertible over R. Recursive comparison by total degree gives a unique formal inverse with every coefficient in R. Over Frac(R), the known polynomial inverse has this same formal expansion. It truncates, so the formal inverse over R is polynomial and two-sided. Thus no denominators in the external parameter are introduced. Applied above, R=C[x] or C[sigma]. The unit condition is indispensable.

## What this changes, and what remains

The immediate downstream use is a stronger research filter: an all-degree nonlinear descent retaining even one constant pivot cannot create an independent HC4 mechanism. A zero pivot returns exactly to a plane Keller map or an invertible case; a nonzero pivot is invertible. This has already redirected the peer's nonlinear triangular-shear search, whose remaining linear dual term is inside the invertible branch.

An actual escape must either solve the plane Keller obstruction, or eliminate every constant linear-direction pivot while retaining polynomiality, a constant nonzero Hessian determinant and a collision. The cycle12 full Hessian-span obstruction shows the published five-dimensional family has no constant symmetric contraction even before descent; it does not forbid nonlinear transformations. Neither this theorem nor the finite symbolic checks rule those out.

Novelty of this synthesis is OPEN. Binary zero-flex geometry, low-dimensional HC and plane Keller gradient lifts have substantial prior art. Lin supplies the new ternary cylinder theorem. Our contribution here is the checked composition into an explicit all-degree selection boundary and its use to discard proposed descent classes. Prior search must precede any priority claim.
