# Cycle19: exact selective-output interface and obstructions

Status: no new algorithm meeting the imported target is proved here. The transposition identity is valid under a precise circuit interface. The two composition shortcuts below fail. The block-cover obstruction is an unconditional theorem for its explicitly restricted algorithm model, not a lower bound against all arithmetic algorithms.

## 1. Target, comparator, and charges

Inputs are dense integer matrices X of shape N×D and Y of shape D×N, plus an arbitrary list W of requested positions. The target scale is D=N^(1/4), |W|≤N^(15/8), and deterministic O(N^(2−η)) work for some fixed 0<η<1/8. No support promise is made about XY, and W is supplied, not discovered from values.

If entries use b bits, unavoidable input and output representation costs are Ω(ND b+|W| log N+|W|(2b+log D)) in a bit model. A bound depending on N alone therefore needs a bit-height promise, such as b=O(log N). With this promise and η<1/8, the list and the requested answers fit within the proposed budget. This does not provide the arithmetic algorithm.

An equally informed full-product comparator is already N^(2+o(1)) field operations at this aspect ratio: even the older α>0.321334 covers D=N^(1/4). The new α>0.465 does not create a subquadratic saving by itself. Computing all N² answers still writes N² values. Taking a subset after computing them does not erase that cost. Individual requested dot products cost |W|D=Θ(N^(17/8)) at the largest mask size.

The source release counts arithmetic operations over fixed fields. It explicitly excludes coefficient heights and numerical stability. A finite algebraic scheme can in principle be converted to integer bit arithmetic using exact number-field coordinates, but its acquisition, coefficient heights, recursion, and decoding must be supplied and charged. No such selected-output scheme is present in family107.

## 2. A legal circuit transposition lemma

For a fixed mask W, introduce independent variables z_ij at the positions in W and set other entries of the N×N matrix Z to zero. Suppose a deterministic preprocessing algorithm depending only on W acquires a division-free straight-line circuit C_W computing

    (Z,Y) ↦ ZYᵀ

for every assignment of the z variables and Y entries. Here Y is D×N, so the circuit has N×D outputs. Let its arithmetic cost be T(N,D,W), its acquisition cost be P(N,D,W), and retain all intermediate values required by reverse differentiation.

Contract its output with X:

    f(Z,X,Y)=Σ_i,k X_ik (ZYᵀ)_ik
             =Σ_(i,j)∈W z_ij Σ_k X_ik Y_kj.

The polynomial identity gives

    ∂f/∂z_ij=(XY)_ij.

Evaluate the circuit, contract in O(ND) operations, and reverse its ADD/SUB/MUL gates. An addition propagates the adjoint to both parents; a multiplication propagates it multiplied by the opposite forward value. This uses O(T+ND+|W|) field operations, O(T+ND+|W|) stored values in the direct implementation, and P preprocessing. All requested gradient writes are included. Z=0 is a legal evaluation point because the identity is polynomial and every structural gate is retained.

This suffices even if C_W has internally nonlinear expressions that later cancel. It does not suffice to differentiate a value-pruned trace. The algorithm “if z=0 return 0; otherwise return z*y” correctly computes z*y, but its executed trace at z=0 has derivative zero instead of y. A generic nonzero execution can sometimes avoid this issue over infinite fields; acquiring a valid branch and proving its correctness on an open set, with charged search and bit costs, are additional requirements.

This is our deduction, independently reconstructed by logic_prior14. It is a reusable interface, not the missing sparse circuit.

## 3. Fully sparse matrix multiplication does not automatically supply C_W

Primary source: Omar Graia, *Optimal Deterministic Fully Sparse Matrix Multiplication*, arXiv:2608.18496v1, submitted 19 August 2026. The introduction and model/recovery sections were read from the saved full HTML. Theorem1.1 is parameterized by the ambient dimension n, total input nonzeros n^δin, and total output nonzeros n^δout. Its exponent is

    β_R(δin,δout)=max{δin, max_(a+b=δout; a,b∈[0,1]) ω_(δin,R)(a,1,b)}.

The sparse rectangular exponent on the right remains an oracle parameter. It is not automatically a dense exponent at a freely reduced ambient dimension.

For the transposition input ZYᵀ, nnz(Z)≤N^(15/8), nnz(Yᵀ)≤ND=N^(5/4), and output size is at most ND. A tempting rescaling n=√nnz(input), followed by a dense-input output-sparse bound, is invalid: Z still has N rows and N middle coordinates, larger than that new n, and the arbitrary supports do not admit the required free packing.

There is a direct obstruction in the recovery routine. Its outer sketch size is min(D,ceil(K/t_prev)) at the relevant scales t≤N, where K=ND is the output bound. This stays D, so the outer sketch may be the identity I_D. At the final inner scale the row sketch has dimension N and may be I_N. The sketched multiplication is then

    I_N (ZYᵀ) I_D = ZYᵀ,

the original sparse-by-dense product. The method still requires the input-sparse rectangular exponent at endpoint (a,b)=(1,1/4). It does not prove that this product has a value-independent subquadratic circuit. Nor can its value-based zero tests and adaptive support recovery be transposed without a separate circuit construction.

The failed rescaling was proposed during exploration and immediately retracted before adoption. The saved finite N=256,D=4 fixture confirms the endpoint dimensions; the argument above is universal.

Related prior: Abboud–Bringmann–Fischer–Künnemann, arXiv:2309.06317v1, submitted 12 September 2023. Their general fully sparse exponent satisfies ω(σ−1,2−σ,1+r−σ)=σ for m_out=m_in^r. Even under ω=2 this gives σ=max{1+r/3,r}, not automatic input-linear time. This source was read in relevant excerpts only.

## 4. Recent output-local algorithm: genuine progress, different regime

Primary source: Josh Alman and Virginia Vassilevska Williams, *Truly Subquadratic 3SUM and Truly Subcubic APSP via Triangles in Sparse Lopsided Graphs*, arXiv:2610.06783v1, submitted 5 October 2026. Theorem1 states deterministic O(N²/D^0.063) operations on O(log N)-bit integers for N≥D^18, polynomially bounded integer entries, and |W|≤N²/√D. More generally D≤N^ε for every ε<0.1204 is permitted, with some positive saving depending on ε and the mask-density exponent. The paper claims concrete 3SUM/APSP improvements through reductions. These are author claims, not results independently certified by this worker.

Technical reading covered §§2.2–2.4 and §§4.2–4.4 in full, including the exact ten-term identity, harmless-error proof, tiling, leaf counting, box data structure, and bit sizes. Reductions in §§3 and5 were not fully audited.

The scheme uses the ten-term Schönhage identity for a 3×1×3 outer product and a 1×4×1 inner product. At L levels, with m inner levels, it simultaneously computes K=binom(L,m) products of shape N0×D×N0, with N0=3^(L−m) and D=4^m. Their grid tile has side √K N0. Output-locality comes from the output-leg support: one private leaf per output and shared leaves classified by their order d. The leaf counts are

    α_d=binom(m,d)9^d,
    β_d=binom(L,m−d)9^(L−m+d).

The full input encodings must be shared across row and column bands. At the limiting useful ratio L/m→10, Stirling's formula gives

    K N0² = 10^(10m) poly(m)^(−1),
    √K N0 = D^(5 log_4 10+o(1)) = D^(8.304820...+o(1)).

Even fitting one tile requires N≥D^(8.3048...+o(1)), giving ε*=log4/(5log10)=0.120411998... . The encoding term has the same limiting threshold. The contemporary dual exponent α is not a parameter in this dependency. Substituting α>0.465 cannot reduce it below4, as would be necessary at D=N^(1/4).

Splitting the inner dimension into blocks D0≤N^ε* makes D/D0 calls. Each call saves only a small power of D0, so this straightforward composition loses its subquadratic saving at D=N^(1/4). A new identity with a different output-leg support or a different packing is needed.

## 5. Complete-block covers require Ω(N²) for some legal masks

This theorem rules out a broad but restricted strategy: cover requested scalar products by independently charged complete rectangular matrix products, optionally using arbitrary inner-coordinate subsets. It allows mask-dependent row and column sets, overlaps, and arbitrary block aspect ratios.

Let ρ=N^(−1/8), D=N^(1/4), and sample each position of W independently with probability p=ρ/2. For sufficiently large N there exists a mask with |W|=Θ(ρN²), |W|≤ρN², and, simultaneously for all row sets R and column sets C of sizes s,t,

    e_W(R,C)≤ρst+C0(s+t)log N,

where C0=6/log2 suffices. To prove this, for E~Bin(st,p) use exponential Markov with λ=log2:

    Pr[E≥2pst+C0(s+t)log N]
      ≤exp(pst−(log2)(2pst+C0(s+t)log N))
      ≤N^(−6(s+t)).

There are at most N^(s+t) choices of R,C of these sizes. Summing the resulting N^(−5(s+t)) bound over positive s,t makes the failure probability tend to zero. A Chernoff bound also makes |W| between ρN²/4 and 3ρN²/4 with probability tending to one. Hence the stated mask exists.

A block uses row set R, column set C, and inner set K of size d≤D. Charge it at least

    c=max{st,sd,dt}.

This is the full-block output/flattening/input size charge. The block covers at most e_W(R,C)d requested triples. The uniform mask inequality implies

    e_W(R,C)d≤ρstd+C0d(s+t)log N
             ≤(ρD+2C0 log N)c.

Every requested pair must be summed over all D inner coordinates, so a complete-block cover of W×[D] has

    Σ_blocks e_W(R,C)d ≥ |W|D.

Consequently its independently charged cost is

    Σ_blocks c ≥ |W|D/(ρD+2C0 log N)
               = Ω(|W|/ρ)=Ω(N²),

because ρD=N^(1/8) dominates log N. This proof applies even if an ideal full-block multiplication attained its flattening lower bound. Input sharing that changes the independent cost model, output linear combinations across blocks, general arithmetic circuits, and value-dependent methods are outside the theorem. Such mechanisms may escape the obstruction and are exactly where a new selective-output primitive would have to act.

## 6. Family135 does not give a masked-product lower bound

The source polynomial IMM_(n,n) is the (1,1) entry of the product of n independent n×n matrices. Its degree is n and it has n³ ambient variables. The model is syntactically homogeneous ΣΠΣΠΣ circuits; shared gates and arbitrary bottom linear support are allowed, and leaves count toward size. The main lower bound is n^(√n/400) over characteristic zero for sufficiently large n, while an upper bound n^(√n+4) holds over every field.

The mixed differentiation/multiplication rank and trace-moment proof is consequential for this restricted high-degree model. It does not apply to the degree-two map (X,Y)↦(XY)|_W, nor to the degree-three scalar contraction in §2. Identifying the number of matrix factors with a matrix width is invalid. Fast recursive arithmetic circuits have no prescribed depth-five homogeneous form.
