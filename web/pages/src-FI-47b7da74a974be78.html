# Derivations and scope audit

This note records the mathematical audit useful for Astra synthesis. The source packet remains the source for the full proofs of its two-contact theorem and contact-shear theorem; the independent checker here covers exact finite identities, not those analytic proofs.

## 1. Two-contact classification reconstructed

Let $F$ be a homogeneous quartic on $\mathbb R^{2\times2}$, rank-one convex, and let $q(A)=\det A$. In the full 35-coefficient planar quartic space, reciprocity gives
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A].
\]
Applying the indefinite non-strict Finsler lemma to the right side yields, for each fixed $A$,
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+\lambda_Aq(X),\qquad P_A\succeq0.
\]
For any balanced second-moment matrix $C\succeq0$ with $\operatorname{tr}(J_{\det}C)=0$, this implies $H_F(C)\succeq0$. If $A\in\ker H_F(C)$, then $\operatorname{ran}C\subseteq\ker P_A$. At rank-one acoustic contacts, global nonnegativity of $D^2F(A+tY)[R,R]$ forces its first derivative to vanish. Cubic tensor symmetry then gives the cubic closure used in the classification. For arbitrary balanced laws, the same argument gives $b_F\in\operatorname{ran}H_F(C)$, so the background minimum is the exact Schur expression, but its sign is not implied.

For the complete left-SO(2)-invariant nine-coefficient quartic cone, the source theorem classifies the rank-two singular case as follows. If some balanced $C$ of rank at least two has singular $H_F(C)$, choose $A\in\ker H_F(C)$; then the Finsler remainder has rank at most two. The proof separates rank-one and invertible $A$, then rank-one, generic, and singular top-block cases after normalization. Rank-one $A$ reduces to a convex binary quartic plus determinant terms, with acoustic PSD tests eliminating the nonconvex cross terms. For invertible $A$, normalize $A=I$, write the quartic in complex variables, and split by the rank of the positive remainder and the determinant form on its kernel plane. An indefinite determinant restriction is contradicted by the cubic-closure coefficient $-4\lambda^3a^3/(r^2p^3)$; a totally null plane yields the convex sum $f|z+w|^4+(g-f)|w|^4$; singular top-block cases are excluded by rank-one acoustic tests. The surviving integrands are exactly
\[
\mathcal P_2=
\{\gamma(\det A)^2+|\alpha x_{\mathbb C}+\beta y_{\mathbb C}|^4:\gamma\ge0\}
\;\cup\;
\{a|Ar|^4+|\alpha x_{\mathbb C}+\beta y_{\mathbb C}|^4:
a\ge0,\ r\in\mathbb R^2\},
\]
where $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C$ are arbitrary. Conversely, each displayed class supplies two independent rank-one contacts. Thus, within this scope, existence of a rank-two balanced singular Hessian, existence of two independent rank-one Hessian contacts, and membership in $\mathcal P_2$ are equivalent. In particular, if $F\notin\mathcal P_2$, every balanced $C$ of rank at least two has $H_F(C)\succ0$.

The larger nullspace-closure statement applies to all 35 planar quartic coefficients, but the final exceptional-class and two-contact classification is only for the nine-coefficient left-SO(2) cone. This theorem does not prove nonnegativity of the positive-definite Schur remainder, full Morrey, or quasiconvexity for the whole cone. The supplied verifier was replayed and its JSON reproduced byte-for-byte; the analytic proof was source-audited, not externally reviewed or formalized.

## 2. Exact quartic acoustic flatness for the fixed integrand $G$

Use row-major coordinates $X=(a,b,c,d)$ and the source integrand
\[
G(X)=3(u+v-2q)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+q^2-\frac52qu,
\quad u=a^2+c^2,\quad v=b^2+d^2,\quad q=ad-bc.
\]
For $e>0$, define
\[
R_\pm(e)=
\begin{pmatrix}\pm2e&1\\6e^2&\pm3e\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D(e)=|R_\pm(e)|^2=1+13e^2+36e^4.
\]
Both determinants vanish exactly. Their dot product is $1-13e^2+36e^4$, so their normalized separation is
\[
s_e^2=1-\frac{(R_+:R_-)^2}{D^2}
=\frac{52e^2(1+36e^4)}{D^2}.
\]

Let $Q_e=D^2G(R_+)+D^2G(R_-)$. In coordinate ordering $(a,d)\oplus(b,c)$ this matrix is block diagonal. Its $(a,d)$ block $A_e$ has
\[
\det A_e=80e^4(243e^4+192e^2+13),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}A_e=12(179e^4+66e^2+4).
\]
The other block $B_e$ has positive leading entry and determinant
\[
(B_e)_{11}=24(6e^2+1)(19e^2+3),\qquad
\det B_e=48(15201e^8+9752e^6+2129e^4+192e^2+6),
\]
so $B_e\succ0$. The large eigenvalue of $A_e$ tends to $48$, while $\det A_e/e^4\to1040$. Hence its small eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(Q_e)}{e^4}\longrightarrow\frac{1040}{48}=\frac{65}{3}.
\]
For the packet's acoustic modulus
\[
\mu_G(s)=\min_{\substack{|R|=|S|=1,\ \det R=\det S=0\\1-(R:S)^2\ge s^2}}
\lambda_{\min}(D^2G(R)+D^2G(S)),
\]
the normalized pair $R_\pm/\sqrt D$ is an admissible test at separation $s_e$. Since $D^2G$ is degree two,
\[
\limsup_{e\downarrow0}\frac{\mu_G(s_e)}{s_e^4}
\le\frac{65/3}{52^2}=\frac5{624}.
\]
This is an upper-asymptotic obstruction to any lower bound $\mu_G(s)\ge c s^\alpha$ with fixed $c>0$ and $\alpha<4$. It does not establish a matching $s^4$ lower bound or any universal exponent for other integrands.

## 3. Full-rank compatible covariance realization

Let $r_\pm=R_\pm/\sqrt D$ and
\[
C_e=\tfrac12(r_+r_+^T+r_-r_-^T).
\]
The two nonzero eigenvalues are $(1\pm r_e)/2$, where $r_e=(R_+:R_-)/D$. For small $e$, $r_e>0$, so the anisotropy is
\[
\tau_e=1-\lambda_{\max}(C_e)=\frac{13e^2}{D}.
\]
The averaged Hessian is $H_G(C_e)=Q_e/(2D)$, hence
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e))}{e^4}\to\frac{65}{6},\qquad
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e))}{\tau_e^2}\to\frac5{78}.
\]

The two-mode covariance is realized by smooth periodic gradients for $e=1/(2m)$, $m\in\mathbb N$. Set
\[
K_+=(1,m),\quad K_-=(-1,m),\quad a_+=(1,3e),\quad a_-=(1,-3e)
\]
and
\[
\varphi_m(x,y)=
\frac{a_+\sin(2\pi K_+\cdot(x,y))+a_-\sin(2\pi K_-\cdot(x,y))}
{2\pi m\sqrt D}.
\]
Differentiating gives exactly
\[
D\varphi_m=r_+\cos(2\pi K_+\cdot(x,y))
+r_-\cos(2\pi K_-\cdot(x,y)).
\]
The integer frequency matrix with rows $K_+,K_-$ has determinant $2m\ne0$, so its torus homomorphism is onto and pushes Haar measure to Haar measure. The two phases are independent uniform phases. Their cosine means vanish, second moments are $1/2$, and cross moments vanish. Every cubic monomial has an odd power of at least one centered phase, so $b_G=\mathbb E DG(D\varphi_m)=0$. Consequently $C_{D\varphi_m}=C_e$ and $H_G(D\varphi_m)=H_G(C_e)$ exactly. The pointwise bound $|D\varphi_m|_F\le2$ gives uniformly bounded gradients and fourth moments, hence fourth-moment equiintegrability. This two-mode construction has rank-two covariance.

There is a full-rank version using two smaller modes. For $m\ge3$, with $e=1/(2m)$, add to the potential
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
e^3\sin(6\pi x)/(6\pi)\\
e^3\sin(8\pi x)/(8\pi)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its gradient adds $e^3E_{11}\cos(2\pi(3x))+e^3E_{21}\cos(2\pi(4x))$, where $E_{11}$ and $E_{21}$ are the row-major coordinate vectors for the matrices with a single $1$ in positions $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$. The four integer frequencies are $K_+,K_-,(3,0),(4,0)$. They are pairwise distinct up to sign for $m\ge3$, so their cosine modes are orthogonal and
\[
C_e^{\rm full}=C_e+\frac{e^6}{2}(E_{11}E_{11}^T+E_{21}E_{21}^T),
\qquad M_2:=\mathbb E|D\varphi_m|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}C_e^{\rm full}=1+e^6.
\]
The four amplitude vectors span $\mathbb R^4$; the exact determinant of the matrix with columns $r_+,r_-,e^3E_{11},e^3E_{21}$ is $6e^7/D$. Hence $\det C_e^{\rm full}=9e^{14}/(4D^2)>0$. The covariance is balanced as well: $\mathbb E\det(D\varphi_m)=(C_e^{\rm full})_{14}-(C_e^{\rm full})_{23}=0$ in row-major coordinates.

The four phases are not jointly independent on a two-torus, and independence is not needed. Since $DG$ is homogeneous cubic, its mean vanishes if no signed sum of three selected frequencies is zero. A signed sum has zero $y$-coordinate only if the number of old modes $K_\pm$ is even and their signed $m$-coordinates cancel. With one or three old modes the $y$-coordinate is a nonzero odd multiple of $m$. With two old modes, cancellation leaves their signed $x$-sum in $\{-2,0,2\}$, which cannot be canceled by the remaining $x$-frequency $\pm3$ or $\pm4$. With no old modes, three signed values from $\{3,4\}$ cannot sum to zero. Thus there is no cubic resonance and $b_G=0$ exactly.

Quadratic dependence of $D^2G$ on its argument and Fourier orthogonality give
\[
H_G(C_e^{\rm full})=H_G(C_e)+\frac{e^6}{2}\big(D^2G(E_{11})+D^2G(E_{21})\big).
\]
Both added Hessian matrices are positive definite (the first four leading principal minors are positive), and $H_G(C_e)\succ0$ for $e>0$ by the exact acoustic blocks above. In particular $C_e^{\rm full}$ and $H_G(C_e^{\rm full})$ are both positive definite. Weyl's inequality and the $O(e^6)$ covariance/Hessian perturbations imply
\[
\tau_e^{\rm full}:=1-\lambda_{\max}(C_e^{\rm full}/M_2)=13e^2+O(e^4),\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e^{\rm full}))=\frac{65}{6}e^4+o(e^4).
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e^{\rm full}))}{M_2(\tau_e^{\rm full})^2}\longrightarrow\frac{65}{1014}=\frac5{78}.
\]
This upgrades the covariance obstruction to actual smooth compatible gradients with full-rank $C$ and $H$, bounded gradient (indeed $|D\varphi_m|_F\le2+2e^3$), and uniformly integrable fourth moments: no lower bound $\lambda_{\min}H_G(C)\ge c M_2\tau^\alpha$ can hold along this family for $\alpha<2$. The pairwise acoustic-modulus bound above remains a pair-test upper bound, not a universal modulus theorem. These fields have growing Fourier bandwidth and do not give strong-$L^4$ convergence to a fixed one-line field, a Schur-loss discontinuity, or a negative Jensen gap.

## 4. What strong $L^4$ does and does not give for Schur loss

If fixed $F$ is quartic and $X_n\to X$ strongly in $L^4$, its moment data converge at least linearly in the $L^4$ distance on bounded $L^4$ sets:
\[
|c_n-c|+|b_n-b|+\|H_n-H\|\le C_F\|X_n-X\|_{L^4}.
\]
This follows from degree-four, degree-three, and degree-two polynomial difference bounds and Hölder. It does not control the inverse of a Hessian whose smallest eigenvalue tends to zero.

For an invertible $H_n$ in the orthogonal splitting $W\oplus N$, with $N=\ker H_0$ and $H_0|_W\succ0$, write
\[
H_n=\begin{pmatrix}A_n&B_n\\B_n^T&D_n\end{pmatrix},\quad
S_n=D_n-B_n^TA_n^{-1}B_n,\quad
r_n=b_{n,N}-B_n^TA_n^{-1}b_{n,W}.
\]
Then exactly
\[
\frac12b_n^TH_n^{-1}b_n
=\frac12b_{n,W}^TA_n^{-1}b_{n,W}
+\frac12r_n^TS_n^{-1}r_n.
\]
Moment convergence handles the first term. Continuity at the singular limit additionally requires $r_n^TS_n^{-1}r_n\to0$. Equiintegrability or strong $L^4$ convergence supplies no such relative rate by itself.

In the source packet, the compatible concentration sequence has fixed fourth-moment mass on shrinking supports and is not equiintegrable; it does not decide the strong-$L^4$ compatible case. The separate bounded law is not compatible. A conditional first-order cancellation holds for this $G$ along a fixed perturbation of a one-line base $\zeta e_{12}$. At $R=e_{12}$, $H_R=D^2G(R)\succeq0$ has kernel $\mathbb RI$, and $R\perp I$. With $m_j=\mathbb E\zeta^j$, $m_1=0$, $m_2>0$, the limiting blocks are $H_0=m_2H_R$ and $b_0=m_3DG(R)$. Quartic Euler gives $H_RR=3DG(R)$, so a range solution is $z_0=(m_3/(3m_2))R$, which lies in $(\ker H_R)^\perp$. For a fixed perturbation $X_\epsilon=\zeta R+\epsilon Z$,
\[
b_1:I=m_2D^2G(R)[Z,I]=0,\qquad
H_1[I,z_0]=\frac{m_3}{3m_2}\mathbb E[\zeta D^3G(R)[Z,I,R]]=0,
\]
because $D^3G(R)[Z,I,R]=2D^2G(R)[Z,I]=0$. This removes the first-order kernel cubic along this fixed perturbation only. It does not control higher-order Schur degeneracy or arbitrary sequences. Compatible strong-$L^4$ loss continuity remains open in this audit.

## 5. Contact shear to a restricted compatible Jensen class

The source contact-shear theorem supplies an invertible $A$, a rank-one radical $R=a\otimes b$, and $v=A^{-1}a$ with $b\cdot v=0$; it also proves invariance of the cubic restriction along $R$ on the contact plane. The straightening map is
\[
\Psi_h(x)=x+v h(b\cdot x),\qquad \det D\Psi_h=1.
\]
The source leaves arbitrary coupled residual profiles unresolved. A narrower transfer can be proved. On the two-torus, assume $b\in\mathbb Z^2$ is primitive; choose $c\in\mathbb Z^2$ so rows $b,c$ form a unimodular matrix. For periodic Lipschitz scalar $h$ and vector-valued $g,k$, define
\[
\Psi_h(x)=x+v h(b\cdot x),\quad
w(z)=g(b\cdot z)+k(c\cdot z),\quad
u(x)=A\Psi_h(x)+w(\Psi_h(x)).
\]
This is an affine map plus a periodic displacement. Change variables $z=\Psi_h(x)$ and set $s=b\cdot z$, $t=c\cdot z$. Then
\[
Du=A+k'(t)\otimes c+
\big(g'(s)+(A+k'(t)\otimes c)v h'(s)\big)\otimes b.
\]
For fixed $t$, the vector increment has mean zero in $s$. Rank-one convexity makes $\eta\mapsto F(A+k'(t)\otimes c+\eta\otimes b)$ convex, so Jensen in $s$ gives an average at least $F(A+k'(t)\otimes c)$. Jensen in $t$, using $\mathbb E k'=0$, then gives
\[
\mathbb E F(Du)\ge F(A).
\]
This covers a separated shear-plus-two-phase profile class only. It does not address general coupled $w(s,t)$, arbitrary compatible gradients, or the full positive-definite Schur inequality.

For $G$, take $A=I$, $a=e_1$, $b=e_2$, $v=e_1$, $c=e_1$, $h'(y)=\cos(2\pi y)$, $g=0$, and $k'(x)=\alpha\cos(2\pi x)e_2$. The actual periodic map
\[
u(x,y)=\left(x+\frac{\sin(2\pi y)}{2\pi},
y+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\sin\big(2\pi x+\sin(2\pi y)\big)\right)
\]
has determinant-one gradient. Exact trigonometric integration gives
\[
\mathbb E G(Du)-G(I)=\frac{309\alpha^4+560\alpha^2+108}{96}>0.
\]
At $\alpha=1/3$ this is $4699/2592$. It is a checked positive example, not a negative Jensen witness.

## 6. Relation to existing Astra capital

N800 already contains an integration-derived near-singular construction for $F=(\det A)^2$: a smooth fixed-bandwidth compatible family has $C,H\succ0$ for each positive parameter, tends to a singular endpoint, and has optimized gap tending to zero while the limiting gap is $1/4$; its minimizing background diverges. That is a separate polyconvex example and a gap discontinuity. The new packet's fixed non-$\mathcal P_2$ integrand $G$ has a compatible concentrating family with $H_\rho\to H_0\ne0$, $b_\rho\to0$, and Schur loss tending to $80089/21313530>0$, while the limiting Schur loss is zero; its actual gaps remain positive. The mechanisms and continuity quantities differ, and neither gives a negative compatible gap.

N797's exact heat/two-form certificates concern the $F_\star$ slice and adjacent $H_L$ family, not the full nine-coefficient cone. N740's pointwise compatible two-form completion still lacks a smooth/global closed selection. Their universal Q4/Morrey gates remain open. The packet should remain source-derived/unreviewed; these additions are scoped derivations and exact checks, not status promotion.
