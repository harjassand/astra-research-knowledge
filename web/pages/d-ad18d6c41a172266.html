## Exact interface and conclusion

The underlying labeled manifold is R/Z. Unknown g=q(x)²dx² has q smooth, positive, and ∫_0^1q=1. A fixed finite list of m experiments supplies smooth strictly positive probability **coordinate measures** μ_j=α_j(x)dx and ν_j=β_j(x)dx. The oracle supplies the entire exact squared-geodesic-cost optimal map, including coordinate labels. It supplies no volume density, transport potential or transport cost value.

For every smooth positive normalized baseline q0, every C^r neighborhood (and every open neighborhood in the usual C∞ topology) contains distinct normalized q+,q− with exactly identical complete maps for all m prescribed pairs. This is a failure of local injectivity of the finite-data observation operator on the unrestricted smooth labeled metric class.

The claim does not imply randomized minimax error when the design is independently randomized: the indistinguishable pair furnished by the theorem can depend on the realized fixed design. It also does not say that arbitrary discontinuous finite feedback is covered, or that a higher-dimensional full map reduces to a scalar.

## One scalar determines each complete map

Set Q(x)=∫_0^xq, A(x)=∫_0^xα and B(x)=∫_0^xβ, using degree-one periodic lifts. All are smooth increasing diffeomorphisms of R. For every real θ,

T_θ(x)=B⁻¹(A(x)+θ),  S_θ(y)=A⁻¹(B(y)−θ).

These maps push μ to ν, are inverse degree-one lifts, and descend to smooth circle diffeomorphisms. For fixed supplied marginals their shape is fixed apart from θ. Define the **lifted** cost

C_q(θ)=(1/2)∫_0^1α(x)[Q(T_θ(x))−Q(x)]²dx.

Differentiation, followed by y=T_θ(x), gives

C'_q(θ)=F_q(θ)=∫_0^1q(y)[Q(y)−Q(S_θ(y))]dy.

The transformed integrand is1-periodic, so its integration interval can be changed to[0,1]. Differentiating the inverse S gives

C''_q(θ)=∫_0^1 q(y)q(S_θ(y))/α(S_θ(y))dy
≥(min q)²/(max α)>0.

Thus C is strictly convex. Degree-one lifts A(x)−x, B⁻¹(x)−x and Q(x)−x are bounded periodic functions, so Q(T_θ(x))−Q(x)=θ+O(1) uniformly as |θ|→∞. This proves coercivity, existence of a minimum and a unique real zero Θ(q) of F.

On a compact parameter sphere of positive q, all these formulas depend continuously on q. F is quadratic in q for fixed θ, while S is entirely marginal-defined. The scalar implicit-function theorem, or monotonic-root continuity, gives continuous (indeed smooth on finite-dimensional perturbation families) Θ(q).

## Direct global optimality and cut-branch certificate

The step from the minimizing lifted cost to the circle cost is load-bearing. The geodesic cost is

c_circle(x,y)=(1/2)min_{k∈Z}|Q(y+k)−Q(x)|².

It is not valid to differentiate an arbitrary shortest-distance branch, or claim that the projected shift cost is globally strictly convex. For example q=α=β=1 gives lifted C(θ)=θ²/2, whereas its projected circle counterpart is dist(θ,Z)²/2. At θ=3/5 these are9/50 and2/25. They agree at the correctly selected optimum but differ elsewhere.

Here is an independent exact bridge. Work in arclength u=Q(x) and define

R(u)=Q(T_Θ(Q⁻¹(u))).

R is smooth increasing and R(u+1)=R(u)+1. Write δ(u)=R(u)−u, which is periodic. The stationarity equation F_q(Θ)=0 changes target-arclength variables to give

0=∫_0^1δ(u)R'(u)du
 =∫_0^1δ(u)du+(1/2)[δ(u)²]_0^1
 =∫_0^1δ(u)du.

Choosing the unit integration interval after substitution is legitimate by periodicity. Let Ψ be a primitive with Ψ'=R. Then Ψ''=R'>0, Ψ is strictly convex and grows quadratically. Its zero-mean displacement implies

Ψ(u+1)=Ψ(u)+u+1/2.

Let Ψ* be its Legendre transform. Substitution in the supremum gives

Ψ*(v+1)=Ψ*(v)+v+1/2.

Consequently the potentials

φ(u)=u²/2−Ψ(u),  χ(v)=v²/2−Ψ*(v)

are1-periodic and descend to the circle. Fenchel's inequality gives

φ(u)+χ(v)≤(1/2)|u−v|².

Because χ is periodic, the same inequality holds for every v+k, hence for the minimum geodesic cost over k. On v=R(u), Fenchel equality gives φ(u)+χ(R(u))=(1/2)|u−R(u)|². Since the geodesic cost is no larger than this lifted cost, the two inequalities force equality with the **shortest geodesic branch**. The transported graph is therefore globally optimal by integration of this feasible dual pair.

The graph avoids antipodes strictly. Equality at |R(u)−u|=1/2 would force Fenchel equality at two distinct lifts v and v±1, contradicting the unique derivative Ψ'(u)=R(u). Thus |R(u)−u|<1/2 everywhere. Compactness gives a positive branch margin for each fixed metric/marginal pair, without claiming a uniform margin over the unrestricted class.

Any optimal circle coupling must have zero nonnegative dual gap. At each source u, equality at a minimizing lift forces that lift to equal Ψ'(u)=R(u). Thus the destination is unique modulo integers and every optimal plan is this deterministic graph. This proves uniqueness of the full circle map and independently checks the global/cut issue. Positive smooth source density means equality of its smooth maps almost everywhere is also equality everywhere.

This reconstruction agrees with Delon–Salomon–Sobolevskii Theorems4.6/5.4, whose hypotheses are satisfied: the lifted metric cost is smooth, c_xy=−q(x)q(y)<0, simultaneously integer-shift invariant, and coercive in |x−y|. Convexity in that source is Lemma5.2, not Definition5.1. Our direct C'' proof and dual certificate do not need their generic convexity proof.

## Exact finite-observation collision

Choose m+1 linearly independent smooth zero-mean functions h_i. For a∈S^m and sufficiently small ε>0 put

q_a=q0+εΣ_i a_i h_i.

By compactness of the parameter sphere, ε can make every q_a positive and place all q_a in any prescribed neighborhood of q0. Every member has length1 exactly. A C∞ open neighborhood constrains finitely many seminorms locally; continuity of this finite-dimensional family makes the same argument apply.

The map a↦(Θ_1(q_a),…,Θ_m(q_a)) is continuous S^m→R^m. Borsuk–Ulam supplies an antipodal pair with identical observation vectors. The shifts can be taken as real numbers with the fixed lift convention; equality is stronger than equality modulo integers. Since T is already marginal-defined apart from its shift, all m **entire maps** are identical. Linear independence makes q_a≠q_−a. Positivity makes q_a²≠q_−a². Common length1 eliminates nontrivial constant rescaling.

For orthonormal h_i, writing h_a=Σa_i h_i gives ||h_a||₂=1 and

||q_a−q_−a||₂=2ε,
||g_a−g_−a||₂=4ε||q0 h_a||₂≥4ε min(q0).

Any deterministic estimator with this same fixed transcript has worst-case coordinate-tensor L² error at least2ε min(q0) on that pair. This is a deterministic two-point statement; randomized-design lower bounds need separate quantifiers.

## Marginals, coordinate labels and normalization

Both metrics are compared under the identity labeling of R/Z. The supplied probability measures remain exactly α_jdx and β_jdx under both models, and their probability integrals remain1. The unknown Riemannian volume form is qdx. Expressed relative to that volume, their densities are α_j/q and β_j/q; these remain smooth and positive but generally change with the metric. The proof never treats those metric-relative density functions as additional fixed observations.

This matches the relevant all-marginal source contract. Zhai–Zhang Remark1.2 explicitly explains that one specifies the measures, rather than the densities relative to the unknown volume. Their Theorem1.1 assumes maps for **every** admissible marginal pair. A finite-list collision is logically compatible with that all-data assertion. I excerpt-read the statement and its interface; I did not independently validate their full anisotropic elliptic reconstruction or infer a dimension-one extension from it.

If the oracle additionally supplied qdx, volume-based densities for enough known coordinate measures, transport cost values, or optimal potentials, that is a different interface. The present fixed-shift proof would not automatically cover it. A finite number of cost scalars could instead be included as additional continuous coordinates in a new Borsuk–Ulam count, but that extension is not needed or adopted here.

All positive unit-length circle metrics are intrinsically isometric by arclength reparametrization. Therefore the collision concerns different **labeled tensor fields**, not different unlabelled intrinsic circle geometries. Up to arbitrary diffeomorphism, the unit-length inverse-circle problem is already trivial. This caveat is explicit in r15's theorem and does not invalidate its supplied labeled interface.

## Secondary claims and replay

Continuous fixed-depth adaptive selection is covered when the selection rule is continuous in a topology controlling the smooth positive marginals: the shift transcript is continuous by induction, and equal prior shifts give equal chosen future marginals and maps. No theorem for arbitrary discontinuous selection follows from this argument. For a realized arbitrary adaptive transcript with linearly independent compatibility derivatives, the stated finite-codimension Banach implicit-function theorem is valid after choosing a positive C^r neighborhood; the kernel is infinite-dimensional and surjectivity admits a finite-dimensional complement. Singular transcripts are appropriately excluded from that conditional claim.

The explicit parity witness also checks. T_k(x)=x+(t/k)sin(2πkx) is increasing for0<t<1/(2π), odd, and degree-one. With source dx and target T_k#dx, both measures are fixed smooth positive coordinate measures. For even normalized q, Q and S=T_k⁻¹ are odd, so the stationary integrand is odd periodic and F(0)=0 exactly. Strict convexity and the global dual bridge certify T_k for every such q. The derivative expansion DF_k(1)[sin(2πℓx)]=(t/k)δ_kℓ+O(t²) is correct: the two derivative terms each contribute displacement times the perturbation, giving the factor2 before Fourier orthogonality. For each fixed finite m its determinant has nonzero leading term t^m/(m!), so sufficiently small positive t gives regular transcripts; this is not a uniform-in-m numerical certification.

It verifies stationarity, neighboring lifted costs, positive C'', mean arclength displacement and absence of cut crossings. The nonsymmetric shift is about0.0423160270. These quadrature calculations are finite consistency evidence, not the Borsuk–Ulam existence proof or an exact numerical collision for arbitrary prescribed pairs.
