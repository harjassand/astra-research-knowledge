S45 OPERATIONAL REALIZATION — actual one-port acquisition and its costs
2026-10-10. Status: FOUNDATIONAL TARGET OPEN / NOT ACHIEVED.

The actual scalar-input/noisy-scalar-output pipeline recovers a three-node
matrix accurately with ordinary full nonlinear system identification. The
cubic-information competitor gives no measured advantage. A weak-coupling
family proves that robust recovery over all generic SPD matrices necessarily
has an arbitrarily large acquisition cost. The new low-order high-opposite
formula avoids jets algebraically but its forward measurement suppresses
the relevant signal severely; weakly damped oscillator resonances do not
automatically remove that suppression. These are model calculations and
synthetic diagnostics, not externally validated physical measurements.

1. Target, interface and strongest feasible baseline

Target: recover a generic unknown dense real symmetric SPD K from
 xdot=-Kx-alpha*x^{componentwise 3}+e1*u, y=x1, x(0)=0,
known n and alpha=.3, quotienting hidden signed permutations. Topology,
hidden trajectories, eigenvectors and internal tensors are not supplied.

The implemented estimator receives only the recorded scalar controls,
1200 noisy scalar outputs, n, alpha, the sampling interval, and common
data-independent initial matrices. It minimizes output squared error using
the FULL nonlinear ODE, analytic parameter sensitivities, symmetric matrix
coordinates normalized for Frobenius norm, Levenberg-Marquardt damping,
SPD feasibility, and six (n=3) or eight (n=5) independent initial matrices.
The common broad numerical search box has eigenvalues in [.05,12]. It is
an implementation bound, not an acquired physical prior or global theorem.
Every start and failed iteration is retained in results_all.json.

The matched cubic-information competitor uses exactly the same observations,
starts, objective and optimizer, but evaluates the linear+cubic Volterra
approximation through xLdot=-K*xL+e1*u and
 xCdot=-K*xC-alpha*xL^3, yhat=xL1+xC1.
This is a structural H3-fitting route with only n(n+1)/2 unknowns; it is
stronger than fitting a free quartic tensor. It still neglects fifth and
higher orders. It is not claimed to implement every possible H3 estimator.
The linear response is used only as an evaluator diagnostic, never as the
comparison baseline. No all-order derivative algorithm is implemented.

Full nonlinear grey-box output/prediction-error estimation is a mature
baseline, including nonlinear ODE structures and parameter constraints:
https://www.mathworks.com/help/ident/grey-box-model-estimation.html
Regularized Volterra identification is likewise established:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10435
No novelty is asserted for the implemented fits.

3. An acquisition obstruction independent of the reconstruction algorithm

Take n>=3 and
 K_epsilon=[a,epsilon*b^T;epsilon*b,B], B>=beta*I,
 K_epsilon>=mu*I, c=||b||, |u(t)|<=U.
Let Q be a fixed hidden orthogonal transformation and
K'_epsilon=diag(1,Q)^T K_epsilon diag(1,Q). Both have exactly the same
linear one-port transfer. Generic choices are not hidden signed permutations,
remain O(1) apart in the hidden block, and have all nonzero first-column
entries with distinct squares. Port cyclicity can also be generic.

Set X=U/mu. Dissipativity and hidden contraction imply
 ||x||,||x'||<=X and ||x_h||,||Q*x'_h||<=epsilon*c*X/beta.
In common hidden coordinates the two cubic maps are G(z)=z^3 and
G_Q(z)=Q*(Q^T*z)^3; ||G(z)-G_Q(z)||<=2||z||^3.
Let D_y=sup|y-y'| and D_h=sup||x_h-Q*x'_h|| on any finite horizon.
Monotonicity of coordinatewise cubes yields
 D_y <= epsilon*c*D_h/a,
 D_h <= epsilon*c*D_y/beta + 2*alpha*epsilon^3*c^3*X^3/beta^4.
For rho=epsilon^2*c^2/(a*beta)<1,
 D_y <= [2*alpha*c^4*X^3/(a*beta^4*(1-rho))]*epsilon^4.
Fixing epsilon<=epsilon0 with rho0<1 makes the bracket uniformly bounded.
The bound holds for every time horizon and every bounded input, including
adaptively chosen controls along the same recorded history. Zero initial
states and independent additive scalar measurement noise are explicit.

For N independent Gaussian measurements, the KL chain rule gives
 KL(P_K,P_K') <= N*C^2*epsilon^8/(2*sigma^2).
Pinsker and two-point testing imply N=Omega(sigma^2*epsilon^-8/C^2) to
distinguish these O(1)-different hidden matrices with fixed success
probability. This proves there is no uniform robust finite-resource bound
depending only on n,alpha,U,sigma and a stability floor mu over generic
SPD matrices. It does not rule out conditioned subclasses or instance-
dependent acquisition, and it does not refute the exact structural theorem.
For continuous-time white observation noise dY=y*dt+sigma*dW, the same
argument charges total observation time T in place of independent N;
oversampling cannot be treated as free independent physical information.

The epsilon^4 exponent is generically attained. Under constant input, put
w=B^-1*b and let y0 solve a*y0+alpha*y0^3=u. The equilibrium port equation
contains alpha*epsilon^4*||w||_4^4*y^3. Hidden rotation replaces this by
alpha*epsilon^4*||Q^T*w||_4^4*y^3. Therefore the output difference at leading
order is
 -alpha*epsilon^4*(||w||_4^4-||Q^T*w||_4^4)*y0^3/(a+3*alpha*y0^2),
nonzero for generic Q,w. This is an acquisition bottleneck, not simply a
bad Vandermonde implementation.

The actual n=3/n=5 forward sweeps measured log slopes4.0044/4.0041 for
epsilon<=.2. Quotient K separation tends to27.1%/31.3%. For n=5,
epsilon=.1,Umax2.5, output RMS contrast is2.035e-8. At sigma1e-4,
about2.41e7 scalar samples have KL~.5 for this particular input ensemble.
At epsilon=.025, contrast7.93e-11 gives1.59e12 samples by the same
ensemble calculation. These numerical counts are not optimal-design lower
bounds; the preceding uniform proof supplies the algorithm-independent claim.

4. Low-order balanced high-opposite extraction: actual forward-data test

Following s41's exact formula, use q(z)=(zI+K)^-1*e1,
s=i*2,t=i,c=(s-t)/2,R=i*Omega, and
 F=-H3=sum_r alpha*q_r(s)*q_r(t)*q_r(R+c)*q_r(-R+c).
Subtract the port term P (r=1); then Omega^4*(F-P) approaches
 B=sum_{r>1}alpha*K_r1^2*q_r(s)*q_r(t).
Balanced frequencies remove the first inverse-frequency bias, leaving
O(Omega^-2). Algebraic matrix recovery after B acquisition is not the same
as acquiring B.

6. New moderate-frequency dual: charged physical noise forecast

Root's dual_frequency.py avoids the high-opposite asymptotic experiment by
constructing a target-functional dual from many moderate oscillator frequency
triples. Its small norm under supplied independent H3 noise is a different
mechanism and is NOT falsified by Section 4. I evaluated it separately in
dual_noise_forecast.py, using the same flat modal overlaps and separated
lambda in [1,4], known alpha=.3, and unknown physical hidden rotation.
All H1 modal data remain supplied here; neither this forecast nor root's
prototype acquires them.

The physical covariance includes eight sign patterns per frequency triple,
two amplitudes and fifth-order Richardson cancellation, independent scalar
noise sigma=1e-5, dt=.05, settling 12/gamma per trial, and measurement
20/gamma per trial. Four real cubic mixing frequencies are jointly regressed;
their exact finite-sample sine/cosine Gram matrix accounts for near-coincident
frequencies. Per-tone amplitudes are adjusted by the known modal resolvent
norm so the conservative linear-state proxy satisfies
 alpha*||q_linear||^2/(gamma*sqrt(lambda_min)) <= .05.
This is a perturbative protocol choice, not a certificate of all remaining
nonlinear bias. H3 extraction divides by the product of three input amplitudes;
resonance-safe amplitudes scale approximately as gamma^(3/2). Thus small
abstract H3-noise gain does not imply small scalar-observation noise gain.

I recomputed the optimal dual AFTER whitening this physical covariance.

two distinct estimator families. The original weighted-quartic prototype

physical test tensor with alpha=.3, but their information rights differ.
The constraints are checked to numerical precision and are never supplied
