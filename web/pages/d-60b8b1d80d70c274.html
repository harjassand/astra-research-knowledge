# A sufficient spectral representation, with the physical identification left explicit

2026-10-06. Independent reconstruction and audit of r03_quantum's channel-to-dispersion interface; no new physical gravity positivity claim.

Let μ>0 after specified low-energy/pole subtractions. Let Q_s(x),Q_u(x) be measurable finite-dimensional positive Hermitian input effects, with ∫_μ^∞ (||Q_s||+||Q_u||)dx/x³ finite. Let X be a specified real-linear map on Hermitian matrices, extended complex-linearly to all matrices. Define

    D(z)=D0+D1 z + z²/(2π)∫_μ^∞
         [Q_s(x)/(x²(x−z)) + X(Q_u(x))/(x²(x+z))] dx.  (1)

D0,D1 are Hermitian. The integral is locally uniformly absolutely convergent off the two cuts: on any compact set there, the two rational kernels are bounded by a compact-set constant times x^−3. Differentiation locally, or Morera with dominated integration, proves holomorphy. It is hermitian analytic. At Lebesgue points on the right cut, Im_H D(x+i0)=Q_s(x)/2; on the left, Im_H D(−x+i0)=−X(Q_u(x))/2. The same holds distributionally for measures. The coefficient is

    D2=(1/(2π))∫_μ^∞ [Q_s+XQ_u]dx/x³.               (2)

For X²=I and shared spectrum Q_s=Q_u, (1) has D(−z)=X(D(z)) when XD0=D0 and XD1=−D1. A general pair requires its actual crossing relation; it is not automatic from positive effects.

If detector transition effects satisfy 0≤Q_E,k≤Q_k, pair (2) with Hermitian R satisfying R≥0 and X*R≥0. Then

    Tr(RD2)≥(1/(2π))∫[Tr(R Q_E,s)+Tr(X*R Q_E,u)]dx/x³≥0. (3)

The factor 1/(2π), rather than 1/π, follows from the reference optical theorem 2 Im_H B=T†T=Q_total. The underlying algebra is V=C+iT, V†V=C†C=I, B=C†T. For a detector F, Q_E=T†FT≤T†T. Raw counts V†FV equal Q_E only if FC=0, or if the reference/interference terms are explicitly measured and removed.

For a common Q and common residual detector effect in both channels, the exact unrestricted test condition is R+X*R≥0. Requiring each summand PSD separately is sufficient but need not be maximal. Partial transpose crossing is not PSD preserving, so unrestricted entangled tests cannot be inferred from CP. r03's Bell-projector/singlet example indeed gives −1/2. Its Eq. (4)–(6) normalization and its shared/independent crossing distinctions independently pass this audit.

Equation (1) is a sufficient analytic representation by construction, not proof that a physical detector has it. To identify D with a physical phase-referenced B, one needs the same spectral effects, crossing prescription, subtraction constants, and a vanishing or controlled large contour. The difference between any two such holomorphic functions with equal cuts can include a real crossing-even z² polynomial if the contour/growth assumption is absent. Hence spectral positivity alone does not determine a second EFT coefficient. This is an analytic nonidentifiability statement, not an assertion that adding that polynomial preserves a complete unitary scattering channel.

Likewise B=C†T on the real axis does not define a holomorphic extension: the dagger cannot simply be evaluated at complex energy. The reduced channel erases environmental phases, while B depends on the reference. An operator-valued retarded-response resolvent can instead have a positive spectral measure and causal analyticity, but identifying its energy variable and coefficient with a Lorentz-invariant crossed scattering amplitude requires a separate physical theorem.
