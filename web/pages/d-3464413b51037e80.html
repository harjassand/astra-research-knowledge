## Question and exact interface

Let its shortest baseline path length be L>=2. Observations are passive endpoint states at exactly calibrated predictably selected positive lags, with no resets. Initial state and controller randomization are parameter independent. All reaching, return, pilot, confirmation and initial reads and their physical time are charged.

The validity statements cover this entire continuous generator class, arbitrary history-dependent lag choices, and arbitrary normalized predictable endpoint predictors. The matching cost theorem below uses a genuinely supplied bracket b in [beta,2beta], beta<=1/2, and tau<=t0, exactly the interface of cycle6/minimum_lag_joint_generator.md. An empirically guessed bracket must not replace that supplied bracket in the confidence class. The original full class may instead be retained and the observable certificate in the companion note used; its coverage is proved, while unknown-scale stopping cost is not inferred here.

Questions: (i) Is the likelihood-ratio confidence set valid under adaptive carried trajectories? (ii) How does saturated lag-by-row prediction affect width? (iii) Can a finite, inexpensive-to-evaluate certificate recover the rare-rate read and physical-time powers while also learning every ordinary rate?

Answer: exact predictable-ratio validity; possible accumulated lag regret for the all-level saturated predictor; a two-block add-one endpoint predictor and a deterministic empirical-log certificate recover the bare resource powers at fixed accuracy/confidence in the supplied-bracket near-identity interface. The split repair is standard universal inference, not a novelty claim. Practical constants remain unresolved.

## Full observation filtration and predictable retention

Let F_i contain every observed state, clock, previous controller action and independent random seed up to the i-th endpoint. Before endpoint i+1 is observed, the start state X_i, lag t_i and retention mask h_i in {0,1} are F_i-measurable. Under fixed Q,

    p_Q,i(y)=exp(t_i Q)[X_i,y].

Irreducibility of the positive baseline graph gives p_Q,i(y)>0 for every y and t_i>0, including b=0. Let q_i(y) be any F_i-measurable probability vector. Define

    Z_0(Q)=1,
    Z_(i+1)(Q)=Z_i(Q) [q_i(X_(i+1))/p_Q,i(X_(i+1))]^(h_i).

If h_i=0 the factor is1. If h_i=1, conditional expectation of the factor is sum_y q_i(y)=1. Therefore Z_i(Q) is a nonnegative mean-one martingale under the full observation filtration. Reaching and return reads can affect future masks and predictions; they are not unobserved or free. For the row-block protocol, retain a departure precisely when the current state is the designated row state and a new complete return cycle is starting. This choice is predictable. Retain no endpoint chosen because of its as-yet-unseen value.

Write M_i=product_(j<i,h_j=1) q_j(X_(j+1)), L_i(Q)=product_(j<i,h_j=1) p_Q,j(X_(j+1)). Then Z_i=M_i/L_i(Q). These are selected-factor products. They are not claimed to be the common likelihood ratio of every record when omitted reads have factor1. In particular, selection decisions may depend on omitted outcomes; the conditional martingale proof above, rather than a fictitious independent selected-data likelihood, establishes validity. An all-record predictor is separately valid by setting every h_i=1.

Ville's inequality gives, for each possible true Q,

    P_Q(exists i: L_i(Q)<delta M_i)<=delta.

Thus C_i={Q in ORIGINAL supplied class: L_i(Q)>=delta M_i}, or its running intersection, contains the true Q for all i with probability>=1-delta. No union over the continuous parameter set is needed: the coverage quantifier fixes the true Q and proves its martingale. Stopping and declared failure caps do not invalidate this statement. Adaptive clocks with known parameter-independent conditional randomization cancel; a parameter-dependent initial law or timing law would require its likelihood factor and is outside this interface.

Validity alone does not imply narrow projection onto b, finite stopping, good sample constants, or correctness of a nuisance optimizer.

## Exact saturated predictor regret and accumulated lag levels

For each cell c=(start state, admitted lag-grid index), use the Dirichlet(1,...,1) predictor. With endpoint counts N_cy and total N_c,

    M_c=(n-1)! product_y N_cy!/(N_c+n-1)!.

Its saturated conditional likelihood is Lsat_c=product_y(N_cy/N_c)^(N_cy), with0^0=1. The multinomial probability of these counts under their empirical probability vector is at most1, so

    Lsat_c/M_c <= binom(N_c+n-1,n-1),
    log(Lsat/M)<=R= sum_occupied c log binom(N_c+n-1,n-1).

This deterministic inequality is exact, including adaptively chosen cells. It does not require independent column counts. For n fixed and K visited lag levels, R=O(K log(N+n)). With K=O(log(1/b)) dyadic stages and polynomial total N, the simple bound can be O(log^2(1/b)). A single balanced final level gives O(log m). A universal regret upper bound is not a proof of an unavoidable lower penalty on every model; it nevertheless prevents claiming a bare Theta resource theorem from this saturated bound alone. The all-self-loop row sequence has exact regret log binom(m+n-1,n-1), showing a deterministic bound independent of m is impossible for this predictor. Treating every distinct continuous lag as a new cell can create K comparable to N and a linear penalty; validity still holds, but a useful width/resource theorem then needs a different predictor or retention scheme.

At a balanced final level with m retained endpoints per row,

    log(Lsat/L_Q)=m sum_x KL(Phat_x || P_Q(t)_x).

Any accepted Q obeys

    sum_x KL(Phat_x || P_Q(t)_x)<=B/m,
    B=log(Lsat/M)+log(1/delta).

For multiple levels this is instead the sum of N_xj KL terms. Nonnegative terms permit extracting one level with m=min_x N_xj, while using the full B. Simply discarding the numerator penalty incurred at earlier levels is invalid.

## Valid restarts and mixtures

For predictably selected stopping times T_j, start a fresh masked predictor-ratio martingale E^j at1 at T_j and hold it1 before T_j. Fixed weights w_j=1/[j(j+1)] give sum_j w_j E^j a nonnegative mean-one martingale. If E^j crosses1/(delta w_j), the mixture crosses1/delta. Equivalently use per-level error delta w_j and a union bound. The current level pays log(1/w_j)=O(log j), rather than stale levels' cumulative saturated regret. The current saturated predictor still pays O(log m).

If exactly one future confirmation process is started at a stopping time selected from previous observations, conditional Ville uses delta, with no level multiplicity penalty. This is valid because only future factors are used after the single chosen start. Trying many starts and then retaining a successful one requires weights/error allocation. Retrospectively choosing a numerator or retained sequence after seeing its future endpoints is invalid. Coverage remains over the original class when the chosen lag came from an uncertified bracket. These constructions do not by themselves prove that unknown-scale lag selection reaches a certifying regime with the desired expected cost.

## A two-block endpoint predictor removes the log m toll

Fix one admitted lag t and collect m_p complete return cycles at every row state, retaining their first endpoints. Let pilot counts be C_xy. Freeze the strictly positive rational predictor

    qhat_xy=(C_xy+1)/(m_p+n).

It need not be the semigroup of any generator. Its only coverage requirement is exact normalization and predictability. Set the confirmation ratio mask to0 throughout the pilot: training endpoints are stored for qhat but do not contribute likelihood factors to that confirmation process. After the entire pilot is completed, collect m_c fresh complete cycles at each row state, using qhat for its retained endpoints and factor1 for every other read. All data after the pilot start are carried by the same physical chain. The strong Markov property makes each retained row sample iid with its true row distribution, conditional on preceding block history; no fresh physical initialization is assumed.

Let p be any true n-point row probability vector. For C_y~Binomial(m_p,p_y),

    E[1/(C_y+1)]
      =[1-(1-p_y)^(m_p+1)]/[(m_p+1)p_y].

This identity follows by writing binom(m,c)/(c+1)=binom(m+1,c+1)/(m+1) and summing. Therefore

    E chi^2(p,qhat)
      =(m_p+n) sum_y p_y^2 E[1/(C_y+1)]-1
      <=(m_p+n)/(m_p+1)-1
      =(n-1)/(m_p+1),
    E KL(p||qhat)<=(n-1)/(m_p+1).

Zero probabilities are handled by continuity; in our CTMC interface all entries are positive. Importantly the bound is uniform in arbitrarily small endpoint probabilities.

For fresh empirical confirmation row Phat with m_c observations,

    E KL(Phat||p)<= E chi^2(Phat,p)=(n-1)/m_c.

The exact decomposition after taking confirmation expectation is

    E[ m_c KL(Phat||qhat) | pilot]
       =m_c KL(p||qhat)+m_c E KL(Phat||p).

Consequently, for D=log Lsat_confirmation-log M_confirmation,

    D=m_c sum_x KL(Phat_x||qhat_x)>=0,
    E D<=n(n-1)[1+m_c/(m_p+1)].          (SPLIT)

For m_p=m_c=m, E D<=2n(n-1), independent of m,b,t and ordinary rates. Markov gives D<=2n(n-1)/delta_w with probability>=1-delta_w. This is a width bound, not a coverage assumption: a bad pilot can make the certificate wide but never invalidates the predictor-ratio coverage. A generator fit from prior data could also be used as normalized predictor, but none is required for(SPLIT), and no optimizer accuracy is credited.

## Deterministic KL-to-generator outer certificate

Use the supplied-bracket constants lambda,t0,kappa,Cp,Cr,V0,M0 from cycle6/minimum_lag_joint_generator.md. In particular, t=max{tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))}<=t0, every candidate satisfies ||P-I||_infinity<=1/4, weighted log influence a_xy has centered row bound M0/t, and sum_x Var_Px(a_xY)<=V0 t^(L-2). These are uniform over all unknown ordinary numerical rates in the class. Put A0=2(n-1), G0=A0^2(4lambda+8/9).

Here is the needed deterministic replacement for Bernstein sampling. If r,s are probability distributions, k=KL(r||s), and a centered under s has |a|<=M and variance v, its Bernstein mgf obeys

    log E_s exp(lambda a)<=v lambda^2/[2(1-M|lambda|/3)].

The variational inequality lambda E_r a<=k+log E_s exp(lambda a), applied in either sign and optimized at lambda=sqrt(2k/v)/(1+(M/3)sqrt(2k/v)), gives

    |E_r a-E_s a|<=sqrt(2vk)+(M/3)k.    (TRANSPORT)

Zero k or v follows by a limit (v=0 makes a constant on the support). Summing rows and applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for any accepted candidate P and k=B/m,

    |[Dlog_P(Phat-P)]_uv/t|
       <=sqrt(2V0 t^(L-2) k)+(M0/(3t))k.

Apply(TRANSPORT) to off-diagonal indicators, whose variance<=lambda t and centered bound<=1. Each entry error is at most sqrt(2lambda t k)+k/3. Since the error has zero row sums,

    ||Phat-P||_infinity<=A0[sqrt(2lambda t k)+k/3].

If k<=t, squaring yields ||Phat-P||^2/t<=G0 k (our G0 is a safe enlargement). Precheck ||Phat-I||<=1/2 with rational arithmetic. When it passes, the straight segment joining every candidate P to Phat has ||matrix-I||<=1/2. The exact log Taylor remainder is at most2||Phat-P||^2/t. Population log P/t=Q exactly. Thus every accepted candidate b is in

    [braw-r(k),braw+r(k)] intersect the supplied b-class,
    braw=[log Phat]_uv/t,
    r(k)=sqrt(2V0 t^(L-2)k)
          +[M0/(3t)+2G0]k.              (OUTER)

This interval is an outer projection of the entire continuous confidence set, rather than a claim that its endpoints are feasible generators. It can be conservative. If a norm check fails, return the full supplied interval/UNKNOWN; do not report a narrow interval. A count-dependent interval for braw must include certified arithmetic error. No likelihood profile optimization is used.

## Bare-exponent confidence acquisition theorem

Fix epsilon in(0,1/4), delta in(0,1), and the genuinely supplied bracket. Use delta_c=delta_w=delta_cap=delta/3. Let

    Bstar is a rational upper enclosure of
          1+6n(n-1)/delta+log(3/delta),
    m >= Bstar [32 V0 t^(L-2)/(epsilon^2 beta^2)
               +(8M0/3+16G0+1)/(epsilon beta t)
               +16G0].                 (COUNT)

Round all sufficient counts upward through rational enclosures, not an undecidable exact ceiling of an arbitrary computable real. Use m pilot and m confirmation endpoints at each row. Compute an upper enclosure B of D+log(3/delta) with additive overestimate at most1/2. If B>Bstar, return full interval/UNKNOWN. Otherwise k=B/m<=t and k<=1/(16G0). On the simultaneous coverage event and D<=6n(n-1)/delta, the true candidate obeys ||Phat-P||^2<=G0 t k<=1/16. Hence the empirical norm precheck necessarily passes. There is no additional uncharged normal-domain statistical assumption.

(COUNT) yields r(k)<=epsilon beta/4+epsilon beta/8=3epsilon beta/8. Reserve epsilon beta/16 for the error in braw and epsilon beta/16 for the upward radius enclosure; the outer interval then has radius<=epsilon beta/2. If the outward interval has empty intersection with the supplied bracket, return the full original bracket/UNKNOWN. Otherwise return its midpoint; it has error<=epsilon beta/2 whenever true Q is covered. The unit of slack in Bstar guarantees that the certified upper B passes the rational threshold on the event D<=6n(n-1)/delta; a mathematical exact equality decision is not required. Coverage, small D and the resource cap together hold with probability>=1-delta. The interval itself remains a confidence outer set under optional stopping; the high-probability width statement is established at this scheduled two-block completion.

Let eta>0 be the supplied uniform stationary/time-one path floor from the previous theorem and k_hit=ceil(1/t). Each of the2n state blocks has initial hit cost<=k_hit/eta expected reads. Each of2nm completed return cycles has expected length<=1/eta by Kac. The initial read is charged. Therefore

    E reads <=H=1+[2n k_hit+2n m]/eta,
    E time <=tH,
    E jumps<=lambda tH.

Cap physical endpoint reads at an integer above3H/delta, declaring failure when reached; Markov gives cap failure<=delta/3. Every cap, norm or B-threshold failure returns the entire original supplied bracket when the output is called a confidence interval. The confidence-set coverage is unconditional; only the narrow-width guarantee spends the extra width/cap error budgets. No reset, ignored transition, return cycle or pilot is free. At fixed n, graph/envelopes, epsilon,delta,

    reads =O[t^(L-2)/beta^2+1/(beta t)],
    time  =O[t^(L-1)/beta^2+1/beta].

Substituting t=max(tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))) gives

    reads =O[beta^(-L/(L-1))+beta^-2 tau^(L-2)],
    time  =O[beta^-1+beta^-2 tau^(L-1)].

The known-baseline subfamily lower bound already supplied in the programme applies to this enlarged unknown-nuisance class, so these are matching powers at nontrivial fixed accuracy/confidence. This construction proves existence within the stated local interface. It does not establish that conservative constants are affordable or that the frozen practical benchmark meets these counts. It does not prove an unknown-scale acquisition theorem simply by substituting a guessed beta. A stage-weighted mixture plus a separate scale/tail argument is needed for that extension.

## Effective computation, status and prior art

The pilot qhat and empirical Phat are rational; qhat is exactly normalized. Frozen-confirmation M is a product of rational powers. For fixed n, D is a sum of O(n^2) count-weighted logarithms, not an optimization problem. Logarithms of rational integers can be enclosed using dyadic normalization and the convergent atanh series; per-log absolute precision O(1/m) suffices for a constant enclosure budget in D. This uses O(log m) precision bits plus requested accuracy bits, with polynomial-time rational interval arithmetic. Exact huge products are optional and require O(m log m) bits, so one must not claim they have constant bit cost.

Check the empirical matrix-log domain rationally. The log-series truncation degree is O(log[1/(epsilon beta t)]), with n^3 operations per degree and polynomial intermediate bit lengths as in the previous proof. Certified upward B and radius enclosures, and certified lag division, are essential. A floating-point interval labelled certified is not enough. Arbitrarily noncomputable calibrated lag/envelope input is outside the effective algorithm, even though its statistical law can be defined.

For a supplied beta that is a general computable real, exact intersection/emptiness at beta or2beta need not be decidable. The effective variant intersects the outward rational score interval with a finite outward rational enclosure J of[beta,2beta], tests emptiness rationally, and returns that rational intersection's midpoint. Its diameter cannot exceed the score interval's diameter, and true b remains included. Every failure returns J/UNKNOWN. Alternatively beta may be required rational for the finite-bit interface. This arithmetic refinement, independently added in r11's gate, leaves the statistical real-beta theorem unchanged and avoids an endpoint-equality oracle.
