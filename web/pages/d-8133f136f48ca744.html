# Finite local stability, an unavoidable anisotropy gap, and the global acquisition boundary

## Question and legal interface

An exact hidden quartic has the form
\[
 f(x)=h_Q(Lx),\qquad h_Q(z)=(z_1^2,\ldots,z_n^2)^TQ(z_1^2,\ldots,z_n^2),
 \quad Q\succ0,\quad L\in GL_n.
\]
The original acquisition interface gives only the expanded rational coefficients of f. Cycle10's internally gated universal exact fallback had maximum Macaulay degree8218. Cycle11's independently gated explicit selector certificate improves this to f^13 J contained in the polar ideal and maximum degree58 (`notes/root_cycle11/EXPLICIT_POLAR_CERTIFICATE_GATE.md`). Full degree58 homogeneous spaces remain impractical in ordinary dimensions, and this exact theorem does not give a noise guarantee. Here the separate interface supplies an **exact nominal candidate** (L0,Q0), a certified coefficient error bound, and a **certified local matching branch**. Construction, fitting, matching and certification of that candidate/branch are charged unresolved steps, except when another procedure actually supplies them. The analytic theorem also applies to real parameters; it is not an algorithm for acquiring irrational frames from noisy coefficients.

The useful question is whether finite coefficient errors control the axes in a specified branch, and which quantity must deteriorate near continuous symmetry. A second question, treated in `ROBUST_ENERGY_AND_PUBLISHED_INTERFACES.md`, is whether useful energy bounds can survive when the axes are ambiguous.

## Norm and gauge

For a degree-d homogeneous polynomial p=sum_alpha c_alpha z^alpha define its Bombieri norm by
\[
 \|p\|_B^2=\sum_{|\alpha|=d}\frac{c_\alpha^2}{\binom d\alpha}.
\]
It equals the Frobenius norm of the unique symmetric d-tensor T with p(z)=T[z,...,z]. The tensor Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives
\[
 |p(z)|\le\|p\|_B\|z\|_2^d,\qquad
 \|p(V\,\cdot)\|_B\le\|V\|_{op}^d\|p\|_B.
\]
Insertion of possibly different matrices in tensor slots has norm at most the product of their operator norms times ||T||F. This follows by vectorizing the tensor and using the operator norm of a Kronecker product, so the constants below do not hide a dimension factor.

Work in z=L0 x, with V0=L0^{-1}. A nearby matched frame is written (I+A)L0 with diag A=0. This fixes the continuous row-scaling gauge. Indeed, for any relative frame R with nonzero diagonal, set D0=diag(Rii), R=D0(I+A); replacing Q by D0^2 Q D0^2 preserves the polynomial and fixes diag A=0. Signs and permutations must first be matched to the candidate. Nothing in the local theorem finds that matching.

Choose rational q,B>0 with Q0>=qI and B>=||Q0||F. These can be certified with rational LDL and an upper bound on the squared Frobenius norm. Write Q=Q0+BE, E=E^T, and use the parameter norm
\[
 p=\|(A,E)\|=\sqrt{\|A\|_F^2+\|E\|_F^2}.
\]
Thus E is a dimensionless, explicitly normalized matrix perturbation. Define
\[
 F(A,E)(z)=h_{Q0+BE}((I+A)z),\quad
 \gamma=\min(2q,B/2),\quad r=\gamma/(92B).
\]
In particular r<=1/184. The parameter ball of radius r is convex, I+A is invertible there, and Q0+BE is positive definite: its smallest eigenvalue is at least q-Br>=45q/46.

## Tangent gap, allowing all unknown biquadratic coefficients to change

At (A,E)=(0,0), the derivative is
\[
 J_0[U,V]=B h_V(z)
 +4\sum_i z_i\Big(\sum_k q_{ik}z_k^2\Big)\Big(\sum_{j\ne i}U_{ij}z_j\Big).
\]
The hV part has only even monomials. Every frame term has exactly two coordinates of odd exponent, so the two ranges are orthogonal in the Bombieri inner product. For each i<j its two principal odd coefficients are
\[
 [z_i^3z_j]J_0=4(q_{ii}U_{ij}+q_{ij}U_{ji}),\quad
 [z_iz_j^3]J_0=4(q_{ij}U_{ij}+q_{jj}U_{ji}).
\]
Those two monomials have multinomial coefficient4. Their squared Bombieri contribution is therefore
\[
 4\left\|Q_{\{i,j\},\{i,j\}}
          (U_{ij},U_{ji})^T\right\|_2^2
 \ge4q^2(U_{ij}^2+U_{ji}^2).
\]
All these selected monomials are distinct. Additional z_i z_j z_k^2 terms only add nonnegative squared norm. Summing proves the **odd tangent gap 2q**. Meanwhile
\[
 \|h_V\|_B^2=\sum_iV_{ii}^2+\frac23\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}^2
 \ge\frac13\|V\|_F^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \|J_0[U,V]\|_B^2\ge4q^2\|U\|_F^2+\frac{B^2}{3}\|V\|_F^2
 \ge\gamma^2(\|U\|_F^2+\|V\|_F^2).
\]
The rational B/2 replaces the slightly larger B/sqrt3 only to simplify the certificate. In ordinary unweighted coefficient norm the selected odd gap is4q, but coordinate changes in that norm carry additional norm-conversion costs.

## A finite derivative bound and a finite inverse certificate

Let a=||A||F, b=||E||F, u=||U||F, v=||V||F, and suppose p<=1. Tensor expansion in the four slots gives
\[
 \|(D_AF(A,E)-D_AF(0,0))[U]\|_B
 \le4B\{(1+a)^3-1+b(1+a)^3\}\,u
 \le B(28a+32b)u.
\]
Here ||hQ0||B<=||Q0||F<=B, ||hE||B<=b, ||I+A||op<=1+a, and a<=1. For the coefficient-direction derivative,
\[
 \|(D_EF(A,E)-D_EF(0,0))[V]\|_B
 \le B\{(1+a)^4-1\}v\le15Bav.
\]
Combining these two estimates uses the **joint** parameter norm, not two unrelated unit bounds:
\[
 \|(DF(A,E)-J_0)[U,V]\|_B
 \le B\sqrt{(28a+32b)^2+(15a)^2}\sqrt{u^2+v^2}
 \le\sqrt{2033}Bp\sqrt{u^2+v^2}<46Bp\sqrt{u^2+v^2}.
\]
The last inequality uses the Frobenius norm of the 2x2 matrix [[28,32],[15,0]]. Explicitly, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its squared output by (28^2+32^2+15^2)(a^2+b^2). No spectral assertion or optimized constant is assumed.

For any theta,theta' in the radius-r ball, integrate DF along the segment between them. The segment stays in the ball, and the last bound is at most gamma/2. Subtract the constant derivative J0 and use the reverse triangle inequality to obtain
\[
 \boxed{\|F(\theta)-F(\theta')\|_B\ge
        \frac\gamma2\|\theta-\theta'\|.}
\]
This proves injectivity in this matched gauge and an inverse Lipschitz bound2/gamma **on the image of this ball**. It neither says that a general noisy quartic lies in that image nor supplies a nearest-point fitting algorithm. No surjectivity onto the ambient coefficient space is claimed.

A sharper centered remainder bound is also available, but not needed for the pairwise theorem. The Q0 part beyond its affine term is bounded by B(6a^2+4a^3+a^4)<=11Ba^2; the BE part beyond BhE is bounded by Bb(4a+6a^2+4a^3+a^4)<=15Bab. Since ab<=(a^2+b^2)/2,
\[
 \|F(\theta)-F(0)-J_0\theta\|_B\le(37/2)Bp^2.
\]

## What a noisy candidate certificate actually says

Suppose ftrue is a promised hidden quartic, ||fhat-ftrue||B<=delta in the input coordinates, and f0=hQ0(L0 x) is an exact rational candidate with ||fhat-f0||B<=rho. Suppose, **as a separate certified fact**, that ftrue corresponds in the matched, normalized gauge above to a point theta in the radius-r ball. Then
\[
 e=\|V0\|_{op}^4(\delta+\rho),\qquad
 p\le2e/\gamma.
\]
Operator-norm upper bounds can replace the exact norm in a runnable rational certificate. The inequality is a posteriori and conditional on the branch certificate. It does not infer that certificate from a small residual alone.

For a<=p<1, the actual inverse frame is V1=V0(I+A)^{-1}. Its ith axis is the line through V1 e_i. Since ||(I+A)^{-1}-I||op<=a/(1-a), the projective sine distance from the matched candidate axis is at most
\[
 \kappa(V0)\,\frac{a(1+a)}{1-a}.
\]
Indeed the numerator of the sine bound is at most ||V0||op a/(1-a), and ||V1 e_i||>=sigma_min(V0)/(1+a). For the coordinate reflection Si=V Di V^{-1}, Di=I-2e_i e_i^T, one similarly has
\[
 \|S_{i,1}-S_{i,0}\|_{op}\le2\kappa(V0)\,a/(1-a),
\]
by (I+A)^{-1}Di(I+A)-Di=(I+A)^{-1}(DiA-ADi).
The chosen-gauge metric G=L^T L obeys ||G1-G0||op<=||L0||op^2(2a+a^2). This metric is gauge-dependent; many positive metrics make the same coordinate reflections self-adjoint. The statement is not recovery of a uniquely distinguished physical metric.

## Why local conditioning does not acquire a global branch

For a compact admissible model class one can define a global separation cost. For example, normalize each row of L to unit Euclidean length, require kappa(L)<=K and qI<=Q<=BI, and use unordered inverse-frame axis distance
\[
 d_{ax}(V,W)=\min_\pi\max_i\|P_{Ve_i}-P_{We_{\pi(i)}}\|_{op}.
\]
For fixed f in this class and radius eta>0 define
\[
 \Delta_f(\eta)=\inf\{\|f-f'\|_B:f'\text{ in that class},\ d_{ax}(f,f')\ge\eta\}.
\]
Compactness and the exact QPD uniqueness theorem imply Delta_f(eta)>0 if the separated set is nonempty. This is only an existence statement. No dimension-uniform lower bound or efficient computation of Delta_f is proved here. A small fitted residual does not itself locate the correct branch without such a global exclusion certificate. The candidate-fitting cost, diagonal normalization, discrete matching and coordinate conditioning are part of the noisy acquisition problem, not free preprocessing.

## A universal obstruction: coercivity and a perfectly conditioned frame do not suffice

For any n>=2 and rational epsilon>0 let
\[
 Q_\epsilon=J+\epsilon I,\quad
 f_{\epsilon,I}(x)=\|x\|_2^4+\epsilon\sum_i x_i^4,\quad
 f_{\epsilon,R}(x)=\|x\|_2^4+\epsilon\sum_i(Rx)_i^4,
\]
where R is rational orthogonal, identity outside coordinates1,2 and with block [[3/5,-4/5],[4/5,3/5]]. Both are legal rational QPD models, their frames have condition number1, and f(x)>=||x||^4 uniformly. Their coordinate-reflection axis sets have distance3/5: the best signed permutation exchanges the rotated first two axes, giving acute sine3/5. All other axes agree. Their coefficient distance is epsilon C with an exact fixed positive C independent of n. The executable checker computes C^2 from the rational quartic coefficients.

Explicit expansion gives C^2=1152/625, hence C=24sqrt2/25. The midpoint observation belongs to a noise ball of radius epsilon C/2 around both models. The bottleneck projective metric satisfies the triangle inequality by composing matchings. Therefore any estimator of the unordered axis set must have error at least3/10 for at least one model, however small the absolute noise becomes as epsilon goes to0. This statement includes estimators given the shared metricI: even a free metric does not remove the anisotropy obstruction.

The local gap also has the necessary order: take a skew perturbation U12=t,U21=-t and all other entries0. For Qepsilon, every additional odd coefficient z1 z2 zk^2 vanishes and the two principal coefficients are4epsilon t and-4epsilon t. Thus ||J0[U,0]||B/||U||F=2epsilon. No inverse bound uniform as epsilon goes to0 is possible. The scale1/lambda_min(Q) in the local theorem is necessary on this family, up to constants, whereas the finite certified radius here is a conservative sufficient radius, not proved optimal.
