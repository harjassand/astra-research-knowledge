# Exact covariance rank gap in a finite passive-network family

Worker r09_algebra, 2026-10-06. Status: **universal finite-family algebraic construction and exact nonradial resistor-network realization.** Novelty is unverified. This is neither a counterexample to a whole-response continuum theorem nor a PDE conductivity realization.

## Interface

For a finite family of real symmetric positive operators F_a on R^d, d≥2, include **all** distinct pair secants

T_ab=(F_a−F_b)/||F_a−F_b||_F.

Define

V_1=max_{||g||₂=1} min_{a≠b} ||T_ab g||₂²,

V_PSD=max_{C≥0,tr C=1} min_{a≠b} tr(T_ab² C).

The operator response is retained in full d output coordinates, with independent equal-variance Gaussian detector noise if a statistical interpretation is wanted. Input budget is total squared Euclidean current norm. Secant normalization is Frobenius norm of the observable operator, **not** conductance-parameter distance. The family, physical graph and detector basis are known. Unknown a selects one member. These choices are part of the theorem.

## Main theorem

Let V contain the d² unit directions

e_i, and (e_i+e_j)/√2, (e_i−e_j)/√2 for 1≤i<j≤d.

Put P_v=vvᵀ and take F_0=bI and F_v=bI+τP_v with b,τ>0. There are d²+1 models, and every operator is SPD. Let

D_d=Σ_{i=0}^{d−1}(1+√2 i)²
    =d+√2 d(d−1)+d(d−1)(2d−1)/3.

Then, for the **whole pair-secant set**,

V_1=1/D_d,  V_PSD=1/d,

V_PSD/V_1=D_d/d
 =1+√2(d−1)+(d−1)(2d−1)/3~(2/3)d².

The unique optimal covariance is C=I/d, so rank d is required to reach the relaxed optimum. The same exact gap holds without secant normalization: replace both values by τ² times the displayed values. All nonzero pair operator distances lie between τ and √2τ in Frobenius norm.

## Proof of the PSD value and its unique optimizer

Every normalized secant has tr(T²)=1, so C=I/d gives value 1/d for every secant. Base-to-coordinate secants are P_{e_i}; for arbitrary trace-one PSD C their values are C_ii. Thus the minimum is at most min_i C_ii≤1/d. This proves optimality.

At any optimal covariance all C_ii≥1/d, and trace one forces equality. The base-to-root secants require

(C_ii+C_jj±2C_ij)/2≥1/d.

Both signs occur, so C_ij=0. Hence the optimizer is uniquely I/d. This conclusion is stronger than merely demonstrating a rank-one gap.

## Rank-one upper bound

For any fixed unit current g, let t be its minimum sensitivity on the base-to-direction secants. Coordinate constraints give |g_i|≥√t. Both signs of every two-coordinate root occur, and

min{(g_i+g_j)²/2,(g_i−g_j)²/2}
   =(|g_i|−|g_j|)²/2.

Consequently the sorted magnitudes a_1≤…≤a_d satisfy a_1≥√t and a_{i+1}−a_i≥√(2t). Therefore

1=Σ_i a_i²≥t Σ_{i=0}^{d−1}(1+√2 i)²=tD_d.

Additional pair secants can only lower the minimum, so V_1≤1/D_d. This uses every current, not a finite random search.

## Rank-one lower bound, including all nonbase pairs

Choose g_i=(1+√2(i−1))/√D_d, all positive. The coordinate and root sensitivities are at least 1/D_d, with equality at e_1 and every adjacent difference root.

For distinct direction projectors P_v,P_w, put ρ=vᵀw. Since the directions are never parallel, |ρ|<1. Direct multiplication gives

(P_v−P_w)²=(1−ρ²)P_span(v,w),
||P_v−P_w||_F²=2(1−ρ²),

so T_vw²=P_span(v,w)/2. It remains to show ||P_span(v,w)g||²≥2/D_d for every pair. There are only the following exhaustive support cases:

1. Two coordinate directions, or a coordinate and a root involving that coordinate: their plane is a coordinate two-plane. Each involved g_i²≥1/D_d, so its projection energy is at least 2/D_d.

2. A coordinate direction and a root with disjoint support: they are orthogonal. Each direction's squared inner product with g is at least 1/D_d, giving at least 2/D_d total.

3. Two roots with disjoint supports: they are orthogonal and the same argument applies.

4. Two roots with the same support and opposite signs: their plane is a coordinate two-plane, already handled.

5. Two roots with exactly one shared coordinate: their plane lies in a coordinate three-space and has normal with three entries each ±1, up to scale. The squared projection of the corresponding three positive g entries is one third of a sum of three signed pair squares. If the normal signs are all the same, the three pair squares are differences. Because the three arithmetic magnitudes are distinct, two index gaps are at least one and the remaining gap is at least two. Their squared differences sum to at least (2+2+8)/D_d=12/D_d, so projection energy is at least 4/D_d. If the normal signs are mixed, two pair squares are sums, each at least 4/D_d, and the remaining difference square is at least 2/D_d. Thus projection energy is at least 10/(3D_d). Either lower bound exceeds 2/D_d.

This proves every normalized nonbase secant gives at least 1/D_d, completing equality. Since |ρ|≤1/√2 for distinct roots/coordinates, every nonbase secant has unnormalized Frobenius square at least τ². The same g therefore achieves at least τ²/D_d for the unnormalized pair differences, while base secants give the matching upper bound. C=I/d similarly achieves τ²/d, with the coordinate pairs giving its upper bound.

## Exact passive resistor realization

Use n=d+1 labeled boundary vertices, with the complete graph and no hidden interior nodes. Let H=1^⊥⊂R^n and let U:R^d→H be any known isometry. This ensures the injected physical current Ug has zero total current and ||Ug||₂=||g||₂. Voltages use the zero-mean gauge and are detected in the orthonormal coordinates Uᵀ.

At baseline, every edge conductance is one. Its graph Laplacian is Λ_0=nP_H, so the Neumann-to-Dirichlet map on H is n⁻¹I. Set b=1/n, choose

τ=1/(2n²),  κ=τn²/(1+τn)<1/2.

For each direction v let u=Uv, and define physical edge conductances

c_ij(v)=1+κu_i u_j  (i<j).

They are strictly positive: u is a unit vector, hence |u_i u_j|≤1/2 and c_ij>3/4. The Laplacian has off-diagonal entries −c_ij and row sums zero. Because Σ_i u_i=0, its diagonal entries are n−1−κu_i², and therefore

Λ_v=nP_H−κuuᵀ.

On H the inverse is, by the rank-one inverse identity,

Λ_v⁻¹=n⁻¹I_H+[κ/(n(n−κ))]uuᵀ
       =n⁻¹I_H+τuuᵀ.

Taking UᵀΛ_v⁻¹U yields exactly F_v=n⁻¹I_d+τvvᵀ. Thus the algebraic family is an exact current-to-voltage family of passive, nonradial finite networks, with d(d+1)/2 edges and uniformly positive conductances. The baseline is permutation-symmetric but the unknown alternatives break that symmetry; the proof uses no radial decomposition. The graph is generally nonplanar, all vertices are exposed boundary vertices, and this construction does not establish a circular-planar or interior-conductivity version.

This realization uses the ordinary Laplacian characterization verified in Curtis–Morrow's primary author-hosted book draft, printed page 4: a symmetric zero-row-sum matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries is realized by assigning edge conductances equal to the negatives of those entries. The realization above is independently derived and does not require their more difficult inverse theorems.

## Acquisition, noise and costs

For covariance C=I/d, an exact factorization is d currents g_j=e_j/√d. Their total squared input norm is one. Reading all d voltage coordinates for each current uses d² scalar samples. One current of norm one uses d samples. The gap compares equal **input energy**, but not equal scalar acquisition. The unique full-rank optimum shows d currents are necessary for this covariance objective. Sensor construction, source switching and noise covariance are explicit experimental resources.

With independent N(0,σ²) noise in each orthonormal voltage coordinate, pairwise Gaussian KL equals Σ_j||(F_a−F_b)g_j||²/(2σ²). The best minimum squared mean separation is τ²/D_d for one current and τ²/d for the full covariance design. Thus the same ratio applies to this KL sensitivity. Since the physical choice has τ=Θ(d⁻²), the absolute separations decrease with d; the dimension-growing ratio is not a dimension-free recovery guarantee.

The covariance experiment uses a fixed designed collection; it is not an average-over-random-currents guarantee for one realized input. The companion `matched_scalar_physical_gap.md` supplies a one-detector-per-current experiment on this same physical family with d retained scalars, matching the single arm's scalar count and beating even jointly optimized arbitrary repeated unit single-current detectors by a guaranteed factor growing linearly with d. The full covariance experiment here has its larger d² scalar cost. The collaborator's compact-block model supplies another abstract matched-count comparison, audited below.

## When rank-one reaches the covariance optimum

Write A_i=T_iᵀT_i≥0. The finite covariance SDP has dual

V_PSD=min_{p_i≥0,Σp_i=1} λ_max(Σ_i p_iA_i).

This follows by optimizing linear functionals over trace-one PSD matrices and finite minimax duality. Rank-one reaches V_PSD iff there exist a unit g and dual-optimal p such that A_i has gᵀA_i g≥V_PSD for all i, equality whenever p_i>0, and g lies in the top eigenspace of Σ_i p_iA_i. Necessity is complementary slackness; sufficiency follows by summing these inequalities and the top-eigenvalue identity. This is an exact criterion, not a heuristic rounding rule.

Two useful sufficient cases:

- If all A_i commute, they have a common orthonormal eigenbasis. For any covariance C, choose g whose coordinate squares equal the diagonal entries of C in that basis. Then gᵀA_i g=tr(A_i C) for every i. Hence the relaxation has no gap. Commutativity of T_i is sufficient but stronger than needed.
- If a dual-optimal weighted matrix has a simple largest eigenvalue, complementary slackness forces every optimal covariance onto that one-dimensional eigenspace, so an optimum is rank one. Degenerate dual top eigenspaces can support strict gaps, as in the construction.

For any **fixed finite** set of nonzero T_i, the rank-one value is strictly positive: their proper kernels are finitely many proper linear subspaces, whose union does not cover the unit sphere. A current outside the union has a positive minimum over finitely many constraints. Thus a literal zero gap numerator/denominator obstruction requires an infinite family or a restricted detector map. Finite families can nevertheless have unbounded relative gap; the two-dimensional equally spaced-direction construction being developed by r06 is another example.

## Novelty, use and next falsifiable step

The directions are the familiar normalized coordinate and signed two-coordinate roots; SDP duality, projector algebra and passive network realization are classical ingredients. In particular, the rank-one versus trace-constrained PSD max-min formulation is explicit in Sidiropoulos–Davidson–Luo, *Transmit Beamforming for Physical-Layer Multicasting*, IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing 54(6), June 2006, sections III–V, printed pages 2241–2243. Their section V interprets the relaxed covariance as attaining a common-information objective and explains that restricting to rank-one beamforming can lose performance. The author-hosted published PDF is preserved with verified first-page date and metadata in `sources/r09_algebra/cycle2/`. Thus the general covariance mechanism and rank relaxation are old; the exact dimension-growing all-secant gap formula, passive inverse-family implementation and matched-detector bounds are the deductions here. No exhaustive prior-art search establishes those deductions as new, and no scientific priority is claimed.

Concrete use: this is an adversarial benchmark for proposed one-current reductions and rank-one SDP rounding. It fixes the whole finite secant set, positive physical conductances, normalization, noise and costs, with an exact optimality certificate. Any universal constant-factor deterministic rank-one rounding guarantee for this operator family is false.

Next falsifiable steps: establish sharp rank-r performance for 1<r<d; sharpen the companion's jointly optimized single-current detector upper bound to an exact optimum; and determine whether a comparable gap can be realized on a sparse circular-planar network or a genuine continuum conductivity family. The last two physical restrictions are unresolved, rather than silently inherited from this complete-graph example.
