Audit date: 2026-10-08. This is a targeted primary-literature check, not a systematic review or priority proof.

## Bedford et al. (2026), v1 bioRxiv primary preprint

Guy C. Bedford et al., “Induction of hysteretic chromatin states by CRISPRa reveals locus-specific encoding of transcriptional memory,” bioRxiv, posted 2026-09-30, v1, DOI [10.64898/2026.09.29.755468](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.29.755468). The source PDF explicitly states it is a preprint not certified by peer review. [Original v1 record](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.29.755468v1) · [PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.29.755468v1.full.pdf) · [Supplement](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/09/30/2026.09.29.755468/DC1/embed/media-1.pdf?download=true).

Downloaded for inspection to `/tmp/c7sources/bedford.pdf` and `/tmp/c7sources/bedford_supp.pdf`; SHA-256: `14b36bc3d58cd988276e3ec2a5a92b48b090acb59900ef3fecd48a5c1826be3b` for the main PDF and `fc0a2d202e13feb334d46cf63eaff92ab9e3d5d61b8b20fc1198950a2b674735` for the supplement. These scratch files are not part of the workspace deliverable. Relevant source locators:

- Main text pp. 4–5 (printed lines 123–171; Results/Fig. 1): trained MSCs receive 24 h IFNγ, then ten-day rest, with 2.3±0.1 cumulative population doublings; after a 10 h challenge, source-reported IDO1-positive fractions are 70.2±0.9% for the *Stimulated* arm and 98.5±0.1% for *Rechallenged*, and positive-subset MFI is 4,834±64 vs 7,961±126. The matched protocol description makes clear that the single *Stimulated* arm receives IFNγ for 10 h, whereas the trained arm's initial priming is 24 h.
- Main text p. 20 (printed lines 701–719; Methods): the exact schedule is 50 ng/ml IFNγ for 24 h, wash, 10-day rest; the matched untrained lineage receives one 10 h stimulus before harvest; the trained lineage is rechallenged for 10 h. This passage is why 70.2% is not the responder fraction at the start of the trained arm's rest.
- Main text p. 21 (printed lines 730–738; Methods): population doublings derive from serial cell counts/trypan-blue exclusion every 72 h across three passages. This is population net yield, not per-class survival or clone-specific growth.
- Supplement Fig. S1A–C: cumulative divisions; IDO1-positive gate/MFI across experimental conditions; and IDO1 transcript time course. The time-course readout is transcript, not a matched protein responder fraction at 24 h priming/washout.
- Main text around p. 9 and Supplement Fig. S6B: IDO1-targeting CRISPRa viability is 82.8±3.2% of nontargeting control at 72 h; CXCL11-targeting CRISPRa viability is 100.4±4.3% of control. These group means raise a differential-survival concern for the IDO1-specific intervention but do not establish which lineages are lost.
- Main text p. 4 and Supplement Fig. S4: 1,030 genes differ in rested versus naïve cells (548 up, 482 down), so IFNγ priming has a broad persistent transcriptional footprint in addition to the two target loci.

## Direct primary prior art on IFNγ response frequency vs per-cell response

1. Wojciech Siwek et al., “Activation of Clustered IFNγ Target Genes Drives Cohesin-Controlled Transcriptional Memory,” *Molecular Cell* 80(3), 396–409.e6 (2020), DOI [10.1016/j.molcel.2020.10.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.10.005); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7657446/). Primary figures/results: lines 196–229 of the publisher-hosted PMC record compare increased reinduction frequency with increased per-cell output; the authors report no IFNγ selective proliferation/lethality, and conclude that a rise in the fraction of GBP5-expressing cells accounts for most bulk memory. They use single-cell RNA-seq, an endogenous EGFP::GBP5 reporter, cytometry, sorting of first-pulse responders/nonresponders, and rechallenge. This preempts a generic proposal to use single-cell response frequency as a new memory mechanism, though it does not validate the separate MSC/IDO1 protocol.

2. Pawel Mikulski et al., “Heritable maintenance of chromatin modifications confers transcriptional memory of interferon-γ signaling,” *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology* 32, 1255–1267 (2025), DOI [10.1038/s41594-025-01522-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-025-01522-8); [article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41594-025-01522-8). Primary Fig. 2 and methods (publisher lines 112–130) report a GBP1-GFP reporter, live-cell reporter imaging during priming and six-day-later reinduction, ~30% first-pulse positive and >90% reinduction positive, sorting first-pulse GFP+ and GFP− populations, and chromatin profiling. This is close measurement prior art for population-frequency change with chromatin readouts. The source does not provide a full pedigree-level accounting through every birth/death event, so it is not treated as proof that all forms of selection are excluded in every cell system.

## Direct primary prior art on epigenetic memory and clonal multi-omics

3. Surya Nagaraja et al., “Epigenetic memory of colitis promotes tumour growth,” *Nature* 652, 774–783 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10258-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10258-4); [article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10258-4). The primary abstract and results (publisher lines 52–55, 127–147) report >100-day chromatin/accessibility memory, SHARE-TRACE joint single-cell RNA, accessibility and clonal history, clonal heritability/heterogeneity, and tumor consequences. This preempts a generic “epigenetic memory plus lineage-linked multiome” advance. SHARE-TRACE is still endpoint lineage/state profiling, not uninterrupted same-cell trajectories, so hidden within-barcode substate selection remains a distinct inferential limit.

4. Kunal Jindal et al., “Single-cell lineage capture across genomic modalities with CellTag-multi reveals fate-specific gene regulatory changes,” *Nature Biotechnology* 42, 946–959 (2024), DOI [10.1038/s41587-023-01931-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01931-4); [article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01931-4). Primary methods/abstract show heritable barcode capture with scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq and application to reprogramming. This is prior art for the proposed barcode-plus-epigenome endpoint layer.

## Novelty boundary and source coverage

The source search directly covered the Bedford v1 primary preprint and supplement, primary IFNγ memory studies in 2020 and 2025, a 2026 primary stem-cell clonal multiome study, and a 2024 primary CellTag multiome study. It was not exhaustive across all epigenetic-memory or lineage-tracing literature. The report therefore rejects a generic novelty claim and retains the source-specific, unmeasured rest-start responder fraction as a concrete experimental gap; it does not assert priority for the proposed assay design.
