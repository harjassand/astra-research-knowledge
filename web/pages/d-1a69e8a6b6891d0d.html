# Exact invariant metric by rational Cesaro projection

7 October 2026. Root independent reconstruction of the lemma proposed by `metric_acquisition`. **Mathematical gate PASS.** This is an ingredient, not a proof that a complete set of polynomial symmetries can always be acquired. Historical novelty is not asserted: averaging representations and spectral projections are established methods. The relevant new use in this programme is removal of a supplied-metric dependency once sufficient verified rational reflections are recovered.

## General statement

Input: finitely many rational invertible matrices S_1,...,S_s of dimension n. Suppose they preserve some common positive definite real form. Then a rational positive definite common invariant form can be found by polynomial-bit rational linear algebra in the input size. The algorithm need not receive an invariant form, group enumeration, convergence rate or condition number.

More strongly, the same algorithm decides the existence of a common positive definite invariant form: construct the projection below if its required direct sum exists, and check its output for positive definiteness and all invariance equations. Reject if any step fails. Completeness follows from the promised-case proof. This concerns equalities S_i^T M S_i=M, not arbitrary Lyapunov inequalities or boundedness of arbitrary matrix semigroups.

On the d=n(n+1)/2 dimensional rational space Sym_n, define

    T(H) = (1/s) sum_i S_i^T H S_i,    A=I-T.

Compute bases N of ker A and R of im A. If the concatenated d-by-d matrix [N R] is singular, the construction rejects. Otherwise let P be the rational projection onto ker A along im A, implemented by solving [N R] c=vec(I) and retaining the N part. Return M=P(I), after exact SPD and invariance checks.

## Why the direct sum and invariance hold

For the proof only, choose any real invariant H_0>0. Its square root conjugates each S_i to an orthogonal matrix. Equivalently, each congruence U_i(H)=S_i^T H S_i is an isometry of Sym_n for

    <H,K>_0=tr(H_0^{-1} H H_0^{-1} K).

Hence T is a contraction in that norm. Its powers are bounded, so a nontrivial Jordan block at eigenvalue 1 is impossible. Consequently ker(I-T) and im(I-T) form a direct sum. No numerical eigensolver is needed to acquire these rational subspaces.

Also ker(I-T) is exactly the intersection of ker(I-U_i). If TH=H, then

    (1/s) sum_i ||U_i H-H||_0^2
       =2||H||_0^2-2<TH,H>_0=0.

Every term is nonnegative, so every U_i H=H. The converse is immediate. The algorithm's range is therefore the common invariant space, not merely a fixed point of an average with possible cancellation.

The Cesaro means P_K=K^{-1}sum_{k=0}^{K-1}T^k converge to P: they act identically on ker(I-T), and on (I-T)Z they give (Z-T^K Z)/K, which tends to zero by power boundedness.

## Why P(I) is strictly positive, not merely semidefinite

Every word g in the generators preserves H_0. Thus there are positive constants a,b, independent of the word length, such that aI <= g^T g <= bI. For example the ratio of the extremal eigenvalues of H_0 supplies such bounds. T^k(I) is an average of exactly these word pullbacks. Its Cesaro means and their limit satisfy the same uniform inequalities. Hence P(I)>0.

The unknown bounds are used only to prove strict positivity. The algorithm constructs the exact rational P(I), verifies it by Sylvester's criterion, and does not estimate how quickly T^k converges. Actual iteration could be very slow; substituting that iteration for the exact projection would change the complexity claim.

## Application to a coercive hidden quartic

Let f(x)=((Lx)^2)^T Q((Lx)^2), Q>0 and L invertible. This is a strictly positive homogeneous quartic away from zero, so there exist a_f,b_f>0 with a_f||x||^4<=f(x)<=b_f||x||^4. All real linear f-automorphisms form a compact group: the bound confines every automorphism and inverse, and coefficient equality is closed. Haar averaging therefore supplies a real invariant H_0. This is a proof of existence only; no integration is an algorithmic input.

Apply the rational algorithm to any polynomial-size list of verified rational f-automorphisms. If that list contains each hidden sign reflection L^{-1}D_iL, then invariance under these reflections makes

    L^{-T} M L^{-1}

positive diagonal. Thus the recovered rational M is aligned with the hidden coordinates. Additional true automorphisms do not invalidate the argument, even if the complete real automorphism group has no rational invariant form: the algorithm averages only the acquired rational generators. Do not require invariance under unacquired irrational automorphisms.

The prior metric-based acquisition theorem can then be invoked with its hypotheses checked at the composition level. For quartic Q>0, rescaling by this positive diagonal preserves positive definiteness of the hidden weight matrix. This lemma alone neither finds the needed generators nor proves a Hessian-derived extactic nonzero.

## Bit accounting and rejection boundary

Building T takes polynomial rational arithmetic. Matrix entries have polynomial bit length in the matrices' encodings and log s. Exact nullspace/column-space bases and [N R]^{-1} have polynomial bit length by determinant bounds; d=O(n^2). SPD and invariance tests use rational elimination and exact matrix multiplication. Thus output metric height and total bit cost are polynomial for standard exact-linear-algebra implementations. No dependence on a supplied spectral separation or unknown invariant-form height is present.

If the common form does not exist, the direct sum may fail (a nontrivial unipotent shear is an example), P(I) may fail strict positivity, or invariance may fail. A positive invariant output is always checked directly; these algebraic checks supply a sound rejection/output procedure for this equality problem.

Finite checks validate the implementation, not the universal proof or novelty.
