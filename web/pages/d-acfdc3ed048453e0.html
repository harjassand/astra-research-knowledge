Cycle10 independent adversarial review: square-block midpoint deficit

Verdict

I found no failed step in the finite square-block derivation. The weighted-edge construction, its boundary compatibility bounds, the exposed-facet perturbation, and the slice/facet inequality fit together with the constants stated in the Luna report. This is an internal hand audit of that finite lemma, not a proof replay or validation of any GFF limit. The square construction does avoid the triangular source's non-tie condition: the nearest tangential shell vertex is graph distance two from the inward neighbor, so it cannot be forced onto the same unit-slope edge constraint.

The exposed-edge step is the part most likely to hide a defect. I checked every boundary class and the sharpest displayed margins. With the smallest admissible parameter point δ=δ0=1/16, k0=64, L=8448, β=1/128, the lower margins in the four cases are respectively 193/128, 3/64, 21567/128, and 333/2. All are strictly positive. An exact graph diagnostic also checks all designated-edge metrics and all vertex pairs on square boxes of side lengths 1 through 8, including boundary edges and cycles. It found no shorter bypass and no violation of dβ≥(1−β)d1. The diagnostic and its limits are recorded below.

Source inspected

The primary source is the pinned OpenAI Math manuscript “Uniform real Lipschitz surfaces on the triangular lattice,” dated 6 October 2026. Its PDF hash is the value stated in the Luna report, dce5ae39087f446aa1a721983682065be578bc7da2d5f25efc8c00960b685cbc. The relevant source theorem is `build/sections/foundations.tex`, especially lines 51–220. The source's formal finite-dimensional facet layer is in `lean/OAI/Probability/LatticeSurface/Geometry/UniformFacet.lean`, `FacetBody.lean`, `BlockAnalytic.lean`, and `BlockEdgeGeometry.lean`, plus `Convexity/FacetDeficitAnalytic.lean`. I used these only to check the cited slice/facet hypotheses and normalization; I did not run Lean.

The triangular source's block proof, lines 70–90 and 105–145, has a real tie obstruction: when its first compressed-ray increment vanishes, the radial edge and a nearby edge can both be forced to saturate. The proposed square argument removes that issue by replacing the triangular metric calculation with a weighted square-graph metric. This is an actual geometric change, not a consequence already contained in the triangular lemma.

1. Weighted edge exposure and shell compatibility

Let e=(x,y) be one selected radial edge. Set its length to 1 and every other square-grid edge length to 1−β, where β=min{δ/4,δ0/8,1/4}. Thus 0<β≤1/4, every edge length is at least 3/4, and the induced path metric dβ satisfies

  (1−β)d1(p,q) ≤ dβ(p,q) ≤ d1(p,q).

The first inequality follows path by path: each edge costs at least 1−β. The selected edge itself has cost 1. Any alternative path between adjacent endpoints has at least two edges in a simple graph, hence costs at least 2(1−β)≥3/2>1. In a square grid an actual alternate route, when one exists, has at least three edges. Positive edge costs mean that adding cycles cannot lower either bound. The exact finite diagnostic confirms these facts on all small boxes and for every selected edge. There are no periodic identifications or cross-block edges in QL, so the boundary calculation is for the induced finite square grid stated in the theorem.

All shell values are compatible with dβ. For boundary vertices b,c, a square Manhattan geodesic stays in QL, so

  |s(b)−s(c)|≤(1−δ)d1(b,c)≤(1−β)d1(b,c)≤dβ(b,c),

using β≤δ. The only remaining pairs are the prescribed endpoint values and shell vertices.

For a deep edge, k≥k0, choose a(x)=h(x), a(y)=h(x)+1. For any shell b, D=d1(x,b)≥k≥k0. The long-pair hypothesis bounds the x discrepancy by (1−δ0)D; for y it bounds the discrepancy by 1+(1−δ0)D. Since dβ(y,b)≥(1−β)(D−1), the smaller compatibility margin is

  (1−β)(D−1)−[1+(1−δ0)D]
       =(δ0−β)D−2+β
       ≥(δ0−β)k0−2+β>0.

The x margin is no smaller. The positivity follows from β≤δ0/8 and k0≥4/δ0.

For a shallow edge, k<k0, choose a(x)=h(v), a(y)=h(v)+1. If b is on the same side as v at tangential distance l, local edge slack along that side gives |h(v)−s(b)|≤(1−δ)l. For l≥1 the y compatibility margin is

  (1−β)(k+1+l)−[1+(1−δ)l]
       =k(1−β)+δl−β(k+l+1)
       =k(1−2β)+l(δ−β)−β
       ≥δ−2β≥δ/2>0.

For l=0,k=0 the selected edge has length 1 and the prescribed difference is exactly 1; for l=0,k≥1 the lower bound (1−β)(k+1)≥1 suffices. The x inequality follows directly from (1−δ)l≤(1−β)(k+l)≤dβ(x,b).

For b on any of the other three sides, D=d1(v,b)≥L/2. The long-pair condition applies to v,b. Triangle inequalities give d1(x,b)≥D−k and d1(y,b)≥D−k−1. Thus the x and y compatibility margins are at least

  (δ0−β)D−(1−β)k,
  (δ0−β)D−1−(1−β)(k+1).

They are minimized at D=L/2 and k=k0−1. The assumed L≥8(k0+2)/δ0, together with β≤δ0/8, makes both strictly positive. This covers corners too; a corner can be assigned to either incident side, and the same-side estimate or the other-side estimate applies.

The selected pair differs by exactly one and has dβ-distance one. Therefore the finite-graph McShane extension of the shell and endpoint data is a filling with the selected edge saturated and every other edge increment at most 1−β. The use of the metric is sound even if a shortest path would otherwise travel around a cycle: every nonselected edge still costs at least 3/4, and a route through the marked edge cannot create a cheaper alternative between its endpoints.

2. Facet survives the shell perturbation

If K(s±χ) have positive volume, then feasibility of their shell constraints gives, on each boundary edge (b,c), both |(s(b)−s(c))+(χ(b)−χ(c))|≤1 and |(s(b)−s(c))−(χ(b)−χ(c))|≤1. Since max(|a+d|,|a−d|)=|a|+|d|, this implies |χ(b)−χ(c)|≤1. The boundary cycle has length 4L, so any two shell values of χ differ by at most 2L.

For α=β/(8L), shift every interior coordinate of the exposed filling by ±αχ(v), while changing the shell to s±αχ. Both endpoints of the selected edge receive the same shift, including the case where its outside endpoint is v, so it stays saturated. Every other interior edge is unchanged. A shell-to-interior edge changes its increment by at most 2Lα=β/4. A boundary-to-boundary edge changes by at most α because |χ(b)−χ(c)|≤1. Hence every nonselected edge retains at least 3β/4 slack.

A Euclidean ball of radius r=β/16 in the selected supporting hyperplane stays within the facet: perturbing the interior vector by norm at most r changes any edge difference by at most √2 r<2r=β/8, less than the retained slack. The body is bounded in each free coordinate by a fixed shell value plus or minus 2L, so its m-dimensional volume is at most (4L)^m. Consequently the selected top facet has area/volume ratio at least

  c0=ω_(m−1)(β/16)^(m−1)/(4L)^m>0.

This ratio is uniform over all four radial paths, all selected edges, and all allowed χ. It may be extremely small, but it does not vanish for fixed finite L and positive β.

3. Slice/facet inequality and constant audit

The source's formal `UniformFacet.exists_uniform_facet_constant` theorem requires compact convex bodies K−,K+,K0; positive volume for K−,K+; projection width at most 2; a radius-r ball in each top slice; an upper volume bound B; and inclusion of every midpoint `(x+y)/2` in K0. `FacetBody.compact_body_facet_deficit` reduces this to the one-dimensional slice-quantile inequality in `FacetDeficitAnalytic.interval_facet_deficit`. The square application meets exactly these conditions:

- all filling sets are compact and convex;
- K(s±αχ) have positive volume;
- the selected edge coordinate has width at most 2 (a shell-to-interior coordinate has interval length at most 2; an interior edge difference has width at most √2);
- the exposed top slice contains the ball above;
- all filling bodies have volume at most (4L)^m;
- midpoint fillings are feasible for midpoint shell s, so (K(s+αχ)+K(s−αχ))/2⊆K(s).

The report's alternate expression uses the lower facet ratio c0 and C=max{2,c0^−1}. Its quantile bound is q′(p)≤C/p: if the normalized top density at the p-quantile is below c0, log-concavity and the top value force all density to its left to be no larger, whence p≤2f(q(p)); otherwise 1/f(q(p))≤c0^−1. The source's formal wrapper instead sets C=2+B/S from a volume cap B and a top-slice-area lower bound S. Both give a valid common width/velocity constant; the candidate's c0 version is at least as strong numerically when the ratio bound is valid.

For quantiles q±, midpoint-slice inclusion and Brunn–Minkowski give the ratio lower bound

  |K0|/sqrt(|K−||K+|) ≥ 1+(1/(8C²))∫₀¹ p²(q′−−q′+)² dp.

The difference d of top-endpoint-to-centroid gaps equals ∫₀¹p(q′−−q′+)dp; Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the integral below by d². The width bound |d|≤2 and log(1+x)≥x/(1+x) give coefficient κ=(8C²+4)^−1. This matches the source's formal pattern. I found no hidden need for a triangular non-tie assumption in this convex-body lemma; the non-tie condition is used only to manufacture the top facet in the triangular graph.

Per interior radial edge, the facet gap controls |ΔU|² by 2gα/κ; the shell-to-interior edge gets the stronger gα/κ. There are L edges on the route between any two side midpoints (L/2 from each midpoint to center). Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  |U(v)−U(w)|²≤2L²gα/κ.

Since U(v)−U(w)=2α(χ(v)−χ(w)),

  gα≥2κα²L⁻²|χ(v)−χ(w)|².

With α=β/(8L), this is κβ²/(32L⁴) times the squared boundary difference. Concavity of t↦F(s+tχ) implies its symmetric midpoint loss at α is at most the loss at 1, so the full stated block constant follows. The factor 32 is consistent; no missing factor appears in the edge direction normalization or the path count.

4. Exact diagnostics and boundary of this review

`exposure_diagnostic.py` uses integer-scaled edge costs 3 and 4, corresponding to ordinary length 3/4 and selected length 1. It checks 480 selected-edge configurations across square grids of side 1–8, and 1,715,160 exact weighted/unweighted vertex-pair inequalities. For each selected edge it checks the direct cost is 1 and that any alternate path is longer; the shortest alternate route observed is three edges. Separately it checks the displayed compatibility margins at δ=δ0=1/16, k0=64, L=8448, β=1/128:

  deep:       193/128;
  shallow same-side (minimum): 3/64;
  shallow other-side x: 21567/128;
  shallow other-side y: 333/2.

These checks target the weighted metric and the worst-case scalar margins. They do not enumerate K(s), compute a filling volume, or independently prove the abstract convex slice inequality. The latter was derived directly and checked against the primary source's hypotheses. I found no exact counterexample under the theorem's assumptions and no failed step in the reported finite proof. The square block lemma is internally supported after this audit; it is not externally certified, kernel-replayed, or connected here to any random-field conclusion.

The local estimate is not a broad GFF result. No square sparse-block, stationary reflected-limit, covariance-identification, tightness, or domain-uniformity claim was audited in this task.
