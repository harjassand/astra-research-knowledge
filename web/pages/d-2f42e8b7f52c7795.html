# Long-horizon chaotic response: inverse-path mechanism closed

Date: 2026-10-10. Status: **failed candidate; no transformative capability established.**

## Result first

The proposed mechanism was to replace unstable forward trajectories by contracting inverse-branch trajectories, retain the exact deterministic objective through likelihood weights, and remove the remaining response horizon by common-prefix telescoping and random truncation.

It fails on an explicitly specified deterministic chaotic family. The family has four coarse cells, sixteen affine branches, forward expansion at least 10/9, and inverse-branch contraction factor at most 9/10. Its stationary response is exactly 1/2. Nevertheless, the selected backward estimator has variance at least

\[
\frac{119\delta}{16000}(6/5)^{n-1}.
\]

For delta = 1/1000, reducing its exact finite-horizon response bias to 1/1000 requires n = 6212. The variance bound then forces more than 10^492 independent paths merely to attain sampling standard deviation 1/1000. A four-by-four exact transfer-operator solve returns 1/2 directly. No invariant density, response existence, spectral gap, or Lyapunov splitting was supplied to that comparator: they are acquired from the native map.

The proposed common-prefix random-truncation repair also fails. On the two-cell member used below, every independent full-support single-term roulette has infinite absolute expectation, so its formal telescoping answer is **not a well-defined unbiased estimator**.

These are route-specific failures. They are not lower bounds for all response algorithms, adaptive proposals, interacting particle systems, or smooth hyperbolic systems. The exact transfer solve is an existing comparator, not an invention.

## 1. Native task and comparison of mechanisms

The desired output is

\[
R=\left.\frac{d}{d\theta}\int A\,d\mu_\theta\right|_{\theta=0},
\]

where the native input specifies a deterministic parameterized map, an observable, and an accuracy requirement. The physical measure and its derivative must be acquired, not assumed.

Two materially different approaches were examined before selecting the second for the decisive test.

1. **Forward trajectory recombination.** Run signed or perturbed trajectory populations and swap or merge nearby future segments, hoping mixing makes exact cancellation possible. For a deterministic transition, the conditional law at a state x is the point mass at T(x). A coupling that preserves both conditional laws has no freedom in either next state. On an injective map, distinct exact states cannot coalesce. For continuous independently drawn states under a finite-to-one map, an exact collision also has probability zero. Merely permuting pairings of two fixed populations leaves the difference of their empirical means exactly unchanged. Approximate merging changes the measure; its bias requires a regularity/error certificate and cannot be inferred from visual proximity. These statements do not prohibit nontrivial changes of the population sampling law.
2. **Backward contracting paths with exact likelihood correction.** Inverse branches are numerically stable and allow independent random histories. Correct their proposal law by exact transfer weights, differentiate the weights, and then couple adjacent horizon levels by their common prefix. This removes forward state instability without changing the deterministic objective. It is concrete enough to test natively. The problem moves into path-weight fluctuations and survives the common-prefix repair.

Adding physical noise was not used. All randomness below belongs to a computational representation of the original deterministic transfer operator, so there is no unreported zero-noise objective or bias.

### Strong existing baselines

S3 already regularizes the unstable response contribution rather than naively differentiating an exponentially unstable trajectory. Fast-response methods already give pointwise formulas with first- and second-order tangent work along the unstable directions. Certified transfer methods already compute response with specified numerical errors for suitable expanding maps. These methods must not be rediscovered or compared only against naive finite differences. See the pinned sources in section 8.

For the finite-dimensional invariant subspace in this counterexample, the strongest directly applicable baseline is the exact transfer solve. A full implementation of S3 or NILSS was neither necessary nor claimed. The map has moving piecewise-affine breakpoints and is not globally C1; it is not a counterexample to guarantees restricted to smooth Anosov or smooth expanding systems.

## 2. Native deterministic class and physical-measure certificate

Input a positive column-stochastic m-by-m rational matrix P(theta), differentiable in theta, with all entries less than one. Divide [0,1) into fixed coarse cells

\[
I_i=[i/m,(i+1)/m),\quad i=0,\ldots,m-1.
\]

Within source cell I_i, form consecutive branch intervals B_ji of widths P_ji(theta)/m. Their left endpoints are

\[
a_{ji}(\theta)=i/m+\frac1m\sum_{k<j}P_{ki}(\theta).
\]

On that branch define

\[
T_\theta(x)=j/m+\frac{x-a_{ji}(\theta)}{P_{ji}(\theta)}.
\]

Thus every branch maps exactly onto its fixed target cell I_j, and its slope is 1/P_ji. Endpoint conventions affect only a null set. The actual affine partition has m squared branches, not merely m branches.

The Perron transfer operator preserves densities constant on coarse cells. If f_i is the density on I_i, then its next density is exactly Pf. A stationary probability vector pi is acquired by solving

\[
P\pi=\pi,\qquad \mathbf1^T\pi=1;
\]

the invariant density is h_i=m*pi_i. Positivity of P gives a unique positive stationary vector.

This is a physical measure, not just an invariant formal vector. Under this density, coarse itineraries have the finite-state stationary Markov law with transition probabilities P_ji from source i to target j. Positivity makes that law mixing. Cylinder lengths are at most (max P_ji)^n/m, hence tend to zero and generate the interval sigma-algebra up to endpoints. The interval system is therefore ergodic under its invariant density. That density is bounded above and away from zero on finitely many cells, so it is equivalent to Lebesgue measure. The ergodic theorem gives its full-Lebesgue-measure physical basin. This also rules out a second physical measure with a positive-Lebesgue-measure basin.

The stationary vector depends differentiably on theta because the normalized finite linear system is nonsingular. Its derivative is acquired from

\[
(I-P)\pi'=P'\pi,\qquad \mathbf1^T\pi'=0.
\]

For cell observables A_i, R=A^T*pi'. More general fixed observables enter through their cell averages. Thus response existence is certified for this explicit class without an oracle. Moving branch endpoints introduce no omitted boundary term: the exact restricted transfer matrix is P(theta), and its derivative already accounts for their changing widths.

This class models deterministic expanding transport with region occupancy as the readout. It is deliberately limited; no extension to turbulence, arbitrary nonhyperbolic attractors, or bifurcations is established.

## 3. Sharp fixed-proposal weight theorem

Let P be positive and column-stochastic. Let h>0 satisfy Ph=h. Fix a positive row-stochastic backward proposal Q. Starting at i_0=j, draw i_(k+1) from Q[i_k, .] and form

\[
W_n=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{P_{i_k i_{k+1}}}{Q_{i_k i_{k+1}}}.
\]

Direct finite summation gives

\[
E_j W_n=(P^n\mathbf1)_j,\qquad
E_j W_n^2=(K^n\mathbf1)_j,\quad K_{ji}=P_{ji}^2/Q_{ji}.
\]

**Theorem.** The spectral radius rho(K) is at least one. Equality holds exactly when

\[
Q_{ji}=Q^*_{ji}:=P_{ji}h_i/h_j.
\]

Otherwise rho(K)>1 and every starting state's second moment grows exponentially with n. For Q*, the weights telescope as W_n=h_j/h_(i_n) and are uniformly bounded.

**Proof.** Q* is row-stochastic because Ph=h. Put v_i=h_i squared. Jensen gives

\[
(Kv)_j=\sum_i Q_{ji}\left(\frac{P_{ji}h_i}{Q_{ji}}\right)^2
\geq\left(\sum_i P_{ji}h_i\right)^2=v_j.
\]

Equality in row j requires P_ji*h_i/Q_ji constant in i, hence Q_ji=Q*_ji. If one row is strict, a strictly positive left Perron eigenvector u of positive K yields rho(K)*u^T v=u^T Kv>u^T v. Conversely, the displayed telescoping identity bounds every second moment under Q*, giving rho(K)=1. Perron-Frobenius then supplies exponential growth when rho(K)>1. QED.

A quantitative form is

\[
\min_j\chi^2(Q^*_j\Vert Q_j)\leq\rho(K)-1
\leq\max_j\chi^2(Q^*_j\Vert Q_j),
\]

by applying Collatz-Wielandt to v=h squared. Exact twisting supplies invariant-density ratios: Q*_ji/P_ji=h_i/h_j. Finding that twist therefore acquires the missing stationary density up to normalization. This is an algebraic equivalence in the fixed-proposal family, not a general complexity lower bound.

**Novelty boundary:** this theorem is an elementary finite-state, one-particle specialization of established eigenfunction twisting results, especially Whiteley and Lee. It is included to expose the failure mechanism, not claimed as new. Adaptive/time-inhomogeneous proposals and interacting particle methods are outside its scope; known particle-filter methods can change the variance behavior.

## 4. Two-cell exact gradient test and failed random truncation

Take

\[
P_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}9/10-\theta&2/5\\1/10+\theta&3/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]

At zero, pi=(4/5,1/5), h=(8/5,2/5), and the right-half occupancy has response 8/5. These values are computed from P, not input assumptions.

The density-free local proposal normalizes each transfer row:

\[
Q_{ji}=P_{ji}/r_j,\qquad r=(13/10,7/10).
\]

Each one-step weight is r_j. Its second-moment matrix is

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}117/100&13/25\\7/100&21/50\end{pmatrix},
\quad \rho(K)\simeq1.21574339.
\]

Freeze Q at theta=0 and define the score and gradient estimator

\[
s_{ji}=P'_{ji}/P_{ji},\quad S_n=\sum_{k<n}s_{i_k i_{k+1}},\quad Z_n=W_n S_n.
\]

Then E_j Z_n=partial_theta(P_theta^n*1)_j. Starting in terminal cell j=1, Y_n=Z_n/2 is unbiased for the finite-horizon right-half response

\[
R_n=\frac85-\left(\frac85+\frac35n\right)2^{-n}.
\]

The proposal itself need not be differentiated because it is frozen. If a parameter-dependent proposal is differentiated instead, its sampling-law correction must be included; differentiating only the sampled weight is generally wrong.

Exact moment recursions, with elementwise operations, are

\[
B=P\odot P'/Q,\quad C=(P')^2/Q,
\]

\[
a_{n+1}=Ka_n,\quad b_{n+1}=Kb_n+Ba_n,
\quad c_{n+1}=Kc_n+2Bb_n+Ca_n,
\]

with a_0=1,b_0=c_0=0. They represent E W squared, E W squared S, and E W squared S squared. Independently enumerating all paths through n=8 verifies these recursions exactly.

### Why the common-prefix multilevel repair fails

Couple levels by sharing their first n-1 transitions and put D_n=Z_n-Z_(n-1). Given the prefix, with current state j,

\[
D_n=W_{n-1}\{(r_j-1)S_{n-1}+r_j s_{j i_n}\}.
\]

The conditional variance is W_(n-1) squared times r_j squared Var_Q(s), whose values in the two rows are 4/9 and 6. Thus

\[
E D_n^2\geq\frac49E W_{n-1}^2.
\]

More strongly, the two possible values of D_n/W_(n-1) are separated by 13/9 in row 0 and 7 in row 1. The smaller atom probabilities are 4/13 and 1/7. For any offset and two atoms, expected absolute value is at least the smaller probability times their separation. Consequently

\[
E(|D_n|\mid\text{prefix})\geq\frac49 W_{n-1}.
\]

Since E W_(n-1) tends to positive h_j, sum_n E|D_n| diverges. For an independent random level N with p_n>0 for every n,

\[
E|D_N/p_N|=\sum_n E|D_n|=\infty.
\]

The means E D_n have a convergent telescoping sum, but that does not interchange with this expectation. Both the positive and negative parts diverge. Calling D_N/p_N unbiased would be false. Finite level support restores integrability but introduces truncation bias. The statement is specific to this common-prefix single-term construction, not every possible debiasing coupling.

## 5. Four-cell stress family: contraction stays fixed while necessary horizon grows

The previous example alone could be dismissed as excessive burn-in. The following family makes a long horizon necessary for the chosen finite-time approximation while keeping forward expansion uniformly strong.

Let B be the two-by-two matrix P_0 above. For 0<delta<=1/100 set

\[
P_\delta=(1-\delta)\operatorname{diag}(B,B)+\frac\delta4\mathbf1\mathbf1^T,
\quad D_\delta=\frac\delta4(-1,-1,1,1)^T\mathbf1^T,
\]

and P_(delta,theta)=P_delta+theta D_delta for |theta|<=1/2. All entries are positive and less than 9/10. The deterministic map therefore has sixteen affine branches, forward expansion at least 10/9, and inverse-branch contraction at most 9/10, uniformly in delta and theta.

Let A be occupancy of the last two coarse cells. From a uniform initial density, its mass y_n obeys the exact scalar equation

\[
y_{n+1}=(1-\delta)y_n+\delta/2+\theta\delta/2.
\]

Thus

\[
R_\infty=1/2,\quad R_n=\tfrac12\{1-(1-\delta)^n\},
\quad |R_\infty-R_n|=\tfrac12(1-\delta)^n.
\]

The slowly mixing block mode is acquired explicitly: its eigenvalue is 1-delta. It was not inferred from short empirical correlations. Uniform inverse contraction does not control this physical mixing time.

### Certified variance lower bound

Again use Q_ji=P_ji/r_j. On vectors constant across the two identical blocks, K=diag(r)P reduces to

\[
\bar K=\operatorname{diag}(\bar P\mathbf1)\bar P,
\quad \bar P=(1-\delta)B+\delta\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/2.
\]

For v=(1,7/80), the components of barK*v-(6/5)v are exactly

\[
939\delta^2/8000-6313\delta/8000+31/2000,
\]
\[
939\delta^2/8000+2557\delta/8000+7/4000.
\]

Both are positive on [0,1/100]: the first decreases there and is still positive at the right endpoint, while the second has positive coefficients. Lifting v to four components gives

\[
E_jW_{n-1}^2\geq (7/80)(6/5)^{n-1}
\]

for every terminal cell j.

For D=P' and s_ji=D_ji/P_ji, the one-step conditional score variance satisfies

\[
r_j^2\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}s
=\frac{\delta^2}{16}\left(r_j\sum_iP_{ji}^{-1}-16\right).
\]

Each row has two opposite-block entries equal to delta/4, and r_j>=7/10. Hence this is at least

\[
\delta r_j/2-\delta^2\geq (17/50)\delta.
\]

Choose the terminal cell uniformly from the last two cells and output Y_n=Z_n/2. Its expectation is exactly R_n. Conditioning on the path prefix and using the variance decomposition gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_n)\geq\frac14\frac{17\delta}{50}\frac7{80}(6/5)^{n-1}
=\frac{119\delta}{16000}(6/5)^{n-1}.
\]

The same bound applies to the second moment of the common-prefix response increment.

For epsilon=1/1000, the minimum n with truncation bias at most epsilon is checked using exact rational powers:

| delta | required n | log10 variance lower bound | log10 independent paths for sampling SD <=epsilon |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/100 | 619 | 44.8054370 | 50.8054370 |
| 1/1000 | 6212 | 486.6661462 | 492.6661462 |

The path count follows specifically for the mean of M independent copies, whose variance is Var(Y_n)/M. These are separate bias and sampling-SD targets, not a claim of total RMSE <=epsilon when both equal epsilon.

The exact baseline solves the normalized stationary and derivative equations from section 2. For delta=1/1000 it obtains

\[
\pi=(4001/10010,502/5005,4001/10010,502/5005),
\]

\[
\pi'=(-4001/10010,-502/5005,4001/10010,502/5005),
\]

so the last-two-cell response is exactly 1/2. These rational outputs are independently checked against both linear equations.

## 6. Precision, endpoint, work, and acquisition accounting

- **Native input:** m, rational entries of P(theta) and P'(0), a certified parameter interval, the explicit affine branch construction, and the cell observable. There is no hidden density or spectral oracle. Input size is O(m squared B) bits for B-bit entries.
- **Class verification:** column sums, positive entries, and branch images/slopes are checked exactly. The code verifies all branch endpoints for both stress instances and positivity/expansion at the parameter interval's affine extrema. The physical-measure proof above supplies existence and uniqueness. No empirical mixing certificate is used.
- **Existing comparator:** dense linear algebra uses O(m cubed) arithmetic operations and polynomial rational bit work; for m=4 the answer is acquired directly. Conditioning or tiny transition entries may increase needed bits, but the exact supplied rational family remains finite and transparent. No long trajectory is needed.
- **Candidate work:** one path costs O(n) transitions once the finite proposal rows are prepared. Rational proposal probabilities can be sampled exactly using unbiased random bits and integer comparison/rejection. Maintaining raw rational weights grows representation length with n; log weights avoid overflow but do not change the variance lower bound. The decisive test uses exact moment equations rather than an unreliable finite Monte Carlo sample of rare high-weight paths.
- **Backward endpoint errors:** contraction is along a fixed sequence of inverse branches. With per-step coordinate error at most eta, endpoint discrepancy obeys e_n <= (9/10)^n e_0 + 10 eta. It is not a global contraction across different branch labels. Cell labels and branch choices are exact in the finite symbolic representation, so the cell-occupancy objective has zero coordinate/endpoint approximation error. This avoids hiding an exponentially accurate forward trajectory inside the counterexample.
- **Derivative and truncation error:** the finite-horizon mean, exact stationary response, and horizon bias are explicit and independently verified. In particular, endpoint contraction and response truncation have different rates.
- **Roundoff:** all mathematical certification and the main moment recursions use exact rational/symbolic arithmetic. Decimal table entries are rounded display. Large base-ten logarithms use 90-digit arithmetic only for presentation; the underlying lower bound is the exact rational expression shown above.
- **Unknown mixing:** the method cannot treat inverse contraction as a mixing oracle. For this class, the relevant slow mode is acquired exactly from native transition masses. An unrestricted system would require a separate valid certification step; no claim is made that short observed correlations provide it.

## 7. Native verification and stopping decision

Run `python verify.py` in this directory. The successful run used Python 3.12.14, SymPy 1.14.0, and mpmath 1.3.0, taking approximately 0.28 seconds on the included CPU (timing is incidental).

The executable checks:

- enumerate every two-state backward path for n=1 through 8 and independently match exact first/second moment recursions;
- match the finite response formula at all n=1 through 500;
- verify exact stationary and derivative solves for both four-state stress instances;
- verify actual deterministic branch endpoints and parameter-range expansion;
- certify the uniform positive-vector growth inequality symbolically;
- certify the minimum required horizons using rational inequalities;
- reproduce the variance and path-count bounds without fitting a curve or relying on Monte Carlo agreement.

`verification.json` records the complete outputs and software versions. `AUDIT.md` records the independent audit, including the correction from merely infinite variance to nonintegrability of the roulette construction.

**Stopping decision:** close this mechanism. The promising state-stability advantage is canceled by a likelihood-weight problem already understood by eigenfunction twisting, and the direct response counterexample makes the loss explicit. No weaker repair or new capability is claimed. A future proposal would need a genuinely new way of acquiring/certifying the relevant response representation at scale, not an uncharged density, splitting, mixing, or shadowing oracle.
