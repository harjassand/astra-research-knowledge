# Endpoint-and-integral tests of non-phantom cosmic expansion

**Investigation date:** 9 October 2026.  
**Status:** Mathematical derivation and primary-data numerical investigation; **no new natural law or foundational scientific discovery established**. Historical novelty of the finite-point characterization is not established. No independent expert or formal verification is claimed.

## 1. Scientific outcome

The released DESI DR2 compressed baryon-acoustic-oscillation (BAO) likelihood does not, by itself, compel a new dark-energy mechanism. A two-parameter flat Lambda-CDM expansion gives chi-square 10.271041 for 13 released observables. A smooth, positive-potential, non-phantom scalar-field expansion also fits, with chi-square 8.686547. An unrestricted four-parameter CPL fit gives 5.618600. These are numerical best fits found in the stated classes, not probabilities that the corresponding physical mechanisms are true.

The mathematical result developed during the investigation is a finite-point characterization of distances generated by a nondecreasing dark-energy density. It replaces an arbitrarily chosen interpolation of the density with exact integral envelopes. This is a conditional result about a specified physical framework. It is not an empirical discovery of dark energy, its equation of state, or a violation of an energy condition.

The broad physical logic overlaps established work, especially Lewis and Chamberlain, arXiv:2412.13894. Their work derives energy-condition restrictions on acoustic-scale observables, including restrictions when the CMB acoustic angle is fixed. This investigation must not be credited with originating that logic. The finite-endpoint formulation below has not received an exhaustive priority review.

## 3. Physical assumptions

Assume a spatially flat FLRW geometry with positive expansion rate. Use general relativity, a separately conserved pressureless-matter component, and a separately conserved positive-density dark-energy component. Assume the same comoving BAO ruler is used at all redshifts. The numerical baseline neglects radiation over the measured low-redshift interval; a small radiation term is separately tested for numerical sensitivity.

Define

\[
h(z)=\frac{H(z)r_d}{c},\qquad
 d_H(z)=\frac{1}{h(z)},\qquad
 d_M(z)=\frac{D_M(z)}{r_d}=\int_0^z\frac{du}{h(u)}.
\]

The isotropic BAO distance is

\[
d_V(z)=\bigl[z\,d_M(z)^2d_H(z)\bigr]^{1/3}.
\]

Write the dimensionless Friedmann equation as

\[
h(z)^2=P(z)+B(z),\qquad P(z)=A(1+z)^3,
\]

where

\[
A=\Omega_m\left(\frac{H_0r_d}{c}\right)^2
\]

and B is proportional to the dark-energy density. The proof also permits an externally specified continuous P containing additional known components, provided the denominators stay positive. It does not permit silently changing P while claiming to test the same physical model.

For the separately conserved dark-energy fluid,

\[
\dot\rho+3H(\rho+p)=0,\qquad \dot z=-(1+z)H.
\]

Consequently, for positive density,

\[
B'(z)=\frac{3[1+w(z)]B(z)}{1+z},\qquad w=p/\rho.
\]

Thus a non-phantom fluid, w at least -1, has nondecreasing B with redshift. This step is established physical background, not a new law.

A statement about this dark-energy component is not a statement about the total stress tensor in every possible gravitational theory. Curvature, energy exchange between components, a different matter evolution, or modified gravitational dynamics require separate treatment.

## 4. Finite-point integral characterization

Consider consecutive redshifts a less than b, with fixed endpoint densities B_a at most B_b. Assume P+B_a is uniformly positive on the interval. Every nondecreasing density with those endpoints obeys

\[
B_a\le B(z)\le B_b.
\]

Since the reciprocal square root is decreasing,

\[
\boxed{\quad
\int_a^b\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+B_b}}
\le d_M(b)-d_M(a)
\le\int_a^b\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+B_a}}.
\quad}\tag{1}
\]

At a redshift where a radial measurement is interpreted as a point value, its implied endpoint density is

\[
B_i=d_H(z_i)^{-2}-P(z_i).
\]

Nonnegative ordered endpoint densities and (1), imposed on each consecutive interval, characterize the closure of the attainable finite-point observations. The statement concerns the specified continuous expansion framework; measured estimates are not substituted as exact truths when assessing statistical evidence.

### Proof of sufficiency in closure

For any switch location s between a and b, set B equal to B_a before s and B_b after s, assigning the prescribed endpoint values. The resulting interval distance is

\[
J(s)=\int_a^s\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+B_a}}
+\int_s^b\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+B_b}}.
\]

This function is continuous and nondecreasing. Its endpoint values are exactly the lower and upper bounds in (1). The intermediate value theorem therefore realizes every distance between them by a monotone step density. Concatenating the constructions for successive intervals preserves global monotonicity.

The step can be replaced by a smooth monotone transition. Positivity of the denominator and dominated convergence imply convergence of the distances. Strictly interior interval distances can also be reached with a sufficiently narrow smooth transition by continuity in its location. If the endpoint densities coincide, the interval density is constant and the two bounds coincide.

Hence the interval conditions are necessary and sufficient in closure. They are not a claim that instantaneous transitions are physical, nor that every smooth fluid history has a globally regular canonical scalar potential.

### Why the closure qualification matters

A Gaussian compressed BAO likelihood at an effective redshift is not an instrument measuring an exact instantaneous H value. Extremely rapid transitions inside a broad survey bin can invalidate that interpretation. The envelope fits are exploratory feasibility calculations in the point-likelihood approximation. They are not survey-level validation of arbitrarily sharp transitions. A separate smooth physical example is evaluated below to avoid relying on such transitions for the compatibility conclusion.

## 5. Stronger bounds for a scalar field with nonnegative potential

For a canonical scalar field with nonnegative potential, kinetic energy and potential energy give

\[
-1\le w\le1,
\qquad 0\le B'(z)\le\frac{6B(z)}{1+z}.
\]

For strictly positive endpoints the necessary endpoint condition is

\[
B_a\le B_b\le B_a\left(\frac{1+b}{1+a}\right)^6.
\]

Integrating the differential bounds forwards and backwards gives pointwise envelopes

\[
L(z)=\max\left\{B_a,\ B_b\left(\frac{1+z}{1+b}\right)^6\right\},
\]

\[
U(z)=\min\left\{B_b,\ B_a\left(\frac{1+z}{1+a}\right)^6\right\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad
\int_a^b\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+U(z)}}
\le d_M(b)-d_M(a)
\le\int_a^b\frac{dz}{\sqrt{P(z)+L(z)}}.
\quad}\tag{2}
\]

These bounds are sharp for the corresponding absolutely continuous fluid histories. Both envelopes satisfy the linear differential inequalities almost everywhere and have the prescribed endpoints. Every convex combination of them also satisfies the inequalities. Its distance varies continuously between the two extreme distances, proving attainability for that fluid class. Regular canonical-potential realization is a further requirement; limiting nonsmooth histories should not be identified automatically with a regular fundamental field theory.

Equation (2) was derived and numerically checked as an interval statement. A complete likelihood optimization over this stronger bounded-slope class was **not** performed. The much smaller smooth family below does satisfy the nonnegative-potential restrictions and was actually fitted.

## 6. Explicit smooth physical competitor

Use the analytic density family

\[
B(z)=b_0+a\frac{z}{1+z},\qquad b_0>0,\quad 0\le a\le6b_0.
\]

It has B'=a/(1+z)^2 and

\[
w(z)=-1+\frac{a}{3(1+z)B(z)}.
\]

For clarity, in units c=1 let H_*=1/r_d and let M denote the reduced Planck mass. The canonical field reconstruction is

\[
\dot\phi^2=M^2H_*^2(1+z)B'(z),
\]

\[
V(z)=M^2H_*^2\left[3B(z)-\frac{1+z}{2}B'(z)\right],
\]

\[
\left|\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right|
=M\sqrt{\frac{B'(z)}{(1+z)[P(z)+B(z)]}}.
\]

For a positive amplitude, phi is strictly monotone on any finite observed interval, allowing elimination of z to obtain a regular potential along the trajectory. Here

\[
\frac{V(z)}{M^2H_*^2}
=3(b_0+a)-\frac{7a}{2(1+z)}
\ge3b_0-\frac{a}{2}\ge0\quad(z\ge0).
\]

The zero-amplitude limit is constant dark energy. This family is a physical counterexample to a claim that these BAO measurements require phantom dark energy, not an identified new mechanism in nature. Its chosen functional form has no established novelty claim. No extrapolation to the future is inferred from its fit.

## 7. Numerical results

All fits use

\[
\chi^2=(m-d)^TC^{-1}(m-d).
\]

The validation script independently uses Cholesky whitening. Its optimizer differs from the initial baseline implementation. Quadrature with 64 and 128 nodes gives differences below 7e-14 in the fitted BAO predictions; this is a numerical agreement check, not interval-arithmetic certification.

| Model actually fitted | Parameters | Fitted chi-square |
|---|---:|---:|
| Flat Lambda-CDM, radiation omitted | 2 | 10.271041003 |
| Smooth positive-potential density above | 3 | 8.686546809 |
| Flat CPL, unrestricted sign of 1+w | 4 | 5.618600031 |

For Lambda-CDM,

\[
\Omega_m=0.297461819,\qquad c/(H_0r_d)=29.524633399.
\]

The nominal chi-square reference tail with 11 degrees of freedom is 0.50618. It is a goodness-of-fit reference under the adopted Gaussian likelihood, not the probability that Lambda-CDM is true. These BAO-only calculations do not refute or reproduce the joint CMB/supernova analyses.

For the smooth family,

\[
A=0.000331271768,\quad b_0=0.000757318180,\quad a=0.000331474054.
\]

It gives w(0) approximately -0.85410 and w(2.33) approximately -0.96646. The improvement over the constant-density fit is only 1.58449 in chi-square, with an additional parameter. The data do not establish this functional form or potential.

For CPL, the numerical best fit found is

\[
\Omega_m=0.386605972,\quad c/(H_0r_d)=32.854095554,
\]

\[
w_0=-0.178832555,\qquad w_a=-2.716679554.
\]

Its improvement of 4.65244 uses two additional parameters. No discovery significance is assigned. The finite-sample likelihood-ratio distribution and the full model selection problem were not calibrated here.

As a limited sensitivity check, adding the dimensionless radiation coefficient R=1e-7 in h squared changes the fitted Lambda-CDM chi-square to 10.283321. This is not a calculation of the full early-universe likelihood or neutrino evolution.

### Exploratory envelope fits

`envelope_fit.py` also searches the enlarged monotone-density point-likelihood class. It finds, for example, chi-square 8.17044 at A=0.00034 and 5.17157 at A=0.00032. A free-matter fit runs to its imposed very-low-matter boundary. This is an identifiability warning, not evidence for negligible cosmic matter. The stored values are feasible numerical candidates from multistart constrained optimization, not certified global optima or calibrated confidence bounds. One high-matter run reports a failed convergence flag despite a near-feasible iterate; the full diagnostics are retained rather than silently treated as a certified solution.

`test_envelopes.py` runs 100 randomized mathematical reconstruction checks and 100 bounded-slope envelope checks. They are implementation diagnostics, not observational or experimental evidence.

## 8. Prior literature, evidence boundaries, and implications

The context is the unresolved physical interpretation of cosmic expansion data, not an established failure of Lambda-CDM in the particular BAO-only likelihood used here. Lewis and Chamberlain already derived strong energy-condition distance bounds. Shlivko and Steinhardt and other authors have also examined the limitations of interpreting simple equation-of-state parameterizations as fundamental scalar dynamics. These precedents prevent claiming the basic conceptual argument as a new discovery.

The DES-Dovekie reanalysis reports weaker evidence for evolving dark energy than the legacy DES-SN5YR analysis. This was checked in the authors' paper and current repository documentation, not recomputed from their supernova covariance. Its result cannot be counted as an independent successful test of the density family constructed here.

What would falsify the class is a statistically robust incompatibility of the joint distance measurements with the endpoint ordering and integral conditions after treatment of uncertainties, matter calibration, survey compression, and the stated physical assumptions. Even such a result would challenge a conjunction of assumptions: it would not uniquely establish a particular new field or interaction.

**Established here:** the conditional interval derivation; reproducible numerical compatibility checks against an actual released BAO likelihood; an explicit smooth non-phantom competitor.

**Not established here:** a novel natural law; a historically new physical mechanism; a detection of dynamical or phantom dark energy; a new independently verified prediction; a joint supernova/CMB analysis; historical priority or expert verification of the mathematical characterization.

The result therefore falls short of the mission's foundational-scientific-discovery criterion. It should be stored as a scoped mathematical/empirical investigation, not as confirmed new physics.
