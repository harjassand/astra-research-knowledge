# Shared-occupancy Hill channels: an FPT tractability island

## Result and boundary

Consider a reaction module with $d$ concentration variables and $m$ labelled channels. Channel $e$ has jump $\nu_e\in\mathbb Q^d$ and rate

\[
  \rho_e(x)=\frac{k_e x^{y_e}}{Q(x)},\qquad
  k_e>0,\quad y_e\in\mathbb Q_{\geq 0}^d,
\]

where **one and the same** finite positive generalized polynomial
\[
  Q(x)=\sum_{\beta\in B}q_\beta x^\beta,
  \qquad B\subset\mathbb Q^d\text{ finite nonempty},\quad q_\beta>0,
\]
is used by every channel. A canonical low-regulator instance is
\(Q(x)=1+\sum_{j=1}^q a_j x_{i_j}^{h_j}\), with positive rational
\(a_j\) and positive integer Hill orders \(h_j\). The numerator is one
positive monomial per channel. All rate functions are smooth and strictly
positive on the positive orthant.

**The decision problem is fixed-parameter tractable in the number of labelled
channels $m$, even when the species dimension $d$ and the number $q$ of
regulators vary.** With rational data encoded in binary, one exact algorithm
uses at most $m3^m$ rational linear-program feasibility calls, each of
polynomial bit complexity in the explicit input length. The common denominator
is parsed once and never expanded. Thus a fixed-size competing-channel module
is polynomial-time decidable in its full input length; this does not give a
polynomial-time algorithm when $m$ is unrestricted.

This is an upper-bound result for a deliberately narrow but mechanistically
natural syntax. It is not a coNP-hardness result and does not imply that every
Michaelis--Menten, Hill, or modular enzyme network has shared denominators.

## Exact interface and theorem

For $r\in\mathbb R^d$, let
\[
  E_r=\{e:r\cdot\nu_e\ne0\},\qquad
  \tau_e(r)=\min_{\alpha\in\{y_e\}}r\cdot\alpha-
                      \min_{\beta\in B}r\cdot\beta.
\]
The property under study is vacuous if $E_r=\varnothing$; otherwise every
label minimizing \(\tau_e(r)\) over \(e\in E_r\) must have
\(r\cdot\nu_e>0\). This is the source-minimum form of the exact
positive-rational test in the C8 parent report. Its semantic rules are:

* remove neutral channels ($r\cdot\nu_e=0$) before taking a minimum;
* keep all ties among the remaining channels; one tied minimum with negative
  projected jump is a counterexample.

For every $r$ and channel $e$,
\[
  \tau_e(r)=r\cdot y_e-c_Q(r),\qquad
  c_Q(r):=\min_{\beta\in B}r\cdot\beta.
\]
The term $c_Q(r)$ is identical across channels. Therefore
\[
  \operatorname*{argmin}_{e\in E_r}\tau_e(r)
   =\operatorname*{argmin}_{e\in E_r}r\cdot y_e,
\]
including on every tie face. In particular, the answer is independent of the
coefficients and support of $Q$, once the common-denominator and positivity
conditions are met. It is exactly the ordinary monomial-source reaction-graph
test on the pairs $(y_e,\nu_e)$.

## FPT algorithm and proof

Enumerate sign vectors \(\sigma\in\{-1,0,+1\}^m\). For one such vector,
write \(J=\{f:\sigma_f\ne0\}\). For every $e\in J$ with
\(\sigma_e=-1\), ask whether the following rational linear system is
feasible, with variable $r\in\mathbb R^d$:

\[
\begin{array}{rl}
 r\cdot\nu_f&=0, && f\notin J,\\
 \sigma_f(r\cdot\nu_f)&\geq 1, && f\in J,\\
 r\cdot(y_e-y_f)&\leq 0, && f\in J.
\end{array}
\tag{LP(e,\sigma)}
\]

The first two rows fix exactly the neutral and active channels and their signs.
The last row says that $e$'s source is a minimum among active channels; it
uses weak inequalities intentionally, so cross-channel ties are included.

**Correctness.** If a real direction $r_0$ violates the test, let
\(J=E_{r_0}\), let \(\sigma_f\) be the sign of
\(r_0\cdot\nu_f\) on $J$, and select a negative minimum channel
$e$. Then the first and third rows hold at $r_0$. Since there are only
finitely many active channels, multiplying $r_0$ by a sufficiently large
positive scalar makes every nonzero projected jump have magnitude at least
one. This scaling preserves every homogeneous source comparison, so
\(LP(e,\sigma)\) is feasible. Conversely, any feasible solution has
exactly active set $J$, makes $e$ negative, and gives
\(r\cdot y_e\le r\cdot y_f\) for every active $f$. The shared-offset
identity then makes $e$ a minimum-$\tau$ negative channel, which is a genuine
failure. Thus a failure exists exactly when one of the enumerated LPs is
feasible. If $J=\varnothing$, there is no negative $e$ to test, which
implements the vacuous case.

The use of $\leq$ in the last row proves the universal tie rule: if a
negative channel ties any number of positive channels at the active minimum,
that negative channel itself produces a feasible LP and refutes the property.
The last row ranges only over $f\in J$, so a neutral channel with a smaller
source value is not allowed to affect the minimum.

There are $3^m$ sign vectors and at most $m$ candidate negative channels
for each one. Each LP has $d$ variables and at most $2m$ constraints,
with rational coefficients drawn from \(\nu_f\) and source differences
\(y_e-y_f\). Coordinates that occur in no jump or source can be deleted:
their direction components have zero coefficient in every constraint. Thus
even with a sparse encoding and a large binary-encoded ambient dimension, the
LP variable count is at most the number of source/jump coordinates listed in
the input. Exact rational LP feasibility is polynomial in its binary input
length. If $L$ is the total explicit input length, the total time is
\[
  O\!\left(m3^m\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\right).
\]
This is FPT in $m$ and polynomial in $L$ for each fixed $m$. It is not a
claim of FPT in dimension $d$, nor of polynomial time for variable $m$.

The proof also works for arbitrary positive sparse common $Q$, not only the
displayed Hill sum. For the explicit sparse encoding, reading and validating
the shared denominator costs at most its contribution to $L$; no support
product or coefficient multiplication is done. A denominator supplied as a
factored product is covered only when that factored syntax is itself the input
contract and the same expression is shared by all channels. Expanding a
product of $q$ binomials may create $2^q$ support terms and is not silently
charged as constant time. Kinetic constants $k_e,q_\beta$ are positive
rationals in the formal input; the decision ignores their values after their
strict positivity is certified. If constants are arbitrary measured reals
without an exact representation, this bit-complexity statement does not
apply.

## Exact tie and neutral audit

The replay file checks exact \(\mathbb Q\)-arithmetic examples, including:

1. **Cross-channel active tie:** at $r=(1,0)$, sources \((0,0)\) and
   \((0,1)\) tie; one channel points outward. The test must fail even though
   the other tied channel points inward.
2. **Lower neutral source:** at $r=(1,0)$, a neutral channel has smaller
   source projection than the only active channel, which points inward. The
   test must pass because the neutral channel is deleted before minimization.
3. **Shared denominator equivalence:** a nonconstant positive denominator
   gives the same outcome as comparing source projections, for every
   direction in a finite rational grid. This is a regression, while the
   shared-offset identity above proves the all-direction statement.
4. **Boundary counterexample:** the two-channel mass-action graph
   \(0\to1, 2\to1\) passes the one-dimensional source test. Replacing
   only the second denominator by \(1+x^{10}\) makes the negative-direction
   minimum outward and fails. Thus a per-channel denominator cannot be
   canceled, even when both denominators are smooth, positive Hill-type
   polynomials.

## Relation to the broad C8 result

The C8 broad input permits an independent positive numerator and denominator
support for every channel. The parent report establishes a polynomial-bit
rational counterdirection certificate, hence coNP membership, and a
polynomial-time recognition algorithm for every fixed species dimension. See
[`EXACT_RECOGNITION.md`](../../../../outputs/research/cycle_checkpoint_04/evidence/c8_implicit_rational_geometry/child_support/EXACT_RECOGNITION.md).

This child result identifies a different tractable parameter regime: the
ambient species dimension can vary, while the number of channels $m$ is the
parameter, provided every numerator is a single monomial and every channel
shares one strictly positive denominator. The shared denominator contributes
no ordering complexity at all, and the monomial restriction removes all
within-numerator minimizer branches. When denominators differ by channel, or
numerators have multiple support terms, this proof no longer gives the stated
FPT bound. No coNP-hardness result is claimed for either boundary case.

The kinetic syntax is a scoped occupancy/competition model rather than all
enzymatic kinetics. Liebermeister, Uhlendorf, and Klipp describe modular
enzymatic laws as rational polynomial expressions whose denominator terms
represent enzyme binding states, and their family includes Michaelis--Menten
and reversible Hill forms as special cases. Their denominators can depend on
the reaction; a shared partition function for alternative catalytic exits is
the narrower mechanistic motivation for the common-$Q$ subclass here.
Extending the theorem to reaction-specific partition functions would be a
distinct problem.
See Liebermeister, Uhlendorf, and Klipp, “Modular rate laws for enzymatic
reactions: thermodynamics, elasticities and implementation,” *Bioinformatics*
26(12), 1528–1534 (2010), [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btq141](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq141), especially §2.1.

## Verification status

The mathematical all-direction result is proved by the shared-offset identity
and the branch/LP equivalence. The replay is an exact finite regression for
the intended tie, neutral, common-denominator, and boundary cases. It is not
used as a proof of the complexity theorem or as an empirical biological
validation. The result establishes tractability only under the stated syntax
and parameter; it leaves unrestricted-dimension, unrestricted-channel
complexity and hardness unresolved.
