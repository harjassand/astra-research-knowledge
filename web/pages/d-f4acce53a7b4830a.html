# Full-family scope audit for the complement-LCU image

**Decision: exact membership barrier; no full-list no-go for the complement-LCU image is established.** The repeated BJSW seed pair is forced by the BMVZ generator after a valid singleton-layer augmentation, but the published QMA-hard family in BMVZ Theorem 7.4 starts from its ABN-derived equitable Hamiltonian and does not specify that augmentation. Moreover, the available seed obstruction concerns other encodings/enforcers or the unencoded Hamiltonian matrix. It does not give a lower bound for arbitrary polynomial-row positive enforcers of the complement-LCU image. The arbitrary-enforcer gate for this $V$ remains **UNKNOWN**.

## 1. The exact BMVZ generator and what it guarantees

BMVZ Definition 1.6 starts with

\[
 H=\sum_{\chi=1}^{g}w_\chi H_\chi,\qquad
 H_\chi=\frac1{m_\chi}\sum_{i=1}^{m_\chi}\Pi_i^\chi,
\]

where each layer consists of commuting projections, $w_\chi>0$, and
\(\sum_\chi w_\chi=1\). For $m_\chi>m_0$, their construction uses a
specified constant-degree expander and lists every length-$t$ walk
\(f\); the generated clause is

\[
 h_{\chi,f}=I-\bigotimes_{a=1}^{t}(I-\Pi_{f(a)}^\chi).
\]

For $m_\chi\le m_0$, including $m_\chi=1$, the paper says to handle the
layer by “trivially repeating” its clauses. Thus a singleton layer really
does yield the repeated clause; no self-loop or guessed expander edge is
needed. If its sole term is $P_v=|v\rangle\langle v|$, then after $\ell$
iterations with $2t$ copies per round its clause is exactly

\[
 G_{v,R}=I-(I-P_v)^{\otimes R},\qquad R=(2t)^\ell.
\]

This follows inductively from
\(I-G_{v,r}=(I-P_v)^{\otimes r}\). The path-list convention may leave
multiple labels for identical singleton clauses; their aggregate layer
weight remains $w_\chi$. Nothing below relies on treating duplicate
labels as distinct or on merging them, since merging can change the chosen
LCU isometry even when it preserves $H$.

The iteration used for the hard family has constant $t$,
\(\ell=\lceil6\log p(n)/\log t\rceil\), polynomially many output terms,
polynomially many output qubits, and polynomial $R$ (BMVZ Theorem 7.3).
These are term-generator and size facts, not row-sparsity facts. BMVZ
explicitly leave the conversion of their structured high-locality terms to
efficient sparse-matrix access open in the sparse-QPCP discussion.

## 2. Seed layers: a valid hard enlargement, not established membership in the fixed image

Let $H_0=\sum_\chi w_\chi H_\chi$ be a BMVZ-admissible QMA-hard input.
Append a fresh five-qubit block and the two BJSW rank-one projections
\(P_\phi=|\phi_5\rangle\langle\phi_5|\) and
\(P_\psi=|\psi_5\rangle\langle\psi_5|\) as separate singleton layers:

\[
 H_+=\frac12 H_0\otimes I
       +\frac14P_\phi+\frac14P_\psi,
\quad
 \phi_5=(|00010\rangle-|10010\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\quad
 \psi_5=(|00010\rangle-|01110\rangle-|10110\rangle-|11010\rangle)/2.
\]

The old layer weights become $w_\chi/2$; the new weights are $1/4$ and
$1/4$. The seed vectors are linearly independent, so their joint
orthogonal complement has dimension 30. Since the base and seed operators
act on disjoint registers,

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H_+)=\tfrac12\lambda_{\min}(H_0).
\]

Thus this is a polynomial-time, hardness-preserving augmentation. It remains
5-local at the start, has a constant number of commuting layers with minimum
weight bounded below by a constant, and adds only two incidences to the new
seed block. BMVZ’s singleton rule then puts both $G_{\phi,R}$ and
$G_{\psi,R}$ into the amplified term list with aggregate layer weights
$1/4$ each. The construction is internally proved and SOURCE-REPORTED as
an admissible hard enlargement; it is not an occurrence claim about the
unmodified ABN term table.

The distinction is concrete. BMVZ Theorem 7.4 says its hard starting family
is the equitable local-Hamiltonian family from Corollary 8.4. Claim 8.1
attributes that family to the ABN circuit-to-Hamiltonian construction and
records 5-local unweighted projections with at most seven terms per qubit;
the conflict graph has degree at most 35 and is equitably colored into
constantly many commuting layers. Neither that theorem nor the stated
construction identifies the two displayed BJSW vectors as terms on a fresh
common five-qubit block in two singleton colors. BJSW supplies these local
Clifford seed types in its propagation decomposition, but that fact alone
does not establish their occurrence in the particular ABN-derived term
table used by BMVZ.

Accordingly, let \(\mathcal I_{\rm fixed}\) denote the image of the specific
published map “ABN starting instance, then BMVZ iteration,” and let
\(\mathcal I_{\rm legal}\) denote BMVZ outputs from every input satisfying
Assumption 7.1. The appended-seed family is in \(\mathcal I_{\rm legal}\)
and is the image of a modified QMA reduction, but the available source
evidence does **not** prove it lies in \(\mathcal I_{\rm fixed}\). A claim
quantified over every admissible BMVZ starting instance must handle it; a
claim only about the fixed published reduction image cannot use this
augmentation without an additional membership proof.

## 3. What an easy frustration-free witness does in this LCU

For the complement-LCU construction, let $H=\sum_j\pi_jh_j$, with
$\pi_j>0$, and $R_j=I-2h_j$. If $H|g\rangle=0$, positivity gives

\[
 0=\langle g|H|g\rangle
   =\sum_j\pi_j\|h_j|g\rangle\|^2,
 \qquad h_j|g\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }j.
\]

Hence every selected reflection fixes $g$: $R_j|g\rangle=|g\rangle$.
With the route’s $A|0\rangle=|\chi\rangle$,

\[
 V|g\rangle=U(|0\rangle|g\rangle)
  =(A^*\otimes I)\,\mathrm{SELECT}(|\chi\rangle|g\rangle)
  =(A^*|\chi\rangle)|g\rangle=|0\rangle|g\rangle.
\]

So an exact frustration-free state is embedded entirely in the zero label.
If $g$ is a computational-basis vector, that physical basis vector lies in
\(\operatorname{ran}(V)\), and every exact-kernel enforcer $C$ has a zero
row and column there. This is the opposite of a dense-row witness; it gives
no lower bound on the other rows or on the gap of a sparse $C$.

For the appended seed pair, $|00000\rangle$ is orthogonal to both seed
vectors. Thus if the base instance is itself exactly frustration-free,
tensoring any base ground state with $|00000\rangle$ gives an exact
frustration-free state of $H_+$. A product of these states across the $R$
repetition blocks is annihilated by every generated clause. But BMVZ’s hard
promise and Theorem 7.4 require negligible yes energy, not exact zero; the
general canonical family need not have this exact kernel. BJSW Theorem 2
likewise uses acceptance $1-2^{-p(n)}$, not a perfect-completeness promise.
An easy YES instance with an exact witness, where one is supplied, remains a
special instance and cannot be promoted to a familywide kernel statement.

For a merely low-energy unit vector $g$, with
\(\langle g|H|g\rangle\le\epsilon\), the label-zero block is
\(K_0|g\rangle=(I-H)|g\rangle\), and
\[
 \sum_{a\ne0}\|K_a|g\rangle\|^2
   =\langle g|(2H-H^2)|g\rangle\le2\epsilon.
\]
This places the LCU output near the zero-label sector, but still supplies
neither an exact image relation nor a row-support lower bound.

## 4. No seed-based full-list obstruction for this $V$

For this route the canonical enforcer is
\(C_0=UBU^*=I-VV^*\). Its entries are computable by the physical-basis
formula in `00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md`, summing all term pairs
\(R_jR_k^*\). With $M=\mathrm{poly}(n)$ labels and $R=\mathrm{poly}(n)$
repeated factors, the stated safe row upper bound is
\(O(M^3 2^{10R})\), while the only general lower bound there is 1. Entry
evaluation takes polynomial arithmetic in $M,R$ (and requested precision)
because each factor pair acts on at most $2k$ local qubits. Neither entry
evaluation nor the exponential upper envelope proves a dense row: the sum
involves cancellations.

The seed-pair audit establishes (i) large computational-basis rows of the
**unencoded amplified Hamiltonian**, (ii) a lower bound for a bare two-branch
seed isometry, and (iii) bounds for named term-frame/star or active-flag
images. Those are distinct objects. Here the output row is a sum of
\(R_jR_k^*\) contributions after the label preparation, and a proposed
arbitrary $C$ need not equal $I-VV^*$, a star, or a graph Laplacian.
No cancellation-resistant row-mass or sparse-relation theorem for this
exact LCU frame is supplied by the seed calculation. Thus:

* **REFUTED:** using the seed singleton occurrence alone as a proof that the
  canonical ABN-to-BMVZ reduction image contains the obstruction.
* **UNKNOWN:** whether $C_0$ has polynomial row support for the full
  generated LCU list.
* **UNKNOWN:** whether any other positive polynomial-row $C$ exists for
  this exact $V$, with $\ker C=\operatorname{ran}(V)$ and
  $C\ge\gamma(I-VV^*)$ for constant $\gamma$.

A positive resolution must give the full row-list algorithm, coefficient
precision, and all-state gap for the exact LCU image. A no-go must lower-bound
arbitrary positive couplings among all exact row relations of that image;
the active-flag or direct-frame theorem cannot be transferred without such
a derivation.

## 5. Conditional verifier consequence if the gate closes

There is a concrete consequence, distinct from the coRE compiler issue. Let
the BMVZ canonical family have YES ground energy at most \(\varepsilon(n)\)
and NO ground energy at least $c>0$. Suppose a uniform construction gives
the complement-LCU $V,B$, and a positive contraction $C$ with

\[
 \ker C=\operatorname{ran}(V),\quad C\ge\gamma(I-VV^*),\quad
 \alpha H\le V^*BV\le\beta H,
\]

for input-independent \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\), with polynomial row
support, row enumeration, and polynomial-bit (or sufficiently precise)
entries. In the present LCU, $B$ is label-only, has one nonzero per row,
and uses $q=O(\log M)$ label qubits. Set \(\tau=\gamma/8\) and
\(K=(C+\tau B)/(1+\tau)\). Then $K$ is a positive contraction on
\(N+q=\mathrm{poly}(n)\) qubits and its row support is at most
\(s_C+1=\mathrm{poly}(n)\).

For completeness, before the harmless normalization $1+\tau$, take any
unit vector $y=Vx+z$, $z\perp\operatorname{ran}(V)$, and set
$t=\|z\|$. Positivity and the reverse triangle inequality imply

\[
 \langle y|(C+\tau B)|y\rangle
 \ge \gamma t^2+
 \tau\big(\max\{0,\|B^{1/2}Vx\|-t\}\big)^2.
\]

In the NO case, with $r=\min\{\alpha c,1\}>0$, one has
$\|B^{1/2}Vx\|^2\ge r\|x\|^2$. If $t^2\ge r/16$, the first
term is at least \(\gamma r/16\). Otherwise \(\|x\|^2>15/16\),
\(\|B^{1/2}Vx\|\ge\sqrt{15r/16}\), and $t<\sqrt r/4$, so the
second term is at least \(\tau r/2=\gamma r/16\). Thus
$\lambda_{\min}(K)\ge\gamma r/[16(1+\tau)]$. In the YES case, a
ground-state witness encoded as $V|\psi\rangle$ has energy at most
$\tau\beta\varepsilon(n)/(1+\tau)$. Therefore the gate would turn the
canonical constant-promise-gap family into a polynomially sparse
constant-promise-gap positive Hamiltonian family, provided the row and
entry access are uniform and efficiently produced. This directly addresses
the sparse-matrix conversion bottleneck BMVZ identify. It does not yield a
constant-local Hamiltonian or a constant row degree; those stronger claims
would need separate results.

**Falsifiers / next decisive tests.** The membership barrier would be overcome by an explicit canonical ABN-derived input and its published term list containing the two required singleton seed layers (or an exact alternate construction proving their membership). The LCU no-go would require an exponential lower bound for arbitrary sparse row relations of the exact $K_a$-frame after all label-pair cancellations. The positive route would be resolved by a uniform polynomial-row $C$ construction with the stated exact kernel and constant full-space gap. Until one of these is supplied, the LCU gate remains open.
