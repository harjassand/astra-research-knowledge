# Ordinary common-lift route: affirmative narrow audit

Assume the rational Hodge conjecture for arbitrary finite products of complex projective K3 surfaces. The proposed common-embedding argument transfers it to rational Tate for arbitrary finite products of ordinary finite-field K3 surfaces. It avoids the separate universal KS-algebraicity and Hodge-CM premises used by the broader all-height transfer.

Primary source: J.-D. Yu, Special Lifts of Ordinary K3 Surfaces and Applications, Lemma 1.6, Lemma 2.2 and its proof, Lemma 2.3, Theorem 2.5. https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~jdyu/Math/clK3.pdf . The paper's rational statements have no explicit restriction on p; later integral Torelli statements separately impose p>3. Current Taelman Theorem 3.1 also restates the rational lifted Frobenius result: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/crelle-2018-0023/html .

Fix one common finite field F_q (q=p^a), its Witt fraction field K and one embedding Kbar -> C. Take each canonical lift S_i over W(F_q), or quasi-canonical lifts after common finite extension. Yu gives rational semisimple lifted Frobenius F_i on the untwisted H^2(S_(i,C),Q), compatibly with special-fiber untwisted l-adic Frobenius and crystalline Frobenius, and F_i is a Hodge endomorphism. On H_i=H^2(S_(i,C),Q(1)) use u_i=F_i/q, which matches Frobenius on the twisted special-fiber cohomology. Passing to a common positive Frobenius power is harmless if necessary.

Let D be the Zariski closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by the common tuple u=(u_1,...,u_s) in product GL(H_i). Diagonalize simultaneously over Qbar. D is a group of multiplicative type and its identity component is a torus. Its character relations are the multiplicative relations among the tuple's eigenvalues. Apply the SAME p-adic valuation, normalized by v(q)=1, to those eigenvalues. Every multiplicative relation is killed by v, so the vector of valuations defines nu in X_*(D_Qbar) tensor Q (and after taking a common power one can work in D^0).

The identification with the Hodge cocharacter is genuine, rather than an inference from equal dimensions. Yu Lemma 2.2's proof explicitly says that Fil^1 H_i,C is the unique one-dimensional eigenspace of F_i with positive p-adic valuation. On H_i its normalized eigenvalue has valuation +1. Because F_i is Hodge, the complex-conjugate eigenspace is H^{-1,1}; the Weil relation identifies its normalized eigenvalue with the inverse, of valuation -1. Every other eigenspace has valuation 0 and lies in H^{0,0}. Thus the simultaneous Hodge cocharacter mu has exactly the weights of nu, up to the harmless global sign convention for cocharacters. In particular mu lies in D_C. Complex conjugation of mu also lies in D_C since D is defined over Q. Consequently every rational D-invariant tensor is a Hodge tensor. This supplies the joint containment that one CANNOT infer merely from Yu Theorem 2.5 separately for each factor.

Rational Tate-space identification: using Kunneth and normalized H^0, H^2(1), H^4(2), each cohomological summand of H^{2r}(product S_i,Q_l(r)) is the scalar extension of a rational tensor construction in the H_i. The Frobenius is the scalar extension of a rational matrix U. Therefore

    ker(U_l - 1) = ker(U_Q - 1) tensor_Q Q_l.

The rational kernel is precisely the D-invariant subspace. It has a rational basis beta_j, and mu containment makes every beta_j a rational Hodge class on the complex product of the chosen canonical lifts. Assumed Hodge-products algebraicity produces cycles representing all beta_j. Then every l-adic Tate class is a Q_l-linear combination of their classes.

For specialization, the canonical lifts need not be globally defined over a number field. The fixed ambient generic product is over K, and its complex algebraic classes have representatives over Kbar by Hilbert-scheme same-class specialization in the constant ambient variety. These representatives descend to some finite extension of K. Close and specialize in the smooth proper canonical-lift model after that finite base change. Smooth proper base change preserves the l-adic classes. Normalized trace returns cycles to the original finite field. Thus the rational-space argument supplies actual cycles, not just an identification after extending scalars to C.

The method's finite-height limitation is exact. At height h>1 the normalized nonzero Newton slopes are +/-1/h on h-dimensional blocks, whereas the Hodge cocharacter selects one-dimensional H^{1,-1} and H^{-1,1} lines with weights +/-1. The Newton cocharacter need not equal mu or force mu into the joint Frobenius torus. Ordinarity supplies this equality; finite height alone does not.

No new nonroutine gap found for the ordinary route. The conditional Hodge-products premise remains load-bearing. Rational and all-codimension conclusions permit the separate semisimplicity/strong-Tate-to-crystalline transfer described in AUDIT.md.
