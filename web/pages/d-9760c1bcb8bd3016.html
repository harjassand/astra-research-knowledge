# Exact boundary for nonlinear residual shears

Worker: hessian_bridge12, cycle13, 7 October 2026. This note is a programme deduction with named prior inputs, not a new HC4 counterexample or a literature-wide priority claim.

## Question and input interface

Can a polynomial nonlinear change of the two remaining dual coordinates in the published Meng–Yang construction yield a four-variable polynomial critical value with nonzero constant Hessian determinant **and a retained gradient collision**? The all-degree affine-dual descent was classified in cycle12 and is not searched again here.

Write the prior completed potential as

\[
 f_0=L t^2+a(x,y)t+N(y\rho_0+2x\eta_0),\qquad
 a=M+N\beta,\quad \beta=y^2(6x^2y^2+15xy+4),\quad LN\ne0.
\]

This is the actual polynomial coordinate expression obtained from the prior determinant-one completion of \(A=(1+xy)^3p+3x(1+xy)^2q-x^3r\) and the prior toric normalizer. It is not a claim that a nonlinear source change preserves gradient collisions. Cycle12 records the complete matrices and primary provenance. Meng–Yang v1 uses \((L,M,N)=(1,11,2)\), v2 uses \((1,13,2)\). The degree14 HC5 construction and general Appendix A bridge are already published.

For a chosen constant critical level \(\sigma\), the solution

\[
 t_*=(\sigma-a)/(2L)
\]

is polynomial. A base translation can remove the critical-value base term:

\[
 D_0=\frac{(\sigma-a)^2-(\sigma-M)^2}{4LNy}
 =\frac{y(6x^2y^2+15xy+4)[-2(\sigma-M)+Ny^2(6x^2y^2+15xy+4)]}{4L}.
\]

The displayed quotient is a polynomial identity, not a restriction to \(y\ne0\). If \(\rho_0=P_1+D_0\), \(\eta_0=P_2\), with \(P_1,P_2\) independent of \(t\), then

\[
 (f_0\circ\Phi-\sigma t)|_{t=t_*}
 =N(yP_1+2xP_2)-(\sigma-M)^2/(4L).
\]

The general theorem below concerns this **base-cancelled** reduction. An arbitrary additional base potential \(h(x,y)\), or coefficients depending on \(y\) or \(t\), is outside that theorem. Constant Hessian determinant at this chosen critical level does not by itself establish a constant determinant for the whole transformed five-variable potential or all critical levels.

## Full Hessian chain rule

For every polynomial source change \(\Phi\),

\[
 H(f_0\circ\Phi)=J_\Phi^THf_0(\Phi)J_\Phi
 +\sum_i(\partial_i f_0)(\Phi)H\Phi_i.
\]

For base and \(t\) fixed, \(\rho_0=P_1+D_0\), \(\eta_0=P_2\), the correction is

\[
 NyH(P_1+D_0)+2NxHP_2.
\]

Its terms are essential even when \(\det J_\Phi=1\). A triangular example \(\rho_0=\rho+\eta^2\), \(\eta_0=\eta\) already adds the nonzero entry \(2Ny\) in the \(\eta\eta\) position. The exact script uses these terms throughout.

## All triangular shears stay in a solved class

Let \(\rho_0=\rho+S(x,y,t,\eta)\), with the other four coordinates fixed. Then

\[
 \partial_{tt}(f_0\circ\Phi)=2L+NyS_{tt}.
\]

A globally constant scalar pivot must equal \(2L\), by evaluation at \(y=0\), and therefore \(S_{tt}=0\). Thus \(S=tC(x,y,\eta)+D(x,y,\eta)\), the critical solution is polynomial, and its critical value has the form

\[
 \psi=Ny\rho+H(x,y,\eta),\qquad
 \det H\psi=N^2(H_{x\eta}^2-H_{xx}H_{\eta\eta}).
\]

More generally, any such triangular shear with any polynomial critical solution still gives this form. If the determinant is a nonzero constant, the gradient first recovers \(y\) through \(\psi_\rho=Ny\), then \((x,\eta)\) by the established two-variable Hessian conjecture at that fixed \(y\), and finally \(\rho\) through \(\psi_y\). Hence it is injective. This is an application of prior HC2, already a special case of Ni Proposition6.1 with affine coefficient; the coordinate-class selection is our deduction, not a new theorem about HC4.

For the explicit nonlinear \(S=\eta^2+D_0\),

\[
 \psi=Ny\rho+2Nx\eta+Ny\eta^2-(\sigma-M)^2/(4L),\qquad \det H\psi=4N^4.
\]

The gradient has the direct inverse, for outputs \((u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)\) in source order \((x,y,\rho,\eta)\),

\[
 \eta=\frac{u_1}{2N},\quad y=\frac{u_3}{N},\quad
 \rho=\frac{u_2}{N}-\eta^2,\quad x=\frac{u_4}{2N}-y\eta.
\]

At v2 parameters and \(\sigma=-19/2\), the published colliding points \((1,-3/2,0,0,0)\), \((-1,3/2,0,0,0)\) in \((x,y,p,q,r)\) both lie on the selected critical sheet after this change. Their reduced sources are \((1,-3/2,-675/16,0)\), \((-1,3/2,675/16,0)\), but their gradients are \((0,-675/8,-3,4)\), \((0,675/8,3,-4)\). Polynomial critical solution and constant determinant pass; retained collision fails. The whole normalization/reduction changes gradient equations, so this does not attribute collision loss to the final shear alone.

## An all-degree two-stage nonlinear class is also closed

Let \(F\in\mathbb C[x,\eta]\) and \(G\in\mathbb C[x,s]\) be **arbitrary polynomials**, with no degree bound. Consider two alternating fibre shears

\[
 P_1=\rho+F(x,\eta),\qquad
 P_2=\eta+G(x,P_1),\qquad
 \psi=N(yP_1+2xP_2)+\text{constant},\quad N\ne0.
\]

The fibre map is a polynomial automorphism with determinant one. Every constant-Hessian member of this class has an explicit polynomial gradient inverse. Thus no collision of any kind can survive within this class.

### Determinant identity from the actual output equations

Set

\[
 s=P_1,\qquad v=y+2xG_s(x,s).
\]

The map \((x,y,\rho,\eta)\mapsto(x,\eta,s,v)\) is a polynomial automorphism of constant Jacobian \(-1\). Divide the gradient outputs by \(N\), and write them in source order as \((h,s,v,k)\). Direct differentiation, including the full nonlinear composition terms, gives

\[
 h=F_xv+2\eta+2(G+xG_x),\qquad k=F_\eta v+2x.
\]

Holding \((s,v)\) fixed reduces the determinant calculation to this two-by-two Jacobian. With \(T=2G_x+xG_{xx}\),

\[
 \boxed{\frac{\det H\psi}{N^4}
 =4+(4F_{x\eta}-2F_{\eta\eta}T)v
 -(F_{xx}F_{\eta\eta}-F_{x\eta}^2)v^2.}
\]

Here \(G\)'s derivatives are taken in independent variables \((x,s)\). Since \(v\) is a free polynomial coordinate, a constant determinant forces exactly

\[
 \det H_{x,\eta}F=0,\qquad
 2F_{x\eta}=F_{\eta\eta}(2G_x+xG_{xx}).
\]

Conversely these two identities make the determinant \(4N^4\), so the constant is automatically nonzero.

### Complete polynomial classification and inverses

Use the established binary singular-Hessian theorem: after subtracting affine terms, a polynomial in two variables with zero Hessian determinant is a polynomial in a linear form. The precise primary statement is de Bondt arXiv1203.6605v3 Theorem2.4(i), whose proof cites the earlier de Bondt–van den Essen theorem; this prior result is not proved by our identity script.

If \(F_{\eta\eta}=0\), write \(F=A(x)\eta+B(x)\). The first determinant condition reads \(-A'(x)^2=0\), hence

\[
 F=f(x)+a\eta,\qquad G\text{ arbitrary in }\mathbb C[x,s],\qquad a\in\mathbb C.
\]

For arbitrary divided outputs \((h,s,v,k)\), recover

\[
 x=(k-av)/2,\quad
 \eta=[h-f'(x)v]/2-G(x,s)-xG_x(x,s),
\]
\[
 \rho=s-f(x)-a\eta,\qquad y=v-2xG_s(x,s).
\]

If \(F_{\eta\eta}\ne0\), the prior theorem and a nonzero coefficient of \(\eta\) in its linear form give

\[
 F=f(cx+\eta)+\ell x+a\eta+d,
 \qquad f''\ne0,\quad c,\ell,a,d\in\mathbb C.
\]

Cancel the nonzero polynomial \(f''\) in the second determinant condition. This gives \(2G_x+xG_{xx}=2c\). Writing \(G=\sum_j g_j(s)x^j\), its coefficient at \(x^{j-1}\) is \(j(j+1)g_j(s)\); characteristic zero forces

\[
 G=cx+g(s).
\]

There are no rational denominators depending on the source and no exception at a root of \(f'\). Set

\[
 z=[h-ck-(\ell-ca)v]/2-g(s).
\]

Then the polynomial inverse is

\[
 x=[k-(f'(z)+a)v]/2,\quad \eta=z-cx,
\]
\[
 \rho=s-f(z)-\ell x-a\eta-d,\qquad y=v-2xg'(s).
\]

Restoring \(N\) means \((h,s,v,k)=(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)/N\). Both substitutions are checked exactly with function-valued expressions, not a finite coefficient search. The binary theorem is a named external mathematical dependency; independent agent reconstruction is internal checking, not independent external validation.

The earlier quadratic-coefficient class \(F=a(x)\eta^2\), \(G=b(x)s^2\) is contained here. Its direct certificate yields \(a=0\) with arbitrary \(b(x)\), or both \(a,b\) constant. It remains useful as an independent elementary check without the prior binary theorem. The correct sparse determinant has the factor \(y+4xbP_1\); an earlier scratch formula with \(2xbP_1\) was wrong and was corrected before the passing certificate.

### An even earlier collision gate for the published witness

For the published v2 pair on \(\sigma=-19/2\), the prior normalized dual coordinates are all zero. Hence the inverse source change gives \(P_1=-D_0\), \(P_2=0\), whatever the polynomials \(F,G\). In the base-cancelled, \(y\)-independent class, \(\psi_y=NP_1=-ND_0\). Its values are \(-675/8\) and \(675/8\). Thus this particular published collision is already lost for **every** member of this class, even before checking the reduced Hessian. The full inverse theorem additionally excludes any other collision when the determinant is constant.

## Prior, downstream use, and missing step

Published inputs: Meng–Yang's degree14 HC5 counterexample and general Schur descent; the toric completion/normalizer in the pinned royvanrijn research repository; HC2 and the binary singular-Hessian classification. Our exact determinant identity, two-stage class closure and explicit collision gate are programme deductions. No literature-wide novelty search establishes their priority; they are not labelled a foundational breakthrough.

The practical result is an all-degree exclusion rule for a substantial nonlinear search class. It prevents treating a determinant-one fibre automorphism or a successful critical-value computation as collision-preserving. No unproved plane-JC statement is used to close this class.

The remaining falsifiable direction is a reduction that genuinely escapes these hypotheses: an additional alternating shear, coefficients depending on both base variables \((x,y)\) or eliminated \(t\), or a nonconstant base potential retained after descent. It must satisfy all three gates simultaneously: a polynomial critical solution, exact nonzero constant four-variable Hessian determinant including chain-rule corrections, and a pair of distinct reduced points with exactly equal gradients. These possibilities are unsearched here. Neither this result nor the all-degree constant-direction route proves HC4 or the normalized singleton-zero plane Keller problem.
