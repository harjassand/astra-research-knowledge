# Prime-dimensional Weyl line-constant comparator: proof record

## Claim

Let `d` be prime. Let `Φ` be a unital CPTP Weyl-covariant channel on `M_d` whose eigenvalue on every nonidentity Weyl operator in a maximal-isotropic line `L` is the same real number `λ_L`. Assume `Φ` has a CPTP two-output extension with both marginals `Φ`. Then there is an EB channel `Ψ` such that, on Hilbert–Schmidt space,

`I - Ψ ⪯ 2(I - Φ)`.

The claim allows signed `λ_L`. It does not include a general Weyl-diagonal channel with eigenvalues varying along a line.

## 1. Channel and Choi parameters

Write `n=d+1` for the number of maximal-isotropic lines and `S=Σ_L λ_L`. For standard Weyl operators `W_x`, `x∈F_d²`, the generalized Pauli channel is a random-Weyl channel

`Φ(ρ)=Σ_g p_g W_g ρ W_g†`,

where the probability vector is the symplectic Fourier inverse of the eigenvalues. The line-constant assumption makes this inverse constant on each nonzero line. For `g=0`, the Fourier sum is

`p_0 = d^(-2) [1 + (d-1)S]`.

For nonzero `g` on line `L`, the character is identically one along `L`, while its sum over every other line is `-1`; hence

`p_g = d^(-2) [1 + d λ_L - S]`.

Complete positivity is equivalent to all these Weyl probabilities being nonnegative.

## 2. Two-extension overlap bound

Let `J_RBB'` be a Choi extension of `Φ`, symmetrized under `B↔B'`. For any maximally entangled rank-one projector `P` on `R⊗B`, write `P_1=P_RB⊗I_B'` and `P_2=P_RB'⊗I_B`. Direct contraction of the two maximally entangled vectors gives

`P_1 P_2 P_1 = d^(-2) P_1`,

so `||P_1P_2||=1/d`. For two projections this implies `||P_1+P_2||=1+1/d`. The two marginals of `J_RBB'` equal the Choi state `J_Φ`; therefore, for every Bell projector `P_g`,

`2 p_g = Tr[J_RBB' (P_{g,1}+P_{g,2})] ≤ 1+1/d`,

and consequently `p_g≤(d+1)/(2d)`.

## 3. Mass of clipped excess eigenvalues

Set

`ν_L=(2λ_L-1)_+`, `A={L:λ_L>1/2}`, and `k=|A|`.

If `k=0`, the sum of the `ν_L` is zero. If `1≤k<n`, let `m=min_{L∉A} λ_L`, so `m≤1/2`. Nonnegativity of the Bell probability on a line attaining `m` gives

`S≤1+dm`.

Every inactive line eigenvalue is at least `m`, so

`Σ_{L∈A} λ_L = S - Σ_{L∉A}λ_L ≤ 1+dm-(n-k)m = 1+(k-1)m ≤ (k+1)/2`.

Thus `Σ_Lν_L=2Σ_A λ_L-k≤1`.

If `k=n=d+1`, use the extension overlap bound for `p_0`:

`1+(d-1)S ≤ d(d+1)/2`,

which gives `S≤(d+2)/2` and then

`Σ_Lν_L = 2S-(d+1) ≤ 1`.

No nonnegativity assumption on the inactive eigenvalues was used.

## 4. Construct the EB comparator

For each maximal-isotropic line `L`, let `Δ_L` be complete dephasing in its joint eigenbasis/MUB basis. It is an EB channel; in Weyl coordinates it has eigenvalue one on `L` and zero on every other nonidentity line. Let `D(ρ)=Tr(ρ)I/d`. Define

`Ψ = (1-Σ_Lν_L)D + Σ_Lν_L Δ_L`.

The coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, so `Ψ` is EB, unital and trace preserving. Its eigenvalue on line `L` is `μ_L=ν_L`. If `λ_L>1/2`, then `1-μ_L=2(1-λ_L)`; if `λ_L≤1/2`, then `μ_L=0` and `1≤2(1-λ_L)`. Both maps fix the identity. Since the Weyl basis is an orthonormal Hilbert–Schmidt eigenbasis, these coordinate inequalities prove the claimed superoperator order.

## 5. Sharpness and scope

The universal `1→2` qudit cloner has a depolarizing marginal with `λ=(d+2)/(2(d+1))`. Its EB Haar measure-and-prepare comparator has eigenvalue `1/(d+1)`, and equality holds in `1-μ=2(1-λ)`. Thus no smaller universal constant can work even in this class.

The proof requires: prime-dimensional Weyl phase space, real line-constant spectrum, a CPTP self-extension, and supplied channel parameters. It is exact algebra and does not depend on a numerical search. It does not establish the N93 arbitrary-channel operator lemma or the bounded-Holevo approximate-broadcasting-to-EB theorem. Priority is unverified.

## 6. Positive-spectrum product closure

Suppose finitely many factors have simultaneous eigenvalues `λ_i∈[0,1]` and EB clipping eigenvalues `μ_i=(2λ_i-1)_+`. For a joint tensor-product or commuting-composition mode, the composite eigenvalues are `Λ=Π_i λ_i` and `M=Π_i μ_i`.

If some `λ_i≤1/2`, then `M=0` and `Λ≤1/2`, so `1-M=1≤2(1-Λ)`. If every `λ_i>1/2`, set `x_i=2λ_i-1=μ_i∈[0,1]`. The inequality

`Π_i(1+x_i) ≤ 2^(r-1)(1+Π_i x_i)`

holds for `r` factors. It is equality for `r=1`; induction from `r` to `r+1` uses `(1+x)(1+q)≤2(1+xq)`, equivalent to `(1-x)(1-q)≥0`. Since `2λ_i=1+x_i`, this becomes `2Π_iλ_i≤1+Π_iμ_i`, equivalently `1-M≤2(1-Λ)`.

The tensor product/composition of EB comparators is EB. This proves factor-two closure under the stated positive-spectrum, simultaneous-eigenbasis conditions. It does not establish closure for signed spectra or noncommuting compositions.
