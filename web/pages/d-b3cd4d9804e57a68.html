\begin{abstract}
We derive a cost-preserving spectral rounding construction for rational points of Boolean polytopes whose actual edges change at most $k$ coordinates. The algorithm returns an exact rational mixture of at most $n+1$ feasible vertices, with the specified fractional point as its mean and a common discrepancy bound
\[
 16R^2+2K+8k+1\leq1161k,
 \qquad K=64k,\quad R=\lfloor\sqrt K\rfloor.
\]
Together with the actual-edge midpoint lower bound $k/2$, this characterizes the worst exact-marginal radius up to a universal factor. Matroid bases have $k\leq2$; their geometric operations are implemented using an independence oracle and exact submodular minimization. General Boolean polytopes require an exact strong optimization oracle. The construction combines an amplified energy barrier, an operator-norm treatment of the spectral background, a retuned dimension reserve, uniform strict curvature, exact cost-slice repair, bounded numerical state, and extraction of a finite convex-hull certificate from a favorable-cost oracle. It implies the originally targeted conservative bound $2^{16}k^2$.

\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, interface, and scope}
\label{sec:statement}

A \emph{Boolean polytope} is $P=\conv V\subseteq[0,1]^n$ for a finite $V\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$. Every member of $V$ is a vertex: a convex combination producing a Boolean coordinate must put that coordinate's value on every supported point. For a nonsingleton $P$, define its actual-edge support parameter by
\[
 k(P)=\max\{|\supp(u-v)|:[u,v]\text{ is an edge of }P\}.
\]
The parameter refers to the polytope's actual edges, rather than a selected graph of permitted local moves. A supplied integer $k\geq1$ may be any upper bound for $k(P)$.
If $P$ is a singleton, or if the promised point $y$ is Boolean, its one-atom law has discrepancy zero and is returned immediately. The formulas below concern the remaining case, in which $n\geq1$ and $k\geq1$.

All rational numbers are explicitly encoded in binary. Polynomial complexity means polynomial in $m,n$ and the total rational input length, including any queried cost vector; oracle calls and their input lengths are charged. A supplied binary upper bound $k$ is capped at $n$, since every Boolean edge has support at most $n$. In the general-polytope model an \emph{exact strong optimization oracle} takes every rational objective and returns its exact optimum and an optimizing Boolean vertex of $P$. Standard exact rational optimization--separation and linear-programming reductions for well-described polytopes are used as subroutines. We give the necessary Boolean coefficient bounds and the reduction to the face operations below. In the matroid model the only input oracle is independence.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:params}
 K=64k,\quad R=\lfloor\sqrt K\rfloor,\quad H=32R^2,\quad
 \eta=\frac1{Kn},\quad \beta=\frac1{2R},
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
 B(k)=\frac H2+2K+8k+1=16R^2+2K+8k+1\leq1161k.
\end{equation}
The constant is deliberately conservative. The large Gram prefactor and the spectral threshold eight permit this height without sacrificing the direction-space reserve.

\begin{theorem}[Constructive exact-marginal rounding]\label{thm:main}
Let $A\in\Q^{m\times n}$ have column Euclidean norms at most one and let $y\in P\cap\Q^n$.
Under the general-polytope oracle model just specified, there is a deterministic polynomial-bit construction of vertices $b^1,\ldots,b^r\in V$, $r\leq n+1$, and rational weights $\lambda_\ell\geq0$, satisfying
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^r\lambda_\ell=1,\qquad
 \sum_{\ell=1}^r\lambda_\ell b^\ell=y,\qquad
 \|A(b^\ell-y)\|_\infty<B(k)\quad(\lambda_\ell>0).
\]
Every supported vertex belongs to the minimal face $F(y)$, so $r\leq\dim F(y)+1$ is possible. Moreover $B(k)<2^{16}k^2$.

For a matroid on $n$ elements specified by an independence oracle, the same result holds for its base polytope using only polynomially many independence-oracle calls and polynomial bit operations. In this case $k=2$ may be used, giving the constant $B(2)=2209$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof organization]
Sections~\ref{sec:spectral}--\ref{sec:algorithm} construct, for every rational cost $c$, a vertex $b\in F(y)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost-oracle}
 c^T(b-y)\leq0,\qquad\|A(b-y)\|_\infty<B(k).
\end{equation}
The face and linear-programming operations used there are implemented in Section~\ref{sec:oracles}. Section~\ref{sec:marginals} converts this fixed-$y$ favorable-response oracle into the stated exact rational distribution, without assuming optimization over the good-vertex set. The final integration status is recorded in Section~\ref{sec:status}.

Since $R^2\leq K=64k$, \eqref{eq:bound} is at most $1024k+128k+8k+1\leq1161k<2^{16}k^2$. For $k=2$, $K=128$, $R=11$, and the explicit bound is $16\cdot121+256+16+1=2209$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Actual-edge lower bound]\label{prop:lower}
If $P$ has an edge $[u,v]$ of support $k$, there are a one-row matrix with column norms at most one and a point $y\in P$ such that every distribution on $V$ with exact mean $y$ has discrepancy $k/2$ on every supported outcome.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $y=(u+v)/2$, and take $a_j=\operatorname{sign}(u_j-v_j)$ on the edge support and zero elsewhere. Expose the edge by a linear functional. Its value at $y$ is the optimum; hence every vertex occurring with positive probability in an exact-mean distribution must lie on the exposed edge. Its only vertices are $u,v$. Their probabilities are each $1/2$, and
\[
 a^T(u-y)=\frac12\sum_j|u_j-v_j|=\frac k2,
 \qquad a^T(v-y)=-\frac k2.
\]
Each column of this one-row matrix has norm at most one.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Worst exact-marginal radius]
For a fixed Boolean polytope $P=\conv V$, let $\mathcal R(P)$ be the supremum, over all $m\geq1$, real $A\in\R^{m\times n}$ with column Euclidean norms at most one, and real $y\in P$, of the least $D$ for which a probability law on $V$ has mean $y$ and satisfies $\|A(b-y)\|_\infty\leq D$ on every supported vertex.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Real-input radius characterization]\label{cor:radius}
For every nonsingleton Boolean polytope,
\[
 \frac{k(P)}2\leq\mathcal R(P)\leq1161k(P).
\]
The upper law can always have support at most $n+1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}. For the upper bound fix real $A,y$ and use $k=k(P)$. Approximate $A$ by rational matrices with column norms at most one: rational coordinate approximants followed by a rational scaling toward zero yield such a convergent sequence. Approximate $y$ by rational convex combinations of the finitely many Boolean vertices. For each rational instance Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supplies a law on the finite set $V$. The probability simplex is compact, so a subsequence converges. Its exact mean tends to $y$, and its supported discrepancy inequalities are closed under the limit. Since there are finitely many possible supports of size at most $n+1$, choose a further subsequence with a fixed support. Its limit has support at most $n+1$ and discrepancy at most $1161k$. This is an existence extension; an algorithm for arbitrary unencoded real inputs is not asserted.
\end{proof}

\section{Literature boundary}
\label{sec:literature}

The source framework is Guo--Fang--Lu's \emph{Vector Balancing in Polynomial Time} \cite{gfl}. Their stated deterministic signing guarantee is $8272$, and their arithmetic operation count is an exact-real statement. This note rebuilds the spectral calculations needed here, retunes the row cutoff and dimension reserve for constrained face tangents, and separately derives the finite-bit continuation. The constrained conclusion is an adaptation; it is not a theorem quoted from that paper.

The checked September frontier materially changes ordinary signing benchmarks. Li \cite{li} states signing discrepancy below $99$, with polynomial rational-bit complexity and a faster spectral algorithm. Akbas--Sra, version~2 \cite{akbas}, state deterministic signing discrepancy below $37.54$ using approximately quartic field-operation complexity; their theorem does not establish polynomial bit length. Neither ordinary signing statement alone supplies an exact-marginal law supported on arbitrary matroid bases. A face-constrained direction space and a constructive exact-mean argument remain necessary.

Ercan \cite{ercan}, Section~5.1, gives hard-feature exact-marginal rounding for strongly base-orderable matroids via simultaneous complementary subset exchanges. Section~5.2 supplies Gaussian convex-order comparison for arbitrary integral base polyhedra. Gaussian comparison is a different guarantee from an outcome-wise dimension-independent feature bound. For example, independent fair selections in $r$ disjoint pairs have exact feasible marginals; the row $(1,-1,\ldots,1,-1)$ nevertheless has supported discrepancies $\pm r$. This observation concerns that law, rather than the existence of a differently correlated good law.

Ordinary basis exchange cannot be substituted for simultaneous subset exchange. In the graphic matroid of $K_4$, take trees $B=\{12,23,34\}$ and $D=\{13,14,24\}$. The only complementary symmetric-exchange partner of either $12$ or $34$ is $14$. Hall's condition therefore blocks a bijection even for simultaneous singleton exchanges, although every base-polytope edge changes two coordinates. Our argument uses the actual face tangent and does not assume that the tangent admits an independent exchange-cube representation.

B\'erczi--Liu--Reis--Tarnawski \cite{blrt} already give cost-preserving constrained discrepancy bounds linear in column sparsity. Cost preservation can imply an existential exact-mean decomposition by separation; the Euclidean-column hard bound and its finite-bit implementation are the substantive additional claims under examination here. The literature check is bounded, and no claim of priority or novelty is made.

\section{Geometry and the maintained state}
\label{sec:geometry}

Work in signed coordinates
\[
 \mathcal P=2P-\one,\qquad x_0=2y-\one.
\]
All moves preserve $c^Tx=c^Tx_0$. The current point $x$ belongs to the relative interior of its current minimal face $F$ of $\mathcal P$. Let $U$ be the coordinates varying on $F$ and $N=|U|$. Since every vertex is signed Boolean, these are exactly the coordinates for which $-1<x_j<1$. Frozen coordinates retain their exact signed values for the rest of the trajectory.

\begin{lemma}[Tangent dimension]\label{lem:dim}
If every actual edge of $\mathcal P$ has support at most $k$, then $\dim F\geq N/k$. Consequently
\[
 \dim\bigl(\lin(F-x)\cap c^\perp\bigr)\geq N/k-1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Edge directions of a polytope span its translation space: the edge graph is connected, and differences along an edge path express every vertex difference. Edges of a face are actual edges of the original polytope. Choose a basis of $\dim F$ such directions. Every varying coordinate occurs in some basis vector; otherwise it vanishes on their span and is constant on the entire face. The union of their supports therefore contains all $N$ varying coordinates and has size at most $k\dim F$. One additional linear equation loses at most one dimension.
\end{proof}

For each feature row $i$, retain a subset $T_i\subseteq U$ of coefficients and put
\[
 b_{ij}=a_{ij}\one_{j\in T_i},\quad
 p_{ij}=b_{ij}^2,\quad q_i=\sum_{j\in U}p_{ij},\quad
 G_i=\sum_{j\in U}p_{ij}(1-x_j^2).
\]
Store a rational tracked sum $d_i$, initially zero. While row data are fixed along a move, its continuation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracking-line}
 d_i(x+th)=d_i(x)+t b_i^Th.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the local offset $d_i-b_i^Tx$ is held fixed during derivative computation. It is \emph{not} necessary to persist that offset numerically. Deleting a coefficient leaves $d_i$ unchanged, absorbing its current value into the conceptual offset. Frozen deletions create no subsequent tracking error; deliberate deletions are charged in Section~\ref{sec:final}.

Repeatedly deliberately delete an entry with $p_{ij}>q_i/K$ until
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spread}
 \max_j p_{ij}\leq q_i/K.
\end{equation}
The column-norm assumption gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-mass}
 0\leq G_i\leq q_i,
 \quad\sum_iq_i\leq N,
 \quad\sum_i p_{ij}\leq1.
\end{equation}
A row is \emph{large} if $q_i>R^2$, \emph{medium} if $0<q_i\leq R^2$, and \emph{completed} if $q_i=0$. Large rows have $d_i=0$ and impose $b_i^Th=0$. At medium entry choose $r_i=R$ and then decrease it by factors two until
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scales}
 q_i\leq r_i^2<4q_i,\qquad r_i\leq R.
\end{equation}
After further deletions, continue these decreasing dyadic updates. Since a nonzero retained coefficient is an original input coefficient, the number and encoding length of the possible scales are polynomial in the original matrix's encoding length.

Let $e\geq0$ be a single global monotone numerical allowance, initially zero. For a medium row and $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$ define
\begin{align}
 u_{i\sigma}&=\frac{\sigma d_i-H}{r_i}
              +\frac{R G_i}{2r_i^2}+12R-e,\label{eq:barrier}\\
 g_{i\sigma}&=\frac{\sigma b_i}{r_i}
              -\frac{R(p_i\circ x)}{r_i^2},\qquad
 \nabla^2u_{i\sigma}=-\frac{R\diag(p_i)}{r_i^2}.
              \label{eq:barrier-derivatives}
\end{align}
The derivatives use \eqref{eq:tracking-line}. The invariant is $u_{i\sigma}\leq0$ for every medium pair. Protect every pair with $u_{i\sigma}\geq-1$ by the exact rational equation $g_{i\sigma}^Th=0$.

\section{The spectral potential and cleanup}
\label{sec:spectral}

Define, on the full $N$-coordinate active space,
\begin{align}
 w_{i\sigma}&=\frac{64K}{2r_i^4}\exp(\beta u_{i\sigma}),\qquad
 W=\sum_{i\text{ medium},\sigma}w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^T,\label{eq:W}\\
 B_{\rm lg}&=\sum_{i\text{ large}}\diag(p_i),\qquad
 M=W+B_{\rm lg}+\eta\one\one^T,\qquad L=\lambda_{\max}(M),\label{eq:M}\\
 \Phi&=(L-8)_+^3-\eta\|x\|_2^2.\label{eq:potential}
\end{align}
The norm in \eqref{eq:potential} always uses \emph{all ambient $n$ signed coordinates}, including frozen ones. The matrix uses active coordinates. Thus freezing leaves the regularizer unchanged, while taking a principal submatrix of $M$. Face equations constrain directions; they do not compress $M$ into a different spectral space.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform top gap]\label{lem:gap}
Whenever there are at least two active coordinates, the top eigenvalue of $M$ is simple, its unit top eigenvector can be chosen strictly positive, and its gap is at least $\eta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $M=M_0+\eta\one\one^T$. The positive semidefinite matrix $M_0$ is entrywise nonnegative. Replacing a maximizing Rayleigh vector by its coordinatewise absolute value gives a nonnegative unit top vector $v_0$. Since $\one^Tv_0\geq1$,
\[
 \lambda_1(M)\geq\lambda_1(M_0)+\eta.
\]
On $\one^\perp$ the rank-one perturbation vanishes; min--max therefore gives $\lambda_2(M)\leq\lambda_1(M_0)$. Every entry of $M$ is strictly positive. A nonnegative top vector $v$ is consequently strictly positive, because $Mv=Lv$ and a zero coordinate on the right would contradict positivity on the left. The gap implies simplicity and smoothness of the top eigenpair while row data are fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Medium entry and cleanup]\label{lem:cleanup}
The height in \eqref{eq:params} ensures that initialization has $L\leq1+1/K<2<8$ and $\Phi\leq0$. As long as $e\leq1/4$ and the medium barriers are nonpositive, cleanup cannot increase $L$ or $\Phi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A newly medium row has $d_i=0$, $G_i\leq r_i^2$, and $r_i\leq R$. For either sign,
\[
 \gamma_i:=2r_i^2w_{i\sigma}
 \leq \frac{64K}{r_i^2}
   \exp\left(-\frac{H}{2Rr_i}+\frac{25}4\right).
\]
The right side increases with $r_i$ on $(0,R]$: its logarithmic derivative is $-2/r_i+H/(2Rr_i^2)>0$. At $r_i=R$ and $H=32R^2$ it is
\[
 \frac{64K}{R^2}e^{-39/4}\leq128e^{-39/4}<1.
\]
Here $R^2\geq K/2$ for $K\geq64$. Also
\[
 u_{i\sigma}\leq-32R+12R+R/2=-39R/2<-1.
\]

Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 p_ip_i^T\preceq q_i\diag(p_i)\preceq r_i^2\diag(p_i).
\]
Summing the two signs yields
$\sum_\sigma w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^T\preceq\diag(p_i)$.
Thus a large-to-medium transition is dominated by the row's former large diagonal contribution. At initialization every row has $d_i=0$, so $M_0\preceq I$ by \eqref{eq:row-mass}. The rank-one term has norm $\eta N\leq1/K$, proving $L\leq1+1/K<2<8$ and $\Phi\leq0$ for arbitrary $x_0$.

Deleting a retained coefficient leaves $d_i$ fixed and decreases $G_i$, hence decreases both barriers and weights at a fixed scale. At fixed $d_i,G_i,e$,
\begin{align*}
 \partial_r u&=\frac{12R-u-e-RG/(2r^2)}r>0,\\
 \partial_r\log w&=\frac{2-(u+e+RG/(2r^2))/(2R)}r>0
\end{align*}
when $u\leq0$, $e\leq1/4$, and $G\leq r^2$. During a decrease to the new valid scale, $r^2\geq q_i\geq G_i$ throughout. A barrier cannot cross into positivity while $r$ decreases, and its weight cannot increase. The Gram entries and vectors therefore decrease entrywise. Entrywise decrease of a symmetric nonnegative matrix cannot increase its top eigenvalue, since a maximizing Rayleigh vector can be replaced by its absolute value. Freezing takes a principal submatrix. Large-to-medium transitions were treated by the semidefinite comparison. The ambient regularizer is unchanged by cleanup, so the same statements hold for $\Phi$.
\end{proof}

The barriers have a simple line protection rule. By \eqref{eq:spread}--\eqref{eq:scales}, $|b_{ij}|/r_i\leq1/\sqrt K$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-bounds}
 \|g_{i\sigma}\|_2\leq2,\quad
 (g_{i\sigma})_j^2\leq\frac{4p_{ij}}{r_i^2},\quad
 \|\nabla^2u_{i\sigma}\|_\op\leq\frac RK\leq\frac1R.
\end{equation}
For a protected pair, exact tangency makes
\[
 u(x+th)=u(x)-\frac{Rt^2}{2r_i^2}\sum_jp_{ij}h_j^2\leq u(x).
\]
An unprotected pair starts below $-1$ and increases by at most $2|t|$ on a cube segment with $\|h\|\leq1$. Therefore all barriers remain nonpositive for $|t|\leq1/4$.

\section{Dimension reserve and strict curvature}
\label{sec:direction}

Assume a cleaned state has $L\leq9$. There are fewer than $2N/K$ large rows, since $\sum_iq_i\leq N$ and $R^2\geq K/2$. Each protected pair contributes
\[
 w_{i\sigma}q_i^2
 >2K\exp\left(-\frac1{2R}\right)
\]
to $\one^TW\one\leq LN\leq9N$. Their number is therefore below
\begin{equation}\label{eq:protected-count}
 \frac92e^{1/(2R)}\frac NK<\frac{5N}K,
\end{equation}
because $R\geq8$ and $e^{1/16}\leq16/15$.
The spread rule also gives
\[
 \|p_i\|_2^2\leq q_i^2/K,
 \qquad\tr W\leq LN/K,
 \qquad\|B_{\rm lg}+\eta\one\one^T\|_\op\leq1+1/K<2.
\]
When $L>8$, let $E_{\rm sp}$ be the span of eigenvectors of $M$ with eigenvalue at least $L/2$. For each of its unit eigenvectors $v_\ell$, the background operator bound gives $v_\ell^TWv_\ell>L/2-2\geq L/4$. Summing over an orthonormal basis gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spec-count}
 \dim E_{\rm sp}<\frac{4\tr W}L\leq\frac{4N}K.
\end{equation}
This uses the background's operator norm; its potentially large trace is not charged to the high spectral subspace.

\begin{lemma}[Restricted diagonal trace]\label{lem:trace}
Let $U\succeq0$ and diagonal $D\succeq0$ satisfy $U_{jj}\leq\rho D_{jj}$. Every subspace $T\subseteq\R^N$ with $\dim T>\rho N$ contains a nonzero $h$ with $h^T(U-D)h\leq0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $D_{jj}=0$, then $U_{jj}=0$ and positive semidefiniteness implies $U_{j\ell}=0$ for every $\ell$. Hence $\ker D\subseteq\ker U$. A nonzero vector of $T\cap\ker D$ already proves the result. Otherwise $D^{1/2}$ is injective on $T$. On the positive-diagonal coordinates let $Q=D^{-1/2}UD^{-1/2}\succeq0$. Its trace is at most $\rho N$. The sum of its Rayleigh quotients on an orthonormal basis of $D^{1/2}T$ is at most this trace. Since $\dim T>\rho N$, some unit vector has quotient below one. Pull it back through $D^{1/2}$ to obtain $h^TUh<h^TDh$.
\end{proof}

Let $v>0$ be the unit top eigenvector of $M$, and define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal U&=\sum_{i,\sigma}w_{i\sigma}\beta^2(p_i^Tv)^2
                              g_{i\sigma}g_{i\sigma}^T,\label{eq:UD}\\
 \mathcal D&=\sum_{i,\sigma}\frac{w_{i\sigma}\beta R}{r_i^2}
                   (p_i^Tv)^2\diag(p_i).
\end{align}
Termwise use of \eqref{eq:g-bounds} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diag-compare}
 \mathcal U_{jj}\leq\frac2{R^2}\mathcal D_{jj}.
\end{equation}
Only $W$ varies during a move. Direct differentiation and the simple-eigenvalue equation yield
\begin{align}
 v^TW''[h,h]v&=h^T(\mathcal U-\mathcal D)h,\label{eq:Wsecond}\\
 L''[h,h]&=h^T(\mathcal U-\mathcal D)h+
 2\sum_{\ell\geq2}\frac{|v_\ell^TW'[h]v|^2}{L-\lambda_\ell(M)}.
 \label{eq:Lsecond}
\end{align}
For completeness, differentiating $Mv=Lv$ with $v^Tv=1$ gives
$v'=\sum_{\ell\geq2}v_\ell(v_\ell^TM'v)/(L-\lambda_\ell)$ and
$L'=v^TM'v$, and a second differentiation gives \eqref{eq:Lsecond}.

Factor $W=CC^T$ with columns $\sqrt{w_{i\sigma}}p_i$. Then $W'[h]v=Cz$, where
$z_{i\sigma}=\sqrt{w_{i\sigma}}\beta(g_{i\sigma}^Th)(p_i^Tv)$.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:motion}
 \|W'[h]v\|_2^2\leq Lh^T\mathcal Uh.
\end{equation}
The additional linear equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spec-equations}
 \operatorname{Proj}_{E_{\rm sp}}W'[h]v=0
\end{equation}
cost at most \eqref{eq:spec-count} dimensions. They include $L'[h]=0$ and remove all denominators less than $L/2$ from \eqref{eq:Lsecond}. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lcurv}
 L''[h,h]\leq h^T(5\mathcal U-\mathcal D)h.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform feasible curvature]\label{lem:curvature}
At any cleaned state with $L\leq9$ and $N>8k$, for every extra vector $g_*$ there is a nonzero direction obeying the exact face, cost, large-row, protected-pair, and $g_*^Th=0$ equations, with
\[
 h^T\nabla^2\Phi h\leq-2\eta\|h\|_2^2.
\]
For $L>8$ it can also obey $L'[h]=0$ and
$L''[h,h]\leq-h^T\mathcal Dh/6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $L>8$, impose \eqref{eq:spec-equations}. In addition to the face equations, the cost and $g_*$ use at most two dimensions, the large and protected rows use fewer than $2N/K+5N/K$, and the spectral equations use fewer than $4N/K$. For $6\mathcal U$, Lemma~\ref{lem:trace} reserves $12N/R^2\leq24N/K$ dimensions by \eqref{eq:diag-compare}. The total reserve is therefore strictly below
\[
 \frac{35N}K+2.
\]
It is strictly smaller than $N/k$: for $K=64k$ the difference is at least
\[
 \frac{29}{64}\frac Nk-2>\frac{13}8>0
 \quad(N>8k).
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:trace} to $6\mathcal U,\mathcal D$ using \eqref{eq:diag-compare}. A nonzero remaining direction satisfies $6h^T\mathcal Uh\leq h^T\mathcal Dh$. Equation~\eqref{eq:Lcurv} then gives $L''\leq-h^T\mathcal Dh/6$. Since $L'=0$,
\[
 h^T\nabla^2\Phi h=3(L-8)^2L''[h,h]-2\eta\|h\|^2
                    \leq-2\eta\|h\|^2.
\]
For $L\leq8$, the cubic spectral term has zero Hessian, including at $L=8$. The face, cost, $g_*$, large-row, and protected-pair equations leave a nonzero direction by the weaker version of the same count. Its curvature is exactly $-2\eta\|h\|^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact flat case]
If the selected direction has $h^T\mathcal Dh=0$, every summand in \eqref{eq:UD} is nonnegative. Since $v>0$, a nonempty $p_i$ has $p_i^Tv>0$, so $\sum_jp_{ij}h_j^2=0$. Thus $h$ vanishes on every medium support. Their tracked sums, energies, barriers, and weights are exactly constant on the line. Consequently $M(x+th)v=Lv$ identically. Simplicity and the gap show that this constant eigenvalue remains the top eigenvalue locally. This proves constancy rather than inferring it from a vanishing second derivative. The ambient quadratic still gives the uniform strict curvature.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Taylor estimate]\label{lem:Taylor}
Keep row data fixed. On a cube segment $x+th$ starting at $L\leq9$, with $\|h\|\leq1$ and $|t|\leq1/4$, the second derivative of $\Phi$ is Lipschitz and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Taylor}
 \left|\Phi(x+th)-\Phi(x)-t\nabla\Phi^Th
 -\frac{t^2}2h^T\nabla^2\Phi h\right|
 \leq\frac{B_3}6|t|^3,
 \qquad B_3=2^{40}K^2n^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equations~\eqref{eq:g-bounds} give $|u'|\leq2$, $|u''|\leq1/R$, and $u'''=0$. With $\beta=1/(2R)$, the normalized absolute weight-derivative ratios are at most
\[
 |w'|/w\leq1/R,\quad |w''|/w\leq3/(2R^2),
 \quad |w'''|/w\leq5/(2R^3).
\]
Weights increase by less than a factor two on the segment, so $L(t)\leq18$. Entrywise absolute-value comparison with $W$ therefore gives
\[
 a_1:=\|M'\|\leq18/R,\quad
 a_2:=\|M''\|\leq27/R^2,\quad
 a_3:=\|M'''\|\leq45/R^3.
\]
The reduced resolvent has norm at most $1/\eta=Kn$. Differentiating the eigenpair equations and the normalization equations gives
\[
 \|v'\|\leq a_1/\eta,\qquad
 \|v''\|\leq a_2/\eta+5a_1^2/\eta^2.
\]
For the second estimate, the component parallel to $v$ is $-\|v'\|^2v$; the orthogonal component is bounded by the reduced resolvent applied to $M''v+2(M'-L'I)v'$. Using $L'=v^TM'v$ then gives
\begin{align*}
 |L'|&\leq18/R,\\
 |L''|&\leq(27+648Kn)/R^2,\\
 |L'''|&\leq(45+2916Kn+69984K^2n^2)/R^3.
\end{align*}
Indeed $L''=v^TM''v+2(v')^TM'v$, and differentiating once more bounds $L'''$ by $a_3+6a_1a_2/\eta+12a_1^3/\eta^2$.
For $f(t)=(t-8)_+^3$, on $[0,18]$ the bounds on $f',f'',f'''$ are $300,60,6$. The chain rule gives, almost everywhere,
\[
 |\Phi'''|\leq\frac{135972+2974320Kn+20995200K^2n^2}{R^3}
             <2^{25}K^2n^2<B_3.
\]
The function $f$ is $C^{2,1}$, although it is not $C^3$ at eight. Integrating the almost-everywhere bound across crossings of $L=8$ proves the Lipschitz second-derivative and Taylor statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Certified rational derivative evaluation]\label{lem:eval}
Given a polynomial-bit cleaned state with $L\leq9$, and positive rational error targets $\epsilon_g,\epsilon_H$ of polynomial encoding length, rational approximations $\widetilde g,\widetilde H$ satisfying
\[
 \|\widetilde g-\nabla\Phi\|_2\leq\epsilon_g,
 \qquad\|\widetilde H-\nabla^2\Phi\|_\op\leq\epsilon_H
\]
can be computed in polynomial bit time in the state length, $m,n,K$, and the logarithms of the reciprocal errors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normalize every Gram summand with $z_i=p_i/r_i^2$. Then
\[
 w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^T=32K e^{\beta u_{i\sigma}}z_iz_i^T,
 \quad\sum_j(z_i)_j\leq1,\quad\max_j(z_i)_j\leq1/K,
 \quad\|z_i\|_2^2\leq1/K.
\]
This removes inverse-small-scale conditioning from the summand norm. The vectors $b_i/r_i$ and $Rp_i/r_i^2$ in row derivatives likewise have uniformly bounded norms and polynomial-bit rational encodings. Analytical differentiation gives
\[
 M_j=\sum_{i,\sigma}w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^T\beta(g_{i\sigma})_j,
\]
\[
 M_{j\ell}=\sum_{i,\sigma}w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^T
 \left[\beta^2(g_{i\sigma})_j(g_{i\sigma})_\ell
       -\frac{\beta R p_{ij}}{r_i^2}\one_{j=\ell}\right].
\]
At a cleaned state, $\|M_j\|\leq9/R\leq9/8$ and $\|M_{j\ell}\|\leq27/(2R^2)$, so both are below four. Their normalized multipliers have absolute values at most one.

Set $\epsilon=\min\{1,\epsilon_g,\epsilon_H\}$ and choose the rational precision
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \kappa=\gamma=\frac{\epsilon\eta^3}{2^{40}(n+1)^6(K+1)^4},
 \qquad \rho=\frac\kappa{4K^2(m+1)}.
\end{equation}
For a nonpositive exponential argument $t=\beta u$, choose an integer $J\geq\max\{1,\lceil\log_2(1/\rho)\rceil\}$. If $t<-J$, return zero; otherwise use the rational Taylor polynomial through degree $32(J+1)$ and clip any negative output to zero. Since $e^{-J}\leq2^{-J}$, the cutoff error is at most $\rho$. On $[-J,0]$, Taylor's factorial remainder is at most $J^{d+1}/(d+1)!$; for $d=32(J+1)$ this is below $2^{-J}\leq\rho$. The Taylor approximation has polynomial degree and bit length even if the original argument's numerical magnitude is enormous. Taking $J$ slightly larger if a stricter target is required makes the inequalities strict.

Use those same nonnegative rational weights in $M,M_j,M_{j\ell}$. Their errors are at most $\kappa$ in operator norm: each of the $2m$ normalized summands has norm at most $32$, so the exponential error contributes at most $64m\rho<\kappa$, including the derivative multipliers. Denote the rational Gram matrix by $\widehat M$. Its positive semidefinite, nonnegative Gram structure, exact diagonal term, and exact $\eta\one\one^T$ preserve the gap at least $\eta$. Its norm is at most ten.

Clear a common denominator to form an integer matrix $N_*=D\widehat M$, and compute the integer vector
\[
 z=N_*^{t_{\rm pow}}\one,\qquad
 J_{\rm pow}=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{16n}{\gamma}\right\rceil,
 \qquad t_{\rm pow}=\left\lceil\frac{64J_{\rm pow}}\eta\right\rceil.
\]
The initial top-eigenvector overlap is at least $1/\sqrt n$, and the other eigenvalue ratios are at most $1-\eta/10$. Thus this power count makes the angular error at most $\gamma$. The integer intermediate bit lengths grow only linearly in $t_{\rm pow}$ and the integer matrix encoding length. Form the rational Rayleigh quotient and projector
\[
 \ell=\frac{z^T\widehat Mz}{z^Tz},\qquad S=\frac{zz^T}{z^Tz}.
\]
No eigenvector normalization or square root is required.

Let $P_v=vv^T$ be the true top projector of $M$. Projecting the perturbed eigenvector equation onto $v^\perp$ and using the gap gives, with the displayed precision,
\[
 \|S-P_v\|_\op\leq3\kappa/\eta,
 \qquad |\ell-L|\leq4\kappa.
\]
The matrix $LI-M+P_v$ has eigenvalue one in the top direction and eigenvalues at least $\eta$ on its orthogonal complement. Thus
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal R}=(\ell I-\widehat M+S)^{-1}-S
\]
exists, can be computed by exact rational elimination, and has error at most $19\kappa/\eta^3$ from
$\mathcal R=(LI-M+P_v)^{-1}-P_v$. This follows from the inverse perturbation identity; the additional inverse-gap factor accounts for the matrix-to-projector error. Determinant bounds keep all inverse entries at polynomial bit length.

Use the exact trace formulas
\begin{align*}
 L_j&=\tr(P_v M_j),\\
 L_{j\ell}&=\tr(P_v M_{j\ell})+
       \tr(P_v M_j\mathcal R M_\ell)+\tr(P_v M_\ell\mathcal R M_j),\\
 \nabla\Phi&=f'(L)\nabla L-2\eta x,\\
 \nabla^2\Phi&=f''(L)\nabla L\nabla L^T+f'(L)\nabla^2L-2\eta I.
\end{align*}
Substitution of rational approximations gives eigenvalue-gradient entry error at most $25\kappa/\eta$ and eigenvalue-Hessian entry error at most $2000\kappa/\eta^3$. For $f(t)=(t-8)_+^3$ and $0\leq t\leq10$, the bounds on $f',f''$ are twelve and their Lipschitz constants are twelve and six. The chain rule then gives potential-gradient norm error at most $444\sqrt n\kappa/\eta$ and potential-Hessian operator error at most $30000n\kappa/\eta^3$. Both are below $\epsilon$ by \eqref{eq:mu}. The scalar positive-part operations at rational $\ell$ are exact; no unresolved comparison with the true algebraic threshold is needed. The ambient regularizer is evaluated exactly. All operations and encoded precisions are polynomial in the stated parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rational negative-curvature witness]\label{lem:rational-direction}
Take $\epsilon_H=\eta/4$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:eval}. Intersect the exact face, cost, large-row, and protected-pair equations with $\widetilde g^Th=0$, and let $Z$ be an exact rational basis. Polynomial-bit rational linear algebra finds a vector $h$ in this kernel with
\[
 \frac12\leq\|h\|_2\leq1,\quad
 |\nabla\Phi^Th|\leq\epsilon_g,\quad
 h^T\nabla^2\Phi h\leq-3\eta/16.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:curvature} holds for the arbitrary extra vector $\widetilde g$. Its unit witness makes
$Z^T(\widetilde H+\eta I)Z$ negative on some vector, since the true curvature is at most $-2\eta$ and the Hessian error is at most $\eta/4$. Exact symmetric elimination detects a negative rational quadratic value and returns a rational vector $v$ witnessing it. Put $h_0=Zv$. Then
\[
 h_0^T\widetilde Hh_0<-\eta\|h_0\|_2^2.
\]
Standard determinant and Schur-complement bounds give polynomial bits for this certificate. Divide by the largest coordinate magnitude, then repeatedly divide by two until the squared norm is at most one; rational comparisons suffice and the final norm lies in $[1/2,1]$. The Hessian error gives true curvature at most
$-(3\eta/4)\|h\|^2\leq-3\eta/16$. Exact orthogonality to $\widetilde g$ gives the gradient estimate. The shift is applied in the physical metric before restriction, so a poorly conditioned $Z$ does not diminish the Rayleigh gap. Unshifted elimination would be insufficient: $\diag(-2^{-B},-1)$ may return a direction with exponentially small curvature if its first negative diagonal is used.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact affine-slice repair and bounded state}
\label{sec:reset}

\begin{lemma}[Boolean facet encoding]\label{lem:facets}
Signed Boolean polytopes and their faces admit integer affine-hull and relative facet equations with coefficient magnitudes at most $C=(2n)^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a coordinate projection injective on the affine tangent of a $d$-dimensional polytope. Its projected vertices are signed Boolean. A facet is determined by $d$ affinely independent projected vertices; the coefficients of an equation through them are cofactors of their augmented coordinate matrix. A nonfacet vertex fixes the orientation. Cofactor bounds, or expansion of the relevant determinants, give magnitude at most $(2n)^n$ after clearing denominators. Lift the inequality with zero coefficients on the unselected coordinates. Equations describing the affine hull are obtained by the same determinant construction. The bound is deliberately larger than needed and is independent of the current rational point's denominator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-bit slice anchor]\label{lem:anchor}
Let $x'\in\relint(F')$ satisfy $c^Tx'=c^Tx_0$, and put
\[
 Q_F=F'\cap\{z:c^Tz=c^Tx_0\},\qquad
 T_F=\lin(F'-x')\cap c^\perp,
 \qquad q=\dim T_F.
\]
Exact rational optimization over this slice constructs an anchor
$z\in\relint(F')\cap Q_F$ and a basis $T=[v_1-v_0,\ldots,v_q-v_0]$ of its tangent, both of polynomial bit length in the original $c,x_0,n$. The columns of $T$ have entries of magnitude at most two.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A small relative ball around $x'$ in $F'$ intersects the cost hyperplane in a relative ball. Thus $\aff Q_F=\aff F'\cap\{c^Tz=c^Tx_0\}$ and its tangent is $T_F$, whether or not the cost hyperplane supports $F'$.

Find one slice vertex $v_0$. If the collected differences span a proper subspace $S$ of $T_F$, choose a nonzero rational $a\in T_F\cap S^\perp$ and optimize both signs of $a^Tz$ over $Q_F$. At least one optimizing vertex has objective different from $a^Tv_0$, or $a$ would annihilate the entire slice tangent. Add it. At most $2q+1$ vertex optimizations suffice. If necessary, lexicographic coordinate optimization obtains a vertex in at most $n$ ordinary exact LP calls. The resulting $q+1$ vertices affinely span $Q_F$.

Each slice vertex is either a signed Boolean vertex or the intersection of the cost hyperplane with one original polytope edge. Indeed, if its minimal original face had dimension at least two, intersection of a local relative ball with one equation would leave positive dimension, contradicting that it is a slice vertex. For an edge with endpoints $u,w$, the intersection is
\[
 u+\alpha(w-u),\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{c^Tx_0-c^Tu}{c^T(w-u)}.
\]
Its bit length depends polynomially on the original cost and target, not on the current denominator of $x'$. Hence the vertices and their average
$z=(v_0+\cdots+v_q)/(q+1)$ have uniformly bounded polynomial bit length.

If a relative facet inequality not containing $F'$ were tight at $z$, its nonnegative slacks at all the $v_j$ would be zero. It would then be tight on their full affine span, hence on $Q_F$, contradicting $x'\in Q_F\cap\relint(F')$. Thus $z\in\relint(F')$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact face- and cost-preserving repair]\label{lem:repair}
For rational $0<\delta\leq1$, the preceding anchor and exact slice LPs construct a rational $\widehat x\in\relint(F')$ with
\[
 c^T\widehat x=c^Tx_0,\qquad\|\widehat x-x'\|_2<\delta,
\]
whose encoding length is polynomial in the original input and $\log(1/\delta)$, independently of the candidate's denominator length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_z$ be a common denominator of $z$, and $C=(2n)^n$. Every non-face integer facet slack at $z$ is at least $1/D_z$. Mix toward the anchor with
\[
 \alpha=\frac\delta{8n},\qquad x_{\rm mix}=(1-\alpha)x'+\alpha z.
\]
Every previously strict facet now has slack at least $\alpha/D_z$. Express $x_{\rm mix}=v_0+T\theta$ and round each coefficient of $\theta$ to a rational mesh satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh}
 \text{mesh}\leq\frac\alpha{16D_z C n^3}.
\end{equation}
Output $\widehat x=v_0+T\widehat\theta$. All face affine equations and the cost equation are exact, since they hold on the entire parameterized slice. The rounding movement has norm at most $n\sqrt n\,\text{mesh}$; its effect on a facet value is at most $nC$ times that norm, strictly below $\alpha/(4D_z)$. Every strict facet remains strict. The mixing movement is at most $2\sqrt n\,\alpha$, and the grid movement is smaller; their sum is below $\delta$.

An invertible minor of the bounded-bit basis $T$ bounds $\|\theta\|_\infty$ by $2^{\operatorname{poly}(n,\bits(c,x_0))}$ using Cramer's rule. The rounded mesh coefficients therefore have polynomial bit length. The mesh's reciprocal has polynomial logarithm because $D_z$ and $C$ do. The resulting encoding does not inherit the candidate denominator. If $q=0$, the slice is a singleton, already equal to the bounded-bit anchor.
\end{proof}

The margin in this proof may have exponentially small value; its encoding is polynomial. No inverse-polynomial lower bound on face width is asserted. Coordinatewise dyadic rounding is inadequate: the slice $x_1=1/3$ contains no point whose every coordinate is dyadic.

After the point repair, reset persistent row data. For medium rows replace
\[
 d_i\longleftarrow d_i+b_i^T(\widehat x-x'),
\]
then round it to a grid of spacing $r_i\delta$. Restore large tracked sums to zero. Completed sums can be kept unchanged; they have no remaining linear updates and already have polynomial bits from their last state. Finally increase $e$ by $4\delta$.
The medium barrier changes by at most $2\delta$ during the point movement: $\|b_i\|/r_i\leq1$ and the normalized amplified energy gradient has norm at most $R/\sqrt K\leq1$. The whole reset segment lies in the cube. Grid rounding adds at most another $\delta$; the conservative increment $4\delta$ therefore makes every medium barrier and weight nonincreasing. The only possible potential increase is the ambient regularizer, bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repair-loss}
 3\eta\sqrt n\,\delta.
\end{equation}
The tracking error introduced in any row is at most
$(\sqrt n+R+1)\delta$: large rows pay $\sqrt n\delta$, medium rows pay grid error at most $R\delta$, and the harmless extra one covers conventions at completion. This error is a separate trajectory-wide charge from deliberate coefficient deletion.

\section{The finite-bit favorable-cost algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Take the rational parameters
\begin{align}
 \tau&=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac\eta{8B_3}\right\},\qquad
 D_{\rm step}=\frac{\eta\tau^2}{64},\qquad
 \epsilon_g=\frac{\eta\tau}{128n},\label{eq:step-params}\\
 S&=\left\lceil\frac{2\eta n}{D_{\rm step}}\right\rceil+2n+4,\qquad
 \delta=\min\left\{\frac{D_{\rm step}}{2^{12}\eta n^2},\,
 \frac1{2^{12}S(n+R+1)}\right\}.\label{eq:reset-params}
\end{align}
Their values and reciprocal values are polynomially bounded in $n,k$, and their encodings are polynomial. Initialize $x=x_0,d_i=0,e=0$, and perform cleanup. While $N>8k$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Compute certified derivatives with errors $\epsilon_g,\eta/4$, and use Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-direction} to find the exact feasible rational direction $h$.
 \item Compute the exact two boundary distances $t_+,t_-$ in the current original face. Put $t=\min\{\tau,t_+,t_-\}$. If $t<\tau$, choose a sign hitting that endpoint; otherwise either sign is legal. A tie at $\tau$ can be recorded as a face event while retaining the full-step decrease.
 \item Move by the selected $\pm th$ and update each $d_i$ using \eqref{eq:tracking-line}. Recompute the minimal original face; perform coefficient and scale cleanup.
 \item Apply the exact face/cost repair and the row-data reset of Section~\ref{sec:reset}. Perform any newly needed cleanup.
\end{enumerate}
All motion directions are supported on active coordinates and preserve the original face and cost equalities exactly. The slice is used for repair, not as a substitute Boolean feasibility polytope.

Taylor's estimate and the selected curvature give, for either sign,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent}
 \Delta\Phi\leq\epsilon_g t-\frac{\eta t^2}{16}
 \qquad(0<t\leq\tau).
\end{equation}
Indeed the quadratic term is at most $-3\eta t^2/32$, and
$B_3t^3/6\leq\eta t^2/48$ by \eqref{eq:step-params}; the displayed weaker coefficient is valid.
A full step decreases $\Phi$ by at least $2D_{\rm step}$ before repair, because
$\epsilon_g\tau\leq\eta\tau^2/128$.
Its repair loss is at most
\[
 3\eta\sqrt n\delta\leq\frac{3D_{\rm step}}{2^{12}n}
                         <\frac{D_{\rm step}}{1024n},
\]
so it decreases the post-repair potential by at least $D_{\rm step}$.

An arbitrarily short step can increase $\Phi$ by at most
$\epsilon_g\tau=D_{\rm step}/(2n)$ before repair and less than $D_{\rm step}/n$ afterward. Such a step reaches an exact boundary of the original face. Its new minimal face is proper: otherwise its relative interior would permit continuing in the same direction. A strictly nested chain of original faces has at most $n$ drops. This counts rank-facet events even when every coordinate remains fractional.

For every finite prefix of the run,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iteration-budget}
 -\eta n\leq\Phi\leq -N_{\rm full}D_{\rm step}+D_{\rm step}.
\end{equation}
Thus $N_{\rm full}\leq\eta n/D_{\rm step}+1$, and the total number of moves is less than $S$. In particular the bound was chosen before execution and is independent of face widths. The same inequality implies
\[
 (L-8)_+^3\leq D_{\rm step}+\eta n<2/K<1,
\]
which keeps $L<9$ and closes the cleaned-state induction used above.

The reset choices also give
\[
 e\leq4S\delta\leq1/1024<1/4,
 \qquad \text{total numerical tracking error}
 \leq S(n+R+1)\delta\leq1/4096.
\]
Medium tracked values have magnitude below $H$ by the two barriers, scales have polynomial bit length, and their grid encodings consequently have polynomial bit length. Large values are zero; completed values cease updating. The point reset gives a uniformly bounded persistent point encoding. Every subsequent exact face LP, matrix inverse, derivative approximation, rational elimination, and line endpoint therefore receives uniformly polynomial-size input. One exact update may increase encoding length polynomially, but the reset prevents a repeated current-bit-length recurrence from being mistaken for an original-input bound. This proves the stated finite-bit continuation once the exact oracle operations are supplied.

\section{Exact geometric oracles}
\label{sec:oracles}

\subsection{Independence-oracle matroids}
Let $\mathcal M=(E,\mathcal I)$ be a matroid and $r$ its rank function. A rank evaluation takes at most $n$ independence queries by greedy scanning. The base polytope is
\[
 P(\mathcal M)=\{z\geq0:z(S)\leq r(S)\ (S\subseteq E),\ z(E)=r(E)\}.
\]
Exact membership and separation at rational $z$ follow by checking nonnegativity and total rank and minimizing $r(S)-z(S)$ by exact integer-valued submodular minimization after clearing denominators. A negative minimizer gives a violated rank constraint; otherwise all are satisfied. Exact polynomial-time integer submodular minimization is a standard subroutine \cite{iwata}. Its values here are rank plus a modular function and have polynomial bit length.

\begin{lemma}[Exact minimal matroid face]\label{lem:matroid-face}
For rational $z\in P(\mathcal M)$, at most $n$ submodular minimizations and polynomial independence queries construct its minimal face tangent with a $0,\pm1$ basis. They also construct a face matroid with an independence oracle, so linear optimization over the face is polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $I_0=\{j:z_j=0\}$, $I_1=\{j:z_j=1\}$, and $U=\{j:0<z_j<1\}$. The first two sets are fixed on the minimal face. Since $z(I_1)=|I_1|\leq r(I_1)$, $I_1$ is independent. Form the minor $\mathcal M'=(\mathcal M/I_1)\setminus I_0$, with
$r'(S)=r(S\cup I_1)-|I_1|$ on $U$.
Write $z_U=X/Q$ and put $f(S)=r'(S)-z(S)\geq0$. Tight sets contain $\varnothing,U$ and are closed under union and intersection, since submodularity gives
$0=f(S)+f(T)\geq f(S\cap T)+f(S\cup T)\geq0$.

For each $i\in U$, minimize over $R\subseteq U\setminus\{i\}$ the integer submodular function
\[
 (N+1)\bigl(Qr'(R\cup\{i\})-X(R\cup\{i\})\bigr)+|R|.
\]
Nonzero slack contributes at least $N+1$, while $|R|\leq N-1$, so every minimizer is tight. Intersection closure gives a unique smallest tight set $T_i$ containing $i$; it is the unique minimum-cardinality tight choice.

Group elements by equality of $T_i$, obtaining classes $C_1,\ldots,C_b$. A tight set contains either every element of a class or none. If $j\in T_i$, then $T_j\subseteq T_i$, strictly if the classes differ. Order classes by increasing $|T_i|$. The equations
\[
 \one_{T_i}=\one_{C_i}+\sum_{j:T_j\subsetneq T_i}\one_{C_j}
\]
are triangular, proving that tight-set incidence vectors span exactly the class-incidence vectors. Hence the face tangent consists of fixed-coordinate zeros and $h(C_j)=0$. Choosing a root $p_j\in C_j$ gives the basis
$e_i-e_{p_j}$ for $i\in C_j\setminus\{p_j\}$.
No further equations are hidden: all remaining finitely many rank constraints have positive slack, so sufficiently small steps of either sign satisfying the active equations remain feasible.

The triangular system also shows that $z(C_j)$ is integral. Since every coordinate in $U$ is strictly fractional, each class has at least two elements. Thus the face dimension $N-b$ is at least $N/2$.

The prefixes $S_j=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_j$ are tight: they are unions of the corresponding $T_i$. The fractional part of the face is the base polytope of
\[
 \bigoplus_{j=1}^b(\mathcal M'|S_j)/S_{j-1}.
\]
Independence of $J\subseteq C_j$ is tested by
$r'(S_{j-1}\cup J)-r'(S_{j-1})=|J|$. Greedy optimization in the direct sum, with $I_1$ mandatory, optimizes over the exact face.
\end{proof}

Additional rational equations, such as the cost and protected-row equations, are imposed by exact elimination in this face basis. Clearing row denominators and using determinant bounds gives a polynomial-bit rational basis of their common kernel. The cost slice need not be integral: in the rank-one matroid on two elements, $z=(1/2,1/2)$ and cost $(1,0)$ give a singleton fractional cost slice. Exact rational LP over the face plus the cost equation is implemented by its face equations and the preceding strong separation oracle, with lexicographic optimization if a slice vertex is required.

\begin{lemma}[Exact matroid line endpoint]\label{lem:matroid-line}
For rational $z=X/Q$ and nonzero rational tangent $h=H/R_h$ with integral $H$, the maximal positive feasible parameter is computable exactly in polynomial bit and independence-oracle complexity. Its reduced denominator is at most $QnH_{\max}$, where $H_{\max}=\max_j|H_j|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coordinate bound
\[
 U_* =\min_{h_j>0}\frac{1-z_j}{h_j}
       \wedge\min_{h_j<0}\frac{z_j}{-h_j}
\]
is finite, positive, and at most $R_h$. Test it by exact submodular membership. If feasible, it is the endpoint. Otherwise the endpoint is set by a positive-slope rank inequality or a coordinate inequality. In the rank case,
\[
 t_* =\frac{R_h[Qr(S)-X(S)]}{QH(S)},\qquad H(S)>0.
\]
Its reduced denominator is at most $QnH_{\max}$ and its pre-reduction numerator at most $R_hnQ$. Exact membership bisection maintains a feasible lower and infeasible upper parameter until their distance is below $1/[2(QnH_{\max})^2]$. Distinct rationals of denominator at most this bound differ by at least its reciprocal square, so exact continued-fraction reconstruction on the rational interval recovers the unique endpoint. Its denominator and membership are verified. The bisection count is polynomial in $\log R_h,\log Q,\log H_{\max},\log n$. Applying this to $-h$ gives the other ray.

At the endpoint the minimal original face is proper, even if all coordinates stay fractional. For example the rank-two matroid on five elements with parallel pair $\{1,2\}$ has
\[
 z=(3/10,3/10,7/15,7/15,7/15),\quad h=(3,3,-2,-2,-2).
\]
The first hit is $t_*=1/15$, yielding $(1/2,1/2,1/3,1/3,1/3)$ and the new rank equality $z_1+z_2=1$. Face dimension drops from four to three.
\end{proof}

The usual matroid edge theorem says every base-polytope edge is parallel to $e_i-e_j$. One direct explanation is that adjacent bases cannot differ in two or more exchanges: symmetric basis exchange then expresses their midpoint as the midpoint of two other bases, contradicting a one-dimensional minimal face. Thus $k\leq2$. A Boolean base is finally certified by rank and independence.

\subsection{General Boolean polytopes with an exact strong oracle}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:facets}, $P$ is well-described with polynomial facet encoding length, even if no explicit list is supplied. Exact strong optimization supplies exact strong separation and rational LP through the standard oracle reductions \cite{gls}. Lower-dimensional affine hulls are included in that model. The following construction makes minimal-face recovery explicit rather than postulating it.

Let $z=X/Q\in P$ and use a conservative integer facet bound $C=(2n)^n$. Put
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac1{2QnC},\qquad
 D_z=\{h\in[-1,1]^n:z+\varepsilon h\in P, z-\varepsilon h\in P\}.
\]
Every inactive integer facet has slack at least $1/Q$, and
$|\varepsilon a^Th|\leq1/(2Q)$ on the cube. Active facets and affine equations force $a^Th=0$ in both signs. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-box}
 D_z=\lin(F(z)-z)\cap[-1,1]^n.
\end{equation}
Strong separation of $D_z$ requires cube checks and two separation calls to $P$.
To recover its span, keep independent rational vectors in $D_z$. Compute a rational basis of the orthogonal complement of their span, and optimize each corresponding linear form over the centrally symmetric $D_z$. A positive optimum adds an independent vector; all-zero optima certify completion. At most $n^2$ exact rational LP calls suffice. Although the construction uses $Q$, the geometric polytope in \eqref{eq:tangent-box} has coefficient complexity bounded by the face's Boolean facet equations, so vertex-output tangent bases have uniform polynomial bit bounds.

These vectors give exact face affine equations and hence exact slice LPs. Along $h=H/R_h$ a facet endpoint has denominator at most $QnCH_{\max}$ after cancellation, and coordinate bounds supply an initial interval. Exact membership bisection and rational reconstruction, or exact one-dimensional LP with the strong separation oracle, give both endpoints. The same proper-face argument counts short events. Exact terminal optimization over the recovered face returns a Boolean vertex; a tie can be resolved lexicographically. These are conditional reductions from an \emph{exact strong} oracle. A merely weak, approximate, or favorable-cost-only polytope oracle is not substituted for it.

\section{Final discrepancy and favorable cost}
\label{sec:final}

At the first deliberate deletion in a row, $p=a^2>q/K$ and $|a|\leq1$ imply $q<K$. For every such deletion,
\[
 \sqrt q-\sqrt{q-a^2}
 =\frac{a^2}{\sqrt q+\sqrt{q-a^2}}
 >\frac{|a|}{2\sqrt K}.
\]
Intervening frozen deletions only decrease $q$. Telescoping shows that the sum of deliberately deleted coefficient magnitudes in the row is at most $2K$. Each deleted signed coordinate can subsequently move by at most two, including the final rounding, so their entire signed tracking error is at most $4K$. This is charged once over the trajectory.

When $N\leq8k$, optimize the original cost over the current original face and obtain a signed Boolean vertex $x_b=2b-\one$. It has cost at most that of $x$, which still equals $c^Tx_0$; hence $c^Tb\leq c^Ty$. At most $N$ coordinates change and each changes by at most two, so retained terminal row sums change by at most $2N\leq16k$.

The two medium barriers give
\[
 |d_i|\leq H-r_i\left(12R-e-\frac{RG_i}{2r_i^2}\right)<H.
\]
Completed rows inherit this bound when completed and need no further numerical updates; initially completed or previously large rows have tracked value zero. At the terminal point there are no large rows, because $q_i\leq N\leq8k<R^2$. Combining the tracked, deletion, terminal, and numerical charges gives
\[
 \|A(x_b-x_0)\|_\infty<H+4K+16k+1.
\]
Conversion to Boolean coordinates divides this by two, proving
\[
 \|A(b-y)\|_\infty<\frac H2+2K+8k+\frac12<B(k).
\]
All original faces visited are nested inside $F(y)$; repair preserves the exact current face, so $b\in F(y)$. This completes the favorable-cost interface \eqref{eq:cost-oracle}.

\section{Exact marginals from the favorable-response transcript}
\label{sec:marginals}

Fix the original $A,y$ and the rational bound $B(k)$ throughout. Let
\[
 G=\{b\in V\cap F(y):\|A(b-y)\|_\infty\leq B(k)\}.
\]
The preceding algorithm returns some $b\in G$ with $c^T(b-y)\leq0$ for every rational cost $c$, of either sign. It does not have to optimize over $G$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite exact hull certificate]\label{lem:hull}
For any finite $G\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ and rational $y=a/Q$, an oracle that always returns $b\in G$ with $c^T(b-y)\leq0$ at every rational $c$ permits a polynomial-bit algorithm returning an exact rational mean-$y$ mixture on at most $n+1$ members of $G$. It uses $O(n^3(\log Q+\log n))$ favorable-response calls of polynomial input length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $y$ is Boolean, query the cost $c_j=1$ when $y_j=0$ and $c_j=-1$ when $y_j=1$. Then $c^T(b-y)$ is the Hamming distance from $b$ to $y$, so the favorable answer is exactly $b=y$; return its one-atom law. Otherwise define $v_b=b-y$ and $w_b=Qb-a\in\Z^n$. The entries of $w_b$ have magnitude at most $Q$, and $\|v_b\|\leq\sqrt n$. Consider the separator system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 c^Tv_b\geq1\qquad(b\in G).
\end{equation}
The oracle always supplies an explicitly violated inequality at a rational queried $c$. We need to certify infeasibility of a finite \emph{collected} subsystem, rather than silently infer it from finitely many directions.

For every finite $J\subseteq G$, if its system is feasible, minimize $t$ under
\[
 w_b^Tc\geq Q\ (b\in J),\qquad -t\leq c_i\leq t,\qquad t\geq0.
\]
There is a finite optimal vertex: bounded-$t$ sublevels are compact and the polyhedron has no lines. At a vertex $n+1$ independent integer constraints determine $(c,t)$; all entries and right sides have magnitude at most $Q$. Cramer's rule bounds every coordinate in magnitude by
\[
 R_0=(n+1)!Q^{n+1}.
\]
Scale this separator by two, put $R_*=4R_0$ and $\rho=1/(2n)$. A Euclidean radius-$\rho$ perturbation changes every $v_b$ dot product by at most $\sqrt n/(2n)\leq1/2$ and remains inside $[-R_*,R_*]^n$. Thus the boxed feasible region
\[
 K_J=\{c:|c_i|\leq R_*,\ c^Tv_b\geq1\ (b\in J)\}
\]
is either empty or contains a full-dimensional radius-$\rho$ ball. This bound is uniform over adaptive finite subsets and eliminates a lower-dimensional feasibility loophole.

Run the polynomial-bit rational ellipsoid feasibility routine \cite{gls}, starting from a rational sphere of radius $nR_*$. At a rational center $c$, return a violated box inequality if necessary; otherwise query the favorable-response oracle, verify its answer, and append $b$ to the collected list $J$. Return the violated integer inequality $w_b^Tc\geq Q$. A central cut through $c$ is valid because its halfspace contains this actual feasible halfspace. Use the standard outward-rounded rational updates, not unrounded real updates involving irrational square roots. Their volume reduction and center encodings are the standard polynomial-bit guarantees of the rational routine.

No feasible center is accepted. After
\[
 T=O\bigl(n^2\log(nR_*/\rho)\bigr)
   =O\bigl(n^3(\log Q+\log n)\bigr)
\]
iterations, the final ellipsoid has volume smaller than a radius-$\rho$ ball. Every cut used contains $K_J$ for the final collected list. If this finite subsystem were feasible, the preceding thickness lemma would contradict that volume. Its unboxed system is therefore infeasible too, since every feasible unboxed subsystem has a bounded separator as proved above.

For a finite list, infeasibility of $c^T(b-y)\geq1$ is equivalent to $y\in\conv J$. Indeed, strict separation from a finite convex hull can be positively scaled to this system; conversely a convex combination summing to $y$ precludes it. Solve the explicit rational feasibility LP
\[
 \sum_{b\in J}\lambda_b b=y,\qquad
 \sum_{b\in J}\lambda_b=1,\qquad\lambda_b\geq0.
\]
A basic feasible solution has at most $n+1$ supported columns $[1;b]$, or at most $\dim F(y)+1$ when all $b\in F(y)$. If a generic exact LP returns more support, remove affine dependencies: integer cofactor determinants give a nonzero dependence $u$ on at most $n+2$ columns with $\sum u_b=0$, $\sum u_bb=0$ and $O(n\log n)$ coefficient bits. Replace $\lambda$ by $\lambda-\theta u$, where $\theta=\min_{u_b>0}\lambda_b/u_b$. This retains nonnegativity and the two exact equations and removes at least one support point. Each operation adds at most $O(n\log n)$ common-denominator bits, so polynomially many operations remain polynomial.

For a support-minimal solution, choose independent rows of its $0/1$ matrix $[1;b]$. Hadamard's determinant bound and Cramer's rule give reduced weight denominators at most
\[
 Q(n+1)^{(n+1)/2}.
\]
Hence each weight has $O(\log Q+n\log n)$ bits. Finally verify the exact mean, weights, and each vertex's feasibility and rational discrepancy. No bound is inflated by this decomposition.
\end{proof}

The guarantee is fixed-point convex-hull extraction under a universal favorable-response promise. It is not optimization or strong separation over all of $G$. For example, in the uniform rank-two matroid on four elements, with $y=(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)$ and all bases good, an oracle may always choose the cheaper of $1100$ and $0011$. Their mean is $y$, so this is favorable for every cost, although it can hide every other base. At cost $(1,-1,1,-1)$ both visible costs are zero while the hidden base $0101$ has cost $-2$. The proof above requires only the weaker interface it actually has. Extraction from ellipsoid transcripts is a standard general technique; see, for example, \cite{cdw}. The explicit argument here checks its applicability to this particular fixed-$y$ threshold oracle.

\section{Audit record and boundaries of the result}
\label{sec:status}

This document is a mathematical research derivation. It is not a formal proof checked by a proof assistant, a completed implementation of the full algorithm, or a claim of novelty. Exact finite fixtures and numerical experiments are distinct from the symbolic proof. The rank-boundary fixture and $K_4$ exchange obstruction were checked independently in finite computations, but their conclusions above are supported by explicit algebraic arguments; no finite test is used to establish the general theorem.

The accompanying final-parameter script records 69 passing local checks, including parameter inequalities at $k=1,\ldots,1000$ and four artificial spectral stress states with $8<L<9$. Its first run had one Hessian-vector finite-difference mismatch. A step-size sweep exhibited quadratic truncation error, and a fourth-order stencil reduced the offending error from approximately $4.99\cdot10^{-5}$ to $4.03\cdot10^{-9}$ without changing the tolerance. The first failure and the full sweep are retained. These are float64 local checks; the near-barrier offsets are not asserted to be reachable states. Neither a full rounding trajectory nor an exact-rational implementation is tested by that script.

The integrated derivation uses the following components, checked symbolically in separate proof and audit notes:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The spectral direction count, singular-diagonal trace argument, exact flat case, and variable-$K$ Taylor estimate have independently checked symbolic derivations (Sections~\ref{sec:direction} and \ref{sec:numerics}). The smaller height has been checked separately against every height-dependent inequality.
 \item The exact matroid minimal face, rank-boundary line endpoint, and fixed-point favorable-response extraction have independently checked oracle proofs (Sections~\ref{sec:oracles} and \ref{sec:marginals}). These standard oracle primitives do not by themselves prove a discrepancy algorithm.
 \item The cost slice can be fractional. Its anchor and affine-basis repair, the shifted negative-curvature selection, and reset of tracked row data have independent finite-precision derivations (Sections~\ref{sec:reset} and \ref{sec:algorithm}). Exponentially small interior margin is charged through its bit encoding rather than mistaken for inverse-polynomial width.
 \item The certified derivative evaluator is expanded in Lemma~\ref{lem:eval}. The final amplified-energy parameters, background operator-norm argument, Taylor bound, and combined reset/cleanup order were checked in the adversarial audit. The finite-bit conclusion uses uniform original-input reset bounds; per-operation polynomial complexity in a growing current encoding would be insufficient.
\end{enumerate}

No result here automatically gives a prefix guarantee, partition into several bases, matroid intersection, or two-base coupled feasibility. The edge parameter belongs to the actual feasible polytope. Coupling two otherwise easy matroid base constraints can create large-support edges. The upper and midpoint lower bounds therefore concern one Boolean polytope with its own edge-support promise. They settle the order of growth in that parameter; optimizing the universal constants remains open. The oracle-based construction is not a practical runtime benchmark or a completed full-algorithm implementation. Priority and independent external refereeing remain unresolved.
