## Question, interface and result

Can arbitrary polynomial tails in the two elementary residual shears used by the collision-compatible quadratic chart repair its Hessian determinant? The coefficients may depend on both base variables, and the tail degrees are unrestricted. A source map must be a global polynomial automorphism, preserve the actual known gradient collision after polynomial critical reduction, and pass the determinant gate on the actual reduced potential.

The answer for this entire fixed-first-jet class is **no**. Let b=(x,y), let D2 and G be defined below, and let h be any polynomial in b. Choose arbitrary polynomials F(b,eta), H(b,s) satisfying the identities

    F(b,0)=F_eta(b,0)=0,
    H(b,0)=H_s(b,0)=0.                                  (T1)

Define

    s=rho+F(b,eta),
    Theta_b(rho,eta)=D2(x) (s, eta+H(b,s))^T,
    psi=h(b)+G1(b)s+G2(b)[eta+H(b,s)].                   (T2)

Then det Hess_(x,y,rho,eta)(psi) is nonconstant, for every h,F,H satisfying (T1), including the branches where one or both shears vanish. In particular, no member has a constant nonzero Hessian determinant. This rules out all degree-seven and higher repairs obtained by changing the two shear tails while keeping this first jet. It is stronger than the earlier bounded-degree potential-repair exclusion, but covers a different, explicit coordinate orbit rather than every repair of a given degree.

The restriction that the old base and pivot remain fixed is essential. This is not a theorem about arbitrary base-moving or pivot-moving charts, arbitrary longer shear words, a different first jet, or all potential repairs. A pivot translation at a nonzero critical level can realize arbitrary direct target repairs; that separate interface does not satisfy the fixed-pivot fibre ideal used here. No full five-dimensional constant-Hessian-determinant assertion is transferred through a nonlinear source automorphism.

## A polynomial cylinder lemma

For a polynomial E in C[x,y], write

    K_E=(grad E)^T adj(Hess E)(grad E),
    M_d(E)=(d-1)E det(Hess E)-d K_E,    d>=2.         (T9)

**Lemma.** For E nonzero, M_d(E)=0 if and only if E is a polynomial in one affine-linear form. Constants are included. The derivation is elementary over C and uses no Keller-map inversion theorem.

First let an irreducible factor g have multiplicity m>=1 in E=g^m h, with g not dividing h. Set q=grad g, r=grad h, A=Hess g, B=Hess h. The exact expansions are

    grad E=g^(m-1) w,          w=m h q+g r,
    Hess E=g^(m-2) T,
    T=m(m-1)h qq^T
        +g[m h A+m(qr^T+rq^T)]+g^2 B.               (T10)

For m=1 the apparent g^-1 is harmless: T is divisible by g. The 2x2 adjugate is linear, adj(qq^T) annihilates q, and

    q^T adj(qr^T+rq^T) q=0.

Consequently the constant term in g of both det T and w^T adj(T)w is zero. With K_g=q^T adj(A)q, their coefficients of g are respectively m^2(m-1)h^2 K_g and m^3 h^3 K_g. Hence

    M_d(E)/g^(3m-3)
       =-m^2(d+m-1)h^3 K_g modulo g.                (T11)

This is a valid polynomial divisibility statement also for m=1: direct reduction of grad(gh) and Hess(gh) modulo g gives -d h^3 K_g. If M_d(E)=0, the nonzero scalar in (T11) implies g divides K_g.

We next show that an irreducible plane polynomial satisfying g|K_g is affine-linear. On the function field L of g=0, the tangent derivation

    D=g_y partial_x-g_x partial_y

is nonzero. If g_y is nonzero in L, then

    D(g_x/g_y)=K_g/g_y^2=0 in L.                     (T12)

The constants of a nonzero C-derivation of a one-variable function field over C are precisely C: a nonconstant invariant would be transcendental over C, making L finite and separable over C of that invariant, which forces the derivation to vanish. Thus g_x/g_y=lambda in C. The polynomial g_x-lambda g_y belongs to (g), and its degree is less than deg g, so it is identically zero. This first-order equation makes g a polynomial in one linear form; irreducibility over C then makes it affine-linear. If g_y is zero in L, degree gives g_y=0 identically, and g is an irreducible univariate polynomial in x, again affine-linear. This proves every irreducible factor of E is affine-linear.

Those lines must also be parallel. Let e=deg E>=2 and E_e be its highest homogeneous part. The highest homogeneous term in (T9) is M_d(E_e). Euler's identities, without any Hessian invertibility assumption, give

    Hess(E_e) (x,y)^T=(e-1)grad E_e,
    K_(E_e)=e/(e-1) E_e det Hess(E_e),
    M_d(E_e)=-(d+e-1)/(e-1) E_e det Hess(E_e).        (T13)

Therefore det Hess(E_e)=0. To reconstruct the binary homogeneous classification, write E_e=x^e p(y/x). Direct differentiation gives

    det Hess(E_e)
      =(e-1)x^(2e-4)[e p p''-(e-1)(p')^2].          (T14)

At a finite root of p with multiplicity j, the bracket has leading coefficient proportional to j(j-e). Thus every root of a nonconstant p has multiplicity e. Since deg p<=e, p is a scalar times (z-z0)^e; if p is constant, E_e is a scalar times x^e. In every case E_e is a pure power of a linear form. The leading linear parts of the already acquired affine factors of E must therefore all be proportional. Hence E is a polynomial in a single affine-linear form. Degrees zero and one are immediate. Conversely, for E=p(ell), both the Hessian and gradient are supported on the same constant one-dimensional direction, so det Hess E=K_E=0 and M_d(E)=0. This proves the lemma.

This lemma is not being claimed as historically new. It is related to the ordinary Hessian chain rule: locally the Hessian determinant of E^(-1/(d-1)) is proportional to E det Hess E-[d/(d-1)]K_E. Fox's primary reparameterization identities are a relevant older prerequisite; the factor-divisor classification and its application here have been reconstructed directly rather than imported from a source.

## The highest coefficient after an exact nonlinear source change

Assume both F,H are nonzero. Condition (T1) gives F_etaeta nonzero and d=deg_s H>=2. Write the highest s coefficient of H as a_d(b), and set

    E(b)=G2(b)a_d(b),     E nonzero.                  (T15)

Change coordinates by Phi(b,s,eta)=(b,rho=s-F(b,eta),eta). It is a polynomial automorphism with det JPhi=1. Put

    V=h+G1s+G2eta+G2H(b,s),
    W=V_s=G1+G2H_s.

The full nonlinear Hessian chain rule, including its gradient correction, is

    JPhi^T (Hess_old psi) JPhi
       = Hess_new V + W Hess_new F.                  (T16)

The plus sign is essential: the only nonlinear component of Phi is rho=s-F, whose Hessian is -Hess F; the old rho derivative of psi is W. A congruence without the correction term would give an invalid obstruction. Determinants of the two sides of (T16) are equal since det JPhi=1.

In the order (x,y,s,eta), the corrected matrix has blocks

    [ B    A    C ]
    [ A^T  U    0 ]
    [ C^T  0    Z ],

    B=Hess_b V+W Hess_b F,
    A=grad_b W,
    C=grad_b G2+W grad_b F_eta,
    U=G2 H_ss,
    Z=W F_etaeta.                                    (T17)

All base derivatives in this display are taken with s,eta fixed. An exact 4x4 block expansion gives

    det=UZ det B-Z A^T adj(B)A-U C^T adj(B)C
                      +(det[A,C])^2.                (T18)

The leading s coefficients and degree bounds are

    B=(Hess E)s^d+O(s^(d-1)),
    A=d(grad E)s^(d-1)+O(s^(d-2)),
    C=d E(grad F_eta)s^(d-1)+O(s^(d-2)),
    U=d(d-1)E s^(d-2)+O(s^(d-3)),
    Z=d E F_etaeta s^(d-1)+O(s^(d-2)).                (T19)

For d=2, U's remainder of order s^-1 means zero. The first two terms in (T18) have possible degree 4d-3; the last two have degree at most 4d-4. The coefficient of s^(4d-3) is therefore exactly

    d^2 E F_etaeta M_d(E).                            (T20)

The count includes base-dependent tails in both shears and the Hessian correction in (T16). No lower coefficient or arbitrary h can cancel (T20). Thus a constant Hessian determinant would force M_d(E)=0.

But G2 has a nonlinear irreducible factor, namely itself up to scalar. Indeed

    G2/2=(x^2-17)y+x^3+2x^2-25x.

The two coefficients are coprime in C[x]: the second has remainder 34-8x modulo x^2-17, and their resultant is 68. A primitive polynomial of degree one in y is irreducible in C[x,y]. Its total degree is three, so it is not affine-linear. Since G2 divides E, the cylinder lemma forbids M_d(E)=0. The nonzero polynomial coefficient (T20) proves the determinant is nonconstant whenever both nonlinear shears are present.

The argument stops this entire unrestricted-tail class without expanding any original composed Hessian determinant. It does not prove that every polynomial whose highest s coefficient contains G2 has this obstruction; the special corrected block structure (T17) is a load-bearing hypothesis.

The same derivation gives a reusable first-jet filter independent of the displayed D2: for arbitrary nonzero base coefficient G2 and two nonzero tails satisfying (T1), a constant Hessian determinant in (T2) forces G2 itself to be a polynomial in one affine-linear form. Indeed E=G2 a_d must be a polynomial in one form by (T20) and the cylinder lemma; all of its irreducible factors are parallel affine lines, so every divisor, including G2, is a polynomial in that same form. This is a necessary condition only. It neither constructs a constant-determinant chart when G2 passes the filter nor excludes longer shear words. For the acquired D2, primitive irreducibility makes rejection immediate.

This generalized necessary condition can be checked without factorization. A nonconstant G2 belongs to C[ell] for some affine-linear ell if and only if its gradient's polynomial coefficient vectors span dimension one. A nonzero constant annihilator of those vectors is a constant direction along which G2 has zero derivative; completing it to linear coordinates shows G2 is univariate. Conversely, a univariate polynomial has every gradient coefficient in the direction grad ell. For the acquired G2, the x^2 and xy gradient coefficient vectors are (6,2) and (4,0), with determinant -8. Thus a two-column rank certificate already rules out arbitrary tails in this class. The irreducible-factor calculation supplies a separate exact description of why the coefficient fails.

## One or both shears absent

If F=0 and H is nonzero, write psi=P(b,rho)+G2(b)eta. For any such potential with G2 independent of rho, expansion along the zero eta,eta entry shows that the coefficient of eta in its Hessian determinant is

    -P_rhorho K_(G2)=-G2 H_ss K_(G2).                (T21)

Here K_(G2)(0,0)=9248. Condition (T1) makes H_ss nonzero, so this coefficient is nonzero. The determinant is nonconstant. The same identity with rho and eta exchanged handles H=0,F nonzero:

    [rho] det Hess psi=-G1 F_etaeta K_(G1),
    K_(G1)(0,0)=-3700.                               (T22)

If both vanish, the residual-residual block is zero and

    det Hess[h+G1rho+G2eta]=(Jac G)^2,                (T23)

which is nonconstant by (T5). This completes all cases of the theorem. The values in (T21) and (T22) are exact polynomial evaluations, not numerical curvature estimates.

The direct determinant in the original composed coordinates was not expanded. No fixture sweep or sparse coefficient search is used to infer the universal conclusion.

For acquired dense rational F,H of total degrees n,m and coefficient bit length L, finding d and a_d, multiplying a_d by fixed G2 and forming its two-variable derivatives are polynomial-cost exact operations in that input representation. The symbolic theorem can reject this class just from the fixed irreducible divisor G2 and the identities (T1); it does not require building the much larger composed potential or its determinant. When both shears are present n,m>=2, deg Theta<=2+nm and deg psi<=max(deg h,3+nm). The displayed inverse has degree at most 3nm in the base and output variables, since multiplication by D2^-1 first gives z of degree at most3. These are upper bounds, not assertions of equality or minimum chart degree.

Explicitly outputting the composed dense polynomial can expand support to O((nm+deg h)^4) in the four variables. Coefficient growth remains polynomial in n,m,L in this dense interface: placing the O(n^3+m^3) rational input coefficients over common denominators costs O((n^3+m^3)L) bits, and substituting into at most m powers of F multiplies this height by at most polynomial factors, with the usual multinomial logarithmic overhead. The degree of h and its coefficient height are paid separately. The actual evidence records runtime and expanded support. No polynomial bound is asserted for arbitrary compressed arithmetic-circuit input without an additional identity-testing interface.
