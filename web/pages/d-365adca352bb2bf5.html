NARROW PRIOR-ART CHECK: POSITIVE FINITE-RANK DENSITY RECONSTRUCTION
Date: 2026-10-07. Scope: primary-source closest analogues, not novelty clearance.

Target operator and translation
-------------------------------
For nonnegative partition of unity phi_j, reference density r, and
m_j = integral r phi_j > 0, the target is
 A_r f = r sum_j phi_j (integral phi_j f)/m_j.
Writing g=f/r and dmu=r dx gives A_r f=r D_mu g, where
 D_mu g=sum_j phi_j (integral phi_j g dmu)/(integral phi_j dmu).
This is the normalized Durrmeyer averaging architecture. In the unweighted
one-dimensional B-spline case it is an already-published positive spline
operator, not a new operator architecture. The arbitrary-measure weighted
polynomial version is also established. These identifications do not assert
that an arbitrary reference-weighted, tensor-hat finite-rank error theorem
appears verbatim in the cited papers.

1. Exact unweighted spline analogue: Sablonniere (1988)
------------------------------------------------------
P. Sablonniere, Positive spline operators and orthogonal splines,
Journal of Approximation Theory 52(1) (1988), 28-42.
Primary publisher identifier: https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9045(88)90035-4
Publisher article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021904588900354

Formula is independently displayed in the author-primary full text of source
2 below, introduction equations (3), (4), (6): V f=sum_i <M_i,f>N_i,
M_i=N_i/(integral N_i), where N_i are normalized B-splines on a finite
interval with repeated boundary knots. Degree one gives the ordinary linear
hat basis. Thus the r=1, s=1 target has exactly this integral-kernel form.
The 1988 paper studies Lp convergence and spectral properties. Its full text
was not obtained in this bounded check; do not attribute a particular rate,
saturation class, or boundary theorem to it without reading the original.
Some later bibliographies incorrectly list volume 54; multiple primary
references and DOI metadata point to volume 52.

Primary author survey: P. Sablonniere, Recent progress on univariate and
multivariate polynomial and spline quasi-interpolants (2004 preprint),
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405033
Pages 2-3 describe positive integral spline operators with normalized spline
kernels and Lebesgue scalar products; the full 1988 theorem still needs
inspection for any precise theorem imported from it.

2. Direct primary text verifying the spline formula and operator properties
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
L. Gori, F. Pitolli, E. Santi, Positive operators based on scaling functions,
Matematicheskoe Modelirovanie 14(5) (2002), 116-126.
Author-institution record: https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/67103
Author-uploaded full text:
 https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260383357_Positive_operators_based_on_scaling_functions
Official journal issue:
 https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?issue=5&jrnid=mm&option_lang=eng&volume=14&wshow=issue&year=2002

Besides recalling the exact normalized B-spline averaging operator, this
paper replaces B-splines by normalized totally positive refinable bases.
Equations (22)-(23) define the same normalized-integral architecture.
Theorem 2.1 states positivity, self-adjointness and unit L2 operator norm,
and equation (24) states preservation of the integral. This is directly
relevant to positive finite rank Markov approximation. Theorem 3.3 gives
real simple positive eigenvalues, with largest eigenvalue 1 and constant
eigenfunction, for the paper's linearly independent totally positive bases.
Scope: unweighted finite interval; no arbitrary reference-density theorem
or s-dimensional corner estimate was verified here.

Related primary precursor: L. Gori, F. Pitolli, E. Santi, Positive Refinable
Operators, Numerical Algorithms 28 (2001), 199-213.
 https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1014003102167
 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1014003102167
Its publisher abstract confirms generalization of the positive B-spline
operator through normalized totally positive scaling-function bases and
study of approximation properties. Only its abstract was read; no precise
rate should be imported from this check.

3. Exact arbitrary-measure polynomial weighting analogue
--------------------------------------------------------
E. Berdysheva and K. Jetter, Multivariate Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators with
arbitrary weight functions, Journal of Approximation Theory 162 (2010),
576-598.
 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jat.2009.11.005

Definition 1.1 / equation (1.4) uses
 M_{n,rho}g=sum_{|alpha|=n} B_alpha
                 <g,B_alpha>_rho/<1,B_alpha>_rho.
It is the target's weighted-likelihood-ratio averaging formula with the
Bernstein partition replacing the hat partition. Assumption P requires
positive rho-integral of every nonzero nonnegative polynomial; rho is
normalized to probability. Section 2 establishes symmetry (2.2) and
constant reproduction (2.4). This is on the simplex, using polynomial
bases, not the target's tensor hats on the cube.

E. Berdysheva and K. Li, On Lp-convergence of Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators
with respect to arbitrary measure, Publications de l'Institut Mathematique
96(110) (2014), 23-29.
 https://doi.org/10.2298/PIM1410023B
Full primary PDF:
 https://ftp.gwdg.de/pub/misc/EMIS/journals/PIMB/110/n110p023.pdf

Definition 1.1 / equation (1.2) gives the same normalized-mu formula. A
support point in the simplex interior suffices for positive denominators.
Theorem 1.1 gives, for 1<=p<infinity,
 ||g-M_n g||_{Lp(mu)} <= 2 K(g, C_p d n^(-1/2) mu(S^d)^(1/p))_p,
where K uses C1 approximants and the supremum gradient seminorm; C_p
depends only on p and is 1/2 for 1<=p<=2. This is a quantitative weighted
Lp convergence result for arbitrary finite measure and the polynomial
basis; it is not a second-order local-hat density bound.

E. Berdysheva, Uniform convergence of Bernstein-Durrmeyer operators with
respect to arbitrary measure, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 394(1) (2012), 324-336.
 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2012.03.004
Theorem 1 gives uniform convergence on the whole simplex for every
continuous function if and only if the measure has full simplex support.
This concerns whole-domain uniform convergence, not merely denominators
being positive or convergence in Lp of the reference measure.

4. Higher-order integral quasi-interpolation is a different interface
--------------------------------------------------------------------
P. Sablonniere and D. Sbibih, Spline integral operators exact on polynomials,
Approximation Theory and its Applications 10(3) (1994), 56-73.
 https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02836819
 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02836819

The publisher abstract specifies spline-integral operators with coefficients
formed using linear combinations of nearby B-splines. It establishes
existence/uniqueness for exactness on polynomial degrees 2m<k, studies Lp
norms and smooth-function error bounds, and partially studies partition-
independent Linfinity boundedness. This should be consulted for higher-order
claims. The abstract does not establish the nonnegative stochastic encoder
and decoder required by the target. Do not treat a signed quasi-interpolant
as an admissible Markov-density reconstruction without checking positivity.

5. Boundary obstruction: exact audit calculation, not a literature theorem
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
For r=1 on [0,1], uniform mesh h=1/n with standard endpoint hats, n>=2,
take g(x)=x. The left endpoint hat has integral h/2 and weighted centroid
h/3. All interior hat centroids equal their nodes. Hence
 D_h x-x=(h-x)/3 on [0,h],
           0 on [h,1-h],
          -(x-(1-h))/3 on [1-h,1].
For 1<=p<infinity:
 ||D_h x-x||_p^p=2 h^(p+1)/(3^p(p+1)).
For p=infinity the error is h/3. In particular L1 error=h^2/3,
whereas the Lp error has order h^(1+1/p), p>1. To make g a likelihood
ratio of a probability density, take g=1+a(x-1/2) with |a|<2; the same
error is simply multiplied by |a|. Tensor-product hats on [0,1]^s with
g depending on x_1 inherit the identical face-layer calculation.
Therefore an unrestricted global O(h^2) Lp claim for p>1 is false for
this operator even on affine, positive smooth likelihood ratios.
An L1 second-order theorem remains compatible with this calculation.

Followup construction suggested to parent (own derivation, not prior-art
claim): smoothly graded knots x_i=F(i/n), F(t)=3t^2-2t^3 have endpoint
widths O(n^-2), maximum width O(n^-1), and adjacent width differences
O(n^-2). A hat on support [x_i-a,x_i+b] has unweighted centroid
x_i+(b-a)/3. Thus the affine bias can be O(n^-2) everywhere, including
the endpoints. This may recover second-order global Lp errors with
positive compact hats at the same rank order; it requires direct proof
and its own precise priority check. A smooth positive reference weight
should perturb each centroid by O(maxwidth^2), not O(maxwidth).

6. Unresolved bibliography lead, not relied on as a verified primary theorem
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
M. W. Muller, Degree of Lp-approximation by integral Schoenberg splines,
JAT 21(4) (1977), 385-393; earlier Dortmund reports in 1976 have a similar
title. The exact averaging kernel and theorem were not inspected. It may
be a Kantorovich interval-averaging variant rather than the self-dual
normalized spline kernel. Do not conflate the two before full-text review.

Check limitations
-----------------
No novelty clearance. No complete review of spline density-estimation,
finite Markov/Ulam approximation, or tensor product weighted spline
literature. Strong conclusion supported: the operator architecture is
classical; the exact claimed weighted/tensor error theorem needs its own
proof and a closer theorem-level priority check. Second-order L1 is not
equivalent to second-order Lp for these ordinary boundary hats.
