# A fully supplied controlled-identification calibration

Status: independently derived scoped theorem; classic mechanism and EXACT MODEL are prior art. No new foundational discovery is claimed. Executable audit and finite-error constants are reconstructed here.

## Exact question and interface

There are three fixed hypotheses i=0,1,2 and three actions u=0,1,2. At a cost of one per experiment, the learner chooses u and sees Y in {0,1}. Under truth i, Y is conditionally Bernoulli(4/5) if u=i and Bernoulli(1/5) otherwise; observations are fresh conditional on the chosen action. All six probabilities are strictly positive. The hypotheses, action meanings, likelihoods, and p,q are supplied to every procedure. The learner does not know truth i. Every procedure may retain its complete transcript, stop using the observed data, and use randomization. A procedure is delta-correct when P_i(decision != i) <= delta for every i and terminates almost surely. Expected experiment count is the scientific cost. Online arithmetic and simulator runtime are separately charged in the ledger.

The fixed-frequency comparator class chooses one deterministic probability vector w before observing anything, then draws actions independently from w. It may use ANY stopping/decision rule and identical knowledge. A stronger fixed comparator cycles through the three actions, with an arbitrary possibly randomized initial offset. The ordinary competent adaptive comparator is classical ML/Chernoff control; our procedure IS this comparator. No claimed benefit over it exists.

## KL, both orientations, and optimization

Put h=log(4), r=p-q=3/5, D=r*h. For i != j, the KL divergence under truth i against j at action u is D when u=i or u=j, and zero otherwise. The equality of the two nonzero orientations uses q=1-p: D(Ber(p)||Ber(q))=D(Ber(q)||Ber(p))=D. For general p,q the two orientations are a=D(p||q) and b=D(q||p), respectively. The per-truth optimized bottleneck is max(a,b/2); uniform fixed actions give (a+b)/3. The executable includes a nonsymmetric p=.8,q=.3 tensor audit so a false orientation swap cannot hide behind symmetry.

In the symmetric model, a fixed vector w has truth-i rate d_i(w)=D*min_{j != i}(w_i+w_j). Its optimal minimax rate is 2D/3, attained by w=(1/3,1/3,1/3): sum of the three pairwise rates equals 2D, so the smallest is at most 2D/3. For the uniform-prior expected-cost objective we also need sum_i 1/d_i. Write sorted weights x<=y<=z. The three rates are D(x+y),D(x+y),D(x+z). Their sum is D(1+2x+y)<=2D because x<=z. Cauchy gives (sum_i 1/d_i)/3 >=3/(2D), with equality only at uniform weights. Zero rates imply infinite cost for vanishing-error identification. This is an analytic optimization over the full simplex; the rational grid is just a check.

If truth i were known for experiment selection, repeatedly selecting i gives KL D against both alternatives. No action can provide more than D against a fixed alternative, so the adaptive optimized rate is exactly D. We next acquire this rate without knowing i.

## Executable ML-target procedure and finite-error certificate

Maintain three integer scores C_k initially zero. When action u returns Y, update only C_u by +1 if Y=1 and -1 if Y=0. The likelihood of hypothesis k equals a common baseline likelihood times exp(h*C_k), so these scores exactly retain all relevant likelihood information.

Choose a current maximum-score hypothesis as the next action, breaking ties by any rule. Stop once the largest score exceeds the second largest by B=ceil(log(2/delta)/h), and output its index. The pairwise log likelihood ratio is h(C_i-C_j). Under truth i the process exp(h(C_j-C_i)) is a nonnegative mean-one likelihood-ratio martingale under ANY predictable randomized action selection. Ville's inequality bounds the probability it ever reaches exp(hB) by exp(-hB). A wrong decision j requires that crossing for pair j versus i. A union bound over the two wrong indices yields P_i(error)<=2exp(-hB)<=delta. This guarantee does not come from finite simulations.

## Expected wrong-target count: complete occupation argument

Fix truth i and one wrong target j. Consider only experiments with actions i or j. At each such experiment the increment of S=C_i-C_j is +1 with probability p=4/5 and -1 with probability q=1/5, independently of its previous values, despite adaptive selection. Action i gives +1 when Y=1; action j gives +1 when Y=0. Both events have probability p under truth i. Thus the pairwise sequence indexed by informative experiments is a simple positive-drift random walk. It can be extended with fresh independent increments if there are finitely many selected pair experiments.

Whenever the ML rule selects wrong target j, its current score is at least C_i, so S<=0 immediately before that informative increment. Different wrong-target-j experiments correspond to different pairwise walk indices. Hence their number is pathwise at most the walk's total visits to nonpositive states, including its initial zero.

For completeness, the probability that a p>q walk starting at zero ever reaches -k is (q/p)^k. This follows by solving the two-sided finite-interval gambler's-ruin recurrence f(s)=p f(s+1)+q f(s-1), whose solutions are A+B(q/p)^s, and sending the upper absorbing boundary to infinity. A walk one level below a target reaches that level almost surely, by the positive-drift strong law. Starting at any level, the probability of returning after departing is p*(q/p)+q*1=2q. Therefore its expected total visits after first reaching that level is 1/(1-2q)=1/(p-q). The expected number of visits to -k from zero is (q/p)^k/(p-q). Summing k=0,1,... gives p/(p-q)^2=20/9. Each wrong target therefore costs at most 20/9 experiments in expectation; the two together cost at most E_i W_infinity<=40/9. Pairwise walks may be correlated; only their individual expectations and linearity are used.

## Expected stopping time: bounded-time optional expectation

Extend the ML policy indefinitely, ignoring its stopping decision. Let Z(t) be the signed sum of outcomes from truth-target-i experiments only, N_i(t) their count, and W(t) the count of other experiments. Set V(t)=Z(t)-W(t). For every j!=i, C_i(t)-C_j(t)=Z(t)+Z_j(t), where Z_j is the signed pairwise contribution from action j, and Z_j>=-N_j>=-W. Hence C_i-C_j>=V. Let T be the first t with V(t)>=B. The actual confidence stopping time tau is at most T because at T every true-versus-wrong margin is >=B.

For T_n=min(T,n), increments from truth-target actions have conditional mean r, whereas other actions reduce V by exactly one. Bounded-time optional expectation gives E V(T_n)=r E N_i(T_n)-E W(T_n)=r E T_n-(r+1)E W(T_n). Before T the potential is less than B; at T its upward step is at most one. Thus V(T_n)<=B+1 pathwise. With E W(T_n)<=40/9,

    E_i tau <= E_i T <= (B+1)/r + (1+1/r)*(40/9)
                       = 5(B+1)/3 + 320/27.                 (A)

The uniform bound on E T_n and monotone convergence prove E T<infinity, so there is no circular integrability assumption. In the integer model an overshoot-free B replaces B+1 and tightens (A); we keep the displayed conservative bound in every reported comparison.

## Lower bounds on all equally informed fixed IID tests

Let K_delta=kl(1-delta,delta)=(1-2delta)log((1-delta)/delta). For any delta-correct test and i!=j, data processing the stopped transcript to the event {decision=i} gives transcript KL>=K_delta. This uses P_i(event)>=1-delta, P_j(event)<=delta and monotonicity of binary KL for these probability ranges. For fresh IID action frequencies w and E_i tau<infinity, the chain rule and bounded increment optional expectation give transcript KL=D(w_i+w_j) E_i tau. Sampling actions themselves contributes no likelihood ratio because the same policy conditional on the same transcript is used under both hypotheses. Therefore E_i tau>=K_delta/d_i(w). The simplex argument yields

    (E_0 tau+E_1 tau+E_2 tau)/3 >= 3K_delta/(2D).          (F)

This lower bound applies to every stopping and decision rule in the stated fixed IID class, not merely our simulated threshold rule. Infinite expected cost already satisfies the bound.

For balanced periodic actions, every prefix of length t has N_i(t)+N_j(t)<=2t/3+2/3, for every initial offset. Chain-rule KL therefore yields E_i tau>=3K_delta/(2D)-1 for ANY delta-correct stopping and decision rule with this action schedule. This covers the stronger reduced-variance fixed baseline too.

For ANY adaptive procedure each pairwise divergence is at most D per experiment, so E_i tau>=K_delta/D. Combining with (A) establishes asymptotic optimality of the ML-target policy, E_i tau~log(1/delta)/D. IID uniform actions and balanced periodic actions attain log(1/delta)/(2D/3): for each pair their score differences have positive drift 2r/3; Hoeffding bounds beyond (1+epsilon)*3B/(2r) give an exponentially summable tail for the probability that either true-versus-wrong margin remains <B. Summing these bounds shows the matching limsup and (F) supplies the liminf. Thus the first-order sample-cost ratio is exactly 3/2.

## Strict finite comparison and run

At delta=10^-12, B=21, (A)=1310/27=48.5185185. Bound (F) is 49.8289214; its balanced-periodic analogue is 48.8289214. The adaptive method is therefore provably strictly cheaper in uniform-prior expected samples than EVERY delta-correct fixed IID test and EVERY delta-correct test under a balanced periodic schedule, even without trusting simulation. The finite KL arithmetic uses ordinary double precision; the analytic gap exceeds .31 samples for the periodic comparator and is insensitive to rounding at this precision.

All three record zero empirical errors at this tolerance. This finite sample cannot verify 10^-12 error probability; the martingale argument provides that guarantee. The decisive limitation is that all action likelihoods and their hypothesis-target correspondence are already supplied. This says nothing about acquiring an unknown class, designing new scientifically meaningful interventions, misspecification, causal identification, or general intelligence. A standard equally informed adaptive statistical baseline obtains the entire advantage. The theorem does not cover arbitrary predetermined nonstationary action schedules with unrestricted unbalanced prefixes; those may correlate sampling proportions with the data-dependent stopping time. Nor does it cover an arbitrary initially randomized mixture of frequency vectors with only unconditional error constraints. Keeping these stronger comparator questions explicit avoids an overbroad open-loop claim.

Concrete downstream use: this is a cheap calibration and falsification fixture for proposed uncertainty-driven research agents. If a new method cannot match the ordinary adaptive ML rule on the supplied three-target interface, its purported experiment-design mechanism is unsubstantiated. If it matches it, that still is only classical active testing. Next falsifiable extension: hide the action likelihoods or add controlled likelihood misspecification and compare against an equally informed joint-estimation/active-testing baseline, charging calibration observations. The bounded wrong-target occupation proof then breaks at the fixed known pairwise increment law; that is the exact assumption to replace.
