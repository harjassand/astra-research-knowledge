2. Exact scientific output and assumptions
Consider a finite, connected, reversible continuous-time Markov chain. Let \(q_{ij}\) be its transition rates and \(w_i>0\) unnormalised equilibrium weights. Reversibility supplies symmetric conductances
\[
c_{ij}=w_iq_{ij}=w_jq_{ji},
\qquad
Z=\sum_iw_i.
\]
For two distinct singleton terminal states \(s,t\), define the capacity
\[
C_G=
\min_{\substack{h_s=1\\h_t=0}}
\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}
c_{ij}(h_i-h_j)^2,
\]
where each undirected edge is counted once.
The requested output is the equilibrium reactive flux
\[
\boxed{\nu_{st}=C_G/Z.}
\]
The acceptance criterion was a numerical enclosure \([\nu_-,\nu_+]\) satisfying
\[
\frac{\nu_+}{\nu_-}\leq1.01.
\]
This asks for relative accuracy in the rare quantity itself, not a small bulk-distribution error.
It also fixes an important boundary. This flux determines the singleton mean round-trip time:
\[
\mathbb E_s[\tau_t]+\mathbb E_t[\tau_s]
=\frac{Z}{C_G}
=\frac1{\nu_{st}}.
\]
It does not, by itself, determine either directional mean first-passage time or a first-passage tail. The exact distinction becomes decisive below.
All conductances and equilibrium masses are assumed supplied. The theorem does not manufacture a complete molecular landscape, discover missing transition states, or validate rate constants. Nor does it extend automatically to driven, nonreversible kinetics.
3. The mathematical mechanism
The operation removes weak connections and contracts strong connections, but uses separate additive budgets in conductance and resistance rather than an uncontrolled threshold.
A terminal-flux sandwich
Delete a set of edges \(D\), with total conductance
\[
S=\sum_{e\in D}c_e.
\]
In the retained graph, select connected clusters to contract, without merging \(s\) and \(t\). Choose a spanning tree within each cluster, giving a forest \(F\), and define
\[
r=\sum_{e\in F}\frac1{c_e}.
\]
Let \(H\) be the resulting reduced terminal component, with capacity \(C_H\).
Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_H}{1+C_Hr}
\;\leq\;
C_G
\;\leq\;
C_H+S.
}
\tag{1}
\]
This bounds the requested terminal response, not all eigenvalues, all transition probabilities, or the complete dynamics.
Why the bound holds
Deletion error. A capacity-minimising potential can be clipped to \([0,1]\). Restoring a deleted edge therefore adds at most its conductance to the trial energy. If \(C_d\) is the capacity after deletion,
\[
C_d\leq C_G\leq C_d+S.
\]
Contraction error. Contraction lowers effective resistance. Conversely, lift the reduced graph’s unit electrical flow back through the cluster trees. The reduced electrical flow decomposes into simple terminal paths of total flow one. Consequently, each chosen tree edge needs to carry absolute flow at most one. The additional dissipated energy is at most \(r\).
Writing \(R=1/C\),
\[
R_H\leq R_d\leq R_H+r.
\]
Combining these inequalities gives (1).
The PROOF.md includes disconnected components, parallel connections, the adaptive selection rule, and numerical arithmetic.
Choosing reductions before solving the full problem
A widest-path calculation supplies a path \(P\) and the lower bound
\[
L_0=
\left(\sum_{e\in P}\frac1{c_e}\right)^{-1}
\leq C_G.
\]
A cut obtained from the same bottleneck construction supplies an upper bound \(U_0\).
The implemented selection rule uses
\[
S\leq\varepsilon L_0,
\qquad
r\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{U_0}.
\]
It deletes a weakest-edge prefix and accepts strongest forest edges while respecting these budgets. The path remains intact, and contraction does not merge the terminals. The resulting reduced capacity satisfies
\[
L_0\leq C_H\leq U_0.
\]
Thus the ratio between the two sides of (1) is bounded by
\[
\frac{C_H+S}{C_H/(1+C_Hr)}
=
\left(1+\frac{S}{C_H}\right)(1+C_Hr)
\leq(1+\varepsilon)^2.
\]
With \(\varepsilon=0.004\),
\[
(1+\varepsilon)^2=1.008016,
\]
leaving room within the 1% target for numerical certification.
A consequence concerning extreme rate ranges
A size-only version yields a retained conductance range polynomial in \(n,m,\varepsilon^{-1}\), even when the original conductances span exponentially many orders of magnitude.
If \(c_*\) is the widest-path bottleneck, deleting below
\[
\frac{\varepsilon c_*}{m(n-1)}
\]
and contracting above
\[
\frac{m(n-1)c_*}{\varepsilon}
\]
gives the same type of relative guarantee. The remaining unaggregated conductance ratio is at most
\[
\left[\frac{m(n-1)}{\varepsilon}\right]^2.
\]
This gives a precise sense in which irrelevant numerical stiffness can be removed for one terminal task.
It does not guarantee a small graph. A network may have many indispensable connections inside that window. It also does not remove input-reading costs or establish a better generic complexity bound than existing Laplacian algorithms.

q(\sigma,\sigma^{(i)})

5. Numerical precision was a real failure mode—and was repaired
The initial float64 prototype exposed precisely the issue your prompt warned about.
At low temperature, its analytic reduction interval could be narrower than floating-point error. Subtracting nearly equal logarithmic endpoints could even display a false zero width. A correct truncation theorem did not make that computation certified.
The final implementation separates the two obligations:
\[
\text{total enclosure}
=
\text{reduction-error control}
+
\text{arithmetic-error control}.
\]
For the Ising model it recomputes conductances from exact model energies, integer multiplicities and exact decimal temperatures. It encloses exponential evaluations, deleted-conductance sums, forest resistances and the full original partition sum. The exponential enclosure relies on documented correctly rounded Decimal operations and outward neighbouring values. Python documentation
The reduced solver uses positive star–mesh updates,
\[
c'_{ij}
=
c_{ij}
+
\frac{c_{ik}c_{kj}}{\sum_\ell c_{k\ell}},
\]
with explicit symmetry preservation after each rounded update.
The rounding argument exploits positivity. Each rounded elimination step is enclosed by coefficientwise multiplicative perturbations. Capacity responds monotonically and homogeneously to such perturbations, allowing them to be accumulated without inserting a generic matrix condition-number factor.
For decimal precision \(p\), take \(u=5\,10^{-p}\). With the recorded operation exponent \(M\), the accumulated inflation is bounded by
\[
F\leq\frac1{1-Mu},
\qquad Mu<1.
\]
In the main run, \(M=14{,}278\) at 45 digits; rounding inflation is negligible beside the deliberate reduction tolerance.
This is a proof-backed numerical enclosure conditional on the stated arithmetic and correct supplied graph. It is not mechanically verified software and does not enclose physical-model uncertainty.
6. The strongest competitor changes the novelty assessment
Three established capabilities prevent a foundational claim here.
Exact Schur/Kron reduction already eliminates internal states while preserving terminal response. A small terminal representation alone is therefore not new; its acquisition cost is central. arXiv
Stable graph transformation already handles highly metastable kinetic networks. I inspected PyGT’s actual implementation: it uses block elimination, stability-triggered single-node fallback, corrections that avoid subtracting nearly equal probabilities, and updates to waiting-time information. An independent classical positive-elimination baseline was executed here; the packaged PyGT implementation was not.  
Most importantly, terminal-specific deletion and contraction already have direct prior art. Li and Schild’s Spectral Subspace Sparsification constructs response-focused reweighted graph minors using contraction, deletion and reweighting, with almost-linear-time results and applications to selected-pair effective resistances. This is much closer than a generic “related graph algorithm.” arXiv
The defensible contribution of this tranche is therefore narrower:
An explicit deterministic conductance/resistance budget and an arithmetic-aware implementation for rare terminal-flux calculations.

Its exact priority remains unestablished. No better generic asymptotic bound was proved, and no victory over the strongest terminal-sparsification or graph-transformation implementation was demonstrated.
The task-first methodology worked here partly by preventing an attractive but unsupported novelty claim.
7. The important scientific obstruction: flux is not directional lifetime
The exact directional identity is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_s[\tau_t]
=
\frac{\sum_iw_i h_i}{C_G},
}
\]
where \(h_i\) is the probability of reaching \(s\) before \(t\).
The current reduction controls \(C_G\) and retains \(Z\). It does not automatically control the numerator
\[
N_{st}=\sum_iw_i h_i.
\]
A three-state example makes the missing information explicit.
Let
\[
w_s=w_t=1,\qquad w_z=M,\qquad c_{st}=1.
\]
Connect \(z\) to \(s\) by an edge of conductance \(\delta>0\). Then
\[
C_G=1,\qquad Z=M+2,\qquad
\mathbb E_s[\tau_t]=M+1.
\]
Move that same leaf edge so that \(z\) is attached to \(t\). Now
\[
C_G=1,\qquad Z=M+2,
\]
but
\[
\mathbb E_s[\tau_t]=1.
\]
Thus identical flux and total equilibrium mass can coexist with arbitrarily different directional lifetimes.
There is a further warning. In the source-attached case, the mean is independent of \(\delta\), while the variance diverges as \(\delta\to0\). Even matching the mean would not certify a rare first-passage tail.
The current operation does preserve the narrower round-trip mean \(Z/C_G\). Extending it to a directional lifetime requires a certified bound on \(N_{st}\), not a relabelling of the flux result. Existing graph transformation’s retention of waiting-time information is a substantive advantage for that task. PyGT
This is the main mathematical obstruction worth preserving: can the occupation-weighted committor be enclosed without reconstructing the full kinetic solution?
8. Acquisition costs and physical accuracy remain unresolved
After the weighted graph is supplied, the present implementation has sorting/aggregation costs of order \(O(m\log m)\), near-linear forest work, and \(O(n+m)\) pre-solver storage. Its current core solver has \(O(k^2)\) dense-storage and \(O(k^3)\) worst-case arithmetic bounds, although the successful cores have much less fill.
There is no general expected-time advantage theorem. In the worst case, reduction leaves essentially the entire graph.
More importantly, the Ising generator still enumerates
\[
N2^{N-1}
\]
microscopic edges and additionally sorts their symmetry-orbit labels. Its storage remains \(O(N2^N)\). The reduction has not removed exponential microscopic acquisition.
For molecular networks, unobserved pathways cannot be assigned a small deleted-conductance budget merely because they have not been found. A valid upper bound on their aggregate contribution must come from additional physical information.
Numerical precision also cannot substitute for parameter accuracy. For an Arrhenius factor, a barrier uncertainty of \(0.001\) at \(T=0.01\) produces a multiplicative change
\[
e^{0.001/0.01}=e^{0.1}\approx1.105.
\]
That is approximately 10.5%, already larger than the chosen numerical tolerance. No such physical uncertainty enclosure was acquired in this work.
