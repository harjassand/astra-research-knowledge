# Vector-valued experiments and ranks of operator differences

Status: independently derived conditional theorem; separate reconstruction by `check_vector_measurements` validates the rank argument and sharpens regularity to C1 for injectivity, C2 for immersion. No assertion of publication novelty. PDE application delegated to `rank_pde`; it is not yet established. Motivation: §1.5 of Alberti–Poletti–Sanna–Santacesaria, arXiv:2608.14324v1 (14 August 2026).

## Exact interface

All vector spaces are real. M is a second-countable C1 manifold of dimension d (C2 for the immersion statement). H is a separable real Hilbert space, Y a real Banach space. F:M→L(H,Y) is C1 in operator norm (C2 for the immersion statement). Each experiment supplies f∼N(0,C), where C is trace class, positive and injective, and observes the **whole noiseless vector** F(a)f. Inputs are independent. This is not a scalar-measurement or finite-precision guarantee.

**Theorem (rank sufficient condition).** Suppose rank(F(a)−F(b))≥r for all a≠b, for a positive integer r. If k r>2d, then with probability one the map a↦(F(a)f₁,…,F(a)f_k) is injective on all of M. In particular, infinite-rank differences imply that one such experiment suffices for every fixed finite-dimensional C1 model class.

If additionally rank(dF_a[v])≥r_t for every a and nonzero v∈T_aM and k r_t>2d−1, the sampled map is an immersion almost surely. When M is smoothly embedded in a Banach space X, on any compact K⊂M the same realization obeys a lower Lipschitz estimate ||a−b||_X≤C_K max_i||(F(a)−F(b))f_i||_Y with finite realization-dependent C_K.

These statements require ranks on **all pairs and all tangent directions**, not almost every parameter separately. Finite-dimensional source differences need not have high rank even when F itself is injective.

## Proof of injectivity

Write f=Σ_j sqrt(λ_j) ξ_j e_j in an eigenbasis of C, λ_j>0; the ξ_j are independent standard normals and the series converges in H almost surely. Let E_n=span(e₁,…,e_n).

1. For each pair p=(a,b) off the diagonal, density of ∪E_n and rank(F(a)−F(b))≥r imply that the restricted operator has rank at least r for some finite n. There then exist r continuous functionals on Y for which the r×n matrix of the restricted operator has rank r. This follows by selecting r independent images and Hahn–Banach functionals dual to their span. A nonzero r×r minor stays nonzero on a parameter-pair neighbourhood U_p.
2. The pair manifold off its diagonal is second-countable, so these neighbourhoods have a countable subcover U. Work in one such coordinate neighbourhood; use its fixed n and r output functionals.
3. Condition on the independent Gaussian tails h_i=(I−P_n)f_i for all k experiments. The projected collision equations in the finite Gaussian coordinates x_i∈R^n are A(a,b)x_i+c_i(a,b)=0 (i=1,…,k). The coefficient matrix A has row rank r throughout U. The map defining these kr equations has surjective derivative in the x coordinates (block diagonal A). Its zero set is therefore a C1 manifold of dimension 2d+kn−kr; this remains true for every fixed collection of H-valued tails because F is operator-norm C1.
4. The projection of this manifold into R^(kn) has Lebesgue measure zero when kr>2d: cover it by countably many smooth charts and use that a C¹ image of lower dimension is null. Hence the conditional probability of any collision with (a,b)∈U is zero. The finite Gaussian coordinates have a nonsingular density and are independent of the tails.
5. Cover each pair neighbourhood by countably many compact parameter pieces K. The event B_K={inputs (f₁,…,f_k): there exists (a,b)∈K with every output difference zero} is closed in H^k: for convergent input sequences, compactness supplies a convergent subsequence of pair witnesses, and operator continuity preserves zero. It is therefore Borel. Apply ordinary Fubini to the Gaussian head/tail decomposition; each tail section is null by step4. Countably many K cover the off-diagonal pair manifold.

The use of **fixed finite coordinates on a neighbourhood** is essential; merely noting that each fixed pair collides with probability zero would not justify an uncountable union.

## Immersion and compact stability

Cover the projectivized tangent bundle by C1 charts. Its dimension is 2d−1. Apply exactly the finite-coordinate argument to the operator dF_a[v], using any local nonzero representative of the projective direction. Bad sampled derivatives form a null event when kr_t>2d−1. Thus sampled map is injective and immersive.

For a compact embedded K, suppose the inverse Lipschitz estimate fails. There are pairs a_j,b_j∈K with sampled output distance divided by ||a_j−b_j||_X tending to zero. Compactness supplies limits a,b. If a≠b, continuity contradicts injectivity. If a=b, use one local smooth coordinate chart. Its derivative is injective, so chart distance and ambient X distance are locally equivalent. Normalize coordinate secants, extract a unit direction, and Taylor-expand the sampled map. The limiting derivative vanishes in that direction, contradicting immersion. Upper Lipschitz is similarly local plus compactness. No computable constant or useful noise tolerance follows from this argument.

## Finite-dimensional refinement

For analytic F:M→R^(m×q), stratify the off-diagonal pair space by analytic rank and smooth strata S of dimension s_S. On a rank-r_S stratum, k random inputs with an absolutely continuous joint law on R^(kq) avoid every collision provided k r_S>s_S. Repeat for tangent-operator rank strata for immersion. Rank-defect loci may therefore matter more than the global minimum rank. Countable local real-analytic stratifications suffice; they are not necessarily computationally available.

## Consequences, examples and limitations

* Rank is a measure of the number of genuinely independent response constraints per input. Counting output coordinates alone is invalid because they may be identical functions of the input.
* For F(a)=diag(a₁,…,a_d), one input with all coordinates nonzero recovers a exactly, although the uniform rank bound is only one. Rank stratification explains this: pairs differing in r coordinates have dimension d+r, but common nuisance coordinates do not enter differences; reducing to the effective difference parameter gives dimension r and injectivity also follows directly. The stated sufficient criterion is not necessary.
* For a scalar row F(a)=aᵀ with a∈R^d, fewer than d vector-input experiments can never recover all a; each output is still scalar. Full operator injectivity alone does not imply one-input identifiability.
* Infinite-rank differences can have singular values decaying arbitrarily fast. Whole-vector noiseless uniqueness does not imply an implementable finite detector count, stable reconstruction or advantageous experimental cost.
* Before claiming a new PDE result one must prove the rank hypotheses in the specific conductivity/scattering class. Full-data uniqueness by itself gives nonzero rank, not infinite rank.

### Nonlinear one-input zero-one law fails

Let M=R and F(t) be the 2×3 matrix [[t,t²,t³],[0,t,t²]]. For t≠s, divide F(t)−F(s) by t−s to get [[1,t+s,t²+ts+s²],[0,1,t+s]], of rank2. With f=(a,b,c), c≠0, a collision is equivalent to t+s=−b/c and ts=a/c, hence exists exactly when b²−4ac>0. A standard Gaussian input has positive probability on both open discriminant signs. Therefore one experiment is globally identifying with positive probability and non-identifying with positive probability. This refutes a blanket nonlinear extension of the linear-parameter/dictionary zero-one theorem suggested as a question in the recent paper. It also shows kr>2d cannot universally be weakened to kr≥2d. Tangent matrices [[1,2t,3t²],[0,1,2t]] have rank2, and the sampled curve is immersive unless the discriminant vanishes, so almost-sure immersion does not rescue global injectivity.

This counterexample does not decide whether the nonlinear zero-one law holds for the specific PDE classes.

### Fixed-truth versus uniform zero-one quantifiers

The cited linear-parameter theorem is stated at a fixed true parameter; the 2×3 example above concerns simultaneous injectivity on the whole model and must not be substituted for that quantifier. A separate fixed-truth counterexample is M=(−1,1), F(t)=[t,t²], true t=0, f=(a,b). A second allowed root exists iff 0<|a|<|b|. For independent standard Gaussians this has probability1/2. Both success and failure occur on open sets of inputs. This refutes an unrestricted nonlinear fixed-truth zero-one extension while leaving specific PDE versions undecided.
