# T11 many-body gap: local defect bounds, Schur complement, and the missing dressing step

Status: internally derived lemmas; no qualifying discovery. The attempted local defect certificate is a useful baseline but collapses to a local frustration-free decomposition. The rank-one Schur lemma is exact but does not by itself give a useful uniform local certificate for extensive sources. Novelty and any extension beyond established stability theorems remain unresolved.

## Exact target and scope

Seek a finite certificate, with computable constants independent of volume, for a uniform gap and a stable phase in a consequential class of local Hamiltonians whose displayed local terms are frustrated. The obstruction to address is that local block gaps alone do not control collective modes whose wavelength grows with the system. The desired certificate should treat a state-dependent dressing or a relative-bound mechanism and handle couplings between the reference ground sector and excited sectors. No numerical extrapolation is used here.

The campaign scope excludes a survey as a substitute for a result. This record therefore separates the exact implications below from the unresolved step that prevents them from meeting the target.

## Baseline theorem: a local defect-count inequality

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite interaction graph. Let \(Q_a\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), be mutually commuting orthogonal projections of finite support, and let
\[
  P_G=\prod_{a\in\mathcal A}(I-Q_a),\qquad
  H_0=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\Delta_a Q_a,\quad \Delta_a>0.
\]
For each self-adjoint local term \(W_X\), choose a finite set \(\mathcal A_X\) of constraints meeting its support and put \(N_X=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_X}Q_a\). Assume
\[
 [W_X,N_X]=0,\qquad W_X P_{N_X=0}=0.
\]
Define the incident load \(c_a=\sum_{X:a\in\mathcal A_X}\|W_X\|\) and \(\gamma=\min_a(\Delta_a-c_a)\). If \(\gamma>0\), then
\[
 H:=H_0+\sum_XW_X\ \geq\ \sum_a(\Delta_a-c_a)Q_a
 \ \geq\ \gamma(I-P_G).
\]
Thus \(P_G\) is an exact ground space and the gap above it is at least \(\gamma\), uniformly over any family of finite graphs for which the same positive margin holds.

Proof. Since the spectrum of \(N_X\) is contained in the nonnegative integers, its positive spectral projection \(R_X=I-P_{N_X=0}\) obeys \(R_X\leq N_X\). The commutation and annihilation assumptions imply \(W_X=R_XW_XR_X\). Hence
\[
 W_X\geq-\|W_X\|R_X\geq-\|W_X\|N_X.
\]
Summing yields the first inequality. For commuting projections, \(\sum_aQ_a\geq I-P_G\), giving the second. Every \(W_X\) annihilates \(P_G\), so this lower bound is attained at zero on the stated ground space.

The local Hilbert dimension does not enter the bound; it enters only the cost of checking the local commutators and annihilation identities. With bounded support, bounded degree, and fixed local dimension \(d\), this check is finite and local. A depth-\(D\), range-\(r\) local unitary dressing replaces each support by its \(Dr\)-neighborhood and leaves the gap bound unchanged.

## Why this is not the requested discovery

The proof gives the explicit identity
\[
 H=\sum_X\bigl(W_X+\|W_X\|N_X\bigr)
       +\sum_a(\Delta_a-c_a)Q_a.
\]
Every summand is positive by the same estimate and annihilates \(P_G\). Thus the input decomposition may be frustrated, but the certificate simply constructs a local frustration-free decomposition with a slightly enlarged support. It is not a new control of collective modes and is not beyond the existing frustration-free framework. Treat it only as a baseline.

Example: for the toric code, local terms that move an existing anyon or attract nearby anyons can satisfy the hypotheses when they preserve the local defect count and vanish on the no-defect sector. The bound is the bare defect energy minus the total incident interaction norm. The rearrangement above again makes the result frustration free, so this example does not rescue novelty.

## Exact Schur-complement/interlacing lemma

Let \(H\) be self-adjoint on \(P\mathcal H\oplus Q\mathcal H\), with \(P\) rank one, \(PHP=0\), and \(QHQ\geq\delta Q\) for \(\delta>0\). The coupling \(B=QHP\) is unrestricted. Cauchy interlacing gives \(\lambda_2(H)\geq\lambda_1(QHQ)\geq\delta\), while the Rayleigh quotient at the reference vector gives \(\lambda_1(H)\leq0\). Therefore
\[
  \lambda_2(H)-\lambda_1(H)\geq\delta.
\]
This allows arbitrary reference-to-excitation coupling if the *compressed excited block* already has a positive floor. It is a cancellation-aware certificate and does not require \(PHQ=0\).

For a reference space of dimension \(r>1\), interlacing only gives \(\lambda_{r+1}(H)\geq\delta\). It does not bound the splitting within the first \(r\) levels. For example, on \(\operatorname{span}\{|g_1\rangle,|g_2\rangle,|e\rangle\}\), set
\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\varepsilon\\0&0&0\\
 \varepsilon&0&\Delta\end{pmatrix},\qquad P=|g_1\rangle\langle g_1|+|g_2\rangle\langle g_2|.
\]
Then \(QHQ=\Delta Q\), but the two lowest eigenvalues are \((\Delta-\sqrt{\Delta^2+4\varepsilon^2})/2\) and \(0\); their gap is \(\varepsilon^2/\Delta+O(\varepsilon^4/\Delta^3)\). A macroscopically degenerate or topological reference space needs a bound on its Feshbach operator, not just on \(QHQ\).

The Feshbach operator below the spectrum of \(QHQ\) is
\[
 F(E)=PHP-E-PHQ(QHQ-E)^{-1}QHP.
\]
For rank one, this is scalar and locates the dressed ground energy; interlacing already controls the first excited state. For rank \(r\), its non-scalar part can split the ground space. A local, volume-uniform control of this operator is the unresolved part.

## Why the Schur lemma is not yet a local result

An extensive local source generally makes \(QHQ\) fail the needed lower bound before any dressing. On \(L\) independent two-level sites, let
\[
 H_L=\sum_{x=1}^L(\Delta n_x+\lambda X_x),\quad
 n_x=|1\rangle\langle1|,\quad X_x=|0\rangle\langle1|+|1\rangle\langle0|,
\]
and let \(P=|0\cdots0\rangle\langle0\cdots0|\). Each site's energies are
\[
 e_\pm=(\Delta\pm\sqrt{\Delta^2+4\lambda^2})/2,
\]
so \(e_-<0\) for \(\lambda\ne0\), and the two lowest energies of \(H_L\) are \(Le_-\) and \(Le_-+\sqrt{\Delta^2+4\lambda^2}\). Compression to \(Q=I-P\) has minimum at most the second eigenvalue by interlacing, which is negative for sufficiently large \(L\). Thus the bare excited block is not uniformly positive even though the true product Hamiltonian has a constant gap. The missing operation is a local dressing that removes the extensive ground-energy shift while retaining control of the excited block and its interactions.

## Adversarial long-wavelength gate

A local spectral gap at one fixed block size cannot exclude a soft collective mode. For a periodic half-filled nearest-neighbor free-fermion chain, the one-particle dispersion is \(\epsilon(k)=-2t\cos k\); choosing boundary twists to avoid exact zero modes, particle-hole energies near the Fermi points scale as \(\Theta(t/L)\). Every fixed open block has a strictly positive finite-size gap, while the periodic gap closes as \(L^{-1}\). Equivalently, for a massive two-band symbol \(h_m(k)=m\sigma_z+t\sin(k)\sigma_x\), the bulk one-particle gap is \(2|m|\); taking \(m_L=L^{-1}\) makes every fixed-window matrix converge to the critical one while the global gap tends to zero. These examples refute a criterion based only on a positive fixed-window gap.

No finite-size gap extrapolation is used as proof. Any proposed certificate must show how its local constant controls the mode at every momentum or every scale.

## Literature comparison (primary sources only)

1. M. B. Hastings, “The Stability of Free Fermi Hamiltonians,” arXiv:1706.02270v2 (10 Jun 2017), https://arxiv.org/html/1706.02270v2. Theorem 1, lines 88–99: for exponentially decaying free-fermion matrix entries with one-particle gap \(\Delta\), and local perturbations with exponential interaction decay strength \(J\leq J_0(K,\nu,\mu,\Delta,D)\), the perturbed many-body gap is at least \(\Delta-c_1J\). The paper explicitly treats free-fermion Hamiltonians not necessarily frustration free; it doubles the system to cancel topological obstructions and maps to an empty-band Hamiltonian (lines 37–57, 89–112). This already covers generic weak perturbations in a broad non-FF family, so a free-fermion example alone is not new.

2. T. Koma, “Stability of the Spectral Gap for Lattice Fermions,” arXiv:2005.04548v2 (29 Sep 2020), https://arxiv.org/html/2005.04548v2. Theorem 2.4, lines 55–60 and 91–101: for a discrete Schrödinger operator with exponentially decaying hopping, a volume-independent one-particle Fermi gap, and exponentially local interactions satisfying the paper's summability assumptions, there is a positive interaction interval \(|s|\leq s_0\) with a unique ground state and uniform gap. Its constant depends on the Fermi projector decay. This preempts a claim to generic weak-interaction stability for gapped lattice fermions.

3. K. S. Rai et al., “A Hierarchy of Spectral Gap Certificates for Frustration-Free Spin Systems,” arXiv:2411.03680v3 (1 Apr 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03680. Abstract lines 16–18: a thermodynamic-limit SDP hierarchy for frustration-free Hamiltonians subsumes Knabe and later finite-size bounds; Quantum 10, 2065 (2026). Its stated scope remains frustration free, so it does not supply the missing frustrated local dressing.

4. D. Gosset and E. Mozgunov, “Local gap threshold for frustration-free spin systems,” arXiv:1512.00088v1 (30 Nov 2015), https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00088. Abstract lines 16–19: for 1D translation-invariant frustration-free chains, a size-\(n\) open-chain gap above \(6/(n(n+1))\) certifies a uniform periodic-chain gap; the paper also proves that gapless frustration-free chains have open-chain gap \(O(n^{-2})\), contrasting with frustrated gapless systems whose finite-size gap can be \(\Theta(n^{-1})\). This sharply marks why a frustrated extension needs a different cancellation argument.

5. T. Cubitt, D. Perez-Garcia, M. M. Wolf, “Undecidability of the Spectral Gap,” arXiv:1502.04573v5 (26 Jul 2022), https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04573. Abstract lines 16–24: no algorithm decides the gap for the constructed translationally invariant nearest-neighbor 2D fixed-local-dimension family, even under a promise of constant-gap versus gapless behavior.

6. J. Bausch, T. Cubitt, A. Lucia, D. Perez-Garcia, “Undecidability of the Spectral Gap in One Dimension,” arXiv:1810.01858v2 (12 Jun 2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01858. Abstract lines 16–29: undecidability also holds for translationally invariant nearest-neighbor 1D chains. This rules out a universal algorithm over all local models, not sound sufficient certificates on restricted structural classes.

## Remaining inference and next gate

The desired mechanism is a finite local construction of a dressing \(U\) and a computable Schur/relative-bound estimate such that
\[
 U^*HU=E_0+K_{\mathrm{exc}}+R,\qquad
 K_{\mathrm{exc}}\big|_{Q\mathcal H}\geq\delta Q,
\]
where \(R\) retains off-diagonal cancellations but has a volume-independent, explicitly summable local norm. It must control the *dressed* vacuum energy and, for \(\dim G>1\), the non-scalar Feshbach operator on \(G\). Existing free-fermion stability already performs a sophisticated quasi-local dressing for weak generic interactions; a new result must give a demonstrably stronger computable radius, handle a broader class, or expose a new invariant. No such improvement is established here.
