## Primary prior art and interface checks

3. Manor Mendel and Assaf Naor, *Spectral calculus and Lipschitz extension for barycentric metric spaces*, arXiv:1301.3963v2 (20 May 2013), [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3963). Read the metric Markov cotype definition, Theorem 1.11, Corollary 1.13, Theorem 1.14, and Question 1.15. Theorem 1.11 gives finite partial extension from a Markov-type source into a metric-Markov-cotype, `W_p`-barycentric target. Corollary 1.13 gives a global extension bound for a dual Banach target. Theorem 1.14 supplies a closed subspace of `ℓ1` failing every finite metric Markov cotype; it blocks a subspace claim. This paper records the open `ℓ1` question answered by the pinned release. Local HTML: `sources/mendel-naor-2013.html`; SHA-256 `fcff47cea400f09ac8656fad53da19a9aec2e4b5933bdffd390dac2d423ac582`.

Qingjin Cheng, Yue Wang, and Bo Xiang, *Sharp Metric Cotype Inequalities for L1 via Nonlinear Cut Smoothing*, arXiv:2609.08749v2 (9 September 2026), [primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08749). Its Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 2.1 use finite cut representations for arbitrary real or complex `L1(Ω,μ)` spaces, but the invariant is metric cotype, not metric Markov cotype.
