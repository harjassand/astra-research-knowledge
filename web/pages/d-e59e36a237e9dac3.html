# Certified Perron powering by bounded-bit normalized squaring

7 October 2026, `rounding_spectral`. Derived numerical implementation
lemma for the previously proved spectral oracle. It has no independent
novelty or measured-runtime claim. No high-L trajectory was executed here.

## Input and need

Let A be symmetric rational, positive semidefinite, entrywise nonnegative,
with positive diagonal, top eigenvalue at most 10, and top gap at least
2eta. These premises hold for the clipped nonnegative Gram approximation
plus exact large diagonal and eta11^T in the D oracle. Assume dimension
N>=2 and `0<zeta<=1/64`. Dimension one is immediate.

The original proof obtains a rational eigenline by applying an integer
matrix T times to ones, where

`T=ceil[64 ceil(log2(16N/zeta))/eta]`.

That construction is formally polynomial but generates O(T) denominator
degree and uses O(T) sequential vector powers if implemented directly.
The lemma below uses only O(log T) matrix squarings, fixed dyadic grids,
and exact rational final projector/Rayleigh computations.

## Exact reference and normalized squaring

Set `X0=A/max_j A_jj`. Define

`F(X)=X^2/max_j(X^2)_jj`, `X_(j+1)=F(X_j)`.

Every X_j is PSD and nonnegative, has maximal diagonal exactly one,
and operator norm at most N. Also

`X_j=A^(2^j)/max_l(A^(2^j))_ll`.

The scalar denominators telescope without changing the eigenvectors or
spectral ratios. For any PSD X with maximal diagonal one,
`maxdiag(X^2)>=1`, because at an index where X_ii=1,
`(X^2)_ii=sum_l X_il^2>=1`. This gives a denominator lower bound
independent of eta, eigenvector coordinates, and face geometry.

For two such matrices with norms at most N,

`||X^2-Y^2||<=2N||X-Y||`,

and the maximum diagonal is 1-Lipschitz in operator norm. Consequently

`||F(X)-F(Y)||<=2N(1+N^2)||X-Y||<=4N^3||X-Y||`.     (1)

Let J be the smallest integer with 2^J>=T. The exact reference q_ref
=X_J ones has norm at least one: its top eigenvalue is at least one
and its overlap with the positive unit Perron eigenvector is at least
one. Its other/top component ratio is at most

`sqrt(N) (lambda2/lambda1)^(2^J)`

` <=sqrt(N) exp(-eta T/5)`.

The displayed oversized T makes this below zeta/4; this follows from
lambda1<=10, gap>=2eta, e>2, and the integer logarithm bound. Hence
the projector of q_ref approximates the top eigenprojector within
zeta/4 in operator norm.

## Rounded PSD/nonnegative implementation

Put C=4N^3. Choose a fixed dyadic mesh delta<=1 with

`delta<=zeta/(64N^3 C^J)`.

Starting from exact X0, iterate

`Y=F(Xhat_j)`,

`Xhat_(j+1)=[Round_delta(Y)+Ndelta I]/(1+Ndelta)`.

Round each symmetric entry to nearest delta, with a fixed tie rule.
Since Y is PSD with diagonal at most one, every entry is in [0,1].
The dyadic grid contains one, so the maximal diagonal of its rounded
matrix is exactly one. Its rounding operator error is at most Ndelta/2.
Adding Ndelta I restores positive semidefiniteness, preserves
nonnegativity, and dividing by 1+Ndelta restores maximal diagonal one.
Thus every Xhat_j stays in the domain of (1), with norm at most N.

One rounding/repair/rescaling perturbs Y by at most 2N^2delta. In fact,
its difference from Y is

`[Round_delta(Y)-Y+Ndelta(I-Y)]/(1+Ndelta)`,

whose norm is at most `(N/2+N^2)delta<=2N^2delta`.
Writing E_j=||Xhat_j-X_j||, (1) gives

`E_(j+1)<=C E_j+2N^2delta`, `E0=0`,

so `E_J<=2N^2delta C^J<=zeta/(32N)`.

Set the rational vector q=Xhat_J ones and compute

`P_hat=q q^T/(q^T q)`, `ell=q^T A q/(q^T q)`.

Then `||q-q_ref||<=sqrt(N)E_J<=zeta/(32sqrt(N))`.
Since q_ref has norm at least one, q is nonzero and its eigenline
projector differs by less than zeta/8. Combining with the exact reference
power bound gives

`||P_hat-P_top(A)||<zeta`.

The Rayleigh deficit is at most `10zeta^2<zeta`, because the
projector distance is the sine of its angle to the top eigenvector.
All statements are certified by rational grid/error arithmetic and the
stated spectral premises; no approximate matrix eigenvalue test is used.

## Encodings and integration into the Hessian oracle

Each persistent Xhat_j has entries equal to dyadic-grid integers divided
by the fixed rational 1+Ndelta. Their magnitudes are at most one, so
their bit length is O(log(delta^-1)+log N). Temporary squaring,
normalization and rounding use one fixed-degree rational expansion,
then discard that denominator. They do not retain degree 2^J.

The grid satisfies

`log2(delta^-1)=O(J log N+log(zeta^-1)+log N)`,

with `J=O(log(eta^-1)+log log(N/zeta))`. For the rounding theorem's
inverse-polynomial eta and tolerance, this is O(log^2(nK)), in addition
to the rational input encodings used once at X0. J dense products are
charged; no empirical speed comparison is claimed.

Apply this lemma to A=M_hat from the original spectral approximation,
whose clipped weights preserve PSD/nonnegativity and whose exact eta
regularizer supplies the gap. Its matrix error to M is the earlier
delta_M<=zeta/256. Perturbation of the eigenprojector is at most
delta_M/eta; the resulting total projector error remains below the
earlier allowance 3zeta/eta. Rayleigh error remains below 2zeta. Thus
the original rational reduced-resolvent, gradient, Hessian and motion-Gram
telescopes continue to apply with unchanged conservative allowances.

Together with `ROUNDED_LEGAL_FILTER.md`, this removes two avoidable

It preserves the exact legal direction interface and the charged
