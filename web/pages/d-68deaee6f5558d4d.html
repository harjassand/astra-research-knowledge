Correction to v1: full channel-allocation range
The v1 interval [43/120,17/24] kBT ln 4 is sharp only on the restricted one-parameter subfamily that imposes the same perturbation parameter at E=+ and E=−. There is no physical or mathematical constraint in the stated model linking those allocations, so they must be varied independently for the full compatibility range.

Let v=(1,-5,4), r=(4,1,1/4), and d_e(theta_e)=d_e(A)+theta_e v separately for e=+ and e=−. Since v·(1,1,1)=0 and v·r=0, both aggregate sensor rates remain exactly fixed for each input state. Strict positivity gives

    -1/20 < theta_+ < 1/12,
    -1/15 < theta_- < 1/15.

Using p=(1/5,3/10,3/10,1/5), channel 1 hydrolysis and channel 3 reverse hydrolysis, the fuel current is

    J_fuel(theta_+,theta_-)
      = 61/120 + (3/2)(theta_+ + theta_-).

For either E state, the derivative is 3/2: explicitly it is

    sum_nu s_nu (p_{e0} r_nu - p_{e1}) v_nu = 3/2,
    s=(1,0,-1).

The linear objective is therefore minimized and maximized at opposite vertices of the product of the two feasible allocation intervals. Its sharp closure over all nonnegative channel allocations matching the complete observed process is

    1/3 <= J_fuel <= 11/15,
    (1/3) kBT ln 4 <= P_chem <= (11/15) kBT ln 4

per time unit. The endpoints set at least one pathway rate to zero under an input state; if every listed pathway must remain strictly positive, these are an infimum and supremum and the interval is open. The two networks A and B from v1 lie strictly inside. By contrast, imposing theta_+=theta_-=theta restricts to the smaller closure [43/120,17/24] for J_fuel, the interval printed in v1. The runnable script now checks both intervals.

This correction follows from a factor-of-two distinction: the derivative with respect to each independent theta_e is 3/2; the derivative along the constrained diagonal theta_+=theta_-=theta is 3. The earlier v1 wording incorrectly called the diagonal-family closure the full sharp range.

Exact finite-horizon prediction remains unchanged
The exogenous input is a symmetric stationary telegraph process with rate 1 in each direction. For both networks, the aggregate M rates are (u_+,d_+)=(2,1), (u_-,d_-)=(1,2), so their complete continuous-time generator on observed states (E,M) is identical. Its stationary law is p=(1/5,3/10,3/10,1/5); H(M)=ln 2; the conditional relaxation rate at fixed E is 3. The signed E,M correlation is 1/5 and E[E(t+tau)|E(t)]=e^(-2 tau)E(t). Thus, for every tau>=0,

    accuracy(tau) = [1 + (1/5)e^(-2 tau)]/2,
    error(tau)    = [1 - (1/5)e^(-2 tau)]/2,
    I(M_t;E_(t+tau)) = ln 2 - h(error(tau)),

where h(x)=-x ln x-(1-x)ln(1-x). These quantities, memory, response rate, and all finite-dimensional observed path distributions coincide for A and B, while their powers differ by (3/20) kBT ln 4 per time unit. This is exact because the state generator is exactly equal, not a simulation-based match.

Sharp allocation theorem for a fixed channel set
For a fixed transition edge with equal endpoint standard free energies, suppose a finite set of reversible channels has prescribed local rate ratios r_nu>0, so u_nu=r_nu d_nu, and the observed aggregate rates are U=sum_nu r_nu d_nu and D=sum_nu d_nu. Let p_0,p_1 be the stationary endpoint probabilities and let A_nu=ln r_nu be the reservoir affinity in units kBT. The channel-resolved steady chemical power contribution is

    beta P_edge = sum_nu (p_0 r_nu d_nu - p_1 d_nu) A_nu.

The allocations compatible with the same observed edge are the polytope

    d_nu >= 0,  sum_nu d_nu=D,  sum_nu r_nu d_nu=U.

Consequently the exact infimum and supremum of this edge's power are the minimum and maximum of a linear functional over this polytope. If the two equality rows (1,...,1) and (r_1,...,r_n) are independent, every vertex has at most two positive channel rates: otherwise its positive-support columns would be linearly dependent and there would be a nonzero feasible perturbation in both directions, contradicting extremality. For multiple observed edges, the optimization factorizes edge by edge once the observed stationary law is fixed. This gives an exact, sharp, finite-dimensional optimization procedure when the pathway affinity set is independently known. It does not give a universal relation from accuracy, memory, and response speed alone: without a fixed pathway set and chemical boundary, the feasible work interval can change or be made arbitrarily large by adding fuel-consuming hidden routes.

For the v1 example, the full interval above is exactly attained on its closure by the product-polytope vertices. At the minimum, theta_+=-1/20 and theta_-=-1/15; at the maximum, theta_+=1/12 and theta_-=1/15. Each setting leaves only two channels active per input state. These endpoint CRNs have the same observed E,M generator and all the same prediction scores. Thus the interval is not merely a relaxation of the stated allocation model.

Observed irreversibility and hidden chemical work
The observed four-state path process has steady path-space irreversibility rate

    sigma_obs/kB = (1/5) ln 2 per time unit.

For both A and B, the full channel-resolved entropy production equals beta P_chem, because every channel's local detailed-balance ratio is its chemical affinity, the E jumps have zero medium affinity, and the stationary system-entropy derivative sums to zero. The hidden excess is nonnegative and can be written edgewise. For each lumped directed edge let x_nu=p_i k^nu_(i->j), y_nu=p_j k^nu_(j->i), X=sum x_nu, Y=sum y_nu, a_nu=x_nu/X and b_nu=y_nu/Y. Then

    sum_nu (x_nu-y_nu) ln(x_nu/y_nu) - (X-Y)ln(X/Y)
       = X D(a||b) + Y D(b||a) >= 0.

This identity quantifies the chemical entropy hidden by not observing the channel label. In the two examples, beta P_A-sigma_obs/kB=(49/60)ln 2 and beta P_B-sigma_obs/kB=(67/60)ln 2. Channel-resolved work is recoverable from pathway/fuel-event labels, but is not a functional of the observed state trajectory in this construction.

Physical realization and boundary
A finite-state chemical realization is given by one environmental catalyst conformation E in {+,-} and one reporter state M in {0,1}. The input reaction E+ <-> E- has equal forward/reverse rate 1 and no chemical affinity. For each fixed conformation e, include the three reversible reactions

    E_e + M0 + ATP <-> E_e + M1 + ADP + Pi,
    E_e + M0       <-> E_e + M1,
    E_e + M0 + ADP + Pi <-> E_e + M1 + ATP.

Use chemostat concentrations and standard chemical potentials giving forward/reverse ratios 4, 1, and 1/4. The environmental conformation changes each reaction's forward and reverse kinetic prefactor by the same factor, so it leaves that channel's ratio fixed; choosing the tabulated positive prefactors realizes the rates in v1. The catalyst is conserved by sensor reactions and its own E+ <-> E- transition does not depend on M, so the input law is exogenous in the model. This is a physically consistent finite-state Markov reaction scheme with chemostatted fuels. It is an idealized construction, not an assertion about a known cellular pathway.

The counted ongoing work is the reservoir free-energy flux for all three channels: channel 1 hydrolyses one ATP-equivalent per forward event, channel 3 synthesizes that equivalent on a forward event, and reverse events carry opposite signs. Maintaining the assumed chemical potentials may itself require cellular metabolism; the quoted power is the sensor's chemical free-energy draw at the declared chemostat boundary, not a full-cell ATP-production cost. A lab device forcing E to switch with some other law would supply additional work; it is excluded here because the model uses the autonomous equilibrium telegraph source.

The calculation is for the stationary sensing regime. It does not claim that reaching stationarity from a prepared initial state is free. A finite experiment must include burn-in work, which depends on the starting distribution and the transient channel currents; the steady-power comparison is exact after the stationary regime is reached. No memory reset is performed between predictions. A fluorescence detector, persistent data store, or reset module is not assigned zero cost: each must be added to the boundary and measured. Appending identical readout and reset modules to A and B leaves their ongoing sensor-power gap intact, although startup and finite-record work still require separate accounting.

Biological implication and its limit
Within this mechanism class, matching single-cell input/output trajectories, response rate, reporter occupancy, and any finite-horizon forecast score does not identify the sensor's ATP free-energy consumption. A test could combine a matched telegraph ligand input, reporter trajectories, and ATP/ADP turnover or pathway-specific isotope flux to distinguish networks with identical output statistics. This is a model prediction. Barato et al. provide a primary model of bacterial chemotaxis with ATP-dependent receptor phosphorylation/dephosphorylation; neither that work nor any source below validates this three-route mechanism in natural chemotaxis. Empirical confirmation would require a real reaction graph and channel-resolved flux measurements.

Novelty and prior-art status
The generic phenomenon behind the construction is established prior art. Freitas & Esposito explicitly note that distinct sets of thermodynamically distinct transition channels can produce the same master equation and different entropy production, and derive a coarse-grained entropy-production lower bound (2022, Eqs. 25–26). The log-sum decomposition above is the corresponding standard relative-entropy identity. Barato, Hartich & Seifert already study an autonomous sensor driven by an exogenous Markov signal and report no universal bound of information-acquisition rate by dissipation (2014). Still et al. give the memory-minus-predictive-information dissipation relation in a specified driven system (2012). Chen & Limmer's 2026 preprint bounds lag error for reversible state-resolved Markov networks with time-dependent inputs; it does not resolve different chemical channel labels that share an observed transition. These precedents make this exact model-specific matched-path prediction calculation a candidate specialization, not a novel general thermodynamic law. The strongest supported extension here is the explicit sharp fixed-channel-set power interval and its finite-time predictive score, with the theorem above being an elementary LP consequence.
