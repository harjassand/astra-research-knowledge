# Exact SLD fibres, rational witnesses, and repair costs

2026-10-06. Mathematical deductions and independent finite computations. Source claims are separately identified below.

## Reconstructed score map

For rho>0, the derivation is partial_A(rho)=-i[A,rho]. In rho's eigenbasis with populations p_i, the lambda-score is

    (S_(A,lambda))_ij = i(p_i-p_j) A_ij / ((1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j).

The SLD case is lambda=1/2. This was implemented by an eigensystem Sylvester solve without importing the authors' code. The transformed SLD frame is

    K_A = T_sigma(A),
    (K_A)_ij = 2 sqrt(p_i p_j)/(p_i+p_j) A_ij

in sigma's eigenbasis. Source locations: Definition2 Eq.9, Lemma5 Eq.10, Lemma6 Eq.16, AppendixA.1 Eqs.41-43 and Theorem15.

## Exact full-state fibre, independently derived

Fix sigma>0 and let its scores be S_a. Put B_a=A_a-i S_a/2, D_a=A_a+i S_a/2. The defining equation is B_a sigma=sigma D_a and K_a=sigma^(1/2) D_a sigma^(-1/2). A positive normalized rho has identical SLD scores if and only if

    R=sigma^(-1/2) rho sigma^(-1/2)>0,
    [R,K_a]=0 for all a,
    Tr(sigma R)=1.

The forward direction is the paper's rearrangement. The reverse direction follows by conjugating the commuting equation back to B_a rho=rho D_a; positivity ensures uniqueness of the SLD solution. Thus the full fibre is the normalized positive cone in the transformed joint commutant, conjugated by sigma^(1/2).

Because K_a are Hermitian, their joint commutant is closed under adjoint. If it is larger than C I, it contains a non-scalar Hermitian Q. Subtract Tr(sigma Q) I and use R=I+tQ for sufficiently small nonzero t. This gives a distinct normalized full-rank state. Therefore the transformed scalar-commutant condition is necessary and sufficient on the unrestricted full-rank state class. On a restricted Hamiltonian model it need only be sufficient: the fibre may intersect that model once. No novelty claim is made without a wider prior-art audit.

## Rational finite witnesses

For d=3 or4, set u=(1,...,1,2), sigma=diag(u_i^2)/sum_i u_i^2, P=J/d. Let E_ij=(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T, and choose

    K_0=J+E_01; K_i=E_(i,i+1), i=1,...,d-2.
    (A_a)_ij=(u_i^2+u_j^2)/(2u_i u_j) (K_a)_ij.

SymPy rational linear algebra proves the original joint commutant has complex dimension1 and the transformed joint commutant has dimension2. In fact it is span{I,P}. For every t in (-d/(d-1),d),

    rho_t=sigma^(1/2)[I+t(P-I/d)]sigma^(1/2)

has the same SLD scores, is full rank and normalized, and has the same diagonal populations as sigma. `score_audit.py` checks exact defining-equation residuals at t=1 and exact commutant ranks. The entire interval follows from the displayed derivation.

The qubit-compatible d=4 witness is

    sigma=diag(1,1,1,4)/7,
    rho_1=(1/28) [[4,1,1,2],[1,4,1,2],[1,1,4,2],[2,2,2,16]].

Its minimum eigenvalue is3/28 and trace distance from sigma is about.164484. It is an arbitrary-frame obstruction; the frame is not the local Xi,Zi frame of the source's high-temperature theorem. Both states are full-rank Gibbs states for some finite Hermitian Hamiltonians. A finite dimension and arbitrary generators do not challenge the paper's specific many-body complexity result.

## Repairs and measured conditioning

All numerical tangent comparisons remove each generator's scalar part and set its remaining Hilbert--Schmidt norm to1. Tangent bases also have unit Hilbert--Schmidt norm. The Jacobian is measured in the target's lambda-weighted score norm. Its Gram matrix is the score-loss Hessian at the target. Hamiltonian tangents use the exact Frechet derivative of the normalized Gibbs map with beta=1; a different beta changes these scales. Numbers below are finite checks, not uniform bounds.

At d=4 the original score Jacobian has rank14 of15, smallest singular value about7.8e-17 in Hamiltonian coordinates. The repairs produce:

| Repair | Generators | Hamiltonian Jacobian rank | Minimum singular value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Add one diagonal generator Q=diag(0,1,2,3) | 4 | 15 | .213504 |
| Complete traceless Hermitian generator basis | 15 | 15 | 1.072247 |
| Endpoint lambda=0 or1 | 3 | 15 | .253370 |
| Add one fixed coherence moment B=E_01+E_10 | 3 plus one moment | 15 | .071031 |

- Complete basis is a universal repair because T_sigma is an invertible linear map and preserves the span of the full matrix space. It costs d^2-1 generators, exponential in qubit count. There is no claim that this is measurement efficient.
- Endpoint scores are a universal repair for any ordinarily irreducible frame. This is **already established in source Lemma16**, because its transformed frame is a common similarity of the original. Endpoint scores are non-Hermitian. Their operator norm and measurement variance can worsen with condition number; restoring identifiability is not restoring efficient low-temperature learning.
- The extra diagonal generator repairs the displayed example exactly: sigma commutes with Q, so its Q-score is zero. A candidate matching it must commute with Q; equivalently the augmented transformed commutant is scalar. The added frame still has the paper's candidate-score observable formulation. This specific Q is not a universal one-generator repair for arbitrary targets.
- On the displayed one-dimensional fibre, B=E_01+E_10 gives Tr[B(rho_t-sigma)]=t/14, so a single coherence moment uniquely fixes t. More generally r-1 anchors suffice exactly when their linear responses on the trace-normalized Hermitian transformed commutant of dimension r have full rank. Necessity follows by a small positive perturbation in any surviving null direction. Target moments are estimated from copies, but candidate model moments generally need the Gibbs normalization or state preparation. Thus a moment anchor may lose the partition-free computational advantage. The displayed finite dense-matrix test includes that cost rather than claiming an efficient many-body repair.

Near lambda=1/2 and for a small amplitude eta on the extra generator, the implemented smallest Jacobian singular value scales linearly with |lambda-1/2| or eta. The corresponding Hessian eigenvalue scales quadratically. The exact coefficient tables are in `score_audit.json`; formal coefficient asymptotics have not been proved. A temperature sweep of the *fixed* rational frame crosses an exact rank loss at p_last/p_first=4. Small loss alone can therefore fail to certify state recovery near a singular frame.
