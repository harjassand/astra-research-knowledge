STRONGEST CLAIM

I independently reconstructed the load-bearing homogeneous counting mechanism in Chen–Liu, arXiv:2610.06724v1, Theorem 5.1, from its exact hypotheses through the complementary-event estimator. The central reusable ingredient is stronger than the theorem statement: the exchange chain on pairs of homogeneous bases has Poincare gap at least one uniformly for every positive external field z and every coordinatewise penalty vector t in (0,1]^U. The coordinatewise uniformity is essential: the rarity-removal tilt changes one penalty from t_u to 1. This establishes the constant-probability hard-intersection reduction without a lower bound on the intersection weight.

This is an internal proof reconstruction of an imported theorem, not a new counting theorem, independent certification, or executed FPRAS. I found no fatal flaw in the exact homogeneous interface.

EXACT INTERFACE

Let |U|=n, let mu be nonnegative and supported on a-subsets, nu nonnegative and supported on b-subsets, with a+b=n. Their homogeneous multiaffine generating polynomials g_mu and g_nu are log-concave on the positive orthant. The algorithm receives value oracles, upper bounds R_mu,R_nu on the ratio of largest to smallest positive coefficient, and supported initial sets S0,T0. It estimates
  Zcap = [x^U](g_mu g_nu) = sum_{S subset U} mu(S) nu(U\S)
to relative error epsilon with probability at least 3/4. Its arithmetic/oracle bound is
  O(n^10 B^2 epsilon^-2 log^2(2B/epsilon)),
  B = n + log(R_mu R_nu),
on every execution. Rational inputs/oracles with polynomial-length rational outputs admit polynomial bit time via the source's finite field and fair-coin caps. This interface requires explicit positive starting supports and the coefficient-range bounds; full-column rank alone or a supplied nonzero factor does not imply Zcap>0.

PROOF RECONSTRUCTION

1. Downward extension and the uniform gap.
For t in (0,1]^U and z>0 define
  g_hat_mu(x)=g_mu(1+z x),  g_hat_nu(x)=g_nu(1+z x),
  psi_t(S,T)=product_{u in S intersect T} t_u.
Their supports are downward closed. For lambda>0, the unnormalized product factor
  w_lambda(S,T)=hat_mu_{lambda z}(S) hat_nu_{lambda z}(T)
has generating polynomial
  g_mu(1+lambda z x) g_nu(1+lambda z y).
Log-concavity of g_mu g_nu, preserved by positive translation and scaling, gives zero upward spectral curvature for w_lambda. The dual penalty factor psi_t^* has upward curvature one: for each coordinate present in neither dual set, the two-copy curvature block is
  ((1-t_u)/t_u^2) [[1,1],[1,1]],
and all other blocks vanish. These blocks are positive semidefinite. The source's integrated Bochner theorem therefore gives Poincare gap at least one for the downward-closed pair law w_lambda odot psi_t, uniformly in lambda,z,t.

Condition this chain on the pairs of full bases. Its stationary law is
  pi_{t,z}(S,T) proportional to mu(S)nu(T)t^{S intersect T} z^S z^T,
because the common factor lambda^(a+b)=lambda^n cancels. The induced-chain Poincare inequality retains gap at least one. From a basis pair, deleting u in S has rate t_u^(-1[u in T]). At the resulting one-element-deficient pair, the total addition rate is proportional to lambda, while the total deletion rate is bounded independently of lambda. As lambda tends to infinity, the first return is an addition with probability 1-O(1/lambda); reinsertion of v has law
  z_v mu(S-u+v) / sum_{w notin S-u} z_w mu(S-u+w).
Multiplying by the deletion rate yields the limiting exchange rates
  q((S,T),(S-u+v,T))
   = t_u^(-1[u in T]) z_v mu(S-u+v)
     / sum_{w notin S-u} z_w mu(S-u+w).
The analogous rates update T. Every finite-state Dirichlet form converges, so the limiting exchange chain is reversible with stationary law pi_{t,z} and satisfies Var_pi(f) <= E_q(f,f). The proof gives this for nonconstant vector t, not only uniform t. The lambda limit is a proof device; the sampler never evaluates translated superset sums or simulates large lambda.

2. Rare-event removal from a balanced field.
Let y=log z and
  Phi(y)=log Z_{t,exp(y)} - sum_u y_u.
For a pair sampled from pi, set G=1_S+1_T-1. Then grad Phi=E G, Hess Phi=Cov(G) is positive semidefinite, each diagonal entry is at most one, and G_u is in {-1,0,1}. For coordinate u, let p_j be the probability u appears in exactly j of S,T. Then partial_u Phi=p2-p0.

If p0 p2>0, replace t_u by 1 and z_u by s z_u with s=sqrt(t p0/p2). Under the tilted law, q0=q2<=1/2 and
  q1/q0 = sqrt(t) p1 / sqrt(p0 p2).
For g=1[u absent], a single exchange can move one copy to zero at total rate at most one, and cannot move two copies directly to zero. Hence its Dirichlet form is at most q1. The uniform gap just proved gives q0(1-q0)<=q1, so q1/q0>=1/2 and
  p0 p2 <= 4 t p1^2.
The zero-product endpoint is immediate. Therefore
  p0+p2 <= |p2-p0| + 2 sqrt(p0 p2)
           <= |partial_u Phi| + 4 sqrt(t),
and a union bound gives
  Pr[T != U\S] <= n (||grad Phi||_infinity + 4 sqrt(t)).
This deduction would fail if only a uniform-scalar-penalty gap were known, because the tilt is coordinate-specific.

3. Acquiring the field and completing the estimator.
Set t=1/(256 n^2). A field with ||grad Phi||_infinity<=1/(4n) makes complementary pairs have probability at least 1/2. Stochastic coordinate descent estimates grad Phi from exchange-chain samples. Phi is convex, coordinatewise 1-smooth, and its gradient samples are bounded in [-1,1]^n. If Zcap>0, each complementary pair contributes unchanged weight, so, with M=max mu * max nu,
  Phi(y) >= log Zcap >= log(M/(R_mu R_nu)),
  Phi(0) <= log(4^n M).
Thus the potential range needed by the capped descent is at most B=n+log(R_mu R_nu) up to absolute constants; no attained minimizer is assumed. The source's bounded coordinate descent uses O(n^4 B log(2B)) gradient samples and keeps ||y||_1=O(nB) on every path. Each approximate gradient sample mixes in O(n^6 B) arithmetic/oracle work, giving O(n^10 B^2 log B) field-search work. On Zcap=0 the same caps still bound runtime.

For any z,
  Zcap = (Z_{t,z}/z^U) Pr_{pi_{t,z}}[T=U\S],
since the penalty is one on complementary pairs and z^S z^T=z^U there. Estimate Z_{t,z} by membership-bit self-reduction and estimate the complementary probability independently. On a good field the latter is at least 1/2, so additive sampling error becomes relative error. Conditioning preserves log-concavity by coordinate derivatives for inclusion and zero specializations for exclusion; coefficient ranges do not increase. The source also bounds rational choices with finite fair-coin caps and zero-output aborts, preserving total polynomial runtime and exact zero output when Zcap=0. Combining the two estimates and field failure yields success above 3/4 and the stated cost.

REUSABLE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ASSIGNED PHYSICAL TRANSFER

For the easy-plane XXZ transfer, the proof above supplies the exact counting primitive needed after constructing local homogeneous log-concave gates; no weaker “only uniform t” version suffices for the general theorem. The already audited physical reduction uses
  A_e=3 alpha_e I + alpha_e(XX+YY) + gamma_e ZZ,
  A_v=(b_v+|c_v|)I+b_v X+c_v Z,
with alpha_e>=|gamma_e| and b_v>=0. A two-qubit Euler gate gives a six-vertex quadratic whose three Hessian eigenvalue constraints reduce to |a-b|<=c<=a+b. A one-qubit gate has q1=d(1+xy)+u x+v y with uv-d^2>=1; its degree-two lift
  d z1 z2 + (u/2)x(z1+z2) + (v/2)y(z1+z2) + dxy
is log-concave, and an auxiliary uniform-matroid factor sums the dummy choices. A wire-partner factor (x_h+x_h') imposes exact-once, so the full coefficient equals the circuit trace. Existing cycle-3 evidence checks this transfer on finite rational fixtures; it does not execute the imported FPRAS.
