Forced 3D Navier–Stokes blowup does not transfer to the unforced Cauchy problem
Date: 2026-10-08
Progress label: reconstructed (scope and transfer obstruction)
Scientific status: source-reported theorem statement; proof validity not independently checked here. The proposed implication to unforced blowup is refuted at the level of hypotheses and equations.

Question and result
-------------------
Audit the released forced 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes singularity result against the unforced global-regularity question. The exact conclusion is conditional on the released theorem being correct: for each positive viscosity, it gives a smooth compactly forced solution from rest that loses bounded velocity in finite time while kinetic energy stays bounded. It addresses the forced breakdown alternatives C and D in Fefferman's Clay formulation. It does not give a singular solution to the unforced equation, does not contradict the unforced assertions A or B, and does not imply that the forced solution survives after its force is removed.

The scope check is logically independent of whether the 166-page proof and Lean formalization are correct. The OpenAI paper states Theorem 1.1 as a forced result and calls C/D its conclusion. The official Clay text defines A/B with f identically zero and C/D with an existential smooth force. Clay's 11 September 2026 announcement described the result as “apparently” settled and said the work would be analysed and interrogated. This leaf did not independently verify the analysis or replay Lean.

Exact theorem statements and quantifiers
---------------------------------------
Unforced whole-space target A (Fefferman, official Clay formulation): for every ν>0 and every smooth divergence-free u° on R^3 satisfying the rapid-decay condition (4), set f≡0. There must exist smooth p,u on R^3×[0,∞) solving the equation with u(·,0)=u°, with uniformly bounded kinetic energy. The periodic target B has the corresponding universal quantifier over smooth periodic u° and again f≡0.

Forced breakdown alternatives C/D (same formulation): for every ν>0, there exist a smooth divergence-free u° and a smooth force f satisfying the stated rapid-decay conditions such that no global smooth solution satisfying the corresponding bounded-energy (C) or periodic smoothness (D) requirements exists.

Released theorem 1.1 (OpenAI, 8 September 2026): for every ν>0 there exist fν∈C_c^∞(R^3×(0,∞);R^3), a compact K⊂R^3, and smooth u,p on R^3×[0,1) such that

  ∂t u+(u·∇)u−νΔu+∇p=fν,   div u=0,   u(·,0)=0,
  supp u(·,t)∪supp p(·,t)⊂K,
  sup_{0≤t<1} ||u(t)||_2<∞,   limsup_{t↑1}||u(t)||_∞=∞.

The paper then proves no global smooth bounded-energy competitor exists for that same force and zero datum, and says this establishes C; compact support yields D. The theorem acquires the force, velocity, and pressure existentially; the initial datum is fixed to zero. It supplies no force-size/smallness bound, no f≡0 condition, and no theorem saying f vanishes on a final interval before t=1.

Energy identity: why the projected force is essential
------------------------------------------------------
Let P be the Leray projector onto divergence-free vector fields. For smooth decaying (or periodic) solutions,

  ∂t u − νΔu + P div(u⊗u) = P f,

and taking the L² inner product with u gives

  (1/2) d||u(t)||_2²/dt + ν||∇u(t)||_2² = ⟨P f(t),u(t)⟩ = ⟨f(t),u(t)⟩.

The last equality uses div u=0. From the released zero datum,

  (1/2)||u(t)||_2² + ν∫₀ᵗ||∇u(s)||_2² ds = ∫₀ᵗ⟨P f(s),u(s)⟩ ds.       (E)

The theorem's u is nonzero (indeed ||u(t)||∞ is unbounded near 1), so the right side of (E) is strictly positive for some t. Thus P f cannot vanish identically. In particular, the force cannot be wholly absorbed into pressure: if f=∇φ so Pf=0, (E) with zero initial data forces u≡0. The paper itself notes that f is nonzero because its energy estimate would otherwise force u=0 (Section 10.4, around PDF p. 123).

The same identity gives the paper's explicit finite-energy control. With F(t)=∫₀ᵗ||f(s)||₂ds, first divide by a regularized ||u||₂ and discard dissipation to obtain ||u(t)||₂≤F(t); then integrate the energy identity once more:

  ||u(t)||₂² + 2ν∫₀ᵗ||∇u(s)||₂²ds ≤ F(t)².

Here F(1)<∞ because f is smooth and compactly supported. This proves bounded energy while retaining force work as the source term.

This identifies the precise source term: the Duhamel contribution of P f is what starts motion from u°=0. The force's smoothness means its derivatives extend through the singular time; it does not mean that its solenoidal component vanishes or is small. The paper's mechanism says an exponentially small external force seeds each oscillatory pulse, while background shear amplifies the pulses and their mean momentum flux cancels the singular background residual. It arranges these pulses on successively finer spatial and temporal scales as the singularity approaches (Section 2.2, PDF pp. 4–5). “Exponentially small” describes pulse seeds in that construction, not a theorem-wide smallness bound on f in a norm that controls global regularity.

Duhamel formula and failed removal operations
----------------------------------------------
For a smooth solution in a class where the mild formula is valid,

  u(t)=e^{νtΔ}u° − ∫₀ᵗ e^{ν(t−s)Δ}P div(u⊗u)(s) ds
                  + ∫₀ᵗ e^{ν(t−s)Δ}P f(s) ds.                         (D)

The third term in (D) cannot simply be dropped while keeping the same u: the resulting equation has residual −f. With u°=0 and f=0, the exact smooth finite-energy solution is u≡0, by the energy identity (or uniqueness). So the released example is not an unforced counterexample with its stated datum.

Subtracting the Stokes response does not remove the forcing. If z solves ∂t z−νΔz=Pf, z(0)=0, and v=u−z, then

  ∂t v−νΔv + P[(v+z)·∇(v+z)] = 0.

Expanding creates the terms P(v·∇z+z·∇v+z·∇z). They encode the forcing through z; v is not a solution of the standard unforced Navier–Stokes Cauchy problem.

The only direct restart transfer is conditional: if one proves Pf(t)=0 on an entire terminal interval [t₀,1), then the pressure-gradient part can be absorbed and u(t₀), which is smooth and compactly supported for t₀<1, is unforced blowup data after a time shift. The released theorem does not assert such a terminal interval. Its construction defines f as the full residual R(u,p) at every t<1, and the stated pulse mechanism uses external seeds at the refining scales. Compact support in all spacetime does not imply that forcing stops before the singular time; the paper explicitly constructs a smooth extension of f across t=1.

Scaling calculation
------------------
For viscosity ν fixed, standard parabolic scaling is

  uλ(x,t)=λu(λx,λ²t),  pλ(x,t)=λ²p(λx,λ²t),  fλ(x,t)=λ³f(λx,λ²t).

Each term ∂t u, (u·∇)u, νΔu, ∇p, and f acquires exactly λ³. Thus forcing is part of the scale-invariant equation; scaling a forced solution does not turn it into a force-free solution. Moreover,

  ||uλ(t)||_2² = λ⁻¹||u(λ²t)||_2²,
  ν∫||∇uλ||_2²dt = λ⁻¹ ν∫||∇u||_2²dt,
  ∫⟨fλ,uλ⟩dxdt = λ⁻¹∫⟨f,u⟩dxdt.

All three energy-budget terms scale identically. For mixed norms,
  ||fλ||_{L_t^q L_x^p}=λ^(3−2/q−3/p)||f||_{L_t^q L_x^p};
the scale-invariant force line is 2/q+3/p=3. Compact smoothness alone places no small upper bound on these norms.

There is a valid local perturbative observation, but it is not a global transfer. A zoom-in around (x*,t*) at spatial scale r is

  u^(r)(x,t)=r u(x*+rx,t*+r²t),  f^(r)(x,t)=r³f(x*+rx,t*+r²t).

On compact subsets where f is bounded, ||f^(r)||∞≤Cr³. Thus a smooth force disappears from a local blowup-scale equation as r↓0, provided a suitable limit exists. Such a limit is a local/ancient tangent object; this calculation does not supply a smooth admissible unforced initial datum or a global unforced blowup solution. Compactness, nontriviality, time-slice data, and continuation would need separate estimates. No stability theorem in the release transfers blowup to zero forcing.

The paper's viscosity normalization also preserves forcing. Starting from its ν=1 construction, it sets uν(x,t)=√νu(x/√ν,t), pν=νp(x/√ν,t), fν=√νf(x/√ν,t). Every equation term scales by √ν; ||uν||₂² and ν∫||∇uν||₂²dt scale by ν^(5/2). The force remains smooth, compactly supported, and nonzero for each fixed ν>0.

Exact hypothesis mismatch
-------------------------
The initial datum is not the mismatch: zero is admissible in A/B and is exactly smooth/rapidly decaying. The mismatch is f. A/B require f≡0 and universally quantify over every admissible unforced u°. The released theorem uses an existential nonzero fν and one datum u°=0. Its conclusion is about failure of a smooth bounded-energy continuation for that forced pair. There is no quantifier conversion from “there exist (u°,f) with forced breakdown” to “there exists u° with unforced breakdown.” Nor does it refute A, which asks for global smoothness for all unforced data.

Strongest equally-informed baseline
-----------------------------------
For the narrow “remove the force but keep the released initial data” comparison, the exact baseline is u≡0 with u°=0 and f≡0. It is global, smooth, and unique in the smooth energy class. It uses the same viscosity and datum, so it is fully equally informed and immediately shows why the forced trajectory cannot be carried over by deleting f. For the broader A/B target, the exact logical baseline is the target's own universal unforced quantifier; a forced existential counterexample is outside that class. No separate computational or model baseline is needed for this scope theorem.

Falsifier / decisive next check
------------------------------
The force-mismatch conclusion would be falsified by a proof from the released construction that there is t₀<1 with Pf≡0 for every t∈[t₀,1). Then u(t₀) would be smooth, divergence-free, compactly supported, and would generate an unforced finite-time blowup after restarting; that would genuinely refute A. A comparable force-removal theorem would also suffice, but it would need to preserve a nontrivial blowup solution while driving the solenoidal forcing to zero in a norm with a proved compactness/stability bridge. The current theorem and its stated assumptions provide neither.
