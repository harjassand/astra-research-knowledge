## Target reconstructed from the polynomial

The target is the actual harmonic polynomial

    P(x,y,z) = z-x²-2y²+3z².

We verify its Laplacian is zero, derive each rational harmonic coefficient c_j by sphere integration, and reconstruct P exactly as sum_j c_j q_j. This verifies all degrees, including the zero degree3 moments of the T3 target. No producer coefficient list is taken as an oracle.

For the averaged unit-charge potential

    V(x) = (1/N) sum_i 1/|x-R_i omega_i|,

the degree-l addition identity gives

    P_l(omega dot u)
      = sum_(j:l_j=l) q_j(omega)q_j(u) / [(2l+1)nu_j].

The Newton expansion inside the source sphere therefore has the degree-l contribution sum_j C_j q_j(x)/[(2l+1)nu_j], where

    C_j = (1/N) sum_i q_j(omega_i) R_i^(-l_j-1).

With W(y)=[V(rho y)-V(0)]/(epsilon rho²), the required moment is

    target_j = (2l_j+1) epsilon rho^(2-l_j) c_j nu_j.

This normalization is independently checked against the complete exported target. It gives the exact weighted norm

    ||target||_nu² = epsilon² [140/3+3rho²].

Our independently generated basis has nonzero target entries at indices2,5,7 (zero-based): epsilon*rho, -1/25165824, 3/16777216. All other entries are exactly zero. The different producer ordering and scaling are handled by S. We then recompute every final C_j from the emitted R_i and directions, not from an iteration state or cached residual, and check

    sum_j (C_j-target_j)²/nu_j <= zeta².

All producer residual entries must also equal S times this independently computed vector.

## Legendre tail and Poisson derivative bound

Every actual final radius exceeds3/4. For |y|<=2, the nonconstant prescribed-moment error before rescaling is bounded by

    zeta sqrt[sum_(l=1)^T (2rho)^(2l)/(2l+1)] <= 4 zeta rho.

The first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz in the normalized moment coordinates and the addition identity sum_j q_j(u)²/nu_j=2l+1 in each degree. The checker evaluates the finite rational sum and its stated upper bound explicitly.

For l>T, |P_l(omega dot u)|<=1 follows directly from the degree-l addition identity and Cauchy-Schwarz. Hence with q=8rho/3<=1/2, the remaining potential tail on B2 is at most

    (4/3) q^(T+1)/(1-q) <= 2(4rho)^(T+1).

The factor4/3 is the inverse source radius bound. This is a uniform analytic tail, not truncation error inferred from the checked low-degree moments. Define

    U = 4zeta/(epsilon rho)
          + 2(4rho)^(T+1)/(epsilon rho²).

Then E(y)=W(y)-P(y) is harmonic and |E|<=U throughout B2. The computed sources are outside physical B_(2rho), and all exact geometric tail inequalities are checked.

For any point y in B1, the closed unit ball about y lies in B2. The probability-sphere Poisson kernel at its center expands as

    1 + 3(theta dot z)
      + (15/2)(theta dot z)² - (5/2)||z||² + O(||z||³).

Thus ||grad E(y)||<=3U by integrating3theta E; for a unit v,

    v^t Hess E(y) v
       = integral [15(theta dot v)²-5] E(y),

whose absolute value is at most10U. The symmetric Hessian operator norm is therefore at most10U. These constants are independently rederived, and do not rely on a numerical fit or sampling of the gradient.

## One exact critical-point existence and index certificate

P has p=(0,0,-1/6), grad P(p)=0 and constant Hessian H=diag(-2,-4,6). The checker reconstructs these derivatives directly from the target polynomial. With ||H^-1||=1/2, use

    G(y)=y-H^-1 grad W(y)=p-H^-1 grad E(y)

on the closed witness ball B(p,1/48). It lies strictly inside B1 because ||p||+1/48=3/16. The exact inequalities

    3U/2 <= 1/96,
    5U <= 1/4,
    10U < 2

pass in each output. The first two make G a contraction mapping the witness ball into itself (even the bound at the center plus the contraction motion gives at most three quarters of the radius). Therefore there is exactly one critical point in that ball. The last inequality and the eigenvalue gap2 of H preserve the two negative and one positive eigenvalues, so it is nondegenerate with Morse index2. Scaling y back to x=rho y gives the stated physical witness ball. The actual positive unit-charge potential is N V; multiplication by N preserves its critical point and index.

This is an analytic existence/uniqueness statement for a ball. It does not claim that a decimal approximate location is the exact critical point.
