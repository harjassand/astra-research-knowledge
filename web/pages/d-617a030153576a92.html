# Near-full-rank Casimir energy: all-matrix capacity bound

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: internally proved asymptotic order for fixed m and k=o(d_N). The lower bound optimizes over every right-kernel subspace; the matching upper is a diagonal radial capacitary taper. No cycle-1 or existing cycle-2 file was edited.

## Result

Fix m>=2 and put d=m-1, H_N=Sym^N(C^m), n=d_N=binom(N+d,d). Use the generator normalization and Casimir L from cycle-2 RESULT.txt, so its kernel is CI and its nonzero eigenvalues are c_l=l(l+d), l=1,...,N, with cumulative spectral dimension
\[
 D(t)=\binom{K+d}{d}^{\!2},\quad
 K=\max\{j\le N:j(j+d)\le t\},
\]
for t<c_N, and D(t)=n^2 for t>=c_N. Define
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)=\inf\{ \langle X,LX\rangle:
       \|X\|_{HS}=1,\ \operatorname{rank}X\le n-k\}.
\]
For fixed m and integer 1<=k<n, the following exact lower bound holds:
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)\ \ge\
 \frac{m k}{m n S_{N,d}(k)+k},\qquad
 S_{N,d}(k):=\int_m^\infty
       \min\!\left\{1,\frac{kD(t)}{n}\right\}\frac{dt}{t^2}.       \tag{1}
\]
For fixed m and k=k_N=o(n), as N tends to infinity this has the matching order
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)\asymp_m
 \begin{cases}
  [\log(n/k)]^{-1},&m=2\ (d=1),\\[2mm]
  (k/n)^{(d-1)/d},&m\ge3\ (d\ge2).
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{2}
\]
The constants in \(\asymp_m\) depend only on fixed m. In terms of N, the second line is
\(k^{(m-2)/(m-1)}N^{-(m-2)}\), up to m-dependent constants. For d=1, n=N+1, so k=o(n) is exactly k/N->0 and makes the logarithm diverge.

## Green compression and the all-matrix lower bound

Let \(\mathcal H_0=\{Y\in HS(H_N):\operatorname{Tr}Y=0\}\). The Casimir is positive definite on \(\mathcal H_0\), with \(L|_{\mathcal H_0}\ge m I\). Fix any k-dimensional right-kernel subspace K in H_N, let P_K be its orthogonal projector, and let
\[
 \mathcal V_K=\operatorname{Hom}(K,H_N),\qquad
 C_K:\mathcal H_0\to\mathcal V_K,\quad C_KY=Y|_K.
\]
The target vector is the inclusion \(J_K:K\hookrightarrow H_N\), with
\(\|J_K\|_{HS}^2=k\). With \(P_0 A=A-(\operatorname{Tr}A/n)I\), identify \(Z\in\mathcal V_K\) with its zero extension \(\widetilde Z=ZP_K\) to \(H_N\). The exact adjoint is
\[
 C_K^*Z=P_0(\widetilde Z).                                                \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, \(\langle Z,Y|_K\rangle_{HS}=\langle ZP_K,Y\rangle_{HS}\), and orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_0\) gives (3).

Define the positive Green compression on \(\mathcal V_K\) by
\[
 G_K=C_K(L|_{\mathcal H_0})^{-1}C_K^*.
\]
It is positive definite for k<n: if \(C_K^*Z=0\), then \(ZP_K\) is scalar; its rank is at most k<n, so it is zero and Z=0. The minimum-energy extension of the boundary value J_K is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}(K):=
 \inf_{\substack{Y\in\mathcal H_0\\Y|_K=J_K}}\langle Y,LY\rangle
 =\langle J_K,G_K^{-1}J_K\rangle.                              \tag{4}
\]
For verification, the minimizer is \(Y_*=L^{-1}C_K^*G_K^{-1}J_K\). It satisfies C_KY_*=J_K; for every Z with C_KZ=0, its energy cross term with Y_* is
\(\langle G_K^{-1}J_K,C_KZ\rangle=0\), proving minimality and (4).

For every Z in \(\mathcal V_K\), take its zero extension A=ZP_K. Then rank(A)<=k and \(\|A\|_{HS}=\|Z\|_{HS}\). Since L^+ kills scalars,
\[
 \langle Z,G_KZ\rangle=\langle A,L^+A\rangle.
\]
The rank-spectral theorem of RESULT.txt §1, applied to A after scaling, gives for every t>=m
\[
 \|\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(L)A\|_{HS}^2
 \le \min\{1,kD(t)/n\}\|A\|_{HS}^2.
\]
Using \(1/c_l=\int_{c_l}^{\infty}t^{-2}\,dt\) on each positive Casimir sector therefore yields the uniform operator bound
\[
 0<G_K\le S_{N,d}(k) I_{\mathcal V_K}.                           \tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)–(5) and \(\|J_K\|^2=k\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Cap}(K)\ge k/S_{N,d}(k),                          \tag{6}
\]
uniformly over every K, coherent or otherwise.

Now let X be any feasible normalized matrix, choose any k-plane K inside ker(X), and write
\[
 a=\operatorname{Tr}X/n,\qquad X=aI+Y,\quad Y\in\mathcal H_0,
 \quad e=\langle X,LX\rangle=\langle Y,LY\rangle.
\]
The kernel condition gives \(Y|_K=-aJ_K\). By homogeneity in (4),
\(e\ge |a|^2\operatorname{Cap}(K)\); this remains true when a=0. Also
\(\|Y\|_{HS}^2\le e/m\) by the spectral gap, and orthogonality to I gives
\[
 1=n|a|^2+\|Y\|_{HS}^2
 \le n|a|^2+e/m
 \le e\{n/\operatorname{Cap}(K)+1/m\}.
\]
Using (6) gives (1). This step uses the right kernel only through its arbitrary k-plane K; no diagonal, normal, or Hermitian assumption on X or K is made.

## Spectral integral estimate and constants

For every t>=0,
\[
 D(t)\le B_d(1+t)^d,\qquad B_d=(d+1)^{2d}/(d!)^2.                \tag{7}
\]
For t<c_N this follows from K<=sqrt(t) and
\(\binom{K+d}{d}\le(K+d)^d/d!\); for t>=c_N use D=n^2 and the same bound with K=N<=sqrt(t). Thus (7) includes the endpoint and the saturated tail.

Set \(B'_d=2^dB_d\) and \(T=(n/(B'_d k))^{1/d}\). If k=o(n), then T->infinity, so eventually T>=m; explicitly this holds when n/k>=B'_d m^d. For m<=t<=T, (7) gives \(kD(t)/n\le B'_dkt^d/n\); above T use the cap by one. Therefore
\[
 S_{N,d}(k)\le B'_d\frac{k}{n}\int_m^T t^{d-2}\,dt+\frac1T.
\]
For d=1 and n/k>=32 this implies
\[
 S_{N,1}(k)\le 16\,\frac{k}{n}\log(n/k).                        \tag{8}
\]
For d>=2 it implies
\[
 S_{N,d}(k)\le A_d(k/n)^{1/d},\qquad
 A_d=(B'_d)^{1/d}\left(1+\frac1{d-1}\right).                    \tag{9}
\]
Substitution into (1) gives, when k=o(n),
\[
 \Lambda_2(N,k)\ge
 \frac{2}{32\log(n/k)+1}
 \ge \frac{1}{17\log(n/k)}
\]
once \(\log(n/k)\ge1\), and, for d>=2,
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)\ge
 \frac{m}{mA_d+1}(k/n)^{(d-1)/d}.                               \tag{10}
\]
The constants are deliberately loose; the exact finite-N statement is (1), not the asymptotic simplification.

## Matching radial capacitary upper bound

In the occupation basis, the shell \(|\beta|=s\) has
\(g_s=\binom{s+d-1}{d-1}\) vectors, for 0<=s<=N. For a diagonal radial operator A with value a_s on shell s, the cycle-2 root-edge identity gives exactly
\[
 \langle A,LA\rangle
 =\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w_s|a_s-a_{s+1}|^2,\qquad
 w_s=(N-s)(s+d)g_s
     =d(N-s)\binom{s+d}{d}.                                    \tag{11}
\]
Choose R minimal with \(V_R=\binom{R+d}{d}\ge k\), and M=floor(N/2). In the range k=o(n), eventually R<M. Put a_s=1 for s<=R, a_s=0 for s>=M, and for R<=s<=M define the resistance-linear profile
\[
 a_s=\frac{\sum_{j=s}^{M-1}w_j^{-1}}
            {\sum_{j=R}^{M-1}w_j^{-1}}.
\]
Then X=I-A vanishes exactly on the V_R occupation states with s<=R and is nonzero elsewhere. Hence rank(X)=n-V_R<=n-k. Its unnormalized energy is exactly
\[
 \langle A,LA\rangle=
 \left(\sum_{j=R}^{M-1}w_j^{-1}\right)^{-1}.                   \tag{12}
\]
For explicit constants, if N>=4d and R+1<=N/4, set
\(C_w=d^d/(d-1)!\) and \(\eta_d=1-(3/4)^d\). For s<M,
\(w_s\le C_wN(s+1)^d\), and
\(\|X\|_{HS}^2\ge n-V_{M-1}\ge\eta_dn\). The resistance sum in (12) is at least
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (2N)^{-1}\log(N/(R+1)),&d=1,\\
 [2(d-1)C_wN(R+1)^{d-1}]^{-1},&d\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus the normalized upper bounds are at most
\(8N/[n\log(N/k)]\) for d=1 and
\(2(d-1)C_wN(R+1)^{d-1}/(\eta_dn)\) for d>=2. Here R+1=k for d=1; for d>=2,
\(R+1\le[1+(d!)^{1/d}]k^{1/d}\). The conditions N>=4d and R+1<=N/4 eventually hold whenever k=o(n), with fixed d. In particular, for d>=2 the explicit bound is
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)\le
 \frac{2(d-1)C_w[1+(d!)^{1/d}]^{d-1}(d!)^{1/d}}{\eta_d}
       (k/n)^{(d-1)/d}.
\]
The coarser order summary is therefore
\[
 \sum_{j=R}^{M-1}w_j^{-1}\asymp_d
 \begin{cases}
 N^{-1}\log\!\bigl(N/(R+1)\bigr),&d=1,\\
 [N(R+1)^{d-1}]^{-1},&d\ge2,
 \end{cases}
\]
and \(R+1\asymp_d k^{1/d}\) (with R+1=k exactly for d=1). Normalizing X in (12) proves
\[
 \Lambda_2(N,k)\le C\, [\log(N/k)]^{-1}
 \quad (k=o(N)),
\]
and, for fixed m>=3,
\[
 \Lambda_m(N,k)\le C_m\,N k^{(d-1)/d}/n
 \le C'_m(k/n)^{(d-1)/d}
 \quad (k=o(n)).
\]
Together with (8)–(10), this proves (2).

## Coherent-hole check and scope

A hard coherent hole is \(X=I-P_{\rm coh}\), where P_coh projects onto a highest-weight coherent vector. Equation (11) gives
\(\langle P_{\rm coh},LP_{\rm coh}\rangle=w_0=dN\), so its normalized energy is \(dN/(n-1)\). For m=2 this is order one, while the optimized rank-(n-1) energy is order \(1/\log N\): the radial harmonic taper improves the hard hole by a logarithm. For a growing coherent cap \(P_R\) (holes in all shells s<=R), the hard boundary cost is \(w_R\asymp_d N(R+1)^d\); the resistance taper costs only \(N(R+1)^{d-1}\) for d>=2 and \(N/\log(N/(R+1))\) for d=1. Thus the hard cap is off by a factor of order R in d>=2 and much more in the critical d=1 case. The leading all-matrix scale is nevertheless controlled by the arbitrary-kernel capacity lower bound, so these improvements do not exploit a diagonal restriction in the lower proof.

Exact hypotheses: N>=1, fixed m, integer 1<=k<n; (1) is finite-N. The asymptotic equivalence (2) assumes k=o(n), so R=o(N), T->infinity, and the outer shell s>=N/2 retains a fixed fraction of the Hilbert-space dimension. No external oracle or computation is used. The argument depends on the exact SU(m) Casimir spectrum and the previously established rank-spectral profile; it is not an operational memory claim.
