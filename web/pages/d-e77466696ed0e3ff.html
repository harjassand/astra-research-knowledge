# Cycle 2: sharp uniform controlled-menu to controlled-menu exact conversion

2026-10-06. Independent mathematical derivation; audit message from check_selection reports the same separately reconstructed batching/repair construction and no current obstruction. Agreement is not independent external validation. Novelty remains unverified. Cycle-1 artifacts preserved.

## Exact interface and theorem

Finite states i=0,...,m-1. Finite source actions a with positive costs c_a and finite observations P_i^a(x)>0 on a common action-specific alphabet. Finite target actions b with positive costs beta_b and finite full-support observations Q_i^b(y). Given the chosen action and past, the next observation is independent of the past with its specified state/action law. The source menu collectively identifies every state pair; the target need not identify every pair. All laws and both menus are supplied and known. Auxiliary state-independent randomization is free. Costs count source/target observations weighted by their stated action cost; offline exact probability computations are excluded. No unknown-law acquisition or efficient numerical exact simulator is claimed.

Let D_ij^a=KL(P_i^a||P_j^a), g_ij^b=KL(Q_i^b||Q_j^b). For a vector v indexed by j!=i, define

phi_i(v)=min_{w_a>=0}{sum_a c_a w_a : sum_a D_ij^a w_a >= v_j for every j!=i}.

We extend this definition to signed v. Since all D>=0, negative constraints are vacuous and phi_i(v)=phi_i(v_+). It is finite, monotone, convex, positively homogeneous, and subadditive. A fixed identifying source design bounds phi_i(v)<=K||v_+||_1, uniformly in i. In particular it is Lipschitz in the l1 norm with a source-dependent constant. Set

gamma_i = max_b phi_i(g_i^b)/beta_b.

**Uniform exact-conversion theorem.** Every finite-output controlled-target stopping policy, with total target cost C_G integrable in each state, has an exact controlled-source reproduction with total cost C_F satisfying simultaneously

E_i C_F <= gamma_i E_i C_G + K sqrt(E_i C_G), all i.

K depends only on the two menus, costs, and state set, not the policy or its finite output space. These coefficients are optimal: repeating target action b for n observations requires any exact source reproduction to cost at least n phi_i(g_i^b) in state i. Thus any policy-uniform first-order coefficient must be >=gamma_i.

## Finite-output gauge conversion lemma

For an arbitrary finite common-support terminal experiment H, put lambda_ij(z)=log(H_i(z)/H_j(z)), Delta(H)=max_ijz |lambda_ij(z)|, and
B_i(H)=E_{Z~H_i} phi_i(lambda_i(Z)).

There are source-dependent K and eta_0>0 such that for every 0<eta<=eta_0 an exact source simulation of H exists with

E_i C_F <= B_i(H)+K[sqrt(1+B_i(H))+log(1/eta)+eta(1+Delta(H))].

The following proof changes the leading cost from one fixed mixture to an output-dependent deterministic allocation; its constants are uniform even when the optimal allocations omit actions or fail to distinguish rival states from each other.

### Classification, allocation, verifier

Fix one identifying iid source action design q_0, for example uniform. Its action/observation experiment distinguishes every pair. Half-likelihood Markov inequality gives an ML classification error bound <=K exp(-kappa ell), since every pair's Hellinger affinity is <1. Classify after ell=O(log(1/eta)) observations with error <=eta in every state. Conditional on classification k, propose z~H_k by fresh randomization.

For candidate/output (k,z), let h=log(1/eta), v=[lambda_k(z)]_+, and r=v+h*1. Choose an optimal LP allocation w(r). Let n_a=ceil(w_a(r)) and execute a deterministic batch using n_a samples of each source action in a fixed predetermined order. Let n=sum_a n_a. Then

batch_cost <= phi_k(r)+sum_a c_a <= phi_k(v)+h phi_k(1)+sum_a c_a,
n <= [phi_k(v)+h phi_k(1)]/c_min + number_of_source_actions.

Under true k, the full batch's mean log-ratio against every j is at least r_j. During the batch reject immediately if ANY reverse score S_jk reaches h. Do not accept before the complete planned batch has been collected. At batch end, if no prior rejection and every S_kj>=r_j, accept. Otherwise continue with fresh q_0 samples, stopping when either all S_kj>=r_j (accept) or any S_jk>=h (reject). Rejection wins a tie.

### Own-state cost and vanishing-drift issue

Under true k, couple the process to a hypothetical complete planned batch even on paths that reject early. Its independent bounded log-ratio increments have variances bounded by a menu-dependent constant; hence for each j,
E_k [r_j-S_kj(n)]_+ <= E_k |S_kj(n)-E_k S_kj(n)| <= K sqrt n.

The first inequality uses E_k S_kj(n)>=r_j. Thus the sum of positive remaining deficits has expectation O(sqrt n). On surviving paths, the baseline continuation has positive fixed mean d_kj(q_0)>0 in every required direction. Conditional on the complete batch scores, its expected duration is at most

t_rem+K sqrt(1+t_rem)+K,
t_rem=max_j [r_j-S_kj(n)]_+/d_kj(q_0).

This is proved by summing Hoeffding simultaneous-passage tails: P(tau>t)<=sum_j P(S_kj(t)<deficit_j). Multiplying by the survivor event only decreases the unconditional expected bound. Jensen therefore gives baseline expected cost O(sqrt n)+O(n^(1/4))+O(1). Combining with batch cost and n's bound, the correct-candidate cost is

<=phi_k(v)+K[sqrt(1+phi_k(v))+h].

No positive drift of the output-specific allocation is invoked. This is the critical reason square-root overhead survives omitted/rare directions. Fixed-mixture regularization is unnecessary.

### Wrong-state cost

Under true i!=k, the total batch duration is <=n. If it survives into the baseline phase, the reverse i/k score starts at least -L_P*n, where L_P bounds every single-source-observation absolute log ratio. Baseline d_ik(q_0)>0, so its one-dimensional passage to h has conditional expected duration O(n+h+1). Consequently wrong-state verification costs <=K(n+h+1)<=K(1+Delta(H)+h), uniformly in z and k. This is deliberately not O(h): classification's <=eta wrong probability charges it at O(eta Delta(H)+eta h), already allowed in the finite-H remainder.

### Verification errors and exact repair

Under true k, exp(S_jk) is a nonnegative martingale throughout the deterministic batch and baseline continuation. Union-bound Ville gives own rejection probability <=(m-1)eta. On acceptance, S_ki>=r_i>=lambda_ki(z)+h. Stopped likelihood change of measure therefore gives
P_i(accept|k,z)<=eta H_i(z)/H_k(z) P_k(accept|k,z).
The verifier is integrable under all states by the preceding bounds. Action-choice factors cancel between states because the policy is state-independent conditional on (k,z).

Let A_i(k) be the initial classifier law and p_i(k,z) the verifier success probability. Thin accepted proposals independently by a_eta=(1-eta)/(1+(m-1)eta). Their resulting output submass is
Q_i(z)=a_eta sum_k A_i(k)H_k(z)p_i(k,z).
The preceding own/cross bounds imply Q_i(z)<=(1-eta)H_i(z), and its total missing mass delta_i satisfies eta<=delta_i<=2m eta. Define R_i(z)=[H_i(z)-Q_i(z)]/delta_i. It is a common-support probability law and Delta(R)<=Delta(H)+log(2m/eta).

Exact bounded-diameter repair under q_0: after n_0 observations the ML confusion matrix A has ||A-I||_infinity<=K exp(-kappa n_0). Its inverse obeys ||A^-1-I||<=K' exp(-kappa n_0). Taking n_0=O(1+Delta(R)) makes K_R=A^-1 R entrywise nonnegative, because R_j(z)<=exp(Delta(R))R_i(z); its rows sum to one. Classify with A and draw from row K_R. The resulting law A K_R=R is exact. Run it on a fresh source tail only on the missing-mass flag. Final output law is Q_i+delta_iR_i=H_i. Its expected repair cost is O(eta[1+Delta(H)+h]).

Averaging own-state costs over H_i and using Jensen gives B_i+K[sqrt(1+B_i)+h]. Wrong classifications add O(eta Delta+eta h), classification costs O(h), and repair adds O(eta[1+Delta+h]). Since eta h is uniformly bounded, this proves the finite-H lemma. Constants depend only on the source menu and costs.

Fresh tails are formalized using an iid row array containing a sample from each action, independent randomizers, and revealing only the selected action's component. Unused rows after a stopping time are conditionally independent under every fixed state. All batch/rejection decisions and output flags are adapted, and each pasted stage is a valid stopping policy.

## Controlled-target bounded policies

Consider target stopping time T<=M and its finite terminal output Z with law H. The entire stopped history includes past actions, observations and revealed randomization. Its i/j likelihood ratio is exp S_ij(T), with

S_ij(T)=sum_{t<=T} g_ij^{B_t}+M_ij(T).

The action/stop kernels cancel in the state likelihood ratio. M_ij is a martingale with bounded increments and predictable conditional variance bounded by a menu-dependent constant. For bounded T,
E_i M_ij(T)^2 <= K E_i T,
by expanding the stopped sum and cancelling martingale cross terms. Common support of H follows from state-independent behavioral kernels and fully supported observation probabilities.

Stopped likelihood change of measure and conditional Jensen give
lambda_ij(Z)<=E_i[S_ij(T)|Z].
Monotonicity and convexity of the signed-vector extension of phi_i then imply
B_i(H)<=E_i phi_i(S_i(T)).
Using subadditivity, the Lipschitz bound, and the definition of gamma,

phi_i(S_i(T))<=sum_{t<=T}phi_i(g_i^{B_t})+K sum_j |M_ij(T)|
<=gamma_i sum_{t<=T}beta_{B_t}+K sum_j|M_ij(T)|.

Therefore B_i<=gamma_i E_i C_G+K sqrt(E_i T). Since target beta_min>0, E_i T<=E_iC_G/beta_min. Also Delta(H)<=M L_Q. Apply the finite-H lemma with eta=eta_0/M to obtain
E_i C_F<=gamma_i E_i C_G+K sqrt M.
Force at least one source observation in every entered block by appending one ignored observation if needed; this changes only K sqrt M.

## Arbitrary integrable policies: dyadic construction

At time zero copy the target's state-independent stop/output decision exactly at zero source cost. On continued paths use target time blocks of lengths L_k=2^k with checkpoints s_{k-1}=2^k-1. For every finite target history h at a checkpoint, the bounded next-block policy reports the full new sequence of actions and observations, its stop position/output if it stops, or a continuation flag at block end. Reports have finite common support. Its target block cost has the supplied positive per-action costs.

Simulate each report exactly using the bounded-policy theorem with the SAME menus, gamma_i, and uniform K. Choose the corresponding next source stage from the previous report; fresh tails preserve its unconditional continuation law under each fixed state. Induction shows the source and target have identical reach-history and stopped-report probabilities. In particular, source stage k is reached with probability P_i(T>2^k-1). Integrability implies these probabilities vanish, so infinitely many stages occur with probability zero. Summing complete stopped-report probabilities proves exact final output law.

Target block costs telescope to C_G. Summing source expected block costs by Tonelli gives
E_i C_F<=gamma_i E_i C_G+K sum_k 2^(k/2)P_i(T>2^k-1).
For integer T, the sum is E_i sum_{k:2^k<=T}2^(k/2)<=[sqrt2/(sqrt2-1)]E_i sqrt T<=K' sqrt(E_iC_G).
Thus the uniform theorem follows without any higher moment assumption. The increasing limit of source stage stopping times is a stopping time; requiring positive source duration for entered stages means infinite stages have infinite duration. The expectation cost bound gives source integrability since c_min>0. Terminal output is measurable at that stopping time.

## Exact lower bound and reusable LP dual certificate

For any exact source reproduction of n repeated observations of target action b, data processing and the controlled stopped-record KL identity give
sum_a E_iN_a D_ij^a >= n g_ij^b, every j!=i.
The stopped identity follows from bounded log-ratio increments, state-independent action kernels, integrability, and predictable indicators 1{N>=t}. Taking w_a=E_iN_a/n in the gauge LP yields E_i C_F>=n phi_i(g_i^b). Since target cost is n beta_b, the maximum over b proves gamma_i optimal for a universal first-order coefficient. This lower bound is exact, not asymptotic.

A certificate for phi_i(v) is primal w>=0 and dual z_j>=0 with D_i^T z<=c and objective equality c·w=z·v. A certificate for gamma_i enumerates the finite target actions and their gauge primal/dual optima, divided by beta_b. One fixed source mixture generally cannot certify this sharp controlled-target coefficient; it must spread effort across target directions even when the target asks only one direction on a given path.

For the large-budget stopping order with statewise COST budgets, gamma_i<=1 in every state is necessary; gamma_i<1 in every state is sufficient, since linear savings absorb K sqrt n uniformly. If no gamma_i equals one, this gives an exact generic characterization. Equality cases are unresolved. Do not infer a matching all-decision-problems order characterization from this supply-side theorem.

## Obstructions, boundaries, source provenance, next test

A fixed output-dependent iid mixture can have vanishing reverse drifts and constants depending on its smallest rates, so simply regularizing it does not justify uniform O(sqrt M). The deterministic allocation plus baseline deficit repair avoids that obstruction. Conversely, taking only maximal pairwise KL rates fails the shared-budget constraints, as the cycle-1 exact full-support 5/3 lower bound demonstrates.
