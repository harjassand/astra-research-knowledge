## Exact author interface

Theorem 1.1 fixes (r(ε)=O(ε^{-2})) before the von Neumann inclusion (A\subset M), selfadjoint (x\in M), and strong neighborhood. For every finite set (F\subset H) and δ>0, it supplies a partition (p_i\in A), a selfadjoint (a\in A) with ∥a∥≤∥x∥, and projection (q\in M), such that
\[ \|(1-q)ξ\|<δ\quad(ξ\in F),\qquad
\|q y q\|\leε\|x\|,quad y=\sum_i p_i x p_i-a. \]

Thus ∥y∥≤2∥x∥. The source does not assert that ∥yξ∥ is small, that the pinching channel approximates identity, or that its partitions/projections are efficiently acquired.

## A valid first-moment consequence

For a normal state with standard vector ξ and α=∥(1-q)ξ∥², write (h=qξ), (e=(1-q)ξ). For a selfadjoint (y) with ∥q y q∥≤ε∥x∥ and ∥y∥≤2∥x∥,
\[ |\langle yξ,ξ\rangle|
\leε\|x\|+4\|x\|\sqrt{α}+2\|x\|α. \]
Proof: expand the four pairings on (h+e); the (h,h) term is bounded by the compressed norm, the two cross terms by ∥y∥∥h∥∥e∥, and the last by ∥y∥∥e∥². This is a scalar expectation bound. In a density-matrix representation, the gentle-projection trace estimate can improve its constants, but is unnecessary here.

## Exact second-moment/weighted-action obstruction

In (M_2), let
\[ ρ_α=\operatorname{diag}(1-α,α),\quad
q=|0\rangle\langle0|,\quad x=y=X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \]
Use the diagonal MASA, one partition block (p_1=I), (a=0), and faithful (0<α<1). Then φ_α(1-q)=α, (qyq=0), ∥y∥=1, but
\[ \|yρ_α^{1/2}\|_2^2=\operatorname{Tr}ρ_α y^2=1. \]
The discarded subspace has arbitrarily small input state mass and still receives order-one action from the accepted subspace. Therefore no universal function tending to zero in α,ε bounds the weighted action or coherence defect from just the strong-paving conclusion.

Even with one fixed faithful state on (B(ℓ2)), choose positive diagonal weights (p_j\to0), (p_1>0), (q_j=I-|j\rangle\langle j|), (y_j=|1\rangle\langle j|+|j\rangle\langle1|). Then (q_j\to I) strongly, (q_j y_j q_j=0), and φ(y_j^2)=p_1+p_j\ge p_1. The operator family varies; this is not a violation of a fixed-operator strong convergence theorem.

An honest squared-action identity for any vector is
\[ \|yξ\|^2=\|q y qξ+q y(1-q)ξ\|^2
  +\|(1-q)y qξ+(1-q)y(1-q)ξ\|^2. \]
The uncontrolled cross block ((1-q)yq) is the missing certificate. If it is independently bounded on the state vector by λ, then ∥yξ∥≤ε∥x∥+2∥y∥√α+λ. A commutation condition ([q,y]=0) is one sufficient interface, but is not part of the source claim. This is a concrete extra obligation for a dynamics or witness-recovery application.

## The source's stronger central-state construction and its elementary control

The preceding obstruction applies to the arbitrary-inclusion final interface. It must not hide a stronger intermediate interface. In §8 Proposition prop:expected, the source assumes a faithful normal φ with \(A\subset M_\varphi\), takes \(a=E_Ax\), constructs an arbitrarily high even moment of \(y=S_P(x)-E_Ax\) below ρε^{2k}, and finally chooses \(q=1_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}(y)\). That q commutes with y. For ρ≤1, positivity/Jensen gives φ(y²)≤(φ(y^{2k}))^{1/k}<ε². Thus its constructed partitions do supply small weighted action on this A-central state.

For that particular weighted-action application, however, a substantially simpler equally informed control is available. Let \(R=(e_a)\) be a finite A partition with ∥S_R(y)∥φ small, where \(y=x-E_Ax\). Color its atoms uniformly and independently with r colors, and let P be the resulting color partition. Since every e_a centralizes φ, the blocks e_a y e_b are mutually orthogonal in the state Hilbert norm. Hence exactly
\[
 \mathbb E\|S_P(y)\|_\varphi^2
 =r^{-1}\|y\|_\varphi^2+(1-r^{-1})\|S_R(y)\|_\varphi^2.
\]
The source's §2 Lemma lem:pinching proves ∥S_R(y)∥φ→0 under refinement. For finitely many prescribed observables, sum this identity and select a coloring. The required palette for weighted squared-action accuracy is O(sum_j ∥y_j∥φ²/ε²); no stable-polynomial, Bernoulli free-product, or mixed-moment transfer is needed for that weaker output. In a supplied finite matrix diagonalization, the atom partition and block scores are explicit. In a general von Neumann algebra the initial fine-partition acquisition remains a separate cost, and random selection alone is not a deterministic bit algorithm.

The release's structural contribution is controlling compressed operator norm with a fixed palette uniformly in the desired strong neighborhood, over arbitrary MASAs, including the part with no central normal functional. Neither the elementary weighted-action control nor our counterexample settles that claim. A downstream proposal using only weighted action must compare against the elementary coloring control before attributing value to the much stronger paving theorem.

## The finite-dimensional comparator

If (M=M_d) acts on ℂ^d and (F) is an orthonormal basis, take δ<1/√d. Then
\[ \operatorname{rank}(1-q)=\sum_{j=1}^d\|(1-q)e_j\|^2<dδ^2<1, \]
so (q=I). Thus strong paving in finite dimension specializes to ordinary norm paving. The genuinely matching prior is Ravichandran–Srivastava, *Asymptotically Optimal Multi-Paving*, arXiv:1706.03737v2 (23 August 2017), whose exact theorem/proof reading is recorded separately. Its quadratic finite matrix paving mechanism is already the source's finite combinatorial input; merely applying the release theorem to a finite quantum matrix is not a new resource class.
