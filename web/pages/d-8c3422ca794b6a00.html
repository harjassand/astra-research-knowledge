GOOD-CENTER L2 PLANE CONTROL AND CRITICAL RECTIFIABILITY
New independent baseline; /root/broadcasting_proof_sol; Sol/max.
2026-10-09. No descendants, no prior/source edits, no new Luna exposure.

This is a distinct quantitative escalation of the frozen abstract
height/stress theorem, not a validation repeat. Primary published
Reifenberg and square-function facts are named below. No harmonic-map
or graph representation is assumed. The main new interface is an L2
best-plane inequality for EVERY finite measure carried by good centers.
The critical sigma-finiteness and rectifiability consequence is proved
here with its explicit density bootstrap. A stronger globally uniform
critical packing claim is kept separate in section 10.

1. EXACT INPUT AND RESULT

Let n>=2 and let nu,f,beta,mu be locally finite Radon measures on B4
in R^n: nu>=0, f real vector-valued, beta symmetric, mu signed. Assume
  M=[nu f^t; f beta]>=0, d=tr beta,
  grad nu=2f, div f=d, div beta=grad mu,
  2mu<=d, |mu|<=C d, nu not zero.                              (1.1)
These are distribution identities and measure inequalities. The sign
of the stress identity is as written. Fix integer p>=4 and the kernels
  phi_{x,r}=r^{-n}(1-|y-x|^2/r^2)_+^p,
  psi_{x,r}=2p r^{-n}(1-|y-x|^2/r^2)_+^{p-1}.
Define H=integral psi dnu, A=r^2 integral phi dmu, P=A/H.
Let Z be a Borel subset of B1 such that, for EVERY x in Z and EVERY
0<r<2,
  H(x,r)>0, 1/2<=P(x,r)<=N<infinity.                          (1.2)
If Z is empty the conclusions are immediate; otherwise N>=1/2.

Put k=n-1. There is B=B(n,p,N)<infinity such that for x in Z,
0<r<1/64, and every finite positive measure gamma supported on Z,

 beta_{gamma,2}^k(x,r)^2
   <=B r^{-k} integral_{B_r(x)} W(z,r) dgamma(z),              (1.3)
 W(z,r)=P(z,6r)-P(z,5r)>=0,
 beta_{gamma,2}^k(x,r)^2
   =r^{-k-2} inf_{affine k-plane L}
                      integral_{B_r(x)} dist(z,L)^2 dgamma(z).

Only gamma restricted to B_r(x) is used. There is NO gamma density,
family-size, atomicity, or rectifiability premise in (1.3). In particular
it applies to finite discrete packing measures before their density is
known. Here 'supported on Z' means CARRIED by Z (gamma(R^n\Z)=0);
topological support need not be contained in a nonclosed Z. Every
restriction/packing measure uses the SAME tuple and SAME P. Constants
in (1.3) do not depend on the amplitude of nu or on
H(0,2). The supplied normalization H(0,2)=1 may be imposed but is not
needed. No extra global energy bound is required: (1.2) supplies the
normalized local mass bounds used below.

There also exist eta_*=eta_*(n,p,N)>0 and A_*=A_*(n,p,N)<infinity
with the following consequence. For R<=1/1024, any Borel S subset Z
such that
  P(z,64R)-P_0(z)<=eta_* for every z in S,
  P_0(z)=lim_{r down to 0}P(z,r),                             (1.4)
satisfies, for every ball B_R(q),
  N_r(S intersect B_R(q))<=A_* (R/r)^k for 0<r<R,             (1.5)
and H^k(S intersect B_R(q))<=A_* R^k, up to the fixed Hausdorff
normalization constant. The same holds on each smaller ball because
the tail in (1.4) decreases when R decreases.

Take R_j=2^{-j}/1024 and
  Z_j={z in Z:P(z,64R_j)-P_0(z)<=eta_*}.                       (1.6)
These Borel sets increase and cover Z. Each has finite H^k measure on
B1, and each is countably k-rectifiable. Thus

  Z has sigma-finite H^{n-1} measure and is countably
  (n-1)-rectifiable.                                        (1.7)

In dimension n=1, Z contains at most one point, so the analogous H^0
and tube conclusions are stronger and need no Reifenberg theorem.
Proof is in section 9.

The theorem does NOT yet assert one uniform critical Minkowski constant
for the whole Z, or local finiteness of H^k on the entire Z. The tail
decomposition can have arbitrarily small starting radii depending on
the tuple. Section 10 gives an additional proved splitting lemma and
the exact remaining framework gate for those stronger assertions.

2. FREQUENCY ALGEBRA AND NORMALIZED MASS CLASS

The frozen qualitative proof derived, with dot=d/dlog r,
  D=r^2 integral phi dd, Rrad=integral psi dbeta[y-x,y-x],
  dot A=2A-D+Rrad, dot H=2D,
  D=integral psi(y-x).df, Rrad H>=D^2, 2A<=D.
Writing n_r=D/H and q_r=Rrad/H gives
  n_r>=2P>=1, q_r>=n_r^2,
  dot P=(q_r-n_r^2)+(n_r-1)(n_r-2P)>=0.                      (2.1)
The finite inward limit P_0>=1/2 follows. All kernel operations are
legal by compact C2 approximation, including moving centers.

For a local normalized tuple on B10, suppose zero is a good center
with H(0,1)=1 and 1/2<=P(0,s)<=N for 0<s<8. There is a constant
K=K(n,p,N) bounding nu(B7)+d(B7), hence the total variation of all
M components there. To see this without a separate energy hypothesis,
  dot P=q_r-(1+2P)n_r+2P>=q_r/2-(1+2N)^2/2.
Integrating on [1,7.8] bounds integral q_r dlog r by
  2N+(1+2N)^2 log(7.8).
Dot log H=2n_r<=2sqrt(q_r) bounds H(0,7.8) in terms of H(0,1)=1.
The inner positive height kernel bounds nu(B7). On an interval strictly
inside (7,7.8), integral q gives a radius with bounded n_r; the inner
positive phi kernel and D=n_r H bound d(B7). PSD then bounds beta
componentwise by d and |f|(B7)<=sqrt(nu(B7)d(B7)). No atom-free or
strong beta convergence premise is used.

For (1.3), translate x to zero, dilate by r, and normalize by
  J=r^n H(x,r),
  nu'=J^{-1}T#nu, f'=rJ^{-1}T#f,
  (beta',d',mu')=r^2J^{-1}T#(beta,d,mu).
The exact congruence and PDE/sign invariance are those of the frozen
qualitative theorem. Physical balls B_{10r}(x) lie inside B4, and all
needed physical radii are less than 2. The normalized mass bound K
applies uniformly. Henceforth this normalized coordinate system is used.

3. COMMON-CUTOFF RADIAL DEFECT

Choose one fixed smooth radial 0<=zeta<=1, equal to one on B2,
supported in B3, and strictly positive in B3. For z in Z intersect B1
put k_z=2P(z,5) in [1,2N]. Define
  E_z=integral zeta(y) (-k_z,y-z)^t dM(y)(-k_z,y-z).
Then
  E_z<=C_1(n,p,N) [P(z,6)-P(z,5)].                           (3.1)

Indeed, at any radius s in [5,6], set k_s=2P(z,s). The full-kernel
quadratic form is
  Q_s(k_s)=k_s^2 H-2k_s D+Rrad
           =H[(q_r-n_r^2)+(n_r-k_s)^2]<=H dot P,             (3.2)
because n_r-1>=n_r-k_s when k_s>=1. Let w=P(z,6)-P(z,5),
0<=w<=N. The coefficient difference a_s=k_s-k_z satisfies 0<=a_s<=2w,
and exact expansion gives
  Q_s(k_z)=Q_s(k_s)+2a_s H(n_r-k_s)+a_s^2 H.
The local mass bound makes H(z,s)<=K_H on [5,6]. With L=log(6/5),
  integral_5^6 q_r dlog s<=2w+(1+2N)^2 L,
  integral_5^6 n_r dlog s<=sqrt(L[2N+(1+2N)^2 L]).
It follows that
  integral_5^6 Q_s(k_z)dlog s<=C(n,p,N) w.
For y in B3, z in B1, s in [5,6],
  psi_{z,s}(y)>=2p 6^{-n}(9/25)^{p-1}>0.
Thus each Q_s(k_z) dominates this constant times E_z. Integration
over the fixed positive log interval proves (3.1).

This is a centered DEFICIT estimate. Ordinary uncentered covariance
bounds would give a fixed additive floor and could not charge the
plane error to the frequency drop.

4. THE PDE COERCIVITY LEMMA

Within the normalized mass class of section 2 there is c_*>0, depending
only on n,p,N, such that for EVERY a in R^n, b in [1,2N], q in Bbar_1,

  E(b,q)+J(a)>=c_*,                                         (4.1)
  E(b,q)=integral zeta (-b,y-q)^t dM(-b,y-q),
  J(a)=integral zeta[dd+2a.df+|a|^2 dnu].

Both terms are nonnegative. The second is the sum of PSD quadratic
forms on (a_i,e_i), i=1,...,n. It is the trace of the sheared stress
  beta_a=beta+a f^t+f a^t+aa^t nu>=0.

Proof by compactness has NO missing integrability premise. Put
v=integral zeta dnu>=1/(2p), e=integral zeta dd<=K, and
F=integral zeta df, |F|<=sqrt(ve). Then
  J(a)>= (sqrt(v)|a|-sqrt(e))^2.
Any sequence with E+J->0 therefore has bounded a, and b,q also range
in compact sets. The normalized M tuples have weak-* subsequences on
B4, with the exact gradient/divergence PDEs and H(0,1)=1 preserved.
The compact zeta integrals pass. If J(a)=0 in the limit, the PSD
density annihilates every (a_i,e_i) on the open set zeta>0. Its rows give
  f=-a nu, beta=aa^t nu, d=|a|^2 nu there.
But grad nu=2f and div f=d give simultaneously
  div f=2|a|^2 nu and div f=|a|^2 nu.
Since nu has positive mass in B1, a=0. Then f=beta=d=0 on zeta>0,
and E(b,q)=b^2 integral zeta dnu>=1/(2p), contradicting E=0.
This proves the strictly positive uniform infimum (4.1).

The coercivity is non-effective as written: it gives an existing
positive constant by compactness. There is no assertion of a computable
numeric c_* or constructive extraction from arbitrary measure input.
It is nevertheless a uniform mathematical constant on the exact class.

5. COVARIANCE REGRESSION: ARBITRARY CENTER MEASURES

Let gamma be a probability measure on Z intersect B1. Set
  qbar=integral z dgamma, bbar=integral k_z dgamma,
  C_z=integral (z-qbar)(z-qbar)^t dgamma,
  lambda=min eigenvalue of C_z.
If lambda=0 the center measure lies on an affine (n-1)-plane and the
desired estimate is automatic. Otherwise define
  a=C_z^{-1} integral (z-qbar)(k_z-bbar) dgamma.
Regress k_z-bbar on z-qbar; the remaining scalar variance t>=0.
An exact expansion of the averaged PSD defect gives

  integral E_z dgamma
    =E(bbar,qbar)+integral zeta[tr(C_z beta_a)+t dnu]
    >=E(bbar,qbar)+lambda J(a)
    >=lambda [E(bbar,qbar)+J(a)]>=c_* lambda.                 (5.1)

The penultimate inequality uses 0<lambda<=1, since gamma is carried by
B1. This calculation handles varying center frequencies: it does not
assume k_z constant or replace their covariance by a false independent
variance bound. Signs in beta_a are PLUS because both centered entries
of (-k_z,y-z) have minus signs.

Combine (5.1) with (3.1). The smallest covariance eigenvalue is the
least squared-distance error to an affine (n-1)-plane. For a finite
nonzero gamma divide by its mass and then multiply back. For mass zero
the assertion is trivial. Rescaling to the physical ball yields (1.3).
The proof imports no harmonic-map representation, no center measure
density, and no preceding Hausdorff-finiteness conclusion.

6. EXACT STANDARD IMPORTS FOR THE CRITICAL CONSEQUENCE

DR: the published discrete Rectifiable-Reifenberg theorem of Naber and
Valtorta, Annals of Mathematics 185 (2017), Theorem 3.4. In the version
used here, let disjoint balls B_a(y) lie in B2 and put
gamma=omega_k a^k sum delta_y. There are delta_DR(n)>0,D_DR(n)<infinity
such that the local Carleson condition
  integral_{B_t(q)} integral_0^t beta_gamma,2^k(u,s)^2 ds/s dgamma(u)
                       <=delta_DR^2 t^k
on EVERY interior ball implies gamma(B1)<=D_DR. Scaling is allowed.
The full beta rather than the optional small-mass cutoff is used; this
is a stronger hypothesis and is allowed by the source's Remark 3.2.

AT: the published Azzam--Tolsa square-function theorem, GAFA 25 (2015),
Corollary 1.3: a finite-H^k measurable set is k-rectifiable if the Jones
square function for its restricted H^k measure is finite almost
everywhere. The positive finite upper density hypothesis of their
Theorem 1.1 is supplied by finite H^k restriction; it is not discarded.

Primary URLs and exact versions are pinned in the companion ledger.
Only these general Euclidean measure theorems are imported. The
harmonic-map-specific best approximation theorems are NOT imported.

7. SMALL-TAIL PACKING: NO DENSITY CIRCULARITY

Let S obey (1.4) at radius R<=1/1024. Fix a finite 10a-separated set
F subset S intersect B_R(q), 0<a<R/10, and put
  gamma=omega_k a^k sum_{y in F} delta_y.
The a-balls are disjoint. We prove gamma(B_s(q'))<=A s^k for ALL
centers q' and all a<=s<=R (indeed to the first dyadic scale above R).
Choose A>=max(omega_k,2^k D_DR,1). At s between a and 2a, separation
makes every such ball contain at most one atom, so this density bound
holds. For s<a, the beta integral at an atomic center is zero; no atomic
upper density bound at those radii is asserted or needed.

Induct upward through t_i=2^i a. Suppose the density bound is known
for all a<=s<=t_i/2. Euclidean covering alone then supplies the weaker
bound with A_w=33^n A on every ball with a<=s<=4t_i. For s<=t_i/2
use the induction hypothesis; otherwise cover by a bounded number of
t_i/2-balls. This weaker bound uses only already proved scales.

Restrict gamma to centers in B_{3t_i/2}(q'), obtaining gamma'. Its
a-balls lie in B_{2t_i}(q'). For ANY ball B_t(v) inside this latter
domain, t<=2t_i, apply (1.3) at the inner integration centers u in F,
then swap the nonnegative integrals. Beta is zero at s<a. For s>=a,
the weak density bound gives

  integral_{B_t(v)} integral_0^t beta_gamma',2^k(u,s)^2 ds/s dgamma'(u)
   <=B A_w integral_{B_{2t}(v)} integral_0^t W(y,s) ds/s dgamma'(y).

The exact logarithmic telescope is
  integral_0^t [P(y,6s)-P(y,5s)] ds/s
    =integral_{5t}^{6t}[P(y,u)-P_0(y)] du/u
    <=log(6/5) eta_*.
We only reach t_i<=2R, so 6t<=24R<64R; (1.4) therefore applies.
Using the weak density bound again gives local Carleson norm
  <=B A_w^2 2^k log(6/5) eta_* t^k.
Choose once and for all
  eta_* <=delta_DR^2/[2B A_w^2 2^k log(6/5)].
DR, scaled on B_{2t_i}(q'), now gives gamma(B_{t_i}(q'))<=D_DR t_i^k.
Dyadic interpolation bounds the whole new interval by 2^k D_DR s^k<=A s^k.
This completes the upward induction without presuming the density being
proved. Balls of radii below a have zero beta, and boundary support is
handled by restricting centers to B_{3t_i/2}; no a-ball crosses the
Reifenberg domain boundary.

At the first dyadic t_i>=R, t_i<=2R, all F is in B_{t_i}(q), so
  #F omega_k a^k<=D_DR (2R)^k.
Take a maximal 10a-separated finite net of S intersect B_R(q). It
covers that set by 10a-balls and has the displayed uniform critical
cardinality bound. Such nets are finite by bounded Euclidean packing,
so this does not assume S compact or already finite-H^k. Let a decrease
to zero. This proves (1.5) and the finite H^k bound. If a>=R/10, the
same covering conclusion follows directly from Euclidean geometry.

The local small-tail packing bound also holds on every smaller ball:
(1.4) at R implies it at each smaller radius. Thus restricted H^k
measure has a uniform k-dimensional upper ball bound below that R.

8. COUNTABLE TAIL DECOMPOSITION AND RECTIFIABILITY

P(x,r) is continuous in x on a common interior region, and P_0 is the
infimum along a countable decreasing radius family, hence is Borel.
So (1.6) is Borel. Monotonicity makes Z_j increasing and their union
Z, since P(x,r)->P_0(x) for each center. Cover B1 by finitely many
R_j-balls and use section 7 for S=Z_j. Then
  H^k(Z_j)<=C R_j^{k-n}=C/R_j<infinity.
This proves sigma-finiteness without assuming it in (1.3) or the
Reifenberg bootstrap.

Let gamma_j=H^k|Z_j. Its upper ball bound is uniform for radii <=R_j,
as section 7 may be applied on every smaller ball. Fubini and (1.3)
give
  integral gamma_j(dx) integral_0^{R_j} beta_gamma_j,2^k(x,r)^2 dr/r
    <=B A_* integral gamma_j(dy) integral_0^{R_j} W(y,r) dr/r
    <=B A_* log(6/5) eta_* gamma_j(R^n)<infinity.
The remaining radii [R_j,1] have finite integral by finite total mass
and the fixed positive lower radius. Hence the Jones square function
is finite gamma_j-almost everywhere. Apply AT to the already finite
H^k set Z_j. Each is countably k-rectifiable; their countable union
proves (1.7). H^k-null exceptions are retained in the usual definition
of rectifiability, not silently discarded as pointwise geometry.

9. DIMENSION ONE AND EXACT CONTROLS

For n=1, nu''=2d>=0 in distributions implies nu is represented by a
continuous convex function on the interval B4. At every good center,
dot H/H>=2 makes H(x,r)->0, so the approximate-identity kernel gives
nu(x)=0. Two distinct zeroes of a nonnegative convex function would
make it vanish on their intervening open interval. The already proved
positive-height continuation lemma forbids this for nonzero nu on
connected B4. Thus Z has at most one point.

Sharp flat control in n>=2:
  nu=y_n^2 dy, f=y_n e_n dy, beta=e_n e_n^t dy,
  d=dy, mu=dy/2, Z=B1 intersect {y_n=0}.
All assumptions hold and P=1/2 at every good center and every radius.
All frequency drops vanish. Every finite center measure lies exactly
on that hyperplane, so (1.3) has zero on both sides. This verifies the
critical case and shows no codimension-two packing bound is possible
for the whole good-center set. One-sided square profiles remain failed
controls because their stress/sign premises are inconsistent.

10. ADDITIONAL PROVED HIGH-FREQUENCY SPLITTING; STRONGER GATE

For any 0<rho,gamma<1 there is eta_0>0, depending on n,p,N,C,rho,gamma,
with the following local alternative (all balls have common interior
room, and all stated physical radii are <2). Suppose
  sup_{z in Z intersect B_{3r}(x)} P(z,3r)<=E.
For eta<=eta_0, either every z in Z intersect B_r(x) satisfies
P(z,gamma r)>=E-gamma, or
  {z in Z intersect B_r(x):P(z,3eta r)>=E-eta}
is contained in a rho r-neighborhood of an affine (n-2)-plane.     (10.1)
For E<=1/2+gamma the first alternative is immediate. If the high set
is empty the second is automatic.

Proof of the remaining case is by normalized compactness, not harmonic
representation. If (10.1) fails as eta down to zero, choose n high
centers which are quantitatively affine independent in n-1 directions.
Translate to the first, normalize a kernel at radius r/2, and dilate
by r. Uniform frequency bounds give local tuple compactness. The high
center frequencies lie between E-eta and E on EVERY fixed interval
between 3eta and 3; in the limit each is constant E. Its degree
n_0=2E>1. PSD equality therefore annihilates (-n_0,y-z_i) on each
ball B3(z_i); all contain a common ball around the first center.
Subtract those full matrix identities. The n-1 independent center
differences force f to be normal to their affine plane and beta to have
only the corresponding normal-normal entry d.

Let t be that normal coordinate. Grad nu=2f and the zero-center radial
identity t f_n=n_0 nu give the local profiles
  nu=c_+ t^{2n_0}dy (t>0), nu=c_- |t|^{2n_0}dy (t<0),
with no mass on t=0. Tangential constancy is distributional; the normal
ODE also rules out a plane atom. At least one coefficient is positive
by normalized positive height in the common ball. Div f=d gives
  d=n_0(2n_0-1)c_+/- |t|^{2n_0-2}dy.
The stress equation with rank-one beta forces mu-d to be a constant
Lebesgue density. Because n_0>1, d tends to zero at t=0. Domination
|mu|<=C d forces that constant to vanish. Thus mu=d, and 2mu<=d
forces d=0, contradicting positive height. This rules out the selected
simplex and proves (10.1). No same-frequency assumption beyond the
explicit high-set pinching to E is made.

This sharper alternative, together with (1.3), matches the usual
critical quantitative-stratification framework. Nick Edelen's primary
notes on the measure-theoretic Naber--Valtorta theorem were inspected
as a route guide. Their packing/rectifiability construction uses center
measures supported on the singular set, but their displayed beta
assumption is formally phrased for ANY measure. Our proved (1.3) is
ONLY for measures supported on Z. An explicit restriction of that
framework to its actually used center measures, plus a checked
stopping-cover implementation, is the remaining gate before asserting
ONE whole-Z critical Minkowski constant from that framework. The notes
also display a polynomial-looking Lambda bound while their final
iteration gives an exponential-in-Lambda bound; no polynomial frequency
dependence is imported here.

Accordingly, the main freeze claims the fully proved sigma-finite
critical measure, countable rectifiability, arbitrary center-measure
L2 estimate, and uniform packing of each small-tail piece. It does
not silently upgrade those conclusions to globally uniform critical
packing or locally finite measure for all Z. These stronger claims
remain a specific general-cover gate, not a counterexample or a
generic unexamined harmonic-map invocation.

11. COROLLARY FOR THE ORIGINAL ABSTRACT GOOD-CENTER SET

The sigma-finite/rectifiable conclusion also follows under the frozen
qualitative theorem's original premises: on a connected open Omega,
each good center has its own sufficiently-small-radius comparison
2P>=1, and |mu|<=C_{Omega'}d locally. First use its proved positive-
height continuation lemma, so P(x,r) is continuous on common compact
interior center/radius sets. Restrict the Borel center set countably to
common comparison cutoffs and compact interior room. On a sufficiently
small base ball of radius a, comparison holds at all center radii
0<r<2a. The finite maximum of P(x,2a) over the compact center region,
together with good-center monotonicity, supplies one finite upper N on
that piece. Choose 2a strictly below the common cutoff. After translation
and scaling the tuple is in the form of section 1. Countably many such
balls cover the piece, and countably many pieces cover the original
good-center set. Apply (1.7) on each.

Thus the original exact abstract measure proposition admits the
stronger conclusion: its Borel good-center set is sigma-finite in
H^{n-1} and countably (n-1)-rectifiable. This uses no critical ambient
Frostman acquisition and does not cure the source06b dt/t divergence
at the critical center dimension. It is solely an exact-tuple corollary.

Exact original
