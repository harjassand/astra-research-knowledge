## Recent operator-algebra comparator

Houdayer–Marrakchi, *The classification of flows on II1 factors and Connes bicentralizer problem*, arXiv 2609.11462v1, 10 September 2026, is the explicit primary comparator for release family 290. I substantively read its introduction TheoremG, process disclosure, and the complete final Section 8.4 argument, Propositions 8.8–8.10 and Theorem 8.11, PDF pages 110–112. I did not read or verify all 115 pages of prerequisites.

That final mechanism introduces averaged left/right isometries, a reflected spectral action and a tensor flip. A spectral phase Ω intertwines left and right action; its joint spectrum lies in a symmetric bilinear resonance condition. Classification of the resulting closed additive subgroup forces a discrete lattice or axis obstruction and contradicts full modular spectrum. The release gives an alternative route through bounded recovery and singular KMS reasoning, rather than claiming that no primary proof was previously available.

The revised primary v2 is dated 5 October 2026,150 PDF pages. I read its introduction TheoremC and the Section 9 introduction/singular-KMS/Plancherel excerpts on pages 137–141. The official version qualification says v1 did not complete classification of the full spectrum of nonouter flows and v2 completes that classification. This is not a statement that the bicentralizer claim was withdrawn. Nor is our final-section reconstruction an audit of every150-page prerequisite.

Consequential scope: family 290's alternative proof and finite recovery mechanism may be useful tools. Neither source existence nor our excerpt comparison establishes a newly solved open bicentralizer problem, priority, or an acquired computation over arbitrary typeIII factors.

## Older bounded-recovery prerequisites

Haagerup–Musat, *On the best constants in noncommutative Khintchine-type inequalities*, arXivmath/0611160v3, dated 23 May 2007. Read: pp 5–8, Eq 2.10 and the complete clipping Lemma 2.6 proof, Eqs2.15–2.20; p10 Remark 2.8 on Steinhaus/lacunary phases. Other30-page prerequisites were not fully read.

This primary already has the exact two-pairing noncommutative fourth-moment expansion, polar cutoff and the sharp scalar clipping inequality. In particular (r−K)_+²≤r⁴/(16K²) is prior, and its use improves the release's loose witness cutoff by a factor four. The parabolic Sidon phase family in our quadratic compiler is a standard finite character construction. A previous exact formulation of this particular finite-certificate compiler has not been ruled out.

The strongest cheap randomized control uses independent uniform fourth roots with 2m random bits, the same moment identities and the same success probability at a paid clip/filter/score call. Our O(m²) finite family reduces deterministic exhaustive support and attains its universal exact-moment support order. It creates no new randomized runtime class or modular certificate-acquisition theorem.

## Older paving comparator and operational target

Ravichandran–Srivastava, *Asymptotically Optimal Multi-Paving*, arXiv 1706.03737v2, dated 23 August 2017. Read: introduction pp 1–2, conditional nonuniform-law machinery and complete Theorem 25/main-Theorem 1/Corollary26 proofs on pp 16–18. Remaining interlacing prerequisites were not fully read.

The primary already gives quadratic finite matrix multi-paving by mixed determinantal polynomials. The release's arbitrary-MASA extension uses that combinatorial input. The revised primary removes an erroneous earlier polylogarithmic commutator claim; its main paving result remains. We do not import the erroneous claim.

For the weaker weighted-action target under an A-central normal state, the equally informed elementary control is independent coloring of a fine A partition. Orthogonal matrix blocks give an exact quadratic-color second moment. Thus an application using only small weighted action must compare with this cheaper mechanism. The release's stronger target is fixed-palette compressed operator norm in arbitrary strong neighborhoods and arbitrary MASAs; our compression/action counterexample does not settle or refute that theorem.
