SEPARATE LOSING-EVENT REFINEMENT: CONDITIONAL SCALAR WRAPPER
==========================================================

0. Provenance and exact added premise
------------------------------------
This addendum is written after the immutable independent H_B baseline
INDEPENDENT_H_B_THRESHOLD_PROOF.txt, SHA256
e2e346f5da05acaf90aedee56ce94b605b0677d9470ac8cf20946637941f67df.
The parent supplied the new inequality below at theorem level. No Luna wrapper
body or quantum producer of this refinement has been read. Its validity for
quantum strategies remains a conditional named input, not a conclusion here.

Use the exact game, binary-score, product-question, commuting-strategy and
conditioning definitions in the independent baseline. Let epsilon=1-v. For
every repeated length n, every legal strategy, and every proper C with p_C>0,
set

  alpha_C=1-q_C in [0,1],
  tau_C=[ln(1/p_C)+|C|ell]/(n-|C|).

The additional explicit conditional premise, with one universal K>=0, is

  (R_K) epsilon <=alpha_C
       +K[sqrt(alpha_C)*tau_C^(1/4)+sqrt(tau_C)].       (0.1)

Crucially this is required for all strategies and all lengths, including the
legal tensor-product amplifiers. Put K_0=max(1,K); replacing K by K_0 only
weakens the premise. If the eventual quantum producer gives a different cost
than tau_C above, the present wrapper cannot be invoked without checking that
cost comparison and all its quantifiers. No source claim is used to supply it.

1. Exact all-n conclusion and all-win consequence
------------------------------------------------
Under R_K alone, for all n>=1 and 0<delta<=epsilon,

  Pr[W>=ceil((v+delta)n)]
  <=exp[-delta^5*n/(16384 K_0^4 epsilon^2(delta+ell))]. (1.1)

The allowed range has epsilon>0. For epsilon=0 there are no positive deltas.
At delta=epsilon, the threshold is n and (1.1) gives

  Pr[W=n]<=exp[-epsilon^3*n/
                       (16384 K_0^4(epsilon+ell))].   (1.2)

Thus the improvement from the H_B all-win fifth power to the third power is
conditional on the genuinely stronger losing-event input. H_B alone is not
being upgraded. No optimality claim is made for either exponent or constant.

2. Averaging preserves the nonlinear losing-event premise
---------------------------------------------------------
Let p_j and w_C be the exact success moments and successful-subset weights
from baseline Section 3, for any j<n with p_j>0. The counting identity gives

  q_bar=sum_C w_C q_C=p_{j+1}/p_j,
  alpha_bar=sum_C w_C alpha_C=1-q_bar.

The entropy identity gives

  sum_C w_C tau_C
   <= [ln(1/p_j)+j ell]/(n-j)=t_j.                    (2.1)

Averaging (0.1) and using Cauchy--Schwarz followed by Jensen,

  sum_C w_C sqrt(alpha_C) tau_C^(1/4)
   <=sqrt(sum_C w_C alpha_C)
                         *sqrt(sum_C w_C tau_C^(1/2))
   <=sqrt(alpha_bar)*(sum_C w_C tau_C)^(1/4),

and sum_C w_C sqrt(tau_C)<=(sum_C w_C tau_C)^(1/2).
Both terms are monotone in this averaged cost, so

  epsilon <=alpha_bar
       +K_0[sqrt(alpha_bar)*t_j^(1/4)+sqrt(t_j)].       (2.2)

No independence, bounded-posterior assumption, or Jensen inequality in the
wrong joint variables is used. The two successive scalar inequalities explicitly
handle the correlation between alpha_C and tau_C in the weighted index set.
Zero-weight events p_C=0 are omitted as in the baseline.

3. A barrier budget of order delta^4/epsilon^2
---------------------------------------------
Choose

  a=delta^4/(256 K_0^4 epsilon^2),
  s=v+3delta/4,
  L=ell+ln(1/s)+a.                                    (3.1)

Since delta<=epsilon<=1, 0<a<=1/256. Whenever t_j<=a, (2.2) implies

  q_bar<=v+delta/2.                                   (3.2)

To prove it, suppose instead q_bar>v+delta/2. Then alpha_bar<epsilon-delta/2,
so alpha_bar<=epsilon and epsilon-alpha_bar>delta/2. However

  K_0 sqrt(alpha_bar) t_j^(1/4)
       <=K_0 sqrt(epsilon) a^(1/4)=delta/4,
  K_0 sqrt(t_j)<=K_0 sqrt(a)=delta^2/(16 K_0 epsilon)
       <=delta/16.

Their sum is at most 5delta/16, contradicting (2.2). This uses only the claimed
losing-event premise, not H_B. Notice that the epsilon denominator is legitimate
only because the gap delta is restricted to delta<=epsilon.

4. Scalar threshold argument for d>=2
------------------------------------
For n>=8L/a put k=floor(a n/(4L)). Then k>=a n/(8L)>=1. Also

  k/n<=a/(4ell)<=delta^4/(1024 K_0^4 epsilon^2 ell)
       <=delta/4,

because delta^4/epsilon^2<=delta^2<=delta and ell>=ln2>1/2. The tail probability
P=Pr(W>=ceil((v+delta)n)), if positive, obeys

  p_j>=P s^j>0 for every j<=k.                         (4.1)

This is the exact sampling-without-replacement inequality in baseline (4.3),
using only W's integer values and k<=delta n/4.

An index j is good if ln(1/p_j)+j ell<=a(n-j). Then t_j<=a and (3.2) gives
p_{j+1}/p_j<=v+delta/2. If some j<=k is not good, (4.1) gives

  ln(1/P)>a n-jL>=3a n/4.                             (4.2)

If every j<=k is good, multiplying the ratio bound and using (4.1) gives

  ln(1/P)>=k ln[s/(v+delta/2)]
           >=k delta/(4s)>=a delta n/(32sL).          (4.3)

Since a<=1/256, the same uniform denominator estimate is even stronger:

  sL<=ell+1/e+a<=2ell<=2(delta+ell).                   (4.4)

Therefore (4.3) gives

  ln(1/P)>=a delta n/[64(delta+ell)]
          =delta^5 n/[16384 K_0^4 epsilon^2(delta+ell)].

Case (4.2) is stronger. This establishes (1.1) at all sufficiently large lengths,
with a cutoff depending only on v, delta, ell, K_0, not on the strategy.

5. Small n, singleton outputs, zero events and exact endpoints
--------------------------------------------------------------
The legal product-block construction of baseline Section 6 applies unchanged:
tensor m complete copies of the original n-copy strategy on the product state,
use tensor-product Alice and Bob POVMs, and retain the same base v, epsilon,
ell, and K_0. The output alphabet per coordinate is unchanged. Alice and Bob
remain commuting. Independent constructed blocks have probability P^m to all
cross the original threshold, and on that event total wins are at least
m ceil((v+delta)n)>=ceil((v+delta)mn). Applying Section 4 for sufficiently large
mn and taking the m-th root gives (1.1) for the original n without any additive
prefactor, delta loss, or original-coordinate independence assumption.

For d=1, singleton outputs and product questions give, exactly as in baseline
Section 5, Pr(tail)<=exp(-2delta^2 n). Since delta<=epsilon,

  delta^4/epsilon^2<=delta^2,

this is stronger than (1.1), whose exponent at ell=0 is
-delta^4 n/(16384 K_0^4 epsilon^2). The same uniqueness-of-answers proof rather
than an unsupported generic ell=0 scalar inference is used.

At C=empty one has tau_C=0. Thus (0.1) gives epsilon<=1-EW/n, equivalently
EW/n<=v. If v=0 then W=0 almost surely and every stated positive threshold has
probability zero. If P=0 there is nothing to prove. A positive tail forces all
success moments actually used to be positive; no undefined conditioning occurs.
At delta=epsilon the integer threshold is exactly n and all formulas, including
the product amplification, hold literally.
