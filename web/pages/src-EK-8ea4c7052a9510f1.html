# Stage I B3 — input acquisition can dominate the solver

**Status boundary.** The source algorithm and the selected OpenAI/math Lean formalization retain their recorded scopes. The one-edge lower bound below is a derivation for an explicit rate-acquisition interface. It does not refute either source result or certify the N719 manuscript.

## 1. North Star

Turn a result that is efficient on a supplied mathematical representation into an end-to-end capability from observations. The system should acquire the task-relevant inputs at a charged cost or report that its answer is not robust to unresolved inputs. The immediate target is rare-event risk estimation, where a long deadline makes an $O(1/T)$ rate error change the log-risk by order one.

## 2. Exact question

For an algorithm that computes $g(\theta)$ in time polynomial in the bit length of an exactly supplied rational $\theta$, when can $g(\theta)$ be estimated with the same resource order if $\theta$ must instead be inferred from finite observations? The test case is the N719 output $g(\lambda,T)=\log\Pr(X\ge T)$ for one exponential activity. Is polynomial bit complexity in the supplied rate and deadline enough for polynomial end-to-end sample cost when the rate is unknown?

## 3. Importance

This isolates an operational gap that a faster counting or integration routine cannot close: parameter acquisition can be exponentially more expensive than computation performed after the parameter is supplied. It also gives a concrete way to evaluate a research system's effective capability per inference expenditure: charge the oracle, calibration, resets, precision, and checking that its headline result leaves outside the theorem.

The same boundary appears in OpenAI/math result 139. Its subpolynomial query theorem is meaningful in its stated exact-oracle model, but the input representation is an exact value-and-gradient oracle and a known minimizer. The paper explicitly leaves real arithmetic and bit cost unrestricted. This suggests a common candidate mechanism—supplied information versus acquired information—but does not establish a transfer between sampling and physical rate estimation.

## 4. Obstruction and fresh derivation

Take a one-edge source-to-target DAG with one activity $X\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$. Its deadline-overrun probability is exactly

$$
U_\lambda(T)=e^{-\lambda T},\qquad \log U_\lambda(T)=-\lambda T.
$$

For an integer $b\ge1$, let $h=2^{-b}$, $T=2^b=1/h$, and compare rates $\lambda_0=1$ and $\lambda_1=1+h$. Both rates and the deadline have $O(b)$-bit binary descriptions, but

$$
|\log U_{\lambda_1}(T)-\log U_{\lambda_0}(T)|=hT=1.
$$

Suppose the rate is acquired from $N$ independent observed durations. For one observation,

$$
D(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\|\mathrm{Exp}(1+h))
=h-\log(1+h)\le h^2/2\qquad(0<h\le1),
$$

so the $N$-sample KL divergence is at most $Nh^2/2$. Any estimator that returns the log-tail within $1/4$ with probability at least $2/3$ under both hypotheses induces a constant-error test, since the two correct-output intervals are disjoint. Such a test requires total variation at least $1/3$; Pinsker then requires KL at least $2/9$. Therefore

$$
N\ge \frac{4}{9h^2}=\frac49\,4^b.
$$

This lower bound counts only independent observations; resets, physical time, rate calibration, and readout costs can only add to the acquisition bill. It already holds for one activity, so graph search is not its cause. An algorithm given the exact rational rate can compute this example's log-tail directly in polynomial bit time. The mismatch is that the source theorem supplies the rate while the observation-driven system must estimate it to order $1/T$.

This is a fresh reconstruction of the two-point calculation, not a claim that the standard KL/Pinsker method is new. Its consequence is specific: for binary deadlines $T=2^b$, exact-input polynomial-bit risk computation does not imply a polynomial-in-$b$ sample-driven risk estimator under this observation model. It suggests a sensitivity-to-information lower-bound route for larger networks, with $T|\partial_\lambda\log U|$ replaced by the task's full parameter sensitivity and the observation channel's Fisher/KL information.

## 5. Missing capability

The missing service is an acquisition contract for rates: a specified observation/reset interface, a finite-sample estimator or uncertainty set, and a guarantee at the precision demanded by the requested risk tolerance. For the one-edge case, estimating the log-tail to constant additive error requires the rate to $\Theta(1/T)$ accuracy, which costs $\Omega(T^2)$ samples near $\lambda=1$. A useful escape must change the interface or promise—for example, a direct calibrated-rate measurement, a lower bound on rate separation, or a robust decision that remains valid over the posterior or uncertainty interval. It must report those costs explicitly.

For result 139, the analogous missing bridge would be a finite-precision/noisy-oracle realization with charged evaluation and arithmetic costs. Its exact-oracle query result alone does not provide such a conversion.
