# Stopped clocks, endpoint conditioning, and information needed for tail risk

15 October 2026. Independent derivation from the supplied pasted clock report. The producer ZIP, original LJ38 files, original scripts, and its exact certificates were not available to this worker. No producer numerical result is replayed or certified here. The mathematics below is conditional on a supplied finite generator. External correctness and historical originality remain UNKNOWN; no physical validation or superiority is claimed.

## 1. Domain and notation

Use row generators. Let `E=A union B` be a finite state space. Stop the original chain on first hitting the target set `B`, and replace the generator rows on `B` by zero. This stopping convention does not change any pre-target observable. Assume every transient state reaches `B`; finite rates and finite state space then give an exponentially decaying absorption tail and all finite moments. Let `T` be the hitting time, and let the initial row distribution `mu` be supported on a mandatory retained set `R` containing `B`. Initial mass already in `B` contributes a zero passage time and zero stopped reward.

Write `I=E\R`, `S=R\B`, `G=(-Q_II)^(-1)`, `m=G 1`,

    K=Q_RR+Q_RI G Q_IR,
    alpha=1+Q_RI m,
    Qhat=diag(alpha)^(-1) K.

The inverse `G` is a nonnegative killed-chain Green matrix; it exists since `I` cannot contain a closed communicating class. Its diagonal entries are strictly positive. Inverses and products are interpreted as empty blocks when `I` is empty. Let `N=(-Q_AA)^(-1)` and `eta_A=mu_A N`, extending `eta` by zero on `B`. This is the original occupation measure and is fixed when `R` changes.

These are supplied-model quantities. Their calculation does not acquire missing pathways, rates, state observables, a valid Markov model, or physical control response.

## 2. Independent proof of the stopped-clock identity

Delete discarded-state excursions from a sample path. The retained trace, indexed by retained local time, has generator `K`. This follows either from the strong Markov property at each return to `R`, or from the harmonic hitting matrix `G Q_IR`; instantaneous excursions can return to the same state and merely thin the trace jump rate. Stretch each unit of retained local time at `r` by `alpha_r`. The resulting CTMC has generator `Qhat`, and its stopped passage time on this coupling is

    That = integral_0^T 1_R(X_t) alpha(X_t) dt.

Extend `h=m` on `I` and `h=0` on `R`. Then `(Qh)_I=-1` and `(Qh)_R=alpha-1`. For the stopped Dynkin martingale

    M_t=h(X_(t wedge T))-h(X_0)-integral_0^(t wedge T) Qh(X_s) ds,

both endpoint terms vanish, so `M_T=T-That`. Finite-state absorption supplies square integrability and passage to the stopped limit. In particular `E[M_T]=0`.

Its predictable quadratic variation has rate

    Gamma(h)=Q(h^2)-2 h Qh.

Stopped Dynkin applied to `h^2`, again with zero endpoint values, gives `E integral Q(h^2)=0`. Consequently

    E[(T-That)^2] = -2 sum_i eta_i h_i (Qh)_i
                  = 2 sum_(i in I) eta_i m_i =: V(R).

Thus `E[T]=E[That]` and `W2(Law(T),Law(That)) <= sqrt(V(R))`. This is a particular coupling cost; equality with the optimal Wasserstein coupling is not asserted. No detailed balance, diagonalization, spectral gap, or absence of cycles is used.

The original retained occupation row is `eta_S=mu_S(-K_SS)^(-1)` and `eta_I=eta_S Q_SI G`. With `m2=2 Gm`, this also proves

    V(R)=mu_S(-K_SS)^(-1) Q_SI m2.

## 3. Exact marginal and supermodularity

Retain a discarded state `j`. The killed inverse on the remaining discarded states is the Schur update

    Gnew_ik=G_ik-G_ij G_jk/G_jj    (i,k != j).

The corresponding return-time vector, extended by zero at `j`, satisfies

    mnew_i=m_i-(G_ij/G_jj) m_j    (all i in I).

This is also the strong Markov decomposition of the old return time at the first visit to `j`. Multiplying by the fixed original occupation measure gives

    V(R)-V(R union {j}) = (2 m_j/G_jj) sum_(i in I) eta_i G_ij
                       = 2 m_j (G^T eta_I)_j/G_jj.

The expression is nonnegative, including unreachable states where the occupation weights vanish.

For completeness, supermodularity is not inferred only from the formula. On an independently started original sample path at `i`, write `tau_R` for first hitting `R`, including `tau_R=0` if `i in R`. For nested retained sets `R subset U` and a new state `j notin U`, the pathwise decrease from retaining `j` is

    tau_R-tau_(R union {j}) = (tau_R-tau_j)_+.

Since `tau_R >= tau_U`, this decrease is at least `(tau_U-tau_j)_+`. Taking expectations and then the fixed nonnegative weighted sum `2 sum_i eta_i E_i[tau_R]` proves

    V(R)-V(R union {j}) >= V(U)-V(U union {j}).

Therefore `V` is decreasing and supermodular on retained sets containing the mandatory set. Its benefit relative to a fixed initial set is monotone submodular. The cardinality-greedy guarantee concerns benefit, and does not become a multiplicative guarantee on the remaining error, deadline error, or actual CDF error. Absorption-time supermodularity has explicit prior art; this proof is not a priority claim.

## 4. Signed additive observables

Let `f,g:E->R` be arbitrary fixed finite state rewards, including signed rewards. Define

    A_f=integral_0^T f(X_t) dt,
    h_I^f=G f_I,    h_R^f=0,
    b_R^f=f_R+Q_RI h_I^f,
    fhat_R=b_R^f/alpha_R.

The reduced reward on the same stretched trace is

    Ahat_f=integral_0^T 1_R(X_t) b^f(X_t) dt.

The endpoint-vanishing Dynkin martingale for `h^f` is exactly `A_f-Ahat_f`. Hence each error has mean zero. The bilinear bracket rate is

    Gamma(h^f,h^g)=Q(h^f h^g)-h^f Qh^g-h^g Qh^f.

The product has zero initial and terminal value. Its Dynkin contribution integrates to zero in expectation, while `(Qh^f)_I=-f_I`. Therefore

    E[(A_f-Ahat_f)(A_g-Ahat_g)]
      = sum_(i in I) eta_i [f_i (Gg_I)_i+g_i (Gf_I)_i].

For several rewards this matrix is positive semidefinite because it is an actual second-moment matrix of errors. Entrywise positivity is neither needed nor true for signed cross covariances. No supermodularity statement is made for general signed rewards. One must not replace a jump reward, event count, reservoir work, or terminal settlement by a state reward without separately justifying that representation.

## 5. A different operation: cause-conditioned clocks and joint deadline risk

Partition `B` into designated absorption causes. Fix a cause `C subset B` and let

    c_i=P_i(X_T in C),    p=mu c > 0.

Set `c=1` on `C` and `c=0` on `B\C`; it solves `(Qc)_A=0`. States with `c=0` cannot occur on the conditioned path and are removed. On states with positive `c`, the conditional generator is the standard Doob transform

    Q^c_ij=Q_ij c_j/c_i,

with unchanged transient diagonal and killing only into `C`. The conditioned initial distribution is `mu^c_i=mu_i c_i/p`. A direct Markov-property calculation gives the conditional occupation measure

    eta_i^c=E[integral_0^T 1_{X_t=i}dt | X_T in C] = eta_i c_i/p.

On the positive-committor support, with diagonal matrices `H_I=diag(c_I)`, `H_R=diag(c_R)`, the killed Green matrix and trace obey

    G^c=H_I^(-1) G H_I,
    K^c=H_R^(-1) K H_R.

In these two similarity identities the original matrices on the right are restricted to that positive support. When `c` vanishes elsewhere, the formulas below that contain `G c` or `G(c f)` and no division by the zero component may still use the full original blocks. A zero-committor state cannot lead to a positive-committor state, which justifies this restriction. No inverse of a diagonal matrix containing zero is intended.

Thus the trace operation commutes with endpoint conditioning. Applying the stopped-clock theorem to `Q^c` gives a different mean-clock correction,

    m_i^c=(G c_I)_i/c_i,
    alpha_r^c=1+(Q_RI G c_I)_r/c_r,
    Qhat_c=diag(alpha^c)^(-1) K^c.

The cause-conditioned reduced process, started with `mu^c`, couples to the conditioned original process with zero-mean clock error and

    V_c(R)=E_c[(T-That_c)^2]
          =(2/p) sum_(i in I) eta_i (G c_I)_i.

For signed rewards the same argument gives

    C_c(f,g)=(1/p) sum_(i in I) eta_i
       [ f_i (G(c_I g_I))_i + g_i (G(c_I f_I))_i ],

and the reduced conditional reward is

    fhat^c_r = [f_r+(Q_RI G(c_I f_I))_r/c_r]/alpha_r^c.

This is a supplied-generator operation for **time or occupation conditional on a designated endpoint**. It does not condition on a late deadline itself; the latter remains a timing-tail question. For fixed `C`, the conditional clock certificate is also decreasing and supermodular by Section 3 applied to the fixed conditioned chain.

For a partition into causes `C_l`, the certificates satisfy the exact accounting identity

    sum_l p_l V_(c_l)(R)=V(R),
    sum_l p_l C_(c_l)(f,g)=C(f,g).

Indeed `sum_l c_l=1` before absorption and the formulas are linear in `c`. One may sample the endpoint at preparation and use a separate reduced copy of the retained states for each endpoint. This mixture preserves endpoint probabilities and each endpoint-conditioned mean. It reallocates the error to the requested endpoint; it does not improve the aggregate clock coupling cost. It can require up to one retained copy per cause and is built from established conditioning and reduction machinery.

### Deadline-risk bands

Let `S_c^hat(t)=P_c(That_c>t)`. For every `e>0`, Chebyshev and the coupling give

    p max(0, S_c^hat(t+e)-V_c/e^2)
      <= P(T>t, X_T in C)
      <= p min(1, S_c^hat(t-e)+V_c/e^2).

Use `S_c^hat(s)=1` for `s<0`. The bounds do not presume independence of clock error and reduced time. A relative risk guarantee around `p S_c^hat(t)` needs

    [S_c^hat(t-e)-S_c^hat(t+e)+V_c/e^2]/S_c^hat(t)

to be small, along with a sufficiently accurate `p`. The endpoint rarity `p` cancels. A late conditional timing tail can still make the denominator arbitrarily small; no universal rare-tail solution follows.

For a joint time/exposure event `{T>d, A_f>a}`, use the reduced joint conditional reward law and tolerances `e_t,e_f`. Expanding each threshold outward gives an upper band, contracting inward gives a lower band, with additive conditional error at most

    V_c/e_t^2+C_c(f,f)/e_f^2.

Multiply the final conditional bands by `p`. More generally a halfspace in a reward vector uses directional variance `v^T C_c v`; a finite intersection uses a union bound over its halfspaces. The reduced joint distribution is still a Markov-reward/phase-type computation. The covariance alone does not compute that distribution.

## 6. Sharp incompatibility: a single clock cannot preserve all cause-conditioned means

Assume for clarity that each cause considered has positive committor at every retained transient state. Consider any common positive row clock `beta` multiplying the fixed trace `K`, so its reduced generator is `diag(beta)^(-1)K`. It preserves all endpoint committors. Conditional on cause `C`, its generator is `diag(beta)^(-1)K^c`, and its vector of conditional mean times starting at retained states solves

    (-K_SS^c) that_C=beta_S.

Eliminating discarded states in the original conditional mean equation instead gives

    (-K_SS^c) t_C=alpha_S^c.

Since the transient matrix is invertible, the two mean-time vectors agree for every retained initial state **if and only if** `beta_S=alpha_S^c`. Therefore one common row clock preserves the conditional mean for all designated causes and all retained initial states if and only if their `alpha^c` vectors coincide. When a cause is unreachable from some retained states the assertion applies on its positive-committor support; do not divide by zero.

For a complete partition into eventual causes, the ordinary correction is the committor-weighted average

    alpha_r=sum_l c_l(r) alpha_r^(c_l).

It therefore need not equal any of the cause-specific corrections. Conditioning the ordinary `Qhat` gives `diag(alpha)^(-1)K^c`, **not** `Qhat_c` in general. This exact obstruction is stronger than the claim that an overall mean may hide endpoint effects.

### Exact two-cause countermodel

A retained source leaves after `X~Exp(1)`. It enters a discarded trap of mean `2` with probability `1/4`, then absorbs into `C`; otherwise it enters a discarded trap of mean `4`, then absorbs into `D`. Retain the source and both causes. All variables are independent conditional on the branch.

    alpha=9/2,    alpha^C=3,    alpha^D=5.

The exact conditional means are `3` and `5`; the ordinary reduced clock gives `9/2` for both. The overall mean `9/2` is nevertheless exact. No common clock can equal both `3` and `5`. The unconditional certificate is `26`, while `V_C=8` and `V_D=32`, with `(1/4)8+(3/4)32=26`. The cause-conditioned construction fixes the conditional means and supplies their separate error certificates. It does not make the exact sum-of-exponentials holding law exponential.

### A rare-cause deadline whose conditional certificate is informative

Use the same topology but set `p_C=10^(-6)`, the `C` trap mean to `1/100`, and the `D` trap mean to `1000`. All these rates are synthetic. The ordinary correction is `1000.99900001`, while the cause corrections are `1.01` and `1001`. At deadline `t=1`,

    P(T>1 | C) = [exp(-1)-(1/100)exp(-100)]/(99/100)
                = approximately 0.371595395122669,
    ordinary reduced P(That>1 | C) = approximately 0.999001496839373,
    cause reduced P(That_C>1) = approximately 0.371539903071873.

Here `V_C=1/5000` whereas the unconditional certificate is approximately two million. Taking `e=1/10` gives the conditional band approximately `[0.316516226086808, 0.430208747375669]`. Multiplying by `10^(-6)` gives the joint rare-cause late risk band. The saved rational Taylor-interval calculation certifies enclosure for this synthetic example; the displayed decimals are for readability. The useful mechanism is endpoint targeting and preserving the time/endpoint correlation. It requires an accurately acquired or supplied rare branch probability, and it does not certify very late conditional tails.

## 7. A stronger tail obstruction: identical reduced model, identical certificate, identical finite moments

Fix any integer `k>=2`. Choose `k+1` distinct positive means `a_0,...,a_k` and strictly positive baseline weights `w_i` summing to one. Let `L_i(x)` be their degree-`k` Lagrange basis polynomials. For a new large mean `M`, choose `p_M=delta M^(-k)` and alter the old weights by

    w_i(M)=w_i-p_M L_i(M),

adding a new exponential component of mean `M` and weight `p_M`. Since each `L_i(M)/M^k` is bounded for all sufficiently large `M`, a sufficiently small fixed positive `delta` makes every altered weight nonnegative. Lagrange interpolation of `x^r`, `0<=r<=k`, proves exactly

    sum_i w_i(M) a_i^r+p_M M^r=sum_i w_i a_i^r.

Thus the two exponential mixtures have exactly the same first `k` moments, since an exponential of mean `a` has `r`th moment `r! a^r`.

Realize each mixture as the discarded holding time after a common retained source with `X~Exp(1)`, followed by absorption at a common retained target. All transient states reach the target. To impose equal dimension with every trap reachable from the source, split one fast baseline component into two distinct trap states with the same fast holding mean and positive weights summing to that component's weight; compare this baseline to the model with `k+1` fast states and one slow state. Both models then have the same trace `K`, the same mean correction `alpha`, the same `V=2 E[a^2]`, the same reduced CTMC, and the same first `k` passage-time moments. Yet at deadline `t=M`, the model with the added component has survival at least `p_M/e`, while the baseline survival decays exponentially. For a direct proof of the latter claim, choose any `0<lambda<min(1,1/max_i a_i)`; the baseline moment-generating function is finite, so the Chernoff bound is `P(T>t)<=E[exp(lambda T)] exp(-lambda t)`. Their relative survival ratio is therefore unbounded.

One may scale every discarded mean by an arbitrarily small fixed `epsilon` while holding the retained source rate at one. The common certificate becomes `2 epsilon^2 E[a^2]`, as small as desired. At `t=epsilon M`, the slow branch still contributes `p_M/e`, while the baseline has an exponential tail. Therefore the failure is not merely that the certificate tends to zero along a different family: **exactly identical reduction/certificate/finite-moment summaries, with any prescribed small positive error scale, admit arbitrarily different late-tail risks**.

This proves an information insufficiency for those summaries. It is not an impossibility for a supplied full generator, an exact excursion transform, a spectral representation including its slow residues, or an acquired tail-sensitive observation model. A valid uniform relative-tail certificate needs additional information excluding or resolving such slow low-weight components.

### Explicit rational second-moment family

Take baseline discarded means `epsilon,2epsilon,3epsilon`, each total weight `1/3`; split the first component into two states of mean `epsilon`, each weight `1/6`, so all four traps are reachable. In the altered four-trap model, for `M>=3`, put

    p_M=1/(12 M^2),
    w_1=1/3-(M-2)(M-3)/(24 M^2),
    w_2=1/3+(M-1)(M-3)/(12 M^2),
    w_3=1/3-(M-1)(M-2)/(24 M^2).

All weights are positive, their total including `p_M` is one, and the first two mixture moments agree exactly. Both models have

    alpha=1+2epsilon,
    V=(28/3)epsilon^2,
    E[T]=1+2epsilon,
    E[T^2]=2+4epsilon+(28/3)epsilon^2.

The saved checker verifies these identities at exact rational `epsilon=1/100` and several `M`. Decimal survival evaluations are diagnostics, not exact transcendental certificates; the displayed asymptotic lower bound and exponential comparison are the proof.

## 8. Input acquisition lower bound for an unobserved rare branch

For a separate simple model pair, a source reaches a long trap with probability `p` versus never reaching it; all non-trap trajectories are coupled identically. From `n` independent fully observed absorbed trajectories, the probability of seeing no rare branch is exactly `(1-p)^n`. If `n p` is small, both models therefore produce an identical data record with substantial probability, despite different late risks. To observe at least one rare branch with confidence `1-delta` requires

    n >= log(delta)/log(1-p),

which is asymptotic to `log(1/delta)/p`. With zero observed branches, the exact one-sided binomial upper limit is `p_U=1-delta^(1/n)` under the stated iid/predeclared-trial assumptions. This is a branch-sampling cost, not a Poisson laboratory-time hazard bound and not a statement about adaptively censored trajectories. Observing that the trap was entered still need not measure its long exit time precisely.

## 9. Matched classical representations and complete costs

For a supplied transient generator `Q_AA`, exact survival is `mu_A exp(Q_AA t) 1`. Cause-specific late risk is `mu_A exp(Q_AA t) c_A`; future absorption into `C` from a state at time `t` has probability `c`. This full-model phase-type matrix-exponential computation is a direct competitor. Its conditional form is `p mu^c exp(Q_AA^c t)1`. A diagonal spectral sum need not exist for a general nonreversible defective generator; Jordan/Schur, Krylov, uniformization and stable matrix-exponential methods remain available. Ordinary eigensums on a well-conditioned reversible benchmark are a legitimate competitor, but are not a universal representation theorem for the domain here.

Eliminating `I` without approximation at Laplace variable `s>=0` uses the resolvent

    G(s)=(s I-Q_II)^(-1),
    L(s)=s I-Q_RR-Q_RI G(s)Q_IR.

For the target transform `u_i(s)=E_i[exp(-sT)]`, set `u_B=1` and solve the corresponding retained Schur system. This frequency-dependent response retains the complete phase-type excursion information. The constant `G(0)`/mean-clock correction keeps only local zero-frequency information. Markov-renewal/phase-type excursion kernels and exact kinetic path sampling are established competitors; none can be omitted from an advantage claim.

If `d=|I|`, `r=|S|`, and `m` causes/rewards are requested, a dense eliminated factorization costs `O(d^3)` arithmetic operations. Forming a dense Schur complement needs solves for up to `|R|` right-hand sides (`O(d^2 |R|)` after factorization), multiplication, and up to `O(|R|^2)` fill storage. The reduced transient factorization is `O(r^3)`. Original occupations require an adjoint solve; the producer's full-G greedy selector stores `O(n^2)` numbers and costs `O(n^3)` preparation plus `O(k n^2)` updates for `k` selections. These are arithmetic, not bit, costs.

Cause conditioning can reuse both factorizations: solve the reduced committor equation, extend it with `c_I=G Q_IR c_R`, then solve `G c_I` for the conditional clock. Each cause thus adds a reduced solve and ordinarily two eliminated-block right-hand-side solves. Each further reward adds an eliminated solve with `c_I f_I`; the conditional clock and reward generators may fill and each reduced conditional distribution still requires its own exponential/reward calculation. The union of cause-specific clocks may enlarge the retained model by a factor equal to the number of causes. An exact joint reward law can require multidimensional inversion or state augmentation; the covariance does not make that cost vanish.

For `J` transform frequencies or deadlines, exact resolvent elimination can require `J` shifted factorizations/solves unless a reusable spectral/Krylov/structured representation is justified. Uniformization must pay for terms dictated by the largest exit rate times the horizon, or use justified splitting/adaptive methods. Exact path sampling must pay for factorization/elimination, reconstructed path data, sample variance, and the rare endpoint frequency; Doob conditioning avoids rejection only after the relevant committor is available. No end-to-end timing comparison is performed here.

Sparse factorization cost depends on ordering and fill and may approach dense cost. Rate dynamic range, cancellation, nonnormality, condition numbers and requested absolute/relative error determine precision. Relative error in tiny `p` must be certified; an absolute residual alone is insufficient. Rational exact fixtures establish only those rational models. Real-rate intervals need validated solves and lower bounds on positive committors before divisions. Acquisition of all rates, rare channels, reward definitions and a justified Markov approximation remains separate from every given-generator method above.
