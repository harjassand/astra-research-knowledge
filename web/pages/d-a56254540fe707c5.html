STATIONARY ERGODIC PREDICTIVE ALIASES AT A FINITE HORIZON
8 October 2026

VERDICT

For every horizon T and every fixed 0 < delta < 1/2, there are two explicit
stationary, irreducible, aperiodic finite-state hidden Markov models with
identical laws for every observation block of length at most T, but whose
next-symbol conditional laws differ by 2 delta at every supported T-symbol
context. Their stationary context distribution is uniform, so the discrepancy
occurs on a context set of probability one. With any number of passive
stationary samples capped at T consecutive symbols, the exact minimax
expected-TV prediction risk is delta; more data and more numeric precision on
the accessible laws do not help.

The exact construction uses 2^T hidden context states. Its context chain is
mixing, with worst-case 1/4 mixing time Theta_delta(T log T) for fixed delta.
This rules out an escape based solely on requiring stationary ergodic,
aperiodic, mixing processes, but it is not a bounded-state or uniformly-fast-
mixing lower bound. A separate periodic de Bruijn-cycle example has the same
finite-horizon alias. One extra observation (horizon T+1) makes both pairs
identifiable. For the full-support aperiodic pair, the high-confidence
testing exponent is C(delta) = -1/2 log(1-4 delta^2); for small delta and
0 < beta <= 1/3 the sample rate is Theta(delta^-2 log(1/beta)) independent
innovations. One exact conditional-probability query at a supported context
reveals the sign immediately.

The supplied-representation comparison is sharp: an oracle that supplies the
correct context update and predictive rule, including the one-bit model sign,
has zero prediction risk. Supplying only the T-bit context coordinates leaves
the two models aliased. These are information and interface results, not a
computational hardness claim, and they do not cover a teacher that supplies
exact predictions at every prefix.

1. MODEL AND EXACT ALIAS

Fix integer T >= 1 and delta in (0,1/2), both known to the learner, and let
the unknown model sign be sigma in {+1,-1}. The hidden state is a binary
context w=b_1...b_T in {0,1}^T. It emits its last bit
deterministically. From w=b_1...b_T it transitions to b_2...b_T y, where

  q_sigma(1 | w) = 1/2 + sigma delta (-1)^b_1,
  q_sigma(0 | w) = 1 - q_sigma(1 | w).

Call the two stationary output processes M_+ and M_-. The transition graph is
the binary de Bruijn shift graph. Every allowed edge has probability at least
1/2-delta > 0; the graph is strongly connected and state 0^T has a positive
self-loop. Every finite output word has positive probability, so the observed
process has full support. Thus each hidden chain is irreducible and aperiodic.
The uniform law U on {0,1}^T is stationary: each target u y has predecessors
0u and 1u, and their transition probabilities to u y sum to one. Initialize
from U. The hidden chains,
and hence their observed factors, are stationary and ergodic.

Every length-k observation block for k <= T is exactly uniform on {0,1}^k
under both models. To see this for k=T, start from a stationary context
b_1...b_T. The first emitted bit is b_T. The next T-1 emitted bits are
independent noisy copies of the distinct independent fair bits b_1,...,b_(T-1).
Each emitted bit is fair and the bits are mutually independent. Shorter block
laws are marginals. Consequently, for every N, the joint law of N independent
stationary blocks of any chosen lengths at most T is the same under M_+ and
M_-. This remains true for adaptive choices among those lengths and for exact
queries to all cylinder probabilities with length at most T.

After a T-bit observed context w, the next-symbol law is q_sigma(.|w).
Therefore, for every w,

  TV(q_+(.|w), q_-(.|w)) = 2 delta,
  P_sigma(W=w) = 2^-T.

The context set with this gap has probability one, not merely a small
exceptional mass. In addition, contexts are distinct predictive states:
given W=w, for each j=1,...,T the future marginal at offset j is

  P_sigma(X_(T+j)=1 | W=w) = 1/2 + sigma delta (-1)^w_j.

Any two different contexts disagree at some coordinate j and are separated
by this future test. Thus each model has 2^T distinct history-based predictive
equivalence classes. The two models share the same class set and update
mechanism but have different predictive readouts.

2. EXACT PASSIVE MINIMAX BOUND AT CUTOFF T

The learner knows T and delta; its only model uncertainty is sigma. The
interface is N independent stationary trajectories, each observed for at
most T consecutive symbols, with arbitrary computation. At evaluation,
the learner receives a fresh T-bit context W and outputs a Bernoulli
prediction with probability p_hat(D,W) of symbol 1. Define expected-TV risk

  R_sigma = E_(D,W under M_sigma)
              |p_hat(D,W) - q_sigma(1|W)|.

Because the training data and W have the same joint law under both signs,
the learner's randomized output has one common law. Pointwise in D,W,

  (|p_hat-q_+(1|W)| + |p_hat-q_-(1|W)|)/2 >= delta.

Thus inf over learners max_sigma R_sigma >= delta. The constant predictor
p_hat=1/2 attains delta, so the exact minimax expected-TV risk is delta.
For squared probability error the same argument gives exact minimax risk
delta^2. If epsilon < delta, the two epsilon-accuracy events are disjoint
under the common transcript law; some model has probability at least 1/2 of
error greater than epsilon.

This is stronger than a small-KL or rare-event coupling: the accessible
experiments are exactly identical, so the lower bound is independent of N.
It is also specific to the cutoff interface. A single uninterrupted path
longer than T, or a length-(T+1) training block, contains a new statistic.

3. APERIODIC MIXING PAIR: EXACT SAMPLE COST AFTER EXTENDING THE HORIZON

For one length-(T+1) block W X, where W is its first T observations, define

  V = (-1)^W_1 (2 X_(T+1) - 1) in {+1,-1}.

Then W is uniform and V is independent of W, with
P_sigma(V=+1)=1/2+sigma delta. For m independent length-(T+1) blocks,
the V values are iid and are sufficient for the sign; the remaining block
contents carry no additional information about sigma. The one-block KL
divergence, in nats, is

  d_delta = D(Ber(1/2+delta) || Ber(1/2-delta))
          = 2 delta log((1+2 delta)/(1-2 delta))
          = 8 delta^2 + O(delta^4) as delta -> 0.

For any test whose two error probabilities are at most beta < 1/2, data
processing gives

  m d_delta >= kl(1-beta || beta).

The likelihood-ratio/majority test has each error at most
exp(-2 m delta^2), so m >= (2 delta^2)^-1 log(1/beta) suffices. In
particular, for small delta and 0 < beta <= 1/3 the minimax sample rate is
Theta(delta^-2 log(1/beta)); for fixed delta the exact symmetric testing
error is the binomial tail

  e_m(delta) = P_(K~Bin(m,1/2+delta))(K < m/2)
               + 1/2 P(K=m/2) when m is even,

and e_m = exp[-m C(delta) + o(m)] with
C(delta) = -1/2 log(1-4 delta^2) = 2 delta^2 + O(delta^4).
The equality e_m for the two type-I/type-II errors uses randomized
tie-breaking when m is even. A predictor that identifies the sign and then
uses its exact rule has expected TV risk 2 delta e_m. By the two-point
absolute-loss decision rule and sign symmetry, this is also the minimax
expected-TV risk for this known two-model family.

The m independent blocks cost m(T+1) observed symbols and m stationary
episode starts. One uninterrupted path costs T+m symbols and one start for
the same m innovations.

For a single stationary observation path of length n > T, there are n-T
successive innovations V_t, each computed from the oldest bit in the current
T-context and the next observation. Conditional on the entire observed past,
P_sigma(V_t=+1)=1/2+sigma delta; hence these innovations are iid and
independent of the initial T-context. The full path is equivalent to that
initial context plus the innovations. It therefore has the same exact
testing rate with m=n-T. Long worst-case mixing time does not impose an
additional factor on this statistic.

The state chain itself is genuinely mixing. Write r=2 delta. After kT
transitions, conditional on any initial state, its T coordinates are
independent bits whose signed biases have magnitude r^k. Relative to the
uniform stationary law U,

  max_w TV(P_sigma^(kT)(w,.) , U)
      <= 1/2 sqrt((1+r^(2k))^T - 1)
      <= 1/2 sqrt(exp(T r^(2k)) - 1).

For initial state 0^T, a Hamming-weight threshold halfway between its mean
and T/2, with Chebyshev's inequality under both laws, gives

  TV(P_sigma^(kT)(0^T,.) , U) >= 1 - 8/(T r^(2k)).

These bounds imply worst-case 1/4 mixing time Theta_delta(T log T) for each
fixed delta in (0,1/2). For example, delta=1/4 keeps every allowed output
probability between 1/4 and 3/4 while the mixing time still grows as
Theta(T log T). The construction pays for its exact horizon-T alias in an
exponential number 2^T of context states and a growing mixing time; it does
not establish a separation with bounded state count or uniformly bounded
mixing time.

4. PERIODIC ERGODIC COMPARISON

Let C_T be the stationary process obtained by choosing a uniform phase on a
binary de Bruijn cycle of order T. It is a deterministic cycle on 2^T
phases, so the stationary process is ergodic but periodic and non-mixing.
Each T-bit word occurs once in the cycle; hence every block of length at most
T has the same fair-iid law as the stationary iid process I. After context
w, C_T has a deterministic next bit f(w), whereas I has next law Ber(1/2).
The task-relevant TV gap is 1/2 on every context. With no longer data, the
exact minimax expected-TV prediction risk for {C_T,I} is 1/4.

There are exactly 2^T permitted (T+1)-blocks on the cycle, one continuation
per T-context. Under I, a block is permitted with probability 1/2. From m
independent (T+1)-blocks, the overlap event A that every block is permitted
has probability 1 under C_T and 2^-m under I; conditional on A, the two
transcript laws agree. The exact minimax classification error, allowing
randomization, is 1/(2^m+1). Since a wrong endpoint predictor costs TV 1/2,
the exact minimax expected-TV prediction risk is 1/[2(2^m+1)]. This gives
an explicit periodic counterpart to the full-support aperiodic mixing pair.
The aperiodic construction shows that the phenomenon does not depend on
periodicity or zero-probability transitions.

5. INTERFACE CHANGE AND SUPPLIED REPRESENTATION

Smallest horizon change: allow one more observed symbol. For M_+ versus M_-,
the exact length-(T+1) laws differ and the innovation statistic above has a
sign-dependent Bernoulli law. For C_T versus I, length-(T+1) support also
differs. More numeric precision on any number of length-at-most-T
probabilities cannot help because those probabilities are identical.

Smallest active change: permit one exact conditional-probability query at
any supported T-context w. Its answer is 1/2+sigma delta(-1)^w_1 and reveals
sigma in one query. If the interface returns a conditional sample rather
than an exact probability, the inverse-square delta^-2 statistical cost
remains. If answers are rounded to a grid of width h, h < 2 delta suffices
to distinguish the pair regardless of grid alignment. Querying only
length-at-most-T cylinder values, even to arbitrary precision, does not.

An oracle-supplied predictive realization can store the current T-context,
shift it and append the next observation, and use the rule q_sigma. Given
(T,delta,sigma), prediction from an observed context is immediate; the
unknown information in this two-model class is just the sign bit. A literal
table has 2^T rows, while the symbolic rule uses T bits for the current
context and a constant-size transition/readout formula (plus the precision
needed to encode delta). Such a supplied realization has zero acquisition
risk under this task. Supplying only context coordinates or a state basis,
without its sign/readout, leaves the exact alias intact. The oracle has
already supplied the missing model identity; this comparison does not show
that acquiring a realization from the truncated passive interface is easy.

6. SCOPE AND PRIOR CONTEXT

The claim is for stationary finite-state output processes, exact contiguous
observation windows capped at T, and one-step prediction after a T-symbol
context. It does not prove hardness for fixed state count, fixed Hankel rank,
or a uniformly fast-mixing family. It does not contradict positive HMM
learning results under supplied separation conditions, nor the canonical
teacher-assisted target that provides exact predictions at every prefix and
complete trajectories. The lower bound vanishes as an obstruction as soon
as the observation interface crosses the first distinguishing horizon.
