# A04 Phase II update: exact prior-art and physical-interface audit

Date: 2026-10-09  
Status: live-primary-source comparison of Sol/Max frozen `work/sol/quantum_faults/v1.txt`; internal novelty assessment, not external validation. This is a new append-only version; v1–v4 are unchanged.

## Finding

I found no published theorem among the primary works checked that has the exact conjunction claimed in Sol v1: a positive-rate, linear-distance QTC family; one fixed-depth measured-recovery cycle per storage round; bounded-depth, faulted controller/decoder and readout; one arbitrary shared bath obeying a global, operator-norm local fault-cylinder bound across all locations and all rounds; and a logical diamond-memory guarantee for exponentially many rounds.

That distinction is narrower than the paper’s broad conceptual story. AKP already proves the shared-bath, coherent aggregate fault-path interface used in Sol v1. Gottesman explicitly anticipated transfer of his constant-overhead construction to non-Markovian noise, while stating he had not checked it. QTC single-shot recovery under Pauli noise and QLDPC single-shot recovery under coherent but location-factorized circuit noise are already published, and constant-overhead FT computation under the latter model is also published. The candidate is therefore best described as a specific theorem-level composition/extension across those interfaces, conditional on its full-circuit support model. The Möbius tail estimate and fault-tolerance composition are standard threshold proof tools, not by themselves a new mechanism. The source audit does not substantiate a major-mechanism or priority claim.

One quantitative source mismatch is material and must be corrected before even the conditional Hamiltonian corollary is claimed: Sol v1 has the wrong AKP prefactor. The correct prefactor is larger, and the resulting positive threshold still exists after replacing it.

## Exact published overlap

### AKP (2005 preprint; PRL 2006): shared-bath cylinders are prior art

The primary source is Aharonov, Kitaev, and Preskill, “Fault-tolerant quantum computation with long-range correlated noise,” arXiv:quant-ph/0510231v1 (30 Oct 2005), *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 96, 050504 (2006), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0510231> and <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.96.050504>.

AKP’s Eq. (9) says that for a specified set `I_r` of `r` faulty macro-locations, the aggregate operator summing paths faulty at every specified location, with **no restriction at other locations**, obeys `||E(I_r)|| <= epsilon^r`. The bath Hamiltonian is unrestricted. This is the same cylinder-norm shape as Sol v1 Eq. (2), once Sol imposes its support-respecting macro-location expansion. AKP then treats pair interactions `H_ij` acting on two system qubits and the bath, with per-qubit row sum

`mu = t0 max_{i,t} sum_{j != i} ||H_ij(t)||`,

and proves a location-cylinder bound for the long-range pair model by counting simultaneous contractions (AKP Eqs. (15)–(24), PDF pp. 1–3). Thus the global shared bath, temporal memory, absence of independence, and aggregation over all paths outside the required set are not new in Sol v1. Nor is the local-to-circuit threshold step, which AKP obtains by applying the AGP local-noise threshold theorem.

**Numerical correction.** AKP Eq. (22) is `eta' = exp(1 + 1/(2e)) sqrt(eta)`. For two-qubit macro-locations, Eq. (23) replaces `eta` by `2 eta`; Eq. (24) therefore reads

`epsilon = C_A sqrt(2 mu)`, with `C_A = exp(1 + 1/(2e))`.

Sol v1 instead writes `C_A = exp(1 - 1/(2e))`. The latter is smaller and does not follow from the cited derivation. The threshold remains strictly positive after the repair, but every Hamiltonian statement using that conversion must use the corrected constant. In particular, if the candidate’s abstract cylinder allowance is `eta_0`, its sufficient pair-Hamiltonian condition becomes `mu <= eta_0^2/(2 C_A^2)` with the corrected `C_A`.

AKP’s published paper is a short theorem about circuit-level quantum locations and system–bath Hamiltonians; it does not describe a noisy classical decoder/readout implementation. The paper has no measurement-record or classical-control section. Its equation-level result can be applied to an augmented set of quantum registers only after the physical/controller circuit is explicitly represented as bounded-size quantum locations and the AKP pair-interaction hypothesis is imposed on **all** of those registers. That extension is compatible with the AKP model, but is an added modeling step in Sol v1, not a statement made by AKP.

### AGP (2005 preprint): non-Markovian concatenated threshold, but classical postprocessing caveat

Aliferis, Gottesman, and Preskill, “Quantum accuracy threshold for concatenated distance-3 codes,” arXiv:quant-ph/0504218v3 (21 Oct 2005), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0504218>, proves a local non-Markovian threshold for concatenated codes (Section 11), with correlated faults and a shared bath. Its aggregate specified-set fault path is the underlying local-noise object used by AKP. The code/gadget construction is recursive concatenation, so this does not itself prove Sol’s growing-block QTC single-shot memory theorem or its `O(n)`-per-round circuit.

AGP explicitly calls out the classical assumption: for its output/measurement gadgets, the final classical decoding must be reliable or the result must remain protected in a classical code block; otherwise one classical decoding fault can cause output failure (PDF pp. 5, 11–13, 26–29). This source does not supply a noisy classical decoder theorem. Sol’s quantum dilation can be a valid way to include finite classical truth tables in the system only if its registers, gates, record wires, readout/reset, and couplings are all counted and the same Hamiltonian bound is assumed to cover them.

### Gottesman (2013/2014): explicit anticipation, not a proved non-Markovian QLDPC theorem

Gottesman, “Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation with Constant Overhead,” arXiv:1310.2984v3 (22 Jul 2014), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.2984>, Theorem 1 assumes a bounded-check/variable-degree LDPC family with asymptotic positive rate and a local-stochastic QEC property: initial errors, new errors per physical qubit per syndrome extraction, and flipped syndrome bits are controlled; syndrome extraction can be repeated polynomially many times and classical decoding has depth `h(n)`. The theorem gives constant qubit overhead for a sequential logical circuit, with its stated size/depth costs. This is not the same as a QTC single-shot decoder theorem under coherent shared-bath noise. The paper also does not establish a noisy classical decoder model.

The non-Markovian remark in the introduction (PDF p. 4) is unusually direct: after noting that a threshold is known for fairly general non-Markovian environments and that its parameter is a system–bath Hamiltonian norm, Gottesman says he “ha[s] not carefully checked” whether that proof transfers to his constant-overhead theorem, but sees no reason it should not. This is explicit anticipation of the natural composition, not a proof that preempts Sol v1. It does, however, show that the conceptual move “Gottesman’s constant-overhead construction should work under non-Markovian noise” was already anticipated. The proof obligation is the exact transfer at the growing-block/single-shot QTC recovery interface.

### Fawzi–Grospellier–Leverrier (2018): constant overhead and single-shot QExpander decoding under local-stochastic faults

Fawzi, Grospellier, and Leverrier, “Constant overhead quantum fault-tolerance with quantum expander codes,” arXiv:1808.03821v2 / FOCS 2018, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1109/FOCS.2018.00076>, primary PDF <https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8574539> (also <https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2018-Papers/pdfs/59f743.pdf>), proves constant-overhead fault-tolerant computation using quantum expander codes. Theorem 1 simulates polynomial-size circuits with `eta k` physical qubits, `O(f(k))` depth and `O(k f(k))` locations, with output-distribution error at most `epsilon`, for sufficiently small local-stochastic location fault rate. The same paper proves a single-round noisy-syndrome small-set-flip decoder and logarithmic decoder depth (Theorems 2/13 and 30, depending on the statement/version).

This is a strong prior-art baseline for the architectural composition, but the model differs in decisive ways: location faults are a random set with `Pr[S subseteq F] <= p^{|S|}`, not coherent operator-valued cylinders; classical computation is explicitly assumed fast and error-free (PDF pp. 1, 3, 12–13); and the code is a quantum expander code, not a QTC with the particular deterministic reduced-weight recurrence in Sol v1. FGL therefore does not equal the candidate theorem, but it makes clear that constant-overhead single-shot FT architecture is already an established mechanism under stochastic noise.

### QTC and coherent QLDPC results (2022–2026): exact code/decoder and nearest coherent baseline

Gu et al., “Single-shot decoding of good quantum LDPC codes,” arXiv:2306.12470v2 (11 Apr 2024), <https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12470>, supplies a QTC single-shot decoder and Pauli support guarantee robust to data and syndrome support errors. Its memory guarantee is stochastic/Pauli support based, not an arbitrary shared-bath coherent fault-path theorem. This is the code/decoder input already used in Sol v1; the candidate does not claim to discover the QTC decoder.

Christandl, Fawzi, and Goswami, “Fault-tolerant quantum computation with constant overhead for general noise,” arXiv:2512.02760v1 (2 Dec 2025), published as *PRX Quantum* 6, 040334 (2025), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02760> and <https://doi.org/10.1103/k4cm-pp9p>, is the closest coherent theorem checked. Its Definition 2.3 allows arbitrary diamond-close channels at quantum/hybrid locations and Theorem 5 proves QLDPC single-shot error suppression with a noisy syndrome-extraction circuit; their Theorem 3 then gives constant-overhead fault-tolerant computation with classical input/output. But their circuit model defines each layer noise as a tensor product of location channels and explicitly assumes purely classical elements are perfect (PDF pp. 8–9). The theorem permits reduced-weight errors correlated across code blocks, and arbitrary coherent channel errors at individual locations, but does not allow one common bath to correlate distinct locations jointly within a layer. Thus it does not imply AKP’s global cylinder condition or Sol’s arbitrary cross-location shared-bath model. Conversely, Sol v1 proves storage only and does not match CFG’s logical-gate, universal-circuit, or physical classical-output capability.

Han et al., “Coherent error threshold for quantum LDPC codes,” arXiv:2609.20537v1 (17 Sep 2026), <https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20537>, gives a coherent code-capacity threshold for qLDPC under perfect stabilizer measurements/recovery. It is relevant to coherent data errors, not to faulty local circuit-level syndrome/control or a shared-bath multiround memory channel.

### Practical physical retrieval baseline

Bravyi et al., “High-threshold and low-overhead fault-tolerant quantum memory,” arXiv:2308.07915v2 (21 Feb 2024), *Nature* 627 (2024), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07915>, is a strong practical memory baseline. It gives a depth-7 syndrome circuit for bivariate-bicycle LDPC codes with `n` data and `n` check ancillas, degree-6 thickness-2 connectivity, circuit-level simulations and BP-OSD, and proposes fault-tolerant logical initialization, measurement, and load/store interfaces. The stated circuit-noise model assumes each operation fails independently with probability `p` (PDF pp. 2, 5); its long-lifetime estimates are finite-size numerical results/extrapolations, not an analytic coherent shared-bath threshold theorem. Its retrieval interface is physically treated more concretely than the ideal-recovery metric in Sol v1.

## Classical/readout dilation: exact model boundary

The source attribution matters. The quoted statement that a measurement record is stable “since it is classical,” and that a coherent record qubit can be treated as error-free/no longer bath-coupled, is **Terhal and Burkard**, “Fault-tolerant quantum computation for local non-Markovian noise,” arXiv:quant-ph/0402104, *Phys. Rev. A* 71, 012336 (2005), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0402104>, not AKP. It is a modeling convention in the Terhal–Burkard derivation. It cannot be used as evidence that AKP’s long-range correlated Hamiltonian theorem already includes noisy classical records.

The candidate’s alternative is sound as a declared model refinement: replace deterministic local decoder/control truth tables by reversible bounded-fan-in quantum circuits; retain readout bits in explicit computational-basis registers; model preparation, reset, readout, idles, controlled corrections, and every local message operation as charged locations; then require the pairwise Hamiltonian row bound to cover those physical registers too. Under that enlarged-system assumption, the record is not treated as a free, protected classical register; it is part of the noisy system. The finite QTC local views and fixed decoder iteration count make the ideal reversible circuit bounded-depth and `O(n)`-size on the stated incidence/controller graph.

This argument is a reduction from noisy classical control to noisy quantum gates inside the chosen idealized model. It does not establish that actual cryogenic readout electronics, classical wires, controller CPUs, clocking, or a real-time interface obey the pair-Hamiltonian row bound. Nor does AKP supply calibration or certification of `mu`. Those are model/acquisition gaps, not algebraic contradictions in the conditional theorem. Fresh pure ancillas and reset/entropy disposal are also physical resources: the statement counts `O(n)` live registers and `O(n)` prep/reset/readout locations per round, but relies on a supply of fresh/reinitialized ancillas over `T` rounds. The clean-state supply and its coupling model must remain explicit when interpreting the result as hardware capability.

## Ideal final recovery versus physical retrieval

Sol v1 Eq. (17) bounds

`|| D_n o Memory_T o Enc - id_L ||_diamond`,

where `D_n` is an ideal isometric final decoder extending the inverse of the correctable syndrome-subsystem embedding. This is a standard, mathematically meaningful *recoverable-memory* channel metric: the claim is that after `T` noisy storage/recovery cycles, the logical information is still recoverable, including entangled logical inputs and an arbitrary fixed initial bath state. The isometric extension and polar normalization are proof tools; they do not enact a postselection on good histories.

The metric does **not** claim a physical noisy readout of the logical qubits, a quantum load/store operation, or an end-to-end classical answer. Sol v1 Section 8(f) correctly disclaims those. The exact QTC decoder is described as an efficient ideal final decoder; that operation and its gates are not included in the per-round cost or noise bound. This scope is not an internal proof defect for a memory theorem, but it must be retained in any title/claim and compared fairly with protocols that count output operations. CFG’s 2025 theorem explicitly covers circuits with classical input/output under its tensor-product noise model; Bravyi et al. add a concrete physical BB-code load/store and logical-readout proposal under their stochastic model. Neither is the same noise model, but each exceeds the Sol theorem’s current output interface.

The repeated protected storage protocol itself uses one noisy syndrome/recovery round of bounded graph depth per time step, `O(n)` live data/check/controller registers and `O(n)` locations; total counted circuit work is `O(nT)`. The ideal final decoder in the metric can require `O(log n)` QTC iterations plus polynomial stabilizer linear algebra. A noisy physical implementation of that final decoder is not proven to have a fixed-noise exponential-memory bound by the per-round argument: it is a separate output gadget. This is an interface limitation, not a counterexample to the storage assertion.

## Exact novelty classification after comparison

1. **Published source result:** AKP/AGP already establish coherent shared-bath local fault-path norms and non-Markovian threshold composition for recursive fault-tolerant circuits. Gottesman already proposes constant-overhead LDPC FT computation under local-stochastic noise and explicitly anticipates non-Markovian transfer.
2. **Published code/decoder result:** QTCs supply asymptotically good code parameters and single-shot support decoding under Pauli data/syndrome faults. FGL supplies constant-overhead stochastic FT computation with single-shot decoding. CFG supplies coherent QLDPC single-shot correction and constant-overhead computation for general per-location channels under layer factorization and perfect classical operations.
3. **Candidate-specific conditional theorem:** Sol v1 attempts to combine (i) an all-location AKP-type global operator cylinder bound, (ii) an explicit bounded-depth noisy QTC controller/recovery circuit, (iii) the deterministic linear reduced-weight recurrence, (iv) the global bad-round operator estimate, and (v) a recoverable-memory diamond metric. I found no exact primary theorem with that same conjunction.
4. **Novelty limit:** the path-mask Möbius identity and high-weight tail are standard inclusion–exclusion/fault-path counting; the code family and single-shot property are prior art; and the extension from a common-bath fault-cylinder condition to a fault-tolerant code-level statement is a natural realization of a transfer explicitly anticipated by Gottesman. Therefore the candidate’s exact contribution, if the proof and corrected noise interface survive independent review, is a specific coherent QTC memory theorem with all local quantumized controller locations charged. It is not a new general QEC mechanism, a general FTQC theorem, or a physical hardware threshold certification.

## Repair and unresolved gates

- Correct Sol v1 `C_A` to `exp(1+1/(2e))`; correspondingly correct every numeric/symbolic Hamiltonian threshold inequality that uses it. The existence of some positive `mu_0` remains.
- Keep the all-location AKP Hamiltonian condition conditional on the augmented-system model. Explicitly include decoder truth-table gates, readout records, reset/preparation circuits, idle wires, and controlled corrections; do not claim AKP directly analyzed noisy classical equipment.
- Retain the ideal-final-recovery metric as a storage/recoverability metric and keep physical logical output outside scope unless a noisy retrieval gadget and its costs are proved.
- Independent proof audit of the QTC support recurrence, fault-support propagation through the dilated measurement/control circuit, syndrome-subsystem factorization for every good path, and polar normalization is still required. This novelty/source audit is not a proof validation.
- No counterexample was found to the abstract theorem solely from the ideal final decoder or the source comparison. The AKP prefactor error is repaired by a stricter positive noise threshold; it does not invalidate the abstract cylinder theorem.
