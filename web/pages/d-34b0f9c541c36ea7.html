Cycle 2, leaf 02 — Repulsive interactions preserve the XX cubic sector-tail envelope
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE (elementary finite-dimensional proof; no
independent proof review). Source-priority finding on uniform range-2 models:
SOURCE-REPORTED from the cited 2025 classification.

Result

For the open hard-core chain with shifted XX kinetic energy

  K_L=J sum_{i=0}^L (b_{i+1}-b_i)^*(b_{i+1}-b_i),  b_0=b_{L+1}=0,

and any number-preserving repulsive density potential

  W_L=sum_{nonempty X, diam(X)<=R} V_X prod_{i in X} n_i, V_X>=0,

the saturation-detuned Gibbs state

  rho_{L,W,lambda}=Z^{-1} exp{-tau L^2[K_L+W_L-lambda M/L^2]},
  M=sum_i n_i,

has a uniform high-occupation bound

  Pr_rho[M>=d] <= C_geo exp[-(4/3)tau J d^3+C_2 d^2],   1<=d<=L.  (1)

Assume fixed public compact ranges tau in [tau_-,tau_+], J in [J_-,J_+]
with tau_-,J_->0 and |lambda|<=B. No upper bound on the nonnegative V_X,
translation invariance, or a bound on their number is required. Constants in
(1) are independent of L, R and W. The displayed cubic coefficient follows
under beta=tau L^2 and the Dirichlet open-chain normalization written above.
For d>L the tail is zero. The precise finite-size constants and proof are in
`work/programmes/spin_statistical/cycle2/leaves/leaf02_noncommuting_scaling/derivation.txt`.

The mechanism is sector min-max, not perturbation theory. Since K_L and W_L
preserve M, W_L>=0 gives in every sector

  Z_m(W)=Tr_{M=m} exp[-tau L^2(K_L+W_L)] <= Z_m(0).             (2)

This inequality compares ordered eigenvalues, so it remains valid when
[K_L,W_L] is nonzero. It does not assert an operator-Loewner inequality for
the exponentials. The open-chain Jordan-Wigner/sine spectrum is
e_k=4J sin^2(pi k/[2(L+1)]), and sin x>=2x/pi implies
e_k>=4Jk^2/(L+1)^2. Fermionic exclusion forces the M-particle sector to occupy
distinct k's, giving a ground-energy cost at least a constant times
sum_{k<=M}k^2. The bounded detuning adds only exp(tau lambda M), while the
vacuum contributes exactly 1 to the partition function. A geometric sum of
sector bounds yields (1). No first-order commutator or Duhamel correction
appears; positivity and exact sector conservation are the load-bearing
assumptions.

Archive/error/cost convention

For 2<=D<=L, the parameter-independent measurement of M followed by a
D-symbol map can store M=0,...,D-2 exactly and send a single overflow label
for M>=D-1. Writing d=D-1, its overflow probability is exactly Pr(M>=d),
hence obeys (1). At D=L+1 use the exact code M->M, with no overflow. The
output is an exact coarse-grained classical sector record; a
decoder that imputes a fixed value on overflow has M-marginal total-variation
error at most this tail. It stores ceil(log2 D) bits per sample. At fixed
compact constants, D=O((1+log(1/epsilon))^(1/3)) symbols suffice for overflow
at most epsilon, so the stored bit count is O(log log(1/epsilon)). If
D=L+1, all sector labels can be retained exactly.

This is not a whole-state compression theorem. The channel outputs only the
classical sector label and discards within-sector quantum information. It
does not approximate rho in trace norm, preserve conditional sector states,
or provide a local-observable decoder. The only cost proved is the number
of retained classical bits. An ideal projective M measurement is assumed;
hardware, measurement time, classical aggregation time, and energy costs are
not included.

Strongest comparator and exact limits

The strongest proved comparator is the unnormalized canonical trace
inequality (2), sector by sector, against the parameter-matched free XX
partition function. Combining it with the vacuum lower bound Z>=1 gives a
normalized interacting tail envelope. It does not imply normalized
stochastic domination of the free M distribution: the two grand partition
functions have different normalizers. No optimality or matching lower bound
for the D-symbol archive is claimed.

The proof actually needs only W>=0 and [W,M]=0, not finite range or density
form. Thus it extends to any finite-dimensional number-preserving positive
operator. It does not cover number-changing terms, attractive/indefinite W,
or a detuning outside a bounded lambda/L^2 window. The first proof-breaking
change is loss of positivity: then sector eigenvalues need not dominate the
free ones, and an attractive cluster can lower the M-sector energy. This is
a falsifier for a broader extension, not a counterexample under the stated
assumptions.

Range-2 priority and model boundary

The uniform periodic pairwise t-V-V' chain has NN hopping and NNN density
coupling. Jordan-Wigner maps it to NN XX+YY exchange plus NNN ZZ (with a
uniform longitudinal field and scalar). Shiraishi's 2025 classification
covers the periodic shift- and inversion-symmetric two-body chain. In its
rank-one NNN-ZZ case with NN XX coupling nonzero, Theorem 6 excludes
translation-invariant k-support conserved quantities for 4<=k<=L/2; the
Bethe-solvable exception instead has a specific NN XZ coupling. This is an
exact source theorem in the stated sense, so generic uniform pairwise range-2
nonintegrability is not a new claim here.

That source does not directly cover the open chain (edge terms break shift
invariance), arbitrary site-dependent V_X, or multi-body finite-range terms
such as n_i n_{i+1} n_{i+2}. Rabson, Narozhny and Millis (2004) give earlier
finite-size level-statistics evidence of an integrability-breaking
crossover, but explicitly say the accessible sizes do not settle its
thermodynamic scaling. These integrability facts are not used in (1).

This local finite-range result is separate from the immutable all-to-all
critical-spin N20 card: N20 concerns a whole-state experiment and a quartic
R^3 limit with noncommuting collective tilts. Here the interface is a
classical M register for an open z=2 chain, and the proof is min-max plus
fermionic exclusion. Neither theorem transfers to the other's interface;
this result is not a generic finite-range lattice theorem.

Assumptions, costs, falsifier and open gate

* Supplied: L, open path hopping J>0, tau>0, |lambda|<=B, beta=tau L^2,
  fixed compact parameter ranges, and coefficients defining W. Each W term
  is a nonempty density monomial of bounded spatial diameter with V_X>=0.
* Acquired: exact one-body sine spectrum, canonical free-fermion partition,
  sectorwise min-max comparison, and the summable distinct-mode bound. No
  oracle, data acquisition, numerical fit, or precision-dependent proof
  step is used.
* Information cost: D classical outcomes, ceil(log2 D) retained bits; at
  target overflow epsilon, D=O((log(1/epsilon))^(1/3)) up to fixed-constant
  additive cutoffs. Computation/measurement implementation costs are
  uncharged and should not be mistaken for free physical implementation.
* Falsifier: an allowed positive number-preserving W for which (2) or the
  explicit tail bound (1) fails. The exact min-max proof rules this out
  under current assumptions. A negative interaction, number-changing term,
  or unbounded field is outside scope and can invalidate the mechanism.
* Unresolved gate: clarify whether the parent programme wants only a
  classical sector statistic or an archive of the entire quantum Gibbs
  state. The present theorem resolves only the former. A further open source
  gate is any desired nonintegrability assertion for open/nonuniform or
  multi-body W; the periodic pairwise literature cannot be extended by
  analogy alone. Independent proof review and historical priority for the
  archive envelope remain open.

Evidence

`work/programmes/spin_statistical/cycle2/leaves/leaf02_noncommuting_scaling/evidence/minmax_exact.py`
is an exact SymPy check of a three-site, two-particle sector where K and W
do not commute. It finds spectra {4-sqrt(2),4,4+sqrt(2)} and {3,4,6} for K
and K+W=n_1n_3, respectively, with ordered shifts {sqrt(2)-1,0,2-sqrt(2)}.
The diagnostic checks normalization and operator ordering only; the proof
does not depend on finite evidence.
