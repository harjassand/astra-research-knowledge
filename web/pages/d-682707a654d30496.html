# Cycle 2: bounded-height rational matrix-oracle compilation

## Consequential result

The exact-matrix acquisition theorem can be compiled to a finite-bit *rounded exact* service on a bounded-height rational subclass, but only under an explicit input-height bound. For a rational recognizable series with state dimension `r`, realization-entry height at most `H`, and a jointly strictly upper-triangular rational query of dimension `D` whose entry height is at most `B`, each exact output entry has a common denominator with bit length at most

\[
h_* = 2rH+(D-1)(nr^2H+nD^2B).
\]

Consequently, `2h_*+2` fractional bits suffice to reconstruct every rational output entry exactly by continued fractions. If an exact adaptive learner makes `T` such queries, a rounded service with that precision reproduces its exact transcript, provided its query-generation bit costs are charged and it stays within the declared `B,D` envelope.

The same calculation does **not** yield a polynomial-sample Gaussian compiler. A uniform sufficient procedure that first estimates a Gaussian mean to the rational-reconstruction tolerance `1/(4Q_*^2)` uses order `sigma^2 Q_*^4` repeats. This is a sufficient cost bound, not a general matching lower bound. The hidden-word family in the first-cycle report supplies a separate, proved interface-specific lower bound of order `n^d` total query dimension/returned entries for fixed noise and excitation `E=1`. Thus bounded rational height makes exact fixed-point access computationally simulable at polynomial precision, while stochastic acquisition can still require exponentially many observations. This is an internal theorem for the specified finite representation; it does not validate L01's universal learner or claim novelty.

## Interface and proof

Let

\[
f(w)=\lambda^\top A_{w_1}\cdots A_{w_k}\gamma,
\qquad A_i\in\mathbb Q^{r\times r},\quad \lambda,\gamma\in\mathbb Q^r,
\]

with every numerator and positive denominator in the reduced realization entries bounded in magnitude by `2^H`. A query is a tuple `X_1,...,X_n in Q^(D x D)` with all entries' reduced numerators and denominators bounded by `2^B`. Assume every `X_i` is strictly upper triangular. Write `Q_lambda,Q_gamma,Q_A,Q_X` for the products of the denominators of all entries in the respective vectors, transition family, and query family. Then

\[
\log_2 Q_\lambda,\log_2 Q_\gamma\le rH,
\quad \log_2 Q_A\le nr^2H,
\quad \log_2 Q_X\le nD^2B.
\]

The block matrix `M=sum_i A_i tensor X_i` is nilpotent of index at most `D`, so

\[
f(X)=(\lambda^\top\otimes I_D)\sum_{k=0}^{D-1}M^k(\gamma\otimes I_D).
\]

Every summand of degree `k` has denominator dividing `Q_lambda Q_gamma (Q_A Q_X)^k`. Since all these are integers and `k<=D-1`, the whole finite sum has denominator dividing

\[
Q_*:=Q_\lambda Q_\gamma(Q_AQ_X)^{D-1},
\quad \log_2Q_*\le h_*.
\]

Thus each reduced output denominator is at most `Q_*`. Two distinct rationals with denominators at most `Q_*` differ by at least `Q_*^-2`, since `|a/b-c/d|=|ad-bc|/(bd)>=1/(bd)`. If a fixed-point answer `z` has absolute rounding error at most `2^(-p-1)` and `p>=2h_*+2`, there is a unique rational with denominator at most `Q_*` within `Q_*^-2/2` of `z`. Continued-fraction reconstruction returns the exact response. A magnitude bound for the numerator is

\[
\log_2\max\{1,|f(X)_{ab}|\}
\le 2H+\log_2(rD)+(D-1)(H+B+\log_2(nrD))+O(1),
\]

so integer-part bits are also polynomial in the stated parameters. This argument is exact rational arithmetic; no floating-point claim is hidden in it.

For an adaptive exact learner with `T` query calls, maximum query dimension `D`, maximum input-entry height `B`, and realization height `H`, a rounded oracle using `p=2h_*+2` fractional bits gives exactly the same transcript if the learner's next query is a bit-computable function of the previously reconstructed rational answers. Per response it returns `D^2` entries, each with `p+O(log max(1,|f(X)_{ab}|))` total bits. Rational reconstruction by continued fractions costs polynomially many bit operations in this answer length per entry; its cost is `T D^2 poly(h_*+H+B+log(nrD))`, in addition to the learner's own exact arithmetic and query-generation cost. Input descriptions cost at least the sum of all rational-entry description bits and are not free. The proof provides no bound if realization height, dimension, query-entry height, or adaptive query-description length is unbounded.

For the Gaussian variant, suppose each response entry is independently observed with variance `sigma^2` on every repeated call. To reconstruct a rational without a class-specific decoder, estimate each entry to error `epsilon=1/(4Q_*^2)`. For `s` repeats, a Gaussian tail bound and union bound over all `TD^2` responses show that

\[
s\ge 32\sigma^2 Q_*^4\log(2TD^2/\delta)
\]

suffices to keep every average within `epsilon`, with probability at least `1-delta`. Repeating multiplies call count, returned entries, total query dimension, and per-call excitation opportunities by `s`. A finite-bit implementation must additionally quantize each sample much more finely than `epsilon` and charge integer-range/overflow handling; N25's ideal real-Gaussian oracle does not specify that digital layer. This bound can be very loose, but its exponential dependence on response bit height is real in the absence of a larger margin.

## Stronger adversarial check

The hidden-word class `f_w(u)=1[u=w]`, `w in [n]^d`, has realization height `H=0` and minimal rank `d+1`. The prime-position query in [the first-cycle exact/noisy report](children/exact_noisy_oracles/FINAL_REPORT.md) has `D=d+1`, satisfies `sum_i X_i^T X_i <= I`, and decodes `w` from one exact rational matrix response. Its denominator is `K^d`, so `O(d log K)` fixed-point fractional bits suffice for that explicitly known common denominator, and this query description has polynomial bit length.

On the Gaussian side, the reconstructed N25 energy lemma gives, for any adaptive strategy with both testing errors at most `delta<1/2`,

\[
\mathbb E_0\sum_tD_t\ge2\sigma^2 n^d\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)
\]

under per-query total excitation `E=1`. Because each response has `D_t^2>=D_t` entries, the same is a lower bound on expected returned-entry count. This comparison shows why bit-precise algebraic access and stochastic matrix acquisition must not be charged as the same service. The lower bound is only for the stated Gaussian interface and is not an independent verification of the source-card theorem or a priority result.

The lower bound scales to a hidden-word coefficient `a!=0`: for targets `a f_w`, the response mean difference is `a X_w`, so every KL term is multiplied by `a^2`. The same proof therefore yields

\[
\mathbb E_0\sum_tD_t\ge 2\sigma^2a^{-2}(n/E)^d\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\delta,\delta).
\]

Taking a supplied rational amplitude `a=2^{-H_a}` preserves rank `d+1` and adds only `H_a` coefficient bits, while multiplying the required Gaussian dimension by `2^{2H_a}`. The prime-position exact response remains decodable with `O(H_a+d\log K)` fractional/output bits. This is an internally derived amplitude refinement of the same source-card energy argument, not a priority claim; its proof is a one-line rescaling of every response mean in the KL chain rule.

There is also an absolute fixed-grid output lower bound. If each exact response entry is rounded to a grid of spacing `q=2^{-b}`, couple a run on zero and `f_w` using the same internal randomness until their rounded transcript first differs. Along the all-zero null transcript, any word with cumulative signal energy `Z_w=Σ_t||X_{w_1}...X_{w_d}||_F^2<q^2/4` produces only entries smaller than `q/2` and hence rounds to zero at every call. A test with both errors at most `delta` must diverge with probability at least `1-2delta` for each word. Markov's inequality and the word-energy bound then imply

\[
C\ge(1-2\delta)\,2^{-2b-2}(n/E)^d.
\]

This result is specific to absolute fixed-point resolution near zero; floating-point responses with unbounded exponent and relative precision are a different interface. It quantifies the precision-versus-total-dimension tradeoff and is distinct from the rational reconstruction *sufficient* precision theorem above.

## Prior art, novelty, and limits

Rational reconstruction from sufficiently accurate approximations and denominator bounds is classical; this report's contribution is a self-contained denominator calculation for a finite evaluation of a rational recognizable series on a jointly nilpotent tuple, together with an explicit simulation ledger. Exact weighted-automaton learning, spectral/noisy weighted-automaton learning, and adaptive Gaussian sensing are established. The exact/noisy hidden-word separation is likewise a finite interface counterexample, not a new general automata-learning algorithm. No priority claim is made.

The inference applies to a rational minimal realization whose parameter heights are bounded **as supplied promises**. The learner does not discover that height bound, and the result is not a uniform numerical stability theorem for arbitrary characteristic-zero series. The rounded-exact interface is a deterministic numerical service with guaranteed per-entry error. Under Gaussian sampling the conservative reconstruction cost is exponential in `h_*`; the N25 lower bound independently confirms exponential acquisition cost for a compact subfamily under an explicit excitation cap. No wet-lab/physical query implementation, external proof review, formal proof, or benchmark was performed.

## Acquiring a recurrent observable from passive trajectories

A separate positive construction attacks feature acquisition directly. Let an unknown mask `s in GF(2)^d` label a persistent bit `Z=s·A mod 2`. Each reset episode emits a uniform input vector `A in {0,1}^d`, followed by `R` independent noisy readouts `B_j=Z xor E_j`, where `P(E_j=1)=eta` and `1-2eta>=beta>0`. This process has an O(d)-state clocked HMM realization. The learner receives ordinary trajectories only.

With

\[
N=d+\lceil\log_2(2/\delta)\rceil,
\qquad
R\text{ odd and }R\ge(2/\beta^2)\log(2N/\delta),
\]

majority recovers every episode's `Z` with failure at most `delta/2`; the uniform `N x d` design matrix has rank `d` except with probability below `delta/2`. Solving over `GF(2)` therefore acquires `s` with probability at least `1-delta`. The acquired predictor is a two-coordinate one-hot parity state. Reading input `A_i` swaps the two coordinates iff `s_i A_i=1`; the state is unchanged during the repeated-readout phase. For each fixed time cut, every bounded future task factors through this state with readout norm at most `sqrt(2)`. It stores `d` model bits and an O(1)-bit parity plus a position counter online.

The source budget is `N(d+R)` observed symbols, `O(NR+Nd^2)` bit operations, and `O(d^2+R)` working bits. To estimate a supplied bounded continuation task at one cut to additive `rho` with confidence `1-delta`, simulate `M>=ceil((2rho^2)^-1 log(8/delta))` futures from each parity state; this costs at most `2M` continuations and O(MH) sampler operations, plus task-evaluation time. If `eta` is rational, finite-bit Bernoulli sampling by rejection and an explicit cap on attempts are charged in the child report. The equal-data baseline runs the same majority/GF(2) decoder on the same trajectories and outputs the same model at the same cost. Thus this is a real passive feature-acquisition construction for a narrow persistence class, not a sample or runtime advantage and not a novelty claim. One-shot noisy parity remains a different source model: independent repetition of a readout of the *same* latent bit is the resource that lets this algorithm denoise before solving the linear system.

Full proof, controlled outputs, precision and costs are in [the persistent-readout report](children/teacher_free_hmm/CYCLE2_REPORT.md). The construction's limits are material: the mask family and repeated-readout mechanism are supplied; it does not cover arbitrary finite-state sources or establish a biological persistence mechanism.

## Chosen-prefix samples versus passive reachability

The adaptive-conditioning cycle-2 route builds a clocked source with hidden target word `s`, uniform input bits, and noisy match markers. A chosen-prefix conditional-sample oracle can recover one next bit at a time from a prefix known to match `s`, using O(T log(T/delta)) calls and O(T^2 log(T/delta)) returned suffix symbols because the oracle returns the whole remaining suffix. The matching prefix has probability exponentially small under ordinary sampling. Adjacent target words differing at the final bit have one-trajectory TV at most `2^-T`, so constant-error passive identification needs `Omega(2^T)` full episodes. Its proof and cost ledger are complete in [the child report](children/adaptive_conditioning/CYCLE2_REPORT.md) and [proof sheet](children/adaptive_conditioning/PROOFS_CYCLE2.md). Liu–Moitra (STOC 2025) already gives arbitrary-finite-rank distribution learning from chosen-prefix conditional samples, so no general learner novelty is claimed. Their polynomial guarantee in `1/epsilon` does not imply polynomial-time exact word identification at TV scale `2^-T`, and a sampler output is not a canonical latent feature map.

This pair separates two meanings of acquisition: conditional query count can be polynomial while the service is allowed to visit exponentially rare histories at unit query cost; a passive physical simulator pays for the reachability probability. Reports must charge both the number of calls and the probability/cost of realizing their conditioning prefixes.
