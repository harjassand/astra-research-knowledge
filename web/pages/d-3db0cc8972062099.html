# Adaptive interventions for acquiring a minimal predictive state

**Result type:** exact finite-model identification theorem, with an explicit but generally impractical cost bound. This is a standard finite-state construction, not a novelty or priority claim.

## Candidate result

Let a resettable black-box system be a deterministic Mealy machine
\[
M=(Q,A,Y,\delta,\lambda,q_0),\qquad |Q|\le n,
\]
where \(A\) is a finite intervention alphabet, \(Y\) a finite observation alphabet, and applying intervention \(a\) in state \(q\) emits \(\lambda(q,a)\) and moves the system to \(\delta(q,a)\). A reset returns the system to exactly the same initial state \(q_0\). Each experimenter-selected action is a hard intervention: it sets the next action to \(a\), regardless of the system's ordinary action-generating process. An experiment applies a finite word \(w\in A^*\) after reset and returns the full output word, including every intermediate readout.

Under this contract, a finite adaptive version-space learner identifies the complete input/output response function and hence the system's **minimal predictive state representation**, up to behavioral isomorphism. It uses at most
\[
M_n-1\quad\text{experiments, each of length at most }n^2,
\qquad
M_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n} k^{k|A|+1}|Y|^{k|A|}.
\]
This is a deliberately conservative finite bound, not a claim of practical efficiency. It removes an uncharged equivalence-query oracle: every separating experiment is physically run and its full cost counted.

For a state \(q\), let \(T_q(w)\) be the output word produced by future intervention word \(w\). Define
\[
q\sim r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_q(w)=T_r(w)\text{ for every }w\in A^*.
\]
The quotient \(Q/{\sim}\) is an exact sufficient state for predicting every future output sequence under every future intervention sequence. It is minimal among deterministic predictive state representations: if two states are merged despite failing this condition, a separating intervention word gives a prediction error. In a deterministic system, equality of the open-loop response maps also suffices for policies that choose later actions from earlier outputs, by induction on the policy's steps.

This representation is behavioral. It does not recover molecular state, a causal DAG, or a mechanistic invariant.

## Learner and proof sketch

Enumerate the finite class of labeled Mealy machines with at most \(n\) states, and quotient them by equality of their full response functions from their initial states. There are at most \(M_n\) such functions: for a labeled \(k\)-state machine, there are \(k\) choices of initial state, \(k^{k|A|}\) transition tables, and \(|Y|^{k|A|}\) output tables.

Maintain the response functions still consistent with all experiments. If two remaining functions disagree, find a shortest distinguishing word by breadth-first search in the product of their state graphs. The product has at most \(n^2\) state pairs. A shortest distinguishing word cannot revisit a pair: removing the cycle between two visits would preserve the later differing output and shorten the word. Thus the word has length at most \(n^2\). Run it from reset, record all outputs, and remove every inconsistent response function. The true function remains and at least one distinct function is removed. After at most \(M_n-1\) runs, all remaining candidates agree on every future intervention word.

Take any remaining machine and merge its states by \(\sim\). All remaining candidates agree on the initial response function; therefore for every reachable history, they agree on its residual future-response function too. The returned quotient is consequently identified up to behavioral isomorphism. Hidden internal state names are never observed or claimed to have been recovered.

### Cost ledger

Write \(a=|A|\), \(b=|Y|\). For a simple exhaustive implementation:

- **Intervention runs:** at most \(M_n-1\) resets and \((M_n-1)n^2\) action applications.
- **Readouts:** at most \((M_n-1)n^2\) observations. The bound assumes one exact output symbol per action; the full sequence is retained, including prefixes.
- **Reset/washout:** charge one reset and any washout/standardization per run. If their costs are \(c_R,c_W\), action cost is \(c_A\), and readout cost is \(c_Y\), the physical cost is at most \((M_n-1)[c_R+c_W+n^2(c_A+c_Y)]\). Parallel cultures are not assumed to make a run free.
- **Search and memory:** enumerating/storing models costs at least order \(M_n a n\) encoded table entries. A deliberately simple implementation that scans all candidate pairs each round and runs product-graph searches has a loose upper bound \(O(M_n^3 a n^2)\) comparison operations and \(O(M_n a n)\) model storage. A smarter learner may improve this computational bound; that would not remove intervention/readout costs.
- **Supplied interface costs:** choosing the finite intervention panel, time grid, reporter panel, output binning, known state cap \(n\), and reset protocol are prerequisites, not learned results. If these choices are costly, their design and validation must be added to the ledger. No graph, state labels, access sequence, or invariant is supplied.

For nontrivial \(n\) and \(a\), \(M_n\) grows extremely quickly. The theorem is an identifiability guarantee, not an efficiency result. The model class enumeration can dominate all laboratory costs.

## Strict controls and falsifiers

A fixed one-step panel is a useful negative control. Consider two binary-output machines that both emit 0 for every action on the first step, but one enters a primed state after action `1` and emits 1 on a later `1`. Every one-step experiment agrees; the sequence `11` separates them. More generally, a non-adaptive fixed panel can fail whenever it omits a distinguishing sequence.

A passive-observation control applies only the natural action sequence. Two machines can agree on that path and disagree under an action never naturally applied, so passive records do not identify the intervention-response map. In the stated experiment contract, action assignment is externally set; this is not adjustment for an observationally confounded treatment.

The known bound \(n\) is load-bearing. If no finite state cap is known, no finite collection of runs certifies the complete response function over unbounded horizons: for any finite experiment set with maximum length \(H\), an all-zero one-state machine and a chain machine that emits a different symbol only after a chosen length-\(H+1\) sequence agree on every completed run. Unknown state growth can always hide a later distinction.

The theorem is falsified as an exact claim if resets do not reproduce the same initial state, transitions drift between trials, outputs are stochastic/noisy, interventions are not actually imposed, or the finite action/output alphabets fail to capture the intended question. These cases need a probabilistic or partially observed model and confidence-dependent sample costs; this report makes no such extension.

## Causal and biological fit

The causal meaning here is operational: the experimenter imposes a sequence of controls and learns the resulting response function. The target is predictive sufficiency under those controls, not a structural causal graph. Pearl and collaborators distinguish observational distributions from distributions under external interventions; the interface above gives intervention assignments directly instead of inferring them from passive observations.

A plausible *idealized* cell-control instantiation would use a clonal, reporter-engineered cell line in microfluidics; define actions as a small, fixed set of ligand or inhibitor pulses at fixed time steps; measure a coarse joint reporter category after each pulse; and start every trial from a validated matched baseline. The learned quotient would merge histories that have identical reporter predictions under all future pulses. It would be useful only for that cell line, reporter binning, pulse panel, timing, and validated state cap.

This is not currently an honest exact model of ordinary cell assays. Fresh wells do not guarantee identical hidden initial states; cells are stochastic, continuous, heterogeneous, and can drift; CRISPR perturbations are often persistent rather than sequential reversible inputs; and exact finite-state bounds are usually unavailable. Hein and Weissman’s primary HCMV study illustrates why dynamic perturbation responses matter: it reports single-cell infection trajectories and host perturbations that stall, delay, or accelerate progression, while viral perturbations can create alternate trajectories. It does not learn a finite predictive machine or certify the assumptions above. That study motivates a target question, not validation of this algorithm.

## Relevant algorithmic comparison

He and Geng (2008) give a genuine active causal-experiment design algorithm for orienting edges in a DAG Markov-equivalence class. They assume causal sufficiency (no latent variables) and faithfulness for the observational independence information; they distinguish randomized interventions that sever incoming causes from quasi-interventions that may not. Their design starts from an observationally obtained equivalence class and chooses node interventions to reduce it. Their batch minimum-design procedure exhaustively searches candidate intervention sets (reported complexity \(O(g!)\) for chain-component size \(g\)); their sequential design adapts later choices to the current class.

That result is a useful control for *graph orientation*, but it does not acquire the graph or a general controlled predictive state from scratch. In particular, the supplied equivalence class and its assumptions must remain visible in the accounting. Shpitser and Pearl’s causal-hierarchy paper makes the underlying distinction explicit: observational, interventional, and counterfactual queries require different information.

## Bottom-line claim status

The finite deterministic theorem is proved by the product-graph and finite-version-space arguments above, subject to its exact contract. It is a scoped construction using standard automata ideas, not evidence of a biological capability, efficient experiment design, or causal graph discovery. The biologically realistic noisy/reset-free extension remains open here.

Moore defines finite deterministic input/output machines and black-box experiments, and explicitly warns that scientific systems may instead be continuous or probabilistic (PDF pp. 1–5).

[JMLR paper PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume9/he08a/he08a.pdf). The paper states faithfulness and no-latent-variable assumptions and compares batch and sequential intervention designs.
- I. Shpitser and J. Pearl (2008), “Complete Identification Methods for the Causal Hierarchy,” *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 9:1941–1979. [JMLR article](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v9/shpitser08a.html). It distinguishes associative, interventional, and counterfactual query levels.
- M. Y. Hein and J. S. Weissman (2022), “Functional single-cell genomics of human cytomegalovirus infection,” *Nature Biotechnology* 40:391–401. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01059-3). Primary biological motivation only; it is not evidence for exact deterministic state learning.
