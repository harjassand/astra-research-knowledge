GENERAL SIMPLE-ROOT-SYSTEM LOWER PROFILE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: self-contained derivation of the lower half; constants depend only on
the fixed irreducible root system. Nonsimply-laced types are included.

1. SETUP

Let G be a compact connected simple Lie group with irreducible root system
Phi, rank r, positive roots Phi+, and p=|Phi+|. Fix the invariant form on
the real weight space with long roots of squared length 2. Let rho be the
Weyl vector and let the conjugation-Casimir on End(V_lambda) be normalized
to have eigenvalue

  c2(nu)=1/2 (nu,nu+2rho)

on an irreducible summand V_nu. Equivalently, define the positive Casimir
energy E(X)=<X,LX> using the corresponding invariant metric on the compact
Lie algebra. A different fixed global normalization rescales all energy
constants but not any exponent below.

For any irreducible carrier V_lambda of dimension d, write

  Lambda_lambda(Q)=inf_{0!=X, rank X<=Q} E(X)/||X||_HS^2.

The argument proves universal lower bounds for every carrier, not just
regular ones. If lambda is nontrivial, the kernel of L is C I. Choose a
safe positive gap floor

  gamma_Phi=min_i (omega_i,rho)>0,

where omega_i are the fundamental weights. For every nonzero dominant
integral nu=sum_i b_i omega_i, b_i>=0 and some b_i>=1, so

  c2(nu)=||nu||^2/2+(nu,rho)
        >=(nu,rho)=sum_i b_i(omega_i,rho)>=gamma_Phi.        (1)

The actual gap in End(V_lambda) may be larger because the center acts
trivially; (1) is a safe root-system-only floor.

2. ALL-CARRIER MULTIPLICITY CAP

For every dominant nu,

  mult(V_nu,End(V_lambda)) <= dim V_nu[0].                     (2)

This follows directly from the general highest-vector coefficient
injection, with no type-A coordinate assumption. Set mu=lambda^*. By
semisimplicity and tensor-Hom currying,

  mult(V_nu,End(V_lambda))
    =dim Hom_G(V_mu,V_mu tensor V_nu).

For an intertwiner phi, decompose phi(v_mu), where v_mu is highest, as
sum_beta w_beta in V_mu[mu-beta] tensor V_nu[beta], beta in the positive
root lattice. Its beta=0 coefficient is v_mu tensor p_phi with
p_phi in V_nu[0]. If p_phi=0 but phi(v_mu)!=0, choose a nonzero w_beta
of least positive root height. The highest-vector equation, projected to
V_mu[mu-beta+alpha_i] tensor V_nu[beta], gives
(e_i tensor 1)w_beta=0 for each simple raising operator: the only possible
other term comes from w_(beta-alpha_i), absent or zero by minimality.
The common kernel of all simple raising operators on V_mu is its highest
line, contradicting that the first-factor weight is mu-beta. Hence
phi -> p_phi is injective into V_nu[0].

The connected-compact-group source Fronsdal-Hirai proves the stronger
whole-representation inclusion End(V_lambda) embeds into L^2(G/T), and
for singular lambda uses the larger coadjoint stabilizer. The source
comparison and exact reference are recorded in the SU(m) SOURCES.txt;
the present argument is not a novelty claim.

3. UNIFORM ALL-T CASIMIR COUNT

Let

  D_lambda(T)=sum_{nu dominant:c2(nu)<=T}
                   mult(V_nu,End(V_lambda)) dim V_nu.

Then for every carrier lambda and T>=0,

  D_lambda(T)<=C_Phi(1+T)^p.                                  (3)

Proof: put q=p-r. Kostant's zero-weight multiplicity formula is

  dim V_nu[0]=sum_{w in W}(-1)^ell(w) P(w(nu+rho)-rho),

where P(beta) counts nonnegative decompositions of beta into p positive
roots. The simple roots form a basis of the root lattice. Choose the q
nonsimple-root coefficients first; each is at most ht(beta), since every
positive root has positive integer height. The simple-root coefficients
are then uniquely determined, or yield no valid decomposition. Thus

  P(beta)<= (1+ht(beta))^q.

For beta=w(nu+rho)-rho in the positive-root cone, height is
ht(beta)=<rho^vee,beta> and is at most
||rho^vee|| (||nu||+2||rho||) after identifying the dual Cartan by the
fixed invariant form. All root-system constants are fixed. Therefore

  dim V_nu[0] <= C_Phi (1+||nu||)^q.                           (4)

This is an upper bound from Kostant's alternating formula; it does not
replace the formula by one unsigned partition term.

Weyl's dimension formula gives dim V_nu<=C_Phi(1+||nu||)^p. Also
c2(nu)>=||nu||^2/2 for dominant nu, so c2(nu)<=T implies
||nu||<=sqrt(2T). The number of dominant lattice weights in that ball is
at most C_Phi(1+sqrt(T))^r. Applying (2) and summing yields exponent

  (q+p+r)/2 = ((p-r)+p+r)/2 = p,

which proves (3). The estimate sums over the entire dominant weight lattice,
so it remains valid after D_lambda(T) saturates at d^2. No stable-range
cutoff is being used in the Green tail.

4. LOW-RANK SPECTRAL-PROJECTOR LOWER BOUND

Let Pi_T be the conjugation-invariant projection onto Casimir spectrum
[0,T]. Since V_lambda is irreducible, Schur's lemma gives

  Tr_2(Pi_T)=(D_lambda(T)/d)I.

For ||X||_HS=1 with rank(X)<=Q, its vectorization has Schmidt rank at
most Q. The partial-trace estimate is

  ||Pi_T X||_HS^2<=Q D_lambda(T)/d.

Layer cake gives

  E(X)>= integral_0^infinity [1-QD_lambda(T)/d]_+ dT.          (5)

Substitute (3). If u=d/Q is larger than a root-system constant, choose
T comparable to u^(1/p) so the integrand stays bounded below on [0,T];
this gives

  Lambda_lambda(Q)>=c_Phi(d/Q)^(1/p).                         (6)

If u is bounded, use the Casimir gap (1). The projection onto the scalar
kernel has mass |Tr X|^2/d<=Q/d; when Q<=d/2, at least half the norm is
outside the kernel, so E(X)>=gamma_Phi/2. This completes (6) for
1<=Q<=d/2. This part applies to every nontrivial carrier.

5. NEAR-FULL GREEN LOWER FOR ARBITRARY COMPLEX MATRICES

Let rank(X)<=d-k with 1<=k<d and choose a rank-k projection P onto a
subspace of ker X. Set X=aI+Y, Tr Y=0, P0=P-(k/d)I. From XP=0 and
cyclicity, ka=-Tr(P0Y). Cauchy-Schwarz in the L^{-1}/L pairing and (1)
give

  ||X||_HS^2<=E(X)[d G(P)/k^2+gamma_Phi^(-1)],
  G(P)=<P0,L^(-1)P0>.                                       (7)

This uses a right kernel and does not require X to be normal or positive.
For the positive Casimir projection Pi_(0,T], the rank-k Schmidt estimate
and the exact traceless mass of P0 give

  F_P(T):=||Pi_(0,T]P0||_HS^2
       <=min{k-k^2/d, k^2(D_lambda(T)-1)/d}.                  (8)

The exact finite-spectrum identity is

  G(P)=integral_0^infinity F_P(T) T^(-2)dT.                   (9)

Write s=d/k>1, delta=s-1, and set

  A_Phi=C_Phi(1+gamma_Phi^(-1))^p.

Using (3), (8), and F_P(T)=0 below gamma_Phi gives the following
gap-safe cutoff bounds. For p>=2, with
S=max{gamma_Phi,(delta/A_Phi)^(1/p)},

  Lambda_lambda(d-k)>=
   [gamma_Phi^(-1)+(p/(p-1))*delta/S]^(-1).                  (10)

For p=1 (the rank-one simple root system A1),

  Lambda_lambda(d-k)>=
   [gamma_Phi^(-1)+A_Phi(1+
     log_+(delta/(A_Phi gamma_Phi)))]^(-1).                   (11)

Indeed, for p>=2 split (9) at S, integrate the low part as
O((k^2/d)S^(p-1)) and the high part as (k-k^2/d)/S; for p=1 the low
integral is logarithmic. The max/log_+ handles a cutoff below the gap.
When 1<=k<=d/2, delta is comparable to d/k. Thus

  Lambda_lambda(d-k)>=c_Phi(k/d)^((p-1)/p),  p>=2,

  Lambda_lambda(d-k)>=c_Phi/log(e d/k),         p=1.          (12)

All statements in (7)-(12) hold for arbitrary complex matrices, including
nonnormal matrices. The only carrier-specific condition is that lambda be
nontrivial, so the kernel is exactly the scalars.

6. NORMALIZATION / ROOT-LENGTH AUDIT

The lower proof only uses the spectral normalization
c2(nu)=1/2(nu,nu+2rho). For upper filters, choose an sl2 root triple
(e_alpha,f_alpha,h_alpha) for each positive root, with
[e_alpha,f_alpha]=h_alpha and h_alpha acting by the coroot
<.,alpha^vee>. In a unitary realization f_alpha=e_alpha^*. The
root-space directions in the compact Casimir have positive coefficients
depending on the invariant metric and on alpha's length. On a fixed
irreducible root system these coefficients take finitely many positive
values, so the full energy is comparable, with constants depending only
on Phi, to the sum over positive roots of the corresponding
||[e_alpha,F]||_HS^2 terms for Hermitian radial filters F. The shell
recurrence itself is exact in the sl2 normalization above; it must not be
confused with a unit-root-vector normalization that silently inserts
long/short-root factors.

The upper-filter audit is delegated separately and is required before
claiming a matching profile for nonsimply-laced types. This file proves
only the lower half and does not infer an operational E/F/G transfer.
