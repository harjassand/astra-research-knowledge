Claim: For a strictly positive column-stochastic finite-state transition matrix P with positive stationary vector h and a fixed strictly positive row-stochastic backward proposal Q, define K[j,i]=P[j,i]^2/Q[j,i]. The source proves ρ(K)≥1, with equality exactly at the Perron twist Q[j,i]=P[j,i]h_i/h_j. In a supplied two-state deterministic piecewise-affine response example, the proposed common-prefix single-term roulette has infinite absolute expectation (and second moment) for every independent full-support level law, while an exact transfer-operator solve gives the response directly. The source also reports four-state slow-mixing stress cases: for δ=10^-3 and absolute-bias target 10^-3, the exact finite-horizon bias requires n≥6212, while its fixed-proposal response-variance lower bound has log10 at least 486.666 and the stated independent-path count for standard deviation 10^-3 has log10 at least 492.666.
Interface: The proposal is fixed at the differentiation point; finite-horizon score estimators are distinguished from roulette estimators. The deterministic realization has a finite Markov partition and is not a claim about globally smooth expanding maps. The four-state family separates uniform inverse-branch contraction from mixing constants that degenerate as δ→0.
Proof spine: A Perron-vector/Cauchy–Schwarz argument gives the spectral radius and equality condition. The explicit map realizes the finite transition matrix exactly. Conditional increment bounds show divergence of the absolute roulette expectation; direct transfer equations provide the exact small-instance comparator.
Costs: The direct finite-state transfer calculation uses standard finite-dimensional linear algebra; the proposed path method samples O(n) transitions per path and has the reported unbounded/infinite roulette cost in this example. No scaling advantage is established. In the reported δ=10^-3 stress case, the variance/path-count lower bounds make this specific estimator impractical; the exact transfer solve remains available for the finite state model.
Failure: The generic eigenfunction-twist principle is prior work, as the source itself notes. The counterexample only defeats the stated fixed stationary proposal and common-prefix single-term roulette. It is not a lower bound for horizon-dependent/adaptive proposals, resampling, control variates, other couplings, all chaotic-response methods, or smooth systems.
Closest comparator: The source compares the Perron twist to Whiteley–Lee, “Twisted particle filters” (arXiv:1210.0220v4), and the single-term estimator identity to Rhee–Glynn/Vihola (arXiv:1512.01022). Generic twist and exact transfer methods are established; priority of this explicit example is unknown.
