## Exact complex-phase identity

Let `F` be an explicitly encoded `n x n` matrix over `Q(i)`, and let `0 <= k <= floor(n/2)`. Define

\[
c_k(F)=\sum_{\substack{|I|=|J|=k\\I\cap J=\varnothing}} |\det F[I,J]|^2.
\]

For `i in [n]`, put `u_i=e_i` and let `v_i` be the transpose of row `i` of `F`. Choose any fixed unit phases `phi_i` with `|phi_i|=1` and independent uniform signs `s_i in {+1,-1}`. Define

\[
A_{\phi,s}=\sum_i \phi_i s_i(u_i v_i^T-v_i u_i^T)
=D_{\phi,s}F-F^TD_{\phi,s},\qquad D_{\phi,s}=\operatorname{diag}(\phi_i s_i).
\]

The transpose in this formula is essential: it makes `A` complex skew-symmetric. The adjoint appears in the later norm coefficient `A* A`, not in the skew construction.

Write `V=(u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_n,v_n)`, and let `P(S)` denote the `2k` columns paired from `S` in that fixed interleaved order. The exterior-form identity

\[
\left(\sum_i \phi_i s_i u_i\wedge v_i\right)^k/k!
=\sum_{|S|=k}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\phi_i\right)\chi_S(s)\bigwedge_{i\in S}(u_i\wedge v_i)
\]

gives, for every `|R|=2k`,

\[
\operatorname{Pf}(A_{\phi,s}[R])=
\sum_{|S|=k} d_{R,S}\left(\prod_{i\in S}\phi_i\right)\chi_S(s),\qquad
d_{R,S}=\det V[R,P(S)].
\]

Identity columns imply `d_{R,S}=0` unless `S subset R`; expansion along those columns gives `|d_{R,S}|=|det F[S,R\\S]|`. The functions `chi_S` are orthogonal under uniform independent signs, and the fixed phase factor has modulus one. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_s|\operatorname{Pf}(A_{\phi,s}[R])|^2=\sum_{S\subseteq R,|S|=k}|\det F[S,R\\S]|^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E_s X_k(A_{\phi,s})=c_k(F),
\]

where `X_k(A)=sum_{|R|=2k}|Pf(A[R])|^2`. This proves that arbitrary fixed unit-modulus site phases preserve unbiasedness. Alternating `phi_i=1` for even `i` and `phi_i=i` for odd `i` is one such choice; it is useful because it removes sign variance on the real paired baseline described below. The exact checker exhausts all `16` sign strings for `k=1,2` on a dense nonsymmetric rational-complex `4 x 4` fixture and confirms the mean, but this finite test is diagnostic, not the proof.

One sample `X_k(A)` can be computed without enumerating minors. The complex skew Youla normal form gives unitary-congruence blocks `[[0,sigma_j],[-sigma_j,0]]` and zeros, so

\[
\sum_j X_j(A)t^j=\prod_r(1+t\sigma_r^2),\qquad
\det(I+tA^*A)=\prod_r(1+t\sigma_r^2)^2.
\]

Thus `X_k` is `[t^k] sqrt(det(I+t A* A))`. Newton identities for `det(I+tB)` with `B=A* A`, followed by the formal square-root recurrence, compute it by `O(k n^3 + k^2)` exact arithmetic operations; bit lengths are polynomial in `n`, `k`, and the encoded bit length after common denominator clearing. The normal-form identity is imported from D. C. Youla, “A Normal form for a Matrix under the Unitary Congruence Group,” *Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 13 (1961), 694–704, [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/normal-form-for-a-matrix-under-the-unitary-congruence-group/964D0AA8DAC0CDB9079F04331B61859D). A complete review of that paper's proof is outside this lane; the present argument uses its stated unitary skew-block form.

## Prefix counter interface and exact cost

For pairwise-disjoint forced-up, forced-down, and forced-empty site sets `U,D,H`, the target prefix mass is

\[
m_{U,D,H}=\sum_{\substack{|I|=|J|=k,\ I\cap J=\varnothing\\U\subseteq I,\ D\subseteq J\\(I\cup J)\cap H=\varnothing}}|\det F[I,J]|^2.
\]

Write `a=|U|`, `b=|D|`. For a free sign string and a real indeterminate `z`, set `tau_i(z)=z` on `U`, `tau_i(z)=s_i` on unconstrained sites, and `tau_i(z)=0` on `D union H`; put `A(z)=diag(tau(z))G-G^Tdiag(tau(z))`, where `G=D_0F` clears all rational real and imaginary denominators by a common positive integer `D_0`. For each `2k`-set `R`, the coefficient `h_R(s)=[z^a]Pf(A(z)[R])` selects exactly the wedge terms with every forced-up pair chosen. Then

\[
Y(s)=\sum_{\substack{R\subseteq[n]\setminus H,\ |R|=2k,\ D\subseteq R}} |h_R(s)|^2\ge0,
\qquad \mathbb E_sY=D_0^{2k}m_{U,D,H}.
\]

The role of `D` is enforced on the row set `R`: since `D` sites have zero activity, they cannot belong to the up set `S`, hence `D subseteq R` forces them into `R\\S`, the down set. The role of `H` is enforced by deleting those coordinates. Character orthogonality then gives exactly the stated physical-prefix mass.

To evaluate a raw `Y(s)` by polynomial-time coefficient calls, for each `T subseteq D` retain `W_T=[n]\\(H union T)` and compute `Q_T(z)=X_k(A(z)[W_T])`. Since `deg Q_T<=2a`,

\[
[z^{2a}]Q_T(z)=\frac1{(2a)!}\sum_{j=0}^{2a}(-1)^{2a-j}\binom{2a}{j}Q_T(j).
\]

Then inclusion-exclusion gives `Y(s)=sum_{T subseteq D}(-1)^|T| [z^(2a)]Q_T(z)`: a row set `R` is counted with multiplicity `sum_{T subseteq D\\R}(-1)^|T|`, which is one iff `D subseteq R`. Intermediate terms can have signs; the final exact integer is the nonnegative squared-coefficient sum above. The program never relies on approximate cancellation.

For each eligible `R`, at most

\[
\rho=\binom{2k-a-b}{k-a}
\]

up-sets contribute. Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz on their Boolean characters gives `0 <= Y <= rho * D_0^(2k) m`, and `E Y=D_0^(2k)m`; hence `E Y^2 <= rho (D_0^(2k)m)^2`. With `eta in (0,1/2]`, `M=ceil(3 rho eta^-2 ceil(log_2(2/delta)))` independent signs suffice by multiplicative Chernoff for relative error `eta` and failure at most `delta`. The raw sample calls at most `2^b(2a+1)` values of `X_k`. Since

\[
2^b\rho\le 2^b\binom{2k-b}{k}\le\binom{2k}{k}=:C_k,
\]

every physical-prefix count has deterministic bit cost `C_k poly(n,L,eta^-1,log(1/delta))`, where `L` is the input bit length. No inverse norm, smallest nonzero determinant, projection probability, or smallest positive prefix probability is charged. Exact-zero prefixes produce zero on every sign word; a positive prefix may still get a zero randomized estimate on the charged failure event, so a zero estimate is not a deterministic zero certificate. For fixed `A`, `k<=A log_2(n+1)` makes `C_k<=4^k<=(n+1)^(2A)` polynomial; for general linear `k` this bound is exponential.

## Magnitude compiler followed by exact phase

Fix a declared Fock basis. In canonical “all up modes, then all down modes” order, set

\[
a(I,J)=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\det F[I,J].
\]

In a site-interleaved mode order, multiply by the reordering sign `(-1)^r`, where `r=|{(i,j) in I x J : i>j}|`. This basis-order sign is material: an early version of my finite checker compared these two conventions directly and failed; that preserved harness error is recorded in `phase_formula_initial_mismatch.log`. The corrected independent wedge checker separately verified the determinant coefficient, canonical pair-order sign, and site-interleaving sign for all 19 disjoint amplitudes of the `4 x 4` fixture.

Under the promise `c_k(F)>0`, the target is `|psi_F>=c_k(F)^(-1/2) sum_(I,J) a(I,J)|I,J>`. First prepare `sum sqrt(p(I,J)) |I,J>`, where `p=|det F[I,J]|^2/c`. Then apply the diagonal phase `a/|a|` on nonzero amplitudes and phase `1` when `a=0`. This recovers the complex determinant amplitudes exactly before finite gate approximation. Compute each determinant and the basis sign from the occupied sets using exact arithmetic. To avoid a cost depending on a tiny determinant magnitude, for nonzero `a=x+iy` put `alpha=max(|x|,|y|)`, normalize `(x/alpha,y/alpha)`, and observe its squared norm is in `[1,2]`. This makes phase normalization well-conditioned; it does not require an inverse of `|a|` in the compiler cost.

For magnitude preparation, use the physical-prefix tree, with three children (up/down/empty) at each site. The shared-depth compiler reconstruction attributed to `c03_s02` can be stated with explicit parameters. Let `Delta in (0,1)` be the compiler-seed failure target and `epsilon in (0,1)` the final trace-distance target. For each depth `d`, choose one random tape `T_d` and reuse it for the three child counters at every prefix of that depth; child counter segments are independent. Put

\[
\eta=\epsilon/(16n),\qquad \beta=\Delta\epsilon^2/(64n^2),\qquad \delta_c=\beta/3.
\]

Each positive child prefix is estimated to relative accuracy `eta` with failure at most `delta_c`; true zero children are exactly zero on every run. At a positive prefix, if all its positive child estimates are good, normalization changes each conditional probability by at most the ratio `(1+eta)/(1-eta)` and the local nonnegative amplitude vector by Euclidean norm at most `4 eta`. For a bad prefix the distance between two unit vectors is at most `2`.

Let `p(x)` be the ideal prefix probability and define `B_d(T_d)=sum_x p(x) 1[x is bad]`. The union bound over at most three child estimates gives `E B_d<=beta`; this uses no union bound over the exponentially many prefixes. For the compiler hybrid, order the telescoping replacements so that all operations before the replaced depth are ideal; its input at that depth then has the ideal prefix distribution. The total magnitude-state norm error is bounded by

\[
4n\eta+2\sum_d\sqrt{B_d}\le4n\eta+2\sqrt{n\sum_d B_d}.
\]

Markov gives `Pr[sum_d B_d > n beta/Delta] <= Delta`. For all other tapes the right side is at most `4n eta+2n sqrt(beta/Delta)=epsilon/2`. A fixed-tape circuit can compute its prefix counts reversibly and pad each branch to the same worst-case counter length; this avoids variable-time control. If all three estimates are zero, choose any child that preserves the remaining cardinality feasibility. This keeps every basis string legal (`|I|=|J|=k`, disjoint) for every compiler seed, although that fallback string may have zero target amplitude and is covered as bad-prefix error.

Allocate the other `epsilon/2` in norm to finite-precision conditional rotations and the determinant phase, with `O(epsilon/n)` local synthesis accuracy. Rational weights, feasibility, determinants, and basis signs have polynomial-bit exact algorithms. Scaling by `alpha` above keeps phase normalization away from zero. Standard reversible exact arithmetic and universal finite-gate rotation synthesis then give, conditionally on a good classical tape, pure-state trace distance at most `epsilon` (pure-state trace distance is no larger than the vector norm error). The claimed randomized classical compiler therefore has success probability at least `1-Delta` and size/work `C_k poly(n,L,epsilon^-1,log(1/Delta))`. This is a construction consequence conditional on those standard compiler/synthesis primitives, not an executed quantum circuit or measured hardware result. The normalized target is undefined when `c_k(F)=0`.

This state compiler is not implemented by `low_sector_sampler.py`: that file implements the exact prefix draw and the classical `born_sample` self-reduction only. Its all-zero-estimate branch returns `FAIL`. On every non-FAIL return it preserves support because a true-zero child estimate is always zero; to force a total classical output, one may map `FAIL` to any fixed legal k-pair pattern and charge its probability to the failure budget. The quantum hybrid instead uses a feasibility-preserving legal fallback in its controlled unitary. I verified the classical sampler only on 32 seeded calls on the complex fixture; these are finite diagnostics and do not execute or certify the compiler.

## Phase matching extension and limits

The `c03_s01` extension sets `phi_i=1` on even sites and `phi_i=i` on odd sites. On its real baseline `G` consisting of diagonal entries plus independent adjacent pair entries `a_j=G[2j,2j+1]`, `b_j=G[2j+1,2j]`, the diagonal cancels in `A`; each skew block has upper entry `s_(2j) a_j - i s_(2j+1)b_j`, whose squared magnitude is `beta_j=a_j^2+b_j^2`, independent of both signs. Therefore `X_k(A_G)=e_k(beta_1,...,beta_(n/2))=c_k(G)` for every sign string. My copied-source exact checker confirms this constant sample for `k=1,2,3` on unit blocks; unphased relative second moments there are `2,4,8`.

The extension accepts `F` if `delta^2=||F-G||_F^2 <= beta_min/(256 k^2)`, where `beta_min=min_j beta_j>0`. Independently, `||A_F-A_G||_2 <= 2 delta`; the Youla singular values of the baseline are all at least `sqrt(beta_min)`. Weyl perturbation therefore pairs each singular value within relative error `r<=2delta/sqrt(beta_min)<=1/(8k)`. Since `X_k=e_k(sigma_1^2,...,sigma_(n/2)^2)`, termwise comparison gives

\[
(1-r)^{2k}c_k(G)\le X_k(A_F)\le(1+r)^{2k}c_k(G),
\]

and hence `3c_k(G)/4 <= X_k(A_F) <= 4c_k(G)/3`. This is the narrow phase-matched certificate: it can cover dense `F` with small Frobenius residual, but it is only a certified neighborhood of a known matching baseline. It gives a bounded-range count estimator without the central-binomial sample factor for accepted matrices. The current `phased_pfaffian.py` supplies a count, not physical-prefix child counters; it is not by itself a state-preparation compiler. A separate prefix extension and proof would be required to plug it into this hybrid. Its JSON's 96 sign words, 352 coefficient comparisons, 10 sector-mean comparisons, and dense `n=8` structure case are attributed peer diagnostics, not an independent run here.
