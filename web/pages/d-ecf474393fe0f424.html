## Fixed subfamily and arbitrary fixed additive precision

Fix an admitted irreducible baseline Q_0 with missing u→v, known reverse rate a=q_vu>0, and reciprocal support for every other ordinary pair. Q_b=Q_0+bB, 0<b≤1. Fixed initialization is independent of b. Observations are passive endpoint state labels and parameter-independent adaptive lags/randomization; no reset or hidden path information is given to the procedure. The fixed baseline subfamily is contained in the unknown-ordinary-rate envelope class, so a valid lower bound here is also a worst-case lower bound for that larger class.

Matrix-tree affinity gives π(b) analytic near zero. The EP identity is

    σ(b)=J(b)log(1/b)+G(b),
    J(b)=aπ_v(b)−bπ_u(b),
    G(b)=Σ_iπ_i(b)c_i+J(b)log a,

where J,G are analytic and J_0=J(0)=aπ_v(0)>0. For fixed K≥2, write c=b/K. Then exactly

    σ(c)−σ(b)=J(c)log K+[J(c)−J(b)]log(1/b)+G(c)−G(b).

The last two terms tend to zero since analytic differences are O(b) and b log(1/b)→0. Therefore the difference tends to J_0 log K.

For **any fixed** additive accuracy ε>0 choose a **fixed, ε-dependent** K≥2 with J_0 log K>4ε. For all sufficiently small b>0, the difference exceeds 2ε. This repairs the loose-accuracy limitation of the earlier b-versus-b/2 reduction. It does not claim a b-independent uniform separation for every b∈(0,1], nor constants independent of ε or Q_0.

An effective choice is available for rational ε and Q_0: take K=2^m with integer m≥1 and J_0m/2>4ε, using log 2>1/2. The earlier C_EP certificate supplies rational bounds M_J≥sup|J'| and M_G≥sup|G'| on [0,1]. One may take M_J=aK_v+1+K_u and M_G=Σ_iK_iC_i+M_J L_a. For b≤2^−k, k≥2, monotonicity of b log(1/b) below 1/e gives

    |σ(b/K)−σ(b)−J_0log K|
      ≤2^−k [M_J(m/K+k)+M_G].

This rational envelope tends to zero as k→∞. Search a dyadic threshold k making it smaller than J_0m/2−2ε>0; then the EP values are separated by more than 2ε throughout 0<b≤2^−k. The limiting claim is therefore not reliant on a diagnostic numerical grid.

## Testing implication, without rate recovery

Assume a procedure estimates σ within ε with probability ≥.9 under **both** hypotheses b and c=b/K. Their ε-neighborhoods are disjoint. Classify the EP output by the midpoint of the two true EP values. On each hypothesis, an ε-accurate output is correctly classified. Each testing error is thus ≤.1, so TV(P_b,P_c)≥.8. Binary data processing also gives KL(P_b||P_c)≥kl(.9||.1)=.8 log9. The tester is a proof device for a fixed pair; it is not a claimed physical inversion of the unknown EP function or a rate-learning reduction.

The procedure must succeed at both rates. If it is only promised accuracy over [β,2β], this argument may not apply for large ε, because its required K can exceed two. It applies to the global unknown-b procedure, or to constant-ratio windows [β,Kβ] with the same K-dependent target constants. An initial parameter-dependent stationary specimen would add information and is outside the premise.

## Integrable adaptive stopping: readings

Put α=(L−2)/(L−1), p=2−α=L/(L−1). The fixed-baseline all-endpoint bound is I_t,x(θ)≤C_Q θ^−α, for every lag t>0, visible starting state x and θ∈(0,1]. Along θ∈[b/K,b], the supremum is ≤C_Q K^α b^−α.

Square-root differentiation bounds a single conditional endpoint experiment by

    H²(kernel_b,kernel_c)≤(b−c)² sup I/4,

where H²=∫(√dP−√dQ)². At a shared retained history, algorithmic actions, randomization and stopping decisions have identical parameter-independent kernels and add no affinity deficit. Let μ_j,ν_j be subprobability laws of alive prefixes before read j+1. Finite-depth affinity telescoping and the almost-sure stopping limit give

    H²(P_b,P_c)≤[(b−c)²sup I/4] Σ_j∫√dμ_jdν_j
               ≤[(b−c)²sup I/4] √(E_bN E_cN).

The last step is Cauchy–Schwarz on the disjoint union of prefix levels; their total masses are the corresponding expected read counts. Neither independent decisions nor a deterministic sample count is assumed. If either expectation is infinite, the worst-case resource lower bound is immediate; otherwise finite means ensure the truncation tails vanish.

Since TV²≤H² and b−c=b(1−1/K),

    √(E_bN E_(b/K)N)
       ≥ 2.56/[C_Q K^α(1−1/K)²] · b^−p.

This is a **geometric-mean pair bound**, hence a lower bound on the larger expectation and on the window minimax expected count. It does not prove the displayed reading order separately at each true rate. Setting b=Kβ changes constants only and gives a β^−p window lower bound.

## Minimum lag qualifier

If every lag is ≥τ, the prerequisite sensor theorem requires 0<τ≤t_0≤1. Its small-lag endpoint bound is C_Q t^(2−L), and its separate large-lag bound is constant. Therefore

    sup_(t≥τ,x,θ∈[b/K,b]) I_t,x(θ)
      ≤C_Q min(K^α b^−α, τ^(−(L−2))).

The same affinity argument yields a pair geometric-mean lower bound, and hence a worst-case window lower bound, proportional to

    max{b^−p, b^−2 τ^(L−2)}.

For L=2 the τ exponent is zero. The expression is not asserted uniformly for arbitrarily large τ: large-lag information remains bounded, and the τ≤t_0 qualifier is load bearing. The topology exponent and sensor floor are supplied by the previous Fisher proof; this EP reduction changes only the two-point separation.

## Physical time, including random stopping

For the complete jump-resolved stopped path, the likelihood ratio under b versus c=b/K is

    log(dP_b/dP_c)=N_uv(T)log K−b(1−1/K)A_u(T),

where A_u is occupation time at u. Parameter-independent observation clocks and random seeds add no likelihood factor. Compensator accounting at integrable stopping gives E_bN_uv(T)=bE_bA_u(T), so

    KL(P_b^path||P_c^path)
      =b(log K−1+1/K)E_bA_u(T)
      ≤b(log K−1+1/K)E_bT.

Retained endpoint records are measurable functions of this stronger full-path experiment and its independent seeds. Binary testing data processing therefore gives

    E_bT≥.8log9/[b(log K−1+1/K)].

Here the lower bound holds under the larger-rate hypothesis itself, unlike the geometric-mean reading claim. If E_bT is infinite it is trivial; in the finite-mean nontrivial case localization and the compensator give the equality. For a finite worst-case time procedure both hypotheses stop almost surely, so the stopped path laws are well defined. This does not grant path observations to the actual endpoint learner.

Also T≥τN under each law. Combining this with the pair reading bound and the path-time bound yields a worst-case time lower bound of order

    max{b^−1, b^−2 τ^(L−1)}.

Replacing max by sum changes constants only. The extra τb^−p term from multiplying the first reading term is dominated by these two terms: compare τ with b^(1/(L−1)).

## What can now be adopted

The global shrinking-lag unknown-rate/unknown-scale EP upper theorem is minimax-optimal in its **polynomial reading and physical-time exponents**, up to its unresolved loglog overhead, for arbitrary fixed additive ε and fixed successful confidence, on sufficiently small constant-ratio windows with K depending on ε,Q_0. Its larger unknown-ordinary-rate class inherits this fixed-subfamily lower bound. This is the missing EP-specific separated-test justification; it is not an automatic transfer from rate estimation.

The fixed-minimum-lag lower terms are also valid under τ≤t_0, but do not close the fixed-lag **unknown-baseline** upper problem. The acquired-baseline protocol relies on lags tending to zero. For the known rational baseline and supplied [β,Kβ], the prior sensor upper can be extended directly with K-dependent endpoint-probability constants: choose t=max{τ,κβ^(1/(L−1))}, acquire a small fixed multiple-of-β rate error, and apply the effective C_EP modulus. Its same reading/time orders then give the known-baseline EP sensor tradeoff. No unknown-support, hidden-state, finite-latency, novelty or external-validation conclusion follows.
