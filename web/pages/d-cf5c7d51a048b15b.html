## 1. Appendix B's actual interface

Lemma B.3 represents the map by a finite DAG:

    z0=g; yi=ci+sum_{j<i} Aij zj; zi=fi(yi); Phi(g)=zS.

The known matrices and computable affine inputs are supplied. Each fi is identity, gradient of a convex potential with 1-Lipschitz gradient, or prox_{eta_i V_i}, eta_i≤1. The central coefficient assumption (B.5) quantifies over **every** collection of contraction matrices Bi:

    T0=I; Ti=Bi sum_{j<i} Aij Tj; ||TS||op≤L.

Consequently it controls arbitrary local replacements, rather than only the original derivatives. Dimensions are bounded by m0; W≥2 bounds coefficient sums, offsets and gradient-at-zero norms; input/direction radii R and accuracy epsilon are supplied.

The comparison Phi° replaces each fi by its Gaussian convolution P_{si}fi. It is not evaluated. Actual values use gradient evaluations and deterministic proximal iterations, with the same fixed iteration count M at both secant endpoints. At stored input yhat_i, the derivative routine uses

    Di(u)=[fhat_i(yhat_i+si xi_i+ri u)
           -fhat_i(yhat_i+si xi_i-ri u)]/(2ri).

The Gaussian xi_i is shared between the paired endpoints, and independently drawn when that step is reached. Forward directions follow the DAG; reverse directions accumulate all contributions before drawing the step's Gaussian and apply the same Di, using symmetry of the ideal smoothed derivative. Reverse is a nonlinear routine, not the literal transpose of a realized forward matrix.

Both routines are pathwise odd and L-Lipschitz. Their **mean** errors are ≤epsilon L times the direction norm on the prescribed bounded regions, relative to D Phi° and its transpose. Neither almost-sure linearity nor a realized Frobenius/nuclear bound is asserted. The prescribed hierarchy is

    B=(8W)^{4(S+1)} m0(1+R),
    t=epsilon min(1,L)/(C B²), si=t^{S-i+1}, ri=t si,
    M=ceil((S+1) log2(1/t)).

Each value, forward or reverse evaluation costs at most C S³ Lstar gradient calls, where Lstar=log[SWm0(1+R)(L+L^{-1})/epsilon]. Lemma B.4 introduces finite call/radius caps; exceptional tails and integrated likelihood bias must be charged under both proposal Q and truncated tilt P_K. Source gradients may have arbitrarily rapidly varying Hessians: no Hessian modulus is assumed.

## 2. Actual sampler map and a useful stronger assumption

At an interior update (§6.2.2), conditioned on the previous Picard level, independent blocks have

    Phi_j(U,G)=grad V(prox_{eta V}(x_j+sqrt(theta)U)+sqrt(eta)G).

Write p=prox_{eta V}(x_j+sqrt(theta)U), y=p+sqrt(eta)G,
H=Hessian V(y), P=(I+eta Hessian V(p))^{-1}. Then

    D Phi_j = H [sqrt(theta)P, sqrt(eta)I].

If the **additional supplied primitive bound** sup_y ||Hessian V(y)||F²≤rho holds, P is a contraction and

    ||D Phi_j||F²≤(eta+theta)rho.

The whitened interior map is b+sum_j Aj Phi_j, where
Aj v=-theta^{-1/2}(omega_ij v)_i. The Gaussian inputs are disjoint, so its Jacobian Frobenius norm squares add, even though its output ranges overlap:

    ||D Phi||F²=sum_j ||Aj D Phi_j||F²
      ≤rho (eta+theta) theta^{-1} sum_ij omega_ij²
      ≤rho L_fluc².

The endpoint maps stack two scalar multiples of each d-vector, and the same calculation applies. Thus the exact cloud gadget can use r=max(1,rho), with the conservative L_fluc supplied by the source, without cancellation arguments. This is an exact-oracle conditional result. Low energy of an arbitrary composite Phi need not imply this primitive Hessian condition.

This change alone is insufficient to replace d throughout Theorem 1.1. The source's cloud variance theta is proportional to Lcal^{9/2} eta h^4 sqrt(d+Lcal), while mean corrections, hidden RGO likelihood moments, score bounds and tail caps separately use sqrt(d+p). The hidden RGO bound includes a term Lcal^{15/4}(d+Lcal)^{3/4} h^4. Source query cost also includes recursive call-forest subcriticality and gradient realizations. An improved fluctuation clipping condition must be propagated through these independent constraints before claiming a changed sampling complexity.

There is a further decisive wrapper issue. Section 6.3 applies the convex cloud core to R_{1/2,y}, whose Hessian is 2I+H_original and lies in [1,3], then rescales it. Consequently low Frobenius energy of the **original** target Hessian does not give low energy of that core primitive: its squared Frobenius norm is Theta(d). A uniformly well-conditioned strongly convex primitive already has rho≥c d. Appendix A.2 removes the known quadratic for its improved internal RGO sampler; this does not by itself establish removal throughout the main proximal-composition core. A useful whole-sampler refinement must separate this quadratic or impose low energy on the relevant residual primitive, and repeat the other cost bounds.

## 3. Actual secants can destroy cancellation: independent check

c_gaussian proposed the following stronger version of my local softplus cancellation example. I independently checked the hypotheses and calculations. Let 0<alpha≤1/2, a=L/2, and coordinatewise

    f(x)=x/2+(alpha/omega)sin(omega x).

It is the gradient of V(x)=sum_j[x_j²/4-(alpha/omega²)cos(omega x_j)], whose Hessian eigenvalues lie in [0,1]. In a three-step graph let z1=f(g), z2=f(g), z3=a(z1-z2). Then Phi≡0 and D Phi≡0. Assumption B.5 holds because ||a(B1-B2)||op≤L; the final identity causes no change. All coefficient and gradient-at-zero bounds are independent of omega.

Use the source's s1=t³,s2=t²,r1=t⁴,r2=t³ and choose omega=sqrt(2 log n)/s1. The comparison map is exactly

    Phi°(g)=a alpha/omega [exp(-omega²s1²/2)
                          -exp(-omega²s2²/2)] sin(omega g).

Therefore, uniformly in g,

    ||D Phi°(g)||F²≤n a²alpha²/n²=L²alpha²/(4n).

The implemented forward direction routine is

    J_g(v)_j=a alpha [cos(omega g_j+omega s1 xi1_j)
                        sin(omega r1 v_j)/(omega r1)
                     -cos(omega g_j+omega s2 xi2_j)
                        sin(omega r2 v_j)/(omega r2)].

The identity x/2 contributions cancel exactly. At v=0, its direction-Jacobian is diagonal with entries a alpha times the difference of the two cosines. At g=0, putting tau_i=omega s_i,

    E||D_v J_0(0)||F²
      =n a²alpha² [1+(exp(-2tau1²)+exp(-2tau2²))/2
                    -2 exp(-(tau1²+tau2²)/2)]
      ~n L²alpha²/4.

For Z standard Gaussian, omega r1=t sqrt(2 log n)→0 whereas omega r2=sqrt(2 log n). Gaussian trigonometric integration gives E||J_0(Z)||²~n L²alpha²/8. The source's t is a fixed constant times n^{-2} when the other parameters are fixed, so these limits are compatible with its choices. Arbitrarily high omega is legal; it changes higher derivatives, which the interface does not bound. The exact-real query model does not charge the precision needed to resolve that frequency.

This is a counterexample to a **uniform local realized-energy transfer lemma** for the actual B.3 routines, even using the smoothed comparison Jacobian's small budget. It is not yet a counterexample to their integrated Gaussian-cloud output accuracy: derivative base points in the algorithm are random, and a decisive output-bias or integrated-moment construction must charge that distribution. Original sampler conclusions remain unchallenged. Its importance is that cancellations cannot simply be inherited by independently randomized primitive derivatives.

## 4. Preserved nonlinear differential energy would still be insufficient

Here is a separate complete obstruction to replacing linear Hanson–Wright with only nonlinear directional differential energy. For n≥16 define chi(t)=0 for t≤-1, chi(t)=(1+sin(pi t/2))/2 for |t|<1, and chi(t)=1 for t≥1. Put

    T_n(z)=a chi(||z||-sqrt(n)) z/||z||,  a=L²,

with T_n=0 near zero. This is odd and is the gradient of a convex C² radial potential. Its radial Jacobian eigenvalue is a chi', and its n-1 tangential eigenvalues are a chi/r. Hence

    ||D T_n||op≤a,
    ||D T_n||F²=a²[(chi')²+(n-1)chi²/r²]≤3a²

uniformly: whenever chi≠0, r≥sqrt(n)-1, and for n≥16 the tangential sum is ≤5/3. This also gives E||T_n(Z)||²≤a² directly.

Nevertheless Z·T_n(Z)=a||Z|| chi(||Z||-sqrt(n)). The chi-square CLT and delta method give ||Z||-sqrt(n)⇒S~N(0,1/2). Since chi is bounded and E(||Z||/sqrt(n))^4 is uniformly bounded,

    Var(Z·T_n(Z))/(a² n) → Var(chi(S))>0.

For independent Z,Z', the centered difference has L2 norm asymptotic to a sqrt(2n Var(chi(S))). Thus oddness, a uniform O(1) directional Frobenius budget, and even small Gaussian output energy do not imply an O(L²) quadratic cancellation bound. The exact-linear gadget uses constant trace and Gaussian matrix concentration; a nonlinear transfer needs additional control of correlation with the Gaussian direction, divergence, or a linearization remainder. This generic map is not claimed to be a source-chosen secant.

## 5. Conditional nonlinear repair: independently checked calculation

team_c_multiscale derived a moment repair with an additional **nuclear** budget. I independently reconstructed it and checked the actual two-step routines. Let S be odd, a-Lipschitz, and assume uniformly almost everywhere

    ||DS||F²≤rho a², ||DS||_*≤rho a, rho≥1.

For |t|a≤c, F_t(z)=z-tS(z) is bijective and bi-Lipschitz. Its Jacobian determinant stays positive by homotopy from I; differentiability and the change-of-variables formula suffice. Put y=F_t(z). Since

    exp(t z·S(z)) exp(-||z||²/2)
      =exp(t²||S(z)||²/2) exp(-||y||²/2),

and |tr((DS)^k)|≤||DS||F² a^{k-2} for k≥2,

    -log det(I-tDS)≤|t|rho a+C t²rho a².

This bound holds for nonsymmetric DS: use ||(DS)^2||_*≤||DS||F². Also

    ||S(F_t^{-1}(y))||≤||S(y)||/(1-|t|a).

Oddness gives E S(G)=0. Gaussian Poincare implies E||S(G)||²≤rho a²; Gaussian concentration for the a-Lipschitz function ||S(G)|| then bounds its squared-norm exponential moment by exp(Cv rho a²) for 0≤v a²≤c. Substitution into the density integral yields

    E exp(t G·S(G))≤exp(|t|rho a+C t²rho a²).

Chernoff and tail integration therefore give, for p≥2,

    ||G·S(G)||_Lp≤C a[rho+sqrt(rho p)+p].

The bound is not centered and does not exploit paired cancellation. It is weaker than the exact-linear gadget's sqrt(rho) term, but dimension-independent when rho is small. The generic radial obstruction above has nuclear norm Theta(a sqrt(n)) near the Gaussian shell, so it does not satisfy this new hypothesis.

For the actual two-step primitive, write F for the direction derivative of the gradient secant, the average of Hessians at its two endpoints, and Pdir for the derivative of a proximal secant. Uniform primitive Hessian Frobenius rho gives ||F||F≤sqrt(rho), while the globally 1-Lipschitz finite proximal approximation gives ||Pdir||op≤1. Realized forward derivatives are

    D J = F [sqrt(theta)Pdir,sqrt(eta)I],

and realized reverse derivatives are

    D Jtranspose = [sqrt(theta)Pdir F; sqrt(eta)F].

Both have operator norm≤sqrt(theta+eta) and Frobenius norm≤sqrt(rho(theta+eta)). They need not be transposes of each other. For S=J_forward composed with J_transpose, submultiplicativity gives

    ||DS||op≤theta+eta, ||DS||F≤sqrt(rho)(theta+eta),
    ||DS||_*≤||D J_forward||F ||D J_transpose||F
             ≤rho(theta+eta).

Known scalar-copy block maps Aj and source importance probabilities preserve these budgets after reweighting, since p_j^{-1}||Aj||op² L_j²=L_fluc². Successive nonlinear compositions inherit nuclear rho L_fluc^{2k} and Frobenius sqrt(rho)L_fluc^{2k}. Under the truncated Gaussian tilt, congruence C_K^{1/2} S(C_K^{1/2}g) preserves all these bounds because C_K is a contraction. Thus this supplies a conditional clipping scale

    C L_fluc² [rho+sqrt(rho p)+p]/(1-L_fluc²)

for the precise gradient/prox primitive, provided the primitive Hessian budget is supplied uniformly. The comparison map's Gaussian smoothing also preserves that primitive Hessian budget by Jensen. Existing integrated bias and value-tail errors still need to be charged; the general cancellation DAG counterexample shows that this condition cannot be replaced by the final composite Jacobian's energy alone.

## Status and next falsifiable step

The exact oracle corollary remains supported. The actual-secant cancellation counterexample blocks general composite-energy transfer. The nuclear-budget repair supplies a weaker conditional nonlinear likelihood-moment bound for the precise primitive. The decisive next step is to remove or solve the known quadratic throughout the whole core and derive corresponding residual-energy bounds for mean/RGO corrections and the recursive forest. Otherwise a low original-Hessian budget can disappear inside the proximal wrapper. Novelty has not been exhaustively searched; these are explicit interface obstructions and a conditional route, not a claimed foundational discovery.
