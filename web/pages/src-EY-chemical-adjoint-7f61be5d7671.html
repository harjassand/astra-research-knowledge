CRAY GENESIS — Result: rate-free chemical control, with a decisive limit
I derived two explicit operations that cancel unknown elementary reaction-rate constants: a finite chemical-state relocation and a physical adjoint construction. The more ambitious adjoint candidate did not survive the full matched-task test as a demonstrated breakthrough. Its favourable state-design case can already be handled by the simpler relocation operation, while realistic enzyme saturation breaks the proposed physical compilation.
The useful result is therefore more specific: a constructive way to eliminate certain kinetic inputs, an exact criterion for when a chemical experiment returns the required sensitivity information, and counterexamples identifying what cannot be omitted. No new practically consequential scientific capability, real-world speedup, or historical priority has been established.

1. The scientific task and its actual obstruction
I concentrated on changing a driven chemical system’s catalyst allocation to improve a selected steady-state output without first identifying its complete kinetic model.
The distinction between computing with a model and acquiring the model matters here. Automated reaction-discovery systems such as Chemoton already perform elementary-step searches based on quantum chemistry. Once a kinetic mechanism is supplied, tools such as KPP already provide tangent-linear and adjoint methods. Consequently, “compute all sensitivities with one adjoint” is not a new capability by itself. The potential advance would be obtaining the needed sensitivity without paying for a sufficiently accurate kinetic model first. arXiv
I developed three different approaches to this obstruction:
Mechanism	Information it attempts to eliminate	Outcome
Finite flux-preserving relocation	Absolute rate constants and individual baseline current magnitudes	An exact cancellation for mass-action kinetics; closely related to established enzyme-cost optimisation
Thermodynamic physical adjoint	The fitted kinetic Jacobian and its numerical inversion	An explicit physical compilation, but only under an additional exact condition
Free-enzyme occupancy control	Hidden catalytic-state dynamics and saturation	A restricted algebraic rescue; its sensing, control and protein-cost requirements remain unresolved


The precise output established below is a state-dependent objective \(\Phi(c)\), such as a selected concentration or concentration ratio. This must not be silently promoted to a result about production throughput, flux selectivity or industrial productivity.
2. First operation: move a chemical steady state without learning its rates
Consider reversible reaction pairs with internal reactant and product vectors \(y_r,y'_r\), and stoichiometric changes
\[
s_r=y'_r-y_r.
\]
Each adjustable positive gate \(e_r\) multiplies both reaction directions. With external reservoirs held fixed, the elementary rates have the form
\[
v_r^+=e_r\alpha_r c^{y_r},
\qquad
v_r^-=e_r\beta_r c^{y'_r},
\]
where \(\alpha_r,\beta_r\) are unknown. Suppose a positive measured concentration \(c\) is stationary:
\[
\sum_r s_rj_r=0,
\qquad
j_r=v_r^+-v_r^-.
\]
Define the dimensionless affinity
\[
A_r=\log\frac{v_r^+}{v_r^-}.
\]
Under the assumed thermodynamic model, this can be obtained from concentrations and equilibrium information without knowing the absolute rates.
The cancellation
Choose a desired target
\[
c'=c\odot e^h.
\]
For a channel with nonzero current, set
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e'_r}{e_r}
=
\rho_r(h)
=
\frac{1-e^{-A_r}}
     {e^{y_r^\top h}-e^{-A_r+y_r'^\top h}}.
}
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
j'_r=j_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_r s_rj'_r=0,
\]
so the target is a steady state of the modified system.
Neither the unknown absolute rate constants nor the individual current magnitudes appear in the control recipe.
The conditions are substantive. The new affinity must retain the original current direction so that the required gate remains positive. A zero-current channel requires a separate treatment: retaining zero current imposes \(s_r^\top h=0\), rather than evaluating a \(0/0\) expression.
A fixed total gate budget does not destroy the construction
Let the original budget be
\[
E=\sum_r\omega_re_r,
\]
and define
\[
B(h)=\sum_r\omega_re_r\rho_r(h).
\]
Then the controls
\[
\boxed{
e'_r=\frac{E}{B(h)}\,e_r\rho_r(h)
}
\]
preserve the budget exactly. At the target concentration, every current becomes
\[
j'_r=\frac{E}{B(h)}j_r,
\]
which still satisfies stationarity.
This produces an important—and initially adverse—consequence for the research direction:
When all relevant currents are nonzero, every target in the same affinity-sign region can already be converted directly into a steady-state gate allocation under this budget-only model. An adjoint experiment is not needed to construct that allocation.

This is a steady-state design result, not a guarantee that the laboratory system will converge to the target from an arbitrary preparation.
Why this is not being claimed as a new optimisation principle
Orienting a nonzero-current reaction so that \(A_r>0\),
\[
\log\rho_r(h)
=
-y_r^\top h+\log(1-e^{-A_r})
-\log\!\left(1-e^{-(A_r-s_r^\top h)}\right).
\]
This is convex on its feasible domain. Consequently, the corresponding positive weighted gate cost is convex.
That structure falls directly alongside enzyme cost minimisation, which already optimises enzyme requirements in logarithmic metabolite coordinates using thermodynamic and kinetic information. The cancellation above changes which inputs need to be supplied in the elementary mass-action case; it does not establish a fundamentally new convex-optimisation method. The fair comparator is the specialised formulation with the same reference-state information, not brute-force kinetic reconstruction. PLOS
3. Second operation: make a chemical system return its own adjoint
The more ambitious construction changes the experiment itself.
Let \(z=(c,u)\) include internal concentrations and externally maintained reservoirs. Reaction \(r\) has full reactant and product vectors \(a_r,b_r\), with internal parts \(y_r,y'_r\).
Assume a positive reference \(q\) satisfying elementary detailed balance:
\[
k_r^+q^{a_r}=k_r^-q^{b_r}=\kappa_r.
\]
The absolute constants \(\kappa_r\) remain unknown. Writing \(w=z/q\),
\[
v_r^+=e_r\kappa_rw^{a_r},
\qquad
v_r^-=e_r\kappa_rw^{b_r}.
\]
An explicit physical compilation
Set
\[
\boxed{
z^\dagger=\frac{q^2}{z},
\qquad
e_r^\dagger=e_r w^{a_r+b_r}.
}
\]
All operations on concentrations are componentwise.
Then
\[
v_r^{\dagger,+}
=
e_r\kappa_rw^{a_r+b_r}w^{-a_r}
=
v_r^-,
\]
and similarly
\[
v_r^{\dagger,-}=v_r^+.
\]
Thus the construction reverses every elementary current without knowing its absolute kinetic constant.
If \(c\) is stationary, the internal part
\[
d=\frac{q_{\rm internal}^2}{c}
\]
is stationary in the conjugate reactor.
This does not reverse thermodynamics. Both the current and its affinity reverse:
\[
j_r^\dagger=-j_r,
\qquad
A_r^\dagger=-A_r.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_rj_r^\dagger A_r^\dagger
=
\sum_rj_rA_r\geq0.
\]
The paired operating states have the same dimensionless dissipation. Reservoir maintenance and preparation still consume resources.
The crucial question: does reversing currents implement the transpose?
Let
\[
J=DF(c),\qquad H=DF^\dagger(d),
\qquad C=\operatorname{diag}(c),\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(d).
\]
The exact identity is
\[
\boxed{
CJ^\top=HD+M,
}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
M=\sum_rj_r
\left(y'_ry_r'^\top-y_ry_r^\top\right).
}
\]
The matrix \(M\) is the obstruction. It is not generally zero at a species-balanced steady state.
The derivation is short enough to expose the assumptions:
\[
JC=\sum_rs_r
\left(v_r^+y_r^\top-v_r^-y_r'^\top\right),
\]
while the swapped rates give
\[
HD=\sum_rs_r
\left(v_r^-y_r^\top-v_r^+y_r'^\top\right).
\]
Subtracting the second expression from the transpose of the first produces the stated \(M\), reaction by reaction.
Current reversal alone is not an adjoint.
When the obstruction vanishes
A sufficient condition is complex balance: every internal reaction complex has equal total incoming and outgoing reaction flow. Grouping \(M\) by complexes then makes every coefficient vanish. This permits nonzero circulating currents; it does not require each pair to be at equilibrium.
There is also a purely stoichiometric sufficient test. Let \(S\) have columns \(s_r\), and let \(R\) have columns
\[
\operatorname{vec}_{\rm sym}
\left(y'_ry_r'^\top-y_ry_r^\top\right).
\]
If
\[
\ker S\subseteq\ker R,
\]
then every stationary current vector necessarily has \(M=0\). This can be checked by exact linear algebra on the supplied reaction list. It avoids assuming favourable unknown currents, but it does not establish that the reaction list correctly describes a real experiment.
The adjoint readout
Let
\[
a=\nabla_c\Phi(c).
\]
Add a small calibrated constant source \(\epsilon Ca\) to the conjugate reactor and measure its nearby equilibrium \(d(\epsilon)\). Differentiating the equilibrium equation gives
\[
H d'(0)=-Ca.
\]
When \(M=0\),
\[
\boxed{
\lambda=D^{-1}d'(0)
}
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
J^\top\lambda=-a.
}
\]
The experiment has supplied the decision-specific adjoint without fitting \(J\).
The general idea of obtaining derivatives from physical dynamics is prior art. Hamiltonian echo backpropagation predates this work, and the August 2026 preprint Unifying Physical Backpropagation explicitly treats broader intertwining conditions. The potentially distinctive contribution here is the chemical concentration-and-gate recipe and its exact defect—not the invention of physical backpropagation. Historical priority for this particular combination remains unresolved. APS Journals
4. A constructive direction that needs no current magnitudes
The adjoint can be used without reconstructing the numerical gradient in full.
For \(\theta_r=\log e_r\),
\[
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\theta_r}
=
j_rs_r^\top\lambda.
\]
Although \(j_r\) is unknown, its sign agrees with that of \(A_r\). Define
\[
u_r=A_rs_r^\top\lambda.
\]
Then the quasistatic update \(\dot\theta_r=u_r\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
=
\sum_rj_rA_r(s_r^\top\lambda)^2\geq0.
}
\]
There is also an exact budget-preserving version:
\[
\bar u=
\frac{\sum_r\omega_re_ru_r}{\sum_r\omega_re_r},
\qquad
\dot\theta_r=u_r-\bar u.
\]
The budget remains constant. The objective derivative remains nonnegative because
\[
\sum_r\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\theta_r}
=
\lambda^\top F(c)=0.
\]
A finite implementation is
\[
e_r^{\rm new}
=
\frac{E\,e_r e^{hu_r}}
{\sum_s\omega_se_s e^{hu_s}}.
\]
It preserves the budget exactly; sufficiently small steps improve the objective whenever the directional derivative is strictly positive. A usable step-size guarantee still requires curvature information or measured backtracking.
These qualifications are essential: all dynamics must be included in the collectively scalable gates; the objective must have no direct gate dependence; and a gate budget is not automatically a total enzyme-protein budget. Uncontrolled dilution, fixed inflows or saturating enzyme pools change the problem.
5. Two counterexamples that prevent an inflated claim
A stable, thermodynamically consistent system can give the wrong optimisation sign
Consider the reversible pairs
\[
0\rightleftharpoons2A,\qquad
A\rightleftharpoons B,\qquad
B\rightleftharpoons0.
\]
At \(c=(1,1)\), take forward fluxes \((2,3,3)\) and backward fluxes \((1,1,1)\). The net currents \((1,2,2)\) satisfy species balance.
The native and conjugate Jacobians are
\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
-7&1\\
3&-4
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
-9&3\\
1&-4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both are stable, but
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For
\[
\Phi=A+\frac35B,
\]
the true log-gate derivative for the \(A\rightleftharpoons B\) catalyst is
\[
-\frac6{125}=-0.048.
\]
The naive conjugate-adjoint prediction is
\[
\frac2{55}\approx0.03636.
\]
The naive construction recommends increasing a catalyst that should be decreased. This was checked with exact rational arithmetic.
Identical baseline observations do not remove unknown saturation
Now consider reversible saturating rates
\[
v^+=\frac{e\kappa s}{Z(s,p)},
\qquad
v^-=\frac{e\kappa p}{Z(s,p)},
\]
with
\[
Z(s,p)=1+\frac{s}{K_S}+\frac{p}{K_P}.
\]
At \((s,p,e)=(2,1,1)\), both models below have exactly the same one-way fluxes \((2,1)\), concentrations, gate and equilibrium constant:
Hidden saturation parameters	\(\kappa\)
\(K_S=K_P=1\)	\(4\)
\(K_S=K_P=10\)	\(13/10\)


The mass-action transformation predicts a conjugate current of \(-1\). The actual currents are
\[
-\frac85
\quad\text{and}\quad
-\frac{26}{23}.
\]
Thus even knowing the baseline one-way fluxes does not determine the transformed response.
Correcting the transformed gate would require the new information
\[
\frac{Z(z^\dagger)}{Z(z)}.
\]
Even that is insufficient for the adjoint: derivatives of the saturation denominator introduce additional terms into \(M\). A baseline fit or a single occupancy ratio does not establish the required response law.
6. The attempted saturation rescue—and why it is not yet the missing capability
One intervention can restore a restricted effective mass-action law: control free active enzyme rather than total enzyme.
Suppose an enzyme has one exposed free state, hidden states \(h\), and substrate/product binding only from the free state. With free-enzyme concentration \(f\) clamped,
\[
\dot h=Qh+f(b_s s+b_p p),
\]
where the hidden transition matrix \(Q\) is independent of \(s,p\). At steady state,
\[
h=-fQ^{-1}(b_s s+b_p p),
\]
so the net product current has the form
\[
j=f(\alpha s-\beta p).
\]
The hidden state count need not appear in the final rate law. This steady-state elimination does not require a fast-timescale approximation.
But it does not complete the mission:
- Every enzyme needs a sufficiently accurate free-state measurement and control channel.
- Total enzyme cost is \(f+\mathbf1^\top h\), not \(f\), so the previous protein-budget interpretation is lost.
- This restricted class already admits low-parameter enzyme descriptions through established linear elimination. A fair baseline need not identify every hidden microscopic rate. arXiv
The remaining problem is therefore not simply “generalise the proof to more hidden states.” It is find a physically affordable interface that exposes the needed response while retaining realistic saturation, coupled resources and meaningful production constraints.

8. Complete accounting changes the comparison
The tempting headline would be “all catalyst sensitivities from two experiments.” That omits most of the cost.
Model acquisition. The reaction list and elementary rate-law validity are still required. Missing channels can alter both stationarity and the transpose defect. I have supplied no justified bound on unobserved chemistry.
Thermodynamic calibration. The full reference \(q\) must be measured or independently justified. Equilibration, low-abundance species, nonideal activities and inaccessible reference conditions are not free.
Actuation. Every required reservoir and gate must reach its conjugate setting. The gate ratio obeys
\[
\log\frac{e_r^\dagger}{e_r}
=
(a_r+b_r)^\top\log(z/q),
\]
so feasible dynamic range does not follow from low molecularity alone.
Readout and precision. A central estimate requires two perturbed equilibria and an \(n\)-component concentration readout at each. Assuming each reading has absolute error at most \(\sigma\), and a justified bound \(\|d'''(\epsilon)\|_\infty\leq K_3\),
\[
\|\widehat\lambda-\lambda\|_\infty
\leq
\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon d_{\min}}
+
\frac{K_3\epsilon^2}{6d_{\min}},
\]
before calibration, incomplete-settling and model errors. Smaller perturbations amplify measurement error. Agreement between two perturbation sizes does not certify the required derivative bound.
Time and physical work. Stability does not give a useful settling-time bound. Slow reactions, sample preparation, reservoir maintenance, discarded replicates and sensor calibration can dominate.
Computation and output. Once the inputs and validity conditions are acquired, the controller uses sparse stoichiometric arithmetic. That excludes structural verification and experimental acquisition. Issuing \(m\) actuator decisions still requires \(m\)-scale output work.
The matched competitors are therefore stronger than independent finite differences. Digital adjoints already avoid one simulation per parameter. Simultaneous perturbation methods already obtain a stochastic gradient estimate from two objective measurements per iteration. The proposed method has different information, noise and hardware costs—not an automatic experiment-count advantage. kpp.readthedocs.io
Most decisively, the finite-relocation construction already solves the favourable budget-only state-placement task within the same affinity region. That undermines the need for the more elaborate physical adjoint before laboratory costs are even considered.
9. What this establishes—and the precise remaining frontier
The retained mathematical result is:
Unknown elementary kinetic constants can cancel from a physically specified control transformation. The transformed response is the needed adjoint exactly when an explicit second-complex-moment defect vanishes. Under that condition, thermodynamic affinities suffice to construct a local budget-preserving ascent direction without measuring current magnitudes.

The investigation also establishes two boundaries by counterexample: species balance and stable current reversal are insufficient; baseline thermodynamics and flux observations do not make saturation irrelevant.
A decisive experiment would first test the response identity itself. Writing native and conjugate static response operators as
\[
R_N=-J^{-1},\qquad R_C=-H^{-1},
\]
the prediction is
\[
R_C=DR_N^\top C^{-1}.
\]
Equivalently, paired source directions \(v,w\) must satisfy
\[
w^\top R_Nv
=
v^\top D^{-1}R_CCw.
\]
This gives a concrete falsification experiment without fitting the whole Jacobian. A few directions can disprove the law, although they cannot certify it universally.
The next consequential target would have to go beyond the favourable problem already solved by relocation: independently capped catalyst resources, coupled enzyme pools, altered flux selectivity, or realistic saturating pathways. It would also need an affordable physical implementation and a matched comparison that charges its calibration and preparation.
The present result removes an identifiable kinetic-input requirement in a defined model class. It does not yet make previously inaccessible, consequential scientific knowledge obtainable. The research packet preserves the construction and its failures without treating that missing capability as an assumed input.
