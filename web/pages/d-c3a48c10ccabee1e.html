RESULT OF THE DEEPER AUDIT

The local cocycle-to-entropy step used in revision 001 is valid for a time-\u03c4-invariant conditional volume measure supported almost everywhere on U={X\u22600}, even when X has zeros outside the code set and log|X| is not integrable. ZERO-SET ENDPOINT LEMMA

Let X be smooth and divergence-free on a closed Riemannian 3-manifold, let T=\u03c6_\u03c4 with \u03c4>0, and let \u03b7 be any T-invariant probability with \u03b7(U)=1. On U put r=|X|, e=X/r, and q=\u27e8(Def X)e,e\u27e9. Since Def X is bounded on compact M, q is bounded on U. The identity q=X(log r) gives

  g(x):=\u222b_0^\u03c4 q(\u03c6_t x)dt = log r(Tx)-log r(x),

and g is bounded even if log r is not \u03b7-integrable. By Birkhoff, n^{-1}\u2211_{j<n}g(T^j x) converges for \u03b7-a.e. x to a T-invariant value a(x). Telescoping identifies this sum with n^{-1}(log r(T^n x)-log r(x)). A positive a(x) is impossible because r is bounded above on M. A negative a(x) would imply r(T^n x)\u21920 on the invariant set E={a<0}. The normalized restriction \u03b7_E is T-invariant, while r>0 \u03b7_E-a.e. For every \u03b5>0, dominated convergence gives \u03b7_E{r(T^n x)<\u03b5}\u2192\u03b7(E), but invariance makes the left side equal to \u03b7_E{r<\u03b5} for each n. Taking \u03b5\u21920 contradicts \u03b7_E(U)=1. Therefore a=0 almost everywhere, and

  [log r(T^n x)-log r(x)]/n \u2192 0.

This proves the needed endpoint cancellation and also that the X-line exponent is zero, with no integrability assumption on log r.

For completeness, the normal quotient cocycle Q_t=P_{\u03c6_t x}D\u03c6_t|_{e(x)^\u22a5} satisfies

  log||Q_t(x)|| \u2264 \u222b_0^t s(\u03c6_u x)du - (1/2)log(r(\u03c6_t x)/r(x)),

where s is the nonnegative eigenvalue of the trace-free part of P(Def X)P on e^\u22a5. The observable s_\u03c4=\u222b_0^\u03c4s\u2218\u03c6_tdt is bounded. Dividing the estimate at t=n\u03c4 by n and applying Oseledets and Birkhoff shows that the largest quotient exponent \u03c7 is at most the Birkhoff average of s_\u03c4. Since div X=0, the full derivative has determinant one and the X-line Jacobian is r(T^n x)/r(x); hence the two quotient exponents sum to zero. The spectrum is therefore \u03c7,0,-\u03c7 with \u03c7\u22650. Ruelle's inequality for the C^1 map T and invariant probability \u03b7 now yields

  h_\u03b7(T) \u2264 \u222b s_\u03c4 d\u03b7.

This argument needs only T-invariance of \u03b7, not ergodicity, absolute continuity of \u03b7, or invariance of the code set under intermediate times. A trajectory starting in U cannot hit a zero of X at finite time, by uniqueness for the smooth ODE.

ORBIT-SWEEP AND POWER, WITH ALL FACTORS

NONINTEGRABLE-SPEED DIAGNOSTIC

The integrability caveat is real, not just formal. On the flat three-torus, let

  f(y)=exp(-1/sin^2(\u03c0y)) for y\notin Z, and f(y)=0 for y\in Z,
  X=f(y)\u2202_x.

The periodic extension is smooth and flat at the zero tori; div X=0. The zero set has volume zero, but log|X|=-1/sin^2(\u03c0y) is not integrable in absolute value near y=0. Nevertheless y is constant along trajectories, so log r(\u03c6_t x)-log r(x)=0 exactly and q=0 on U. This example does not have positive entropy; it demonstrates why a proof must not silently invoke Birkhoff for log r. The bounded-q telescoping argument above handles it.
