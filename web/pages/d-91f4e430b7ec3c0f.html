## Lead A: a graph-domain repair for distributional Hessians

### Exact question and interface

Let `(X,d,m)` be complete, separable, full support, finite measure on bounded sets, and unreduced `RCD(K,N)`, `1<N<infinity`. Let `F` be bounded and globally Lipschitz and `G` bounded continuous. Test functions are bounded globally Lipschitz Laplacian-domain functions with Sobolev Laplacian. For compact test gradients `Y=grad g` and nonnegative compact Lipschitz `h`, suppose

\[
H_F(Y,Y)(h):=-\int\operatorname{div}(hY)\langle\nabla F,Y\rangle\,dm
-\int h\langle\nabla F,\nabla_Y Y\rangle\,dm
\leq\int hG|Y|^2\,dm.
\]

The authors' Theorem 1.1 claims that **every** constant-speed minimizing geodesic `gamma` of length `ell` satisfies `(F o gamma)'' <= ell^2 G o gamma` distributionally, including geodesics in null sets and constant paths. Their Proposition 4.1 first obtains `CD(K-lambda G,infinity)` for the unchanged metric with `m_lambda=exp(lambda F)m`.

### Why the domain is the load-bearing issue

A bounded Lipschitz drift `V` preserves weak gradients and Sobolev spaces under `m_V=exp(V)m`, and integration by parts gives

\[
D(\Delta_V)=D(\Delta),\qquad \Delta_Vu=\Delta u+\Gamma(V,u).
\]

The graph norms are equivalent: the energy identity bounds `||grad u||_2` by `||u||_2+||Delta u||_2`, and the drift is bounded by `Lip(V)||grad u||_2`. This **does not** preserve the test class, because `Gamma(V,g)` need not be Sobolev. The example `V=-|x_1|`, `g=x_1` near the origin gives weighted Laplacian `-sign(x_1)`. Any proof that simply substitutes original test functions into the weighted test class fails at this point.

The useful repair in Lemma 3.1 is resolvent regularity without presuming weighted curvature. The original heat flow supplies

\[
\|R_a h\|_p\le a^{-1}\|h\|_p,
\qquad\|\nabla R_a h\|_p\le C_Ka^{-1/2}\|h\|_p,
\qquad p=2,\infty,
\]

for sufficiently large `a`, with `R_a=(a-Delta)^{-1}`. On bounded Sobolev functions with bounded gradient, the map `z -> R_a(h+Gamma(V,z))` is a contraction once `C_K Lip(V)/sqrt(a)` is small. Its fixed point is the unique weak solution of `(a-Delta_V)z=h`. Thus a bounded generator-domain function with bounded weighted Laplacian is Lipschitz. This supplies the precise multipliers required for the weighted Bochner inequality.

### Reconstruction of the closure

Set `V=lambda F`, `w=exp(V)`, `q=Gamma(g)/2`, `b=Gamma(V,g)`, and let `xi>=0` be bounded with bounded weighted Laplacian. The previous argument makes `xi` Lipschitz. The integrated weighted expression is

\[
B_V(g,\xi)=-\int w\Gamma(\xi,q)\,dm
+\int w[\xi\Delta_Vg+\Gamma(\xi,g)]\Delta_Vg\,dm.
\]

Expanding the products and using `div(w xi grad g)=w[Gamma(xi,g)+xi Delta_Vg]` yields the exact identity

\[
B_V(g,\xi)-B_0(g,w\xi)=-H_V(\nabla g,\nabla g)(w\xi).
\]

Original Bochner and the weak Hessian inequality imply `B_V >= integral (K-lambda G) xi Gamma(g) dm_V`. Crucially, the left side can be rewritten as

\[
\tfrac12\int(\Delta_V\xi)\Gamma(g)\,dm_V
+\int(\Delta_Vg)\Gamma(\xi,g)\,dm_V
+\int\xi(\Delta_Vg)^2\,dm_V.
\]

All three terms are continuous in the generator graph norm; no derivative of the nonsmooth drift is required. The common compact original core therefore extends the inequality to `D(Delta_V)`. Only after closure does one impose `Delta_Vg in W^{1,2}` and integrate by parts to obtain the actual weighted `BE_2(K-lambda G,infinity)` condition. The cited constant-curvature equivalence first supplies a weighted `RCD(K_V,infinity)` structure; Braun–Habermann–Sturm's Theorem 1.1 then applies to bounded continuous variable curvature. Its Definition 1.4 matches exactly the multiplier domain just obtained.

### Reconstruction of the all-geodesic passage

Deng's nonbranching result for finite-dimensional RCD spaces makes every **strict interior subsegment** of a prescribed minimizing geodesic the unique minimizer between its endpoints: rerouting an interior portion would create two full minimizers agreeing on a nontrivial initial interval. This is stronger than merely having unique optimal transport almost everywhere.

Approximate the two subsegment endpoints by normalized ball measures. Properness and unique interior routing make all joining minimizing paths concentrate on that subsegment. Weighted entropy has the exact decomposition

\[
\mathrm{Ent}_{m_\lambda}(\mu)=\mathrm{Ent}_m(\mu)-\lambda\int F\,d\mu.
\]

Dividing the variable-curvature entropy inequality by `lambda` gives a lower chord bound for `F`, with the Green-kernel correction from `G`. Choose `lambda` faster than the endpoint entropies; intermediate entropy is bounded below on the common compact support. This kills the entropy error. Apply on every strict subinterval, rather than assuming uniqueness between the original endpoints. The resulting corrected function lies above every chord, giving its distributional second-derivative upper bound.

### Our deduction A1: a finite-parameter inequality with its missing effective input

This deduction assumes the authors' Proposition 4.1 and the standard variable `CD` entropy formula; it does not assume Theorem 1.1. Fix a unique interior subsegment `tau` of length `ell`, and `epsilon>0`. Let `Q` contain both endpoint epsilon-balls and all minimizing paths between them. Define

\[
\omega(\epsilon)=\sup_\gamma\sup_{0\le s\le1}d(\gamma_s,\tau_s),
\]

where the supremum is over those endpoint-constrained minimizing paths. Properness and uniqueness imply `omega(epsilon)->0`, but provide no rate. Put `L=ell+2epsilon`, `M=||G||_infinity`, `L_F=Lip(F)`, and let `omega_G` be the uniform continuity modulus of `G` on `Q`. For any `lambda>0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\tau_t)\ge{}&(1-t)F(\tau_0)+tF(\tau_1)
-\ell^2\int_0^1 g(s,t)G(\tau_s)\,ds-\mathcal E,\\
\mathcal E={}&L_F[\omega(\epsilon)+\epsilon]
+\frac{t(1-t)}2\left[L^2\omega_G(\omega(\epsilon))
+(4\ell\epsilon+4\epsilon^2)M\right]\\
&+\frac1\lambda\left[
\log\frac{m(Q)}{\min\{m(B_\epsilon(\tau_0)),m(B_\epsilon(\tau_1))\}}
+\frac{K_-L^2}{8}\right],
\end{aligned}
\]

where `g(s,t)=min(s,t)-st`, `K_-=max(-K,0)`. All quantities must be supplied or certified; `Q` has positive finite measure.

**Derivation.** Under the weighted entropy plan, set `A_t=integral F dmu_t`, `E_t=Ent_m(mu_t)`, `C_t=integral integral g |gamma'|^2`, and `D_t=integral integral g G(gamma_s)|gamma'|^2`. The entropy inequality rearranges to

`A_t >= chord(A_0,A_1)-D_t+[E_t-chord(E_0,E_1)+KC_t]/lambda`.

Here `E_t>=-log m(Q)`, `E_i=-log m(B_epsilon(tau_i))`, `C_t<=L^2/8`. Thus the bracket is at least the negative of the last displayed numerator. Replacing `A_t` by `F(tau_t)` costs at most `L_F omega`; replacing the endpoint chord costs at most `L_F epsilon`. Since each joining length differs from `ell` by at most `2epsilon`, the error in `D_t` is at most the middle displayed term, using `integral g=t(1-t)/2`. This proves the inequality for finite parameters.

This is a quantitative proof-transfer statement, not a numerical certification algorithm. For noncollapsed n-dimensional volume control, the entropy numerator grows at most as `n log(1/epsilon)+O(1)` on a fixed compact region. Making just this term at most `delta` permits `lambda=O((n log(1/epsilon)+1)/delta)`. But then the weighting has density ratio `exp(lambda osc_Q F)`, and the given heat estimates certify resolvent contraction by choosing `a` at least a fixed multiple of `lambda^2 Lip(F)^2`, up to the original heat constants and their large-a threshold. This is a sufficient parameter cost for the displayed proof, not a lower bound for every possible method. Shifting `F` changes neither density ratio nor this drift scale. Moreover, `omega(epsilon)` and the heat/cutoff constants are not acquired by finitely checking the weak Hessian. These are the decisive obstacles to claiming inexpensive effective certificates from the limiting theorem.

## Lead B: unfolding degenerating harmonic values through directional energy

### Exact question and author claims

The coordinate paper assumes exact reference measure `H^n`, integer `n>=2`, unreduced `RCD(K,n)`, and that every pointed tangent at the prescribed point `p` is Euclidean. Its Theorem 1.1 claims an ambient-open neighborhood bi-Lipschitz to an open subset of `R^n`, with distortion `L_n` depending only on `n`. Neither the neighborhood radius nor the quantitative flatness threshold is made effective. No bounded Laplacian is asserted for the limiting coordinate components.

The genuinely reusable candidate is not a uniform positive Gram bound. For harmonic coordinates `u`, the mean Gram matrices `R_i` can degenerate. The inverse heat Gram forms `H_i` can grow without bound and need not commute across scales. The paper constructs positive local mass forms `D_i,y`, scalar directional budgets `E_i(x)`, and a smooth covector field on value space

\[
v_i(P)=\sum_y\phi_{i,y}(P)D_{i,y}(P-c_{i,y}),
\qquad h_{i+1}=h_i+(Dh_i)^{-T}v_i,\quad h_0=P.
\]

Proposition 4.10 claims the crucial estimate

`D_i,y[u(x)-u(z)] <= C r_i^2(E_i(x)+E_i(z))`, with `sup_x sum_i E_i(x)` small. This is a squared directional budget; small mass on individual balls is insufficient. Lemma 3.7 compares cumulative local masses with `H_k`. Proposition 5.4 then constructs the smooth finite stages with `Dh_i^T Dh_i` comparable to `H_i` and all-order jet bounds. Proposition 5.6 takes the uniform limit and proves every-pair ambient-distance estimates.

### The algebra checked independently

For a smooth map `h`, `J=Dh`, and `W=J^{-T}v`, differentiating `J^TJ^{-T}=I` gives

\[
|(J+DW)\xi|^2-|J\xi|^2
=2\xi^TDv\,\xi-2\langle D^2h(\xi,\xi),J^{-T}v\rangle+|DW\,\xi|^2.
\]

Thus the inverse transpose converts the leading covector derivative into a direct increment of the pullback metric. Omitting the differentiated inverse term would be wrong. Its diagonal second derivative obeys the recurrence, with `q_i=|xi|_{H_i}`,

\[
b_i=\frac{r_i|D^2h_i(\xi,\xi)|}{q_i},\qquad
b_{i+1}\le\tfrac12b_i+C_A(\sqrt{Z_i[\xi]}+\sqrt{E_i}\,q_i).
\]

Convolution with `2^{-j}` has `ell^1` norm two and hence `ell^2` operator norm at most two. Therefore `sum b_i^2 <= C_A H_k[xi]` using the cumulative positive matrix bound. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds `sum b_i q_i sqrt(E_i)` by `C_A sqrt(epsilon) H_k[xi]`, even when `sum sqrt(E_i)` diverges. This is the mechanism that prevents a logarithmic or Hölder loss from repeated renormalizations.

The source's Gevrey majorant is also essential because each update differentiates the preceding Jacobian inverse. The weights `L_m=(m+1)((m+1)!)^sigma`, `sigma>=1`, have `sum_{l=1}^{m-1}L_lL_{m-l}/L_m ->0`. They permit inverse coefficients bounded by one fixed constant times `B^m L_m`, preserving `B`; an extra exponential base at every scale would not close the induction. This provides a distinct tool for iterations with derivative loss. Neither this algebra nor model agreement independently proves the geometric pinching inputs.

### Our deduction B1: exact cone obstruction to finite-stage all-scale certificates

Consider the flat two-dimensional cone with angle `2pi alpha`, `0<alpha<1`, metric

`ds^2=dr^2+alpha^2 r^2 dtheta^2`, `0<=theta<2pi`, and `beta=1/alpha`.

Its harmonic value map is

\[
u(r,\theta)=r^\beta(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
\]

The Laplacian away from the tip is `partial_rr+r^{-1}partial_r+(alpha^2 r^2)^{-1}partial_thetatheta`; applying it to either component gives coefficient `beta^2-alpha^{-2}=0`. The bounded local gradient extends the weak harmonic equation through the tip, since the shrinking boundary flux is `O(r^beta)` and tends to zero. The Gram field is `G=beta^2 r^{2beta-2} I`; its mean on a tip-centered radius-r ball is `R(r)=beta r^{2beta-2} I`. Thus `H(r)=beta^{-1}r^{-2(beta-1)}I` genuinely diverges although adjacent-scale growth can be arbitrarily small when `alpha` is close to one.

The cone also checks the units of the directional input. Set `q=2beta-2>0` and lower-curvature parameter zero. Direct radial integration gives `M(B_{Lr})=pi alpha beta^2 q(Lr)^q I`, hence the tip-centered twice-normalized mass is `D_r=pi alpha q L^q r^{-q}I`. For the pair consisting of the tip and a point at radius `rho<=C_sep r`,

`D_r[u(rho)-u(0)]/r^2=pi alpha q L^q(rho/r)^{2beta}`.

For a fixed off-tip point this cost is concentrated near its radial distance scale and decays geometrically on larger scales. It does not force a divergent per-point sum of directional defects. This adversarial calculation is consistent with the proposed pinching mechanism; it explains why a scale-independent small error is insufficient and why the defect must depend on the point as in the manuscript. It is not a counterexample to Proposition 4.10.

For **every smooth value-space map** `h` near zero with finite derivative, `|h(u(r,theta))-h(0)| <= C_h r^beta` for small `r`. Dividing by the ambient cone distance `r` makes this ratio tend to zero. Consequently no finite smooth stage of any such iteration is bi-Lipschitz on a neighborhood containing the tip. This does not contradict Theorem 1.1: the tip is singular, and the authors explicitly permit a nonsmooth limiting value-space map and domains containing singular points. It does rule out treating a finite smooth stage, or a finite list of pair tests, as an all-scale chart certificate.

The exact limiting unfolding is `h_infinity(P)=|P|^{alpha-1}P`, so

`F=h_infinity o u=r(cos theta,sin theta)`.

For two cone points with radial coordinates `r,s` and principal angular separation `vartheta in [0,pi]`,

\[
d_C^2=(r-s)^2+4rs\sin^2(\alpha\vartheta/2),\qquad
|F(x)-F(z)|^2=(r-s)^2+4rs\sin^2(\vartheta/2).
\]

Monotonicity and concavity of sine on `[0,pi/2]` give

`d_C <= |F(x)-F(z)| <= alpha^{-1}d_C`.

There is also a constructive smooth approximation

`h_delta(P)=(|P|^2+delta^2)^{(alpha-1)/2}P`.

Its radial image satisfies

\[
\frac{|h_\delta(u(r,\theta))|}{r}
=\left(1+\delta^2/r^{2\beta}\right)^{(\alpha-1)/2}\to0
\quad(r\downarrow0),
\]

while `sup_{0<=r<=1}|F-h_delta o u| <= delta^alpha`. To prove the latter, for `r<=delta^alpha` the error is at most `r`; for larger `r`, use `1-(1+x)^{-c}<=cx`, `c=(1-alpha)/2`, to bound the error by `c delta^2 r^{1-2beta} <= c delta^alpha`. The transition radius is `delta^alpha`, and the derivative at zero is `delta^{alpha-1}I`. Uniform approximation thus hides an unresolved arbitrarily fine collapse at every finite smoothing parameter. The included script samples these exact formulas only as a reproducibility check; the obstruction is proved analytically.

### Our deduction B2: local Lipschitz contractions and filling constants

Assume one of the claimed `L`-bi-Lipschitz charts `F:U->V` has been established. Choose `r` small enough that `B_r(p) subset U` and the Euclidean ball `B_{Lr}(F(p)) subset V`. Then

\[
H(x,t)=F^{-1}((1-t)F(x)+tF(p))
\]

contracts `B_r(p)` to `p` inside `B_{L^2r}(p)`. For each `t`,

`d(H(x,t),H(z,t)) <= L^2(1-t)d(x,z)`,

and `d(H(x,t),H(x,s)) <= L^2 r |t-s|`. The triangle inequality supplies a joint Lipschitz bound on the product metric. This conclusion concerns coordinate straight-line paths; they need not be minimizing geodesics, so Lead A's Hessian comparison cannot simply be applied along them.

For an integral k-cycle `S` supported in `B_r(p)`, `k>=1`, push it through `F`, take the Euclidean cone from `F(p)`, and pull back. Boundary commutes with pushforward. The standard Euclidean cone estimate and Lipschitz mass distortion give a filling `T` supported in `B_{L^2r}(p)` satisfying

\[
\partial T=S,\qquad
\mathbf M(T)\le\frac{L^{2k+2}}{k+1}r\,\mathbf M(S).
\]

Indeed `M(F#S)<=L^k M(S)`, the Euclidean cone has mass at most `Lr M(F#S)/(k+1)`, and pullback costs `L^{k+1}`. The same construction works for finite Lipschitz singular cycles, and for zero-dimensional integral cycles with total coefficient zero. These are ordinary consequences of a genuine chart, not new general theorems about bi-Lipschitz spaces. Conditional on family 357, they yield local filling/contraction control at **every prescribed regular point**, with constants determined by dimension, enabling chain homotopies, local bundle constructions, and geometric-measure arguments. Acquisition of the chart and its admissible radius remains unpaid.

### Prior art, novelty, obstruction, and next step

Honda–Zhang, arXiv:2608.16021v1 (17 August 2026), Section 1.2 explicitly describes the regular-point chart conjecture. Theorem 3.9 obtains charts for analytically regular maps under a neighborhood-uniform Gram determinant lower bound and Morrey Laplacian control. The release instead allows harmonic Gram degeneration and does not promise that the final chart has bounded Laplacian. David–Toro's square-summable extrinsic changes of planes, and the metric Reifenberg theorem of Gigli–Violo, are relevant antecedents; this run did not fully read those prior proofs and makes no priority determination.

The high-consequence potential is the derivative-loss iteration with a squared directional budget, reusable beyond RCD geometry only if one can supply its exact cumulative-positive-matrix and directional hypotheses. Arbitrary preconditioning, ordinary per-scale smallness, or finite observations do not supply these inputs. There is no established route here from this mechanism to cheap numerical algorithms or improved generic machine learning.

The best next allocation is an independent proof of Proposition 4.10, especially Lemmas 4.7–4.9's two-center commutator and normalized residual argument, followed by the noncommuting matrix bootstrap of Proposition 5.4. A concrete discriminator is to test this geometric input on almost-flat cones with a regular base point and an included singular tip, matching all rescaling/defect powers. A separate investigation could seek effective thresholds and finite approximate-chart acquisition, with the cone obstruction retained as a mandatory comparator.
