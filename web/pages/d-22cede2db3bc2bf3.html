## Exact theorem: keep the reference and input effect

Let C,V:H→K be isometries with a common outgoing space, V=C+iT, and define B=C†T. C is a specified physical reference, such as free evolution or a full Coulomb reference. Flux factors must already be included in the Hilbert inner product. Expanding V†V=C†C=I gives

    i(B−B†)+T†T=0,
    2 Im_H B=T†T,   Im_H B=(B−B†)/(2i).                (1)

For any detector effect0≤F≤I on K (including identity on unresolved radiation),

    Q_F=T†FT ≥0,   Q_F≤T†T=2 Im_H B.                  (2)

This is an input-space Gram effect. For a single coherent incoming ψ, its transition value is ψ†Q_Fψ; off-diagonal entries are retained. It applies equally to an instrument with positive effects F_a and ∑F_a≤I. Summing gives ∑Q_a≤2Im_H B. If the outgoing space has a hard/soft factorization, Γ_F(ρ)=Tr_soft[√F TρT†√F] is CP and Γ_F^*(I)=Q_F. Full Choi information includes output coherences that the optical identity does not identify with a forward absorptive matrix. The positive matrix in(1) is not generically Γ_F's Choi matrix.

Raw counts have a different effect:

    V†FV=C†FC+iC†FT−iT†FC+Q_F.                        (3)

They equal Q_F whenever FC=0. Conversely, without such annihilation or measured reference subtraction/interference, raw event counts do not directly give the transition effect. A nonforward angular mask annihilates a free plane-wave reference, but need not annihilate a Coulomb reference with support throughout angles. Wavepacket preparations must check this condition explicitly. check_causal's independent shock fixture uses an incoming momentum packet below E/2 and a momentum effect supported E≤|q|≤Q, so the free reference is annihilated. It is an ideal asymptotic POVM, not a compact local detector construction.

The hard/soft factorization and preparation are part of the interface. In an IR-singular limit they are not innocuous canonical tensor factors; an operator theorem at finite regulator does not decide which dressed observables a real apparatus measures.

### A normalized ultraviolet envelope, with its export boundary

Since||V||=||C||=1, ||T||=||V−C||≤2. Therefore0≤Q_F≤Q_total≤4I uniformly in energy, E and the finite regulator. For R≥0 and μ>0,

    ∫_μ^∞Tr(RQ_F(x))dx/x³ ≤2Tr(R)/μ².                (2a)

This closes ultraviolet integrability for normalized wavepacket effects without any scalar multiplicative radiation ansatz. If their reference transition effects converge pointwise as G→0, bounded convergence under this envelope controls their weighted moment. The pointwise convergence is an additional assumption; ordered soft limits can violate it. The conclusion is not an EFT-amplitude bound: plane-wave normalization, flux factors, infinite-volume/delta densities, energy-dependent wavepacket preparations and crossing may change the spectral measure and weights. A packet-to-amplitude limiting operation needs its own uniform estimate. A bound on a normalized probability cannot be silently attached to a dimensionful amplitude.

## Two exact nonidentifiability results

1. Reduced channels do not fix the forward reference amplitude. For Φ(ρ)=Tr_soft VρV†, replacing V by(I_hard⊗W_soft)V leaves Φ unchanged. In particular a phase V→e^{iθ}V is invisible even to full hard process tomography. With C fixed, B=(C†V−I)/i changes. Already V=C and V=−C give identical channels but T†T=0 and4I, respectively. This is an algebraic real-energy obstruction; it does not claim that these arbitrary phase choices both obey a physical QFT's full dispersion axioms. Those additional axioms/reference conventions are precisely information absent from Φ.

2. Positivity cannot be holomorphically imposed throughout an open complex domain. A holomorphic Choi matrix that is Hermitian PSD at every complex point must be constant: every diagonal and quadratic form is a real-valued holomorphic function, hence constant, and polarization fixes every entry. Physical CP is a boundary real-energy property. T(z)†F(z)T(z) also depends on z-bar; it is not the holomorphic continuation of a linear amplitude. It can be a positive spectral density without being an analytic amplitude.

Even if a relative reference C(s) is available on the physical axis, B(s)=C(s)†T(s) is not thereby analytic or crossing-compatible. A unitary boundary C may permit an inverse analytic continuation, but its zeros, poles, growth and crossing transformation must be supplied. The dagger is not an analytic prescription. This is a separate gate from(1).

## Conditional dispersion theorem with coherent input effects

Assume, rather than infer:

* The flux-normalized reference forward matrix B(z) has a specified Hermitian-analytic continuation on a twice-cut complex plane, threshold μ>0, after known pole/low-energy subtractions. Its coefficient B_2 at an interior crossing-symmetric subtraction point0 has the dispersion relation below. Regge/arc control suffices to close that contour; an entire z² subtraction is not left free.
* Its physical right-cut Hermitian absorptive matrices are Q_s(x)/2 and Q_u(x)/2, with Q_s,u=T_s,u†T_s,u. The phase/reference and IR limits used here are the same as in the detector construction.
* The u contribution is transported by a stated real-linear crossing map X on Hermitian matrices. Use even-order kernels x^−3; shifts/masses or nonforward functionals need their own explicit positive kernels.

With these conventions,

    B_2=(1/(2π))∫_μ^∞ [Q_s(x)+X(Q_u(x))] dx/x³.       (4)

Let detector effects obey0≤Q_E,s≤Q_s and0≤Q_E,u≤Q_u. Let R be Hermitian with

    R≥0,   X^*(R)≥0,                                 (5)

where X* is the trace-pairing adjoint. Pair(4) with R and subtract detector moments. The result is

    Tr(R B_2) ≥ (1/(2π))∫_μ^∞
                {Tr(R Q_E,s)+Tr[X*(R)Q_E,u]} dx/x³ ≥0.(6)

Proof: each omitted spectral effect is PSD; trace of two PSD matrices is nonnegative. This remains valid for bounded measures rather than ordinary spectral functions, with the same integrability/subtraction hypotheses. No single hard Kraus operator is assumed. It keeps coherent-input off-diagonal information in Q_E; it is a matrix-input version of improved positivity with omitted positive spectral weight. It does not determine B_2 from the detector, and it is not a new optical theorem. No error bound from the cycle4 scalar kernel alone establishes the assumptions of(4).

If a self-crossing sector has the SAME spectral Q in Q+XQ and the SAME residual detector spectral effect, the maximal unrestricted dual test condition is instead R+X*(R)≥0. Then a single positive omitted effect is paired with this sum. Conditions(5) are a convenient sufficient cone for independent s/u effects; claiming them optimal in the correlated case would be wrong. If additional Bose, parity or flavor restrictions constrain spectral Q, the dual cone can enlarge further.

## Exact crossing obstruction and nearest cone prior

In a self-conjugate two-particle flavor basis, the exchange j↔l acts as partial transpose on one leg of the flattened ij,kl matrix, up to charge-conjugation conventions. Partial transpose is not PSD-preserving. Let P be the two-qubit Bell projector. It is PSD, but P^{T_B} has eigenvalues(1/2,1/2,1/2,−1/2). For the singlet ψ_−,

    ψ_−†(P+P^{T_B})ψ_−=−1/2.                         (7)

Thus a positive right-cut Gram effect plus its crossing transform need not yield a PSD coefficient on every entangled incoming state. Product tests pass: for R=|a,b><a,b|, R^{T_B}=|a,b*><a,b*|≥0. PPT tests satisfy(5). Equation(7) is an exact cone-level counterexample, not an independently constructed fully unitary gravity UV completion.

Nearest primary prior is Li, Xu, Yang, Zhang and Zhou,[2101.01191v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01191v2),25August2021. Their Eq3–4 derives the crossing-symmetrized cone; the following spectrahedron proof takes the dual within the crossing-invariant tensor subspace. Its PSD requirement there is compatible with(7): it is imposed on dual tests, not on every flattened EFT coefficient. Their symmetric/antisymmetric spectral tensor assumptions encode Bose symmetry or vector parity. They already show stronger tests beyond factorized elastic states. We do not claim a new crossing cone or treat PPT as the complete cone in every sector.

## Soft radiation: ordered limit and the recovery loophole

For a specified leading-soft coherent-state factorization, V correlates a branch α with a radiation displacement f_α. Tracing those radiation modes multiplies a branch coherence by

    |r_αβ|=exp[−||f_α−f_β||²/2].                     (8)

If||Δf||²/2=G b_αβ log(E/λ_IR), b_αβ>0 fixed, then at every G>0 the limit λ_IR→0 is zero; subsequently G→0 leaves zero. Even E(G)=exp[−κ/(GM²)] does not reverse that iterated limit. In contrast choose λ_IR=E exp(−a/G): the overlap is exp(−ab), or choose log(E/λ_IR)=o(1/G): it tends to1. Limits and preparations matter. If Δf=0 the leading-soft argument says nothing about decoherence; finite-frequency and subleading radiation remain.

Carney et al.,[1710.02531v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02531v1),SecII–IV, supplies the dressed-radiation trace mechanism. Recent Matsui,[2609.08584v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08584v1),8September2026,SecII–IV Eq12–15/30–34/53, supplies a complete conserved leading soft-graviton branch current, its Ward identity and nonnegative overlap exponent. One must include recoiling partners/apparatus to conserve momentum. The leading-soft calculation omits finite-frequency/subleading effects and is not an all-energy dispersive bound.

Hirai and Sugishita,[2209.00608v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00608v3),15May2023,Sec5.1/5.5 Eq5.65–5.67, explicitly changes the sector-compatible dressed state/trace prescription and obtains hard interference for generalized Chung wavepackets. A coherent preparation including the soft dressing, a dressing-sensitive output observable or a radiation-assisted quantum eraser changes the channel/interface. It is not recovery of missing phase/reference information from the original inclusive counts.

For two equal arms with overlap3/5, the bare plus effect yields4/5 and the sector-adapted plus effect yields1. Both calculations are CP and correct for their specified effect.
