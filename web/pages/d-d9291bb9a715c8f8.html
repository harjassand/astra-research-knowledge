# A lower bound on the original fixed-step runtime

7 October 2026. This is an independently derived mathematical obstruction
to the practicality of the original step constants, not a counterexample
to the discrepancy theorem or its polynomial-time statement.

## Exact question and interface

Does the original `tau=min(1/4,eta/(8B3))` rule permit a practical favorable-
cost call even on product matroid bases, with a genuine retained row?

Consider `r` independent two-item rank-one quotas, with `r` odd, `n=2r`,
`k=2`, and `y_j=1/2`. Give the one-row matrix entries `(+1,-1)` on each
pair. Every column has Euclidean norm one, and the polytope's strong
optimizer is the exact cheaper-item choice in every pair. Take cost `c=0`.
This is fully inside the claimed interface. A comparator receives the same
polytope, `A,y,c`, and explicit product knowledge.

For `r>=65`, the original constants are `K=128`, `R=11`, `H=3872`.
Until the first original-face hit, all coordinates remain active. The row
has `p_j=1`, `q=n>R^2=121`, so it is a **retained large row**; spread
`1<=n/K` holds, and no coefficient or mode cleanup occurs. The original
large equation is `A.h=0`. Its spectral matrix is exactly

`M=I+eta 11^T`, hence `L=1+eta n=129/128<8`.

Thus `Phi=-eta||x||^2` on the whole pre-first-face regime. The construction
is not an empty-row or inactive-algorithm example.

## Universal iteration lower bound for this family

The source only permits motion length `s<=tau` and `||h||<=1`, and its
true gradient bound gives

`|x.h| <= eps_g/(2eta) = tau/(256n)`.

Either sign of such a motion increases squared norm by at most

`tau^2 [1+1/(128n)]`.

Every legal reset has `||dx||<delta_o`, and both its endpoint and reset
point lie in the cube. Consequently

`||x+dx||^2-||x||^2 <= 2sqrt(n)delta_o <= 2n delta_o`.

The first component of the source delta minimum gives

`delta_o <= tau^2/(2^18 n^2)`,

so the per-iteration squared-norm increase is at most

`C_n tau^2`, where `C_n=1+1/(128n)+1/(2^17 n)`.

Initially the norm is zero. A proper face of this product polytope fixes
at least one entire pair to signed `(+1,-1)` or `(-1,+1)`, and therefore
has squared norm at least two. A reset cannot create a face earlier,
because it must preserve the current minimal original face. At the first
hit the reset preserves the frozen signs. The number of source iterations
up to and including the first face hit is therefore at least

`ceil(2/[C_n tau^2])`.

This bound allows the source's approximate gradient equation and arbitrary
permitted reset choices. It does not assume that the direction is the
specific sparse one used in the executed first-step fixture. Since
`n>8k=16`, the terminal active-coordinate condition cannot end the routine
before the first hit.

For `r=65`, `n=130`, exact arithmetic gives

```
tau = 1/40527496729939884900352000,
minimum iterations =
3284758388270375771949395331475166428411465433540550.
```

This is more than `3.28*10^51` iterations before any first original-face
progress. It is nevertheless within a polynomial in `n,k` with enormous
constants/exponents; it does not refute the formal runtime theorem.

The same derivation applies without changing `k,A`'s normalization or
the original oracle interface to `r=2211`, `n=4422`. The reported bound
remains `B(2)=2209`, while an all-first-items base has error 2211.
The bound is therefore **nonvacuous** on this family member. Its exact
lower-bound integer is recorded in the result JSON; no enormous trajectory
or 4422-dimensional reset was executed.

## Competent exact comparator

Choose a base with the first item in `(r+1)/2` pairs and the second item in
the remaining `(r-1)/2` pairs. Its feature discrepancy is exactly `+1`.
Its complement is another base, with discrepancy `-1`. Equal weights give
exactly `y` in every coordinate. This two-atom law has exact marginals and
almost-sure feature error one, with linear construction cost.

Every base's discrepancy is a sum of `r` signs. Odd `r` forces its absolute
value to be at least one, so radius one is optimal for this fixed `A,y`.
For ANY rational cost query, the cheaper atom has cost at most their
average `c.y`. The law therefore also provides a favorable-cost responder
for every cost, preserving the cost/exact-mean link rather than relying on
a zero-cost loophole. The runtime lower-bound input itself uses `c=0`.

No general-P algorithmic superiority follows. The example says that this
simple quota instance should use its structure, and that an eventual
general implementation needs certified event steps or another mechanism
to become practical. The event certificate and two-grid proof are in
`EVENT_RESET_AND_TRACE.md`; that accelerated variant has not removed the
high-potential fallback's runtime issue.
