INDEPENDENT AUDIT: ALL-MATRIX NEAR-FULL LOWER BOUND
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: right-kernel projection, complex Hilbert-space Green inequality, Schmidt-rank
capture, and spectral cutoffs. This is an operator audit, not an external priority
or validation claim.

Conclusion
----------
The right-kernel/Green argument is valid for arbitrary complex, nonnormal A under
the stated irreducibility and spectral-growth hypotheses. I found no false
operator inequality in the chain reproduced below. The key hypotheses are that
L is self-adjoint and positive, ker L = C I, L commutes with conjugation by an
irreducible unitary carrier U on C^d, and its positive spectrum has gap gamma>0.
For Sym^N(C^m) with the standard SU(m) Casimir, gamma=m (long roots squared
length 2 and Tr_fund(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2). For other carriers use their actual
gap, or a uniform lower bound on their nonzero Casimir eigenvalues.

One specialization must be kept explicit: the polynomial cumulative count with
exponent m-1 is exact for H=Sym^N(C^m), whose End decomposition is multiplicity
free. It should not be silently assigned to every regular highest-weight carrier.
For general SU(m) irreducible carriers, the safe bound derived from the standard
zero-weight multiplicity cap has exponent s=|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2. The operator proof
below applies to either exponent once D(t)<=C(1+t)^s is supplied.

1. Kernel projection and the scalar mode
----------------------------------------
Let A be any complex d-by-d matrix with rank(A)<=d-k, 1<=k<d. Rank-nullity
provides a k-dimensional subspace K of the right kernel ker(A). Let P be its
orthogonal projection. Then P=P*=P^2, rank(P)=k, and AP=0. Thus

  Tr(PA)=Tr(AP)=0.

Set a=Tr(A)/d and X=A-aI. Then Tr X=0 and

  Tr(PX)=Tr(PA)-a Tr(P)=-ka.

With the convention <B,C>_HS=Tr(B* C), P0=P-(k/d)I is traceless and
Hermitian, so

  <P0,X>_HS=Tr(P0 X)=Tr(PX)=-ka.                         (1)

No self-adjointness, positivity, or normality of A is used. Choosing a right
kernel is important: the condition is AP=0. If instead one chooses a left-kernel
projection, PA=0 also implies Tr(PA)=0, but it is a different projection and
should not be conflated with the right-kernel convention.

The scalar/traceless decomposition is HS orthogonal. Since L(I)=0,

  ||A||_HS^2 = d|a|^2+||X||_HS^2,   E(A)=E(X).

The complex Green/Cauchy--Schwarz step is legitimate. On the traceless space
define L^{-1} by the positive inverse, and put

  G(P)=<P0,L^{-1}P0>_HS >=0.

Using the positive self-adjoint square root of L,
  |<P0,X>|^2
    = |<L^{-1/2}P0,L^{1/2}X>|^2
    <= G(P) <X,LX>_HS.
Consequently k^2|a|^2<=G(P)E(A). Also E(X)>=gamma||X||_HS^2. Therefore

  ||A||_HS^2 <= E(A) [d G(P)/k^2 + 1/gamma].          (2)

This handles complex phases correctly: (1) is a complex equality, and only its
modulus enters Cauchy--Schwarz. It does not use a false inequality such as
|Tr A|<=||A||_HS, which would miss the factor sqrt(d).

2. Schmidt-rank capture, including the exact dimension factor
-------------------------------------------------------------
Vectorize with vec(B)=sum_ij B_ij e_i tensor conjugate(e_j), so vectorization is
an HS isometry and conjugation acts as U tensor conjugate(U). Let Pi be any
orthogonal projector on End(C^d) that commutes with this action, and let
b=rank(Pi). Its partial trace Gamma=Tr_2(Pi) commutes with irreducible U, hence
Schur's lemma and Tr Gamma=b give

  Tr_2(Pi)=(b/d)I.                                      (3)

The unit vector v=vec(P)/sqrt(k) has Schmidt rank k. Its first Schmidt support
is ran(P), with projection P, and |v><v| <= P tensor I. Positivity of Pi then
gives

  ||Pi vec(P)||^2/k = <v,Pi v>
      <= Tr[(P tensor I)Pi]
       = Tr[P Tr_2(Pi)] = kb/d.

Restoring ||vec(P)||^2=k yields the exact capture bound

  ||Pi vec(P)||^2 <= k^2 b/d.                           (4)

The two factors k have different origins: rank-k support in the first Schmidt
factor, followed by the squared norm k of vec(P). For the positive Casimir
cutoff Pi_(0,t]=1_(0,t](L), b=D(t)-1 because the scalar sector is removed.
Since Pi_(0,t]P0=Pi_(0,t]P, (4) gives

  F_P(t):=||Pi_(0,t]P0||^2 <= k^2(D(t)-1)/d <= k^2D(t)/d. (5)

The exact total-mass bound is

  F_P(t)<=||P0||^2=k-k^2/d<=k.                          (6)

The first inequality in (4) uses only positivity and the partial-trace identity;
it is valid for any invariant spectral projector, including one containing
multiplicities. It is not an assertion that a low-energy eigenvector itself has
low Schmidt rank.

3. Exact Green identity
----------------------
Write the positive eigenvalues of L as lambda_j, with spectral projectors Pi_j.
The cumulative positive mass F_P(t) is sum_{lambda_j<=t}||Pi_j P0||^2. Hence,
term by term,

  integral_0^infinity F_P(t) t^(-2) dt
    = sum_j ||Pi_j P0||^2 integral_{lambda_j}^infinity t^(-2)dt
    = sum_j ||Pi_j P0||^2/lambda_j
    = G(P).                                               (7)

The value at jump points is immaterial. The integral starts effectively at
gamma, since F_P(t)=0 below the first positive eigenvalue. The weight t^(-2)
is exact for the inverse-eigenvalue Green sum; changing it to t^(-1), for
example, would be incorrect.

4. Cutoffs from a cumulative spectral count
-------------------------------------------
Assume for a parameter s>=1 and constant C that

  D(t):=rank 1_[0,t](L) <= C(1+t)^s,

For t>=gamma, (1+t)^s<=(1+1/gamma)^s t^s, so (5)--(6) imply

  F_P(t)<=min{k, A_s (k^2/d)t^s},
  A_s=C(1+1/gamma)^s.                                  (8)

Let r=d/k>1. If s>1, choose S=max{gamma,r^(1/s)} and split (7):

  G(P)<= A_s(k^2/d) integral_gamma^S t^(s-2)dt + k/S,

where the integral is zero when S=gamma. If r^(1/s)>=gamma, evaluating the
integral and using k/S=k r^(-1/s) gives

  G(P)<= [A_s/(s-1)+1] k r^(-1/s).

If r^(1/s)<gamma, the integral is zero and k/gamma<=k r^(-1/s), so the same
bound holds. Thus with C'_s=1+A_s/(s-1), (2) gives

  E(A)/||A||_HS^2 >= [C'_s r^(1-1/s)+1/gamma]^(-1)
                    >= (C'_s+1/gamma)^(-1)(k/d)^(1-1/s). (9)

The last comparison uses r>=1. This includes s=2; no endpoint term is dropped.
The high-frequency tail is bounded using F<=k all the way to infinity, so it
remains valid after the finite Casimir spectrum has ended.

For the borderline s=1, set A_1=C(1+1/gamma) and S=max{gamma,r}. If r>=gamma,

  G(P)<= (k^2/d)[A_1 log(r/gamma)+1].

If 1<r<gamma, the low interval is empty and G(P)<=k/gamma<=k/r=k^2/d.
Therefore, uniformly,

  E(A)/||A||_HS^2 >=
    [1/gamma + 1 + A_1 log(d/k)]^(-1).                  (10)

For Sym^N(C^2), gamma=2, D(t)= (j+1)^2 for lambda_j=j(j+1), and C=4 is valid;
then A_1=6 and (10) is [3/2+6 log(d/k)]^(-1), exactly the stated coarse
constant. For Sym^N(C^m), s=m-1 and C=(p+1)^(2p)/(p!)^2 with p=m-1; since
gamma=m>=2, using the coarser A_s=C(3/2)^s gives the earlier constants, hence
the corresponding power for m>=3 and logarithm for m=2.

5. What is and is not justified for general regular SU(m) carriers
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The proof from (1) through (10) is representation-independent once D(t) is
bounded. For general irreducible SU(m) carriers V_lambda, use the actual gap
gamma or the uniform positive lower bound gamma_m=min_{nu nontrivial}c_2(nu)>0
in (2) and the constants. In (8)--(10), a uniform version uses gamma_m in
A_s and in the additive 1/gamma term; the actual gap gamma remains the lower
integration endpoint and the cutoff parameter. This replacement is valid
because gamma>=gamma_m. The safe uniform spectral-count exponent from the
standard multiplicity and Weyl-dimension bounds is s=|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2:
multiplicities in End(V_lambda) are at most
dim(V_nu[0]); this is O_m((1+||nu||)^(|Phi^+|-(m-1))), dim(V_nu) is
O_m((1+||nu||)^|Phi^+|), and the number of dominant weights with Casimir at
most t is O_m((1+t)^((m-1)/2)). Since ||nu||=O_m(sqrt(t)), these exponents
give D(t)<=C_m(1+t)^|Phi^+|. Thus (9) applies with s=|Phi^+| and constants
depending only on m (power case for m>=3, logarithmic case for SU(2)). This
count is a supplied representation-theoretic input, separate from the operator
argument.

The exponent s=m-1 in the earlier Sym^N calculation follows from its exact
multiplicity-free End decomposition and D_j=binom(j+m-1,m-1)^2. It is not
automatically the general regular-highest-weight exponent. If a general regular
carrier admits a sharper D(t) estimate, substituting it into (8) is valid, but
that sharper count must be proved separately. No KLS/log-Sobolev condition is
used anywhere in this lower bound.

Audit disposition
-----------------
No correction to the right-kernel trace identity, complex Green Cauchy--Schwarz,
partial-trace/Schmidt-rank factor k^2/d, Green layer-cake identity, or cutoff
integrals is needed under the explicit hypotheses above. The correction is to
keep the spectral-growth exponent tied to the carrier family: s=m-1 for the
exact symmetric-power family, and the generally safe s=|Phi^+| for arbitrary
SU(m) irreducible carriers. The bounds are one-sided converses; they assert no
matching upper bound or optimizer.
