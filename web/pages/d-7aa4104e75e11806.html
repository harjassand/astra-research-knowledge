## Finite certificates and the dimension statement

Taking alpha=1/2 makes the Gram inequality strictly satisfied at every exact design. The zero moment b=0 also strictly satisfies the chosen positive residual bound. By continuity, sufficiently close distinct directions satisfy both strict tests. Directions obtained by normalizing nonzero rational vectors are dense on the sphere and have algebraic coordinates. In a standard algebraic real-harmonic basis, their finite moments and Gram entries admit outward algebraic/interval enclosures. Strict inequalities can therefore be certified with sufficiently precise arithmetic. This proves a locally checkable input interface, not an efficient algorithm to find a passing configuration. The all-N existence conclusion inherits the same N>C₂(2D)² prerequisite from BRV.

For M nonconstant jet coordinates, an analytic map from an open 3N-dimensional position domain to R^M cannot have image containing an open set if 3N<M: every point is critical and Sard's theorem makes the image measure zero. With fixed rays the parameter domain has dimension N, giving N>=M instead. These are valid necessary conditions for the precise open exact-jet image claim. They do not upper-bound the critical points of an N-charge potential and do not constrain a compiler restricted to one special family of harmonic templates.

There is a slightly stronger necessary count for the draft's actual moment/Gram certificate. Namely,

The exact-design case gives the same +1 because E1=0 and rank(E)=M. This sharper integer fact does not change the optimal O(D²) source-count order.
