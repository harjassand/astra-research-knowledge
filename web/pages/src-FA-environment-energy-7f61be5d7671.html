CRAY result: sharp energy comparisons without solving the environment
I derived and implemented an operation that computes the strongest ground-energy comparison justified by a specified local Hamiltonian pair, without reconstructing or solving its common environment.
The result is not just a sufficient inequality. It has a constructive converse: every stronger comparison can be contradicted by a finite compatible environment. The computation therefore distinguishes a weak certificate from genuinely insufficient physical information.
A nonperturbative spin example has an exact rational certificate and a nearly matching counterexample. A frustrated kagome example fails—and an explicit counterenvironment proves why.
This establishes a bounded-scope mathematical capability, not yet a consequential chemistry calculation or a demonstrated advantage over the strongest specialized methods. Historical novelty of the characterization also remains unestablished.
The complete proof, implementation, exact certificates, and numerical controls are available here:

The selected question became:
What can be proved about a local energy change without determining the many-body state that produces it?

The useful mathematical step was not another approximation to that state. It was an exact characterization of what the supplied local information permits.
2. The precise task
Partition the system into an active region \(A\), an interface \(C\), and an additional environment \(R\).
The supplied inputs are two finite Hermitian matrices \(h_0,h_1\) on \(A\otimes C\). They contain all interactions touching \(A\), and all changes between the two systems.
Write the full Hamiltonians as
\[
H_j(K)=h_j\otimes I_R+I_A\otimes K,
\qquad j=0,1,
\]
where \(K\) is an arbitrary Hermitian operator on \(C\otimes R\), identical in both systems.
The target quantity is
\[
\Delta(K)=\lambda_{\min}H_1(K)-\lambda_{\min}H_0(K).
\]
The operation computes the smallest closed interval containing \(\Delta(K)\) for every compatible finite environment.
There is no assumption of a spectral gap, weak boundary coupling, a prepared ground state, rapidly decaying correlations, or a supplied reduced model.
There is also an important restriction on the claim: the environment is unrestricted. Its locality, interaction signs, and norm are not supplied. Consequently, this is a robust comparison problem—not the energy difference of one fully specified material.
That distinction determines both the positive result and the failure below.
3. The operation and its completeness theorem
Let \(\Phi\) range over unital completely positive maps acting on operators of \(A\). Define
\[
\boxed{
L(h_0,h_1)=
\max_{\Phi}
\lambda_{\min}
\left[
h_1-(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_C)(h_0)
\right].
}
\]
The map is a mathematical certificate computed classically. It does not have to be implemented on a quantum device.
Theorem: complete environment-uniform comparison
For every finite Hermitian pair \(h_0,h_1\),
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{R,K}\Delta(K)=L(h_0,h_1),
\qquad
\sup_{R,K}\Delta(K)=-L(h_1,h_0).
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Delta(K)\ge c
\quad\text{for every compatible environment}
\]
holds if and only if there exists such a map satisfying
\[
h_1-(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_C)(h_0)\succeq cI.
\]
Thus the optimization is not merely one certificate family among potentially stronger methods using the same information. It characterizes the exact answer to the stated robust task.
The proof below is self-contained; it has not been independently refereed or formalized in a proof assistant.
Why the environment disappears
Suppose the local operator inequality holds. Because \(\Phi(I_A)=I_A\),
\[
H_1(K)-(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{CR})(H_0(K))
=
\left[h_1-(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_C)(h_0)\right]\otimes I_R.
\]
The unknown \(K\) cancels algebraically.
Since
\[
H_0(K)\succeq E_0(K)I,
\]
complete positivity and unitality give
\[
(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{CR})(H_0(K))
\succeq E_0(K)I.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_1(K)\succeq(E_0(K)+c)I,
\]
which proves \(\Delta(K)\ge c\).
Complete positivity matters: ordinary positivity on \(A\) is insufficient when \(A\) is entangled with the untouched degrees of freedom. For example, transposing one factor of a singlet projector creates a negative eigenvalue, despite transposition being positive on a single isolated factor.
The harder direction: constructing a counterenvironment
The converse is what turns the inequality into a complete operation.
Finite-dimensional convex minimax gives
\[
L=
\min_{\rho_{AC}}
\left\{
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho h_1)
-
\min_\Phi\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho\,\Phi(h_0)\right]
\right\}.
\]
Choose a minimizing \(\rho\), and purify it to a vector \(\lvert\psi\rangle\) on \(A\otimes C\otimes R\). Put \(B=C\otimes R\) and
\[
\sigma=\operatorname{Tr}_A|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|.
\]
Every state on \(A\otimes B\) with marginal \(\sigma\) can be produced by a channel on the \(A\) part of this purification. Consequently, the inner channel optimization equals the conditional-energy problem
\[
g_0(\sigma)
=
\min_{\omega:\operatorname{Tr}_A\omega=\sigma}
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(h_0\otimes I_R)\omega\right].
\]
Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}\sigma\), with inclusion \(V:S\to B\). Compress \(h_0\otimes I_R\) to \(A\otimes S\), obtaining \(\widetilde h_0\). The conditional problem has the dual
\[
g_0(\sigma)
=
\max_Z \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_S Z),
\qquad
\widetilde h_0\succeq I_A\otimes Z.
\]
There is no missing strict-feasibility promise: the primal product state \(I_A/d\otimes\sigma_S\) is strictly positive on its support, and a sufficiently negative scalar \(Z\) is strictly feasible for the dual.
Now construct
\[
K=-VZV^\dagger+M(I-VV^\dagger).
\]
The first term enforces the dual comparison on the relevant support; the second penalizes leaving it.
If \(B_0\ge\|h_0\|\), choosing
\[
M\ge B_0+\frac{B_0^2}{\eta}
\]
gives, by a block-matrix bound,
\[
E_0(K)\ge-\eta.
\]
Using \(\psi\) as a trial state for \(H_1(K)\) gives
\[
E_1(K)\le L.
\]
Hence
\[
\Delta(K)\le L+\eta.
\]
Since \(\eta>0\) is arbitrary, the converse follows. Exchanging \(h_0,h_1\) proves the upper endpoint.
This construction also bounds the size of the witness: with \(d=\dim A\), \(r=\dim C\), an additional space of dimension at most \(dr\) suffices. The total adversarial Hamiltonian dimension is at most \((dr)^2\).
The penalty can become large. The note explicitly controls this: smoothing the minimizing state allows an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate counterenvironment with energy scales growing polynomially in \(1/\varepsilon\), rather than hiding arbitrarily bad conditioning in an assumed marginal.
4. Constructing the map rather than assuming it
Represent \(\Phi\) by its Choi matrix \(C_\Phi\), using
\[
C_\Phi[(i,a),(j,b)]
=
\Phi(|i\rangle\langle j|)[a,b].
\]
The conditions are
\[
C_\Phi\succeq0,
\qquad
\sum_i C_\Phi[(i,a),(i,b)]=\delta_{ab}.
\]
Since \(\Phi(h_0)\) is linear in these entries, the operation is the explicit semidefinite program
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}\quad &c\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&C_\Phi\succeq0,\\
&\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{input}}C_\Phi=I_d,\\
&h_1-(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_C)(h_0)-cI\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
No optimal channel, state, comparison dictionary, or favorable curvature is supplied.
The prototype uses eigenvector separation to generate linear outer approximations to the positive-semidefinite constraints. For the isotropic two-active-spin examples, symmetry reduces the affine representation to eight real parameters.
Floating-point optimizer output was not accepted as a rigorous certificate. For the principal result, I rounded the map to rational entries, repaired unitality exactly, introduced a small rational depolarizing component, and checked both positive-semidefinite inequalities by exact rational factorization.
5. A nonperturbative positive result
Take active spins \(0,1\) and interface spins \(2,3\), all spin \(1/2\). In units where the initial exchanges equal one,
\[
h_0=
\mathbf S_0\cdot\mathbf S_1+
\mathbf S_0\cdot\mathbf S_2+
\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_3,
\]
\[
h_1=
2\mathbf S_0\cdot\mathbf S_1+
\mathbf S_0\cdot\mathbf S_2+
\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_3.
\]
The two boundary exchanges are as strong as the original central exchange. This is not a weak-coupling expansion.
The exact rational certificate proves
\[
\boxed{
-\frac34
\le
E_1(K)-E_0(K)
\le
-\frac{22421}{100000}
=
-0.22421
}
\]
for every common environment on spins \(2,3\) and any additional degrees of freedom.
Thus doubling the central exchange is guaranteed to lower the ground energy by at least \(0.22421\), without calculating either global ground state.
A transparent symbolic witness
There is also a weaker certificate with a compact analytical description.
Let \(P_s\) and \(P_t\) project onto the active pair’s singlet and triplet sectors. Set \(q=1/2\), and define Schrödinger-picture Kraus operators
\[
K_0=P_s+qP_t,
\qquad
K_m=\sqrt{1-q^2}\,|s\rangle\langle t_m|,
\quad m=1,2,3.
\]
They satisfy \(\sum_mK_m^\dagger K_m=I\). For the corresponding Heisenberg map, exact symbolic diagonalization gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left[h_0-\Phi(h_1)\right]
=
\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}8.
\]
Therefore, already,
\[
\Delta(K)\le-\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}8
\approx-0.17430609.
\]
The stronger rational certificate improves this to \(-0.22421\).
A unitary conjugation, or a mixture of unitary conjugations, cannot provide this strictly positive reversed residual for these trace-zero Hamiltonians: it preserves trace, so the residual cannot have positive minimum eigenvalue. The nonunitary channel is doing substantive work.
Near-sharpness, not just a loose successful inequality
A small compatible environment nearly saturates the optimized result:
\[
K=t\,\mathbf S_2\cdot\mathbf S_3,
\qquad
t=-\frac{59307}{100000}.
\]
Exact rational positive-definite shifts and rational Rayleigh quotients enclose its energy difference in
\[
\Delta(K)\in
[-0.22421874,\,-0.22421872].
\]
Consequently, the sharp environment-uniform upper endpoint lies between
\[
-0.22421874
\quad\text{and}\quad
-0.22421.
\]
The supplied certificate is within \(8.74\times10^{-6}\) of optimal for this information contract. This statement follows from exact arithmetic, not agreement between floating-point solvers.

These controls are small enough for ordinary diagonalization. They do not demonstrate a runtime advantage over modern many-body methods, and no independently supplied hard molecular benchmark was completed.
The kagome failure is nevertheless decisive for evaluating the mechanism.
An explicit counterenvironment reverses the sign
The active kagome bond has five distinct interface spins. Each active spin couples to three of them.
Consider the compatible common environment
\[
K_M=
M\left[
\frac52 I-
\sum_{c<c'}\mathbf S_c\cdot\mathbf S_{c'}
\right].
\]
Its ground subspace is the fully symmetric interface-spin \(S_C=5/2\) multiplet. Within that subspace,
\[
P\mathbf S_cP=\frac15\mathbf S_C.
\]
The projected active Hamiltonian becomes
\[
J\,\mathbf S_0\cdot\mathbf S_1
+
\frac35(\mathbf S_0+\mathbf S_1)\cdot\mathbf S_C.
\]
For both \(J=1\) and \(J=2\), the lowest projected energy is
\[
e_J=\frac J4-\frac{21}{10}.
\]
Its change is therefore positive:
\[
e_2-e_1=\frac14.
\]
This is not merely an infinite-penalty argument. At the finite value \(M=100\), a block-compression estimate gives
\[
\Delta(K_{100})
\ge
\frac14-\frac{36}{238}
=
\frac{47}{476}>0.
\]
The actual antiferromagnetic kagome control has a negative energy change, but a different environment with the same supplied local patch has a positive one.
The lower endpoint \(-3/4\) is approached by binding each interface spin into a strong singlet with an additional environment spin. Thus the entire interval \([-3/4,+1/4]\) is sharp in the closure of compatible environments.
No algorithm receiving only this patch and the same unrestricted-environment promise can determine the sign. More optimization or universally valid ground-state stability constraints cannot fix the missing information.
This preserves the scientific problem while rejecting an inadequate information contract.
7. Full resource accounting
Input acquisition and model accuracy. The theorem requires accurate \(h_0,h_1\) and a correct common-environment specification. It does not derive an electronic Hamiltonian, calibrate exchange constants, or infer omitted interactions. Checking the common-environment promise from arbitrary global input lists can require reading those lists.
Arithmetic and memory. With \(d=\dim A\), \(r=\dim C\), the local matrices require \(O(d^2r^2)\) storage and the Choi witness \(O(d^4)\). Direct image construction costs \(O(d^4r^2)\) arithmetic operations. Dense spectral verification additionally costs
\[
O\!\left(d^6+(dr)^3\right).
\]
A general semidefinite solver can require substantially more memory.
Bit complexity. For rational input with explicit norm bounds, the bounded Choi domain and an explicit strictly feasible point permit standard additive weak optimization with polynomial-bit complexity in the matrix dimensions and precision parameters. This is not a polynomial claim in the number of physical spins. The prototype’s cutting-plane iteration count is empirical, not a proved polynomial bound.
The exponential bottleneck remains local. For \(k\) active spin-\(1/2\) variables, \(d=2^k\), so the Choi matrix has \(16^k\) entries. At \(k=8\), that is approximately \(4.3\) billion entries—about 69 GB as complex doubles, before solver overhead. No favorable low-rank representation is assumed to remove this cost.
Precision. The unknown extensive environment cancels before arithmetic, so two large global energies never need to be subtracted. However, the finite residual still requires certified spectral accuracy. An interval can remain wide even with exact arithmetic, as the kagome example proves.
Physical-model discrepancy. If noncommon errors in the two modeled Hamiltonians have operator norms bounded by \(\eta_0,\eta_1\), expand the certified interval by \(\eta_0+\eta_1\) on both sides. Exactly common environment-only error cancels. Common error touching \(A\) generally does not: its residual is \(\delta h-\Phi(\delta h)\).
Sector and chemistry restrictions. The theorem concerns unconstrained ground energies on the full Hilbert space. Fixed electron number, other superselection sectors, long-range interaction truncation, nuclear relaxation, and finite-temperature free energies require additional analysis. They are not covered automatically.
Randomness, hardware, and output. The operation requires no quantum device, state preparation, tomography, or experimental samples. Exact certificate verification is deterministic. Its output is two bounds and finite matrix witnesses—not a global wavefunction. The generic constructive counterenvironment pipeline is proved, but only explicit special cases were implemented.
What refinement can and cannot accomplish
For a fully supplied local lattice model, there is a constructive refinement procedure: enlarge the active region, collect its complete interaction interface, and solve the comparison again. Existing certificates lift by tensoring their maps with the identity, so the intervals cannot widen.
At the full finite system, the comparison becomes exact—but exponentially expensive.
I have not established that an affordable active region suffices for an important correlated chemical system. That is the remaining computational obligation, not an assumption the theorem is allowed to absorb.
8. Prior art and significance
The basic ingredients are not new mathematical primitives. Semidefinite characterization of local energy extraction is already developed in the literature on completely positive local passivity. Quantum majorization also provides complete channel-comparison criteria in closely related settings. arXiv
The candidate contribution is narrower and precise:
An exact identification of paired ground-energy extrema over arbitrary common environments with one local completely positive comparison, together with a finite counterenvironment construction and explicit resource bounds.

I have not established historical priority for that characterization.
Nor is “rigorous local ground-energy bounds” itself a new capability. Bootstrap methods already provide certified ground-energy bounds, and recent defect-bootstrap work introduces additional structure through ancillary defect models under a defect-diamagnetism condition. No measured advantage over those methods on the same specified physical inputs was demonstrated here. arXiv
The supplied research repositories were consulted selectively after developing the opportunity. No theorem from either repository is used as an unverified premise of the proof, and no repository changes were made.
What this investigation actually changes
The established result replaces an unbounded search over possible quantum environments with a finite optimization on the supplied local matrices. It also supplies the converse needed to interpret failure: a finite compatible Hamiltonian can witness that a requested comparison is impossible from those inputs.
That is a substantive operational result. It supports an exact, nonperturbative energy decision for one local interaction pattern and exposes an information-theoretic failure for another.
It does not yet meet the larger scientific mission. The missing advance is a constructive way to exploit realistic environmental restrictions while keeping the comparison region small enough to compute. The kagome counterexample shows precisely why unrestricted environment elimination alone will not supply that advance. Further isolated-spin successes would not close the gap.
