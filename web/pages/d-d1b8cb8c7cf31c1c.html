Status: **PARKED, 2026-10-10. No discovery of a natural mechanism.**

This pass produced a conditional tensor diagnostic and a reproducible extraction
of published intervention plots. The available observations do not identify a

## Brief independent phenomenon comparison

1. **Neutron lifetime discrepancy.** J-PARC's electron-counting beam result is
   877.2 s with statistical 1.7 s and asymmetric systematic +4.0/-3.6 s errors.
   It differs in detection channel from the proton beam measurements. A common
   invisible decay branch predicts a common electron/proton partial lifetime,
   giving a useful closure relation, but the reported 2.3-sigma tension does not
   identify a new mechanism. Not pursued.
   Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19519
2. **Liquid/solid electrification.** A 2026 phase/temperature intervention compares
   polar, nonpolar and frozen drops. The authors interpret persistent frozen and
   nonpolar charging as evidence for an additional route beyond simple mobile-ion
   transfer. The chemical carrier remains insufficiently identified in the
   accessible abstract; no mechanistic analysis was undertaken here.
   Primary source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03449-3
3. **SrTiO3 phonon thermal Hall effect.** Selected because annealing and simultaneous
   contact-pair observations are publicly plotted and permit within-sample
   comparisons. Its neutral transverse heat transport could matter broadly, but
   that potential is not evidence of a new fundamental law.

## Primary observation boundary

Xiang et al. report that air annealing changes transverse heat transport while
longitudinal conductivity changes little. They already describe contact-pair
inhomogeneity and suggest both strain-domain cancellation and altered
quantum-paraelectric behavior. Metallic versus insulating contacts are compared
separately. Additional data are request-only. Figures provide neither a complete

## What was extracted, and what it means

`extract_observations.py` reads the actual PDF vector markers from preprint page 7,
Figure 2. Each exported record includes the source drawing index and PDF
coordinates. The units on these axes are **per mille**, not percent. Repeated
plotted positions are retained, not declared independent experimental repeats.
The published version was downloaded and visually checked against the preprint
figure. Numbers below are descriptive plotted-point statistics, not raw-data
parameter estimates.

Define a finite-field odd-response statistic s = sum(B theta)/sum(B^2), with theta
in per mille and B in tesla. This is not automatically the B=0 derivative.

For sample #3, short anneal at 28.4 K and long anneal at 28.3 K:

| Contact pair | Short s | Long s | Long/short |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| T2-T3 | -0.10231 | -0.05550 | 0.54248 |
| T1-T4 | -0.03362 | -0.03448 | 1.02578 |

The annotated longitudinal conductivities are 18.2 and 17.3 W/(m K), ratio
0.95055. The short-state two-pair contrast D=|s1-s2|/(|s1|+|s2|) is 0.50538 and
the long-state contrast is 0.23358. These quantify the already discussed
inhomogeneity; they do not establish the source of that inhomogeneity.

A restricted scalar null has s_i(state) = g_i F(state), with unchanged
contact/geometry gains g_i and one common transport multiplier F. It requires

    s1(long) s2(short) - s2(long) s1(short) = 0.

The plotted values do not satisfy that algebraic equality. `check_predictions.py`
allows every extracted field to move independently by +/-0.1 T and every Hall
angle by +/-0.01 per mille. The resulting deterministic interval for the left
side is [-0.0024082, -0.0009393] (per mille/T)^2. This is a figure-extraction
sensitivity interval, **not a confidence interval or an experimental error bar**.

Unreported contact/geometry changes could explain it: the relative gain change
needed to retain this null is approximately 1.89, with the above deliberately
broad numerical perturbations allowing 1.44 to 2.49. The original experiment's
uncertainty and contact-reinstallation information are not supplied in the
figure. Small temperature mismatch, spatial heat-flow changes and true material
changes also remain. Consequently this analysis does not assert a statistical
rejection of a microscopic scattering mechanism.

The sample #2 finite-field statistic changes by 5.81997, but its temperatures
are 30.2 and 28.7 K. **Do not use that ratio as a same-temperature exclusion or
apply the random-domain bound below to it.** The initial domain population is
also unknown.

## Conditional structural-domain mechanism

Use kappa^A_ij = epsilon_ijk h_k. In a nonmagnetic fixed structural state,
Onsager reciprocity allows h(B)=K B+O(B^3). Both h and B are axial vectors.
For every orthogonal structural transformation R, the two determinant signs
cancel and K transforms as an ordinary tensor:

    K' = R K R^T.

Tetragonal D4h symmetry gives K0=diag(a,a,c). A domain with tetragonal director n
has

    K(n)=a I+(c-a) n n^T.

Antiparallel n and -n produce the same tensor. Antiphase variants related solely
by a lost translation do not reverse a homogeneous bulk transport coefficient.
The simple ferromagnetic-domain analogy therefore does not itself establish
cancellation of a field-induced Hall signal in a nonmagnetic material.

### Required effective-medium assumptions

The following conclusions assume weak Hall response, a local diffusive
constitutive law, the same scalar zero-field longitudinal conductivity kappa0
everywhere, and fixed a,c across the intervention. With a prescribed average
gradient E, write the local gradient E+grad(phi). The zero-field corrector is
uniform. At first order in B,

    <j> = -kappa0 E - <kappa^A> E,

because <grad(phi)>=0. Thus arithmetic averaging is exact at this order under
these assumptions, rather than being assumed for a general composite.

Let Q=<n n^T>, tr Q=1. Then

    K_eff = a I+(c-a)Q,
    Bhat . K_eff . Bhat = a+(c-a)<(n.Bhat)^2>.

### Derived constraints

* The projected coefficient is between a and c. Same-sign strictly nonzero
  principal coefficients cannot cancel exactly in this model.
* **Same-sign is not implied by symmetry, Onsager reciprocity, or positive entropy
  production.** The antisymmetric thermal conductivity is nondissipative.
* A counterexample is K0=diag(1,1,-2). Equal axis populations give K_eff=0.
* Isotropic populations give K_eff=(2a+c)I/3. If a,c have the same sign, the
  retained magnitude is at least one third of the largest principal magnitude.
  A strongly textured population can nevertheless give a tiny signal if one
  principal coefficient is tiny. “Random” must not be substituted for a measured
  population.
* Exact isotropic cancellation needs c=-2a. That model predicts a zero in the
  single-domain conventional Hall projection at cos^2(theta)=1/3, approximately
  54.7356 degrees from the tetragonal axis.

These are deductions under assumptions, not measurements of a and c.

## Sharpest unperformed discriminating protocol

For the three ideal SrTiO3 tetragonal variants, n is along pseudocubic x,y,z.
For B along [111], (n.Bhat)^2=1/3 in every domain, independent of populations.
Therefore

    S_[111] = (2a+c)/3.

Within the specified model, annealing-induced repopulation cannot change the
low-field conventional Hall-conductivity slope in a transport plane perpendicular
to [111]. A change would require at least one assumption to fail. It would not
by itself identify which microscopic mechanism changed.

More generally, full-tensor measurements in three orthogonal conventional Hall
geometries give

    Sx+Sy+Sz = tr K_eff = 2a+c,

which pure orientation redistribution preserves even for continuously rotated
domains under arithmetic averaging. Trace preservation is necessary but not
sufficient for that explanation.

**Geometry caution:** h projected along B is the quantity above. A fixed Hall
contact normal m with B rotated measures m.K.B, generally a sin/cos projection,
not the cos^2 law. Reconstruct the conductivity tensor, including longitudinal
anisotropy; a raw temperature-gradient ratio alone does not suffice.

No suitable before/after [111] or full three-axis raw dataset was located during
the targeted search. That is a limited search result, not a proof none exists.
This is the stopping condition for the present branch.

## Important failure modes and attribution

* Spatially varying longitudinal conductivity/anisotropy changes homogenization
  weights to local corrector-field/cofactor expressions. Arbitrary 3D composites
  are outside the simple theorem. Electrical Hall-sign inversion in actual 3D
  metamaterials is a known counterexample to unrestricted sign arguments:
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.021030 and
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.118.016601 .
* Domain walls, strain-dependent a,c, altered resonant skew scattering, changes
  in mode-resolved scattering, and quasi-ballistic transport can modify Hall
  response without substantially changing one measured kappa_xx. The present
  evidence does not distinguish them. No intrinsic/extrinsic identification is
  certified here.
* Low Hall angle does not guarantee linear response all the way to 9 T.
* The anisotropy/domain question is prior work, not an invention here. Campillo,
  Baglo and Taillefer explicitly described different cuts, cooling histories and
  contact regions in an APS 2025 presentation:
  https://meetings-archive.aps.org/smt/2025/mar-x20/13/ .
* The original 2020 SrTiO3 Hall paper already discusses domain orientation:
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.105901 .
* Structural variants and antiphase caution were independently checked against
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925838821031844 .

No historic novelty claim is made for the tensor identities or proposed protocol.
