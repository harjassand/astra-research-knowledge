1. Result proved conditionally

Let q=1024=2^10. Use the BSSZ curve sequence and parameters
  α=34, β=40.53, s=√q−1=31.
Define
  F_q(x,y)=(x+y)log_q(x+y)−xlog_q(x)−ylog_q(y),
  L=log_q(1−q^{-1}),
  ρ_- = F_q(β,31)+α+62L−2,
  ρ_+ = α−1+31L+F_q(β,31).

The exact numerical intervals in §2 give
  ρ_-=39.05239901777024556…,
  ρ_+=40.05676868555972676…,
  6ρ_+=240.34061211335836056… .
For any fixed real C>6ρ_+, choose prime integers m_i with m_i/g_i→C, where g_i is the genus of the source curve C_i. The evaluation argument below gives sets B_i⊂F_{Q_i}, Q_i=q^{m_i}, with n_i=|B_i| such that
  liminf_i log_{Q_i} n_i ≥ ρ_-/C,
  limsup_i log_{Q_i} n_i ≤ ρ_+/C,
  |B_i+B_i|≤n_i^1.889,   |B_iB_i|≤n_i^1.909,
and, for all sufficiently large i,
  |B_i∩(a+ξK)|≤1024                                         (1)
for every proper subfield K⊊F_{Q_i}, every a∈F_{Q_i}, and every ξ∈F_{Q_i}^×.

For the explicit choice C=242, the strict rate margins give
  Q_i^0.16≤n_i≤Q_i^0.17
eventually. The original B_i is F_q^×-stable, contains no zero, and generates F_{Q_i} as a field.

There is a further exact finite-field consequence. Choose one representative from each F_q^×-orbit in B_i and call the transversal B_i^∘. Then
  |B_i^∘|=n_i/(q−1),
  |B_i^∘+B_i^∘|≤|B_i^∘|^1.89,
  |B_i^∘B_i^∘|≤|B_i^∘|^1.91
for all sufficiently large i, and
  |B_i^∘∩cK|≤|K|^{1/2}                                  (2)
for every subfield K⊆F_{Q_i} and every c∈F_{Q_i}. Thus B_i^∘ meets the exact multiplicative-coset hypothesis in Li–Roche–Newton, Theorem 1.4. For C=242, |B_i^∘| also lies between Q_i^0.16 and Q_i^0.17 eventually; dividing by the fixed factor 1023 does not change either strict exponent margin.

The main new mechanism is not an energy inequality: it counts the degree-m evaluation places where any nonconstant three-point ratio lands in one of the exceptional proper subfields. Choosing m/g above the threshold 6ρ_+ makes the expected number of such triples less than one, so a place exists with none. This proves (1) for this construction. No claim is made that this mechanism classifies arbitrary sets with small sum and product sets.

2. Source theorem and parameter verification

Bloom–Sawin–Schildkraut–Zhelezov (BSSZ), “The sum-product conjecture is false for real numbers,” arXiv:2605.28781v1, Theorem 7.8, equations (7.18)–(7.23), is the source-reported input. For square q and α>√q+1, their construction has curves C_i with g_i→∞ and |C_i(F_q)|/g_i→√q−1, and sets A_i=P_iG_i⊂F_q(C_i). In characteristic two, the product threshold is their equation (7.20). At the present parameters, set
  D=F_q(β,31)+α+62L−2,
  a_*=(α+β−1)/D,
  b_*=1+[log_q(2)/33+F_q(2β,31)−F_q(β,31)+α+31L−1]/D.
The source theorem gives, for any a>a_* and b>b_*, sets on this curve sequence with |A_i+A_i|≤|A_i|^a and |A_iA_i|≤|A_i|^b for all sufficiently large i.

Exact rational interval arithmetic gives
  D∈(39.05239901777024,39.05239901777026),
  a_*∈(1.88285487830187,1.88285487830188),
  b_*∈(1.90831572087931,1.90831572087932),
  ρ_+∈(40.05676868555972,40.05676868555974).
Thus a=1.889 and b=1.909 are valid strict choices, and ρ_-=D. These decimals are certified by rational intervals, not just floating-point evaluation. One reproducible method is to reduce each positive rational logarithm to ln x=k ln 2+ln y with 1≤y≤2, then use
  ln y=2Σ_{j=0}^{49} z^{2j+1}/(2j+1)+R,
  z=(y−1)/(y+1),   0≤R≤2z^101/(101(1−z²)).
All operations, including the final threshold comparisons, can then be performed with rational endpoints. In particular α=34>33=√q+1.

For the source set sizes, BSSZ equations (7.11), (7.14), and (7.22) give
  liminf_i (log_q |A_i|)/g_i ≥ ρ_-
  = F_q(β,31)+α+62L−2.
For an upper bound, the source construction has d_P=⌈αg⌉, d_G=⌈βg⌉, and N=|C(F_q)| with N/g→31. The P count is
  |P|=q^{d_P+1−g}(1−q^{-1})^N.
An element of G is determined, up to a scalar in F_q^×, by an effective divisor of degree d_G supported on those N rational points, so
  |G|≤(q−1) binom(d_G+N−1,d_G),
and the source has |PG|=|P||G|/(q−1). Stirling’s formula therefore gives
  limsup_i (log_q |A_i|)/g_i ≤ ρ_+
  = α−1+31L+F_q(β,31).

After dividing by a fixed nonzero section of the line bundle L_P⊗L_G, the functions A_i lie in L(D_i) for an effective divisor D_i of degree
  d_i=deg D_i=d_{P_i}+d_{G_i}=(α+β)g_i+O(1)=74.53g_i+O(1).
They are nonzero, and A_i+A_i⊂L(D_i) under this identification.

3. Closed-point evaluation and sum-product transfer

Fix C>6ρ_+; below take C=242. By the prime number theorem, choose prime integers m_i with m_i/g_i→C. Then m_i>d_i eventually. Let V_i be the set of degree-m_i closed points of C_i. Since m_i is prime,
  |C_i(F_{q^{m_i}})|−|C_i(F_q)|=m_i|V_i|.
Weil’s bound gives
  |C_i(F_{q^{m_i}})|−|C_i(F_q)|
  ≥ q^{m_i}−q−2g_i(q^{m_i/2}+q^{1/2})
  ≥ q^{m_i}/2
for all sufficiently large i. Hence |V_i|≥q^{m_i}/(2m_i).

For v∈V_i, the residue field is F_{q^{m_i}}. Evaluation at v is injective on L(D_i): if a nonzero h∈L(D_i) vanished at v, its zero divisor would have degree at least m_i, whereas its total zero degree equals its pole degree and is at most deg D_i=d_i<m_i. Set B_{i,v}=ev_v(A_i). Evaluation is a ring homomorphism on functions regular at v and is injective on L(D_i), so
  |B_{i,v}|=|A_i|,
  |B_{i,v}+B_{i,v}|=|A_i+A_i|≤|A_i|^1.889,
  |B_{i,v}B_{i,v}|≤|A_iA_i|≤|A_i|^1.909.
Only the sum map needs injectivity; product collisions are allowed.

The size-rate bounds and m_i/g_i→C give
  liminf_i (log_{Q_i}|A_i|)≥ρ_-/C,
  limsup_i (log_{Q_i}|A_i|)≤ρ_+/C,
where Q_i=q^{m_i}. For C=242, ρ_-/242>0.16 and ρ_+/242<0.17, so Q_i^0.16≤|A_i|≤Q_i^0.17 eventually. Because the BSSZ construction is stable under F_q^×, so is B_{i,v}.

4. Ratio-triple incidence count and the constant subfield cap

Assume m=m_i is prime and m>5, and write F=F_{q^m}=F_{2^{10m}}. Its proper subfields are F_{2^e} for proper divisors e of 10m. The divisors are 1,2,5,10,m,2m,5m,10m. The ones contained in F_q=F_{2^{10}} have e∈{1,2,5,10}; the only other proper subfields are
  K_1=F_{2^m},  K_2=F_{2^{2m}},  K_5=F_{2^{5m}}.
Their sizes and intersections with F_q are
  (|K_1|,|K_1∩F_q|)=(q^{m/10},2),
  (|K_2|,|K_2∩F_q|)=(q^{m/5},4),
  (|K_5|,|K_5∩F_q|)=(q^{m/2},32).
These are all the proper subfields not contained in the construction base field. The lattice is exact because m is prime and m>5.

Fix i and write A=A_i⊂L(D_i), d=deg D_i. For every ordered triple (f_1,f_2,f_3)∈A^3 with f_1≠f_2, form
  r=(f_3−f_1)/(f_2−f_1)∈F_q(C_i)
when this rational function is nonconstant. The denominator is nonzero. Its rational-map degree is at most 2d: the pole divisor of f_3−f_1 has degree at most d, and the zero divisor of f_2−f_1 has degree at most d, so the poles of their ratio have total degree at most 2d. The safe factor 2 is sufficient here.

For each fixed nonconstant r and each K_j, at most 2d|K_j|/m degree-m closed points v have r(v)∈K_j. Indeed, for each k∈K_j the geometric fiber r^{-1}(k) has at most deg(r)≤2d points. If one geometric point in a degree-m Frobenius orbit has r-value in K_j, all m conjugates do too, because K_j is stable under q-Frobenius. Therefore the number of such closed places is at most 2d|K_j|/m. Places where r has a pole are not counted.

For v∈V_i, let Z(v) count ordered triples (f_1,f_2,f_3)∈A^3 with f_1≠f_2 such that r is nonconstant and r(v) lies in K_1∪K_2∪K_5. Each triple contributes to the sum of Z(v) over at most
  (2d/m)(|K_1|+|K_2|+|K_5|)
places, and there are at most |A|^3 triples. Averaging over |V_i|≥q^m/(2m) yields
  E_{v∈V_i} Z(v)
  ≤ 4d |A|^3 (|K_1|+|K_2|+|K_5|)/q^m
  ≤ 12d |A|^3 q^{-m/2}.                                  (3)
The exponent of the right side, divided by g_i, has limsup at most 3ρ_+−C/2. Thus it tends to zero for every fixed C>6ρ_+. For C=242,
  3ρ_+−121=−0.8296939433208197…,
so (3) tends to zero exponentially in g_i, up to the factor d=O(g_i). Since Z(v) is a nonnegative integer, an average below 1 implies that some v_i∈V_i has Z(v_i)=0.

Let B_i=ev_{v_i}(A_i). Fix j∈{1,2,5}, any a∈F, ξ∈F^×, and set
  C_0=B_i∩(a+ξK_j),  t=|C_0|,  r_j=|K_j∩F_q|∈{2,4,32}.
For each ordered pair x_1≠x_2 in C_0, each x_3∈C_0 determines
  λ=(x_3−x_1)/(x_2−x_1)∈K_j.
At most r_j choices of x_3 give λ∈F_q: those choices lie on
  x_1+(x_2−x_1)(K_j∩F_q).
Every other x_3 gives λ∈K_j\F_q. By injectivity of evaluation on A, each such ordered triple lifts uniquely to (f_1,f_2,f_3)∈A^3. Its rational ratio cannot be constant: the constant field of F_q(C_i) is F_q, so a constant ratio would evaluate in F_q. Consequently these triples are counted by Z(v_i). If t≥r_j+1, there is at least one such triple, contradicting Z(v_i)=0. Thus
  |B_i∩(a+ξK_j)|≤r_j≤32
for every affine coset of each K_j.

If K⊂F_q, every affine coset a+ξK has at most |K|≤q=1024 elements. The subfield lattice in §4 is exhaustive. Hence (1) follows for every proper K. In particular no assumption that “not contained in a proper subfield” implies quantitative nonconcentration is being made.

The original B_i generates F_{Q_i}: it is nonzero and F_q^×-stable, so for any b∈B_i, the generated field contains every c=(cb)/b∈F_q^× and hence F_q. Since |B_i|>q eventually and m_i is prime, B_i cannot lie in F_q and the only larger intermediate field containing F_q is F_{q^{m_i}}.

5. Orbit transversal and the exact Li–Roche–Newton condition

The source P_i and G_i are stable under F_q^×, so A_i and B_i are too. They contain no zero: P_i sections are nonvanishing on rational points and G_i sections are nonzero; evaluation is injective on L(D_i). Therefore the scalar action is free and B_i is a disjoint union of orbits of size q−1=1023. Choose one representative from each orbit and call the resulting set B_i^∘. Put n_i^∘=|B_i^∘|=n_i/(q−1). Since the factor is fixed,
  liminf_i log_{Q_i} n_i^∘≥ρ_-/C,
  limsup_i log_{Q_i} n_i^∘≤ρ_+/C.
For C=242 this implies Q_i^0.16≤n_i^∘≤Q_i^0.17 eventually.

Because B_i^∘⊂B_i, its sum and product sets are no larger than those of B_i. Using n_i=1023n_i^∘ and the strict source exponents 1.889 and 1.909,
  |B_i^∘+B_i^∘|≤1023^1.889(n_i^∘)^1.889≤(n_i^∘)^1.89,
  |B_i^∘B_i^∘|≤1023^1.909(n_i^∘)^1.909≤(n_i^∘)^1.91
for all sufficiently large i. The final inequalities hold because (n_i^∘)^0.001 eventually exceeds each fixed factor.

We check (2) for every subfield K⊆F=F_{q^m}. If K⊂F_q and c≠0, then cK^×⊂cF_q^× is one F_q^×-orbit, so the transversal meets cK in at most one point; c=0 gives no point because 0∉B_i^∘. This is ≤|K|^{1/2}. If K is one of K_1,K_2,K_5, the affine cap gives |B_i^∘∩cK|≤r_j, where r_j=2,4,32. For m>5, respectively,
  r_j≤|K_j|^{1/2};
indeed |K_j|^{1/2}=2^{m/2}, 2^m, 2^{5m/2}. Finally for K=F, the intersection has size at most n_i^∘≤Q_i^0.17<Q_i^0.5 for large i. These cases exhaust all subfields and prove (2).

Li–Roche–Newton, Theorem 1.4, assumes A⊂F_{p^n}^* and |A∩cG|≤|G|^{1/2} for every subfield G and every c∈F_{p^n}; it concludes
  max(|A+A|,|AA|) ≫ |A|^{12/11}/(log_2|A|)^{5/11}.
Thus B_i^∘ satisfies their exact hypothesis. This removes the base-field scalar-orbit obstruction that prevented B_i itself from satisfying that hypothesis. The resulting lower bound is compatible with our upper bounds n^{1.89} and n^{1.91}; no contradiction is claimed. B_i also has the stronger affine proper-subfield cap (1).

6. Literature comparison, scope, and claim ledger

BSSZ, Theorem 7.2, gives source-reported examples in prime fields F_p for sufficiently large p, with a small unspecified saving and a size interval p^{fδ}<|A|<p^δ. Their Theorem 7.8 gives the function-field construction used here. In §7.2, BSSZ explicitly suggest reducing the function-field construction modulo a prime of F_q(C), while warning that distance from subfields must be checked (arXiv:2605.28781v1, lines 577–579). The selected-place ratio count supplies a quantitative check for a finite extension field and, after scalar-orbit quotienting, yields the exact Li–Roche–Newton condition. I did not establish whether this precise finite-extension construction or the ratio-triple selection argument appears elsewhere; novelty and priority are unknown.

The chosen target is not a prime-field theorem, a lower bound showing large expansion, an inverse theorem, or a classification of all sets with small sum and product sets. It is an explicit conditional construction in characteristic 2 with near-quadratic sum/product upper bounds, a constant cap on every affine proper-subfield coset, and a scalar-orbit transversal satisfying a standard exact finite-field nonconcentration hypothesis. Its ambient field size is Q=n^{C/ρ+o(1)}; for C=242, the set size is between Q^0.16 and Q^0.17. It does not cross the BSSZ sum-product exponent barrier itself.
