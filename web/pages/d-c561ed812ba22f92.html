# An explicit convex certificate where the standard entropy barrier fails

Status: a complete, independently derived mathematical construction for a specified planar family. It does not depend on either release148 or release149 being correct. Exact rational computation checks the constants and polytope; the continuum proof is below. This is a mechanism demonstration and possible certificate-design template, not a claim of a new general permanence theorem or independently established novelty.

## Question, source, and exact interface

Can the finite affine-envelope idea yield a fully explicit robust certificate on a system that obstructed a familiar smooth Lyapunov function?

The baseline planar field is the one in Boros–Hofbauer, arXiv:1903.03071v2 (13March2019), Section7.2. That section explains why the classical centered entropy function cannot be an exterior Lyapunov function. Its permanence is already known from older planar results. We use the same baseline vector field and give our own reversible reaction realization and a rate-uncertain certificate. We did not read the whole prior manuscript; Sections3–4 and7 were read as technical excerpts.

Let six measurable rates vary arbitrarily within

    a,d∈[9/5,11/5],     b,c∈[63/10,77/10],     u,v∈[9/10,11/10].

On x,y>0 define

    g(t,x,y)=−a x³+b x²y−c xy²+d y³,
    x'=g,     y'=−g+u−v y.                         (1)

A reversible mass-action realization is

    3A ⇄ 2A+B   (forward a, reverse b),
    A+2B ⇄ 3B   (forward c, reverse d),
    0 ⇄ B       (forward u, reverse v).

This realization has three linkage classes; its reaction subspace is R². With a=d=2, b=c=7, u=v=1 it reproduces the prior paper's field. We do not claim this is the same reaction graph as its diagram. The rates may switch or oscillate arbitrarily fast, and need not converge; Carathéodory solutions are used.

## Construction and result

Define six affine functions

    L1=x−1/16,
    L2=y−x/4,
    L3=x+y−1/2,
    L4=10−x−y,
    L5=4x+2−y,
    L6=8−x,

and the concave envelope F=min(0,L1,…,L6). Its plateau

    K={F=0}={L_i≥0 for i=1,…,6}

is a compact convex pentagon with vertices

    (1/16,7/16), (1/16,9/4), (8/5,42/5), (8,2), (2/5,1/10).

It contains (1,1) and has exact coordinate extrema

    1/16≤x≤8,     1/10≤y≤42/5.

For every admissible measurable rate choice and every positive initial point, the solution is global, remains positive, enters K, and remains there. A supplied quantitative bound is

    T_entry≤−F(x0,y0)/δ,     δ=2379/40960.          (2)

Unlike the general release envelope existence theorem, all coefficients and thresholds here are rational and explicit.

## Two polynomial estimates

For z=y/x∈[0,1/4], coefficient bounds give

    g/x³ ≤ P(z)=−9/5+(77/10)z−(63/10)z²+(11/5)z³.

For that range P'(z)≥77/10−(126/10)(1/4)=91/20>0, so

    g≤P(1/4)x³=−(15/64)x³.                        (3)

For w=x/y∈[0,1/4], similarly

    g/y³ ≥ Q(w)=9/5−(77/10)w+(63/10)w²−(11/5)w³.

Here Q'(w)≤−77/10+(126/10)(1/4)=−91/20<0, so

    g≥Q(1/4)y³=(15/64)y³.                         (4)

For the smaller range w∈[0,1/7], discarding Q's positive quadratic term gives the useful bound

    g/y³≥9/5−(77/10)(1/7)−(11/5)(1/7)³
          =2379/3430.                             (5)

These are universal inequalities across the full rate box. They are not conclusions from sampling.

## Every active branch increases away from the plateau

Suppose F(x,y)<0 and Li is active, so Li=F≤0 and Li≤Lj for every j.

1. L1 active: x≤1/16, and L1≤L3 forces y≥7/16. Thus x/y≤1/7. From(5),

    L1'=g≥(2379/3430)(7/16)³=2379/40960.

2. L2 active: y≤x/4. Equation(3) yields

    L2'=u−v y−(5/4)g
         ≥9/10−(11/40)x+(75/256)x³.

For 0<x≤1 this is at least 5/8. For x≥1 use x³≥x and
75/256−11/40=23/1280>0; it is then at least 9/10. Thus L2'≥5/8 everywhere in its active exterior region.

3. L3 active: x+y≤1/2, hence y≤1/2 and

    L3'=u−v y≥9/10−11/20=7/20.

4. L4 active: x+y≥10; the comparison L4≤L6 additionally forces y≥2. Therefore

    L4'=v y−u≥18/10−11/10=7/10.

5. L5 active: y≥4x+2, so y≥2 and x/y≤1/4. Equation(4) makes g positive, and

    L5'=5g−u+v y≥7/10.

6. L6 active: x≥8, and L6≤L4 forces y≤2. Thus y/x≤1/4. From(3),

    L6'=−g≥(15/64)x³≥120.

The minimum of these six rational margins is δ=2379/40960. The inequalities also hold for a nonconstant branch active on the plateau boundary.

Along an absolutely continuous solution, the finite affine minimum is absolutely continuous. At almost every time its derivative is an active branch derivative; derivatives at branch ties agree almost everywhere on equality sets. Hence

    F'(x(t),y(t))≥δ        a.e. while F<0,
    F'(x(t),y(t))≥0        a.e. at F=0.

The constant zero branch gives the global upper bound F≤0. Integrating establishes finite entry(2) and invariance of K.

## Global existence and positivity before entry

The derivative reasoning applies on any positive local solution. Since F is nondecreasing, F≥F0:=F(x0,y0)≤0. Every Li is then ≥F0. In particular

    x≤8−F0,     y≤4x+2−F0≤34−5F0.

These bounds prevent finite-time blowup. On this bounded nonnegative box the polynomial equations satisfy

    x'≥−(a x²+c y²)x,
    y'≥−(b x²+d y²+v)y.

The coefficients in parentheses are bounded uniformly over admissible rates; Gronwall bounds keep both coordinates positive at every finite time. Bounded measurable time coefficients and polynomial dependence on state give unique local Carathéodory solutions and continuation. The solution is thus global before the entry argument is applied. This avoids assuming permanence as an input to the proof.

## A decisive failed variant

L6 is redundant as a halfspace defining K: y≥x/4 and x+y≤10 already imply x≤8. Nevertheless L6 is essential to this global envelope proof. If it is removed, at (x,y)=(100,1/10), L4 is uniquely minimal among the five remaining branches. Under the baseline u=v=1 it has derivative y−1=−9/10<0. The five-face envelope therefore fails even though its plateau is the same pentagon. The additional branch repairs the active-region partition by forcing a total-concentration upper branch to compete against a coordinate upper branch.

This is the reusable design lesson: geometric redundancy can improve the global active-slope dynamics of an affine certificate. An invariant polytope's geometric facets alone need not produce an absorbing Lyapunov envelope.

## Why the smooth entropy function was blocked

For the baseline, g=(y−x)(2y−x)(y−2x). The centered entropy function has gradient (log(x/x*),log(y/y*)). On y=αx its cubic contribution is

    g log(y*/(α x*)).

For any positive center, choose α∈(1/2,1) if y*/x*≥1, and α∈(1,2) if y*/x*≤1. The cubic contribution is then positive and of order x³. The remaining immigration/outflow contribution has order x log x. Thus its derivative is positive sufficiently far out along that ray. The affine envelope succeeds on this explicit obstruction without requiring a globally decreasing smooth entropy function.
