EXACT ONE-QUERY ACQUISITION OF SPARSE NONCOMMUTATIVE POLYNOMIALS
Audit of prior card N68; 8 October 2026

STATUS

The exact reconstruction claim survives reconstruction of its proof. Under the
promise that f is a rational noncommutative polynomial with at most s nonzero
words, one fixed rational tuple of 2s-by-2s matrices has an injective full
matrix response map, with no supplied degree bound. The decoder returns the
ordered words and exact rational coefficients. This is a theorem about an
ideal exact matrix-evaluation oracle. It does not make the complete acquisition
cost bounded by n and s: response bits and oracle work can grow without bound.

The 2s dimension is optimal for a fixed one-query universal identification
tuple whose entries and the polynomial coefficients lie in the same field.
There is also an information-theoretic output lower bound of order sH plus
the number of bits needed to specify the support, for bounded-degree,
bounded-height subclasses. A one-variable refinement below reduces the
response description from linear in the degree to logarithmic in the degree
when monomials may be represented by binary exponents.

These are reconstructed algebraic proofs, not proof-assistant verification or
external expert review. Historical priority is UNKNOWN. Prior card N68 is
marked source-derived and unreviewed; its exact fixture results are diagnostics,
not universal proof evidence.

INTERFACE AND INJECTION

Let n,s >= 1, d=2s, B=n+1, and let the unknown be

    f = sum_{j=1}^r c_j w_j,     0 <= r <= s,

with distinct words w_j over x_1,...,x_n and nonzero c_j in Q. The empty word
is allowed. For 1 <= i <= n, define d-by-d upper-triangular integer matrices,
with indices a,b=0,...,d-1, by

    A_i[a,b] = binom(b,a) B^a i^(b-a)   if a <= b,
               0                       otherwise.

For v_d(z)=(1,z,...,z^(d-1)), the binomial theorem gives

    v_d(z) A_i = v_d(Bz+i).

If w=x_{i_1}...x_{i_l}, composition in the written word order gives

    alpha(w) = B^l + sum_{t=1}^l i_t B^(l-t),
    v_d(1) A_w = v_d(alpha(w));

set alpha(empty)=1. Since 1 <= i_t <= n=B-1, alpha(w) has base-B digits
1,i_1,...,i_l. The leading digit and all letter digits are in [1,B-1], so
the code is injective and decodes the ordered word. Also
log_2(alpha(w))=O(l log(n+1)).

The one exact oracle response is F=f(A_1,...,A_n). Since v_d(1) is the all-ones
row, its product with F gives the column sums:

    t_k = (v_d(1)F)_k = sum_{j=1}^r c_j alpha(w_j)^k,
    k=0,...,2s-1.

Thus one full response contains 2s exact power moments. Let V_s be the s-by-r
matrix (alpha_j^a) for 0<=a<s. The s-by-s Hankel matrix H=(t_(a+b)) factors

    H = V_s diag(c_1,...,c_r) V_s^T.

The first r rows of V_s form a square Vandermonde matrix, so V_s has column
rank r. The first r-by-r principal block H_r is invertible, with

    det(H_r) = (product_j c_j) (product_{i<j}(alpha_j-alpha_i)^2) != 0.

The full Hankel matrix has rank exactly r. This argument does not assume
positive coefficients: signed cancellation in early moments cannot reduce the
rank. Compute r by exact rational elimination. Then solve the invertible
leading Hankel system for the coefficients of

    q(z)=z^r + q_(r-1) z^(r-1) + ... + q_0
         = product_j (z-alpha_j).

The recurrence equations use t_0,...,t_(2r-1), all present in the response.
The roots are distinct positive integers. Exact Sturm root isolation over Q,
followed by rounding each promised integer root and checking q(alpha_j)=0,
recovers them without an uncharged polynomial-factorization oracle. Base-B
decoding recovers the words. A Vandermonde solve on t_0,...,t_(r-1) recovers
the c_j. If all moments vanish, the same Vandermonde argument gives r=0 and
f=0.

All decoder arithmetic is polynomial in n, s, and the actual bit length L_F of
the exact response. Clearing denominators and using determinant bounds gives
polynomial bit length for q. A Cauchy root bound has polynomial bit length;
Sturm bisection to intervals narrower than 1/2 takes polynomially many
bit-time steps because the roots are promised simple integers. Base-B
decoding and explicit word output therefore take polynomial bit time in
n,s,L_F.

LOWER BOUNDS

1. Query dimension for universal one-query identification.

Suppose one fixed d-by-d tuple T over a field K identifies every polynomial
with at most s nonzero words and coefficients in K. If d<2s, the minimal
polynomial mu of T_1 is a nonzero univariate polynomial of degree at most d,
so it has at most d+1<=2s nonzero terms. Partition those terms into two groups
of at most s terms and write mu=g-h. Then g and h are distinct, each is at
most s-sparse, and g(T_1)=h(T_1). This contradicts identification. Therefore
d>=2s. The construction above attains equality.

This lower bound is about identification, not identity testing. Lakhani and
Mukhopadhyay's primary source proves a dimension-s hitting point and the
matching dimension-s lower bound for detecting zero; a hitting point need not
distinguish two different nonzero polynomials. The split-minimal-polynomial
argument is the stronger identification lower bound. It applies to fixed
same-field tuples; it does not by itself cover extension-field query entries,
randomized per-input success, or a different interface whose query may depend
on supplied degree/height bounds.

2. Scalar-equation and response-bit lower bounds.

Fix s distinct words w_1,...,w_s and consider f_c=sum_j c_j w_j with arbitrary
coefficient vector c in K^s. For q matrix queries of dimensions d_1,...,d_q,
each returned matrix entry is a linear equation in c. If sum_t d_t^2<s, follow
the all-zero response path and choose a nonzero c in the common kernel of all
equations on that path. This nonzero polynomial and the zero polynomial then
produce the same transcript. The argument also handles adaptive queries,
because the next query is fixed by the shared zero transcript. Consequently
unrestricted coefficient acquisition needs sum_t d_t^2>=s field-valued
response coordinates. For one query this gives d>=sqrt(s); the universal
same-field word-identification bound d>=2s is stronger.

There is a separate information lower bound in bits. Fix n>=2, a maximum word
length D with n^D>=s, and choose s words of length exactly D. Choose each
coefficient independently from {1,...,2^H}. There are

    binom(n^D,s) 2^(Hs)

different inputs. An exact response that determines f must distinguish them,
so its worst-case encoded length is at least

    sH + log_2 binom(n^D,s) - O(1)

bits. In the regime n^D much larger than s this is
Omega(sH + s(D log_2 n - log_2 s)). With unbounded coefficient height or
unbounded degree, no uniform response-bit bound follows from n,s alone.
This lower bound applies to any uniquely decodable response representation;
it is not an assumption about a particular rational-number serializer.

For n=1, support choices among exponents 0,...,D require only
log_2 binom(D+1,s) bits if exponents are stored in binary. Thus a blanket
Omega(sD) support-bit lower bound would be false in the univariate case.

3. Refinement for one variable.

When n=1, write f(x)=sum_j c_j x^(e_j), with distinct e_j>=0. Let N be the
2s-by-2s nilpotent matrix with ones on the first superdiagonal and zero
elsewhere, and query J=I+N. Since N^(2s)=0,

    J^e = sum_(q=0)^(2s-1) binom(e,q) N^q.

The response F=f(J) exposes the 2s values

    mu_q = sum_j c_j binom(e_j,q)

on its q-th superdiagonal. Convert them to ordinary power moments using

    t_k = sum_(q=0)^k S(k,q) q! mu_q
        = sum_j c_j e_j^k,

where S(k,q) are Stirling numbers of the second kind. The same Hankel/Prony
decoder recovers the distinct integer exponents and rational coefficients.
This still uses one exact query of dimension 2s, and the minimal-polynomial
lower bound still applies. The query entries are only 0 and 1. For e_j<=D,
the moment numerators have bit length polynomial in s, coefficient heights,
and log(D+1), rather than proportional to D. The reusable representation can
store each exponent in binary, yielding O(s log(D+1)) support bits. If the
required output is the expanded word x repeated e_j times, expansion still
costs Omega(D); the refinement concerns a compact symbolic representation.

COST AND REUSABILITY

The exact decoded list of words and rational coefficients is a reusable sparse
representation: it can be evaluated at later rational matrix tuples without
the original oracle. The one-query claim is therefore an acquisition theorem
under the sparse promise, not merely a zero/nonzero test.

The query has n matrices of dimension 2s. Each entry has O(s log(n+1)) bits,
and an explicit dense input encoding costs O(n s^3 log(n+1)) bits (the matrices
are triangular, so a sparse encoding can be shorter). The oracle must accept
exact noncommuting matrix assignments and return all 4s^2 rational entries.
The total output length depends on the hidden words and coefficient
denominators; it is not bounded by n,s. As a conservative entrywise estimate,
if all word lengths are at most D and each coefficient numerator and
denominator has at most H bits, each returned rational has O(sH+sD log(n+1))
bits, so an ordinary full-matrix encoding has at most
O(s^2(sH+sD log(n+1))) bits. The lower bound above shows that substantial
degree/height dependence is unavoidable for n>=2.

The decoder is polynomial in the actual response size, not in a succinct
circuit-size description that may encode exponentially long words. The cost
of evaluating f on the query tuple, transferring the exact response, and
storing it must be charged. Matrix dimension and query count alone hide those
costs.

Validation is exact and checkable once the oracle response is trusted. A
validator can rebuild the decoded words' codes, check the annihilator roots,
recompute the 2s moments from the returned coefficient list, and compare them
with the response's column sums. Equality of those moments is sufficient:
the difference of two at-most-s-sparse candidates has at most 2s terms, and
the 2s distinct-code Vandermonde columns are independent. Recomputing the
whole matrix F is a stronger consistency check, with cost polynomial in the
explicit word and response sizes. These checks certify consistency with the
observed response and sparse promise; they do not certify that an untrusted
oracle returned the true f(A). That remains an oracle-trust or external
validation assumption.

NOISY/ROUNDED VARIANT: FIXED ABSOLUTE PRECISION FAILS

Take n=s=1. Then B=2, d=2, and the N68 query matrix is

    A_1 = [[1,1],[0,2]].

Round each response entry to the nearest multiple of Delta=2^(-p). For
f(x)=2^(-p-4) x, every entry of f(A_1) is strictly smaller than Delta/2 in
absolute value, so the rounded response is the zero matrix. The zero
polynomial has exactly the same rounded response. Thus no fixed p gives
universal acquisition over Q when coefficient heights are unbounded. Under an
adversarial absolute-error model with fixed epsilon, choose 0<|c|<epsilon;
the response c A_1 is compatible with the same zero observation.

The exact Hankel-rank and recurrence steps fail because rounding can turn all
moments into zero; exact rank is discontinuous at singular matrices. A
fixed-error or fixed-precision guarantee also cannot be inferred from the
exact code injection alone. A useful precision guarantee needs a separation
promise or a finite-height model, and the integer part of the response must
still be transmitted at its full, potentially unbounded size.

There is a direct positive variant for this original integer tuple. Suppose
only that every reduced coefficient denominator b_j is at most 2^H; the
numerators and word lengths may be unbounded. Let Q_max=2^(sH). The product
of the coefficient denominators is at most Q_max, and every exact response
entry F_ab is a rational with reduced denominator at most Q_max. If the oracle
returns the nearest multiple of 2^(-P), with P=2sH+2, then

    |F_ab - Y_ab| <= 2^(-P-1) < 1/(2 Q_max^2).

Distinct rationals with denominator at most Q_max are separated by at least
1/Q_max^2. The exact reduced fraction for F_ab is a convergent of the
continued fraction of Y_ab by Legendre's rational approximation bound.
Enumerate convergents with denominator at most Q_max and retain the unique one
within the certified error. This recovers every entry of F exactly; then the
original Hankel decoder applies unchanged. Continued fractions cost polynomial
bit time in the encoded rounded entry and sH. No degree bound or numerator
height bound is needed for the fractional-bit count. However, the integer part
of Y_ab still needs O(log |F_ab|) bits and can grow linearly with word length.
This trades degree-independent fractional precision for a response whose
integer payload and oracle evaluation cost grow with the degree. It is a
bounded-denominator exact-rational recovery statement, not generic robustness
to arbitrary floating-point noise.

A separate internal bounded-interface extension in the prior research
package uses a norm-one scaled affine tuple, supplied maximum degree D and
coefficient height H, and a caller-requested certified entrywise error. Its
stated sufficient precision is P=O(H+sD log(n+1)+s+log(s+1)) bits per entry.
That package reports an internal proof and finite diagnostics; it is not an
external validation, and its result does not rescue fixed precision or the
unbounded-height/unbounded-degree interface above. The scaled tuple keeps the
integer payload small and pays a degree-dependent fractional precision cost;
the unscaled integer tuple above can instead use bounded-denominator rational
reconstruction and pay in integer payload. In either interface the caller pays
to produce and transfer the requested precision and output bits.

PRIOR ART AND NOVELTY STATUS

Lakhani and Mukhopadhyay, ECCC TR26-120 revision 2 (July 2026), prove the
integer affine-action identity and a one-query hitting point of dimension s
for identity testing of s-sparse noncommutative polynomials of unbounded
degree. Their theorem is about detecting zero; their paper does not state the
2s-dimensional exact reconstruction result audited here. The 2s query is the
same affine action with twice the sparsity parameter, followed by standard
Prony/Hankel recovery.

Arvind, Mukhopadhyay, and Srinivasan, ECCC TR08-025 / Computational Complexity
19 (2010), already give deterministic black-box reconstruction of sparse
noncommutative polynomials in time polynomial in the degree bound, variable
count, and sparsity, via automata-based isolation and prefix search. Thus
sparse noncommutative interpolation itself is old. Ben-Or and Tiwari's 1988
primary paper gives classical deterministic sparse multivariate interpolation
in the commutative setting; the Prony/Hankel decoder is not claimed as new.

The narrower increment supported here is one fixed exact full-matrix query,
independent of degree, that identifies the entire sparse noncommutative
polynomial, with optimal 2s dimension in the same-field universal model and
all output bits charged. The novelty/priority of this composition is UNKNOWN:
the source comparison was bounded, not a complete literature search or
specialist review.
