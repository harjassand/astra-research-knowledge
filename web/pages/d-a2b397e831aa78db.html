# A sharp fractional-matching control of the annihilation/edge-cover gap

Status: independently reconstructed theorem with a complete mathematical argument below; not machine-formalized. Bibliographic novelty is unresolved. This is a modest graph-theoretic continuation of recent automated-conjecturing work, not a foundational breakthrough.

## Exact interface

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected graph, n=|V|, m=|E|. Sort degrees d_1 <= ... <= d_n. Define a(G)=max{k: sum_{i<=k} d_i <= m}. Let nu be maximum matching size and nu_f the optimum of max sum_e z_e subject to z_e>=0 and sum_{e incident v} z_e<=1. Let oct(G) be the minimum number of vertices whose deletion makes G bipartite. If G has no isolated vertices, let ec(G) be the minimum number of edges covering all vertices.

**Theorem.** For graphs without isolated vertices,

    ec(G) - a(G) <= ceil(nu_f(G)-nu(G)) <= ceil(oct(G)/2).

The intermediate statement a(G) >= floor(n-nu_f(G)) holds also with isolated vertices. No learning oracle, numerical tolerance, random graph assumption, or bounded order is assumed.

## 1. Fractional degree knapsack

Set

    K = max {sum_v y_v: 0<=y_v<=1, sum_v d_v y_v<=m}.

An exchange argument proves that an optimum fills the lowest degree entries first: if d_i<d_j, y_i<1, y_j>0, move a small amount from j to i, preserving cardinality and decreasing degree cost. Continue until at most one positive-degree item is partly filled. Consequently floor(K)=a, including zero-degree items and the case a=n. Equivalently, any fractional vector of total cardinality at least integer k has degree cost at least sum_{i<=k}d_i; this follows from the same exchange argument.

Let

    alpha_f = max {sum_v y_v: 0<=y_v<=1, y_u+y_v<=1 for uv in E}.

For every feasible y,

    sum_v d_v y_v = sum_{uv in E}(y_u+y_v) <= m.

Thus alpha_f <= K and floor(alpha_f)<=a. Under x_v=1-y_v, the alpha_f program is n minus fractional vertex cover. Upper bounds x_v<=1 do not change the minimum because replacing x_v>1 by 1 keeps every covering constraint valid. Finite-dimensional LP duality gives fractional vertex-cover optimum nu_f. Therefore

    a >= floor(n-nu_f).

This proof uses a supplied graph and its degree sequence. It does not establish that these quantities are autonomously acquired from scientific observations.

## 2. Edge cover versus matching

For an isolate-free graph, ec=n-nu. Here is an independent reconstruction. Take a maximum matching M. Unmatched vertices form an independent set; cover each by an incident edge in addition to M. This makes an edge cover of size nu+(n-2nu)=n-nu. Conversely, take a minimum edge cover and delete redundant edges until it is inclusion-minimal. Each remaining component is a star: a path of three edges would have a redundant middle edge, and a cycle would have a redundant edge. Selecting one edge from each star gives a matching of n-|C| edges. Hence nu>=n-ec and equality follows.

Combining with step 1,

    ec-a <= n-nu-floor(n-nu_f) = ceil(nu_f-nu).

The equality here is elementary integer arithmetic, since n and nu are integers.

## 3. Why the fractional matching gap is at most oct/2

Choose an optimal extreme point z of the fractional matching polytope. Its entries equal to 1 form disjoint edges; those vertices have no other positive incident entry. Consider the support graph of the strictly fractional edges. At an extreme point, the positive-edge columns restricted to the tight vertex constraints must be linearly independent: otherwise a small positive and negative perturbation preserves tight equalities, retains strict positivity, and satisfies all slack inequalities, contradicting extremality.

In any connected fractional component with p edges, q vertices, and L leaves, a leaf is slack because its unique incident edge has weight strictly below 1. Thus the number t of tight constraints is at most q-L. Extremality requires p<=t. A tree has p=q-1 and L>=2, impossible. A component with more than one cycle has p>q, impossible. A unicyclic component has p=q, so every vertex must be tight and L=0, and consequently it is a cycle. An even cycle permits an alternating perturbation, also impossible. An odd cycle with all constraints tight has every edge weight 1/2.

Thus z consists of disjoint unit matching edges and vertex-disjoint odd cycles with half-weight edges. If there are c odd cycles, replace each odd cycle of length ell by a matching of (ell-1)/2 edges. The resulting integer matching has size nu_f-c/2, so nu_f-nu<=c/2. Every odd-cycle transversal must hit each of these vertex-disjoint odd cycles, so c<=oct. This proves the second inequality.

This decomposition is classical fractional-matching theory; its proof is included to avoid treating a remembered theorem as decisive evidence.

## 4. Sharpness and boundary cases

For q disjoint triangles: n=3q, m=3q, all degrees 2, a=floor(3q/2), nu=q, nu_f=3q/2, ec=2q, oct=q. Therefore

    ec-a=ceil(q/2)=ceil(nu_f-nu)=ceil(oct/2).

The coefficient 1/2 and rounding are sharp on the stated class. This is not a claim of sharpness restricted to connected graphs.

Bipartite graphs have oct=0 and yield ec<=a. This includes the September AutoGraphForge example. Positive-degree regular graphs have a=floor(n/2), independently giving the reverse inequality a<=ec. Zero-regular graphs need separate treatment: ordinary edge cover does not exist, and a=n, so dividing by the common degree in the regular proof is invalid at degree zero.

With isolated vertices, retain the intermediate theorem or explicitly define ec_star=n-nu as an edge-and-singleton cover number. Never silently call ec_star an ordinary edge-cover number.

## 5. Falsification evidence and limitations

`work/r01_discovery/graph_probe.py` exhaustively enumerated all isolate-free labelled simple graphs of orders 2 through 6: 28,263 graphs. No violation of ec-a<=ceil(oct/2) occurred. `fractional_probe.py` additionally checked all 814 such graphs of orders 2 through 5 against exhaustive half-integral fractional-cover assignments. Independent brute-force edge-subset matching and edge-cover calculations agreed with the vertex-DP matching and Gallai formula. Results are in `results/r01_discovery/`. These are finite diagnostics, not the universal proof. The universal proof is the argument above; formal kernel verification remains undone.

## 7. Concrete downstream use and next step

This theorem replaces a graph-class dichotomy by a quantitative obstruction: positive ec-a implies a fractional matching integrality gap of at least ec-a-1 (strict inequality when using the ceiling) and forces oct >= 2(ec-a)-1. For integer t=ec-a>0, ceil(oct/2)>=t implies oct>=2t-1. This gives a cheap degree/matching certificate that a graph is far from bipartite, although it can be weak or negative and is not an OCT algorithm.

A genuine research-improvement question is whether a conjecturing engine can acquire the fractional relaxation as a reusable explanatory variable from the reversal between bipartite and regular examples, with all derivation and checking costs charged. The fractional variable was supplied by this investigator, not learned by a tested engine. The next falsifiable step is to hide nu_f/oct from the initial invariant table, supply the two directional examples plus a trusted linear-programming definition library, and preregister a task requiring the engine to propose the relaxation and prove this exact bound.
