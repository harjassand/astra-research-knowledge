Observability from Measurable Sets for One-Dimensional Heat
Equations with Bounded Spacetime Potentials ∗
Xiaomin Zhu †
Abstract
This paper study observability for the Dirichlet heat equation on a bounded interval with
a real bounded potential depending on space and time. We prove an observability inequality
from every measurable subset of spacetime with positive measure. The constant is uniform
over potentials with a prescribedL∞ bound. By duality, positive spacetime measure is
equivalent to null controllability by square-integrable distributed controls. Controls can also
be chosen bounded in time with values inL2. If almost every spatial slice of the observation
set has a fixed positive lower bound on its measure, the observability constant is bounded
above byC0eC1/T, uniformly in the locations of the slices. The proof combines observation
estimates on finite-dimensional spaces transported by the evolution with decay estimates for
the distance to those spaces.
Keywords.Heat equation; measurable observation sets; bounded potentials; null controllability.
Mathematics Subject Classification (2020).93B07, 93B05, 35K10.
1 Introduction
A zeroth-order term in a heat equation can describe local absorption or growth in a diffusive
medium. When the local rate varies with the position and changes during the evolution,
the corresponding coefficient depends on both space and time. Such spacetime-dependent
potentials arise, for example, in heat-conduction models involving moving inhomogeneities; see
[6]. Motivated by such models, we study a heat equation with a general bounded spacetime
potential.

These results lead naturally to the question studied here: does positive spacetime measure
remain sufficient when the potential is merely bounded and measurable in both space and time?
This question combines two difficulties. The observation set may have spatial slices with
empty interior and need not contain a product of two positive-measure sets. At the same time, a
general bounded potentialb(t,x )gives no spatial analyticity and no fixed spectral decomposition
that is preserved by the evolution.
Two lines of previous work are particularly relevant to this question. The first concerns
observability for parabolic equations with time-dependent lower-order terms. Phung and Wang
[12] considered
∂tu−∆u+a(x,t)u+β(x,t)·∇u= 0
on bounded convex domains. Their zeroth-order coefficient may depend on both space and time,
and they proved observability fromE×ω , whereE⊂ (0,T )has positive measure andω is a
fixed nonempty open subset of the spatial domain. Phung et al. [13, Theorem 4] also established
measurable-time observability for heat equations with bounded spacetime potentials, with explicit
dependence on the potential norm. More recently, Zhu and Zhuge [16] studied the dependence of
observability constants on time-dependent potentials and obtained refined estimates for Lipschitz
spacetime potentials. In these time-dependent results, the spatial observation region remains
open.
The second line concerns observation from measurable sets. For the free heat equation, Zhang
[15] proved observability from a product of measurable sets of positive measure. Apraiz et al. [3]
went further and established observability from an arbitrary measurable subset of spacetime with
positive measure. Thus, for the free heat equation, no product structure of the observation set
is required. For parabolic equations with analytic coefficients, Escauriaza et al. [7, 8] obtained
measurable-set observability from quantitative analyticity estimates. For equations with a
potential, measurable spatial observation sets are also available in some autonomous settings. In
particular, Zhu and Zhuge [16, Theorem 1.5] proved in one dimension an observability inequality
from E×ω , with bothE and ω of positive measure, for bounded time-independent potentials
b=b(x). Related measurable-set results for stationary operators can be found in [1, 2, 5, 11].
The present paper combines these two directions. We keep the general spacetime geometry
of [3], while allowing a real potentialb = b(t,x )∈L∞((0,T )×I )with arbitrary measurable
dependence on both variables. More precisely, we prove that every measurable setD⊂ (0,T )×I
with|D|> 0is an observation set for the one-dimensional heat equation with such a potential. For
everyM≥ 0, the observability constant can be chosen uniformly for allb satisfying∥b∥L∞≤M .
By duality, this yields null controllability of(1.1) from every positive-measure spacetime set.
We also obtain controls inL∞(0,T;L 2(I)). If, in addition, the spatial slices
Dt ={x∈I: (t,x)∈D}
satisfy|Dt|≥m> 0for almost everyt, the observability constant admits an upper bound of
the formC 0eC1/T,uniformly in the locations and measurable motion of the slices.
The main difficulty is caused by the interaction between the spacetime potential and the
spectral decomposition. For a time-independent potentialb =b(x), the spectral subspaces of the
operator−∂2
x +b(x)are preserved by the evolution. Observation on low frequencies can then be
combined with the decay of the complementary high frequencies. This structure is lost for a
general b(t,x ). Indeed, multiplication byb(t,x )transfers energy between fixed Dirichlet modes.
Hence the equation for the high-frequency component contains a forcing term generated by the
low-frequency component, and the usual high-frequency decay estimate is no longer closed. One
might instead use the spectral subspaces of−∂2
x +b(t,· )at each time, but these subspaces need
not be compatible with the evolution. Moreover, the assumptionb∈L ∞ gives no time regularity
with which to differentiate the corresponding spectral projections.
To overcome this difficulty, we replace fixed spectral subspaces by finite-dimensional subspaces
transported by the actual evolution. Starting from the span of the firstN Dirichlet eigenfunctions,
2
we evolve this space with the equation itself. The resulting moving subspaces are compatible
with the nonautonomous dynamics by construction and require no time derivatives ofb. We
prove a measurable-set sampling estimate on these spaces, with cost of ordereCN, and a decay
estimate of ordere−cN 2(t−s) for the distance of a solution to the moving subspace. Balancing
these two estimates gives a one-time interpolation inequality. A spatial slicing argument and the
telescoping series method of [12, 3, 14] then give observability from the full spacetime setD.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 states the main results and introduces
the evolution framework. Section 3 establishes the observation and decay estimates for the moving
subspaces. Section 4 combines these estimates to prove observability and null controllability,
and ends with examples of measurable observation sets.
