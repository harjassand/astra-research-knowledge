1. CLASSIFICATION OF REAL COMPONENTWISE TERNARY SUBSPACES

Let U be a finite-dimensional real linear subspace of R^N closed under
componentwise triple products: u*v*w belongs to U for u,v,w in U.
Call coordinate i active if its evaluation functional ell_i on U is nonzero.
On active coordinates, group i~j exactly when ell_i=+/-ell_j. Within a
block B, choose signs s_i so ell_i=s_i ell_(representative).

Choose a generic a in U such that a_i is nonzero on every active
coordinate and a_i^2 differs across different blocks. This is possible
because each forbidden equality is one of finitely many proper linear
hyperplanes ell_i=0 or ell_i+/-ell_j=0. Within B, a_i=s_i a_B.

Ternary closure implies a^(componentwise (2m+1)) belongs to U for
all m>=0. If there are b blocks, the b distinct positive numbers a_B^2
give an invertible Vandermonde matrix. Linear combinations of the first
b odd powers therefore isolate, for each B, its signed indicator
  b_B,i=s_i if i belongs to B, and 0 otherwise.
Each b_B belongs to U. Conversely, by the definition of the coordinate
functional equivalence, every u in U is constant with signs on each B
and is zero on inactive coordinates. Hence
  U=span{b_B : B a block}.
The block supports are disjoint and the number of blocks equals dim U.
This proves the classification, including potentially inactive coordinates.

2. WEIGHTED CUBIC REDUCTION

For the model f=-Kx-alpha*x^3 with alpha_i>0, let W be the smallest
K-invariant alpha-weighted-ternary-closed subspace containing e1.
Set D=diag(sqrt(alpha_i)) and U=D W. Since
  D(alpha*u*v*w)=(Du)*(Dv)*(Dw),
U is an ordinary ternary-closed subspace, so the classification applies.

For every block B, set Z_B=sum_(i in B)1/alpha_i and define
  v_B,i=s_i/(sqrt(alpha_i)*sqrt(Z_B)) if i belongs to B,
  v_B,i=0 otherwise.
These vectors are Euclidean orthonormal and span W. Let V have these
columns and choose their ordering/sign so the measured-node block is first.
The fact that e1 belongs to W forces coordinate 1 to be a singleton block:
a signed block with multiple members could not contain the vector e1.
Thus v_1=e1 and V^T e1=e1 in reduced coordinates.

Write x=V xi on W. Since K preserves W,
  Kbar=V^T K V is symmetric positive definite, K V=V Kbar.
For i in B,
  alpha_i (V xi)_i^3
    =s_i xi_B^3/(sqrt(alpha_i) Z_B^(3/2))
    =v_B,i xi_B^3/Z_B.
Consequently the zero-initial IO map is exactly that of
  xi_dot=-Kbar xi-alphabar*xi^3+e1 u, y=xi1,
  alphabar_B=1/Z_B>0.
The dimension is r=dim W. This is an exact invariant-subspace reduction,
not an approximation or Schur complement.

The reduced model has full closure. Otherwise a proper Kbar-invariant,
alphabar-ternary-closed subspace containing e1 would map under V to a
proper subspace of W with all its defining properties, contradicting
minimality of W. Thus the previously proved full-closure recovery theorem
applies to the reduced system.

For a homogeneous physical coefficient alpha, a block of size |B|
has reduced coefficient alpha/|B|. The heterogeneous-positive class is
therefore needed to express arbitrary reductions, even if the original
physical model was homogeneous. The effective coefficients do not
identify the individual alpha_i when blocks contain multiple nodes.

3. SUPPORT POLYNOMIALS WITHOUT A SUPPLIED PHYSICAL DIMENSION

For any nonzero paired direction v, its moments are
  p_m=sum_i v_i^2 (alpha_i v_i^2)^m.
The associated positive measure has some finite number s of distinct
positive atoms. Acquire successive moments until H_s is positive definite
and H_(s+1) is singular, where H_l=(p_(i+j))_(i,j<l). The needed moments
are p0,...,p_(2s). No upper bound on N is needed for this stopping rule.

Why flatness is an exact certificate rather than a guessed support size:
solve H_s c=-(p_s,...,p_(2s-1)) and set
  P(t)=t^s+sum_(j<s)c_j t^j.

  integral P(t)^2 dmu(t)=0.
For ANY positive measure compatible with these finitely many moments,

physical beta values aggregate positive weights; no cancellation can hide
additional distinct atoms. Since actual beta>0, P(0) is nonzero.
