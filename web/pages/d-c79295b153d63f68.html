## An elementary cylinder lemma

For nonzero E in C[x,y] and integer d>=2, define

`M_d(E)=(d-1)E det Hess E-d gradE^T adj(HessE) gradE`.

Then M_d(E)=0 **if and only if** E is a polynomial in one affine-linear
form, with constants included. The proof below has no Keller-map or HC
inversion prerequisite.

Write E=g^m h for an irreducible g not dividing h. Set q=grad g,
r=grad h. Rescale its exact derivatives as

```
gradE=g^(m-1)[m h q+g r],
HessE=g^(m-2)[m(m-1)h qq^T
             +mg(h Hessg+qr^T+rq^T)+g² Hessh].
```

The determinant and adjugate identities for a 2x2 rank-one matrix give

`M_d(E)/g^(3m-3) = -m²(d+m-1)h³ K_g modulo g`,
`K_g=gradg^T adj(Hessg) gradg`.

This is also valid for m=1: the apparent g^-1 in the rescaled Hessian
cancels, and direct reduction modulo g gives -d h³K_g. The extra
`qr^T+rq^T` matrix contributes zero to its contraction by q. Since
d+m-1 is a nonzero integer in characteristic zero, M_d(E)=0 makes
g divide K_g.

On the function field of the irreducible curve g=0, the tangent
derivation `D=g_y partial_x-g_x partial_y` is nonzero. If g_y is not
zero in that field, then `D(g_x/g_y)=K_g/g_y²=0`. Its constants are
exactly C: a nonconstant invariant would be transcendental, and the
one-variable function field would be a finite separable extension of
C of that invariant, forcing D to vanish. Thus g_x/g_y=lambda in C.
The polynomial g_x-lambda g_y belongs to(g) and has smaller degree
than g, so it is identically zero. It follows that g is univariate
in a linear form; irreducibility over C makes g affine-linear. If
g_y vanishes in the function field, its smaller degree forces g_y=0
identically, giving the vertical affine-line case. Every irreducible
factor of E is therefore a line.

These lines must be parallel. For the top homogeneous part E_e of
degree e>=2, Euler's identities imply

`M_d(E_e)=-(d+e-1)/(e-1) E_e det Hess(E_e)`.

It follows that det Hess(E_e)=0. To prove the needed binary homogeneous
classification directly, write E_e=x^e p(y/x). Its Hessian determinant
is `(e-1)x^(2e-4)[e p p''-(e-1)(p')²]`. At a root of p of multiplicity
j, the lowest coefficient is a nonzero multiple of j(j-e). Hence every
root has multiplicity e. Since deg p<=e, p is either constant or one
full e-th power, and E_e is a power of one linear form. The leading
parts of all acquired affine factors are thus parallel. Their product
is univariate in that form. Degree1 and constant E are immediate cases;
the converse follows from its rank-one Hessian and parallel gradient.

## Full nonlinear Hessian correction and decisive coefficient

Use the global coordinate map `(b,s,eta)->(b,s-F(b,eta),eta)` and set
`W=h+G1s+G2eta+G2H`. Its determinant is1. The corrected old Hessian is

`J_Phi^T H_old J_Phi=H_new W+W_s Hess_(b,eta)F`,

where the correction matrix has a zero s row/column. Dropping this
second-derivative term would change the conclusion.

Suppose both tails are nonzero. Their global zero first jets imply
F_etaeta!=0 and d=deg_s H>=2. Let H_d be its nonzero highest coefficient,
and E=G2 H_d. The leading base/base block is s^d HessE; the base/s
column is d s^(d-1)gradE; the s,s entry is d(d-1)E s^(d-2).
The eta,eta entry has leading d E F_etaeta s^(d-1). All eta/base
entries have s-degree<=d-1, so their determinant contributions have
degree<=4d-4. The unique highest determinant degree is4d-3, with
coefficient

`d² E F_etaeta M_d(E)`.                            (1)

The universal bordered3x3 coefficient is d M_d(E), giving(1) with
no bound on d. Constancy would force M_d(E)=0. The cylinder lemma
therefore forces E and all its divisors to be polynomials in one common
linear form. In particular it would force G2 cylindrical.

For the acquired G2 this fails with a small exact certificate: the x²
and xy coefficients of gradG2 are(6,2) and(4,0), determinant-8.
A cylindrical polynomial's gradient coefficients span at most one
constant direction. Conversely a constant annihilator integrates to a
linear-coordinate cylinder. This rank filter avoids polynomial
factorization. An independent divisor certificate is also saved:
`G2/2=(x²-17)y+x³+2x²-25x` is primitive and irreducible, because the
coefficient and constant have remainder34-8x and gcd1. This nonlinear
irreducible factor cannot divide a cylindrical polynomial.

The same argument for arbitrary nonzero G2 is a reusable **necessary
first-jet filter**: simultaneous normalized nonlinear two-shear tails
and constant determinant require G2 cylindrical. Passing this filter
does not supply constancy, invertibility, a legal chart, or a collision.

## All missing-tail cases

If H=0,F!=0, write psi=P(b,eta)+rho G1(b). The coefficient of rho
in its full Hessian determinant is `-G1 F_etaeta K_G1`, with
`K_G1(0,0)=-3700`; it is nonzero. If F=0,H!=0, exchange residual
variables: the eta coefficient is `-G2 H_ss K_G2`, with
`K_G2(0,0)=9248`, also nonzero. If both vanish, the determinant is
`(Jac G)²`, where

`JacG=-4[(x²+8x+17)y+x³+10x²+25x+2]`,

which is nonconstant. Thus no omitted affine or zero-tail branch can
evade the theorem under the global zero-jet hypotheses.
