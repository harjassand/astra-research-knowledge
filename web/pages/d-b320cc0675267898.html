# Leaf 13 — What degree, singularities, Hessians, and smoothness certify

**Status.** The Fermat counterexample below is an explicit, independently derived exact determinant representation. The statements attributed to Mignon–Ressayre, Alper–Bogart–Velasco, and the 2026 permanent manuscript are source-reported from their primary texts; I did not independently replay the full proofs of those theorems. The prior N79 card was only a supplied-summary extraction. This leaf replaces its O(rd) slogan with a sparse matrix construction, exact dimensions, and the relevant theorem boundaries. No superpolynomial permanent lower bound is claimed.

## Interface and counterexample

Work over `C`; write `dc` for exact affine determinant size and `bdc` for the least fixed size whose affine determinants converge coefficientwise to the polynomial. For integers `r,d >= 2`, set

\[
F_{r,d}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^r.
\]

It has degree `r` in exactly `d` essential variables. Its projective hypersurface in `P^{d-1}` is smooth: its partial derivatives are `r x_i^{r-1}`, whose common affine zero is only the origin. In particular its affine singular locus has codimension `d`.

There is an exact affine determinant for `F_{r,d}` of order

\[
N=d(r-1)+3,
\]

with `dr` variable-bearing entries and only constants `0,1,-1` besides those entries. Here is a direct construction. Take a directed acyclic graph with source `s`, sink `t`, and vertices `v_{i,j}` for `1<=i<=d`, `1<=j<=r-1`. For each `i`, put a length-`r` path from `s` through its own `r-1` internal vertices to `t`, and label every edge on that path by `x_i`. There are `V=d(r-1)+2` graph vertices and exactly `d` source-to-sink paths, with path weights `x_i^r`.

Let `A` be the weighted adjacency matrix, with `A_{v,u}` equal to the weight on `u -> v`, in a topological ordering. Then `A` is strictly triangular, `B=I_V-A` has determinant one, and `(B^{-1})_{t,s}` is the sum of all source-to-sink path weights. Therefore

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}B&e_s\\-e_t^\top&0\end{pmatrix}
= e_t^\top B^{-1}e_s
= \sum_{i=1}^d x_i^r.
\]

The displayed matrix has order `V+1=d(r-1)+3`. It has `dr` variable entries, `V` diagonal ones, and two nonzero border entries, so its sparse description has `2dr-d+4` nonzero entries. For comparison, the binary arithmetic formula has exactly `d(r-1)+(d-1)=dr-1` operation gates; Valiant's 1979 formula-to-determinant construction gives the coarser order bound `dr+1`. The graph construction above is the explicit sharper bound used here.

The zero

\[
p=(1,\ldots,1,\alpha),\qquad \alpha^r=-(d-1),
\]

has every coordinate nonzero and satisfies `F_{r,d}(p)=0`. Its Hessian is diagonal with entries `r(r-1)p_i^{r-2}`, so it has rank exactly `d`. Thus this one family simultaneously has degree `r`, a smooth projective zero set, affine singular locus `{0}`, and full-rank Hessian at a zero, while its exact determinant order is at most `d(r-1)+3=O(rd)`.

This is the decisive counterexample to any proposed universal lower bound based only on degree, number of variables, smoothness, and such Hessian/singular-locus data that grows asymptotically faster than `rd`. It does **not** rule out a stronger invariant that uses additional structure of the permanent.

## What the familiar invariants buy

**Degree.** An affine-linear determinant of order `n` has degree at most `n`; the same is true in the fixed-order coefficientwise border closure. Hence `dc(f), bdc(f) >= deg(f)` for a nonzero polynomial. For `perm_m`, this is only `m`. On the Fermat family it is only `r`, while the explicit upper bound is `d(r-1)+3`.

**Singular locus.** The singular locus of the ambient determinant hypersurface `det_n=0` is the rank-`<=n-2` locus, of codimension four in `Mat_n`. Alper–Bogart–Velasco prove, for an exact homogeneous representation of degree greater than two, that `dc(f) >= codim Sing(V(f))+1` whenever this codimension exceeds four. For `F_{r,d}` this gives `d+1` when `r>2,d>=5`, which is only linear in `d` despite degree `r` and explicit order `O(rd)`. For `perm_m`, matrices with two zero columns form a `m^2-2m` dimensional subspace of its singular locus, so `codim Sing(V(perm_m)) <= 2m`; this method therefore yields at most a linear-scale bound. The 2026 cubic paper itself records this limitation.

**Hessian at a zero.** Mignon–Ressayre show that the Hessian of `det_n` at any zero has rank at most `2n`. By the chain rule, if `f=det_n(L(x))` and `f(a)=0`, then `rank Hess(f)(a)<=2n`. For every `m>=2` in characteristic zero, `perm_m` has a zero where its Hessian has full rank `m^2`; this gives the classical exact lower bound `dc(perm_m)>=m^2/2`. Fermat gives only `dc(F_{r,d})>=d/2` from its full-rank Hessian witness.

At a smooth zero of a target polynomial, that same Hessian implication also passes to a fixed-size border representation over `C`: if determinant polynomials converge coefficientwise to `f`, the implicit function theorem supplies nearby zeros converging to the target zero; Hessians converge there, and the rank-`<=2n` condition is closed. Fermat's displayed point is smooth, so `bdc(F_{r,d})>=d/2`. This is still much weaker than the polar-count bound below when `r` grows.

**Smoothness / polar count.** The 2026 cubic manuscript proves that every nonzero homogeneous degree-`r` form in `d>=2` variables with smooth projective zero set satisfies

\[
\operatorname{bdc}(G),\operatorname{dc}(G)\ \ge\ \frac{(r-1)(d-1)}{4e}.
\]

It also proves the same bound from a smooth lowest-degree homogeneous Taylor term of a possibly nonhomogeneous polynomial. Its proof counts `r(r-1)^{d-2}` simple intersections with first polars and transfers them through the determinant's left/right-kernel incidence. This is a real improvement over degree, singular-locus codimension, or one Hessian rank: it certifies a product-scale lower bound and applies to border representations. On `F_{r,d}`, it is order `rd`, matching the explicit `O(rd)` determinant construction above. A June 2026 primary arXiv preprint gives the diagonal instance `bdc(sum_{i=1}^d x_i^d) >= (d-1)^2/(4e)`, also matching its `O(d^2)` upper bound in order.

## Application and ceiling for the permanent

Every affine restriction of `perm_m` has degree at most `m` and at most `m^2` essential variables. Further affine restrictions cannot increase either bound. The smooth-initial-form theorem therefore has an `O(m^3)` ceiling on this input, even if the same theorem is reapplied after restrictions. Fermat shows why smoothness and the degree-variable product cannot be iterated as a universal route beyond its own `O(rd)` scale. This is a method ceiling, not an upper bound on `dc(perm_m)`.

The cubic manuscript reaches this ceiling up to constants. For `k>=128`, set

\[
q=\lfloor(k-1)/3\rfloor,\quad r=k-2q,\quad d=\lfloor k^2/100\rfloor.
\]

It constructs a smooth homogeneous `G` of degree `r` in `d` variables and an affine map `Phi_m` with `G=perm_m o Phi_m` for every `m>=11k-2q`. It records `r-1>=k/4` and `d-1>=k^2/200`, hence `bdc(perm_m)>=dc(perm_m)>=k^3/(3200e)`. Taking `k=floor(m/11)` for `m>=1408` and using `k>=m/12` yields the paper's explicit `m^3/(5,529,600 e)` lower bound. The claim is polynomial, not superpolynomial.

## Evidence, costs, and status

- **Exact Fermat witness:** input dimension `d`; degree `r`; formula gates `dr-1`; determinant order `d(r-1)+3`; `dr` variable entries; no border limit or coefficient growth is needed. The displayed identity is the proof. The construction works over any field; the smoothness/Hessian discussion here is over characteristic zero.
- **Degree test:** `n>=r`; exact and border.
- **Singular-locus test:** `n>=codim Sing+1` under the Alper–Bogart–Velasco hypotheses; exact only. On `perm_m`, the known two-zero-column subset bounds this codimension by `2m`.
- **Hessian test:** `n>=rank(Hess_f(a))/2` at a zero for exact representations; also for border at a smooth zero by the local continuation argument above. It gives `m^2/2` for the permanent in characteristic zero, not cubic.
- **Smooth polar test:** `(r-1)(d-1)/(4e)` for exact and border, including smooth initial forms. For permanent restrictions this is at most cubic in `m`; the explicit Fermat determinant gives an `O(rd)` obstruction to universal degree/smoothness-only escalation.
- **Not replayed here:** the full primary proof of the permanent's cubic smooth-restriction theorem and the cited Hessian witness construction. The N79 repository card was source-derived from a supplied summary; this leaf now links the primary texts but does not claim independent external review.

## Falsifier and restart

**Falsifier.** A symbolic check of the displayed block determinant that does not equal `sum x_i^r` would falsify the new explicit counterexample (the Schur-complement/path-sum identity exposes exactly what to check). A claim that degree, `d`, projective smoothness, affine singular-locus codimension, and Hessian rank alone imply a universal lower bound `omega(rd)` is falsified by `F_{r,d}` and its order-`d(r-1)+3` representation. At the permanent level, the stated `O(m^3)` ceiling is falsified only if the proposed argument uses additional information beyond `r<=m,d<=m^2` and the cited smooth-form theorem; a degree/smoothness-only claim cannot pass this barrier.

**Restart.** Look for a permanent-specific invariant that survives arbitrary affine restriction and coefficientwise degeneration yet sees more than degree, dimension, smoothness, singular-locus codimension, and one Hessian rank. The natural next audit is whether a genuinely permanent-specific dual/conormal or representation-theoretic quantity has a lower bound that is not controlled solely by the `r x d` smooth-polar profile. Keep the source's cubic result separate from any claim of superpolynomial separation.
