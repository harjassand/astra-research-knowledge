# Independent concentration audit

Date: 16 October 2026. Frozen input: `originals/conformal_packet/RESEARCH_REPORT.md`, together with the algebraic calibration identity in its `verify_compatibility.py`. No constructor report was consulted. This audit was requested independently and does not constitute external verification or establish historical novelty.

## Verdict and exact scope

The proposed bound is valid for each fixed `r>0`, for compatible periodic `W^{1,4}` maps, and for zero-boundary `W^{1,4}_0` maps on bounded planar domains. All quantities must use one common integration measure. Write `G=|Dphi|`, `t=sqrt(Q_r)>0`, and assume `A_r=1`. There is a finite constant `K_r`, independent of the map, `M`, and `Q_r`, such that

\[
 \frac{|C_r|}{t}\le K_r\left[(Mt)^{1/3}
       +\left(\int_{G>M}G^4\right)^{1/4}\right],\qquad Mt\le1. \tag{A}
\]

The constants produced below are uniform for `r` in any compact subinterval of `(0,infinity)`, provided the interpolation constants are chosen uniformly there. They are not asserted uniform as `r` approaches zero or infinity. No optimal constant is claimed.

For a fixed positive lower bound `|C_r|/t>=rho`, a sufficiently small `delta=delta(r,rho)>0` gives a fixed positive fourth-power tail above `delta/t`. In fact the selected superlevel set has **two-sided** measure bounds `c(r,rho) Q_r^2 <= measure <= C(r,rho) Q_r^2`. This does not imply spatial localization into finitely many balls: independently checked disjoint clouds have the same ratio and can have any prescribed weak energy limit, including a diffuse limit.

This proves neither `Gamma_r<=1` nor a compatible negative Jensen field. Its analytic input is the source's written mixed-derivative/Hodge proof of (10); the accompanying finite checks do not formalize that analytic theorem.

## 1. Coercivity and constants

Use the source notation

\[
 \sigma=r+1,\quad \kappa=r(r+2)/3,\quad q=\sigma a+d,
 \quad P_r=q[2aq-(r+2)a^2+rc^2-2bc]-\kappa a(a^2+c^2).
\]

Thus `C_r=integral P_r` and `Q_r=integral(q^2+kappa c^2)`. Matrix norms are Frobenius norms.

Here is a direct coercivity check rather than an inference from a status label. Put `alpha=3r/[8(r+2)]`, `tau=(2r+1)/3`, `w=ab+cd`, `z=(u,v,j)`, and

\[
 M_r=\begin{pmatrix}
 \tau^2&1/3&2\tau\\
 1/3&1&-2\\
 2\tau&-2&16/3+1/\alpha
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

The exact `B=0,k=2/3` calibration identity is

\[
 J_r=\alpha z^T M_rz+\frac{4\alpha}{3}w^2. \tag{B}
\]

Its leading principal minors are `tau^2`, `4r(r+1)/9`, and `16(r+1)/27`, all positive for `r>0`. Consequently, with

\[
 m_r=\frac{2\alpha\det M_r}{(\operatorname{tr}M_r)^2}>0,
 \qquad J_r(A)\ge m_r |A|^4, \tag{C}
\]

we have `B:=||G||_4 <= B_0:=m_r^{-1/4}` when `A_r=1`. Indeed `lambda_min(M_r)>=4 det(M_r)/(tr M_r)^2` and `(u+v)^2<=2|z|^2`. The deliberately crude constant in (C) is explicit and sufficient.

Let `N_r` be a finite constant in the source's estimate (10), after the weighted defect is absorbed:

\[
 \|a\|_{8/3}^2\le N_r t B. \tag{D}
\]

One can take `N_r=C_sigma max(1,kappa^{-1/2})`, where `C_sigma` is the constant in source (10). Let

\[
 h=\sqrt{\sigma^2+1},\quad
 L_a=2h+r+2+\sqrt\kappa+\kappa N_r,\quad L_c=r+2.
\]

For **every measurable set E**, termwise Holder gives the useful local bound

\[
 \frac{\int_E|P_r|}{t}
 \le B\left[L_a\|a1_E\|_4+L_c\|c1_E\|_4\right]. \tag{E}
\]

For clarity, the six relevant bounds after division by `t` are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 2\int_E|aq^2|/t & 2hB\|a1_E\|_4\\
 (r+2)\int_E|qa^2|/t &(r+2)B\|a1_E\|_4\\
 r\int_E|qc^2|/t &rB\|c1_E\|_4\\
 2\int_E|qbc|/t &2B\|c1_E\|_4\\
 \kappa\int_E|ac^2|/t &\sqrt\kappa B\|a1_E\|_4\\
 \kappa\int_E|a^3|/t &\kappa N_r B\|a1_E\|_4.
\end{array}
\]

The last line uses `||a||_{8/3}^2 ||a1_E||_4`; the fifth uses `||c||_2 ||a1_E||_4 ||c||_4`. Thus (E) retains a genuinely local factor despite the global compatible interpolation. It even controls the absolute cubic density.

## 2. Truncation and the exponent 1/3

Let `L={G<=M}`, `H={G>M}`, and `T(M)=integral_H G^4`. Equality belongs to the low set. By (D),

\[
 \int_L |a|^4\le M^{4/3}\int |a|^{8/3}
 \le M^{4/3}(N_rtB)^{4/3}.
\]

The fourth root, not a square root, gives

\[
 \|a1_L\|_4\le N_r^{1/3}(MtB)^{1/3},\qquad
 \|c1_L\|_4\le\kappa^{-1/4}(Mt)^{1/2}. \tag{F}
\]

The second inequality follows from `integral_L c^4 <= M^2 integral c^2 <= M^2 t^2/kappa`. On the high set both localized fourth norms are at most `T(M)^{1/4}`. Define

\[
 K_0=L_aN_r^{1/3}B_0^{4/3}+L_c\kappa^{-1/4}B_0,
 \qquad K_1=(L_a+L_c)B_0.
\]

Equations (E)-(F) imply, whenever `Mt<=1`,

\[
 \frac{\int_L|P_r|}{t}\le K_0(Mt)^{1/3},\qquad
 \frac{\int_H|P_r|}{t}\le K_1T(M)^{1/4}. \tag{G}
\]

Here `(Mt)^{1/2}<=(Mt)^{1/3}` is used only in the stated range. Taking `K_r=max(K_0,K_1)` proves (A). Without that range, one should retain both powers rather than absorb them.

## 3. Quantitative tail, area, and higher moments

Assume `|C_r|/t>=rho>0` and choose

\[
 \delta=\min\{1,(\rho/(2K_0))^3\},\qquad M=\delta/t.
\]

Then the low cubic mass is at most `rho t/2`, so

\[
 \left|\int_H P_r\right|\ge\rho t/2,\qquad
 T(M)\ge\eta:=\left(\rho/(2K_1)\right)^4. \tag{H}
\]

This is a positive fraction of total fourth-power mass, since `T(M)/integral G^4 >= m_r eta`. It is also a positive fraction of `J_r` energy: `integral_H J_r >=m_r eta`, while total `J_r` energy is one.

Chebyshev gives the proposed area upper bound

\[
 \operatorname{measure}(H)\le B_0^4\delta^{-4}t^4
 =m_r^{-1}\delta^{-4}Q_r^2. \tag{I}
\]

There is also a lower bound. A crude pointwise constant is

\[
 |P_r(A)|\le D_r|A|^3,\qquad
 D_r=h(2h+2r+4)+\kappa.
\]

Using (H) and Holder on `H`,

\[
 \rho t/2\le D_r B_0^3\operatorname{measure}(H)^{1/4},
\]

so

\[
 \operatorname{measure}(H)\ge
 \left(\frac{\rho}{2D_rB_0^3}\right)^4Q_r^2. \tag{J}
\]

Thus the selected small-threshold superlevel set has measure comparable to `Q_r^2`. This statement is about total measure and amplitude, not diameter or the number of connected components.

One may even select a fixed amplitude band. Choose the low threshold using `rho/(4K_0)` and take `R>=max(delta,4D_r B_0^4/rho)`. Since

\[
 \frac{\int_{G>R/t}|P_r|}{t}\le D_rB_0^4/R\le\rho/4,
\]

the band `delta/t<G<=R/t` carries signed cubic integral of magnitude at least `rho t/2` and positive fourth-power mass. Its measure has the same two-sided `Q_r^2` bounds, with the corresponding constants.

For every finite `p>4`, (H) also gives the necessary higher-moment divergence

\[
 \int G^p\ge \eta\delta^{p-4}Q_r^{-(p-4)/2}. \tag{K}
\]

Consequently bounded higher moments exclude fixed positive cubic ratio as `Q_r` tends to zero. Optimizing (A) with `T(M)<=M^{4-p} integral G^p` yields, for bounded `integral G^p` and sufficiently small `Q_r`, the valid, not claimed optimal, rate

\[
 |C_r|/\sqrt{Q_r}
 \le C_{r,p}\left(\int G^p\right)^{1/(3p-8)}
 Q_r^{(p-4)/(2(3p-8))}. \tag{L}
\]

The amplitude `Q_r^{-1/2}`, area `Q_r^2`, and moment exponent in (K) are attained by isotropic concentration and by the clouds below. The truncation exponent `1/3` itself is not asserted optimal.

## 4. Integration measure and domain scope

For general `A_r>0`, the homogeneous version is

\[
 \frac{|C_r|}{\sqrt{A_rQ_r}}
 \le K_r\left[
  \left(\frac{M\sqrt{Q_r}}{\sqrt{A_r}}\right)^{1/3}
  +\left(\frac{\int_{G>M}G^4}{A_r}\right)^{1/4}\right],
 \quad M\sqrt{Q_r}\le\sqrt{A_r}. \tag{M}
\]

This formula is invariant under multiplication of the entire integration measure by a positive constant. It resolves the normalized-average issue: use the same measure in `A,Q,C`, norms, and the tail. The threshold is `delta sqrt(A_r/Q_r)` before normalization; the corresponding measure bound is `measure(H)<=Q_r^2/(m_r delta^4 A_r)`.

On a torus of physical area `V` with normalized averages, (I)-(J) concern normalized area. Physical area is `V` times that quantity. With unnormalized Lebesgue integrals throughout and `A_r=1`, (I)-(J) concern physical area directly. One cannot mix normalized `A,Q,C` with a physical area conclusion and silently omit `V`.

The interpolation constant is unchanged by isotropic rescaling of the square torus. For `W^{1,4}_0(Omega)`, zero extension into a surrounding square preserves compatibility and unnormalized integrals. Formula (M) transfers the result to normalized averages on `Omega` as well, with the same constants. Arbitrary boundary data or arbitrary fields without the gradient constraint are outside this statement. At `Q_r=0`, the source's determinant-null argument gives `a=c=d=0` and `C_r=0`; the ratio is undefined and excluded.

## 5. Equality of the relevant suprema

Define `Gamma_r` using the quotient `C_r^2/(4A_rQ_r)`, with positive denominator. For the following classes the suprema agree:

1. smooth periodic maps on a square torus;
2. periodic `W^{1,4}` maps on that torus;
3. smooth compactly supported maps on `R^2`;
4. `W^{1,4}_0(Omega)` maps for any nonempty bounded open planar domain `Omega`.

Here normalized versus unnormalized integration has no effect on the quotient.

For one direction, compact maps can be isotropically shrunk and translated to fit inside any such domain or a torus fundamental square. The quotient is unchanged: for `phi(x)=alpha psi((x-x0)/lambda)`, unnormalized `(A,Q,C)` acquire factors `(alpha^4/lambda^2, alpha^2, alpha^3/lambda)`, respectively. Zero extension of a zero-boundary map is periodic on a surrounding square. Strong `W^{1,4}` density preserves all three polynomial integrals and a quotient with positive denominator.

For the converse, periodically extend a smooth map, and multiply by a smooth cutoff equal to one on an `N by N` array of period cells, supported in one additional fixed-width outer layer. The periodic map and its derivatives are bounded; the extra derivative of the cutoff contributes only `O(N)` to each polynomial integral, whereas the exact bulk contributes `N^2(A,Q,C)` per cell. Thus the compact-map quotient tends to the periodic quotient. This also works for nonzero periodic potential means, since smooth periodic potentials remain bounded. No anisotropic rescaling is invoked.

It follows that concentrating compact approximations preserves access to the full supremum at arbitrarily small `Q_r` after `A_r=1`. Near-kernel analysis cannot discard spatial concentration merely because its original candidate was periodic.

## 6. Exact nonzero compact datum and single-bubble sharpness

A convenient explicit zero-boundary datum on the unit square is `phi=(F(x)G(y),0)`, where

\[
 F(x)=\begin{cases}x,&0\le x\le2/3,\\2(1-x),&2/3\le x\le1,\end{cases}
 \quad
 G(y)=\begin{cases}2y,&0\le y\le1/2,\\2(1-y),&1/2\le y\le1.\end{cases}
\]

It is Lipschitz and has zero trace. Exact Lebesgue integrals are

\[
 A_r=\frac{r(12r^2+12r+35)}{60(r+2)},\quad
 Q_r=\frac{2(r+1)^2}{3},\quad
 C_r=-\frac{r(2r+1)}6\ne0. \tag{N}
\]

For `r=1` these are `(59/180,8/3,-1/2)`, giving `Gamma` quotient `135/1888`. The scalar-row cubic coefficient is exactly `r(2r+1)/3`. Strong interior smoothing preserves a nonzero cubic and supplies a smooth compact template for each fixed `r>0`.

For a smooth compact template `psi` with `A_r=1`, put `psi_lambda(x)=lambda^{1/2}psi((x-x0)/lambda)`. Then

\[
 A_lambda=1,\quad Q_lambda=lambda Q_0,\quad
 C_lambda=lambda^{1/2}C_0.
\]

The ratio is fixed; gradient amplitude is `lambda^{-1/2}` and the selected tail area is exactly `lambda^2` times the template tail area. Thus the `Q^{-1/2}` amplitude and `Q^2` measure scalings are sharp.

## 7. Disjoint-cloud audit

Let the smooth compact template satisfy `A_0=1`, `Q_0=q_0>0`, `C_0=c_0`, and `rho=c_0^2/(4q_0)>0`. The `rho` in this section is the **squared** Jensen quotient; in Sections 1-4 it denotes a lower bound on `|C|/sqrt(Q)`. The conversion is `|C|/sqrt(Q)=2 sqrt(rho)`.

For finitely many positive weights `sum_i w_i=1`, define

\[
 \ell_i=\varepsilon\sqrt{w_i}/q_0,\qquad
 \phi_i(x)=\sqrt{\varepsilon w_i/q_0}\,
       \psi((x-x_i)/\ell_i). \tag{O}
\]

Their gradients have the **common** multiplier `sqrt(q_0/epsilon)`. If the supports are disjoint, exact polynomial homogeneity gives

\[
 A_i=w_i,\quad Q_i=\varepsilon w_i,\quad
 C_i=\sqrt\varepsilon\,w_i c_0/\sqrt{q_0}.
\]

For their sum,

\[
 A=1,\quad Q=\varepsilon,\quad
 C=2\operatorname{sign}(c_0)\sqrt\rho\sqrt\varepsilon,
 \quad C^2/(4AQ)=\rho. \tag{P}
\]

The finite sums are smooth and compatible. Disjointness is an essential packing condition, not a consequence of homogeneity. For any finite collection of distinct centers in an open domain, sufficiently small `epsilon` guarantees both disjointness and boundary fit.

For an arbitrary Borel probability measure on a fixed torus, or on the closure of a bounded domain, choose finitely supported probability measures with distinct interior centers that converge weakly to it. At each stage choose `epsilon_n` small enough for support disjointness, boundary fit, and maximum support diameter tending to zero. The positive `J_r` energy measure of each copy has total mass `w_i`. Therefore the cloud energy measures converge weakly to the prescribed probability measure: continuous test functions vary by `o(1)` across each support. The same statement holds for the normalized fourth-power measures; their individual masses are `w_i` after division by the template's total fourth-power mass. At boundary limit points the centers are taken inside the domain. No uniform boundary clearance is required.

For the explicit diffuse example on a unit square or flat torus, take `k=n^2` grid centers and `w_i=1/n^2`. Then `ell_i=epsilon_n/(n q_0)`. If the template support lies in a ball of radius `R`, supports fit inside their grid cells whenever `R epsilon_n/q_0` is sufficiently small, independently of `n`. Hence any `epsilon_n->0`, with `n->infinity`, works after discarding a finite prefix.

Each cell has energy mass `1/n^2`; a ball of radius `s` intersects at most `C(ns+1)^2` cells for bounded small `epsilon_n`. Uniformly in the ball center,

\[
 \mathcal E_n(B(x,s))\le C(s+1/n)^2. \tag{Q}
\]

Consequently for **every** sequence `s_n->0`, `sup_x E_n(B(x,s_n))->0`. The energy measures converge to normalized Lebesgue measure. Any fixed finite number of balls whose radii tend to zero carries vanishing energy, so no reduction demanding finitely many shrinking spatial profiles can capture a fixed positive energy fraction of this cloud. This does not refute profile methods with infinitely many profiles, different scales, or diffuse defect measures; no historical novelty is claimed.

The total support area is exactly a template constant times `epsilon_n^2`, independently of the number and weights of copies. For every finite `p` for which the template has finite moment,

\[
 \int |D\phi|^p
 =\varepsilon^{2-p/2}q_0^{p/2-2}\int |D\psi|^p. \tag{R}
\]

Thus the cloud simultaneously saturates the higher-moment exponent in (K), converges strongly to zero in gradient `L^p` for `p<4`, and fails uniform integrability at the fourth power. A diffuse weak energy limit is fully consistent with concentration on sets of vanishing area.
