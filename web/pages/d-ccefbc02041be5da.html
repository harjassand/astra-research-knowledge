MAIN RESULT

The strongest exact stable-polynomial result applicable here is the coefficient-
one square-root exchange for a nonnegative multiaffine Hurwitz-stable polynomial
supported on one parity class. For supported S,T and a in D=S△T,

  sqrt(f(S)f(T)) <= sum_{j in D minus {a}}
       sqrt(f(S△{a,j})f(T△{a,j})).

A complete proof by reciprocal twist, boundary specialization, odd-degree root
factorization, and positive-field optimization is frozen in
work/cycle6/c02_l01/INITIAL.txt. It includes the exact hard-BCS specialization
from signed ambient-hole stability and handles all shared cores. This theorem
and its application were already present in cycle-5 baseline; this worker's
contribution is a primary-source/prior-art reconstruction, not a new theorem
claim. Fixed-cardinality square-root exchange and the odd-degree endpoint
inequality are explicit prior art in Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant–Vuong,
Log-Concave Polynomials IV (arXiv:2004.07220v2, Lemmas 26 and 28). Branden's
stable-support delta-matroid results and stable Puiseux valuation exchange
control support/valuations, not this finite coefficient magnitude bound.

A deeper exact obstruction to one microscopic route is in
work/cycle6/c02_l01/revisions/micro_rejection_obstruction.txt. Let P be the
directed cyclic shift on even n and F=P+I/4. For the unrestricted fixed-size
paired-minor law

  mu(I,J) proportional to |det F[I,J]|^2, |I|=|J|=n/2,

the row and column marginals are all exactly 1/2 by cyclic symmetry, so the zero
field globally minimizes the convex marginal-balancing dual. F has only 2n
nonzero rational entries and condition number at most 5/3. Nevertheless, the
hard single-occupancy event I∩J=empty has exactly two configurations of unit
weight. Cauchy–Binet gives the unrestricted normalizer e_(n/2)(F*F), which is
at least binom(n,n/2)(3/4)^n. Thus

  Pr_mu[I∩J=empty] <= 2/[binom(n,n/2)(3/4)^n]
                       <= 2(n+1)(2/3)^n.

This is an all-n exponentially small acceptance probability for direct
rejection from the unprojected paired-minor k-DPP, even at exact balance and
constant condition number. It is a route-specific obstruction only. The hard
instance itself has two states, so it is not hardness for the target problem.
Kulesza–Taskar (arXiv:1207.6083, Sections 5.1–5.2) supply the k-DPP normalizer
and sampling interface; Cauchy–Binet gives a two-stage k-DPP decomposition of
the unrestricted pair-minor law. Finite precision and bit-cost details for a
sampler were not implemented here.

FPRAS has been constructed. No numerical or exact finite-bit implementation
of the sampling algorithms was attempted.
