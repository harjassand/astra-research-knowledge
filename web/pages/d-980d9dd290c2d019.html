Assume every off-diagonal Q entry is nonzero and there are signs s_i in {−1,1} such that s_i s_j Q_ij>0 for every i≠j. Strict entrywise-positive Q is the main case. The hidden frame, signs, Q, metric, attraction rates and hidden coefficient heights are not supplied.

The coefficient-derived field F=adj(Hess f) grad(det Hess f) has a nonzero coordinate extactic. Consequently the already internally gated rational-line acquisition algorithm obtains all hidden rational axes, an aligned rational SPD metric and an exactly checked positive weighted-square representation in expected polynomial bit time. This is a complete guarantee on a full-dimensional promised subclass, extending beyond the previous exchangeable/rank-one families. It does not cover arbitrary signed QPD or zero couplings.

## A hereditary field that keeps all omitted-coordinate effects

First assume positive Q entries. For an active set I define H_I=Hess f_I, h_I=det H_I and

    R_I=product_(l outside I) [4 sum_(k in I) Q_lk y_k^2],
    G_I=R_I adj(H_I) grad(h_I R_I).

Empty products are one. All fields have degree4n−3, where n is the original dimension, and every coordinate face is invariant. On removal of t∈I, the Hessian's normal block is g_t=4 sum_(k in I\{t})Q_tk y_k^2, its determinant gains g_t, and its tangential adjugate also gains g_t. Thus G_I restricts **exactly** to G_(I\{t}); the new R includes g_t. Using the unmodified principal-submatrix field would miss this factor and invalidate induction.

For proof of signs one may normalize Q_ii=1 by positive real diagonal scaling. Write r_ij=Q_ij in these normalized coordinates; then0<r_ij<1 by strict two-by-two positive definiteness. At axis e_i, put p0=(h_I R_I)(e_i)=12·4^(n−1) product_(l≠i)r_il. This positive number is independent of the active set. Root independently differentiated the determinant and product to obtain

    (h_I R_I)_(jj)/p0
       =6/r_ij+2 sum_(k≠i,j)r_jk/r_ik−2r_ij,
    (G_I)_i(e_i)=n p0^2/6,
    [partial_j(G_I)_j−(G_I)_i](e_i)=(p0^2/2) B_ij,
    B_ij=3/r_ij^2+sum_(k≠i,j)r_jk/(r_ik r_ij)−(n+1).

The omitted factors in R supply exactly the omitted terms in the full-n sum. Off-diagonal transverse derivatives vanish by parity. If every B_ij>0 for active j≠i, the projective field −G_I has a hyperbolic attracting axis at e_i.

## Simpler scalar ordering, derived by root and independently checked

Define the full-original row product P_i=product_(k≠i)r_ik. For fixed i,j,

    product_(k≠i,j)(r_jk/r_ik)=P_j/P_i.

If P_j≥P_i and n≥3, AM–GM gives sum_(k≠i,j)r_jk/r_ik≥n−2. Hence

    B_ij≥3/r_ij^2+(n−2)/r_ij−(n+1)>0.

Strictness follows from0<r_ij<1. For n=2 the formula is directly3/r_ij^2−3>0. Thus choosing a minimum of the **full-original** P_i over any active set supplies its attracting sink. A bad edge B_ij≤0 must strictly decrease P_i; acyclicity follows immediately. Products must not be recomputed using only the active set, because slopes still contain omitted-coordinate effects.

This simplification was derived by root after the cycle proof and independently checked by both workers. It retains all ties correctly. A rational proof score is product_(k≠i)Q_ik^2/Q_ii^(n−2), since P_i^2 equals that score divided by the common product of all diagonal entries. No square roots or hidden-product computation are added to the learner. The longer cycle proof below remains as an independent route.

## Alternative directed-cycle inequality

Draw i→j when B_ij≤0, including zero slopes. Suppose a simple directed cycle has length ell≥2 and edge correlations e_t∈(0,1). Summing B along it:

* The first terms contribute3 sum e_t^-2>3ell.
* For each outside k, the product of its ell ratios telescopes to (product e_t)^-1>1, so their sum exceeds ell by AM–GM. There are n−ell such indices.
* The ell(ell−2) internal ratios, if ell>2, have product (product e_t)^-(ell−2)>1. Their remaining numerator/denominator factors cancel because correlations are symmetric: the omitted predecessor and successor edge products coincide. AM–GM bounds their sum below by ell(ell−2). At ell=2 this group is empty.

Therefore the cycle sum is strictly greater than

    3ell+ell(n−ell)+ell(ell−2)−ell(n+1)=0,

contrary to all its edges being nonpositive. The bad-slope graph is acyclic. Every active induced subgraph therefore has a sink, providing at least one attracting axis. Other axes may be saddles; the theorem does not resurrect the disproved claim that all QPD axes attract one orientation.

## Extactic nonvanishing, including the pole issue

For D_I=−G_I·grad and N=|I| consider det[D_I^r y_j] with r=0,...,N−1. The N=1 case is y_j. Inductively choose an attracting sink i. The minor using the other coordinate columns and rows0,...,N−2 restricts on y_i=0 to the smaller nonzero extactic, so it is a nonzero polynomial M.

If the full determinant vanished, Cramer's rule would give rational functions c_j satisfying D_I^r y_i=sum_(j≠i)c_j D_I^r y_j for r=0,...,N−1. Differentiating and subtracting consecutive equations proves D_I c_j=0, since M is invertible in the rational-function field. Equal row degrees show each c_j has homogeneous degree zero and is invariant under projective normalization.

Choose an initial point in the open attracting chart with M≠0. The c_j are finite there and constant locally along the normalized analytic trajectory. Fix those initial scalar coefficients. The linear identity y_i=sum c_j(initial)y_j holds initially and extends for every finite trajectory time by analytic continuation. This does not evaluate rational functions across their poles or restrict them to a coordinate face. Taking the attracting limit in y_i=1 gives1=0. Thus the full determinant is nonzero, completing induction.

## Sign-balanced extension by complex proof coordinates

Let S=diag(s_i). Choose D=diag(d_i) with d_i=1 for s_i=1 and d_i=i for s_i=−1. Then

    f_Q(Dz)=(z^2)^T(SQS)(z^2).

SQS is real positive definite by real congruence, and all its entries are positive. The polynomial GL covariance of the Hessian field and extactic is valid over C; hence nonvanishing of the transformed extactic implies nonvanishing of the original one. Real sign changes alone would not work, since they leave squared coordinates unchanged. The complex change is solely a proof device: the implemented field, line jets, factorization and reflection/metric/SOS checks use the original rational coefficients. Multiplication of a hidden coordinate by i does not alter its hyperplane.

For nonzero off-diagonal entries, the existence of S is equivalent to all triangle sign products being positive. Necessity follows by cancellation of vertex signs. For sufficiency choose s_1=1 and s_j=sign(Q_1j); every remaining switched edge has the sign of triangle(1,i,j). This equivalence is proved here and does not rely on an unread signed-graph citation. It is a property of hidden Q, not a supplied recognition oracle. Zero edges and unbalanced sign patterns remain outside this corollary.

There is also a direct real proof, independently pointed out by the geometry checker: the full-n slope is invariant under Q→SQS because signs cancel in Q_jk/(Q_ik Q_ij), and its other terms use squared couplings or positive diagonals. Every hereditary factor remains nonzero at an axis when all couplings are nonzero. Although h_I R_I may change sign, the axis radial and transverse coefficients use its square. The same sink/face induction therefore applies over the reals without requiring R_I positive everywhere. This agrees with the proof-only complex transport above; neither route supplies extra information to acquisition.
