Abstract.
We prove that every PPT linear map has finite entanglement breaking index, thereby establishing the eventual entanglement breaking property of PPT channels in full generality. Furthermore, by utilizing completely positive maps with low entanglement dimensionality, we show that a large family of PPT maps, which strictly contains the class of 2-superpositive maps, has entanglement breaking index bounded above by 3, uniformly in the dimension. In particular, these results provide evidence that the PPT-cubed conjecture may hold in full generality.

1. Introduction
In quantum information theory, entanglement breaking (EB) channels represent a fundamental threshold beyond which quantum communication becomes effectively classical: a quantum channel Φ\Phi is entanglement breaking if (id⊗Φ)​(ρ)(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Phi)(\rho) is separable for every bipartite state ρ\rho, or equivalently if its Choi matrix is separable [HSR03]. For a channel Φ:Md→Md\Phi:M_{d}\to M_{d}, its entanglement breaking index is defined by
nEB​(Φ):=inf{n≥1:Φn​is​entanglement​breaking},n_{\rm EB}(\Phi):=\inf\{n\geq 1:\Phi^{n}\ {\rm is\ entanglement\ breaking}\},
with nEB​(Φ)=∞n_{\rm EB}(\Phi)=\infty when no such nn exists. This notion was first introduced and systematically studied by Lami and Giovannetti [LG15, LG16] as a measure of the lifetime of entanglement under repeated applications of the same noise. Operationally, if one half of an entangled state is subjected successively to Φ\Phi, then nEB​(Φ)n_{\rm EB}(\Phi) is the first time at which the resulting dynamics necessarily destroys its entanglement with any external reference system.
A particularly important instance of this problem is provided by channels with positive partial transpose (PPT), namely completely positive maps Φ\Phi for which ⊤∘Φ\top\circ\Phi is also completely positive. The PPT-squared conjecture, proposed by Christandl [Chr12] and subsequently studied in various works, predicts that PPT noise destroys entanglement after only two successive uses:
Φ\Phi is PPT  ⟹\implies  Φ2\Phi^{2} is entanglement breaking,
or equivalently nEB​(Φ)≤2n_{\rm EB}(\Phi)\leq 2. One may ask more generally whether the composition of two PPT maps is necessarily entanglement breaking, which turns out to be equivalent to the above weaker assertion [CMHW19]. Besides its intrinsic relation to the geometry of PPT and separable cones, this problem has a natural interpretation through entanglement swapping [CF17]: PPT-entangled states may be regarded as bound-entangled links of a quantum network, and composition of their associated maps corresponds to joining such links by an intermediate swapping operation. If the resulting map is entanglement breaking, no entanglement can be established between the terminal systems through this procedure, which is closely related to limitations on PPT-based quantum repeaters and long-distance quantum key distribution [BCHW15, AL26].
Although the PPT-squared conjecture remains substantially stronger, the weaker question of whether the EB index of a PPT map is finite has gradually become accessible. The first affirmative result in this direction was presented by Kennedy, Manor, and Paulsen [KMP17] who showed that
the iterates of every unital or trace-preserving PPT map approach the
entanglement breaking cone asymptotically. Subsequently, Rahaman, Jaques, and
Paulsen [RJP18] proved that every unital PPT channel has finite entanglement breaking
index. This result was later extended to any faithful PPT channels, and more generally to PPT
completely positive maps having full-rank left and right Perron-Frobenius
eigenmatrices, by Hanson, Rouzé, and Stilck França [HRSF20]. On the side of the PPT-squared conjecture, the qutrit case d=3d=3
was fully proved independently by Christandl, Müller-Hermes, and Wolf and by
Chen, Yang, and Tang [CMHW19, CYT19]. Further positive
results cover Choi-type maps [SN22], random covariant models
[NP26], symplectic and Cartan-covariant maps [Par26, Pru25], and strengthened qutrit composition statements
[AL26]. Finally, a recent work proves that every PPT composition through a
qubit intermediate algebra is entanglement breaking [Hen26].
The contribution of the present paper is twofold. First, we establish the finite entanglement breaking index property for arbitrary PPT channels, removing the full-rank eigenmatrices assumptions required in previous results. Second, by combining low-rank Kraus factorizations with composition mechanisms through qubit and qutrit intermediate systems, we derive explicit dimension-independent bounds on the EB index under additional low-Schmidt-number assumptions.
1.1. Eventual entanglement breaking property of PPT channels
In our first main theorem, we remove all the above restrictions and show that every PPT quantum channel, and more generally every PPT linear map, has finite EB index.
Theorem 1.1.
Let Φ:Md→Md\Phi:M_{d}\to M_{d} be a completely positive and completely copositive linear map. Then
nEB​(Φ)<∞.n_{\rm EB}(\Phi)<\infty.
Equivalently, there is an integer n0=n0​(Φ)n_{0}=n_{0}(\Phi) such that Φn\Phi^{n} is entanglement breakingfor every n≥n0n\geq n_{0}
As mentioned earlier, the full-support eigenmatrices case of Theorem 1.1 is derived from
[HRSF20]. The missing case is that in which a right or left Perron-Frobenius
eigenmatrix ρ∈Md\rho\in M_{d} is singular. As the key intermediate steps of the proof of Theorem 1.1, we first construct explicit decompositions of Φ\Phi into sum of two CP maps 𝒜,ℬ:Md→Md\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}:M_{d}\to M_{d} where 𝒜\mathcal{A} and ℬ\mathcal{B} are supported on the Hilbert subspaces Ran⁡(ρ){\rm Ran}(\rho) and its orthogonal completement Ran​(ρ)⟂{\rm Ran}(\rho)^{\perp}, respectively (Theorem 3.5). This in particular allows us to estimate nEB​(Φ)n_{\rm EB}(\Phi) explicitly in terms of nEB​(𝒜)n_{\rm EB}(\mathcal{A}) and nEB​(ℬ)n_{\rm EB}(\mathcal{B}). As another crucial step, we show that the entanglement dimensionality (i.e., Schmidt number [TH00]) of the PPT map Φ\Phi is governed by the rank of the eigenstate ρ\rho (Theorem 3.8):
Φ∈𝒫​𝒫​𝒯⟹SN⁡(CΦ)≤max⁡(rank⁡ρ,d−rank⁡ρ).\Phi\in\mathcal{PPT}\implies{\rm SN}(C_{\Phi})\leq\max(\rank\rho,d-\rank\rho).
In particular, when ρ\rho is singular, this implies that SN⁡(CΦ)≤d−1{\rm SN}(C_{\Phi})\leq d-1 and hence we can conclude the proof using the induction hypotheses.

1.3. Organization of the paper
Section 2 review various notions and preliminary results in entanglement theory. 2. Preliminaries on quantum entanglement
In this section, we briefly review basic notions in quantum entanglement theory used throughout this paper. Let Md:=Md​(ℂ)M_{d}:=M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) denote the algebra of d×dd\times d complex matrices. We use Dirac’s bra-ket notations: column vectors v∈ℂdv\in\mathbb{C}^{d} are written as kets |v⟩\ket{v} and their conjugate transpose v∗∈(ℂd)∗v^{*}\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{*} are written as bras ⟨v|\bra{v}.
The standard inner product is denoted by ⟨v|w⟩:=v∗​w∈ℂ\langle v|w\rangle:=v^{*}w\in\mathbb{C} and the outer product (rank-one operator) by |v⟩​⟨w|:=v​w∗∈Md|v\rangle\langle w|:=vw^{*}\in M_{d}. The standard basis in ℂd\mathbb{C}^{d} is denoted by {|j⟩:=ej}j=1d\{\ket{j}:=e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}, and we write |i​j⟩:=|i⟩⊗|j⟩\ket{ij}:=\ket{i}\otimes\ket{j} to denote tensor products of standard basis vectors. We allow unnormalized quantum states: a state is simply a positive operator ρ∈B​(H)+\rho\in B(H)^{+} on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space HH. The unit-trace condition is immaterial for the homogeneous cone-theoretic definitions below.
Let HA=ℂdAH_{A}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{A}} and HB=ℂdBH_{B}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{B}} be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Any nonzero bipartite vector ξ∈HA​B:=HA⊗HB\xi\in H_{AB}:=H_{A}\otimes H_{B} admits a Schmidt decomposition [NC00]
|ξ⟩=∑i=1kλi​|vi⟩⊗|wi⟩|\xi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda_{i}}|v_{i}\rangle\otimes|w_{i}\rangle
where λ1≥⋯≥λk>0\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}>0, and {vi}i=1k\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k} and {wi}i=1k\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k} are orthonormal subsets in HAH_{A} and HBH_{B}, respectively. The integer kk and the coefficients {λi}i=1k\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k} are uniquely determined, and we call kk the Schmidt rank of ξ\xi and write SR⁡(|ξ⟩)=k{\rm SR}(|\xi\rangle)=k. For a natural number kk, define
𝖲𝖭k(dA⊗dB):=conv{|ξ⟩⟨ξ|:ξ∈HA​B,SR(|ξ⟩)≤k}⊂B(HA​B)+\mathsf{SN}_{k}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B}):={\rm conv}\{|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|:\xi\in H_{AB},\;\;{\rm SR}(|\xi\rangle)\leq k\}\subset B(H_{AB})^{+}
and write simply 𝖲𝖭k\mathsf{SN}_{k} when no confusion arises. Then the Schmidt number of a quantum state ρA​B∈(MdA⊗MdB)+\rho_{AB}\in(M_{d_{A}}\otimes M_{d_{B}})^{+} is defined as the least kk for which ρA​B∈𝖲𝖭k\rho_{AB}\in\mathsf{SN}_{k}, and we denote it by SN⁡(ρA​B)=k{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})=k. By definition,
𝖲𝖭k={ρ∈B​(HA​B)+:S​N​(ρ)≤k},\mathsf{SN}_{k}=\{\rho\in B(H_{AB})^{+}:SN(\rho)\leq k\},
and 𝖲𝖭1⊂𝖲𝖭2⊂𝖲𝖭3⊂⋯\mathsf{SN}_{1}\subset\mathsf{SN}_{2}\subset\mathsf{SN}_{3}\subset\cdots. Note that 1≤SN⁡(ρA​B)≤min⁡(dA,dB)1\leq{\rm SN}(\rho_{AB})\leq\min(d_{A},d_{B}) and 𝖲𝖭k=B​(HA​B)+\mathsf{SN}_{k}=B(H_{AB})^{+} whenever k≥min⁡(dA,dB)k\geq\min(d_{A},d_{B}). The cone 𝖲𝖭1​(dA⊗dB)\mathsf{SN}_{1}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B}) is precisely the cone 𝖲𝖾𝗉⁡(dA⊗dB)\mathsf{Sep}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B}) of separable states; states outside the set 𝖲𝖾𝗉\mathsf{Sep} are called entangled.
Another important cone is 𝖯𝖯𝖳⁡(dA⊗dB)\mathsf{PPT}(d_{A}\otimes d_{B}), consisting of the states with positive-partial-trasnpose (PPT), namely the operators ρA​B∈B​(HA​B)+\rho_{AB}\in B(H_{AB})^{+} satisfying
ρA​BΓ:=(idA⊗⊤B)(ρA​B)≥0.\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma}:=(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\top_{B})(\rho_{AB})\geq 0.
The Perez-Horodecki- criterion [Per96, HHH96] is reformulated as 𝖲𝖾𝗉⊆𝖯𝖯𝖳\mathsf{Sep}\subseteq\mathsf{PPT}, providing a simple yet still powerful necessary condition for separability. In particular, in 2⊗22\otimes 2 and 2⊗32\otimes 3 systems, this condition is also sufficient for separability [Stø63, Wor76].
A linear map Φ:MdA→MdB\Phi:M_{d_{A}}\to M_{d_{B}} is called positive if Φ⁡(MdA+)⊆MdB+\Phi(M_{d_{A}}^{+})\subseteq M_{d_{B}}^{+}. For an integer k≥1k\geq 1, Φ\Phi is called kk-positive if its ampliation idk⊗Φ:Mk⊗MdA→Mk⊗MdB\mathrm{id}_{k}\otimes\Phi:M_{k}\otimes M_{d_{A}}\to M_{k}\otimes M_{d_{B}} is positive. It is completely positive (CP) if it is kk-positive for every kk, and completely copositive (co-CP) if ⊤∘Φ\top\circ\Phi is CP, where ⊤:X↦X⊤\top:X\mapsto X^{\top} denotes matrix transposition. Finally, Φ\Phi is decomposable if it is the sum of a CP map and a co-CP map. We denote by
𝒫​𝒪​𝒮​(dA,dB),𝒫​𝒪​𝒮k​(dA,dB),𝒞​𝒫​(dA,dB),𝒟​ℰ​𝒞​(dA,dB)\mathcal{POS}(d_{A},d_{B}),\quad\mathcal{POS}_{k}(d_{A},d_{B}),\quad\mathcal{CP}(d_{A},d_{B}),\quad\mathcal{DEC}(d_{A},d_{B})
the cones of positive, kk-positive, CP, and decomposable maps from MdAM_{d_{A}} to MdBM_{d_{B}}, respectively; we often omit the dimensions when they are clear. It is well-known that the complete positivity of Φ\Phi is equivalent to the min⁡(dA,dB)\min(d_{A},d_{B})-positivity [Cho75].
The Choi–Jamiołkowski isomorphism provides an efficient way to analyze the entanglement properties of linear maps [Jk72, Cho75].
For a linear map Φ:MdA→MdB\Phi:M_{d_{A}}\to M_{d_{B}}, we use the (unnormalized) Choi matrix

Choi’s theorem [Cho75] says that Φ\Phi is completely positive if and only if

We call Φ\Phi PPT (resp. entanglement breaking; EB) when CΦ∈𝖯𝖯𝖳C_{\Phi}\in\mathsf{PPT} (resp. CΦ∈𝖲𝖾𝗉C_{\Phi}\in\mathsf{Sep}). Let us denote the corresponding cones of maps from MdAM_{d_{A}} to MdBM_{d_{B}} by 𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​(dA,dB)\mathcal{PPT}(d_{A},d_{B}) and ℰ​ℬ​(dA,dB)\mathcal{EB}(d_{A},d_{B}), respectively. which justifies the terminology. so that p0+q0=2d+2p_{0}+q_{0}=\frac{2}{d+2} and d−32​d−3​p0+q0=1d+1\frac{d-3}{2d-3}p_{0}+q_{0}=\frac{1}{d+1}. Then (p0,q0)(p_{0},q_{0}) further satisfies

and therefore, ℒp0,q0∈𝒮​𝒫2\mathcal{L}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\in\mathcal{SP}_{2} by [Par26, Theorem 4.2]. On the other hand, since VV is a skew-symmetric unitary, one further has
⊤∘ℒq0,p0=AdV∗∘ℒp0,q0∈𝒮𝒫2.\top\circ\mathcal{L}_{q_{0},p_{0}}={\rm Ad}_{V^{*}}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\in\mathcal{SP}_{2}.
Thus we have Φ0∈𝒟​𝒮​𝒫2\Phi_{0}\in\mathcal{DSP}_{2}.
We remark that the PPT-squared conjecture already holds within the class {ℒp,q}\{\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\} [Pru25, Par26]:
ℒp,q,ℒp′,q′∈𝒫​𝒫​𝒯⟹ℒp,q∘ℒp′,q′∈ℰ​ℬ.\mathcal{L}_{p,q},\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{PPT}\implies\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{EB}.
Theorem 5.5 newly implies the following strong PPT-cubed assertion
ℒp,q,Ψ1,Ψ2∈𝒫​𝒫​𝒯⟹Ψ2∘ℒp,q∘Ψ1∈ℰ​ℬ,\mathcal{L}_{p,q},\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}\in\mathcal{PPT}\implies\Psi_{2}\circ\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\circ\Psi_{1}\in\mathcal{EB},
where Ψ1\Psi_{1} and Ψ2\Psi_{2} are arbitrary PPT maps.
As verified in Examples 5.4 and 5.10, the cone 𝒟​𝒮​𝒫k\mathcal{DSP}_{k} with k≥2k\geq 2 contains a large family of positive maps and clearly 𝒫​𝒫​𝒯⊆𝒟​𝒮​𝒫d=𝒟​ℰ​𝒞\mathcal{PPT}\subseteq\mathcal{DSP}_{d}=\mathcal{DEC}. We leave open the following question.
Question 5.11.
What is the smallest integer k≤dk\leq d such that
𝒟​𝒮​𝒫k⊇𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​?\mathcal{DSP}_{k}\supseteq\mathcal{PPT}?
Theorem 5.5 implies that the PPT-cubed conjecture would hold on MdM_{d} whenever 𝒟​𝒮​𝒫2​(d,d)\mathcal{DSP}_{2}(d,d) contains 𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​(d,d)\mathcal{PPT}(d,d). When d=3d=3, it is known that [YLT16]
𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​(3,3)⊆𝒮​𝒫2⊆𝒟​𝒮​𝒫2,\mathcal{PPT}(3,3)\subseteq\mathcal{SP}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{DSP}_{2},
while it is unknown whether 𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​(4,4)⊆𝒟​𝒮​𝒫2​(4,4)\mathcal{PPT}(4,4)\subseteq\mathcal{DSP}_{2}(4,4). On the other hand, we can consider the duality between mapping cones: for a cone 𝒞⊆𝒫​𝒪​𝒮​(dA,dB)\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{POS}(d_{A},d_{B}), we define its dual cone
𝒞∘:={Ψ∈Bh​(MdA,MdB):Tr⁡(CΨ​CΦ)≥0​∀Φ∈𝒞}.\mathcal{C}^{\circ}:=\{\Psi\in B^{h}(M_{d_{A}},M_{d_{B}}):\Tr(C_{\Psi}C_{\Phi})\geq 0\;\;\forall\,\Phi\in\mathcal{C}\}.
If 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a mapping cone, then so is 𝒞\mathcal{C}. Furthermore, we have 𝒮​𝒫k∘=𝒫​𝒪​𝒮k\mathcal{SP}_{k}^{\circ}=\mathcal{POS}_{k} [TH00, SSrZ09], and therefore,
𝒟𝒮𝒫k∘=𝒮𝒫k∘∩(⊤∘𝒮𝒫k)∘=𝒫𝒪𝒮k∩(⊤∘𝒫𝒪𝒮k).\mathcal{DSP}_{k}^{\circ}=\mathcal{SP}_{k}^{\circ}\cap(\top\circ\mathcal{SP}_{k})^{\circ}=\mathcal{POS}_{k}\cap(\top\circ\mathcal{POS}_{k}).
Thus, we have the following equivalent formulation of Question 5.11.
Proposition 5.12.
For all dA,dB≥2d_{A},d_{B}\geq 2 and for each integer 1≤k≤min⁡(dA,dB)1\leq k\leq\min(d_{A},d_{B}), the following are equivalent:
(1)
𝒫​𝒫​𝒯​(dA,dB)⊆𝒟​𝒮​𝒫k​(dA,dB)\mathcal{PPT}(d_{A},d_{B})\subseteq\mathcal{DSP}_{k}(d_{A},d_{B});
(2)
𝒫𝒪𝒮k(dA,dB)∩(⊤∘𝒫𝒪𝒮k(dA,dB))⊆𝒟ℰ𝒞(dA,dB)\mathcal{POS}_{k}(d_{A},d_{B})\cap(\top\circ\mathcal{POS}_{k}(d_{A},d_{B}))\subseteq\mathcal{DEC}(d_{A},d_{B}). In other words, every linear map Φ:MdA→MdB\Phi:M_{d_{A}}\to M_{d_{B}} which is both kk-positive and kk-copositive is decomposable.
