Q8: CONSTANTS, NORMALIZATION AND ACCEPTANCE
The sector-weight contribution is <=sum_b|hat(q_b)-q_b|<=epsilon/8.
For the geometric symmetric state, positive normalization gives
 ||sigma_K(q)-sigma_K(hat(q))||_1
 <=2 sum_(r0..K)|q^r-hat(q)^r|/Z_K(q)
 <=K(K+1)|q-hat(q)|<=epsilon/8.
Z_K(q)>=1 and both q values lie in[0,1], including the endpoint cases.

Couple uniform x to midpoint cells and phase to uniform circular grid cells.
The amplitude square-root inequality gives projector error <=2/sqrt(M)
from x; the exact phase projector formula gives <=2pi/M from phase.
Their sum is <=10/sqrt(M). F_hat(q)=diag(1,sqrt(hat(q))) has operator
norm<=1. Filtered local factors have trace norm<=1, so tensor telescoping
gives unnormalized average error u<=10K/sqrt(M).

The true unnormalized trace is S=Z_K/(K+1)>=1/(K+1). For positive operators
with positive traces, normalized trace error is <=2u/S. Thus the grid error
is <=20K(K+1)/sqrt(M)<=epsilon/8 under the stated factor160.
Also u<=epsilon/[16(K+1)] because K(K+1)<=N(N+1). Therefore the GRID
acceptance mass satisfies S_g>=[1-epsilon/16]/(K+1), as asserted.

Flooring the exact rational acceptance w=a^K to C/2^S loses at most eta=2^-S
in both mass and unnormalized density. Positivity makes this loss a PSD
operator with trace<=eta. Normalizing the rounded law costs at most
 2eta/S_g <=[32/15](K+1)eta <3(K+1)eta<=3epsilon/64.
The rounded acceptance probability is at least
 [1-5epsilon/64]/(K+1)>=59/[64(K+1)]>1/[2(K+1)].
Thus the stated accept floor1/[2(N+1)] is valid with substantial slack;
threshold rounding does not silently normalize a zero acceptance law.

Independent trials capped at the stated T have timeout probability<=epsilon/16.
Changing the normalized output on that event costs at most2*Pr(timeout),
hence <=epsilon/8. Conditional on success before the cap, the accepted grid
law is the same normalized rounded law as in an uncapped sampler.

Since tau=epsilon/(32N)<=1/32, the approximate rational vector z is nonzero.
Unit normalization changes its Euclidean error by at most2tau; its exact
rational rank-one projector differs by <=4tau in trace norm. K identical

Q8: BIT INTERFACE AND A CONCRETE INTEGER CAP
Classical descriptions, rather than a2^N matrix or exact irrational quantum
gates, are output. a>=1/(2M) on the midpoint grid, so inverse-square-root
conditioning costs only O(log M) additional precision. Rational powers and
threshold floors use polynomial-length integers. Certified square-root,
pi and bounded-angle trigonometric approximation therefore need polynomial
work in B,log M,log(1/tau). The rational projector remains exactly PSD/pure.

A common denominator may simply be the PRODUCT of the supplied denominators;
no factorization or least-common-denominator oracle is needed. It has O(NB)
bits. Exact categorical and Fisher-Yates integer draws have acceptance>1/2
per rejection trial (or are trivial when the range has size1). Their tails
are geometric; the WHOLE bit-work/randomness claim is EXPECTED polynomial,
not a deterministic worst-case bound. The continuous-proposal part is capped.

For a directly executable cap, use
 T'=2(N+1)*ceil(log_2(16/epsilon)).
The inner integer is obtained by exact rational comparisons with powers of2.
Since log_2 y>=log y for y>=1, this cap is no smaller than the required real
bound, preserves the timeout estimate and has the same asymptotic cost.
This avoids an unnecessary exact-ceiling decision about an irrational log.
Similarly M and S can be chosen by exact rational/power-of-two comparisons.
As usual, supply epsilon as a finite accuracy budget (for example2^-m), or
charge the bit length of its rational encoding as input. A huge unreduced
encoding of a tolerance near1 cannot be read for free merely because
log(1/epsilon) is small. These are implementation clarifications, not changes
to the mathematical error bounds or author frozen algorithm/proof.

Q9: EXPONENT CLIPPING AND WEIGHTED-NORM ACQUISITION
The bare rational exponents include the signed alpha,delta,h terms exactly;
their differences from their largest Emax are all nonpositive. For one
maximizing term exp(e-Emax)=1 and d_b>=1, so the TRUE scaled total S>=1.
It is not necessary to include log d_b in the maximum. d_b<=2^N safely bounds
all multiplicity factors, and L=sum_b(K_b+1)=O(N^2).

Choose dyadic zeta<=epsilon/(16L*2^N) and R=ceil(log_2(1/zeta)). An argument
below -R contributes an exponential <=exp(-R)<=2^-R<=zeta, so clipping it
down to zero is valid. Remaining arguments lie in[-R,0], with
R=O(N+log L+log(1/epsilon)), regardless of the numerical magnitudes of the
supplied rational alpha,delta,h. Exact comparisons and subtraction still
charge their input bit length B; huge magnitudes are not evaluated as huge
unscaled exponentials.

Certified downward dyadic exponentials in this bounded interval have a
standard explicit polynomial implementation: range-reduce to absolute
argument<=1/8, bracket exp(-y) by alternating Taylor partial sums, square
outward, then round the lower endpoint downward. The required Taylor degree
and precision are polynomial in R,log(1/zeta),B; the range-restoration power
is O(R). At argument0, return1 exactly. No transcendental exact sign oracle
or tiny-normalizer assumption is required.

The total DOWNWARD weighted error is <=L*2^N*zeta<=epsilon/16. The approximate
sum is positive (at least1-epsilon/16, or at least1 when exp0 is exact).
After exact rational normalization the l1 sector-weight error is <=2 times
this error divided by the TRUE S, hence <=epsilon/8. Exact dyadic term sums
divided by their positive total give nonnegative rational hat(q_b) summing
EXACTLY to1. Their output bit lengths are polynomial in the acquired precision,
N and B, so composition with Q8 preserves expected polynomial bit work.
Known rational nu gives q=nu/(nu+1) exactly. Real-oracle precision costs remain
explicitly charged; arbitrary finite real parameters alone are not numerical
input access.
