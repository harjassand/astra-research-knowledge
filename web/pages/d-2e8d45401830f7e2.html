# Irreducible involution frames need not identify symmetric scores

Status: independently derived universal construction, not a claim of literature novelty. 2026-10-06.

## Exact question and interface

Let a full-rank normalized density matrix sigma on C^d have symmetric logarithmic scores S_a defined by -i[A_a,sigma]=(sigma S_a+S_a sigma)/2. Does the ordinary condition {A_a}'=C I guarantee uniqueness of sigma from all these scores if, additionally, every A_a is a Hermitian involution A_a^2=I? **No, for every d>=3.** This addresses unrestricted generators. It does not resolve the special local single-qubit X_i,Z_i frame, whose generators satisfy extra tensor-product, trace, and anticommutation relations.

The source paper correctly states a condition on the **transformed** frame, not the ordinary one. Our result strengthens an independently constructed obstruction to replacing that condition by ordinary irreducibility. It does not contradict Lemma 6, Theorem 15, Lemma 16, or the high-temperature local-frame theorem in arXiv:2609.28391v1.

## Construction

Fix d>=3. Choose R>1 and u_i=R^i for i=0,...,d-1. Put p_i=u_i^2/Z, Z=sum_i u_i^2, sigma=diag(p_i), and

    C_ij = 2 u_i u_j/(u_i^2+u_j^2).

For this diagonal sigma, its symmetric-score transformed generator is T_sigma(A)=C Hadamard A. The matrix C is real positive definite: for real or complex z,

    z* C z = 2 integral_0^infinity |sum_i z_i u_i exp(-u_i^2 t)|^2 dt,

and distinct exponential rates are linearly independent. Also C_ii=1 and C_ij<=2 R^{-|i-j|} off the diagonal, so ||C-I||_2<=4/(R-1) and C->I as R->infinity.

On each open sign orthant O_s, s in {+1,-1}^d, define

    f_C(v) = (1/2) v^T C v - sum_i log |v_i|.

This function diverges when a coordinate approaches zero and is coercive at infinity because C is positive definite. Its Hessian C+diag(v_i^-2) is positive definite, so it has a unique interior minimizer v_s. Its stationary equations are

    C v_s - v_s^{-1} = 0,
    (v_s)_i (C v_s)_i = 1 for every i.

Here inverse is coordinatewise. At C=I this minimizer is the sign vector s itself. The implicit function theorem applies because the Jacobian of C v-v^{-1} with respect to v at (I,s) is 2I. Consequently v_s(C)->s as C->I. Alternatively, strong convexity gives the explicit bound

    ||v_s-s||_2 <= ||(C-I)s||_2/lambda_min(C)
                 <= epsilon sqrt(d)/(1-epsilon),
    epsilon=||C-I||_2<1.

Take d sign vectors s_0=(1,...,1) and s_j obtained from s_0 by flipping coordinate j, j=1,...,d-1. They span R^d: det[s_0 ... s_(d-1)]=+/-2^(d-1). Every s_j has nonzero overlap s_0^T s_j=d-2. Hence for all sufficiently large finite R the corresponding v_j also span and each v_0^T v_j is nonzero.

Define d Hermitian involutions

    A_j = I - 2 v_j v_j^T/(v_j^T v_j), j=0,...,d-1.

## Ordinary irreducibility

If a complex matrix X commutes with all A_j, it commutes with each rank-one projector onto v_j. Therefore X v_j=c_j v_j and v_j^T X=c_j v_j^T. A nonzero overlap v_0^T v_j implies c_0=c_j. Since the vectors span, X=c_0 I. Thus {A_j}'=C I. Hermiticity and A_j^2=I follow directly from their rank-one reflection form.

## Transformed reducibility and exact score collisions

Let w=(1,...,1)^T. Stationarity implies

    (C Hadamard v_j v_j^T) w = w.

Hence K_j=T_sigma(A_j) has the common eigenvector

    K_j w = (1-2/(v_j^T v_j)) w.

All K_j are Hermitian, so P=ww^T/d commutes with all of them. The transformed frame is reducible despite ordinary irreducibility and the involution property.

For every t in the open interval (-d/(d-1),d), define

    R_t=I+t(P-I/d),
    rho_t=sigma^(1/2) R_t sigma^(1/2).

R_t has eigenvalues 1+t(1-1/d) on w and 1-t/d on w-perpendicular, so rho_t is positive definite. Its diagonal equals sigma's diagonal, hence it is normalized. It differs from sigma for every t!=0, and it has **exactly the same symmetric scores** for all generators A_j.

To reconstruct the last claim without relying on the paper's statement: for sigma's score S_a, define B_a=A_a-i S_a/2 and D_a=A_a+i S_a/2. The score equation is B_a sigma=sigma D_a. Also K_a=sigma^(1/2) D_a sigma^(-1/2). Because R_t commutes with K_a, B_a rho_t=rho_t D_a, which rearranges to -i[A_a,rho_t]=(rho_t S_a+S_a rho_t)/2. Positivity makes the SLD solution unique.

## A fully explicit finite parameter bound

One sufficient choice is R=16 d^2+1. Then epsilon<=1/(4d^2). Let V=[v_0 ... v_(d-1)] and S=[s_0 ... s_(d-1)]. The bound above yields

    ||V-S||_2 <= d epsilon/(1-epsilon).

From the explicit inverse of S, ||S^-1||_F=sqrt(d^2-3d+6)/2, hence sigma_min(S)>=2/sqrt(d^2-3d+6). The perturbation bound is smaller than this for d>=3, so V is invertible. For the star overlaps,

    |v_0^T v_j-(d-2)| <= 2d epsilon/(1-epsilon)+d epsilon^2/(1-epsilon)^2 < d-2.

Thus every required overlap remains nonzero. This makes the construction exact at a specified finite R, rather than using a limiting rank-deficient state. The eigenvalue floor can nevertheless be very small; this is an identifiability obstruction, not an efficient high-temperature example.

## Assumptions, limits, nearest prior art, downstream use

- All density matrices and all scores are full rank / well defined. Generators are a fixed finite frame constructed from one target, then held fixed while rho_t varies.
- The frame depends on sigma during the construction, as counterexample construction may; an estimator supplied that fixed frame still cannot distinguish the exhibited targets by their SLD scores.
- The rank-one reflections are not balanced Pauli involutions in d=2^n and are not restricted to local tensor-product generators. Their trace is d-2. The special local-Pauli all-temperature question remains open here.
- Nearest verified prior art is Dong and Leng, arXiv:2609.28391v1, Definition 2, Lemma 6, and Theorem 15: the transformed-frame sufficient condition and its proof supply the comparison. Lemma 16 already gives ordinary-frame identifiability for endpoint scores. We have not established that this counterexample or full-fibre characterization is absent from earlier literature.
- Downstream use: a proposed symmetric-score frame can require a genuine transformed-frame gap audit; ordinary algebra generation, Hermitianity, and involution spectra alone are insufficient. Endpoint scores provide a universal repair when the ordinary frame is irreducible, subject to their own conditioning/measurement costs.
- Next falsifiable step: either find a collision satisfying the actual local Pauli tensor relations, or prove a positive gap for that special frame at every full-rank state. Generic involution counterexamples do not resolve that task.
