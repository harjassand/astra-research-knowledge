## Executive claim ledger

1. **Internally derived, exact:** for 2-D radial conductivity, each angular DtN mode is propagated through a constant-conductivity annulus by an exact Möbius map. In the normalized reflection coordinate, crossing log-radius thickness `d` multiplies the interior reflection by `exp(-2 n d)`. This holds for any finite number of layers and does not assume the layer count is supplied.
2. **Internally derived, exact pairwise information bound under the stated sensor model:** a buried annular conductivity perturbation has an exact all-mode response. With white current-readout noise and finite injected electrical energy, every adaptive strategy is subject to a two-hypothesis lower bound. At log-depth `d` large, the optimal angular mode is asymptotically `n=1`; the energy needed to detect a fixed-width, fixed-contrast annulus scales as `exp(4d)`. For a thin annulus of width `h`, the leading cost is proportional to `exp(4d)/h^2`. This is a pairwise detection theorem for the specified acquisition model, not a stable reconstruction theorem for arbitrary profiles.
3. **Source-reported, independently checked live:** the radial DtN spectrum has a moment representation; the moment extraction is the ill-posed step, while the Born-approximation-to-conductivity map is Hölder stable on stated classes. High-mode data have a locality law controlled by the outer annulus. This is developed in Daudé–Macià–Meroño–Nicoleau, arXiv:2510.22585v2 (2026-01-30).
4. **Source-reported, independently checked live:** Mandache proves severe instability for the Schrödinger DtN inverse problem and says it extends to EIT. I have not verified that his adversarial profiles are radial or concentric. No radial step-profile density argument is used here.
5. **Source-reported, independently checked live:** one-dimensional wave reflection has a propagation-based layer mechanism and a travel-time/impedance gauge, but classic work already provides inverse algorithms, stability analysis, piecewise-constant treatment, and large numerical models. No new wave reconstruction theorem is established here.
6. **Unresolved:** an optimal adaptive strategy that recovers every identifiable layer to an instance-specific certified resolution under unknown layer count; a useful extension beyond radial symmetry; and a matched computational theorem against equally informed regularization. These are not claimed as results.

## Exact target and information interface

The primary model is the unit disk in two dimensions with a positive, radial, piecewise-constant conductivity `sigma(r)`. The unknown is the full radial profile, including all interface radii, conductivity levels, and the finite but unbounded a priori unknown number of layers. For boundary drive `f_n(theta)=cos(n theta)` (or its sine quadrature), rotational symmetry gives

`Lambda_sigma f_n = lambda_n[sigma] f_n`,  `n >= 1`.

The ideal data are calibrated scalar estimates of these Fourier eigenvalues. Acquiring modes `1,...,N` means `N` boundary patterns and corresponding current projections, or an equivalent multiplexed measurement whose total energy and dynamic range are charged. A physical boundary array must resolve angular scale `1/N` (sensor/actuator pitch on the order of `1/N`); position error must be small compared with that scale. Conductivity units and source/readout gain require calibration. Repeats reduce independent current noise by `m^(-1/2)`. The model does not include electrode contact impedance or unknown boundary geometry.

For acquisition lower bounds below, a unit-L2 boundary mode is driven at amplitude `A_n` for duration `T`; the measured current projection is `A_n lambda_n + epsilon`, with independent `epsilon ~ N(0,sigma_J^2)`. The electrical work in a repeat is `T A_n^2 lambda_n`. This idealization gives an explicit sensor-noise and physical-energy interface. It does not assume that noise is independent of frequency in an actual instrument; a frequency-dependent transfer function must replace this model for a device-specific result.

The comparison model is lossless normal-incidence 1-D acoustics,

`rho(z) u_tt - d/dz(mu(z) u_z)=0`,

with target impedance `Z(tau)=sqrt(rho mu)` as a function of travel time `tau(z)=integral_0^z sqrt(rho/mu) dz`. The source/receiver observes `Y(omega)=H(omega) S(omega) R_Z(omega)+eta(omega)`. It does not identify density, speed, and physical depth separately without additional information: the reflection response is parameterized by impedance in travel-time coordinates. This is a candidate alternate physical mechanism, not a resolution of the EIT problem.

## Exact radial transfer construction

Set `x=-log r`. For one angular harmonic, write `u(r,theta)=v(x) cos(n theta)`; inside a constant shell, `v''=n^2 v`. Define the inward normalized admittance at a shell boundary by

`y_n = -sigma v'(x)/(n v(x))`.

This convention makes `y_n=lambda_n/n` at the exterior boundary and gives positive `y_n` for a regular homogeneous disk. Suppose an outer shell has conductivity `s>0` and log-thickness `d`, and the inward load at its inner edge is `y_in`. Propagating `v''=n^2v` across the shell and solving for the outer admittance gives

`y_out = s (y_in + s tanh(n d)) / (s + y_in tanh(n d))`.      (1)

Define reflection relative to the shell material by `R=(y-s)/(y+s)`. Direct substitution into (1) gives

`R_out = ((1-tanh(nd))/(1+tanh(nd))) R_in = exp(-2 n d) R_in`.      (2)

This is exact, not a Born or small-contrast approximation. It composes across any finite stack: each known outer homogeneous segment attenuates the deeper reflection by the product of its factors `exp(-2 n d_j)`. Inverting that transfer multiplies an additive error in `R_out` by `exp(2 n sum_j d_j)`. Thus identifiability of a deeper layer from exact data is separate from stability under noisy finite-energy data.

For one disk of conductivity `sigma_1` inside exterior conductivity `sigma_0`, separated by log-radius distance `d`, put `rho=(sigma_1-sigma_0)/(sigma_1+sigma_0)`. Then

`R_n = rho exp(-2 n d)`,  `lambda_n = n sigma_0 (1+R_n)/(1-R_n)`.      (3)

This already shows why simply requesting higher angular modes is not a universal way to see deeper structure: at fixed depth, the reflected contrast decreases exponentially in mode number.

### Exact annular adversarial pair

Let `sigma^(0)=sigma_0` throughout the disk. Let `sigma^(1)=sigma_1` only on the annulus `d < x < d+h`, and `sigma_0` elsewhere, with `h>0`. Let `rho=(sigma_1-sigma_0)/(sigma_1+sigma_0)`, so `|rho|<1`. Transfer through the annulus and then the outer `sigma_0` region yields

`R_n(d,h) = exp(-2 n d) rho (1-exp(-2 n h)) / (1-rho^2 exp(-2 n h))`.      (4)

For the algebra, set `q=exp(-2 n h)` and `t=tanh(nh)=(1-q)/(1+q)`. The admittance at the outer edge of the `sigma_1` annulus, measured relative to its inner `sigma_0` core, is `y=sigma_1 (sigma_0+sigma_1 t)/(sigma_1+sigma_0 t)`. Substitution into `(y-sigma_0)/(y+sigma_0)` gives `rho(1-q)/(1-rho^2 q)`. Propagating through the exterior `sigma_0` region multiplies by `exp(-2 n d)` as in (2).

The corresponding DtN change is exactly

`Delta lambda_n = lambda_n^(1)-lambda_n^(0) = 2 n sigma_0 R_n/(1-R_n)`,
`lambda_n^(0)=n sigma_0`.      (5)

Equation (4) is valid for every `n>=1`, every `d>=0`, every `h>0`, and every positive `sigma_0,sigma_1`. For fixed `rho != 0`, fixed `h>0`, and `d -> infinity`, `R_n` is exponentially small uniformly in `n`; the mode-1 change is order `exp(-2d)`. If also `h -> 0`, then

`rho (1-exp(-2h))/(1-rho^2 exp(-2h)) = 2 rho h/(1-rho^2) + O(h^2)`,

so `Delta lambda_1 = 4 sigma_0 rho h exp(-2d)/(1-rho^2) + O(h^2 exp(-2d) + h^2 exp(-4d))` as `d` is large and `h` small.

### Adaptive all-mode lower bound, with energy and noise charged

Under the acquisition model above, an experiment at mode `n` repeated `m` times with amplitude `A` has Gaussian KL divergence between the two annular hypotheses

`KL_n = m A^2 (Delta lambda_n)^2/(2 sigma_J^2)`.

Its physical work under the more demanding of the two hypotheses is

`E_n = m T A^2 lambda_n^*`,  `lambda_n^* = max(lambda_n^(0),lambda_n^(1))`.

Therefore `KL_n = E_n kappa_n/(2 sigma_J^2 T)`, where

`kappa_n = (Delta lambda_n)^2/lambda_n^*`.      (6)

Using `lambda_n^(1)=n sigma_0(1+R_n)/(1-R_n)` and `lambda_n^*=n sigma_0(1+|R_n|)/(1-|R_n|)`, this simplifies exactly to

`kappa_n = 4 n sigma_0 R_n^2/(1-R_n^2)`.      (6a)

For an adaptive sequence, the chain rule for KL divergence sums the conditional KL contributions. Each chosen action, including its mode and amplitude, is measurable from past observations and contributes at most its energy times `max_n kappa_n/(2 sigma_J^2 T)`. Hence for any adaptive or non-adaptive strategy of worst-case total energy `E`,

`KL(total) <= E max_{n>=1} kappa_n/(2 sigma_J^2 T)`.      (7)

By the two-point testing bound, achieving a fixed error probability strictly below `1/2` requires `KL(total)` bounded below by a positive numerical constant. Consequently

`E >= c sigma_J^2 T / max_{n>=1} kappa_n`      (8)

for a numerical `c>0` depending only on the desired error probability. This is a pairwise lower bound: it already holds when the two candidate profiles and the annulus location are known to the algorithm. It therefore does not rely on an unknown-profile algorithm failing, nor on a claim that the annulus pair is the hardest pair in the full profile class.

For fixed `h>0` and `rho!=0`, equations (4)-(6) imply

`kappa_n = 4 n sigma_0 rho^2 ((1-exp(-2 n h))/(1-rho^2 exp(-2 n h)))^2 exp(-4 n d) (1+o(1))`

as `d -> infinity`; the `o(1)` is uniform for integer `n>=1`. The ratio of the `n`th term to the `n=1` term is bounded by a constant depending on `h,rho` times `n exp(-4(n-1)d)`. Thus `n=1` maximizes it for all sufficiently large `d`, and

`max_n kappa_n ~ 4 sigma_0 a_1(h,rho)^2 exp(-4d)`,
`a_1(h,rho)=rho (1-exp(-2h))/(1-rho^2 exp(-2h))`.      (9)

Equations (8)-(9) give necessary work `E = Omega(sigma_J^2 T exp(4d)/(sigma_0 a_1^2))`. For `h` small, this is `Omega(sigma_J^2 T (1-rho^2)^2 exp(4d)/(sigma_0 rho^2 h^2))`. Because `r=e^{-d}`, the fixed-width cost is proportional to `r^{-4}`. If modes are combined in one boundary drive, orthogonality makes both electrical work and independent-mode Fisher information additive, so (7) remains an upper bound under the same total work. Parallelizing modes can save experiment time, not this information-energy barrier.

This lower bound is scoped to quasi-static 2-D radial EIT, the specified current-noise model, and this exact homogeneous-vs-annulus pair. It does not prove an all-profile minimax rate, an optimal reconstruction procedure, or a barrier for other sensor-noise laws. It gives an exact, physically interpretable counterexample to a claim that adaptive angular probing alone can reveal arbitrarily deep layers at bounded work.

## Unknown layer count and reconstruction implications

For any fixed `N` scalar harmonic measurements, the union of radial step profiles with arbitrary finite layer count is not injectively identified by those measurements. Fix a sufficiently large number `K` of shells and restrict radii and positive conductivity levels to an open parameter set with strict interface separation. This is a smooth parameter manifold of dimension greater than `N`; the map to `(lambda_1,...,lambda_N)` is continuous. Invariance of domain/topological dimension rules out a continuous injection from that open set into `R^N`. Adaptive choices of `N` scalar measurements do not add information through their choices: the choices are functions of the earlier scalar observations, so the transcript has at most `N` real response coordinates. This finite-data nonidentifiability is distinct from uniqueness under the complete infinite DtN data.

For a known finite shell count and a positive minimum layer width, fitting (1) or using monotonicity methods can exploit the structural promise. Those promises cap the parameter dimension or complexity; they are acquired-model assumptions and must be declared. Under the stated noise and work model, standard Tikhonov/TV regularization cannot reliably distinguish this homogeneous-versus-annulus pair below the bound (8); it chooses one element from an uncertainty set. An adaptive scheme can spend more energy on uncertain modes, but (7) says it cannot defeat even this known-pair detection barrier. No algorithm in this note resolves arbitrary unknown `K` with a certified output listing every recoverable interface. The credible next target is a partial reconstruction with a noise-calibrated unresolved-interior certificate, not a claim of exact all-layer recovery.

The exact forward recursion is executable in `wave_layer_resolution.py`; it costs `O(K)` arithmetic for one mode and `O(KN)` for `N` modes and `K` shells. A one-dimensional scan over modes computes `max_n kappa_n` for the two-hypothesis acquisition bound; modes beyond a cutoff can be bounded using the explicit exponential tail in (4). A full inverse fit would require optimization over layer count, radii, and contrasts and is not supplied. The narrow three-mode two-layer closed form was checked but is deliberately not promoted as the result.

## Wave-reflection alternative and why it did not become the result

For impedance levels `Z_j` separated by interfaces in travel-time coordinates, let

`Delta_j = log(Z_{j+1}/Z_j)`,  `r_j = (Z_{j+1}-Z_j)/(Z_{j+1}+Z_j)=tanh(Delta_j/2)`.

The primary return from interface `j`, before overlapping multiples are included, has amplitude

`A_j = r_j product_{i<j}(1-r_i^2)`.

Since `1-r_i^2=sech^2(Delta_i/2) >= exp(-|Delta_i|)`, a total-variation promise `sum_i |Delta_i| <= V` gives `|A_j| >= |r_j| exp(-V)` for the transmitted primary component. In the ideal lossless 1-D model this bound has no depth factor: waves propagate instead of decaying elliptically. The promise does not prevent echo overlap, weak interfaces, attenuation, or unknown time-zero. Recovering the profile still needs bandwidth and calibration.

For one isolated interface with reflectivity `r` shifted in travel time by `h`,

`Delta R(omega)=r exp(2 i omega tau_0)(exp(2 i omega h)-1)`,
`||Delta R||^2_{L2[-B,B]}=4 |r|^2 (B-sin(2Bh)/(2h))`,

which is `(8/3)|r|^2 B^3 h^2 (1+O((Bh)^2))` when `Bh << 1`. This is an algebraic shift sensitivity independent of depth in the ideal lossless one-interface model. An instrument observes `H S R + eta`; deconvolution divides noise by `|H S|`. Repeating `m` independent shots reduces standard error as `m^-1/2`; an unknown gain requires at least one reference calibration. Bandwidth `B` sets time resolution on the order of `1/B`; the observation window must exceed twice the maximum travel time, so uniform frequency spacing is at most order `1/T`; a time-zero error `delta_t` gives a depth error `c delta_t/2` only when the local speed `c` is known. These costs and gauge limits rule out an unqualified stable-recovery claim.

This is not a novel discovery route as stated. Bamberger, Chavent, and Lailly (1979) treat the 1-D boundary-wave inverse problem, equivalence classes of indistinguishable coefficient pairs, a stability theorem for piecewise-constant parameters, and simulated models with 300 unknown values. Gray and Symes (1985) distinguish algorithmic stability from conditioning and identify total variation of impedance and accuracy of low-frequency data as governing factors; they document failure by error amplification and the accuracy loss from smoothing. These primary sources already cover the central mechanism and its limitations. I found no verified gap to promote from the short layer-echo calculation.

## Matched baselines and exact barriers

* **Static radial EIT, exact infinite data:** radial symmetry diagonalizes the DtN map and gives one scalar eigenvalue per mode. Exact infinite data can identify the profile in the standard uniqueness setting. This does not imply finite/noisy stability.
* **Static radial EIT, finite data:** arbitrary unknown finite layer count is nonidentifiable from any fixed number of scalar mode coefficients by parameter dimension. An optimization or regularizer cannot create information missing from the data.
* **Static radial EIT, finite energy/noisy data:** the explicit annulus pair and (7)-(9) quantify a pairwise lower bound that also applies to adaptive mode selection.
* **Finite-dimensional promise:** Alberti–Santacesaria prove global reconstruction/stability from finitely many measurements for a known finite-dimensional coefficient subspace. This is a relevant baseline and makes clear why a known basis or finite complexity cap would change the information model. It does not answer the present unknown unbounded layer-count target.
* **Layered inclusion methods:** Garde proves constructive uniqueness/reconstruction for piecewise-constant layered conductivities using local Cauchy data and monotonicity, including nested positive and negative perturbations. This is a stronger standard baseline than a generic Tikhonov fit when its geometric promise and local data are available.
* **General elliptic EIT:** Mandache's obstruction shows exponential instability for a broad coefficient class, but his construction was not verified here to be radial. It must not be transferred to the radial step class without an actual radial adversarial construction. Conversely, exact formula (4) supports only the stated radial pair and metric/noise model.
* **Wave reflection:** Bamberger–Chavent–Lailly and Gray–Symes preclude presenting the impedance echo recursion or TV/layer stripping as a new general capability. Added attenuation, bandwidth limitations, or non-1-D geometry require new analysis.

For an equally informed comparison, the relevant static baseline uses the same positive conductivity bounds and boundary modes and solves a Tikhonov/TV objective over radial profiles; a layered baseline uses the same declared number/min-width promise and monotonicity or nonlinear least squares. Active probing is allowed the same prior bounds and total physical work. Equation (7) compares active and passive acquisition for a fixed pair; a full finite-sample reconstruction comparison is not performed. There is no experimental or external validation.

## Failed routes and next decisive gates

* The exact 3-mode invariant for a two-region radial conductivity gives closed-form two-layer parameter recovery, but it is a narrow finite-dimensional extension and conditioning worsens with deeper layers. It is retained only as a diagnostic, not as the main claim.
* A direct Mandache-to-radial-step argument by density is invalid unless the hard pair is radial and the approximation preserves separation in the target norm. This verification was not found; the argument is excluded.
* A raw Prony/multilayer extension can recover an unknown finite exponential sum from enough exact samples, but close exponents and weak coefficients are exponentially ill-conditioned. No improvement over equally informed regularization has been established.
* The lossless 1-D wave idea avoids elliptic evanescence only by changing the physical model and target; both its main mechanism and stability hazards are established prior art.
* The concrete continuation gate is to prove or refute a partial-identification theorem for arbitrary unknown finite radial stacks: given a declared angular bandwidth, measured-mode noise covariance, physical work cap, and known ellipticity bounds, output all outer layers whose interface location and contrast are estimable within certified intervals, and an uncertainty set for the remainder. The first proof obligation is a uniform lower/upper distinguishability bound for competing stacks with unknown layer count, not an optimization result. Include a matched TV/monotonicity baseline with the same bounds. Without this, no “recover all recoverable layers” claim is supported.

The exact annulus pair is a scoped barrier and useful measurement-design calibration. It is not scientific success under the campaign contract. No priority, novelty, or transformative capability is claimed.
