# Adversarial check of r12's complementary-Slater coherence repair

Received from r12 during cycle2. This note checks the proposed witness and its finite-phase measurement interface; r12 owns the main lead. Independent proof below, novelty unresolved.

## Proposed observable inequality

Fix 2l orthonormal fermionic modes partitioned into A={a1,...,al} and B={b1,...,bl}. Let |A> and |B> denote the complementary l-particle occupation determinants. For a normalized state on those modes, write pA=<A|rho|A>, pB=<B|rho|B>, c=<A|rho|B>. Every convex mixture of number-conserving pure Slater states satisfies

(2^l−2)|c| <= 1−pA−pB.

This statement permits mixtures across local particle-number sectors. It is useful for l>=2; the l=1 coefficient is zero and gives no information.

## Complete single-Slater proof

For one normalized l-particle Slater, represent its occupied single-particle subspace by a 2l-by-l matrix V with V^dagger V=I. Split its rows into VA,VB, so VA^dagger VA+VB^dagger VB=I. If lambda1,...,l are the eigenvalues of VA^dagger VA, then

pA=|det VA|²=prod_i lambda_i,
pB=|det VB|²=prod_i(1−lambda_i),
|c|=sqrt(pA pB).

Expand prod_i[lambda_i+(1−lambda_i)]=1 over all subsets. Remove the empty and full subsets, whose terms are pB and pA. Pair each remaining subset with its complement; their product is pA pB, so AM–GM bounds their sum below by 2sqrt(pA pB). There are (2^l−2)/2 pairs. This gives the inequality, including boundary lambda=0/1 by continuity. All lambda_i=1/2 saturate it, so the coefficient is sharp for this single-Slater family.

For a Slater in a different local number sector, pA=pB=c=0 and the inequality is automatic. For arbitrary convex mixtures, c is affine and the triangle inequality gives |sum p_tau c_tau|<=sum p_tau|c_tau|; the right side is affine. Thus the same inequality holds for the normalized mixture.

## Spectators and basis scope

Tracing spectators out of a gauge-invariant quasifree state produces a gauge-invariant quasifree local state. Diagonalize its one-body covariance; its density operator is a product of occupied/unoccupied probabilities in the natural orbital modes. This gives a convex mixture of local Slaters, possibly of different local particle numbers. Consequently tracing spectators from a convex mixture of global Slaters preserves the stated local null class and inequality.

The gauge-invariance/number-conservation promise is explicit. The above reasoning does not by itself establish the same null class for paired, non-number-conserving Gaussian states.

Single-particle unitary changes of the chosen orbitals map Slaters to Slaters. Therefore a violation in **any specified orbital basis** excludes the convex-Slater null class. The values pA,pB,c themselves are not invariant under changing that basis, so call this a basis-universal null inequality rather than an invariant scalar. Data-driven basis selection requires finite-sample uncertainty accounting.

## Exact l+1-setting Fourier extraction

Apply balanced mixers separately to each disjoint pair (ai,bi), with a shared controllable phase theta, and measure the parity of the l selected output modes. Multiply that shot outcome by the indicator that the local total number equals l. Do not divide by the observed sector probability. Let F(theta) be the expectation of this unrenormalized parity/sector-indicator observable.

On a pair with zero or two particles, the output parity is phase independent. On a singly occupied pair, it is an off-diagonal two-level operator with entries proportional to exp(+/-i theta). In the l-particle local sector, if d pairs are doubly occupied, there are r=l−2d singly occupied pairs. Thus every possible harmonic k has parity l and lies in

k=-l,-l+2,...,l.

The extremal harmonic +/-l requires every pair to be singly occupied and all its flips to have the same orientation. It uniquely connects |A> to |B>. Consequently its coefficient a_l has |a_l|=|c|, up to a known sign/conjugation convention fixed by CAR ordering and mixer definition.

Take the phase grid

theta_j=pi j/(l+1), j=0,...,l.

Then the coefficient extractor is

a_l=(1/(l+1))sum_j F(theta_j)exp(-i l theta_j).

Indeed any other allowed harmonic differs from l by -2s, 1<=s<=l, so its grid sum vanishes as a nontrivial (l+1)th-root sum. No truncation or asymptotic Fourier argument is needed.

**Aliasing warning.** Using the superficially natural full-circle grid theta_j=2pi j/(l+1) is not always equivalent. When l is odd, l+1 is even and allowed harmonics can collide modulo l+1. For l=3, harmonic +3 aliases harmonic -1 on four equally spaced full-circle points. A claimed l+1-setting implementation must specify the half-period grid/demodulation above or another nonaliasing scheme. Using 2l+1 full-circle settings would also separate all harmonics but forfeits the l+1 count.

One additional unmixed occupation measurement setting estimates the two local configuration probabilities pA,pB. Thus the ideal specified-basis protocol needs l+2 settings, with arbitrary shots per setting; this is a setting count, not a sample-complexity guarantee. All means are bounded shot observables, but estimating the modulus of a noisy complex coefficient requires confidence bounds. Convexity should be applied to the measured absolute coherence, not to an uncontrolled positive plug-in bias.

## Decisive scope and next check

The inequality detects the coherent complementary l-particle GHZ state: pA+pB=1, |c|=1/2, while the matching classical Slater mixture has c=0 and obeys the bound. This repairs the exact observational bottleneck of the 1RDM/2RDM cumulant when l>=3 by measuring an l-body extremal coherence rather than rethresholding the same two-body tensor.

The proposed argument is sound under the stated number-conserving convex-Slater null, local sector definition, and exact phase convention. Next falsifiable implementation check: construct the CAR mixer/parity observable and extract a_l on an arbitrary state with lower harmonics, verifying the l+1 half-grid and demonstrating the full-circle alias for odd l.
