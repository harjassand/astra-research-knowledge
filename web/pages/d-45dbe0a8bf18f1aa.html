CYCLE 4 FREE-RADIUS SOURCE AUDIT
Checked 2026-10-08

Question and conclusion

I independently checked Proposition 5.1 of Zhen, Zhu, Chen, Wang,
“Almost One Bit Violation of Minimum-Output Renyi Entropy Additivity
Simultaneously at All Orders,” current public arXiv:2609.18747v1, against the
cycle4 coefficient polynomial and its actual two-leg Haar U(N) representation.
The source's regular-radius proof is valid as written, and its coefficient
convention matches cycle4 exactly. The finite tensor-leg U(N) assignment is an
exact representation of F_64 x F_64, and Bordenave-Collins' product-group
Lemma 9.3 is stated for precisely this Haar-unitary tensor-leg model. Therefore
there is no real-versus-complex obstruction to transferring the one-copy
free-radius score. The Bell-fibre problem found in the separate source audit
does not infect this one-copy argument.

At r=2,d=64, Proposition 5.1 gives Gamma_(2,64)^2 <= 50, hence C=sqrt(50).
The arithmetic is correct. It is an upper bound, not the exact value of the
radius. The paper's Theorem 5.2 also gives the lower bound
Gamma_(2,64)^2 >= 3,203,583/65,024 = 49.26770115649607..., so its own
reported finite-parameter interval is narrow. Cycle4 may safely use the upper
bound 50 in its purity calculation.

Source proof: exact group and coefficient reduction

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18747v1 . Section 2.1, Eqs. (1)-(5),
defines G_(r,d)=product_(j=1)^r F_d^(j), K=2rd, S_j={g_(j,i)^(+/-1)},
and the tensor-leg representation. This is a DIRECT product of free groups,
not a free product: generators within one factor have no relations beyond
inversion, while generators in different factors commute. Section 3.2, Eq. (16),
spells out this group relation. At r=2,d=64 there are 128 free generators and
256 inverse-closed branches, K=256.

The source radius polynomial (Eq. (58)) is

  P_A = sum_(s,t in S) a_(s,t) lambda(s^-1 t),

for A=A* with Tr(A)=0 and ||A||_HS=1. Cycle4 defines
Q_W(A)=sum_(s,t) a_(s,t) W_s* W_t. With W_s=pi(s) for any unitary
representation pi of G_(r,d), W_s*W_t=pi(s^-1t), so this coefficient order
and group word match exactly. No transpose, conjugate representation, or
realness condition enters the one-copy polynomial.

Here is the full estimate in Proposition 5.1 (source lines 741-825). Put
k=|S_j|=2d. Define x_j^2 as the sum of |a_st|^2 over off-diagonal pairs in
S_j x S_j, and y_(j,l)^2 as the sum over both ordered cross-factor blocks
S_j x S_l and S_l x S_j. These coefficient blocks are disjoint, and the
Hilbert-Schmidt normalization gives

  sum_j x_j^2 + sum_(j<l) y_(j,l)^2 <= 1.

For s=t, lambda(s^-1t)=I, hence the whole diagonal contribution is
(sum_s a_ss)I=(Tr A)I=0. There is no other identity-producing pair, since
s^-1t=e implies s=t. This removal uses exactly the cycle4 net's trace-zero
constraint and holds for every unitary representation.

Within a single factor, if s!=t, s^-1t is a reduced length-two word in that
free group. The ordered two letters recover s and t, so the map (s,t)->s^-1t
is injective on this block. Its collected coefficient l2 norm is x_j. Its
multidegree is 2e_j; the tensorized Haagerup inequality gives

  ||Q_j|| <= (2+1)x_j = 3x_j.

Across two distinct factors j,l, cross-factor commutation gives the exact
abstract-group fibre

  mu^(-1)(s^-1t) = {(s,t),(t^-1,s^-1)}.

The two coordinates of the relative operation identify the two factors and
letters, so there are no further preimages. The collected coefficient is
c_g=a_st+a_(t^-1,s^-1), and |z+w|^2<=2(|z|^2+|w|^2) gives
sum_g |c_g|^2<=2 y_(j,l)^2. The support has multidegree e_j+e_l; the
product Haagerup constant is (1+1)(1+1)=4. Thus

  ||Q_(j,l)|| <= 4 sqrt(2) y_(j,l).

These hypotheses are exactly Lemma 3.3 in the primary source: scalar
coefficients supported on a fixed multidegree in the left regular
representation of the direct product. The same-factor and mixed support
claims are exact because nonidentity relative operations have respectively
two letters in one coordinate or one letter in each of two coordinates.
Applying the triangle inequality and then Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  ||P_A|| <= 3 sum_j x_j + 4 sqrt(2) sum_(j<l) y_(j,l)
          <= sqrt(9r + 32 binom(r,2))
             sqrt(sum_j x_j^2 + sum_(j<l) y_(j,l)^2)
          <= sqrt(16r^2 - 7r).

At r=2, this is sqrt(18+32)=sqrt(50), since
9r+32 binom(r,2)=16r^2-7r=64-14=50. The proof is uniform in d; d=64
only sets K and the finite support sizes. For the actual parameters, k=128:
there are r k(k-1)=32,512 same-factor off-diagonal pairs and
r(r-1)k^2=32,768 ordered cross-factor pairs, arranged into 16,384
size-two mixed fibres. These counts match the separate Bell-fibre audit.

The exact purity identity in primary-source Lemma 4.1 applies to every finite
unitary tuple. Combining that identity with the regular bound C^2=50 gives

  max_X Tr(Phi(X)^2) <= 1/256 + 50/256^2
                       = 306/65,536 = 153/32,768.

The corresponding von Neumann lower bound is
-ln(306/65,536). The identity is all-input: it is not limited to a selected
pure or coherent input.

Check against cycle4's actual complex tensor-leg model

Cycle4 sets, for independent Haar U_i,V_j in U(N),

  pi_N(g_(1,i)) = U_i tensor I_N,
  pi_N(g_(2,j)) = I_N tensor V_j.

Each factor is free, so assigning arbitrary unitary generators defines a
homomorphism from each F_64. The two factor images commute exactly because
they act on separate tensor legs. Therefore this is an exact unitary
representation pi_N:F_64 x F_64 -> U(N^2), for every finite choice of the
matrices, including the Haar choices. Inverse branches are the adjoints.
The source free polynomial evaluates as

  P_A,N = sum_(s,t) a_(s,t) pi_N(s^-1t)
        = sum_(s,t) a_(s,t) W_s*W_t,

which is precisely cycle4's one-copy score polynomial. Accidental additional
relations in a finite matrix sample do not change that homomorphism or the
identity above.

The finite-N norm is NOT bounded by the reduced norm for an arbitrary unitary
representation of this nonamenable group merely because it is a representation.
Cycle4 appropriately invokes a separate probabilistic transfer. I checked the
primary Bordenave-Collins source, https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05714v2:

- Its Section 9.3, Eqs. (69)-(70), constructs independent Haar U(N) generators
  on k separate tensor legs as a random representation of F_d^k and states
  strong convergence to the regular representation (Theorem 9.2).
- Its Lemma 9.3 gives the quantitative expectation bound for that same product
  group and coefficient-matrix polynomial, assuming N>=d^70 and even
  2<=p<=N^(1/(16d+80)). Here d=64,k=2, so the exponent is 1/1104, exactly
  cycle4's p-range.
- Its Section 8.1, Lemma 8.1, is explicitly formulated for any finitely
  generated group with a finite symmetric generating set and a group
  homomorphism. It therefore permits the product group's sum word metric;
  it is not restricted to a free group without cross-leg relations.

For cycle4's degree-two P_A, the sum-metric radius-two ball has
|B_1|=1+2rd=1+256=257. Bordenave-Collins Lemma 8.1 produces a degree-one
linearization with coefficient size 2|B_1|=514 and theta<=|B_1| ||P_A,free||.
Since ||P_A,free||<=sqrt(50), this gives
||Q_A,free||^2=||P_A,free||+theta<=258 sqrt(50), the bound used in
RESULT.txt. The Bordenave-Collins Lemma 9.3 coefficient dimension is n=514;
its factor (2n)^(3/p) is consequently 1028^(3/p), matching cycle4's formula
for k=2. The additive polynomial includes the identity coefficient and the
256 directed generator/inverse coefficients, as required by that lemma.

This checks the group, word-metric, coefficient-order, support-radius,
coefficient-dimension, and Haar U(N) tensor-leg hypotheses. It also confirms
that the cycle4 one-copy transfer is structurally on the right theorem. The
phrase “free-product representation” in cycle4 CONSTANT_AUDIT.txt should be
changed to “tensor-leg representation of the direct product F_64 x F_64”; the
mathematics elsewhere in the packet uses the correct direct product.

Arithmetic at d=64: source Theorem 5.2 also gives, with k=2d=128,

  Gamma_(2,64)^2 >=
       2(3*128-4)^2/(128*127) + 32(127/128)^2
     = 3,203,583/65,024
     = 49.26770115649607... .

Together with Proposition 5.1, 49.2677011565... <= Gamma_(2,64)^2 <= 50.
The source's uniform finite-parameter error bound (65) only gives a gap from
50 of at most 16r^2/d=1 here; the explicit Theorem 5.2 lower formula sharpens
that to about 0.732299. This reinforces that 50 is a conservative upper bound,
not the exact finite-d radius.

Evidence classification and remaining limits

SOURCE-PROVED: Proposition 5.1's regular-representation estimate, including
trace-zero diagonal removal, the same/mixed relative-operation fibres, the
Haagerup constants 3 and 4, and the Cauchy-Schwarz constant sqrt(16r^2-7r).
Lemma 4.1's exact unitary-general purity identity is a separate source theorem.

INDEPENDENTLY RECONSTRUCTED HERE: the match between cycle4's W_s*W_t
coefficient convention and pi(s^-1t); the exact product-group relations of
its U(N) tensor-leg assignment; the B_1 and coefficient-dimension arithmetic;
the r=2,d=64 constant and Theorem 5.2 lower-bound substitution; and the
conclusion that no realness assumption is needed for this one-copy transfer.

PRIMARY-SOURCE CROSS-CHECKED, NOT FORMALLY VERIFIED: Bordenave-Collins
Theorem 9.2/Lemma 9.3 and Lemma 8.1 explicitly cover the product-group Haar
unitary tensor model and general-group linearization. Its Lemma 9.4 states
the tensorized matrix-coefficient Haagerup bound. These are published/source
statements, not a new proof or proof-assistant check in this audit.

NOT RE-AUDITED HERE: cycle4's extraction c_E<=780 from the detailed
Weingarten/path-count proof, the numerical expectation-budget solver, and its
separate concentration/net inequalities. Bordenave-Collins Lemma 9.3 leaves
its numerical c as a source constant; cycle4's 780 is an internal
reconstruction, not a printed constant in that lemma. Thus this audit finds no
unsupported free-radius-to-U(N)-score step once that reconstructed quantitative
constant and cycle4's separate concentration proof are accepted. It does not
validate the Bell transfer: that remains invalidated by the real-versus-complex
mismatch in the companion source audit.
