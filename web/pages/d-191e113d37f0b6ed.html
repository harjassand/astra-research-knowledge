# Cycle 3 — ecDNA stress response and spatial lineage observability

## Finding and novelty boundary

Shibata et al. already make the central claim that stress can alter ecDNA segregation: in the COLO320DM-derived clone EC2 under 500 nM JQ1, they report a leftward MYC/CDX2 copy-number shift, lower copy number in shared barcoded lineages after treatment, and more unequal AURKB-marked daughter-cell FISH pairs at 24 and 48 hours. EN2 knockdown attenuates the response. The article is a peer-reviewed 2026 *Nature Communications* paper, not an unreviewed hypothesis. A generic “stress-triggered ecDNA partitioning” mechanism therefore collides with direct prior art. The authors also report clone- and drug-specific counterexamples: EC12 fits pre-existing fitness/selection better, and paclitaxel did not reproduce EC2's JQ1-like tracking result. The phenomenon is not established as universal.

The removable bottleneck is **lineage resolution at the mitotic transition**. The study links copy-number distributions to barcode-defined clone descendants, and separately measures AURKB-positive daughter pairs by FISH. Its methods do not report a barcode readout on those same sister pairs. Thus the strongest unresolved alternative is selection among latent, stable partition/copy-number states *within* one observed barcode, rather than selection among the barcode-level clones the paper tests. A same-barcode, parent-copy-conditioned daughter allocation measurement would make the mechanism more identifiable. That is a proposed validation target, not a novel mechanism or priority claim.

Spatial sampling worsens the issue in tumors. A 2025 *Cancer Discovery* multiregion study already documents spatially restricted ecDNA: among 211 regions from 57 urothelial-carcinoma patients (2–6 regions per patient), 44/59 ecDNA species were subclonal across regions and 11 cases had ecDNA only in selected regions. This establishes that ecDNA spatial heterogeneity and multiregion measurement are not new. It does not measure treatment-triggered mother-to-daughter partition changes. A biopsy series can map which lineages and copy states occur where, but destructive snapshots do not show that a cell changed its segregation kernel.

## What the 2026 study measures

The paper's targeted Tapestri scDNA-seq framework combines copy-number loci, cellular barcodes and clone tracking across about 11,000 cells. It normalizes against 40 control regions and diploid RPE1 cells; its own sampling analysis recommends at least 100 cells for a representative overall copy-number distribution. FISH validates ecDNA versus HSR configuration and supplies an orthogonal copy-number readout.

For the first tracking experiment, lentiviral PseuMO-Tag barcodes were introduced into EC2, EC11, EC12 and HSR4. Single-cell-founded colonies were expanded to 4–8 cells, pooled at roughly 100–120 clones per mixture, split into paired wells and exposed to control/drug conditions. Clone identity was linked between pretreatment scDNA profiles and subsequent barcode recovery after four weeks. The paper explicitly kept clones physically separated, so these cultures had no between-clone competition or spatial migration.

The focused EC2/EC12 follow-up used paired sister cultures of roughly 50–60 clones: DMSO was grown for 10 days and JQ1 for 24 days before scDNA-seq. EC2 had 326 detectable control subclones and 119 JQ1 subclones; the 34 JQ1 survivors had higher and more heterogeneous pretreatment MYC copy number than non-survivors, and shared barcodes had lower MYC/CDX2 copy number under JQ1. EC12's paired shifts were small and its surviving barcodes were already dominant in control. Separately, AURKB immunofluorescence plus MYC FISH identified daughter pairs after 0, 24 or 48 h JQ1. The article fits binomial and beta-binomial allocation models and reports a stronger unequal-segregation shift in EC2 than the other tested clones. The FISH pairs are direct daughter-pair observations, but are not described as barcode-resolved.

The authors themselves limit the broad inference: strongest evidence is EC2+JQ1; other clone/stress combinations differ; EN2's molecular connection to ecDNA segregation is not resolved; and the experiments are cell lines, with patient-derived and clinical extensions still needed. The paper's model is a fitted beta-binomial allocation law plus fitted copy-number distributions. It is not evidence that all tumors have the same transition law.

## Exact counterexample: same moments under selection and switching

Here is a small, explicit witness that mean/SD tracking cannot distinguish selection from reconfiguration when a barcode hides latent states. It is a model construction, not a fit to EC2.

Let a stable high-copy state H have C=9 or 11 with probability 1/2 each, so E[C|H]=10 and Var(C|H)=1. Let a stable low-copy state L have C=1 or 3 with probability 1/2, so E[C|L]=2 and Var(C|L)=1. Both states share the observed barcode.

At baseline, 90% of cells are in H and 10% in L. Then

\[
E[C]=9.2,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(C)=1+(0.9)(0.1)(10-2)^2=6.76,
\]

so SD is 2.6. Under a **static-selection** model, JQ1 changes the H:L relative abundance from 9:1 to 1:9 while leaving each state's copy-number and partition laws fixed. At endpoint, E[C]=2.8 and Var(C)=1+(0.1)(0.9)8^2=6.76, again SD 2.6. This requires an 81-fold shift in the low:high odds. If there are g effective divisions, the corresponding relative expansion factor per division is 81^(1/g) (1.245 for the illustrative g=20); the required g and fitness are not known for the study's cultures.

Under a **within-lineage switching** model with no differential survival, take P(H→L)=0.9 and P(L→H)=0.1 during stress. Starting from P(H)=0.9, this gives endpoint P(H)=0.1, the same endpoint mixture and therefore the same full single-cell copy-number histogram as static selection. Baseline and endpoint mean and SD match exactly under both models. The switching probabilities are illustrative, not biological estimates. This construction shows a stronger non-identifiability result than moment matching: all cross-sectional copy-number data can agree if hidden states share the measured barcode.

The static model can also change the aggregate daughter-pair asymmetry if hidden states have different but treatment-invariant allocation kernels (K_H(k|n)) and (K_L(k|n)), because JQ1 changes their weights. An aggregate change in unequal-sister counts alone is therefore not proof that a given lineage changed its kernel.

## A quantitative transition discriminator

For each barcode-resolved lineage b, condition on total ecDNA signal count n=C₁+C₂ across an AURKB-marked daughter pair, and record k=min(C₁,C₂). Compare the **conditional allocation kernel**

\[
K_{b,t}(k\mid n)=P\{\min(C_1,C_2)=k\mid C_1+C_2=n,\ b,\ t\}
\]

between randomized JQ1 and DMSO sister lineages. A compact statistic is

\[
A_{b,n,t}=E\left[\left.\left({C_1-C_2\over n}\right)^2\right|b,n,t\right].
\]

For a symmetric beta-binomial partition law with mean allocation 1/2 and intraclass correlation \(\rho=1/(\alpha+\beta+1)\),

\[
A_n={1+(n-1)\rho\over n}.
\]

Thus a static-selection null predicts stable K_b,t(k|n) within a sufficiently fine lineage state; it may alter barcode/state weights. A stress-reconfiguration alternative predicts a within-lineage change at matched b,n. This conditions away the changing total-copy distribution that can otherwise change raw asymmetry. For example, at n=20, binomial p=1/2 gives A=0.05; a beta-binomial with α=β=4.5 (ρ=0.1) gives A=0.145. The event that the smaller daughter receives at most 30% of copies has probabilities 0.115 and 0.386, respectively. Under independent daughter pairs, a two-proportion normal approximation gives about 37 pairs per arm for 80% power at two-sided 5%; this lower bound applies to the illustrative n=20 stratum and precedes image/clone clustering and biological-replicate variation. Use at least three independent cultures and make the barcode, not the image or pair, the resampling unit.

This statistic is not itself novel: the 2026 article already measures sister-pair FISH and fits beta-binomial laws. The discriminator that remains missing is the **same-barcode, matched-n, within-lineage** comparison, with a sufficiently fine lineage/state label so a treatment-induced weight shift among hidden static modes is not mistaken for switching. A prospective analysis should fit two equal-data models: (i) fixed K_b,z with treatment-dependent growth/survival weights over hidden states z; (ii) stress-dependent K_b,t or state-transition rates. Both must use the same single-cell CN observations, barcode counts and pair images. Hold out a later transition-timepoint; a better fit to the same endpoints alone is not a decisive win.

### Exploratory check of the published sister-pair source table

I downloaded the publisher's Source Data workbook for Fig. 6e–f / Supplementary Fig. 9d and recomputed A=(C₁−C₂)²/(C₁+C₂)² for EC2's 0 h, 24 h and 48 h fields. It contains 187, 99 and 216 daughter pairs, across 96, 47 and 132 images, respectively. The raw pair-weighted means are 0.120, 0.171 and 0.164. On the exact-total-count values represented in all three groups (18 shared n values, n=3–25, weighted by the minimum per-condition count at each n), the means are 0.116, 0.146 and 0.148. Image-cluster bootstrap (5,000 resamples) 95% intervals for the standardized differences from 0 h are −0.018 to 0.081 (24 h) and −0.008 to 0.071 (48 h). These are exploratory estimates from the published source table, not a replacement for the authors' analysis; the resampling does not represent independent biological culture replicates, and the workbook does not barcode these pairs. The adjustment reduces the point shift and the intervals include zero. This makes the conditional, barcode-resolved test a real discriminating need rather than a claim that the published FISH signal is already decisive.

## Spatial observability and finite sampling

In a tumor region r, a snapshot distribution is a mixture

\[
F_r(c,t)=\sum_{b,z} w_{r,b,z}(t)F_{b,z}(c,t).
\]

A change in F_r can come from a change in a within-lineage state F_b,z, selection/migration changing w_r,b,z, or both. A multiregion pretreatment/post-treatment biopsy measures region-indexed mixtures; it does not identify which term changed. Drug penetration, oxygen/nutrient gradients, local growth, tissue barriers and immigration from unbiopsied regions add unobserved spatial weights. The in-vitro EC2 assay avoids those ecological confounds by separating clones in wells, but consequently does not test them.

The empirical spatial prior is material. Lv et al. analyzed 211 tumor regions from 57 urothelial-carcinoma patients, with 2–6 regions per case. They found 44/59 ecDNA species subclonal across regions and 11 cases with ecDNA confined to selected tumor regions. As a finite-sampling illustration, if a viable refuge occupies fraction f of independently sampled equal-area tiles, then the probability of missing it in m tiles is (1-f)^m; 95% detection requires m ≥ log(0.05)/log(1-f). This is 59 tiles for a 5% patch, 29 for 10%, and 14 for 20%. Two to six independent tiles would still miss a 5% patch with probability 0.90–0.74. Real adjacent tiles are spatially correlated, so the effective sample count is smaller. Without a minimum patch scale and a coverage design, no finite set of negative regions rules out an unsampled sanctuary.

For a tissue-level assay, each registered region would need: ecDNA copy/structure by DNA-FISH or targeted single-cell DNA, a natural lineage marker (SNV/mtDNA) or experimental barcode, local treatment exposure/target engagement, viability/division state, and coordinates. These measurements estimate the mixture and gradients. To identify partitioning, add actual transition observations: in a model system use live ecDNA imaging through mitosis followed by daughter-cell recovery and barcode/genotype sequencing; validate the reporter against endpoint FISH and include an untagged control because reporter construction may alter ecDNA maintenance. In patient material, current destructive multiregion snapshots can test spatial prevalence and selection but cannot supply same-cell mother-to-daughter transitions. State that boundary explicitly.

## Acquisition, controls and cost boundary

The published acquisition burden is unusually well specified in cell/well units: targeted scDNA-seq on roughly 11,000 cells with 40 normalizer loci and RPE1 reference; PseuMO-Tag lentiviral barcode clone tracking in 96-well plates; 500 nM JQ1; 10-day DMSO versus 24-day JQ1 for the paired-clone scDNA follow-up; 24/48-hour JQ1 for sister-pair AURKB/FISH; and three independent FISH experiments reported. The first tracking experiment uses about 100–120 clones per pool and a four-week stress phase; the focused assay pools about 50–60 clones per pair. The paper gives no per-assay dollar price, labor hours, sequencing quote or full per-condition biological-replicate budget, so an exact monetary total is not available.

For the decisive in-vitro extension, preserve paired randomized DMSO/JQ1 sister lineages and add barcode recovery on the same sister pairs; stratify or match on total ecDNA count n, score at least the power-example 37 usable pairs per arm in the illustrative n=20 stratum, and inflate for clone/image clustering using a pilot. Include EC2+JQ1, EC12+JQ1 as the paper's internal contrast, EC2+DMSO, and HSR4 or COLO320HSR as a chromosomal-amplification control. For an EN2-mechanism test, add EN2 knockdown plus scrambled-siRNA control; it is not needed just to distinguish static selection from partition reconfiguration. Dollar cost remains unknown until platform, sequencing depth, imaging throughput and local quotes are specified.

For an in-vivo spatial extension, same-region paired biopsies plus systematic tiles would be costly and still observational. A 5% patch requires 59 independent tiles in the ideal uniform model; this is a sampling burden, not a clinical recommendation. A patient-derived organoid or window-chamber model with a controlled spatial drug gradient and engineered lineage labels is a more feasible mechanism test. Compare it with an equal-data static-mixture baseline on the same barcodes, local drug measurements, cell counts and timepoints. A bulk well-mixed mean-field fit is a poor comparator if it lacks the spatial measurements; provide both models the same coordinate and lineage data, then assess held-out barcode transitions or spatial outcomes.
