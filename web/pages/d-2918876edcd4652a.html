# Family 029: a candidate 49/50 strip and general-field scope extension

Status: an internally reconstructed extension of a released analytic architecture. The finite certificate checks rational inequalities only. It does not certify the source's normalized cubic theta representation, its exact reflected expansion, or its global automorphic realization. The argument below is a real deduction from those load-bearing identities, with a complete new parameter schedule. It is not an unconditional externally verified theorem.

## A first consequence without re-tuning

The printed source already gives
\[
I_Z\ll_\epsilon Z^{933/2000+\epsilon},\qquad
I_Z=c_0f_\eta(Z)+O(Z^{7/15-0.0004}),\qquad c_0\ne0.
\]
Since `7/15-933/2000=1/6000`, the arbitrary epsilon gives zero-freeness in `Re s>1-1/6000`. The paper records the weaker `1-1/20000` intermediate conclusion and states `1-10^-6`. This first strengthening needs no new contour.

## The re-tuned zero detector

Keep the truncated Möbius inverse through `U^2`, but replace the smoothing length `U^10` by `U^(181/50)` and discard its tail only beyond `U^(363/100)`.

Detect zeros with
\[
\beta\ge917/1000,\qquad |\gamma|\le4U^\rho.
\]
At a zero the smoothing integral shifts to real part `1/2-beta`; the residue at zero vanishes. The inverse polynomial there is `O_F(U)`, and the conservative source convexity bound costs `U^(1/2+epsilon)(1+|gamma|)^A_F`. Thus the shifted integral costs
\[
U^{(181/50)(1/2-917/1000)+3/2+A_F\rho+\epsilon}
=U^{-477/50000+A_F\rho+\epsilon}.
\]
Choose `rho>0` sufficiently small after the fixed field is chosen, for example `rho<min(0.001,0.002/(A_F+1))`, then choose all power losses sufficiently small. This is `o(1)`. The tail beyond `U^(363/100)` is `exp(-U^(1/100))` times a fixed power, so it also tends to zero. Therefore some prefix on a dyad between `U^2/2` and `U^(363/100)` detects the zero.

The conductor sieve and the source's prefix/height maximal inequalities bound the number of exceptional characters by
\[
U^{(363/100)(2-2\cdot917/1000)+\rho+O(\epsilon)}
=U^{30129/50000+\rho+O(\epsilon)}.
\]
The exponent before the losses is `0.60258`. It is strictly below `0.61`; take the remaining losses within the gap and include the possible principal character. The exceptional row count is consequently `O_F(U^(61/100))`.

For a nonexceptional row, use disks centered at `2+it` with radii
\[
R_2=1.0825,\quad r_2=1.082,\quad r_1=1.081,\quad r_0=0.999.
\]
The outer disk lies to the right of `0.917`. Borel–Carathéodory bounds `log L` by `O_F(log U)` on `r_2`; the Euler logarithm is bounded on `r_0`. Three circles gives `O_F((log U)^theta)` on `r_1`, with `theta<1`. Consequently the source's reciprocal bound `U^epsilon` holds on `Re xi>=0.919, |Im xi|<=2U^rho`. Omitting the finitely many conductor factors still costs only `U^epsilon` because this real part is positive.

## Euler correction on the new contours

The source's exact closed good-prime factor gives an error controlled by
`VW`, `D1V`, `D1W`, `Q D1WV`, and `E1`, where their absolute powers are respectively
\[
Q^{-w-6z},\ Q^{-\xi-6z},\ Q^{-\xi-w},\
Q^{1-\xi-w-6z},\ Q^{4-6\xi-6z}.
\]
Therefore normal convergence follows whenever
\[
w+6z>1,\quad \xi+6z>1,\quad \xi+w>1,\quad
\xi+w+6z>2,\quad \xi+z>5/6.
\]
At a bad prime, the `t=1,k=1` term requires `xi+w>1`; the first higher-theta term requires
\[
3\xi-2-\max(0,1/2-w)>0.
\]
All inequalities hold with strict margins on the following rectangular regions and on sufficiently small open enlargements:

| Region | xi minimum | w minimum | z minimum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nonprincipal rows | 0.919 | 0.082 | 0.4 |
| Principal w shift | 0.98 | 0.8 | 1/6 |
| Principal z shift | 0.98 | 1 | 0.04 |

The weakest good-prime margin in the first region is `xi+w-1=0.001`. The bad higher-theta margin there is `0.339`. Excluding sufficiently many fixed small primes gives a nonvanishing principal correction, exactly as in the source. In particular `H_eta(s)=H_1(s,1,1/6)` is holomorphic and nonzero for `Re s>=0.98` and in a small neighborhood. These statements follow from the local factor formula, not from an unsupported extension of the originally printed rectangular region.

## New probe scales and complete comparison

Set
\[
a=159/200,\quad h=1-a=41/200,\quad b=7/30,\quad M=a+b=617/600.
\]
Here `0<b<a` and `M>1`. The reflected moment and additive large sieve give
\[
I(Z^a,Z^b,Z)\ll_\epsilon Z^{(2M-1-b)/2+\epsilon}
=Z^{247/600+\epsilon}.
\]
The principal reference exponent is
\[
C(1)=a/2+h/6=259/600,
\]
so the probe saving is exactly `1/50`.

For general contours the exponent relative to `C(1)` is
\[
E(\xi,w,z)-C(1)=\xi-1+h(z-1/6)+b(w-1).
\]
Use `delta_init=10^-7` for the small displacement of the initial principal lines; eta continues to denote the target Hecke character. Shift the principal w line to `0.8`; in its residue shift z to `0.04`. For nonprincipal rows use `w=0.082,z=0.4`, separate at `U=Z^0.13`, and end the intermediate interval at `U=Z^(h+10^-5)`. On good intermediate rows shift xi to `0.919`. On the far rows keep `xi=w=4` and shift z to `400000`. All paths stay within one of the Euler regions above; the only crossed poles are the two intended principal numerator poles.

| Contribution | Saving below C(1), before arbitrary small losses |
|---|---:|
| Reflection/probe | 0.020000000000 |
| Principal w remainder | 0.046666546167 |

The source's height-decay argument controls the horizontal and discarded good-row pieces. Since `U>Z^0.13` and `rho>0` is fixed for that field, taking its arbitrary decay exponent large enough supplies any required extra power. Dyadic counts and all arbitrary power losses can be taken below `0.0005` in total. Every nonprobe term then has saving greater than `0.0203`; in particular one may use a common `0.0202` comparison remainder.

The comparison therefore gives, for every sufficiently small positive epsilon,
\[
f_\eta(Z)\ll Z^{247/600+\epsilon}.
\]
Its exact inverse Mellin exponent is now `s-341/600`; the same rightward contour shift as in the source gives `f_eta(Z)=O_A(Z^A)` at zero for arbitrary `A`.

The Mellin transform
\[
\int_0^\infty f_\eta(Z)Z^{-s+341/600}\,dZ/Z
\]
is holomorphic for `Re s>49/50+epsilon`. Fourier inversion on `Re s=2` identifies it with
\[
e^{(s-5/6)^2}H_\eta(s)/L_F^S(s,\eta).
\]
Its numerator is nonvanishing in the required half-plane. Given any `s` with `Re s>49/50`, choose epsilon smaller than its distance from `49/50`. A zero of `L` would give a pole of this holomorphic transform. Removed Euler factors have zeros only on real part zero. Thus the proposed strengthened conclusion is
\[
L_F(s,\eta)\ne0\quad(\Re s>49/50).
\]

## General-field scope audit

The released statement restricts F to a cyclotomic field containing mu12. Its analytic proof uses cyclotomicity only in declarations, in the inspected sections. The load-bearing field requirements are:

* `mu12 subset F`, which implies all archimedean places are complex and makes the sextic Hilbert pairing alternating;
* ordinary S-principalization, Kummer/class-field theory, S-unit theorem, and balanced S-generators;
* the norm map `F -> Q(omega)` for the Gauss correction. This exists for every F containing mu12, without a Galois or abelian hypothesis;
* the cubic exceptional representation of Kazhdan–Patterson for an arbitrary global number field containing mu3, specialized to rank two, cover order three, and c=2;
* the quadratic Hecke-family sieve of Goldmakher–Louvel over an arbitrary number field.

The latter two general scopes were checked against their primary sources. The source's norm-sheet profile proof eliminates a chosen complex norm variable and uses products of one-dimensional weighted integrals; its power exponents have no dependence on the number of complex places. Fixed-field constants may be extremely large, but do not change the Mellin convergence half-plane.

This is evidence for replacing cyclotomic F by any F containing mu12 in the analytic architecture. It is not a substitute for a complete review of the exact theta/reflection identities. The field-scope extension inherits that unresolved source gate; there is no separate observed cyclotomic obstruction.

If the generalized architecture is valid, take an arbitrary number field K and `F=K(mu12)`. This is an abelian extension of K of degree at most four. For a finite-order Hecke character chi of K, its norm pullback chi_F has finite order and
\[
L_F(s,\chi_F)=\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)}}L_K(s,\chi\psi).
\]
Away from s=1, the factors have no poles; a zero of `L_K(s,chi)` cannot cancel. At s=1 the ordinary nonvanishing theorem handles nonprincipal characters, and a principal character has a pole. The candidate strip therefore holds over **every number field**, with the same `49/50` boundary. It also holds for every Dedekind zeta function. The extension does not prove GRH or full Artin holomorphy.

It verifies that this schedule is internally feasible. It does not execute the analytic transforms, acquire the theta representation, prove the reflected expansion, or test zeta zeros.
