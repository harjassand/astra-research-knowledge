# Exact detection, bounded output uncertainty, and query costs

This note records the preregistered discriminator and the negative evidence that remains after the linear-dimension proof. All universal statements below concern the explicit generator and are proved analytically; finite checks validate the implementation within their listed scopes.

## Normalized nonzero family

Let C=[x1,x2] and p_k=2^(-k) C^k. The 2^k concatenated words are distinct, so the coefficient l1 norm is exactly one. Encoding subtraction as addition of a product with scalar -1 gives a formula of size 10k+1, including the final scalar normalization. No enormous or hidden coefficient height is needed: the normalization denominator has k+1 bits.

For a>=0, direct expansion gives

\[
 [J_1,J_2]z^a
 =-\frac{z^{a+3}}{4(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)}+O(z^{a+4}).
\]

The degree a+2 terms cancel. Therefore

\[
 p_k(T)e_0
 =\frac{(-1)^k}{8^k(3k)!}e_{3k}+\text{higher Taylor coordinates}
\]

whenever d>3k. This proves nonvanishing directly for the discriminating family, independently of the universal hitting theorem.

## A uniform full-matrix attenuation bound

Both original input matrices have infinity norm at most one: T1's r-th absolute row sum is one, and T2's is (1-2^(-r))/r<=1.

For a word w of length L, every coefficient of J_w z^q at degree r has common sign (-1)^(r-q-L). Evaluate at z=-rho, 0<rho<1. Absolute Taylor coefficient sums become positive iterated integrals with kernels 1/(1-t),1/(2-t). Summing over every binary word of length L replaces the kernels by their sum. For inputs t^q<=rho^q, the resulting repeated single-kernel integral is bounded by

\[
 \rho^q A(\rho)^L/L!,\qquad
 A(\rho)=-\log(1-\rho)-\log(1-\rho/2).
\]

The normalized commutator contains a subset of 2^k words of length 2k, each of absolute coefficient 2^(-k). Thus every output entry satisfies

\[
 |p_k(T)_{r,q}|
 \le\rho^{q-r}\frac{A(\rho)^{2k}}{2^k(2k)!}.
\]

Choose rho=1-1/d. Then rho^(-(d-1))<e and A(rho)=log(2d^2/(d+1))<log(2d), giving

\[
 \max_{r,q}|p_k(T)_{r,q}|
 < e\frac{\log(2d)^{2k}}{2^k(2k)!}.
 \tag{1}
\]

For a fully rational certificate use e<3 and log(2)<7/10. The latter follows, for example, from log(2)=2 sum_(j>=0) (1/3)^(2j+1)/(2j+1)<2/3+1/36<7/10. Hence

\[
 U_{k,d}=3\frac{((7/10)\lceil\log_2(2d)\rceil)^{2k}}{2^k(2k)!}
\]

is an exact rational upper bound. The executable stores its numerator and denominator; display decimals are secondary.

At any dimension d bounded by a fixed polynomial in k, Stirling's lower factorial bound yields

\[
 \log_2 U_{k,d}
 \le -2k\log_2 k+2k\log_2\log k+O(k).
\]

Consequently no fixed positive **entrywise absolute-error tolerance** makes this family uniformly distinguishable from zero through this tuple. If each reported entry may incur error at most eta>=U, an all-zero response is admissible both for p_k and for the zero polynomial. This is an adversarial bounded-uncertainty statement. It does not say that IEEE floating point necessarily rounds every result to zero, or that an evaluator using relative accuracy and additional algebraic structure cannot compute it.

## Controls and what they discriminate

The first control is the predetermined diagonal similarity D_rr=r!L^r, L=lcm(1,...,n). At n=2 it makes the first nonzero entry of p_k equal (-1)^k. Every homogeneous rank test is invariant under this similarity. Its input norms and integer bit lengths grow greatly: removing small output coordinates does not establish numerical stability.

The second control is B1=S, B2=Dalt*S, where S is the unit lower shift and (Dalt)_rr=(-1)^r. These matrices have infinity norm one. Their commutator is -2Dalt*S^2, with Dalt commuting with S^2, so p_k(B) has entries of absolute value one whenever d>2k. This control uses the same dimensions and target-independent definitions. It is limited: B2^2=-S^2, so the nonzero polynomial x1*x2^2-x2^2*x1 evaluates to zero at every dimension. The original tuple detects that polynomial. The comparison demonstrates different robustness/alias tradeoffs; it does not establish superiority over a universal prior hitting construction.

## Explicit query cost and the effect of the new proof

For n=2 and the original rational tuple, define the preregistered payload format as separate reduced numerator magnitude, sign bit, and denominator magnitude for each nonzero entry, with no shared-denominator or structure compression. Its exact logical bit count, excluding indices/container/zero entries, is

\[
 B(d)=2\sum_{r=1}^{d-1}r\,\mathrm{bitlength}(r)
       +(d^3-d)/6+2d(d-1).
 \tag{2}
\]

This is a cost for that explicit dense encoding, not an information-theoretic storage lower bound. The generator has a short arithmetic description, and a structured-operator API can avoid writing the entries. Exact target response bit sizes still depend on coefficient encoding and evaluation.

The initial preregistered table uses the source's formula dimension d_old=4(10k+1)^2:

| k | d_old | rational payload under (2) | certified max-entry upper bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 6,724 | 6.415 GB | 3.95631e2 (uninformative) |
| 8 | 26,244 | 377.992 GB | 3.43360e1 (uninformative) |
| 16 | 103,684 | 23.2465 TB | 2.83353e-5 |
| 32 | 412,164 | 1.45914 PB | 1.23863e-25 |
| 64 | 1,643,524 | 92.4959 PB | 4.24321e-83 |

The bound at k32 makes a per-entry error 2^(-82) sufficient to erase the response; k64 similarly permits erasure at 2^(-273). These are conservative necessary-precision witnesses, not sufficient precision budgets or tight separation estimates.

The linear-dimension theorem, if its audit closes, changes this family's formula-bound query dimension to d_new=4ns=8(10k+1), greatly reducing (2). This changes generator dimension and dense query description; it preserves the absolute-error obstruction because d_new remains polynomial in k. We do not silently transfer the old response interface: one old first-column response contains Theta(nm^2) field elements, whereas a new full response contains Theta(n^2m^2). At fixed n both are of order m^2. Full residual learning also requires full-width prefix blocks or multiple seeded calls, as documented in the proof note and independent audit.

## Decisive cyclic obstruction

The proper noncommutative rational expression

\[
 R(A,B)=AB-B(I+A)^{-1}A(I+B)
\]

has nonzero free formal series with lowest homogeneous part AB-BA. Yet it vanishes as an operator when A=J1 and B=J2, so it also vanishes at **every** finite jet tuple. Each I+T1 is invertible by nilpotence.

A direct proof avoids illegal two-sided inverses of the integration operators. For arbitrary input a, set F(z)=((z+2)/(z+1))Ba(z). Then F(0)=0 and

\[
 (F+AF)'=a/(z+1)+(z+2-1)Ba/(z+1)^2
           =[A(I+B)a]'.
\]

Both sides vanish at zero, so F=(I+A)^(-1)A(I+B)a. Also

\[
 (BF)'=F/(z+2)=Ba/(z+1)=(ABa)',
\]

with zero initial values. Hence R(J1,J2)a=0 for every a. This is stronger than a first-column collision. It rules out extending the unmarked kernel construction to arbitrary cyclic/proper rational formulas. It does not refute the acyclic theorem or the release's separate rational hitting-list construction.

## Scientific inference and next experiment

The initial cancellation/rank discriminator changed the investigation: improved first-column compression is constrained by the quadratic state lower bound, while complete matrix responses led to the Volterra linear-dimension proof. The next experiment should implement that new constructor through a precisely specified full-response oracle and charge input encoding, all seed evaluations, output bit growth, and memory. It should compare against an equally informed generic randomized matrix test and, when target structure is supplied, the competent white-box prior. Fixed-tolerance numerical or physical transfer requires a different robust primitive or an explicit conditioning/normalization theorem; integer coordinates alone do not supply one. The acyclic-to-cyclic bridge requires new marking or another faithful representation because the exact rational alias above blocks the naive transfer.
