EXACT MODEL AND RESULTS

On the full N-qubit tensor Hilbert space, let

 J_a=(1/2)sum_i sigma_a^(i), J^2=sum_a J_a^2,
 rho_N=exp(2J^2/N)/Z_N, Z_N=Tr exp(2J^2/N).

The state is invariant under permutations and under common SU(2)
rotations. Its one-body marginal is tau=I/2. Let

 chi_N(r)=2^r Tr rho_r^2-1,
 K_N(r)=D(rho_r||(I/2)^tensorr),
 T_N(r)=(1/2)||rho_r-(I/2)^tensorr||_1,
 a=4/3,
 I_0=int_0^infinity x^2 exp(-a x^4)dx
     =(1/4)a^(-3/4)Gamma(3/4),
 g(x)=x^2 exp(-a x^4)/I_0, x>0,
 gamma=a^(-1/2)Gamma(5/4)/Gamma(3/4),
 k_star=(8/3)gamma^2=2[Gamma(5/4)/Gamma(3/4)]^2.

The square-root window is sharp:

1. For every sequence 2<=r=r_N=o(sqrt(N)),

 chi_N(r)=[k_star+o(1)]r(r-1)/N.                       (T1)

In particular chi_N(r),K_N(r)=O(r^2/N), and T_N(r)=O(r/sqrt(N)).
At r=1 all three quantities are exactly zero. Explicit uniform constants
and a finite range are given below.

2. Let r/sqrt(N) tend to a fixed c in (0,infinity). Let X have density g,
U be independent and uniform on [-1,1], and W=2XU. For any s>0 define

 delta(c,s)=exp(-s^2/2)[1-E cos(s sqrt(c) W)]
           =exp(-s^2/2)[1-E sinc(2s sqrt(c) X)] >0,

where sinc(0)=1. A bounded z-magnetization observable proves

 liminf T_N(r)>=delta(c,s)/2,
 liminf K_N(r)>=delta(c,s)^2/2.                        (T2)

This is a trace/KL lower bound from an actual bounded measurement, not
an inference from a chi-square lower bound. The measured normalized
magnetization has characteristic functions tending to those of
Normal(0,1)+sqrt(c)W.

3. For any 1<=r<=N with r/sqrt(N) tending to infinity,

 T_N(r) tends to 1,
 K_N(r) tends to infinity.                            (T3)

In fact the proof gives
K_N(r)>=[1/2-o(1)]log(r/sqrt(N))-log 2.

Thus trace distance, KL, and centered chi-square tend to zero exactly
when r=o(sqrt(N)); if this condition fails, they have a nonzero lower
bound along a subsequence. No full quantum crossover function or sharp
KL constant is asserted.

PROOF

1. Exact spin weights and the quartic sector law.

Allowed spins are j=0,1,...,N/2 for even N, and j=1/2,3/2,...,N/2 for
odd N. The multiplicity is

 m_(N,j)=binomial(N,N/2-j)-binomial(N,N/2-j-1)
         =[(2j+1)/(N/2+j+1)]binomial(N,N/2-j).

The first formula follows by subtracting consecutive magnetic-space
dimensions; each spin-j irrep contributes one vector to every allowed
magnetic level between -j and j. In sector j the Gibbs state is scalar.
Write the unnormalized tilt of the white sector probability as

 w_(N,j)=[(2j+1)^2/(N/2+j+1)]
          *[binomial(N,N/2-j)/2^N] exp[2j(j+1)/N],
 calZ_N=sum_j w_(N,j)=Z_N/2^N,
 p_(N,j)=w_(N,j)/calZ_N.                              (P1)

Let S=N^(3/4), x=j/S, and

 I(u)=[(1+u)/2]log(1+u)+[(1-u)/2]log(1-u).

For |u|<=1,

 I(u)=sum_(k>=1)u^(2k)/[2k(2k-1)],
 I(u)>=u^2/2+u^4/12.                                 (P2)

For |u|<=1/2 the coefficient bound for k>=2 also gives
I(u)<=u^2/2+u^4/[12(1-u^2)]<=u^2/2+u^4/9.

Stirling's factorial bounds yield, uniformly for j/S in a fixed compact
subset of (0,infinity),

 binomial(N,N/2-j)/2^N
  =sqrt(2/(pi N))
    *exp[-2j^2/N-(4/3)j^4/N^3+o(1)],

since the next entropy term is O(j^6/N^5)=O(N^-1/2) on such compacts.
The residual tilt 2j/N is o(1). The prefactor in (P1) is asymptotic to
8j^2/N. Therefore

 w_(N,j) tends uniformly on these scaled compacts to
 8sqrt(2/pi)x^2 exp(-4x^4/3).                          (P3)

Allowed j have spacing one under both N parities. The uniform tail
bound proved next justifies the Riemann-sum limit

 calZ_N/S tends to 8sqrt(2/pi)I_0,
 j/N^(3/4) under p_(N,j) converges in distribution to X with density g.
                                                                    (P4)

All polynomial moments converge as well. In particular

 E j(j+1)/N^(3/2) tends to gamma.                      (P5)

The primary factorial bound used here is Robbins' formula
n!=sqrt(2pi)n^(n+1/2)exp(-n+r_n),
1/(12n+1)<r_n<1/(12n), for integers n>=1. It is stated on journal
page 26, PDF page 2, equations (1)-(2), in the original article:
Herbert Robbins, "A Remark on Stirling's Formula," American Mathematical
Monthly 62 (1955), 26-29, DOI 10.2307/2308012. The original scan is
retained locally; primary source checked during this derivation:
https://dornsife.usc.edu/sergey-lototsky/wp-content/uploads/sites/211/2024/02/Stirling-Robbins.pdf

2. Uniform exponential-square bound and explicit finite constants.

The following crude constants are enough for a completely uniform
bound, without assuming the asymptotic normalization in advance. Put

 U_*=648+24exp(6)(480/e)^(5/4),
 A_*=216 U_*.

For every N>=256,

 calZ_N>=S/216,
 (1/S)sum_j w_(N,j) exp[(j/S)^2]<=U_*,
 E_(p_N) exp[(j/S)^2]<=A_*.                           (P6)

Here is the full tail calculation. In the central range j<=N/4,
Robbins' factorial bounds imply

 exp[-NI(2j/N)]/(3sqrt(N))
  <=binomial(N,N/2-j)/2^N
  <=2exp[-NI(2j/N)]/sqrt(N).                          (P7)

Indeed the binomial prefactor is [2pi Np(1-p)]^(-1/2), with
p in [1/4,3/4], and the remainder is between -1/6 and 1/12. These
give the stated looser constants 1/3 and 2.

For j in [S/4,S/2], this central range applies when N>=256.
The lower prefactor in (P1) is at least 2j^2/N. From the upper
entropy inequality after (P2),

 w_(N,j)>=(1/24)exp(-1/9)>=1/27.

There are at least S/8 allowed j in this interval: its length is S/4,
which is at least two, and the grid has spacing one. This proves
calZ_N>=S/216.

For j<=N/4, (P7) and the lower entropy inequality give

 w_(N,j)<=16exp(1/2)(x+1/S)^2 exp(-4x^4/3)
          <=54(x^2+1)exp(-4x^4/3).

The elementary completed-square bounds

 -4x^4/3+x^2<=27/64-x^2/2,
 (x^2+1)exp(-x^2/2)<=2exp(-x^2/4), exp(27/64)<2,

then give w_(N,j)exp(x^2)<=216exp(-x^2/4).
The decreasing Gaussian grid sum is at most
1+S int_0^infinity exp(-x^2/4)dx<=3S. Thus the central contribution
to the second expression in (P6) is at most 648.

For j>N/4, the entropy bound
binomial(N,N/2-j)/2^N<=exp[-NI(2j/N)]
holds even at the endpoints. Using (P2) and 2j/N<=1 gives

 w_(N,j)<=24N exp(-N/192).

Also x^2<=sqrt(N)/4 and there are at most N allowed j. Hence the
far contribution to the unnormalized exponential-square sum is at most

 24N^2 exp[-N/192+sqrt(N)/4]
 <=24exp(6)N^2 exp(-N/384).

Divide by S. The maximum of z^(5/4)exp(-z/384) over z>=0 is
(480/e)^(5/4), proving (P6). These estimates also give

 P_(p_N)(j>N/4)<=5184N^(5/4)exp(-N/192) tending to zero. (P8)

The unnormalized exponential-square bound in (P6) provides uniform
tails for the Riemann sums in (P3), and its normalized version gives
uniform integrability of every polynomial moment. This completes
the justification of (P4)-(P5), for both N parities.

3. Local chi-square and KL upper bounds below sqrt(N).

Every one-qubit Hermitian H with ||H||<=1 can be written
H=h_0 I+h.sigma with |h|<=1. Centering at tau=I/2 leaves h.sigma.
In spin sector j, S_H=sum_i(H_i-Tr tau H I)=2h.J has spectrum in
[-2j,2j]. Therefore (P6) and 2|t|j<=(j/S)^2+t^2 S^2 give for all real t

 Tr rho_N exp(tS_H)
     <=E_(p_N)exp(2|t|j)
     <=A_* exp(N^(3/2)t^2).                           (P9)

This is the exact concentration hypothesis of this agent's earlier
frozen theorem with d=2,kappa=1/2,A=A_*, and B_N=sqrt(N). Its proof is
pointwise in N and B, so substituting this N-dependent B is legal.
That proof supplies

 L_N=10e(sqrt(N)+2), q=r/N, y_N=L_N q/(1-q),
 chi_N(r)<=A_*^2 y_N^2/(1-y_N) when y_N<1.

For N>=256 and r<=sqrt(N)/(24e), its finite range
q<=1/(2L_N+1) holds, because 2L_N+1<=24e sqrt(N). Consequently

 chi_N(r),K_N(r)<=2000e^2 A_*^2 r^2/N,
 T_N(r)<= (1/2)sqrt(chi_N(r)).                        (P10)

The chi-square constant follows from 8A_*^2 L_N^2 q^2 and
(sqrt(N)+2)^2<=(9/8)^2 N. The KL and trace inequalities are the
noncommuting Jensen and weighted Cauchy-Schwarz estimates proved in
the earlier frozen theorem. Since the comparator is the identity,
the trace estimate is also the immediate Hilbert-Schmidt bound.
Every sub-square-root sequence eventually enters this explicit range.

There is also a sharp chi-square leading term. SU(2) invariance gives
the exact centered pair tensor C_ab=c_N delta_ab with

 c_N=[(4/3)E j(j+1)-N]/[N(N-1)].

The degree-two Gram contribution is exactly

 chi_2=3binomial(r,2)c_N^2.

All odd centered tensors vanish: for a single real direction, the
distribution of the collective products of its spin components is
invariant under reversing that direction by an SU(2) rotation, so its
odd homogeneous contractions vanish; polarization gives the whole
odd tensor equal to zero.

The coefficient-by-coefficient bound in the frozen concentration proof
is chi_k<=A_*^2 y_N^k for k>=2. Thus the tail starting at degree four
is at most A_*^2 y_N^4/(1-y_N)=O((r/sqrt(N))^4) below the window.
By (P5), c_N=[4gamma/3+o(1)]/sqrt(N). This tail is o(r(r-1)/N)
for every 2<=r=o(sqrt(N)), proving (T1).

4. Exact classical z measurement and its limiting global bias.

In spin sector j the state is maximally mixed in the spin irrep. Thus
conditional on j, the magnetic quantum number m is uniform on
{-j,-j+1,...,j}. Define the global measurement bias and its scaled form

 ell_N=2m/N, W_N=2m/N^(3/4), so ell_N=N^(-1/4)W_N.

The joint convergence in (P4), and the Riemann sum of the uniform
magnetic grid, give

 W_N converges in distribution to W=2XU,               (P11)

where U is uniform on [-1,1] independently of X. One can verify this
for bounded continuous test functions on x in [epsilon,R], where j
tends uniformly to infinity; the probabilities outside this interval
then vanish as epsilon decreases to zero and R increases to infinity.
The law W has no atom at zero, and its characteristic function is
E sinc(2sX).

Measuring all N qubits in the z basis gives an exchangeable bit string.
Given total magnetic quantum number m, its diagonal is uniform over
strings with K=N/2+m plus signs, because the permutations are transitive
on that set. Hence the first r measured signs have exactly the
hypergeometric count distribution Hyp(N,K,r), irrespective of any
off-diagonal coherences of rho_N.

For r>=6 and j<=N/4, K/N is in [1/4,3/4], so
r(K/N)(1-K/N)>=1. The primary hypergeometric approximation theorem
then gives

 TV(Hyp(N,K,r),Bin(r,K/N))<=(r-1)/(N-1).

Mixing and (P8) show that the local z count is within total variation

 epsilon_(N,r)=(r-1)/(N-1)+P(j>N/4)                    (P12)

of the corresponding binomial mixture whenever r>=6.

hypergeometric and the binomial distribution," May 1998, theorem on
PDF page 2, with the exact hypothesis np(1-p)>=1 and TV convention
sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|. The author manuscript gives a proof. It was checked

5. Critical-window bounded measurement and genuine trace/KL lower bounds.

Suppose r/sqrt(N) tends to c in (0,infinity), so r tends to infinity
and epsilon_(N,r) tends to zero. Write S_r=sum_(i=1)^r sigma_z^(i).
In the binomial mixture, conditional on ell_N, the characteristic
function of S_r/sqrt(r) is

 [cos(s/sqrt(r))+i ell_N sin(s/sqrt(r))]^r.

For fixed s the logarithm expands, uniformly for |ell_N|<=1, as

 i s sqrt(r)ell_N -(s^2/2)(1-ell_N^2)+O_s(r^-1/2).

By (P11), ell_N tends to zero in probability and
sqrt(r)ell_N tends in distribution to sqrt(c)W. The characteristic
functions are bounded in modulus by one. Thus their averages converge
to exp(-s^2/2)E exp(i s sqrt(c)W). The hypergeometric error in (P12)
changes this expectation by at most 2epsilon_(N,r), which tends to zero.
For the white comparator
the characteristic function is cos(s/sqrt(r))^r, tending to
exp(-s^2/2).

Use the actual bounded Hermitian observable

 O_(N,r)=cos(s S_r/sqrt(r)), ||O_(N,r)||<=1.

Its expectation gap tends to delta(c,s)>0 as defined in (T2), since
X>0 almost surely and sinc(2s sqrt(c)X)<1. Equivalently the two-outcome
POVM (I+O_(N,r))/2 has a nonzero outcome-probability gap. Therefore

 T_N(r)>=|Tr[(rho_r-(I/2)^tensorr)O_(N,r)]|/2,

proving the trace lower bound in (T2).

For completeness, a bounded-measurement KL lower bound needs no
unproved chi-square-to-KL reversal. Let P,Q be the z-basis distributions
of rho_r and the white reference. For any f taking values in [-1,1],
the elementary bounded-variable log-mgf estimate under Q gives
log E_Q exp[lambda(f-E_Q f)]<=lambda^2/2. Relative entropy to the
exponentially tilted Q then gives

 D(P||Q)>=lambda(E_P f-E_Q f)-lambda^2/2
         >=(E_P f-E_Q f)^2/2

after optimization over real lambda. Also
K_N(r)>=D(P||Q): writing each diagonal entry of rho_r as a convex
combination of its eigenvalues, convexity of x log x gives
sum_x P(x)log P(x)<=Tr rho_r log rho_r. The comparator is uniform.
Taking f to be the measured cosine proves the KL lower bound in (T2).

6. Above-window separation from a shrinking-threshold event.

This step works for any r<=N with r/sqrt(N) tending to infinity;
no r=o(N) or binomial approximation is needed. Put

 Y_N=N^(1/4)S_r/r, v_N=sqrt(N)/r tending to zero,
 eta_N=v_N^(1/4), E_N={|Y_N|>eta_N}.

In the exact hypergeometric law conditional on the global magnetic
number m,

 E(Y_N|m)=W_N,
 Var(Y_N|m)
   =[sqrt(N)/r](1-ell_N^2)(N-r)/(N-1)<=v_N.

Thus P_rho(|Y_N-W_N|>eta_N)<=v_N^(1/2). Since W_N converges to W and
W has no atom at zero, P(|W_N|<=2eta_N) tends to zero. Indeed for every
fixed epsilon>0 the shrinking interval is eventually inside
[-epsilon,epsilon], and the closed-set bound under weak convergence
then tends to P(|W|<=epsilon); let epsilon decrease to zero. Consequently

 P_rho(E_N) tends to 1.

Under the white comparator the measured signs are independent, with
E Y_N=0 and Var Y_N=v_N. Chebyshev therefore gives

 P_white(E_N)<=v_N^(1/2) tending to zero.

This is an actual event of a bounded projective measurement. Its
probability gap tends to one, so T_N(r) tends to one. Grouping the
z-measurement outcomes into E_N and its complement cannot increase
classical relative entropy; the log-sum inequality proves this directly.
If p_N=P_rho(E_N) tends to one and q_N=P_white(E_N), the binary KL is
at least p_N log(1/q_N)-log 2. Since q_N<=v_N^(1/2),

 K_N(r)>=[p_N/2]log(r/sqrt(N))-log 2 tending to infinity.

This proves (T3), with the stated lower rate.

7. Exact indistinguishability scale, without a full crossover claim.

The estimates below the window prove all three divergences tend to zero
when r=o(sqrt(N)). If that condition fails, a subsequence has
r>=c sqrt(N) for some c>0. Apply the critical bounded observable to
floor(c sqrt(N)) of the available r sites and ignore the others.
The same trace and KL expectation-gap argument gives a fixed positive
lower bound on that subsequence. The inequality
T_N(r)<=(1/2)sqrt(chi_N(r)) also forces a nonzero chi-square lower bound.
Hence each of trace, KL, and centered chi-square tends to zero if and
only if r=o(sqrt(N)). Above the window the stronger conclusions (T3)
hold. This establishes the scale requested, while making no assertion
about an exact quantum trace/KL crossover function at finite c.
