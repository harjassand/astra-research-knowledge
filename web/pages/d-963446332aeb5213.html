Conditional Gaussian-count sampler by inflated isotropic P proposal
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: exact-real sampler theorem is internally proved candidate; finite-bit implementation has an explicit error/cost schedule under standard rational interval primitives

Interface and assumptions
=========================

There are d optical modes and a supplied centered Gaussian state with a strictly
positive Gaussian P representation. After loss, let x in R^(2d) be its coherent
quadrature vector, with

    x ~ N(0, t A),   t>0,   A=A^T>0.

Assume A is rational and has a supplied rational certificate

    (1-eps_bar) I <= A <= (1+eps_bar) I,
    0 <= eps_bar <= 1/4.

In particular c=1+eps_bar is rational and cI-A is positive semidefinite. A
binary or general PNR herald specifies k_j>=0 photons in each of s herald modes;
write kappa=sum_j k_j. The other d-s modes form the output count record. The
target herald probability is positive (automatic here for t>0 and A>0).
The numerical value kappa is charged, not just its binary description length.

For coherent quadratures x_j=(q_j,p_j), a PNR outcome n in mode j has
likelihood

    L_n(x_j)=exp(-||x_j||^2/2) (||x_j||^2/2)^n/n!.

Thus the conditional latent density on all modes is the Gaussian prior density
times L_H(x)=prod_{j in H}L_{k_j}(x_j), normalized by the actual herald mass.
Given the latent x, unheralded counts are independent Poisson random variables
with means ||x_j||^2/2. This is a positive-P mixture identity, not a truncation
of the Gaussian Fock expansion.

Exact proposal and rejection rule
=================================

Use the inflated isotropic Gaussian proposal prior

    x ~ N(0, c t I_(2d)),    c=1+eps_bar,

conditioned on the same herald. This proposal posterior is explicit and
factorizes by mode. For a heralded mode j, its phase is uniform and its radius
squared has Gamma distribution

    ||x_j||^2 ~ Gamma(shape k_j+1, scale 2ct/(1+ct));

for an unheralded mode, the phase is uniform and

    ||x_j||^2 ~ Gamma(shape 1, scale 2ct).

Equivalently, after y=x/sqrt(t), the scales are 2c/(1+ct) and 2c. These are
integer-shape gamma variables, samplable as sums of k_j+1 (or one) exponential
variables. The proposal herald probability is known exactly:

    q_H = (ct)^kappa/(1+ct)^(kappa+s).

Let f_A and f_c be the unconditioned Gaussian densities in y coordinates for
N(0,A) and N(0,cI), respectively. Since A<=cI,

    f_A(y)/f_c(y) = M exp(-y^T D y/2),
    M = c^d / sqrt(det A),
    D = A^(-1) - c^(-1)I >= 0.

The determinant term M is a valid global upper bound on this density ratio.
After drawing y from the explicit proposal posterior conditioned on H, accept
with probability

    a(y)=exp(-y^T D y/2) in [0,1].

Then draw the remaining modes' Poisson counts using the accepted coherent
amplitudes. The accepted latent density is exactly

    f_c(y)L_H(y) a(y) / (q_H E[a|H])
       = f_A(y)L_H(y)/p_H,

so the resulting count vector has exactly the requested conditional law. No
oracle for p_H, q_H, or det(A) is needed by the accept/reject step; D is the
only covariance-derived matrix evaluated there. The determinant appears only
in the proof of the envelope.

Rare-herald-free overhead bound
===============================

Write Y=y under the proposal posterior. The squared radii remain independent
gamma variables, so

    E||Y||^2
       = 2c(d-s) + 2c(s+kappa)/(1+ct)
       <= 2c(d+kappa).

The covariance certificate gives eigenvalues of A in [1-eps_bar,1+eps_bar].
Therefore

    ||D|| <= 1/(1-eps_bar)-1/(1+eps_bar)
          = 2 eps_bar/(1-eps_bar^2).

For the proposal acceptance probability alpha=E exp(-Y^T D Y/2), Jensen
and D>=0 yield

    alpha >= exp(-||D|| E||Y||^2/2)
           >= exp(-C (d+kappa) eps_bar)

for an absolute constant C, since c<=5/4. Direct normalization also gives

    alpha = p_H/(M q_H),
    E[proposal trials] = M q_H/p_H
                       <= exp(C (d+kappa) eps_bar).

The bound has no inverse-p_H factor. The expected proposal count is polynomial
in L when (d+kappa)eps_bar=O(log L). A polynomial-time implementation also
requires numeric kappa (for the Gamma draws) and the requested output-count
scale to be polynomially bounded; the finite-bit statement below charges both
explicitly. Otherwise the rejection overhead or per-proposal work can be
exponential. Numerical d, kappa, expected output count, and all matrix bit
lengths remain separately charged.

Application to N81 weak-Gaussian noise
======================================

For N81's detector-plane source B with ||B||<=g, add isotropic displacement
noise nu before uniform loss eta. Its post-loss P covariance is

    C = t(I+E), t=eta nu, ||E||<=eps_bar:=g/((1-g)nu).

When eps_bar<=1/4, use c=1+eps_bar. The covariance and c are rational for the
N81 rational B, nu=R^(-a) (fixed integer a>=1), and eta=1/R; the inequality
cI-A>=0 follows from the analytic norm bound and is a certificate that can be
checked by exact rational LDL arithmetic. N81 has d=Theta(R), kappa=h=O(R),
g<=2^(-ell), ell>=R. Hence

    (d+kappa)eps_bar = O(R^(a+1) 2^(-ell)) = o(1).

The exact conditional sampler above has expected proposal count 1+o(1) on these
noisy instances, regardless of their rare herald mass. This is stronger than
the output-TV-to-vacuum observation as an algorithmic statement for the N81
family, but still says nothing about arbitrary full-rank Gaussian inputs.
For the noise-only reference, the binary herald mass is

    p_ref = t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0),  t=eta nu=R^(-(a+1)),

and the actual N81 herald mass is p_H=p_ref exp(O(d eps_bar)); hence
log(1/p_H)=O(R log R), even though p_H itself is exponentially small. The
added input mean photon number is O(d nu)=O(R^(1-a)) and the post-loss total
mean is O(eta d nu)=O(R^(-a)); the pre-noise squeezed-source contribution is
O(d g^2). Under the reference posterior, the unheralded standardized norm
S_U has E[S_U^2]=O(d^2), so its output count mean is O(t d). The target
posterior density ratio r has E[r^2]<=exp(O(d eps_bar)) and normalization
E[r]>=exp(-O(d eps_bar)); Cauchy-Schwarz therefore gives
E_target[S_U]=E_ref[r S_U]/E_ref[r]=O(d) when d eps_bar=o(1). Thus the full
unheralded output mean remains O(t d)=O(R^(-a)) (and the two retained modes
alone have mean O(t)). These energy statements are separate from the TV-to-
vacuum statement.
The N81 source/compiler reduction remains conditional. Under this specified
all-mode noise, the retained law is vacuum-like and its encoded constant-TV
amplification is erased; subleading probabilities may still encode
information.

Finite-bit implementation and costs
====================================

The exact-real identity above uses continuous gamma radii/phases and an exact
Bernoulli coin. It is not itself a Turing-machine sampler. Here is a separate
finite-bit approximation statement. Let eps_out in (0,1) be the requested TV
error and xi in (0,1) the allowed timeout probability. Choose a dyadic upper
bound Lambda >= exp(C(d+kappa)eps_bar), of bit length O((d+kappa)eps_bar+1).
Then the exact acceptance probability obeys alpha>=1/Lambda. Put

    rho = eps_out/(C0 Lambda),
    K = (d-s) + sum_(j in H)(k_j+1) = d+kappa,
    T = 4c[K log 2 + log(8/rho)].

The proposal's total squared radius is a sum of K independent exponential
variables, each scaled by at most 2c. Its mgf therefore gives

    Pr[||Y||^2>T] <= 2^K exp(-T/(4c)) <= rho/8.

To realize the gamma draws, generate K dyadic uniforms, apply -log, sum the
appropriate groups, and take square roots; generate d dyadic phases and
evaluate sine/cosine by rational interval arithmetic. For a coupling proof,
discard the event that any of the K uniforms is below zeta=rho/(16K), whose
probability is at most rho/16. Use a dyadic grid of mesh at most zeta*delta_e/4
and evaluate each logarithm to absolute error delta_e, where

    delta_e <= delta_y^2/(64cK),
    delta_phase <= delta_y/(8 sqrt(T)),
    delta_y = rho/[C1(1+sqrt(eps_bar)+sqrt(t))].

On the remaining event, the squared radii differ in total by O(cK delta_e),
and the phase error on the T-ball contributes O(sqrt(T)delta_phase), hence
the coupled vector error is at most delta_y after increasing fixed constants.
Project the dyadic vector onto the closed radius-sqrt(T) ball using an
interval approximation to the radial scale; metric projection is nonexpansive,
so this preserves the coupling error up to the extra interval precision.
The required random-bit precision is
O(log(K/rho)+log(K/delta_y)+log(T/delta_y)); rational interval evaluation of
log, square root, sine, and cosine is polynomial in these bit lengths and in
the input bit length. The bad-uniform and radius-tail probabilities together
are O(rho).

The acceptance function has global Lipschitz constant at most sqrt(||D||/e),
because ||grad exp(-y^T D y/2)||<=sqrt(||D||/e). Since ||D||<=2eps_bar/(1-eps_bar^2), its change under the coupled latent error is O(sqrt(eps_bar)delta_y). Evaluate the acceptance exponential to additive error rho by rational interval arithmetic; if u=y^T D y/2 >= log(2/rho), replace exp(-u) by zero, which adds at most rho/2 error. The output product-Poisson kernel changes in TV by at most sqrt(t/2) times the latent Euclidean error, by the exact Poisson Hellinger affinity and the classical Fuchs-van de Graaf bound. For dyadic y, the Poisson rates are rational. On ||y||^2<=T the total output mean is at most M_out=tT/2. Truncate the total count at Q=O(M_out+log(1/rho)) using a Poisson Chernoff bound, and use rational interval inversion for each rate with total CDF error O(rho). The one-attempt unnormalized accepted-output measure then differs from the ideal one by O(rho).

After normalization by alpha>=Lambda^(-1), the per-success output law has TV error O(rho Lambda)<=eps_out/C0. Cap the number of attempts at N=ceil(C2 Lambda log(2/xi)); the timeout probability is at most xi/2 after constants are enlarged. On timeout return a fixed count vector, adding at most xi/2 to TV. Thus the finite-bit output law is within eps_out+xi of the target, with worst-case time polynomial in

    L, d, numeric kappa, M_out, eps_out^(-1), log(xi^(-1)), Lambda,

where L includes the exact rational covariance, noise/loss parameters, and
certificates. In particular, this is polynomial in L when
(d+kappa)eps_bar=O(log L), numeric kappa and M_out=tT/2 are polynomially
bounded in L, eps_out^(-1) and log(xi^(-1)) are polynomially bounded, and the
certificate/input bit lengths are included in L. The stated runtime is
polynomial in these charged parameters; it is not asserted polynomial in the
binary length of an arbitrarily large herald count, because the gamma
implementation uses O(d+kappa) exponential variates. N81 has binary heralds
with kappa=h<=d=Theta(R), t=R^(-(a+1)), and polynomial M_out, so that
distinction causes no blowup there. The algorithm does not use log(1/p_H), and
the physical expected number of attempts to
obtain a herald remains 1/p_H if one insists on generating the optical event
instead of simulating its conditional law.

Covariance preprocessing is also charged. The theorem takes a rational
certificate (1-eps_bar)I<=A<=cI as supplied and validates both PSD differences
by exact rational LDL/fraction-free elimination in polynomial bit time. It
computes D=A^(-1)-c^(-1)I by rational elimination, again with polynomial bit
complexity in d and L. If no useful small eps_bar is supplied, the theorem gives
no small rejection cost. For N81, A is formed from the rational B, nu, and eta;
the analytic bound eps_bar=g/((1-g)nu) supplies the interval, and exact LDL
checks cI-A>=0. Computing A, c, and these checks costs poly(d,L). Finding a
tight certificate for an arbitrary covariance is preprocessing, not free
oracle access.

Scope and falsifiers
====================

This sampler allows arbitrary real phase correlations in the centered
positive-P covariance A; it uses no entrywise positivity of a Bargmann kernel.
It requires an isotropic domination certificate cI>=A and binary/arbitrary
finite PNR herald counts represented numerically. It does not cover displaced
Gaussian states as written (the proposal mean would need to match and dominate
the displacement), non-positive-P inputs, or nonisotropic proposal baselines.

Falsifiers are: a nonpositive D despite cI>=A; an error in the gamma posterior
or density ratio; a value of alpha below the Jensen bound; or a finite-bit
implementation whose coupled proposal/acceptance/count errors exceed the stated
budget. Independent review remains needed. The mathematical sampler is a
family-specific result; no generic complexity collapse or hardness conclusion
follows.

Primary-literature comparison (checked 8 October 2026)
======================================================

Rahimi-Keshari, Ralph, and Caves, "Sufficient Conditions for Efficient
Classical Simulation of Quantum Optics," arXiv:1511.06526 (2015; PRX 2016),
develops positive-quasiprobability sufficient conditions for sampling complete
output distributions and analyzes loss/noise thresholds in boson-sampling
models. It is the closest phase-space baseline, but the checked theorem/abstract
does not give an event-conditioned runtime bound independent of a specified
rare herald mass.

Quesada et al., "Simulating realistic non-Gaussian state preparation," arXiv:
1905.07011 (2019), explicitly compute the conditional output state's Fock
matrix elements and the herald probability using loop hafnians, then study
particular Fock/cat/cubic-phase heralding schemes with loss. The paper gives a
direct conditional-state calculation baseline, but not the present polynomial
conditional count sampler; its displayed matrix-element costs grow
exponentially with modes/photon occupations.

Quesada and Arrazola, "Exact simulation of Gaussian Boson Sampling in
polynomial space and exponential time," arXiv:1908.08068 (2019; PRR 2020),
explicitly samples sequentially from Gaussian marginal probabilities by
conditional-probability ratios, with time O(m N^3 2^N) after N detected
photons, and includes displaced/threshold variants. By direct inference from their chain rule, putting
the specified herald modes first, fixing their supplied counts, and sampling
remaining modes with p(H,o_<j,n)/p(H,o_<j) gives a conditional adaptation.
This handles a rare herald without necessarily paying 1/p_H, but remains
exponential in total detected photons
(including the fixed herald count and sampled output counts). It is a stronger
baseline than full-law rejection and narrows the present capability claim to
the near-isotropic positive-P regime where the cost becomes polynomial.

Oh et al., "Classical algorithm for simulating experimental Gaussian boson
sampling," arXiv:2306.03709 (2023; Nature Physics 2024), gives a tensor-network
approximation for lossy experimental GBS whose accuracy improves with runtime.
Its discussion identifies postselected GBS state preparation as a possible
application, but the algorithm analyzed is for full lossy output distributions
and it does not state a sampler for a specified exponentially rare PNR herald.

These comparisons are source-scope observations, not an exhaustive priority
search and not a novelty claim. The Quesada-Arrazola conditional-chain
adaptation should be independently checked for bit precision and truncation
under a fixed rare event. The candidate advance is the explicit near-isotropic
Gaussian-P posterior sampler with polynomial rather than exponential photon-
count dependence when (d+kappa)eps_bar=O(log L).
