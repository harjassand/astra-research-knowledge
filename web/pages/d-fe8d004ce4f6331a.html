## Selection

The recent implicit log-grid evolution method [Zima et al.2606.02930v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02930v1), submitted1June2026, does not resolve the previous MPS certificate's load-bearing gap. It explicitly conditions its near-logarithmic total cost on observed inexpensive compressed linear solves, and does not certify global trajectory errors. A-stability controls amplification, not normalized physical accuracy. An exact scalar construction below exposes the difference. This does not refute its reported Heisenberg/Kondo experiments or fixed-absolute-accuracy benefits.

A stronger foundational lead is [Pilatowsky-Cameo, Styliaris, Bakshi, Malz2609.35973v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35973v1), submitted28September2026. It proves exact bounded-block decompositions for all bounded-range1D Gibbs states at fixed positive temperature, accompanied by a constructive classical sampling algorithm. This is a structural result about thermal quantum entanglement, stronger than approximate compactness or an assumed evolution primitive. The main positive continuation is now [RATIONAL_CLUSTER_THEOREM.md](RATIONAL_CLUSTER_THEOREM.md): an explicit all-temperature block mixture for supplied homogeneous thermal cluster strips, with singly exponential temperature/width bound, a non-enumerating positive sampler, and a logarithmic strip-width necessity for nonzero single-copy localizable end-to-end entanglement. The local-depth obstruction below is a supporting interface clarification, not the principal new result. The full general source theorem has not passed an independent complete proof/sampler audit.

## Author results and scope

Input: H=sum_j h_j on a qubit ring, each term supported on r adjacent sites and ||h_j||<=1, fixed0<=beta<infinity. Theorem1: exact Gibbs mixture over pure product states on contiguous blocks, with block length and MPS bond bounded by

C_beta=exp(exp(c_r max(1,beta))), c_r=exp(16r),

independent of N. Theorem2 gives a poly(N,1/epsilon) classical sampler at fixed beta/r with trace-norm error epsilon. ## Independent general positive-cut absorption lemma

Let G_j,G'_j be strictly positive operators on disjoint elementary blocks of at most m qubits, j=0,...,L-1. Suppose G'_j factorizes across a chosen midpoint cut in its block and

G'_j <= G_j <= kappa G'_j, kappa>1.

Write Delta_j=G_j-G'_j. Then0<=Delta_j<=(1-1/kappa)G_j. Choose block0 and define

R=G'_0 tensor (tensor_(j>=1) G_j),

Q0=R+tensor_j Delta_j,

Qb=(tensor_(j<b)Delta_j) tensor G'_b tensor (tensor_(j>b)G_j), b=1,...,L-1.

The telescoping identity sum_b Qb=tensor_j G_j is exact. For b>=1,Qb has the chosen cut in blockb. To show Q0 has a positive separable decomposition across cut0, diagonalize

G_j^(-1/2)Delta_jG_j^(-1/2)=sum_t lambda_jt P_jt,

where0<=lambda_jt<=1-1/kappa. After whitening block0 by the product operator G'_0^(-1/2), each spectral label of the other blocks leaves

I+K_t, K_t=(product_(j>=1)lambda_jt)G'_0^(-1/2)Delta_0G'_0^(-1/2),

||K_t|| <=(kappa-1)(1-1/kappa)^(L-1).

If this is at most4^(-m), expand K_t in the4^m Pauli strings. Each coefficient's absolute value is at most||K_t||, so sum_P|alpha_P|<=1. Hence

I+K_t=(1-sum_P|alpha_P|)I+sum_P |alpha_P|(I+sign(alpha_P)P).

Every summand is positive and diagonal in a product eigenbasis of P. This is an explicit finite positive product decomposition; undoing the product whitening preserves separability. A sufficient block count is therefore

L=1+ceil(log[4^m(kappa-1)]/[-log(1-1/kappa)]),

with L>=1 if the numerator is negative. This establishes a positive cut somewhere in every superblock. It reconstructs the source's Eq19–21 mechanism; it is not asserted historically novel. Worst-case construction enumerates up to (2^m)^(L-1) spectral tuples and4^m Pauli coefficients per tuple: the constant can be exponential in mL. Local eigendecompositions cost O(L2^(3m)) exact-real arithmetic before coefficient/decomposition work. The full bit cost also depends on local conditioning/eigenvalue gaps or interval treatments. No small-cost conclusion follows from fixed m,L alone.

## Completely constructive nontrivial thermal toy

Take Bell projector P=(I+XX-YY+ZZ)/4 and G=I+3P. This is the unnormalized Gibbs operator for H=-J P at beta J=log4. Its normalized state G/7 is entangled: the partial transpose of G has minimum eigenvalue1-3/2=-1/2. A chain of L disjoint dimers has Gibbs operator G^tensorL, exact trace7^L, readily handled by deterministic contraction.

Set G'=I, Delta=3P. Then kappa=4. In Q0, diagonalizing other dimers yields a nonzero perturbation only when every other dimer is in the Bell eigenstate. Its coefficient is

k_eff=3(3/4)^(L-1).

For this family the generic Pauli ball is unnecessarily conservative. The exact identity

I+kP=(1-k/2)I+(k/4)[(I+XX)+(I-YY)+(I+ZZ)]

is a positive product decomposition whenever k<=2. Its necessity follows from partial transpose: the smallest eigenvalue of (I+kP)^Gamma is1-k/2. Thus this absorption step is valid exactly when k_eff<=2, first occurring at L3: k_eff=27/16, identity coefficient5/32. For L2,k_eff=9/4 and Q0 has a negative partial-transpose eigenvalue-1/2, so this designated absorption step fails. This does not prove that every possible different decomposition fails at L2.

Explicit global cut probabilities at L3 are223/343,84/343,36/343 for b0,1,2, from trace weights

tr Q0=4*7^(L-1)+3^L, tr Qb=3^b*4*7^(L-b-1).

All are exact rational probabilities. A direct sampler draws b, then independent Bell eigenstates for factors G (probabilities4/7,1/7,1/7,1/7), fixed Bell states for factors Delta, and product basis states for G'. For b0, split the other-dimer spectrum into the all-Bell configuration and its complement; the all-Bell branch uses the explicit three Pauli product bases above. The complement can be drawn by rejecting the all-Bell tuple, with expected attempts1/[1-(4/7)^(L-1)]<=49/33 for L>=3. Work/output O(L), O(L) random discrete choices; exact rational sampling uses expected O(L) fair bits for fixed alpha3. The comparator computes trace7^L and samples the ordinary product Gibbs mixture in O(L) directly, so no computational advantage is claimed. The purpose is an exact mechanism discriminator, not a benchmark or a general Gibbs implementation.

## A complete local-preparation cost barrier

Here is a distinct end-to-end theorem; it avoids any assumed MPS/evolution oracle.

Fix beta J=log3/2 and J1, so t=tanh(beta J)=1/2. For any proposed physical depth D, set ell=2D+1. Form a legal1D nearest-neighbor Ising Hamiltonian consisting of m disjoint segments, each with ell edges, separated by ell unused spins. Each occupied segment has Hamiltonian -sum_(i=0)^(ell-1)Z_iZ_(i+1), all term norms1. There are N=m(2ell+1)=m(4D+3) sites; unused trailing sites permit arbitrary larger N.

The two endpoints of each segment have Gibbs marginal

p(a,b)=[1+c ab]/4, c=t^ell=2^(-ell), a,b in{±1}.

Endpoint marginals from different segments are independent. All2m selected endpoints are pairwise separated by more than2D. Any nearest-neighbor depth-D CPTP circuit from independent site/ancilla states and independent local classical randomness has disjoint backward light cones for these endpoints. Therefore its joint endpoint output is a product of2m one-site density matrices. Local measurements/feedforward count as physical layers; pre-shared correlated randomness is excluded from this interface.

Dephase the output in Z. Trace norm contracts, and the output stays product. For one target pair, let u_a=sqrt(q_A(a)),v_b=sqrt(q_B(b)); both vectors have Euclidean norm1. The root fidelity is u^T B v, where

B=1/2 [[sqrt(1+c),sqrt(1-c)],[sqrt(1-c),sqrt(1+c)]].

Its largest singular value is[ sqrt(1+c)+sqrt(1-c)]/2, attained at unbiased product marginals. Hence the maximal **squared** fidelity with any product pair is

f(c)=[1+sqrt(1-c^2)]/2 <=1-c^2/4.

Tensorization over m pairs bounds the full endpoint root fidelity by f(c)^(m/2). The classical fidelity/variation inequality gives the full-chain trace-norm bound

**||rho_beta-sigma_D||_1 >=2[1-f(c)^(m/2)] >=2[1-exp(-m c^2/8)].**

The last inequality uses1-x<=exp(-x). For fixed trace-norm error epsilon<2, necessarily

m*2^(-4D-2) <= -8log(1-epsilon/2).

With m=floor[N/(4D+3)], a uniform fixed-temperature/fixed-error algorithm for all such bounded local chains requires

**D >=(1/4)log_2 N - O(log log N)**

for fixed epsilon; more precisely retain the explicit preceding inequality rather than use this shorthand when epsilon varies. Thus D=Omega(log N) at fixed epsilon. At epsilon0 exact preparation additionally requires overlapping endpoint light cones, so fixed D cannot prepare this family exactly once m>=1.

This is consistent with the September constant-quantum-depth theorem: its labels are sampled through **globally correlated classical preprocessing**. The classical computer can communicate distant spin labels, and these may then drive on-site quantum gates. Those operations fall outside the independent-randomness local-circuit interface. Constant quantum depth does not remove the spatial information-acquisition/communication cost. The ingredient is standard light-cone independence plus an elementary fidelity calculation; priority of this explicit bound is unverified.

A fully constructive comparator for this same family is especially strong. For each segment draw the root spin uniformly and independent edge signs b_i with P(b_i=+1)=3/4; output s_j=s_0 product_(i<j)b_i. This exactly generates the Gibbs law. Each edge needs two fair bits (choose-1 only for the11 outcome), each root one bit. Work/output/random bits O(N), sequential latency O(ell) for a segment or O(log ell) bounded-fan-in parallel prefix with arbitrary wiring. Quantum output preparation requires only local basis-state flips after these labels are delivered. Partition function is exactly2^N cosh(beta J)^(m ell), with logZ computed in O(N) arithmetic or from aggregate counts. Deterministic transfer, free finite-temperature treatment, stochastic Lanczos/Chebyshev and tensor sketches receive the same known H; the exact transfer control dominates the stochastic methods here.

## Why implicit logarithmic grids do not close the previous gap

The June method's exact scalar trapezoid multiplier is R(z)=(1-z/2)/(1+z/2). Consider diagonal H=diag(0,3/5,1), so its actual maximum eigenvalue is1, and initial state(a,sqrt(1-a^2),0), a=2^-100. Use panels[0,1],[1,2],[2,4],... with n power-of-two steps per panel. No populated excited-mode factor is zero. For large panel k,z_k=(3/5)2^k/n and

|R(z_k)|=1-4/(z_k+2).

The late tail sum of1/z_k is finite, so the infinite product of nonzero |R(z_k)|^n converges to a **strictly positive floor**. The exact excited mode exp(-3tau/5) tends to zero. Thus A-stability alone does not guarantee arbitrary projection accuracy as tau grows at fixed steps per panel. In particular, a floor small in absolute norm can still overwhelm the ground amplitude a and produce a wrong normalized state.

At60 panels,n8 the floor is about10^-23.39, much larger than a≈7.89e-31; the normalized excited amplitude remains essentially1, while the exact state is ground-like. n16 reduces the floor to about10^-46.79 and resolves this input. Linear solves are exact in this test: the failure is a time-step/accuracy interface issue before any tensor truncation. Source AppendixA explicitly offers a grid justification rather than a complete error theorem; source empirical states need not have this adverse overlap. L-stable solvers or error-adapted n can repair the example. It is not a universal exponential cost lower bound or a refutation of logarithmic stepping at fixed absolute tolerance. A certified residual test should reject the inaccurate trajectory; the test still has to be cheaply implemented on compressed states.

## Decision, downstream use and next falsifiable step

Checkpoint the earlier random-MPS partition-certificate lane: no cheap general compressed imaginary-time error acquisition has been established. Free/commuting and fixed-block subclasses have constructive solutions, but equally informed deterministic algorithms dominate those comparators.

Promote the September exact block-structure source as a foundational literature lead. Our contribution here is a reconstructed positive mechanism and a complete communication/depth distinction, not a new theorem about all interacting Gibbs states. It can guide research into thermal entanglement length, localizable entanglement and what classical coordination buys quantum preparation. In particular, low quantum depth can coexist with extensive classical correlations and nonlocal preprocessing.
