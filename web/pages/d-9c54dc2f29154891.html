LEAF 09 — MASK ENTROPY AND INCIDENCE COUNTEREXAMPLE
Date: 2026-10-08

Status: scoped combinatorial partition and counterexample. No arbitrary-mask algorithm or general circuit lower bound.

For a supplied mask W, group rows by their exact neighborhood S. If R_S is the set of rows with neighborhood S, then W is the disjoint union of complete rectangles R_S x S and sum_S |R_S||S|=|W|. Given a uniform exact D-square multiplication circuit of arithmetic size D^(omega+o(1)), the groups with |R_S|,|S|>=D cost O(|R_S||S|D^(omega-2+o(1))) each by D-square tiling. Thin groups cost O(|R_S||S|D) by direct dot products. For omega=9/4, the total arithmetic bound is

    O(m_good D^(1/4+o(1)) + m_thin D).

Thus the strategy is subquadratic when m_thin<=N^(7/4-delta), since the thick part is at most N^(31/16+o(1)). One exact favorable family is W=[N]xS with |S|=N^(7/8), giving N^(31/16+o(1)) arithmetic operations. This is conditional: the N12 card says the 9/4 exponent alone does not supply a uniform integer-bit circuit or coefficient-height ledger. The report fully charges mask/input/output representation and keeps circuit-generation and coefficient costs explicit.

Conversely, a uniformly random exact m-edge mask, m=floor(N^(15/8)), has entropy

    log binom(N^2,m)=(1/8+o(1))m log N,

while its edge-list representation costs only O(m log N)=o(N^2) bits. With high probability every row and column degree is Theta(N^(7/8)) and all row and column neighborhoods are distinct. Therefore the exact-neighborhood partition consists of singleton rows (and symmetrically singleton columns), and this strategy costs mD=N^(17/8+o(1)). It defeats identical-neighborhood reuse only; general mixed arithmetic remains open.
