# Enzyme-constrained FBA: transient capacity certificate and scope audit

**Verdict.** The broad gap between steady-state FBA and enzyme-expression transients is already addressed by dynamic enzyme-cost FBA (deFBA) and regulatory deFBA. The defensible result here is a sharp, finite-horizon exposure bound for one enzyme-limited reaction with measurable synthesis, dilution, turnover, and thermodynamic inputs. It makes an exact counterexample to treating an eFBA/TFA optimum as an immediately available transient flux. The inequality is a direct variation-of-constants argument and is not claimed as a novel theorem or field-level advance.

## Exact interface

Fix an enzyme-catalysed forward reaction (j). All enzyme and flux quantities use one declared basis, such as mol per gram dry cell weight and mol per gram dry cell weight per hour. Its active enzyme abundance (E(t)) is absolutely continuous and obeys

\[
\dot E(t)=p(t)-\gamma(t)E(t),\qquad E(0)=E_0\ge 0,
\]

where (p(t)\) is the specific synthesis flux and \(\gamma(t)=\mu(t)+\delta(t)\) combines biomass dilution with degradation/inactivation. Inputs are measurable on ([0,T]). The measured uncertainty interface supplies nonnegative, locally integrable envelopes

\[
0\le p(t)\le \bar p(t),\qquad \gamma(t)\ge\underline\gamma(t)\ge0,
\]

and a turnover upper bound \(k_{cat}(t)\le\bar k(t)\). If a total proteome budget is used, it must include the enzyme molecular-mass conversion and the available budget; it can add an individual cap \(E(t)\le E_{max}(t)\). Any shared translation budget must be converted into a valid per-enzyme synthesis envelope \(\bar p\), or retained as a coupled allocation constraint in a stronger optimization.

For an internal metabolic model, the static comparator has the same stoichiometric matrix, uptake/secretion bounds, active-enzyme mapping, and thermodynamic activity intervals. It imposes the standard steady-state constraints \(S_{int}v=0\), an enzyme capacity \(v_j\le k_{cat}E_j\), proteome allocation, and TFA directionality. In the one-reaction example below, upstream supply and downstream demand have slack and the reaction is the objective, so the steady-state enzyme-constrained/TFA ceiling is exactly \(V_{FBA}=k_{cat}E_{max}\) (or (q k_{cat}E_{max}) if a fixed substrate-saturation factor (q\) is included in both models).

Thermodynamic data enter through measured activity intervals (a_i(t)\in[a_i^-(t),a_i^+(t)]\), a standard transformed Gibbs interval \(\Delta G_j^{\circ\prime}\in[g_j^-,g_j^+]\), temperature (T_c), pH, and ionic-strength convention. For stoichiometric coefficients \(\nu_{ij}\), compute conservative Gibbs bounds by maximizing/minimizing

\[
\Delta G_j=\Delta G_j^{\circ\prime}+RT_c\sum_i\nu_{ij}\ln a_i.
\]

A measured interval wholly below (-g<0) certifies that forward direction under the chosen TFA convention. An interval wholly above (+g) rules it out. If the interval crosses the zero margin, thermodynamic direction is unresolved and the safe capacity bound keeps the direction available. Thermodynamic feasibility does not imply saturation or a kinetic lower bound.

## Finite-horizon enzyme-exposure bound

Let

\[
\Gamma(t)=\int_0^t\underline\gamma(r)\,dr,\qquad
\bar E(t)=E_0e^{-\Gamma(t)}+\int_0^t\bar p(s)e^{-[\Gamma(t)-\Gamma(s)]}\,ds.
\]

If the reaction is represented as forward-only with a positive thermodynamic driving-force margin, let \(\chi(t)=0\) when the measured Gibbs interval certifies the forward direction is forbidden, and \(\chi(t)=1\) otherwise. Then every admissible trajectory satisfies

\[
E(t)\le\bar E(t),\qquad
0\le v_j(t)\le\chi(t)\bar k(t)\bar E(t),\qquad
\int_0^T v_j(t)\,dt\le\int_0^T\chi(t)\bar k(t)\bar E(t)\,dt.
\]

If an enzyme upper bound is also measured, replace \(\bar E(t)\) by \(\min\{\bar E(t),E_{max}(t)\}\). The result is a necessary flux certificate; it does not assert that the network can realize the bound or that this reaction controls growth.

**Proof.** The scalar comparison equation \(\dot{\bar E}=\bar p-\underline\gamma\bar E\), \(\bar E(0)=E_0\), has the displayed variation-of-constants solution. For (d=E-\bar E\),

\[
\dot d=(p-\bar p)-(\gamma-\underline\gamma)E-\underline\gamma d\le-\underline\gamma d,
\]

using (E\ge0), so (d(t)\le0). The enzyme-capacity inequality (v_j\le k_{cat}E\), the conservative thermodynamic direction gate, and integration give the flux and exposure bounds. Equality is possible when synthesis and turnover attain their supplied envelopes and the chosen direction remains thermodynamically allowed.

## Sharp static-versus-transient counterexample

Take one thermodynamically downhill enzyme-limited conversion, with fixed substrate availability (or a fixed saturation factor (q\in(0,1])), adequate upstream supply, and adequate downstream sink. Let (\gamma>0) be constant, start from (E_0=0), constrain synthesis by (p\le\gamma E_{max}), and set the reaction's effective saturated turnover to (k=qk_{cat}). The static eFBA/TFA optimum is

\[
V_{FBA}=kE_{max}.
\]

The exact maximal dynamic enzyme state is (E(t)=E_{max}(1-e^{-\gamma t})), attained by constant maximal synthesis. Therefore

\[
v_{max}(t)=V_{FBA}(1-e^{-\gamma t})<V_{FBA}\quad(t<\infty),
\]

and the maximal horizon-average flux is

\[
\frac1T\int_0^T v_{max}(t)\,dt
=V_{FBA}\left[1-\frac{1-e^{-\gamma T}}{\gamma T}\right].
\]

This is sharp within the stated interface: constant maximal synthesis, fixed saturation, and fixed negative Gibbs energy attain it. For example, at (T=0.5/\gamma), the average is only (0.2131\,V_{FBA}), although both models have the same stoichiometry, medium, turnover bound, proteome cap, and favorable thermodynamic direction. The static state has selected an enzyme allocation that is absent initially.

With a reserve (E_0\in[0,E_{max}]), the normalized maximum average becomes

\[
1+\left(\frac{E_0}{E_{max}}-1\right)\frac{1-e^{-\gamma T}}{\gamma T}.
\]

Thus identical steady-state eFBA optima can correspond to different transient productivity solely because the initial enzyme reserve differs. This is a model-level counterexample, not a claim that any particular cell follows this one-enzyme kinetic law.

## A discriminating experiment

Use an isogenic *E. coli* strain in which native **lacZ** is deleted, **lacY** is expressed constitutively, and a single-copy inducible **lacZ** supplies the sole lactose-hydrolysis activity. Precondition two arms to create low and high active LacZ reserves while matching total protein mass with an inert-protein replacement; at (t=0), apply the same (^{13}\mathrm C\)-lactose pulse and induction protocol in a controlled microfluidic/turbidostat setup. This provides the same steady-state reaction ceiling and environment but distinct measured (E_0\).

Measure active LacZ abundance by absolute targeted proteomics plus an activity/active-fraction assay; (p(t)\) by pulse-label incorporation; cell-specific growth/dilution (\mu(t)\) and degradation (\delta(t)\); lactose and hydrolysis-product activities by rapid-quench LC-MS; and lactose-to-product flux by time-resolved (^{13}\mathrm C\) MFA. Measure temperature, pH, and ionic strength and propagate errors into Gibbs-energy intervals. Purified/lysate turnover measurements provide an upper confidence bound for (k_{cat}\). The thermo gate must be declared unresolved if the Gibbs interval crosses the adopted direction margin.

Pre-register the envelope calculation from (E_0,\bar p,\underline\gamma,\bar k\), with uncertainty bounds chosen conservatively. A decisive falsifier is a lower confidence bound on the measured interval flux \(\int_0^T v_j\,dt\) above the certificate's upper bound, or a forward flux during a window where the lower Gibbs bound is strictly positive under the declared TFA convention. In the constant-envelope regime (\bar p=\gamma E_{max}\), the low-reserve arm additionally predicts the explicit average-flux curve above; the high-reserve arm begins at the static ceiling. The experiment must also include a constitutive-LacZ control, no-inducer control, and the same-medium/pre-expression arm to distinguish enzyme lag from transport, isotope-pool, or global growth effects.

## Strongest matched controls and prior-art boundary

The static comparator is enzyme-constrained FBA with TFA using identical stoichiometry, uptake/secretion limits, (k_{cat}\) uncertainty, proteome budget, and metabolite-activity intervals. It answers a steady-state capacity question. The strongest dynamic comparator is deFBA or r-deFBA with the same initial protein composition, enzyme synthesis costs, dilution/degradation, regulation, extracellular balances, and optimization horizon; those methods already represent the broad transient gap. A matched kinetic ODE model with the same measured (K_M,k_{cat},E_0,p(t),\mu(t)), and thermodynamic ranges is the mechanistic control. Plain dFBA that reoptimizes fluxes instantaneously is a weaker comparator because it omits enzyme history.

Primary literature establishes the boundary. Waldherr, Oyarzún, and Bockmayr introduced deFBA for time-varying metabolic adaptations and enzyme production costs in 2015 ([DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2014.10.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2014.10.035)). Liu and Bockmayr's 2020 r-deFBA includes continuous dynamics of fluxes, external substrates, enzymes, and regulatory proteins in a hybrid dynamic-optimization framework ([DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2020.110317](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2020.110317)). Wu et al. measured enzyme recovery after amino-acid downshift in *E. coli*; onset times varied with pre-shift enzyme reserves, and the model captured enzyme recovery while initially missing the full growth lag ([Nature Microbiology, DOI 10.1038/s41564-022-01310-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-022-01310-w)). Seeger, Pinheiro, and Lässig's August 2026 study combines enzyme kinetics with proteomics/metabolomics for balanced-growth resource allocation and identifies pathway switching and near-equilibrium kinetics as open extensions ([PLOS Computational Biology, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014642](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014642)). Henry, Broadbelt, and Hatzimanikatis's TMFA supplies the primary thermodynamic-flux formulation ([Biophysical Journal, DOI 10.1529/biophysj.106.093138](https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.106.093138)).

This leaves the certificate as a compact audit lemma and an experiment interface, not as a new dynamic FBA framework. A stronger research advance would need to prove a network-level objective bound that improves on deFBA/r-deFBA under the same enzyme-state and thermodynamic data, or show a reproducible held-out prediction gain against those matched controls. Neither is established here.
