STRONGEST CLAIM

For the fixed-parameter critical mean-field spin family

  rho_(N,A,b) = exp[2 J^2/N + Q_A(J)/N^(3/2) + b.J/N^(3/4)] / Z,
  J = (1/2) sum_(i=1)^N sigma^(i),
  Q_A(J) = sum_(a,c) A_ac (J_a J_c + J_c J_a)/2,

assume real symmetric A,b with public fixed bounds ||A||<=M and |b|<=B.
The saved critical-spin channel result gives parameter-independent channels in
both directions between this whole N-qubit state and ONE classical observation
X in R^3 with density

  p_(A,b)(x) = Z_(A,b)^(-1) exp[-4|x|^4/3 + x^T A x + b.x],

with each direction's uniform trace-distance/TV error at most
delta_N = C_(M,B) N^(-1/2). The sharper channel uses a total-spin/coherent-state
measurement, radial jitter, coherent-state decoding, and multiplicity reset.
It is different from the parameter-independent product-state decoder whose
proved recovery error is O(N^(-1/4)); replacing one rate by the other is invalid.

Consequence: for R independent preparations at the same unknown (A,b), the
quantum experiment and R i.i.d. quartic observations have Le Cam distance at
most R delta_N. In particular, for every bounded loss in [0,1], their optimal
Bayes risks and minimax risks differ by at most R delta_N. Therefore when
R=R_N=o(sqrt(N)), no collective quantum measurement or quantum decision rule
on those R Gibbs blocks improves any bounded-loss decision risk by a nonvanishing
amount over the classical R-sample experiment. This is a finite-resolution
decision statement, not a claim that the finite-N quantum Fisher information
equals the classical Fisher information.

DERIVATION

Write T_N for the fixed quantum-to-classical channel and S_N for the fixed
classical-to-quantum channel. The inherited theorem states, uniformly in the
compact parameter set,
  TV(T_N(rho_(N,A,b)), p_(A,b)) <= delta_N,
  T(S_N(p_(A,b)), rho_(N,A,b)) <= delta_N.
Tensoring R copies and telescoping the product measures/density matrices gives
both errors <= R delta_N. For a quantum POVM decision rule M, the classical
kernel K(a|x)=Tr[M_a S_N(dx)] has outcome law within TV R delta_N of M applied
to rho_(N,A,b)^(tensor R), by trace-distance contraction. Conversely, a
classical rule K can be implemented on the quantum input by first applying
T_N^(tensor R) and then K; its outcome law is within TV R delta_N. For loss
0<=ell(theta,a)<=1, changing the outcome law by TV q changes expected loss by
at most q. Taking infima and suprema proves the Bayes/minimax risk bound.
This is exactly the operational content of the two channel simulations; no
additional quantum statistical comparison theorem is being assumed.

For field metrology in the limiting experiment, fix A and set
  psi_A(b)=log int_R3 exp[-4|x|^4/3+x^T A x+b.x] dx.
Quartic tails make psi analytic on bounded b-sets. The score is
  grad_b log p_(A,b)(x) = x - E_(A,b) X,
so its classical Fisher matrix is
  I_b(A,b) = Hess_b psi_A(b) = Cov_(A,b)(X).
It is finite and positive definite uniformly on the stated compact parameter
set. To see a quantitative bound, let
  Zplus = int exp[-4|x|^4/3 + M|x|^2 + B|x|] dx,
  cball = exp[-4/3-M-B]/Zplus.
Then p_(A,b)(x)>=cball on the unit ball. For any unit u,
  u^T I_b u = Var(u.X)
    = inf_t E[(u.X-t)^2]
    >= cball inf_t int_(|x|<=1) (u.x-t)^2 dx
    = 4 pi cball/15 > 0.
For an upper bound, put V1=4pi/3. Since
  Z_(A,b)>=V1 exp[-4/3-M-B],
we have
  lambda_max(I_b) <= E|X|^2
    <= exp[4/3+M+B]/V1 * int |x|^2
           exp[-4|x|^4/3+M|x|^2+B|x|] dx < infinity.

At A=b=0 isotropy gives an exact closed form. With a=4/3,
  E|X|^2 = a^(-1/2) Gamma(5/4)/Gamma(3/4),
  I_b(0,0) = (sqrt(3)/6) Gamma(5/4)/Gamma(3/4) I_3
            = 0.213523984564... I_3.
The gamma identity follows from
  int_0^infty r^q exp(-a r^4) dr
    = (1/4) a^(-(q+1)/4) Gamma((q+1)/4).

The parameter b multiplies J/N^(3/4). If h=b/N^(3/4) denotes the physical
coefficient multiplying J, then the classical Fisher matrix for h is
N^(3/2) I_b. For R classical samples, standard regular exponential-family
theory, as R tends to infinity with b in the interior, gives asymptotic
covariance I_b^(-1)/R for an efficient estimator of b, and hence
N^(-3/2) I_b^(-1)/R for h. The two-way channel theorem transfers bounded-loss
decision performance at this critical field scale to the N-spin experiment
whenever R=o(sqrt(N)). It does not by itself identify the exact
quantum Fisher matrix, and it gives no conclusion for R of order sqrt(N) or
larger because the accumulated comparison error need not vanish.

SCOPE, COSTS, AND LIMITS

- Parameters A,b and their fixed bounds are supplied to the family definition;
  the channels do not receive A,b. The statement covers the complete compact
  family, including unknown anisotropy as nuisance, while the displayed Fisher
  matrix is conditional on known A.
- The field scale is h=b/N^(3/4). A fixed nonzero h makes b grow with N and
  leaves the theorem's fixed-B window; no such transfer is claimed.
- The forward channel is an ideal collective spin-sector/coherent-state POVM
  with an added public radial randomization. Its circuit, Schur transform,
  readout, latency, and noise costs are not certified here. The risk result is
  information-theoretic.
- The sharper O(N^(-1/2)) two-way comparison comes from the prior coherent/
  singlet-sector construction. The simpler fully separable decoder is only
  O(N^(-1/4)). The known-parameter O(N^(-3/2)) distance to the separable cone
  is a third, distinct statement.
- The separately audited finite-bit known-parameter product preparation is a
  mathematical construction, not executed certified code or hardware. This
  metrology corollary neither implements it nor lowers its bit costs.
- No local-lattice, finite-range, dynamic sensing, external-reference recovery,
  purification, non-equilibrium protocol, or growing-M/B conclusion follows.
- Existing critical-metrology papers study different observables/models and
  protocols. This does not establish novelty; the 1991 Fannes--Kossakowski--
  Verbeure critical-fluctuation paper and other classical/coherent Gibbs
  ancestors remain priority-critical.
