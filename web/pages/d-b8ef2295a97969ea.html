# Cancer evolution and treatment resistance: first-wave result

## Verdict

The first wave reconstructed an exact branching-process mechanism showing why equal bulk/net response does not determine lineage-establishment risk. In a two-type model, the same resistant-compartment mean trajectory can imply opposite local effects of a collateral pulse because birth/death turnover changes the number and correlation of candidate descendants. This is a model result, not a new cancer result: time-inhomogeneous multitype branching, phenotype switching, and stochastic treatment scheduling already have direct prior art. No biological data were fitted, and no treatment effect was tested.

The strongest empirical anchor is the V299L intermediate in BCR–ABL1-positive leukemia. Zhao et al. reported a collateral sensitivity to foretinib/vandetanib and reduced tumor burden in mice, but did not estimate lineage birth, death, switching, or mutation rates and did not establish clone extinction ([Zhao et al., Cell 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5152932/)). The link from a mutation-specific vulnerability to a calibrated extinction decision therefore remains untested.

## Exact model mechanism

Let R cells divide into two R cells at rate \(b_R\), die at rate \(d_R\), and switch one-for-one to E at rate \(\nu\) during a pulse. E cells have pulse birth/death rates \(b_E,d_E\). At pulse end, a remaining R or E founder has maintenance-treatment establishment probability \(q_R\) or \(q_E\). Assume constant rates during each phase, independent branching lineages, and no spatial or density-dependent interactions.

If \(p_i(T)\) is the probability that one founder of type i at pulse start produces no lineage that establishes under the continuation treatment, then \(p_i(0)=1-q_i\) and

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_R'&=b_R(p_R^2-p_R)+d_R(1-p_R)+\nu(p_E-p_R),\\
p_E'&=b_E(p_E^2-p_E)+d_E(1-p_E).
\end{aligned}
$$

For \(n_R,n_E\) independent founders, the no-establishment probability is \(p_R(T)^{n_R}p_E(T)^{n_E}\). This exact PGF composition is established branching-process machinery.

At zero pulse duration,

$$
p_R'(0)=q_R[(d_R-b_R)+b_Rq_R]-\nu(q_E-q_R).
$$

Thus a short pulse initially decreases establishment risk exactly when \(p_R'(0)>0\). For \(q_R=.2\), \(q_E=.8\), and \(\nu=.005\) per day, the two pulse models below have equal \(b_R-d_R=-.01\) per day and the same R-compartment mean trajectory \(E[R_t]=R_0e^{(b_R-d_R-\nu)t}=R_0e^{-.015t}\), but opposite risk derivatives:

| Pulse model | \(b_R\) | \(d_R\) | \(p_R'(0)\) | Initial risk derivative \(-p_R'(0)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low turnover | .01 | .02 | −.0006/day | +.0006/day |
| High turnover | .10 | .11 | +.003/day | −.003/day |

The R mean is identical in both worlds, but the low-turnover world predicts that the pulse initially raises risk and the high-turnover world predicts that it lowers risk. Event-resolved division/death and switching data, plus maintenance establishment assays, separate these models; a bulk net-growth curve cannot.

**Correction to the first-wave note:** the earlier file mechanism_attack.md printed \(E[R_t]=R_0e^{-.01t}\). That omitted the R-to-E loss. The correct exponent is \(b_R-d_R-\nu=-.015\) per day for both rows. The sign-flip conclusion is unchanged.

## Boundaries and practical test

A safe first-moment bound can combine residual founders with newly generated escape founders:

$$
\Pr(\text{relapse})\le \bar q_R\,E[R_T]
 +\int_0^T \bar r_E(s;T)\,\nu(s)E[R_s]\,ds
 +\int_0^T \bar r_E(s;T)I_E(s)\,ds.
$$

Here \(\bar r_E(s;T)\) bounds the probability that an E founder created at pulse time s establishes after the remainder of the pulse and maintenance. Using only its post-pulse \(q_E\) is valid only if the founder is unaffected by the remaining pulse. Otherwise the bound must use a time-to-go PGF calculation or the conservative upper bound one. A source-rate floor is a floor of this particular upper bound only when pulse-time source success is duration-independent; it is not automatically a lower bound on nature’s relapse risk.

The bound needs absolute viable clone counts, rate/source confidence limits, establishment bounds, and valid sample coverage. If any is absent, the answer is UNKNOWN. Even if a tissue or plasma sample detects no clone, an unsampled zero-shedding refuge can contain an arbitrary hidden population while leaving all observed data unchanged. A whole-tumor risk statement therefore needs a quantified spatial inclusion and detection model.

A discriminating preclinical test would use isogenic WT, V299L, and compound-mutant BCR–ABL1 lineages with neutral barcodes. Training replicates would estimate division, death, switching, mutation, viable founder counts, and post-pulse establishment; an action and duration would be frozen before held-out barcode-resolved regrowth. Controls should include continued parent TKI, the best mutation-specific TKI, a fixed pulse, and an exposure-matched combination. A held-out relapse frequency above the frozen upper-risk envelope, rates outside the training region, or an untracked escape genotype falsifies the model interface. Required resources include independent cultures, lineage imaging, genotype/state-call calibration, barcoding/sequencing, washout/rechallenge follow-up, and assay turnaround. None of this experiment was performed.

## Prior art and evidence status

- **Primary biological observation:** mutation-specific collateral sensitivity and mouse tumor-burden/survival response for BCR–ABL1 V299L, Zhao et al. ([2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5152932/)); it does not establish extinction or provide the rates above.
- **Schedule/branching prior art:** nonhomogeneous multitype birth–death models under continuous, pulsed, and general time-varying schedules ([Foo & Michor 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/); [Foo & Michor 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2009.11.022)).
- **Switching/extinction prior art:** reversible cancer phenotype switching with branching extinction thresholds ([Gunnarsson et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/)); barcode-based phenotype-transition inference is also recent prior art ([Whiting et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7)).
- **Data limitations:** total tumor volume can leave birth/death parameters structurally unidentifiable ([Greene et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2020.00501)); incomplete lineage trees can bias fitness estimation if dead branches are omitted ([Genthon et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXLife.1.013014)).

The equations, local derivative, and counterexample are internally derived conditional model results. The cited empirical findings are source-reported. Novelty, clinically useful parameter identifiability, transport from culture to tumors, and benefit against an equally informed schedule baseline remain UNKNOWN. No patient advice, biological fit, experiment, or clinical efficacy claim is made.
