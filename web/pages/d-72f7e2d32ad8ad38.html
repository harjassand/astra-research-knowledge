INDEPENDENT AUDIT: THE SU(2) NEAR-FULL-RANK LOG PROFILE
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt, §4, and
       spectral_geometry/cycle2/near_full/constructive_uniform_upper.txt,
       p=1. No edits to either source file.

CONCLUSION

Write d=N+1 and let k=d-Q be the rank deficiency, 1<=k<d. For the
spin-N/2 SU(2) conjugation Casimir normalized by Tr_fund(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2,
the lower bound in ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt is valid for every
finite N>=1 and 1<=k<d:

  Lambda_{N,2}(d-k) >= 1/[7/2+log(d/k)].                         (A)

The p=1 upper theorem is also valid on its stated finite range
N>=16 and 1<=k<=floor(d/32), with the stated (deliberately loose) constant:

  Lambda_{N,1}(k) <= 12/log(e d/k).                            (B)

Here Lambda_{N,1}(k) in the upper file denotes deficiency k, so its rank
budget is d-k; the lower file writes this same profile as
Lambda_{N,2}(d-k). For every sequence 1<=k=o(d), both bounds apply for all
sufficiently large N, and an exact resistance calculation sharpens the
upper construction to leading constant one. Therefore

  Lambda_{N,2}(d-k)=(1+o(1))/log(d/k),       k=o(d).             (C)

The finite lower is uniform over all k<d. The quoted finite upper is only
uniform on k<=floor(d/32); the asymptotic range k=o(d) is eventually inside
that domain. Natural logarithms are used throughout.

1. AUDIT OF THE FINITE S=1 GREEN CUTOFF

For Sym^N(C^2), d=N+1, the nontrivial conjugation sectors have

  lambda_j=j(j+1),   b_j=2j+1,   1<=j<=N,   lambda_1=2.

For x=d/k>1, the cutoff prescribed by §4 is

  K=min(N,floor(sqrt(x))).

The exact low-frequency sum is

  S_K=sum_{j=1}^K (2j+1)/(j(j+1))
     =H_K+H_{K+1}-1,                                           (1)

because (2j+1)/(j(j+1))=1/j+1/(j+1). If K<N, the tail in the Green
bound is at most k/(K+1)^2. Thus

  B_K(k) <= (k^2/d)[S_K+x/(K+1)^2].                            (2)

This implies the stated uniform constant, not just an asymptotic estimate.
Indeed, when K<N, K^2<=x<(K+1)^2. The function
log x-x/(K+1)^2 is increasing on this interval, so the worst comparison
with 3+log x is at x=K^2. Using H_q<=1+log q there, the remaining margin
is at least

  2-log(1+1/K)-K^2/(K+1)^2 > 0.

When K=N, x>=N^2 and the tail is zero; H_N+H_{N+1}-1<=3+log x
directly from the same harmonic bound. Consequently

  B(k)<= (k^2/d)[3+log(d/k)]                                  (3)

for every finite N>=1 and 1<=k<d. Substitution into the exact converse
of ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt, §2, adds 1/lambda_1=1/2 to the
denominator and gives (A). No hidden condition K<N is needed; when K=N,
lambda_{N+1}=infinity and the tail is zero.

The additive 7/2 is sharp only as a bookkeeping constant for this cutoff
inequality; it is not claimed to be an optimal finite-dimensional
constant. In the near-full regime log(d/k)->infinity, (A) is
(1-o(1))/log(d/k).

2. EXACT P=1 RESISTANCE AND FINITE UPPER CHECK

In the upper file p=1, the occupation layers are singletons, V_R=R+1,
and the edge conductance from s to s+1 is

  w_s=(N-s)(s+1).

Thus the least R with V_R>=k is exactly R=k-1. With M=floor(N/2), the
resistance used by the taper has the exact harmonic-number value

  R_{k-1,M}=sum_{j=k-1}^{M-1}1/[(N-j)(j+1)]
   ={H_{N-k+1}-H_{N-M}+H_M-H_{k-1}}/d.                         (4)

This follows from
1/[(N-j)(j+1)]=(1/d)[1/(N-j)+1/(j+1)]. The resistance-linear profile
has exact unnormalized energy 1/R_{k-1,M}. Since X=I-A is zero exactly
on layers 0,...,k-1 and strictly positive on all later layers, its rank
is exactly d-k; there is no rank-rounding loss for p=1.

For clarity, its exact squared norm is

  S_{N,k}=||X||_HS^2
    =d-M+sum_{s=k}^{M-1}(1-a_s)^2,
  a_s={H_{N-s}-H_{N-M}+H_M-H_s\over
        H_{N-k+1}-H_{N-M}+H_M-H_{k-1}}.                        (5)

The finite theorem uses only S_{N,k}>=eta_1 d=d/4. In fact,
S_{N,k}>=d-M>=d/2 for every N, since the layers s>=M already have
X_s=1. The published constant 12 remains valid with its looser d/4 bound.

Check the explicit finite domain in the upper theorem:

  N>=16,   1<=k<=floor(d/32),   M=floor(N/2).

Whenever this range is nonempty, N/k>=32N/(N+1)>30. Also R=k-1<M.
Since w_j<=N(j+1),

  R_{k-1,M} >= (1/N)sum_{q=k}^M 1/q
             >= (1/N)log((M+1)/k)
             >= log(N/(2k))/N
             >= log(N/k)/(2N).                                (6)

The last step only needs N/k>=4. Combining (6) with S_{N,k}>=d/4 gives

  Lambda_{N,1}(k) <= 8N/[d log(N/k)].                          (7)

To get the stated 12/log(e d/k), put L=log(N/k) and
delta=log(d/N). Since N/k>30 and delta<=log(17/16),
L>=2+2delta. Hence
8N/(dL)<=8/L<=12/(1+L+delta)=12/log(e d/k), proving (B).
The inequality is valid uniformly on the theorem's finite domain. That
domain is empty when floor(d/32)=0; this is vacuous, not a failure.
No upper claim for all finite 1<=k<d follows from this particular theorem.

3. LEADING CONSTANT FOR k=o(d)

The loose norm and resistance estimates in (6)-(7) lose the leading
constant. Equations (4)-(5) recover it. If k=o(N), then

  H_{N-k+1}-H_{N-M}=log 2+O(k/N+1/N),
  H_M-H_{k-1}=log(N/(2k))+O(1),

where the O(1) is uniform for integer k>=1 (for fixed k it includes the
finite harmonic-number offset). Therefore

  R_{k-1,M}={log(d/k)+O(1)\over d}
           =(1+o(1)){log(d/k)\over d}.                        (8)

Also S_{N,k}/d->1. To see this, fix any delta in (0,1/2). For
delta N<=s<M, the numerator resistance sum defining a_s is O_delta(1/N),
whereas the denominator (4) is asymptotic to log(N/k)/N. Hence a_s=o(1)
uniformly on those layers, so X_s=1-o(1) there. Layers s>=M already have
X_s=1. Thus liminf S_{N,k}/d>=1-delta; let delta decrease to zero and use
S_{N,k}<=d. From E(X)=1/R_{k-1,M} and (8),

  E(X)/||X||_HS^2=(1+o(1))/log(d/k).                            (9)

The lower (A) and the construction (9) match. This proves (C), including
fixed k such as k=1, and also sequences k growing at any rate o(d).
The finite upper constant 12 is not the asymptotic leading constant.

4. PRECISE OVERLAP WITH THE S>1 FORMULAS

The parameter s in ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt is m-1, while p in
constructive_uniform_upper.txt is the same integer m-1. For every fixed
s>=2, the root-codimension result gives a lower bound of order

  (k/d)^(1-1/s),

and the constructive upper, for N>=max(4s,16) and
1<=k<=floor(32^(-s)d), gives the same power with an explicit constant U_s.
Thus the two results overlap in order for fixed s>=2 throughout k=o(d):
the upper's fixed-density cutoff is eventually satisfied, while the lower
is valid at every 1<=k<d. Their explicit leading constants do not match,
and no exact constant is asserted for s>=2.

The s=1 result is the marginal logarithmic case of the same Green-cutoff
mechanism. If the low-band count scales as D(t)~t^s, the inverse-Casimir
integral below cutoff t_*~rho^(-1/s) has scale

  rho integral_1^{t_*} t^(s-2) dt.

For s=1 this is rho log(1/rho); after the kernel/codimension conversion
the energy is 1/log(1/rho). For s>1 it is of order rho^(1/s), giving
energy rho^(1-1/s), matched in order by the s-dimensional radial taper.
The exact p=1 resistance is logarithmic; the p>=2 taper has a power-law
resistance. This is the precise overlap: a common capacity mechanism and
matching exponents/orders, with an exact leading constant established here
only at s=1.

The finite lower cutoff and the stated p=1 finite upper constants check out.
The p=1 upper is rank-exact. The sharp asymptotic constant one follows from
the exact resistance and norm formulas plus the finite lower. No external
