The strongest unconditional result here is a sharp obstruction and repair

the primitive already has pre-release prior art. The extension does not

## Question, legal outputs, and dependencies

The proposed experimental-design interface is: given rational feasible
marginals `y`, rational feature contributions, and explicit resource
constraints, construct a rational law on feasible Boolean allocations `b`
with `E b = y` and a hard feature bound on every positive-weight outcome.
Computational costs include original feasibility/optimization oracles,
source signing calls, all queried cost encodings, exact hull extraction,
and the rational output. A count or an almost-uniform sample is a different
output. Physical calibration of the supplied feature matrices is also a
separate acquisition cost.

Imported D reports an output `l_infinity` discrepancy bound `O(k(P))`
for feature matrices with Euclidean column norms at most one, where
`k(P)` is the support size of the largest actual edge of the Boolean
polytope. Its output norm is not a Euclidean or operator-norm promise.
It also reports a favorable-cost routine and exact mean
extraction. D's full spectral/finite-bit construction is not independently
validated here and is not used to prove an upper bound. Two relevant
pieces are reconstructed below: its exposed-edge lower bound, and the
general rational favorable-response-to-mixture reduction. Neither depends
on the unexecuted D trajectory.

## 1. Independently checked imported edge obstruction

Let `P = conv V` for finite Boolean `V`, and let `[u,v]` be an actual edge
with `k = |supp(u-v)|`. Put `y = (u+v)/2`. An edge is an exposed face, so
there is a linear functional `ell` with `ell(z) <= alpha` on `P` and
equality exactly on `[u,v]`. If a law on `V` has mean `y`, then

`E[alpha - ell(b)] = alpha - ell(y) = 0`.

The integrand is nonnegative, so every supported atom is on the edge.
The only vertices on this edge are `u,v`; its midpoint therefore forces
their weights to be exactly `1/2`. Take the single feature row
`a_j = sign(u_j-v_j)` on changed coordinates and zero elsewhere. Every
column has Euclidean norm at most one, and

`a(b-y) = +k/2` or `-k/2`.

Thus every exact-marginal law has hard radius at least `k/2`; moreover
its feature variance is `k^2/4`, and its covariance matrix is
`(u-v)(u-v)^T/4`, whose nonzero eigenvalue is `k/4`. This is a proof for
all finite Boolean polytopes, not an inference from finite fixtures.
It validates D's lower-bound premise only. It does not validate D's upper
bound, its constant, or its finite-bit favorable-cost construction.

## 2. Sharp two-budget allocation obstruction and minimal repair

Let `r >= 2`, and let the resources be the vertices of the even cycle
`C_(2r)`. An allocation is a Boolean edge vector `b`. Require exactly one
selected incident edge at every vertex. These constraints are precisely
common bases of the two partition matroids defined by the two sides of
the bipartition. They are also a `0/1` table with unit row/column budgets
and structural zeros.

Number edges cyclically by `e = 0,...,2r-1`. The degree equations are

`b_(e-1) + b_e = 1`.

They force alternation, hence exactly two feasible allocations: `u` selects
the even edges and `v` selects the odd edges. The entire common-base
polytope is the segment `[u,v]`, with actual edge support `2r`. Feasible
counting is trivial (`Z=2`), and an exact uniform sample uses one fair bit.
This grants a stronger count/sample interface than family 114, so it is
an equally informed negative control for any proposed counting bridge.

Take `y_e = 1/2` and scalar feature `a_e = (-1)^e`. The columns again
have norm one. Any exact-mean feasible law is the fair two-atom law, and

`a(b-y) = +/- r`, `Var[a(b-y)] = r^2`.

Even exact counting, exact sampling, and exact marginal acquisition
cannot produce a dimension-independent outcome-wise balancing bound in
this interface. The obstruction is feasibility geometry, rather than
failure of the sampler. Local negative correlation incident on one
resource is consistent with this global variance.

The same example uses positive rank-one information contributions,
not only signed scalar features. Assign
`H_e=diag(1,0)` to even edges and `H_e=diag(0,1)` to odd edges.
All matrices are positive semidefinite, rank one, and norm one, while
`V=sum H_e^2=r I` and the fractional information matrix is
`F=sum y_e H_e=(r/2) I`. The only feasible outputs have information
matrices `diag(r,0)` and `diag(0,r)`: both are singular, despite a
positive definite fractional target. Their operator error is `r/2`,
whose ratio to `sqrt(||V||)` is `sqrt(r)/2`. Thus a dimension-independent
rank-one spectral rounding promise for arbitrary two-partition common
bases is impossible even in matrix order two.

There is a sharp repair tradeoff. Retain exact coordinate marginals and
the hard total budget `sum b_e = r`, but allow degree constraints to fail.
Write `Z(b) = sum_e (-1)^e b_e`, and let
`q = Pr[all vertex budgets are exact]`. Then

`Var Z >= q r^2 + (1-q)(r mod 2)`.

Indeed, exact marginals give `E Z = 0`. On an exact-budget outcome
`Z^2 = r^2`. For every cardinality-`r` outcome, if `s` selected edges
are even then `Z = 2s-r`, so `Z^2 >= r mod 2`. These two observations
prove the lower bound without assuming any particular law.

It is attained for every rational `q in [0,1]`. Put `t=floor(r/2)`.
Define `z` by using the even-edge matching on the first `2t` consecutive
edges and the odd-edge matching on the remaining edges. Then

* `sum z_e = r`;
* every degree is one except degree two at vertex zero and degree zero
  at vertex `2t`;
* `Z(z)=2t-r`, of magnitude `r mod 2`;
* the complement `1-z` has the opposite two defects and opposite feature.

Use the four-atom law with weights `q/2,q/2,(1-q)/2,(1-q)/2` on
`u,v,z,1-z`. Each coordinate mean is exactly `1/2`. Its exact-budget
probability is `q`, and its variance equals the lower bound.

For the information matrices, every cardinality-`r` allocation satisfies
`sum b_e H_e-F=(Z/2)diag(1,-1)`. The same sharp curve therefore reads
`E ||sum b_e H_e-F||^2 >= [q r^2+(1-q)(r mod 2)]/4`.
The two-defect repair has smallest information eigenvalue
`floor(r/2)>0`, with operator error zero for even `r` and `1/2` for odd
`r`. A law requiring every supported information matrix to be
nonsingular has `q=0` in this instance.

Consequently, any hard balancing promise `|Z| < r` requires `q=0`.
For even `r`, two unit degree defects permit zero feature error; for odd
`r`, they permit error one. Two defects are optimal: because total degree
is `2r`, any failure of degree one must include at least one degree zero
and one degree two. This establishes the minimum `l1` degree-error cost
of two. The discontinuity is in hard resource feasibility, not in the
availability of exact marginals or total resource accounting.

For a feasible two-atom law with `Pr(u)=p` whose coordinate marginals are
only within `epsilon=|p-1/2|` of the target, its centered feature variance is
`4r^2 p(1-p) = r^2(1-4 epsilon^2)`. Small marginal bias alone therefore
does not repair the macroscopic variance either.

This cycle geometry and ordinary degree-preserving dependent rounding
are classical. The explicit Pareto curve is our reconstruction and is
useful as a protocol-admission test; its historical novelty is unknown.
It is not itself a high-significance foundational discovery.

## 3. What the thin-and-cheap oracle does imply

Fix rational fractional edge masses `x >= 0` with every nontrivial cut
having mass at least one, and let `G_gamma` be the finite family of trees
in the positive support whose every cut load is at most
`gamma x(delta(S))`. Corollary 8.1, conditional on its strong thin-tree
algorithm, provides for every nonnegative rational cost `c` a member
`b in G_gamma` satisfying

`c b <= gamma c x`.

It follows that there is a law on at most `|E|+1` such thin trees with

`E b <= gamma x` coordinatewise.

Proof: write `K=conv G_gamma` and `D={z:z<=gamma x}`. If these sets were
disjoint, their positive distance and finite-dimensional separation would
give `c` with `min_(z in K) c z > sup_(d in D) c d`. The supremum over
the downward orthant is finite only for `c>=0`, and then equals
`gamma c x`. This contradicts the oracle premise. Caratheodory reduces
support to `|E|+1`; rational LP gives rational weights for rational data.
The same argument applies to any finite Boolean feasible family and
nonnegative-cost approximation oracle.

This is a dominated-marginal output, with a constant inflation factor.
Its expected costs are bounded for every nonnegative cost simultaneously,
and every supported design is thin. It does not imply `E b = x`.
Signed separating costs are necessary for that equality. Shifting a
signed cost to a nonnegative one is insufficient: even if all outputs
have fixed cardinality `h` and `x(E)=h`, shifting by `M*1` turns
`c b <= gamma c x` into a bound with additive loss `(gamma-1) M h`.

A small abstract counterexample exposes this gap. Let the available good
outputs be `{(1,0)}`, take `y=(1/2,1/2)`, and `gamma=2`. Every
nonnegative cost has `c(1,0)<=gamma c y`, but no law on the good family
has mean `y`. This counterexample only refutes the inference from the
nonnegative-cost interface; it is not a counterexample to the existence
of an exact-marginal thin-tree law under additional graph structure.

Writing all cuts as scalar discrepancy rows also does not make D a
strong thin-tree theorem. For all cuts modulo complementation, a fixed
edge occurs in `2^(n-2)` cut rows. Its unweighted Euclidean column norm
is `2^((n-2)/2)`. Normalizing columns and then undoing normalization
charges that exponential factor. The metric-cone shortcut in family 174
is additional structure; it cannot be silently substituted by D's
terminal Euclidean-column bound.

## 4. A conditional rank-one information-matrix law

There is a useful positive cube-only conversion, which does not need
D's constrained rounding theorem.

**Hereditary signing-to-law lemma.** Let `h_1,...,h_N` be vectors in a
finite-dimensional normed space. Suppose that for every subset `J` one
can find signs `s_i` with `||sum_(i in J) s_i h_i|| <= D`. For every
`y in [0,1]^N`, there is a law on at most `N+1` Boolean vectors with
`E b=y` and `||sum_i (b_i-y_i)h_i|| <= D` on every supported outcome.

For dyadic `y` with denominator `2^L`, proceed from bit `L` to bit one.
At bit `ell`, let `J` contain the odd numerators at that scale. Obtain
one signing on `J`, and choose it or its negative with equal probability;
add the chosen signs times `2^(-ell)` to those coordinates. Both choices
stay in `[0,1]` and leave even numerators. Each step is conditionally
mean-preserving, and its norm error is at most `2^(-ell)D`. Every final
outcome is Boolean, the mean is exactly the starting vector, and its
total error is at most `(1-2^(-L))D`. The law is finite, although the
uncompressed decision tree need not be small. Affine dependence removes
atoms until at most `N+1` remain, preserving the mean and hard support.

For a general `y`, take dyadic approximations tending to it. The simplex
of laws on the finite Boolean cube is compact. A convergent subsequence
has exact limiting mean `y`, and every positive limiting atom satisfies
the closed bound `D`. Caratheodory again bounds support. This proves
existence only; an algorithm should not enumerate the decision tree or
claim that approximate marginals are exact.

Here is a finite-bit favorable-cost algorithm for rational input. Given
an arbitrary rational cost `c`, floor `y` to a dyadic vector `y^(L)`.
Let `Q_y,Q_c` be common positive denominators for `y,c`. Any nonzero
value `c(b-y)` is an integer multiple of `g=1/(Q_y Q_c)`. Choose `L`
such that

`2^(-L) ||c||_1 < g/2`,

and such that the norm error of the initial floor approximation is at
most a requested positive rational slack `epsilon`. At each dyadic bit
choose the sign orientation whose change in `c` is nonpositive. The
same hereditary norm bounds hold for either orientation, and

`c(b-y) <= c(y^(L)-y) < g/2`.

The rational gap forces `c(b-y)<=0` exactly. The returned atom has norm
error at most `D+epsilon`. The dyadic bit count is polynomial in the
original and queried rational encodings and `log(1/epsilon)` plus a
bound on the contribution norms. No exact objective optimization over
good atoms is required.

Apply this to rational Hermitian rank-at-most-one `H_i` and operator
norm. Put `V=sum_i H_i^2`, `tau=max_i |Tr H_i|`, and
`T=sum_i |Tr H_i|`. If all `H_i` are zero the law is trivial. Otherwise
`tau>0`, `tau<=||V||^(1/2)`, and each hereditary signing from the
family-174 Appendix A has bound at most `40 ||V||^(1/2)`, by PSD
monotonicity of the subset sum of squares. Choose the floor precision
also to satisfy `2^(-L)T <= tau`. Then the finite-bit favorable-cost
routine returns a Boolean `b` with

`||sum_i (b_i-y_i)H_i|| <= 41 ||V||^(1/2)`,
`c(b-y)<=0`.

Exact hull extraction yields a rational law on at most `N+1` atoms with
that hard operator bound and exact mean `y`. Conditional complexity is
polynomial, charging `O(L)` hereditary signing calls per favorable
response and polynomially many responses/bit operations in hull
extraction. The Appendix A primitive is a mathematical dependency, not
an implementation we have run.

For information contributions `H_i=a_i a_i^* >= 0`, let
`F=sum_i y_i H_i`. Every supported design then satisfies

`F - 41 sqrt(||V||) I <= sum_i b_i H_i <= F + 41 sqrt(||V||) I`.

If `lambda_min(F)>41 sqrt(||V||)`, each outcome is nonsingular with
that explicit lower eigenvalue. Expected resource costs are preserved
by exact marginals. This is useful only when contributions and their
normalization are supplied/acquired, and when outcome-wise resource
budgets are absent or separately established.

This does **not** resurrect an arbitrary-operator extension of D. The
rank-one hypothesis is essential. For `N=s^2`, index diagonal coordinates
by `eta in {+/-1}^N`, and put `H_j(eta,eta)=eta_j/s`. Then
`sum H_j^2=I` and `||H_j||=1/s`, but every signing has operator norm
`s=sqrt(N)` because the coordinate `eta=signing` aligns all terms.
These matrices have full rank, rather than rank one. Likewise, replacing
the variance quantity by `sum y_i(1-y_i)H_i^2` is impossible for a hard
exact-marginal law: a single nonzero rank-one `H`, with `y=p` arbitrarily
small, must include the selected outcome with error `(1-p)||H||`.

## 5. Independent favorable-response extraction

This checks the imported extraction premise used above. Fix `y=a/Q`
rational and a finite family `G` of Boolean good atoms. Suppose that for
every rational `c` a polynomial-bit routine returns `b in G` with
`c(b-y)<=0`. Separation immediately proves `y in conv G`; the issue is
recovering a finite exact certificate with a response oracle rather
than optimization over `G`.

Consider separator constraints `c(b-y)>=1` for all `b in G`. Every
oracle response is a violated constraint. Any feasible finite collected
subsystem has a bounded rational separator: minimize an auxiliary
infinity-norm variable. After clearing the common denominator `Q`,
integer matrix entries have magnitude at most `Q` or one, and right
sides at most `Q`. A basic solution has coordinates bounded by
`R_0=(N+1)! Q^(N+1)` by Cramer's rule and determinant expansion. The
recession issue is removed by the auxiliary norm minimization and an
optimal basic solution in the bounded optimal slice.

Doubling this separator gives slack at least one in every collected
constraint. Since `||b-y||_2<=sqrt(N)`, a Euclidean ball of radius
`1/(2N)` is feasible. It lies inside the box `|c_i|<=4 R_0` around the
doubled separator. An outward-rounded rational central-cut ellipsoid
procedure with a volume cutoff below this ball therefore cannot keep
all collected constraints feasible indefinitely. Each query uses the
favorable response to give a valid central cut and stores that atom.
After polynomially many queries, the collected subsystem is infeasible.
The fixed inner-ball guarantee is the step that rules out mere failure
to find a separator. Standard rational ellipsoid arithmetic is required;
it is not implemented by the finite fixture.

Infeasibility proves `y` is in the hull of the collected atoms. An exact
rational LP gives nonnegative weights with exact sum one and exact mean.
Affine-dependence reduction leaves at most `N+1` atoms. Boolean atom
coefficients and rational `y` bound certificate weight encodings by
`O(log Q + N log N)` through Hadamard determinant bounds. The method
needs no optimum over the good family, and cannot manufacture an atom
outside the oracle's feasibility/hard-bound promises.

## Prior art, novelty, and verification limits

The dyadic hereditary-to-linear rounding mechanism is classical,
associated with Lovasz--Spencer--Vesztergombi (1986). Its exact-marginal
version follows by choosing opposite orientations or favorable costs
and finite-dimensional convexity. The two-budget cycle is the standard
alternating-cycle geometry of bipartite dependent rounding; Gandhi,
Khuller, Parthasarathy, and Srinivasan explicitly preserve marginal
probabilities and integer vertex degrees, while their negative
correlation assertion concerns edges incident to one vertex. These
sources do not supply a dimension-free global feature bound in the
cycle fixture. No novelty is claimed for their mechanisms.

The rank-one primitive predates the 6 October release:
Ezeunala--Jiang, [2609.17266v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17266v1),
submitted 15 September 2026, Theorem 1.1 claims deterministic polynomial
signing with constant 13, and its proof explicitly permits an arbitrary
rational starting coloring. Kyng--Luh--Song (2020), *Four deviations
suffice for rank 1 matrices*, [1901.06731v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.06731v4),
Corollary 1.6 gives the older hereditary signing existence bound
`4 ||sum H_i^2||^(1/2)` for positive rank-one matrices. The generic
signing-to-law proof above therefore already implies an exact-marginal
cube law with hard bound `4 ||V||^(1/2)` from that older theorem, without
an acquired polynomial signing routine. Its stronger weighted-variance
Theorem 1.4 promises one good outcome, not a law with prescribed
marginals; the single-coordinate obstruction above distinguishes those
outputs. The primary version history verifies first submission on
20 January 2019 and v4 on 4 August 2020; its full interlacing proof was
not independently audited here. The
release Appendix A uses constant 40 and explicit finite-bit estimates.
Thus the positive exact-marginal cube consequence is not dependent on
family 114, family 175, or a uniquely new thin-tree theorem. It is a
consequence of rank-one hereditary signing plus old transference and
certificate extraction. The broader programme's formerly excluded
arbitrary-rank operator claim remains excluded.
