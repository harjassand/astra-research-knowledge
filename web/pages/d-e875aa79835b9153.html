PINNED INPUT
N spin-1/2 particles. J_a=(1/2)sum_i sigma_a^(i), J^2=J_x^2+J_y^2+J_z^2. Initial state I/2^N. The supplied stationary state for (nu+1)D[J_-]+nu D[J_+] is
 rho_N = direct_sum_j w_Nj tau_j,beta tensor I_mult/mult,
 w_Nj = (2j+1) mult_Nj / 2^N,
 tau_j,beta = exp(-beta J_z)/Z_j,
 beta_N = log(1+1/(c sqrt(N))), c>0 fixed.
The direct-sum weights preserve exactly Tr rho_N J^2=3N/4.
The target distance is d_N(c)=inf_{sigma in fullSEP} (1/2)||rho_N-sigma||_1.

THEOREM 1: EXACT ASYMPTOTIC FULLSEP HEAT-EFFECT BOUND
For each t>0 and each fixed vector v in R^3, define
 B_N(v) = sum_a (J_a/sqrt(N)-v_a)^2,
 W_N(t,v) = exp[-t B_N(v)].
Then 0<=W_N<=I and
 lim_{N->infinity} sup_{sigma in fullSEP} Tr sigma W_N(t,v)
       = 1/(1+t/2).
The supremum allows arbitrary inhomogeneous product states and arbitrary mixtures, without permutation invariance or an IID assumption.

Proof, reduction to product arrays.
W_N is a linear objective, and fullSEP is the convex hull of pure product states. It is enough to bound any sequence pi_N=product_i rho_Ni, allowing mixed factors as well. Write r_Ni for the local Bloch vectors, |r_Ni|<=1, and put
 u_N = (1/(2sqrt(N))) sum_i r_Ni,
 Q_N = (1/N) sum_i r_Ni r_Ni^T,
 Sigma_N = (I-Q_N)/4.
Sigma_N is positive semidefinite, each eigenvalue is at most 1/4, and Tr Sigma_N>=1/2. In a pure product array the trace is exactly 1/2.

Bounded-mean subsequences.
If u_N is bounded, pass to a subsequence with u_N->u and Q_N->Q. Let
 F_Na = (1/sqrt(N)) sum_i (sigma_a^(i)/2-r_Ni,a/2).
Every ordered joint moment of F_N converges to the corresponding CLASSICAL Gaussian moment with covariance Sigma=(I-Q)/4. Here is a direct moment proof that also handles nonidentical local factors. Expand a fixed degree-k ordered word. Independence and centering kill every site index that occurs once. Contributions whose site partition has fewer than k/2 blocks are O(N^{-1/2}) or smaller, uniformly in the Bloch vectors, since each centered local factor has bounded norm. The surviving pair partitions use the average ordered two-point covariance
 (1/N) sum_i E[(s_ia-r_ia/2)(s_ib-r_ib/2)]
   = (delta_ab-Q_N,ab)/4 + (i/4) epsilon_abk (1/N)sum_i r_Ni,k.
The imaginary part is O(N^{-1/2}) when u_N is bounded. Excluding repeated sites among distinct pairs changes the average by O(1/N). Thus the limit is the ordinary Wick formula with real covariance Sigma.

Consequently all moments of the positive observable B_N(v) converge to those of |G+u-v|^2, where G~Normal(0,Sigma). This scalar limiting distribution is moment determinate: its moment generating function is finite in a neighborhood of zero (all eigenvalues Sigma<=1/4). Moment convergence, tightness from the first moment, and uniform integrability from the next moment therefore give weak convergence of the spectral distributions of B_N. In particular,
 Tr pi_N exp[-t B_N(v)]
   -> det(I+2t Sigma)^(-1/2)
       * exp[-t (u-v)^T(I+2t Sigma)^(-1)(u-v)]
   <= det(I+2t Sigma)^(-1/2).

If lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3 are the eigenvalues of Sigma, then
 0<=lambda_i<=1/4, sum_i lambda_i>=1/2.
The determinant product_i(1+2t lambda_i) is minimized at a permutation of (0,1/4,1/4). One way to see this is to first lower the sum to 1/2, then minimize the concave function sum_i log(1+2t lambda_i) over the resulting polytope; its extreme points are exactly these permutations. Hence
 det(I+2t Sigma)>= (1+t/2)^2,
and the claimed upper bound follows on every bounded-mean subsequence.

Escaping-mean subsequences: the uniformity gate.
Suppose d_N=|u_N-v|->infinity, and n_N=(u_N-v)/d_N. Set
 S_N = n_N dot (J/sqrt(N)-v).
Then E_pi S_N=d_N and Var_pi S_N<=1/4. The exact operator inequality B_N(v)>=S_N^2 holds by orthogonally rotating the three shifted spin operators and summing their squares. Let P_N(R) be the spectral projection of B_N onto [0,R]. Since
 P_N S_N^2 P_N <= P_N B_N P_N <= R P_N,
we have ||S_N P_N||=||P_N S_N||<=sqrt(R).
Purify the product state as a unit vector Psi_N, with the observables acting on the physical factors. Then
 d_N ||P_N Psi_N||
  = ||P_N[(d_N-S_N)Psi_N+S_N Psi_N]||
  <= sqrt(Var_pi S_N)+sqrt(R)
  <= 1/2+sqrt(R).
Therefore
 Tr pi_N W_N <= Tr pi_N P_N(R)+exp(-tR)
             <= (1/2+sqrt(R))^2/d_N^2+exp(-tR).
First N->infinity and then R->infinity give expectation tending to zero. This rules out all products whose mean escapes the central-limit scale, with no moment-tail assumption on candidate separable mixtures.

If a uniform limsup bound failed, choose a violating product array. Its means have either a bounded subsequence, treated by the Gaussian argument, or an escaping subsequence, treated by the last paragraph. This proves the uniform bound over all product states, hence over their convex hull.

Attainment.
For v=mu e_z, take pure local +z and -z states with a count imbalance giving J_z=mu sqrt(N)+O(1). Q_N=e_z e_z^T and u_N->mu e_z. The Gaussian limit is centered at v with Sigma=diag(1/4,1/4,0), attaining 1/(1+t/2). For general v, choose local antipodal states along n=v/|v| with the analogous O(sqrt(N)) imbalance (any n when v=0). This proves the limit of the supremum, not just a limsup upper bound. QED.

THEOREM 2: ROBUST ENTANGLEMENT WINDOW FOR THE SUPPLIED TARGET
Define
 p(r)=8 sqrt(2/pi) r^2 exp(-2r^2), r>=0,
 m(c)=integral_0^infinity p(r)[r coth(r/c)-c] dr.
Let c_* be the unique positive solution m(c_*)=1/2. Then, for every 0<c<c_*,
 liminf_N d_N(c)
   >= [1-sqrt(3/2-2 m(c)^2)]^2 >0.
For c>=c_* this theorem asserts only the trivial lower bound zero.

Target first-moment limit.
The spin multiplicities are
 mult_Nj=binom(N,N/2-j)-binom(N,N/2-j-1).
Stirling's formula gives, for j=r sqrt(N) in compact subsets of r>0,
 w_Nj=(1/sqrt(N)) p(r)+o(N^{-1/2}).
Tightness follows from E[j(j+1)/N]=3/4. The limiting radial law is thus p(r)dr (the radius of a three-dimensional centered Gaussian of covariance I/4). Conditional on j, z=m_z/sqrt(N) tends to the truncated exponential law
 density(z|r)=exp(-z/c)/(2c sinh(r/c)), -r<=z<=r.
Its mean is c-r coth(r/c). Because |J_z|<=j and the second radial moment is uniformly bounded, these first moments are uniformly integrable. Hence
 Tr rho_N J_z/sqrt(N) -> -m(c).

Fix mu=-m(c) and v=mu e_z. The exact inherited Casimir mean yields
 Tr rho_N B_N(v) -> b(c)=3/4-m(c)^2.
Since exp(-t x)>=1-tx for x>=0,
 liminf_N Tr rho_N W_N(t,v)>=1-t b(c).
For every effect W, every pair of states obeys
 (1/2)||rho-sigma||_1 >= Tr W(rho-sigma).
Theorem 1 therefore gives
 liminf_N d_N(c) >= 1-tb(c)-1/(1+t/2), t>0.
When b<1/2, optimize at t=2[(2b)^(-1/2)-1] to obtain
 (1-sqrt(2b))^2.
When b>=1/2, the optimum over t>=0 of this linear target bound is zero. QED.

Threshold existence and uniqueness.
Writing r coth(r/c)-c=r L(r/c), with L(x)=coth(x)-1/x, shows m(c) is continuous and strictly decreasing because L'(x)=1/x^2-csch(x)^2>0 for x>0. Its endpoints are m(0+)=E r=sqrt(2/pi)>1/2 and m(infinity)=0. Thus c_* exists uniquely. The endpoint of this particular proven window is not a claimed fullSEP phase transition.

EXACT LIMIT OF THIS WITNESS ON THE TARGET (OPTIONAL STRONGER LOWER BOUND)
For q=1/c and any t>0, mu real, the limiting target effect expectation is
 F_c(t,mu)= exp(-t mu^2) integral_0^infinity p(r) exp(-t r^2)
              * [q/(q-2tmu)] sinh[(q-2tmu)r]/sinh(qr) dr,
with the continuous extension when q=2tmu. Therefore
 liminf_N d_N(c) >= sup_{t>0,mu real} [F_c(t,mu)-1/(1+t/2)]_+.
The above integral follows from the same joint (r,z) limit and the bounded continuous integrand exp[-t(r^2-2mu z+mu^2)]. Unlike the explicit closed-form lower bound, it uses the full limiting target law.

FAILED APPROXIMATION ROUTES AND REMAINING GATE
1. Mixtures of identical weakly polarized product states have central-limit noise I/4. This class is too restrictive: Gaussian center mixtures can only add fluctuation covariance, whereas the target has total centered variance 3/4-m(c)^2<3/4. It must not be substituted for fullSEP.
2. Inhomogeneous balanced pure products have noise (I-Q)/4, including rank-two plane Gaussian noise. Thus arbitrary fullSEP can evade the IID argument. I did not construct a positive Gaussian-mixture representation of the target for c>=c_*.
3. A possible limiting fullSEP characterization by mixtures of Gaussians with these covariances would at most first concern weak collective-observable limits. It would not automatically prove full trace-norm approximation; a local limit theorem for the complete twirled (j,m_z) law is still required. No such theorem is supplied here.
4. Second-moment criteria alone are known prior art. Their violation proves entanglement, but normalizing the unbounded quadratic operator directly can lose the constant trace-distance gap. The bounded heat effect and the uniform product-array analysis close that particular operational gap.
5. Exact d(c), separable upper approximants for fixed c, and any actual sharp fullSEP threshold remain unresolved.
