# Finite-volume chemical safety from an inward-polytope certificate

**Strongest outcome:** an independently proved, explicit finite-volume exit bound, uniform over predictable time/feedback rate controls. Its use for every endotactic system is conditional on obtaining the deterministic inward-polytope certificate. A concrete closed two-species reaction example below needs no release theorem and has a certified exponential safety horizon. This is an explicit application of exponential martingale methods, not a new large-deviation principle or a proof of stochastic permanence.

## 1. Exact input contract

Supply finitely many integer-complex reactions `y_r→y'_r`, `y_r,y'_r∈N^d`, jumps `ν_r=y'_r−y_r`, and the stoichiometric span `S`. Let

\[
Q=\{x\in c+S:g_i(x)=a_i\cdot x+b_i\ge0,\ i=1,\ldots,m\}
\]

be compact and contained in the strictly positive orthant. Facets are relative to `c+S`; constant conservation inequalities are removed. Supply rational upper coordinate bounds `U_j>0` on `Q` and, for each remaining facet, rational numbers `ℓ_i,γ_i>0` such that for every admissible mode and rate vector,

\[
a_i\cdot\sum_r k_r x^{y_r}\nu_r\ge\gamma_i
\quad(x\in Q,\ 0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i). \tag{1}
\]

Controls may be arbitrary predictable functions of time, the full past, and independent randomness. Active rates lie in the specified admissible box, in particular `0≤k_r≤K`; inactive modes may set reactions to zero. Condition (1) must hold for each allowed mode, not for an enlarged rate box that silently changes the deterministic premise. The source teams provide facet margins; a compactness/quantifier-elimination step must acquire the actual positive **layer** and margin.

The molecule counts `Z^V` take values in `N^d`, concentrations are `X^V=Z^V/V`, and reaction intensity is

\[
V k_r(t,\text{history})\phi^V_{y_r}(X^V_{t-}),\qquad
\phi^V_y(x)=V^{-|y|}\prod_j(Vx_j)_{y_j}. \tag{2}
\]

An insufficient reactant count makes (2) zero. The convention omits the factorial division in binomial propensities; that factorial is absorbed into the deterministic `k_r`. Jump size in concentration is `ν_r/V`.

The initial count must be **supplied** as a lattice-compatible point `Z^V_0/V∈Q∩(c+S)`, with fixed positive facet gaps `g_i(X^V_0)≥d_i>0`. Conserved totals and the integer reaction lattice are charged constraints, not repaired by unconstrained coordinate rounding. For rational data, a rational `x_*∈relint Q` gives one compatible sequence at denominator-multiple volumes with `Z^V_0=Vx_*`; prescribed conserved count totals or a communicating coset still need their corresponding feasibility check. Every reachable count lies in the reaction-lattice coset `Z^V_0+span_Z{ν_r}`; this does not assert that the entire coset is one communicating class.

## 2. Uniform finite-volume correction

Put

\[
E_y(U)=\sum_{j:y_j\ge2}\binom{y_j}{2}
                    U_j^{y_j-1}\prod_{l\ne j}U_l^{y_l}.
\]

For all lattice points in the coordinate box, including insufficient-reactant points,

\[
0\le x^y-\phi_y^V(x)\le E_y(U)/V. \tag{3}
\]

One direct proof writes a lattice falling factorial as
`∏_{j,l<y_j} max(x_j−l/V,0)`. The first zero factor makes this equal to the true factorial when the count is insufficient. Each factor differs from `x_j` by at most `l/V`; telescope the nonnegative product and bound the other factors by their coordinate maxima. The sum of `l` is `binom(y_j,2)`, yielding (3).

Define certified rational bounds

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_i&=K\sum_r|a_i\cdot\nu_r|E_{y_r}(U),\\
 J_i&=\max_r|a_i\cdot\nu_r|,\\
 Q_i&=K\sum_r(a_i\cdot\nu_r)^2U^{y_r},\\
 \Lambda&=K\sum_rU^{y_r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For

\[
V\ge V_0:=\max_i\lceil2D_i/\gamma_i\rceil,
\quad \mu_i=\gamma_i/2,
\]

the actual finite-volume inward drift in each layer is at least `μ_i`. The stopped total reaction hazard is at most `VΛ`, and projected quadratic jump intensity divided by `V` is at most `Q_i`. Essential facets have `J_i,Q_i>0`; a zero projected jump means an irrelevant conserved inequality.

## 3. Sum-exponential proof

Set

\[
\theta_i=\min\{J_i^{-1},\mu_i/(3Q_i)\},\qquad
c=\min_i\theta_i\ell_i>0,
\qquad \Phi(x)=\sum_i e^{-\theta_iVg_i(x)}.
\]

Let `τ_Q` be the first jump outside the closed polytope `Q`. For a single facet function `Φ_i`, its predictable generator inside `Q` is

\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_i(x)}{\Phi_i(x)}
 =V\sum_rk_r\phi_{y_r}^V(x)
                         (e^{-\theta_i a_i\cdot\nu_r}-1).
\]

Using `e^z−1≤z+z²e^{|z|}/2` and `e^{θ_iJ_i}≤e<3`, in its layer this is at most

\[
V\{-\theta_i\mu_i+(3/2)\theta_i^2Q_i\}
 \le -V\theta_i\mu_i/2\le0.
\]

Outside its layer `Φ_i≤e^(−θ_iVℓ_i)`, so its positive generator contribution is at most `2VΛe^(−θ_iVℓ_i)`. Summing gives

\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi(x)\le2mV\Lambda e^{-cV}
\quad(x\in Q). \tag{4}
\]

At the first exit at least one gap is negative, hence `Φ(X_τ)≥1`. Overshoots do not require an extra union or traversal count: each projected overshoot is at most `J_i/V`, so the stopped `Φ` is bounded by `Σ_i e^(θ_iJ_i)≤3m`. The bounded stopped hazards give a nonexplosive stopped process. Dynkin's formula with predictable intensities, stopping at `T∧τ_Q`, is therefore justified for every finite `T`, even when `T` is exponentially large. Equations (4) and nonnegativity give

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)
\le\sum_i e^{-\theta_iVg_i(X_0)}
                +2mV\Lambda T e^{-cV}.
} \tag{5}
\]

This proof requires neither a Markov controller nor an equilibrium law, recurrence, irreducibility, a global nonexplosion theorem, or an action-minimizing path. It treats corners and exiting jumps directly. A random initial state with the same deterministic gap bounds gives the same uniform bound by conditioning.

For `T=e^(cV/2)`, (5) yields

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le e^{cV/2})
\le m e^{-c_0V}+2mV\Lambda e^{-cV/2},
\quad c_0=\min_i\theta_i d_i>0. \tag{6}
\]

If every initial gap is at least half its layer width, the right side is at most `m(1+2VΛ)e^(−cV/2)`. A count-scale initial gap `O(1/V)` does **not** provide exponentially small initial error; a fixed concentration buffer is essential. No deterministic outside-to-core absorption time is automatically a safe stochastic entrance protocol.

## 4. A complete unconditional instance with stronger constants

Use the closed reactions

\[
2A\longrightarrow A+B,\qquad
2B\longrightarrow A+B,
\quad 1\le k_A(t,\text{history}),k_B(t,\text{history})\le4.
\]

Total population is exactly `V`. Let `x=A/V`, `y=1−x`,

\[
Q=\{x+y=1,\ 1/4\le x\le3/4\},\quad
g_L=x-1/4,\quad g_R=3/4-x.
\]

Start at `(A,B)=(V/2,V/2)`, so `V` is even and both initial gaps are `1/4`. The deterministic facet margin is `5/16`, and both inward layer margins at width `ℓ=1/32` are at least `205/1024`. These follow from
`(1−x)²−4x²=1−2x−3x²`, decreasing on the left layer ending at `x=9/32`, and symmetry for the right.

The actual concentration birth and death intensities divided by `V` are

\[
b=k_B y(y-1/V),\qquad d=k_A x(x-1/V).
\]

In the left layer, either `d=0` or

\[
\frac bd\ge\frac{y(y-1/V)}{4x(x-1/V)}
 \ge\frac{y^2}{4x^2}\ge\frac{529}{324}.
\]

The middle inequality follows from `y>x`: the falling factors improve the ratio. It covers every enabled count; a zero death term only helps. The right layer is identical with birth and death exchanged.

Choose `θ=12/25`. The rational series bound

\[
e^\theta\le1+\theta+
          \frac{\theta^2}{2(1-\theta/3)}
       =\frac{283}{175}<\frac{529}{324}
\]

holds because `j!≥2·3^(j−2)` for `j≥2`. Thus the **exact** finite-volume exponential facet generator is nonpositive in both layers:

\[
b(e^{-\theta}-1)+d(e^\theta-1)
 =(e^\theta-1)(d-b/e^\theta)\le0.
\]

No asymptotic factorial correction threshold is needed for this example. On all of `Q`, the total scaled activity is at most

\[
\Lambda=4\max_{x\in[1/4,3/4]}[x^2+(1-x)^2]=5/2.
\]

Outside the layers the two positive generator bounds sum to at most `(108/35)V e^(−3V/200)`. The final exact certificate is

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)
 \le2e^{-3V/25}+\frac{108}{35}VT e^{-3V/200}.
} \tag{7}
\]

In particular, at `T=e^(3V/400)`,

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)
 \le2e^{-3V/25}+\frac{108}{35}V e^{-3V/400}.
\]

The constants and signs were independently reviewed by the `reaction_control_audit` worker. `check_jump_safety.py` passed 16,610 exact factorial-correction checks (4,847 include insufficient reactants), 28,004 count-activity checks at rate corners, and 3,656 exact collar-generator checks. Its output `check_jump_safety.json` records numerical horizon/error evaluations and the 0.266-second arithmetic runtime. They verify scoped arithmetic and finite generators, not an external novelty claim. At `V=3000` the horizon is `5.910522063×10^9` assumed rate-time units and the exit bound is `1.566214077×10^(-6)`; the expected thinning candidate count is bounded by `4.432891547×10^13`. No full-horizon simulation was performed.

For fixed constant positive rates and every even `V≥6`, this example nevertheless exits `Q` eventually with probability one: the states `A=1,...,V−1` form a finite irreducible birth-death class, and `A=1` lies outside `Q` for `V>4`. Both species' extinction states are inaccessible from this class, because the final molecule cannot undergo a same-species bimolecular loss. Thus long safe operation and infinite-time exit coexist even in this closed example. Networks with accessible absorbing faces require their own extinction analysis; (5) is a finite-horizon containment result and makes no contrary claim.

A strict inward polytope alone is even compatible with inevitable extinction. For the closed reactions `A+B→2A` at rate `2` and `A→B` at rate `1`, fix `A+B=V`. The deterministic concentration equation is `x'=x(1−2x)`, so `Q={1/4≤x≤3/4}` has strict inward facet margins `1/8` and `3/8`. Its finite count chain has birth rate `2j(V−j)/V` and death rate `j`; `j=0` is absorbing and reachable from every other state, giving extinction almost surely by finiteness. The network is not endotactic and is used only to show the scope limit of the inward-polytope theorem. The general exponential-horizon theorem still applies to this deterministic certificate.

## 5. Scope, acquisition, and cost audit

- The source teams' all-endotactic/common-span-switching extension supplies a proposed deterministic inward polytope through a finite affine-min plateau construction. The chemical certificate must still be instantiated and certified. The general stochastic theorem assumes (1); it does not validate the source's affine lemma or its extension.
- Ordinary molecular mass action requires **integer reactant and product complexes**, not merely rational exponents. For rational `y` but integer jumps, an artificial density-dependent jump process with rates `V k x^y` can use the same proof inside a positive `Q`, with no factorial correction; it is a different microscopic model. If that artificial process retains nonnegative integer counts, one also needs enough volume that every jump is feasible throughout `Q`: `V min_Q x_j≥max_r(-ν_rj)_+` for every coordinate, or an explicitly charged propensity cutoff. Rational/noninteger stoichiometric jumps cannot be silently represented as ordinary molecule counts. A commensurate rational lattice is a mathematical alternative with separately supplied units.
- Once the certificate is supplied, the constants in (3)–(6) use `O(mdR)` scalar operations plus fixed-degree monomial evaluation. Finding `Q`, `ℓ_i`, and `γ_i` may require costly nonlinear or quantifier-elimination certification. No efficient all-network acquisition algorithm is claimed.
- A simulator can stop at the first exit and use rate-`VΛ` thinning. Up to horizon `T`, its expected candidate-event count is at most `VΛT`. A fixed reaction set permits count storage `O(d log V)` and supplied certificate storage; each event requires the rate-controller service and propensity evaluation. Simulating the full exponential safe horizon is itself exponential work. No such simulation was run.
- Predictable feedback may be computationally unbounded or noncomputable. The probability theorem does not acquire or implement that controller. A practical implementation needs a supplied finite evaluator and rate-budget accounting.
- For open systems, maintaining inflow and rate bounds for `T=e^(cV)` may require cumulative feed, fuel, or controller work of order `VT`. Compact concentrations do not bound that expenditure. The concrete example conserves molecule count and needs no material inflow, but still has reaction turnover of order `VT`; energetic and control costs of physically realizing the assumed rates remain supplied requirements.
- No large-deviation rate optimality, sharp quasipotential, mean exit-time equivalence, stationary concentration, or stochastic permanence is established. The explicit `c` is a conservative safety certificate.

Prior art: [Agazzi–Dembo–Eckmann, large deviations for chemical jump processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126) already establishes chemical Wentzell–Freidlin theory and exponential transition-time estimates under its hypotheses. [Their geometry/Lyapunov companion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240) is an important network-geometric antecedent. The contribution here is the elementary **finite, explicitly certified, control-uniform** reduction from an acquired strict polytope to (5), and the worked finite-count certificate (7); broad historical novelty has not been established.

## 6. Decisive checks and remaining gate

The theorem can be falsified by a missing layer margin, a controller outside its admissible rate class, uncharged jump sizes/propensity discrepancies, or an initial lattice point without the stated buffer. These inputs are sufficient to check the generator inequality directly. For the source-wide consequence the load-bearing gap is the general deterministic inward-polytope certificate, particularly its affine lemma and actual finite construction. For the explicit two-species example that gap is absent: all required inequalities are proved above.
