# c03_l04 deep attack R1 — exact operator boundary and attribution

Date: 2026-10-07. `INITIAL.txt` is frozen and unchanged (SHA-256 `0784ed06e10d9827e31a4a81e892c3418b50d3b81464ebcadeaf3e87f30d7f8e`). This revision records the authorized post-freeze peer exposure and narrows the strongest reusable result to an explicit operator interface.

## Claim and exact interface

Let `P,Q` be orthogonal projectors on the same finite-dimensional Fock space, and let `K=QP` be the accepted Kraus operator for two successive hard filters. If `K` could be represented as one hard projective filter, with arbitrary unitary changes of basis before and after and one overall scalar, then its nonzero singular values would all be equal. More exactly, `K = alpha W R V` for unitaries `W,V`, an orthogonal projector `R`, and scalar `alpha` implies `K* K = |alpha|^2 V* R V`; hence `K* K` has only eigenvalues `0` and `|alpha|^2`.

Conversely, for an unrestricted finite-dimensional operator `T`, the condition that all nonzero singular values equal the same `s>0` is sufficient: polar decomposition gives `T=s W R` after extending its polar partial isometry to a unitary. This converse is only about unrestricted unitaries/projectors; it does not assert the extended unitary or projector is fermionic Gaussian. Thus the spectral condition is a necessary obstruction to every Gaussian-unitary/projector representation, but is not a full Gaussian-classification theorem.

For two projectors, `K* K=PQP`. Restricted to `Ran(P)`, the eigenvalues of `PQP` are the squared cosines of the principal angles between `Ran(P)` and `Ran(Q)`. A hard-projector reduction is possible only if every nonzero principal-angle cosine is the same (and any intersection contributes cosine one). A common range direction plus one nontrivial angle therefore gives a minimal two-mode witness.

## Rational two-mode witness

In basis `(|00>,|10>,|01>,|11>)`, let `P=diag(1,0,1,0)` (first mode empty). Let the number-conserving Gaussian rotation `U` fix vacuum and full occupancy and rotate the one-particle sector by

`[[3/5,-4/5],[4/5,3/5]]`.

Put `Q=UPU*` and `K=QP`. Exact arithmetic gives

`K* K = P Q P = diag(1,0,9/25,0)`.

The two nonzero singular values are `1` and `3/5`; the principal angles are `0` and `arccos(3/5)`. This rules out `K=alpha W R V` for any orthogonal `R`, including the larger class with unrestricted `W,V`; a fortiori it rules out Gaussian `W,V`. On Gaussian input `I/4`, the normalized accepted state has eigenvalues `25/34,9/34`, whereas a single rank-two hard projection after a unitary has two equal nonzero eigenvalues. The supplied exact checker reproduces the matrix and spectrum.

The sequence is still a composition of Gaussian filters. Bravyi's definition of FLO explicitly includes mode occupation projectors, allows interleaved unitary/measurement operations, and describes them by Gaussian maps (source below). So the result separates a composed Gaussian postselection map from one same-register orthogonal hard projection surrounded by unitaries; it does not separate it from arbitrary Gaussian postselection, nor from a terminal construction with added ancillas, Gaussian dilation, or a nonunitary Gaussian filter. Any claim that “one terminal projection” simulates arbitrary Gaussian postselection must state whether those resources are allowed.

## Three interfaces kept distinct

1. **Fixed transcript of Gaussian filters.** A resolved transcript is a product of Gaussian operators/maps. The two-projector example is inside this class. It remains efficiently simulable under the cited FLO framework.
2. **Adaptive filtering with erased record.** The INITIAL four-mode gadget produces a mixed data state with Wick violation and both parities after branch-dependent continuation and transcript erasure. It is not one fixed Gaussian branch or a projection to one data charge/parity sector. But its accepted event is a single joint ancilla subspace projector, so it is not a separation from unrestricted terminal subspace projection with ancillas.
3. **One terminal global charge projection.** The INITIAL BCS example projects onto `N=2` and yields `(|12>+|34>)/sqrt(2)`, whose two-form has nonzero wedge square. This is a non-Gaussian fixed-number state. It is a reverse state-class separation; it is not an approximation-hardness statement.

The controlled distinctions are therefore (i) map singular values, (ii) state Gaussianity after coarse-graining, and (iii) non-Gaussianity after global charge projection. None alone proves a classical complexity gap or transfers to bosonic postselection.

## Limits and next gate

The assigned phrases “arbitrary Gaussian postselection” and “single terminal hard projection” do not determine an operation class. Under the precise same-register projective interface, the two-mode map obstruction is decisive. If the target admits an arbitrary Gaussian contraction, ancillary Gaussian dilation, a final arbitrary subspace projector, or an outcome-dependent coarse-graining, this obstruction does not settle equivalence. The next decisive task is to fix that interface and test the proposed equivalence as a map (not merely on a single pure input), including its ancilla and transcript resources. No hardness, sampling separation, novelty, or general projection simulation is claimed here.
