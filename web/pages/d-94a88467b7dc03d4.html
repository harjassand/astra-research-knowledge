# An acquired linear symmetry excludes a plane Keller counterexample

## Question and exact interface

The residual-affine Hessian descent leaves an arbitrary polynomial pair

    G=(y,2x)D,  D in SL2(C[x,y]),  det JG=-2.

The component ideal is (x,y), so the zero fibre is the singleton origin. The checked constructive converse in hessian_bridge12/cycle12/FIBRE_AFFINE_DESCENT_BOUNDARY.md means that no additional global restriction may be assumed from the original Meng–Yang coefficient row. We seek a stronger invariant that can actually be acquired from a particular G and excludes a candidate without repeating a universal version of JC2.

Let M=JG(0), and normalize H=M^{-1}G. Thus H(0)=0 and JH(0)=I. Define the four-dimensional coefficient kernel

    L(H)={W in Mat2(C): JH(z) Wz = W H(z) identically}.

The identity is linear in the four entries of W. It asks for the same linear infinitesimal action on source and normalized target, and supplies no unknown axis, grading or properness promise.

**Theorem.** If H is a plane Keller map with H(0)=0, JH(0)=I and L(H) is nonzero, then H is a polynomial automorphism. Consequently a noninvertible member of the residual class must have L(H)=0.

The singleton-fibre assumption is unnecessary for this theorem beyond giving the programme's fixed origin. Its conclusion is strictly a subclass filter, not an equivalent reformulation of unrestricted JC2.

## Complete reconstruction, including every Jordan case

Choose a nonzero W in the kernel. Constant conjugacy in source and target permits Jordan normal form for W. Write H=z+sum H_r, where H_r is homogeneous of ordinary degree r>=2. The kernel equation holds separately for every r.

### 1. W is diagonalizable

Write W=diag(lambda1,lambda2). A monomial x^i y^j in H_k has

    i lambda1+j lambda2=lambda_k.                         (1)

If both eigenvalues are nonzero and their ratio is not rational, (1) has only the two linear possibilities. Any nonlinear solution would give a nontrivial integer relation between the eigenvalues. Thus H=z.

If both eigenvalues are nonzero with rational ratio, multiply W by a nonzero scalar so the eigenvalues become coprime nonzero integers. This changes no kernel equation.

For eigenvalues of the same sign, negate W if necessary and exchange coordinates to obtain 0<a<=b. If a=b, all admissible monomials are linear. If a<b, the first component is x, while the second is y plus possibly c x^k, where k=b/a is an integer >=2. Normalization fixes the linear coefficients to1. Hence H is either the identity or one triangular shear, with explicit polynomial inverse.

For opposite signs write the eigenvalues as (a,-b), a,b positive coprime integers. Put u=x^b y^a. Equation (1) gives

    H1=x P(u),  H2=y Q(u),  P(0)=Q(0)=1.

An exact derivative computation gives

    det JH=PQ+u[b P'Q+a P Q'].                          (2)

If deg P=m and deg Q=n, the highest coefficient in (2) is the product of their leading coefficients times 1+b m+a n. This scalar is strictly positive as an integer. Thus a constant nonzero determinant forces m+n=0, and H=z. This argument does not infer a cancellation sign from complex polynomial coefficients; the integer multiplying their nonzero product cannot vanish.

If exactly one eigenvalue is zero, arrange W=diag(a,0), a!=0. Then H1=x P(y), H2=Q(y), with det JH=P Q'=1. A product of univariate polynomials is a nonzero constant only if both are constants. Normalization gives H=z. The excluded case W=0 is exactly the case where no conclusion is drawn.

These multiplicative cases reconstruct the diagonal part of Shaska2607.20210v2, Section3.2 Theorem3.4. The primary theorem is stronger in allowing nonlinear polynomial changes which linearize the given algebraic actions. We do not acquire those nonlinear changes.

### 2. W is a nonzero nilpotent Jordan block

Scale and conjugate W to [[0,1],[0,0]]. The kernel identity reads

    y H2_x=0,   y H1_x=H2.

Thus H2=q(y); polynomiality implies y divides q, and

    H1=x p(y)+k(y),  H2=y p(y).

The determinant is p(y)[p(y)+y p'(y)]. If deg p=m, its leading coefficient is (m+1) times the square of the leading coefficient of p, which is nonzero in characteristic zero. Constancy forces p constant. The normalization gives p=1, k(0)=k'(0)=0, so

    H=(x+k(y),y),  H^{-1}=(x-k(y),y).

No unproved Jordan-algebra or global flow-completeness property is used. This is a direct polynomial identity argument. It extends the acquired filter to an additive linear symmetry; historical priority of this elementary case is unknown here.

### 3. W=lambda I+N, lambda!=0, N nonzero nilpotent

On homogeneous degree r>=2, the kernel operator is

    (r-1)lambda I + [D_(Nz)-N].

The two operators in brackets commute and are nilpotent: the derivative along the linear nilpotent field is nilpotent on a fixed homogeneous piece, and multiplication by N is nilpotent. Their difference is nilpotent. A nonzero scalar plus a nilpotent operator is invertible by a finite geometric-series formula. Hence every H_r=0 and H=z.

The three cases exhaust all nonzero 2×2 matrices over C. Each supplies an explicit inverse, so properness follows immediately from polynomial invertibility. No appeal to singleton zero implies properness is hidden in the proof.

## Acquisition, actual degree and bit charges

For a rational expanded pair of degree d and maximum coefficient bit length tau, M and M^{-1} require constant-dimensional rational arithmetic. Coefficients of H have bit length O(tau) up to a constant factor from the 2×2 inverse. Expanding JH(z)Wz-WH(z) supplies at most 2 binomial(d+2,2) rows and exactly4 columns. Matrix entries have bit length O(tau+log d). Computing its rank/kernel uses O(d²) arithmetic operations with fixed-size rational minors; polynomial bit cost follows, with no condition number or hidden geometry supplied.

A nonzero complex kernel exists iff a nonzero rational kernel exists for rational H, because rank is unchanged under field extension. Any nonzero rational kernel vector is enough; the algorithm need not search for a semisimple vector. If a kernel exists, the proof's conjugacy may use an algebraic extension of degree at most2 to diagonalize W. The resulting inverse descends to Q because it is the unique formal inverse of rational H. Its degree is at most d in every normal form above. This charge concerns the acquired certificate; no implementation speed advantage over an ordinary inverse test is claimed.

For unspecified complex coefficients this is an exact arithmetic statement, not a finite-bit model. An effective bit bound would need an explicit rational/algebraic coefficient representation.

Under independent constant linear source/target changes G'(z)=T G(Sz), its normalization is H'(z)=S^{-1}H(Sz), and L(H')=S^{-1}L(H)S. Thus vanishing of this kernel is an invariant of the linear coordinate class. Nonlinear source/target changes can alter it; those are outside the acquired four-variable test.

## The residual family supplies no automatic nonzero kernel

Here is an exact countercontrol satisfying the full residual and Keller interface, and already invertible. Set a=x+y² and

    H=(a,y+a³),  G=(y+a³,2a),
    D=[[1+y a²,2y],[a²/2,1]].

Then det D=1, (y,2x)D=G and det JG=-2. H is the composition of two triangular shears, with inverse

    H^{-1}(p,q)=(p-(q-p³)²,q-p³).

For W=[[alpha,beta],[gamma,delta]], four coefficient equations of the kernel defect are

    component1 y^6: -beta=0;
    component1 xy:   2gamma=0;
    component1 y²:  -alpha+2delta=0;
    component2 y^6:  5delta=0.

Their 4×4 determinant is10. Hence L(H)=0. The arbitrary residual class does not force the extra symmetry, even for actual proper Keller maps. This rules out an attempted universal proof based only on inheriting a grading from the three-variable Meng–Yang/Alpöge construction.

The degree-one Bezout growth filter also fails for this map: exact linear coefficient systems for expressing x and y from G with affine-linear multipliers are inconsistent. This is not evidence of nonproperness, since the displayed polynomial inverse proves properness.
