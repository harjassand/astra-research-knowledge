1. HSU, KAKADE, ZHANG (2009/2012), “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models”
Full arXiv HTML, version 6 (6 July 2012):
https://arxiv.org/html/0811.4413v6
Abstract/version record:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413
Journal reference recorded by arXiv: JCSS 78(5), 1460–1480 (2012).

Reconstructed source claims:
- The HMM is represented by symbol operators and sequence probabilities (HTML §§2.1, 3.1–3.2).
- Their Condition 1 requires π>0 coordinatewise and full column rank of the emission matrix O and transition matrix T (HTML §§2.3, lines 104–111).
- Their estimator samples independent triples to estimate P1, P21, and P3,x,1; it takes the m leading left singular vectors of P21 and returns an observable-operator model (HTML §4.1).
- Theorem 6 gives a joint length-t probability error guarantee and sample bound in terms of σ_m(O), σ_m(P21), m, and n0(ε0) (HTML §4.2.1). It is a much smaller moment construction than a complete q^R-by-q^R Hankel block on its full-rank subclass.
- The article notes that knowing m is not essential in practice and suggests cross-validation, but its algorithm input includes m; this is not a theorem proving exact rank selection under a threshold for unknown m (HTML §4.1).
- The observable model is not the original O,T. The paper says its algorithm does not explicitly recover those parameter tables; Appendix C adds a recovery procedure, described as potentially unstable (HTML Introduction footnote and Appendix C).
- The paper explicitly notes that the predicted probabilities of the operator model need not lie in [0,1] (HTML §4.1). Thus “observable representation” does not mean a nonnegative stochastic HMM realization.
- The analysis uses independent sequences initialized from the same law and deliberately uses early observations to avoid mixing concentration (HTML §2.4).

Gap relative to this leaf:
Hsu–Kakade–Zhang do not prove the arbitrary rank≤R complete-prefix Hankel theorem here, do not guarantee exact unknown rank selection from a vanishing-margin class, and do not furnish original HMM parameter recovery from the observable model. Their full-rank and spectral-separation assumptions exclude many singular/nonminimal HMMs included in this leaf. They are an efficiency comparator on the overlapping class, not a theorem subsuming this statement.

2. BALLE AND MOHRI (NeurIPS 2012), “Spectral Learning of General Weighted Automata via Constrained Matrix Completion”
Full author-hosted PDF:
https://cs.nyu.edu/~mohri/pub/swa.pdf
NeurIPS proceedings record:
https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/700fdb2ba62d4554dc268c65add4b16e-Abstract.html

Reconstructed source claims:
- The paper states the finite-Hankel-rank characterization of WFA series and identifies rank as minimal WFA state count, citing Carlyle–Paz and Fliess (PDF §2.1).
- Its spectral method takes a user-specified basis B=(P,S) and desired number of states n. It computes a rank-n truncated SVD and shift operators through pseudoinverses (PDF §§3.2).
- The authors’ Lemma 4 gives stability of the learned operators under Hankel perturbation, with denominators involving the singular gap σ_n(Hε)^2−σ_{n+1}(Hε)^2 and the least retained singular value σ_n. This provides a relevant precedent for explicit margin-conditioned shift recovery (PDF pp. 6–7).
- Their generalization theorem is for labeled strings from a distribution, uses constrained Hankel completion, and assumes bounded labels plus a light tail on string lengths (PDF §4). The authors explicitly note operator error may grow exponentially with string length (PDF §4).

Gap relative to this leaf:
Their theorem does not give unknown-rank threshold selection: n is an algorithm input. Their sample is labeled-string data with a distribution that may be unrelated to the target WFA, not the independent raw-prefix probability experiment used here. The stability lemma informs the perturbation mechanism but is not copied as a claim that our exact reset sampling or universal prefix basis has their generalization guarantee.

3. ARRIVAULT ET AL. (PMLR 2016), “A Toolbox for the Spectral Learning of Weighted Automata”
Full proceedings PDF:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v57/arrivault16.pdf

Reconstructed source claims:
- §2.3 shows the rank factorization H=PS induced by a minimal weighted automaton and states that a complete sub-block gives a rank factorization from which a minimal WA can be recovered.
- The source’s recovery discussion makes explicit the role of a complete basis and pseudoinverse/rank-factorization coordinates.

Gap relative to this leaf:
This is a deterministic/algebraic realization construction given a complete basis. It does not prove that noisy raw trajectories reveal the entries, select an unknown rank, or preserve positivity in SVD coordinates. Our depth-R−1 all-words basis is one explicit finite complete basis under the supplied rank ceiling, with its exponential cost charged.

4. CARLYLE AND PAZ (JCSS 1971), “Realizations by Stochastic Finite Automata”
Publisher record and abstract:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000071800053
DOI: 10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3

The publisher abstract states a finite-rank characterization for string functions and a realization connection to stochastic finite automata / real weighted sequential systems. The algebraic finite-Hankel rank antecedent is relevant to the minimal real WFA target.

Gap:
The publisher record is not a noisy sampling theorem and does not cover statistical rank selection, numerical conditioning, or positivity of the SVD gauge. The proof in this leaf is self-contained for the finite witness block.

5. HO AND KALMAN (1966), “Effective Construction of Linear State-Variable Models from Input/Output Functions”
NASA technical record:
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19670049337
DOI/publisher PDF record:
https://doi.org/10.1524/auto.1966.14.112.545

The NASA record describes the work as an algorithm for minimal realization of a finite-dimensional linear dynamical system from Markov parameters. This is the classical deterministic input/output analogue of finite-Hankel realization.

Gap:
This is not a stochastic HMM learning result and does not supply a noise model, raw-sample concentration, unknown rank selection, finite word horizon for HMM probabilities, or positivity constraints. The source was used only for attribution of the realization lineage.

6. WEDIN (1972), “Perturbation Bounds in Connection with Singular Value Decomposition”
DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01932678

This is the classical singular-subspace perturbation reference behind the Wedin step. The leaf states its own coarse consequence under an absolute operator perturbation η and retained singular margin γ. The remaining explicit pseudoinverse bound is derived in the proof from the Gram inverse identity.
