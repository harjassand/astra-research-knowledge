STRONGEST CONCRETE RESULT

There are explicit finite faithful quantum experiments E_q for which, with a
fixed 0<a<1 and natural logarithms,

 C(E_q)=c(a)<ln 2,
 (1-a) sigma_q <= rho <= (1+a) sigma_q for every rho in E_q,
 delta(E_q)=g(a)/(q+1) -> 0,
 I_acc(uniform prior,E_q)=c(a)/q -> 0,
 e_EB(E_q)=a(1-1/q)/2 -> a/2,

where
 c(a)=[(1+a)ln(1+a)+(1-a)ln(1-a)]/2,
 g(a)=sqrt(1-a^2/2)-(1+sqrt(1-a^2))/2>0.

Thus bounded Holevo capacity does not repair the pair fidelity-affinity
criterion, even under a fixed TWO-SIDED operator likelihood bound and even
when accessible information tends to zero. This strengthens the N69 Pauli
benchmark by adding delta -> 0. It does not contradict N46, whose trace-cover
complexity hypothesis fails here.

Crucial falsification of the tempting stronger conclusion: the SAME family
has b_2 >= a(1-1/sqrt(2))/2>0. An elementary collective sum-of-squares witness
proves this for every flagged Clifford-clique construction, regardless of
flag geometry or clique size. Therefore these are not counterexamples to
the unresolved mixed bounded-Holevo + b_2 -> 0 target.

For the smaller perfect-matching family, b_2 is EXACTLY
 a(1-1/sqrt(2))/2,
while e_EB=a/4 and delta=g(a)/(n-1). Its exact full-domain broadcaster is a
tensor product of unital real-plane qubit cloners, with identical output
flags. No efficiency or independent-clone assertion accompanies these bounds.

The mechanism also supplies two exact functional consequences:

 * Pairwise normalized Hilbert-Schmidt commutators vanish, but the minimum
   uniform distance to a simultaneously commuting family is
   sqrt(1-1/q) -> 1.
 * If the unknown index i is revealed AFTER encoding the input, retaining
   the quantum state guesses its sign with success (1+a)/2; every pre-index
   classical measurement, with unrestricted alphabet, has optimal success
   1/2+a/(2sqrt(q)) -> 1/2.

These are scoped boundary theorems, not a claimed field-breaking discovery.

1. EXACT INTERFACE AND CONSTRUCTION

For a finite experiment E={rho_theta} on a finite H, use

 T(rho,tau)=||rho-tau||_1/2,
 delta(E)=sup_rho,tau [||sqrt(rho)sqrt(tau)||_1
                         -Tr sqrt(rho)sqrt(tau)],
 e_EB(E)=inf_{measure-and-prepare Lambda:H->H} sup_theta T(Lambda rho_theta,rho_theta),
 b_K(E)=inf_{CPTP B:H->H^tensor K} sup_{theta,k} T(B_k rho_theta,rho_theta),
 C(E)=sup_prior [S(sum_theta pi_theta rho_theta)-sum_theta pi_theta S(rho_theta)].

One channel must work for every unknown member on the full input algebra.
All output correlations, entanglement, flag changes, and output coherences
are permitted. No additional copies, side quantum registers, preshared
entanglement, purification preservation, or independent output copies.

General clique version. Let r>=2 divide n. Choose a finite list of partitions
P_f of {1,...,n} into n/r groups of size r. A local space C^h carries r
traceless anticommuting Hermitian involutions Gamma_1,...,Gamma_r; take
h=2^floor(r/2). For each partition flag f, put one independent C^h tensor
factor on every group. The within-group assignment of labels to Gamma_j is
fixed and known. Set

 H=direct_sum_f (C^h)^tensor(n/r),  D=|F| h^(n/r), sigma=I_D/D,
 A_i=direct_sum_f [Gamma assigned to i on its group, I on other groups],
 rho_{i,s}=(I_D+s a A_i)/D,  i=1,...,n, s in {-1,+1}.

These are finite faithful density matrices. Each A_i is a traceless
involution and tau(A_i A_j)=delta_ij, where tau(X)=Tr(X)/D.
Every state has eigenvalues (1+a)/D,(1-a)/D, each multiplicity D/2.

Minimal matching version. n is any even integer, r=2, h=2, and the flags
are a 1-factorization of K_n, so |F|=n-1. For an ordered edge {i,j}, assign
X to i and Z to j on its qubit. D=(n-1)2^(n/2).

Growing-clique balanced version. Let q be any prime power, n=q^d with d>=2,
r=q. Labels are points of F_q^d. Flags are one-dimensional directions,
|F|=(n-1)/(q-1), and each partition consists of parallel affine lines.
Every pair of labels is in the same group for exactly one flag. For the
displayed limit choose d=2, hence n=q^2 and |F|=q+1. All asymptotic statements
hold through prime powers q->infinity; the diagnostics use primes only.

Costs: these are explicit mathematical state/channel descriptions, not a
materialization of the large Hilbert spaces. The register has
 log_2 D=log_2 |F|+(n/r)floor(r/2) qubits-equivalent dimension.
There are 2n unknown states. At q->infinity,n=q^2 the dimension is enormous,
approximately (q+1)2^(q^2/2). A supplied abstract state from E is the input;
acquiring/preparing it and acquiring an apparatus are not free capabilities
proved here. Classical comparators below may use arbitrary POVMs/alphabet.

2. THE ONE LOAD-BEARING NUMERICAL-RANGE LEMMA

Reusable interface before specializing. For ANY finite list of traceless
Hermitian involutions A_1,...,A_n on C^D, define the collective quantities

 mu=(1/n) sup_{state eta} sum_i [Tr(eta A_i)]^2,
 nu_2=(1/(2n)) ||sum_i A_i^T tensor
                         (A_i tensor I+I tensor A_i)||_infinity.

For the full-domain sign experiment rho_{i,s}=(I+s a A_i)/D, the proofs
below, without Clifford, orthogonality, or graph assumptions, give

 C=c(a),
 e_EB >= a(1-mu)/2,
 I_acc(uniform prior) <= mu c(a),
 inf_{commuting Hermitian C_i} max_i ||A_i-C_i||_(2,tau)
                                      >= sqrt(1-mu),
 optimal pre-index classical sign success <= 1/2+a sqrt(mu)/2,
 b_2 >= a(1-nu_2)/2.

For clarity these are certificates, not converses. The EB inequality follows
by writing a measure-and-prepare channel and applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the
two expectation vectors of its normalized effect and prepared state. The
accessible-information inequality follows from c(av)<=v^2 c(a). Dephasing
in a common eigenbasis proves the commuting-distance inequality. The late
index inequality uses sum_i |v_i|<=sqrt(n sum_i v_i^2). The Choi expectation
and the two signed trace tests prove the broadcasting inequality exactly as
in section 5. Traceless involutions alone give the common state entropy and
the capacity identity. No growing-dimensional Choi optimization is asserted.

These quantities express aggregate incompatibility missed by an individual
pair diagnostic. They are robust under uniform operator-norm perturbations:
if A'_i are also traceless Hermitian involutions and max_i||A'_i-A_i||<=xi,
then sqrt(mu')<=sqrt(mu)+xi and nu'_2<=nu_2+2xi, by the Euclidean and operator
triangle inequalities. The premise here is operator norm; a normalized
Hilbert-Schmidt perturbation does not uniformly control state expectations.
For any two experiments on the same labels with sup_theta T(rho'_theta,
rho_theta)<=epsilon, contractivity also gives |e'_EB-e_EB|<=2epsilon and
|b'_2-b_2|<=2epsilon. Thus the lower bounds survive an explicit trace-error
margin, independently of dimension and cardinality.

Numerical-range radii and the two-receiver monogamy mechanism are established
objects. The reusable contribution here is the exact common interface and
the balanced flagged packing realizing mu=1/r while its every pair gap
vanishes. Historical novelty of this combination remains unassessed.

For every state eta on H, let v_i=Tr(eta A_i). Then

                    sum_i v_i^2 <= n/r.                 (NR)

Proof. Pinching the classical flags does not change v. On one flag, restrict
eta to one group's marginal. If its Gamma expectations are u_j, the operator
sum_j u_j Gamma_j has norm ||u||_2, since its square is ||u||_2^2 I. Its
expectation in that marginal is ||u||_2^2, so ||u||_2<=1. Summing over n/r
groups gives (NR). Mixtures over flags preserve the bound by convexity of
the squared Euclidean norm. This proof includes every state, not only product
states, not only diagonal states, and not only one flag.

Also, for every sign vector x in {-1,+1}^n,

                  ||sum_i x_i A_i||_infinity = n/sqrt(r). (SN)

On each group, the signed sum has norm sqrt(r) with both extreme signs.
Different groups are tensor factors, so simultaneous extreme eigenvectors
make the norms add. Every flag therefore has the same norm n/sqrt(r).

3. EXACT HOLEVO CAPACITY, PAIR GAP AND ACCESSIBLE INFORMATION

Capacity. All member entropies equal ln D-c(a). Entropy of the average is
at most ln D, and the uniform-sign average is exactly sigma. Hence
C(E)=c(a). The fixed two-sided likelihood bound is immediate.

Pair gap. Different labels in different groups commute. Within one group,
two Gamma generators form a Pauli X/Z algebra tensored with multiplicity.
For the associated qubit states (I+aX)/2,(I+/-aZ)/2, root fidelity is
sqrt(1-a^2/2), whereas affinity is (1+sqrt(1-a^2))/2. The signs do not change
the gap. Block direct sums with common flag probabilities add both root
fidelity and affinity. Therefore

 delta(E)=g(a) max_{i!=j} Pr_f[i,j belong to the same group].

For 1-factorizations this is g(a)/(n-1). For affine partitions this is
g(a)(q-1)/(n-1), or g(a)/(q+1) in the affine plane. Same-index opposite-sign
states commute. The square-root-fidelity convention is essential.

Trace-cover obstruction is explicit. For distinct labels i,j and any signs,
put p=Pr_f[same group]. On a shared group, |s A_i-t A_j|=sqrt(2)I; on
different factors its normalized trace is 1. Hence

 T(rho_{i,s},rho_{j,t})=(a/2)[1+p(sqrt(2)-1)] >= a/2.

Opposite signs of the same label have distance a. Every trace ball of
radius epsilon<a/4 therefore covers at most one of the 2n members, even
if its center is outside the experiment. The covering number is at least
2n at every such fixed radius. Thus no uniform trace-cover hypothesis from
N46 applies; bounded Holevo and fixed two-sided domination do not supply it.

Accessible information. This exact value concerns the UNIFORM prior on 2n
states, not the capacity-optimized measurement/prior pair. For a POVM effect
M_y with p_y=Tr(M_y)/D and eta_y=M_y/Tr(M_y), put v_i(y)=Tr(eta_y A_i).
The posterior is (1+s a v_i(y))/(2n), so

 I((i,s):Y)=sum_y p_y [1/n sum_i c(a v_i(y))].

For |v|<=1, c(av)<=v^2 c(a). One proof expands
c(z)=sum_{k>=1} z^(2k)/[2k(2k-1)]. Applying (NR) gives I<=c(a)/r.
Attainment: measure the flag and, in each group, any one chosen Gamma
projectively. Each normalized effect has expectation +/-1 for that chosen
label and 0 for the other r-1 generators. Thus I_acc=c(a)/r exactly.

4. EXACT EB ERROR — ARBITRARY CROSS-FLAG CHANNELS INCLUDED

Let Lambda be an arbitrary EB channel,
 Lambda(rho)=sum_y Tr(M_y rho) omega_y.
For any member, T(Lambda rho_{i,s},rho_{i,s}) is at least
(s/2)Tr[A_i(rho_{i,s}-Lambda rho_{i,s})]. Averaging over i and s gives

 e(Lambda) >= (a/2) [1-(1/n) sum_i tau(A_i Lambda(A_i))].

For each outcome y, both eta_y=M_y/Tr(M_y) and omega_y satisfy (NR).
Consequently

 sum_i tau(A_i Lambda(A_i))
   =sum_y tau(M_y) sum_i Tr(eta_y A_i)Tr(omega_y A_i)
   <=sum_y tau(M_y) n/r =n/r.

Thus every EB channel has worst-member error at least a(1-1/r)/2. No
condition on flag changes, output coherences, product preparation, or POVM
structure entered the lower bound.

Attainment. First read flag f. On every local group, choose j uniformly in
{1,...,r}, measure Gamma_j, and prepare its normalized +/- eigenspace
projector. This local measure-and-prepare channel fixes I/h and maps Gamma_i
to Gamma_i/r. Its product over groups, followed by preparation of the same
flag f, is EB on the entire full input algebra and sends rho_{i,s} to
(I+s(a/r)A_i)/D. Its uniform trace error is exactly a(1-1/r)/2.

5. COLLECTIVE BROADCASTING SOS — WHY THE COUNTEREXAMPLE ATTEMPT FAILS

This is the reusable structural certificate. The following identity was
derived by the large-clique-broadcast worker and independently checked by
this worker and the matching-classicality auditor.

Let G_1,G_2 be anticommuting Hermitian involutions on an input system, and
let B_1,B_2 and C_1,C_2 be arbitrary Hermitian involutions on two DIFFERENT
output systems. No commutation relation within B or within C is required.
All input operators commute with output operators, and B's commute with C's.
Define

 K_pair=G_1(B_1+C_1)+G_2(B_2+C_2),
 F=iG_1G_2,
 M=(B_1-C_1)+i F(B_2-C_2).

Then exactly

                  8I-K_pair^2=M^*M >=0.                (SOS)

To check signs: put D_j=B_j+C_j,E_j=B_j-C_j. Then
K_pair^2=D_1^2+D_2^2-iF[D_1,D_2],
M^*M=E_1^2+E_2^2+iF[E_1,E_2],
D_j^2+E_j^2=4I, and [D_1,D_2]=[E_1,E_2]. Thus ||K_pair||<=2sqrt(2).

For r>=2 anticommuting G_i, average this over every pair:

 sum_i G_i(B_i+C_i)
  =(r-1)^(-1) sum_{i<j} [G_i(B_i+C_i)+G_j(B_j+C_j)],
 ||sum_i G_i(B_i+C_i)|| <= r sqrt(2).                 (CL)

Now let B:H->H tensor H be ANY proposed broadcaster. Write its ordinary
Choi operator J_B, so J_B>=0 and Tr J_B=D. Set

 K=sum_i A_i^T tensor (A_i tensor I+I tensor A_i).

Its expectation in the trace-one state J_B/D is

 Tr[(J_B/D)K]=sum_i [tau(A_i B_1(A_i))+tau(A_i B_2(A_i))].

K is block diagonal in the input flag labels. On every input flag block,
split its labels into n/r input groups. Every such group's transposed scores
are anticommuting. The output scores are arbitrary involutions on their
respective output systems, so (CL) applies irrespective of output flags.
Triangle inequality over input groups gives ||K||<=n sqrt(2).

If worst-member/worst-marginal error is b, the two sign tests for each i,
after cancelling the channel's output on sigma, imply
tau(A_i B_k(A_i))>=1-2b/a. Therefore

 2n(1-2b/a)<=Tr[(J_B/D)K]<=n sqrt(2),
                   b>=a(1-1/sqrt(2))/2.

This proof permits arbitrary output flag reorganization and coherences:
block diagonality belongs to the witness K, not to the admissible Choi state.
It permits all entangled/correlated outputs. It works for arbitrary partition
lists, not only the affine geometry. Thus growing clique size is NOT a way
to obtain b_2->0 in this mechanism.

Broad extension (derived by the large-clique-broadcast worker). The same
broadcast lower bound holds for rho_{i,s}=tau_0(I+s a A_i), where tau_0 is
ANY density matrix commuting with all A_i. Neither faithfulness nor a flat
flag distribution is required. In each input sector the labels need only
partition into anticommuting cliques, possibly unequal sizes, each at least
two; tensor factorization is unnecessary. In each sector, a clique partner
and commutation with tau_0's diagonal block give zero A_i expectation;
summing sectors implies Tr(tau_0 A_i)=0. The states are normalized and their
target score is s a. With the unnormalized Choi matrix J, use

 omega=((tau_0^T)^(1/2) tensor I)J((tau_0^T)^(1/2) tensor I).

It is positive with trace one by trace preservation. Because [tau_0,A_i]=0,
Tr[omega(A_i^T tensor A_i^(k))]=Tr[A_i B_k(tau_0 A_i)]. Sign subtraction
again gives each score >=1-2b/a. The input-clique SOS still bounds the same
K by n sqrt(2), proving the lower bound. This extension is a broadcasting
obstruction only: the displayed exact capacity, EB, accessible-information,
and Hilbert-Schmidt formulas above use the stated flat tensor construction.

6. EXACT MATCHING BROADCASTER AND AN ALTERNATIVE JOINT-MEASUREMENT LOWER

For r=2 the bound in section 5 is tight. An explicit local symmetric clone
for the X,Z plane has unital marginals
 Phi(I)=I, Phi(X)=X/sqrt(2), Phi(Z)=Z/sqrt(2), Phi(Y)=Y/2.

Construction. In the computational basis set
 V|0>=|00>, V|1>=(|01>+|10>)/sqrt(2).
Let K_0=V/sqrt(2), K_1=(X tensor X)V X/sqrt(2). Their squares sum to I.
This mixture of two isometries has symmetric UNITAL marginals and shrinks
the X,Y plane by 1/sqrt(2). With R=(I-iX)/sqrt(2), replace the Kraus operators
by (R tensor R)K_j R^*, rotating the preserved plane to X,Z. Tensor this
channel over every input matching edge, measure the input flag, and put that
flag in BOTH output registers. Because the spectator maximally mixed factors
are fixed, every marginal is exactly (I+s a A_i/sqrt(2))/D. Hence
b_2=a(1-1/sqrt(2))/2. Merely using an unsymmetrized economical cloner would
give a translated spectator state and would not prove attainment.

Alternative lower useful beyond the Choi method. For every pair of output
matching flags f,g, their union consists of even alternating cycles (shared
edges cause no problem). Choose either complementary 2-coloring uniformly.
In output 1, measure label observables of color 0; in output 2, measure color
1. Each chosen set is commuting within its receiver. The simultaneous +/-1
outcomes have first moment for each i exactly
 (A_i tensor I+I tensor A_i)/2.
Pull this joint POVM back through an arbitrary broadcaster. The signed-sum
bound (SN), with r=2, constrains its average-sign score by n/sqrt(2), while
trace-accurate marginals require score >=n(1-2b/a). This reproduces the exact
lower bound and is a direct operational incompatibility certificate.

More generally, for r output partitions, the bipartite graph between labels
and slots (receiver,group) is r-regular on n+n vertices. A decomposition into
r perfect matchings assigns each label once to each receiver and at most one
label to every group. The corresponding group measurements, averaged over
the matchings, have moments r^(-1)sum_k A_i^(k). Therefore b_r>=
a(1-1/sqrt(r))/2. The weaker b_2 lower obtained by iterating broadcasts loses
ceil(log_2 r); section 5 removes that loss. This is a combinatorial certificate,
not an assertion that output quantum copies are independent.

7. STRONGER SIMULTANEOUS COMMUTING OBSTRUCTION

For these scores,

 inf_{pairwise commuting Hermitian C_i} max_i ||A_i-C_i||_(2,tau)
                     =sqrt(1-1/r),
 max_{i!=j} ||[A_i,A_j]||_(2,tau)=2 sqrt(max_{i!=j} Pr_f[same group]).

Lower proof: dephase in a common eigenbasis of all C_i. Conditional
expectation P(A_i) is the best Hilbert-Schmidt diagonal approximant.
The average squared residual is at least
1-(1/n)sum_i tau(P(A_i)^2). For each common basis vector, (NR) bounds the
sum of squared diagonal entries by n/r. Hence the residual is at least
1-1/r and its maximum is at least this average.

Upper proof: in each group put B_G=(sum_i Gamma_i)/sqrt(r), an involution,
and let every C_i in that group be B_G/sqrt(r). All C_i commute, and every
residual squared is 1-1/r. Direct sums over flags preserve this equality.

This uses normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm, not operator norm: the operator
norm of a nonzero pair commutator is 2. For fixed r and growing n this gives
an explicit obstruction to cardinality-independent simultaneous tracial
almost-commuting stability. It does not contradict fixed-family results.

8. A CONCRETE QUANTUM-STATISTICAL DECISION TASK

Input: one member rho_{i,s}, with (i,s) uniform. The encoder does not know i
or s. After encoding, the index i is supplied as classical query information.
The decoder must guess s. Every method receives the same post-encoding
index. No extra quantum input copies and no quantum side assistance.

Quantum retention stores the original register and measures A_i after the
query. Helstrom optimal success is (1+a)/2. A purely classical encoding is
an arbitrary POVM {M_y}; its decoder may use unlimited classical storage and
any answer b_i(y) in [-1,1]. Its sign correlation is
(a/n)sum_y tau(M_y) sum_i v_i(y)b_i(y)
<= (a/n)sum_y tau(M_y) sum_i |v_i(y)| <= a/sqrt(r)
by (NR) and Cauchy-Schwarz. Thus success<=1/2+a/(2sqrt(r)).

Attain this by reading the flag and measuring B_G=(sum_i Gamma_i)/sqrt(r)
on every group. Report the group's measured sign for any subsequently
queried label in it. The exact coefficient is a/sqrt(r). The required record
has at most log_2 |F|+n/r bits after the fixed measurement choice, whereas
quantum retention has log_2 D qubits-equivalent dimension. Unlimited extra
classical record cannot improve its success bound.

This is the established postmeasurement-information/Clifford-encoding type
of advantage. The boundary contribution is that it survives alongside
delta->0, bounded Holevo, fixed likelihood bounds, and vanishing accessible
information in the flagged family. It is not an algorithmic superintelligence
or efficient compression assertion.

9. GLOBAL MIXED TARGET: PRECISE REMAINING BRIDGE AND A CAUTION

The target from N69 is
 C(E_n)<=C<infinity and b_2(E_n)->0  ==>  e_EB(E_n)->0
with arbitrary growing dimensions/cardinalities. It remains OPEN here.
Neither small intrinsic pair gap nor measured residuals suffice, by sections
3-4. The collective Choi/SOS witness is an obstruction useful for testing
counterexamples, not a proof for general families.

One candidate reduction uses the standard Holevo relative-entropy center
sigma with sup_theta D(rho_theta||sigma)<=C and the Quantum Substate Theorem.
For any eta in (0,1), each member has an individual approximant rho'_theta
with T(rho'_theta,rho_theta)<=eta and
 rho'_theta<=M(C,eta)sigma,
 M(C,eta)=(1-eta^2)^(-1) exp[(C+ln 2)/eta^2].
The bound follows from Jain–Nayak Theorem 1 with squared fidelity >=1-eta^2
and observational divergence <=relative entropy in bits+1.

Define G_M(b) as the supremal EB error among all finite experiments dominated
by M times a common state and admitting b_2<=b. Then the VALID reduction is

 e_EB(E)<=2eta+G_{M(C,eta)}(b_2(E)+2eta).

A merely qualitative fixed-M theorem G_M(b)->0 DOES NOT close the general
bounded-Holevo implication: inherited broadcast error has a 2eta floor, and
M grows like exp(C/eta^2) as eta goes to zero. A quantitative bound with
adequate joint M,b dependence or common smoothing that preserves broadcasting
would be required. My initial suggestion that qualitative domination alone
would close the bridge was corrected explicitly during the run.

Next decisive question: can a trace-distance EB minimax witness force a
dimension-independent collective broadcast friction using ONLY bounded
information radius? The frame witness above does so for Clifford families,
even when every fixed pair hides the friction, but no argument extends it
to an arbitrary mixed experiment here. A counterexample must evade collective
monogamy as well as keep information capacity bounded; ordinary almost
commuting or data-hiding constructions do not do this.
