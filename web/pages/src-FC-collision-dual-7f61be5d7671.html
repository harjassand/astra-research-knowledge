I developed a concrete sampling construction: for a restricted nonlinear Boltzmann model, it generates a particle velocity at an arbitrarily large collision time with finite expected work independent of that time, without replacing the answer by equilibrium.
The construction combines a finite marked collision genealogy, a bounded signed density estimator, and an established Bernoulli-factory algorithm. This is a mathematical research result, not an established civilisation-changing capability. I considered several different operations before retaining the collision problem.
Exact local correction of AI-generated molecular samples. The proposed operation was to repair only the portions of a generated configuration responsible for violating the target distribution, rather than reject the entire configuration. The central mechanism substantially overlaps with partial rejection sampling. A meaningful advance would require proving that physically important molecular distributions admit inexpensive local repair—not assuming that a learned representation makes their residual dependencies favourable. I did not establish that missing relationship. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07744)
Stable differentiation through chaotic dynamics. The proposed operation was to suppress unstable trajectory separation while compensating exactly for the intervention through the driving noise. This led to close prior art: Fang and Giles already introduce spring coupling and importance sampling to replace exponential-in-time variance growth of a standard sensitivity estimator by linear growth in their setting. I did not retain that mechanism as an invention. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12160)
Sampling long-time nonlinear collision dynamics without reconstructing their history. Here the obstruction was explicit: a standard exact backward representation branches into a collision ancestry whose expected size grows exponentially with elapsed time. The promising question was not how to simulate those ancestors faster, but whether the output could be generated while integrating almost all of them out.
There are already strong alternatives. Time-relaxed Monte Carlo methods use recursive collision representations and treatments of high-order terms involving equilibrium; Maxwell models also possess tractable moment hierarchies. Any claimed advance must be compared with those methods, not merely with naive time stepping. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1009.2768v1)
The operation evolved through three stages:
\[ \text{full collision history} \longrightarrow \text{finite marked dependencies} \longrightarrow \text{bounded signed density information} \longrightarrow \text{an actual sample}. \]
The last transition is essential. An inexpensive observable estimator alone would not complete the sampling task.
Let
\[ G_s(v)=(2\pi s)^{-3/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|v|^2}{2s}\right), \qquad v\in\mathbb R^3. \]
The initial velocity distribution is a centered Gaussian scale mixture:
\[ f_0(v)=\int_a^b G_s(v)\,\nu_0(ds). \]
The supplied information is a known interval \(0<a<b\), an independent sampler for the initial mixing variable \(V_0\sim\nu_0\), and a requested time \(t\ge0\). An explicit finite mixture satisfies this input contract. Its moments and evolved distribution are not supplied.
The collision model is the spatially homogeneous, constant-rate, isotropic-cutoff Maxwell equation. I fix its normalization through the Fourier equation
\[ \partial_t\phi_t(k) = \int_{S^2} \left[ \phi_t(k^+)\phi_t(k^-)-\phi_t(k) \right]\frac{d\sigma}{4\pi}, \qquad k^\pm=\frac{k\pm |k|\sigma}{2}. \]
This belongs to an established kinetic-model family; the general Maxwell moment structure is not a new result. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/0903.1054v2)
For the stated initial distributions, the equation closes on scalar mixing laws:
\[ f_t(v)=\mathbb E\,G_{V_t}(v), \]
where
\[ \partial_t\nu_t = \operatorname{Law}\!\left(BV_t^{(1)}+(1-B)V_t^{(2)}\right)-\nu_t, \]
and \(B\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]\), independently of two copies of \(V_t\).
To see the closure, use
\[ |k^+|^2+|k^-|^2=|k|^2. \]
Uniform angular measure in three dimensions makes the corresponding fraction of squared Fourier magnitude uniform on \([0,1]\). Products of Gaussian characteristic functions therefore become convex averages of their scalar variances.
The output is one velocity distributed according to \(f_t\). Independent executions give independent one-particle samples. This is not a physical particle trajectory, a correlated many-particle state, or a spatially resolved gas simulation.
A complete backward collision tree represents the latent variance as
\[ V_t=\sum_j w_j V_{0,j}, \qquad w_j\ge0,\quad \sum_jw_j=1. \]
Each active branch splits at rate one. Its expected leaf count is therefore \(e^t\).
Instead of constructing the entire tree, normalize
\[ P_t=\frac{b-V_t}{b-a}\in[0,1] \]
and consider the order-\(k\) quantity
\[ m_k(t)=\mathbb E[P_t^k]. \]
The scalar collision equation gives
\[ m_0=1,\qquad m_1'=0, \]
and, for \(k\ge2\),
\[ m_k' = -\frac{k-1}{k+1}m_k + \frac{1}{k+1} \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}m_jm_{k-j}. \tag{1} \]
The coefficient follows from the elementary identity
\[ \binom{k}{j}\int_0^1u^j(1-u)^{k-j}\,du=\frac1{k+1}. \]
Start with one block containing \(k\) marks and remaining horizon \(t\).
A block of size \(q\ge2\) waits an exponential time with rate
\[ \lambda_q=\frac{q-1}{q+1}. \]
If the waiting time exceeds its remaining horizon, it becomes terminal. Otherwise, choose \(j\) uniformly from \(1,\ldots,q-1\), split into blocks \(j\) and \(q-j\), and continue both with the reduced horizon. A singleton is terminal immediately.
At every terminal block of size \(q\), draw an independent \(P_0\) and contribute \(P_0^q\). Let the product be \(H_{k,t}\), with \(H_{0,t}=1\).
For all \(k,t\),
\[ \boxed{ 0\le H_{k,t}\le1, \qquad \mathbb E H_{k,t}=m_k(t). } \]
For \(k\ge1\), an execution uses at most
\[ \boxed{ k-1\text{ effective splits},\quad 2k-1\text{ visited nodes},\quad k\text{ initial draws}. } \]
These bounds do not depend on \(t\).
Proof. Conditioning on the first effective split produces exactly equation (1), with the correct initial condition. Uniqueness follows successively in \(k\), since the equation for \(m_k\) involves only lower-order moments and a linear term in \(m_k\). Every split increases the number of positive-size blocks by one while their sizes still sum to \(k\), so there can be at most \(k-1\) splits.
The underlying interpretation explains why this works. Put \(k\) mass-biased marks on a shared full collision tree. A collision at which all marks choose the same child does not separate the information being requested. Such events can be integrated out. Only separating events remain.
Shared ancestry cannot be discarded. Replacing the marks by independent initial draws gives the wrong distribution.
The marked process already gives inexpensive unbiased observable estimates. For example, with
\[ K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(z(b-a)), \]
the quantity
\[ Y_z=e^{-az}H_{K,t} \]
satisfies
\[ \mathbb E Y_z=\mathbb E e^{-zV_t}, \qquad 0\le Y_z\le e^{-az}, \qquad \mathbb E K=z(b-a). \]
But this is still an estimator, not a velocity sample.
The stronger step is to construct a signed estimate of a density ratio with a deterministic bound valid for every velocity and every time.
Assume now
\[ 0<a<b<\frac32a. \tag{2} \]
Choose
\[ r=1-\frac ab, \qquad r<\rho<\frac{2a}{b}-1, \qquad g=b(1+\rho), \]
and define
\[ C= \frac{\left(\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\right)^{3/2}} {1-r/\rho}. \tag{3} \]
Use \(G_g\) as the proposal density.
The standard generalized Laguerre generating function is
\[ (1-u)^{-3/2} \exp\!\left(-\frac{xu}{1-u}\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L_k^{1/2}(x)u^k. \]
This identity is established prior mathematics, not a new polynomial representation. [DLMF](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.12)
Putting \(x=|v|^2/(2b)\) gives
\[ f_t(v) = G_b(v)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} r^kL_k^{1/2}(x)m_k(t). \tag{4} \]
Draw a geometric degree,
\[ \Pr(K=k)=(1-r/\rho)(r/\rho)^k, \qquad k=0,1,\ldots, \]
and evaluate one marked process \(H_{K,t}\). Return
\[ W(v) = \frac{G_b(v)}{G_g(v)} \frac{\rho^K L_K^{1/2}(x)H_{K,t}} {1-r/\rho}. \tag{5} \]
Then
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb E W(v)=\frac{f_t(v)}{G_g(v)}, \qquad |W(v)|\le C. } \tag{6} \]
The expected degree is
\[ \mathbb E K=\frac{r}{\rho-r}, \]
again independent of time.
On the complex circle \(|u|=\rho\),
\[ |1-u|\ge1-\rho, \qquad \min\Re\frac{u}{1-u}=-\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}. \]
Cauchy’s coefficient estimate therefore gives
\[ |\rho^kL_k^{1/2}(x)| \le (1-\rho)^{-3/2} e^{x\rho/(1+\rho)}. \]
Meanwhile,
\[ \frac{G_b(v)}{G_g(v)} = (1+\rho)^{3/2} e^{-x\rho/(1+\rho)}. \]
The velocity-dependent exponential factors cancel. This proves the uniform bound in (6), rather than merely observing bounded values on a numerical grid.
The geometric probabilities cancel the series weights in expectation, proving the identity in (6).
A signed density estimate cannot simply be treated as an acceptance probability. That would generally be invalid.
Here an existing probability-conversion algorithm supplies the missing step.
Write
\[ R(v)=\frac{f_t(v)}{G_g(v)}. \]
The initial support constraint gives the explicit lower bound
\[ R(v)\ge \ell(v) := \left(\frac gb\right)^{3/2} \exp\!\left[ -\frac{|v|^2}{2}\left(\frac1a-\frac1g\right) \right]>0. \tag{7} \]
From a fresh realization of \(W(v)\), generate a coin with conditional probability
\[ \frac{1-W(v)/C}{2}. \]
This is a legitimate probability because \(|W|\le C\). Its unconditional mean is
\[ p(v)=\frac{1-R(v)/C}{2}. \]
Consequently,
\[ 2p(v)\le1-\ell(v)/C. \]
Huber’s linear Bernoulli factory converts independent \(p\)-coins into an \(Ap\)-coin when a positive margin \(Ap\le1-\epsilon\) is known. Its expected input-coin count is at most \(9.5A/\epsilon\). This theorem is a credited, load-bearing prior result. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.1562v2)
Apply it with
\[ A=2,\qquad \epsilon=\ell(v)/C. \]
Complementing its output yields an acceptance coin with mean
\[ \frac{R(v)}{C} = \frac{f_t(v)}{C\,G_g(v)}. \]
Thus the complete algorithm is ordinary rejection sampling, except that its acceptance probability is generated without evaluating \(f_t\):
\[ v\sim G_g \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{bounded signed queries} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{exact acceptance coin}. \]
The accepted density is exactly \(f_t\) in the ideal-arithmetic model.
Define
\[ I= \left[\frac{ab}{g(2a-g)}\right]^{3/2}. \]
The condition on \(\rho\) ensures \(g<2a\), and direct Gaussian integration gives
\[ \int_{\mathbb R^3}\frac{G_g(v)}{\ell(v)}\,dv=I<\infty. \]
Conditional on a proposal \(v\), the expected number of signed-estimator calls is at most \(19C/\ell(v)\). The overall proposal acceptance probability is \(1/C\).
Therefore,
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{t\ge0} \mathbb E[N_{\mathrm{signed}}] \le19C^2I. } \tag{8} \]
If an initial variance draw has expected cost at most \(c_0\), then an ideal implementation has
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb E[\mathrm{work}] = O\!\left( C+ 19C^2I \left[1+\frac{r}{\rho-r}\right](1+c_0) \right), } \tag{9} \]
uniformly in elapsed collision time.
This includes marked-tree work, polynomial evaluation, rejection attempts and Bernoulli-factory work. It is an expected, not worst-case, bound.
The important distinction is:
The algorithm does not approximate a long-time distribution by equilibrium. Its ideal output retains the actual time-dependent law, while its expected operation count does not grow with that time.
Condition (2) is a sufficient condition for this particular envelope and cost proof. It is not a proof that exact finite-mean sampling is impossible outside that interval.
For arbitrary \(0<a<b\), without the narrower condition (2), there is a simpler approximation.
Start \(n\) marks. If the terminal block sizes are \(q_j\), draw independent \(V_{0,j}\) and set
\[ \widehat V_{n,t} = \frac1n\sum_jq_jV_{0,j}, \qquad \widehat X_{n,t}\mid\widehat V_{n,t} \sim N(0,\widehat V_{n,t}I_3). \]
Then
\[ \boxed{ d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left( \mathcal L(\widehat X_{n,t}),f_t \right) \le \frac{\sqrt{15/2}}{4a^2n} \operatorname{Var}(V_0)(1-e^{-t/3}) } \]
and hence
\[ \boxed{ d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left( \mathcal L(\widehat X_{n,t}),f_t \right) \le \frac{\sqrt{15/2}}{16n} \left(\frac{b-a}{a}\right)^2. } \tag{10} \]
Each output uses at most \(n-1\) effective splits.
The proof is a distributional argument, not a moment-matching assertion. Conditional on the complete weighted tree, the marked average is unbiased for \(V_t\), with
\[ \mathbb E(\widehat V_{n,t}-V_t)^2 = \frac{\operatorname{Var}(V_0)(1-e^{-t/3})}{n}. \]
Also,
\[ \|\partial_s^2G_s\|_1\le\frac{\sqrt{15/2}}{s^2}. \]
A Taylor expansion cancels the first-order term after conditional expectation; integrating the second-order remainder proves (10).
This broader sampler is useful, but it must not be confused with the exact rejection construction. Increasing \(n\) reduces its approximation error; it does not make a fixed-\(n\) sample exact.

The Wild representation, moment hierarchies, Laguerre generating function, randomized-series ideas and Bernoulli factory are not new components. The candidate contribution is their particular composition into a finite-dependency, all-time sampler with the integrated cost bound.
The literature examined did not establish that this composition is already known, but that is not sufficient to establish priority. Neither selective repository searches nor the numerical diagnostics constitute novelty clearance.
Initial-data acquisition. The algorithm assumes an actual sampler for a bounded scalar mixing distribution. An explicit mixture table provides one. General measured velocity data do not automatically provide it, and learning that latent representation is not free.
There is an initial-data robustness result: if two mixing laws on the same interval differ by at most \(\eta\) in Wasserstein-1 distance, then their evolved velocity laws satisfy
\[ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(f_t,\widetilde f_t) \le \frac{\sqrt{3/2}}{2a}\eta \]
uniformly in time. This controls a specified mixing-law error; it does not certify the model itself.
Precision. The theorem assumes ideal random primitives and real arithmetic. The executable uses NumPy/SciPy floating-point operations.
A floating-point output distribution is discrete. Its literal total-variation distance from a continuous target density is therefore one. Consequently, the continuous-law theorem must not be attached directly to the executable. A completed finite-machine result needs a quantized-target or observation-noise specification, certified decisions and a charged bit-complexity analysis. Those remain unfinished.
Timing and energy. The theorem counts ideal operations, not physical joules. Input representation, large integers inside the factory, increasingly precise comparisons and hardware energy are not silently included in a constant-time assumption. No physical energy advantage has been established.
Physical actuation. This is a classical sampling algorithm. It neither prepares a gas in the target state nor supplies a new cooling or materials-processing actuator.
Scientific scope. Spatial transport, anisotropic distributions, velocity-dependent hard-sphere rates, chemistry, path-dependent events and quantum many-body dynamics are outside the proof. The closed scalar mixture representation is essential. Extending the result requires a new argument, not a relabelling of variables.
The useful mechanism is precise:
Limit the sampled dependency of a nonlinear evolution before evaluating it, then convert bounded signed information into a genuine output distribution.
For this model, the required dependency is controlled by a random polynomial degree rather than by the number of physical collision events. That is why elapsed time disappears from the expected operation count.
This is a concrete example of changing the computational operation rather than merely improving an error certificate. But its present scientific reach is restricted, and exactness alone does not make it technologically transformative.
