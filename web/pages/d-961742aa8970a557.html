# Resource-allocation safety: coupled-flux certificate

**Verdict:** this yields a precise, application-facing refinement, but I do not see a defensible new scientific advance yet. The stochastic safety mechanism is already in Astra N35 and exponential-martingale/CTMDP literature. The useful extension is to preserve measured *coupled* enzyme/ATP/redox constraints in the safety certificate instead of widening them to an independent rate box. Its novelty, data-acquisition algorithm, and utility on a real network remain open.

## Candidate result

For integer-complex molecular reactions with a supplied compact positive operating polytope $Q$, let each predictable rate allocation $k_t$ lie in a measured compact flux/resource polytope $K(X_t)$. On facet $g_i(x)=a_i^\top x+b_i\ge0$, test the joint exponential-generator support function

\[
H_i(x,k;\theta)=\sum_r k_rx^{y_r}(e^{-\theta a_i^\top\nu_r}-1).
\]

If some $\theta_i,\eta_i>0$ give $\sup_{x\in Q,0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i,k\in K(x)}H_i(x,k;\theta_i)\le-\eta_i$, the finite-volume molecule-count chain obeys the explicit, controller-uniform exit bound in [the proof note](proofs/flux_coupled_exit.md). The result charges the exact falling-factorial correction and requires a lattice-compatible initial count. It applies to every predictable allocation in the declared set; it does not infer the allocation set from stoichiometry.

For the two-reaction example $2A\to A+B$, $2B\to A+B$, the coupled capacities $k_A=k_B\in[1,16]$ preserve $Q=[1/4,3/4]$ with inward collar margin $7/16$. An exact rational enclosure gives $\theta=1/2$, $\eta=3/20$, $c_*=1/64$, $V_0=223$, and at $V=3000$, $A_0=B_0=1500$, a bound below $2.17\cdot10^{-6}$ through $T=e^{23.4375}\approx1.5093046832\cdot10^{10}$ model time units. The full-domain constants are $C_i=109/20$, $D_i=2086/125$, and $\Lambda_Q=10$. Widening this measured coupling to the independent box $[1,16]^2$ fails: at $x=1/4,k_A=16,k_B=1$, the drift is $-7/16$ and the $\theta=1/2$ facet generator obeys $H_L>1470431/3440640>0$. This is a toy witness for preserving resource coupling, not a biological validation.

## Measurable interface and costs

The measurable inputs are absolute molecule counts or calibrated concentrations plus volume; the complete integer stoichiometric boundary, including exchange/dilution terms; initial counts and compatible reaction-lattice totals; active enzyme abundance and $k_{cat}$ intervals; ATP/redox pool levels; isotope-traced flux intervals; and time-stamped allocation/intervention logs. For fixed concentration $x$, these must be translated into a joint feasible rate set such as

\[
K(x)=\{k\ge0:\ Ak\le p,\quad \underline v\le\operatorname{diag}(x^{y_r})k\le\overline v\},
\]

where rows of $A(x)k\le p$ encode shared enzyme-turnover limits (from measured $k_{cat}$ and active enzyme abundance), proteome budgets, and ATP/redox constraints. Measurement uncertainty must be included in the outer feasible set. If active enzyme abundance changes over the safety horizon, the theorem needs a rate set uniformly valid at every time or an augmented state model; a single static proteomics snapshot does not establish that premise. The exact mass-action propensity law is another application assumption: enzyme saturation, allostery, compartment effects, and enzyme-complex dynamics require a valid reduction or an expanded molecular network. ATP/ADP or NAD(P)H sums are invariants only when the full reaction boundary makes the corresponding left-kernel relation exact. Uptake, nucleotide synthesis, exchange, dilution, or degradation can destroy that invariant unless their reservoirs are represented inside a larger closed model. A primary moiety-conservation study reports that such cofactors do not generically appear in conserved pools across genome-scale reconstructions. Proteome and ATP-turnover budgets constrain rates; they are not automatically stoichiometric invariants.

Finding one strictly positive conservation vector is an exact rational feasibility problem; finding every chemically interpretable nonnegative integer moiety can be much harder and is NP-hard in worst case. At a fixed metabolite state, optimizing $H_i$ over a polyhedral resource set is an LP. Global certification over each collar and $Q$ needs rational interval subdivision or real-algebraic optimization, with rigorous exponential enclosures. It can be exponential in dimension; no practical acquisition or complexity theorem is supplied. After certification, evaluating the bound costs $O(mR)$ facet/reaction terms. Here the coupled total-activity envelope is $\Lambda_Q=\sup_{x,q}q[x^2+(1-x)^2]=10$, so the full-horizon thinning envelope is about $V\Lambda_QT=4.52791405\cdot10^{14}$ candidate events at $V=3000$ (an upper bound on proposals before stopping at exit). No full simulation was run; the stated prediction is the analytic bound. Open-system feed, fuel, turnover, and controller work over that horizon must also be charged. No assays or external data were obtained.

As an independent representation, a strictly positive integer conservation vector $w^\top S=0$ makes the fixed-$V$ count class finite, with at most $\prod_j(\lfloor w^\top z_0/w_j\rfloor+1)$ count states. The exact worst-case hitting probability then solves a finite-state Bellman ODE with a per-state LP over $K(z/V)$. This is the ordinary CTMDP route; it is a useful comparator on small models and can return a worst-case resource allocation. The crude state bound is $O(V^d)$ when the conserved total scales with volume, often reduced by additional invariants; Bellman integration solves one control problem per state over the time interval.

## Falsifier and attacks

The direct mathematical falsifier is one feasible state/allocation in a certified facet collar with $H_i(x,k;\theta_i)>-\eta_i$, or a compatible finite-volume policy whose exact-generator reachability exceeds the reported bound. For a physical test, a replicated first-exit frequency with a valid lower confidence bound above the predicted risk would falsify the model-bound combination, after validating the initial counts, pool conservation, flux-set membership, propensity law, and observation error.

Two scope attacks are decisive. Astra N35 itself gives a closed inward-polytope network $A+B\to2A$, $A\to B$ whose finite stochastic chain reaches absorbing $A=0$ almost surely (exact source spans `pages/d-326696af15b5e02b.txt:1365-1370` and `pages/d-bea4150c129f63b3.txt:201`). So a long finite safe horizon cannot be promoted to infinite-time survival. Astra N77 reports a stronger classwide tightness failure: a deterministic class can attract to one equilibrium while stationary stochastic laws escape through unbounded directions in the same nonnegative stoichiometric plane. N77 is only a supplied summary here; its full proof/code was not present at the pinned commit, so I use it as a flagged obstruction, not independently verified evidence.

## Prior art and Astra evidence

Primary literature already covers the ingredient mechanisms: linear moiety conservation is the left kernel of the stoichiometric matrix; enzyme/proteomics constraints are established in enzyme-constrained metabolic models; finite-horizon reachability control is established for CTMDPs; and large-deviation/exponential transition-time methods are established for chemical jump networks. Exact links and source limits are in [source notes](SOURCE_NOTES.md). The new combination is therefore best treated as an application-targeted refinement unless a literature and implementation audit finds a nontrivial gap.

The pinned Astra source is commit `8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83`.

- N35 is the main antecedent. Its exact supplied proof is in `web/pages/d-326696af15b5e02b.html`, extracted here with theorem/lemma spans at `pages/d-326696af15b5e02b.txt:1159-1310`. It uses a per-reaction upper rate envelope and a quadratic exponential estimate. The proposed extension replaces that estimate by a support-function calculation over the joint feasible allocation set.
- N34 gives the exact $[1,16]^2$ outward witness in the same manuscript at `pages/d-326696af15b5e02b.txt:1120-1151`; it refutes that interval certificate only.
- N33 supplies the deterministic all-endotactic absorber candidate. Its full affine reconstruction, essential-source drift, and plateau argument are in the same manuscript at `pages/d-326696af15b5e02b.txt:103-139` and `:142-738`. A global absorber is not an input that can be assumed acquired for a real metabolic network.
- N50 conditionally closes the all-scale recognition gap for promised rational endotactic input only; the general CAD implementation and complexity bound remain absent. It is not used as a theorem premise here.
- N77 is summary-only at this snapshot. The exact card states its boundary and reports the full proof packet absent.

### Recommended next gate

Do not rank this as a frontier advance yet. The decisive next gate is an actual closed or explicitly budgeted metabolic subnetwork with public or newly acquired enzyme/flux data, followed by (i) exact conservation/coset verification, (ii) a globally certified coupled-flux facet margin, (iii) a small-volume Bellman comparator with equal information, and (iv) a material risk/exponent gain over the best independent-box or equally informed flux-control baseline. If the local MGF margin fails, preserve the outward allocation as the rejection witness.
