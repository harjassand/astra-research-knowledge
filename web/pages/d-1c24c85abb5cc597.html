# Beyond uniform retries: any iid law and the raw-query boundary

7 October 2026, cycle 10. **New scoped deductions, complete elementary analytic proofs below; no historical novelty or external validation claimed.** Uses the independently reconstructed cap-forcing lemma in `CHARGE_IID_OBSTRUCTION_GATE.md`. No nested worker, supplied design, or finite test is used to prove these statements.

## Exact questions and retained acquisition interface

The target remains N=O(T^2) distinct rational unit directions on S^2 with normalized harmonic mean <=beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5] and Gram matrix >I/2. Does changing the iid sampling law or selecting a passing subset from a large iid candidate pool remove the uniform whole-set obstruction?

Two answers:

1. Every fixed iid sampling law, even a T-dependent nonuniform or discrete rational law, has an exponential-in-T^2 whole-set obstruction when N<=C T^2 with fixed C.
2. Adaptive selection/rejection of nodes from a fixed iid pool can bypass whole-set retry, but every successful acquisition must wait for required-cap coverage. Its expected raw-query count is Omega(T^2 log T), and an explicit sub-threshold query-budget success bound holds.

These do not prove superpolynomial cost for all acquisition algorithms. Stratified laws changing across nodes, adaptive proposal locations, deterministic construction, and position optimization remain outside this boundary.

## 1. Necessary caps are distribution independent

The gate proves that ||b||<=2 beta_T forces every radius-r cap to be occupied, where

    q=floor((T-1)/2)+2,
    r=arccos(1-16/q^2), T>=8.

Fix K disjoint such caps obtained from maximal 2r packing. Then K>=T^2/128. This geometric necessity concerns the output's harmonic moments against uniform surface measure; it does not depend on the sampling distribution.

For an arbitrary probability law nu on S^2, let p_i=nu(cap_i). The open caps are disjoint, so sum_i p_i<=1. Boundaries carrying nu mass cause no problem: that mass contributes to none of the open caps, which are the required sets. If some p_i=0, iid acquisition through these caps has zero success probability.

## 2. Whole-set theorem for every fixed iid law

Let N independent candidates have common law nu. In a Poissonized experiment with mean lambda, counts in the K caps are independent Poisson(lambda p_i), including discrete laws. Therefore

    Pr(all caps occupied)=product_i(1-exp(-lambda p_i)).

For p>0 the function f(p)=log(1-exp(-lambda p)) is increasing and strictly concave:

    f''(p)=-lambda^2 exp(lambda p)/(exp(lambda p)-1)^2<0.

Jensen and sum_i p_i<=1 give

    product_i(1-exp(-lambda p_i))
        <=(1-exp(-lambda/K))^K
        <=exp[-K exp(-lambda/K)].                     (B1)

Set lambda=2N and apply the gate's Poisson comparison f_fixed(N)<=6 E f_fixed(Z). A passing whole set must occupy all caps, hence

    Pr(passing)<=6 exp[-K exp(-2N/K)].                 (B2)

For N<=C T^2 and K>=T^2/128,

    Pr(passing)<=6 exp[-T^2 exp(-256C)/128].           (B3)

The constant is weaker than the uniform-sphere constant, because nu may put all mass in the required caps. It remains a positive fixed exponential coefficient for every fixed C. This is a law-uniform bound: nu can depend arbitrarily on T and N, and its sampling/preprocessing cost need not be bounded for (B3) to apply.

Consequently a whole-set retry protocol may even choose a new nu and N from previous failures; if each attempted set is conditionally iid with N<=C T^2, its conditional acceptance probability is bounded by (B3). The tail of its attempt count dominates a geometric tail with that parameter whenever the bound is below one. Its expected number of attempts is at least exp[T^2 exp(-256C)/128]/6. Correlation or changing the law within an attempted set is a different interface and can escape this statement.

Deterministic per-node maps of iid uniforms, including rational rounding, produce some iid law nu and are covered directly by (B3). No rounding mesh assumption is needed for this version if the candidates themselves lie on S^2. The sharper uniform-sphere bound survives mesh rounding through the gate's perturbation argument.

## 3. Query-budget boundary for selection and rejection

Now an algorithm may keep, discard, reorder, duplicate, or jointly select from a sequence of candidates with one fixed iid law nu. It may inspect exact harmonic moments and choose its final cardinality adaptively. Every returned direction must be a candidate, or lie within delta_T of an associated candidate. The algorithm is not permitted to generate new uncharged directions away from that pool.

If it produces a passing output within M raw queries, the prefix pool of those M candidates must occupy all K required caps. For rounded outputs, apply the gate's mean stability to their associated preimages first; their possibly repeated multiset still has mean <2 beta_T, so the same pool necessity holds. The selection rule's adaptivity is irrelevant to this pathwise implication.

Let epsilon>0 be fixed and Poissonize at lambda=(1+epsilon)M. Paley-Zygmund with threshold M=lambda/(1+epsilon) gives, for M>=1,

    Pr(Z>=M)>=epsilon^2 M^2/[lambda^2+lambda]
        >=epsilon^2/[(1+epsilon)(2+epsilon)].

Monotonicity and (B1) imply the distribution-independent bound

    Pr(success by M queries)
       <=A_epsilon exp[-K exp(-(1+epsilon)M/K)],
    A_epsilon=(1+epsilon)(2+epsilon)/epsilon^2.         (B4)

In particular, for M<=c T^2 log T and fixed c<1/64, choose epsilon>0 so that 128(1+epsilon)c<2. Then

    Pr(success by M queries)
       <=A_epsilon exp[-T^(2-128(1+epsilon)c)/128]->0. (B5)

For uniform-sphere raw candidates, p_i=8/q^2 and the stronger exact cap masses give

    Pr(success by M queries)
       <=A_epsilon exp[-T^2 exp(-32(1+epsilon)M/T^2)/128]. (B6)

Thus every fixed c<1/16 in M<=c T^2 log T gives a stretched-exponentially small success probability after choosing epsilon small enough. These constants are conservative packing constants; no sharp optimum is claimed.

## 4. Exact expected raw-query lower bound

Let tau be the first raw candidate index at which all K caps have appeared. Any correct procedure returning a passing solution almost surely has total raw-query count >=tau pathwise. A procedure allowed to abort with UNKNOWN is instead covered by (B4); a cheap abort is not acquisition.

Attach independent mean-one exponential waiting times to the raw draws. Their labels are independent of those waiting times, so the expected continuous coverage time equals E tau by nonnegative Fubini, even if infinite. Poisson thinning makes the first arrival in each cap independent exponential with rate p_i. Hence

    E tau=integral_0^infinity [1-product_i(1-exp(-t p_i))]dt.

Applying (B1) at every t,

    E tau>=integral_0^infinity [1-(1-exp(-t/K))^K]dt
           =K integral_0^1 [1-(1-u)^K]/u du
           =K H_K, H_K=sum_(j=1)^K 1/j.              (B7)

Therefore every fixed iid law satisfies

    E raw queries >=(T^2/128)log(1+T^2/128)
                   =(1/64+o(1))T^2 log T.           (B8)

For uniform-sphere raw draws, each remaining cap has the same probability p_cap=8/q^2. With j unoccupied caps, the waiting time to a new cap is geometric of mean 1/(j p_cap), so exactly

    E tau=(q^2/8)H_K
      >=(T^2/32)log(1+T^2/128)
       =(1/16+o(1))T^2 log T.                        (B9)

This boundary charges discarded candidates as well as the O(T^2) selected output. It rules out constant-factor raw oversampling at optimal output count with meaningful asymptotic success probability. It is compatible with polynomial acquisition using an iid pool of size O(T^2 log T); coverage alone does not prove that such a pool contains a subset satisfying the very small mean tolerance and Gram interface.

## 5. A concrete rational cover bypasses the occupancy obstruction

The geometric invariant forced by the low-mean test is itself easy to acquire deterministically with O(T^2) rational nodes. This provides a useful scope countercheck: the coupon argument is an iid acquisition boundary, not hardness of cap coverage.

For m=q and integers -m<=k,h<=m take both stereographic maps

    F_+(k/m,h/m)
        =(2km,2hm,m^2-k^2-h^2)/(m^2+k^2+h^2),
    F_-(k/m,h/m)
        =(2km,2hm,-m^2+k^2+h^2)/(m^2+k^2+h^2).

Remove exact duplicates. Every node is a rational unit vector, every coordinate has O(log T) bits, and the count is at most 2(2q+1)^2<=2(T+4)^2=O(T^2). Generation and exact duplicate removal use polynomial arithmetic.

Every point on a hemisphere has inverse stereographic coordinates in the unit disk, hence inside [-1,1]^2. The nearest grid coordinate is within sqrt(2)/(2m). The map's differential has operator norm 2/(1+u^2+v^2)<=2; reflection preserves that bound. Thus the sphere chord distance to some grid node is <=sqrt(2)/m, and the geodesic distance is <=pi sqrt(2)/(2m). Meanwhile

    1-cos r=16/q^2<=r^2/2, so r>=4sqrt(2)/q.

At m=q, pi sqrt(2)/(2q)<4sqrt(2)/q<=r. Hence every required open radius-r cap contains a generated node, with a strict margin. Exact duplicate removal preserves coverage.

This is a complete construction of the necessary cap invariant. It does NOT establish ||b||<=beta_T or S>I/2 at equal weights. The charts' empirical measure is not asserted to be normalized surface measure. Claiming efficient direction acquisition from this cover alone would be an interface error. The remaining falsifiable step is a quantitatively controlled moment-correction algorithm that starts from a covering/Gram configuration while retaining O(T^2) equal-weight nodes; no such theorem is adopted here.
