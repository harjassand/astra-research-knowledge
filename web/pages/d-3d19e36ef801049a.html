ATTRIBUTED FOUR-PHASE GENERAL-FRAME REFINEMENT
c03_s02, 7 October 2026. Cross-exposure authorized; INITIAL unchanged.

Origin: c01_s01/phase2/FOUR_PHASE_VARIANCE_PROOF.txt, based on Luna c03_l10's
Pfaffian estimator and c03_l06's projected-prefix route. I read this proof
after saving AUDIT_REPORT.txt and independently reconstructed the load-bearing
tensor and probability steps. It is a distinct later contribution, not my
original-site-sign pointwise inequality. Exact priority remains UNKNOWN.

Let a_S be arbitrary coefficient vectors in a Hilbert space, with all |S|=r,
and p(z)=sum_S a_S z_S for independent uniform fourth roots. Put Z=sum||a_S||²
and X=||p||². Then X²=||p tensor p||². The polynomial p tensor p has exponent
vectors alpha=1_S+1_T, whose coordinates lie in {0,1,2}. These distinct
monomials are orthogonal on the fourth-root product grid, because their
coordinate differences lie in {-2,-1,0,1,2}, and only zero is divisible by4.
Thus E X²=sum_alpha ||sum_(S,T:alpha) a_S tensor a_T||².

If |S minus T|=l, alpha has r-l double positions and 2l singleton positions.
Every ordered decomposition chooses l singleton positions for S, giving
binom(2l,l) possible ordered pairs. Hilbert-space Cauchy--Schwarz therefore
bounds each alpha norm² by that multiplicity times
sum_(S,T:alpha)||a_S||²||a_T||². Summing gives
 E X²<=binom(2r,r)Z².
If the nonzero coefficient support has Johnson diameter d, replace r by d.
This proof needs no identity columns. Its application to paired-volume
exterior coefficients is immediate from the already-reconstructed Pfaffian
expansion and exact determinant-square-root computation. Fourth roots are
Gaussian integers, so exact bit costs and common denominators persist.

Unlike the special original-sign inequality in AUDIT_REPORT, this is a
SECOND-MOMENT bound and does not prove X<=binom(2r,r)Z on every draw. It also
does not supply a deterministic certificate when an approximate output is0.
The distinction matters after Gram projection: original e_i identity columns
need not remain identities, so the residual-degree pointwise argument cannot
be imported. The four-phase tensor bound can legitimately replace 9^r by
binom(2r,r) in a general projected-frame residual estimator.

Consequently an ACQUIRED forced-pair core of size k-r, factored by the exact
Gram Schur projection in c03_l06, admits the same relative approximation with
binom(2r,r) sample factor. c01_s02 is separately acquiring such a mandatory
core with a charged randomized support/PIT budget. I have not executed or
certified that acquisition here. Its row-first decision tree plus exact
conditional column-volume counts is the appropriate downstream sampler;
my physical-prefix inclusion-exclusion may pay 2^b and a large original-k
binomial coefficient after many down sites are forced.

I also checked c01_s01's raw-phase dense-perturbation probability obstruction.
For m unit swap blocks, p0(z)=product_j(z_(2j)-z_(2j+1)). It is nonzero only
when every phase pair differs, an event of probability (3/4)^m. Its proposed
dense perturbation has coefficient l1 difference <=1/128 for every phase
word. Outside that event the sample is <=1/128², whereas the target norm is
>1. If all T draws avoid the event, every sample mean and median of batch
means is below half the target. A union bound gives success probability at
most T(3/4)^m. Thus this family forces exponentially many draws for those
raw routines. It does not obstruct selective correlated/fixed phases or
algorithms that exploit the explicit near-matching structure. The count of
the counterexample itself is easily approximated; no hardness follows.
