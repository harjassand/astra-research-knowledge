Simulating correlated noise without generating its time history
The strongest result of this investigation is a finite-seed quantum simulator for systems driven by correlated classical noise. It does not depend on the earlier time-history or Lindblad candidates.
The construction replaces a potentially enormous noise trajectory with a private seed that reproduces every correlation the simulation circuit can detect. It then simulates the complete noise-averaged quantum channel, including correlations across prescribed control and measurement times.
For the noise models specified below, the derived gate bound is
\[
\boxed{
G=\widetilde O\!\left(\mathcal A\,C_{\mathrm{sel}}+\mathcal A^2\right)
+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}},
\qquad
\mathcal A=1+p+T\left(\alpha_0+\sum_a\alpha_a\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}\) is the actual cost of selecting and implementing the supplied physical operators and parameters, and \(p\) is the number of control slots. The dependence on the noise correlation rates and channel accuracy is through logarithmic factors, not the number of resolved time steps or noise-switching events.
The quadratic term is real: it pays for evaluating the finite-seed function. There is no free noise oracle, uncharged random-access memory, or cryptographic pseudorandomness assumption.
This is a resource-accounted algorithmic candidate, not yet a certified original breakthrough. Its strongest consequence is a uniform way to simulate noncommuting dynamics under Gaussian and non-Gaussian colored noise while retaining environmental memory. I have not established superiority over every specialized multiscale or pseudomode algorithm.
1. The selected task and the prior-art boundary
The initial alternatives included stiff reaction-network simulation and inverse recovery from snapshots. Correlated-noise simulation offered the clearest missing operation: efficient Hamiltonian simulation can query many times coherently, but a classical procedure that generates noise sequentially does not automatically supply those queries consistently.
Several ingredients are established and must not be counted as discoveries.
Virtual Brownian trees already generate correlated paths nonchronologically from a seed, with logarithmic dependence on time resolution. Jelinčič–Foster–Kidger also construct the required Brownian integrals and establish joint-distribution guarantees in their classical query setting. arXiv
The fact that \(2Q\)-wise independence suffices to replace independent random-function values for a \(Q\)-query quantum algorithm is also established. Zhandry’s construction explicitly uses this fact and the polynomial method to avoid an additional pseudorandom-function assumption. Mark Zhandry
Likewise, truncated-Dyson simulation already implements time ordering through coherent time queries with logarithmic precision dependence. arXiv
The proposed contribution is the complete composition: explicit correlated-noise bridges, finite-bit approximation, bounded-query replacement, physical-channel error bounds, persistent memory under interventions, and paid gate costs.

2. Precise computational theorem
Consider an \(n\)-qubit Hamiltonian
\[
H_X(t)=H_0+\sum_{a=1}^{r}X_a(t)A_a,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(H_0,A_a\) are Hermitian. No commutation is assumed, either among the \(A_a\) or with \(H_0\).
The independent stationary noise modes may be of either type:
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck noise
\[
dX_a=-\lambda_aX_a\,dt+\sqrt{2\lambda_a}\,dW_a,
\qquad
\mathbb E[X_a(t)X_a(s)]=e^{-\lambda_a|t-s|}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\lambda_a=0\), this means one time-independent standard Gaussian variable.
Symmetric random telegraph noise
\[
X_a(t)\in\{-1,1\},
\]
with switching rate \(\lambda_a/2\), again giving covariance \(e^{-\lambda_a|t-s|}\).
Physical noise amplitudes are included in \(A_a\); unit variance does not remove their computational cost.
The target channel is
\[
\Phi_T(\rho)
=
\mathbb E_X\!\left[U_X(T)\rho U_X(T)^\dagger\right].
\tag{4}
\]
The input provides finite descriptions of the rates and actual controlled, inverse, and indexed implementations of
\[
H_0/\alpha_0,\qquad A_a/\alpha_a.
\]
The normalization factors bound the operator norms. Their preparation, selection, coefficient loading, and working-precision costs are included in \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)\).
The algorithm constructs a circuit whose reduced output channel satisfies
\[
\boxed{\|\widetilde\Phi_T-\Phi_T\|_\diamond\le\varepsilon.}
\tag{5}
\]
The input may be unknown and entangled with a reference, but it is initially independent of the noise.
More strongly, insert \(p\) arbitrary supplied instruments at prescribed times. Their choices may depend on earlier recorded outcomes. The same construction approximates the complete controlled output channel, including those records, uniformly over such controllers. The controller cannot inspect the private noise seed.
Define
\[
S=\sum_a\alpha_a,
\qquad
\mathcal A=1+p+T(\alpha_0+S).
\tag{6}
\]
Then (1) holds. The suppressed factors are fixed polynomials in the parameter bit length and logarithms of
\[
r+2,\quad
1+\lambda_{\max}T,\quad
1+\mathcal A,\quad
1/\varepsilon.
\]
They do not conceal powers of a noise-grid size.
The proof follows.
3. A correlated path value from logarithmically many innovations
Let \(M=2^m\), with grid spacing \(\Delta=T/M\). We first construct the exact grid law using independent innovations, without generating the whole grid.
Gaussian bridge
For one Ornstein–Uhlenbeck mode, generate its endpoints as
\[
X(0)=Z_0,
\qquad
X(T)=e^{-\lambda T}Z_0+
\sqrt{1-e^{-2\lambda T}}\,Z_T,
\]
where the \(Z\)'s are independent standard normals.
For an interval \([u,v]\), put
\[
h=(v-u)/2,\qquad a=e^{-\lambda h}.
\]
Its conditional midpoint is
\[
\boxed{
X\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)
=
\frac{a}{1+a^2}\bigl(X(u)+X(v)\bigr)
+
\sqrt{\frac{1-a^2}{1+a^2}}\,Z_{[u,v]}.
}
\tag{7}
\]
This follows directly from the conditional covariance formula for three jointly Gaussian variables. Conditional on the midpoint and endpoints, the two half-bridges are independent.
To evaluate \(X(j\Delta)\), descend only along the binary path containing \(j\). This requires at most \(m+2\) innovations, addressed by the mode and tree node.
The representation is numerically stable:
\[
\frac{2a}{1+a^2}\le1,
\qquad
1+a^2\ge1.
\tag{8}
\]
There is no repeated division by a small conditional probability.
Telegraph bridge
The initial sign is uniform. The endpoint differs from it with probability
\[
\frac{1-e^{-\lambda T}}2.
\]
For a midpoint with the same definitions of \(h,a\):
- Opposite endpoints give either midpoint sign with probability \(1/2\).
- Equal endpoints \(x\) give midpoint \(-x\) with probability\[
  \boxed{
  \frac{(1-a)^2}{2(1+a^2)}.
  }
  \tag{9}
  \]
These formulas follow by multiplying the two half-interval transition probabilities and conditioning on the endpoints. They again require only one independent uniform variable per visited node.
Thus both Gaussian and telegraph values admit
\[
\boxed{O(\log M)}
\tag{10}
\]
innovation queries per coherent time lookup.
This is a construction of the joint law, not merely its covariance.
4. Approximating the physical channel without controlling an entire path uniformly
For two deterministic noise paths \(x,y\), Duhamel’s formula and unitarity give
\[
\|U_x(T)-U_y(T)\|
\le
\int_0^T\|H_x(t)-H_y(t)\|\,dt.
\]
Consequently, for any coupling of two noise processes,
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi_X-\Phi_Y\|_\diamond
\le
2\int_0^T\sum_a\alpha_a
\,\mathbb E|X_a(t)-Y_a(t)|\,dt.
}
\tag{11}
\]
The same estimate holds with intervening instruments: dilate the instruments, retain their records, and telescope the complete evolution. No error is divided by the probability of a measurement outcome.
Grid error
Replace each process by its left-endpoint value in each cell. For Gaussian modes,
\[
\mathbb E|X(t)-X(s)|
\le\sqrt{2\lambda|t-s|}.
\]
For telegraph modes,
\[
\mathbb E|X(t)-X(s)|
=1-e^{-\lambda|t-s|}
\le\lambda|t-s|.
\]
Set
\[
C_G=\sum_{\mathrm{Gaussian}\ a}\alpha_a\sqrt{\lambda_a},
\qquad
C_R=\sum_{\mathrm{telegraph}\ a}\alpha_a\lambda_a.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi-\Phi_{\mathrm{grid}}\|_\diamond
\le
2\sqrt2\,T\sqrt\Delta\,C_G
+
2T\Delta C_R.
}
\tag{12}
\]
A sufficiently large power of two satisfying
\[
M\ge
C\left(
1+\frac{T^3C_G^2}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{T^2C_R}{\varepsilon}
\right)
\tag{13}
\]
controls this error. Additional conditions below ensure convenient segmentation and control-time rounding.
Equation (13) may demand an enormous grid. Only \(\log M\), not \(M\), is used in the circuit.
Finite Gaussian innovations
A finite-bit normal generator can be made explicit.
Take a uniform \(b\)-bit integer, convert it to the midpoint of its uniform quantile interval, apply the inverse normal distribution function, and truncate to \([-R_Z,R_Z]\). Enforce antisymmetric rounding between opposite quantiles.
For
\[
R_Z=O(\sqrt{\log(1/\eta)}),
\qquad b=O(\log(1/\eta)),
\]
the resulting variable \(Z_b\) has a coupling to a standard normal \(Z\) with
\[
\mathbb E(Z-Z_b)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E|Z-Z_b|^2\le\eta^2.
\tag{14}
\]
For completeness, if \(f\) is the clipped quantile function, monotonicity gives a midpoint-discretization squared error at most
\[
4R_Z^2\,2^{-b}.
\]
The omitted Gaussian tail has exponentially small second moment. The clipped inverse CDF can be evaluated by bisection and finite-precision evaluation of the normal CDF in polynomial bit complexity; no quantile table is stored.
Every exact Gaussian grid value is a linear combination
\[
X_j=\sum_\ell c_{j\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
\sum_\ell c_{j\ell}^2=1.
\]
Under independent innovations, the mean-zero approximation errors in (14) therefore imply
\[
\mathbb E|X_j-Y_j|^2\le\eta^2
\tag{15}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), rather than an error proportional to the total number of grid points.
The stable recursion (8) also bounds arithmetic-error accumulation by a polynomial in the tree depth and working precision.
For telegraph bridges, finite-bit uniforms and threshold rounding can be coupled with the exact process. A union bound over its \(O(rM)\) potential nodes costs only
\[
b=O(\log(rM/\varepsilon))
\]
precision bits. It does not require visiting those nodes.
A deterministic norm cap
The simulation query budget must be fixed before choosing a seed. Gaussian paths are unbounded, so impose a final cap
\[
B=
\left\lceil
\sqrt{2\log\frac{64(1+ST)}{\varepsilon}}
\right\rceil.
\]
Clip Gaussian field values to \([-B,B]\).
Using
\[
\mathbb E|X-\operatorname{clip}_B(Y)|
\le
\mathbb E(|X|-B)_+
+\mathbb E|X-Y|,
\]
equation (11) bounds the resulting channel error by choosing
\[
\eta=O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{1+ST}\right).
\]
Every finite seed now defines a Hamiltonian satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\|H(t)\|\le\Lambda
=
\alpha_0+B\sum_{\mathrm{Gaussian}\ a}\alpha_a
+\sum_{\mathrm{telegraph}\ a}\alpha_a.
}
\tag{16}
\]
The Gaussian clipping argument uses marginal moments, not a supremum over the entire fine grid.
5. Simulating the gridded Hamiltonian without visiting its cells
This step uses established truncated-Dyson simulation, but there is a useful exact identity that avoids a differentiability assumption on the noise. arXiv
Consider a segment of duration \(d\), containing equally sized cells with constant Hamiltonians \(H_j\). For independent uniform cell indices \(j_1,\ldots,j_k\), sorted into nondecreasing order,
\[
\boxed{
\int_{0<t_1<\cdots<t_k<d}
H(t_k)\cdots H(t_1)\,dt_1\cdots dt_k
=
\frac{d^k}{k!}
\mathbb E\!\left[
H_{j_{(k)}}\cdots H_{j_{(1)}}
\right].
}
\tag{17}
\]
This is exact. It follows by first sorting independent continuous uniform times, then taking their cell indices. Repeated cell indices cause no error because those factors contain the same \(H_j\).
Uniform quantum time registers, reversible sorting, and selected Hamiltonian encodings implement the right-hand side. Choose segments with \(\Lambda d\le1/2\). Truncating at order \(K\) has error at most
\[
e^{\Lambda d}\frac{(\Lambda d)^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}.
\]
Taking
\[
K=O\!\left(\log\frac{1+p+\Lambda T}{\varepsilon}\right)
\]
is sufficient. The linear-combination normalization is below two; standard oblivious amplification implements an approximate unitary without postselection.
The resulting number of Hamiltonian-encoding calls is
\[
\boxed{
q=O\!\left[
(1+p+\Lambda T)
\log\frac{1+p+\Lambda T}{\varepsilon}
\right].
}
\tag{18}
\]
Intervals between controls can be padded with zero Hamiltonians to permit simple uniform time-register preparation. This increases their lengths by at most a constant factor, and does not advance the physical noise clock.
We additionally choose
\[
M\ge C\Lambda T(1+p/\varepsilon).
\tag{19}
\]
This permits short segments and bounds the error from rounding prescribed control times to grid cells. Under the clipped Hamiltonian, shifting one control by at most \(\Delta\) changes the complete channel by at most \(4\Lambda\Delta\).
Each Hamiltonian lookup computes only the selected noise mode, in superposition. Including computation and uncomputation, the whole circuit uses at most
\[
\boxed{Q=8q(m+2)}
\tag{20}
\]
queries to a finite innovation table.
Up to this point, that table is conceptual. The next operation removes it.
6. Replacing all independent innovations by a finite seed
Let the conceptual innovation oracle be
\[
O_f|x,z\rangle=|x,z\oplus f(x)\rangle,
\]
where each \(f(x)\) is an independent uniform \(b\)-bit value.
The following established bounded-query fact is the key algebraic tool. Mark Zhandry
Exact replacement lemma
A quantum circuit making at most \(Q\) calls to \(O_f\) has exactly the same averaged output channel when \(f\) is drawn from a \(2Q\)-wise independent uniform family instead of a fully independent table.
Proof. A matrix entry of \(O_f\) is linear in indicators
\[
\mathbf 1\{f(x)=y\}.
\]
After \(Q\) queries, each amplitude is a polynomial of degree at most \(Q\) in these indicators. Each output-density-matrix entry therefore has degree at most \(2Q\).
Every monomial depends on values at at most \(2Q\) distinct addresses. Its expectation is identical under the two distributions. This proves equality of the output density matrices, including arbitrary reference systems and retained measurement records. ∎
This is stronger than passing statistical tests, but weaker than reproducing a complete path distribution.
Explicit implementation
The innovation address domain has size at most \(D=O(r(M+1))\). Choose a field \(\mathbb F_{2^b}\) large enough to label every address, and let
\[
f_c(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2Q-1}c_\ell x^\ell,
\tag{21}
\]
where the coefficients are independent uniform field elements.
The Vandermonde matrix proves the required independence. Prepare the coefficient registers uniformly using Hadamard gates. They remain private and unchanged throughout the simulation.
Each evaluation of (21) uses reversible Horner evaluation:
\[
O(Q\,\operatorname{poly}(b))
\]
gates. Its scratch registers are uncomputed after copying the function value; otherwise they could leak additional seed information and invalidate the query-interface argument.
Across all \(Q\) innovation calls, the total cost is
\[
\boxed{O(Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b))}
\tag{22}
\]
gates, with \(O(Qb)\) seed and polynomial-evaluation workspace.
Field construction need not be an uncharged randomized preprocessing step. Round \(b\) up to a power of two and build a quadratic tower. Starting from \(\mathbb F_2\), maintain an element \(a\) of absolute trace one and adjoin
\[
u^2+u+a=0.
\]
This polynomial is irreducible because the image of \(z\mapsto z^2+z\) is the trace-zero subspace. The next trace-one element is \(ua\), since its relative trace is \(a\). Repeating gives explicit polynomial-size field-arithmetic circuits.
Why the proof order matters
The error estimates in Sections 3–5 are proved under fully independent innovations. Only afterwards is the finite compiled circuit replaced by (21), using exact equality of its averaged output channel.
I do not assume that the short-seed process is globally Gaussian, globally Markovian, or close in total variation to a complete independent innovation table.
The cap (16) fixes \(q\), hence \(Q\), before the seed family is selected. There is no circular dependence between the random family and its required independence order.
Combining the approximation errors with this exact replacement proves (5).
7. Complete resource accounting
A sufficient working precision \(b\), before the harmless power-of-two rounding, is
\[
b=
O\!\left(
B_{\mathrm{in}}+m+
\log\frac{(r+2)(q+1)(1+ST)}{\varepsilon}
\right),
\tag{23}
\]
with enlarged constants for the reversible arithmetic and elementary-gate synthesis budgets.
Exponentials of very large negative arguments can be replaced by zero once below the error budget. Small differences such as \(1-e^{-x}\) are evaluated without subtractive cancellation. None of these routines requires time proportional to the numerical value of \(\lambda_{\max}T\).
The explicit gate bound is
\[
\boxed{
G=
O\!\left(
q\,C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)
+Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b)
\right)
+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
}
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(m\) and \(b\) are logarithmic in the grid resolution and \(\Lambda\) differs from \(\alpha_0+S\) only by the Gaussian cap, this yields (1).
If a selected encoding uses \(a_{\mathrm{sel}}\) ancillary qubits, a conservative workspace bound is
\[
n+O\!\left(q\,a_{\mathrm{sel}}+Q\operatorname{poly}(b)\right)
+W_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
\tag{25}
\]
It includes seed storage, bridge evaluation, time sorting, and operator-encoding ancillas. No \(M\)-entry noise array is present.
Classical preprocessing reads the supplied coefficients and parameters, compiles their selectors, and constructs the arithmetic routines. Its cost is polynomial in their encoded size and working precision. Generating the expanded quantum circuit is charged in proportion to its gate description.
For an ordinary explicit list of \(O(n)\) bounded-locality Pauli terms and local noise operators, a scan-based selector costs
\[
C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)=O(n\,\operatorname{poly}(b)).
\]
This requires no quantum random-access memory.
The output is a quantum channel implementation. Writing a full density matrix or a complete noise trajectory remains an exponentially or grid-sized output task where applicable.
8. Strongest scientific consequence: controlled dynamics with persistent noise memory
The same private seed is used before and after every intervention.
Consequently, a system measurement can reveal information about the noise, and future evolution retains the corresponding conditional correlations automatically. The simulator does not reset the bath, independently redraw a suffix, or estimate a classical hazard from the unknown quantum state.
This supports a concrete physical workload:
\[
H_X(t)=
\sum_{(i,j)}J_{ij}P_iP_j
+\sum_i h_iP_i
+\sum_i \sigma_iX_i(t)Z_i,
\tag{26}
\]
with bounded-degree interactions, arbitrary supplied coefficients, and prescribed control pulses. The deterministic terms may fail to commute with the noise and with one another.
Colored-noise Hamiltonians are already used to investigate excitonic transport and finite environmental memory. The existing trajectory approach generates correlated fluctuations and propagates along time steps; its scientific relevance does not by itself establish a fault-tolerant complexity bound. arXiv
For \(O(n)\) terms and modes, bounded strengths \(J,\sigma\), and \(p\) control slots,
\[
G=
\widetilde O\!\left(
n\mathcal A+\mathcal A^2
\right)+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}},
\quad
\mathcal A=1+p+O(n(J+\sigma)T).
\tag{27}
\]
At \(T=O(1/J)\), \(\sigma=O(J)\), and \(p=O(n)\), this is
\[
\boxed{G=\widetilde O(n^2)+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.}
\tag{28}
\]
The bound remains independent of the number of grid points used to certify noise resolution, apart from logarithms.
This is a many-body statement. I am not claiming an exponential quantum advantage by treating a single-excitation problem, whose active state space is only linear in the number of sites, as an unrestricted \(2^n\)-dimensional workload. The distinction is explicit in the excitonic-model literature. arXiv
Actual observable and control-quality estimation
Suppose an input-preparation circuit and its inverse cost \(C_{\mathrm{in}}\), and a bounded effect \(0\le F\le I\) has a coherent implementation costing \(C_F\).
Prepare the seed coherently and retain the simulator’s work registers. The complete simulation has an inverse. Compiling to channel error \(O(\delta)\), amplitude estimation yields additive error \(\delta\) with failure probability at most \(\zeta\) at cost
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left[
\frac{G(\delta)+C_{\mathrm{in}}+C_F}{\delta}
\log\frac1\zeta
\right].
}
\tag{29}
\]
The amplitude-estimation improvement is established prior mathematics; the construction here supplies the coherent noise-averaged circuit to which it applies. arXiv
For example, prepare Bell pairs between the simulated system and a reference, apply a noisy control sequence, undo its intended ideal unitary, and measure the Bell projector. This estimates the sequence’s entanglement fidelity under the specified correlated-noise model. Bell preparation and readout cost \(O(n)\), in addition to the supplied ideal-control circuit.
When only independent input copies are available, ordinary sampling instead gives the usual \(\delta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)\) statistical factor.
Amplitude estimation does not require enlarging the seed to cover all its repetitions: it estimates the success amplitude of the already compiled, already accurate simulation circuit. It is not using that seed as an unlimited physical-noise service.
9. Comparison against competent alternatives
The result should not be compared only with first-order trajectory stepping.
Succinct stochastic-path algorithms already exist. Bouland–Dandapani–Prakash give a quantum spectral method producing amplitude encodings of stochastic trajectories. That is a different interface from a coherent binary-value oracle needed inside a noncommuting Hamiltonian simulator; converting arbitrary path amplitudes into accurate values is not free. arXiv
Pseudomode methods are a serious alternative. The current coupled-Lindblad pseudomode theory constructs completely positive Gaussian-bath representations under explicit feasibility conditions. Such a representation can be preferable. Its mode-count and representation theorems are not, by themselves, the same total-gate bound as (24); moreover, Gaussian bath-correlation matching does not determine the non-Gaussian telegraph process covered here. arXiv
Telegraph noise has an exact finite Markovian embedding. Introduce one maximally mixed classical bath qubit per mode and use
\[
H_{\mathrm{ext}}=H_0+\sum_a A_a\otimes Z_a,
\qquad
L_a=\sqrt{\lambda_a/2}\,X_a
\]
on bath qubit \(a\). The bath remains block diagonal in the \(Z\)-basis, giving exactly the prescribed switching process.
This embedding uses \(r\) bath qubits—not exponentially many qubits. Applying the current general gate-efficient Lindblad theorem gives normalized time
\[
\tau=
T\left(
\alpha_0+S+\frac12\sum_a\lambda_a
\right).
\tag{30}
\]
That algorithm is essentially linear in \(\tau\), with logarithmic precision overhead. arXiv
The present construction trades a quadratic dependence on Hamiltonian action for removal of the leading switching-rate cost in this uniform bound. When rates are moderate, the embedding may be cheaper. When rates are large, specialized averaging limits may also be cheaper if their model-approximation errors can be certified.
Thus the defensible comparison is:
A uniform explicit-gate bound controlled by system–noise interaction strength and control count, rather than by resolved bath updates.

It is not a proved lower-bound separation from all specialized algorithms.
10. Counterexamples that delimit the mechanism
Covariance matching is insufficient
Let \(X_1,X_2,X_3\in\{-1,1\}\). Compare independent signs with
\[
X_1=a,\quad X_2=b,\quad X_3=ab,
\]
where \(a,b\) are independent.
The two distributions agree on every one- and two-variable marginal. Yet three unit-duration qubit pulses
\[
H_j=(\pi/4)X_jZ
\]
multiply coherence by
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left[-\frac{i\pi}{2}(X_1+X_2+X_3)\right].
\]
This equals \(0\) for independent signs and \(i\) for the pairwise-independent construction.
Even this commuting example has an order-one channel difference. The simulator therefore uses exact bounded-query independence, not a covariance test.
Re-seeding destroys memory
For a static Gaussian \(X\), the Hamiltonian \(H=\sigma XZ\) gives coherence
\[
e^{-2\sigma^2T^2}.
\]
Redrawing \(X\) independently in each of \(M\) equal cells instead gives
\[
e^{-2\sigma^2T^2/M}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Keeping the same seed throughout is essential.
The seed is not a full path sampler
A degree-\((k-1)\) field-polynomial family contains at most \(2^{bk}\) tables, versus \(2^{bD}\) fully independent tables on \(D\) addresses. For \(D\gg k\), their complete-table distributions can be almost maximally different.
The theorem concerns the specified bounded-query simulation and its output, not unlimited subsequent queries or recovery of an entire fine trajectory.
Fixed-amplitude and white-noise limits differ
At fixed \(\|A_a\|\), the rate dependence is logarithmic. But maintaining fixed integrated Gaussian noise strength while increasing \(\lambda_a\) requires noise amplitude proportional to \(\sqrt{\lambda_a}\). Then \(S\), and hence the leading computational cost, grows.
The theorem does not hide that physical scaling.
Finally, the model describes prescribed classical noise. It does not automatically represent a finite-temperature quantum bath with backreaction or detailed balance. Nor does it learn the noise parameters from experimental data, certify hardware oracles, or make rare conditional outputs inexpensive.
11. Established outcome and remaining barrier
Small exact and numerical checks reproduced the Gaussian bridge covariances, telegraph bridge joint laws, and sorted-Dyson identities for noncommuting matrices, with residuals at floating-point roundoff. The covariance-only counterexample above was also evaluated directly. No full quantum circuit or formal proof-assistant development was executed.
The strongest surviving construction is
\[

It closes a specific operational gap: the required noise values are acquired by actual circuits, globally consistently, without generating or storing the resolved time history. Finite precision, randomness, private memory, operator selection, and observable estimation are all charged.
What remains unestablished is the claim that this constitutes an independently major original advance. That requires priority assessment of the complete theorem and comparison with optimized specialized methods in a scientifically demanding parameter regime. The present result is a constructive uniform capability with explicit costs—not yet a demonstrated universal speedup or a certified historic breakthrough
