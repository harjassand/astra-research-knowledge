# Cycle 2: resource-aware stochastic feedback under a shared birth cap

## Decision

The most useful exact result from this pass is a finite-state occupation-flow formulation for stationary molecular birth/death controllers, together with a two-output counterexample showing why separate scalar checks and an average-resource check do not settle joint reliability under a shared instantaneous synthesis cap. In the worked case, each output is individually feasible at peak rate 0.2 and the total average production cost is exactly 0.2, but the joint model needs peak rate at least

\[
B_* = \frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{\sqrt{34}-5} \simeq 0.203439.
\]

This is an exact theorem for the declared finite-state model. Its general optimization method is already covered by queue-control and constrained continuous-time Markov decision process (CTMDP) literature, including a paper that treats history-dependent policies and controlled birth/death systems. I therefore mark the specific two-gene calculation as a model lemma with **novelty unknown**, and the occupation-LP capability as **established prior art**, not a new general method. None of this by itself establishes a biological reliability gain.

## Model and exact occupation-flow statement

Let there be \(d\) output species, with count state

\[
S=\prod_{i=1}^d\{0,1,\ldots,M_i\}.
\]

Species \(i\) is born one molecule at a time at a predictable controlled intensity \(u_i(t)\), with \(u_i(t)=0\) at \(N_i=M_i\). Deaths occur independently at intensity \(\gamma_iN_i(t)\), where \(\gamma_i>0\). The shared instantaneous peak constraint is \(\sum_i u_i(t)\le B\). A unit birth is the charged production event. Controllers may use full count-state feedback and arbitrary history, but may not evade the cap by producing batches.

For a stationary occupation law, write \(\pi_x=P(N=x)\), and let \(z_{x,i}\) be the stationary birth flow of species \(i\) while the count state is \(x\) (rate multiplied by occupancy). Set \(z_{x,i}=0\) when \(x_i=M_i\), and set terms with out-of-state-space indices to zero. The exact feasible occupation-flow constraints are

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\pi_x\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{x\in S}\pi_x=1,\qquad z_{x,i}\ge 0,\\
&\sum_i z_{x,i}\le B\pi_x \quad (x\in S),\\
&\sum_i\bigl(z_{x,i}+\gamma_i x_i\pi_x\bigr)
 =\sum_i\bigl(z_{x-e_i,i}+\gamma_i(x_i+1)\pi_{x+e_i}\bigr)
 \quad (x\in S).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last line is stationarity flow conservation at every state. Every stationary causal policy yields such flows. Conversely, any feasible \((\pi,z)\) is realized by stationary full-state feedback

\[
u_i(x)=z_{x,i}/\pi_x \quad\text{when }\pi_x>0,
\]

with zero rates on zero-occupancy states. Flow conservation makes \(\pi\) invariant; because every occupied nonzero state has positive death transitions down toward the all-zero state, the recurrent support is the one class containing zero. Thus the construction is implementable from the all-zero state under the stated positive-degradation model.

Consequently, optimizing a stationary joint threshold such as
\(P(N_i\ge h_i\ \forall i)=\sum_{x:x_i\ge h_i\ \forall i}\pi_x\)
under shared peak and average-resource limits is a finite linear program. Per-birth costs \(c_i\) give the linear average cost \(\sum_{x,i}c_i z_{x,i}\); state-running costs \(r(x)\) add \(\sum_x r(x)\pi_x\). Coordinate flow balance also gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_x z_{x,i}=\gamma_i\sum_x x_i\pi_x,
\]

so for unit-cost births the long-run average production rate equals the expected degradation flux. A controller overhead depending on its state/action can be added as another linear occupation cost if the relevant finite controller state/action is included. Switching costs require recording the previous action in the state. If a ribosome pool, ATP store, or other resource has its own dynamics, that pool must be part of the state; the count-only reduction does not model hidden resource dynamics.

This is the finite-state occupation-measure LP specialized to unit births and linear degradation. Its number of state variables scales as \(\prod_i(M_i+1)\), so the exact formulation is not dimension-free. With censored observations, the full-count LP is an information relaxation: for maximizing reliability it gives an upper bound on the best censored-controller reliability, and need not be attainable by a policy that sees only censored counts.

## Exact shared-cap counterexample

Take two binary outputs, \(N_i\in\{0,1\}\), with \(\gamma_1=\gamma_2=1\), shared peak cap \(B\), and target stationary marginals

\[
P(N_1=1)=P(N_2=1)=p=0.1.
\]

For either output considered alone, a constant birth rate \(b=p/(1-p)=1/9\) gives stationary occupancy \(p=0.1\), satisfies its own peak bound \(b<0.2\), and costs average production flux \(p=0.1\). Thus the two scalar checks pass at an individual peak bound 0.2 each, and their summed average production demand is 0.2.

For the joint model, let \(q=\pi_{11}\). The marginals force

\[
\pi_{10}=\pi_{01}=0.1-q,\qquad \pi_{00}=0.8+q,
\]

with \(0\le q<0.1\). Let \(z_1(01)\) and \(z_2(10)\) be the only births that enter state 11. Its stationarity balance gives

\[
z_1(01)+z_2(10)=2q,
\]

because state 11 loses molecules at total rate \(2q\). Marginal birth/death balance requires total birth flux 0.1 for each species, hence total flow from state 00 is \(0.2-2q\). The shared-cap constraints at states 00, 01, and 10 then require

\[
B\ge \frac{0.2-2q}{0.8+q},\qquad
B\ge \frac{q}{0.1-q}.
\]

The first lower bound decreases with \(q\), and the second increases. Their intersection minimizes the maximum, at

\[
q_*=(6-\sqrt{34})/10,
\qquad
B_*=\frac{q_*}{0.1-q_*}
=\frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{\sqrt{34}-5}\simeq0.203439.
\]

This lower bound is attained. From state 00, set both birth rates to \((0.1-q_*)/(0.8+q_*)\); from 10, birth species 2 at rate \(B_*\); from 01, birth species 1 at rate \(B_*\); from 11, set both birth rates to zero. Each edge flow balances its reverse death flow: 00-to-10 and 00-to-01 each carry flow \(0.1-q_*\), while 10-to-11 and 01-to-11 each carry flow \(q_*\). The invariant probabilities are exactly \((0.8+q_*,0.1-q_*,0.1-q_*,q_*)\) in state order 00, 10, 01, 11.

Therefore shared peak \(B=0.2\) is infeasible despite scalar marginal feasibility and the mean-cost equality \(E[u_1+u_2]=0.2\). The obstruction is that the same budget must support births in different joint states, while the full state 11 has no admissible births. This is a counterexample to checking marginal capacity and mean production independently; it is not a claim that every biochemical resource behaves as a hard shared rate cap.

## Prior-art decision and biological scope

The queue-control overlap is direct. Serfozo’s 1981 paper treats controlled rates in birth/death processes and queues under discounted or average rewards, and discusses a linear program for computing optimal policies ([Cambridge Core primary article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-applied-probability/article/optimal-control-of-random-walks-birth-and-death-processes-and-queues/CF6CACA2D553B8766F1B31EB6E447708)). More decisively, Guo, Huang, and Song’s 2012 SIAM paper develops an LP equivalent to constrained average-cost optimization for CTMDPs under randomized history-dependent policies and illustrates the method with a controlled birth/death system ([SIAM primary article](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/100805169)). That prior work subsumes the general claim “bounded-rate feedback under average resource constraints is exactly optimizable by an occupation LP.” The special shared-cap two-gene formula above is a derived instance; its priority relative to queueing or molecular-control literature has not been established.

Biological resource-aware feedback is also active. Chakravarty, Zhang, and Tian’s 2024 IEEE CDC study compares antithetic-feedback architectures in a two-module resource-competition model using stochastic simulation and fluctuation-dissipation analysis of protein noise ([primary conference paper, DOI 10.1109/cdc56724.2024.10886586](https://doi.org/10.1109/cdc56724.2024.10886586); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11987709/)). It reports controller-dependent noise reductions, but its objective is a noise statistic rather than a joint threshold reliability optimum under a hard shared peak birth cap. The paper also says it does not model variation in resource availability or gene-copy number and holds transcription/controller-RNA production rates constant; its molecular/resource assumptions therefore differ from the hard-cap process here.

Separately, Sokolowski et al. optimize a mechanistic Drosophila gap-gene network for positional information under maximum molecular production/degradation rates and average resource utilization ([PNAS 2025 primary paper, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2402925121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2402925121)). This establishes that resource-constrained optimization of spatial gene-expression patterns is not itself a new principle. It does not settle the narrower stochastic reliability problem here: its target is positional information in the modeled spatial pattern, while this result concerns a finite-state stationary joint threshold under an explicit instantaneous shared birth cap.

Accordingly, the useful research distinction is narrow: **general CTMDP/queue optimization is prior art; resource-aware gene-circuit optimization is prior art; the exact binary shared-cap threshold calculation is a model-specific derivation whose novelty is unknown.** A biologically meaningful advance would require an explicit biochemical resource-state model, calibrated parameters, controller/resource costs, and a comparison against an equally informed baseline. The hard-cap count model supplies an analytic benchmark, not that validation.

## Scope gates

- The theorem assumes finite count caps, single-molecule births, positive independent linear death rates, and one shared instantaneous intensity cap. Burst reactions, growth dilution depending on state, promoter memory, maturation delays, cell-to-cell exchange, or stochastic resource pools need an enlarged transition model.
- It optimizes stationary long-run averages. A finite observation deadline, first-passage requirement, switching penalty, or rare-event risk criterion may require a different state/action augmentation and is not established here.
- If action costs depend only on current state/action, add them linearly in the occupation LP. If controller molecules or fuel stores have their own stochastic dynamics, include them in the state. If observations are censored, the full-state LP is a relaxation, not an exact implementation result.
- The mathematical optimization framework is established prior art. Do not claim scientific novelty from the LP reduction alone; first search direct shared-cap controlled birth/death and molecular-resource optimization literature for the specific two-output boundary calculation.
