# Uniform-weight quantum score objective: conventions, nonconvexity and acquisition boundaries

Worker r09_algebra, 2026-10-06, cycle4. Supplied candidate Hamiltonian H, copy access to faithful target σ, known physical involutions A. Target matrix, target eigensystem and target scores are not supplied to a learner. Exact algebraic deductions and one-qubit counterexample; no efficient all-temperature noncommuting learner is claimed.

## Normalization and signs independently verified

Use ∂_Aq=−i[A,q] and I_{q,λ}(X)=(1−λ)qX+λXq. The source potential is

G_λ(q)=½Σ_A Tr[(∂_Aq)† I_{q,λ}^−1(∂_Aq)].

Entrywise integration gives ∫_0^1 I_{q,λ}^−1dλ=Dlog_q, the Fréchet derivative of the matrix logarithm. For a Hermitian involution A²=I,

Ḡ_A(q)=½Tr(∂_Aq Dlog_q(∂_Aq))
 =−½Tr([A,q][A,log q])
 =½Tr(q[A,[A,log q]])
 =Tr(q(log q−Alog q A))
 =D(q||AqA).

The first equality uses covariance Dlog_q(−i[A,q])=−i[A,log q]. The next is cyclic trace integration by parts; the involution gives the double commutator 2(log q−Alog q A). Consequently the root's unhalved integrated Fisher expression is **2D(q||AqA)**, while the source potential Ḡ_A is **D(q||AqA)**. Doubling the objective doubles gradients and curvature but changes no sign or optimizer.

For arbitrary faithful δq, differentiating the relative entropy expression gives

∇Ḡ_A(q)=log q+I−Alog q A−Dlog_q(AqA).

The derivative superoperator Dlog_q is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, which transfers the final variation to δq. Thus the integrated source candidate observable is Ō_A(q)=−∇Ḡ_A(q). This is the actual copy objective Tr(σ Ō_A(q)); its candidate-dependent scalar part cannot be discarded.

For q_H=e^−βH/Z and Q=e^−βH,

Ō_A(H)=β(H−AHA)+Dlog_Q(AQA)−I.

Indeed the partition scalar cancels from Alog q A−log q, and Dlog_{Q/Z}(AQA/Z)=Dlog_Q(AQA). No target density matrix or partition function occurs in this expression. Computability from H does not itself guarantee inexpensive implementation.

The source's proper-regret identity remains valid after integration. c_priorart reconstructed that the uniform mixture is strictly proper for an ordinary irreducible frame; this audit confirms its potential and observable conventions. The potential is convex in q, but the population objective parameterized by Gibbs H is a different function.

## Exact failure of Hamiltonian convexity with complete physical XYZ

Consider one qubit, β=1, physical frame A∈{X,Y,Z}, candidate H=h·Pauli and r=||h||, n=h/r. Then q_H=(I−tanh(r)n·Pauli)/2. Each involution contributes 2r tanh(r)(1−n_A²), hence

Ḡ(q_H)=4r tanh r.

Writing q=(I−t n·Pauli)/2 gives Ḡ(q)=4t artanh t. Differentiating traceless directions determines the Pauli-vector part of Ō. The scalar part follows from the homogeneous identity −Tr(q Ō)=Ḡ(q). Together,

Ō(H)=4sinh²r I+4(r+sinh r cosh r)n·Pauli.

Both parts are retained in the objective below. Let the faithful target be

σ=½[I−(999/1000)(16X+63Z)/65].

Because 16²+63²=65², its eigenvalues are exactly 1/2000 and1999/2000. It is itself a finite-temperature Gibbs state, with Hamiltonian artanh(999/1000)(16X+63Z)/65. Neither purity nor a rank-deficient limit is used.

Take the affine candidate path

H(t)=3Z+t(X+Z).

Define J(t)=Tr(σ Ō(H(t))). The path involves a genuine noncommuting change in H. At h=(0,0,3), its direction is u=(1,0,1). Put C=cosh6, S=sinh6 and

k=4C/3−2S/9.

For a(r)=4sinh²r and b(h)=4(r+sinh r cosh r)h/r, the directional second derivatives are

a''[u,u]=8C+4S/3,

b_x''[u,u]=2k=8C/3−4S/9,

b_z''[u,u]=8S+k=4C/3+70S/9.

The target Bloch vector is m=−(999/1000)(16,0,63)/65. Therefore

J''(0)=(19673/3250)cosh6−(593609/97500)sinh6
 =−(263/15000)e^6+(1183799/195000)e^−6 <0.

This strict sign has a purely rational certificate: it is equivalent to e^12>1183799/3419. Taylor positivity gives e^12>1+12+12²/2+12³/6=373, and 373·3419>1183799. Numerically J''(0)≈−7.05840359, but the numerical value is unnecessary for the proof.

Thus the integrated proper copy objective is **not convex in Gibbs Hamiltonian coordinates even for one qubit and the complete physical XYZ frame**. Candidate and target have finite bounded Hamiltonian norm (3 at the tested candidate, artanh(.999)≈3.80 at the target). This rules out a universal convex-H optimization argument. It does not show spurious local minima, refute strict propriety, or exclude another optimization/stability theorem.

For this qubit objective, ||Ō(H)||=4r+2(e^(2r)−1). The known scalar part can be computed without measurement, but the measured Pauli coefficient 4(r+sinh r cosh r) also grows exponentially in r. Thus integrating weights does not remove low-temperature/candidate-spectrum variance costs even in dimension two.

## Exact local acquisition on commuting Pauli candidates

Suppose the supplied H is a commuting Pauli sum H=Σ_P J_P P and A is a physical local Pauli. Put Δ_A=β(H−AHA). Then H and AHA commute, so Q and AQA commute and Dlog_Q(AQA)=Q^−1AQA=e^Δ_A. Consequently

Ō_A(H)=Δ_A+e^Δ_A−I,

Δ_A=2βΣ_{P:AP=−PA}J_P P.

If each site meets at most Δ terms of locality≤k and coefficient magnitude≤J, then at most Δ terms occur in this sum, supported on at most1+Δ(k−1) sites. The exact Pauli expansion of e^Δ_A has at most2^Δ terms. Writing M=2|β|Σ_{AP=−PA}|J_P|≤2|β|ΔJ,

||Ō_A||≤M+e^M−1.

The scalar function x+e^x−1 is increasing, and its positive endpoint dominates the magnitude of its negative endpoint, proving this bound. It is independent of system size for fixed β,Δ,J,k, but exponentially dependent on local inverse-temperature strength.

All these candidate observables belong to the same commuting Pauli algebra. They can be evaluated using joint measurements of its known commuting Pauli generators and classical functions of the outcomes, without candidate Gibbs-state preparation or partition evaluation. Implementing the joint basis change and charging its gates remains necessary; supplied H is not a free unitary oracle. Alternatively the finite local Pauli expansions give directly specified local measurements. Estimating the extensive total objective with additive errorε is different from estimating its per-generator average: uniform generator selection multiplies the outcome range by the number of directions, and generally costs its square in copies for fixed extensive additive error.

This is an all-temperature exact locality statement for commuting candidates. It does not extend to general noncommuting H merely because A is local.

## Noncommuting representation and explicit high-temperature bound

Let Q=e^−βH and B=e^(βH/2) A e^−(βH/2). Entrywise in an H-eigenbasis,

Dlog_Q(AQA)=k(ad_{βH})(BB†),

k(ν)=ν/[2sinh(ν/2)].

The positive Fourier representation is

k(ν)=∫_{−∞}^∞ (π/2)sech²(πt)e^(itν)dt.

It follows by substituting u=e^(2πt): the integral becomes ∫_0^∞u^(iν/2π)/(1+u)²du=Γ(1+iν/2π)Γ(1−iν/2π)=ν/[2sinh(ν/2)]. Thus the derivative term is a positive unitary average of BB†. Its time-tail mass outside[−R,R] is exactly1−tanh(πR). In particular its operator norm is at most ||B||².

The hard object is the imaginary-time-conjugated B. Generic spectral estimation only gives ||B||²≤exp(|β|osc(H)), which can grow exponentially with system size. A local high-temperature bound can be reconstructed without that global spectrum:

Assume H is a supplied local Pauli sum of locality≤k, and the sum of absolute coefficients incident on any site is at most J_loc. For single-site A of norm1, nested commutators obey

||ad_H^m(A)||≤(2J_loc)^m ∏_{j=0}^{m−1}[1+j(k−1)].

At each step only terms meeting the current support contribute, its size is at most1+j(k−1), their absolute coefficient sum is at most J_loc times that size, and each commutator contributes factor2. For k≥2, set x=|β|J_loc(k−1), a=1/(k−1). If x<1, the imaginary-time expansion implies

||B||≤Σ_{m≥0}(a)_m x^m/m!=(1−x)^−a.

The same estimate bounds the Pauli coefficient absolute sum when H is supplied in that Pauli representation. For a truncation at orderM and any x<y<1, the remainder is bounded by

(x/y)^(M+1)(1−y)^−a.

Combining this with the real-time tail gives a candidate-H-only controlled representation of Ō_A with dimension-independent coefficient/range bounds at fixed x<1. It requires orderM=O(log1/ε) and time cutoffR=O(log1/ε) at fixed margin1−x. Each nested term has support at most1+M(k−1); the number of explicitly enumerated branches can grow factorially with M. Therefore this elementary construction alone does not prove polynomial classical compilation in accuracy or efficient gate implementation. Real-time candidate-H simulation is a charged operation, not an input oracle. The k=1 case instead has the elementary entire bound ||B||≤e^(|β|J_loc).
