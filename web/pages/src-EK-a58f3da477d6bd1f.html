# S4 — finite-horizon prediction from a coarse Navier–Stokes snapshot

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: original-to-wave analytic derivation, internally checked; external correctness and novelty UNKNOWN. No fluid-wide capability or 3D regularity claim.

## Main findings

The original four-mode A5 example does give a positive-time obstruction, but its error in a **fixed** low output is of order `E²/(νK²)` at fixed `E,ν` and large `K`. A negative Sobolev estimate gives the same cutoff power for every smooth initial field with zero coarse snapshot. Thus the derivative calculation cannot establish a cutoff-independent error in that fixed output.

The actual A5 target is larger: it predicts **all of the growing projection `P_K`**, with a supremum over the whole interval starting at zero. For that target a coherent packet of `O(K²)` unresolved modes gives a cutoff-independent obstruction. A matching upper uses the heat evolution of the observed snapshot. The exact parameter scaling in the unforced model, including arbitrary coarse snapshots, is established here up to explicit universal constants:

\[
10^{-8}E^2\min\{K^2T,(\nu+E)^{-1}\}
\ \le\ \mathfrak E_K^0(T;E,\nu)
\ \le\ \mathcal E_K(T;E,\nu,f=0)
\ \le\ 10E^2\min\{K^2T,(\nu+E)^{-1}\},\qquad K\ge16.
\tag{1}
\]

The constants are deliberately conservative. The lower is a genuine PDE estimate with a controlled nonlinear remainder, not a Galerkin simulation. The viscosity is the same fixed positive number throughout. The lower occurs at a time of order `K^{-2}` and in modes with frequencies of order `K`. It **does not** imply constant error at a fixed terminal time `T>0`. In the zero-snapshot fibre, a uniform terminal-time upper bound below tends to zero.

The restriction is **zero forcing**, an explicit allowed supplied forcing. The upper handles every coarse snapshot, while the lower already occurs on the zero-snapshot fibre. No corresponding law for arbitrary nonzero forcing is claimed.

## 1. Contract and normalization

Work on `(R/2πZ)^2` with normalized area measure, so Parseval reads

\[
\|u\|_2^2=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z^2}|u_j|^2.
\]

All initial fields are real, smooth, mean zero and divergence free. There is no forcing. Let `u(t;u0)` be the unique classical 2D solution of

\[
u_t+\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)=\nu\Delta u,
\quad \operatorname{div}u=0,\quad \nu>0.
\]

The square cutoff is exactly `|j|∞≤K`; write its orthogonal projector as `P_K`. Here `E` is a bound on the velocity **norm**, not its square: `||u0||2≤E`. We study

\[
\mathfrak E_K^0(T;E,\nu)
=\inf_z\sup_{\substack{u_0\text{ as above},\;\|u_0\|_2\le E\\P_Ku_0=0}}
\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|P_Ku(t;u_0)-z(t)\|_2.
\tag{2}
\]

The predictor receives the exact zero snapshot and the supplied `K,E,ν,T`, and no hidden coefficients. It may be arbitrarily expensive. Consequently every lower bound is an information obstruction before computation is charged. Choosing `z=0` is a legitimate upper bound. For fixed output `k=(1,1)`, let `e_{K,k}^0` replace the normed target in (2) by the complex vorticity coefficient `ω_k(t)`. Its absolute value equals the velocity norm of the real pair `{k,-k}` because `|k|²=2`.

Here `𝓔_K(T;E,ν,f=0)` is the original A5 minimax over **all** such initial fields of norm at most `E`: its algorithm receives `P_Ku0` and returns the `P_K` trajectory. Restriction of the supremum gives `𝔈_K^0≤𝓔_K`. The admissible predictor for its upper is the explicit function `z(t)=e^{νtΔ}P_Ku0`.

The zero-energy and zero-horizon cases have zero error and can be handled separately. Below assume `E,T>0`.

## 2. A negative Sobolev estimate for the entire zero-snapshot fibre

Let `A=-Δ` on mean-zero divergence-free fields and put

\[
X(t)=\|u(t)\|_2^2,\quad Z(t)=\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2,
\quad Y(t)=\|A^{-1/2}u(t)\|_2^2.
\]

The ordinary energy identity gives

\[
X(t)\le E^2,\qquad \int_0^\infty Z(t)\,dt\le {E^2\over2\nu}.
\tag{3}
\]

For this normalization the elementary periodic Ladyzhenskaya estimate

\[
\|u\|_4^2\le10\|u\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2
\tag{4}
\]

is sufficient. For completeness: apply the one-dimensional fundamental theorem to `F=|u|²` in each coordinate to get
`F(x,y)≤(2π)^{-1}∫F(s,y)ds+∫|∂xF(s,y)|ds` and its `y` version. Multiply these bounds, integrate, use Cauchy–Schwarz and `Dx+Dy≤√2||∇u||2`. In normalized norms this gives
`||u||4²≤||u||2²+2√2π||u||2||∇u||2`. Mean-zero Poincaré and `1+2√2π<10` give (4). This proof works for vector fields through `|∂i|u||≤|∂iu|`.

Testing the equation against `A^{-1}u`, integrating the nonlinear term by parts, and using (4) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac12Y'+\nu X
&\le \|u\|_4^2\|\nabla A^{-1}u\|_2
\le10\sqrt X\sqrt Z\sqrt Y\\
&\le {\nu\over2}X+{50\over\nu}ZY.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
Y'+\nu X\le {100\over\nu}ZY.
\]

An integrating factor and (3) prove the explicit all-time estimate

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\int_0^\infty\|u(t)\|_2^2\,dt
\le {Y(0)\over\nu}\exp\!\left({50E^2\over\nu^2}\right)
\le {E^2\over\nu(K+1)^2}\exp\!\left({50E^2\over\nu^2}\right).}
\tag{5}
\]

The last inequality uses the **square** cutoff: every nonzero initial coefficient has Euclidean frequency at least `K+1`. No uniform enstrophy or higher regularity bound was added. The proof uses smoothness only to justify the identities; the estimates do not depend on the initial Sobolev norms.

For `B(u,u)=P div(u⊗u)`, convolution and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

\[
|B_j(u,u)|\le |j|X(t).
\tag{6}
\]

Since `u_j(0)=0` for every resolved `j`, its mild formula gives

\[
|u_j(t)|\le |j|\int_0^t X(s)\,ds.
\tag{7}
\]

For an arbitrary fixed square output `P_J`, `J≤K`, define

\[
S_J=\left(\sum_{|j|_\infty\le J}|j|^2\right)^{1/2}
=(2J+1)\sqrt{2J(J+1)/3}.
\]

Equations (3), (5), (7) yield

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|P_Ju(t)\|_2
\le\min\left\{E,\ S_JE^2T,\ {S_JE^2e^{50(E/\nu)^2}\over\nu(K+1)^2}\right\}.}
\tag{8}
\]

For `J=K`, use `S_K≤4K²` and `S_K/(K+1)²≤2` to get

\[
\mathfrak E_K^0\le
\min\{E,4K^2E^2T,2E^2e^{50(E/\nu)^2}/\nu\}.
\tag{9}
\]

If `E≤ν`, the last bound is at most `4e^{50}E²/(ν+E)`. If `E≥ν`, the first is at most `2E²/(ν+E)`. This already gives the desired parameter powers on the zero fibre. The much better universal constant `10`, and the arbitrary-snapshot upper in (1), follow from the next direct energy comparison.

There is also a terminal-time conclusion. `X` is nonincreasing, so `TX(T)≤∫0^T X` and (5) imply, for every fixed `T>0`,

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|P_Ku(T)\|_2\le\|u(T)\|_2
\le\min\left\{E,{Ee^{25(E/\nu)^2}\over(K+1)\sqrt{\nu T}}\right\}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Thus a zero-snapshot full-field terminal-time obstruction cannot stay constant as `K→∞` at fixed `E,ν,T`. The distinction between (1) and (10) is the supremum down to time zero.

## 3. A reusable heat-comparison lemma

### A global upper for arbitrary coarse snapshots

For any admitted initial field, put `v=e^{νtΔ}u0`, `w=u−v`. Then `w(0)=0` and `w_t−νΔw=−B(u,u)`. Integration by parts, (4) and Young's inequality give

\[
\tfrac12(\|w\|_2^2)'+\nu\|\nabla w\|_2^2
\le10\sqrt X\sqrt Z\,\|\nabla w\|_2
\le {\nu\over2}\|\nabla w\|_2^2+{50\over\nu}XZ.
\]

Since `X′=−2νZ`,

\[
\int_0^t XZ\,ds={X(0)^2-X(t)^2\over4\nu}\le {E^4\over4\nu},
\qquad\|w(t)\|_2\le {5E^2\over\nu}.
\]

Although `v` uses the full initial field in this proof, its requested projection is exactly the computable `P_Kv=e^{νtΔ}P_Ku0`. Energy gives `||P_Kw||≤2E`; (6) gives `||P_Kw(t)||≤S_KE²t≤4K²E²t`. Therefore the supplied-snapshot heat predictor satisfies

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|P_Ku(t)-e^{\nu t\Delta}P_Ku_0\|_2
\le\min\{2E,5E^2/\nu,4K^2E^2T\}.}
\tag{11a}
\]

For `E≤ν`, use `5E²/ν≤10E²/(ν+E)`. For `E≥ν`, use `2E≤4E²/(ν+E)`. Combining with the short-time term proves the upper of (1) for every coarse snapshot, with constant `10`. No unresolved tail is read by the predictor.

### A local residual bound for the lower witnesses

Let `v(t)=e^{νtΔ}u0`, `w=u-v`, and suppose on a chosen interval that

\[
\|\nabla v(t)\|_\infty\le G,
\qquad\|B(v(t),v(t))\|_2\le R.
\]

The error equation tested against `w` loses both `B(v,w)` and `B(w,w)` by incompressibility. The remaining terms give

\[
\tfrac12(\|w\|_2^2)'+\nu\|\nabla w\|_2^2
\le G\|w\|_2^2+R\|w\|_2,
\]

and therefore, using a regularization at zeros of `||w||2`,

\[
\boxed{\|w(t)\|_2\le Rt e^{Gt}.}
\tag{11}
\]

For any finite Fourier set `D`, if

\[
\|P_D[B(u,u)-B(v,v)]\|_2\le H\|w\|_2,
\]

the difference from the second Picard term

\[
V_D(t)=-\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}P_DB(v(s),v(s))\,ds
\]

satisfies, provided `P_Du0=0`,

\[
\boxed{\|P_Du(t)-V_D(t)\|_2\le\tfrac12HRt^2e^{Gt}.}
\tag{12}
\]

This is an estimate for the exact PDE. No Fourier-support invariance is assumed after time zero.

## 4. The four-mode A5 pair at a nonzero time

Let `N=K+2`, `p=(N,0)`, `q=(-N+1,1)`, `k=(1,1)`, `r=p-q`, and

\[
P=N^2,\quad Q=(N-1)^2+1,\quad S=P+Q,
\quad\alpha=E/3,\quad a=\alpha N.
\]

Take `ω_{±p}=a`, `ω_{±q}=σa`, `σ∈{−1,+1}`, all other coefficients zero, and `u_j=-ij^⊥ω_j/|j|²`. The fields are real and `P_Ku0=0`. For `N≥3`,

\[
\|u_0\|_2^2=2\alpha^2(1+P/Q)<9\alpha^2=E^2.
\]

The two ordered Fourier interactions of `p,q` give

\[
c=a^2N(Q^{-1}-P^{-1})
={2\alpha^2N(N-1)\over Q}\ge2\alpha^2.
\tag{13}
\]

For the heat flow, the vorticity of `−B(v,v)` has only the four coefficients at `±k,±r`, with values `σc e^{-νSt}` at `±k` and `−σc e^{-νSt}` at `±r`. In particular,

\[
\|B(v,v)\|_2=Fc e^{-\nu St},
\qquad F=\sqrt{1+2/|r|^2}\le2,
\quad\|\nabla v\|_\infty\le4\alpha N.
\]

Write

\[
I(t)=\int_0^t e^{-2\nu(t-s)}e^{-\nu Ss}\,ds
={e^{-2\nu t}-e^{-\nu St}\over\nu(S-2)}.
\]

The leading coefficient is `σcI(t)`. By (6) applied to the difference of the two quadratic tensors, its vorticity remainder obeys

\[
|\omega_k(t)-\sigma cI(t)|
\le6\alpha Fc\,t^2e^{4\alpha Nt}.
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed the coefficient difference is at most
`|k|²(||u||2+||v||2)||w||2≤12α||w||2`, and (11) gives (14).

Set

\[
\tau_A=\min\left\{T,{1\over2\nu S},{1\over32\alpha N}\right\}
=\min\left\{T,{1\over2\nu S},{3\over32EN}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]

For `t≤τ_A`, `I(t)≥t e^{-1/2}`, `4αNt≤1/8`, and the ratio of the bound (14) to `cI(t)` is at most
`6Fαt e^{5/8}≤24αt≤1/4` because `N≥3`. Thus

\[
\boxed{e_{K,k}^0(T;E,\nu)\ge|\omega_k^+(\tau_A)|
\ge {E^2\tau_A\over12}.}
\tag{16}
\]

The two exact target trajectories are negatives: translation by `(0,π)` changes `σ` and multiplies `ω_k` by `−1`. This justifies the minimax lower, rather than merely a lower on one solution.

For comparison, (6) gives `|curl B_k|≤2X`, so (5) gives the all-input fixed-output upper

\[
\boxed{e_{K,k}^0(T;E,\nu)
\le\min\left\{E,2E^2T,{2E^2e^{50(E/\nu)^2}\over\nu(K+1)^2}\right\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

If `νN≥(8/3)E`, both caps in (15) are at least `1/(4νN²)`: use `S≤2N²` for the viscous cap and the displayed hypothesis for the nonlinear cap. Thus (16) implies the convenient bound

\[
e_{K,k}^0\ge {E^2\over12}\min\{T,(4\nu N^2)^{-1}\}.
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (17)–(18) match the cutoff and short-time powers for fixed `E,ν` as `K→∞`. They are not a uniform sharp Reynolds-number law. The derivative lower alone was insufficient; (14) is the missing nonlinear-remainder step.

## 5. Coherent packets: a lower bound for all of `P_K`

Take `K≥16`, `L=floor(K/16)≥1`, `a=8E`, and the two rectangles

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_L&=\{(40L+i,j):0\le i,j\le L\},\\
B_L&=\{(-33L+i,8L+j):0\le i,j\le L\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Set `ω_j=a` on `±A_L`, `ω_j=σa` on `±B_L`, and zero elsewhere. The negative copies make the field real. Each input is a trigonometric polynomial with `4(L+1)²` nonzero coefficients. Every input frequency has magnitude at least `32L`, and `32L>K` since `K≤16L+15`. Thus **both snapshots are exactly zero**, including the boundary case `K=31,L=1`. The energy bound is

\[
\|u_0\|_2^2
\le {4(L+1)^2a^2\over(32L)^2}
\le {a^2\over64}=E^2.
\tag{20}
\]

Define the integer interval and the positive target set

\[
I_L=\{\lceil L/2\rceil,\ldots,\lfloor3L/2\rfloor\},
\qquad D_L^+=\{(7L+s,8L+t):s,t\in I_L\}.
\]

Let `D_L=D_L^+∪(−D_L^+)`. It is contained in the square resolved by `P_K`, has `|D_L^+|≥L²`, and all its frequencies obey `|k|≤15L`. For each positive `k`, at least `L²/4` ordered choices `p∈A_L,q∈B_L` satisfy `p+q=k`. The coefficient `C(p,q)` below already includes the two convolution orderings, so this count does not count them twice.

There are no other initial support-pair types whose sums lie in `D_L^+`: `A−A` and `B−B` are near zero, `A−B` is near `(73L,−8L)`, and like-sign sums lie farther away. For every desired pair,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p\times q&\ge320L^2,\\
|p|^2&\ge1600L^2,\qquad |q|^2\le1170L^2,\\
C(p,q)&=(p\times q)(|q|^{-2}-|p|^{-2})\\
&\ge320\left({1\over1170}-{1\over1600}\right)>{1\over16},\\
|p|^2+|q|^2&\le(1682+1170)L^2=2852L^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

All contributions have the same sign `σ`. For the heat flow `v^σ`, let `V_D^σ` be its second Picard term from §3. Equations (21) and the pair count give at every `k∈D_L^+`

\[
|\operatorname{curl}V_{D,k}^\sigma(t)|
\ge {a^2L^2t\over64}e^{-2852\nu L^2t}.
\]

After dividing by `|k|≤15L` and summing the `≥L²` positive target coefficients,

\[
\boxed{\|V_D^\sigma(t)\|_2
\ge {a^2L^2t\over960}e^{-2852\nu L^2t},
\qquad V_D^-=-V_D^+.}
\tag{22}
\]

Only the heat **approximation** needs this Fourier support calculation. The exact solution immediately generates additional modes, which are included in the following error bound:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla v^\sigma\|_\infty&\le4(L+1)^2a\le16aL^2,\\
\|v^\sigma\|_\infty&\le {4(L+1)^2a\over32L}\le aL/2,\\
\|\nabla v^\sigma\|_2&\le2a(L+1)\le4aL,\\
\|B(v^\sigma,v^\sigma)\|_2&\le2a^2L^2\le4a^2L^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Both `u` and `v` have norm at most `a/8`. Applying the coefficient difference bound (6), using `|D_L|≤8L²` and `|k|≤15L`, gives

\[
\|P_D[B(u,u)-B(v,v)]\|_2
\le 12aL^2\|u-v\|_2.
\]

Thus (12) proves the exact PDE remainder

\[
\boxed{\|P_Du^\sigma(t)-V_D^\sigma(t)\|_2
\le24a^3L^4t^2e^{16aL^2t}.}
\tag{24}
\]

Choose

\[
\tau_P=\min\left\{T,{1\over6000\nu L^2},{1\over100000aL^2}\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]

The ratio of (24) to the lower bound (22) is at most

\[
23040aL^2\tau_P\,
e^{16aL^2\tau_P+2852\nu L^2\tau_P}
< {46080\over100000}<{1\over2},
\]

because the exponent is less than `1/2` and `e^{1/2}<2`. Also the exponential in (22) exceeds `1/2`. Consequently any single predicted path from the shared zero snapshot must incur

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak E_K^0
&\ge\tfrac12\|P_Du^+(\tau_P)-P_Du^-(\tau_P)\|_2\\
&\ge {a^2L^2\tau_P\over3840}
= {E^2\over60}\min\left\{L^2T,{1\over6000\nu},{1\over800000E}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

For `K≥16`, `L≥K/32`. Each entry of the last minimum is at least `1/800000` times the corresponding smaller-scale expression, hence

\[
\mathfrak E_K^0\ge {E^2\over48000000}
\min\{K^2T,(\nu+E)^{-1}\}
\ge10^{-8}E^2\min\{K^2T,(\nu+E)^{-1}\}.
\]

This completes (1).

The sum-over-modes mechanism matters: each target coefficient has velocity size of order `1/K` over the short interval, while there are order `K²` coherent target coefficients. A fixed finite collection of modes cannot retain that norm. Both features—growing output and inclusion of the initial transient—are necessary to interpret this construction.

## 6. Corrections and boundaries retained

- The initial packet attempt used `B={(-32L+i,8L+j)}`. It failed the exact square-cutoff condition at `K=31,L=1`: the edge `q_x=-31` was resolved. The corrected `−33L` in (19) fixes every `K≥16`. Constants in (21)–(26) refer only to the corrected packet.
- The four-mode A5 pair alone gives (16), not the full growing-projection lower (1).
- No finite Fourier closure is used for the exact dynamics. All omitted modes enter the energy-stability remainder (11)–(12).
- The full minimax result (1) permits arbitrary coarse snapshots and assumes zero forcing. The zero-snapshot fibre supplies its lower bound. Nonzero supplied forcing is an extension not proved here.
- Fixed-terminal-time error tends to zero by (10). The lower does not establish a persistent macroscopic uncertainty, turbulence prediction hardness, or an impossibility for a continuing accurately observed trajectory.
- The fixed-output and terminal-time negative-norm estimates carry `e^{50(E/ν)²}` or its square root, which are poor practical certificates at large Reynolds number. The full growing-projection upper (11a) instead has the elementary universal constants `2,5,4`.
- The methods are standard Fourier interaction, energy stability, and negative Sobolev estimates. The packet/minimax combination is new to this wave; its literature novelty is UNKNOWN.

## 7. What a history sensor can and cannot repair

### A precise bounded-noise impossibility

Fix an observation cutoff `J` independently of `K`, with `J≤K`. The sensor may supply the entire vector `P_Ju(t)` at any number of adaptively selected times, or continuously, on `0≤t≤h`. Its error is adversarial with `L²` norm at most `δ` at each time. It supplies no other information about `u0`.

Let

\[
U_{J,K}={S_JE^2e^{50(E/\nu)^2}\over\nu(K+1)^2}.
\]

If `δ≥U_{J,K}`, **the identically zero transcript is admissible for every initial state in the zero-snapshot fibre**, by (8): the noise is simply `−P_Ju(t)`. Sample count and adaptive timing cannot remove this ambiguity. In particular take the two packet states and `h=τ_P/2`. Their observation transcripts through `h` can agree exactly, while their future full `P_K` states at `τ_P` have half-separation at least the right side of (26). Any causal forecast from this noisy history retains that lower bound.

At fixed `E,ν,J,δ>0`, the sensor condition holds for all sufficiently large `K`. Thus bounded-precision history at a fixed spatial bandwidth does not overcome the full-output initial-layer obstruction. Avoiding this particular obstruction requires spatial bandwidth to grow, or an absolute error smaller than the stated `O(K^{-2})` level. That requirement is necessary here, not a sufficient recovery theorem. The result is a worst-case deterministic-noise statement, not an information-theoretic lower for independent Gaussian noise, which could be averaged.

The observation interval is specified causally. A fixed positive observation delay would pass beyond the `K^{-2}` initial layer for large `K`, so this argument would no longer prove a future constant error; (10) instead becomes relevant. Nor is this a result about pre-zero histories on a global attractor. Our initial polynomials are admitted at time zero and no bounded backward trajectory is supplied.

### A small positive result when the uncertainty class really is two packets

On the explicitly admitted family (19), `K,E` and the packet shape are known and the only unknown is `σ`. An initial Fourier measurement of one coefficient in `B_L`, for example `q=(-33L,8L)`, determines the sign if its **vorticity-coefficient** error is less than `a/2=4E`. Equivalently a velocity-coefficient measurement in its known polarization requires absolute error less than `a/(2|q|)=Θ(E/K)`. The retained representation is the one-bit sign together with the supplied family parameters. This is an acquisition of previously missing information; it cannot be computed from the zero snapshot.

Given that bit, the explicit predictor `V_D^σ(t)` satisfies the certified error (24) on `0≤t≤τ_P`. The snapshot-only minimax lower on the same family is `||V_D^+(t)||−R_D(t)`, while the sign-informed predictor has error at most `R_D(t)`, where

\[
R_D(t)=24a^3L^4t^2e^{16aL^2t}.
\]

At `t=τ_P/2`, the relative bound is below `0.2304`, so the added bit strictly improves the certified error for the requested packet output. At `t=τ_P`, it is below `0.4608`. To get arbitrarily small tolerance one must shorten the interval or compute further terms/full dynamics with an additional certificate. A sign bit alone is not a general Navier–Stokes closure.

There is an analogous cheaper formula for the four-mode family: one acquired phase sign gives `σcI(t)` with error (14), whereas a snapshot-only rule has the lower (16). A sample of `ω_k(t_s)` can acquire that sign after a positive delay whenever the measurement error is less than the lower signal bound `E²t_s/12`, for `t_s≤τ_A`. This uses precision of order `E²/(νK²)` on a diffusive-scale sample. It can forecast the remainder of `[t_s,τ_A]`; it cannot retroactively forecast the interval before the sample arrived.

\[
I_{p,q,k}(t)
=t e^{-\nu|k|^2t}\phi\!\left(\nu(|p|^2+|q|^2-|k|^2)t\right),
\quad\phi(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z,\quad\phi(0)=1.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{curl}V_{D,k}^\sigma(t)
=\sigma a^2\sum_{\substack{p\in A_L,q\in B_L\\p+q=k}}

\]

All terms are positive for the positive packet output; bounded-argument exponential/Taylor interval evaluation gives the stated polynomial bit bound without subtracting two nearly equal exponentials. The chosen working precision must enclose both roundoff and the analytic remainder. No claim of an efficient arbitrary-input PDE predictor follows from this family formula.

The grid-acquisition row admits a strong baseline: once the known bandlimited field has been sampled on that grid, an FFT already supplies the complete initial polynomial at `O(K² log K)` arithmetic. The one-bit representation reduces retained uncertainty for this special family but does not establish an acquisition-inclusive computational advantage over that full-data baseline. For the unrestricted smooth energy ball, a finite grid cannot certify the absent high-frequency tail without an extra tail bound; no such bound is silently assumed in (1).

## 9. Primary prior work and what is actually different

- [Foias–Prodi (1967), *Sur le comportement global…*](https://eudml.org/doc/107242) is the foundational determining-modes result. It concerns long-time determination by sufficiently many low-mode trajectories. Our zero-snapshot initial polynomials are unrestricted transient states, and the claim to be disproved is a forecast from one snapshot. No contradiction follows.
- [Foias–Jolly–Kravchenko–Titi (2012), *Determining forms…*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5134) places low-mode histories in a Banach space of bounded continuous functions. Its determining form is an infinite-dimensional system containing the attractor dynamics. A history supplied at sufficient precision is a stronger interface than (2); our noise argument also explains why sample count without precision need not substitute for that interface.
- [Azouani–Olson–Titi (2013/2014), *Continuous data assimilation using general interpolant observables*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0997) proves convergence to a reference solution from continuing observations under sufficient resolution and feedback conditions. It acquires ongoing data and pays a synchronization interval. It is a positive prior method; (1) does not refute it or supply a cheaper replacement.
- [Gouasmi–Parish–Duraisamy (2016/2017), *A priori estimation of memory effects…*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06277) explains the exact Mori–Zwanzig memory/noise representation and the difficulty of its orthogonal dynamics. Its approximate kernel method uses full-order snapshots, is exact in the linear case, and is illustrated on Burgers and Kuramoto–Sivashinsky. The packet phase is an explicit initial unresolved-forcing datum. Encoding it as a noise or memory variable does not acquire it from a zero snapshot.
- [Gozzi–Sritharan–Święch (2002), *Viscosity solutions of dynamic-programming equations…*, Proposition 1](https://www.afit.edu/BIOS/docs/viscosity.pdf) gives negative Sobolev continuous-dependence estimates for 2D Navier–Stokes, including an integrated positive-norm difference estimate from a negative-norm initial difference. This is close prior machinery for §2. We reconstructed the zero-reference case with explicit constants; the negative-norm method itself is not claimed original. A full comparison of the packet/minimax statement with the prior approximation and continuity literature remains necessary before a novelty claim.
- [Chernyshenko–Constantin–Robinson–Titi (2006/2007), *A posteriori regularity…*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607181) derives a robustness certificate around a numerical 3D trajectory and proves conditional finite termination when a strong solution exists. Our heat-comparison bound is a simple analytic residual/stability certificate for known 2D solutions, using no numerical trajectory. A residual certificate controls discretization/model error for specified data; it does not identify which of two indistinguishable initial fields was present.

The actionable conclusion is a sharp distinction among three contracts: fixed low outputs, a growing coarse field including its initial layer, and prediction after a fixed positive delay. This distinction, the explicit packet construction and its closed remainder, and the fixed-bandwidth noisy-history impossibility are the substantive progress here. External proof reconstruction and a targeted novelty audit remain open gates.
