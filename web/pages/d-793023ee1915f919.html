# Intervention-resolved motor assembly: negative outcome

Closed 2026-10-10. This investigation did **not** establish an original identifiable natural mechanism. The data failed to support identifiable delayed mechanical priming. This does not rule priming out.

## Selection

The question was whether externally imposed motion directly enables productive stator engagement, or prepares a latent state that becomes torque-generating after motion stops. A positive result could inform how mechanical work regulates biological-machine assembly.

Three substantially different candidates were compared before selection:
- Optogenetic T-cell proofreading: important recognition question, with independently manipulated ligand lifetime. Public single-cell tables exist, but cluster integration/nucleation mechanisms are extensively anticipated and separate reporters do not directly identify molecular intermediates.
- Osmotic membrane adaptation: many knockout and osmotic interventions; hydraulic, ionic, tension, membrane-area and trafficking variables remain coupled.
- Forced bacterial motor assembly: externally imposed rotation in either direction, with short OFF intervals and public high-frequency raw angles. Selected for the most direct immediate intervention test.

The indispensable observation was first sustained autonomous motion *inside* an OFF interval, rather than speed averaged across that interval. This is observable; stator attachment and occupancy are not independently observed.

## Derived candidate and prediction

Candidate: D --alpha(u)--> P --beta--> A, with P --gamma--> D. D is uncommitted, P mechanically prepared but latent, A detectable autonomous torque. During OFF, alpha=0 and ordinary activation D->A can continue at baseline rate b0:

D' = -b0 D + gamma P
P' = -(beta+gamma) P
A' = b0 D + beta P.

The initial-precursor contribution to activation density is beta P(0) exp[-(beta+gamma)t]. The conditional hazard is (b0 D+beta P)/(D+P), **not universally a decaying exponential**. An entirely primed population with gamma=0 has constant hazard beta. No universal exponential-hazard fit was claimed. The test sought a robust excess of resolved OFF onsets following motion.

A fast precursor can mature during ON or within the edge exclusion; a precursor with beta near b0 can be invisible to this comparison. This candidate is not an established novel molecular mechanism.

PREANALYSIS.md predates raw outcome inspection, although published figures were known. Extraction was developed on discovery and frozen before validation parsing. An initial freeze had a logical error: it could label a brief initial speed excursion an event without the required persistence. Corrected before validation parsing; both sources and hashes are retained. An exploratory plotting sign error is also documented; preliminary plots are not the final analysis.

Frozen observation rule:
- Use the raw long-axis/PCA revolution field at 4 kHz; centroid-angle tracking failed in some discovery traces.
- Normalize sign to each measured 1–9-s baseline, not the forcing label.
- Velocity: degree-1 Savitzky–Golay derivative over 401 samples, then 100-Hz analysis grid.
- Primary activity threshold: 5% baseline; fixed sensitivities 2.5%, 7.5%.
- Persistence: 14 of 15 successive 10-ms samples above threshold.
- Primary edge exclusion 100 ms; sensitivity 200 ms. Both exceed the derivative's ~50-ms lookahead.
- Initial activity or an onset within 50 ms of the guard is immediate/unresolved.
- A resolved later onset additionally requires initial 100-ms mean speed below half threshold and onset at least 100 ms after the guard.
- Stop at the first detected event; later pulses are not independent first arrivals.

Ten synthetic positive/negative-direction cases passed timing/classification checks. This is numerical QA, not biological calibration.

## Held-out findings

At the primary settings:
- Discovery pulse traces: 13 immediate/unresolved, 1 ambiguous, 1 resolved post-pulse onset (~0.29 s), 1 onset before the first pulse.
- Validation pulse traces: **15 immediate/unresolved, 1 resolved post-pulse onset (~0.25 s)**, among 16 traces from 15 cells.

At the 200-ms exclusion, the discovery late event becomes ambiguous and the validation late event becomes immediate/unresolved. A different validation trace is then labeled later. Candidate identity is not stable. Across all six fixed threshold/guard combinations, validation post-pulse counts range from 0 to 2 of 16.

At primary settings, validation unforced recovery gives 5 events over 80.72 s of at-risk time, descriptive rate 0.0619/s. Eligible pulse-OFF exposure is only 4.85 s, with 1 resolved event. Discovery gives 5 baseline recoveries over 57.53 s, 10.29 s eligible pulse-OFF exposure, and 1 resolved event. These sparse observations do not establish a reproducible excess over ordinary recovery.

The summary's plug-in Poisson tail values are **descriptive only**, not calibrated hypothesis tests. Baseline rates use few cells; cell heterogeneity, repeated cells, selected eligibility, shared-data sensitivity settings, noise and pinning are not fully modeled. Smaller tail values in non-primary settings were not selected as a successful result.

## Identifiability limits

Zero observed rotation does not independently establish zero bound or torque-capable stators. Glass contact and internal bearing barriers can mask torque. Forced direction also does not establish switch conformation; opposite-sign autonomous baselines occur in these raw traces.

A latent chemical precursor and an already engaged but mechanically pinned state can have the same inactive/active emissions and transition generator. Relabeling that hidden state leaves the distribution of those observed trajectories identical. Distinguishing them requires justified physical restrictions or another observable.

A fixed sinusoidal pinning model has conditional speed-plateau invariants, derived in sources/pinning_plateau_discriminant.md. They were **not tested on validation cells**. Even equal increments do not exclude general mechanical masking. This is not claimed as a new law.

## Closure/falsification ledger

- Novelty of merely a hidden stator state: rejected; established prior art.
- Mean next-OFF speed proves attachment during ON: logically unwarranted; an OFF onset can yield the same average.
- These data identify robust delayed priming: not supported.
- Priming is ruled out: explicitly not claimed.
- Bearing pinning explains these data: not established.
- Foundational natural mechanism discovered: not claimed.

The candidate is closed. No additional exploratory tests on validation cells were performed after the primary/sensitivity analysis. No protected/excluded datasets were inspected. No external contact, paid job, physical experiment, publication/push, or user-computer work occurred.
