1. EXACT ASSUMPTIONS, SIGNS AND NORMALIZATION
Origin equation (1) is an inequality of real quadratic forms on EVERY
Hermitian A, with tau=Tr/d. If Psi is not self-adjoint, it is an order on
the symmetric HS part of I-Psi; a nonsymmetric operator Loewner order is
not needed. Both outputs in the residual are normalized positive states,
because the maps are CPTP. Unitality makes I/d stationary. The square-root
argument A=sqrt(d rho_t) is Hermitian and lies in the declared full domain.
It must not be replaced by comparison on only a chosen observable span.

Bistochasticity supplies HS contraction: CP unital Schwarz gives
Psi(A)^2<=Psi(A^2) for Hermitian A; TP, trace and Cauchy-Schwarz then imply
0<=tau[A(A-Psi(A))]. Nonnegative Dirichlet forms do not require positive
HS eigenvalues. Complete positivity is used in the source's 2-by-2 block
Schwarz proof; ordinary positivity alone is not the stated premise.
D is in natural units and residuals use HALF trace distance. Switching to
full trace residuals without changing constants would be an error; the
source and both blind proofs retain the half convention consistently.

2. LEGAL SEMIGROUP AND ENTROPY DERIVATIVE
The Poisson series exp[t(Phi-I)]=exp(-t) sum t^n Phi^n/n! is norm-convergent
in finite dimension and is a convex mixture of bistochastic CP channels.
It is therefore physical even if Phi has negative HS eigenvalues. Phi
commutes with its exponential, and trace-norm contraction holds on
Hermitian inputs. No assumption of Markovian implementation, physical
access or efficient simulation of the semigroup is involved.

For faithful rho, unitality preserves a positive lower spectral bound
throughout finite times. The trace derivative of X log X is
Tr[X'(log X+I)] even if X and X' do not commute; in an instantaneous
eigenbasis the trace uses diagonal entries of the Fréchet derivative.
Trace preservation removes the I term. Hence the entropy-production
identity in origin (6) is valid and the sign is correct. The proof does
not require a fixed eigenbasis along the flow or a differentiable choice
of eigenvectors at degeneracies.

4. THE SOURCE'S CP JENSEN/AFFINITY ESTIMATE

complement gives Psi(sqrt X)^2<=Psi(X). Positivity of Psi(sqrt X), followed
by operator monotonicity of square root, yields
Psi(sqrt X)<=sqrt(Psi(X)). The source's positive-resolvent integral proves
that monotonicity, including the singular case by regularization/limits.

For A>=0, a positive difference Z>=0 has Tr(AZ)>=0 even if A and Z do not
commute, since Tr(AZ)=Tr(sqrt A Z sqrt A). Thus the source's trace pairing
gives affinity a>=1-E_Psi(A). Trace-normalized HS Cauchy-Schwarz and
Schwarz/TP show 0<=E_Psi(A)<=1 and 0<=a<=1. These sign bounds justify
squaring a>=1-E; no reversal caused by a negative lower bound occurs.

Canonical vectorizations of the two square-root states are unit vectors

7. INDEPENDENT REFERENCE EXTENSION AND FORM AMPLIFICATION

for ANY density sigma fixed by Phi and any rho supported on supp(sigma),
 e^3<=(27C/8)D(rho||sigma)b, with the SAME Psi.
For a bistochastic CP map L and Hermitian u, the exact identity
 sum_j ||K_j u-u K_j||_2^2=2Tr[u(I-L)(u)]
forces every Kraus operator to commute with sigma whenever L fixes sigma.
Form dominance forces the same zero energy for Psi, so Psi fixes sigma
as well. Support reduction is legitimate and both adjoints fix log sigma
on that support. The relative-entropy derivative therefore has exactly
the same production as the tracial derivative. Nonnegative terminal
relative entropy supplies the new budget. Regularization by sigma handles
all rank-deficient rho and sigma within the finite-relative-entropy domain.

Full Hermitian form order on B amplifies to id_A tensor Phi_B and
id_A tensor Psi_B for every finite A by expansion in a Hermitian orthonormal
basis on A. Each coefficient on B remains Hermitian, and orthogonality
expresses the global form as a sum of local forms. No self-adjointness of
Psi_B is needed; no complete-positivity property of the difference
C(I-Phi_B)-(I-Psi_B) is asserted or required.

With sigma=rho_A tensor I_B/dB, the relative entropy is exactly
I(A:B)+D(rho_B||I_B/dB). If rho_B is tracial, it is I(A:B). The SAME
