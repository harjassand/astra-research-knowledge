# Finite interior score weights cannot universally repair ordinary-frame identifiability

Status: **proved arbitrary-finite-list construction**, independently audited from r06's Vandermonde-circuit mechanism, plus exact classification of the rank-one blind frame space. Three-observable rational certificate for a four-weight list passes direct whole-score equations. This is a team deduction, not a source claim or externally established novelty result.

## Exact interface, including the weight-selection quantifier

Choose any nonempty **finite, prespecified** set of weights inside \((0,1)\). Close it under \(\lambda\mapsto1-\lambda\); let \(\Lambda\) be this closed set and \(k=|\Lambda|\). Supply every complete weighted score operator
\[
 -i[A,\omega]=\lambda\omega S_A^{\omega,\lambda}
                 +(1-\lambda)S_A^{\omega,\lambda}\omega
\]
for each frame observable and each selected weight. Faithfulness makes each score uniquely defined. Reversing the convention just exchanges complementary weights.

For dimension \(d\ge k+2\), there exist a fixed ordinarily irreducible Hermitian frame and a fixed distinct faithful state pair whose entire scores coincide at **all** weights in the list. The frame and states stay unchanged as the list is evaluated. At \(d=k+2\), three observables suffice, each scalable to norm at most one. The frame may depend on the chosen finite list; this is a worst-case obstruction to a fixed universal score-definition list.

Endpoints are excluded. Including an endpoint already supplies the endpoint identification theorem under ordinary frame irreducibility. This construction also does not rule out continuously random real weights with an ideal infinite-precision interface, adaptive weights selected from extra information, or target-restricted local-Hamiltonian theorems.

## Exact classification via a Sylvester pencil and Krylov moments

Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_d)\) with distinct positive entries, \(e=\mathbf1\), and \(P=ee^T/d\). For a real symmetric observable A, put
\[
 H=D^{1/2}AD^{1/2},\qquad
 \mathcal L_\lambda(Z)=\lambda DZ+(1-\lambda)ZD.
\]
These mutually commuting linear maps are diagonal in the matrix-unit basis, with strictly positive entry multiplier \(\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j\). The weighted filtered observable is exactly
\[
 K_\lambda=\mathcal L_\lambda^{-1}(H),\qquad
 (K_\lambda)_{ij}=\frac{\sqrt{p_ip_j}}{\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j}A_{ij}.
\]
Suppose \([K_\lambda,P]=0\) for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda\). Then
\(K_\lambda e=\kappa_\lambda e\) and \(e^TK_\lambda=\kappa_\lambda e^T\). Since
\[
 e^THe=e^T\mathcal L_\lambda(K_\lambda)e
      =\kappa_\lambda e^TDe,
\]
all \(\kappa_\lambda\) are the same scalar \(\kappa\). Subtract \(\kappa I\) from A, equivalently \(\kappa D\) from H, so every K now annihilates e on both sides.

Define
\[
 \mathcal M=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal L_\lambda,
 \qquad Z=\mathcal M^{-1}(H).
\]
Complement closure makes the entry multiplier of \(\mathcal M\) symmetric, so Z is real symmetric. The annihilation constraints are
\[
 \Bigl(\prod_{\mu\ne\lambda}\mathcal L_\mu\Bigr)(Z)e=0
 \quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda.
\]
The k homogeneous polynomials
\[
 Q_\lambda(u,v)=\prod_{\mu\ne\lambda}[\mu u+(1-\mu)v]
\]
form a basis of the homogeneous polynomials of degree k−1. Indeed, evaluate \(Q_\lambda(1,t)\) at the k distinct roots \(t_\nu=-\nu/(1-\nu)\); the evaluation matrix is diagonal with nonzero diagonal. At each fixed \(u=p_i>0\), their coefficients in powers of v remain independent. Therefore the row constraints are equivalent to
\[
 ZD^je=0\qquad (0\le j<k).
\]
The same conditions give the left constraints by symmetry. Put
\(W=\operatorname{span}\{e,De,\ldots,D^{k-1}e\}\).
Distinct p imply \(\dim W=\min(k,d)\) by the Vandermonde determinant.

Thus the complete admitted **real symmetric** frame space is
\[
 \mathbb RI\ \oplus\
 D^{-1/2}\mathcal M\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}(W^\perp)\bigr)D^{-1/2}.
\]
The sum is direct because the second summand's filtered operators annihilate e, whereas a nonzero scalar does not. Its exact dimension is
\[
 1+\frac{(d-k)(d-k+1)}2\quad(k\le d),
\]
and it is only the scalar subspace when \(k\ge d\). This proves the observed dimension pattern instead of extrapolating finite ranks. For \(k=d-1\), the admitted space is span of I and one symmetric matrix, so every frame in it is reducible. Accordingly \(d\ge k+2\) is sharp for ordinary irreducibility within this rank-one ansatz.

## Explicit circuit observables and ordinary irreducibility

For a subset S of k+1 coordinates, define a Vandermonde circuit
\[
 z_{S,i}=\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac1{\prod_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}(p_i-p_j)},&i\in S,\\[4pt]
 0,&i\notin S.
 \end{cases}
\]
Lagrange interpolation shows \(\sum_i z_{S,i}p_i^a=0\) for \(0\le a<k\): these sums are the leading-degree coefficients of the interpolants of degree-a polynomials on k+1 nodes. Thus \(z_S\in W^\perp\), with every coordinate in S nonzero.

For \(Z_S=z_Sz_S^T\), let
\[
 A_S=D^{-1/2}\mathcal M(Z_S)D^{-1/2},
\]
or explicitly
\[
 (A_S)_{ij}=\frac{z_{S,i}z_{S,j}}{\sqrt{p_ip_j}}
 \prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}[\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j].
\]
These are real symmetric. The filtered K at each weight has zero row and column sums by the moment argument above.

Let
\[
 g(u,v)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}[\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v]
       =\sum_{a=0}^k c_a u^a v^{k-a}.
\]
Every coefficient \(c_a\) is strictly positive because every weight is interior. On a k+1-coordinate support, the matrix \(g(p_i,p_j)\) equals a square Vandermonde matrix times the antidiagonal coefficient matrix times the Vandermonde transpose. Both factors are invertible. Therefore \(A_S\) is invertible on S, and its kernel is exactly the complementary coordinate subspace. All its entries inside S are nonzero.

Any matrix commuting with \(A_S\) preserves its range and kernel, and therefore commutes with the coordinate projection onto S. Taking all k+1-coordinate supports forces the common commutant to be diagonal. Nonzero offdiagonal entries of the A then force every diagonal value equal, so the ordinary common commutant is scalar.

Only d supports are actually necessary for every \(d\ge k+2\): take the cyclic windows \(S_a=\{a,a+1,\ldots,a+k\}\) modulo d. Distinct coordinates have distinct membership patterns in these windows: those patterns are rotations of a nonconstant binary block with a unique zero-to-one boundary. Thus common commutation with the d coordinate projections forces diagonal matrices. Each cyclic neighboring pair lies in a window and has a nonzero matrix entry, so the diagonal values agree around the connected cycle.

At the minimum dimension \(d=k+2\), **three observables suffice**. Here \(\dim W^\perp=2\). Three different omitted-coordinate circuits are pairwise nonparallel, since each has its unique zero coordinate and all other entries nonzero. Their three rank-one matrices span \(\operatorname{Sym}(W^\perp)\): if z0,z1 are a basis and z2=a z0+b z1 with ab≠0, its outer product supplies the missing symmetric cross term. Their images therefore span all circuit observables, whose common commutant is scalar. This argument does not depend on a genericity guess or a numerical rank threshold.

## Fixed faithful states and all-weight equality of whole scores

Normalize \(\sigma=D/\operatorname{Tr}D\), and set
\[
 Y=\tfrac12I+\tfrac d2P,\qquad
 \rho=\frac{D^{1/2}YD^{1/2}}{\operatorname{Tr}D}.
\]
The target expectation of P is1/d, so rho has trace one. Y has positive eigenvalues \((d+1)/2\) and \(1/2\), making rho faithful and distinct from sigma. Every circuit frame commutes with P after filtering, at every selected weight.

For each A and weight, define the score explicitly by
\[
 S_{ij}=i\frac{p_i-p_j}{\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j}A_{ij}.
\]
Then
\[
 A-i(1-\lambda)S=D^{1/2}K_\lambda D^{-1/2},\qquad
 A+i\lambda S=D^{-1/2}K_\lambda D^{1/2}.
\]
Substituting rho turns the score equation into \([K_\lambda,Y]=0\), which holds. The same supplied entire score therefore solves both faithful-state equations, and uniqueness proves equality. There is no discarded adjoint or imaginary score component.

Choose \(q_i=1+i/(4d)\), \(p_i=q_i^2\), with indices0 throughd−1. These distinct nodes lie in[1,2]. Square roots, circuit coefficients, frame observables and both states are rational for rational weights. Each A can be scaled independently by a nonzero rational to norm at most one; the commutant and equalities are preserved.

This target has condition number at most2 and \(\sigma\ge I/(2d)\). The competitor satisfies \(\rho=\sigma/2+vv^T/2\), where \(v=D^{1/2}e/\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}D}\), hence \(\rho\ge I/(4d)\). Testing the projector vvT gives
\[
 D_{\rm tr}(\rho,\sigma)\ge
 \tfrac12\left(1-\frac{\sum_i p_i^2}{(\sum_i p_i)^2}\right)
 \ge\tfrac12(1-2/d)\ge1/6.
\]
Thus this obstruction does not require an extreme target spectrum or a nearly coincident state pair. Uniform conditioning of the generator frame as k grows is not claimed.

## Exact four-weight certificate and costs

`work/r03_quantum/cycle3/finite_weight_circuit_certificate.py` uses standard-library Fraction arithmetic for
\(\Lambda=\{1/4,3/4,1/3,2/3\}\), \(d=6\).
It constructs six omitted-coordinate circuits, verifies all their moments and nonzero support determinants, and saves exact linear coefficients expressing all six circuit observables in the first **three**. It scales each to norm at most one and directly verifies the whole score equations for both fixed states, every one of the three observables, and all four weights. The target condition number is at most2, sigma/rho minimum eigenvalue bounds are1/12 and1/24, and the exact projector trace-distance lower bound is saved. Certificate: `results/r03_quantum/cycle3/finite_weight_circuit_certificate.json`. An earlier geometric-spectrum version is preserved in `finite_weight_circuit_geometric_certificate.json`; the theorem does not depend on either particular node choice.

The six-dimensional example is a quantum qudit witness. The universal construction also works at any larger power-of-two dimension, with the cyclic-window family, if a qubit Hilbert space is required. Its observables are unrestricted generally nonlocal frames; this is not a finite-weight counterexample for the fixed physical local X/Y/Z interface.

For a universal list fixed in advance in dimension d, this family gives a cardinality obstruction whenever the complement-closed weight count is at most d−2. A list of r non-midpoint weights has closed count at most2r, so constant-size lists cannot repair arbitrary-dimensional identifiability in this frame class. The dimension of an n-qubit Hilbert space is exponential in n. This is a lower bound for this proposed fixed-list interface, not a lower bound for every possible adaptive or randomized experiment. Ideal continuous random weights deserve separate analysis: analytic dependence and endpoint identification can make bad weights a measure-zero set for each fixed state/frame, even though each fixed weight has a worst-case collision. Finite precision and quantitative conditioning must be charged before using that escape as a practical repair.
