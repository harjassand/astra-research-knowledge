## Strongest results from this pass

1. **An exact local test for the positive prism formula (317).** For monotone
   f<=g on a poset, the one-term right-prism prescription is a chain map on
   every simplex precisely when, for each comparable x<y, f(x)=g(x) or
   g(x)=g(y). The universal proof gives both necessity in degree 1 and
   sufficiency by an all-degree cancellation. It relaxes the manuscript's
   global constant-complement and sieve assumptions for this formula. An
   antichain complement with a right-adjoint retraction admits a single
   stationary contraction extending across arbitrary attaching targets.
   The fork a<b,a<c gives an explicit example beyond the original local
   lemma. Dropping the criterion fails on three vertices. This claim is
   proved at the normalized-chain level; its strict Gray realization uses
   the published tensor comparison. Novelty is unclaimed.

2. **A quadratic family of conditional chromatic losses (314).** The explicit
   class-two group F_p^d x Lambda^2 F_p^d, for odd p, has d generators,
   exponent p, and order p^{d(d+1)/2}. Every cyclic section then has order p,
   giving ell(G,H)=log_p[G:H]. Conditional on release Theorem 1.1, the full
   fixed-point loss from the trivial subgroup is d(d+1)/2 at every target
   height, exceeding the elementary-quotient lower bound by binom(d,2).
   The group proof is independent and complete; the chromatic lower bound
   remains conditional. The d=2 odd-primary gap is already visible as an
   unresolved comparison in Kuhn-Lloyd's checked prior source. No novelty
   claim is made for the consequence.

3. **A reusable profinite recognition mechanism (314).** The independently
   reconstructed Theorem 5.1 proof converts all open-subgroup weighted
   congruences plus a finite-section rank bound and homomorphism-count
   integrality into a bounded series of Z_p factors. The key discriminator
   is a count c_h tending to zero by congruences but to a nonzero p-adic
   limit if the remaining kernel has finite abelianization. These are
   infinite hypotheses, so this is not a finite pro-p group decision tool.
