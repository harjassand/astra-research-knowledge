# A14 — Graph-free causal policy values under hidden confounding

Date: 2026-10-09  
Status: internally derived identification boundary, lower bound, finite-confidence policy race, and bounded-IV sensitivity interval; executable finite diagnostics; no sample/computation obstruction removed beyond known best-arm methods; novelty and external review unresolved. This is not a validated breakthrough claim.

## Target and exact information contract

The target is to select a useful intervention protocol from a finite set without a supplied causal graph, a bound on hidden-state cardinality, or an assumption that the physical actuator perfectly sets the received treatment. The exact estimand is the population value of deploying each operational protocol. An operational protocol includes its random assignment rule, imperfect actuator, adherence behavior, and any direct consequences of the offer or device. Its intention-to-treat value is actionable because it describes the policy that can actually be deployed.

Let the finite protocol catalogue be \(\mathcal A=\{0,1,\ldots,K-1\}\). Each fresh experimental unit has an arbitrary latent state \(U\sim P\) and potential outcomes \(Y(a;U)\in[0,1]\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\). No factorization, graph, Markov property, latent-state cardinality, or relationship between distinct actions is imposed. Units do not interfere. At round \(t\), after history \(H_{t-1}\), the learner selects \(A_t\); assignment is generated by a certified randomizer independent of the new unit's full potential-outcome table conditional on \(H_{t-1}\). The learner observes only \(Y_t=Y(A_t;U_t)\). The reset/new-unit interface must return an independent draw from the target population; its acquisition and validation costs are charged below.

The estimand is

\[
\mu_a=\mathbb E[Y(a;U)],\qquad
\Delta_a=\mu_a-\mu_0,
\qquad
a^*\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\{\mu_a-\lambda d_a\},
\tag{1}
\]

where \(d_a\) is a known deployment cost and \(\lambda\ge0\) is the decision maker's known exchange rate. Protocol 0 may be the status quo. If a trial arm is an observational regime, its value is simply the outcome under that regime; observational data are not presumed to identify the value of another regime.

For each arm, let \(v_a\) be its one-time assignment/actuator specification and safety-audit cost, \(c_a^{\mathrm{act}}\) its per-trial actuation cost, \(c_a^{\mathrm{unit}}\) the cost of a fresh unit or validated reset, and \(c_a^{\mathrm{assay}}\) the outcome measurement cost. The charged per-trial cost is

\[
c_a=c_a^{\mathrm{act}}+c_a^{\mathrm{unit}}+c_a^{\mathrm{assay}},
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_a v_a+\sum_a c_a N_a+C_{\mathrm{IV}}+C_{\mathrm{obs}}+C_{\mathrm{compute}}.
\tag{2}
\]

If units are reused, the reset procedure has to be validated as returning the required independent target state; its validation cost belongs in \(v_a\), and each reset belongs in \(c_a^{\mathrm{unit}}\). If this independence condition is false, the confidence guarantee below does not apply. For \(N_{\mathrm{obs}}\) passive observations at unit cost \(c_{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{obs}}=c_{\mathrm{obs}}N_{\mathrm{obs}}\). Set \(C_{\mathrm{IV}}=0\) for direct policy-value selection. If the target is a receipt effect, \(C_{\mathrm{IV}}=c_{\mathrm{IV}}\) is the external cost of acquiring exclusion/monotonicity evidence or a defensible sensitivity bound; without such evidence, the report remains at the policy-assignment estimand. Computation for maintaining all arms is \(O(K)\) per naive update and can be reduced to \(O(\log K)\) for selection with a heap; validation, sampling, reset, and assay costs are not hidden in this count.

The randomizer's exogeneity is itself an acquired design property. Its audit cost is included in \(v_a\) (or a shared setup cost). If no credible randomization design is available, observed arm differences are associational and (1) is not identified. For an actual-receipt estimand, exclusion and monotonicity are separate assumptions that cannot be acquired from trial data alone; the external evidence or sensitivity bound supporting them has a separate cost \(c_{\mathrm{IV}}\), defined below.

## What is identified

### Operational policy values

Under the stated assignment and sampling contract,

\[
\mathbb E[Y_t\mid H_{t-1}, A_t=a]=\mu_a.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the mean of every *directly randomized, actually deployable protocol* is identified by its own trial samples. The actuator may be imperfect and may affect outcomes through more than treatment receipt; those features are part of \(Y(a;U)\). This requires no graph, latent-state count, or exclusion restriction. It identifies a protocol effect, not an effect of a hidden treatment received by a unit.

An observational sample from a natural regime can estimate that regime's own value. Without additional assumptions, it gives no information about \(\mu_a\) for a different intervention. In the unrestricted model, potential-outcome tables for distinct policies may be unrelated.

### Receipt effects and instrument validity

For binary randomized assignment \(Z\), binary treatment receipt \(D\), and outcome \(Y\in[0,1]\), define potential receipts \(D(z)\) and potential outcomes \(Y(z,d)\). Randomization identifies

\[
\Delta_Y=\mathbb E[Y\mid Z=1]-\mathbb E[Y\mid Z=0],\qquad
\Delta_D=\mathbb E[D\mid Z=1]-\mathbb E[D\mid Z=0].
\tag{4}
\]

These are the causal effects of assignment and assignment on receipt. They do not by themselves identify the effect of receipt. Under consistency, no interference, random assignment, monotonicity \(D(1)\ge D(0)\), and exclusion \(Y(z,d)=Y(d)\), with \(p_C=\Delta_D>0\), the complier average effect is

\[
\theta_C=\mathbb E[Y(0,1)-Y(0,0)\mid D(1)>D(0)]
 =\frac{\Delta_Y}{\Delta_D}.
\tag{5}
\]

This is not the population average treatment effect in general, and it does not identify which individuals are compliers. If exclusion is only supported up to the externally acquired sensitivity bound

\[
\sup_{z,z',d,u}|Y(z,d;u)-Y(z',d;u)|\le\gamma,
\tag{6}
\]

then the monotonicity decomposition gives the valid outer bound

\[
\left|\Delta_Y-p_C\theta_C\right|\le\gamma,
\qquad
\theta_C\in[-1,1]\cap
\left[\frac{\Delta_Y-\gamma}{p_C},\frac{\Delta_Y+\gamma}{p_C}\right].
\tag{7}
\]

The interval need not be sharp when compliance-type outcome distributions impose extra restrictions; it is sharp in the full-compliance submodel. If \(p_C\) is small, the direct-effect uncertainty expands as \(\gamma/p_C\). If \(\gamma\) is unknown, the data do not justify a point claim about \(\theta_C\). A randomization audit can establish assignment exogeneity, and first-stage samples can estimate relevance. Exclusion and monotonicity are not generally testable from the observed \((Z,D,Y)\) distribution; their validation is external design evidence, with cost \(c_{\mathrm{IV}}\), or a stated sensitivity parameter \(\gamma\). No causal graph is needed for this distinction.

### Finite confidence interval for the IV sensitivity model

Take \(n_z\) independent units in each assignment arm. Estimate the four means of \(Y,D\) in the two arms. For \(0<\delta<1\), set

\[
r_z=\sqrt{\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{2n_z}},\qquad e_Y=e_D=r_0+r_1.
\tag{8}
\]

Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over these four means imply, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), that both true contrasts lie in

\[
I_Y=[\widehat\Delta_Y-e_Y,\widehat\Delta_Y+e_Y],\qquad
I_D=[\widehat\Delta_D-e_D,\widehat\Delta_D+e_D].
\tag{9}
\]

Under monotonicity, intersect \(I_D\) with \([0,1]\) to obtain \([p^-,p^+]\). If this intersection is empty, the data conflict with the monotonicity model and no CACE interval is reported. If \(p^-=0\), the data do not certify a positive first stage and the conservative CACE interval remains \([-1,1]\). If \(p^->0\), a simultaneous \(1-\delta\) interval is

\[
[-1,1]\cap\left[
\min_{p\in\{p^-,p^+\}}\frac{\widehat\Delta_Y-e_Y-\gamma}{p},
\max_{p\in\{p^-,p^+\}}\frac{\widehat\Delta_Y+e_Y+\gamma}{p}
\right].
\tag{10}
\]

As the first stage becomes unresolved or the direct-effect sensitivity bound \(\gamma\) grows, this interval can widen to all of \([-1,1]\); without any external bound, use the full interval. When \(\gamma=0\) and \(p_C\ge p_0>0\), a rough sufficient scaling for Wald error at most \(\varepsilon\) is \(n_0,n_1=O((\varepsilon^2p_0^2)^{-1}\log(1/\delta))\). This is the weak-instrument cost, in addition to the external validity-acquisition cost.

## Finite-confidence policy-selection procedure

For direct operational policy values, a cost-aware confidence race gives a finite guarantee without using an inferred graph. Assume all candidate protocols have passed the externally justified randomization and execution audit; the fixed total audit cost \(V=\sum_a v_a\) is paid before selection. Let \(\widehat\mu_a(n)\) be the average of the first \(n\) fresh/reset independent outcomes from arm \(a\). Define an anytime radius

\[
r(n)=\sqrt{\frac{\log(K\pi^2 n^2/(3\delta))}{2n}},
\qquad
L_a=\widehat\mu_a-r(n_a)-\lambda d_a,
\quad
U_a=\widehat\mu_a+r(n_a)-\lambda d_a.
\tag{11}
\]

For every arm and sample count \(n\), Hoeffding gives a two-sided failure probability at most \(6\delta/(K\pi^2n^2)\). Summing over all arms and all \(n\ge1\) gives total failure probability at most \(\delta\). This event is simultaneous over adaptive sample choices and stopping times.

The procedure maintains an active set. At each update it eliminates \(a\) only when

\[
U_a<\max_{b\ \mathrm{active}}L_b-\varepsilon.
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(\ell\) maximize \(L_a\). It returns \(\ell\) if \(\max_{a\ \mathrm{active}}U_a-L_\ell\le\varepsilon\), and samples another active arm otherwise. To account for heterogeneous trial costs, the executable version samples an active arm maximizing \(r(n_a)/\sqrt{c_a}\), which prioritizes uncertainty reduction per square-root unit cost; this heuristic affects cost, not validity. A deterministic finite fallback is used: choose \(n_0\) with \(r(n_0)\le\varepsilon/2\), sample every still-active arm until it reaches \(n_0\), and return the active arm with largest empirical net value \(\widehat\mu_a-\lambda d_a\). On the simultaneous confidence event, an optimal arm is never eliminated by (12). The stopping rule therefore returns an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal policy, and the fallback does also because every surviving arm has radius at most \(\varepsilon/2\). Thus

\[
\Pr\{\max_a(\mu_a-\lambda d_a)-(\mu_{\widehat a}-\lambda d_{\widehat a})\le\varepsilon\}\ge1-\delta.
\tag{13}
\]

The fallback has worst-case sample cost at most \(\sum_a c_a n_0\), plus \(V\), computation, and any observational samples. The adaptive race can stop earlier on separated instances, but this record proves no cost-optimality or asymptotic improvement. A known sensitivity bound \(\eta_a\) on the difference between the trial-frame value and target-frame value is included by replacing \(L_a,U_a\) with \(L_a-\eta_a,U_a+\eta_a\); without a justified bound, model shift is unresolved. For \(Y\in[0,1]\), a total-variation bound \(\eta_a\) on the relevant latent-state distribution implies such a mean shift bound.

The procedure is executable in `diagnostics.py`. It uses only direct randomized policy samples; it does not use observational outcomes as counterfactual labels or pool one policy's outcomes into another's estimate. If an observational arm is an allowed deployable policy, include it as an ordinary candidate with its own measurement cost. Otherwise observational samples can be retained for diagnostics, but the unrestricted model gives them no guaranteed information about untried policy means.

## Exact graph-free obstruction

### Receipt effect is not identified from an apparently perfect first stage

Let \(Z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(D(z)=z\), and consider two models:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&Y(z,d)&\mathbb E[Y(z,1)-Y(z,0)]\\ \hline
M_1&d&1\\
M_2&z&0.
\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]

Both produce exactly \(P(Z,D,Y)=1/2\) on \((0,0,0)\) and \(1/2\) on \((1,1,1)\). Both have a randomized assignment, perfect first stage, and monotonicity. Their treatment-receipt effects differ. The only relevant distinction is exclusion. Therefore no sample size, adaptive intervention schedule, or estimator based on \((Z,D,Y)\) alone can decide whether the Wald ratio is a receipt effect. Direct assignment-policy effect is 1 in both models and remains identified. The script verifies the exact observed distributions with rational arithmetic.

### Observations do not identify unrandomized effects

Let \(U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and naturally observed \(X=U\). In model \(O_1\), set \(Y=U\) and let \(Y\) ignore \(X\). In model \(O_2\), set \(Y=X\). Both have the same observational law \(P(X=0,Y=0)=P(X=1,Y=1)=1/2\), while

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
&\mathbb E[Y\mid do(X=0)]&\mathbb E[Y\mid do(X=1)]&\text{effect}\\ \hline
O_1&1/2&1/2&0\\
O_2&0&1&1.
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]

The hidden variable is binary. Unlimited passive sampling cannot choose between these causal effects. Randomized intervention samples or additional acquired assumptions are necessary.

### An unrestricted catalogue has an armwise lower bound

Let \(K\ge2\) be the number of candidate intervention protocols, \(0<\varepsilon<1/8\), \(0<\delta<1/2\), and use bounded Bernoulli policy outcomes; set the deployment penalties equal so the objective is to maximize \(\mu_a\). Any passive observational stream or fixed status-quo arm is held to the same distribution across the instances below. For each putative best intervention arm \(j\), define instance \(P_j\) by

\[
\mu_j=1/2+2\varepsilon,\qquad \mu_i=1/2\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{16}
\]

For each candidate intervention \(k\ne j\), define alternative \(Q_{jk}\) to agree on every intervention arm except \(k\), where \(\mu_k=1/2+4\varepsilon\). A fixed baseline, if available, has mean \(1/2\) under all these instances. In \(P_j\), every non-\(j\) action has regret \(2\varepsilon>\varepsilon\), so an \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-PAC method outputs \(j\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\). In \(Q_{jk}\), arm \(k\) is the unique \(\varepsilon\)-optimal action and outputting \(j\) has probability at most \(\delta\). Data processing for the event \(E=\{\widehat a=j\}\) and the adaptive likelihood-ratio chain rule give

\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\mathrm{transcript}}\|Q_{jk}^{\mathrm{transcript}})
=\mathbb E_{P_j}[N_k]
\cdot D\!\left(\mathrm{Bern}(1/2)\middle\|\mathrm{Bern}(1/2+4\varepsilon)\right)
\ge \operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta),
\tag{17}
\]

and therefore

\[
\mathbb E_{P_j}[N_k]\ \ge\
\frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{D(\mathrm{Bern}(1/2)\|\mathrm{Bern}(1/2+4\varepsilon))},
\qquad
D(\mathrm{Bern}(1/2)\|\mathrm{Bern}(1/2+4\varepsilon))
=-\tfrac12\log(1-64\varepsilon^2).
\tag{18}
\]

Only samples from arm \(k\) have different conditional laws under \(P_j,Q_{jk}\), so adaptivity cannot obtain this information from other arms. Summing (18) over all \(k\ne j\) yields

\[
\mathbb E_{P_j}\!\left[\sum_{k\ne j}N_k\right]
\ge (K-1)\frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{-\tfrac12\log(1-64\varepsilon^2)}
=\Omega\!\left(\frac{K}{\varepsilon^2}\log\frac1\delta\right)
\quad(\varepsilon\to0,\ \delta\to0).
\tag{19}
\]

If a sample of arm \(k\) costs \(c_k\), the corresponding cost lower bound on instance \(P_j\) is

\[
\mathbb E_{P_j}\!\left[\sum_{k\ne j}c_kN_k\right]
\ge\frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{-\tfrac12\log(1-64\varepsilon^2)}
\sum_{k\ne j}c_k.
\tag{20}
\]

Add the fixed validity/setup cost separately. Any observational samples whose distribution is held identical across the instances contribute zero KL and cannot weaken this lower bound. The construction is a valid potential-outcome model: one fresh latent variable can encode the potential-outcome vector for all policies, with the required Bernoulli marginal for each selected policy. No graph structure ties the arms together. For a catalogue of all binary interventions on \(d\) controls, \(K=2^d\), so unrestricted graph-free best-policy discovery requires exponentially many intervention samples in the worst case. This lower bound is an identifiability and information obstruction, not a claim that every structured problem is hard.

### Attempted compression and why it is not a result here

An additive or sparse low-order model for \(a\mapsto\mu_a\) would link arm values and could reduce the number of samples. That is an additional assumption on the *interventional response surface*; it does not follow from absence of a supplied graph or from arbitrary latent confounding. It also does not solve treatment-receipt identification when assignment exclusion is unknown. I did not acquire domain evidence that validates such a response-surface promise, derive a new sample/computation bound under realistic actuator costs, or beat a matched combinatorial-bandit method. No compression claim is made.

## Matched-method comparison

The fair baseline for the exact unrestricted model is a stochastic best-arm identification algorithm with arm-specific sampling costs and the same direct assignment rights. Each protocol is an arm. A causal graph, if available and validated, can provide side information; this task supplies none. The confidence race above is an anytime-Hoeffding version of ordinary pure exploration, not a new causal-discovery method. Garivier and Kaufmann's fixed-confidence Track-and-Stop gives the unit-cost information-theoretic lower bound and an asymptotically optimal allocation for one-parameter bandits. Replacing sample allocation by cost allocation yields the immediate characteristic-rate expression

\[
\Gamma_c(\mu)=\sup_{x\in\Delta_K}\inf_{\mu'\in\mathrm{Alt}(\mu)}
\sum_{a=1}^K\frac{x_a}{c_a}\,D(\nu_{\mu_a}\|\nu_{\mu'_a}),
\tag{21}
\]

where \(x_a\) is the fraction of total experimental cost spent on arm \(a\); hence any δ-correct procedure in that bandit family needs expected cost at least \(\operatorname{kl}(\delta,1-\delta)/\Gamma_c(\mu)\). This is a direct cost reparameterization of the standard change-of-measure argument, not an independently established optimal algorithm in this record. A cost-tracking Track-and-Stop is the strongest equal-information comparator for separated Bernoulli instances; developing and proving its finite-cost optimality is not claimed here.

Malek, Aglietti, and Chiappa (ICML 2023) study unknown-graph interventions and additive combinatorial bandits, but their unknown-graph result assumes no hidden confounder between outcome \(Y\) and any ancestor of \(Y\), access to global interventions, and an additive outcome structure. They explicitly give an exponential-hardness example before imposing additivity. This A14 model removes the no-hidden-confounding restriction for *policy values* through direct random assignment, while its lower bound shows that removing graph assumptions and response structure leaves a generic exponential catalogue.

Jamshidi, Etesami, and Kiyavash (CLeaR 2024) allow hidden confounders and nonuniform observation/intervention costs, and combine observational and interventional data through side information, but their formal setting supplies the underlying ADMG. Their causal transfer cannot be used under this A14 information contract. Maiti, Nair, and Sinha (UAI 2022) also study hidden confounders but supply a causal graph and state their horizon in exploration rounds without an explicit nonuniform arm-cost model. These are stronger methods when their structural input is acquired and charged; the direct-policy bandit is the matched fallback when no graph is supplied.

Angrist, Imbens, and Rubin (JASA 1996) establish the complier-effect interpretation of the IV ratio under random assignment, exclusion, monotonicity, and relevance, and explain that without those assumptions the ratio has no such causal interpretation. Their discussion also treats sensitivity to exclusion violations. The two-model witness (14) is the minimal finite obstruction for this task's requested validity-acquisition accounting.

## Counterexamples, costs, and limits

* **Unknown exclusion:** models (14) give identical observed trials and different receipt effects. More outcome samples do not acquire the exclusion restriction. External design validation or an explicit \(\gamma\) sensitivity contract is required.
* **Observational confounding:** models (15) have identical passive data and different intervention effects. Observational cost is not credited toward causal-effect estimation unless an additional identification condition is supplied.
* **Unstructured action catalog:** (19)–(20) preclude a sublinear-in-\(K\) worst-case intervention sample or cost guarantee. An action graph, response-surface constraint, instrument validity, or other structure must be acquired before a compression theorem is possible.
* **Weak instrument:** when \(p_C\) is small, ratio error scales inversely with the first stage; when external direct-effect uncertainty \(\gamma\) exceeds the effect tolerance times \(p_C\), sampling cannot recover the desired resolution.
* **Reset failure, interference, selection drift, or randomizer failure:** these invalidate (3) unless a quantified target shift \(\eta_a\) or a dependent-data theorem is supplied. Independence of repeated resets is not inferred from the reward stream.
* **Finite action catalog:** computation is linear in \(K\) for straightforward confidence maintenance, but enumerating a catalog of size \(2^d\) already incurs exponential representation/search cost. No oracle-free polynomial optimization result is established.

## Executable finite evidence

`diagnostics.py` uses only the Python standard library. It verifies exactly, with rational probabilities, that the two exclusion models have the same observed \((Z,D,Y)\) distribution and different receipt effects; it also checks the observational-confounding witness. It computes the closed-form KL lower bound and a finite uniform-sampling PAC budget, then runs the confidence race on seeded synthetic Bernoulli arms as an illustration. The script is evidence that the finite constructions and example policy execute as written; it is not a data experiment, an external validation of assumptions, or evidence of novelty.

## Claim ledger

* **INTERNALLY DERIVED:** direct randomized policy means (3); IV nonidentifiability witness (14); observational nonidentifiability witness (15); unrestricted action lower bound (18)–(20); finite confidence race guarantee (13); finite-sample IV sensitivity interval (10).
* **FINITE-TESTED:** `diagnostics.py` checks the finite witness tables, the Bernoulli KL formula, the uniform finite-sample bound, and one seeded confidence-race run.
* **KNOWN:** standard best-arm fixed-confidence lower bounds and Track-and-Stop; standard IV complier interpretation under randomization, exclusion, monotonicity, and relevance; graph-based causal-bandit methods under their stated structural inputs.
* **UNRESOLVED:** novelty; a graph-free structural response class that survives realistic intervention/reset costs and yields a verified improvement; cost-optimal finite-confidence tracking with heterogeneous fixed and per-sample validation costs; external acquisition protocol for exclusion/monotonicity; dependent reset or drifting-population extensions.
* **NOT ACHIEVED:** a transformative method for learning arbitrary receipt effects or compressing arbitrary intervention catalogs without structure. The supported consequence is a precise capability boundary: learn randomized deployable-policy values directly; require externally acquired assumptions for receipt effects; expect exponential cost in an unstructured exponential catalog.

No Astra theorem or historical causal claim was used to set or support this agenda.
