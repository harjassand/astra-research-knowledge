ERRATUM TO FROZEN INITIAL — FINITE-N SPIN MULTIPLICITY
Worker c07_l09. 7 October 2026. INITIAL.txt is preserved unchanged.

The multiplicity line in INITIAL.txt is transcribed incorrectly. It states

  m_j=(2j+1)/(N/2+j+1) * binom(N+1,N/2-j),

which mixes the prefactor from one equivalent formula with the binomial from
the other. The correct equivalent forms are

  m_j=(2j+1)/(N+1) * binom(N+1,N/2-j),

and

  m_j=(2j+1)/(N/2+j+1) * binom(N,N/2-j).

For N=2 these give m_0=m_1=1 and sum_j (2j+1)m_j=4, as required by the
four-dimensional two-qubit Hilbert space. The frozen line gives m_0=3/2 and
m_1=1, for total weighted dimension 9/2.

The displayed finite-N coefficient formula is therefore not literally correct
as written. There is a simple repair for its order bound: if \tilde m_j is the
frozen multiplicity and m_j is the correct one, then

  \tilde m_j/m_j=(N+1)/(N/2+j+1) in [1,2].

All terms defining U_N and D_N in INITIAL.txt are nonnegative. Thus the
correct numerator is at most the frozen numerator, and the correct
denominator is at least half the frozen denominator. The corrected quotient
is bounded by twice the quotient computed with the frozen weights. This
salvages the same O_(M,B)(N^-3/2) order argument, with a factor-2 looser
constant, provided the original scalar bounds are valid. It does not salvage
the exact displayed finite-N certificate or its stated coefficient; those
must be recomputed with m_j before numerical use. The Duhamel operator estimate
itself and the fixed-M,B asymptotic order argument use the correct
representation decomposition conceptually; the transcription error is in the
displayed finite-N coefficient formula.

Independent check: for spin-half tensor powers,

  (C^2)^(tensor N) = direct_sum_j V_j tensor C^(m_j),
  dim(C^2)^(tensor N)=2^N=sum_j (2j+1)m_j.

At N=2 the j=0 singlet and j=1 triplet each occur once. For the N=2 exact
product-mixture witness in the accompanying small-N audit, the density is
computed directly from singlet/triplet projectors and does not use the
incorrect frozen multiplicity formula.
