C03_L10 FINAL — ARBITRARY-MATRIX LOW-SECTOR ESTIMATOR
7 October 2026

RESULT

For an explicit F in Q(i)^(n x n) and 0<=k<=floor(n/2), define

 c_k(F)=sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty} |det F[I,J]|^2.

There is an exact-arithmetic nonnegative unbiased Rademacher estimator X_k
with E X_k=c_k and E X_k^2 <= 4*9^k*c_k^2. Hence independent batch means and
their median give relative error epsilon and failure probability delta using
O(9^k epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) samples. Each sample is computed in polynomial
bit time as the t^k coefficient of the formal series

 sqrt(det(I+t A_s^* A_s)),   A_s=diag(s)F-F^T diag(s),

where s is uniform on {+1,-1}^n. The determinant's formal square root is exact;
no spectral approximation or minor-sum oracle is used. Thus c_k is
approximable in 9^k poly(n,L,epsilon^-1,log(1/delta)) bit time. This is
polynomial for fixed k and for k=O(log n), but remains exponential for dense
sectors k=Theta(n). It does not solve the unrestricted hard-BCS counting
problem or give a dense-sector FPRAS.

DEEPER ATTACK AND COUNTEREXAMPLE

Near-top Jacobi duality for invertible F is

 c_k(F)=|det F|^2 sum_{|K|=|L|=n-k, K union L=[n],
                              |K intersect L|=n-2k} |det F^-1[K,L]|^2.

It is an exact identity, but when k is near n/2 it produces large inverse
minors with a small, exact overlap; it does not produce disjoint small
minors of F^-1. The naive conversion to c_{n-2k}(F^-1) fails on n=4,
k=1, F=I+2E_12: c_1(F)=4, while c_2(F^-1)=0. The correct Jacobi overlap sum
equals 4. The finite audit `revisions/near_top_jacobi_audit.py` verified the
identity on 50 invertible rational examples and emits the exact counterexample
in `revisions/near_top_jacobi_audit.json`. This rules out only the naive dual
shortcut, not all high-density approaches.
