# SOL/A — Finite-power Bernoullicity investigation

## 1. Status of the primary problem

No exact Bernoulli conjugacy has been established. In particular, I have not proved that \\(K^m\\) is Bernoulli for any finite \\(m\\), nor obtained \\(2^G\cong_G4^G\\).

The central difficulty is stronger than constructing arbitrarily many independent Bernoulli factors. It is to construct an independent generating process whose translates recover all of \\(K^m\\), modulo null sets.

A relevant general problem was explicitly formulated by Tim Austin in The Geometry of Model Spaces for Probability-Preserving Actions of Sofic Groups (2016):

> Is every complemented factor of a Bernoulli system itself Bernoulli?

Austin identified this as unresolved for nonamenable groups at the time.&#x20;

\\[ X\cong_G X\times K \\]

makes it an independently complemented factor of a Bernoulli shift. Thus a theorem establishing Bernoullicity for these particular algebraic retracts would solve a substantially more structured problem than arbitrary Bernoulli-factor classification.

The existence of non-Bernoulli factors of Bernoulli shifts, demonstrated through Popa's constructions, does not settle this case: those examples do not automatically possess the required independent complement.&#x20;

## 2. Exact partial theorem: Bernoullicity on free subgroups

There is a rigorous structural theorem that does not require the inherited coloring lemma.

Theorem (free-subgroup Bernoulli restriction). Suppose

\\[ ab=1,\qquad p=1-ba\ne0 \\]

in \\(\mathbb F_2[G]\\), and let \\(K=\ker T_a\\). If \\(H\leq G\\) is a free subgroup of infinite index, then

\\[ \boxed{ K\\!\restriction_H\ \cong_H\ (\mathbb F_2^{\mathbb N})^H } \\]

as compact abelian group actions, hence also as Haar probability-preserving actions.

In particular, every nontrivial infinite cyclic subgroup \\(H=\langle h\rangle\\) of the torsion-free \\(G\\) in the problem satisfies

\\[ K\\!\restriction\_{\langle h\rangle} \cong\_{\langle h\rangle} (\mathbb F_2^{\mathbb N})^{\mathbb Z}. \\]

This is stronger than a measure-theoretic restriction result: the conjugacy is a continuous group isomorphism. The proof uses the free-ideal-ring theorem for free group algebras, rather than entropy or measurable extraction.&#x20;

### Proof

Put \\(k=\mathbb F_2\\), \\(R=k[G]\\), and \\(M=\widehat K\\), the discrete Pontryagin dual of \\(K\\).

With the right-convolution convention of the problem, the dual of \\(T_a\\) is right multiplication by \\(a\\). Therefore

\\[ \widehat K \cong R/Ra \cong Rp, \qquad p=1-ba. \\]

The second isomorphism follows from the idempotent decomposition

\\[ R=Rba\oplus Rp,\qquad p^2=p. \\]

Now restrict scalars to \\(k[H]\\). Choosing representatives of the left cosets \\(H\backslash G\\) gives

\\[ R\cong\bigoplus\_{t\in H\backslash G}k[H]t. \\]

Thus \\(R\\) is a free left \\(k[H]\\)-module, and \\(Rp\\), being the image of an idempotent \\(k[H]\\)-linear map, is a submodule of this free module.

For free \\(H\\), Cohn's free-ideal-ring theorem implies that \\(Rp\\) is free over \\(k[H]\\):

\\[ Rp\cong k[H]^{(I)} \\]

for some countable index set \\(I\\).

It remains to prove \\(I\\) is infinite.

Write \\(S=\operatorname{supp}(p)\\). Since \\(H\\) has infinite index and \\(S\\) is finite, we can inductively choose \\(g_1,g_2,\ldots\in G\\) so that the finite collections of left cosets

\\[ \\{Hg_i s:s\in S\\} \\]

are pairwise disjoint for different \\(i\\). At each step, only finitely many left \\(H\\)-cosets are forbidden.

Consider the elements

\\[ v_i=g_i p\in Rp. \\]

They are linearly independent over \\(k[H]\\). Indeed, in any finite relation

\\[ \sum_i u_i g_i p=0,\qquad u_i\in k[H], \\]

the summands belonging to different \\(i\\) have disjoint left-\\(H\\)-coset supports. Consequently each \\(u_i g_i p\\) must vanish separately.

Since \\(k[H]\\) is a domain and \\(g_i p\ne0\\), multiplication by \\(g_i p\\) on the left by a nonzero \\(u_i\\) cannot vanish: expand \\(g_i p\\) over the free \\(k[H]\\)-basis of \\(R\\), and use that at least one nonzero coefficient remains nonzero after multiplication by \\(u_i\\).

Hence every \\(u_i=0\\), proving that \\(Rp\\) contains a free submodule of countably infinite rank. Since it is itself countably generated,

\\[ Rp\cong k[H]^{(\mathbb N)}. \\]

Pontryagin duality now gives

\\[ K\\!\restriction_H \cong \widehat{k[H]^{(\mathbb N)}} \cong (\mathbb F_2^{\mathbb N})^H. \\]

The duality respects \\(H\\)-translations and sends the compact Haar measure to the product Haar measure. This proves the theorem. \\(\square\\)

### Consequence and limitation

For every such subgroup, \\(K\\) is already a full Bernoulli shift with a nonatomic base; no finite power is required.

However, these subgroup conjugacies need not be compatible with the remaining elements of \\(G\\). They do not assemble automatically into a \\(G\\)-conjugacy.

Indeed, since \\(\varepsilon(a)=1\\) under the augmentation map \\(R\to\mathbb F_2\\),

\\[ (K^m)^G=\\{0\\} \\]

for every finite \\(m\\), whereas a nontrivial full shift has nonzero constant configurations fixed by \\(G\\). Therefore no global topological conjugacy to a nontrivial finite-group full shift is possible. An exact measurable conjugacy remains possible because it may ignore null sets, including fixed configurations.

The theorem is an exact consequence of classical module theory applied to this setting. I have not established that it is new to the literature, and it does not resolve the primary problem.

## 3. A failed mechanism: iid random pivot selection

The free-subgroup theorem suggests trying to globalize an algebraic basis using auxiliary randomness. There is a precise obstruction to the simplest version.

Proposition. Let

\\[ M=Rp,\qquad p=1-ba\ne0. \\]

No positive-density iid Bernoulli thinning of the generating family

\\[ \\{gp:g\in G\\} \\]

is linearly independent almost surely.

### Proof

The identities imply

\\[ ap=a(1-ba)=a-aba=0. \\]

Writing \\(a=\sum\_{g\in F}a_g g\\), with \\(F=\operatorname{supp}(a)\\), gives a finite nontrivial relation

\\[ \sum\_{g\in F}a_g gp=0. \\]

Suppose a random subset \\(B\subseteq G\\) includes each element independently with probability \\(q>0\\).

For any \\(t\in G\\), the event \\(tF\subseteq B\\) has probability

\\[ q^{|F|}>0. \\]

Because \\(G\\) is infinite and \\(F\\) finite, infinitely many pairwise disjoint translates \\(t_iF\\) can be selected. Their inclusion events are independent. Almost surely, at least one—and indeed infinitely many—occur.

Each such event places a nontrivial linear relation entirely inside

\\[ \\{gp:g\in B\\}. \\]

Hence the selected vectors are almost surely dependent. \\(\square\\)

This rules out selecting an algebraic basis by ordinary iid site thinning.

It does not rule out a basis chosen using correlated random priorities, nonlinear combinations of generators, or a more elaborate equivariant elimination procedure.

There is a second limitation: a translation-equivariant random well-ordering of \\(G\\) cannot exist. Such an ordering would supply a least element, giving a \\(G\\)-equivariant random selection of one group element. Its law would be a translation-invariant probability measure on the infinite countable group \\(G\\), which is impossible.

Thus ordinary globally ordered Gaussian elimination cannot be made equivariant merely by assigning iid priorities.

## 4. Strongest unresolved lemma: a random equivariant basis

A more promising route bypasses finite-power Bernoullicity altogether and aims directly for the unequal-entropy conjugacy.

The algebraic identity already supplies

\\[ X\cong_GX\times K. \\]

It would therefore suffice to convert the independent \\(K\\)-coordinate into a second iid binary field, using the existing \\(X\\)-coordinate as a source of randomization.

This leads to a concrete algebraic problem.

### Random equivariant basis problem

Let \\(M=Rp\\), and let \\(\Omega=\mathbb F_2^G\\) carry the iid Haar Bernoulli measure.

Construct measurable maps

\\[ v_g:\Omega\longrightarrow M,\qquad g\in G, \\]

such that, almost surely:

Basis condition

\\[ \boxed{ \\{v_g(\omega):g\in G\\} \text{ is an }\mathbb F_2\text{-vector-space basis of }M. } \\]

Equivariance condition

\\[ \boxed{ v\_{hg}(h\omega)=h\\,v_g(\omega) \quad(h,g\in G). } \\]

The vectors must lie in the finitely supported algebraic module \\(M\\), although their supports may depend measurably on \\(\omega\\) and need not have a uniform radius bound.

This is not simply a reformulation of Bernoullicity. It demands a particular conditionally linear realization of the desired conjugacy, using a random basis of a specified projective group-ring module.

### Conditional theorem: such a basis gives exact entropy collapse

Theorem. If the random equivariant basis above exists, then

\\[ \boxed{ 2^G\cong_G4^G } \\]

as measure-preserving \\(G\\)-actions.

Proof.

Identify \\(K\\) with \\(\widehat M\\). For \\((\omega,k)\in\Omega\times K\\), define

\\[ z(g)=\langle v_g(\omega),k\rangle\in\mathbb F_2. \\]

Consider

\\[ \Phi:\Omega\times K\longrightarrow\Omega\times\mathbb F_2^G, \qquad \Phi(\omega,k)=(\omega,z). \\]

Fix \\(\omega\\) for which the basis condition holds. Evaluation against the basis \\(\\{v_g(\omega)\\}\\) is a continuous group isomorphism

\\[ K=\widehat M\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^G. \\]

Consequently, it sends Haar measure to iid fair-coin measure.

The resulting map \\(\Phi\\) is measurable, and its inverse is measurable modulo null sets. Its equivariance follows from the covariance of the basis:

\\[ \begin{aligned} z\_{h\omega,hk}(hg) &=\langle v\_{hg}(h\omega),hk\rangle\\\ &=\langle h v_g(\omega),hk\rangle\\\ &=\langle v_g(\omega),k\rangle\\\ &=z\_{\omega,k}(g). \end{aligned} \\]

Because \\(k\\) is independent of \\(\omega\\), the output \\(z\\), conditional on every admissible \\(\omega\\), has the same iid Haar distribution. Hence \\(z\\) and \\(\omega\\) are independent.

Therefore

\\[ \Omega\times K\cong_G\Omega\times\mathbb F_2^G. \\]

But the original algebraic Haar splitting gives

\\[ X\cong_GX\times K. \\]

Combining,

\\[ X\cong_GX\times X=4^G. \\]

All conjugacies preserve probability and are \\(G\\)-equivariant. \\(\square\\)

The unresolved statement is the existence of this random basis. The conditional theorem is rigorous, but it does not establish that the requisite maps \\(v_g\\) exist.

### Why this mechanism is worth pursuing

It replaces the task of proving that an entire factor is Bernoulli with a concrete problem about a finite-support binary matroid.

The required basis may use the iid environment \\(\omega\\) to choose different finite linear combinations of the generators \\(gp\\). The basis changes under the group action, while the resulting coordinate field still transforms exactly by the left shift.

The free-subgroup theorem gives abundant algebraic bases after restricting the acting group. The difficulty is constructing a single globally covariant random family indexed by \\(G\\).

A productive continuation would examine random matroid basis constructions, particularly procedures that use local priorities without a global well-order. Any candidate must establish both independence and spanning, not merely a positive-density independent subset.

The underlying exact requirement is

\\[ \operatorname{span}\_{\mathbb F_2} \\{v_g(\omega):g\in G\\}=Rp \\]

with no nontrivial finite linear relations among the chosen vectors.

That spanning requirement is the decisive strengthening over the inherited independent-evaluation construction.

### Final conclusion

The strongest unconditional result obtained here is the exact Bernoulli restriction theorem for free subgroups. It is a structural application of established algebra and should not be presented as a historic breakthrough.

The strongest new proposed attack is the random equivariant basis problem for \\(Rp\\). A successful construction would bypass cancellation of \\(R_0\\), avoid the canonical residual tail, and directly produce the desired exact \\(2^G\cong_G4^G\\) conjugacy.

At present, that construction is missing. There is no verified resolution of Bowen's question from this investigation, and no result meeting the stipulated 10/10 standard.
