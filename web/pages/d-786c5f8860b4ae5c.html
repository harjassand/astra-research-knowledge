# C8 genome engineering: centromere stability and synthetic chromosome topology

## Verdict

The strongest distinct high-ceiling target is **replication-age-gated centromere reload that preserves one functional kinetochore while preventing boundary invasion and structural damage**. The available source profiles do not establish a new stability-control principle. They support a corrected, conditional phase-shape reanalysis and a sharply defined next mechanism test. The important negative result is that pooled domain counts do not cancel within-domain copy weights, and the corrected clone comparison rejects a clone-independent three-hour relaxation law.

## Corrected copy-normalized contrast

The original raw pooled D1:D2 H3K9me3/CENP-A cross-odds is withdrawn. With two tags in D1 having DNA-copy weights `(1,10)`, CENP-A occupancies `(1,0.001)`, H3K9me3 occupancies `(0.001,1)`, and a unit D2 reference, the pooled interaction is `9.90198` although the equal-tag per-copy interaction is `1`. The copy weights remain inside the pooled sums, so shared weights do not algebraically cancel.

The repaired estimator forms a same-tag phase fold first, then an equal-tag log-mean contrast over D1/D2 or D3/D4:

`Delta[m,p] = mean_A log2(Y[m,p,i]/Y[m,THYM,i]) − mean_B log2(Y[m,p,j]/Y[m,THYM,j])`,

`log2(Phi[p]) = Delta[H3K9me3,p] − Delta[CENP-A,p]`.

The reported `Phi=1.987` is calculated from **raw per-tag PC ChIP read counts followed by same-tag phase folds**; it is not a pooled domain sum and the implementation does not first DNA-normalize each count. If the same position-specific genomic-DNA denominator is used for the phase and THYM profile, it cancels exactly inside that same-tag fold, so the domain interaction is algebraically the same. This does not show that the physical copy structure or the H3K9me3/CENP-A aliquot mixtures stayed fixed.

For each tag, a stable assay bias and phase-invariant effective copy weight cancel in the phase fold; the domain contrast cancels marker/phase-wide scale because its coefficients sum to zero. In the H3K9me3-minus-CENP-A interaction, phase-varying copy weights cancel only if both separately harvested marker aliquots share the same tagwise phase/reference copy-ratio vector. If their per-tag log2 copy-ratio mismatch is bounded by `epsilon`, the interaction bias is at most `2 epsilon`; the public data do not bound `epsilon`. Unresolved multiple copies whose mark states differ are an additional mixture ambiguity.

Reanalysis of the primary [Ohzeki et al. 2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkag597), [GEO GSE287042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE287042), gives:

| HAC / domain pair | Phase | log2(Phi) | Phi | 95% mapped-read-only interval for Phi |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HAC120 D1−D2 | COL vs THYM | 0.991 | 1.987 | 1.827–2.162 |
| HAC120 D1−D2 | CR3 vs THYM | 0.559 | 1.473 | 1.361–1.596 |
| HAC465 D3−D4 | COL vs THYM | 0.227 | 1.171 | 1.013–1.353 |
| HAC465 D3−D4 | CR3 vs THYM | 0.929 | 1.904 | 1.638–2.213 |

These are descriptive equal-tag phase-shape interactions, not absolute occupancy, same-cell joint chromatin, a functional-centromere score, or retention. The confidence intervals reflect mapped-read sampling only. Each marker/condition has one PC-sequencing library; biological variance, phase purity, clone selection, full-HAC structure and marker-aliquot copy mismatch are not estimated. HAC120’s contrast decreases COL→CR3 while HAC465’s increases, so there is no support for a universal relaxation/recovery curve. The source’s separate qPCR triplicates do not make the PC-sequencing profiles biological replicates.

The algebraic identity is checked on 10,000 synthetic fixed-copy cases to floating-point precision; this verifies the implementation, not biology. The source-supported tag maps use codes single-hit in partial supplementary assemblies only: 12 D1/8 D2 tags for HAC120 and 23 D3/8 D4 for HAC465. Partial-assembly singleton status does not establish physical uniqueness in the full HAC.

## Mechanistic lead and missing lemma

The 2026 synthetic-HAC paper supplies 2-kb position codes and phase-associated CENP-A/H3K9me3 profiles. The independent [Carty et al. centromere-boundary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-025-01706-2) reports CENP-A domain drift/expansion and new CENP-A/CENP-C domains when heterochromatin boundaries are perturbed in its human centromere context. [Giunta et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2015634118) identifies centromeric replication stress and structural damage as a distinct consequence of CENP-A loss. These are prior biological findings, not evidence that the same transition law holds on the barcoded HAC. Two CENP-A-dense regions also cannot be treated as two kinetochores: count HAC-colocalized CENP-C plus KNL1/NDC80 directly.

The proposed target is a **replication-age-aligned reload**: deliver equal total HJURP/KAT7 recruitment either at fixed early-G1 time or aligned to the measured completion time of replication in each vulnerable 2-kb run. The aim is to keep the boundary stable without creating extra functional kinetochore foci, then separately measure DNA breakage/rearrangement and segregation.

A conditional quantitative screening model is derived in `proof/BOUNDARY_MODEL_AND_DECISIVE_EXPERIMENT.md`. For a uniform run of `m` vulnerable codes, with deficit `q=1−C/C*`, effective neighbor coupling `D`, deficit-linked H3K9 amplification `a`, and clearance `gamma`, its low-occupancy growth rate under the simplest `n=1` feedback is

`lambda_m = a q − gamma − 4 D sin^2(pi/(2(m+1)))`.

The `lambda=0` condition gives a run-length-dependent critical CENP-A level. This turns “restore enough CENP-A” into a measurable, barcode-run-specific threshold hypothesis. No value for `D`, `a`, `gamma`, local replication age, or the resulting threshold is present in the source. The eigenvalue calculation is standard mathematics; the proposed advance would be the experimentally identified relation from replication-age-aligned local recovery to held-out boundary movement and then to one-versus-multiple functional kinetochores and structural loss. If feedback is superlinear (`n>1`), this linear gate is insufficient and a finite-nucleus threshold must be measured.

The load-bearing missing lemma is: **after conditioning on full HAC structure and cell mixture, does local replication-to-reload delay determine whether the H3K9 boundary’s dominant growth mode is positive, and does crossing that threshold predict a later change in kinetochore multiplicity or structural loss?** Current THYM/COL/CR3 snapshots cannot answer this: arbitrary unmeasured replication/recruitment forcing can fit any spatial coupling or front speed. The decisive study fits only training timepoints/one clone, then predicts held-out edges, later times and the second clone.

## Experiment, cost and stop rule

First resolve each clone’s structure and barcode multiplicity (long-read/optical or PFGE plus barcode ddPCR and HAC-specific FISH). Use matched phase-specific input DNA and independent cultures. Map local replication completion and collect a time course from release through 6 hours with per-sample spike-in, CENP-A, H3K9me3, HJURP/KAT7 and input DNA. Compare sham, D1-only, D2-only and both-core recruitment, with matched-dose fixed early-G1, replication-age-aligned and late-G1 controls. Then measure HAC-colocalized CENP-C/KNL1/NDC80 foci, anaphase missegregation, and structural state independently through selection-free passage.

At the source-reported `~2×10^7` cells per ChIP sample, an 8-time × 4-arm × 3-replicate × 2-mark design requires `3.84×10^9` cell-assays before controls. Start with a four-time pilot or validate lower-input CUT&RUN/targeted barcode profiling against ChIP-qPCR. Do not use the mapped-read interval as a biological power estimate. Stop promotion if phase-matched DNA, full structure, cell-mixture control, or replication timing cannot bound the copy/selection bias; if equal-dose late recruitment performs as well as lag-aligned recruitment; if a predicted stable boundary does not hold out; or if apparent mark recovery increases `K>1`, structural loss, or missegregation.
