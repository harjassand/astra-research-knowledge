2. GEODESIC TRANSPORT AND ATTENUATION ARE DIFFERENT TERMS

For x=|<v,w>|^2=cos^2 theta, choose phase so <v,w>=cos theta>=0, write
w=cos theta v+sin theta e with unit e in v^perp. Rotate the (v,e) plane
by its horizontal SU3 geodesic and leave the other orthogonal direction

  D_(theta,e)=(cos theta)^N_e.                            (2.1)

At v=e3,e=e1, the transported plane basis is cos theta e1-sin theta e3,e2;
projecting it onto v^perp gives diag(cos theta,1), which proves (2.1).
The phases outside this chart cancel from the energy. Q is canonical
bundle parallel transport: its derivative vector along e is normal to the
moving plane, so its projected covariant derivative is zero.

Writing f_w^Q=Q f(w)Q^dag, split the difference as

  f(v)D-D f_w^Q=[f(v),D]+D[f(v)-f_w^Q].

Since ||D||<=1 and ||X+Y||^2<=2||X||^2+2||Y||^2, (1.2) gives

        {||[f(v),D]||^2+||f(v)-f_w^Q||^2} dv dw.         (2.2)

3. EXACT HORIZONTAL/FIBER ESTIMATE

Suppose f takes values in the spin-l irreducible component of
End(Sym^k(C2)), with 0<=l<=k. Let E_H(f) be the canonical horizontal
Dirichlet form with SU3 generators normalized Tr_fund T_a T_b=delta_ab/2.

The geodesic generator A=e v^dag-v e^dag equals 2i times a unit horizontal
generator. For e uniform on the real unit S3, its four coefficients are
isotropic. Thus the average squared derivative along A is exactly the sum
of the four horizontal generator derivatives, E_H. Cauchy-Schwarz along
the length-theta path and invariance of Haar plane/direction measure give

  int_(v,e)||f(v)-f_w^Q||^2 <=theta^2 E_H(f).             (3.1)

The overlap-squared law is 2(1-x)dx, so the h_m=x^m-normalized beta law has
mean 1-x=2/(m+3). Since theta^2<=(pi^2/4)(1-x),

  a int h_m theta^2 dw <=(a/d_m) pi^2/[2(m+3)],
  d_m=(m+1)(m+2)/2.                                     (3.2)

For the other term, t=-log cos theta=-(1/2)log x>=0. On the nonnegative
occupation spectrum, s->exp(-ts) has Lipschitz constant <=t. Entrywise
divided differences therefore give ||[f,D]||<=t||[f,N_e]||.
The scalar k/2 in N_e drops from its commutator, and the Bloch direction of
uniform e is uniform on S2. Hence

  average_e ||[f,N_e]||^2
      =(1/3) sum_(i=1..3)||[f,J_i]||^2
      =l(l+1)||f||^2/3.                                 (3.3)

Direct beta integration gives

  a int h_m t^2 dw
    =a[1/(m+1)^3-1/(m+2)^3]
    <=(a/d_m) 3/[2(m+1)^2].                              (3.4)

Indeed the normalized expectation is half of
(m+2)/(m+1)^2-(m+1)/(m+2)^2, bounded by 3/[2(m+1)^2].
If k<=m+2 then a/d_m=1+k/(m+2)<=2. Combining (2.2)-(3.4),

  E_K(f)<= pi^2 E_H(f)/(m+3)

All estimates are finite and uniform in growing k; no fixed-bundle large-m
