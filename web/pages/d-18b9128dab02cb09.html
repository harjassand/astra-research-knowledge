# Leaf 04: all-fixed-field matrix multiplication exponent

Audit date: 8 October 2026. Scope: the field transfer behind `L03-all-field-nine-fourths`, especially the exact field hypotheses, extension overhead, characteristic two, and the distinction between an arithmetic exponent and an explicit algorithm artifact.

## Verdict

The primary 9/4 manuscript proves its headline only over `C`. The all-field 9/4 statement is a separate, internally reconstructed corollary, not a theorem stated by that manuscript. I found no algebraic gap in the finite-field transfer steps routed through the card: each extension-field operation can be descended with an explicit `k^3` copy charge; all asymptotic losses vanish in the relevant root or entropy limit; and the characteristic-two steps use no division by two or semisimplicity. The result remains conditional on the correctness of the 9/4 manuscript's character/profile proof and on the manual field-independent adaptation of its detecting-character argument. This is a scoped mathematical reconstruction, not a kernel-checked or external validation.

The exact arithmetic conclusion supported by that reconstruction is:

> For every field `F` and every `ε>0`, there is a finite division-free arithmetic program for square matrix multiplication over `F` with cost `O_{F,ε}(n^(9/4+ε))`. More sharply, the construction can be made over the prime subfield: for each prime `p`, the same `O_{p,ε}` program works over every field of characteristic `p`; in characteristic zero a rational-coefficient program works over every such field. No constant uniform over all characteristics is established.

The conclusion is an exponent statement. It does not give a displayed low-rank decomposition, a procedure that efficiently finds one, a practical crossover size, or a bit-complexity bound.

## Exact finite-field transfer lemma

Let `F=F_q` be a fixed finite field, `E/F` a degree-`k` extension, and `ell:E→F` an `F`-linear functional with `ell(1)=1`. Such an `ell` exists by choosing an `F`-basis of `E` containing `1`. If an `E`-linear restriction of `A_E` has all final coefficients in `F`, expand each of its three substituted linear forms in that basis. Applying `ell` to each resulting trilinear coefficient gives

`ell(UVW) = Σ_{i,j,h} ell(e_i e_j e_h) U_i V_j W_h`.

For each triple `(i,j,h)`, the coordinate forms `U_i,V_j,W_h` are `F`-linear substitutions on the three legs of one whole copy of `A`; absorb the scalar `ell(e_i e_j e_h)` into one leg. Thus an `E`-restriction of `L` copies whose output is `F`-rational descends to an `F`-restriction of at most `L k^3` full copies. For a one-parameter degeneration, apply the same operation coefficientwise: zero lower coefficients stay zero, and an `F`-valued leading tensor is fixed by `ell`. The projection is linear, not multiplicative; the cubic coordinate expansion is essential.

### Separator and charged overhead

For `A=Σ_{h=1}^M A_h` with a shared first leg and matched, disjoint second- and third-leg sectors, set

`k = min{j≥1 : q^j ≥ 3M+1}`, `L=q^k−1`.

The group `E^×` is cyclic of order `L`; choose a primitive `L`th root `ζ`. In `L` source copies use the phase substitutions in the L03 reconstruction. Their coefficient average is

`L^(-1) Σ_{r=0}^{L−1} ζ^{r e}`, where `e=u−v+2(g−h)`.

Since `|e|≤3(M−1)<L`, orthogonality selects exactly `e=0`, as an integer equality. The surviving target coefficients lie in `F`. The target-variable weights `g²`, `hu−h²`, and `−hv` give total weight `(g−h)²` on every surviving term, so the minimum-weight tensor is exactly `⊕_{h=1}^M(A_h⊗B_X(M))`. Descent therefore gives the base-field degeneration

`A^(⊕ L k^3)  ↠  ⊕_{h=1}^M(A_h⊗B_X(M))`.

Minimality of `k` gives `L < q(3M+1)` and `k≤1+log_q(3M+1)`. Hence the charged number of source copies is

`L k^3 = O_q(M(1+log M)^3)`.

The factor cannot be discarded at finite `M`. In the entropy argument, after dividing by the number `M` of type words, the extra factor is only `O_q((1+log M)^3)`. For a tensor power indexed by `N`, `log M=O(N)`, so its `N`th root tends to one. The original entropy exponent is preserved. Constants depend on the chosen base field (or can be routed through `F_p` to depend only on its characteristic).

### Degeneration monotonicity over a finite field

Suppose a fixed witness of degree `D` over `F` degenerates `A` to `B`. For its `j`th tensor power, the coefficient polynomial has degree at most `jD` and constant coefficient `B^⊗j`. Put `s_j=jD+1`, and choose `E_j/F` of degree `k_j` with `q^{k_j}−1≥s_j`. Interpolate the constant coefficient from `s_j` distinct nonzero points of `E_j`; each evaluated tensor is an `E_j`-restriction of `A_Ej^⊗j`. Descend the whole interpolation once. For every `F`-tensor character `lambda`,

`lambda(B)^j ≤ s_j k_j^3 lambda(A)^j`, with `k_j=O_{q,D}(log(j+2))`.

Taking `j`th roots proves `lambda(B)≤lambda(A)`. If `D=0`, the exact restriction suffices. This repairs the finite-field failure of trying to interpolate from only `F_q^×` after `jD+1>q−1`. The overhead is polynomial in `j` and logarithmic-degree cubed; no continuity of `lambda` is assumed.

### Convolution profile

For fixed `a,b`, let `m=a+b−1` and choose one finite extension `E/F` with at least `m` elements. Evaluation/interpolation gives `rank_E C(a,b)≤m`, where `C(a,b)` is univariate polynomial convolution. Tensor there first, then descend once using the fixed degree `k=[E:F]`:

`rank_F(C(a,b)^⊗j) ≤ k^3 m^j` for every `j`.

Consequently `lambda_F(C(a,b))≤m` after taking `j`th roots. The extension degree is fixed as `j→∞` because `a,b` are fixed. Charging a new extension-coordinate factor at each tensor factor would be an incorrect exponential loss; the proof does not do that.

## Characteristic two and the remaining source argument

For `q=2`, `L=2^k−1` is odd, so `1/L` exists in `E=F_{2^k}` and `E^×` has a primitive `L`th root. The Fourier exponent `u−v+2(g−h)` is an integer group exponent, not a field coefficient; the bound `|e|<L` still forces `e=0`. Formal degeneration weights are integer valuations and are not reduced modulo the field characteristic.

The determinant filtration in the 9/4 proof can be checked directly in every characteristic. Its map is diagonal substitution in a polynomial ring over the field; with `D=uw−vs`, the kernel is `D V_{e−1}`. Multiplication by the nonzero polynomial `D` is injective because the polynomial ring is a domain, and the dimensions give exactness. The quotient lifts map to the standard monomial basis with coefficient `1`; the appended kernel basis has unit pivots (`±1`, both units in every characteristic). Multiplication commutes with diagonal substitution and takes `Dg` to `D(fg)`. This uses no division by two and no semisimplicity of an `SL_2` representation. In characteristic two, the minus sign in `D` becomes plus, while the exact-sequence and basis argument remains unchanged.

The three-sector construction uses only integer coordinate weights, deletion of coordinates, index translations, and a basis reversal. The formal weights and the coordinate maps remain meaningful in characteristic two. The appendix's detecting-character proof can likewise be rerun over a chosen field: its tensor semiring, rank bounds, finite-dimensional rational cone certificate, compact convex state space, and fixed-point step use only exact restrictions, direct sums, tensor products, integer ranks, and real compactness. No complex topology or algebraically closed coefficient assumption enters that argument. This adaptation is part of the reconstructed corollary; it is not asserted by the complex-only statement of the primary paper.

## Exponent versus a decomposition or bit algorithm

Once the character/profile proof gives `nu_F≤9/4`, the definition `nu_F=inf_d log_d rank_F(T_d)` implies that for every `δ>0` some fixed block size `u` has an exact rank decomposition of `T_u` with `r<u^(9/4+δ)` terms. Recursing this fixed bilinear identity gives `S(N)≤r S(N/u)+O_{u,r}(N^2)` on powers of `u`; padding to an arbitrary dimension changes size by only a fixed factor. This proves `O_{F,ε}(n^(9/4+ε))` field operations.

That argument is existential. It does not provide `u`, the `r` rank-one tensors, a search procedure with useful runtime, a practical crossover, or bounds on coefficient heights. The 9/4 source's own number-field remark handles characteristic zero by choosing coefficients in one finite extension of `Q` and simulating each extension-field operation by a constant number (depending on that fixed extension) of rational operations; the resulting rational program embeds in every characteristic-zero field. In characteristic `p`, the finite-field proof can be carried out over `F_p`, and the resulting program embeds in every field of that characteristic. Neither route is a bit algorithm: operation counts ignore coefficient encoding, construction of extensions/bases, and coefficient acquisition.
