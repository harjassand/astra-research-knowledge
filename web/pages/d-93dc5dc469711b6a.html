# Finite-state CTMC discrimination: information is limited by active exposure

**Result.** For a finite CTMC whose signal-dependent transition hazards are all multiplied by one scalar \(\theta\), while reset/leakage rates stay fixed, the optimal full-path likelihood ratio depends only on the count of sensitive jumps and their integrated baseline hazard. The path KL divergence is exactly active exposure times a Poisson-rate divergence. Consequently, any false-alarm/miss target requires a minimum observation time that is independent of the number of internal states once the maximum sensitive hazard is fixed. More states can change active exposure, but cannot beat the always-active Poisson information ceiling.

The model theorem and complete proof are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md). It assumes complete labelled, time-resolved paths and common signal-independent reset/leak rates. Its scope is mathematical; it does not identify a natural sensor or establish that any biological pathway follows this generator.

The most informative baseline uses exactly the same sufficient path data \((K_T,A_T)\) and the Neyman–Pearson likelihood-ratio test. This avoids comparing a full trajectory against an uninformed mean-only baseline. A terminal-state threshold has less information by data processing; in the explicit reset chain its errors remain bounded away from zero as duration grows, while the path estimator \(K_T/A_T\) is consistent. An ideal continuously active Poisson counter supplies a ceiling, not an equal-resource replacement: it removes terminal-state downtime and reaches the theorem’s per-time KL cap.

For the chain with forward rate \(\theta\) and reset rate \(\rho\), the stationary full-path KL rate is \([1-(\theta_1/(\theta_1+\rho))^N]d(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)\). State count therefore matters through the inactive-time fraction, with a geometric diminishing return. The report also proves two identifiability limits: a gain and signal entering only as their product cannot be separated even from full paths; a stationary endpoint under a two-state reset chain identifies only the rate ratio, not the common time scale.

**Costs and limits.** The sensor uses \(N+1\) states (\(\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil\) state bits). The ideal observation is a complete CTMC path over duration (T); a timestamped event record has expected event count at most \((\theta_1+\rho)T\) in the reset example, plus state labels and timestamp precision. No finite-resolution implementation or biological measurement system is asserted. The sharp unresolved extension is partial or noisy path observation, or reset/leakage rates that also depend on the signal; those changes need a new information calculation.

**Prior-art boundary.** The path-density/KL identity is a specialization of standard jump-process likelihood theory. Kaiser et al. derive a relative-entropy formula on path space in Appendix A of [“Canonical Structure and Orthogonality of Forces and Currents in Irreversible Markov Chains”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-018-1986-0). Pfeuffer’s [CTMC likelihood treatment](https://journal.r-project.org/articles/RJ-2017-038/) states the complete-path likelihood in terms of transition counts and sojourn times and the estimator \(q_{ij}=N_{ij}/R_i\), while distinguishing this from endpoint-only data. The present result is a scoped algebraic specialization and reset-chain consequence; no priority or external proof-review claim is made.
