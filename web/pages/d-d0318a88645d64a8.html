The Rao–Blackwell calculation in lines 1669–1706 is a strong exact same-information comparator. For iid rollouts, binary success reward (r_i\), **true aggregate** success probability (p=P(r_i=1)\), and score (s_i\), the selected-rollout estimator

\[
N(1-p)^{N-1}r_i s_i
\]

has expectation (\nabla[1-(1-p)^N]\). Its covariance is no larger than that of (NA_Is_i\), where (A_i=r_i\prod_{j\ne i}(1-r_j)\), by conditional variance. The comparison is to the random selected-index estimator, **not automatically to the full batch sum**. It assumes the true aggregate (p\), raw scores and finite second moments, before estimating probabilities, clipping or GRPO normalization; the source says this explicitly. It does not acquire (p\) or prove that a four-rollout plug-in or single-trace proxy remains unbiased.

For the new renewal result the equally informed baseline is therefore: use the same acquired recovery law in the exact configuration/renewal Bellman gradient, and compare with unit retry, UA, CE and a mean-cost heuristic. Restricting that baseline to a supplied trace while giving the new method its off-trace law would be an unfair comparison.

## A useful derivative boundary: diagonal partials are extension-dependent

This is an independent elementary deduction, not a claim that the source's explicitly chosen extensions are illegal. A deployment map is initially defined on the probability simplex. Its unconstrained coordinate partial derivatives require an off-simplex extension. For a one-draw correct-answer map (J(\pi)=\pi_0\), define

\[
J_a(\pi)=\pi_0+a\Big(\sum_i\pi_i-1\Big).
\]

Every (J_a\) agrees on the simplex and has the same parameter-space gradient for every normalized policy, since (\sum_i\nabla_\theta\pi_i=0\). Yet (\partial_{\pi_0}J_a=1+a\), every rival partial is (a\), and the diagonal elasticity is (1+a\). At (a\ge0\) these nonnegative diagonal magnitudes can be arbitrarily large, even though the exact deployed loss is unchanged. The residual cancels the added normal component exactly.

Thus a diagonal coefficient or a “competitive”/“orthogonal” classification based on **unconstrained** partials is not an invariant of the on-simplex deployment map alone. A declared extension or a declared admissible reallocation tangent is part of the interface. The newer search paper's convention—hold off-trace conditional laws fixed and reallocate proportionally—is a concrete tangent specification; our renewal proof uses it. Even that specification does not acquire the missing deployment laws or the actual model-gradient geometry. This boundary sharpens what a theorem based on partial derivatives can certify without alleging an error in an identity with its extension fixed.
