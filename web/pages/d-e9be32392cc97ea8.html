Claim: For exact Stieltjes samples g_i=G_nu(z_i) at m distinct upper-half-plane nodes, where nu is a law with exactly k distinct real atoms of positive weight convolved with semicircle variance t_0, the source proves {t>=0:S(t)>=0}=[0,t_0] whenever m>=k. The monotone feasibility boundary identifies t_0; when m=k, a Hermitian realization recovers atoms and weights.
Interface: Exact limiting transform samples; known k or an upper bound to choose m>=k; exactly k distinct real atoms with positive weights; additive semicircular noise. The m=k realization assumes the stated nondegenerate positive Pick matrix.
Proof spine: An entrywise Pick-kernel identity gives strict downward feasibility. At the planted variance the Pick covariance has rank at most k-1; strictness then identifies the entire feasible interval and rules out feasibility above it.
Costs: For m nodes, bisection uses O(m^3 log(U/epsilon)) arithmetic for Hermitian tests, plus O(Nm) to form samples from N supplied eigenvalues and O(m^3) for realization. Input matrix eigendecomposition is a separate shared cost.
Failure: This is not atom-count-free or exact for finite-N deformed-Wigner data. Adaptive-node finite-N stability is not certified, ordinary floating PSD signs are not certified, and non-atomic signals can confound removable semicircular variance.
