SHARP WINDING-ERASURE COST AND THE C*-NORM ABSORPTION OBSTRUCTION
Independent derivations, 2026-10-09. No novelty claim or external validation.

Notation. D = C*_r(F_{n+1}), n >= 1, in its regular representation;
A_1,...,A_n,C are its canonical unitaries. The canonical trace is faithful.
For a unitary C with Haar spectral law, ||f(C)||_2^2 is the normalized
Lebesgue integral of |f(e^{i theta})|^2 over [-pi,pi].

THEOREM 1. SHARP ERROR COST OF A CONTINUOUS LOGARITHM REPLACEMENT

Let h:R -> R be 2pi-periodic and L-Lipschitz, with L >= 0. Then

  (1/(2pi)) integral_{-pi}^{pi} |exp(i h(theta))-exp(i theta)|^2 dtheta
       >= 2/(L+1).

Also max_theta |exp(i h(theta))-exp(i theta)| = 2. For every L >= 1
the lower bound is attained by an explicit centered h with ||h||_infty < pi.

Proof. A Lipschitz function is absolutely continuous and differentiable
almost everywhere, with |h'| <= L. Set phi(theta)=h(theta)-theta and
V(x)=2-2 cos x. Then |phi'| <= L+1 almost everywhere, while periodicity
gives phi(pi)=phi(-pi)-2pi. The primitive F(x)=2x-2 sin x satisfies
F(phi(pi))-F(phi(-pi))=-4pi. The absolutely continuous chain rule gives

  4pi = | integral V(phi(theta)) phi'(theta) dtheta |
       <= (L+1) integral V(phi(theta)) dtheta.

Division by 2pi proves the assertion. The intermediate value theorem,
applied to the lift phi over an interval on which its endpoint values
differ by 2pi, makes phi attain pi modulo 2pi. The pointwise error there
is 2, and the reverse bound is the unitary triangle inequality.

Sharpness with the centeredness required by the source flow. Let

  b = pi L/(L+1).
  h_L(theta) = theta                       for |theta| <= b,
               L(pi-theta)                for b <= theta <= pi,
              -L(pi+theta)                for -pi <= theta <= -b,

and extend periodically. This is continuous, odd on [-pi,pi], centered
under Haar measure, and L-Lipschitz when L >= 1. Its sup norm is b < pi.
On the central interval the error is zero. On the two remaining
intervals, phi ranges once over [-pi,pi], with speed L+1 in absolute
value. Consequently its squared error is

  (1/(2pi(L+1))) integral_{-pi}^{pi} (2-2 cos x) dx = 2/(L+1).

At theta=pi, h_L=0 and exp(i theta)=-1, giving norm error 2.

Corollary 1a (charged regularity). If the Haar L2 error is < epsilon,
then L > 2 epsilon^{-2}-1. No fixed finite regularity budget can support
arbitrarily accurate logarithm replacement. This includes any centered
continuous phase; centeredness does not improve the bound because h_L
already obeys it.

Corollary 1b (finite Laurent coefficient cost). If

  h(theta)=sum_{k=-N}^{N} a_k exp(i k theta),
  a_{-k}=conj(a_k),

then |h'| <= Lambda := sum_k |k a_k|. Thus squared Haar error is at least
2/(1+Lambda), and epsilon accuracy requires Lambda > 2 epsilon^{-2}-1.
This conclusion is elementary and does not use Bernstein's inequality.
If one additionally imports the classical trigonometric Bernstein
inequality ||h'||_infty <= N ||h||_infty, a sup-norm bound ||h|| <= B
implies N > (2 epsilon^{-2}-1)/B. The report uses the elementary weighted
coefficient cost as the independently reconstructed assertion; the N-B
version is explicitly a further consequence of an imported inequality.

Comparison to the source's Mobius phase. For 0 <= r < 1 define

  z_r(z) = (1+r z)/(1+r conj(z)),   |z|=1.

It has a continuous real log h_r(theta)=2 Arg(1+r exp(i theta)), centered
by oddness and bounded by pi. Its winding number is zero. The source's
LaurentApproximation.lean proves its use as an L2 log replacement. Direct
algebra gives

  z_r(z)=r z+(1-r^2)/(1+r conj(z)),
  trace(conj(C) z_r(C))=r,
  ||z_r(C)-C||_2^2=2(1-r),
  ||z_r(C)-C||=2,
  ||h_r'||_infty=2r/(1-r).

The trace identity follows by the norm-convergent geometric expansion
of 1/(1+r conj(C)); all nonconstant Haar moments vanish. At z=-1 the
value of z_r is 1, proving the norm assertion. Differentiating the phase
gives h_r'=2(r cos(theta)+r^2)/(1+2r cos(theta)+r^2), whose largest
absolute value is 2r/(1-r). The sharp h_L can improve the maximal phase
derivative by an asymptotic factor two at equal L2 accuracy. No resulting
end-to-end computational improvement is claimed; cocycle/word/support
costs remain uncontrolled by the manuscript.

THEOREM 2. SHARP K1 OBSTRUCTION TO NORM ABSORPTION

Suppose U_1,...,U_n are arbitrary unitaries IN D with
||U_j-A_j|| < 2 for all j. For every group word w in F_n,

  ||w(U_1,...,U_n)-C|| = 2.

Proof. For unitaries u,v with ||u-v||<2, the spectrum of u* v excludes
-1. A continuous logarithm on the remaining arc gives a norm-continuous
unitary path from u to v. Therefore [U_j]=[A_j] in K1(D).
Pimsner-Voiculescu 1982, Corollary 3.2, identifies K1(D) with Z^{n+1},
with basis [A_1],...,[A_n],[C]. The K1 class of a word is

  [w(U)] = sum_j exponent_j(w) [A_j],

so it lies in the first-n-coordinate subgroup, whereas [C] does not.
If ||w(U)-C||<2 the same logarithm argument would identify these classes,
a contradiction. The triangle inequality gives the matching upper bound
2. No Haar distribution, automorphism, or small-displacement flow is
needed for this theorem.

Corollary 2a (continuous functional-calculus transfer is REFUTED).
There cannot be an automorphism of D, or merely unitaries in D norm <2
from the first n canonical generators, with a word in those unitaries
at norm error <2 from C. In particular, substituting a Laurent or other
continuous phase in the paper's flow cannot retain its operator-norm
one-step absorption estimate while keeping the trajectory in D.

This is NOT a refutation of the manuscript. The paper uses the bounded
BOREL logarithm arg(C), which is outside D: if it were in D, the path
exp(i t arg(C)) would make [C]=0 in K1(D), contradicting the same basis.
The paper's trajectories live in the larger C*-algebra containing arg(C).
The formalization correctly uses L2 error for the absorbed word.

THEOREM 3. EXACT NORM DISTANCE OF THE FREE HAAR SUBALGEBRA

In addition to Theorem 2, assume the tuple U is free Haar under the
faithful trace. Set B=C*(U_1,...,U_n) inside D. Then

  dist(C,B)=1,
  ||C-v||=2 for every unitary v in B.

Proof. Faithful-trace moment identification gives B isomorphic to
C*_r(F_n): polynomial operator norms are determined by their trace
moments, and the U tuple has precisely the canonical free Haar moments.
Pimsner-Voiculescu identifies K1(B) with Z^n, generated by [U_j]. Under
the inclusion B->D its image lies in the first-n-coordinate subgroup.
Every unitary v in B has K1 class in that image. Hence ||v-C|| cannot be
<2, proving the second assertion.

For the first, suppose b in B satisfies ||b-C||<1. Then the segment
C+t(b-C) is invertible in D for every t in [0,1], since multiplying by
C* gives an element within distance <1 of 1. In particular b is
invertible in D. Spectral permanence for the unital C*-subalgebra B
makes it invertible in B. Its K1 class consequently lies in the image
of K1(B), but the invertible segment gives [b]=[C] in K1(D), again a
contradiction. Thus dist(C,B)>=1; b=0 supplies the upper bound 1.

CONDITIONAL THEOREM 4. THE PRECISE SURVIVING TRANSFER

Supply the following one-step premise H_n, n>=2. For every free Haar
(n+1)-tuple (V,E) norm-generating D and every eta>0, there exist a
trace-preserving C*-automorphism alpha of D and w in F_n such that

  max_j ||alpha(V_j)-V_j|| < eta,
  ||alpha(V_w)-E||_2 < eta.

Then for every delta>0 there exist free Haar unitaries U_1,...,U_n in D
with max_j ||U_j-A_j||<delta, W*(U)=L(F_{n+1}), and the exact norm
separations in Theorem 3. The logical dependency is H_n; no conclusion
requires identifying two reduced group C*-algebras.

Proof of the implication. Run the paper's generating-limit construction
with all alpha_k automorphisms of D and initial future budget r_0 chosen
less than delta. Choose a countable L2-dense set y_j in the canonical
group algebra. At stage k the current completed tuple is the image of
the initial tuple under a C*-automorphism of D, so its finite word
polynomials are norm dense in D and in particular L2 dense. Choose
F_{j,k} approximating the first k targets in L2 to 2^{-k}. After their
finite Lipschitz constant H_k is known, choose eta_k so small that
H_k eta_k<2^{-k} and eta_k<r_{k-1}/2. Invoke H_n; substitute the new word
for the old completion in F_{j,k}, obtaining n-variable polynomials
G_{j,k} with L2 error <2^{1-k}. After the substituted words are known,
choose r_k<=min(r_{k-1}/2,2^{-k}/(1+max_j L(G_{j,k}))). The active
tuple converges in norm INSIDE the complete C*-algebra D. Every fixed
word moment persists, and the future budgets make
||G_{j,k}(U)-y_j||_2<=3*2^{-k}. Thus the final word algebra is L2 dense
in L(F_{n+1}); the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto W*(U)
is the identity. The total active displacement is at most r_0<delta.
When delta<2, Theorem 3 supplies the exact norm separations. Larger
delta follows from choosing any smaller budget. QED.
