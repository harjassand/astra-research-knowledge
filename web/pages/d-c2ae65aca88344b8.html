# Team A: analytic random measurements, sparse count, and conditioning

Status: complete conditional mathematical results and explicit obstructions; no foundational novelty established. The finite-dimensional identifiability theorem predates the 2026 paper (Sontag 1996/2002). No PDE-specific effective stability theorem has been established.

Their Theorem2.2 has a second-countable embedded analytic d-manifold in a Banach space; jointly analytic prescribed scalar family on a connected finite-dimensional analytic sampling manifold, law absolutely continuous w.r.t. volume; full-data pair separation. It gives almost-sure global injectivity with M>=2d+1. The Gaussian architecture replaces finite-dimensional designs by independent blocks of a full-support Gaussian Hilbert-space variable and bounded multilinear forward measurements. Count reduction may be a straightforward correction of a loose bound, not a novel theorem in the field.

## 1. Independent sparse reconstruction

Identify the coefficient subspace with R^d. For a support I, E_I=span(e_j:j in I), |I|<=s. For ordered supports I,J form P_IJ={(a,b) in (A∩E_I)×(A∩E_J):a!=b}. This is an open subset of a manifold of dimension p=|I|+|J|<=2s, even when supports overlap (the two parameters are independent). It is second countable. Cover it by countably many compact semianalytic chart pieces, staying off the diagonal. On each piece P, Phi((a,b),z)=m(a,z)-m(b,z) is analytic and nonzero as a function of z by full-data separation. For dim Z=q each zero fiber has dimension at most q−1. Thus the incidence of M undistinguished observations has dimension <=p+M(q−1), and its projection to Z^M has that same upper bound. For M>p it is a null set. Union over compact pieces and finitely many support pairs proves uniform sparse injectivity for M>=2s+1. If s>=d this is 2d+1; more generally bound is 2min(s,d)+1. Separation only on sparse pairs is sufficient in the finite architecture; the source assumes the stronger separation on all A∩R^d.

Gaussian reconstruction: For a fixed compact pair piece P the differences are a continuous family of nonzero bounded r-multilinear forms. Dense finite-coordinate approximation and compactness produce a common N so their restrictions to (Z_N)^r are all nonzero. Choose Z_N from covariance eigenvectors, so Gaussian heads have a positive density and are independent of tails. Condition on all M tails w_i. The head polynomial Phi(p,z_i+w_i) has nonzero homogeneous degree-r part Phi(p,z_i); shifts contribute strictly lower total degree, so every fiber remains nonzero for every tail. Apply the same analytic incidence bound with q=rN and base dimension p, giving head null measure whenever M>p. The projected bad set is closed for fixed P (P compact); hence measurable. Fubini with independent head/tail product distributions gives unconditional null measure. Union over compact pieces/supports proves M>=2s+1. Gaussian full support/dense physical feature span and multilinearity remain load-bearing assumptions. A general infinite-dimensional analytic family need not survive tail conditioning.

## 2. Smooth models: tangent incidence

Additional hypothesis (T): for every a∈M∩A and nonzero v∈T_aM, z↦D_a m(a,z)[v] is not identically zero. Choose an analytic chart a=phi(t) and a Euclidean unit direction u∈S^(d−1). h((t,u),z)=D_t(m(phi(t),z))[u] is jointly analytic. The chart-direction base dimension is 2d−1. The preceding incidence proof gives almost-sure no sampled zero tangent with M>=2d. Union over a countable atlas. Combine with M>=2d+1 pair injection. Complex scalars are treated as real analytic real/imaginary components, requiring only codimension one, so no unearned factor-two advantage.

For any compact K⊂M∩A, injection+immersion gives sampled inverse Lipschitz in ambient Banach norm. Proof by contradiction: take pairs a_n,b_n in K with data separation/norm(a_n−b_n)→0. A convergent subsequence has limits a,b. If a!=b, injectivity contradicts numerator→0; bounded denominator suffices. If a=b, use one chart a_n=phi(t_n),b_n=phi(u_n). On a small convex chart neighbourhood, phi is Lipschitz (bounded derivative); after subsequence (t_n−u_n)/|t_n−u_n|→v of unit length. C1 Taylor expansion gives data difference/|t_n−u_n|→D(m_M∘phi)_t v !=0. Hence data/norm(a_n−b_n) stays bounded away from zero. An upper Lipschitz bound follows near the diagonal from C1 and local chart norm equivalence, and far from the diagonal by compactness. Constants depend on the realized sampling system and K.

## 3. Exact sparse stability interface

Let K⊂R^d be compact, s-sparse. The correct extra visibility condition is on limits of normalized cross-support secants, not merely the tangent spaces of each support. A simple sufficient condition is:

(CS) For every |I|,|J|<=s, every a∈K∩E_I∩E_J, and every nonzero v∈E_I+E_J, z↦D_a m(a,z)[v] is not identically zero.

This is stronger than necessary for general K; the exact necessary family comprises only directions realizable by secants from K∩E_I and K∩E_J approaching a. If source full differential is injective on R^d then (CS) follows. In a d-dimensional basis inside a Banach space, equivalent finite-dimensional norms preserve the conclusion with charged norm-equivalence constants.

For each I,J define the lifted positive secant set
 B_IJ^+={(a,v,t):a∈K∩E_I, a+tv∈K∩E_J, |v|=1, t>0}
with t<=diam(K), and B_IJ its closure. B=union B_IJ is compact. If K is semianalytic, B is compact globally subanalytic, with dimension <=2s: B_IJ^+ is definably bijective to distinct endpoint pairs, and taking closure preserves dimension. Define
 g(a,v,t,z)=[m(a+tv,z)−m(a,z)]/t (t>0),
 g(a,v,0,z)=D_a m(a,z)[v].
It is continuous on B×designs. It is analytic on a neighbourhood of each point of B×designs: for t>0 use endpoint composition and divide by nonzero t; for t=0 the convergent local power series is divisible by t. No convexity assumption or integration-definability claim is needed. At t>0 full pair separation makes g nonzero in z. At t=0, limit endpoints coincide at a∈E_I∩E_J and v∈E_I+E_J, so (CS) makes g nonzero.

For arbitrary compact K the same conclusion follows by separate support-pair injection and support-intersection tangent incidence: the tangent base (E_I∩E_J)×P(E_I+E_J) has dimension |I|+|J|−1<=2s−1. If visibility is imposed only at K, compactness of (K∩E_I∩E_J) times its unit directions and continuity provide an open neighbourhood base retaining visibility (a finite collection of witnessing designs suffices); the incidence argument applies there. Thus no semianalytic assumption on K is needed for qualitative Lipschitz stability. For semianalytic K, with M>=2s+1, incidence on the compact subanalytic base B (stratify or represent as a compact semianalytic projection) gives a null set of designs where any g vanishes at all M samples. Continuity+compactness then gives
 κ(z_1,...,z_M)=min_{b∈B} max_i |g(b,z_i)|>0 almost surely,
 ||a−b||_2 <= κ^-1 ||m_M(a)−m_M(b)||_∞ for all a,b∈K.
The same statement holds for Gaussian multilinear measurements by applying the finite-head/tail proof to the compact lifted base. Analyticity on charts of B or compact semianalytic representation suffices; the finite-head form at t=0 is the differentiated forward r-multilinear form, still degree r. This gives qualitative stable sparse count 2s+1 under (CS), without an explicit conditioning estimate.

## 4. Sparse cross-support obstruction, including the source's ambient pair hypothesis

On R^2 let m((x,y),z)=x+y+z x^2+z^2 x^3, z∈(0,1). The full-data map is injective on all R^2: polynomial coefficients identify x^3, hence x, then y. The one-sparse set is the union of the coordinate axes. Every individual branch is immersive in full data: the y derivative is 1; the x derivative 1+2zx+3z²x² cannot vanish identically as a polynomial in z. At the origin both axis tangent images are the constant function1. The cross-support direction (1,−1) is invisible, violating (CS).

For a_h=(h,0), b_h=(0,h), parameter distance=√2|h| and sampled difference_i=z_i h²+z_i² h³. For any fixed bounded finite sample, the ratio tends to0 as h→0. Therefore no finite sampled map admits a uniform Lipschitz inverse on a compact sparse neighbourhood of0, even though generic M>=3 samples are injective and each individual support is immersive. Inverse Holder exponent at most1/2 follows from this pair family. This explicitly defeats a naive per-support derivative argument.

## 5. Polynomial conditioning tails at the same sample count (restricted design theorem)

Additional assumptions beyond the source: K compact semianalytic; finite-dimensional design coordinates Z connected; Q⊂Z compact semianalytic with m analytic on a neighbourhood of K×Q; iid design law supported on Q with density bounded by R w.r.t. coordinate Lebesgue (or finitely many analytic chart volumes with bounded density); sparse pair separation and (CS). Then for M>=2s+1 there exist model-dependent C>0 and integer N>=1 such that
 P(κ<=t) <= C t^(1/N), 0<t<=t0.
Thus E[κ^(−p)]<∞ for every 0<p<1/N and deterministic confidence inverse constants are O(epsilon^(−N)). This is a conditional deduction from classical subanalytic geometry, not asserted novel.

Proof: On Q^M, κ is continuous (min over fixed compact B of continuous functions) and globally subanalytic (finite chart restriction of analytic g; definable compact minimum). Its zero locus D is compact globally subanalytic and dimension<=qM+2s−M<qM by the same incidence bound. If D empty, κ has a positive minimum and the claim is immediate. Otherwise dist(.,D) and κ are continuous globally subanalytic on compact Q^M with identical zero sets. The compact Lojasiewicz inequality yields dist(z,D)^N<=Aκ(z) for integer N and A>0. Therefore {κ<=t}⊂{dist(z,D)<=(At)^(1/N)}. For a bounded compact subanalytic set D of dimension at most qM−1, its rho tube in a fixed bounded box has volume<=H rho for small rho. This follows from the uniform (qM−1)-volume bound on tube boundaries plus coarea; Soufflet2003, proof of Lemma3.2 Step2 pp774–775 gives this argument. Joint sample density<=R^M converts volume to probability, C=R^M H A^(1/N). Tail integration establishes positive inverse moments. This theorem gives existence of charged constants, not a way to compute A,N,H from a PDE model. Bad-set codimension alone does not determine N; see arbitrary analytic order below. No claim of a dimension-only exponent.

Why each restriction matters: Under the source's arbitrary absolutely continuous law, even bounded compact analytic measurements need not have any polynomial conditioning tail. Let z∈(0,e^(−1)), density f(z)=1/[z(log(1/z))²], whose integral is1, and m(x,z)=xz. Then κ=max_i z_i and P(κ<=t)=[log(1/t)]^(−M), t<=e^(−1), so every positive inverse moment diverges. Density is unbounded. Separately uniform z∈(0,1), m(x,z)=x exp(−1/z²) is analytic on the open sampling manifold but has no analytic extension to its compact closure. κ=max_i exp(−1/z_i²), and for t<=e^(−1), P(κ<=t)=[log(1/t)]^(−M/2). Again all positive inverse moments diverge. Thus compact analytic extension/bounded density are scientific assumptions, not harmless implementation details.

## 6. Effective certificate and unresolved step

Worker a_quant gives the complete elementary net theorem: if the normalized secant family has pointwise sup-norm entropy N(S,r)<=(A/r)^q and uniform small-ball P(|g(b,Z)|<=u)<=C_0 u^beta, then
 P(κ<t)<=A^q(C_0 2^beta)^M t^(beta M−q)
for appropriately small t. It is effective once A,q,C_0,beta and scales are actually supplied/certified, but requires beta M>q and generally loses the minimal2s+1 count. Uniform x-derivative upper bounds can give entropy; they cannot give small-ball exponent. m_p(x,z)=xz^p with z uniform(0,1) has normalized full-data differential1 and parameter Hessian0, but P(κ^−1>=R)=R^(−M/p). Arbitrary p destroys dimension-only conditioning. It also shows polynomial-tail order N is model-dependent even with compact polynomial designs.

Exact unresolved quantitative step: provide verifiable lower bounds on normalized sparse secant observability (including all cross-support collision limits), analytic orders/small-ball constants and their sampling-law dependence for a real Calderon/scattering model, then control them as basis dimension/resolution grows. Qualitative full-data injectivity does not supply this information. Efficient robust reconstruction/global optimization remains separate.
