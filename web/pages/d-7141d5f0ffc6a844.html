# Independent audit: common-denominator time lift for positive rational rates

**Scope.** Deterministic ODEs only. This audit checks the positive-rational lift against arbitrary supplied Lebesgue-measurable coefficients $a_e(t)\in[\kappa,K]$, with $0<\kappa\le K<\infty$. It does not audit stochastic safety or N35. Status: **the lift is valid under the explicit contract below, conditional on the N33 permanence theorem; the physical-time paragraph needs the bounds made explicit as below.** N33 remains a source-derived proof candidate, not externally validated.

## Exact interface

Let $E$ be finite, $\nu_e\in\mathbb R^d$ be fixed, and
\[
 \dot x(t)=\sum_{e\in E}a_e(t)\rho_e(x(t))\nu_e,
 \qquad x(t)\in\mathbb R^d_{>0}.
\]
For each e, assume
\[
 \rho_e(x)=\frac{P_e(x)}{Q_e(x)},\quad
 P_e(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in A_e}p_{e\alpha}x^\alpha,\quad
 Q_e(x)=\sum_{\beta\in B_e}q_{e\beta}x^\beta,
\]
with finite nonempty supports and strictly positive coefficients $p,q$. Exponents may be rational (indeed real exponents are allowed by N33) on the positive orthant. Thus $Q_e(x)>0$, and every $\rho_e$ is smooth and positive on that domain. The supplied $a_e$ are measurable and lie in the same fixed box almost everywhere. The tropical condition needed for the permanence conclusion is
\[
 E_r=\{e:r\cdot\nu_e\ne0\},\qquad
 \tau_e(r)=\min_{\alpha\in A_e}\alpha\cdot r-
              \min_{\beta\in B_e}\beta\cdot r,
\]
and, for every $r$ with $E_r\ne\varnothing$, every minimizer of $\tau_e(r)$ over $E_r$ has $r\cdot\nu_e>0$. Positivity of $\rho$ alone is not enough.

Inputs exclude signed numerator/denominator coefficients with cancellations, denominators that vanish on the positive orthant, state-dependent jumps, and unbounded or independently vanishing time coefficients. Rational exponents/jumps and exactly represented rational coefficients are needed for an exact finite algebraic certificate; they are not needed for the analytic algebraic identity itself.

If $E=\varnothing$, or if every $\nu_e=0$, the state is constant and the dynamical claim is trivial; below assume at least one edge term is present.

## Lift identity and orientation

Put
\[
 D(x)=\prod_{f\in E}Q_f(x)>0,
 \qquad s(t)=\int_0^t D(x(u))^{-1}\,du.
\]
On the maximal positive solution, $s(t)$ is strictly increasing. Write $t=t(s)$ for its inverse. Then $dt/ds=D(x(s))$ a.e., so the correct transformed equation is
\[
 \frac{dx}{ds}=D(x)\frac{dx}{dt}
 =\sum_e a_e(t(s))H_e(x)\nu_e,
 \qquad H_e=P_e\prod_{f\ne e}Q_f.
\]
This is the orientation that clears the denominators. If instead $ds/dt=D(x)$, then $dx/ds=(dx/dt)/D(x)$; denominators are retained (and generally enlarged).

Expand $H_e(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_e}c_{e\gamma}x^\gamma$, combining coincident exponents. Positive coefficients imply $c_{e\gamma}>0$, with no cancellation. The transformed system is exactly
\[
 \frac{dx}{ds}=\sum_{e,\gamma}
       k_{e\gamma}(s)x^\gamma\nu_e,
 \qquad k_{e\gamma}(s)=c_{e\gamma}a_e(t(s)).
\]
Each summand is a finite labelled power-law edge $\gamma\to\gamma+\nu_e$. The displacement span is unchanged: $\operatorname{span}\{\nu_e\}$. N33 permits real, including negative, source/target coordinates, so the lift need not be an ordinary nonnegative-integer chemical reaction network.

Let $c_-=\min_{e,\gamma}c_{e\gamma}>0$ and $c_+=\max_{e,\gamma}c_{e\gamma}<\infty$. Once measurability is established, all lifted rate paths lie a.e. in the common compact positive interval
\[
 [\kappa',K']=[\kappa c_-,Kc_+].
\]
The rates for monomials arising from one e are correlated because they share $a_e$; this is harmless: the N33 theorem allows every lifted edge rate to vary independently in the larger box, so it covers this subset of paths.

## Exact endotactic/tropical equivalence (including ties)

The support of a product is the Minkowski sum
\[
 \Gamma_e=A_e+\sum_{f\ne e}B_f,
\]
with colliding terms combined positively. Define $m_e(r)=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma_e}\gamma\cdot r$ and $C(r)=\sum_f\min_{\beta\in B_f}\beta\cdot r$. Additivity of the minimum under finite Minkowski sums gives
\[
 m_e(r)=\min_{\alpha\in A_e}\alpha\cdot r+
        \sum_{f\ne e}\min_{\beta\in B_f}\beta\cdot r
       =\tau_e(r)+C(r).
\]
For direction $w=-r$, N33's essential sources are the *maximal* $w\cdot\gamma$, equivalently the *minimal* $r\cdot\gamma$, among edges with $r\cdot\nu_e\ne0$. The common shift $C(r)$ does not change which e attain that minimum. A lifted edge is essential exactly when its e minimizes $\tau$ and its $\gamma$ attains $m_e$. N33 requires $w\cdot\nu_e<0$, which is $r\cdot\nu_e>0$. Hence the lifted diagram is endotactic **iff** the stated tropical condition holds. This reasoning preserves all ties: every lifted essential edge at a tied minimum has the same sign test as its base e. Neutral edges are excluded on both sides.

## Measurability and rate bounds after the change

On any compact physical-time subinterval inside a maximal positive solution, $x(t)$ has compact image in the positive orthant. Thus $D(x(t))$ is bounded above and bounded away from zero there. Consequently $s(t)$ and its inverse $t(s)$ are locally bi-Lipschitz. A bi-Lipschitz change of one-dimensional time maps null sets to null sets in both directions, so composition of a Lebesgue-measurable representative $a_e(t)$ with $t(s)$ remains Lebesgue measurable; changes to $a_e$ on a physical-time null set affect $a_e(t(s))$ only on an s-null set. The a.e. bounds $\kappa\le a_e(t(s))\le K$ therefore persist, and multiplication by fixed $c_{e\gamma}>0$ gives the displayed $\kappa',K'$ bounds.

This argument is local on the current positive solution; it does not assume the transformed time map is globally bi-Lipschitz before globality is proved. To invoke N33 rigorously, take any maximal original solution, form $b_{e\gamma}(s)=c_{e\gamma}a_e(t(s))$ on its transformed interval, and extend these bounded measurable paths arbitrarily within $[\kappa',K']$ beyond that interval. N33 gives a unique global solution for the extended exogenous paths. Local Carathéodory uniqueness identifies it with the time-transformed original solution on their common interval. This avoids treating the state-dependent composition as an exogenous input chosen in advance.

## Physical-time globality and finite entry

For a transformed global path, let $Q$ be N33's final compact positive absorber and let $S_{\rm ent}$ be its finite s-time entry. On $Q$, continuity and strict positivity give
\[
 0<d_Q:=\min_{x\in Q}D(x)\le\max_{x\in Q}D(x)=:D_Q<\infty.
\]
Therefore $t(s)=\int_0^s D(x(u))\,du$ grows at least as $d_Q(s-S_{\rm ent})$ after $S_{\rm ent}$. In particular $t(s)\to\infty$; an infinite s-trajectory cannot be compressed into a finite physical-time interval. Before $S_{\rm ent}$, continuity alone would show only that every finite s-interval has finite physical duration; it would **not** prove divergence as $s\to\infty$. The positive lower bound on the post-absorber compact set is the missing tail argument.

The N33 scale proof supplies the needed pre-entry bound too. For fixed $x^0$, it chooses $h_0\le h_P$ with $x^0\in\mathcal K_{h_0}$, then $h_1=\min(\sqrt{h_0},h_P)$. Its inner-box relation gives $\mathcal K_{h_0}\subset B_{h_1}$, where $B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d$. The subsequent scales increase from $h_1$ to $h_P$, and every transition path is in the corresponding $B_{h_j}\subseteq B_{h_1}$; later plateaux stay in that same outer box. Thus, until final entry, $D(x(s))\le D_{h_1}:=\max_{B_{h_1}}D<\infty$, uniformly over all rate paths. If $\bar S(x^0)$ is the N33 path-independent s-entry bound and $S_{\rm ent}\le\bar S(x^0)$ is actual entry, the physical entry bound transfers as
\[
 T_*(x^0)=\int_0^{S_{\rm ent}}D(x(s))\,ds
 \le D_{h_1}\bar S(x^0)<\infty.
\]
Both factors are independent of the measurable coefficient schedule. The bound is generally not practical: N33's affine-family/scale constants have no supplied useful size or precision bound. In the special case $h_0=h_P$, entry is immediate and the same conclusion is trivial.

For the maximal-solution dichotomy: if its terminal s-time were finite, the N33 extension has a finite positive limit there, $D$ is bounded on that finite segment, and $t(s)$ has a finite limit; local existence for the original measurable-coefficient ODE then continues the physical solution, contradiction. If terminal s-time were infinite while terminal physical time were finite, the N33 path enters $Q$ at $S_{\rm ent}$ and the lower bound $D\ge d_Q>0$ makes $t(s)$ diverge, again a contradiction. Hence the original solution is global and positive. The shared final absorber remains $Q$ because reparametrization changes only speed, not the state orbit.

## Counterattacks and limits

1. **Wrong orientation check.** For $\dot x=a(t)/(1+x)$, the correct $D=1+x$, $dt/ds=1+x$ gives $dx/ds=a(t(s))$, a polynomial lift. Reversing the orientation gives $dx/ds=a(t(s))/(1+x)^2$, which does not clear the denominator.
2. **Positivity alone does not imply permanence.** $\dot x=-\kappa/(1+x)$ has a strictly positive rational speed factor but reaches zero at $T=(x^0+(x^0)^2/2)/\kappa<\infty$. Its one-edge diagram fails the tropical/endotactic sign test. Thus the structural condition is essential.
3. **Sign/cancellation attack.** If a numerator has negative coefficients, its asymptotic support minimum need not bound the function below: cancellation can remove the apparent leading monomial or create zeros. The positive-coefficient hypothesis cannot be dropped without a new uniform noncancellation/positive lower-envelope assumption.
4. **The lift is not ordinary mass action.** Rational/real exponents and possibly negative lifted vertices are allowed by N33's deterministic positive-orthant model. This algebra does not supply an integer molecular realization or any stochastic theorem.
5. **Cost.** If $p_e=|A_e|$ and $q_e=|B_e|$, then the lifted-edge count is at most
\[
 \sum_e p_e\prod_{f\ne e}q_f,
\]
while the expanded common denominator can have up to $\prod_f q_f$ terms. With $n$ denominators $Q_f=1+x_f$ in dimension $n$, D has exactly $2^n$ distinct monomials, and the sum of all $H_e$ supports can have $n2^{n-1}$ labelled terms before any additional collisions. No cancellation is available to shrink this worst case. For rational input, each product coefficient's numerator/denominator bit lengths add across its factors; summing collided terms adds the exact rational-sum cost, and exponent Minkowski sums can increase numerator/denominator bit lengths. Thus the lift is finite but not polynomial-size in the input in general. A tropical/Newton-fan checker can avoid materializing this product for structural recognition, but it does not remove the explicit expansion cost of this direct N33 reduction. No useful bound on the N33 absorber or transferred physical time follows merely from finiteness.
