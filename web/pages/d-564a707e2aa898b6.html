## Exact determinant, positive patch and convex domain

In canonical hidden coordinates take

    f(y)=sum_i a_i y_i^4-t(sum_i y_i^2)^2,
    A0=sum_i 1/a_i, S=sum_i m_i, m_i=y_i^2,
    d_i=12a_i m_i-4tS.

The Hessian is H=diag(d_i)-8t yy^T. The rank-one determinant identity gives

    h=det H=(prod_i d_i) r,
    r=1-8t sum_i m_i/d_i
     =c0-c1 S sum_i 1/(a_i d_i),
    c0=1-2t A0/3>1/3, c1=8t^2/3>0.

H>0 iff every d_i>0 and r>0: necessity follows from diag(d)=H+8t yy^T>0; sufficiency is the rank-one Schur condition. On S=1 these are positive affine denominators and the strict convex reciprocal sublevel

    sum_i 1/(a_i d_i)<c0/c1.

Their intersection is convex. It is nonempty: m_i=1/(a_i A0) has S=1 and d_i=d=12/A0-4t; H=dI-8t yy^T has eigenvalues d and d-8t=12(1/A0-t)>0. This witness permits repeated residuals and has full support. Every point in the H-positive domain has m_i>t/(3a_i)>0.

## Strict concavity, with the matrix inequality proved

For a nonzero tangent mass variation w with sum_i w_i=0, put u_i=12a_i w_i/d_i and z_i=c1/(a_i d_i r)>0 on S=1. Direct logarithmic differentiation gives

    D log h[w]=(1+z)^T u,
    -D^2 log h[w,w]=u^T M u,
    M=diag(1+2z)+zz^T.

The affine denominators ensure that these are actual directional derivatives, not an approximation. Let v=1+z. To prove v^T M^-1 v<n, put

    p_i=z_i/(1+2z_i), K=sum_i p_i,
    s=sum_i p_i^2/(1-2p_i).

Sherman–Morrison, together with (1+z_i)^2/(1+2z_i)=1+z_i^2/(1+2z_i), gives

    v^T M^-1 v=n+s-(s+K)^2/(1+s).

Strict inequality with n is equivalent to s(1-2K)<K^2. If K>=1/2 the left side is nonpositive and the right side positive. If K<1/2, n>=2 and every p_i>0 imply p_i<K, so

    s < [sum_i p_i^2]/(1-2K) < K^2/(1-2K).

This proves the inequality in all cases. Cauchy–Schwarz in the M inner product yields (v^T u)^2<n u^T M u for u!=0. Consequently

    D^2(h^(1/n))[w,w]
       =(h^(1/n)/n)[-u^T M u+(v^T u)^2/n]<0.

Thus h^(1/n) has a negative-definite Hessian on the S=1 tangent space throughout the positive domain. No distinctness, spectral separation or axis attraction is assumed.

## Unique nondegenerate projective maximum

Let N(m)=h(m)^(1/n), g(m)=sqrt(f(m)), where f(m)=m^T[diag(a)-tJ]m. Positive definiteness makes g a norm; it is convex, and positive on the compact simplex S=1. The H-positive closure in that simplex is compact. Its boundary has h=0: H remains semidefinite on the closure and failure of strict positivity forces a zero determinant. It cannot reach a coordinate boundary because m_i>=t/(3a_i)>0. The ratio N/g therefore has a positive interior maximum.

If two distinct points maximized N/g at value q>0, strict concavity of N and convexity of g would give N-qg>0 at their midpoint, a contradiction. The maximizer is unique. At that maximizer N-qg has negative-definite Hessian on the S=1 tangent space, so the ratio is a nondegenerate strict maximum, not merely a uniquely identified stationary point.

Rescale m by f(m)^(-1/2), then take positive square roots, to move to f(y)=1. The map is a smooth projective coordinate change with invertible derivative, because all m_i>0. Maximizing N/g is equivalent to maximizing h on f=1; the resulting positive point p has full support and the constrained Hessian of log h is negative definite.

## Attracting basin and radial transfer

On h!=0 define V=H^-1 grad log h. At p, Euler's identities give grad log h=(n/2)grad f and H p=3 grad f. Hence V(p)=(n/6)p. On f=1 put

    W(y)=V(y)-[df_y(V(y))/4]y.

W is tangent to f=1. Because H y=3 grad f, y is H-orthogonal to that tangent space, so W is exactly the positive H-gradient of log h restricted to f=1. Its derivative at p is the positive-definite tangent metric inverse times the negative-definite constrained Hessian. It has strictly negative real eigenvalues. Therefore p has an open local hyperbolic attracting basin for W.

This also yields a projective basin for +V, without claiming that unnormalized trajectories converge to a finite point. Write x=r y with f(y)=1. Homogeneity V(rx)=r^-3 V(x) gives

    r_dot=r^-3 a(y), y_dot=r^-4 W(y),
    a(y)=df_y(V(y))/4.

In fact a(y)=n/6 everywhere: grad f dot V=(Hy/3)^T H^-1 grad log h=2n/3. Thus r^4=r(0)^4+(2n/3)t exactly, and integral r^-4 dt diverges logarithmically. The projective time reaches infinity and y approaches p. Positive radial multiples of the open f=1 basin form an open set in the full R^n; this full-dimensional openness is required in the next rational-identity step. The polynomial field F=h^2 V=adj(H)grad h changes time by a strictly positive factor on this basin. It has the same spatial/projective trajectories, possibly with finite-time radial escape, and the same limiting direction p.

## Rational rank and the sign-orbit argument

Let D_F act on rational functions and write X_k=D_F^k x, k>=0. The existing flow extactic determinant is E=det[X_0,...,X_(n-1)]. Suppose E were identically zero. Over the differential field R(x), let r<n be the first index where X_r belongs to the span of X_0,...,X_(r-1). Differentiating that relation shows this r-dimensional span is D_F-stable and contains every later X_k.

At a regular initial point in the open attracting basin, these columns have rank r and their span stays fixed along a short trajectory interval. Equivalently, every fixed linear functional annihilating the initial span annihilates x(t) on that interval. Analytic continuation extends the identity along the maximal trajectory, including its limiting projective direction p. Thus p belongs to the span at every regular initial point in an open subset of the basin.

Append p to the first r columns. All (r+1)-minors are polynomials/rational numerators in x with fixed real coefficients, and vanish on an open set; they therefore vanish identically. Hence p belongs to the generic differential-field span. Reflection equivariance gives identical reasoning for each sign-reflected open basin and its limiting point D_epsilon p. These points span R^n: averaging epsilon_i D_epsilon p over all signs gives p_i e_i, and p_i!=0. The generic span must consequently have dimension n, contradicting r<n. Therefore E is not identically zero.

The argument needs an open basin, a nondegenerate maximum, full support and all sign images. One stationary point or a finite witness rank would not suffice. It uses no supplied maximum, attraction rate or conditioning margin. Positive diagonal congruence and any hidden real GL change preserve nonvanishing; the original observed input remains rational, and no real witness computation is performed by the learner.

## Unknown direct sums, including t=0 and singleton boundaries

The mass root N=h^(1/n) is homogeneous of degree1, so Hess(N)m=0. Every mass direction w can be changed to w-(sum w/S)m without changing its Hessian quadratic form. The latter is S-tangent. The strict transverse calculation therefore proves Hess(N)<=0 on the entire convex core cone. For t=0, N=12(prod_i a_i m_i)^(1/n) is a positive multiple of a geometric mean; its Hessian is negative semidefinite by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. For a singleton N=12q_ii m_i is positive linear. In all these cases the nonzero boundary has h=0.

For unknown blocks b of dimensions n_b, H and logdet V split and h=prod_b h_b. The product mass cone is convex and

    N=prod_b N_b^(n_b/n).

The weighted geometric mean is increasing and concave: its Hessian quadratic form is G[(sum_b w_b v_b/x_b)^2-sum_b w_b(v_b/x_b)^2]<=0, with w_b=n_b/n. Its increasing composition with concave N_b is concave. On every nonzero boundary at least one h_b=0, so global N vanishes. Maximize N on the compact intersection of the cone closure and m^T Q m<=1. An interior full-support maximum exists and has f=1 by homogeneity. Two different maxima contradict strict convexity of the Q norm followed by radial rescaling, exactly as above.

At the maximum Euler gives grad N=(N/2)grad f. The constrained Lagrangian Hessian is

    Hess(N)-(N/2)Hess(f)=Hess(N)-NQ<0.

This establishes nondegeneracy even when individual singleton roots are linear. The positive square-root map, H-gradient basin and sign-orbit argument now apply in the full ambient dimension. No block partition, relative block amplitude, common maximum or metric is supplied to the learner. This independently verifies the direct-sum extension without relying on a spectral CRT composition.

## Proof-only complex phase transport for signed downdates

Allow a hidden block S Q S for an unknown diagonal sign matrix S. If P=diag(sqrt(s_i)), with entries1 or i, then P^2=S and

    f_(SQS)(Pz)=f_Q(z).

For any complex invertible P and f'(z)=f(Pz), direct Hessian/adjugate transformation gives

    F'(z)=(det P)^4 P^-1 F(Pz),
    E_F'(z)=(det P)^[2n(n-1)-1] E_F(Pz).

Thus a nonzero extactic on the real negative core remains nonzero after phase transport. The analytic attracting-point argument is used only on that real core; it is not asserted for the signed real block.
