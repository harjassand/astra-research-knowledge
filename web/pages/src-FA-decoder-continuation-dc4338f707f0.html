# Transport rheology memory gate

**Status:** a passive finite-memory counterexample shows that the pipe result is not identified by the supplied steady curve and one-rate active rheometry. It is a mathematical, source-based float-model counterexample, not a fit to the Lin suspension at pipe conditions and not physical device validation.

## What the source actually measures

Lin et al. fix the primary shear at 0.2 s^-1 for the OSP measurements. The author-deposited Fig. 1c primary and OSP files each have 26 rows; column 0 is physical angular frequency 0.314159–94.2478 rad/s, and column 1 is viscosity in Pa s. The inherited pipe mode is 452.931 rad/s (72.086 Hz), 4.806 times above that physical sweep. The paper's Fig. 1d master curve uses the relative rate chi = gamma_OSP*omega/dotgamma_0. For the selected 1% curve, its 26 points span chi=0.01570795–4.71239, and the source array verifies chi=0.01*omega/0.2 to 4.4e-16 absolute error.

There is a source-axis discrepancy worth preserving. Deposited `fig3a_frequency.csv` runs from 2.4674 to 740.22 and follows omega*pi/(2*0.2) to relative error 2.67e-5; row index 2 of the 7x26 `fig3a_exp_matrix.csv` exactly equals the Fig. 1c primary-viscosity values. The accepted paper's Fig. 3 axis reads omega/(2*pi*dotgamma_0), a factor pi^2 away from the deposited coordinate; the SI phase-map axis is also typographically ambiguous. The deposited 740.22 is not a physical rad/s endpoint. The transport solver uses Fig. 1d, not Fig. 3a, so this correction changes the frequency-range claim, not the solver's stored output.

The inherited operation is a 20 m, 62.5 mm-radius, fixed-end tube at 4.60194 L/min, driven at wall speed 0.010 m/s. Its full-resolution source case uses 2 m supports, 100 N m/rad support stiffness, assumed steel G=79 GPa, assumed structural Q=20, pump efficiency 0.70, and two H2W coils. The mode is 72.086 Hz. It uses the 1% Fig. 1d active curve and pure-dissipative cross phase, then transfers that active law from measured dotgamma_0=0.2 s^-1 to local pipe rates 0.3727–0.8009 s^-1. The source steady flow curve is digitized from SI Fig. 6 rather than raw rheometer samples. Maximum inherited chi=3.260 is within the Fig. 1d range, but the primary rate and physical frequency are not. Its modeled maximum material strain is 0.576%; the code selects the 1.0% active curve although an author-deposited 0.5% curve also exists.

## Passive matched-data counterexample

Let eta_0(gamma_dot) be the inherited measured/digitized no-OSP steady curve. Let eta_A(gamma_dot,chi) be the inherited active generalized-Newtonian closure, calibrated from the author Fig. 1d curve at gamma_dot=0.2 s^-1 and transferred to other primary rates. Both are positive, and eta_A=eta_0 at zero OSP. Model A is the inherited instantaneous transfer.

For Model B, define a causal thixotropic state q by

    tau(gamma_dot) * q_dot + q = gamma_OSP(t)
    tau(gamma_dot) = 0.020 s * x^2/(1+x^2)
    x = max(gamma_dot - 0.2 s^-1, 0)/0.05 s^-1.

For gamma_OSP(t)=a sin(omega t), the cycle-correlated state fraction is

    m = 2 <q gamma_OSP>/a^2 = 1/(1+(omega*tau)^2).

Set Delta=eta_0-eta_A and eta_B=eta_0-m*Delta. At zero OSP, Delta=0, so both models reproduce every point of the same unactuated steady curve. At the only measured active primary rate, 0.2 s^-1, tau=0 and m=1, so Model B reproduces Model A's entire Fig. 1d active curve exactly. At other primary rates, the memory law is unconstrained by the supplied active data.

This is a passive cycle-averaged thixotropic model: 0<=m<=1 and 0<eta_A<=eta_B<=eta_0, so primary dissipation is eta_B*gamma_dot_parallel^2>=0. With a positive cross dashpot eta_perp and zero recoverable structural free energy for q, total mechanical dissipation is eta_B*gamma_dot_parallel^2 + eta_perp*gamma_dot_OSP^2 >=0. It is an explicit finite-memory constitutive counterexample, not a proposed fit or a full transient waveform model. The choice that tau rises above 0.2 s^-1 is deliberately exposed as an unmeasured witness parameter. Primary literature on dense suspensions establishes that contact and thixotropic relaxation can exist, but those measurements are on different suspensions and do not determine tau here: [Richards et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.038004), [Völtz et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.051402).

At the pipe mode, this witness gives tau=18.45–19.86 ms over local primary rates 0.373–0.801 s^-1, hence m=0.0141–0.0122. It removes 98.6–98.8% of the transferred active thinning. The bounded 11x24 replay preserves the 2 m support layout. Model B's fixed-mode iteration converged at 73.324 Hz and gives 19.8875 W before driver idle, versus the same-flow passive baseline 17.9984 W (10.5% higher). Its maximum chi=7.443 exceeds the measured Fig. 1d maximum 4.712 and is clipped at the inherited endpoint. That clipping preserves extra thinning and therefore makes this already-losing result optimistic. The paired 11x24 Model A run was not marked mode-converged at the eight-step cap; its result is not used as the decisive comparison. The source's original 11x48 inherited Model A case is converged at 9.27838 W, but it is not a same-resolution comparison with Model B.

## Measurement and cost gate

At omega=452.931 rad/s, retaining at least 95% of the instantaneous active thinning under this one-pole model requires tau<=0.5065 ms. Half the thinning remains at tau=2.208 ms; only 5% remains at tau=9.624 ms. The witness predicts 18–20 ms. The discriminating rheology measurement is therefore a log sweep at the actual local primary rates (0.2, approximately 0.373, 0.55, and 0.801 s^-1), strain amplitudes around 0.005–0.010, and angular frequencies spanning the existing 0.314–94.25 rad/s data through the pipe's 452.93 rad/s and toward 2000 rad/s. Measure both primary viscosity and orthogonal complex viscosity magnitude/phase, plus startup, cessation, and shear-reversal memory. The data must resolve relaxation below about 0.5 ms if the device is to claim at least 95% retention of the transferred thinning. These values are model-derived measurement targets, not material properties.

In the inherited full-resolution operating case, the modeled OSP fluid dissipation is 0.49497 W, structural loss 0.37359 W, and mechanical drive 0.86855 W. The assumed coils add 1.75396 W copper loss, so modeled actuation draw is at least 2.62252 W before driver idle, seals, or support losses. If axial thinning vanishes while the same actuation continues at the same flow, the conditional electric floor is 17.99838+2.62252=20.62090 W, above passive pumping. The floor is conditional on the inherited 72 Hz mode, Q, wall motion, and coil assumptions; it is not universal across other architectures.
