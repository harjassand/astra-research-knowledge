# Independent audit: stationary backward importance sampling obstruction

Date: 2026-10-10. Verdict: the finite-state spectral theorem, two-state deterministic realization, response calculation, and common-prefix second-moment obstruction are correct. The strongest correction is that the proposed single-term roulette is not even integrable in the two-state example. The general twisting theorem is established prior work; the explicit response example is an application, not a new general no-go theorem.

## 1. Spectral theorem

Let P be strictly positive and column-stochastic, let Ph=h with h>0, and let Q be any fixed strictly positive row-stochastic proposal. Starting at i_0=j, sample i_{k+1} from Q[i_k,·] and set

W_n = product over k=0,...,n−1 of P[i_k,i_{k+1}]/Q[i_k,i_{k+1}].

Direct path summation gives E_j W_n=(P^n 1)_j and E_j W_n²=(K^n 1)_j, where K[j,i]=P[j,i]²/Q[j,i]. Componentwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives

(K h²)_j = sum_i (P[j,i]h_i)²/Q[j,i] ≥ (sum_i P[j,i]h_i)² = h_j².

Equality in row j holds exactly when P[j,i]h_i/Q[j,i] is independent of i, hence exactly when Q[j,i]=P[j,i]h_i/h_j. Multiplication by the strictly positive left Perron vector of K proves rho(K)≥1, with strict inequality if any row is not this twist. Conversely, at the twist, K h²=h² and rho(K)=1. The weights telescope to W_n=h_j/h_{i_n}, so they are uniformly bounded, though generally not constant or zero-variance.

For every non-twisted Q, positivity yields (K^n1)_j ~ c_j rho(K)^n with c_j>0. Since P^n1 remains bounded and converges, the actual weight variance also grows exponentially.

Scope: this is a statement about fixed, stationary, one-path proposals for these unnormalized path weights. It does not rule out horizon-dependent proposals, adaptive proposals, resampling, Rao–Blackwellization, control variates, signed-source response estimators, or alternative couplings. Weight variance alone does not imply derivative-estimator variance for arbitrary perturbations; the explicit score calculation below is essential.

## 2. Deterministic interval realization and exact response

For m equal coarse cells I_i of length 1/m, split source cell I_i into consecutive subintervals of lengths P[j,i]/m, in target-cell order j. Map each subinterval affinely and increasingly onto I_j. The slopes are 1/P[j,i]. For a density constant at f_i on I_i, the transfer operator gives (L f)|_{I_j}=sum_i P[j,i]f_i. Thus its restriction to this finite-dimensional invariant space is exactly P, not a discretization approximation.

The actual affine Markov partition has m² atoms, while the invariant density space uses m equal coarse cells. The map is piecewise affine, with parameter-dependent source breakpoints and fixed branch images. It is not globally C¹/C². A claim limited to smooth expanding maps would require an additional smooth realization or a separate argument.

There is no missing moving-boundary term in the restricted calculation: branch images, target-cell boundaries, and source coarse-cell boundaries are fixed; their varying widths are exactly the entries of P_theta. The constructed positive invariant density defines an ergodic physical measure: its symbolic process is the positive finite-state Markov chain, and expansion makes the partition generating. Equivalence to Lebesgue measure gives the physical interpretation of bounded-observable averages.

For P_theta=[[0.9−theta,0.4],[0.1+theta,0.6]], theta in a neighborhood of zero, normalize density by h_1+h_2=2. Then

h_theta = (0.8/(0.5+theta), 2(0.1+theta)/(0.5+theta)).

At zero, h=(1.6,0.4). The invariant expectation of the right-half indicator is (0.1+theta)/(0.5+theta), whose derivative at zero is 1.6. This factor 1/2 from coarse-cell length must be retained when converting the derivative of a density coordinate to the observable response.

The second eigenvalue is 0.5−theta. Starting from uniform density 1,

partial_theta (P_theta^n 1)_2 at zero = 3.2(1−2^(−n)) − 0.6 n 2^(−(n−1)).

This converges to 3.2, hence the integrated response converges to 1.6. A transfer-operator calculation solves this example exactly and cheaply; it is the required baseline.

## 3. Row-normalized proposal and derivative increment

At theta=0, row sums are r=(1.3,0.7),

Q=[[9/13,4/13],[1/7,6/7]],
K=[[1.17,0.52],[0.07,0.42]],
rho(K)=(1.59+sqrt(0.7081))/2=1.2157433897282286.

Write b[j,i]=P'[j,i]/P[j,i], S_n=sum_{k<n}b[i_k,i_{k+1}], Z_n=W_n S_n, and Z_0=0. Finite path summation proves E_j Z_n=partial_theta(P_theta^n1)_j at zero. The two score rows are (−10/9,0) and (10,0).

This unbiased identity holds for any proposal evaluated at the differentiation point, even a theta-dependent proposal. If instead one claims Z_n is obtained by directly differentiating W_n while allowing Q_theta to change, the proposal derivative must be included and canceled by its likelihood-score term. Freezing Q avoids that implementation ambiguity.

Couple horizons n and n−1 by the same prefix, and set Delta_n=Z_n−Z_{n−1}. Given F_{n−1}, put a=i_{n−1}. Because P[a,i]/Q[a,i]=r_a,

Delta_n=W_{n−1}[(r_a−1)S_{n−1}+r_a b[a,i_n]].

Consequently,

Var(Delta_n | F_{n−1})=W_{n−1}² r_a² Var_Q(b[a,i_n]),

and the two row constants r_a² Var_Q(b) are exactly 4/9 and 6. Therefore

E_j Delta_n² ≥ (4/9)(K^(n−1)1)_j.

For an independent full-support level N with probabilities p_n, the nonnegative identity

E[(Delta_N/p_N)²]=sum_n E Delta_n²/p_n

holds even when infinite, and proves infinite second moment for every such p.

### Stronger: this roulette is not integrable

For a two-point random variable with atom probabilities q,1−q and values separated by d, its expected absolute value is at least min(q,1−q)|d|, regardless of a common additive shift. Conditionally, the two values of Delta_n/W_{n−1} are separated by 13/9 in row 1 and 7 in row 2. The smaller probabilities are 4/13 and 1/7, respectively. Thus

E[|Delta_n| | F_{n−1}] ≥ (4/9)W_{n−1}.

As E_j W_n→h_j>0, sum_n E_j|Delta_n| diverges. For every independent full-support level law,

E|Delta_N/p_N|=sum_n E|Delta_n|=infinity.

Moreover E Delta_n→0, so both positive and negative parts of the roulette have infinite expectation. Its formal telescoping series converges, but the roulette does not have a defined Lebesgue expectation. Only the finite-horizon Z_n should be described as unbiased without qualification.

## 4. Four-state slow-mixing extension

Let B=[[0.9,0.4],[0.1,0.6]], P_delta=(1−delta)diag(B,B)+delta J_4/4, and G_delta=(delta/4)(−1,−1,+1,+1)^T 1^T. Set P_{delta,theta}=P_delta+theta G_delta, with 0<delta≤0.01 and |theta|≤1/2. Every entry is positive and at most 0.9. The m=4 realization therefore has all inverse branches contracting by at most 0.9, uniformly in both parameters.

For the indicator of the last two coarse cells (indices 2,3 if zero-based), block transition probabilities imply the invariant expectation (1+theta)/2. From uniform initial density, its finite-horizon derivative is exactly [1−(1−delta)^n]/2. The response is 1/2 and its exact bias is (1−delta)^n/2. For delta=0.001 and absolute bias tolerance 0.001, n≥6212 is necessary.

At theta=0, row-normalized weights have r=(1.3−0.3delta,0.7+0.3delta), repeated across blocks. On block-symmetric vectors, K reduces to

Kbar=diag(r)[(1−delta)B+delta J_2/2].

For v=(1,7/80),

(Kbar v)_1=1.2155−0.789125delta+0.117375delta²≥1.2076204875>1.2,
(Kbar v)_2=0.10675+0.319625delta+0.117375delta²≥0.10675>1.2(7/80).

Thus Kbar v≥(6/5)v. Lifting v to (1,7/80,1,7/80), and using 1≥v, proves K^n1≥(6/5)^n v. This gives an explicit lower bound with no asymptotic qualification.

The derivative-increment variance also has a clean bound. For row j, G_delta[j,i]=d_j=±delta/4 independently of i. Therefore

r_j² Var_Q(G_delta[j,i]/P_delta[j,i])
  = (delta²/16)[r_j sum_i 1/P_delta[j,i]−16]
  ≥ delta r_j/2−delta²
  ≥ 0.34delta.

The first inequality uses the two opposite-block entries P_delta[j,i]=delta/4; the last uses r_j≥0.7 and delta≤0.01. Hence E Delta_n²≥0.34delta E W_{n−1}², and the same second-moment roulette obstruction holds.

This extension correctly separates uniformly fast inverse contraction from slow physical mixing. It does not maintain uniform mixing or uniform lower bounds on branch probabilities: those constants degenerate as delta tends to zero. It therefore contradicts claims based only on expansion, not guarantees which additionally impose uniform mixing/covering assumptions.

## 5. Primary literature and novelty scope

[Whiteley and Lee, Twisted Particle Filters, Annals of Statistics 42 (2014), 115–141](https://people.maths.bris.ac.uk/~manpw/filtering_aos.pdf), Theorem 1 and §4.1, establish eigenfunction twisting as the essentially unique zero-growth change of measure, and exponential replication requirements for ordinary sequential importance sampling otherwise. The finite-state stationary spectral statement above is a direct specialization of that established phenomenon. Their discussion also points to Bucklew, Ney, and Sadowsky (1990) for earlier Markov-chain results.

[Vihola, Unbiased estimators and multilevel Monte Carlo](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.01022), §3, Theorem 3, states the independent single-term second-moment formula and its finiteness condition, attributing the underlying estimators to Rhee–Glynn. Here the nonnegative formula itself already proves divergence; one cannot apply its unbiasedness conclusion without integrability.

The defensible contribution is an explicit, analytically checkable deterministic-response failure case and coupling diagnosis. No claim of novelty for the Perron twist or generic SIS instability is supported by this audit, and the search does not establish novelty of the example itself.
