CYCLE06: INDEPENDENT CQ-BASIS STABILITY GATE
Authoring role: broadcasting_blind_review; inherited Sol/max; no descendants.
Date: 2026-10-09. No Cycle06 sibling proof was read.
Scope: finite-dimensional A and B. Natural logarithms. T=one-half trace norm.
Verdict: UNKNOWN. No legal bounded-I counterexample with q bounded away from
zero, and no complete dimension-free orthonormal-basis rounding theorem.
This is a structural gate, not a new foundational theorem or priority claim.

1. EXACT INTERFACE AND SUPPLIED PREMISE

For a state rho_AB, define beta(rho) as the infimum, over ONE full-domain CPTP
C:B -> B tensor B, of max_j T((id_A tensor C_j)(rho),rho). Define
e(rho)=inf_{EB Lambda:B->B} T((id_A tensor Lambda)(rho),rho).
Define q(rho) as the distance to states classical on B in ONE orthonormal
basis, with arbitrary positive A blocks of total trace one. Entangled B bases
are allowed when B itself is a tensor product. The comparison states may
change both marginals; fixing their A weights is not part of the definition.

Supplied, not independently proved in this assignment: an actual local
broadcaster of error b, at sigma=rho_B, gives one sigma-preserving EB channel
with joint error at most
g(I,b)=min{1,6 sqrt[(I+1)/ln(1/b)]}, 0<b<1, with exact reconstruction at b=0.
This was supplied by the coordinator as the finite local theorem; its fresh
full audit is a separate dependency. This record does not assume an arbitrary
EB output to be classical on B.

The target is a function h(C,b) tending to zero as b->0, uniformly over all
finite dimensions and all rho with I(A:B)_rho<=C, such that q(rho)<=h(C,beta).

2. PROVED REDUCTIONS: THE TWO TARGET FORMULATIONS ARE EQUIVALENT

An EB channel has a finite measure-and-prepare form
Lambda(Y)=sum_y Tr(M_y Y) omega_y. The full-domain channel
C(Y)=sum_y Tr(M_y Y) omega_y tensor omega_y
has both marginals equal to Lambda. Consequently beta<=e.

For a B-classical state chi in basis b, let D_b be dephasing in that basis.
D_b chi=chi. Contractivity and the triangle inequality give
T(rho,(id tensor D_b)rho)<=2T(rho,chi).
Dephasing is EB and its measurement-and-copy channel is an actual broadcaster.
Thus
                 beta <= e <= 2q.

Let r(rho)=inf_b T(rho,(id tensor D_b)rho). Since D_b rho is B-classical,
q<=r, and the preceding argument proves r<=2q. The distinction between q and
dephasing disturbance cannot change the qualitative gate.

Taking infima in the supplied theorem, with continuity of g for 0<b<1, gives
                 e(rho) <= g(I(rho),beta(rho)).
At beta=0 finite-dimensional compactness supplies an exact broadcaster and
the supplied exact statement applies. In particular, on I<=C, beta->0 iff
e->0. Hence the target based on small optimal EB error is neither weaker nor
stronger as a qualitative bounded-I question: it is the same unresolved gate.

More formally, define h_beta(C,b) as the supremum of q over I<=C,beta<=b, and
h_E(C,e0) as the supremum of q over I<=C,e<=e0, across all finite dimensions.
Then h_E(C,e0)<=h_beta(C,e0) and
h_beta(C,b)<=h_E(C,g(C,b)). Either envelope tends to zero iff the other does.

The elementary information control q<=T(rho,rho_A tensor rho_B)<=sqrt(I/2)
only resolves families with I->0. A product state is B-classical in an
eigenbasis of rho_B. Holding I merely bounded does not make this bound vanish.

For each FIXED pair of finite dimensions there is a qualitative modulus by
compactness: the CPTP broadcaster set and CQ state set are compact, beta and
q are continuous, and the supplied exact no-broadcasting theorem says
beta=0 iff q=0. This argument has no uniform control as dimensions grow.

3. CONCRETE BOUNDED-INFORMATION WEAK-QUBIT PRODUCT STRESS TEST

This is a valid low-broadcast-error family, NOT a proved CQ counterexample.
It isolates a nonunital reconstruction mechanism that a uniform positive
theorem must handle. N>=4, s=N^(-1/2), kappa=sqrt(1-s^2). A single A is a
classical bit a in {+1,-1}, uniformly distributed. On a single B qubit put

 sigma_a=(I+s X+a s Z)/2,
 sigma=(I+s X)/2,
 rho_s=(1/2) sum_a |a><a| tensor sigma_a.

These are strictly positive because sqrt(2)s<1. On the full qubit domain
measure Z and prepare the pure states
 omega_z=(I+s X+z kappa Z)/2.
The channel Lambda_s fixes sigma exactly and satisfies
 Lambda_s(sigma_a)=(I+s X+a s kappa Z)/2=:sigma'_a.
The channel measuring Z and preparing omega_z tensor omega_z is an actual
two-output broadcaster with both marginals Lambda_s.

Now take rho_N=rho_s^(tensor N), with A=(C^2)^(tensor N) and
B=(C^2)^(tensor N), and tensor the actual broadcasters, grouping all first
outputs and all second outputs. This defines ONE legal full-domain B->B B
channel, not an operation defined only on the displayed conditional states.
It fixes the B marginal sigma^(tensor N) exactly. Its joint marginal error,
and the lifted EB reconstruction error, are at most 1/N, as follows.

Let delta=s(1-kappa)=s^3/(1+kappa)<=s^3. Then
sigma_a-sigma'_a=a delta Z/2. Both states have minimum eigenvalue >=1/8 for
N>=4. For S,T with this eigenvalue bound, Y=sqrt(S)-sqrt(T) solves
sqrt(S)Y+Ysqrt(T)=S-T. Taking the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product and applying
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 ||Y||_2 <= ||S-T||_2/(sqrt(lambda_min S)+sqrt(lambda_min T)).
For the two qubit states above this is <=delta. Therefore their root fidelity
F_r=||sqrt(S)sqrt(T)||_1 is at least Tr sqrt(S)sqrt(T)
=1-||Y||_2^2/2 >=1-delta^2/2. Multiplicativity and the fidelity-to-trace-distance
bound imply, for every conditional a^N,
 T(tensor_i sigma_{a_i},tensor_i sigma'_{a_i})
 <=sqrt(1-(1-delta^2/2)^(2N)) <=sqrt(N)delta <=1/N.
Taking the uniform average over the classical A blocks proves beta(rho_N)
<=e(rho_N)<=1/N. There is also an entirely elementary telescoping bound
N delta/2<=1/(2sqrt(N)), sufficient for the qualitative conclusion.

Write f(r)=h((1+r)/2) for binary entropy in nats. The EXACT mutual information
is
 I_N=N[f(s)-f(sqrt(2)s)]
 =N sum_{k>=1}(2^k-1)s^(2k)/(2k(2k-1))
 =1/2+1/(4N)+O(N^(-2)).
Since artanh(u)<=u/(1-2s^2) for u<=sqrt(2)s,
 I_N<=N s^2/[2(1-2s^2)]<=1, N>=4.

Here even the MAX-PRIOR Holevo capacity of the full product conditional
family is I_N, so the displayed bounded fixed-prior information is not being
silently substituted for a capacity bound. For any prior on a^N, subadditivity
of output entropy and the local entropy maximum at uniform a show
S(sum p(a^N) tensor_i sigma_{a_i})<=N f(s). Every conditional has entropy
N f(sqrt(2)s). The uniform independent prior attains the bound.

What is missing: a lower bound on q(rho_N) allowing arbitrary entangled
orthonormal bases of the entire B space. Dephasing each B qubit in Z destroys
the B marginal's X signal; dephasing in X destroys the A-B Z correlations.
Neither observation is a lower bound for a global basis. The total modular
observable of sigma^(tensor N) is proportional to sum_i X_i. Its normalized
commutators with the individual Z_i shrink with N, leaving a real possibility
of a collective basis with q->0. No q lower bound is asserted.

Pinching A in its classical label basis preserves CQ-on-B comparison states
and cannot increase the comparison error. Thus a q lower bound can be sought
among commuting unnormalized B blocks indexed by a^N. Their A weights may
vary. Restricting them to uniform A weights is permissible only after paying
an extra error at most the A marginal discrepancy (and thus a factor two in
a qualitative estimate). A product-basis calculation would not settle the
stated quantifiers. This stress test is frozen with its q status UNKNOWN.

4. EXACT FAILED CLIFFORD-FRAME CONTROL: AN ORTHONORMAL BASIS DOES ROUND IT

This construction initially looked like a nonorthogonal-frame counterexample.
It is not one; an explicit basis removes the apparent obstruction.

In an ambient m=2^n dimensional space, n>=2, take diagonal bit observables Z_i
and Jordan-Wigner involutions
 G_i=Z_1 ... Z_{i-1} X_i, i=1,...,n.
They anticommute pairwise. H=n^(-1/2)sum_i G_i is an involution. Let
P=(I+H)/2, rank d=m/2, and regard B=P C^m. The m pure states
psi_y=sqrt(2)P|y> form a uniform tight frame. Its canonical POVM consists of
M_y=P|y><y|P, and its channel is
 Gamma(Y)=2P diag(P Y P)P, with Y acting on B.
For B_i=P Z_i P, direct diagonal calculation gives
 diag(P Z_i P)=(1/2)(1-1/n)Z_i,
 hence Gamma(B_i)=(1-1/n)B_i.

On A take any n anticommuting traceless Hermitian involutions K_i. For
0<=alpha<=1 define
 rho=[I+(alpha/sqrt(n))sum_i K_i tensor B_i]/(d_A d).
This is a state: its perturbation is the compression of an involution
sum_i K_i tensor Z_i/sqrt(n), since the Z_i commute. Its marginals are
tracial and I(A:B)<=ln(1+alpha)<=ln2. Gamma reconstructs it with error at
most alpha/(2n); Gamma is EB and supplies an actual full-domain broadcaster.

An explicit B basis, however, gives q<=alpha sqrt(3/n)/2. Put R=G_n and
S=sum_{i<n}G_i/sqrt(n-1), so R,S anticommute. Let cos(theta)=1/sqrt(n),
sin(theta)=sqrt(1-1/n), and
U=cos(theta/2)I+sin(theta/2)S R.
Then U R U^*=H. The +1 eigenspace of R has the ON basis consisting of a
deterministic prefix z of n-1 Z bits and the X_n eigenstate of eigenvalue
product_i z_i. Apply U to this basis to obtain an ON basis of B.

For every vector of this basis, a prefix bit i<n is flipped with probability
u/(n-1), u=sin^2(theta/2)<1/2, while the final Z_n mean is zero. Consequently
 sum_i Var(Z_i)<=1+4u<3.
The corresponding compressed-observable dephasing D satisfies
 (1/n)sum_i tau_B[(B_i-D B_i)^2]<=3/n.
Orthogonality of the K_i under normalized trace, followed by
||Y||_1<=sqrt(d_A d)||Y||_2, proves the displayed bound on
T(rho,(id tensor D)rho). That dephased state is CQ on B.

Thus a large number of slow modes of a redundant canonical pure frame, even
with bounded joint I and genuinely quantum A, does not by itself falsify the
gate. The missing simultaneous ON-basis argument must actually be checked.

5. NARROW MISSING LEMMA; WHY THE PRESENT ROUTES DO NOT PROVE IT

A sufficient new lemma is:
For every fixed C and epsilon>0, every finite-dimensional rho with I<=C and
sigma=rho_B, and every sigma-preserving EB Lambda satisfying
T((id tensor Lambda)rho,rho)<=epsilon, there is ONE B dephasing D such that
T((id tensor D)rho,rho)<=f(C,epsilon), where f(C,epsilon)->0 independent of
both dimensions. Composing with the supplied local theorem settles the gate.
Conversely, a legal sequence violating this lemma must also be analyzed
against the original beta/e gate: an arbitrary EB near-fix already broadcasts.

Taking the ergodic projection of Lambda only proves the exact statement.
A finite iterate or Cesaro average disturbs rho by at most time times epsilon,
while its convergence to a fixed-point projection has no uniform mixing time.
Classical metastability demonstrates this failure of a proof step but is not
a counterexample to q, since classical metastable states have q=0.

Bounded relative entropy gives information-tail estimates but no uniformly
finite metric cover of all conditional states. For example many high-rank
conditional density matrices can be pairwise trace-distance separated while
each has bounded relative entropy to their common average. Therefore one
cannot claim that fixed-prior I bounds the number of distinguishable latent
measurement labels or the max-prior capacity. No such replacement is used here.

An ultraproduct/near-commutative approach would still need a lifting theorem
strong enough for GLOBAL trace distance, not merely convergence of finitely
many product observables. Bounded I alone does not bound operator Schmidt rank
or remove data-hiding-type global correlations. This approach is a possible
mechanism with a load-bearing missing lemma, not a proof.
