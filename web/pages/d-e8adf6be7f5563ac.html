## A literal determinant and why base automorphisms cannot make it zero

In original base coordinates put u=1+xy and

```
g=(u^3,3xu^2,-x^3),
h=(y^2u(4+3xy),y+3xy^2(4+3xy),2x-3x^2y),
g_p=a g_x+b g_y, h_p=a h_x+b h_y.
```

A direct exact calculation with arbitrary a,b gives

`det[g,g_p,h_p]=6(1+xy)(a-bx^2)^2`.                (1)

The identity is algebraic in a,b, so they may be arbitrary polynomial
direction components, not merely constants. For a nonzero constant
direction p in the new base, its pushforward through phi is a nonzero
locally nilpotent derivation delta on C[X,Y]. Write
`delta X=a`, `delta Y=b`.

If a=bX^2 identically, then delta X belongs to the principal ideal (X).
For any locally nilpotent derivation on a characteristic-zero domain,
`delta f in(f)` implies delta f=0. Indeed the finite delta-degree obeys
`deg_delta(fg)=deg_delta f+deg_delta g`; if delta f=fk is nonzero,
its degree would be both deg_delta f-1 and at least deg_delta f, a
contradiction. Thus delta X=0, then b=0 and delta Y=0, impossible.

Consequently (1) is nonzero over the base fraction field after every
base automorphism. Constant output D multiplies the determinant by detD,
so for bar g=D^T g(phi(B)), bar h=D^T h(phi(B)), the three vectors

`bar g`, `bar g_p`, `bar h_p`

are linearly independent over K=C(B). The factor u may vanish on a
special base locus; only generic independence is claimed here. Global
irreducibility requires the separate primitive argument below.

## Generic fibre is a coordinate, at every selected level

Write the orbit potential as

`f'=A^2+13A+2bar h dot z+c0(B)`,
`A=bar g dot z+theta(B)`,

where theta and c0 include the complete dual-translation corrections.
For v=(p,w), put `d0=theta_p+bar g dot w`. The actual derivative is

`P=(2A+13)(bar g_p dot z+d0)`
`  +2bar h_p dot z+2bar h dot w+(c0)_p`.

Over K, define three independent dual linear forms

`L=bar g dot z`, `M=bar g_p dot z`, `N=bar h_p dot z`.

Then for a suitable base scalar C0,

`P-sigma=2LM+(2theta+13)M+2d0L+2N+C0-sigma`.

This is twice the polynomial coordinate

`N'=N+LM+(theta+13/2)M+d0L+(C0-sigma)/2`

of K[L,M,N]. Its inverse subtracts the displayed shift from N'. The
quotient by P-sigma is K[L,M], so P-sigma is irreducible over K[z] for
every sigma, rather than merely for generic target values.

These field-level linear forms are used only to prove irreducibility.
Their determinant can vanish on a base divisor; no globally polynomial
source normalization by them is claimed.

## Global primitivity, including every degenerate base point

It remains to rule out a vertical base factor. In fact the positive-
dual coefficients of P generate the unit ideal in C[B]. Suppose all
quadratic and linear dual coefficients vanished at some base B0.
Unimodularity of bar g makes it nonzero there. The quadratic part

`2(bar g dot z)(bar g_p dot z)`

then forces bar g_p(B0)=0. Its linear part consequently forces

`bar h_p(B0)=-d0(B0)bar g(B0)`.

But the derivative of barF=bar h+t bar g in the nonzero constant base
direction p is then a multiple of its t column. Thus

`JbarF(B0,t)(p,d0(B0))=0`

for all t, contradicting its everywhere invertible Jacobian and p!=0.
There is no common zero of the positive-dual coefficients. Over C the
weak Nullstellensatz makes their ideal the whole base ring. This is a
stronger statement than absence of a common irreducible factor.

Hence P-sigma is primitive in C[B][z] at every level, regardless of its
constant coefficient. Gauss's lemma descends the field irreducibility
to the full source polynomial ring. This proves the theorem even over
the base loci where the generic dual frame degenerates.

## Polynomial critical sections would force a forbidden global pivot

Align a nonzero constant source direction v with an affine coordinate
tau, using any constant complementary frame, and denote its other four
coordinates by U. If a polynomial tau=s(U) solves P=sigma globally,
division by the monic polynomial tau-s gives

`P-sigma=(tau-s(U))R(tau,U)`.

For a mixed direction, irreducibility makes R a constant nonzero unit.
Then P_tau is globally constant, giving a constant second directional
derivative of f'. The audited orbit has no such pivot. This rules out
the section without assuming a unit corner on it.

For a pure-dual direction, the exact solution already has coefficients
`-betaj/alpha` in the remaining duals. Polynomiality forces alpha to
divide every other component of a unimodular g row, hence to be a
constant unit, contrary to the actual g's lack of a constant combination.
Together these exhaust all nonzero constant directions and all levels.

The weaker general component gate remains useful beyond this orbit:
if a polynomial section has unit P_tau on its graph and no global pivot,
the cofactor R is nonconstant and its zero set gives an additional
disjoint fibre component. Hessian invertibility makes these derivative
fibres smooth/reduced, but does not itself prove connectedness. The
control P=tau+u tau^2 has two disjoint zero-fibre components and a
noncritical polynomial graph; it is not claimed to have a constant-
Hessian gradient completion. Here the actual-map irreducibility proof
supplies the missing connectedness for all mixed directions and levels.
