Complexity scout — Phase A (2026-10-09)

Scope and evidence. I read the shared worker contract, ASTRA's current open gates and the pinned proof cards for the logarithmic-contact, detyping, EPR, and conditional-sampler branches. I selectively read the original OpenAI Math source for the noncommutative formula hitting-point construction and its finite-coefficient descent, plus the OpenAI common-integer-polymatroid FPRAS source. I also downloaded the official arXiv v1 source archive for Lin's MIPco paper. ASTRA is pinned at 39cdea532f19217f633f70315d92c314a6ce5e3e; OpenAI Math is pinned at fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. None of the ASTRA candidate proofs was independently reconstructed in this scout. Their recorded status remains source-derived/unreviewed, and correctness and priority remain unknown.

The strongest opportunity is the existing G-CIRCUIT-LOGARITHMIC-CONTACT gate. The precise target is: for every characteristic-zero field F, every n-variate commutative arithmetic circuit of size at most s and formal degree at most D computing a nonzero P in F[x_1,...,x_n], prove a uniform explicit bound B(n,s,D)=poly(n,s,D) on ord_{z=0} P(log(1+z),...,log(1+z/n)); or give a uniform polynomial-size circuit family with superpolynomial contact. Coefficients are arbitrary elements of F; the bound must not depend on their encoding. Circuit description size and degree are charged; black-box evaluation is kept separate.

This bound is sufficient (not necessary for every PIT route) for deterministic black-box PIT in characteristic zero. Truncate each logarithm after L=B terms, giving rational polynomials phi_i of degree at most L-1. The contact premise makes P(phi_1,...,phi_n) nonzero, of univariate degree at most D(L-1). Evaluating at D(L-1)+1 distinct rational points yields a hitting set with polynomially many points and polynomial bit-length/generation cost; circuit evaluation remains a separate oracle cost. This is the exact open gate, not a claimed result. ASTRA's N250 card says the four finite fixtures and prior small-derivative theorem do not settle it.

The useful hidden mechanism in the pinned OpenAI sources is the full chain, not a proposed linear-state lower bound. In “One Rational Matrix Hitting Point for Noncommutative Formulas” (OpenAI Math, pinned source), a size-s formula is converted to an acyclic path representation with at most 2s states; word coefficients are encoded by linearly independent iterated integrals; the resulting scalar series is a readout uGv of a rational differential system; and a Wronskian/Moura multiplicity bound controls its first nonzero Taylor coefficient. The source gives d=2ns^2 for a single rational matrix evaluation over characteristic-zero formula inputs. In “Uniform Matrix Hitting Points in Every Positive Characteristic,” the system's rational operators are truncated in a local coefficient ring and represented by multiplication matrices; the source gives explicit finite loops, an extension-field descent argument, and d=O(n^3s^6) for formulas. This suggests attacking the nonlinear circuit readout directly while preserving word/contact structure. It does not provide the missing circuit bound: shared circuit gates can make derivative-closure or faithful linear-state dimension exponential. N246's example P=product_i x_i has size O(n), derivative dimension 2^n, yet contact only n; hence that representation barrier does not rule out a direct nonlinear contact proof.

Prior art is unusually close and must be credited. Bhardwaj–Saptharishi, “Hitting Sets for Polynomials with Small Partial Derivative Spaces,” arXiv:2609.34436v1 (submitted 2026-09-28), proves a poly(n,d,r) hitting set in characteristic zero for degree-d polynomials whose partial-derivative space has dimension at most r; it includes depth-3 powering. Its formal derivation/Wronskian argument is already prior work, not a new discovery here. It supplies no general bound on r for arbitrary circuits, and therefore does not close the gate. The exact source scope is in its abstract and theorem: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34436.

Load-bearing missing lemma: a circuit-operation-sensitive contact estimate for P composed with the logarithmic curve that avoids constructing the full derivative closure. An early falsifier should search for an explicit small circuit P_s and parameter regime n,s,D with contact exceeding every plausible polynomial, using exact rational-series/Hankel computations only as counterexample discovery, not proof. In parallel, test any proposed recurrence on products and powers where derivative dimension is large but contact is small, and separate a theorem for circuits from one for formulas/ABPs. If successful, this would yield a general deterministic PIT breakthrough; if a counterfamily is found, it would delimit this specific generator, not prove PIT impossible.

A lower-confidence second opportunity is an exact reduction for the EPR circuit-counting gate. Define W as a length-m physical EPR gate word with gates G_e=a_e I+c_e C+d_e D, C=2|psi+><psi+|, D=(X tensor X)SWAP, rational a_e>0 and c_e,d_e>=0, with physical equality sewing on every wire; all rational input bit lengths count. Let Z(W) be that exact tensor contraction. The target is an FPRAS returning relative error epsilon with probability at least 3/4 in bit time poly(m,B,epsilon^{-1}), including a bounded-bit per-draw evaluation. N190 supplies a source-reported exact positive representation Z(W)=prod_e a_e E|det K(eta xi)| for a 4m-by-4m matrix and signs xi; its proof scope covers arbitrary gate interleavings/repeated gates at the equality-sewn level. This is only a nonnegative expectation: no polynomial relative-variance bound follows.

A potentially useful hidden bridge is the OpenAI source “An FPRAS for Common Integer Polymatroid Bases with Binary Capacities.” Its theorem counts unlabelled common integer bases from exact rank-value oracles with binary-encoded ranks; its proof develops addition/removal quadratic signatures and balanced summation/factorial lifts. At a physical wire, equality x=y becomes the one-hot balance x+(1-y)=1 after complementing the second selector bit. A research target is to prove that an EPR gate-word lift built from these balanced pairs has an exact parsimonious common-integer-base representation, or a positive two-log-concave-measure representation with acquired evaluation, support, initialization, and coefficient-range bounds. The latter could use Chen–Liu's arXiv:2610.06724v1 Theorem 1.6; the former could use the OpenAI polymatroid counting theorem. The complement identity is only a selector observation: it does not establish the required global signatures, rank oracles, or an exact reduction.

The strongest early falsifiers are already scoped in ASTRA. N193's exact bowtie fixture defeats naive scalar block gluing; its one-gate identity-transition example blocks the unchanged [I|A|I+A] basis encoding for the selected minimum; and the paired {00,11} physical selectors do not automatically inherit local Pfaffian-basis log-concavity. These failures do not rule out alternative lifts. N191 also shows a specific legal nonidentity family with exponential relative second moment under the tested gauges, but identity gauges have zero variance, so there is no universal all-gauges obstruction. N192's consecutively scheduled cactus family is already solvable by an exact transfer dynamic program and does not cover arbitrary interleavings. Chen–Liu already proves FPRAS results for the EPR Hamiltonian on arbitrary loopless graphs and for bipartite Quantum Max-Cut; their result does not by itself establish this distinct arbitrary gate-word contraction target. Verify any novelty claim against their exact application before proceeding. The original Chen–Liu theorem needs evaluation oracles, log-concavity, a nonzero support initializer, and coefficient-range upper bounds; its arithmetic-operation bound is not an acquired bit-time compiler.

The MIPco branch is a necessary audit, not a third breakthrough candidate. Lin's official arXiv v1 claims MIPco=coRE through compression and tracially embeddable strategies: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07162. I retrieved the v1 source archive (SHA-256 06005b6bd5f4d4520961c3102cc204aee5ba1e774fbe65aa2a99587a10c9ada5); CondLineardist.tex states the typed and detyped sampler, nontrivial-seed probability lower bound 1/(4t^2 16^t), the completeness/soundness lemma, and a fixed-answer fallback on malformed questions (lines 133–145, 161–214, 227–258; local file SHA-256 9ee3f696ace30c544744bcbb9a5614343e8163c98eff277a001094aa6e8b9154). A nearby TeX comment says the detyping/oracularization argument is wrong, but it is a comment, not a proof. N183's exact CHSH counterexample applies to the specified symmetric processed-Bob-private-prefix completion/equality fallback only; it does not refute arbitrary asymmetric completions or MIPco=coRE. N242's exact wire concern is narrower still: an event of probability 1/3 cannot be generated exactly from a finite uniform binary seed, but the adjusted four-role/prefix wire remains unaudited. The next useful task is an exact source/parser/sampler and downstream-register audit, not a broad theorem refutation.

The conditional-sampler gate also remains operationally open. N95's positive-word approximation has explicit positivity, support, minimum-entry and cost assumptions; it does not imply general positive-matrix counting. N109 is conditional on Chen–Liu's theorem and a finite-bit oracle interface. No end-to-end sampler/compiler has been acquired. This is not an endpoint for the quantum-counting opportunity.
