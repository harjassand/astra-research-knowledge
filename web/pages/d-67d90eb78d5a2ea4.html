STATUS
======

The cycle yields one complete conditional theorem and one exact approximation-class gate.

1. If a finitely presented group G has a nonidentity word w killed by every homomorphism from G to every sofic group, then every exact finite-dimensional representation G -> GL_d(K), over every field K, kills w. This follows from Mal'cev residual finiteness of finitely generated linear groups.
2. If the same G is linear-sofic or hyperlinear, its approximate models separate w while every exact same-dimension representation makes w the identity. A word-length telescoping inequality therefore gives a quantitative lower bound on distance to every exact representation. In particular, the finite presentation is not stable in the relevant normalized rank or Hilbert-Schmidt metric.
3. Independently, fixed-field rank-metric algebra embeddings imply stable finiteness of the coefficient-field group algebra. The obstacle for complex-linear-soficity is precisely the fixed-field step: the standard reduction gives finite fields that may vary with the finite model, and the primary source leaves the fixed finite-field question open.

This is a conditional representation/stability result, not a nonhyperlinearity theorem. The common-kernel word/presentation in the N06/N27/N65 candidate remains unacquired and externally unvalidated. No novelty or priority claim is made.

1. EXACT LINEAR REPRESENTATIONS KILL A UNIVERSALLY SOFIC-KERNEL WORD
====================================================================

Theorem. Let G be a finitely generated group and let w be a nonidentity element of G such that every homomorphism from G to every sofic group sends w to the identity. Then for every field K, every finite d, and every homomorphism rho:G -> GL_d(K), rho(w)=I.

Proof. Fix K,d,rho, and put H=rho(G). Since G is finitely generated, H is a finitely generated linear group over K. Mal'cev's theorem says that every finitely generated linear group is residually finite. If rho(w) were nonidentity, residual finiteness would give a finite quotient pi:H -> Q with pi(rho(w)) nonidentity. Finite groups are sofic, so pi composed with rho contradicts the assumed universal-kernel property. Therefore rho(w)=I. The field, dimension, and homomorphism were arbitrary. ∎

The proof is independent of characteristic and does not use group-ring trace or K-theory. In particular, it applies to exact representations over F_p and to exact unitary representations, viewed inside GL_d(C). Equivalently, w lies in the intersection of the kernels of all finite-dimensional linear representations over all fields. For the conditional N06 common-kernel witness, this strengthens the finite-quotient statement to all finite-dimensional linear representations.

2. QUANTITATIVE PRESENTATION-INSTABILITY THEOREM
==================================================

Let P=<S|R> be a finite presentation of G and let w in the free group F(S) represent the above nonidentity element. Write L=|w| for the expanded word length. For matrices A_s and B_s indexed by S, use either normalized rank distance

  d_rank(A,B) = rank(A-B)/D

over GL_D(K), or normalized Hilbert-Schmidt distance

  d_2(A,B) = (Tr((A-B)^*(A-B))/D)^(1/2)

over U(D).

For every word v of expanded length L_v, if max_s d(A_s,B_s)<=epsilon, then

  d(v(A),v(B)) <= L_v epsilon.                         (1)

Proof. Telescope the difference of the two word products. Each summand is a left and right product of invertible factors around one generator difference. Normalized rank is invariant under such multiplication. In the unitary case the normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm is invariant under left and right unitary multiplication. Inverse letters obey the same bound: B_s^{-1}-A_s^{-1}=B_s^{-1}(A_s-B_s)A_s^{-1}, and these outside factors are invertible; for unitaries this also preserves the Hilbert-Schmidt norm. Summing the L_v summands proves (1). ∎

Every exact representation of P in either class satisfies w=I by Theorem 1. Therefore every approximate tuple A satisfies

  max_s d_rank(A_s,B_s) >= d_rank(w(A),I)/L                (2)

for every exact rank-metric representation B of P in the same dimension and field, and

  max_s d_2(A_s,B_s) >= d_2(w(A),I)/L                      (3)

for every exact unitary representation B of P in the same dimension.

If G has an injective homomorphism into a normalized-rank ultraproduct of general linear groups, then a representative sequence has relator defects tending to zero and d_rank(w(A_n),I) tending along the ultrafilter to some gamma_w>0. Equations (1)-(2) imply that no such sequence can be exactified with same-dimension generator error tending to zero; on an ultrafilter-large set its distance to every exact representation is at least gamma_w/(2L).

Likewise, if G is hyperlinear in the unitary normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt metric, choose unitary lifts of an embedding. The finite presentation relators have defects tending to zero, and injectivity gives an ultrafilter limit gamma_w=d_2(w(A_n),I)>0. Equation (3) gives a positive lower bound gamma_w/(2L) from every exact representation. This is a failure of normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt stability of the finite presentation, conditional on hyperlinearity and the common-kernel premise. It does not rule out hyperlinearity.

Stronger tracial variant. If the chosen hyperlinear model also approximates the canonical group trace, then tr_D(w(A_n))->0 and, for unitary W,

  d_2(W,I)^2 = 2 - 2 Re tr_D(W).

Hence d_2(w(A_n),I)->sqrt(2), and the exactification lower bound approaches sqrt(2)/L. This stronger constant requires the canonical-trace condition; it is not inferred from an arbitrary faithful metric-ultraproduct embedding alone.

3. SAME-FIELD RANK MODELS FORCE DIRECT FINITENESS
=================================================

Lemma. For all square matrices A,B in M_n(K) over any field K,

  rank(I-AB)=rank(I-BA).                                  (4)

Proof. Let U=ker(I-AB) and V=ker(I-BA). The map B:U->V is injective: ABx=x implies BA(Bx)=Bx, and Bx=0 would force x=ABx=0. Similarly A:V->U is injective. Thus dim U=dim V and (4) follows by rank-nullity. ∎

For a fixed field K and dimension sequence n_j, form the rank-metric ultraproduct

  R_K = product_j M_{n_j}(K) / { (A_j): lim_omega rank(A_j)/n_j = 0 }.

Equation (4), applied coordinatewise and to block matrices of size m n_j, proves that R_K is stably finite. If a unital algebra k[G] has an injective algebra homomorphism into R_k, then k[G] is stably finite: any AB=I in M_m(k[G]) maps to an equation in M_m(R_k), stable finiteness gives BA=I there, and injectivity reflects BA=I back to the group algebra.

The exact group-level bridge is theorem-backed: Arzhantseva–Păunescu, Theorem 7.4 and Corollary 7.8, extend an injective rank-metric group representation over a fixed field to an injective group-algebra representation. Sofic groups admit such representations over every field, so their group algebras are stably finite over every field. A positive answer to their Question 8.6—whether every complex-linear-sofic group has a rank-metric representation over any specified finite field—would therefore imply Kaplansky direct finiteness for linear-sofic groups.

The characteristic boundary is the point. Arzhantseva–Păunescu Theorem 8.2 reduces a complex-linear-sofic representation to finite-field models F_j, but the characteristic may vary with j. Their Question 8.6 explicitly asks for a fixed finite field and says that a positive answer would settle direct finiteness for linear-sofic groups. No uniform fixed-characteristic reduction follows from their varying-field construction.

An exact finite control explains why Hilbert-Schmidt errors do not repair the rank gate: U_n=e^(i/n) I_n has d_2(U_n,I_n)=|e^(i/n)-1|->0 while d_rank(U_n,I_n)=1 for every n. Thus small normalized Hilbert-Schmidt error does not imply small normalized rank error. Coefficient fields also have no cross-characteristic scalar map F_p -> C. These are exact obstruction mechanisms to the naive transfers; they are not counterexamples to a deeper bridge theorem.

Fixed-phase monomial positive theorem. A stronger special-case bridge survives. Suppose a hyperlinear model takes values in monomial unitary matrices whose nonzero entries all belong to one fixed finite phase group μ_k, with k fixed across the approximating sequence. For Ue_j=ω^{a(j)}e_{σ(j)}, ω=e^(2πi/k), define its phase lift Π_k(U)(j,s)=(σ(j),s+a(j)) on [D]×Z/kZ. This is a group homomorphism into permutations. With c_1=2 and c_k=min{2,4 sin²(π/k)}, for any allowed U,V,

  d_H(Π_k(U),Π_k(V)) <= ||U-V||_2²/c_k,
  ||U-I||_2² <= 4 d_H(Π_k(U),id).                     (5)

The first inequality follows columnwise: different support permutations contribute squared column distance 2; equal support but unequal phases contribute at least 4 sin²(π/k); either disagreement changes every point in the corresponding phase fibre. The second follows because each differing column contributes at most 4 squared norm and exactly one full fibre of lifted Hamming disagreements.

For a faithful HS-ultraproduct model and finite F, put γ_F=min_{g≠h in F} lim_ω ||φ(g)-φ(h)||_2>0. On an ultrafilter-large set a coordinate model has all pair distances at least γ_F/2. By the second inequality and bi-invariance, its lifted permutations separate pairs by at least γ_F²/16. An m-fold product action raises this separation to at least 1-(1-γ_F²/16)^m. For relation tests, if every requested multiplication defect is at most e in normalized HS norm, the first inequality gives lifted Hamming error at most e²/c_k; the m-fold product has error at most m e²/c_k. Choose m so that (1-γ_F²/16)^m<=η and e²<=η c_k/m. This produces an (F,η)-sofic approximation. Thus a hyperlinear group admitting a faithful monomial microstate scheme over one fixed finite phase group is sofic. This proof uses only metric separation, not a canonical-trace condition.

The fixed phase alphabet is essential to this rounding argument because c_k is the minimum separation between distinct allowed phases. With k growing, U_k=I_D and V_k=e^(2πi/k)I_D have ||U_k-V_k||_2²=4sin²(π/k)->0, while their phase lifts have Hamming distance 1. This is an exact obstruction to a uniform HS-to-Hamming inequality when phase order is unbounded; it is not a model of the full N06 presentation. The lift increases the base size from D to kD, and product amplification has size (kD)^m, so the theorem has no efficiency guarantee.

4. WHAT A CONDITIONAL N06/N47 APPLICATION WOULD SAY
====================================================

If the N06/N27/N65 finite presentation and common-kernel word were validated, then:

* The common word would be killed by every exact finite-dimensional linear representation over every field.
* If the candidate nevertheless were linear-sofic, it would be rank-unstable in its fixed finite presentation by at least gamma_w/L, where gamma_w is its nonzero rank-ultraproduct separation.
* If it were hyperlinear, it would be normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt unstable by at least gamma_w/L. Under canonical-trace microstates the bound is sqrt(2)/L asymptotically.
* If it admitted fixed-finite-phase monomial unitary microstates, the phase-lift theorem would make it sofic; the candidate's independent sofic obstruction therefore rules out that monomial subclass, conditional on its finite witness.
* A separately valid failure of direct finiteness of F_p[G] would rule out soficity and fixed-F_p linear-soficity by the Elek–Szabó / fixed-field rank theorem. It would not decide complex linear-soficity or hyperlinearity.

5. FALSIFIERS AND NEXT DECISIVE GATES
=====================================

Theorem 1 is falsified by a homomorphism rho:G->GL_d(K) with rho(w) nonidentity; this would refute either the universal-sofic-kernel premise or Mal'cev residual finiteness. Inequalities (1)-(3) are falsified by a tuple violating the telescoping norm bound. The direct-finiteness bridge is falsified by an explicit fixed-field rank-ultraproduct algebra embedding together with a one-sided inverse defect, or by a failure of the finite-dimensional rank identity (4).

The strongest baseline is the exact permutation/rank obstruction: it excludes sofic approximations for the candidate under its finite rank and geometry inputs, but it does not exclude rank-metric GL approximations or unitary Hilbert-Schmidt microstates. The all-field finite-representation theorem adds a finite-dimensional radical/stability consequence, while preserving this boundary.

The next decisive gate is one of:

1. Materialize and independently validate the finite presentation and common word; then prove fixed-p rank stability or normalized-HS stability to turn Theorem 1 into a class exclusion.
2. Prove a fixed-finite-field representation theorem for complex-linear-sofic groups. This would settle the AP Question 8.6 route to direct finiteness and exclude fixed-characteristic group-ring witnesses from that class.
3. Construct a genuine hyperlinear nonsofic example satisfying the universal-kernel word premise, or prove a direct obstruction to its unitary microstates. No such group is certified here.

Neither nonsoficity nor failure of direct finiteness is transferred to nonhyperlinearity. All group-specific applications remain CONDITIONAL / UNKNOWN until their acquisition and independent proof gates close.
