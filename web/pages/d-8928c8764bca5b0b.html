## G1. Exact polar columns and constant overlap

Let M normalized vectors psi_c be the orbit of a genuine unitary
representation of a finite abelian group, and write them as columns
Phi. Assume G=Phi*Phi is positive definite. Translations commute G,
so normalized characters diagonalize it and also its positive square
root. In particular every diagonal entry of sqrt(G) is the same

    s=Tr sqrt(G)/M, 0<s<=1.

The upper bound follows scalar Cauchy--Schwarz and TrG=M. Set
Mu=Phi G^(-1/2). Then Mu*Mu=I. The columns mu_c are orthonormal,
Mu*Phi=sqrt(G), and <mu_c,psi_c>=s. For a character vector chi,
G chi=lambda_chi chi with lambda_chi>0, so

    Mu chi=Phi chi/sqrt(lambda_chi).

All these assertions concern acquired matrices or supplied ideal
coherent access; polar decomposition does not itself supply an efficient
circuit. Extra phases of projective group representations must first
be handled explicitly. The binary shift representation in the stated
source is a genuine one.

## G2. Any reversible projective realization uncomputes exactly

Let an ideal decoder isometry send mu_c to xi_c tensor |c>, with
normalized garbage xi_c. Such an isometry is realized by a decoder
unitary on a fresh ancilla. An arbitrary realization of the rank-one
projective effects has this form on the ensemble span. Decomposing
psi_c in the mu basis, its correctly labelled decoder component is
s xi_c tensor |c>; the phase/garbage is identical to that for mu_c.

Prepare M^(-1/2)sum_c chi_c psi_c tensor |c>_R. Subtract the decoded
label from R and postselect R=0. The resulting unnormalized vector
is

    (s/sqrt(M))sum_c chi_c xi_c tensor |c>_D tensor |0>_R.

The D labels are orthogonal, so its norm is s and success is s^2,
independently of the character. Applying the SAME decoder inverse
then returns

    (s/sqrt(M))Mu chi tensor |0>_D tensor |0>_R.

After successful normalization this is exactly

    Phi chi/sqrt(M lambda_chi).

The ideal coherent sum has norm sqrt(lambda_chi), whereas the accepted
unnormalized sum has norm s. Their exact proportionality establishes
the normalized statement. A mere success-rate estimate, measurement
channel or inaccessible garbage would not establish this coherent
inverse interface. Every preparation, reversible decoder and inverse,
reference label operation and mean1/s^2 attempts must be charged.

## G3. Optimality and precision, reconstructed directly

Uniform prior PGM success is s^2. For completeness, its optimality can
be proved without assuming an external decoder theorem. On the span
put Y=(s/M)(Phi Phi*)^(1/2). For every c,
psi_c*Y^(-1)psi_c=M, since this matrix element equals
(M/s)(sqrt G)_(c,c). Cauchy--Schwarz in the Y inner product gives
Y>=|psi_c><psi_c|/M. This is a feasible minimum-error-measurement dual,
and TrY=s^2 equals the projective PGM primal value. Thus any decoder
of this ensemble with failure p_fail has s^2>=1-p_fail. Existence of
that decoder does not make the PGM circuit efficient.

If the implemented decoder and its inverse differ in operator norm
by at most epsilon from the ideal coherent pair, the accepted vector
differs by at most2epsilon. Postselection is a contraction. For
epsilon<=s/4 its actual norm is at least s-2epsilon and its success
probability is at least(s-2epsilon)^2. Normalizing the two vectors
costs at most4epsilon/s in vector norm, hence at most8epsilon/s in
pure-state trace norm. Subsequent exact source transforms and partial
trace are contractions. The same proof holds for an input-subspace
isometry approximation ||U I0-V||<=xi: U*V-I0 has norm<=xi, and the
ideal accepted vector lies in ranV. A classical failure probability
alone supplies none of these operator-norm guarantees.

For the commuting source interface, the known codeword/syndrome/Bell
transforms map the normalized character sum to its canonical Gibbs
purification. Their exact source-transform correctness is an inherited
interface; this gate checks the new PGM mechanism, normalization and
precision, rather than claiming a new spin--boson Gibbs sampler.

## G4. Frame randomization does not acquire independent signs

For binary Pauli-label matrix H, physical Pauli conjugation changes
the Hamiltonian signs by H^T J r. The symplectic matrix J is invertible,
so the possible changes are imH^T=(kerH)^perp. Thus b dot c=0 for
every relation c in C=kerH. Every character chi_y(c)=(-1)^(y dot c)
is unchanged throughout the physically undoable frame coset. So is
the interference factor involving c1+c2 in C and the corresponding
Gram eigenvalue. Full independent-sign averaging cannot be replaced
by that physical frame randomization.

For P1=Z1,P2=Z2,P3=Z1Z2, the relation space is{000,111}. At
beta=log2 its overlap is tanh(beta)^3=27/125. The two character
eigenvalues are152/125 and98/125; a physical Pauli frame preserves
the relation parity. The full uniform-sign mean eigenvalue is1.

For L>=2 independent triangles with overlap a=L^(-3/4), the optimal
two-state block failure is(1-sqrt(1-a^2))/2<=a^2/2. Tensor-product
decoding has failure at most L a^2/2, which tends to0. Nevertheless
the aligned eigenvalue(1+a)^L grows as exp(Theta(L^(1/4))). This
rejects a small-unnormalized-normalization inference from failure
alone. It does not show inaccurate NORMALIZED Gibbs preparation;
the projective PGM actually prepares these small tensor factors
exactly. The example changes its overlap/temperature with L and is
an information-interface obstruction, not a fixed-temperature memory
failure theorem.
