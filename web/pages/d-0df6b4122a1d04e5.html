## Exact interface and source boundary

For each fixed n>=3 and 0<beta<1/2 there exist real C-infinity functions u,V on all of R^n, with u not identically zero,

    -Delta u+Vu=0,
    |V(x)|<=C(1+|x|)^(-beta),
    |u(x)|<=C exp(-c|x|^((4-2beta)/3)).

Constants are finite existential constants depending on n,beta and the selected construction parameters. No uniform bound as beta tends to1/2, sign restriction on V, prescribed potential, numerical construction complexity, or bounds on derivatives of V are established here.

Primary source: Frank–Ivanisvili (FI), arXiv2608.00802v1, submitted1 August2026. Its Theorem1 proves the beta=0, p=4/3 bounded-real-potential case. I read its mathematical construction in Sections2–10 and AppendixA from the primary PDF/text and visually checked Theorem1. The weighted case is r15's deduction, not FI's stated theorem. ## 1. Scale ledger and regularized origin

Use R=(K^2+|x|^2)^(1/2), alpha=(1-2beta)/3, a=R^alpha, ell=R^((1-alpha)/2), kappa=R^(-beta), and

    Phi=(R^(1+alpha)-K^(1+alpha))/(1+alpha), E=exp(-Phi).

Writing d=(1+alpha)/2, the exact exponent identities are

    a/ell = a^2*ell/R = kappa,
    1/(a*ell)=ell/R=kappa/a^2=R^(-d).

All auxiliary terms ell^(-2), a/R and R^(-2) are at most kappa for R>=1. Since alpha>0, a>=K^alpha>=1. The microscopic radius rho/a has a globally small Lipschitz constant; ell has gradient at most C K^(-d). Thus K can make both covers satisfy one fixed slowly-varying-radius condition.

Direct Cartesian differentiation gives

    partial_i Phi=x_i R^(alpha-1),
    partial_ij Phi=delta_ij R^(alpha-1)
                   +(alpha-1)x_i*x_j R^(alpha-3),
    |grad Phi|^2-a^2=-K^2 R^(2alpha-2).

Hence the origin defect is real and cannot be bounded by a K-independent multiple of kappa. The proposed argument retains it. On a macro patch of radius C ell, |a(y)^2-a(x)^2|<=C a(x)^2 ell(x)/R(x)=C kappa(x). On a micro ball of radius rho/a, all power weights are comparable and |Phi(y)-Phi(x)|<=C rho. These comparisons have constants uniform in large K and rho<=1. There is no comparison of E across a macro patch.

## 2. Cover, tangent choice and noncancellation

I reconstructed FI Lemmas6–11 with ell replacing their macro scale A. Greedy disjoint cores at a fixed fraction of ell give a countable locally finite cover by enlarged balls. Intersecting centers have comparable radii: the global Lipschitz constant is small compared to the fixed enlargement. Packing their disjoint cores gives a degree bound independent of K. Normalize fixed smooth bumps by their locally finite sum to obtain nonnegative chi_j, sum chi_j=1, with derivatives O(ell_j^(-m)). The same packing bound controls all indices whose support meets one microscopic ball because 1/(a ell)=R^(-d) is uniformly small.

For each nonzero center x_j its unit tangent directions form S^(n-2). At most twice the fixed degree many caps are forbidden by previously assigned neighboring directions. A cap intersected with that tangent sphere has relative measure at most C delta^(n-2), independently of the normal direction. For n>=3 choose a single positive delta leaving a direction available. Every edge is enforced when its later endpoint is assigned. At a zero center, tangency x_j dot t_j=0 is automatic; an arbitrary direction works. No derivative of the assignments t_j is taken. The argument fails in n=2, where one cosine frequency pair is the entire tangent sphere modulo sign.

Set p_j=cos(a_j*t_j dot (x-x_j)) and q=sum chi_j p_j. Only a uniformly bounded number of terms contribute. Leibniz gives |q|<=1, |grad q|<=C a, |D^2 q|<=C a^2. More specifically,

    Delta q+a^2 q
      =sum[(a^2-a_j^2)chi_j*p_j
             +2 grad chi_j dot grad p_j+(Delta chi_j)p_j].

Every term is O(kappa): the three scales are a^2 ell/R, a/ell, and ell^(-2). For the radial cross term, x dot t_j=(x-x_j) dot t_j, so

    |grad Phi dot a_j*t_j|<=C a^2 ell/R=C kappa.

The partition-gradient part costs a/ell. Therefore f=E q obeys

    |Delta f|<=C E[kappa+K^2 R^(2alpha-2)|q|],            (A)
    |grad f|<=C a E, |D^2 f|<=C a^2 E.

On a rescaled micro ball y=x+z/a(x), freeze only the partition coefficients, not the phases. The resulting model is a convex sum of finitely many cosines with frequency magnitudes in[1/2,2] and pairwise separation modulo sign. Relative frequency errors are O(ell/R)=O(K^(-d)); the freezing error is O(rho/(a ell))=O(rho K^(-d)). Indices entering the ball with chi_j(x)=0 are included in the error estimate, then removed from the model.

This model family is compact, including zero coefficients, since it is a finite union of compact parameter spaces. None is identically zero: restricting its distinct signed frequencies to a generic line produces an invertible Vandermonde system; some coefficient is nonzero because the nonnegative coefficients sum to1. Each model is analytic, so its zero set has measure zero. Compactness then supplies, for every fixed theta,rho, a threshold tau>0 for which {|q|<tau} occupies at most theta of every micro ball once K is large. This proves the exact sparsity interface; no uniform gradient lower bound at zeros or smooth zero-set boundary is assumed.

## 3. Weighted sparse inverse, including exhaustion

Here is an independent formulation of the key replacement for FI Lemma27. Suppose U occupies at most theta of every B(x,rho/a(x)), its radius has a comparable bounded-overlap cover, and a,h,E are positive and locally comparable there, with a bounded below and hE in L^2. Then for sufficiently small theta, the Dirichlet solution of -Delta z=hE in U satisfies

    0<=z<=C rho^2 h a^(-2)E.                            (B)

All constants depend only on dimension and those comparison/cover constants. In the actual application h=kappa or h=R^(2alpha-2), these are independent of K sufficiently large and rho<=1.

Proof reconstruction: extend v in H^1_0(U) by zero. On each cover ball v vanishes on the fraction at least1-theta. The ordinary ball Poincare inequality for v-v_B and the identity v=0 on that set bound its mean. Sum over the bounded overlap to get integral a^2 v^2<=C rho^2 integral |grad v|^2. Because a is bounded below, the Dirichlet form is coercive on H^1_0(U); Lax–Milgram gives z, and testing its negative part gives z>=0.

Its zero extension Z is a distributional subsolution -Delta Z<=hE*1_U. To check the direction, test with phi*min(1,z/epsilon), phi>=0. The extra term involving |grad z|^2 is nonnegative. Passing epsilon to0 yields integral grad Z dot grad phi<=integral hE*1_U*phi. The gradient on the level set z=0 vanishes almost everywhere, so this limit does not assume boundary regularity.

Subtract the Dirichlet Green potential in a ball B(x,b) from Z. The remainder is subharmonic. The mean-value inequality on B(x,b/2), together with nonnegativity of the Green potential, gives

    z(x)<=C integral_(U intersect B(x,b))
                       h(y)E(y)|x-y|^(2-n)dy
             +C b^(-n) integral_(U intersect B(x,b)) z(y)dy.

For b=rho/a(x), the first term is at most C Psi(x), Psi=rho^2 h a^(-2)E: the integral of the Newton kernel over the ball is C b^2. First work in a bounded smooth D compactly contained in U. Its solution is continuous on the closure and Psi has a positive minimum, so M=sup_D z/Psi is finite. Local comparison and sparsity bound the second term by C theta M Psi(x). Thus M<=C+C theta M, and theta can enforce C theta<=1/2.

Exhaust an arbitrary open U by nested bounded smooth domains, each a finite union of components. They inherit the same sparsity inequality. Energy estimates give a weakly convergent H^1_0(U) subsequence. Compactly supported test functions identify its limit as the unique global solution. The order interval 0<=z<=C Psi is a convex closed subset of L^2 and hence weakly closed, so the bound survives exhaustion. Smoothness of hE gives interior continuity and the bound pointwise. No regularity of partial U is used. This proves(B), not merely an unweighted operator-norm estimate.

## 4. Harmonic repair: exact parameter order

Apply(B) for h_1=kappa and h_2=R^(2alpha-2). Choose theta first using their uniform comparison constants; choose rho small enough for the eventual C rho^2 tau contribution; only then choose tau from sparsity; finally enlarge K for all geometric conditions and C rho^2 K^(-d)<=tau/40. Solve -Delta w=Delta f in Omega={|q|<tau}. Comparison with the two positive Dirichlet solutions and(A) gives

    |w|<=C rho^2 E[R^(-d)+tau K^2/R^2].                 (C)

The origin term uses |q|<tau before inversion. Since K^2/R^2<=1, the second contribution is made at most tau E/40 by rho; the first is made at most tau E/40 by K after tau is selected. The choice is noncircular. Thus |w|<=tau E/20 globally on Omega. For fixed K the stronger weighted bound also follows:

    |w|<=C_K kappa a^(-2)E,

because K^2 R^(-2)/(kappa a^(-2))=K^2 R^(d-2)<=K^d. The factor C_K is permitted; it is not claimed uniform as K varies.

Extend w by zero to W. It lies in H^1, although its higher derivatives need not exist across the irregular boundary. The function f*=f+W is smooth harmonic in Omega, by the sign -Delta w=Delta f and Weyl's lemma, and has envelope O(E). No assertion of smoothness across the boundary is needed.

## 5. Variable radius and safe quotient

Choose fixed sigma after tau, sufficiently small that |q(y)-q(x)|<=tau/8 on the ball of radius epsilon(x)=sigma/a(x). A nonnegative normalized radial smooth kernel defines F(x)=integral psi(z)f*(x-epsilon(x)z)dz. Local compact support and positivity of epsilon make F smooth to all orders despite W's boundary.

If |q(x)|<tau/2 the whole smoothing ball lies in Omega. At each such point the radial mean-value identity gives F=f*. This equality holds on an open set, so Delta F=0 there even though epsilon varies with x. If |q(x)|>=tau/2, q has fixed sign and magnitude at least3tau/8 over the entire ball. Subtracting |W|<=tau E/20 and using micro comparison of E gives |F(x)|>=c tau E(x). Thus every zero of F has an open harmonic neighborhood; quotient estimates are never applied through a zero.

For the smoothed smooth part G, set Y_i=x_i-epsilon z_i. The exact Laplacian chain rule is

    Delta G = integral psi[Delta f(Y)
       -2 sum_(i,j) z_i*(partial_j epsilon)*partial_ij f(Y)
       +|grad epsilon|^2 sum_(i,j) z_i z_j partial_ij f(Y)
       -(Delta epsilon) z dot grad f(Y)]dz.

Here |grad epsilon|<=C/(R a), |D^2 epsilon|<=C/(R^2 a). The three extra terms cost aE/R, E/R^2 and E/R^2, all at most C kappa E. For fixed K, (A)'s origin residual is at most C_K kappa E since 2alpha-2+beta<0. Therefore |Delta G|<=C_K kappa E.

For the correction part H, differentiate the variable kernel only. With t=a/sigma, K_x(y)=t^n psi(t(x-y)), one has |grad t|<=Ct/R and |D^2t|<=Ct/R^2. On its support, |x-y|<=1/t. Thus |D_x(t(x-y))|<=C a and |D_x^2(t(x-y))|<=C a/R<=C a^2. Product differentiation gives |Delta_x K_x(y)|<=C a^(n+2), supported in the same radius-epsilon ball. Consequently

    |Delta H|<=C a^(n+2)*a^(-n)*[C_K kappa a^(-2)E]
              <=C_K kappa E.

This is the decisive cancellation: two kernel derivatives are paid for by the inverse's a^(-2). Differentiating W itself would have been invalid, but is not performed. We get |Delta F|<=C_K kappa E and |F|<=C E.

Let O={|q|<tau/2} and U={F!=0}. They form an open cover. On O set V=0; on U set V=Delta F/F. The definitions agree on O intersect U because F is harmonic there. Hence V is smooth and real globally, and its size is at most C kappa. Every micro ball has a point where |q|>=tau, hence F!=0 there; F is not identically zero. Finally E<=C_K exp(-c|x|^(1+alpha)), proving the stated theorem.

## Audit limits, prerequisites and consequence

The source's two error scales in AppendixA are a/ell and a^2 ell/R. Requiring both to be kappa yields a^3/R<=kappa^2, hence the exponent p=(4-2beta)/3. The construction does not arise by rescaling FI's already built bounded V; its changing macro scale, weighted inverse and weighted kernel repair are indispensable. I found no hypothesis in FI's geometry that forces ell=a: slow variation, bounded degree and a*ell tending to infinity suffice.

The standard functional-analytic prerequisites used explicitly here are: ball Poincare, Lax–Milgram, truncation chain rule for H^1, Newton/ball Green estimates for n>=3, the subharmonic mean-value inequality, smooth domain exhaustion, weak closure of convex L^2 order intervals, interior elliptic regularity/Weyl's lemma, and the harmonic radial mean-value identity. Their hypotheses have been checked in the reconstructed steps. Neither an assumption of smooth nodal boundary nor a uniform inverse on all of R^n is imported.

Cruz-Sampedro's weighted upper theorem requires H^2 and all-strength weighted L^2 decay. Our u is in L^2, V is bounded, and Delta u=Vu in L^2, which gives u in H^2 by Fourier characterization; thus its regularity interface is met. A stronger power p+epsilon would yield all-strength p-weighted integrability and hence compact support, then zero by local unique continuation for smooth V. This audits the claimed sharp power, not optimal constants. The real cylinder precursor does not give global radial decay, and complex weighted examples do not establish the real extension by themselves.

Scientific consequence if adopted: weak pointwise real decay V=O(r^(-beta)), beta<1/2, does not improve the complex unique-continuation decay power in n>=3. The construction builds the potential and state jointly; it supplies no state acquisition, physical preparation or particular-material claim. Potential sign and derivative restrictions remain separate questions. Priority/novelty is UNKNOWN; FI already provide the new real mechanism, and the weighted exponent is classical for complex potentials.
