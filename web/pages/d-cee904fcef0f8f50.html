# Prediction inside the action span does not require full representation identification

Status: independently derived population theorem, sharp boundary, explicit nonlinear ambiguity, and conditional stability bound. Elementary exponential-family algebra; no priority or breakthrough claim. Finite acquisition remains unresolved without additional restrictions. Source: von Kügelgen et al., arXiv 2504.18522v3, revised 15 May 2026 (initial 25 April 2025).

## Exact interface and source comparison

Known labeled action vectors a_0,...,a_M in R^K; exact observed probability laws P_0,...,P_M. The action labels and the validity of an additive Gaussian shift model are supplied. The model is

    P_a = f_# N(W(a-a_0), Sigma),  Sigma positive definite,

where W, the ambient latent dimension d, Sigma, and the decoder f may be unknown. f is a common measurable isomorphism from latent space onto its observed image; the paper's C^2 diffeomorphism is more than sufficient. Population access means exact laws, not a finite-copy or density oracle implementation. The finite-copy case is treated separately below.

Author claim: Theorem 4.7 identifies P_a* when a*-a_0 lies in the span of observed relative actions, **under the same assumptions as Theorem 4.1**, including full row rank of W A. Appendix B.4 uses Theorem 4.1 to identify the entire decoder up to an orthogonal map before proving extrapolation. Section 4.3 explicitly repeats the diversity condition for prior action embeddings. The result is a correct sufficient theorem; it does not state that full row rank is necessary.

Our deduction: the span prediction conclusion holds at arbitrary rank of W A, with no known latent dimension or identified entire decoder. In the unrestricted-W class, membership in the observed action span is also necessary for identification. The source has no density-ratio, exponential-tilting, or partial-rank prediction argument in the archived text. This is a scope improvement of its displayed prediction theorem, not a refutation.

## Theorem 1: observed-law formula at arbitrary active rank

Let a*-a_0 = sum_e c_e(a_e-a_0), where the known real c_e may be negative. Define r_e=dP_e/dP_0, which is strictly positive P_0-almost everywhere. Then

    dP_*/dP_0 = product_e r_e^{c_e} / E_{P_0}[product_e r_e^{c_e}].       (1)

The denominator is positive and finite. Any model satisfying the interface and reproducing the training laws gives the same right side, even if its latent dimension or representation differs. Thus P_* is identified from the observed laws and action relation alone.

Proof. Whiten and recenter the latent Gaussian without requiring these operations to be known to the learner. Write Z~N(0,I), mu_e=Sigma^{-1/2}W(a_e-a_0), and absorb whitening into f. Injectivity gives the Radon–Nikodym chain rule

    r_e(f(z)) = exp(mu_e^T z - ||mu_e||^2/2).

With mu_*=sum c_e mu_e, the product in (1) is exp(mu_*^T z - sum c_e||mu_e||^2/2). Gaussian integration gives denominator exp(||mu_*||^2/2 - sum c_e||mu_e||^2/2). Its normalization is exactly the likelihood ratio of N(mu_*,I) to N(0,I), and pushforward proves (1). If an action has several coefficient representations, their normalized products coincide: the difference has zero latent shift and a constant log likelihood ratio. No rank assumption was used. QED.

This is a logarithmic density pool with signed weights: p_*=constant p_0^{1-sum c_e} product p_e^{c_e} when observed densities exist. For general densities such signed pooling can fail to be integrable; the Gaussian shift model proves integrability here. Do not replace the model with arbitrary distributions and retain this conclusion.

## Theorem 2: sharp action-span boundary for unrestricted W

If a*-a_0 is outside S=span{a_e-a_0}, there is a linear functional v with v|_S=0 and v(a*-a_0) nonzero. Fix any compatible model of positive latent dimension and any nonzero latent vector u. Set W'=W+u v^T, holding f and Sigma fixed. All training means and laws remain unchanged, while the target means differ. Gaussian laws with distinct means differ; an injective f preserves their distinction. Therefore P_* is not identified. If one also requires full rank of W itself, a sufficiently small nonzero perturbation preserves that open condition.

Consequently the action-span condition is the exact prediction boundary in this model class. This does not identify physical actuator validity, action embeddings, or the additive shift assumption. Supplied constraints on W can enlarge the prediction set and must be charged separately.

## Canonical observed predictive statistic

Let G_ef=mu_e^T mu_f. Pairwise observed KL divergences recover G:

    G_ef = D(P_e||P_0)+D(P_f||P_0)-D(P_e||P_f).                         (2)

Define T_e(x)=log r_e(x)+G_ee/2. Under P_0, T is centered Gaussian with covariance G; under a span action with coefficients c it is Gaussian with mean Gc and covariance G. Its Gaussian linear dimension is rank G=rank(W A), not ambient d. Equation (1) depends only on T, so this statistic suffices for all span predictions. Its sigma-field is minimal sufficient for this dominated family: any sufficient statistic must make each likelihood ratio r_e measurable, hence each T_e measurable; conversely all span likelihood ratios are functions of T.

There is no claim that r is the smallest number of arbitrary real coordinates: measurable encodings can change apparent coordinate dimension. The invariant claims are the likelihood-ratio sigma-field and the rank of this canonical Gaussian covariance. Exact rank recovery is a population statement; finite acquisition needs a nonzero eigenvalue threshold.

## Explicit representation ambiguity with identifiable span predictions

Use d=3, K=2, W=(e_1,e_2), Sigma=I, a_0=(0,0), and only actions a_1=(1,0) (optionally a_2=(2,0)) for training. Compare f_0(z)=z with

    h(z)=(z_1, cos(z_1)z_2-sin(z_1)z_3, sin(z_1)z_2+cos(z_1)z_3).

h is a C-infinity global diffeomorphism with inverse replacing the angle z_1 by -z_1. Its Jacobian is block triangular with determinant det R(z_1)=1. It is nonlinear, hence not one global orthogonal map. Conditional on z_1, the last two coordinates of N(t e_1,I) are independent standard Gaussians; rotation leaves their conditional distribution unchanged. Thus h_#N(t e_1,I)=N(t e_1,I) for **every** t. Both models produce the same training laws and every span-target law, despite different decoders.

The off-span target a*=(0,1) separates them. For f_0, E[X_1 X_3]=0. For h, conditional E[X_3|Z_1]=sin Z_1, giving E[X_1 X_3]=E[Z sin Z]=E[cos Z]=exp(-1/2), by integration by parts and the Gaussian characteristic function. The corresponding training shift rank is one although d=3. This also shows representation ambiguity can persist even with exact laws at an entire continuum of doses along an observed direction.

An exact signed-coefficient fixture uses mu_1=e_1, mu_2=2e_1, c=(-2,3). Its unnormalized log weight is 4z_1-5, log normalizer is 3, and normalized log ratio is 4z_1-8, exactly N(4e_1,I)/N(0,I). ## Load-bearing injectivity: a span counterexample without it

For the common noninjective decoder f(z)=|z|, d=1, actions a_0=(0,0), a_1=(1,0), a_2=(0,1), compare W=(1,1) and W'=(1,-1). Both training nonbaseline laws are |N(1,1)| and agree because |N(-1,1)| has the same law. The sum action (1,1) is in the training action span, but its target laws are |N(2,1)| and |N(0,1)|, with second moments 5 and 1. Hence the additive Gaussian assumption alone does not suffice after information-losing observation. The theorem's injective observation interface is essential. This is a deterministic information-loss example, not a theorem about all noisy measurement channels; known Gaussian convolution can be population-invertible.

## Conditional stability, with the acquisition assumption explicit

Suppose a learner has approximate log ratios satisfying |log rhat_e-log r_e|<=epsilon_e globally P_0-a.e. Let E=sum |c_e|epsilon_e. Its normalized product prediction has

    TV(Phat_*,P_*) <= tanh(E/2).                                        (3)

Proof. Relative to the true target, the unnormalized weight perturbation t lies in [a,b]=[exp(-E),exp(E)]. Write z=E_{P_*}t. Convexity of |t-z| gives TV<= (b-z)(z-a)/(z(b-a)). Maximizing over z in [a,b] gives z=sqrt(ab)=1 and bound (sqrt b-sqrt a)/(sqrt b+sqrt a)=tanh(E/2). A two-point distribution on {a,b} with mean one attains equality, so the distribution-free bound is sharp. Fixtures replay (3) at four E values.

This is not a finite-copy guarantee: a uniform log-ratio guarantee over Gaussian tails is a strong supplied/acquired premise. Negative coefficients magnify ratio error through sum |c_e|epsilon_e. Estimating ratios, KLs, their rank, and the normalizer must be charged. No latent samples paired with observed x are supplied. The alternative direct quantile baseline in one dimension uses only observed samples and the known dose difference; see COVERAGE_AND_CLOSURE.md.

## Prior art, consequence, and next falsifiable step

Nearest structural prior is ordinary exponential-family likelihood-ratio sufficiency and logarithmic density pooling. More directly, Bravo-Hermsdorff et al., 2306.04027v2 (NeurIPS 2023), Theorem 3.2 and its Appendix C proof, identify intervention laws by normalized products of observed densities with **real signed exponents**, under supplied interventional factor constraints. Section 4 explicitly proposes density-ratio estimation and reports direct likelihood fitting often worked better. Xu et al., 2409.07594v1, Theorem 3.6 / Eq. (6), derive observed density-ratio multiplication for separable latent interventions under a diffeomorphic observation map. Both decisive primary excerpts were read and archived. Neither cited theorem states the exact arbitrary-rank Gaussian-action-span corollary above, but their methods make a mechanism-level novelty claim untenable. Genest, McConway, Schervish (1986), DOI 10.1214/aos/1176349934, is an older pooling comparator whose primary full text could not be accessed in this pass; it is not evidence for a signed-shift theorem or priority claim. The recent paper's displayed prediction theorem is the closest exact-interface comparison. Broader priority remains unverified. Classify this as a simple but useful scope correction, not a foundational discovery.

Concrete use: perturbation benchmarks should include rank-deficient training action sets and a direct observed likelihood-ratio/quantile predictor. Such data can support identified intervention predictions even when full latent reconstruction is impossible. Requiring latent full-rank diversity to admit every prediction can reject valid scientific experiments. Conversely, claiming successful representation recovery is unnecessary evidence for the prediction task.

Next falsifiable step: freeze a rank-one, d=3 nonlinear-decoder family and compare PDAE against an equally informed density-ratio pooling baseline at matched sample and computation budgets, keeping tail coverage and signed-coefficient amplification explicit. The theorem predicts representation ambiguity and identical population span targets; it does **not** predict an algorithmic advantage. Before further optimization, independently audit (1) and search for an existing partial-rank version in exponential-family causal representation literature. The remaining hard problem is affordable acquisition under an explicit decoder/density-ratio class, not the population span proof.
