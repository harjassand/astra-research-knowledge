# Finite phase-potential certificates expose hidden stochastic reaction classes

## Result and status

I independently reconstructed the exact count-level invariant and stationary laws behind Astra U/N77, then audited a separate reversible completion from the sibling metabolic-thermodynamics lane. The two models must stay distinct:

- **Original N77:** a one-way three-reaction core cycle plus the reversible catalytic pair \(2C\rightleftarrows2C+D\). Its stationary core has nonzero cyclic circulation, so the full CTMC is not detailed balanced.
- **Reversible completion:** three reversible core pairs plus the reversible D pair, with a clamped balancing species \(Q\) at unit activity. This second graph is detailed balanced at the stated unit constants.

Both have the same finite phase graph, nonlinear pathwise count invariant, and escaping stationary family. I checked both graph variants. The pinned U/N77 entry itself is summary-only; its original note, proof/code packet, and replay logs were not supplied. Accordingly, the U assertions remain source-reported, while the derivations below are independently reconstructed. The reversible completion is a sibling candidate, not part of the pinned N77 source claim. Neither construction has external novelty or priority clearance.

The exact tool extracted from the example is a graph-potential test. Once a finite phase projection is supplied, it decides whether the change of an unbounded count is the difference of a potential on phase vertices. This recovers a nonlinear pathwise lattice invariant that ordinary stoichiometric nullspace and reaction-graph endotacticity do not provide.

## Exact graph-potential criterion

Let \(G=(Z,E)\) be a finite directed multigraph. Each edge \(e:u\to v\) has an exact integer label \(\delta_e\in\mathbb Z^r\), recording the change of \(r\) selected unbounded counts \(U\) in the projected transition. Parallel edges are retained. Then the following are equivalent:

1. There is a potential \(g:Z\to\mathbb Z^r\) such that \(\delta_e=g(v)-g(u)\) for every edge.
2. The signed sum of labels around every closed walk in the underlying undirected multigraph is zero.

If the condition holds, \(U-g(z)\) is conserved along every transition represented by the phase graph. Necessity follows by telescoping. For sufficiency, set one root potential to zero in each weakly connected component and propagate labels along a spanning forest; reject if any already assigned vertex receives a different value. Such a conflict returns a finite signed-cycle certificate.

Given the finite phase graph, this is an exact \(O(|Z|+|E|)\)-arithmetic-operation decision procedure with \(O(|Z|+|E|)\) storage. Integer arithmetic bit cost is charged; potential bit length is at most the input-label bit length plus \(O(\log |Z|)\). Constructing or acquiring the phase set can dominate. For \(s\) nonnegative bounded species with supplied total at most \(L\), naive enumeration costs up to \(\binom{L+s}{s}\) states, before checking reactions. The certificate does not discover a useful projection, prove its finite closure, or prove recurrence.

For both networks here, use \(U=A\) and phases \(z=(B,C)\) in the supplied linear slice \(B+C=2\):
\[
z_0=(0,2),\qquad z_1=(1,1),\qquad z_2=(2,0).
\]
The core edges have labels \(+1,+2,-3\) around \(z_0\to z_1\to z_2\to z_0\); the D pair gives zero-labelled loops at \(z_0\). The graph-potential test returns \(g(z_0)=0,g(z_1)=1,g(z_2)=3\), hence
\[
A-g(B,C)=A-\frac{B(B+1)}2=n.
\]
The reversible completion adds reverse edges with labels \(-1,-2,+3\); these also satisfy the same potential equations.

This is a standard graph-coboundary consistency criterion specialized to projected reaction jumps. I do not claim a new general graph-theory theorem or historical priority. Its useful consequence here is that it exposes an exact nonlinear stochastic component label from a three-vertex projection. If \(Z\) is finite and \(g\) bounded, and each integer \(n\) indexes a closed irreducible positive-recurrent class supported on \(U=n+g(z)\), then every stationary law on that class has \(U\ge n+\min_Z g\). Thus the family cannot be uniformly tight as \(n\to\infty\). This corollary requires separate proofs of closure, irreducibility, nonexplosion, and positive recurrence.

## Original N77: exact reconstruction

Use species order \((A,B,C,D)\) and unit-volume stochastic mass action
\[
\lambda_{y\to y'}(x)=k_{y\to y'}\prod_i x_i^{\underline{y_i}},
\qquad
m^{\underline j}=m(m-1)\cdots(m-j+1).
\]
The original graph at unit rates is
\[
2C\to A+B+C\to3A+2B\to2C,
\qquad 2C\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]
Its reaction vectors are
\[
(1,1,-1,0),\quad(2,1,-1,0),\quad(-3,-2,2,0),\quad(0,0,0,1),\quad(0,0,0,-1).
\]
Their rank is three and their left nullspace is generated by \((0,1,1,0)\), so \(B+C\) is the only linear conservation law. There are four complexes in one strongly connected linkage class; the deficiency is \(4-1-3=0\). The reversible \(2C\rightleftarrows2C+D\) pair is catalyzed by \(2C\); \(C\) also participates in the core cycle.

For every \(n\in\mathbb N_0\),
\[
\Gamma_n=\{(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d):d\in\mathbb N_0\}
\]
is closed. On its three phases, the core transition rates are
\[
r_0=2k_1,\qquad r_1=(n+1)k_2,\qquad
r_2=2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)k_3.
\]
At phase 0, D birth and death rates are \(2k_4\) and \(2k_5d\); they vanish in the other phases. Every \(\Gamma_n\) is irreducible: the directed core cycle visits phase 0, where D can be changed to any nonnegative count. At fixed \(n\), core rates are bounded over the three phases, D immigration is bounded, and D deaths are linear. The number of core jumps on a finite interval is dominated by a bounded-rate Poisson process; D births are likewise bounded and D deaths cannot exceed initial D plus births. This proves nonexplosion.

For arbitrary positive constants \(k_1,\ldots,k_5\), put \(q=n+1\),
\[
w_0=\frac1{2k_1},\quad
w_1=\frac1{qk_2},\quad
w_2=\frac1{2q(q+1)(q+2)k_3},\quad
W=w_0+w_1+w_2,\quad \rho=\frac{k_4}{k_5}.
\]
Then
\[
\pi_n(z_j,d)=\frac{w_j}{W}e^{-\rho}\frac{\rho^d}{d!}
\]
is stationary. Indeed, each core phase carries the same circulating flux, since \(w_0r_0=w_1r_1=w_2r_2=1\). In phase 0 the D birth/death edge is separately balanced because
\[
\left(e^{-\rho}\frac{\rho^d}{d!}\right)2k_4
=
\left(e^{-\rho}\frac{\rho^{d+1}}{(d+1)!}\right)2k_5(d+1).
\]
These equations give global balance at every \((z_j,d)\). The whole CTMC is **not** detailed balanced: its one-way core cycle has nonzero stationary circulation. The phase weights are a circulation balance, not a detailed-balance identity. The D subchain alone is detailed balanced. Because the class is irreducible, nonexplosive, and has an invariant probability, it is positive recurrent.

For unit constants, \(D_n=q^3+5q^2+8q+5=n^3+8n^2+21n+19\), and
\[
(p_0,p_1,p_2)=
\frac{(q(q+1)(q+2),\,2(q+1)(q+2),\,1)}{D_n}.
\]
The D marginal is \(\mathrm{Pois}(1)\), independently of phase. At \(n=9\),
\[
(p_0,p_1,p_2)=\left(\frac{1320}{1585},\frac{264}{1585},\frac1{1585}\right),
\quad
\mathbb E_{\pi_9}A=9+\frac{267}{1585}\approx9.16845.
\]
Since \(A\ge n\) on \(\Gamma_n\), every compact set has probability zero under \(\pi_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). The family is not uniformly tight, even though all its measures lie in \(B+C=2\).

The nonlinear label is not a deterministic first integral. For the original unit-rate ODE, with \(H(a,b)=a-b(b+1)/2\),
\[
\dot H=(1/2-b)c^2+(3/2-b)abc+(2b-2)a^3b^2.
\]
At \((a,b,c)=(1/2,1/2,3/2)\), this equals \(11/32\). The count-level invariant uses the integer lattice and enabled reaction jumps; it is not inherited by the continuous ODE.

## Reversible four-pair completion: separate audit

The sibling candidate replaces each core arrow by a reversible pair and adds a clamped balancing species \(Q\):
\[
2C+Q\rightleftarrows A+B+C,\quad
A+B+C+2Q\rightleftarrows3A+2B,\quad
3A+2B\rightleftarrows2C+3Q,\quad
2C+Q\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]
At \(a_Q=1\), all eight effective internal directional constants are set to one. The Q terms balance a one-unit elemental moiety when Q is counted; Q is externally clamped in the model. If Q is dynamic, that enlarged CTMC is outside the claim.

After clamping Q, the internal stoichiometric matrix is the same as above for the core plus the D vector, so rank three and sole linear conservation \(B+C\). There are four internal complexes, one linkage class, and deficiency zero. At \((1,1,1,1)\), every directional pair has equal equilibrium mass-action flux. Therefore this is detailed balanced and, by the single-linkage global-attractor theorem for complex-balanced systems, the unique attractor in the positive class \(B+C=2\).

The reverse channels preserve the same \(\Gamma_n\) and \(H\). With unit constants the stationary law is the product of four independent \(\mathrm{Pois}(1)\) weights, conditioned on \(\Gamma_n\). The unnormalized phase weights are
\[
\left(\frac12,\frac1{n+1},\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}\right),
\]
and D remains independent \(\mathrm{Pois}(1)\). Each paired edge satisfies detailed balance: for any enabled \(y\rightleftarrows y'\) and \(x'=x-y+y'\),
\[
\frac1{x!}\,x^{\underline y}
=\frac1{(x-y)!}
=\frac1{(x'-y')!}
=\frac1{x'!}\,(x')^{\underline{y'}}.
\]
This verifies the exact graph, not merely an imported product-form expectation. Each of the eight oriented channels has mean stationary gross event rate \(1/Z_n\), where \(Z_n\) is the sum of the three unnormalized phase weights; each reversible pair has zero net current. Nevertheless \(A\ge n\) and the stationary family is still not tight in the common linear class.

For the reversible unit-rate ODE, the same test point gives \(\dot H=41/16\), so the stochastic invariant remains non-invariant in concentration dynamics. A nonzero pair current, a transition changing \(B+C\) or \(H\), phase frequencies incompatible with the displayed law, or a D distribution inconsistent with \(\mathrm{Pois}(1)\) falsifies the detailed-balanced model.

This completion sharpens the separation: no net thermodynamic cycle current is needed for the stochastic stationary escape. It is a formal, high-molecularity model, not evidence that a natural metabolic pathway has this behavior. The chemostat activity, complete reaction inventory, count propensities, and eight directional rates must all be controlled or measured. The candidate does not establish a large-volume limit; in particular, maintaining \(B+C=2\) as volume grows sends that pool's concentration to zero.

## Experimental falsifier and full costs

A direct count-level validation can use independently initialized reference-volume chambers at class index \(n=9\), with the exact unit-rate propensities and complete reaction-event logging. After a separately justified burn-in, 10,000 independent stationary snapshots predict phase-1 frequency \(264/1585\approx0.16656\); the approximate 95% Wald band is \(0.1593\)–\(0.1739\). Phase 2 has probability \(1/1585\approx0.000631\), so 4,747 independent stationary snapshots give at least 95% probability of seeing it once. The required time resolution is order \(n^{-3}\), because the top-phase high-order reaction intensity grows as \(n^3\). D counts should follow \(\mathrm{Pois}(1)\). No stationary burn-in bound is presently supplied; independent replication or regenerative sampling, temporal resolution, molecule-count readout, and loading at least \(n\) A molecules are charged. No experiment has been run.

The sharp structural falsifier is any correctly resolved reaction jump that changes \(B+C\) or \(A-B(B+1)/2\). For the reversible completion, a persistent nonzero stationary current in any reversible pair also falsifies the stated unit-rate detailed-balance parameterization. Such observations can instead indicate a missing channel, a wrong propensity, an uncontrolled Q activity, or measurement error; they do not alone distinguish which model assumption failed.

## Matched baselines and boundaries

- **Deterministic global attraction:** the network is weakly reversible with one linkage class and complex balanced, so established theory gives convergence to the class equilibrium. This predicts the same limit for every positive deterministic initial point, but does not give a tight family of stochastic stationary measures across all count components.
- **Weak reversibility and endotacticity:** the graph is weakly reversible and single-linkage; it is also strongly endotactic. Established deterministic permanence/global-attractor results apply. N33 is a broader all-endotactic measurable-rate permanence candidate, not accepted external proof. Craciun–Nazarov–Pantea Theorem 19 is explicitly a two-species theorem; their paper presents arbitrary-species endotactic permanence as a conjecture. Do not promote N33 to established multidimensional prior art.
- **Stochastic baselines:** Anderson–Craciun–Kurtz product form gives exactly the within-class stationary law once the closed irreducible component is specified. The phase-potential criterion supplies a finite structural way to expose that component label; it does not improve the conditional product-form law. Published examples show strongly endotactic stochastic systems can be transient or explosive. Recent nonexplosion results for second-order endotactic systems do not cover this fifth-order core.
- **Graph-inference baselines:** linear nullspace computation returns \(B+C\). Existing MILP methods classify endotactic/strongly endotactic geometry with more than two species, but do not compute nonlinear lattice components. Exact full-state reachability could also expose the invariant, but the CTMC is infinite because A and D are unbounded. The phase-potential test is a finite preprocessor when a finite phase projection is already supplied.
- **Astra certificate/safety boundaries:** N34 distinguishes CERTIFIED/REFUTED/UNKNOWN for acquired finite certificates and does not provide general unbounded global recognition. N50's rational global-recognition compiler remains conditional on the affine reconstruction and has no implemented general CAD engine or complexity bound. N35 gives exponential finite-horizon jump-safety bounds from an acquired strict inward polytope, not infinite-time recurrence or classwise stationary tightness. N76 gives an exact falling-factorial upper-increment bound for the embedded event chain, not negative drift or physical-time recurrence. N54 is conditional on N33 and common scalar diffusion; it is not a stochastic class result.

For precision, the N33 claim is conditional/source-derived: a finite actual exponent-arrow diagram (real or negative exponents allowed on the positive orthant), a supplied fixed rate box $0<κ≤k_e(t)≤K<∞$, and arbitrary measurable rate paths. For each positive stoichiometric class $P=(c+S)∩R^d_{>0}$, it asserts one common compact convex invariant absorber, a unique global positive trajectory from every $x_0∈P$ under every rate path, and a finite entry-time bound depending on $x_0$ but not on the rate path. It does not give one entry-time bound uniform over an unbounded class. The N35 stochastic theorem instead assumes integer molecular complexes, predictable bounded controls, a supplied strict inward polytope with buffered lattice-compatible start, and yields an exponential finite-horizon exit bound; it does not assert infinite-time nonexplosion or recurrence. N76's exact summary inequality is $F(x-y+y')−F(x)≤log(M(x)/x_under_y)$, with $F(x)=min_{y≤x}log((x-y)!)$ and $M(x)=max_{y≤x}x_under_y$; this controls upward increments of the event-chain potential but supplies no negative drift.
