C02_L08 — DEEPER FINITE-BIT / GAUGE AUDIT
7 October 2026. Continuation after immutable INITIAL checkpoint.

The new result is an interface audit: the numerical-conditioning burden can
be made polynomial in the explicit bit length, but ambient balancing is a
gauge change and cannot improve the microscopic determinant-weight law. The
remaining obstacle is therefore energy/mixing, not a hidden square-root or
floating-precision requirement.

1. EXTERNAL FIELD IS EXACTLY A GAUGE ON FULL BASES

Let V be any rational vector-pair representation with n ambient rows. For a
full basis A, its probability is proportional to

  w(A) = a(A) |det V_A|^2,

where a(A) is the product of rational pair activities. For a positive rational
diagonal D, every full basis satisfies

  a(A)|det (DV)_A|^2 = det(D)^2 w(A).

Thus the normalized law on microscopic basis states is exactly invariant.
Moreover, the ratio of the weights of any two full basis states is invariant.
For any fixed proposal graph and Metropolis-Hastings proposal probabilities,
the acceptance ratio min(1,w(B)q(B,A)/(w(A)q(A,B))) is unchanged if the
proposal probabilities are held fixed. External row fields can improve the
hole coordinates g(ij), but cannot change a conductance bottleneck in the
same full-state chain. They are a calibration/gauge device, not a cure for
small determinant ratios.

This clarifies the scope of the delta^4 reassignment counterexample in
work/cycle4/parity_matching_transfer_attack.txt. There, a four-row rational
matrix has two replica reassignments with unit amplitudes and another pair
with amplitudes delta, delta. The product squared-weight ratio is delta^4;
for delta=2^-L it is 2^-4L. Any proposal whose only direct connection between
those states is that reassignment has Metropolis acceptance at most 2^-4L.
This refutes a uniform polynomial local-ratio claim for that proposal. It is
not a general mixing lower bound: another path, a defect register, or a
different kernel may avoid the reassignment.

2. LEGAL SUBDIVISION PRESERVES THE SAME MICROSCOPIC RATIOS

For a paired line (v,w) of activity lambda, replace it by the private-row
chain (v,e_s), (e_s,e_t), (e_t,w), with activities a,b,c. Expanding along
the two private rows gives only two nonzero alternatives: the middle line
when the old pair is absent, and both outer lines when it is present. The
effective activity is ac/b. Iteration yields an odd path profile. If adjacent
profile activities match and the center is lambda, the two path tilings have
exactly the old activity ratio; every logical full basis has one unique
path-tiling lift and its weight is multiplied by the same known product C0.
Therefore the ratio between any two projected logical basis weights is
unchanged. A graph energy proof might still benefit from the larger state
space, but the subdivision identity alone pays no determinant conditioning
debt and proves no gap.

If the activities are rational with bit length P, the source profile's
parameter K can be chosen O(P), since its maximum numerical activity is at
most 2^P. Each profile entry is a max of rationals and a power-of-two
rescaling, so its bit length is O(P) and the number of private coordinates
per logical coordinate edge is O(P). In the coordinate-dominated path of
INITIAL, all coordinate activities lie in [D0^-1,1], the floor gives
B_ij=H_i=1, and p=2: the graph expansion is only nine edges per logical
coordinate edge. The entries of F do not enter this path length. They still
enter determinant arithmetic and the unresolved transition comparison.

3. EXACT COMPUTATION SIZE VERSUS ENUMERATION TIME

Let L be the total bit length of an explicit rational-complex F and P the
maximum bit length of pair activities. Clearing denominators in any selected
n-by-n minor gives a Gaussian-integer determinant with O(nL+n log n) bits
by Hadamard. Its squared modulus has O(nL+n log n) bits after restoring the
common denominator. A product of at most n/2 activities adds O(nP) bits.
Thus every nonzero microscopic weight and every ratio of two such weights
has polynomial bit length. Fraction-free elimination computes a specified
weight in polynomial bit operations. The full normalization may nevertheless
sum exponentially many weights; the output-size bound does not evaluate it.

If a future local kernel has rational proposal/acceptance probabilities,
these exact ratios can be compared with a b-bit uniform integer using exact
integer arithmetic. Replacing each exact Bernoulli draw by a 2^-b grid
changes its law by at most 2^-b. Over T transitions the total variation
error is at most T 2^-b, so b=ceil(log2(T/delta)) suffices. This is
polynomial-bit simulation even when an acceptance probability itself is
exponentially small. Such a probability may still make the chain mix slowly;
simulation precision and spectral gap are separate costs.

4. BALANCE OBSERVATION COST AT THE WEAK-PROBE ENDPOINT

For a coordinate pair of activity w, the partition expansion gives the
exact selected-pair probability p_ij = w g_ij (before any separate bag
inflation). If a marked bag has inflation I<=2, the sole-probe probability
is w g_ij/I. With row balance g_ij>=1/4 on some edge of each row, the
corresponding mean is at least w/8. Estimating g to absolute error alpha by
the scaled empirical probe frequency requires absolute probability error
O(w alpha); Hoeffding therefore uses

  S = O(w^-2 alpha^-2 log(nK/delta)).

At w=D0^-1 or w=D^-1 this is polynomial because D0=10^8(n+1)^4 and the
subdivision dimension N is polynomial. This is a fully charged observation
cost, conditional on having a sampler for the marked law. It also shows why
the coordinate-dominated initialization matters: raw estimates of a tiny
unscaled g cannot be handled with fixed additive precision, but after balance
the row-max event is only inverse-polynomially rare, even at the weak probe.

5. DECISION

The initial external field can be produced deterministically with exact
rational output and polynomially bounded encoding; the robust iterative
update can also be carried out with finite-bit arithmetic once its interval
estimates are supplied. The coordinate-dominated base plus the bounded
activity schedule avoids an exponentially fine absolute-precision demand.
No external-field optimization theorem is needed for these steps.

What remains unavailable is the source of later estimates and samples. The
known exact-support algorithm returns zero/nonzero and a witness, not
partition ratios. The positive stable hole polynomial and aggregate exchange
do not supply a coefficient oracle. Exact row rescaling leaves microscopic
full-state weights unchanged, and exact subdivision preserves projected
weight ratios. Therefore neither can defeat the known exponential local
ratio examples by itself. The decisive missing result is still an acquired
kernel/representation with polynomial two-replica additive Dirichlet energy
or a different polynomial sampler. Any claim that balance or subdivision
alone resolves the FPRAS would be false.

6. NEAR-TIGHT DETERMINISTIC BALANCE WOULD ALREADY COUNT MATCHINGS

There is a precise barrier to an unconditional deterministic acquisition
theorem that outputs near-tight fields. Let G be a complete weighted graph
on an even vertex set S, with positive rational edge weights, and let Z(S)
be its perfect-matching partition. Define

  g_ij(S) = Z(S minus {i,j}) / Z(S).

Suppose a deterministic routine runs in time polynomial in the graph input
and 1/zeta, and on every residual S with Z(S)>0 and every 0<zeta<1 returns
positive rational ambient row fields tau_i and, for each vertex i, a witness
neighbor j_i such that

  g_ij(S) <= tau_i^2 tau_j^2 for every j,
  g_i,j_i(S) >= (1-zeta) tau_i^2 tau_j_i^2.

Run it recursively. At the current S choose its least vertex i, take the
reported j_i, set q=tau_i^2 tau_j_i^2, and recurse on S minus {i,j_i}. The
lower bound ensures every residual partition stays positive. Telescoping gives

  Z(S) = product_t 1/g_i_t,j_t(S_t),
  Y = product_t 1/q_t,
  (1-zeta)^(n/2) Z(S) <= Y <= Z(S).

Setting zeta=epsilon/n yields (1-epsilon)Z(S)<=Y<=Z(S) by Bernoulli's
inequality. The routine plus this recursion is a deterministic FPTAS for the
weighted perfect-matching partition, using polynomially many calls and
polynomial-bit rational products. This reduction uses the exact coordinate-
pair subfamily of determinant-weighted parity. It does not prove that such a
routine is impossible; it shows that acquiring near-tight fields
deterministically is already a substantial approximate-counting capability.

The same benchmark covers sparse unweighted graphs if the routine requires
strictly positive complete weights. Replace each missing edge by rational
weight eta=epsilon/(4 n^(n/2)) and each present edge by weight one. If G has a
perfect matching, its count is at least one; the total contribution of all
matchings using a missing edge is at most eta (n-1)!! <= epsilon/4 times its
count. The padded complete graph has polynomial input bit length and its
weighted count approximates the sparse count. A deterministic FPTAS for the
positive-complete case would therefore give one for general perfect-matchings
counting.

This near-tight routine is stronger than the fixed-window balance needed for
the imported capacity theorem. The rational update itself is not the barrier
in the normalized regime used above. Assume G_ij<=3, start from v_i=2, and assume each true row maximum is
at least gamma. If g_ij<=G_ij<=g_ij+delta, the one-pass proof gives an
unrounded tight-edge ratio at least 1-4 delta/gamma^2. Use rational
beta=1+zeta/256 and choose the least integer m_i with beta^(2m_i)>=v_i;
set tau_i=beta^m_i and q_i=tau_i^2. Thus q_i rounds v_i upward, and a tight
pair denominator grows by less than beta^4<=1+zeta/32. With
delta=gamma^2*zeta/16, the resulting ratio is at least
(1-zeta/4)/(1+zeta/32)>1-zeta. The integer exponents and rational powers
have polynomial bit cost in 1/zeta and the input length. For the initial
coordinate-dominated F instance, the same conclusion follows by setting the
contamination budget to zeta/8, choosing the uniform q_i above the known
upper bound, and using the same grid. This is a representation/rounding
claim conditional on the normalized estimates; it does not bound the cost of
acquiring those estimates. The obstacle is later hole-ratio acquisition, not
representing accurate fields once estimates or a partition oracle are given.

7. THE FIXED-WINDOW CAPACITY GATE IS TOO WEAK FOR DETERMINISTIC COUNTING

The near-tightness requirement above is not cosmetic. On the coordinate-pair
subfamily for the unweighted complete graph K_n, every even residual S of
size r has

  Z(S)=(r-1)!!,     g_ij(S)=1/(r-1).

Write d=r-1 and let tau_star=sqrt(2)*d^(-1/4), so tau_star^4=4/d.
Choose b=ceil(log2(4 sqrt(d))) and the dyadic rational

  tau = 2^-b floor(2^b tau_star),

where the floor is computed by an integer fourth root. Since
2^-b<=1/(4 sqrt(d))<=tau_star/4, one has
(3/4)tau_star<=tau<=tau_star. Therefore

  81/(64d) <= tau^4 <= 4/d,
  1/4 <= g_ij(S)/tau^4 <= 64/81 < 1.

Using this same rational external row scale tau on every row of each
residual gives a valid [1/4,1] fixed-window balance certificate, with
O(log n) bits per scale. The pair denominator is tau_i^2 tau_j^2=tau^4.

Now recursively select any pair, and use q=tau^4 as the partition-ratio
estimate at that residual. The exact recurrence is Z(S)=Z(S-ij)/g_ij(S).
The resulting product Y=product 1/q obeys

  Y/Z(K_n) = product_{t=1}^{n/2} g_ij(S_t)/q_t
             <= (64/81)^(n/2).

Thus even perfectly valid rational external fields that balance every
residual to the imported [1/4,1] window can yield exponentially poor
deterministic count estimates. The example is not a lower bound against
other estimators; it shows why the weak capacity window and the near-tight
(1-zeta) window are materially different acquisition targets. Near-tightness
with zeta=epsilon/n avoids this loss and, by the previous reduction, would
already imply a deterministic FPTAS for perfect-matchings counting.

The coordinate construction is an exact embedded instance: assign each
edge-pair (e_i,e_j) unit activity, so every matching minor has determinant
plus or minus one and the paired-basis partition is exactly (r-1)!!.
No appeal to sampling, floating point, or determinant cancellation is used.
