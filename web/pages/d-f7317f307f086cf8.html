# A binary-weight rank-one support interface, and why it does not compress general arrows

7 October 2026; metric_geometry10, cycle18, root-verified GPT-6.1 Sol/max. No nested workers. This is a precise deduction from the elementary rank-one closure equations and the established submodular-minimization prerequisite. It is not a new general quiver optimizer, and no submodular-minimization implementation has been executed here. Historical novelty is UNKNOWN; the main construction is very close to Iwamasa–Oki–Soma's existing Theorem5.6.

## R1. Input and exact target

Let K be any field with an exact arithmetic interface. A finite quiver may have cycles and loops. Its arrows are given as dense matrices, vertex dimensions are positive integers, and every nonzero arrow has rank one. Write each such arrow as u_e f_e, with a nonzero target column u_e and a nonzero source row f_e. Zero arrows are removed. Signed integer weights sigma_i are encoded in binary. Isolated vertices are handled separately: their support is max(sigma_i,0)alpha_i and their largest optimizer is the full space for sigma_i>=0, or zero otherwise. These components have short descriptors even if an isolated dimension is large and encoded in binary; printing their full bases must be charged separately. On the active vertices, A=sum alpha_i is at most twice the total dense arrow-entry count, so polynomial-in-A costs are polynomial in this expanded dense input. The target is

    h_V(sigma) = max_W sum_i sigma_i dim W_i,

and, optionally, the unique largest maximizing subrepresentation. No balancing condition sigma(alpha)=0 is imposed. The following combinatorial reduction is field-independent and makes no appeal to geometric invariant theory over a finite field.

## R2. A finite graph and a submodular objective

For each arrow make two separate tokens, f_e at its tail and u_e at its head; identical vectors on distinct arrows retain distinct tokens. Make a graph D on these 2E tokens with the edges

    f_e → u_e,
    u_e → f_g whenever head(e)=tail(g) and f_g(u_e) != 0.

A subset X is called forward closed when every edge beginning in X also ends in X. This is the convention called a lower set in the source. These subsets form a ring family under union and intersection, even if D has cycles.

At vertex i define

    L_i(X) = span{u_e : head(e)=i and u_e in X},
    H_i(X) = intersection{ker f_g : tail(g)=i and f_g not in X}.

An empty span is zero and an empty intersection is the entire vertex space. Forward closure ensures L_i(X)⊆H_i(X), because a selected incoming vector cannot pair nontrivially with an unselected outgoing row.

Write sigma_i^+=max(sigma_i,0), sigma_i^-=max(−sigma_i,0), and C=sum_i sigma_i^+ alpha_i. Define the integer set function

    F(X) = sum_i [ sigma_i^+ rank{f_g : tail(g)=i, f_g not in X}
                  + sigma_i^- rank{u_e : head(e)=i, u_e in X} ].

Each term is a matroid rank, or a matroid rank applied to a complement. Both are submodular; multiplication by a nonnegative scalar and summation preserve submodularity. F is defined on all token subsets, has nonnegative values, and has no weight-copy expansion.

## R3. Exact support equality and largest-optimizer acquisition

For a forward-closed X put

    W_i(X) = H_i(X) if sigma_i>=0, and L_i(X) otherwise.

These spaces form a subrepresentation. If the source W_tail is not contained in ker f_e, then f_e must be selected: otherwise W_tail⊆H_tail⊆ker f_e. The edge f_e→u_e then selects u_e, and u_e∈L_head⊆W_head. Thus the original arrow closure is satisfied. Its score is exactly C−F(X).

Conversely, for any subrepresentation W select u_e when u_e∈W_head and select f_e when W_tail is not contained in ker f_e. Original arrow closure and the pairing implication show that the resulting X(W) is forward closed. Moreover

    L_i(X(W))⊆W_i⊆H_i(X(W)).

Positive coefficients favor the right endpoint and negative coefficients favor the left endpoint, so C−F(X(W)) is at least the score of W. Consequently

    h_V(sigma) = C − min_{X forward closed} F(X).

The proof uses no acyclicity and no balancing condition. It follows directly from the original containment equations over K.

Let X* be the unique largest minimizer of F over the ring family. Its existence follows from submodularity: unions and intersections of minimizers are minimizers. For any optimal W, X(W) minimizes F; otherwise W(X(W)) would score above the maximum. At every vertex with a nonzero weight, equality of scores forces W_i(X(W))=W_i. At a zero-weight vertex, W_i(X(W))=H_i(X(W)) contains W_i. Both L_i and H_i grow with X. Therefore W(X*) contains every optimal W and is the unique largest optimizer.

One can find X* by minimizing

    F_tilt(X) = (2E+1) F(X) − |X|

over the same ring family. The factor ensures strict priority of the integer F value; maximizing cardinality among minimizers selects the unique largest one. This adds only O(log E) bits to the scalar oracle values.

## R4. Charged finite-set minimization interface

The source's §5 uses strongly polynomial submodular minimization on a ring family. An entirely explicit reduction to ordinary submodular minimization is available here. Let s=2E, let R=sum_i |sigma_i| alpha_i, and set

    M=(s+1)R+s+1.

Minimize over all subsets of the s-token set the function

    G(X)=(s+1)F(X)−|X| + M·number{(a,b) in edges(D) : a in X, b not in X}.

The directed-cut count is submodular. Every nonclosed X has G(X)>0 because F>=0 and −|X|>=−s. A closed X=empty has G(empty)=(s+1)F(empty)≤(s+1)R. More precisely, a nonclosed X has G(X)≥M−s>(s+1)R, so it cannot minimize. Thus the ordinary minimizer is closed and gives X*. All function values have O(log(R+1)+log(s+1)+log(|edges(D)|+1)) bits. Numerical weights are handled by integer arithmetic in a value oracle, not by repeated bases or copies.

Graph construction uses E original-token edges and, conservatively, O(E²) potential local pairings. After factorization, a value-oracle call performs exact ranks of submatrices formed from original rows/columns and the scalar weighted sum, plus a scan of D's edges. A coarse dense Gaussian bound is O(A E² + E³) field operations per call, where A is the total active dimension; smaller vertexwise bounds are available. Rank-one factorization also scans each original dense arrow and verifies its rank; this preliminary cost is polynomial in the dense input. Over a caller-certified Fp the field part is polynomial bit work in the dense data and log p; the scalar values have polynomial bit length in log(1+max|sigma_i|), A and E. Over Q a rank-one factorization can be obtained by choosing a nonzero entry; its rational heights and all fixed-submatrix ranks have polynomial input-bit bounds by ordinary determinant estimates. The returned active H_i and L_i also involve only kernels or spans of subsets of fixed input vectors, so their explicit rational output heights are polynomial. Isolated full-space output is descriptor-valued or charged separately as in R1. This class has no analogue of the alternating-meet height obstruction.

For integer Q input with entry height H, the scalar output height can be charged more explicitly. Choose a nonzero arrow entry A_ab; its b-th column u and a-th row g have integer height H, and A=u g/A_ab. The nonzero scalar 1/A_ab does not affect the closure implication, so the signature/rank construction can use the integer u and g. A kernel of at most E such rows has pivot minors of size r≤A and determinant height at most r(H+log2(r)/2)+O(1) by Hadamard's inequality. Cramer's rule bounds its rational basis coordinates by the same polynomial scale. Spans can select input columns or use the same minor bound. Hence active optimizer coordinates have O(A(H+log(A+1))) scalar bits, independent of numerical weights. For rational input, clear each entire arrow by one denominator and charge the resulting height. Over Fp choose a nonzero pivot in the actual reduced arrow rather than assuming an old Q pivot survives.

An established SFM algorithm, with this explicit integer evaluation oracle and its own error/cost interface retained, therefore gives a binary-weight support algorithm and largest optimizer for the rank-one class. This is a theorem-level composition; an executable SFM backend has not been acquired here. ## R5. No finite rank-one-arrow gadget realizes a free identity inclusion over an infinite field

Here is a precise obstruction to transferring this service by decomposing every general arrow into a rank-one gadget. Consider two boundary vertices with spaces K² and restrict their selected subspaces to lines. Suppose a finite gadget with arbitrary finite-dimensional auxiliary vertices and only rank-at-most-one arrows were to realize exactly the relation U⊆W between these two boundary lines, after existentially choosing all auxiliary subspaces. On lines, this relation is equality.

For every boundary vertex, collect the finitely many exceptional lines consisting of kernels of nonzero outgoing rows and spans of nonzero incoming columns. Loops contribute both kinds. Over infinite K there are at least two distinct lines outside this finite set. For any such generic line the local rank-one signature is identical: every nonzero outgoing row is nonzero on it, and it contains no incoming nonzero target column.

By the claimed identity relation, a generic diagonal pair (U,U) has a feasible auxiliary extension. Replace its two boundary lines by any other generic lines U' and W', keeping all auxiliary subspaces unchanged. Every rank-one-arrow closure condition still has the same truth value: the condition is solely 'source row annihilates the source subspace, or target contains the arrow column.' Boundary signatures and dimensions have not changed. Hence the extension remains feasible even when U'!=W', contradicting exact identity inclusion. If scalar dimension weights are present, the same replacement preserves the objective. Thus an optimization gadget that is supposed to allow and couple all generic diagonal lines cannot repair this failure by vertex weights alone.

There is also a charged finite-field version, communicated by hessian_check12 and independently reconstructed here. Over Fp, let s be the number of distinct exceptional lines in the union of the two boundary lists, under the boundary identification K²=K². If s<p, then P1(Fp), which has p+1 lines, contains at least two common generic lines. The same diagonal-to-crossed replacement gives the contradiction. Therefore an exact gadget realizing all free boundary identity inclusions over Fp requires at least p distinct boundary exceptions and hence at least p nonzero incident rank-one arrow **ports** (an arrow between boundaries or a boundary loop may contribute two ports). This entails size Omega(p), rather than polynomial in log p, under this literal boundary interface. It is not a lower bound for a differently encoded support oracle.

The argument rules out a fixed finite rank-one-arrow gadget with unchanged line-valued boundary interfaces over an infinite field, and polynomial-in-log-p gadgets of this form over unbounded prime fields. It does **not** rule out a different representation encoding, a direct matrix-space cost-scaling algorithm, or finite-field constructions of size depending numerically on p. It does not imply a hardness result for generic quiver support. The literal two-projection split of I2 already fails at U=W=span(1,1): I2 preserves that line while either coordinate projection does not.
