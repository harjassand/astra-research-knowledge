## 53BP1 measurement context

- Schultz LB, Chehab NH, Malikzay A, Halazonetis TD. “P53 Binding Protein 1 (53bp1) Is an Early Participant in the Cellular Response to DNA Double-Strand Breaks.” *J Cell Biol* 151(7):1381–1390 (2000), DOI [10.1083/jcb.151.7.1381](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.151.7.1381). The primary paper reports 53BP1 foci after DSB-inducing exposures, with peak focus counts approximating induced DSB counts and declining with repair kinetics in that irradiated-cell context. This supports foci as a useful damage-response proxy; it does not establish a one-to-one lesion count in 2C-like mitosis or under DDR inhibitors.
- Baldock RA et al. “ATM Localization and Heterochromatin Repair Depend on Direct Interaction of the 53BP1-BRCT2 Domain with γH2AX.” *Cell Reports* 13 (2015), DOI [10.1016/j.celrep.2015.10.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2015.10.074). Primary study shows 53BP1 binding/recruitment relationships with γH2AX and effects on repair in heterochromatin, motivating orthogonal lesion measurements when perturbing DDR pathways.
- Ferrand J et al. “Mitotic chromatin marking governs the segregation of DNA damage.” *Nature Communications* 16, 746 (2025), DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-56090-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56090-8). Primary study directly measures UV-induced CPDs on sister chromatids and in early-G1 daughter pairs; it reports approximately 15% average daughter CPD bias and negligible repair during the first hours. This preempts the generic “measure physical damage segregation rather than only a DDR focus” proposal, leaving only the specific spontaneous 2C-like mESC/fate setting open.
- Lezaja A et al. “RPA shields inherited DNA lesions for post-mitotic DNA synthesis.” *Nature Communications* 12, 3827 (2021), DOI [10.1038/s41467-021-23806-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23806-5). Primary live imaging shows postmitotic RPA foci arise before, rarely colocalize with, and persist longer than 53BP1 foci, marking a distinct inherited lesion class. This precludes interpreting a 53BP1 trace as total damage without a validated measurement model.
- Panagopoulos A et al. “Multigenerational cell tracking of DNA replication and heritable DNA damage.” *Nature* 642, 785–795 (2025), DOI [10.1038/s41586-025-08986-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08986-0). Its public processed-data boundary and the 3x2 source-data reanalysis are documented in [time_lapse_data_audit.md](time_lapse_data_audit.md).
