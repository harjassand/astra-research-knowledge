REVISION 01 — quantitative family-size obstruction to the obvious CP-range lift
Worker c05_l06 | 2026-10-07 | INITIAL remains immutable

Finding

The cycle3 CP-range argument gives, for every pair of Hermitian contractions,

  ||[Phi^m(A),Phi^m(C)]||_(2,tau)
      <= delta_m := 2/sqrt(m+1)+4/(m+1),

uniformly over dimension and trace weights. This does not itself give a single
commuting approximation to the whole image, nor an EB map approximation.

I checked the current primary source for dimension-independent normalized-HS
joint diagonalization: Bansil--Kachkovskiy, arXiv:2608.31000v1 (31 Aug 2026),
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31000 . Its Theorem 1.1 gives a dimension-free
bound for a pair of self-adjoint contractions. The paper explicitly says that
this stronger pair theorem cannot be iterated directly to larger tuples.
Theorem 1.3, for one anchor X and N further contractions Y_j, gives

  sum_(j=0)^N ||X_j-X'_j||_(2,d)^2
    <= 24 sqrt(N) (sum_(j=1)^N ||[X,Y_j]||_(2,d)^2)^(1/3),

where the rounded Y'_j commute with the rounded X', but are not asserted to
commute with one another. Corollary 1.4 then yields pairwise commuting
approximants for an N-tuple with a bound C(N) max_ij ||[X_i,X_j]||^(2/3^(N-1));
the exponent and constant depend on N.

For a d-dimensional full matrix algebra, a real basis of the Hermitian
operator space has N=d^2 elements. Substituting the CP-range estimate into
any basiswise common-diagonalization route therefore leaves a growing tuple
parameter, and the published tuple bounds do not produce a dimension-free
error. Even if a tuple were rounded, the EB target is a single CP map close
on every contraction in the input ball; it is stronger than separately
rounding selected outputs. This explains a concrete quantifier break in the
tempting inference “uniform pair commutators imply a uniform EB lift.”

This is a failure of that proof route, not a counterexample to beta_m -> 0.
The special CP-range structure of Phi^m may still permit a better lift. The
load-bearing open question remains a map-level approximation, uniform in
dimension, or an explicit sequence disproving it. There is no hidden family
factor in delta_m itself; the factor enters only when one tries to turn a
pairwise estimate into a common approximation of a growing-dimensional
operator space.

Interpretation boundary

The matrix-algebra dimension d^2 count applies to a basis of the entire
Hermitian operator space, not necessarily the actual linear range of Phi^m,
whose dimension may be smaller. The tuple theorem only rounds a finite list;
it does not produce a CP approximation, preserve positivity/trace conditions,
or yield measure-and-prepare form. Thus it identifies a missing uniformity
step but does not establish impossibility.
