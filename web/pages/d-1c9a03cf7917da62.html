ADVERSARIAL RECEIPT: FREE RADIUS COEFFICIENT COLLECTION
2026-10-08

Verdict

Proposition 5.1's estimate is supported by its stated tensorized Haagerup
inequality and the exact relative-operation fibres. With the coefficient
convention P_A = sum_(s,t) a_(s,t) lambda(s^(-1)t), the mixed-fibre
coefficient really is a_(s,t)+a_(t^(-1),s^(-1)), and the displayed factor
sqrt(2) is valid. The C_r^2=16r^2-7r number is an upper-bound constant from
Cauchy–Schwarz; it is not proved to be the exact finite-(r,d) radius.
I found no counterexample or coefficient-counting defect.

Interface and source

Let S_j={g_(j,i)^(+/-1):1<=i<=d}, S the disjoint union of these sets,
K=2rd, and Gamma=product_(j=1)^r F_d^(j). For A=(a_(s,t)) indexed by S,
A=A*, Tr(A)=0, ||A||_HS=1, the operator audited is

  P_A = sum_(s,t in S) a_(s,t) lambda(s^(-1)t).

This matches the cycle4 finite polynomial because pi(s^(-1)t)=W_s^*W_t.
The convention also makes P_A self-adjoint: take the adjoint and reindex,
using a_(s,t)=conj(a_(t,s)).
The source is Zhen et al., arXiv:2609.18747v1 (16 Sep 2026), Lemma 3.3
(tensorized Haagerup) and Proposition 5.1, especially (58)–(62):
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18747v1
The norm estimates below use Lemma 3.3 as stated there:
||lambda(f)|| <= product_j (ell_j+1)||f||_2 for support of exact multidegree
(ell_1,...,ell_r).

Exact sector proof

Partition ordered coefficient pairs into diagonal pairs, same-factor
off-diagonal sectors, and mixed sectors indexed by unordered factor pairs.
Define

  x_j^2 = sum_(s,t in S_j, s!=t) |a_(s,t)|^2,
  y_jl^2 = sum_(s,t in (S_j x S_l) union (S_l x S_j)) |a_(s,t)|^2.

These sets are disjoint and cover all off-diagonal pairs, so
sum_j x_j^2 + sum_(j<l)y_jl^2 = sum_(s!=t)|a_(s,t)|^2 <= 1.
The diagonal contribution is (sum_s a_(s,s))lambda(e)=Tr(A)lambda(e)=0.
No diagonal Hilbert–Schmidt mass is incorrectly charged to x or y.

Same factor. For s,t in S_j with s!=t, s^(-1)t is a reduced two-letter
word: cancellation would require t=s. Unique reduced-word parsing recovers
both letters and hence the ordered pair (s,t). Thus the coefficient map is
injective, its collected l2 norm is exactly x_j, and its support has
multidegree 2e_j. Lemma 3.3 gives ||Q_j||<=3x_j.
This includes the inverse-letter case t=s^(-1): the word is still reduced
(two copies of the same oriented letter), and the preimage remains unique.

Mixed factors. Fix j<l and take s in S_j, t in S_l. The relative operation
g=s^(-1)t has nonidentity coordinates g_j=s^(-1), g_l=t. Since the
coordinates commute, the second and only other preimage is
(t^(-1),s^(-1)), because (t^(-1))^(-1)s^(-1)=t s^(-1)=s^(-1)t.
Conversely, the two coordinates of g recover s and t, so there are no more
preimages. This is the exact two-element fibre (one orientation from each
of S_j x S_l and S_l x S_j), not a same-factor reversal. Therefore the
collected coefficient is

  c_g = a_(s,t) + a_(t^(-1),s^(-1)).

Each ordered mixed pair belongs to exactly one such fibre. Hence

  sum_g |c_g|^2
    <= 2 sum_g (|a_(s,t)|^2+|a_(t^(-1),s^(-1))|^2)
    = 2 y_jl^2.

The l2 norm is at most sqrt(2)y_jl. Every word has multidegree
e_j+e_l, so Lemma 3.3 gives ||Q_jl||<=4sqrt(2)y_jl. Hermiticity imposes
additional relations among a's, but the estimate does not need them; in
particular it does not mistakenly identify a_(t^(-1),s^(-1)) with
conj(a_(s,t)).

The diagonal term vanishes and the sector decomposition is exact. Triangle
inequality and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz now give

  ||P_A|| <= 3 sum_j x_j + 4sqrt(2) sum_(j<l)y_jl
    <= sqrt(9r + 32 binom(r,2))
       sqrt(sum_j x_j^2 + sum_(j<l)y_jl^2)
    <= sqrt(9r + 32 binom(r,2))
     = sqrt(16r^2-7r).

The first Cauchy–Schwarz weight is 3 for each of r same-factor sectors;
the other weights are 4sqrt(2), one for each of binom(r,2) mixed sectors.
All arithmetic is exact. The proof would fail if y_jl counted only one
orientation, but the source's definition explicitly takes their union.

Finite parameter and exactness status

At cycle4 r=2,d=64, K=256, the result is Gamma_(2,64)^2<=50 (radius
<=sqrt(50)). Proposition 5.1 does not assert equality. The source's Theorem
5.2 provides the additional finite-d lower bound, with k=2d=128:

  Gamma_(2,64)^2 >= 2(3*128-4)^2/(128*127)
                     + 32(127/128)^2
                   = 3,203,583/65,024
                   = 49.26770115649607... .

Thus the source proves a squared-radius interval [49.2677...,50], not the
exact finite value. The upper endpoint could still be attained; the cited
bounds do not decide that. Theorem 5.2 proves convergence to 16r^2-7r as
d->infinity for fixed r, which is asymptotic sharpness, not finite-d
equality. The coarser displayed deficit bound 16r^2/d gives a squared-radius
deficit <=1 here; the explicit lower bound above tightens it to <=0.732299.

Status: internally reconstructed upper-bound proof; no coefficient
