- Local extracted text SHA-256: `10452c6b16f15495084fb9e45ab19f37f6f1ae9e00b127a60873078c39b21413`

Read depth: PDF pages 1–9 plus 12–13, with decisive construction/application
reconstructed from pages 4–9.

- Pages 1–2 state the exact interface: asymptotic arithmetic complexity over
  \(\mathbb C\), and emphasize that the theorem does not specify a competitive
  finite matrix size. The mechanism combines tensor degenerations, characters,
  and polynomial multiplication.
- Proposition 3.1, pages 4–5, gives a finite Fourier/weight degeneration for
  separating blocks that share one tensor leg while the other two legs carry
  matching disjoint sector labels. Corollary 3.2 extracts the entropy factor.
- Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, pages 7–8, derive discrete concavity and shifted tripling
  for a symmetrized polynomial-multiplication profile.
- Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 1.1, page 9, imply the 9/4 exponent and then convert
  it to a recursive exact rank algorithm: for fixed \(\epsilon\), choose a
  fixed base size \(u\) and a rank decomposition of length below
  \(u^{9/4+\epsilon}\); recurse and pad arbitrary sizes.
- Remark 5.2, page 9, says recursive coefficients can be represented in a
  number field if algebraic coefficients are used. This audit does not rely on
  rational descent. More generally, a nonempty rank-decomposition variety
  defined over \(\mathbb Q\) has a point over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), so a
  finite number-field representation is enough for exact integer inputs.

The selective-mask construction here uses only the resulting exact square
matrix-multiplication circuit as a tile primitive. It does not claim that
Proposition 3.1 itself computes arbitrary selected output coordinates.

## Primary selective-output prior

Josh Alman and Virginia Vassilevska Williams, “Truly Subquadratic 3SUM and
Truly Subcubic APSP via Triangles in Sparse Lopsided Graphs,” arXiv:2610.06783v1,
October 5, 2026:

- Abstract and manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06783
- HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06783v1

Read the abstract and introduction, especially Theorem 1 / lines corresponding
to HTML paragraphs 157–166. It gives an arbitrary-mask thin-product algorithm
when \(D\le N^{1/18}\), and a more general arbitrary-mask regime for every
\(D\le N^\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon<0.1204\). Its ten-multiplication
Schönhage identity prunes output-local computation. The target aspect ratio
\(D=N^{1/4}\) is outside that guarantee; this source is a comparator and is
not a premise in the structured theorem.
