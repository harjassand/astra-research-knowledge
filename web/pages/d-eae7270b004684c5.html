# Equilibrated flux certifies continuum derivative and secant transfer

Independent proof, 2026-10-06. Domain Ω is the square (more generally a bounded connected Lipschitz domain). V=H¹(Ω)/R; use any fixed linear gauge such as zero mean in Ω. Gauge choice does not change energies or meanfree current/voltage pairings. Conductivity σθ=σ₀+∑θ_jκ_j lies between positive a and finite b. κ_j are bounded; the frozen model has two disjoint piecewise constant nonradial regions. Boundary loads g∈L²(∂Ω) have integral zero. A Lipschitz-domain trace theorem makes ℓ_g(v)=∫∂Ωg v bounded on V, and Lax–Milgram supplies the weak solution u_g. No H² regularity is assumed or used.

## Exact equilibrated energy estimator

Let u_h,g∈V_h⊂V be its conforming Galerkin solution. Let q_g∈H(div;Ω) satisfy div q_g=0 distributionally and normal trace q_g·n=g in H^−1/2(∂Ω). The H(div) Green identity gives ∫Ωq_g·∇v=ℓ_g(v) for every v∈H¹. Thus, with e=u_g−u_h,g,
∫Ωσθ∇e·∇v=∫Ω(q_g−σθ∇u_h,g)·∇v.
Set v=e and apply weighted Cauchy–Schwarz to obtain
||e||σθ≤r_g(θ):=||σθ^−1/2(q_g−σθ∇u_h,g)||L²,
where ||w||σθ²=∫σθ|∇w|². The estimator constant is exactly one. Galerkin orthogonality is unnecessary for this inequality, although used below for parameter variation.

A discontinuous approximate flux or merely approximate boundary trace is insufficient: a residual boundary/load term must then be explicitly bounded. Only the exactly feasible flux allows the displayed constant-one estimator.

## Constructive BDM1 feasibility on the square

For the positively oriented boundary take tangent t=(-n_y,n_x). If ψ is continuous piecewise quadratic and q=(∂yψ,−∂xψ), then q·n=∇ψ·t=dψ/ds. Integrate the frozen continuous piecewise linear g along the boundary to prescribe ψ; ∫g=0 makes this quadratic boundary primitive single-valued. Assign those boundary vertex and edge-midpoint values to a continuous P2 function on the conforming triangle mesh; choose any interior values. On each triangle q is affine, div q=0, and on an interior edge the shared polynomial trace ψ makes its tangential derivative identical, hence its q-normal trace continuous in a fixed orientation. Therefore q∈BDM1∩H(div), is exactly divergence-free, and has the exact P1 normal boundary load. All statements are polynomial identities. No regularity of the PDE solution enters.

Equivalent edge-normal BDM1 construction: each triangle has six coefficients and six affine normal-trace DOFs (two per edge); these uniquely determine the affine vector field because vanishing normal on all three edges forces an affine field to vanish. Its divergence is constant, so zero integrated boundary flux is equivalent to zero divergence. Assign shared edge DOFs in a common orientation. Prescribed boundary DOFs and per-cell integrated flux conservation are feasible on a connected mesh because interior-edge integrated fluxes solve the connected dual-graph incidence equations, whose only compatibility is total prescribed boundary flux zero. Slope DOFs are independent of integrated flux conservation. These slope DOFs can be set freely after satisfying means. Unnormalized rational edge normals avoid unnecessary irrational diagonal-edge scaling, provided the line element and normal conversion are tracked consistently.

Minimizing r_g² over exactly feasible BDM1 fluxes is a convex quadratic problem. A numerical minimizer is not necessary: any exactly verified feasible q gives a valid upper bound. Rational coefficients, exact polynomial cell integration for piecewise constant σ, and rational upper bounds on square roots produce a rigorous certificate. Approximate floating KKT feasibility must be repaired or bounded; small residual alone is not exact equilibration. RT1 is also valid, but its divergence is P1 and must be constrained coefficientwise; BDM1 needs only its constant-divergence constraint.

## Differentiability and entrywise continuum transfer

Since σθ depends affinely on θ and is uniformly coercive, the inverse variational operator is continuously differentiable in operator norm, with derivative −Aθ^−1 A_v Aθ^−1. For conductivity direction v define κ_v=∑v_jκ_j. Reciprocity gives the exact scalar response derivative
D F_g,h(θ)[v]=−∫Ωκ_v∇u_g·∇u_h.
The conforming response F^h_g,h=ℓ_h(u_h,g) has the identical derivative formula with Galerkin solutions. Set U_g=||u_h,g||σθ, e_g=||u_g−u_h,g||σθ. Expanding u_g=u_h,g+error_g and u_h=u_h,h+error_h yields
|D F_g,h−D F^h_g,h|≤μ_v(θ)[e_g U_h+e_h U_g+e_g e_h],
μ_v(θ)=esssupΩ |κ_v|/σθ.
Consequently if ||v||₂=1 and Kκ=esssup sqrt(∑κ_j²), then μ_v≤Kκ/a. For disjoint indicator regions Kκ=1, hence μ_v≤1/a; for θ∈[-.05,.05]² and σ₀=1, a=.95. This is a derivative estimate rather than a forward discrepancy estimate.

For finite output tests g₁,…,g_D and input tests h₁,…,h_N, certified entry bounds Δ_lk give ||D F−D F^h||op≤||Δ||F. This conservative Frobenius bound is rigorous even when the entry error signs are unknown. For fixed physical input currents f_i and weighted outputs one may bound each selected column directly; retain the same frozen input and detector transformations. If input covariance Q has trace one, ||(D F−D F^h)Q^1/2||F≤||D F−D F^h||op. No additional output-rank factor is needed once an operator norm is certified; acquisition still charges every detector scalar.

## Uniform parameter coverage using fixed feasible center fluxes

At a center c compute conforming u_h,g(c), energy U_g,c and exact feasible q_g,c, with residual r_g,c. For θ in a covering box C_c require
τ_c≥esssup |σθ−σc|/σc, τ_c<1.
Then (1−τ_c)σc≤σθ≤(1+τ_c)σc. The load is unchanged. Subtracting Galerkin equations gives, with w=u_h,g(θ)−u_h,g(c),
aθ(w,z)=−∫(σθ−σc)∇u_h,g(c)·∇z.
Taking z=w and comparing energies yields ||w||σc≤τ_c U_g,c/(1−τ_c).
Writing q_c−σθ∇u_h(θ)=(q_c−σc∇u_h(c))−(σθ−σc)∇u_h(c)−σθ∇w gives
r_g(θ;q_c)≤[r_g,c+τ_c U_g,c+(1+τ_c)τ_c U_g,c/(1−τ_c)]/sqrt(1−τ_c)
=[r_g,c+2τ_c U_g,c/(1−τ_c)]/sqrt(1−τ_c)=:ē_g,c.
This holds with the same exactly feasible q_c throughout the covering box, because boundary loads and divergence constraints are parameter independent.

A sharper energy bound follows from the Galerkin dual norm. U_g(θ)=||ℓ_g||_{(V_h,aθ)*}≤U_g,c/sqrt(1−τ_c)=:Ū_g,c, since u_h,g(c) is the Riesz representative of the fixed load on V_h with metric ac. This proof does not substitute an estimated continuum load norm. Use ē_g,c and Ū_g,c in the derivative entry inequality, with certified lower conductivity a_c in each covering box. Take the maximum across a finite cover of the entire parameter box. All mesh/box edges are included; finite center tests alone without these covering remainder bounds are not uniform evidence.

For σ₀=1 and disjoint indicator coefficients, a box with coordinate radius h has τ≤h/min σc. A single reference center at zero and global radius .05 gives τ=.05, while a finer center grid reduces τ proportionally. The fixed-flux continuity term is first order in covering radius and may be conservative; if its transfer bound consumes the finite-element lower margin, the certificate is inconclusive and must not be called a continuum proof.

## Uniform derivatives imply global normalized-secants transfer

Let T be the fixed physical design and output transformation. Suppose the above procedure certifies ||T(D F(θ)−D F^h(θ))[v]||≤δ for every θ in the convex parameter box and every unit v. For θ≠η integrate along their segment:
||T[(F(θ)−F(η))−(F^h(θ)−F^h(η))]||/||θ−η||≤δ.
Therefore a rigorous frozen-design finite-element global lower secant constant c_h transfers to the continuum with c_cont≥c_h−δ. The strict test c_h>δ is decisive. Forward error bounds alone cannot establish this because dividing a fixed forward discrepancy by arbitrarily small parameter separation diverges.

## Audit checkpoints and costs

Verify exact meanfree boundary loads, consistent orientation/line elements, conformity/gauge, coefficient region alignment, exact flux-divergence and normal trace, cellwise residual integration with outward rounding, and a complete parameter cover. Keep coefficient/linear-solve rounding in r, U and c_h. The frozen raw loads may be combined after certification only with explicitly bounded transformation matrices; fluxes and errors transform linearly, but their norm bounds must be propagated. No hidden H², elliptic regularity constant, or finite-grid-to-continuum leap appears.

Costs include every center Galerkin solve and flux optimization, exact feasibility repair/verification, residual integration, covering count and derivative-bound arithmetic. No extra online currents or outputs are introduced by this proof. It certifies the already frozen measurements if its margin is positive; it does not authorize redesign, expanding the SDP, or claiming continuum transfer before the numerical certificate is checked.

# Sharper sensitivity-product transfer (Lead deduction independently reconstructed)

The following argument confirms the proposed quadratic residual-product bound and clarifies its flux interface. Fix θ and basis direction κ_i. Let e_g=u_g−u_h,g. Galerkin orthogonality gives aθ(e_g,z_h)=0 for every conforming z_h.

For each frozen load h define the *auxiliary continuum sensitivity driven by the discrete state* w_{h,i}∈V through
aθ(w_{h,i},z)=b_i(u_h,h,z):=∫κ_i∇u_h,h·∇z for every z∈V.
Define w^FE_{h,i}∈V_h by the same equation restricted to V_h. It equals the positive inverse stiffness product Aθ,h^−1 A_i,h u_h,h. It is the negative discrete state derivative; it is not the continuum state derivative, because its RHS deliberately uses u_h,h. This distinction is necessary for the following identity.

Then b_i(e_g,u_h,h)=aθ(e_g,w_{h,i})=aθ(e_g,w_{h,i}−w^FE_{h,i}). Expanding the continuum response derivative difference as above yields
|D_i(F_g,h−F^h_g,h)|≤e_g e_{w_h,i}+e_h e_{w_g,i}+μ_i e_g e_h,
where e_{w_h,i}=||w_{h,i}−w^FE_{h,i}||σθ and μ_i=esssup|κ_i|/σθ. This is an exact estimate; there is no regularity assumption beyond bounded coefficients and conformity. It can be much sharper than the earlier state-error-times-state-energy bound, but is not automatically small without sensitivity residual certificates.

For the auxiliary sensitivity define p=σθ∇w_{h,i}−κ_i∇u_h,h. Its weak action is ∫p·∇z=0 for every z∈H¹, so p has divergence zero and zero normal trace in the weak H(div) sense. For any exactly feasible q∈H(div) with divq=0 and q·n=0,
∫σθ∇(w_{h,i}−w^FE_{h,i})·∇z=∫[κ_i∇u_h,h+q−σθ∇w^FE_{h,i}]·∇z.
Consequently
e_{w_h,i}≤||σθ^−1/2[q−(σθ∇w^FE_{h,i}−κ_i∇u_h,h)]||L²=:r_{w_h,i}.
The shift −κ_i∇u_h,h is essential; equilibrating σ∇w alone as a zero-load Neumann solution would certify the wrong problem. q=0 is always exactly feasible, although usually not optimal. BDM1 divergence-free zero-normal fluxes are feasible using a continuous P2 streamfunction constant on the boundary. RT0 is also admissible: its exactly divergence-free local fields are constant, and its edge normal continuity and zero boundary values supply feasibility; it may approximate less well than BDM1 but the estimator remains valid.

For d parameters combine component bounds rather than prematurely replacing them by a generic unit-direction coefficient bound. For each scalar pair define δ_i=e_g r_{w_h,i}+e_h r_{w_g,i}+μ_i e_g e_h. Then for any unit v, |D(F−Fh)[v]|≤sqrt(∑δ_i²). For a transformed multimeasurement design propagate the complete bounded derivative matrix through the frozen transformations; Frobenius norm is a safe upper bound. This may retain substantial directional improvements.

## Uniform continuation of sensitivity residuals from covering centers

It remains necessary to control r_w over all parameter boxes. Fix center c, use σc norm, let τ=sup|σθ−σc|/σc<1, μ_i,c=esssup|κ_i|/σc, U_h,c=||u_h,h(c)||σc, W_h,i,c=||w^FE_{h,i}(c)||σc. The earlier discrete state difference satisfies ||u_h(θ)−u_h(c)||σc≤τ U_h,c/(1−τ). Subtract auxiliary discrete sensitivity equations to obtain
||w^FE_i(θ)−w^FE_i(c)||σc≤[μ_i,c τ U_h,c/(1−τ)+τ W_h,i,c]/(1−τ)=:D_w.
Indeed the RHS contains b_i(u_h(θ)−u_h(c),z) and −(aθ−ac)(w_i(c),z); coercivity in the σc norm supplies the denominator.

Keep the center zero-boundary feasible q_w,c. Set p^FE_i(θ)=σθ∇w^FE_i(θ)−κ_i∇u_h(θ). Relative to the center,
||σc^−1/2[p^FE_i(θ)−p^FE_i(c)]||≤τ W_h,i,c+(1+τ)D_w+μ_i,c τ U_h,c/(1−τ).
Therefore the uniform sensitivity residual bound is
r_w(θ;q_w,c)≤[r_w,c+τ W_h,i,c+(1+τ)D_w+μ_i,c τ U_h,c/(1−τ)]/sqrt(1−τ).
Insert this alongside uniform e_g,e_h into the sensitivity-product derivative bound. This supplies a complete finite-cover-to-uniform-secant proof. It can be conservative for large covering boxes, and must be checked numerically against the frozen finite-element margin. The zero-normal flux is independent of θ; the shifted target p^FE_i varies with θ and is explicitly accounted for.
