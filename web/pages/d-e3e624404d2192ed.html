# Binary panels acquire unknown feedback structure: exact interface, sharp panel count, finite certificate

Status, 6 October 2026: complete written proof and executable diagnostics for a restricted experiment interface. No scientific priority, external validation, universal nonlinear discovery, or revolutionary breakthrough is claimed. Population reconstruction alone is not noisy-data acquisition; section 5 gives the latter conditional on a supplied Gaussian sampling model. The deliberately conservative executed certificate requires 268,431,360 simulated snapshots for a two-variable radius of .15. This is not a practical laboratory budget.

## 1. Chosen question and supplied/acquired boundary

Given n>=2 physically labelled variables, can an apparatus acquire the unknown coefficient matrix of a linear feedback system from fewer environments than singleton interventions, without knowing the local actuator gains or the baseline confounding covariance?

The supplied family is the equilibrium SEM

    A x = epsilon + u,  A=I-B, diag(A)=1, A invertible.

The unknown A may be dense or cyclic; no faithfulness, cycle-product bound, graph support, causal order, or signs are supplied. Baseline epsilon has unknown finite covariance Omega and invariant distribution. It may be correlated across coordinates. Correlated exogenous influences are allowed; arbitrary unobserved mediators or nonlinear hidden-state dynamics are not thereby identified. The graph being learned is exactly the nonzero off-diagonal pattern of this closed, labelled equilibrium representation, not a uniquely reconstructed microscopic latent graph.

The apparatus supplies binary on/off masks c_r in {0,1}^n. Its unlogged random local inputs obey

    Cov(u_r)=diag(d_i c_ri), d_i>0 unknown and fixed across environments,
    Cov(u_r,epsilon)=0.

For an off actuator the input is zero; when on it has the same gain d_i regardless of other enabled actuators. Zero mean is convenient but covariance centering allows fixed unknown means. The direct-local intervention law, independence/uncorrelatedness, fixed gain, unchanged A, known target labels, and legal simultaneous masks are supplied physical premises. They have NOT been acquired from these covariance observations. Section 7 gives an exact counterfeit if actuator locality is removed. Baseline and each panel provide fresh equilibrium observations of all n coordinates, but no realized input draws. Population access supplies exact covariances; the finite implementation obtains sample covariances from independent Gaussian draws.

Independent invariant additive sensor noise contributes the same covariance to all environments and cancels in differences, so it does not change the population recovery of B. Its independence and invariance remain supplied. It would prevent separate recovery of Omega from the baseline without sensor calibration.

Acquired outputs: B, local variance gains d_i, and, when noise calibration allows it, Omega. Finite output is an estimated B with a confidence radius or UNKNOWN. Exact finite-data zero/nonzero edge decisions require an additional minimum-nonzero-coefficient promise; absent that promise the radius yields only confident nonzero edges and unresolved small coefficients.

Costs: n actuator channels, known local targeting/masks, calibration or physical justification of locality and stable gains, preparation/reset/mixing to produce independent equilibrium specimens, n sensors per specimen, q simultaneous panels plus baseline, all snapshots and covariance arithmetic, code-design search, O(n^3+qn^2) reconstruction after covariance acquisition, and O(n^2) retained learned-matrix memory. Keeping all environment covariance summaries, as in the implementation, uses O(qn^2) memory; accumulating only the S,F sufficient summaries can reduce that workspace to O(n^2). A panel typically enables O(n) actuators: a reduction in environment changes is NOT a reduction in number of actuator channels or a proved total sample/resource saving. Covariance accumulation from N snapshots costs O(Nn^2). No free stationary preparation, hidden gain oracle, or microscopic graph oracle is used in the learner.

## 2. Constructive theorem: baseline plus ceil(log2(n+1)) panels

**Theorem.** Under the above family, q=ceil(log2(n+1)) fixed binary panels suffice to identify B and d uniquely from their population covariance matrices. Among fixed nonadaptive binary-mask designs whose gains and invariant baseline covariance are unknown, this panel count is necessary for uniform identification when n>=2. The population claim applies to arbitrary invertible A with diagonal one. If physical stationary preparation instead requires rho(B)<1, the construction and lower-bound witnesses also apply inside that stable subclass.

Choose n distinct nonzero q-bit columns c_i. The ordinary binary representations of integers 1,...,n work. Let w_i=sum_r c_ri, L=A^-1, and covariance differences

    Delta_r = Sigma_r-Sigma_0 = L diag(d_i c_ri) L^T.
    S=sum_r Delta_r=L diag(d_i w_i)L^T >0.

Take W=S^-1/2 and O=W L diag(sqrt(d_i w_i)). Then OO^T=I, hence O is orthogonal. For each r,

    W Delta_r W = O diag(c_ri/w_i) O^T.

The known normalized signatures v_i=c_i/w_i are pairwise distinct. Indeed distinct nonzero binary codewords have different supports, and normalization does not alter supports. Thus the joint eigenspaces are exactly the labelled one-dimensional columns of O. This recovers O up to independent column signs, with NO unknown permutation.

For a convenient single eigendecomposition, choose alpha in [-1,1]^q with distinct known z_i=alpha^T v_i and form

    F=sum_r alpha_r Delta_r,
    T=W F W=O diag(z_i) O^T.

The exceptional choices of alpha are finitely many hyperplanes. This need not be an uncharged real-valued genericity promise: test finite rational candidate vectors, compute their signatures exactly, and accept only a positive measured separation. One constructive deterministic choice is alpha_r=t^r/t^(q-1), t=q+2. If two weighted code polynomials agreed, the nonzero integer polynomial with coefficients of absolute value <=q would vanish at t; its highest coefficient has magnitude >=1, while the sum of lower coefficients has magnitude <t^degree because q/(t-1)<1, a contradiction. This deterministic fallback can have a poor gap. The executable instead measures and rejects small gaps of pseudorandom finite-precision candidates; n=2,3 uses alpha=(-1,1), known to separate those codes. No structure data choose alpha.

A probabilistic design bound clarifies the search: for any two signatures one differing coordinate has absolute difference >=1/q. Condition on the other coordinates of a continuous uniform alpha. Then P(|z_i-z_j|<a)<=aq. A union bound gives bad-gap probability <=n(n-1)aq/2. Consequently a=1/(4qn^2) is passed with probability >7/8; finite-bit choices still require their actual measured gap, not this continuous bound.

After labeling O's columns by z_i, let G=S^1/2 O and H=G^-1. For signed s_i=+/-sqrt(d_i w_i),

    G=L diag(s_i), H=diag(1/s_i) A.

Since A_ii=1, H_ii=1/s_i!=0. Normalize row i:

    A_ij=H_ij/H_ii, B=I-A, d_i=1/(w_i H_ii^2).

The inverse-row normalization is essential. Normalizing a column of L by L_ii would fail on some cyclic systems with L_ii=0. Here no such assumption is needed. Independent audit supplied the exact stable witness B=[[0,1,0],[-1,0,1],[0,1,0]]: B^3=0 and diag(I+B+B^2)=(0,1,2), so L_11=0 while the inverse-row procedure is valid. The unknown gains are estimated from H rather than calibrated numerically in advance. The q masks and labels supply the disentangling intervention semantics. If a physical actuator is written u_i=g_i z_i, covariance identifies d_i=g_i^2 and does not identify the sign of g_i. No signed actuator-amplitude recovery is claimed.

**Uniqueness:** any other compatible A',d' produces the same S,T. Distinct known z_i force the same labelled O up to signs. Therefore its normalized H is the same A and the displayed d_i agree. This explicitly solves the permutation freedom that BACKSHIFT handles by a cycle-product restriction.

## 3. Sharp lower bound with full Gaussian transcript twins

At most 2^q-1 distinct nonzero q-bit column codes exist. If n>2^q-1, either some node has the zero code or two nodes have the same nonzero code. Neither supports uniform identification. The following examples show equality of ALL Gaussian environment laws, not merely moments, and both models can be stable.

**Zero code:** let node j never receive input. Compare B0=0 to B1=t e_k e_j^T, k!=j, t!=0. B1 is a DAG and B1^2=0, L1=I+t e_k e_j^T. Every active actuator has i!=j and L1 e_i=e_i. Thus every covariance difference is identical with the same gains. Give model 0 baseline covariance I, and model 1 error covariance Omega1=(I-B1)(I-B1)^T. Then their baseline observed covariances are both I. Centered Gaussian noises and inputs make every available environment law exactly equal, despite the hidden directed edge.

**Duplicate nonzero codes:** on the duplicated two-node block compare B0=0 to

    B1=[[0,t],[-t,0]], 0<|t|<1.

Set original error covariance and equal actuator gains to I and 1. Since A1 A1^T=(1+t^2)I, choose new error covariance and both new gains (1+t^2)I and 1+t^2. Then L1 Omega1 L1^T=I and L1 diag(d') L1^T=I. The two actuators are simultaneously on or off in every recorded panel, so every observed Gaussian environment is the same as model 0. The changed feedback is stable since rho(B1)=|t|<1. Embed this block with independent untouched coordinates to cover any n. Thus q>=ceil(log2(n+1)) is necessary.

These are fixed-design uniform lower bounds. They do not establish lower bounds for adaptive intervention selection, real-valued amplitude ratios, logged input commands, higher-moment/non-Gaussian access, or a known sparsity/DAG/noise class. For n=1, B=0 is supplied by diagonal normalization and no panel is needed to learn B; do not apply the n>=2 formula to that trivial case.

## 4. Feedback beyond BACKSHIFT's cycle-product premise

An exact three-node fixture is

    B=[[0,2,2],[1,0,0],[-1,0,0]].

B^3=0 exactly, hence rho(B)=0 and the ordinary iteration reaches equilibrium in finitely many steps. Two intersecting length-two cycles have absolute product 2. Thus CP(B)=2>1. Binary additive-noise panels leave B fixed and remain stable, and our reconstruction identifies it. This does not contradict BACKSHIFT: its stated cycle-product restriction is used to resolve unknown intervention permutation, and its discussion of stability under interventions additionally considers surgical blocking of cycles. Those are different interventions from our invariant additive-noise panels. If the downstream task requires hard-blocking stability, this fixture is not promised stable under every such operation.

## 5. A finite-data, observed-quantity error certificate

Let ||Sigma_hat_r-Sigma_r||op<=eps_r hold simultaneously, including baseline r=0. From observed differences construct S_hat,F_hat,T_hat,G_hat,H_hat as above, matching eigenvalues in increasing order to the sorted known signatures. Define

    eS=sum_r(eps_0+eps_r),
    eF=sum_r |alpha_r|(eps_0+eps_r),
    ell=lambda_min(S_hat), a=ell-eS.

Return UNKNOWN if a<=0. Otherwise let

    eroot=eS/(sqrt(ell)+sqrt(a)),
    eW=eS/[sqrt(ell)*sqrt(a)*(sqrt(ell)+sqrt(a))],
    eT=eF/ell+(||F_hat||op+eF)*eW*(1/sqrt(ell)+1/sqrt(a)).

The first bound follows from the Sylvester equation for the difference of SPD square roots: if P=sqrt(S_hat),Q=sqrt(S), then P(P-Q)+(P-Q)Q=S_hat-S, and the integral solution gives ||P-Q||<=eS/(lambda_min(P)+lambda_min(Q)). Inverting P,Q gives the displayed inverse-root bound. The T bound follows by adding/subtracting W_hat F W_hat and using operator submultiplicativity. All quantities on its right are observed or input confidence radii.

Let gamma=min_{i!=j}|z_i-z_j|, known from the frozen code design. Return UNKNOWN unless eT<gamma/2. Weyl then matches the sorted labels uniquely and |lambda_hat_i-z_i|<=eT. Project a unit eigenvector of T_hat onto the other true eigenspaces. The eigenvalue equation implies

    sin(angle)<=eT/(gamma-eT).

To see this directly, for every k!=i, (z_k-lambda_hat_i)<u_k,u_hat_i>=<u_k,(T-T_hat)u_hat_i>. The left denominators have absolute value at least gamma-eT, and the squared right coefficients sum to at most eT^2. Align signs. Unit-vector distance is at most sqrt(2)sin(angle). Therefore

    eO=sqrt(2n)*eT/(gamma-eT),
    eG=eroot+sqrt(||S_hat||op+eS)*eO

bounds ||G_hat-G||op (using Frobenius to bound the orthogonal-matrix error). If hnorm=||H_hat||op satisfies hnorm*eG<1, the inverse perturbation identity yields

    eH=hnorm^2*eG/(1-hnorm*eG) >= ||H_hat-H||op.

Otherwise return UNKNOWN. Let b_i=|H_hat_ii|. Return UNKNOWN if any b_i<=eH. The true row diagonal is then bounded away from zero. Decomposing the difference of normalized rows gives

    row_error_i <= eH/b_i
      +(||H_hat_i,:||2+eH)*eH/[b_i*(b_i-eH)].

The square root of the sum of these squared row bounds is a certified Frobenius radius for both A and B. Column sign choices cancel in the estimate; the analysis chooses matching true signs. No unknown coefficient norm, unknown actuator gain, supplied inverse gap, or truth-selected tuning parameter enters this certificate. Calibration/model errors are NOT included unless translated into valid covariance residual radii; an accurate data estimate cannot rescue an invalid intervention model.

For iid Gaussian observations in each environment, N draws yield m=N-1 centered covariance degrees of freedom. An independently reconstructed chi-square Chernoff/net argument gives, with

    x=n log9+log(2/delta), rho=4sqrt(x/m)+4x/m,
    ||Sigma_hat-Sigma||op<=rho||Sigma||op

with probability at least 1-delta. Proof: the standardized quadratic form is chi-square_m/m. Its upper centered mgf is <=exp[m lambda^2/(1-2lambda)] and lower centered mgf <=exp[m lambda^2] for 0<lambda<1/2. Chernoff gives upper deviation 2sqrt(x/m)+2x/m and lower deviation 2sqrt(x/m), probability at most 2e^-x combined. A 1/4-sphere net has at most 9^n points, and symmetric operator norm is <=2 times maximum absolute net quadratic form. Rescale from standardized covariance by Sigma^1/2. If rho<1, triangle inequality acquires

    eps=rho||Sigma_hat||op/(1-rho).

Use per-setting stage budgets delta/[(q+1)t(t+1)]. Their total is at most delta over all stages/settings. At fixed staged fresh-sample sizes, stop only when the B radius is at most the declared tolerance; otherwise retain UNKNOWN or a larger certified radius. The union event supports such stopping. For every fixed valid system and gamma>0, these radii vanish as stage sizes grow sufficiently quickly. This is pointwise finite acquisition, with no uniform cap over arbitrarily small d or ill-conditioned A. Unknown exact edges cannot be determined from a nonzero radius without a coefficient-separation promise.

## 7. Decisive calibration obstruction and downstream use

Unknown actuator cross-talk destroys the interpretation even with every possible panel. Take actual B=0, local command variance diag(d c), but physical injection matrix M=(I-C)^-1 for any zero-diagonal C with I-C invertible. Then x=M z+epsilon. A purported model with B=C and local injection z, whose baseline error is adjusted to reproduce the same baseline covariance, has precisely the same panel response M diag(d c) M^T for every mask. Conversely the structure learner attributes physical actuator mixing to causal propagation. This is an exact all-panels gauge, not finite-data weakness. In the family above direct locality is supplied; we have not solved how to acquire it without an additional sensor/intervention interface. Logging a command vector alone also gives L M, so it does not separate uncalibrated actuator mixing.

Concrete use: an apparatus with trustworthy independent local noise switches, expensive environment changes, all-coordinate readout, and unlogged input fluctuations could acquire feedback coefficients and unknown gains using logarithmically many prepared environments even under invariant confounding and cancellation. This could support planning soft input responses in controlled linear circuits or biochemical linearization experiments. It does not supply the trustworthy local switches, linearity, equilibrium preparation, or nonlinear representation. These load-bearing experimental premises are the bottleneck for broader foundational structure discovery.

Next falsifiable steps: (1) add outward-rounded arithmetic for the executed confidence certificate; independent mathematical reconstruction of the population theorem, lower bound and finite certificate has passed in the r09 audit; (2) nearest-prior-art audit for exact binary-code panel theorem and known-label cycle-bound removal before any novelty claim; (3) same-total-shot n>=4 pooled-vs-singleton covariance comparison with actuator work and reset cost charged; (4) if a real apparatus logs commands, switch to the one-panel comparator; (5) independently measure actuator cross-talk, and test withheld masks and forcing intensities for model rejection. No finite fit certifies absence of hidden mixing or nonlinear mechanism changes.

## Root selection decision

Deprioritize absent an actual covariance-only apparatus with expensive resets, reliable local switches and unlogged stochastic inputs. Known relative analog variance levels need only a positive global panel and a distinct-ratio panel, plus baseline; unknown fixed gains cancel from the generalized eigenvalues. The on/off restriction is therefore load-bearing. The programme has no empirical evidence that establishing binary direct locality/stable gains costs less than calibrating the analog ratios, nor a same-total-shot advantage. The present exact construction is an incremental heteroskedastic-identification specialization with a sharp interface boundary, not a priority foundational discovery.
