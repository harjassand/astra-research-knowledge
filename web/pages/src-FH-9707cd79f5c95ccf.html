# Static-matched spectral substitution: audit and design correction

**Audit date:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** scoped independent reconstruction and countermodel; no experiment, target-material prediction, external proof review, historical-priority finding or technology demonstration.

## Finding

The proposal contains a sound, useful static-matching idea, but its eight-layer Thue–Morse construction is stronger than the mechanical task requires. For an actual flip pair, isotope inventory is conserved by reversal. Exact matching of the classical laminate (A,B,D) matrices requires only zero centered first moment of the isotope stiffness profile. A non-palindromic seven-layer pair meets this condition, uses a 3:4 internal isotope ratio, and retains about 94.8% of the eight-layer vacuum-model optical contrast at the same 80 nm internal thickness. It has four unlike-isotope interfaces including the identical 5 nm caps, versus six in the eight-layer reference. No exact seven-layer stack has been fabricated or tested; its contrast remains a model scenario.

The central scientific gap remains. The source computes an optical evanescent reflection proxy for a 90 nm hBN stack in vacuum. It does not model κ-ET's complex, anisotropic, low-temperature substrate response, calculate a target susceptibility, or predict a Meissner signal. The reported near-resonance contrast cannot supply those missing quantities. Exact static equality holds in an ideal mass-scaled dielectric model; published endpoint parameters yield a small but nonzero static residual. Physical static matching and the target response therefore remain measurements to acquire.

The electromagnetic calculation uses familiar layered-medium transfer matrices and the imaginary part of the planar reflection coefficient. Its near-field proxy `I(z, omega) = integral q^2 exp(-2qz) Im(r_p) dq` is standard Green-response theory, not a new Casimir law or a superconducting interaction calculation ([Chance, Prock and Silbey, 1978](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9780470142561.ch1); [Lifshitz, 1956](https://jetp.ras.ru/cgi-bin/e/index/e/2/1/p73?a=list)). The potentially distinct element is the experimental operation—flipping a spectrally different yet mechanically balanced isotope stack—not the transfer matrix, fluctuation–dissipation relation, Thue–Morse sequence or isotope heterostructuring. Priority for that constrained operation remains unknown.

## Exact construction and scope

For N equal-thickness layers, Q_j = Q_H + (Q_L - Q_H) chi_j, and layer centers z_j = d(j - (N-1)/2), reversal preserves A_el = integral Q dz and D_el = integral z^2 Q dz, while it reverses B_el = integral z Q dz. Therefore a stack and its geometric reverse have identical (A,B,D) for any fixed isotope-specific stiffnesses exactly when

\[
\sum_j\left(j-\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\chi_j=0.
\]

For seven internal layers, h10 positions {0,4,5} give `L H H H L L H`; the reverse is `H L L H H H L`. In doubled centered coordinates the moment is -6 + 2 + 4 = 0. Identical 5 nm h¹⁰BN caps preserve the equality. Under uniform temperature and fixed isotope-specific Q alpha, the thermal force and moment resultants also match. The conditions do not cover local strain fields, interface slip, adhesion, wrinkles, twist domains, edge contamination, nonlinear/dynamic mechanics or spatial nuclear-spin effects; isotope inventory matching does not erase the isotope spatial distribution.

An exact subset-sum argument shows that every zero-centered-first-moment binary word with N <= 6 is palindromic, so seven is the minimum internal layer count for a nontrivial equal-thickness flip pair under this one mechanical criterion. If 50:50 composition inside each laminate is added, the first nontrivial flip pair appears at eight layers. The source's complement/Prouhet condition—matching isotope moments of degrees 0, 1 and 2 between A and B—is stronger still. `DERIVED_RESULTS.md` gives the proof and keeps these design classes separate.

## Independent calculations

The separate global amplitude transfer-matrix implementation in `analysis/independent_checks.py` does not import producer optical code. It reproduces the eight-layer reference points and independently calculates the seven-layer candidate. A separate cross-implementation check compares it with the unchanged producer recursion across the complete 121-row frequency CSV and 801 static-wavevector points; the largest relative complex-reflection difference is below 3.0e-14 in the frequency grid and 3.9e-15 in the static sweep. The producer's finite-check tolerances and grid ranges are listed in `replay/replay_receipt.md`. For an 80 nm internal stack, 5 nm h¹⁰BN caps, a 20 nm witness height, and the source Lorentz inputs in vacuum:

| Design/model | Nominal 1470 cm⁻¹ witness | Sixfold damping | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Source 8-layer 50:50 pair | -13.295% | -13.086% | Reproduced reference calculation |
| 7-layer h10 positions {0,4,5} pair, d = 80/7 nm | -12.600% | -12.595% | New mirror-matched model scenario |

These are differences divided by the pair mean, exactly as in the source. They are not predicted target changes. Two passive, synthetic oscillator spectral weights centered at 1470 and 1572 cm⁻¹ give opposite signs when integrated against the ideal pair's delta Im(r_p) at fixed q=51 μm⁻¹, despite identical ideal static reflection. This establishes a precise identifiability limit: static equality plus a local optical witness does not determine a target-weighted response when the target's frequency weighting is unknown. The oscillator weights are mathematical countermodels, not κ-ET parameters.

To expose the omitted substrate boundary, the independent code also replaces the *terminal* vacuum with synthetic, real, nondispersive isotropic permittivities from 1 to 20. For these fixtures, the source design remains between -13.295% and -13.261%, and the seven-layer design between -12.600% and -12.566%. Ideal static reflection remains equal to roundoff at all five synthetic loads. These cases show only behavior under simple model loads; they cannot stand in for measured, anisotropic, complex, temperature-dependent κ-ET data. No material values were invented for κ-ET.

Local one-at-a-time \(±1%) mathematical probes show the largest witness sensitivity to the assumed h¹¹BN in-plane LO and TO endpoints (up to about 2.7 percentage points of contrast from these probes); this is not an uncertainty interval. The proposal has no joint h¹¹BN damping, high-frequency permittivity or normal-axis fit. Thickness/cap probes are likewise not fabrication tolerances. The full parameter table is preserved in `analysis/independent_results.json`.

The ideal mass-scaled model has equal static response to floating-point roundoff. The published-endpoint reference leaves a maximum relative static-reflection residual 1.91893e-5 over the tested grid. Equal ε(0) for every hBN layer makes order irrelevant at zero frequency even with a different terminal substrate, but actual isotope films require measurement because structural renormalization can alter their static tensor.

The Lorentz form with positive damping is the source model's causal linear response under its stated time convention. The validator checks nonnegative evanescent Im(rp) only on a sampled finite frequency/wavevector grid; it does not certify passivity over all bands or acquire a full measured dielectric. The source's q integral is truncated to 1.2e6–1e9 m⁻¹. Its imaginary-frequency comparison samples xi from 0.01 to 1e5 cm⁻¹ and q from 1e6 to 4e8 m⁻¹; there is no tail estimate or rigorous continuum supremum. In the unloaded symmetric vacuum geometry, reversing the stack reflects the boundary-value problem and preserves its mode spectrum, while exchanging the fields seen from the two faces. Thus the one-face reflection witness can differ even when the isolated mode spectrum agrees. A one-sided substrate load removes that symmetry and requires the full loaded stack to be measured or recomputed.

## What has actually been demonstrated

Keren et al. ([Nature, 25 February 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-10062-6)) reported s-SNOM evidence for hybridization of hBN hyperbolic modes with the 1470 cm⁻¹ C=C vibration in κ-ET, and MFM evidence of suppressed local Meissner response under hBN. Their representative sample used a 60 nm hBN crystal on an approximately 200 μm κ-ET crystal; they also compared hBN and RuCl₃ regions on the same sample. Their MFM data do not uniquely determine the depth profile. This is the achieved material baseline that motivates the proposal; it is not a measurement of the proposed isotope reversal.

Prior work has already demonstrated isotope-stack-dependent hBN polariton dispersions using ¹⁰B and ¹¹B layers ([Chen et al., Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40449-w)). Direct indentation work reported different mechanical properties for monolayer ¹⁰BN and ¹¹BN ([Falin et al., Nature Communications, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41148-2)), reinforcing that matched mechanics should be tested rather than presumed. Since the source proposal was written, Wang et al. ([Nature, published 19 August 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11037-x)) have also reported a split-ring-cavity NbSe₂ superconducting response, including a cavity-associated Tc increase and changes in critical current/field. These studies are not matched replacements for the proposed κ-ET intervention, and none proves the proposal's target response. They do make a broad “first cavity effect on superconductivity” framing untenable. The narrower spectral-substitution priority question is still open.

The strongest near-term same-target comparator is the existing hBN-versus-RuCl₃ and bare-surface κ-ET MFM comparison: it is experimentally simpler and already reports an effect, but it changes cover chemistry and mechanics. A reversible in-situ cavity-control experiment on a different quantum Hall material is a stronger comparator for same-sample reversibility ([Enkner et al., Nature, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08894-3)). A practical claim of advantage for the isotope laminate must compare the full transfer/fabrication, optical qualification, interface matching, cryogenic, probe, calibration and acquisition costs against the simplest adequate existing method. The source's eight-minutes-per-pair assumption and sigma_pair values are planning scenarios, not measured throughput. Wall-plug energy and full preparation cost are unmeasured.

The source's 2 pN/micrometre MFM difference-in-differences target and 0.4 pN/micrometre systematic budget are chosen gates. Its planning model gives 25 pairs (3.3 h at an assumed 8 min/pair) if sigma_pair is 2 pN/micrometre, and 625 pairs (83.3 h) if sigma_pair is 10 pN/micrometre. These exclude specimen fabrication, optical qualification, cooling, failed pairs and complete wall-plug energy. Neither sigma_pair nor eight-minute throughput is acquired data.

## Decision and decisive next measurement

The mathematically sound improvement is the seven-layer mirror construction. It is a more parsimonious candidate under the stated elastic model, with a small calculated optical-contrast reduction relative to the source word. Whether seven layers are physically easier to fabricate, adhere or qualify has not been measured, and the three-to-four isotope ratio could have practical constraints. Do not describe the pattern as validated or historically novel.

Before any superconductivity interpretation, the decisive qualification is a joint, uncertainty-bearing optical fit of both flipped orientations *on the intended κ-ET load*, including actual layer thicknesses, h¹¹BN anisotropic damping/endpoints, terminal target response, and withheld-frequency/momentum validation. Measure static reflection/capacitance, force–curvature response, contact potential, strain and interface variation on the same specimen family. Only then test the dark-equilibrium difference-in-differences MFM observable with a measured independent-pair variance and nuisance budget. The source's 2 pN/micrometre target and 0.4 pN/micrometre systematic limit are chosen experimental gates, not predictions.
