1. NORMALIZATION AND EXACT PRODUCT-KERNEL CALCULUS

Choose traceless Hermitian T_a, a=1,...,m=d^2-1, with
 Tr(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2.
Let J_a=sum_{i=1}^N T_a^{(i)}, J_B=sum_i B^{(i)}, and for a real symmetric C
 Q_C=sum_{a,b} C_ab (J_a J_b+J_b J_a)/2.
For a density matrix rho define
 k_N(rho)=rho^{tensor N}, x_a=Tr(rho T_a),
 v_T(rho)={T,rho}-2 Tr(rho T)rho, r_T(rho)=-i[T,rho].
Both fields have trace zero; v_I=r_I=0. Write V_a=v_{T_a}.grad,
R_a=r_{T_a}.grad and B_a=2N x_a+V_a. Direct tensor differentiation gives

 {J_a,k_N}=B_a k_N,       [J_a,k_N]=i R_a k_N.                           (1)

For a fixed axis T, the anticommutator/com-mutator identities imply
 (B_T^2-R_T^2)k_N=2(J_T^2 k_N+k_N J_T^2).
Diagonalizing symmetric C, or polarizing this identity, yields

 M_H k_N := (H k_N+k_N H)/2 = (L+V)k_N,
 H=epsilon Q_C+eta J_B.                                                (2)

This is an equality of FULL d^N-by-d^N matrices for every rho; it is not an
expectation, marginal, pressure, or low-body assertion. Explicit coefficients:

 D_C=sum_{a,b} C_ab(v_a tensor v_b-r_a tensor r_b),
 Lf = a.grad f + (epsilon/4) D_C : Hess f,
 T_{Cx}=sum_a (Cx)_a T_a, Z_C=sum C_ab {T_a,T_b}/2,
 a=epsilon[(N-1)v_{T_{Cx}}+(1/2)v_{Z_C}]+(eta/2)v_B,
 V=epsilon[N(N-1)x^T C x+N Tr(rho Z_C)] + eta N Tr(rho B)
   =Tr(H k_N).                                                        (3)

The SDE covariance is (epsilon/2)D_C. The drift simplification follows from
 Dv_T[v_T]-Dr_T[r_T]=2 v_{T^2}-4 Tr(rho T)v_T.
No derivative of C occurs because its coefficients are parameters, and C
is diagonalized only to verify the identity, not to define its computation.

2. GLOBAL ISOTROPIC PRINCIPAL POSITIVITY

Completeness of this normalized traceless basis says, for Hermitian Z,
 sum_a (Tr T_a Z)^2 = (1/2)[Tr Z^2-(Tr Z)^2/d].
For a Hermitian test direction Y put y=Tr(rho Y). The matrices
 {rho,Y}-2y rho and -i[rho,Y] are traceless. Therefore

 <Y,D_I Y>
  =(1/2)[Tr({rho,Y}-2y rho)^2-Tr(-i[rho,Y])^2]
  =2 Tr[rho(Y-yI)rho(Y-yI)] >=0.                                      (4)

The last trace is the squared HS norm of sqrt(rho)(Y-yI)sqrt(rho).
A global covariance factor is
 w_a=2[sqrt(rho)T_a sqrt(rho)-x_a rho],   D_I=sum_a w_a tensor w_a.        (5)
Indeed the completeness identity applied to sqrt(rho)(Y-yI)sqrt(rho),
which has zero trace, proves (5). Thus the negative rotation term in the
quadratic generator cancels globally for isotropic C; it need not be
discarded or assumed positive separately. Arbitrary anisotropic C can
fail positivity away from a full-rank region, so this result does not give
a global anisotropic SDE without localization.

Also sum_a T_a^2=c_d I, c_d=(d^2-1)/(2d), and
 T_x=(rho-I/d)/2. Thus for H=kappa Q_I+J_B, the exact globally real SDE is

 d rho = [kappa(N-1)(rho^2-Tr(rho^2)rho)
               +{B,rho}/2-Tr(rho B)rho]dt
          +sqrt(2kappa) sum_a
                 [sqrt(rho)T_a sqrt(rho)-x_a rho] dW_a.                 (6)

Its trace stays one. It has smooth locally Lipschitz coefficients on the
strictly positive density interior. Starting rho_0=I/d, it stays strictly
positive at every finite time, as proved next. For kappa=0, the same assertion
follows from the exact positive filtering ODE.

3. LOG-DETERMINANT IDENTITIES AND GLOBAL EXISTENCE

Let ell(rho)=log det rho on the interior. Since T_a and B are traceless,
 V_a ell=-2d x_a, R_a ell=0, and V_a x_b=Tr rho{T_a,T_b}-2x_a x_b.
Consequently the exact Markov generator and quadratic variation are

 L ell = -epsilon d[(2N-1)x^T C x+Tr(rho Z_C)]
                              -eta d Tr(rho B),
 Gamma_L(ell)= (epsilon/2)<grad ell,D_C grad ell>
             =2epsilon d^2 x^T C x.                                 (7)

For isotropic (6), epsilon=kappa,C=I,eta=1. Both drift and quadratic
variation are bounded on ALL positive trace-one rho: purity lies in [1/d,1]
and |Tr rho B|<=||B||. Thus, on successive stopping domains rho>=a I,
ell is its finite starting value plus bounded-time-integral drift plus a
continuous martingale of uniformly bounded quadratic variation. This process
cannot tend to -infinity at a finite time. Exiting the positive interior
would force det rho to zero and ell to -infinity. Local solutions therefore
extend for all finite times and remain positive, with no reflection/local time.
Boundedness of trace-one positive states also prevents explosion.

With w_t=exp(int_0^t V(rho_u)du), potential V is bounded by ||H|| because
k_N is a density. Ito, followed by expectation, proves
 d/dt E[w_t k_N(rho_t)] = M_H E[w_t k_N(rho_t)].
The finite-dimensional linear ODE is unique. Its start is I/d^N, so

 E[w_1 rho_1^{tensor N}] = exp(H)/d^N.                                 (8)

Normalize the strictly positive weighted endpoint law to obtain the exact
iid-product mixture in claim1. This law is explicitly specified by (6) and V;
no unknown reference/channel, pure ensemble, or heat seed is given for free.

4. COMPACT POSITIVITY FOR ANISOTROPY

On the real traceless Hermitian tangent space with HS metric, if rho>=e I,
then (4) implies D_I>=2 e^2 I: Tr(Y-yI)^2=Tr Y^2+d y^2 for Tr Y=0.
For ||A||<=M, write linear maps from coefficient vectors to v and r.
Completeness gives
 sum_a (Tr Y v_a)^2 <=8 ||Y||_HS^2,
 sum_a (Tr Y r_a)^2 <=2 ||Y||_HS^2.
Here ||rho||_HS,||rho||_op<=1, |Tr rhoY|<=||Y||_HS, and
||{rho,Y}-2y rho||_HS<=4||Y||_HS, ||[rho,Y]||_HS<=2||Y||_HS.
Hence

 |<Y,D_A Y>|<=10M ||Y||_HS^2.                                         (9)

Now C_N=beta I+N^{-delta}A_N and k=max(1,sqrt(10M/beta)). Choose

 e_N=k N^{-delta/2}.                                                  (10)

Then on rho>=e_N I,
 D_{C_N}>= [2beta k^2-10M]N^{-delta}I
          >= beta k^2 N^{-delta}I.                                   (11)
For N^delta>=2M/beta, also (beta/2)I<=C_N<=(3beta/2)I<=2beta I.
Thus C_N is positive and the compact principal covariance has a strictly
positive known margin. For each N, use the unique positive square root in
the HS tangent coordinates; it is smooth on this compact positive region.
There is no unverified eigenaxis choice or imaginary diffusion.

5. DIRECT STOPPED PROCESS, WITHOUT A SEED

Use the exact full generator (3) for epsilon=1/N, C=C_N, eta=1, B=B_N.
Start rho_0=I/d and stop at the first time tau when lambda_min(rho)=e_N.
Assume e_N<1/d. Freeze BOTH rho and its accumulated weight after tau;
this is stopping, not a reflecting boundary condition. On times before tau,
the real diffusion exists by (11). The endpoint is always a density and
therefore W_1=E[w_{1 wedge tau} rho_{1 wedge tau}^{tensor N}] is positive.
Let sigma_N=W_1/Tr W_1, an actual normalized iid-product mixture.

Let
 D0=3beta(d-1)+d B0, H0=2beta(d-1)/d+B0.
For N>=d+1, C_N<=2betaI and (7) give the uniform bounds

 L ell>=-D0,   Gamma_L(ell)<=2beta d(d-1)/N.                            (12)

Proof: ||x||^2=(Tr rho^2-1/d)/2 <=(d-1)/(2d). Thus xCx<=beta(d-1)/d.
Also 0<=Z_C<=2beta c_d I. The first drift term is at most 2beta(d-1),
the Z_C term at most beta(d^2-1)/N<=beta(d-1), and the field at most dB0.
The quadratic-variation bound follows from (7) and the same xCx bound.

Put
 L_N=(delta/2)log N-log k-d log d-D0.                                 (13)
On {tau<=1}, ell(rho_tau)<=log e_N because the other eigenvalues are <=1.
The initial ell is -d log d and the drift integral is >=-D0. Hence the
martingale part must reach <=-L_N. The exponential-martingale inequality
for a continuous stopped martingale of quadratic variation <=v/N proves,
when L_N>0,

 P(tau<=1)<=exp[-N L_N^2/(4beta d(d-1))].                              (14)

This controls reaching small determinant, and permits trajectories in any
ordered full-rank region. It does not assume a maximally mixed critical
phase or make a central-ball seed claim in d>=3.

6. EXACT STOPPING ERROR AND NORMALIZATION

Completeness also gives
 Q_I=N c_d I+sum_{i<j}(F_ij-I/d),
 so Q_I<=N(d-1)(N+d)/(2d) I.                                         (15)
C_N positive and <=2betaI implies Q_C<=2beta Q_I, by diagonalizing C
and summing positive J_a^2. Consequently

 ||K_N||<=h_N=beta(d-1)(N+d)/d+N B0 <=H0N    (N>=d).                  (16)

Both |V| and the trace-norm operator bound for M_K are <=h_N. Define
W_t with frozen stopped paths. Its exact differential defect is
 W'_t-M_K W_t=-E[1_{tau<=t} w_tau M_K k_N(rho_tau)].
The difference from G_t=e^{tM_K}(I/d^N) is therefore the exact Duhamel
integral

 G_1-W_1=int_0^1 e^{(1-t)M_K}
             E[1_{tau<=t}w_tau M_K k_N(rho_tau)]dt.                    (17)

There is no missing boundary-local-time term: after stopping both coordinates
and weight are constant. Since ||e^{uM_K}||_{1->1}<=e^{u h_N} and
w_tau<=e^{tau h_N}, tau<=t makes the integrand norm at most
h_N e^{h_N}P(tau<=t), rather than h_N e^{2h_N}. Thus

 ||exp(K_N)/d^N-W_1||_1 <=h_N e^{h_N}P(tau<=1).                        (18)

The exact target trace g=Tr exp(K_N)/d^N is >=1. Indeed scalar eigenvalue
Jensen gives g>=exp(Tr K_N/d^N); the mean field term is zero and the
quadratic mean is Tr Z_C/d>=0. For positive G,W with trace g,z, normalized
half trace distance is <=||G-W||_1/g: subtract with denominator g and use
|g-z|<=||G-W||_1. Combining (14)-(18) proves the nonasymptotic certificate

 T(exp(K_N)/Tr exp(K_N),sigma_N)
 <=min{1, h_N exp[h_N-N L_N^2/(4beta d(d-1))]}.                        (19)

All norms here are ordinary full trace norms on dimension d^N; there is
no hidden dimension prefactor or restriction to a finite number of marginals.

7. EXPLICIT THRESHOLDS AND RATE

The conditions for (19) are
 N>=d+1, N^delta>=2M/beta, e_N<1/d, and L_N>0.
All are decidable scalar inequalities in known fixed bounds.
Put C0=log k+d log d+D0. If additionally
 log N>=4 C0/delta,
 (log N)^2>=128 beta d(d-1) H0/delta^2,
then L_N>=(delta/4)log N. Substitution in (19), with h_N<=H0N, proves
(20). Thus an explicit sufficient N0 is any integer at least
 max{d+1,(2M/beta)^{1/delta},
     exp(4 C0/delta),
     exp(sqrt(128 beta d(d-1)H0)/delta)}.
The bound is mathematically explicit but often enormous. It may be useless
at practical N. The full certificate (19) records that limitation instead
of hiding it behind the asymptotic notation.

For the original c07 interface, d=2, beta=2, delta=1/2 and
B_N=b.sigma/(2N^{3/4}), this improves the asymptotic rate to
exp[-cN(log N)^2] while retaining the known-parameter whole-density interface.
Using B0 as a bound on this decreasing field is conservative. A tighter
N-dependent field estimate improves constants but is unnecessary for the
asserted rate. If A_N=0, claim1 already gives exact separability for every N.

The same localization argument covers a supplied anisotropy E_N tending to
zero at any known rate: C_N=beta I+E_N, choose e_N>=sqrt(10||E_N||/beta)
with e_N->0. Replace (delta/2)log N-log k in (13) by log(1/e_N).
The error is h_N exp[h_N-N(log(1/e_N)-dlogd-D0)^2/(4beta d(d-1))].
Unknown convergence rates do not give an acquired numeric N0 for free.
