## The family and theorem

Work over any characteristic-zero field. Write z=(x,y), v=(a,b,c), u=1+xy, and

    g(z)=(u³, 3xu², −x³),       A(z,v)=g(z)·v.

Let λ≠0 be a scalar, let B(z,v) be **any polynomial of degree at most one in v**, with arbitrary polynomial z coefficients, and let C₂(z,v) be any polynomial of total degree at most two. Set

    Φ(z,v)=λ A(z,v)²+B(z,v)+C₂(z,v).

(The base-only part is included in B.) If a constant symmetric5×5 matrix K satisfies

    trace(K Hess Φ) = a constant polynomial,

then **K=0**. Thus the positive-degree coefficient matrices of HessΦ span all Sym₅. No nonzero constant direction w has constant D_w²Φ, including zero. The property persists under invertible affine changes of source variables and under arbitrary quadratic perturbation.

This theorem makes no assertion that an arbitrary member of the family has constant Hessian determinant or a noninjective gradient. It applies to the published Ψ=A²+11A+2B₀, and also to the other scalar Schur-family parameters. Unlike one earlier sufficient minor proportional to λ¹⁰ν⁵, the theorem does not require the coefficient ν of the published B₀ to be nonzero.

## Proof by dual-variable degree

Decompose K into its2×2 base block Kzz,2×3 cross block E, and3×3 dual block Kvv. Remove the constant contraction with HessC₂ from the right side. Inspect the polynomial identity by degree in v.

**Degree two forces Kzz=0.** The coefficient of c² comes from λx⁶. Its contraction is30λ Kxx x⁴, so Kxx=0. The coefficient of a² comes from λu⁶. With Kxx=0 its contraction is

    2λ Kxy(6u⁵+30xyu⁴)+30λ Kyy x²u⁴.

At x=0 this gives12λKxy=0. Then Kyy=0. B is at most linear in v and cannot contribute at this stage.

**Degree one forces E=0.** Write the two rows of E as ex,ey and put

    P=ex·g,   Q=ey·g,   R=P_x+Q_y,
    D=P∂x+Q∂y.

After Kzz=0, the coefficient of v_i in the contraction is4λ(Dg_i+Rg_i); hence Dg_i+Rg_i=0 for all i. B's mixed base/dual derivatives are independent of v, so it does not affect these equations. In the fraction field, subtraction gives

    D(g₂/g₁)=0,       g₂/g₁=3x/u,
    ∂x(x/u)=1/u²,    ∂y(x/u)=−x²/u².

Consequently P=x²Q. The substitution k[x,u]→k[x,y], u↦1+xy is injective: on x≠0 its inverse is y=(u−1)/x. Viewed in independent variables x,u, both P and Q are homogeneous of degree3. The identity equates degrees3 and5, so P=Q=0. The three components of g are distinct monomials in x,u, hence their linear independence gives ex=ey=0.

**Degree zero forces Kvv=0.** Only

    2λ gᵀ Kvv g

remains; B's dual Hessian is zero. In independent x,u, its six symmetric monomials are

    u⁶, xu⁵, x³u³, x²u⁴, x⁴u², x⁶,

with nonzero numerical factors. They are distinct and homogeneous of degree6, and thus cannot form a nonzero constant or cancel each other. Every entry of Kvv vanishes. This proves the theorem.

For an affine source change T with invertible linear part L, a constant contraction transforms by K↦LKLᵀ. Its nonzero status is preserved. The quadratic perturbation was already absorbed at the start. For a directional derivative take K=wwᵀ; over a characteristic-zero field w≠0 implies wwᵀ≠0, including complex isotropic w.
