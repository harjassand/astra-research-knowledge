## 1. Complete derivatives and the preserved correction

With \(M=W^{\circ2}\), along \(W\exp(tA)\), \(A^T=-A\), put
\(V=WA\), \(Z=WA^2\), \(DM=2W\circ V\),
\(DDM=2V^{\circ2}+2W\circ Z\). Then
\[
F''=\operatorname{tr}(G K_A),\quad
K_A=2DM\,DM^T+M\,DDM^T+DDM\,M^T.
\]
Equivalently, with \(S=W^T[W\circ(GM)]\),
\[
F''=8\operatorname{tr}[(W\circ V)^TG(W\circ V)]
+4\sum_{ij}(GM)_{ij}V_{ij}^2+4\operatorname{tr}(S A^2).
\]
All three terms are essential. My first message to root/r16 omitted the middle Euclidean Hessian term and gave a wrong individual mixed-direction coefficient. That early formula is withdrawn, not a theorem. Its corrected version appears below and is independently replayed. The source aggregate subsequently supplied by r16 exposed the error; the corrected calculation agrees with that independently reconstructed aggregate. Communication history preserves the failed derivation.

The trace over the standard skew basis \(E_{ij}-E_{ji}\) is, for every \(W\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{\rm skew}F''
=4[\mathbf1^TG\mathbf1+2\operatorname{tr}G-(n+2)F_G(W)].
\]
For a column polynomial \(f(x)=(x^{\circ2})^TGx^{\circ2}\), the ambient Laplacian is
\(4\mathbf1^TGx^{\circ2}+8\operatorname{diag}(G)^Tx^{\circ2}\). Its sphere Laplacian subtracts \(4(n+2)f\). Summing columns uses \(\sum_km_k=\mathbf1\), proving the formula. Thus a strictly positive trace is a sufficient full-Hessian escape test. Writing the gap as \(\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}P_{ij}\), any hypothetical nonglobal local maximum must satisfy
\(\text{gap}\le\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}/(n+2)\). This is only a necessary bound on traps.

## 2. Householder family: universal aggregate, no stationarity needed

Let \(W=I-2\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/n\), \(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/n\), and
\[
A_i=(W^Te_i)e_i^T-e_i(W^Te_i)^T
=\tfrac2n(e_i\mathbf1^T-\mathbf1e_i^T).
\]
For every symmetric \(G\) and \(n\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{\quad\sum_{i=1}^n F_G''(W;A_i)
=\frac{64(3n-8)}{n^3}\operatorname{tr}(PG).\quad}
\]
This is the r16 aggregate, independently verified directly from the complete coefficient derivative above for \(n=3,\ldots,10\). It excludes a local maximum at any such \(W\) for every \(G\succ0\): the right side is strictly positive, so at least one feasible direction has positive curvature. No stationarity premise or sampled weight coverage is needed for the identity.

Independent universal reconstruction: the sum's linear coefficient in \(G\) is invariant under every simultaneous permutation because \(W\) is permutation-invariant and \(A_i\) are permuted. Any such symmetric coefficient is \(aI+b\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\). Constant-objective gauge changes \(G\mapsto G+\mathbf1v^T+v\mathbf1^T\) leave every derivative unchanged, hence the coefficient annihilates \(\mathbf1\) and equals \(aP\). To find \(a\), evaluate at \(G=I\). Put
\[
c=4/n^2,\quad m=(n-2)^2/n^2,\quad t=(n-4)/n,\quad
s=m^2+(n-1)c^2,\quad C_{ii}=t^3(1-1/n)+1/n.
\]
Direct unweighted differentiation for this pivot gives
\(F_I''(A_i)=4[3C_{ii}+3m-4m^2-2s]\).
Substitution simplifies its bracket to
\(16(3n-8)(n-1)/n^4\). Summing \(n\) identical terms and dividing by \(\operatorname{tr}P=n-1\) gives the boxed coefficient. The pivot evaluation agrees with Jin2026 v2 equation8; the symmetry/gauge extension to arbitrary \(G\) and direct derivative replay do not assume a weighted transfer of Jin's theorem. r06 independently derived the aggregate by a separate entrywise expansion.

For a sharper individual check, gauge-center \(G_0=PGP\), \(e=\mathbf1/\sqrt n\). For \(n\ne4\), Householder stationarity is equivalent to \(\operatorname{diag}(G_0)=\gamma\mathbf1\). Indeed \(M=c\mathbf1\mathbf1^T+tI\); the symmetric first term in \(S\) drops out, and the remaining condition is that \(-2G_0/n+\operatorname{diag}(G_0)\) commute with the all-ones matrix. At a stationary centered metric, for \(u\perp e\),
\[
F''(e u^T-u e^T)
=\frac{32\gamma}{n}\|u\|^2
+\frac{16(n-6)}{n^2}u^TG_0u.
\]
This is the **corrected** individual formula. For \(n\ge6\) it is positive; for \(n=5\), \(u^TG_0u<\operatorname{tr}(G_0)\|u\|^2=n\gamma\|u\|^2\) gives positivity. For \(n=3\), centered constant diagonal forces \(G_0=(3\gamma/2)P\), giving \((8\gamma/3)\|u\|^2>0\). At \(n=4\), the flat Hadamard aggregate already handles all \(G\). The corrected formula sums exactly to the boxed aggregate when \(u_i=(2/\sqrt n)Pe_i\) represents \(A_i\).

## 3. Exact dimension-three stationary determinant

Use quaternion coordinates \((1,r,t,u)\), \(D=1+r^2+t^2+u^2\), and
\[
W=D^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}
1+r^2-t^2-u^2&2(rt-u)&2(ru+t)\\
2(rt+u)&1-r^2+t^2-u^2&2(tu-r)\\
2(ru-t)&2(tu+r)&1-r^2-t^2+u^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let the three contrast coordinates be the distances \(w_{12},w_{13},w_{23}\), represented by \(G=P G_{\rm off}P\), \(G_{\rm off,ij}=-w_{ij}/2\), zero diagonal. The stationarity matrix is formed directly from \(S=W^T[W\circ(GM)]\). Exact determinant expansion and factorization give
\[
\det\Phi_W=-8P_6P_{12}/D^9.
\]
For \(x=(1,r^2,t^2,u^2)\), let \(e_j\) be its elementary symmetric functions and \(\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)\). Then
\[
P_6=e_1^3-8e_1e_2+48e_3,
\qquad
P_{12}=rtu[(e_1^2-4e_2)^2-64e_4]+4\Delta.
\]
The orthostochastic determinant is exactly \(\det M=P_6/D^3\). All compact identities were checked by exact polynomial expansion, not inferred from numerical roots. The complete expanded recoverable factorization is retained in `results/r09_algebra/cycle6/n3_stationarity_factorization.json`; code is `work/r09_algebra/cycle6/factor_n3.py`.

This chart misses rotations with quaternion scalar zero; signed column permutations can change charts but a complete classification must prove coverage and handle all rank degeneracies. Neither the determinant factorization nor the finite branch probes constitute that classification.

One useful first-branch obstruction is universal. If \(M\) has contrast rank one, write \(M_0=PMP=a b^T\ne0\). For \(C=M_0M_0^T\), every variation has the form \(a v^T+v a^T\). If the stationary map has rank two, its annihilator among centered symmetric \(G_0\) is one-dimensional, namely \(a_\perp a_\perp^T\). Such a form has rank one on the two-dimensional contrast plane and cannot be positive definite there. Therefore a PD stationary point on \(P_6=0\) must have stationary-map rank at most one. In dimension three \(M_0=0\) would require every squared entry equal to \(1/3\), which real orthogonality excludes by the odd number of signs in any row inner product. Rank drops/intersections remain to be classified.

## 4. A complete weighted stationary branch, with analytic escape

For quaternion \((1,0,z,z)\), set \(y=z^2\),
\[
W(z)=\frac1{1+2y}
\begin{pmatrix}1-2y&-2z&2z\\2z&1&2y\\-2z&2y&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For finite nonzero \(z\) and \(y\ne1/2\), exact stationarity equations imply
\[
(w_{12},w_{13},w_{23})=(a,a,b),\qquad
a(1-8y+4y^2)+3yb=0.
\]
The third skew equation has coefficient \(2z^2(2z^2-1)/(1+2z^2)^3\) multiplying \(w_{12}-w_{13}\), forcing equality; substitution in either remaining equation gives the displayed relation. If \(G\succ0\), all distances are positive, so \(b>0\) and \(d(y)=1-8y+4y^2<0\). The roots of \(d\) cannot support PD stationarity because the relation forces \(b=0\).

For fixed such metric, the gap along this feasible curve is
\[
L(y)=\operatorname{tr}G-F_G(W(z))
=\frac{16a y(1-2y+4y^2)+24b y^2}{(1+2y)^4}.
\]
Differentiate while holding \(a,b\) fixed:
\[
L'(y)=\frac{16(1-2y)[a,d(y)+3by]}{(1+2y)^5},
\qquad
L''(y)\big|_{\rm stationary}
=\frac{48b(1-2y)^2}{d(y)(1+2y)^4}<0.
\]
Because \(z\ne0\), varying \(y\) gives a nonzero feasible tangent. At a stationary point the second variation is intrinsic, independent of parametrization acceleration. Thus this is a strict positive-Hessian direction for \(F\).

The exceptional \(y=1/2\) has stationary kernel dimension two: distances \((a,a,b)\) with no fixed ratio. In the skew basis for column pairs \((12),(13),(23)\), its complete Hessian is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
(9b-4a)/4&(4a+3b)/4&0\\
(4a+3b)/4&(9b-4a)/4&0\\
0&0&4a-b
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The vector \((1,1,0)\) has eigenvalue \(3b>0\), again excluding a nonglobal local maximum. At \(z=0\) the frame is a signed permutation, so this endpoint is global. These cases exhaust this one-parameter family; they do not exhaust the general degree-twelve stationary surface.

A second independent sufficient check on the generic PD branch is its Hessian trace. Substitution of \(a/b=-3y/d(y)\) into the universal trace formula gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{\rm skew}F''/b
=-\frac{4(16y^4-40y^3+72y^2-10y+1)}{(1+2y)^2d(y)}>0.
\]
The numerator is strictly positive: with \(v=2y>0\), divide by \(v^2\) to get
\((v+v^{-1})^2-5(v+v^{-1})+16
=(v+v^{-1}-5/2)^2+39/4\). This agrees with the explicit curve escape.
