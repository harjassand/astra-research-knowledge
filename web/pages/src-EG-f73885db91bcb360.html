# Internal prestress in solid electrolytes: closest prior art

Research date: 12 October 2026. Scope: targeted primary-source audit following `scientific_obstruction/OBSTRUCTION.md`; no experiment or materials acquisition. **The circular tensile-ring proposal is already the motivating experiment's method.** A smaller tiled grid might pose a useful manufacturing problem, but neither a fresh prestress mechanism nor an improvement over the strongest comparator has been established.

## Decisive matched baseline

[Cui et al., *Nature* 655, 631–637 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10734-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10734-x) uses a **Ni38Ti48Nb14 shape-memory-alloy ring** around a garnet pellet. Methods, “Biaxial stress application,” describes heating to 170 °C, a 30-minute hold, then cooling. Ring inner diameter contracts from 11.96 to 11.43 mm and remains contracted at room temperature. A PEEK inner coating prevents shorting. This is a retained, self-contained circumferential load path already; calling it a removable or continuously powered compression fixture is misleading. Its nominal stress is approximately 330 MPa. [Official supplementary information](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10734-x/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10734_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), Fig. S3, shows the ring/pellet geometry and 6 mm lithium pads; Table S1 reports XRD stresses from 420 ± 30 to 473 ± 21 MPa across cycling stages. The experiment additionally uses 5 MPa stack pressure.

The methods and quantitative SI table were independently retrieved by the materials worker. This branch additionally read official SI PDF pages 3–5 and 23–25 in memory, confirming the retained-ring cell geometry and that ring removal requires mechanically cutting it. Direct Nature Methods access was intermittent. The distinction that survives is **manufacturing a thin, large-area or tiled version with demonstrably better total performance and resource cost**, not introducing passive ring prestress.

## Scaffold, grid and patent disclosures

[WO2024009109A1, “Solid electrolyte”](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024009109A1/en) (published 11 January 2024; listed priority 8 July 2022), by Todd and Tannesia, is especially close to the laminated alternative. It explicitly discloses compressed surface electrolyte layers balanced by tensile internal electrolyte layers, generated by cooling a layered structure; it includes Al-LLZO/Ta-LLZO and co-sintering. The disclosure contains fabricated examples and indentation/cycling measurements. It establishes the mechanism's explicit prior disclosure; it does not establish a uniformly compressed core, a scalable separator, or the ring experiment's performance. The precise build measurements were inspected independently by the materials worker; this branch checked the primary description and claims.

[US20250349944A1, “Systems and methods for controlling dendrite propagation in solid-state electrochemical cells”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250349944A1/en) (published 13 November 2025; listed priority 3 June 2022) explicitly describes thermal mismatch producing electrolyte compression balanced by cathode tension. Section 1.2 includes other supporting materials and an electrically inert substrate added to alter stress; it also describes multilayer and chemically generated residual stress. Claim 19 combines thermal residual compression with a casing applying no stack pressure. This is a close technical disclosure, not evidence that every architecture is built or durable. No legal validity or freedom-to-operate conclusion is made.

[US8147663B2, “Scaffold for an ion-conductive membrane”](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8147663B2/en) (2010 application publication; listed 2009 priority) describes multiple ceramic NaSICON/LiSICON wafers within a thermoplastic plate's apertures. Heating expands the apertures; cooling traps and edge-seals the wafers, optionally against compressed O-rings. Thus tiled ion-conductive ceramic scaffolds and thermally assembled perimeter seals are explicit. This disclosure does **not** establish 330 MPa compression throughout the wafers or lithium-dendrite suppression. Its PP/HDPE frame and compliant sealing contact should not be equated with the metal SMA load path.

The targeted search did not establish an exact prestretched honeycomb/grid separator combining retained 330 MPa compression, thin full-thickness ion paths, and practical cycling. That exact implementation's priority is **UNKNOWN**. Failed keyword retrieval supplies no novelty evidence.

For mechanically shielded paths, [Hu et al., *Joule* 8, 2623–2638 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2024.06.024) already demonstrates crack deflection at weak interfaces with dissimilar elastic moduli, lateral deflection within oriented inner layers, and reaction products blocking cracks. Its sulfide/halide multilayers plate above 15 mA/cm² at 2.5 MPa stack pressure. This is a relevant alternative baseline, not an identical garnet/prestress comparison.

## Constructibility and the remaining gate

Retained prestress needs a measured tensile counterpart and a reliable interface. For a free, bonded composite with ceramic volume fraction f compressed by P in either in-plane direction, force balance requires average tensile stress of at least fP/(1−f) in the remaining load-bearing material. At P = 330 MPa and f = 0.9, this is 2.97 GPa **before** ring peak stresses or concentration factors. Voids and ion-permeable holes do not carry that tension. This necessary balance is not a design certificate; the root report derives the sharper annular limit.

For skins, charge series ionic resistance and require electronic insulation. For rings/grids, charge inactive area, ceramic edge finishing, insulation, preload tolerances, contact traction and sealing. Resolve the complete biaxial stress tensor near holes and joints: a favorable area-average compression can leave connected weak lanes. Measure stress before and after assembly, thermal cycling and electrochemical cycling; bound creep, yielding, debonding and local buckling. PEEK insulation in the existing ring is a useful construction precedent, not evidence that a thinned coating survives every grid geometry.

The closest matched experiment is therefore **Cui's retained SMA ring**, followed by a co-sintered or thermally mismatched laminate for the manufacturable residual-stress route. A prospective grid must beat those at matched ceramic chemistry, thickness, active area, stack pressure, current, areal capacity and cycle count, including discarded parts and fabrication/inspection cost. No such advantage is established here. The circular-ring discovery claim is rejected by direct prior art; the manufacturing question remains open.
