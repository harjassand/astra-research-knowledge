# Root gate: universal matrix-Krylov recovery for binary blocks

7 October 2026, cycle11. **PASS** for `notes/metric_acquisition/cycle11/BINARY_BLOCK_KRYLOV_NONVANISHING.md`, also separately reconstructed by `metric_geometry10`. This is a universal theorem for a specified arbitrary-dimensional subclass, not a genericity or finite-example claim. Historical priority remains unestablished.

Input remains only the expanded rational QPD quartic with unknown rational hidden frame and Q. Suppose Q, after an unknown permutation, is block diagonal with blocks of size at most two. No partition or aligned metric is supplied. The unchanged coefficient-only matrix-Krylov line algorithm then has its required nonvanishing guarantee and expected polynomial bit cost.

For H=Hess(f), h=det(H), T the quartic polarization and C=adj(H)(T:adj(H)), put M=C/h^2 wherever h≠0. M transforms by similarity under coordinate changes and is block diagonal for a direct sum of quartics. Scaling one block's input by t scales its M block by t^-4.

For a binary block f=a x^4+2b x^2y^2+c y^4, ac−b^2>0, direct multiplication gives

    det[(x,y), C(x,y)] =144(ac−b^2)xy(a x^4−c y^4).

Thus the input vector is generically cyclic. If b≠0, C(1,0) has diagonal entries32ab^2 and144a^2c+16ab^2, both nonzero; H is also invertible there. If b=0, H and C are invertible off the axes and the displayed cyclicity polynomial is nonzero. Their three nonzero-polynomial conditions intersect at a rational point. A singleton has M=1/(144a x^4), so is cyclic and invertible at any nonzero x.

Choose such a point in each block solely for the proof. Its finitely many eigenvalues are nonzero, including when a block is not diagonalizable. Positive rational block scales avoid every cross-block eigenvalue collision: each collision forbids only finitely many scale ratios. Each block's minimal polynomial has its full block degree by cyclicity, and now these polynomials are pairwise coprime. The Chinese remainder theorem implies that the direct-sum vector is cyclic for the direct-sum matrix. Hence det[x,Mx,…,M^(n−1)x]≠0, and multiplying its columns by h^(2j) proves K=det[x,Cx,…,C^(n−1)x] is not the zero polynomial.

The rational witnesses and spectral separation are existence devices only. The learner evaluates the intrinsic polynomial circuit and its derivatives on rational lines using the previously charged degree and height bounds; it does not acquire hidden blocks, spectra or witness heights first. Exact reflection verification, rational invariant-metric recovery, axis selection and the final expanded QPD/SOS check remain mandatory.

This covers zero couplings, arbitrary signed binary couplings and repeated blocks. It does not imply that the matrix detector is universally nonzero for dense ternary or larger blocks. The universal ternary flow detector is a different construction, so its nonvanishing cannot be substituted. The useful downstream change is a complete fast guarantee on this nongeneric subclass; arbitrary-Q fast recovery remains open despite the universal high-degree fallback.
