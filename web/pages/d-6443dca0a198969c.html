This addendum supplements, and does not alter, work/theory/t18_kakeya_incidence/v1.txt. It records exact source locators and makes three precision corrections: the moving-box class used in the packing argument, the base dependence of K_3, and the rank-zero case. The mathematical status of every proposed inequality remains candidate/unproved.

1. Exact moving-box convention and endpoint exponent accounting
---------------------------------------------------------------

Set N=delta^{-1}. In the line chart M_i(t)=b_i+t u_i, interpret the moving box as

    B_t = c_0+t c_1 + R( [-a/(2N),a/(2N)] x [-b/(2N),b/(2N)] x [-c/(2N),c/(2N)] ),

where R is one fixed orthogonal map for all t in [0,1], and c_0,c_1 are fixed vectors. A line index i is counted in n_B exactly when M_i(t) belongs to B_t for every t in [0,1]. Thus the box translates affinely, while its orientation and widths stay fixed. The phrase “moving box” in v1 was underspecified.

For two counted lines i,j, the common center c_0+t c_1 cancels. Each of M_i(0)-M_j(0) and M_i(1)-M_j(1) lies in the difference box of the fixed shape. Since

    u_i-u_j = [M_i(1)-M_j(1)] - [M_i(0)-M_j(0)],

the slopes of all counted lines lie in a translate of a box with widths O(a/N), O(b/N), O(c/N). N^{-1}-separated slopes in this box number at most C abc, by packing disjoint balls of radius c_0/N in a constant enlargement. Hence Delta_box=O(1) for unit weights. If the box orientation is allowed to rotate with t, this abc packing conclusion has not been established and must not be used without a replacement bound.

With I=nN lambda, m=I/|E|, and Delta_box=O(1), the candidate (1) in v1 yields

    |E| >= c_{p,eta} N^{-eta} n N lambda^(p+1),   p>3.

This family of estimates implies the density-four endpoint with an arbitrary N^{-epsilon} loss, rather than by formally setting p=3. Indeed, for lambda>=N^{-1},

    lambda^(p+1) = lambda^4 lambda^(p-3)
                 >= N^{-(p-3)} lambda^4,

so choose p=3+epsilon/4 and eta=epsilon/4. For lambda<N^{-1}, delta-separated directions give n<=C N^3 and therefore nN lambda^4<=C; if E is nonempty, |E|>=1 proves the same N^{-epsilon} bound after adjusting constants. The lambda=0 case is trivial. This is only a consequence conditional on (1), not a proof of (1). The standard duality/shading reduction from this density estimate to the L^4 Kakeya maximal statement is not reconstructed here.

2. Frame statistic: base dependence and a symmetric candidate
-------------------------------------------------------------

For three frame points (t_i,theta_i) with theta_i in R^2, the v1 scalar based at i is

    K^(i) = (t_j-t_i)(t_k-t_i) det(theta_j-theta_i, theta_k-theta_i),

where {i,j,k}={0,1,2}. The oriented theta-area has the same absolute value for each base, but the time-gap product generally changes with the base. Thus K_3 in v1 is not base-invariant, even up to a uniform factor over degenerate triples. If K^(i)=0, the corresponding 2x2 matrix has rank at most one; rank zero exposes no normal-velocity combination, while rank one exposes at most one. This sharpens the “only one” wording in v1.

A base-symmetric coordinate statistic is

    K_sym = |det(theta_1-theta_0, theta_2-theta_0)|
            max_{i in {0,1,2}} product_{j != i} |t_i-t_j|.

The theta-area magnitude is invariant under permutation of the three frame points, and the maximum is symmetric; K_sym equals max_i |K^(i)|. Under a chart change t -> alpha t+beta and theta -> A theta+gamma, it scales by |alpha|^2 |det A|. It is therefore a coordinate-scale statistic in the fixed normalized chart, not an invariant under arbitrary line-space coordinate changes. The two-frame identity (2) still gives only a conditional linear-algebra observation: if the other terms are known and the selected comparison matrix is nonsingular, its two cross-term observations determine V,W with inverse norm O(1/|K^(i)|) when gaps are bounded. No incidence or entropy estimate follows from this algebra.

The product/regulus and hairbrush examples in v1 remain countertests to a stand-alone curvature classifier. Their directions or line parameters do not specify the event-frame selection law needed to turn generic nonzero K_sym into a Kakeya estimate. The missing theorem remains a curvature-weighted, incidence-sensitive transport inequality summed across scales with N^{o(1)} total loss.
