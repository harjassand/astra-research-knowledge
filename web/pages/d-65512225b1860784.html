## 1. Inherited margins and the affine coordinate map

The read and independently gated module is `notes/metric_use10/cycle17/BOUNDED_SPACE_RECONSTRUCTION_AND_MARGIN_MODULE.md`, root gate `notes/root_cycle17/SOS_RECONSTRUCTION_MODULE_GATE.md`. Define

    U=S_f(nK)^4,
    ell=2^(-n)/(nU)^(n-1),
    rho=ell/[8n(nK)^4],
    mstar=ell/[n(nK)^2],
    t=min(1/(2n),rho/(4n)).                          (1)

It proves, by transport of a hidden Gram solely as an existence witness, that some observed Gram R_0>=rho I satisfies A(R_0)=F. M_0=tI and B_0=[[R_0,m(M_0)],[m(M_0)^T,1]] obey B_0>=rho I/8. Neither R_0 nor V is supplied. It also proves M_*>=mstar I for the canonical maxdet metric.

Use the plain quadratic feature vector z=(x_i^2; x_i x_j, i<j). For a symmetric metric, m(M) has diagonal entries M_ii and mixed entries 2M_ij. A(G) is the quartic coefficient vector of z^T Gz. Every degree4 monomial has at most three unordered quadratic pairings. If its pairing p is diagonal, its coefficient weight w_p is1; otherwise w_p=2. Classes of Gram entries belonging to distinct quartic exponents are disjoint. Choose one entry p0 in each class as a pivot, and solve

    G_p0=(F_exponent-sum_{p!=p0} w_p G_p)/w_p0.       (2)

All other Gram entries and all independent M entries are coordinates x. Thus

    d=N+N(N+1)/2-binom(n+3,4)=O(n^4),
    B(x)=[[G(x),m(M(x))],[m(M(x))^T,1]]

is an exact rational affine map satisfying A(G(x))=F identically. The particular affine origin need not be positive. No nullspace solve or equality tolerance is hidden here.

For a coordinate increment u=(u_M,u_G), pivot ratios have absolute value at most2 and at most two free entries occur in one class. Therefore

    ||delta G||_F^2<=18||u_G||^2,
    ||delta M||_F<=sqrt(2)||u_M||,
    ||delta m||<=2||u_M||,
    ||delta B||_F^2<=18||u_G||^2+8||u_M||^2<=18||u||^2.

In particular ||delta B||_F<=5||u|| and ||delta M||_op<=2||u||. Conversely, free Gram entries and all metric coefficients occur directly in B, so

    ||delta B||_F>=||u||.                            (3)

The last inequality proves injectivity and will give a quantified strong concavity.

The exact Gaussian quartic functional has feature moment with diagonal block 2I+J and mixed block I; its minimum is at least1. For every feasible B>=0, M>=0,

    tr G<=E F(g)<=3S_f,  ||B||_op<=3S_f+1.

Also M_ii^2<=F(e_i)<=S_f, so each metric entry has absolute value at most S_f by positivity. Each free Gram entry has absolute value at most3S_f. Thus every feasible coordinate vector lies within radius3S_f sqrt(d). We use the known integer outer radius

    R=8(d+1)(S_f+1).                                (4)

All feasible points are inside the radius R/2 ball about the coordinate origin. Gaussian evaluation is a deterministic coefficient functional, not sampling.

## 2. Exact separation and acquired phase I

First search for a point satisfying the closed conditions

    M(x)>=tI/2,  B(x)>=rho I/16.                     (5)

The unknown witness in Section1 contains a Euclidean ball of radius

    r_I=min(t/4,rho/80,R/4)                         (6)

within this body: metric perturbations are at most2r_I and block perturbations at most5r_I. The inner center is not required by the algorithm.

At any rational center, exact rational symmetric elimination decides whether both shifted matrices in (5) are PSD. If one fails, obtain a rational vector v with v^T T v<0 for the failed shifted matrix T. The affine functional v^T T(y)v is nonnegative on the search body and negative at the center. Its central halfspace therefore retains the entire search body. Its normal is the exact affine adjoint of vv^T.

If this normal is zero while its constant value is negative, the search body is empty; return UNKNOWN. This branch cannot occur under the promised nonempty-body margins. It is not passed into the nonzero-normal ellipsoid update.

A rational negative witness is constructive. Recursively eliminate positive diagonal pivots by Schur complement. A negative diagonal immediately yields a standard coordinate witness. If a zero diagonal has a nonzero entry b in its row, the restriction to those two indices is [[0,b],[b,a]]; choose a rational s opposite in sign to b with 0<|s|<=min(1,|b|/(|a|+1)), then 2bs+as^2<0. Lift the witness through the rational elimination congruence. If every remaining zero-diagonal row is zero, the remainder is PSD. Determinants/minors bound all elimination and witness encodings polynomially in the matrix dimension and query bit size. This handles singular matrices exactly; it is not an eigenvalue-tolerance test.

The finite-bit ellipsoid routine in Section4 starts at the radius R ball and uses only these central cuts. Its volume argument with r_I returns a rational center satisfying (5) within the stated number of iterations. Verify (5) exactly and save the point as the acquired phase-I certificate. ## 3. Barrier optimization with an acquired point and exact gradients

Choose

    mu=min(eta_goal/(4N),1/(64N)).                   (7)

On strict feasibility define

    Phi_mu(x)=logdet M(x)+mu logdet B(x)
             =logdet M(x)+mu logdet S(x),
    S=G-mm^T.

Strict feasibility and compactness give an interior maximizer x_mu: a singular M or B makes the corresponding log determinant tend to minus infinity, while the other determinant is bounded above. Injectivity (3) makes Phi strictly concave. The inherited stationarity and margin module gives

    K_mu=mu S_mu^-1=A*lambda_mu,
    w_mu=K_mu m_mu,
    m_mu^T K_mu m_mu=n/2,
    lambda_mu(F)=mu N+n/2,
    c_mu=n+mu N.                                   (8)

The actual central point is not supplied. Define public bounds

    tauS=mu rho/(mu N+n/2),
    tauK=mu/(3S_f),
    tauM=mstar/2,
    beta=min(tauS,1)/[4(1+3S_f)].                   (9)

Then M_mu>=tauM I, S_mu>=tauS I, K_mu>=tauK I, and B_mu>=beta I. The last inequality follows from the triangular Schur factorization: ||m_mu||^2<=3S_f and the inverse triangular factor has squared norm at most4(1+3S_f).

Optimize over the closed trimmed domain

    D_mu={x: M(x)>=tauM I/2, B(x)>=beta I/2}.       (10)

It contains the central maximizer and its radius

    r_margin=min(tauM/4,beta/10,R/4)                (11)

ball. Matrix PSD separation is as in Section2. On D_mu, directional derivatives give the rational global Lipschitz bound

    L_0=4n/tauM+10mu(N+1)/beta.                    (12)

The exact Hessian has

    -D^2 Phi_mu[u,u]
       >=mu ||B^-1/2 delta B B^-1/2||_F^2
       >=sigma ||u||^2,
    sigma=mu/(3S_f+1)^2.                          (13)

This uses the feasible outer bound on B and (3). Hence objective accuracy, not an assumed convergence of a numerical solver, gives coordinate accuracy.

At a feasible rational center the gradient is rational: its components are tr(M^-1 M_j)+mu tr(B^-1 B_j). If this gradient is zero, the center is the maximizer. Otherwise use the central halfspace g(x)^T(y-x)>=0. For any kappa>0, the fixed ball about x_mu of radius

    r_opt=min(r_margin,kappa/(2L_0))               (14)

has objective at least Phi_mu(x_mu)-kappa/2. At a queried feasible center whose objective gap is larger than kappa, concavity gives g(x)^T(y-x)>=Phi_mu(y)-Phi_mu(x)>kappa/2 on this whole ball. Thus every such bad-center gradient cut preserves the ball, not merely x_mu. Every infeasible-center cut also preserves it. The rounded volume lemma forces at least one feasible center of objective gap at most kappa after the prescribed iterations.

The algorithm need not know which center has this gap. Approximate each feasible objective to absolute error kappa/16 and retain the largest approximated value. The selected rational center has actual gap at most9kappa/8. Choose

    C=2+N+3S_f+1/mu+1/tauS+1/tauM+1/tauK,
    xi=mu/[10^6 C^30],
    kappa=sigma xi^2/16.                           (15)

By (13), its coordinate distance to x_mu is at most3xi/8, in particular at mostxi. Every quantity prescribing the run is public rational arithmetic; log(1/xi) and log(R/r_opt) are polynomial in the charged parameters.

Objective values are acquired at finite bit cost. Compute each positive rational determinant by exact elimination. For rational a>0, write a=2^j u with 1<=u<2 using binary comparisons. Then

    log u=2 sum_{k>=0} ((u-1)/(u+1))^(2k+1)/(2k+1)

has ratio at most1/9 in its absolute terms, and log2 uses the same series at u=2. Truncate using its geometric tail, allocating error to |j|log2 and the two determinants. ## 4. Rounded central-cut ellipsoid with explicit containment

This is an explicit specialization of classical ellipsoid methods, not a new claim of optimization-method priority. It makes rational encoding and the unknown inner center explicit. For dimension d>=2, let E(c,Q) be the ellipsoid (y-c)^T Q^-1(y-c)<=1 with rational Q>0. Orient a nonzero rational normal a so that the retained central halfspace is a^T(y-c)<=0, and put gamma=a^TQa. The exact minimal enclosing central-cut update is

    Q'=d^2/(d^2-1)[Q-2 Qaa^TQ/((d+1)gamma)],
    c'=c-Qa/((d+1)sqrt(gamma)).                    (16)

Shape Q' is rational; center c' need not be. The two shape factors relative to Q are d^2/(d+1)^2 and d^2/(d^2-1), so Q'>=Q/4. The exact volume ratio obeys

    log(vol E(c',Q')/vol E(c,Q))<=-1/[2(d+1)].     (17)

For d>=3, apply log(1+s)<=s and log(1-u)<=-u-u^2/2 to (d/2)log(1+1/(d^2-1))+(1/2)log(1-2/(d+1)); the difference from -1/[2(d+1)] is at most(3-d)/[2(d-1)(d+1)^2]. For d=2, the squared ratio16/27 is smaller than25/36, so log ratio<log(5/6)<=-1/6. Containment in (16) has a direct proof: transform to the unit ball and put t in[-1,0] for the cut-normal coordinate. The orthogonal squared norm is at most1-t^2. Substituting into the new ellipsoid quadratic expression gives at most1+2(d+1)t(t+1)/d^2<=1. This supplies containment independently of any real optimization oracle.

Fix the total number of iterations T in advance and define rational grids

    q_0=R^2 4^(-T), a_0=R2^(-T),
    e=1/[100(d+1)^2],
    delta_c=e a_0/(10d),
    Delta=e q_0/(10d).                             (18)

Inductively, every exact Q' during these T iterations has minimum at leastq_0. Approximate theta=1/sqrt(gamma) rationally with error at most delta_c/[4max(1,||Qa||_1)]. Use it in (16), then round every center coordinate to the nearest multiple of delta_c. Each coordinate differs from c' by at most3delta_c/4, hence ||c_bar-c'||<=e a_0/4. In the Q' metric this error is at moste/4. Consequently E(c',Q') is contained in E(c_bar,(1+e)^2Q').

Round the symmetric entries of (1+e)^2Q' to the nearest multiples of Delta, then add d Delta I. The rounding error has operator norm at mostd Delta/2. The resulting rational shape Q_bar satisfies

    (1+e)^2Q' <=Q_bar
       <=(1+e)^2Q'+3d Delta I/2 <=(1+3e)Q'.       (19)

Thus containment is preserved exactly and Q_bar>=Q'>=Q/4. Volume inflation is at most(3/2)de, so (17) gives the net shrink

    vol E(c_bar,Q_bar)/vol E(c,Q)
        <=exp(-1/[4(d+1)]).                       (20)

There is no floating containment test. Rounding grids, rational square-root enclosures and the Loewner inequalities prove containment before any fixture.

Choose

    T=8d(d+1)[1+ceil(log2(R/r))]                   (21)

for the relevant r=r_I or r_opt, with r<=R/4. The ceiling is determined by rational binary comparisons. If every central cut had retained a radius-r ball, (20) would put its volume below that ball's volume after T cuts, a contradiction. This proves both the phase-I and optimization run guarantees.

Bit bounds are also explicit. The stored centers lie on one fixed rational grid delta_c, and shapes on one fixed grid Delta (the added diagonal remains on that grid). Q_bar<2Q at each step; the looser bound lambda_max(Q_k)<=R^2 4^k suffices. Each center displacement is at mostsqrt(lambda_max(Q_k))/(d+1)+e a_0/4, so centers stay within radius R2^(T+2). Numerators and denominators of stored values therefore have O(T+log R+log(d+1)) bits, up to the encoding of R. Rational PSD witnesses, inverses, trace gradients and cut normals have polynomial bit size in these stored encodings and n,d; normalize a by its largest absolute entry, using only rational arithmetic. Then gamma is betweenq_0 andd R^2 4^T, so the prescribed inverse-square-root enclosure is obtainable by rational bisection/Newton enclosure at polynomial bit cost. A large exact intermediate fraction is rounded at every step; repeated exact unrounded shape iteration is not claimed to have small height.

For d=1 use intervals, retain the half interval selected by the central cut, and bisect rational endpoints. The length halves and endpoint bit lengths increase linearly in T. Formula (16) is not used in dimension1. In our parameterization d=1 occurs at n=1; the optimizer module still works, while the sole rational axis can separately be returned directly. ## 5. Constructing the rational dual instead of guessing central multipliers

Let x_c be the acquired rational point with ||x_c-x_mu||<=xi. Its affine coefficient equality is exact. Compute

    S_c=G_c-m_c m_c^T, K_tilde=mu S_c^-1.

Acquire K_c by orthogonal Frobenius projection of K_tilde onto range A*. This is especially simple here: a Hankel matrix has K_ij=lambda_(exponent_i+exponent_j), constant on each pairing class. Set each lambda_c to the weighted average of K_tilde entries in that class, weights1 on diagonal entries and2 off diagonal. The denominators of these averaging weights are at most6. This is exact rational projection, not an unknown dual optimum.

Define w_c from the exact inverse M_c^-1: each entry is half the corresponding independent entry of that inverse. The mixed metric feature has coefficient2M_ij, so this gives exactly

    2w_c^T m(H)=tr(M_c^-1 H).

Then compute alpha_c=w_c^T K_c^-1 w_c. If K_c>0, the dual block [[K_c,-w_c],[-w_c^T,alpha_c]] has zero Schur complement and is exactly PSD.

Here are sufficient explicit perturbation bounds, using every scalar factor and reciprocal in C from (15). All norms below are Euclidean/Frobenius or their dominated operator norms. Both central and queried feasible points have ||m||<=sqrt(3S_f)<=C. Equations (2)--(3) give

    ||delta M||<=2xi, ||delta G||<=5xi,
    ||delta m||<=2xi, ||delta S||<=9C xi.

The chosen xi ensures S_c>=tauS I/2 and M_c>=tauM I/2. Inverse perturbation and nonexpansiveness of the orthogonal projection give

    ||K_c-K_mu||<=18C^3 xi,
    K_c>=tauK I/2,
    ||w_c-w_mu||<=2C^2 xi,
    ||K_c^-1-K_mu^-1||<=36C^5 xi.                 (22)

We have ||w_mu||<=C^2, ||w_c||<=2C^2. Split the scalar difference into (w_c-w_mu)^T K_c^-1(w_c+w_mu)+w_mu^T(K_c^-1-K_mu^-1)w_mu. It follows that

    |alpha_c-alpha_mu|<=48C^9 xi,
    |lambda_c(F)-lambda_mu(F)|<=18C^4 xi,
    |c_c-(n+mu N)|<=66C^9 xi<=mu N.               (23)

No central lambda coordinates are acquired or rounded separately. The certificate is computed entirely from the acquired primal point. Its lower gap bound is exact: the trace pairing of the two PSD block matrices is c_c-n, because 2w_c^T m_c=n. Thus

    0<=c_c-n<=2mu N<=eta_goal.                    (24)

Verify every claimed equality and PSD inequality with exact arithmetic before output. All rational inverses, projection averages, w,alpha and common denominators have bit size polynomial in the query encoding and the dimensions.
