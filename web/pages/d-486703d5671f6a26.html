# Kohn–Sham frontier transfer: final verdict

## Verdict

No breakthrough or novelty-cleared scientific advance emerged. The strongest
quantitative-looking consequence is a standard convex-analysis result: for a
fixed finite local-potential norm budget, the pinned source density has a
strictly positive error floor in the strong (L^1\cap L^3) norm. The floor is
not effective, has no reported value or rate, and is not a practical DFT error
bound. A complementary Brøndsted–Rockafellar argument shows that exact
ensemble local-potential densities nevertheless approximate the target in this
same norm, while every such approximating sequence needs unbounded quotient
potential norm. This strengthens the qualitative quantifier picture but is a
standard consequence of Lieb's ensemble functional framework.

Two further conditional scope extensions were reconstructed: an exact local
realization using the Friedrichs operator of the source's (L^2_{\rm loc})
formal potential, and an engineered finite-rank nonlocal realization. Neither
is a conventional physical generalized-Kohn–Sham remedy. The source's finite
charge remains non-effective, so there is no proved modest-parameter molecule
or common-chemistry conclusion.

## Main reconstructed consequence

For every finite (C), let \(\mathcal R_C\) be the exact ensemble densities
generated by potentials with quotient (V)-norm at most (C). The direct
hostile compactness proof establishes strong-(X) closedness under the stated
interface. Strong density convergence bounds potential pairings. A single
smooth trial determinant upper-bounds the ground energy, and the exact ground
energy identity then gives a uniform ensemble kinetic bound. Strong (L^1)
convergence supplies spatial tightness; kinetic control and tightness give
trace-norm compactness of the ensembles by cutoffs, Rellich compactness, and
finite-rank approximation. Weak (L^{3/2}) and weak-* (L^\infty) limits retain
the norm budget and pairings, and variational comparison makes the limiting
ensemble a ground ensemble. This does not assume a uniform infinitesimal form
bound over a critical (L^{3/2}) ball.

Conditional on source nonrepresentability, (n_*\notin\mathcal R_C), so
\(\delta_C=\operatorname{dist}_X(n_*,\mathcal R_C)>0\) whenever the set is
nonempty. The gap is existential, not computable. Lieb's ensemble
noninteracting constrained-search functional (F_{s,\mathrm{ens}}) is proper,
convex and lower semicontinuous on (X); the pinned target is in its effective
domain by the finite-kinetic source determinant. Brøndsted–Rockafellar gives
density of its subgradient domain in that domain. A subgradient is an exact
ensemble local-potential ground state, so exact representable densities
approach (n_*) in (X). This step uses the ensemble functional, not
determinant-only (T_s). Fixed-budget closedness and nonrepresentability force
the quotient norms of every such convergent sequence to tend to infinity.
Consequently \(\delta_C\downarrow0\), but no error-versus-potential-norm
modulus is known.

Primary prior checked: Lieb, “Density functionals for Coulomb systems,”
[DOI 10.1002/qua.560240302](https://doi.org/10.1002/qua.560240302), §§3–4 and
Theorem 3.14; and Penz et al., “The Structure of the Density-Potential Mapping,
Part I,” [DOI 10.1021/acsphyschemau.2c00069](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphyschemau.2c00069), §§VI–VIII. Both put the density/potential duality and
ensemble representability in the established convex framework. The main result
here is therefore retained as an audited corollary, not a novelty claim.

## Physicality and effectivity attacks

The source's formal potential (V=\Delta g/(2g)\in L^2_{\rm loc}) is not in
the source's (L^{3/2}+L^\infty) class. From the source-reported positive
ground-state transform, finite weighted-potential energy, and global weighted
gap, the minimal form on (C_c^\infty) is closable and nonnegative. Its
Friedrichs realization has (g/\sqrt2) as its spatial ground orbital and
source orbital (h) at the first excited level (d>0). Pauli filling then
makes the source density an exact three-electron pure ground-state density.
Two independent hostile domain audits found no defect in this conditional
form-domain argument. Ground-state transforms and Friedrichs closure are
standard tools, however; this establishes an enlarged operator-class
realization, not physical nuclear-potential representability, a Fock/hybrid
operator, or a useful chemical system.

A second conditional construction uses a finite-rank nonlocal quadratic-form
perturbation with relative kinetic form bound zero. It represents the same
density and preserves its constrained-search kinetic energy, but is explicitly
density-tailored and is not shown to belong to Fock, screened-exchange, hybrid,
or standard pseudopotential families.

The effective-charge attack stops at the exact bottleneck. Eq. (7.10) chooses
\(Z\) from the supremum of the bad-coupling set; Lemma 7.4 proves only that the
set omits an unspecified interval \((0,\tau)\). A numerical (\tau>0\) would
give a charge, but the source supplies no effective bounds for the isolated
H\(_2^+\) bonding/antibonding splitting, analytic perturbation radius,
uniform exterior remainders, convergence rate of all variational minimizers,
or positive angle-plane margin. No effective charge or modest-parameter
counterexample was derived; no common-chemistry failure is inferred.

## Costs, falsifiers, and next gate

The closure and Friedrichs arguments are analytic and require no numerical
computation or experimental data. They assume the pinned source target and
orbital certificates as inputs; the nonnumerical charge and long source
construction remain unvalidated dependencies. There is no operational
algorithm, measured biological/chemical parameter, or physical experiment in
this package.

The fixed-budget claim would fail if the energy-identity kinetic bound,
ensemble tightness/trace compactness, weak-potential passage, or ground-state
variational comparison fails. The dense-approximant claim would fail if the
ensemble functional/subgradient interface or the cited Brøndsted–Rockafellar
application is inapplicable on (X). The Friedrichs extension would fail if
the source weighted identity, orbital form-domain inclusion, gap, or closed-form
eigenvector step fails. The physical nonlocal construction would fail if its
finite-rank form estimates or ground-space calculation are incorrect.

The next decisive gate is a rigorous quantitative route to the source's
\(\tau\), beginning with an explicit fixed-separation lower bound on the
H\(_2^+\) doublet splitting and then independent numerical bounds for every
remaining uniform margin in the finite sufficient tests. Alternatively, a
separately specified physically structured generalized-Kohn–Sham class must
be attacked directly; the present broad finite-rank construction does not
answer that question. Neither gate was crossed here.
