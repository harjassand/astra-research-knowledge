# Compact scalar regularization exists exactly for commuting observables

Root reconstruction, 2026-10-15, from the first-tilted-moment operation independently discovered by quantum_functional_astra and operator_adversary_v3. The elementary spectral-block proof below was reconstructed directly by root. Their proofs are preserved separately. Mathematical status: internally complete; historical priority, external review and formal verification UNKNOWN.

Let A1,...,Am be bounded self-adjoint operators on any complex Hilbert space, including a nonseparable one. An exact representation means a scalar probability nu on R^m and a normalized positive weak POVM M such that

    Mhat(t)=nuhat(t) exp(i sum_j t_j A_j), every t in R^m.

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent:

1. The operators Aj commute pairwise.
2. Some exact representation has compactly supported scalar noise nu.
3. Some exact representation has compactly supported outcome POVM M.
4. Some exact representation has a smooth compactly supported scalar noise density.

For any fixed exact representation, compactness of nu and M are equivalent. Commutativity is an existence criterion; commuting tuples also have noncompact representations.

## 1. A stronger two-coordinate lemma

Let A,B be bounded self-adjoint. Suppose an exact representation has scalar noise (Z1,Z2), with |Z2|<=S almost surely and E exp(s Z1)<infinity for arbitrarily large positive s (equivalently for every positive s). Then [A,B]=0. Ordinary moments of Z1 alone are insufficient for this argument.

Every projected outcome law is a positive convolution of the matching scalar noise projection with the spectral probability law of the corresponding observable. This follows by uniqueness of characteristic functions, in each state and each direction. In particular the second outcome Y is supported in [-L,L], L=||B||+S, in every state. The POVM effect outside that strip is zero: all its nonnegative quadratic forms vanish.

For finite positive s put m(s,t)=E exp(s Z1+t Z2). The bounded Z2 makes this finite for t near zero. The same positive projected marginals, followed by polarization, give the actual operator exponential-moment identity

    Q(s,t)=integral exp(s X+t Y) M(dX,dY)
          =m(s,t) exp(s A+t B).

This is an identity of bounded positive operators at each real tilt, not an analytic continuation of unacquired moments. The positive normalized tilted POVM is

    M_s(dX,dY)=exp(s X) C_s M(dX,dY) C_s,
    C_s=m(s,0)^(-1/2) exp(-s A/2).

Its Y first moment has norm at most L. Differentiation in t is justified in operator norm by bounded Y and Z2 and the finite moment at s. Duhamel's formula gives

    integral Y M_s = kappa_2(s,0) I + J_s,
    J_s=integral_(-1/2)^(1/2) exp(v s A) B exp(-v s A) dv,

where kappa=log m and |kappa_2(s,0)|<=S. Therefore

    ||J_s|| <= ||B||+2S                                     (1)

uniformly at all permitted positive tilts. No norm-countably-additive operator vector measure is presumed.

## 2. Uniformly bounded tilted means force commutation

Choose any real a and delta>0. Let P=E_A([a+delta,infinity)), Q=E_A((-infinity,a]). On their reducing subspaces set A_H=A|P, A_L=A|Q, X=P B Q. Consider the bounded Sylvester operator

    D(Y)=A_H Y-Y A_L

on bounded maps QH to PH. Its semigroup obeys

    exp(-sD)Y=exp(-sA_H)Y exp(sA_L),
    ||exp(-sD)|| <= exp(-s delta), s>=0,
    ||D|| <= 2||A||.

Spectral projection of the displayed integral for J_s, and integration of the bounded operator exponential, give the exact identity

    (I-exp(-sD))X=s D exp(-sD/2) (P J_s Q).

No inverse of D, spectral eigenvectors, or compression of exp(sA+tB) to a different tuple is used. The triangle inequality and the semigroup bound imply

    (1-exp(-s delta))||X||
       <= 2s||A|| exp(-s delta/2) ||J_s||.                  (2)

Let the allowed positive tilts tend to infinity. Equation (1) makes the right side tend to zero, so P B Q=0 for every separated pair of spectral cuts. Strong convergence as delta decreases to zero gives

    E_A((a,infinity)) B E_A((-infinity,a])=0.

Since B is self-adjoint, the reverse block also vanishes. Thus B commutes with every spectral projection of A, hence with A. This proves the lemma for arbitrary Hilbert spaces.

The independent operator_adversary proof instead uses the entire averaged conjugation J_z, bounds on its real and imaginary axes, Phragmen-Lindelof and Liouville. The present proof supplies an elementary alternative and an explicit finite-s bound.

## 3. Compactness and sufficiency

Compact nu has bounded coordinate projections and all positive exponential moments, so the lemma applied to every pair proves pairwise commutativity.

Conversely, pairwise commuting bounded self-adjoint operators have a joint spectral PVM E, supported in a compact box. Taking nu=delta_0 and M=E gives the exact representation. Alternatively convolve E with any smooth compact scalar probability density; positivity, normalization and the exact multiplier identity are retained. This proves all four existence equivalences.

For a fixed exact representation, compact nu implies each outcome coordinate is bounded by the noise-coordinate bound plus ||Aj||. The union of finitely many zero outside-strip effects is zero, so M is supported in a compact box.

If M is compact, choose any unit state. Its jth projected outcome has bounded support and is the convolution of nu_j with a spectral law supported in [-||Aj||,||Aj||]. Positive convolution cannot cancel a noise tail beyond the outcome bound plus ||Aj||. Thus every nu_j is bounded; finitely many coordinates put nu in a compact box. This also proves the fixed-representation equivalence without assuming moments in advance.

## 4. Consequences and the paid approximation boundary

Within the all-exponential scalar-noise class, a bounded noise projection in direction v implies that A(v) commutes with every A(u). Thus noncentral observable directions require unbounded noise projections. For a noncommuting Pauli tuple, every nonzero scalar-noise projection is unbounded. The heavy unbounded tails in the positive constructions are not merely a weakness of the chosen template.

For a separated block X, equation (2) supplies the quantitative necessary bound

    ||X|| <= [2s||A|| exp(-s delta/2)/(1-exp(-s delta))]
             (||B||+2S).

An approximate representation requires an explicitly bounded error in these *normalized tilted first moments* before this formula can be relaxed. Uniform error on a finite real Fourier grid does not automatically bound exponentially weighted moments. No free analytic continuation, physical impossibility of ordinary finite-resolution measurements, or finite-sample advantage is claimed. Bounded devices can implement approximations, whose truncation, resolution and error contracts must be stated.

Closest inspected prior art proves positivity of the unsmoothed generalized Wigner law iff finite-dimensional observables commute. That argument uses sharp spectral-hyperplane support and does not directly cover nonzero compact scalar smearing. The exact historical status of the stronger smearing criterion remains unresolved; the primary-literature audit is retained separately.

## 5. Subsequent decisive primary comparison: a known theorem implies the result

The priority worker subsequently acquired and read Mitsuru Uchiyama, *Commutativity of selfadjoint operators*, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 161 (1993), 385–392, Theorem 7 on printed page 389. Root independently read the primary theorem and its proof. [Original journal PDF](https://msp.org/pjm/1993/161-2/pjm-v161-n2-p10-s.pdf), SHA256 3fd0fe097881cc1c174bff4194ea71a96cd284584ef6e719b295496d9b09738c.

It supplies commutation from a uniform exponential-order sandwich for A+epsilon X, with X nonnegative bounded and a scalar upper shift. Our exact outcome/noise support bounds give

    exp(-C|t|) exp(sA) <= exp(sA+tB) <= exp(C|t|) exp(sA),
    C=||B||+2S, s>0, t real.

Take X=B+C I>=0, t=s epsilon and a=2C+1>||X||. The sandwich then becomes

    exp(sA) <= exp(s(A+epsilon X)) <= exp(s(A+epsilon a I)),
    every s,epsilon>0.

These satisfy the classical theorem's hypotheses and imply [A,B]=0. Thus the all-Hilbert compact-noise result is already a short corollary of established operator-order machinery. The two direct proofs are useful independent reconstructions and a quantitative elementary route; they must not be promoted into a historically new foundational theorem. Whether the compact-smearing corollary itself was previously stated was not cleared. The precise bounded-direction variant and finite-s estimate retain their explicit scope.
