# Biomolecular capability lead: kinetic design after equilibrium optimization

## Verdict

I found no defensible new biomolecular breakthrough in the audited space. The useful quantitative boundary is that an equilibrium ensemble, even if known exactly, does not certify native-state occupancy at a fixed finite assay time. A two-state reversible model proves this exactly: a common rescaling of folding and unfolding rates preserves the complete equilibrium distribution while driving occupancy at the assay time to zero. This is a model-level design gate, not a new folding theory or a claim about nature.

The stronger capability needed to turn this boundary into design is an acquired sequence-to-pathway map: a state-resolved transition network or barrier model for candidate mutations, calibrated against kinetics, plus a fixed-time native-occupancy or activity assay. Direct prior work already demonstrates kinetic/response-time design in proteins and kinetic control in RNA, so I do not claim the general idea is new. The highest-value next gate is a blinded test of published Top7 packing/cooperativity mutation predictions; the source paper reports computational results, while a later primary study says no Top7 mutants had yet been published with cooperative, intermediate-free folding. Verify the exact construct history before ordering a construct.

## Exact model result and practical use

For a reversible two-state CTMC `U <-> N`, started unfolded, let the folding and unfolding rates be `k_f` and `k_u`. The equilibrium target probability is `pi_N = k_f/(k_f+k_u)`. The exact target-state occupancy at assay time `tau` is

`P(N at tau) = pi_N * (1 - exp(-(k_f+k_u) tau))`.

Replacing both rates with `s k_f` and `s k_u` leaves `pi_N` unchanged but sends the occupancy at a fixed assay time to zero as `s -> 0`. Thus no equilibrium score or target-basin energy gap alone gives a nonzero lower bound on native occupancy at a fixed time. For target occupancy `y < pi_N`, the exact model gate is `k_f+k_u >= -log(1-y/pi_N)/tau`.

The runnable witness in [finite_time_yield.py](finite_time_yield.py) fixes `pi_N=0.95`. At `tau=1`, the rate pairs `(0.95, 0.05)` and `(0.95e-8, 0.05e-8)` give target-state occupancy approximately `0.600515` and `9.5e-9`, respectively, despite identical equilibrium occupancy. This is occupancy at the sampling time, not first-passage probability. The script was run once to evaluate the formula. The derivation is in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

This gives a concrete two-axis design/assay contract: meet the equilibrium target-basin requirement and separately meet the fixed-time rate requirement. In a real protein, the second quantity must be acquired from stopped-flow or single-molecule kinetics (or a validated barrier model); the equation does not identify it from sequence. At 298 K, 95% equilibrium occupancy in a two-basin model requires a target-vs-other basin free-energy advantage of about `kBT log(19) = 1.74 kcal/mol`, but this remains insufficient without the rate threshold. The result does not cover chaperones, aggregation, multi-domain pathways, cotranslational folding, or activity after folding.

## Prior-art boundary and candidate test

Top7 is a useful stress case because it shows why structure and kinetics must be kept separate. Its computationally designed structure was experimentally close to the intended fold, while the 2007 study reported multiple folding phases and a stable nonnative conformation. That study tested 11 point mutants; all retained three kinetic phases. These are direct primary findings, not evidence that the model above describes Top7 as a two-state system. The relevant sources are [Kuhlman et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1089427) and [Scalley-Kim and Baker 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2006.12.042).

The proposed Top7 intervention is itself prior art. Yadahalli and Gosavi (2014) computationally increased cooperativity in structure-based models by improving N-terminal packing so the C-terminal intermediate proceeds, or by weakening C-terminal packing to destabilize that intermediate; the paper reports amino-acid substitutions intended to realize these model changes ([DOI 10.1002/prot.24393](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.24393)). DeGrave et al. (2018) used computational design with biophysical experiments to improve the response time of an engineered Ca²⁺-sensor protein switch, reporting up to a 32-fold increase in switching rate ([DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-03228-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03228-6)). Therefore, mutation-guided kinetic design and barrier-aware objectives are not new at the general level.

A bounded, discriminating follow-up would obtain the exact 2014 Top7 sequence variants and verify whether they were tested experimentally. If they were not, express the predicted combinations alongside wild-type Top7 and static-energy-matched controls. Acquire stopped-flow traces, equilibrium unfolding/structure measurements, and a fixed-time native-occupancy assay. Freeze the variants before seeing the traces; fit the kinetic state model on one condition and predict held-out denaturant or temperature conditions. A useful result would be a prospectively calibrated sequence-to-pathway model that selects a variant with higher native occupancy at a prespecified assay time while preserving equilibrium stability, at matched compute and assay budgets. Failure to remove the relevant slow phase, or loss of native stability/function, falsifies that specific intervention. No sequence was synthesized or tested here.
