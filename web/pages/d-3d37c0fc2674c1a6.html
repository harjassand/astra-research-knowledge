ALL-RANK CASIMIR PROFILE FOR REGULAR SU(3) CARRIERS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally derived; exact finite formulas and scoped constants below.

1. CLAIM AND SCOPE

Let V=V_(a,b) be a regular SU(3) irrep, with N<=a,b<=2N, N>=16, and

  d=dim(V)=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2,  m=min(a,b).

Use Hermitian generators normalized by Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2 and
define

  E(X)=sum_(A=1)^8 ||[T_A,X]||_HS^2,
  Lambda(q)=inf{ E(X)/||X||_HS^2 : X!=0, rank(X)<=q }.

There are absolute constants, for example the deliberately loose pair
c=1/10000 and C=20000, such that

  c(d/q)^(1/3) <= Lambda(q) <= C(d/q)^(1/3),        1<=q<=d/2,
  c(k/d)^(2/3) <= Lambda(d-k) <= C(k/d)^(2/3),     1<=k<=d/2,
  Lambda(d)=0.

The first line is the low-rank branch. The second is the near-full-rank
branch, written in terms of nullity k. In particular, the exponent changes
at full rank. This result concerns the Casimir Dirichlet form on all complex
matrices, not only positive filters. It does not assert a closed-form
optimizer or a leading asymptotic constant.

2. LOW-RANK LOWER BOUND FROM THE STABLE SPECTRAL PROFILE

Write C2(r,s)=(r^2+rs+s^2+3r+3s)/3, and let D(T) be the dimension,
including multiplicities, of the sum of conjugation irreducibles with
Casimir at most T. For Delta=min(a+1,b+1), the stable-range character count
gives

  D(T)<=729 T^3,  1<=T<Delta^2;       D(T)=1, 0<=T<3.             (1)

For reference, the exact stable multiplicity is
  m_(r,s)=1_(r=s mod 3)(min(r,s)+1),  C2(r,s)<Delta^2;
the full derivation and the loose finite bound (1) are in
`profile_asymptotics.txt`.

The invariant-projector/Schmidt-rank inequality gives

  Lambda(q)>=Phi(q):=int_0^infinity [1-qD(T)/d]_+ dT.             (2)

Indeed, for the conjugation projector Pi_T, irreducibility of V gives
Tr_2(Pi_T)=D(T)I/d. The normalized vectorization of a rank-q matrix has
Schmidt rank at most q, so its Pi_T mass is at most qD(T)/d. Integrating
the complementary spectral mass yields (2).

Put H=(d/q)^(1/3), so H>=2^(1/3). If H>=12, choose
alpha=1458^(-1/3). On 0<=T<=alpha H, (1) and D(T)=1 below 3 imply
D(T)<=H^3/2. The cutoff is valid because H<=d^(1/3)<=2N+1 and
alpha H<Delta^2. Therefore Phi(q)>=alpha H/2. If H<12, use D(T)=1 on
[0,3): Phi(q)>=3(1-H^(-3))>=3/2>=H/8. Since alpha/2>1/32, both cases
give

  Lambda(q)>=Phi(q)>=(1/32)(d/q)^(1/3),  1<=q<=d/2.             (3)

3. LOW-RANK UPPER FILTER FROM A PRINCIPAL-CARTAN CAP

Use H0=diag(1,0,-1) in the defining three-dimensional representation.
In a GT basis of V_(a,b), write the depth from the highest H0-weight as

  ell=2x+y+z,  0<=x<=a, 0<=y<=b, 0<=z<=a-x+y.

For 0<=ell<=m, every nonnegative solution is valid, and the exact shell
dimension and cap volume are

  g_ell=floor((ell+2)^2/4),  V_R=sum_(ell=0)^R g_ell.             (4)

For the three positive roots 12, 23, 13, the exact source-shell
conductances are

  c_12(q)=sum_(p=0)^(q-1)(a-p/2)g_p,
  c_23(q)=sum_(p=0)^(q-1)(b-p/2)g_p,
  c_13(q)=sum_(p<=q-2, p=q mod 2)(a+b-p)g_p.                      (5)

The first two roots move depth by one; 13 moves it by two. Set
c(q)=c_12(q)+c_23(q). For 1<=q<=m, the GT/Casimir recurrences imply

  c(q)<=5m q^3,   c_13(q)<=c(q-1)  (2<=q<=m+1).                  (6)

These formulas and their derivation are independently recorded in
`principal_cartan_filter.txt`.

For an integer R<=floor(2m/3), define

  F_R|_(depth ell)=(1-ell/R)_+.

Its rank is exactly V_(R-1). The norm satisfies

  ||F_R||_HS^2=sum_(ell<R) g_ell(1-ell/R)^2 >= R^3/384.           (7)

For example, restrict the sum to ell<=floor(R/2), use
g_ell>=(ell+1)^2/4 and (1-ell/R)^2>=1/4; R=1 also satisfies (7).

The one-step part of the energy is
  E_1=R^(-2)sum_(q=1)^R c(q)<=5mR^2.
For the two-step root, |F_R(q-2)-F_R(q)|<=2/R and (6) gives
  E_13<=4R^(-2)sum_(q=2)^(R+1)c_13(q)
       <=4R^(-2)sum_(q=1)^R c(q)<=20mR^2.
Thus

  E(F_R)/||F_R||_HS^2<=9600 m/R.                                  (8)

To fit every rank budget q<=d/2, put R0=floor(2m/3) and
q0=V_(R0-1). If q<q0, choose the largest R<=R0 with V_(R-1)<=q.
Then q<V_R and the elementary bound V_R<=4.5R^3 gives
R>(q/4.5)^(1/3). Equations (8) and d>=m^3 give
Lambda(q)<=20000(d/q)^(1/3). If q>=q0, the fixed filter F_(R0) has rank
q0<=q; since R0>=m/2, (8) is at most 19200, which is also bounded by
20000(d/q)^(1/3). This proves the low-rank upper branch for every integer
1<=q<=d/2 without extrapolating the cap-shell formula beyond ell=m.

4. NEAR-FULL LOWER BOUND FOR ARBITRARY MATRICES

The first positive Casimir is 3 (the adjoint occurs twice for a,b>0), so
the spectral gap of L(X)=sum_A[T_A,[T_A,X]] on traceless matrices is
lambda_1=3.

Fix 1<=k<d and any nonzero A with rank(A)<=d-k. Choose a rank-k
orthogonal projection P onto a subspace of ker(A), so AP=0. Write
A=aI+X with Tr(X)=0 and P0=P-(k/d)I. Then

  ka=-Tr(P0 X),
  k^2|a|^2<=G(P)E(A),  G(P)=<P0,L^(-1)P0>,
  ||X||_HS^2<=E(A)/3.

Orthogonality of scalar and traceless parts gives

  ||A||_HS^2<=E(A)[dG(P)/k^2+1/3].                               (9)

This bridge holds for complex, nonnormal A; it uses only AP=0 and
self-adjoint spectral calculus for L.

For each cumulative nontrivial Casimir projector Pi_T, vectorization and
Schmidt rank k give

  M(T):=||Pi_T P0||_HS^2<=k^2D(T)/d,
  ||P0||_HS^2=k-k^2/d<=k.                                          (10)

The Green integral identity is
  G(P)=int_3^infinity M(T)T^(-2)dT.
Set kappa=(d/k)^(1/3), K=max(3,kappa). Because k<=d/2,
K<=3kappa<=3d^(1/3)<=3(2N+1)<Delta^2 for N>=16. From (1) and (10),

  G(P)<=729(k^2/d)int_3^K T dT + k/K
       <=365(k^2/d)K^2+k/K
       <=3286 k^(4/3)d^(-1/3).                                    (11)

Substitute (11) in (9), use dG/k^2<=3286(d/k)^(2/3), and absorb
1/3 into this term. Uniformly over every admissible A,

  E(A)/||A||_HS^2 >= (1/3287)(k/d)^(2/3).                         (12)

This is the all-matrix near-full lower bound. It does not rely on a
positive-filter optimizer or coherent-state extremality.

5. NEAR-FULL UPPER FILTERS

For 1<=k<=floor(d/512), use the harmonic principal-cap filter from
`principal_cartan_filter.txt`: nullity V_R>=k, rank d-V_R<=d-k, and

  E(X)/||X||_HS^2<743(k/d)^(2/3).                                 (13)

For larger k<=d/2, use the single global filter
  X=(-T_(H0)/(a+b))_+.
The Weyl element swapping coordinates 1 and 3 sends H0 to -H0, so the
spectrum is symmetric and rank(X)<=d/2<=d-k. The quadratic trace identity
gives

  ||X||_HS^2=Tr(T_(H0)^2)/(2(a+b)^2)
            =d C2(a,b)/(4(a+b)^2)>=d/16.                          (14)

The function x -> (-x/(a+b))_+ is 1/(a+b)-Lipschitz, and each positive
root changes H0-weight by at most 2. The sum of positive-root matrix-element
masses is at most the U(3) quadratic-Casimir trace
d C_U3, where C_U3=(a+b)(a+b+2)+b^2<=3(a+b)^2. Therefore

  E(X)<=4 d C_U3/(a+b)^2<=12d,
  E(X)/||X||_HS^2<=192.                                             (15)

If k>floor(d/512), then k/d>1/512, so (15) is at most
12288(k/d)^(2/3). Together with (13), this proves the near-full upper
branch with C=12288. Combining (3), (8), (12), (13), and (15) gives the
single loose absolute pair stated in Section 1.
