# Independent audit: endpoint read cost and physical cost

The recent-literature context is that worker's programme; our new statements below are deductions, not claims attributed to its literature. Historical novelty is unestablished. ## Interface, convention, and verdict

States are Z/3Z, with row generator Q=S+bS^T-(1+b)I, where S[i,i+1]=1 modulo3. Clockwise jump rate1 fixes physical time units; anticlockwise rate b>0 is unknown. State labels and orientation are supplied. The initial label is known, or obtained with one extra read; its law must be independent of b. Stationary initialization is admissible because this particular family's stationary law is uniform for every b. No independently prepared state rows are needed for the positive protocol below.

After each read choose lambda>0 from retained history and private randomness. Wait an independent Exp(lambda) interval and read the endpoint. Original interface retains endpoint labels and chosen rates, but not the realized intervals, their sum, or other information about them. Such quantities cannot be secretly used for stopping decisions. Initial preparation, rate calibration and passive full state observation are supplied. Resets, state-dependent sensor errors, unknown time units, and hidden states are outside the theorem. The initial read is a cost if not already supplied.

**Verdict: PASS for the original universal Fisher bound and bounded-read adaptive lower bound.** An expected-read extension follows below. The read lower bound does not imply an Omega(b^-2) physical-time or physical-jump lower bound. A complete bracket protocol achieves quadratic reads and only linear physical time/jumps. A separate, newly reconstructed timestamp bound also shows quadratic reads when timestamps are retained. That is an extension with its own proof, not an inference from the original interface.

## Independent exact algebra

Directly invert lambda I-Q, without reading r17's replay. The row0 endpoint law is

p0=(b²+2b lambda+b+lambda²+2lambda+1)/D,

p+=(b²+b+lambda+1)/D,

p-=(b²+b lambda+b+1)/D,

D=3b²+3b lambda+3b+lambda²+3lambda+3.

All three numerators and D are positive for b>=0, lambda>0. The inverse gives row sums1 and translation invariance. Strong Markov plus independent interval clocks gives independent increments for a fixed lambda; adaptively selected lambda gives the same conditional displacement law at every retained history.

Compute I=sum_y (d_b p_y)²/p_y=A/B in factored rational form. Its denominator is

B=(b²+b+lambda+1)(b²+b lambda+b+1)(b²+2b lambda+b+lambda²+2lambda+1)D²>0.

Our independently expanded B-A has exactly60 nonzero integer coefficients, all positive, minimum1. The complete own coefficient certificate is stored in `results/r08_information/cycle5/algebra_and_protocol.json`; `work/r08_information/cycle5/endpoint_audit.py` reconstructs it from Q. Thus I<=1 on the entire declared domain, including b=0 as a one-sided limiting comparison. This is a symbolic polynomial proof, not a parameter grid. At b=0 our expression reduces to the same rational expression in the audited note, with limit1 as lambda increases to infinity.

Entropy production has the convention k_B=1 and stationary rate

sigma(b)=(1-b) log(1/b).

Each of the three oriented edge pairs contributes (1/3)(1-b)log(1/b), so neither a missing factor3 nor an extra factor2 occurs. At c=b/2,

sigma(c)-sigma(b)=log2+(b/2)log[1/(2b)]>=log2 for 0<b<=1/2.

## Adaptive Hellinger bound, including random stopping

Use H²(P,Q)=sum(sqrt(P)-sqrt(Q))²=2(1-affinity). At a fixed rate, the square-root law has derivative squared norm I/4<=1/4. Integrate that vector derivative and use its length bound:

H²(p_b,p_c)<=|b-c|²/4,

so the conditional affinity is at least1-|b-c|²/8. Rate selection, private randomization and stopping kernels are the same parameter-independent kernels at a shared history. Integrating over selected rates preserves this deficit bound. Including private coins explicitly in the transcript can only give the learner more data and makes the shared-kernel statement immediate.

For N bounded reads, conditional affinity recursion yields H²<=N|b-c|²/4. TV<=H under this convention, giving TV<=sqrt(N)|b-c|/2. A stopped procedure with a hard cap can be padded; its recorded stopping decision/length remains a measurable function of the padded transcript.

The same claim extends to a.s. finite random stopping with E_b N and E_c N finite, but it needs a stopped-prefix argument rather than substituting a random N in a fixed-N bound. Let mu_k and nu_k be the subprobability distributions of retained prefixes just before read k+1, restricted to alive paths. At each alive prefix, the affinity lost by making one observation is at most |b-c|²/8 times its shared prefix mass. Stop/randomization actions do not lose affinity. Telescoping the tree through depth m gives

H²(complete stopped transcripts through m)
 <= |b-c|²/4 sum_{k<m} integral sqrt(dmu_k dnu_k).

For an unfinished prefix at depth m use a common absorbing truncation symbol; its laws converge in total variation to the actual stopped transcript since stopping is a.s. under both hypotheses. The tail probabilities vanish, so affinity converges too. Monotone summation and Cauchy-Schwarz on the disjoint union of all prefix levels give

sum_k integral sqrt(dmu_k dnu_k)
 <=sqrt[(sum_k mu_k(total))(sum_k nu_k(total))]
 =sqrt(E_b N E_c N).

Consequently

H²(L_b,L_c)<=|b-c|²/4 sqrt(E_b N E_c N),

TV(L_b,L_c)<=|b-c|/2 (E_b N E_c N)^(1/4).

If an estimator has additive error epsilon<log2/2 and success >=.9 under b and c=b/2, thresholding halfway between the two entropy values gives test errors <=.1. Hence TV>=.8, so

sqrt(E_b N E_(b/2) N)>=10.24/b².

In particular at least one expected read count is this large. The original hard-cap statement N>=10.24/b² is recovered. No one-sided claim about E_b N alone follows from this affinity argument. If a procedure never stops under one hypothesis, these finite-mean assumptions fail; if stopped time itself is observed it belongs to the stronger timestamp interface below.

## Separately audited timestamp extension

r17 sent a new route; the following reconstructs its steps. For a known lag t>0 let p_b(t) be the displacement law. Clockwise and reverse clocks are independent Poisson processes, so p_b(t) is the mod3 convolution of Pois(t) with -Pois(bt). Differentiation gives d_b p=t(S_- -I)p. For a signed vector v and a nonnegative noise kernel K, weighted Cauchy gives

sum_y [(vK)(y)]²/(pK)(y)<=sum_x v(x)²/p(x).

Because shifts commute with convolution, I_t(b)<=I_t(0).

At b=0, a backwards displacement shift is a +2 shift modulo3. Before wrapping, the likelihood ratio of Pois(t)+2 to Pois(t) at K is (K)_2/t². Poisson factorial moments give

E[(K)_2²]=t⁴+4t³+2t²,

therefore its chi-square divergence is4/t+2/t². Wrapping is a Markov kernel, hence I_t(0)<=t²(4/t+2/t²)=4t+2<=6 for t<=1.

For t>=1, write r=exp(-3t/2). Three-state Fourier diagonalization gives min_y p0(t,y)>=(1-2r)/3 and sum_y[p0(t,y-1)-p0(t,y)]²=2r². Thus

I_t(0)<=6t² exp(-3t)/(1-2exp(-3t/2)).

The numerator decreases for t>=1 and the denominator increases, so its maximum there is6 exp(-3)/(1-2exp(-3/2))<1. Together I_t(b)<=6 for every t>0 and b>=0. This proves the new uniform bound, without claiming the suggested sharper constant2.

The Hellinger proof now has factor6. For any parameter-independent adaptive lag choice, including random lags whose realized timestamps are observed and future timings adapt to them,

TV<=sqrt(6N)|b-c|/2,

or TV<=sqrt(6)|b-c|/2 (E_b N E_c N)^(1/4) for integrable stopping. With c=b/2, quadratic reads persist with constant10.24/6. The clock realization has the same conditional law under shared histories and contributes no parameter information itself. It is not permissible to choose lags using unobserved path information, parameter-dependent timing or event-triggered sensing without charging that different data access.

## A positive protocol with all three costs

Supply a nontrivial local bracket b in[a,2a], 0<a<=1/4. This is a scale promise, not a free acquired estimate; scale acquisition for completely unknown b is not included. Fix lambda=1/a. Collect N successive endpoint increments, retaining only the count K of reverse increments. These increments are iid Bernoulli with success probability q(b)=p-(b,1/a). No prepared-state row sampling is needed.

For b<=2a,

q(b)<=a²[b²+b/a+b+1]<=4a².

Differentiation gives

q'(b)=lambda(lambda+3)(2b+lambda+1)/D².

For a<=1/4, b<=1/2,

D/lambda²=1+3a+3ab+3a²(1+b+b²)<=157/64<5/2,

so q'(b)>=4a/25. Clip K/N to [q(a),q(2a)] and invert the strictly increasing q to obtain bhat in[a,2a]. Projection does not enlarge error. If |K/N-q(b)|<=tau, then |bhat-b|<=25tau/(4a).

For u in[a,2a], |sigma'(u)|=log(1/u)+1/u-1<=2/a. Therefore entropy error is <=25tau/(2a²). Set tau=2epsilon a²/25. For epsilon<=1, Bernstein's Bernoulli bound and Var<=q<=4a² give

Pr(|K/N-q|>tau)
 <=2 exp[-N tau²/(8a²+2tau/3)]
 <=2 exp[-N epsilon²a²/(1250+25/3)].

Thus the complete protocol

N=ceil[1260 log(2/delta)/(epsilon² a²)]

returns sigma(bhat) within epsilon with probability>=1-delta uniformly on the bracket in the stated mathematical probability interface. The large constant is a conservative proof constant, not optimized numerical design. A numerical implementation, a synthetic aggregate-count diagnostic and exact algebra are in `work/r08_information/cycle5/endpoint_audit.py`. Its80-step floating inverse is a diagnostic implementation, not a certified arbitrary-precision version of the entire uniform theorem; a deployable certificate must reserve a precision margin or return an interval evaluated with enclosed arithmetic.

Charged resources: N endpoint reads (plus an initial read if necessary); N comparisons/count updates and a scalar inversion; O(logN) count storage, plus probability arithmetic. Expected physical time is N/lambda=Na. Because the CTMC escape rate is exactly1+b in every state, expected physical jumps are (1+b)Na. Endpoint reads may report the same state and are not themselves physical jumps. Calibration/preparation prices and an instrument's maximum observation rate remain supplied/unmeasured. This shows simultaneously optimal orders: reads O(a^-2), time O(a^-1), jumps O(a^-1), at fixed epsilon,delta. It does not claim the globally best constants or an optimal rate when other sensor costs are present.

## Physical-time lower bound and the timing tradeoff

Full jump-resolved paths up to a stopping time T are a stronger experiment. Under b versus c=b/2, their stopped log likelihood is Z_-(T)log2-(b/2)T, with clockwise rate and initial law identical. For integrable stopping, E_b Z_-(T)=b E_b T by Poisson compensator accounting. Observation-clock choices are parameter-independent conditional kernels, so they add no likelihood. Data processing implies retained-transcript KL <=b(log2-1/2)E_b T. A test with errors<=.1 has KL>=kl(.9||.1)=.8log9. Hence

E_b T>=[.8log9/(log2-1/2)]/b.

This is Omega(1/b), not Omega(1/b²). With constant escape rate it also lower-bounds expected physical jumps by (1+b) times this expression. Jump-resolved access attains that order with reverse Poisson counts; the bracket endpoint protocol above attains it too, despite many more readings.

The algebra shows an explicit asymptotic tradeoff. Set b=ra and lambda=c/a, with r,c fixed positive and a tending to0. Then q_-=a²(r/c+1/c²)+O(a³), q'_-=a/c+O(a²), while the other endpoint probabilities contribute vanishing Fisher information. Therefore

I_(c/a)(ra) ->1/(1+rc).

At b small with lambda=gamma/b, Fisher per read tends to1/(1+gamma). More frequent reading sacrifices a constant factor in read information but reduces elapsed time: information per unit time tends to gamma/[b(1+gamma)], approaching full-path information rate1/b as gamma grows. If lambda increases with b lambda tending to0, Fisher per read tends to1, but the associated quadratic-read physical time exceeds order1/b. The family lambda=c/a with constant c is order-optimal for both resources on a known bracket, not a claim that one timing criterion has a unique optimal lambda.

Mechanism: a reverse endpoint may be a genuine rare reverse jump or two clockwise jumps. Exp(lambda) averaging gives false-reverse background of orderlambda^-2. Selecting lambda on the inverse rare-rate scale keeps this background comparable to the genuine reverse probability b/lambda. The hidden path ambiguity limits information per reading, while sufficiently frequent readings preserve the order of information per physical time.

Downstream consequence: distinguish sensor-read budget from experiment-duration budget in rare reverse-rate measurements. A quadratic read requirement does not by itself establish an experiment-time penalty against jump sensing. An actual instrument with maximum rate, state misclassification, missing orientation or dead time changes the interface and must be modeled.
