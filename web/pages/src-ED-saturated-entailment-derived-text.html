  Rare network failures via saturated entailment: proof
                                                    candidate
                                                     12 October 2026

Status. This is an internally derived, unreviewed proof candidate for a polynomial-time relative
approximation scheme for the probability of an odd-signed cycle in an independently retained signed
graph. It includes a spectral-gap argument, an annealing and bit-cost contract, and a planar two-terminal
failure corollary. Originality is not established. There is no external or formal certification and no
practical speed benchmark.

The candidate changes the marked state space: an odd cycle anywhere makes every pair label feasible.
This saturation is essential to the proof. The argument does not establish rapid mixing for an unsaturated
affine-fiber chain. The primary prior framework is Feng, Fu and Guo, Approximating two-terminal
network reliability, arXiv:2608.02523, especially its root-layer flow and comparison lemmas [1].

7 Zero and certain probabilities and loops

Remove edges of probability zero. If the probability-one subgraph is unbalanced, return U                       =1. Otherwise
choose a consistent sign gauge on each of its connected components and contract those components. The
remaining random edges stay independent and have probabilities strictly between zero and one; their
signs are adjusted by the gauge. Contraction can create parallel edges and loops. If the resulting full graph
is balanced, return U     =  0.

A positive loop never belongs to a witness and can be discarded for the target probability, or
independently restored when a complete physical sample is desired. A negative loop has                         Ae =(0  ,1) and is
an odd certificate of length one. DFS tests it on inspection; its reverse certificate route goes from o
directly to zero. The label walk exchanges 0 and o, or flips a pair’s sign. Presence of a negative loop
makes every label feasible, so (1), (2), the replay injection and the capacity argument are unchanged. This
closes the multigraph and endpoint cases used by the planar corollary.

8 Planar two terminal failure corollary

Let a finite plane graph have terminals s,         t and independent physical edge failures with supplied rational
probabilities f   e. If the terminals are disconnected even when every edge works, failure probability is one.
Otherwise fix any simple primal s–t path P.
Construct the planar dual. Give dual edge e* sign one exactly when its primal edge belongs to P, and
retain it independently with probability f         e. Dual loops and parallel edges are retained. Then
    {sandt   are disconnected by failed edges}={the retained signed dual contains an odd cycle}.                          (11)

To prove the forward direction, failed edges contain a separating cut ∂S. Its dual is Eulerian and has total
signature |∂   S ∩  P |mod   2 =1. A cycle decomposition therefore contains an odd cycle of failed dual edges.
Conversely, a simple odd dual cycle crosses P oddly and separates s from t by planar cycle-cut duality.
All crossed primal edges are failed, so no working terminal path remains. A dual loop represents a primal
bridge and obeys the same argument.

Consequently, the candidate signed-graph FPRAS gives a candidate FPRAS for planar two-terminal
unreliability, using polynomial preprocessing and the unchanged failure probabilities. It estimates failure
relatively and directly. A supplied planar embedding, or polynomial-time embedding construction, is
required. The reduction is elementary; no novelty is asserted. It gives no general nonplanar unreliability
result or consequence for general cographic cuts, and it does not cover correlated failures.

9 Finite checks and remaining validation

Two finite checks support debugging of the argument; neither substitutes for the general proof.

The first enumerated 143,528 base paths and 517,912 transition encodings across all eight gauge-
inequivalent signings of K      4, a remote odd triangle, a signed parallel-edge cycle, and a lollipop with a
shortcut. The balanced K       4 signing was detected separately. At        p=1   / 2, checks covered prefix feasibility,
complementary certificates, omitted-edge injectivity, replay reconstruction and exact root load-to-
capacity ratios. Every reported check passed, and the measured ratios stayed below 24                     N  m.

A separate implementation checked an odd five-cycle, a remote path plus odd triangle, competing odd
triangles and a negative loop at       p=1   /2, 0.05 and 0.001. It enumerated 157,956 routes across those 12
cases, including U    = 10−15. Reported maximum load-to-capacity ratios divided by N                  m ranged from
approximately 3.82 to 10.10, below the proposed bound 24. These were small-instance route and
congestion checks, not a full annealed sampler benchmark or a test of all rational probability
assignments.

Independent mathematical review is still needed for the general flow construction, its composition with
the comparison lemma and the complete randomized algorithm. The result remains a proof candidate.
No claim of external certification, established originality, practical acceleration or coverage of arbitrary
binary XOR systems follows from these checks. General XOR systems need not have this polynomial-size
family of bounded-defect exploration certificates.
