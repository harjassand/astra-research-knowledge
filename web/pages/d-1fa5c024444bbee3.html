# An exact locality–reusability benchmark for conserved-charge many-body states

Date: 2026-10-06. Status: complete elementary construction and sharp selected-instrument theorem; finite replays pass; independent audit passes, with 238 exact block/exterior/record fixtures and 9 rational graph fixtures in `notes/r11_logic/cycle5/EXCLUSION_RECOVERY_AUDIT.md` and `results/r11_logic/cycle5/independent_exact_replay.json`. Historical novelty is unverified. This is a commuting charge-sector benchmark, not a theorem about arbitrary interacting quantum phases, not a replacement for known thermal shadow tomography, and not a new no-broadcasting theorem.

## 1. Question, interface, and relevance

Can a single many-body specimen supply reusable local measurement records, and how do the recovery region and physical dynamics constrain their information? Separate two targets: the specimen's fixed charge density q=k/n, and the preparation parameter p of an ensemble that randomly chooses its charge. These have different attainable precisions.

Our exact model has n qubits, computational-basis occupation ηx∈{0,1}, and initial density matrix

    σ(n,k) = [binom(n,k)]^(-1) Σ|η|=k |η><η|.

The unknown is k (unless a theorem explicitly conditions on it). The detector measures only ηa at one designated site a; it is the projective binary instrument P1=|1><1|a, P0=I−P1. A recovery channel R acts only on a chosen m-site block B containing a, with identity action on its complement C. The channel, control randomness, and detector are independent of k. All registers retained as measurement records are counted as records; the permutation seed and unused classical control randomness are discarded in the minimal-record protocol.

The recovery error is the **instrument** trace-norm defect

    ε(R;σ) = Σb=0,1 || R(Pb σ Pb) − pb σ ||1,
    pb = Tr(Pb σ).

This is Chen's measurement version, Definition I.5 / Eq. (10), specialized to this one instrument. It is not the operator version requiring every arbitrary local K. Trace distance convention: ||·||1 is unhalved, while classical TV is half the l1 norm.

One initial n-qubit state is supplied. State preparation is not solved or declared free in total cost. Access to arbitrary block permutations is an explicitly stronger control interface than a fixed nearest-neighbor autonomous dynamics; the latter is analyzed separately below. No thermodynamic bath with an unknown p is supplied. Every control channel in the unknown-p comparison is p-independent.

## 2. Sharp theorem: optimal recovery at a given support size

**Our theorem, reconstructed from elementary conditional laws.** Let q=k/n, n>1 and 1≤m≤n. Let S be hypergeometric: drawing m sites without replacement from n sites with k occupied. Then

    min_R supported on B ε(R;σ(n,k))
       = εopt(n,k,m) = 2 E|S/m−q|,
    Pr(S=s)=binom(m,s)binom(n−m,k−s)/binom(n,k).

A single attaining channel, independent of k, is the uniform permutation twirl on B,

    R_B(X) = (1/m!) Σπ∈Sym(B) Uπ X Uπ†.

No optimization over exponentially many channels is required to use this construction.

**Lower bound, valid for every CPTP recovery on B.** Trace out B. Such a recovery cannot change the subnormalized exterior state on C. Given an exterior configuration c, the block has exactly s=k−|c| particles and is uniform over that charge slice. Therefore Pr(ηa=1|c)=s/m. The exterior part of the outcome-1 defect has l1 norm E|S/m−q|. The outcome-0 defect has the same norm. Trace-norm contraction under partial trace gives the displayed lower bound. The argument even allows the competitor to know k and to use arbitrary ancillas inside B; it uses only support locality.

**Attainment.** Twirling the block after outcome 1 gives exactly

    R_B(P1 σ P1) = σ · (S_B/m),

where S_B is the diagonal block charge operator. Indeed, for every fixed exterior c the permutation averages the marked occupation over all m block sites, assigning weight s/m to each state in the s-shell. Similarly R_B(P0 σ P0)=σ(1−S_B/m). The two trace norms are E|S/m−q|. This independently reconstructs both sides, with no appeal to a quantum Gibbs recovery theorem.

The optimal defect obeys

    εopt ≤ 2 sqrt(v),
    v = Var(S/m) = q(1−q)(n−m)/[m(n−1)].

The upper bound is Cauchy–Schwarz. It is not a uniform reverse bound at the endpoints q=0,1. An elementary iterated-limit asymptotic is sufficient to exhibit the reservoir-size scale: first n→∞, k/n→q∈(0,1), with m fixed, so S converges to Bin(m,q) by the displayed mass formula; then m→∞. The iid central-limit theorem and uniform integrability (the normalized second moment equals 1) give εopt/sqrt(v)→sqrt(8/π). We make no uniform finite-n CLT claim. Full support m=n gives zero defect and exact repeatability for every charge k under the same twirl. For fixed m and n→∞ at q∈(0,1), the optimal defect stays positive even though pairwise occupation covariance is −q(1−q)/(n−1)→0. Small-small clustering cannot substitute for the all-exterior clustering used in the recent strong-Markov paper.

## 3. Exact record law and finite-reservoir precision

After each detector readout apply R_B. Conditional on the conserved block charge S=s, every branch returns the original conditional block shell and the exterior shell. Consequently, for every r≥1 the detector record has exactly the law

    Q_r(b1,…,br)=Σs Pr(S=s) (s/m)^Σb (1−s/m)^(r−Σb).

The proof is induction using R_B(Pb σ_s Pb)=p_b(s) σ_s. Thus the bits are iid **conditional on S**, but the unconditional record is a mixture of iid laws. Its empirical mean qhat is unbiased for global q and has exact mean-square error

    E(qhat−q)^2 = v + [q(1−q)−v]/r.

The law of total variance proves this immediately. Its limiting floor v is a property of this record channel, not an assertion that every quantum or spatially larger experiment has the same floor. If m=n, the floor vanishes and arbitrary precision in the discrete specimen density is possible. If m<n, all detector records only refine the already acquired block charge S. Reading S directly, followed by classical iid simulation, gives exactly the same record experiment. Under the arbitrary controlled all-to-all SWAP primitives used to implement twirling, this comparator already uses the same single detector: route each original block bit to the detector using m reads and m−1 controlled swaps, and sum the readings. A final block twirl prepares its conditional uniform shell. Generate Ber(S/m) records classically, then discard S. This reproduces even the final joint detector-record/quantum-state experiment. The comparator uses the same primitives and can dominate unlimited repeated twirling. Under autonomous nearest-neighbor evolution without routing control, that comparator is unavailable; its access must not be conflated with the distinct trajectory interface below.

The unlimited record does not preserve the original full state conditionally: its posterior over S changes, along with correlations to the exterior. The marginal state averaged over all outcomes is preserved, because Σb R_B(Pb σ Pb)=σ. This averaged preservation is strictly weaker than independent branch recovery.

## 4. Unknown grand-canonical preparation parameter

Take ρ_p = diag(1−p,p)^⊗n, 0<p<1, with **unknown p**, and use full n-site twirling and the local detector. The charge K is Bin(n,p). Conditional K=k the record is Ber(k/n)^⊗r. Therefore

    E(phat−p)^2 = p(1−p)[1/n + (1−1/n)/r].

The one-step strong instrument defect is exactly

    ε = 2 E|K/n−p|,

while ordinary averaged recovery is exact. The full record is the classical Markov experiment p→K→record. Its Fisher information satisfies

    I_record(p) ≤ I_K(p) = n/[p(1−p)]

for every r. To see this without invoking a quantum metrology theorem, differentiate the record probabilities: its score equals E[score_K(p)|record]. Jensen gives the bound, since score_K=(K−np)/[p(1−p)]. Direct measurement of K is a sufficient statistic and attains I_K. As r→∞, K/n becomes identifiable from the record and the information approaches this bound. Exact asymptotic posterior identification follows because the n+1 possible Bernoulli rates are distinct; no numerical convergence claim is needed.

A general p-independent adaptive quantum protocol on the same ρ_p, with p-independent ancillas and channels, cannot exceed this information: the diagonal input can first be viewed as a classical n-bit draw η, and every quantum output law is a p-independent stochastic channel from η. Its score is again a conditional expectation of the input score. Conversely, a bath or control parameter depending on p would introduce a further information source, outside this interface. This is an information bound and an exact empirical-mean variance formula; it is not a claim that all biased estimators have a universal pointwise mean-square lower bound equal to 1/I.

There is also a uniform-risk statement that includes biased estimators. K is sufficient: conditional on K, the input configuration is a uniform weight-K string, independent of p. Thus K Blackwell-dominates every permitted experiment. Under the prior p~Uniform[0,1], its predictive law is K~Uniform{0,…,n}, with posterior Beta(K+1,n−K+1). The posterior variance is

    (K+1)(n−K+1)/[(n+2)²(n+3)].

Averaging this polynomial over K gives exactly 1/[6(n+2)]. Bayes squared-error risk is minimized by the posterior mean, and a garbling cannot improve it. Therefore, for every estimator based on every permitted experiment,

    sup_0<p<1 E_p[(phat−p)²] ≥ 1/[6(n+2)].

Directly observing K and returning K/n gives supremum risk 1/(4n). The preparation-parameter minimax mean-square scale is therefore Θ(1/n), even with unlimited p-independent recovery, readouts, control time, and ancillary registers. This theorem concerns one n-spin initial state; it does not count a supplied p-dependent bath as an independent control.

**Exact classical minimax refinement, supplied and independently checked in r11's audit.** Put a=√n/2 and use estimator (K+a)/(n+2a). Its risk is

    [n p(1−p)+a²(1−2p)²]/(n+2a)²
       = 1/[4(√n+1)²],

constant for every p. It is the posterior mean under the Beta(a,a) prior, so its integrated risk is the Bayes optimum for that prior. A constant-risk Bayes estimator is minimax; hence observing K has exact minimax squared-error risk 1/[4(√n+1)²]. Every permitted p-independent quantum protocol has at least this minimax risk by the same garbling argument. Direct K observation attains it. These are established binomial decision-theory mechanisms; the local many-body interpretation does not create historical novelty.

For the full twirl record, convexity of relative entropy and D(Ber(u)||Ber(p))≤(u−p)^2/[p(1−p)] give

    D(Q_r || Ber(p)^⊗r) ≤ r/n,
    TV(Q_r, Ber(p)^⊗r) ≤ min(1,sqrt[r/(2n)]).

The divergence inequality follows from log x≤x−1. This is often much sharper than the generic r ε bound. It still shows that accurate estimation of an ensemble parameter and unlimited iid record approximation are distinct tasks.

## 5. Physical local dynamics: exact recovery and variance via one-particle diffusion

Replace arbitrary twirling by known nearest-neighbor swap dynamics on a finite connected graph G of n sites. Each undirected edge swaps the two occupations at rate D; this is the symmetric exclusion process on the fixed charge shell. In a quantum register it can be implemented as the random-SWAP Lindbladian

    L_quantum(X)=D Σ{x,y} [SWAPxy X SWAPxy − X].

Our initial state and detector remain diagonal, so this embedding is exactly the classical exclusion process. We do not claim noncommuting quantum measurement behavior from this calculation. Let L_G be the positive one-particle graph Laplacian including rate D, p_t=e^(−t L_G), and χ=q(1−q)n/(n−1).

**Exact branch recovery identity.** With M_t the exclusion evolution,

    M_t(P1 σ P1) = σ Σx p_t(a,x)ηx.

Detailed balance makes the evolved density relative to σ equal to the backwards observable P_t ηa. Directly applying the exclusion generator to ηa yields the one-particle random-walk generator; uniqueness of the finite linear ODE gives P_t ηa=Σx p_t(a,x)ηx. This is a proof for every finite graph, not a reliance on a cutoff theorem.

Hence the selected-instrument defect is exactly

    ε_t = 2 E_shell |Σx p_t(a,x)ηx−q|
         ≤ 2 sqrt{χ[p_(2t)(a,a)−1/n]}.

The variance identity uses Cov_shell(ηx,ηy)=q(1−q) on the diagonal and −q(1−q)/(n−1) off-diagonal; Σx p_t(a,x)=1 and symmetry give Σx p_t(a,x)^2=p_(2t)(a,a).

**Observable cost.** A nondestructive occupation detector at a may continuously record the classical diagonal trajectory. Its stationary covariance is

    Cov(ηa(t),ηa(0)) = χ[p_t(a,a)−1/n].

For qhat_T=T^(-1)∫0^T ηa(t)dt,

    Var(qhat_T) = (2χ/T²)∫0^T (T−t)[p_t(a,a)−1/n]dt
               ≤ 2χ (L_G^+)_(a,a)/T.

With one-particle eigenpairs (λj,uj), j≥1,

    Var(qhat_T)=(2χ/T²) Σj |uj(a)|²
                 [T/λj − (1−e^(−λj T))/λj²].

No inverse global mixing time is substituted for this exact local spectral measure. The upper bound is a rigorous mean-square guarantee. A desired failure probability δ can be obtained via Chebyshev at cost T≥2χ(L_G^+)aa/(δ τ²); we do not silently claim logarithmic δ dependence. Independent repetitions would require further initial specimens or an additional decorrelation protocol.

For a cycle of n≥3 sites, eigenvalues are 4D sin²(πj/n) and |uj(a)|²=1/n. The elementary cosecant-squared sum gives

    (L_G^+)aa=(n²−1)/(12 D n),
    Var(qhat_T) ≤ q(1−q)(n+1)/(6 D T).

The coefficient n replaces the n² scale of the longest-wavelength one-particle relaxation time. For a d-dimensional torus of side L with n=L^d,

    (L_G^+)aa=(1/L^d) Σz≠0 [4D Σi sin²(π zi/L)]^(-1).

Using signed momenta |zi|≤L/2, the inequalities 2|zi|/L≤sin(π|zi|/L)≤π|zi|/L compare each summand with L²/(D|z|²). Counting lattice shells gives order L/D in d=1, log(L)/D in d=2, and 1/D in fixed d≥3. Thus local estimator variance is bounded independently of volume for d≥3 even as the longest-wavelength mode remains diffusive. These are established exclusion/Green-function mechanisms; no historical novelty is claimed.

**Finite detector schedule.** An ideal projective occupation measurement every Δ>0 also leaves the underlying classical diagonal dynamics intact. For r calls, with sample mean over times 0,Δ,…,(r−1)Δ, its exact variance is

    Var(qhat_r) = (χ/r²) Σj |uj(a)|²
                     [r+2 Σh=1^(r−1) (r−h)e^(−λj hΔ)]
                 ≤ q(1−q)/r + 2χ (L_G^+)aa/(rΔ).

The first formula sums all covariance pairs. The bound uses the geometric-series sum and 1/(e^x−1)≤1/x. Thus neither infinite observation rate nor a continuously monitored detector is required for a finite-call mean-square guarantee. Detector infidelity remains a separate assumption. This schedule uses r detector calls and about |E|D(r−1)Δ attempted swap gates, plus the initial state preparation.

**Control and readout cost.** The time bound measures physical evolution time only. The all-edge random-SWAP implementation attempts about |E| D T gates (null equal-occupation swaps still count if the schedule does not know the state). A continuous ideal nondemolition detector is an additional access assumption for the continuum formula, but the finite schedule above suffices instead. Detecting ηa by observing it after every edge event requires O(|E| D T) detector calls, rather than zero-cost continuous monitoring. For a digital simulator these extensive costs can remove an apparent walltime benefit. In an autonomous analog system, all edge updates happen physically in parallel; its engineering and energetic costs remain relevant.

The exact twirl on an m-site block can be sampled using Fisher–Yates: m−1 swap gates under all-to-all connectivity and O(m log m) expected fresh random bits, with unbiased bounded-integer sampling implemented by rejection if necessary. A nearest-neighbor path realizes an arbitrary sampled permutation with O(m²) swaps in the worst case. The random seed is discarded, not treated as free observed data. The n-site initial shell occupies n qubits. With the same arbitrary controlled swap access and the same one-site detector, routing permits m-read charge acquisition as described above. Thus measuring charge once and simulating records classically is an already available comparator in that interface; the sharp defect theorem remains a benchmark rather than a total-cost advantage over this sufficient-statistic acquisition.

## 6. All-local-Kraus positive construction under a stronger global interface

To avoid mistaking selected occupation repeatability for the full strong operator property, there is also a simple **global** exact construction. Choose a known set C of charge values separated by more than 2t. For any operator K supported on at most t sites, K σ(n,k) K† has charge support in [k−t,k+t]. These windows are disjoint for k∈C. Define a channel that measures total charge j, decodes its unique window k (choose any fixed output on unassigned j), and prepares the uniform shell σ(n,k). Then

    R(K σ(n,k) K†) = Tr(K σ(n,k) K†) σ(n,k)

for every k∈C and every such K, including complex noncommuting K. The proof is simply the charge-support property and the disjoint windows. The map is CPTP and independent of the unknown k. It is entanglement breaking; no unknown coherent logical state is preserved. Global charge measurement and shell preparation have to be paid for. One explicit digital implementation measures all n bits, computes the charge, and prepares an n-bit weight-k string followed by a uniform permutation. O(n) readouts, O(n) resets/gates plus permutation costs, O(n log n) control bits, and O(log n) working storage suffice with all-to-all swaps. This is a classical error-correcting charge code embedded in a quantum state family, not a new quantum code construction.

## 7. Exact phase-mixture defect and prior art

If a fixed instrument and recovery exactly return σ1 and σ2 branchwise, then for σ=πσ1+(1−π)σ2 and binary outcome-1 probabilities q1,q2,

    ε(R;σ)=2π(1−π)|q1−q2| ||σ1−σ2||1.

Expand each branch using linearity; its difference from p_b σ is ±π(1−π)(q1−q2)(σ1−σ2). This is an exact strengthening in this special exact-constituent case of the recent paper's local extremality estimate. In the shell construction the states are orthogonal, so ||σ1−σ2||1=2. This observation is not novel by itself: it is covariance algebra consistent with no information without disturbance / Koashi–Imoto.

Likewise, if two states under the same recovery produce different exact iid detector laws for arbitrarily many rounds, trace-distance contraction forces the initial states to be orthogonal. The proof uses distinguishability of iid laws as r→∞; it is the standard no-imprinting boundary, not a discovery.

## 8. What is complete, what it can change, and the exact next gap

Complete: sharp locality-dependent optimum for a selected binary occupation instrument; explicit k-independent attaining channel; exact reusable record law and finite-reservoir precision; unknown-p data-processing/Fisher bound; finite-graph local-dynamics variance and recovery formula; global sparse-charge all-Kraus example. The replay checks explicit finite diagonal operator maps, arbitrary complex local contractions, full exclusion generators versus one-particle reductions, record distributions, variances, and Fisher information. They validate fixtures only. Proofs above establish the general scoped statements.

Concrete downstream use: this gives an adversarial calibration benchmark for claims of single-copy many-body tomography or finite-reservoir reusability. Given block size, compute εopt and the exact estimator floor before designing a local recovery protocol. Given a graph and detector location, compute its Laplacian pseudoinverse diagonal rather than paying a generic mixing-time bound. A strong branchwise recovery objective can be strictly more expensive than estimating one observable from its stationary trajectory, as already anticipated by thermal-trajectory prior art.

Falsifiable next step: take a truly noncommuting, charge-conserving Lindbladian with a known stationary charge-shell state and a nondestructive local detector whose observable lies in a closed low-dimensional Heisenberg subspace. Either derive an exact/controlled one-particle-like spectral measure and preserve full measurement back-action costs, or exhibit a term that prevents closure. The load-bearing missing lemma is a controlled closure of the detector observable and its branch-conditioned state under noncommuting dynamics. No assertion that locality, an area law, pairwise clustering, or agreement of finite simulations supplies that lemma. A competitive many-observable theorem would additionally need a same-access cost advantage over the September thermal protocol; this is not established.
