# Log-frequency regularity discriminator

7 October 2026. **Proposed deduction, separate independent audit pending.** Builds on `WEIGHTED_REAL_LANDIS_CONSTRUCTION.md`; it is not an author claim in FI2608.00802v1 and does not acquire an unknown potential. Historical novelty UNKNOWN.

## Proposed statement

For every n≥3 there exist real smooth u≠0 and V on R^n with −Δu+Vu=0 such that

    |u(x)|≤C exp(−c |x| log(2+|x|)),
    |D^m V(x)|≤C_m (log(2+|x|))^(m+3/2) (1+|x|)^(−1/2),
                         every integer m≥0.

Here D^m means each Cartesian derivative of total order m, with its own finite constant. Thus V and every fixed-order derivative tend to zero at infinity. No uniform dependence on m, Gevrey/analytic estimate, prescribed constants, compact support, or relative spatial slow variation is asserted. In particular the bound is a^mκ, not κR^(−m). The solution has decay faster than exp(−A r) for every fixed A, but this is not exp(−r^(1+ε)) for a fixed ε>0. Thus the all-order derivative statement discriminates exponential unique continuation, not every formulation of a fixed-power weak Landis condition.

If correct, the construction excludes restoring exponential Landis merely by requiring that the real smooth potential and all its derivatives be bounded, or even vanish at infinity. It does not exclude symbol estimates with one inverse-radius gain per derivative, analytic hypotheses, sign conditions, or a potential bound O(r^(−1/2)) without the logarithmic loss.

## Scales and envelope

Take K>e^4, eventually large, R=(K²+|x|²)^(1/2), and

    a=log R,
    ℓ=√(R/a), κ=a^(3/2)/√R,
    η=κ/a²=1/√(Ra),
    Φ=R log R−R−(K log K−K), E=e^(−Φ).

Then a/ℓ=a²ℓ/R=κ, ℓ/R=1/(aℓ)=η, and all small parameters are bounded by their values at K. For every fixed j≥1,

    |D^j a|≤C_j a R^(−j),
    |D^j ℓ|≤C_j ℓ R^(−j),
    |D^j κ|≤C_j κ R^(−j),
    |D^j Φ|≤C_j a R^(1−j),
    |D^j E|≤C_j a^j E.

The envelope identities are exact:

    ∇Φ=(a/R)x, |∇Φ|≤a,
    |∇Φ|²−a²=−K²a²/R²,
    |ΔΦ|≤C a/R.

Every power/log weight above and E are uniformly comparable on balls of radius ρ/a, ρ≤1. The constants for the particular two repair weights κ and a²/R² are independent of large K. The inequality a/R≤κ follows from 1/√(Ra)≤1.

## Cover and sparsity

Use exactly the slow-radius cover/coloring reconstructed in Sections2–3 of the power proof, now with radius ℓ. On overlapping macro balls the frequencies a_j/a(x)=1+O(ℓ/R)=1+O(η(K)). Partition derivatives of arbitrary fixed order satisfy |D^jχ_i|≤C_jℓ_i^(−j), obtained by choosing a fixed C∞ bump and differentiating its positive normalized sum. Define q=Σχ_i cos(a_i t_i·(x−x_i)).

The bounded-degree tangent coloring and analytic compact-family proof are unchanged. The normalized freezing error is O(1/(aℓ))=O(η(K)), so for prescribed θ,ρ there is τ>0 and a large K for which Ω={|q|<τ} occupies at most θ of every micro ball. No limiting q at K=∞ is taken. K is selected ONCE using the cover, frequency separation, C0/C1 freezing and zeroth-order repair conditions. The fixed C∞ bumps on that one cover supply constants C_m for every m. No requirement of the form C_m η(K)≪1 is imposed for higher m; consequently this constructs one V satisfying every fixed-order bound, not a different V for each m.

## All-order residual estimate

We need the stronger derivative estimate

    |D^m Δf|≤C_(m,K) κ a^m E, f=Eq, every fixed m.  (1)

Here is the bookkeeping rather than an appeal to smoothness. On every patch, |D^jχ_i|≤C_jℓ^(−j) and differentiating a plane wave costs a_j~a. Since ℓ^(−1)≤a, |D^mq|≤C_ma^m. Expanding

    Δq+a²q = Σ_i [(Δχ_i)cos_i
                     +2∇χ_i·∇cos_i
                     +(a²−a_i²)χ_i cos_i]

gives the zeroth-order bound C(ℓ^(−2)+a/ℓ+a²ℓ/R)≤Cκ. For k≥1,

    |D^k(a²−a_i²)|=|D^k a²|≤C_k a²/R^k≤C_k κ a^k;

for k=0 use the macro difference bound Cκ. Differentiating either cutoff term multiplies its size by at most a per derivative. Therefore

    |D^m(Δq+a²q)|≤C_mκa^m.                       (2)

For ∇Φ·∇q, the cutoff part has base size a/ℓ=κ and each additional derivative costs at most a. In the plane-wave part the small coefficient is

    h_i(x)=a_i(a/R)(x·t_i),

with x_i·t_i=0 and |x·t_i|≤Cℓ. Thus |h_i|≤Ca²ℓ/R=Cκ. For k≥1, differentiate h_i explicitly:

    |D^k h_i|≤C_k a²[ℓ/R^(k+1)+1/R^k]
               ≤C_kκa^k.

The first summand is κR^(−k); for the second the ratio to κa^k is a^(1/2−k) R^(1/2−k), bounded for k≥1. This proves |D^m(∇Φ·∇q)|≤C_mκa^m.

The remaining radial coefficient is |∇Φ|²−a²−ΔΦ=−K²a²/R²−ΔΦ. Its k-th derivative is bounded by C_(k,K)κa^k; the K-dependent term has ratio K²√a/R^(3/2) to κ, finite globally, and derivatives add inverse powers of R. Multiplying by q and by E, whose derivatives cost at most a, proves (1). Separately |D^mf|≤C_ma^mE for every fixed m. K has already been fixed once at order zero. Constants may then depend on m and that K; m is fixed only when taking its derivative limit at infinity. No subsequent enlargement of K is needed in (1).

## Weighted harmonic repair

The sparse-domain inverse lemma applies with h_1=κ,h_2=a²/R². On Ω, forcing is bounded by CE[κ+τK²a²/R²]. Hence

    |w|≤Cρ²E[η+τK²/R²].                         (3)

Choose θ for the two weighted inverses, then ρ so that Cρ²≤1/40, then τ from sparsity, and finally K so that Cρ²η(K)≤τ/40 and all previous cover/freezing requirements hold. This gives |w|≤τE/20. For this fixed K, K²/R²≤C_Kη, so also

    |W|≤C_KηE=C_Kκa^(−2)E                       (4)

for the zero extension W. This section uses no all-order derivative bound on w and none across the boundary of Ω.

## All-order smoothing bounds

Use the same fixed radial mollifier and ε=σ/a, with σ chosen after τ. Then |D^jε|≤C_j εR^(−j), every fixed j≥1. Harmonic averaging yields ΔF=0 on O={|q|<τ/2}, and |F|≥cτE outside O, just as before. We claim

    |D^m F|≤C_(m,K)a^mE,
    |D^m ΔF|≤C_(m,K)κa^mE, all m≥0.             (5)

For the repair part H(x)=∫K_x(y)W(y)dy, differentiating the kernel j times gives |D_x^jK_x(y)|≤C_j a^(n+j), supported in a ball of volume O(a^(−n)). This follows by differentiating t^nψ(t(x−y)), t=a/σ: |D^kt|≤C_k t R^(−k), |x−y|≤1/t on the support, and every derivative of the argument has size at most C_k a^k. Equation(4) then gives |D^jH|≤C_(j,K)κa^(j−2)E. Apply j=m+2 for the second bound in(5), and j=m for the first using η≤1.

For the smooth part G=∫ψ(z)f(Y(x,z))dz, Y=x−ε(x)z, the exact Laplacian identity is

    ΔG = ∫ψ(z)[Δf(Y)
              −2 Σ_(i,j) z_i ε_j f_ij(Y)
              +|∇ε|² Σ_(i,j)z_i z_j f_ij(Y)
              −(Δε) z·∇f(Y)] dz.

For derivatives of order m of the first term, (1), micro comparison and the chain rule give C_(m,K)κa^mE: D Y is uniformly bounded and |D^jY|≤C_jεR^(−j) for j≥2, so these higher jets never cost more than the a powers already allowed. For the remaining terms, the base bounds are aE/R, E/R², E/R², all ≤CκE. Differentiating the coefficient ε or its derivatives costs at most 1/R≤a per derivative; differentiating the composed derivatives of f costs at most a per derivative by the same jet estimate. Thus every derivative of order m of each commutator is ≤C_(m,K)κa^mE. This proves the second bound in(5). The first follows directly from |D^mf|≤C_ma^mE and the composition rule.

## Quotient bounds and final consequence

Define V=0 on O and V=ΔF/F where F≠0; the two formulas glue smoothly on the open cover because ΔF=0 on O. On R^n\O, |F|≥cτE. Induct on m in the identity VF=ΔF. Leibniz gives

    F D^mV = D^mΔF − Σ_(j<m) binomial(m,j) D^jV D^(m−j)F.

Bounds(5) and the inductive hypothesis yield |D^mV|≤C_(m,K)κa^m there. On O all derivatives of V vanish. This is a global bound, including the interface |q|=τ/2, where the denominator is bounded below. At possible zeros of F we only use the identically-zero potential formula.

Finally κa^m=R^(−1/2)(logR)^(m+3/2)→0 for every fixed m, while E has the r log r decay stated above. Nontriviality follows from sparsity as in the power proof. This completes the proposed derivation.

## Gate and selection

The changed-scale power theorem is being independently audited by r07. This log-frequency/all-order statement needs a **separate audit**, particularly (1) and the uniform kernel jets in(5). Finite replay checks only scale bookkeeping; it cannot check all derivatives or global nodal repair. Closest prior remains FI/Filonov–Krymskii. A literature search found no primary real all-derivative-decaying formulation; that absence is not evidence of priority.

Best research continuation after a pass: identify which derivative condition actually matters. Absolute bounded derivatives have a candidate counterexample here, while a relative condition such as |∇V|≤Cκ/R is not supplied and would change the interface. Avoid calling the construction slowly varying merely because its derivatives vanish: its oscillation frequency a=logR grows.

**Sharp scope check from r07:** Sparsity and the safe lower bound give, for every x, a point y∈B(x,ρ/a(x)) with |F(y)|≥cτE(y)≥c'τE(x). For x=re1→∞ this is a lower bound exp(−C r log r) at points with |y|~r. Hence this particular all-order construction cannot satisfy any global bound exp(−c r^(1+ε)) for fixed c,ε>0. The limitation is proved, not just unverified. Fixed-power counterexamples with any prescribed finite number of vanishing derivatives are handled instead in `FINITE_JET_COROLLARY.md`.
