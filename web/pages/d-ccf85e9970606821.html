# Exact angular export of the leading Mellin model

2026-10-06. Independent reconstruction by r12. Scope: the specified leading eikonal amplitude projected against an exact physical Legendre kernel. This is not a proof of finite-detector gravity positivity, nor of the all-partial-wave positivity cone of Bellazzini et al.

## Interface and result

For every integer l>=0, every 0<d<=1/sqrt(2),

    integral_0^d |P_l(1-2u^2)-J0((2l+1)u)| du/u <= 6d.       (1)

The constant does not depend on l. In particular l can depend arbitrarily on G and s, including exponentially moving semiclassical partial waves. The integral starts at zero, so every truncated [E,Q] inherits the same bound without a dependence on log(Q/E).

Let G,s>0, g=Gs, 0<E<Q<=sqrt(s/2), b_l=(2l+1)/sqrt(s), and chi any real phase. Define

    L_l = 8*pi*G*s^2 integral_E^Q P_l(1-2r^2/s)
                    sin(2g log(r/E)+chi) dr/r,
    B_l = 8*pi*G*s^2 integral_E^Q J0(b_l*r)
                    sin(2g log(r/E)+chi) dr/r.

Then

    |L_l-B_l| <= 48*pi*G*Q*s^(3/2).                       (2)

For s>=M^2 and a fixed cutoff Q<=M/sqrt(2),

    integral_(M^2)^infinity |L_l-B_l| ds/s^3
        <=96*pi*G*Q/M.                                   (3)

These quantifiers allow arbitrary measurable l(s,G), E(s,G), and chi(s,G). With a multiplier a(s,r), (2) acquires sup_[E,Q]|a|, without requiring smoothness. More generally the sufficient condition is integrability of Q(s,G)*sup|a| against s^(-3/2), with a uniform bound if a vanishing error as G->0 is desired. A cutoff proportional to sqrt(s) cannot be substituted into (3): this particular error envelope would then be nonintegrable.

Combining (3) with the proved cycle4 gamma-phase Bessel estimate, for GM^2<=1 and chi(g)=-2 Im log Gamma(1+ig),

    F_l=4*pi*s*(1-cos(2z_l))>=0,
    z_l=clip(g log(1/(b_l*E)),0,g log(Q/E)),
    integral |L_l-F_l| ds/s^3
      <=73*pi*G log(1/(GM^2))+808*pi*G+96*pi*G*Q/M.       (4)

Thus the exact Legendre leading amplitude has the same O(G log(1/G)) approximate positivity budget, uniformly in the UV/semiclassical double limit at fixed M,Q. The cycle4 estimate is in notes/r13_field_theory/cycle4/proof_and_physics.md; its independent reconstruction is in notes/r10_probability/cycle4/gravity_independent_audit.md. The new ingredient here is the angular export, not the Mellin theorem.

## Complete proof of (1)

Put lambda=2l+1, theta=2 arcsin u, nu=l+1/2, x=lambda*u, y=nu*theta. For u<=1/sqrt(2), theta<=pi/2.

The exact Mehler–Dirichlet formula is

    P_l(cos theta)=(sqrt(2)/pi) integral_0^theta
                    cos(nu*t)/sqrt(cos t-cos theta) dt.  (5)

NIST DLMF14.12.1 verifies its hypotheses 0<theta<pi and order mu=0<1/2. For completeness one can reconstruct (5) for all integer l without importing a uniform asymptotic theorem. Multiply its proposed right side by z^l and sum for 0<z<1. The cosine sum is (1-z)cos(t/2)/(1-2z cos t+z^2). Substitution v=sin(t/2)/sin(theta/2) turns the resulting integral into

    (2(1-z)/pi) integral_0^1
      dv / [sqrt(1-v^2)*((1-z)^2+4z sin^2(theta/2)*v^2)].

The substitution v=sin phi, then w=tan phi, evaluates this as (1-2z cos theta+z^2)^(-1/2), the Legendre generating function. Termwise summation is dominated by a geometric series times the integrable endpoint kernel. Analytic coefficient uniqueness proves (5). This also checks its prefactor and l=0 normalization.

The angular Bessel representation is

    J0(nu*theta)=(2/pi) integral_0^theta
                 cos(nu*t)/sqrt(theta^2-t^2) dt.

Let a=(theta+t)/2, c=(theta-t)/2. The ratio of the two nonoscillatory weights is

    R=sqrt((a/sin a)*(c/sin c)).

Use sin v>=v(1-v^2/6) for 0<=v<=pi/2, with the limiting ratio at v=0. Thus 1<=R<=1/(1-theta^2/6). Since theta^2<=pi^2/4<3,

    R-1 <=theta^2/(6-theta^2)<=theta^2/3.

The normalized Bessel weight has integral one. Also arcsin u<=sqrt(2)*u on this interval, by integrating its derivative. Consequently

    |P_l(1-2u^2)-J0(y)|<=theta^2/3<=8u^2/3,
    integral_0^d this difference du/u <=4d^2/3.           (6)

Crucially, no absolute value is taken on a high-l polynomial representation with exponentially large coefficients. The bound comes from the positive endpoint weight and is independent of nu.

Next compare angular and chord Bessel arguments. For 0<=u<=1/sqrt(2),

    arcsin u-u = integral_0^u t^2 /
                     [sqrt(1-t^2)*(1+sqrt(1-t^2))] dt
                <=u^3/3.

The denominator is at least (1+sqrt(2))/2>1. Hence 0<=y-x<=x*u^2/3.

From the angular integral, |J0|<=1 and |J0'|<=1. For x>=1, set H=sqrt(x)*J0(x). Its equation is H''+(1+1/(4x^2))*H=0. The energy e=H^2+H'^2 satisfies |e'|<=e/(4x^2). At x=1, |H|<=1; the differentiated angular integral gives |J0'(1)|<=1/2, whence |H'|<=1 and e(1)<=2. Therefore e(x)<=2 exp(1/4)<4, so

    |J0(x)|<=2/sqrt(x), |J0'(x)|<=3/sqrt(x), x>=1.      (7)

For x<=1, the angular derivative bound yields |J0(y)-J0(x)|<=lambda*u^3/3. Its weighted integral over [0,min(d,1/lambda)] is <=d^2/9.

For x>=1, (7), y>=x and the mean-value integral give two bounds:

    |J0(y)-J0(x)|<=sqrt(lambda*u)*u^2,
    |J0(y)-J0(x)|<=4/sqrt(lambda*u).                     (8)

Their crossing is a=(4/lambda)^(1/3), which is >=1/lambda. If d<=a, integration of the first bound gives at most (2/5)*sqrt(lambda)*d^(5/2)<=4d/5, because lambda*d^3<=4. If d>a, integrate the first bound up to a and the second from a to d:

    first <=4a/5,
    second <=8/(sqrt(lambda*a))=4a,
    total <=24a/5<=24d/5.

Dropping lower endpoints only enlarges these integrals. Combining with (6) gives

    integral difference <=(13/9)d^2+(24/5)d<6d,

since d<=1/sqrt(2). Triangle inequality proves (1). Multiplication and the elementary integral integral_(M^2)^infinity s^(-3/2)ds=2/M prove (2)-(3).

## Actual source interface and remaining obstruction

Bellazzini et al., arXiv2512.13780v2, v2 submitted2026-04-17: the read passages are section4.2, section5 equations5.8-5.13, section7, AppendixA.2 equationsA.23-A.29 and discussion. Authors' Eq5.13 requires nonnegative smeared partial-wave coefficients for every l and s>=M^2. Eq5.8 then sums (2l+1)|a_l|^2 times these coefficients. Those coefficients do not contain the leading sine phase used above. Our theorem does not establish their positivity. A uniform termwise approximation error cannot be summed over infinitely many l unless an appropriate weighted spectral budget is supplied, and a vanishing Bessel cone margin cannot absorb an unsigned approximation error. This is the decisive unclosed implication.

The actual massless gravity STU angle is cos theta=sqrt((s-3q^2)/(s+q^2)), A.29, and qmax=M/sqrt(3), A.25. It maps exactly to 1-2r^2/s with r=sqrt(-t) on the forward branch, so r10's independent exact change-of-variable/BV weight analysis can use (1). The cutoff remains uniformly bounded: rQ^2/s=(1-sqrt((s-3Q^2)/(s+Q^2)))/2 and rQ^2<=2Q^2. Thus the physical r cutoff satisfies rQ<=sqrt(2)Q and rQ<=sqrt(s/2). A multiplier with uniformly bounded sup norm can be handled in (3); the Bessel oscillatory part additionally needs r10's BV argument. The fixed-transfer result here is not a replacement of the source's STU variable by its small-angle approximation.

Source Eq5.12 literally prints a plus sign in 1+2q^2/(s-4m^2), as confirmed in the saved PDF pixels. For physical negative transfer t=-q^2 the conventional angle has a minus sign. We do not infer the intended convention or refute the whole paper from this display; the theorem above explicitly specifies its physical minus-sign kernel. The gravity A.29 expression has no such ambiguity.

## Novelty, discrimination, downstream use

Nearest prior art: exact Mehler–Dirichlet representation, classical Bessel ODE energy bounds, and Legendre-to-Bessel small-angle estimates. No broad novelty claim: this elementary weighted all-l error bound may be known. The scientific use is a uniform mathematical export of an already controlled leading amplitude, preventing an illicit fixed-l asymptotic exchange with a UV limit. It removes one real technical gap while exposing the separate cone/spectral-summability and detector/channel gates.

The executable finite check uses the three-term polynomial recurrence and the platform libm J0, rather than the same Mehler formula used in the proof. It scans the phase-curvature crossover lambda*d^3 around one, small l including zero, and the full d<=1/sqrt(2) range. Its numerical quadrature is a regression check, not the universal proof.

Next falsifiable step: obtain an exact admissible smearing psi for Eq5.13 with an all-l quantitative margin or an independently justified summable spectral error interface. Radiation/reduced hard channels and analyticity/crossing remain physical prerequisites; the leading scalar sine model alone does not supply them.
