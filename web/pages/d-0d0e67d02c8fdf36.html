# Uniform random reduction of the entire subrepresentation-dimension polytope

## Interface

Let Q be any finite quiver, including loops/cycles, with fixed nonnegative vertex dimensions alpha_v. Input arrow matrices M_a are EXPLICIT DENSE arrays of integers of absolute value at most 2^H, H>=1; vertex dimensions may be binary encoded. For rational input, multiply EACH ENTIRE arrow matrix by one nonzero common denominator before computing H and reducing modulo p. This preserves all characteristic-zero subrepresentations and has polynomial bit growth in the explicit dense input. Columnwise clearing would change an arrow as a linear map and is not used. The integerized arrow presentation is reduced even at primes dividing original denominators; reduction of original rational entries at such primes is not asserted. Define

D_K={ (dim_K W_v)_v : W is a K-subrepresentation of M },
T_K=conv(D_K) in the real vertex-coordinate space.

There is an explicit polynomial-bit random-prime acquisition procedure such that, with probability at least 1-delta,

T_Fp=T_Q=T_C.

The guarantee preserves ALL real signed linear weight objectives on the dimension polytope simultaneously, hence all King inequalities/weight cones. Unlike the packing presentation, it needs no separate rank-admissibility condition: dimensions at vertices are fixed, while arrow rank loss is itself covered by the forbidden-profile loci. This does NOT say D_Q=D_C or D_Fp=D_C, nor that individual subrepresentations lift.

First remove zero-dimensional vertices and their zero-size arrows. Then remove isolated vertices: an isolated vertex contributes the exactly known factor [0,alpha_v] to T_K over every field and has no exceptional primes. In all bounds below, A is the sum of dimensions of the REMAINING active vertices, and E is their arrow count. If none remain, every prime works and p=2 may be returned. For each active vertex, an incident dense matrix between positive-dimensional spaces has at least alpha_v entries. Consequently A<=2 times the total number of supplied dense matrix entries, which makes the claimed polynomial dependence on A a polynomial dependence on actual input length. Without this preprocessing, a binary-large isolated dimension would invalidate that inference. For sparse or compressed matrices with huge dimensions, only polynomial cost in expanded A and expanded data is asserted; no polynomial compressed-input guarantee is claimed.

## 1. Characteristic-zero descent for every signed objective

Fix any real signed weight vector sigma. The value sigma dot dim W takes only finitely many values. Subrepresentations form a lattice closed under vertexwise sum/intersection; dimension is MODULAR vertexwise. Hence two maximizers have maximizing sum and intersection. There is a unique largest maximizing subrepresentation W*.

Fix its exact dimension vector k. Its product Grassmann chart equations are rational. A complex point implies a point over Qbar, having exactly the SAME k and hence the same objective. Such a point is a maximizer contained in W*. The dimensions agree at all vertices, so it equals W*. Galois invariance of the unique largest maximizer gives rational RREF coordinates. Thus the maximum over Q equals the maximum over C, for EVERY real sigma. Equality of support functions proves T_Q=T_C.

This is the established canonical-maximizer/descent mechanism. It does not infer rational realizability of each complex profile: only an exposed largest maximizer is descended.

## 2. Reduction gives one inclusion at every prime

For a rational subrepresentation W, put L_v=W_v intersect Z^(alpha_v). These are saturated lattices of the given dimensions and inject under reduction at every prime. Integer arrows map L_tail into L_head because they already map the corresponding rational subspaces into one another. Therefore their reductions form a subrepresentation with exactly the same dimension vector. Thus

D_Q is contained in D_Fp, and T_Q is contained in T_Fp,

for EVERY prime, regardless of rank losses of the input arrows.

## 3. Quadratic affine chart equations

For each k_v between0 and alpha_v, choose k_v pivot coordinates P_v and represent W_v by an alpha_v x k_v matrix Y_v whose P_v rows form identity. There are n=sum_v k_v(alpha_v-k_v)<=A^2 variables. At an arrow a:t->h, closure is equivalent to

M_a Y_t=Y_h (M_a Y_t)_(P_h,:).

The pivot rows are automatic; retain only nonpivot rows on the left. Identity pivot blocks ensure the matrices represent subspaces in every characteristic, so there is no missing nonvanishing condition or auxiliary inverse.

Every entry of this equation has degree at most2. It has at most A+A^2 summands with original coefficient size<=2^H, so coefficient logarithmic height is bounded conservatively by

h_q=H+2ceil(log_2(A+1))+3.

There are at most E A^2 equations per chart. The total number of dimension-vector/chart pairs, summing over ALL k, is exactly

product_v sum_(k_v=0)^(alpha_v) binomial(alpha_v,k_v)=2^A.

Thus no extra exponential factor for dimension profiles is needed. The acquisition procedure enumerates none of these charts.

## 4. Explicit bad-prime count and sampler

Set N=A^2, e=max(1,E),
l_q=ceil(log_2(e+1))+2ceil(log_2(A+1)),
C_q=4N(N+1)2^N [h_q+l_q+(N+7)*2*(N+3)],
B_q=2^(A+1) C_q.

For every C-EMPTY chart, KPS math/9911094v1 Introduction Theorem1 gives a nonzero integer in its equation ideal with log absolute value<=C_q; constant-only/zero-variable charts are handled by choosing a nonzero input constant. At most2C_q primes divide it. The union across at most2^A charts consequently has size<=B_q. If E=0 there are no empty profiles, and every prime is already good; the displayed bound remains a harmless overestimate.

For p outside that union, a finite-field subrepresentation belongs to some chart, which cannot be C-empty. Its EXACT dimension vector k therefore occurs over C. Thus D_Fp is contained in D_C. Combining with Section2 and T_Q=T_C proves the claimed polytope equality.

The same sampler as the packing note applies with B_q. Choose q=ceil(1/delta), L=4[bit_length(B_q)+bit_length(q)+20], and an exact uniformly random L-bit prime by odd-candidate AKS rejection. Its failure probability is<=delta and expected bit time is polynomial. The bounded variant uses q=ceil(2/delta), T=2L bit_length(q) trials, and chargedUNKNOWN with total failure<=delta. All integers here have polynomial bit length; specifically

log_2 B_q=O(A^2+A+log(H+log(E+2)+A)).

The bound is independent of the subsequent external weight values, even binary-large signed weights. The runtime of an optimizer called afterward may still depend on their NUMERICAL size. No claim removes that separate dependence.

## 5. Individual-profile obstruction and prior boundary

For a one-loop, two-dimensional representation M=[[0,-1],[1,0]], there is no rational invariant line, while complex invariant lines exist. Mod5, span(1,2) is invariant; mod3 no invariant line exists. Thus D_Q={0,2}, D_C={0,1,2}; finite fields can have either of those sets. Yet every dimension polytope is the interval[0,2]. Complete polytope preservation therefore cannot supply a rational lift of a particular finite-field flag or force preservation of every algebraically possible dimension profile. No extension-field assumption or GRH is needed for the polytope guarantee, because only C-EMPTY loci must be excluded.
