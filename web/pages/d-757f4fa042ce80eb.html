# Cycle 2: finite-time identification and robust phenotype scheduling

## Verdict

The proposed pure-state, initial-slope identification claim is already proved in a close primary source, including its stronger cell-number form. Gunnarsson, Foo and Leder (2023) analyze a continuous-time multitype branching process with state-specific birth, death and switching. Their Proposition 1 recovers every switching rate and net birth rate from the first derivatives of pure-start **mean counts**; Proposition 2 recovers each outgoing switching row from the first derivatives of pure-start **mean fractions**. The new headline is therefore not a novelty result. Their model and data assumptions are not identical to a deterministic population ODE, but the first-moment identities are the same after transposing the matrix convention.

Two qualifications sharpen the proposed claim. First, (K) starts are necessary only when the observation is restricted to first derivatives at time zero for an unrestricted (K)-state rate matrix. A single full exact trajectory can identify all rates in a two-state example, and generically whenever its state trajectory spans the full state space. Second, an explicit (O(\tau)) secant bias bound and a conservative finite-grid robust schedule certificate can be derived under rate caps. They are elementary, conditional tools; neither supplies a new biological controllability theorem. The biological generator, confidence set, state labels, actuator calibration, and stochastic safety bridge remain unacquired.

## Exact model convention and interface

Use a row-generator convention for switching:

\[
Q=(q_{ij})_{i,j=1}^K,\qquad q_{ij}\ge 0\ (i\ne j),\qquad q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij},\qquad Q\mathbf 1=0.
\]

Here (q_{ij}) is the rate of a cell in source phenotype (i) switching to phenotype (j). Let (r_i) be the phenotype-specific **net** per-capita count growth rate (birth minus death), not a birth rate. For the column vector (x(t)\ge0) of mean counts, the linear mean-population model is

\[
\dot x=A x,\qquad A=Q^T+D_r,\qquad D_r=\operatorname{diag}(r_1,\ldots,r_K).
\]

This is the transpose of the row-count convention used by Gunnarsson–Foo–Leder: their off-diagonal (a_{ij}=\nu_{ij}), diagonal (a_{ii}=\lambda_i-\sum_{j\ne i}\nu_{ij}), and row mean (m(t)=m(0)e^{tA_{\rm row}}). In our convention (A=A_{\rm row}^T). The off-diagonal entries of (A) are switching rates, while its diagonal combines net growth and outgoing switching.

Set (N=\mathbf1^T x), (p=x/N\in\Delta^{K-1}). Because (Q\mathbf1=0),

\[
\frac{\dot N}{N}=r^Tp,\qquad
\dot p=F(p):=Q^Tp+D_rp-(r^Tp)p.
\]

For a pure start (p(0)=e_i), these imply the exact identities

\[
\dot p_j(0)=q_{ij}\quad(j\ne i),\qquad
\dot p_i(0)=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij},\qquad
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\log N(t)\right|_{t=0}=r_i.
\]

The count identity concerns total **mean counts** in the deterministic mean ODE. In a stochastic branching population, observed counts and observed fractions fluctuate; a sample-path derivative is not the same quantity. If only fractions are measured, adding a common constant (c) to every (r_i) multiplies all counts by (e^{ct}) and leaves every fraction unchanged, so absolute net growth is not identifiable from fraction-only data.

## Finite-τ secant lemma

Assume a constant generator during the observation interval, pure initial state (e_i), switching exit caps

\[
\lambda_i^{\rm out}:=\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}\le\Lambda,
\]

and net growth caps (|r_i|\le R). Define the finite-time estimators

\[
\widehat q_{ij}(\tau)=\frac{p_j(\tau)}{\tau}\quad(j\ne i),\qquad
\widehat r_i(\tau)=\frac{\log(N(\tau)/N(0))}{\tau}.
\]

For every (\tau>0),

\[
|\widehat q_{ij}(\tau)-q_{ij}|
\le (\Lambda+R)(2\Lambda+3R)\tau,
\qquad
|\widehat r_i(\tau)-r_i|
\le R(\Lambda+R)\tau.
\tag{1}
\]

These are deterministic truncation bounds for the stated mean ODE, not finite-sample confidence bounds.

**Proof.** On the simplex,

\[
\|F(p)\|_1\le 2\Lambda+2R=:L_p.
\]

Indeed, (\|Q^Tp\|_1\le2\sum_i p_i\lambda_i^{\rm out}\le2\Lambda); the selection term has norm (\sum_i p_i|r_i-r^Tp|\le2R). For (p,q\in\Delta^{K-1}),

\[
\|F(p)-F(q)\|_1\le(2\Lambda+3R)\|p-q\|_1=:L_F\|p-q\|_1.
\]

The linear switching and growth terms contribute at most (2\Lambda+R), and the map (p\mapsto(r^Tp)p) contributes at most (2R). Thus (\|p(t)-e_i\|_1\le L_p t). For (j\ne i), (F_j(e_i)=q_{ij}), so

\[
\left|\frac{p_j(\tau)}{\tau}-q_{ij}\right|
\le\frac1\tau\int_0^\tau\|F(p(s))-F(e_i)\|_1ds
\le\frac{L_FL_p}{2}\tau
=(\Lambda+R)(2\Lambda+3R)\tau.
\]

Also (\log(N(\tau)/N(0))=\int_0^\tau r^Tp(s)ds), and hence

\[
|\widehat r_i-r_i|
\le\frac{R}{\tau}\int_0^\tau\|p(s)-e_i\|_1ds
\le\frac{RL_p}{2}\tau=R(\Lambda+R)\tau.
\]

If measured endpoint composition has (\ell_1) error at most (\eta_p), and the measured log count ratio has absolute error at most (\eta_\ell), add (\eta_p/\tau) and (\eta_\ell/\tau) to the two radii in (1). Those error budgets must come from an explicit sampling/instrument model. With endpoint error fixed independently of (\tau), the elementary radius (C\tau+\eta/\tau) is minimized at (\tau=\sqrt{\eta/C}); this is only a design guide if sampling error actually stays fixed as the endpoint time changes.

The constants are intentionally simple and can be conservative. The finite diagnostic `verify_finite_tau.py` checked 1,000 random four-state pure-start fixtures at \(\Lambda=0.3, R=0.2\), over \(10^{-4}\le\tau\le0.25\). The maximum observed composition-bias/bound ratio was 0.132166; the maximum log-count-bias/bound ratio was 0.429457. This verifies only those fixtures and the implementation of the displayed bound, not its biological assumptions or novelty.

For action \(u\) and pure start \(i\), observed secants and simultaneous measurement-error radii can define a bounded confidence set

\[
\mathcal C_u=\left\{(Q_u,r_u):\begin{array}{l}
q_{ij}\ge0,\quad \sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}\le\Lambda,\quad |r_i|\le R,\\
|\widehat q_{ij}-q_{ij}|\le(\Lambda+R)(2\Lambda+3R)\tau+\eta_p/\tau,\\
|\widehat r_i-r_i|\le R(\Lambda+R)\tau+\eta_\ell/\tau
\end{array}\right\}.
\]

This set contains the true rates only on the event that the endpoint-error bounds and rate caps hold. For stochastic cell counts, \(\eta_p,\eta_\ell\) must include sampling/branching uncertainty at the chosen confidence level; the deterministic lemma alone does not supply them. Preserve correlations or construct simultaneous coverage across starts, actions and rates before using the set in a robust guarantee.

## What is minimal about (K) starts

The unrestricted parameter vector ((q_{ij}:i\ne j,r_i)) has (K(K-1)+K=K^2) real degrees of freedom in the interior of the rate constraints. At each known pure start, the (K-1) independent composition slopes and one log-total slope provide (K) scalar first-derivative observations. (K) distinct pure starts identify every row and every (r_i) by the displayed formulas. Fewer than (K) such starts cannot identify an open (K^2)-dimensional parameter family from first-derivative records alone: fewer than (K^2) scalar observations are available. More directly, if a pure start (e_i) is omitted, its outgoing row (q_{ij}) and (r_i) do not enter any of the other starts' derivatives at time zero. This is the valid minimality statement for this **initial-derivative-only design**. It presumes unrestricted unknown rates; known sparsity, shared parameters, or other measurements change the count.

It is false that (K) starts are necessary when full continuous time courses are observed. For (K=2), write

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}-a&a\\b&-b\end{pmatrix},\quad
A=\begin{pmatrix}r_1-a&b\\a&r_2-b\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Start once from (e_1), with (a>0), and observe exact absolute counts continuously. Then (a=\dot p_2(0)) and (r_1=(d/dt)\log N(0)). For any (t>0), (x_2(t)>0), and

\[
b=\frac{\dot x_1(t)-(r_1-a)x_1(t)}{x_2(t)},\qquad
r_2=\frac{(\dot N(t)/N(t))-r_1p_1(t)}{p_2(t)}.
\]

Thus one full exact trajectory identifies all four parameters. If (a=0), state 2 is never reached from (e_1) and (b,r_2) are unidentifiable; the counterexample is informative rather than universal. For general (K), one exact trajectory suffices if its state curve spans (\mathbb R^K): choose (K) times with columns (X=[x(t_1)\ \cdots\ x(t_K)]) nonsingular, and (A=[\dot x(t_1)\ \cdots\ \dot x(t_K)]X^{-1}). A cyclic initial vector, equivalently full Krylov rank in the exact autonomous model, is a sufficient condition. This can be badly conditioned under finite noisy data. Fraction-only trajectories still cannot determine a common additive shift of all (r_i).

## Conservative finite-action reach–avoid certificate

For each finite action \(u\in\mathcal U\), suppose the same action has an unknown but time-constant matrix \(A_u=Q_u^T+D_{r_u}\) in a joint confidence set \(\mathcal C_u\). Assume all \(A_u\) are Metzler, equivalently, their off-diagonal switching entries are nonnegative, and that rate caps \(\lambda_i^{\rm out}\le\Lambda\) and \(|r_i|\le R\) hold throughout. A schedule has \(M\) slots of length \(h\), action \(u_m\) fixed during slot \(m\), and propagation

\[
x_{m+1}=e^{hA_{u_m}}x_m.
\]

Let \(T\) be target phenotypes and \(U\) unsafe phenotypes, assumed disjoint. A concrete robust design problem is

\[
\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal U^M}\ \inf_{A\in\prod_u\mathcal C_u} p_T(Mh;\sigma,A)
\]

subject to \(\sup_{A\in\prod_u\mathcal C_u,\,0\le t\le Mh}p_U(t;\sigma,A)\le\delta\), optional total-count/viability constraints, and an actuation budget \(\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}c(u_m)h\le B\). This is an **open-loop mean-population composition** reach–avoid problem. A target fraction can rise through differential growth/survival without individual-cell conversion. If avoiding \(U\) means bounding the fraction of lineages that ever visited an unsafe state, augment the model with a first-hit history state (or fit a separate lineage-event process); the current-occupancy bound \(p_U(t)\) alone does not bound cumulative entry. This model is not individual-cell controllability or a stochastic path-safety statement.

A conservative finite algorithm is:

1. For each action, compute an entrywise interval hull \(A_u^-\le A_u\le A_u^+\). From interval bounds \(q_{ij}\in[q_{ij}^-,q_{ij}^+]\), \(r_i\in[r_i^-,r_i^+]\), use \((A_u^-)_{ji}=q_{ij}^-\), \((A_u^+)_{ji}=q_{ij}^+\) for \(j\ne i\), and diagonal bounds \(r_i^- -\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}^+\) and \(r_i^+ -\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}^-\). These hull matrices need not themselves be feasible generators; they only need to enclose every feasible \(A_u\).
2. For each candidate schedule, propagate \(x^-_{m+1}=e^{hA_{u_m}^-}x^-_m\) and \(x^+_{m+1}=e^{hA_{u_m}^+}x^+_m\), from enclosing initial vectors. Positive-system comparison gives \(x^-_m\le x_m\le x^+_m\) componentwise for every feasible matrix choice. Apply the same calculation at interior grid times.
3. At each sampled time, lower-bound target occupancy and upper-bound unsafe occupancy by
   \[
   \underline p_T=\frac{\sum_{i\in T}x_i^-}{\sum_{i\in T}x_i^-+\sum_{i\notin T}x_i^+},\qquad
   \overline p_U=\frac{\sum_{i\in U}x_i^+}{\sum_{i\in U}x_i^++\sum_{i\notin U}x_i^-}.
   \]
   These ratios are conservative because the componentwise extrema may not be jointly attainable.
4. Check path safety on a subgrid of spacing \(s\) inside every action slot. Since \(\|\dot p\|_1\le L_p=2\Lambda+2R\), a sampled upper bound plus \(L_p s\) bounds unsafe composition until the next grid point. Require this buffered upper bound to be at most \(\delta\). Count-floor constraints can use \(|d\log N/dt|\le R\) for a corresponding buffer.
5. Enumerate all \(|\mathcal U|^M\) schedules, discard those failing the conservative safety or cost tests, and maximize the certified target lower bound among the remainder. Return the best certified schedule or UNKNOWN if none passes.

This enumerates the specified fixed-grid schedule class; it does not certify schedules with arbitrary continuous switching times.
For a finite action menu and fixed time grid, enumeration terminates. With dense (K\times K) matrices, direct exponential costs are (O(K^3)) per propagation; reuse action exponentials when (h) is fixed, and the schedule search remains exponential in (M). Certified matrix-exponential rounding is an added numerical obligation. An interval-hull certificate can be very conservative and rejecting every schedule is **not** a proof of physical unreachability. If durations are allowed to vary continuously, a duration grid or global optimizer is additional work; the algorithm as stated certifies only the specified finite schedule class. Confidence regions must have joint coverage for the whole action/rate family; independent marginal intervals without a multiplicity/correlation treatment do not produce a joint confidence guarantee.

If \(\mathbb P(\theta\in\mathcal C)\ge1-\alpha\) and the mean ODE and actuator model are valid, a passing certificate yields the corresponding mean-population schedule guarantee on that event. It does **not** bound random branching-process paths, extinction, rare unsafe clones, or adverse biological effects. Those need a separate stochastic model and acquired finite-sample/finite-horizon bounds. The reaction-network N35 safety result in the Astra corpus has a different jump-process interface and supplied molecular polytope/collar certificates; it does not transfer to this cell model.

## Comparison with primary literature and novelty boundary

**Gunnarsson, Foo & Leder (2023).** Their primary result is direct prior art for the initial-slope lemma. They model each type-\(j\) cell dividing at rate \(b_j\), dying at rate \(d_j\), and switching to type \(k\) at rate \(\nu_{jk}\), with net rate \(\lambda_j=b_j-d_j\); the mean count matrix is \(e^{tA}\). From first derivatives of the pure-start mean count curves they identify all (\nu_{jk}) and (\lambda_j); from first derivatives of mean fractions they identify the outgoing switching rates only. In the fraction-only case, relative net-growth differences appear in second derivatives/equilibrium, while a common growth shift remains invisible. Their statistical interface is stronger than an exact deterministic lemma: cell-number or cell-fraction data at one or more times, sorted/expanded initial subpopulations, a multitype branching-process likelihood, and likelihood-based confidence intervals. They warn that structural identifiability does not mean the estimator is well-conditioned from real finite data. The Cycle2 model's observation equation matches their first-moment convention, but its rate-cap secant bound is not a result established by their propositions as read here.

**Transcompp (Jagannathan et al., 2020).** Inputs are time-series single-cell phenotype measurements or bulk fractional compositions, across any number of replicates and flexible initial mixtures; phenotype-specific proliferation can be known/bounded or fitted. It estimates a discrete transition matrix and diagonal proliferation factors by minimizing fit error over snapshots and uses resampling to construct rate distributions. Its published model predicts \(A_n=A_0[\mathrm{Prolif}\,T]^{\Delta t/\omega}\) and normalizes to fractions. That is a discrete-time fit/simulation interface; it is not the continuous-time exact mean-count derivative theorem above or a confidence-set robust reach–avoid certificate. The paper does describe using fitted rates to simulate trajectories under perturbations, so it would be inaccurate to characterize it as incapable of forward simulation. The proposed cycle's main possible addition is a conservative safety-aware schedule layer, not rate estimation itself.

**Direct control prior art.** Paryad-Zanjani, Saint-Antoine and Singh (IFAC 2021) already estimate a state-switching melanoma model and compare continuous and alternating therapy schedules. Their model has S/P/R populations, logistic growth, treatment input \(u(t)\in\{0,1\}\), and an exposure timer for delayed resistance; they use a switching-rate fluctuation-test estimate and parameter values from a separate melanoma dataset, then optimize simulated time to a critical tumor size. This defeats any claim that switching-cell models plus schedule selection are new. The present interval-box method could add an explicit confidence-set robustness certificate, but the numerical/biological value and novelty of that wrapper are not established.

Accordingly, status is: exact initial-slope identification = **established prior art**; equations (1) = **internally proved conditional lemma**, elementary \(O(\tau)\) analysis; finite-grid positive-system enclosure = **internally proved conservative certificate algorithm**; biological model validity, fitted confidence coverage, cell-path safety, effectiveness, external validation and novelty = **UNKNOWN**. Do not use the conditional calculation as a clinical recommendation or claim nature follows the model.

## Falsifiable assay and information/cost ledger

For a named cell line/donor, freeze phenotype labels, action menu, exposure, assay, initial-cell purity threshold, and time unit. For each action \(u\), seed sorted \(e_i\) populations and collect absolute total counts plus phenotype fractions at \(\tau\) and at additional early times, with independent biological replicates. Measure actual intracellular factor/drug trajectory for each action; a nominal inducer label is not a kinetic input. Include sham/vehicle and the best fit-based competing schedule at matched cumulative exposure and delivery cost. Fit \(A_u\) and a **joint** confidence region on training replicates, freeze it, then test the predicted endpoint composition and unsafe trajectory on held-out donors/replicates.

The equations predict a quantitative rate interval for each measured secant: composition-rate error is at most \((\Lambda+R)(2\Lambda+3R)\tau+\eta_p/\tau\), and log-total growth error at most \(R(\Lambda+R)\tau+\eta_\ell/\tau\), provided the stated caps, constant-rate action, pure-start assumption, and measurement-error bounds hold. A blinded direct lineage/event estimate or a held-out time-course outside these intervals falsifies at least one stated cap/model/error premise. If fitted rate sets are too broad to certify a safe target-reaching finite schedule, return `UNKNOWN` rather than relaxing the bound after seeing the test data.

Costs: (K\) sorted start states per action for the simple first-derivative design, multiplied by number of actions and biological replicates; cell counts/flow or single-cell assay; sorting/purity measurement; intracellular exposure tracking; lineage labels; animal/donor as experimental unit; finite-time assay and culture costs; possible sequencing/imaging; and safety follow-up after washout. The full-trajectory one-start shortcut is only structural in exact noise-free data and can be ill-conditioned; it does not justify reducing replicate counts. The action-sequence search is exponential in horizon slots. Sample size, measurement radii, phenotype classification error, confidence-set width, delivery burden, and long-term safety are all `UNKNOWN` without a pilot. Population-composition control does not establish that any individual cell has been fate-reprogrammed or that tissue function regenerated.
