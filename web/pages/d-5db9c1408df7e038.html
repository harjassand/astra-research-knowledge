## 1. Accepted input and exact normalization

Let sigma be area probability measure on S². Let e=(e_1,...,e_M) contain orthonormal nonconstant real harmonics of degrees1,...,T, where M=T(T+2). Suppose distinct rational unit nodes x_i satisfy

    b=N^-1 sum_i e(x_i),   |b|<=gamma<=beta_T,
    S=N^-1 sum_i e(x_i)e(x_i)^t >=s0 I,
    s0=13/19,   beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].             (1)

The root gate and author proof supply this input with N=N_T. The addition theorem with normalized measure gives the exact pointwise identity

    |e(x)|²=sum_(l=1)^T(2l+1)=M.                  (2)

Set

    H=S-bb^t,
    a=H^-1 b,
    w_i=N^-1[1-(e(x_i)-b)^t a].                  (3)

Since gamma<=beta_1=2^-16 and

    13/19-2^-32 >2/3,

we have H>=(s0-gamma²)I>(2/3)I. Every inverse in (3) therefore exists. Summing gives sum_i w_i=1, because the centered features sum to zero. Also

    sum_i w_i e(x_i)
      =b-N^-1 sum_i e(x_i)(e(x_i)-b)^t a
      =b-Ha=0.                                  (4)

Thus the rule Q_w f=sum_i w_i f(x_i) integrates every spherical polynomial of degree at most T exactly, including constants.

An equivalent formula, useful for checking the normalization, is

    t=b^t S^-1 b,
    w_i=[1-e(x_i)^t S^-1b]/[N(1-t)].             (5)

Here 0<=t<=gamma²/s0<1. The factor1/(1-t) is necessary. Omitting it leaves total mass1-t even though all nonconstant moments vanish. The exact finite replay below checks this nonzero omitted-normalization defect.

## 2. Positivity and rational computation

For every i, (2)–(3) yield

    |Nw_i-1|
      <=(sqrt(M)+gamma) gamma/(s0-gamma²)
      <=(3/2)(sqrt(M)+gamma)gamma
      <2(T+1)gamma
      <=1/[1024(T+1)^4]=:r_T<1.                 (6)

The last strict inequality uses sqrt(M)<T+1 and gamma<=2^-16<(T+1)/3. Consequently

    (1-r_T)/N <=w_i<=(1+r_T)/N,   w_i>0.         (7)

In particular the cubature weights have absolute sum1. Positivity follows uniformly from the acquired mean and Gram bounds; it is not a numerical observation or an assumed separation property.

To compute (3), use the programme's unnormalized rational orthogonal harmonic basis q_j and its rational squared norms nu_j. If q=D e in a normalized reference basis, then b_q=D b, H_q=D H D^t, and

    (q(x_i)-b_q)^t H_q^-1 b_q
       =(e(x_i)-b)^t H^-1b.                     (8)

Everything on the left is rational. Thus exact rational linear algebra computes the same weights without computing square roots for orthonormalization. The construction is invariant under an invertible change of harmonic basis.

There are M=O(T²) linear equations/unknowns in the H solve and N=O(T²) final evaluations. If node coordinates have L bits, polynomial harmonic coefficients have O(T log(T+1)) bits; evaluating and averaging them has polynomial height in T and L. Exact rational matrix inversion and the final dot products have polynomial bit complexity and polynomial output height, by denominator clearing and determinant bounds. Weight height may be substantially larger than node height; it is not claimed to be O(log T). This postprocessing can be omitted from an equal-weight output, where it is only an auxiliary mathematical construction.

These are classical minimum-L2 cubature weights. Indeed, among all vectors satisfying the constant and harmonic constraints, w is the minimum-Euclidean-norm solution: its difference from the uniform vector lies in the centered feature column space and is orthogonal to the homogeneous constraint kernel. Since the constant constraint fixes sum_i w_i=1, minimizing ||w||_2 is also minimizing the squared distance from the uniform vector. Chen–Womersley2017 §2 eq13–14 instead uses an L-infinity objective to keep relative deviations small; An–Hu–Yuan2026 §4.4 eq4.20 calls the L2 formulation classical. No weight-correction novelty is asserted.

## 3. Published Sobolev theorem and all pointwise constants

Use the specific normalized Sobolev norm

    ||f||_(H^s)²
      =sum_(l=0)^infinity sum_(m=1)^(2l+1)
          [1+l(l+1)]^s |fhat_(l,m)|²,            (9)

where the constant harmonic is1. For every real s>1, its reproducing kernel is finite on the diagonal. Cauchy–Schwarz and the addition theorem give

    ||f||_infinity <=E_s ||f||_(H^s),
    E_s²=sum_(l=0)^infinity
             (2l+1)/[1+l(l+1)]^s
         <=4+3/[2(s-1)].                         (10)

For the last bound, the l>=1 summands are at most3 l^(1-2s), and the decreasing positive series is at most1+1/[2(s-1)]. This gives an explicit finite point-evaluation constant for every real s>1, with no factor of T or N. The Fourier series also converges absolutely uniformly under this estimate, supplying the continuous representative used for evaluations.

Ian H. Sloan and Robert S. Womersley, *QMC Designs – Cubature on the Sphere Without Polynomial Exactness*, [published29May2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00365-026-09760-9), Theorem1.1 on printed page185, states the following established theorem: a family of positive cubature rules exact for every polynomial through degree T on S^d has worst-case H^s error bounded by C_(s,d) T^-s for every s>d/2. Corollary1.1 adds N(T)=O(T^d) to obtain the optimal QMC order. No separation, nesting or specially chosen nodes are required in that statement. The theorem is credited to Hesse and colleagues, with the arbitrary-dimension version Brauchart–Hesse2007. This worker directly read the 2026 primary statement and its Sobolev normalization; the older full proof is not claimed independently reconstructed here.

Their §2.3 permits any positive harmonic weights equivalent to [1+l(l+d-1)]^-s, which includes exactly the norm (9). Therefore a constant C_s=C_(s,2), independent of T and the specific positive rule, satisfies

    |Q_w f-integral f| <=C_s T^-s ||f||_(H^s).    (11)

C_s is the constant supplied by the published theorem; an explicit numerical formula for C_s was not recovered or invented. The pointwise constant E_s and all conversion factors below are explicit. Since (3) integrates constants exactly and (7) gives positive weights, all assumptions of this published theorem hold for our auxiliary rule.

## 4. Return to the equal-weight rule without losing a power of T

Let Q_u f=N^-1 sum_i f(x_i). Centering in (3) gives

    Q_w f-Q_u f=-a^t Q_u[(e-b)f].

Discrete Cauchy–Schwarz proves

    |Q_w f-Q_u f|
      <=[Q_u{a^t(e-b)}²]^(1/2) [Q_u f²]^(1/2)
      =(b^t H^-1 b)^(1/2) [Q_u f²]^(1/2)
      <=gamma/(s0-gamma²)^(1/2) ||f||_infinity
      <=sqrt(3/2) E_s gamma ||f||_(H^s).          (12)

This is stronger than bounding the relative weight deviations pointwise, which would unnecessarily introduce sqrt(M). Combining (11)–(12) yields the exact downstream estimate

    |Q_u f-integral f|
      <=[C_s T^-s+sqrt(3/2)E_s gamma]||f||_(H^s).
                                                    (13)

With the current gamma=beta_T, (13) gives O(T^-s)=O(N_T^-s/2) for every real1<s<=5. It does not, from that tolerance alone, establish the same rate for s>5. Such a claim needs a sharper residual or another argument.
