# Independent gate: the second-order spectral compensation

7 October 2026, cycle 8. **PASS after a harmless annulus-error exponent was corrected.** This is an independent reconstruction of `notes/pde_negative/SPECTRAL_COMPENSATION_SCALE.md`, not an independent external validation or a finite-index counterexample with a fast zero state.

## Interface checked

The frequency a is smooth, nondecreasing, at least one, tends to infinity, is subpower, and satisfies |a^(j)| <= C_j a/r^j. On a tail set b=(ra)^(-1/2), Theta'=a, g=b sin Theta; complete g smoothly, constant near zero. Let Vminus=Delta g and Vplus=Delta g+|grad g|^2 on Euclidean R^n. All conclusions below apply to these potential-only fixtures. Neither fixture is substituted for the actual repaired Landis potential.

## Reconstruction

For compactly supported chi, the cross term in |grad(e^g chi)|^2 is the divergence of e^(2g) grad(g) chi^2 minus e^(2g)(Delta g+2|grad g|^2)chi^2. Hence

    q_V(e^g chi)= integral e^(2g)[|grad chi|^2+(V-Delta g-|grad g|^2)chi^2].

There is no nodal quotient. This identity holds first for smooth chi and then for compact H^1 cutoffs by approximation. Thus Hplus is nonnegative because e^g is positive and multiplication by it bijects compact test spaces. For Hminus the effective term is exactly -|grad g|^2.

Differentiating independently gives

    g'=ba cos Theta+b' sin Theta,
    Delta g= -ba^2 sin Theta
       +(b''+(n-1)b'/r)sin Theta
       +(2b'a+ba'+(n-1)ba/r)cos Theta.

The derivative assumptions imply b^(j)=O_j(b/r^j). Therefore the Laplacian remainder is O(ba/r)=O(kappa/(ar)), kappa=a^(3/2)/sqrt(r), and

    (g')^2=(a/r)cos^2 Theta+O(r^(-2)).

Every fixed Cartesian derivative of both potentials is O_m(kappa a^m), tending to zero by subpower growth. The compensation a/r is smaller than kappa by (ar)^(-1/2), despite changing the spectral answer.

The first annulus proof is valid with the corrected integrated error. The pointwise estimate

    (g')^2 >= (a/(2r))cos^2 Theta-C/(a r^3)

integrated over an annulus of thickness comparable to s has error O(s^(n-3)/a(s)), not O(s^(n-4)/a(s)). The author corrected this after this gate identified it. The leading negative contribution is at least c a(s)s^(n-1), whereas the cutoff cost is O(s^(n-2)); the correction cannot change the sign. Monotonicity of a gives

    |integral_I exp(2iTheta) dr| <= C/a(s), I subset [s,2s],

because integration by parts has boundary cost at most 1/a(s) and remaining integral bounded by (1/2)integral a'/a^2 <= 1/(2a(s)). This does not require a'>0 or a quantitative lower bound on a'. Long flat intervals are covered.

For the sharper shell take L=B sqrt(s/a(s)) and chi=sin(pi(r-s)/L) on [s,s+L], zero otherwise. It is an admissible compact H^1 cutoff. Uniformly on this shell, L/s ->0, a(r)/a(s)->1, r/s->1, g->0, and La(s)->infinity. All follow from the actual derivative bounds, not regular variation or a fixed power law.

The weighted shell norm is (1+o(1))|S^(n-1)|s^(n-1)L/2. Its cutoff energy divided by that norm is (pi^2/B^2+o(1))a(s)/s. For the attractive term, the phase integral

    integral_0^1 sin^2(pi t)cos^2 Theta(s+Lt) dt ->1/4

follows by one integration by parts in the cosine term. Its phase derivative is 2La(s+Lt), bounded below by 2La(s), and its reciprocal derivative is controlled by C/(s a(s)). Thus the remainder tends to zero, also on flat frequency intervals. Uniform shell coefficient errors and e^(2g)=1+o(1) then give

    q_minus(e^g chi)/||e^g chi||^2
       =(pi^2/B^2-1/2+o(1)) a(s)/s,
    q_plus(e^g chi)/||e^g chi||^2
       =(pi^2/B^2+o(1)) a(s)/s.

For fixed B>pi sqrt(2), choose any escaping subsequence of pairwise disjoint shells. The minus tests span arbitrarily large negative-definite spaces because cross terms vanish. The bounded decaying potential has essential spectrum [0,infinity), so negative index infinity is precisely infinitely many negative eigenvalues accumulating at zero. No negative eigenvalue asymptotic is needed.

Finally, the wavelength-scale impossibility follows from the one-dimensional slab Poincare constant pi^2/d^2 and kappa/a^2 ->0. A test supported in a fixed C/a ball is eventually strictly positive for any potential obeying the actual O(kappa) bound. This statement concerns whole supported tests, not an energy contribution with its boundary cost omitted.

## Scientific consequence and limit

The actual repair is controlled at O(kappa), larger than the decisive compensation a/r by sqrt(ar). Neither its amplitude, vanishing jets, nor an o(kappa) approximation settles the index. The exact residual V-Delta g-|grad g|^2 at the scale a/r must be acquired on the actual construction, or another state-dependent argument must replace it. The matched fixtures do not prove this residual is negative for the actual pair. Their radiality in fact excludes using them as all-exponential zero-state examples.
