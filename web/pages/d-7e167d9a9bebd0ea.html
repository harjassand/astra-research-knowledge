## Question and legal interface

Can an origin-fixing nonlinear polynomial residual chart retain the actual Meng–Yang gradient collision after polynomial critical reduction, avoid every constant Hessian contraction, and make the constant-determinant obstruction more accessible? The known collision must pass the first-jet chain-rule condition before any large determinant expansion. A successful chart must still pass a separate constant-Hessian-determinant gate: nonlinear source automorphisms do not preserve that determinant.

The input is the v2 potential of Meng–Yang, arXiv2607.22198v2, Section 4, Theorem `thm:psi5`, and the pinned prior relative toric completion. The root's constant-pivot classification and the bridge's collision-compatibility note are programme prerequisites. We reconstruct the normalization identities and the actual original collision in the exact script. The primary theorem's general low-dimensional status assertions are not needed for the elementary obstruction below.

Use base variables b=(x,y), residual variables R=(rho,eta), and column residual outputs. The prior polynomial source normalization gives

    f0 = t^2 + a(b)t + c(b)R0,
    beta = y^2(6x^2y^2+15xy+4),
    a = 13+2beta,                  c = (2y,4x).

The two original zero-dual sources are b+=(1,-3/2), b-=(-1,3/2), with t=R0=0. Their normalized gradients are (0,0,sigma,c+) and (0,0,sigma,c-), where sigma=-19/2, c+=(-3,4), c-=-c+. Thus the normalized potential alone does not retain the collision. For an origin-fixing residual chart R0=Theta_b(R), put D(b)=D_R Theta_b(0). The exact compatibility condition for this pair is

    c+ D(b+) = c- D(b-),
    equivalently c+ [D(b+)+D(b-)] = 0.                 (J1)

For t-independent charts, the critical section is polynomial:

    t* = (sigma-a)/2 = -45/4-beta,
    psi(b,R) = f0(b,t*,Theta_b(R))-sigma t*
             = h0(b)+c(b)Theta_b(R),
    h0 = -(beta+45/4)^2.                              (J2)

At both chosen sources beta=-45/4, so t*=0. Equality of the reduced gradients is therefore equality of the full transformed gradients at the same critical level, with the t-component sigma restored. We work with this **actual reduced potential**. For a t-dependent chart, vanishing of a precritical residual jet alone is insufficient: eliminating t can introduce a nonzero residual Schur term. The theorem below must then be checked on the reduced potential itself.

## All-degree contracted-quadratic-jet obstruction

Let Theta_b be a polynomial fibre automorphism over C[x,y], fixing the origin. Its first jet D(b) has determinant delta in C*, and its entries are polynomials. Let h(b) be any polynomial, and set

    psi = h(b)+c(b)Theta_b(R),     G(b)=c(b)D(b).

Assume only that the contracted second residual jet of this reduced potential vanishes identically:

    sum_i c_i(b) D_R^2(Theta_b)_i(0) = 0.             (Z1)

Individual second jets of Theta need not vanish. On the entire zero section,

    Hpsi(b,0) = [ Hh(b)   DG(b)^T ]
                [ DG(b)       0  ],
    det Hpsi(b,0) = (Jac G(b))^2.                     (Z2)

The block identity follows by swapping the two two-row blocks; its sign is (-1)^4=+1. Consequently a constant nonzero full Hessian determinant forces Jac G to be a nonzero constant. The ideal generated by G1,G2 equals (x,y), since multiplication by the unimodular matrix D preserves the row ideal generated by (2y,4x). Thus the general two-base case returns to a plane Keller map with a unique scheme-theoretic zero. No general invertibility conclusion is drawn from this alone.

There is, however, an elementary all-degree conclusion when D depends on x alone. Write

    D(x) = [ a(x) b(x) ],   ad-bc=delta,
           [ c(x) d(x) ]
    G1 = 2ay+4xc,          G2 = 2by+4xd.

Direct differentiation gives

    Jac G = 4[(a'b-ab')y - 2delta + 2x(c'b-ad')].      (Z3)

If Jac G is constant, its y coefficient gives a'b-ab'=0. The unit determinant implies gcd(a,b)=1. If both are nonzero, the derivative of a/b is zero, so a/b is constant in characteristic zero; coprimality then forces both to be constants. If one vanishes, the determinant identity directly forces the other to be a nonzero constant. Differentiating ad-bc=delta gives ad'-bc'=0, hence the final x term in (Z3) vanishes and

    Jac G = -8delta.

An explicit polynomial inverse is

    x = (-bG1+aG2)/(4delta),
    y = (G1-4xc(x))/(2a)             if a != 0,
        (G2-4xd(x))/(2b)             otherwise.        (Z4)

Thus G is a polynomial automorphism. In particular, the last two coordinates of grad psi on the zero section already distinguish every pair of distinct bases. A fixed-base, origin-fixing residual chart with D=D(x) and (Z1) cannot both retain the known pair and have constant nonzero Hessian determinant, irrespective of the degrees or number of its higher fibre terms or of h. The condition is restricted to the reduced zero-section jet; it is neither a global constant-pivot assumption nor a global fibre-affinity assumption. A full Hessian-span example below shows the distinction.

## Minimum-degree compatible first jet

Every polynomial first-jet matrix D(x,y) of total degree at most one fails (J1). Indeed b-=-b+, so D(b+)+D(b-)=2D(0). Since D(0) is invertible, no nonzero c+ can annihilate this sum. Arbitrarily many higher fibre terms cannot repair that first-jet failure. This proves a degree-two lower bound **for the first-jet matrix**, not a lower bound for every possible full potential or for charts moving the base.

That lower bound is attained without the unnecessary stronger demand D(1)=I and D(-1)=-I. Set

    P = [ 1   4 ],       S(x) = [ x  x^2-1 ],
        [ 1/2 3 ]                [ 1    x   ]
    D2(x)=P S(x) P^-1
          = [ -x^2/2+x+25/2      x^2-17         ]
            [ 37/4-x^2/4        x^2/2+x-25/2 ].   (M1)

Both determinants are one. Since c+P=(-1,0),

    c+D2(1)=c+,       c+D2(-1)=-c+,
    c-D2(-1)=c+.                                     (M2)

Therefore (J1) holds. Put G=cD2. Its exact components are

    G1 = -x^3-x^2y+2xy+37x+25y,
    G2 =  2x^3+2x^2y+4x^2-50x-34y,
    Jac G = -4[x^3+x^2y+10x^2+8xy+25x+17y+2].        (M3)

The Jacobian is already nonconstant: it is -8 at the origin, 4 at b+, and -4 at b-. This exposes the precise zero-section obstruction for any chart with this first jet and (Z1).

## Odd nonlinear chart: all-degree obstruction without a constant pivot

Let

    T3=(rho+eta^3, eta+(rho+eta^3)^3),
    Theta_b=D2(x)T3,
    psi3=h0+G1(T3)_1+G2(T3)_2.                        (O1)

These are compositions of polynomial shears and a unimodular base matrix, so Theta is a polynomial fibre automorphism, fixes the origin, and has first jet D2. The individual second residual jets of T3 vanish at zero; hence (Z1) holds. The actual full and reduced collisions follow from (M2) and (J2).

Nevertheless psi3 has no nonzero constant symmetric Hessian contraction. Its ten positive-degree Hessian coefficient columns have rank10; an exact selected minor is recorded in the runnable evidence. This also excludes every nonzero constant direction v with D_v^2 psi3 constant. Thus zero-section jet vanishing does not imply a global constant pivot. At the same time

    det Hpsi3(b,0)
      =16[x^3+x^2y+10x^2+8xy+25x+17y+2]^2,           (O2)

so this whole odd-chart branch fails the constant-determinant gate. The obstruction extends to every other higher-order fibre automorphism sharing a one-base first jet and (Z1), not only to this finite example. Its use is to stop an entire superficially pivot-free class before expanding a determinant.

## Quadratic chart and a smaller exact repair frontier

The complementary nonlinear chart is

    T2=(rho+eta^2, eta+(rho+eta^2)^2),
    Theta_b=D2(x)T2,
    psi2=h0+G1(T2)_1+G2(T2)_2.                        (Q1)

It passes the actual collision and polynomial critical-section gates. Its reduced contracted quadratic jet is nonzero, so it escapes the particular hypothesis (Z1). It still has no nonzero constant symmetric Hessian contraction: the nonconstant Hessian coefficient matrix has shape57x10 and rank10. A selected exact minor has determinant89020579184640000, on rows eta, eta^2, eta^3, eta^4, y, y eta, y eta^2, y^2, y^4, x. The column order is (Hxx,Hxy,Hxrho,Hxeta,Hyy,Hyrho,Hyeta,Hrhorho,Hrhoeta,Hetaeta). Total potential degree is12 and Hessian determinant degree28. These are exact properties of one explicitly derived chart, not claims of optimality among arbitrary nonlinear charts.

For source order (x,y,rho,eta), the maximal cofactor degrees are

All Hessian entries have degree at most10; therefore every two-by-two minor has degree at most20. With L=x+y, the decisive homogeneous components are

Degree28 can only use two base-only entries; multiplying the leading determinant above by 8eta^2 g^2 gives the first identity in (Q3). This is sufficient for all universal repair exclusions: only rho,rho can affect degree28 and only rho,rho or rho,eta can affect degree27. The sharper exact table (Q2), including its15 entries, is retained evidence and is not a missing premise of the universal proof. All two-by-two minors have degree at most20 directly from the degree10 entry bound, and the script checks them individually as well.

Let R be an arbitrary polynomial potential repair, with k=deg HR. Determinant multilinearity gives

    deg[det(Hpsi2+HR)-det Hpsi2]
       <= max(25+k,20+2k,10+3k,4k).                   (Q5)

If deg R<=4, then k<=2 and this bound is27. The nonzero degree28 component survives. This excludes all repairs in that degree class, with unrestricted coefficients and support, whether or not they retain the collision.

For deg R=5, k<=3. Terms with two or more repair Hessians have degree at most26; only the linear cofactor term affects degrees28 and27. At degree28 only the rho,rho cofactor can contribute. Therefore the homogeneous part R5 is forced to satisfy

    (R5)_{rho,rho}=-(4/3)x^2L,
    R5=-(2/3)x^2L rho^2+rho A4(x,y,eta)+B5(x,y,eta),  (Q6)

with arbitrary homogeneous A4,B5 of degrees4,5. This is the full integrated solution of the leading equation, not a sparse search ansatz. The forced part's gradient vanishes at both zero-residual sources, so it preserves the known collision.

After adding that forced part, the degree27 determinant component restricted to eta=0 is

    -9732096 rho x^10 y^14 L^2.                       (Q7)

For an elementary derivation, the forced residual rho,rho Hessian changes 2g to (4/3)g. At eta=0 the degree7 term of the residual determinant becomes (16/3)rho g^2. Its product with the degree20 base determinant gives (Q7). Terms with at most one base-only Hessian factor have degree<=25 even after the forced repair.

The only free degree27 changes allowed by the full cofactor degree table are

    2 (adj Hpsi2)_{rho,eta,24} (A4)_eta
      +(adj Hpsi2)_{rho,rho,25}(R4)_{rho,rho}
    =7299072 eta x^8y^14L (A4)_eta
      -10948608 eta^2 x^8y^14L (R4)_{rho,rho},          (Q8)

where R4 is the arbitrary homogeneous degree4 part of the repair. Both vanish at eta=0. B5 and all other cofactor products have degree at most26. Thus (Q7) cannot vanish as a polynomial identity. **No polynomial potential repair of degree at most five can make det Hpsi2 constant**, even without a collision constraint.

Degree six is the first degree not excluded by this argument. That is a necessary frontier, not an existence theorem. Compared with the earlier degree-five base first jet and its degree-nine first possible repair, the degree-two chart gives a substantially smaller exact graded problem. It does not demonstrate that this problem has a solution, and it does not constitute progress on HC4 until the determinant and retained-witness gates can both be passed.

## Unrestricted degree-six leading compatibility

Let an arbitrary degree-six repair have homogeneous parts R6,R5,R4,... . Terms containing two repair Hessians have degree at most28, so degree29 can only come from the rho,rho cofactor of degree25. It forces (R6)_{rho,rho}=0, hence

    R6=rho A5(x,y,eta)+B6(x,y,eta).                  (S1)

Put p=(A5)_eta. At degree28 the two base-only factors reduce the complete leading equation to

    8g^2 eta^2+12g eta^2(R5)_{rho,rho}
       -8g eta p-p^2=0.                             (S2)

This includes the quadratic repair-Hessian product -p^2. All determinant terms with at most one base-only factor have degree<=25; all remaining original and repair fibre-Hessian factors have lower degree. Thus (S2) is the unrestricted leading equation, not a linearization or a choice of sparse coefficients.

At eta=0 it forces p=0, so p=eta v3 for a homogeneous polynomial v3 of degree3 in x,y,eta. Equation (S2) implies g divides v3^2. Since g=2x^2(x+y), unique factorization and coprimality of x and x+y give

    v3=x(x+y)ell,       ell=alpha x+beta1 y+gamma eta,
    (R5)_{rho,rho}=(x+y)(ell^2+16xell-32x^2)/24.     (S3)

The symbol beta1 is an independent scalar, not the base polynomial beta in (J2). The right side is independent of rho, so this condition fixes the full quadratic-in-rho part of R5.

Degree27 gives another universal restriction without expanding its entire free-polynomial equation. The degree8 leading determinant of the modified residual Hessian vanishes by (S2). Thus at degree27 the next base-determinant component cannot contribute, and only the degree20 base determinant times the degree7 residual determinant matters. At eta=0, put ell0=ell(x,y,0). The highest residual entries are

    Crr,3=(x+y)(ell0+8x)^2/24,
    Cre,4=0,
    Cee,4=rho x(x+y)(ell0+8x)+(B6)_{eta,eta}(x,y,0).

Their determinant's coefficient of rho is

    x(x+y)^2(ell0+8x)^3/24.                           (S4)

No free term in B6 or in the lower homogeneous repair parts carries rho into this coefficient. Nonvanishing of the base determinant therefore forces ell0=-8x. Consequently every degree-six candidate must have

    ell=-8x+gamma eta,
    A5=-4x^2(x+y)eta^2+(gamma/3)x(x+y)eta^3+A5,0(x,y),
    (R5)_{rho,rho}=-4x^2(x+y)+(gamma^2/24)(x+y)eta^2,  (S5)

where gamma is one scalar and A5,0 is an arbitrary homogeneous base polynomial of degree5. At this stage B6, the rho-linear and rho-constant parts of R5, and all lower repair parts remain free. Continuing the full leading equations gives the stronger selection result next.

## Every degree-six constant-determinant repair has a zero pivot

**Theorem.** If a polynomial repair R of degree at most six makes det H(psi2+R) constant, then (psi2+R)_{rho,rho}=0 identically. No collision, coordinate-legality or coefficient-support assumption is imposed. When the determinant is nonzero, root's independently reconstructed constant-pivot classification returns to an invertible branch or the exact plane Keller problem. Therefore no degree-at-most-six repair of this chart can produce a new constant-determinant potential escaping all constant pivots. This is not a proof that every degree-six repair fails to have constant determinant, and it does not settle the residual plane Jacobian problem.

Proof. Repairs of degree at most five are already excluded above, so use (S1)–(S5). Split the full Hessian into the base-only Hessian of h0 and the Hessian of the fibre part plus the entire repair. Write C for the latter's residual two-by-two block. After (R6)_{rho,rho}=0, the non-base-only entrywise degree bounds are

    [5 5 4 5]
    [5 5 4 5]
    [4 4 3 4]
    [5 5 4 5].                                      (P1)

Terms with zero base-only entries have degree<=20. For one base-only entry, the remaining three-by-three determinant has one base, rho and eta row and column. Its possible degree sums are bounded by13: 5+3+5, 5+4+4, 4+4+5, 4+4+5, or5+3+5. The base-only entry has degree<=10, so every such term has degree<=23. Thus

    det H(psi2+R)=(det H_base h0) det C + E,
    deg E<=23.                                      (P2)

Since det H_base h0 has degree20 and nonzero leading component, constancy of the full determinant forces deg(det C)<=3. In particular all homogeneous components of det C of degrees8,7,6,5,4 vanish. This argument includes all base-only and base-mixed repair terms.

After (S5), abbreviate L=x+y and let G2,j be the homogeneous degree-j part of G2. The leading residual entries and their next components are

    Crr,3=gamma^2 L eta^2/24,
    Cre,4=gamma xL eta^2,
    Cee,5=24x^2L eta^2,
    Crr,2=2G2,2+(R4)_{rho,rho},
    Cre,3=4eta G2,2+rho gamma^2 L eta/12+(A4)_eta,
    Cee,4=12eta^2G2,2+2gamma rho xL eta+(B6)_{eta,eta}.

The coefficient of rho in (det C)_7 therefore satisfies

    24x^2L eta^2 * coeff_rho[(R4)_{rho,rho}]
        -gamma^3 xL^2 eta^3/12=0.                    (P3)

All other free terms in that coefficient are rho-independent. Polynomiality forces coeff_rho[(R4)_{rho,rho}]=gamma^3 L eta/(288x). Multiplying by x and setting x=0 gives gamma^3 y eta=0, hence gamma=0. The entire degree7 equation then reduces to Crr,2*Cee,5=0, so

    (R4)_{rho,rho}=-2G2,2=-8x^2.                     (P4)

Now Crr has degree<=1, Cre has degree<=3, and Cee still has degree5 with the same nonzero leading component. The degree6 equation is

    24x^2L eta^2 [2G2,1+(R3)_{rho,rho}]
        -[16x^2eta+(A4)_eta]^2=0.                    (P5)

At eta=0 the square vanishes, so (A4)_eta=eta v2 for a homogeneous degree2 v2 independent of rho. Unique factorization implies xL divides16x^2+v2, because its square is divisible by x^2L. Both have degree2, hence16x^2+v2=kappa xL for one scalar kappa. Therefore

    Cre,3=kappa xL eta,          Crr,1=kappa^2 L/24,
    (A4)_eta=eta(-16x^2+kappa xL),
    (R3)_{rho,rho}=kappa^2 L/24-2G2,1.               (P6)

In particular (R3)_{rho,rho} is independent of rho and eta. The degree5 equation also vanishes by (P2). At degree4, only Crr,1*Cee,3 can carry a rho coefficient: Crr,0 is constant; Cee,4 is rho-independent after gamma=0; Cre,2 is independent of rho by (P4); and Cre,1 is independent of rho by (P6). The rho coefficient of Cee,3 is kappa xL, because the original4rho G2,2 and the rho(A4)_{eta,eta} part cancel except for that term. Consequently

    coeff_rho[(det C)_4]=kappa^3 xL^2/24.             (P7)

It must vanish, so kappa=0. With Crr,1=Cre,3=0, the already required degree5 equation is simply Crr,0*Cee,5=0, hence Crr,0=0. Thus Crr has no homogeneous component left and is identically zero. The degree4 equation additionally forces Cre,2=0, although that is not needed for the zero-pivot conclusion. This proves the theorem.

The minimal degree-two chart is still a useful scaffold, but its first possible degree-six determinant repair cannot escape root's prior constant-pivot boundary. A genuinely pivot-free repair would need degree at least seven. The argument neither supplies such a repair nor excludes higher degrees; it closes a complete degree-six class rather than a sparse failed search.

## Additional coordinate-realizability gate

The bridge's new complementary observation, saved in `notes/hessian_bridge12/cycle14/FIBRE_GRADIENT_IDEAL.md`, gives a useful distinction between a potential repair and a source-chart repair. Even if Theta depends on t, a polynomial critical section t=sigma*(b,R) for

    f=t^2+a(b)t+c(b)Theta(b,t,R),   partial_t f=sigma

satisfies the exact envelope identity

    partial_R[(f-sigma t)|critical] = c(b) J_R Theta|critical.

The critical-section derivative terms cancel by partial_t f-sigma=0. A base/t-fixed polynomial residual source automorphism has a residual Jacobian with a polynomial matrix inverse and a constant nonzero determinant. These identities persist after substituting the polynomial critical section. Multiplication by that matrix preserves the component ideal, so the stronger necessary condition is

    (psi_rho,psi_eta)=(x,y) in C[x,y,rho,eta].         (L0)

In particular **every such reduced potential has no fibre critical point over any nonzero base**. Origin fixing is not needed for this necessary condition. It does not imply the constant-Hessian condition or a global inverse. The ideal equality is stronger than its nonvanishing consequence and is needed in the next corollary.

Our universal repair exclusions permitted every potential repair, so they remain valid under this stronger coordinate-realizability requirement. But the forced leading repair in (Q6) is itself illegal as a source-chart candidate, even though its gradient preserves the known collision. At b=(1,0), G=(36,-44), and the repaired fibre derivatives vanish simultaneously whenever

    rho=(27-66eta^2)/67,
    44eta^3-18eta+737=0.                              (L1)

The second equation has a complex root, and the first has no denominator depending on that root. Exact substitution gives partial_rho=0 and partial_eta=-4(44eta^3-18eta+737)/67. Thus the leading cancellation cannot be promoted to a polynomial source chart in the declared class. These are coordinate-legality and determinant failures with separate proofs; retaining a collision and eliminating constant pivots alone passes neither gate.

The degree-six selection theorem concerns arbitrary potential repairs. Any remaining constant-pivot candidate must additionally pass this fibre-ideal gate, as well as the original critical-level and collision gates, before any source-coordinate realization can be claimed.

## Legal degree-six repairs lie in the invertible branch

**Corollary, with the inherited HC2 prior.** Suppose R has degree at most six, det H(psi2+R) is a nonzero constant, and the exact ideal equality (L0) holds. Then grad(psi2+R) is a polynomial automorphism. Therefore a degree-six repair available through the declared base/pivot-fixed residual source-coordinate interface cannot retain the actual collision while passing the constant-determinant gate. We do not assume that every potential repair is coordinate-realizable; (L0) is a necessary additional hypothesis.

Proof. The selection theorem makes the repaired potential

    psi= rho Q(x,y,eta)+P(x,y,eta).

The leading compatibility equations (S5), (P4), (P6), with gamma=kappa=0, cancel all higher-degree eta dependence of its rho coefficient. The remaining terms are

    Q=G1+A5,0(x,y)+A4,0(x,y)+A3,0(x,y)
          +A2(x,y,eta)+A1(x,y,eta)+A0,

where Aj is homogeneous of degree j. Consequently Q_eta has total degree at most one. The ideal hypothesis is

    (Q,P_eta+rho Q_eta)=(x,y).                        (I1)

Here is an elementary conormal lemma that uses the actual ideal, not merely its zero set. Put I=(x,y), S=C[rho,eta]. The free S-module I/I^2 has basis x,y. Since Q belongs to I, Q_eta does also, and its degree bound gives

    Q_eta=alpha x+beta1 y,
    Q mod I^2=(a0+alpha eta)x+(b0+beta1 eta)y,
    P_eta mod I^2=c(eta)x+d(eta)y.

Equality (I1) says these two generator classes span I/I^2, so their coefficient matrix over S has unit determinant:

    M=[ a0+alpha eta      b0+beta1 eta ]
      [ c(eta)+rho alpha d(eta)+rho beta1 ],
    det M in C*.                                     (I2)

The rho coefficient of its determinant is a0 beta1-b0 alpha, which must be zero. If (alpha,beta1) were nonzero, (a0,b0) would be a scalar multiple lambda(alpha,beta1). The first row would then vanish at eta=-lambda, contradicting the unit determinant. Hence alpha=beta1=0 and

    Q_eta=0,       Q=Q(x,y).                          (I3)

The Hessian's rho row is now (Q_x,Q_y,0,0); a nonzero constant full determinant implies grad Q is nowhere zero. Moreover P_etaeta is nonzero: its uncancellable degree5 part is24x^2(x+y)eta^2, from the original total-degree7 term2x^2(x+y)eta^4. A degree-six repair cannot remove that degree7 potential term.

The exact bordered determinant formula is

    det Hpsi=(Q_x P_yeta-Q_y P_xeta)^2
      -P_etaeta[Q_y^2P_xx-2Q_xQ_yP_xy+Q_x^2P_yy+rho K_Q],
    K_Q=Q_y^2Q_xx-2Q_xQ_yQ_xy+Q_x^2Q_yy.             (I4)

Its rho coefficient and P_etaeta!=0 force K_Q=0. The root's binary zero-flex lemma now makes Q affine. For completeness, its elementary global argument is as follows. On each smooth irreducible fibre component, the tangent derivation Q_y partial_x-Q_x partial_y annihilates Q_x/Q_y when Q_y does not vanish identically. A nonzero derivation of a characteristic-zero function field of one variable has only constant elements in its kernel: a nonconstant invariant would make the field a finite separable extension of a subfield on which the derivation vanishes.
