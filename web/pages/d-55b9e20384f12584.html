Let W_kj=binom(l,j)binom(N-l,k-j)/binom(N,k), with out-of-range terms0.
The degree elevation identity B_l,j=sum_k W_kj B_N,k has W>=0. Integration
on[0,1] and integral B_m,i=1/(m+1) gives EXACTLY
 sum_k W_kj=(N+1)/(l+1), for every column j.
Thus for Legendre coefficients d_l,j=(-1)^(l-j)binom(l,j),
 sum_k|sum_j W_kj d_l,j|
 <=sum_j |d_l,j|sum_k W_kj=(N+1)2^l/(l+1).
If each population residual is bounded by tau, the correction functional obeys
 |E(R_l)|<=tau(N+1)2^l/(l+1).
The exact Legendre dual correction still integrates to E(f) for all degree<=N
polynomials. Its uniform norm is therefore bounded by

 ||PC||inf <=(N+1)tau sum_l=0^N [(2l+1)/(l+1)]2^l
            <(N+1)(2^(N+2)-2)tau <(N+1)2^(N+2)tau.

Every step is an exact identity or triangle inequality. It requires no favorable
residual sign, positivity of PC, numerical integration or external moment
extension theorem. The column-sum bound is reusable with any positive approximate
Bernstein density and a uniform population residual. It improves the original
sup-coefficient bound by a factor of order N and can lower analytic admissions
at larger delta. I independently reconstructed this derivation after source
