## G3. Independent derivation of the full derivative constants

For an orthonormal probability-area real harmonic basis of degree l, the addition kernel is (2l+1)P_l(x·y). The normalization follows from the standard area-normalized addition theorem and orthogonality in [NIST DLMF14.30.8–9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/14.30#E9). This gate independently differentiates that kernel; the constants below are not taken from a numerical backend.

Restrict x(t),x(s) to one unit-speed great circle, so x(t)·x(s)=cos(t−s). Put λ=l(l+1). The Legendre differential equation at1 gives P_l'(1)=λ/2 and P_l''(1)=λ(λ−2)/8. Two derivatives of the kernel therefore give the squared normalized directional gradient norm (2l+1)λ/2. Four derivatives give squared directional covariant Hessian norm

    (2l+1)[P_l'(1)+3P_l''(1)]
      =(2l+1)λ(3λ−2)/8.

Summing l=0,...,d yields

    A_d²=d(d+1)²(d+2)/4,
    B_d²=d(d+1)²(d+2)[d(d+2)−1]/8.

Both formulas can be proved without a symbolic summation oracle: subtract their values at d−1; the differences are exactly the corresponding summands at l=d, and both vanish at d=0. Passing to an invariant orthonormal subvector can only reduce these Euclidean feature norms.

For a vector-valued symmetric Hessian T, ||T(w,w)||≤B|w|² also implies ||T(v,w)||≤B|v||w|. First rescale v,w to equal length, then use T(v,w)=[T(v+w,v+w)−T(v−w,v−w)]/4. The parallelogram identity supplies the stated constant. No factor2 or missing tangent dimension is required.
