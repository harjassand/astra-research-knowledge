## Interface and theorem

Supply a known list of distinct canonical nonidentity Hermitian Pauli strings `P_j`, each supported on at most k qubits. At most r listed strings act on each site. The target is

\[
\sigma=\frac{e^{-\beta H_*}}{Z_*},\qquad
H_*=\sum_j\theta_j^*P_j,\qquad|\theta_j^*|\le J,
\]

with known `β>0`. A candidate uses the same supplied list,
`H=Σ_j θ_j P_j`, `ρ=e^{-βH}/Z`. No candidate coefficient bound is needed for the population inequality. Identity coefficients are excluded because Gibbs normalization makes them unidentifiable.

For a known Hermitian direction A, define the entire weighted score through

\[
-i[A,\tau]=(1-\lambda)\tau S_A^{\tau,\lambda}
+\lambda S_A^{\tau,\lambda}\tau.
\]

Let `I_{σ,λ}=(1−λ)L_σ+λR_σ` and use the source normalization

\[
\overline J(\rho;\sigma)=\frac12\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
\int_0^1\langle S_A^{\rho,\lambda}-S_A^{\sigma,\lambda},
I_{\sigma,\lambda}(S_A^{\rho,\lambda}-S_A^{\sigma,\lambda})\rangle_F\,d\lambda.
\tag{1}
\]

The measure is uniform on the **entire** interval `(0,1)`; endpoints have measure zero. This is not a uniform law clipped to `[δ,1−δ]`. Set

\[
s=\min\{n,1+r(k-1)\},\qquad b=\beta Jr s,
\qquad\alpha=\frac{e^{-2b}}{1+b\,2^{s/2}e^b}.
\tag{2}
\]

For the physical frame `{X_i,Z_i}`,

\[
\boxed{\overline J(\rho;\sigma)
\ge2\beta^2\alpha\sum_j|\operatorname{supp}P_j|
(\theta_j-\theta_j^*)^2
\ge2\beta^2\alpha\|\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2.}
\tag{3}
\]

For the full frame `{X_i,Y_i,Z_i}`, the factor 2 in (3) becomes 4. For fixed β,J,k,r this is independent of n. The precise support-weighted X/Z count is between one and two times each support size; the displayed lower bound uses the smaller value.

This proves global quadratic growth from the correct parameter and state uniqueness within the prescribed model at every finite β. It does not prove convexity, the absence of other stationary points, a gradient-dominance inequality, an optimizer, an efficiently measurable loss, or a polynomial-copy learner. If the objective is averaged over sites instead of summed, the right side is divided by n as well; the intensive version then controls the mean squared coefficient error.

## 1. Correct metric produced by uniform integration

Define the logarithmic-mean superoperator

\[
\Omega_\sigma(X)=\int_0^1\sigma^tX\sigma^{1-t}\,dt.
\]

In a σ eigenbasis, its multiplier is
`L(p_i,p_j)=(p_i−p_j)/(log p_i−log p_j)`, with `L(p,p)=p`. Direct scalar integration gives

\[
\int_0^1I_{\sigma,\lambda}^{-1}\,d\lambda
=\Omega_\sigma^{-1},\qquad
\int_0^1S_A^{\tau,\lambda}\,d\lambda=-i[A,\log\tau].
\tag{4}
\]

Write `X_λ=S_A^{ρ,λ}−S_A^{σ,λ}`, `z=∫X_λ`, and `Y=Ω_σ(z)`. Integrating the nonnegative quantity
`||X_λ−I_{σ,λ}^{−1}Y||²_{σ,λ}` and expanding shows

\[
\int_0^1\|X_\lambda\|_{\sigma,\lambda}^2d\lambda
\ge\langle z,\Omega_\sigma(z)\rangle_F.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\overline J(\rho;\sigma)
\ge\frac12\sum_A
\|-i[A,\log\rho-\log\sigma]\|_{\Omega_\sigma}^2
=\frac{\beta^2}{2}\sum_A
\|-i[A,H-H_*]\|_{\Omega_\sigma}^2.
\tag{5}
\]

The metric is logarithmic mean/BKM, not the arithmetic/GNS metric `Trσ X†X`. Equation (4) is exactly why the full interval is essential. No target eigenbasis or inverse metric is supplied to an estimator; they occur in the proof of this population inequality.

## 2. An elementary local Gibbs marginal floor

For a region S with local Hilbert dimension `d_S`, split the target Hamiltonian as

\[
H_*=I_S\otimes H_{\bar S}+V_S,
\qquad h_S=\|V_S\|_{\rm op},
\]

where V contains all terms whose supports intersect S. Then, for every unit vector ψ on S,

\[
\langle\psi|\sigma_S|\psi\rangle
\ge\frac{e^{-2\beta h_S}}{d_S}.
\tag{6}
\]

Proof: take an eigenbasis `e_a` of `H_{bar S}`, with eigenvalues E_a. Scalar convexity of the exponential in the spectral measure of `H_*` gives

\[
\langle\psi e_a|e^{-\beta H_*}|\psi e_a\rangle
\ge e^{-\beta\langle\psi e_a|H_*|\psi e_a\rangle}
\ge e^{-\beta h_S}e^{-\beta E_a}.
\]

Summing a bounds the marginal numerator below by `e^{−βh_S}Z_{bar S}`. Weyl eigenvalue perturbation, applied to `I_S⊗H_{bar S}` and the Hermitian perturbation V of norm h_S, bounds the full partition function above by `d_S e^{βh_S}Z_{bar S}`. Their ratio gives (6). This uses scalar Jensen and an eigenvalue/trace bound; it never uses the false assertion that the matrix exponential is operator monotone.

Thus, for any supported operator X,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma X^\dagger X)
\ge e^{-2\beta h_S}\|X\|_{\tau_S}^2,
\qquad\tau_S=I_S/d_S.
\tag{7}
\]

For supported Hermitian X, additionally
`TrσX²≥(e^{−2βh_S}/d_S)||X||op²`. This is a bounded-support statement: it does not claim such a linear lower bound for arbitrary extensive sums with many separated supports.

For the supplied target list, at most r|S| terms intersect S, so `h_S≤Jr|S|`. Neither the global norm `||H_*||` nor the global smallest Gibbs eigenvalue occurs.

## 3. Logarithmic mean from a modular spectral moment

For Hermitian X, set

\[
G=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma X^2),\qquad
E_2=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma[\beta H_*,X]^\dagger[\beta H_*,X]).
\]

If X≠0, then

\[
\boxed{\|X\|_{\Omega_\sigma}^2
\ge\frac{G}{1+\tfrac12\sqrt{E_2/G}}.}
\tag{8}
\]

To prove this, put `a_ij=(p_i+p_j)/2`, `u_ij=(log p_i−log p_j)/2`, and probability weights `w_ij=a_ij|X_ij|²/G`. Hermiticity ensures `Σa_ij|X_ij|²=G`. The logarithmic/arithmetic multiplier ratio is `tanh(u_ij)/u_ij`, interpreted as 1 at zero. The scalar inequality

\[
\frac{\tanh u}{u}\ge\frac1{1+|u|}
\]

follows for u≥0 from `e^{2u}≥1+2u`, and is even. Jensen for the convex function `t↦1/(1+t)`, then Cauchy–Schwarz, gives

\[
\sum_{ij}w_{ij}\frac{\tanh u_{ij}}{u_{ij}}
\ge\frac1{1+\sum w_{ij}|u_{ij}|}
\ge\frac1{1+\sqrt{\sum w_{ij}u_{ij}^2}}.
\]

Because `logσ=−βH_*−logZ_*`, the last second moment is exactly `E_2/(4G)`. This proves (8) for any faithful state and any Hermitian X.

If X is supported on S, only V_S enters its Hamiltonian commutator. Therefore

\[
E_2\le4\beta^2 h_S^2\|X\|_{\rm op}^2,
\qquad
G\ge\frac{e^{-2\beta h_S}}{d_S}\|X\|_{\rm op}^2.
\]

Combining these inequalities with (7)–(8) yields the supported-operator BKM faithfulness bound

\[
\boxed{\|X\|_{\Omega_\sigma}^2
\ge\alpha_S\|X\|_{\tau_S}^2,\qquad
\alpha_S=\frac{e^{-2\beta h_S}}
{1+\beta h_S\sqrt{d_S}e^{\beta h_S}}.}
\tag{9}
\]

This is independent of exterior system size for fixed support and interaction strength. It uses only a second modular moment. A KMS/geometric-mean norm is smaller than the logarithmic mean, but a comparable elementary second-moment lower bound need not hold for it; existing complex-time/KMS local-faithfulness theorems solve a different metric problem.

## 4. Local stars and coefficient orthogonality

For each physical single-site Pauli A_i, let
`C_{A_i}=−i[A_i,H−H_*]`, a Hermitian operator. Its support is contained in the union of listed strings acting at i. This union has at most `s=1+r(k−1)` sites. On that region, `h_S≤Jrs` and `d_S≤2^s`, so (9) bounds every local commutator with the common α in (2).

The maximally mixed norm agrees whether evaluated on S or on the full system. Distinct Pauli strings are orthonormal, and multiplication by fixed A_i preserves that orthogonality. Thus

\[
\|C_{A_i}\|_\tau^2
=4\sum_{j:\{A_i,P_j\}=0}(\theta_j-\theta_j^*)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

For each nonidentity single-site Pauli component, at least one of X_i and Z_i anticommutes with it; exactly two of X_i,Y_i,Z_i do. Summing gives

\[
\sum_{i,A=X,Z}\|C_{A_i}\|_\tau^2
\ge4\sum_j|\operatorname{supp}P_j|\,\delta\theta_j^2,
\]

\[
\sum_{i,A=X,Y,Z}\|C_{A_i}\|_\tau^2
=8\sum_j|\operatorname{supp}P_j|\,\delta\theta_j^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Insert (9)–(11) into (5) to obtain (3). The same marginal bound also proves the originally requested GNS inequality

\[
\sum_{i,A=X,Z}\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma C_{A_i}^2)
\ge4e^{-2\beta Jr s}\|\delta\theta\|_2^2,
\]

with 8 replacing 4 for XYZ. However, the mixture controls BKM; (8)–(9) are the necessary transfer step.

## 5. Independent integrated-potential and copy-observable audit

For a faithful unnormalized τ, let
`G_λ(τ)=.5Σ_A⟨∂_Aτ,I_{τ,λ}^{−1}∂_Aτ⟩_F`. Its variational representation as a supremum of affine functions of τ makes it convex and positively homogeneous. Each G_λ is the source proper-score potential. Entrywise integration gives

\[
\overline G_A(\tau)
=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(\tau[A,[A,\log\tau]]).
\tag{12}
\]

For involutions `A²=I`, which includes physical Paulis,

\[
\overline G_A(\tau)
=\operatorname{Tr}\tau(\log\tau-A\log\tau A)
=D(\tau\|A\tau A).
\]

Differentiate using selfadjointness of the Fréchet logarithm derivative:

\[
\nabla\overline G_A(\tau)
=\log\tau+I-A\log\tau A-D\log_\tau(A\tau A).
\tag{13}
\]

Positive homogeneity supplies `Trτ∇Gbar(τ)=Gbar(τ)`. The integrated score objective is its Bregman regret with target first and candidate second,

\[
\overline J(\rho;\sigma)
=\overline G(\sigma)-\overline G(\rho)
-\operatorname{Tr}(\nabla\overline G(\rho)(\sigma-\rho))
=\overline G(\sigma)+\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma\overline O(\rho)),
\]

where `Obar=−∇Gbar`. For dimensionless candidate `K=βH`, the involution summand is

\[
\overline O_A(K)=K-AKA-I
+D\log_{e^{-K}}(Ae^{-K}A).
\tag{14}
\]

The partition function cancels because the logarithm derivative scales inversely with its base. These identities were independently reconstructed and agree with `c_priorart`. Convexity of Gbar in density matrices does not establish convexity of the reverse Bregman regret as a function of Gibbs Hamiltonian parameters. `r09_algebra` is independently auditing that distinction.

Equation (14) depends only on a supplied candidate, but its last term is generally global and modular. A formula for an observable is not a cheap measurement compiler. Uniform λ also approaches the endpoint scores, so a bound for each clipped interior λ cannot justify uniformly sampling and measuring the full mixture at bounded cost. Candidate operator norms, frequency tails, locality/truncation, shot variance, repeated optimization queries and numerical precision must be charged. The coefficient lower bound does not settle any of them.

### Independent nonconvexity audit

`r09_algebra` supplied an exact faithful one-qubit counterexample to Hamiltonian convexity, which we independently reconstructed. For the full physical XYZ frame and dimensionless candidate `K=h·Pauli`, put `r=||h||`, `n=h/r`. Equations (12)–(14) reduce to

\[
\overline G(\rho_h)=4r\tanh r,\qquad
\overline O(h)=4\sinh^2r\,I
+4(r+\sinh r\cosh r)n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma.
\]

Take `h(t)=(t,0,3+t)` and target
`σ=(I−q·Pauli)/2`, `q=(999/1000)(16,0,63)/65`. The target is faithful, with smallest eigenvalue `1/2000`. Up to a constant, its candidate loss is
`f(t)=4sinh²r−4(r+sinh rcosh r)q·n`. At t=0,
`r=3`, `r′=1`, `r″=1/3`, `n′_x=1/3`, `n′_z=0`, `n″_x=−2/9`, `n″_z=−1/9`. Substituting these exact derivatives gives

\[
f''(0)=-\frac{263}{15000}e^6
+\frac{1183799}{195000}e^{-6}<0.
\]

The sign follows from `e^{12}>1+12+12²/2+12³/6=373>1183799/3419`. Our independent symbolic simplification agrees exactly, and the numerical value is approximately −7.0584. Rescale physical Hamiltonians by β=4 if a unit coefficient cube is wanted: the target physical coefficients are `(atanh(.999)/4)(16,0,63)/65`, both below one, and candidates `(3Z+t(X+Z))/4` lie inside the cube near t=0. This nonconvexity and (3)'s quadratic growth are compatible; a global optimizer still requires its own construction.
