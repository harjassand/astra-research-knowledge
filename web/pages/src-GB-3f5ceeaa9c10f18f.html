## 1. Exact optimal simulator

A decoder of E_q is determined by three output states: an erase state omega and two revealed states tau_i. It cannot exploit coherences between source outcomes, because E_q's output algebra is classical. The proposed decoder prepares north pole on erase and Bloch vector `(i a,0,b-s)/L` on reveal. Its Bloch error is exactly

`(1-q/L)(i a,0,b-s)`.

For qubits, trace norm of the state difference equals Euclidean distance of the Bloch vectors. Thus each column error is `L-q`, with no factor 1/2.

For completeness, the CQ diamond equality covers arbitrary ancillary operators. For any input/ancilla operator X, dephasing leaves blocks X_ii and the channel difference is `sum_i Delta_i tensor X_ii`. Its trace norm is at most

`max_i ||Delta_i||_1 sum_i ||X_ii||_1 <= max_i ||Delta_i||_1 ||X||_1`.

The last inequality is pinching contractivity, obtainable by averaging unitary conjugations; it holds without Hermiticity of X. A basis-state input attains the bound. Hence the diamond norm is exactly the maximum column trace norm. This argument depends on both maps measuring the same input basis and makes no statement about a coherence-preserving qubit-input channel or a purified oracle.

To prove optimality, average any decoder with the transformation exchanging inputs and conjugating the output by Z. Convexity of the maximum column error cannot increase it. The erase Bloch vector is then `(0,0,z)`, `-1<=z<=1`, and the revealed states are related by Z. For fixed z, the reachable plus-input output is the full ball of radius q centered at `(0,0,s z)`; symmetry gives the identical minus-input error. Its distance to the target is

`max(0,sqrt[a^2+(b-s z)^2]-q)`.

Because `q>=c`, `b>=sqrt(s)>s`, so the minimizing center is z=1. Also `L>q` because `s>0` and `b<1`. Thus

`min_(D CPTP) ||T_a-D E_q||_diamond = delta_*=L-q`.

The stated parameter region is sufficient and correct. No extension beyond it is needed for this receiving audit.

## 2. Risk and adaptive discrimination factors

For arbitrary binary-label prior and arbitrary finite reference, the label/output/reference states under T_a and its simulator are normalized and have trace-norm distance at most delta_*. Clipping estimates to [-1,1] gives a loss observable `0<=K<=4I`. Their state difference has trace zero, so

`|Tr K(rho-rho')|=|Tr (K-2I)(rho-rho')|<=2||rho-rho'||_1`.

Therefore the factor in the MMSE upper bound is **2 delta_***, or four times trace distance. The source can run the physical simulator and target receiver, and its optimum only improves upon that choice. Unequal reference marginals cause no problem. The extra risk bound one follows from the prior-mean estimator.

For n adaptive uses, hold the complete strategy fixed and replace one channel use at a time. Standard interleaving CPTP operations and arbitrary finite quantum memory are allowed. Each replacement changes the final state by at most delta_*; future processing is trace-norm contractive. Hence final trace-norm separation is at most `n delta_*`.

Equal-prior discrimination obeys `p_success=1/2+||rho-rho'||_1/4`, giving

`p_success<=1/2+n delta_*/4`.

Thus success at least 3/4 requires `n>=1/delta_*` (and an integer use count requires the ceiling). This normalization is consistent with the diamond convention without a factor 1/2.

The hypotheses are the target memoryless channel and this fixed source-simulable memoryless alternative, with ordinary channel access and inaccessible environments. Extra calibration promises, access to preparation randomness, or a coherent Stinespring oracle change the discrimination problem. The bound is consequently not a universal lower bound for every task-specific calibration method.

## 3. Matching ideal IID order

Fix `0<q<1` and `0<kappa<1` while c tends to zero. At the Bell-family optimum,

`x_*=(1-c/sqrt(q))/(1-c)` and `t^2=kappa delta`.

The ordinary-dominance assumption q>=c places the construction in the stationary branch q>=c^2. The exact source and target explained variances are

`G_source=kappa c sqrt(q)(1-sqrt(q))/(1-c)`,

`G_target=kappa c (1-sqrt(q))/(1-c)`.

Their sum is `kappa c(1-q)/(1-c)`, and their difference is `kappa c(1-sqrt(q))^2/(1-c)`. Both the variance scale and the risk gap are therefore proportional to c with fixed positive constants in this regime.

Optimal binary scores have spectrum in [-1,1]. One direct proof evaluates the SLD equation in a score eigenvector: its eigenvalue is the ratio of a tangent expectation to a positive baseline expectation; positivity of both hypotheses bounds that ratio by one. For score Y and label ell,

`loss-1=Y(Y-2ell)` and `|Y-2ell|<=3`.

Thus `Var(loss)<=E[(loss-1)^2]<=9E[Y^2]=9G`. The last equality uses the optimal SLD receiver. It is not asserted for an arbitrary suboptimal fixed score merely from its risk reduction.

Across two independent evaluation arms, the variance sum is O(c), each centered loss is bounded by four, and the true gap is Theta(c). Bernstein therefore has exponent proportional to

`n gap^2/(variance_sum+constant*gap)=Theta(n c)`.

A fixed nonzero-confidence ordering or a confidence interval of radius proportional to the gap needs a sufficient `O(1/c)=O(a^-2)` trials per arm. The total across two arms has the same order. No calibration, fitting, loss, drift, reset, or preparation cost is included.

Meanwhile

`delta_*=(1-q)c/(2q)+O(c^2)`

at fixed q, so rejecting the displayed simulator needs `Omega(1/c)` channel uses in the discrimination task. These are matching orders for the explicitly related audit/evaluation problems, not an exact minimax sample complexity for all possible experimental evidence interfaces.

The reference preparation remains a paid resource: optimal delta is Theta(c), and its smaller eigenvalue is Theta((1-kappa)c). A target Bell sector occurs with probability Theta(c) and carries an order-one score. This explains why the variance shrinks with the payoff while conditioning and rare-sector calibration become harder.
