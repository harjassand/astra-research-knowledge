\begin{abstract}
We derive explicit channels giving uniform strong equivalence between the full iid quantum experiment near the maximally mixed state and a classical Gaussian location experiment. For fixed $d$, on $\|d\rho-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta\le1/2$, the target is $\mathcal N(\sqrt n(\rho-I/d),I_{\HS}/d)$ on traceless Hermitian matrices. Both channel errors are at most $C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8})$. Thus every shrinking neighborhood is covered, with unknown spectrum and eigenbasis and no cubic-radius restriction. The mechanism combines coherent classicalization, a Schur--HCIZ cancellation, a matrix log-Gamma density, and invertible lattice jitter. Separately, for a known moving spectrum, put $B_n(p)=\prod_{i<j}(1+n(p_i-p_j)^2)$. A classical code uses at most $C_d\varepsilon^{-2(d^2-1)}B_n(p)$ labels with error at most $1-\prod_{i<j}p_j/p_i+\varepsilon$; every quantum or hybrid code of error at most $1/8$ needs at least $c_dB_n(p)$ dimensions. We also give optimal leading storage for unknown-spectrum balls, a cloning consequence, interacting Gibbs examples, and a precision obstruction. All new proofs remain externally unreviewed; priority is unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Interface and the principal law}
All errors are $T(\sigma,\rho)=\frac12\|\sigma-\rho\|_1$. The input is $\rho_U^{\otimes n}$ with $\rho_U=U\diag(p)U^*$ and unknown $U$. The spectrum is known in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The cost is $\log_2 D$ for the entire retained classical, quantum, or hybrid memory. Exact Schur labels, real outcomes, and auxiliary registers are not free. Decoder randomness is allowed. Reconstruction preserves the density matrix; it need not preserve a purification. Complexity of ideal channels and compilation is distinguished from retained storage.

Write $p_1\ge\cdots\ge p_d>0$ and define
\begin{equation}
 S(p)=\sum_{i<j}\log(p_i/p_j),\quad c(p)=e^{-S(p)},\quad
 B_n(p)=\prod_{i<j}\bigl(1+n(p_i-p_j)^2\bigr).
 \label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
Unsubscripted logarithms are natural. Constants with subscript $d$ depend only on fixed $d$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite known-spectrum bound]\label{thm:main}
Fix $d\ge2$. If $1/(2d)\le p_i\le3/(2d)$, then for every $n\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ there is a finite classical code satisfying
\begin{equation}
 D\le C_d\varepsilon^{-2(d^2-1)}B_n(p),\qquad e\le1-c(p)+\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:upper}
\end{equation}
Every quantum or hybrid code with uniform error $e\le1/8$ satisfies $D\ge c_d B_n(p)$.
\end{theorem}
The finite upper bound is an information-theoretic construction. Its precision factor is not optimized. It makes no useful vanishing-error assertion for a fixed nontrivial spectrum: then $1-c(p)$ stays positive.

\begin{corollary}[Arbitrary moving collisions]\label{cor:leading}
If $p_n\to(1/d,\ldots,1/d)$ and $H_n=\log_2 B_n(p_n)\to\infty$, every vanishing-error code eventually obeys $\log_2D_n\ge H_n-O_d(1)$, and a classical code obeys
\[
 \log_2D_n\le H_n+O_d(\log(2+H_n)),\qquad e_n\to0.
\]
Thus the optimal leading storage is $H_n$, with zero retained qubits achievable.
\end{corollary}
Choose $\varepsilon_n=1/(2+H_n)$ once it is below $1/4$. The condition $H_n\to\infty$ is essential: a bounded positive statistical signal generally needs an unbounded memory to achieve vanishing error, although that divergence may be arbitrarily slow.

For $\rho=I/d+\delta UAU^*$ with fixed nonzero traceless $A$ and eigenvalue multiplicities $m_1,\ldots,m_r$, the orbit dimension is $s=d^2-\sum_bm_b^2$. If $\delta\to0$ and $\kappa=n\delta^2\to\infty$, the law becomes
\[
 \log_2D=\frac{s}{2}\log_2\kappa+o(\log\kappa).
\]
For three levels with two gaps of order $n^{-\alpha}$ and the remaining gap of order $n^{-\beta}$, $0<\alpha<\beta$, the leading coefficient of $\log_2n$ is
\[
 2(1-2\alpha)_++(1-2\beta)_+.
\]
This is a collision-sensitive law, not a substitution into a fixed-spectrum expansion.

\section{One universal coherent channel}
Schur--Weyl decomposition gives
\[
 \rho^{\otimes n}\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}
 p_\lambda\tau_{\lambda,\rho}\otimes I_{m_\lambda}/m_\lambda,
 \qquad \tau_{\lambda,\rho}=\pi_\lambda(\rho)/Z_\lambda,
 \quad p_\lambda=m_\lambda Z_\lambda.
\]
Here $\pi_\lambda$ is the polynomial $\mathrm{GL}(d,\C)$ representation, $Z_\lambda=\tr\pi_\lambda(\rho)$, and $h_\lambda=\dim V_\lambda$. A highest-weight coherent projector $P_\lambda(F)$, indexed by a complete flag, satisfies
\[
 h_\lambda\int P_\lambda(F)\dd\mu(F)=I_{V_\lambda}.
\]
Measure this POVM and prepare the same projector. Denote the resulting channel by $C_\lambda$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coherent domination]\label{thm:dom}
For every faithful $\rho$, every highest weight, and every $n$,
\[
 C_\lambda(\tau_{\lambda,\rho})\ge c(p)\tau_{\lambda,\rho}.
\]
The corresponding Schur-sector channel $C_{n,d}$ is independent of both spectrum and eigenbasis and obeys
\[
 C_{n,d}(\rho^{\otimes n})\ge c(p)\rho^{\otimes n},\qquad
 T(C_{n,d}(\rho^{\otimes n}),\rho^{\otimes n})\le1-c(p).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a flag represented by a unitary $U$, write $U_k$ for its first $k$ columns and
\[
 Q_\lambda(B;U)=\langle\lambda,U|\pi_\lambda(B)|\lambda,U\rangle
 =\det(B)^{\lambda_d}\prod_{k<d}\det(U_k^*BU_k)^{\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}}.
\]
For $gU=VR$ in positive-diagonal QR form, the real flag Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
 J_g(U)=\prod_{i<j}(R_{jj}/R_{ii})^2
 =\frac{\det(g^*g)^{d-1}}{\prod_{k<d}\det(U_k^*g^*gU_k)^2}.
 \label{eq:jac}
\end{equation}
Indeed the lower-root complex tangent coordinate $ji$ is multiplied by $R_{jj}/R_{ii}$; the real determinant is its squared complex determinant modulus.

Filter the coherent resolution by $\pi_\lambda(\rho^{1/2})$ and change flags. This gives an exact positive mixture
\[
 \tau_{\lambda,\rho}=\int P(U)P_\lambda(U)\dd\mu(U),\qquad
 P(U)=\frac{h_\lambda}{Z_\lambda}
       \frac{J_{\rho^{-1/2}}(U)}{Q_\lambda(\rho^{-1};U)}.
\]
The measurement density is $f(U)=(h_\lambda/Z_\lambda)Q_\lambda(\rho;U)$. Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive operator $\pi_\lambda(\rho)$ gives
\[
 \frac{f(U)}{P(U)}=
 \frac{Q_\lambda(\rho;U)Q_\lambda(\rho^{-1};U)}{J_{\rho^{-1/2}}(U)}
 \ge\frac1{\sup J_{\rho^{-1/2}}}.
\]
Every compression determinant of $\rho^{-1}$ is bounded below by the product of its smallest eigenvalues. Equality holds simultaneously at the ordered eigenflag, so (\ref{eq:jac}) yields
\[
 \sup J_{\rho^{-1/2}}=\prod_{i<j}p_i/p_j.
\]
Integrating the pointwise density inequality proves the operator inequality. If two states satisfy $\sigma\ge c\tau$, then $\sigma=c\tau+(1-c)\omega$, proving the half-trace bound. Apply it in each exact Schur sector.
\end{proof}
Repeated highest-weight entries permit a stronger coefficient $\prod_{i<j:\lambda_i>\lambda_j}p_j/p_i$ by using the actual partial flag. The full coefficient suffices here. Also
\[
 S(p)=\sum_i(d+1-2i)\log p_i\le\lfloor d^2/4\rfloor\log\cond\rho.
\]
This supplies a sufficient growing-$d$ condition for the continuous channel, but the finite-memory asymptotics in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} keep $d$ fixed.

\section{An exact sector concentration estimate}
The quadratic Casimir yields
\begin{equation}
 \E\|\lambda-np\|_2^2\le n(d-\|p\|_2^2)\le nd.
 \label{eq:casimir}
\end{equation}
For completeness, $\sum_{ij}E_{ij}^{\rm tot}E_{ji}^{\rm tot}$ has eigenvalue
$\sum_i\lambda_i(\lambda_i+d+1-2i)$ and expectation
$nd+n(n-1)\|p\|_2^2$. The occupation weights $N$ of the representation satisfy highest-weight dominance $p\cdot\lambda\ge p\cdot N$ for sorted $p$, and $\E N=np$. Finally $\sum_i(d+1-2i)\lambda_i\ge0$. Expansion of the square proves (\ref{eq:casimir}).

Choose $T=C_d\varepsilon^{-1/2}$. Sectors outside the coordinate box $|\lambda_i-np_i|\le T\sqrt n$ have total probability at most a chosen small multiple of $\varepsilon$. The box intersected with the dominant cone is convex, which is useful for interpolation. No normal approximation is used.

\section{Orbital integrals control the coherent likelihood}
Put $P=\diag(p)$, $\ell_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}$, and
\[
 b_k(U)=\log\prod_{i\le k}p_i-\log\det(U_k^*PU_k),\quad
 \Phi_\lambda(U)=\sum_{k<d}\ell_k b_k(U).
\]
The coherent density is $f_\lambda=e^{-\Phi_\lambda}/\int e^{-\Phi_\lambda}\dd\mu$. This formula extends analytically to real dominant $\lambda$ of fixed total weight.
Define the nonnegative alignment loss
\[
 E_{\lambda,p}(U)=\tr(\lambda P)-\tr(PU\lambda U^*)
 =\sum_{k<d}\ell_k\left(\sum_{i\le k}p_i-\tr(PU_kU_k^*)\right).
\]
Compression eigenvalue interlacing and the derivative of $\log$ give
\begin{equation}
 p_{\max}^{-1}E_{\lambda,p}\le\Phi_\lambda\le p_{\min}^{-1}E_{\lambda,p}.
 \label{eq:potential}
\end{equation}

The one imported analytic estimate is the type-$A$ orbital-integral bound of Graczyk--Sawyer~\cite{GS}:
\begin{equation}
 K(t):=\int e^{-tE_{\lambda,p}(U)}\dd\mu(U)
 \asymp_d\prod_{i<j}[1+t(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)(p_i-p_j)]^{-1}.
 \label{eq:HCIZ}
\end{equation}
It is uniform on the closed Weyl chamber, including repeated entries. Scalar shifts cancel, and fixed normalization changes are absorbed in $t$.

Let $a=1/p_{\max}$ and $b=1/p_{\min}$. From $x e^{-a x}\le(2/a)e^{-a x/2}$ and (\ref{eq:potential}),
\[
 \E_{f_\lambda}E_{\lambda,p}
 \le\frac2a\frac{K(a/2)}{K(b)}\le C_d,
\]
since $b/a\le3$ and every factor of the ratio in (\ref{eq:HCIZ}) is bounded by a fixed constant.
For $\lambda=np+\eta$ with $\|\eta\|\le C_dT\sqrt n$, let $R(U)=\|P-U^*PU\|_F$. Then
\[
 E_{\lambda,p}=\frac n2R^2+\tr[\eta(P-U^*PU)],\qquad
 R^2\le\frac{C_d}{n}(E_{\lambda,p}+T^2).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \E_{f_\lambda}R\le\frac{C_d(1+T)}{\sqrt n}.
 \label{eq:ER}
\end{equation}

Two score bounds now replace the nonuniform fixed-gap approximation. First,
\[
 \|\partial_{\ell_k}f_\lambda\|_1\le2\E b_k
 \le\frac{C_d(1+T)}{\sqrt n},
\]
because $0\le b_k\le p_{\min}^{-1}\sqrt{k}\,R$. Second, a body-root rotation of columns $i,j$ changes only minors $i\le k<j$. Each log-determinant derivative is bounded by $C_dR$: in moving coordinates its off-block part is controlled by $U^*PU-P$. Summing the coefficients gives
\begin{equation}
 \|X_{ij}f_\lambda\|_1
 \le C_d(1+T)^2\sqrt{1+n(p_i-p_j)^2}.
 \label{eq:root-score}
\end{equation}
These estimates hold throughout the typical convex box, even when adjacent gaps vanish.

\section{Uniform geometry and finite angular labels}
Let $q=d(d-1)$ and give each real two-dimensional root plane $ij$ of the complete flag length weight
\[
 w_{ij}=\sqrt{1+n(p_i-p_j)^2}.
\]
The resulting invariant metric has total volume $V_dW$, where
$W=\prod_{i<j}w_{ij}^2=B_n(p)$; its normalized volume is Haar measure.

Here are details ensuring constants do not hide inverse spectral gaps. Extend the metric to the Lie algebra by giving the diagonal directions weight one. The inequalities $w_{ij}\ge1$ and $w_{ik}\le w_{ij}+w_{jk}$ uniformly bound all structure constants in an orthonormal weighted basis. Thus
$\|\operatorname{ad}X\|_w\le C_d\|X\|_w$ and
\[
 \operatorname{dexp}_X=\int_0^1e^{-t\operatorname{ad}X}\dd t=I+O_d(\|X\|_w).
\]
For off-diagonal $X$, the quotient chart $X\mapsto e^XT^d$ has horizontal differential equal to the off-diagonal projection of this expression. On a fixed small weighted ball it is uniformly bi-Lipschitz. It is injective there: this ball lies in a fixed ordinary flag chart, since the weighted norm dominates the Frobenius norm. Equivalently, if $e^X=e^Ye^Z$ with small diagonal $Z$, the diagonal BCH equation is $Z+O(\|Y\|_F\|Z\|_F)=0$, forcing $Z=0$ and then $X=Y$.

A first-exit argument shows that small metric balls stay in these charts. Their Haar mass is therefore comparable to $h^q/W$. A maximal separated net and coordinate bump functions yield a partition of unity $\{\psi_a\}$ with bounded overlap, support diameter $O_d(h)$, and
\[
 N_h\le C_dWh^{-q}.
\]
Use weighted-coordinate cubes containing the supports for the usual Euclidean $L^1$ Poincare inequality. For
\[
 z_a=\int\psi_a\dd\mu,\qquad
 (\Pi f)(U)=\sum_a\psi_a(U)z_a^{-1}\int\psi_a(V)f(V)\dd\mu(V),
\]
bounded overlap and the chart bounds give
\begin{equation}
 \|f-\Pi f\|_1\le C_dh\|\nabla_w f\|_1.
 \label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
This soft partition avoids assumptions about the shapes of clipped Voronoi cells. Equation~(\ref{eq:root-score}) gives $\|\nabla_w f_\lambda\|_1\le C_d(1+T)^2$. Taking $h=\varepsilon/[C_d(1+T)^2]$ uses at most $C_dB_n(p)\varepsilon^{-2q}$ angular labels.

\section{The complete finite encoder and decoder}
Partition the first $d-1$ coordinates of the typical sector box into bins of side
\[
 a=\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt n}{C_d(1+T)}.
\]
The score bound and convex interpolation show that two sector likelihoods in a bin differ in TV by at most a chosen small multiple of $\varepsilon$. There are at most $C_d\varepsilon^{-2(d-1)}$ nonempty bins.

The encoder measures the Schur sector transiently and then its coherent POVM. From flag $U$ it draws angular label $a$ with probabilities $\psi_a(U)$. It retains only the sector-bin and angular label, or a single tail label. The decoder resamples $\lambda$ from its known sector distribution conditioned on the bin. Given angular label $a$, it prepares
\[
 \omega_{\lambda,a}=z_a^{-1}\int\psi_a(U)P_\lambda(U)\dd\mu(U),
\]
regenerates the mixed multiplicity state, and inverts Schur. This is one fixed density operator per finite pair of labels; it does not require storing a continuous flag.

If $\bar f_b$ is the sector-law mixture of likelihoods in bin $b$, the output in sector $\lambda\in b$ uses density $\Pi\bar f_b$. Its extra half-trace error is at most
\[
 \TV(f_\lambda,\bar f_b)+\TV(\bar f_b,\Pi\bar f_b).
\]
Within-bin variation, (\ref{eq:quant}), and tail probability sum to at most $\varepsilon$ after fixing the constants. The product of the radial and angular counts proves (\ref{eq:upper}).

\section{The matching quantum-memory converse}
A second use of (\ref{eq:HCIZ}), now with $\lambda=p$, gives
\[
 \int e^{-t\|P-UPU^*\|_F^2}\dd\mu(U)
 \asymp_d\prod_{i<j}(1+t(p_i-p_j)^2)^{-1}.
\]
The Haar mass of the orbit ball of Frobenius radius $r$ consequently obeys
\begin{equation}
 v_p(r)\asymp_d\prod_{i<j}\left(1+\frac{(p_i-p_j)^2}{r^2}\right)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:ball}
\end{equation}
For the upper bound use $1_{R\le r}\le e\,e^{-R^2/r^2}$. For the lower bound, subtract the tail from the integral at $t=A/r^2$:
\[
 v_p(r)\ge K(A/r^2)-e^{-A/2}K(A/(2r^2)).
\]
The ratio of the two kernel estimates is uniformly bounded, so a fixed sufficiently large $A=A_d$ proves the claim. This includes all degeneracies.

Homogeneity and maximal separated sets turn (\ref{eq:ball}) into an orbit packing of at least $c_dB_n(p)$ states, separated by $L_d/\sqrt n$. A fixed informationally complete product measurement estimates a $d$-dimensional state with Frobenius mean square error at most $C_d/n$. Choose $L_d$ so nearest-neighbour discrimination succeeds with probability at least $7/8$. Reconstruction error at most $1/8$ leaves success at least $3/4$.

For $N$ equiprobable packed states, let $\omega_i$ be their encoded memory states and $F_i$ the decoded discrimination POVM pulled back to that memory. Then
\[
 \frac34\le\frac1N\sum_i\tr(F_i\omega_i)\le\frac1N\sum_i\tr F_i=\frac DN.
\]
This charges the entire memory and proves the lower bound for arbitrary quantum and hybrid codes.

\section{Unknown spectrum: the full shrinking state ball}
Consider all states
\[
 \mathcal B_d(\delta)=\{\rho:\tr\rho=1,\ \|d\rho-I\|_{\rm op}\le\delta\},
 \qquad 0<\delta\le1/2,\quad\kappa=n\delta^2.
\]
The encoder and decoder must now be independent of both spectrum and eigenbasis. The posterior-grid construction in the evidence package already proves that for bounded $\kappa$, every diverging classical capacity suffices. The stronger construction below resolves its growing-signal storage cost as well.

\begin{theorem}[Full state ball]\label{thm:ball}
For fixed $d$, there is a classical code uniform over $\mathcal B_d(\delta)$ with
\[
 D\le C_d\varepsilon^{-2(d^2-1)}(1+\kappa)^{(d^2-1)/2},\qquad
 e\le1-\exp\!\left[-\lfloor d^2/4\rfloor
                  \log\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\right]+\varepsilon.
\]
Every quantum or hybrid code of error at most $1/8$ has
$D\ge c_d(1+\kappa)^{(d^2-1)/2}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Construction and load-bearing cancellation]
The joint sector/flag likelihood is exactly
\[
 P_\rho(\lambda,U)=m_\lambda h_\lambda
       \exp\left[\sum_i\lambda_i\theta_i(U,\rho)\right],\qquad
 \theta_i=\log\Delta_i-\log\Delta_{i-1},
\]
where $\Delta_i=\det(U_i^*\rho U_i)$, $\Delta_0=1$, and $\Delta_d=\det\rho$. This identity is important: all unknown-state dependence is linear in $\lambda$ in the logarithm, while the multiplicity factor is known.

Use one global sector box of radius $n\delta/d+T\sqrt n$ around $n/d$, and the same bin width $a=\varepsilon\sqrt n/[C_d(1+T)]$. For each bin choose a representative $\lambda^b$. Let $r_b$ be the Euclidean projection of $\lambda^b/n$ onto the sorted probability simplex with entries in $[1/(2d),3/(2d)]$. Define a reference law on that bin by
\[
 \nu_b(\lambda)\ \propto\ m_\lambda h_\lambda\prod_i(r_{b,i})^{\lambda_i}.
\]
This is computable from the bin and known model bounds; it uses no unknown spectrum.

A bin containing a sector within $T\sqrt n$ of the true $np$ lies entirely in a slightly enlarged typical box. Nonexpansiveness of the projection gives $\|r_b-p\|\le C_dT/\sqrt n$. Moreover
$\theta_i-\log p_i=-b_i+b_{i-1}$, with $b_0=b_d=0$. Equation~(\ref{eq:ER}) bounds its expected absolute value by $C_d(1+T)/\sqrt n$.

Write $\widetilde Z_\lambda=\int\exp(\sum_i\lambda_i\theta_i)\dd\mu$. The logarithm of the ratio between the actual sector weight and the reference unnormalized weight has derivative
\[
 \nabla_\lambda\log\left(\widetilde Z_\lambda
                     e^{-\lambda\cdot\log r_b}\right)
 =\E_{f_\lambda}\theta-\log r_b.
\]
It is bounded by $C_d(1+T)/\sqrt n$ on the enlarged typical box. Its oscillation within a bin is $O_d(\varepsilon)$. Hence the actual conditional sector law is $O_d(\varepsilon)$-close in TV to $\nu_b$. The angular likelihoods in that bin are also close by the earlier centered-score estimate.

The decoder now samples from $\nu_b$, rather than from an unavailable true sector law. Use the isotropic flag metric with all root weights $\sqrt{1+\kappa}$. Its score is at most $C_d(1+T)^2$ after division by these weights. Angular quantization uses at most $C_d(1+\kappa)^{q/2}\varepsilon^{-2q}$ labels. The radial count is at most $C_d(1+\kappa)^{(d-1)/2}\varepsilon^{-2(d-1)}$. Bins missing the enlarged true typical set carry only the tail probability. Adjusting the constants makes all new errors total at most $\varepsilon$.

For the converse, pack a fixed-dimensional ball of traceless Hermitian matrices at spacing $L_d/\sqrt n$ inside $\mathcal B_d(\delta)$. There are $c_d(1+\kappa)^{(d^2-1)/2}$ points. Product tomography and the memory-dimension discrimination bound above apply unchanged.
\end{proof}
For $\delta_n\to0$, $\kappa_n\to\infty$, this gives leading storage
$\frac12(d^2-1)\log_2\kappa_n$ entirely classically. This proof is separately audited in the evidence package; it is not inferred from fixed-parameter local asymptotic normality.

\section{Uniform tracial equivalence to a matrix Gaussian experiment}
\label{gauss:section}
The continuous coherent statistic yields a stronger experiment-level conclusion.
Here both the spectrum and the eigenbasis are unknown.  Let $\mathcal H_0$
be the real Hilbert space of traceless Hermitian $d\times d$ matrices,
with the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product, and put
\[
 r=d^2-1,\qquad R=\frac{d(d-1)}2,\qquad
 c_H=\prod_{k=1}^{d-1}k!,\qquad N=n+R.
\]
For $0\le\delta\le1/2$, define
\[
 \mathcal B_\delta=\{\rho\ge0:\tr\rho=1,\ \|d\rho-I\|_{\rm op}\le\delta\},
 \qquad
 G_{n,\rho}=\mathcal N_{\mathcal H_0}
       \bigl(\sqrt n(\rho-I/d),I_{\mathcal H_0}/d\bigr).
\]
Thus the Gaussian coordinates in any orthonormal real Hilbert--Schmidt
basis have independent variance $1/d$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Gaussian equivalence at the tracial state]
\label{gauss:theorem}
For fixed $d\ge2$ there are channels $T_n$ from $n$ qudits to
$\mathcal H_0$ and $S_n$ in the reverse direction, depending only on
$n,d$, such that
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\rho\in\mathcal B_\delta}
 \TV\bigl(T_n(\rho^{\otimes n}),G_{n,\rho}\bigr)
 &\le C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8}),\label{gauss:forward}\\
 \sup_{\rho\in\mathcal B_\delta}
 \frac12\|S_n(G_{n,\rho})-\rho^{\otimes n}\|_1
 &\le C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8}).\label{gauss:reverse}
\end{align}
Consequently the two experiments are uniformly equivalent for every
$\delta_n\to0$, without a condition such as $n\delta_n^3\to0$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The channels.}
Perform the Schur/coherent measurement used in Theorem~\ref{thm:dom},
obtaining a sector $\lambda$ and a complete flag represented by $W$.
Set $l_i=\lambda_i+d-i$.  The vector $l$ has strictly decreasing
nonnegative integer coordinates and $\sum_i l_i=N$.  Independently draw
$\xi$ uniformly from
\[
 C=\{\xi\in\R^d:\ \sum_i\xi_i=0,
                 \ \max_i\xi_i-\min_i\xi_i\le1\},
\]
and output
\begin{equation}
 T_n:\quad Y=\frac{W\diag(l+\xi)W^*-(N/d)I}{\sqrt n}.
 \label{gauss:channel}
\end{equation}
Given $Y$, the channel $S_n$ diagonalizes
$M=\sqrt nY+(N/d)I$ as $W\diag(x)W^*$ with ordered eigenvalues.
It rounds $x$ to its nearest integer vector $l$ of sum $N$.
If $l$ is strictly decreasing and nonnegative, it prepares the coherent
state in sector $\lambda_i=l_i-d+i$, attaches the maximally mixed
multiplicity state, and inverts the Schur transform.  Otherwise it
returns a fixed state.  Ties use fixed measurable rules.

The cell $C$ is the Voronoi cell of the root lattice
$A_{d-1}=\{z\in\mathbb Z^d:\sum_i z_i=0\}$, with covolume $v_d=\sqrt d$.
Indeed its root inequalities are $|\xi_i-\xi_j|\le1$; conversely these
imply $\langle\xi,z\rangle\le\sum_{z_i>0}z_i\le\|z\|^2/2$ for every
$z\in A_{d-1}$.  The basis $e_i-e_d$ has Gram determinant $d$.
Moreover $|\xi_i|<1$ almost surely and jitter preserves the order of
every legal $l$.  Rounding therefore recovers $l$ exactly, and
\begin{equation}
 S_nT_n(\rho^{\otimes n})=C_{n,d}(\rho^{\otimes n})
 \quad\hbox{exactly}.\label{gauss:composition}
\end{equation}
The jittered matrix can have negative eigenvalues; its eigenvalues
are greater than $-1$, and its rounded sector remains valid.

\begin{proof}
We prove the forward bound and then use \eqref{gauss:composition}.
It suffices to treat $\delta+n^{-1/8}$ smaller than a dimension-dependent
constant; the other cases follow by increasing $C_d$.
Write $p_1\ge\cdots\ge p_d$ for the eigenvalues of $\rho$.
All formulas are covariant under its unknown eigenbasis, so angular
calculations may take $P=\diag(p)$.

\paragraph{1. Uniform angular replacement.}
The coherent conditional flag density is proportional to
$e^{-\Phi_\lambda(W)}$, where
\[
 \Phi_\lambda=\sum_{k<d}(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1})
  \left(\sum_{i\le k}\log p_i-\log\det(W_k^*PW_k)\right).
\]
Put $E=\tr(\diag(\lambda)P)-\tr(PW\diag(\lambda)W^*)\ge0$ and
$\Gamma_\lambda=\tr(\diag(\lambda)\log P)
                  -\tr(\log P\,W\diag(\lambda)W^*)$.
With $a=1/p_1$, $b=1/p_d$, both potentials satisfy
\[
 aE\le\Phi_\lambda,\Gamma_\lambda\le bE,
 \qquad |\Phi_\lambda-\Gamma_\lambda|\le(b-a)E\le C_d\delta E.
\]
For $\Phi$ this is the compression/logarithm bound
\eqref{eq:potential}.  For $\Gamma$, decompose the prefix trace losses
into the ordered increments of $P$: each logarithmic increment is
between $a$ and $b$ times its corresponding eigenvalue increment.
The coefficients are nonnegative, being
$\min(k,j)-\tr(W_kW_k^*P_j)$ for the rank-$j$ eigenprojection $P_j$.
Every interpolation of the two potentials lies between $aE$ and $bE$.
The uniform HCIZ estimate \eqref{eq:HCIZ}, with $b/a\le3$, gives
\[
 \E E\le\frac2a\frac{K(a/2)}{K(b)}\le C_d
\]
under every such interpolated density.  Differentiating its normalized
density gives an $L^1$ bound $2\E|\Phi_\lambda-\Gamma_\lambda|$.
Thus the two conditional laws differ in TV by $C_d\delta$, uniformly
in every $\lambda$, including all spectral collisions.
Replacing $\lambda$ by $l=\lambda+(d-i)_{i=1}^d$ adds a nonnegative
angular potential bounded by $R\log(p_1/p_d)\le C_d\delta$.
The conditional law may therefore be replaced, at total joint-law cost
$C_d\delta$, by the density proportional to
$e^{\tr(\log\rho\,W\diag(l)W^*)}$, while retaining the exact Schur law.

\paragraph{2. Exact discrete normalization.}
Let $\Delta(x)=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)$.  The multiplicity, character,
and HCIZ identities are
\begin{align*}
 m_\lambda&=\frac{n!\Delta(l)}{\prod_i l_i!},&
 s_\lambda(p)&=\frac{\det[p_i^{l_j}]}{\Delta(p)},\\
 I_l(\log p)&:=\int e^{\tr(\log P\,W\diag(l)W^*)}\dd\mu(W)
  =\frac{c_H\det[p_i^{l_j}]}{\Delta(\log p)\Delta(l)}.
\end{align*}
Multiplying the exact Schur probability $m_\lambda s_\lambda(p)$ by
the replaced conditional density cancels the character determinant:
\begin{equation}
 Q_\rho(l,\dd W)=\frac{u_\rho(l,W)}{Z_{
 \rm disc}(\rho)}\dd\mu(W),\quad
 u_\rho(x,W)=\frac{\Delta(x)^2e^{\tr(\log\rho\,W\diag(x)W^*)}}
                    {\prod_i\Gamma(x_i+1)},\quad
 Z_{\rm disc}=\frac{c_H\Delta(p)}{n!\Delta(\log p)}.
 \label{gauss:discrete}
\end{equation}
The sum is over legal $l$; all repeated-spectrum ratios are interpreted
by continuity.  After uniform cell jitter its density on $l+C$ is
$u_\rho(l,W)/(v_dZ_{\rm disc})$.

\paragraph{3. A continuous model centered at the true state.}
On Hermitian $M>-I$ with $\tr M=N$, consider the matrix density
\[
 q_\rho(M)\ \propto\ e^{-V_\rho(M)},\qquad
 V_\rho(M)=\tr h(M)-\tr(M\log\rho),\quad h(x)=\log\Gamma(x+1).
\]
In ordered eigenvalues and normalized flag measure its raw density is
$u_\rho(x,W)$.  Denote its normalizer in Euclidean trace-hyperplane
eigenvalue measure by $Z_{\rm cont}$.  Center at
$M_0=n\rho+(R/d)I$ and write $M=M_0+\sqrt nZ$, $Z\in\mathcal H_0$.
Digamma asymptotics give
\begin{align*}
 \nabla V_\rho(M_0)&=\log n\,I+O_d(n^{-1}),\\
 D^2V_\rho(M_0)[H,H]&=
 \left(\frac dn+O_d\left(\frac\delta n+\frac1{n^2}\right)\right)
 \|H\|_{\HS}^2.
\end{align*}
The latter notation denotes a uniformly bounded quadratic-form error:
the diagonal coefficients are $h''(m_i)$ and the off-diagonal
coefficients are $(h'(m_i)-h'(m_j))/(m_i-m_j)$, with continuous
values at repeats.  On $\|Z\|_{\HS}\le c_d\sqrt n$ all eigenvalues
along the centered segment are at least $c_dn$; scalar polygamma
bounds and divided differences give $|D^3\tr h(M)[H,H,H]|
\le C_dn^{-2}\|H\|_{\HS}^3$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 V_\rho(M_0+\sqrt nZ)-V_\rho(M_0)
 =\frac d2\|Z\|_{\HS}^2+\mathcal R(Z),\quad
 |\mathcal R(Z)|\le C_d\left[
 \delta\|Z\|_{\HS}^2+
 \frac{\|Z\|_{\HS}+\|Z\|_{\HS}^3}{\sqrt n}\right].
 \label{gauss:taylor}
\end{equation}
There is also global domination.  The positive trigamma series gives
$h''(x)\ge1/(x+1)$ for $x>-1$.  Every eigenvalue in the trace domain
is at most $N+d-1$, so the matrix Hessian is bounded below by
$1/(N+d)$.  Strong convexity and the centered gradient estimate imply
\begin{equation}
 V_\rho(M_0+\sqrt nZ)-V_\rho(M_0)
 \ge c_d\|Z\|_{\HS}^2-C_d/n.\label{gauss:domination}
\end{equation}
Apply $|e^{-A}-e^{-B}|\le|A-B|e^{-\min(A,B)}$ to
\eqref{gauss:taylor}--\eqref{gauss:domination} and integrate polynomial
Gaussian moments.  Outside the centered region both integrals have
exponentially small tails.  The Gaussian mass outside $M>-I$ is also
exponentially small.  Normalizing yields
\begin{equation}
 \TV\left(\frac{M-(N/d)I}{\sqrt n},G_{n,\rho}\right)
 \le C_d(\delta+n^{-1/2}),\qquad M\sim q_\rho.
 \label{gauss:continuous}
\end{equation}
This expansion is centered at the actual $\rho$; it does not incur
an uncentered remainder $n\delta^3$.

\paragraph{4. The normalizers, including the root-lattice covolume.}
The ordered trace-zero Gaussian Vandermonde integral is
\[
 J_d=\int_{\substack{x_1>\cdots>x_d\\\sum_i x_i=0}}
       \Delta(x)^2e^{-d\|x\|^2/2}\dd x_{\HS}
 =c_H(2\pi)^{(d-1)/2}d^{-r/2}.
\]
For verification, determinant integration in monic Hermite polynomials
gives the full integral $d!c_H(2\pi)^{d/2}d^{-d^2/2}$.
Divide by $d!$ and by the trace-direction integral $\sqrt{2\pi/d}$.
Stirling at $M_0$ gives
\[
 e^{-V_\rho(M_0)}=
 e^n n^{-(n+R+d/2)}(2\pi)^{-d/2}(\det\rho)^{-1/2}
 \{1+O_d(n^{-1})\}.
\]
The centered matrix volume scales by $n^{r/2}$; its Gaussian integral
is translation invariant.  Weyl integration therefore expresses its
eigenvalue/flag integral as $J_d$, irrespective of the moving mean.
The normalization argument for \eqref{gauss:continuous} then gives
\begin{align}
 Z_{\rm cont}
 &=\frac{c_H}{n!}d^{-r/2}(\det\rho)^{-1/2}
       \{1+O_d(\delta+n^{-1/2})\},\nonumber\\
 \frac{Z_{\rm cont}}{\sqrt d\,Z_{\rm disc}}
 &=1+O_d(\delta+n^{-1/2}).\label{gauss:normalizers}
\end{align}
Here $\Delta(p)/\Delta(\log p)=d^{-R}\{1+O_d(\delta)\}$,
$(\det\rho)^{-1/2}=d^{d/2}\{1+O_d(\delta)\}$, and
$-r/2+d/2=-R+1/2$.  These constants are decisive: no discrete
close-gap or tail approximation has been used to obtain this ratio.

\paragraph{5. Uniform Gaussian eigenvalue repulsion.}
For any deterministic traceless Hermitian mean with ordered eigenvalues
$t$, the ordered eigenvalue density of a Gaussian matrix with covariance
$I_{\mathcal H_0}/d$ is, by \eqref{eq:HCIZ}, bounded by
\[
 C_de^{-d\|x-t\|^2/2}
 \prod_{i<j}\frac{(x_i-x_j)^2}
                     {1+d(x_i-x_j)(t_i-t_j)}.
\]
For $z,u\ge0$, $z^2/(1+dzu)\le C_d(1+|z-u|)^2$:
if $u\ge z/2$ the ratio is bounded, and otherwise $z\le2|z-u|$.
When a selected adjacent gap is at most $h$, its factor is at most
$h^2$; all other factors are bounded by a fixed polynomial in
$\|x-t\|$.  Integrating that gap over a width-$h$ slab on the trace
hyperplane and the remaining coordinates against the shifted Gaussian
gives, uniformly in every mean,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{\min_i(x_i-x_{i+1})\le h\}\le C_dh^3,
 \qquad 0<h\le1.\label{gauss:repulsion}
\end{equation}
The bound includes repeated eigenvalues and arbitrarily large means.

\paragraph{6. Good cells and ordinary jitter.}
Set $L_n=K_n=n^{1/8}$.  A legal lattice center $l$ is good if
$\min_i(l_i-l_{i+1})>K_n$ and
$|l_i-np_i|\le2L_n\sqrt n$ for every $i$.
Every matrix with $\|M-M_0\|_{\HS}\le L_n\sqrt n$ and eigenvalue
gaps exceeding $K_n+2$ lies in a good cell for large $n$: eigenvalues
move by at most the Hilbert--Schmidt displacement, nearest-lattice
rounding changes each coordinate by less than one, and
$np_d\ge n/(2d)$ excludes the positivity boundary.
Equations~\eqref{gauss:continuous} and \eqref{gauss:repulsion}, and
Gaussian tails, show that $q_\rho$ assigns good cells mass at least
$1-C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8})$.

Uniformly for $\xi\in C$ in a good cell,
$\Delta(l+\xi)^2/\Delta(l)^2=1+O_d(K_n^{-1})$.
For the remaining factor write
$a_\rho(x,W)=u_\rho(x,W)/\Delta(x)^2$.
The source entries $(W^*\log\rho W)_{ii}$ lie in
$[\log p_d,\log p_1]$, whereas
$h'(l_i)=\log n+\log p_i+O_d(L_n/\sqrt n+n^{-1})$.
The scalar $\log n$ cancels because $\sum_i\xi_i=0$.  Hence,
uniformly in every flag $W$,
\[
 \left|\log\frac{a_\rho(l+\xi,W)}{a_\rho(l,W)}\right|
 \le C_d(\delta+L_n/\sqrt n+n^{-1}),\qquad
 \frac{u_\rho(l+\xi,W)}{u_\rho(l,W)}
 =1+O_d(\delta+n^{-1/8}).
\]
There is no $\delta L_n$ term.  By \eqref{gauss:normalizers}, on good
cells the normalized uniform-jitter law of \eqref{gauss:discrete} and
$q_\rho$ have density ratio $1+O_d(\delta+n^{-1/8})$.
Summing over those cells also forces the jitter law's good-cell mass
to be $1-O_d(\delta+n^{-1/8})$.  Their two complementary masses
and the density error on the good set bound the total variation by
$C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8})$.  Thus close lattice gaps are controlled
without presupposing a discrete repulsion estimate.

Finally, angular replacement costs $C_d\delta$, and jitter and the
matrix map contract TV.  Combining the preceding comparison with
\eqref{gauss:continuous} proves \eqref{gauss:forward}.
Contractivity, \eqref{gauss:composition}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:dom} give
\[
 \frac12\|S_n(G_{n,\rho})-\rho^{\otimes n}\|_1
 \le\TV(G_{n,\rho},T_n(\rho^{\otimes n}))+1-c(p)
 \le C_d(\delta+n^{-1/8}),
\]
which proves \eqref{gauss:reverse}.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Scope and status.}
This is a continuous density-operator experiment equivalence, with
fixed $d$ and tracial limiting covariance.  It does not assign finite
storage cost to real outcomes, preserve arbitrary purifications, or
establish efficient implementation.  Its imported uniform HCIZ bound
is established literature~\cite{GS}; the remaining derivation has
separate agent audits and finite checks, but no external certification
or novelty determination is claimed.


\section{Exact cloning error on shrinking full-state balls}
\label{gauss:cloning}
The experiment equivalence yields a concrete operational consequence.
Let $\delta_n\to0$, $n\delta_n^2\to\infty$, and $m_n/n\to\gamma>1$.
The underlying parameter set is the same full ball
$\mathcal B_{\delta_n}$ for the input and target states.
Write $q=d^2-1$, and let $F_q$ be the $\chi_q^2$ distribution function.

\begin{corollary}[Cloning throughout the tracial collision]
The optimal worst-case half-trace reconstruction error satisfies
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\Phi_n}\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal B_{\delta_n}}
 \frac12\|\Phi_n(\rho^{\otimes n})-\rho^{\otimes m_n}\|_1
 =F_q(b)-F_q(b/\gamma),\qquad
 b=\frac{q\gamma\log\gamma}{\gamma-1},
\]
where the infimum is over all quantum channels.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Whiten the Gaussian coordinates by multiplying by $\sqrt d$.
The input and target experiments become
$\mathcal N(\theta,I_q)$ and $\mathcal N(a_n\theta,I_q)$,
where $a_n=\sqrt{m_n/n}$.  Their parameter domains contain cubes
$Q_{t_n}=[-t_n,t_n]^q$ with $t_n\to\infty$; for example take
$t_n=\delta_n\sqrt n/\sqrt{dq}$, since
$\|\rho-I/d\|_{\rm op}\le\|\rho-I/d\|_{\HS}$.
Theorem~\ref{gauss:theorem} compares the quantum and Gaussian minimax
risks with an additive error at most
$C_d(\delta_n+n^{-1/8}+m_n^{-1/8})$.
Indeed a quantum channel $\Phi_n$ induces the arbitrary classical
kernel $T_{m_n}\Phi_n S_n$, and a classical kernel $K_n$ induces
$S_{m_n}K_nT_n$; contractivity gives both comparisons.

Deterministic scaling $Y=a_nX$ produces covariance $a_n^2I_q$.
Its TV error is independent of $\theta$ and equals
$F_q(b_n)-F_q(b_n/a_n^2)$, where
$b_n=qa_n^2\log(a_n^2)/(a_n^2-1)$: the target density exceeds the
scaled density precisely on the ball of squared radius $b_n$.

For the converse no equivariance assumption is needed.
Average $\theta$ uniformly over $Q_t$, and test the output on
$A_\theta=B(a_n\theta,s)$.  If $\phi$ is the standard Gaussian
density and $K_x$ is any output law conditional on $x$, the average
output probability of this event is
\[
 S_t=\frac1{|Q_t|}\int_{Q_t}\int_{\R^q}
 \phi(x-\theta)K_x(B(a_n\theta,s))\dd x\dd\theta.
\]
For $x\in Q_{t+L}$, extend the inner parameter integral to all
$\R^q$ and interchange it with $K_x$.
For every output $y$, the resulting Gaussian mass of
$B(y/a_n,s/a_n)$ is at most $F_q(s^2/a_n^2)$.
This centered-ball bound follows by successively centering each
one-dimensional Gaussian slice of the ball.
For $x\notin Q_{t+L}$, the average source mass is at most
$\Pr(\|Z\|_\infty>L)$, $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_q)$.
Consequently
\[
 S_t\le(1+L/t)^qF_q(s^2/a_n^2)+\Pr(\|Z\|_\infty>L).
\]
The target probability of $A_\theta$ is $F_q(s^2)$, so every kernel's
worst-case TV risk is at least this target probability minus the
displayed bound.  Set $t=t_n$, $L=\sqrt t$, and $s^2=b_n$.
The volume ratio tends to one and the Gaussian tail tends to zero.
This matches scaling and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
For a qubit and gain $\gamma=2$, the limit is approximately $0.3112722$.
This is a consequence for a full shrinking state ball and the trace
loss; it is not the fixed-spectrum fidelity theorem of~\cite{cloning}.
The Gaussian observation is continuous, so this corollary does not
assign it a finite memory cost.  Its proof is separately audited in
the package, conditional only on the preceding experiment theorem.


\section{Zero and critical signal; precision below the classical scale}
For known moving spectra in fixed dimension, zero memory has vanishing error exactly when $B_n(p_n)\to1$. Sufficiency follows from Pinsker and $n\|p_n-I/d\|^2\to0$. For necessity, swap the largest and smallest eigenvalues. Their single-copy root fidelity is
\[
 F=1-(\sqrt{p_{\max}}-\sqrt{p_{\min}})^2
 \le1-(p_{\max}-p_{\min})^2/4.
\]
Any common reconstructed state forces $2e\ge1-F^n$.

If $H_n$ remains bounded away from zero and bounded above, no bounded quantum memory suffices for vanishing error. On a subsequence $\sqrt n(p_n-I/d)$ converges to a nonzero traceless diagonal matrix. A rotation in a two-dimensional eigenspace and observables $\exp(is\sum_lY_l/\sqrt n)$ yield limiting expectations of the form $e^{-c s^2+i t s}$. With $D^2+1$ parameter values and frequencies in arithmetic progression, these form a full-rank Vandermonde matrix. Any channel through $D$ dimensions produces an expectation matrix of rank at most $D^2$, a contradiction. Every diverging classical capacity suffices by taking $\varepsilon$ to zero slowly in the finite upper bound. The analogous full-ball regimes use $\kappa$ instead of $H$.

Vanishing error does not mean an arbitrary prescribed error is achievable classically. A separate direct qubit argument, allowing arbitrary changes of Schur sector, gives for $\kappa=n\delta^2\ge64$ and $0<\delta\le1/2$:
\begin{equation}
 e_{\rm EB}\ge\frac\delta{20}-\frac{16}{3}e^{-\kappa/2}.
 \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
It uses a bounded sector-dependent thermal effect, an exact total-spin product-twirl norm, and a real coupling of input and output sector probabilities. The full proof and a weaker always-positive bound are in the package. Thus $e=o(\delta)$ requires nonclassical memory whenever $e^{-\kappa/2}=o(\delta)$, including every polynomial collision schedule. This does not determine the necessary number of qubits. The sharp Gaussian constant is older prior art~\cite{GBA}; no uniform moving-limit transfer of that constant is asserted.

\section{A consequence for interacting Gibbs experiments}
Let a known finite spin Hamiltonian $H_0$ commute with the collective $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ action. At known inverse temperature $\beta$, consider the family
\[
 \rho_h=\frac{\exp(-\beta H_0+h\cdot J)}{Z_h},\qquad h\in\R^3.
\]
Schur decomposition writes $H_0=\bigoplus_j I_{V_j}\otimes H_{0,j}$. The multiplicity state in each block is independent of $h$ and can be regenerated. Measuring $j$ and its spin-coherent POVM, then preparing the coherent spin state and the known multiplicity state, gives one entanglement-breaking channel satisfying
\[
 C(\rho_h)\ge e^{-|h|}\rho_h,\qquad T(C(\rho_h),\rho_h)\le1-e^{-|h|}.
\]
There is no system-size, spectral-gap, or interaction-strength factor. This concerns a known symmetric Hamiltonian and unknown field, not arbitrary unknown many-body states. Unknown multiplicity states may still carry unrestricted quantum information. If temperature is also unknown, retaining joint energy/representation labels gives a related construction, but the energy alphabet can be exponentially large and must be charged. The package provides an interacting example where the field tends to zero while opposite fields remain strongly distinguishable.

More generally, highest-weight coherent states of a compact connected semisimple group give the factor $e^{-\nu_\lambda(H^+)}$, where $\nu_\lambda$ is the sum of the roots tangent to the coherent orbit. An exact positive density is the reciprocal coherent symbol at weight $\lambda+\nu_\lambda$. This representation-theoretic extension is written separately; no all-root-system analogue of the multiscale memory law is inferred without the required orbital-integral bounds.

\section{Prior art, frontier changes, and significance}
Positive thermal spin representations and coherent quantization are established machinery~\cite{Sidles,ARS}. The 2024 semiclassical channel theorem already implies qualitative uniform qubit $O(\delta)$ reconstruction after a short thermal calculation. The 2010 teleportation benchmark~\cite{GBA} studies the same measure-and-prepare reconstruction task at fixed nonzero polarization. These are not new discoveries here.

The October 5 minimum-description paper~\cite{QMD} explicitly fixes the spectrum and disclaims uniformity as eigenvalues collide. September 28 mixed-state cloning work likewise excludes moving spectral degeneracies~\cite{cloning}. The finite collision law and its full-state-ball extension avoid a fixed-gap normal approximation. The stronger Gaussian result additionally gives universal channels for every shrinking full-state neighborhood. The classical tracial Gaussian limit and covariance are established weak-QLAN predictions~\cite{YFG}; they are not new here. Recent strong QLAN with arbitrary degeneracies covers unitary orbits of a fixed reference state, with restricted local growth~\cite{YT}. At the tracial state that orbit is a singleton. No checked source directly supplies the full unknown-spectrum, arbitrary-shrinkage channel theorem. This bounded search is not priority clearance.

A brief April--October 2026 refresh also located a new KLS exposition, the non-hyperlinear-group construction of Alekseev--Liu--Thom, and locality-preserving amplification with combinatorial soundness. Source statements were checked; their long proofs were not certified. Original work on those branches did not remove the main outstanding hardness or soundness barriers, so effort concentrated on the complete positive compression mechanism.

The scientific payoff supported by the derivation is a statistical storage principle: each pairwise spectral gap contributes only when it is resolved above the $n^{-1/2}$ scale, and near a tracial state this information can be retained classically. It also yields one classical statistic from which arbitrary later bounded-loss measurement decisions can be approximately simulated. It does not solve quantum PCP, provide generic efficient classical simulation of quantum circuits, preserve entanglement with a reference, or settle a major open conjecture.

\section{Other branch results retained}
The package contains full derivations and scope boundaries for these secondary results.
\begin{itemize}
\item Selective products: extensions to $D\le N^{1/7}$, $|W|\le N^2/D$, with time $O(N^2/D^{0.0045})$, and a denser-mask result at $D\le N^{1/8}$. An independent reconstruction of the encoding and decoder passed exact tests. The $N^{1/4}$ target and improved end-to-end APSP/3SUM exponents remain open.
\item Sparse quantum PCP: a commuting stabilizer layer has a deterministic positive sparse encoding, including the actual five-qubit projectors. An anchored mixed-basis encoding has a stronger sparse-consistency gap obstruction. This excludes that encoding mechanism, not arbitrary auxiliary isometries.
\item Discrepancy: conditional on the handoff's unverified main theorem, weighted longest-edge mass controls exact-marginal radius, and integral base polyhedra admit capacity-independent nearest-cell hard rounding. Independently, constant hard discrepancy on disjoint pairs forces positive correlations; negative correlation cannot be retained universally.
\item Uniform low-degree learning: exact sublogarithmic expurgation still leaves sufficiently large Reed--Muller circuits whose biclique reuse creates low-degree advantage. The desired uniform sampler remains unresolved.
\item Operator algebras: a finite commutant-gap estimate gives an explicit word-trace obstruction when an exact conjugacy system is supplied. Deriving that gap and exact system from finite approximate relations remains unresolved.
\end{itemize}

\section{Validation and remaining work}
Fourteen specialist subagents were used, all configured as GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Work was allocated first across distinct mechanisms, then to independent proof attacks on the strongest claims. Agreement among these agents is not external verification.

Checks include exact rational Schur/Casimir identities, exact symmetric-power channel eigenvalues, independent total-spin twirls, finite flag Jacobians, multiscale score inequalities, and direct integer replay of the selective-output algorithm. Their scripts, seeds where relevant, outputs, and exact scopes are preserved. These are local mathematical diagnostics; no full large-$n$ quantum encoder or formal proof assistant implementation was executed.

The most useful next validation is an external review of the Schur--HCIZ cancellation, matrix log-Gamma approximation, exact continuous/discrete normalization ratio, weighted-flag quantization, and unknown-spectrum radial-reference cancellation. Sharp precision dependence, additive storage constants, quantum-memory dimension below the classical error scale, growing-dimension rates, and broad interacting-system computational costs remain unresolved. The Gaussian extension has a complete derivation and two independent language-model proof audits in the package. A fixed-dimension polynomial circuit-compilation argument is also recorded, but no complete circuit has been built or independently certified.
