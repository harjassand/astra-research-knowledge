# Sensor resolution changes the resource law for rare-edge inference

2026-10-06. Root deduction and complete proof, independently reconstructed by r08. Read `notes/r17_nonequilibrium/GENERAL_GRAPH_CANDIDATE.md` for the underlying graph theorem and `notes/r08_information/cycle5/general_graph_gate.md` for its independent gate. This is a mathematical resource theorem for a specified observation model. Historical priority is unverified; it is not a claim of a new thermodynamic law or a realized detector experiment.

## Statement and supplied interface

Fix a known finite irreducible row generator Q0. Exactly one missing directed edge u->v is added at unknown rate b:

    Qb = Q0 + b e_u(e_v-e_u)^T.

Let L=d0(u,v)>=2 be the shortest directed baseline path length. Constants below may depend on this entire fixed baseline, confidence and desired relative accuracy. In particular they are not uniform in graph size, vanishing ordinary rates, or mixing time.

The apparatus passively reads the full state label, retains timestamps, and may choose its next lag adaptively using retained data and independent randomness. It cannot inspect events between reads. The initial state distribution is independent of b. A sensor imposes a minimum lag tau>0. A local bracket b in[beta,2beta] is initially supplied. Baseline acquisition and bracket acquisition are separate subsequent extensions, not hidden assumptions in this theorem.

Fix relative accuracy epsilon<1/4 and success probability .9, uniformly over the bracket. There is a baseline-dependent t0>0 such that, for 0<tau<=t0 and all sufficiently small beta, the optimal worst-case expected resource orders are

    reads  = Theta(beta^[-L/(L-1)] + beta^-2 tau^[L-2]),
    time   = Theta(beta^-1          + beta^-2 tau^[L-1]).

One protocol attains both orders simultaneously. Expected resource minimax means the supremum over b in the bracket of the expectation, minimized over procedures meeting the common accuracy requirement. The two-hypothesis affinity argument does not assert the same reading lower bound separately at every b.

Equivalently, the informative lag is of order max(tau,beta^[1/(L-1)]). With fixed tau>0, both readings and elapsed time are quadratic in beta^-1. When tau is small enough to resolve the alternative-path scale, elapsed time is linear in beta^-1 and the number of readings has exponent L/(L-1). All costs include reaching the source state and returning to it; no reset oracle is admitted.

## Reading lower bound

For a known lag t and starting state x, the audited graph theorem proves endpoint Fisher information

    I_t,x(b) <= min(t/b, C t^[2-L])      for 0<t<=1,
    I_t,x(b) <= C                       for t>=1.

The first term is full-path data processing. The second follows from the shortest-path support of the marked semigroup derivative and a positive baseline-path lower bound. A separate Doeblin argument supplies the large-lag estimate; no reversible baseline is required. In particular, with alpha=(L-2)/(L-1),

    sup_(t>=tau,x) I_t,x(b)
       <= C min(b^-alpha, tau^[-(L-2)]),

where tau<=t0<=1 permits absorption of the large-lag constant. For L=2 the exponent of tau is zero.

For completeness, this Fisher bound extends to integrable adaptive stopping. Let P and Q be complete stopped-transcript laws at b and c. Use squared Hellinger distance H2=integral(sqrt(dP)-sqrt(dQ))^2. Each conditional endpoint experiment obeys

    H2(kernel_b,kernel_c) <= (b-c)^2 sup I/4.

Adaptive actions, private randomness and stop decisions use identical conditional kernels at a shared retained history. They add no affinity deficit. If mu_k,nu_k are the subprobability laws of alive prefixes before read k+1, telescoping affinity through a finite depth and then taking the a.s.-stopping limit yields

    H2(P,Q) <= [(b-c)^2 sup I/4]
                sum_k integral sqrt(dmu_k dnu_k)
             <= [(b-c)^2 sup I/4] sqrt(E_b N E_c N).

The final inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz on the disjoint union of all prefix levels. Truncation tails vanish under a.s. finite stopping; finite mean costs make the bound useful. This argument also allows continuous clock randomization and timestamps.

Take b=beta,c=2beta. Accurate estimation gives a test with both errors at most .1 and hence TV>=.8, while TV<=sqrt(H2). Consequently the supremum expected reading count is at least a constant times

    beta^-2 max(beta^alpha,tau^[L-2])
      = max(beta^[-L/(L-1)], beta^-2 tau^[L-2]).

Replacing max by a sum changes only constants. A fixed maximum-reading budget is a special case. A parameter-dependent stationary initial law is extra data and must not be silently substituted for the independent initialization premise.

## Physical-time lower bound

Since each lag is at least tau, T>=tau N. The preceding reading lower bound implies a worst-case time lower bound c beta^-2 tau^[L-1]. Independently, even a complete jump-resolved record needs time of order beta^-1.

Indeed, for b versus b/2, the stopped full-path likelihood ratio has expected log

    KL = b(log2-1/2) E_b A_u(T)
       <= b(log2-1/2) E_b T,

where A_u is occupation time at u. The equality uses the rare-edge counting-process compensator, with integrable stopping. Parameter-independent observation clocks introduce no extra likelihood. Binary data processing for a test with both errors<=.1 gives KL>=.8 log9. Taking b=2beta gives the second lower bound. Thus the worst-case expected time is at least a constant times beta^-1+beta^-2 tau^[L-1]. This comparison uses a stronger full-path experiment only for a valid lower bound, not as the endpoint learner's supplied access.

## Simultaneously matching protocol

Choose a fixed sufficiently small kappa>0 and

    t = max(tau,kappa beta^[1/(L-1)]) <= t0.

The rare endpoint probability r_b=P_b(t)[u,v] satisfies, uniformly on the bracket,

    r_b <= 2 beta t + C0 t^L,
    d_b r_b >= t/2.

For the first inequality, couple the baseline chain to an extra Poisson rare clock; without any rare clock event, reaching v from u requires at least L baseline jumps. For the second, the exponential derivative series is tB plus a uniformly bounded O(t^2) remainder. Choose t0 small enough to dominate the remainder.

From m sampled transitions starting at u, form the Bernoulli average of the event that the next label is v. Clip the average to [r_beta,r_2beta], then invert the known increasing map. Bernstein's inequality, at probability tolerance epsilon beta t/2, gives the sufficient number of source departures

    m <= C_epsilon,delta [1/(beta t) + t^[L-2]/beta^2] + 1.

The outgoing transitions encountered at successive visits to u are iid draws from its sampled-chain row; the stopping times selecting them do not grant free returns. Let eta>0 be a uniform lower bound on all entries of exp(Qb) for b in[0,1], and k=ceil(1/t). The k-step sampled propagator has all entries at least eta. Initial source acquisition takes at most k/eta expected readings. After a departure from u, return requires at most

    1 + [1-P_b(t)[u,u]] k/eta
      <= 1 + 2(R+1)/eta

expected readings, where R bounds baseline escape rates. Thus total expected readings are O(m)+O(1/t)=O(m), because m>=c/(beta t). Expected physical time is O(mt), since all lags equal t. No state preparation or reset per informative transition is needed.

Substituting the chosen t yields the stated two upper bounds. A fixed cap at a constant multiple of the uniform expectation and a reserved failure probability preserves the exponent by Markov's inequality. Returning a rigorous numerical rate or entropy-production interval additionally requires validated evaluation/inversion of exp(tQb); that arithmetic has not been implemented in this root supplement.

## Significance, prior art and actual downstream use

The mechanism is competition between a genuine rare jump (probability about bt) and a false endpoint reached along an L-jump ordinary path (probability about t^L). The result distinguishes sensor calls, physical duration and jump-resolved observation. It explains exactly when a claimed rare-event measurement penalty is a physical-time penalty rather than merely an expensive sampling schedule.

Classical CTMC semigroup likelihoods, Poisson sampling identifiability, Fisher information, adaptive experimental design and rare-event difficulty are established. See the r17 and r04 source audits; in particular the competent comparator is adaptive CTMC inference, not a fixed-lag strawman. Concrete downstream use in this programme: this theorem changed the acquisition investigation from a known-generator asymptotic example to three required tests: (1) acquire the initially unknown rare scale, (2) acquire ordinary rates with the same passive readings, and (3) expose a calibrated sensor's minimum lag. r17 is constructing an adaptive scale search; r10 is reconstructing a support-sensitive nuisance-rate bound. Their outcomes must be read separately before strengthening this theorem's interface.

No unrestricted foundational breakthrough is established. The substantial next gap is a uniformly valid, finite-cost procedure with unknown generator structure, imperfect state observations and calibrated instrument limits. Those premises materially change the experiment and cannot be removed by renaming a known-model estimator as discovery.
