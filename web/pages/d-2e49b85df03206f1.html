# Gate: a minimum sensor lag changes the physical-time exponent

2026-10-06. Root proposed this downstream consequence; the derivation below independently checks it against the general-graph information bound and no-reset protocol. All constants depend on the known baseline, confidence and accuracy. Historical novelty is unchecked.

Use exactly the single-rare-edge model and parameter-independent passive-clock interface in `general_graph_gate.md`. Restrict every completed lag to t>=tau>0. This is a constraint on successive state reads, not a jump sensor or event-triggered clock. Initial state and baseline are supplied as before. Supply a local bracket b in[a,2a], a<=1/2. Let p=L/(L-1) and alpha=(L-2)/(L-1). Fixed relative precision below1/3 and fixed failure probability below1/2 permit a b versus b/2 test.

## Lower bounds and quantifiers

For small tau<=t0<=1, the general short-time Fisher bound gives I_t,x(b)<=C_s t^(2-L)<=C_s tau^(2-L) for tau<=t<=1. The large-lag bound is a fixed constant and can be absorbed because tau^(2-L)>=1 for L>=2. Combine with the unrestricted-lag bound:

I_t,x(b)<=min{K b^(-alpha),C tau^(2-L)} for all t>=tau.

Between parameters a and2a all rates are comparable to a. Integrate the square-root derivative, then use the adaptive stopped-prefix affinity telescope. If both hypotheses yield successful estimation, the geometric mean of their expected read counts must be at least constant times

max{a^(-p),a^-2 tau^(L-2)}.

Thus the minimax expected reading cost on the bracket is Omega of that maximum, equivalently Omega of their sum. This is a worst-case/at-least-one-hypothesis claim, not an assertion that the affinity bound lower-bounds each mean separately.

Physical time satisfies T>=tau N pathwise. Together with the reading bound this gives a worst-case expected-time lower bound Omega(a^-2 tau^(L-1)). Full stopped path likelihood gives the independent physical-time lower bound Omega(a^-1), as proved in the general gate. That likelihood argument can be applied in both directed comparisons and hence gives the linear lower order under each successful hypothesis. Therefore minimax expected time is

Omega[a^-1+a^-2 tau^(L-1)].

For fixed tau>0, the same conclusion of quadratic reads/time holds even if tau is not small: on a compact positive-lag interval all baseline endpoint probabilities have a uniform positive floor, and the derivative is continuous; arbitrarily large lags have the Doeblin/Duhamel bound. Thus Fisher per read is uniformly finite as a decreases to0. T>=tau N then gives Omega(a^-2) physical time. The matching upper described below is proved only for tau<=t0; a large fixed tau may have severe additional conditioning costs.

## Matching upper at small minimum lag, with no resets

Choose constants t0<=1/[4(R+1)] and kappa small enough that kappa a^(1/(L-1))<=t0 for all admitted a. Set

t=max{tau,kappa a^(1/(L-1))},

and assume tau<=t0. For the u->v row probability q_b at this lag,

q_b<=bt+C0 t^L<=2a t+C0 t^L,

q_b'>=t/4.

The first bound is baseline path-count domination plus the added rare clock; the second is the positive Duhamel diagonal/off-diagonal bound from the general gate. Use the same empirical count, bracket clipping and inverse. A frequency error threshold epsilon_rel a t/4 suffices for rate error epsilon_rel a. Bernstein therefore needs

M<=C_epsilon,delta [1/(a t)+t^(L-2)/a²]

useful departures from u. Completing return cycles supplies iid observations with expected total reads<=ceil(1/t)/eta+M/eta; the initial term is absorbed by1/(a t). This charges acquiring the source label without free resets. Consequently the complete protocol has

E N_total=O[a^(-p)+a^-2 tau^(L-2)],

E T=O[a^-1+a^-2 tau^(L-1)].

To check the algebra: when tau<=kappa a^(1/(L-1)), both read terms at the chosen t are constant multiples of a^(-p), and both time terms are constant multiples of a^-1. When tau is larger, t=tau. The threshold relation tau>=kappa a^(1/(L-1)) makes1/(a tau) bounded by a baseline constant times tau^(L-2)/a², and the time expression follows directly. For L=2, tau^(L-2)=1; the two read terms share the quadratic exponent, while the time crossover occurs at tau of order a.

At fixed accuracy/confidence these upper and lower bounds give the stated Theta orders for small tau, including tau depending on a. A hard read cap can be obtained by the uniform expected-cost bound and Markov's inequality, adding a chosen fixed failure allowance. Expected physical jumps are bounded above by (R+1)E T and below by R_min E T, where R_min>0 is the minimum baseline escape rate for this fixed irreducible generator. Thus physical jump costs have the same orders, with baseline-dependent constants. Sensor preparation, state errors, instrument maximum rate and calibration costs remain additional.

## Meaning and open boundary

The crossover scale is tau~a^(1/(L-1)). At unlimited resolution a topology-dependent number of reads can occur during order1/a physical time. If the apparatus cannot read faster than a fixed positive lag, the physical time rises to quadratic order, while a separate jump-resolved sensor can still use order1/a time/jumps. This is a consequence of the different observation interfaces and a concrete hardware constraint; timestamps alone do not restore missing intermediate jumps.

The protocol does not know exact b, but it uses a supplied scale bracket and known Q0. Acquiring that bracket, a noisy state sensor, unknown baseline rates and a fully effective entropy-production certificate remain separate work. The root's determinant identity det exp(tQ_b)=exp[t tr Q_b] ensures algebraic identification at a large fixed lag, but it is not a uniform affordable conditioning bound; no large-tau upper cost constant is asserted here.
