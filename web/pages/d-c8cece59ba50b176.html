# A stationary-defect correction and a quasilocal counterexample

## Exact interface and decisive result

For a channel M with stationary state rho and a subspace A, let p_A=Tr(P_A rho)>0 and rho_A=P_A rho P_A/p_A. Suppose M has an r-local channel approximation R with diamond error at most f(r). Let B be the 2r-Hilbert-space boundary of A, and Delta=||P_B rho||_1/p_A. The v1 wrapper claims a conditional one-step bound 10 Delta+f(r), without requiring R to preserve rho. That claim is false. A universally safe replacement obtained directly from its sound stationary local-channel proof is

    ||M(rho_A)-rho_A||_1 <= 10 Delta + (1+1/p_A) f(r).

The factor 1/p_A is relevant for rare conditional states. In a balanced logical sector p_A bounded below by a constant it costs only a constant. This result alone is neither a coherent-memory theorem nor a failure of the current BDL Gibbs witness.

## Robust bottleneck proof

Use the source's four orthogonal subspaces A,B1,B2,C and Kraus support conditions. Put B=B1+B2, K=P_B2+P_C, Q=I-K=P_A+P_B1, D=P_B+P_C. For a not necessarily stationary reference state define r=R(rho)-rho. The exact starting identity is

    R(P_A rho P_A)-P_A rho P_A
      = r + R(D rho D-D rho-rho D) -(D rho D-D rho-rho D).

The same Kraus block identities as in the stationary proof replace its three stationary terms respectively by K(rho+r), (rho+r)K and K(rho+r)K. All ten boundary terms are unchanged. The entire remaining nonstationary contribution is

    r+K r K-K r-r K = Q r Q.

Every boundary term has trace norm at most ||P_B rho||_1: channels contract the trace norm, including for non-Hermitian operators (dual unital completely positive operator-norm contraction), and projector multiplication contracts it. Consequently

    ||R(P_A rho P_A)-P_A rho P_A||_1
       <=10||P_B rho||_1+||R(rho)-rho||_1.

Divide by p_A. If M(rho)=rho and ||M-R||_diamond<=f, then ||R(rho)-rho||_1<=f, and the additional change on normalized rho_A is at most f. More generally an acquired stationarity defect d=||M(rho)-rho||_1 contributes d/p_A. No approximation is assumed to be stationary for free.

The Hilbert-boundary construction from the source gives the four-block Kraus conditions for an r-local R. This is independent of the failed quasi-local inference.

## Exact four-qubit counterexample, including full rank

In four physical qubits let a0=|0>|000>, a1=|1>|000>, c=|+>|111>. Let A=span(a0,a1), R=X on the first physical qubit. These three vectors form a reducing space for R, with R exchanging a0,a1 and fixing c. Set p=1/16, psi=sqrt(p)a0+sqrt(1-p)c, q=R psi, s=1-p and t=sqrt(1-s^2). The vector w=(psi-s q)/t is unit and orthogonal to q. Define, extended by identity outside this three-dimensional space,

    W=I+(s-1)(q q*+w w*)+t(w q*-q w*),
    U=W R,   M=Ad_U.

W is a planar rotation, U psi=psi, and ||U-R||=sqrt(2p). Thus ||M-Ad_R||_diamond<=2sqrt(2p)<3/4. Ad_R is genuinely one-qubit local. M is trivially four-qubit local, giving an admissible quasi-local approximation family.

Use the full-rank stationary state rho=(1-eta)|psi><psi|+eta I/16, eta=p^3=1/4096. The 2-Hilbert boundary of A consists exactly of the twelve computational states whose three ancilla bits have weight one or two. Hence P_B psi=0, ||P_B rho||_1=3eta/4, and p_A=(1-eta)p+eta/8.

Direct exact algebra gives

    <a0|U|a0>=p(3-2p)/(2-p),
    <a1|U|a0>=2(1-p)/(2-p).

Measuring |a1><a1| lower-bounds the trace-norm drift by

    2[(1-eta)p/p_A * (2(1-p)/(2-p))^2 - eta/(16p_A)]
       >1.8716.

The printed wrapper's RHS is bounded above by 10(3eta/4)/p_A+3/4<0.7793. This is a strict rational separation with a full-rank stationary state; it does not rely on a zero-probability boundary convention.

## Uniform exponentially quasilocal family

For n=m+1 qubits replace the three ancillas by m ancillas, choose p_n=exp(-4 kappa n), eta=p_n^3 and the same rotation. For 2r<m the 2r-Hilbert boundary still annihilates psi. Its mixed-state contribution obeys Delta<=eta/((1-eta)p_n)=O(p_n^2). The error to the one-site R is at most 2sqrt(2) exp(-2 kappa n). Take the common decay function f(r)=2sqrt(2)exp(-kappa r); approximate by R for r<n and by M exactly for r>=n. These inequalities meet a uniform exponential quasilocality definition. At r=floor((n-2)/2), both 10Delta and f(r) tend to zero, while conditional drift tends to two.

This is a general channel counterexample. Although its full-rank rho can formally be represented as a Gibbs state, the corresponding -log rho need not be a local Hamiltonian. It is not a counterexample to local-Hamiltonian memory or to a balanced coherent encoding.

## Other local corrections, preserved without broad invalidation

The v1 product-of-channels argument uses a minimum one-step drift. The correct telescoping bound is the sum of the one-step drifts, at most t times their maximum. Its common uniform 10Delta bound survives this repair. The mixing lower bound is [2(1-p_A)-epsilon]_+/(10Delta); epsilon must be subtracted before division. The valid inequality ||P rho||_1<=sqrt(Tr(P rho)) follows immediately by Schatten Holder on P sqrt(rho) sqrt(rho), without replacing an operator by its elementwise absolute value as the source proof does.

These repairs preserve the substantive stationary quantum bottleneck mechanism and its qualitative slow-mixing uses. The strongest comparator remains that theorem, plus older classical conductance, rather than an unsupported claim of an entirely new bottleneck method.

## Scientific consequence and next falsifiable step

For a proposed engineered exact-stationary quasilocal thermostat, acquire both the tail f(r) and the actual logical-sector probability p_A, and prove the relevant Hilbert boundary lies in a controlled Gibbs event. Balanced sector probability avoids the rare-mass amplification, but all logical coherences still need a full logical-algebra estimate. For finite-resolution physical baths, a Gibbs stationarity defect must be charged explicitly before conditioning. The next falsifiable step is whether a claimed sampler has exact stationarity for the same full H and sufficient physical tail/local algebra control; a Gibbs preparation algorithm or ground-band theorem does not supply those conditions.
