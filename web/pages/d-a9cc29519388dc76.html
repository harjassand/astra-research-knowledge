1. The base theorem is a genuinely stronger interface than scalar tails
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Q8 says Var(X^T M X)<=8||M||_HS^2 for every symmetric M and every isotropic
log-concave X. It implies, for the third cumulant tensor,

    ||kappa_3(u,.,.)||_HS^2<=8||u||^2.

Proof: pair the matrix with M, express the pairing as covariance between
u.X and X^T M X, use Cauchy-Schwarz, then take the matrix-norm dual.
This controls all quadratic directions collectively, not only one-dimensional
third moments. Q8 is imported from Letwin arXiv:2607.24164. Its exact statement
was verified, but its full source proof is an unresolved validation gate.

2. Why inverse covariance localization generates higher cumulants
----------------------------------------------------------------
For an isotropic compactly supported log-concave base, Eldan localization
uses dQ=A^(-1)dt and dc=A^(-1)a dt+A^(-1/2)dB, where a and A are the current
mean and covariance. Its density martingale gives

    da=A^(1/2)dB,
    dA=sum_i H_i dB_i-A dt,
    H_i=kappa_3(.,.,A^(-1/2)e_i), E A_t=e^(-t)I.

For the log-MGF Lambda_t(z), direct Ito differentiation yields

    dLambda_t=<A^(-1/2)(grad Lambda_t-a),dB>
               -|A^(-1/2)(grad Lambda_t-a)|^2 dt/2.

Taking m derivatives at zero gives, for m>=3,

    dkappa_m=kappa_(m+1)(.,...,.,A^(-1/2)dB)
                -(m kappa_m+L_m)dt.

L_m sums paired lower-order cumulants over subsets J containing the first
index, 2<=|J|<=m-2. Complementary subsets have equal terms; the factor 1/2
from Ito cancels their duplication. Singleton and co-singleton terms yield
exactly m kappa_m. This subset counting was checked explicitly.

3. The metric energy prevents a dimension factor
------------------------------------------------
For fixed u, define

    E_m=||kappa_m(u,A^(-1/2).,...,A^(-1/2).)||_HS^2.

There are m-1 whitened slots; the u slot is NOT whitened. Q8 gives
sum_i H_i A^(-1) H_i<=8A. In coordinates whitened at a fixed time, let
Sigma_H=sum_s H^(s), acting in the m-1 tensor slots. The exact joint
second differential of F(A,T)=<T,(A^(-1))^(tensor m-1)T> gives

    drift E_m >= (1/2)E_(m+1)-16 m^2 E_m
                    -2<kappa_m(u),L_m(u)>_t.             (A)

The stated coefficient 16 is sufficient: after discarding nonnegative
terms, the metric error is at most 16(m-1)^2 E_m and the deterministic
decay costs m+1, so m+1+16(m-1)^2<=16m^2.

Crucial point: the martingale coefficient of kappa_m generates E_(m+1)
with a POSITIVE coefficient in the energy drift. This turns a time integral
of the already controlled mth energy into control of the next energy. A
fixed scalar-covariance localization does not give this normalization.

4. Integrating energy and the stopping/martingale justification
---------------------------------------------------------------
BKL writes expectation integration of (A) and then sends T to infinity.
Its compact-support discussion alone does not explicitly bound A^(-1).
This can be repaired without a dimension-free theorem:

For each fixed n and m, elementary one-dimensional log-concave moment bounds
give a finite rough constant C(n,m) for every entry of the cumulant tensor
of ANY isotropic log-concave law. Rotate a test vector to a coordinate and
sum entries to get E_m(t)<=C(n,m) u^T A_t u. The compact base support bounds
u^T A_t u by a deterministic support-diameter bound. The same bound holds
for E_(m+1), while Q8 bounds the covariance metric noise. The squared
martingale coefficient of E_m is bounded by a constant times
E_m E_(m+1)+(m-1)^2 E_m^2. It is therefore bounded on each finite interval.
The local martingale is a genuine mean-zero martingale. Moreover
E E_m(T)<=C(n,m)e^(-T), so the boundary term actually tends to zero.

This supplies both expectation and terminal-limit justifications. It is
not an independent construction/existence proof for the localization SDE;
the standard compact-support process remains imported. Alternatively,
integrability by itself only guarantees a subsequence with terminal energy
tending to zero; the source's last sentence should then use that subsequence.
Neither issue is a demonstrated fatal gap.

5. Factorial cancellation makes the induction uniform
------------------------------------------------------
Set b_m=K^(m-1)((m-1)!)^2. In a lower-cumulant product with |J|=a,
the factor carrying u is controlled by the integrated energy bound, and
the other factor is controlled by the pointwise lower-order cumulant bound.
The squared time-integrated norm of one product is at most
2 b_a b_(m-a+1). There are binomial(m-1,a-1) permutations, and

    binomial(m-1,a-1) sqrt(b_a b_(m-a+1))=sqrt(b_m).

The factorials cancel exactly. There are m-3 sizes. Integrating (A) gives

    E_m(0)+(1/2) integral E E_(m+1)
      <= b_m [ (32/K)m^2/(m-1)^2
                  +(4/sqrt K)(m-3)/(m-1) ].

K=144 is a sufficient explicit choice for this reconstructed estimate.
The bracket is <=1 for all m>=3. The base E E_3<=8e^(-t) supplies the
start. The resulting conditional cumulant statement is

    ||kappa_m(u,.,...,.)||_HS^2
       <=144^(m-1)((m-1)!)^2 ||u||^2, for all m>=2.

6. Suspension is the non-obvious bridge to arbitrary observables
---------------------------------------------------------------
For regular isotropic mu with Hess V>=aI, take a normalized smooth f
orthogonal to affine functions, with globally bounded Hessian H. Form
F_N=N^(-1/2)sum_i f(X_i), add independent centered Laplace noise eta_beta,
and standardize S=(F_N+eta_beta)/sqrt(1+2/beta^2).

The joint potential of (S,X_1,...,X_N) is
sum_i V(x_i)+beta|sigma_beta s-N^(-1/2)sum_i f(x_i)|. It is the maximum
of two convex functions when sqrt(N)>=beta H/a. The joint law is exactly
isotropic, because f is orthogonal to all affine functions.

Its mixed (d+1) cumulant with first slot S and all remaining indices in one
copy block equals d! T_d f/(sigma_beta sqrt N), where
T_d f=grad^d E_(mu_z) f|_(z=0)/d!. Different copy blocks vanish. Summing
the squared tensor entries over the N disjoint blocks CANCELS the N in
the denominator. Thus the dimension-uniform cumulant bound gives

    ||T_d f||<=sqrt(b_(d+1))/d!

after beta tends to infinity with N separately increased for each beta.
Adding back the linear part costs sqrt(2). Density of smooth bounded-Hessian
functions after affine projection handles arbitrary L2 f.

No central-limit theorem is used. No limit in N of the experiment is claimed.
The suspension dimension is at least n ceil(beta^2 H^2/a^2)+1 and diverges
as the added noise is removed. This is a proof device, not an efficient
procedure for acquiring an arbitrary observable or its tensor coefficients.
Universality over EVERY dimension is essential in this step.

7. The dyadic criterion and the exact convexity gate
---------------------------------------------------
For a normalized first eigenfunction h_1, construct
h_(i+1)=alpha_(i+1)(-L)^(-1) grad_0 h_i, normalized to preserve the centered
gradient energy. Write v_i=||grad h_i||_2^2 and
chi_i=||Hess h_i-alpha_(i+1)grad h_(i+1)||_2^2. Then

    v_i-v_(i+1)=|E grad h_i|^2=p_i,
    ||Lh_i||_2^2>=||Lh_(i+1)||_2^2+chi_i+a v_i.

The second inequality is Bochner and convexity. It implies v_i tends to
zero. Before half the initial energy is lost, alpha_i^2<=2 lambda and
sum chi_i<=lambda^2. Repeated Poisson integration turns a first Taylor
tensor at later generations into a high-order one at earlier generations.

The resulting tensors are not fully symmetric. A subset-incidence identity
recovers a tensor symmetric in d slots and its next 2d slots from a 2d-slot
symmetrization with cost binomial(2d,d)<=4^d. Its Gram expansion coefficient
on a pair of d-subsets of overlap r is binomial(d+r,d); the Vandermonde
identity and positive sum of squares prove the bound. This combinatorial
identity was checked, not merely presumed from symmetry.

Defects of adjacent swaps are controlled by chi_i. Dyadic degree doubling
gives S_d<= (C lambda)^d S_(2d)+4d C^d lambda R^(2d). Multiplication by
(C lambda)^(d-1) and summing over dyadic d makes the S terms telescope.
If lambda R^2 is sufficiently small, S_1 is too small to lose half the
initial energy, a contradiction. This proves C_P<=C R^2 conditionally.

The smooth two-Gaussian mixture in proof.txt T5 satisfies the Taylor premise
uniformly but has an exponentially small gap. It fails precisely at the
convex Bochner inequality. There is no detected contradiction within BKL's
stated classical hypotheses.

8. Finite-degree extension was checked against primary prior art
----------------------------------------------------------------
Truncating the dyadic argument yields an interpolation of the form
C_P<=C R^2 max{1,(aR^2)^(-1/(d+1))} when coefficient bounds are known
through a suitable finite dyadic degree d. This is NOT a new result:
Song-Zhang v2, 20_upper.tex lines 588-600, states the more precise
coefficient/curvature comparison
16(1+epsilon)R^2 ell(d)^2 max{1,a^(-1/(d+1))}.
The potentially attractive finite-degree certificate direction was therefore
not promoted to novelty.

Audit scope and remaining gates
--------------------------------
Algebra, factorial cancellation, the suspension dimension, and the Bochner
dependency were reconstructed directly. A rough fixed-dimension moment
argument fills the expectation/terminal-energy justification. All these
are internal checks, not a refereed audit. Letwin's original proof,
localization existence and approximation details, exact eigenfunction domains,
and the complete Song-Zhang proof remain external dependencies. It would be
false to mark KLS EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED on this task's evidence alone.

The old N75 gap is separate. Its scale-integrated MI bound uses a specific
matrix field made from a hierarchy's one-slot reduction. The general scalar
Taylor and Q8 conclusions do not supply that field's likelihood entropy
bound. The spectral_geometry team is handling that interface.
