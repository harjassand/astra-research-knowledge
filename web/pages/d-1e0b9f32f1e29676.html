# Hostile audit: the formal local potential has a Friedrichs realization

## Verdict

Conditional on the pinned source's stated orbital and gap propositions, the answer is **yes**. The formal real multiplication function

`V = Δg/(2g) ∈ L²_loc(R³)`

defines a densely defined, nonnegative, closable Schrödinger quadratic form on `C_c^∞(R³)`. Its Friedrichs closure contains both `g` and `h`. The associated local one-particle operator has `g/√2` as its simple spatial ground orbital at energy 0, and `h` as an orbital at the bottom energy `d>0` of the orthogonal complement. After adding spin with the same local potential, Pauli filling makes

`Φ = (g/√2 ↑) wedge (g/√2 ↓) wedge (h ↑)`

a three-electron noninteracting ground state. Its spin-summed density is exactly `n=g²+h²`. The source separately proves `V∉L^{3/2}+L^∞`, so this is an extended local realization outside the exact class excluded by Theorem 1.1.

This is a conditional consequence of the source's own Proposition 4.3, weighted gap (4.23), and Proposition 5.1 plus standard Friedrichs/ground-state-transform arguments. It does not contradict the source theorem, which explicitly restricts to `L^{3/2}+L^∞`; it does show that the title or an unqualified “no Kohn–Sham representation” summary must retain that class restriction. The construction is not a practical potential construction and is not a claim that `V` belongs to a conventional global Sobolev-dual or Kato class.

## Exact source contract

The pinned source is `work/agents/c9_frontier_transfer/sources/kohn_sham.pdf`.

Its Section 4 establishes `g>0`, `g,h∈H¹(R³)∩H²_loc(R³)`, `||g||²=2`, `||h||²=1`, `⟨g,h⟩=0`, `n=g²+h²`, `V=Δg/(2g)∈L²_loc`, and the distributional equations

`(-Δ/2+V)g=0`, `(-Δ/2+V)h=dh`.

The full weighted gap (4.23) says, with `w=g²`,

`E_w[ξ] := (1/2) ∫ w |∇ξ|² ≥ d( ∫w|ξ|² - |∫wξ|²/∫w )`, for every `ξ∈H¹(w)`.

Here `H¹(w)={ξ∈H¹_loc: ∫w(|ξ|²+|∇ξ|²)<∞}`. The beginning of source Section 4 states radial cutoffs followed by local smoothing are dense in this full weighted form norm for positive continuous `w`.

Section 5 proves the ground-state identity

`q_V[f] := (1/2)∫|∇f|² + ∫V|f|² = (1/2)∫g²|∇(f/g)|²`

for `f∈C_c^∞`, extends it to compactly supported `H¹` and to the source class `E_V`, and proves `q_V[g]=0`, `q_V[h]=d`. It explicitly stops short of assuming that this expression is a closed form or an ordinary Hamiltonian. The argument below supplies that missing form step.

## Closability and the precise form domain

Set `w=g²`. Since `g` is continuous and strictly positive, for every compact `K⊂R³` there are `0<c_K≤C_K<∞` with `c_K≤g≤C_K` on `K`. Multiplication by `1/g` is an isometry

`U:L²(dx)→L²(w dx)`, `Uf=f/g`,

because `∫w|f/g|²=∫|f|²`; it is onto, with inverse `ξ↦gξ`. For `f∈C_c^∞`, the source identity gives

`q_0[f] = (1/2)∫w|∇Uf|² ≥0`.

The image `U(C_c^∞)` is dense in `L²(w dx)` since `U` is unitary and `C_c^∞` is dense in `L²(dx)`.

To prove closability directly, let `f_j∈C_c^∞` satisfy `f_j→0` in `L²(dx)` and suppose `∇(f_j/g)` is Cauchy in `L²(w dx)`. On each compact `K`, the local upper and lower bounds on `w` imply `f_j/g→0` in `L²(K)` and that the gradients converge in `L²(K)` to a local vector field `G`. Testing against compactly supported smooth vector fields and integrating by parts shows `G=∇0=0` distributionally on `K`. Exhausting space gives `G=0` locally everywhere; since the original gradients were Cauchy in the global weighted `L²(w dx)` space, their global limit is zero. Thus `q_0[f_j]→0`, which is the closability criterion.

The closure can be identified exactly. The weighted Dirichlet form `E_w` has domain `H¹(w)` and is closed: a Cauchy sequence is Cauchy in ordinary `H¹` on every compact set, while its weighted function and gradient limits are the same local limits; global weighted integrability follows by completeness of the weighted `L²` spaces. The source's radial-cutoff/local-smoothing argument makes `C_c^∞` dense in `H¹(w)`.

Although the transformed core is `U(C_c^∞)=\{f/g:f∈C_c^∞\}`, not `C_c^∞` itself, it is also form-dense in `H¹(w)`. For `ξ∈C_c^∞`, `gξ∈H¹` with compact support. On a compact set, `1/g∈H²_loc` and is an `H¹` multiplier: `1/g` is bounded and `∇(1/g)∈L⁶`, while `H¹` embeds in `L³` locally. Approximate `gξ` in `H¹` by `f_j∈C_c^∞`; then `(f_j/g−ξ)→0` in local `H¹`, hence in the weighted form norm. Density of `C_c^∞` in `H¹(w)` completes the core argument. Therefore

`D(q̄_V) = { g ξ : ξ∈H¹(w) }`,
`q̄_V[gξ] = E_w[ξ]`.

The form `q̄_V` is closed and nonnegative. It is the Friedrichs form closure of `q_0`; no global `H¹` form-domain assertion is needed. On `C_c^∞`, `V∈L²_loc` makes `Sφ=-Δφ/2+Vφ` an `L²` function and `q_0` its symmetric operator form, so the associated Friedrichs operator is an extension of the local differential expression `-Δ/2+V`.

## Inclusion and eigenfunction checks

The source orbitals correspond under `U` to

`Ug=1`, `Uh=h/g`.

Both lie in `H¹(w)`. For `1`, `∫w=||g||²=2` and its gradient is zero. For `h/g`,

`∫w|h/g|²=||h||²=1`,
`(1/2)∫w|∇(h/g)|²=q_V[h]=d`.

Local `H¹` regularity follows because `g` is positive and `g,h∈H²_loc`. Thus `g,h∈D(q̄_V)` and `q̄_V[g]=0`, `q̄_V[h]=d`.

Let `ĝ=g/√2`. The source weighted gap, after substituting `f=gξ`, becomes the closed-form inequality

`q̄_V[f] ≥ d( ||f||² - |⟨ĝ,f⟩|² )`, for all `f∈D(q̄_V)`.

The nonnegative form has kernel exactly `span{g}`. Consequently `q̄_V[g,φ]=0` for every form-domain `φ`, so the Friedrichs operator `A` satisfies `Aĝ=0`. For `h`, `⟨ĝ,h⟩=0`, `||h||=1`, and `q̄_V[h]=d`; hence the nonnegative form

`b[f]=q̄_V[f]-d( ||f||²-|⟨ĝ,f⟩|² )`

has `b[h]=0`. Cauchy–Schwarz for `b` gives `b[h,φ]=0` for all `φ∈D(q̄_V)`, therefore `q̄_V[h,φ]=d⟨h,φ⟩`. The representation theorem for closed forms yields `h∈D(A)` and `Ah=dh`. The gap inequality also gives `A≥d(I-|ĝ⟩⟨ĝ|)` in form sense; no spectrum below `d` occurs orthogonally to `ĝ`.

## Three-electron ground-state verification

Lift `A` to spin orbitals with the same local potential in both spin channels. The scalar zero orbital `ĝ` gives a rank-two spin ground projection

`P_0=|ĝ up⟩⟨ĝ up|+|ĝ down⟩⟨ĝ down|`.

The form inequality becomes `A⊗I_spin ≥ d(I-P_0)`. For any normalized three-fermion state or ensemble with one-particle density matrix `γ`, Pauli exclusion gives `0≤γ≤I`, `Tr γ=3`, and `Tr(γP_0)≤rank(P_0)=2`. Therefore the noninteracting three-body form satisfies

`Tr(γ(A⊗I_spin)) ≥ d(3-Tr(γP_0)) ≥ d`.

The normalized determinant `Φ=(ĝ up)∧(ĝ down)∧(h up)` has one-particle energies `0,0,d`, so it attains this lower bound and is a ground eigenstate of the spinful three-electron Friedrichs Hamiltonian. Its density is

`n_Φ=2|ĝ|²+|h|²=g²+h²=n`.

Thus `Γ=|Φ⟩⟨Φ|` is a genuine pure ground-state ensemble for a local multiplication potential `V∈L²_loc`, with finite potential expectation because the source proves `∫n|V|<∞`. The same determinant is the source's minimizer of `Tens[n]`; this extended realization does not alter the constrained kinetic value.

The physical integer-charge molecule in Theorem 1.1 follows by the source's dilation `x=Zr`. If `V_s` is the scaled formal potential, define `V_phys(r)=Z²V_s(Zr)` and orbitals `f_phys(r)=Z^{3/2}f_s(Zr)`. The one-particle dilation unitarily maps the physical Friedrichs form to `Z²` times the scaled form, maps `C_c^∞` onto itself, and therefore carries its closure and ground eigenspaces with it. The physical density is `n_phys(r)=Z³n_s(Zr)` and the spinful three-electron determinant remains a ground state. Local L² membership is preserved, and the source's Proposition 2.1 scaling argument shows nonmembership in `L^{3/2}+L^∞` is preserved as well. Thus the extended local realization applies to the exact physical density specified in Theorem 1.1, conditional on that theorem's finite selected integer `Z`.

## Scope, caveats, and prior art

The source proves `V∉L^{3/2}+L^∞`; adding a constant cannot put it in that space. The extended realization therefore does not weaken or contradict Theorem 1.1, Proposition 5.2, or Corollary 8.1, whose potential class is explicitly the standard multiplier space. It does refute an unqualified interpretation that the source density has no local-potential noninteracting ground-state ensemble under *any* reasonable self-adjoint realization.

This is a density-tail-specific potential class: `V` is constructed from the source orbitals, and its global form domain is `g H¹(g²)`, generally different from ordinary `H¹`. It is mathematically self-adjoint and local, but the report does not show that `V` lies in a preselected global distribution space such as `H^{-1}+L^p`, obeys common electrostatic boundary/asymptotic constraints, can be computed stably, or has practical chemical meaning.

The exact ground-state identity used here is established prior art: the pinned paper cites Frank and Seiringer's ground-state representation, and Kato's first representation theorem supplies the closed-form-to-operator step. Sutter et al. (2024) prove one-dimensional torus v-representability for potentials with distributional contributions that still generate self-adjoint Hamiltonians; Corso (2026) gives a rigorous distributional-potential DFT formulation for spinless fermions on a one-dimensional interval. These papers show why the local potential class is a substantive axis of any representability claim, but neither proves this three-dimensional Coulomb instance. Chen and Stott (1993) state in their abstract that any “reasonable” density for spinless fermions is pure- or ensemble-v-representable; the accessible abstract does not specify the potential topology, so it is a prior claim requiring interface-level audit rather than a directly comparable theorem here.

## Independent hostile-audit addendum (2026-10-08)

**Verdict: no mathematical objection found under the source hypotheses.** In particular, the noncompact tail step is valid. For an eigenorbital \(f\in E_V\) at energy \(\varepsilon\), first establish the cutoff identity for compactly supported \(H^1\) multipliers by local \(H^1\) approximation. For the tail multiplier \(\eta_R=1-\chi_R\), apply that identity to \(\zeta_M\eta_R f\), where \(\zeta_M\) is an outer cutoff equal to one on \(B_M\), then send \(M\to\infty\). The \(H^1\) and \(\int|V||\cdot|^2\) terms converge because \(f\in E_V\); the extra outer-cutoff energy is bounded by \(C M^{-2}\|f\|_2^2\to0\), with cross terms absent once the outer annulus is disjoint from the fixed transition annulus of \(\chi_R\). Thus

\[
q_V[\eta_R f]=\varepsilon\|\eta_Rf\|_2^2+\tfrac12\int|\nabla\eta_R|^2|f|^2.
\]

For \(f=g\), \(\varepsilon=0\) and the right side is \(O(R^{-2})\|g\|_2^2\); for \(f=h\), both terms tend to zero by the \(L^2\) tail and the same \(R^{-2}\) bound. Positivity of the source-extended \(q_V\) on \(E_V\) then makes the cutoff orbitals Cauchy in the form norm. Local smooth approximation is legitimate: on a fixed compact set \(V\in L^2\), and strong \(H^1\to L^4\) convergence controls \(\int V|u|^2\). This validates the alternative \(E_V\)-based core argument as well as the weighted-transform domain proof above.

The direct mathematical prior is the positive ground-state representation (the pinned paper cites Frank–Seiringer) plus Kato's closed-form representation/Friedrichs theorem; neither is a same-instance 3D DFT theorem. The Chen–Stott abstract claims broad spinless ensemble \(v\)-representability for “reasonable” densities, but its potential space and hypotheses could not be checked from accessible primary full text, so it remains a prior-art flag rather than a demonstrated overlap. Sutter et al.'s distributional-potential result and Corso's rigorous DFT formulation are one-dimensional, hence also not a direct proof of this 3D instance. No exact prior for this particular source density and Friedrichs realization was located; this is not a novelty or priority clearance.

The main physically relevant boundary is unchanged: ordinary Coulombic, bounded, and standard \(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) local potentials remain in the source's excluded class. The constructed \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) potential has a canonical semibounded self-adjoint realization but a source-tail-adapted form domain; this audit does not establish membership in the global Kato class, a kinetic-form-bounded multiplier class, \(H^{-1}+L^p\), or any customary molecular effective-potential class. Those are plausible further classifications, not consequences of \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) or of the Friedrichs construction.
