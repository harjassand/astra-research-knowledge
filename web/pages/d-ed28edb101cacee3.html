# C13 primary source notes

Read selectively; this is a screen, not a full literature review or reproduction.

1. Ying-Jen Yang and Ken A. Dill, “A principled basis for nonequilibrium network flows,” *Nature Communications* 17, 4980 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71239-9. The paper introduces Caliber Force Theory over node probabilities, edge traffic and cycle currents. The companion design preprint below applies inverse/forward design to motors, proofreading and enzyme inhibition. These sources preempt a generic network-flow/kinetic-selectivity compiler. Primary page: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71239-9

2. Ying-Jen Yang and Ken A. Dill, “Nonequilibrium Theory for Molecular Machine Design,” arXiv:2605.09105 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09105. The source states that CFT Design optimizes non-equilibrium flow networks, including motor speed, proofreading speed/accuracy/cost and enzyme inhibition; the source reports an inverse-design framework and forward barrier-routing rules. In October 2026, APS accepted a version in *Physical Review Research*. Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09105

3. Shiling Liang, Paolo De Los Rios, and Daniel Maria Busiello, “Thermodynamic Space of Chemical Reaction Networks,” arXiv:2407.11498v4, 16 September 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11498v4. It bounds stationary reaction affinities by driven elementary flux modes and stationary concentration ratios by extremal pathway effective equilibrium constants, including via chemical probes; TACOS enumerates EFMs for small/medium CRNs. It excludes persistent oscillations and concerns steady-state concentration/affinity regions, not a measured harvested-flux compiler. The version identifies *Physical Review X* (2026, accepted). Code: https://github.com/Shiling42/TACOS

4. Sara Morandi, Oliver Loveday, Thomas Renningholtz et al., “An end-to-end framework for reactivity in heterogeneous catalysis,” *Nature Chemical Engineering* 3, 169–180 (2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s44286-026-00361-8. CARE composes rule-generated CHO networks, GAME-Net-UQ kinetic/thermodynamic estimates and microkinetics. The paper reports 0.3–0.8 eV barrier uncertainty in the Fischer–Tropsch network and states that rate/selectivity-control functionality, network pruning, electrochemical detail and richer reactor models remain future work. It also reports 83% valid transition-state ordering and C6+ boundary accumulation in its truncated chain model. Code/data: CARE v0.1.3, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17977395 ; source code https://github.com/LopezGroup-ICIQ/care
