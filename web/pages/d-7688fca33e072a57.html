## Strongest independently derived claim

    A+B -> 2A,   2A -> A+B,   A+B -> 2B,   2B -> A+B,

with total count `A+B=4`, falling-factorial mass-action propensities

independently controlled rates in `[1,4]`, the first hitting time of the core
`C={(2,2)}` has expectation at most `11/36` from every state in the class,
uniformly over predictable measurable rate controls. The derivation is exact
and uses a rational Foster potential; it is a finite-class result, not a
stochastic permanence theorem.

The synthesized potential in state order `(0,4),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1),(4,0)` is

    h = (11/36, 2/9, 0, 2/9, 11/36).

It is nonnegative and zero on `C`. For each state outside `C`, the generator
is affine in the four rates, so its maximum over the rate box occurs at a
corner. At `(0,4)`, only `2B -> A+B` is enabled, with propensity `4*3=12`;
therefore `L_t h = 12 k_4 (2/9-11/36) = -k_4 <= -1`. The `(4,0)` case is
symmetric. At `(1,3)`, the enabled channels and potential increments are:

    A+B -> 2A: propensity 3, increment -2/9;
    A+B -> 2B: propensity 3, increment +1/12;
    2B -> A+B: propensity 6, increment -2/9.

Thus `L_t h = -(2/3)k_1 +(1/4)k_3 -(4/3)k_4`; its maximum on `[1,4]^4`
is `-1`, attained at `(k_1,k_3,k_4)=(1,4,1)`. The `(3,1)` calculation is
the species-swapped identity. Hence `L_t h <= -1` at every non-core state,
for every allowed rate vector and every time/history. Stopped Dynkin's formula
for the bounded finite-state process gives

    E_x[t ^ tau_C] <= h(x), then E_x[tau_C] <= h(x) <= 11/36.

Here `tau_C` is the first entrance time to `C`. The limit follows by monotone
convergence; no stationary law or Markov control policy is assumed.

## Adversarial boundary/coset example

For `2A <-> 2B` with total count two and target `{(1,1)}`, the state `(0,2)`
can only jump to `(2,0)`, and `(2,0)` can only jump back to `(0,2)`. Both
channels have rate one and falling-factorial propensity two when enabled.
The target is unreachable because the integer reaction lattice preserves
`A mod 2`; the exact checker returns a closed two-state set. Yet the
deterministic mass-action ODE has `x' = 2(y^2-x^2)` and points inward at the
positive-orthant boundary `x=0`; with `x+y=2` it has the interior equilibrium
`(1,1)`. A deterministic boundary vector or positive equilibrium therefore
does not certify molecule-lattice recovery to a supplied target. The finite
CTMC is nonexplosive and recurrent on its own closed classes; the counterexample
only refutes recovery to a target in a different lattice coset.
