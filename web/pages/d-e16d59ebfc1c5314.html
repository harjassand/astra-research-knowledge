1. Threshold inequality correction

In v1 §5, the sentence “If t>n_i^{2/3}, then t(t−1)(t−h)>n_i^2 for large i” is too strong as written: for t just above n_i^{2/3}, the two factors (1−1/t)(1−h/t) are below one. The required implication remains valid because the averaged incidence bound is exponentially o(n_i^2). Explicitly, for t≥n_i^{2/3} and fixed h≤32,

  t(t−1)(t−h) ≥ n_i^2 (1−1/t)(1−h/t)
                 = n_i^2(1−o(1)),

whereas (3) and the strict rate gap give Z(v_i)/n_i^2→0. Thus t(t−1)(t−h)>Z(v_i) for large i, contradicting (4). Therefore the stated t≤n_i^{2/3} conclusion is proved.

The same correction applies to the sharper rate statement: from t(t−1)(t−h)≤Z and t>h, one gets t≤max(h, Z^{1/3}(1+o(1))) as t→∞; the exponential estimate then yields every fixed exponent γ>(ρ_+−85/6)/ρ_−.

2. Scalar-action exponent correction

In v1 §5, the elementary scalar-action comparison should use the sumset estimate |B_i+B_i|≤n_i^{1.89}, hence the injection bound is n_i^{1.89/2}=n_i^{0.945}. The printed n_i^{1.91/2}=n_i^{0.955} used the product exponent by mistake. The incidence bound n_i^{2/3} still improves it strictly.
