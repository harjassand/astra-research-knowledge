# Derivation notes: adaptive hidden-word queries

## Predictive family and cost contract

Let (N=n^d\ge2), and identify the (N) words of length (d) with coordinates (1,\dots,N). For (u\in[N]), define

\[
 f_u(w)=\Delta\,\mathbf 1\{|w|=d,\ w=u\},\qquad \Delta=2^{-H}.
\]

This is a deterministic acyclic weighted automaton with (d+1) states, transitions in {0,1}, and final weight (\Delta). Its length-(d) coefficient table is the one-sparse rational vector (\theta_u=\Delta e_u\).

**Adaptive point-query interface.** At call (t), the learner chooses one word (w_t\in[N]) and an excitation (e_t\in[0,E]) as a function of earlier responses. Before digital encoding the response is

\[
 Y_t=\sqrt{e_t}\,f_U(w_t)+Z_t,\qquad Z_t\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\sigma^2).
\]

The learner receives at most b bits per call. The upper bound below assumes the learner can request the one-bit comparator B_t=1{Y_t >= Delta*sqrt(e_t)/2}; richer encodings can simulate it. A fixed sign-of-Y point channel is not enough to simulate this midpoint comparator: under a miss, its bit stays fair even at high excitation. If the service fixes a quantizer that does not expose a known useful threshold, no general upper bound follows without a non-aliasing condition. Set

\[
 \lambda=\frac{\Delta^2E}{\sigma^2}.
\]

Assume E>0 and sigma>0. The model is a scalar evaluation of one word with bounded per-call excitation. Query calls, total excitation (sum e_t), query-description bits, returned bits, and local arithmetic are separate costs. When excitation is used at its cap, q calls cost qE total excitation. If the physical service has no gain/precision control, put E=1.

## Adaptive point-query minimax frontier

For any adaptive (including randomized) point-query policy with a fixed call budget (q) and worst-case exact-identification error at most (\delta<1/2), the following lower bounds hold:

\[
 q\ge (1-\delta)N-1,
 \qquad
 q\ge \frac{2(N-1)}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{kl}\!\left(\frac12\middle\|\delta\right),
 \qquad
 qb\ge (1-\delta)\log_2N-h_2(\delta).
\tag{P-LB}
\]

The bit-capacity term is Fano's inequality and is useful when (b) varies. For the first term, query choices before the first hit reveal only noise and the fact that a queried word was not the hidden one. With a uniform hidden word, (q) distinct point queries hit with probability at most (q/N), and conditioned on no hit the posterior is uniform over at least (N-q) unseen words. Average success is therefore at most ((q+1)/N), proving the claim. Repeated queries cannot improve the hit event.

For the second term, introduce a reference oracle (P_0) whose response mean is zero for every word. This reference need not be in the target family. The adaptive policy is unchanged; under (P_0), its chosen word and excitation are functions of the noise transcript. The Gaussian chain rule gives

\[
 D(P_0\|P_u)=\frac{\Delta^2}{2\sigma^2}\,
 \mathbb E_0\!\sum_{t=1}^q e_t\mathbf 1\{w_t=u\}.
\]

If \(\alpha_u=P_0(\widehat U=u)\), then \(\sum_u\alpha_u=1\), so for at least (N-1) indices (u), (P_0(\widehat U\ne u)=1-\alpha_u\ge1/2). Under (P_u), this event has probability at most (\delta). Binary data processing yields

\[
 D(P_0\|P_u)\ge\mathrm{kl}(1/2\|\delta).
\]

Summing over those indices and using \(\sum_u e_t\mathbf 1\{w_t=u\}=e_t\le E\) on each history proves the second lower bound. For any finite response quantizer, the KL can only decrease by data processing, so the same lower bound applies. Equivalently, any pathwise total-excitation budget (B) must satisfy (B\ge 2\sigma^2(N-1)\Delta^{-2}\mathrm{kl}(1/2\|\delta)).

### Equally informed adaptive point-query baseline

An explicit tournament matches these bounds. At each round, pair the remaining candidates and pass an unpaired candidate (if any) through unchanged. For each pair, query one designated representative r_j times at energy E, with threshold Delta*sqrt(E)/2, and keep it if the majority bit is 1; otherwise keep its partner. If the pair contains U, the representative bit is Bernoulli with parameter 1-p when the representative is U, and p otherwise, where

\[
 p=\Phi(-\sqrt\lambda/2).
\]

Thus the majority test makes an error with probability at most \(\tfrac12(2\sqrt{p(1-p)})^{r_j}\). There is a universal (c>0) such that

\[
 -\log(2\sqrt{p(1-p)})\ge c\lambda.
\]

For small (\lambda), this follows from (1-2p=2\Phi(\sqrt\lambda/2)-1\ge c'\sqrt\lambda); for large (\lambda), the Gaussian tail gives (p\le\tfrac12e^{-\lambda/8}). Set round error targets (\delta_j=\delta 2^{-j-1}) and take

\[
 r_j=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{1}{c\lambda}\log\frac1{2\delta_j}\right\rceil\right\}.
\]

Only the pair containing U can lose the true candidate; a union bound over rounds gives total error at most delta. The number of point calls at round j is at most ceil(N/2^j)*r_j/2. Summing the geometric series gives

\[
 q\le C\left[N+\frac{N}{\lambda}\left(1+\log\frac1\delta\right)\right]
\tag{P-UB}
\]

for a universal constant (C). The noiseless one-call-per-pair version uses (N-1) calls exactly, matching the no-hit lower bound. For (0<\delta\le1/4), (P-LB) and (P-UB) give the adaptive point-query minimax rate

\[
 q^*_{\rm point}(N,\lambda,\delta)
 =\Theta\!\left(N+\frac{N}{\lambda}\left(1+\log\frac1\delta\right)\right),
\]

with universal constants on this confidence range. The tournament is the strongest matched baseline here: it knows the complete one-hot WFA family, coefficient amplitude, noise variance, energy cap, and error target; it uses only the same legal one-word scalar queries as the learner.

## Which stronger interface removes the (N)-call floor?

The collapsed-cost interface is **adaptive dense linear sensing of the complete length-(d) coefficient table**, not ordinary WFA word evaluation. It permits a public vector (a_t\in\mathbb R^N), chosen adaptively, with (\|a_t\|_2^2\le E), and returns one bit about

\[
 Y_t=\langle a_t,\theta_U\rangle+Z_t=\Delta a_{t,U}+Z_t.
\]

Take the response bit to be 1{Y_t >= 0}. For general N, split the current candidate set S_j into two parts of sizes floor(|S_j|/2) and ceil(|S_j|/2). Use a vector equal to +sqrt(E/|S_j|) on one part, -sqrt(E/|S_j|) on the other, and zero outside. It has norm squared E, and the response sign says which part contains U. If legal weights must be rational, replace the normalized coefficient by a dyadic value within a factor 2 and reduce the overall scale if needed to keep norm squared at most E; this changes only constants. Repetition plus majority gives the same Gaussian separation/round-error calculation as above. There are at most ceil(log_2 N) stages, and the candidate-set size at stage j is at most 2N/2^j. Hence

\[
 q\le C\left[\log_2N+\frac{N}{\lambda}\left(1+\log\frac1\delta\right)\right].
\]

For the lower bound, the same (P_0)-reference argument works because \(\sum_u a_{t,u}^2\le E\); it gives \(q\ge2(N-1)\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{kl}(1/2\|\delta)\). A one-bit response also gives \(q\ge\Omega(\log N)\) by Fano for fixed (\delta\le1/4). Therefore, for a fixed finite alphabet (in particular one bit),

\[
 q^*_{\rm group}(N,\lambda,\delta)
 =\Theta\!\left(\log N+\frac{N}{\lambda}\left(1+\log\frac1\delta\right)\right),
 \qquad 0<\delta\le1/4,
\]

At high per-query SNR this changes (N-1) point calls to (ceil(log_2 N)) aggregate calls. At low SNR the (N/\lambda) noise cost dominates both interfaces.

The exact distinction is visible in the noiseless case: a point query asks whether one named coefficient is nonzero, so (N-1) negative answers may be needed; a signed group query asks whether the nonzero coefficient lies in one half of the remaining index set, so one bit halves the candidate set. The stronger query transmits a length-N weight vector. Charge its N entries and their coefficient precision, plus evaluation work; call count alone does not measure acquisition. A point query names one length-d word (log2 N input bits); a dense group call needs N coefficient entries, so transmitting or materializing its action costs Theta(N) entries per call.

A second call-count counterexample uses one dense query with arbitrary multilevel output. Set (a_j=j) for (j=0,\dots,N-1). In the noiseless case, (Y=\Delta U); a known (N)-level, (b=\lceil\log_2N\rceil)-bit quantizer returns (U) in one call. But \(\|a\|_2^2=\sum_jj^2=\Theta(N^3)\), the query contains (N) coefficients, and its evaluation touches (N) entries. With Gaussian noise, index spacing (g) and nearest-level decoding, taking \(\Delta g\ge2\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2N/\delta)}\) suffices by a Gaussian-tail union bound; energy is \(g^2\sum_jj^2=\Theta(\sigma^2N^3\Delta^{-2}\log(N/\delta))\). This is a valid one-call code only when the high energy and multilevel response alphabet are part of the contract.

Its recorded lower bound is

\[
 C\ge2\sigma^2(n/E)^d\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta).
\]

Here the resource is expected dimension and (E) is a per-letter matrix-excitation constraint. In the present point/group scalar model, the resource is calls and/or scalar excitation, and (E) bounds a flattened vector or per-call word gain. ## Rational height, separation, and quantizer alignment

For (\Delta=2^{-H}),

\[
 \frac{N}{\lambda}=\frac{N\sigma^2 2^{2H}}{E}.
\]

So coefficient height (H) gives an exponential-in-(H) noisy acquisition term despite only (d+1) states. For a general pair with observable contrast (\Delta_c) attenuated by the actual query/evaluation map to (\gamma=\Delta_c/\kappa), replace (\Delta) by (\gamma); the noise term grows by (\kappa^2). A condition number caused solely by changing realization basis does not attenuate the represented series and cannot be inserted without an observation-map argument.

Reduced rationals with numerator and denominator at most (2^H) can differ by as little as (2^{-2H}). A family containing such near-neighbors can therefore require a pairwise noisy cost on the order of \(\sigma^2\kappa^2 2^{4H}\mathrm{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta)/E\). This is a worst-case separation statement, not a height-only theorem for arbitrary families.

Finite precision is an interface property. A one-bit chosen comparator at the known midpoint distinguishes zero from (\Delta\) at any (H); noise drives the (2^{2H}) cost. But a fixed noiseless uniform quantizer with bin width (h), zero centered in a bin, returns the same symbol for all point-query responses if \(\Delta\sqrt E<h/2\), making (U) exactly unidentifiable. Adding Gaussian noise before quantization removes the exact collision in general; the analog KL lower remains by data processing. No fixed-grid upper is claimed without its bins and threshold behavior.

## Exact finite verifier

`verify_adaptive_queries.py` exhaustively checks exact rational fixtures. For (N=8,\Delta=1/8), the adaptive point tournament identifies every hidden word in (N-1=7) point calls; the one-bit balanced signed-group query identifies all words in 3 calls with per-call energy at most 1 and maximum path energy 2. For (N=4), a one-shot two-bit dense multilevel query identifies all words with exact energy 14. `verification.json` records the assertions. These fixtures verify noiseless finite query trees and arithmetic only; they do not validate Gaussian error probabilities or the asymptotic proofs.

## Source and status ledger

- Davenport and Arias-Castro, [“Compressive Binary Search”](https://arxiv.org/html/1202.0937), gives the one-sparse adaptive Gaussian linear model, a half-versus-half total-variation lower bound, and binary-search upper. The adaptive group lower/upper here specialize that established setting; the (P_0)-reference precision lower and point-query tournament are independently derived here.
- Malloy and Nowak, [“Near-Optimal Compressive Binary Search”](https://arxiv.org/html/1203.1804), Theorem 1 gives an analog-response near-optimal CBS allocation with (N/\lambda\cdot\log(1/\delta)+\log N) calls. Our one-bit construction is separately derived.
- Castro, [“Adaptive sensing performance lower bounds for sparse signal detection and support estimation”](https://arxiv.org/html/1206.0648), treats coordinate observations with adaptive precision and compressed sensing under Frobenius-energy constraints. Its risk and neighboring-support assumptions differ; no result is imported as a premise.
- Local interface cards: `work/astra_prior/cards/N25-acquisition-interface-tests.txt`, `work/astra_prior/cards/L01-cyclic-grid.txt`; local blocker `B-EXACT-ORACLE-INTERFACE` records the mismatch as source-derived and unresolved.

The adaptive point-query frontier is a scoped theorem for the known one-hot rational WFA family and the stated noise/energy model; the aggregate-query extension is a separate, strictly richer interface. Sparse-support sensing and CBS are established prior art. No novelty or priority claim is made for the underlying mechanisms. The fixed-quantizer contract, general unknown-WFA learning, and any N25/L01 bridge remain open.
