# Filters and Schur parents on the complement-LCU image

**Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE** for the exact spectral/row identities and
the scoped filter barrier below. The detectability-lemma estimate is
**EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED** at the cited primary source. The analysis rules out
scalar polynomial and rational filtering of the fixed complement-LCU projector
as a route to polynomial row access. It does not rule out another positive
enforcer, another PREPARE completion, or a sparse parent built directly from
the LCU circuit.

## Decisive result

For the complement-LCU isometry in `00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md`, the
canonical enforcer is already a projection

\[
C_0=U\bigl((I-|0\rangle\langle0|)\otimes I\bigr)U^*=I-VV^*,
\qquad C_0^2=C_0=C_0^*,
\]

with `ker(C0)=ran(V)`, operator norm one, absolute gap one, and normalized gap
one. Therefore, for every polynomial or rational scalar function `f` defined
at 0 and 1,

\[
f(C_0)=f(0)(I-C_0)+f(1)C_0.
\]

Exact kernel preservation requires `f(0)=0`; a nonzero enforcer also requires
`f(1)>0`. In that case `f(C0)=f(1)C0`: the norm and absolute gap both scale by
`f(1)`, the normalized gap stays one, and **the physical row support is exactly
unchanged**. In particular, Chebyshev filters, polynomial powers, QSVT
polynomials, and rational resolvents of this projector cannot sparsify it.

For the explicit legal PREPARE completion fixed in
`01_canonical_C.md`, one same-output-label block row of `C0` has exactly `5^R`
nonzeros. Thus every nonzero scalar polynomial/rational filter that preserves
the exact image still has a row with `5^R` nonzeros. Since the BMVZ repetition
parameter `R` grows as a positive power of the source input size, this is a
superpolynomial row-output lower bound for this fixed `V,B,C0`. It is not a
lower bound for all PREPARE completions or all positive enforcers.

The standard two-by-two rational Schur parent also fails to bridge the gap.
For a positive contraction `C` with gap `delta`, set

\[
P_\tau(C)=\frac1\tau\begin{pmatrix}C&C\\C&C+\tau I\end{pmatrix},\qquad \tau>0.
\]

Its Schur complement after eliminating the lower-right block is
`C(C+tau I)^(-1)`. If `C=C0`, this is just `C0/(1+tau)` and has the same
`5^R` row. If `C` is a genuinely polynomial-gap sparse parent with gap
`delta`, choosing `tau=delta` makes the Schur complement have constant gap,
but the parent has norm `Theta(1/delta)` and constant absolute gap; after
normalizing the parent to a contraction, its gap is only `Theta(delta)`. The
parent is row-sparse only when `C` is row-sparse in the first place.

## Exact physical entries and access costs

Let `D=M+1=2^q` be the padded LCU label dimension, `N` the data-qubit count,
and `R` the BMVZ repetition count. Include an identity SELECT branch labelled
0, with `R_0=I`, and term branches `j=1,...,M` with

\[
H=\sum_{j=1}^M\pi_jh_j,\quad h_j=I-Q_j,\quad
Q_j=\bigotimes_{s=1}^R Q_{j,s},\quad Q_{j,s}=I-\Pi_{j,s},\quad
R_j=I-2h_j=2Q_j-I.
\]

Here each `Pi_(j,s)` is a fixed-arity projector (arity five in the BJSW
instance), and factors are on the disjoint repetition copies. The PREPARE
column has `chi_0=1/sqrt(2)` and `chi_j=sqrt(pi_j/2)`; choose the polynomial-size
algebraic completion `A` fixed in `01_canonical_C.md`. With
`U=(A* tensor I) SELECT (A tensor I)`, its full computational-basis entries
are

\[
U_{(a,x),(b,y)}=\sum_{j=0}^M\overline{A_{ja}}A_{jb}(R_j)_{x,y}.
\]

The output blocks of `V=U(|0> tensor I)` are

\[
K_a=\sum_{j=0}^M\overline{A_{ja}}\chi_jR_j,
\quad (VV^*)_{(a,x),(b,y)}=(K_aK_b^*)_{x,y},
\]

and hence

\[
(C_0)_{(a,x),(b,y)}=\mathbf1[a=b]\mathbf1[x=y]
-\sum_{j,k=0}^M\overline{A_{ja}}\chi_j A_{kb}\overline{\chi_k}
 (R_jR_k^*)_{x,y}.\tag{1}
\]

For `j,k>=1`,

\[
R_jR_k^*=4Q_jQ_k-2Q_j-2Q_k+I,
\qquad (Q_jQ_k)_{x,y}=\prod_{s=1}^R(Q_{j,s}Q_{k,s})_{x_s,y_s}.
\]

Each local product acts on at most ten data qubits per repetition copy. A
specified entry of (1) therefore takes `O(M^2 R)` fixed-size algebraic
operations plus the PREPARE/weight arithmetic; its `b`-bit approximation is
computable in polynomial time in `M,R,b` and the coefficient bit lengths.
Termwise precision `O(2^-b/M^2)` suffices for total entry error `O(2^-b)`.
This is **entry access**, not row enumeration.

For a data row `x`, a product factor has row support

\[
\sigma_x(Q_j)=\prod_{s=1}^R\sigma_{x_s}(Q_{j,s})\le 2^{5R},
\]

so `h_j` and `R_j` have row support at most `1+2^(5R)`. For each pair
`(j,k)`, `R_jR_k*` has support on the union of at most `10R` data qubits and
at most `2^(10R)` columns in a fixed row. Summing over the `D` output labels
and `(M+1)^2` term pairs gives the safe bound

\[
1\le s(C_0)\le
\min\{D2^N,\;O(DM^2 2^{10R})\}
=\min\{D2^N,\;O(M^3 2^{10R})\}.
\]

The lower bound `s(C0)>=5^R` is for the explicit completion in
`01_canonical_C.md`; there the selected seed block has
`K_b=\tau_0(R_{\phi,R}-R_{\psi,R})`, `\tau_0=1/(4\sqrt2)`, and the row
`x=00010^R` has exactly the `5^R` nonzero same-label columns indexed by
`{00010,10010,01110,10110,11010}^R`. The other entries in the full physical
row cannot cancel entries in that fixed column-label block. Listing the row
costs at least `5^R` output operations even though any one entry costs only
polynomial time. The upper bound is not an exact global support count.

`U` and `C0` also have polynomial-size application circuits (controlled
clause reflections use `O(R)` local gates per term, given the local term
descriptions). Circuit access and the specified-entry formula do not imply a
polynomial row-list algorithm. Finite-gate-set synthesis is approximate; an
operator-norm synthesis error must be charged separately, and a nonzero error
does not preserve an exact kernel automatically.

The diagonal compression remains

\[
B=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)\otimes I,\quad s(B)=1,\quad\|B\|=1,
\quad H\le V^*BV=2H-H^2\le2H.
\]

All scalar filters of `C0` below leave `V` and `B` unchanged, so this positive
logical comparison is preserved exactly. They also leave the dense row
unchanged.

## Route tests

### Chebyshev and polynomial filters

If `p` is any polynomial with `p(0)=0`, idempotence gives

\[
p(C_0)=p(1)C_0.
\]

Thus a degree-one polynomial is already enough to retain a constant normalized
gap; increasing the Chebyshev degree only increases the circuit/query cost and
does not change the exact matrix support. On the fixed completion, every
nonzero `p(C0)` still has the selected `5^R` row. A formal expansion of a
degree-`d` polynomial in a general `s`-row-sparse matrix has the loose bound
`1+s+...+s^d`, but that generic bound is irrelevant here: the exact spectral
identity collapses the polynomial to one scalar multiple of `C0`.

This calculation is about filtering the **image enforcer**. A polynomial
filter of the logical Hamiltonian `H` is a different operator and does not
automatically have kernel `ran(V)`. For context, the QMA promise is already a
constant yes/no energy separation; polynomial filtering of `H` is not needed
to create that promise. The artificial fine-level spacing used in earlier
compression tests is not the BMVZ yes/no promise gap.

### Rational resolvents and the two-by-two Schur parent

On the exact projector,

\[
(C_0+\tau I)^{-1}=\tau^{-1}(I-C_0)+(1+\tau)^{-1}C_0,
\qquad C_0(C_0+\tau I)^{-1}=(1+\tau)^{-1}C_0.
\]

The filtered enforcer has norm and absolute gap `1/(1+tau)` and normalized
gap one. Its row support is exactly `s(C0)`, including the `5^R` selected
row. The unfiltered inverse has no kernel. In its off-diagonal entries it is
`-1/(tau(1+tau))` times the corresponding `C0` entry, so it also retains every
off-diagonal nonzero of that row. This is an exact support statement, not a
generic claim that inverses of sparse matrices are dense.

Filtering the logical BMVZ operator `H` is a separate use of a resolvent.
The scalar response `r_tau(lambda)=lambda/(lambda+tau)` can separate a
negligible yes energy from a constant no energy when the pole scale is chosen
between those promise ranges. But `r_tau(H)` is not an image enforcer: for a
positive yes minimum it is still positive on the logical ground vector, and
its kernel is not `ran(V)`. In the physical data basis the input terms obey
`s(H)<=M(1+2^(5R))`; hence the standard Schur parent with `C=H` has only the
upper row bound `2s(H)+1`, already superpolynomial at this level of accounting.
The rational Schur complement may have still denser rows. No full-family
lower bound on the support of `(H+tau I)^(-1)` is claimed. Likewise, a
degree-`d` polynomial in `H` has the safe row bound
`min{2^N, 1+s(H)+...+s(H)^d}`, while QSVT supplies circuit/query access and
does not supply a row-list algorithm.

For a general positive contraction `C`, the parent `P_tau(C)` above is
positive and its lower-right block is invertible. On a `C`-eigenvector of
eigenvalue `lambda`, its two eigenvalues are exactly

\[
m_\pm(\lambda;\tau)=
\frac{2\lambda+\tau\pm\sqrt{4\lambda^2+\tau^2}}{2\tau}.
\]

The Schur complement identity is exact:

\[
\operatorname{Schur}_{22}(P_\tau(C))
=C/\tau-(C/\tau)((C+\tau I)/\tau)^{-1}(C/\tau)
=C(C+\tau I)^{-1}.
\]

If `s(C)=s`, every parent row has support at most `2s+1`; the Schur
complement need not have this row bound because it contains an inverse. For the
fixed LCU `C0`, the parent already has a row with at least `5^R` entries,
because one of its diagonal blocks is `C0/τ`. The Schur complement itself is
`C0/(1+τ)`, with the same lower bound.

For a sparse enforcer `C` with norm one and gap `delta`, choose `tau=delta`.
The Schur complement gap is
`delta/(delta+tau)=1/2`, while the parent has
`m_-(delta;delta)=(3-sqrt(5))/2` on the first excited eigenspace and
`m_+(1;delta)=Theta(1/delta)` at the top of the spectrum. Its unnormalized
gap is constant, but after scaling the parent to norm at most one its gap is
`Theta(delta)`. Thus the standard rational lift moves the constant gap into a
dense Schur complement; the sparse parent retains the polynomial normalized
gap. This is a barrier for this explicit linearization, not for all possible
sparse parents.

### Detectability sweeps on the amplified terms

The imported detectability lemma has a frustration-free projector interface.
For `F=sum_i Q_i`, with common kernel `G`, gap `gamma` above `G`, and at most
`g` noncommuting neighbors per projector, any ordered sweep
`D=product_i(I-Q_i)` obeys

\[
\|D|_{G^\perp}\|^2\le (1+\gamma/g^2)^{-1}.
\]

Consequently `C_DL=I-D*D` is positive, has kernel `G`, norm at most one, and
gap at least `gamma/(g^2+gamma)`. After `q` sweeps,

\[
C_{DL,q}=I-(D^q)^*D^q,\quad
\lambda_{\min}^+(C_{DL,q})\ge1-(1+\gamma/g^2)^{-q};
\]

`q=Theta((g^2/gamma) log(1/eta))` gives gap at least `1-eta`.

This does not supply an enforcer for the exact full-data LCU image. `C_DL`
annihilates the common ground space of the term checks, whereas `C0` must
annihilate `ran(V)`, which contains the encoded copy of **every** logical data
vector. In the BMVZ hard family, completeness is approximate rather than an
assumption of an exact common kernel; on a no-instance the positive minimum
energy makes the frustration-free hypothesis fail. In addition, an
amplified term has locality `5R` and maximum row support `2^(5R)`. With `M`
terms a safe noncommuting-degree bound is `g<=5R(M-1)`, and a product over the
terms has the loose physical row bound `min{2^N,2^(5RM)}`. A `q`-sweep loss
operator has the corresponding safe bound

\[
s(C_{DL,q})\le\min\{2^N,\,1+2^{10qMR}\}.
\]

The sweep circuit uses `O(qMR)` local-factor operations, but this does not
enumerate a row of `C_DL,q`; no polynomial row-list algorithm follows from the
detectability estimate. The weights in the LCU sum also matter: the DL is
applied to an unweighted projector sum, whose gap and norm must be related to
the normalized weighted operator before quoting a contraction. If
`H_w=sum_i pi_i Q_i`, `F=sum_i Q_i`, and `pi_max=max_i pi_i`, then
`H_w<=pi_max F`; on a shared frustration-free kernel this gives
`gap(F)>=gap(H_w)/pi_max`, while `||F||<=M`. For uniform weights `1/M`, the
unnormalized gap of `F` is `M` times that of `H_w`; it must not be confused
with a normalized gap.

The same issue appears if one builds a sparse circuit-history parent for the
polynomial-size circuit `U`. With `T` elementary gates `G_t`, its path terms

\[
E_t=\tfrac12\bigl(|t+1\rangle\langle t+1|\otimes I
+|t\rangle\langle t|\otimes I
-|t+1\rangle\langle t|\otimes G_t
-|t\rangle\langle t+1|\otimes G_t^*\bigr)
\]

plus an input-label penalty have a history-image kernel and a path gap
`Theta(T^-2)` before extra clock-validity penalties. For two-qubit gates, each
clock/data row has at most `1+2*2^2=9` entries in the abstract path-clock
matrix; the circuit and row support are polynomial/constant, respectively.
The clock is a `T+1`-level register (binary encoding gives `O(log T)` clock
locality; a constant-local unary encoding uses `O(T)` clock bits and must
charge validity terms). The uniform history isometry is

\[
\widetilde V|\psi\rangle=(T+1)^{-1/2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}|t\rangle
U_t(|0\rangle\otimes|\psi\rangle).
\]

Testing the original diagonal `B` only at the final clock time gives
`\widetilde V^*(|T><T| tensor B)\widetilde V=(V^*BV)/(T+1)`. Restoring a
constant lower compression factor by scaling this test by `T+1` violates its
contraction bound. Thus this sparse history parent changes the image and loses
the common-compression constant before amplification. A different clock
distribution or a different circuit parent is not excluded here.

## Scoped theorem and boundary

**Projector-filter barrier.** Fix the explicit complement-LCU completion and
`C0` above. Let `f` be any real polynomial or rational function with no pole
on `{0,1}`, and suppose `f(C0)` is positive with exact kernel `ran(V)` and
nonzero gap. Then `f(C0)=cC0` for `c=f(1)>0`; its absolute gap and norm are both
`c`, its normalized gap is one, and its maximum physical row support is at
least `5^R`. Hence no scalar functional-calculus filter of this fixed `C0`
produces a polynomial-row enforcer. The exact falsifier is a nonzero value in
the fixed selected row disappearing after applying `f`; idempotence makes
that impossible.

This theorem does **not** cover a non-scalar matrix transform, a new isometry,
a PREPARE completion not fixed in `01_canonical_C.md`, an arbitrary positive
matrix with kernel `ran(V)`, or a sparse local parent whose Schur complement
is not a scalar function of `C0`. The unresolved gate is a polynomial-row
positive `C` with a full-space constant gap for the exact LCU image while
retaining `H <= V*BV <= 2H`. Circuit access, specified-entry access, and
row-list access remain three distinct requirements.
