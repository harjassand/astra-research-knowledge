Contract and resource statement
------------------------------
Public data are L (equivalently N=L-1), J,tau, the detuning bound B, a
certified g_->0, and target 0<epsilon<=1. Unknown values satisfy
|lambda|<=B and g>=g_-; there is no required upper bound on g. The input is
the normalized Gibbs state of

  H_L(lambda,g)=Hcrit+(g/L)W-(lambda/L^2)Npart,
  beta=tau L^2,

on the S=L-1 physical sites. The encoder/decoder use no actual lambda or g.
For every L there are CPTP maps whose output trace error is at most epsilon,
with persistent register dimension independent of L and

  log_2 D = O((1+log(1/epsilon))^(3/2))

uniformly for 0<epsilon<=1, equivalently O(log^(3/2)(1/epsilon)) as
epsilon tends to zero at fixed public constants. The `1+` makes the bound
literal up to epsilon=1. Number labels and overflow flags are included in D;
reversible workspace, physical input/output spins, gate synthesis, and
runtime are charged separately. This is an achievable upper bound, not an
optimality result.

1. Compression map and multiplicity constant
---------------------------------------------
In sector m, ordered positions x_1<...<x_m map bijectively to
1<=y_1<=...<=y_m<=n, n=L-m, by y_i=x_i-i+1. Contacts correspond exactly
to equal adjacent y's. If value v occurs r_v times, the sorted tuple has

  w(y)=m!/prod_v r_v!

distinct labeled permutations. The map

  (K_m psi)(z)=psi(sort(z))/sqrt(m!)

has diagonal Gram matrix K_m^*K_m with entry w(y)/m!=1/prod_v r_v!.
Thus 0<=K_m^*K_m<=I, it is exactly isometric off the contact set, and

  I-K_m^*K_m <= 1_{W>0} <= W.

This counts contact configurations correctly even when a contact cluster
has multiplicity greater than two.

The free one-particle Dirichlet operator has form
2J[sum |u(v+1)-u(v)|^2+|u(1)|^2+|u(n)|^2]. In its m-fold tensor sum,
every labeled cube edge that sorts to a physical hop contributes
2J/m! times the squared amplitude difference. For a source tuple with
multiplicity r_v at v, the number of such labeled edges is w(y)r_v, and

  w(y)r_v = m!/[(r_v-1)! r_{v+1}! prod_{u notin{v,v+1}} r_u!] <= m!.

So each physical hop coefficient is at most its 2J exclusion coefficient.
Ghost-boundary edges have counts w(y)r_1 or w(y)r_n, also at most m!, and
map to the two physical endpoint-killing terms. The n=1 sector includes
both faces and obeys the same inequality. Therefore

  K_m^* Hfree_{n,m} K_m <= Hcrit,m.

The inequality is a quadratic-form comparison for arbitrary complex
vectors. It does not rely on bosonic input statistics, a continuum limit,
or a parameter-dependent eigenbasis.

2. Energy certificate and truncation budgets
---------------------------------------------
Let Hpos=Hcrit+(g/L)W and G0=tau L^2 Hpos. The vacuum has zero energy, so
the grand partition function is at least one. Scalar Jensen under the exact
Gibbs eigenvalue distribution gives

  Tr(G0 rho)/2 <= log Tr(rho exp(G0/2))
                  = log[Z_L(tau/2,2lambda,g)/Z_L(tau,lambda,g)].

Apply the supplied uniform all-sector bound at tau/2 and detuning bound
2B. Its constants are

  c=min(g_-/96,J/72),
  C_half=1+2 sum_{k>=1} exp(-2J tau k^2),
  A_half=e C_half exp(tau B+tau c/2).

The vacuum denominator and the sector sum yield

  Tr(G0 rho)<=E_*=2 log sum_{m>=0} A_half^m exp(-tau c m^2/2).

Completing the square and bounding the shifted lattice Gaussian by its
integral plus one peak gives the stated computable upper

  E_bar=(log A_half)^2/(tau c)
          +2 log(1+sqrt(2 pi/(tau c))) >= E_*.

Since Hcrit,W>=0, this implies uniformly

  Tr(Hcrit rho)<=E_bar/(tau L^2),
  Tr(W rho)<=E_bar/(tau g_- L).

The parameter substitutions are correct: halving beta leaves the chemical
exponent tau lambda m unchanged, hence the half-temperature theorem uses
2lambda; g is unchanged. No g upper bound enters. This step depends on the
all-sector theorem; this audit does not duplicate the continuum lead's
independent audit of that theorem's local-coercivity and heat-trace proof.

Choose r so t_r=Pr(Npart>r)<=epsilon^2/54, and choose M,L0 with

  M+1 >= sqrt(54 E_bar/(8 tau J epsilon^2)),
  L0 >= max(2,54 E_bar/(tau g_- epsilon^2)).

For sectors m<=r, the low-mode projector P_{M,m,n} keeps all one-particle
Dirichlet labels <=min(M,n). If n>M, every omitted vector has free energy
at least

  eps_{n,M+1}=4J[1-cos(pi(M+1)/(n+1))]
             >=8J(M+1)^2/(n+1)^2
             >=8J(M+1)^2/L^2.

If n<=M, there is no mode leakage. The form inequality therefore gives

  Tr(K_m rho_m K_m^*(I-P_{M,m,n}))
       <= L^2 Tr(Hcrit,m rho_m)/(8J(M+1)^2).

These are unnormalized sector inequalities; summing them does not divide by
a small sector probability. The three budgets are respectively number tail,
contact loss, and mode loss, each at most epsilon^2/54.

3. Explicit CPTP maps and trace error
-------------------------------------
For L>=L0 define the success encoder operator

  A_L = direct-sum_{0<=m<=r} P_{M,m,n} K_m,

padding n<=M by zero occupation in unused modes and using the one-dimensional
vacuum sector K_0=P_0=1. This direct sum includes the particle-number label.
It is a contraction. An exact trace-preserving encoder is given by success
Kraus A_L and overflow Kraus |ov><.| sqrt(I-A_L^*A_L). The decoder success
operator is the direct sum of K_m^* restricted to the archived low-mode
space; complete its missing trace by a vacuum-output Kraus and map overflow
to vacuum. Thus the decoder is also CPTP. The success operator of the
composite on the physical input is

  R_L = direct-sum_{0<=m<=r} K_m^* P_{M,m,n} K_m,

and every other encoder/decoder path outputs the same vacuum. The resulting
channel is exactly

  rho -> R_L rho R_L + Tr[(I-R_L^2)rho] |vac><vac|.

This accounts for both the nonisometric encoder loss and the decoder's
failure branch; it does not treat K_m^* as a free inverse or postselect on
success.

For 0<=R_L<=I, I-R_L^2<=(2)(I-R_L). The contact, mode, and number bounds
above give

  p:=Tr[(I-R_L^2)rho]
    <=2[t_r+E_bar/(tau g_- L)+E_bar/(8 tau J(M+1)^2)]
    <=epsilon^2/9.

For a purification |Psi> of rho, compare |Psi><Psi| to
(R_L tensor I)|Psi><Psi|(R_L tensor I). The successful vector has squared
norm 1-p and overlap alpha=Tr(R_L rho)>=Tr(R_L^2 rho)=1-p. The exact
two-vector trace norm is

  sqrt[(2-p)^2-4|alpha|^2] <= 2 sqrt(p).

Partial trace contracts trace norm; adding the vacuum failure state costs
another p. Hence the error is at most 2sqrt(p)+p<=3sqrt(p)<=epsilon.
This argument permits within-sector coherences. A coherent direct-sum
implementation also preserves cross-sector coherences; measuring m and
keeping its label dephases those coherences but is sufficient for the
number-block-diagonal Gibbs inputs in the theorem.

For L<L0, retain the physical occupation basis only in sectors m<=r and
send m>r to an overflow flag. Because the Gibbs state commutes with Npart,
the decoded error is at most 2t_r<=epsilon. Since S=L-1<=L0-2, its memory
dimension is bounded by

  D_small=1+sum_{m=0}^{min(r,L0-2)} binom(L0-2,m).

For large L the low-mode memory dimension, including its overflow flag, is

  D_large=1+sum_{m=0}^r binom(M+m-1,m)=1+binom(M+r,r).

One persistent register of dimension max(D_small,D_large) works for every
L. With r=O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))), M=O(epsilon^-1), and L0=O(epsilon^-2),
both log D bounds are O(log^(3/2)(1/epsilon)) as epsilon tends to zero.
For literal uniformity through epsilon=1, use 1+log(1/epsilon).

4. Cost and scope audit
----------------------
The memory bound is for the persistent archive register only. It includes
the m direct-sum label and the overflow state. The construction additionally
uses O(r log N) reversible workspace, outputs N physical spins, and pays
for number counting/ranking, variable-multiplicity permutation ranking,
controlled contraction amplitudes, sine-transform synthesis, and precision
of finite rotations. The source charges these separately and does not claim
N-independent total runtime, exact free rotations, or efficient Gibbs-state
preparation. The conservative dense sine transform cost is stated as
O(r N^2 poly(log N)); no fast transform is assumed.

The code preserves full trace norm for the Gibbs family but not the
unbounded scaled-energy observable. Contact amplitudes are attenuated by
the map, so energy-preserving decoding is a separate open problem. The
uniform particle tail is not a fixed-m trace-semigroup limit and does not
by itself establish a Lieb-Liniger continuum archive or limit state.
