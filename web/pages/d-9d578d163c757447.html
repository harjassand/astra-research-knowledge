SCOPE
This supplementary material provides the finite-volume identities and estimates underpinning the Bott metricMb
and its relation to the real-space integrated quantum metric. The two main statements proved here are Theorem III.7
and Proposition IV.1. Theorem III.7 has two parts. First, at finite volume on a square torus it identifiesMb with
aperiodicreal-space metric trace built from the compressed one-step defects, up to a controlledO(L−2)trace-log
remainder. Second, under an explicit thermodynamic-limit assumption on the displacement-averaged projector
correlators, this periodic finite-volume metric converges to the standard infinite-volume real-space integrated metric
traceTrG. Thus the theorem yields
lim
L→∞
Mb = TrG.
We note that all finite-dimensional algebraic steps are independent of analytic hypotheses. Analytic input enters only
through Assumption I.1 and Assumption I.2. In addition, the discussion in Sec. IV of how large values ofMb arise
near topological or localization transitions is intended as a mechanism-level interpretation of the finite-volume algebra
under degradation of locality bounds; except where explicitly stated as propositions, these remarks are not claimed as
theorem-level consequences of Assumptions I.1–I.2 alone.

Once this perturbative control is lost, Eq.(48) shows the mechanism for large growth ofMb: if a nontrivial portion of
the defect spectrum moves close to1, then|det(W P )| becomes very small and the log-volume contraction becomes
large.
It is important, however, to distinguish loss of perturbative control from exact finite-volume blow-up. Loss of locality
by itself implies that the expansion underlying Theorem III.7 can fail and thatMb may become very large. What
the finite-volume algebra proves exactly is only the sharper statement(49): true divergence at fixed finite volume
requires an exact singularity of the projected plaquette, equivalently an eigenvalue1ofEx or Ey. Therefore the
near-critical discussion should be read as a physically motivated blow-up mechanism – strong leakage, exponentially
small |det(W P )|, and possible divergence – rather than as a theorem asserting generic finite-size divergence from
locality loss alone.
This interpretation is also consistent with the behavior ofTrG in the localized regime and near transition points:
deep in a gapped or localized regime, both quantities are controlled by the same quadratic defect operators and remain
finite and smooth, whereas near criticality the control of these defect operators is precisely what is lost. The Bott
metric thus furnishes a contraction-based probe of the geometric deterioration that, in the localized regime, is encoded
by the integrated quantum metric [14, 15, 19], while retaining its own exact finite-volume singularity criterion through
Proposition IV.1. When one observes sharp peaks or rapid growth ofMb in the numerics, the algebra above explains
them as a signal that the projected twist steps are ceasing to be almost isometries on the occupied subspace. Only
when the singularity criterion is actually met does one obtain a true divergence inMb.
