UNIFORM GRAND-CANONICAL PARTICLE TAIL AT THE ATTRACTIVE XX RESONANCE
Internal mathematical candidate, 2026-10-08. No external validation or
priority claim. Owner: /root/sol_kls_consequences; no new workers.

1. EXACT MODEL AND THEOREM

Fix J,tau>0, B>=0 and g_->0. For integers L>=2, write S=L-1 and use
the spin/occupation Hilbert space on the sites {1,...,S}. Let

  Npart=sum_i n_i,
  W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1} n_i n_{i+1},
  Hcrit=J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-sigma_i.sigma_{i+1})
          +J(I-sigma_1^z)+J(I-sigma_S^z).

For S=1 the two endpoint terms both act on that one site. In the sector
with m particles, identify an orthonormal basis with the m-element subsets
X of {1,...,S}. Two subsets are adjacent when a particle makes one permitted
nearest-neighbor hop into a vacancy. The exact quadratic form is

  <psi,Hcrit psi>
    =2J sum_{unordered X~X'} |psi(X)-psi(X')|^2
      +2J sum_X (1_{1 in X}+1_{S in X}) |psi(X)|^2.       (1.1)

The convention is the resonant attractive XX model from the continuum lead:
the original nearest-neighbor coupling is V_L=-4J+g/L, and the physical
chemical potential is lambda/L^2. Its Hamiltonian is

  H_L(lambda,g)=Hcrit+(g/L)W-(lambda/L^2)Npart.

Define the sector partition function at beta_L=tau L^2 by

  Z_{L,m}(lambda,g)
    =Tr_m exp[-tau L^2(Hcrit+(g/L)W)+tau lambda m].

It is zero for m>S and one for m=0. No vacuum-energy subtraction is needed
for the following estimate: the particle vacuum has exactly zero energy.

THEOREM. Uniformly in L>=2, m>=0, |lambda|<=B and g>=g_->0,

  Z_{L,m}(lambda,g) <= A^m exp(-tau c m^2),               (1.2)

where the explicit admissible constants are

  c=min{g_-/96,J/72},
  C=1+2 sum_{k>=1} exp(-4J tau k^2),
  A=e C exp[tau(B+c)].                                  (1.3)

The result is stronger than uniformity on a compact positive g interval:
no upper bound on g is used. The constants are deliberately conservative.

The proof has two independent parts. Local contact coercivity leaves half
of Hcrit available, and an elementary occupation-moment comparison gives
an exponential-in-m heat-trace bound for that retained kinetic operator.
Neither a fixed-m continuum theorem nor an independent-particle assumption
is used.

2. A DISCRETE ROBIN INTERVAL INEQUALITY

Let u=(u_1,...,u_r) be complex, r<=ell, and let e be either endpoint of
{1,...,r}. Set D(u)=sum_{j=1}^{r-1}|u_{j+1}-u_j|^2. Telescoping from e and
Cauchy-Schwarz give

  ||u||^2 <= 2r |u_e|^2+r(r-1)D(u)
           <= 2ell |u_e|^2+ell^2 D(u).                  (2.1)

Indeed |u_i|^2<=2|u_e|^2+2|i-e|D(u); summing |i-e| over the interval is
r(r-1)/2. For b,q>0, (2.1) implies

  q D(u)+b|u_e|^2
     >= min{b/(4ell),q/(2ell^2)} ||u||^2.                (2.2)

To check constants, multiply the right side of (2.1) by that minimum:
the endpoint coefficient is at most b/2 and the gradient coefficient at
most q/2. Thus even twice the bound in (2.2) would be valid. The weakened
constant is retained to keep subsequent estimates transparent. The r=1
case is included and has D(u)=0.

3. LOCAL EXCLUSION BY CONDITIONING ORDERED PARTICLES

Consider an interval of ell lattice sites with reflecting boundaries.
Let H^Neu_{ell,n} be 2J times its n-particle exclusion graph Laplacian,
and let W_ell count its occupied adjacent pairs. For 0<alpha<1 and a>0,

  (1-alpha)H^Neu_{ell,n}+a W_ell
    >= n min{a/(8ell),J(1-alpha)/ell^2} I,  n>=2.         (3.1)

For n=0 or 1 the left side is simply nonnegative.

Proof. Write a configuration as x_1<...<x_n. For a fixed index i, fix all
ordered positions other than x_i. The possible values of x_i form an
integer interval I_i between the nearest fixed neighboring particles,
or between a particle and the reflecting box boundary. This interval has
length at most ell. If n>=2, at least one of its two ends is immediately
adjacent to a fixed particle. Put a potential a/2 at each such contact end.
When both contact ends coincide, both contributions are present; dropping
one of them is sufficient for the lower bound.

The conditional kinetic form is

  2J(1-alpha) sum_{x,x+1 in I_i}|psi(...,x+1,...)-psi(...,x,...)|^2.

Apply (2.2) with q=2J(1-alpha), b=a/2 and retain one contact endpoint.
The conditional form is at least

  min{a/(8ell),J(1-alpha)/ell^2}
       sum_{x in I_i}|psi(...,x,...)|^2.

Sum over all feasible fixed positions of the other particles and then
over i=1,...,n. Every allowed hop changes exactly one ordered coordinate,
so its kinetic edge is counted exactly once. Every occupied adjacent pair
is a contact endpoint for both of its particles, so the two a/2 weights
sum to a. For each i, the conditional squared norms sum to ||psi||^2.
This proves (3.1) for arbitrary complex psi. In particular the argument
does not require factorization, stochastic ordering, or a bosonic limit.

4. FINITE-L COERCIVITY WITH A RETAINED KINETIC FRACTION

For m>=2 put b=floor(m/2). Partition the S sites into b consecutive boxes
of lengths at most ell_max=ceil(S/b). Since S>=m and b>=m/3,

  ell_max<=2S/b<=6L/m.                                  (4.1)

Write a=g/L. Retain alpha Hcrit in the full chain. In the remaining
(1-alpha)Hcrit+aW, discard the positive cross-box exchange bonds, positive
cross-box contacts, and endpoint killing. The resulting operator is the
sum of the local forms in (3.1), block diagonal with respect to the box
particle counts n_1,...,n_b, sum_j n_j=m. All discarded terms are positive
as quadratic forms; this is a variational comparison, not an assumption
that particles fail to cross the boxes.

At most b particles belong to boxes containing exactly one particle.
Consequently sum_{j:n_j>=2} n_j >=m-b>=m/2. By (4.1),

  min{a/(8ell_j),J(1-alpha)/ell_j^2}
   >= min{g_- m/(48L^2),J(1-alpha)m^2/(36L^2)}
   >= min{g_-/48,J(1-alpha)/18} m/L^2,                   (4.2)

where m>=2 was used in the second inequality. Therefore, in every m-sector,

  Hcrit+(g/L)W
    >= alpha Hcrit+c_alpha m^2/L^2 I,  m>=2,             (4.3)
  c_alpha=min{g_-/96,J(1-alpha)/36}.

Choosing alpha=1/2 gives precisely c in (1.3). The partition of sites may
depend on the fixed sector m: (4.3) is a sectorwise form inequality and
does not prescribe a physical channel or a parameter-dependent decoder.

5. AN ELEMENTARY EXCLUSION HEAT-TRACE BOUND

Let H^Neu_{S,m} be the exclusion graph Laplacian in (1.1) without endpoint
killing. Its Markov generator is L_m=-H^Neu_{S,m}: every allowed exchange
on an edge of the path occurs at rate 2J. Let p_t(i,j) be the one-particle
reflecting path transition kernel. Starting the process in an m-set X,
let eta_i(t) denote site occupation and put

  u_i(t)=E_X eta_i(t)=sum_{j in X}p_t(i,j).

For an arbitrary k-set Y define

  v_t(Y)=E_X prod_{i in Y}eta_i(t),
  F_t(Y)=prod_{i in Y}u_i(t).

Directly apply the exchange generator to the occupation monomial. An edge
with both ends in Y leaves the monomial unchanged; an edge with exactly
one end in Y replaces that selected site by the other endpoint. Therefore

  partial_t v_t=L_k v_t,
  v_0(Y)=prod_{i in Y}1_{i in X}=F_0(Y).                (5.1)

No quoted duality theorem is required. The one-point functions solve the
single-particle heat equation. Applying their product rule gives

 (partial_t-L_k)F_t(Y)
   =2J sum_{path edges {i,j} subset Y}
       (u_i(t)-u_j(t))^2 prod_{l in Y\{i,j}}u_l(t) >=0. (5.2)

The external-edge terms cancel the L_k terms; for an internal edge the
two time derivatives give (u_j-u_i)u_j+(u_i-u_j)u_i=(u_i-u_j)^2.
All one-point functions are nonnegative. Positivity of the finite Markov
semigroup and Duhamel's formula, applied to F_t-v_t with initial zero,
give v_t(Y)<=F_t(Y).

Take k=m and Y=X. Since particle number is exactly m, the event that all
sites of X are occupied is precisely the event that the full configuration
equals X. Hence

  [exp(-t H^Neu_{S,m})]_{X,X}
      <=prod_{i in X}sum_{j in X}p_t(i,j).               (5.3)

The reflecting path one-particle eigenvalues are

  nu_k=4J(1-cos(pi k/S)), k=0,...,S-1.

The normalized nonconstant cosine eigenfunctions are bounded in absolute
value by sqrt(2/S). Using 1-cos(pi x)>=2x^2 for 0<=x<=1 gives

  p_t(i,j)<= [1+2 sum_{k>=1} exp(-8Jt k^2/S^2)]/S.

At t=alpha tau L^2, L/S>=1, this is at most C_alpha/S, where

  C_alpha=1+2 sum_{k>=1}exp(-8J alpha tau k^2).            (5.4)

The S=1 kernel is identically one and satisfies the same bound. Sum (5.3)
over all m-sets. Using binom(S,m)<=S^m/m! and m!>=(m/e)^m,

  Tr_m exp(-alpha tau L^2 H^Neu_{S,m})
     <=binom(S,m)(C_alpha m/S)^m <=(e C_alpha)^m.         (5.5)

Since Hcrit>=H^Neu as forms, eigenvalue min-max gives

  Tr_m exp(-alpha tau L^2 Hcrit)<=(e C_alpha)^m.          (5.6)

This last use of a form inequality is through eigenvalue ordering.
It does not use the false assertion that matrix exponentiation is operator
monotone. The bound is crude near m=S, but uniform in both m and S.

6. COMBINING THE TWO ESTIMATES

For m>=2, min-max applied to (4.3), then (5.6), yields

 Z_{L,m}(lambda,g)
   <=exp[tau Bm-tau c_alpha m^2]
       Tr_m exp(-alpha tau L^2 Hcrit)
   <=(e C_alpha exp(tau B))^m exp(-tau c_alpha m^2).      (6.1)

For m=1, W=0 and Hcrit is positive; the same heat-trace estimate at time
alpha tau L^2 bounds its trace at the larger time tau L^2. Thus
Z_{L,1}<=e C_alpha exp(tau B). With

  A_alpha=e C_alpha exp[tau(B+c_alpha)],

the right side of A_alpha^m exp(-tau c_alpha m^2) covers m=1, m=0, and
the already stronger bound (6.1) for m>=2. Set alpha=1/2. Equations
(1.2)-(1.3) follow.

7. CONSEQUENCES AND THE EXACT REMAINING CONTINUUM GATE

The full grand partition function obeys

  1<=Z_L(lambda,g)<=sum_{m>=0} A^m exp(-tau c m^2)<infinity,

uniformly in L and all stated parameters. The normalized Gibbs probability
of Npart>=r obeys the explicit, dimension-independent estimate

  Pr_L(Npart>=r)<=sum_{m>=r} A^m exp(-tau c m^2).         (7.1)

For a directly computable simplified bound let a=tau c, b0=log A. If
r>=max{1,2b0/a}, then

  Pr_L(Npart>=r)
      <=exp(-a r^2/2)/(1-exp(-a r/2)).                   (7.2)

Indeed b0 m-a m^2<=-a m^2/2 for m>=r, and
(r+j)^2>=r^2+rj. Every exponential particle-number moment is likewise
uniformly bounded by sum_m exp[(b0+s)m-a m^2].

A number-only quantum cutoff with projection P_r onto m<r, followed by
the identity on that subspace and a fixed overflow state, has decoded
trace error at most 2 Pr_L(Npart>=r): the exact Gibbs state commutes with
Npart. For arbitrary coherent inputs the standard projection channel has
an error bounded by 2 sqrt(p)+p, where p=Tr((I-P_r)rho). This is only a
number cutoff: its retained Hilbert-space dimension is
sum_{m<r}binom(S,m), still dependent on L. It is not yet the sought finite
continuum/archive theorem.

To conclude a whole interacting continuum Gibbs experiment one still
needs a common embedding and fixed-m convergence of the heat semigroups
in trace norm, uniformly on compact lambda and g intervals. A sufficiently
strong fixed-sector compactness/Mosco-plus-trace argument could supply that
without controlling each free sine-mode occupation individually. Strong
resolvent convergence alone is insufficient, because spectral weight can
escape through increasingly many states. The present estimate removes the
unbounded m sum after such a fixed-sector result, but does not by itself
prove that result, identify the precise Lieb-Liniger boundary/contact
normalization, or construct an L-independent spatial-mode archive.

No Shale criterion, fermionic quasi-free representation, or Gaussian
particle-number generating function was used: this is an interacting,
number-conserving finite-chain inequality.

8. COSTS, BASELINES, AND FALSIFIERS

Supplied inputs are the exact local Hamiltonian convention, J,tau,B and a
certified positive lower g_-. The proof does not acquire lambda or g.
The series C can be outward bounded by finite Gaussian summation plus an
integral tail; fixed public constants and error epsilon give a cutoff
r=O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))) after the constant shift log A/(tau c). The
number cutoff can be implemented by coherent population counting, but
its dimensional cost still scales with L. Preparing the input Gibbs state,
an L-independent decoder, and a local interacting continuum simulator are
not supplied by this theorem.

The strongest equally informed baseline before this result was the exact
SSEP identity plus W>=max{0,2m-L}, giving a binomial factor and suppressing
only sectors above roughly half filling. Fixed-m Lieb-Liniger energy
coercivity did not imply a uniform finite-L trace bound. Here both the
finite-L retained-kinetic estimate and the trace multiplicity are charged.

Direct falsifiers are an allowed interval/particle sector violating (3.1),
a finite-chain eigenvalue violating (4.3), an occupation-moment comparison
violating (5.2)-(5.3), or a sector partition function exceeding (1.2),
within the exact stated convention. The positive lower coupling and
compact upper chemical bound are essential hypotheses of this proof;
no assertion is made about their necessity in every alternate model.
