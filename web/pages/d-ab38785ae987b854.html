TRANSFER THEOREM (FIXED LOCAL SPIN s)

Let s be any fixed positive half-integer, with local irreducible generators
S=(S_1,S_2,S_3), dimension 2s+1, and J=sum of the N site generators. Define

  v_s=s(s+1)/3,  a_s=1/(2v_s)=3/[2s(s+1)],  q=N^(3/4).

For fixed M,B>=0 and real symmetric ||A||<=M, |b|<=B, set

  K_s=a_s J^2/N + Q_A(J)/N^(3/2) + b.J/q.

For all sufficiently large N, the normalized exp(K_s) is exponentially
close in trace distance to a known-parameter positive mixture of identical
local spin-s thermal products tau_y^tensor N, where

  tau_y=exp(y.S)/chi_s(|y|),  chi_s(r)=Tr exp(r S_3).

The construction is a stopped diffusion in the natural parameter y, with
positive Feynman--Kac weights. It proves mathematical full separability. It
does not give a finite-bit sampler, complexity bound, compiler, or hardware
preparation. For spin 1 (a qutrit), a_1=3/4, v_1=2/3, and valid character
constants are c_1=1/240, C_1=1/9. The critical quadratic coefficient follows
from the local character's variance; no novelty claim is made.

1. KERNEL COORDINATES AND EXACT DIFFERENTIAL IDENTITIES

Write r=|y|, u=n.y for a unit n, and define

  phi_s(r)=chi_s(r)/(2s+1),  m_s(r)=d log chi_s(r)/dr,
  g_s(r)=2m_s(r)/r, with g_s(0)=2v_s,
  V_n(y)=2(u/r^2)y + r coth(r/2)[n-(u/r^2)y],
  R_n(y)=n cross y.

The apparent singularities at zero are removable. In the fundamental
two-dimensional representation, symmetric filtering sends

  exp(y.sigma/2) -> exp(alpha n.sigma/2)
                         exp(y.sigma/2) exp(alpha n.sigma/2).

This is positive Hermitian with determinant one. Differentiating its unique
logarithm at alpha=0 gives dy/dalpha=V_n(y), and its log character derivative
is g_s(r)u. Since the spin-s representation is the corresponding
representation of SL(2,C), the same flow holds exactly for every s. Thus,
for k_N(y)=tau_y^tensor N and

  B_n=N g_s(r)u + V_n(y).grad_y,   R_n=R_n(y).grad_y,

  {J_n,k_N}=B_n k_N,     [J_n,k_N]=i R_n k_N,
  {J_n^2,k_N}/2=(B_n^2-R_n^2)k_N/4.                    (T1)

The last equality follows by writing left and right multiplication as L,R:
the anticommutator square is ((L+R)^2+(L-R)^2)/4, while [J_n,k]=iR_n k.
It is an exact identity, not a saddle-point approximation.

2. POSITIVE HEAT SEED AND SANDWICH GENERATOR

The compact SU(2) heat identity, on every finite representation, is

  exp(t J^2)=int exp(y.J) nu_t(dy),
  nu_t(dy)=(4 pi t)^(-3/2) exp(-t/4-r^2/(4t))
          sinh(r/2)/(r/2) d^3y.

Since exp(y.J)=chi_s(r)^N k_N(y), this is a positive mixture of identical
spin-s products. Put Lambda=M+1, C=A+Lambda I, Lc=2M+1, Tc=Tr C<=3Lc,

  t0=a_s/N-Lambda/N^(3/2),
  H=Q_C(J)/q^2+b.J/q,       K_s=t0 J^2+H.

Then I<=C<=Lc I. Let C=sum_i lambda_i n_i n_i^T. The positive quantum
generator M_H(X)={H,X}/2 has, on k_N, the scalar differential expression

  G=sum_i lambda_i (B_i^2-R_i^2)/(4q^2)+B_b/(2q)
   =V_N(y)+a_N(y).grad + D_C(y):Hess/(4q^2),             (T2)

where B_b=N g_s(r)(b.y)+V_b.grad and V_b=sum_i b_i V_i. If
beta_i=N g_s(r)(n_i.y), then

  D_C=sum_i lambda_i(V_i V_i^T-R_i R_i^T),
  a_N=sum_i lambda_i[2 beta_i V_i+(V_i.grad)V_i
                            -(R_i.grad)R_i]/(4q^2)+V_b/(2q),
  V_N=sum_i lambda_i[beta_i^2+V_i.grad beta_i]/(4q^2)
                         +N g_s(r)(b.y)/(2q).

The potential is exactly Tr(H k_N(y)); therefore |V_N|<=||H||. The sector
bound j<=Ns gives the explicit global cost

  h_N=||H||<=Lc Ns(Ns+1)/q^2+B Ns/q
       =Lc(s^2 sqrt(N)+s/sqrt(N))+Bs N^(1/4).            (T3)

Since [H,J^2]=0, exp(M_H) exp(t0 J^2)=exp(K_s).

3. ELLIPTICITY, STOPPING, AND A FINITE ERROR CERTIFICATE

For 0<=r<=1, h(r)=r coth(r/2) satisfies 2<=h(r)<=2+r^2/6. Hence
|V_n-2n|<=r^2/6<=r^2/3 and |V_n|<=7/3. Also |R_n|<=r. The identity

  D_C=(I-yy^T)C(I-yy^T)-[y]_cross C[y]_cross^T

and C>=I,C<=Lc I imply

  D_C >= [4-(22/9)Tc r^2]I,       D_C <= (49/9)Tc I.    (T4)

Set r0^2=1/[16(Lc+1)]. Then D_C>3I throughout |y|<=r0.
For an explicit drift constant, the partial-fraction expansion

  x coth x=1+2x^2 sum_(k>=1)(x^2+pi^2 k^2)^(-1)

gives ||DV_n||<=121/180 on |y|<=1. Also |g_s(r)|<=2s^2 because
|m_s'(r)| is the variance of S_3 under a local thermal state, at most s^2.
Thus the drift on the stopped ball is bounded by

  beta_N=Tc[ (7/3)(2s^2) r0 N^(-1/2)
                 +(847/540+r0)/(4N^(3/2)) ]
          +(7/3)B/(2q).                                  (T5)

Choose N large enough that beta_N<=r0/8. Solve the SDE until its first hit
tau of |y|=r0:

  dy=a_N(y)dt+sqrt(D_C(y)/(2q^2))dW,
  w_t=exp(int_0^(t wedge tau) V_N(y_u)du).

The process stays on the closed elliptic ball until tau and then freezes.
Every path contributes a positive product w_t k_N(y_(t wedge tau)). Since
the covariance is D_C/(2q^2), each coordinate martingale has quadratic
variation at most 49Tc/(18q^2). Starting at |y_0|<=r0/2, an exit by time one
requires a coordinate martingale to exceed 3r0/(8 sqrt(3)) in absolute
value. The exponential-martingale maximal inequality and a union bound give

  P(tau<=1)<=6 exp[-9 r0^2 q^2/(3136 Lc)]
             =6 exp(-kappa1 N^(3/2)),
  kappa1=9r0^2/(3136Lc).                                  (T6)

For the heat seed, let

  delta_s(r)=v_s r^2/2-log(phi_s(r)),
  c_s=inf_(r>0) delta_s(r)/min(r^4,r^2)>0,
  C_s=sup_(0<r<=1) delta_s(r)/r^4<infinity.

For fixed s these are positive finite character constants. On choosing
sqrt(N)>=2Lambda/a_s, the coefficient of r^2 left after combining the heat
Gaussian and character is nonpositive. The radial heat density is therefore
bounded above by r^2 sinhc(r/2) exp[-N delta_s(r)]. Its normalizer is at
least

  L_s,Lambda N^(-3/4),
  L_s,Lambda=(7/3) exp[-16C_s-8v_s^2 Lambda],

by restricting R=N^(1/4)r to [1,2], provided N>=16. For a0=r0/2, use
sinhc(r/2)<=exp(r^2/24). On [a0,1], delta>=c_s r^4; on [1,infinity),
delta>=c_s r^2. For N>=1/(2c_s), a direct split of those integrals yields

  p_bad=P_heat(r>a0)<=C_tail N^(3/4) exp(-kappa0 N),
  kappa0=min(c_s a0^4,c_s/4),
  C_tail=L_s,Lambda^(-1)[(exp(1/24)/3)
                              +sqrt(pi)/(4)(4/c_s)^(3/2)].

The constants above are deliberately coarse; the finite expressions are
explicit in the one-dimensional character bounds. The spin-1 choices
c_1=1/240 and C_1=1/9 are elementary. Replace every bad seed branch by
y=0; the normalized seed changes in trace norm by at most 2p_bad.

The stopped Feynman--Kac expectation W_t satisfies
W_t'=E[1_(t<tau) w_t M_H k_N(y_t)], whereas
M_H W_t=E[w_t M_H k_N(y_(t wedge tau))]. Duhamel therefore gives the exact
stopped-hit residual. With |V_N|<=h_N and ||M_H k_N||_1<=h_N,

  ||exp(M_H)rho0-W_1||_1
    <=2 exp(h_N) p_bad+6h_N exp(2h_N-kappa1 N^(3/2)).   (T7)

The target trace is at least one after scaling by Z0=Tr exp(t0J^2): in each
spin-j sector, Tr H_j/d_j=Tc j(j+1)/(3q^2)>=0, so scalar trace Jensen gives
Tr exp(H_j)>=d_j. For positive G,W,
T(G/TrG,W/TrW)<=||G-W||_1/TrG. Combining this with (T7) yields

  T(rho_s,N,sigma_s,N)
    <=2C_tail N^(3/4)exp(h_N-kappa0N)
       +6h_N exp(2h_N-kappa1N^(3/2)).                    (T8)

Since h_N=O_s,M,B(sqrt(N)), (T8) is Ce^(-cN) for all sufficiently
large N. All constants depend only on fixed s,M,B. The qutrit specialization
uses s=1 and the explicit c_1,C_1 above.

4. EVIDENCE, LIMITS, AND ATTRIBUTION

The algebraic derivation above is exact conditional on the standard finite-
dimensional heat identity, the ordinary stopped Ito formula, and the stated
character inequalities. A separate finite-difference qutrit diagnostic is
provided in spin1_stopped_identity_check.py; it checks small fixtures only
and is not evidence for the all-N theorem. The proof is mathematical, not a
sampler/compiler result. The SDE law uses known A,b; it does not acquire them.

The broad prior-art boundary is substantial. The original stopped qubit
mechanism is attributed to c07_s02, and the SU(2) heat kernel is standard
representation theory. Hall, “The Segal-Bargmann ‘Coherent State’ Transform
for Compact Lie Groups,” JFA 122 (1994), DOI 10.1006/jfan.1994.1064, is a
primary framework reference. Fannes–Kossakowski–Verbeure, “Critical
fluctuations for quantum mean-field models,” J Stat Phys 65 (1991), DOI
10.1007/BF01053756, reports critical Ginzburg–Landau/spin-fluctuation work
for some spin-1/2 models; only its publisher abstract was inspected here.
Novelty, full overlap, and priority remain UNKNOWN pending specialist review.

No claim is made for growing s,M,B, general SU(d), local non-mean-field
Hamiltonians, noncritical coefficients, a lower bound, finite-bit sampling,
polynomial preparation cost, or hardware. The fixed-spin transfer here is a
derived candidate that needs independent specialist review.
