# The exponent-two gate: matched embeddings and a norm obstruction

2026-10-08 UTC. A scoped continuation of ALGEBRAIC_LOGARITHM_CANDIDATE.md. These are research derivations, with the same moving-center interpolation dependency. No novelty claim is made.

## 1. A precise extension of the norm ledger

Let lambda be a nonzero real number, let c != 0 and alpha != 0,1 be algebraic numbers, and suppose exp(c lambda)=alpha. For the determinant extension in this section also assume alpha is not a root of unity. Put F=Q(alpha,c), d=[F:Q], and let S be the set of embeddings sigma:F->C for which

    sigma(alpha)=exp(sigma(c) lambda).

Write r=|S|, so r>=1. The same determinant construction, with centers (alpha^j, j c p_i/q_i), suggests the conditional bound

    mu(lambda)<=2d/r.

This is the ordinary rational approximation exponent of the real lambda. It is not a number-field approximation exponent.

Reason: choose positive integers b0,b1 making b0 alpha and b1 c algebraic integers. The previous column scaling acquires b1^|gamma|, with the matching b1 row benefit optional. Its normalized arithmetic cost is O(log b1/w_min), which can be made small. Each unmatched embedding has the same entry-growth estimate, with |sigma(c)|(|lambda|+1) replacing the bound on the rational centers. At each matched embedding use omega_sigma=sigma(c)lambda, so the same rational approximants satisfy

    |sigma(c)p_i/q_i-omega_sigma|<=|sigma(c)|q_i^(-nu).

The constants |sigma(c)| and |omega_sigma| are fixed before the dimension choice. Thus every matched determinant has the original translated-row/collision upper bound, with the same bbar, L, and nu. The norm gives

    0 <= d(1-bbar) + r max[-c0 L, -nu(A(1-eta)-bbar)] + small errors,

where c0=(log 2)/4. Choose the original parameters with effective arithmetic cost d/r in place of d. This closes whenever nu>2d/r. Nonzero minors stay nonzero under every embedding.

If alpha has finite multiplicative order, its powers are not distinct, so the moving-center theorem with distinct Y coordinates cannot simply be used. One may replace it by the original common-fiber interpolation at a fixed power, provided the chosen c and logarithm branch give nonzero coordinatewise-distinct additive centers. The generic positive-real alpha!=1 case and the norm-one quadratic examples below are nontorsion. No torsion case is needed for the generic 2d theorem.

## 2. Exact upper bound on the number of matched embeddings

There is an important restriction: for F generated by alpha and c as above, r<=2.

Proof. For a matched sigma, put rho=sigma(c)/c. It is algebraic, and

    sigma(alpha)=exp(rho c lambda)

is a value of alpha^rho, using the logarithm c lambda of alpha. If rho were algebraic irrational, the Gelfond–Schneider theorem (valid for every value of the power) would make this transcendental, contradiction. Thus rho is rational.

Extend sigma to an automorphism g of a finite normal closure of F. Since g fixes the rational rho and g(c)=rho c, finite order n gives c=g^n(c)=rho^n c. Since c!=0 and rho rational, rho=1 or -1.

If rho=1, matchedness says sigma(alpha)=alpha and sigma(c)=c, so sigma is the identity on F. If rho=-1, it says sigma(alpha)=alpha^(-1) and sigma(c)=-c. At most one embedding of F has these values on its two generators. Hence r<=2.

Consequently the matched-embedding mechanism does not make the number of useful Archimedean places grow with the field degree. The endpoint 2 from this mechanism occurs only when d<=2 (apart from choosing an unnecessarily larger coefficient field, which cannot improve the ratio).

Examples already anticipated by current unverified discussion, not claimed as new:

- alpha on the unit circle in an imaginary quadratic field, c a nonzero purely imaginary element of that field, and lambda=log(alpha)/c real with a chosen branch. Complex conjugation gives the inverse pair, so d=r=2.
- A positive real quadratic unit alpha with conjugate alpha^(-1), c=sqrt(D), and lambda=log(alpha)/sqrt(D). The quadratic involution gives the inverse pair, so d=r=2.

For c=1 and general positive algebraic alpha, only the identity can be matched: another matched embedding would fix alpha, hence fix Q(alpha). Thus this cannot lower the generic logarithm bound below 2d.

## 3. A direct falsification of the tempting norm improvement

To reach mu(log alpha)=2 in the generic real-algebraic case using the existing analytic comparison, one would want an arithmetic lower bound

    log|Delta_H|/(MH) >= -(1-bbar)-small errors,

in place of the proved -d(1-bbar) bound. A general claim that clearing a rational denominator Q and keeping all other conjugates bounded suffices for |Delta|>=Q^(-1-o(1)) is false.

Explicit counterexample in F=Q(sqrt(2)): take positive integers p_n,q_n defined by

    (1+sqrt(2))^n=p_n+q_n sqrt(2),
    Delta_n=p_n/q_n-sqrt(2).

Then Q_n=q_n clears the denominator, z_n=p_n-q_n sqrt(2) is a nonzero algebraic integer, and

    |N(z_n)|=1,
    |sigma(Delta_n)|=p_n/q_n+sqrt(2) ->2sqrt(2),
    |Delta_n|=1/(q_n^2(p_n/q_n+sqrt(2))).

Thus the principal value is asymptotic to a constant times Q_n^(-2), although the other conjugate is uniformly bounded. The norm lower bound is exactly sharp at exponent d=2. This does not refute the logarithm target or any lower bound exploiting additional special structure of the logarithm-jet determinant. It refutes eliminating the field-degree cost using only integrality plus bounded other embeddings, which is all the current generic ledger has.

Multiplying by field units cannot repair this general bound: for a unit u the sum of log|sigma(u)| over all embeddings is zero. Any reduction of the other-embedding product is paid by growth at the distinguished embedding (counting complex embeddings with their multiplicities). The same compensation appears when the analytic determinant is multiplied by u.

## 4. Why naive conjugate-vanishing does not solve it

Suppose a proposed rational Siegel-lemma construction imposes exact vanishing of a rational-coefficient polynomial's jets at algebraic conjugate centers while hoping to preserve the principal value. The jet coefficients are algebraic and conjugate-equivariant. A zero at one entire conjugate packet is carried by Galois to the corresponding principal packet. More simply, for any algebraic determinant Delta, sigma(Delta)=0 implies Delta=0. Exact annihilation of the unwanted conjugate determinants therefore annihilates the principal determinant as well.

Approximate smallness is not ruled out by this argument, but then its coefficient-height/denominator cost must be included. A potential sufficient lemma would construct a nonzero admissible logarithm-jet determinant with

    sum_(sigma != id) log|sigma(Delta_H)|
       <= -(d-1)(1-bbar) MH + o(MH),

while retaining the same clearing cost, principal analytic comparison and parameter independence. This would cancel the extra norm cost and recover the degree-one contradiction for every nu>2. No such construction is supplied. The Pell example shows it cannot follow from denominator size, rank, or a general norm estimate alone.

## 5. Stop decision

The current generic method stops at 2d. The matched-embedding variant is precise and useful for the known-looking quadratic cases, but Gelfond–Schneider limits it to at most two embeddings. Neither multiplying by units nor forcing exact conjugate zeros removes the obstacle. Approximate conjugate suppression at unchanged arithmetic cost is the single explicit remaining lemma. No credible proof of it emerged in this pass; it should not be relabeled as an established route to the general exponent-two problem.
