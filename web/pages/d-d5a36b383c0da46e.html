1. SHARP SPECTRAL ENVELOPE: EXPECTED POLYNOMIAL REJECTION

Let g be any supplied invertible local filter and E=g*g. Its eigenvalues are
a_1>=...>=a_d>0. Define
  M_lambda=product_{i=1}^d a_i^{lambda_i},
  t_lambda(U)=product_columns det[(U*EU)_{1:h_c,1:h_c}].
For each height h, the determinant is the expectation of the positive operator
wedge^h E on the unit vector Ue_1 wedge ... wedge Ue_h. Its operator norm is
product_{i<=h} a_i. Multiplying over columns gives the exact envelope
  0<t_lambda(U)<=M_lambda.

The Haar mean from the main proof is
  A_lambda=chi_lambda(E)/D_lambda.
The polynomial GL(d) representation of E is positive Hermitian. Its largest
eigenvalue is M_lambda: write log E in a dominant diagonal Cartan chamber;
the highest weight lambda maximizes the weight pairing. This eigenvalue occurs
in the character trace, with positive multiplicity, so
  M_lambda<=chi_lambda(E)<=D_lambda M_lambda.
Consequently rejection from Haar U with acceptance t_lambda(U)/M_lambda has
  alpha_lambda=A_lambda/M_lambda in [1/D_lambda,1],
and expected trials at most D_lambda, INDEPENDENT of cond(g) and field size.

For fixed d,
  D_lambda=product_{i<j}(lambda_i-lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i)
            <=(N+d)^{d(d-1)/2}/product_{i<j}(j-i).
Each proposal needs only d by d operations, at most d different Gram
determinants, and integer powers from the column multiplicities
m_h=lambda_h-lambda_{h+1}. Emit the normalized product of the filtered
column wedges and independently assign a uniform site permutation.
No Schur-transform circuit and no exponentially large full state vector are
needed for this ensemble construction.

This proves an expected polynomial proposal count for fixed d in an exact
real-arithmetic interface. It is a strict improvement over the valid but
unnecessarily coarse ||g||^(2N) rejection envelope in the preserved proof.
The old exponential upper bound must not be read as an impossibility result.

2. A FINITE-PRECISION STABILITY LEMMA

This additional lemma is useful for c01_s01's separately assigned finite-bit
compiler; it is not a claim that a complete compiler ran here.

Define a scaled unnormalized accepted vector
  z(U)=g_N U_N v_lambda/sqrt(M_lambda), ||z(U)||<=1,
and accepted kernel F(U)=|z(U)><z(U)|. Its Haar average, after the independent
permutation twirl, has trace alpha_lambda>=1/D_lambda. Normalizing it gives
the exact filtered block.

If U' is unitary and ||U'-U||_op<=a, both vectors before filtering remain in
the lambda block. On that block ||g_N||=sqrt(M_lambda). The tensor telescoping
bound gives
  ||z(U')-z(U)||<=N a,
  ||F(U')-F(U)||_1<=2N a.
An arbitrary coupling of Haar U to such U' therefore gives accepted-average
trace-norm error <=2N a. Normalization obeys
  ||rho'-rho||_1<=2 ||K'-K||_1/Tr K,
so the emitted density trace distance is <=2D_lambda N a. Its approximate
acceptance probability is >=1/D_lambda-2N a, hence >=1/(2D_lambda) when
a<=1/(4D_lambda N). This error estimate contains no condition-number factor.

There is also a stable per-column form. Let s_i(g)=sqrt(a_i), sorted, and
  S_h=(wedge^h g)/product_{i<=h}s_i(g), ||S_h||=1.
Then z(U) is exactly the tensor product of the column vectors
S_h (Ue_1 wedge ... wedge Ue_h). If each such scaled column operation/vector
is approximated by a contraction to norm error a, tensor telescoping again
gives vector error <=N a and accepted-kernel error <=2N a. Combining this
with the common-unitary rounding gives a conservative emitted trace-distance
bound <=4D_lambda N a. Absolute approximation of contractions is the relevant
interface; estimating a very small cluster norm relatively is unnecessary
for the average-state bound. Zero-norm proposed approximate vectors may be
rejected. All emitted nonzero terms still factor into the same <=d clusters.

An absolute Bernoulli-acceptance error e per proposal alters the accepted
kernel by at most e in trace norm, giving an additional O(D_lambda e) state
error after normalization. Mixture weight total-variation error is charged
directly. Thus a local accuracy O(epsilon/[D_lambda N]), acceptance accuracy
O(epsilon/D_lambda), and controlled block-weight errors suffice; exponentially
small local accuracy in N is not forced by common-filter conditioning.

This lemma does not prove the running time of acquiring those approximations.
Distinguish a supplied rational filter g, supplied contraction matrices S_h,
and a rational B from which g=exp(B/2) must be acquired. Forming the raw g can
require exponentially many output bits when ||B|| is exponentially large in
its binary input length. A compiler can potentially work with the bounded
S_h or their shifted exponentials instead, but must prove and charge that
matrix-function acquisition, Haar rounding, Bernoulli generation, state
preparation gates, and mixture-weight precision. Root assigned that full
algorithmic interface separately to c01_s01; no such run is represented by
the exact algebra checks attached here.
