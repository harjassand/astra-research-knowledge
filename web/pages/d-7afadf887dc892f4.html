# Exact recognition for reversible rational enzyme modules

## Result

The paired-reversible module interface is recognized in polynomial time in
its **factorized input length**, even though its ordinary positive-denominator
lift can contain exponentially many reactions. Each module may have a
different, nonsingular denominator and its own positive numerator factor
shared by the forward/reverse channels. The algorithm returns an exact
certificate of ordinary essential-source endotacticity. It also certifies
standard strong endotacticity in the schema's rank-one case; at higher
stoichiometric rank its strong answer is `UNKNOWN` unless another proof is
supplied.

This is a distinct structure from the shared-denominator FPT result in
`child_complexity/REPORT.md`: here denominator supports may differ by module,
but each enzyme reaction's forward and reverse channels share their own
denominator and positive numerator modifier, and are paired at the same two
endpoint monomials.

## Input and exact algorithm

The accepted JSON tag is `paired-reversible-rational-modules-v1`. It
requires a positive integer dimension and a nonempty modules list. Source and
target are sparse zero-based coordinate-to-rational maps in that dimension;
both are nonnegative and must differ, so self-loops are INVALID_INPUT.
Directional constants and all listed term coefficients are strictly
positive rationals, encoded as JSON integers or exact strings in integer,
integer-ratio, or finite-decimal form without scientific exponents. JSON
floating-point values and exponent-form strings are rejected, keeping the
coefficient's binary bit length charged by its explicit digits. Every
polynomial factor is an explicit nonempty list of terms; each term has a
sparse nonnegative-rational exponent and an optional
coefficient (default 1). The denominator-factors list is nonempty, and every
denominator factor must contain a positive constant term. The
shared-numerator-factors list is optional (empty means 1); its factors need
not contain constants but are positive on the positive orthant. Boolean
values are not rationals. No product-support or arithmetic-circuit shorthand
is accepted.

The input has dimension `d` and modules `r=1,...,R`. A module specifies two
distinct nonnegative rational complexes `alpha_r,beta_r`, positive rational
numbers `c_r^+,c_r^-`, optional shared positive numerator factors `A_r`, and
one factorized denominator `Q_r`

\[
 A_r(x)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{a_r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{u_{r\ell}}p_{r\ell j}x^{\delta_{r\ell j}}\right),
 \quad
 Q_r(x)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{s_r}
       \left(\sum_{j=1}^{t_{r\ell}}q_{r\ell j}x^{\gamma_{r\ell j}}\right),
 \qquad p_{r\ell j},q_{r\ell j}>0,\quad
 \delta_{r\ell j},\gamma_{r\ell j}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^d.
\]

Each denominator factor must include a positive constant term, so `Q_r`
stays nonsingular on the nonnegative orthant. Numerator factors need not have
a constant term but are strictly positive on the positive orthant. The two
channel rates and jumps are

\[
 \rho_r^+(x)=c_r^+x^{\alpha_r}A_r(x)/Q_r(x),\quad \nu_r=\beta_r-\alpha_r;
 \qquad
 \rho_r^-(x)=c_r^-x^{\beta_r}A_r(x)/Q_r(x),\quad -\nu_r.
\]

This covers a reversible enzyme law whose net flux is
`A_r(x)*(c_r^+x^{alpha_r}-c_r^-x^{beta_r})/Q_r(x)`, when the numerator orders
are the endpoint complexes. The shared positive numerator can encode a
common activator while its saturating denominator is included in `Q_r`. It
permits arbitrary positive rational kinetic constants and different
regulators or binding polynomials for different modules. The parser treats
the listed positive supports/factors as supplied input; it does not acquire
them from data or expand factors.

The parser requires \(R\ge1\) and rejects every self-loop
\(\alpha_r=\beta_r\). Thus every accepted module jump is nonzero and the
network has stoichiometric rank at least one; rank zero is outside this
schema. The exact decision is `CERTIFIED` for ordinary endotacticity. The
strong subdecision uses the standard global maximum over **all** lifted
sources, including neutral channels:

* `CERTIFIED` if the module jumps have rational rank exactly one;
* `UNKNOWN` otherwise.

The rank test is exact sparse rational arithmetic. This is a sufficient,
not necessary, strong test. In particular weak reversibility alone is never
promoted to strong endotacticity.

## No-expansion proof

Let

\[
 D(x)=\prod_{s=1}^R Q_s(x),\qquad G(x)=D(x)F(x).
\]

For a fixed module `r`, the positive common multiplier on its forward and
reverse channels after clearing denominators is

\[
H_r(x)=D(x)/Q_r(x)=\prod_{s\ne r}Q_s(x).
\]

After denominator clearing, both channels of module `r` are multiplied by the
same positive polynomial `A_r H_r`.

For every direction `w`, the forward lifted source height is
`w dot alpha_r + h_{A_r}(w) + h_{H_r}(w) = h_D(w) + (w dot alpha_r + h_{A_r}(w) - h_{Q_r}(w))`;
the reverse formula is identical with `beta_r`. Thus the lifted source order
is exactly the original positive-rational source order up to the one common
offset `h_D(w)`. This is the support-function equivalence that makes the
following graph proof a certificate for the rational-rate predicate itself.

Choose one monomial term from every factor of `A_r H_r`, and let `eta` be the
sum of their exponent vectors. That one tuple contributes the two labeled
edges

\[
 \alpha_r+\eta\;\longrightarrow\;\beta_r+\eta,
 \qquad
 \beta_r+\eta\;\longrightarrow\;\alpha_r+\eta,
\]

with positive coefficients `c_r^+` and `c_r^-` times the corresponding
positive factor-coefficient product. Thus each tuple is a 2-cycle. The full
finite lifted diagram is weakly reversible. Products and supports need not be
materialized.

For any direction `w`, neutral edges are removed before taking the maximum
essential source, as in the standard endotactic definition. If an active
edge at the maximum active-source score had `w dot nu > 0`, its paired
reverse edge would be active and have a strictly higher source score. This
contradicts maximality. Therefore **every** active edge at the maximum,
including every tied edge, has `w dot nu < 0`. This proves ordinary
endotacticity with the requested tie/neutral semantics directly.

Colliding Minkowski sums do not break the argument. A collision aggregates
strictly positive coefficients; the reverse of each tuple remains present
with a positive coefficient. Signed cancellation is excluded by the input
promise.

## Strong boundary

Suppose all module jumps lie in one line `S`. Every lifted linkage
class contains a nonzero paired edge and all its reaction vectors lie in
`S`, so its stoichiometric subspace is exactly `S`. If `w` is not orthogonal
to the full lifted stoichiometric subspace, no nonzero module jump is
orthogonal to `w`. At a globally maximal source, the paired endpoint is also
a reactant and cannot have a greater score; the edge from the maximal endpoint
to its paired endpoint points inward. The lifted system is strongly
endotactic.

Rank zero is not accepted: the schema requires at least one module and
rejects equal endpoints. Thus neutral self-loops cannot enter the global
strong maximum. The gate cannot be dropped. Fixture
`fixtures/weak_not_strong_distinct_linkage_spans.json` has two reversible
modules with positive-constant denominators. Its lifted graph is weakly
reversible and endotactic, but `w=(1,0,-1)` leaves only neutral `A<->A+B`
sources at the global maximum; the active `0<->C` pair is strictly lower.
Thus it fails the standard strong condition. `replay_canonical.py` explicitly
expands this small fixture and checks the witness with exact fractions.

## Coefficient bounds and the conditional permanence gate

For each factor let `qmin` be its smallest listed coefficient and `qsum` the
sum of listed coefficients. For a term tuple from `H_r`, its coefficient is
at least the product of all `qmin` values in other modules. Each lifted tuple
also chooses a term from every numerator factor in module `r`; include their
factorwise minima in the lower bound. Upper bounds use the corresponding
factorwise coefficient sums, which also bound support collisions. Therefore
the compiler emits exact rational `kappa>0` and `K<infty` that contain every
coefficient in the aggregated simple directed graph. The bit sizes of these
products are polynomial in the listed rational bit sizes.

Concretely, if module `r` has directional constants `c_r^+,c_r^-`, define
its tuplewise lower bound \(m_r\) and modulewise upper bound \(M_r\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_r&=\min(c_r^+,c_r^-)
       \prod_\ell\min_j p_{r\ell j}
       \prod_{s\ne r}\prod_\ell\min_j q_{s\ell j},\\
M_r&=\max(c_r^+,c_r^-)
       \prod_\ell\sum_j p_{r\ell j}
       \prod_{s\ne r}\prod_\ell\sum_j q_{s\ell j}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The emitted interval for the **simple directed graph convention**, which
sums coincident same-direction tuple edges across and within modules, is

\[
\kappa=\min_r m_r,\qquad K=\sum_{r=1}^R M_r.
\]

Every tuple contribution from module \(r\) is at least \(m_r\), so any merged
edge has coefficient at least \(\kappa\). For each fixed module, its total
contribution to a merged edge is at most \(M_r\), by the product-of-sums
bound over all numerator and other-denominator factor tuples. Summing
\(M_r\) over modules therefore bounds cross-module collisions too. For
example, two identical \(0\leftrightarrow X\) modules with unit coefficients
and denominator \(1\) have \(\kappa=1\) and merged-edge bound \(K=2\).

For rank greater than one, this report does not prove permanence. If the N33
all-endotactic permanence candidate is accepted with its common-class
hypotheses, the lifted finite diagram is endotactic and its coefficients lie
in the compiler's positive box, so that conditional theorem applies. This
package does not re-prove N33.

For the rank-one gate, the lifted graph is strongly endotactic and
published strong-endotactic permanence applies to its fixed positive
coefficients. The original rational field is `F=G/D`. If `y(s)` is a
permanent trajectory of `G`, the original clock is

\[
 t(s)=\int_0^s D(y(u))\,du.
\]

On the eventual compact positive set for `y`, the continuous positive `D` has
finite positive lower and upper bounds; hence `t(s)` tends to infinity and
the two systems have the same eventual compact positive orbit. This transfers
permanence to `F`. It does not claim convergence to a particular equilibrium
or validate any QSSA approximation.

## Charged complexity and comparison

Let `U_r=product_l u_{r,l}` and `T_r=product_l t_{r,l}` be the term-tuple
counts in `A_r` and `Q_r`. The fully expanded lift has at most

\[
2\sum_{r=1}^R U_r\prod_{s\ne r}T_s
\]

labeled reactions. This is exact as a tuple-count upper bound and can be
exponential in the module count. For example, `R` modules with two-term
denominators and no shared numerator factors have upper bound
`2 R 2^(R-1)` lifted reactions. Shared numerator factors multiply the
corresponding module term count by `U_r`. The program
computes this integer and coefficient bounds but not the reactions.

For input bit length `L`, parsing the sparse endpoints/factor supports,
checking positivity and constant terms, computing rational coefficient
bounds, and testing the common-line condition take polynomial bit time in
`L`. No LP calls, direction fan cells, common-denominator products, or
Minkowski supports are enumerated. Standard MILP algorithms for explicit
reaction diagrams remain the strongest comparator once a finite diagram has
been materialized; the result here is a specialized certificate for an
implicit rational interface and avoids its potentially exponential lift.

## Scope limits

This is not a polynomial-time recognizer for arbitrary positive rational
channels. It depends on a reversible endpoint pair sharing its own
denominator. Schnell–Mendoza's multiple-alternative-substrates model is a
canonical common-denominator example but is irreversible; the companion
compiler therefore produces an exact weak-endotactic counterdirection and
substrate-decay bound instead of applying this reversible theorem. Arbitrary
different denominators in the C8 input class retain coNP-complete recognition
from `work/agents/c8_implicit_rational_geometry/PROOF.md`; that lower bound is not
claimed to hold inside this reversible module syntax.
