Fundamental highest-line map and a fixed-group global overlap bound
Date: 2026-10-08

Scope/status: internally derived for a compact connected group G with
simple Lie algebra, with a fixed choice of invariant metric. The map is an
embedding after projectivizing the fundamental representations, including
for non-simply-connected G. An explicit positive, effective root-data
constant is given below. The global far-from-T optimization is computable
but not evaluated numerically for a higher-rank group in this note. No
dimension-uniform-in-G claim is made.

1. Cover, center, and the exact stabilizer
------------------------------------------

Let G_tilde be the simply connected compact group with the given root
system, T_tilde a maximal torus, and omega_1,...,omega_r the fundamental
weights. Let V_i=V_(omega_i), with highest unit vector v_i and projective
representation pi-bar_i:G_tilde->PU(V_i). For any compact connected form
G=G_tilde/Z with Z central, every z in Z(G_tilde) acts by a scalar in
V_i. Hence pi-bar_i kills the center and descends to a projective action
of G. The formula

  Fmap:G/T -> product_i P(V_i),
       gT |-> ([pi_i(g)v_i])_i                                  (1)

is independent of the lift g_tilde of g. It is a map of projective
representations; an individual V_(omega_i) need not be a linear
representation of the quotient G.

The common projective stabilizer of the highest lines in G_tilde is exactly
T_tilde, with no extra finite Weyl stabilizer. For the Lie algebra
statement, write X in t plus real root planes. For a positive root alpha,
the negative root operator sends v_i nontrivially to the weight space
omega_i-alpha exactly when <omega_i,alpha^vee> > 0. Every positive coroot
has at least one positive simple-coroot coefficient, so for every alpha
some i detects its root plane. Different alpha give different weight
spaces, and no nonzero root-plane component can preserve all highest
lines. Thus the Lie algebra of the common stabilizer is t.

For the group statement, let H be that common stabilizer. Since H^0=T_tilde,
any h in H normalizes T_tilde, so h represents a Weyl element w. It fixes
each projective highest line. A normalizer element sends the weight line
of omega_i to the weight line of w omega_i; equality of the lines forces
w omega_i=omega_i for every i. The fundamental weights span the real
weight space, hence w=1 and h is in T_tilde. The center is already inside
T_tilde. Quotienting by Z gives stabilizer T in G. This also rules out a
finite stabilizer larger than T.

Consequently (1) is injective. The same root-plane argument makes its
differential injective at the basepoint, and equivariance gives this at
every point. Since G/T is compact and the target is Hausdorff, (1) is a
smooth embedding. Equivalently, the full collection of fundamental
highest lines identifies the complete flag manifold, even though each
individual line map generally has a larger stabilizer.

2. Root-normalized local Hessian
--------------------------------

Use the basic bi-invariant metric whose dual inner product has long roots
of squared length 2 (equivalently -Killing/(2 g^vee)). Let d(g,T) be its
quotient distance, F(g)=sum_i(1-|a_i(g)|^2), and

  a_i(g)=<v_i,pi_i(g)v_i>.

F is left- and right-T-invariant. In rank-one projector notation
P_i(g)=pi_i(g)|v_i><v_i|pi_i(g)^*,

  F(g)=1/2 sum_i ||P_i(g)-P_i(e)||_HS^2.                    (2)

For a positive root alpha, choose an orthonormal real basis
X_alpha,Y_alpha of its root plane for the basic metric. If
X=sum_(alpha>0)(x_alpha X_alpha+y_alpha Y_alpha) is a unit tangent in
t-perp, direct sl_2 root-string calculation gives

  F(exp(tX))=c(X)t^2+O(t^3),
  c(X)=sum_(alpha>0) [ |alpha|^2/4 *
                      sum_i <omega_i,alpha^vee> ]
                    (x_alpha^2+y_alpha^2).                 (3)

Indeed, for A_i=d pi_i(X),
1-|<v_i,e^(t A_i)v_i>|^2 is t^2 times the variance of A_i in v_i,
and for a unit root-plane vector
||A_i v_i||^2=|alpha|^2<omega_i,alpha^vee>/4. The lowered vectors for
distinct roots are orthogonal. If alpha=sum_j n_j alpha_j, then

  sum_i <omega_i,alpha^vee>
       = sum_j n_j |alpha_j|^2/|alpha|^2,

so the coefficient of that root plane in (3) is
(1/4)sum_j n_j|alpha_j|^2. It follows that the smallest local quadratic
coefficient is exactly

  c_G = (1/4) min_j |alpha_j|^2
      = 1/(2 ell_G),                                         (4)

where ell_G is the lacing number (1 for simply laced, 2 for B,C,F4, and
3 for G2), under the long-root-squared-2 normalization. In particular
the Hessian is positive definite on t-perp; this is the quantitative
immersion statement.

An explicit local radius follows from a third-derivative bound. Put

  B_G=8 sum_i ||omega_i||^3,
  r_G=3 c_G/B_G.                                             (5)

For a unit-speed normal geodesic exp(tX)T, the representation generator
has operator norm at most ||omega_i||: every weight lies in the convex
hull of the Weyl orbit of omega_i. Thus |a_i^(k)(t)|<=||omega_i||^k.
Differentiating |a_i|^2 three times gives |(1-|a_i|^2)'''|<=8||omega_i||^3.
Taylor's theorem, (3), and (5) imply

  F(g) >= (c_G/2) d(g,T)^2       whenever d(g,T)<=r_G.     (6)

Normal homogeneous geodesics from eT have the form exp(tX)T, so this
estimate applies to every point in that metric ball, including points
past a coordinate-chart boundary.

3. A computable global constant for each fixed G
------------------------------------------------

Let Dbar_G be any computable upper bound on diam(G/T). One elementary
choice is the largest norm in a bounded fundamental parallelepiped for the
exponential kernel lattice in t: every group element is conjugate into T,
and each torus element has a logarithm in that parallelepiped, so its
distance from the identity is at most that logarithm's norm. This bounds
diam(G), hence diam(G/T), and is directly computable from the cocharacter
lattice. A Weyl-alcove bound can be sharper. If the set
below is nonempty, put

  m_G = min { F(g): gT in G/T, d(g,T)>=r_G }.

The embedding proof shows F>0 on this compact set, so m_G>0. If the set is
empty, take m_G=+infinity. Then

  kappa_G = min(c_G/2, m_G/Dbar_G^2) > 0,

and for every g in G,

  sum_i (1-|a_i(g)|^2) >= kappa_G d(g,T)^2.                 (7)

This is an effective fixed-group constant, not only an unspecified
compactness minimum. The root system supplies the fundamental matrices,
the metric, c_G, B_G, and Dbar_G. The remaining m_G is a finite compact
optimization of explicit matrix coefficients. One certified procedure
is to cover G/T by successively finer root-coordinate nets; F is Lipschitz
with explicit constant 2 sum_i||omega_i||, while distance-to-eT is
1-Lipschitz. Retain net cells that can meet d>=r_G and subtract the
Lipschitz mesh error from the smallest sampled F. Since the far set is
compact and F has strictly positive minimum there, this adaptive lower
bound eventually becomes positive and returns a rational lower bound
below m_G. Distances and the diameter bound are computed from the
exponential map and affine Weyl alcove. This may be expensive, but it
terminates for each fixed root system and gives a reproducible positive
certificate. No numerical m_G for rank greater than one is claimed here.

4. Small-root checks
--------------------

The local coefficients from (3) can be checked using only positive-root
expansions. With long roots squared 2, c_alpha=(1/4)sum_j n_j|alpha_j|^2.

  type / simple-root squared lengths / positive-root coefficient list / c_G
  A1    (2)       (1)                                  1/2
  A2    (2,2)     (1,0),(0,1),(1,1)                    1/2
  B2    (2,1)     (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2)              1/4
  C2    (1,2)     (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1)              1/4
  G2    (2/3,2)   (1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)  1/6

For reproducibility, evaluate each listed coefficient vector n by
`sum(n[j]*length2[j] for j in range(rank))/4` and take the minimum; the
whole check is the four-line routine

  def cstar(length2, positive_roots):
      return min(sum(n*l for n,l in zip(root,length2))/4
                 for root in positive_roots)

with the lists shown in the table.
For B2 the minimum is the short simple root 1/4; for G2 it is the short
simple root 1/6. These check the root normalization and lacing dependence
in (4), not the far-set numerical optimizer.

For A1 the global constant is also exact. Write

  g = t_1 [[cos(s),sin(s)],[-sin(s),cos(s)]] t_2,
  t_1,t_2 in T,   0<=s<=pi/2.

In the fundamental representation, |a(g)|^2=cos^2(s), so F(g)=sin^2(s).
For the basic metric, the horizontal generator has norm sqrt(2), hence
d(g,T)=sqrt(2)s. Therefore

  inf_(g notin T) F(g)/d(g,T)^2
     = min_(0<s<=pi/2) sin^2(s)/(2s^2)=2/pi^2.

The minimum is at s=pi/2; the local limit at s=0 is c_G=1/2, as in (3).
This exact A1 calculation is a global small-root check of (7).

Boundary: the embedding and local root constant are analytic. The general
global constant uses a certified compact optimization and is not reported
as a closed-form universal number; external validation and priority are
not assessed.
