NORMALIZATION AND CONSTANT AUDIT — 2026-10-07
Scope: direct algebra and edge cases only. The underlying Dyson monomial interpolation inequality is supplied by the parent and is not independently certified here.

Assumptions: finite-dimensional faithful density omega, Hermitian A,B, ||A||,||B||<=C, C>=0. Set H=log omega, P=exp(H+A), Q=exp(H+B), a=Tr P, b=Tr Q, delta=||P-Q||_1, D=A-B, epsilon=Tr omega D^2, q=2C. All estimates use natural logarithms.

1. PARTITION FUNCTIONS AND NORMALIZATION
The Hermitian eigenvalue inequalities lambda_i(H)-C <= lambda_i(H+X) <= lambda_i(H)+C imply e^{-C} <= Z_X <= e^C for X=A,B. This is a trace-exponential statement; it does not assert that the matrix exponential is operator monotone.

Assume a>=b. The identity
 P/a-Q/b=(P-Q)/a + Q(1/a-1/b)
therefore gives
 T(P/a,Q/b)<= [delta+(a-b)]/(2a).
Interchange P,Q if b>a. Consequently
 T <= [delta+|a-b|]/[2 max(a,b)] <= delta/max(a,b) <= e^C delta.
Thus the proposed normalization multiplier is correct. Retaining max(a,b) and |a-b| gives a stronger bound. When a=b the factor is 1/(2a), but it cannot be uniformly halved.

Sharpness of the uniform multiplier e^C: for C>0, choose 0<h<=2C and 0<r<1, omega=diag(1-r,r), B=-C I, A=diag(-C,-C+h). Put u=r(e^h-1). Then
 delta=e^{-C}u,
 T=u(1-r)/(1+u),
 T/delta=e^C(1-r)/(1+u) -> e^C as r->0.
The example is faithful and obeys the potential bounds. This establishes sharpness only of the multiplier converting a generic unnormalized trace norm delta; it does not establish sharpness of the final epsilon-dependent theorem constants.

2. DYSON REMAINDER ALGEBRA
The supplied simplex bound for order n>=2 is
 n q C^{n-1} integral_0^1 r(1-r)^{n-2}/(n-2)! exp(-L r/2) dr,
where L=log(q^2/epsilon). Writing m=n-2 and summing
 sum_{m>=0}(m+2)[C(1-r)]^m/m! = [2+C(1-r)]exp(C(1-r))
gives precisely
 R=q C integral_0^1 r[2+C(1-r)]exp(C(1-r)-L r/2)dr.
Set k=C+L/2. Then
 R=q C e^C [(C+2)I1(k)-C I2(k)],
 I1(k)=[1-(1+k)e^{-k}]/k^2,
 I2(k)=[2-(k^2+2k+2)e^{-k}]/k^3.
For q=2C this agrees with the supplied exact formula. Both I1,I2 are nonnegative. Dropping -C I2 and enlarging the integration domain yields
 R <= q C(C+2)e^C/k^2 = 8 C^2(C+2)e^C/(L+2C)^2.
For L>0 this implies the originally proposed 8 C^2(C+2)e^C/L^2. The coefficient of L^{-2} is exactly the leading large-L coefficient of this supplied integral bound; a smaller leading constant would require improving the preceding Dyson bound.

Accordingly a strictly stronger simple normalized conclusion is
 T <= min{1, e^C sqrt(epsilon) + 8 C^2(C+2)e^{2C}/(L+2C)^2}.
The exact integral gives the stronger conclusion
 T <= min{1, e^C[sqrt(epsilon)+R]}.
The data-dependent normalization estimate in section 1 can also replace e^C.

At L=0 the exact integral gives R=q(e^C-1)=2C(e^C-1), since integral_0^1 r(1-r)^{n-2}/(n-2)! dr=1/n!. Hence sqrt(epsilon)+R=q e^C at epsilon=q^2. There is no singularity in the Dyson integral at this endpoint. The displayed rational closed form is numerically ill-conditioned when k is very small; the integral or series is then preferable.

An optional sharper scale is q'=||D|| rather than 2C. If q'>0, define L'=log(q'^2/epsilon)>=0 and replace q by q' throughout R. This improves the integral pointwise because q' exp[-L'r/2]=(q')^{1-r}epsilon^{r/2} is increasing in q' for 0<=r<=1. This does not alter C, which bounds the other Dyson insertions.

3. DOMAIN AND ENDPOINTS
Always 0<=epsilon<=q^2. For a theorem using a finite real L>0, state C>0 and 0<epsilon<4C^2 explicitly.

C=0: A=B=0, epsilon=q=0 and T=0. The ratio q^2/epsilon is undefined and must not be inserted into the formula.

epsilon=0 with faithful omega: Tr omega D^2=0 implies D=0, hence A=B and T=0. Treat this as a separate branch or define the right side by its zero continuous extension (L=+infinity).

epsilon=q^2 with C>0: L=0, outside the original stated domain. Since q^2I-D^2>=0 and omega is faithful, its zero omega expectation implies D^2=q^2I. On the +/-q eigenspaces of D, equality in the bounds on A and B forces A= D/2 and B=-D/2. The original L^{-2} display diverges; the exact integral or denominator (L+2C)^2 extends the estimate finitely. T<=1 remains available.

Dimension one: every normalized state is the sole scalar density 1, so T=0 regardless of the scalar potentials. More generally A-B=hI implies rho_A=rho_B, although epsilon=h^2 can be nonzero. The bound is consistent but does not exploit this gauge invariance.

No flaw was found in normalization, summation, the stated constants, or the interior domain. The stronger denominator and exact integral are routine sharpenings. The load-bearing remaining audit is the supplied Schatten interpolation estimate for each Dyson monomial, including its adjacent-power exponent r/2; normalization cannot repair any failure there.

5. UPDATED STRONGER S2 SPLIT (PARENT-SUPPLIED INTERPOLATION)
The parent subsequently supplied an improved core estimate: q exp(-L r) for 0<=r<=1/2, and sqrt(epsilon) for 1/2<=r<=1. Its interpolation proof remains outside this constants-only audit.

Define F(r)=exp(C(1-r))[1+C r(1-r)]. Direct differentiation gives
 F'(r)=-C r[2+C(1-r)]exp(C(1-r)).
Consequently the high-r Dyson weight is exactly
 B(C)=C integral_{1/2}^1 r[2+C(1-r)]exp(C(1-r))dr
     =exp(C/2)(1+C/4)-1.
Combining this with the order-one term yields
 delta <= exp(C/2)(1+C/4)sqrt(epsilon)
          +q C e^C[(C+2)I1(1/2,k)-C I2(1/2,k)], k=L+C,
where
 I1(t,k)=[1-(1+kt)e^{-kt}]/k^2,
 I2(t,k)=[2-((kt)^2+2kt+2)e^{-kt}]/k^3.
For q=2C, the simple normalized bound is
 T <= min{1, exp(3C/2)(1+C/4)sqrt(epsilon)
              +2 C^2(C+2)e^{2C}/(L+C)^2}.
The exact split is at least as strong as the original integral estimate pointwise in r; the simplified displays need not dominate one another at every epsilon, because the coefficient of sqrt(epsilon) increases.

Endpoint check L=0: sqrt(epsilon)=q, and the low weight divided by q is
 F(0)-F(1/2)=e^C-e^{C/2}(1+C/4).
It cancels the added high coefficient to give delta<=q e^C, exactly as in the original integral. The low and high weights are nonnegative. C=0 and epsilon=0 still need the explicit branches in section 3. The leading L^{-2} coefficient of the new remainder is four times smaller than the old one, conditional on the stronger parent-supplied interpolation.
