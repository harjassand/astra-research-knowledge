# Real decaying Landis construction: scoped primary prior audit

7 October 2026. Assigned prior-art audit, not a proof gate. Read the r15 power construction and log-frequency regularity derivations. Their independent mathematical gates belong to r07/r12. Historical priority **UNKNOWN**; no exhaustive novelty assertion.

## Verdict

No primary source read here states the whole proposed conclusion: in every n≥3, real smooth nonzero u with −Δu+Vu=0, |u|≤C exp(−cr log r), and |D^mV|≤C_m r^(−1/2)(log r)^(m+3/2) for every fixed m. No source read here establishes that conclusion as impossible either. The more modest power-decay extension is closely suggested by the source itself: Frank–Ivanisvili Appendix A gives exactly the two macro errors whose weighted balance yields its exponent. Treat this as an extension of supplied machinery, not discovery of a new cancellation or exponent.

The log-frequency consequence would, if its separate gate passes, distinguish absolute smoothness from relative variation and spectral positivity. It would not resolve analytic/symbol classes or the β=1/2 no-log threshold. It excludes restoring strong exponential Landis by requiring only V and each fixed Cartesian derivative to vanish. It does not give a fixed-power exp(−r^(1+ε)) counterexample under these derivative bounds.

## Source-supplied balance versus added work

FI's errors become a/ell≤κ and a²ell/r≤κ when the target potential size is κ. Compatibility gives a³≤rκ². Equalizing yields ell=√(r/a), κ=a^(3/2)/√r. For a~r^α and κ~r^(−β), α=(1−2β)/3 and envelope power1+α=(4−2β)/3. These calculations are immediate from AppendixA; Cruz-Sampedro already has that exponent. What still needs proof is weighted inversion/repair across sparse nodal regions, global smoothing with a nonconstant radius, and all-order quotient bounds in the real construction.

Taking a=log r gives κ=r^(−1/2)(log r)^(3/2) and ∫a~rlogr. Taking a^mκ provides vanishing for every fixed m, whereas a power a~r^α only gives vanishing for orders m with αm<β. The choice of slowly growing a is an intelligible constructive regularity modification, not a derivative theorem contained in FI.

## Derivative discriminator: exact elementary obstruction

To verify that the candidate does not inherit radial/dilation hypotheses merely from absolute jets, consider a scalar profile on r≥3,

    v(r)=r^(−1/2)(log r)^(3/2) sin(rlogr),

and extend smoothly over the compact interior. Product/chain differentiation gives |v^(m)|≤C_m r^(−1/2)(log r)^(m+3/2)→0 for every fixed m; the corresponding radial Cartesian derivatives obey the same estimate. At radii r_k with r_klogr_k=2πk,

    rv'(r_k)=r_k^(1/2)(log r_k)^(3/2)(log r_k+1)→∞.

At the alternating phases π+2πk the sign reverses. The same phases show ∂r(r²v) takes both signs at arbitrarily large r. Thus absolute C∞ decay does not imply bounded dilation derivative, inverse-radius derivative gains, or Pan–Zhang's monotonicity. **This scalar profile is not claimed to generate a decaying PDE solution**; it is only a counterexample to an implication between potential classes. Rossi rules out treating radial v as the desired PDE construction.

For the Bakri comparator, under macroscopic coordinate dilation x=x0+Ry the rescaled potential is W_R(y)=R²V(x0+Ry), so ∂yW_R=R³∂xV. A bounded physical gradient therefore does not keep the rescaled C1 norm at O(R²). Even in an exterior region with the candidate bound |∇V|≲R^(−1/2)log^(5/2)R, the derivative term is O(R^(5/2)log^(5/2)R), whose square root is O(R^(5/4)log^(5/4)R). This is a scaling calculation, not application of Bakri's compact global theorem; it invalidates a purported immediate exp(−CR) contradiction.

## Downstream consequence conditional on mathematical gate

If the proposed real bounded V and superexponential u construction passes, then the Sirakov–Souplet theorem (or Rossi1.5) implies λ1(−Δ+V,Rn)<0 by contraposition. Indeed the sphere integral upper bound is at most surface area times Cexp(−cRlogR), whereas ∫B_R|u| stays bounded below by a positive compact-ball mass for all large R. A fixed exponential lower bound cannot hold. Consequently the example targets a zero-energy state of an operator that fails global nonnegativity; it does not refute nonnegative-operator Landis theorems. With V→0, negative bottom spectrum is compatible with zero at the essential-spectrum threshold. This is a useful experimental/theoretical criterion: regularity and decay alone leave the counterexample route open; spectral positivity closes it.
