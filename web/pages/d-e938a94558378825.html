CYCLE 4 LEAF | PUBLISHED STOCHASTIC WR THEOREM COVERAGE OF THE SPARSE PAIRED-EDGE FAMILY
Checked: 2026-10-08
Status: bounded primary-source comparison; no novelty or priority claim

VERDICT
-------
No published all-rate weakly-reversible stochastic recurrence theorem located
here covers the nontrivial sparse paired-edge family as a whole in active
dimension at least three. The network has a single weakly reversible linkage
class and full rank on its active species, but the published ACK structural
theorem requires binary complexes and pure single-species/dimer complexes;
the published tier criteria also fail at an explicit reachable catalyst-free
boundary sequence. The one-dimensional result is out of rank, and the
complex-balanced product-form results cover only a constrained rate
subfamily. The two-species all-rate theorem is author/project-reported, but
its manuscript is still listed as in preparation; it is not an inspectable
published proof as of this check.

The sparse Foster and exponential-Foster arguments in the workspace may prove
recurrence for larger rate families by new, family-specific Lyapunov
arguments. They are internally reconstructed candidates, not coverage by the
published general WR theorems audited below. The reported two-species theorem
does cover the two-active-species specialization if its announced theorem is
confirmed. None of these comparisons transfers to the separate deterministic
N33/N54 permanence/PDE claims, nor from fixed rate constants to predictable
time-varying rate controllers.

1. Exact family and structural invariants
-----------------------------------------
Let finite J and I index catalyst species A_j and substrate species B_i. Let
E subset J x I be the paired support; for each (j,i) in E, k_ji>=1 and all
six rate constants below are fixed, finite, and strictly positive:

    0 <-> A_j,
    A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
    A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i.

Propensities are standard stochastic mass action: kappa x_under_y,
coordinatewise, with zero rate if x does not dominate source y. For every j,
0 <-> A_j is included. No substrate reaction is included for a nonedge.
Let m=|E| and let t be the number of substrates incident to at least one
edge. Inert substrates (degree zero in E) are conserved labels and should be
removed from the reacting species list; conclusions for the sparse candidate
are classwise after fixing those labels.

For the active network, d=r+t. The reaction vectors 0<->A_j supply each
coordinate vector e_Aj, and any incident A_j<->A_j+B_i pair supplies e_Bi.
Consequently the stoichiometric rank is d. The CTMC is irreducible on the
active nonnegative lattice: basal births/deaths change catalyst counts, and
while a neighbor catalyst is present, the A_j<->A_j+B_i pair changes B_i
both ways. Any state with all A counts zero and B_i=n is reachable from the
origin by immigrating a neighbor A_j, firing A_j->A_j+B_i n times, then
removing A_j. In particular, the boundary sequence used below is genuinely
reachable, not just an ambient-lattice artifact.

The complex graph is one linkage class and weakly reversible for every E,
including disconnected or sparse support: every catalyst node is connected
to 0, and every edge (j,i) adds the branch
 A_j -- A_j+B_i -- (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i.
Its underlying undirected graph is a tree. The complex count is 1+r+2m;
the active stoichiometric rank is r+t; hence deficiency is

    delta = (1+r+2m)-1-(r+t) = 2m-t.

If m>0, t<=m and delta>=m>0. Thus the general weakly reversible
deficiency-zero, all-rate product-form theorem applies only to the degenerate
no-edge case, not to any nontrivial paired-edge support. For E=J x I this
reduces to delta=s(2r-1), as recorded in the cycle-3 audit.

2. Published WR recurrence results and exact comparisons
-------------------------------------------------------

A. Anderson--Cappelletti--Kim (2020), Theorem 4.1
   Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2

The theorem is for a weakly reversible, binary mass-action system with one
linkage class, and requires for every species S that S or 2S be a complex.
The sparse target meets weak reversibility and one-linkage-class, but fails
both other assumptions for every nonempty E: each high complex has
molecularity k_ji+2>=3, so the graph is not binary; and no B_i-only or
2B_i-only complex occurs. A_j itself is a complex, so the pure-complex
condition is met for catalysts but not for active substrates. This theorem
does not cover a nontrivial sparse or complete-pair instance.

B. Anderson--Cappelletti--Kim (2020), Theorem 6.1 (conditional tier result)
   Same primary source, theorem lines 337-343.

This is a broader all-rate theorem for weakly reversible single-linkage
mass-action systems, provided every proper tier sequence of the chosen
process satisfies T^{S,1} subset T^{D,1}, where S and D are stochastic and
deterministic top tiers. The graph and fixed-rate mass-action hypotheses are
met, but the tier premise fails. Fix any nonisolated B_i, choose a neighboring
A_j, and let

    x_n: a_l=0 for every l; b_i=n; b_h=0 for h != i.

Each x_n is reachable from 0. At x_n the only enabled source complex is 0,
through 0->A_l immigration, so T^{S,1}={0}. In deterministic tier order,
(x_n vee 1)^(A_l+B_i)=n and
(x_n vee 1)^((k_li+1)A_l+B_i)=n; all such edge complexes are in T^{D,1},
while 0 has order 1. Therefore 0 is not in T^{D,1}, and the inclusion fails.
The exact obstruction is that an unbounded substrate is stranded on the
catalyst-free face: the deterministic monomial tier remembers complexes
disabled by a_j=0. The published theorem's proof does not infer recurrence
from weak reversibility alone, so its failure here is a scope gap, not a
counterexample to recurrence.

Clock check: Theorem 6.1 uses a fixed-step embedded jump chain but its paper's
Theorem 3.1 converts the result to nonexplosion and physical-time positive
recurrence when inf_x q(x)>0 on the reachable class. This family has
q(x)>=sum_j alpha_j>0, so the clock-conversion premise is automatically
satisfied. The reason the theorem does not apply is its tier premise, not
holding-time behavior.

C. Anderson--Kim (2018), Theorem 4.2 and structural corollaries
   Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11263v3

Theorem 4.2 is an all-rate CTMC Foster theorem for networks whose every tier
sequence has a top stochastic-tier source of a D-descending reaction. It
does not require weak reversibility, but the target fails its premise on the
same sequence x_n. At x_n, T^{S,1}={0}; the D-top contains A_l+B_i because
this complex has deterministic order n and the reverse edge
A_l+B_i->A_l descends to order 1. Thus D is nonempty but
T^{S,1} cap D is empty. Since this is a reachable sequence in the closed
irreducible class, it witnesses failure for the theorem's actual
class-specific condition. Theorem 4.2's conclusion is in physical time, so
there is no missing event-to-time conversion to repair.

The paper's structural single-linkage theorem (Theorem 2.1 / Theorem 1 in
the proof) covers binary weakly reversible networks after adjoining unary
0<->S inflow/outflow channels for every species. The target only has those
channels for A_j and is nonbinary. Its other structural extensions retain
binary/double-full conditions and do not cover this higher-order B_i
coupling. Failure of these sufficient hypotheses is not evidence of
transience or explosion.

D. Wiuf--Xu (2023 version), Corollary 4.10
   Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2012.07954v3

The result proves positive recurrence (with their exponential-ergodicity
conclusion) for weakly reversible mass-action systems satisfying H2--H3,
where H2 is one-dimensional stoichiometric subspace; H1 is the standard
enabled-source condition and H3 excludes purely one-way trivial dynamics.
The target satisfies weak reversibility and standard propensities, but when
m>0 its active rank r+t>=2, so it is outside H2. With no paired edges and
r=1 the remaining network is rank one and this theorem applies; that case
is only the elementary immigration/death subsystem with frozen substrate
labels.

E. Complex-balanced product form and nonexplosion
   Primary sources:
   https://arxiv.org/html/0803.3042v4
   https://arxiv.org/html/1708.09356v2

Anderson--Craciun--Kurtz (2010), Theorem 4.1, gives a product-Poisson
stationary distribution whenever the deterministic mass-action system has a
positive complex-balanced equilibrium c. Anderson--Cappelletti--Koyama--Kurtz
(2018), Theorem 3, proves nonexplosion for complex-balanced stochastic
mass-action systems, which supplies the physical-time clock and, on each
closed irreducible class carrying the restricted product law, positive
recurrence. These theorems allow arbitrary molecularity but not arbitrary
rate vectors.

For this tree complex graph, complex balance is equivalent to zero net flux
on each reversible edge (a divergence-free flow on a tree vanishes edge by
edge). It holds exactly when there is c>0 satisfying, for every j and
(j,i) in E,

    c_Aj = alpha_j/delta_j,
    c_Bi = p_ji/q_ji,
    c_Aj^(k_ji) = u_ji/v_ji.

Shared substrate/catalyst coordinates impose consistency across incident
edges. These equations define a proper rate subfamily; weak reversibility
alone does not imply them. The deficiency-zero theorem (ACK 2010,
Theorem 4.2) gives the all-rate consequence only when delta=0, which for
this family means E is empty.

F. Xu (2026 preprint), Theorem 4.6(v), and higher-order regularity
   Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05340v2

The theorem says second-order weakly reversible mass-action systems are
regular/nonexplosive, not positive recurrent. For every nonempty paired edge
the reverse source (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i has molecularity k_ji+2>=3, so the target
is not second-order. The more general regularity tests in that preprint also
do not provide recurrence and their stated linear-growth hypotheses do not
apply automatically to the higher-order channels here. This is not a route
to an all-rate recurrence theorem for the target.

3. Announced 2D result: relevant but not published-proof coverage
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The author-maintained ConStRAINeD results page says every weakly reversible
stochastic reaction network with two species is positive recurrent for
arbitrary rate constants. Its same page currently lists the five-author
paper, “A proof of the chemical recurrence conjecture in two dimensions,”
under “In preparation”:
https://constrained.polito.it/publications/

The claim is also repeated in the authors' conference abstracts. This is
strong SOURCE-REPORTED evidence, but not an inspectable published theorem or
proof. If the active species count is exactly two (r=1 and one nonisolated
substrate), it would cover every positive-rate sparse target of that size
provided the announced theorem has its advertised all-order, fixed-rate
scope. Do not label this EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED or use it as a checked proof
premise while the manuscript remains unavailable.

4. Larger proposed families and clock/interface differences
-----------------------------------------------------------
* The complete-support family E=J x I is a special case of the sparse graph
  above and inherits the same published-theorem gaps for d>=3. Cycle 3 has
  an internally reconstructed exponential-Foster argument for the
  complete-support family and a separate sparse-support argument. Those are
  family-specific proof candidates, not existing published WR theorems.
* The compact-rate-box extension allows predictable time-varying rates and
  seeks uniform drift over a controller. The cited recurrence theorems are
  formulated for fixed rate constants and a time-homogeneous CTMC. They do
  not by themselves imply a uniform controlled-process result. The internal
  box proof has its own hypotheses and evidence record.
* The sparse classwise result fixes inert substrate counts. It does not claim
  uniform stationary tightness over all such class labels. None of the
  structural recurrence theorems above supplies that cross-class uniformity
  for this family.
* N33 is a deterministic ODE permanence reconstruction; N54 is a conditional
  common-scalar-diffusion PDE barrier/attraction result. Neither is a
  theorem about the stochastic mass-action CTMC or its holding-time clock.

5. Conclusion and exact unresolved gap
--------------------------------------
For nonempty E with active rank r+t>=3, the audited published general WR
recurrence theorems do not cover arbitrary positive rates in this family.
The family passes the graph-level WR and one-linkage requirements but fails
the binary/pure-complex hypotheses and has an explicit reachable sequence
violating both published general tier criteria. Rank-one results do not
apply. Complex balance gives all-order classwise recurrence only under the
explicit rate equations above. In active dimension two, the all-rate claim
is reported by its authors but remains manuscript-in-preparation in the
current project listing. For other rates and rank at least two, the bounded
literature audit leaves the all-rate external coverage question UNKNOWN;
this says nothing against the internal Foster candidates or against actual
recurrence.

The precise gap is not an abstract mismatch of model names: on
{a=0,b_i=n}, every higher deterministic-tier source involving B_i is
disabled, the only active reactions are immigration from 0, and each
published tier test needing an enabled top-tier descent loses its premise.
The sparse internal Lyapunov uses a catalyst-free boundary correction to
handle exactly this geometry, a mechanism not supplied by these published
structural criteria.
