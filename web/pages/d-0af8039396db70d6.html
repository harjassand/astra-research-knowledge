## Polynomial pattern and exact Hessians

Let

    P=x⁴+y⁴+z⁴−3(x²y²+y²z²+z²x²)+x²+y²−2z².

Its Laplacian is identically zero: the quartic pure terms contribute 12(x²+y²+z²), the cross terms contribute their negative, and the quadratic terms have Laplacian 2+2−4=0.

Its gradient has components

    x(4x²−6y²−6z²+2),
    y(4y²−6x²−6z²+2),
    z(4z²−6x²−6y²−4).

Thus the origin, (0,0,±1), and (±1,±1,0) are critical points. Their Hessians are, respectively,

    diag(2,2,−4),
    diag(−4,−4,8),
    [[8,−12ab,0], [−12ab,8,0], [0,0,−16]], a,b in {−1,+1}.

The final matrix has eigenvalues 20,−4,−16. The minimum absolute eigenvalue over all seven centers is 2, so every inverse-Hessian norm is ≤1/2. The origin has index 1; the other six have index 2. The independent computation checks these matrices exactly, rather than relying on numerical eigenvalues.

The seven centers have minimum pairwise distance 1, and norm at most sqrt(2). Hence radius-s=1/100000 balls are disjoint and contained in B2. Every third derivative entry of P has absolute value at most 48 on the larger cube [-2,2]³. Bounding the trilinear operator norm by 27 times the largest entry gives ||D³P||≤1296 there. These are the constants used in the persistence proof.

## Direct reconstruction from the actual final sites

Write the exact unit directions as omega_i and actual saved radii as R_i. Both verifiers establish

    |omega_i|=1,  3/4<R_i<5/4,

and all directions, hence all positive-radius physical sites, are distinct. The normalization for analysis is V(x)=N^(-1)sum_i|x−R_i omega_i|^(-1), with N=192. Multiplying by N recovers the physical potential with every strength exactly 1 and changes no critical point or Hessian inertia.

Take epsilon=2^(-40), rho=2^(-96), and define

    F(y)=[V(rho y)−V(0)]/(epsilon rho⁴).

For s=omega·y and r²=|y|², the first four solid Legendre polynomials are

    H1=s,
    H2=(3s²−r²)/2,
    H3=(5s³−3sr²)/2,
    H4=(35s⁴−30s²r²+3r⁴)/8.

Thus the degree-l term of F contributed by source i is

    rho^(l−4) R_i^(−l−1) H_l(y,omega_i)/(N epsilon).

The independent verifier forms these exact rational Cartesian coefficients. For each source-monomial coefficient before the final division by N, it encloses the scaled value between consecutive multiples of 2^(-300), using integer floor and ceiling. Summing lower and upper endpoints and dividing by N preserves containment, including for negative coefficients. Subtracting the exact target polynomial gives an interval coefficient for every degree-1-through-4 monomial. No generator residual or claimed inverse accuracy is needed.

For a coefficient interval [a,b], a derivative monomial on |y_j|≤2 is bounded by max(|a|,|b|) times its exact differentiation factor and 2 to the remaining degree. Summing gives componentwise derivative bounds. Multiplying the largest first-derivative bound by 2 safely bounds the Euclidean gradient norm because sqrt(3)<2; multiplying the largest Hessian-entry bound by 3 bounds its operator norm.

This directly verifies the low-order jet of the actual final Coulomb sites. The original verifier's alternative basis method is also correct: homogeneous harmonicity, the 2l+1 nonzero mutually orthogonal basis elements per degree, their norms, the Newton factor 1/(2l+1), and interval-sign propagation all check. Its dictionary differentiation is safe because subtracting a fixed coordinate exponent is injective on the surviving monomials.

## Infinite tail and continuum certificate

The proof does not stop at a Taylor polynomial. On B3 in y, the Newton expansion has ratio at most 4rho, because every R_i>3/4. The degree-≥5 tail of F has sup norm at most

    T=3·4⁵ rho/epsilon.

Indeed the scalar Legendre bound |P_l(t)|≤1 gives the geometric tail, and the prefactor `(4/3)/(1−4rho)` is below 3. All sites remain outside this ball. The tail is harmonic on a neighborhood of B3.

On a unit ball centered at any point of B2, the first and second directional derivatives of the three-dimensional Poisson kernel at its center are `3(theta·v)` and `15(theta·v)²−5`. Their absolute values are bounded by 3 and 10. Thus the tail gradient and Hessian operator norms on B2 are at most 3T and 10T. This bounds the entire omitted infinite physical field, uniformly on every witness ball.

Adding the certified low-jet errors gives strict bounds

    ||grad(F−P)|| < 2^(-42),
    ||D²(F−P)|| < 2^(-41)

on B2. The independent exact values have decimal diagnostics about 1.279·10^(-13) and 4.263·10^(-13); decimal values are not used for any decision.

For a center p with H_p=D²P(p), consider `T_p(y)=y−H_p^(-1)grad F(y)`. On its radius-s ball,

    ||DT_p|| ≤ (1296s + ||D²(F−P)||)/2 < 1/128,
    ||T_p(p)−p|| ≤ ||grad(F−P)||/2 < 2^(-43).

The exact rational checks also establish displacement plus contraction times s is less than s. Hence the closed ball is mapped into itself and Banach gives one critical point in each of the seven disjoint balls. The bound ||H_p^(-1)(D²F−H_p)||<1 also makes the straight symmetric Hessian path invertible, preserving nondegeneracy and inertia. Returning to physical coordinates x=rho y preserves distinctness and inertia, and multiplying the potential by N preserves unit strengths.

This is a continuum certificate for seven actual critical points, not a finite point sampling test. Other critical points are not counted or excluded.
