This note extends incoming Theorem 2.1 from first transverse derivatives to
every fixed finite derivative order. It also checks the critical-trace and
concatenation arguments. No repository or supplied original was changed.

All profiles U,V are arbitrary C-infinity periodic maps T -> R^2, where T has
period 2*pi. Their means are NOT constrained. E denotes normalized y-average.
F:R^(2x2)->R is continuous and differentiable at the fixed background B. No
growth bound, rotational symmetry, homogeneity, convexity, or rank-one
convexity is required for the rigidity theorem. Smoothness of the storage
density means C-infinity on the whole finite-dimensional jet space.

The action and domination condition are

  L_B(U,V) = E [F(B+(V|U'))-F(B)],
  DP(U)[V] <= L_B(U,V)                         for every smooth U,V.  (D)

Equivalently, when the density is smooth, (D) is domination on all positive
duration smooth compatible strips, by the chain rule and short straight
profile paths. No limiting regularity of P on nonsmooth profiles is assumed.

THEOREM A: EVERY SMOOTH FINITE-JET DENSITY REDUCES TO FIRST ORDER

Fix an integer k>=1 and suppose

  P(U) = E p(U,U',...,U^(k)),

where p is smooth and satisfies (D). Then there are smooth A:R^2->R^2,
c:R^2->R and a smooth finite-jet H of order at most k-1 such that, identically
on the jet space,

  p(j^k U) = A(U).U' + c(U) + D_y H(j^(k-1)U).              (A1)

The last term integrates to zero for every smooth periodic U. Thus finite
higher derivatives do not enlarge the set of possible smooth local storage
functionals under universal domination.

Proof, step 1: a uniform L1 bound on the Euler derivative.

Write q_j=U^(j), with q_0=U, and let D be total y differentiation. The Euler
derivative of p is the smooth vector-valued differential expression

  e_p(j^(2k)U) = sum_(j=0)^k (-D)^j p_(q_j)(j^k U),
  DP(U)[V] = E e_p(j^(2k)U).V.                              (A2)

For R<infinity put

  M_R = max_(|q|<=R, |v|<=1) |F(B+(v|q))-F(B)| < infinity.

If ||U'||_infinity<=R, use the smooth test velocity

  V_eta(y) = e_p(y) / sqrt(|e_p(y)|^2+eta^2).

Its pointwise norm is <=1. Condition (D) gives E e_p.V_eta <= M_R. Let eta
decrease to zero, using dominated convergence for this fixed smooth U:

  E |e_p(j^(2k)U)| <= M_R.                                  (A3)

This is the crucial bound. It controls the Euler derivative independently
of every profile derivative above first order. It uses only local
boundedness of F. In particular, there is no hidden polynomial growth
assumption on either F or p.

Proof, step 2: an oscillation lemma.

Suppose a smooth differential expression has the form

  e(j^r U) = C(j^(r-1)U) U^(r) + R_0(j^(r-1)U),  r>=2,     (A4)

and has a uniform L1 bound on every set ||U'||_infinity<=R. Then C=0 at
every jet. Indeed, choose any fixed smooth periodic U_0, any h in R^2,
0<epsilon<1, and integers N tending to infinity, and set

  U_N(y)=U_0(y)+N^(-r+epsilon) h sin(Ny).

All jets through order r-1 converge uniformly to those of U_0. In
particular ||U_N'||_infinity is uniformly bounded. Dividing (A4) by
N^epsilon gives, uniformly in y,

  N^(-epsilon)e(j^r U_N)
    = C(j^(r-1)U_0(y)) h sin(Ny+r*pi/2) + o(1).

Periodic averaging of |sin| against the continuous coefficient yields

  lim_N N^(-epsilon) E|e(j^r U_N)|
    = (2/pi) E|C(j^(r-1)U_0)h|.                            (A5)

The uniform L1 bound makes the left side zero. Thus C(j^(r-1)U_0)h is
identically zero. Every finite jet at any point is realizable by a smooth
periodic map: multiply its local Taylor polynomial by a bump supported
in a coordinate interval. Hence C=0 on the whole jet space. This also
explains why the same argument cannot remove an order-one skew term:
r>=2 is needed to keep U_N' bounded.

Proof, step 3: eliminate the highest-jet Hessian.

The highest derivative in the Euler expression (A2) is linear, with

  e_p = (-1)^k p_(q_k q_k)(j^k U) U^(2k)
          + R_1(j^(2k-1)U).                               (A6)

For j<k the summand (-D)^j p_(q_j) has order at most k+j<=2k-1.
In the last summand, the only order-2k term comes from differentiating
p_(q_k) once with respect to q_k and then applying all k total derivatives
to that argument. This proves (A6), including for nonlinear smooth p.

The oscillation lemma with r=2k and (A3) gives p_(q_k q_k)=0 everywhere.
Consequently

  p(q_0,...,q_k) = a(q_0,...,q_(k-1)).q_k
                     + b(q_0,...,q_(k-1)).                (A7)

Proof, step 4: eliminate the highest affine curvature when k>=2.

Let D_(q_(k-1))a denote the matrix with entries
partial a_i / partial(q_(k-1))_j. Directly from (A2) and (A7),

  e_p = (-1)^k [D_(q_(k-1))a - (D_(q_(k-1))a)^T]
                    U^(2k-1) + R_2(j^(2k-2)U).             (A8)

Only (-D)^k a and the q_(k-1) derivative of a.q_k contribute at order
2k-1. Their coefficients are respectively (-1)^k Da and
(-1)^(k-1) Da^T, proving (A8). When k>=2, r=2k-1>=3, so the same
oscillation lemma makes the bracket zero.

For fixed lower jets q_0,...,q_(k-2), the one-form a.dq_(k-1) is therefore
closed on the entire Euclidean q_(k-1) space. A global smooth primitive is

  h(q_0,...,q_(k-1))
    = integral_0^1 a(q_0,...,q_(k-2),t q_(k-1)).q_(k-1) dt.

It satisfies h_(q_(k-1))=a. Therefore p-D_y h depends only on jets through
order k-1. The integral of D_y h is zero on each periodic profile, so the
reduced density defines exactly the same functional and still obeys (D).
Repeat this reduction until k=1. Step 3 at k=1 makes the resulting density
affine in q_1, giving (A1). QED.

THEOREM B: FIXED-BACKGROUND EXISTENCE IS EXACTLY DETERMINANT-AFFINE SUPPORT

Under the hypotheses above, the following are equivalent:

  (i) There is a dominating smooth local storage of some finite order.
 (ii) There is a real kappa such that for every X in R^(2x2),

  F(B+X)-F(B)-DF(B):X >= kappa det X.                       (B1)

If (ii) holds, a first-order storage is

  P(U)=F_1(B).E U + (kappa/2) E det(U,U').                  (B2)

Here F_1(B),F_2(B) are the derivatives with respect to the first and second
matrix columns. Thus permitting ANY fixed finite number of transverse
derivatives cannot go beyond the incoming first-order theorem.

Proof of necessity and the complete surviving first-order structure.

By Theorem A we may use p=A(z).q+c(z). Put

  kappa(z)=partial_1 A_2(z)-partial_2 A_1(z).

Integration by parts gives

  DP(U)[V] = E [grad c(U).V + kappa(U) det(V,U')].          (B3)

For U=z constant and V=t v constant, domination and differentiability of
F at B, with both signs of t, give grad c(z)=F_1(B). Thus c is affine.

Fix z_0 and arbitrary smooth v and smooth mean-zero q. Write q=w' with
w periodic, use U_N=z_0+w(Ny)/N and V_N=v(Ny), and let N tend to infinity.
The left side of (B3) converges by uniform convergence of U_N to z_0,
while the action's average is exactly unchanged by integer dilation. Hence

  E [F(B+(v|q))-F(B)-F_1(B).v-kappa(z_0)det(v,q)] >=0.     (B4)

For arbitrary v_0,q_0, choose (v,q)=(v_0,q_0) on a fraction theta of the
period and (v,q)=(0,-theta q_0/(1-theta)) on the rest. Periodic smoothing
preserves mean(q)=0 and has uniformly bounded values; continuity of F
therefore justifies this piecewise-constant test. Divide (B4) by theta and
let theta decrease to zero. Differentiability of F at B handles the small
compensating transverse column and gives

  F(B+(v_0|q_0))-F(B)-F_1(B).v_0-F_2(B).q_0
      >= kappa(z_0)det(v_0,q_0).

This is (B1). It holds for EVERY frozen z_0. In particular one may choose
one such z_0 and replace the original functional by the constant-curvature
area storage (B2).

Conversely, the first variation of (B2) is

  F_1(B).E V + kappa E det(V,U').

Apply (B1) pointwise with X=(V|U') and average. The F_2(B).U' term has
zero average. This proves domination.

A qualification that must be retained: the original surviving curvature
kappa(z) need NOT be constant. Precisely, a first-order density is
dominating if and only if grad c=F_1(B) and each value kappa(z) belongs to
the set of global coefficients allowed by (B1). Sufficiency follows by
using the appropriate coefficient pointwise at z=U(y). For example at
B=0 and F(X)=|X|^2, all |kappa|<=2 are allowed because
2|det X|<=|X|^2. Taking A_1=0, A_2=-cos(z_1) gives
kappa(z)=sin(z_1), a nonconstant valid curvature. Rigidity concerns the
existence class, not uniqueness or constancy of every supplied density.

Explicit periodic y-dependence does not enlarge the EXISTENCE class:
average P(U(.+s)) over s. Translation invariance of L_B preserves (D), and
Fubini gives an autonomous density p_bar(j)=E_y p(y,j). Theorem A then
applies to the averaged density. This does not assert that every original
y-dependent density itself equals an autonomous density plus a derivative.

COROLLARY C: ALL BACKGROUNDS ARE EQUIVALENT TO POLYCONVEXITY

Suppose F is C1 on all matrices. A dominating smooth finite-order local
storage exists at EVERY B if and only if F is polyconvex.

For the forward direction select kappa_B from (B1), and put

  ell_B(A,t)=F(B)+(DF(B)-kappa_B cof B):(A-B)
                        +kappa_B(t-det B),
  g(A,t)=sup_B ell_B(A,t).

The determinant identity det(B+X)=det B+cof B:X+det X shows
ell_B(A,det A)<=F(A); the B=A term gives equality for g on the graph.
The supremum is convex. It is also FINITE everywhere, so no extended-value
domain issue is hidden: given (A,t), choose X with det X=t-det A, for
example diag(t-det A,1). Then

  (A,t)=1/2 (A+X,det(A+X)) + 1/2 (A-X,det(A-X)).

Every ell_B is bounded above there by [F(A+X)+F(A-X)]/2. A fixed ell_B
also gives a finite lower bound. Thus g is a finite convex lift with
g(A,det A)=F(A).

Conversely, any convex lift finite on the determinant graph is finite on
its convex hull, which is the whole (A,t) space by the same two-point
construction. Choose a subgradient (M,kappa) of g at (B,det B).
Differentiability of F and the determinant expansion, tested with both
signs of a small displacement, give M+kappa cof B=DF(B).
The subgradient inequality then yields (B1), and (B2) supplies the storage.

For a homogeneous quartic with F(A_*)<0, no (B1) is possible at B=0:
t^4 F(A_*) >= kappa t^2 det A_* fails for sufficiently large positive t.
Hence NO smooth finite-jet local storage exists even at zero for the
negative-valued examples in the incoming packet.

TRACE-CONCENTRATION AUDIT

The incoming trace obstruction is sound under its stated arbitrary-width
strip domination. It does not require the endpoint quasiconvexity theorem.
For F_DM(A)=|A|^4-(4/sqrt(3))|A|^2 det A, the entry strip

  Phi(x,y)=(x/2)(cos y,sin y),  0<=x<=2,

has action 103/120-sqrt(3)/2=-delta<0. Its simultaneous x,y reversal is a
negative return from U_-(y)=(cos y,-sin y) to zero. Both are smooth on
their closed cylinders. Integer dilation and amplitude N^(-3/4) leave
these directed actions exactly equal to -delta. Thus any dominating P,
normalized by P(0)=0, satisfies

  P(U_N^+)<=-delta,  P(U_N^-)>=delta,
  U_N^+(y)=N^(-3/4)(cos Ny,sin Ny),
  U_N^-(y)=N^(-3/4)(cos Ny,-sin Ny).

For definiteness take the periodic Slobodeckij norm W^(s,4)=B^s_(4,4),
0<s<1, with circular difference h in [-pi,pi]. Up to the fixed norm
normalization its seminorm to the fourth power is EXACTLY

  16 N^(4s-3) integral_(-N*pi)^(N*pi)
                 sin^4(t/2)/|t|^(1+4s) dt.                (T1)

The integral is bounded above and below by positive constants depending
only on s: near zero its integrand is O(|t|^(3-4s)); at infinity it is
O(|t|^(-1-4s)); a fixed interval away from zero gives the positive lower
bound. Consequently the seminorm is comparable to N^(s-3/4).

For s<3/4 the sequence tends strongly to zero. At s=3/4 it is bounded and
has a nonzero seminorm scale, but its L4 norm tends to zero. The critical
space is reflexive: its difference-quotient embedding is a closed
subspace of a product of L4 spaces. Every weak subsequential limit is
zero by the strong L4 convergence. Weak sequential compactness then
gives weak convergence of the WHOLE sequence to zero. The positive and
negative bounds separately contradict weak upper and weak lower
semicontinuity at zero. No claim of strong critical discontinuity follows.

The precise trace scale is the Slobodeckij/Besov space B^(3/4)_(4,4).
Using the different Bessel-potential convention H^(3/4,4) without further
qualification is unnecessary. The incoming notation is correct when
W^(s,4) has its stated Slobodeckij meaning.

BOUNDARY AND CONNECTOR REGULARITY

1. Domination on smooth strips is sufficient for the displayed negative
   entries and returns. If a piecewise smooth connector is used, apply
   domination separately to its smooth pieces and add; matching trace
   values make the P terms telescope. No continuity of P under smoothing
   is needed for this step.

2. A closed concatenation of matching smooth profiles is continuous and
   piecewise smooth, hence W^(1,4) on the periodic cylinder; normal
   derivatives may jump at interfaces. For the polynomial quartic F_DM,
   periodic smoothing converges strongly in W^(1,4) and preserves the
   closed geometry. The estimate
   |F(A)-F(C)|<=C(|A|^3+|C|^3)|A-C|
   then gives convergence of the actions by Holder. Thus using already
   established quasiconvexity to bound a closed concatenated action is
   legitimate. This corollary, unlike the no-go inequalities above, DOES
   use endpoint quasiconvexity as a separate premise.

3. The general left-SO(2) negative-entry construction can be made smooth
   without a corner. If F(A)<0, A=(a_1|a_2) invertible, set
   n=sign det A, v=-J a_2/n, a_1=alpha v+beta Jv with alpha>0.
   The exponential spiral Phi=e^(alpha x)R_(beta x+n y)v has gradient
   e^(alpha x)R_(beta x+n y)A and action F(A)/(4alpha) on x<=0.
   To make a finite smooth entry, choose a smooth scalar chi with chi=0
   for t<=0 and chi=1 for t>=1, and use
   chi(x+T+1)e^(alpha x)R_(beta x+n y)v on [-T-1,0].
   Its cutoff energy is O(e^(-4alpha T)); its unchanged main action
   tends to F(A)/(4alpha)<0. It has exactly zero initial trace and exactly
   the desired final trace. This removes the only piecewise-regularity
   issue in that construction. Simultaneous x,y reversal gives the
   opposite-winding negative return because F(-A)=F(A).

4. The trace-energy statement can be made uniform in arbitrary strip
   width. For mean-zero U, take any finite strip extension with zero
   initial trace. Subtract its transverse mean on each slice; this
   changes neither endpoint trace and increases the derivative energy
   by at most a fixed factor. Transverse Poincare controls its L4 norm
   by its y derivative. Extend by zero across the initial boundary and
   apply the half-cylinder trace theorem at the other boundary; the
   trace constant is independent of the original width by translation.
   This gives a lower bound by c||U||_(W^(3/4,4))^4. A fixed-width trace
   right inverse with zero opposite boundary gives the matching upper
   bound. Constants are properly quotiented out: a constant trace c has
   ramp energy |c|^4/T^3, whose infimum over T is zero. Nothing in the
   explicit concentration contradiction needs this trace-equivalence
   theorem, since its strips and scaling were given directly.

OPEN OBLIGATIONS AND LIMITS

- Theorem A is an analytic argument, not a proof-assistant derivation.
  The companion exact script checks the principal Euler coefficients
  and total-derivative cancellations at finite orders only.
- Smooth density on an entire finite jet space and domination for ALL
  smooth periodic U,V are essential stated hypotheses. Restricted
  profile libraries, constrained velocities, nonsmooth densities,
  infinitely many derivatives, or genuinely nonlocal dependence are
  not covered by this theorem.
- The conclusion is about local STORAGE. It does not obstruct the
  existing nonlocal heat certificates and does not refute quasiconvexity.
- Strongly continuous nonlocal storage in the critical trace topology is
  not excluded. Neither trace concentration nor finite-jet rigidity
  constructs such a storage or proves the full invariant-cone theorem.
