RESULT A: THE LEMMA IS TRUE IN DIMENSION ONE.

First prove a gauge-tagged finite partition. There are
a=t_0<t_1<...<t_K=b and tags r_j in [t_{j-1},t_j] such that

  [t_{j-1},t_j] subset (r_j-E(r_j), r_j+E(r_j)).

This is Cousin's elementary lemma, valid for arbitrary positive E.
For completeness, if an interval has no such finite tagged partition,
one of its two equal halves also has no such partition. Iteration gives
nested bad closed intervals whose lengths tend to zero. Their common
point r lies in every interval. Since E(r)>0, a late interval is strictly
inside the E(r)-neighborhood of r and can itself be tagged r, a
contradiction. No continuity or positive global lower bound is used.

The tags are nondecreasing since r_j<=t_j<=r_{j+1}. If adjacent tags
coincide, merge the intervals: their union still lies strictly inside
the SAME E(r)-neighborhood. After merging, the tags are strictly
increasing. Define

  b_1(h)=0,
  b_{j+1}(h)=b_j(h)-(r_{j+1}-r_j) h^{t_j}.

All t_j here are internal and exceed a, so every b_j(h)=o(h^a).
For L_j(x)=r_j x+b_j(h), adjacent differences satisfy

  L_{j+1}(x)-L_j(x)=(r_{j+1}-r_j)(x-h^{t_j}).

The thresholds h^{t_j} decrease in j. Summing these differences shows
that label j is active exactly when h^{t_j}<=x<=h^{t_{j-1}}, with the
first and last intervals extended to all positive x on their exterior
side. At each threshold both adjacent labels are active. Consequently
if label j is active at x=h^{p^nu} and p^nu->p in [a,b], then p lies
in [t_{j-1},t_j]. Thus |p-r_j|<E(r_j), INCLUDING the boundary ties and
exterior approaches required by the exact source statement.

This removes the discontinuous-gauge objection in m=1. The offsets may
be negative, which the exact statement allows. The family is fixed and
finite; no h-dependent regridding occurs.

RESULT B: A SEPARABLE PRODUCT-PARTITION PROOF FAILS IN m=2.

For a<b choose 0<epsilon<(b-a)/2, and define

  E(s,t)=epsilon if s=t,
         |s-t|/100 if s!=t.

This is everywhere positive. Any label r=(s,t) with s!=t cannot be
allowed active at ANY diagonal limit p=(q,q):

  ||p-r||_infinity >= |s-t|/2 > E(r).

Suppose a construction's limiting active cells are the closed
rectangles of a finite Cartesian product of two interval partitions,
and every Cartesian label is active on its entire closed rectangle
(as for a separable sum of one-dimensional affine minima). Each such
rectangle that meets the diagonal must have a diagonal tag (s=t).
At every breakpoint q in the first-coordinate partition, both adjacent
intervals meet some second-coordinate interval at (q,q). If q is a
breakpoint in the second partition, all four adjacent rectangles meet
there. Thus the two adjacent first-coordinate tags must equal the same
second-coordinate tag. Induction makes ALL first-coordinate tags equal;
the same argument handles the second coordinate. A single common tag
cannot be within epsilon of every q in [a,b]. Contradiction.

Therefore even an adaptive fixed PRODUCT grid cannot prove the stated
arbitrary-tolerance lemma. Boundary ties are load-bearing here. This
does NOT refute a nonseparable finite affine-minimum construction.
