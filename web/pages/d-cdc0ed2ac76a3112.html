## Primary literature consulted

1. S. Schuster and T. Höfer, “Determining all extreme semi-positive conservation relations in chemical reaction systems: a test criterion for conservativity,” *J. Chem. Soc., Faraday Trans.* 87 (1991), 2561–2566. [Publisher page](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/1991/ft/ft9918702561). The paper gives an algorithmic characterization of positive conservation relations and conservativity. This supports the left-kernel preprocessing, not the safety conclusion.
2. A. De Martino, D. De Martino, R. Mulet, and A. Pagnani, “Identifying All Moiety Conservation Laws in Genome-Scale Metabolic Networks,” *PLOS ONE* 9(7) (2014), e100750. [Primary article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0100750). It grounds conservation vectors in genome-scale metabolism, reports that identifying all nonnegative integer moieties is an integer optimization problem with NP-hard worst-case complexity, and notes that cofactors including ATP, ADP, AMP, NADH and NADPH do not generically appear in conservation relationships in genome-scale reconstructions. This is a material acquisition/modeling obstacle: no adenylate or redox invariant should be assumed without auditing the exact reaction boundary.
3. I. Domenzain et al., “Reconstruction of a catalogue of genome-scale metabolic models with enzymatic constraints using GECKO 2.0,” *Nature Communications* 13, 3766 (2022). [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-31421-1). It integrates enzyme and absolute-proteomics constraints, including shared protein-pool limits and condition-dependent exchange fluxes. The authors explicitly note biological/technical variability in proteomics and that constraints may need loosening; exchange-flux data are also typically sparse. This motivates, but does not supply, the uncertain joint allocation set $K(x)$; it does not provide stochastic rates, first-passage bounds, or real-time feedback constraints.
4. M. Rabe and S. Schewe, “Finite Optimal Control for Time-Bounded Reachability in CTMDPs and Continuous-Time Markov Games,” [arXiv:1004.4005](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4005). The abstract establishes finite-horizon optimal scheduling for CTMDPs and timed-positional structure. The finite-state Bellman representation here is a model-specific application of that established route, not a novelty claim.
5. A. Agazzi, A. Dembo, and J.-P. Eckmann, “Large deviations theory for Markov jump models of chemical reaction networks,” [arXiv:1701.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126). Their abstract proves sample-path LDP and Wentzell–Freidlin asymptotics for a class of mass-action jump networks. It is prior art for exponential transition times under its assumptions; it does not supply the exact coupled-allocation certificate derived here.
6. A. Agazzi, A. Dembo, and J.-P. Eckmann, “On the geometry of chemical reaction networks: Lyapunov function and large deviations,” [arXiv:1710.07240](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240). The authors develop a geometric Lyapunov/large-deviation account based on reaction-polytope structure. This is a mechanism antecedent, not a direct proof of our conditional support-function bound.
