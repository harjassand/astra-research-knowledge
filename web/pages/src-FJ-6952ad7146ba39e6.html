## Closest direct-flow prior art: flow tunnelling

The note cites Coquinot et al., “Momentum tunnelling between nanoscale liquid flows,” arXiv:2501.01253v1, later listed on arXiv with a Nature Nanotechnology (2025) journal reference and DOI 10.1038/s41565-024-01842-8. I read the primary arXiv abstract page and the complete HTML manuscript including its supplementary-information sections. The paper is closer prior art for *neutral-liquid-to-neutral-liquid* remote flow than for the proposed electronic terminal transducer: it predicts a pressure-driven water film inducing a steady flow in a second water film across a stationary multilayer solid by fluid–solid–fluid momentum transfer.

Its strong numerical example is explicitly simulation/model evidence, not a fabricated device: non-equilibrium molecular dynamics with a Drude-oscillator representation reports about `v_B/v_A = 0.1` across a 2 nm, seven-layer wall for `omega_p = 0.1 THz` and `gamma = 0.001 THz`; induced velocity was linear in the simulated drive range. The supplementary methods state that flow was imposed by applying an external force to water molecules, and that velocity was averaged over about 10 ns after at least 1 ns equilibration. The paper's realistic-graphene estimate is also model-based: using `omega_p ≈ 100 THz`, `gamma ≈ 0.6 THz`, it estimates a hydron mean free path around 4 nm and predicts non-negligible tunnelling through a 10 nm wall, while warning that the estimate may be low because perpendicular electron transport is omitted. None of these results measures actual-device K, a neutral-liquid/electronic C, terminal G, electrical work, or conversion efficiency.

This prior art narrows any novelty statement: stationary-wall inter-liquid flow coupling is not a new general concept (the paper itself cites Andreev and Meierovich, 1971), and the 2025 paper gives a modern theory/simulation route. The candidate composition can at most claim the specific remote electronic-network arrangement and operating contract, pending a historical search and experiment. The direct flow-tunnelling route is a potentially cleaner *observable* for a neutral-flow coupling demonstrator, because the claimed output is flow in port B rather than an unmeasured electronic cross-coefficient. It is not yet a stronger established or obtainable device operation: its cited performance remains simulation/theory, the numerical example relies on a stylized Drude spectrum and low damping, and the realistic-graphene result is an extrapolation.

## Practical operation assessment

For a near-term, experimentally demonstrated flow-to-electrical information signal, the WGT device already summarized in the frozen report remains the stronger obtainable operation: it reports a flow-dependent voltage/current signal but requires a biased transistor and an immersed Ag/AgCl reference, so it is a sensor, not a passive wet work transducer. This recovered archive adds no stronger measured neutral-liquid generator or reciprocal voltage-to-volume-flow actuator. If a direct neutral-liquid coupling experiment is preferred after the electronic wet gate fails, flow tunnelling is a distinct follow-up hypothesis: measure steady flow on both sides of a sealed, characterized multilayer wall while pressure-driving only one side, with an impermeable-wall/pinhole-leak check. A positive result would not satisfy the candidate's electrical Q,I gate or establish hydraulic-to-electric work conversion.
