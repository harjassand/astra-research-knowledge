STRONGEST SUPPORTED CLAIM AT THIS CHECKPOINT

The existing internally audited universal, fully separable one-observation decoder is

  S_N(x) = tau_N(x)^{tensor N},
  tau_N(x) = exp(2 N^(-1/4) x.sigma)/Tr exp(2 N^(-1/4) x.sigma),
  p_(A,b)(x) proportional to exp[-4|x|^4/3 + x^T A x + b.x].

For fixed public bounds ||A||<=M and |b|<=B, its whole-density trace-distance error tends to zero uniformly and has the proved rate O_(M,B)(N^(-1/4)). The known-parameter separable approximation with rate O(N^(-3/2)) is a distinct construction and does not establish this parameter-independent decoder rate.

The claim follows from the audited certificate (20) in work/cycle1/spin_full_separability_audit.txt. Its terms are: the O(N^(-3/2)) semigroup-versus-Gibbs trace defect; the filter-replacement term 4 sqrt(G2 K2_N) N^(-1/4); the integrated scalar expectation bound; and the weighted prior discrepancy Rprior_N. The moment constants G2,K2_N are bounded for fixed M,B. The scalar prior term is separately O(N^(-1/2)) by Addendum C6, so the N^(-1/4) filter/scalar bounds govern the published rate.

Derivation and present rate bottleneck:

1. The exact isotropic seed has scaled density q_N,M(x) proportional to sinch(2N^(-1/4)|x|) exp[N logcosh(2N^(-1/4)|x|)-2 sqrt(N)|x|^2/d0], with d0=1-M/(2 sqrt(N)). Its limit is q_inf,M proportional to exp[-4|x|^4/3-M|x|^2]. Compact Taylor expansion, quartic/Gaussian envelopes, and normalization give weighted L1 error O_(M)(N^(-1/2)); this is the C6 expansion.
2. The separable positive semigroup has a local 2x2 stochastic filter g. Under the trace-weighted mixture, w=[Tr(g tau_N(x) g*)]^N. Jensen gives E w>=1 and the central p-replica bound gives E w^2=O_(M,B)(1), so the weighted law is well-defined with a uniform second moment.
3. For each filter, the normalized qubit tau_g=g tau g*/Tr(g tau g*) obeys T(tau^{tensor N},tau_g^{tensor N})<=4 sqrt(N)||g-I||. The Ito estimate gives E||g-I||^2<=G2 N^(-3/2). Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz then yields T(sigma,chi_N)<=4 sqrt(G2 K2_N)N^(-1/4). This is the decisive existing loss.
4. In the scalar weight comparison, C4 bounds the centered quadratic/linear collective fluctuation by its standard deviation O(N^(-1/4)). That use of absolute first moment may also discard cancellation; C6 already shows the base-prior Taylor remainder is smaller, O(N^(-1/2)).

Independent rate-attack hypothesis, NOT a theorem at this checkpoint: in the rescaled Bloch coordinate x, the leading filter displacement is of order N^(-1/2), with conditional mean linear in the score parameters. A first-order expansion of tau_N(x+delta x)^{tensor N} has a trace-norm derivative of order N^(1/4), explaining the current N^(-1/4) bound. After integrating over the smooth quartic-tilted density, integration by parts could move that first derivative onto the density and reduce its contribution to O(N^(-1/2)); the second-order displacement term is also plausibly O(N^(-1/2)). The missing gate is a uniform operator-valued Taylor expansion for the weighted matrix-filter law, with integrable derivative/tail bounds and no uncharged rare-event term. I have not established the leading coefficient, cancellation, improved rate, or sharpness.
