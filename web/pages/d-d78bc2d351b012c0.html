# Failed cubulation shortcut — precise negative findings

This follows the first bounded report. It does not prove that the candidate group is noncubulable, virtually nonspecial, or nonsofic. Its purpose is to test whether a published separability theorem contradicts the source geometry. No such applicable contradiction was obtained.

## Exact published separability interface

Haglund–Wise, *Special Cube Complexes*, Theorem7.3 and Corollary7.4, author-hosted corrected PDF pp24–25, prove quasiconvex subgroup separability for compact hyperbolic special, respectively virtually special, cube complexes. Lemma7.5 transfers it over finite-index extensions. The source complex is a triangle complex; the derivation of convex label trees establishes no cube structure or specialness. The theorem therefore cannot presently be applied.

## Two elementary conversion failures

**Subdivide x and reinterpret every triangle as a square.** Subdivide each x_(i,u,v) edge at M_(i,u,v); a source triangle then has four boundary edges O→V→M→W→O. Suppose x_(i,u,v) belongs to at least two distinct triangles, as occurs for labels incident to at least two regular points. At M, every such square gives a distinct link edge between the same two directions (towards V and W). Two parallel link edges form a cycle of angular lengthπ, violating the CAT(0) square-complex link criterion. Thus this natural square replacement is not a nonpositively curved cube complex. This says nothing about other group actions on cube complexes. It is deliberately not asserted for x-edges having fewer than two incident triangles.

**Three-square subdivision of each triangle.** The familiar subdivision using edge midpoints and a triangle centre replaces a triangle by three quadrilaterals. At its centre the link is a three-cycle, of angular length3π/2 for unit Euclidean squares. This also violates the square-complex link criterion. Merely knowing that any triangle complex has a square subdivision does not supply a CAT(0) cubulation.

These are exact local failures, independent of the nonsofic residual premise. They rule out the obvious conversions, not cubulation in general.

## Cone graphs and why C(4) does not supply the missing theorem

Split the original vertex O into one apex O_b for every Cartesian block b. The resulting complex is a graph Θ (all x-edges between V and W) with immersed cone attachments. For block b the cone's base Γ_b has left vertices (u,j), right vertices (v,k), and one edge for each pair. Its map to Θ labels that edge by the unique i determined by

    α_b(i)=j−v mod s_b,   β_b(i)=k−u mod t_b.

Hence Γ_b≅K_(r s_b,r t_b), with its edge labelled x_(i,u,v). The map is an immersion: fixing a vertex and the x-edge label determines the opposite endpoint. Every label i appears once for each pair u,v, and its Γ_b subgraph is a matching. Different blocks at one point have disjoint label sets. Different points share at most one label, because an incidence four-cycle has been excluded. Thus any nontrivial common essential path between different cone graphs has at most one edge. Each Γ_b has girth4. This reconstructs the graphical C(4) premise. A full disk-diagram certification of the associated T-condition was not completed in this follow-up; it is unnecessary for the negative conclusion below.

The graphical C(4) observation is not a cubulation theorem. Chalopin–Chepoi–Genevois–Hirai–Osajda, *Helly groups*, primary arXiv2002.06895v3 (2023), §1.3 p7 explicitly distinguishes the stronger B(4)–T(4) cubulation result from the C(4)–T(4) class. Their Definition6.14 p45 and Corollary6.20 p48 concern graphical C(4)–T(4) complexes and Helly actions. A Helly action supplies no virtual-specialness conclusion. The paper also includes all hyperbolic groups as Helly groups, so that label alone changes no residual-finiteness question. The paper's historical statement that a cubulation question was open in2023 is not asserted as independently confirmed current in2026.

There is also a local wall warning. In K_(a,b) with a,b≥3, the equivalence relation generated by declaring opposite edges of every four-cycle equivalent has a single class. Two edges in different rows and columns are already opposite; two with a shared row or column are connected using an intermediate edge whose row and column avoid both. Consequently that canonical opposite-edge wall relation cannot distinguish vertices in the same part. This defeats the most immediate wall construction. It does not rule out other wall systems, cubical subdivisions, or arbitrary cubulations, and no unverified claim that all B(4) hypotheses fail is made.
