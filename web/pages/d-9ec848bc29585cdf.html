# Exact three-variable comparator: Ciani quartics and implemented automorphism recovery

Worker `metric_prior`, 2026-10-07. This closes the fixed-three-variable prior boundary. It does not settle variable-dimension acquisition or establish measured runtime superiority. Primary algorithm code was read and preserved; Magma was not run or installed.

## Exact match and primary locations

Every promised rational ternary quartic

\[
f(x)=m^TQm,\quad m_i=(Lx)_i^2,\quad L\in GL_3(\mathbb Q),\quad Q\succ0
\]

defines a smooth plane quartic over C. Its three hidden sign changes give a projective Klein four group defined over Q. This is a **Ciani quartic**, rather than merely a tensor-symmetry analogy.

Lachaud–Ritzenthaler, arXiv:0710.3303v1 (submitted 2007-10-17), §2.1 and Proposition2.1.1, PDFpp3–4, state and prove the rational Ciani normal form once two commuting involutions defined over the field are available. The proof scales projective involution matrices to square to I, then simultaneously diagonalizes them over that field. Proposition2.2.1, PDFp5, gives the discriminant as a nonzero constant times

\[
q_{11}q_{22}q_{33}\prod_{i<j}(q_{ii}q_{jj}-q_{ij}^2)^2\det(Q)^4.
\]

Thus QPD satisfies its smoothness conditions on **every** input. Proposition2.3.2, PDFp6, constructs a rational (2,2,2)-isogeny from the Jacobian to a product of three elliptic curves. These are direct symbolic-geometry uses of the acquired coordinate reflections.

The source's supplied-involution proof does not itself find the involutions from expanded coefficients. That missing step is covered at fixed dimension by the established plane-quartic automorphism algorithms below.

The [Magma handbook](https://docs.magma-maths.org/ArithmeticGeometry/PlaneQuartics/quartic_isomorphisms.html), opening paragraphs and `AutomorphismsOfTernaryQuartic`, documents rational as well as geometric automorphism recovery. It uses van Rijnswou's covariant method when I12 is nonzero, with direct Gröbner elimination for the exceptional cases. The [v2.26 release notes](https://docs.magma-maths.org/releasenotes/2.26.html), dated April2021, §ArithmeticGeometry/PlaneQuarticCurves, already advertise automorphisms over either the base field or its algebraic closure. Therefore this is established prior, independent of a recently changed webpage.

## Reconstructed complete direct acquisition baseline

The direct algorithm solves for projective matrices M such that f(Mx) is proportional to f(x). Its first matrix row is normalized in three disjoint charts:

\[
(0,0,1),\qquad (0,1,a),\qquad (1,a,b).
\]

Write p(M) for the fifteen degree-four monomial coefficients of f(Mx), and q for those of f. The source sets every 2×2 minor of the two-row matrix [q;p(M)] to zero and adds

\[
s\det M-1=0.
\]

There are at most nine affine variables, including s, and each equation has degree at most four. Rational point enumeration returns the automorphisms defined over Q; its geometric variant returns points over a splitting field. The code checks the full coefficient proportionality for every returned M. No Euclidean metric is supplied or used.

The projective solution set is finite because the curve is smooth of genus3; the standard genus3 automorphism bound is 168. Alternatively, the independently reconstructed Lie-algebra argument for QPD already proves finiteness, without needing that numerical bound. This makes the fallback a zero-dimensional algebraic problem of constant dimension and degree. Standard exact fixed-dimensional elimination followed by rational factor/root extraction supplies a complete finite baseline with polynomial coefficient-bit cost for this fixed interface. This is a statement about the reconstructed theoretical comparator, **not** a proved complexity bound or empirical runtime for Magma's implementation. Expanding this scheme to n variables changes the number of matrix unknowns to n² and does not give polynomial variable-n cost.

Now convert the acquired rational projective automorphisms to a rational frame. For each projective involution represented by B, check B²=αI and that B is not scalar. In dimension3 its two eigenspace dimensions are 2 and1, so

\[
(\operatorname{tr}B)^2=\alpha\ne0,\qquad R=B/\operatorname{tr}B
\]

is a rational reflection with R²=I and rank(I−R)=1. Since f(Rx)=c f(x), involutivity gives c²=1. Positivity of the promised quartic on every nonzero real x gives c>0; hence c=1. This avoids adjoining a fourth root to normalize a projective matrix into a linear f-automorphism.

Scan distinct commuting rational reflections R1,R2. Put E_i=(I−R_i)/2. Distinct commuting rank-one projectors satisfy E1E2=0, and E3=I−E1−E2 also has rank one. Choose one nonzero rational column from each E_i to form an invertible matrix V. Then

\[
R_1V=V\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1),\qquad
R_2V=V\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1).
\]

The transformed quartic f(Vy) is even in y1,y2 and, by its even total degree, in y3. Read its six coefficients into a candidate Q'. Check Q' positive definite and the exact polynomial identity. Return L'=V^{-1}, axis columns V, and rational SPD metric M=L'^T L'. Multiplying M by its denominator gives an integral SPD aligned form; no integral/unimodular orthonormal basis is claimed.

Completeness follows because the hidden rational coordinate reflections occur among the acquired automorphisms and give at least one accepted pair. Other commuting involutions can produce even frames whose Q' fails the PD promise; the verifier must retain this rejection. The axis-identifiability theorem in `COMPLEX_MONOMIAL_AUTOMORPHISMS.md` implies every accepted QPD frame has the promised unordered axes. No ordering or original scale is recovered.

`work/metric_prior/ciani_involution_bridge.py` checks this rational bridge on six mixed QPD fixtures and a rational mixed frame of the Fermat quartic that must fail the QPD check. These are bridge checks with supplied fixture matrices, not runs of the automorphism-acquisition solver.

## Generic covariants and historical monomial boundary

The fast three-linear-covariant method is unsuitable for this whole family: its Klein four sign subgroup has no invariant linear form, so determinant-weighted linear covariants vanish under the determinant-one sign subgroup. The source explicitly rank-tests that covariant basis and dispatches on failure. This does **not** reject its conic/binary method or the complete fallback.

Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling, arXiv:1403.0562v1 (submitted 2014-03-03), §3.2 PDFpp5–6 and Appendix6.1 Theorem6.1(3), PDFp15, describe the usual Ciani family and the monomial normalizer D(K)⋊S3 of its **specified** Klein four subgroup. On the stratum with automorphism group exactly that subgroup, all isomorphisms lie in this normalizer. The paper excludes singular curves and larger automorphism groups when using those strata. This establishes the generic normalizer prior, but does not automatically prove the all-QPD complex-monomial theorem: exceptional Ciani quartics may have additional Klein four subgroups and larger automorphism groups. QPD's strict maximum-modulus argument is the separate universal gate in our note.

Both old arXiv PDFs have compilation dates later than their submission/version dates: the retrieved 2007v1 PDF prints October31,2018 and its metadata says November25,2021; the retrieved 2014v1 PDF prints December6,2024. Recoverable bytes, version-labelled URLs, and date discrepancies are retained. Recompilation timestamps are not treated as a new result or recent source date.

## Consequence and next falsifiable comparison

Exact rational acquisition in n=2 has the classical binary sextic comparator; n=3 has this established automorphism-plus-Ciani comparator; n=4 has the recent all-smooth polar/quasi-Galois certificate and an exhaustive algebraic baseline. The parent route's potential contribution must therefore be stated in variable dimension, with its current generic or special-family coverage and complete bit charges. No speedup, all-QPD extactic nonvanishing, or historical novelty follows from this comparison.

An informative experiment would run the acquired-coefficients routes on identical mixed n=3 exact inputs, including Fermat and I12=0 cases, then report algebraic factor/solve time and coefficient height. No such runtime comparison was performed here.

## Quotient torsor caveat (cycle10)

For QPD Ciani quartics the real projective curve has no point. A degree-two genus-one quotient can therefore be a torsor with no rational point, and must not be called a rationally isomorphic elliptic curve merely because its Jacobian has an elliptic model. The Lachaud–Ritzenthaler proposition is used here for the degree-eight Jacobian isogeny to the product of quotient Jacobians. For the Fermat fixture the quotient is v²=−u⁴−1, while its Jacobian is Y²=X³−4X. Worker `metric_use10` independently preserved exact identities and finite-field checks in `results/metric_use10/downstream_bridge.json`; these are downstream verification, not a new Ciani/isogeny claim.
