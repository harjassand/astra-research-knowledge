## Exact question, source premises, and claims

Let F be any characteristic-zero field, n,m >= 1, and let a noncommutative polynomial have coefficient realization

\[
 f_w=uB_wv,\qquad B_{i_1\cdots i_k}=B_{i_1}\cdots B_{i_k},
\]

with u a constant row, v a constant column, and B_i strictly upper triangular m-by-m matrices. An m-vertex acyclic ABP with affine-linear edge labels has this realization after eliminating scalar edges. The generator receives only n,m. It does not receive the representation, support, or target coefficients.

For d >= 1 define the rational matrices

\[
 T_i(r,q)=\begin{cases}
 (-1)^{r-q-1}/(r i^{r-q}),&0\le q<r<d,\\
 0,&q\ge r.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]

The released manuscript, author OpenAI, dated 24 September 2026, pinned commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`, proves first-column detection at

\[
 d_{\rm col}=(n-1)\binom m2+m.
\]

Its formula model is an ordered fan-in-two division-free **tree**, with all leaves and gates counted, and its representation lemma gives m <= 2s. Its finite hyperlogarithm faithfulness lemma and representation lemma are the source dependencies of the deduction below. The scalar Moura multiplicity theorem and Lean declaration are not substitutes for the new proof.

**Our acyclic deduction:** the same tuple at d = 2nm detects every nonzero polynomial with that m-state acyclic representation, using all matrix entries. Thus d = 4ns suffices uniformly for the source's size-s formula trees. The theorem does not improve the first-column dimension to 2nm.

**Our cyclic deduction:** every proper recognizable formal series with an arbitrary m-state realization is detected, using all entries, by

\[
 W_i=S_{m+1}\otimes T_i^{(2nm)},\qquad
 E=2nm(m+1),
 \tag{2}
\]

where S_{m+1} is multiplication by lambda modulo lambda^(m+1). These matrices are jointly nilpotent, so every proper recognizable realization has a defined finite matrix evaluation. Arbitrary rational-expression syntax and its individual inverse domains are a separate interface; they are not silently identified with proper-series semantics.

For small parameters the older d_col can be smaller than 2nm. The new statements are asymptotic improvements and do not claim pointwise superiority over every existing tuple. A complete-response detector may use the better available dimension. In particular, m=5,n=2 gives d_col=15 versus 2nm=20; the example below uses the new expression for a transparent replay, not an optimized detector.

## Independent acyclic derivation

Define formal integration operators

\[
 J_i a(z)=\int_0^z\frac{a(t)}{t+i}\,dt,
 \qquad h_w=J_w1.
\]

Every J_i increases z-adic order by at least one, and (1) is exactly its matrix on F[z]/(z^d) in the column basis 1,z,...,z^(d-1). The source's finite-word faithfulness says that finite collections of h_w are F-linearly independent. This worker reconstructed its truncated-free-algebra monodromy proof and the finite nonzero rational minor transfer to every characteristic-zero field in the earlier audit.

Put

\[
 A(z)=\sum_i B_i/(z+i)=P(z)/q(z),\qquad
 q(z)=\prod_i(z+i),\qquad \deg P\le n-1.
\]

Here q(0)=n! is nonzero. With

\[
 G(z)=\sum_{|w|<m}B_wh_w(z),
\]

the left-letter convention gives G'=AG and G(0)=I. For any seed a(z), the nonempty-word sum H_a= sum_(1<=|w|<m) B_w J_w a has

\[
 H_a'=AH_a+A aI,\qquad H_a(0)=0.
\]

Consequently, formal variation of constants gives

\[
 f(J)a=(uv)a(z)+\int_0^z K(z,t)a(t)\,dt,
 \qquad K(z,t)=uG(z)G(t)^{-1}A(t)v.
 \tag{3}
\]

If uv != 0, the diagonal of f(T) detects it. Assume uv=0. Finite faithfulness gives f(J)1 != 0 for nonzero f, so K != 0. All coefficients of K belong to F[[z,t]], because A is regular at zero and G(t) is invertible there.

Define rational covariant derivative rows

\[
 R_0=u,\qquad R_{j+1}=R_j'+R_jA.
\]

Induction gives (uG)^(j)=R_jG, and

\[
 R_j=Z_j/q^j,\quad
 Z_{j+1}=qZ_j'-j q'Z_j+Z_jP,\quad
 \deg Z_j\le j(n-1).
 \tag{4}
\]

Since nonzero f requires u != 0, choose the first rational dependence R_k in span_(F(z)){R_0,...,R_(k-1)}. It has k <= m. That span is stable under R -> R'+RA: the first k-1 generators shift to the next generator, the last shifts to a dependent row, and the Leibniz rule handles rational coefficients. Thus every R_j is in the same rational span. Possible poles in the dependence coefficients do not cause a problem: the implication being used is a rational identity, while the actual R_j and the quantities below remain regular at zero.

The diagonal derivative identity is

\[
 \left.\partial_z^jK(z,t)\right|_{z=t}
 =R_j(t)A(t)v=:b_j(t).
 \tag{5}
\]

If all b_j for j<k were zero, rational span closure would make every b_j zero. The formal Taylor expansion K(t+x,t)=sum_j b_j(t)x^j/j! would then vanish. The linear substitution (z,t)=(t+x,t) is an invertible substitution of formal power series, contradicting K != 0. Therefore some j<k<=m has b_j != 0.

By (4), b_j has denominator q^(j+1), a unit at zero, and a nonzero numerator Z_j P v of degree at most (j+1)(n-1). Thus its t-order is at most that degree. Equation (5) yields a nonzero term in K(t+x,t) of total degree at most

\[
 j+(j+1)(n-1)=n(j+1)-1\le nm-1.
 \tag{6}
\]

An invertible linear variable change preserves the first nonzero total homogeneous degree, so K(z,t) has a first nonzero homogeneous part of degree L <= nm-1. Write it as

\[
 K_L(z,t)=\sum_{b=0}^{L}c_b z^{L-b}t^b,
 \qquad c\ne0.
\]

Let ell=L+1. Applying (3) to t^q gives

\[
 [z^{q+\ell}]f(J)z^q
 =\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{c_b}{q+b+1}.
 \tag{7}
\]

Only homogeneous degree L contributes to that output degree. For q=0,...,ell-1 the linear map in (7) is the ell-by-ell Cauchy matrix 1/(q+b+1), which is invertible over every characteristic-zero field. For example, a rational function sum_b c_b/(X+b+1) with those ell distinct zeros has numerator degree at most ell-1, forcing it to be identically zero; evaluation at its distinct poles then forces every c_b=0.

At least one entry f(T)_(q+ell,q) is therefore nonzero. Its row is at most 2ell-1 <= 2nm-1, retained at d=2nm. This proves the complete-response deduction.

## A proper cyclic series killed by the entire unmarked tuple

The original cyclic example only killed one seed. There is a stronger obstruction. For two distinct letters A,B, define the proper free rational series

\[
 R=AB-B(I+A)^{-1}A(I+B).
 \tag{8}
\]

Its leading homogeneous part is AB-BA, so R != 0. A five-state realization has states 0,...,4 and edges

\[
 0\to1:A,\quad1\to3:B,\quad0\to2:B,\quad
 2\to4:-A,\quad4\to4:-A,\quad4\to3:B,
\]

with u=e_0^T and v=e_3+e_4. Its negative branch produces (-1)^ell B A^ell and (-1)^ell B A^ell B for ell>=1, exactly the expansion of (8).

For any i,j >= 1, set A=J_i and B=J_j. The topologically nilpotent operator J_i makes (I+J_i)^(-1) well defined as a formal geometric series. Put C=(I+J_i)^(-1)J_i. For every a(z), y=Ca solves

\[
 y'+y/(z+i)=a/(z+i),\qquad y(0)=0,
\]

and hence

\[
 Ca=(z+i)^{-1}\int_0^z a(t)\,dt.
\]

Direct differentiation also gives

\[
 \int_0^z (a+J_j a)(t)\,dt=(z+j)J_j a(z).
\]

Therefore C(I+J_j)a=((z+j)/(z+i))J_j a. The derivative of J_j C(I+J_j)a is J_j a/(z+i), exactly the derivative of J_iJ_j a, and both have initial value zero. Thus

\[
 J_iJ_j=J_j(I+J_i)^{-1}J_i(I+J_j).
 \tag{9}
\]

Equation (8) consequently vanishes as a **complete operator on all seeds**, and R(T_i,T_j)=0 for every truncation dimension. All displayed inverse domains are legal on the nilpotent matrix tuple: I+T_i is invertible. No inverse of J_i itself is invoked.

This is a genuine rational relation among these integration operators, consistent with their first-order differential origin. It blocks extending acyclic full-response faithfulness to arbitrary unmarked proper cyclic series. It does not refute the acyclic theorem or imply that arbitrary matrix tuples fail.

The exact replay `check_full_operator_alias.py` verifies full expression and independently specified five-state realization matrices at all d=1,...,20. A legal control A=E_(0,1), B=E_(1,2) in dimension 3 gives R=E_(0,2), proving that the collision belongs to the chosen generator. It also checks the reversal orientation: R_rev(T^T)=R(T)^T. A draft harness assertion incorrectly equated R(T^T) with R(T)^T; this was corrected, and the correction is retained in `FULL_OPERATOR_ALIAS_CHECK.json` rather than hidden. This test failure concerns a draft transpose assertion, not equation (9).

## Short marker repair for arbitrary proper recognizable series

Let R=sum_w uB_wv x_w now have arbitrary, possibly cyclic, m-state transitions. Introduce an indeterminate lambda and define

\[
 G_\lambda(z)=\sum_w\lambda^{|w|}B_wh_w(z).
\]

The coefficient of z^r is polynomial in lambda of degree at most r, so this is well defined even with cycles. It satisfies G_lambda'=lambda A_0 G_lambda, G_lambda(0)=I, where A_0=P_0/q as before. For each fixed homogeneous word length k, the coefficient of lambda^k in uG_lambda v is the hyperlogarithm image of the finite homogeneous polynomial R_k. If R != 0, some such coefficient is nonzero by finite faithfulness. Thus uG_lambda v != 0 over F(lambda)[[z]].

Assume uv=0; the nonzero constant case is already detected by the diagonal. The same kernel (3), with A=lambda A_0, is nonzero. The covariant rows have

\[
 R_j=Z_j/q^j,\qquad
 \deg_z Z_j\le j(n-1),\qquad \deg_\lambda Z_j\le j.
\]

The recurrence (4) proves both degree bounds because only its final product contains the extra lambda. Some j<m has

\[
 b_j(t,\lambda)
 =Z_j(t,\lambda)\lambda P_0(t)v/q(t)^{j+1}\ne0.
 \tag{10}
\]

Its lambda-degree is at most j+1 <= m. Choose a nonzero lambda^h coefficient, h <= m. Its t-numerator degree is at most (j+1)(n-1), and its denominator is a unit, so the corresponding coefficient of K_lambda(t+x,t) has a nonzero x^j t^a term with total degree at most nm-1. Hence **that specific K_h** has a nonzero first homogeneous part of degree L <= nm-1. The Cauchy calculation (7) detects its coefficient lambda^h in some entry of R(lambda T^(2nm)).

This establishes existence of a detected coefficient with h <= m. It does **not** assert that every retained response entry has lambda-degree <= m. Replacing lambda by S_(m+1) preserves the selected coefficient and kills higher ones. This is applied to polynomial response entries and their regular representation, not through an invalid field homomorphism F(lambda) to an algebra with nilpotents. It proves (2).

Root's additional sharpening reduces the alternative scalar grid to **m** tuples:

\[
 (\ell T_1^{(2nm)},...,\ell T_n^{(2nm)}),\qquad
 \ell=1,...,m.
 \tag{11}
\]

The numerator N=Z_j lambda P_0 v of (10) is divisible by lambda, and N/lambda has lambda-degree at most j<=m-1. Choose one nonzero t-coefficient in N/lambda. It cannot vanish at all m distinct nonzero integers 1,...,m, so b_j(ell,t) is nonzero at some grid point. The explicit b_j, G and kernel can be specialized without poles in lambda; the rational row-dependence coefficients are never specialized. Its t-order bound and Cauchy extraction prove a nonzero full response. Every query is jointly nilpotent and has defined proper-series semantics. A single direct-sum tuple therefore has dimension 2nm^2, but separate grid queries return fewer redundant matrix entries.

For the alias (8), the lambda^2 coefficient of R(lambda T_1,lambda T_2) is T_1T_2-T_2T_1. At row 3,column 0 it equals -1/24. The replay checks this exact factorized coefficient. With n=2,m=5,d0=20 and marker height m+1=6, the corresponding entry of the 120-dimensional tuple is row 2*d0+3=43,column 0. The wider d0-height regular representation is also a legal check. The shortened marker changes the retained block count, not this particular coefficient or entry. Direct scalar-grid replay gives row 3,column 0 equal to ell^2(ell-1)/24 for ell=1,...,5: the entire ell=1 response is zero, while all remaining grid points detect the alias.

## Output-only acquisition: correct extraction and complete tradeoff

Let U be a complete-response detector of dimension E for the relevant state-bound class. Reversal preserves a proper m-state recognizable realization by transposing transitions and exchanging u,v. Therefore

\[
 \Phi(g)=g(U_1^T,...,U_n^T)
\]

is injective on the linear space of residual series of the hidden target. Linear combinations of residuals share the same hidden transitions and only change their initial row, preserving the m-state promise. Flatten all E^2 matrix entries for Gaussian elimination. The residual g_w has coefficients g_w(v)=R(wv).

For a prefix w of length k, append k prefix blocks of width E to a suffix block containing C_i=U_i^T. Put I_E on the requested-letter edge between successive prefix blocks, with all other prefix edges zero. A path from the first block to the suffix block must spell w before entering the suffix; the returned block is exactly R_w(C). Every query is still jointly nilpotent. This gives one full feature using a query of dimension (k+1)E.

The old first-column learner's residual-span BFS and minimality proof now apply to these full matrix features: add independent residuals, extend selected prefixes by all n letters, and skip descendants of a truly dependent residual because derivatives preserve dependencies. At most r<=m basis words are selected; the total query count is at most 1+nr and prefix lengths at most r. The maximum query dimension is (r+1)E, feature length E^2, and constant terms can be read from any feature diagonal entry. The learned weighted automaton has the minimal Hankel rank r. This uses the classical residual/Hankel learning principle; the new component is the exact generator-based compilation of residual features.

There is a second correct tradeoff. Use scalar prefix chains and bridge the last edge to each suffix seed row e_a^T, a=0,...,E-1. Each query returns one row of R_w(C) at dimension E+k. All E rows recover the full feature. The empty-prefix matrix requires only one query, so the total is at most 1+E nr queries, maximum dimension E+r. It is invalid to substitute the smaller full-response detector into the original learner and continue reading only one row.

For acyclic polynomials E=2nm. For arbitrary proper m-state series the shorter marked E=2nm(m+1). Thus full-width cyclic marked extraction has maximum dimension O(nm^3), while seed extraction has O(nm^2) dimension but O(n^2m^3) queries. These are cost tradeoffs, not a demonstrated total runtime advantage. The original tested cyclic first-column learner has E=d_col^2=O(n^2m^4), feature length E and at most 1+nr scalar-prefix queries of size E+r. Different feature sizes, entire returned matrices, encoding costs, hidden-oracle work, exact elimination and response bit lengths remain charged.

The smaller scalar-grid interface (11) permits the following more useful ledger. Let M be the detector state bound, d=2nM, and r<=m be the hidden minimal rank. Native residual-span comparisons may use M=m because they all share the hidden transitions. Comparing the hidden target with an unrelated m-state hypothesis requires M=2m, and the conservative choice M=2m also works throughout learning. Full features now concatenate M separate d-by-d responses, rather than interpreting one row as sufficient.

| Charged quantity | Full-width prefix chains | Individual scalar-seed chains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grid points | M | M |
| Retained feature length | M d^2 | M d^2 |
| Exact output queries | <= M(1+nr) | <= M(1+d nr) |
| Maximum query dimension | <= (r+1)d | <= d+r |
| Fully returned scalar entries, safe upper bound | M(1+nr)(r+1)^2 d^2 | M(1+d nr)(d+r)^2 |
| Dense input scalar entries, safe upper bound | n times returned-entry bound | n times returned-entry bound |

At M=2m, d=4nm and the full-width learner uses at most 2m(1+nr) queries, each of dimension at most 4nm(r+1), retaining 32n^2m^3 field elements per feature. The scalar-seed alternative uses at most 2m(1+4nm nr) queries and dimension at most 4nm+r. These explicit counts meet an unrelated-hypothesis comparison bound of 2m. For native learning M=m halves the grid and d: at most m(1+nr) queries, dimension at most 2nm(r+1), feature length 4n^2m^3. Prefix chains have nilpotency depth at most d+r despite their wider ambient matrix dimensions.

Over Q, the generator descriptions have polynomial bit length. Exact response processing is polynomial in n,m and total encoded response length. A bound H on hidden rational realization entries yields a polynomial response-height bound because joint nilpotency makes every evaluation a finite sum of polynomially many state/matrix recurrences of polynomial depth; the learner does not need H supplied. Without such a bound, or an explicitly charged response height, n,m alone cannot bound bit cost. No numerical stability, sample-based simulation, nonnegative realization or statistical learning guarantee follows.

The original first-column implementation and its independent `frontier_crossfield` replay remain separate evidence. The new script `reconstruct_scalar_grid.py` implements the **full-width** scalar-grid learner using only the output oracle,n,m, with an optional conservative M=2m detector bound. It charges all returned and described matrix entries and rational response heights. Its replay includes a two-state cyclic source at both M=m and M=2m, and the reversed five-state alias that vanishes on the upper unscaled tuple. Equality with each learned realization is checked on every word by a separate finite reachable-space closure. The individual scalar-seed extraction alternative is derived but not implemented here. None of these finite runs proves the universal detector or supplies external validation.

## Lower bounds, nearest prior art, and remaining question

For binary homogeneous degree 2t, every coefficient tensor has an acyclic representation with m_t=3*2^t-2 vertices: a prefix trie of depth t-1, a reversed suffix trie of depth t, and independently chosen affine-linear bridge labels. The coefficient space has dimension 4^t. A fixed seed feature f -> f(T)e in F^d must have d>=4^t=(m_t+2)^2/9 to detect this whole space. A complete response in Mat_d only requires d^2>=4^t, hence d>=(m_t+2)/3. The new full-response theorem is therefore order-optimal in m at fixed binary alphabet, up to constants, while the source's quadratic first-column dependence is also of the correct order for that different interface.

The exact prior comparison in the earlier source audit remains controlling: Arvind–Mukhopadhyay–Srinivasan 2008 has deterministic polynomial ABP reconstruction with an any-gate matrix oracle; Forbes–Shpilka arXiv1209.2408v2 Theorem 1.5 already has output-only NC ABP same-class reconstruction in randomized polynomial or deterministic quasipolynomial time, using a quasipolynomial hitting set. Classical exact weighted-automaton learning uses membership and counterexample equivalence oracles. Output-only reconstruction and residual-span BFS are therefore not new by themselves. Historical priority of this specific linear full-response tuple, short-marker proper-series extension and exact-query compilation has not been established.

There is also an explicit old-primitives cyclic comparator. For a nonzero m-state proper recognizable series, some nonzero coefficient has word length at most m-1: the reachable row spaces generated by words of increasing length start in dimension at most one, increase until they stabilize, and once stable remain invariant under all transitions. If v annihilates all rows up through length m-1, it annihilates the full reachable space and the series is zero. Truncating at length m-1 can be unrolled into at most m^2 acyclic states. The old Forbes–Shpilka nilpotent quasipolynomial hitting construction, at matrix dimension sufficient to kill all longer words, therefore gives a quasipolynomial proper-series detector and composes with residual features. This is a deduction from old primitives, also recorded in `metric_prior`'s matched comparison; it prevents treating cyclic exact matrix-oracle capability itself as new. The candidate improvement here is polynomial cost and this particular explicit construction.

Concrete downstream use: a deterministic exact universal detector for bounded acyclic ABPs, and a deterministic exact residual-feature oracle for proper recognizable series with a supplied state bound. This creates an acquisition primitive under a stronger matrix-query interface than ordinary word samples. It does not solve teacher-free statistical HMM acquisition or commutative PIT, and it requires no claim of broad research-programme resolution.

The best next falsifiable step is a primary-source search for this precise operator/full-matrix dimension result and recognizable-series compilation, then an independent benchmark comparing both extraction tradeoffs under a meaningful exact matrix oracle. A counterexample to a claimed kernel degree bound or marker truncation would be decisive; finite rank experiments alone cannot establish the universal theorem.
