RATIONAL TREE-CUT CONSTRUCTION AND ACQUIRED SUPPORT-SEPARATOR CLASSES
c03_s03 independent post-INITIAL attack, 7 October 2026.
Complete author derivation and exact reference implementation; independent
verification and target-specific priority remain pending.

THEOREM / SUPPLIED VERSUS ACQUIRED

For F in Q(i)^(n x n), let T be a binary tree whose leaves partition the physical
sites. For each subtree site set S define
 c(S)=rank F[S,S^c]+rank F[S^c,S], D(S)=2^c(S).
There is a deterministic acquired rational tree tensor representation of the
unfiltered BCS amplitude, with bond dimensions D(S). For D=max_S D(S), it gives
exact norms in every canonical pair sector, arbitrary nonnegative rational
onsite squared filters, exact physical-prefix counts, norm-zero detection and
exact expected-time conditional Born samples, with polynomial bit cost in
n,D,L. Uniform c(S)<=C log_2(n+1), fixed public C, makes all these costs
polynomial in binary input. All matrix phases and cancellations are retained.

A supplied tree is a charged polynomial-size certificate and is explicitly
verified; existence of an unknown optimal tree is not an acquired input.
The following automatic construction removes that certificate boundary for
important admitted classes:
 - Off-diagonal support forests: acquired binary tree with c(S)<=2.
 - Successful recursive balanced support separators of fixed public size b:
   acquired tree with c(S)<=2b(ceil(log_2 n)+1). Search costs n^(b+O(1)); no
   separator oracle is used. Every support graph of treewidth<=b-1 succeeds.
These constructions are implemented. Admission can also use a prescribed
balanced order tree or INITIAL's exact ordered-prefix rank test. General
optimal algebraic tree-width recognition is not claimed.

COMPLETE TREE-TENSOR DERIVATION

1. Order all sites by the tree's left-to-right leaf order. This is an acquired
permutation. In this order every subtree is contiguous. Reorder F rows and
columns identically. Born weights are unchanged; the code also tracks the
exact fermionic occupation-dependent phase when returning original-order
amplitudes. Moving sites i,j across each other contributes (-1)^(N_i N_j), so
the total sign is the inversion parity of the occupied mode permutation.

2. For a subtree S, acquire U as a column basis of F[S,S^c] and V as a column
basis of F[S^c,S]^T. Define
 L_S[A,B]=exp(G_SS) product_{alpha in A} U_alpha(up)
                         product_{beta in B} V_beta(down).
The same commuting cross-generator expansion as INITIAL shows this is the
exact unfiltered Schmidt support, with dimension 2^c(S), and gives a full
rational basis. For an interior contiguous S there is a fermionic ordering
point: moving S to the first tensor factor introduces (-1)^(N_S N_before).
The global BCS state has even parity, hence N_S=N_outside modulo 2. This sign
is a diagonal unitary on the OUTSIDE factor alone, so it does not change the
ordinary site-tensor Schmidt support. This justifies using ordinary tree
contractions rather than silently ignoring a Jordan–Wigner string.

3. Choose independent physical up/down pivot rows of U,V, as in INITIAL. The
evaluation matrix K_S on all occupations of those c(S) pivot modes is square
and invertible: restricted exterior linear changes are invertible, as is
restricted multiplication by exp G_SS. No eigenvectors, numerical ranks,
orthogonalization, small singular-value promise, or full Fock-vector oracle
is required.

4. If S is the disjoint ordered union of children A,B, then
 span L_S subset span L_A tensor span L_B.
For any finite pure coefficient tensor, its support on AB is contained in the
tensor product of its marginal supports on A and B: a functional annihilating
one marginal support annihilates the full tensor, and these annihilators span
the complement of the product subspace. Equivalently, expand the global
coefficient tensor in bases of both child Schmidt supports. Therefore each
L_S column has a unique expansion in L_A tensor L_B.

Evaluate its columns on the unions of child pivot occupations. Let H_S be the
matrix of these evaluations, rows indexed by y_A,y_B and columns by parent
basis labels. Since the two child intervals are ordered and disjoint, their
ordinary tensor coefficients on these rows multiply. The acquired transition
tensor is the matrix

 T_S=(K_A^(-1) tensor K_B^(-1)) H_S.                        (T)

Each H query uses at most c(A)+c(B) physical modes. At a leaf, evaluate L_S on
its four local occupations directly. At the root c=0 and the single basis
column is exp G. Induction in (T) therefore reconstructs the exact full BCS
amplitude. Returning to the original site order adds the explicit occupied
site inversion phase above. The implementation's amplitude tests check this
phase against independent complex-rational determinants.

EXACT COUNTING AND SAMPLING

For local physical weights w_i(x) and charge u(x), a leaf Gram coefficient is
 E_S[q]_[b,c]=sum_{x:u(x)=q} conj(L_S[b](x)) w_i(x) L_S[c](x).
At an internal node the coefficient convolution is

 E_S[q]=sum_{q_A+q_B=q} T_S^* (E_A[q_A] tensor E_B[q_B]) T_S.       (C)

Every matrix is a Gram matrix. The root coefficient is the exact real
nonnegative norm c_q. As before, equal up/down number is already enforced by
the Gaussian amplitudes, so up charge alone specifies the physical pair
sector. Local hard/soft zeros and forbidden prefix occupations occur directly
in leaf weights. In particular an original-order prefix can span several
tree branches without changing the representation or introducing an oracle.

For one exact sample in original physical order, maintain permitted local
occupation sets. At each site contract (C) under each of its four possible
new restrictions, and draw from the resulting exact rational masses. Their
sum is the current completion norm, so the chain rule proves the exact
conditional Born law. Past restrictions are fixed; initial caller restrictions
are respected. Sector zero is decided before any draw. The current reference
sampler uses at most 4n acquired tree contractions rather than a more advanced
tree-conditional cache. Its polynomial cost is charged below. Integer draws
use unbiased random bits with expected fewer than two range-rejection attempts;
default SystemRandom is an OS bit interface, while seeded PRNG calls in checks
are reproducibility fixtures. No backend entropy certification is claimed.

COMPUTATION / BIT / MEMORY COSTS

Let c=max c(S), D=2^c and input bit length L. All cut bases/pivots cost O(n^4)
rational operations by naive exact elimination over the O(n) tree nodes.
At an internal node there are at most D^3 entries of H; a query on at most 2c
modes costs O(c^2 2^(2c)) arithmetic. Dense Kronecker and matrix operations in
(T) give conservative acquisition cost
 O(n^4+n D^5(c+1)^2).
Leaf tensors have physical dimension four, and their cut ranks are at most two.

Each coefficient-pair contraction in (C) costs O(D^5) arithmetic. The number
of coefficient pairs summed across the tree is O(n^2): sum over nodes of
|A||B| counts each ordered leaf pair at its least common ancestor, and the
additional linear/constant terms are at most O(n^2). Therefore one all-sector
norm/prefix pass costs O(n^2 D^5). The reference four-way prefix sampler costs
O(n^3 D^5) per draw, plus polynomial exact random-bit cost. Keeping all node
coefficient matrices uses O(n^2 D^2) rational storage; transitions use at most
O(n D^3). The implementation uses explicit postorder loops for leaf collection,
tensor acquisition and contraction, including unbalanced forest trees.

Exterior evaluation entries have polynomial heights in c,L. The child pivot
inverses have heights polynomial in D,c,L by adjugate/Hadamard bounds. Every
tree tensor therefore has polynomial(n,D,L) bit heights. A complete contraction
has O(n) tensor factors and a common denominator formed from their denominators
and local table denominators; the number of paths adds O(n log(4D)) bits.
Consequently all exact rational intermediate values have polynomial bit length
in n,D,L. These are binary bit bounds, not numerical inverse norm bounds.
The reference max_dimension cap rejects exceeded resources; it makes no
unreported truncation. Fixed logarithmic width guarantees a polynomial theorem,
not a small practical exponent or an affordable arbitrary input run.

ACQUIRED FOREST DECOMPOSITION

Build the undirected support graph with edge ij whenever F_ij or F_ji is
nonzero, ignoring diagonal loops. Recognize a forest by DFS, O(n^2) in the
explicit matrix representation. Root each component. Recursively obtain each
child-subtree partition; combine the complete child subtrees in a binary tree,
then merge their union with the parent site's leaf. Merge distinct components.

Every constructed cluster is either a complete rooted subtree, or a union of
complete child subtrees at one vertex. In the former case its support cut has
only the parent edge. In the latter all crossing edges meet the common parent
vertex. Hence each directed cross block has rank<=1, so c(S)<=2. A component
union has rank zero. Arbitrary diagonal pairing entries and all t_i>=0 remain
allowed. The resulting unconditional exact algorithm upgrades implementation
on this graph class without invoking the conditional bipartite Slater FPRAS.

ACQUIRED BALANCED-SEPARATOR DECOMPOSITION

Fix a public integer b>=1. At a current induced support graph on S, enumerate
all subsets X of size<=b until every component of S\X has size<=|S|/2. If no
candidate works, emit the specific induced site set as a failed-separator
certificate. Recurse independently on the components, then combine all whole
component trees and singleton X vertices into a binary tree. At one call there
are O(n^b) candidates and O(n^2) graph work per candidate; at most O(n) calls
give the safe O(n^(b+3)) bound. The component recursion has depth<=ceil(log_2 n).

Every intermediate cluster from binary grouping crosses the rest only through
the current separator and ancestor separators. Thus these crossing edges have
a vertex cover of size<=b(ceil(log_2 n)+1). A cross matrix whose nonzero support
is covered by v physical vertices splits into its covered rows and its covered
outside columns, and has rank<=v. The reverse block has the same bound, giving

 c(S)<=2b(ceil(log_2 n)+1), D<=constant_b n^(2b).             (B)

Both the tree and its exact actual ranks are acquired, so acceptance is an
explicitly recognized structural class. The bound gives a high but finite
polynomial counting/sampling algorithm for each fixed b. It can admit graphs
of larger treewidth; success is not labeled an exact treewidth certificate.

Why all treewidth<=b-1 support graphs succeed: take any tree decomposition
whose bags have size<=b. Assign each physical vertex to one bag containing it,
and take a weighted centroid bag of that decomposition tree. Its deletion
leaves graph components of size<=|S|/2, since every remaining connected component
lies within a single branch of the bag tree and that branch has at most half
the assigned weight. Induced subgraphs preserve this bag-size bound by
intersecting all bags with their sites. Therefore every recursive call has an
enumerated legal separator. No decomposition was supplied to the algorithm;
this argument proves that its deterministic search succeeds on the stated
class. A rejection is a search/recognition boundary, not approximation hardness.

DELIVERED EVIDENCE AND LIMITS

treecut_bcs.py implements acquired forest/separator partitions, supplied and
prescribed balanced trees, exact rational tensors, norms/prefix counts and
conditional samples in original site order. check_treecut_bcs.py tests the
actual tensor amplitudes against determinant evaluation, not merely norms.
checks_tree.json records 3328 amplitude comparisons, 12 full norm vectors,
20 prefix vectors and conditional sample support checks, all PASS. It includes
noncontiguous original tree labels, directed triangular pairing, soft complex
forest diagonals, a nonbipartite triangle/chain support, n=12 dense full-rank
semiseparable counts, and an exact K6 failed-separator certificate at b=2.
The latter means the declared recognizer rejected that case, not that K6 is
hard or that the algebraic width engine cannot admit it with another resource cap.

The broader tree-cut representation is a standard tensor-network mechanism;
Gaussian MPS/TTN conversion and local projection have established prior art.
INITIAL records primary Gaussian-conversion sources. The present rational
pivot gauge, determinant-pair admission test, bit accounting and automatic
support-separator specializations are independently constructed here; exact
target-specific priority is unverified. No claim of a field-breaking result,
general parity FPRAS, quantum circuit implementation or hardware execution is
made. High algebraic width remains exponential by these methods. The shared
frontier's even-windable and microscopic replica obstructions are not bypassed
for arbitrary explicit F.
