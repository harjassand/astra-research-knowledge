1. EXACT INTERFACE AND ENERGY CONVENTION
There are finitely many bosonic modes but each Fock space is infinite dimensional.
Use [q_j,p_k]=i delta_jk, vacuum covariance I/2, a=(q+ip)/sqrt2, and
  N=sum a_j^dagger a_j, E=Tr rho N=(tr V+||d||^2-m)/2.
Split Ec=(tr V-m)/2 and Ed=||d||^2/2. Both are nonnegative for physical V.
Project a selected set H onto a SPECIFIED Fock pattern of total h photons, with
positive probability; keep any selected other modes, optionally discard more.
No mixture over an unbounded number of herald photons, nonGaussian initial
state, arbitrary filter, or post-herald active operation is covered.
The result is a conditional photon/energy promise, not increased laboratory
success probability or a classical algorithm for arbitrary-phase Gaussian states.

Claim audited (Q(E)=E^2+3E):
  conditional mean <=3h+6(2h+1)E+3Q(E),
  T_delta=ceil(24(1+2E)[(h+1)ln2/2+ln(1/delta)]+6Q(E)ln2),
  Pr(N>=T_delta)<=delta, 0<delta<1.
Total retained N is bounded by total N in a chosen purification, so discarding
additional modes contracts the photon-tail event. The charged E is the original
FULL physical input energy, including displaced herald modes.

2. VACUUM-CONDITIONED COVARIANCE/DISPLACEMENT ANCHOR: RECOMPUTED
Let C=V_RH, D=V_H+I/2, B=C D^-1. The vacuum-conditioned Gaussian factor has
  V0=V_R-C D^-1 C^T, d0=d_R-B d_H.
The original positive covariance with I/2 added to the H block stays positive.
Its Schur complement yields C D^-1 C^T<=V_R. Since D>=I/2,
  B B^T=C D^-2 C^T<=2C D^-1 C^T<=2V_R.
This inequality does NOT replace the physical covariance by a classical one;
positive real covariance is enough for this particular algebraic step.

For physical m-mode V, lambda_max(V)<=2Ec+1. To verify without importing a
trace variational theorem, take a maximal unit eigenvector x. A passive real
symplectic orthogonal rotation sends it to q1 and its conjugate to p1. The q1,p1
covariance vanishes because x is an eigenvector and x^T Omega x=0. Robertson
therefore gives Var(p1)>=1/[4 lambda]. Every remaining marginal mode has sum of
its q,p variances >=1. Thus
  tr V >=lambda+1/(4lambda)+(m-1),
  2Ec+1>=lambda+1/(4lambda)>=lambda.
The passive unitary group acts transitively on the real unit sphere, so the
rotation used here is legal and preserves tr V. A principal V_R has no larger
operator norm, hence ||B||^2<=4Ec+2.

The vacuum factor covariance energy satisfies Ec0<=Ec: V0<=V_R and each
original marginal covariance energy is >=0. Its displacement energy obeys
  Ed0=||[I,-B](d_R,d_H)||^2/2
      <=(1+||B||^2)Ed <=(4Ec+3)Ed.
The first block norm is exactly sqrt(1+||B||^2); no factor of mode count occurs.
Therefore
  E0<=Ec+(4Ec+3)Ed<=E^2+3E,
using 4EcEd<=(Ec+Ed)^2 and Ec+3Ed<=3E. The same upper bounds Ed0 alone.
All signs and vacuum factors match the author's convention.

3. CANONICAL PURIFICATION: SEPARATE ENERGY ACCOUNTING PASSES
The vacuum factor sigma may be mixed. A canonical Gaussian purification has
retained covariance V0, mean d0, and environment thermal partners with occupations
n_j=nu_j-1/2, where nu_j are sigma's symplectic eigenvalues. Set environmental
mean zero and unnecessary environmental modes to vacuum.
Its Bogoliubov vacuum squeezing energy is
  es=Ec0+sum_j n_j<=2Ec0<=2Ec,
and its displacement energy is exactly ed=Ed0<=(4Ec+3)Ed<=Q(E).

Here sum n_j<=Ec0 is sound. A direct proof: in Williamson form V0=S diag(nu,nu)
S^T, write the real symplectic column pairs x_j,y_j. Their symplectic pairing
is one, so ||x_j|| ||y_j||>=1 and ||x_j||^2+||y_j||^2>=2. Hence
tr V0=sum_j nu_j(||x_j||^2+||y_j||^2)>=2sum_j nu_j.
Subtract the retained mode count and divide by two.
Displacement contributes to ed, not es. Folding ed into es would unnecessarily
produce an E^2*h term and a worse tail; the author's separation avoids that.

Canonical purification does not require retaining a high-energy original
environment. All pure Gaussian purifications of sigma are Gaussian-unitarily
equivalent on their environments after padding with vacuum modes. This is the
only Gaussian purification normal-form import; its standard finite-mode scope
matches the use here. It does not assume sigma itself is pure.

4. POLYNOMIAL EXCITATION REPRESENTATION: INFINITE-FOCK AUDIT
Take a finite-mode pure Gaussian purification of the original input. In Bargmann
form its vector is exp(1/2 z^T A z+g^T z) times a nonzero scalar, ||A||<1.
Fock projection in H differentiates a total of h times in those variables and
sets them to zero. The surviving vector is
  P_h(z_R,z_env) G0(z_R,z_env), degree(P_h)<=h,
where G0 is precisely a pure Gaussian purification of the vacuum-conditioned
factor. A feasible herald means this vector is nonzero; normalization can have
arbitrarily large coefficients and this does not change degree.

A Gaussian unitary W on the environment takes G0 to the canonical purification.
Conjugation of each creation operator is affine linear in creators/annihilators.
Normal ordering uses only scalar CCR contractions, which LOWER degree; acting
an annihilator on a Gaussian factor gives an affine linear polynomial times that
factor. Thus the W-transformed polynomial factor still has degree at most h.
Finally undo the full Gaussian unitary U preparing the canonical factor. Affine
ladder conjugation and normal ordering on vacuum produce a normalized chi with
TOTAL photon support <=h. The actual conditional purification is U chi.
This is not a claim that the output state has finite photon support: U generally
spreads chi through infinitely many Fock levels. Finite MODE count remains crucial.

Domain justification, absent from the initial compact prose but repairable:
For any polynomial P and finite-mode pure Gaussian factor with ||A||<1, choose
t>0 so e^t||A||<1. Bargmann scaling z->e^(t/2)z leaves a normalizable Gaussian
times polynomial. Therefore <PG0,e^(tN)PG0> is finite, so PG0 lies in every N^k
domain. Gaussian affine-ladder conjugation is valid on these vectors. Equivalently,
all finite-particle chi are in the domains of every power of U^dagger N U.
The support ladder identities are genuine infinite-Fock identities, not artifacts
of finite matrix truncation. The author agreed to add this clarification.

5. QUADRATIC-LADDER CONSTANTS: RECOMPUTED TERM BY TERM
Write U^dagger a U=u a+v a^dagger+alpha, es=||v||F^2, ed=||alpha||^2.
Canonical relations give ||u||op<=sqrt(1+es), ||v||op<=sqrt(es).
For normalized chi supported on <=r photons, the vector annihilation/creation
norms give
  ||(u a+v a^dagger+alpha)chi||
    <=sqrt[r(1+es)]+sqrt[(r+1)es]+sqrt(ed).
Squaring by (x+y+z)^2<=3(x^2+y^2+z^2) yields mean
  <=3r+3(2r+1)es+3ed.

For N'=U^dagger N U, exact normal ordering gives
  a^dagger H a + a^dagger Q a^dagger + a^T Q* a
       +beta^T a^dagger+beta^dagger a +es+ed,
with bounds
  ||H||op<=1+2es, ||Q||F<=sqrt(es(1+es)),
  ||beta||<=2sqrt((1+es)ed).
The pair-annihilation column has squared norm N(N-1); its adjoint gives pair
creation. Consequently their operator norms on the r sector are bounded by
sqrt(r(r-1))||Q||F and sqrt((r+1)(r+2))||Q||F. This DOES NOT introduce dimension
factors or conflate a scalar pair operator with independent creation outputs.

Summing the terms and using
  sqrt(r(r-1))+sqrt((r+1)(r+2))<=2(r+1),
  sqrt(es(1+es))<=es+1/2,
  4sqrt[(r+1)(1+es)ed]<=2(r+1)(1+es)+2ed,
gives exactly
  ||N' P<=r|| <=4r+3+(6r+5)es+3ed
              <=6(r+1)(1+es)+3ed.
N' raises total photon support by at most 2.

6. MOMENTS AND MGF: NO INVALID NONCOMMUTATIVE ORDER STEP
The proof does not raise an operator inequality to its kth power. Instead iterate
the norm estimate on the actual successive support sectors r=h+2j:
  ||(N')^k chi||
   <=[12(1+es)]^k (alpha)_k,
  alpha=(h+1)/2+ed/[4(1+es)].
For the positive self-adjoint N', its scalar moment is nonnegative and
  <chi,(N')^k chi><=||(N')^k chi||.
The latter is ordinary Cauchy-Schwarz, not a moment-order conjecture. On scalar
number spectra, exp(tN)=sum t^kN^k/k! with nonnegative terms; Tonelli/spectral
monotone convergence justifies summation. Thus
  Tr tau exp(tN)<=(1-12(1+es)t)^(-alpha)
for 0<=t<[12(1+es)]^-1.
At t=[24(1+es)]^-1, Chernoff gives
  Pr(N>=T)<=exp[-T/(24(1+es))+alpha ln2].
Taking
  T=24(1+es)[(h+1)ln2/2+ln(1/delta)]+6ed ln2
and bounding es<=2E, ed<=Q(E) proves the advertised cutoff. Substituting r=h
in the separate mean estimate proves the advertised mean. There is no herald
probability in any of these normalized-vector bounds.

7. QUADRATIC WITNESS: EXACT PHYSICAL CHECK AND STRONGER CUTOFF NECESSITY
Start from two-mode squeezed vacuum with thermal n=1. Before displacement its
blocks are V_H=1.5I, V_R=1.5diag(s^2,s^-2), C=sqrt2 diag(s,-s^-1).
Set d_H=(s,0), d_R=0. These are produced by legal local Gaussian operations.
Then
  E=(5/4)s^2+(3/4)s^-2+1/2,
  p_vac=.5 exp(-s^2/4),
  V0=.5diag(s^2,s^-2), d0=(-s^2/sqrt2,0),
  M:=E_vac=s^4/4+(s^2+s^-2-2)/4.
Thus M/E^2->4/25 while p_vac becomes arbitrarily small. This invalidates any
universal O(E) vacuum-herald mean theorem for arbitrary phases/displacements.

More strongly, Wick/ladder calculation gives the exact conditional variance
  Var(N)=s^6/4+(s^4+s^-4-2)/8.
Hence Var(N)/M^2->0. For any fixed eta in (0,1), Chebyshev yields
  Pr[N<(1-eta)M]<=Var(N)/(eta^2 M^2)->0.
Any sequence of cutoffs T=o(E^2) therefore has omitted conditional mass->1.
The E^2 term is necessary for a fixed-error cutoff, not merely the mean.
This strengthening was derived independently during the audit and sent to author.

8. h*E WITNESS AND LOG-FAILURE NECESSITY
A second family proves the hE mean/cutoff contribution is also real. Start with
the same TMSV n=1 but d=0, apply retained squeeze s, and herald exactly h photons
in the unsqueezed partner. The remaining pure state is S(s)|h> and herald
probability is 2^[-(h+1)]. Put a=(s^2+s^-2-2)/4. Then
  original E=3a+2,
  conditional M_h=h+(2h+1)a,
  conditional Var(N)=2(h^2+h+1)a(a+1).
For h>=1,a>=0, Var/M_h^2<=1: the difference of the squared mean and variance is
  (2a^2+2a+1)h^2+2a^2h-a^2-2a>=3a^2+1>0.
Thus
Paley-Zygmund gives Pr(N>=M_h/2)>=1/8. Thus for any target delta<1/8, a uniform
cutoff below M_h/2 fails. As h,a grow, M_h/(hE)->2/3, proving Omega(hE) in a
fixed-error regime. To separate the h term from E, take TMSV with thermal
occupation eta>0, no retained local squeeze. Its total E=2eta can approach zero,
while an h-photon partner herald leaves exactly |h>, necessitating an h cutoff.
Finally, a retained thermal Gaussian of mean E without nontrivial herald has
Pr(N>=T)=[E/(1+E)]^ceil(T). This necessitates the E*ln(1/delta) scale when E is
large, and ln(1/delta) for fixed nonzero E. These are separate worst-case regimes;
I do not claim a sharp constant or uniform lower bound for every E,h,delta.
