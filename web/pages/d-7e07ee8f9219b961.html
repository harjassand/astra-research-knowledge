SPECTRAL GEOMETRY — CYCLE 2: RANK-CONSTRAINED CASIMIR PROFILES
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; novelty and priority UNKNOWN.

MAIN RESULT

There is a direct rank-versus-spectrum principle for invariant Dirichlet
forms on the matrix coefficients of an irreducible compact-group
representation. The key quantity is the partial trace of a low-energy
spectral projector. Schur's lemma makes that partial trace scalar, and
Schmidt rank then limits how much mass a low-rank matrix can put in the
low-energy band. Applied to Sym^N(C^m), this gives a matching rank-Q
Casimir-energy law of order N Q^(-1/(m-1)) throughout an explicit
small-rank range. This is a rigorous cross-representation extension of the
spin-j mechanism. It is a candidate Faber–Krahn-type inequality, not a
novelty claim or an operational memory theorem.

1. GENERAL RANK–SPECTRAL PROFILE THEOREM

Let G be compact, π:G→U(H) an irreducible unitary representation with
d=dim H, and L≥0 a self-adjoint operator on HS(H) that commutes with the
conjugation action X↦π(g)Xπ(g)^*. For t≥0 put

  Π_t = 1_[0,t](L),       D(t)=Tr Π_t.

For every X∈HS(H) with ||X||_HS=1 and rank X≤Q,

  <vec(X), Π_t vec(X)> ≤ min{1, Q D(t)/d}.             (1.1)

Consequently,

  <X,LX> ≥ Φ_π(Q)
       := ∫_0^∞ [1−Q D(t)/d]_+ dt.                     (1.2)

In particular, for every λ≥0,

  <X,LX> ≥ λ [1−Q D(λ)/d]_+.                           (1.3)

Proof. Use vec(X)=Σ_{i,j}X_{ij}e_i⊗ē_j, so vec is an isometry
HS(H)→H⊗H̄, its Schmidt rank is rank(X), and its first reduced operator
is XX*. The projector Π_t commutes with π⊗π̄. Let P be the projection
onto ran(X), so rank(P)≤Q and
|vec(X)><vec(X)|≤P⊗I. The partial trace Γ_t=Tr_2 Π_t commutes with π.
Irreducibility and Schur's lemma give Γ_t=(D(t)/d)I. Therefore

  <vec(X),Π_t vec(X)> ≤ Tr[(P⊗I)Π_t]
                         = Tr(P Γ_t) ≤ QD(t)/d.

The left side is also at most one. If Z is the spectral random variable
of L in the state vec(X), then E Z=∫_0^∞ Pr(Z>t)dt. Since
Pr(Z≤t)≤min{1,QD(t)/d}, integration proves (1.2). Restricting the
integral to [0,λ], where D(t)≤D(λ), proves (1.3). No log-Sobolev,
Poincaré, or KLS premise is used.

For reducible π the scalar D(t)/d is generally false. The exact replacement
from this argument is the Ky Fan quantity

  κ_Q(t)=sum of the Q largest eigenvalues of Γ_t=Tr_2 Π_t,

with κ_Q(t)=Tr Γ_t if Q≥d. Then low-band mass is at most
min{1,κ_Q(t)}, and the integrated bound is
∫[1−κ_Q(t)]_+dt. The weaker Q||Γ_t||op also works.
The Ky Fan bound is sharp for the relaxation to an arbitrary rank-Q range
projection P in Tr(PΓ_t); for a fixed Π_t it need not be attained by an
actual Schmidt-rank-Q vector. It remains a valid universal upper bound.

More explicitly, if H=direct-sum_λ(V_λ⊗C^{a_λ}) is the isotypic
decomposition, then Γ_t belongs to the commutant and has block form
direct-sum_λ(I_{V_λ}⊗B_{λ,t}) for positive matrices B_{λ,t} on the
multiplicity spaces. Each eigenvalue of B_{λ,t} is repeated dim(V_λ) times
in Γ_t. The Ky Fan sum uses these repeated eigenvalues; replacing it by
D(t)/dim(H) loses the actual multiplicity profile.

Reducible-carrier counterexample. Take H=1⊕W, where W is an irreducible
representation of dimension r>1. For the conjugation Casimir, the zero
eigenspace is span{vec(P_1), vec(P_W/√r)}. Thus D(0)=2 but

  Γ_0=Tr_2 Π_0=P_1+P_W/r,

whose largest eigenvalue is 1, not D(0)/dim(H)=2/(r+1). The rank-one
matrix X=P_1 has zero energy and low-band mass one. Hence no universal
QD/d projector-mass formula holds for reducible carriers; the partial-trace
or Ky Fan profile is essential.

2. EXACT SU(m) SYMMETRIC-POWER SPECTRUM

Fix m≥2, let d=m−1, and let H_N=Sym^N(C^m), with

  d_N=dim H_N=binom(N+d,d).

Use Hermitian su(m) generators normalized by
Tr_fund(T_a T_b)=δ_ab/2, and define

  L(X)=sum_a [T_a,[T_a,X]],
  E(X)=<X,LX>=sum_a ||[T_a,X]||_HS^2.

The conjugation representation on End(H_N) is multiplicity-free:

  End(H_N) = direct-sum_{k=0}^N H_{k,k},
  c_k=k(k+m−1),
  dim H_{k,k}=binom(k+d,d)^2−binom(k+d−1,d)^2,
  D_K=sum_{k=0}^K dim H_{k,k}=binom(K+d,d)^2.       (2.1)

Here binom(d−1,d)=0 in the k=0 term. One derivation identifies End(H_N)
with bihomogeneous polynomials of bidegree (N,N). Fischer decomposition for
Δ=Σ_i ∂_{z_i}∂_{bar z_i} splits this space as
Σ_{k=0}^N ||z||^{2(N−k)} H_{k,k}; the harmonic bidegree-(k,k) component
is the irreducible highest-weight representation k(ω_1+ω_{m−1}). Its
dimension is the difference of the dimensions of bidegrees (k,k) and
(k−1,k−1), since Fischer decomposition makes Δ onto the lower bidegree.
With long roots of squared length 2, the generator convention above gives
C_2(λ)=(λ,λ+2ρ)/2. For λ=k(ω_1+ω_{m−1}),
(ω_1+ω_{m−1},ω_1+ω_{m−1})=2 and
(ω_1+ω_{m−1},ρ)=m−1, so C_2=k(k+m−1). For m=2 this is the spin-k
sector in End(V_{N/2}).

The exact integrated lower profile from (1.2) is

  Φ_{N,m}(Q)=sum_{k=0}^{N−1} (2k+m)
       [1−Q binom(k+d,d)^2/binom(N+d,d)]_+.          (2.2)

Indeed c_{k+1}−c_k=2k+m and D(t)=D_K on
[c_K,c_{K+1}). Define the exact last positive cutoff

  K_+(N,Q)=max({k∈{0,...,N−1}:
       Q binom(k+d,d)^2 < binom(N+d,d)}∪{−1});

then (2.2) is the sum from k=0 to K_+. Terms at equality are zero and may
be included or omitted. The pointwise profile also gives, for every integer
0≤K≤N,

  E(X) ≥ (K+1)(K+m)
       [1−Q binom(K+d,d)^2/binom(N+d,d)]_+.           (2.3)

For the convenient exact half-mass cutoff

  K_1/2(N,Q)=max({K∈{0,...,N−1}:
       2Q binom(K+d,d)^2 ≤ binom(N+d,d)}∪{−1}),

one has E(X)≥(K_1/2+1)(K_1/2+m)/2 when K_1/2≥0.

3. MATCHING TAPERED HIGHEST-WEIGHT UPPER

The lower rate is sharp in order for small rank. In the occupation basis of
H_N, index basis vectors by β=(β_2,...,β_m)∈Z_{≥0}^d, with
s=|β|≤N and β_1=N−s. There are
g_s=binom(s+d−1,d−1) states in layer s. For an integer 0≤R<N, define
the diagonal operator

  X_R |β> = c (R+1−s) |β>    if s≤R,
           0                  if s>R.

Its rank and squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm are

  rank X_R=D_R=binom(R+d,d),
  ||X_R||_HS^2=c^2 Z_R,
  Z_R=sum_{s=0}^R g_s(R+1−s)^2
     =binom(R+d+2,d+2)+binom(R+d+1,d+2).             (3.1)

For diagonal X, Cartan commutators vanish. Roots joining two coordinates
among 2,...,m preserve s and also commute with X_R. For each root joining
coordinate 1 to i≥2, the edge from layer s at β to layer s+1 at β+e_i
has squared ladder coefficient (N−s)(β_i+1). The two Hermitian root
generators together contribute this coefficient times
|x_s−x_{s+1}|^2. Summing i gives
Σ_{i=2}^m(β_i+1)=s+d, and summing β gives the exact energy

  E(X_R)=c^2 W_{N,R},
  W_{N,R}=sum_{s=0}^R (N−s)(s+d)binom(s+d−1,d−1)
       =N d binom(R+d+1,d+1)
         −d(d+1)binom(R+d+1,d+2).                    (3.2)

Choose c=Z_R^(−1/2). Writing A=binom(R+d+2,d+2), one has
Z_R=A(2R+d+2)/(R+d+2) and
binom(R+d+1,d+1)/A=(d+2)/(R+d+2). Since
W≤N d binom(R+d+1,d+1),

  E(X_R)=W/Z_R ≤ N d(d+2)/(2R+d+2).                  (3.3)

For integer 1≤Q<d_N let

  R(N,Q)=max{r∈{0,...,N}: binom(r+d,d)≤Q}.

Then R<N and Q<binom(R+d+1,d)≤(R+d+1)^d/d!, so
R+d+2>(d!Q)^(1/d). Since 2R+d+2≥R+d+2, the rank-D_R taper is
feasible at budget Q and gives

  inf_{rank X≤Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X)
       < [d(d+2)/(d!)^(1/d)] N Q^(−1/d).             (3.4)

Combining (2.3) and (3.4), set

  α_m=d!/[2(2d)^(2d)],
  c_m=(d!/2)^(1/d)/8,
  C_m=d(d+2)/(d!)^(1/d).

For integers N≥d and 1≤Q≤floor(α_m N^d),

  c_m N Q^(−1/d) ≤ inf_{rank X≤Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X)
                  ≤ C_m N Q^(−1/d).                  (3.5)

Lower-bound verification with exact integer cutoff. Put
R_*=(d!N^d/(2Q))^(1/(2d)) and K=floor(R_*)−d. The Q-range gives
R_*≥2d. Since binom(K+d,d)≤(K+d)^d/d! and
binom(N+d,d)≥N^d/d!, one gets QD_K/d_N≤1/2. Also
K+1≥R_*/2, hence (2.3) gives

  E(X)≥(K+1)(K+m)/2≥R_*^2/8=c_m NQ^(−1/d).

The cutoff is admissible: using d!≤d^d and N≥d,
R_*≤sqrt(dN)≤N, so K≤N−1. The constant c_m is intentionally loose.
The same Q-range gives Q/d_N≤(d!)^2/[2(2d)^(2d)]≤2^(−2d−1)<1,
so Q<d_N and the upper cutoff R<N is valid.
For rank exactly Q rather than rank at most Q, add arbitrarily small
nonzero diagonal entries on Q−D_R additional basis vectors and renormalize;
continuity of E preserves (3.4) as an infimum statement.

The profile (3.5) includes SU(2) with N=2j and d=1, so it recovers the
order j/Q rank-constrained commutator energy. Its extension to m≥3 is a
mathematical energy result only. Turning it into a Gibbs-orbit or memory
reconstruction lower bound requires a separate channel/score inequality;
this packet does not assert that transfer.

4. PRODUCT-GROUP PROFILES AND FAILURE OF SCALAR TRANSFER

The exact projector theorem distinguishes different highest-weight
geometries. A fixed-r family gives a second matching-order example; a
variable-r family refutes the tempting rule that the SU(2) symmetric-power
law can be transferred by replacing its degree with the sum of product
highest weights.

For G=SU(2)^r with fixed r and carrier
H_{n,r}=V_{n/2}^{⊗r}, d_{n,r}=(n+1)^r, conjugation decomposes as

  End(H_{n,r})=direct-sum_{k∈{0,...,n}^r} V_{k_1}⊗...⊗V_{k_r},
  λ_k=sum_q k_q(k_q+1),   m_k=product_q(2k_q+1).

Thus the exact cumulative spectral dimension is

  D_{n,r}(t)=sum_{0≤k_q≤n} product_q(2k_q+1)
                         1{sum_q k_q(k_q+1)≤t},

and the rank-Q profile is the exact integral

  Φ_{n,r}(Q)=∫_0^{r n(n+1)}[1−Q D_{n,r}(t)/(n+1)^r]_+dt.

For fixed r and Q=o((n+1)^r), the weighted-cell Weyl estimate is
D_{n,r}(t)=t^r/r!+O_r(t^(r−1/2)) for 1≪t≪n². It gives

  Φ_{n,r}(Q) ~ [r/(r+1)](r!(n+1)^r/Q)^(1/r).

The product occupation cap |i|≤R with a linear layer taper has rank
binom(R+r,r) and energy O_r(n Q^(−1/r)); hence the lower profile and
explicit upper match in order on the memo's stated integer range. At Q=1,
the exact minimum is rn. Full derivation and cutoff constants are in
leaves/heat_spectral_profile.txt. This family has r independent highest-
weight coordinates; its effective exponent depends on r, not on a single
scalar highest-weight sum.

For explicit upper constants, put

  Z_{r,R}=binom(R+r+2,r+2)+binom(R+r+1,r+2),
  W_{n,r,R}=r[n binom(R+r,r)+(n−2)binom(R+r,r+1)
                   −2binom(R+r,r+2)].

The taper has exact energy W/Z and rank Q_R=binom(R+r,r). If
Q_R≤Q<Q_{R+1}=binom(R+r+1,r), its energy is at most

  r(r+2)n/(R+r+2) < [r(r+2)/(r!)^(1/r)] nQ^(−1/r).

One explicit two-sided cutoff range is n≥r≥2 and
1≤Q≤min(floor((n+1)^r/4^(r+1)), binom(n,r)); there the profile gives
at least [3/(4·4^((r+1)/r))](n+1)Q^(−1/r). For Q=o((n+1)^r), the exact
profile has the sharper leading asymptotic stated above. See the leaf memo
for the weighted-cell proof and integer endpoint definitions.

For an exact variable-r counterexample to that scalar substitution, take
G=SU(2)^n on H=(C²)^{⊗n}, d=2^n, with local fundamental generators
T_a=σ_a/2 and L=Σ_{i,a}[T_a^(i),[T_a^(i),·]]. Locally
End(C²)=1⊕3 with Casimir eigenvalues 0 and 2. A subset S of sites carrying
the adjoint representation has eigenvalue 2|S| and dimension 3^|S|. Hence

  m_s=binom(n,s)3^s,    D_r=sum_{s=0}^r binom(n,s)3^s,
  Tr_2 Π_{≤2r}=(D_r/2^n)I,
  E(X)≥2 sum_{r=0}^{n−1}[1−Q D_r/2^n]_+.             (4.1)

The partial trace follows locally from Tr_2 Π_0=I/2 and
Tr_2 Π_1=3I/2, then tensoring across sites. At Q=floor(n/8), put
r=floor(n/8). Since binom(n,s)3^s increases through this range,

  D_r≤(r+1)binom(n,r)3^r
     ≤(n+1)2^{β n},
  β=H_2(1/8)+(log_2 3)/8<0.742<1.

Therefore QD_r/2^n=o(1), and (4.1), summing the first r+1 positive
intervals, gives inf_{rank X≤Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X)≥(n/4)(1−o(1)). A
rank-2^k product witness

  X_k=(I_{2^k}/sqrt(2^k))⊗(|0><0|)^{⊗(n−k)}

has energy n−k. For general Q it gives the upper n−floor(log_2 Q). In
particular, at Q=floor(n/8) the true infimum is Θ(n), whereas substituting
the total highest-weight size n into the SU(2) symmetric-power law N/Q
would predict order one. That scalar transfer is false; the full spectral
multiplicity profile, or at least the product geometry, must be retained.
At Q=2^(n−1), the profile gives the exact optimum 1, matched by X_{n−1}.
This is not a counterexample to (1.2), which correctly predicts the
representation-specific profile. Full details are in
leaves/block_mutual_information.txt.

These product-group results are Casimir-energy statements, not operational
reconstruction bounds.

5. PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON AND CLAIM BOUNDARY

The closest primary antecedent located is Alagic–Russell, “Uncertainty
Principles for Compact Groups,” arXiv:math/0608702v2 (2008; journal version,
Illinois J. Math. 52(4), 1315–1324). Their Theorem 1 is an operator
uncertainty principle for multiplication-localized and left-equivariant
operators. Their Theorem 2 gives

  μ(supp f) sum_ρ d_ρ rank(fhat(ρ)) ≥ 1.

Thus rank-sensitive representation-theoretic uncertainty and a projection
overlap mechanism are established prior art. The present argument is close
in structure: it uses rank to bound overlap with invariant spectral
projectors via a partial trace. Its additional checked statements here are
the energy-distribution integral (1.2), the exact Sym^N(C^m) Casimir profile,
and matching explicit tapered profiles for several named highest-weight
families. A related support-constrained energy profile is already an
immediate consequence of their Theorem 1: for the group Laplacian A,
R_t=1_[0,t](A), and D(t)=Tr R_t, take P=1_S. Their identity gives
||P R_t||_HS^2=μ(S)D(t), so a unit function supported in S has low-band
mass at most μ(S)D(t), and layer-cake yields
<u,Au>≥∫[1−μ(S)D(t)]_+dt. This is a spatial-support profile, whereas (1.2)
uses matrix/Schmidt rank on one irreducible carrier. No claim is made that
the matrix-rank profile or its applications are new relative to all
literature; priority and novelty remain UNKNOWN.

Jiang–Liu–Wu, “Noncommutative Uncertainty Principles,” arXiv:1408.1165
(2014; JFA 270 (2016), 264–311), proves uncertainty inequalities for finite
index subfactors, finite groups, and finite-dimensional Kac algebras,
including Donoho–Stark-type support bounds. Its stated scope is related
uncertainty theory; no novelty inference follows from its abstract alone.

Bérard–Meyer, “Inégalités isopérimétriques et applications,” Ann. Sci. Éc.
Norm. Sup. 15 (1982), Theorem 1.5, proves a volume-constrained Dirichlet
Faber–Krahn inequality under a positive-Ricci hypothesis. It has no
matrix-rank parameter; the direction of its volume comparison also does
not follow from a lower bound on support measure alone.

Urakawa, “Spectra of some Domains in Compact Lie Groups and their
Applications,” Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 97 (1986), 717–723, computes zonal
Dirichlet eigenvalues on a specific family of domains in simply connected
compact simple groups. This is explicit Casimir/domain spectral prior art,
but not a rank-Q matrix profile.

Arhancet, “A reverse Riesz estimate combined with a spectral gap implies a
Poincaré inequality,” arXiv:2607.08322v3 (2026-10-02), treats general
Banach-space Poincaré inequalities from reverse Riesz estimates plus a
spectral gap, with noncommutative examples. Junge–Wang, “Generalized
Poincaré inequality for quantum Markov semigroups,” arXiv:2601.06005v2
(2026-08-31), proves noncommutative p-Poincaré inequalities from spectral
gaps for detailed-balance QMSs. These are relevant Dirichlet-form
antecedents; their source abstracts do not state the present rank-profile
bound. Full-text/priority comparison beyond this bounded check remains open.

6. STATUS, COST, AND NEXT GATE

The proofs above are finite-dimensional and analytic. Inputs are an exact
finite-dimensional irreducible representation, its invariant positive
operator L, and an integer rank budget Q. No sampler, oracle, numerical
precision, or external data is used. The fixed-r product-group Weyl law is
proved with an explicit cell-bound error, not assumed as an approximation.
The proofs do not depend on the earlier KLS or N75 packet, and those files
were left unchanged.

Main falsifier: a counterexample to (1.1) under irreducibility and
conjugation invariance, or to the root-edge conductance formula (3.2), would
invalidate the claim. A proof transfer to reducible representations using
D/d in place of the partial-trace/Ky-Fan quantity is explicitly refuted by
the example above.

The separate m≥3 Gibbs-orbit score/channel audit is in progress under
outputs/research/sol_hyperbolic_audit; until its full derivation and cost
record are reviewed, the present packet does not claim an operational
reconstruction theorem. Casimir energy alone is insufficient. A deeper
literature and independent expert audit is also required before any novelty
claim.

An independent root-generator audit confirms (3.2) and (3.3), and notes
that the linear ramp is not generally optimal among fixed-cutoff radial
tapers; see outputs/research/spectral_geometry/cycle2/leaves/
explicit_polytope_models.txt. This affects constants, not the proved order.
