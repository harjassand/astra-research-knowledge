# Local-filter transfer for finite-temperature charge fields

## Exact theorem

Let the finite-dimensional multipartite Hilbert space be \(\bigotimes_{i=1}^N\mathcal H_i\). Let H0 be Hermitian and let Q=Σ_i Q_i, where each Hermitian Q_i acts only on site i. For finite real β and λ, suppose [H0,Q]=0 and define

$$
\rho_{\beta,\lambda}=
\frac{\exp(-\beta H_0+\lambda Q)}
{\operatorname{Tr}\exp(-\beta H_0+\lambda Q)}.
$$

Put Aλ=exp(λQ/2)=⊗_i exp(λQ_i/2). Since [H0,Q]=0,

$$
\rho_{\beta,\lambda}
=
\frac{A_\lambda\,\rho_{\beta,0}\,A_\lambda}
{\operatorname{Tr}(A_\lambda\,\rho_{\beta,0}\,A_\lambda)}.
$$

Every factor exp(λQ_i/2) is positive and invertible for finite λ in finite dimension. Therefore this is a reversible local filter. Multiplying each local factor by a sufficiently small scalar makes it a valid Kraus operator with nonzero success probability; the scalar disappears after state normalization. The inverse filter gives the converse.

Consequently, for each fixed β, membership is identical at λ and at zero for every multipartite state class preserved by invertible local filters. This is an exact equivalence of the *unknown full boundary*, not a computation of its numerical location.

## What the equivalence preserves

The same local congruence and inverse preserve:

- full separability;
- separability across any fixed partition;
- k-producibility, entanglement depth, biseparability, and genuine multipartite entanglement (each is defined by convex mixtures of pure states with specified tensor-factor structure);
- PPT status across each bipartition, and hence simultaneous PPT across any collection of cuts;
- Schmidt number across each bipartition.

For PPT on a cut A|B, the partial transpose obeys a congruence of the form
$$
(A_A\otimes A_B)\rho(A_A\otimes A_B)^\dagger
\quad\longmapsto\quad
(A_A\otimes \overline{A_B})\rho^{T_B}
(A_A^\dagger\otimes A_B^T),
$$
so positivity is equivalent in both directions. For Schmidt number, an invertible product across the cut preserves the Schmidt rank of every pure vector in a decomposition; applying the inverse proves equality, not just a one-way monotonicity.

The filter generally changes the amount of entanglement, local spectra, magnetization, partition function, and the magnitude of negativity. It preserves zero/nonzero entanglement properties and Schmidt number, not numerical entanglement measures. A statement about the full state also does not automatically transfer to reduced-state entanglement: tracing out filtered sites is not an invertible local operation on the retained marginal.

## Conditions and limits

- **Finite positive temperature / finite chemical potential:** for a grand-canonical state exp[-β(H0−μQ)]/Z, take λ=βμ. Any finite β and μ give finite λ and an invertible filter. A direct finite field coefficient hQ is covered by taking λ=h.
- **Zero temperature or infinite field:** the theorem is finite-parameter. As β→∞ or |λ|→∞, the filters can converge to singular projections. Ground-state sectors can change, so no zero-temperature equivalence follows by taking the limit. This is not a contradiction: the limit of reversible filters need not be reversible.
- **Canonical fixed-charge sectors:** if the state is projected into one eigenspace of Q, exp(λQ) is scalar on that sector, so the field has no effect there for this simpler reason. The grand-canonical theorem compares all charge sectors by local reweighting.
- **Nonuniform fields:** for Q_h=Σ_i h_i Q_i, the result holds exactly whenever [H0,Q_h]=0. It does not require the h_i to be equal. For connected XXZ exchange graphs, a nonuniform z field generally fails this commutation condition; the exchange terms conserve total z charge but transfer charge between sites.
- **Nonadditive or nonlocal charges:** exp(λQ/2) need not factor across sites, so separability invariance does not follow.
- **Superselection:** if a local charge SSR is imposed and each Q_i is its local charge, these filters commute with each local SSR and preserve the SSR-restricted separable set. Ordinary separability and SSR-restricted separability are different notions and should not be conflated.

## Application to the collective XXZ Gibbs family

For spin one-half, Jz=Σ_i σ_z^(i)/2. The dimensionless target
$$
\rho_h\propto\exp[(\alpha J^2-\delta J_z^2)/N+hJ_z]
$$
has H0=−(\alpha J²−δJz²)/N and Q=Jz, with [H0,Jz]=0. Thus
$$
A_h=e^{hJ_z/2}=\bigotimes_i e^{h\sigma_z^{(i)}/4}
$$
relates the full h=0 and h≠0 Gibbs states. At every finite N and finite real h, the exact full-separability, k-producibility, entanglement-depth, cut-PPT, and cut-Schmidt-number boundaries are therefore independent of h. The same statement applies to grand-canonical μ for any finite β.

This transfers any solved zero-field boundary exactly across the field. It does not turn a sufficient zero-field range into a sharp numerical threshold. In particular, the existing δ≤5/4 theorem transfers to all finite h, and any exact zero-field boundary (if established) would transfer as well.

## Primary-source overlap

Rossignoli and Canosa, [*Global thermal entanglement in n-qubit systems*](https://arxiv.org/html/1312.7370), already prove a close field-independence result for NPT thresholds. For their finite n-qubit XXZ models—fully connected and nearest-neighbor—they write the thermal partial transpose at field b as a positive scalar times an invertible congruence of the zero-field partial transpose (their Eqs. (6)–(7)). They conclude that each full-system bipartition’s nonzero-negativity/limit-temperature threshold is independent of uniform b at every T>0. Their scope is cutwise PPT/negativity, not full multipartite separability; the paper explicitly warns that PPT/separability across all bipartitions need not imply full separability. It also states that reduced-density negativity thresholds need not share the same field invariance.

Sperling and Vogel, [*The Schmidt number as a universal entanglement measure*](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.3974), §II.3, state that invertible local transformations preserve mixed-state entanglement; their discussion also identifies Schmidt number as invariant. This supplies direct prior art for the bipartite property behind the transfer. The multipartite k-producibility statement follows by applying the same invertible map and inverse to each pure component of the convex decomposition.

For contrast, Zhang and Li, [*Thermal entanglement in a two-qubit Heisenberg XXZ spin chain under an inhomogeneous magnetic field*](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0509009), study a nonuniform field and find field-dependent thermal entanglement. Their two-site Hamiltonian separates uniform B and staggered b components; the latter couples to Q1−Q2, which does not commute with the XX exchange. This is consistent with the commutation boundary above, rather than a counterexample to the uniform-field theorem.

## Exact counterexample when the coupled charge does not commute

A simple two-qubit XX example makes the limit concrete. Let
$$
H_0=-K(X_1X_2+Y_1Y_2),\qquad Q_{\rm stag}=Z_1-Z_2,\qquad H(b)=H_0-bQ_{\rm stag},
$$
with K>0. H0 commutes with the uniform charge Z1+Z2, but [H0,Qstag]≠0. In the odd subspace {|01>,|10>}, the unnormalized Gibbs matrix is
$$
\exp[2\beta(K\sigma_x+b\sigma_z)]
=\cosh(2\beta r)I+\frac{\sinh(2\beta r)}r(K\sigma_x+b\sigma_z),
\qquad r=\sqrt{K^2+b^2},
$$
while |00> and |11> each have unnormalized weight 1. Partial transpose moves the odd-sector coherence into the even block, so the two-qubit state is separable iff
$$
\gamma_b:=\frac K{\sqrt{K^2+b^2}}
\sinh\!\left(2\beta\sqrt{K^2+b^2}\right)\le1.
$$

At finite βK=2/5, γ0=sinh(4/5)≈0.888106<1, so the state is separable at b=0. At b=2K,
$$
\gamma_{2K}=\frac1{\sqrt5}\sinh(4\sqrt5/5)\approx1.300373>1,
$$
so the partial transpose has eigenvalue 1−γ_{2K}<0 and the state is entangled. This is a finite-temperature, finite-field counterexample to extending the invariance theorem to a charge that fails to commute with H0.

## Priority conclusion

The reversible-filter lemma is generic and follows from the standard local-invertible invariance of entanglement. Field-independent global PPT/negativity thresholds for finite-temperature XXZ models are explicit prior art in Rossignoli–Canosa (2013). The exact full-separability and entanglement-depth transfer is stronger than that paper’s computed criterion but is an immediate application of the same local-filter structure. Its historical novelty as a statement for the particular collective model is **UNKNOWN**; do not claim the field-independence mechanism or cutwise PPT threshold as new.
