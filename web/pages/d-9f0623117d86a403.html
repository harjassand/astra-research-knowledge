## Literal input and question

The map in Meng–Yang arXiv2607.22198v2, Introduction equation `eq:Fmap`, is, with u=1+xy,

    F1=u^3 t+y^2 u(4+3xy),
    F2=y+3x u^2 t+3xy^2(4+3xy),
    F3=2x-3x^2 y-x^3 t.

Its exact Jacobian determinant is -2. Our fresh code independently verifies that determinant and the three stated primary source points, before any field-coordinate argument. We use the literal polynomial, not an assumption about global invertibility or the correctness of all primary conclusions.

The precise question is whether a global polynomial change of the three source coordinates can make F affine in a genuinely different new scalar coordinate. This is equivalent to a locally nilpotent derivation delta of C[x,y,t] with delta^2 F_i=0 for all three outputs. The claim is

    delta^2 F=0  iff  delta=p(x,y) partial_t.

Consequently, a global source automorphism Phi(B1,B2,tau) making F composed with Phi affine in tau must have

    Phi=(phi(B), a tau+H(B)),

where phi is a polynomial base automorphism and a is a nonzero constant. Base automorphisms are allowed; new base/pivot mixing is excluded in this affine-pencil interface only.

## Complete generic-field reconstruction

Let K=C(x,y,t), w=y+1/x, v=1/x, c=F3. The inverse is

    x=1/v, y=w-v, t=5v^2-3wv-cv^3.

Thus c,w,v are algebraically independent and K=C(c,w,v). Independent substitution gives

    a=F1=-cw^3+w^2+wv,
    b=F2=-3cw^2+4w+2v,
    x(b-4w+3cw^2)=2.

The reciprocal cubic is

    P(W)=cW^3-2W^2+bW-2a=(W-w)T(W),
    T(W)=cW^2+(cw-2)W-2cw^2+2w+2v.

Its quadratic discriminant is (3cw-2)^2-8cv. In C(c,w)(v), this nonzero linear polynomial in v has valuation one at its own zero, so it cannot be a rational square. Therefore T is irreducible in K[W]. T(w)=2v is nonzero. These are the complete, coprime linear and quadratic factors of P. The nonzero variables here are fraction-field elements; no polynomial source locus is removed from the final conclusion.

Now take a locally nilpotent delta with delta^2F=0. Its exponential supplies x(s),y(s),t(s) in C[x,y,t][s], and F(s)=F+s q with q=delta F. If q=0, the invertible Jacobian already forces delta=0. Otherwise q is a nonzero vector of fixed elements of K for this polynomial-in-s calculation. The rational function

    w(s)=y(s)+1/x(s), w(0)=w,

satisfies P(w(s))+s Q(w(s))=0, where

    Q(W)=q3 W^3+q2 W-2q1.

The missing W^2 coefficient is essential. Let G=gcd(P,Q) in K[W]. The complete factorization above permits only 1, W-w, T, or P. G=P is impossible because a nonzero scalar multiple of P has W^2 coefficient -2 times that scalar, whereas Q has coefficient zero.

For completeness, dividing by G gives coprime polynomials Ptilde,Qtilde. Ptilde+s Qtilde is primitive in K[W][s] and irreducible over K(W) as a linear polynomial in s. Gauss therefore makes it irreducible in K[W,s]. Since it has positive W degree, it is also primitive over K[s] and irreducible over K(s)[W]. Thus it has no rational root when its W degree is at least two. If G=1 this degree is three; if G=W-w it is two and the only possible rational root of the original product is the fixed root w. T also remains irreducible after the purely transcendental extension K(s). The only possible moving rational root is consequently the linear remainder when G=T.

This step is a universal irreducibility argument for arbitrary q in K, not a generic numerical factorization or a restriction to constant target velocities.

## Exact moving branch and polynomial-flow obstruction

When T divides Q, q3 cannot be zero. Write q3=c^2 k, k in K nonzero. The absent W^2 term fixes

    Q=ck T(W)(W+2/c-w),
    q2=k(-3c^2w^2+6cw+2cv-4),
    q1=k(cw^2-w-v)(2-cw).

The remaining root is

    w(s)=w-2sk/(1+sck).

Putting c(s)=c+sc^2k and b(s)=b+s q2 in the exact source-recovery identity yields

    b(s)-4w(s)+3c(s)w(s)^2=2N(s)/(1+sck),
    N(s)=v+sk(2cv-3cw+2)+s^2ck^2[c(v-3w)+4],
    x(s)=(1+sck)/N(s).

Algebraic independence makes the quadratic coefficient of N nonzero for every nonzero k in K. N has degree two in s and the numerator has degree one. A polynomial x(s) in K[s] would require N to divide the numerator, which is impossible. Even potential cancellation is absent: direct substitution gives N(-1/(ck))=2/c, nonzero. The source dependence of k does not alter these degree statements because k is a fixed element of K in this calculation.

The corresponding y(s)=w-v+sk(-cv+3cw-4) does happen to be polynomial. This was independently checked and retained so that one recovered polynomial coordinate cannot be mistaken for a global polynomial flow. The failed x(s) is decisive. No assumptions about t(s) are needed.

Hence every locally nilpotent flow under the hypothesis has w(s)=w.

## LND and global-coordinate conclusions, without imported black boxes

Differentiating w(s)=w gives delta x=x^2 delta y, hence delta x belongs to the ideal (x). For a nonzero element f in a characteristic-zero domain with a locally nilpotent delta, let d_delta(f) be the maximum n with delta^n f nonzero. The leading coefficients of exponential flows show d_delta(fg)=d_delta(f)+d_delta(g). If delta f were nonzero and equal to f k, the same element would have degrees d_delta(f)-1 and d_delta(f)+d_delta(k), a contradiction. Therefore delta x=0 and then delta y=0.

It follows that delta=p(x,y,t) partial_t. Localizing in the unchanged base gives a locally nilpotent derivation of C(x,y)[t]. If p has positive t degree m, repeated application to t has nonzero leading coefficients and degrees n(m-1)+1 for n>=1; it never vanishes. Thus p has t degree zero and lies in C[x,y]. Conversely any such p gives a locally nilpotent derivation and annihilates the second derivatives of F because F is affine in t. This proves the claimed equivalence on the entire polynomial ring, including x=0 and c=0.

For a global source automorphism Phi, pushing forward partial_tau gives such a delta with a polynomial slice S satisfying delta S=1. Thus p partial_t S=1, forcing p to be a nonzero constant a. Old x,y are independent of tau, and old t=a tau+H(B). The inverse new base coordinates are invariants of a partial_t and therefore polynomials in old x,y; they provide a polynomial inverse for the base map phi. This proves the displayed global form.

## Additional scalar-repair interface: polynomial section forces an affine ruling

The peer also supplied this corollary after the initial identity run. We independently reconstruct it here without additional computation. Let Phi be ANY global polynomial automorphism of three source coordinates (B1,B2,tau), put Fhat=F composed with Phi, and use the six-variable potential

    L(B,tau,z)=2 z dot Fhat(B,tau)-(tau-mu)^2/(2 lambda),

where lambda is a nonzero constant and mu is a scalar. Its full Hessian has the doubling block form [arbitrary base block, 2 JFhat^T; 2 JFhat, 0]. Thus its determinant is the nonzero constant -64(det JFhat)^2. This statement precedes descent and does not assert constancy of the reduced determinant.

Suppose the chosen scalar critical equation at level sigma has a globally polynomial section tau=s(B,z):

    2 z dot Fhat_tau(B,s)-(s-mu)/lambda=sigma.

Work over the field C(B), and let d be the tau degree of the nonzero vector Fhat_tau. If d>=1 and m=deg_z s>=1, the highest z-homogeneous term of the first summand is `2(z dot A_d)s_m^d`, nonzero because A_d is a nonzero coefficient vector and s_m is nonzero in a polynomial domain. Its degree md+1 strictly exceeds m and exceeds every lower tau-degree contribution, so it cannot be canceled by the scalar repair. If m=0, the coefficients of z force Fhat_tau(B,s)=0. But substituting this polynomial section in the nonzero constant Keller Jacobian would give a zero column and zero determinant, a contradiction. Thus d cannot be positive. If d=0, Fhat is affine in tau, and the section is explicitly polynomial.

Consequently, within this exact doubling plus one scalar quadratic repair interface, a global polynomial critical section exists only when the source change preserves an affine scalar ruling, and the already proved theorem classifies those changes. This covers Phi initially producing non-affine outputs as well. It does not exclude rational/algebraic selected sections, more general dual-dependent repairs, or arbitrary five-dimensional changes.
