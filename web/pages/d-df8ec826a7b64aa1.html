## B5. Positive constructive quadrature and its actual cost

For any finite T and target delta>0, omit frequency tails below omega_min>0
and above W, chosen so that their v-weight integral
integral_tail j_q(omega)coth(beta omega/2)d omega is <=delta/3.
B1 and integrability permit this. Partition [omega_min,W] into bins of
maximum width h and set omega_l to the RIGHT endpoint of each bin,
c_l^2=integral_bin j_q(omega)d omega. Both coth(beta omega/2) and1/omega
are decreasing, so automatically

    v_(q,m)<=v_q and chi_(q,m)<=chi_q.                         (B15)

There is no need to assume the discrete Euclidean susceptibility matches
the continuum exactly or silently double its thermal admissibility.

On |t|<=2T,0<=u<=beta and omega>=omega_min, put
d0=1-exp(-beta omega_min). For the B2 integrand f_omega(z), direct
differentiation of

    f_omega(t-iu)=[e^(-iomega t-omega u)
                         +e^(iomega t-omega(beta-u))]/
                                                   (1-e^(-beta omega))

gives the uniform conservative bound

    |partial_omega f_omega(z)|
             <=L_f:=2(2T+beta)/d0+2beta/d0^2.                 (B16)

Since integral j_q<=v_q, the interior covariance replacement error is
<=h L_f v_q. Choosing h<=delta/(3L_f v) pays the second third. Weights

third. Thus a fully acquired quadrature requires tail bounds, integrals
or certified approximations, and at most

    m<=1+(W-omega_min)/h                                   (B17)

oscillators PER physical site, plus their coefficient/frequency precision.
The integrals and bit precision are costs, not oracles of unknown price.

is beta-KMS. Put j(omega)=gamma_phi(omega)(1-exp(-beta omega))/(2pi).
