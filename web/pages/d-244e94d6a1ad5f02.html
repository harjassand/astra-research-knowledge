## Older primary comparison

Bjorn Poonen's *Using elliptic curves of rank one towards the undecidability of
Hilbert's Tenth Problem over rings of algebraic integers* was read in full from
the author's MIT PDF, 10 pages. Its first-page publication note identifies
ANTS-V, July 2002, LNCS 2369, pp.33–42; the arXiv primary metadata separately
dates its initial submission 29 March 2002. The exact theorem assumes number
fields F⊆K and a curve defined over F with both ranks equal to one, then
defines O_F existentially over O_K. It does not assert unique complete natural
witnesses. Lemma 8 uses denominator growth after fixed scalar amplification;
Lemma 9 converts denominator divisibility into being an integral multiple of
a point. This is a genuinely substantive antecedent of the release's elliptic
method, but its ring and multiplicity interfaces differ.

One sharp distinction is the error strength. Poonen's Lemma 8 derives
`log Norm den(x(mP1))=(1−o(1))m² hhat(P1)` from the full height together with
the archimedean approximation theorem. The release's six-height window needs
the stronger **bounded additive** error for the full reduced-coordinate height.
One cannot simply replace its observable by any quadratically growing
denominator sequence.

For an exact obstruction, let

`W_j=2^(8j²+floor(j^(3/2)))` for j≥1 and W0=1.

This is a strictly increasing integer sequence with
`log2 W_j=8j²+o(j²)`. For a true product k=l=10,r=100, the polarization
window's logarithmic perturbation equals

`floor(20^(3/2))+floor(100^(3/2))+1−2floor(10^(3/2))−floor(101^(3/2))=13`.

Thus it rejects the true product at κ=2⁷. More generally the same perturbation
for k=l=t is `(2sqrt2−2)t^(3/2)−(3/2)t+O(1)`, which tends to infinity.
Every fixed multiplicative window eventually rejects a true product.
This is an obstruction to that exact-window method under only relative-error
hypotheses, not a theorem about all denominator encodings or Poonen's theorem.
