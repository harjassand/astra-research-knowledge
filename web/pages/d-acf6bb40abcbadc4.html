# Cycle 3 continuation: hidden CTMC identification beyond actionwise CLE fields

## Verdict

Yes: an intervention policy can separate finite hidden-state CTMCs even when their chemical-Langevin drift and covariance agree at every observable state under every allowed action. I give a 10-state witness with unknown rate nuisance, exact actionwise moment equality, identical passive observation laws, and a one-pulse active separator. The experiment sees a second-order-in-pulse-time difference in a first-exit probability that the CLE fields cannot contain.

The equally important no-go is exact input-output equivalence: if two models induce the same law for every finite action/observation word, then no adaptive policy separates them. In a sampled finite-alphabet model, any non-equivalent pair has an open-loop distinguishing word of length at most the sum of the hidden-state counts minus one. These are finite-state model results. Neither the witness nor the design establishes that any cell system has these dynamics.

The state-count, observation, and cost contract is explicit below. The key measured output is the scalar observed state x and its jump size. The hidden phase H is not read directly. It can be inferred in the witness by a passive marked jump and preserved during reset. If an assay records only coarse snapshots and misses the jump marks, this interface is unavailable and the proposed design does not apply.

## Exact controlled input/output representation

For a finite sampled controlled HMM/CTMC, let m index a candidate mechanism, with n_m hidden states, finite action alphabet U, finite output alphabet O, initial row distribution pi_m, and nonnegative matrices M_m^(u,o). At sampling gap Delta, one valid CTMC convention is

    M_m^(u,o) = exp(Q_m^u Delta) D_m^u(o),

where Q_m^u is a row generator and D_m^u(o) is the diagonal emission-probability matrix. Thus, for action/output word w=((u_1,o_1),...,(u_L,o_L)),

    p_m(w) = pi_m M_m^(u_1,o_1) ... M_m^(u_L,o_L) 1.

This fixes the row/column convention and the time interface. The theorem below is for fixed sampling gaps and finite O; continuous event times can be binned, but then the binning error must be charged. It is not a theorem about arbitrary continuous observation kernels without a finite representation.

### Exact equivalence lemma

Two such models have identical transcript laws under every finite-horizon deterministic or randomized adaptive policy if and only if p_1(w)=p_2(w) for every finite action/output word w.

Proof: under a deterministic policy, each output string o_1,...,o_L induces one action string u_1,...,u_L. Its transcript probability is exactly p_m(w). Equality of all word probabilities therefore gives equality of every policy transcript law. Randomized policies are mixtures over their independent random seeds, so the equality persists. Conversely, if p_1(w) differs from p_2(w), the fixed open-loop schedule u_1,...,u_L has a separating output-cylinder event o_1,...,o_L. Thus an adaptive policy cannot separate an IO-equivalent pair, and whenever separation is possible, adaptivity is unnecessary for the existence of some finite separator. It may still improve cost or robustness for composite unknown-parameter classes.

For an exactly equivalent pair with equal prior probabilities, any classifier based on any finite or infinite policy transcript has Bayes error 1/2. More generally, the error is at least the smaller prior mass. No sample-size increase changes this because the transcript distributions are equal.

The word-length bound follows from a standard linear-span argument. Set D=n_1+n_2, B_(u,o)=diag(M_1^(u,o),M_2^(u,o)), v=(pi_1,-pi_2), and c=(1_(n_1),1_(n_2))^T. Then p_1(w)-p_2(w)=v B_w c. Let S_k=span{vB_w: |w|<=k}. If S_k changes, its dimension rises by at least one; once it stabilizes it is invariant under every B_(u,o). Since dim S_0<=1 and the ambient dimension is D, by k=D-1 the span has either stabilized or is the full space. If c annihilates all words through D-1, it annihilates every word. Therefore, if distinguishable at all, a witness exists with L<=D-1. This is an upper bound, not a claim of worst-case tightness.

For unknown model parameters, let C_1,C_2 be compact rate-confidence sets and assume fixed supplied upper bounds n_1,n_2 on hidden-state counts. Let W contain all open-loop action schedules of length at most D-1. If no cross-class parameter pair is IO-equivalent, then

    delta0 = min_(theta1 in C1, theta2 in C2) max_(w in W)
             TV(P_theta1^w, P_theta2^w) > 0.

Continuity and compactness make the minimum positive. A uniform random schedule over W gives a joint schedule/output TV margin at least delta0/|W|. A maximin schedule mixture can improve it. If the confidence sets intersect in an equivalent pair, or approach one without a positive margin, no finite sample count gives a uniform error guarantee; report non-identifiable/UNKNOWN. The margin is a property to compute or certify, not a consequence of distinct parameter names.

## Actionwise CLE collision with an active separator

Let the observable state be x in {0,1,2,3,4}; let the hidden phase be h in {0,1}. This is a finite 10-state CTMC. The finite action set is passive p, active a, and reset r. Rates c>0 and kappa>0 are nuisance rates, while theta>=0 is the mechanism parameter to be learned.

At x=2, regardless of action:

- if h=0, x jumps to 1 and 3 at rate c each (increments -1,+1);
- if h=1, x jumps to 0 and 4 at rate c/4 each (increments -2,+2).

At x not equal to 2, action r jumps x to 2 at rate kappa; p and a leave x fixed. Independently, h flips 0<->1 at rate 0 under p and r, and at rate theta under a, at every x. A hidden flip has observable x-increment zero. No simultaneous jumps occur.

For the observable scalar x, the CLE drift and covariance are

    b(x,h,u) = sum_j q_j(x,h,u) nu_j,
    A(x,h,u) = sum_j q_j(x,h,u) nu_j^2.

At x=2, both phases and all actions give b=0 and A=2c: phase 0 has two unit jumps at rate c; phase 1 has two size-2 jumps at rate c/4. At x!=2, action r gives b=kappa(2-x), A=kappa(2-x)^2, while p and a give b=A=0. Hidden flips contribute zero. Thus b and A agree exactly for every observable x, every hidden h, every action, every theta, and the same nuisance pair (c,kappa). The channels differ in higher jump law and latent memory while the complete actionwise first-two-moment fields coincide.

For passive-only experiments, theta is never used. Given a common initial hidden distribution, the complete passive x-path laws are identical under every theta. So passive data cannot identify theta even with infinitely many noiseless trajectories.

Under active action from x=2, the x-exit hazard is lambda_0=2c in phase 0 and lambda_1=c/2 in phase 1. The hidden phase generator is G_theta=[[-theta,theta],[theta,-theta]]. Let S_h(theta,c,t) be the probability that x has not left 2 by time t, starting in hidden phase h. Killing at the first observed x-jump gives

    S_h(theta,c,t) = e_h^T exp((G_theta - diag(2c,c/2))t) 1.

In particular,

    S_0'(0) = -2c,
    S_0''(0) = 4c^2 + (3/2)c theta,
    S_1'(0) = -c/2,
    S_1''(0) = c^2/4 - (3/2)c theta.

Hence for a fixed starting phase,

    S_0(theta_1,c,t)-S_0(theta_2,c,t)
       = (3/4)c(theta_1-theta_2)t^2 + O(t^3),
    S_1(theta_1,c,t)-S_1(theta_2,c,t)
       = -(3/4)c(theta_1-theta_2)t^2 + O(t^3).

The first derivative is independent of theta; phase switching is visible first at second order. This is a falsifiable prediction: after conditioning on the same measured c and phase, the small-t coefficient of the survival-probability contrast is plus/minus 3c(theta_1-theta_2)/4. Failure of this coefficient rejects this CTMC witness model; it does not reject active identification in general.

The exact survival formulas use k=sqrt(theta^2+(3c/4)^2):

    S_0 = exp(-(theta+5c/4)t) [cosh(kt)+(theta-3c/4)sinh(kt)/k],
    S_1 = exp(-(theta+5c/4)t) [cosh(kt)+(theta+3c/4)sinh(kt)/k].

For known phase 0, the full time-to-exit law also jointly identifies the two unknown rates c and theta. For theta*c>0 its Laplace transform is reduced and identifies both; at the boundary theta=0 a pole cancels and the law reduces to Exp(2c), which still identifies c and the zero-switching boundary.

    Laplace(S_0)(s) = (s+2theta+c/2) /
        (s^2+(2theta+5c/2)s+c^2+(5c/2)theta).

For theta*c>0 there is no pole-zero cancellation: evaluating the denominator at s=-(2theta+c/2) gives -3theta*c/2. From the reduced rational curve, write the numerator constant as n=2theta+c/2 and the denominator's linear and constant coefficients as a=2theta+5c/2 and d=c^2+(5c/2)theta; then c=(a-n)/2 and theta=(n-c/2)/2. The full active event-time distribution therefore identifies both unknown rates under this restricted model. A single endpoint survival probability is only one scalar and cannot identify both continuous rates without a rate prior/confidence interval; use multiple pulse durations or time-resolved first-exit data.

### Hidden-start acquisition and one-pulse protocol

The active test need not assume a directly observed hidden-state label. Start each independent replicate at x=2. Under passive p, h is frozen, and the first jump magnitude is 1 iff h=0 and 2 iff h=1. Record that mark, then use reset r until x returns to 2; reset also freezes h. This yields a known active-pulse starting phase using only x-jump observations. Then apply a fixed active pulse of duration t=1 and record the binary endpoint event Z=1{x(1)=2}. The action word has one informative active pulse; the passive mark/reset are preparation and acquisition actions. Exact jump-time tracking is an assay assumption and its cost must be included.

If a simpler measured initial phase is supplied, the passive pilot/reset is unnecessary. If state observation is only snapshot-based and cannot establish the phase or detect first exit, the numerical sample bound below does not apply; use the finite-alphabet IO design with the actual observation matrices instead.

## Composite unknown rates and a numerical finite-data certificate

Here is one fully bounded example. Compare mechanism classes M_A: theta=0.1 and M_B: theta=3, with unknown nuisance c in [0.9,1.1]. Kappa is unknown but lies in [kappa_min,kappa_max] with kappa_min>0. The exact theta values specify two candidate hypotheses; all rates are not known to the experimenter because c,kappa and each replicate's hidden phase are unknown. To be conservative, the probability intervals below allow c to vary independently across the two candidate classes; the actionwise-CLE-matched subproblem is the subset c_A=c_B, so it inherits at least this margin. A general implementation should allow intervals for theta as well and certify the worst-case separation over them.

Because S_h is decreasing in c (it is E[exp(-c integral a_H)] with a_H>0), endpoints give the exact survival-probability ranges for these point theta hypotheses:

| active start h | M_A: theta=0.1 | M_B: theta=3 | gap between ranges |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | [0.126805, 0.182516] | [0.240182, 0.307353] | 0.057666 |
| 1 | [0.549265, 0.610589] | [0.314974, 0.384005] | 0.165260 |

The active survival endpoint alone therefore separates the two classes uniformly over c and either hidden start. Reorient the h=1 Bernoulli outcome as exit rather than survival. If the starting phase is fixed within a stratum, the phase-specific class means have gaps delta_0=0.05766 (h=0) and delta_1=0.16526 (h=1), so midpoint-threshold Hoeffding bounds give N>=1,802 pulses in the h=0 stratum or N>=220 in the h=1 stratum for at most 5% error under each candidate. When initial phases vary across independent replicates with an unknown mixture, center each oriented Bernoulli outcome at the midpoint for its observed phase. The centered values lie in [-1,1] and have expectation at most -delta/2 under M_A and at least +delta/2 under M_B, where delta=min(delta_0,delta_1). Hoeffding then gives per-hypothesis error at most exp(-N delta^2/8), so N>=7,207 total independent pulses suffices for 5% error without a lower bound on either hidden-phase prevalence. These are conservative bounds, not biological power calculations.

At the action/observation level, this pair needs at least one active pulse because every all-passive word has the same law; one fixed active pulse and one binary endpoint bit suffice under the stated c cap, so one active action block is minimal for this pair. If both c and theta are treated as unrestricted continuous unknowns, one binary probability is insufficient to recover both; the time-resolved first-exit distribution above is structurally identifying, while a finite-duration design should use at least two independently informative timing summaries and certify their sensitivity/rate-set separation.

For a lower bound when the active endpoint is deliberately coarsened to the survival bit and exit marks are discarded, fix the matched nuisance c=0.9 and start h=0. The two Bernoulli survival parameters are 0.182516 and 0.307353, with KL(P_A||P_B)=0.0403464 nats per independent unit. The passive-pilot and reset-time laws are common to both mechanisms, so they add no theta information. Bretagnolle-Huber gives sum of type-I and type-II errors at least (1/2)exp(-N KL); requiring each error at most 5% therefore needs at least ceil(log(5)/0.0403464)=40 units. This is a weak lower bound, not a claim that 40 units attain the target. It uses a matched-c pair whose CLE fields are exactly equal; the 7,207-unit upper bound is conservative and covers arbitrary hidden-phase mixtures.

Per active replicate, charge one passive first-exit observation (expected wait at most 2/c_min), one reset (expected wait at most 1/kappa_min), one active pulse of duration 1, and one endpoint read. For c_min=0.9 this is at most 2.223 + 1/kappa_min + 1 time units in expectation, plus assay/culture costs. Pilot estimation of c and kappa can replace supplied caps: for passive dwell time T_i started in observed phase h_i, put a_0=2 and a_1=1/2. Then W_i=a_(h_i)T_i are iid Exp(c), and with S_N=sum_i W_i, the exact two-sided confidence interval is [chi2_(2N,alpha/2)/(2S_N), chi2_(2N,1-alpha/2)/(2S_N)]. Reset waiting times are iid Exp(kappa) and have the analogous interval. Use an independent calibration split or a valid joint confidence sequence before applying the active-test guarantee; recompute the robust margin on the resulting confidence set. If the margin vanishes, return UNKNOWN. Do not plug a point estimate into the guarantee.

The numerical values are also reproduced by the local script [cycle3_controlled_ctmc_check.py](cycle3_controlled_ctmc_check.py). Its floating-point checks support arithmetic only; the field equality and survival derivatives are established algebraically above.

## Robust finite-action identification and reach-avoid design

The input contract is: finite hidden-state upper bounds; finite action and observed-alphabet sets; a fixed sampling/observation interface; a compact rate confidence set C with explicit action and assay costs; target and unsafe observable sets; and a finite horizon H. For each candidate finite observation-history policy pi, form the CTMC kernels M_theta^(u,o), propagate hidden-state probability vectors down its finite observation tree, and compute (i) its full output law, (ii) its target-before-unsafe probability, and (iii) its unsafe-hit probability.

Use the following robust design objective:

    maximize_pi  inf_(theta0 in C0, theta1 in C1) TV(P_theta0^pi,P_theta1^pi)

subject to

    sup_(theta in C) P_theta^pi(hit unsafe by H) <= alpha,
    inf_(theta in C) P_theta^pi(reach target before unsafe by H) >= rho,
    intervention dose/time/readout cost(pi) <= B.

Enumerate finite action/observation trees for a finite horizon, or branch-and-bound them. Inside each policy, certify the infimum/supremum over C using interval enclosures for matrix exponentials; a discretized rate grid is a candidate finder, not a robust certificate. Compare only policies satisfying the safety/target constraints, and choose the one with greatest worst-case TV per charged cost. If every feasible policy has zero separation margin, return non-identifiable under the available safe actions; if the interval optimizer cannot certify a sign, return UNKNOWN.

For identification alone, it is enough to enumerate the finite open-loop schedule set W of lengths at most n_1+n_2-1, compute each schedule's output law, and solve the maximin mixture problem over W. With a certified composite margin delta>0 and a finite joint schedule/output alphabet of size m, nearest-set testing of N independent replicates has error bounded by the categorical L1 inequality

    P(error) <= (2^m-2) exp(-N delta^2/2),

so N>=ceil(2 delta^-2 [m log(2)+log(1/epsilon)]) suffices. For one binary event and a single separated pair, the sharper Hoeffding bound above applies. All policies have finite horizon, but enumerating them is exponential in horizon/output-tree size; robust optimization over continuous rate sets may require certified global optimization. Matrix exponential precision, rate-set acquisition, cell culture, interventions, reporter/jump tracking, and replicate independence are all charged.

The reach-avoid objective is a design rule, not a new theorem about biology. It may be infeasible even when the mechanisms are statistically distinguishable, and an information-maximizing action may violate the safety constraints. Conversely, a safe target-reaching action can be model-indistinguishable. The algorithm must keep these predicates separate.

## Baseline comparison and novelty boundary

- Kiefer and Sistla, “Distinguishing Hidden Markov Chains,” LICS 2016, formalize equivalence/distinguishability for two known finite hidden Markov chains and construct likelihood monitors with exponential error decay in the number of observations. Their finite known-model result is the closest exact baseline for the word-equivalence boundary. The controlled action matrices above are the direct extension of their string-probability representation; this is not claimed as a new HMM theorem.
- Vanluyten, Willems, and De Moor, “Equivalence of state representations for hidden Markov models,” Systems & Control Letters 2008, characterize equivalent state representations and equivalent-model sets under their Mealy/Moore conventions. This reinforces that latent-state parameters need not be uniquely recoverable even when output laws are known.
- Input-output HMMs (Bengio and Frasconi, 1996) map input sequences to output sequences and use EM/GEM with latent paths. They provide a modeling/estimation baseline but do not make an observationally equivalent pair separable. A fitted parameterization is not an identification certificate.
- Mutny, Janik, and Krause, AISTATS 2023, “Active Exploration via Experiment Design in Markov Chains,” optimize policy-driven measurement allocation for a controlled Markov chain. This is a strong active-design baseline. Our finite robust mixture over action words is a small exact comparator; any practical algorithm should be compared at equal dose, trajectories, readouts, and computation. Their result does not imply that every latent CTMC mechanism is identifiable, and our no-go lemma applies regardless of an optimization heuristic.
- Classical persistency-of-excitation/system-identification results (e.g. Markovsky, Prieto-Araujo, and Dörfler, Automatica 2023) recover finite-horizon behavior of specified LTI systems from sufficiently informative inputs under structural assumptions. Those are different model and observability interfaces. Exciting all actions does not defeat exact IO equivalence.
- Cell-state priors: Transcompp uses time-series fractions and can fit transition and phenotype proliferation quantities; Gunnarsson, Foo, and Leder use CTMC branching likelihoods with phenotype fractions or absolute counts, with absolute counts important for net growth. Those works are richer and closer biological rate-estimation comparators; this cycle does not improve their fitting accuracy or claim biological controllability. The new mathematical witness isolates a different limitation: actionwise CLE equality leaves higher-order hidden output structure unresolved, and an active law can reveal it only if the assay observes the needed output.

No theorem here says real cells are finite-state CTMCs, that their transition rates are controllable as assumed, or that the pulse is safe/useful. No external mathematical proof review, wet-lab validation, or novelty/priority search was performed. The candidate's only defensible application claim is a prospective assay principle: when the competing models match all measured drift/covariance fields, acquisition should target higher-order/time-resolved outputs under a safe discriminating action and verify a held-out prediction.
