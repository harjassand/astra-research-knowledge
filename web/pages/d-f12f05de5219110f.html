# Positive composition: a sharp memory–range law

**Research date:** 10 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane.

**Status:** Complete written derivations from the hypotheses explicitly stated below. The concrete algebraic gate corollary imports the established Bourgain–Gamburd spectral-gap theorem. Finite diagnostics have been replayed, but these are not external peer review, proof-assistant verification, a certification of historical novelty, or a historic foundational breakthrough. No repository claims have been promoted.

This continuation replaces the ineffective single-step coefficient for a finite alphabet by a Gaussian-smoothed, convex-order-compatible Lyapunov argument. The principal output is a sharp joint spectral radius theorem for all positive lifts of a gapped rotation action. It implies both the specified finite-gate state law, including zero probability error, and a sharp numerical-range cost for unbiased positive interpolation.

## 1. Exact model and principal statements

Let A be a finite subset of SO(3), with a fixed probability nu assigning positive mass to every listed gate. Define

\[
 (Qf)(x)=\sum_{g\in A}\nu(g)f(g^{-1}x).
\]

Assume its action on the sphere has an absolute L2 spectral gap:

\[
 \|Qf\|_{L^2(S^2)}\le\lambda\|f\|_{L^2(S^2)},\quad
 \int f\,d\sigma=0,\qquad \lambda<1.
 \tag{SG}
\]

Set beta=1-lambda^2. The proof works for a nonsymmetric averaging operator if (SG) holds; symmetry and laziness are used to verify (SG) for the concrete gates.

For every family P(g) of m-by-m row-stochastic matrices, define an operator on (R^3)^m by

\[
 (\mathcal T_Pb)_j=\sum_g\nu(g)\sum_iP(g)_{ij}gb_i,
 \qquad \|b\|_{1,2}=\sum_i\|b_i\|.
\]

Let

\[
 F_m(T)=\sup_{P_1,\ldots,P_T}
 \|\mathcal T_{P_T}\cdots\mathcal T_{P_1}\|_{1,2},\quad F_m(0)=1,
 \qquad
 \rho_m=\lim_{T\to\infty}F_m(T)^{1/T}.
 \tag{1}
\]

The supremum includes arbitrary time-inhomogeneous stochastic kernels; the current gate may enter the whole transition matrix. The matrices are not required to commute, be reversible, or have any fixed stationary distribution. The family of such operators is compact and bounded. Since F_m is submultiplicative, the limit is the infimum of the displayed roots. This is an ordinary joint spectral radius for a particular structured family, not a new definition of spectral radius.

**Theorem A (uniform composition bound and sharp exponent).** Under (SG), put

\[
 c_\nu=\frac{\beta\log 2}{98305}.
\]

For all m>=1 and T>=0,

\[
 F_m(T)\le 2\exp(-c_\nu T/m).
 \tag{2}
\]

For m>=16 there is one fixed positive lift with eigenvalue alpha=1-8/m in the dual representation, giving

\[
 (1-8/m)^T\le F_m(T),\qquad
 1-\frac8m\le\rho_m\le e^{-c_\nu/m}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Consequently 1-rho_m=Theta_nu(1/m). Constants are not optimized.

For a five-element alphabet, F_m(1)=1 whenever m>=5: put all initial mass at one unit direction and route each of the at most five rotated images to a different state. Thus (2) cannot follow from a strictly contracting one-step operator norm. The accumulation argument is indispensable.

### Concrete finite-gate process

Let

\[
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&3/5&-4/5\\0&4/5&3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 R_z=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-4/5&0\\4/5&3/5&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A=\{I,R_x,R_x^{-1},R_z,R_z^{-1}\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

For x,u in the unit ball and a word of length t, write G_t=g_t...g_1 and

\[
 p_*(+\mid x,g_1,\ldots,g_t,u)=\frac{1+(1/3)u\cdot G_tx}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]

A simulator uses a preparation distribution p_x, transition P_{t,T}(g_t), and a terminal response h_{t,T,u}(i) in [0,1]. Its only persistent record of earlier inputs is its m-valued state. Fresh independent randomness is permitted. A correlated seed, a stored gate word, or an external accumulated rotation is not free. The time index and deadline are supplied as in the previous model, but carry no past-gate information. Preparations need not be affine functions of x. Matrices and probabilities may be exact real numbers; description length, acquisition, sampling precision, and running time are not counted here.

**Theorem B (fixed finite alphabet, exact and approximate).** For the process (5), uniformly for 0<=epsilon<=1/72,

\[
 \frac{c_\nu}{\log24}T\le m_*(T;\epsilon)\le16(T+1).
 \tag{6}
\]

Here nu is uniform on the five gates in (4), and (SG) follows from the established theorem verified in Section 7. The upper bound is exact (epsilon=0), works for every x,u, and in fact works for every rotation, not only the finite alphabet. The lower bound needs only the six preparations +/-e_1,+/-e_2,+/-e_3 and the single final query u=e_3. A separate simulator designed solely for these six preparations and that one query still obeys the lower bound.

Thus the state count is Theta(T), and the state-register bit count is log_2 T+O(1), not Theta(T) bits. The static *full* x,u observation family has four-state minimum, as proved in the previous packet. Do not confuse that full static family with the six-preparation, single-query restriction: the latter has a two-state static realization.

## 2. A matched-Gaussian relative-entropy lemma

For a compactly supported probability mu on R^d, let gamma_r be N(0,r^2 I), f_mu=mu*gamma_r, and

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)=D(f_\mu\|\gamma_r).
 \tag{7}
\]

For V~mu and independent standard Gaussian Z,

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)=\frac{\mathbb E\|V\|^2}{2r^2}-I(V;V+rZ).
 \tag{8}
\]

This follows by expanding the Gaussian cross entropy and using I(V;V+rZ)=h(V+rZ)-h(rZ). In particular, for mu supported in the unit ball,

\[
 0\le\mathscr E_r(\mu)\le\frac1{2r^2}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Write eta <=cx mu if integral phi d eta <= integral phi d mu for every finite convex phi. This is the established convex order.

**Lemma 1 (convex-order reverse triangle).** If eta <=cx mu, then

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)\ge\mathscr E_r(\eta)+D(f_\mu\|f_\eta).
 \tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** The function

\[
 \ell_\eta(z)=\log\frac{f_\eta(z)}{\gamma_r(z)}
 =\log\int\exp\!\left(\frac{z\cdot v}{r^2}-\frac{\|v\|^2}{2r^2}\right)d\eta(v)
 \tag{11}
\]

is convex, as a log-Laplace transform. Common independent Gaussian addition preserves convex order: for convex phi, x -> E phi(x+rZ) is convex. Thus f_eta <=cx f_mu. Direct subtraction yields

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(\eta)-D(f_\mu\|f_\eta)
 =\int(f_\mu-f_\eta)\ell_\eta\ge0.
\]

Compact support bounds ell_eta by affine growth in norm, and all densities have Gaussian tails; the integrals are finite. This proves the lemma. Gaussian convolution is injective, so equality in E_r monotonicity forces mu=eta. QED.

The reference Gaussian must match the convolution Gaussian. The lemma is not a statement that arbitrary KL divergences are monotone in convex order. Nor is mutual information alone monotone in that order (Section 10).

## 3. Every stochastic encoder gives a convex-order evolution

Let X_0 lie on the unit sphere, initially correlated with an m-state variable S_0. Generate independent gates with law nu, independent of (X_0,S_0), and let X_t=g_t X_{t-1}. Update S_t with an arbitrary kernel P_t(g_t), using fresh randomness.

Define

\[
 q_i(t)=\Pr(S_t=i),\quad v_i(t)=\mathbb E[X_t\mid S_t=i],\quad
 \mu_t=\sum_iq_i(t)\delta_{v_i(t)}.
\]

Zero-weight states may be assigned any vector. Every mu_t has at most m atoms in the unit ball. Put

\[
 M_t=\int\|v\|^2d\mu_t(v),\qquad
 R_t=\int\|v\|d\mu_t(v).
\]

**Lemma 2.** With Qmu the average of the rotated measures,

\[
 \mu_t\le_{cx}Q\mu_{t-1},\quad
 M_{t-1}-M_t=
 \mathbb E\|g_tv_{S_{t-1}}(t-1)-v_{S_t}(t)\|^2\ge0.
 \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Given S_{t-1} and g_t, the new state is conditionally independent of X_{t-1}. Therefore, if U=g_tv_{S_{t-1}}(t-1),

\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid S_t]=\mathbb E[X_t\mid S_t]=v_{S_t}(t).
\]

The law of U is Qmu_{t-1}. Conditional Jensen proves convex order, and conditional Pythagoras proves the variance identity. QED.

This is an unconditional coupling argument. It does not incorrectly assume that the output-state distribution is independent of the current gate. In general that independence is false.

Combining Lemma 1 with (12), stochastic merging can only decrease E_r further after gate averaging.

## 4. Spectral mixing produces a definite entropy drop

Let J be orthogonal projection onto radial functions in L2(R^3,dx), and let F=sqrt(f_mu), so ||F||_2=1. Applying (SG) on each sphere and integrating in the radial variable gives

\[
 \|QF\|_2^2\le\|JF\|_2^2+\lambda^2\|F-JF\|_2^2.
 \tag{13}
\]

**Lemma 3.**

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(Q\mu)
 \ge\beta\|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2.
 \tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** Rotations preserve gamma_r and commute with Gaussian smoothing. Therefore

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(Q\mu)
 =h(Qf)-h(f)=\sum_g\nu(g)D(g_\#f\|Qf).
\]

For densities p,q,

\[
 D(p\|q)\ge\|\sqrt p-\sqrt q\|_2^2.
\]

One proof is Jensen: D(p||q)>=-2 log integral sqrt(pq)>=2(1-integral sqrt(pq)). Averaging this bound gives

\[
 \sum_g\nu(g)D(g_\#f\|Qf)
 \ge2-2\langle QF,\sqrt{Qf}\rangle
 \ge2-2\|QF\|_2
 \ge1-\|QF\|_2^2.
\]

Use (13) and ||F||_2=1. QED.

The key feature is that the same E_r is decreased by both steps: the rotations' averaging and the encoder's convex-order contraction. Neither operation can recharge the other one's loss.

## 5. An m-atomic law with nonzero moment cannot be angularly smooth at scale m^{-1/2}

**Lemma 4.** Suppose mu has at most m atoms in the unit ball and integral ||v||^2 dmu >=a, where 0<a<=1. Set

\[
 r=\frac{a}{8\sqrt{3m}}.
\]

Then

\[
 \|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2\ge a/16.
 \tag{15}
\]

**Proof.** Set s=sqrt(a/2) and L=2sqrt(3). The mu-mass of centers with norm at least s is at least a/2, since E||V||^2<=s^2+Pr(||V||>=s). Let E be the union of the Euclidean balls of radius Lr about those centers. Markov's inequality gives Pr(||Z||<=L)>=3/4 for standard Z in R^3. Hence

\[
 \int_E f_\mu\ge3a/8\ge a/4.
\]

A ball of radius b about a point of norm at least s, with b<s, is seen from the origin inside an angular cap with sine of angular radius at most b/s. Its normalized spherical area is at most (b/s)^2/2. This holds for its intersection with every sphere centered at the origin. Here

\[
 \frac{(Lr)^2}{s^2}=\frac{a}{8m}<1.
\]

Consequently E occupies at most angular fraction a/16 of each such sphere. Because J sqrt(f_mu) is radial,

\[
 \int_E(J\sqrt{f_\mu})^2\le(a/16)\|J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2\le a/16.
\]

The reverse triangle inequality on E now yields

\[
 \|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2
 \ge\sqrt{a/4}-\sqrt{a/16}=\sqrt a/4.
\]

Squaring proves (15). QED.

## 6. Proof of Theorem A, upper bound

If M_T>=a, monotonicity of M ensures M_t>=a at all previous times. Use the fixed r in Lemma 4 throughout that interval. Lemmas 1–4 show a drop at least beta a/16 in E_r at each step. Since its initial value is at most 1/(2r^2)=96m/a^2 and its final value is nonnegative,

\[
 T\le\frac{1536m}{\beta a^3}.
 \tag{16}
\]

This applies to every possible normalized initial vector b in the operator problem: set Pr(S_0=i)=||b_i|| and X_0=b_i/||b_i|| on nonzero components. Then b_i(t)=E[X_t 1_{S_t=i}] evolves exactly by T_{P_t}, and R_t=||b(t)||_{1,2}.

If R_T>1/2, Jensen gives M_T>=R_T^2>1/4. Setting a=1/4 in (16) gives T<98304m/beta. Thus, for

\[
 n_m=\lfloor98304m/\beta\rfloor+1,
\]

we have F_m(n_m)<=1/2. Submultiplicativity and F_m(t)<=1 give

\[
 F_m(T)\le2^{-\lfloor T/n_m\rfloor}
 \le2e^{-(\log2)T/n_m}
 \le2e^{-\beta(\log2)T/(98305m)}.
\]

This proves (2). Notice that no logarithmic factor in m was introduced to approximate Haar measure by a block of discrete gates. QED.

## 7. Why the specified gates have the required spectral gap

The SU(2) lifts

\[
 U_x=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2&-i\\-i&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 U_z=\frac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}2-i&0\\0&2+i\end{pmatrix}
\]

have algebraic entries, determinant one, and give exactly (4) on Bloch vectors.

Their angle theta satisfies cos theta=3/5. If theta/(2pi) were rational, exp(i theta) would be a root of unity and 2cos theta=6/5 would be a rational algebraic integer, which is impossible. Hence the powers of each lift are dense in its one-parameter rotation subgroup. The x and z infinitesimal rotations and their commutator span su(2); the closed subgroup containing both circles is SU(2).

The established Bourgain–Gamburd theorem, *A Spectral Gap Theorem in SU(d)*, arXiv:1108.6264v1, supplies a spectral gap for finitely generated dense algebraic subgroups. The symmetric averaging on these four nonidentity lifts therefore has a top spectral gap. Adding the identity with weight 1/5 bounds the bottom of the spectrum by -3/5, so the full five-element averaging has absolute norm below one on mean-zero functions. Restricting from SU(2) to the sphere quotient preserves that bound.

This is an explicitly imported theorem. Its proof was not independently reproduced or formally checked in this session. The existence beta>0 follows; no numerical value valid for all harmonic degrees has been certified. Degrees 1–24 in the verifier are diagnostics only.

## 8. Matching positive lift and exact finite-horizon simulation

Let 0<r_0<=1. A maximal chord-distance r_0-separated subset {u_i} of S^2 has N<=16/r_0^2 points: the disjoint caps of chord radius r_0/2 have normalized area r_0^2/16. Maximality makes it an r_0-net. Therefore, with alpha=1-r_0^2/2,

\[
 \alpha B^3\subseteq L:=\operatorname{conv}\{u_i\}\subseteq B^3.
\]

Indeed every unit support direction x has a code point with x dot u_i>=1-r_0^2/2. Let w_i=u_i/alpha and P=conv{w_i}, so B^3 is contained in P.

For every g in SO(3),

\[
 \alpha gw_i=gu_i\in B^3\subseteq P.
\]

Choose nonnegative barycentric coefficients P(g)_{ij}, summing to one, such that

\[
 \sum_jP(g)_{ij}w_j=\alpha gw_i.
 \tag{17}
\]

For a finite alphabet these are finitely many feasible linear systems. For continuous controls a fixed triangulation of the polytope gives a measurable barycentric selection; boundaries can be assigned by a fixed index rule.

The dual of T_P is

\[
 (\mathcal A_Pz)_i=\sum_g\nu(g)g^{-1}\sum_jP(g)_{ij}z_j.
\]

Equation (17) says A_P W=alpha W. Taking r_0=4/sqrt(m) for m>=16 gives N<=m and alpha=1-8/m. Padding unused states does not affect the eigenvector. Consequently ||T_P^T||>=alpha^T, proving (3).

### Exact readout compensation

Encode any x in B^3 by barycentric coordinates in P. After t gates, the expected grid vertex is alpha^t G_t x. Define

\[
 h_{t,u}(i)=\frac{1+\delta\alpha^{-t}u\cdot w_i}{2}.
 \tag{18}
\]

This is a valid [0,1] response whenever delta alpha^{-(T+1)}<=1. Its expectation is *exactly*

\[
 \frac{1+\delta u\cdot G_tx}{2}.
\]

For delta=1/3 choose r_0^2=1/(T+1), so alpha=1-1/[2(T+1)]. Bernoulli's inequality gives alpha^{T+1}>=1/2>delta. Thus N<=16(T+1) gives zero probability error at every t<=T. The shrinking internal mean is compensated by increasing the readout slope within its permitted range; there is no negative probability and no use of past-gate history at readout.

This construction uses precisely the allowed time-dependent readout. The same conclusion is not asserted for a model requiring one fixed readout at all times. The fully sharp visibility delta=1 is also outside this particular headroom argument.

## 9. Finite preparations and a single observable prove Theorem B

Take X_0 uniform on the six signed coordinate vectors, and take all gates iid uniform on (4). Its covariance is I/3 and stays so under every fixed product of rotations. Set f=e_3 dot X_T. Then E f^2=1/3 and |f|<=1.

If Y is the terminal bit, its target conditional mean as a +/-1 variable is (1/3) f. Hence the bounded score Z=(2Y-1)f has target mean 1/9. Uniform probability error at most epsilon changes its mean by at most 2epsilon. At epsilon<=1/72, the simulator must therefore satisfy

\[
 \mathbb E Z\ge1/12.
\]

But its readout depends on S_T only, so

\[
 |\mathbb EZ|
 =\left|\sum_i(2h_i-1)e_3\cdot b_i(T)\right|
 \le\sum_i\|b_i(T)\|\le F_m(T).
\]

Theorem A implies 1/12<=2exp(-c_nu T/m), which rearranges to the lower bound in (6). The exact upper bound is Section 8. QED.

An alternative direct, less sharp constant follows from R_T>=1/12, M_T>=1/144, and (16):

\[
 m\ge\frac{\beta T}{4,586,471,424}.
\]

The theorem concerns a realizable quantum experiment: prepare rho_x=(I+x dot sigma)/2, apply the SU(2) lifts, and measure the binary effect (I+(1/3)u dot sigma)/2. The Born probability is (5). This is a one-qubit versus log(T)-classical-bit separation in the stated streaming model, not a general quantum algorithmic speedup.

## 10. A second application: unbiased positive interpolation must spend numerical range

Consider arbitrary time-varying grids W_t={w_{t,1},...,w_{t,m}} in R^3. Let B_t=max_i||w_{t,i}|| and suppose B_0>0. For each g and t assume there is a row-stochastic kernel satisfying exact unbiasedness on every grid point:

\[
 \sum_jP_t(g)_{ij}w_{t,j}=g w_{t-1,i}.
 \tag{19}
\]

These are positive interpolation or stochastic-rounding updates for the controlled linear action. No quantum assumption is used, and the grids need not be regular, fixed, symmetric, or uniform in spacing.

**Theorem C (memory–range tradeoff).** Under (SG),

\[
 \frac{B_T}{B_0}\ge\frac1{F_m(T)}\ge\frac12 e^{c_\nu T/m}.
 \tag{20}
\]

For m>=16 there is such an exact construction with

\[
 \frac{B_T}{B_0}\le e^{16T/m}.
 \tag{21}
\]

**Proof.** Equation (19) gives A_{P_t}W_t=W_{t-1}. Therefore W_0=A_{P_1}...A_{P_T}W_T, whose dual operator norm equals that of T_{P_T}...T_{P_1}. Taking max vector norms proves B_0<=F_m(T)B_T. For the upper construction use Section 8 with alpha=1-8/m and W_t=alpha^{-t}W. Equation (17) gives (19). Finally, -log(1-8/m)<=16/m for m>=16. QED.

For a fixed grid with damped interpolation sum_j P(g)ij w_j=alpha g w_i, the same proof yields alpha<=rho_m; the matching construction shows optimal unavoidable damping Theta(1/m). Signed interpolation is not covered, nor are general deterministic floating-point algorithms. A large coordinate on a very rare state is still counted in B_t; this is a range theorem, not an assertion about typical variance or arithmetic time.

The exact weak-observation simulator in Section 8 is the same expanding-grid construction with the range increase moved into the readout slope. Valid probabilities bound the usable readout range, converting (20) into a state cost.

## 11. Exact qualitative classification without a spectral gap

**Theorem D (finite orbit criterion).** For a finite orthogonal alphabet in any finite-dimensional real space, with all gate weights positive and the identity included,

\[
 \rho_m=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the generated group has a nonzero unit-vector orbit of size at most }m.
 \tag{22}
\]

**Proof.** A finite orbit gives permutation transition kernels and a nonzero dual fixed vector, so rho_m=1.

Conversely, rho_m=1 and F_m(T)<=1 force F_m(T)=1 for every T. The compact family and compact unit norm sphere imply a maximizer for F_m(m). Realize its initial b by a unit-vector variable X_0 as in Section 6. Then R_m=1 implies M_m=1, while M_0=1 and the M_t are nonincreasing. Hence E||X_t-E[X_t|S_t]||^2=0 at every time: conditional on each active state, X_t is a single unit vector.

Let C_t be the set of these active directions. For every g, gC_{t-1} is contained in C_t because each active state's stochastic row has at least one positive entry and every g has positive weight. Identity gives C_{t-1} subset C_t. There are at most m directions and m inclusions starting from a nonempty set, so at least one inclusion is equality. At that step every g maps the same finite set into itself, hence bijectively onto itself. This contains a finite group orbit of size at most m. QED.

For a dense SO(3) action no such nonzero finite orbit exists, so rho_m<1 for every fixed m even without importing (SG). This compactness argument gives no rate as m increases. The quantitative theorem is substantially stronger.

## 12. Counterexamples and unsuccessful routes

1. **One-step retention:** F_m(1)=1 for m>=5. A per-step strict-contraction claim is false for the finite alphabet.
2. **Gate-by-gate transport shortcut:** after a stochastic update, the distribution of S_t generally depends on g_t. Treating each gate as transporting the same output marginal is invalid. The proof uses the unconditional convex-order coupling instead.
3. **Mutual information alone:** let Y be uniform on +/-0.02. Conditional on Y=0.02 take X=1 with probability .02 and X=0 otherwise; use the reflected rule for Y=-0.02. Then E[X|Y]=Y, so law(Y)<=cx law(X). As r goes to zero, I(Y;Y+rZ) approaches log2, whereas I(X;X+rZ) approaches H(.01,.98,.01), which is smaller. Mean-preserving compression can increase this information quantity. The variance correction in (8) matters.
4. **Raw KL profile is incomplete:** mu=(2/3)delta_{-1}+(1/3)delta_2 and its reflection have identical E_r for every r but are incomparable in convex order. Their call functions cross at thresholds -1 and 1. A complete likelihood-probe profile is developed separately; it is not an efficient new decision algorithm.
5. **Arbitrary Gaussian reference:** N(0,.25)<=cx N(0,.5), but relative entropy to N(0,1) decreases between them. Matching the smoothing and reference Gaussian is essential in Lemma 1.
6. **Finite groups or fixed directions:** a finite orbit allows indefinite lossless positive implementation by permutations, as Theorem D requires.
7. **No gap-free universal polynomial rate:** for planar rotation by 2pi times a Liouville number, regular m-gons give rational-rotation permutation models. If p/m approximates the angle divided by 2pi, the probability error through T is at most delta*pi*T*|theta/(2pi)-p/m|. For theta/(2pi)=sum_{k>=1}10^{-k!}, denominators m_k=10^{k!} have tail error at most 2/m_k^{k+1}. Taking T_k=m_k^k makes the error vanish while m_k grows more slowly than every fixed positive power of T_k. A polygon circumscribing the unit disk has vertex norm sec(pi/m), so its readout is valid for any fixed delta<1 and sufficiently large m. Density alone therefore cannot substitute for the stated quantitative mixing assumption across arbitrary compact actions.
8. **Signed or uncharged-memory models:** the theorem does not obstruct ordinary exact real rotation coordinates, negative interpolation weights, a stored input history, or an uncharged correlated random process. These violate the positive finite-state contract or change the counted resources.

## 13. Scope, novelty, and the remaining foundational gate

Established mathematics used includes convex order, Gaussian mixtures, KL/Hellinger inequalities, joint spectral radius, spherical cap geometry, convex polytopes, and the Bourgain–Gamburd spectral-gap theorem. Positive realizability is already a substantial subject, including classical and quantum realization theory. Existing work also combines martingale transitions and quantization, and reverse KL triangle inequalities under shape/order conditions have precedents. These facts rule out claiming that the individual ingredients are new primitives.

The candidate contribution is the fully quantified bridge: a fixed angular spectral gap forces a sharp gap for *every* finite-state positive lift, including time-inhomogeneous encoders with unrestricted real transition probabilities, and the bridge transfers to exact interpolation range. Literature searches did not establish priority for this exact package; absence of a retrieved equivalent theorem is not a novelty certificate.

The independent Gaussian-probe exploration yields a complete reformulation of convex order but not a new efficient capability. Its positive result and counterexamples are preserved rather than inflated into a replacement foundation.

The single next mathematical gate is the following implication, restricted to symmetric lazy finitely supported laws generating a dense subgroup of SO(3):

\[
 \inf_{m\ge1}m(1-\rho_m(\nu))>0
 \quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
 \|Q_\nu\|_{L^2_0(S^2)}<1.
 \tag{23}
\]

The converse direction follows from Theorem A. The displayed direction is not proved or asserted as a previously unknown open problem. The missing step is converting an almost-invariant high-frequency L2 function into a finite positive lift with sufficiently small dissipation. Such functions need not be localized point clouds, so that conversion cannot simply be assumed.

A proof or a counterexample would test whether the positive-lift profile detects the full infinite-dimensional spectral obstruction or merely one consequence of it. Even an equivalence would need independently important consequences before being called foundational. No historic 9–10/10 result has been established in this packet.

## 14. Explicit rational compiler for the finite-input upper bound

The existential 16(T+1)-state net is not needed as an inaccessible oracle. There is a slightly larger O(T)-state rational construction for (4), with an elementary, albeit inefficient, polynomial-time compiler.

Let n=ceil(sqrt(2(T+1))), and for integers -n<=a,b<=n use both signs of

\[
 u_{a,b}=\frac{(2an,\ 2bn,\ n^2-a^2-b^2)}{n^2+a^2+b^2}.
 \tag{24}
\]

These are rational unit vectors. There are at most N=2(2n+1)^2 of them, including duplicates. The stereographic map

\[
 (s,t)\longmapsto\frac{(2s,2t,1-s^2-t^2)}{1+s^2+t^2}
\]

has differential operator norm 2/(1+s^2+t^2)<=2. Every point of the upper hemisphere has inverse coordinates (x/(1+z),y/(1+z)) in [-1,1]^2. A nearest grid point differs in parameter norm by at most sqrt(2)/(2n), hence on the sphere by at most sqrt(2)/n. The negative chart covers the lower hemisphere. Thus the code is a chord sqrt(2)/n-net.

Set alpha=1-1/n^2 and w_i=u_i/alpha. The proof of Section 8 gives B^3 subset conv{w_i} and alpha^(T+1)>=1-(T+1)/n^2>=1/2. Therefore exact compensated readout at delta=1/3 is valid through T.

All chart coordinates, alpha, gates, and the six preparations are rational with O(log(T+2)) bit length. Every required barycentric problem has four linear equality constraints (three coordinates and total mass). A point in a 3D convex hull can be represented with at most four vertices: if a representation uses more, an affine dependence allows the coefficients to be shifted until one vanishes, without violating nonnegativity. Repeating proves the claim. An affinely independent support can be extended to four vertices with zero extra coefficients because the full polytope is three-dimensional.

Consequently a compiler may enumerate all four-vertex bases and solve their 4-by-4 rational systems, accepting the first nonnegative solution. There are O(N) target points for each of five gates, and O(N^4) bases per target: O(N^5) constant-size rational systems suffice, plus preparation systems. Fixed-dimension determinant formulas show the transition weights have O(log(T+2)) bit length, with constants independent of T. Each row has at most four nonzero entries. This yields a polynomial compilation bound and an O(T log(T+2))-bit sparse-kernel description, not a claim of a competitive compiler.

The time-dependent compensated readout coefficient alpha^(-t) can have O(T log(T+2)) bits. Evaluating and sampling those rationals incurs additional arithmetic and randomness costs, neither of which is bounded by the m-state register count. For arbitrary continuously supplied x,u, their input precision and encoding costs remain separate; the fully finite six-preparation/single-readout experiment avoids that input-precision issue.

Finite rational mesh and sampled rational barycentric identities are checked by verify_rational_mesh.py. These are finite checks of the formulas, not an independent proof of the asymptotic estimates.

## 15. Reconstructing the static four-state contrast

For completeness, the earlier static claim needed for this comparison can be recovered without trusting a prior audit. Let e_1,...,e_4 be regular tetrahedron unit vectors, so sum_i e_i=0 and sum_i e_i e_i^T=(4/3)I. For ||x||,||u||<=1 and 0<delta<=1/3 set

\[
 p_i(x)=\frac{1+e_i\cdot x}{4},\qquad
 h_u(i)=\frac{1+3\delta e_i\cdot u}{2}.
\]

These are nonnegative normalized preparation probabilities and valid responses. Expanding the product and using the two tetrahedron identities gives sum_i p_i(x)h_u(i)=(1+delta x dot u)/2.

For the lower bound, use preparations 0,a f_1,a f_2,a f_3 for a>0 small enough and queries 0,f_1,f_2,f_3, where f_j are the coordinate basis. Subtracting the first row from the remaining rows shows the resulting 4-by-4 probability matrix has determinant (1/2)(a delta/2)^3, which is nonzero. A factorization through m classical states has ordinary rank at most m, so m>=4. This is a static rank argument only; it does not entail positive composability.
