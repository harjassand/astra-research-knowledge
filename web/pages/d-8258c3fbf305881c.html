Strongest initial claim

For a smooth divergence-free field X on a closed Riemannian 3-manifold, the sustaining strain-viscosity work has an exact nonnegative deficit decomposition that resolves entropy across invariant components and the strain components. In particular it gives a sharper active-volume cost floor. At positive-entropy equality, even if zeros were initially allowed, the field is forced to be nowhere zero; after a cover of degree at most four, the metric and flow are a compact homogeneous Sol or universal-cover-of-SL(2,R) model with constant expansion rates. This exact geometric conclusion is internally derived below. The dynamical classification overlaps strongly with Ghys 1987; no novelty claim is made.

Interface and definitions

Let M be a closed Riemannian 3-manifold, V its volume, mu=dVol/V, X a smooth divergence-free vector field, phi_t its complete flow, S=Def X, nu>0. Let H=h_mu(phi_1), natural logarithms. For any smooth F,p satisfying nabla_X X-2nu div S+grad p=F, closedness and div X=0 give

  W := integral <F,X> dVol = 2nu integral |S|^2 dVol.

Indeed the advection and pressure pairings vanish, and integration by parts gives -2nu integral <div S,X>=2nu integral |S|^2. Thus W/(4nu V)=E_mu |S|^2/2. No force-acquisition, actuator, or finite-precision interface is specified by this identity.

Put U={X!=0}, Z={X=0}, alpha=mu(U). On U let r=|X|, e=X/r, P=I-e tensor e, q=<S e,e>=X(log r), b=PSe, B=PSP|_(e-perp), B0=B+(q/2)I, and s>=0 be the positive eigenvalue of the trace-free symmetric 2x2 map B0. Then

  |S|^2 = 2s^2+(3/2)q^2+2|b|^2   on U.

For integrals, set s=0 on Z; q,b remain defined only on U. Define s1(x)=integral_0^1 s(phi_t x)dt on U and s1=0 on Z. Set Gamma=E_mu[integral_0^1 s(phi_t x)^2dt - s1(x)^2]. Since U,Z are flow invariant and mu is flow invariant, pointwise Jensen followed by integration gives Gamma>=0 and Gamma=E s^2-E s1^2.

Normal cocycle and componentwise entropy bound

For x in U, Q_t=P_(phi_t x)Dphi_t|_(e(x)-perp) is an invertible quotient cocycle. If v(t)=Dphi_t v0=w(t)+a(t)e with w=P v, torsion freeness and nabla_X e=nabla_e X-qe give

  P nabla_X w=P nabla_w X,
  d/dt log|w|=<S w,w>/|w|^2 <= s(phi_t x)-q(phi_t x)/2.

The line exponent is zero: Dphi_n X(x)=X(phi_n x), and its exponent exists by Oseledets and equals zero almost everywhere. For x in U the endpoint term (log r(phi_n x)-log r(x))/n is precisely this line exponent; hence it vanishes a.e. even when log r is not integrable near Z. Volume preservation makes the two normal exponents chi,-chi. The estimate above, Birkhoff for s1 and Ruelle's inequality therefore yield h_lambda(phi_1)<=integral s1 d lambda for every invariant probability lambda supported on U. This uses the standard compact C1 Ruelle theorem. On Z the time-one map is the identity and entropy is zero.

Now take the ergodic decomposition mu=integral lambda_omega d tau(omega) for T=phi_1, and define h_omega=h_(lambda_omega)(T), m_omega=integral s1 d lambda_omega. Every component is supported either on U or on Z because both are T-invariant; for zero-set components h_omega=m_omega=0. On active components h_omega<=m_omega. Entropy is affine under this decomposition, H=integral h_omega d tau. The exact identity is

  Delta := W/(4nu V)-H^2
   = Gamma
     + integral Var_(lambda_omega)(s1) d tau
     + integral (m_omega^2-h_omega^2) d tau
     + Var_tau(h_omega)
     +(3/4) integral_U q^2 dmu
     + integral_U |b|^2 dmu
     +(1/2) integral_Z |S|^2 dmu.                    (A)

Proof of (A): split E|S|^2/2 using the block identity on U and retain the Z term. Write E s^2=Gamma+E s1^2 and disintegrate E s1^2=integral[Var_(lambda_omega)(s1)+m_omega^2]d tau. Finally integral m^2-H^2=integral(m^2-h^2)+Var_tau(h). Every summand is nonnegative by the componentwise Ruelle bound. All quantities and measures in (A) are mathematical, not acquired computationally.

Consequences for robust volume and cost

(A) implies

  W >= 4nu V integral h_omega^2 d tau >= 4nu V H^2.

If alpha>0, h_omega=0 on Z-components, so Cauchy gives integral h_omega^2 d tau >= H^2/alpha and therefore

  W >= 4nu V H^2/alpha.                                (B)

More locally, for any T-invariant measurable A contained in U up to null sets, let alpha_A=mu(A)>0 and h_A=h_(mu|A/alpha_A)(T). Ergodic components in A contribute separately to the integral in (A), so

  W >= 4nu V alpha_A h_A^2.                            (C)

Thus a positive-volume invariant computation region with a certified metric-entropy rate h_A forces a work floor linear in its volume fraction and quadratic in its rate. A finite observed coding partition can supply a lower bound on h_A only after its entropy rate and the invariant region are actually established. A universal-computation label, topological entropy, or a null-volume encoding alone supplies no such premise. Time rescaling X->cX changes H by c and W by c^2, so there is no speed-independent work-per-instruction conclusion.

A second quantitative consequence controls pointwise strain shape. Write O_H={R^T diag(H,0,-H)R:R orthogonal}. At x in U, diagonalize B0 in the normal plane. In that orthonormal frame the difference from diag(H,0,-H) has squared Frobenius norm

  2(s-H)^2+(3/2)q^2+2|b|^2,

so dist(S(x),O_H)^2 is no larger. Also, the simpler exact deficit formula following from H<=E s is

  Delta=Var_mu(s)+2H(E_mu s-H)+(E_mu s-H)^2
        +(3/4)E_U q^2+E_U|b|^2+(1/2)E_Z|S|^2.

Combining the two displays gives integral_U dist(S,O_H)^2 dmu <=2 Delta. Hence

  mu{x in U: dist(S(x),O_H)>=epsilon} <= 2 Delta/epsilon^2.

This is a genuine quantitative near-equality statement about the strain eigenvalues/orbit almost everywhere. It does not control derivatives of the eigenframe or imply metric closeness to a homogeneous flow.

Exact positive-entropy equality rigidity

Assume Delta=0 and H>0. By (B), alpha=1; otherwise H^2/alpha>H^2. Since Z is closed and null, U is dense. The simple deficit identity forces q=b=0 and s=H throughout U by continuity. Thus SX=0 on U and, by continuity, on M. For x in U, S has eigenvalues H,0,-H; density and continuity of S imply this spectrum and rank two at every point.

A zero p is impossible. Let A=DX_p and k be a unit vector spanning ker S(p). Differentiating SX=0 at p (where X(p)=0) gives S(p)A=0, so im A is contained in span{k}; write A=k tensor w. But S(p)=sym A and S(p)k=0 imply w is parallel to k and then w=0, contradicting rank S(p)=2. Therefore X is nowhere zero.

Let E+ and E- be the smooth normal eigenline bundles of S with eigenvalues +H and -H. The normal cocycle is now the actual derivative on N=X-perp because SX=0: for v(t)=Dphi_t v0 with v0 perpendicular to X,

  d/dt <v(t),X(phi_t x)>=2<Sv(t),X>=0.

The line exponent is zero and the normal exponents are chi,-chi. Since s=H, Ruelle and the cocycle estimate give H<=integral chi<=H, hence chi=H almost everywhere. Let L+(x) be the measurable Oseledets line with exponent H. The autonomous flow commutes with phi_1, and Dphi_t,Dphi_-t are bounded for each fixed t, so Dphi_t maps L+(x) to L+(phi_t x). Put a(x)=<Sv,v>/|v|^2 on L+(x); then a<=H. For v in L+, the exact norm derivative gives

  integral [log|Dphi_n v|/|v|]/n dmu = integral a dmu.

The integrands on the left are bounded by H in absolute value, and converge a.e. to H. Dominated convergence gives integral a=H, so a=H a.e.; equality in the Rayleigh quotient forces L+=E+ a.e. Smoothness of E+ and full support extend Dphi_t-invariance to every x, for each t. Orthogonality is preserved between E+ and E- because d/dt< Dphi_t v+,Dphi_t v->=2H< Dphi_t v+,Dphi_t v-> and the initial pairing is zero. Therefore E- is invariant too, and

  |Dphi_t v+|=e^(Ht)|v+|, |Dphi_t v-|=e^(-Ht)|v-|.

This is a uniform Anosov splitting. Every smooth first integral is constant: its differential vanishes on E+ by t->-infinity, on E- by t->+infinity, and on X by invariance. Since q=X(log r)=0, r is a first integral and hence constant; then nabla_X X=0 follows from 2SX=nabla_X X+grad(r^2/2).

Pass to the joint orientation cover of E+,E-; a connected component has degree at most four. Choose smooth unit sections U+,U-. The exact expansion rates imply

  [X,U+]=-H U+,  [X,U-]=H U-.

Write [U+,U-]=cX+dU++eU-. Jacobi gives Xc=0, Xd=Hd, Xe=-He. Compactness and completeness of X force d=e=0; the first-integral argument makes c constant. The frame has constant metric coefficients (|X|=r, |U+|=|U-|=1, mutually orthogonal) and constant brackets

  [X,U+]=-H U+, [X,U-]=H U-, [U+,U-]=cX.

For c=0 this is the Sol Lie algebra; for c!=0 it is sl(2,R). The Maurer-Cartan equations integrate on the universal cover to the corresponding simply connected Lie group with its left/right-invariant metric. Completeness of the lifted compact metric makes the developing local isometry a covering; since both universal covers are simply connected it is an isometry. Thus the orienting cover is a compact quotient of a Sol or universal-cover-of-SL(2,R) homogeneous metric. This is a global equality classification, not a near-equality rigidity theorem.

At H=0, Delta=0 is exactly S=0, so the equality branch is a Killing field; the positive-entropy Anosov conclusion cannot be extended to this case. Connectedness is used to make c one constant and to conclude smooth first integrals are constant; on disconnected M the result applies componentwise. Smoothness, incompressibility, closedness, dimension three and positive entropy are load-bearing.
