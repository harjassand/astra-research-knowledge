# Finite response bounds, Gaussian smoothing, and acquired sampling

This is a complete independent derivation of a scoped finite sampling reduction and two access obstructions. It is not a breakthrough claim. ## Exact question and interface

Can families 093, 221/222, 231, and 281 be combined into a reusable **acquired** inference/sampling primitive, without silently treating structural existence or a thermodynamic variational law as an available conditional-mean oracle?

The positive result below takes a finite strongly-Rayleigh probability law `mu` on `{0,1}^n` and an evaluator for its positively tilted means

\[
 p_\mu(h)=\frac{\sum_x x\,\mu(x)e^{h\cdot x}}
                    {\sum_x\mu(x)e^{h\cdot x}},\qquad h\in\mathbb R^n.
\]

The evaluator must return all coordinates with a certified error at every field queried by the algorithm. Its acquisition, representation, arithmetic, and precision costs are charged separately. Base samples, a mass-value oracle, a generating polynomial, and a supplied DPP kernel are distinct interfaces.

## 1. Uniform spectral response, including nonhomogeneous laws

Let `C(h)=Cov_mu_h(X)`. Positive coordinate scaling preserves stability. Symmetric homogenization extends the tilted law to `2n` bits of exactly `n` occupied coordinates and retains its original marginal. The Rayleigh inequality gives nonpositive off-diagonal covariance in this extension. Its covariance rows sum to zero, since the total count is fixed. For an original coordinate,

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n|C_{ij}(h)|\le 2C_{ii}(h)
 =2p_i(h)(1-p_i(h))\le\frac12.
\]

Since `C` is symmetric positive semidefinite, the maximum absolute row sum bounds its operator norm:

\[
 0\preceq C(h)\preceq\tfrac12 I\quad\text{for every }h.
 \tag{1}
\]

Differentiating a finite partition sum gives `D p_mu(h)=C(h)`, so the mean map is globally `1/2` Lipschitz in Euclidean norm. No homogeneity or full-support assumption is required. Uniform mass on `(1,0),(0,1)` has covariance eigenvalue `1/2`, so the universal constant is sharp.

## 2. Autonomous finite sampler

Sample `X~mu` and independently Brownian motion `B`. Write

\[
 H_t=t(X-\tfrac12\mathbf1)+B_t.
\]

Bayes' formula is especially simple because each `(x_i-1/2)^2=1/4`: conditional on `H_t=h`, the density ratio of `X` is `exp(h.x)` times a factor independent of `x`. A Brownian bridge is independent of this endpoint and `X`, so the full-history posterior mean is also `p_mu(H_t)`. The classical innovations identity, or a direct finite-dimensional filtering calculation, yields

\[
 dH_t=[p_\mu(H_t)-\tfrac12\mathbf1]dt+dW_t,\qquad H_0=0.
 \tag{2}
\]

The drift is bounded and globally Lipschitz, hence there is a unique strong solution. Its entire path law equals the reference observation law. The time dependence in the source formula disappears after this deterministic shift; it is not an extra stationarity assumption.

At time `T`, threshold the coordinates at zero. In the reference coupling each coordinate fails with probability `Phi(-sqrt(T)/2)`, so

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf1\{H_T\ge0\}),\mu)
 \le n\Phi(-\sqrt T/2)\le n e^{-T/8}.
 \tag{3}
\]

### Explicit approximate-mean Euler theorem

Let a deterministic measurable mean evaluator `p_hat` take values in `[0,1]^n` and satisfy

\[
 \sup_h\max_i|\widehat p_i(h)-p_i(h)|\le\zeta.
 \tag{4}
\]

For `K` steps of length `eta=T/K<=1`, run

\[
 \widehat H_{k+1}=\widehat H_k+
 \eta[\widehat p(\widehat H_k)-\tfrac12\mathbf1]
 +\sqrt\eta\,G_k,\qquad G_k\overset{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,I_n).
 \tag{5}
\]

Then the output `X_hat=1{H_hat_K>=0}` satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\operatorname{Law}(\widehat X),\mu)
 \le n\Phi(-\sqrt T/2)+
 \sqrt{\frac{nT\eta}{16}+\frac{nT\eta^2}{96}
                        +\frac{nT\zeta^2}{2}}
 \le n e^{-T/8}+
 \sqrt{\frac{7nT\eta}{96}+\frac{nT\zeta^2}{2}}.
 \tag{6}
\]

**Complete derivation.** Compare the exact law `P` of (2) with the continuous Euler interpolation `Q` of (5), whose drift is frozen at the last grid point. Both drifts are bounded; finite-time Girsanov applies. Under the exact observation law, for `delta=t-k eta`, the reference Brownian increment is independent of `X`. Therefore

\[
 E_P\|H_t-H_{k\eta}\|^2=n\delta+n\delta^2/4.
\]

By (1), the squared difference between exact drift and true drift at the grid point is at most one quarter of this. Equation (4), the inequality `|a+c|^2<=2|a|^2+2|c|^2`, and the expected Girsanov identity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D(P\Vert Q)
 &=\frac12 E_P\int_0^T
   \|p(H_t)-\widehat p(H_{\lfloor t/\eta\rfloor\eta})\|^2dt\\
 &\le \int_0^T
     [n\delta/4+n\delta^2/16+n\zeta^2]dt\\
 &=nT\eta/8+nT\eta^2/48+nT\zeta^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Pinsker gives `TV(P,Q)<=sqrt(D(P||Q)/2)`. Taking the threshold map cannot increase TV; (3) gives (6). For an exact mean evaluator, omit the factor-two split and obtain the stronger drift term

\[
 \sqrt{nT\eta/32+nT\eta^2/192}.
 \tag{7}
\]

There is no exponentially amplified pathwise discretization estimate in (6). It uses the exact observation path's averaged increments, and it controls the entire finite output law, not only one coordinate or normalized Wasserstein distance.

For `0<epsilon<1`, an explicit sufficient choice is

\[
 T=8\log(2n/\epsilon),\qquad
 K=\left\lceil\max\{T,7nT^2/(12\epsilon^2)\}\right\rceil,
 \qquad \zeta=\epsilon/(2\sqrt{nT}).
 \tag{8}
\]

The two squared terms under the last root in (6) are each at most `epsilon^2/8`, while the decoding error is at most `epsilon/2`. Thus TV is at most `epsilon`. This costs `K=O(n log^2(n/epsilon)/epsilon^2)` complete mean evaluations and `O(nK)` additional real arithmetic/Gaussian draws. Total evaluator cost is `K C_mean(n,zeta,h)`, with storage and any field-dependent bit precision still charged. Ideal Gaussian draws and real arithmetic are the interface in (5); a bit-complexity claim requires separately budgeting Gaussian generation, rounding, and evaluator precision.

This reduction applies after an arbitrary known field `a`, by using `p_{mu_a}(h)=p_mu(a+h)`. The evaluator must support the translated fields; a base-law simulator alone does not supply them.

### A finite-precision implementation contract

The real/Gaussian interface can be converted to a finite-bit one without assuming a dimension-free arithmetic cost. Take rational `0<epsilon<1` (a rational smaller than a requested real tolerance also suffices). Let `L` be the smallest positive integer with `2^L>=4n/epsilon`, found by exact rational comparisons, and take the acquired integer `T=8L`. Choose an **exact-mean ideal Euler comparator**, `K=ceil(max(T,7nT^2/(12 epsilon^2)))`, and rational `eta=T/K`. The integer ceiling is also an exact rational calculation. Since `T>=8 log(4n/epsilon)`, equations (3) and (7) give its output TV at most `epsilon/2`. Let `A=2^(7L)`; the elementary bound `e<3` implies `e^(T/2)<A`, so no transcendental threshold must be represented exactly.

Let the finite implementation approximate each true mean coordinate to `zeta_num`, and couple each generated Gaussian **increment** to the comparator's increment with maximum coordinate error `omega`, outside a total exceptional probability `rho`. Include arithmetic rounding in `omega`. The row bound in Section 1 and the recursion imply

\[
 \|H_K^{\rm finite}-H_K^{\rm ideal}\|_\infty
 \le 2(e^{T/2}-1)(\zeta_{\rm num}+\omega/\eta).
\]

The comparator's final increment is an independent Gaussian of variance `eta`; hence every marginal endpoint density is at most `(2 pi eta)^(-1/2)`. Set

\[
 r=\epsilon\eta/(8n),\quad
 \rho=\epsilon/4,\quad
 \zeta_{\rm num}=r/[4(A-1)],\quad
 \omega=\eta r/[4(A-1)].
\]

All these parameters are rational. The endpoint error on the coupled good event is at most `r`. Since `eta<=1`, our `r` is no larger than `epsilon sqrt(2 pi eta)/(8n)`; the chance any comparator coordinate is within `r` of zero is therefore at most `epsilon/4`. Thus the finite and ideal output laws are at most `epsilon/2` apart, giving total TV at most `epsilon`.

A finite-bit Gaussian generator satisfying this contract exists explicitly. Couple a uniform `U` to the midpoint `U_hat` of its dyadic cell of length `2^-b`, set `alpha=rho/(4nK)`, and restrict to `U in [2alpha,1-2alpha]`. The excluded event has probability `rho/(nK)`. If `2^-b<=2alpha`, both arguments of the normal quantile lie in `[alpha,1-alpha]`. On that interval its derivative is at most `sqrt(2pi)/(2alpha)` by the Gaussian tail bound. Choosing

\[
 2^{-b}\le\min\{2\alpha,\omega\alpha/3\}
\]

works by exact dyadic/rational comparisons, since `sqrt(2 pi eta)<3`. Evaluating the quantile to increment error `omega/4` leaves enough room for grid error and update rounding within `omega`; this includes approximating `sqrt(eta)` during increment generation. A union bound covers all `nK` increments. Since `A<128(4n/epsilon)^7`, `b=O(log(n/epsilon))`; evaluator and arithmetic precision also require only this order of **fractional** bits (with the represented field magnitudes accounted for separately). Quantile evaluation can be implemented by finite bisection with certified CDF error. Parameter acquisition, rational arithmetic and transcendental evaluation are charged. The resulting evaluator accuracy is substantially stronger than (8), and its cost must be charged. This is a rigorous finite-bit *reduction contract*, not a claim that any given symbolic/matrix evaluator already meets it or that its bit complexity is dimension free.

## 3. Gaussian smoothing: the cross-family route exists but is unnecessary

Let `Z=X+sqrt(s)G` for independent standard Gaussian `G`, and let `q_s` be its smooth positive density. The posterior given `Z=z` is the tilt with `h_i=z_i/s-1/(2s)`. Direct differentiation gives

\[
 D^2[-\log q_s](z)=I/s-C(h)/s^2.
 \tag{9}
\]

Consequently every finite SR law has log-concave Gaussian smoothing for `s>=1/2`. This is a sharp universal threshold: for uniform mass on `(1,0),(0,1)`, at `z=(1/2,1/2)` the curvature along `(1,-1)/sqrt(2)` is `1/s-1/(2s^2)`, negative whenever `s<1/2`.

Integrating the tilted covariance bound twice gives the centered linear MGF bound

\[
 \log E e^{t v\cdot(X-EX)}\le t^2\|v\|^2/4.
\]

Thus centered `Z` has linear MGF variance proxy `s+1/2`. A Gaussian integration identity shows that its squared-Orlicz parameter in 093's convention can be taken as

\[
 a^2=(8/3)(s+1/2):\quad E e^{(v\cdot(Z-EZ))^2/a^2}\le2
 \quad(\|v\|=1).
\]

**Conditional use of 093.** Its Theorem `thm:main` would therefore give an entropy coefficient `C (8/3)(s+1/2)` for `s>=1/2`. This follows from the exact interfaces; it is not an analogy. We have not adopted the independently unaudited decisive steps of 093 for this conclusion.

**Stronger direct fact, independent of 093.** In the convention `Ent(f^2)<=2R int|Df|^2`, the Gaussian-smoothed SR law satisfies

\[
 R(q_s)\le s^2(e^{1/s}-1)\quad\text{for every }s>0.
 \tag{10}
\]

Proof: the endpoint of (2) is a smooth functional of the Wiener input. Its Cameron-Martin endpoint derivative from time `u` to `T` has operator norm at most `e^{(T-u)/2}` by (1). For `F(W)=f(H_T/T)`,

\[
 \|D_{\mathcal H}F\|^2
 \le T^{-2}\int_0^T e^{T-u}du\,|Df(H_T/T)|^2
 = (e^T-1)T^{-2}|Df(H_T/T)|^2.
\]

Apply the Wiener Gaussian LSI `Ent(F^2)<=2E||D_H F||^2` and take `T=1/s`. The law of `H_T/T` is the centered-by-`1/2` version of `q_s`; translations preserve the inequality. Smooth cylinder approximation, or the differentiability of this bounded smooth finite-dimensional SDE, justifies the Wiener calculation. In particular at critical smoothing `s=1/2`, `R<1.598` (entropy coefficient `<3.195`). No log-concavity is needed for (10).

The independent check worker also found an older heat inverse contraction/Bakry–Émery route giving `R(q_{1/2})<=2`. The existence of these old-input proofs removes the supposed new dependence on 093.

## 4. Exact acquisition obstruction A: finding support

Give an algorithm only the blackbox mass-value oracle `mu(x)` on the finite cube, and the promise that `mu` is strongly Rayleigh. No nonzero support point or sample is supplied. For each secret `v in {0,1}^n`, let `mu_v=delta_v`. Its generating polynomial is the monomial `prod_i z_i^{v_i}`, which is real stable; every tilted covariance is zero, and every Gaussian smoothing is simply a translated Gaussian.

For a uniform secret `v`, any randomized algorithm with at most `q` value queries can output `v` with average probability at most `(q+1)/2^n`. Condition on its private randomness: before the first hit, all oracle answers are zero, so the algorithm can inspect at most `q` candidate points and make at most one additional guess. Hence uniform TV accuracy `epsilon` for every `mu_v` requires

\[
 q\ge(1-\epsilon)2^n-1.
 \tag{11}
\]

This is an oracle-interface lower bound, not a hardness result for a supplied DPP matrix, symbolic monomial, initial feasible state, or base-law sample. Those richer inputs identify the point immediately. Even the best possible response bound cannot replace an acquisition interface.

## 5. Exact acquisition obstruction B: uniform tilted inference from base samples

In one dimension, all Bernoulli laws are strongly Rayleigh. Compare `mu_0=delta_0` with `mu_1=Bernoulli(p)`, `p=1/(1+e^B)`, for known positive field `B`. Then

\[
 (\mu_0)_B=\delta_0,\qquad(\mu_1)_B=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2).
\]

A base-sample algorithm accurate in TV to `epsilon<1/4` under both hypotheses must produce output laws at distance at least `1/2-2epsilon`. Couple the sample streams to remain all-zero until the first success under `mu_1`. If the stopping rule under `mu_0` takes `N_0` samples, the chance this coupling separates before that stopping time is at most `p E_0 N_0`. The output laws therefore differ by at most that value. Consequently

\[
 E_0 N_0\ge(1/2-2\epsilon)(1+e^B).
 \tag{12}
\]

This covers private randomness, adaptive stopping, and unbounded stopping time with finite expectation. It refutes dimension-free or polynomial-in-`B` uniform tilted inference from base samples. It does **not** prove that approximating the drift in averaged error along its own typical trajectory is exponentially hard, nor does it obstruct sampling the base law when fresh base samples are already provided. That distinction is load-bearing.

## 6. Why the spin-law and QAOA interfaces do not currently compose

221's finite Gibbs laws need not be SR. Take its permitted two-spin interaction `theta=beta J sigma_1 sigma_2`, with symmetric `J in {-1,1}`, `a=cosh beta`, `b=tanh(beta J)`, `f_i(sigma)=sigma`, and zero external field. This satisfies the source factorization/positivity conditions: odd moments of `-b` vanish and even moments are nonnegative. Conditional on a positive interaction joining two distinct sites, the binary bits `(1+sigma_i)/2` have positive off-diagonal covariance `tanh(beta)/4`, violating Rayleigh. Thus the uniform SR response is not available just because the thermodynamic pressure has a variational formula.

222 permits bounded even activations rewarding aligned signs. With two Gaussian pattern coordinates near the same positive value, such an activation gives positive spin covariance on an event of positive disorder probability. The spherical priors also are not Lebesgue-density log-concave laws on the ambient space. The supplied Gaussian **disorder** is not a log-concave **Gibbs target**. No dimension-free target response theorem is supplied by the stated pressure limits.

281's source Theorem `thm:main` gives `for every epsilon, exists finite p and deterministic angles, then n->infinity`, in expected energy. Its introduction explicitly states no quantitative finite-size estimate, depth bound, or efficient angle-selection procedure. This does not provide the SR mean evaluator or a sampling-distance guarantee. Its additional quantum controls and their eventual removal need their own proof audit; they are not used here.

As a simple general discriminator, the non-SR law placing equal mass on `0^n` and `1^n` has covariance operator norm `n/4`. Its Gaussian smoothing at the symmetric center has curvature eigenvalue `1/s-n/(4s^2)`. Any attempt to import an absolute constant response/log-concavity threshold into arbitrary spin mixtures fails on this explicit family. This example is not claimed to arise at every parameter of any named spin glass.

## Concrete downstream use and next falsifiable step

For a **supplied** finite DPP kernel `0<=K<=I`, a stable mean evaluator can be built from

\[
 A=I-K+K^{1/2}\operatorname{diag}(e^h)K^{1/2},\qquad
 p_i=e^{h_i}[K^{1/2}A^{-1}K^{1/2}]_{ii}.
\]

`A` is positive definite even if `K` has eigenvalues zero or one, and its condition number is at most `max(1,e^{h_max})/min(1,e^{h_min})`. This is a fully typed route to the evaluator, with field-dependent precision charged. A finite weighted graph likewise supplies the tilted mean through weighted Laplacian solves. Standard DPP/tree samplers are competent and generally faster comparators; this route is principally a reusable proof/robustness primitive, not a claimed performance gain.

The highest-value next allocation is an evaluator-acquisition theorem for a nontrivial target class beyond supplied matrices, with averaged drift error certified under the true observation path and explicit representation/sample/bit costs. First test any candidate against (11), (12), and the positive-covariance spin example. For the infinite FUSF claim, a separate effective finite-volume/coding modulus remains essential; the finite theorem here does not furnish one.
