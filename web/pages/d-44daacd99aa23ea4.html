# Boundary-penalty calculation for the mechanical SSH chain

## Exact interface

Take (Nge2) scalar generalized coordinates (u_1,dots,u_N) and (N-1) linearized bond-extension constraints

\[
(Cu)_i=q_1u_i+q_2u_{i+1},\qquad i=1,dots,N-1,
\]

with (q_1,q_2>0), (q_1\ne q_2). Set the spring/mass normalization to one as in the mechanical SSH example of Kane–Lubensky. The ideal Hessian is (H_0=C^TC). A weak boundary support/hinge penalty at each endpoint is modeled by

\[
H_\kappa=H_0+\kappa P,\qquad P=e_1e_1^T+e_Ne_N^T,\quad \kappa>0.
\]

This is a linear, conservative, finite-dimensional model. It does not include prestress, nonlinear buckling, damping, friction, material yield, or manufacturing disorder.

## Ideal mode and gap

The Bloch compatibility symbol is (q_1+q_2e^{ik}), so its singular value is (|q_1+q_2e^{ik}|), whose minimum is (|q_2-q_1|). Its winding about zero is one for (q_2>q_1), and zero for (q_1>q_2). The open chain has one exact zero mode because (C) has (N-1) rows and full row rank:

\[
u_{0,i}=Z^{-1/2}(-q_1/q_2)^{i-1},\qquad
Z=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(q_1/q_2)^{2j}.
\]

For (q_2>q_1), this mode is left-localized; for (q_1>q_2), it is right-localized. This distinction must be read together with the local boundary count in the Maxwell–Calladine index: in the latter termination the single mode comes from the finite chain's excess coordinate, and the bulk winding is zero.

The (N-1) nonzero eigenvalues of (H_0) are those of (CC^T), a tridiagonal matrix with diagonal (q_1^2+q_2^2) and off-diagonal (q_1q_2). They are

\[
\lambda_j=q_1^2+q_2^2+2q_1q_2\cos(j\pi/N),\qquad j=1,\dots,N-1.
\]

Hence the finite-chain nonzero gap obeys

\[
\lambda_2(H_0)=q_1^2+q_2^2-2q_1q_2\cos(\pi/N)>(q_2-q_1)^2=:\Delta.
\]

## Penalty theorem

Let (\rho=u_0^TPu_0=|u_{0,1}|^2+|u_{0,N}|^2>0). Since (P\succeq0) and ‖P‖=1:

1. The variational principle gives (0<\lambda_1(H_\kappa)\le \kappa\rho). The strict lower inequality follows because the only null vector of (H_0) has nonzero endpoint entries and is penalized by (P).
2. Eigenvalue monotonicity gives (\lambda_2(H_\kappa)\ge\lambda_2(H_0)>\Delta). Thus if (\kappa\rho<\Delta), exactly one eigenvalue lies below the original bulk gap.
3. The zero eigenvalue of (H_0) is simple and separated from the rest by (>\Delta). First-order eigenvalue perturbation therefore yields

\[
\lambda_1(H_\kappa)=\kappa\rho+O(\kappa^2/\Delta),\qquad\kappa\downarrow0.
\]

4. For (\kappa\le\Delta/2), the Davis–Kahan theorem bounds the angle between the perturbed lowest eigenspace and (u_0) by (\sin\angle\le\kappa/(\Delta-\kappa)\le2\kappa/\Delta).

All four statements are finite-matrix facts under the stated interface. They do not prove a universal claim about a fabricated lattice.

## Static stiffness asymptotics

For a unit boundary force vector (a), define compliance (\chi_a(\kappa)=a^TH_\kappa^{-1}a), and effective stiffness (k_a(\kappa)=1/\chi_a(\kappa)). Decompose the coordinates into (\operatorname{span}(u_0)\oplus u_0^\perp). On the complement, (H_0\) is positive definite with inverse norm at most (1/\Delta). A Schur complement of the block matrix of (H_\kappa) shows

\[
\chi_a(\kappa)=\frac{|a^Tu_0|^2}{\kappa\rho}+O(1),\qquad\kappa\downarrow0.
\]

For (q_2>q_1), write (r=q_1/q_2<1). Then

\[
|u_{0,1}|^2=\frac{1-r^2}{1-r^{2N}},\qquad
|u_{0,N}|^2=\frac{(1-r^2)r^{2(N-1)}}{1-r^{2N}},\qquad
\rho=|u_{0,1}|^2(1+r^{2(N-1)}).
\]

Thus the singular compliance terms are

\[
\chi_L(\kappa)=\frac{1}{\kappa(1+r^{2(N-1)})}+O(1),\qquad
\chi_R(\kappa)=\frac{r^{2(N-1)}}{\kappa(1+r^{2(N-1)})}+\chi_R^{(0)}+O(\kappa),
\]

where (\chi_R^{(0)}) is the finite regular part of the right-end compliance after removing its (1/\kappa) pole. It depends on the full chain and support placement. Let (k_R^{(0)}=1/\chi_R^{(0)}). For large (N) and in the regime where these leading terms dominate,

\[
\frac{k_R}{k_L}\approx
\frac{k_R^{(0)}}{\kappa+k_R^{(0)}r^{2(N-1)}}.
\]

This gives two engineering scales for target hard/soft stiffness ratio (R): roughly (\kappa\lesssim k_R^{(0)}/R), and a sufficiently small overlap (r^{2(N-1)}\lesssim1/R), equivalently

\[
N\gtrsim 1+\frac{\xi}{2}\log R,\qquad \xi=\frac{1}{\log(q_2/q_1)}.
\]

These are leading-order design scales, not a uniform bound for general lattices. In particular, (k_R^{(0)}), probe shape, support layout, and the map from a real hinge to (P) must be measured or computed for the actual design. Swapping (q_1,q_2) moves the low-compliance edge to the other end in this matched finite-chain calculation.

## Prior-art boundary

The exact model is the 1D mechanical SSH construction in Kane–Lubensky, arXiv:1308.0554v2, especially their (Q(k)=q_1+q_2e^{ik}) example and local index theorem. Their 2D result is

\[
\nu^S=\nu_L^S+\nu_T^S,\qquad
\nu_T^S=\int_{\partial S}\frac{d^{d-1}S}{V_\mathrm{cell}}R_T\cdot\hat n,
\]

with the edge count per cell (\tilde\nu_T=G\cdot R_T/(2\pi)). The derivation requires a gapped bulk and a standard unit-cell gauge; the local count depends on termination. Rocks–Mehta, arXiv:2208.07419v2, explicitly incorporate heterogeneous stiffness and prestress through susceptibility-based Maxwell–Calladine identities. Xiu et al., PNAS 119 (2022), report linear soft-edge stiffness growth with their auxiliary spring stiffness, convergence of opposite-edge stiffnesses as (k_2/k_1) approaches one, and measured finite stiffness contrast in 3D-printed lattices. Consequently, the penalty calculation here is a transparent specialization and design-scale derivation; it is not a novelty claim.
