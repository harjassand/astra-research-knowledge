## Exact fixture and N76 potential

Use the unit-rate reversible network

    A+C <-> A+B <-> B,     C <-> 0.

Its complex set is `{0,C,B,A+B,A+C}`.  At `x=(a,b,c)`, put

    M(a,b,c) = max(1,b,c,ab,ac),
    exp(F(a,b,c)) = a! b! c! / M(a,b,c).

This is exactly the residual-factorial N76 definition for this complex set,
since each available source's falling-factorial monomial is respectively
`1,c,b,ab,ac`.  In particular at `x_a=(a,0,1)`, `F=log((a-1)!)` and the
physical-time drift is `LF(x_a)=log(a)>0`, as c10_s02 established.

## Reconstructed two-event skeleton drift

Let `Y_n` be the embedded reaction-event chain; its transition probabilities
are the enabled propensities divided by their sum.  Starting at `x_a`, the
total propensity is `a+2`, with three possible first events:

* `A+C -> A+B`, rate `a`, reaches `u=(a,1,0)` with no change in F.
* `C -> 0`, rate `1`, reaches `z=(a,0,0)` with `+log(a)`.  The only next
  event at z is `0 -> C`, which returns to x_a with `-log(a)`, so the net
  two-event change is zero.
* `0 -> C`, rate `1`, reaches `w=(a,0,2)` with no change in F.  At w, the
  transitions `A+C -> A+B` (rate `2a`) and `C -> 0` (rate 2) preserve F;
  `0 -> C` (rate 1) has F increment `log(2)`.  Thus its one-next-event
  expected increment is `log(2)/(2a+3)`.

At `u=(a,1,0)`, the total rate is `2a+2`: `A+B -> A+C` (rate a) and
`0 -> C` (rate 1) preserve F; `A+B -> B` (rate a) has increment
`-log(a-1)`; `B -> A+B` (rate 1) has increment `log(a)`.  Directly summing
all two-event paths therefore gives, for every integer `a>=2`,

    E[F(Y_2)-F(Y_0) | Y_0=x_a]
      = - a^2 log(a-1) / ((a+2)(2a+2))
        + a log(a) / ((a+2)(2a+2))
        + log(2) / ((a+2)(2a+3)).                    (1)

Equation (1) is a jump-count skeleton identity; it is not `LF`, and it does
not by itself give a global Foster condition.  It is positive at `a=2`.  It is
strictly negative for every `a>=3`: set `D=a log(a-1)-log(a)`.  Then
`D >= a log(2)-log(a)`.  The latter is increasing for real `a>=3` and its
value at 3 is `log(8/3)>log(2)`, so `D>log(2)`.  Also
`a/(2a+2)>=3/8` and `1/(2a+3)<=1/9`.  After factoring `1/(a+2)`, the
negative term in (1) has magnitude greater than `(3/8)log(2)`, while the
positive remainder is at most `(1/9)log(2)`.  This proves the sign without
numerical approximation.

This is an exact path correction of the bad ray: one-step `F` drift is
positive, but the two-jump expected additive-F drift is negative after the
finite exception `a=2`.  It uses the actual catalytic sequence and the
low-rate `0 <-> C` return, rather than applying a monotone scalar transform
or the N76 exponential-moment bound.  A bounded script reconstructs the
complex minima, transitions, two-step formula, detailed-balance edges and
component predicate; see `s02_boundary_audit.py` and its captured output.

## Event count versus physical time

The total exit rate is

    Lambda(a,b,c) = ac + 2ab + b + c + 1 >= 1.

Hence each embedded-chain holding time has conditional mean
`1/Lambda <= 1`.  If a global jump-chain skeleton Foster proof supplied finite
expected jump count to a finite core, this bound would charge an upper bound
of one physical-time unit per jump.  The ray calculation alone supplies no
such global hitting-time bound: states outside `{(a,0,1)}` can be reached in
the second jump, and every state needs its own controlled drift or a common
finite-step inequality.

An exploratory scan found no positive `P^2 F-F` values among 8,575 points in
`Gamma={(a,b,c): a+b>=1}` with each coordinate at most 20 and total count at
least 15. In the whole 0..20 cube it found 45 positive two-step drifts, all
with total count at most 13. It used floating logarithms and is finite
diagnostic evidence only; it cannot establish the missing all-state
inequality. The scan is reproducible with `s02_skeleton_scan.py`; output is
saved under `outputs/round6/c10_l02/`.

The other conspicuous unbounded boundary ray `z_n=(0,n,0)` also admits a
closed two-step formula. First, the only events are `B->A+B` (rate n) and
`0->C` (rate 1), both F-preserving. From `(1,n,0)`, the only F-changing event
is `A+B->A+C` at rate n, with increment `-log(n-1)`; the total rate there is
`3n+1`. From `(0,n,1)`, the only F-changing next event is `0->C` at rate 1,
with increment `log(2)`; its total rate is `n+2`. Thus

    E[F(Y_2)-F(Y_0) | Y_0=z_n]
      = - n^2 log(n-1) / ((n+1)(3n+1))
        + log(2) / ((n+1)(n+2)).                    (2)

This is positive at `n=2` and negative for every `n>=3`: after factoring
`1/(n+1)`, the negative coefficient is at least `(9/10)log(2)`, whereas the
positive term is at most `(1/5)log(2)`. Again, this repairs a ray only.

## Scope and prior art

The three-species fixture is positive recurrent: it has the product-Poisson
law conditioned on `a+b>=1`, and `LN=b-ab+1-c<=N+1` gives a direct
nonexplosion bound.  Thus this path correction is about a certificate
transfer, not recurrence novelty.  N76's full proof/code packet was absent;
only its supplied summary and this fixture's independently reconstructed
identities are used.

Path-averaging/catalytic prior art is close: Anderson, Cappelletti, Fan and
Kim (arXiv:2309.06970, Example 2.3) analyze the two-species catalyst chain
with `X2` catalyzing `X1` creation/destruction and use a path/spectral method
where ordinary Foster and canonical paths are inadequate.  This is neighboring
prior art, not the same three-species `F` identity or the ray-specific
two-jump formula.  The general bimolecular weakly reversible recurrence
question also remains open as an existence-of-stationary-distributions gate:
Xu, arXiv:2409.05340v2, Theorem 4.13 says second-order weakly reversible
systems are positive recurrent iff every communicating class has a stationary
distribution.  Wiuf-Xu, arXiv:2012.07954v2, Corollary 4.12 already proves
positive recurrence on one-dimensional weakly reversible classes; this
overlaps the initial rank-one transfer.  Historical novelty is UNKNOWN.
