# Independent audit: d(d−1) noncomplementary weights give deterministic exact identification

Status: **PASS**, independently reconstructed from b_math's proposed proof in `notes/b_math/cycle3/FINITE_WEIGHT_BOUNDARY.md`. This is an exact-identifiability upper bound, not an acquisition or numerical-stability theorem. External novelty is unverified.

Fix dimensiond≥2, a known Hermitian frame with scalar ordinary commutant, and any two faithful trace-one states sigma/rho. Use the weighted convention
\[
 -i[A,\tau]=(1-\lambda)\tau S_A^{\tau,\lambda}
                  +\lambda S_A^{\tau,\lambda}\tau.
\]
Suppose the entire scores agree atd(d−1)weights whose values \(h=\lambda(1-\lambda)\) are pairwise distinct. Then sigma=rho.

In an eigenbasis of tau, with positive eigenvaluesp_i, the Hermitian part of the score is
\[
 H_A^\tau(h)=\tfrac12(S_A^{\tau,\lambda}+S_A^{\tau,1-\lambda}),
\]
\[
 (H_A^\tau(h))_{ij}
 =\frac{i(p_i-p_j)(p_i+p_j)}{2[p_ip_j+h(p_i-p_j)^2]}A_{ij}.
\]
The adjoint identity \(S^{\lambda\dagger}=S^{1-\lambda}\) means that equality of an entire score already supplies equality of this Hermitian part, without measuring the complementary weight separately. Diagonal and equal-eigenvalue entries vanish.

LetR=d(d−1)/2. A scalar common denominator over unequal unordered eigenvalue pairs has degree at mostR in h; every numerator has degree at mostR−1. This remains true after rotation into the physical basis because the eigenbasis is fixed for each state as h varies. No spectrum is supplied to the estimator; spectral coordinates are used only in the proof. For the difference of the two physical-basis score parts, multiply the two state-specific scalar denominators. Every numerator has degree at most2R−1, and its denominator is nonzero on[0,1/4]. Equality at2R distinct h values forces all numerator polynomials to vanish identically. Repeated eigenvalues reduce degrees and cause no singularity.

Consequently the Hermitian parts agree for every h in[0,1/4], and symmetry of the complementary weights gives
\[
 \int_0^1 S_A^{\rho,\lambda}\,d\lambda
 =\int_0^1 H_A^\rho(\lambda(1-\lambda))\,d\lambda
 =\int_0^1 H_A^\sigma(\lambda(1-\lambda))\,d\lambda
 =\int_0^1 S_A^{\sigma,\lambda}\,d\lambda.
\]
Entrywise integration yields
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{p_i-p_j}{(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j}\,d\lambda
 =\log p_i-\log p_j,
 \qquad\int_0^1S_A^{\tau,\lambda}\,d\lambda=-i[A,\log\tau].
\]
The equality therefore implies \([A_a,\log\rho-\log\sigma]=0\) for every frame direction. Ordinary irreducibility makes the logarithm difference scalar. Normalization then makes the scalar zero andrho=sigma.

This proof does not infer simultaneous identification from pairwise probability-one events; it establishes equality for every competitor directly through a deterministic polynomial identity. It also does not omit a target-dependent eigenbasis: physical-basis rotation preserves rational degree, and each state receives its own scalar common denominator before the difference is formed.

Complementary weights have the same h, so they do not count as distinct interpolation points. Choose all weights strictly below1/2 if necessary to get distinct h without approaching endpoints.

For n qubitsd=2^n, the directional count is exponential in n. The proof assumes complete operator scores already supplied. Near-polynomial-root conditioning, candidate-observable compilation, repeated loss estimation and optimization costs remain unbounded here.
