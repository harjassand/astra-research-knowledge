## Exact question and legal interface

Input is the expanded sparse rational coefficient list of a polynomial p in n named variables. No Gram matrix, factors, zeros, positive margin, or hidden parameters are supplied. Desired output is an exact nonnegativity certificate, or a scoped class rejection. General polynomial nonnegativity and membership in the real SOS cone are separate questions. A failed rational-certificate search must not imply either negativity or non-SOS.

Recent entry source: Zuo et al., NSPI, [2605.15445v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15445v1), submitted 14 May 2026 (abstract page submission history verified). ## A. Any included zero forbids ambient positive definiteness

Let v(x) be a monomial vector and G PSD with p=v^T Gv. If p(z)=0, then ||G^(1/2)v(z)||²=0, hence Gv(z)=0. If v(z) is nonzero, G is singular. This argument applies to one zero, finitely many zeros, or a manifold of zeros. With the full degree-at-most-d basis, the constant coordinate guarantees v(z) is nonzero.

Concrete finite-zero counterexample: p(x)=(x−1)², v=(1,x). Coefficient matching fixes G=[[1,−1],[−1,1]], rank one. The unique global minimizer is the nonzero point x=1. Exact full rank cannot result from refinement. An approximately coefficient-matching full-rank matrix is possible, but its least eigenvalue must disappear when the residual vanishes. This is exactly the distinction the paper's finite-zero sentence misses.

Also, attainment of a positive minimum alone does not force singularity: p=(x−1)²+1 has positive-definite Gram [[2,−1],[−1,1]]. The relevant condition is p(z)=0, not merely that z is a minimizer.

This kernel lemma is pre-existing: Laplagne 2018 Proposition 3.2 and Sections 3.1–3.2. The counterexample independently reconstructs its consequence.

## B. Fixed rank can impose a positive residual floor

For 0<epsilon<=1 and n>k, consider p_e=x1²+epsilon sum_(i=2)^n xi². In the homogeneous linear basis x its unique Gram is D=diag(1,epsilon,...,epsilon). Any k-square linear ansatz has Gram H=LL^T of rank at most k. The coefficient residual obeys

  ||coeff(x^T(H−D)x)||² = sum_i(Hii−Dii)² + 4 sum_(i<j) Hij²
                           >= ||H−D||_F² >= (n−k)epsilon².

The last inequality is independently obtained by the usual rank-approximation variational argument: H annihilates a subspace of dimension at least n−k; select orthonormal u_j there and sum ||(D−H)u_j||²>=epsilon² per vector. Choosing H with the first k diagonal entries of D attains the bound. Therefore the exact infimum coefficient residual is epsilon sqrt(n−k)>0. Arbitrary precision cannot remove it while factor rank remains k. The residual floor can nonetheless be made arbitrarily small by choosing rational epsilon small.

This refutes an unrestricted reading of Proposition C.1; a qualified theorem would need correct factor rank, local regularity and a convergence basin. It does not show that an implementation trying enough alternative structures fails on these trivial inputs. A competent comparator immediately reads D and returns n weighted coordinate squares in linear time.

## C. Positive theorem: acquired face for a class with exponentially many isolated zeros

Let Q be rational symmetric positive definite and a in Q^n with all ai>0. Define q_i(x)=xi²−ai and

  p(x)=q(x)^T Q q(x).

The zero set is exactly z_s=(s1 sqrt(a1),...,sn sqrt(an)), s in {−1,1}^n. It has 2^n isolated points; no supplied listing or algebraic-field representation is needed for the algorithm below.

**Factor-space lemma.** Every polynomial g of degree at most two that vanishes at all z_s lies in span_R{q1,...,qn}. Write

  g=c0+sum_i ci xi+sum_i di xi²+sum_(i<j) eij xi xj.

On z_s this becomes a Fourier expansion on the sign cube with constant c0+sum_i di ai, singleton coefficients ci sqrt(ai), and pair coefficients eij sqrt(ai aj). The sign characters are independent: multiply by a chosen character and average over all signs. Vanishing of g therefore forces ci=eij=0 and c0=−sum_i di ai, proving the claim. This is a universal derivation, not a finite enumeration.

**Unique Gram theorem.** Let v contain every monomial of degree at most two, N=(n+1)(n+2)/2. Every PSD G with p=v^TGv is the SAME matrix BQB^T, where the columns of B are the coefficient vectors of q_i. In particular its rank is n, so it has N−n exact null eigenvalues despite the finite zero set.

Proof: any PSD G decomposes into squared quadratic factors; each factor vanishes at every zero, and the lemma places each coefficient vector in range(B). Thus G=BHB^T for some H PSD. Comparing coefficients of xi^4 gives Hii=Qii. Comparing coefficients of xi²xj² gives 2Hij=2Qij. Hence H=Q, proving uniqueness. Since B has full column rank and Q is PD, the rank is n. There is no ambient strictly feasible solution to recover.

**Coefficient-only discovery algorithm.** Inspect the expanded input. Read Qii from xi^4, and Qij from half the coefficient of xi²xj². Read bi from minus half the coefficient of xi². Obtain exact LDL^T of Q; reject this class if it is not PD. Solve Qa=b rationally. Check ai>0. Finally expand q^TQq and compare every coefficient, including all zero/missing monomials and the constant a^TQa. A match yields the certificate

  p=sum_k Dkk [sum_i Lik (xi²−ai)]²,

where Q=LDL^T and Dkk>0. A mismatch rejects only this class. The factors and weights are rational; weighted squares suffice for nonnegativity. They can be converted to unweighted rational SOS using rational four-square representations, but that extra number-theoretic work is unnecessary for this interface.

The algorithm acquires a,Q and the unique face from the input; they are hidden only in fixture generation. Source zero enumeration and a learned SOS proposer are not used. Generic rational linear algebra requires O(n³) rational arithmetic operations plus coefficient inspection/expansion. Bit complexity is polynomial in n and input coefficient bit length under a standard fraction-free implementation; the provided Fraction implementation is a transparent replay, not an optimized bit-complexity benchmark. Explicit certificate size is O(n²) rational coefficients. In the dense degree-two basis an arbitrary symmetric Gram has Theta(n^4) scalar entries, but an equally informed competent algebra system recognizes the quadratic-in-xi² structure and exactly matches this O(n³) algorithm. There is no separation from that comparator.

## D. Correct recovery theorem on an acquired or supplied rational face

Let B be a full-column-rank rational matrix and w=B^Tv. Define coefficient map A_B:H -> coeff(w^T H w) on symmetric matrices with Frobenius norm. Restrict coefficient coordinates to a rational basis of its image. Its adjoint is rational and A_B A_B* is invertible on that image.

Take a rational PD H0 and target coefficient vector c in the map's image. Put r=c−A_B(H0) and

  Hhat=H0+A_B* (A_B A_B*)^-1 r.

Then Hhat is rational and coefficient-exact, with

  ||Hhat−H0||_op <= ||Hhat−H0||_F <= ||A_B^dagger||_(2->F) ||r||_2.

If lambda_min(H0)>||A_B^dagger|| ||r||, Weyl's inequality gives Hhat PD. Thus BHhatB^T is an exact rational PSD Gram even though its ambient minimum eigenvalue is zero. Exact LDL can be used instead of estimating a spectral margin in implementation. This is a direct reconstructive version of the established Peyrl–Parrilo projection mechanism after facial reduction.

Its load-bearing missing input on arbitrary polynomials is a correct rational face B on which strict feasibility actually holds. A numerically guessed nullspace is not that input. The explicit class in C acquires B; the general statement supplies it and charges discovery separately. The correct spectral margin is linear in a norm bound. NSPI C.3 mixes squared and unsquared norms in its eigenvalue argument; the scalar example 1 -> 1+t, 0<t<1, already violates the asserted universal bound |delta lambda|<=||delta G||_F². The stated C.3 itself assumes a rational optimizer of a PSD constrained exact-identity problem, so its claimed exact-PSD conclusion is already in the premises. We do not advertise this as a counterexample to that tautological statement.

## E. Rational-certificate impossibility and a pre-existing algebraic repair

Scheiderer 2016 Theorem 2.1 constructs rational real-SOS polynomials with no rational polynomial SOS. Its construction and proof in arXiv1209.2976v3 Sections 2.2–2.8 were read. Let K be totally imaginary of degree d>=4 with 2-transitive Galois action; norm a generic linear form l. Pair conjugate factors to write the rational norm as a real sum of two squares. Any rational SOS factors must vanish on real intersections of conjugate hyperplanes, hence on all pair intersections by Galois transitivity. A polynomial of degree d/2 cannot do that: restricting to any hyperplane gives divisibility by the other d−1 linear factors, and an induction gives minimum vanishing-ideal generator degree d−1. Since d/2<=d−2, the factor is zero, a contradiction. This is a genuine certificate-field obstruction, not a numerical failure.

Example2.8 uses alpha^4−alpha+1=0 and l=x0+alpha x1+alpha²x2. The rational norm is

  f=x0^4+x0 x1^3+x1^4−3x0²x1x2−4x0 x1²x2+2x0²x2²
    +x0 x2³+x1 x2³+x2^4.

With beta the unique root of beta³−4beta−1 in (−2,−3/2), set

  A=2x0²+beta x1²−x1x2+(2+1/beta)x2²,
  B=2x0x1−x1²/beta+2x0x2/beta+beta x1x2−x2².

Then 4f=A²−beta B² and −beta>0. The identity was replayed exactly in Q[beta]/(beta³−4beta−1), using 1/beta=beta²−4. Endpoint values are −1 and13/8; derivative is >=11/4 on the isolating interval, establishing a unique real embedding and weight positivity. `algebraic_boundary.py` outputs `algebraic_replay.json`.

This algebraic weighted certificate is copied in structure from the source and independently expanded; it is NOT acquired from f in our experiment. It supplies a discriminating fixture: any rational-only polynomial-SOS discovery engine must fail, whereas an engine admitting isolated algebraic weights can succeed. Taking traces in the cubic field does not repair this certificate: another real embedding has beta>0 and the displayed weight becomes negative. Totally real descent of unweighted sums of squares therefore does not apply to these weighted expressions. An unweighted two-square certificate uses sqrt(-beta) and a degree-six field that is not totally real.

## Prior assessment, downstream use and next falsifiable step

Nearest prior art: Peyrl–Parrilo 2008 interior rational projection (known here through the actual theorem treatment in Laplagne); Laplagne2018 Sections3.1–3.2 exact zeros, algebraic conjugates and traces to acquire faces; Scheiderer2016 certificate-field impossibility. Our isolated-zero class combines elementary coefficient completion of squares with sign-character interpolation. It has not been identified as historically novel; these mechanisms are established and direct structured algebra matches its discovery costs.

Concrete downstream use: exact face-sensitive adversarial inputs for future SOS discovery systems; replace the false finite-zero/full-rank heuristic; distinguish rank failure, wrong rational face, and impossible rational certificate; expose a minimum contract for numerical-to-formal bridges. These results do not justify allocating a major foundational-discovery campaign to merely reproducing this pipeline.

Load-bearing missing lemma for a broader useful discovery advance: from a rational polynomial and a floating PSD near-certificate, acquire a correct rational face or certify the necessity of an algebraic coefficient field with a useful bit-cost bound, including singular/irrational cases. Existence of a real SOS, finite zeros, small residual, or successful verification does not provide it. General CAD is a complete expensive comparator for algebraic real feasibility; rational reconstruction and facial-reduction solvers are competent practical comparators. No universal polynomial-cost lemma is proposed.

Next falsifiable research step: hide the square-variable structure by an unknown rational invertible change of variables and test whether a polynomial-time face-acquisition method can recover the n-dimensional factor subspace from quartic coefficients without solving all zeros. Compare against simultaneous quadratic/tensor decomposition and Gröbner/facial reduction with identical coefficients and costs. This extension is not solved. A method that only succeeds when the coordinate transform or factors are supplied does not count.
