The input is the expanded rational quartic f, promised to possess some unknown rational invertible L and rational QPD with f(x)=m(Lx)^T Q m(Lx). No frame, metric, contacts, conditioning bound, residual spectrum or rational-space height is supplied. Exact representation of the optimizer/its individual residual Gram is not promised rational.

Let z be the ordinary quadratic monomials, N=n(n+1)/2, let v(M) be the coefficient vector of x^TMx, and let A(G)=z^TGz. The coefficient-only problem maximizes logdetM subject to

    M>0, A(G)=f, [[G,v(M)],[v(M)^T,1]]>=0.

The block size is N+1; this alone establishes neither iteration complexity nor an affordable general exact learner. Ordinary SOS determinant maximization is this method, not a weaker comparator.

## Exact optimizer and residual-space reconstruction

A strictly positive Gram for f exists. In hidden coordinates give every mixed feature a small positive diagonal Gram entry epsilon, and subtract epsilon/2 from the corresponding offdiagonal mass-block entry of Q. The polynomial is unchanged and the mass block remains PD for sufficiently small epsilon. The invertible quadratic feature action of L transports this Gram to observed coordinates. Taking a sufficiently small positive M gives strict Schur feasibility. This is a proof of Slater, not an acquired quantitative margin.

Feasible M are bounded by M_ii^2<=f(e_i) and |M_ij|^2<=M_ii M_jj. Feasible positive Gram matrices are bounded as well: the unit-sphere moment E[z z^T] is SPD, and tr(G E[z z^T])=E[f] is fixed. Closed bounded feasible Gram/metric pairs give an attained positive determinant maximum. Strict logdet concavity gives a unique M_*. Hidden sign flips preserve all constraints and the determinant, so uniqueness forces M_*=L^Tdiag(d)L, d>0.

For its exact characterization, minimize on p>0

    p^TQp/2-(1/n)sum_i log p_i.

QPD gives strict convexity, coercivity at infinity and divergence at finite positive-orthant boundary. The unique critical point satisfies Qp=d with d_i=1/(n p_i). Thus p^TQp=d^Tp=d^TQ^-1d=1. The matrix Q-dd^T is PSD of rank n-1, by Q-inner-product Cauchy. Consequently diag(d) is SOS feasible. Any other positive aligned feasible d' satisfies d'^Tp<=1 by evaluating its pointwise inequality at squared coordinates p; AM-GM then gives product d'<=product d. This proves it is the unique optimizer without assuming an unknown contact support is interior.

The equality rays are exactly the sign choices of sqrt(p), times a common scale. After whitening M_* and rotating to the hidden orthonormal axes, all f=1 contacts become y_i=sign_i/sqrt(n), because d_i p_i=1/n. No contact is given to the optimization or checker.

Every quadratic in any PSD residual SOS must vanish at each such contact. Walsh independence of the mixed sign characters forces its mixed coefficients to vanish; its remaining diagonal coefficients sum to zero in these whitened coordinates. Hence every residual Gram has range in the same D_0, the trace-zero diagonal quadratic space of dimension n-1. Restricted to all diagonal quadratics, Gram-to-quartic coefficient mapping is injective. Thus the optimal residual Gram S_* is unique, has rank n-1 and range D_0. Adding the metric quadratic gives exactly D_L=span{ell_i^2} in original coordinates.

Although D_L is rational under the rational-hidden promise, D_0 and S_* can be irrational. Rounding the individual optimizer or residual range to rationals is not a valid substitute for reconstructing the complete rational D_L.

For an explicit exact obstruction, f=x^4+2y^4 with L=I and Q=diag(1,2) has d=(1/sqrt(2),1). Its residual is (x^2/sqrt(2)-y^2)^2, so D_0=span{x^2-sqrt(2)y^2} and the unique residual Gram has irrational offdiagonal -1/sqrt(2). The complete D_L=span{x^2,y^2} remains rational. This example refutes a rational-optimizer/individual-range shortcut, not hidden-axis acquisition.

## Posterior metric gap reconstructed from rational data

Take rational M_c>0,G_c with A(G_c)=f and S_c=G_c-v_c v_c^T>=0. Let lambda be a rational quartic functional, K=A^*lambda, and w satisfy 2w^T v(M)=tr(M_c^-1 M). A rational dual block [[K,-w],[-w^T,alpha]]>=0 gives, for every feasible M,

    tr(M_c^-1 M)<=v(M)^TKv(M)+alpha<=lambda(f)+alpha=:c.

Therefore c>=n and logdetM_*-logdetM_c<=n log(c/n)<=delta:=c-n. All these bounds are consequences of checkable polynomial equality and PSD/PD tests. No fitted floating-point gap is used.

Let A_0=M_*^-1/2 M_c M_*^-1/2. Optimality along the feasible segment to M_c gives trA_0<=n. The eigenvalues obey

    sum_i[lambda_i-1-loglambda_i]<=delta.

If 0<a<1/2 is rational with 2delta<=a^2, the integral inequality t-1-logt>=(t-1)^2/(2max(t,1)) gives

    (1-a)I<=A_0<=(1-a)^-1 I.                         (1)

This is the acquired confidence certificate. Finding it at useful precision is still a separate computation.

## Strong residual leakage: delta, not sqrt(delta)

Use Frobenius-orthonormal quadratic features in the true M_* whitening: diagonals y_i^2 and mixed sqrt(2)y_i y_j. A Gram transforms by the corresponding quadratic feature congruence; denote the transformed candidate residual by S_c^*. Let P be the orthogonal projector onto D_0 and P_perp=I-P. For n>=2 the exact moment of the full contact cube is

    Gamma=diag_block(J/n^2, (2/n^2)I_mixed).

Its kernel is D_0 and every positive eigenvalue is at least 2/n^2. On every contact q_c=y^TA_0y is positive and <=1, since S_c is PSD and f=1. Moreover E[q_c]=trA_0/n. The elementary inequality logdetA_0<=trA_0-n and the logdet gap imply n-trA_0<=delta. Consequently

    tr(S_c^* Gamma)=E[1-q_c^2]
        <=2E[1-q_c]<=2delta/n,
    tr(P_perp S_c^*)<=n delta=:ell.                    (2)

The unknown contacts are used only to prove this observable bound; the right side is computed from the coefficient-only rational certificate. n1 is handled directly and is not included in the 2/n^2 constant.

Let T_* be an upper bound for trS_c^*. Positivity of the block Gram gives ||P S_c^* P_perp||_F^2<=tr(P S_c^*)tr(P_perp S_c^*). Hence with E=S_c^*-P S_c^*P,

    ||E||_F<=h_0:=sqrt(2T_*ell+ell^2).                 (3)

This controls leakage and cross blocks, rather than assuming a near optimizer has the optimal rank.

## Entire Gram distance and an observable range certificate

Equation(1) and the scalar entropy bound give

    ||A_0-I||_F<=b_0:=sqrt(2delta/(1-a)).

Represent quartics by their fully symmetric fourth-order coefficient tensor with the Euclidean Frobenius norm. Quadratic feature Gram-to-tensor mapping is full symmetrization of an isometry, hence contractive. Also

    ||tensor(q_c^2-q_*^2)||_F
        <=||A_0-I||_F(||A_0||_F+sqrt(n))
        <=r_0:=b_0(2sqrt(n)+b_0).                     (4)

Let B=P S_c^*P-S_*^*. Both terms lie on the hidden diagonal quadratic space. For its n-by-n mass Gram R, tensor norm is exactly

    sum_i R_ii^2+(2/3)sum_(i<j)R_ij^2
        >=||R||_F^2/3.                               (5)

The 2/3 comes from the six permutations of i,i,j,j, each tensor entry R_ij/3. Using the residual polynomial identities, tensor(B) is the sum of the metric-square difference and -tensor(E). Thus ||B||_F<=sqrt(3)(r_0+h_0). B and E occupy orthogonal Gram blocks, so

    ||S_c^*-S_*^*||_F
        <=e_*:=sqrt(3(r_0+h_0)^2+h_0^2).              (6)

All unknown-coordinate quantities in this bound admit coefficient-only upper bounds. Let B_i be the symmetric matrices of the original plain quadratic monomials: E_ii for a diagonal and (E_ij+E_ji)/2 for a mixed feature. Define the rational matrix

    W_ij=tr(M_c^-1 B_i M_c^-1 B_j),
    T_c=tr(S_c W).

W is SPD, since it is the Gram matrix of an invertible quadratic coefficient change into M_c whitening. The relative metric interval(1) gives T_*<=(1-a)^-2 T_c. The same congruence bounds the operator norm in candidate whitening by

    ||S_c^c-S_*^c||_op<=e_c:=(1-a)^-2 e_*.            (7)

Rational outward square-root bounds suffice; no actual M_* or matrix square root is evaluated.

Suppose an exact rational inertia check of S_c-gamma W^-1 gives exactly n-1 positive eigenvalues and the remainder negative, with rational gamma>e_c. By congruence this says S_c^c has exactly n-1 eigenvalues above gamma. Let U_c be their exact spectral space and P_*^c project onto rangeS_*^c. Since S_*^c has rank n-1, projecting the eigen-equation onto its nullspace gives

    ||(I-P_*^c)U_c||_op<=e_c/gamma.                   (8)

This is a computable posterior bound on the sine of the largest principal angle. The proof uses the inverse of the candidate eigenvalue diagonal, not a supplied hidden gap. It does not itself construct a rational U_c or reconstruct D_L exactly. Candidate approximate eigenvectors require their own residual/error accounting before their output is treated as the exact spectral space in(8).

## Charged exact frame selection after the full rational space is acquired

There is a direct way to acquire the remaining SPD member, conditional on already acquiring an exact rational basis for D_L. Orthogonally project the acquired M_c onto that space in the rational inner product tr(M_c^-1 B M_c^-1 C). In quadratic coefficient coordinates this is the W projection used above, computed by an n-by-n rational normal equation. The target M_* belongs to the space. Orthogonal projection contracts its error, and the reciprocal version of(1) gives

    ||M_c-M_*||_(Mc,F)<=sqrt(n) zeta,
    lambda_min(Mc^-1/2 M_* Mc^-1/2)>=1-a,
    zeta=a/(1-a).

Therefore the projected H is SPD whenever the entirely rational posterior test n zeta^2<(1-a)^2 passes. The computation supplies no hidden metric and needs no additional SDP. If the space is only a proposal, H's PD and the subsequent identity must still be checked independently; this sufficient guarantee applies when it is exact D_L.

For a rational quadratic basis B_0,...,B_(n-1), scan A(t)=sum_j t^j B_j. The generalized eigenvalues in the rational hidden frame are a_i(t)/h_i, all rational. For each pair their difference is a nonzero polynomial of degree<=n-1: it cannot be identically zero since the basis spans every diagonal coordinate. There are therefore at most(n-1)binom(n,2) bad integer t. Scanning that many plus one candidates, rationally factoring the characteristic polynomial, computing one-dimensional nullspaces, then verifying the full transformed quartic/even-QPD identity gives deterministic polynomial-bit frame selection in the acquired basis/coefficient/certificate bit sizes. Each candidate's polynomial factorization/substitution and SPD checks is charged. This completes the post-space module; exact D_L reconstruction and a bounded-cost optimizer remain its unresolved prerequisite.
