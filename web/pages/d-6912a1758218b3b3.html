1. CONVENTIONS AND THEOREM

Sigma>0 is a density on C^d, s=sqrt(sigma), Gamma(X)=s X s. L is the
POSITIVE Heisenberg generator, so exp(-tL) is UCP, L(I)=0, L_*(sigma)=0,
and L is KMS self-adjoint. H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2)>=0 is its HS
self-adjoint implementation. Gamma^(1/2)(X)=s^(1/2)X s^(1/2).
Use the supplied finite representation

 H=sum_B H_B, H_B(X)={V_B,X}/2-B X B,
 B=B^dagger,
 s V_B+V_B s=2 B s B,
 K_B=s^(1/2)B s^(-1/2).                                (1)

Thus Lyap_s denotes X->sX+Xs. For any pure P=|psi><psi| and
rho_epsilon=(1-epsilon)P+epsilon I/d, define

 J(rho)=Tr[L_*(rho)(log rho-log sigma)],
 E(P)=Tr[P H(P)].

Then the extended limit J_partial(P)=lim_epsilon_down0 J(rho_epsilon)
exists and

 J_partial(P)>=pi E(P).                                (2)

No self-compatibility, EB, GNS/modular covariance, primitivity, fixed-state
commutation or reference amplification premise is added. This concerns
the pure-state boundary, not an interior-state entropy inequality.

2. EXACT PHYSICAL GENERATOR AND THE FINITE-BOUNDARY CLASSIFICATION

KMS symmetry gives L_*=Gamma^(1/2)H Gamma^(-1/2). For one summand put
G=s^(1/2)V s^(-1/2). Direct transport of (1) gives

 L_B,*(rho)=(G rho+rho G^dagger)/2-K rho K^dagger,
 G+G^dagger=2K^dagger K.                               (3)

The second equality follows by conjugating sV+Vs=2BsB with s^(-1/2).
It also displays the usual CP dissipator plus a Hermitian Hamiltonian
term, with positive-generator sign. Each summand has trace zero.

Let a_epsilon=1-epsilon+epsilon/d, c_epsilon=epsilon/d. Since
log rho_epsilon=log c_epsilon I+log(a_epsilon/c_epsilon)P,
trace preservation gives the EXACT identity

 J(rho_epsilon)=log(a_epsilon/c_epsilon)Tr[P L_*(rho_epsilon)]
                             -Tr[L_*(rho_epsilon)log sigma].

At the pure state, (3) gives
 ell=Tr[P L_*(P)]
    =sum_B(||K_B psi||^2-|<psi,K_B psi>|^2)>=0.          (4)
If ell>0, J(rho_epsilon) tends to +infinity. If ell=0, EACH variance
vanishes, so psi is a common eigenvector of all K_B. Conversely common
eigenvectors give ell=0. The log-ratio term is then O(epsilon log(1/epsilon))
and tends to zero, while the other term tends to a finite number. Thus

 J_partial(P)=-Tr[L_*(P)log sigma]                      (5)

exactly on the common-eigenvector boundary. This proves the supplied
classification, including the fact that finite boundary J does NOT force
L_*(P)=0. For d=1, the entire theorem is zero and may be handled separately.

3. CENTER EACH HERMITIAN JUMP WITHOUT CHANGING H

Every K_B is similar to Hermitian B, so its pure-state eigenvalue k_B
is real. Replace B by B-k_B I. Its Lyapunov coefficient changes to
V-2k_B B+k_B^2 I, and direct expansion shows H_B is unchanged.
After these independent identity shifts, K_B psi=0 for every B.
Set u=s^(-1/2)psi. Then B u=0 and u^dagger s u=1.

Diagonalize s=diag(s_i)>0, put t_i=log s_i, C=B s B>=0, and define

 W_ij=conj(u_i) C_ij u_j.

W=diag(u)^dagger C diag(u)>=0, and W 1=0 because C u=0. Also
V_ij=2C_ij/(s_i+s_j). Zero entries of u cause no difficulty; no inverse
of diag(u) is used. All quantities below are finite at fixed faithful sigma.

Using (3) with the centered jump, (5) gives
 J_partial,B=-2 Re[u^dagger s(log s)V u]
   =-2sum_ij W_ij(s_i log s_i+s_j log s_j)/(s_i+s_j).
The ratio in this sum is
 (t_i+t_j)/2+(t_i-t_j)tanh((t_i-t_j)/2)/2.
W1=0 and 1^dagger W=0 remove the first piece. Therefore

 J_partial,B=-sum_ij W_ij f(t_i-t_j),
 f(t)=t tanh(t/2).                                     (6)

Similarly, with m_B=<psi,Bpsi> real,

 Q_B=<psi,V_B psi>=sum_ij W_ij h(t_i-t_j),
 h(t)=sech(t/2),
 E_B(P)=Q_B-m_B^2.                                    (7)

These are exact identities, not likelihood-root replacements of sqrt(P).

4. DIRECT FOURIER IDENTITIES AND THE CENTERED COMPARISON

For every real t, the following ordinary integrals converge:

 h(t)=integral_R exp(ixt)/cosh(pi x) dx,
 f(t)=integral_R (1-cos(xt))w(x) dx,
 w(x)=pi cosh(pi x)/sinh(pi x)^2.                       (8)

Here w has an inverse-square singularity at zero. The factor 1-cos makes
its integral locally bounded; at infinity w decays exponentially.
For completeness, derive both identities without importing an unknown
Fourier statement. Integrate exp(itz)/cosh(pi z) around the rectangle with
imaginary height 1. Vertical edges vanish as their real parts tend to
infinity. The top integral is -exp(-t) times the bottom integral, and
the sole pole at z=i/2 has residue exp(-t/2)/(pi i). Hence the bottom
integral is 2exp(-t/2)/(1+exp(-t))=sech(t/2).

Next integrate exp(itz)/sinh(pi z) in the height-1/2 rectangle, indenting
the real-axis pole by a SMALL UPPER semicircle traversed clockwise. The
indentation tends to -i, and no pole lies in the remaining interior.
The top integral equals -i exp(-t/2)h(t). Thus
 PV integral_R exp(ixt)/sinh(pi x) dx
       =i[1-exp(-t/2)h(t)]=i tanh(t/2).
Taking imaginary parts gives the absolutely convergent sine integral.
Finally w(x)=-d[csch(pi x)]/dx. Integration by parts in the second integral
of (8), with symmetric zero cutoffs, gives
 t integral_R sin(xt)/sinh(pi x) dx=t tanh(t/2).
The cutoff boundary terms vanish as O(cutoff); this proves (8).
All contours work for negative t as well, since strip height is bounded.

For v_i(x)=exp(-ixt_i) let S_W(x)=v(x)^dagger W v(x)>=0. Centering gives
S_W(x)=(v(x)-1)^dagger W(v(x)-1)=O(x^2) near zero. It is bounded globally.
Substituting (8) into the finite sums (6)-(7) therefore gives

 J_partial,B=integral_R S_W(x)w(x) dx,
 Q_B=integral_R S_W(x)sech(pi x) dx.                    (9)

In the first identity the cosine initially gives the symmetrized S_W;
integration against the even weight w gives precisely the displayed
integral even when W is complex. All integrals in (9) converge. The
pointwise density comparison, for x!=0, is

 w(x)/sech(pi x)=pi coth(pi x)^2>=pi.                   (10)

Thus J_partial,B>=pi Q_B>=pi E_B(P). Summing the finitely many summands
proves (2) on the finite boundary, and the divergent case proves it
elsewhere. In fact the stronger bound
 J_partial(P)>=pi sum_B <psi,V_B psi>
holds after the boundary-centering identity shifts. No constant optimality
or new reference theorem is asserted by this blind baseline.

5. EXACT NONSTATIONARY FINITE-BOUNDARY CONTROL / FAILED SHORTCUT

In particular J_partial/E=6log(2)>pi. V is positive definite, so even
positivity of V together with (sV+Vs)u=0 does not imply Vu=0.
This control checks the actual physical boundary and centered formulas.
It is finite diagnostic evidence for the analytic proof, not its proof.
