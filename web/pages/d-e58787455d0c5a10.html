# Cycle 3: causal transport under an exact controlled $b/A$ interface

**Decision:** equality of drift and quadratic-variation fields at every fully observed state and every action does not identify the controlled jump law, nor does it guarantee transport of an endpoint/hitting effect. A five-state CTMC gives a target-specific sign reversal while satisfying this interface exactly in every domain and action. This is a deliberately small mathematical witness, not a fitted cell model or a claim about a biological perturbation. The standard controlled-HMM and reaction-network identifiability literature already establishes the relevant boundary; no novelty claim is made.

## Exact counterexample at the target interface

Use the fully observed state space $\mathcal X=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, actions $u\in\{0,1\}$, and initial state $X_0=2$. States 0, 1, 3, and 4 are absorbing. At state 2, the four possible increments are $(-2,-1,+1,+2)$, with rates

\[
q_{2,0}^{(\theta)}(u)=1+u\theta,\quad
q_{2,1}^{(\theta)}(u)=1-3u\theta,\quad
q_{2,3}^{(\theta)}(u)=1-u\theta,\quad
q_{2,4}^{(\theta)}(u)=1,
\]

and $q_{2,2}^{(\theta)}(u)=-(4-3u\theta)$. Take $|\theta|<1/3$, so every off-diagonal rate is positive. For a jump process, define the drift and covariance-rate interface by

\[
b_\theta(x,u)=\sum_y q_{x,y}^{(\theta)}(u)(y-x),\qquad
A_\theta(x,u)=\sum_y q_{x,y}^{(\theta)}(u)(y-x)^2.
\]

At $x=2$, the baseline increments have rate vector $(1,1,1,1)$. The action-dependent perturbation direction is $c=(1,-3,-1,0)$. Directly,

\[
\sum_j c_j\Delta_j=(-2)+3-1=0,\qquad
\sum_j c_j\Delta_j^2=4-3-1=0,
\]

so for every $u$ and every admissible $\theta$, $b_\theta(2,u)=0$ and $A_\theta(2,u)=10$. At all absorbing states both quantities are zero. Thus the **entire state- and action-indexed $b/A$ fields agree exactly** between any two $\theta$ values. The third jump moment does not:

\[
\sum_y q_{2,y}^{(\theta)}(u)(y-2)^3=-6u\theta.
\]

This is a genuine generator difference hidden from the two-moment interface, not a numerical or finite-resolution approximation.

Now compare source and target worlds. In both worlds, the source has $\theta_S=\kappa>0$. In the invariant world, the target has $\theta_T=\kappa$; in the target-reversal world, it has $\theta_T=-\kappa$, with $0<\kappa<1/3$. All source and target $b/A$ fields agree for all states and both actions in both worlds. At $u=0$, the complete target generator also agrees, so all passive target path laws and every passive cumulant agree.

For the bounded target endpoint $Y_\tau=\mathbf 1\{X_\tau=0\}$, all exits from state 2 are followed by absorption. Under treatment $u=1$,

\[
F_\tau(\theta):=\Pr_\theta(X_\tau=0\mid X_0=2,u=1)
=\frac{1+\theta}{4-3\theta}\left(1-e^{-(4-3\theta)\tau}\right).
\]

Under control, $F_\tau(0)=\tfrac14(1-e^{-4\tau})$. For $\tau>0$, set $G(r)=(1-e^{-r\tau})/r$. Then $G(r)>0$,

\[
G'(r)=\frac{r\tau e^{-r\tau}-(1-e^{-r\tau})}{r^2}<0
\]

because $e^{r\tau}>1+r\tau$. Hence

\[
F_\tau'(\theta)=G(4-3\theta)-3(1+\theta)G'(4-3\theta)>0.
\]

The source treatment effect is positive. The invariant target effect $F_\tau(\kappa)-F_\tau(0)$ is positive, while the target-reversal effect $F_\tau(-\kappa)-F_\tau(0)$ is negative. For example, $\kappa=0.1,\tau=1$ gives control probability 0.245421, invariant/source probability 0.289947 (effect $+0.044526$), and reversed-target probability 0.206462 (effect $-0.038959$). The exact identities and numerical values are checked in [verify_witness.py](verify_witness.py) and [witness_results.json](witness_results.json).

The example intentionally uses a fate/hitting endpoint rather than the linear mean $E[X_\tau]$. Here $b=0$ at every state, so that mean is constant; nevertheless the fate distribution changes. Whether an application cares about a fate probability, tail risk, lineage success, or a particular nonlinear reporter must be specified in the estimand.

## What the no-go says about transport and adaptivity

If source experiments and passive target observations are available but the target is never exposed to $u=1$, the two worlds induce exactly the same observed data law: source controlled laws coincide because their source $\theta$ is the same, and target passive laws coincide because $u=0$ removes $\theta_T$. This remains true under any adaptive source-side action policy: conditional source output kernels agree at every history, so induction gives identical transcript laws. No larger passive sample, higher passive cumulant, or adaptive source policy can decide whether the target follows the source response or reverses it.

The witness gives the observer the full state, which is more informative than a noisy cell assay. Replacing the full-state readout by the same unknown emission kernel in both worlds preserves the passive observational equivalence. Under target treatment, however, an unknown or rank-deficient emission can hide the difference too; the randomized outcome must be an anchored fate readout, or its emission channel must be calibrated/identifiable under the chosen input design. This is a measurement-identifiability cost separate from the causal randomization cost.

There is a second, sharper boundary. Even if the target is randomized to both actions and drift/covariance are measured perfectly, the $b/A$ interface still cannot distinguish these mechanisms, since those fields agree under treatment too. A target randomized assay of $Y_\tau$, transition marks, or another readout sensitive to higher jump structure **does** distinguish them. A fixed randomized target contrast suffices for this witness; adaptivity is unnecessary. In a broader model class, adaptive target policies may improve information per experiment by selecting among informative action histories, but they cannot distinguish mechanisms whose observable output laws agree under every permitted policy.

The reusable decision lemma is behavioral equivalence: for an experiment class \(\Pi\), write $\theta\sim_\Pi\theta'$ when the complete observed transcript laws agree for every $\pi\in\Pi$. A target functional is identifiable from that experiment class only if it is constant on every such equivalence class. If two equivalent models have target effects separated by $d$, any estimator has worst-case absolute-error risk at least $d/2$; any test of the two equally likely worlds has error at least $1/2$. This is the elementary identifiability criterion applied to the controlled-output interface, not a new statistical theorem.

Consequently, transporting a general endpoint response requires an invariant **full controlled output kernel** (or a sufficient structural model for it), not only equality of its first two infinitesimal moments. For a named bounded endpoint, a weaker sufficient contract is source-target invariance of the conditional causal endpoint mean given pre-treatment state, with common intervention versions and state overlap; this is the standard transport formula already documented in [cycle 1](../PROOF_AND_TRANSPORT.md). Equality of $b/A$ may still be a useful model restriction or approximation, but it does not prove that endpoint-specific condition. An in-vitro result remains limited to the assayed cell models and culture conditions.

## Controlled-HMM identifiability: what it does and does not buy

The strongest directly relevant controlled-HMM theorem located is David, Bellot, Le Corff, and Lehéricy, [*Identifiability of discrete input–output hidden Markov models with external signals*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-023-10364-7), *Statistics and Computing* 34 (2024), article 54. Their model lets the hidden transition and observation emission depend on an external signal. Theorem 1 identifies hidden-state cardinality, signal-conditioned transitions, initial probabilities, and emissions from the law of three consecutive signal/output observations, up to bijections of hidden labels that may depend on time and signal. Its assumptions include invertible transition matrices, linearly independent emission distributions, positive latent-state probabilities, and the specified conditional HMM structure. This is a useful exact input-output identifiability result with unknown rates/emissions, but it is not itself a causal theorem: an external observed signal is not automatically a randomized intervention. To read signal-conditioned laws causally, the signal/action must be assigned (or sequentially randomized), the required signal histories must have support, and the observation model must be valid. Signal-specific state permutations also do not establish biological state alignment across contexts.

Liu and Culpepper, [*Designing Learning Intervention Studies: Identifiability of Heterogeneous Hidden Markov Models*](https://doi.org/10.1017/psy.2025.10024), *Psychometrika* 90 (2025), 1258–1283, give complementary design results for time-varying emissions, including an absorbing-state case. Their strict HMM conditions use full-rank transition/emission structures and repeated/parallel emission measurements to anchor the latent decomposition; their absorbing-state theorem uses multi-item emissions and additional rank conditions. This is useful evidence that latent dynamics need deliberate measurement design, not just more observations. It studies educational intervention sequences, however, and does not establish causal transport between cell contexts or say that a fitted latent label has the same biological meaning across domains.

Faul, Hoessly, and Xia, [*Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382), *European Journal of Applied Mathematics* (2026), supply an especially close prior-art warning. Their theorem characterizes mass-action rate identifiability through linear independence of per-source jump-moment vectors $(\nu,\nu\nu^\top)$; they give two distinct reaction networks with exactly the same Langevin drift and diffusion fields and therefore the same SDE generator/law under the stated well-posedness setup. This is already stronger than a passive-moment anecdote. The present finite CTMC is a transparent controlled witness matching $b/A$ for every state and action while reversing a target fate effect. It is not claimed to be mass-action or a biological model, and it does not improve the general identifiability theory established by these sources.

Classical transportability supplies the cross-domain reading. Pearl and Bareinboim's [selection-diagram/do-calculus framework](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-STS486) makes target outcome-mechanism shifts explicit; a source intervention does not determine a target effect when the target response mechanism is unrestricted. The positive result is obtained only after an outcome-relevant mechanism restriction (for example, conditional causal-response invariance) or target intervention data are supplied. Neither the controlled-HMM identifiability theorem nor equal $b/A$ fields supplies that cross-domain restriction.

## Discriminating experiment and charged costs

For the witness, randomize the same declared action and matched control in source (S), target (T), and a context-level holdout $H$. Lock the endpoint, harvest time, target-state definition, exposure protocol, target-engagement criterion, and margins before opening (T/H) outcomes. Use matched donor/clone or independently randomized culture blocks, not cells as independent units. Measure the fate endpoint or action-specific jump-mark/event-time distribution; drift/covariance alone is not the discriminating readout. If state is hidden, include a direct state anchor or an externally calibrated, sufficiently informative emission panel; otherwise report the output-defined estimand and do not label latent states biologically. A fixed target RCT is enough for this pair; sequential/adaptive allocations are optional and need anytime-valid inference or a predeclared finite allocation cap.

For a bounded binary block outcome, let $Z_{cia}\in[0,1]$ be the observed arm-level outcome mean for context $c\in\{S,T,H\}$, block $i$, and randomized arm $a\in\{0,1\}$. The within-block contrast $D_{ci}=Z_{ci1}-Z_{ci0}\in[-1,1]$ estimates the assignment effect; assume the $n$ block contrasts are independent within each context. Hoeffding gives each effect mean a two-sided radius $r=\sqrt{(2/n)\log(8/\alpha)}$; this gives joint coverage at least $1-\alpha/2$ for the source and target effects. The held-out difference $D_{Hi}-D_{Si}\in[-2,2]$ has radius $s=\sqrt{(8/n)\log(4/\alpha)}$, giving coverage at least $1-\alpha/2$. Together the three intervals cover with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Declare a reversal only when the source interval is wholly above a predeclared $+\gamma$, the target interval wholly below $-\gamma$, and the $H-S$ effect-difference interval lies wholly inside a predeclared equivalence margin $[-\delta,\delta]$. Return INCONCLUSIVE if intervals overlap decision margins or matching/assay controls fail. These are finite-sample sufficient intervals, not an exact zero-error classification. More endpoints, state strata, guides, and action histories require the corresponding multiplicity adjustment; the fuller multi-guide/state protocol and its block, exposure, target-engagement, held-out-context, and cost accounting are in [cycle 2](../cycle2/FINITE_SAMPLE_DESIGN.md).

Charge at least $6n$ randomized arm/block units for the simple two-arm, three-context panel, plus mock delivery, assay-positive controls, matched negative controls, target-engagement assays, baseline-state aliquots, reporter calibration or orthogonal validation, and any higher-frequency imaging needed to observe jump marks. A common nominal dose and an engagement-matched regimen are different estimands; if doses vary by context, report the context-specific intervention version. For species or tissue transfers, orthology/reagent performance and context-specific target engagement are separate gates. The held-out $H$ context must not be used to choose the model, endpoint, exposure, or margins. This supports a finite context-specific effect comparison only.

## Ceiling and reopening data

**Cycle 3 novelty/status:** exact internally derived controlled-CTMC no-go under pointwise equality of the full observable $b/A$ interface; standard-prior-art rejection for broad identifiability/transport novelty. The new witness's algebra and sign calculation are finite-checked; the derivative proof is analytic. No cells, external dataset, source/target biological perturbation, or wet-lab response were analyzed. The remaining biological question is open until a named perturbation, endpoint, and cell-domain panel are provided with randomized target exposures, matched controls, and informative outcome/state measurements. The source-to-target conclusion remains UNKNOWN without those data or a defensible full-kernel/endpoint-specific invariance assumption.
