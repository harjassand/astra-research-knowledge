# Root reconstruction: rank-three stars separate raw degree from prime cost

7 October 2026. Analytic independent PASS for the explicit coordinate-star
claim in `notes/metric_geometry10/cycle16/COORDINATE_STAR_DEGREE_OBSTRUCTION.md`.
Root reconstructed the character/stabilizer lower bound and matching determinant,
then checked the full author proof. No claim of historical novelty, efficient
witness construction, algorithm lower bound, or external validation.

Fix k>=0. Let r=m=3(k+1), with columns a_i,b_i,z_i, 0<=i<=k. The rows of A
are a_i+z_i, b_i+z_i, a_i+b_i+a_(i+1) for i<k and the analogous three terminal
rows with a_k+b_k last. All column degrees are two or three. Subtracting the
first two equations gives a_i=b_i; eliminating z_i leaves
2a_i+a_(i+1)=1 and 2a_k=1. Thus |det A|=D=2^(k+1), and the unique solution
x of Ax=1 has a_i=1/3+(-1/2)^(k-i)/6, b_i=a_i, z_i=1-a_i. Its entries are
positive. The numerator of a_0 at denominator D is (D+(-1)^k)/3, an odd
integer, so its reduced denominator is exactly D.

Let V_j be the coordinate inclusion supported on column j of A. For any
field K, any W admits an injective projection onto its pivot coordinates P.
Consequently dim(W intersect S_j)<=|P intersect support(j)|. This proves
P_K={x>=0:Ax<=1}; testing coordinate lines gives the reverse implication.
In particular every prime is good for this packing polytope, even though A
is singular over characteristic two. A is a matrix of REAL weight inequalities,
not a matrix whose inverse over K is needed for this argument.

Now consider the action V_j -> g_0 V_j g_j^(-1) and the convention
f(g^(-1)V)=product det(g_v)^(sigma_v) f(V). A homogeneous positive-degree
semi-invariant has total degree T=-r sigma_0, and leaf multidegree
d_j sigma_j>=0. Scaling a single central coordinate and the corresponding
coordinate of every incident leaf fixes V. A nonzero evaluation therefore
forces sigma_0+sum_j A_(l,j) sigma_j=0 for every row l. Hence
sigma_leaf=lambda x, lambda=-sigma_0 a positive integer. Integrality of the
a_0 character forces D to divide lambda; therefore T>=rD.

This also bounds general homogeneous SL invariants: their finite-dimensional
homogeneous space decomposes under the residual determinant torus into integer
character spaces. Nonzero evaluation of a sum requires nonzero evaluation of
some component. No cancellation between characters evades the preceding bound.

For attainability put sigma_j=D x_j and choose M=sum sigma_j distinct complex
numbers. Partition a D by M Vandermonde matrix into blocks T_j with sigma_j
columns. The determinant of the concatenation [T_j tensor V_j]_j has size
rD by rD, since sum d_j sigma_j=rD. Under the inverse action its determinant
character is sigma_0=-D and sigma_j at the leaves. Permuting rows and columns
by central coordinate makes it block diagonal: row l receives exactly
sum A_(l,j) sigma_j=D distinct Vandermonde columns. All r blocks are invertible.
This attains degree rD and proves the exact minimum.

The determinant is exponentially large; its existence is not an inexpensive
algorithm. The scientific use is an explicit falsifier for a polynomial RAW
degree shortcut, while leaving the random-prime reduction intact. The author's
3,343 finite checks are separate implementation evidence, not the universal
proof supplied here. Derksen--Makam's alternating-chain obstruction and Maar's
2026 star-gap result are close prior art. Originality of the rank-three exact
degree variant remains unestablished.
