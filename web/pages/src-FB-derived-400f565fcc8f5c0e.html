## New finite-chain consequence: certify rewards, survivors and costs together

This is a restricted application of standard absorbing Markov reward and
supermartingale mathematics, not a claim to invent that mathematics. It composes
the source's optical loading gate with N777's endpoint-certificate viewpoint.

Let E be a finite set of *full active states*. A state must include the carrier's
type/location, relevant defect configuration, charge/environment and previously
repaired sites wherever they change future outcomes. A coarse projection is
legal only after a certified closure. At encounter k, an allowed action u can
depend on the entire past. Let

    P^u_ij = probability of continuing active in state j from i,
    r^u_i  = conditional expected newly acceptable retained repair count,
    d^u_i  = conditional expected unacceptable damage count,
    c^u_i  = conditional expected charged cost.

P is substochastic: missing row mass is chain termination. r, d and c must come
from the same joint outcome law, not independently assembled favorable models.
They can correlate with the next state. Counting only distinct sites prevents a
reversible flicker from producing arbitrarily many apparent retained repairs.
The nonnegative-reward proof below requires credited repairs to remain acceptable
through the declared retention horizon under every admitted continuation, for
example after a separately certified sealing operation. This is a conditional
promise, not an acquired hBN property. Merely adding sites to the state does not
make a reversible repair count monotone. If later reversal/damage can revoke
credit, carry the actual configuration and pay the final acceptable count only
at the assessment/termination event; that terminal reward can exceed one. The
per-encounter r<=1 bound below then cannot be used without a further argument.
Alternatively a signed-reward account requires additional convergence controls.
No branching carrier network is included in the one-carrier r<=1 specialization.

For a fixed time-homogeneous policy with spectral radius rho(P)<1 and bounded
nonnegative rewards/costs, the exact expected values from state i are

    G=(I-P)^-1 r,   D=(I-P)^-1 d,   C=(I-P)^-1 c.       (1)

Proof. The probability row distribution before encounter k is a P^k, so its
expected reward is a P^k r. Sum the nonnegative series. In finite dimension
rho(P)<1 implies convergence to the Neumann inverse. The identical reasoning
applies separately to d and c. No independence between reward and next state is
needed beyond the acquired conditional state law. QED.

The same structure supplies an adaptive certificate without solving every
policy. If a nonnegative vector v satisfies, for *every legal* i,u,

    r^u_i + sum_j P^u_ij v_j <= v_i,                   (2)

then every history-dependent protocol has total expected repair gain <=a v for
initial distribution a. Proof: set v=0 at terminated states. Conditional
expectation of reward_k+v(next) is at most v(current). Sum through n encounters,
discard the nonnegative unfinished potential, and take the monotone limit of
cumulative nonnegative rewards. No finite mean completion time is required for
this upper bound. A policy freezing an active state does not silently count it
as repaired. QED.

More generally, if `r^u+P^u v-v<=epsilon b^u` with epsilon>=0 and b>=0, the
same proof gives

    expected gain <= a v + epsilon expected(sum b).    (3)

This is the acquisition-sensitive analogue of N777's generator-residual bound.
It requires an acquired cumulative-b bound; a tiny residual alone is insufficient
over a long near-nonterminating chain. For a charged cost c, the certificate
`r^u-lambda c^u+P^u v<=v` yields

    expected gain-lambda expected cost <=a v            (4)

when those two cumulative quantities are integrable. Thus a physical network can
be rejected against a specified matched resource target using an objective
potential, without pretending that every microscopic rate was recovered.
The inequalities themselves and all acquisition/calibration costs remain gates.

Changing only the holding-time clock while preserving the full embedded joint
outcome kernel preserves (1), including both damage and repair gain. It can change
duration, not the completed reward/cost-count law; time-dependent physical costs
must still be charged. This is the precise reward extension of the existing
shared-committor obstruction, not evidence that excitation transport is useless.

### Necessary gain, termination and supply gates

If every legal active encounter terminates with conditional probability at least
delta>0 and its expected repair reward is at most y_max, adaptive protocols obey

    G <= y_max/delta.                                  (5)

Proof. Survival through k encounters has probability at most(1-delta)^k by
conditional induction. Sum its expected reward bound y_max over k>=0. Equality
is realized by a homogeneous synthetic chain with reward y_max per encounter
and constant survival1-delta. This is a sharp abstract bound. QED.

For this optical preparation route, `Phi=eta aG`. Consequently `Phi>1` is
impossible under a valid uniform floor `delta>=eta y_max`. Equivalently, escape
from this obstruction requires some reachable low-termination regime, an
alternative preparation route, or a changed encounter/reward contract. It does
not suffice that a carrier is long-lived in a different material. With y_max=1,
the relevant floor is approximately2.4e-7 per consistently defined encounter.

Finite defect supply gives an independent pathwise cap. If no additional defects
are supplied/recreated, each successful site counts only once, and at most M

    G <= M,      Phi <= eta M.                         (6)

Thus the selected input arithmetic requires at least4,166,668 accessible distinct

## Energy balance and causal identification

Many exothermic repairs per photon violate no energy conservation: initially
metastable defects contain energy. For a complete event in one fixed composition
and charge contract, the energy ledger is

    E(D)+E(X)+W_in = E(H)+E(X')+Q_out+W_out+Delta E_other.

All terms must refer to the same microscopic state. A7.2eV isolated formation
energy is not automatically heat deposited, the excited repair barrier, or energy
available in an outgoing carrier. For a closed repeatable materials/energy cycle,
restoring the initial defective material is charged. For repair as a one-time
useful service, the wall energy and pre-existing defect-energy release should be
reported separately. Superunit product yield proves neither net energy gain nor
whole-process advantage.

Endpoint counts and event times alone also cannot identify the carrier: relabel
the hidden active state as an electronic excitation, chemical radical or thermal
reactive packet while leaving its acquired P,r,d and waiting law identical; every
such observable then has exactly the same likelihood. This is an explicit
observational nonidentifiability, not a claim that those mechanisms are equally
plausible physically. Identification needs an independently validated outgoing
excitation fingerprint correlated with the same structural repair, plus an
intervention whose relevant effects on competing mechanisms are calibrated.
Direct illumination of the next defect, beam action, heating and oxygen chemistry
must be quantified. A quencher changing many rates is not an exclusive causal
instrument by assertion.
