## Why the original QPD input fails that power-sum theorem

For every n>=2 QPD f, the original quartic has an interior positive definite Gram on the full N=n(n+1)/2-dimensional quadratic feature space. In hidden coordinates, choose a small positive epsilon and the block-diagonal Gram

    G_epsilon=diag(Q-(epsilon/2)(J-I), epsilon I_mixed).

The square block contributes mixed coefficient2qij-epsilon, while its mixed-feature diagonal contributes epsilon; hence its quartic is exactly f. Sufficiently small epsilon makes both blocks positive definite. The invertible quadratic feature action of L transports this to observed coordinates.

For any quadratic u with coefficient vector a, subtracting a sufficiently small lambda aa^T from that Gram remains PSD. Therefore

    sosupp f = R[x]_2.

This full space is not cubically independent when n>=2: for its features u=x_i^2, v=x_j^2 and w=x_i x_j, the relation uv-w^2=0 has degree two in these features; multiplying it by u gives a nonzero formal cubic relation. Thus Blomenhofer Theorem3.2's support condition fails for **every** original QPD input with n>=2, not merely an exceptional fixture.

The original Gram is also nonunique. The nonzero symmetric kernel matrix K with K_(x_i^2,x_j^2)=K_(x_j^2,x_i^2)=1/2 and K_(x_i x_j,x_i x_j)=-1 satisfies z^T Kz=0. Both G_epsilon+delta K and G_epsilon-delta K are positive definite for small nonzero delta. This prevents an uncharged proposal to obtain the hidden-square span from an arbitrary Gram of f. The missing supplied sextic f3 is another independent mismatch; it must not be synthesized by choosing an arbitrary Gram factor and treated as measured input.

## How the maxdet residual supplies the special bridge

The internally gated QPD metric theorem gives positive p,d with Qp=d and d_i p_i=1/n, and residual matrix Q-dd^T>=0 of rank n-1. Let the transformed coordinates be y=Lx. Its residual

    r(y)=(y^2)^T(Q-dd^T)(y^2)

has SOS component span

    U={sum_i a_i y_i^2 : a^T p=0}.

A basis is u_i=y_i^2-(p_i/p_n)y_n^2,1<=i<n. Their common-zero cone is exactly the2^(n-1) real sign lines

    y_i=sign_i sqrt(p_i/p_n) y_n.

The ideal generated by u_i is radical. One direct proof views its quotient as a free R[y_n]-module with basis products of y_1,...,y_(n-1) having exponents0/1, and embeds it into the product of the sign-line coordinate rings. The matrix of this map is the invertible sign-character matrix times nonzero powers of the real sqrt(p_i/p_n), separately in each total degree. Consequently no nilpotent or embedded component occurs. All components are real, so the real points are Zariski dense.

Blomenhofer Lemma4.4 now directly gives sosupp r=U. The forms u_i are algebraically independent: set y_n=0 to see u_i=y_i^2, whose monomials are linearly independent in R[y_1,...,y_(n-1)]. Thus there are no quadratic relations. The proof of Proposition4.8 implies the residual has a unique Gram. This credits the published general SOS-support principle; the optimizer's production of exactly this residual on every QPD input is the separate bridge.

Adding the optimal metric q_* to U gives the entire hidden-square space

    U+span(q_*)=span{ell_1^2,...,ell_n^2}=D_L,

because d^T p=1. This space is rational under the hidden-rational promise, although its particular residual basis and q_* may be irrational. The same-input axes are then recoverable from an acquired rational SPD H in D_L and an acquired separating A in D_L by generalized simultaneous diagonalization. Those steps and their accuracy/height certificates are not supplied by the source's f2/f3 model.

Even a perfect metric alone is insufficient: for f=sum_i y_i^4, q_*=(1/sqrt(n))sum_i y_i^2 is isotropic and carries no Euclidean eigenbasis preference. The residual support is the trace-zero diagonal quadratic space; adding q_* reveals D_L and its n rank-one rays. This is an exact special case explaining the function of the residual, not an experiment or a new practical runtime claim.

## SOS-FOOBI: supplied positive fourth powers and conditioning differ

It is a competent established tensor/SOS control when its actual model and conditioning hold.

It cannot cover the full QPD class by rewriting f as positive fourth powers. The rational QPD example Q=[[1,-1/2],[-1/2,1]] gives f=x^4-x^2y^2+y^4. Its Hessian at(1,0) has diagonal entries12,-2, so it is not convex. Every sum of positive fourth powers of real linear forms is convex. More invariantly, its symmetric middle catalecticant has a negative mixed eigenvalue. Thus no positive-powers model of any length exists for this promised instance. The QPD theorem makes no smoothing or FOOBI condition-number promise.

## A recent worst-case Kruskal algorithm also has a sharp mismatch

Theorem2 supplies the reshaped version. The authors explicitly distinguish variable k from a uniform polynomial runtime and state rational implementation when the decomposition is rational. Those statements do not by themselves charge a universal output-height bound for our tensor.

For a dense QPD quartic with all qij!=0, define its symmetric4-tensor by f(x)=T(x,x,x,x). Its middle catalecticant on symmetric matrices, in Frobenius-orthonormal hidden features, has square-feature block

    A=(2 diag(Q)+Q)/3 >0

and mixed eigenvalues2qij/3. Therefore its rank is N=n(n+1)/2, invariant under the unknown invertible feature action. Every ordinary4-way CP decomposition has at least N terms, since each term contributes at most rank one to this flattening. Reshaping four modes into three has dimensions(n,n,n^2). The two singleton mode Kruskal ranks are at most n and the paired rank is at most r. The Kruskal condition would force

    2n+r>=2r+2, hence r<=2n-2.

But N>2n-2 for every n>=2. Thus no such reshaped Kruskal guarantee applies to any dense QPD tensor with n>=2. This is a precise assumption obstruction to that theorem, not tensor-decomposition hardness, failure of its algorithm outside its promise, or an absence-of-prior proof. Unknown zeros can change flattening rank; the dense counterclass suffices to prevent an all-QPD substitution.
