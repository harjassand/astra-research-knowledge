## Interface and decisive premise

The question is whether the **fixed** physical local six-qubit frame `{X_i,Z_i}_{i=1}^6` identifies every faithful state via unitary-orbit SLD scores. Ordinary irreducibility is automatic. Let `d=64`, `q=12`, `m=q(d−1)=756`.

Take the real maximally entangled basis with columns `vec(P)/√8`, where `P` runs over the 64 real orthogonal three-qubit tensor products of `I,X,Z,J`, `J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]`. The trace inner products are `Tr(PᵀQ)=8δ_PQ`, so these columns form an orthogonal matrix `U_Bell`. Every column has both three-qubit marginal states `I_8/8`. For rational real orthogonal `V,W∈O(8)`, put `U_0=(V⊗W)U_Bell`. Its columns remain maximally entangled across the 3+3 partition. Each physical local `A_a=X_i` or `Z_i` is real symmetric, traceless, and has exactly zero diagonal in this basis:
\[
B_a^0=U_0^T A_aU_0,\qquad (B_a^0)_{ii}=0.
\]
The matrices `B_a^0` are rational: the two factors `1/√8` cancel to `1/8`. This removes a potential irrational-rank ambiguity.

Choose `m` skew-coordinate matrices from the `d(d−1)/2=2016` standard skew basis `E_ij−E_ji`. Let `Ω(y)=Σ_l y_l Ω_l` and `U(y)=U_0 exp Ω(y)`. Define a real analytic multiplier near `s=0`,
\[
C_{ii}(s)=1,\qquad C_{ij}(s)=\frac{2s^{|i-j|}}{1+s^{2|i-j|}}\quad(i\ne j),
\]
where indices range from 0 to 63. For each `a`, form
\[
K_a(y,s)=C(s)\odot(e^{-\Omega(y)}B_a^0e^{\Omega(y)}),\qquad
F_a(y,s)=R\,K_a(y,s)\mathbf1,
\]
with `R` the first-63-coordinate restriction after orthogonal projection `I−11ᵀ/d`. Concatenate all `q` blocks to obtain `F∈R^m`. This is exactly the constant-row-sum condition, not a selection of unrelated entries. `F(0,0)=0`.

The **decisive exact premise** is that the selected square derivative `J=D_yF(0,0)` is nonsingular. At `s=0` the derivative is the first 63 entries of `diag[B_a^0,Ω_l]`; its full diagonal has trace zero, so subtracting the mean changes nothing. A skew coordinate `(i,j)` contributes opposite values `±2(B_a^0)_ij` at rows `i,j`. Thus `J` is a rational incidence matrix and a nonzero determinant modulo a prime not dividing its denominators proves exact nonsingularity. A floating-point singular value does not prove this premise.

## No hidden positivity, phase or trace obstruction

\[

\]

The analytic extension to negative `s` is only an IFT device and need not represent a physical multiplier. ## Explicit finite positive s via contraction

Let `Q_den≥1` be any integer clearing all denominators of the certified square matrix `J`. Since `Q_den J` is integer and has nonzero determinant,
\[
|\det J|\ge Q_{\rm den}^{-m}.
\]
Every entry of `J` has absolute value at most 2. Hadamard's cofactor bound and `||M||_2≤m max_ij|M_ij|` therefore give the explicit integer
\[
K=m(2m)^{m-1}Q_{\rm den}^{m}\ge\|J^{-1}\|_2.
\tag{K}
\]
This bound is deliberately loose and needs no numerical inverse.

For real `|s|≤1/2` and arbitrary real `y`, take `A=4dq` and `L=16dq`. Then
\[
\|F(0,s)\|_2\le A|s|,\qquad
\|D_yF(y,s)-J\|_2\le L(\|y\|_2+|s|).
\tag{E}
\]
Here are sufficient explicit estimates. `R` has norm at most 1; the row-sum map has Frobenius-to-vector norm at most `√d`; `||B_a^0||_F=√d`; and offdiagonal `|C_ij(s)|≤2|s|` give `||F(0,s)||≤2d√q|s|≤A|s|`. Combining this with the multiplier change yields `12√(qd)||y||+4√(2qd)|s|`, bounded by the stated `L`.
