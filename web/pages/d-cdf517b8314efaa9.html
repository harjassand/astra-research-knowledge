filter, with ||vi||<=sqrt(2M) and sum ||vi||^2=Tr C<=6M. All previously used

No division by sqrt(lambda_i) or eigenvalue gap is needed.

Here is a constructive way to approximate sqrt(C) without a spectral-gap oracle.
For desired matrix error q, choose alpha=(q/4)^2 and D=C+alpha I. Scalar spectral
calculus gives ||sqrt(D)-sqrt(C)||<=sqrt(alpha)=q/4. Choose rational gamma in
[sqrt(2M+alpha),2sqrt(2M+alpha)]; the smallest dyadic power of two whose square
is >=2M+alpha has this property using exact rational comparisons. Run

Exact iterates commute with D; each scalar eigenvalue ratio z=x/sqrt(lambda)
is >=1 and obeys z_next=(z+1/z)/2. Until z<=2 it decreases by at least a factor
5/8 per step; afterwards its excess e=z-1 obeys e_next<=e^2/2. Thus
O(log((2M+alpha)/alpha)+loglog(1/q+2)) iterations suffice for operator error q/4.
For rounded matrices use the symmetric version
 (X+[D X^(-1)+X^(-1)D]/2)/2, equal on the exact commuting path. All exact
iterates have least eigenvalue >=sqrt(alpha). Retain rounding error below
sqrt(alpha)/4 so the approximate inverse has norm <=2/sqrt(alpha). A conservative
per-step Lipschitz constant is Lnewt=1+4(2M+alpha)/alpha. If there are k steps,
absolute rounding <=min{q,sqrt(alpha)}/[16k Lnewt^k] keeps the total perturbation
within min{q,sqrt(alpha)}/8 by telescoping. Its required guard precision is
O(k log Lnewt+log(1/q)), polynomial in Lin+log(1/q) for fixed M. Constant-size
rational inversion and interval rounding have polynomial bit cost at that
precision. The root approximation is only O(q), sufficient because its columns
enter individual filter matrices continuously. Compute the columns sufficiently
accurately that the per-factor operator error below is <=h. For |Z|<=L, this
requires O(ell+Q+log(L+1)+log(m+1)) root-precision bits, independent of any gap.

Replacing all weights

For a simple finite-bit implementation, retain R scalar logweights and R local
qubit descriptions; no trajectory matrix history is needed. Subtract the largest
computed logweight, evaluate the R scaled weights to absolute error
<=epsilon/(256R), and round to nonnegative dyadics with total per-weight error
