VERDICT
There is no extra-dimensional Brownian or eigenaxis oracle in the candidate:
the stochastic state is three real Bloch coordinates, three Brownian inputs,
one scalar logweight, and supplied known parameters. The direct exact-stopping
law is not itself an executed finite-bit sampler. Its apparent exp(O(sqrt N))
worst-case normalization/moment cost can, however, be removed analytically.
Below I prove a uniform lower bound on every path weight and a public uniform
p-th moment bound. I then replace first-hit acquisition by a globally Lipschitz
cutoff law which retains exponential proximity, CONDITIONAL only on the peer's
quantum comparison theorem, and derive an O_(M,B)(N eta^-4) real-arithmetic
Euler/importance construction. The finite-bit resource route is polynomial at
fixed public M,B, but the integrated seed/Euler/resampling compiler has NOT been
executed. Existing own normal/root/local-state code implements constituent
pieces, not this complete stochastic sampler. Constants are conservative and
can be enormous. No practical, hardware, unknown-parameter or priority claim.

I. BASIS-FREE ACQUIRED COEFFICIENTS
Use Lambda=M+1, C=A+Lambda I, I<=C<=Lc I, Lc=2M+1, Tc=Tr C<=3Lc,
s=N^(3/4). For an axis n, u=n.m, v=n-u m and r=n cross m,
 (v.grad)v=-u n+(2u^2-1)m,
 (r.grad)r=u n-m,
so their difference is -2u(n-u m)=-2u v. Substituting in the author's S4 gives
 a_N(m)=(N-1)/(2s^2)[C m-(m^T C m)m]
          +[b-(b.m)m]/(2s),                                      (A1)
 V_N(m)=[(N^2-N)m^T C m+N Tc]/(4s^2)+N b.m/(2s),               (A2)
 D_C(m)=(I-mm^T)C(I-mm^T)-[m]_cross C[m]_cross^T.                (A3)
These are matrix-polynomial expressions in supplied C,b, with scalar algebraic
N powers. No eigenvectors or eigenvalue gaps must be acquired. All parameter
norm promises can be checked exactly in dimension three using principal minors
of M I+/-A and the rational inequality |b|^2<=B^2. Algorithms require rational
data, or a polynomial-time precision interface whose cost is explicitly added;
arbitrary unknown/noncomputable real parameters are not algorithmic input.

On |m|<=r0, r0^2=1/[16(Lc+1)], D_C>=3I/4 and Tr D_C<=Tc. The latter follows
because P=I-mm^T has 0<=P<=I, Tr(P C P)<=Tr C, and the subtracted outer product
is PSD. The sharper drift bound is
 |a_N(m)|<=[Lc/(2sqrt N)] |m|+B/(2s).                            (A4)
It follows directly from a's P C m form and ||P||<=1. The peer's looser Tc/3
terms are unnecessary. Drift <=r0/8 is ensured, for example, by allocating
r0/16 to each term: N>=64Lc^2 and N>=(8B/r0)^(4/3). Conservative rational
upper enclosures supply legal integer thresholds without transcendental ceilings.

Nine exact Fraction checks in check_diffusion_coefficients.py compare the

II. THE NORMALIZATION COST IS UNIFORMLY BOUNDED
Put y=N^(1/4)|m|. C>=I and N>=2 give, for every physical m in the inner ball,
 V_N(m)>=(1-1/N)y^2/4-B y/2>=y^2/8-B y/2>=-B^2/2.             (A5)
Thus every stopped weight exp(int_0^(1 wedge tau) V) is >=exp(-B^2/2), and
its mean mu has the same lower bound. This does not require the peer's
whole-state comparison, nor a normalization integral or trace oracle.
The result also holds for a cutoff process with potential V evaluated at a
Bloch-ball projection, and for its Euler/Riemann-sum weights.
There is no exp(-O(sqrt N)) acceptance denominator in this argument.

III. A PUBLIC UNIFORM p-TH WEIGHT MOMENT
This proof applies to the original stopped process, the cutoff process below,
and exact Gaussian Euler increments of the cutoff process on ANY time mesh.
For any fixed p>=1, it supplies Kp(M,B)<infinity for all sufficiently large N.

Let M_t denote the vector martingale part, stopped when applicable. Each
coordinate quadratic variation is <=Tc/(2s^2). With
 Z=N^(1/4) sup_(t<=1)|M_t|,
the exponential-martingale maximal bound on signs/three coordinates gives,
uniformly conditional on every supplied initial branch,
 P(Z>x | m0)<=6 exp[-N x^2/(3Tc)].                              (A6)
For Euler increments the same proof uses the conditional Gaussian exponential
martingale and discrete maximum. Therefore, whenever N>=6Tc K,
 E[exp(K Z^2)|m0]<=1+6K/[N/(3Tc)-K]<=7.                         (A7)
The conditional form is essential; Z need not be independent of m0.

From (A4), pathwise continuous/discrete Gronwall gives
 Y:=N^(1/4) sup|m_t|
   <=exp[Lc/(2sqrt N)] [Y0+B/(2sqrt N)+Z]
   <=exp(Lc/2)[Y0+B/2+Z],  Y0=N^(1/4)|m0|.                    (A8)
The stopped case remains valid after its path freezes. The cutoff case uses
|a_cut(m)|<=Lc |m|/(2sqrt N)+B/(2s) globally. The discrete case uses the
cumulative Gaussian martingale and (1+alpha Delta)^steps<=exp(alpha).

The potential upper bound is
 V_N(m)<=Lc Y^2/4+Tc/4+B Y/2
        <=[(Lc+2)/4]Y^2+Tc/4+B^2/8.                            (A9)
Set Dp=p(Lc+2)/4 and K=3Dp exp(Lc). From (A8)-(A9),
 E w^p<=exp[pTc/4+pB^2/8+K B^2/4]
           E[exp(K Y0^2) exp(K Z^2)]
       <=7 exp[pTc/4+pB^2/8+K B^2/4] E exp(K Y0^2).             (A10)
This step uses (A7) conditionally, not an unjustified independence assertion.

ACQUIRED HEAT-SEED MOMENT. For the exact baseline heat seed at t0 and original
field radius r, let R=N^(1/4)r. Bloch magnitude tanh(r/2)<=r/2 gives
Y0<=R/2. Replacing or contracting an outer initial branch only decreases Y0.
With d0=1-Lambda/(2sqrt N) and y=R/(2N^(1/4)), its unnormalized radial law is
 Q_N(R)=R^2 sinch(y) exp[-Lambda R^2/(16d0)-N f(y)],
 f(y)=y^2/2-logcosh(y).
For N>=Lambda^2 the normalizer is >=Lbase=(7/3)exp[-Lambda/2-1/12].
For y<=1, Q_N<=exp(1/2)R^2exp(-R^4/768). For y>=1 and the displayed large-N
thresholds, the tilted outer law Q_N exp(d R^2)<=R^2exp(-R^2) when
sqrt N>=8(d+1)/delta, delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0.
The completed square
-R^4/768+d R^2<=-R^4/1536+384d^2
then gives the entirely public bound
 E_seed exp(d R^2)
   <=J(d):=[768 exp(1/2+384d^2)+1/2]/Lbase.                    (A11)
Here int R^2 exp(-R^4/1536)dR=1536^(3/4)Gamma(3/4)/4<768,
because Gamma(3/4)<=4/3+exp(-1)<2, and int R^2exp(-R^2)dR=sqrt(pi)/4<1/2.
All required constants can be conservatively evaluated by rational intervals.
Consequently, substitute d=K/4 in (A10)-(A11):
 Kp=7 exp[p(3Lc)/4+pB^2/8+K B^2/4] J(K/4)                     (A12)
is a uniform computable upper bound on E w^p for N above the explicit moment/
heat thresholds and with Tc replaced by 3Lc where needed.
This constant can be astronomically large. It is fixed by fixed M,B, not free,
not polynomial in growing M,B, and not a practical performance certificate.

For p=2, (A5),(A12) imply the usual averaged importance-resampling bound
 sqrt(K2)/(mu sqrt(Rprop))<=exp(B^2/2)sqrt(K2/Rprop).
Thus Rprop=ceil(K2 exp(B^2)/eta^2) proposals suffice for eta error. The proposed
sampler must acquire this conservative bound; it may not replace it by a finite
empirical variance or a source-reported Monte Carlo success.

IV. REMOVE THE EXACT FIRST-HIT ORACLE
Define P(m) as Euclidean projection onto radius r0 and
 chi(m)=clip(3-4|m|/r0,0,1).
It is one on radius <=r0/2, zero on radius >=3r0/4, and globally 4/r0-Lipschitz.
Define globally bounded/Lipschitz coefficients
 a_cut(m)=chi(m) a_N(P(m)),
 Sigma_cut(m)=chi(m) sqrt(D_C(P(m))/(2s^2)),
 V_cut(m)=V_N(P(m)).                                          (A13)
An initial branch in radius r0/4 stays in radius 3r0/4: coefficients vanish at
that boundary and pathwise uniqueness makes it absorbing. No stopping time is
required to sample this law. The output is always a physical identical-product
kernel. Euler approximations may overshoot; project ONLY their final Bloch state
and clamp their potential with P, which are nonexpansive/continuous operations.

Initial acquisition can also avoid an exact radial-threshold comparison. From
the heat seed Bloch vector m use
 T_seed(m)=clip(2-8|m|/r0,0,1) m.                              (A14)
This equals m for |m|<=r0/8, vanishes for |m|>=r0/4, is globally Lipschitz with
constant <=3, and has output magnitude <=r0/8 (maximize 2r-8r^2/r0 on the taper).
It changes the seed density by trace norm <=2P_seed(|m|>r0/8)=O(exp(-c0 N))
using the peer's heat-tail argument with the smaller FIXED threshold. Any change
in c0 must be retained: it is not the same numerical exponential constant.

Couple this cutoff SDE to the peer's radius-r0 stopped SDE from the same contracted
seed and Brownian motion. They agree until hitting radius r0/2. Starting within
r0/4, a drift displacement <=r0/8 means that hit requires martingale sup at
least r0/8. Hence, conservatively,
 P(hit r0/2 by time 1)<=6 exp[-r0^2 N^(3/2)/(768 Lc)].           (A15)
Each raw weighted output has trace <=exp(hN); therefore their unnormalized
operator expectations differ by at most 2exp(hN) times (A15). The continued
cutoff potential does NOT freeze at this hit, but its exp(hN) bound is still
valid. Conditional on the peer's exact quantum-Duhamel comparison, replacing
its stopped mixture by (A13)-(A14) retains a known-parameter whole-density
error C exp(-cN), with possibly smaller c. It does not improve the unknown-
parameter decoder or imply exact separability at finite N.

V. DIRECT EULER/IMPORTANCE RESOURCE CONSTRUCTION
For the cutoff SDE, all Lipschitz and magnitude constants are computable from
fixed M,B. In particular on the inner ball,
 ||D_C(m)-D_C(n)||op<=6Lc r0 |m-n|,
 ||sqrt(D_C(m))-sqrt(D_C(n))||F<=6Lc r0 |m-n|,
using the Sylvester inverse/integral and its >=sqrt(3/4) spectral floor.
The coefficient Sigma has Frobenius size <=sqrt(Tc/2)/s. The cutoff adds at
most (4/r0)sqrt(Tc)/(sqrt(2)s) to its Lipschitz constant. Formula (A1) gives
an a Lipschitz bound from Lc(1+3r0^2)/(2sqrt N)+B r0/s, plus the cutoff's
4/r0 times its magnitude. These are bounded uniformly in N by constants
depending only on fixed M,B. No unknown coefficient or spectral regularity
is being assumed. The projection P is 1-Lipschitz.

Let X_t be the continuous Euler interpolation with step Delta=1/T, and m_t
the true cutoff path. Let global magnitude bounds be A,S and Lipschitz bounds
La,Lsigma. A one-step conditional Gaussian increment gives
 E|X_t-X_floor(t)|^2<=2(A^2+S^2)Delta=:Cinc Delta.
Subtract the true/Euler integral equations. Cauchy-Schwarz for the drift and
Doob L2 plus Ito isometry for the stochastic integral give
 d(t):=E sup_(u<=t)|m_u-X_u|^2
 <=Lstar int_0^t[d(u)+Cinc Delta]du,
 Lstar=4La^2+16Lsigma^2.
Gronwall therefore yields
 d(1)<=Lstar Cinc exp(Lstar)Delta=:CE Delta.                    (A16)
Integrated/endpoint lag bounds are at most a public constant times Delta.
These elementary bounds are deliberately loose. They use standard Ito/Doob
facts, not a numerical convergence diagnostic. Euler--Maruyama is established
prior art; see the primary author article D.J.Higham, SIAM Review 2001,
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0036144500378302 .
Its publisher full HTML introduction/method sections were inspected; this
particular coefficient/moment accounting is derived here.

Let ell_w=int V_cut(m_t)dt and ell_hat=Delta sum V_cut(X_j).
Because ||grad V||<=[(Lc r0+B)/2]sqrt N on the clamped ball,
 E|ell_w-ell_hat|^2<=C_(M,B) N Delta.                           (A17)
The exact Euler weights also satisfy the uniform moment (A12), by the discrete
version of (A6)-(A8). For scalars,
 |exp(x)-exp(y)|<=(exp(x)+exp(y))|x-y|,
so weighted Cauchy-Schwarz with K2 bounds the L1 weight error by C sqrt(N Delta),
not by an exp(hN) worst-case factor.

For Bloch vectors in radius r0, single-qubit states have eigenvalues >=
lambda_*=(1-r0)/2>0. The Sylvester square-root bound gives
 ||sqrt(tau_m)-sqrt(tau_n)||F<=|m-n|/[2sqrt(2lambda_*)].
Use these square roots as purifications. Their real overlap has
1-overlap^2<=their squared vector distance; tensor fidelity then gives
 T(tau_m^tensor N,tau_n^tensor N)
 <=sqrt(N)|m-n|/[2sqrt(2lambda_*)].                            (A18)
This is why the branch factor is sqrt(N), not the looser N telescoping factor.
Projection of the Euler endpoint cannot increase |m-X|. Combining (A16)-(A18)
and uniform second moments gives
 ||W_cut-W_Euler||1<=C_(M,B) sqrt(N Delta).
Normalizing costs at most the constant exp(B^2/2) from (A5). Thus
 T(sigma_cut,sigma_Euler)<=C_(M,B) sqrt(N/T).                   (A19)
Choose T=ceil(C^2 N/eta^2), then Rprop=O_(M,B)(eta^-2) from (A12).
The averaged resampled output has error <=C exp(-cN)+2eta under the peer's
quantum comparison. Real scalar/matrix work is
 O_(M,B)(N eta^-4)+N local emissions.                          (A20)
Public constant/preprocessing costs must be added. This construction uses a
three-dimensional Euler path and one scalar accumulated logweight per proposal;
it does not require an acquired exact stopped path, a high-dimensional quantum
state, continuous Brownian-path oracle, or eigenaxis oracle.

VI. FINITE-BIT GUARDS AND UNEXECUTED INTEGRATION
Rprop and T are selected from conservative rational upper bounds. At most
D=3Rprop T normal prefixes plus capped seed draws are required. Finite inverse-
CDF normals have a COUPLING guarantee, not TV to a continuous normal. A cap
and rational L upper bound on sqrt(log(D/accuracy)) give the charged tail budget.
Clipped symmetric Gaussian increments also have conditional subgaussian mgf
bounded by Gaussian increments, so the moment proof applies to them. Exact
finite prefix approximations can be coupled uniformly to those clipped draws.

On the retained finite draw set, a perturbation of Euler coefficient/draw
values accumulates through factors bounded by
 prod_j[1+La Delta+Lsigma |Z_j|sqrt(Delta)]
 <=exp[La+C Lsigma L sqrt(T)].
Hence an explicit logarithmic precision budget includes O(La+Lsigma L sqrt(T))
bits plus log(N T Rprop/accuracy); its size is POLYNOMIAL in N,eta^-1, full
supplied input length at fixed M,B. Even a conservative worst-case exp(hN)
weight guard adds hN=O(sqrt N) BITS, not exp(sqrt N) bits. Relative selection
can instead use individually enclosed logweights, avoiding this max-weight
amplification. Required function/matrix evaluations and complete updates must
be enclosed at their final amplified tolerances, not at an unguarded primitive
epsilon. Root D_C has an acquired uniform margin; my own rational root engine
and normal-CDF engine provide relevant certified building blocks.

Once all Rprop local states and logweights are enclosed, subtract a maximum
logweight, approximate scaled weights to a shared dyadic denominator while
preserving one maximal weight exactly one, and use exact bounded-integer bit
selection. The baseline proves these categorical/array guards; my N=2 compiler
executes the normalized dyadic/local-output case. N independent native local
emissions have a declared per-qubit allocation, generic angle/compiler cost,
and device noise/energy interface. No physical filter/postselection is used.
