Closed-span loop transport: exact conditional results and countermodels
15 October 2026. Scope: finite-dimensional overdamped model only.

1. Kinematics and geometric transport

Let x_C<x_N, ell=x_N-x_C, c=(x_N+x_C)/2. Assume a fixed planar
shape family, translation-invariant total mechanical energy/loading,
equal-and-opposite net endpoint forces (-F,+F), and a diagonal law with
strictly positive mobilities m_C,m_N on each traversed branch. Then

  dot{x_C}=-m_C F, dot{x_N}=m_N F,
  dot{ell}=S F, dot{c}=(m_N-m_C)F/2,
  S=m_C+m_N, A=dc/dell=(m_N-m_C)/(2S).

For an expansion ell0 -> ell1 followed by contraction back to ell0,
without uncounted motion during switching, the exact result is

  d = int_ell0^ell1 [A_out(ell)-A_in(ell)] d ell.

For constant D outward and L inward mobilities, a=ell1-ell0>0,

  d = a(A_D-A_L)
    = a(m_N^D m_C^L-m_C^D m_N^L)/(S_D S_L).

Changing only the common multiplicative mobility clock gives d=0.
If either endpoint is permanently immobile, closure of ell forces
closure of the other coordinate and gives d=0 independently of the
diagonal-law derivation. Pinning an arm only during one stroke is a
different case and does not give this null theorem.

With nonnegative diagonal coefficients, |A|<=1/2 and |d|<=a. For
m_N=m>0 and m_C^D=p>q=m_C^L>0, the displacement fraction is

  |d|/a = m(p-q)/((m+p)(m+q))
         <= (sqrt(p/q)-1)/(sqrt(p/q)+1),

with equality exactly at m=sqrt(pq). This proves displacement per span
excursion, not useful velocity, energy efficiency, or device benefit.

If M is a symmetric positive definite endpoint mobility matrix rather
than diagonal, set e=(-1,1), h=(1,1)/2. Then

  A=(h^T M e)/(e^T M e), S=e^T M e>0.

The path integral and common-scaling null remain valid; |A|<=1/2 need
not hold. M=[[1,2],[2,5]] is positive definite and gives A=1. The
diagonal bound must not be promoted to a universal crystal-line bound.

2. Four response branches and phase reversal

Let A_D^+, A_D^-, A_L^+, A_L^- be acquired outward/inward responses at
the actual forces, shape, position, temperature, charge history and
optical states. For each ell define the four-cycle drifts by integration:

  d_DL=int(A_D^+-A_L^-) d ell,
  d_LD=int(A_L^+-A_D^-) d ell,
  d_DD=int(A_D^+-A_D^-) d ell,
  d_LL=int(A_L^+-A_L^-) d ell.

Under branch reproducibility and negligible or explicitly subtracted
switching displacement,

  d_DL+d_LD=d_DD+d_LL.

Therefore d_LD=-d_DL requires that the sum of the two fixed-state
drifts vanish; the simple two-state theorem has the stronger assumption
A_D^+=A_D^-, A_L^+=A_L^-. The optical phase-antisymmetric part is

  (d_DL-d_LD)/2 = (1/2)int(A_D^++A_D^- -A_L^+-A_L^-) d ell.

If state preparation changes hidden pinning/core variables between
cycles, these four branches need not exist as single-valued functions.
Failure of the identity then rejects the proposed reduced acquisition
contract, not all possible defect transport.

3. Stress-only countermodel and admissible comparator

At one fixed optical state, take dimensionless activated odd glide laws

  v_C(F)=sinh(F), v_N(F)=sinh(2F)/2,

with C moving to the left and N to the right for F>0. Their effective
mobilities are sinh(F)/F and sinh(2F)/(2F). Expansion at F=0.2 and
contraction at F=-2 give A_out=0.0049668546, A_in=0.2900128292 and
d/a=-0.2850459746. This is an exact constitutive counterexample to the
claim that all fixed-light mechanical cycles must be null. It assigns
no force scale, activation volume, or mobility to ZnS.

A fair stress-only comparator optimizes the allowed stress tensor,
amplitude, waveform and frequency within the same measured temperature,
damage, strain-closure and time constraints. A fixed-light comparator
also optimizes a constant intensity/wavelength, including darkness.
An optical advantage needs a measured expansion of the attainable
displacement/cost/time frontier, not merely superiority over one
symmetric sinusoidal waveform. Spatial stress gradients and deliberate
pinning control belong in the allowable baseline when feasible.

4. Dissipation and optimal time allocation

For positive diagonal mobilities the endpoint dissipation metric is

  P = (dot{c}-dot{ell}/2)^2/m_C
      +(dot{c}+dot{ell}/2)^2/m_N
    = S/(m_C m_N) (dot{c}-A dot{ell})^2 + dot{ell}^2/S >=0.

Under only equal-and-opposite forces, dot{c}=A dot{ell} and
P=S F^2=dot{ell}^2/S. With constant S_D,S_L, excursion a, and total
moving time T=t_D+t_L, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  E_glide >= a^2/(S_D t_D)+a^2/(S_L t_L)
           >= a^2(1/sqrt(S_D)+1/sqrt(S_L))^2/T.

The first inequality is equality for constant span speed in each stroke;
the second is equality when t_D:t_L=1/sqrt(S_D):1/sqrt(S_L). With
span-dependent S, replace a/sqrt(S_s) by
Lambda_s=int_ell0^ell1 d ell/sqrt(S_s(ell)); then
E_glide >=(Lambda_D+Lambda_L)^2/T for that prescribed path. Force
limits can make equality unattainable and impose further minimum times.
For required d=a Delta A, the constant-state bound is

  E_glide >= d^2(1/sqrt(S_D)+1/sqrt(S_L))^2/(T Delta A^2).

This is dissipated glide energy. F is the NET generalized shape force.
If optical charging changes an internal potential U(ell,z), measured
external mechanical work is not automatically int F d ell. The combined
mechanical, optical/electrical state-setting work must satisfy the
complete cyclic energy balance. Heater/cooler, wall-plug, actuator,
metrology, preparation, failed attempts and sink qualification costs
are absent from this bound and must be added for technology accounting.

5. Force-limited throughput corrects the sharp displacement optimum

Assume the same unchanged neutral m, charged p>q, a fixed allowed net
force magnitude Fmax on both strokes, and fastest strokes at that cap.
Ignoring settling time only for this ideal model,

  t_motion=(a/Fmax)[1/(p+m)+1/(q+m)],
  |d|/t_motion=Fmax m(p-q)/(p+q+2m).

Holding p fixed, taking q->0 and choosing displacement-optimal
m=sqrt(pq) makes |d|/a ->1 but |d|/t_motion ->0. Strong contrast and
large displacement per cycle do not imply throughput.

For a fixed measured dead time t0>0 per full cycle,

  v_useful = a m(p-q)/[t0(m+p)(m+q)+(a/Fmax)(p+q+2m)].

Its maximum as a function of m occurs at
m^2=pq+a(p+q)/(Fmax t0), not at pq. This derivative calculation is
conditional algebra; it is not a prescription to optimize unknown ZnS
parameters before acquisition.

6. Direct signed-force alternative

Let g_C,g_N denote additional, experimentally acquired generalized
endpoint forces (for example from a direct electric-field response),
not an optical mobility change. Then

  dot{x_C}=m_C(-F+g_C), dot{x_N}=m_N(F+g_N).

Choosing F=(m_C g_C-m_N g_N)/S keeps span constant and gives

  V=dot{c}=m_C m_N(g_C+g_N)/S,
  P=V^2(1/m_C+1/m_N)=V(g_C+g_N).

A nonzero net translational drive can thus bypass the relative-mobility
switching requirement, if both full arms move and the force balance is
feasible. Opposite additional forces with g_C+g_N=0 give V=0. A
permanently pinned full arm still gives V=0. Electric-field motion of
one charged partial does not supply these full-arm coefficients or
guarantee unchanged stacking-fault width. These must be acquired.

7. Falsification bound

If independent trajectory acquisition yields
|A_out(ell)-A_in(ell)|<=epsilon on an excursion of width a, then
|d|<=a epsilon, before measured switching/closure/registration errors.
Choose a useful required drift d_req from a declared destination,
maximum cycle count and cycle time, rather than inventing a favorable
noise threshold. Reject the candidate in the tested admissible window
if the upper confidence bound on useful drift is below d_req, either
full arm cannot move reversibly, shape closure requires damage, or the
best matched baseline reproduces the output at no greater full cost.
