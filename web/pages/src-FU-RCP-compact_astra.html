# Compact scalar noise is possible exactly for commuting bounded observables

Date: 2026-10-15. Independent worker derivation, frozen separately from the earlier polynomial packet. Status: complete internal proof; external review and historical priority remain open. No finite-dimensional compression argument is used.

## 1. Exact statement and quantifiers

Let \(\mathcal H\ne\{0\}\) be an arbitrary complex Hilbert space, let \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\in B(\mathcal H)\) be bounded self-adjoint operators, and write \(A(t)=\sum_jt_jA_j\). An exact scalar-noise representation is a scalar Borel probability measure \(\nu\) and a normalized positive operator-valued Borel measure \(M\) on \(\mathbb R^m\) such that

\[
 \int e^{it\cdot x}M(dx)
 =\widehat\nu(t)e^{iA(t)}\qquad(t\in\mathbb R^m).
 \tag{1}
\]

Weak countable additivity of the POVM suffices. No density, finite-dimensional Hilbert space, discrete spectrum, or distributional support theorem is assumed.

**Theorem.** The following conditions on the tuple are equivalent.

1. The operators \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) commute pairwise.
2. There exists an exact representation (1) with compactly supported scalar noise \(\nu\).
3. There exists an exact representation (1) with compactly supported output POVM \(M\).

For every fixed exact pair \((M,\nu)\), compact support of \(M\) and compact support of \(\nu\) are equivalent. The converse implication from commutativity concerns **existence** of a compact representation: commuting tuples also admit noncompact noise and noncompact output.

A stronger two-direction statement will prove necessity.

**Directional theorem.** Let \(A,B\) be bounded self-adjoint, and suppose a scalar law of \((U,V)\) and a POVM of \((X,Y)\) satisfy

\[
 \int e^{i(tX+rY)}M(dX,dY)
 =\mathbb E e^{i(tU+rV)}e^{i(tA+rB)}.
 \tag{2}
\]

Suppose \(V\in[a,b]\) almost surely and

\[
 \mathbb E e^{sU}<\infty\qquad\hbox{for every }s\ge0.
 \tag{3}
\]

Then \([A,B]=0\).

Condition (3) means all **positive exponential moments**, not all ordinary moments. Finite exponential moments at an unbounded sequence of positive parameters already imply (3). No negative exponential moments of \(U\) are needed. The proof below does not establish the same conclusion from ordinary moments alone.

## 2. Marginal identities and support

For each real direction \(u\), the pushforward \(M_u\) of \(M\) under \(x\mapsto u\cdot x\) satisfies

\[
 M_u=\nu_u*E_{A(u)},
 \tag{4}
\]

where \(E_{A(u)}\) is the spectral PVM of \(A(u)\). To see this, scalarize against a unit vector: both sides have the same scalar characteristic function by (1), so uniqueness of scalar Fourier transforms gives equality. Polarization then gives equality of operator measures.

If \(\nu\) is compact, each \(\nu_j\) is supported in an interval \([a_j,b_j]\), and (4) puts \(M_j\) in

\[
 [\inf\sigma(A_j)+a_j,\ \sup\sigma(A_j)+b_j].
\]

Positivity shows that the complement of the product of these intervals is \(M\)-null: it is the finite union of coordinate-null sets. Thus \(M\) is compactly supported.

Conversely, suppose \(M\) is supported in a coordinate rectangle \(\prod_j[\ell_j,r_j]\). Fix any unit vector. Its \(j\)-th marginal is the convolution of \(\nu_j\) with a scalar spectral law supported in \([\inf\sigma A_j,\sup\sigma A_j]\). If
\(U_j>r_j-\inf\sigma A_j\), then the sum exceeds \(r_j\) regardless of the spectral value. Likewise \(U_j<\ell_j-\sup\sigma A_j\) forces the sum below \(\ell_j\). Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{supp}\nu_j
 \subseteq[\ell_j-\sup\sigma A_j,\ r_j-\inf\sigma A_j].
 \tag{5}
\]

All coordinate laws are bounded, so \(\nu\) is supported in their product rectangle. This proves the fixed-pair compact-support equivalence without deconvolving an entire function.

## 3. Positive exponential tilting gives a bounded first moment

Work under the directional theorem. Equation (4) for the \(Y\) marginal gives

\[
 \inf\sigma(B)+a\le Y\le\sup\sigma(B)+b
 \quad M\hbox{-almost everywhere}.
 \tag{6}
\]

For \(s\ge0\), put \(m_s=\mathbb E e^{sU}\). The \(X\) marginal and (3) give the bounded positive operator

\[
 Q_s=\int e^{sX}M(dX,dY)=m_s e^{sA}.
 \tag{7}
\]

The integral is legitimate even if the output is not compact: its scalar quadratic forms are the finite exponential moments specified by the convolution marginal. They are bounded by \(m_se^{s\|A\|}\|v\|^2\). Positive truncations consequently define a bounded operator. Also \(Q_s\) is boundedly invertible because \(m_s>0\) and \(A\) is bounded.

Define the normalized tilted POVM

\[
 N_s(dX,dY)=Q_s^{-1/2}e^{sX}M(dX,dY)Q_s^{-1/2}.
 \tag{8}
\]

It is positive, normalized, and has the same bound (6) on its \(Y\) coordinate. In particular its first \(Y\) moment is a bounded self-adjoint operator.

Let

\[
 m(s,r)=\mathbb E e^{sU+rV},\qquad
 \kappa_s=\left.\partial_r\log m(s,r)\right|_{r=0}
 =\frac{\mathbb E[V e^{sU}]}{m_s}\in[a,b].
 \tag{9}
\]

Since \(V\) is bounded, \(m(s,r)\) is finite for every real \(r\). The marginal identity in direction \((s,r)\) gives

\[
 \int e^{sX+rY}M(dX,dY)=m(s,r)e^{sA+rB}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Differentiation in \(r\) at zero is valid in operator norm. Indeed \(Y\) is bounded, and the uniform scalar Taylor remainder on its bounded range is multiplied by the positive integrable weight \(e^{sX}\); the remainder's operator norm is bounded by its scalar uniform bound times \(\|Q_s\|\). The identical argument applies to the scalar noise.

Using the norm-valid Duhamel derivative

\[
 \left.\partial_r e^{sA+rB}\right|_{r=0}
 =\int_0^1 e^{(1-u)sA}B e^{usA}\,du,
\]

and conjugating (10) by \(Q_s^{-1/2}=m_s^{-1/2}e^{-sA/2}\), gives

\[
 \int Y\,N_s(dX,dY)=\kappa_s I+J_s(A,B),
 \tag{11}
\]

where

\[
 J_s(A,B)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{svA}B e^{-svA}\,dv.
 \tag{12}
\]

This integral is a bounded self-adjoint operator for each \(s\). Self-adjointness follows by taking adjoints and changing \(v\) to \(-v\).

Equations (6), (9), and (11) yield the uniform order bound

\[
 [\inf\sigma(B)-(b-a)]I
 \preceq J_s(A,B)
 \preceq[\sup\sigma(B)+(b-a)]I.
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{s\ge0}\|J_s(A,B)\|\le\|B\|+(b-a)<\infty.}
 \tag{13}
\]

The first tilted moment is enough. No second-moment Schur complement, covariance cancellation, trace argument, or highest eigenvalue is needed.

## 4. A bounded symmetrized conjugation average forces commutation

**Lemma.** For bounded self-adjoint \(A,B\),

\[
 \sup_{s\ge0}\left\|\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{svA}B e^{-svA}\,dv\right\|<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad[A,B]=0.
 \tag{14}
\]

Only the forward implication needs proof. Write \(K=\sup_s\|J_s\|\). Fix a real \(c\) and a gap \(\delta>0\), and take the actual spectral projections of \(A\)

\[
 H=E_A([c+\delta,\infty)),\qquad
 L=E_A(( -\infty,c]).
\]

Their ranges reduce \(A\). On these ranges put \(A_H=A|_{H\mathcal H}\), \(A_L=A|_{L\mathcal H}\), \(X=HBL\), and \(Y_s=HJ_sL\). Both \(X,Y_s\) are operators from \(L\mathcal H\) to \(H\mathcal H\). Define the bounded Sylvester operator on that operator space by

\[
 D(Z)=A_HZ-ZA_L.
\]

It has \(\|D\|\le2\|A\|\), and

\[
 e^{-tD}Z=e^{-tA_H}Ze^{tA_L},\qquad
 \|e^{-tD}\|\le e^{-t\delta}\quad(t\ge0).
 \tag{15}
\]

The latter estimate follows directly from \(A_H\succeq(c+\delta)I\) and \(A_L\preceq cI\). No assertion about eigenvectors or a compressed POVM is involved.

Restrict (12) to this block, so that
\(Y_s=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{svD}X\,dv\). Differentiating the exponential and integrating gives the exact identity

\[
 sD Y_s=(e^{sD/2}-e^{-sD/2})X.
\]

Multiplying by \(e^{-sD/2}\),

\[
 (I-e^{-sD})X=sD e^{-sD/2}Y_s.
 \tag{16}
\]

Taking norms and using (15) gives

\[
 (1-e^{-s\delta})\|X\|
 \le 2s\|A\|e^{-s\delta/2}\|Y_s\|
 \le2s\|A\|K e^{-s\delta/2}.
 \tag{17}
\]

Letting \(s\to\infty\) proves \(HBL=0\). Equivalently, one can invert \(I-e^{-sD}\) by its norm-convergent Neumann series; the direct norm inequality (17) makes that extra step unnecessary.

Now fix \(c\) and let \(\delta\downarrow0\). The high projection converges strongly to \(E_A((c,\infty))\). Hence

\[
 E_A((c,\infty))B E_A((-\infty,c])=0.
\]

Taking adjoints gives the other off-diagonal block. Thus \(B\) commutes with every spectral cut \(E_A((-\infty,c])\), and consequently with \(A\). This proves the lemma, and (13) proves the directional theorem. ∎

The argument applies unchanged to continuous spectrum, nonseparable Hilbert spaces, and off-diagonal blocks of infinite rank. Spectral cuts reduce \(A\); no claim is made that a finite-dimensional compression retains the joint Fourier identity.

## 5. The full compact-noise equivalence

If \(\nu\) is compact in (1), then for every pair \(j,k\) its two-coordinate pushforward satisfies the directional theorem: the second noise coordinate is bounded and the first has every positive exponential moment. Therefore \([A_j,A_k]=0\) for every pair. Compact output implies compact noise by Section 2 and so gives the same conclusion.

Conversely, a finite tuple of commuting bounded self-adjoint operators has a joint spectral PVM \(E\) supported in \(\prod_j\sigma(A_j)\), with

\[
 \int e^{it\cdot x}E(dx)=e^{iA(t)}.
\]

Taking \(M=E\) and \(\nu=\delta_0\) gives a compact exact representation. More generally, every compact scalar \(\nu\) gives the compact positive convolution \(M=E*\nu\). This completes all implications of the theorem.

For the stronger directional interpretation, let an exact tuple representation have every positive exponential moment in direction \(u\). If its scalar noise is bounded in direction \(v\), then the two-dimensional pushforward gives

\[
 [A(u),A(v)]=0.
 \tag{18}
\]

If the scalar noise has every exponential moment in every direction, each bounded noise direction must correspond to an observable commuting with the whole tuple. This is a necessary condition; no unproved converse about every partial support geometry is asserted.

## 6. A quantitative spectral witness

Equation (17) also makes the obstruction explicit. If, for some separated spectral intervals, \(X=HBL\ne0\), then

\[
 \|J_s(A,B)\|
 \ge\frac{(1-e^{-s\delta})e^{s\delta/2}}{2s\|A\|}\|HBL\|.
 \tag{19}
\]

The denominator is nonzero whenever the spectral block exists. One may improve \(2\|A\|\) to \(\operatorname{diam}\sigma(A)\) by shifting \(A\) by the midpoint of its spectrum, which leaves both \(J_s\) and \(D\) unchanged.

Thus any noncommuting bounded pair has a nonzero separated spectral block and an exponentially growing sequence of first tilted moments. A hypothetical bounded scalar-noise width would have to satisfy

\[
 b-a\ge
 \frac{(1-e^{-s\delta})e^{s\delta/2}}{2s\|A\|}\|HBL\|-\|B\|
\]

for every \(s>0\), an impossibility. This is an exact-representation obstruction. It does not, by itself, give an error lower bound for a finite-window or approximate measurement contract.

For the elementary Pauli pair \(A=R\sigma_z\), \(B=\sigma_x\), one finds

\[
 J_s(A,B)=\frac{\sinh(sR)}{sR}\sigma_x.
 \tag{20}
\]

This immediately contradicts (13) when \(R>0\). The spectral-block proof is the continuous-spectrum replacement for this finite-dimensional calculation.

## 7. Dependency and novelty audit

The proof uses scalar Fourier uniqueness, the spectral theorem for bounded self-adjoint operators, positivity of POVM integrals, the Duhamel derivative of the bounded-operator exponential, and an elementary separated-spectrum semigroup estimate. It does not use the sufficiency direction of PR94, compact support of generalized Wigner distributions, Paley–Wiener theory, a matrix trace, or the Pauli covariance formula.

The supplied exact scalar multiplier contract is essential. Ordinary joint measurements of finitely many noisy coordinate observables need not satisfy (1) for every linear combination; no impossibility statement for that broader class is made.

Focused primary-source searches were made for compact-noise generalized Wigner positivity, joint measurements with compactly supported noise, and bounded conjugation averages. They did not locate a directly matching theorem during this worker pass. This is not a historical-priority clearance. The root's independent priority audit is a separate gate.

The stronger directional statement retains its exponential-moment hypothesis explicitly. The proof does not establish commutation from bounded noise in one coordinate plus only finite or all ordinary moments in the other.
