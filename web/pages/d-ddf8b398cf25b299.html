## 1. Exact interface and verdict scope

The cell input is a fixed flat torus of volume C, L0=Delta+1, smooth real P,Q with L0P=L0Q=0, P nonzero, and Omega={|P|<tau} with the explicitly retained Dirichlet gap lambda1(-Delta,Omega)>=Lambda>1. The Dirichlet solution r_Q lies in H0^1(Omega), its zero extension is R_Q, and A_Q=L0R_Q-Q is a distribution. Boundary smoothness is unnecessary for the response identity.

The Euclidean input is n>=3 and one fixed a>=1, smooth, nondecreasing and unbounded, with |a^(m)(R)|<=C_m a(R)/R^m for every fixed m and a(R)=R^(o(1)). The existing weighted construction supplies a global W, the zero extension of the unique Dirichlet repair on {|q|<tau}, with

    Delta(f+W)=0 in {|q|<tau}, f=E q,
    |W|<=C_K eta(R) E, eta(R)=(R a(R))^(-1/2),
    |W|<=tau E/20.

Only those exact inherited estimates are used. The final pair is literally

    F(x)=integral psi(v)[f+W](x-sigma/a(R(x)) v)dv,
    V=0 on {|q|<tau/2}, V=Delta F/F on the safe complement.

The local assertion holds for every compact H1 test supported in the compensated boxes, with one fixed K, cover, threshold, radial kernel and positive sigma. No substitute V or local replacement of the actual W is used. All constants below are independent of j; they may depend on the fixed a,n,h,tau,K,sigma and geometry. Fixed finite derivatives of a or the partition need finite constants, with no simultaneous all-order smallness requirement.

The explicit cover also has escaping disjoint negative-test boxes. Therefore its whole-space operator has infinite Morse index and infinitely many discrete negative eigenvalues. The positive local regions demonstrate spatial redistribution of the form; they do not indicate a finite-index construction.

## 2. Boundary measures reconstructed without a normal trace assumption

Let z+ and z- solve (-Delta-1)z+=Q+ and (-Delta-1)z-=Q- in Omega with zero Dirichlet trace. The gap gives unique solutions and positivity: testing their negative parts uses the coercive form integral(|grad z|^2-z^2). Thus r_Q=-z++z-.

For z>=0 with -Delta z=f>=0, its zero extension Z satisfies Delta Z+f 1_Omega>=0 distributionally even on an arbitrary open Omega. Indeed, for a nonnegative smooth periodic phi, test the equation with phi theta_epsilon(z), where theta_epsilon(0)=0, 0<=theta_epsilon<=1, theta_epsilon'>=0, and theta_epsilon(z)->1 on {z>0}. Then

    integral theta_epsilon(z) grad z dot grad phi
       +integral phi theta_epsilon'(z)|grad z|^2
       =integral f phi theta_epsilon(z).

Composition and multiplication preserve H0^1. Dropping the nonnegative second term and passing to the limit gives integral grad Z dot grad phi<=integral_Omega f phi, since grad z=0 almost everywhere on {z=0}. A positive distribution on the compact torus is a finite Radon measure. Applying this to f=Q+ +z+ and f=Q- +z- gives positive boundary measures mu+ and mu-. Their support is contained in boundary(Omega), because the equation is exact inside and the extensions vanish outside. The constant test gives

    mu+(T)=integral_Omega(Q+ +z+),
    mu-(T)=integral_Omega(Q- +z-).

Put mu_Q=-mu++mu-. Then

    L0R_Q=Q 1_Omega+mu_Q,
    A_Q=-Q 1_(T\Omega)+mu_Q.                         (G1)

The sum z++z- solves (-Delta-1)(z++z-)=|Q|. Energy and Cauchy-Schwarz give

    integral_Omega(z++z-)<=|Omega| ||Q||_infinity/(Lambda-1),
    ||A_Q||_TV/C<=||Q||_infinity[1+1/(Lambda-1)].       (G2)

Every boundary point has |P|=tau, so A_Q/P is a well-defined finite signed measure on its support. In a smooth collar, (G1) agrees with the familiar mu_Q=-partial_nu r_Q dS. That representation is not used for arbitrary Omega.

Self-adjoint periodic integration by parts gives

    0=<L0R_Q,P>=integral_Omega P Q+integral P dmu_Q.

On the boundary 1/P=P/tau^2. Consequently the exact response, including every zero-extension defect, is

    M_tau(P;Q)=mean(A_Q/P)
      =-mean[1_(T\Omega) Q/P+1_Omega P Q/tau^2]
      =-mean[rho_tau(P) Q],                           (G3)

where rho_tau(t)=t/tau^2 inside the threshold and 1/t outside it. In particular

    M_tau(P;P)=-mean[min(P^2/tau^2,1)]<0.              (G4)

The strict sign follows directly because P is nonzero, regardless of the separate sparsity bound. In the sparse construction it is quantitatively at most -(1-|Omega|/C). The boundary contribution is -mean(1_Omega P^2)/tau^2; omitting it would change the exact response.

If j_tau'=rho_tau, differentiation under the integral gives

    M_tau(P;partial_lambda P)=-partial_lambda mean j_tau(P).

Thus a changing mixture is a distinct channel. For Q=partial_i P the unsmoothed mean is zero by periodic integration of partial_i j_tau(P). Fixed smoothing preserves that conclusion in this two-wave application by parity; no general parity assumption was silently inserted into (G3).

## 3. Fixed tilted smoothing and the two-wave sign

In n dimensions use

    S_sigma h(zeta)=integral_R^n psi(v)e^(sigma v1)
                             h(zeta-sigma(v2,v3))dv.

For n>3 one may integrate out the unused v coordinates; the resulting kernel on R^3 remains smooth, nonnegative, radial and normalized. This makes the two-dimensional cell notation consistent with the actual n-dimensional smoothing. Radial harmonic averaging of e^(-s)cos(y) and e^(-s)cos(z) gives exactly S_sigma P=P for every fixed two-wave weight.

If L=||grad P||_infinity and sigma L<=tau/4, the convolved residual is supported in {|P|>=3tau/4}. Choose a smooth reciprocal cutoff g=1/P on {|P|>=tau/2} and g=0 on {|P|<=tau/4}. It has ||g||<=C/tau and ||grad g||<=C L/tau^2. The adjoint convolution, (G2), and symmetry in v1 give

    |mean[(S_sigma A_Q)/P]-M_tau(P;Q)|
      <=C ||Q||_infinity[1+1/(Lambda-1)]
                         (sigma L/tau^2+sigma^2/tau). (G5)

Here the translated cutoff costs the first term; the tilted kernel mass differs from one by O(sigma^2), accounting for the second. No continuity of arbitrary domains is required in this mean estimate. The quotient is smooth and set to zero in the inner collar.

For P_lambda=lambda cos y+(1-lambda)cos z, lambda in [7/10,4/5], and tau<=1/100, write Q_lambda=cos y-cos z. On {|P_lambda|<=tau},

    |partial_yP_lambda|^2
      =lambda^2-[P_lambda-(1-lambda)cos z]^2
      >=(7/10)^2-(31/100)^2>9/25.

At each fixed z there are two y intervals, each of length at most 10tau/3. Slice Dirichlet Poincare proves

    lambda1(-Delta,Omega_lambda)>=(3pi/(10tau))^2>16.  (G6)

It also gives |Omega_lambda|/C<=10tau/(3pi)<1/10. Neither conclusion is inferred just from small total volume. The boundaries are explicit smooth periodic graphs

    y=arccos[(+-tau-(1-lambda)cos z)/lambda]

and their reflected branches. Their lambda and fast-coordinate derivatives are uniformly bounded at fixed tau. Pulling these graphs to a fixed strip converts the Dirichlet equation into a smoothly parameterized uniformly elliptic coercive problem. Differentiation and boundary regularity give bounded finite derivatives of r_Q up to the boundary, uniformly over this compact weight interval. Only this regular family is used in the actual-transfer argument.

For fixed z, d=(1-lambda)cos z satisfies |d|<lambda. The principal value of mean_y 1/(lambda cos y+d) is zero (equivalently, the real part of the arcsine Cauchy transform vanishes on its support). Since Q_lambda/P_lambda=1/lambda-cos z/(lambda P_lambda),

    PV mean(Q_lambda/P_lambda)=1/lambda.

This can also be obtained directly with t=tan(y/2), integrating the rational function 2/[lambda+d-(lambda-d)t^2]; its two logarithmic pole terms cancel in the principal value.

Changing y to p=P_lambda near both roots gives the root-summed Q density

    F(p,z)=2(p-cos z)/[lambda^2 sqrt(1-((p-d)/lambda)^2)].

For |p|<=tau, |(p-d)/lambda|<=31/70<0.45. Direct differentiation gives |partial_p F|<16 uniformly. For example use lambda>=0.7, sqrt(1-0.45^2)>0.89 and |p-cos z|<=1.01 in its explicit derivative. The odd regularization difference rho_tau(p)-1/p pairs only F(p,z)-F(0,z), and

    integral_(-tau)^tau |rho_tau(p)-1/p| |p| dp=4tau/3.

Including normalization yields

    |M_tau(P_lambda;Q_lambda)+1/lambda|<=32tau/(3pi),
    M_tau(P_lambda;Q_lambda)<-6/5.                    (G7)

Choose the same one fixed sigma small enough that (G5) is <=1/20 for Q=P and Q=Q_lambda throughout the interval, as well as satisfying the original zero-safe restriction. Then, with m_P=mean[(S_sigma A_P)/P] and m_Q=mean[(S_sigma A_Qlambda)/P],

    -21/20<=m_P<-4/5, m_Q<-1.                         (G8)

The two coordinate derivative responses have mean zero: P and Omega are even; uniqueness makes r_partial_iP and its full residual odd in coordinate i; the tilted radial convolution respects that reflection; division by even P preserves oddness. This is an exact fixed-sigma statement.

## 6. Moving zero extensions and the O(mu) actual potential ledger

Because lambda depends only on S and r has zero trace,

    partial_S R_Y=1_Omega partial_S r,
    partial_Yi R_Y=1_Omega partial_Yi r.

Neither has a delta. The tangential derivative partial_Yi r also has zero trace, so partial_zetai partial_Yi R_Y is regular and bounded. Thus D1(b0R_Y) is a bounded regular function. In contrast, partial_S^2 R_Y has the finite boundary measure (partial_S 1_Omega)partial_S r. All its coefficients are uniformly bounded by the explicit graph and shape estimates. D2(b0R_Y) has bounded cell total variation. There is no uncharged order-kappa moving-boundary term.

Using (G11) distributionally, the full residual of f plus the zero-extended ansatz is

    -kappa H b0 A_(Q_Y)
       +O(mu H) regular
       +O(mu eta H) finite boundary measure.          (G13)

L0R_Y contains ALL fast second-derivative defects. The first-order slow derivative is regular; the slow second-derivative measure carries the extra eta. A fixed-sigma convolution bounds that latter contribution by C_sigma mu eta H.

Physical convolution changes (Y,zeta) to (Y-sigma eta v,zeta-sigma(v2,v3)). The leading boundary measure can be parameterized by the regular graph in zeta1. The graph, density and surface Jacobian change by O(eta) as the slow argument shifts. Pairing with the fixed smooth kernel therefore changes the convolved leading response by O_sigma(eta). This also follows from differentiating the smooth graph pullback; the nonzero zeta1 derivative gives a uniformly safe delta pullback Jacobian. The variation of b0 and the scalar coefficients in Q_Y has the same order. Multiplied by kappa, all these changes cost O(mu H). This statement is after convolution, not a false pointwise bound on displaced delta measures.

Convolve (G12) at fixed radius sigma/A and differentiate only its kernel twice. The cost is C_sigma A^2 times the bracket. Its three resulting ratios to mu are bounded, K^2 A/R_j->0, and e^(-c delta/eta)/eta->0. Thus replacing the ansatz by the actual global W costs O(mu H) in the numerator.

The actual radius is still epsilon(x)=sigma/a(R(x)). Its ratio to sigma/A is 1+O(eta), with |grad epsilon|<=C/(A R_j) and |D^2 epsilon|<=C/(A R_j^2). For the smooth f part, chain differentiation gives the average of Delta f and the exact commutators

    -2 sum_(i,k) v_k epsilon_i f_ik,
    |grad epsilon|^2 sum_(k,l) v_k v_l f_kl,
    -(Delta epsilon) v dot grad f.

Their bounds are O(mu E), O(R_j^(-2)E), O(R_j^(-2)E). Moving the averaged Delta f from radius sigma/A costs O(eta/A) times the inherited residual gradient C kappa A E, also O(mu E).

For W write t(x)=a(R(x))/sigma, u=t(x)(x-y), K_x(y)=t(x)^n psi(u), and Dpsi=n psi+u dot grad psi. Direct differentiation gives

    Delta_x K_x=t^(n+2)Delta psi
      +2t^n sum_i t_i[(n+1)psi_i+(u dot grad)psi_i]
      +t^(n-1)(Delta t)Dpsi
      +t^(n-2)|grad t|^2[(n-1)Dpsi+(u dot grad)Dpsi]. (G14)

The non-leading terms are bounded by C[A^(n+1)/R_j+A^n/R_j^2]. The leading kernel differs from its frozen counterpart by C eta A^(n+2), including differences of support because psi extends smoothly by zero. Integrating against |W|<=C eta E over volume O(A^(-n)) gives

    |Delta smoothing(W)-Delta frozen_smoothing(W)|<=C mu E.

This differentiates no global W. The denominator difference costs only O(eta E); the frozen numerator is O(kappa E). Both denominators are bounded below by c_tau E where |P|>=tau/2. Inside |P|<tau/2 both averages equal the actual harmonic f+W as functions, and their Laplacians vanish. Since the leading residual is zero safely near P=0, the regions combine to yield the actual uniform expansion

## 7. Positive form for every test, via a fixed-torus corrector

Linearity in Q and the exact derivative-response parity give

    bar V1=-2beta S m_P(lambda)+2lambda'(S)m_Q(lambda).

On the compensated box lambda'=2beta S and S<-0.9. By (G8),

    -m_P+2m_Q<=21/20-2=-19/20,
    bar V1=2beta S[-m_P+2m_Q]>=beta.                  (G16)

A positive mean alone would not prove the form statement. On the SAME fixed torus T^2 define the mean-zero corrector explicitly by Fourier series:

    G(Y,zeta)=-sum_(k in Z^2\{0})
                    V1_hat(Y,k)/|k|^2 exp(i k dot zeta).

Then Delta_zeta G=V1-bar V1. The fast smoothed quotient and its required finite Y derivatives are uniformly smooth. Their Fourier coefficients decay uniformly, so G and the required fast/slow derivatives are bounded. The denominator |k|^2>=1 makes this a genuinely fixed coercive inverse; no quasiperiodic or small-frequency inverse is assumed.

Set g_j(x)=eta_j G(Y,zeta). The exact chain rule gives

    |g_j|<=C eta_j, |grad g_j|^2<=C mu_j,
    Delta g_j=kappa_j(V1-bar V1)+O(mu_j).             (G17)

The mixed term is eta A/ell=mu; the purely slow term is eta/ell^2=mu eta. Together with (G15)-(G16),

    V-Delta g_j-|grad g_j|^2
       >=kappa_j beta_j-C mu_j
       >=(1/2)kappa_j beta_j>0                       (G18)

eventually, since mu/(kappa beta)=eta/beta->0. For every compact H1 chi supported in that box, exact integration by parts gives

    q_H(e^g chi)=integral e^(2g)
      [|grad chi|^2+(V-Delta g-|grad g|^2)chi^2].

The bounded smooth multiplication e^g bijects the compact H1 test space. Therefore

    q_H(v)>=(1/2)kappa_j beta_j ||v||_2^2

for EVERY such v and all late j. This is a form certificate for the full actual V. It is not a claim that V is pointwise positive or that a global positive solution exists.
