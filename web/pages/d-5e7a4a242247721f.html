# Scalar Euclidean finite-index question: angular discriminator and failed Green shortcut

Status, 2026-10-07: **universal finite-Morse implication UNKNOWN**. The angular condition below is proved independently. It does not follow from finite Morse index. The compact-source and finite-rank intermediate claims below are false. No claim that the target question is open in the external literature is made.

## Exact target and supplied inputs

Let H=−Δ+V on L²(Rⁿ), V real, smooth, bounded, V(x)→0 uniformly as |x|→∞. Let u≠0 be a smooth L² solution Hu=0, with e^{α|x|}u∈L² for every α>0. Does this force N₋(H)=∞, counting multiplicity?

Finite index m supplies an exterior nonnegative form and hence a positive exterior zero solution ψ. The earlier threshold Courant argument gives at most m+1 global nodal domains, but supplies no geometric bound on their widths or Harnack-chain lengths. The index-one polynomial fixture and the finite-index anisotropic cylinder example in my cycle6 notes do not resolve this scalar Euclidean problem. This note does not identify arbitrary signed inner boundary traces with positive or zero traces.

## 1. A Hilbert-valued radial ODE lemma

Let y:[R,∞)→𝓗 be C² into a real or complex Hilbert space. Suppose

    ||y''(r)|| ≤ a(r)||y(r)||,     a(r)→0,

and ∫_R^∞ e^{2αr}||y(r)||²dr<∞ for some α>0. Then y≡0 on a tail, and hence everywhere wherever its differential inequality is available.

Proof. Choose 0<δ<α and Rδ with a≤δ². Set Y=(δy,y') in 𝓗⊕𝓗. Then

    ||Y'||² = δ²||y'||²+||y''||² ≤ δ²||Y||².

Consequently |(d/dr)||Y|||≤δ||Y|| wherever the norm is nonzero. Gronwall in both directions shows that if Y(r₀)≠0 then

    ||Y(r)|| ≥ ||Y(r₀)|| exp(−δ(r−r₀)),   r≥r₀.

For completeness the exponential L² hypothesis also controls the phase state, not just y. Take a smooth cutoff χ on (r−2,r+2), equal to one on (r−1,r+1), with uniformly bounded derivative. Integration by parts gives

    ∫χ²||y'||² = −Re∫χ²⟨y,y''⟩−2Re∫χχ'⟨y,y'⟩
              ≤ δ²∫χ²||y||² + (1/2)∫χ²||y'||² + 2∫|χ'|²||y||².

Thus local L² norms of y' and, by the assumed inequality, y'' are bounded by a constant times the outer local L² norm of y. The one-dimensional Hilbert-valued H¹ point-evaluation bound, applied separately to y and y', yields

    ||Y(r)|| ≤ Cδ ||y||_{L²(r−2,r+2;𝓗)} ≤ Cδ,α e^{−αr}.

This contradicts the lower bound unless Y vanishes. The differential inequality itself gives uniqueness of zero phase data by the same Gronwall estimate; it is not necessary to represent y'' as a bounded linear operator applied to y. ∎

This proof is valid for signed vector functions and for infinite-dimensional 𝓗. There is no positivity assumption.

## 2. Exact angular-frequency discriminator

Assume n≥2, and define the spherical trace and its radial conjugation by

    y(r,θ)=r^{(n−1)/2}u(rθ),    𝓗=L²(S^{n−1}).

The scalar PDE gives the exact identity

    y'' = V(rθ)y − r^{−2}Δ_S y + [(n−1)(n−3)/(4r²)]y.

Smooth u makes this a C² Hilbert-valued identity on each compact radial interval. Also

    ∫e^{2αr}||y(r)||²dr = ∫_{|x|>R}e^{2α|x|}|u(x)|²dx.

If, on a tail, the trace satisfies

    ||Δ_S u(r·)||₂ ≤ ε(r)r² ||u(r·)||₂,   ε(r)→0,

then the ODE lemma applies with

    a(r)=sup_{|x|=r}|V(x)|+ε(r)+|(n−1)(n−3)|/(4r²)→0.

Therefore **no nonzero zero state satisfying this angular condition can carry even one positive exponential L² weight**. If the resulting conclusion is u=0 on an exterior open set, ordinary unique continuation for smooth bounded scalar V gives u=0 globally.

Equivalently, a proposed exponentially weighted nonzero state must fail every eventual o(r²) relative angular-Laplacian bound. A quantitative version is useful: if e^{αr}u∈L², then for every δ<α it is impossible that ||y''||≤δ²||y|| eventually. Because V→0 and the radial correction is O(r^{−2}), for every η<α² it is impossible that

    ||Δ_S u(r·)||₂ ≤ η r²||u(r·)||₂

on the entire sufficiently distant tail. Thus violations recur arbitrarily far out. At a zero spherical trace this statement is understood as the displayed norm inequality, not as division by its norm. This does not say that every radius has high angular frequency, or supply an eigenvalue counting argument.

The spherical angular scale here is structural: Δ_S acts on the unit sphere. Modes of degree ℓ have eigenvalue ℓ(ℓ+n−2), so degree comparable to r is needed to maintain an O(1) physical angular second derivative. The condition is a relative L² norm bound; it is not a pointwise nodal-topology condition.

## 3. Finite bandwidth and radial exterior corollaries

For arbitrary V→0, suppose u's exterior angular expansion is supported in one fixed finite set of spherical harmonics. Then ||Δ_S u||≤Λ||u|| for a finite Λ, so Section2 applies. Thus every nonzero exponentially weighted solution must leave every fixed finite angular subspace.

An independent finite-dimensional reconstruction makes the assumptions especially transparent. With orthonormal harmonics Y_a of degrees ℓ_a, write u=Σf_a(r)Y_a. Projection of multiplication by V gives a matrix

    V_ab(r)=∫ over S V(rθ) conjugate(Y_a)Y_b,
    ||V_ab(r)||op ≤ sup_{|x|=r}|V(x)|.

After setting y_a=r^{(n−1)/2}f_a,

    y''=Q(r)y,
    Q=V_ab+diag([((ℓ_a+(n−2)/2)²−1/4)/r²]),   ||Q||→0.

The ODE proof applies. The fact that the full PDE also imposes equations outside the chosen harmonic span does not invalidate this necessary projected equation.

If V is radial outside a compact set, no finite-bandwidth hypothesis on u is needed. Projection onto any single harmonic is decoupled. A nonzero exterior u has at least one nonzero coefficient f of a fixed degree ℓ. That coefficient obeys

    f''+(n−1)f'/r−[ℓ(ℓ+n−2)/r²+V_rad(r)]f=0,
    y=r^{(n−1)/2}f,
    y''=[V_rad(r)+((ℓ+(n−2)/2)²−1/4)/r²]y.

The coefficient tends to zero, so the lemma excludes every positive exponential weight. In n=1 the same result follows directly on either exterior half-line from u''=Vu. Finite index plays no role in these corollaries. If the radial potential is bounded but does not tend to zero, the unscaled bounded-generator argument instead excludes all-exponential decay, without excluding every single exponential rate.

Novelty status: separation of variables, Gronwall, and this Hilbert-valued extension are elementary tools; no originality claim is made. The resulting necessary angular-transfer criterion is useful for selecting a construction or missing proof lemma.

## 4. Finite index strengthens the positive ground-state transform, but does not close the gap

Let ψ>0 solve Δψ=Vψ outside a ball. For any large L, sufficiently far out a ball B(x,2L) remains in the exterior and satisfies L² sup_B|V|≤1. Scaled positive Harnack and an interior Poisson gradient estimate give

    |∇ψ(x)|/ψ(x) ≤ C_n/L.

One derivation is to decompose ψ into a harmonic function with its boundary data and a Green-potential correction on the ball. Harnack bounds sup_Bψ by Cψ(x); the harmonic gradient contributes O(L^{−1})supψ, the correction O(L sup|V|)supψ. First choose L arbitrarily large and then let |x| be large. It follows that ∇logψ→0 uniformly.

Integrating |∇logψ|≤ε along ordinary exterior radial segments and the compact starting sphere gives, for every ε>0,

    cε e^{−ε|x|} ≤ ψ(x) ≤ Cε e^{ε|x|}

outside a larger compact set. For v=u/ψ,

    div(ψ²∇v)=0,   or Δv+2∇logψ·∇v=0,

and v retains every exponential L² weight. The drift is curl-free and tends to zero. Also div(∇logψ)=V−|∇logψ|²→0. These facts do not imply a uniform angular derivative bound or a maximum principle for signed arbitrary inner boundary data. A theorem excluding all-exponential signed solutions for exactly this exterior gradient-drift class would close the target implication; none is established here.

## 5. Two false Green-function intermediate claims

### Finite Morse index does not make the exterior response finite rank

Even H₀=−Δ with index0 has infinitely many independent decaying exterior harmonic modes. In R³, for each ℓ≥1,

    u_ℓ(x)=(x₁+i x₂)^ℓ / |x|^{2ℓ+1}

is harmonic outside zero and is L² outside the unit ball. Real or imaginary parts give real examples. Its trace has harmonic degree ℓ, so the modes are independent. Their tails decay as |x|^{−ℓ−1}; arbitrarily high algebraic decay is possible in the exterior without negative spectrum. This is an exterior boundary-response statement, not a construction of a whole-space homogeneous threshold state. Matching to an interior PDE is the unclosed restriction.

### Positive Green kernels do not forbid silent signed compact sources

Let Γ be the Newton fundamental solution in Rⁿ, n≥3. For any nonzero w∈C_c^∞ set f=−Δw. Distributional convolution gives

    Γ*f = Γ*(−Δw) = (−ΔΓ)*w = w.

Thus f is a nonzero compact signed source whose Green potential vanishes identically outside its support. For every entire harmonic polynomial P,

    ∫fP=−∫wΔP=0.

All harmonic moments cancel, and disjointly supported choices of w give an infinite-dimensional silent-source space. Positivity of Γ only rules out such cancellation if f has one sign. A compact perturbation rewriting H₊u=Wu produces a signed source whenever u changes sign; it does not supply the required sign.

This counterexample does not refute the target conjecture: f here is arbitrary forcing. Enforcing f=Wu, retaining the original homogeneous equation with a bounded scalar V, and excluding compact support by UCP are the missing constraints. It does refute “a positive Green kernel and nonzero compact forcing automatically yield a nonzero tail,” and “finite negative spectrum makes the arbitrary exterior trace response finite-dimensional.”
