# A polynomial-bit random prime preserving the complete subspace packing cone

7 October 2026. Worker metric_geometry10, cycle16, verified Sol/max. The core cone/probability/bit-length proof has an independent root PASS in notes/root_cycle16/RANDOM_PRIME_INDEPENDENT_GATE.md; second source/optimizer audit is being finalized by stat_exec15. These are internal reconstructed proof gates, not independent external validation. The general modular inconsistency principle is old. No priority, deterministic certification, or executed optimizer claim.

## 1. Exact interface and consequence

Input consists of integer matrices B_i in Z^(r x d_i), i=1,...,m, of full column rank, with r,m>=1, 1<=d_i<=r, and entries of absolute value at most 2^H, H>=1. Rational input can be cleared columnwise first, with polynomial input bit cost. Zero subspaces can be removed and restored as unconstrained coordinates. Put S_i=im B_i, and

P_K={x>=0: sum_i x_i dim_K(S_i intersect W)<=dim_K W for every W<=K^r}.

There is an unconditional expected polynomial bit-time procedure which, given rational 0<delta<1, outputs a prime p of polynomial bit length such that

Pr[all B_i retain rank and P_Fp=P_Q]>=1-delta.

The equality is simultaneous for ALL nonnegative real weight vectors x, including adaptively chosen ones. The procedure computes only an explicit size bound and samples a prime. It does not enumerate profiles or compute the Nullstellensatz integers, and it does not supply a deterministic certificate that its particular p is good.

Combining this with Hirai2608.13972v1 Theorem3.5 gives a randomized polynomial BIT-time additive approximation to the rational packing OPTIMAL VALUE, and the relaxed BL membership interface of his Lemma4.4/Theorem4.5. It does not automatically provide a rational fractional hitting-subspace solution or a rational violating flag: lifting an individual finite-field flag is a separate task. Common-denominator-dependent exact threshold tests remain pseudopolynomial in that denominator and exponential in r under the cycle15 threshold bound.

## 2. Previously gated ingredients, with quantifiers retained

The cycle15 root gate establishes P_Q=P_C (also P_R) for rational input. A proof uses supermodularity of the defect and the unique largest maximizing subspace, whose RREF descends by Galois invariance. It does NOT assert rational realizability of every incidence profile.

For every prime preserving all input ranks, P_Fp is contained in P_Q. Indeed, primitive lattices for rational S_i,W and their intersections reduce injectively, so each intersection dimension can only increase. Clearing the supplied B_i gives the same reduced subspace when rank is preserved. Thus finite-field positive feasibility is sound; arbitrary rejection need not be sound.

We use these results exactly as scoped in notes/root_cycle15/FINITE_FIELD_AND_THRESHOLD_GATE.md. The proof below makes the exceptional-prime set quantitatively bounded and does not replace either ingredient by a ground-matroid comparison.

For completeness, the descent can be reconstructed without an assumption on individual profile points. Define Phi_x(W)=sum_i x_i dim(S_i intersect W)-dim W. It has finitely many possible values and is supermodular. The sum and intersection of two maximizers are again maximizers, because their values cannot exceed the maximum. There is therefore a unique largest maximizer W*. Choose its dimension k and exact intersection profile a. A complex point in its standard affine chart implies that the rational polynomial incidence system has a point over the algebraic closure of Q. Its lower intersection bounds make Phi_x at least the complex maximum, hence equal to it. That algebraic point has dimension k and is a maximizing subspace, so it equals W* by maximality and dimension. Galois automorphisms preserve every dimension and hence W*; its unique RREF coordinates are rational. This argument also works when the x_i are arbitrary real nonnegative numbers: only the integer profiles, not Galois action on those external weights, enter. Testing rational W thus detects every complex violation. No claim that the particular initial profile point is rational is used.

For the lattice inclusion, let L_i=S_i intersect Z^r, L_W=W intersect Z^r, and L_I=(S_i intersect W) intersect Z^r. Each is saturated in Z^r and reduces injectively with unchanged rank. The reduced L_I is contained in both reduced L_i and L_W, so their intersection has at least its old rank. Smith normal form of the supplied columns shows that full rank of B_i mod p makes their span equal to reduced L_i: all invariant factors of the finite-index column lattice must be invertible mod p. Reduced L_W is a k-plane. Nonnegative x then transfers every finite-field packing inequality to the corresponding rational one. This uses rank admissibility of INPUT B_i, not preservation of all ground-set ranks or an unknown W denominator.

## 3. All possible characteristic-zero forbidden profiles

For k=0,...,r and a=(a_1,...,a_m), 0<=a_i<=min(d_i,k), consider the incidence locus

Z_(k,a)={W in Gr(k,r)_C: dim(S_i intersect W)>=a_i for every i}.

There are at most (r+1)^m profiles for each k. Cover Gr(k,r) by its binomial(r,k) standard affine charts: choose k pivot rows, set the corresponding r x k basis matrix Y to identity there, and use n=k(r-k) variables in the other rows. A minor of the pivot block is already 1, so this parametrizes actual k-planes over every field; no localization or nonvanishing constraint is missing.

On a chart, the incidence constraints are

rank[B_i Y]<=d_i+k-a_i.

They are imposed by all minors of size d_i+k-a_i+1 when that size is <=r. Sizes >r impose no constraints. Provided B_i retains its rank, this equation is equivalent to the requested intersection dimension, by the dimension formula for S_i+W.

Uniform bounds on these chart equations:

- n<=r^2 (the tighter r^2/4 is unnecessary).
- Degree<=r. Every minor has at most r! terms, and each term has coefficient at most 2^(rH), so logarithmic coefficient height is at most rH log2+log(r!).
- Number of equations s<=m*8^r: the number of row choices is <=2^r and column choices <=2^(d_i+k)<=2^(2r).
- Total number of chart/profile pairs over all k is at most G=2^r(r+1)^m.

These bounds describe equations used in the proof; the acquisition procedure never expands them.

## 4. Arithmetic certificate and a single bound for all bad primes

Krick--Pardo--Sombra, arXiv:math/9911094v1, Introduction Theorem1, proves that polynomials f_1,...,f_s in Z[t_1,...,t_n] with no common complex zero admit a nonzero integer A and integer-polynomial coefficients g_i such that A=sum_i g_i f_i. With d=max degree and h=max log coefficient height, their bound is

log|A| <= 4 n(n+1)d^n [h+log s+(n+7)d log(n+1)].

The exact last term d log(n+1) was inspected in the preserved primary HTML, rather than inferred from a search excerpt. Degree bounds also hold but are not needed by the sampler. A prime at which the equations have a common zero over algebraic closure must divide A. Thus the number of such primes is <=log_2|A|. This standard implication is already explicit in Hägele--Morais--Pardo--Sombra, On the intrinsic complexity of the arithmetic Nullstellensatz, Introduction, pp1--2.

For an entirely integer-computable conservative bound, put

N=r^2, d=2r,
h*=r[H+ceil(log_2(2r))+2],
l*=ceil(log_2(m+1))+3r,
C=4N(N+1)d^N [h*+l*+(N+7)d(N+1+d)],
B=2GC+2mh*.

Then each empty chart has at most 2C exceptional primes. This dominates the quoted natural-log bound because n<=N, d_actual<=d, h<=h*, log s<=l*, and log(n+1)<=N+1+d. Charts with n=0, or only constant equations, have a nonzero integer constant among their equations; choosing it as A gives log|A|<=h*, also covered. Identically zero equations may be omitted.

Input rank failures have at most 2mh* primes: select one nonzero full-size minor for each B_i and bound the logarithm of their product. Hence at most B primes lie in the union of all input rank failures and all exceptional primes for all characteristic-zero EMPTY chart/profile pairs.

No choice of which pairs are empty is needed to compute B. The bound deliberately counts more pairs than occur and is insensitive to whether arbitrary nonempty profiles have rational points.

## 5. Why the union controls the full cone

Let p avoid that union. All ranks are preserved, so P_Fp is contained in P_Q. If some x in P_Q fails the finite-field constraints, choose W over Fp with dimension k and exact profile a_i=dim(S_i mod p intersect W). Then sum_i x_i a_i>k.

If Z_(k,a) were nonempty over C, any W' in it would satisfy sum_i x_i dim(S_i intersect W')>=sum_i x_i a_i>k, since x_i>=0. This contradicts x in P_C=P_Q. Thus Z_(k,a) is empty over C. The finite-field W belongs to one of the standard affine charts and solves that chart's minor equations; therefore p divides its arithmetic certificate integer, contrary to the choice of p.

This proves P_Q is contained in P_Fp, hence equality. The proof uses a forbidden PROFILE for each hypothetical discrepancy, rather than vertices or weights. One bounded exceptional set consequently protects all weights simultaneously.

## 6. Sampling and exact bit charge

Let q=ceil(1/delta), b=bit_length(B), and L=4[b+bit_length(q)+20]. Sample uniformly from the odd L-bit integers, applying a deterministic polynomial bit-time primality test until a prime is found. Conditioned on acceptance the prime is exactly uniform in [2^(L-1),2^L). L>=80; prime endpoints are irrelevant here.

The Rosser--Schoenfeld1962 inequalities pi(x)>x/log x for x>=17 and pi(x)<1.25506 x/log x for x>1 imply, for L>=6,

pi(2^L)-pi(2^(L-1)) >= 2^(L-1)/(4L).

Indeed [1/L-1.25506/(2(L-1))]/log2 >=1/(8L); the inequality is weakest at L=6 and follows already by replacing 1.25506 with 1.26 and log2 with 1. Thus the expected number of odd candidates is <=2L. AKS (published2004, first author version2002) supplies unconditional deterministic polynomial bit-time primality testing, so exact uniform sampling has expected polynomial bit complexity. A Miller--Rabin implementation would require a separately charged error; none is substituted silently here.

The probability of a bad prime is at most 4BL/2^(L-1). Since Bq<2^(L/4-20), this is smaller than 1/q<=delta. Rank may be tested afterward and a failure reported as UNKNOWN; avoid conditioning by repeated rank-based resampling unless its probability charge is adjusted.

The giant integers B and C have polynomial BIT length:

log_2 B=O(r^2 log(r+1)+m log(r+1)+log(H+m+2)).

They can be computed by integer exponentiation in polynomial bit time. The hidden arithmetic certificates themselves need not have polynomial bit length and are never acquired. L and the prime p have polynomial bit length in the supplied input and log(delta^-1).

A worst-case polynomial-time Monte Carlo variant is also available. Set q=ceil(2/delta) in the SAME L formula, perform at most T=2L*bit_length(q) independent odd-candidate trials, and return UNKNOWN if no prime is found or the accepted prime loses input rank. Each trial has prime probability at least 1/(2L); hence timeout probability is at most exp[-T/(2L)]<=1/q<=delta/2. Conditional on finding a prime by the cutoff, its distribution is still exactly uniform among the L-bit primes: every prime has the same geometric sum of acceptance probabilities. The bad-prime probability is at most 1/q<=delta/2. Consequently this bounded procedure returns a good prime with probability at least 1-delta, using a deterministic polynomial bound on all bit operations (with the imported AKS algorithm). The expected-time version always returns a prime; the bounded version permits explicitly reported UNKNOWN. Neither version was executed here with AKS.

## 7. Optimizer interface and prior boundary

Hirai's algorithm is defined over an arbitrary field; basis operations over Fp take polynomial bit time in log p. Theorem3.5 bounds the rational building coordinates' bit length by a polynomial in c_max,r,m,epsilon^-1 and the bit length of u. For the BL objective c_i=d_i<=r and caps u in [0,1], the number of field operations is O(r^7 m^3 epsilon^-2). Its returned rational objective v lies between tau_p and tau_p+epsilon. On the high-probability full-cone equality event tau_p=tau_Q. Thus v is a polynomial-size additive approximation to the original optimal VALUE in expected polynomial bit time, with error event <=delta.

The flag in the output remains over Fp; equality of cones does not turn its coordinates into a rational original-field flag. Do not advertise an original rational dual witness, exact BL constant, or finite-field flag lift. On exceptional admissible primes, the cone inclusion still protects positive feasible weights, but an objective underestimate or a negative decision can be wrong.

More precisely, input to the BL decision is rational u in [0,1]^m with sum_i d_i u_i=r; other necessary conditions can be checked exactly first. Run the finite-field capped packing objective with c_i=d_i and accuracy epsilon. If v>=r, then tau_p>=r-epsilon. By P_Fp contained in P_Q and the original-field version of Lemma4.4, u belongs to the original relaxed BL polytope: this affirmative relaxed answer is sound on EVERY admissible prime. If v<r, the conclusion u not in the original exact BL polytope is sound on the full-cone equality event, and otherwise remains probabilistic. The relaxed set means sum_i u_i dim(B_i^T V)>=dim V-epsilon for all real V, together with the balance tolerance from the source. It is not an unspecified Euclidean-distance membership test.

For fixed input subspaces, one sampled prime protects any number of subsequent or adaptive caps, objectives and accuracies on the SAME simultaneous event. There is no need for a union bound over such optimizer queries. This is a probabilistic event guarantee, not an acquired deterministic check of that event.

Garg--Gurvits--Oliveira--Wigderson1607.06711v4, Section1.2 Theorem1.2, already gives exact BL membership/separation in poly(input bits,D) where D is the numerical common denominator. This does not supply poly(log D) exact membership. The older moment-polytope weak membership methods are also strong approximate comparators. We make no claim of first randomized weak BL membership; the useful deduction is an explicit finite-field, complete-cone random reduction for the 2026 algorithm, subject to independent gate.
