## G1. Reconstructed kernel-row inequality

Let c be a unit vector, let p0=|c0|²>0, and let K be a Hermitian positive contraction with Kc=0. Write a=K00, and let S be the row's off-diagonal neighbors on the support of c. Put pS=Σ(j in S)|cj|². The zero row equation gives

\[
a^2p_0=\left|\sum_{j\in S}K_{0j}c_j\right|^2
\le p_S\sum_{j\in S}|K_{0j}|^2.
\]

Functional calculus gives K²≤K, so the full off-diagonal squared norm is at most a−a². If a>0, division proves

\[
a\le\frac{p_S}{p_0+p_S}.\tag{G1}
\]

For a=0 the same inequality holds. Coordinates where c vanishes cannot improve the kernel equation and only spend the full squared-norm budget. This is why allowing couplings to arbitrarily many unused frequency sectors does not invalidate G1.

If K≥γ(I−P), Pc=c, and the chosen row has P00=p0, then a≥γ(1−p0). For c=(1/√2,1/√(2m),…,1/√(2m)), an r-neighbor tail gives pS=r/(2m), hence

\[
\gamma\le\frac{2r}{m+r},\qquad
r\ge\frac{\gamma m}{2-\gamma}.\tag{G2}
\]

Here 0<γ≤1 whenever the complementary space is nonzero. At r=m, K=I−cc* attains γ=1 and a=1/2. A fixed normalized enforcement gap therefore forces a linearly dense heavy row.
