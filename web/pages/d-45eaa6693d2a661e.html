## Exact interface and why this changes the cycle17 gap

Input: a dense integer quiver representation, signed integer vertex weights in binary, and total active dimension A. Output: an explicit rational basis of the unique largest weight-maximizing subrepresentation. Cycle17 proved conditional canonical modular commutation and all-equation Hensel certification, but did not bound the original-input rational coordinate height. The construction below shows that no such polynomial height bound can hold for this unrestricted interface. It uses only dimension-three vertices, constant-sized arrow entries, an acyclic quiver and a unique optimizer over every field. The obstruction therefore survives canonical selection and zero Hom rigidity.

The input family has N=9+12k vertices, 8+24k arrows, A=3N, arrow entries in {0,1,2}, and total absolute-weight binary bit length N^2. Its final one-dimensional optimizer component is span(2^(2^k),0,1). Either of its legal identity-pivot charts contains 2^(2^k) or its reciprocal; both need 2^k+1 scalar bits. Explicit output cannot be polynomial in the original binary input size O(k^2). A compressed circuit description is a different output and remains small.

## L1. A compiler for finite meet/join programmes

Fix ambient K^d and a chronological list of fixed subspaces and operations. Each new join is the sum of two already available subspaces; each new meet is their intersection. Let N be the number of programme vertices plus one frame vertex. Make every representation vertex K^d. The first frame has full subspace K^d, selected by a positive objective digit. A fixed generator X=im C has an arrow C from the frame to its vertex and a negative objective digit. For a join J=X+Y, put identity arrows X→J and Y→J and give J a negative digit. For a meet M=X∩Y, put identity arrows M→X and M→Y and give M a positive digit. All parents precede the operation in programme order; graph arrows need not follow that order.

Let epsilon_i be +1 for the frame/meets and −1 for fixed generators/joins, and put weight_i=epsilon_i B^(N−1−i), with B=d+1. Since every dimension is in [0,d],

    d sum_(j<i exponent)(B^j)=B^i−1 < B^i.

Thus the integer score strictly implements chronological lexicographic optimization of epsilon_i dim W_i. No unknown solver or subspace enumerator is invoked.

Every feasible prefix extends to a complete subrepresentation: set each future join/fixed-generator vertex to K^d and every future meet vertex to 0. Every edge between future vertices or across the prefix then obeys closure. The frame is first, so its full-space choice extends. Once previous choices are fixed uniquely, a negative generator digit forces im C; a negative join digit forces precisely X+Y; a positive meet digit forces precisely X∩Y. The minimum/maximum feasible dimension has only that one subspace. Induction gives a unique global optimizer equal to the programme at every vertex, over every field, including all degenerate ranks. In particular it is the largest optimizer.

## L2. A uniform nondegenerate squaring programme in dimension three

Write affine projective points as one-dimensional subspaces spanned by (x,y,1). Fixed generators are H=(1,0,0), V=(0,1,0), U=(1,0,1), the x-axis X=span((0,0,1),H), the y-axis Y=span((0,0,1),V), the diagonal Z=span((0,0,1),(1,1,0)), infinity I=span(H,V), and initial A0=(2,0,1). Each is the image of a 3×3 integer matrix with entries in {0,1,2}.

Starting from A=(a,0,1), append the six joins and six meets:

    B=(A+V)∩Z=(a,a,1);
    C=(B+H)∩Y=(0,a,1);
    J=(U+C)∩I=(1,−a,0);
    D=(A+J)∩Y=(0,a²,1);
    E=(D+H)∩Z=(a²,a²,1);
    Anew=(E+V)∩X=(a²,0,1).

Each intermediate join has dimension2 because one point has last coordinate1 and the independent direction has last coordinate0, or because U and C have distinct first coordinate. The joined planes have respective normals

    (1,0,−a), (0,1,−a), (−a,−1,a),
    (a,1,−a²), (0,1,−a²), (1,0,−a²).

These differ from the second meet plane by a coefficient ±1 for every a over every field. Thus all meets have dimension1, even at a=0 and in characteristic2. Solving the two plane equations gives the displayed points with no division by a. Repeating k rounds yields a=2^(2^k).

Every non-frame identity arrow in this particular compiler points from a point vertex to a plane vertex. The frame has no incoming arrows. Hence the quiver is acyclic with directed path length at most2, even though programme order is not a quiver topological order.

## L3. Input and output costs

There are eight fixed-generator vertices, one frame and twelve new vertices per round. There are eight frame arrows and two identity arrows per operation. All ambient dimensions are3. The largest absolute weight is4^(N−1), of bit length2N−1; summing the weight bit lengths gives exactly N^2. Dense arrow data has9(8+24k) integer entries of at most2 bits. Endpoint labels add O(k log k) bits. Total explicit binary input is O(k^2); numeric weights are exponential in k, so the source algorithms polynomial in numeric weight magnitude are not contradicted.

At the final component a=2^(2^k). With first pivot the chart is (1,0,1/a); with third pivot it is (a,0,1). The second coordinate is zero, so no other pivot is possible. Each normalized chart has maximal scalar bit length2^k+1. Even an arbitrary rational rescaling cannot make every scalar numerator and denominator polynomial-bit: their ratio is a, and the combined numerator/denominator length must account for log2 a=2^k. The identity-chart lower bound is exact.

The unique optimizer exists with the same dimensions over every prime field. Its saturated Q lattice reduces to the programme's modular optimizer. This is a direct selected-objective theorem for this family, not a certificate that every prime preserves the whole dimension polytope. Largest-optimizer Hom(W,V/W)=0 therefore holds by the previously reconstructed modular-score argument. Rigidity and a good modular chart do not remove the height obstruction.
