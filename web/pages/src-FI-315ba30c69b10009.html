## Verdict

The packet is careful about its central open gate: it does **not** prove the full nine-coefficient rank-one-convexity-to-quasiconvexity implication and supplies no negative compatible Jensen gap. Its strongest headline results are plausible, sharply scoped candidates for internal proof, rather than an established resolution of the positive-definite sector.

The claimed two-contact theorem, its covariance-to-acoustic lower bound, and the contact-shear statement require line-by-line proof review. The main mathematical pressure points are the reductions for a rank-one distinguished background, the singular top-block cases in the invertible-background classification, and the joint acoustic perturbation used to eliminate the nonshear semidefinite branch. Exact finite algebra can check the displayed identities but cannot by itself close those analytic case reductions.

The concentration construction has the right scaling for a genuine compatible counterexample to continuity of the *optimized cubic loss*: the inserted field has gradient size of order \(\rho^{-1/2}\) on area \(\rho^2\), so its fourth-moment contribution remains order one while its second-, third-, and fourth-degree integrated moments scale as \(\rho,\sqrt\rho,1\). Its claimed limit is not a negative quasiconvexity gap. The packet expressly says fourth moments are bounded but not equiintegrable and Lipschitz norms diverge, so it does not support a weak/equiintegrable or uniformly bounded-gradient extension.

The contact-shear change of variables is an exact straightening operation under its stated rank-one/tangency hypotheses. It leaves a position-dependent transformed integrand in the residual problem. Averaging that integrand would be invalid absent a new estimate; thus this operation does not currently solve the remaining interaction problem.
