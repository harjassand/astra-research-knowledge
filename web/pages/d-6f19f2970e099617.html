ASSIGNMENT AND BOTTOM LINE

Target: given rational Dicke-diagonal N-qubit probabilities p_0,...,p_N,
promised to be fully separable, output a positive mixture of iid pure qubit
products approximating the input to trace error epsilon, in bit complexity
polynomial in N, input bit length, and log(1/epsilon). The proposed route adds
eta=epsilon/8 times the uniform spin-coherent mixture P_sym/(N+1), so a
representing measure gains a Lebesgue component eta dx, then reconstructs a
positive finite rule using Gaussian / Gauss-Radau quadrature with certified
rational precision. The exact separability decision remains imported from the
finite Hankel criterion; this is a promised-input compiler, not a new SEP
criterion.

The high-level decomposition and a numerical O(N^3) moment-to-decomposition
algorithm are already explicit in primary literature. Thus neither finite
positive iid decomposition existence, polynomial-size support, nor the
moment/Hankel-to-atomic-representation architecture should be presented as
new. In the bounded primary-source check below, I found no explicit guarantee
in the same bit model: no rational-input encoding, epsilon-controlled output,
uniform conditioning bound, or certified precision sufficient to keep all
quadrature weights positive. Any novelty claim should be restricted to that
quantitative bit-complexity / certification interface, and only after proving
the stated lower bounds and output error. This is a priority lead, not a
novelty certification.

CLOSEST EXACT PRIOR ART

1. Nengkun Yu, “Separability of a mixture of Dicke states,” Phys. Rev. A 94,
060101(R) (2016), arXiv:1501.02957, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.94.060101.
Theorem 1 gives the exact Hankel criterion for Dicke-diagonal symmetric
N-qubit states. The paper also states that every separable such state has a
decomposition into at most (N+1)(N+2) product states, and establishes that
separable terms for a bosonic state can be taken identical across sites. This
already gives an exact polynomial-size iid-pure-product existence result.
The proof is a criterion/existence argument; the source does not state a
precision-controlled bit algorithm for producing the decomposition.

2. N. S. Bassler, “Absence of entanglement growth in Dicke superradiance,”
Phys. Rev. A 112, 053713 (2025), DOI 10.1103/qxx1-xr44,
arXiv:2504.13646 (v3, 26 March 2026).
Appendix A identifies phase-averaged spin-coherent products with Bernstein
moments and maps arbitrary separable Dicke-diagonal populations to a truncated
Hausdorff moment vector. Appendix C then gives a reconstruction procedure:
identify the Hankel rank by SVD; solve a Hankel linear system for the monic
annihilating polynomial; find support points from companion-matrix
eigenvalues; and solve a Vandermonde system for the positive weights. It
states O(N^3) computational complexity. Its Appendix C begins “Given a moment
vector” and is not limited to one particular trajectory, so it is the closest
direct compiler prior found.

The distinction is computational model and certification. Bassler’s stated
O(N^3) is the count of linear-algebra operations in a numerical reconstruction;
the method uses numerical rank selection and eigenvalue/linear-system steps.
The paper gives no bit-operation bound as a function of rational input length
and requested epsilon, no uniform condition-number or root/weight separation
bound, and no certified rounding analysis preserving positivity. The proposed
uniform-measure admixture is potentially the load-bearing step that supplies
an explicit interior margin independent of the promised state's proximity to
the SEP boundary. The sufficient quantitative moment/Jacobi bounds and
positive-output rounding still need proof; they are not established by this
literature audit.

3. A. Rutkowski, M. Banacki, M. Marciniak, “Necessary and sufficient condition
of separability for D-symmetric diagonal states,” Phys. Rev. A 99, 022309
(2019), arXiv:1810.05522, and J. Tura et al., “Separability of diagonal
symmetric states: a quadratic conic optimization problem,” Quantum 2, 45
(2018), arXiv:1706.09423. These are relevant criteria / moment and conic
reformulations. In the targeted passages checked, they do not supply a
rational bit-complexity, epsilon-certified decomposition compiler that
supersedes the more direct Bassler Appendix C reconstruction.

FINITE PURE-PHASE DETAIL

The moment reconstruction yields positive nodes and weights for a
phase-averaged coherent product. To express each phase average as a finite
mixture of iid pure products, use N+1 equally spaced phases: the Dicke
off-diagonal Fourier orders range from -N through N, and the N+1 roots of
unity cancel every nonzero order in that range. The displayed N-point sum in
Bassler Appendix A, Eq. (A9), appears off by one for a generic coherent
amplitude: Fourier order N survives an N-point average. This does not affect
the continuous phase-average moment identity or the compiler's corrected
N+1-point conversion; do not rely on that displayed count without checking
the typeset equation.

With at most O(N) quadrature nodes and N+1 phases per node, the explicit
pure-product output has O(N^2) terms. Yu's larger stated O(N^2) existence
bound already covers this scale. The uniform admixture changes the state by
at most 2 eta = epsilon/4 in trace norm, before adding any separately
controlled numerical quadrature/rounding error.

PRIORITY CONCLUSION

Safe wording: “Yu provides the finite SEP criterion and polynomial-size
decomposition existence; Bassler gives a direct O(N^3) numerical moment
reconstruction. We give a promised-input, epsilon-controlled bit-complexity
compiler by regularizing with a uniform coherent component and proving
quantitative precision/positivity bounds.” Do not say “first constructive
decomposition,” “first polynomial-time reconstruction” without specifying
the bit model, or “new SEP criterion.” The quantitative contribution remains
conditional on validating the claimed explicit eigenvalue/Jacobi precision
and weight-positivity bounds for all promised inputs.

FOLLOW-UP: BASSLER APPLICATION SCOPE AND THE FINITE-PHASE CHECK

Primary-source check of the full Bassler v3 manuscript (arXiv:2504.13646,
dated 26 March 2026), including Sections II–IV and Appendices A, C, D:

• The paper studies the Dicke-limit collective-emission Lindblad semigroup,
  restricted to the maximal-spin symmetric subspace, with density matrices
  diagonal in the Dicke basis. Section II formulates the trajectory from the
  fully excited state; Section IV's moment-cone argument is broader and shows
  that this semigroup maps every already-separable symmetric Dicke-diagonal
  state to a separable state. The proof checks the extremal coherent-product
  moment vectors and extends by convexity. This is a dynamical preservation
  theorem with SEP as the initial premise, not a static characterization of
  which Dicke-diagonal inputs are SEP.
• The manuscript does not specialize to or analyze
  p_k ∝ exp[-delta (k-N/2)^2/N], state a delta threshold (including
  2 log 2), or study the Gibbs operator
  exp[beta g J^2/N - beta u J_z^2/N + beta h J_z]. There is no anisotropy
  parameter or temperature-dependent separability region in its main theorem
  or appendices. Consequently it does not prove the required Gaussian-Dicke
  moment positivity, even in the maximum-spin limit.
• The thermal family at finite beta g also has support across multiple total
  spin-J sectors with multiplicities. Bassler's theorem is confined to the
  J=N/2 symmetric sector. Thus the model and Hilbert-space mismatch is stronger
  for the full finite-coupling thermal theorem. At most, Bassler gives a
  conditional consequence: if an initial state in its symmetric diagonal
  class is known independently to be SEP, its collective-decay trajectory
  stays SEP. It cannot supply the missing initial-state SEP gate or an
  all-temperature full-space result.

The finite-phase caveat is now checked against both the source TeX for v3 and
an exact N=2 matrix element. The TeX prints Appendix A, Eq. (A9), as
  (1/N) sum_{k=0}^{N-1} rho(epsilon,2 pi k/N)^{tensor N}.
For N=2,
  |psi(epsilon,phi)> = sqrt(1-epsilon)|g> + exp(i phi)sqrt(epsilon)|e>,
and
  <ee|rho(epsilon,phi)^{tensor 2}|gg>
    = epsilon(1-epsilon) exp(2 i phi).
The two displayed phases 0 and pi both give exp(2 i phi)=1, so the average
leaves this off-diagonal entry equal to epsilon(1-epsilon), nonzero for
0<epsilon<1. The continuous phase integral is correctly diagonal, but the
printed finite sum is not an exact implementation for generic epsilon. Three
phases 0, 2 pi/3, 4 pi/3 cancel this N=2 Fourier mode; in general N+1 equally
spaced phases cancel every nonzero Fourier order in [-N,N]. This confirms an
off-by-one error in Eq. (A9) as typeset in v3, rather than a parsing artifact.
It does not undermine the continuous phase-average moment criterion or the
Appendix C support reconstruction; it only requires N+1, not N, phase terms
when materializing a finite iid-pure-product decomposition.
