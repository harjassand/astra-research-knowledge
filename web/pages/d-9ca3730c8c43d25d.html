# Explicit bounded reflection-frame obstruction

Origin: b_compute found the signed positive-definite matrix scaling construction; team_b_quantum reconstructed it and added the explicit finite spectral-separation bound below. It is an exact existence/construction theorem. Minimizer approximations are computational illustrations, not the proof. No literature priority is claimed.

**Theorem.** For every d>=3, there exist a faithful diagonal state sigma and d real symmetric Hermitian involutions A_j, each ||A_j||=1, with ordinary commutant CI, but with a nonscalar common commutant after SLD dressing. The same construction supplies an irreducible frame of rank-one projectors with this failure.

Set R=(16d^2+1)^2 and p_i=R^(i-1)/sum_{k=0}^{d-1}R^k. Put sigma=diag(p_i) and C_ij=2sqrt(p_i p_j)/(p_i+p_j). For sign patterns s_0=(1,...,1) and s_j=s_0-2e_j, 1<=j<=d-1, define v_j as the unique minimizer, in its prescribed sign orthant, of

f(v)=v^T C v/2 - sum_i log|v_i|.

Put P_j=v_jv_j^T/||v_j||^2 and A_j=I-2P_j.

## Existence and exact shared blind direction

C is positive definite: C_ij=2sqrt(p_i p_j)int_0^infinity exp[-(p_i+p_j)t]dt is the Gram matrix of sqrt(2p_i)exp(-p_i t), which are linearly independent for distinct p_i. The Hessian of f is C+diag(1/v_i^2)>0. On each orthant f diverges at the boundary and at infinity, so it has exactly one minimizer. Its first-order equations are

v_i(Cv)_i=1 for every i.

Let w=(1,...,1)^T. The SLD filter is T_sigma(B)=C Hadamard B. Consequently

T_sigma(P_j)w=w/||v_j||^2,

T_sigma(A_j)w=(1-2/||v_j||^2)w.

Each dressed operator is Hermitian, so P_w=ww^T/d commutes with all of them. The dressed frame is reducible exactly, independent of approximations.

Signed SPD scaling existence/uniqueness is older mathematics: O'Leary, *Scaling symmetric positive definite matrices to prescribed row sums*, Linear Algebra Appl370(2003):185–191, DOI10.1016/S0024-3795(03)00387-2. The proof above reconstructs the required instance. The connection to SLD ambiguity is the candidate new witness, not the matrix-scaling theorem.

## Explicit irreducibility at finite R

Let epsilon=1/(4d^2). Since C_ii=1 and for i!=j, C_ij<=2R^(-|i-j|/2), the maximum offdiagonal row sum is <=4/(sqrt(R)-1)=epsilon. Hence ||C-I||_2<=epsilon and C>=(1-epsilon)I.

On orthant s write v=diag(s)u, u_i>0. The Hessian of f_s(u) is at least (1-epsilon)I, while at u=1,

||grad f_s(1)||=||diag(s)(C-I)s||<=epsilon sqrt(d).

Strong monotonicity of the gradient along the segment from 1 to its minimizer therefore gives

||v_s-s||<=delta=epsilon sqrt(d)/(1-epsilon).

Let V_0 have columns s_0,...,s_{d-1}, and V the corresponding v columns. V_0 is invertible. Its inverse sends y to c_j=(y_d-y_j)/2 for 1<=j<=d-1 and c_0=(sum_{j<d}y_j+(3-d)y_d)/2. Thus

||V_0^-1||_F^2=(d^2-3d+6)/4<=d^2/4,

sigma_min(V_0)>=2/d.

Meanwhile ||V-V_0||_2<=sqrt(d)delta=d epsilon/(1-epsilon)=d/(4d^2-1)<2/d. So V is invertible and the vectors v_j span C^d.

For every j>=1, s_0^T s_j=d-2. The perturbation of this overlap is at most

2sqrt(d)delta+delta^2=d[(1-epsilon)^(-2)-1]<1<=d-2 (d>=3).

Thus v_0^T v_j!=0 and the nonorthogonality graph is connected. A matrix M commuting with every P_j sends v_j to lambda_j v_j and has v_j^T M=lambda_j v_j^T. For any nonorthogonal pair, multiplication gives lambda_i=lambda_j. The graph is connected and the vectors span, hence M is scalar. Since A_j=I-2P_j, the reflection frame has the same ordinary commutant CI. This proof works for arbitrary complex M, not just Hermitian M.

## Exact distinct equal-score state

Take X=I+(P_w-I/d)=(1-1/d)I+P_w and rho=sqrt(sigma)Xsqrt(sigma). X>0, tr(sigma X)=1 because tr(sigma P_w)=1/d. Its diagonal equals p_i and its offdiagonal entries are sqrt(p_i p_j)/d, so rho!=sigma. The exact fiber theorem implies all SLD scores agree for both reflection and projector frames. Both states are faithful.

This excludes a general rescue based solely on bounded generators, projections, Hermitian involutions, ordinary-frame irreducibility or dimension-independent normalization. It does not exclude a rescue exploiting additional fixed tensor Pauli relations, equal rank eigenspaces, locality, prescribed Hamiltonian classes or quantitative high-temperature bounds. Here the eigenvalue ratios are very large; no claim violates the seed's high-temperature theorem.
