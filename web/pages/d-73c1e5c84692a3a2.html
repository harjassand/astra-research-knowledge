## A downstream question which does not require stable axes

Given an acquired or supplied exact rational candidate f0=hQ(L0 x), Q positive definite, and aligned quadratic g0=a^T(L0 x)^2, bound the global minimum of the actual energy P=f-g. The target f and g may depart from the hidden sign-symmetric class. Put V=L0^{-1}, z=L0 x, m=z^2 and J=11^T. Assume certified **pointwise** uncertainties
\[
 |f(Vz)-h_Q(z)|\le\delta\|z\|^4,\qquad
 |g(Vz)-a^Tm|\le\eta\|z\|^2,
\]
with rational delta,eta>=0. Coefficient Bombieri error bounds imply these inequalities, with coordinate factors ||V||op^4 and ||V||op^2 respectively. A candidate-fitting error must be added to the observation uncertainty before transformation. The candidate and its uncertainty metric are supplied to this downstream stage, so acquisition/fitting and coordinate conditioning remain charged. No proof below acquires the candidate from noisy input.

Define
\[
 S_-=Q-\delta J,\quad b_-=a+\eta1,\qquad
 S_+=Q+\delta J,\quad b_+=a-\eta1.
\]
Then **for every z**,
\[
 P_-(z):=m^TS_-m-b_-^Tm\le P(Vz)\le
 P_+(z):=m^TS_+m-b_+^Tm.
\]
The scalar controlling positivity is
\[
 \tau(Q)=\frac1{1^TQ^{-1}1}.
\]
If delta<tau(Q), both S_- and S_+ are positive definite. To verify the criterion, factor Q^{1/2}(I-delta vv^T)Q^{1/2} with v=Q^{-1/2}1; I-delta vv^T has eigenvalues1 and1-delta||v||^2. The rational threshold is checkable by solving Qw=1. It can remain well away from zero when lambda_min(Q) becomes small.

Consequently the two **strictly convex quadratic programs over m>=0** give
\[
 \boxed{\min_{m\ge0}(m^TS_-m-b_-^Tm)
 \le\min_x P(x)\le
 \min_{m\ge0}(m^TS_+m-b_+^Tm).}
\]
Surjectivity of z->z^2 onto the nonnegative orthant proves the minima equality at each endpoint. Coercivity of P_- ensures a finite attained actual minimum. The interval is sharp for the larger pointwise uncertainty class: taking f=hQ minus/plus delta||z||^4 and g=a^Tm plus/minus eta||z||^2 attains the respective endpoints. **It is not asserted sharp for a Bombieri coefficient ball.** A radial polynomial delta||z||^4 has Bombieri norm delta sqrt(n(n+2)/3), generally exceeding delta. Thus coefficient-to-pointwise conversion can be conservative and must not be conflated with attainment in the coefficient ball.

For either rational endpoint (S,b), solve its KKT system
\[
 m_*\ge0,\quad \mu=2Sm_*-b\ge0,\quad \mu_i m_{*,i}=0.
\]
The universal exact identity is
\[
 m^TSm-b^Tm-P_*=(m-m_*)^TS(m-m_*)+\mu^Tm.
\]
A rational LDL factorization of S turns the first term into at most n positive rational weights times squares of quadratic polynomials; the second has at most n positive rational weights times z_i^2. This is an exact degree-four weighted SOS certificate, transported to x by z=L0 x. In particular, the ordinary order-two SOS relaxation on the raw coefficients attains the same endpoint objective: the displayed witness gives the SOS lower bound, and an actual minimizing point gives the opposite inequality. This is a competent equally informed comparator, not a presumed numerical solver failure. The witness is available to both methods; no runtime advantage is established. Our executable uses support enumeration only for small exact fixtures, not as a scalable quadratic-programming algorithm.

These bounds control global energy values. An off-class perturbation can introduce or remove local minima, and the certificate does not promise preservation of the all-local-minima-global property from the exact aligned class.

## Separation of energy and axis conditioning on a source-relevant family

Take Q=uJ+vI with v>0 and v+nu>0, and a=a0 1. For s=sum m_i, the identity
\[
 m^TQm=v\sum_i(m_i-s/n)^2+(u+v/n)s^2
\]
reduces both endpoint programs to one scalar s>=0. Therefore
\[
 P_-^*=-\frac{(a0+\eta)_+^2}{4(u-\delta+v/n)},\qquad
 P_+^*=-\frac{(a0-\eta)_+^2}{4(u+\delta+v/n)}.
\]
Here tau(Q)=u+v/n. With u=1 and v=epsilon tending to0, the certified energy interval has a finite limit, with width controlled by delta and eta. The matched-axis tangent gap is2epsilon and axes need not be continuously recoverable at fixed absolute noise. The two statements concern different observables and different condition parameters. This gives a useful uncertainty set for energy even if a noisy method must return ambiguous axes. The algebra is elementary convex optimization and is not claimed as a new mean-field solution.

## Actual published variable-dimensional statistical class

Mesters and Zwiernik, *Non-Independent Components Analysis*, arXiv2206.13668v4, revised2024-03-19, supplies the model AY=epsilon=τ η in §2.1, Eq(2), PDFpp3–4. A is an unknown invertible unmixing matrix; τ is a positive random scalar independent of η, and the entries remain uncorrelated but generally dependent. The paper develops reflectionally invariant higher-moment/cumulant restrictions, identification under an explicit generic contraction-profile condition, and minimum-distance/GMM estimation using covariance plus higher-order tensors (§5.2, §6; Theorem5.10, Theorem5.14, Corollary5.15, Proposition6.2 and6.3). Retained Crossref metadata verifies the formal version, *Non-independent component analysis*, Annals of Statistics52(6), December2024, DOI10.1214/24-AOS2373. It is an older prerequisite. Direct inspection of the retained BSE listing says September2022; its separately retained legacy PDF cover says September2022, revisedOctober2023. Search-date impressions and a site modification timestamp were rejected as evidence of new2026 mathematics.

Our own specialization, with verified source assumptions, is:

* η_i independent, symmetric, mean0, variance1, excess kurtoses kappa_i>0;
* Eτ^2=1, Eτ^4=c>1, with finite fourth moments;
* if the original exact coefficient interface is desired, restrict the calibration A and the moment parameters to rational numbers. This is our restriction, not the paper's.

For t in R^n, independence and symmetry give
\[
 E(t^T\eta)^4=3\|t\|^4+\sum_i\kappa_i t_i^4.
\]
Since Var(t^T epsilon)=||t||^2,
\[
 \operatorname{cum}_4(t^T\epsilon)
 =3(c-1)\|t\|^4+c\sum_i\kappa_i t_i^4.
\]
Thus the latent fourth-cumulant quartic is QPD with
\[
 Q=3(c-1)J+c\operatorname{diag}(\kappa_i)\succ0.
\]
For observed Y=A^{-1}epsilon its expanded cumulant polynomial is hQ(A^{-T}t). This is the hidden quartic interface in arbitrary variable dimension. The inverse-frame columns are columns of A^T, hence the **directions of the rows of the unmixing matrix A**. Recovery of their signs, ordering and scales still requires the ordinary matching/normalization conventions.

Crucially,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y)=A^{-1}A^{-T}=L^TL,\qquad L=A^{-T}.
\]
The source estimator receives/estimates covariance from the same observations, so covariance supplies the aligned positive metric for the quartic's argument t. An equally informed comparator can whiten. It would be unfair to count coefficient-only metric acquisition as a gain while withholding covariance from this comparator. Equation(17) of the source explicitly includes both covariance and the odd-index tensor restrictions; Eq(20) globally minimizes a weighted sample criterion over a supplied compact parameter set. Its consistency conditions are stronger than merely having small quartic residual, and its asymptotic normality formula contains the derivative G through (G^T W G)^{-1}.

Equal positive kappa_i=kappa is a particularly useful stress case. The contraction profiles in the paper's condition(14) are equal, so Theorem5.10's sufficient hypothesis does **not** cover this case. It is nonetheless QPD and falls under the existing exact QPD theorem and the local derivative calculation here. This is a comparison of theorem hypotheses, not a claim that the established minimum-distance estimator fails or that the case is historically new. In fact the stronger equally informed eigenmatrix control below recovers this case too. As kappa->0, the fourth cumulant tends to a positive radial quartic while the axis tangent gap is2c kappa (in an orthonormal latent gauge). The shared covariance remains I after whitening, yet the limiting tensor has continuous rotational symmetry. Our midpoint obstruction therefore expresses a real weak-identification mechanism in a published dependent-components model, rather than only an arbitrary planted quartic.

An explicit finite-moment realization makes this interface independently checkable. Let τ^2 equal1/2 or3/2 with probability1/2 each; then Eτ^2=1,c=5/4. Let each η_i independently have mass1-1/s at0 and mass1/(2s) at each of +/-sqrt(s), with s=3+kappa. Then Varη_i=1 and Eη_i^4=s. All assumptions above hold, and Q=(3/4)J+(5kappa/4)I. After multiplying the cumulant polynomial by4/3 this becomes J+(5kappa/3)I. Choosing kappa=3epsilon/5 gives exactly the near-isotropic obstruction family. These distributions and numeric choices are our instantiation of the published model, not a published numerical experiment.

For clarity, a possible observation-error route is also elementary but does not solve branch acquisition. If i.i.d. mean-zero Y obeys a **supplied bound** ||Y||<=M, let empirical raw tensors be Mhat4 and Chat2, and define Chat4=Mhat4-3 sym(Chat2 tensor Chat2). Allocating failure probability alpha/2 to each Hilbert-space second-moment bound gives, with probability>=1-alpha,
\[
 \|Mhat4-M4\|_F\le M^4\sqrt{2/(N\alpha)},\quad
 \|Chat2-C2\|_F\le M^2\sqrt{2/(N\alpha)},\quad
 \|Chat4-C4\|_F\le7M^4\sqrt{2/(N\alpha)}.
\]
The proof is E||sample-average minus mean||F^2<=M^{2d}/N and Markov's inequality; symmetrization is an orthogonal contraction, and ||Chat2||F,||C2||F<=M^2. The zero population mean is assumed known in this raw-tensor formula; centering estimated means requires additional error terms. For the finite-support realization M itself depends on the calibration and dimension, e.g. M<=||A^{-1}||op sqrt((3/2)n(3+kappa)). These deliberately conservative bounds charge sample count, confidence, support and calibration; they do not give an unproved efficient recovery rate. The same tensor/covariance error information must be provided to the established estimator.

## A stronger matched eigenmatrix control, including equal kurtoses

The declared common-variance subclass is f(t)=alpha(t^TGt)^2+sum beta_i ell_i(t)^4, alpha>0,beta_i>0, with supplied covariance G=L^TL and rows ell_i of L. A competent comparator receives this subclass and G as well as the expanded coefficients. Let T be the symmetric coefficient tensor, H=G^{-1}, and define an operator on symmetric forms by
\[
 \mathcal T_G(M)_{ij}=\sum_{k,l}T_{ijkl}(HMH)_{kl}.
\]
It is self-adjoint in the inner product <M,N>_G=tr(HMHN). The conjugacy M=L^TDL sends it to the latent operator
\[
 \mathcal T_I(D)=\frac{\alpha}{3}(2D+\operatorname{tr}(D)I)
                 +\operatorname{diag}(\beta_i D_{ii}).
\]
This identity follows directly from the radial fourth tensor alpha(delta_ij delta_kl+delta_ik delta_jl+delta_il delta_jk)/3 and the axis tensors beta_i e_i^{tensor4}. Off-diagonal symmetric matrices have eigenvalue2alpha/3. On diagonal matrices the restriction is2alpha I/3+diag(beta_i)+alpha J/3, strictly greater than2alpha/3. Thus the bottom eigenvalue identifies alpha, and the **top n-dimensional eigenmatrix subspace is exactly the space of latent diagonal forms**, even if every beta_i coincides. Its separation from the lower subspace is at least min beta_i, with equality for equal beta when n>=2. This is another explicit location of the unavoidable anisotropy gap; it is not removed by a free covariance metric.

Equivalently, the image of mathcalT_G-(2alpha/3)Id is this subspace. Subtracting the full radial tensor instead gives a positive rank-n orthogonally decomposable flattening with the same image. For any basis M_1,...,M_n of that image, K_j=HM_j=L^{-1}D_jL commute and are G-self-adjoint. Their joint eigenprofiles are distinct because the image is the full diagonal algebra. A generic linear combination, or the explicit finite moment-curve search in the code, acquires the axis vectors by ordinary matrix diagonalization. The moment-curve weights(1,t,...,t^{n-1}) exclude at most(n-1) integers per pair of joint profiles, so testing t=0,...,n(n-1)^2/2 suffices. The stored rational n4 fixture uses t=2; the callable receives only the coefficient list and covariance, and checks the recovered even polynomial, SPD Q, reflections and metric identities. It does not read the latent Q or calibration from the producer data.

This is a reconstructed application of classical eigenmatrix/orthogonal-tensor methods, not a claimed new estimator. Cardoso–Souloumiac's *Blind beamforming for non-Gaussian signals*, corrected author reprint postedJanuary1997 of the December1993 article, §3.4–4.1, uses the n most significant cumulant eigenmatrices after covariance whitening and joint diagonalization (Propositions3–4, Eq(22)). Its stated identification theorem assumes independent sources; the top-subspace identity above is our exact derivation for this dependent positive-common-variance subclass, not an assertion that their independence theorem already states it. The primary author-uploaded reprint excerpt was read on ResearchGate after the former author site and publisher access failed. Boralevi–Draisma–Horobet–Robeva, arXiv1512.08031v1(2015-12-25), Proposition7, PDFp5, proves odeco uniqueness and its proof provides a matrix-SVD acquisition method even when component norms coincide; Proposition14, PDFpp9–10, uses commuting self-adjoint multiplication operators. He–Kressner's current exact matrix-family theorem is another competent diagonalization primitive. This prior makes an improvement claim from equal-kurtosis hypothesis coverage alone untenable.

The control's complete input costs are estimating/acquiring G and the full fourth cumulant, representing a symmetric operator of size n(n+1)/2, eigen/subspace extraction, then matrix-family diagonalization. Rational inversion and ordinary coefficient/tensor conversions are executed exactly for the retained n4 fixture. The implementation checks that its middle spectrum is rational; it is not a complete algebraic-root implementation for arbitrary unequal-kurtosis inputs. The analytic subspace identity above does not require that implementation guard. Arbitrary-n numerical runtime, sample error, covariance whitening errors, eigenprofile gaps and rational output heights are not benchmarked away. Neither this control nor the local certificate solves global noisy fitting for general QPD quartics. An exact-instance timing is not a statistical or practical performance advantage.

## Actual published physical polynomial, with its supplied metric charged

Carmona, Pelissetto and Vicari, *The N-component Ginzburg–Landau Hamiltonian with cubic anisotropy: a six-loop study*, arXivcond-mat/9912115v2 (2000-02-16), Eq(1.1), PDFpp2–3, has quartic local potential
\[
 \frac1{24}\sum_{i,j}(u0+v0\delta_{ij})\phi_i^2\phi_j^2,
\]
and quadratic term (r0/2)sum phi_i^2. Its arbitrary-component quartic is our Q=(u0 J+v0 I)/24 in the **restricted regime** v0>0 and v0+n u0>0. The broader stable physical model need not satisfy this stronger QPD promise. Its supplied gradient term fixes an identity kinetic metric and its component coordinates are explicit. An unknown rational measurement calibration would be our extension, with the transformed kinetic metric supplied to both comparators; it is not in the cited paper. The preceding envelope bounds the local potential's minimum under specified coefficient/quadratic errors. It does not calculate critical exponents, solve the renormalization-group problem, or solve a spatial PDE/domain-minimization problem. The physical anisotropy-vs-radial distinction is source-real, but the local-potential minimum and SOS witness are classical.

## Recent literature and nearest equally informed joint-structure control

He and Kressner, *A randomized algorithm for simultaneously diagonalizing symmetric matrices by congruence*, retained arXiv2402.16557v3 (2025-04-28), Algorithm1 and Theorems11,14,15, gives a strong comparator once an SDC matrix family is actually available. Its exact success probability is1; its perturbation results control the family off-diagonal residual normalized by ||Xhat||op^2, with O(epsilon^2) remainders. Theorem15 assumes a **PD family**: every strictly positive linear combination is SPD, equivalently all members are PSD and their common kernel is zero (Definition3). This is stronger than one SPD combination but does not require every individual member to be SPD. Lemma16 additionally bounds the congruence matrix's condition number; Theorem11 contains the generalized-eigenprofile gap through Dmax, Eq(4.10). The matrix family and regularity are inputs. Its acquisition from quartic coefficients is a separate cost.

The journal version, DOI10.1002/nla.70121, first published2026-09-27, is now verified directly on Wiley and by retained Crossref metadata. Initial raw publisher retrieval failed403; a later web-tool normalized excerpt of §3–4 was retained. The journal statements differ from v3: Theorem9 uses D_vec(mu)=max_{i!=j}||lambda_i-lambda_j||/|mu^T(lambda_i-lambda_j)|, Eq(4.3); Theorem12 gives an asymptotic normalized residual bound(1+R)epsilon+O(epsilon^2) with failure probability at most3d m(m-1)/(sqrt(2pi)R), under the same PD-family definition and m>=2 distinct joint profiles. Theorems7 and11 provide exact recovery and the regular-family asymptotic bound. These results concern supplied matrix families and sufficiently small perturbations, not a finite quartic branch certificate or unique-axis recovery at coincident profiles. Our record preserves version differences and does not infer an axis guarantee from a residual guarantee.

Jung and Kim, *Constrained weak identification of Allen–Cahn free energies with surface-tension calibration*, arXiv2609.18403v1 (2026-09-16), is a verified recent source. Proposition2 and Theorem1, PDFpp5–7, distinguish trajectory-acquired effective force from an **independently supplied surface tension** fixing its scale. Their finite perturbation bound explicitly requires a nonzero integral margin for inversion; it remains valid at vanishing pure-phase potentials. This is useful recent precedent for charging an external physical datum and stating the inverse conditioning parameter. Its scalar phase-field interface does not supply a variable-n hidden quartic result or a baseline for axis acquisition, so no such implication is drawn. Only the identified theoretical sections, introduction and methodological assumptions were read; the paper's empirical results are author claims, not independently reproduced here.

## Consequence, limitations and next falsifiable step

The substantive outcome is a precise finite local inverse certificate, an unavoidable anisotropy-dependent obstruction even with a perfect supplied metric, and a robust downstream energy interval which uses a different radial margin. The published common-variance model meets the quartic promise in a transparent parameter regime and makes the conditioning distinction consequential for latent component interpretation. This improves the research interface; it is not a new estimator or foundational breakthrough.

Next falsifiable work is to test a real globally certified fitter against the midpoint ambiguity and provide either a charged branch/exclusion certificate or an explicit ambiguity set. Any claimed new practical gain should be measured against covariance-informed reflectional minimum-distance estimation and the identical order-two SOS certificate, with candidate/metric acquisition and error propagation included. Historical priority for the local QPD gap/finite bound remains unassessed beyond this primary-source map.
