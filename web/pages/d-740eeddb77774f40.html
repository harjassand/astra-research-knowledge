1. Target selected after frontier check

The candidate mechanism is exact Fourier decoupling of a stationary block-circulant operator. It replaces entry independence by a verifiable symmetry: translation invariance makes the finite-group Fourier transform a direct-sum decomposition, and a proper Gaussian coefficient field makes distinct Fourier blocks independent. The blocks have fixed matrix size p>=2, so they can be nonnormal; the full np by np matrix has only n p^2 Gaussian degrees of freedom and repeats each coefficient block n times. The coefficient covariance across spatial shifts may have arbitrary range.

This is a structural Poisson mechanism, not Ginibre repulsion. Independent Fourier blocks contribute rare, asymptotically single eigenvalue hits at the microscopic scale. The result is a local Poisson process despite strong entry dependence and extensive nonnormality. It also gives an exact pseudospectrum representation. It does not establish universality for arbitrary dependent non-Hermitian matrices.

2. Model and exact representation

Let G_n=Z/nZ and let p be fixed. For each n, take independent proper complex Gaussian p by p matrices B_{k,n}, k in G_n, with

    vec(B_{k,n}) ~ CN(m_{k,n}, Sigma_{k,n}).

Assume uniformly in k,n that ||m_{k,n}||<=M and c I <= Sigma_{k,n} <= C I for fixed 0<c<C<infinity. Define matrix coefficients and the block-circulant operator C_n on C^n tensor C^p by

    A_{r,n} = (1/n) sum_{k=0}^{n-1} exp(2 pi i k r/n) B_{k,n},
    (C_n x)_r = sum_{s=0}^{n-1} A_{r-s,n} x_s,

with indices modulo n. If F_n is the unitary discrete Fourier transform, then

    (F_n tensor I_p) C_n (F_n tensor I_p)^* = direct_sum_{k=0}^{n-1} B_{k,n}.

Thus spec(C_n) is the multiset union of the eigenvalues of the B_{k,n}. The centered coefficients A_{r,n}-E A_{r,n} form a stationary proper Gaussian p by p field on the cyclic group, with

    E[vec(A_{r,n}-E A_{r,n}) vec(A_{s,n}-E A_{s,n})^*]
      = (1/n^2) sum_k exp(2 pi i k(r-s)/n) Sigma_{k,n}.

No decay or finite-range condition is imposed on that covariance. For example, any uniformly elliptic frequency covariance profile Sigma_{k,n} gives a valid stationary centered field after inverse Fourier transform. The deterministic mean profile m_{k,n} is simply a deterministic block-circulant convolution term. Each random block coefficient is reused in n locations of C_n, so matrix entries are strongly dependent even in the special case where different centered A_r are independent.

3. Candidate theorem: microscopic Poisson statistics

For a parameter theta=(m,Sigma) in the compact set specified above, let q_theta(z) be the one-point intensity density of the eigenvalues of a p by p matrix with law CN(m,Sigma); q counts eigenvalues, rather than normalizing by p. Suppose the empirical parameter measures

    nu_n = (1/n) sum_k delta_(m_{k,n},Sigma_{k,n})

converge weakly to nu. Define the limiting mean empirical spectral density

    rho(z) = (1/p) integral q_theta(z) nu(d theta).

For any z_0 with rho(z_0)>0, set N=np and

    Xi_n = sum_{lambda in spec(C_n)} delta_{sqrt(N rho(z_0)) (lambda-z_0)}.

Claim. Xi_n converges in distribution, in the vague topology on locally finite point measures on C, to the homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity one with respect to planar Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

Step 1: uniform one- and two-point bounds for one block. The density of vec(B_{k,n}) is bounded above by

    K exp(-a ||B||_F^2)

for constants K,a>0 independent of k,n; this follows from the uniform covariance bounds and bounded means. For a complex p by p matrix, Schur triangularization B=U T U^* has diagonal entries lambda_1,...,lambda_p and strict upper entries t_ij. Its real Jacobian contains the factor |Delta(lambda)|^2, where Delta(lambda)=product_{i<j}(lambda_i-lambda_j). The Frobenius norm satisfies ||T||_F^2=sum_i |lambda_i|^2+sum_{i<j}|t_ij|^2. Integrating the Gaussian upper bound over the strict upper entries and the compact unitary factor therefore bounds the unordered joint eigenvalue density by

    K_p exp(-a_p sum_i |lambda_i|^2) |Delta(lambda)|^2.

The constants depend only on p,c,C. Integrating out p-1 or p-2 coordinates shows that the first and second factorial correlation densities of every block are uniformly bounded on compact subsets of C and C^2. In particular, for every compact K there is C_K such that, for every measurable D subset K,

    E Z_{k,n}(D) <= C_K |D|,
    E [ Z_{k,n}(D) (Z_{k,n}(D)-1) ] <= C_K |D|^2,

where Z_{k,n}(D) counts eigenvalues of B_{k,n} in D and |D| is planar area. These estimates do not assume normality or independent entries inside a block.

Step 2: each block makes a rare, almost-single contribution. Let K be compact in the rescaled plane and D_n=z_0+(N rho(z_0))^(-1/2)K. Since N=np and p is fixed, |D_n|=O(1/n). The preceding estimates give uniformly in k

    E Z_{k,n}(D_n)=O(1/n),
    E [Z_{k,n}(D_n)(Z_{k,n}(D_n)-1)]=O(1/n^2).

Summing the second estimate over n modes gives O(1/n), so the probability of any mode contributing two or more points to a fixed rescaled compact set tends to zero.

Step 3: Laplace functional. Fix f>=0 continuous with compact support, and let xi_{k,n} be the rescaled eigenvalue point measure from block k. The measures xi_{k,n} are independent because the B_{k,n} are independent. The pair estimate implies

    E[1-exp(-<f,xi_{k,n}>)]
      = integral (1-exp(-f(sqrt(N rho_0)(z-z_0)))) q_{k,n}(z) dA(z)
        + O(n^-2),

uniformly in k, where q_{k,n}=q_(m_{k,n},Sigma_{k,n}) and rho_0=rho(z_0). Uniform covariance bounds and bounded means imply continuous dependence of q_theta(z) on (theta,z), with locally uniform domination. Weak convergence nu_n=>nu then gives locally uniform convergence of (np)^(-1) sum_k q_{k,n}(z) to rho(z). Independence, the change of variables w=sqrt(N rho_0)(z-z_0), and rho(z_0+w/sqrt(N rho_0))=>rho_0 uniformly for w in a fixed compact set yield

    E exp(-<f,Xi_n>) -> exp(- integral_C (1-exp(-f(w))) dA(w)).

This is the Laplace functional of unit-intensity planar Poisson measure, proving the claim. The microscopic scale is exactly N^(-1/2) up to the local density factor. No local law is used in this proof.

4. Exact nonnormality and robust spectral prediction

For p>=2, full support of each Gaussian block implies P([B_{k,n},B_{k,n}^*]=0)=0. Moreover, the Gaussian parameter bounds give uniform moments of Y_{k,n}=||[B_{k,n},B_{k,n}^*]||_F^2. Independence and a triangular-array variance bound show

    (1/N)||[C_n,C_n^*]||_F^2
      - (1/(np)) sum_k E Y_{k,n} -> 0 in probability.

If nu_n=>nu, continuity and uniform integrability give convergence in probability to p^(-1) integral E_theta ||[B,B^*]||_F^2 nu(d theta), which is strictly positive for p>=2 because a nondegenerate complex Gaussian matrix is nonnormal with probability one. Thus the theorem includes operators with an extensive Frobenius nonnormality density; it is not a normal-matrix example disguised by coordinates.

An explicit family makes this density arbitrarily large. Take p=2, Sigma_{k,n}=I_4, and m_{k,n}=L J for every k,n, where J=e_1 e_2^* and L>0 is fixed while n tends to infinity. Then B_{k,n}=LJ+G_{k,n} with standard complex Gaussian G. The Schur eigenvalue density gives q_(LJ,I_4)(0)>0, so z_0=0 is a valid bulk point. Since [J,J^*]=diag(1,-1),

    L^(-4) E ||[LJ+G,(LJ+G)^*]||_F^2 -> 2 as L -> infinity.

For each fixed L the Poisson theorem applies, and its normalized commutator limit grows like L^4 as L is increased. Thus the proved class includes arbitrarily large fixed nonnormality, although this argument does not cover an L=L_n that diverges with dimension.

For every epsilon>=0, the Euclidean epsilon-pseudospectrum also has an exact block formula:

    sigma_epsilon(C_n)
      = union_k { z : s_min(z I_p-B_{k,n}) <= epsilon }.

Indeed, unitary block diagonalization makes s_min(zI-C_n)=min_k s_min(zI_p-B_{k,n}). This gives an exact prediction interface: Fourier-transform the stationary coefficients, compute p by p singular values, and take the union. It accounts for nonnormality through singular values rather than inferring stability from eigenvalues alone.

5. Falsifier to stable extension beyond exact stationarity

Finite-rank or small-norm boundary changes do not preserve spectra for highly nonnormal operators. Let S_n e_j=e_(j+1) for j<n and S_n e_n=0, and define

    T_n = 2 S_n + 2^(-(n-1)) e_1 e_n^*.

Then T_n^n=I, so spec(T_n) is the full set of n-th roots of unity, whereas 2S_n is nilpotent and has spectrum {0}. Yet T_n-2S_n has rank one and norm 2^(-(n-1)) -> 0. In fact, T_n=D_n U_n D_n^(-1), where U_n is the cyclic shift and D_n=diag(1,2,...,2^(n-1)); the eigenvector condition number is 2^(n-1). The same failure is visible in singular values: (I-2S_n)^(-1)=sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(2S_n)^j, so ||(I-2S_n)^(-1)e_1||=(sum_{j=0}^{n-1}4^j)^(1/2) and s_min(I-2S_n) is exponentially small, while s_min(I-T_n)=0 because 1 is an eigenvalue. This is the exact obstruction to extending the Fourier proof perturbatively to open-boundary Toeplitz operators: the issue is pseudospectral instability. Entrywise covariance or rank of a boundary defect alone cannot repair it.

6. Exact remaining inequality for a broader innovation class

The Gaussian theorem obtains independent modes exactly. If the stationary spatial innovations are non-Gaussian, their Fourier modes are not independent; a fixed-dimensional central limit theorem is insufficient at the rare-event scale O(1/n). A natural sufficient missing bridge for the same bulk Poisson conclusion is a summed rare-event cumulant estimate. For compact planar sets A_1,...,A_r with regular boundary, put D_n(A_i)=z_0+(N rho_0)^(-1/2)A_i and let Z_{k,n}(A_i) count eigenvalues in D_n(A_i) for the k-th Fourier block. Along with uniform one- and two-point bounds within a block, one would need, under explicit assumptions on the innovation law, for every fixed r>=2,

    sum_{k_1,...,k_r all distinct}
      | cum(Z_{k_1,n}(A_1),...,Z_{k_r,n}(A_r)) | -> 0,

along with the one-mode intensity asymptotic

    sum_k E Z_{k,n}(A) -> |A|.

The exact Fourier representation does not prove these bounds for non-Gaussian inputs. The r=2 case is covariance decoupling, but it is not enough by itself: higher-order dependence can survive with zero pairwise covariances. Finite-dimensional CLT errors do not suffice after summing over mode tuples. This is the finite unresolved gate; no non-Gaussian universality claim is made.

7. Primary-source comparison and scope

Sources checked 2026-10-09; these are primary paper records, not validation of the derivation above.

* Alt--Kruger, “Inhomogeneous Circular Law for Correlated Matrices,” arXiv:2005.13533v2 (24 Feb 2021), abstract and scope. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13533 . They treat general decaying entry correlations and derive a deterministic Brown-measure density plus local empirical-density convergence just above the typical spacing. This is a stronger general-purpose density result, but it does not state a microscopic point-process limit for the nondecaying block-circulant dependence used here.
* Tao--Vu, “Random matrices: Universality of local spectral statistics of non-Hermitian matrices,” arXiv:1206.1893v7 (25 May 2024), abstract and introduction. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1893 . Their bulk Ginibre point-process universality assumes jointly independent entries and matches four moments; they emphasize non-Hermitian spectral instability and log-determinant control. The present candidate has strong dependence and Poisson rather than Ginibre statistics.
* Osman, “Bulk Universality for Sparse Complex non-Hermitian Random Matrices,” arXiv:2508.03631v1 (5 Aug 2025), abstract. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03631 . The reported sparse bulk universality theorem assumes independent entries and uses multi-resolvent local laws. It does not cover repeated block-circulant entries.
* Bose--Hazra--Saha, “Poisson Convergence of Eigenvalues of Circulant Type Matrices,” Extremes 14 (2011), 365--392, primary author manuscript: https://repository.ias.ac.in/5488/1/327.pdf . The abstract gives Poisson convergence for extreme-eigenvalue point processes of scalar circulant, symmetric circulant, reverse-circulant and k-circulant models, with a partial two-sided moving-average extension. This is close structural precedent, but its displayed point process is an extreme process, not the bulk microscopic point process proved here, and its matrices are scalar structured examples rather than fixed-size nonnormal block symbols. This comparison does not establish novelty or priority.
