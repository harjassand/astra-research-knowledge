## Ji et al. (2026), intentional Mg–La inoculant

Xiao Ji et al., “Void suppressive lithium anodes for all-solid-state batteries,” *Nature Materials* (2026), [doi:10.1038/s41563-026-02729-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02729-w).

- Abstract: explicitly defines void-suppression capability (VSC) using full-stripping capacity times applied current density; reports LiMgLa performance and says **phase-field modelling** predicts overpotential above a critical electrolyte overpotential beyond 70% of model full-depletion capacity. The 70% statement is not a universal experimental law.
- Supplementary Note 1, Eq. (1) and Eq. (3), local PDF p. 3: \(\tau^{-1/2}=2i/[\sqrt{\pi D_{eff}}c_0F]\), \(Q_{max}=i\tau\), \(VSC=iQ_{max}=i^2\tau=\pi D_{eff}c_0^2F^2/4\). Direct render inspection verified the radical covers \(\pi D_{eff}\), not \(D_{eff}^{-1}\) as in an earlier internal transcription. There is no source equation defect.
- Supplementary Table 3, PDF p. 33: reports Li, LiMg, LiMgLa \((D_{eff},c_0,VSC)\) as \((5.6\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{cm^2/s},7.64\times10^4\,\mathrm{mol/m^3},2.39\times10^5)\), \((3.4\times10^{-11},7.45\times10^4,1.42\times10^5)\), and \((3.5\times10^{-10},7.45\times10^4,1.46\times10^6)\) in the respective units.
- Publisher Source Data Fig. 2 workbook, sheet `Fig2f`: eight raw LiMgLa and three raw LiMg depletion points plus source-labeled affine fit values. Since Sand's equation is through-origin under the stated assumptions, preserve the published nonzero-intercept fitted curve as an empirical choice; a free-intercept slope should not silently be called the exact Sand coefficient.
- Supplementary Note 6 and Fig. 4 source data: phase-field domain 200×80 μm, minimum grid spacing 4 μm; the LLZTO pellet physics are not solved. Eq. (15) normalizes modeled local current by the shrinking effective contact area, \(i_{app,loc}=I_{app}\xi S_0/S(t)\). Fig. 4f source data are model outputs, not directly measured local currents. As contact tends to zero this normalization becomes singular.
- Supplementary Note 8, PDF pp. 12–13: its cycling-VSC scaling first assumes ideal contact (70%); rough-interface adjustment gives 53%; with fatigue and continued SEI growth the authors estimate about 30%. Their cycling demonstrations are at 0.25 VSC/1000 cycles, 0.32/200 cycles, 0.5/30 cycles, plus a 0.5 VSC/125-cycle stepwise-current test.
- Supplementary Note 3 explicitly says grain-size dependence of creep/plasticity/fatigue needs further quantitative measurement. Supplementary Fig. 13 maps Mg/La in a LiMgLa|LLZTO interface after 2 mAh cm−2 and after fully stripping. It does not quantify particle size/fraction, electrical or ionic conductivity, or the post-strip transport mechanism. Supplementary Note 4's microstructure-retention account is based on liquid-electrolyte cycling; do not transfer that mechanism to LLZTO without testing.

## Vema et al. (2026), impurity-particle contact loss

Sundeep Vema et al., “Contact loss in all-solid-state Li-ion batteries via deposition of impurities,” *Joule* 10 (2026), [doi:10.1016/j.joule.2026.102539](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2026.102539). [ScienceDirect primary record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2542435126002230).

- The study investigates high-purity (manufacturer specified ~99.9%) Li stripping against LLZO with stack pressure 5–15 MPa, three-electrode voltage/resistance measurements, PEIS, and cryo-PFIB/SEM/EDS. It reports oxygen-rich deposited impurity layers, pores in those layers, resistance recovery over strip-rest, and rapid return to the previous elevated resistance upon restarting. The measured rest-decay constants are about 5–15 min; the authors attribute persistent memory to deposited particles remaining in place while Li slowly creeps through pores.
- Their Supplementary Eq. (S18) defines the critical inter-particle spacing from a Li creep/stripping balance. Eq. (S19) gives the ideal no-initial-particle monolayer thickness \(H=4a^3/(3f\ell_{cr}^2)\). Eq. (S25) includes initial interface resistance/particles and gives the corresponding \(C_{cr}\). Eq. (S25) is an idealized uniform-particle relation; the article explicitly reports patchy/nonuniform deposition and the uniform model misses the gradual precritical voltage increase.
- Limitation for transfer: Vema's material is nominally pure Li and LLZO; Ji's is intentional Mg/La inoculant in Li and LLZTO. The particle chemistry, size/fraction, ionic/electronic conductivity, creep law, and pressure/cycling protocols need new measurement. Vema explicitly notes that cycling effects remain to be studied.

## Operando competing mechanism

Lihong Zhao et al., “Imaging the evolution of lithium-solid electrolyte interface using operando scanning electron microscopy,” *Nature Communications* 16 (2025) 4283, [doi:10.1038/s41467-025-59567-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59567-8).

Their operando microscopy compares Li and Li–Mg at lower pressure and reports void coalescence/delamination in Li versus void splitting/collapse and better contact recovery in Li–Mg; they interpret increased overpotential as loss of contact from voiding, rather than Mg accumulation. This is a strong material control and warns that generic Mg-at-interface reasoning is already disputed. It does not test La-bearing inoculant particles in the Ji LLZTO setup.

## Prior art that blocks a generic local-current novelty claim

- S. Narayan and L. Anand, “A phase field electro-chemo-mechanical formulation for predicting void evolution at the Li–electrolyte interface in all-solid-state batteries,” *Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids* 167 (2022) 104999, [doi:10.1016/j.jmps.2022.104999](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2022.104999). It couples Li diffusion, void mechanics, electrolyte electrostatics and current hotspots at void edges.
- “Chemo-electro-mechanical phase-field simulation of interfacial nanodefects and nanovoids in solid-state batteries,” *Communications Materials* (2024), [doi:10.1038/s43246-024-00600-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43246-024-00600-6), models geometry, pressure, defects and local current at Li/LLZO interfaces.
- Muhammad Haseeb Rasool and Hosop Shin, “Time- and Geometry-Driven Void Dynamics at the Lithium-Solid Electrolyte Interface Revealed by Phase-Field Modeling,” *ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces* 18 (2026) 43870–43883, [doi:10.1021/acsami.6c06437](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsami.6c06437). It reports in a fully coupled phase-field model that void evolution depends on time at fixed capacity, while geometry/roughness/defect position govern current amplification; pressure is not uniformly beneficial. This directly preempts any novelty claim for the generic idea that shrinking contact amplifies current or that a single area fraction predicts stability.
- Luhan Ye et al., “Fast cycling of lithium metal in solid-state batteries by constriction-susceptible anode materials,” *Nature Materials* 23 (2024) 244–251, [doi:10.1038/s41563-023-01722-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-023-01722-x), already treats dynamic interface constriction susceptibility as a design strategy.
