## A precise general obstruction to a shortcut

For every d>=1 consider the affine rational system

    x1-2=0,
    xi-x_(i-1)^2=0, i=2,...,d.

Its original polynomials have degree at most2 and coefficients of absolute value at most2. Repeated substitution identifies the quotient with QQ, and its unique point is

    xi=2^(2^(i-1)).

The ideal is therefore radical and zero-dimensional of length ONE. Nevertheless the last rational coordinate needs2^(d-1)+1 numerator bits. The input total coefficient/monomial encoding is polynomial in d. Thus neither reducedness, a small final degree/cardinality, nor an inverse representative in the abstract quotient supplies a polynomial rational-point height from raw coefficient height. This universal construction is outside the hidden-QPD quartic promise; it is a rejection of an uncharged general elimination shortcut, not a counterexample to our frame theorem. It also shows why merely producing algebraic roots of degree one is insufficient: their defining linear coefficients can already be huge.

The QPD budget instead charges a uniform degree29/30 observed preimage, bounded integer templates/evaluations, actual coker/section minors, rational characteristic coefficients, and cofactor eigenkernels. Its special fixed-degree presentation is the load-bearing extra fact. Cardinality<=2n² alone would not yield it.
