QUANTITATIVE POWER-RANGE COMMUTATORS FROM ONE COMPATIBLE DILATION

Status: complete internal derivation; independent of the EB-lifting conjecture.
The exact general theorem below is a sharper route than the previous
near-fixed-point comparison. Numerical checks cover an explicit qubit
universal cloner and seeded Hermitian contractions; they are diagnostics only.

INTERFACE

Let (A,tau) be a finite-dimensional C*-algebra with faithful normalized trace.
Let Phi:A->A be UCP, tau-preserving, positive and selfadjoint on L2(A,tau).
Assume Phi is selfcompatible: there is a UCP map J:A tensor A->A with

  J(a tensor I)=J(I tensor a)=Phi(a) for every a in A.

Use ||X||_2^2=tau(X*X). The spectrum of Phi on L2 lies in [0,1]: positivity
is assumed, and UCP plus tau preservation gives the L2 contraction by Schwarz.
For Hermitian A, write mu_A(S)=<A,1_S(Phi)A>_2 for its spectral measure.

THEOREM: EXACT SPECTRAL-WEIGHTED BOUND

For Hermitian contractions A,C and each integer m>=1, define

  Q_{m-1}(A) = integral_[0,1] (1-lambda^2) lambda^(2m-2) d mu_A(lambda).

Then

  ||[Phi^m(A),Phi^m(C)]||_2
      <= 2 sqrt(min{Q_{m-1}(A), Q_{m-1}(C)}).                 (1)

A symmetric but weaker form replaces the right side by
sqrt(Q_{m-1}(A))+sqrt(Q_{m-1}(C)). More generally, before using contraction
of the inputs, the RHS in (1) is

  2 min{ ||Phi^(m-1)(A)||_infinity sqrt(Q_{m-1}(C)),
         ||Phi^(m-1)(C)||_infinity sqrt(Q_{m-1}(A)) }.

The bound is dimension-free, does not require a spectral gap, and makes the
fixed spectral subspace contribute exactly zero. It controls the whole
operator-norm unit ball, not a selected finite family.

PROOF BY THE COMPATIBLE DILATION

Set x=Phi^(m-1)(A), y=Phi^(m-1)(C). Let J(z)=V* pi(z) V be a Stinespring
dilation, P=VV*, Q=I-P, and pi_1(a)=pi(a tensor I), pi_2(a)=pi(I tensor a).
The represented first and second tensor factors commute. Put

  R_x=Q pi_1(x)V,       R_y=Q pi_2(y)V.

The two equal-marginal identities give

  Phi(x)=V* pi_1(x)V=Phi^m(A),
  Phi(y)=V* pi_2(y)V=Phi^m(C),
  R_x*R_x=Phi(x^2)-Phi(x)^2,
  R_y*R_y=Phi(y^2)-Phi(y)^2.

Writing F=R_x*R_y and inserting P+Q between the commuting represented
operators gives the exact identity

  [Phi(x),Phi(y)] = F* - F.                                  (2)

Thus ||[Phi(x),Phi(y)]||_2<=2||F||_2. Since ||R_x||_infinity<=||x||_infinity
and ||R_y||_infinity<=||y||_infinity, Schatten multiplication gives both

  ||F||_2 <= ||x||_infinity sqrt(tau(R_y*R_y)),
  ||F||_2 <= ||y||_infinity sqrt(tau(R_x*R_x)).

Taking the smaller estimate proves the dilation inequality. Trace preservation
and selfadjointness now identify the two defects exactly:

  tau(Phi(x^2)-Phi(x)^2)
     = ||x||_2^2-||Phi(x)||_2^2
     = integral (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2m-2) d mu_A(lambda),

and likewise for y,C. UCP maps contract operator norm, so ||x||_infinity,
||y||_infinity<=1. This proves (1). No approximation of Phi^m(A) by
Phi^(m+1)(A) is needed.

DYADIC SPECTRAL-BAND FORM

For j=0,1,... let I_j=[1-2^(-j), 1-2^(-(j+1))) and let E_j be the
L2 spectral projection 1_{I_j}(Phi); let E_* be the projection at lambda=1.
These orthogonal projections partition the spectrum. Set a_j(A)=||E_j A||_2.
The fixed band E_* contributes zero. For k>=0, put

  w_{j,k}=sup_{lambda in I_j} (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2k).

Then the exact bandwise estimate is

  Q_k(A) <= sum_(j>=0) w_{j,k} a_j(A)^2.                    (3)

In particular, using 1-lambda<=2^(-j), 1-lambda>2^(-(j+1)), and
lambda^s<=exp(-s(1-lambda)),

  w_{j,k} <= 2^(1-j) exp(-k 2^(-j)).                        (4)

So only bands with spectral gap 1-lambda on the order of 1/k contribute
substantially; much slower modes have a small defect factor, while faster
modes have exponential suppression. Equations (1),(3) give a data-dependent
bound for each input pair, stronger than a single worst-case estimate.

The spectral projections are Hilbert-space projections, generally not CP
maps. Equations (3),(4) are diagnostics for the proof; they do not define a
physical band-filter or an EB reconstruction.

UNIFORM ENVELOPE AND IMPROVEMENT

For k>=1, elementary one-variable optimization gives

  max_(0<=lambda<=1) (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2k)
      = d_k = k^k/(k+1)^(k+1),

with d_0=1. Hence (1) implies

  sup_(A,C Hermitian contractions) ||[Phi^m(A),Phi^m(C)]||_2
      <= 2 sqrt(d_(m-1)).                                   (5)

As m tends to infinity this is 2/sqrt(e m)+O(m^(-3/2)). The earlier
near-fixed-point proof gives 2 sqrt(d_m)+4d_m. The direct compatible-dilation
proof removes the separate drift term; it keeps the same m^(-1/2) leading
rate, with the exact index shift m-1. It is not an EB-rounding theorem.

A useful spectral-gap corollary: if mu_A is supported on {1} union
[0,1-gamma], then Q_k(A)<=sup_(0<=lambda<=1-gamma)(1-lambda^2)lambda^(2k)
||A||_2^2, which decays exponentially in k for each fixed gamma>0. The
lambda=1 component contributes exactly zero; compatibility forces a fixed
output to commute with every output in the range of Phi.

POWER-DILATION CHECK

Iterating the two-output broadcaster gives a 2^r-output extension of Phi^r,
so Phi^r is itself two-compatible for every r>=1. Applying its Stinespring
dilation to Phi^(m-r)(A), Phi^(m-r)(C) yields the same bound with spectral
weight

  q_(m,r)(lambda)=lambda^(2(m-r))(1-lambda^(2r)),  1<=r<=m.

For 0<lambda<1 this is lambda^(2m)(lambda^(-2r)-1), increasing in r.
Thus among these integer power dilations, r=1 minimizes the defect pointwise
and gives (1). This comparison is confined to iterated power dilations; it
does not rule out a different compatible lifting.

BOUNDARY AND NEXT GATE

This result strengthens the approximate-commutativity estimate for the full
CP power range. It does not convert that range to one EB map in normalized
infinity-to-one norm. The flagged Clifford example in
work/cycle3/large_clique_broadcast.txt shows why pairwise near-commutation of
a selected experiment is insufficient; the present estimate is stronger
because it is uniform over every pair in the whole range, but the uniform
EB-lifting step remains open. Spectral projections do not supply a CP
rounding construction. Slow classical modes are retained rather than removed.

No source-reported result is promoted here. The argument is an internal
Stinespring/spectral derivation. Relevant primary context: W.F. Stinespring,
"Positive functions on C*-algebras," Proc. AMS 6 (1955),
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1955-0069403-4; Barnum et al.,
"Noncommuting mixed states cannot be broadcast," PRL 76 (1996),
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9511010. The latter is exact no-broadcasting
prior art, not this quantitative power-range estimate.

Costs: the theorem is existential and finite-dimensional. It needs a supplied
compatible UCP map and its Stinespring representation for the proof; no
algorithm searches for or acquires that dilation. Evaluation of the band
formula requires the spectral measure of Phi and the input coordinates. No
numerical precision or channel implementation cost is claimed. Checks use
only 2x2/4x4 matrices and NumPy; they are transcription diagnostics, not
proof evidence.
