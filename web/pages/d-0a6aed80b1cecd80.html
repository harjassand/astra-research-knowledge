FIXED SIMPLE LIE ALGEBRA: PBW SUPPORT, MULTIPLICITY, AND REALIFICATION AUDIT
8 October 2026. Independent representation-side audit; kept separate from
the regular-SU3 proof. This establishes the representation-theoretic support
and growth estimates only. It does not establish the general coherent-kernel
or actual-compatibility estimate needed to turn them into a full theorem.

1. SETUP AND CLAIM

Fix a compact connected group G with simple Lie algebra g, complexification
g_C, rank r, root system Phi, positive roots Phi+, p=|Phi+|, Weyl group W,
and dual Coxeter number h^vee. Normalize the invariant weight inner product
so every long root has squared length 2. Let theta be the highest root and
rho the Weyl vector. Use the half-Casimir convention

  c2(nu) = (||nu||^2 + 2(nu,rho))/2.

Let V_Lambda be any irreducible carrier. The degree-k enveloping image is

  F_(Lambda,k) = pi_Lambda(U_<=k(g_C)) subset End(V_Lambda),

where U_<=k is the PBW filtration. Its complex G-types are contained in

  S_k = {nu in P_+ intersect Q : nu <= k theta} ,                 (1)

where Q is the root lattice and <= is the dominance order. In particular,
for every supported type,

  ||nu|| <= sqrt(2) k,
  c2(nu) <= k(k+h^vee-1),
  dim_C V_nu <= (1+a_G^W k)^p,                                   (2)

with the explicit root constant

  a_G^W = max_(alpha in Phi+) sqrt(2)||alpha^vee||/(rho,alpha^vee).

For k>=1, the real irreducible types in the Hermitian score space satisfy

  dim_R W_nu <= 2 dim_C V_nu,
  c_W = c2(nu),
  dim_R(W_nu)c_W <= 2 h^vee(1+a_G^W)^p k^(p+2).                  (3)

Thus, if the analytic estimate on each FULL real isotypic block has the
form C_W <= K_(G,eta) dim_R(W_nu)c_W, with K_(G,eta) independent of
Lambda, nu, and the block multiplicity, the degree-k constant obeys

  C_(G,eta)(k) <= 2 K_(G,eta) h^vee(1+a_G^W)^p k^(p+2).           (4)

This is the requested O_(G,eta)(k^(p+2)) representation-side growth. The
conditional premise in (4) is analytic; it is not proved by PBW or Weyl
counting. In particular, this note does not promote the SU3-specific
Berezin estimate to general G.

2. PBW SUPPORT AND THE ROOT-LATTICE ISSUE

The adjoint G-module g_C is the irreducible highest-weight module V_theta.
Its weights are the roots together with zero, and every such weight mu
satisfies theta-mu in Q_+. The product map

  g_C tensor ... tensor g_C -> End(V_Lambda),
  X_1 tensor ... tensor X_s |-> pi_Lambda(X_1)...pi_Lambda(X_s),

is G-equivariant for the diagonal adjoint action: conjugating a product
conjugates each factor. The sum of these images for 0<=s<=k is exactly
F_(Lambda,k). Equivalently, PBW symmetrization is G-equivariant and
Sym^s(g_C) is a submodule of g_C^tensor s in characteristic zero. Hence
every constituent of degree at most k occurs in some g_C^tensor s,
s<=k.

Every weight in g_C^tensor s is a sum mu_1+...+mu_s of adjoint weights.
Since mu_i in Q and theta-mu_i in Q_+, its sum nu obeys

  nu in Q,       s theta - nu = sum_i(theta-mu_i) in Q_+.

If nu is a highest weight of a constituent, it is itself a weight of the
tensor product, so nu<=s theta<=k theta. This proves (1), including the
carrier truncation: finite-dimensional relations in End(V_Lambda) can
remove types but cannot add types beyond the adjoint tensor powers.

There are two compatible ways to treat weights outside Q, and neither
requires silently assuming them away. First, adjoint tensor powers have
trivial action of the simply connected center, so their highest weights
are in Q. Independently, the center acts by scalars on V_Lambda and hence
trivially on End(V_Lambda); any occurring constituent therefore has
trivial central character, equivalently nu in Q. Second, for any dominant
weight nu outside Q, all weights of V_nu lie in nu+Q, so V_nu[0]=0.
The multiplicity bound in Section 4 then gives m_Lambda(nu)=0. In all
dimension/count upper bounds below one may sum over the entire dominant
weight lattice P_+, which only enlarges the set; the outside-Q terms have
zero End(V_Lambda) multiplicity. For a non-simply-connected G, only its
actual character lattice is allowed in the first place, and the same
root-lattice exclusion follows after lifting to the simply connected
cover.

3. NORM, CASIMIR, AND WEYL-DIMENSION BOUNDS

The norm estimate is not inferred from dominance order alone. Use the
tensor-weight realization: each adjoint weight mu_i is zero or a root,
so ||mu_i||<=||theta||=sqrt(2). Therefore any weight nu of g_C^tensor s
has ||nu||<=s sqrt(2)<=k sqrt(2). This gives the first part of (2).

For a supported highest weight, k theta-nu is a nonnegative sum of simple
roots. Since (rho,alpha_i)=||alpha_i||^2/2>0,

  (nu,rho) <= k(theta,rho) = k(h^vee-1),

where the standard identity (rho,theta^vee)=h^vee-1 and
theta^vee=theta use the long-root normalization. Combining with
||nu||^2<=2k^2 proves the exact safe Casimir cap

  c2(nu) <= k^2+k(h^vee-1) = k(k+h^vee-1).                 (5)

If the convention is the unhalved Casimir (nu,nu+2rho), double (5).
For k>=1, (5) is at most h^vee k^2.

Weyl's dimension formula, in coroot form, is

  dim_C V_nu = product_(alpha>0)
     [1 + (nu,alpha^vee)/(rho,alpha^vee)].

Here (rho,alpha^vee)=ht(alpha^vee) is a positive integer. By Cauchy--
Schwarz and the norm bound,

  0 <= (nu,alpha^vee)/(rho,alpha^vee)
     <= k sqrt(2)||alpha^vee||/(rho,alpha^vee)
     <= k a_G^W.

There are p positive-root factors, giving dim_C V_nu<=(1+a_G^W k)^p.
Equivalently, the fully explicit product bound

  dim_C V_nu <= product_(alpha>0)
       (1 + k sqrt(2)||alpha^vee||/(rho,alpha^vee))             (6)

can be used instead of a_G^W. This derivation uses coroot heights in the
Weyl denominator; it does not confuse them with ordinary root heights.

With long roots of squared length 2, a simple coarse table is

  type             p=|Phi+|             h^vee      safe a_G^W
  A_r              r(r+1)/2            r+1        2
  B_r (r>=2)       r^2                  2r-1      2 sqrt(2)
  C_r (r>=2)       r^2                  r+1       2 sqrt(2)
  D_r (r>=4)       r(r-1)               2r-2      2
  G2               6                    4          2 sqrt(3)
  F4               24                   9          2 sqrt(2)
  E6               36                   12         2
  E7               63                   18         2
  E8               120                  30         2

The safe a_G^W column follows simply from ht(alpha^vee)>=1 and the maximum
coroot length: sqrt(2) for simply-laced systems, 2 for the root-length-ratio
2 systems, and sqrt(6) for G2. It is intentionally coarse; (6) is sharper
and remains an explicit exact root-system constant.

4. MULTIPLICITIES: ZERO-WEIGHT CAP AND PBW/KOSTANT GROWTH

Write m_Lambda(nu) for the multiplicity in End(V_Lambda), and
z_nu=dim_C V_nu[0]. The direct highest-vector injection (equivalently the
Fronsdal--Hirai torus-induction inclusion) gives

  m_Lambda(nu) <= z_nu.                                     (7)

For clarity, the direction of currying is

  Hom_G(V_Lambda tensor V_Lambda^*,V_nu)
       ~= Hom_G(V_Lambda^*,V_Lambda^* tensor V_nu).

Put mu=Lambda^* and choose a highest vector v_mu. For an intertwiner
phi, w=phi(v_mu) is a highest vector of total weight mu. Decompose

  w = sum_(beta in Q_+) w_beta,
  w_beta in V_mu[mu-beta] tensor V_nu[beta].

The beta=0 term is v_mu tensor p_phi with p_phi in V_nu[0]. If p_phi=0,
choose the least ordinary root height beta with w_beta nonzero. Project
the equation (e_i tensor 1+1 tensor e_i)w=0 to
V_mu[mu-beta+alpha_i] tensor V_nu[beta]. The second contribution would
come from w_(beta-alpha_i), absent by minimality. Hence every simple
raising operator kills the first tensor factor of w_beta. That factor
has weight mu-beta != mu, impossible in the irreducible V_mu except on
its highest line. Thus phi maps injectively to p_phi, proving (7). This
proof works for all simple types and all actual highest weights; no
regularity or PRV-only occurrence assumption is used.

Let q=p-r. Kostant's multiplicity formula and the PBW partition function
give an explicit polynomial cap. If eta in Q_+, write P(eta) for the
number of nonnegative decompositions of eta into positive roots. There
are p positive roots, q of them nonsimple, and r simple roots. Every
occupation number is at most ht(eta)=(rho^vee,eta). Choose the q
nonsimple-root occupations; the remaining r simple coefficients are
uniquely determined because the simple roots are a basis. Thus

  P(eta) <= (1+ht(eta))^q.

Kostant's formula is

  z_nu = sum_(w in W)(-1)^ell(w) P(w(nu+rho)-rho).

If a term is nonzero, its argument belongs to Q_+, and

  ht(w(nu+rho)-rho)
    <= ||rho^vee|| (||nu||+2||rho||)
    <= ||rho^vee|| (sqrt(2) k+2||rho||).

Consequently, with q=p-r and u=||rho^vee||, v=||rho||,

  m_Lambda(nu) <= z_nu
    <= |W| [1+u(sqrt(2)k+2v)]^q
    <= Z_G (k+1)^q,
  Z_G=|W|[1+sqrt(2)u+2uv]^q.                              (8)

For nu outside Q every Kostant partition term is zero; indeed its
argument remains congruent to nu modulo Q. This recovers z_nu=0 directly.
For q=0 (type A1), (8) is a coarse |W| bound; the exact zero-weight
multiplicity is at most one.

The multiplicity factor is NOT needed in (3)--(4), which use a maximum
over types. It is needed if one also wants a bound on the size of the
entire degree-k score space. Define

  B_G = product_(i=1)^r (1+sqrt(2)||alpha_i^vee||),
  D_G = (1+a_G^W)^p.

The supported dominant labels obey
0<=a_i=(nu,alpha_i^vee)<=sqrt(2)k||alpha_i^vee||. Thus the number of
possible labels is at most B_G(k+1)^r, even if one counts all of P_+
and does not use the Q restriction. Equations (6)--(8) yield

  dim_C F_(Lambda,k)
    <= B_G Z_G D_G (k+1)^(r+q+p)
    = B_G Z_G D_G (k+1)^(2p).                              (9)

The exponent is r+(p-r)+p=2p. Since the filtration is closed under
adjoint (reverse a word and conjugate its coefficients), the Hermitian
real score space has real dimension equal to the complex dimension in
(9). This is an auxiliary support-size bound, not the exponent in (4).

5. REAL TYPES, CONJUGATE PAIRS, AND MULTIPLICITY SPACES

The complexification of the Hermitian real space End(V_Lambda)_sa is
End(V_Lambda). The adjoint operation is an antilinear G-equivariant
involution: it exchanges the complex isotypics for nu and nu^*.

If nu is not self-dual, m_Lambda(nu)=m_Lambda(nu^*) and the real Hermitian
block corresponding to their pair has real dimension
2 m_Lambda(nu) dim_C V_nu. It is m_Lambda(nu) copies of the underlying
real irreducible of complex type, whose real dimension is 2 dim_C V_nu.
The pair must be counted once as a real type, not once for nu and again
for nu^*; the factor 2 in its real dimension already accounts for both.

If nu is self-dual, the complex irrep can be of real or quaternionic
Schur type. In real type its irreducible real form has dimension dim_C V_nu;
in quaternionic type the underlying real irreducible has dimension
2 dim_C V_nu (and the self-adjoint multiplicity is even). In either
case every real irreducible constituent W in the Hermitian block obeys

  dim_R W <=2 dim_C V_nu.

The Casimir is unchanged under duality and realification: on W it is
c_W=c2(nu) in our half normalization. Combining this with (2) and (5)
proves (3). If the proof's Dirichlet estimate is written with the
unhalved Casimir, the same real-dimension bound holds and only the
coefficient doubles.

Repeated copies do not multiply the constant in (4), provided its
analytic premise is a full-isotypic operator inequality. Covariance makes
the superoperators block diagonal across inequivalent complex types but
does not make them scalar on multiplicity spaces. For a nonselfdual pair,
adjoint-preservation identifies the conjugate blocks. Within one
selfdual isotypic, an equivariant self-adjoint operator has a matrix on
the multiplicity space. One may diagonalize the self-adjoint Dirichlet
part there; applying the same per-copy floor then gives the same full
block floor, if that floor truly holds for every copy. On the coherent
side, a bound proved only for one selected copy is insufficient: it must
control the full isotypic block (for example via the full Berezin-symbol
operator and its zero-weight multiplicity space). Once both estimates
hold on full blocks, orthogonality across nu allows the maximum constant
to control their direct sum; there is no sum over multiplicities.

6. WHY THE POWER IS p+2, AND WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE

The growth in (4) is

  real type dimension  O_G(k^p),
  sector Casimir       O_G(k^2),
  per-type coefficient O_(G,eta)(dim_R(W)c_W).

The direct-sum comparison takes the maximum over supported inequivalent
types, not the sum. If one mistakenly sums r_W c_W over all O(k^r)
types, one gets the weaker O(k^(2p+2)) scale. Such summation is not
justified as necessary here and is avoided only by a genuine orthogonal
full-block estimate. Conversely, if the analytic proof couples distinct
types or is not G-covariant, this max argument fails and the p+2 conclusion
does not follow from representation theory alone.

The support/casimir results above are carrier-independent, apply to every
fixed compact connected simple group, and are valid whether or not V_Lambda
is regular. Regularity and eta enter only through the separate analytic
per-type coherent-kernel/compatibility coefficient K_(G,eta). No
approximate covariance, capacity-to-degree inference, or global rank-uniform
constant is obtained here. The support bound uses the actual enveloping
degree promise; bounded Holevo information alone gives no such promise.

Audit verdict: the representation-side exponent p+2 is correct and follows
from adjoint PBW support plus Weyl dimension and Casimir bounds, with the
realification factor at most two. The multiplicity cap is valid and gives
the additional total-support bound (9), but does not enter the comparison
constant. Highest weights outside Q are absent (and have zero zero-weight
multiplicity); allowing them in coarse label counts is harmless. The
remaining general-G step is analytic, not representation-theoretic.
