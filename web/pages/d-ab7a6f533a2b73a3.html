# Cycle 2: reach–avoid control under hidden states and unequal growth

This report extends the action/barrier analysis in FINAL_REPORT.md and
action_timing_theorem.md. It attacks a different failure mode: an endpoint
population fraction is not generally a lineage-level transition probability
when cell states grow at different rates. The result is an exact
three-state-model counterexample, followed by a finite-sample rule for a
fixed-dose randomized comparison that corrects partial observation, debits
control uncertainty, and gives each founder path equal weight.

Every numeric result below is synthetic. The theorem is about the stated
population-balance and observation models; it is not evidence that a particular
biological system obeys them.

## Three-state model and counterexample

Use three states: source \(S\), reached target \(R\), and avoid \(A\). Under a
fixed dose \(u\), a single representative founder path leaves \(S\) at
constant competing hazards \(\lambda_u\) and \(\mu_u\), then is absorbed at
\(R\) or \(A\). Its horizon-\(T\) reach probability is

\[
p_R(u,T)=\Pr(\tau_R\le T,\ \tau_R<\tau_A)
=\frac{\lambda_u}{\lambda_u+\mu_u}
\left(1-e^{-(\lambda_u+\mu_u)T}\right).
\]

The conditional reach probability after eventual resolution is
\(\lambda_u/(\lambda_u+\mu_u)\).

Now let \(n_i(t)\) be continuum population abundance in state \(i\), with
state-specific net growth \(g_i\). The exact model is

\[
\dot n_S=(g_S-\lambda-\mu)n_S,\qquad
\dot n_R=\lambda n_S+g_R n_R,\qquad
\dot n_A=\mu n_S+g_A n_A.
\]

Starting with one source cell, its solution is

\[
n_S(T)=e^{(g_S-\lambda-\mu)T},
\quad
n_R(T)=\lambda e^{g_RT}
\frac{1-e^{-(g_R-g_S+\lambda+\mu)T}}
{g_R-g_S+\lambda+\mu},
\]

with the continuous limit \(n_R(T)=\lambda T e^{g_RT}\) if the denominator is
zero. The formula for \(n_A\) is obtained by replacing \(\lambda,g_R\) with
\(\mu,g_A\). The endpoint bulk target fraction is
\(n_R/(n_S+n_R+n_A)\), which depends on the hazards and growth rates.

**Counterexample.** Set \(T=3\), \(g_S=g_A=0\). For model 1, let
\((\lambda,\mu,g_R)=(0.1,0.9,1)\). Then
\[
p_R(T)=0.0950213,\qquad
(n_S,n_R,n_A)=(0.0497871,1.0017875,0.8551916),
\]
so the endpoint bulk target fraction is \(0.5253856\). For model 2, set
\((\lambda,\mu)=(0.9,0.1)\) and solve for \(g_R=-0.8858958\). It gives the
same endpoint bulk fraction \(0.5253856\), but its horizon reach probability
is \(0.8551916\) and its eventual conditional reach probability is \(0.9\).
Thus the same exact one-time bulk fraction is compatible with horizon reach
probabilities near \(0.095\) and \(0.855\). A target fraction above one half
does not imply that a random founder had a high chance of reaching the target.

This is a structural nonidentifiability result for a single snapshot when
growth is unknown. It does not show that informative time series, purified
initial states, growth measurements, or lineage tracking cannot identify
transition rates. It instead makes those data requirements explicit.

## Partial observation and robust fixed-dose rule

For an equally weighted founder path, let \(Z=1\) mean that it reached \(R\)
by \(T\). A binary assay \(Y\) has sensitivity \(s\) and specificity \(c\).
Then
\[
\Pr(Y=1)=(1-c)+(s+c-1)\Pr(Z=1).
\]
If \(s+c>1\), a known confusion matrix identifies the reach probability from
the assay mean. If the calibration is uncertain, propagate its entire
confidence rectangle through this inversion. A binary observation of the
three-state bulk mixture alone cannot reconstruct its three state weights.

The controller below compares vehicle with one fixed dose. Each randomized
founder contributes one predesignated representative lineage, selected
without descendant-count weighting. This prevents differential expansion
from turning one founder into many observations. The assumption is that the
single-path endpoint remains observable even if a clone grows slowly or dies;
track loss must be recorded as censoring and handled with a validated model,
not silently discarded.

For a concrete lineage estimand in an imaging experiment, label each initial
founder and, at every division, continue one daughter selected by a
pre-registered random rule. Score reach or avoidance only along that
representative daughter chain. If the scientific endpoint is instead “any
descendant of the founder reaches target,” this controller does not apply:
that branching event probability depends on division and must be modeled
directly.

Split familywise error \(\alpha=0.05\) across five quantities: the two
endpoint-arm means, the two calibration proportions \(s,c\), and the
actuation certificate. Each endpoint assay mean then gets the Hoeffding radius
\[
h_N=\sqrt{\frac{\log(200)}{2N}}.
\]
With calibration intervals \(s\in[0.88,0.92]\),
\(c\in[0.93,0.97]\), the robust reach interval for observed mean \(\bar Y\) is
\[
\left[
\frac{\max(0,\bar Y-h_N+c_{\min}-1)}{s_{\max}+c_{\min}-1},
\frac{\min(1,\bar Y+h_N+c_{\max}-1)}{s_{\min}+c_{\max}-1}
\right]\cap[0,1].
\]
The dose is authorized in this model only if
\[
p_{R,\mathrm{dose}}^{\mathrm{lower}}
-p_{R,\mathrm{vehicle}}^{\mathrm{upper}}
>c_{\mathrm{dose}}+\rho_{\mathrm{act}},
\]
where \(c_{\mathrm{dose}}\) is intervention harm/opportunity cost in
reach-probability utility units and \(\rho_{\mathrm{act}}\) is a one-sided
actuation-error debit in the same units. If the inequality fails, the rule is
NO-GO or collect more data.
The displayed corner formula applies when the observed-mean confidence
interval stays in the assay's informative range
\(1-c_{\max}<\bar Y-h_N\le\bar Y+h_N<s_{\min}\). If it touches the
uninformative outer range, the implementation returns \([0,1]\) and abstains
instead of extrapolating the linear correction.

### Synthetic finite calculation

Suppose randomized founder-path endpoint assays produce positive rates
\(0.22\) under vehicle and \(0.392\) under the candidate dose. Assume assay
calibration yields the intervals above. A Hoeffding calibration radius below
\(0.02\), with this five-quantity familywise error split, needs 6,640
known-target and 6,640 known-nontarget calibration cells. The corresponding
synthetic calibration counts are 5,976 assay-positive target cells and 6,308 assay-negative
nontarget cells, giving point estimates \(s=0.90,c=0.95\).

Use intervention cost \(c_{\mathrm{dose}}=0.05\) and a supplied, separately
calibrated one-sided \(99\%\) actuation debit
\(\rho_{\mathrm{act}}=0.02\). The latter number is an assumption for this
worked calculation, not an inferred biological dose-response.

| Founder paths per arm | Positive counts, vehicle / dose | Corrected vehicle reach interval | Corrected dose reach interval | Lower gain before costs | Net lower gain after 0.05 + 0.02 | Decision | Total assays including calibration |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 500 | 110 / 196 | [0.0908, 0.3092] | [0.2932, 0.5115] | -0.0160 | -0.0860 | NO-GO | 14,280 |
| 3,000 | 660 / 1,176 | [0.1415, 0.2585] | [0.3439, 0.4608] | 0.0854 | 0.0154 | APPLY in this model | 19,280 |

The count includes \(2N\) trial assays and 13,280 assay-calibration assays; it does
not include lineage-imaging time, biological replication across wells, or
acquisition of the actuation certificate. Those are separate costs and must
be budgeted in a real study. The result is deliberately close to the decision
boundary: at \(N=3{,}000\) per arm, the robust net gain is only 0.0154. Small
unmodeled dependence or calibration error can reverse the action.

## Independent finite check

cycle2_reach_avoid.py computes the continuum solution in closed form and
independently integrates the three ODEs using RK4 with 30,000 time steps. The
largest abundance discrepancy is \(8.6\times10^{-15}\); the two parameter sets
match the bulk fraction within \(10^{-12}\). The script also checks both
finite-sample decisions as assertions and writes
CYCLE2_MODEL_OUTPUT.json. This verifies the arithmetic and implementation,
not biological validity or the assumptions behind Hoeffding independence.

## Comparison with existing methods

Jagannathan et al.'s Transcompp directly addresses phenotype-specific growth:
it fits a discrete-time Markov transition matrix and a proliferation matrix
to replicate time-series fractions, can fit unknown relative proliferation,
and uses resampling to quantify rate uncertainty. Its published equations and
benchmarks go well beyond the single-snapshot setup attacked above. The
counterexample therefore identifies a data-design boundary, not a failure of
Transcompp. The difference in this report is the explicit hidden-label
confusion interval, equal-founder endpoint objective, familywise finite-sample
bound, actuation debit, and abstaining fixed-dose decision. Transcompp's
published method estimates best-fit rates with empirical resampling; it is
not the same as this distribution-free robust reach–avoid guarantee.
[Primary paper, Bioinformatics 36 (2020), 2813–2820](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021).

Optimal Least Action Control (OLAC) combines a network of state transitions
with least-action estimates and optimizes model occupancy subject to
constraints such as avoiding a bifurcation. It is a model-control method for
noise-driven switching and is closer to the first report's action/barrier
calculation than to an endpoint estimator. Its output is conditional on the
specified network, dynamics, and noise model; it does not by itself correct a
growth-biased cell-count sample or a hidden endpoint assay.
[Primary paper, Physical Review X 5 (2015), 031036](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.031036).

Lv et al.'s 2025 landscape-control (LC) method optimizes model barrier heights
and stable-state occupancy by perturbing gene-network parameters, and compares
that output to OLAC on MISA, EMT, and HESC networks. It uses truncated-moment
landscape calculations and model-specified noise. Their reported EMT
occupancies are simulated control outcomes; the discussion identifies
landscape quantification and direct empirical target validation as remaining
challenges. LC and OLAC can rank interventions inside an acquired GRN model;
neither makes a bulk cell fraction equal a random-lineage reach probability.
[Primary paper, Communications Physics 8 (2025), article 240](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02160-8).

The 2026 article-in-press by Liu, Zanca, Stumpf, and Ham, “Molecular noise
modulates transitions in the cell-fate differentiation landscape,” further
warns that non-equilibrium currents and molecular noise can displace reactive
paths from deterministic least-action or simple barrier pictures. That concern is
orthogonal to the growth bias here and reinforces the need to distinguish a
conditional model action from an experimentally identified fate probability.
[Primary paper, npj Systems Biology and Applications (2026), article in
press](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41540-026-00762-8).

## Falsifiable prediction and biological boundary

If a biological experiment meets the randomized founder-path, calibrated
assay, independent-lineage, and actuation-bound assumptions above, then an
observed 0.22 versus 0.392 positive-readout split at 3,000 founders per arm
implies a \(95\%\) robust lower reach-probability gain of 0.0854 in this model.
After the declared intervention and control debits, the model predicts a
positive net gain of at least 0.0154. Failure to reproduce that bound in a
preregistered independent experiment falsifies at least one model or
measurement assumption and should trigger NO-GO.

The present calculation does not say that any specific cell system has those
rates, growth constants, classifier, or actuation bound. It does not account
for dose-dependent growth changing the within-lineage hazards, non-Markov
memory, state-dependent censoring, cell-cell interactions, multiple
descendant fate branches, or persistence after dose withdrawal. Those require
new measurements or a different model. The robust controller is a
falsifiable experimental decision template, not a validated biological
recommendation.
