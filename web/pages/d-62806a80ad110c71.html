# Discrete convexity for heterogeneous product measures

Status: independently reconstructed adaptation of the released proof, not external validation or a historical novelty claim. The new consequence below is not stated in the inspected release manuscripts. It uses no numerical experiments as universal evidence. Its proof is written in full. Exact finite checks are supplementary.

## Question and interface

Can the original-density discrete-convexity conclusion survive when different coordinates have different inclusion probabilities, without replacing their actual event costs by a common worst-case probability?

Let X be a finite nonempty set and let p=(p_x) have 0<p_x<1. Write

\[
\mu_p(A)=\Pr\{X_p\in A\},\qquad
p(I)=\prod_{x\in I}p_x,\qquad
c_p(G)=\sum_{I\in G}p(I),
\]

where X_p includes coordinates independently and empty products equal one. A containment cover of A is a family G for which every S in A contains some I in G. Define

\[
E_m(D)=\{S\subseteq X:S\nsubseteq D_1\cup\cdots\cup D_m
             \text{ for every }D_1,\ldots,D_m\in D\}.
\]

Tuples may repeat members. D can be any family; no monotonicity is assumed.

**Derived theorem.** Set L=2^70 and k=32L=2^75. If

\[
\mu_p(D)\ge1-1/k,
\]

then E_k(D) has a containment cover G with c_p(G)≤1/2. Moreover, G can be chosen to use only the coordinates

\[
I=\{x\in X:L p_x<1\}.
\]

If I is empty, E_k(D) itself is empty.

The cover costs are the actual heterogeneous containment probabilities. This conclusion does not give prescribed marginals for a distribution on the selected D_i, a polynomial algorithm for finding those D_i or G from an oracle, or a practical constant. k≈3.78×10^22; the theorem is an asymptotic existence tool.

## 1. Product-weight covering lemma

For a vector ρ with 0<ρ_x<1, write ρ(A)=∏_{x∈A}ρ_x. Let H be an r-uniform family, where 1≤r≤h and h≥64, satisfying

\[
\sum_{e\in H}\rho(e)\le2^{h+1}.
\]

Then the family of sets containing at least m=2^(12h) edges of H has a cover of ρ-cost at most 3·2^(-h).

**Proof.** Put T=2^(4h), π=2^(-8h), and define

\[
d(A)=\sum_{e\in H:A\subseteq e}\rho(e\setminus A)
\quad (|A|\le r).
\]

Take C_0={A: |A|≤r, d(A)>T}. The exact identity

\[
\sum_{|A|\le r}d(A)\rho(A)
=2^r\sum_{e\in H}\rho(e)
\]

gives c_ρ(C_0)≤2^(r+1−3h)≤2^(1−2h). Let H' consist of edges containing no C_0 generator. Every A⊆e∈H' then has d(A)≤T. Grouping regular edges by their exact intersection with any B⊆X gives

\[
\sum_{e\in H'}\rho(e\setminus B)
\le T\sum_{j=0}^r\binom{|B|}{j}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Independently sample each ordered pair (e,e')∈(H')², including diagonal pairs, with probability π, and put its union into C_1. Distinct pair indices remain independent even when their unions coincide. Using (1) with B=e gives

\[
\mathbb E c_\rho(C_1)
\le\pi\sum_{e,e'\in H'}\rho(e\cup e')
\le\pi 2^r T\sum_{e\in H'}\rho(e)
\le2^{1-2h}.                                    \tag{2}
\]

For every ordered tuple of m distinct regular edges, add its union to C_2 when none of the m² associated ordered pairs was sampled. C_0∪C_1∪C_2 covers the target for every outcome: a target containing no C_0 contains m regular edges; it contains a sampled pair union, or their residual full union.

Let W_t be the sum of ρ(union) over ordered t-tuples of distinct regular edges, with W_0=1. A prefix union B has |B|≤rt≤rm. By (1) and the multiplicative identity ρ(B∪e)=ρ(B)ρ(e\B),

\[
W_{t+1}\le T(1+rm)^r W_t,
\qquad W_m\le[T(1+rm)^r]^m.
\]

The probability a fixed m-tuple survives is (1−π)^(m²)≤2^(−πm²). Consequently

\[
\mathbb E c_\rho(C_2)
\le 2^{[4h+r\log_2(1+rm)]m-\pi m^2}\le2^{-m}.
\]

Indeed, 1+rm≤(h+1)2^(12h)≤2^(13h), and

\[
4h+13rh+1\le18h^2\le2^{4h}=\pi m
\]

for h≥64. Summing (2) and the C_0 bound gives

\[
\mathbb E c_\rho(C_0\cup C_1\cup C_2)
\le2^{2-2h}+2^{-m}\le3\cdot2^{-h}.
\]

Some finite sampling outcome has cost at most its expectation. This proves the lemma. The proof uses multiplicative coordinate costs, not equal coordinate probabilities. ∎

## 2. Heterogeneous half-density proposition

Let q=(q_x) with 0<q_x<1 and μ_q(F)≥1/2. Then E_32(F) has a cover of cost at most 1/2 at ρ_x=q_x/L, L=2^70.

**Proof.** Put f=1_F and, for U⊆X, define

\[
b(U)=\left[\prod_{x\in U}(1-q_x)\right]
 \sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{U}}(-1)^{\sum z_x}
 \mathbb E[f(z,Y)],\qquad w(U)=b(U)^2,
\]

where the remaining coordinates of Y are independent Bernoulli q_x. The orthonormal product basis

\[
\chi_U(y)=\prod_{x\in U}\frac{q_x-y_x}{\sqrt{q_x(1-q_x)}}
\]

satisfies

\[
\widehat f(U)=\left[\prod_{x\in U}\sqrt{\frac{q_x}{1-q_x}}\right]b(U).
\]

Parseval therefore gives

\[
\sum_{U\subseteq X}q(U)w(U)
=\sum_U\left[\prod_{x\in U}(1-q_x)\right]\widehat f(U)^2
\le\mu_q(F)\le1.                               \tag{3}
\]

Fix S∈E_32(F). For coordinate x∈S, give a 32-row column y the signed mass

\[
R_x(y)=\prod_{j=1}^{32}q_x^{y_j}(1-q_x)^{1-y_j}
       -(1-q_x)^{32}(-1)^{\sum_j y_j}.
\]

For x∉S use its ordinary product mass. R_x(0,…,0)=0, so any array of nonzero product signed mass has a 1 in every S-column. Its row union contains S. The 32 rows cannot all belong to F. Thus the product of their f-values vanishes pointwise on the signed support. Expansion by the corrected coordinate set U yields the exact identity

\[
0=\sum_{U\subseteq S}(-1)^{|U|}b(U)^{32}.
\]

No positivity of R_x is needed. Isolating the empty term gives

\[
\sum_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq S}w(U)^{16}
\ge\mu_q(F)^{32}\ge2^{-32}.                    \tag{4}
\]

At ρ=q/L include all nonempty U with w(U)≥2^(−64|U|) as generators G_0. Since ρ(U)=q(U)2^(−70|U|), (3) gives c_ρ(G_0)≤1/64.

Bin the remaining positive weights by size r and integers h:

\[
H_{r,h}=\{U:|U|=r,\ U\notin G_0,
              \ 2^{-(h+1)}<w(U)\le2^{-h}\}.
\]

An occupied bin has h≥64r, and (3) gives

\[
\sum_{U\in H_{r,h}}\rho(U)
\le\sum_{U\in H_{r,h}}q(U)<2^{h+1}.
\]

The product-weight lemma supplies a cover G_{r,h}, of cost ≤3·2^(−h), of all sets containing at least 2^(12h) members of that bin. Only finitely many bins occur. Set G=G_0∪⋃G_{r,h}. Then

\[
c_\rho(G)\le\frac1{64}
 +3\sum_{r\ge1}\sum_{h\ge64r}2^{-h}
=\frac1{64}+\frac6{2^{64}-1}<\frac12.
\]

If S contains no G-generator, then its subsets have no large weight and fewer than 2^(12h) members of each bin. Hence

\[
\sum_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq S}w(U)^{16}
\le\sum_{r\ge1}\sum_{h\ge64r}2^{-4h}
=\frac{16}{15(2^{256}-1)}<2^{-32}.
\]

By (4), S∉E_32(F). Thus G covers E_32(F). ∎

## 3. Restore every original coordinate rate

Assume μ_p(D)≥1−1/k. Write J={x:Lp_x≥1}, I=X\J.

Construct L rows R_1,…,R_L, independently across coordinates, as follows.

- For x∈I choose one label in {0,1,…,L}, with probabilities 1−Lp_x for 0 and p_x for each positive label. Include x only in the indicated positive row.
- For x∈J choose one mandatory row uniformly. Put x there, and independently put it in each other row with probability
  \[
  t_x=\frac{p_x-1/L}{1-1/L}\in[0,1).
  \]

Each fixed row has inclusion probability p_x at coordinate x and independent coordinates. Thus every row has exactly law μ_p; rows need not be independent. Their union always contains J and on I has independent inclusion probabilities q_x=Lp_x∈(0,1).

The union bound gives

\[
\Pr\{R_j\in D\text{ for all }j\}\ge1-L/k=31/32.
\]

Let U_L(D) be the family of exact unions of L members of D, and let

\[
F=\{A\subseteq I:J\cup A\in U_L(D)\}.
\]

The union of the rows has the form J∪A with A∼μ_q. The preceding event implies J∪A∈U_L(D); hence μ_q(F)≥31/32.

If I is empty, a good-row outcome exists and its L rows cover X. Repeating their tuple 32 times shows E_k(D)=∅.

Otherwise apply the half-density proposition on I. It supplies G⊆2^I covering E_32(F), with

\[
\sum_{U\in G}\prod_{x\in U}(q_x/L)
=c_p(G)\le1/2.
\]

For any S∈E_k(D), its restriction S∩I belongs to E_32(F). Otherwise S∩I is contained in a union of 32 sets A_j∈F. Each J∪A_j is an exact union of L D-members, so their union covers all of S, contradicting S∈E_k(D). Therefore S∩I contains a G-generator; that generator is also contained in S. This proves the theorem, including the support restriction on G. ∎

## Checked deductions, prior art, and obstruction

This is an adaptation of the source's full Fourier, pair-sampling, and row-coupling argument. It does not independently establish historical priority for either the uniform theorem or this extension. Primary prior art checked in this cycle includes Pham's 2412.03540v2 (revised September 6, 2026), Park's 2609.14681v1 (September 13, 2026), Li's 2609.08967v1 (September 8, 2026), and Ascoli–He–Park–Talagrand's 2608.11183v1 (August 11, 2026). These sources retain common-p formulations in the inspected statements. Earlier signed-product/Fourier work is credited by the released source; its full historical comparison remains unaudited.

The decisive computational obstruction is explicit cover acquisition. The construction enumerates Boolean subsets, weight bins, and residual m-tuples, m=2^(12h). Existence and internally checked algebra do not turn that enumeration into polynomial oracle work. For a fully supplied rational truth table and rational probabilities, exhaustive enumeration of covers is an effective finite algorithm with at most 2^|X| output generators; checking all candidate covers can take 2^(2^|X|) candidates. An oracle representation of D has no corresponding efficiency guarantee here.

The natural next falsifiable step is an independent audit of the saturated-coordinate section in Section 3 and a formal statement under vector probabilities. A more consequential algorithmic target is to obtain the cover from sparse Fourier/weighted-degree access with a charged acquisition interface; no such algorithm has been proved. The threshold-rounding paper's existence statement does not supply this service.

## Concrete downstream use

The theorem provides exceptional containment certificates when coordinate rates differ, as in heterogeneous random graph hosts or uneven feature sampling. It keeps each certificate's probability equal to its true product cost. It also shows that exceptional certificates can avoid coordinates with p_x≥2^(−70). This is a structural conclusion under a very strong high-probability premise, not a practical sample-size or discovery guarantee.

The companion graph-decomposition manuscript provides another reusable device: its Lemma 3.1 compresses a family of requirements of size at most s to at most ⌈2r^(−s)⌉ requirements after a fresh random extension. Its Proposition 3.2 then establishes all compressed tests before selecting the target and applying Hall's theorem, avoiding a choice-of-tests gap. The full manuscript was read; its decomposition uses separately embedded pieces whose shared vertices need not agree. Heterogeneous discrete convexity does not by itself give simultaneous consistent embeddings.
