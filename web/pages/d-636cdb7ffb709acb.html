# A constructive median lift for stationary Markov cotype

Status: independently reconstructed mathematical argument and original deductions,
supported by exact finite computations and a second internal reconstruction by
`frontier_check_median`. No independent external review or formal kernel
verification has been performed. Source publication and finite checks are not
being substituted for the derivation below.

## Exact interface and source separation

Input: a finite stochastic matrix `A`, a stationary probability vector `π`
(`πA=π`, zero coordinates allowed), an integer `t≥1`, and finitely many real
`L1(μ)` vectors `x_i`. The output must lie in the **whole ambient** `L1(μ)`,
and may leave the linear span of the input. The required inequality is

\[
\sum_i\pi_i\|x_i-y_i\|_1^2+
t\sum_{ij}\pi_i a_{ij}\|y_i-y_j\|_1^2
\le C^2\sum_{ij}\pi_i\Bigl(\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^t A^s\Bigr)_{ij}
\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2. \tag{1}
\]

Author claim: OpenAI, *Metric Markov Cotype Two of ℓ1*, dated October 5,
2026, pinned commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`, Theorem 1.2,
proves (1) for real ℓ1 with `C²=3024`. Lemma 2.1 constructs a finite cut
encoding with an ambient contraction; Lemma 3.1 supplies a cubic/quartic
estimate with constant 108; Corollary 3.2 telescopes it along a martingale;
Lemma 4.1 compares geometric and Cesàro endpoint costs with constant 28.
Sections 5 and 6 give the stopped-chain assembly, expected-median identity,
and extension consequence. The entire manuscript source was read.

Our deductions: the local constant can be replaced by the **sharp 18**;
the general geometric comparison can be bounded by 18; consequently
`C≤18` suffices in (1). The same proof works in every real `L1(μ)`.
We give an exact polynomial-cost compiler for finite coordinate input,
an equally informed linear-control obstruction, and a simplex repair.
These are internal mathematical deductions, not claimed new literature priority.

## 1. The finite ambient lift

Fix measurable representatives of the finite family. Define, pointwise,

\[
v=\min_i x_i,\qquad
b_B=\bigl(\min_{i\in B}x_i-\max_{i\notin B}x_i\bigr)_+,
\quad w_B=\|b_B\|_1,
\]

for nonempty proper `B⊂[n]`, discarding zero weights. These are integrable:
their absolute values are bounded by a constant times `Σ_i|x_i|`. Put

\[
z_i(B)=1_{i\in B},\quad T(u)=v+\sum_B b_Bu_B,
\quad \|u\|_H^2=\sum_Bw_Bu_B^2,\quad
\|u\|_{1,w}=\sum_Bw_B|u_B|.
\]

At every underlying coordinate, distinct input levels produce nested upper
cuts, weighted by their successive gaps. Telescoping those gaps gives

\[
Tz_i=x_i,\quad
\|z_i-z_j\|_H^2=\|z_i-z_j\|_{1,w}=\|x_i-x_j\|_1,
\quad \|Tu-Tu'\|_1\le\|u-u'\|_{1,w}. \tag{2}
\]

There are at most `2^n−2` cuts, but they need not be enumerated by the
finite coordinate implementation below. This proof uses integrability and
measurability only; countable original coordinates are unnecessary.

## 2. Sharp local inequality

Let `φ(r)=3r²−2r³`, and apply `φ` to each cube coordinate to define `Φ`.
For `u,u'∈[0,1]^B` and binary `e`, write

\[
a=u-e,\quad b=u'-e,\quad\delta=b-a,\quad
U=\|a\|_H^2,\quad V=\|\delta\|_H^2,\quad c=\langle a,\delta\rangle_H.
\]

The quartic Bregman remainder is exactly

\[
R=\|b\|_H^4-\|a\|_H^4-4U c
=2UV+(2c+V)^2. \tag{3}
\]

For one coordinate, the two numbers `a_B,b_B` have the same sign, since
`e_B` is an endpoint of `[0,1]`. Integrating
`|φ'(r)|=6r(1−r)≤6|r−e_B|` therefore gives

\[
|\phi(u'_B)-\phi(u_B)|\le3|b_B^2-a_B^2|.
\]

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, followed by elementary algebra, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Phi(u')-\Phi(u)\|_{1,w}^2
&\le9\Bigl(\sum_Bw_B|\delta_B||a_B+b_B|\Bigr)^2\\
&\le9V\|a+b\|_H^2=9V(4U+4c+V)\\
&\le18R.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]

The last inequality follows from the nonnegative identity

\[
2R-V(4U+4c+V)=8c^2+4cV+V^2
=8(c+V/4)^2+V^2/2\ge0. \tag{5}
\]

**Sharpness of 18 for (4):** use two unit weights, `e=(0,0)`,
`u=(r,r)`, `u'=(r+ε,r−ε)`, with `0<ε<r<1/2`. First let `ε/r→0`.
The ratio of the left side of (4) to `R` tends to `18(1−r)²`.
Then let `r→0`. No smaller universal local constant works. The exact
sequence `r=10^−k, ε=r²` has ratios tending to 18; the last computed
ratio, at `k=5`, is `17.999640000900005`.

The endpoint/binary condition is load-bearing. The scalar integral estimate
is not an arbitrary Hilbert-to-L1 Lipschitz assertion. In particular this
argument does not supply unrestricted high moments from a trace bound.

## 3. Martingale assembly

Put `p=1/(t+1)`, `q=1−p`, and

\[
G=p(I-qA)^{-1}=[I+t(I-A)]^{-1},\quad h_i=(Gz)_i,
\quad y_i=T\Phi(h_i). \tag{6}
\]

The inverse exists because `q<1` and `A` is stochastic; its Neumann
series is nonnegative and has row sums one. The harmonic identity is
`h_i=pz_i+qΣ_j a_ijh_j`.

Start an `A`-walk from `π`. From an alive state `i`, kill it at `i`
with probability `p`, otherwise make one `A` transition. Attach `h_i`
while alive and `z_i` after killing. The attached process `M_m` is a
bounded Hilbert-valued martingale. If `S` is the number of surviving
transitions, then `P(S=s)=pq^s`, independently of the original walk,
and `M_∞=z_{X_S}`. The binary center `e=z_{X_0}` is measurable at time
zero, so the linear term in (4) has zero conditional expectation.
Telescoping (4), bounded convergence for the potential, and monotone
convergence for the nonnegative energy give

\[
\mathbb E\sum_m\|\Phi(M_{m+1})-\Phi(M_m)\|_{1,w}^2
\le18\,\mathbb E\|z_{X_S}-z_{X_0}\|_H^4.
\]

At time `m`, the alive event has probability `q^m` and is independent of
the stationary walk index. Summing its death and survival costs gives

\[
B+tE_A\le18\,\mathbb E\|x_{X_S}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2, \tag{7}
\]

where `B=Σπ_i‖z_i−Φh_i‖²_{1,w}` and
`E_A=Σπ_i a_ij‖Φh_i−Φh_j‖²_{1,w}`. Applying the ambient contraction
in (2) bounds the desired left side of (1) by `B+tE_A`.
Only stationarity is used. Reversibility is needed by the conventional
definition of metric Markov cotype, and is stronger than this proof needs.

## 4. A general metric geometric comparison with constant 18

For any stationary chain and any metric data, set
`D(s)=E d(x_{X_s},x_{X_0})²` and `W=t^−1Σ_{s=1}^tD(s)`.
Minkowski plus stationarity gives `√D(r+s)≤√D(r)+√D(s)`.
Averaging `D(t)≤2D(r)+2D(t−r)` gives `D(t)≤4W`.

Write `S=tJ+R_0`, `0≤R_0<t`. If `t=1`, subadditivity implies
`E D(S)≤E S² D(1)=3W`. If `t≥2`, put `α=q^t`.
The function `s log(s/(s+1))` is decreasing, since its derivative is
`log(1−1/(s+1))+1/(s+1)<0`; hence `α≤4/9`.
The geometric block law gives

\[
EJ^2=\frac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{(1-\alpha)^2}\le52/25,
\quad P(R_0=r)=\frac{pq^r}{1-\alpha}\le\frac9{5t},
\quad E D(R_0)\le9W/5.
\]

Using `√D(S)≤J√D(t)+√D(R_0)` and Minkowski in the random `S` variable,

\[
E D(S)\le\bigl(4\sqrt{13}/5+3/\sqrt5\bigr)^2 W
=\frac{253+24\sqrt{65}}{25}W<18W. \tag{8}
\]

The strict comparison is exact: `197²>24²·65`. Combining (7) and (8)
proves (1) with `C²=324` in every real `L1(μ)`. No finite computation is
used in this universal conclusion.

## 5. An executable compiler and charged costs

For rational `A∈Q^{n×n}` and finite rational coordinates
`x∈Q^{n×d}`, compute `G` once by rational Gaussian elimination. For each
original coordinate, sort its `n` values. Let `α_1≤…≤α_n` be the sorted
values and `j_1,…,j_n` their indices. For every output row `i`, form
suffix probabilities `h_{ir}=Σ_{s>r}G_{i,j_s}` and set

\[
y_i(k)=\alpha_1+\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}
(\alpha_{r+1}-\alpha_r)\phi(h_{ir}). \tag{9}
\]

Zero gaps make ties harmless. This equals the expectation of the coordinate
median of three independent input samples with common law `G_i`.
Thus (9) computes the source construction without enumerating all subsets.

Arithmetic cost: `O(n³+d n²+d n log n)` exact rational operations,
plus output storage `O(nd)` and resolvent storage `O(n²)`. These are
arithmetic counts, not unit-cost bit or hardware-runtime claims. For input
rationals of at most `L` bits, common-denominator integer matrix entries
have `O(n²L+log t)` bits; determinant/adjugate bounds give polynomial
bit lengths in `n,L,log t`. Fraction-free elimination and the fixed-degree
cubic therefore give a polynomial bit algorithm. Reading `nd` input entries,
the coordinate ordering, the full resolvent, and all output coordinates
are charged. The current Python implementation is ordinary fractional
Gauss–Jordan, not an optimized fraction-free complexity implementation.

For infinite-coordinate input, (6) still defines an integrable finite sum
of medians. An efficient finite representation or approximation oracle is
additional acquisition input; the theorem does not supply it.

## 6. A universal obstruction for the equally informed linear control

Let the Markov chain be the simple walk on `{0,1}^d`, uniformly choosing
one bit to flip; `π` is uniform. Use data `x_v=e_v` in `ℓ1^{2^d}` and
the same `G=[I+t(I−A)]^−1` for both constructions. Linear averaging gives
`\widetilde y_v=G_v`, the row probability vector.

For coordinate `k`, the character `χ_k(v)=(−1)^{v_k}` is an `A` eigenvector
with eigenvalue `1−2/d`. Therefore `Gχ_k=aχ_k`, where
`a=(1+2t/d)^−1`. If vertices `v,w` differ in bit `k`, duality against
the bounded character gives `‖G_v−G_w‖_1≥2a`.
Consequently the linear left side of (1) is at least `4ta²`.
All original distances are at most 2, so its right-side Cesàro cost is
at most 4. The linear ratio is therefore at least

\[
\frac{t}{(1+2t/d)^2};\qquad t=d\Longrightarrow d/9\to\infty. \tag{10}
\]

This is an analytical obstruction, not extrapolation from a finite test.
The two methods receive precisely the same chain, endpoint matrix, and data.
The median map suppresses the diffuse tiny probabilities by `φ(r)≈3r²`
and is allowed to leave their affine span. This explains why preserving
linear averaging cannot recover the endpoint theorem.

Exact symmetry reduction to the Hamming-distance birth/death chain lets
the experiment handle `2^d` states without creating them. At `t=d`:

| d | Linear energy ratio | Median energy ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.67556 | 0.64666 |
| 4 | 1.09845 | 0.42764 |
| 8 | 1.69193 | 0.26636 |
| 16 | 2.66933 | 0.24853 |
| 32 | 4.49884 | 0.24917 |

The admissible constant-zero output has fidelity cost 1 and edge cost 0,
so its ratio is `1/W`, approaching `1/4` on this family. It is better than
the median in the small tested dimensions. A constant stationary-mean output
also has bounded ratio. These equally informed controls are recorded in the
revised evidence code. Thus (10) rules out the **prescribed linear resolvent**,
not all linear or nonlinear smoothing algorithms, and supplies no objective
approximation or learning-advantage guarantee.

## 7. Distribution-valued use and a target constraint

Coordinatewise medians of probability vectors need not have mass one:
three independent uniform draws from `{e_1,e_2,e_3}` give expected-median
mass `3φ(1/3)=7/9`. With `n` equiprobable basis vectors the mass is
`nφ(1/n)=3/n−2/n²`, tending to zero. Blind normalization has an unbounded
sensitivity near such data.

There is nevertheless a direct repair for the full discrete probability
simplex `Δ={z≥0:Σz=1}`. Fix a reference distribution `r∈Δ` (one may take
the first input distribution, `r=x_1`). For `z∈ℓ1`, take `z^+`, set
`s=‖z^+‖_1`, and define

\[
R(z)=\begin{cases}
z^++(1-s)r,&s\le1,\\
z^+/s,&s\ge1.
\end{cases} \tag{11}
\]

Positive-part projection is 1-Lipschitz in ℓ1. The first branch is
2-Lipschitz. On the second branch, if `s≥s'≥1`, add and subtract
`z'^+/s` to bound the distance by
`(‖z^+−z'^+‖_1+|s−s'|)/s≤2‖z^+−z'^+‖_1`.
For different branches, split the nonnegative segment at mass one; the
two subsegment ℓ1 lengths sum to the total. Thus `R` is 2-Lipschitz
and is the identity on `Δ`.

For probability input, use `Ry_i`. Its fidelity and edge costs are at
most four times those of `y_i`, so (1) holds in `Δ` with `C≤36`.
The real Hilbert→ℓ1 extension consequence in the source, followed by
`R`, yields a universal Hilbert→Δ extension bound with an additional
factor at most two. The reference is supplied or acquired target structure
and is charged; choosing `r=x_1` needs no extra distribution acquisition.
Every common support face containing `r` is preserved. This construction
does not preserve other linear constraints or arbitrary closed subspaces.

## 9. An additional sample-oracle compiler, independently rechecked

The checker supplied the pointwise scalar inequality
`|med(a,b,c)|²≤|ab|+|ac|+|bc|`: at least two magnitudes are at least
the magnitude of the signed median. Consequently, for independent identical
copies of any scalar `X` with a finite first absolute moment,

\[
E|\operatorname{med}(X_1,X_2,X_3)|^2\le3(E|X|)^2. \tag{12}
\]

The constant 3 is sharp along rare Bernoulli variables: the ratio is
`3−2p`. This is a median-functional bound, not estimation of `EX` and not
an unrestricted vector or noncommutative moment theorem.

For each row `i`, sample `L` independent triples of geometric endpoints,
take their coordinate medians `Z_{iℓ}`, and output their empirical average
`ŷ_i`. Subtracting the fixed center `x_i` commutes with median. Applying
(12) in each coordinate and Minkowski in the sampling `L2` space gives

\[
E\|\widehat y_i-y_i\|_1^2\le\frac3L
\bigl(E_{G_i}\|x_J-x_i\|_1\bigr)^2. \tag{13}
\]

This extends to countable coordinates by monotone convergence and to
`L1(μ)` by integral Minkowski. Jensen, (8), and stationarity imply
`Σ_iπ_i E‖ŷ_i−y_i‖₁²≤54W/L`, and the associated fidelity-plus-edge
error is at most `(1+4t)` times this quantity. Minkowski for the objective
direct sum therefore yields

\[
EJ_t(\widehat y)\le
\left[18+\sqrt{\frac{54(1+4t)}L}\right]^2W. \tag{14}
\]

Markov's inequality gives the confidence version with `L` replaced by
`Lδ` inside the square root, with probability at least `1−δ`.
After one common simplex repair (11), multiply these objective bounds by 4.
No logarithmic confidence dependence for the relative objective was proved.

Each queried row uses `3L` geometric endpoint walks, expected `3Lt`
transitions, `3L` data-vector reads, and the actual median/output arithmetic.
Under a supplied sparse representation of support size at most `s`, median
and aggregation work is `O(sL)` with a suitable support dictionary. All
rows cost expected `3nLt` transitions and their actual output storage.
Achieving expected factor `(18+ε)²` requires `L≥54(1+4t)/ε²`, hence
`O(nt²/ε²)` expected transitions. Transition sampling, coordinates,
stationarity, arithmetic precision, and the common repair reference are
supplied or acquired inputs; none is inferred from existence alone.

This permits an implicit graph-smoothing map with sparse distribution-valued
outputs. It does not learn arbitrary unknown distributions in dimension-free
`L1` samples, and it does not approximate `min_y J_t(y)` by a constant factor.
The next sharply falsifiable acquisition target is a cheap joint endpoint
coupling whose graph-edge sampling error avoids the factor `t`. Independent
row estimation and arbitrary common random numbers do not provide that bound.
