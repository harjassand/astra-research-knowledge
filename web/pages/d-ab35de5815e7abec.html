# Gaussian weak-signal archives: a dimension-uniform upper construction

Date: 2026-10-07. Status: complete internal derivation under the statistical-kernel interface below; external correctness and priority review remain open. The supplied N30 summary was read, but its missing proof was not used as a premise. This construction uses hats linear in the original Gaussian coordinate on a Cauchy-spaced grid, rather than claiming to have recovered N30's hats linear in the Cauchy coordinate.

## Result and interface

Write `P_t=N(t,1)` and use TV equal to half L1. There is an absolute finite constant `K`, independent of all dimensions and signals, such that for every integer `J>=1` there is a blind classical archive with at most `J` labels and output law `Q_t` satisfying, simultaneously for `|t|<=1`,

\[
Q_0=P_0,\qquad
\chi^2(Q_t\Vert P_t)\le K t^2 J^{-4},\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q_t,P_t)\le \frac{\sqrt K}{2}|t|J^{-2}.
\tag{1}
\]

The encoder sees one Gaussian observation, not `t`. The decoder receives the retained label and outputs a fresh real observation through a parameter-independent stochastic kernel. The supplied model, `J`, and decoder program are input-independent. All input-dependent records are discarded except the label. No entangled receiver reference or other input-dependent side record is used. Independent seeds are unnecessary.

Consequently, tensoring the same channel independently across `d` coordinates gives, for every mean vector with `max_i |theta_i|<=1`,

\[
Q^{(d)}_\theta=\bigotimes_i Q_{\theta_i},\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(Q^{(d)}_\theta,N(\theta,I_d))
\le \sqrt{K/2}\,\|\theta\|_2 J^{-2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus for `0<r<=1`, on the full Euclidean ball `||theta||_2<=r` and hence on `{0,+/-r e_i}` or its `k`-sparse subball, one can attain error `epsilon` with

\[
\log_2D\le \frac d2\log_2(r/\epsilon)+O(d)
\tag{3}
\]

when `epsilon/r` is small. The constant in `O(d)` is absolute. There is no dimension-dependent tail allocation and no `sqrt(k)` penalty. Inactive coordinates are reconstructed **exactly** as null Gaussians; the retained alphabet nevertheless contains all coordinate labels because the encoder is blind to the active direction.

The stronger chi-square statement in (1), rather than coordinatewise TV addition, is what removes the sparse-support factor. If `epsilon >= r/sqrt(2pi)`, the one-label null decoder already works; (3) is not a claim of a sharp transition constant.

## 1. Exact reference-preserving positive channel

Let `w(x)=phi(x)` be the standard Gaussian density. For any finite partition of unity `a_j(x)>=0`, `sum_j a_j(x)=1`, put

\[
\alpha_j=\int w(x)a_j(x)\,dx>0,
\qquad \Gamma_j(dy)=\frac{w(y)a_j(y)}{\alpha_j}\,dy.
\tag{4}
\]

The encoder draws label `j` with probabilities `a_j(X)`; the decoder draws from `Gamma_j`. With

\[
L_t(x)=e^{tx-t^2/2},\qquad f_t=L_t-1,
\]

the reconstructed density is

\[
q_t(y)=w(y)\left[1+Tf_t(y)\right],\qquad
Tf(y)=\sum_j a_j(y)\frac{\int w(x)a_j(x)f(x)\,dx}{\alpha_j}.
\tag{5}
\]

`T` is a positive Markov operator, `T1=1`, stationary and self-adjoint for `P_0`. In particular `q_0=w` exactly. Its associated `(X,Y)` null kernel has joint density

\[
w(x)w(y)\sum_j\frac{a_j(x)a_j(y)}{\alpha_j},
\tag{6}
\]

which is symmetric. These statements are identities, including the tails; no approximate normalization or estimated unknown mean is involved.

## 2. Grid and elementary mesh bounds

It suffices first to construct the channel for `J>=J_0=2^16`; smaller `J` use one label and `P_0`. Define

\[
\delta=\pi/J,\quad z_j=\tan(\pi(j/J-1/2)),\quad 1\le j\le J-1,\quad
T_J=6+\sqrt{16\log J},\quad S_J=T_J+2.
\tag{7}
\]

Keep all the nodes `z_j` with `|z_j|<=S_J`, in increasing order. The kept nodes are symmetric, contain zero when `J` is even, and number at most `J-1`. Denote the largest kept node by `B` and its preceding node by `L`. Put ordinary nonnegative, piecewise x-linear triangular hats on this grid. The left endpoint hat is one on the left exterior ray and decreases linearly to zero at the next node; the right endpoint hat is zero before `L`, increases linearly to one at `B`, and is one on `[B,infinity)`. The hats sum to one on the entire line.

At a nonendpoint node `z`, let `h_-` and `h_+` be the adjacent widths and let `tau=tan(delta)`. The tangent addition formula gives exactly

\[
h_+=\frac{\tau(1+z^2)}{1-z\tau},\qquad
h_-=\frac{\tau(1+z^2)}{1+z\tau},\qquad
h_+-h_-=
\frac{2z\tau^2(1+z^2)}{1-z^2\tau^2}.
\tag{8}
\]

For `J>=2^16` and all kept nodes, elementary estimates give

\[
H_z:=\max(h_-,h_+)\le\frac{2\pi(1+z^2)}J<1,
\qquad
|h_+-h_-|\le\frac{11\pi^2|z|(1+z^2)}{J^2},
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
S_J\frac{2\pi(1+S_J^2)}J+
\frac12\left[\frac{2\pi(1+S_J^2)}J\right]^2\le1.
\tag{10}
\]

For completeness, `S_J=8+4sqrt(log J)`; the left side of (10) is less than one at `J=2^16`, and is decreasing thereafter. The derivative in `log J` of `log[S_J(1+S_J^2)]` is less than one in this range. Also `S_J tan(pi/J)<1/4` and `tan(pi/J)<=3pi/(2J)`, giving the first part of (9); the second follows directly from (8), with room in its numerical constant. The next unkept node lies above `S_J` and its gap from `B` is less than one, so `B>S_J-1`. The endpoint-hat support starts above `S_J-2=T_J`. Moreover `B(B-L)<=1` by (10).

The conservative `J_0` is only a universal constant used to simplify a uniform proof. It is not an experimentally optimized operating threshold.

## 3. Interior posterior means have second-order drift

Fix an interior node `z` and write `s=x-z`. Let `nu_z` be the probability measure with density proportional to the flat hat with respect to Lebesgue measure. Direct integration gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_z}s=(h_+-h_-)/3.
\tag{11}
\]

The actual decoder posterior at this node is obtained by weighting `nu_z` with

\[
v(s)=w(z+s)/w(z)=e^{-zs-s^2/2}.
\]

On the hat support, (10) gives `e^-1<=v<=e` and `|v'|<=e(|z|+H_z)`. The covariance identity

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_z}(s,v(s))=
\tfrac12\mathbb E[(s-s')(v(s)-v(s'))]
\]

therefore implies

\[
\left|\mathbb E_{\Gamma_z}(X-z)-\mathbb E_{\nu_z}s\right|
\le e^2(|z|+H_z)H_z^2.
\tag{12}
\]

Together with (9), this yields the global interior bound

\[
|\mathbb E_{\Gamma_z}(X-z)|
\le C_1 J^{-2}(1+|z|)^5,
\qquad C_1=11\pi^2/3+4e^2\pi^2.
\tag{13}
\]

It is essential to include both mesh asymmetry and Gaussian-weight drift. Bounding either conditional displacement merely by the cell width would lose the second-order rate.

For `|t|<=1`,

\[
|f_t(x)|\le |t|(1+|x|)e^{|x|},\quad
|f_t'(x)|\le |t|e^{|x|},\quad
|f_t''(x)|\le |t|e^{|x|}.
\tag{14}
\]

The first inequality follows by integrating the derivative in the parameter from zero to `t`; it retains the crucial linear factor `|t|` even in the far tails.

Taylor's theorem and (13) show that at an interior node,

\[
\left|\mathbb E_{\Gamma_z}f_t(X)-f_t(z)\right|
\le C_2 |t|J^{-2}e^{|z|}(1+|z|)^5
\tag{15}
\]

for an absolute constant `C_2`: use (13) for the linear term and `E(X-z)^2<=H_z^2` for the quadratic term. The local supremum of the second derivative costs at most a factor `e` since `H_z<=1`.

Between two adjacent nodes, linear interpolation reproduces every affine function exactly. Its error on `f_t` is at most one half the local supremum of `|f_t''|` times the squared interval length. If `|x|<=T_J`, all participating nodes and their supports are interior. Combining this interpolation error with (15) gives

\[
|Tf_t(x)-f_t(x)|
\le C_3 |t|J^{-2}e^{|x|}(1+|x|)^5,
\qquad |x|\le T_J,
\tag{16}
\]

where `C_3` is absolute. Polynomial growth in `x`, rather than a worst-case power of the cutoff, is important: all the Gaussian integrals below are bounded independently of `J`.

## 4. Reversible tails, including the exterior-ray hats

The tails cannot simply be discarded under `P_t`: the channel must preserve `P_0` exactly. We use its actual reversible kernel.

First,

\[
T(e^{|\cdot|})(x)\le C_4 e^{|x|}
\quad\text{for every real }x,
\tag{17}
\]

with an absolute constant. For an interior hat, any two points of its support are less than one apart, so this is immediate. For the positive endpoint hat,

\[
\alpha_+\ge \overline\Phi(B),\qquad
\int \phi(y)e^y a_+(y)\,dy
\le e^{1/2}\overline\Phi(L-1).
\]

The standard elementary Mills inequalities, for `u>0`, are

\[
\frac{u}{1+u^2}\phi(u)\le\overline\Phi(u)\le\phi(u)/u.
\tag{18}
\]

The upper inequality follows by comparing `u` with the integration variable. Integration by parts gives `Q(u)=phi(u)/u-integral_u^infinity phi(v)/v^2 dv`, and bounding the integral by `Q(u)/u^2` gives the lower inequality. Since `B-L<=1/B`, `L-1>=B/2`, and `B>=3`, (18) yields

\[
\mathbb E_{\Gamma_+}e^Y
\le e^{1/2}\frac{\overline\Phi(L-1)}{\overline\Phi(B)}
\le4e\,e^B\le4e^2e^x
\quad\text{whenever }a_+(x)>0.
\tag{19}
\]

In the exponent calculation, `1/2+[B^2-(L-1)^2]/2 <= B+B(B-L)<=B+1`. The negative endpoint follows by reflection. This proves (17), for example with a larger constant than `4e^2`.

Second, hats are local at the central boundary. If a hat's decoder has any support in `|y|>T_J`, then its encoder support is entirely in `|x|>T_J-1`. This follows from the width bound in (9); the endpoint supports themselves start beyond `T_J`. Thus

\[
T(e^{|\cdot|}{\bf1}_{\{|\cdot|>T_J\}})(x)
\le C_4 e^{|x|}{\bf1}_{\{|x|>T_J-1\}}.
\tag{20}
\]

By Jensen and self-adjointness,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{|y|>T_J} \phi(y)e^{|y|}(Tf_t(y))^2\,dy
&\le \int\phi(x)f_t(x)^2
 T(e^{|\cdot|}{\bf1}_{\{|\cdot|>T_J\}})(x)\,dx\\
&\le C_4t^2\int_{|x|>T_J-1}
\phi(x)(1+|x|)^2e^{3|x|}\,dx.
\end{aligned}\tag{21}
\]

The same last integral bounds the direct `f_t^2` tail. Completing the square gives, for `u>=3`,

\[
\int_u^\infty (1+x)^2e^{3x}\phi(x)\,dx
=e^{9/2}\{(u+5)\phi(u-3)+17\overline\Phi(u-3)\}.
\tag{22}
\]

At `u=T_J-1=5+4sqrt(log J)`, `u-3=2+4sqrt(log J)` and its Gaussian exponential is at most `J^-8`. The polynomial factor is bounded by an absolute constant times `J^4`. Consequently both tails in (21) are bounded by an absolute constant times `t^2 J^-4`.

Finally,

\[
\chi^2(Q_t\Vert P_t)=
\int \phi(x)\frac{[Tf_t(x)-f_t(x)]^2}{L_t(x)}\,dx,
\qquad L_t(x)^{-1}\le e^{|x|+1/2}.
\tag{23}
\]

On the central region, (16) bounds this by

\[
C_3^2 e^{1/2}t^2J^{-4}
\int \phi(x)e^{3|x|}(1+|x|)^{10}\,dx,
\]

a finite absolute constant. On the complement, use `(Tf-f)^2<=2(Tf)^2+2f^2` and (21)-(22). This proves the chi-square part of (1) for large `J`. Cauchy-Schwarz gives its TV consequence.

For `J<J_0`, decode `P_0`. In this case `chi^2(P_0||P_t)=exp(t^2)-1<=(e-1)t^2`; enlarging the same universal `K` by `(e-1)J_0^4` proves (1) for all `J`. The theorem claims a finite universal constant, not an optimized numerical constant.

## 5. Tensorization and unequal weak axes

The inequality `KL(Q||P)<=chi^2(Q||P)` and product additivity give

\[
\operatorname{KL}(Q_\theta^{(d)}\Vert P_\theta^{(d)})
\le K J^{-4}\sum_i\theta_i^2.
\]

Pinsker proves (2). No independent-decision argument or assumed support estimator enters this step.

A useful anisotropic consequence is also dimension-uniform. Suppose `a_i<=1` are known positive axes of an ellipsoid

\[
\sum_i\theta_i^2/a_i^2\le1.
\tag{24}
\]

Keep only axes larger than a fixed multiple of `epsilon`. On a kept axis choose `J_i^2` a sufficiently large constant times `a_i/epsilon`; on every other axis decode an independent null Gaussian with no retained label. For kept axes use (1); on omitted axes use `KL(P_0||P_{theta_i})=theta_i^2/2`. Each scalar KL coefficient is bounded by a constant times `epsilon^2/a_i^2`, so (24) gives total KL at most `2epsilon^2`, after adjusting constants to obtain any requested TV margin. Hence

\[
B_{\rm cl}\le
\frac12\sum_{a_i>c\epsilon}\log_2(a_i/\epsilon)+O(k),
\qquad k=\#\{i:a_i>c\epsilon\}.
\tag{25}
\]

If the axes tend to zero, only finitely many are kept. Countable-product KL is the supremum of finite-prefix KL, so the same bound defines an infinite-sequence archive with procedural output. For exponential axes `a_i=A exp(-c i^p)`, `0<A<=1`, fixed `c,p>0`, put `H=log(A/epsilon)`. Power-sum asymptotics give

\[
B_{\rm cl}\le
\frac{p}{2(p+1)\log2}c^{-1/p}H^{1+1/p}
+o(H^{1+1/p}).
\tag{26}
\]

The unequal-needle lower bound in `../sparse_gaussian_converse/report.md` has the same leading sum, minus `O(k)`, and therefore matches this coefficient under the same archive interface. This report establishes the upper independently; it does not claim that reading the companion proof is an external correctness review.

## 6. Finite observation bits and procedural execution

The mathematical channel does not require an infinitely precise retained measurement. It has a version whose encoder uses only a finite quantized prefix of its observation and finitely many random bits, while its **ideal procedural decoder still preserves the continuous Gaussian exactly**. The distinction between a procedural real output and a finite floating-point number is essential.

**Rational nodes.** For the computable version, approximate all `z_j`, `1<=j<=J-1`, symmetrically by dyadics within `rho<=J^-5`, and compute a rational buffered cutoff `s` with `S_J-1/4<=s<=S_J-1/8`. Keep nodes by the decidable rational comparison `|z_j^rat|<=s`; do not ask an algorithm to decide an exact transcendental cutoff equality. The buffer and small approximation error leave all central hats in place, with outer node above `S_J-1`, and endpoint supports beyond `T_J`. The minimum gap is at least a constant times `1/J`, so order is unchanged; widths change by at most `2rho`, adjacent-width differences by at most `4rho`. Equations (9)-(16) survive after enlarging absolute constants. The maximal node magnitude remains `O(sqrt(log J))`. A table requires `O(J log J)` input-independent bits and can be computed in `J poly(log J)` bit operations using convergent elementary-function bounds. An implicit grid can replace the table if grid-generation time is charged. In the implicit version the cutoff indices can be found by `O(log J)` rational comparisons and a requested node can be generated in `poly(log J)` operations, so the expensive table is optional rather than hidden in the retained-bit bound.

**Finite encoder.** Fix `q_b(x)=2^-b floor(2^b x)` also for negative `x`, and saturate it at dyadic endpoints at least one beyond the outer nodes. Read this fixed-point prefix and evaluate the rational-grid hats there. On a neighboring-node interval, round the right-label probability down to a multiple of `2^-b` and use its complement for the left label; use the endpoint label with probability one outside the node range. There are only two possible labels. This defines another exact partition of unity, say `A_j`, and uses at most `b` random bits. Globally,

\[
\sum_j|A_j(x)-a_j(x)|\le C J2^{-b},
\tag{27}
\]

since the minimum hat width is bounded below by `c/J`. Away from a bounded interval slightly larger than the node range, the two encoders agree exactly. The decoder is now **defined from these actual finite-bit functions**,

\[
\Gamma_j^A(dy)=\phi(y)A_j(y)\,dy/\alpha_j^A.
\tag{28}
\]

Consequently `Q_0^A=P_0` is still exact. Reusing the old decoder after changing the encoder would not have this property.

Here is a quantitative stability bound sufficient for (1). For `|f|<=F`, total null joint-encoder discrepancy `eta=integral phi sum|A-a|`, and node range bounded by `S`, normalization and the two unnormalized joint measures give

\[
\int\phi(y)e^{|y|}|T_Af(y)-T_af(y)|\,dy
\le C F e^{S+2}\eta.
\tag{29}
\]

To see this, write the output signed measure as `sum_j Gamma_j b_j`, `b_j=integral phi a_j f`. The changes of `b_j` cost at most `F integral phi |A_j-a_j|` times `E_{Gamma_j^A}e^{|Y|}`. For decoder changes use

`alpha_j integral e^{|y|}|Gamma_j^A-Gamma_j| <= integral phi e^{|y|}|A_j-a_j| + |alpha_j^A-alpha_j| E_{Gamma_j^A}e^{|Y|}`.

Both exponential moments are at most `C e^{S+2}`; the endpoint proof is the same Mills-ratio argument as (19). Also `|T_Af-T_af|<=2F`, so (29) yields a weighted squared-error bound `C F^2 e^{S+2} eta`.

Apply this to `f_t` truncated at `|x|<=S+2`, where `F<=|t|(S+3)e^{S+2}`. Its omitted tails have weighted squared norm `O(t^2 J^-4)` by (22); positive Jensen and the analogue of (17) give the same bound after either operator. Thus choosing

\[
b\ge 5\log_2J+\frac{3(S+2)}{\log2}
+2\log_2(S+3)+C_5
\tag{30}
\]

retains `chi^2(Q_t^A||P_t)<=K' t^2/J^4` for another universal constant. In particular `b=O(log J)`. The extra precision is transient computation, not additional retained archive bits. If it is kept, it must be counted.

**Decoder without a normalizer table.** Sampling (28) needs no numerical evaluation of `alpha_j^A`. The floor-prefix encoder can extend a hat's support slightly beyond its ideal support, especially at a negative endpoint. Therefore use actual support endpoints, or the following explicit enclosing intervals. With `delta_b=2^-b`, an interior label at node `c_j` is supported in `[c_{j-1}-2delta_b,c_{j+1}+2delta_b]`. Propose uniformly on this expanded rational interval and accept with probability `exp[-(y^2-m^2)/2] A_j(y)`, where `m` is its point closest to zero. Gaussian density oscillation is bounded by (10), and the stepwise hat's average remains bounded away from zero, so acceptance is bounded below by an absolute constant. For the positive endpoint replace the ideal support start `L` by `L_A=L-2delta_b>=1`, propose `Y=L_A+E/L_A`, `E~Exp(1)`, and accept with probability `exp[-(Y-L_A)^2/2] A_j(Y)`. Its acceptance probability is `L_A alpha_j^A/phi(L_A)`, bounded below by an absolute constant using `alpha_j^A>=Q(B+2delta_b)` and the slightly enlarged bound `B(B-L_A)<=1+2B delta_b`. For the negative endpoint use the reflected **expanded** interval and proposal. This is an explicit rejection sampler for the actual reference-conditional density, including its exterior ray.

Under a computable-real/random-bit model, uniform real proposals and elementary exponential/logarithm acceptance tests can be executed with convergent rational bounds. An independent uniform acceptance threshold requires more precision only with probability proportional to that precision interval. Any requested `m`-bit output prefix therefore costs `poly(b+m)` expected bit operations per scalar, with `O(1)` expected proposals; finite encoding uses `poly(b)` work per scalar and `O(log J)` comparisons with a stored grid. Exact uniform real outputs are procedural bit streams, not completed infinite files. The observation-prefix specification, computable supplied `J`/nodes, and this output model are premises of the bit-complexity assertion.

For `d` coordinates, retained fixed-length indices cost at most `d ceil(log_2 J)` bits; processing can stream the coordinates and discard all temporary observations and random bits. A table costs `O(J log J)` **input-independent** stored bits separately, and time to acquire/read the `d` finite prefixes must also be charged. The theorem does not acquire an unknown Gaussian covariance, basis, active support, or model.

**Finite floating-point limitation.** If a program returns only finitely many floating-point numbers interpreted as exact reals, its output is supported on a countable set and has TV one from every continuous Gaussian. The exact continuous statement requires a continuous random source or a procedural random-real interpretation. Alternatively apply the same finite readout to both target and reconstruction: TV contracts, and the reference readout law is still preserved exactly. Replacing the reference sampler by an approximate floating-point routine can accumulate an error on inactive coordinates; no dimension-uniform claim about such a routine is made here.

## 7. Search for a joint sparse encoder and strongest comparators

No legal joint encoder invalidating the linear-in-ambient-dimension retained-bit conjecture was found. The companion converse now gives `log D >= (d/2)log(r/epsilon)-O(d)` even for the finite `2d` signed means, with arbitrary joint quantum/hybrid encoders. Its load-bearing mechanism is a bounded scale-sensitive proper score for each signed needle, transfer to one common null experiment, scalar entropy power, information summation across independent null coordinates, and the decoder POVM trace bound `I(X;Y)<=log D`. These are substantive restrictions on every legal joint encoder, rather than failure of one quantizer.

Concrete proposed escapes fail or change the interface:

* Keeping a random coordinate and regenerating the rest gives the mixture `(1/d)P_{r e_i}+(1-1/d)P_0`; error is `(1-1/d)[2Phi(r/2)-1]`, of order `r`, even granting an infinitely precise retained scalar.
* Projecting onto an `m`-dimensional known subspace and regenerating the orthogonal null noise gives `N(Proj theta,I)`. Its error is `2Phi(||(I-Proj)theta||/2)-1`. Since `sum_i ||(I-Proj)e_i||^2=d-m`, a worst needle loses at least `r sqrt(1-m/d)`. Making `epsilon/r` small forces `m` close to `d`; compressed sensing does not identify a weak active direction from this single noisy draw.
* A visible encoder given the active mean can reconstruct exactly with `2d+1` labels, showing why parameter-label counting is not a valid blind upper bound.
* Lossy Gaussian source coding or a high-dimensional joint codebook can improve constants and engineering performance. It cannot beat the leading retained-bit coefficient if the companion converse is correct. The product construction here does not instantiate a `J^d`-entry codebook.
* Shared independent randomness, variable fresh decoder coins, and analog quantum amplitudes are admitted by the converse. Uncharged input-dependent side records or an entangled receiver reference are different resource interfaces.

The mathematical lower bound is supplied in the companion report, not proved anew here. A counterexample to it would need to break the proper-loss transfer, the common-null distortion sum, or the information cap while respecting that exact interface.
