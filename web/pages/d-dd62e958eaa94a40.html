## Strongest exact claim

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a finite-dimensional matrix algebra with normalized trace, and let \(\Phi\) be a unital, trace-preserving, completely positive map that is self-adjoint on Hilbert–Schmidt space. If \(\Phi^2\) is entanglement breaking, then the entanglement-breaking map \(\Psi=\Phi^2\) obeys the exact Dirichlet comparison
\[
I-\Psi \preceq 2(I-\Phi).
\]
Here \(\preceq\) is the quadratic-form order on Hilbert–Schmidt space. This needs no self-compatibility assumption.

A concrete unbounded-dimension family is supplied by the symplectic-covariant PPT channels in Prudhoe, *Entanglement Breaking Structure of Cartan-Covariant Quantum Channels* (arXiv:2501.03959v3). Its extreme channel, for every even \(d\ge4\), is
\[
\Phi_0(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(X)I+X+V X^\top V^*}{d+2},
\]
where \(V^\top=-V\) is a symplectic unitary. The source proves that \(\Phi_0\) is PPT and not EB, while \(\Phi_0^2\) is EB. Thus \(\Phi_0\) gives a nontrivial EB Dirichlet-rounding example with constant 2 in every even dimension, conditional on the source’s PPT² derivation.

A broader consequence uses Park, *Every PPT channel has finite entanglement breaking index* (arXiv:2608.13551v2): for any bistochastic PPT map in the source’s class \(\mathcal{DSP}_2\), \(\Phi^3\) is EB. If also Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, set \(P=\Phi^2\). Then \(P\) is a bistochastic self-adjoint positive contraction and \(P^2=\Phi^4\) is EB, so \(I-P^2\preceq2(I-P)\). This gives c=2 after a two-step coarse-graining. It does not give a uniform one-step c for arbitrary PPT channels; Park’s general finite index has no channel-independent bound.

## Complete derivation of the power-to-Dirichlet lemma

Put \(A=I-\Phi\). Self-adjointness gives
\[
2(I-\Phi)-(I-\Phi^2)=I-2\Phi+\Phi^2=(I-\Phi)^2=A^*A\succeq0.
\]
The CP and EB properties are stable under composition, so \(\Phi^2\) is an EB channel by hypothesis and is an admissible positive self-adjoint comparison map. For the Park corollary, \(\Phi^3\) is EB and \(\Phi^4=\Phi\circ\Phi^3\) is EB; apply the same identity to \(P=\Phi^2\). These identities do not require \(\Phi\) to have nonnegative spectrum. The only input to the operator-order proof is self-adjointness.

For the symplectic example, \(V\) can be taken real with \(V^\top=-V\), \(V^\top V=I\), and \(V^2=-I\). Define \(\Theta(X)=V X^\top V^\top\) and \(R(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I\). Then \(\Theta^2=I\), \(\Theta\) is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, \(R\) is self-adjoint, and \(\Phi_0=(R+I+\Theta)/(d+2)\) is self-adjoint. Since \(\Phi_0^2\) is EB by Prudhoe’s symplectic PPT² theorem, the identity above proves the claimed c=2 comparison. The source already gives the channel and EB-square theorem; the Dirichlet corollary is the derived contribution here.
