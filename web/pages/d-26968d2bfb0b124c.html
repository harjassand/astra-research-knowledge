# Evolutionary control: can a treatment pulse reveal hidden resistance?

**Status:** attacked candidate; conditional algebra reconstructed; **reject as a novelty claim**. The pulse identity below is exact for a deliberately narrow two-clone model, but input/output identifiability and first-cycle inference are already primary-literature results. The potentially removable bottleneck—obtaining clone composition from aggregate tumor burden without tissue/ctDNA—is also too weak for rare clones at plausible measurement noise. This remains useful as a falsifiable assay-design calculation, not a new therapy result.

## Scoped question and interface

Can total tumor burden, with one deliberately chosen short treatment pulse, identify the resistant fraction well enough to improve a future control decision without clone-specific sequencing?

Use two non-switching clones, sensitive `S` and resistant `R`, with total burden `N=S+R`. On a short challenge where density feedback is negligible and the drug concentration is spatially uniform,

\[
\dot S=(r-k_Su)S,\qquad \dot R=(r-k_Ru)R,
\]

where `u(t)` is known administered exposure; `k_S,k_R` are known from independent calibration; `r` is a common untreated rate; and `f=R(0)/N(0)`. The sole runtime observation is a noisy aggregate `Y(t)=N(t)+noise`. The candidate protocol has baseline and post-pulse measurements, and applies constant dose `u>0` for duration `\tau`.

This model excludes competition beyond the low-density common growth approximation, mutation, reversible phenotype switching, metastases with unequal shedding, drug pharmacokinetics, and clone-specific tumor-marker production. It is a model interface, not a description of nature.

## Reconstructed result

Under no treatment, `N(t)=N(0)e^{rt}` for every `f`; passive aggregate burden cannot identify composition in this model. After the pulse,

\[
\frac{N(\tau)}{N(0)e^{r\tau}}=(1-f)e^{-k_Su\tau}+f e^{-k_Ru\tau}.
\]

If `k_S != k_R`, then

\[
\widehat f=\frac{N(\tau)/(N(0)e^{r\tau})-e^{-k_Su\tau}}
{e^{-k_Ru\tau}-e^{-k_Su\tau}}
\]

recovers the exact fraction with noiseless readings. If `k_S=k_R`, the input has no compositional contrast and the state is unobservable from this output for every pulse length and dose. This is a direct calculation, not a theorem about cancer populations.

For additive Gaussian measurement error with standard deviation `sigma` on the post-pulse aggregate, treating the baseline `N(0)` and `r` as known, the local Fisher information for `f` is

\[
I_f=\frac{N(0)^2e^{2r\tau}}{\sigma^2}
       (e^{-k_Ru\tau}-e^{-k_Su\tau})^2.
\]

If the post-pulse measurement error is `eta` times the untreated reference burden (so `sigma=eta N(0)e^{r\tau}`), the delta-method standard error is approximately

\[
\operatorname{SE}(\widehat f)\simeq
\frac{\eta}{|k_S-k_R|u\tau}
\]

when `|k_S-k_R|u\tau` is small. Thus identifiability is not the limiting condition; finite measurement precision, clone-response calibration, challenge exposure and sampling delay are.

**Acquisition/control bill:** two aggregate measurements (baseline and post-pulse); estimate/validate `k_S,k_R` and `r` in a functional assay; clinical or organoid exposure `u\tau`; the waiting time `\tau`; and any safety cost of the challenge. If response parameters are not known, the displayed one-pulse formula does not identify `f`; additional inputs and measurements are needed. A direct ctDNA/biopsy assay is a stronger composition comparator where a validated clone marker exists, but has its own assay, shedding and sampling costs.

## Rare-clone stress test and falsifier

For a fully resistant clone (`k_R=0`) and a sensitive clone (`k_S=k`), the relative signal between an all-sensitive tumor and a tumor with resistant fraction `f` is

\[
\Delta=f(1-e^{-ku\tau})\le f.
\]

At `f=0.01`, even a saturating pulse changes the total-burden signal by at most 1%; at `f=0.001`, at most 0.1%. A single reading with 1% relative error therefore cannot even separate the 1%-resistant and all-sensitive cases at 2 standard errors, irrespective of pulse duration. For a genuinely short pulse with `ku\tau=0.1`, the 1%-clone signal is only `0.095%`. This makes the assay weak precisely in the low-frequency regime where early warning matters. More measurements can increase precision, but add imaging/assay and treatment-exposure costs; they do not defeat the per-reading signal bound without additional information. Baseline measurement noise, uncertainty in `r`, and calibration error also worsen the displayed bound.

**Pre-registered falsifier of practical utility:** in a barcoded co-culture/organoid system with a calibrated two-clone response contrast, blinded resistant fractions from 0.1% to 10%, and aggregate imaging noise measured before analysis, test whether the pulse estimate attains absolute fraction error `<=0.02` at the intended pulse AUC while using no more measurements than the strongest aggregate-only response-kinetics baseline. Fail if its held-out RMSE is no better than that baseline or if any benefit requires pulse AUC that materially reduces viable burden or selects additional phenotypic states. The prediction from the formula is failure below the noise-adjusted threshold `f(1-e^{-ku\tau}) < z\eta` (for a `z`-sigma distinction from zero); it is not a clinical efficacy prediction.

**Counterexamples to the model:** resistance is induced during drug exposure; resistant cells re-sensitize off drug; drug sensitivity is continuous rather than two-valued; the `k` values differ across lesions; burden proxy secretion/shedding differs by clone; or density/spatial competition changes during the probe. Any of these can make the one-pulse estimate biased even when its algebra is correct. Spatially varying ctDNA shedding has been modeled as a source of biased clonal profiles, and observed liquid-biopsy samples can represent lesions unequally.

## Prior-art attack and novelty boundary

1. **Structural identifiability from total burden is already published.** Greene, Sanchez-Tapia & Sontag formulate treatment as the input and total volume as the only output, then prove structural identifiability of the parameters in a two-state model with drug-induced and spontaneous transition. They also cite explicit treatment protocols for recovering the model's observables. Their model assumes, among other things, a known two-state mechanism, control-affine dose response, a pure-sensitive initial state in the identifiability section, and no explicit pharmacokinetics. This substantially overlaps the proposed “pulse reveals resistance” mechanism. Their result is structural identifiability, not finite-noise experimental utility or validation in patient tumors.
   - Greene, J. M., C. Sanchez-Tapia, and E. D. Sontag (2020), [Mathematical Details on a Cancer Resistance Model](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2020.00501), especially model assumptions in Sections 2 and 7.1.

2. **Estimating evolutionary properties from the first adaptive cycle is also prior art.** Gallaher et al. use response/regrowth timing in 16 mCRPC patients plus a multiscale model; they report cycle times depend on metastatic sizes, resistance, and turnover. They conclude that first-cycle observation can reveal disease features, while also acknowledging model omissions and limited data. Thus a proposal to infer resistant fraction from a designed first pulse/cycle is not independently novel.
   - Gallaher, J. et al. (2023), [Intermetastatic and Intrametastatic Heterogeneity Shapes Adaptive Therapy Cycling Dynamics](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-22-2558).

3. **Bulk two-compartment adaptive rules and spatial failure modes are established.** Strobl et al. compare the 50%-response/return-to-baseline policy to continuous therapy in an on-lattice sensitive/resistant model and fit cycling dynamics to 65 patients' PSA records. Their simulations show outcomes depend on initial resistant fraction and spatial arrangement; spatially separated resistance nests can escape, and some stochastic realizations favor continuous treatment. They study a model, not a prospective biomarker-guided control trial.
   - Strobl, M. A. R. et al. (2022), [Spatial structure impacts adaptive therapy by shaping intra-tumoral competition](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-022-00110-x).

4. **Longitudinal ctDNA already measures and forecasts selected resistance mechanisms.** PROSPECT-C combined serial cfDNA, matched biopsies, imaging, and evolutionary modeling in anti-EGFR-treated metastatic colorectal cancer, reporting pre-treatment RAS-pathway alterations and patient-specific forecasts of time to treatment failure. Spatial/shedding models warn that plasma clone fractions may not equal tumor cell fractions.
   - Khan, K. H. et al. (2018), [Longitudinal liquid biopsy and mathematical modelling of clonal evolution forecast waiting time to treatment failure in PROSPECT-C](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-17-0891).
   - Rachman, T. et al. (2024), [Modeling the Effect of Spatial Structure on Solid Tumor Evolution and Circulating Tumor DNA Composition](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers16050844).

5. **Nature-level adaptive control remains context-dependent.** The phase 1b prostate study is feasibility evidence, not randomized confirmation across tumor types. The model here cannot justify clinical dose changes or assert that adaptive therapy is appropriate.
   - [Phase 1b Adaptive Androgen Deprivation Therapy Trial in Metastatic Castration Sensitive Prostate Cancer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9656891/).

## Strongest baseline and candidate verdict

For this exact low-density, two-clone interface, the strongest non-molecular baseline is a richer aggregate response-kinetics fit using several on/off observations, as in first-cycle adaptive-therapy analyses. Where the resistance marker is known and the plasma/tissue assay is informative, clone-specific longitudinal ctDNA is stronger. The pulse has not been shown to dominate either under equal sampling, measurement, and exposure budgets.

**Verdict:** principled rejection as a new evolutionary-control advance. The only positive result is the elementary conditional inversion and its cost/precision boundary. The load-bearing biological gap is not solved: whether a stable, known differential response signature for the clinically relevant resistant states exists and remains stable across space, plasticity, and treatment. The most useful next test is the blinded co-culture pulse experiment above, but only if a partner line finds a disease setting with no validated clonal marker and an affordable non-destructive aggregate sensor. Otherwise close this branch rather than repackage adaptive therapy.
