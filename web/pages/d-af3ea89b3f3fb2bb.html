CONVENTION AND RESULT
Ground |g> has single-spin Jz=-1/2, excited |e> has+1/2. Let
 Jz=sum_i (|e><e|-|g><g|)_i/2, J_-=sum_i |g><e|_i,
 E=Jz+N/2, R=J_+J_-.
The evolution is dot(rho)=Gamma D[J_-](rho), Gamma>0,
 D[L](rho)=L rho Ldagger-(1/2){Ldagger L,rho}.
Thus L=J_- means Gamma1; L=sqrt(Gamma)J_- has this convention. Rates are
Gamma times the ladder eigenvalues, not twice those rates. If the jump is
normalized by1/sqrtN instead, replace Gamma by the actual coefficient Gamma/N.

For any initial state define its conserved total-spin weights q_b, with
j=N/2-b, b=0,...,floor(N/2), and beta=sum_b b q_b. If beta>0, the state is
NOT fully separable at every sufficiently large FINITE time. In particular
every full-rank, initially fully separable state of N>=2 qubits becomes
entangled. This includes every admitted finite-parameter thermal state in the
optimal theorem, on the whole Hilbert space. It does not claim genuine
N-partite entanglement or a lower bound on entanglement depth.

1. A WITNESS FOR ALL FULLY SEPARABLE STATES
For a product of possibly mixed one-qubit states put p_i=<|e><e|_i> and
c_i=<|g><e|_i>. Positivity gives |c_i|^2<=p_i(1-p_i). Therefore
 <R>=sum_i[p_i-|c_i|^2]+|sum_i c_i|^2
      >=sum_i p_i^2>=(sum_i p_i)^2/N=<E>^2/N.
For a convex mixture of product states, average this inequality and apply
Jensen to the excitation means. Thus EVERY fully separable state satisfies
 <R> >= <E>^2/N.                                           (1)
Different product factors are allowed; no iid or maximal-spin premise is used.
Violation detects failure of full separability. A fixed linear witness useful
below is W_beta=R-(2beta/N)E+(beta^2/N)I: (1) implies <W_beta>>=0 on SEP,
because <W_beta>>=<(<E>-beta)^2/N.

2. CONSERVED SECTORS AND POPULATION DYNAMICS
Schur decomposition is direct_sum_j V_j tensor M_j. Collective J_-,Jz act
on V_j and as identity on multiplicity M_j. Each sector projector commutes
with the generator, so its total weight q_b is conserved. The joint diagonal
populations at j,m obey the autonomous pure-death chain, even if the initial
state has coherences or arbitrary multiplicity operators. Tracing the
multiplicity yields the same scalar chain; coherences cannot enter R or E.

Write K=2j, k=m+j=0,...,K. The transition k to k-1 has rate
 Gamma lambda_k, lambda_k=k(K-k+1), k>=1.
The dark level k0 has R0 and E=b. Hence <E(t)>>=beta for all times and
 <R(t)> tends to0, <E(t)> tends to beta.                    (2)
These expectation claims hold for ANY initial state with beta>0. The precise
limiting density can retain dark/multiplicity coherences for a general input;
no diagonal-density assumption is needed for (2) or the witness.

For a finite-parameter full-rank thermal state, every nonzero sector projector
has strictly positive weight. At N>=2 a lower-spin sector exists, with b>=1.
Thus beta>0 automatically. Maximal-spin-supported states instead have beta0;
this proof draws no entanglement conclusion for them.

3. SHARPER UNIFORM ABSORPTION AND EXPLICIT FINITE TIME
In a nonzero-spin sector K>=1, every nonzero ladder rate satisfies
 lambda_k-K=(k-1)(K-k)>=0.
Absorption from initial level k requires at mostK independent exponential
holding times. In dimensionless time tau=Gamma t, its absorption time T has
 E[e^(T/2)]<= [K/(K-1/2)]^k<=[1-1/(2K)]^(-K)<=2.
The last inequality is Bernoulli: (1-1/(2K))^K>=1-K/(2K)=1/2.
At k0 absorption already occurred; K0 is wholly dark. Markov's inequality gives
 P(not yet dark at tau)<=2 exp(-tau/2), uniformly in K,N and the initial level.
The root's coarser2^N exp(-tau/2) bound is also valid, but is unnecessary.

The maximum emission eigenvalue in spin j is
 M_j=floor((2j+1)^2/4)=j(j+1)+1_{Nodd}/4.
All j share the parity of N; this formula also gives M_00. Put
 C=sum_b q_b M_j=<J^2>_initial+1_{Nodd}/4.
Then, for ANY initial state,
 <R(t)> <=2 C exp(-Gamma t/2).                            (3)
If C0 and beta>0, the state is already dark and violates (1) at every time.
Otherwise every
 t>max{0,(2/Gamma)log[2N C/beta^2]}                        (4)
has <R(t)><beta^2/N<=<E(t)>^2/N, and <W_beta><0. Thus finite-time
entanglement is proved analytically, with an explicit bound valid at ALL
later times. For an initially SEP input with beta>0, C>=beta^2/N, so the
logarithm in (4) is positive. No limiting-state inference alone is used.

For comparison C<=floor((N+1)^2/4), so (4) is stronger than the supplied
coarse sufficient bound (2/Gamma)log[N(N+1)^2 2^(N-2)/beta^2]. Both use
the same D[L] convention. Observed photon intensity is Gamma<R>; the
separability inequality is intensity/Gamma >= remaining-excitation^2/N.

4. SCALAR THERMAL SECTOR WEIGHTS AND COST
For rho0 proportional to exp[(alpha J^2-delta Jz^2)/N+hJz], let
 j_b=N/2-b, d_b=binom(N,b)-binom(N,b-1), binom(N,-1)=0.
The multiplicity d_b follows by subtracting the dimensions of adjacent Jz
weight spaces. Define positive finite sums
 A_b=d_b exp[alpha j_b(j_b+1)/N]
           sum_(k=0)^(N-2b) exp[-delta(k-j_b)^2/N+h(k-j_b)],
 Z=sum_b A_b, q_b=A_b/Z, beta=sum_b b A_b/Z,
 C=sum_b M_(j_b) A_b/Z.                                   (5)
These are O(N^2) scalar terms, with exact integer multiplicities and no2^N
matrix construction. Known computable parameters permit directed exponential
enclosures and normalized positive sums; no runtime claim for huge parameter
values or an uncharged real oracle is inferred. For the thermal input the
dark limit is sum_b q_b |j_b,-j_b><j_b,-j_b| tensor I_(d_b)/d_b, embedded
in the Schur basis. Formula (5) retains every sector and multiplicity.

One conservative explicit lower bound is beta>=1-q_0>=q_1>0. If
B=alpha(N+2)/4+|delta|N/4+|h|N/2 bounds the exponent's operator norm,
then q_1>= (N-1)^2 2^(-N) exp(-2B), N>=2. Thus the onset cost is bounded
in numerical parameter magnitudes but need not be polynomial in their bit
lengths. Example N2,delta0,h0: beta=1/(3e^alpha+1), C=6e^alpha/(3e^alpha+1),
and (4)'s logarithmic argument is24e^alpha(3e^alpha+1). The sufficient
dimensionless time is asymptotic to4alpha+O(1); binary alpha may itself be
exponential in its description length. No free physical-time gate is claimed.

5. MAXIMALLY MIXED INPUT: CONSTANT UNIVERSAL ONSET
For I/2^N, q_b=d_b(2j_b+1)/2^N. Adding a mixed qubit sends j to j+1/2
with probability(j+1)/(2j+1), and to j-1/2 with probability j/(2j+1).
The spin defect b stays fixed on the first branch and rises by1 on the second.
Consequently beta_(N+1)-beta_N=1/2-(1/2)E_N[1/(2j+1)]. But
 E_N[1/(2j+1)]=sum_b d_b/2^N=binom(N,floorN/2)/2^N<=1/2, N>=1.
The last central-binomial ratio starts at1/2, decreases from even to the
following odd N, and is unchanged from odd to the following even N. Thus
 beta_N>=(N-1)/4, since beta_10.
Also <J^2>=3N/4, so C=(3N+1_{Nodd})/4. The argument in (4) is <=96:
for evenN it is <=24N^2/(N-1)^2<=96; for oddN>=3 it is
<=8N(3N+1)/(N-1)^2<=60 (this rational function decreases for N>1).
Therefore t=10/Gamma suffices for EVERY N>=2. The exact seven-term bound
exp5>sum_(r=0)^6 5^r/r!=16289/144>96 proves strict violation. This is a
uniform physical-time upper bound with the declared Gamma normalization,
not a simulation benchmark or a claim about all other thermal parameters.

6. EXACT N2 CORROBORATION
At Gamma1 and rho0=I4/4, the singlet stays at1/4. In the triplet,
 p_ee=e^(-2t)/4, p_sym1=(1+2t)e^(-2t)/4,
 p_gg=3/4-(1+t)e^(-2t)/2.
Hence <R>=(1+t)e^(-2t), <E>=1/4+(3+2t)e^(-2t)/4.
At t3, exp6>sum_(r=0)^5 6^r/r!=899/5>128 gives
<R><1/32=(1/4)^2/2<=<E>^2/2. The limiting density is

These exact tiny identities corroborate the all-N derivation; they do not
