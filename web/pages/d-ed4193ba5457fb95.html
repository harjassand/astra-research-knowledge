T14 — transported-chart decoder for an explicit good qLTC
Version: v1 (draft frozen 2026-10-09; all unresolved gates retained)
Status: source-reported code family; internally derived one-sided decoder reduction; full CSS decoder and claimed capability unresolved. Not a validated discovery.

0. Objective and current frontier

The campaign target was an explicit decoder for a constant fraction of adversarial Pauli data errors with adversarial noisy syndrome and near-linear resources, on a precisely specified finite-degree asymptotically good qLDPC family. That target is not novel as stated. Gu–Tang–Caha–Choe–He–Kubica (arXiv:2306.12470v2, Thm. 3.4 and §§4.4–4.5) give sequential O(N) and parallel single-shot decoders for quantum Tanner codes; Nguyen–Pattison (arXiv:2411.03632v2, Thm. 3.3, Props. 6.23 and 6.41) give O(N) sequential single-shot decoders for their cubical-complex qLDPC families. The proposed new target is narrower and stronger in its code-family axis: lift such a decoder to the unconditional, explicit, constant-rate, constant-relative-distance, constant-soundness binary CSS qLTC family of Gay–Granha Jeronimo (arXiv:2609.20780v1, Thms. 2.4.2–2.4.3), whose cosheaf charts need not agree across different anchors.

The exact possible contribution is a gauge-covariant decoding theorem for compatible cosheaves with nontrivial anchor transitions, applied to that family. This document does not claim that theorem. It separates the subproof that follows from local data from the currently missing cross-anchor/transpose decoder lemma.

1. Explicit code family and interfaces

Take the GGJ construction with t=4, k=2, F=F_{2^s}, and fixed local parameters selected in the order of §§13.1–13.2 of arXiv:2609.20780v1. For each direction i, fix n_i=q_i+1, endpoint dimensions m_{i,b}, and a compatible base-code cosheaf F_ν on the arithmetic cubical quotient X_ν. The index ν grows the quotient; t, n_i, s, all stalk dimensions, and all local search spaces remain fixed. Theorem 13.2.1 fixes λ≤2/√q_- and λL(4,2,ρ,K)<1/2; Theorem 2.4.3 gives a binary CSS family with N_ν=s dim_F C_2=Θ(|X_ν(2)|), positive rate, relative distance and specified-check soundness, and constant check weight and qubit incidence. The geometry is (n_1,…,n_4)-regular. Each parallel-face graph component is connected bipartite with normalized nontrivial spectrum at most λ; the inactive parity fibers are equal-size components (Defs. 2.1.4, Lemma 9.3.2, Cor. 9.3.3).

The cosheaf has stalk F(f)=F^{d_f}, incidence maps R_{gf}:F(f)→F(g), R_{ff}=I, R_{hg}R_{gf}=R_{hf}. Its cochain spaces and differential are C^j=⊕_{f∈X(j)}F(f) and (δ^j x)(g)=Σ_{f≤g, dim f=j}R_{gf}x(f); δ^{j+1}δ^j=0 (GGJ Def. 2.2.1, §2.3, Lemma 2.3.1). The ordered F-basis β identifies each F-block with s d_f binary qubits. H_X=bin_β(δ^2), H_Z=bin_β(δ^1)^T. A physical Pauli error is represented by x∈C^2 for X errors or z∈C^2 for Z errors. In the adversarial noisy-syndrome model, the decoder receives

    σ_X = δ^2 x + m_X ∈ C^3,
    σ_Z = (δ^1)^T z + m_Z ∈ C^1,

where errors and measurement faults are arbitrary, fixed before decoding; no stochastic or independence assumption is made. Output corrections x̂,ẑ induce binary Pauli corrections bin_β(x̂),bin_β(ẑ). The desired guarantee is: for constants η_data,η_syn,γ>0 independent of ν, if |x|_R≤η_data N and |m_X|_blocks≤η_syn|X(2)| then |x+x̂|_{R,bin}≤γ|m_X|_blocks·B; and the corresponding bound for z,m_Z, with O(N) worst-case time and O(N) space. Here |·|_R is minimum block support modulo the applicable stabilizer image; B=max_f s d_f is a fixed block-size constant. Converting to physical weights costs at most B: |u|_{R,blocks}≤|u|_{R,bin}≤B|u|_{R,blocks}; input block support is at most physical bit support and syndrome block support is at most the number of faulty syndrome bits. All constants, including B, are fixed with ν.

2. Intrinsic local-chart data and gauge transitions

GGJ Def. 2.2.2 supplies, for every face f, an anchor vertex x_f≤f, simultaneous isomorphisms Ψ_{f,g}:F(g)→⊗_{i∈codir(g)}U_{f,i} for all g≥f, and encoders E_{f,i}, with the incidence identity (2.8). It explicitly requires no agreement between charts with different anchors beyond their intertwining the same incidence maps. For v≤f≤g, write Ψ^v_g for the chart based at v and define the anchor transition on the actual stalk F(g) by

    G^{v,w}_g := Ψ^w_g (Ψ^v_g)^{-1} : chart_v(g) → chart_w(g)

whenever v,w≤g and both charts are being compared. The actual incidence square commutes:

    G^{v,w}_h A^v_{h g} = A^w_{h g} G^{v,w}_g,
    A^v_{h g}:=Ψ^v_h R_{h g}(Ψ^v_g)^{-1}.

For a fixed shared stalk, transitions compose strictly, G^{w,u}_g G^{v,w}_g=G^{v,u}_g, by cancellation of Ψ^w_g. This removes any holonomy around a loop that stays at that same stalk. It does not by itself identify chart coordinates at distinct faces, since R_{hg} need not be invertible; nor does the displayed square show that local tensor-code projections, local syndrome preimages, or decoder tie-breaking commute with G. These are the precise transport obligations; “the maps are functorial” alone does not prove the X/transpose decoder.

GGJ’s anchored-array construction is a concrete possible replacement. For 0≤r≤p≤t, set A_{r,p}(f)=⊕_{u∈X(p), f≤u}F(u)^*, with horizontal (Δa)(g)[u]=Σ_{f<g}a(f)[u] and vertical (Ta)(f)[v]=Σ_{u>v}R^T_{uv}a(f)[u]. Theorem/ Lemma 7.1.1 proves Δ²=T²=0, ΔT=TΔ and anchor-support bounds from the cubical interval identities and cosheaf functor law. K(f)=ker(T_f^t) defines a cosheaf K, whose differential is Δ restricted to ⊕K(f) (Lemma 7.1.2); Theorem 7.2.1 supplies its compatible charts from the dual local codes. These equations do establish a gauge-covariant bicomplex on anchored arrays. They do not yet establish a bounded-noise decoding map from observed B_2 z to a K-syndrome whose local blocks and decoded correction map back to a correction of z. That is the open bridge below.

3. One-sided local-flip decoder that the source ingredients support

For the X-error side, the Nguyen–Pattison sequential Z-syndrome algorithm is intrinsic once its hypotheses are phrased on actual local cochain complexes. Given s=δ^2x+m_X, initialize σ=s. At each step, enumerate every vertex v and every w∈C^2 supported on X_{≥v}(2), and choose any with |σ|_b−|σ+δ^2w|_b>|δ^2w|_b·0 (strict decrease); update σ←σ+δ^2w and x̂←x̂+w. Stop when no such w exists. The supplied syndrome determines this procedure; the unknown x is used only in the proof. Since each chosen correction strictly decreases integer block syndrome weight, there are O(|X(3)|)=O(N) flips. Precompute local candidates and update only the O(1)-radius neighborhood affected by v; with all local alphabets, t and n_i fixed, candidate count and per-update cost are O(1), giving O(N) deterministic worst-case time and O(N) memory.

For noiseless and noisy correctness, N-P Prop. 6.23’s proof uses: (i) co-locally minimal representatives; (ii) local robust expansion in every upward star (their Claim 6.20); (iii) purely geometric neighbor/opposite-face counts (Claim 6.18); (iv) the component-wise spectral estimate for the cubical walk (Lemma 6.16); and (v) a small-set co-boundary inequality (Lemma 6.22). No comparison of different anchors is needed in these Z-side inequalities: each local flip and each robustness invocation takes place in one upward star, expressed in its actual stalks. GGJ Thm. 4.0.1 gives for a star at an ℓ-face

    |δ_v z|_b ≥ n_- κ_{4-ℓ,2-ℓ}(ρ,K) min_y |z+δ_v y|_b,

and the dual version is available from Thm. 7.2.1. The factor n_- converts to NP’s uniform-degree convention n_+ with κ_*:=K^{-1} min_{0≤ℓ≤2}κ_{4-ℓ,2-ℓ}(ρ,K)>0. Geometry is anisotropic but n_+/n_-≤K; each bounded path-count constant can use n_+, while each local lower bound uses n_-; equal-size parity components give a fixed density parameter r_*>0 (at worst 2^{-4}). Thus NP’s Z proof appears to extend with changed fixed constants. This is an internal proof-transfer assessment, not an independently checked theorem.

For clarity, the exact NP noisy-syndrome inequalities at t=4,k=2 become, with

    a := 8^{-16} κ_*^3 − λ,
    b := 8^{-16} (κ_*/2)^3 − λ,

the requirements a>0, b>0 and

    |m_X|_b ≤ (n_+/4)· b²/(4n_+)^4 · r_* |X(2)|,
    2·4 n_+ |x|_b + 2|m_X|_b
       < (n_+/4)· a/(4n_+)^4 · r_* |X(2)|
          · min{a²/2^6, b}.

The output residual is bounded by 2/[n_+ b]·|m_X|_b in block support. These are positive constant-fraction data and syndrome tolerances, and a constant noise-amplification factor, because all parameters are fixed as ν grows. GGJ selects ρ and hence κ_* before local lengths; increasing the fixed local parameter makes λ≤2/√q_- arbitrarily small without changing κ_*. Therefore a,b>0 are satisfiable at fixed finite degree. The exact NP statement is Prop. 6.23, arXiv:2411.03632v2, PDF pp. 88–89; GGJ data are Def. 2.2.2, Thm. 4.0.1, Thm. 7.2.1, and §§13.1–13.2.

This is the most concrete supported new result: a candidate O(N) noisy-syndrome local-flip decoder for one Pauli sector on the explicit GGJ good-qLTC family, with the above explicit constants after the anisotropic graph-count substitution. It is not enough for the original full quantum decoding objective, because the other Pauli sector remains.

4. Exact obstruction for the transpose/X-syndrome sector

The needed algorithm must decode σ_Z=B_2 z+m_Z, B_2=(δ^1)^T. NP Algorithm 5, Props. 6.36 and 6.41, resolves this on their tensor-product cubical complex by (a) making local syndrome preimages at vertices, (b) assembling a globally consistent syndrome-explanation sequence, (c) unencoding a top-level local tensor code to a dual cochain, (d) calling the Z decoder on that dual complex, and (e) reverse-assembling a global correction. For the noisy proof, see NP Prop. 6.41, PDF pp. 110–113.

The vulnerable identities are not the abstract chain identities alone. In NP’s proof, the compatibility of the reconstructed local values at different anchors is used in Claim 6.34 and the last step of Claim 6.40 (PDF pp. 102–109); the top-code unencoding/re-encoding and preservation of a kernel image use the same global tensor factors and their fixed directionwise codes (notably Eq. (197) and the final Eq. (210), pp. 106–109). These imply that the assembled output is one global cochain and that the extra term has zero original coboundary, so the correction is homologically equivalent to z. GGJ allows anchor charts to differ; its cosheaf functor law gives the incidence square in §2, but does not yet show that those local inverse/unencode operations are natural under every G^{v,w}, or that the transformed residual has zero B-boundary. This is an exact missing implication, not a vague appeal to “holonomy.”

Required transport lemma (not proved here). For every admissible GGJ compatible-chart cosheaf and every nested sequence of local syndrome-explanation arrays, define local operations only in actual stalks using Ψ at the anchor and transport all outputs along actual R maps. Then:

    (T1) all overlap restrictions agree as actual stalk vectors, or their disagreement is supported on a set of blocks bounded by C|m_Z|;
    (T2) the assembled top-level local word belongs to the local dual-code image in every actual stalk, so it admits a single K-cochain lift;
    (T3) after applying the noisy K-decoder, reverse transport preserves B_2-cycles, and the resulting global residual satisfies
         |z+ẑ|_{R,b} ≤ C' |m_Z|_b;
    (T4) each lift, chart conversion, and neighborhood repair has O(N) worst-case cost with constants fixed by t,n_i,s and the local codes.

GGJ Lemma 7.1.1–Theorem 7.2.1 give candidate algebraic infrastructure for T1–T3: Δ,T form a commuting bicomplex, local transpose exactness holds, and K carries the dual compatible charts. What is still missing is an explicit map from (B_2 z+m_Z) to a noisy δ_K-syndrome and back, including exact support inflation. In particular, the error vector z is not supplied to the decoder; “copy z to anchored arrays” is proof-only and cannot be an operational input step. A constructive repair must start from the observed σ_Z alone and show every locally chosen right inverse has gauge-compatible overlap behavior. Without this, applying the one-sided decoder to K would be a circular/inaccessible reduction.

5. Alternatives considered and decisive tests

Alternative A: force all GGJ charts to share one global tensor basis. This is invalid as a general inference: Def. 2.2.2 explicitly imposes no cross-anchor agreement. A theorem can use the arithmetic quotient’s specific transition functions, but must calculate them and show decoder covariance; a global trivialization is not supplied.

Alternative B: use only constant soundness/qLTC. Insufficient. qLTC gives a weight-versus-syndrome inequality, not an operational noisy-syndrome decoder; it does not provide the missing transpose lift.

Alternative C: apply NP directly to K. K has dual local codes and a decoder candidate, but one still needs a supplied-syndrome reduction from B_2 z+m_Z to δ_K error syndrome plus a reverse map. Theorem 7.0.1 is a distance/soundness transfer, not a decoding algorithm.

Alternative D: assert all chart-transition loops are trivial. At one fixed stalk transition products telescope, but this alone does not compare different face stalks through noninvertible incidence maps or prove invariance of local syndrome inverses. The true transport criterion is the naturality of the whole local decoder map, including its kernel representatives, under overlap transition squares.

Decisive next proof gate: derive T1–T4 in the GGJ arithmetic family by writing actual transition matrices from the tree-lift and representation construction (§§12.3–12.4), and test the proposed naturality on the smallest two-anchor square. An explicit finite counterexample with two compatible anchor charts and functorial incidence maps that satisfy (2.8) but break N-P’s overlap assembly would refute any theorem based only on the abstract GGJ axioms. If the concrete arithmetic transitions are monomial/permutation scalars as in the proof of GGJ Thm. 12.0.1 (PDF pp. 70–72), check whether the NP local choice rule is equivariant under those transformations; a failure here must be repaired, not assumed away.
