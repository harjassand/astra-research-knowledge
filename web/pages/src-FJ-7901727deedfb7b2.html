# Compiling bounded nonlinear corrections into a noisy observation channel

Integration derivation, 14 October 2026. This is a concrete composition of the
incoming bounded PDE correction theorem and the already published N722 positive
Gaussian-mean factory. Neither branching PDE representations nor stochastic
FORS/Gaussian factories are new here. Historical originality of this composition,
external mathematical review, finite-bit certification and practical superiority
remain unestablished. The theorem specifies an ideal-real computational operation.

## 1. The scientific output and the supplied versus acquired interface

Let u solve an admitted nonlinear field equation. The requested outputs are
u(x_1),...,u(x_r), where r is the number of requested observations, not the field
mesh size. Supply evaluable predictors v_i and verified correction certificates
giving fresh random variables E_i with

    |E_i|<=h_i almost surely,  E E_i=u(x_i)−v_i.

The incoming construction supplies E_i=h_i Z_i using a finite random tree,
under its exact model, global enclosure, coefficient and finite-work assumptions.
The certificates, model parameters, predictor and exact diffusion/branching
operations are inputs whose acquisition is charged. They are not inferred from
sampled residuals. Different coordinates may use independent trees; acquisitions
of a complete vector must be independent and identically distributed.

Fix a declared observation-noise scale sigma>0. The task is to simulate the
entire joint channel

    N((u(x_1),...,u(x_r)), sigma^2 I_r)

within total variation epsilon. This task does not require estimating the mean
vector to sigma*epsilon and then adding noise. It also does not return the
noise-free solution to that accuracy or reconstruct the full field. Noise is
an explicit part of the target, never a silently weakened accuracy objective.

Set X=(E_1,...,E_r)/sigma and R=||h||_2/sigma. Then ||X||<=R surely and
E X=(u_vector−v_vector)/sigma. This discharges N722's bounded-iid oracle
hypothesis exactly. N722 needs no independent-coordinate assumption and no
conservative-gradient structure. Unknown physical model errors remain outside
the computational certificate.

For a supplied positive definite noise covariance S, whiten the correction by
S^(-1/2), supply a certified radius for that transformed vector, then map the
factory output back by S^(1/2). A sufficient radius is
`||h||_2/sqrt(lambda_min(S))`; matrix acquisition, factorization and conditioning
are paid. This describes a *declared observation kernel*, not the PDE field's
physical uncertainty distribution or an unknown heteroscedastic instrument.

If the target has an unnoised direction whose mean is unknown, this mechanism
cannot silently regularize it. For example let a bounded oracle return
Bernoulli(p), p unknown in (0,1), and ask for the noiseless law delta_p. Any
algorithm making finitely many oracle queries almost surely has output equal
to p with probability zero for Lebesgue-almost every p. To prove this, condition
on its independent random seed and a finite binary transcript. Its output is a
fixed number not depending on p; the set where that number equals p has measure
zero. Integrate the transcript likelihood over p, use Fubini, and sum over the
countably many finite transcripts. Thus its TV error is one for almost every p.
This standard support obstruction explains why the known noise kernel is an
essential operation. Commonly rounded finite targets have different semantics.

## 2. Expected-cost observation compiler

Choose 0<epsilon_g<1. Let m be any integer on the decreasing branch m>=8R^2,
also m>=1, satisfying

    10m exp(−m/(8R^2))<=epsilon_g.                         (1)

For R=0 return v+sigma G directly. One always valid positive-radius choice is
`ceil[64(1+R^2) log(64(1+R^2)/epsilon_g)]`. The smaller m from (1) uses a proved
error inequality, not empirical calibration. For each of m factors repeatedly
propose y~N(0,I_r/m), draw J~Poisson(2), acquire J fresh X_j, and accept y with
probability

    product_j [1+clip_[−1,1](y·X_j)]/2.

Return v+sigma*sum(accepted y). This is the existing N722/FORS rule with the
newly supplied bounded correction oracle; the mean is never evaluated.

**Theorem 1.** Its full joint output law is epsilon_g-close to the requested
Gaussian channel. It terminates almost surely and uses at most 2e^2 m vector
acquisitions in expectation. If a vector acquisition has expected complete cost
C_X (including all coordinate trees, coefficient/diffusion evaluation and vector
reading), expected total cost is

    C_build+C_verify+O(m*r)+2e^2 m C_X.                    (2)

For uniform source child mean q_i<1 and per-node cost C_i,
`C_X<=sum_i C_i/(1−q_i)` plus paid vector assembly. This bound is not a statement
that certificate construction, field evaluation or physical acquisition is cheap.

**Proof.** Conditional on y, Poisson averaging makes acceptance
`exp(E clip(y·X)−1)`, between e^−2 and 1. Thus the accepted density is the
positive clipped exponential tilt of N(0,I/m). Without clipping the tilt is
N(E X/m,I/m). The N722 proof uses the one-dimensional projection y·X and the
known bound R to obtain factor-TV<=10 exp(−m/(8R^2)) when m>=R^2. Product-TV
and contraction under summation give (1). Affine scaling gives the desired
channel. The accepted-factor recurrence gives expected acquisitions 2/a<=2e^2,
without assuming independence of J and acceptance.

Tree cost and its returned value may be correlated. This does not invalidate
(2): the event that acquisition j is requested is decided before its fresh
independent oracle pair (X_j,cost_j) is generated. Tonelli and conditioning on
that pre-acquisition history give `E sum_requested cost_j<=C_X E[number requested]`.
Replacing this argument by an assumption that cost is independent of acceptance
would be incorrect. Reused or conditioned oracle outputs require a new proof.

## 3. An exponential work certificate for nonlinear random trees

Finite expected work alone gives only a Markov tail, which can erase a claimed
polylogarithmic accuracy cost after imposing a hard cap. A stronger reusable
certificate is available for bounded-degree source trees.

**Theorem 2.** For a tree whose node has state x, suppose z>1 and t(x)>=1 satisfy

    z E_x[product_over_children t(child_state)]<=t(x).     (3)

Then total node count N is almost surely finite and

    E_x z^N<=t(x),  P_x(N>n)<=t(x) z^−n.                 (4)

The statement allows spatially dependent, multitype offspring. It does not
require a pointwise subcritical first-generation mean.

**Proof.** Explore the tree, retaining all unvisited nodes in a frontier.
`z^(visited nodes)*product_frontier t(state)` is a nonnegative supermartingale
by (3). Its expectation is bounded by t(root). Since t>=1, truncating at any
finite number of visited nodes or generations bounds the corresponding z-power
of the node count. Fatou gives (4); an infinite tree would make this power
infinite on a positive-probability event and is therefore excluded. This is a
standard branching/positive-supermartingale argument, used here as a checkable
resource certificate, not a new general probability principle.

For bounded degree b and uniform conditional child mean<=q<1, another sufficient
tail is obtained from the exploration queue and conditional Hoeffding:

    P(N>n)<=exp[−2(n(1−q)−1)_+^2/(n b^2)]
           <=exp[−n(1−q)^2/(2b^2)] if n>=2/(1−q).        (5)

Dummy offspring zero after extinction make the stopped queue argument valid.
For sharper constants, use a dominating offspring PGF f(s). Any s>1 with
f(s)<s yields (3) with constant t=s and z=s/f(s)>1. Individual upper offspring
probabilities produce a valid stochastic domination when their sum is<=1.
An arbitrary spectral-radius assertion is not itself an acquired PGF certificate.

## 4. A compiler with a deterministic node cap

Let delta>0, B0=mK and

    K=ceil[e^2 log(3m/delta)],
    Jcap=ceil[2(e−1)+log(3B0/delta)],
    B=B0*Jcap*r,
    ncap=ceil[log(3B*tmax/delta)/log(z)],                 (6)

where tmax bounds all starting states in (4). Allow at most K proposals per
factor, at most Jcap oracle acquisitions per proposal, and at most ncap nodes
per coordinate tree. Abort to a fixed known fallback output at the first cap.
Sample capped Poisson degrees with one uniform and at most Jcap+1 cumulative
probability comparisons; an uncapped Poisson routine would leave an uncharged
primitive-level loop. All parameters in (6) must be rounded outward.

The prototype instead selects conservative caps using exact rational powers:
`(7/8)^K<=delta/(3m)`, `2^Jcap>=24mK/delta`, and
`z^ncap>=3B*tmax/delta`. These follow from e^2<8 and E[2^Poisson(2)]=e^2<8.
It also selects an integer j and m=ceil(8R^2*j) with
`10m/2^j<=epsilon_g`; e>2 gives (1). Thus the cap and budget inequalities
can be verified using integers/Fractions without rounding logarithms inward.
This certifies the mathematical cap parameters, not the floating random draws.

**Theorem 3.** In the same ideal-real model, the capped compiler uses at most
B*ncap PDE nodes on every execution and its output-TV error is at most
epsilon_g+delta. For fixed certificate margins, r, R and node primitives, the
hard cap is polynomial in log(1/epsilon) when epsilon_g=delta=epsilon/2.

**Proof.** Couple to the uncapped sampler until the first cap. A factor needs
more than K proposals with probability at most e^(−e^−2 K); union over m
factors costs delta/3. Exponential Markov at parameter one gives
`P(Poisson(2)>Jcap)<=exp(2(e−1)−Jcap)`; union over B0 proposals costs delta/3.
Conditional on every prior acquisition, (4) bounds each fresh tree's tail.
There are at most B trees before the earlier caps; union costs delta/3 by (6).
Thus coupled outputs differ with probability at most delta. The coupling
inequality and Theorem1 prove the law claim. The fallback atom is included
in this bound; it is not conditioned away as an unsuccessful run.

## 6. What this adds, comparator and remaining operation

The new input representation is an acquired bounded unbiased nonlinear
correction, rather than a solved field or a supplied unknown-mean vector law.
The composed output is a generative noisy observation of selected nonlinear
solutions, with full joint-law error and nested cost accounted for. This is a
proved compatible interface between the two capital mechanisms. It does not
solve the general unbounded conservative-gradient N687 sampler, whose oracle
and every-run query target are different.

The strongest matched sampler is stochastic FORS, already reconstructed in
N722. This compiler *uses* it and claims no algorithmic advantage over an
equally informed FORS implementation supplied the same bounded PDE oracle.
Agarwal–Claisse branching/supersolution methods can also supply bounded inputs
in covered regimes. Sample-mean Gaussian postprocessing is a useful control,
not the strongest baseline. No historical novelty or previously unattainable
scientific answer has been established by the composition alone.

For equal radii h and a simple independent tree per requested point,
`R^2=r*h^2/sigma^2` and each vector acquisition costs order r tree roots.
Consequently naïve total node work can grow quadratically in the number of
observations. Dimension-free oracle counts at fixed R are not a field-wide
speed theorem. Small sigma also increases cost as approximately sigma^−2;
the compiler does not evade high-precision mean acquisition by renaming it.

For downstream M independent synthetic observations, product-TV costs at most
M*epsilon. Conditioning on a rare event of probability rho generally needs
error small compared with rho. A Gaussian observation sampler is not an
evaluated likelihood density or a free rare-event/posterior oracle. Noise scale
sigma, predictor/tube, full model and primitive costs remain essential inputs.

The supplied prototype and replay use floating-point Gaussian, exponential and
acceptance calculations. They are diagnostic implementations, not interval-
validated continuous-TV executables: finite machine output has TV1 from an
unrounded continuous target. Deployment needs a commonly rounded target or
exact-real semantics plus certified primitive/acceptance errors. Outward cap
rounding, node ceilings and empirical moments do not close that arithmetic gate.
