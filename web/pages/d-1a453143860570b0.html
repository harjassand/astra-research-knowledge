# Certified EIG bounds: diagnostic acquisition is the missing interface

Primary: Mohamed Doumbouya, Arthur Vidard, Olivier Zahm, *Certified Bayesian optimal experimental design: from Expected Information Gain to Signal-to-Noise Ratio*, [arXiv:2609.32282v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32282v1), submitted 2026-09-26. Date and all three authors verified on arXiv metadata and full HTML. Full source preserved. Read closely: Theorem2.1, Corollaries1/2, Propositions2/3, Sections3.1–3.4 and Appendix C's Schur-complement structure. Remaining numerical/proof sections inspected, not all independently reconstructed.

## Question and source claims

Given a smooth known joint model (theta,Y), select m linear observations W^T Y to maximize mutual information I(theta;W^T Y). The interface supplies or constructs four p by p covariance/Fisher diagnostic matrices. Theorem2.1 gives incremental conditional-information bounds through Schur complements. Corollary1 reduces to

0.5 log det(W^T Sigma_signal W)/det(W^T Sigma_Y|theta W)
 <= I(theta;W^T Y)
 <= 0.5 log det(W^T Sigma_Y W)/det(W^T Sigma_noise W).

Validity requires Sigma_signal^-1 >= Fisher(Y) and Sigma_noise^-1 >= E Fisher(Y|theta), in Loewner order. The matrix inequalities are **certificates**, not ordinary point estimates. Gaussian linear models attain equality with exact matrices.

Once matrices are assembled, many candidate designs can be evaluated without new forward solves; this is credible reusable computational structure. Optimization is still over specified linear observations of a supplied model. The paper does not solve arbitrary experiment discovery, acquire an unknown model, or provide a general adaptive-data reuse guarantee.

Theorem2.1's incremental quantity is the **average** conditional information I(theta;Y_new|Y_m) for fixed design matrices, not a pointwise information guarantee after a particular realized Y_m=y. Choosing the next design using realized data requires conditioning/rebuilding the model or a further policy-level theorem. Algebraic sequential addition of columns must not be silently equated with outcome-adaptive experiment selection.

The diagnostics have three practical regimes. Pick-and-freeze covariance estimates can require conditional latent sampling. The authors explicitly neglect that cost when the goal map H is cheap; it is nevertheless a cost in a fully charged interface. Denoiser diagnostics approximate conditional expectations and reverse the required Fisher inequality. Gradient bounds give the safe population direction but still need numerical expectation control. Section4 uses 10^4 samples and treats sampling error as negligible; that does not supply a finite-sample certificate.

## Our exact obstruction to an unsafe inference

This is **not** a counterexample to Theorem2.1: its assumptions are not met by the unsafe plug-in. It concretely instantiates the warning in Section3.2.

Let theta~N(0,1), Y=theta+e, e~N(0,2). All population laws are smooth, the true EIG is0.5log(3/2)=.2027325541, and Fisher(Y)=1/3. Use the crude population denoiser f(Y)=0 (optimal in the constants-only approximation space). Its residual covariance is E(theta-f)^2=1. The Section3.2 residual expression gives

J_hat = 1/2 - (1/2)^2*1 = 1/4,
Sigma_signal_hat = 4.

It is positive definite, yet **too large** as a covariance certificate. Substitution into the putative lower bound produces0.5log(4/2)=.3465735903, strictly greater than true EIG. This error persists with infinite data and exact arithmetic. Positivity or apparent numerical consistency does not certify the inequality. The constants-only space does not meet Proposition3's affine-richness sufficient condition; the point is that invertibility alone, which holds here, does not repair the inequality direction.

## Conditional repair lemma (our derivation)

For Y=G(eta)+e with independent e~N(0,S), define m(Y)=E[G(eta)|Y], C=E[(G-m)(G-m)^T], R_f=E[(G-f)(G-f)^T], and D_f=E[(f-m)(f-m)^T]. Conditional orthogonality gives R_f=C+D_f. Therefore

Fisher(Y)=S^-1 - S^-1 R_f S^-1 + S^-1 D_f S^-1.

If a supplied/derived certificate proves D_f <= delta I, and a numerical certificate proves R_f >= R_hat - eta I, then

Fisher(Y) <= S^-1 - S^-1 R_hat S^-1 + (delta+eta) S^-2.

When this right-hand matrix J is positive definite, Sigma_signal=J^-1 is valid for Theorem2.1. This restores the required direction. An analogous argument conditions additionally on theta for Sigma_noise. In the scalar example D_f=1/3 and eta=0, so J=1/4+(1/3)/4=1/3, exactly recovering the true covariance3.

The missing datum is a certified bound on denoiser excess risk D_f. An ordinary held-out residual-risk estimate estimates R_f, not D_f; the inaccessible Bayes risk is the difference. Learning an accurate-looking f or comparing it against one weaker predictor supplies no universal upper bound on this excess risk. The repair lemma is conditional, not a solved acquisition procedure.

## Novelty, consequence, falsifiable continuation

Orthogonal projection and Gaussian score/MMSE identities are existing foundations. The authors themselves disclose the reverse inequality and leave finite-sample analysis for future work. Our scalar witness and correction are an independent audit/conditional extension; no novelty claim.

Concrete downstream use: an experimental-design tool may reuse expensive forward samples across many designs, but must display/populate **certified** matrix intervals if it advertises bounds. A promising question is whether model-class approximation theorems plus independent covariance concentration can produce useful delta+eta budgets uniformly across adaptively selected W, at less total cost than equally informed EIG estimators. Next discriminating experiment: freeze f and a held-out matrix certificate, compare corrected widths against gradient-safe widths and nested EIG on a non-Gaussian smooth toy model. Reject the avenue if conservative correction wipes out useful design discrimination.
