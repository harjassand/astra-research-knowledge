One-stream post-selection future-KL certificates for stationary HMMs
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: scoped impossibility for nonvacuous uniform high-probability certificates; exact lower bounds for a same-path gap and for IID holdout length. No novelty or priority claim.

1. Verdict and scope

A representation selected from one finite raw trajectory cannot, in general, support a uniformly valid, nontrivial high-probability certificate that its length-L future law is close in KL to the true conditional future law, when the HMM parameters and mixing rate are unknown and no lower mixing bound is supplied. The obstruction already occurs for a two-state, binary-output, stationary, ergodic HMM with deterministic emissions and an arbitrarily small positive transition probability. With L proportional to the observed path length, two models put substantial probability on exactly the same transcript but have conditionals over future blocks separated by a constant total-variation distance. Pinsker's inequality then forces one of the two future KL errors to be bounded below by a positive constant on that transcript.

The result applies to every measurable fitting or adaptive-selection rule: its acquired basis, candidate model, validation statistic, predictor and reported certificate are all functions of the same transcript and its own random bits. It is not a claim that no confidence statement can be issued. A universal loose bound, including the trivial bound L log |Sigma| for a uniform future predictor, can be valid. The impossibility is about issuing a small bound with uniformly high coverage. Nor does the result rule out fixed-horizon rates, time-uniform methods in a more restricted class, or certificates backed by independent data or a known mixing/dependence bound.

A full-path retainer can run the same selection and certification program on the same trajectory and random tape. It reproduces the candidate, certificate, and failure event exactly. Thus an acquired representation does not create an information advantage over the matched baseline that retains all raw observations. Any representation benefit must instead be a separately priced storage, latency, or computation benefit.

2. Exact interface and quantifiers

Let Sigma be a finite alphabet with at least two symbols. Let Theta_HMM be the class of all stationary finite-state, time-homogeneous HMM laws on Sigma with unknown parameters and no supplied state dictionary, Hankel/oracle answers, state-count bound, or positive lower bound on the mixing rate. The lower-bound models below use at most two hidden states, so the result still holds if a state-count cap K >= 2 is supplied. The alternative model is ergodic; its positive spectral gap is allowed to approach zero with sample size.

A learner observes exactly D_n = Y_1,...,Y_n from one stationary realization. It may fit a representation, select/adapt features, query or test candidates using D_n, and then output any total sequential predictor. For a fixed future block length L, the predictor induces a distribution q_D on Sigma^L (by multiplying its sequential output probabilities, including its updates after each hypothetical future symbol). The learner also reports U_D in [0,infinity], claimed to upper-bound the conditional future KL

    K_theta(D,q_D) = KL( P_theta(Y_(n+1):(n+L) in . | D_n) || q_D ).

All KL values use natural logarithms (nats). Histories used below have positive probability under both models. The advertised uniform coverage property is

    for every theta in Theta_HMM:
    P_theta,learner-randomness { K_theta(D_n,q_D) <= U_D } >= 1-alpha.

The probability covers the one observed path and all internal learner randomness. No hidden state, parameter, fresh trajectory, or external truth oracle is supplied. If the purported representation is adaptive, its entire data-dependent selection is included in the map D -> (q_D,U_D). A fallback predictor is required on every transcript so that q_D is a probability law.

The theorem below concerns a high-probability bound at the endpoint of the observed path, conditional on that exact observed history. It is not merely an expected next-symbol risk statement, an average over future histories, or a claim about an empirical validation score.

3. Impossibility theorem

Theorem 1 (same-transcript obstruction for future-block KL).
For every n >= 1, take L = 100 n and define c = (1 - exp(-1))^2 / 2 > 0.19. There are two stationary binary-output HMMs theta_0 and theta_rho in Theta_HMM, with rho = 1/(100 n), such that:

(a) Under theta_0, D_n = 0^n with probability 1. Under theta_rho,

    P_theta_rho(D_n = 0^n) = (1/2)(1-rho)^(n-1) >= 0.495.

(b) Given D_n = 0^n, the two true conditional laws of the L-symbol future have total-variation distance at least 1-exp(-1). Consequently, for every single probability law q on {0,1}^L,

    max { KL(P_theta_0(future | 0^n) || q),
          KL(P_theta_rho(future | 0^n) || q) } >= c.

(c) Therefore, for any deterministic learner, if it reports U_D < c on D=0^n, its uniform coverage failure probability is at least 0.495 under one of theta_0 or theta_rho. In particular, no deterministic learner with coverage 1-alpha for alpha < 0.495 may issue U_D < c on that transcript.

For a randomized learner, let s = P_omega{U_(0^n,omega) < c}. If its coverage failure probability is at most alpha under both laws, then

    s <= 2 alpha / 0.495.

For example, at alpha=0.05 it can report a bound below c on this common transcript for at most about 20.3 percent of its internal random tapes. The randomized statement allows the identity of the bad model to vary with the random tape; it does not assume a deterministic seed.

This is a horizon-uniform obstruction in the following exact sense: for every sample length n, there is a horizon L=100n and a stationary finite-state HMM pair for which a small certificate is impossible on a transcript of probability at least 0.495 in one model. It does not give a constant lower bound for a horizon L held fixed while n tends to infinity. For fixed L <= 100n the same construction gives the weaker explicit threshold

    c_(n,L) = (1 - exp(-L/(100n)))^2 / 2,

which is of order L^2/n^2 when L/n is small. Thus the theorem does not rule out useful fixed-horizon guarantees whose error tolerance is above this scale.

4. Proof of Theorem 1

Define theta_0 to be a one-state HMM that emits 0 surely. Define theta_rho by hidden state X_t in {0,1}, deterministic emission Y_t=X_t, stationary initial law P(X_1=0)=P(X_1=1)=1/2, and transition matrix

                 [ 1-rho   rho   ]
                 [ rho     1-rho ].

For rho>0 this chain is irreducible, aperiodic, and stationary under the stated initial law. Its nontrivial eigenvalue is 1-2rho, so its (reversible) absolute spectral gap is 2rho=1/(50n). The small gap is not an added pathology; it is the missing structural promise in the question.

Under theta_0, D_n=0^n surely. Under theta_rho, Y_1=0 with probability 1/2 and no transition from 0 occurs during the n-1 transitions in the training block with probability (1-rho)^(n-1). Thus p := P_theta_rho(D_n=0^n)=(1/2)(1-rho)^(n-1). Bernoulli's inequality gives

    p >= (1/2)(1-(n-1)rho)
      = (1/2)(1-(n-1)/(100n)) > 0.495.

Conditional on D_n=0^n, deterministic emissions reveal X_n=0. Under theta_0 the future block law is delta_(0^L). Under theta_rho the chance of the all-zero future block is (1-rho)^L, so

    d_TV(delta_(0^L), P_theta_rho(future | 0^n))
      = 1-(1-rho)^L
      >= 1-exp(-rho L)
      = 1-exp(-1).

Here d_TV(P,Q)=sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|. For any q, the triangle inequality gives

    d_TV(P_0,P_1) <= d_TV(P_0,q)+d_TV(P_1,q).

Pinsker in nats states KL(P||q) >= 2 d_TV(P,q)^2, equivalently d_TV(P,q) <= sqrt(KL(P||q)/2). If both KL terms were less than d_TV(P_0,P_1)^2/2, both total-variation terms on the right would be less than d_TV(P_0,P_1)/2, a contradiction. Hence the larger KL is at least d_TV(P_0,P_1)^2/2 >= c. If q assigns zero mass to a sequence supported by a true law, the corresponding KL is infinite and the conclusion holds immediately.

On the transcript D=0^n a deterministic learner must produce the same q and U under both parameter values: it sees the same symbols and has no parameter oracle. If U<c, the preceding bound says at least one model has K_theta(D,q)>=c>U. Under theta_0 this failure occurs with probability one if theta_0 is the bad model. Otherwise theta_rho is bad, and the failure event includes D=0^n, of probability p>=0.495. This proves (c).

For a randomized learner, couple the two runs using the same internal random tape omega. On D=0^n, the output (q_omega,U_omega) is then the same in both worlds. For every tape with U_omega<c, at least one of the two conditional KL values is greater than U_omega. Let F_i be the overall certificate failure probability under theta_i, and let A={omega:U_omega<c}. The training transcript has probability 1 under theta_0 and p under theta_rho. For each omega in A, the weighted sum of the two failure indicators, with these transcript probabilities as weights, is at least p. Integrating over omega gives

    F_0 + F_rho >= p P_omega(A).

If F_0,F_rho <= alpha, then P_omega(A)<=2alpha/p<=2alpha/0.495. This proves the randomized claim.

5. Why selection and raw-path retention do not change the result

The selector may build any observable basis, choose its dimension from the path, try many models, stop adaptively, or validate on a carved-out block. For Theorem 1 all of these actions are a measurable function of D_n and the learner's random tape. On the common all-zero transcript they cannot distinguish theta_0 from theta_rho, so post-selection does not remove the ambiguity. This is a two-point indistinguishability proof, not a claim that data processing alone implies a confidence failure.

Matched baseline. Give a baseline the identical complete raw path D_n, the public learner program, and the same random tape. It runs the same acquisition/selection/validation steps, stores the same acquired representation, and emits the same q and U. Coupling on the input and random tape makes its candidate, report, and certificate event exactly equal path by path. Therefore its coverage and future-KL performance equal the representation procedure's. If it retains the raw path, it can also simulate any later refit the representation procedure performs using only that path. This baseline has no informational deficit. A representation may still reduce retained bits or per-query time under an explicitly constrained resource model, but those gains must be measured and charged separately; they do not strengthen the certificate.

6. Same-path validation: dependence and cost

6.1 No finite gap yields uniformly fresh validation without a mixing promise.

Let a proposed split use n training symbols, discard a gap of g symbols, then use v>=1 validation symbols; let T=n+g+v. For any such finite split, choose the same stationary two-state HMM with rho=1/(100T). Let A be the event that the training block is all zero and B the event that the validation block is all zero. Since each event requires the state at its block start to be zero and no within-block switch, P(A)<=1/2 and P(B)<=1/2, so P(A)P(B)<=1/4. The event that the chain starts at zero and makes no switch from the start of training through the end of validation is contained in A intersect B and has probability at least

    (1/2)(1-rho)^(T-1) >= 0.495.

Thus

    d_TV( Law(training, validation),
         Law(training) x Law(validation) )
      >= |P(A intersect B)-P(A)P(B)| >= 0.245.

The gap symbols are unrestricted in the intersection event, so the containment remains valid whether or not the gap is observed. Consequently no finite choice of g makes the validation block uniformly close to independent over the stated HMM class. Increasing g or v only changes finite T; the adversary can choose rho smaller. The alternative chain is still ergodic and has positive gap 2rho. Its gap tends to zero as T grows.

This lower bound is about statistical dependence, not merely overlap of indices. It shows why a chronological split by itself is not a fresh holdout. The same two-model argument also prevents a uniformly small future-block certificate from being rescued by treating an all-zero held-out block as independent evidence.

6.2 What a known dependence bound would buy.

For a specified split, define its dependence distance

    delta_g = d_TV( Law(S,V), Law(S) x Law(V) ),

where S is the full selection transcript and V the later validation block separated by g unused observations. This definition fixes the total-variation convention. Suppose a validation procedure selects the candidate using S alone and, under the product law Law(S)xLaw(V), its failure event has probability at most alpha. The event-probability characterization of total variation immediately gives

    P_actual(failure) <= alpha + delta_g.

Thus any known bound delta_g<=b(g) contributes an explicit additive b(g) to the coverage error. If one is supplied a geometric envelope b(g)<=C exp(-g/tau), it is sufficient to take

    g >= ceil( tau log(C/eta) )

(to a nonnegative integer) to charge at most eta dependence error. This is a conditional implication from the assumed envelope, not a derived HMM guarantee. No finite universal g can be selected when tau, or a valid upper envelope for delta_g, is unknown and unbounded.

This gap condition only decouples the selection block from the validation block. It does not make the symbols inside V independent. A general-HMM validation procedure still needs a concentration or confidence result under the marginal law of V; the IID sample-count calculation below is a necessary lower bound in a simple subclass, not such a general-HMM upper bound.

The total path length for this protocol is n+g+v. It pays the n selection symbols, the g discarded buffer symbols, and the v validation symbols. The split does not make the collection or discarding cost vanish.

6.3 Information-theoretic validation-sample lower bound, even IID.

There is a separate minimum cost even when the validation block really is independent. Consider the one-state IID Bernoulli HMM family, a fixed candidate predictor q=Ber(1/2), and a requested risk threshold epsilon with 0<epsilon<=1/64. Define the one-step future KL risk

    R_p(q)=KL(Ber(p)||Ber(1/2)).

Under p_0=1/2 this risk is zero. Under p_1=1/2+delta with delta=2 sqrt(epsilon), Pinsker gives

    R_(p_1)(q) >= 2 delta^2 = 8 epsilon > epsilon.

Suppose a validation-only certificate uses v independent validation symbols, is valid with failure probability at most alpha at p_1, and is useful at p_0 in the sense that it reports U<=epsilon with probability at least 1-alpha. The event E={U<=epsilon} is then a test with P_(p_0)(E)>=1-alpha and P_(p_1)(E)<=alpha. By data processing of KL under the indicator of E,

    v KL(Ber(p_0)||Ber(p_1))
      >= kl(P_(p_0)(E),P_(p_1)(E))
      >= kl(1-alpha,alpha),

for 0<alpha<1/2, where kl is binary relative entropy. Since delta<=1/4,

    KL(Ber(1/2)||Ber(1/2+delta))
      = -(1/2) log(1-4 delta^2)
      <= (8/3) delta^2
      = (32/3) epsilon.

Therefore every such validation procedure must use

    v >= (3/(32 epsilon)) kl(1-alpha,alpha)
      = Omega( epsilon^(-1) log(1/alpha) )

as alpha decreases. This lower bound is proved inside the IID subclass, so it does not rely on slow mixing or a difficult representation. It is a necessary validation-only sample count for certifying this fixed candidate. If the same symbols used for selection are also reused as validation evidence, this particular split lower bound no longer isolates those symbols; a method using all data needs its own post-selection or time-uniform proof. In the full HMM setting with a separated single-path validation block, a known dependence allowance adds the gap cost above. A necessary path budget for this split design is thus n+g+v with v at least the displayed order; without a uniform mixing/dependence bound no finite g supplies the required dependence guarantee.

For an overall desired error alpha_total, an explicit error allocation is to spend eta on block dependence and alpha_val=alpha_total-eta on the validation procedure, with 0<eta<alpha_total<1/2. Under the supplied envelope b(g)<=C exp(-g/tau), take g>=max(0,ceil(tau log(C/eta))); the IID-subclass validation lower bound then gives

    v >= (3/(32 epsilon)) kl(1-alpha_total+eta, alpha_total-eta).

So this split design has the necessary/charged path count n+g+v, and its no-dependence statistical and dependence costs are separately visible. The formula is not a sufficient total path bound for general HMMs: it assumes the validation procedure has a valid marginal-law guarantee, and no such general-HMM guarantee is proved here.

7. Failure controls and interpretation

- The result has a declared future horizon. Its constant gap uses L=100n; it is not a constant-error lower bound at a fixed L as n grows.
- A certificate U=infinity is valid. For a uniform q on Sigma^L, KL(P||q)<=L log |Sigma| for every P, so even a finite but loose certificate is available. The theorem concerns certifying error below c on the hard transcript.
- The probability is over paths and learner random bits, while KL is conditional on the realized history. These are different sources of randomness and are both stated in the coverage event.
- The lower-bound alternative is stationary, ergodic, has two states, and uses a finite binary alphabet. It needs no reducible chain, hidden continuous state, nonstationarity, or target drift.
- The proof compares predictive distributions, not internal parameter estimates. Label switching or HMM parameter nonidentifiability is not used.
- The IID validation lower bound assumes the validation stage gets v independent symbols and the candidate q is fixed at Ber(1/2). Its displayed lower bound is not an asserted optimal general-HMM sample complexity.
- An estimate of mixing can itself be attempted from one path, but a data-dependent estimate is not a supplied lower bound on the true gap. Wolfer and Kontorovich study single-trajectory mixing-time estimation for finite ergodic chains; this report neither proves their estimator invalid nor derives a certified mixing bound for an unknown HMM.

8. Primary literature and novelty boundary

Jaeger, H. (2000), “Observable Operator Models for Discrete Stochastic Time Series,” Neural Computation 12(6), 1371–1398, DOI https://doi.org/10.1162/089976600300015411. The paper supplies the standard matrix-probability / predictive-process context for HMMs and observable operators. It is not used as a premise for the two-point lower bound.

Shalizi, C. R. and Crutchfield, J. P. (2001), “Computational Mechanics: Pattern and Prediction, Structure and Simplicity,” Journal of Statistical Physics 104, 817–879, author-hosted paper page and PDF: https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/compmech/pubs/cmppss.htm. This is background on predictive sufficient causal states and representation minimality, not a finite-sample confidence result.

Hao, Y., Orlitsky, A., and Pichapati, V. (2018), “On Learning Markov Chains,” arXiv:1810.11754, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11754. They study expected/minimax next-symbol KL prediction risk and transition estimation for observable finite Markov chains; the paper notes that arbitrarily small transition probabilities obstruct nontrivial uniform transition-estimation upper bounds. The present target is a post-selection, high-probability certificate for a conditional future block from an HMM trajectory, so the interfaces are different.

Wolfer, G. and Kontorovich, A. (2019), “Estimating the Mixing Time of Ergodic Markov Chains,” COLT, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 99, 3120–3159, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/wolfer19a.html. This studies mixing-time estimation from a single trajectory of an unknown ergodic finite Markov chain. It is relevant to how a mixing promise might be acquired; its existence does not turn the theorem's unknown true gap into an assumption.

Lunde, R. (2019), “Sample Splitting and Weak Assumption Inference For Time Series,” arXiv:1902.07425, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07425. This proves asymptotic validity of specific post-selection inference procedures under dependence assumptions. Its introduction distinguishes dependent conditional inference from IID split inference, and the results use explicit weak-dependence conditions and a different target. It supports the boundary that dependence assumptions matter; it is not cited for our finite-sample HMM bound.

The lower bounds in this note are elementary constructions and data-processing/Pinsker arguments, not claims imported from these papers. The search was bounded to primary literature on HMM predictive representations, Markov-chain prediction/mixing, and time-series sample splitting. This is not an exhaustive novelty review. No priority or first-proof claim is made.

9. UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN: whether a nontrivial time-uniform confidence sequence for conditional future KL can be obtained from the same path for a restricted HMM class when a computable positive mixing lower bound, state-count bound, and emission/transition regularity are supplied. This report gives no upper theorem in that regime.

UNKNOWN: the optimal finite-sample validation length for general selected HMM predictors at a fixed horizon, with known state count, known mixing envelope, arbitrary output distributions, and precise dependence between the selected candidate and a discarded validation block. The IID two-point lower bound gives only a necessary order in a simple subclass.

UNKNOWN: whether a data-dependent mixing-time estimate can be converted into an honest post-selection future-KL certificate without an independent path, under additional identifiable-HMM assumptions. No such conversion is asserted here.

UNKNOWN: a complete complexity result for an acquired basis under a memory/latency budget. The raw-path simulation proves equality of information and certificate behavior, but does not compare all possible time-space strategies or price the representation.
