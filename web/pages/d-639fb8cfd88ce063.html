## 1. Exact interface and scope

Input consists of the expanded rational coefficients of a homogeneous quartic

\[
f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2),\qquad L\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Q),\quad Q=Q^T\in\mathbb Q^{n\times n},\quad Q\succ0.
\]

Neither L, Q, a metric, a reflection, nor a hidden condition/height bound is supplied. Hidden coordinates are used to prove uniform ideal containments; every acquisition operation is constructed in observed coordinates. For n=1 the problem is trivial, and the existing binary comparator handles n=2. This gate concerns n≥3.

The result acquires every rational projective reflection direction of f. The pool contains the n hidden axes and has at most 2n²−n points. Composing it with the separately gated rational invariant-metric projector and axis selector yields the hidden axes. This note does not independently re-prove that entire downstream selector.

## 2. Polar equations and the excluded divisor

Let T be the four-linear polarization, F=f(v), and g(x)=T(v,v,v,x). Define

\[
P(v,x)=F^2T(v,x,x,x)-3F g(x)T(v,v,x,x)+2g(x)^3.
\]

Its x-coefficient ideal I has at most \(\binom{n+2}{3}\) homogeneous degree-nine generators. Direct quartic expansion gives

\[
f\left(x-2v\frac{g(x)}F\right)-f(x)=-\frac{8g(x)}{F^3}P(v,x).
\]

For F≠0, g(v)=F and this map is an involutive reflection. Conversely any preserving involutive reflection with minus-one direction v has this covector: comparing the coefficient linear in the complementary fixed subspace forces \(\ell=T(v^3,\cdot)/F\). Thus the nonzero-F projective zero set of I is exactly the reflection directions.

There are no projective zeros with F=0. There P=2g³, so P≡0 implies g≡0 and ∇f(v)=0. In hidden coordinates write S for the nonempty active support of v. The gradient condition implies \(Q_{SS}(v_S^2)=0\). Every real positive-definite principal submatrix is invertible over C, contradicting the nonzero active coordinates. This is a complex smoothness argument; no real-point restriction is imposed.

## 3. Radical point ideal and its Hilbert function

Use the previously gated complex monomial theorem. Reflection directions then have support one or two. For a fixed pair i,j put

\[
p=q_{ii}v_i^4-q_{jj}v_j^4,\qquad c_k=q_{ik}v_i^2-q_{jk}v_j^2\quad(k\ne i,j),
\]

and let \(p_{ij}=\gcd(p,c_k:k\ne i,j)\), including the convention that all zero c_k give p. Its degree k_ij is 0, 2, or 4. Since p has nonzero endpoints and four distinct complex roots, the gcd is squarefree and its roots are nonzero pair directions. Every such direction has F≠0: its ratio squared is a signed square root of q_jj/q_ii, and the resulting binary F cannot vanish because \(|q_{ij}|<\sqrt{q_{ii}q_{jj}}\).

Let K3 be the squarefree triple-monomial ideal. The point ideal is

\[
J=(K3,\ v_i v_jp_{ij}:i<j).
\]

Here is the needed scheme-level check. Modulo K3, each degree d≥1 splits into the n pure-axis monomials and mutually disjoint pair mixed sectors. A pair sector consists of \(v_i v_j\) times binary forms of degree d−2. Quotienting by p_ij gives dimension min(k_ij,d−1). Its evaluation on the distinct nonzero pair roots is a consecutive-power Vandermonde matrix of precisely that rank; it vanishes on the axes and all other pairs. The pure-axis evaluations give an identity block. Therefore the displayed ideal and the reduced point ideal agree in every degree:

\[
\dim(\mathbb Q[v]/J)_d=n+\sum_{i<j}\min(k_{ij},d-1).
\]

This proves radicality and saturation, including missing pair roots and repeated symmetry patterns. J is generated in degrees≤6; its Hilbert function is r for d≥5. Low regularity of J is not by itself used as a bound on the raw ideal I.

## 4. Six-variable complex zero exclusion

For any fixed PD principal triple introduce independent auxiliary variables m_i,s_i, i=1,2,3, and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_i&=(1-2m_is_i)(q_{ii}-s_i^2),\\
H_{ij}&=q_{ij}(1-4m_is_i)+s_j(-s_i-2q_{ii}m_i+6m_is_i^2),\\
B&=3s_1s_2s_3-q_{23}s_1-q_{13}s_2-q_{12}s_3.
\end{aligned}
\]

These ten polynomials have no common complex zero. A_i=0 implies s_i≠0 and either the mass branch m_i s_i=1/2 or the root branch s_i²=q_ii. Overlap may be assigned either branch. For a mass index let \(\kappa_i=2-q_{ii}/s_i^2\). H_ij=0 gives \(q_{ij}/(s_is_j)=\kappa_i\). Two mass indices have equal κ by symmetry. Dividing B by s_1s_2s_3 requires the sum of the three normalized edges to equal 3.

- With three mass indices, all normalized edges equal κ, so κ=1; a 2×2 principal minor vanishes.
- With two mass indices, all three normalized edges again equal their common κ, so the same contradiction holds.
- With one mass index, the two root indices have real signed square roots. Their normalized edge r is real and |r|<1. The mass edges equal κ=(3−r)/2>1 and q_ii/s_i²=2−κ=(1+r)/2 is positive and below 1. Thus s_i²>q_ii and an edge satisfies \(|q_{ij}|>\sqrt{q_{ii}q_{jj}}\), a contradiction.
- With zero mass indices all s_i are real signed square roots; each normalized edge has absolute value below 1, so their sum cannot equal 3.

This exhausts complex solutions. No relation s=Qm, no global Euler identity, and no auxiliary variable depending on n enters the argument. Zero or negative off-diagonal coefficients cause no exception.

## 5. Effective bound and parity descent

[Kollár, *Sharp Effective Nullstellensatz*, JAMS 1(4), October 1988, Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.9, printed p.965](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf), gives a unit certificate with max degree of multiplier times generator at most 3072: six variables, nine degree-four polynomials, one degree-three polynomial, and no degree-two generator. The safe even bound 4096 used by the candidate is therefore valid. The source theorem and its degree convention were read directly in the primary journal scan; this imported theorem is not represented as independently proved here. Rational certificates follow from the rational linear coefficient system; Remark 1.6(i) makes the field-descent point explicit.

A_i,H_ij are even under (m,s)→(−m,−s), while B is odd. Averaging a certificate with its sign transform makes the corresponding multipliers even, even, and odd. Substitute m/√F and s/√F. The cleared generators are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde A_i&=(F-2m_is_i)(q_{ii}F-s_i^2),\\
\widetilde H_{ij}&=q_{ij}F^2-Fs_j(s_i+2q_{ii}m_i)-4Fq_{ij}m_is_i+6m_is_i^2s_j,\\
\widetilde B&=3s_1s_2s_3-F(q_{23}s_1+q_{13}s_2+q_{12}s_3).
\end{aligned}
\]

An even multiplier monomial of degree e≤4092 contributes denominator F^(e/2+2). An odd B-multiplier monomial of degree e≤4093 contributes denominator F^((e+3)/2). Both exponents are integers≤2048. Consequently

\[
F^{2048}\in(\widetilde A_i,\widetilde H_{ij},\widetilde B)
\]

is a polynomial identity in independent F,m,s after clearing denominators. This is an identity also on F=0; no choice of a square root remains.

Substitute m_i=v_i², s_i=(Qv²)_i and F=f(v), including outside coordinates. Direct polarization gives

\[
[x_i^3]P=v_i\widetilde A_i,\qquad [x_i^2x_j]P=v_j\widetilde H_{ij},\qquad [x_1x_2x_3]P=4v_1v_2v_3\widetilde B.
\]

Multiplying the certificate by v_1v_2v_3 makes every term a polynomial multiple of an actual coefficient of P. Thus \(f^{2048}K3\subset I\), uniformly for all dimensions and all promised Q.

## 6. Independent binary lifting check

All following identities are taken modulo K3 after multiplication by v_i v_j. Set a=q_ii, b=q_ij, c=q_jj, u=q_ik, w=q_jk, m_i=v_i², m_j=v_j², Δ=ac−b². On the pair,

\[
F=a m_i^2+2b m_i m_j+c m_j^2,\quad p=a m_i^2-c m_j^2,\quad s_i=a m_i+b m_j,\quad s_j=b m_i+c m_j,\quad s_k=u m_i+w m_j.
\]

Direct symbolic expansion verifies

\[
\widetilde A_i=-\Delta m_j^2p,\qquad \widetilde A_j=\Delta m_i^2p.
\]

For G=v_i v_jp, the diagonal polar coefficients give m_i²G,m_j²G∈I+K3. Since the binary F² has total m-degree four, every monomial belongs to (m_i²,m_j²), proving f²G∈I+K3.

For C=v_i v_j(u m_i−w m_j), outside polar coefficients give v_i v_j F E_i and v_i v_j F E_j in I+K3, with

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_i&=uF-s_i s_k=s_i(u m_i-w m_j)-up,\\
E_j&=wF-s_j s_k=-s_j(u m_i-w m_j)+wp,\\
m_iE_i-m_jE_j&=F(u m_i-w m_j)-s_kp.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence f²C−f s_kG∈I+K3. Multiply by f and absorb f²s_kG to get f³C∈I+K3. Combining with the triple certificate gives f^2051G,C∈I.

It remains to lift gcd(p,c_k). This is genuinely binary and uniform. If all c_k vanish, the gcd is p. If their span has dimension two it contains v_i²,v_j², hence F. If their span has dimension one, its nonzero diagonal quadratic c either divides the even quartic p in its entirety or is coprime to p. A partial degree-one gcd cannot occur: p and c are even and p has nonzero endpoints; a common nonzero root brings its negative, and a pure square c is coprime. In the coprime case the homogeneous complete intersection of degrees two and four has quotient Hilbert series (1+t)(1+t+t²+t³), so all degree≥5 binary forms vanish in the quotient. This proves F²p_ij∈(p,c_k) in every case.

Multiplying by v_i v_j and restoring the global f produces only K3 terms: every omitted term contains a third coordinate alongside v_i v_j. Then multiplication by f^2051 and the preceding containments gives

\[
\boxed{f^{2053}J\subset I.}
\]

No assertion that I is radical or saturated is needed. This is the decisive missing bound that point counting and final-ideal regularity alone would not provide.

## 7. Observed-coordinate extraction and complete costs

For d=5,6 let D_d=d+4·2053 (8217 or 8218), and compute the row space W_d=I_Dd of all degree-D_d monomial multiples of the degree-nine observed generators. Then

\[
J_d=\{h\in\mathbb Q[v]_d:f^{2053}h\in W_d\}.
\]

The containment just proved gives one inclusion. Evaluation at every projective zero of I, where f≠0, gives the converse. Linear coordinate transport preserves the polar ideal and degrees, so the hidden-coordinate proof establishes this equality for the coefficient-only observed algorithm.

The quotients A5,A6 have the same dimension r≤2n²−n. To choose a nonvanishing chart, scan \(\ell_t(v)=v_1+t v_2+\cdots+t^{n-1}v_n\) for integers 0≤t≤r(n−1). Each point forbids at most n−1 trial values; an invertible multiplication map A5→A6 detects an acceptable chart. Compute the rational matrices M_i=mult(ell)^−1 mult(v_i). Their joint eigenvalues are the normalized coordinates v_i/ell(v).

For a separating matrix, scan the same moment-curve combinations of M_i for 0≤t≤\(\binom r2(n-1)\). Each pair of distinct normalized points forbids at most n−1 values. Squarefreeness of the characteristic polynomial detects separation. There are O(n³) chart trials, O(n⁵) separator trials, and O(n log n)-bit trial coefficients.

Factor the separating rational characteristic polynomial. Each rational eigenvalue has a one-dimensional rational eigenspace. Commuting M_i act on that line by rational scalars, yielding exactly one rational normalized point. Conversely a rational point gives a rational eigenvalue. A simple rational eigenvalue cannot hide a nonrational point, because its rational eigenline forces all scalar coordinates to be rational. Thus no algebraic field computation is left uncharged. Rational univariate factorization is an established deterministic polynomial-bit primitive: [Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász 1982](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Lovasz/LovaszLenstraLenstrafactor.pdf), §3 algorithm (3.5), Theorem (3.6), supplies the prerequisite. The primary theorem page was independently inspected visually: it gives O(n^6+n^5 log|f|) arithmetic operations on integers of bit length O(n^3+n^2 log|f|), and therefore polynomial bit complexity. Here its n denotes univariate degree, rather than the hidden quartic's variable count. The theorem is imported rather than independently re-proved.

Let B be the expanded input bit length. Clearing all input denominators costs O(B) bits. The fixed powers and fixed-degree derivatives have coefficient heights O(B+log n), with absolute constants depending on 2053. The ambient column count is \(\binom{n+D_d-1}{D_d}\); the row count is at most \(\binom{n+2}{3}\binom{n+D_d-10}{D_d-9}\). Both are polynomial in n with a very large absolute exponent. Fraction-free rational elimination has polynomial bit cost by determinant/Hadamard bounds; its kernels, quotient bases, inverses and resulting r×r matrices have polynomial bit height in the full matrix dimensions and B. Characteristic-polynomial, gcd, factorization and rational-eigenspace costs consequently remain polynomial. No hidden-L height bound, no computed hidden certificate, and no analytic spectral gap is assumed.

The construction proves a theoretical polynomial bound. Degree 8218 makes a direct implementation impractical on ordinary inputs. No full bound-sized Macaulay run was performed. An adaptive smaller-degree solver cannot claim this universal guarantee merely from successful examples; it requires an independently sound stopping certificate or reaching the proved bound.
