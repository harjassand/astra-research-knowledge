# N35 generator reconstruction and scope attack

## Inherited operation interface

Supply a compact positive polytope \(Q\) in a stoichiometric affine class, facet gaps \(g_i(x)=a_i\cdot x+b_i\), a positive collar width \(\ell_i\), and a strict deterministic inward drift \(\gamma_i\) in that collar for every admitted rate mode. For integer complexes and volume \(V\), the actual concentration jump intensity is \(V k_r\phi^V_{y_r}(x)\), where the falling-factorial monomial satisfies

\[
0\le x^y-\phi_y^V(x)\le E_y(U)/V.
\]

The source proof obtains this inequality by replacing each lattice factor by \(\max(x_j-l/V,0)\), using the first zero factor when there are insufficient reactants, and telescoping the product. The resulting correction is uniform over the entire bounded box, including infeasible-reactant points.

For projected facet jump \(z_{ir}=a_i\cdot\nu_r\), define the rational bounds

\[
D_i=K\sum_r|z_{ir}|E_{y_r}(U),\quad
J_i=\max_r|z_{ir}|,\quad
Q_i=K\sum_r z_{ir}^2 U^{y_r},\quad
\Lambda=K\sum_r U^{y_r}.
\]

When \(V\ge2D_i/\gamma_i\), actual lattice drift in the collar remains at least \(\mu_i=\gamma_i/2\); projected quadratic activity is at most \(Q_i\), and total activity at most \(V\Lambda\). These bounds are inherited from the supplied model; estimating them from rare events is not part of the proof.

## Generator inequality

Set \(\theta_i=\min(J_i^{-1},\mu_i/(3Q_i))\), \(\Phi_i=e^{-\theta_i Vg_i}\), \(\Phi=\sum_i\Phi_i\), and \(c=\min_i\theta_i\ell_i\). The predictable generator is

\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_i(x)}{\Phi_i(x)}
=V\sum_r k_r\phi_{y_r}^V(x)(e^{-\theta_i z_{ir}}-1).
\]

In the collar, use \(e^z-1\le z+z^2e^{|z|}/2\) and \(e^{\theta_iJ_i}\le e<3\):

\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi_i/\Phi_i
\le V(-\theta_i\mu_i+3\theta_i^2Q_i/2)
\le -V\theta_i\mu_i/2\le0.
\]

Outside its collar but inside \(Q\), \(\Phi_i\le e^{-\theta_iV\ell_i}\) and every jump factor has positive part below \(2\) because \(\theta_iJ_i\le1\). Summing the at most \(m\) facet leakage terms gives

\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi(x)\le2mV\Lambda e^{-cV}.
\]

At first exit, at least one affine gap is negative, so \(\Phi(X_\tau)\ge1\). Bounded overshoots and bounded stopped total hazard justify stopped Dynkin on every finite \(T\), giving

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le\Phi(X_0)+2mV\Lambda T e^{-cV}
=\sum_i e^{-\theta_iVg_i(X_0)}+2mV\Lambda T e^{-cV}.
\]

This derivation makes no Markov assumption on the controller: the instantaneous generator inequality holds pathwise for each predictable rate vector inside the supplied admitted class. It is not uniform over unbounded rates, uncharged hidden exogenous changes, rates that violate the collar condition, or a learned model that might differ from the plant.

## Worked constants and direct check

For \(2A\to A+B\), \(2B\to A+B\), rates in \([1,4]\), conserved total \(A+B=V\), and \(Q=[1/4,3/4]\) in concentration \(x=A/V\), the left-layer inward ratio obeys

\[
\frac{b}{d}\ge\frac{(1-x)^2}{4x^2}
\ge\frac{529}{324}>\frac{283}{175}\ge e^{12/25}
\quad(1/4\le x\le9/32),
\]

where the finite-volume falling factors only improve the ratio because \(1-x\ge x\). Thus \(b(e^{-\theta}-1)+d(e^\theta-1)\le0\) for \(\theta=12/25\); the right collar is symmetric. The total scaled activity is bounded by \(\Lambda=5/2\), each collar width is \(1/32\), and the leakage coefficient is

\[
2\cdot(5/2)\cdot(e^{12/25}-1)
\le 5\cdot(108/175)=108/35.
\]

Initial gaps are \(1/4\), so \(\theta V g_i=3V/25\), and \(\theta\ell=3/200\). This independently recovers source equation (7):

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le2e^{-3V/25}+\frac{108}{35}VT e^{-3V/200}.
\]

## Scope attack: infinite-time containment is false

For fixed rates \(k_A,k_B>0\), the count \(A=j\) has birth intensity proportional to \((V-j)(V-j-1)\) and death intensity proportional to \(j(j-1)\). The states \(1,\ldots,V-1\) communicate: from each state below \(V-1\), births have positive rate; from each state above 1, deaths have positive rate. At the endpoints the absent same-species pair makes the outward transition rate zero, so this is a finite irreducible closed class. It visits each state almost surely, hence visits \(j=1\). If \(V\ge6\), then \(1<V/4\), and that visit exits \(Q\). Therefore \(\Pr(\tau_Q<\infty)=1\) while equation (7) allows the exit probability to be exponentially small for very long finite horizons. Any theorem that removes finite \(T\) or calls the result permanence is false on this exact model.
