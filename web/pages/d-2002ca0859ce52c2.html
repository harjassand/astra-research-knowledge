# Fixed-power counterexamples with any prescribed finite jet regularity

7Oct2026. **Our deduction from the audited weighted construction and explicit all-order bookkeeping below.** Root independently derived the parameter implication. Not an FI author claim; historical novelty UNKNOWN. This differs from the logarithmic example controlling all derivative orders simultaneously.

For n≥3, any fixed β∈(0,1/2), the same constructed V satisfies

    |D^mV|≤C_m κa^m
          =C_m R^(m(1−2β)/3−β), every fixed m≥0.  (1)

Constants depend on m,n,β and the ONE already chosen K. We do not select a different K for each m. The power solution retains |u|≤C exp(−cr^p), p=(4−2β)/3.

Consequently, for every prescribed finite integer N≥0, choose

    N/(2N+3)<β<1/2.

Then V and all Cartesian derivatives of order≤N tend to zero, while

    1<p<1+1/(2N+3).

At equality β=N/(2N+3), N≥1, the Nth derivative bound is constant rather than vanishing, and p=1+1/(2N+3). For N=0 the equality β=0 is FI's endpoint, separate from the positive-decay construction. In particular N=1 gives β>1/5 and p<6/5, consistent with r12's separately reconstructed first-gradient extension.

Thus any specified FINITE list of bounded absolute derivative norms cannot by itself imply a fixed-power weak Landis statement for all smooth real potentials in dimensions n≥3. The derivative norms are finite existential constants, not prescribed numerical bounds. Dilation can adjust a finite norm list if needed, but no such adjusted constants are charged or claimed here. There is no single fixed β for which bound(1) decays at every order: m(1−2β)/3−β eventually becomes positive. The all-order logarithmic construction instead has envelope r log r and cannot satisfy a fixed-power superlinear upper bound.

## Verification of (1)

Use the original power notation a=R^α, ℓ=R^s, κ=R^(−β), α=(1−2β)/3,s=(1−α)/2. Its normalized C∞ bump partition satisfies |D^jχ_i|≤C_jℓ_i^(−j) for each j on the already fixed cover. The radius derivatives satisfy |D^ja|≤C_j aR^(−j), |D^jℓ|≤C_jℓR^(−j), and |D^jε|≤C_j/(aR^j), ε=σ/a. Also |D^jΦ|≤C_j aR^(1−j) for j≥1 and |D^jE|≤C_j a^jE. These follow by Cartesian differentiation of R=(K²+|x|²)^(1/2) and power compositions, using Ra≥1.

Differentiate the canceled Δf residual exactly as in the log proof, not the crude |D²f| bound:

* For Δq+a²q, the mismatch coefficient is O(κ), its k-th derivative for k≥1 is O_k(a²/R^k), and

      (a²/R^k)/(κa^k)=R^(1−k)/(ℓa^k)≤1.

  Cutoff and cosine differentiation costs at most a because ℓ^(−1)≤a. Thus D^m(Δq+a²q)=O_m(κa^m).

* The tangent coefficient is h_i=a_i R^(α−1)(x·t_i), x_i·t_i=0. Its k-th derivative is bounded by C_k a²[ℓ/R^(k+1)+1/R^k]. The zeroth-order tangency bound is κ, and the displayed ratios show every k≥1 term is ≤C_kκa^k. Partition-gradient radial terms have base a/ℓ=κ and differentiating them costs at most a per derivative.

* D^kΔΦ=O_k(a/R^(k+1))≤C_kκa^k. The origin coefficient K²R^(2α−2), including every derivative, is ≤C_(k,K)κa^k since 2α−2+β<0 and 1/R≤a. The K-dependent constant is permitted after K was fixed once at order zero.

Together with derivatives of E, these prove |D^mΔf|≤C_(m,K)κa^mE. Also |D^mf|≤C_ma^mE. The weighted correction already supplies W=O_K(κa^(−2)E), without differentiating it.

The smooth-part Laplacian identity in the log proof remains exact. Its m-th differentiated commutators have largest scale a^(m+1)E/R≤κa^mE. Arbitrary (m+2)-kernel derivatives of the repair part have size O_m(a^(n+m+2)), on volume O(a^(−n)); multiplying W gives O_(m,K)(κa^mE). Therefore D^mΔF=O_(m,K)(κa^mE), and D^mF=O_(m,K)(a^mE). The induction in D^m(VF)=D^mΔF on |F|≥cτE, plus V identically zero on the harmonic region, proves(1). The gluing is the already audited open-cover gluing.

The one-K quantifier is essential: higher-order bounds introduce finite C_m but no new smallness conditions. This constructs one smooth potential for each β, controlling every fixed derivative by(1); the finite-N conclusion only uses the orders whose exponents are nonpositive.
