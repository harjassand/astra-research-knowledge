# Universal fast matrix-Krylov acquisition for arbitrary binary block contrasts

Cycle11. **Independent mathematical gates PASS:** `notes/root_cycle11/BINARY_BLOCK_KRYLOV_GATE.md` and `notes/metric_geometry10/cycle11/BINARY_AND_BLOCK_KRYLOV_INDEPENDENT_GATE.md`. Historical novelty UNKNOWN. This is an arbitrary-dimensional nongeneric subclass of the original rational hidden-QPD interface, without a supplied metric or block decomposition.

## Exact statement

Input only expanded rational f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2), with unknown rational invertible L and unknown rational Q>0. Assume that, after a permutation of its hidden axes, Q is block diagonal with each block of size1 or2. Every positive-definite binary block is permitted, including negative, positive or zero off-diagonal coefficients, equal diagonals and repeated block copies. The algorithm does not receive the partition, axes, Q, L, an aligned metric or a condition number.

The root's matrix-Krylov covariant has nonzero determinant K for every such input. Consequently its existing coefficient-only exact line/reflection/invariant-metric/axis-selector algorithm acquires all rational axes and a checked positive weighted-square representation in expected polynomial bit time. The theoretical universal polar fallback is not needed for coverage on this class.

## 1. Matrix covariant and block factorization

Let T be the symmetric quartic polarization, H=Hess f, h=det H, A=adj H, B=T:A and C=A B. The detector is

    K(y)=det[y,Cy,...,C^(n-1)y].

The previously gated identities give C_{f composed L}=det(L)^4 L^-1 C_f(Lx)L and hidden-axis divisibility. Work in hidden coordinates only for this proof. On h!=0,

    C/h^2 = H^-1 (T:H^-1).                     (1)

If f is a sum of disjoint block quartics f_b, H and the tensor contraction in(1) are block diagonal. Therefore

    C/h^2=blockdiag(C_b/h_b^2).                 (2)

Scaling block coordinates y_b->t_b y_b multiplies its H_b by t_b^2 and the normalized block in(2) by t_b^-4. Cyclicity of y_b for C_b is unchanged by multiplying C_b by a nonzero scalar or y_b by a scalar.

## 2. Every binary block has a generically cyclic invertible covariant

For p(u,v)=a u^4+2b u^2v^2+c v^4, QPD means a,c>0 and ac-b^2>0. The block detector is

    K_b=det[z,C_b z]=(1/24)det[z,F_b]
       =(1/8)Jac(p,det Hess p),

using F_b=24C_b z and the binary Euler identity. The established sextic

    Jac(p,det Hess p)=1152uv(ac-b^2)(a u^4-c v^4)

is nonzero. Hence a generic rational point is cyclic for C_b.

We also require det C_b not identically zero; a shared zero eigenvalue cannot be removed by block scaling. If b!=0, at z=(1,0), H_b=diag(12a,4b), A_b=diag(4b,12a), and direct contraction gives

    B_b=diag(8ab,12ac+4b^2/3),
    C_b=diag(32ab^2,144a^2c+16ab^2).

Both entries are nonzero, and their difference16a(9ac-b^2)>0 also establishes generic simple spectrum, although simplicity is not needed for the composition proof. If b=0, at a point uv!=0 the diagonal formula C_ii=h_b^2/(144q_ii y_i^4) gives invertibility and cyclicity whenever a u^4!=c v^4. For a one-variable block C_b=a!=0 and its unique coordinate is cyclic whenever nonzero.

Thus the product h_b det C_b K_b is a nonzero polynomial for every permitted block. A rational point satisfying all three exists. It is used only to prove nonzero K; no search for it is performed by the acquisition algorithm.

## 3. Separate block spectra without supplying them

Choose one such rational point per block and let M_b=C_b/h_b^2 there. Each M_b is invertible and its coordinate vector is cyclic. It has a finite multiset of nonzero complex eigenvalues, possibly repeated within a block. Vary the positive rational t_b independently. For any two blocks their rescaled spectra overlap only when (t_b/t_c)^4 equals one of finitely many nonzero eigenvalue ratios. A choice avoiding all these equations exists over Q; equivalently the pairwise characteristic-polynomial resultants are nonzero rational polynomials in the scaling variables.

At that choice the block characteristic polynomials are pairwise coprime, and each block vector is cyclic. Their direct sum is cyclic: for each block, polynomials in M_b applied to its cyclic vector generate its whole block; the Chinese remainder theorem allows those polynomials to be prescribed independently on the pairwise coprime block characteristic polynomials. The full span of y,M y,... is all R^n, where M=C/h^2. Cayley-Hamilton reduces it to the first n columns. Thus their determinant is nonzero. Multiplication of column k by h^(2k) recovers K, also nonzero.

This proof treats block spectra only as existence devices. It charges no algebraic-number arithmetic, eigenvalue separation, hidden scaling height or block recognition procedure to the learner. Polynomial nonvanishing plus the existing rational-line algorithm supplies the complete cost interface from the original coefficients.

## 4. Cost, verification and limitations

The covariant entry degree is4n-4 and deg K=n+2n(n-1)^2. The established dual QQ[t] circuit evaluates K and all ambient derivatives with polynomial coefficient heights, without multivariate determinant expansion. The standard good-line polynomial has degree<2n deg K, so a grid of size>8n deg K has success probability at least3/4. Rational root factorization, multiplicity-aware normal extraction, exact reflection checks, rational invariant-metric projection, score/orthogonality selection and final even-QPD/SOS re-expansion retain their already gated polynomial bit charges. Expected retries are at most4/3. The same final verifier remains mandatory; the promise is used only for coverage.

No implementation change is required: root's `work/root_cycle10/krylov_line_acquisition.py` receives solely f and variables. This theorem proves its coverage on a lower-dimensional family with many off-diagonal zeros and repeated block symmetries. It is not merely a finite fixture or a generic distinct-spectrum claim. The algorithm does not detect the hidden binary partition first. The polynomial bit theorem invokes standard polynomial-time rational linear algebra and factorization; it does not derive worst-case complexity for the SymPy prototype from finite runtimes.

General composition principle: any block families for which h_b, det C_b and K_b are nonzero polynomials compose in the same way. Proving that property for all ternary QPD blocks would extend this practical guarantee further, but the universal ternary **flow** extactic theorem does not imply it for the different matrix-Krylov detector. Arbitrary-n universal K remains open. Classical binary acquisition is prior; the direct-sum covariant consequence's historical status has not been established.
