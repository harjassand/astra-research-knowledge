## Exact interface and claim

Supply the labeled three-state baseline graph 0↔1↔2 and2→0, ordinary rates in[.7,1.3], and one added edge0→2 with b∈[0,1]. No ordinary rate values or local b bracket are supplied. Observe one carried CTMC through calibrated positive lags and full endpoint labels, with parameter-independent initial label0. All readings, elapsed time and returns are charged; path events and resets are unavailable. The initial label costs a reading but carries no parameter information.

Let H_(k−1) include all previous labels, actions and auxiliary randomization. The next lag t_k and starting label x_k are known before endpoint Y_k. Under candidate Q,

    p_Q,k(y)=exp(t_k Q)[x_k,y].

Choose any H_(k−1)-measurable normalized positive prediction q_k(y). Put

    M_K=product_(k≤K)q_k(Y_k),
    L_K(Q)=product_(k≤K)p_Q,k(Y_k),
    C_K={Q:L_K(Q)≥delta M_K}.

For every admitted true Q, M_K/L_K(Q) is a nonnegative martingale starting at1. Indeed, conditional expectation of its multiplier is sum_y p_Q,k(y)q_k(y)/p_Q,k(y)=1. All admitted baseline generators remain irreducible even at b=0, so every positive-lag kernel entry is positive. With non-full support the same proof gives a supermartingale if predicted mass outside true support is dropped in the expectation. Common parameter-independent action probabilities cancel; adaptive timing and a carried state do not require independent endpoint observations or stationary initialization.

Ville's inequality therefore gives

    Pr_Q(exists K:Q notin C_K)≤delta.

There is no union over candidate Q or nuisance dimension. For each fixed true Q the point-indexed martingale gives its coverage; inversion supplies a confidence sequence over the full continuous class. Predictably skipped observations can have multiplier1 while remaining charged, but observations cannot be skipped after examining their endpoint. Initial randomization/state may be conditioned on only if its distribution is parameter-independent.

A predictor fitted retrospectively to an endpoint is unsafe. For one fair binary observation, assigning probability1 to the already observed outcome gives apparent numerator1 for both possible data strings; its ratio to the fair likelihood has expectation2. Each retrospectively selected probability vector is normalized, yet the selected numerator is not predictable. Our structured predictor uses only preceding blocks.

## Three implemented normalized numerators

1. **Saturated Dirichlet**: a symmetric Dirichlet(1,1,1) predictor independently for each visited(start label,lag). If cell counts are C_j and N=sum C_j, its ordered-sequence probability is2! product C_j!/(N+2)!. Over all cells,

       M=1/D,
       D=product_cells[binom(N+2,2) multinomial(N;C)].

   D is an integer. No multinomial coefficient belongs in the observed-sequence likelihood L(Q); the coefficient appears only in this algebraic inverse-predictor formula. Counts suffice to reconstruct M because this predictor is exchangeable within each cell, even when visits were selected adaptively.

2. **Previous-fit structured predictor**: the joint fitted generator from the preceding1,024-reading block predicts the next block. An approximate floating semigroup row is converted to positive integer weights floor(2^52 p_j), each at least1, then divided by its exact integer row sum. These rational rows are exactly normalized, regardless fitting or semigroup approximation. They define a valid numerator even if the fitted generator is inaccurate. Current-block outcomes do not enter its prediction. Global MLE optimality and a nuisance confidence certificate are unnecessary for numerator validity.

3. **Fixed half mixture**: M_mix=(M_Dirichlet+M_structured)/2. This is the likelihood of an initial model-predictor choice, or equivalently a predictable posterior-weighted mixture. Consequently its ratio to L(Q) is a nonnegative martingale. Its log-loss is at most the better component's log-loss plus log2. Selecting the better component after seeing data without this mixture/payment would not justify the same guarantee.

All retrospective frozen-data comparisons below are fixed-data width audits, not new coverage experiments. Future use must predeclare the predictive recursion or account for data-driven predictor selection. Candidate inclusion proofs remain deterministic statements about the specified set on those data.

## Cheap global outer projection, with all nuisance values unknown

For all observed lag/state cells, the saturated likelihood Lsat satisfies the algebraic identity

    log Lsat−log L(Q)=sum_cells N_cell KL(phat_cell||P_Q,cell).

For accepted Q, this sum is at most

    Bobs=log Lsat−log M+log(1/delta).

All terms are nonnegative. Hence for any visited source-0 cell,

    N0 kl(phat_02||P_Q,02)≤Bobs.

Binary KL contraction follows by grouping the other two outcomes. Rational bisection of the two KL roots, with interval log evaluations and outward bounds, gives P_Q,02∈[l_t,u_t]. This is a deterministic necessary condition for every accepted continuous Q, not an iid binomial confidence assertion and not a nuisance plug-in.

The largest escape rate is2.6: state0 has at most1.3+1=2.3, states1/2 at most2.6. Exactly one rare jump and no others gives P02≥bt e^(−2.6t). Baseline endpoint2 requires an ordered0→1 then1→2 pair.     [max(0,(l_t−.845t²)/t), min(1,e^(2.6t)u_t/t)].
