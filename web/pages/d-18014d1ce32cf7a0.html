Every projective variety is a quiver Grassmannian



Markus Reineke





Quiver Grassmannians are varieties Gr𝐞Q​(V){\rm Gr}_{\bf e}^{Q}(V) parametrizing subrepresentations of dimension vector 𝐞{\bf e} of a representation VV of a quiver QQ [1]. The aim of this note is to prove the claim in the title, which implies that no special properties of quiver Grassmannians can be expected without restricting the choices of QQ, VV and 𝐞{\bf e}. More precisely we prove:




Theorem 0.1



Every projective variety is isomorphic to a quiver Grassmannian Gr𝐞Q​(V){\rm Gr}^{Q}_{\bf e}(V), for an acyclic quiver QQ with at most three vertices, a Schurian representation VV, and a thin dimension vector 𝐞{\bf e} (i.e. 𝐞i≤1{\bf e}_{i}\leq 1 for all i∈Q0i\in Q_{0}).





The author would like to thank B. Keller for posing the question answered here, and G. Cerulli-Irelli, E. Feigin, O. Lorscheid and A. Zelevinsky for helpful discussions.

So let XX be an arbitrary projective variety, given by a set of homogeneous polynomial equations on the coordinates of 𝐏n{\bf P}^{n}. Without loss of generality, we can assume these equations to be homogeneous of the same degree dd. Using the dd-uple embedding j:𝐏n→𝐏M−1j:{\bf P}^{n}\rightarrow{\bf P}^{M-1}, we can define j⁡(X)≃Xj(X)\simeq X by equations defining j⁡(𝐏n)j({\bf P}^{n}) inside 𝐏M−1{\bf P}^{M-1}, together with linear equations φ1,…,φk\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k} on the coordinates of 𝐏M−1{\bf P}^{M-1}.





To make this more explicit, let Mn,dM_{n,d} be the set of tuples 𝐦=(m0,…,mn){\bf m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{n}) in 𝐍n+1{\bf N}^{n+1} summing up to dd, so that MM is the cardinality of Mn,dM_{n,d}, and jj maps homogeneous coordinates (x0:…:xn)(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n}) to (:x𝐦:)𝐦∈Mn,d(:x^{\bf m}:)_{{\bf m}\in M_{n,d}}, where x𝐦=x0m0​…​xnmnx^{\bf m}=x_{0}^{m_{0}}\ldots x_{n}^{m_{n}}. A point xx in 𝐏M{\bf P}^{M} with homogeneous coordinates (:x𝐦:)𝐦∈Mn,d(:x_{\bf m}:)_{{\bf m}\in M_{n,d}} belongs to the image of jj if and only if the quadratic conditions x𝐦​x𝐦′=x𝐦′′​x𝐦′′′x_{\bf m}x_{\bf m^{\prime}}=x_{\bf m^{\prime\prime}}x_{\bf m^{\prime\prime\prime}} for all 𝐦+𝐦′=𝐦′′+𝐦′′′{\bf m}+{\bf m^{\prime}}={\bf m^{\prime\prime}}+{\bf m^{\prime\prime\prime}} are fulfilled. Actually it suffices to impose the conditions (denoting by 𝐞i{\bf e}_{i} the tuple with entry 11 in the ii-th position)




x𝐧+𝐞ix𝐧′+𝐞j=x𝐧+𝐞jx𝐧′+𝐞i for all 𝐧∈Mn,d−1,i=0,…,n.x_{{\bf n}+{\bf e}_{i}}x_{{\bf n^{\prime}}+{\bf e}_{j}}=x_{{\bf n}+{\bf e}_{j}}x_{{\bf n^{\prime}}+{\bf e}_{i}}\mbox{ for all }{\bf n}\in M_{n,d-1},\,i=0,\ldots,n.



These conditions can be rewritten as follows: we define a matrix A⁡(x)A(x) with rows indexed by the 𝐧∈Mn,d−1{\bf n}\in M_{n,d-1} and columns indexed by the i=0,…,ni=0,\ldots,n, with (𝐧,i)({\bf n},i)-th entry being x𝐧+𝐞ix_{{\bf n}+{\bf e}_{i}}. Then the previous quadratic conditions can be compactly reformulated as the rank of AA being 11, because they translate precisely to vanishing of all two-by-two minors of AA. Thus we can define (a variety isomorphic to) XX as the set of points x∈𝐏Mx\in{\bf P}^{M} such that A⁡(x)A(x) has rank 11 and φj​(x)=0\varphi_{j}(x)=0 for j=1,…,kj=1,\ldots,k.





These defining equations are easily realized as a quiver Grassmannian: let QQ be the quiver with set of vertices {1,2,3}\{1,2,3\}, with kk arrows from 22 to 11 and n+1n+1 arrows from 22 to 33. Define a representation VV of QQ of dimension vector (1,M,M′)(1,M,M^{\prime}), for M′M^{\prime} the cardinality of Mn,d−1M_{n,d-1}, as follows: let V1=𝐂V_{1}={\bf C}, the space V2V_{2} has a basis (v𝐦)𝐦∈Mn,d(v_{\bf m})_{{\bf m}\in M_{n,d}}, and V3V_{3} has a basis (v𝐧)𝐧∈Mn,d−1(v_{\bf n})_{{\bf n}\in M_{n,d-1}}. The maps representing the arrows from 22 to 11 are given by the linear forms φj\varphi_{j}. The ii-th arrow from 22 to 33, with i=0,…,ni=0,\ldots,n, is given by the linear map fif_{i} sending v𝐦v_{\bf m} to v𝐦−𝐞iv_{{\bf m}-{\bf e}_{i}} if mi≠0m_{i}\not=0, and to 00 otherwise. Define the dimension vector 𝐞=(0,1,1){\bf e}=(0,1,1). Then a point of Gr𝐞​(V){\rm Gr}_{\bf e}(V) is determined by a vector vv in V2V_{2} which is annihilated by all φj\varphi_{j}, and such that the span of the images f0​(v),…,fn​(v)f_{0}(v),\ldots,f_{n}(v) is one-dimensional. Writing v=∑𝐦∈Mn,dx𝐦​v𝐦v=\sum_{{\bf m}\in M_{n,d}}x_{\bf m}v_{\bf m}, the image fi​(v)f_{i}(v), written in coordinates v𝐧v_{\bf n}, is precisely the ii-th column of A⁡(x)A(x), so that the span being one-dimensional means that the rank of A⁡(x)A(x) equals 11. This proves all claims except VV being Schurian.





To prove this, assume we are given endomorphisms φ\varphi of V2V_{2} and ψ\psi of V3V_{3} such that fi​φ=ψ​fif_{i}\varphi=\psi f_{i} for all i=0,…,ni=0,\ldots,n. Writing φ⁡(v𝐦)=∑𝐦′a𝐦,𝐦′​v𝐦′\varphi(v_{\bf m})=\sum_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}v_{{\bf m}^{\prime}} and ψ⁡(v𝐧)=∑𝐧′b𝐧,𝐧′​v𝐧′\psi(v_{\bf n})=\sum_{{\bf n}^{\prime}}b_{{\bf n},{\bf n}^{\prime}}v_{{\bf n}^{\prime}}, this condition means that, for all i=0,…,ni=0,\ldots,n such that mi′≠0{m}^{\prime}_{i}\not=0, we have

(i)




a𝐦,𝐦′=0a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=0 if mi=0{m}_{i}=0,

(ii)




a𝐦,𝐦′=b𝐦−𝐞i,𝐦′−𝐞ia_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=b_{{\bf m}-{\bf e}_{i},{\bf m}^{\prime}-{\bf e}_{i}} if mi≠0{m}_{i}\not=0.



The second condition implies that a𝐦,𝐦′=a𝐦−𝐞i+𝐞j,𝐦′−𝐞i+𝐞ja_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=a_{{\bf m}-{\bf e}_{i}+{\bf e}_{j},{\bf m}^{\prime}-{\bf e}_{i}+{\bf e}_{j}} if mi≠0≠mi{m}_{i}\not=0\not={m}_{i}, and thus the following translation principle: a𝐦,𝐦′=a𝐦+𝐪,𝐦′+𝐪a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=a_{{\bf m}+{\bf q},{\bf m}^{\prime}+{\bf q}} for all 𝐪=(q0,…,qn){\bf q}=(q_{0},\ldots,q_{n}) with ∑iqi=0\sum_{i}q_{i}=0 whenever all coefficients are defined. Given a pair 𝐦≠𝐦′{\bf m}\not={\bf m}^{\prime}, we can find an index ii such that mi<mi′{m}_{i}<{m}^{\prime}_{i}, thus a𝐦,𝐦′=a𝐦−mi​𝐞i+mi​𝐞j,𝐦′−mi​𝐞i+mi​𝐞ja_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=a_{{\bf m}-{m}_{i}{\bf e}_{i}+{m}_{i}{\bf e}_{j},{\bf m}^{\prime}-{m}_{i}{\bf e}_{i}+{m}_{i}{\bf e}_{j}} for some j≠ij\not=i by translation. Condition (i) now implies a𝐦,𝐦′=0a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=0. Moreover, all coefficients a𝐦,𝐦′a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}} coincide by translation, thus a𝐦,𝐦′=C​δ𝐦,𝐦′a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}=C\delta_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}} for some scalar CC. All coefficients b𝐧,𝐧′b_{{\bf n},{\bf n}^{\prime}} being determined by the a𝐦,𝐦′a_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}} by condition (ii), we have b𝐧,𝐧′=C​δ𝐧,𝐧′b_{{\bf n},{\bf n}^{\prime}}=C\delta_{{\bf n},{\bf n}^{\prime}} as well. Now a non-zero φk\varphi_{k} forces the endomorphism of VV to be just CC times the identity. In case all φk\varphi_{k} are zero, we just omit the vertex 11 from the quiver. The theorem is proved.
