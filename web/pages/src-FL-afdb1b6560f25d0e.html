# Product-state work recovery after chemistry

**Disposition.** A complete ideal cycle gives a countermodel to extending the source's survivor-only bound to policies that can extract work from a reaction product. For an exact matched case with source productive branching fraction a = 1/2, it reduces the ideal work requirement from E/a = 12.28 eV per CO to E + ΔG = 8.8738 eV per CO by recovering the product's residual resource E − ΔG. With no irreversible attrition, the fully reversible limit is ΔG. A policy-invariant lower bound remains: a fully reset process must supply at least the products' chemical free-energy increase; conditioning and irreversible costs contribute to the nonnegative excess above that floor unless they are already included in the product-stream boundary. No compatible molecule, reaction, product-state work extractor, or net returned work was acquired. The CO2 calculation is a quantified opportunity screen, not a physically qualified invention or demonstrated advantage.

## The candidate missing operation and its ideal cycle

The supplied audit prepares an excited reactant, permits reaction, and recovers work only from molecules still in the original addressable excited-reactant state. Its lower bound is correct for that operation. The extension examined here is to extract non-equilibrium free energy from the product after chemical identity changes, before collisions, internal conversion, fluorescence, or quenching turn ordered energy into heat.

The countermodel is deliberately favorable and explicit. E is an ideal accessible free-energy increment, not simply molecular energy. Substituting the observed 6.14 eV high-J energy for E is an optimistic screen; it does not establish that the ensemble or state can deliver that much work. Let the ready reactant be R, with its zero of free energy set to zero. Preparing the addressed reactant R* costs E of work and gives it excess free energy E. Let the specified output streams in their ground/passive states have chemical free-energy change ΔGchem > 0. Suppose the product has an addressable pure excited state P* whose excess work potential over the passive product P is RP = E − ΔGchem ≥ 0. Thus the loaded reactant resource and the excited product's total free-energy increase agree:

    F(R*) − F(R) = E
    F(P*) − F(R) = ΔGchem + RP = E.

The product-state operation is an energy-conserving map on a work-storage ladder,

    |P*> |n>W  ->  |P> |n+1>W,       HW |n>W = n RP |n>W.

This exact map is defined for the ideal model with a pure product-state pair whose energy gap equals RP; the work store rises by precisely the energy removed from P*. An ideal reversible chemical leg connects R* to P* while exchanging heat with a single bath at T as required by the entropy change. The controller, battery, and any measurement memory return to their initial states. The reaction leg has equal endpoint free energies in this model, the extractor returns RP to the same bus that paid for activation, and the final product streams retain ΔGchem. The full cycle therefore requires ΔGchem net work per product and has zero entropy production in the ideal limit.

This is a mathematically complete stochastic thermodynamic countermodel, not a proposed molecular Hamiltonian. It assumes the very missing physical operation under investigation: a reaction-compatible, product-state coupling that captures work at the bus. It also identifies why an ergotropy number, anti-Stokes photon, coherence change, or energy lost from a molecule is insufficient. The credited amount is only net work delivered through the complete return path.

For a molecule in a mixed state ρ with Hamiltonian H, unitary ergotropy is

    Werg(ρ,H) = Tr(ρH) − min_U Tr(UρU†H).

This is not generally equal to total internal energy or to E − ΔGchem. The ideal RP credit is valid only for the specified pure-state model (or a separately justified work-extraction protocol with an equivalent net return). Passive or thermal product states have zero unitary ergotropy.

## Exact stochastic ledger

Use mutually exclusive outcomes per activation attempt:

- p: one saleable product; its recoverable product-state resource is RP = E − ΔGchem.
- s: unchanged excited reactant, with resource E.
- ℓ: nonproductive attrition, with at most γE residual resource, where 0 ≤ γ ≤ 1.
- p + s + ℓ = 1.

Let η be the wall-to-addressed-state preparation efficiency. Let ρS, ρP, and ρL be the fractions of available branch resources delivered back to the same cyclic work bus after conversion and return losses. In particular, ρP is the joint, branch-conditional net return in the same reaction regime; it includes relaxation or attrition during unloading, extractor-field input, capture, conversion, and return losses. It is not an independent ergotropy multiplier assumed compatible with reaction. Let h ≥ 0 be expected per-attempt auxiliary work for sensing, actuation, feedback, memory reset, and other controls not already deducted in the net ρ terms. This disjoint definition avoids charging an extraction pulse twice. Let cout ≥ 0 be per-saleable-product work for separation, compression, cooling, and other output operations not included in ΔGchem. The expected net work per useful product is

    w = cout + [E/η + h − sρS E − pρP RP − ℓρL γE] / p.

For E ≥ ΔGchem, this is exactly

    w = cout + ΔGchem
              + (1 − ρP) RP
              + (E/p)(1/η − 1)
              + (sE/p)(1 − ρS)
              + (ℓE/p)(1 − ρL γ)
              + h/p.

Each term after ΔGchem is nonnegative. To verify the identity, expand the right-hand side and use p + s + ℓ = 1 and E − RP = ΔGchem. Thus partial attrition, imperfect survivor recovery, imperfect product recovery, loading loss, paid feedback, and output conditioning are all explicit; no branch is silently credited with work.

Any history-dependent policy induces some joint outcome and return law. It can change p, s, ℓ, γ and the ρ values, but it cannot evade the global cyclic free-energy inequality below. In particular, partial attrition helps only if the residual energy is actually captured: the loss penalty is ℓE(1 − ρLγ)/p. State-selective recovery must be measured in the same reaction regime as the branch probabilities.

## Scope of the supplied bound and the countermodel

In the supplied constant competing-hazard model, a = λr/(λr + λℓ), s = exp(−Kt), p = a(1 − s), and ℓ = (1 − a)(1 − s). With perfect loading, no overhead, full loss of excitation on the nonproductive branch, and recovery only from unchanged survivors, the expected work per product is

    wsource = E(1 − s)/p = E/a = E(1 + λℓ/λr).

The holding-time terms cancel; that is the exact lower bound for that operation.

For a direct matched example, choose a = 1/2 and s = 1/2. Then p = 1/4 and ℓ = 1/4. The survivor-only ideal costs E/a = 12.28 eV per CO when E = 6.14 eV. Give the extended policy perfect survivor return and perfect product-state return, but no recovery from the attrition branch. The paid activation energy per useful product is E/p = 24.56 eV; returned work is sE/p = 12.28 eV from survivors plus RP from each product, so

    wextended = E + ΔGchem = 8.8738 eV/CO

for the 14-bar CO output screen below. The 3.4062 eV improvement equals RP. This exact comparison preserves the same branch probabilities and gives credit only for the added product-state extractor. It refutes extending E/a to that extra operation; it does not establish that the operation exists in a real reaction.

This a = 1/2 example still costs more work than the 3.344 eV/CO ISPT electricity figure. Only the loss-free reversible endpoint reaches 2.734 eV/CO, below that electrical comparator before all unmeasured device costs; neither establishes a practical process advantage.

A second exact case makes the entropy/attrition distinction clear. If ℓ = 0, then every nonproduct is a recoverable survivor (a = 1 in the supplied competing-hazard parameterization). With ideal preparation and survivor and product return, the expected work is ΔGchem for every 0 < p ≤ 1. This is the reversible limit, not a case with a < 1. In a real case with ℓ > 0, the loss term remains unless that branch's residual work potential is also captured.

There is a policy-invariant obstruction for the fully cyclic physical boundary. For one bath at T, define Wnet as net work supplied to the entire process, Q as heat entering it, ΔU as total internal-energy change, and ΔS as total entropy change. With controllers, battery, and memory restored to their starting states, the process free-energy change is the chemical stream change ΔGchem. The first law and second law give

    Wnet = ΔU − Q
    Σ = ΔS − Q/T ≥ 0
    Wnet − ΔGchem = ΔU − Q − (ΔU − TΔS) = TΣ ≥ 0.

The work cannot be less than ΔGchem, and the excess equals irreversible free-energy destruction under this single-bath model. This is ordinary thermodynamics, not a new theorem. If the process uses multiple heat reservoirs, electrical/thermal utilities or stored fields, include their exergy changes and source resets at the same boundary. An unreset measurement record is a growing resource, not free feedback. If an explicit three-outcome classical record is reset at 298.15 K, its Landauer floor is kBT H(p,s,ℓ) per attempt; the balanced binary and maximally uncertain three-outcome examples are 0.01781 and 0.02823 eV per attempt. Those are reset floors only; measurement and control costs are additional. A coherent feedback interaction can avoid a classical record but not its physical control costs.

## CO2 thermochemical scale and conventional comparator

The activation source uses E = 6.14 eV as a high-energy rotational-state example, not as an ensemble mean or evidence of chemical yield. The primary optical-centrifuge study reports this energy only at the high-J tail at 380 Torr. It reports that a centrifugal barrier prevents dissociation at those rotational energies; its simulations find stronger rotation-to-vibration transfer for moderate rotational excitation, while states above J = 200 are not important contributors. The same experiment therefore does not provide evidence for a state that both reacts usefully and unloads efficiently.

From NIST-JANAF formation data at 298.15 K and 0.1 MPa for

    CO2(g,1 bar) -> CO(g,1 bar) + 0.5 O2(g,1 bar),

ΔG° = 257.226 kJ/mol CO and ΔH° = 282.995 kJ/mol CO. The derived reaction entropy is ΔS = 86.4315 J/(mol K), so the reversible isothermal heat duty is TΔS = 25.770 kJ/mol. It is recorded separately from work. Reversibly compressing pure CO from 1 bar to 14 bar absolute (13 barg) adds RT ln(14) = 6.542 kJ/mol. Holding the oxygen activity at its 1-bar standard state, the output free-energy floor is therefore

    ΔGchem = 263.768 kJ/mol = 2.73376 eV/CO = 2.616 kWh/kg CO.

For the idealized E = 6.14 eV input, the pure product-state model has RP = 3.40624 eV/CO. This recovery returns the excess above product chemical and pressure free energy; it does not make CO2 conversion free.

The matched system comparator retrieved is the ISPT/TNO/Air Liquide SOEC use-case model. It targets CO at 13 barg and at least 96 mol% purity, with a 95/5 CO2/CO stack feed, 750°C operation, 50% single-pass conversion, and enriched air at 30% O2. It reports 3.2 kWh/kg CO electricity: 2.7 stack, 0.2 feed heating, 0.1 air heating, and 0.2 compression. The report assumes excess steam and does not charge steam generation. This is a modeled system figure, not a demonstrated plant result.

The ideal reversible work floor leaves a conditional margin of 0.584 kWh/kg (0.61058 eV/CO) below the modeled 3.2 kWh/kg comparator. Every unmeasured loss, control, source conversion, conditioning, separation, and output cost must fit within this one margin. This is not an apples-to-apples process advantage: the thermodynamic screen assumes pure CO at 14 bar, slightly stricter than the SOEC report's minimum 96 mol% purity; activation-route heat demand and temperature, oxygen-side handling, CO separation, cooling, laser wall-plug efficiency, and throughput have not been acquired. As a deliberately conservative no-heat-credit screen, ΔH plus ideal CO compression is 2.871 kWh/kg and leaves 0.329 kWh/kg before the same additional costs. It is an enthalpy-based screen, not a full utility model.

Useful one-at-a-time thresholds against the modeled SOEC boundary are:

| Loss considered alone | Requirement to remain below 3.2 kWh/kg |
|---|---:|
| Product-state work returned to bus | ρP > 0.82075 |
| Wall-to-addressed-state loading | η > 0.90955 |
| Full-loss attrition with perfect product recovery | ℓ/p < 0.09944; if all nonproducts are attrition, p > 0.90955 |
| All remaining control, reset, conditioning, heat, separation costs | combined remainder < 0.61058 eV/CO |

These conditions spend the same margin and cannot be added independently. At 80% product return or 90% loading efficiency, modeled work rises to 3.415 and 3.416 eV/CO respectively before output conditioning. No matched measurements exist.

A separate Haldor Topsoe presentation reports a dry-CO2 stack run of about 14,000 h, 30,000 Nm3 (35.5 t) CO, 3.44 kWh/Nm3, and Faradaic efficiency of 99 ± 1%. The reported energy and mass imply about 2.91 kWh/kg at stack boundary. This is a scale and durability cross-check; it is not the same purified-product system boundary as ISPT.

## Attrition, useful cascades, and throughput

A useful cascade can increase products per absorbed quantum, but the products' free energies still add. At the target output pressure, one 6.14 eV input can pay at most two CO output free-energy increments in a zero-auxiliary-work idealization because floor(6.14/2.73376) = 2; the residual is only 0.672 eV. This is an energy ceiling, not a proposed kinetic mechanism or yield. A real cascade needs a specified intermediate, transfer coupling, selectivity, termination law, and cost for every energetic feed. If it consumes an energetic substrate, its incoming free energy must be charged.

For Naddr simultaneously addressed molecules, per-molecule cycle frequency fcyc, and jointly measured expected number K of saleable products per addressed cycle,

    NdotCO = Naddr × fcyc × E[K].

Density or faster cycling may raise throughput. It lowers energy per product only if it changes the jointly measured reaction, attrition, and extraction law. No addressed rate, product rate, cycle frequency, lifetime, or joint kinetics were acquired. Coherence decay by itself is not evidence of lost extractable energy.

## Capital applicability and prior-art boundary

N783 was checked against its pinned dossier, review card, gates, producer research record, and repair/critic review. Its conditional reward certificates concern a specific defect-repair event that leaves a mobile excitation capable of another retained repair, with full carrier/configuration state, a retention horizon, and a joint next-state/reward/damage/cost law. Those premises do not hold here: CO is a terminal chemical product, there is no reusable carrier chain or retained repair count, and no joint state kernel was acquired. N783 is not applicable and is not imported or rebranded.

The idea of extracting work from a non-passive state is prior art (ergotropy); measurement and feedback costs are also prior art. Coherent Raman control and molecular rotational energy transfer are established. The narrow candidate distinction is reaction-compatible, net-to-work-bus product-state recovery in the same useful-chemistry regime. No complete priority search was performed, so novelty remains unknown. The result is a scoped candidate operation and ideal countermodel, not a novelty claim.

## Decisive physical gate

The candidate is falsified for a chosen chemistry if no product state with enough extractable free energy exists before relaxation, or if the measured return/attrition/conditioning terms exceed the available budget. The first experiment must make one joint measurement at a fixed chemical composition, pressure, temperature, and throughput regime of:

1. wall work needed to prepare the addressed reactant and its state distribution;
2. probabilities p, s, and ℓ in that same reaction environment;
3. product density matrix, or a state-resolved proxy with a validated lower bound on extractable work, before collisional relaxation;
4. energy lost by molecules, collected optical/other output, heat, product composition, oxygen-side output, and net work actually delivered back to the cyclic bus;
5. complete detector, pulse, feedback/memory reset, source, extraction, cooling, separation, compression, and thermal-utility costs;
6. saleable CO rate, purity, and pressure at the comparator boundary.

Controls: no reaction/no extractor; prepared reactant with no extractor; reaction plus product-state extractor; detuned extractor; and a matched SOEC output run. An anti-Stokes photon, lost coherence, or reduced gas temperature alone does not pass. The same run must produce saleable CO at the specified purity and pressure and demonstrate lower total charged electricity and thermal exergy than a matched SOEC boundary, with uncertainty and throughput reported. The product-state work must be traced to a bus that subsequently reduces source work; a changed molecular population alone is not enough.

The current CO2 centrifuge result fails the co-occurrence gate: the 6.14 eV tail is protected from dissociation by a centrifugal barrier, while the more effective transfer states are less rotationally excited. The result therefore stops at a mathematically complete countermodel, conventional thermodynamic obstruction, quantified thresholds, and an unpassed physical gate. It is not a validated chemistry, scalable throughput claim, or civilization-enabling capability.
