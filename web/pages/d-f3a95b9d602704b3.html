## Independent finite-coordinate proof

Fix n distinct route centers, outside paths and independent base probability laws F_i for their complete vertex factors. Let J be the incident hard-check indicator and ν=J∏F_i/Z the target conditional law. Opponent assignments may violate checks between themselves, but must satisfy individual restrictions. Assume every single-factor admissible set A_i(x) has F_i-mass at least a=1−χ_D>0.

Changing one other factor j changes at most two checks, one per copy. Under F_i each old or new set of those two check failures has mass at most2e_D. Thus

\[
 F_i(A_i(x)\triangle A_i(y))\le4e_D.
\]

For any sets A,B with masses at least a, their normalized indicator densities have overlap F_i(A∩B)/max(F_i(A),F_i(B)). Their total variation distance is

\[
 1-\frac{F_i(A\cap B)}{\max(F_i(A),F_i(B))}
 \le\frac{F_i(A\triangle B)}a.
\]

Hence each neighbor influence is at most4e_D/a. The intermediate configurations in a coordinate-by-coordinate telescoping may be globally forbidden. The primary's explicit individually permitted quantifier is exactly what keeps their conditional kernels well defined. A premise only on globally valid configurations would not have licensed this proof.

Confinement makes factors at supremum lattice distance greater than30 noninteracting; at most Δ=61³−1 other route centers can be within distance30. Therefore the influence row sum is at most c=4Δe_D/a. Periodic identification can only decrease this count. Fixed outside paths affect acceptance but add no moving coordinates.

For random-scan updates, couple to a stationary copy and use maximal coupling on the chosen coordinate. Such couplings exist measurably on the standard Borel factor spaces: the conditional laws here have measurable indicator densities with respect to the same F_i, so their common-part construction is explicit at the proof level. If p_i(s) is the probability of coordinate disagreement, then

\[
 p_i(s+1)\le(1-1/n)p_i(s)+(1/n)\sum_jc_{ij}p_j(s).
\]

Taking the maximum over coordinates proves, without any column-sum assumption,

\[
 \max_i p_i(s)\le[1-(1-c)/n]^s,\qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L(X_s),\nu)
 \le n e^{-(1-c)s/n}.
\]

For c<1, s=ceil[n log(n/ε)/(1−c)] updates suffice. This is the standard Dobrushin coupling mechanism specialized to the supplied continuous factor interface, not a new general mixing theorem.
