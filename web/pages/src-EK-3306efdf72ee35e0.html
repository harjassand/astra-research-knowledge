# A4 — intervention-response transport across evolution

**Status key:** **Known** means supported by a cited primary source; **internal candidate** means a mathematical formulation or derivation made in this note; **speculative** means a proposed assumption, mechanism, or experiment not yet validated; **falsified** is limited to the explicitly stated model class.

## Candidate selection

I compared three routes. Predicting unseen perturbations in one fixed cell background is now a crowded benchmark problem, and recent work shows that standard metrics can reward shared perturbation-versus-control shifts rather than perturbation-specific prediction. Learning intervention-faithful coarse states for cell dynamics is mathematically appealing, but causal abstraction already provides a formal language for intervention-faithful abstractions ([Geiger et al., 2025](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-0058.html)); the biology-specific advance would be hard to isolate. The strongest A4 candidate is narrower: **predict how a fixed intervention's effect changes as a biological genotype evolves**. It has a direct assay interface, a precise non-identifiability obstruction, and a tractable first test. This is a candidate research direction, not a novelty or priority claim.

## North Star

**[Speculative objective]** Predict and control biological systems reliably as their inherited state changes. A concrete contribution would let an experimenter forecast whether a chosen gene perturbation or drug response will retain its sign and magnitude in an unseen evolved genotype, with stated uncertainty and experimental cost. This could matter for robustness of cell engineering, microbial production, and treatment strategies, but this note establishes none of those downstream benefits.

## Exact question

Fix an assay environment, a phenotype `Y` (start with log growth rate), and a target intervention `a` (start with one inducible CRISPRi guide). For genotype `g`, define the causal response contrast

\[
\theta_a(g)=\mathbb E[Y\mid \mathrm{do}(a),g]-\mathbb E[Y\mid \mathrm{do}(0),g],
\]

where `0` is the matched non-targeting control. Given sequence-resolved training genotypes and a budget for paired intervention/control measurements, **what explicit structural assumptions and assay designs are necessary and sufficient to predict \(\theta_a(g_*)\) for an unseen genotype `g_*` reached along an evolutionary lineage, with a finite-sample error and calibration guarantee?** The immediate mathematical target is a minimax characterization of the assay placements and sample count under a declared sparse or low-order genotype-by-intervention interaction model. Generalization to new alleles absent from training is outside this first question.

## Importance

**[Known]** Genetic background can change the effect of a perturbation. Hale et al. used double barcodes and pooled competition to assay 8,046 CRISPRi guides across 169 *S. cerevisiae* segregants, estimating roughly 875,000 genotype–guide combinations. They reported hundreds of guides with background-dependent effects and mapped perturbation–locus interactions; the paper explicitly identifies predicting perturbation outcomes in an unseen background as a longer-term goal and describes the needed scale as technically challenging and costly ([Hale et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48626-1)). This is already a named application problem.

**[Known]** In a distinct protein setting, phylogenetic deep mutational scans across steroid-receptor DNA-binding domains found that many single-amino-acid mutational effects changed with evolutionary background ([Park, Metzger & Thornton, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abn6895)). That supports evolutionary contingency, but it is evidence about *mutation effects*, not direct evidence that a CRISPRi or drug intervention's effect drifts by the same law.

**[Known]** Predicting unseen perturbations at a fixed background is a different axis. Systema reports that common response metrics can be dominated by systematic shifts and that perturbation-specific prediction is substantially harder than standard scores indicate ([Viñas Torné et al., 2025/2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-025-02777-8)). A4 must score the intervention contrast and split by genetic background, not reuse a fixed-background benchmark unchanged.

## Strongest obstruction: exact toy counterexample

**[Internal candidate; elementary non-identifiability, not a novel theorem]** Let `g ∈ {0,1}^n` record selected substitutions from an ancestor, `u ∈ {0,1}` indicate whether the intervention is applied, and let the measured binary outcome have probability

\[
\Pr_\pm(Y=1\mid g,u)=\tfrac12\;\pm\;\gamma u\prod_{i\in S}g_i,
\qquad 0<\gamma\le\tfrac12,
\]

for a set `S` of `k` substitutions. These are valid probabilities. The two response laws agree exactly on every assayed context with at least one required allele in `S` absent—including the ancestor and every single-mutant background when `k ≥ 2`. Yet at the evolved target carrying all substitutions in `S`, the intervention contrasts are `+γ` and `−γ`. Thus no method can infer even the sign uniformly from those observations. **[Falsified, under unrestricted interactions]** “The ancestral response plus all single-mutant backgrounds suffices to transport the response to every evolved genotype.” The counterexample does not rule out prediction under measured low-order structure, sparse modifiers, or informative target-context assays.

More generally, with `n` binary loci and an unrestricted degree-`d` response polynomial, there are `p = Σ_{j=0}^d C(n,j)` coefficients. A design whose feature matrix has rank below `p` leaves a nonzero response polynomial invisible on its queried genotypes. This rank observation is a first identifiability check, not a sample-complexity result for noisy, biologically selected genotypes.

## Missing mathematical capability

**[Internal candidate]** A useful result would specify a response class, an evolutionary target distribution, an observation-noise model, and an assay budget, then give (i) an identifiability criterion; (ii) a measurement design and estimator with an out-of-sample bound for held-out evolutionary backgrounds; and (iii) a matching lower bound showing when no design of that budget can work. Candidate response classes include sparse pairwise genotype-by-guide interactions or a shared low-rank interaction map across guides. Their validity is **speculative** and must be tested, not built into the conclusion.

The hard step is connecting an experimentally plausible genotype distribution and guide panel to a well-conditioned design while accounting for guide efficacy, baseline growth, lineage sampling, and higher-order interactions. Ordinary regression guarantees conditional on a chosen feature map would not by themselves establish that the feature map transports to descendants. The result should make allele support explicit: a predictor cannot receive a guarantee for a novel allele that was neither represented nor covered by a stated biological prior.

## Original first investigation

**[Proposed; not run]** Start with Hale et al.'s public yeast barcode-sequencing/source data and code. Reconstruct the guide-by-segregant response contrasts, then compare a matched baseline ladder: mean guide effect; mean plus baseline fitness; additive locus-by-guide terms; and a sparse pairwise or shared low-rank interaction model. Freeze genotype-grouped, distance-aware folds before fitting. Hold out whole clusters/related genotype patterns, so the test asks transfer to backgrounds rather than interpolation between near-duplicate segregants. Score held-out contrast error, sign accuracy for effects above a preregistered measurement threshold, interval coverage, and worst-case error. Use a separate guide panel or a held-out group of functionally coherent guides to test whether any learned interaction structure transfers across interventions.

**[Decisive interpretation]** If sparse/low-rank models do not beat the mean and baseline-fitness controls on background-grouped splits, stop claiming mechanistic transport from this data source. If they do, that only admits a prospective test: the segregants are related cross progeny, not independently evolved lineages.

## Prior work and costs

**Known closest work.** Hale et al. already measure genetic-background-by-CRISPRi interactions at impressive scale and explicitly state the unseen-background prediction goal. The article reports measurement from pooled lineages, double-barcode sequencing, and time-course frequencies; it also notes that effect estimates depend on timing and that the measured interaction network is only a subset of the species-wide network. A4 should not present background dependence, barcode pooling, or a generic unseen-background prediction objective as new.

**Known adjacent work.** Park et al. measure how mutation effects change over protein evolutionary history, but do not test gene-perturbation transport. Systema examines held-out perturbations and metric bias in largely fixed backgrounds. PRESCIENT learns stochastic cell-fate trajectories from time-series single-cell data and uses in-silico interventions, but does not answer genotype-background transport for evolved descendants ([Weinreb et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23518-w)). These are complements, not equivalent demonstrations.

**Public-data stage: supplied resources and costs.** The source study makes data available through SRA project PRJNA986287 and supplementary/source files, with analysis code linked through Figshare. At this stage those are externally supplied data and software; no new biological measurements have been acquired and no model has been run here. A reproducible reanalysis must record download bytes, storage, CPU/GPU and RAM peaks, elapsed time, software versions, guide/genotype filtering, and all failed fits. No measured compute totals or dollar costs are available yet.

**Prospective wet-lab stage: not authorized or priced.** For `B` backgrounds, `A` intervention guides plus a matched control, and `R` independent biological replicates, a full factorial requires `B(A+1)R` background–guide–replicate arms before sequencing/QC coverage. A pooled barcode design can reduce separate vessel count, but barcode counts do not replace independent biological replicates. Physical inputs include construction or recovery of clonal backgrounds, genome sequencing, guide/library construction, induction and target-engagement checks, media and culture consumables, selection time, barcoding, sequencing depth, instrument/automation time, and staff labor. Measurement uncertainty includes guide efficacy, barcode sampling, culture-to-culture variation, batch effects, and time-point dependence. Computation includes WGS/QC, barcode demultiplexing and count processing, growth/contrast estimation, model fitting, calibration, and independent analysis. The cited paper does not provide prices for this proposed study; obtain lab-specific quotes and a power/coverage calculation before claiming affordability.

For the evolutionary validation, use independently evolved replicate lineages, sequence endpoint clones, and reserve entire lineages for test. Apply the same guide/control panel under the same environment to ancestral, training-descendant, and held-out-descendant clones. This tests a fixed intervention across evolved sequence backgrounds without conflating the first pass with changing culture conditions or mixed-clone population composition. A later population-level study would also need current clone-frequency measurement and would predict the population contrast as the frequency-weighted sum of clone-level contrasts.

## Decisive next test / lemma

**First test (cheap gate):** perform the public-data, genotype-grouped reanalysis above. Required evidence is a clean improvement over all mean/baseline-fitness controls, calibrated uncertainty, and stability to guide-efficacy and fold definitions. Failure means the data do not admit the proposed transport claim.

**First lemma (mathematical gate):** for a chosen evolutionary target distribution `Q`, characterize when the assay feature matrix for the proposed sparse or degree-`d` response class identifies all contrasts on the support of `Q`; then derive a finite-sample prediction bound under noisy contrast estimates and a query lower bound when the matrix is rank deficient. The toy construction above is a mandatory lower-bound special case. If the theorem requires assaying essentially every relevant genotype–guide pair, the proposed scalable capability has failed at its intended budget.

**Prospective go/no-go:** only after the cheap gate and lemma pass, test frozen predictions on independent evolved lineages with no retuning on their outcomes. A practical success criterion should be fixed before data collection (for example, a chosen tolerance on contrast error and sign accuracy for non-negligible effects, with coverage at the advertised confidence level). Until then, the capability and evolutionary transport are **speculative**.

## Other routes considered

- **Fixed-background unseen perturbations:** established and actively benchmarked. Systema shows why mean/control-shift baselines and contrast-specific scoring must be included. Useful as a control task, weaker as the core A4 question.
- **Intervention-sufficient macrostate discovery:** could ask for the smallest history-derived cell state that predicts outcomes under every future control sequence. This is a good cellular-control question, and PRESCIENT provides one time-resolved intervention-prediction precedent. But predictive-state and causal-abstraction theory already provide much of the formal language; a new result would need to name an experimental capability beyond state abstraction itself. I did not select it here.
