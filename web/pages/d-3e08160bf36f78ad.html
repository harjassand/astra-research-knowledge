1. DECLARED MATHEMATICAL INTERFACE

Let X=CP^p with invariant probability dv. For finitely many labels lambda,
let H_lambda be finite Hilbert spaces and P_(lambda,v) orthogonal projectors
of constant rank r_lambda. Suppose there are isometries W_(lambda,v) onto
their ranges and positive integers m_lambda such that

  int_X P_(lambda,v)dv=I_lambda/a_lambda,
  a_lambda=dim(H_lambda)/r_lambda,
  ||W_(lambda,v)^dag W_(lambda,w)||<=|<v,w>|^m_lambda.     (1.1)

The varying fiber spaces are allowed; no common gauge/isometry is required.
The CP maps below depend only on P, and the overlap norm is gauge invariant.
Put m=min_lambda m_lambda>=1, a_max=max_lambda a_lambda and
d_(m,p)=binom(m+p,p). Assume a_max>=d_(m,p), as in the exact applications;
if a_max<d_(m,p), replacing a_max by d_(m,p) only weakens the bounds.

On H=direct_sum_lambda H_lambda define

  F_v=direct_sum_lambda sqrt(a_lambda) P_(lambda,v),
  S(B)=int F_v B F_v dv,
  E_S(B)=<B,(I-S)B>_HS,
  Lambda_S(Q)=min_(rank B<=Q, ||B||_HS=1)E_S(B).          (1.2)

S is unital TP, self-adjoint and positive on HS space, with spectrum in
[0,1]. All complex rectangles H_mu->H_lambda are present in (1.2).

2. EXACT CHAIN AND COMPLEX SCHATTEN-NORM BOUND

For unit x,y in H set f_x(v)=||F_v x||^2, f_y(v)=||F_v y||^2. Their
integrals are one by (1.1). In each sector insert the W isometries between
successive projections. For any t>=2, then Cauchy-Schwarz over lambda,

 |<x,F_(v1)...F_(vt)y>|^2
   <=a_max^(t-2) f_x(v1) f_y(vt)
                    prod_(i=1..t-1)|<v_i,v_(i+1)>|^(2m). (2.1)

There is no factor for the number of sectors, fiber ranks or rectangles.
In particular the normalization sqrt(a_lambda) is retained at both ends;
using the frame trace kernel instead would not prove this bound.

Let h_m(v,w)=|<v,w>|^(2m), K_m=d_(m,p) h_m. Integrating (2.1) over all
intermediate planes gives

  ||S^t||_(1->infinity)
     <=(a_max/d_(m,p))^(t-2) ||K_m^(t-1)||_infinity/d_(m,p). (2.2)

For positive trace-one inputs, this follows directly from Kraus-product
matrix elements and int f_x=int f_y=1. For a rank-one complex input
|u><v|, the CP Kraus Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds an arbitrary output
bilinear form by the geometric mean of two such positive-input elements.
Then singular-value decomposition proves (2.2) for every complex matrix.
The norm in (2.2) is therefore the full Schatten1-to-operator norm.

3. EXACT SCALAR CP^p WALK AND A FINITE HEAT BOUND

The squared overlap has beta(1,p) density p(1-x)^(p-1), giving
int h_m=1/d_(m,p). The scalar CPp coherent symbol calculation gives the
nonzero walk eigenvalues and harmonic dimensions

  beta_l=m!(m+p)!/[(m-l)!(m+p+l)!], l=0,...,m,
  h_l=binom(l+p,p)^2-binom(l+p-1,p)^2,
  ||K_m^s||_infinity=sum_(l=0..m)h_l beta_l^s.            (3.1)

Conventions: binom(p-1,p)=0, h0=1. This is the SCALAR walk after the
operator inequality (2.1). It is not the spectrum of S. A finite proof
uses End(Sym^m(C^(p+1)))=direct_sum_l H_(l,0,...,0,l) and the highest
operator X_l=(a1^dag)^l a_(p+1)^l. Its coherent symbol is
[m!/(m-l)!](conj(v1)v_(p+1))^l. Dirichlet(1,...,1) integration gives
int |v1 v_(p+1)|^(2l)=p!(l!)^2/(2l+p)!, while
||X_l||^2=(l!)^2 binom(m+l+p,2l+p). Dividing yields beta_l.
For p=1 the same formula is the spin-l SU2 harmonic. The h_l dimensions
sum to d_(m,p)^2, completing the finite nonzero spectrum. The addition
theorem gives zonal projection diagonal h_l and modulus <=h_l, so the
diagonal attains the supremum in (3.1).

The product formula gives
beta_l<=exp[-l(l+p)/(m+p+l)]<=exp[-l^2/((p+2)(m+1))].
Also, for all l>=0,

  h_l<=A_p(l+1)^(2p-1),
  A_p=2 p^(2p-1)/[p!(p-1)!].

This follows from binomial differences: B_l-B_(l-1)=binom(l+p-1,p-1),
and B_l+B_(l-1)<=2(l+p)^p/p!. Put u=s/[(p+2)(m+1)]<=1 for
1<=s<=m+1. Integrating on [l-1,l] for l>=1 bounds

 sum_(l>=0)(l+1)^(2p-1)exp(-u l^2)
 <=1+int_0^infinity(x+2)^(2p-1)exp(-u x^2)dx
 <=(1+B_p)u^-p,
 B_p=2^(2p-3)[Gamma(p)+2^(2p-1)sqrt(pi)].

The last inequality uses (x+2)^(2p-1)<=2^(2p-2)(x^(2p-1)+2^(2p-1))
and u<=1. Since d_(m,p)>=(m+1)^p/p!, the explicit finite constant

  H_p=A_p p!(1+B_p)(p+2)^p

obeys

  ||K_m^s||_infinity/d_(m,p)<=H_p/s^p,
                                1<=s<=m+1.              (3.2)

For p=2 the sharper elementary constant H_2=128 proved in
SANDWICH_WALK_CONVERSE §4 can be used. Constants here are deliberately
loose but finite for each fixed p. They contain no growing fiber rank.

4. THE RANK-Q MASTER AND ITS NORMALIZATION-LIMITED WINDOW

For a unit HS rank-Q matrix, ||B||_1^2<=Q. The spectrum S in [0,1] gives
<B,S^t B>>=1-t E_S(B). Equations (2.2),(3.1) yield the exact master

 Lambda_S(Q)>= [1-Q(a_max/d_(m,p))^(t-2)
                     sum_l h_l beta_l^(t-1)/d_(m,p)]_+/t, t>=2. (4.1)

All near-identity scalar modes, particularly beta0=1, are still present.
The factor (a_max/d_(m,p))^(t-2) has not been omitted.

Set kappa=log(a_max/d_(m,p))>=0, L_p=(2 exp(1)H_p)^(1/p)+2, and
t=ceil((2 exp(1)H_p Q)^(1/p))+1. If t-1<=m+1 and kappa(t-2)<=1,
then Q||S^t||_(1->infinity)<=1/2 and t<=L_p Q^(1/p). Hence

  Lambda_S(Q)>=1/[2 L_p Q^(1/p)].                        (4.2)

For T=min{m+2,2+floor(1/kappa)}, with 1/kappa=infinity at zero, a sufficient
window is Q<=floor([(T-3)/(2 exp(1)H_p)^(1/p)]^p), provided it is nonempty.
In particular, if kappa=O(sqrt(log(n/epsilon)/n)) and m=Theta(n),
the permitted window has order (n/log(n/epsilon))^(p/2).

5. WEIGHTED SELF-SCORE CONTRACT FOR AN OPERATIONAL LOWER

Suppose an actual target ensemble on H has scalar-block mean sigma>0,
commutes with sigma, and normalized likelihood L_v=sigma^-1/2 rho_v sigma^-1/2.
Assume each L_(lambda,v) is scalar t_lambda on P_(lambda,v), with complement
eigenvalues in [0,qbar t_lambda], and

  t_min=min t_lambda, M=t_max=max t_lambda,
  Delta=t_min-qbar t_max>0, L_v<=M I.                     (5.1)

Every full rectangle then satisfies
int||[L_v,B]||^2>=Delta^2 int||[P_v,B]||^2. For a rectangle lambda,mu,
the latter is exactly

  (1/a_lambda+1/a_mu)||B||^2-2int||P_lambda B P_mu||^2
  =2/sqrt(a_lambda a_mu) E_(S,lambda,mu)(B)
    +(1/sqrt(a_lambda)-1/sqrt(a_mu))^2||B||^2.

The block energy is nonnegative. Thus
int||[P_v,B]||^2>=2 E_S(B)/a_max on the entire matrix space.

For any legal archive with maximum branch Q, put B_i=A_i sqrt sigma for
its rank-Q composite Kraus maps. Their squared HS norms sum to one. The
reference-weighted arbitrary-TP identity, with C=int L_v^2, is

  D/2=int Tr L_v(rho_v-Phi rho_v)+(1/2)Tr C(Phi sigma-sigma),
  D=sum_i int||[L_v,B_i]||^2, I<=C<=M I.

Bounded effects and mean trace-distance contraction imply e>=D/(3M-1).
Consequently the exact operational lower is

  e_Q>=theta Lambda_S(Q),
  theta=2Delta^2/[a_max(3M-1)].                            (5.2)

No unitality, covariance, sector preservation, restricted labels or scalar
whole-space reference is required. If theta is bounded below and the
normalization window contains epsilon^-p, (4.2) forces Q=Omega(epsilon^-p).
If it does not, only the min-limited lower follows. The assumptions in (5.1)
are genuine acquisition/model gates, not true for arbitrary mixed ensembles.

6. COSTS AND FAILURE BOUNDARIES

This abstract theorem is a converse for maximum quantum branch. It permits
unlimited charged separately classical outcomes and no quantum side input.
It constructs no optimal filter, finite-label code, eigenbasis oracle, gate
circuit or total-memory upper. Strongest baseline is exact H storage with
one label. General-d ambient Schur/harmonic embeddings and the actual target
likelihood gap must be established before using (5.2); they are supplied by
the exact top-fold-collision proof in the companion file, not by analogy.

The theorem survives rectangular sector transport because (2.1),(5.2)
are whole-space inequalities. It does not make any scalar or full-Casimir
claim for the spectrum of S. SANDWICH_CONNECTION_OBSTRUCTION.txt exhibits
an explicit failure of a tempting full-SU3-Casimir comparator even on
critical fibers. Below the restricted-time window a sharper actual bundle
walk/rank theorem is needed. Finite evidence and internal audits are not
external correctness or novelty validation.

Primary mathematical controls are classical scalar CPp Berezin theory,
e.g. Demni-Mouayn-Yaqine, arXiv:1610.02875, and matrix-valued/bundle
frameworks, Grunbaum-Pacharoni-Tirao math/0108042 and Adachi-Ishiki-Kanno
2210.01397. All scalar quantities used here are independently reconstructed;
none of those works is assumed to prove the present growing-fiber rank
