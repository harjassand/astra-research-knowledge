2. **Huang–Monagan, 2609.10626v1 (9 Sep 2026)**. Read introductory algorithm/cost comparison and term-separation/GCD-preservation/prime-encoding excerpts (Theorems2.6/2.9). 3. **Huang–Cao–Qiu–Gao, 2607.02364v1 (2 Jul 2026)**. Read exact-power sparsity/introduction, §2 root-expansion lemma, and §5 Frobenius/normalization/verification/complexity proof excerpts. Authors explicitly charge scalar-root cost R(e), including Frobenius inverses. Their bounded numeric degree exponent s^{O(Dd)} is not a polylog-degree algorithm. Important limitation: printed Lemma2.7 treats division by i! as defined merely because e is invertible; in positive characteristic this expression fails when i>=p. Formal e-th-root uniqueness for e invertible can instead be reconstructed coefficient by coefficient, dividing by e, but that repair is NOT a new breakthrough and we do not adopt the printed formula as written. Our GCD theorem does not use this lemma. Manuscript body gives August24,2026 while primary v1 submission is July2; both are preserved rather than inferring a version date from manuscript text.

4. **Arvind–Joglekar, 2609.37319v1 (29 Sep), v2 (2 Oct2026)**. Read v1's uniform succinct simulation, pencil construction/entry oracle, and correctness wrapper; then v2 Lemma4.9 and §6 proofs directly. Author result: deterministic polynomial SPACE for degree-unrestricted noncommutative rational-circuit identity testing, with invalid inversion identified. Exponential unfolded pencil is accessed by entry oracle. The descent tracks one path, and sequential zero tests reuse space. The result relies on a uniform NC rank procedure from CGGRT26; explicit finite-field representation and rational bit inputs matter. This is not a polynomial-time proof-search procedure. Potential alternative: exploit known circuit depth/shared-subexpression structure for parameterized time improvement; no new such theorem proved here. v2 is preserved because it lies inside the window; we have not claimed a full change audit between versions.

5. **Qiu–Cao–Huang–Feng–Gao, 2606.12144v1 (10 Jun2026)**. Read §2 encoding, random compression Lemma17, and promised-solver Theorem18 proof; preceding prime construction/isolation assumptions screened. Boolean assignments correspond to roots using CRT; random linear compression preserves zero sets except degree/field-size error. Unique isolation yields GCD either constant or linear, while degrees are exponentially encoded. Off-promise calls remain time bounded and are charged in amplification. Consequence: an output-sensitive solver polynomial in sparsity, logD, logQ would put SAT in BPP. This is compatible with our proposed numeric-D algorithm; replacing its numeric degree dependence by polylogD is not a routine optimization.
