# Falsifier and charged interface

## Testable predictions

For a chamber started in `Γ_n`, hold the balancing chemostat at normalized activity one and set/calibrate the four reversible pairs to the stated dimensionless constants. After stationarity:

- `B+C=2` and `A−B(B+1)/2=n` on every recorded state;
- the three phase frequencies are exactly `p₀=1/(2Z_n)`, `p₁=1/((n+1)Z_n)`, and `p₂=1/(2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)Z_n)`;
- `D` is independent of the phase and has `Poisson(1)` law;
- every direction of every reversible pair has mean stationary event rate `1/Z_n`, hence each pair has zero net current;
- `A≥n` always, while the deterministic concentration model from any positive initial condition with `B+C=2` converges to `(1,1,1,1)`.

The sharp structural falsifier is a correctly observed transition changing `B+C` or `A−B(B+1)/2`; this means an omitted reaction, an incorrect propensity, or measurement/model error. For distributional falsification, collect independent replicate chambers or regenerative post-burn-in observations and compare phase counts with a multinomial law at the displayed probabilities and D counts with `Poisson(1)`. A consistent failure of the corresponding confidence intervals rejects the exact model. A persistent nonzero net current in any reversible pair rejects the detailed-balance parameterization.

The exact top-phase probability is

`p₂(n)=1/[2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)Z_n] ~ n⁻³`.

To see at least one top-phase state among independent stationary samples with probability at least `0.95`, one needs

`m ≥ ceil(log(0.05)/log(1−p₂(n))) ≈ 3/p₂(n) = Θ(n³)`.

This is a sample-count lower bound for that observation, not an estimator guarantee. The top-state reaction intensity is `Θ(n³)` because the source complex has three A and two B molecules. Temporal sampling must resolve dwell times of order `n⁻³`; a run that undersamples these events cannot test the phase law.

## Acquisition and resource costs

The mathematical input is four internal species, one clamped balancing moiety, four reversible pairs, exact source/product complexes, unit activity for Q, and eight rate constants after standard-state normalization. A physical test must acquire or control the concentration/chemical activity of Q, standard-state Gibbs energies, all directional rate constants, and count-resolved reaction events. It must maintain the chemostat and distinguish A/B/C/D. The three- and five-molecule complexes make this a formal/synthetic CRN rather than a claim about an identified natural pathway.

For class index `n`, at least `n` A molecules must be loaded. Storage and molecule-count readout therefore scale at least linearly in `n`; event records require reaction identity and time stamps, with timing precision scaling as `n⁻³` in the rare top phase. The formula predicts `D` mean one and unit-scale gross event rates for each channel, but no wet-lab experiment or hardware runtime was performed. The exact unit-volume stochastic law is specified; extrapolation to a fixed-concentration volume limit is not established. In fact, keeping `B+C=2` while increasing volume drives that pool concentration to zero, so the example is not a large-volume counterexample to the deterministic limit.

## Model failure modes

Unmodelled side reactions can change the class label; Q activity drift changes pseudo-first-order rates; unequal forward/reverse constants break the specified detailed balance and stationary weights; nonideal activities require calibrated activity coefficients; finite sampling can miss the rare top phase. The witness illustrates a model-level gap only and does not assert that natural metabolism exhibits this mechanism.
