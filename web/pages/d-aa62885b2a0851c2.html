# General graph: an independent rare-count proof and source-acquisition gate

2026-10-06. Root requested a generalization after the three-state audit. This proof was developed from a path decomposition before reading r17's `GENERAL_GRAPH_CANDIDATE.md`; the latter's series-support proof and cost accounting were then audited. Both mathematical routes pass under the interface below. This is model-worker reconstruction, not external validation; historical novelty is unchecked. Constants depend on supplied baseline structure, not uniformly on graph size or rates.

## Exact question and interface

Q0 is a supplied known finite irreducible row generator on n states. A directed edge u->v is absent, u!=v. Let L=d0(u,v)>=2 be its shortest directed baseline path length. Add a single unknown rare rate b in(0,1]: Q_b=Q0+bE, E=e_u(e_v-e_u)^T. The graph, all baseline rates, state labels and physical units are supplied. Each passive read reveals the current state; an independently calibrated next lag can be chosen from retained history. The lag may be deterministic or random, its timestamp may be retained, and future timing may adapt. The clock's conditional kernel is parameter-independent; no path information is available inside an interval. Initial state is fixed independently of b. No observation-triggered jump sensor, hidden-state model, reset oracle or unknown-baseline acquisition is implied.

Question: how many endpoint reads are necessary to identify b versus b/2, and can a no-reset protocol attain that exponent while spending only linear physical time in1/b?

Let R=max baseline escape rate and d_xy=d0(x,y), d_xx=0. For each pair select a shortest baseline path with positive rate product w_xy. Define c_xy=e^(-(R+1)) w_xy/d_xy!, including w_xx=1. All constants are computable from supplied Q0; none is fitted free of data.

## Short-time endpoint information from three path components

Fix initial state x and t in(0,1]. Count actual uses of the added u->v edge. Its intensity is b 1{X_s=u}; denote its count N_r and occupation A_u<=t. Separate the endpoint probability into g0(y),g1(y),h(y), corresponding to zero, one, and at least two rare jumps. These are unnormalized nonnegative components, not conditional probability laws. This distinction prevents omitting the information in their masses.

**Zero rare jumps.** The killed baseline semigroup exp[t(Q0-b diag(1_u))] gives g0. A shortest baseline path with exactly d_xy jumps and no other events has

g0(y)>=c_xy t^d_xy.

The Feynman-Kac weight exp(-b A_u) has derivative -A_u exp(-b A_u); hence |g0'(y)|<=t g0(y).

**Exactly one rare jump.** Let k=d0(x,u), h0=d0(v,y), r_xy=k+h0+1. The component has the positive Duhamel representation

g1(y)=b integral_0^t K_b(s)[x,u] K_b(t-s)[v,y] ds,

where K_b is the killed baseline semigroup. K_b<=P0 entrywise by Feynman-Kac. To reach u from x takes at least k baseline jumps; their count is dominated by Pois(Rs). Factorial Markov bounds give P0(s)[x,u]<=(Rs)^k/k!, and similarly P0(t-s)[v,y]<=[R(t-s)]^h0/h0!. The beta integral therefore gives

g1(y)<=C_xy b t^r_xy,  C_xy=R^(r_xy-1)/r_xy!.

The representation is b times a nonnegative path integral weighted by exp(-b A_u). Differentiation thus gives g1'=g1/b minus an occupation-weighted term between0 and t g1. For f=g0+g1,

|f'|<=t f+g1/b,

and so

(f')²/f <=2t²f+2 g1²/(b²f)
 <=2t²f+2 min{g1/b²,g1²/(b²g0)}.

For every y, replacing the rare edge by a shortest length-L baseline path produces a baseline walk of length k+L+h0. Thus d_xy<=k+L+h0=r_xy+L-1. Because r_xy>=1,

2r_xy-d_xy>=2-L.

Summing over y, the second term is bounded by constant times t^(2-L). This is an all-endpoint, all-initial-state statement, not merely the rare-edge endpoint. It also implies a min{t/b,t^(2-L)} bound term by term, although the full-path bound below is cleaner.

**At least two rare jumps.** The complete-path score is S=N_r/b-A_u. By Cauchy for each endpoint, then summing,

sum_y (h'(y))²/h(y)<=E_b[S² 1{N_r>=2}].

Rare jumps are a thinning of a rate-b Poisson proposal process; pathwise N_r<=K with K~Pois(bt). The function k²1{k>=2} is increasing. Its Poisson expectation is (bt)²+bt-bt exp(-bt)<=2b²t². Using S²<=2N_r²/b²+2t² gives

sum_y (h')²/h<=6t².

Zero mass components use the conventional0 contribution. Weighted Cauchy across the two components f,h gives (p')²/p<=f'²/f+h'²/h. Since sum f<=1, the entire endpoint Fisher information is therefore

I_t,x(b)<=8t²+2 sum_y (C_xy²/c_xy)t^(2r_xy-d_xy)
 <=C_s t^(2-L),

C_s=8+2 max_x sum_y C_xy²/c_xy.

This proof controls unnormalized mixture components and all multiple-rare paths. It does not infer Fisher information from a finite-grid check or from only the leading rare-edge probability.

The full-path score is a compensated rare-count martingale divided by b, so full-path Fisher is E A_u/b<=t/b. Endpoint data processing gives I_t,x(b)<=t/b. Combining,

I_t,x(b)<=min{t/b,C_s t^(2-L)}
 <=C_s^(1/(L-1)) b^(-(L-2)/(L-1)) for0<t<=1.

For L=2 this is constant. For L>2 the increasing/decreasing bounds balance at t=(C_s b)^(1/(L-1)); their maximum intersection gives the stated exponent. The argument does not require the chosen design to know b.

## All large lags, without reversibility or spectral diagonalization

Set eta=min{1/(2n),min_xy c_xy}>0. Every P_b(1) entry is at least eta for b in[0,1]. Therefore P_b(t) entries are >=eta for t>=1, and the time-one Dobrushin contraction is rho=1-n eta<1. Since E's nonzero row is the difference of rows v and u,

||E P_b(s)||_infty<=2 rho^floor(s).

Duhamel and the Markov contraction ||P_b(s)||_infty=1 give

||partial_b P_b(t)||_infty<=integral_0^t 2 rho^floor(s) ds<=2/(n eta).

The row squared Euclidean norm is at most its squared l1 norm. Thus I_t,x(b)<=4/(n²eta³) for t>=1. No assumption that the baseline is reversible, normal or diagonalizable is needed. Define

alpha=(L-2)/(L-1), p=2-alpha=L/(L-1),

K=max{C_s^(1/(L-1)),4/(n²eta³)}.

Then I_t,x(b)<=K b^(-alpha) for all t>0, all x, and b in(0,1]. For random observed lags the conditional clock has no parameter score and averaging preserves this bound. Erasing timestamps can only reduce information.

## Adaptive and stopped-transcript minimax consequence

Compare b and c=b/2. Along their parameter segment the uniform information bound is K 2^alpha b^(-alpha). Integrating the square-root derivative gives conditional

H²<=K 2^alpha b^p/16.

Every shared history has the same bound, even if the starting label and chosen lag adapt. The bounded-read affinity argument yields

TV²<=N K 2^alpha b^p/16.

The stopped-prefix argument in `endpoint_audit.md` instead yields

TV²<=K 2^alpha b^p/16 sqrt(E_b N E_c N)

for a.s. stopping and finite expectations under both hypotheses. A test with errors<=.1 requires TV>=.8, hence

N>=10.24/(K 2^alpha) b^(-p),

or sqrt(E_b N E_c N)>=10.24/(K 2^alpha) b^(-p).

Relative-error intervals b(1+-epsilon_rel) and (b/2)(1+-epsilon_rel) are disjoint when epsilon_rel<1/3, so a uniformly successful relative-rate estimator provides such a test. At least one mean read count has the lower order; this is not a one-sided expected-count conclusion. Parameter-dependent initialization is extra data. In this general family stationary initialization varies with b, unlike the uniform three-cycle, and cannot silently be treated as a common initial law.

## Matching no-reset protocol on a supplied bracket

Supply b in[a,2a], a<=1/2, but not its exact value. Let

kappa=min{1,1/[4(R+1)],(L!/R^L)^(1/(L-1))},

t=kappa a^(1/(L-1)).

The baseline probability P0(t)[u,v] is at most (Rt)^L/L!<=a t. A rare jump occurs with probability at most b t. Therefore q_b=P_b(t)[u,v]<=3a t.

Duhamel gives

q_b'=integral_0^t P_b(s)[u,u] [P_b(t-s)[v,v]-P_b(t-s)[u,v]] ds.

Because (R+1)t<=1/4, diagonals are >=exp(-1/4), and off-diagonal probability <=1/4. The integrand is at least exp(-1/4)[exp(-1/4)-1/4]>1/4. Hence q_b'>=t/4 throughout the bracket. This independently obtains monotonicity without the candidate's derivative-series constant.

Collect M departures whose currently observed label is u; record whether the next endpoint label is v. Their Bernoulli laws have mean q_b. Clip the empirical frequency to[q_a,q_2a] and invert the known monotone function. If the frequency error is <=tau=epsilon_rel a t/4, then |bhat-b|<=epsilon_rel a. Bernstein with variance<=3a t implies success>=1-delta from

M=ceil[100 log(2/delta)/(epsilon_rel² a t)]

for epsilon_rel<=1. Thus M=O(a^(-p)). With known computable baseline rates, the finite matrix exponential and inverse can be evaluated to an explicitly allocated fraction of tau and relative-error tolerance; extra accuracy allocation changes constants. Arbitrary noncomputable supplied real rates do not supply an effective numerical implementation. Rate precision, graph size and matrix-evaluation work are separate from the physical read exponent.

**Charge finding u and obtaining its samples.** Run one sampled chain with fixed lag t. Let m=ceil(1/t). At times m steps apart the propagator has entries >=eta since m t>=1. From any initial observed state, expected reads to first reach u are <=m/eta. No reset or stationary preparation is used.

Starting at u, run a complete positive return cycle to u. Its first transition is one Bernoulli observation. Strong Markov at return times makes successive cycle observations iid, even though an observation and its own cycle length can be dependent. The sampled chain's stationary distribution is the CTMC stationary pi(b); pi_u(b)>=eta follows from pi P_b(1)=pi. Kac's mean return identity gives expected cycle length1/pi_u<=1/eta, independent of t. Completing M such cycles therefore uses expected total reads

E N_total <=m/eta+M/eta.

An equally direct proof without Kac uses 1-P_b(t)[u,u]<=(R+1)t and the uniform hitting bound; it gives cycle mean<=1+2(R+1)/eta. Either bound retains the exponent. Charging maximum hitting time after every read would incorrectly insert an extra1/t factor: most departures stay at u, and complete-return accounting handles rare long excursions.

Since m=O(1/t) and M=O(1/(a t)), total expected reads are O(a^(-p)). Total expected physical time is exactly t E N_total=O(1/a). Total expected physical jumps are <=(R+1)t E N_total=O(1/a) by the jump-count compensator. The sampler stops at observed return times and these identities apply to its integrable physical stopping time. Cap total reads at the uniform expected bound divided by a chosen cap-failure tolerance to obtain a hard-cap protocol. Markov's inequality adds that failure tolerance; fixed confidence retains the same exponents.

The scale bracket is supplied for this local theorem. No exact b is used, no resets are free, and source-label collection is charged. A separate later extension removes the bracket for known Q0, with an explicit loglog(1/b) confidence overhead; its independent gate is `acquired_scale_gate.md`. A separate early-cycle6 extension acquires compact ordinary rates while retaining the local bracket; its gate is `unknown_baseline_gate.md`. These are distinct interfaces and have not been composed here.

## Entropy production, cost distinction and status

For an EP implication require the reverse v->u rate a0>0 and reciprocal support for every other baseline edge. Then Q_b has finite entropy production for b>0. Irreducibility at b=0 gives analytic, positive stationary probabilities near0. Separate the affected edge pair:

sigma(b)=J(b)log(1/b)+G(b),

J(b)=pi_v(b)a0-pi_u(b)b,

where G,J are analytic near0 (all other positive pair rate ratios remain fixed). Thus sigma(b/2)-sigma(b) tends to pi_v(0)a0 log2>0. Fixed additive EP error below half that limiting separation inherits the read lower exponent for sufficiently small b. The upper relative precision converts to EP precision through a supplied/certified bound on b|sigma'(b)| and a stable stationary solve; those costs are not silently free. If other reverse rates vanish, EP may be infinite already and this deduction fails.

For general graphs full-path score again yields a physical-time lower bound of order1/b: stopped directed KL between b and b/2 is (log2-1/2)b E_b A_u <=(log2-1/2)b E_b T. A .9/.9 test forces this KL to be >=.8log9. Thus time cannot be uniformly smaller than order1/b. Unlike the constant-escape three-cycle, a general graph's jump-count lower bound additionally uses a uniform positive minimum escape rate. Such a bound exists for this fixed irreducible baseline, but its constant may be poor. Upper jump cost needs only R+1. The read exponent p>1 therefore does not imply a physical time or jump exponent above1.

## Adversarial audit of r17's alternative proof

After this derivation, read `GENERAL_GRAPH_CANDIDATE.md`. Its marked-power support lemma passes: a prefix ending at marked u can remove earlier rare u->v ... ->u loops; a suffix starting at marked v can remove later v ... ->u->v loops. For a marked diagonal u->u term, an outgoing suffix either stays baseline or uses the rare edge once before its last baseline v->y tail. Minimizing over the marked diagonal/off-diagonal alternatives gives its m_xy=d0(x,u)+1+min{d0(v,y),d0(u,y)}. Diagonal stays do not shorten a directed reachability walk. Its all-pair exponent calculation, derivative-tail constants, large-lag Dobrushin argument, and weighted useful-departure hitting cost also pass. The independent path proof above does not depend on that support lemma.

Concrete downstream use: distinguish physical sensor-read burden from elapsed experimental duration, and select a calibrated sampling lag on the rare-rate scale set by shortest baseline ambiguity. The positive result is a known-baseline measurement design theorem; any competent equally informed experimenter can use it. Nearest established prerequisite is classical random-sampling identifiability (Duffie-Glynn2004); priority of this exact topology-dependent finite-cost result needs a dedicated primary-source search.
