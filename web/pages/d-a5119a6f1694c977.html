GAMES AND HAMILTONIANS — CYCLE 3 HIGH-CEILING ROUTE
Active-flag branch encoding: a sparse-enforcer barrier beyond N26
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS

INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. The report gives (i) a full-list coherent
active-flag encoding of the actual BMVZ term generator with a common diagonal
positive test, and (ii) a dimension-free row-mass/Chebyshev lemma proving
that every positive polynomial-row enforcer for this specific isometry has
vanishing normalized gap on a BMVZ-admissible augmented hard family. The
argument applies to arbitrary positive enforcers, not just a chosen graph
Laplacian or a normalized positive-term decomposition. It is a scoped
obstruction to one concrete coherent verifier/enforcer architecture, not a
general no-go for active workspace encodings or a quantum-PCP theorem.

The construction also exposes the exact gap in an attempted universal
extension of N26: the common physical flag is meaningful only on the
branch-dependent valid-workspace subspace. N26 requires a fixed-sector
positive budget and an all-auxiliary-state floor. The new obstruction uses
instead the row distribution of the exact image projector.

## 1. Exact full-list coherent encoder

Take an explicit BMVZ output in the layered format

    H = sum_(j in J) w_j h_j,       w_j >= 0, sum_j w_j = 1,
    h_(chi,f) = I - tensor_(a=1)^T (I - Pi^chi_(f(a))).

Here J is the actual generated clause table: chi is a color/layer and f is
one of the length-T paths in that layer's expander. For an iterated output,
flatten each nested OR clause to its T base local-projector factors. The
BMVZ construction gives polynomially many clause labels and polynomial T in
its iterated hard family; each factor is a fixed-k local projector on its
own data copy. No simultaneous diagonalizer for the noncommuting full list
is assumed: each individual clause is diagonalized factor-by-factor across
its separate copies.

For each input local projector P_i, obtain a local diagonalization

    P_i = U_i* D_i U_i,

with D_i diagonal and Boolean-valued. This is exact for the BJSW 5-local
Clifford-Hamiltonian family. More generally it is polynomial-time for
constant-k exact algebraic input matrices with polynomial-bit descriptions,
subject to the standard exact algebraic representation being closed under
the required constant-size diagonalizations. For a clause j, take the
product decoder U_j over its T disjoint copies and define the diagonal OR
predicate

    F_j(z) = 1 iff at least one of the T local D_(i(j,a)) predicates is 1,
    D_j = diag(F_j),              h_j = U_j* D_j U_j.

Let J_F|z> = |z>|F(z)> and W_j = J_(F_j) U_j. Thus W_j is an isometry
from logical data to decoded data plus one active flag qubit, and

    W_j* (I_data tensor |1><1|) W_j = h_j.

Use one orthogonal branch label for every actual generated clause and set

    V|psi> = sum_(j in J) sqrt(w_j) |j> W_j|psi>,
    B = I_branch tensor I_data tensor |1><1|_flag.

Then V*V=I and, as an operator identity on every logical vector,

    V* B V = sum_j w_j h_j = H.                  (1)

B is a common diagonal positive contraction with exactly one nonzero per
computational-basis row. This differs from a fixed-syndrome test: the
one-bit flag is computed from all T local outcomes, with its allowed value
depending on both branch j and the decoded data z.

### Circuits and exact access costs

V has a polynomial-size coherent circuit for the explicit BMVZ term input:

1. Prepare the branch state sum_j sqrt(w_j)|j>. Within a color the path
   labels are generated by a uniform start vertex and uniform choices of
   expander edges; the color weights are part of the input. The singleton
   small-layer case has one repeated clause. For polynomially many labels,
   reversible term-index lookup costs polynomial time/size.
2. Apply the branch-controlled product decoder U_j. It has T constant-size
   local factors; a direct lookup implementation costs polynomial size
   O(poly(|J|,T,n)) even without an optimized QROM implementation.
3. Reversibly compute the T local diagonal predicates and their OR into the
   one flag bit. This costs O(poly(|J|,T,n)) gates; for an explicit local
   predicate table it is O(T) fixed-size gates plus the charged term lookup.

For B, entry and row access are constant time: return the flag bit; row
support is one. With BJSW Clifford terms and rational layer weights the
branch preparation and local decoding can be exact over a fixed algebraic
gate set. For a general polynomial-bit algebraic term input, exactness and
bit complexity must be charged in the chosen algebraic-number model. If
branch amplitudes or local gates are approximated to operator error delta,
(1) becomes an approximate compression; that does not certify an exact
kernel for C.

The natural exact enforcer is

    P = V V*,                C_dense = I - P.

It is a positive projection, ker(C_dense)=ran(V), and its gap is exactly 1.
It also has a polynomial-size circuit implementation: extend the preparation
circuit for V to a unitary and conjugate the projector onto the initialized
branch/flag ancillas. This is circuit access, not sparse-row access. With
d=2^(nT) logical basis states and M=|J| branches, a row of P can have up to
M d nonzeros. An entry of its (j,k) block can be evaluated directly from

    P_(j,k) = sqrt(w_j w_k) W_j W_k*,

using the term descriptions and a product of local overlap entries; for
the fixed BJSW Clifford gate set this is polynomial-time exact entry access.
Enumerating the row can nevertheless take exponential time. The seed-pair
construction below gives an explicit 8^T lower bound on one such row.

## 2. General sparse-projector lemma

This lemma is the higher-ceiling mechanism. It has no positive-term-budget
premise and no restriction on how C couples branches or auxiliary registers.

Let P be a projection on a register Hilbert space with a fixed computational
basis, and let 0 <= C <= I be a positive contraction with

    ker(C) = ran(P),           C >= gamma (I-P),       gamma > 0.

Let s be the maximum number of nonzero entries in any row of C. Suppose a
row i and a column set J satisfy

    sum_(j in J) |P_ij|^2 >= m > 0,
    |P_ij|^2 <= a                         for every j in J.

Define

    D = log(m/(2a)) / log(s+1).

If 0<gamma<1 and D>1, then

    gamma <= [ log(8/m) / (4(D-1)) ]^2.             (2)

The bound is independent of the total register dimension. A contraction
with gap one also satisfies the hypothesis for every chosen gamma<1. The
bound remains valid with arbitrary additional spectator Hilbert space A when
P and V are tensored with I_A and C is row-sparse in the product basis:
choose the same A basis label in the row and the selected columns. No bound
on dim(A) enters.
More generally, arbitrary active auxiliary systems are allowed whenever the
displayed row-mass hypotheses hold in their chosen basis; the theorem does
not assert that every workspace design preserves such a witness.

Proof. For integer ell>=1, define the Chebyshev filter

    q_ell(x) = T_ell((1+gamma-2x)/(1-gamma))
              / T_ell((1+gamma)/(1-gamma)).

It has q_ell(0)=1. The affine map sends [gamma,1] to [1,-1], so

    ||q_ell(C)-P|| <= 1/cosh(ell arcosh((1+gamma)/(1-gamma)))
                   <= 2 exp(-2 ell sqrt(gamma)).     (3)

For the last inequality, arcosh((1+gamma)/(1-gamma))=2 atanh(sqrt(gamma))
>=2sqrt(gamma). A degree-ell polynomial in an s-row matrix has at most
(s+1)^ell nonzeros in each row. Put ell=floor(D). Then ell>=D-1>0, and
(s+1)^ell a <= m/2. The q_ell(C) row can therefore cover at most m/2 of
the P-row squared mass in J. Outside its support, q_ell(C) is zero, so

    sqrt(m/2) <= ||(q_ell(C)-P)_i,*||_2
               <= 2 exp(-2 ell sqrt(gamma)).

Rearranging and using ell>=D-1 proves (2). The argument uses operator
functional calculus only for (3); the row-count estimate is exact for every
finite polynomial. It does not assume C is a sum of commuting terms, a
covariant graph Laplacian, or a positive decomposition with normalized
budget. ∎

Equivalently, if gamma>=gamma_0>0, then

    log(s+1) >= log(m/(2a)) / (1 + log(8/m)/(4 sqrt(gamma_0))).       (4)

Thus exponentially small entrywise coherence in a fixed-mass row forces
exponential row degree for every constant-gap positive enforcer of that
same image.

## 3. Exact seed-pair witness in a generated BMVZ hard family

The seed pair can be forced into an actual allowed BMVZ input without
claiming it occurs in every unmodified circuit-to-Hamiltonian term table.
Start from the constant-color, bounded-incidence BJSW-derived 5-LCH hard
family used as the BMVZ starting point. On five fresh ancilla qubits append
two separate singleton colors of weight 1/4, carrying

    |phi> = (|00010> - |10010>)/sqrt(2),
    |psi> = (|00010> - |01110> - |10110> - |11010>)/2.

Scale the original color weights by 1/2. The augmented input is

    H_aug = (1/2) H_base tensor I + (1/4) P_phi + (1/4) P_psi.

The two rank-one projectors have a common kernel of dimension at least 30 on
their 32-dimensional register. Hence lambda_min(H_aug)=lambda_min(H_base)/2;
the QMA promise gap changes by only a constant. There are still O(1) colors,
all weights are bounded below by a constant, and each added qubit has
incidence two. Under BMVZ Definition 1.6's small-layer rule, each singleton
layer repeats its sole clause. At every amplification round the two actual
generated clauses are therefore

    h_(v,T) = I - (I-P_v)^(tensor T),       v in {phi,psi}.

Their clause weights remain exactly 1/4, since each singleton color has one
path. They coexist with the complete amplified hard base list; the argument
below is for the full augmented H, not a two-term replacement. The
augmentation is an internal reduction preserving hardness, not a claim in
the BMVZ paper. It requires the documented small-layer repetition rule; if
one used only a path definition without that rule, singleton membership
would not follow.

For the BJSW decoders, exact Clifford arithmetic gives

    |<phi,psi>| = 1/(2 sqrt(2)),
    every row of U_phi U_psi* has exactly 8 nonzero entries,
    each nonzero has squared magnitude 1/8.

The first equality follows directly because the only common computational
support vector in the displayed kets is |00010>. The relative-matrix facts
are independently checked by
`python3 outputs/research/games_hamiltonians/cycle2/bmvz_bjsw_seed_pair_test.py`
using Q(sqrt(2)) arithmetic; its test checks the Clifford preparations and
all 32 rows of the five-qubit relative unitary. The tensor-power row then
has exactly 8^T entries, each of squared magnitude 8^(-T). On the full
augmented data register, each seed decoder acts as identity on the hard-base
registers, so its relative unitary is this five-qubit matrix tensored with
identity. Fix one computational word in those untouched registers in both
the selected row and selected columns; the seed overlap figures are
unchanged.

In the full active-flag encoder of Section 1, choose the branch row indexed
by phi and by the diagonal basis word r_phi^T, where U_phi*|r_phi>=|phi> on
each five-qubit block. Let J_psi map |z> to |z>|F_psi(z)>; this is an
isometry onto the valid psi-branch computational rows. The cross block is
exactly

    P_(phi,psi) = sqrt(w_phi w_psi) W_phi W_psi*
                = sqrt(w_phi w_psi) J_phi U_phi U_psi* J_psi*.

For a valid row |r_phi^T,F_phi(r_phi^T)> and any valid psi column
|z,F_psi(z)>, its entry is

    <r_phi^T,F_phi(r_phi^T)|P_(phi,psi)|z,F_psi(z)>
       = sqrt((1/4)(1/4)) <r_phi^T|U_phi^(tensor T)
                                             U_psi*^(tensor T)|z>.

There is no requirement that the two flag bits match: J_psi* first maps its
valid column |z,F_psi(z)> back to |z>, then J_phi writes the target branch's
own flag. Thus summing over all valid psi rows retains the full relative-row
mass. The selected row has

    sum_(z in J) |P_(phi,r_phi^T ; psi,z)|^2 = m = 1/16,
    |P_(phi,r_phi^T ; psi,z)|^2 <= a = (1/16) 8^(-T).

The equality is the unit norm of the relative-unitary row; the entry bound
is exact from the Clifford check. All other BMVZ branches are irrelevant to
this selected column set; they cannot cancel or change these block entries
of P=VV*.

As a cross-check, if one restricts J to psi columns with F_psi(z)=1 (flag
one), only words with at least one per-copy r_psi are retained. In the exact
Clifford fixture this gives mass (1/16)(1-(7/8)^T), at least 1/128 for
T>=1. It is not needed: the full valid-column set above gives the stronger
exact m=1/16.

Apply (2). For any positive contraction C with exact kernel ran(V) and max
row support s, if

    D = (T log 8 - log 2)/log(s+1) > 1,

then

    gamma(C) <= [ log(128) log(s+1)
                  / (4 (T log 8 - log 2 - log(s+1))) ]^2.           (5)

Therefore gamma(C)=O((log(s+1)/T)^2) whenever T/log(s+1) tends to infinity.
If the physical register has polynomially many qubits and s is polynomial
in the input size, then log(s+1)=O(log n). The BMVZ iterated family has a
polynomially growing repetition count T when the constant per-round
amplification parameter t_0 is iterated enough times to clear an inverse-
polynomial input gap: Theorem 7.3 chooses
ell=ceil(6 log p(n)/log t_0) rounds and the flattened copy count is
T=(2t_0)^ell. For p(n)=n^c and fixed t_0, this is n^Omega(1), while the
clause count d^(2t_0 ell) remains polynomial. Thus (5) tends to zero for
polynomial row degree. A fixed lower bound gamma_0 instead forces the
explicit exponential row lower bound (4), with m/(2a)=8^T/2.

For arbitrary factorwise diagonalizers of the same rank-one seeds, without
the special Clifford tableau convention, one still gets a basis-independent
weaker estimate. In the row r_phi, the entry at r_psi has squared magnitude
1/8, so every entry is at most 7/8 by row normalization. Tensoring gives
a <= (1/16)(7/8)^T and the same conclusion with T log 8 replaced by
T log(8/7). Thus the obstruction is not an artifact of the selected
Clifford basis among factorwise seed diagonalizers.

The natural dense enforcer C_dense=I-P has at least 8^T nonzeros in the
selected row for the exact Clifford choice (at least (8/7)^T for the weaker
basis-independent estimate). The theorem is stronger: no alternative
positive enforcer of this exact image can have both polynomial row support
and a size-independent gap.

## 4. What this says about N26 and the concrete architecture

N26's interface is a fixed auxiliary Hilbert space and a sector-preserving
family

    K_x = sum_a w_a E_(a,x|S_a),
    w_a >= 0, sum_a w_a=1, 0<=E_(a,z)<=I, |S_a|<=r,

with the same all-auxiliary-state lower bound K_x>=cI for every nonzero
syndrome. It implies c<=eta+(r/T)(1-eta) when inf spec K_0<=eta. Arbitrary
auxiliary dimension and noncommutation are already allowed there.

The active-flag encoding does not instantiate that interface. Its branch
label and decoded data are correlated with the logical input. On the encoded
image, the one-bit physical flag represents an OR over all T positions; on
the rest of the same physical sector, B has flag-zero states even when the
logical syndrome is nonzero. The enforcer is supposed to remove those
invalid states by restricting to ran(V), so there is no all-auxiliary-state
floor in each fixed syndrome block. Recasting the flag as an auxiliary
system does not repair this: the requirement would then be an inequality on
all flag/work states, which is false. N26 neither proves nor refutes the
active construction.

The new row-mass theorem closes a different, sharply scoped route: the
coherent term-label verifier that (a) samples every actual clause with its
Hamiltonian weight, (b) decodes each clause to its diagonal basis, (c)
computes its full OR outcome into one shared flag, and (d) seeks any exact
positive polynomial-row constant-gap enforcer for that image. It blocks the
route on a hardness-preserving full BMVZ family with the two permitted
singleton seed colors. The common diagonal B and the polynomial V are
available; the missing property is polynomial-row constant-gap C.

It does not constrain a redesigned isometry that changes the branch
coherences, mixes decoded words in an active quantum workspace, drops or
reweights the seed branches, only enforces a gap on a restricted subspace,
or abandons computational-basis row access. A unitary that mixes data rows
inside the two degenerate flag eigenspaces leaves B diagonal as the same
operator but changes the row witness, and is outside the theorem unless it
still satisfies the hypotheses of Section 2. Spectator logical auxiliaries
of arbitrary dimension are covered by tensoring with identity; arbitrary
actively prepared auxiliary states are covered only if their resulting
image projector retains a row witness with m/(2a) exponentially large and
log(8/m)=O(log n). That row condition is the exact gate for any extension.

### Conditional spectral lift: why there is no universal active-V no-go

For completeness, an explicit counterexample to any universal obstruction
based only on “common diagonal B + positive row-sparse C” is available when
the whole H is supplied with an efficiently implementable exact spectral
decomposition

    H = U* diag(lambda_z) U,       0<=lambda_z<=1.

Define

    V|psi> = sum_z <z|U|psi> |z>
             (sqrt(1-lambda_z)|0> + sqrt(lambda_z)|1>),
    B = I tensor |1><1|,
    C = sum_z |z><z| tensor |eta_z^perp><eta_z^perp|,
    |eta_z^perp> = sqrt(lambda_z)|0> - sqrt(1-lambda_z)|1>.

Then V*V=I, V*BV=H, C=I-VV* is a positive projection with exact kernel
ran(V) and gap one, B has row support one, and C has row support at most
two. V is polynomial-size only if the spectral unitary U, a polynomial-time
exact lambda_z table, and the controlled rotations have polynomial-size /
polynomial-bit descriptions. C entry/row access then costs polynomial time.
This is a fully valid active-workspace escape for spectrally explicit H,
including a BMVZ instance if such spectral data were supplied. The BMVZ
term generator supplies local projectors and path labels, not an efficient
diagonalizer of their noncommuting sum; therefore this conditional lift does
not solve the full BMVZ target. It shows why a theorem for the branch encoder
must retain its explicit V-class hypothesis.

## 5. Costs, falsifiers, and remaining gate

* Logical compression: exact (1), full-space operator identity. B is a
  common diagonal positive contraction, one nonzero per row.
* V: polynomial circuit size in the actual term-generator size, T, local
  gate description, and precision. The branch weights are exact rationals
  in the augmented singleton witness; general input weight preparation
  costs its stated bit precision.
* C: any candidate positive contraction is allowed in the barrier. Under
  exact image kernel and polynomial row support, (5) forbids a constant
  gap. The dense exact C=I-VV* has gap one, polynomial circuit and entry
  oracle, but exponentially many row entries. Positivity is preserved;
  there is no normalized positive-term-decomposition assumption to hide or
  spend. If one separately demands a positive sum decomposition, its total
  norm budget must be reported but cannot invalidate this stronger matrix
  obstruction.
* Finite evidence: the standard-library Q(sqrt(2)) checker passes the
  BJSW seed preparation, overlap and relative-matrix support identities.
  Formula (5) and tensorized row-mass statements are analytic, not inferred
  from those finite checks.
* Falsifier: exhibit, for the exact V above and a sequence T/log(s)->infty,
  a positive contraction C with ker(C)=ran(V), row support s, and a certified
  constant positive eigenvalue floor on ran(V)^perp. This would contradict
  the proved Chebyshev row-mass lemma; likely error locations are the
  projector block, the selected-column mass, or the degree-to-row-support
  count. To escape the theorem, supply a different V and show its image
  projector no longer has this row witness.
* Open gate: determine whether an actively prepared, data-dependent quantum
  workspace can change the image projector enough to permit polynomial-row
  constant-gap C while retaining a common diagonal B and exact full-list
  compression. This report does not settle that broad target or the
  constant-locality cost of B,C.
