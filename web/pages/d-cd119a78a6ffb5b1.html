# Finite-field acquisition for fractional subspace packing

Status: independently derived theorem and exact counterexample, awaiting parent gate. This is a specialization safeguard, not a polynomial-time solution of general Brascamp–Lieb membership. The general good-prime acquisition bound remains OPEN. No priority claim is made for the specialization principle; the sharper complete-ground-matroid control is a preserved deduction whose nearest exact prior is still UNKNOWN.

## 1. Question, interface, and recent starting point

Input: rational matrices whose columns span nonzero subspaces S_i of Q^r, nonnegative rational weights x_i, and optionally rational capacities u_i in [0,1]. Define

    P_K(S) = {x >= 0 : sum_i x_i dim_K(S_i intersect W) <= dim_K W for every K-subspace W}.

Weights stay rational external scalars when the geometric field is changed. They are never reduced modulo a prime. For surjective rational maps B_i with row spans S_i and ranks d_i, the additional balance sum_i d_i x_i = r identifies the Brascamp–Lieb dimension polytope over characteristic zero. The finite-field version here is a dimension polytope, not an assertion about an analytic integral inequality over a finite field.

Hiroshi Hirai, arXiv:2608.13972v1, submitted 14 August 2026 05:32:26 UTC, gives an additive FPTAS for the packing dual through optimization on a Euclidean building. Theorem 3.5 charges O(c_max^2 r^2 m^2 epsilon^-2) iterations and O(r^3 m) geometric-field operations per iteration. Theorem 4.5 obtains relaxed BL membership in O(r^7 m^3 epsilon^-2) algebraic-RAM time. The last paragraphs of Section 4.2 explicitly identify exponential rational flag-coordinate height and leave a finite-field reduction for general BL data unresolved. We read Sections 1–4.2, including the complete flag algorithm and Lemma 2.3 basis update; Section 5 has only been screened.

The exact question is which direction of this reduction can already be made sound without supplying a good prime, and whether preserving the entire ground matroid is enough for the other direction.

## 2. Canonical maximizer and characteristic-zero descent

For x_i >= 0, put D(W) = sum_i x_i dim(S_i intersect W) - dim W. The dimension identity and the inclusion

    (S_i intersect U) + (S_i intersect V) subset S_i intersect (U+V)

show D(U)+D(V) <= D(U intersect V)+D(U+V). Only finitely many dimension profiles occur, so the maximum M is attained. Two maximizers have both meet and join maximizing: each has value at most M and their values sum to at least 2M. Consequently there is a unique largest maximizer W_*; choose a maximizer of largest dimension and use the join property to establish uniqueness and containment of every other maximizer.

When the S_i are defined over Q, W_* over C is also defined over Q. Here is the descent argument, including the algebraicity step. Fix the dimension and intersection profile of W_*. In a Grassmannian chart, that profile is expressed by finitely many polynomial equalities and nonvanishing minors with rational coefficients. A nonempty such constructible set over C has a point over Qbar, by introducing inverses for the nonvanishing minors and applying the weak Nullstellensatz. That point is a maximizer, hence equals W_* by the unique-largest property after taking its join with W_*; since both have the chosen maximal dimension they are equal. Every Q-automorphism of Qbar fixes the S_i and all integer intersection dimensions, hence fixes W_*. The unique reduced-row-echelon matrix of W_* therefore has Galois-fixed entries, which belong to Q.

This argument only uses the weights to compare real numbers attached to integer profiles, so rationality of the weights is not needed for this descent; nonnegativity is load-bearing. It follows that

    max_{W over Q} D(W) = max_{W over R} D(W) = max_{W over C} D(W),
    P_Q(S) = P_R(S) = P_C(S).

There is a parallel finite-field statement. A largest maximizer over F_pbar is invariant under Frobenius. Its canonical row-echelon entries are therefore in F_p. Thus extending a finite field to its algebraic closure does not change this packing polytope. Neither descent statement is an efficient construction of W_*.

## 3. Every admissible prime gives a safe inner polytope

An admissible prime preserves the rank of every supplied integer column matrix after denominators have been cleared. Equivalently, one may compute primitive/saturated integer lattices L_i = S_i intersect Z^r and reduce those lattices; then all primes are admissible. Both rank testing at a specified prime and computing a saturated lattice basis are polynomial-bit linear/integer algebra tasks. One may use a common denominator for each input matrix, but one must actually test its reduced rank; rank preservation is not automatic from clearing denominators.

Let W be any rational k-subspace. Its primitive lattice L_W = W intersect Z^r is a direct summand of Z^r, so its reduction has dimension k for every prime. Likewise L_i intersect L_W = (S_i intersect W) intersect Z^r is primitive in Z^r. Its reduction injects into

    (L_i mod p) intersect (L_W mod p).

Therefore

    dim_Fp(S_i,p intersect W_p) >= dim_Q(S_i intersect W).

For an admissible original column presentation, its reduced span equals the reduction of L_i: over Z_p, a full-rank minor makes the original column lattice span the same local direct summand as its saturation. This also follows from Smith normal form, as rank preservation excludes p from the index of the original lattice inside its saturation.

Nonnegative weights now give D_p(W_p) >= D_Q(W). Every characteristic-zero violation survives at every admissible prime. Taking the contrapositive and Section 2 gives the universal inclusion

    P_Fp(S_p) subset P_Q(S) = P_R(S).

With preserved d_i, the same inclusion holds for the balanced BL dimension faces. A finite-field **acceptance** is thus a sound characteristic-zero membership certificate. A finite-field rejection must return UNKNOWN for the original field unless separately lifted or accompanied by a acquired good-prime certificate. This orientation is easy to reverse incorrectly.

The same argument applies to relaxed packing inequalities with an additive constant on the right. For the relaxed BL definition in Hirai Section 4.2, first use the algebraic dual-space relation (his (4.9)); the pairing is nondegenerate in every characteristic and requires no positive-definite metric. Preserve the balance defect as well as the ranks.

## 4. Exact ground-matroid preservation does not ensure equality

Let v(t)=(1,t,t^2), and take six integer columns

    v(1), v(-1), v(2), v(-2), v(3), (1,-3,16).

Group them into consecutive pairs, and let S_1,S_2,S_3 be the three pair spans. Over F_7, the last column is v(-3), and the six parameters 1,-1,2,-2,3,-3 are distinct. Every three-column determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant modulo 7. It follows at once that the complete ground matroid is U_{3,6} both over Q and F_7, including the rank of every subset. All three input spans have rank two in both fields.

Over Q, convenient plane normals are

    n_1=(-1,0,1), n_2=(-4,0,1), n_3=(75,-7,-6).

Their determinant is nonzero (its absolute value is 21). Hence no line belongs to all three planes. At x=(1/2,1/2,1/2), a line contributes at most two intersections and satisfies the packing inequality. For a plane W equal to some S_i, the three intersection dimensions are 2,1,1, and the load is exactly 2. For any other plane they are 1,1,1, giving load 3/2. On Q^3 the load is 3. Thus x is feasible. The full-space constraint gives 2 sum_i x_i <= 3, proving that the unweighted packing optimum is exactly 3/2.

Modulo 7, all three normals have second coordinate zero. They span a two-dimensional normal space, so the three planes share the line ell=span(e_2). Its load is sum_i x_i, which forces sum_i x_i <= 1. Conversely that condition is sufficient because dim(S_i intersect W) <= dim W for every i and nonnegative weights. The finite-field packing optimum is exactly 1. The characteristic-zero BL datum with rows the transposed pair columns is feasible at x, while its finite-field dimension analogue rejects x.

The loss 1/2 persists despite **every** subset rank of the supplied six-vector ground set agreeing. An independence-oracle comparator has all the same information and cannot distinguish these two arrangements. This disproves a specific good-prime proxy, not the existence of better finite-field reductions. It does not contradict the rank-two nc-rank reduction in prior work: that reduction preserves selected blow-up determinant coefficients, a stronger geometric object than this ground matroid.

## 5. Eventual equality is qualitative, not a charged acquisition theorem

For any fixed nonnegative x in P_Q, all violating intersection profiles are empty incidence loci over C in the appropriate Grassmannians. These loci are projective closed conditions: require dim(W intersect S_i) >= a_i and choose profiles with sum_i x_i a_i > dim W. There are finitely many profiles. Spread each incidence locus over Z, inverting only input-presentation denominators/minors as needed. Its generic fiber is empty; properness makes its image a closed subset of Spec Z missing the generic point, hence a finite set of primes. Thus x is feasible over F_p for all but finitely many admissible p.

Since P_Q is a bounded rational polytope for nonzero S_i (x_i <= 1), it has finitely many vertices. Apply the preceding argument to all vertices and take the union of their finite exceptional sets. For all but finitely many admissible primes,

    P_Fp(S_p) = P_Q(S).

This proves existence and supplies neither a polynomial number of test primes nor a polynomial bit bound on the exceptional certificate. Enumerating primes until one accepts is only a one-sided semidecision. It is not a complete polynomial-bit algorithm. The counterexample in Section 4 prevents replacing that missing acquisition result by preservation of all ground-set ranks.

There is a second, separate charge. For rational capacities with common denominator D, the exact optimum of Hirai's capped dual has denominator at most D*r^{r/2}. To see this, fix an optimal flag, partition the bounded p-domain by the hyperplanes c_i-sum_{j in J_i}p_j=0, and minimize the resulting affine objective on a cell vertex. The n-variable vertex equations have entries 0,1,-1, integer right-hand sides, and row norms at most sqrt(r) for r>=2. Hadamard gives the stated determinant bound; the external capacities multiply the objective denominator by at most D. Thus distinguishing optimum r from optimum <r by the additive FPTAS can require epsilon < 1/(D*r^{r/2}). Substitution in Theorem 4.5 costs O(D^2*r^{r+7}*m^3) field operations. This is exponential in the bit length of D and in variable r. Good-prime acquisition alone does not turn this exact-decision route into polynomial-bit complexity.

Finite-field field operations have polynomial bit cost in log p and avoid rational flag-height explosion. External rational p-coordinates have the polynomial height bounds explicitly charged in Hirai Theorem 3.5. These are real advantages of that computational representation, but no affordable full acquisition has been established here.

### A direct fixed-threshold consequence

The same charge gives a modest positive algorithmic refinement in the source's field/RAM model. Let c_i=d_i and take capacities u in [0,1] with common denominator D; write tau for the capped packing optimum. The whole-space constraint gives tau<=r, regardless of whether sum_i d_i u_i=r. Take the integer B=ceil(r^(r/2)) and run Theorem 3.5 with epsilon=1/(2DB), obtaining a feasible dual value v with tau<=v<=tau+epsilon. If tau=r then v>=r. If tau<r, the denominator bound gives tau<=r-1/(DB), hence v<r. Thus the comparison v>=r decides exactly whether the cap admits a balanced packing, in O(D^2 r^(r+7)m^3) field operations. Computing B by integer square-root arithmetic costs polynomially many bits in r log r.

In particular, D=1 and u=1 give exact BL-polytope nonemptiness in O(r^(r+7)m^3) field/RAM operations. Hirai's stated Theorem 4.6(2) obtains O(r^(2r+7)m^3) through full rational reconstruction of the optimum. Only the threshold r is needed here, so the denominator appears once in the required accuracy rather than twice. This is an independently derived fixed-parameter arithmetic bound; the threshold technique is elementary and no general novelty claim is made. It retains the source's potentially exponential rational flag height. It therefore does not resolve polynomial-bit membership, and it is not a benchmarked practical solver.

## 6. Prior, novelty, use, and next falsifiable step

Hirai–Iwamasa–Oki–Soma, arXiv:2310.15502v1, Theorem 2.7 and the paragraph immediately after it, already use determinant coefficients of bounded nc blow-ups to choose a polynomial number of primes of polynomial bit length. Their Theorem 4.9 gives polynomial-bit membership for rank-two BL data. We read these portions and the relevant Section 4.2 comparison, not their complete algorithm proof. They are the stronger completed comparator on that subclass.

Iwamasa–Oki–Soma, arXiv:2407.06493v3 (12 May 2025), Theorem 1.1/3.4, charges polynomial dependence on absolute integer weights, not just their bit lengths. King-subrepresentation maximizers and canonical shrunk-subspace results are established prerequisites; no new claim is made for canonical-maximizer uncrossing or Galois descent itself.

Bürgisser–Franks–Garg–Oliveira–Walter–Wigderson, arXiv:1804.04739v2 (16 April 2018), Corollary 1.8, already gives randomized weak moment-polytope membership with cost polynomial in input bits and 1/epsilon; its BL application is identified in Section 1.1. We read the algorithm, theorem, proof outline, and finite-bit truncation argument in Section 3.3. The complete reduction from arbitrary BL data has not been reconstructed here. An additive approximate-decision advantage is therefore not claimed. Franks, arXiv:1801.01412v2, Theorem 21, is a distinct specified-marginal comparator with explicit inverse-minimum-marginal dependence.

Hirai–Nieuwboer–Walter, published 7 July 2026 in Foundations of Computational Mathematics, DOI 10.1007/s10208-026-09756-8, Theorem 1.8, gives noncommutative optimization iterations O((1+R_0) N(pi) log(N(pi)R_0/epsilon)). The supplied radius R_0 and per-iteration representation/linear-algebra costs remain charges; this is not a polynomial-bit resolution of exact BL membership. We read Sections 1.1–1.4 and the theorem statement, not its full Section 6 proof. The article identifies an earlier 2023 preprint history, so its 2026 journal publication is a recent starting point rather than evidence of a newly discovered 2026 mechanism.

Concrete downstream use: a finite-field implementation of Hirai's algorithm can safely issue positive characteristic-zero packing/relaxed-membership certificates, with UNKNOWN on uncertified rejections. The six-column fixture distinguishes this interface from a tempting but invalid ground-matroid-preservation control. It also distinguishes ordinary finite-ground matroid data from ambient-subspace geometry.

Strongest next step: identify a bounded-degree, bounded-height certificate that preserves the **full packing/King cone**, rather than only the ground matroid, for a class beyond rank two. The discriminating experiment is to calculate the relevant semi-invariant/blow-up coefficients for a first mixed-rank-three arrangement, then test whether the same charged prime bound survives arbitrary rational weight denominators. A successful fixture is not enough: the required theorem is a uniform certificate-degree and coefficient-height bound in r, m, and the input bit length. That lemma remains OPEN.
