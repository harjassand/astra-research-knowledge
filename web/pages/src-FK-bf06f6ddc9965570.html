Adaptive rank-one regression and full affine stability
I established a Jensen theorem that controls all four affine-background directions near a simple transverse acoustic contact. It bounds the actual moving Schur numerator against the actual reduced denominator—not the unreduced quantity used in the previous fixed-contact estimate.
The constructive step is to fit the rank-one reference line to the gradient’s covariance, move the acoustic eigenvector with that fitted line, and subtract the complete pure-line cubic contribution before taking any inverse.
An important qualification is that this theorem requires only centeredness and determinant balance. 1. The theorem: positivity after eliminating every affine direction
Identify planar matrices with \(\mathbb R^4\), and write
\[
q(X)=\det X=X^T\mathsf JX,
\]
where
\[
\mathsf J_{14}=\mathsf J_{41}=\frac12,\qquad
\mathsf J_{23}=\mathsf J_{32}=-\frac12.
\]
Let \(F\) be a rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic. No rotational invariance is assumed.
At a unit rank-one matrix \(R_0\), suppose \(F(R_0)>0\) and there is a unit matrix \(A_0\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\ker D^2F(R_0)=\mathbb RA_0,
}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
P_0:=D^2F(A_0)-\lambda_0\mathsf J\succeq0,
\qquad
\ker P_0=\mathbb RR_0.
}
\tag{2}
\]
I call this a simple transverse contact. In planar matrix space, both positive matrices have rank three. The tangent contacts studied previously have a rank-two remainder and do not satisfy (2).
Full affine-stability theorem
There exist constants \(\delta_0,\eta>0\), depending on \(F,R_0,A_0\), with the following property.
Let \(X\) be any centered random matrix with finite fourth moment and
\[
\mathbb E\det X=0.
\]
Write
\[
X=s_0R_0+Y_0,\qquad s_0=X:R_0,\qquad Y_0:R_0=0.
\]
If
\[
\boxed{
\|Y_0\|_2\le\delta_0\|s_0\|_2,
\qquad
\|Y_0\|_4\le\delta_0\|s_0\|_4,
}
\tag{3}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{B\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}}
\mathbb E\bigl[F(B+X)-F(B)\bigr]
\ge
\eta\,\mathbb E|X|^4.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The zero law has gap zero. The theorem also holds, with a simpler proof, when \(D^2F(R_0)\) is positive definite.
For periodic or zero-boundary \(W^{1,4}\) potentials, \(X=D\varphi\) has the required mean and determinant balance. Thus (4) is an actual global gradient inequality for every perturbation satisfying (3).
There is no bound on \(B\), no positive lower bound assumed for \(\mathbb E|X|^2\), and no pointwise amplitude bound. Concentration is permitted when the two relative transverse-norm conditions hold.
This is stronger than the preceding contact-plane result, which controlled only backgrounds \(tA_0+uR_0\). The previous note explicitly left the other affine directions and their Schur coupling untreated.    CONTACT_COMPENSATION    CONTACT_COMPENSATION
2. Reconstructed foundation—and the representation change
For a centered determinant-balanced law, define
\[
C=\mathbb E[XX^T],\qquad
H=\mathbb ED^2F(X),\qquad
b=\mathbb EDF(X),\qquad c=\mathbb EF(X).
\]
Here \(XX^T\) uses the vectorized matrix.
Quartic symmetry gives
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A]
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[F(B+X)-F(B)]
=c+b:B+\frac12B^THB.
}
\tag{5}
\]
The indefinite Finsler lemma supplies, for every \(A\),
\[
D^2F(A)=P_A+\lambda_A\mathsf J,\qquad P_A\succeq0.
\]
Averaging and using determinant balance proves \(H\succeq0\). This quadratic ingredient is classical; the indefinite case is explicitly distinguished from false unrestricted semidefinite versions in the primary literature. arXiv
When \(H\succ0\),
\[
\Delta_F(X):=\inf_B\mathbb E[F(B+X)-F(B)]
=c-\frac12b^TH^{-1}b.
\tag{6}
\]
The previous Hodge correction was checked to preserve compatibility, and its interpolation exponents were reconstructed. The new proof does not depend on that analytic estimate or on completeness of the previous contact classification. It starts from the explicit spectral assumptions (1)–(2).
Fit the reference line to the actual covariance
Choose a unit rank-one matrix \(R\) maximizing
\[
R^TCR
\]
over
\[
\mathcal N=\{R:|R|=1,\ \det R=0\}.
\]
Then set
\[
s=X:R,\qquad Y=X-sR,\qquad
m_j=\mathbb Es^j,\qquad v=\mathbb E|Y|^2.
\]
The maximizer exists by compactness. When (3) holds, its line remains close to \(\mathbb RR_0\).
The decisive identity is
\[
\boxed{
z:=\mathbb E(sY)
=
-\frac{\mathbb E\det Y}{2|\mathsf JR|^2}\,\mathsf JR.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Proof. Lagrange stationarity on \(\mathcal N\) gives
\[
CR=m_2R+\theta\mathsf JR,
\]
hence \(z=\theta\mathsf JR\). Since \(R:\mathsf JR=0\),
\[
0=\mathbb E\det X
=\mathbb E\det Y+2R^T\mathsf Jz
=\mathbb E\det Y+2\theta|\mathsf JR|^2.
\]
This proves (7).
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{|z|\le Cv.}
\tag{8}
\]
The potentially first-order covariance correlation is now quadratic in the transverse field.
For example, when \(R=e_{12}\) and
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&s\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\]
the identity reads
\[
\mathbb E(sa)=\mathbb E(sd)=0,\qquad
\mathbb E(sc)=\mathbb E(ad).
\]
This improves the averaged-Hessian expansion. Put \(M_R=D^2F(R)\). Then exactly
\[
H=m_2M_R+D^3F(R)[z,\cdot,\cdot]+\mathbb ED^2F(Y),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\|H-m_2M_R\|\le Lv.}
\tag{9}
\]
With an arbitrary fixed reference line, the middle term generally has only an \(O(\sqrt{m_2v})\) bound. The fitted line removes that first-order obstruction.
The fit also preserves the needed smallness: its \(L^2\) residual is no greater than the original residual, and
\[
\sin\angle(R,R_0)
\le
2\frac{\|Y_0\|_2}{\|s_0\|_2},
\]
\[
\|Y\|_4
\le
\|Y_0\|_4+\sin\angle(R,R_0)\|s_0\|_4.
\]
Thus fitting does not silently replace the original hypotheses with stronger ones.
3. Controlling the moving Schur denominator
The next step is to move the background direction as well.
Let \(\epsilon\ge0\) be the least eigenvalue of \(M_R\), and let \(A_R\) be its continuously chosen unit eigenvector near \(A_0\). The other three eigenvalues remain uniformly positive.
Reciprocity and (2) give
\[
0\le\epsilon
\le A_0^TM_RA_0
=R^TP_0R
\le L|R-R_0|^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose a feasible determinant-corrected remainder
\[
P_R=D^2F(A_R)-\lambda_R\mathsf J\succeq0.
\]
A key analytic point is that its transverse positivity persists:
\[
\boxed{
P_R(Y)\ge\nu|Y|^2\quad(Y:R=0),
\qquad
R^TP_RR=\epsilon.
}
\tag{11}
\]
This is not inferred from an arbitrary continuous choice of \(\lambda_R\). Feasible scalars are uniformly bounded by testing positive and negative directions of \(\mathsf J\). Moreover,
\[
R_0^TP_RR_0
=(A_R-A_0)^TM_0(A_R-A_0).
\]
Positive semidefiniteness then bounds \(P_RR_0\), forcing \(\lambda_R\to\lambda_0\). Thus \(P_R\to P_0\), and the positive transverse gap in (2) persists.
Now write the averaged Hessian in the moving frame
\(A_R\oplus A_R^\perp\):
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}h&k^T\\k&D\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equation (9) gives
\[
D\succeq c\,m_2I,\qquad |k|\le Lv.
\tag{12}
\]
Also,
\[
h=\mathbb EP_R(X)
=m_2\epsilon+2(P_RR):z+\mathbb EP_R(Y).
\]
Because \(|P_RR|\le L\sqrt\epsilon\), equations (8) and (11) yield
\[
h\ge m_2\epsilon+(\nu-L\sqrt\epsilon)v
\ge m_2\epsilon+\frac{\nu}{2}v.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore the actual Schur denominator
\[
\sigma=h-k^TD^{-1}k
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\sigma\ge c(m_2\epsilon+v).
}
\tag{14}
\]
More precisely, with \(\delta_2=\sqrt{v/m_2}\),
\[
\boxed{\sigma\ge(1-L\delta_2^2)h.}
\tag{15}
\]
This is the comparison that was previously missing. It is obtained only after adapting both reference directions, and only under the stated transverse spectral hypothesis.
The same proof gives
\[
H\succeq c\,\operatorname{diag}(m_2\epsilon+v,m_2I_3).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus complementary affine elimination no longer destroys the controlled quadratic scale.
4. Remove the whole pure-line cubic before inversion
A denominator estimate alone is insufficient. The cubic must be treated in the same moving representation.
Set
\[
\boxed{
u=\frac{m_3}{3m_2},
\qquad
\ell=b-uHR.
}
\tag{17}
\]
This is a change of affine origin, not a restriction of the minimization.
For the exact rank-one law \(X=sR\), Euler’s identity gives
\[
M_RR=3DF(R),\qquad
H=m_2M_R,\qquad b=m_3DF(R),
\]
so
\[
\ell=0
\]
in every background direction, even if \(M_R\) is singular.
For general \(X=sR+Y\), quartic expansion gives the exact vector identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell={}&M_R\!\left[\mathbb E(s^2Y)-2uz\right]\\
&+\frac12\mathbb E\!\left[sD^3F(R)[Y,Y,\cdot]\right]
+\mathbb EDF(Y)-u\,\mathbb E[D^2F(Y)R].
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Define
\[
S=m_4^{1/4},\qquad
\delta_4=\frac{\|Y\|_4}{S}.
\]
Hölder’s inequality and the eigenvector equation imply
\[
|\ell_D|\le LS^2\sqrt v,
\]
\[
|\ell_A|\le LS^2\sqrt v\,(\epsilon+\delta_2+\delta_4).
\tag{19}
\]
The additional \(\epsilon\) in the dangerous component comes from
\(M_RA_R=\epsilon A_R\). The second-order covariance identity (7) controls the terms containing \(z\).
Combining (16) and (19) proves
\[
\boxed{
\ell^TH^{-1}\ell
\le
L(\delta_2^2+\delta_4^2)m_4.
}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
|\ell_A-k^TD^{-1}\ell_D|
\le
L\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{m_4}\,(\delta_2+\delta_4).
}
\tag{21}
\]
Equation (21) controls the reduced numerator with its reduced denominator. It does not replace \(\sigma\) with \(h\).
The completely optimized value
Shift \(B=-uR+Z\). Then
\[
\Delta_F(X)
=
Q-\frac12\ell^TH^{-1}\ell,
\]
where
\[
Q=c-u(b:R)+\frac12u^2R^THR.
\]
Polynomial expansion and Hölder now yield
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\Delta_F(X)
-
F(R)\left(m_4-\frac{2m_3^2}{3m_2}\right)
\right|
\le
L(\delta_2+\delta_4)m_4.
}
\tag{22}
\]
The reference expression has a fixed positive margin:
\[
m_3^2\le m_2m_4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(R)\left(m_4-\frac{2m_3^2}{3m_2}\right)
\ge\frac{F(R)}3m_4.
\]
For sufficiently small transverse ratios, this absorbs the error and proves (4).
When \(\epsilon=v=0\), the law lies exactly on a rank-one line. Euler’s identity directly supplies the range condition and the exact value in (22), without an inverse.
This completes all affine elimination. The minimizing direction was not fixed in advance.
5. An actual compatible example defeats fixed-frame denominator comparison
The new adapted representation is necessary; a fixed-frame comparison can fail even for uniformly bounded compatible gradients.
Use the previously certified tangent quartic
\[
G(A)=3(u+v-2q)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+q^2-\frac52qu,
\tag{23}
\]
where \(u,v\) are squared column norms and \(q=\det A\). Its rational acoustic certificate is independently reconstructed in the new verifier.
Let
\[
R_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad M(t)=D^2G(R_t).
\]
For \(t\ne0\), \(M(t)\succ0\). In the fixed background direction \(I\), after eliminating the complement spanned by
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),e_{12},e_{21}\),
\[
h=I^TM(t)I=\frac92t^2,
\]
whereas
\[
\sigma=
\frac{10t^4(563t^4+1296t^2+1248)}
{3(595t^6+7992t^4+8064t^2+3072)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\frac{\sigma}{h}\sim\frac{65}{216}t^2\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{24}
\]
This is realized by genuine periodic potentials. Put
\[
s(\theta)=\cos\theta+\frac12\cos2\theta,
\qquad
V(\theta)=\sin\theta+\frac14\sin2\theta,
\]
\[
\varphi_n(x,y)=\left(\frac{V(x+ny)}n,0\right).
\]
Then
\[
D\varphi_n=s(x+ny)R_{1/n}.
\]
These gradients are uniformly bounded, and their averaged Hessians are positive definite.
Their scalar moments are
\[
m_2=\frac58,\qquad m_3=\frac38,\qquad m_4=\frac{99}{128}.
\]
The full answer is exact:
\[
\boxed{
B_{\mathrm{opt}}=-\frac15R_{1/n},
\qquad
\Delta_G(D\varphi_n)
=\frac{399}{640}G(R_{1/n})>0.
}
\tag{25}
\]
The reduced cubic numerator is exactly \(t/10\) times the reduced averaged-Hessian denominator. It vanishes sufficiently rapidly.
Thus this is a counterexample to uniform fixed-frame denominator comparability, not to quasiconvexity. It illustrates why one must retain the numerator’s full algebraic cancellation.
6. Concentration and escaping optimal backgrounds are genuinely included

\[
F_{\mathrm{DM}}(A)=|A|^4-\frac4{\sqrt3}|A|^2\det A

\]
has a simple transverse contact. Its global quasiconvexity is already proved by Bruno and Pasqualotto; it is not a new result here. Their theorem also covers quartics invariant under both left and right rotations, not the entire left-invariant cone. arXiv
At \(R_0=e_{12}\), a kernel background is
\[
\bar A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1/\sqrt3\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The corresponding determinant-corrected remainder has eigenvalues
\[
0,\quad \frac83-\frac{8\sqrt3}{9},\quad
\frac83+\frac{8\sqrt3}{9},\quad\frac{16}{3}.
\]
Its transverse gap is therefore explicit.
A compatible concentrating sequence with divergent optimizer
On the unit torus, take a base potential
\[
\varphi_0=(h_0(y),0),
\]
where \(h_0'\) is \(+1\) on \((0,1/4)\), \(-1\) on \((1/4,1/2)\), and zero elsewhere. Its averaged Hessian has kernel \(\mathbb R\bar A\), and \(b_0=0,c_0=1/2\).
In a region where its gradient vanishes, insert
\[
\rho^{3/4}\psi\!\left(\frac{x-x_*}{\rho}\right),
\]
using the zero-boundary square profile
\[
\psi(s,t)=\bigl(f(s)t(1-t),\,s(1-s)t(1-t)\bigr),
\]
with \(f(s)=2s\) for \(s\le1/3\), and \(f(s)=1-s\) otherwise.
Exact integration gives \(H_\psi\succ0\) and
\[
b_\psi:\bar A=\frac{296}{25515},
\qquad
\bar A:H_\psi\bar A=\frac4{15}.
\]
The combined moments are exactly
\[
H_\rho=H_0+\rho^{3/2}H_\psi,\qquad
b_\rho=\rho^{5/4}b_\psi,\qquad
c_\rho=\frac12+\rho c_\psi.
\]
Full block inversion gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac12b_\rho^TH_\rho^{-1}b_\rho
=
\rho\frac{10952}{43401015}+o(\rho),

and
\[
\boxed{
\rho^{1/4}B_{\mathrm{opt},\rho}
\longrightarrow

The optimizer diverges, but its optimized energy contribution vanishes. The transverse \(L^2\) and \(L^4\) ratios tend to zero, so the theorem covers the sequence. Its concentrated fourth-moment mass is \(O(\rho)\), and the fourth powers are equiintegrable.

7. Why the argument cannot simply extend to tangent contacts
For \(G\) in (23),
\[
D^2G(I)[X,X]
=
\frac12(3a-2d)^2+\frac56c^2-\det X.
\]
The positive remainder has rank two. There is an additional transverse matrix direction on which it vanishes. The key lower bound (11) therefore fails.
This is not merely a missing estimate in the proof. Determinant-balanced laws actually violate the proposed unrestricted tangent extension.
Fix \(m=9\), let \(\varepsilon>0\) be small, and put

w=1-\frac32\varepsilon,\qquad
t=\frac{(9/2)m^2\varepsilon}{w},

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(1,3/2),\qquad
R_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\t&0\end{pmatrix}.

Assign mass \(w/2\) to each of \(\pm R_t\), mass \(\varepsilon\) to \(mS\), and mass \(\varepsilon/2\) to \(-2mS\).
The laws are centered, determinant-balanced and uniformly bounded. Their transverse \(L^2\) and \(L^4\) ratios relative to \(e_{12}\) tend to zero. Moreover, their best covariance-fitted rank-one line is exactly \(\mathbb Re_{12}\) for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\); this was verified algebraically, not numerically.
Nevertheless,
\[
\beta=\mathbb EDG(X)[I]=\frac54\varepsilon m^3,

\[
h=\mathbb ED^2G(X)[I,I]
=\frac{135m^4\varepsilon^2}{8w},

and

\boxed{

\longrightarrow

These are not compatible gradient laws. Their support lies in a two-dimensional matrix subspace, where actual periodic gradients satisfy the two-phase rank-one Jensen argument.
Equation (28) establishes the boundary of the new moment-based operation: covariance adaptation alone cannot resolve tangent contacts. Any successful extension must use additional differential compatibility.
8. What the competing search found, and what remains productive
A separate search used actual finite Fourier potentials for the exactly rank-one-convex \(G\). It eliminated all affine-background directions and allowed invertible input transformations, hence deformed periodic cells and nearly aligned spatial directions.
The run used cutoff three, a dealiased \(13\times13\) grid, six initializations and 600 optimization steps per initialization. The fourth gradient moment was normalized to one. A \(10^{-9}I\) regularizer was used only in the linear solve; saved backgrounds were evaluated in the unregularized polynomial gap.
The smallest directly reevaluated gap was
\[
0.9318892963523981.
\]
No negative compatible gap was found. This is finite local-search evidence, not a lower bound, a quasiconvexity certificate for \(G\), or coverage of the full cone.
The new theorem’s generality is real but conditional. It uses no rotational symmetry and therefore applies to any of the 35-coefficient planar quartics satisfying (1)–(2). The same proof works for a nondegenerate indefinite quadratic null cone in any finite-dimensional Euclidean space, with analogous spectral assumptions. It does not automatically extend to higher-dimensional matrix rank-one cones, which require several minors.
Nor is a novelty claim justified merely by combining familiar ingredients. Cubic and fourth-order resonant necessary conditions already appear in Gutiérrez’s foundational work. Lagrange multipliers, spectral perturbation, Schur complements and Hölder estimates are established tools. The result derived here is their specific integration into the all-affine estimate (22). heldermann-verlag.de
The remaining mathematical operation
The investigation resolves the moving Schur difficulty in transverse near-line neighborhoods. Repeating fixed-contact estimates there is unnecessary: all affine directions are now handled.
Two substantive obstructions remain.
At tangent contacts, the previous Hodge/mixed-derivative theorem must be made uniform under both covariance fitting and motion of the acoustic eigenvector. The relevant cubic is no longer \(b:A_0\); it is the regressed vector
\[
\ell=b-\frac{m_3}{3m_2}HR
\]
measured in the moving averaged-Hessian geometry. The bounded noncompatible laws above prove that determinant-moment algebra cannot supply the missing estimate.
Away from the near-line regime, substantial transverse fourth-moment mass and internally resonant profiles remain. The theorem provides neither a global decomposition into its covered neighborhoods nor a positive rule for recombining unresolved profiles. Critical concentration can preserve those profiles rather than make them perturbative.
The decisive positive continuation would therefore control
\[
\ell^TH^{-1}\ell
\]
by a sufficiently sharp, genuinely differential quartic remainder at moving tangent contacts and beyond the near-line regime. The competing outcome remains an actual compatible gradient for which the fully optimized gap is negative.
The advance is an affine-adaptive operation, not another fixed-contact inequality: fit the rank-one line to the covariance, transport the acoustic direction, remove the complete pure-line cubic, and only then perform Schur elimination. It closes that elimination problem under explicit transverse hypotheses while demonstrating exactly why the remaining tangent sector requires new differential information.
