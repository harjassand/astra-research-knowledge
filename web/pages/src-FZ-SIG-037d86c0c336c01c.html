## 2. Notation and the exact contract

For `A=[[a,b],[c,d]]`, set

\[
u=a^2+c^2,\quad v=b^2+d^2,\quad w=ab+cd,\quad j=ad-bc.
\]

Then `uv=w^2+j^2`. All matrix norms here are Frobenius. For `r>0`, put

\[
\alpha=\frac{3r}{8(r+2)},\quad\beta=\frac{r(r-1)}6,
\quad t=\frac{2r+1}{3},\qquad
J_r(A)=\alpha(u+v-2j)^2+\beta u^2+j^2+ruj.
\]

Two exact parameter relations simplify the proof:

\[
\beta=\alpha(t^2-1),\qquad r-4\alpha=4\alpha t.
\]

Thus

\[
J_r=\alpha t^2u^2+\alpha v^2+2\alpha uv
+(4\alpha+1)j^2+4\alpha tuj-4\alpha vj. \tag{A1}
\]

The result is quasiconvexity **at a specified constant background `B`**:

\[
\int_\Omega[J_r(B+D\phi)-J_r(B)]\,dx\ge0
\]

for every bounded Lipschitz planar `Omega` and every
`phi in W_0^{1,4}(Omega;R^2)`, together with the periodic analogue. There
is no smallness, determinant-sign, injectivity, Fourier cutoff, or rank
restriction on `B+Dphi`. The adjective quasiconvex without a background
qualifier would require every `B`; that conclusion is not obtained.

## 3. Reconstruction of the universal Gram translation

Let `V=A-B`, `g=A^T A`, `g_B=B^T B`, and
`C(X)=(X11-X22,X12+X21)`. Elementary two-dimensional determinant identities
give

\[
\det(g-g_B)=j_A^2+j_B^2-|A\operatorname{adj}B|^2,
\qquad |X|^2-2\det X=|CX|^2. \tag{A2}
\]

Because `B adj(B)=j_B I` and `C(I)=0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(g-g_B)-(j_A-j_B)^2
&=2j_Aj_B-|A\operatorname{adj}B|^2\\
&=-|C(A\operatorname{adj}B)|^2\\
&=-|C(V\operatorname{adj}B)|^2. \tag{A3}
\end{aligned}
\]

There is no inverse and therefore no singular-background exclusion.
The checker verifies (A2) and (A3) for arbitrary eight matrix entries.

Write `Delta u=u_A-u_B`, and similarly for `v,w,j`, and put
`z=(Delta u,Delta v,Delta j)^T`. Let `f(u,v,j)` be (A1). The physical
Taylor expansion is particularly transparent:

\[
J_r(A)-J_r(B)-DJ_r(B):V
=\alpha z^TM_{r,0}z+f_u(B)u_V+f_v(B)v_V+f_j(B)\det V. \tag{A4}
\]

Indeed, `Delta u=Du(B):V+u_V`,
`Delta v=Dv(B):V+v_V`, and
`Delta j=Dj(B):V+det V`. The quadratic form in invariant differences is
exact, since `f` is quadratic. Set

\[
M_{r,k}=\begin{pmatrix}
t^2&1-k&2t\\1-k&1&-2\\2t&-2&4+2k+1/\alpha
\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
L_{r,k}(B)=\begin{pmatrix}
t^2u_B+(1-k)v_B+2tj_B&kw_B\\
kw_B&(1-k)u_B+v_B-2j_B
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{A5}
\]

The difference between the invariant blocks at `k` and zero, including
`2alpha k(Delta w)^2`, is

\[
-2\alpha k[\Delta u\Delta v-(\Delta w)^2-(\Delta j)^2]
=2\alpha k|C(V\operatorname{adj}B)|^2.
\]

Also `L_{r,k}=L_{r,0}-k cof(g_B)` and

\[
\operatorname{tr}(V\operatorname{cof}(g_B)V^T)
=|V\operatorname{adj}B|^2.
\]

Subtracting this quadratic term leaves precisely
`-4alpha k j_B det V`, because
`det(V adj B)=j_B det V`. Consequently, with

\[
\lambda_{r,k}(B)=f_j(B)+4\alpha kj_B
=(2+8\alpha+4\alpha k)j_B+(r-4\alpha)u_B-4\alpha v_B,
\]

the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
R_{r,k}(A,B)&:=J_r(A)-J_r(B)-DJ_r(B):V
-\lambda_{r,k}(B)\det V\\
&=\alpha z^TM_{r,k}z+2\alpha k(\Delta w)^2
+2\alpha\operatorname{tr}(VL_{r,k}(B)V^T).
\end{aligned}} \tag{A6}
\]

The checker expands both sides independently in arbitrary `A,B,r,k` and
obtains the zero rational polynomial. This is a global pointwise identity,
not an identity tested on selected matrices.

## 4. Matrix positivity over the complete positive parameter range

\[

\[
\begin{aligned}

If `1-t` is negative its lower-bound role is redundant; otherwise

## 5. All-domain and periodic quasiconvexity passage

Assume `L_{r,k_*}(B)` is PSD. Every term on the right of (A6) is then
nonnegative. The left is a supporting plane in the minors at `B`. For a
smooth compactly supported perturbation,

\[
\int_\Omega D\phi=0,\qquad \int_\Omega\det D\phi=0.
\]

The second equality follows, for example, from
`det Dphi=div(phi1 phi2_y,-phi1 phi2_x)`; the mixed derivatives cancel.
There is no orientation assumption. Integrating (A6), with its constant
background-dependent slope, removes both the linear and determinant
terms. A `W_0^{1,4}` field admits strong smooth approximation. Its
determinants converge strongly in `L^2`, and all degree-four energy terms
converge in `L^1` by Hölder and strong `L^4` convergence. This proves the
stated bounded-domain inequality. On a periodic cell the boundary terms
cancel between opposite faces; periodic smooth density gives the same
Sobolev extension. The argument works on every stated domain and imposes
no finite-dimensional perturbation restriction.

\[

\[

(A6) with rational `k=2/3` gives a strictly positive quartic lower bound.

The source's compactness proof of mixed coercivity is valid. The following
explicit receiving derivation supplies a constructive substitute. It is
not claimed optimal. Set

\[
P(r)=15r^3+31r^2+46r+40,
\qquad \mu_r=\frac{\alpha}{\operatorname{tr}(M_{r,2/3}^{-1})}
=\frac{3r^2(r+1)}{2(r+2)P(r)}>0. \tag{A10}

The trace identity is checked exactly:
`tr(M^{-1})=P(r)/(4r(r+1))`. For a positive definite matrix,
`lambda_min(M)>=1/tr(M^{-1})`, so the invariant block in (A6) bounds
`mu_r |z|^2`. In particular `J_r(A)>=mu_r |A|^4/2` at `B=0`, since
`u^2+v^2>=|A|^4/2`.

For singular `B`, put `x=p^2`, `y=q^2`, `s=x+y=|B|^2`. At `k=2/3`,
two exact identities provide a uniform determinant lower bound:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det L-\frac{r(r+1)}6s^2
&=\frac{1-t^2}{24}(x-3y)^2 &&(0<r\le1),\\

Thus `det L>=d_r s^2`, where

\eta_r=2\alpha d_r/T_r=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3r^2(r+1)}{32(r+2)},&0<r\le1,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{9r}{16(r+2)(r^2+r+1)},&r\ge1.
\end{cases} \tag{A11}

For singular `B`, `j_B=0`; the slope and residual in (A6) are independent

\[
R_r(A,B)\ge\mu_r|z|^2+\eta_r|B|^2|V|^2. \tag{A12}

Write `S=|V|^2`, `X=B:V`. Then
`S=Delta u+Delta v-2X`, and `X^2<=|B|^2 S`. Therefore

\[
S^2\le2(\Delta u+\Delta v)^2+8X^2
\le4|z|^2+8|B|^2S.

Consequently, a fully explicit choice is

\[
\boxed{c_r^{\mathrm{audit}}=
\min\!\left(\frac{\mu_r}{4},\frac{\eta_r}{9}\right)>0,\qquad
R_r(A,B)\ge c_r^{\mathrm{audit}}(|V|^4+|B|^2|V|^2).} \tag{A13}

Integration gives the all-domain and periodic mixed bound. Both functions

## 11. The conformal obstruction is a support obstruction, not a QC map

## 14. Primary comparators and the priority boundary

Kirchheim–Kristensen, *On rank-one convex functions that are homogeneous
of degree one*, Corollary 1.2, proves convex support at the rank-at-most-one
cone for positively **one**-homogeneous ROC functions. Its primary
statement does not cover these degree-four quartics. Moreover, the tempting
fourth-root reduction fails explicitly: for `F=J_1`, at `I` in rank-one
direction `e11`, `F=2`, `DF[e11]=5`, `D^2F[e11,e11]=8`. Hence
`D^2(F^{1/4})[e11,e11]=-11/(16*2^{7/4})<0`. This fourth root is not ROC.
This check rules out that automatic application; it does not establish
novelty. [Primary PDF, Corollary 1.2, page 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.04654).

Guerra–Kristensen, *Automatic quasiconvexity of homogeneous isotropic
rank-one convex integrands*, Theorem 1.3, assumes nonnegativity, ROC,
positive `p`-homogeneity with `p>=n`, and two-sided SO(n) isotropy. It
concludes polyconvexity at conformal matrices. Their definition of isotropy
is `F(QAR)=F(A)` for both independent rotations; left invariance alone
does not suffice. Their Proposition 3.7 separately illustrates why isotropy
matters. `J_r` fails right invariance: if `Q` is the quarter-turn matrix,
`J_r(diag(2,1))-J_r(diag(2,1)Q)=r(5r+7)/2>0` for every `r>0`.
The automatic theorem therefore supplies neither this singular-background
calibration nor a conformal resolution. [Primary PDF, Theorem 1.3 and
definition (1.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10563).

Bruno–Pasqualotto's Theorem 2 in arXiv:2608.06367v1 likewise assumes both
left and right SO(2) invariance for its degree-four ROC/QC equivalence.
The same exact right-invariance failure blocks direct application. Its
heat/exact-form strategy is relevant predecessor machinery; the present
proof uses an ordinary minor support at selected backgrounds.
[Primary Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1).

Determinant null Lagrangians, supporting planes for convex lifts, Gram
identities, and translation of quadratic constraints are classical
algebraic/variational tools. The appropriate family-specific object here
is (A6), its positive parameter range, and its background geometry. This
bounded primary comparison does not clear historical priority of that
object, or rule out another older rank-one-background theorem under other
hypotheses. No general novelty or foundational-breakthrough conclusion
is admitted by this audit. Pedregal's separate proof claim and its permitted
mold counterexample are outside this worker's assignment and were not
independently re-audited.
