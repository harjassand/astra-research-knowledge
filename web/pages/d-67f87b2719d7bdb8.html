# Canonical SOS metric and the hidden diagonal quadratic space

It is not yet a polynomial-bit exact learner or a practical advantage over ordinary SOS.

## Exact question and interface

Input is the expanded rational quartic f, promised to have an unknown rational invertible frame L and rational Q positive definite with f(x)=( (Lx)^2 )^T Q ( (Lx)^2 ). Neither L, Q, an aligned metric, a spectral gap, a height bound nor a conditioning radius reaches the proposed learner. Proofs may use the hidden frame. The first new object is an aligned real metric and a rational posterior certificate for its approximation. The second is the complete n-dimensional space D_L=span{ell_i(x)^2}, potentially useful for small generalized simultaneous diagonalization. Acquiring exact rational D_L from approximate optimization remains a separate cost and precision obligation.

Let z(x) be the N=n(n+1)/2 plain degree-two monomials, with diagonals followed by i<j mixed monomials. Write m(M) for the coefficients of x^T M x, so diagonal coordinates are M_ii and mixed coordinates are 2M_ij. Let A(G) be the coefficients of z^T G z. Consider

    maximize log det M, M positive definite,
    A(G)=f,
    [[G,m(M)],[m(M)^T,1]] positive semidefinite.                 (1)

The Schur complement is S=G-m m^T >=0 and A(S)=f-(x^T M x)^2. The program has a PSD block of order N+1, an n-by-n metric, and binomial(n+3,4) coefficient equalities. This size is genuinely smaller than the direct ambient-degree30 matrix. Size alone is not a bit-complexity or runtime guarantee.

## Existence, uniqueness and alignment

The QPD promise implies an interior SOS Gram. In hidden coordinates take mixed Gram diagonals epsilon>0 and mass block Q-(epsilon/2)(J-I). Their polynomial is f. For sufficiently small epsilon the mass block is positive definite. The invertible degree-two feature action of L transports a positive definite Gram to observed coordinates. A sufficiently small positive rational M then makes the Schur complement strictly positive. This proves Slater feasibility; it does not supply its unknown margin to an optimizer.

The feasible metric set is convex by the block-LMI representation. It is bounded: feasibility implies f(e_i)>=M_ii^2, and PSD bounds the off-diagonal entries. The SOS cone is closed, so its closure is compact. There is a feasible positive definite metric; a determinant maximizer therefore exists and is positive definite. Strict concavity of log det gives a unique M_*.

Each unknown hidden coordinate sign flip R preserves f and has determinant of absolute value one. Congruence M -> R^T M R preserves (1), SOS and the objective. Uniqueness gives R^T M_* R=M_*. Consequently M_*=L^T diag(d) L for d_i>0. This acquires alignment conceptually without acquiring or supplying R. The optimal metric is generally algebraic even when f,L,Q are rational; a numerical SPD matrix by itself is not an exact aligned metric.

## Pointwise problem is exact and has full signed-cube contact

For the clean exact characterization, use root's independently derived alternative to contact-support KKT. Minimize on p>0

    psi(p)=p^TQp/2-(1/n)sum_i log p_i.

This function is strictly convex and coercive by QPD, and diverges at the finite positive-orthant boundary. Its unique minimizer is interior and satisfies

    d_i p_i=1/n, all p_i>0, Qp=d, d^T Q^-1 d=1.                (2)

The positivity follows directly from the barrier critical equation. Cauchy in the Q inner product gives Q-dd^T >=0, of rank n-1, so diag(d) is SOS feasible. Any other feasible aligned d'>0 obeys d'^Tp<=1 by evaluating its pointwise bound at squared coordinates p. AM–GM on the numbers d'_i p_i gives product d'<=product d, proving optimality. The SOS and pointwise maxima coincide for this promised class. No convexity of f as a polynomial is needed. This proof avoids assuming an unknown contact support is interior.

Equality f(y)=(sum_i d_i y_i^2)^2 holds precisely on rays y_i^2 proportional to p_i. On f=1 the contacts are all sign choices of sqrt(p). After whitening M_*, their coordinates have squared magnitudes d_i p_i=1/n. This is a full signed cube, not a supplied contact orbit.

## Unique residual Gram and a small quadratic space

Any quadratic q whose square occurs in an SOS of the optimal residual vanishes on every contact sign choice. On that sign cube the diagonal part is one constant character and each mixed term y_i y_j is a distinct nonconstant character. Fourier independence forces all mixed coefficients to zero and sum_i a_i p_i=0. Therefore every such q belongs to

    D_0={sum_i a_i ell_i(x)^2 : a^T p=0}, dim D_0=n-1.

Every PSD residual Gram has range inside D_0. In hidden coordinates it consequently has only its diagonal-monomial mass block; the polynomial coefficients then uniquely determine that block as Q-dd^T. Thus the optimal residual Gram S_* is **unique**, rank n-1, and its range is D_0. Its range together with the metric quadratic spans D_L, because d^T p=1. This is an exact theorem about the optimizer, not a rank inference from a floating-point iterate.

After exact rational D_L has been acquired, any positive definite quadratic H in that space and a separating A in it give a generalized eigenproblem H^-1 A with the hidden primal axes as eigenvectors. Under the rational-hidden promise the eigenvalues of a rational H,A are rational. A rational separating linear combination can be scanned and checked. Finding H, representing D_L exactly and certifying separation remain charged. A numerical Euclidean eigenbasis of M_* alone is not the hidden frame.

## Rational primal/dual metric confidence certificate

The following is checkable from coefficients and candidates alone. Let M_c>0 be rational and let rational G_c satisfy A(G_c)=f and G_c-m_c m_c^T>=0. Let lambda be a rational linear functional on quartics, K=A^*lambda, and w the rational vector satisfying

    2w^T m(M)=tr(M_c^-1 M) for every symmetric M.

Supply rational alpha with [[K,-w],[-w^T,alpha]]>=0. Put c=lambda(f)+alpha. For any feasible M,

    tr(M_c^-1 M)=2w^T m <= m^T K m+alpha <= lambda(f)+alpha=c.

Hence c>=n and det M_* / det M_c <= (c/n)^n. A fully rational safe upper bound on the log-det gap is delta=c-n, using n log(c/n)<=c-n. No true-metric norm is supplied.

Let A_*=M_*^-1/2 M_c M_*^-1/2. Convex optimality at M_* gives tr A_*<=n. With the gap bound,

    sum_i [lambda_i-1-log lambda_i]<=delta,

so every eigenvalue obeys that scalar bound. If rational 0<a<1/2 and 2delta<=a^2, the integral bound t-1-log t >= (t-1)^2/(2 max{t,1}) implies

    (1-a) M_* <= M_c <= (1-a)^-1 M_*,
    (1-zeta) M_c <= M_* <= (1+zeta) M_c,
    zeta=a/(1-a)<1.                                           (3)

The upper eigenvalue endpoint is excluded by evaluating the scalar lower bound at (1-a)^-1; the lower endpoint follows directly. Reciprocal generalized eigenvalues give the reversed congruence. Rational PSD/PD checks and polynomial equality are sufficient to verify the certificate. If no such certificate is found, output UNKNOWN; fitted residuals do not imply (3).

Equation (3) can replace the previously supplied aligned-metric confidence premise in the sealed global quartic ambiguity certificate, for **this exact coefficient/QPD interface**. Row rescaling of L by sqrt(d) preserves QPD, so the true positive pair gaps remain a model promise. A candidate's positivity does not certify a different physical source model. With noisy coefficients this optimum is aligned to the observed polynomial only if its own promise holds; no unproved stability of M_*(f) across arbitrary coefficient perturbations is imported.

## Obstacles, controls and next falsifiable step

Covariance/eigenmatrix/MaxKurt controls, when applicable, receive the same acquired metric confidence and posterior check. No advantage is obtained by providing contacts, latent Q or an optimizer. Existing exact degree30 acquisition remains the competent same-input universal comparator.
