# Metric Markov cotype and Lipschitz extension into ambient (L_1)

## Result and evidence boundary

There are two internally reconstructed consequences of pinned OpenAI/math family 332, dated October 5, 2026. Both are conditional on the correctness of that paper's proof of
\[
N_2(\ell_1)\le 12\sqrt{21},\qquad N_2(\ell_1)^2=3024.
\]

1. **Ambient cotype transfer.** For every real measure-space (L_1(\Omega,\mu)), the same finite metric Markov-cotype-two inequality follows conditionally with (N_2\le12\sqrt{21}). This is an internal transfer from family 332, not a new claim that Lebesgue (L_1[0,1]) has this property.
2. **Extension consequence.** If a metric source (X) has finite Markov type 2 constant (M_2(X)), then every Lipschitz map from any subset (S\subseteq X) to any real ambient (L_1(\Omega,\mu)) has a global extension satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Lip}(F)\le C_{\rm MN}\,12\sqrt{21}\,M_2(X)\,\operatorname{Lip}(f),
\]
where (C_{\rm MN}\) is the unspecified universal factor in Mendel–Naor Corollary 1.13. The domain and range of (f) may be arbitrary; in particular neither needs to be separable or finite-range. This is an existence theorem, not a constructive algorithm.

The second statement is **not** a new solution of the Hilbert-to-(L_1) existence question: Naor's September 2026 preprint already proves that every map from an arbitrary subset of \(\ell_2\) into Lebesgue (L_1[0,1]) extends globally. More decisively, its exact v2 added-in-proof reports forthcoming Mendel–Naor [MN26] work proving that (L_1) has metric Markov cotype 2. The paper's notation defines (L_p) as Lebesgue space on [0,1] (v2, line 40 footnote); [MN26] is identified at reference line 408 as *Metric invariants from de-Höldering factorization* (2026 preprint), but no URL or full text is given there. Hence the qualitative cotype claim and, by Mendel–Naor Corollary 1.13, the qualitative linear-(M_2(X)) extension consequence are author-source-reported forthcoming claims, not open gaps established here. The full [MN26] proof was not located or independently audited. The exact family-332 constant (12\sqrt{21}) and the finite rational constructor in [FINITE_MEDIAN_CONSTRUCTOR.md](FINITE_MEDIAN_CONSTRUCTOR.md) remain conditional candidates, not priority-cleared results.

## Exact release claim and proof mechanism

The family-332 manuscript defines **real sequence space**
\[
\ell_1=\{x=(x(k))_{k\ge1}:\sum_k|x(k)|<\infty\}
\]
on page 1 and states Theorem 1.2, (N_2(\ell_1)\le12\sqrt{21}). Its abstract and Corollary 6.2 explicitly give global extension from arbitrary Hilbert subsets into this sequence space. It does not state an ambient function-space (L_1) theorem. The release's proof was re-read and internally reconstructed; no external referee or formal proof checker validated it.

The load-bearing mechanism, with its exact constants, is:

* **Finite cut factorization (Lemma 2.1).** For a finite family (x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\ell_1), define the baseline (v(k)=\min_i x_i(k)), and for each nontrivial cut (B\subset[n]),
  \[
  b_B(k)=\big(\min_{i\in B}x_i(k)-\max_{j\notin B}x_j(k)\big)_+.
  \]
  The binary codes (z_i(B)=1_{i\in B}), weights (w_B=\|b_B\|_1), and affine recovery (T(u)=v+\sum_Bu_Bb_B) satisfy
  \[
  \|x_i-x_j\|_1=\|z_i-z_j\|_{1,w}=\|z_i-z_j\|_H^2,
  \quad T(z_i)=x_i,
  \quad \|T(u)-T(u')\|_1\le\|u-u'\|_{1,w}.
  \]
  There are at most (2^n-2) cuts. Pointwise, the positive cuts are exactly the nested upper level sets of the finitely many coordinate values; their gaps telescope.
* **Nonlinear smoothing (Lemma 3.1).** For (Q=[0,1]^B), (φ(r)=3r^2-2r^3), and (Φ) coordinatewise, the fourth-power potential (F_e(u)=\|u-e\|_H^4) gives
  \[
  \|\Phi(u')-\Phi(u)\|_{1,w}^2
  \le108\big(F_e(u')-F_e(u)-4\|u-e\|_H^2\langle u-e,u'-u\rangle_H\big)
  \]
  for each binary (e). In the martingale, (e) is measurable at time zero and the linear term has conditional expectation zero.
* **Stopped-walk accounting.** With killing probability (p=1/(t+1)), (q=1-p), stationary (A)-walk, and independent geometric (S\) with \(\Pr[S=s]=pq^s), define
  \[
  h_i=\mathbb E_i z_{X_S}=p z_i+q\sum_j a_{ij}h_j,
  \qquad y_i=T(\Phi(h_i)).
  \]
  The alive/dead martingale has terminal value (z_{X_S}). Its expected sum of squared weighted-ℓ1 jumps is exactly (B+tE_A), the two costs before applying (T). Corollary 3.2 bounds this by (108\,\mathbb E\|x_{X_S}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2).
* **Endpoint conversion (Lemma 4.1).** Stationarity and the metric triangle inequality imply the geometric endpoint estimate
  \[
  \mathbb E d(x_{X_S},x_{X_0})^2\le {28\over t}\sum_{s=1}^t\mathbb E d(x_{X_s},x_{X_0})^2.
  \]
  Thus (108\cdot28=3024). Remark 5.1 notes that stationarity alone suffices; reversibility is not used after stationarity is available.

For ambient (L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)), choose representatives of a finite family and use the same (v,b_B) formulas pointwise in ω. They are measurable and integrable, since 
\[
|v|\le\sum_i|x_i|,\qquad 0\le b_B\le2\sum_i|x_i|.
\]
Integrating the nested-level-set identities yields the same finite weighted-cut code and contractive affine recovery into the **given ambient (L_1)**. The martingale and geometric endpoint estimates use only this finite code and recovery map, so every line and both constants (108,28) remain unchanged. This proves the stated ambient metric Markov cotype inequality conditionally on Theorem 1.2. It does not assert cotype inheritance by every closed subspace; the recovery map need not land in such a subspace.

## Finite-data extension into ambient (L_1)

Let (X) have Markov type 2 constant (M_2(X)<\infty), and let (f:A\to E=L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)) be (L)-Lipschitz on a finite nonempty set (A\subseteq X). Its range contains at most (|A|) values. Apply the ambient finite cut construction to these values to obtain a weighted finite ℓ1 code (z_a\) and contractive affine recovery (T) with (T(z_a)=f(a)). Embed that code isometrically into the first (d\le2^{|A|}-2) coordinates of ℓ1, apply Mendel–Naor Corollary 1.13 to the coded data (the global theorem applies because ℓ1 is the dual (c_0^*)), project back to those (d) coordinates, and compose with (T). This gives a **global** finite-data extension (F_A:X\to E) with
\[
F_A|_A=f|_A,\qquad \operatorname{Lip}(F_A)\le K L,
\quad K=C_{\rm MN}M_2(X)12\sqrt{21}.
\]
The coefficient (C_{\rm MN}) is not made explicit by Corollary 1.13, so neither is the numerical extension constant. The required interface is actual range vectors in (L_1), and the construction is non-effective when the values/integrals or the Mendel–Naor extension are not represented effectively.

Mendel–Naor Theorem 1.11 alone is insufficient here: it only extends the data to each finite subset of the source. Corollary 1.13 is the exact global interface, and it applies to the coded dual target ℓ1.

## Nonseparable finite-to-global bridge by L-embeddedness

Use the following source fact: (L_1)-spaces are L-embedded Banach spaces, so the canonical copy (J(E)\subset E^{**}) is the range of an L-projection (P:E^{**}\to J(E)) of norm one. The primary source checked here is Pfitzner, *L-embedded Banach spaces and measure topology* (2000), which defines an L-projection and says that ℓ1 and (L_1)-spaces are L-embedded; it records the decomposition (E^{**}=J(E)\oplus_1 E_s). This is source-reported standard Banach-space structure, not reproved from the Yosida–Hewitt theorem in this report.

Assume (S\ne\varnothing), choose (s_0\in S), put (\Lambda=KL), and for every (x\in X) define
\[
R_x=\|f(s_0)\|_1+\Lambda d(x,s_0).
\]
In the product (\prod_{x\in X} R_xB_{E^{**}}), use the weak* topology \(\sigma(E^{**},E^*)\) in each coordinate. The balls are weak*-compact by Banach–Alaoglu. Impose the closed constraints
\[
u_s=Jf(s)\quad(s\in S),\qquad
\|u_x-u_y\|_{E^{**}}\le\Lambda d(x,y)\quad(x,y\in X).
\]
The boundary constraints are weak*-closed; the pair constraints are weak*-closed because the bidual norm is weak*-lower-semicontinuous. Every finite collection of constraints involves finitely many boundary points (B\subseteq S) and finitely many source pairs. Apply the finite-data extension property above to (A=B\cup\{s_0\}). Its global extension (F_A:X\to E), embedded in (E^{**}), satisfies every selected constraint. It also lies in every coordinate ball, since
\[
\|F_A(x)\|_1\le\|f(s_0)\|_1+\Lambda d(x,s_0)=R_x.
\]
Thus the constraint family has the finite-intersection property. Tychonoff's theorem gives a tuple ((u_x)_{x\in X}) satisfying all constraints. Set
\[
F(x)=J^{-1}P(u_x).
\]
Since (P) is linear, contractive and the identity on (J(E)), this map extends (f) and has Σ Lipschitz constant at most Λ. No separability, common-support, joint measurability, or weak compactness of (L_1) itself is needed. If (S=\varnothing), take (F\equiv0). This is a standard compactness bridge; it is not the source of novelty.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

* **Family 332, pinned release.** The release's exact target is real sequence ℓ1, explicitly defined on page 1. Theorem 1.2 says (N_2(\ell_1)\le12\sqrt{21}). Corollary 6.2 says arbitrary Hilbert-subset maps into ℓ1 extend globally. The release does not claim ambient (L_1), and it does not give an algorithm for computing extensions. Proof details and page pointers are in [the extracted source](children/metric_representation/sources/l1-markov-cotype-extracted.txt).
* **Mendel–Naor (2013).** Theorem 1.11 is a finite-partial-extension statement. Corollary 1.13 supplies a global extension with bound \(\lesssim M_p(X)N_p(Y)\) only for dual Banach target (Y). Thus the finite-cut code into ℓ1 and the target L-embedding bridge are both necessary in the proof of the ambient, arbitrary-range claim.
* **Naor (2026), de-Höldering.** The Sep 10, 2026 v2 explicitly proves \(e(\ell_2;L_1[0,1])<\infty\), answering Ball's Hilbert-source question. Earlier Remark 9 (lines 117–118) says L1 metric Markov cotype had not yet been investigated, but the later added-in-proof (lines 367–369) supersedes that text: forthcoming Mendel–Naor [MN26] is reported to demonstrate L1 metric Markov cotype 2 and the predicted \(e(L_q;L_1)\lesssim\sqrt q\) for every \(q\ge2\). The v2 defines \(L_p\) as Lebesgue [0,1] at line 40; [MN26] is cited at line 408 without a linked full text. Treat this as author-source-reported prior art, not independently proof-audited validation. Do not present the qualitative cotype or Markov-type-to-L1 extension conclusion as a new result.
* **Subspace boundary.** Mendel–Naor Theorem 1.14 exhibits a closed subspace of ℓ1 with no finite metric Markov cotype. Nothing here says the transferred ambient bound holds for all (L_1) subspaces or that extensions can be constrained to a prescribed subspace.

## Costs, limits, and validation

The exact finite cut representation has at most (2^r-2) coordinates for (r) distinct range values. For explicit (m)-coordinate rational vector data, forming all cuts costs (O(mr2^r)) arithmetic operations before bit-growth charges; arbitrary (L_1) input requires access to measurable representatives and cut-weight integration. Global extension is existential: the cited extension corollary and Tychonoff step provide no runtime, bit complexity, finite-precision selector, or output representation. No implementation or computational test was relevant to this theorem.

The finite-to-global proof was independently audited in the separable case using Komlós averaging by a second workstream. The nonseparable product/L-projection proof above was hostile-checked directly against its interfaces; the L-embedded premise is supported by the cited primary source. These are internal audits only. The family-332 theorem remains the load-bearing release premise and has not been externally validated or proof-assistant checked. Novelty remains `UNKNOWN`.

The family-332 Remark 5.3 expected-median identity yields a deterministic exact constructor for finite rational vector inputs, with polynomial rational bit complexity and a locally sampleable endpoint representation. The implementation and charged interface are recorded in [FINITE_MEDIAN_CONSTRUCTOR.md](FINITE_MEDIAN_CONSTRUCTOR.md) and [finite_l1_median_rounding.py](finite_l1_median_rounding.py). Since the release already states the median representation and no historical search covered an unpublished MN26 construction, this is a reusable candidate implementation consequence, not a novelty claim.
