## Exact local interface and premises

Let `1 <= k <= n`, `K=64k`, `R=floor(sqrt(K))`,
`H=32R^2`, `eta=1/(Kn)`, and `beta=1/(2R)`. Then `R>=8` and
`K/2 <= R^2 <= K`.

Use the full `N` active coordinates as the spectral space; do not project
the spectral matrix into a face tangent. The signed point is in the cube,
and feasible directions are separately restricted to the tangent `L_F`
of the current **original** face. The geometric premise is
`dim L_F >= N/k`. Frozen coordinates have zero displacement, while the
quadratic regularizer includes all `n` ambient coordinates.

Each retained nonzero row has coefficients `b`, entries `p_j=b_j^2`, mass
`q=sum p_j`, and, after cleanup,

`p_j <= q/K`, `sum_i p_ij <= 1`, and `sum_i q_i <= N`.

Large rows have `q>R^2` and give the diagonal background
`D_L=sum_large diag(p)`. Medium rows have a positive rational scale `r`
with `q <= r^2 < 4q`, `r<=R`. Their tracked displacement evolves locally
as `d(t)=d+t b^T h`; this is essential to the derivatives below. The
offset defining `d` may have changed at an earlier deletion/reset, but is
constant inside this local segment.

For each medium row and sign `sigma` define

`G=sum p_j(1-x_j^2)`,

`u=(sigma d-H)/r + R G/(2r^2) + 12R-e`,

`g=sigma b/r - R(p circ x)/r^2`,

`w=32K r^(-4) exp(beta u)`.

Assume both signed barriers are at most zero. The numerical allowance
`e` is fixed during a segment and belongs to `[0,1/4]` for cleanup
arguments. Put

`W=sum_(i,sigma) w p p^T`,

`M=W+D_L+eta 11^T`, `L=lambda_max(M)`,

`Phi=(L-8)_+^3 - eta ||x||^2`.

At a state where a direction is requested assume `L<=9` and `N>8k`.
During one Taylor segment all retained data, scales, signs, and active
coordinates stay fixed; only `x` and its affine tracked `d` move. The
segment is inside the cube, with `||h||<=1`, `|t|<=1/4`.

For exact protected-barrier preservation impose `g^T h=0` for every pair
with `u>=-1`. For large rows impose `b^T h=0`. Exact face, cost, and one
additional scalar equation are permitted. That additional equation will
be the rational approximate-gradient equation in the implementable
direction routine.

## 1. Perron gap on the full active coordinates

Every off-diagonal entry of `M` is at least `eta`. A Rayleigh maximizer
can be chosen nonnegative by replacing it by its coordinatewise absolute
value; strict positivity of off-diagonal entries then gives a strictly
positive top eigenvector `v`. Normalize `||v||=1`.

There is also an elementary quantitative gap. Let `z` be an eigenvector
for a different eigenvalue `mu`, orthogonal to `v`, and set `f_j=z_j/v_j`.
The maximum `a` of `f` is positive and its minimum `b` is negative. At
indices `i,j` attaining them, the eigenvalue equations give

`(L-mu) f_i = sum_l M_il (v_l/v_i)(f_i-f_l)`.

The equation at the maximum is a sum of nonnegative terms; the equation
at the minimum is a sum of nonpositive terms. Subtracting and retaining
the two cross terms yields

`(L-mu)(a-b) >= eta (v_j/v_i + v_i/v_j)(a-b) >= 2eta(a-b)`.

Hence `L-lambda_2 >= 2eta`, and the top eigenvalue is simple. This proof
requires neither a lower bound on the smallest coordinate of `v` nor a
bound on the diagonal. For `N=1`, simplicity is automatic and no reduced
resolvent is needed. The active regime here has `N>=9`.

The same proof applies to every rational approximate matrix with clipped
nonnegative Gram weights and the exact `eta 11^T` term. A Gram matrix
projected into a face tangent would not retain this entrywise premise.

## 2. Barrier derivatives and preservation

The spread/scale bounds imply

`||b/r||=sqrt(q)/r <= 1`,

`||R(p circ x)/r^2|| <= R sqrt((max p)q)/r^2 <= R/sqrt(K) <= 1`.

Thus `||g||<=2`. The barrier Hessian is
`-R diag(p)/r^2`, with norm at most `R/K <= 1/R`.
The exact segment identity is

`u(t)=u(0)+t g^T h - [R/(2r^2)] t^2 sum_j p_j h_j^2`.

A protected pair therefore never increases when its exact `g^T h=0`
equation is imposed. An unprotected pair starts with `u<-1` and has
increase at most `2|t|<=1/2`; it remains below `-1/2`. These statements
hold for both signs of `t`. They are exact algebra, not a discretized
or finite-difference check. Barrier preservation also implies `|d|<H`
because `12R-e>0` and `G>=0`.

## 3. Reserve bounds, without charging the background trace

The diagonal background has norm at most one, and
`||eta 11^T||=eta N<=1/K`; put `B=D_L+eta 11^T`. Thus
`||B||<=1+1/K<2`. Its trace may be large; the proof below uses its norm.

The number of large rows is less than `N/R^2<=2N/K`.
For a protected pair, `u>=-1`, `q>r^2/4`, and `R>=8` imply

`w q^2 > 2K exp(-1/(2R)) >= (15/8)K`.

Here `exp(-1/16)>=15/16` follows, for example, from
`exp(s)<=1/(1-s)` at `s=1/16`. Since
`sum w q^2 = 1^T W 1 <= L N <= 9N`, the number of protected
pairs is less than `(24/5)N/K < 5N/K`.

Spread gives `||p||^2 <= q^2/K`. Consequently

`tr W <= (1/K) 1^T W 1 <= L N/K`.

If `L>8`, let `E` be the eigenspace of `M` with eigenvalues at least
`L/2`. On `E`, `W=M-B` has Rayleigh quotient strictly greater than
`L/2-2 > L/4`. Positivity and the trace bound yield

`dim E < 4 tr W/L <= 4N/K`.

The norm estimate for the background is the necessary replacement for
charging its potentially large diagonal trace.

## 4. Restricted diagonal trace, including singular diagonals

Define the positive semidefinite `U_s` and diagonal `D_s` by

`U_s=sum w beta^2 (p^T v)^2 g g^T`,

`D_s=sum [w beta R/r^2] (p^T v)^2 diag(p)`.

Coordinatewise,

`beta^2 g_j^2 <= p_j/(2R^2 r^2) + p_j^2/(2r^4)`.

The corresponding `D_s` coefficient is `p_j/(2r^2)`. Since
`p_j/r^2<=1/K<=1/R^2`,

`(U_s)_jj <= (2/R^2)(D_s)_jj`.

For completeness, if `U>=0`, diagonal `D>=0`, and
`U_jj<=alpha D_jj`, then a zero diagonal entry of `D` gives
`U_jj=0`; positive semidefiniteness gives `U_jl=0` for every `l`.
Hence `ker D subset ker U`. On the positive coordinates define
`T=D^(-1/2) U D^(-1/2)`, and extend by zero on `ker D`.
Then `tr T <= alpha N`.

Remove the eigenspace of `T` with eigenvalues greater than `1/6`, by
imposing its orthogonality on `D^(1/2)h`. Its dimension is strictly less
than `6 alpha N`, and every remaining direction satisfies

`6 h^T U h <= h^T D h`.

Applying this to `(U_s,D_s)` uses fewer than
`12N/R^2 <= 24N/K` scalar equations. Singular `D_s` creates no extra
reserve charge or inverse-small-diagonal assumption. These irrational
equations are only used in the existence proof; the actual rational
routine below searches the approximate Hessian.

## 5. Top-eigenvalue second derivative and total reserve

For fixed `h`, write `a_i=beta g_i^T h` and
`b_i=-beta R sum p_ij h_j^2/r_i^2`. Direct differentiation gives
`w_i'=w_i a_i` and `w_i''=w_i(a_i^2+b_i)`, and hence

`v^T W'' v = h^T(U_s-D_s)h`.

Let `Q` have columns `sqrt(w_i) p_i`, and let its coefficient vector
have entries `sqrt(w_i) beta (g_i^T h)(p_i^T v)`. Its squared norm is
`h^T U_s h`, and `Q` applied to it is `W'[h]v`. Because
`||Q||^2=||W||<=L`,

`||W'[h]v||^2 <= L h^T U_s h`.

Impose `Proj_E W'[h]v=0`. It gives `L'[h]=0` because `v in E`.
The standard differentiated eigenvalue equation, solved on `v^perp`,
gives

`L''=v^T W''v + 2 sum_(ell!=top) |v_ell^T W'v|^2/(L-lambda_ell)`.

The terms in `E` vanish and all other denominators are strictly greater
than `L/2`. Therefore

`L'' <= h^T(5U_s-D_s)h <= -h^T D_s h/6 <= 0`.

The scalar constraints have total codimension strictly less than

`(2+5+4+24)N/K + 2 = 35N/K+2`.

The two unit charges are cost and one arbitrary additional scalar
equation. Intersecting with `L_F` leaves positive dimension because

`N/k -35N/(64k)-2 = 29N/(64k)-2 > 13/8 > 0`

when `N>8k`. Thus a nonzero exact direction exists with the required
barrier/face/cost/extra equations and

`h^T Hess(Phi) h <= -2eta ||h||^2`.

For `L<=8`, including equality, the scalar cubic has first and second
derivatives zero, so the Hessian is exactly `-2eta I`; the additional
spectral and diagonal-trace equations are unnecessary. The remaining
row/face/cost/extra count is smaller than the count above.

If `h^T D_s h=0`, every medium-row summand has positive `w`, positive
`p^T v`, and nonnegative `sum p_jh_j^2`. Therefore `h_j=0` on every
medium support. Both the tracked medium displacements and their `G`
values stay constant along this direction, so `W` and `M` remain exactly
constant. This proves the claimed flat-case conclusion rather than
assuming that a vanishing second derivative controls higher derivatives.

## 6. Cleanup transitions and initialization

Three monotonicity mechanisms should be distinguished.

**Entry deletion at fixed scale.** Deleting entries decreases `p`
coordinatewise and decreases `G` inside the cube; tracked `d` stays
fixed. Thus `u` and `w` decrease. Each entry of `w pp^T` decreases,
as does the large diagonal background. The matrices remain nonnegative,
so their Perron top eigenvalue cannot increase. This is entrywise
monotonicity; `pp^T-p' p'^T` is not generally positive semidefinite.

**Scale halving.** Suppose cleanup changes `r` to `r/2`, so
`q<=r^2/4`. Write `C=12R-e`. Then

`u_new = 2u_old + R G/r^2-C`,

`u_new-u_old <= -47R/4+e`.

The weight ratio is at most
`16 exp(-47/8+e/(2R))<1`, since `e<=1/4`, `R>=8`. Repeated
halvings preserve this property. The same retained vector `p` then gives
a genuine Loewner decrease of its Gram contribution. This calculation
assumes the scales are decreased by dyadic halvings; a differently
specified arbitrary scale update needs its own check.

**Large-to-medium.** At transition `d=0`, choose `r<=R` with
`q<=r^2`. Then

`beta u <= -16R/r+25/4`,

`sum_sigma w pp^T <= [64K q/r^4 exp(-16R/r+25/4)] diag(p)`

`<= [64(K/R^2) exp(-39/4)] diag(p) < diag(p)`.

The last maximum is at `r=R`: `a^2 exp(-16a)` decreases for
`a=R/r>=1`. Also `K/R^2<=2` and `128 exp(-39/4)<1`.
This is a genuine Loewner domination of the removed large diagonal.
It works at initialization for each medium pair because initially
`d=0`. Summing gives `W+D_L<=I`, so initially
`L<=1+1/K<2`.

Freezing a coordinate at `x_j=+/-1` removes a principal row/column of
the spectral matrix. Its `G` contribution was already zero. Subsequent
cleanup can only lower the top eigenvalue by the arguments above.
Because the quadratic regularizer retains all ambient coordinates,
freezing does not create a positive potential jump by discarding the
frozen coordinate's squared value. Increasing `e` only decreases weights.

## 7. Uniform Taylor bound across threshold 8

At a cleaned state with `L<=9`, the exact quadratic barrier identity
and `||g||<=2` give

`w(t)/w(0) <= exp(beta/2) <= exp(1/(4R))<2`.

Thus `W(t)<=2W(0)` in Loewner order and `L(t)<=18` on the segment.
At every cube point of that segment, for a unit or shorter `h`,

`|a_i(t)|<=1/R`, `|b_i|<=1/(2R^2)`.

Consequently

`||M'||<=18/R<3`,

`||M''||<=27/R^2<1`,

`||M'''||<=45/R^3<1`.

These follow from `w'''=w(a^3+3ab)` and signed Loewner bounds by
the positive matrix `W(t)`. The simple-eigenvalue derivative equations,
with reduced resolvent norm at most `1/eta`, give

`|L'|<=3`,

`|L''|<=1+18/eta`,

`|L'''|<=1+18/eta+324/eta^2`.

For the last inequality differentiate the reduced-resolvent equation,
or use

`L'''=v^T M'''v +6v^T M'' T M'v`

`       +6v^T M'T(M'-L'I)TM'v`,

where `T` is the reduced resolvent. The estimate is
`c+6ab/eta+12a^3/eta^2` for derivative norms `a,b,c`.

On `0<=L<=18`, the scalar cubic has bounds `|f'|<=300`,
`|f''|<=60`, and `|f'''|<=6` wherever its third derivative exists.
The chain rule gives the almost-everywhere bound

`|Phi'''| <= 6*27 + 3*60*3*(1+18/eta)`

`                 +300*(1+18/eta+324/eta^2)`

`             <= 113322/eta^2 < 2^17/eta^2`.

The scalar cubic is `C^2` with Lipschitz second derivative, so the
composition is `C^{2,1}` across `L=8`. Its quadratic regularizer has
third derivative zero. Integrating the Lipschitz second-derivative
bound proves

`|Phi(t)-Phi(0)-t Phi'(0)-t^2 Phi''(0)/2| <= B3 |t|^3/6`

with the reported `B3=2^40 K^2 n^2=2^40/eta^2`. This proof is uniform
over the stated segments and does not use finite differences or
artificial spectral fixtures. It also establishes the smaller allowance
`2^17 K^2 n^2` under the same premises; changing the algorithm's
parameters to exploit that improvement is outside this audit.

## 8. Certified rational gradient and Hessian

This is a local implementation statement polynomial in the binary
encoding of the current rational state and requested precision. It
does not by itself prove that current-state encodings stay bounded by
original input size over the trajectory.

Normalize `z_i=p_i/r_i^2`. Then `sum z_i<=1`,
`max z_ij<=1/K`, and `||z_i||^2<=1/K`. The Gram matrix is

`W=32K sum_(i,sigma) exp(beta u_i) z_i z_i^T`.

This representation removes inverse-small-scale norm growth. The
directional derivative coefficients are bounded independently of the
small scale: `|beta g^T h|<=1/R`, and the second-derivative coefficient
has absolute value at most `3/(2R^2)` for `||h||=1`.

Use the handoff parameters

`epsilon=min(1,epsilon_g,epsilon_H)`,

`zeta=epsilon eta^3 / [2^40(n+1)^6(K+1)^4]`,

`rho=zeta/[4K^2(m+1)]`.

Choose integer `J>=ceil(log_2(1/rho))`, using its smallest allowed
value (or at most one additional integer). If a rational exponent
`s=beta u<-J`, replace its exponential by zero; the error is at most
`exp(-J)<=2^(-J)<=rho`. Otherwise approximate `exp(s)` on `[-J,0]`
by its Taylor polynomial at zero of degree `32(J+1)` and clip the
result below at zero. The Lagrange remainder is at most
`J^(d+1)/(d+1)! <= (eJ/(d+1))^(d+1) < rho`.
Clipping preserves this absolute error. The resulting Gram weights
are nonnegative rational numbers.

Keep the large diagonal and `eta 11^T` exact. Write the resulting
matrix as `M_hat` and use the same approximate exponential weights
in the exact derivative formulas; these are approximations of the
true derivatives, rather than derivatives of the clipping rule.
For matrix and first/second directional derivative errors,

`||M_hat-M||, ||M_hat'[h]-M'[h]||,`

`||M_hat''[h,h]-M''[h,h]|| <= delta=64m rho <= zeta/256`.

The last inequality uses `K>=64`. The first/second derivative bounds
are no larger than the matrix bound because `R>=8`. Approximate `M`
still has Perron gap at least `2eta` and top eigenvalue below 10.

Clear a common denominator to obtain a nonnegative integer matrix,
and apply it repeatedly to the all-ones integer vector, with

`T_power=ceil[64 ceil(log_2(16n/zeta))/eta]`.

If `v_hat` is its unit top eigenvector, the initial overlap is at least
one and the initial norm at most `sqrt(n)`. The ratio of other to top
components after `T_power` powers is at most
`sqrt(n) exp(-eta T_power/5) <= zeta`.
No square root is needed in the implementation: for the integer power
vector `q`, use the rational projector `S=qq^T/(q^Tq)` and Rayleigh
quotient `ell=q^T M_hat q/(q^Tq)`.

The elementary projected eigenvalue equation for the perturbed
eigenvector gives `||P_hat-P||<=delta/eta`. Combining it with the
power error and Rayleigh bound gives

`||S-P||<=3zeta/eta`, `|ell-L|<=2zeta`,

where `P=vv^T`. Define the rational reduced-resolvent approximation

`T_rat=(ell I-M_hat+S)^(-1)-S`.

For `A=L I-M+P`, `||A^(-1)||<=1/eta`. The approximate `A` error is
at most `6zeta/eta`, which is below `eta/2` by the chosen precision.
The resolvent identity then yields

`||T_rat-T|| <= 15zeta/eta^3 <= 19zeta/eta^3`.

In particular the rational inverse exists. The handoff's claimed
projector and reduced-resolvent constants are safe.

For a unit direction put

`a(h)=tr(P M'[h])`,

`b(h)=tr(P M''[h,h]) +2tr(P M'[h] T M'[h])`.

Then the spectral gradient/Hessian formulas are

`grad Phi[h]=f'(L)a(h)-2eta x^T h`,

`Hess Phi[h,h]=f''(L)a(h)^2+f'(L)b(h)-2eta||h||^2`.

Replace `L,P,T,M',M''` by `ell,S,T_rat,M_hat',M_hat''` in these
formulas. At the local state `||M'[h]||<=2`, `||M''[h,h]||<=1`,
while their approximate versions are bounded by 3 and 2. Rank-two
projector differences satisfy `||S-P||_*=2||S-P||`. Straight
telescoping of the formulas gives the conservative bounds

`|a_hat(h)-a(h)| <= 13zeta/eta`,

`|b_hat(h)-b(h)| <= 512zeta/eta^3`.

On `[0,10]`, `f'<=12`, `f''<=12`, `f'` is 12-Lipschitz and
`f''` is 6-Lipschitz. Thus

`||grad_hat-grad|| <= 2^8 zeta/eta <= epsilon_g`,

`||H_hat-Hess Phi|| <= 2^13 zeta/eta^3 <= epsilon_H`.

The displayed operator bounds follow from all-unit-direction bounds
and polarization of the symmetric Hessian. The precision formula is
far smaller than needed for these error estimates. All integer powers,
Taylor coefficients, matrix inverses, and derivative traces have
polynomial binary length in the local input and precision: the Taylor
degree and power count are polynomial; denominator clearing and
Hadamard/Cramer bounds control rational matrix operations. This is
where persistent-state bit bounds from the other audit must be used
to obtain an original-input complexity theorem.

## 9. Physical-metric shift and rational negative direction

Set `epsilon_H=eta/4`. Form the exact rational kernel `V` of the
face, cost, large-row, protected-pair, and approximate-gradient
equations, and let the columns of rational `Z` be any basis of it.
Section 5, using the approximate-gradient vector as the arbitrary
extra equation, supplies a nonzero `h=Za` with

`h^T Hess(Phi)h <= -2eta ||h||^2`.

Therefore `Z^T(H_hat+eta I)Z` has a negative direction, regardless
of conditioning or arbitrary rescaling of `Z`. Exact symmetric
congruence elimination finds a rational `a` with its quadratic form
negative. This is constructive: a negative diagonal is immediate;
otherwise eliminate a positive diagonal by a Schur complement;
if a zero diagonal has nonzero off-diagonal, choose a rational
two-coordinate vector making the form negative. If all such entries
vanish and the remaining block is positive semidefinite, no negative
eigenvalue could have remained. Exact fractions control signs and
polynomial bit length.

The resulting physical vector `h0=Za` satisfies

`h0^T H_hat h0 < -eta ||h0||^2`,

`h0^T Hess(Phi) h0 < -(3eta/4)||h0||^2`.

Scale by a rational power of two, determined by exact comparisons of
`||h0||^2`, to obtain `1/2<=||h||<=1`. Then

`h^T Hess(Phi)h < -3eta/16`,

`|grad(Phi)^T h| <= epsilon_g`

because `grad_hat^T h=0` exactly. This proves the requested local
direction inputs used by the trajectory audit.

The wording that the shift avoids a weak pivot needs a precise reading:
it guarantees uniform negative curvature **after physical normalization**.
It does not guarantee a large numerical pivot in an arbitrarily scaled
basis. For example, in one physical dimension, `H_hat=-2eta` and
`Z=2^(-B)` give shifted coefficient pivot `-eta 2^(-2B)`. This
artificial basis diagnostic is not a reachable rounding-state
counterexample and does not invalidate the method; exact bit arithmetic
and physical normalization remove the irrelevant basis scaling.

## Decisive downstream use and next falsifiable obligation

These lemmas supply the negative direction, gradient tolerance, and
Taylor remainder needed for the favorable-cost trajectory proof. The
next discriminating task is to compose them with exact original-face
endpoint/reset primitives and verify the stated invariant over the
whole trajectory, including arbitrarily short non-coordinate face hits
and non-dyadic cost anchors. Passing local algebra or finite artificial
states cannot settle that obligation. A complete implementation can
reuse the rational formulas in sections 8–9; it still needs exact
oracle and reset machinery, persistent-bit accounting, and final
discrepancy telescoping.
