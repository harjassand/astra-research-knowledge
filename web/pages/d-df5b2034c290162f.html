## 1. Exact Hamiltonian and locality hypotheses

Let C_a be the original commuting Pauli generators of the actual
reachable X–Z realization. Let S be their stabilizer group, and write
the reference Hamiltonian in excitation-energy units:

    H0=sum_a h_a (I-C_a)/2,  0<h_min<=h_a<=h_max<infinity.

All constants below are fixed in the recursive depth i. Suppose

    H=H0+V,  V=sum_X V_X,
    V_X=V_X*,  diam(supp V_X)<=R,
    [V,C_a]=0 for every a,
    J=sup_q sum_{X:q in supp V_X} ||V_X|| < infinity.        (1)

Scalar terms can be discarded. The commutation premise in (1) is
with **each check**, and is stronger than [V,H0]=0. Commutation only
with the total H0 permits mixing degenerate syndrome energies and
does not supply the scalar syndrome energy used below.

The actual-family geometric hypotheses used are:

* a physical ball of each fixed radius has uniformly bounded qubit
  count;
* the distance d_i tends to infinity;
* every stabilizer supported in a physical ball B(q,r), r>=1, is a
  product of original checks whose whole qubit supports lie in
  B(q,C r), with C independent of i.

The mixed-Pauli version of the last statement follows by splitting
into the two CSS sectors and using the same whole-check ball. The
cycle13 proof supplies these premises for the legal inset X–Z
template and admitted fixed constants; no ideal continuum mesh is
substituted here.

Put q_R=sup_q |B(q,max(R,1)) intersect qubits|. Work at depths with
d_i>q_R. Choose one actual-support qubit q_X for each nonzero term,
and put

    R_f=C max(R,1),
    G_X={a: supp C_a is contained in B(q_X,R_f)},
    B=sup_q |B(q,R_f) intersect qubits|.                    (2)

The constants B, q_R, C are charged even if extremely large. This is
not a practical lower bound on an admissible perturbation strength.

## 2. Local algebra membership without a coefficient-norm loss

Twirl each V_X over S. Conjugation by a tensor-product Pauli preserves
the physical support and the operator norm is nonincreasing under
the average. Because V commutes with every C_a, the twirled terms
still sum to V. Their intensive bound remains <=J. We may therefore
assume [V_X,C_a]=0 for every a.

Expand a twirled V_X in the Pauli basis on its support. Each nonzero
component P centralizes S. Its weight is <=q_R<d_i, so P is, up to
phase, in S rather than a logical coset. Local filling represents P
using checks from G_X. Consequently

    V_X belongs to Alg{C_a:a in G_X}.                       (3)

This is a common local algebra for **all** Pauli components. The
argument is not a bound on the sum of their expansion coefficients.
In particular no 4^(q_R) coefficient factor is introduced.

For every allowed syndrome sigma, V_X acts as a scalar
v_X(sigma|G_X) on the entire associated logical multiplicity space,
and |v_X|<=||V_X||. Thus H is logical-independent on the full
Hilbert space. It has an exact full-spectrum logical matrix factor,
including all Gibbs support, and not merely a stable ground band.

For two allowed syndromes sigma,sigma', put
D={a:sigma_a != sigma'_a}. Equation (3) gives

    |V(sigma')-V(sigma)|
      <=2 sum_{a in D} sum_{X:a in G_X} ||V_X||.             (4)

Fix one incident qubit t_a of each nonempty original check. If
a in G_X then q_X is in B(t_a,R_f). Grouping terms by q_X,

    sum_{X:a in G_X} ||V_X||
      <=sum_{q in B(t_a,R_f)} sum_{X:q_X=q} ||V_X||
      <=B J.                                               (5)

Every anchor q_X is in its term's actual support, which is why (1)
pays this bound. Hence the uniform physical congestion inequality is

    |V(sigma')-V(sigma)| <=2BJ |D|.                          (6)

It holds for any two allowed syndromes, with arbitrary cross-sector
check interactions. A representation chosen separately for each
Pauli component cannot increase the common-ball congestion used in
(5). Extensive ||V|| is irrelevant to this estimate.

## 3. The actual source eraser removes only checks

The primary source, paper.tex3635–3664 and3745–3759, uses a witness
foot tau=tau_{2k} satisfying

    tau subset sigma,  tau in im(delta^1).

The sector syndrome sigma also belongs to im(delta^1). For a fixed
tau, the map

    T_tau(sigma_X,sigma_Z)=(sigma_X+tau,sigma_Z)              (7)

is injective on syndromes containing tau, and its output is a valid
joint syndrome. Addition is over F2. Since tau subset sigma_X, (7)
removes exactly |tau| excited checks and creates none. The opposite
sector is fixed. CSS validity separates into the two syndrome images;
cross-sector energies need not separate.

The source proof of the foot's coboundary property needs component
locality only at ordinary levels i>=3. At the bottom it uses
tau_2=sigma_2 in im(delta_2^1); lift a preimage of sigma_2 and add the
ordinary correction chain. It does not require the arbitrary
finite level-2 lookup to remain locally computable.

By (6), with

    kappa=h_min-2BJ>0,                                      (8)

the energy removed by (7) obeys

    E(sigma)-E(T_tau sigma)>=kappa |tau|.                    (9)

The multiplicity of allowed syndromes is constant for a stabilizer
code and H is logical-independent, so the Gibbs syndrome measure is
proportional to exp(-beta E(sigma)). For this fixed tau,

    Pr_beta(tau subset sigma_X)
      <=exp(-beta kappa |tau|)
        [sum_{sigma:tau subset sigma_X}
         exp(-beta E(T_tau sigma))]/Z
      <=exp(-beta kappa |tau|).                             (10)

No estimate of Gibbs partition-function ratios is needed. The
injection and its preimage multiplicity are exactly the same as in
the reference proof. The source's witness entropy is unchanged.
The same argument applies to erasing the other sector.

This is the decisive positive interface: sign-indefinite diagonal
interactions can be extensive, but sufficiently small intensive
strength pays an energy drop on each erased witness check.

## 4. Corrected low-level witness ledger

The source's literal domination statement overlooks nodes marked at
G0,G1 although sigma_0=sigma_1=0. It also overlooks the arbitrary
finite lookup at G2, for which ordinary local computability is not
promised. Denote

    C_low=sum_{j=0}^2 |V(G_j)|,
    nu=|V(G2)|,  D=Delta_level,
    K_dom=2(1+D^2).                                         (11)

All are fixed in k. At each ordinary level i>=3, a marked node is
within undirected level-graph distance <=2 of a syndrome-bearing
node in the same witness component: correction input is at distance
<=1 and correction-deposited syndrome is at distance <=1 from the
correction. A maximum-degree-D distance-two ball has at most
1+D+D(D-1)=1+D^2 nodes. Therefore the number of syndrome-bearing
regions in a witness with m nodes is at least

    (m-C_low)/(1+D^2) >= m/K_dom
       whenever m>=2C_low.                                 (12)

Only i>=3 is used. Choosing the more conservative denominator
2(1+D^4) would also be valid; no dependence on k is lost.

The actual source's ordinary cluster reduction and connected
witness lemma imply m>=Q_k=2^(k-1) and
sum_i |tau_i|<=4|tau_top|. Retain the fixed root inventory nu in
the graph count:

    #{witness graphs of size m}<=nu(Delta+1)^(2m).

This section independently repairs domination and the subsequent
entropy arithmetic; the ordinary component reduction and the
single-error stability lemma remain explicit primary decoder inputs.
Require Q_k>=2C_low. The low-level lookup's local support is not
inferred from its being a lookup table.

Let M_ell bound the number of syndrome positions in any local
region. The source region assignments partition check labels;
allowing every nonempty subset of a local region overcounts the
allowed syndromes and costs

    W=(1+exp(-beta kappa/4))^(M_ell)-1.

A fixed witness graph's compatible weighted witnesses cost at most
2^m W^(m/K_dom) when W<=1, using (12) and the summed syndrome bound.
Thus for one sector

    p_bad,1<=nu sum_{m>=Q_k}
                  [2(Delta+1)^2 W^(1/K_dom)]^m.             (13)

Put

    qcrit=[1/(4(Delta+1)^2)]^(K_dom),
    beta_* = 4 log[M_ell/log(1+qcrit)].                      (14)

Then beta kappa>=beta_* ensures W<=qcrit and the bracket in (13)
is <=1/2. The elementary inequality used is (1+x)^M<=exp(Mx);
the source's displayed log(1+x)<=x log2 is reversed and is not used.
For both sectors, take worst constants if they differ:

    p_bad,1<=C_ell 2^(-Q_k),
    C_ell=2(nu_X+nu_Z),  Q_k>=2C_low.                       (15)

This keeps the source's omitted fixed prefactor and the finite
depth threshold. It has the same stretched-exponential exponent in
system size as the primary construction at fixed ell.

## 5. Arbitrary bounded local bath couplings

Let S_alpha be Hermitian physical bath operators in a fixed local
support family of maximum weight q. They are not required to be
Paulis or to commute with checks. The Davies generator is taken at
the actual H in (1), with nonnegative KMS rates gamma_alpha(omega).
Assume independent channels and

    g_alpha=sup_omega gamma_alpha(omega)<infinity,
    r_bath=sup_j sum_{alpha:j in supp S_alpha}
                         g_alpha ||S_alpha||^2<infinity.    (16)

A correlated-channel extension requires a uniform positive
rate-matrix bound, and is not asserted from entrywise bounds.
All rates in (16) are actual perturbed-H rates; no continuity in H
is assumed. Scalar couplings can be discarded.

Let G1 be the source's single-Pauli good set in both sectors. For
every admitted support choose Pauli factorizations, and erode G1 by
all pre-factor prefix shifts of length <=q-1. If M_prefix denotes
the number of admitted distinct prefix shifts including identity,
the resulting Gq protects every Pauli on these supports by induction.
It protects non-Pauli S_alpha by linearity of the Pauli expansion.

A prefix of length j toggles at most z_v j check bits, z_v<=7 in
the actual family. Equation (6) and the reference penalties give

    |E(sigma+s_Q)-E(sigma)|
       <= (h_max+2BJ) z_v j.

Validity is preserved because s_Q is a Pauli syndrome. Change of
variables and a union bound therefore give

    p_bad,q<=M_prefix
             exp[beta(h_max+2BJ)z_v(q-1)]
             C_ell 2^(-Q_k).                               (17)

For fixed physical range, covering all supports by N anchor balls
with at most v_bath qubits gives M_prefix<=N 4^(v_bath).
The sharper distinct-prefix inventory can replace this bound.
The single-Pauli good set does not itself protect arbitrary products;
erosion is an essential retained cost.

Decoder-dressed logicals commute with H on its full spectrum:
the bare logicals commute with the check algebra, and the syndrome
projectors and their Pauli decoder dressings preserve this logical
algebra. P_Gq commutes with H and both dressed logicals. Bare bath
protection passes to their spectral components because of these
commutators. The Chesi theorem, independently proved in the baseline,
and the actual Davies norm bound give

    ||L||_1<=2N r_bath,
    error(t)<=16N r_bath t p_bad,q.                         (18)

Here error is the trace distance for the supplied thermal encoding,
followed optionally by the decoder. For any fixed delta>0, a
guaranteed interval is

    t<= delta 2^(Q_k) /
        [16N r_bath M_prefix C_ell
         exp(beta(h_max+2BJ)z_v(q-1))].                      (19)

Temperature beta, perturbation range R, intensive strength
J<h_min/(2B), bath range, rate intensity and all admitted geometry
constants are fixed before k tends to infinity. Also require
d_{2k}>q_R and Q_k>=2C_low. These are finite initial-depth
restrictions. Fixed-depth isospectral dressing U(H0+V)U* may be
added by pulling the original physical bath back through U and
paying its fixed light cone in (17); full Gibbs conjugation is exact.
