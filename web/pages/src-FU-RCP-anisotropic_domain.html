# Scalar regularization with an arbitrary convex moment domain

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: internally proved theorem candidate, subject to independent mathematical review. The full anisotropic extension holds in finite-dimensional physical space. The moment domain may be unbounded, asymmetric, have corners, or contain lines. No boundary regularity is required.

The main additional construction is a convex potential with arbitrarily small local derivatives as a function of its gradient, while its gradient range and its exponential-moment domain are both a prescribed open convex set. A large-Hessian dual barrier controls the second derivative. A fixed compact mollification of the primal potential controls every higher derivative needed by the distribution. A uniform bound on the second derivative makes that mollification a bounded perturbation, so it preserves the exact moment domain.

## 1. Statement

Let \(E\) be a real Banach space and \(C\subsetneq E\) a closed convex cone with nonempty interior. Fix \(e\in\operatorname{int}C\). Let \(T\) be a compactly supported \(E\)-valued distribution on \(\mathbb R^m\), with \(T(1)=e\), and write
\[
H_T(g)=T_y(e^{g\cdot y}).
\]
Let \(D\subset\mathbb R^m\) be any open convex set containing zero. For a scalar probability measure \(\nu\), define its full finite-moment domain by
\[
\mathcal D(\nu)=\{g\in\mathbb R^m:\int e^{g\cdot z}\,\nu(dz)<\infty\}.
\]
In particular this definition includes any boundary points where a moment might be finite.

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent.

1. \(H_T(g)\in\operatorname{int}C\) for every \(g\in D\).
2. There is a scalar probability measure \(\nu\), with \(D\subseteq\mathcal D(\nu)\), for which \(T*\nu\) is a \(C\)-positive distribution.
3. For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), there is an everywhere positive probability density \(p=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\), where \(V\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^m)\) has positive-definite Hessian everywhere, such that
   \[
   \mathcal D(p)=D,
   \qquad \nabla V:\mathbb R^m\longrightarrow D
   \text{ is a smooth diffeomorphism},
   \tag{1}
   \]
   and, with \(M=T*p\),
   \[
   (1-\varepsilon)p(x)H_T(\nabla V(x))
   \preceq_C M(x)
   \preceq_C(1+\varepsilon)p(x)H_T(\nabla V(x))
   \quad(x\in\mathbb R^m).
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Thus \(M(x)\in\operatorname{int}C\) everywhere and
   \(\int M(x)\,dx=e\) in Banach norm. For every multi-index \(\alpha\) and every \(g\in D\),
   \[
   \int e^{g\cdot x}|\partial^\alpha p(x)|\,dx<\infty,
   \qquad
   \int e^{g\cdot x}\|\partial^\alpha M(x)\|\,dx<\infty.
   \tag{3}
   \]

Consequently, the exact moment domains attainable by smooth strictly log-concave scalar regularizers of \(T\) are precisely the open convex neighborhoods of zero contained in
\[
\{g:H_T(g)\in\operatorname{int}C\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, Hölder's inequality makes every finite-moment domain convex, and the convex-sublevel argument in Section 7 applied at each finite tilt makes the moment domain of an everywhere positive log-concave density open.
The condition is local to \(D\); no positivity assumption is made outside \(D\). For bounded self-adjoint operators, interior positivity means a positive lower spectral bound at each \(g\), rather than merely a positive quadratic form on every nonzero vector.

## 2. The local error estimate

Choose \(R>0\), \(N\ge0\), and \(C_T<\infty\) such that
\[
\|T(h)\|\le C_T\max_{|\alpha|\le N}
\sup_{|y|\le R}|\partial^\alpha h(y)|.
\tag{5}
\]
Cutoff factors for the compact support are absorbed into \(C_T\). Put
\[
K=\max(2,N),\qquad B=m(1+R)^2.
\]
Suppose that, for some \(a>0\),
\[
\max_{2\le|\alpha|\le K}\sup_{|z-x|\le R}
|\partial^\alpha V(z)|\le a,
\qquad Ba\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
Set \(g=\nabla V(x)\) and
\[
F_x(y)=V(x-y)-V(x)+g\cdot y.
\]
Taylor's formula for order zero, the fundamental theorem of calculus for order one, and direct differentiation for orders at least two give
\[
\|F_x\|_{C^N(B_R)}\le Ba.
\]
Let \(b_*\) be the maximum of the Bell numbers of orders \(0,\ldots,N\). The partition form of the chain rule gives
\[
\|e^{-F_x}-1\|_{C^N(B_R)}
\le \exp(1)b_*Ba.
\tag{7}
\]
This includes the undifferentiated term, using \(|e^{-t}-1|\le\exp(1)|t|\) when \(|t|\le1\).

The identity
\[
p(x-y)=p(x)e^{g\cdot y}e^{-F_x(y)}
\]
is exact. Hence
\[
\frac{(T*p)(x)}{p(x)}=H_T(g)+E_x,
\qquad
\|E_x\|\le A e^{R|g|}(1+|g|)^N a,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(A=C_T\exp(1)b_*B\). This follows from (5), (7), and Leibniz's rule. The estimate requires no geometric property of \(D\).

The rest of sufficiency is supplied by the following convex-geometric lemma.

## 3. A potential with prescribed gradient range and local derivative bounds

**Lemma.** Let \(D\) be open and convex with \(0\in D\). Given a continuous positive function \(\tau:D\to(0,\infty)\), an integer \(K\ge2\), and \(R>0\), there is a smooth function \(V\) such that:

- \(D^2V(x)\) is positive definite everywhere, and \(\nabla V\) is a smooth diffeomorphism from \(\mathbb R^m\) onto \(D\).
- For every \(x\),
  \[
  \max_{2\le|\alpha|\le K}\sup_{|z-x|\le R}
  |\partial^\alpha V(z)|\le\tau(\nabla V(x)).
  \tag{9}
  \]
- Every derivative of \(V\) of order at least two is globally bounded.
- \(\int e^{g\cdot x-V(x)}\,dx<\infty\) if and only if \(g\in D\).

The proof occupies Sections 4–7. The pointwise Hessian bound is not used as a substitute for bounds on higher derivatives: those are obtained by an explicit mollification argument.

## 4. A smooth strongly convex exhaustion of every open convex domain

Put \(P=(\overline D)^\circ\), the polar with the convention
\[
P=\{u:u\cdot g\le1\text{ for every }g\in D\}.
\]
Because \(D\) contains a ball about zero, \(P\) is compact. Let \(d=\dim\operatorname{span}P\). When \(d>0\), let \(\lambda_P\) be normalized \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure on \(P\), in its linear span. When \(d=0\), \(P=\{0\}\) and use \(\lambda_P=\delta_0\).

Elementary polarity and separation give
\[
D=\{g:\max_{u\in P}u\cdot g<1\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Define on \(D\)
\[
\Phi(g)=1+|g|^2+
\int_P(1-u\cdot g)^{-(d+1)}\,\lambda_P(du).
\tag{11}
\]
The denominators have a positive lower bound on each compact subset of \(D\), so \(\Phi\) is smooth. Each integrand is convex, and consequently
\[
D^2\Phi(g)\succeq2I.
\tag{12}
\]

The sublevel sets of \(\Phi\) are compact subsets of \(D\). Boundedness follows from \(\Phi\ge1+|g|^2\). Here is a direct proof of divergence at a finite boundary point, including corners. Let \(g_n\in D\) tend to \(b\in\partial D\). By (10), some \(u_0\in P\) satisfies \(u_0\cdot b=1\). Set \(s_n=1-u_0\cdot g_n\downarrow0\), passing to a subsequence if needed; only convergence to zero is required below. Choose a relative ball \(Q\subset P\) with positive \(d\)-volume. For large \(n\),
\[
P_n=(1-s_n)u_0+s_n Q\subset P.
\]
Its relative volume is a fixed positive multiple of \(s_n^d\). On \(P_n\), boundedness of \(g_n\) and \(Q\) gives
\[
1-u\cdot g_n\le C s_n.
\]
The integral in (11) is therefore at least a fixed positive multiple of \(s_n^{-1}\), which tends to infinity. If \(d=0\), then \(D=\mathbb R^m\), and there is no finite boundary to check. This also handles domains containing lines without any full-dimensional-polar assumption.

The existence of convex barriers and Legendre functions on arbitrary open convex sets is classical; the polar integral above is proved explicitly to supply the exact hypotheses used here. Borwein and Vanderwerff, *Convex Functions of Legendre Type in General Banach Spaces*, J. Convex Anal. 8 (2001), 569–581, especially Section 4, give related universal-barrier constructions. [Primary paper](https://journalofconvexanalysis.com/articles/jca08030/jca08030.pdf?v=2).

## 5. An arbitrarily small Hessian in gradient coordinates

Extend \(\min(1,\tau)\) to all of \(\mathbb R^m\) by setting it equal to zero outside \(D\); call this extension \(\widetilde\tau\). Set
\[
w(g)=\inf_{h\in\mathbb R^m}
\bigl\{\widetilde\tau(h)+|g-h|\bigr\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(w\) is globally 1-Lipschitz, \(0\le w\le1\), and
\[
0<w(g)\le\tau(g)\quad(g\in D),
\qquad w(g)=0\quad(g\notin D).
\tag{14}
\]
For positivity at a fixed \(g\in D\), choose a small ball on which \(\tau\) is bounded below by a positive constant. Points in that ball contribute a positive first term in (13); points outside it contribute a positive distance. This proves the strictly positive lower bound. Thus no smoothness of \(w\) is needed.

Fix a nonnegative even \(\rho\in C_c^\infty(B_1)\) with integral one. Put
\[
A_K=\max_{|\gamma|\le K-2}\|\partial^\gamma\rho\|_{L^1}\ge1,
\qquad
\eta=\min\left\{1,\frac{\log(3/2)}{R+1},\frac1{3A_K}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
We construct a smooth strongly convex function \(U:D\to\mathbb R\) with
\[
D^2U(g)\succeq\frac1{\eta w(g)}I,
\qquad U(g)\ge\Phi(g).
\tag{16}
\]
For an explicit scalar construction, choose one smooth nondecreasing step \(\theta\), zero on \(( -\infty,0]\) and one on \([1,\infty)\). Define
\[
a_n=\max\left\{1,\sup_{\Phi(g)\le n+3}\frac1{2\eta w(g)}\right\},
\qquad n\ge0.
\]
Each \(a_n\) is finite, by compactness of the sublevel set, and they are nondecreasing. The sublevels here are nonempty because \(\Phi(0)=2\). Let
\[
a(t)=a_0+\sum_{n\ge0}(a_{n+1}-a_n)\theta(t-n),
\qquad F(t)=\int_0^t a(s)\,ds,
\qquad U=F\circ\Phi.
\tag{17}
\]
The sum is locally finite. The function \(a\) is smooth and nondecreasing, with \(a\ge1\). If \(\Phi(g)\in[n,n+1]\), then \(a(\Phi(g))\ge a_n\ge(2\eta w(g))^{-1}\). Equations (12) and (17) prove (16).

For each \(x\in\mathbb R^m\), the function \(g\mapsto U(g)-x\cdot g\) has compact sublevels in \(D\): the quadratic term controls infinity and the barrier controls its finite boundary. Its Hessian is positive definite, so it has a unique minimizer \(g(x)\), characterized by
\[
\nabla U(g(x))=x.
\]
The inverse function theorem shows that \(g\) is smooth. Define
\[
V_0(x)=x\cdot g(x)-U(g(x)).
\tag{18}
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
\nabla V_0(x)=g(x),\qquad
0\prec D^2V_0(x)=[D^2U(g(x))]^{-1}
\preceq\eta w(g(x))I\preceq\eta I.
\tag{19}
\]
In particular \(\nabla V_0\) is a diffeomorphism onto \(D\). No bound on the higher derivatives of \(U\) or \(V_0\) is asserted or required at this stage.

## 6. Mollification controls all required local derivatives

Set
\[
V=V_0*\rho.
\tag{20}
\]
Its Hessian is an average of positive-definite Hessians, and is positive definite. The uniform upper bound in (19), evenness of \(\rho\), and Taylor's formula yield
\[
0\le V(x)-V_0(x)\le\eta/2
\quad\text{for every }x.
\tag{21}
\]

The following transport estimate is the reason a small Hessian at one gradient value remains small throughout the required physical neighborhood. Write \(g_0=\nabla V_0(x)\). Along the segment \(x+tv\), use (19) and the 1-Lipschitz property of \(w\). The resulting absolutely continuous scalar function satisfies the usual differential inequality, so Gronwall's elementary integral estimate gives
\[
w(\nabla V_0(x+v))\le e^{\eta|v|}w(g_0),
\tag{22}
\]
and integration of the gradient derivative gives
\[
|\nabla V_0(x+v)-g_0|
\le(e^{\eta|v|}-1)w(g_0).
\tag{23}
\]
These formulas also follow directly by integrating
\(|\frac{d}{dt}\nabla V_0(x+tv)|\le\eta|v|w(\nabla V_0(x+tv))\).

Let \(h=\nabla V(x)\). Averaging (23) over the support of \(\rho\), and using (15), gives
\[
|h-g_0|\le\tfrac12 w(g_0),
\qquad w(h)\ge\tfrac12 w(g_0)>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus \(h\in D\) by (14), even when \(g_0\) is arbitrarily close to a nonsmooth boundary.

For \(|y-x|\le R\) and \(|z|\le1\), (15), (19), and (22) give
\[
\|D^2V_0(y-z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\tfrac32\eta w(g_0)\le3\eta w(h).
\tag{25}
\]
For each multi-index \(\alpha\) of order at least two, choose a multi-index \(\beta\le\alpha\) of order two. Integration by parts against the compactly supported mollifier gives
\[
\partial^\alpha V
=(\partial^\beta V_0)*(\partial^{\alpha-\beta}\rho).
\]
It follows from (25) and (15) that, for \(2\le|\alpha|\le K\),
\[
\sup_{|y-x|\le R}|\partial^\alpha V(y)|
\le3\eta A_K w(h)\le w(h)\le\tau(h).
\tag{26}
\]
This proves (9). The same convolution identity, together with the global Hessian bound \(\eta I\), proves that every fixed derivative order at least two is globally bounded, including orders greater than \(K\).

## 7. Exact moment domain and surjectivity after mollification

For clarity, the boundary argument is included; a statement only about the interior of a domain would be insufficient.

The convex conjugate of \(V_0\) is finite exactly on \(D\). If \(g\in D\), the supporting inequality for \(U\), or evaluation at \(x=\nabla U(g)\), gives
\[
V_0^*(g)=U(g).
\tag{27}
\]
If \(b\in\partial D\) and \(V_0^*(b)\) were finite, convexity and \(0\in D\) would give a uniform upper bound on
\[
U(tb)=V_0^*(tb)
\le(1-t)V_0^*(0)+tV_0^*(b),\qquad0<t<1,
\]
contradicting the boundary divergence of \(U\). If \(b\notin\overline D\), separation provides \(v\) with
\(b\cdot v>\sup_{g\in D}g\cdot v=:s<\infty\). Since \(U\) is bounded below,
\[
V_0(tv)\le ts-\inf_DU\qquad(t\ge0),
\]
and therefore \(V_0^*(b)=\infty\). This establishes the claimed exact conjugate domain without an unproved gradient-range inference.

For \(g\in D\), choose \(r>0\) with \(\overline B_r(g)\subset D\). Boundedness of \(U\) on that ball yields
\[
V_0(x)\ge g\cdot x+r|x|-M_g.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(\int e^{g\cdot x-V_0(x)}\,dx<\infty\).

Conversely, suppose this integral is finite for some \(g\), and put \(\psi(x)=V_0(x)-g\cdot x\). This is a finite continuous convex function. Choose \(c>\psi(0)\) sufficiently large that its sublevel set \(L_c=\{\psi\le c\}\) contains a ball about zero. The integral bound makes \(L_c\) have finite volume. A convex set containing a ball and points at arbitrarily large distance has infinite volume, because the convex hull of that ball and one such point has volume at least a positive constant times the distance. Hence \(L_c\) is bounded. Convexity along rays then gives
\[
\psi(x)\ge a|x|-b\qquad(a>0, b<\infty).
\tag{29}
\]
For example take \(R_c\) with \(L_c\subset B_{R_c}\); outside that ball the ray inequality has slope at least \((c-\psi(0))/R_c\), and a constant handles the ball. Equation (29) implies that \(V_0^*\) is finite in a neighborhood of \(g\). By (27) and the boundary/exterior argument, \(g\in D\). In particular every boundary moment diverges.

The bounded perturbation (21) makes the exponential integrals for \(V\) finite at exactly the same points. It also preserves (28) with an adjusted constant. For each \(g\in D\), the function \(V(x)-g\cdot x\) therefore attains a unique minimum. Its first-order condition is \(\nabla V(x)=g\). Combined with (24) and the positive-definite Hessian, this proves that \(\nabla V\) is a smooth diffeomorphism onto \(D\), and completes the lemma.

## 8. Sufficiency for the distribution

Assume \(H_T(g)\in\operatorname{int}C\) on \(D\). Set
\[
\mu(g)=\operatorname{dist}(H_T(g),E\setminus C)>0,
\]
which is continuous. For the constants in Section 2, choose
\[
\tau(g)=\min\left\{1,\frac1B,
\frac{\varepsilon\mu(g)}{A e^{R|g|}(1+|g|)^N}\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
Apply the lemma to \(D,\tau,R,K\), and normalize \(p=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\). The value \(Z\) is finite and positive because \(0\in D\). For each \(x\), take \(a=\tau(\nabla V(x))\) in (6). Equations (8) and (30) give
\[
\|E_x\|\le\varepsilon\mu(\nabla V(x)).
\tag{31}
\]
The closed ball of radius \(\varepsilon\mu(g)\) about \(\varepsilon H_T(g)\) lies in \(C\). Hence
\[
-\varepsilon H_T(g)\preceq_C E_x\preceq_C\varepsilon H_T(g),
\]
which is exactly (2). Its lower bound is an interior cone element since \(\varepsilon<1\).

To check all integration claims, the global Hessian bound gives \(|\nabla V(x)|\le c_0+|x|\). The globally bounded derivatives of orders at least two and the chain rule imply
\[
|\partial^\alpha p(x)|\le C_\alpha(1+|x|)^{|\alpha|}p(x).
\tag{32}
\]
For every \(g\in D\), (28) and (21) bound \(e^{g\cdot x}p(x)\) by a fixed constant times \(e^{-r|x|}\). Thus (32) proves the first part of (3), including exponential integrability of all derivatives and their local suprema over fixed translates of \(B_R\). Apply (5) to the translates of \(p\), with any additional \(x\)-derivatives, to obtain the second part of (3). The same integrable seminorm bound permits interchange of \(T\) and the Bochner integral, giving
\[
\int (T*p)(x)\,dx=T_y\left(\int p(x-y)\,dx\right)=T(1)=e.
\tag{33}
\]
It also gives the usual moment identity for every \(g\in D\):
\[
\int e^{g\cdot x}(T*p)(x)\,dx
=\left(\int e^{g\cdot x}p(x)\,dx\right)H_T(g).
\tag{34}
\]

## 9. Necessity does not require a symmetric moment domain

Let \(\nu\) satisfy condition 2 and set \(S=T*\nu\). Choose smooth radial cutoffs \(\chi_L\), equal to one on \(B_L\), supported in \(B_{2L}\), with \(0\le\chi_L\le1\). For fixed \(g\in D\), the functions
\[
h_{L,g}(y)=\int e^{g\cdot(y+z)}\chi_L(y+z)\,\nu(dz)
\]
converge in \(C^N(B_R)\), with the fixed support ball from (5), to
\[
m_\nu(g)e^{g\cdot y},\qquad
m_\nu(g)=\int e^{g\cdot z}\,\nu(dz)<\infty.
\]
For the zero derivative, dominated convergence is uniform on the fixed \(y\)-ball by a tail bound. Derivatives falling on a cutoff have an additional factor \(L^{-j}\), and derivatives falling on the exponential are bounded by fixed powers of \(|g|\). Thus (5) gives
\[
S(e^{g\cdot x}\chi_L(x))\longrightarrow m_\nu(g)H_T(g)
\quad\text{in norm}.
\tag{35}
\]
At \(g=0\), \(S(\chi_L)\to e\). Choose a finite \(A_0\) with \(Q=S(\chi_{A_0})\in\operatorname{int}C\). For \(L\ge2A_0\),
\[
e^{g\cdot x}\chi_L(x)-e^{-2A_0|g|}\chi_{A_0}(x)\ge0.
\]
Positivity of \(S\), then (35) and closedness of \(C\), imply
\[
m_\nu(g)H_T(g)-e^{-2A_0|g|}Q\in C.
\]
Since \(Q\) is interior and \(m_\nu(g)>0\), this proves \(H_T(g)\in\operatorname{int}C\). Only the moment at \(g\), and total probability at zero, were used. In particular no moment at \(-g\) is required. Condition 3 implies condition 2 immediately, so all equivalences are proved.

## 10. An explicit unbounded asymmetric example

In one dimension let
\[
T=\delta-\delta',\qquad H_T(g)=1+g,
\qquad p(x)=e^{x-e^x}.
\]
The substitution \(t=e^x\) gives \(\int p=1\) and
\[
\int e^{gx}p(x)\,dx=\Gamma(g+1)<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g>-1.
\]
The potential \(V(x)=e^x-x\) has \(V''(x)=e^x>0\), and
\[
T*p=p-p'=e^xp>0.
\]
Thus the exact moment domain is \((-1,\infty)\), and \(H_T\) is negative at some points outside it. Tensoring this density with a Gaussian in the remaining coordinates gives the half-space \(D=\{g:g_1>-1\}\), including lineality. Taking products of the one-dimensional construction and the corresponding factors of \(T\) gives shifted open orthants, whose boundaries have corners.

This example also shows why a proof of necessity based only on paired moments at \(g\) and \(-g\) would miss the stated theorem. The compact-cutoff proof avoids that restriction.

All of those geometric steps are proved above; historical priority for their combined application to scalar regularization has not been established. Classical barrier and Legendre constructions are explicitly credited in Section 4. This is finite-dimensional in the physical variable \(x\); the target ordered Banach space may be infinite-dimensional. No assertion is made for an infinite-dimensional physical variable, where the Lebesgue integral and compact convex geometry used here are unavailable.

The proof is qualitative and can require arbitrarily large dual curvature. It assumes the ability to majorize the inverse positivity margin on each compact subset of \(D\); it gives no efficient certification, sampling, variance optimization, or acquisition procedure.
