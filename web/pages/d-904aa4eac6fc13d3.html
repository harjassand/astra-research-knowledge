# Pointwise random interior weights avoid the exceptional blind set

Status: exact elementary theorem independently derived from the weighted-score fiber and endpoint similarity. No quantitative conditioning or acquisition theorem, and no novelty claim.

Fix a faithful density matrix sigma and a finite supplied Hermitian frame with scalar ordinary commutant. For each real lambda in[0,1] the weighted transformed score filter in an eigenbasis of sigma is

K_lambda(A)_ij = sqrt(p_i p_j) A_ij / [(1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j].

Let V be the real vector space of traceless Hermitian matrices. Define the linear map

M_lambda(X) = ([X,K_lambda(A_a)],[X,K_lambda(A_a)^dagger])_a.

In any fixed orthonormal real basis of V, its squared Frobenius Gram matrix is real symmetric, positive semidefinite, and has entries rational in lambda. All denominators are nonzero on[0,1], since each eigenvalue p_i is positive. The faithful score fiber is singleton exactly when this Gram matrix is positive definite: a Hermitian nonscalar commutant element can be made traceless, and conversely a kernel vector yields a normalized positive perturbation through the fiber.

At lambda0 the matrices K0 are one common invertible similarity of the original Hermitian A frame. Their ordinary commutant is scalar. Thus M0 is injective and its Gram determinant is strictly positive. The determinant is a rational function of lambda which is not identically zero. It therefore has only FINITELY MANY zeros on[0,1]. Away from those roots the score identifies sigma among all faithful competitors.

Consequently, for EACH FIXED target and supplied frame, drawing one interior weight independently from an absolutely continuous distribution gives identification with probability one. This is simultaneous over competitors to that fixed target. It is not a claim that the same random weight works for every target, nor that the score has a uniform calibration gap.

A crude explicit exceptional-root bound is available. Let r=d(d-1); clear denominators with the product of all r oriented offdiagonal linear factors. Each transformed matrix entry has numerator degree at most r, and the Gram determinant on m=d^2-1 real dimensions has numerator degree at most2rm. Hence there are at most2d(d-1)(d^2-1) bad real weights (usually much fewer). A uniformly sampled finite grid of N distinct interior points has pointwise failure probability at most this bound/N. This loose algebraic count gives no control on how close a grid point can lie to a bad root.

The theorem is compatible with the finite-list Vandermonde counterexample: that counterexample chooses its dimension, frame and target after the finite weight support is specified. It also agrees with b_math's stronger exact finite-dimensional repair using d(d-1) distinct values of h=lambda(1-lambda), from rational interpolation of the Hermitian score parts. Endpoint scores already provide universal ordinary-frame identification and remain the clean known deterministic option.

Costs and limits: an ideal continuous real draw is not a finite-precision implementation. A near-exceptional weight can have arbitrarily poor score calibration; no preparation, operator norm, finite-copy, normalized Gibbs-model expectation or numerical interpolation guarantee follows. The result changes the identification quantifier and offers a falsifiable measurement-design question, rather than an efficient learner. Next step: bound the measure of weights with a small commutator gap for a specified structured target/frame, while charging score estimation costs.

## Simultaneous identification on an analytic finite-parameter model

There is a stronger uniform-model variant, using standard analytic experiment-design dimension methods (Sontag2002 is being checked as nearest primary prior). Specify a compact semianalytic parameter domain Theta contained in R^p. Let theta->rho_theta be real analytic on an open neighborhood of Theta, with rho_theta faithful throughout that neighborhood; alternatively a compact analytic parameter manifold with finite semianalytic charts is sufficient. Fix the same known ordinary-irreducible Hermitian frame. Pick m>=2p+1 weights independently from an absolutely continuous distribution supported on a fixed compact interior interval I=[delta,1-delta], 0<delta<1/2.

Then with probability one, the JOINT score tuple at those m weights is injective in the state rho_theta over the ENTIRE model. Redundant parameterizations are permitted: injectivity refers to states, not necessarily theta.

Proof. For any distinct state pair, the score difference is analytic and rational in lambda, and is not identically zero because endpoint ordinary-frame identification holds. Thus its zero set on I is finite. Let E_m be the incidence set of (theta,theta',lambda_1,...,lambda_m) with rho_theta!=rho_theta' and equality of every entire frame score at every sampled weight. Scores are analytic in the parameters and weights: the weighted Sylvester superoperator is invertible on the positive-state neighborhood. Equality can be written as the vanishing of a sum of squared real/imaginary matrix entries. State inequality is the strict positivity of the squared Frobenius state difference. On the compact domains these are relatively compact semianalytic conditions, hence subanalytic, with a uniform faithful eigenvalue floor. The fibers of E_m over (theta,theta') are finite products of finite sets. The subanalytic fiber dimension theorem therefore gives dim E_m<=2p. Its projection into I^m has dimension<=2p<m and Lebesgue measure zero. Every bad weight tuple lies in this projection; absolutely continuous sampling avoids it almost surely.

The finite exceptional-set statement already suffices for one fixed unknown target and ALL faithful competitors. This uniform-model version allows one sampled experiment design to identify every state in a prescribed model simultaneously, and its channel count depends on model dimension rather than Hilbert-space dimension. For n-qubit local models p may be polynomial in n; the positive theorem is still only exact population identification. It neither supplies a positive uniform calibration constant on the offdiagonal pair domain nor controls optimizer convergence, finite-copy error, spectrum-dependent preparation, score operator evaluation or the size of the subanalytic projection computation. It is an application of the analytic experiment-design method rather than a new general dimension theorem.

## The repair retains the published copy-expectation objective

Directly read source AppendixA Eq51-56 from the preserved2609.28391v1 snapshot. The author-defined weighted Fisher divergence is

J_lambda(rho||sigma)=.5 sum_a ||S_lambda,a(rho)-S_lambda,a(sigma)||_(sigma,lambda)^2,

where ||T||²_(sigma,lambda)=tr sigma[lambda T^dagger T+(1-lambda)T T^dagger]. Faithfulness makes this positive definite; J=0 is exactly equality of the entire scores. Lemma14 Eq56 expresses it as tr(sigma M_lambda(rho))+C_lambda(sigma), with the candidate-only Hermitian observable

M_lambda=.5 sum_a [lambda S_a^dagger S_a+(1-lambda)S_a S_a^dagger+partial_a(S_a+S_a^dagger)].

Therefore the one-weight randomized repair can be implemented at the POPULATION interface simply by drawing the weight once and minimizing the existing objective. It needs no additional target normalization oracle or target eigenbasis. Several weights can be summed using the same identity. The target-dependent additive constant is irrelevant to optimization. This is not a proof that evaluating the candidate observable, preparing target copies or finding its global minimum is efficient at low temperature. Interior Fourier multipliers are bounded away from the endpoint exponential energy amplification, but that fact alone does not bound the full algorithm.

## Finite-precision one-weight population repair

Independent r03/b_math sharpened the earlier Gram bound. Stack only [X,K_lambda(A_a)] as a complex-linear map on traceless complex matrices of dimension m=d²-1. At lambda0 this map has full column rank, so at least one m-square minor is nonzero. The product of all r=d(d-1) oriented offdiagonal linear denominators clears every matrix entry, yielding polynomial entries of degree at most r. The selected minor numerator has degree at most

B=d(d-1)(d²-1).

Every truly nonidentifying weight must make this minor vanish: a nonscalar Hermitian commutant yields a traceless complex kernel. Its real roots therefore contain every exceptional weight, and number at most B. This improves the earlier factor-two Gram estimate. The finite degree argument remains valid with irrational eigenvalues or frame entries; one need not acquire the coefficients or locate the roots.

For any faithful target and supplied ordinary-irreducible frame FIXED before the random draw, uniformly choose one of N distinct rational weights inside[delta,1-delta]. Then

P(the entire weighted-score objective is nonidentifying) <= min(1,B/N).

For n qubits choose b=ceil(log2(B/eta)), N=2^b, draw a b-bit uniform integer j, and set lambda=1/4+j/2^(b+1). These N exactly representable rational points lie in[1/4,3/4). Failure probability is at most eta. Only 4n+log2(1/eta)+O(1) random/weight bits are sufficient. For six qubits B=16511040, eta=10^-6, b=44 suffices. Thus the population repair does not require ideal infinite-precision random real sampling. A worst-case target can be fixed with full knowledge of the grid/distribution; it simply cannot adapt to the realized random weight.

This is a pointwise randomized correctness guarantee uniformly bounded over fixed targets, not one design injective for all targets simultaneously. The latter follows with2p+1 draws under the separately stated analytic-model assumptions. It is also not a lower calibration-gap guarantee: even a nonexceptional grid point may be very near a bad root. Candidate weighted-score evaluation, target preparation, finite-copy estimation and optimization remain the decisive cost gaps. The metric on this interior interval is uniformly comparable to the SLD metric, but that does not compare the score maps or cure their calibration near exceptional weights.

The finite-list negative theorem is compatible: it takes dimension at least k+2 after closing a prescribed grid of k weights under complements. Such dimensions exceed the degree-count regime where the fixed-d grid guarantee applies. This resolves the exact quantifier and finite-precision distinction rather than evading it.
