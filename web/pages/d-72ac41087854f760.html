# Matched full-likelihood comparison

2026-10-09. Targeted computational check, not a statistical-efficiency theorem.

## What is matched

`positive_fit_benchmark.py` uses the same fixed-seed observations, known rates,
log grid, density cap M=2, observation window L=4, time-rate product a=2,
and optional prefix mass repair for every method. Data are Poisson counts with
exposure 100,000 from the same finite-lattice forward model. The continuous
binning/noise test is separate; these timings isolate numerical computation.
No specimen preprocessing or instrument calibration is being benchmarked.

The nonlinear comparator minimizes the full finite-time Poisson objective
under exactly the same coordinate bounds [0,Mh]. Its positive uniformization
forward and analytic reverse adjoint share a single stored forward table.
There is no finite-difference gradient and no redundant second forward sweep.
It uses a parameter-adaptive rigorously bounded tail order (usually 20), while
the direct inverse uses a conservative cap-based order 64. L-BFGS-B receives
both a cheap first-generation initializer and the proposed inverse as a warm
start. Initialization time is counted for both. Rate setup, repair and the
optional independent forward-defect check are separately recorded.

The optimizer reports relative-objective convergence, not necessarily its
strict projected-gradient target; the latter residual is recorded explicitly.
No global optimum is claimed. Both starts reach very similar objectives.
The benchmark is a strong implemented full-likelihood comparator, not proof
that every possible optimizer, preconditioner or regularizer is inferior.

The last-but-one column is an ORACLE retrospective diagnostic, not an
implementable stopping rule: it uses the known synthetic truth. A fit starting
at the direct inverse already meets that error at time zero of optimization.
In the final case the cheap-start fit reaches the same error almost as quickly
as the direct sweep. There is no uniform same-error speedup claim.

Full likelihood lowers the observed repaired L1 error by approximately
0.02% to 2.2% in these four realizations. This small experiment does not rank
expected statistical risk. It shows that avoiding optimization does not
require a conspicuously worse reconstruction in these cases, but likelihood
refinement can improve it.

For both 101-bin cases, the positive sweep and the 100-digit causal renewal
inverse agree within 2.6e-16 in coefficient l1. At gamma=.02 the renewal
transform reaches 2.7e40; the positive sweep never forms that transform.
The prior precision-failure examples remain in `no_sc_noise_results.json`.

The advance substantiated here is stable constructive evaluation of the
causal estimator with an explicit truncation/error budget, including nearly
constant rates. It is not a universal fitting speed advantage.

## Validation and reproduction

The new baseline forward was compared with an independently formed dense
matrix exponential (maximum difference 1.4e-16); four central finite-difference
gradient checks differed by at most 6.3e-10 at step 1e-6.

    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python positive_fit_benchmark.py

Raw observations, truth, direct and both fitted estimates are retained in
`positive_fit_raw_arrays.npz`. All iteration traces, projected gradients,
settings, environment versions and outcomes are in `positive_fit_results.json`.
`positive_fit_log.txt` preserves the unabridged run summaries.
