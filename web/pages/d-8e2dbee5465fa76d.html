INDEPENDENT AUDIT RECEIPT: D=4 QUARTIC-DISPERSION BOSON FILTER
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: exact product-filter/commutator, ghost boundary, Fock normalization,
fixed fugacity tilt, entropy, and closed-form Gibbs comparison.
Status: internally checked proof candidate under the hypotheses below;
not external validation and not a novelty/priority result.

1. RESULT AND INTERFACE

Let Lambda_L={1,...,L-1}^4, S=(L-1)^4, and let h_L be the Dirichlet
nearest-neighbor graph Laplacian with edge weight J>0. The convention used
here is

  (Delta u)(x)=sum_{e in {+/-e_1,...,+/-e_4}}(u(x+e)-u(x)),
  h_L=-J Delta

on zero-extended functions, so h_L has diagonal 8J and internal neighbor
entries -J. Let K_L=dGamma(h_L^2/128), beta=tau L^4, and

  H_0=K_L-lambda M/L^4,   tau>0,   lambda in [-B,0].

For each L, let W be any nonnegative closed quadratic form on full boson
Fock space such that D(q_0) intersect D(w) is dense, q_0+w is closed,
and the full no-double-occupancy subspace P is contained in D(w) with
w[v]=0 for every v in P. No locality, number conservation, boundedness,
or uniform strength bound on W is required. In fact the proof only uses
that W vanishes on P; the filter below has range in P.

Then the estimates in INDEPENDENT_DERIVATION.txt follow:

  D(rho_W || rho_0) <= C (log L)^(-1/2),
  ||rho_W-rho_0||_1 <= sqrt(2C) (log L)^(-1/4),

for all sufficiently large L, with C depending on J,tau,B and the fixed
cutoff choice, but not on W. The same trial-state comparison covers any
hard compression to a closed allowed subspace containing P for which the
restricted free quadratic form is densely defined. This is a whole-Fock
Gibbs comparison to the matched free reference at the same beta and lambda.

2. SPECTRAL AND FREE-STATE INPUTS

The sine eigenvalues satisfy
  4J|k|^2/L^2 <= epsilon_k <= J pi^2 |k|^2/L^2,
  k in {1,...,L-1}^4.
Thus the dimensionless free mode energy is
  q_k=beta epsilon_k^2/128-tau lambda >= a |k|^4,
  a=tau J^2/8.
Choose alpha=a/4. Then q_k-alpha >=(3a/4)|k|^4. The sums

  N_r=sum_{k in N^4} |k|^r/(exp((3a/4)|k|^4)-1), r=0,1,2,4,

are finite. They bound all needed mode moments in both rho_0 and the
single tilted free state rho_alpha proportional to exp(alpha M)rho_0.
For fixed integer p, M^p <= c_{p,alpha} exp(alpha M), and
Tr rho_0 exp(alpha M) <= exp(N_0). This is one L-independent tilt;
polynomial particle-count factors are not paid once per pair or triple.

The normalized 4D sine eigenfunctions obey |phi_k(x)|<=4/L^2 and, after
zero extension, |phi_k(x+e)-phi_k(x)|<=4 pi |k_j|/L^3 for a coordinate
edge e in direction j, including edges whose endpoint is a ghost site.
Consequently under rho_0 and rho_alpha,

  gamma_xx=O(L^-4), gamma_{bb}=O(L^-6),
  Gamma_2(x,y)=<a_x^*a_y^*a_y a_x>=O(L^-8),
  Gamma_3(x,y,z)=<a_x^*a_y^*a_z^*a_z a_y a_x>=O(L^-12),
  Gamma_{2,grad}(x,y)=<b^*a_y^*a_y b>=O(L^-10).

These follow from the exact gauge-invariant boson Wick/permanent formula;
repeated sites are included, with normal-ordered factorial occupations.
For the derivative-pair term,
Gamma_{2,grad}=gamma_bb gamma_yy+|gamma_by|^2 and
|gamma_by|^2<=gamma_bb gamma_yy. No heat-kernel entrywise positivity for
exp(-beta h_L^2/128) is used.

Also Tr rho_0 M=O(1) and beta Tr rho_0 K_L=O(1), by the same convergent
fourth-power mode sums.

3. FILTER AND DISCRETE PRODUCT ESTIMATES

Take ell=floor(sqrt(L)), t=log ell, and a fixed C^2 endpoint-flat
function chi on [0,1], with chi(0)=0, chi(1)=1 and chi'=chi''=0 at both
ends. For example chi(s)=10s^3-15s^4+6s^5. Put

  f(z)=0                         if |z|_2<=1,
       chi(log |z|_2/t)          if 1<|z|_2<ell,
       1                         if |z|_2>=ell.

Define F_m(X)=product_{i<j} f(x_i-x_j), F_0=F_1=1. Then 0<=F<=I,
[F,M]=0, ran F is in P, and F has finite rank: every configuration with
more than S particles has a duplicate site and F_m=0. The endpoint-flat
choice makes the radial extension C^2 across both cutoff spheres.

With E={+/-e_1,...,+/-e_4}, define
  A_1=sum_z sum_e |f(z+e)-f(z)|^2,
  A_2=sum_z |Delta f(z)|^2,
where z ranges over Z^4 and Delta uses the eight-direction convention in
Section 1. The derivative bounds are |grad f|<=C/(t r) and
|D^2 f|<=C/(t r^2) on the nonconstant annulus. The number of lattice
points in a radius-r shell is O(r^3), so

  A_1<=C ell^2/t^2,
  A_2<=C t^-2 sum_{1<=r<=ell}1/r <= C/t.

Since 1-product u_i<=sum(1-u_i) for 0<=u_i<=1, the free collision loss
and number-weighted loss satisfy

  delta=1-Tr rho_0 F^2 <= C ell^4/L^4=O(L^-2),
  q_M=Tr rho_0 M(I-F^2) <= C ell^4/L^4=O(L^-2).

For q_M, use the one tilt M<=c exp(alpha M) and the two-point Wick bound.

4. EXACT GHOST-BOUNDARY COMMUTATOR

This is the requested sign and boundary check. Extend every labeled
wavefunction psi by zero outside Lambda_L^m, and evaluate the same pair
product F_m on ghost coordinates as well. For x_i in Lambda_L, the
Dirichlet operator is precisely the restriction of -J Delta_i on that
zero extension. The full-lattice identity

  Delta_i(F_m psi) = F_m Delta_i psi + (Delta_i F_m) psi
                     + sum_e (D_e F_m)(D_e psi),
  D_e g(X)=g(X^{i,e})-g(X),

therefore gives, after restriction to Lambda_L^m,

  [h_i,F]psi = (h_i F_m)psi - J sum_e (D_e F_m)(D_e psi),
  h_i F_m := -J Delta_i F_m.                         (4.1)

The sum is over all eight directions, including those for which x_i+e is
outside the box. Such a ghost value of psi is zero, but D_e psi=-psi at
that edge; the corresponding D_e F term in (4.1) is retained. Thus the
identity does not silently drop Dirichlet boundary terms.

For a fixed coordinate i, factor F_m=H_i product_{j!=i}g_j, where H_i is
the product of factors not involving i, 0<=H_i<=1, and
g_j=f(x_i-x_j). The exact discrete Leibniz rule

  Delta(uv)=u Delta v+v Delta u+sum_e(D_eu)(D_ev)

and induction give

  |Delta product_j g_j| <= sum_j |Delta g_j|
       +sum_e sum_{j<k}|D_eg_j||D_eg_k|,
  |D_e product_j g_j| <= sum_j |D_eg_j|.

There are eight directions and at most m^2 pair terms. Cauchy-Schwarz
with explicit count gives

  |Delta_i F_m|^2 <= 2m sum_{j!=i}|Delta f(x_i-x_j)|^2
       +8m(m-1) sum_{e,j<k; j,k!=i}
               |D_ef(x_i-x_j)|^2 |D_ef(x_i-x_k)|^2,
  |D_e F_m|^2 <= m sum_{j!=i}|D_ef(x_i-x_j)|^2.

The displayed first coefficient may be enlarged to C m^2 as needed. The
important powers are at most m^2 for pair/triple diagonal terms and m for
the derivative-pair term.

5. NO LOST FACTORIAL OR PARTICLE-COUNT POWER

On the symmetric m-sector, use the normalized labeled-coordinate
embedding into ell^2(Lambda_L^m). For diagonal normal-ordered observables,
the ordered pair and triple sums are exactly the corresponding Fock
expectations. For example, an occupation vector n has
m!/product_x n_x! labeled representatives and the lifted diagonal
weight at each representative is the physical weight divided by this
number. Summing representatives cancels it; ordered distinct labels give
n_x(n_y-delta_xy), or the analogous third falling factorial. Thus the
pair/triple sums above carry no sector m! loss.

For the derivative term, for x in Lambda_L and a direction e set
  b_{x,e}=a_{x+e}-a_x,
with a_y defined to be zero for a ghost y outside Lambda_L. Then the exact
coordinate/Fock identity is

  sum_{i!=j} sum_X w(x_i,x_j)|D_e psi(X)|^2
   =sum_{x,y in Lambda_L} w(x,y)
       <psi,b_{x,e}^* a_y^* a_y b_{x,e} psi>,

for w(x,y)=|f(x+e-y)-f(x-y)|^2, with the evident sector sum. This is
normal ordered in the differentiated particle and the distinct y-particle;
it remains exact if x=y because the annihilators enforce the falling
factorial. If x+e is a ghost, b_{x,e}=-a_x. The zero-extended sine
estimate still gives
  |phi_k(x+e)-phi_k(x)|<=4 pi |k_j|/L^3:
at a face, the internal endpoint has coordinate 1 or L-1 and
|sin(pi k_j/L)|<=pi k_j/L, while the ghost endpoint is zero. Hence the
boundary-edge covariance is also O(L^-6), not O(L^-4). No surface-order
loss is introduced.

The pointwise commutator bound from (4.1) is a positive form bound by
these diagonal pair, triple, and derivative-pair observables, with
coefficients at most C J^2 and the particle factors above. Each observable
commutes with M. For any such positive number-conserving O and r=1,2,

  Tr rho_0 M^r O <= c_{r,alpha} Tr rho_0 exp(alpha M) O
                    = c_{r,alpha} C_alpha Tr rho_alpha O.

Thus one fixed tilt absorbs the complete m or m^2 multiplier, once, even
after summing all labeled pairs/triples. Applying the exact Wick bounds and
sum_x over S=O(L^4) sites yields

  C_F:=sum_i Tr rho_0 [h_i,F]^*[h_i,F]
       <= C[J,tau] (L^-4 A_2 + L^-8 A_1^2 + L^-6 A_1),
  beta C_F <= C[J,tau]/t.                            (5.1)

The three terms are respectively the pair Laplacian density (L^-8),
triple cross-gradient density (L^-12), and derivative-pair density
(L^-10). With ell=floor(sqrt(L)), they scale as
L^-4/t, L^-6/t^4, and L^-5/t^2 before multiplying by beta=L^4;
only the first determines the O(1/t) bound.

6. SUMMED KINETIC INEQUALITY AND ENTROPY

The free Gibbs block on Sym^m need not commute with each labeled h_i.
No such commutation is used. For any positive rho supported on Sym^m,
0<=F<=I, and eta>0, expand h_iF=Fh_i+[h_i,F] in Hilbert-Schmidt norm:

  Tr rho F K F = 128^-1 sum_i ||h_iF rho^(1/2)||_2^2
    <= (1+eta) Tr rho K
       +(1+eta^-1) C_F/128.                          (6.1)

Indeed the first term is bounded by
Tr rho h_i F^2 h_i <= Tr rho h_i^2 as a positive-form inequality;
it does not require [rho,h_i]=0. The Young constants are exactly
1+eta and 1+eta^-1. No stoquasticity assumption on h_i^2 is used.

Set p=Tr rho_0F^2=1-delta and sigma=F rho_0 F/p. It is a finite-rank
state supported in P, so w[ sigma ]=0. Since [F,M]=0,

  beta Tr(sigma-rho_0)H_0
    <= { beta(eta+delta)Tr rho_0K
       + beta(1+eta^-1)C_F/128
       + tau B delta Tr rho_0 M }/p.                 (6.2)

The entropy estimate is valid on full Fock space. Let G=(I-F^2)^(1/2)
and tau_fail=G rho_0 G/delta when delta>0. The two-outcome isometric
filter with Kraus operators F,G, keeping a classical outcome flag, gives

  S(rho_0)-S(sigma) <= h_2(delta)+delta S(tau_fail).

Since Tr tau_fail M=q_M/delta, maximum entropy against exp(-sM) gives

  delta S(tau_fail) <= s q_M + delta log Tr exp(-sM),
  Tr_Fock exp(-sM)=(1-exp(-s))^-S.

Choose s=log(S+1). Then log Tr exp(-sM)=S log(1+1/S)<=1, proving

  S(rho_0)-S(sigma)<=h_2(delta)+q_M log(S+1)+delta.  (6.3)

This uses no finite number cutoff. Equivalently, finite cutoffs pass to
the limit using the finite free exponential number moment and the displayed
entropy bound. Since delta,q_M=O(L^-2), all terms in (6.3) are
O(L^-2 log L).

Combining (6.2), (6.3), Tr rho_0M=O(1), beta Tr rho_0K=O(1), and
beta C_F=O(1/t), then choosing eta=t^-1/2, gives

  D(sigma||rho_0)=S(rho_0)-S(sigma)
       +beta Tr(sigma-rho_0)H_0 <= C t^-1/2.

7. CLOSED FORM DOMAIN AND GIBBS VARIATIONAL PASSAGE

For each finite L, the one-particle space has S modes and q_k>0, so
Z_0=Tr exp(-beta H_0)=product_k(1-exp(-q_k))^-1 is finite. The uniform
lower bound q_k>=a|k|^4 also makes the half-temperature free partition
finite uniformly in L. Therefore rho_0 has finite energy and entropy.

By hypothesis the form sum q_W=q_0+w is closed and densely defined.
Because w>=0, H_W>=H_0>=0 in form order. H_0 has compact resolvent;
the form domain of H_W embeds into that of H_0 with bounded q_0 norm on
q_W-bounded sets, so H_W has compact resolvent. Min-max gives ordered
eigenvalues E_n(H_W)>=E_n(H_0), hence Z_W<=Z_0<infinity. The free
half-temperature trace bounds the interacting half-temperature trace by
the same comparison; this gives finite Gibbs energy/entropy. The form
identity implies Tr rho_W w<infinity.

Because F has finite rank, ran F is a finite-dimensional subspace of
D(q_0) intersect D(w), and q_W=q_0 there. Gibbs variational with the
finite-rank trial sigma gives

  log(Z_0/Z_W) <= D(sigma||rho_0).

For the interacting Gibbs state, the energy identity in the form sense is

  D(rho_W||rho_0)=log(Z_0/Z_W)-beta Tr rho_W w
                <=log(Z_0/Z_W).

Together with quantum Pinsker this proves the announced bounds. W need not
preserve M; only the reference and trial filter do.

8. COSTS, BASELINE, FALSIFIER, AND STATUS

Matched baseline: rho_0 at the same finite-volume dispersion, beta,
chemical scaling, and full Fock space. The result compares to this exact
reference; it does not compare against a differently prepared state.
The only primary literature comparison checked for this interface is
Wu, Zhou, and Wu, “Quantum criticality of bosonic systems with the Lifshitz
dispersion,” submitted 2015-09-14, revised 2017-05-30, PRB 96, 085140
(2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03953. It treats Lifshitz phi^4
critical scaling and logarithms near the upper critical dimension; it does
not establish this lattice finite-volume, whole-Fock relative-entropy
comparison. This is qualitative physical context, not a proof dependency.

Costs: no numerical computation, oracle, interacting spectrum, or acquired
input is used. Constants are convergent free-mode sums depending on
J,tau,B. The asymptotic spatial size is S=(L-1)^4; the filter radius is
ell~sqrt(L); the error rates are KL O((log L)^-1/2) and trace norm
O((log L)^-1/4). There is no bit-complexity or efficient state-preparation
claim. A finite-dimensional proof verification costs no claimed oracle
queries; exact constants were not optimized.

Falsifier for this derivation: a wrong Dirichlet convention that changes
the zero-extension identity; an interaction form not actually null on
ran F; failure of the stated C^2 discrete A_2=O(1/log ell) estimate; a
missing labeled multiplicity in one of the three observables; or inability
to absorb the resulting particle-count power under the fixed alpha tilt.
The exact boundary, product, multiplicity, and entropy checks above close
these proposed falsifiers under the stated interface. No explicit
counterexample to the result was found.
