# Finite-horizon safety for species-specific diffusion on a finite-volume graph

## Claim status

This is an internally reconstructed conditional theorem, with no external correctness or priority review. It is a box-specialized extension of the finite-volume chemical-jump certificate recorded in `work/agents/systems_metabolism/chemostat_control/astra/jump_safety.txt`. A source card reports that the earlier graph extension required equal local volumes and common species hopping, and that unequal-species diffusion defeated the general-polytope argument (`work/agents/systems_metabolism/chemostat_control/astra/N35-controlled-chemical-safety.txt`, line 8). The present argument uses a componentwise box and a symmetric finite-volume exchange law. It does not establish safety for arbitrary convex polytopes, nonreciprocal transport, growing graphs, or a physical electrode without validation of the model.

## Model and certificate

Let (G=(I,E)) be a fixed finite undirected graph with (n=|I|) sites and (d) species. Give site (i) a volume factor α_i>0; the physical volume is scaled by a common integer (V). Write the concentration as

\[
x_{i,s}=Z_{i,s}/(\alpha_i V),\qquad i\in I,\quad 1\le s\le d.
\]

Fix coordinate bounds (0<a_s<b_s<\infty) and the box
\[
Q=\{x:a_s\le x_{i,s}\le b_s\text{ for every }(i,s)\}.
\]
At site (i), reaction (r) has integer reactant complex (y_{ir}\in\mathbb N^d), integer jump \(\nu_{ir}\in\mathbb Z^d\), predictable rate coefficient (0\le k_{ir}(t)\le K_{ir}\), and count intensity
\[
\alpha_i V k_{ir}(t)\,\phi^{\alpha_i V}_{y_{ir}}(x_i),\qquad
\phi^W_y(x)=W^{-|y|}\prod_s(Wx_s)_{y_s}.
\]
Here \((z)_q=z(z-1)\cdots(z-q+1)\), and an insufficient reactant count gives zero intensity. A reaction changes only site (i), by concentration jump \(\nu_{ir}/(\alpha_iV)\).

For each undirected edge \(\{i,j\}\) and species (s), a transfer (i\to j) changes the two counts by −1 at (i), +1 at (j), with intensity
\[
V c_{ij,s}(t) x_{i,s},\qquad 0\le c_{ij,s}(t)=c_{ji,s}(t)\le C_{ij,s}.
\]
The coefficient may be predictable, provided the same coefficient is used for both directions of the edge at that instant. This is the detailed-balance finite-volume exchange form; (c) may depend on species, so species have unequal mobilities. At site (i), this contributes concentration drift
\[
m_{i,s}(x,t)=\frac1{\alpha_i}\sum_{j\sim i}c_{ij,s}(t)(x_{j,s}-x_{i,s}).
\]

Let \(f^{\rm rxn}_{i,s}(x_i,k)=\sum_r k_{ir}x_i^{y_{ir}}\nu_{ir,s}\) be the deterministic reaction drift. Supply positive collar widths \(\ell_{i,s}^{-},\ell_{i,s}^{+}\) and reaction margins \(\gamma_{i,s}^{-},\gamma_{i,s}^{+}>0\) such that, for every (x\in Q) and every allowed predictable rate vector,
\[
f^{\rm rxn}_{i,s}\ge\gamma_{i,s}^{-}
\quad\text{if }a_s\le x_{i,s}\le a_s+\ell_{i,s}^{-},
\]
\[
-f^{\rm rxn}_{i,s}\ge\gamma_{i,s}^{+}
\quad\text{if }b_s-\ell_{i,s}^{+}\le x_{i,s}\le b_s.
\]
These are uniform conditions over the other species and all controls. They are strong; one failing face invalidates this certificate.

For either face (q=(i,s,\pm)), put
\[
C_{i,s}=\sum_{j\sim i}C_{ij,s}.
\]
The exchange drift of the corresponding inward gap is at least \(-C_{i,s}\ell_q/\alpha_i\) throughout its collar: for a lower face, use (x_{j,s}\ge a_s); for an upper face, use (x_{j,s}\le b_s). Hence the full deterministic inward margin is at least
\[
\bar\gamma_q=\gamma_q-C_{i,s}\ell_q/\alpha_i.
\]
Require \(\bar\gamma_q>0\). This is the graph maximum-principle step; it is where the box and reciprocal edge coefficient enter.

## Explicit finite-volume constants

Let (U_s=b_s), and for a reactant complex (y\), define
\[
E_y(U)=\sum_{t:y_t\ge2}\binom{y_t}{2}
 U_t^{y_t-1}\prod_{u\ne t}U_u^{y_u}.
\]
The factorial propensity obeys (0\le x^y-\phi^W_y(x)\le E_y(U)/W) on the count lattice, including insufficient-reactant points. This follows by writing each falling-factorial factor as \(\max(x_t-h/W,0)\), telescoping the nonnegative products, and summing the deficits (h/W).

For a face (q=(i,s,\pm)), define
\[
D_q=\frac1{\alpha_i}\sum_r K_{ir}|\nu_{ir,s}|E_{y_{ir}}(U),\qquad
J_q=\frac1{\alpha_i}\max\bigl(\{|\nu_{ir,s}|:r\}\cup\{1:C_{i,s}>0\}\bigr),
\]
\[
B_q=\frac1{\alpha_i}\sum_rK_{ir}\nu_{ir,s}^2 U^{y_{ir}}
 +\frac{2b_s}{\alpha_i^2}\sum_{j\sim i}C_{ij,s},
\]
\[
\Lambda=\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_rK_{ir}U^{y_{ir}}
 +2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\sum_s C_{ij,s}b_s.
\]
Use (J_q>0) and (B_q>0); an inert coordinate face should be removed from the claimed safety event. Set
\[
V_0=\max_q\left\lceil\frac{2D_q}{\bar\gamma_q}\right\rceil\vee1,
\quad \mu_q=\bar\gamma_q/2,
\quad \theta_q=\min\{J_q^{-1},\mu_q/(3B_q)\},
\quad c_* =\min_q\theta_q\ell_q.
\]

## Theorem

Suppose (Z_0/(\alpha_iV)\in Q) and (V\ge V_0). Let \(\tau_Q\) be the first jump that leaves (Q). Uniformly over all predictable reaction controls and symmetric predictable edge coefficients satisfying the bounds above, for every finite (T\),
\[
\boxed{\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)
\le \sum_q e^{-\theta_q V g_q(X_0)}
 +2M V\Lambda T e^{-c_*V},\qquad M=2nd,}
\]
where (g_q) is the nonnegative coordinate gap for face (q).
In particular, if all initial gaps satisfy (g_q(X_0)\ge d_q>0), then with (c_0=\min_q\theta_qd_q>0\),
\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le e^{c_*V/2})
\le M e^{-c_0V}+2M V\Lambda e^{-c_*V/2}.
\]
This is a finite-horizon conditional model guarantee. It does not imply infinite-time containment, a sharp exit exponent, mesh-uniformity, or physical validation.

## Proof

Fix a face (q=(i,s,\pm)). Its gap has jumps \(\Delta g=\xi/(\alpha_iV)\), with 
\(|\xi|\le \max_r|\nu_{ir,s}|\) for reactions and 
\(|\xi|=1\) for migration. The exact finite-volume reaction drift differs from the polynomial drift by at most (D_q/V). Migration has no factorial correction. Therefore its total predictable gap drift is at least
\[
\bar\gamma_q-D_q/V\ge\bar\gamma_q/2=\mu_q
\]
inside the face collar. The displayed (B_q) bounds (V^{-1}\sum_{\text{jumps}}\text{rate}\,(V\Delta g)^2\): the reaction contribution is bounded by the first term, and the two directed migrations on each incident edge contribute at most (2C_{ij,s}b_s/\alpha_i^2\). The total jump intensity in (Q) is at most (V\Lambda).

Set \(\Phi_q(x)=e^{-\theta_q Vg_q(x)}\) and \(\Phi=\sum_q\Phi_q\). For a face jump mark \(\xi=V\Delta g\),
\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_q}{\Phi_q}
=\sum_{\text{jumps}}\text{rate}\,(e^{-\theta_q\xi}-1).
\]
When (g_q\le\ell_q), use 
\(e^{-z}-1\le-z+z^2e^{|z|}/2\), \(\theta_qJ_q\le1\), and (e<3):
\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_q}{\Phi_q}
\le V\{-\theta_q\mu_q+(3/2)\theta_q^2B_q\}
\le -V\theta_q\mu_q/2\le0.
\]
When (g_q>\ell_q), its positive generator contribution is at most
\[
V\Lambda(e^{\theta_qJ_q}-1)e^{-\theta_qV\ell_q}
\le2V\Lambda e^{-\theta_qV\ell_q}.
\]
Summing over (M) faces gives \(\mathcal L_t\Phi\le2MV\Lambda e^{-c_*V}\) everywhere before exit.

At the first exit some face gap is negative, so \(\Phi(X_{\tau_Q})\ge1\). Every face overshoots by at most (J_q/V), giving the stopped bound \(\Phi(X_{t\wedge\tau_Q})\le\sum_q e^{\theta_qJ_q}\le3M\). Before exit the total hazard is bounded by (V\Lambda), so the stopped process has no finite-time explosion. Dynkin's formula for predictable intensities yields
\[
\mathbb E\Phi(X_{T\wedge\tau_Q})
\le\Phi(X_0)+2MV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V}.
\]
Nonnegativity and the exit lower bound prove the theorem. The argument uses no controller Markov property and handles jumps across box faces directly.

## Worked two-site example (mathematical model only)

Take one species, one edge, \(\alpha_1=1,\alpha_2=3\), local immigration \(\varnothing\to A\) at rate coefficient 1, local removal \(A\to\varnothing\) at coefficient 1, and symmetric edge coefficient (c=1/10). Use (Q=[1/2,3/2]^2), collar width (1/4) on all four faces, and (X_0=(1,1)). Reaction gap margins on these collars are (1/4). The exchange loss is at most (1/40) at site 1 and (1/120) at site 2, so
\[
\bar\gamma_1=9/40,\qquad\bar\gamma_2=29/120.
\]
There is no factorial correction ((D_q=0)). The quadratic and total activity bounds are
\[
B_1=14/5,\quad B_2=13/15,\quad J_1=1,\quad J_2=1/3,
\quad\Lambda=103/10.
\]
Thus
\[
\theta_1=3/224,\quad\theta_2=29/624,\quad c_*=3/896,
\quad c_0=3/448.
\]
For every integer (V\ge1),
\[
\Pr\{\tau_Q\le e^{3V/1792}\}
\le 2e^{-3V/448}+2e^{-29V/1248}
 +(412/5)V e^{-3V/1792}.
\]
At (V=20{,}000), the displayed bound is about (4.8\times10^{-9}) over a horizon of about (3.5\times10^{14}) rate-time units. This is not an observed material result. The corresponding rate-(V\Lambda\) thinning envelope would permit about (7\times10^{19}) candidate events over that horizon, so direct simulation is infeasible. Birth/death exchange with symmetric hopping has a product-Poisson stationary law, and the irreducible chain eventually visits states outside (Q) almost surely; the theorem is explicitly finite-time only.

## Application boundary and decisive test

One possible interface is an adsorption/diffusion layer with local concentration limits. The conditional model inputs are: voxel volumes \(\alpha_iV\); reaction complexes and bounded, possibly feedback-controlled rate coefficients; edge geometry and reciprocal hopping coefficients; the safe interval \([a_s,b_s]\); a measured inward reaction margin on each collar; and an initial buffer. The local maxima can be measured by operando concentration maps; reaction rates by short-window event counts; migration coefficients by tracer pulse/chase or frequency-dependent impedance only after checking the finite-volume rate law. The certificate fails if any operating mode lacks the required face margin, if a measured edge is nonreciprocal, or if observed departures exceed the bound under a calibrated model.

The physical model gap is substantial: real electrodes have nonideal activities, correlated hopping, evolving interfaces, spatially varying volumes, temperature/SOC-dependent rates, and often no strict reaction restoring drift near a safety cap. A fixed graph and fixed (V) are assumed. No new material law, nature-level battery prediction, clinical/engineering recommendation, or physical experiment is claimed. Parameter calibration, mesh choice, full-history controller implementation, feedstock and drive energy, reaction turnover, instrument resolution, and graph-growth scaling all remain charged. Since the useful bound is exponentially small, empirically detecting a violation may require many trials; the theorem can serve as a model-based design certificate after input validation, not as proof from a small test set.
