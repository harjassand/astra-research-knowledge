1. Result and interface
----------------------
The input is one exact raw binary trajectory X_1,...,X_n from a stationary
binary renewal source. Its iid positive-integer interarrival law mu has
arbitrary finite mean; there is no uniform mean, tail, support, mixing,
reset, event oracle, or independent training path. The estimator may use the
entire observed path and unlimited computation. It returns a categorical
distribution qhat_n(.|X^n) for the actual same-trajectory continuation
X_{n+1}.

Define the path-conditional terminal KL risk

  R_n(mu,x) = KL(Ber(p_mu(x)) || Ber(qhat_n(1|x))),
  p_mu(x) = P_mu(X_{n+1}=1 | X^n=x).

This is a random variable through X^n. It is not the realized one-step log
score, the expected terminal KL E_{X^n} R_n(mu,X^n), or risk on an
independent stationary test history. Logs below are natural. In particular,
conditional on X^n the expectation of the realized log-score regret over
X_{n+1} equals R_n; averaging that identity over X^n gives E R_n, while
neither identity equates R_n with the realized score on a particular
continuation.

For the explicit type-first suffix predictor in
`../renewal_integer_code/RESULT.txt`, put

  B_n = [pi sqrt(2n/3) + 2 log(n+1) + log 2 + log(n+2)]/n.

Its proved expected-risk bound and Markov's inequality give, at each fixed n,

  sup_mu P_mu{R_n(mu,X^n) > epsilon} <= min(1, B_n/epsilon).

Thus its fixed-n (1-delta) risk threshold is at most B_n/delta, which is
O(1/(delta sqrt(n))). This is a horizon-specific exact rational predictor
using all n observed bits, not a fresh-history estimator.

Conversely, let a_n=2 ceil(8 sqrt(n)). There are absolute constants

  c_* = (log 2 - 1/2)/18 > 0,   c_0 = 1/160,

such that for every n>=40000 and every possibly randomized full-path
predictor qhat_n,

  sup_{mu: finite mean} P_mu{R_n(mu,X^n) >= c_*/sqrt(n)} >= c_0.       (1)

The lower-bound sources are finite-support renewal laws, but their supports
and means grow with n. Define

  q_{n,delta}=inf_{qhat_n} inf{r: sup_mu P_mu(R_n>r)<=delta},

where the outer infimum ranges over all full-path predictors. Consequently,
for every fixed delta<c_0 this minimax fixed-n confidence quantile is bounded
below by c_*/sqrt(n) and above by B_n/delta. In particular its order is
Theta_delta(n^-1/2). This matches the
expected-KL minimax order at every fixed confidence below c_0. It rules out a
uniform o(n^-1/2) high-confidence terminal conditional-risk rate at those
confidence levels, even for an unrestricted full-path baseline.

It is a per-n minimax assertion: the hard prior depends on n. It does not
produce one fixed renewal law with bad events infinitely often, and it does
not establish a time-uniform confidence sequence.

2. Explicit lower-bound prior and event
---------------------------------------
Fix n>=40000 and write a=a_n. Draw S_L uniformly among the (a/2)-subsets of
[a], independently draw S_H uniformly among the (a/2)-subsets of
{a+1,...,2a}, and set S=S_L union S_H. Let mu_S be uniform on S. Each source
has finite support and finite mean

  m_S = (1/a) sum_{g in S} g in [a/2,2a].

Write s_j(S)=mu_S{g>=j}. The stationary forward-wait/age bias gives

  P_mu(k=n+1-K | mu) = s_k(S)/m_S,

where K is the last observed renewal position in X^n. If K exists, k is the
number of positions from K through n+1. Let w be the first observed 1's
position, let g_1,...,g_R be all complete observed gaps between 1s, and let
A be their set of distinct values. The exact stationary block likelihood is

  P_mu(x) = [s_w(S)/m_S] [product_{i=1}^R mu_S(g_i)] s_k(S).            (2)

For the all-zero block it is

  P_mu(0^n) = m_S^{-1} sum_g (g-n)_+ mu_S(g).

The two censoring terms in (2) are retained. In particular, no step treats
the terminal residual k as an unweighted draw from mu. Since every high
support point is greater than a, s_k(S)>=1/2 whenever k<=a.

Let m0=floor(sqrt(n)). Define the path event E_n by all three requirements:

  (i)  K exists and k<=a;
  (ii) at most sqrt(n) complete gaps between observed renewals are visible;
  (iii) k is absent from the last min(m0,R) complete gaps read backwards
       from K.

For each S, the equilibrium residual law gives

  P_mu(k<=a) = sum_{k=1}^a s_k(S)/m_S >= a/(4a)=1/4,                 (3)

because s_k>=1/2 and m_S<=2a. Here n>=a, so all k=1,...,a are observable
terminal distances. This is the stationary length-bias contribution.

For (ii), if more than sqrt(n) complete gap occurrences are visible, then at
least m0 backwards iid gaps G_1,...,G_m0 have sum at most n. Uniformly over
the prior, G_i lies in [1,2a] and E G_i>=a/2>=8 sqrt(n). For n>=4,
m0>=sqrt(n)/2, so E sum>=4n. Hoeffding's inequality yields

  P{sum_{i=1}^{m0}G_i<=n} <= exp(-sqrt(n)/72).                         (4)

For (iii), use the Palm construction of a stationary renewal process at an
event K. Conditional on source S and an event at K, the complete gaps
immediately preceding K, read backwards, are iid mu_S. The following gap is
independent of this backward sequence. The condition that K is the last
renewal in X_1^n with terminal residual k only imposes that the following
gap is at least k; it does not change the law of the preceding iid gaps.
Hence, conditional on S and k, write those preceding gaps as
G^-_1,G^-_2,... iid mu_S. On event (ii), R<=sqrt(n), so R<=m0; all R
observed complete gaps are among the last m0 gaps G^-_1,...,G^-_{m0} in
this sequence. For each i, P(G^-_i=k|S,k)=mu_S(k)<=1/a. Thus, conditional
on S,k, event (ii) together with failure of (iii) is contained in the event
that k occurs among G^-_1,...,G^-_{m0}, whose probability is at most
m0/a. Since event (i) depends on k and is independent of these preceding
gaps conditional on S,k, this gives

  P{(i),(ii), failure of (iii)} <= (m0/a) P{(i)} <= 1/16.

From (3)-(4), for n>=40000,

  P(E_n) >= 1/4 - 1/16 - exp(-sqrt(n)/72) >= 1/8.                    (5)

The construction exploits a rare, unseen terminal gap type: the observed
complete gaps reveal at most sqrt(n) types, while the stationary residual
distance lies among the low half of a support with a=Theta(sqrt(n)) types.

3. Posterior balance, including both censoring factors
-----------------------------------------------------
For every prior-positive x in E_n, k<=a and k is not in A: since
R<=sqrt(n), the last min(m0,R) complete gaps are all the complete gaps in
x. Set T=1{k in S_L}. Its prior odds
are one. Let r_L=|A intersect [a]|. Since r_L<=sqrt(n)<=a/16, the
hypergeometric inclusion probabilities satisfy

  P(A subset S | T=1) / P(A subset S | T=0)
     = (a/2-r_L)/(a/2) in [7/8,1].                                   (6)

The high-set inclusion factor cancels, because T changes only S_L.

The remainder of the likelihood in (2), after factoring out a^{-R} and
the indicator A subset S, is s_k(S)s_w(S)/m_S = pi_k(S)s_w(S), where
pi_k=s_k/m_S. For every prior support and k<=a,

  1/(4a) <= pi_k(S) <= 2/a.                                         (7)

If w<=a, then 1/2<=s_w(S)<=1, so for any two conditional support groups
the ratio of their conditional expectations of pi_k s_w is in [1/16,16].
If w>a, s_w depends only on the high support S_H. Conditional on the
observed high gaps, S_H has the same law under T=0 and T=1; applying (7)
again bounds the expectation ratio in [1/8,8]. Thus the conservative
bound [1/16,16] applies in all cases with positive likelihood. Combining
with (6), Bayes' rule gives

  7/128 <= P(T=1|X^n=x) / P(T=0|X^n=x) <= 16.

Consequently, both posterior probabilities are at least 1/20. The argument
uses the complete stationary likelihood (2), including the left initial
wait s_w/m_S and the right terminal survival s_k; it does not drop either
censoring factor.

4. From posterior ambiguity to a conditional-risk tail
------------------------------------------------------
At the terminal history x, the true next-one hazard under S is

  p_S(x)=mu_S(k)/s_k(S).

If T=0, p_S(x)=0. If T=1, mu_S(k)=1/a and s_k<=1, so p_S(x)>=1/a.
Fix any output q=qhat_n(1|x), including an output after fixing the
learner's internal random seed.

* If q>=1/(2a), then on the posterior event T=0,
  KL(Ber(0)||Ber(q))=-log(1-q)>=q>=1/(2a).
* If q<1/(2a), then on T=1, q<p_S(x)/2. The elementary inequality
  (1-p)log((1-p)/(1-q))>=q-p gives
  KL(Ber(p)||Ber(q))>=p log(p/q)+q-p. The right-hand side is decreasing
  in q for q<p, so it is at least p(log 2-1/2) when q<=p/2. Therefore
  the risk is at least (log 2-1/2)/a.

Since log 2-1/2<1/2, in either case the posterior probability of
R_n(mu_S,x)>=(log 2-1/2)/a is at least 1/20. As
a=2 ceil(8 sqrt(n))<=18 sqrt(n), this threshold is at least c_*/sqrt(n).
Average over x in E_n and then over the finite prior, using (5):

  E_{S}[ P_{mu_S}{R_n>=c_*/sqrt(n)} ] >= (1/8)(1/20)=1/160.

At least one fixed support S therefore attains the lower bound (1). This
averaging argument is valid for every full-history algorithm, randomized or
deterministic; it is not restricted to a sufficient statistic, renewal-gap
estimator, or computational class. It also covers the strongest meaningful
matched baseline: any predictor supplied the same raw n bits and no source
parameter/oracle. A source-aware oracle knows p_S(x) and has zero risk, but
has strictly more information and is not a matched baseline.

This is a high-probability strengthening of the Bayes ambiguity mechanism
in Han--Jiang--Wu Appendix E, not a new prior family. Their paper proves the
expected minimax lower bound; the threshold case split above turns posterior
mass on T=0 and T=1 into a tail lower bound. No novelty or priority claim is
made.

5. Conditional risk, realized score, and independent histories
--------------------------------------------------------------
The conditional risk R_n is computed before observing X_{n+1}; its mean over
the training path is the expected terminal conditional-KL risk. Define the
realized one-step log-score regret

  Lambda_n = log( P_mu(X_{n+1}|X^n) / qhat_n(X_{n+1}|X^n) ).

The same prior argument gives the weaker but distinct bound

  sup_mu P_mu{Lambda_n>=c_*/sqrt(n)} >= 1/(2880 sqrt(n)).               (8)

Indeed, if q>=1/(2a), T=0 gives X_{n+1}=0 and score at least 1/(2a)
with posterior probability >=1/20. If q<1/(2a), T=1 has posterior mass
>=1/20 and then X_{n+1}=1 with probability p>=1/a, producing score
log(p/q)>log 2. Averaging over E_n gives probability >=1/(160a), and
a<=18sqrt(n) yields (8). This is not a constant-probability score tail.

An independent-history test is another quantity. For example, one could
train on X^n and evaluate the trained rule on an independent stationary
history X'^n, using

  KL(P_mu(X'_{n+1}|X'^n) || qhat_n(.|X'^n; X^n)).

Neither the expected-risk bound nor (1) is a statement about this metric.
Here the same raw trajectory is both the observed context and the input to
the terminal conditional-risk random variable. No independent-history
guarantee is established.

6. Counterexamples and scope limits
-----------------------------------
An expected bound by itself cannot imply pathwise control. Take the
stationary iid Bernoulli(1/2) process, which is a renewal process with
geometric gaps mu(j)=2^{-j} and finite mean 2. Define a predictor that
outputs qhat_n(1|x)=exp(-n) on x=0^n and 1/2 on every other path. Its risk
is zero off 0^n, while

  P(X^n=0^n)=2^{-n},
  R_n(0^n)=n/2-log 2-(1/2)log(1-exp(-n))=Theta(n).

Its expected risk is O(n 2^{-n}), despite a linear pathwise loss on the
all-zero history. This explicit example only refutes a deterministic
pathwise inference from an expected bound. The prior lower bound (1) is
stronger for minimax confidence: it forces constant-probability conditional
risk at scale n^{-1/2} for every full-path predictor.

The anytime predictor in `../renewal_anytime/RESULT.txt` has an expected
terminal-risk guarantee at every prefix length. This fixed-n lower-bound
prior changes with n, so (1) does not imply that one fixed source has bad
prefixes infinitely often. A useful time-uniform high-probability bound, or
a time-uniform lower obstruction for one fixed source, is UNKNOWN. Applying
Markov separately with a summable failure budget gives only thresholds that
need not vanish, and proves no stronger anytime statement.

7. Cost, exact diagnostic, and falsifiers
-----------------------------------------
The upper learner reads n exact binary symbols once and uses no test sample.
For all suffix predictions together, the type-first exact DP uses
O(n^3) coefficient/summand updates, O(n^2) cells with O(n)-bit integer
coefficients, and O(n^3 log n) bits for the exact final mixture; its
arithmetic is polynomial bit complexity and has no numerical-rounding
error. A conservative schoolbook bound is O(n^7 log^2 n) bit operations
when O(n^3) updates manipulate O(n^2 log n)-bit rational intermediates.
The lower bound is information-theoretic and applies even at unlimited
computation. It is about one next-symbol prediction, not parameter
identification or reconstruction of the renewal law.

The exact finite diagnostic is

  python3 work/programmes/acquisition_learning/cycle4/renewal_terminal_tail/check_posterior.py

It enumerates every support and every block for (n,a)=(1,4),(2,4),(3,6),
(4,6) with Fraction arithmetic; checks stationary block normalization;
and checks that both posterior T masses exceed 1/20 on every enumerated
E_n path. `CHECKS.json` records PASS. These tests check the likelihood
transcription on small instances only; (1)-(7) are proved analytically.

Direct falsifiers for review are: a positive-probability E_n block whose
exact stationary posterior violates the [7/128,16] odds interval; a support
family for which any step in (3)-(7) fails; or a full-path predictor at
some n>=40000 whose worst-case probability of R_n>=c_*/sqrt(n) is below
1/160. The first two falsify this proof; the third falsifies its theorem.

8. Limited inspection of Sol's hidden-mark compiler gate
-------------------------------------------------------
I inspected the actual `outputs/research/sol_hidden_renewal/RESULT.txt`,
`check.py`, and `CHECKS.json`. The artifact says its exact projected
hidden-mark prefix mass is #P-hard for q=2, by reducing it to a factorial-
weighted hidden-path word sum and then a graph partition function. Its
finite checker reports 16 tensor-transform cases, 24 padding identities,
12 interpolation recoveries, 6 medial-graph identities, and 3 source
partition recoveries. I have not independently audited its all-input
hardness proof or those reduction details.

The additional obstacle relative to the observed-mark DP is that the
observed compiler is handed the full transition-gap count tensor
c_{i,d,g}, the outgoing row totals, and the last mark. With hidden marks,
all state paths compatible with the binary event times must instead be
summed. The factorial weights couple repeated uses of each (origin,
destination,gap) label, while row-factorial denominators and terminal
survival depend on global visit totals and the hidden last mark. The current
hidden state alone is not a sufficient DP key; an exact key must retain
latent transition-count information. Sol's artifact claims #P-hardness for
exact evaluation of this specific projected code prefix and gives an
exp(O(q sqrt(N))) count-vector fallback, with polynomial leaves for bounded
distinct observed gap types. This is a reported gate plus finite checks,
not an independent validation of the hidden-mark theorem.

for models with infinite memory, with applications to hidden Markov and
renewal processes,” arXiv:2404.15454v1, Theorem 4 and Appendix E. Theorem 4
