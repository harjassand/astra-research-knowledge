CYCLE 3: FINITE-SAMPLE IMPOSSIBILITY FOR A ONE-TRAJECTORY SEQUENTIAL RISK CERTIFICATE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: theorem proved for the stated finite-horizon binary prediction interface; UNKNOWN for a supplied uniform-in-N mixing/rank envelope and for pointwise eventual certification.

1. Finding and scope
--------------------
Without a supplied state/rank cap, quantitatively useful uniform mixing/minorization bound, or an independent validation trajectory, no sequential procedure can give a nontrivial uniform finite-sample certificate of next-symbol classification risk from one raw trajectory over all finite-state hidden processes. This remains true when every conditional next-symbol probability has a known positive floor. For every finite observation budget N, two time-homogeneous finite-state hidden Markov sources have identical, full-support laws on the complete observed prefix Y_1,...,Y_N, while their predictive laws at time N are separated. A uniform level-delta certificate that stops by N can therefore be issued with probability at most 2 delta on this hard pair.

The claim concerns a rank-free learner and the semantic prediction target, not recovery of a hidden-state naming or a particular matrix realization. The proof does not use path-DFA query or equivalence-oracle access, nor the Cycle-2 adaptive-action construction. It uses one uncontrolled raw trajectory, and the learner receives every observed symbol.

The theorem is for conditional one-step 0-1 risk after the realized data-dependent prediction. The counterexample itself is stationary and irreducible/aperiodic; the result does not settle subclasses with a supplied uniform mixing rate, fixed rank/state cap, or other structural promise. It also makes no claim that pointwise eventual certification on each fixed source is impossible. Independent fresh trajectories change the contract and may enable confidence sequences.

2. Interface and exact target
-----------------------------
The observation alphabet is {0,1}. A source P is a time-homogeneous finite-state hidden Markov output process with a specified initial hidden-state distribution and an emission law on {0,1}. The learner sees one raw prefix, in order, with no reset, chosen intervention, word-probability query, feature dictionary, Hankel entry, or equivalence oracle. It may keep all observations and use arbitrary computation. The lower bound continues to hold if the learner is told N, mu, and that the source belongs to the two-element parity family; only the sign sigma is hidden.

At a stopping time tau, based on the observed prefix and private learner randomness U, the learner outputs a deterministic binary predictor g_tau in {0,1} for Y_(tau+1) and may emit a certificate C_tau. Its target is the conditional one-step misclassification risk

    r_P(tau,g_tau) = P_P(Y_(tau+1) != g_tau | Y_1,...,Y_tau, U).

Conditioning on U makes the target the risk of the realized predictor. A certificate claims r_P <= epsilon. A level-delta guarantee over a class P means

    sup_(P in P) P_P(C_tau=1 and r_P(tau,g_tau)>epsilon) <= delta.

The stopping rule may inspect all prefix windows or any predictive likelihood ratios. It may stop at any tau <= N; at most one certificate is counted. The theorem also applies to a fixed horizon as a special case.

3. Matched stationary finite-state source pair
-----------------------------------------------
Fix any N >= 1 and 0 < epsilon < 1/2. Choose rational mu with 0 < mu < epsilon and put theta=1/2-mu. A hidden state is an N-bit context h=(h_1,...,h_N) in {0,1}^N. Its observation is its last bit. Under sign sigma in {+1,-1}, from context h the hidden chain transitions to (h_2,...,h_N,b) with probability

    P_sigma(b=1 | h) = 1/2 + sigma theta chi(h),
    chi(h) = (-1)^(h_1+...+h_N),
    P_sigma(b=0 | h) = 1 - P_sigma(b=1 | h).

Here b is the next observation, equivalently the last coordinate of the next context; the N=1 case is the one-bit context (b). Start H_1 in its uniform distribution on {0,1}^N. This is a standard finite-state HMM with a deterministic state-output map. Call the two output sources P_+ and P_-. The predictive/Hankel rank is finite and at most 2^N; no rank bound is supplied to the learner.

Uniform contexts are stationary. For a destination v=(v_1,...,v_N), its two predecessors are 0v_1...v_(N-1) and 1v_1...v_(N-1). Their parities are opposite, so the probabilities of appending v_N sum to 1 under either sign. Thus the total incoming stationary mass at v is 2^(-N). All transition probabilities are in [mu,1-mu]; the context de Bruijn graph is irreducible, and the all-zero context has a positive self-loop, so the stationary chain is aperiodic as well.

The process is stationary, and every N-consecutive-symbol block is a uniform context: after N-1 transitions, H_N=(Y_1,...,Y_N), while H_N has the uniform stationary law. Consequently, for every binary string y of length k <= N,

    P_+(Y_1...Y_k=y) = P_-(Y_1...Y_k=y) = 2^(-k).

The complete raw N-symbol likelihoods are equal and strictly positive for all 2^N strings. The likelihood ratio is exactly 1 on every observed prefix through N; KL and total-variation distances between the two N-prefix data laws are zero. A full-data decoder, all overlapping windows, and every statistic therefore receive exactly the same information under the pair.

If k<N symbols have been seen, the next symbol is still inside an N-symbol block whose joint law is uniform, so its conditional law given the shorter prefix is Bernoulli(1/2). At k=N, the context is the observed word h, and

    P_sigma(Y_(N+1)=1 | Y_1...Y_N=h) = 1/2 + sigma theta chi(h).

For any binary prediction g, the two terminal risks are

    r_sigma(N,h,g) = 1/2 + (1-2g) sigma theta chi(h),
    r_+(N,h,g) + r_-(N,h,g) = 1.

As mu < epsilon < 1/2, at time N one source gives risk mu and the other risk 1-mu for each fixed h and g. Before N, both sources give risk 1/2 > epsilon. The conditional next-symbol probability for every observable history, including histories longer than N, is at least mu. Thus the obstruction does not rely on zero-probability strings or a missing likelihood floor.

4. Theorem and proof
--------------------
Theorem (uniform finite-horizon sequential certificate obstruction).
For the interface above, let an arbitrary possibly randomized learner stop and emit at most one certificate by time N. Let q be its probability of emitting a certificate by N under either P_+ or P_-. These probabilities are equal because the complete N-prefix laws are identical. Let B_sigma be the event that a certificate is emitted and its claimed risk is false under P_sigma. Then

    P_+(B_+) + P_-(B_-) >= q,
    max{P_+(B_+), P_-(B_-)} >= q/2.

Consequently, if the learner is uniformly level-delta valid over these finite-state sources, then q <= 2 delta. This holds for every N, so there is no finite sample bound, uniform over unrestricted finite-state source size, for issuing the certificate with probability greater than 2 delta.

The obstruction includes a genuinely good target instance: the parity classifier g_+(h)=1 when chi(h)=+1 and 0 otherwise has terminal risk mu at every context under P_+, and risk 1-mu under P_-. Yet the entire N-symbol training law is the same under both. A uniformly valid procedure must therefore withhold a high-probability certificate even on this good P_+ instance; it cannot identify which sign is present from the data it retained.

Proof. Couple the learner's private random seed and raw observations through time N under P_+ and P_-. The observed prefixes have identical laws, so the learner has the same joint distribution of stopping time, certificate decision, and predicted label under both. Partition certificates into those issued at tau<N and those issued at tau=N. Every early certificate is false under both sources because its conditional risk is 1/2 > epsilon. At tau=N, for each realized predicted label g, the two true risks sum to 1, so exactly one is greater than epsilon. Hence, summing the two false-certificate probabilities counts every early certificate twice and every terminal certificate at least once. This proves P_+(B_+)+P_-(B_-) >= q, and the maximum is at least half the sum. If each false-certificate probability is at most delta, q <= 2 delta. The construction is finite-state for each N, so taking N arbitrarily large rules out any uniform finite stopping/sample cap. QED.

Randomized probability-valued predictors do not evade the result if their action is a randomized binary prediction: condition on the realized action, as in the interface. Alternatively, if the report is the Bernoulli parameter and loss is expected 0-1 loss over that random action, the two terminal risks still sum to 1, while early risk is 1/2. The result also holds if the learner reports more than one statistic: the certificate event is still a function of the same complete prefix.

Transcriptwise version (zero-error certificates). For any fixed finite transcript x_1...x_n, make two deterministic finite-state sources that emit exactly x_1...x_n, then emit 0 forever or 1 forever. Both assign likelihood 1 to that transcript; their next-symbol laws at n have total-variation distance 1. Any deterministic learner that certifies on that transcript is wrong under one source. If a randomized learner certifies there with conditional probability p, the sum of its false-certificate probabilities across the pair is p, so one is at least p/2. This version needs no stochastic emissions; the stationary context-pair theorem above additionally supplies a positive conditional-likelihood floor and handles every stopping time through N under a common full-support prefix law.

5. Why post-selection diagnostics do not help
---------------------------------------------
A stopped predictor, a sequence of prefix-window estimates, a likelihood-ratio threshold, and an arbitrary computation on the retained sample all see the same complete N-prefix under P_+ and P_-. On its support, every prefix likelihood ratio is exactly 1. Overlapping windows reuse observations and create no independent validation sample. The theorem even grants the learner the full raw-data likelihood, hidden-source family, and optimal decoder; indistinguishability, not computational weakness, is the obstruction.

A time-uniform confidence sequence is valid only under its stated probabilistic conditions. For example, confidence-sequence constructions in the literature assume martingale/concentration conditions and cannot be invoked as distribution-free certificates for this unrestricted finite-state source class. In the cycle-2 rank-free conditional construction, explicit rank cap, witness horizon, singular-value gap, and mixing promises were supplied. Those are substantive restrictions, not conclusions one can infer for free from a single prefix.

6. Costs and baseline
---------------------
The strongest fixed-schedule/full-retention baseline receives the same N raw symbols before predicting. It may retain all N bits, enumerate all prefix windows, calculate exact empirical predictive likelihoods, or use an unrestricted decoder. The two source laws on that whole retained object are still identical. Its testing error for distinguishing P_+ from P_- under equal priors is exactly 1/2 because total variation is zero. A sequential learner that stops earlier receives a deterministic prefix of the same stream; it does not obtain a fresh sample by recomputing windows.

Resource accounting for the displayed pair:
- A cap-N run reads at most N one-bit observations, costing at most N c_Y; a fixed-N full-retention learner stores N bits, while a streaming learner can store its counters and predictor state. Both receive one trajectory with the same stationary initialization; no additional reset is available. If starting that trajectory costs c_start (or c_R), charge it once to both. The learner emits a one-bit class label and a certificate flag, plus a time index of ceil(log2(N+1)) bits if the stopping time must be transmitted.
- No reset or independent validation trajectory is included. If a protocol supplies an independent trajectory, each new trajectory/reset and every observed symbol must be charged separately; that is a different information interface.
- The stationary adversarial pair uses 2^N hidden contexts. An explicit transition table costs O(N 2^N) bits (each row has two outgoing edges); a succinct parity-rule encoding uses O(N + log(1/mu)) bits. The unrestricted class supplies neither representation nor a state/rank cap. If a state cap D is supplied, this pair applies when 2^N <= D; no conclusion is claimed for longer horizons under that cap.
- The observable conditional-likelihood floor is mu. The stationary context chain has an explicit N-step minorization: for every initial context h and destination v, P_sigma^N(h,v) >= mu^N, so P_sigma^N(h,.) >= alpha_N nu(.) for uniform nu and alpha_N=(2mu)^N. This is exponentially small in N when mu is fixed below 1/2; there is no supplied uniform-in-N mixing bound. A theorem using a known fixed minorization or mixing envelope must charge that promise, and its constants cannot be treated as free. A positive likelihood floor alone does not exclude this pair.
- Reading Y_(N+1) costs another c_Y but does not create a uniform future guarantee: instantiate the construction with a larger context length, so the split moves beyond the added observations. Every finite budget has a finite-state pair that delays the predictive distinction past that budget. The counterexample's growing state-description cost is explicit; the no-uniform-bound statement allows the hidden state count to grow with the proposed data budget.
