# Cycle 2 report: what equilibrium leaves unidentified in nucleation rates

## Result

For a finite reversible Arrhenius Markov model, the low-temperature escape-rate exponent is a minimum-over-paths, maximum-over-edges functional of the **transition** levels. It is not identified by the Gibbs law, state energies, or the usual state-energy saddle. I proved this directly from the Dirichlet principle and gave a sharp static-only minimax counterexample. I also derived a finite-sample, dynamics-aware capacity-rate interval from edge transition counts, with an exact optional mapping to low-temperature barrier intervals.

This does not establish a new metastability principle or a material law. The result is an exact finite-graph reconstruction and an acquisition interface for standard reversible potential theory. The general path-cost formulation and escape exponent already appear in primary literature. The strongest practical consequence is a measurable sufficiency test: record kinetic transition hazards on a Markov-sufficient state graph; equilibrium sampling alone cannot close the rate prediction.

The earlier cycle-1 hard/soft Ising result is input context only. Cycle 2 replaces that single model-family comparison with an arbitrary finite-graph identifiability theorem and a rate-measurement interface.

## Exact claim and proof location

See [CYCLE2_PROOF.md](CYCLE2_PROOF.md) for all assumptions and proofs.

For finite connected $G=(V,E)$, disjoint nonempty $A,B$, fixed energies $E_x$, positive symmetric edge prefactors $a_e$, and edge transition levels $S_e$, take

\[
\mu_\beta(x)=e^{-\beta E_x},\qquad
q_\beta(x,y)=a_e e^{-\beta(S_e-E_x)}.
\]

Detailed balance gives conductance $c_\beta(e)=a_e e^{-\beta S_e}$. Define

\[
\gamma(S)=\min_{\omega:A\to B}\max_{e\in\omega}S_e,
\qquad E_A=\min_{x\in A}E_x.
\]

The proof gives the two-sided constant-factor bound

\[
C_1e^{-\beta\gamma(S)}\le\operatorname{cap}_\beta(A,B)
\le C_2e^{-\beta\gamma(S)},
\]

by Cauchy–Schwarz along a minimizing path and a trial cut formed by vertices reachable below the bottleneck. Thus the capacity exponent is $\gamma$ and the capacity-normalized rate $\operatorname{cap}(A,B)/\mu(A)$ has exponent $\gamma-E_A$. If the edge transition levels dominate endpoint state energies, the state-only communication saddle is a lower bound on $\gamma$; the missing kinetic slack can be arbitrarily large without a bound on the edge levels.

At fixed temperature the identifiability gap is exact: every positive symmetric conductance assignment $c_{xy}$ defines a reversible chain with the same supplied vertex measure by $q(x,y)=c_{xy}/\mu(x)$, and every reversible generator is represented this way. Gibbs data fix vertex masses while leaving one kinetic conductance for each allowed edge.

The sharp obstruction uses the path $0-1-2$, energies $(0,1,-1)$, unit prefactors, and $A=\{0\}, B=\{2\}$. Both models have the same complete Gibbs family, state-energy landscape, state saddle 1, and topology. Edge levels $(1,1)$ give exponent 1; $(1,1+\delta)$ give $1+\delta$. The exact mean hitting time is

\[
\mathbb E_0\tau_2=e^{\beta S_{01}}+e^{\beta S_{12}}(1+e^{-\beta}),
\]

confirming the same exponents. Any predictor using only their shared static inputs has worst-case exponent error at least \(\delta/2\). Since \(\delta\) is unrestricted, there is no finite uniform guarantee from static inputs alone. This is a lower bound on prediction, not merely the prior observation that two named Ising rules differ.

## Estimator and acquisition interface

The finite-temperature estimator observes both equilibrium weights and kinetic hazards. At fixed temperature, for every graph edge $e=\{x,y\}$, record the directed count $N_e$ from source $x$ over cumulative occupation time $t_e$. Under the CTMC model, $N_e\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(t_e q(x,y))$. For a count $n$, the exact two-sided Garwood rate limits are $q^- = \chi^2_{2n,\alpha_e/2}/(2t_e)$ (zero at $n=0$) and $q^+ = \chi^2_{2(n+1),1-\alpha_e/2}/(2t_e)$. Bonferroni allocation $\alpha_e=\alpha/m$ gives simultaneous conductance intervals $c^-_e\le c_e\le c^+_e$ with coverage at least $1-\alpha$. Monotonicity of Dirichlet capacity then gives

\[
\operatorname{cap}(c^-)/\mu(A)\le k_\beta(A,B)
\le\operatorname{cap}(c^+)/\mu(A)
\]

with the same coverage. Both endpoints require solving a finite weighted-Laplacian Dirichlet problem. This is a finite-sample confidence interval for a capacity-normalized rate at the measured temperature, without assuming Arrhenius prefactors.

When edge prefactors $a_e$ are independently known and the Arrhenius model is appropriate, transform the edge-rate intervals into transition-level intervals, then compute the lower and upper bottleneck-path heights. The resulting exponent interval is sharp given the edge interval box; its midpoint is the minimax absolute-error estimate, with radius half the interval width. A zero edge count gives zero lower rate bound and therefore no finite upper transition-level bound; an unobserved bottleneck cannot be certified away. If a prefactor is unknown, one-temperature data confound it with the transition level, so the asymptotic barrier exponent remains `UNKNOWN` unless prefactors are calibrated or several temperatures are measured.

For heterogeneous nucleation, the proposed concrete coarse state is two-coordinate $z=(n,j)$, where $n$ is the largest stable-phase cluster size and $j$ is a preregistered candidate nucleation zone. Estimate transition hazards on adjacent $(n,j)$ states, construct the weighted network, and predict the held-out capacity/rate interval. This is exact only under Markov sufficiency (strong lumpability suffices) and reversibility of the projected chain. Committor distributions, lag/Chapman–Kolmogorov checks, and a measured separation between intrabasin mixing and exit are admission tests. A broad committor conditional on $(n,j)$, non-exponential dwell times, or lag-dependent transition frequencies falsifies the two-coordinate model and calls for a shape, strain, defect, or history coordinate, or a return to the microscopic graph. The estimator predicts a first-nucleation rate for the finite sample; conversion to a per-volume rate requires a measured volume and a justified size-scaling or homogeneity assumption.

This coordinate choice is motivated by primary model evidence, not claimed as a universal prescription: Yao and Jack's 3D random-field Ising study found cluster size alone inadequate at moderate/strong disorder, improved committor concentration after localizing by nucleation site, and increasing disagreement of Becker–Döring predictions with direct forward-flux rates as disorder strengthened; their analysis attributes remaining deviations to cluster-shape fluctuations. The proposed hazard-capacity interval adds a direct transition-network estimator, but its methodological novelty has not been established.

## Costs, assumptions, and falsifiers

- **Representation:** the theorem assumes the complete finite Markov graph, all relevant transition edges, state energies, and fixed edge prefactors. A spin system with $N$ sites has $2^N$ microscopic states before graph reduction. A coarse graph is acquired and validated information, not automatically implied by the Hamiltonian.
- **Data:** transition estimation needs state-labelled events and cumulative source-state occupation exposure on each relevant edge. With a rare hazard $q_e\asymp e^{-\beta(S_e-E_x)}$, obtaining $r$ events costs exposure of order $r/q_e$, exponential in the edge barrier at low temperature. A count of zero gives an unbounded upper barrier interval. Aggregated Gibbs masses may require umbrella sampling or exhaustive energy-state counting; those costs are not included in the edge-count interval.
- **Compute:** bottleneck levels are computed by threshold connectivity, $O(|E|\log|E|)$; finite-temperature capacity endpoints require two sparse Laplacian/Dirichlet solves (dense direct worst-case $O(|V|^3)$). Full state graph acquisition dominates in many-body systems. The system's model energies are distinct from laboratory work and compute energy; no joule or physical-sample cost was measured.
- **Falsifier:** preregister $(n,j)$, measure its committor spread, residence-time law, lag-dependent transition probabilities, and intrabasin/exit timescale separation, then predict a held-out nucleation rate interval from measured hazards. If committors remain broad or Markov/local-equilibration tests fail, reject the two-coordinate model. If it passes admission but the held-out first-passage/FFS rate lies outside the capacity interval beyond its stated sampling coverage, reject at least one of the graph, rate, equilibrium-mass, or count-model assumptions. No such experiment was run in this workspace.
- **Nature boundary:** the chain theorem is conditional mathematics. It does not prove Arrhenius edge rates, reversibility, Markov sufficiency of size and location, local equilibration, disorder coverage, or a mapping to any real material. Those need independent measurement and validation.

## Verdict and next gate

**Mathematical result:** proved for the stated finite reversible Arrhenius graph. **Prior-art status:** the mechanism and escape exponent are established theory; the proof here is a transparent specialization plus a simple exact minimax non-identifiability witness. **Estimator:** exact finite-sample coverage conditional on the CTMC edge-count model; no physical-system validation. **Novelty:** no new theorem of nature or verified priority claim. 

The next decisive gate is a full-state or empirically lumpable $(n,j)$ graph for a named disordered nucleation model, with prefactor/energy acquisition, committor and lag tests, and a held-out rate prediction compared against forward-flux sampling. Until then, report `UNKNOWN` for material transfer.
