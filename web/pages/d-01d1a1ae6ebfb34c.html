1. EXACT STATEMENT AND CONVENTIONS

For m>=2, let sigma be a nonnegative Radon measure on R^m satisfying
  sigma(B_r(x))<=A r^(m-1) for EVERY x in R^m and EVERY r>0.  (1.1)
Let u in C_c^infinity(R^m) be scalar, real or complex. Then
  J(u)=integral sigma(dx) integral dy
           |u(y)-u(x)|^2/|x-y|^(m+1)
       <=C_m A integral |grad u|^2 dy,                      (1.2)
with a finite constant depending only on m. Infinite total sigma mass is
allowed. Every value at a sigma point is the actual smooth function value;
no representative of a Lebesgue-a.e. Sobolev class is substituted.
If A=0, sigma=0 and the conclusion is immediate. Below assume A>0.

Changing variables y=x+h rewrites the nonnegative extended integral as
  J(u)=integral dh/|h|^(m+1) ||u(.+h)-u||_(L^2(sigma))^2.  (1.3)
We will prove finiteness rather than assume it. Only ordinary Fourier
inversion, Plancherel, weighted Cauchy, Tonelli/Fatou, and summable sequence
convolution are used; the needed band trace estimate is proved here.

2. GROWTH ALONE GIVES THE BANDLIMITED TRACE ESTIMATE

Let g be a Schwartz function with Fourier support in |xi|<=2lambda.
Fix a smooth compactly supported Fourier cutoff eta equal to 1 on B2,
and let K be its inverse Fourier transform. Set K_lambda(x)=lambda^m K(lambda x).
Then g=K_lambda*g POINTWISE, and ||K_lambda||_1=||K||_1. Weighted Cauchy
gives
  |g(x)|^2<=||K||_1 integral |K_lambda(x-z)||g(z)|^2 dz.
For any fixed M>m-1, the Schwartz estimate and growth (1.1), summed over
the ball of radius lambda^-1 and the doubling annuli, give
  sup_z integral |K_lambda(x-z)| sigma(dx)
    <=C_m A lambda^m lambda^(-(m-1))
         [1+sum_{ell>=0}2^(-ell M)2^((ell+1)(m-1))]
    <=C_m A lambda.
All cutoffs and M can be fixed once for each dimension. Tonelli therefore
proves
  ||g||_(L^2(sigma))^2<=C_m A lambda ||g||_(L^2(dy))^2.     (2.1)
Every translate of sigma satisfies (1.1) with the SAME A. Thus (2.1)
also holds for g(.+v), uniformly in v. No geometric fact about sigma's
support has appeared, and all equality-of-function assertions are pointwise.

3. A BAND INCREMENT ESTIMATE

Every derivative of g has the same band support and its Lebesgue L2 norm
is bounded by 2lambda times ||g||_2. Along the segment from x to x+h,
the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy give
  |g(x+h)-g(x)|^2<=|h|^2 integral_0^1 |grad g(x+t h)|^2 dt.
Use Tonelli and (2.1) for the translated derivative to obtain the small-h
bound. Using (2.1) separately on g(.+h) and g gives the large-h bound.
Together,
  ||g(.+h)-g||_(L^2(sigma))
    <=C_m sqrt(A) lambda^(1/2)
                       min(1,lambda|h|)||g||_2.            (3.1)
The derivative and every translate are their Schwartz representatives.
No line-segment integral against arbitrary unlocalized gradients is used.

4. SUMMABLE PAIRING OF DIFFERENT FREQUENCIES

Fix a radial smooth cutoff theta equal to 1 on B1 and zero outside B2,
nonincreasing in radius. Put
  psi(xi)=theta(xi)-theta(2xi),
  g_j=Fourier^{-1}[psi(2^-j xi) Fourier(u)(xi)], j in Z.
Each g_j is Schwartz, with band support 2^(j-1)<=|xi|<=2^(j+1).
Set lambda_j=2^j and e_j=2^j||g_j||_2.

For the mixed bilinear form J_jk, Cauchy in L2(sigma) and (3.1) give
  |J_jk|<=C_m A 2^((j+k)/2)||g_j||_2||g_k||_2
       integral_0^infinity
        min(1,2^j r)min(1,2^k r) dr/r^2.                   (4.1)
The sphere area from dh is included in C_m. For 0<lambda<=Lambda,
splitting the scalar integral at Lambda^-1 and lambda^-1 gives EXACTLY
  integral_0^infinity min(1,lambda r)min(1,Lambda r) dr/r^2
       =lambda[2+log(Lambda/lambda)].                       (4.2)
In particular every J_jk is absolutely finite. It follows that
  |J_jk|<=C_m A e_j e_k kappa_(j-k),
  kappa_l=[2+|l|log 2] 2^(-|l|/2).                         (4.3)
The logarithmic factor is charged, not discarded. The exponential factor
is enough: kappa belongs to ell^1(Z), with a dimension-independent finite
sum. For ANY finite index set F, expand the quadratic form and use
Young/Cauchy for sequence convolution to obtain
  J(sum_{j in F}g_j)<=C_m A sum_{j in F}e_j^2.               (4.4)
Thus the constant is uniform in the number and separation of bands.

5. ENERGY AND THE LIMIT AT SINGULAR MEASURE POINTS

By Plancherel and the fixed finite overlap of the band supports,
  sum_j 2^(2j)||g_j||_2^2<=C_m ||grad u||_2^2.              (5.1)
Indeed on each support 2^j<=2|xi| and only a bounded number of multipliers
are nonzero at each xi; the multipliers are bounded by 1.

For u_M=sum_{j=-M}^M g_j, the summed multiplier telescopes to
  theta(2^-M xi)-theta(2^(M+1)xi),
lies between 0 and 1, and tends to 1 for every xi!=0. Since u is smooth
and compactly supported, its Fourier transform is Schwartz and in L1.
Dominated convergence in Fourier L1 gives u_M->u UNIFORMLY on R^m.
Hence each increment converges at EVERY sigma point and every h, not
merely Lebesgue almost everywhere. Fatou applied to the nonnegative
product measure in (1.3), then (4.4)--(5.1), gives
  J(u)<=liminf_M J(u_M)<=C_m A||grad u||_2^2.
This proves (1.2), including its possible-infinity formulation before the
proof. Global growth makes sigma sigma-finite; the h kernel is sigma-finite
away from h=0. There is no hidden finite-total-mass or convergence-in-trace
assumption, nor a passage from only Lebesgue L2 convergence at sigma points.
