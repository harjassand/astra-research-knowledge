THE COST OF CONDITIONAL HISTORY ACCESS
Status: exact finite-HMM calculations and testing bounds for the stated interfaces.

Setup and target
----------------
Fix a prefix length T >= 1, a survival parameter q in (0,1), and an emission
contrast delta in (0,1/2). Let h = 1^T and p = q^T. The two models H_+ and H_-
are identical except for a Bernoulli emission reached only after h. Conditional
on h, the next bit has probability 1/2+delta under H_+ and 1/2-delta under
H_-. Thus the target conditional laws have TV distance 2 delta. The history
has positive probability p under both models.

An explicit time-homogeneous HMM has hidden states A_1,...,A_T,
D_1,...,D_T,G,F,R. Its initial distribution is

  Pr(A_1)=q,  Pr(D_1)=1-q.

For t<T, A_t transitions to A_{t+1} with probability q and to D_{t+1} with
probability 1-q; D_t transitions to D_{t+1} with probability 1. Each A_t
emits the symbol 1, and each D_t emits 0. From A_T transition to G; from D_T
transition to F. State G emits Bernoulli(1/2+delta) in H_+ and
Bernoulli(1/2-delta) in H_-. State F emits 0. Both G and F then transition to
R; R emits fair bits and transitions to itself. All state emissions are
conditionally independent given the hidden path. The two HMMs have 2T+3
states and differ only in the emission law at G.

The observed prefix h occurs exactly when the hidden path stays in the A
states, so Pr(h)=q times q^(T-1)=p=q^T. Every other length-T prefix leaves
the process in the D path and has the same continuation under both models.
Writing b_+ = 1/2+delta and b_- = 1/2-delta,

  Pr_+(h,1)=p b_+,       Pr_-(h,1)=p b_-,
  Pr_+(h,0)=p(1-b_+),    Pr_-(h,0)=p(1-b_-).

The complete length-(T+1) laws therefore differ only on the witness history.
This example is deliberately simple and the witness h is known to the learner;
it isolates access cost from the separate problem of discovering which
history is predictive.

(i) Passive episodes: witness probability and generation cost
-------------------------------------------------------------
One passive episode is a reset followed by T+1 observations. Its witness
indicator W = 1{X_1:T=h} is Bernoulli(p) under both models. Conditional on W=1,
X_{T+1} is Bernoulli(b_+) or Bernoulli(b_-); conditional on W=0, the whole
episode law is identical under H_+ and H_-.

The exact one-episode divergences are

  TV(P_+^episode, P_-^episode) = 2 p delta,
  KL(P_+^episode || P_-^episode) = p d(delta),
  d(delta) = 2 delta log((1+2 delta)/(1-2 delta)) nats.

For n independent passive episodes, the KL is exactly n p d(delta). For any
test whose two error probabilities are at most alpha < 1/2, data processing
through the test output gives

  n >= kl(1-alpha || alpha) / (p d(delta)),
  kl(1-alpha || alpha) = (1-2 alpha) log((1-alpha)/alpha).

As delta -> 0, d(delta)=8 delta^2+O(delta^4). Hence for fixed alpha the
passive episode requirement is Omega(1/(p delta^2)); as alpha -> 0 the lower
bound is Omega(log(1/alpha)/(p delta^2)). Since p=q^T, this is exponential in
T for any fixed q<1. The lower bound holds even though h is known in advance.

The history-generation cost is also exact. The number of independent episodes
until the first witness is geometric with mean 1/p and variance
(1-p)/p^2. The number N_k of episodes until k witness continuations is
negative-binomial with

  E[N_k]=k/p,       Var(N_k)=k(1-p)/p^2.

Thus collecting k useful continuation observations costs k/p episodes in
expectation, or (T+1)k/p emitted symbols when each full episode is charged.
If the sampler can stop an attempt as soon as it observes the first 0, the
expected symbol cost is smaller and can be computed exactly. A failed attempt
whose first zero occurs at position t has probability (1-q)q^(t-1) and costs
t symbols; a successful attempt costs T+1 symbols. The expected cost to obtain
k successful witnesses under this early-stop policy is

  k(T+1) + k(1-q)/p * sum_{t=1}^T t q^(t-1).

For fixed q=1/2 and large T, this is k(T+1)+k*2^(T+1), still exponential
in T. Reset or restart costs, if any, add k(1-p)/p times the cost per failed
attempt. The full-episode symbol cost above applies when episodes cannot be
censored early.
For a fixed budget n, the witness count K is Binomial(n,p) and
Pr(K=0)=(1-p)^n; obtaining at least one witness with probability 1-beta
requires exactly n >= log(beta)/log(1-p), rounded up. More generally,
Pr(N_k <= n)=Pr(Binomial(n,p) >= k), which gives the exact high-probability
generation cost. A convenient sufficient budget is

  n >= (2 k + 8 log(1/beta))/p,

by the Chernoff lower-tail bound.

An exact finite-sample testing expression is available. Let e_m(delta) be the
optimal equal-prior error after m informative continuation bits:

  e_m(delta) = (1/2) sum_{j=0}^m C(m,j) min{
       b_+^j b_-^(m-j), b_-^j b_+^(m-j) }.

The likelihood-ratio test is majority vote, randomized on ties. With n passive
episodes, the optimal equal-prior error is exactly

  R_n = sum_{m=0}^n C(n,m) p^m (1-p)^(n-m) e_m(delta).

Consequently n_passive(alpha)=min{n:R_n<=alpha} is the exact episode count
for this test. The preceding KL bound gives its sharp rare-event and
small-contrast order.

For high-confidence asymptotics, let rho=sqrt(1-4 delta^2), the
Bhattacharyya coefficient of the two conditional Bernoulli laws. The
Chernoff information per passive episode is exactly

  C_passive(p,delta) = -log(1-p+p rho).

Thus the optimal equal-prior error exponent is C_passive, and
n_passive(alpha) is asymptotic to log(1/alpha)/C_passive as alpha tends to
zero. For small p, C_passive = p(1-rho)+O(p^2); for small delta,
1-rho = 2 delta^2+O(delta^4). This recovers the 1/p penalty at the sharp
small-contrast/high-confidence exponent level.

(ii) Conditional-sample oracle: the witness cost is omitted
-----------------------------------------------------------
Define O_cond(h) to return an independent draw from
Law(X_{T+1} | X_1:T=h), on every call, for any specified h with positive
probability. A call must at least receive the T-symbol history h (unless a
reusable handle is supplied) and returns one Bernoulli sample. The sample is
Bernoulli(b_+) under H_+ and Bernoulli(b_-) under H_-. After m calls, the exact
optimal error is e_m(delta), and the KL is
m d(delta). Therefore the exact query requirement at error alpha is

  m_cond(alpha)=min{m:e_m(delta)<=alpha},

with the necessary bound m >= kl(1-alpha||alpha)/d(delta). A majority test and
Hoeffding give the sufficient bound

  m >= log(1/alpha)/(2 delta^2).

The Chernoff information per conditional sample is

  C_cond(delta) = -(1/2) log(1-4 delta^2),

so the optimal error exponent is C_cond. Hence, as alpha tends to zero,
m_cond(alpha) is asymptotic to log(1/alpha)/C_cond(delta). For small delta,
C_cond=2 delta^2+O(delta^4), so conditional sampling costs
Theta(delta^-2 log(1/alpha)), with no factor 1/p. The asymptotic ratio of
passive to conditional sample counts is C_cond/C_passive, which is
approximately 1/p when delta is small. Relative to passive episodes, the
oracle has omitted the cost of generating the witness history and its
probability mass.

At fixed error and delta=1/4, one conditional sample already gives 3/4
success. For alpha=0.1, the exact formula gives m_cond=7: e_6=53/512>0.1,
whereas e_7=289/4096<0.1. This constant query count is independent of T even
when p=q^T is exponentially small.

This interface is a teacher if the learner may name h before h appears in its
own data and the oracle returns the continuation without charging for the
history's probability or generation. It supplies a counterfactual
history-conditioned observation that passive P-sampling almost never sees.
If a policy must first observe h before it may call O_cond(h), the expected
waiting cost k/p for k conditional draws remains and the oracle adds no such
free access. The history must satisfy P(h)>0; conditioning on an off-support
history is undefined and is not licensed by this construction.

(iii) Full law-evaluation oracle: exact ratios expose the conditional
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Define O_law(x) to return the exact joint probability of any requested
observation string x. The query string (h,1) has T+1 symbols. Since p=q^T is
known in this construction, one query at x=(h,1) returns
p(1/2+delta) or p(1/2-delta); a single exact answer
distinguishes the models. If p is not known, two queries, O_law(h) and
O_law(h,1), recover

  Pr(X_{T+1}=1 | h) = O_law(h,1) / O_law(h).

Thus exact law evaluation takes one query here (or two when the denominator is
unknown), independent of p, but this charges neither sampling nor the
precision needed to return a tiny joint probability. With additive error eta
on O_law(h,1) and known p, distinguishing the two models by this one answer
requires eta < p delta. A binary fixed-point answer therefore needs more than
For truncation error at most 2^-b, it suffices to take
b > log_2(1/(p delta)) fractional bits. For p=q^T this is T log_2(1/q) plus
log_2(1/delta) bits, up to rounding convention. If the law evaluator itself
estimates probabilities by sampling, or reports only additive accuracy much
larger than p delta, this constant-query conclusion no longer applies; the
rare-event cost returns.

Comparison and accounting boundary
-----------------------------------

Interface                         Informative access cost
--------------------------------  -----------------------------------------
Passive length-(T+1) episodes      n >= kl(1-alpha||alpha)/(q^T d(delta));
                                   expected k/q^T episodes for k witnesses
Conditional samples at h           m >= kl(1-alpha||alpha)/d(delta) calls;
                                   no q^-T cost if h can be named freely;
                                   each call specifies T history symbols
Exact full-law evaluation          1 query if q^T known, else 2 exact queries;
                                   input has T+1 symbols; precision resolves
                                   q^T delta

For q=1/2 and delta=1/4, the conditional continuation gap is 1/2 and
p=2^-T. At alpha=0.1, the passive episode-count lower bound is

  n >= [0.8 log 9 / (0.5 log 3)] 2^T ~= 3.20 * 2^T episodes,

whereas seven conditional samples suffice. Collecting seven passive witnesses
takes 7*2^T episodes in expectation. This costs 7(T+1)2^T emitted symbols
when episodes run to completion, or 7(T+1)+7*2^(T+1) expected symbols with
early stopping at the first zero. One exact full-law query returns a joint
probability separated by 2p delta=2^(-T-1),
so the query-count advantage hides an exponentially small probability scale
in its numeric answer.

Conclusion: conditional sampling does not merely repackage passive samples.
When the user can submit an arbitrary on-support prefix and receive a
continuation without paying for history generation, it supplies the missing
history-conditioned evidence. A fair comparison must charge either the
history's probability (passive episode generation), the query mechanism that
conditions on it, or the exact probability precision / computation of the
full-law evaluator. The lower bounds here are for this explicit HMM family;
they are not a general impossibility theorem for learning predictive state.
