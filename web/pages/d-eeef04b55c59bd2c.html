# Branch extinction under a mutation-specific collateral strike

## Candidate mechanism

The strongest concrete lead is a genotype-specific vulnerability at an intermediate resistance step in BCR–ABL1-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia (Ph+ ALL). Zhao et al. selected leukemia cells under BCR–ABL1 inhibitors and found that the dasatinib-resistance-associated BCR–ABL1 V299L mutant became collaterally sensitive to non-classical ABL1 inhibitors, including foretinib and vandetanib. The proposed mechanism is on-target: these compounds inhibited mutant ABL1 signaling (pSTAT5) and induced cell-cycle arrest in V299L cells, while the same effects were absent or much weaker in wild-type cells. In syngeneic mice bearing V299L leukemia, one week of foretinib or vandetanib reduced measured tumor burden by about three log units and extended median survival by three to four weeks; a subset had long-term survival without relapse. The paper also found that some compound mutants, notably V299L/F317L, attenuated the collateral sensitivity, while other tested compound alleles retained it. Thus the potentially removable bottleneck is the V299L intermediate before it produces or is replaced by a compound escape lineage.

Primary report: [Zhao et al., *Cell* (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5152932/). This is preclinical evidence of a mutation-specific drug vulnerability and tumor response. It does **not** estimate resistant-lineage birth/death rates or establish a branching-process extinction probability. A survival benefit, including long-term survival in some mice, is not by itself proof that the resistant clone was extinguished.

An alternative assay-ready mechanism is ribociclib resistance in ER+ CAMA-1 breast cancer cells: a downregulated G2/M checkpoint was associated with sensitivity to the WEE1 inhibitor adavosertib, and fluorescently labeled sensitive/resistant 3D spheroid cocultures showed reduced selection for the resistant cells under ribociclib plus adavosertib. This is useful precedent for clonal measurement, but it is one cell-line model and the study measured spheroid growth/composition rather than lineage extinction ([Grolmusz et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7301982/)). Later work used long-term alternating palbociclib and fulvestrant plus low-dose adavosertib in MCF7 cells and modeled delayed resistance; it was a single-line model and explicitly left cross-line and in-vivo generalization open ([He et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12624050/)).

## Exact two-phase branching calculation

Suppose one resistant founder is a linear continuous-time birth–death process during a collateral-drug pulse of length \(\tau\), with per-cell division rate \(\lambda_1\) and death rate \(\mu_1\). After the pulse, the original treatment resumes and the lineage has rates \(\lambda_2,\mu_2\). Assume cells reproduce independently, rates are constant within each phase, the resistance state is stable, and no new mutations or state switches occur. These assumptions isolate extinction of the *existing* resistant lineage.

For the post-pulse phase, the eventual extinction probability from one resistant cell is

\[
q_2=\begin{cases}1,&\lambda_2\leq\mu_2,\\[2pt]\mu_2/\lambda_2,&\lambda_2>\mu_2.\end{cases}
\]

The probability-generating function for the number of descendants at the end of the pulse, starting from one cell, is

\[
G_{\tau}(s)=\frac{\mu_1(1-s)-e^{-(\lambda_1-\mu_1)\tau}(\mu_1-\lambda_1s)}
{\lambda_1(1-s)-e^{-(\lambda_1-\mu_1)\tau}(\mu_1-\lambda_1s)},
\]

with the equal-rate limit \(G_{\tau}(s)=1-(1-s)/(1+\lambda_1\tau(1-s))\). If there are \(n\) independent founders, the probability that all their descendants ultimately go extinct is exactly

\[
P_{\rm ext}=G_{\tau}(q_2)^n,\qquad P_{\rm establish}=1-G_{\tau}(q_2)^n.
\]

This is the finite-pulse result; it is not the deterministic net-growth approximation. For an illustrative, *unfitted* example, set pulse rates \((\lambda_1,\mu_1)=(0.02,0.08)\) per day for 30 days, then \((\lambda_2,\mu_2)=(0.08,0.02)\). The post-pulse extinction probability is 0.25, and \(G_{30}(0.25)=0.89743\). Predicted establishment falls from 0.75 to 0.103 for one founder, but is still 0.661 for ten founders. At 100 founders the pulse barely helps (risk 0.99998). The key measured quantity is therefore the number of viable resistant founders at the trigger, not merely the resistant fraction or bulk-tumor nadir.

Net growth alone does not identify even this one-type risk. Keep the same post-pulse rates above and pulse duration, but compare pulse rates \((0.02,0.08)\) with \((0.5,0.56)\), both per day. Both have the same net rate \(b-d=-0.06\) and hence the same expected pulse endpoint, \(E[N(30)]=e^{-1.8}\), from one founder. Yet the exact establishment risks are 0.103 and 0.0199, respectively. The high-turnover process reaches zero more often while preserving the same mean. If the only observation is the mean population trajectory, separate division and death events (or another observation model that identifies their distribution) are required to identify branching risk.

### Minimal two-type extension

The smallest model that resolves reversible phenotype escape has two cell states: type 0, sensitive to the current treatment, and type 1, transiently tolerant/resistant. A type-i cell creates one additional same-type daughter at rate \(b_i(t)\), dies at rate \(d_i(t)\), and switches state at rate \(\sigma_i(t)\) (0→1 at \(\alpha\), 1→0 at \(\beta\)). For a constant phase, eventual extinction probabilities from one cell of each state are the least solution \((q_0,q_1)\in[0,1]^2\) to

\[
0=b_0(q_0^2-q_0)+d_0(1-q_0)+\alpha(q_1-q_0),\qquad
0=b_1(q_1^2-q_1)+d_1(1-q_1)+\beta(q_0-q_1).
\]

These equations follow by conditioning on the first birth, death, or switch event. For a finite phase with changing rates, define \(q_i(t)\) as the probability that a clone starting in state i at time t is eventually extinct under the future regimen. The exact backward equations are

\[
\dot q_0=(b_0+d_0+\alpha)q_0-b_0q_0^2-d_0-\alpha q_1,\qquad
\dot q_1=(b_1+d_1+\beta)q_1-b_1q_1^2-d_1-\beta q_0,
\]

with terminal values at the end of the pulse equal to the post-pulse extinction vector. Integrate backward through each phase. From \(n_0\) sensitive and \(n_1\) tolerant founders, \(P_{\rm ext}=q_0(0)^{n_0}q_1(0)^{n_1}\). This resolves two failures of the one-type law: resistant state can be entered during the pulse and can be left after it. One-way genotype mutation at division is a different transition mechanism; the offspring mixture equation in the next subsection gives its exact two-type PGF.

This is a stress-test extension, not an asserted feature of the V299L model. Fit \(\alpha,\beta\) only if reversible state changes are observed in the chosen leukemia cells; otherwise set them to zero and retain the V299L-to-compound mutation model.

Escape mutation during the pulse can also be included exactly. For a constant environment, let a V299L cell divide at rate \(\lambda_V\), die at \(\mu_V\), and at division produce two V cells with probability \(1-u\), or one V and one compound mutant M with probability \(u\). If \(q_M\) is M's eventual extinction probability in that environment, the smallest root in \([0,1]\) of

\[
\lambda_V(1-u)q_V^2+\bigl[\lambda_Vu q_M-\lambda_V-\mu_V\bigr]q_V+\mu_V=0
\]

is the V-lineage extinction probability. For a finite pulse followed by a different drug environment, use the two-type probability-generating functions instead: during the pulse, \(\dot F_V=\lambda_V[(1-u)F_V^2+uF_VF_M-F_V]+\mu_V(1-F_V)\) and \(\dot F_M=\lambda_M(F_M^2-F_M)+\mu_M(1-F_M)\), with initial arguments \((s_V,s_M)\). Evaluate the resulting pulse PGF at the post-pulse extinction vector \((q_V^{(2)},q_M^{(2)})\); from \(n\) V founders, the extinction probability is \(F_{V,1,\tau}(q_V^{(2)},q_M^{(2)})^n\). This makes the bottleneck testable: if M can establish under the second drug, V299L cells have an escape route even when their own net growth is negative. Zhao et al.'s compound-mutant results give a reason to measure this route rather than assume it away.

There is also a source term from sensitive cells that mutate during treatment. Under a Poisson approximation with sensitive population path \(S(t)\), division rate \(\lambda_S(t)\), and per-division mutation probability \(u\), the probability of no established de-novo resistant lineage is

\[
\exp\!\left[-\int_0^T u\lambda_S(t)S(t)\bigl(1-q_R(t)\bigr)\,dt\right],
\]

where \(q_R(t)\) is the future extinction probability for a resistant cell born at time \(t\). Multiply this by the extinction probability for pre-existing founders only under the stated independence approximation. A pulse that clears existing V299L cells but leaves a large sensitive population and high mutation supply can therefore fail to prevent rescue.

Two-type branching, switching, and mutation are established mathematical prior art, so this is not a new extinction equation. Gunnarsson et al. give a cancer-specific two-phenotype switching branching model and solve nonlinear extinction equations ([*Journal of Theoretical Biology* (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/)); Bozic et al. use multitype branching for targeted-therapy resistance and compare combination with sequential treatment ([*eLife* (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3691570/)); Antal and Krapivsky give an exact two-type birth–death process with one-way mutation ([2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1157)). More directly for measurement, Whiting et al. (2025) infer phenotype dynamics from barcode lineage tracing plus population-size data, without direct phenotype observations, and validate distinct resistance paths in two 5-FU-treated colorectal lines ([*Nature Communications*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12181405/)). That method is a plausible lower-cost alternative to direct event observation, but its assumptions and parameter identifiability must be checked for this rare-founder, genotype-specific extinction endpoint. A defensible novelty target is prospective, held-out prediction of extinction from identifiable data in a biologically specified collateral-sensitivity branch, with a superior result against optimized schedule and combination controls.

## Quantitative test and strongest controls

The focused test would use barcoded, isogenic BCR–ABL1 wild-type, V299L, and selected compound-mutant lines in the same Ph+ ALL background, then repeat the decisive arms in the Zhao syngeneic model. First estimate clone- and state-specific division/death hazards under dasatinib, foretinib, vandetanib, and combinations from time-resolved single-cell imaging. Directly record transitions between sensitive/tolerant states with a validated live reporter or repeated single-cell RNA/protein state calls linked to lineage barcodes; estimate V299L-to-compound mutation supply with error-corrected targeted sequencing and fluctuation-style replicates. These event counts identify \(b_i,d_i,\alpha,\beta,u\) that a net-growth slope cannot. A validated lineage-tracing/population-size inference model could substitute, but it must recover the necessary rates on synthetic data with the experiment's sampling and detection limits before it is used to predict extinction. Seed known numbers of resistant founders (for example 1, 5, and 10) into a mostly sensitive population. Apply a prespecified V299L-triggered collateral pulse while maintaining adequate wild-type control, wash out, and follow long enough for any surviving lineage to rebound under the original selective treatment. Compare predicted establishment probabilities with held-out replicate cultures/mice, using barcode-resolved relapse as the endpoint.

Controls must include continued dasatinib, a best available mutation-directed TKI control appropriate to the model, foretinib/vandetanib alone, and an upfront or concurrent combination. Also include a variant-blind switch near the bulk-tumor nadir, since a current theoretical two-strike study predicts that switching near the minimum can maximize extinction probability ([Patil et al., *Genetics* (2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13270307/)). Match or explicitly price cumulative drug exposure, sensitive-cell suppression, toxicity, and time-to-detection. The broader idea of stochastic resistance under pulsed dosing and toxicity constraints is already established ([Foo & Michor, *PLoS Computational Biology* (2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/)); the research claim should be mutation-informed, measured extinction prediction, not that pulse schedules or two-strike treatment are new.

Pre-register the rates, mutation model, switch trigger, follow-up window, and prediction interval using training replicates; freeze them before a blinded holdout. A direct falsifier is that the held-out fraction of cultures or mice with resistant-barcode rebound falls outside the model's predictive interval, or is not lower than the best exposure/toxicity-matched control. Mechanism-specific failure is also clear: achievable foretinib/vandetanib exposure fails to suppress pSTAT5 or drive V299L death; compound escape arises often enough that the multitype prediction loses its advantage; or measured viable founder counts remain high despite the pulse.

## Costs and limits

This is a preclinical research direction, not a treatment recommendation. It requires repeated error-corrected sequencing or single-cell genotyping to count V299L and compound lineages, live state tracking to measure rare switching, clone-resolved pharmacology, and enough follow-up to distinguish temporary cytostasis from extinction. Rare transition rates demand many cells and independent replicate lineages; live reporters can perturb cell state, and destructive single-cell sampling only gives snapshots. In patients, serial bone-marrow or blood sampling, assay latency, and absolute clone-size inference add cost; allele fraction alone does not determine founder number. Foretinib and vandetanib have off-target kinase activity and exposure/toxicity constraints, and the cited leukemia experiments do not establish a safe or effective human regimen. Spatial marrow niches, immune effects, variable pharmacokinetics, phenotypic switching, and cell-to-cell dependence can also violate the independent constant-rate branching model.

High-complexity barcoding has already shown why the pre-existing-clone baseline matters: in two clinically relevant models, resistant clones were mostly small pre-existing subpopulations detectable before therapy, and barcoding could quantify combination suppression ([Bhang et al., *Nature Medicine* (2015)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nm.3841)). Accordingly, initial clone count and de-novo mutation supply must be modeled alongside the response of a single resistant lineage.
