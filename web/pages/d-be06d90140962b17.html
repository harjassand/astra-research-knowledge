# A uniform Hermitian positivity gap for the explicit quadric bundle

Status, 7 October 2026: complete author reconstruction; our two consequences below are submitted for independent programme review. This is not independent external validation, a claim of priority, or a Jacobian/Hessian-conjecture result. No effective numerical gap is obtained.

## Question and exact interface

Can the explicit bundle in OpenAI's family050 be algebraically nef while remaining uniformly separated from smooth Griffiths-semipositivity? Can the paper's fixed-twist, eventual strict-positivity obstruction be upgraded to a uniform obstruction when the twist-to-power ratio tends to zero?

The base is the compact projective surface S=P¹×P¹, P=O(1,1), and f_m is the coordinatewise mth-power map. All metrics here are smooth positive-definite Hermitian metrics. Griffiths-semipositivity means nonnegative Chern curvature on every decomposable tangent/fibre vector. A curvature lower bound Θ_h≥−εω·Id is likewise in the Griffiths sense, for a fixed smooth positive Kähler form ω on S. This metric property is explicitly specified; no competing definition of “nef metric” is silently substituted.

## Explicit construction and the decisive obstruction

Put a=exp(iπ/3), s=1/2−i(1+2√2)/(2√3),

    A=diag(a,a⁻¹),    B=[[s,1],[√2/3,1−s]].

Both determinants are1; trA=trB=1, tr(A⁻¹B)=−√2, tr(AB)=1+√2>2. The matrices A,B have order6, A⁻¹B order8, their projective actions have order3, and A,B have no common eigenline. The last fact follows because the eigenlines of diagonal A are the two coordinate axes and both off-diagonal entries of B are nonzero. AB has reciprocal eigenvalues of distinct moduli.

Let D=Γ_I∪Γ_A∪Γ_B. The graphs are smooth (1,1)-curves with six transverse nodes and no triple point. The exponent24 makes the three isomorphisms L_1⁻¹|Γ_M→L_2⁻¹|Γ_M induced by M^⊗24 agree at every node. Nodal gluing gives α:L_1⁻¹|D→L_2⁻¹|D. With β the inverse of its dual, G is the equalizer of u₂|D=βu₁|D inside L_1⊕L_2. Locally, if d defines D and h represents β as a unit, (1,h),(0,d) form a basis. This verifies local freeness even at nodes and gives the two quotient sequences.

On F=G*, the dual inclusions ι_i:L_i⁻¹→F span outside D and satisfy ι₂α=ι₁ on D. Suppose q is a nonzero finite nonnegative PSH fibre-Hermitian quadratic. Restricting q to ι_i and holding its fibre vector fixed, the complementary P¹ variable gives a finite subharmonic function on a compact connected curve, hence a constant. Thus q yields squared seminorms a_i on O_P¹(−24). Γ_I identifies them; Γ_A,Γ_B make the resulting seminorm a invariant under A^⊗24,B^⊗24. It is nonzero: otherwise positivity of a Hermitian form, including |H(v,w)|²≤H(v,v)H(w,w), makes q vanish on the two spanning lines outside D and hence almost everywhere; the PSH submean inequality makes it zero everywhere.

In a line frame, a(z,w)=u(z)|w|². Joint PSH implies logu is subharmonic, including at its zeros. One rigorous derivation convolves u in the base, adds δ>0, and uses the positive determinant of the2×2 Levi matrix to obtain (logu_δ)_{z z̄}≥0. Radial means give u_δ↓u. A positive value of u supplies a submean lower bound on ∫logu_δ and hence a uniform bound on its negative part; connectedness propagates local L¹ integrability. Thus dd^c logu is a positive Radon measure μ. Comparing with the weight (1+|z|²)^24 on O(−24) and using distributional Stokes gives μ(P¹)=48π, with all atoms retained.

The graph identities make μ invariant under A,B. In a projective coordinate where AB acts as z↦λz, |λ|=R>1, all disjoint half-open annuli R^k≤|z|<R^{k+1} have the same finite mass, hence zero mass. Therefore μ is supported on the two fixed points of AB. Both projective A,B have order3, so their permutations of a support of size≤2 are trivial; each support point would be a common eigenline. This is impossible. Consequently the cone of finite nonnegative PSH Hermitian quadratics on F consists of zero.

This proof checks the exact analytic object, including degeneration and branch-locus representatives. It does not assume restriction of a weak limit to D converges.

## A compactness lemma used by both new deductions

Let X be compact, F a finite-rank holomorphic vector bundle. Suppose local PSH functions q_{j,i}(x,v)=v*H_{j,i}(x)v are nonnegative Hermitian quadratics, are bounded on every compact total-space chart, and their transition laws differ from the ordinary F-law by scalar factors converging locally uniformly to1. Assume they are normalized by a continuous global quadratic N_j with max on a fixed background unit sphere equal to1, and that q_{j,i}/N_j→1 locally uniformly in the scalar base factor. Then a subsequence produces a nonzero finite global PSH function q on F, Hermitian quadratic on every fibre.

Proof: weak L² compactness and a countable chart/exhaustion diagonal give local L∞ Hermitian positive-semidefinite coefficient limits H_i. The vanishing scalar transition errors give the ordinary F-law almost everywhere. For noncollapse, maximizing unit vectors (x_j,v_j) have a convergent subsequence. In one chart, their coordinates c_j→c≠0. The uniform matrix bound gives q_j(x_j,c)→1. For large j it is≥1/2. The submean inequality on a ball of fixed radius about x_j yields a fixed positive lower bound on ∫χ c*H_jc for a single compactly supported cutoff χ. Weak convergence preserves that bound, so H is nonzero.

To obtain the correct representative on all fibres, convolve H_i in the base by a nonnegative smooth radial approximate identity. The convolutions are jointly PSH: they are limits of averages of holomorphic base translations of q_j. For each fixed v, radial convolution is a nonnegative average of spherical means of the subharmonic function q_j(·,v). Spherical means increase with radius, so the convolved quadratics decrease as the radius tends to zero. A local quadratic bound makes their decreasing PSH limit finite. Polarization at e_k,e_k+e_l,e_k+ie_l gives a single finite Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix at every base point. It represents H_i almost everywhere. On overlaps the locally bounded PSH functions agree almost everywhere; the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity determine a PSH function by its almost-everywhere values, so they agree everywhere. The positive cutoff integral guarantees nonzero q.

This is a rederived version of the source's §4 compactness mechanism, with an abstract asymptotically trivial scalar twist. It does not assume pointwise convergence of q_j, convergence on divisors, or strict positivity. The raw trace sequence (1+|x|^{2/m})|v|² illustrates why that distinction matters: its pointwise limit has a lower value at x=0 and is not the PSH representative.

## New deduction A: a uniform twist-to-power gap

Define E_{m,r}=f_m*G⊗P^r for integers m≥1,r≥0. There exists ε₀>0 such that

    0≤r/m<ε₀  ⇒  E_{m,r} has no smooth Griffiths-semipositive metric.

Suppose otherwise. We can choose a sequence of such metrics with ε_j=r_j/m_j→0. Their smooth dual norms on f_m*F⊗P^(−r) are PSH on the total space; strict negativity is unnecessary. In a nonvanishing holomorphic frame t of P⁻¹ define

    q_{m,r,t}(x,v) = m⁻² Σ_{y∈f_m⁻¹(x)} ||v⊗(f_m*t)^(r/m)(y)||²,

counting multiplicity. The local root is well defined up to a unit-modulus root of unity: in a source frame of P⁻¹, f_m*t=η s^m, so η^(r/m)s^r is a holomorphic local frame factor. Off the branch locus each summand is a holomorphic pullback of a PSH squared norm. Continuous motion of the multiset of roots gives continuity across the branch locus; the locally bounded removable-singularity theorem gives PSH there. Changing t to gt gives q_{t'}=|g|^(2r/m)q_t.

Let |·|₀ be smooth background norms on F and P⁻¹ and set N_{m,r}=q_t|t|₀^(−2r/m). This is a global continuous quadratic. Scale the dual metric so max_{|v|₀=1}N_{m,r}=1. Then0≤q_t≤|t|₀^(2r/m)|v|₀². All scalar frame errors and normalization factors tend locally uniformly to1. The compactness lemma yields a forbidden nonzero PSH Hermitian quadratic on F. This contradiction proves the uniform positive gap. An unbounded sequence of m is not needed; only r/m→0 is used.

For every r≥1 these same E_{m,r} are algebraically ample. More precisely, O_PP(E_{m,r})(n) is very ample for every even n with nr−1≥3m. The source's alternating filtration, twisted by P^(nr−1), has factors with bidegrees

    (18mj+nr−1, 18mj+24m(n−2j)+nr−1),
    (18mj+24m(n−2j)−3m+nr−1, 18mj−3m+nr−1),
    (9mn+nr−1, 9mn+nr−1),

where0≤j<n/2 in the first two lines. Every entry is nonnegative. Global generation and H¹-vanishing for the line factors lift through the locally free filtration. Relative Veronese, evaluation, base embedding by P, and Segre then make the n-th tautological power very ample, exactly as in §3. This extends the algebraic side together with the analytic gap.

There is also an explicit **upper** twist threshold, so the conclusion is a growth law rather than only a prohibition. E_{m,3m} is globally generated. Indeed, just one source quotient sequence, twisted by P^(3m), is

    0 → O(24m,0) → E_{m,3m} → O(3m,27m) → 0.

Both line bundles are globally generated and H¹(O(24m,0))=0, so quotient generators lift and together with kernel generators span E_{m,3m} at every point. For any globally generated holomorphic bundle E, evaluation of finitely many generating sections embeds E* into a trivial bundle. The induced squared norm is a sum of squares of holomorphic functions on the total space, hence PSH and positive-definite on each fibre. Its dual metric is smooth Griffiths-semipositive. Thus E_{m,3m} has such a metric. Tensoring by the product Fubini–Study positive metric on P gives a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive metric on E_{m,3m+1}.

The quotient sequence also gives h⁰(E_{m,3m})=(24m+1)+(3m+1)(27m+1)=81m²+54m+2. Evaluation of a basis gives a finite metric construction of this ambient dimension. The actual lifted section basis or metric matrix has not been computed here, and no runtime bound for acquiring it is claimed; only the threshold and dimension are explicit.

If r_sem(m) and r_pos(m) denote the least integer twists supporting smooth semi- and strictly positive Hermitian metrics, respectively, they are finite and

    ε₀m ≤ r_sem(m) ≤ 3m,
    ε₀m ≤ r_pos(m) ≤ 3m+1.

Both minimal metric twist budgets therefore grow linearly in m, while the fixed algebraic twist r=1 already makes every member ample. The lower proportionality constant is ineffective. These inequalities are confined to this exact coordinate-power family; no general metric-complexity law for arbitrary ample bundles is claimed.

## New deduction B: nef but uniformly outside approximate Hermitian semipositivity

First G is algebraically nef. For **every** even n≥2, Sym^nG⊗P³ is globally generated: use the same filtration at m=1 and twist by the fixed P³. Its only negative contribution is−3, canceled by that fixed twist; all factors have nonnegative bidegrees and H¹=0. Thus T^n⊗π*P³ is globally generated on PP(G), T=O_PP(G)(1). For every integral curve C⊂PP(G),

    n·deg_C T + 3·deg_C π*P ≥0

for arbitrarily large even n, hence deg_C T≥0. This is nefness of T and therefore of G. G is not ample: its quotient G→L₁ restricts to a degree-zero quotient on the ruling {x₀}×P¹. An ample bundle has positive-degree line quotients on every projective curve.

Fix any smooth positive Kähler form ω on S. There exists ε*>0 such that no smooth positive-definite Hermitian metric h on G satisfies Θ_h≥−ε*ω·Id in the Griffiths sense.

Indeed, if metrics h_j satisfy this bound with ε_j↓0, their dual curvature on F is at most ε_jω·Id. Choose bounded local real potentials φ_i with dd^cφ_i=ω. The local squared dual norms

    q_{j,i}(x,v)=exp(ε_jφ_i(x)) ||v||²_{h_j*}

have Griffiths-semnegative curvature and hence are jointly PSH. This sign follows from Θ_(exp(εφ)h*)=Θ_h*−εdd^cφ·Id. Normalize the unweighted global dual quadratic by its maximum on the background unit sphere; scaling changes no curvature. The local weighted matrices are uniformly bounded, and their extra overlap scalar factor exp(ε_j(φ_i−φ_l)) tends locally uniformly to1. The compactness lemma gives a forbidden nonzero PSH Hermitian quadratic on F.

The same argument proves a general criterion: on a compact Kähler base, a bundle whose dual has no nonzero finite nonnegative PSH Hermitian fibre quadratic cannot admit smooth Hermitian curvature lower bounds approaching zero. The explicit G supplies the geometric example and the algebraic nefness proof above.
