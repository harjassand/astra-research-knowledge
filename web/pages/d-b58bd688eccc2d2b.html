1. Exact covariant minimax reduction under a shared unknown nuisance

Let H be a finite-dimensional input Hamiltonian and let
P = 1_[0,Q](sum_(i=1)^n H_i). Assume a compact group G has a unitary
representation U_g on one use with [U_g,H]=0, and let
E_g=U_g E U_g*. For a fixed n-use parallel probe density operator rho with
rho=P rho P, define its worst-case all-no-click probability

    R(rho) = sup_(g in G) Tr[rho E_g^tensor n],
    Omega_n = integral_G E_g^tensor n dg,

where dg is normalized Haar measure and the same g acts on every use.
Then

    inf_(rho=P rho P) R(rho)
      = min_(rho=P rho P) Tr(rho Omega_n)
      = lambda_min(P Omega_n P | Ran(P)).                         (1)

Proof. For every legal rho, sup_g Tr(rho E_g^tensor n) is at least its Haar
average Tr(rho Omega_n). This proves the lower bound by the minimum eigenvalue
of the compressed positive operator. Let rho_0 be a legal state attaining the
minimum average, and set

    rho_bar = integral_G U_g^tensor n rho_0 (U_g*)^tensor n dg.

Because U_g commutes with H, rho_bar remains supported in P. For any h in G,
Haar invariance gives

    Tr[rho_bar E_h^tensor n]
      = integral_G Tr[rho_0 E_(g^{-1}h)^tensor n] dg
      = Tr[rho_0 Omega_n],

which is constant in h and equals the minimum average. This proves the reverse
bound and (1). The minimum over all legal states of a positive linear
functional is the smallest eigenvalue of its compression to Ran(P).

For the fully separable comparator, replace the legal convex set by the set
of density operators in P that are separable across the n use factors. The
same average lower bound holds. Collective twirling U_g^tensor n maps every
product state to a product state and maps separable mixtures to separable
mixtures. Twirling a separable minimizer therefore proves the same minimax
identity with the minimum restricted to separable states. In general
Omega_n is not (integral E_g dg)^tensor n: the nuisance is shared across uses.

To interpret (1) as a detection miss, use the common flagged channel

    Phi_g(rho) = Tr(E_g rho)|v><v| + Tr((I-E_g)rho)|c><c|,
    <v|c>=0,

with common pure null output |v> on the ground input. The zero-false-alarm
test rejects whenever the output is outside |v>^tensor n. Its miss on rho is
exactly Tr(rho E_g^tensor n). The theorem is about the supplied fixed
parallel-probe class; it does not optimize channel acquisition, adaptive
feedback, preparation, or drive work.

2. Exact qutrit example: unknown phase erases a calibrated advantage

Use input basis |0>,|l>,|h> and

    H = diag(0,1,2),
    E = 1 direct-sum E_+,
    E_+ = [[1/2, sqrt(2)/4], [sqrt(2)/4, 3/4]].

The nuisance is one unknown phase shared across the two uses:

    U_theta = diag(1,e^(i theta),e^(2 i theta)),
    E_theta = U_theta E U_theta*.

Take n=2 and hard ceiling Q=3. The legal computational basis is
|00>, |0l>, |l0>, |0h>, |h0>, |ll>, |lh>, |hl>; only |hh> is excluded.
Haar averaging over theta makes Omega_2 block diagonal by total energy:

    energy 0: [1]
    energy 1: diag(1/2,1/2)
    energy 2: diag(3/4,3/4,1/4) in basis |0h>,|h0>,|ll>
    energy 3: [[3/8,1/8],[1/8,3/8]] in basis |lh>,|hl>.

The eigenvalues of the last block are 1/4 and 1/2; hence the least
eigenvalue of P Omega_2 P is exactly 1/4. The product probe |ll> has total
energy 2 and miss <ll|E_theta^tensor 2|ll>=(1/2)^2=1/4 for every theta.
By (1),

    beta_entangled^robust(2,3) = beta_separable^robust(2,3) = 1/4.

For known theta, the supplied N62/N32 qutrit example has separable optimum
1/8 and entangled probe miss (3-sqrt(5))/8 < 1/8. The useful coherence is
between total energies 2 and 3, and the shared-phase average removes it. This
is an exact counterexample to any blanket claim that the known-effect
entanglement gain survives an uncalibrated symmetry nuisance. It does not say
that entanglement never helps for other groups or larger budgets.

3. Mixed null and fixed type-I budget when an orthogonal outcome flag exists

Let the output Hilbert space be K_0 direct-sum K_1 with K_0 perpendicular to
K_1. Let the null channel prepare any density operator sigma supported on
K_0 (sigma may be mixed). Let the alternative channel be

    A(rho) = Tr(E rho) sigma + Tr((I-E)rho) tau,

where tau is a density operator supported on K_1. For n parallel uses, the
all-no-click summand of the alternative output is

    p_0(rho) sigma^tensor n,
    p_0(rho) = Tr(rho E^tensor n).

Write T for the rejection effect. Its type-I error is
Tr(sigma^tensor n T) and is required to be at most delta, where
0 <= delta < 1. Its type-II error is
Tr(A^tensor n(rho)(I-T)). Positivity and the type-I constraint imply

    Tr(A^tensor n(rho)(I-T))
      >= p_0(rho) Tr(sigma^tensor n(I-T))
      >= (1-delta) p_0(rho).

With P_0 the projector onto K_0^tensor n, set

    T_delta = I - P_0 + delta P_0.

Then Tr(sigma^tensor n T_delta)=delta. Every alternative click pattern has at
least one factor in K_1 and is rejected with probability one; the
all-no-click pattern is rejected with probability delta. Thus its type-II
error is exactly (1-delta)p_0(rho), proving the fixed-input optimum

    beta*(rho;delta)=(1-delta)Tr(rho E^tensor n).                    (2)

Optimizing over the same hard-support input set multiplies every N62
entangled/separable miss by 1-delta. For fixed delta<1 this adds the constant
-log(1-delta) to log miss and does not change a leading hard-energy exponent.
If E,H satisfy N62's assumptions, its source-derived exponent conclusions
therefore transfer conditionally. If E varies over a supplied nuisance family
but K_0,K_1 and the null support are common, T_delta works simultaneously;
the robust input objective becomes inf_rho sup_theta Tr(rho E_theta^tensor n).

This lemma needs a common orthogonal flag and equality of the alternative's
no-click output to the null output. It does not cover a globally full-rank
null whose support overlaps the click outputs.

4. Exact finite-false-alarm counterexample: E,H are not sufficient

Keep the N62 qutrit H,E above. Let the null be the replacer
N(rho)=|0><0| on output basis |0>,|1>. For q in [0,1/4], set

    |psi_q> = sqrt(q)|0> + sqrt(1-q)|1>,
    F_q = (E-qI)/(1-q),
    M_q(rho) = Tr(F_q rho)|0><0|
             + Tr((I-F_q)rho)|psi_q><psi_q|.

Because qI <= E <= I, 0<=F_q<=I, so M_q is CPTP. Since E|0>=|0>,
F_q|0>=|0> and M_q(|0><0|)=|0><0|. Directly,

    M_q^*(|0><0|) = F_q + q(I-F_q) = E.                              (3)

Thus q=0 and q=1/10 have the same H,E and the same exact zero-false-alarm
miss for every parallel input: zero false alarm forces acceptance of the
all-|0> output, whose probability is Tr(rho E^tensor n).

At Q=1, every legal n-use input is supported on |0>^tensor n and the
single-|l> excitation subspace. The compression of E^tensor n to this legal
space has eigenvalues 1 and 1/2, so its smallest eigenvalue is 1/2 for all
n>=1. In the orthogonal-flag channel M_0, the alternative click strings are
orthogonal to the null string. With false-alarm allowance alpha, the optimal
test rejects every click string and rejects the null string with probability
alpha. Its power on a probe with all-no-click probability p is
1-(1-alpha)p. The global parallel optimum is therefore

    d_0^*(alpha,Q=1)=1-(1-alpha)/2.

For alpha=1/100 this is 101/200=0.505, including optimization over every n,
entangled legal probe, and output test.

For q=1/10, use the one-use legal probe |l>. It has
Tr(E|l><l|)=1/2 and Tr(F_q|l><l|)=4/9, so the alternative output is
(4/9)|0><0|+(5/9)|psi_q><psi_q|. Test with

    R=|varphi><varphi|,
    |varphi>=sqrt(alpha)|0>+sqrt(1-alpha)|1>.

Its false-alarm probability under N is exactly alpha. The alternative power
is

    (4/9)alpha + (5/9)(sqrt(alpha q)
                +sqrt((1-alpha)(1-q)))^2
      = 1/2 + sqrt(11)/100
      > 101/200.

Thus positive-false-alarm performance is not determined by H and the
pure-null overlap effect E alone. The gap is exact, not a numeric tolerance.
It arises because the q=1/10 click state overlaps coherently with the null
output, while q=0 has orthogonal click flags. This does not challenge N62's
alpha=0 theorem and does not rule out tighter finite-alpha theorems when the
full output channel is supplied.

5. Boundary and baseline checks

The strict condition E>0 in N62 is load-bearing. For example
H=diag(0,1,2), E=diag(1,1/2,0) admits a legal excited zero-eigenvector at
finite ceiling, so the miss can be exactly zero and the finite Cholesky
inverse used by N62 is undefined. This is a boundary counterexample to
dropping strict positivity, not to the theorem.

The same qutrit separates expected and hard resource contracts:
N18's expected-cost exponent is (5/3)log(2), while N62's hard-support
exponent is log((1+sqrt(17))/2); the all-separable hard exponent is log(2).
Expected-energy typical projections do not establish hard support. The
detector results charge accepted input support energy only; state preparation,
filter retries, drive work, calibration, precision, and reference restoration
are separate resources.

6. Adaptive stop-on-click flattening under the whole-sequence support contract

Let Phi be any finite-dimensional channel with Phi(|0><0|)=|v><v|, where
|v> is pure, and define E=Phi*(|v><v|), with 0<E<=I and E|0>=|0>.
At each use, measure {|v><v|, I-|v><v|}, stop on the first click, and allow
arbitrary finite retained memory and CPTP controls between calls. Define the
resource contract as follows: unroll the unique all-no-click control
schedule, retain every past query register as an inaccessible spectator,
and let sigma be the resulting virtual n-slot signal state. Require
sigma=P_(n,Q) sigma P_(n,Q), with P_(n,Q)=1_[0,Q](sum_j H_j).
This is the sequential continuation of N62's exact whole-sequence support
condition, and charges neither the controls nor preparation.

For one call, projecting the channel output onto |v> gives the completely
positive map

  (id_R tensor Phi_v)(omega_RS)
    = Tr_S[(I_R tensor E) omega_RS] tensor |v><v|.

Along the all-no-click history, every inter-use control is fixed. Deferred
effects E_j on the query registers commute with subsequent controls acting on
other accessible registers. Therefore the physical all-no-click probability
is exactly Tr(sigma E^tensor n). The legal sigma makes this at least
p_n(Q)=min_(rho=P rho P) Tr(rho E^tensor n). Conversely any legal parallel
sigma can be queried sequentially and stopped on click, attaining the same
miss. Thus the adaptive optimum equals p_n(Q), even with retained quantum
memory. If N62's compressed-Gram/rate theorem is accepted, it transfers to
this scoped adaptive extension. It remains an internal proof candidate, and
does not by itself define every operational sequential energy ledger.

For the N62 qutrit with n=2,Q=3, the exact parallel/adaptive miss is
(3-sqrt(5))/8. For the stated witness, alternative-side expected signal
energy is 5/2-sqrt(5)/10 when both queries are made, and
(31-3sqrt(5))/16 when stopping on the first click; the maximum signal support
on any branch remains 3. The ground-state null makes both calls and uses zero
signal energy. The exact check script is in evidence/leaf_03_check.py.

Different contract: if hard energy is enforced by a hidden per-call energy
measurement and pathwise energy-history cap, a noncommuting E can steer
retained memory on the no-click branch. A separate leaf proves adaptive to
parallel equality for an energy-commuting measure-and-prepare channel pair;
the noncommuting qutrit under that altered contract remains UNKNOWN. This
distinction is substantive, not a contradiction between the two leaves.

    n g(c)-2s_H(D-1)h_max-D log(n+1)-C_D
