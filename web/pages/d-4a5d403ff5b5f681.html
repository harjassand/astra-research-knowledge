1. Exact rejection sampler for each pure-product ensemble
---------------------------------------------------------
For 0<A<1, b fixed, draw N iid uniform R_i on S^2, compute
U_N=sum R_i/sqrt N, and accept with probability
  p_A(U_N)/p_max,
  p_A(u)=exp[-alpha|u|^2+h u_z],
  alpha=9A/[4(1-A)], h=-3b/[2(1-A)],
  p_max=exp[h^2/(4alpha)]=exp[b^2/(4A(1-A))].
Upon acceptance, prepare the N pure qubits omega(R_i). This samples exactly
the mathematical sigma_N(A,b) ensemble. No rejected proposal needs physical
qubits prepared; the rejection work is classical.
The expected number of proposals is exactly
  M_N(A,b)=p_max/E p_A(U_N).
Since E|U_N|^2=1 and E(U_N)_z=0, Jensen gives E p>=exp(-alpha), hence the
explicit finite-N bound
  M_N(A,b)<=exp[b^2/(4A(1-A))+9A/(4(1-A))].
This bound is loose but holds for every N. From the scalar CLT normalizer in
the report one obtains the sharper asymptotic value
  M_N(A,b)->[(1+A/2)/(1-A)]^(3/2)
                exp[b^2/(2A(2+A))].
As A approaches0 the limiting cost can be exponential in b^2/A. As A
approaches1 it diverges as (1-A)^(-3/2) times exp(b^2/6). Neither endpoint
is assigned an uncharged ideal sampler.
Each classical proposal costs N sphere samples and O(N) arithmetic; accepted
output costs N pure-qubit preparations. Bit precision and mixture selection
are additional costs, detailed below. There is no entangling preparation step.

2. Endpoint truncation for a finite accuracy target
---------------------------------------------------
Choose a in (0,1/2). Use only components A=s b_N^2 in [a,1-a], and replace
all remaining components by the maximally mixed product state. The resulting
state is exactly FULLSEP for every N and has asymptotic error upper bound
  delta_a(c)=integral_{s outside [a c^2,(1-a)c^2]} K_{1/c}(s)mu(ds).
By continuity and absence of atoms, delta_a(c)->delta(c) as a->0. The
extra sacrificed good-component mass is exactly
  delta_a(c)-delta(c)
     =integral_[0,a c^2) K mu + integral_((1-a)c^2,c^2) K mu.
Thus an arbitrarily small additive epsilon is achievable by an explicit
one-dimensional choice of a, at a cost that must be paid in the sampler.
A certified numerical choice of a would require interval/error-controlled
evaluation of these integrals. The present quadrature files are diagnostics,
not such certification.
For fixed a,b, the finite-N proposal bound is independent of N. The cost can
be substantial as a->0; no claim uniform in c or epsilon is made.

3. Selecting the component and evaluating the mixture law
---------------------------------------------------------
The exact W_N density relative to mu is
  g_N(s)=C_beta exp[-s beta_N^2/4] Z_N(s b_N^2,b_N),
  C_beta=sinh(beta_N/2)/(beta_N/2).
It satisfies 0<=g_N(s)<=M_W:=C_beta exp(b_N^2/8). Thus one can propose
S~mu and accept with probability g_N(S)/M_W. Because integral g_N dmu=1,
the expected number of component proposals is exactly M_W. The finite
sector character sum evaluating Z_N uses O(N) spin blocks and O(N) arithmetic
with closed sinh character expressions (stable logs for large arguments).
Input b_N and beta_N and these arithmetic evaluations require precision.

mu is the law of S=sum Exp(pi^2 n^2), an infinite sum; free exact sampling is
not asserted. For S_L=sum_{n<=L} E_n, the positive remainder obeys
  E(S-S_L)<=1/(pi^2 L),
  P(S-S_L>r)<=1/(pi^2 L r).
Its law also has explicit theta-series density (used by the check script),
but truncating that series similarly requires a tail bound.
The mixture weight is uniformly Lipschitz in s:
  |g_N'(s)|<= C_beta b_N^2 exp(b_N^2/8)
                    [3/4+b_N^2/16+1/(4N)].
Proof: drop the commuting radial exponential, and calculate J^2 under the
product z-tilt. The exact ratio is
  tau[J^2 exp(-b_N Jz/sqrt N)] / tau[exp(-b_N Jz/sqrt N)]
    =3N/4+(N^2-N)tanh^2[b_N/(2sqrt N)]/4,
then use tanh t<=t. Also the density of mu is <=pi^2 because S is an
Exp(pi^2) variable convolved with an independent probability measure.
On A in [a,1-a], direct differentiation of normalized classical weights gives
  D(sigma_N(A,b),sigma_N(A',b))
     <= [9N/4+3|b|sqrt N/2] a^(-2) |A-A'|.
This follows from |partial_A log p| <=[9N/4+3|b|sqrt N/2]/(1-A)^2;
the total-variation derivative of a normalized weighted law is bounded by
the supremum absolute log-weight derivative.
Together these bounds permit choosing r=O_{a,b}(epsilon/N), then
L=O_{a,b}(N/epsilon^2), so S_L coupling, component-weight error, and cutoff
misclassification contribute O(epsilon). The cutoff-neighborhood mass is
controlled using the pi^2 density bound and g_N<=M_W. This is a conservative
explicit cost for approximating the mathematical SEP ensemble, not a claim
of an optimal sampler. Each mixture proposal also costs O(L) exponential
draws, O(N) sector arithmetic, and the accuracy of exponential generation.
Further roundoff can be budgeted in total variation by interval arithmetic.

4. Finite direction precision
------------------------------
Suppose each Bloch vector is coupled to a rounded unit vector within eta.
For a fixed accepted tuple the product-state trace distance changes by at
most N eta/2, by telescoping the product trace-distance inequality.
If the same rounded directions are also used to compute the acceptance
weight, then |U-U'|<=sqrt N eta and
  |log p(U)-log p(U')|
    <=(2alpha N+|h|sqrt N)eta+alpha N eta^2=:kappa.
Ratios of normalized weights lie between exp(-2kappa) and exp(2kappa), so
their total-variation error is conservatively <=exp(2kappa)-1.
The total state error is therefore bounded by
  N eta/2 + exp(2kappa)-1.
Choosing eta=O(epsilon/[N(1+alpha+|h|)]) controls this error.
Uniform sphere directions can be generated from z uniform[-1,1] and azimuth
uniform[0,2pi]. A z-grid of spacing O(eta^2) and azimuth-grid O(eta) gives
vector error O(eta), including the polar endpoints. This charges
O(log[N(1+alpha+|h|)/epsilon]) random/precision bits per direction up to a
constant factor. Actual control/calibration errors must be added separately.

5. Finite trace-lift diagnostics
--------------------------------
scripts/check_trace_lift.py uses exact spin weights and complex characters
to check H normalizers, H^2 norms, and the load-bearing cross trace for
(A,b)=(.2,1),(.5,2),(.8,.5) and N=16,64,256,1024,4096.
All 15 floating-arithmetic instances approach the predicted Gaussian limits.
Cross-trace errors decrease by about a factor4 when N quadruples. At N4096
they range from 3.22e-6 to 8.42e-6. The fixed-Fourier tracial-L2 squared error
in the exact B tensor expansion decreases from 1.6865e-6 at N16 to
2.5500e-11 at N4096. trace_lift_checks.json preserves the outputs.
This does not replace the proof and does not establish a uniform rate after
integrating the whole mixture near its endpoints.

CRITICAL LIMITATION
-------------------
We have an explicit finite-N SEP construction, finite classical sampling
and precision bounds for approximating that construction, and an asymptotic
trace-distance guarantee to rho_N. We do NOT have an explicit convergence
rate D(rho_N,Sigma_N)-delta(c). Consequently this is not yet a certified
finite-N efficient simulator for the stationary thermal state at a requested
trace tolerance. Efficiency of preparing Sigma_N and correctness of the
asymptotic comparison are distinct statements; no finite-N target-fidelity
claim is being inferred from the sampler bounds.
