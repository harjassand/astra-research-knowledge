Square dual-NN interface: checkerboard resolution and finite upper-half transfer
Date: 2026-10-09

Status
------
I give a finite, deterministic construction of a square-lattice dual interface
that crosses only actual nearest-neighbor constraints, joins the two Dobrushin
boundary transition edges, and has an exact sign-only adaptive exploration.
Its completed path satisfies the finite conditional-kernel contract in the
square upper-half transfer report. Consequently, under the domain and boundary
hypotheses below, the finite square hard-Lipschitz field has conditional mean
at most 1/2 at every vertex on the + boundary side of this interface.

This is a new dual-NN interface rule. It is not the zero contour of the fixed
SW--NE affine interpolation audited in `cycle10_square_barrier_geometry`, and
no identity between those two interfaces is asserted. No square scaling limit,
SLE statement, continuum trace, or universal height-gap conclusion follows.

Exact finite theorem
-------------------
Let G be the 1-skeleton of a finite simply connected union of unit square
faces whose boundary is a simple cycle (equivalently, use a 2-connected square
cellulation of a disk). Pin one boundary arc to +1/2 and the complementary
arc to -1/2, with exactly two transition edges. Sample normalized Lebesgue
measure on

    P(G) = { h: V(G)->R : h|boundary is the prescribed pinning,
             |h(u)-h(v)| <= 1 for every square nearest-neighbor edge uv }.

This polytope has nonempty relative interior: set all free vertices to zero.
Thus, almost surely, no free vertex has height zero. (The construction itself
is deterministic for any nonzero sign configuration.)

Orient each dual edge crossing a sign-changing primal edge so the positive
endpoint is on its right. In a face with two crossed sides, join their dual
arcs. In a checkerboard face, pair the two crossed sides incident to each
negative corner. Draw each pair as a small arc around that negative corner.
This is a fixed, noncrossing smoothing of the degree-four dual vertex. Start at
the boundary transition whose oriented dual half-edge points into the disk
and follow its oriented successor. The resulting path gamma is a simple
resolved-dual crosscut to the other transition; every edge it crosses is an
actual square nearest-neighbor edge with opposite endpoint signs.

For every vertex x on the side containing the +1/2 boundary arc, the completed
interface obeys

    E[h(x) | gamma] <= 1/2.

This is a one-sided upper-half bound for the finite field and this explicitly
defined interface law. No lower-half bound on the opposite side under the
same gamma is claimed: the exploration is deliberately asymmetric, and direct
reflection changes which checkerboard corners are smoothed and which signs
are queried.

Sign-only local exploration
---------------------------
The smoothing has a local decision-tree implementation. Suppose the oriented
path enters a face across a crossed side whose positive endpoint is p and
negative endpoint is q. Let r be the corner opposite p and s the corner
opposite q. Query signs only, not height values:

    if h(r) > 0:       exit across the side qr;
    if h(r) < 0 and h(s) > 0:
                       exit across the side rs;
    if h(r) < 0 and h(s) < 0:
                       exit across the side sp.

Each selected exit is a crossed side. In the first branch q<0<r; in the
second r<0<s; in the third s<0<p. In the third branch r is the only queried
vertex that is not on the selected crossed edges, and it is negative. The
local sector check puts that r on the left/negative side of the oriented arc.
Every queried positive vertex is therefore a positive endpoint of an exposed
cut edge, with an actual negative nearest-neighbor partner across that edge.
The only possible non-cut query is negative and on the other side. The rule
never inspects a positive vertex in the retained right-side free interior.

For an incoming top side with NW=p>0 and NE=q<0, write SE=r and SW=s. The
complete local table is:

    sign(SE)   sign(SW)   selected exit   effect
       +          +          right         SW is not queried
       +          -          right         checkerboard; SW is not queried
       -          +          bottom        r,s are both cut endpoints
       -          -          left          r is extra, negative, and left-side

Rotations give the other incoming sides. The two checkerboard patterns are
also checked directly in `LOCAL_RULE_CHECK.json`: the pattern with + at NW/SE
pairs top-right around NE- and bottom-left around SW-; the opposite pattern
pairs right-bottom around SE- and left-top around NW-. Thus the pairing is
fixed by the sign pattern and does not depend on which half-edge arrives
first. This resolves the degree-four issue without revealing all four signs:
for example, when NW=SE=+ and NE=SW=-, the top-to-right path never queries SW.

Global topology and exact information boundary
-----------------------------------------------
Every face has zero, two, or four sign-changing sides. A two-crossing face has
one incoming and one outgoing oriented dual edge. In a checkerboard, the
negative-corner pairing is a bijection between its two incoming and two
outgoing half-edges, and its two arcs are disjoint. Replacing every face center
by these local arcs therefore gives an oriented 1-manifold in the disk. The
only boundary crossing edges are the two prescribed transition edges; one
half-edge points inward and one outward. The component from the inward mark is
therefore a proper simple arc ending at the other mark. Other components, if
present, are closed loops and are not queried by this exploration.

Let C be the primal edges crossed by gamma. A primal edge joining vertices on
opposite Jordan sides must intersect gamma, and every such intersection is
the midpoint of an edge in C. Thus deleting C separates the two geometric
sides, including at both endpoint marks. It need not leave exactly two
connected components in the primal graph. The global 3x3-face check below
finds a path that visits both disjoint smoothing arcs in one checkerboard
face; its C isolates one positive interior vertex, so G\C has three graph
components. This does not mix the Jordan sides: each graph component lies
entirely on one side, and each right-side component meets either a +1/2
boundary pin or a positive endpoint of a cut edge. For the latter assertion,
any component not touching the outer boundary must have an incident edge in C
because G is connected; the endpoint on the right side of every edge in C is
positive. Hence every right-side component has a fixed +1/2 anchor or a
positive cut wall. No negative exterior pin is incident to a right-side
component except through C; all other fixed pins incident to it lie on the
+1/2 boundary arc.

For each positive endpoint p of a cut edge pq, h(p)>0>h(q) and
|h(p)-h(q)|<=1 is an actual unit-edge constraint. In particular, each
exposed positive cut vertex has an opposite nearest-neighbor partner. If a
graph component is isolated inside the right Jordan side, its boundary still
consists of positive cut variables, so it can be compared separately.

The path event is a stopping-time event in the local sign queries. At a face,
an exit through qr records r>0; an exit through rs records r<0<s; and an exit
through sp records r<0 and s<0. In the last case the extra sign r is negative
and lies in the left-side graph component: the uncut side qr joins r<0 to the
negative cut endpoint q<0. In the first case s is not queried at all,
including the checkerboard completion s<0. Consequently, conditional on
gamma, no sign of a free vertex in a right-side component is revealed. The
only sign walls there are h(p)>=0 at positive endpoints p of C. The path does
not condition on unvisited loops or on any right-side free interior signs.

DLR kernel and upper-half proof
-------------------------------
Condition on gamma and on all heights on the left Jordan side. The uniform
Lebesgue density factors over the resulting right-side graph components after
these exterior heights are fixed. In each right-side component, the only sign
constraints are h(p)>=0 at positive cut endpoints. For any such p, every
crossed edge pq has q<=0 fixed outside, and hence the original unit-edge
constraint gives

    0 <= h(p) <= h(q)+1 <= 1.

Delete the constraints across C and replace the sign/cross-edge restrictions
at positive cut endpoints by interval walls [0,1]. This comparison is
monotone for the specific endpoint reason here. Given q<=0, the original
boundary wall at p, after imposing h(p)>=0, is [0,min_q(q+1)]; the lower
edge bounds q-1<0 are redundant. Replacing only this wall's upper endpoint
by 1 leaves its lower endpoint zero. The full single-site interval also
intersects this wall with the unchanged constraints from right-side neighbors.
Interior edge constraints are unchanged; there are no extra sign walls in
the free interior. Every other fixed pin incident to the component is +1/2.
No arbitrary boundary data on another right-side component are conditioned
on; distinct components have no connecting non-cut edge and their conditional
kernels factor.

The enlarged kernel on each right-side component is invariant under h -> 1-h,
so every coordinate has mean 1/2. It stochastically dominates the original
kernel. To see the monotonicity directly, the single-site conditional interval is

    [ max_y(h(y)-1), min_y(h(y)+1) ] intersected with its wall interval.

At a free cut vertex the wall lower endpoint stays zero and its upper endpoint
rises from min_q(q+1) to 1; the internal-neighbor endpoints in the full
single-site interval are unchanged monotone functions of those neighbors.
Fixed outer pins are +1/2. Using the same uniform quantile at each heat-bath
update therefore preserves order. The finite conditional polytopes have
interior: choose a constant c>0 below 1/2 and below every free cut-wall upper
endpoint (or c=1/4 when there is no free cut wall). Feasibility and the
positive cut signs make these upper endpoints positive. This gives strict edge gaps
and strict wall inequalities. The finite heat-bath chains are therefore
irreducible on the feasible component polytopes; passing the order-preserving
coupling to their invariant laws gives stochastic domination. Thus
E[h(x) | gamma, left-side heights] <= 1/2 for every right-side component
vertex x. Integrate over the left-side heights to obtain the stated
conditional bound given gamma.

This verifies the square finite interface assumptions in
`cycle10_square_upper_half_transfer/UPPER_HALF_TRANSFER.txt`: a simple
crosscut of constrained edges; exact conditional-kernel locality; an opposing
negative NN partner for every positive cut vertex; and separation at the
marks. The conditional density factors over every free graph component; a
Jordan side may contain more than one. The rule also has an adaptive prefix
property: each completed local step only reveals cut signs and, in one branch,
a left-side extra negative sign. An open prefix is not a crosscut and receives
no upper-half conclusion.

Falsifiers, costs, and remaining gates
--------------------------------------
`local_rule_check.py` exhausts all 16 face sign patterns (each locally
realizable by heights +/-1/2 with NN gaps at most one), checks all 16 directed
incoming transitions, checks that every successor is an outgoing crossed
edge, checks the checkerboard pairing, and checks that every queried positive
is on the selected edge while every unselected query is negative.
`global_check.py` additionally checks all 16 free-vertex sign assignments on a
3x3-face square with Dobrushin boundary signs: every traced path reaches the
other mark, and removing its crossed edges never puts anchors from opposite
Jordan sides into one primal component. The maximum number of primal
components is three. One pattern isolates a positive interior vertex; the
script verifies that all four of its NN edges belong to C. Both JSON outputs
record these finite checks. They audit the construction but do not replace the
general face-pairing and planar-separation proof above.

The earliest falsifier for a proposed variant is any local branch that queries
a positive corner not lying on the outgoing cut edge. A naive rule that
inspects the fourth checkerboard corner when r>0 fails this information
boundary: the local pattern NW=SE=SW=+ and NE=- has the same top-to-right
interface as NW=SE=+ and NE=SW=-, so SW must remain unqueried. The rule here
does so. A second important falsifier is the claim that C is a primal bond or
that G\\C has exactly two components. In the checked 3x3-face grid, set the
four free signs in order (1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(2,2) to (-,+,+,-), with the
bottom/right boundary positive and top/left boundary negative. The path visits
both disjoint smoothing arcs in the central checkerboard face and isolates
(1,2), a positive vertex; all four incident NN edges lie in C, and G\\C has
three components. This is compatible with the theorem because the isolated
vertex is a positive cut endpoint and the three components do not mix Jordan
sides. The actual global falsifiers are an edge joining opposite Jordan sides
without crossing gamma, or a repeated/self-intersecting path in the resolved
dual; the crosscut intersection argument and local successor bijection
exclude these. The path may pass through two different smoothing arcs in the
same original face, so it need not be vertex-simple in the unsmoothed dual.

No regularity, compactness, or limiting cost is paid: this is a finite graph
statement. The exploration reads at most two signs per visited face and uses
no height magnitudes; the path has length at most the number of dual edges.
The exact finite DLR/monotonicity comparison is the only analytic input.

For a continuum height-gap/SLE application, the critical missing gate is no
longer this finite upper-half interface. One still needs square-model control
of the relevant annular walk/crosscut geometry, convergence and Markov-prefix
identification for this *new* dual-NN interface, and a continuum boundary
trace/landing theorem. None is proved here. In particular, this result alone
does not produce a deterministic l in (0,1/2], a square SLE law, or a stiffness
lower bound.
