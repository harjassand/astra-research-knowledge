# C4 — Integer-clock alias in a controlled three-state CTMC

## Model and exact alias

Use row generators on states \(0,1,2\), with clockwise rate \((s+d)/2\), counterclockwise rate \((s-d)/2\), and total exit rate \(s\):
\[
Q(d,s)=\begin{pmatrix}
-s&(s+d)/2&(s-d)/2\\
(s-d)/2&-s&(s+d)/2\\
(s+d)/2&(s-d)/2&-s
\end{pmatrix},\qquad s\ge |d|.
\]
This is the natural parameterization in which the stated \(d_*=4\pi/(\sqrt3\,\tau)\) aliases exactly. The common \(s\) is unknown but fixed across the two hypotheses; \(s\ge d_*\) makes both generators valid.

Let \(\Pi=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3\), \(U=I-\Pi\), and
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q(d,s)=-\tfrac32sU+\tfrac12dA,
\]
where \(A^2=-3U\). Thus the nonstationary eigenvalues are \(-3s/2\pm i\sqrt3d/2\). For rate multiplier \(c>0\) and hold time \(t\), put \(\alpha=ct/\tau\), \(r=e^{-3sct/2}\). Then
\[
P_d(c,t)=e^{ctQ(d,s)}=\Pi+r\left[\cos(2\pi\alpha)U+\frac{\sin(2\pi\alpha)}{\sqrt3}A\right]\quad(d=d_*),
\]
while \(P_0(c,t)=\Pi+rU\). At the supplied observation interval \(t=\tau\), every allowed integer multiplier \(c\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) has \(\alpha=c\), so
\[
P_{d_*}(c,\tau)=P_0(c,\tau)\quad\text{exactly for every allowed action.}
\]
This is not an approximation and does not depend on knowing \(s\).

Let the common initial law be any \(\mu\), and let emissions be any common state-dependent kernel \(E(y\mid i)\), including an unknown one. For a fixed action word \(c_1\cdots c_T\), every output-word probability has the form
\[
\mu D_{y_0}P(c_1,\tau)D_{y_1}\cdots P(c_T,\tau)D_{y_T}\mathbf1,
\]
where \(D_y=\operatorname{diag}(E(y\mid0),E(y\mid1),E(y\mid2))\). Since each transition matrix is identical under \(H_0:d=0\) and \(H_1:d=d_*\), all these probabilities agree. For an adaptive or randomized policy, condition on each observed history: the next action-selection rule is the same and the conditional observation law is the same, so induction gives identical complete transcript laws. No number of resets, integer-scaled actions, or adaptive choices can identify which generator produced the sampled behavior. This remains true with fully revealing emissions. The H1 chain has nonzero directed cycle current; the sampled integer-clock chain erases it.

## Smallest phase-breaking probe and two-read statistic

For any added probe, the transition matrices differ exactly when
\[
\alpha=ct/\tau\notin\mathbb Z,
\]
since \(P_{d_*}-P_0=r[(\cos2\pi\alpha-1)U+(\sin2\pi\alpha)A/\sqrt3]\), with \(r>0\) for finite \(s\). The simplest robust choice is a half-turn, \(\alpha=1/2\): add multiplier \(c=1/2\) while holding for \(\tau\), or retain \(c=1\) and add a read after hold time \(\tau/2\). Then
\[
P_0=\Pi+rU,\qquad P_{d_*}=\Pi-rU,\qquad r=e^{-3sct/2}.
\]
The second option requires the observation schedule to admit that extra half-interval read. For an arbitrary noninteger phase, kernels differ, but a half-turn makes a two-read test particularly simple and nuisance-robust.

Assume the system is reset to the common uniform stationary distribution \(\pi=\mathbf1/3\), and emissions are conditionally independent across reads given the states. Write \(f_y=(E(y\mid0),E(y\mid1),E(y\mid2))^\top\), and \(m_y=\frac13\mathbf1^\top f_y\), the one-read output marginal. A single read has law \(m\) under both hypotheses. With two reads separated by the half-turn probe, define \(S=\Pr(Y_0=Y_1)\). Direct expansion gives
\[
S_0=\sum_y m_y^2+\frac r3 K_E,\qquad
S_1=\sum_y m_y^2-\frac r3 K_E,\qquad
K_E=\sum_y\|Uf_y\|_2^2.
\]
Indeed \(f_y^\top\Pi f_y/3=m_y^2\), \(f_y^\top U f_y=\|Uf_y\|^2\), and at half-turn the transition matrices are \(\Pi\pm rU\). Therefore the unknown shared emission kernel need not be calibrated: compare the paired same-output frequency to the square-sum baseline from the one-read marginal. H0 is above that baseline and H1 below it. This works iff \(K_E>0\), equivalently the emissions depend on state. If all emission distributions are identical across states, then every output sequence is iid from the same marginal under every generator and no action or hold-time probe can distinguish H0 from H1. That is a genuine observational impossibility, not a limitation of the chosen statistic.

One read is insufficient under this uniform reset because every \(P_d(c,t)\) is doubly stochastic and the uniform law is stationary, so the output marginal is always \(m\). Thus two reads and one phase-breaking transition are minimal in this reset contract. If a run protocol only reads after actions, apply the probe for two consecutive intervals and use the two resulting readouts; the transition between those reads is the phase-breaking one. A nonuniform or known state-specific initialization could change the one-read minimum, so the two-read minimality statement is scoped to the uniform stationary reset and shared unknown emissions.

## Experiment and information costs

The exact kernel witness costs one new probe action and one transition. To implement the two-read statistic with a baseline read, each independent replicate costs one reset, two readouts, one probe action, and either elapsed time \(\tau\) at multiplier \(1/2\) or \(\tau/2\) at multiplier 1. If readouts are only available after actions, charge two probe intervals, two readouts, one reset, and their full elapsed time. The old integer-only protocol costs \(T\) actions/readouts per word but has zero distinguishing information for every \(T\).

In the stochastic/emission setting one replicate is not a reliable statistical decision. Let \(\gamma=rK_E/3\); the paired statistic has means \(B+\gamma\) and \(B-\gamma\), with unknown but shared baseline \(B=\sum_y m_y^2\). Estimate \(B\) from the same replicate set's one-read marginal and \(S\) from paired reads, then classify by the sign of \(\widehat S-\sum_y\widehat m_y^2\). For fixed finite output alphabet size \(b\), Hoeffding plus a union bound gives a sufficient replicate count \(N=O(b^2\gamma^{-2}\log(b/\delta))\) for error at most \(\delta\); each replicate still pays the reset/action/two-read costs above. This count is in terms of the actual gap. There is no uniform finite sample guarantee when \(s\) is unbounded or \(K_E\) can approach zero: \(r\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\), and emissions can become arbitrarily uninformative. A guaranteed finite budget needs bounds \(s\le s_{\max}\) and \(K_E\ge K_{\min}>0\), plus independent resets and conditionally independent emissions.

The action/hold/readout schedule is an experimental resource and must be charged. This audit is given the three-state cycle family, its state labeling, and the two candidate generators; these are model assumptions, not acquired results. The exact positive result is only that the additional probe separates this specified pair under a stated observability condition.

## Prior-art boundary

This is a concrete controlled instance of the classical **Markov embedding problem**: recover a continuous-time generator from a discrete-time transition matrix even though the matrix exponential can have multiple valid generator logarithms. Kingman (1962) introduced the finite-chain problem; Johansen (1974) studied finite-chain embedding results including the three-state case; Davies (2010) surveys embeddable Markov matrices. The calculation above is an elementary explicit circulant alias, not a new general embedding theorem. Moore's finite-state experimental framework and subsequent automata theory identify systems only up to input/output behavior. Under the original integer-clock actions the two controlled sampled machines are behaviorally identical even with fully revealing emissions, so no automata learner can recover the unobserved continuous-time generator without extra timing/action information. The half-phase experiment is a minimal system-specific discriminator, not a new general automata-learning algorithm.
