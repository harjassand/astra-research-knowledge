# Sharp approach to the critical amplitude with real smooth potentials

7 October 2026. **Internally reconstructed mathematical contribution; historical novelty UNKNOWN.** The construction is an extension of Frank–Ivanisvili2608.00802v1, not a new solution of the conjecture their paper already resolves. ## Exact combined statement

For every n>=3 and every positive function L(r) tending to infinity, there exist real smooth V and nonzero real smooth u on R^n such that

    -Delta u + V u = 0,
    |V(x)| <= C |x|^(-1/2) L(|x|) for all sufficiently large |x|,
    D^m V(x) -> 0 for every fixed Cartesian derivative order m,
    exp(A|x|) u(x) -> 0 for every fixed A>0.

The same single pair satisfies every derivative order and every fixed exponential rate. The last assertion also gives exp(A|x|)u in L^2 for every A. The potential bound is eventual: arbitrary positive L need not be bounded below on compact intervals. Both V and u are constructed; no unknown-potential acquisition is asserted.

Cruz-Sampedro1999 Theorem1 excludes such a nonzero solution when |V|=O(r^(-1/2)), even with complex V. In the smooth bounded class, its compact-support conclusion followed by local unique continuation gives u=0. Thus the prescribed loss cannot be replaced by a bounded loss. This combines our real all-derivative-decaying construction above the threshold with an established endpoint exclusion. Historical priority of this combined extension is not established.

## Why the arbitrary-loss quantifier holds

The complete proof is `notes/r15_geometric_analysis/cycle6/ADMISSIBLE_SLOW_ENVELOPES.md`; independent gate: `notes/r07_analysis/cycle6/ADMISSIBLE_FREQUENCY_ARBITRARY_LOSS_AUDIT.md`. The audited general interface is any smooth nondecreasing a(R)>=1 tending to infinity with a(R)=R^{o(1)} and |a^(j)(R)|<=C_j a(R)R^(-j).

Use R=(K^2+r^2)^(1/2), ell=(R/a)^(1/2), kappa=a^(3/2)R^(-1/2), Phi=integral_K^R a, and E=exp(-Phi). Exact balance a/ell=a^2ell/R=kappa controls the tangential construction. The weighted sparse-domain inverse gives |W|<=C_K kappa a^(-2)E. Kernel derivatives of order m+2 cost a^(m+2), canceling the repair gain and yielding |D^m Delta F|<=C_(m,K)kappa a^m E. The harmonic-region/safe-quotient argument gives |D^mV|<=C_(m,K)a^(m+3/2)R^(-1/2). Subpower a makes this tend to zero for every fixed m.

One fixed K is selected using low-order geometric, sparsity and repair inequalities. The origin term K^2a^2/R^2 cannot be dropped; its eventual absorption after fixing K requires only sup_(R>=K) K^2 sqrt(a)/R^(3/2)<infinity, supplied by subpower growth. Higher derivative constants need not be small and never require K to depend on m. Arbitrarily long flat intervals of a do not break any of these estimates.

For prescribed L, replace it eventually by its nondecreasing lower envelope ell_L(r)=inf_(s>=r)L(s). Choose a fixed C-infinity step theta from0 to1 on[0,1] and separated numbers T_j>=j^2 with T_(j+1)>=T_j+2 and ell_L(exp(T_j)/2)>=(j+2)^(3/2). Set

    a(R)=1+sum_(j>=1) theta(log R-T_j).

The sum is locally finite, nondecreasing and divergent. At most one transition is active; chain differentiation gives |a^(m)(R)|<=C_m R^(-m). The growth bound a<=1+sqrt(logR) implies subpower growth. On the jth interval a<=j+1, whereas ell_L(R/2)>=(j+2)^(3/2). For r>=K/sqrt(3), R/2<=r, so a(R)^(3/2)<=ell_L(r)<=L(r) eventually. Since R>=r, the required potential bound follows. The stronger T_j>=exp(j) used in r15 is also valid.

Finally Phi(R)/R tends to infinity because a tends to infinity. This proves decay faster than every fixed exponential without assuming regular variation or a fixed power of r. These are existence choices, not an efficient construction from an arbitrary oracle for L.
