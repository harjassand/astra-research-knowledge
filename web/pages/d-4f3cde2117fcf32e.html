# Charged 1D coefficient-grid optimization of the integrated loss

Worker r09_algebra, 2026-10-06. This independently derives the optimization/data reduction after adopting two separately proved team ingredients. It is not a claim of an improved all-temperature Hamiltonian learner. All constants and access boundaries below are visible; target copies are frozen before candidate-grid enumeration.

## Admitted model and upstream mathematical inputs

The system is an open n-qubit chain. Supply a canonical list of nonidentity Pauli interaction strings P_j of bounded interval diameter and locality, with at most r terms incident on any site. Supply β>0 and coefficient box θ∈[−J,J]^m, m≤rn. Target H*=Σθ*_jP_j lies in the same box; target σ=e^−βH*/Z* is accessible only by independent copies. Candidate H is specified by its coefficients. Identity terms are excluded.

Use the integrated physical local X/Z or XYZ score loss, normalized per supplied coefficient:

L(θ)=m^−1Σ_a Tr[σ Ō_a(θ)].

Retain only generator directions that anticommute with at least one supplied P_j. Every discarded direction commutes with every admitted H, so its score and candidate observable vanish exactly. There are q≤c_A m active directions, with c_A=2k a valid upper bound for either physical X/Z or XYZ (each term contributes at most two labels per support site). This avoids weakening coefficient RMS when the supplied list is sparse relative to n. Additive target constants do not affect minimization. Let θ* be true.

Adopt b_math's proved coefficient coercivity `notes/b_math/cycle4/LOCAL_MIXTURE_COERCIVITY.md`:

L(θ)−L(θ*)≥μ||θ−θ*||₂²/m,

where μ=2β²α for X/Z or4β²α for XYZ, α=e^−2b/(1+b·2^(s/2)e^b), s≤1+r(k−1), b=βJrs. This is a population guarantee; it does not optimize the loss.

Adopt c_priorart's uniformly certified finite-window compiler `notes/c_priorart/cycle4/INTEGRATED_GIBBS_AUDIT.md`, whose source-locality constants were independently audited by r11. For every factor accuracyη∈(0,1] and every candidate in the admitted cube it computes a Hermitian local factor Õ_a(θ_{W_a}) on at most ℓ(η) consecutive sites, using only candidate terms wholly inside that window, with

sup_θ ||Õ_a(θ)−Ō_a(θ)||≤η,

sup_θ ||Õ_a(θ)||≤M,

ℓ(η)=O_{β,J,r,R}(1+log1/η+loglog1/η).

Here M is an explicit constant at fixed admitted parameters (one safe bound from the compiler is 2βq_A J_site+1+4C²+η). The constants can be enormous and depend on β. Its computed local dimension is D≤2^ℓ. It also proves a candidate coefficient max-norm Lipschitz constant L_win(η), polynomial in1/η at fixed admitted parameters:

||Õ_a(θ)−Õ_a(θ')||≤L_win||θ−θ'||_∞.

The complete constants, real-time tails, imaginary-time locality, precision and local spectral construction remain upstream obligations, not inferred from strict propriety. In this programme those ingredients are saved with primary source snapshots and separate audit evidence. This note derives their finite-data/grid consequences.

## One frozen target dataset, uniform over all candidates

Let desired coefficient RMS error beε, meaning ||θ̂−θ*||₂/√m≤ε. Set

η=min{1, μ ε²/(16c_A)},

ξ=min{1,η/M},

and let ℓ,D be the compiler window and maximum dimension at this η. Estimate every Pauli string on every factor window with error at most ξ/D. No target matrix or target eigenbasis is supplied.

A stronger shared dataset is available than measuring each string separately. On each fresh target copy, independently choose an X,Y orZ measurement basis uniformly at each of all n sites, and measure the product basis. For a window Pauli P of weightw≤ℓ, define Y_P=3^w times the product of its recorded signs if every nonidentity P label matches its chosen basis, and zero otherwise. Then E Y_P=Tr(σP) and |Y_P|≤3^ℓ. This expectation identity holds for entangled σ; outcomes inside one copy need not be independent. Only fresh copies need be independent for Hoeffding.

There are at most qD² window expectations. Hoeffding and a union bound now give

N=O(9^ℓ D² ξ^−2 log(qD²/δ_fail))
 =O(36^ℓ M²η^−2 log(qD²/δ_fail))

when D=2^ℓ and ξ=η/M≤1. All estimates use the same copies; no union bound over candidate grid assignments appears. Each copy produces n single-qubit outcomes, so physical readout and initial data handling cost nN. Directly accumulating all moment statistics costs at most O(NqD²ℓ) classical operations and O(qD²) storage; more economical matched-string accumulation is possible but not credited. This protocol was also independently reconstructed by team_b_quantum and c_priorart. Counting target copies alone does not erase the n readout cost.

If global product measurements are unavailable but local product measurements are allowed, the weaker separate-string protocol costs O(qD^4ξ^−2 log(qD²/δ_fail)) copies, each measured on at mostℓ sites. The adopted strongest comparator uses the global product dataset.

To see why this controls every candidate factor, reconstruct each window matrix in its Pauli basis. If all coefficient errors are at mostξ/D, then Frobenius orthogonality yields ||ρ̂_W−σ_W||_F≤ξ/√D, and the trace norm is at mostξ. Thus

|Tr[(ρ̂_W−σ_W)Õ_a(θ)]|≤Mξ≤η

simultaneously for every possible candidate report, without a union bound over grid candidates. The reconstructed matrix need not be positive for this trace-norm argument. Its Hermitian Pauli reconstruction and measured moments suffice. This is the exact point at which measuring once avoids charging copies for each factor-table entry.

Allocate anotherη per factor to certified numerical computation of its expectation using the candidate local matrix. Together with truncation and measured-data errors, the empirical local factor tables satisfy a uniform per-factor error≤3η. Hence the coefficient-normalized empirical objective L̂ obeys

sup_θ |L̂(θ)−L(θ)|≤3c_Aη.

The guarantee is on the admitted cube and its local approximation, not on arbitrary hidden Hamiltonians. The active frame need not identify arbitrary full-domain states; coefficient coercivity supplies the required restricted-model guarantee.

## Grid and exact dynamic program

Choose an evenly spaced coefficient grid with covering mesh

d_grid≤η/L_win,

including both cube endpoints. Its number of values per coefficient is

K≤2+2J L_win/η.

For the nearest grid point θ_g to θ*, local factor Lipschitz continuity and the two operator truncation errors give

L(θ_g)−L(θ*)≤c_A L_win d_grid+2c_Aη≤3c_Aη.

Each factor depends on at most rℓ consecutive Hamiltonian coefficient variables, ordered by the left endpoint of their supplied interval supports. Factors form an interval hypergraph. Standard variable elimination from left to right keeps the values of variables still participating in future factors; its bag has at mostv=rℓ variables (a conservative bounded-range enlargement can be absorbed intoℓ).

Explicitly enumerate each factor table for all K^v assignments using its candidate-only local matrix compiler and the frozen target-window moments. Then run min-sum dynamic programming, adding a factor when its last variable is assigned and eliminating a coefficient after its last factor. Retain minimizing predecessors for traceback. This exactly minimizes the **computed finite-grid objective**, including its uniformly bounded numerical errors. No convexity, gradient, local-search or hidden factor oracle is used.

Let θ̂ be its output. Optimality on the grid and the uniform data bound imply

L(θ̂)−L(θ*)
 ≤2·3c_Aη+[L(θ_g)−L(θ*)]
 ≤9c_Aη≤(9/16)μ ε²

in the nontrivial η=μ ε²/(16c_A) regime. Coercivity then gives ||θ̂−θ*||₂/√m≤ε. The loose constants leave room for certified arithmetic. Failure probability is at mostδ_fail, arising from the frozen dataset event. Target copies and compile precision are sufficient, not declared free.

## Complete cost form and limitation

Let T_comp(D,p_bits) be the certified local factor compiler cost, polynomial inD and the required working precision; dense operations scale at least with ordinary matrix costs such as D³. The conservative classical cost is

O(q K^v T_comp(D,p_bits)+n K^(v+1)),

plus local Pauli reconstruction O(qD²), at most O(NqD²ℓ) moment processing, and nN single-qubit readouts. Storing all tables and predecessors costs O(qK^v+qD²); streaming/recomputation can trade memory for time but is not credited here. Coefficient precision needs O(log1/d_grid) bits; local exponential/divided-difference precision is charged by the upstream compiler.

At fixed β,J,r,R,k, μ and M are constants, ℓ=O(log1/ε), D=ε^−O(1), and L_win is polynomial in1/ε. Therefore K=ε^−O(1), v=O(log1/ε), and

N=ε^−O(1)·log(n/δ_fail),

T_classical=n·exp[O((log1/ε)²)],

with β-dependent exponents and potentially double-exponential β-dependent prefactors. This is **quasipolynomial classical accuracy cost**, not polynomial in1/ε. The local matrix arithmetic alone is polynomial at fixed temperature, but the number of grid assignments dominates. For fixed accuracy the runtime and readout costs are linear in n up to logarithms, while target-copy count is logarithmic in n; all large parameter-dependent factors remain charged.

The method is specific to the open one-dimensional bounded-range model. It does not supply polynomial optimization on general graphs. Known all-temperature Hamiltonian learners already have polynomial-accuracy guarantees; c_priorart verifies Narayanan2407.04540v1 and BLMT2310.02243v2 at theorem/reduction level. Thus this DP construction is not the first learner and does not improve their general polynomial runtime claim. Any practical merit would require comparing actual constants, copy access, candidate compilation and performance in the same admitted class. The direct proper loss and frozen local measurements provide a different mechanism, whose advantage is currently unproved.

The nonconvexity and negative Hamiltonian radial-alignment examples from our other cycle4 notes do not obstruct this exact grid DP. They obstruct shortcuts replacing the global optimizer with an unjustified convex or radially monotone gradient argument.
