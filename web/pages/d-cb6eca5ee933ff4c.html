PROPOSITION (derived here): the source positive hot generator has no uniformly
finite Landau grazing limit on any fixed nonzero separated cold/hot pair.

Precisely: let B,h >=0 have finite nonzero masses, compact velocity supports
separated by d>0, and take kinetic factor |v-w|^gamma with fixed real gamma
(or any positive continuous kinetic factor on these compact separated sets).
Let b_delta be a nonnegative symmetric outgoing angular measure supported on
the union of grazing cones of aperture delta at +/- incoming relative direction.
Use particle-exchange symmetry so the two child marginals define a symmetric
bilinear gain P_delta. Let
  beta_delta = integral b_delta(d sigma),
  m2_delta = integral sin^2(theta) b_delta(d sigma),
and require 0<c<=m2_delta<=C (the nondegenerate Landau time normalization).
Because sin^2(theta)<=delta^2 on its support,
  beta_delta >= c delta^(-2).
Let nu_delta,B(v)=beta_delta integral |v-w|^gamma B(w)dw, with harmless
normalization constants absorbed into beta_delta. Define
  A_delta(B,h)=2P_delta(B,h)-nu_delta,B h.
Elastic incoming/outgoing symmetry gives exactly
  integral A_delta(B,h)dv
    = integral nu_delta,B(v)h(v)dv
    = beta_delta integral integral |v-w|^gamma B(w)h(v)dwdv.
The last pair integral is bounded below by a strictly positive constant
depending only on fixed B,h,gamma and their compact separated supports.
Consequently integral A_delta >= c(B,h) delta^(-2).
Since B,h supports lie in a common fixed compact ball and elastic collisions
keep both outputs in a common compact ball, this divergence is witnessed by
one fixed smooth compactly supported velocity test equal to 1 on all those
supports. It is a distributional divergence, not merely a failure at infinity.

The same argument works for smooth cold beams of any fixed width and a hot
density away from them. A point beam B=delta_w is permissible in the source
kernel and makes the location of the divergent source even clearer: one child
remains near v, while the other remains near w, and both are labeled hot.

Why the standard grazing limit does not fix this:
The actual derivative of Q(F,F) at B is
  L_delta,B h = 2P_delta(B,h) - nu_delta,B h - nu_delta,h B
             = A_delta(B,h) - nu_delta,h B.
Its integral is 0. The omitted negative cold-parent loss is exactly the
delta^(-2) cancellation. The collision weak form for L has the combination
  phi(v')+phi(w')-phi(v)-phi(w),
which is second order after angular averaging, whereas A omits -phi(w).
A finite Landau linearization thus requires retaining the term that destroys
the source's positive hot population and positive-history lower bounds.
