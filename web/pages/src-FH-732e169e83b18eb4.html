## Primary literature

1. **T. J. Meijer et al., “A General Non-Strict Finsler’s Lemma,” arXiv:2403.10306v1 (2024).** Official HTML: <https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10306v1>. Read lines 64–75 for the failure of unrestricted non-strict Finsler and the role of indefiniteness, and lines 75–91 for the indefinite non-strict lemma. Used only to attribute that quadratic lemma as established mathematics. The audit independently reconstructs the needed scalar version and does not rely on the paper for the proof. The source note supplies its own proof.

2. **G. Bruno and F. Pasqualotto, “Quasiconvexity for the Dacorogna–Marcellini Energy,” arXiv:2608.06367v1 (2026).** Official HTML: <https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1>. Read lines 27–34 for the theorem's scope (Dacorogna–Marcellini energy and quartics invariant under left and right rotations), lines 120–125 for its exact-two-form correction, lines 132–165 for the heat-flow criterion, and lines 349–393 for the invariant quartic representation and rank-one-convexity cone. Used as a comparison and attribution boundary: this is a smaller bi-rotation-invariant class than the nine-coefficient left-SO(2) cone in the audited note. No claim of priority or novelty is inferred from this comparison.

3. **A. Guerra and R. Teixeira da Costa, “Numerical evidence towards a positive answer to Morrey’s problem,” arXiv:2006.09063v1.** Official HTML: <https://arxiv.org/html/2006.09063v1>. Read lines 55–70 for the distinction between rank-one convexity, finite-support gradient Young measures, and laminate recognition; lines 175–205 for the cited three-wave results and the planar-versus-higher-target distinction; and lines 205–247 for the authors' expressly heuristic rank-one-connection statistics. Used only as background for the role of three-wave/laminate geometry and to avoid treating computational evidence as a proof. The exact 3x2 field and cubic identity in Section 8 of the audited note were not located in this source's accessed passages; the ledger does not attribute those exact formulas to it.

4. **“Polyconvexity with Moments and Sums of Squares,” arXiv:2604.11124v1 (2026).** Official HTML: <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11124v1>. Read lines 258–265, especially Proposition 2.4 and Remark 2.2. The proposition states that for its specified `f(X)=|X|²(|X|²−2 det X)`, any convex `g(X,det X)=f(X)` cannot be polynomial. Used only to preserve the distinction between polyconvexity and a polynomial convex lift; no SOS or lift restriction is equated with polyconvexity.
