## Clipped Gram, eigenline and resolvent

Let m0<n^2 be the retained input row count after l1-small-row screening.
Put

`epsilon=min(1,eps_g,eta0/16)`,

`q=epsilon eta0^5/[2^40(n+1)^6(K+1)^4]`,

`rho=q/[4K^2(m0+1)]`.

For each medium row z_i=p_i/r_i^2, max z_ij<=1/K and sum z_ij<=1.
Thus ||z_i||^2<=1/K. Approximate each exp(beta u_i) by a rational within
rho and clip to nonnegative. The Gram approximation remains PSD and
entrywise nonnegative. Keep the large diagonal and eta11^T exact.

There are two signs per row and the prefactor is at most 32K. Therefore
the matrix error is at most

`64 m0 rho <=16q/K^2 <=q/256`.

For a unit physical direction the relative first and second weight
derivative coefficients have magnitude below one. Indeed
|beta g.h|<=1/8 and |beta u''[h,h]|<=1/(2K), so their first/second
absolute coefficients are <=1/8 and <=1/64+1/(2K)<1. Using the same
approximate weight gives the same q/256 bound for M' and M'' as a
directional operator estimate.

The original independently proved eigenline/reduced-resolvent construction,
or its bounded-bit normalized-squaring replacement, gives rational
rank-one P_hat, scalar L_hat, and symmetric R_hat with

`||P_hat-P||<=3q/eta`, `|L_hat-L|<=2q`,

`||R_hat-R||<=19q/eta^3`, `||R_hat||<=2/eta`.

Here P=vv^T and R is the reduced inverse of LI-M. The earlier q/256
Gram error, gap and inverse proof are reused, rather than assuming a
numerical eigensolver supplies these certificates. Since L<8.5 and q is
tiny, L_hat<9. In the estimates below eta<=1, ||R||<=1/eta, and the
physical-direction derivatives satisfy ||M'||,||M''||<=2. The derivative
Gram error above gives ||M_hat'||<=3.

## Gradient and eigenvalue Hessian

For any unit h write

`A(h)=tr(P M'[h])`,

`B(h)=tr(P M''[h])+2tr(P M'[h] R M'[h])`.

The difference of two rank-one projectors has rank at most two, hence
its nuclear norm is <=6q/eta. Projector and derivative-factor errors give

`|A_hat-A| <=12q/eta+q/256 <13q/eta`.

The first term of B has the same bound. For C=tr(P M' R M'), telescope
the projector, first derivative, inverse, and second derivative factors.
Their errors are respectively bounded by

`24q/eta^2`, `2q/(256eta)`, `114q/eta^3`, `6q/(256eta)`.

For example, the inverse term uses ||M_hat'||<=3, ||M'||<=2 and
||R_hat-R||<=19q/eta^3. Summing is below 139q/eta^3. Thus

`|B_hat-B|<512q/eta^3`.

Also |A|<=2, |A_hat|<=3 and |B|<=2+8/eta<=10/eta. For
f(L)=(L-8)_+^4 on L<=9,

`|f'|<=4`, `|f''|<=12`,

and the respective Lipschitz constants are 12 and 24. Applying these
bounds to f'A and f''A^2+f'B gives gradient error below

`(52+48)q/eta <2^7 q/eta`,

and Hessian directional error below

`[2048+240+192+780]q/eta^3 <2^12 q/eta^3`.

For the four terms in the last bound, use the B error, the f' error
times |B|, the f'' error times A^2, and
12|A_hat-A|(|A_hat|+|A|), respectively. The positive-part quartic has
the needed scalar differentiability at L=8; no branch singularity enters.

## Motion Gram and exact PSD approximation

For a unit direction h, its exact motion Gram is

`D(h)=tr(P M'[h] R^2 M'[h])=||R M'[h]v||^2`.

The approximation replaces all three matrix ingredients with the same
symmetric R_hat and rank-one rational P_hat. Its coefficient matrix has
entries tr(P_hat M_j_hat R_hat^2 M_l_hat). This is a rational PSD Gram
matrix: taking a rational representative qv of P_hat, its quadratic
forms equal ||R_hat M'_hat qv||^2/(qv^T qv). A unit-vector square root
is unnecessary.

The squared-resolvent error is <=57q/eta^4. Telescope P first, then the
two derivative factors using exact R^2, and finally R^2 using the two
approximate derivatives. The three total contributions are

`24q/eta^3`, `5q/(256eta^2)`, `513q/eta^4`.

Consequently |D_hat-D|<538q/eta^4. Let c(L)=a^3L/2. On L<=9,
|c|<=9/2 and c is 14-Lipschitz. Since D<=4/eta^2,

`|c_hat D_hat-cD|`

` <=(9/2)*538q/eta^4+28q*4/eta^2`

` <=2533q/eta^4`.

Combining this with the quartic Hessian gives

`||F_hat-F|| <[4096+2533]q/eta^4 <2^13q/eta^4 <eta0/16`.

For symmetric matrices the supremum over all unit directional quadratic
forms is the operator norm, so no entrywise-to-operator conversion is
missing. The gradient directional bound similarly gives its Euclidean
norm error below eps_g, by the definition of q.

A safe filter norm bound is M0=256/eta0^2. Directly, the true form is
bounded by 48+8+32/eta+2eta+1+18/eta^2<=109/eta^2; the peer's looser
118/eta0^2 is therefore safe. The tiny approximation and eta/16 shift
are below the remaining margin. M0 dominates the positive perpendicular
block as required by the exact-legal filter proof.

For a scalar endpoint evaluation additionally require q<=eps_v/8. The
Lipschitz bound gives |f(L_hat)-f(L)|<=8q<=eps_v. The other potential
terms are exact. All precision parameters have polynomial original-input
