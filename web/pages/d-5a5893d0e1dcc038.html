## Exact interface

Input: a real harmonic polynomial P of degree at most D>=1, a finite collection of its nondegenerate critical points, and an exact equal-weight spherical 2D-design of N distinct directions. Output: N distinct positions near the unit sphere, all with charge exactly 1, whose potential has corresponding nondegenerate critical points in a small ball. Further arbitrarily small changes of positions, preserving all strengths, make the whole potential Morse with a finite critical set. The construction does not claim to retain every critical point of P, to preserve a prescribed shell exactly, or to provide an efficient design algorithm.

## 1. Independent moment and normalization reconstruction

Let sigma be normalized area on S² and let Y_lm be a real orthonormal spherical-harmonic basis. For the nonconstant degrees 1 through D there are M=D(D+2) coordinates. Set E_(lm),i=Y_lm(omega_i). Design quadrature applies both to each Y_lm and to every product Y_lm Y_l'm', because their degrees are at most 2D. Hence E1=0 and EEᵀ=N I. Distinctness is separately supplied by the design premise.

The normalized potential of radial sites (1+t_i)omega_i has degree-l solid-harmonic coefficient

    A_lm(t)/(2l+1),
    A_lm(t)=N⁻¹ sum_i (1+t_i)^(-l-1) Y_lm(omega_i).

The denominator 2l+1 is necessary: orthonormality is with normalized area rather than unnormalized area. It follows from the addition formula sum_m Y_lm(u)Y_lm(v)=(2l+1)P_l(u·v). Differentiating gives J=-(Lambda E)/N with Lambda_(lm)=l+1. The proposed B=-Eᵀ Lambda⁻¹ satisfies JB=I exactly. There is no hidden variable-strength operation: B acts only on radial positions. Multiplying the final normalized potential by N gives actual unit charges and leaves its critical set and Hessian invertibility unchanged.

For every h, Cauchy–Schwarz and the addition formula give

    ||Bh||∞ <= [sum_l (2l+1)/(l+1)²]^(1/2)||h||₂
             <= sqrt(M)||h||₂/2 < (D+1)||h||₂/2.

All signs, dimensions and factors of N in the draft are correct.

## 2. Quantitative inverse, with every norm conversion

Take s=1/[2(D+1)]. If ||t||∞<=s, each scalar second derivative has multiplier (l+1)(l+2)(1+t_i)^(-l-3). The largest multiplier is at most 8(D+1)(D+2), since

    -(D+3)log(1-s) <= (D+3)/(2D+1) <= 4/3,
    exp(4/3)<8.

For a fixed site, the Euclidean norm of its full harmonic evaluation vector is sqrt(M). Applying the triangle inequality before averaging over sites gives, for arbitrary v,w in R^N,

    ||D²A(t)[v,w]||₂
      <=8(D+1)(D+2)sqrt(M)||v||∞||w||∞.

There is no missing factor sqrt(N): the summands are averaged, and this estimate does not invoke a Euclidean norm on the site space. Composing both inputs with B yields

    ||D²F(h)|| <=2(D+1)³(D+2)sqrt(M)
               <=4(D+1)^5,
    F(h)=A(Bh).

Thus K=16(D+1)^5 is valid. For r=1/(4K), the closed Euclidean h-ball of radius r lies inside ||Bh||∞<=s. On that ball F(0)=0, DF(0)=I, and the integral Taylor formulas imply

    ||F(h)-h|| <=K||h||²/2,
    ||DF(h)-I|| <=K||h||.

If ||z||<=r/2, T_z(h)=z+h-F(h) maps the ball into itself with image radius <=5r/8 and Lipschitz constant <=1/4. Banach yields the required unique fixed point in this ball. Its norm is <=(8/7)||z||, so the claimed weaker <=2||z|| bound holds. Choosing the target amplitude smaller makes radii arbitrarily close to 1; sending rho to zero alone need not do so because the highest-degree target does not depend on rho.

## 3. Polynomial realization and the C² tail

Write P=P_0+sum p_lm |y|^lY_lm(y/|y|), and S²=sum (2l+1)²p_lm². Fix epsilon>0 no larger than r/[2 max(1,S)]. The target

    z_lm=(2l+1)epsilon rho^(D-l)p_lm

has norm at most r/2 for 0<rho<=1. This matches every nonconstant coefficient through degree D exactly after x=rho y. Subtracting V_rho(0) removes the uncontrolled constant moment, which cannot affect equilibria.

All resulting radii are at least a_min=1-s>=3/4. On |y|<=R+1, with q=rho(R+1)/a_min<1/2, the omitted Newton series is bounded by a_min⁻¹ q^(D+1)/(1-q). This bound is uniform over the rho-dependent sites. The remainder is a harmonic function of y on the larger ball, so ordinary interior derivative estimates on unit balls bound its first and second derivatives on B_R by a dimension-only constant times that supremum. Consequently the scaled remainder is bounded in C²(B_R) by C_(D,R)rho/epsilon and tends to zero.

The quantifier order matters and is correct: D, P, finitely many witness balls, R and epsilon are fixed; only then rho is made sufficiently small. No uniformity in D or polynomial placement precision is inferred. The gradient contraction argument in disjoint witness balls gives distinct nondegenerate critical points and preserves their Hessian inertia. Arbitrarily small epsilon and sufficiently small rho can satisfy radial-shell and target-ball requirements simultaneously.

## 4. Generic Morse configurations at fixed strengths

Let G(a,x)=grad_x sum_i |x-a_i|⁻¹ on the open manifold where sites are pairwise distinct and x is not a site. For v=x-a_i, the derivative in the parameter a_i is

    D_(a_i)G=|v|⁻³ I-3|v|⁻⁵ vvᵀ.

Its eigenvalues are -2|v|⁻³ radially and |v|⁻³ twice transversely. Hence this single parameter block is invertible and G is a submersion. Apply the finite-dimensional parametric transversality theorem, or Sard's theorem to the projection G⁻¹(0)->configuration space: generic parameters make D_xG invertible at every zero. The noncompact domain causes no logical gap; it is a second-countable finite-dimensional manifold, and a countable exhaustion gives the same residual/full-measure conclusion. Every neighborhood of the constructed configuration therefore contains one with a globally Morse potential.

The selected finite critical points survive a sufficiently small position perturbation. All equilibria of positive unit charges lie in the convex hull of their sites, because the equilibrium equation writes x as their positive weighted average with weights |x-a_i|⁻³. A neighborhood of each site is free of zeros: the singular self-gradient dominates the bounded other gradients. The full critical set is thus a closed subset of a compact set separated from all sites. Nondegeneracy makes every zero isolated; compactness then forces finiteness. This step requires positive charges and must not be exported unchanged to arbitrary signs.

## 5. All-N composition and primary source boundary

I independently opened Bondarenko–Radchenko–Viazovska, *Well-separated spherical designs*, arXiv:1303.5991v2. The verified version date is 2013-07-10. The exact equal-weight definition appears on PDF page 2. Theorem 1 on PDF page 4 supplies, for every t and every N>C₂t², an N-point spherical t-design with separation at least lambda₂ N⁻¹/². Thus it supplies distinct directions and an all-N condition, not merely a sparse subsequence. I checked these statements, not the complete proof of the design theorem.

Conditional on the separately audited grid theorem (degree <=256m and >=8m³ Morse critical points), take t=512m and any integer m with C₂(512m)²<N. The largest such m is bounded below by a positive constant times sqrt(N) for all sufficiently large N. The compiler therefore gives >=cN^(3/2) equilibria for exactly N unit charges, for a universal c>0. No padding by small charges is involved. The genericity step may leave the original radial rays but changes only positions and preserves the charge count and strengths.

Kintali's available superlinear manuscript uses variable positive strengths in its compilation. Its already claimed N^(3/2) positive-charge exponent preempts presenting that exponent alone as new. Searches for equal-charge superlinear equilibria and the spherical-design realization interface did not identify an exact earlier unit-strength statement; this is limited evidence and does not establish priority. The classical potential-theory motivation already connects spherical designs to approximate field cancellation, so the design prerequisite and zero-jet cancellation are established prior. Our audited deduction is the radial right-inverse realization of arbitrary small nonconstant harmonic jets and its equal-strength consequence, with novelty unresolved.
