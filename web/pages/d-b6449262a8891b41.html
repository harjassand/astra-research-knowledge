# Independent audit: hidden cycle quartic SOS-face acquisition

2026-10-06. **PASS as a restricted exact coefficient-input acquisition theorem.** The odd-cycle determinant/logdet proof reconstructs correctly. Its determinant obstruction at even cycles is a failure of that route, not a failure of face acquisition. A simpler coefficient-only construction below acquires the hidden diagonal space directly and works for both parities. No hidden coordinates, zeros or Gram factors are supplied to either acquisition routine. The promised normal form is supplied and is consequential. Novelty or separation from competent algebraic algorithms is not established.

## 1. Exact interface and coefficient-derived metric

The promised input is the expanded rational coefficient dictionary of

\[
p(x)=q(Lx)^TQq(Lx),\qquad q_i(y)=y_i^2-1,\quad
Q=I+\epsilon A(C_n),\quad L\in GL_n(\mathbb Q),\quad
0<\epsilon<1/4,
\]

originally for odd \(n\ge5\). The learner receives \(p\), variable names and this class promise; it receives neither \(L\), \(Q\), a graph coordinate labelling, nor any zeros. Final exact polynomial identity and positive rational LDL weights establish certificate soundness even outside the promise. Failure gives UNKNOWN or rejection of this class, never a nonnegativity verdict. The replay's assertions are not a total arbitrary-input classifier.

Writing \(b=1+2\epsilon\), row regularity gives \(Q\mathbf1=b\mathbf1\), hence

\[
p_0=nb,\qquad p_2(x)=-2b\,x^TL^TLx,\qquad
M=-\tfrac14\operatorname{Hess}(p_2)=bL^TL>0.
\]

Thus the coefficient input supplies the metric, **and also**
\(b=p_0/n\), \(\epsilon=(p_0/n-1)/2\). Unknown epsilon does not require spectral inference. This observation was absent from the original pipeline.

Let \(T\) be the fully symmetric fourth tensor obtained by dividing each quartic coefficient by its multinomial multiplicity. Define
\[
\mathcal C(S)_{ab}=\sum_{cd}T_{abcd}(M^{-1}SM^{-1})_{cd}.
\]
For \(S=L^TS'L\), direct substitution in all four tensor indices gives
\(\mathcal C(S)=L^T\mathcal C'(S')L\). In canonical coordinates,
\(T_{iiii}=1\), \(T_{iijj}=\epsilon/3\) on edges, and zero otherwise. Consequently the diagonal block is
\(b^{-2}[I+(\epsilon/3)A(C_n)]\); the edge block is scalar
\(\lambda_e=2\epsilon/(3b^2)\); and the nonedge block is zero. Since
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal C'|_{\mathrm{Diag}})
\ge (1-2\epsilon/3)/b^2 > 2\epsilon/(3b^2)>0,
\]
the edge and zero blocks are disjoint from the entire diagonal block. All constants, factors of two and covariance/congruence conventions in the source are correct.

## 2. The original determinant/logdet acquisition passes

The edge block has dimension \(n\) and rational eigenvalue \(\lambda_e\). For odd \(n\), the pencil in any acquired rational edge-space basis has
\[
\det S(z)=2\det(L)^2\prod_{i=1}^n\ell_i(z),
\]
where the \(\ell_i\) are independent rational linear forms. A determinant permutation decomposes into cycle covers. A proper simple cycle in the cycle graph is only a two-cycle, and an odd number of vertices cannot be covered by two-cycles. The two oriented full cycles remain, each with positive permutation sign. This proves the identity without determinant expansion or supplied edge coordinates.

At a nonsingular rational basepoint, put \(r_i=\nabla\ell_i/\ell_i(z_0)\), \(R=[r_1\cdots r_n]\), and \(\alpha_i=\ell_i(h)/\ell_i(z_0)\). Differentiation gives
\[
H_2=-\operatorname{Hess}\log|\det S|=RR^T,
\quad H_3=\tfrac12D_h\operatorname{Hess}\log|\det S|
=R\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_i)R^T.
\]
The sign and one-half factor in the source are correct. With \(A_i=S(z_0)^{-1}B_i\), the trace formulas are
\(H_{2,ij}=\operatorname{tr}(A_iA_j)\) and
\(H_{3,ij}=\frac12\operatorname{tr}(A_hA_iA_j+A_iA_hA_j)\).
Right eigenvectors of \(H_2^{-1}H_3\) are \(R^{-T}e_i\), not columns of \(R\). The source uses the correct orientation. Since
\(\ell_j(u_i)=\ell_j(z_0)r_j^Tu_i=0\) for \(j\ne i\), \(S(u_i)\) is one acquired edge matrix.

For \(S_i=cL^TE_{jk}L\),
\(M^{-1}S_i=(c/b)L^{-1}E_{jk}L\). Hence
\(\kappa_i=\frac12\operatorname{tr}[(M^{-1}S_i)^2]=(c/b)^2>0\),
and the normalized square is \(L^{-1}(E_{jj}+E_{kk})L\). These projectors commute, and each cycle vertex has a distinct incident-edge signature. Joint splitting therefore gives precisely the \(n\) rational coordinate axes. No Euclidean symmetry of these similarity-transformed projectors is assumed.

The bad basepoints are the union of \(n\) rational hyperplanes. Conditional on a valid basepoint, ratio collisions are at most \(\binom n2\) nonzero rational hyperplanes in \(h\). Their grid probability bound is valid. There is no additional exceptional promised polynomial or transform. However, the checked implementation fixes its pseudorandom seed: the theorem's probability and expected-attempt guarantee apply to genuinely independently sampled choices, not a worst-case guarantee for that deterministic seeded replay. A capped search returns UNKNOWN when it does not complete; exact return soundness is unaffected.

At \(n=5\), the zero block also has dimension five and its complement graph is another five-cycle, so selecting it may still recover the same axes. This special successful branch is legitimate. For even cycles the stated determinant does not factor into independent edge forms; it does not follow that the axes are inaccessible.

## 3. Stronger competent comparator: directly acquire the diagonal cluster

**Universal deduction from the same coefficient input:** for every cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\), with the stated epsilon interval,
\[
\boxed{\quad\mathcal D=\operatorname{im}
 [\mathcal C(\mathcal C-\lambda_e I)]
=L^T\mathrm{Diag}_nL,\quad
\lambda_e=(b-1)/(3b^2),\quad b=p_0/n.\quad}
\]

Proof: the polynomial \(t(t-\lambda_e)\) kills both the zero and edge blocks and is invertible on the diagonal block by its strict spectral separation. The image is therefore the entire diagonal block. This argument is independent of parity, repeated diagonal eigenvalues, graph twins, and any determinant factorization. It uses rational matrix multiplication and columnspace computation only, with **no catalecticant characteristic-polynomial factorization**.

For a rational basis \(D_1,\ldots,D_n\) of \(\mathcal D\), the matrices
\(A_k=M^{-1}D_k\) span \(L^{-1}\mathrm{Diag}_nL\) (the common factor \(1/b\) does not affect the span). They commute and their common eigenspaces are precisely the hidden axes. A simple rational element can be acquired deterministically:
\[
A(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t^kA_{k+1}.
\]
In hidden coordinates each pairwise diagonal difference is a nonzero polynomial in \(t\) of degree at most \(n-1\), because a full diagonal-space basis separates every pair. There are therefore at most
\(B=(n-1)\binom n2\) bad integer values. Testing \(t=0,\ldots,B\) guarantees a simple-spectrum element. Its eigenvalues are rational: all hidden diagonal entries are rational. Univariate factoring of its degree-\(n\) characteristic polynomial and rational nullspaces recover a rational axis matrix \(V\). The coefficient normal form and exact LDL verification complete the certificate as below.

This is a deterministic polynomial-bit acquisition theorem for the entire promised cycle class, including \(C_6\). It removes the determinant and Jordan-multiplier steps altogether. The all-single-quadratic-covariants obstruction remains true, but it does not obstruct this equivariant matrix subspace and joint diagonalization. Thus it supplies no separation from a competent same-input tensor/algebra algorithm.

Optional broader mathematical scope: for a supplied regular degree \(d\ge1\), \(Q=I+\epsilon A(G)\), \(0<\epsilon<1/d\), one has \(b=1+d\epsilon=p_0/n\) and the same cluster image with \(\lambda_e=2\epsilon/(3b^2)\). The lower diagonal bound \((1-d\epsilon/3)/b^2>\lambda_e\) follows from \(\epsilon(d+2)<3\), which follows from \(\epsilon d<1\). No twin-free promise is needed. This algebraic extension is proved here but not tested as a broad graph benchmark; it does not resolve arbitrary positive quartic tensors or unequal diagonal metrics.

## 4. Subsequent Jordan multiplier lemma independently passes

For any loopless undirected graph define \(U(G)=\operatorname{span}\{E_{ij}:ij\in E(G)\}\). A symmetric matrix \(X\) satisfies
\(XU+UX\subset U\) exactly when its offdiagonal entries can be nonzero only between **nonadjacent false twins**, pairs \(a,b\) with \(N(a)=N(b)\). Diagonal entries are arbitrary.

Necessity: for input \(E_{ij}\), its \((i,i)\) Jordan-product entry is \(2X_{ij}\), so every edge entry of \(X\) is zero. Suppose \(a,b\) are a nonedge and \(X_{ab}\ne0\). For every neighbor \(c\) of \(b\), input \(E_{bc}\) has product entry \((a,c)=X_{ab}\), so \(a,c\) must be an edge. Thus \(N(b)\subseteq N(a)\); symmetry gives the reverse inclusion. Sufficiency: a diagonal multiplier rescales each edge; a symmetric false-twin entry substitutes one endpoint by its twin, again giving an allowed edge. These basis checks extend by linearity.

For a twin-free graph the multiplier space is exactly the full diagonal space. In input coordinates the correct linear constraint is on **M-self-adjoint** matrices \(X\), \(X^TM=MX\), acting on \(U_{\rm op}=M^{-1}U\): require \(XA+AX\in U_{\rm op}\) for every basis element \(A\). It is similarity-equivariant; restricting ordinary Euclidean symmetric \(X\) after a general hidden \(L\) would be incorrect. The new source code uses \(X=M^{-1}H\), \(H=H^T\), correctly. Both its annihilator membership equations and commuting checks reconstruct the right space.

False twins are a real exception for this route: \(C_4\) has two twin pairs and multiplier dimension six, rather than four. Rotations within the twin blocks preserve the edge space, so that space alone does not distinguish its axes. This does not refute coefficient-only SOS recovery: the direct cluster comparator above still recovers them. All cycles \(C_n\), \(n\ge5\), are twin-free, including \(C_6\).

## 5. Unique Gram and face dimension: one wording correction

If \(LV\) is a scaled permutation, \(p(Vy)\) equals
\(q'(y)^TQ'q'(y)\), \(q'_i=y_i^2-a_i\), with rational \(a_i>0\) and \(Q'>0\). For diagonal scaling \(d_i\), explicitly \(a_i=d_i^{-2}\) and \(Q'=\operatorname{diag}(d_i^2)Q\operatorname{diag}(d_i^2)\), up to permutation. Read \(Q'_{ii}\) from \(y_i^4\), \(Q'_{ij}\) from half \(y_i^2y_j^2\), and solve \(Q'a=-\operatorname{coeff}_{y^2}(p)/2\). Check positivity and every coefficient, including the constant and absent monomials. A rational positive LDL gives rational weighted squares.

The zero set consists exactly of the sign cube \(y_i=\pm\sqrt{a_i}\); this is deduced in the proof, not provided to the algorithm. A quadratic vanishing on all sign choices has no linear or cross terms, by independence of cube sign characters, and its constant is minus \(\sum_i d_i a_i\). Thus its factor vector lies in \(\operatorname{span}\{y_i^2-a_i\}\). Every PSD Gram's squared factors vanish on the cube and therefore lie in this space. Quartic coefficient comparison uniquely fixes their compressed Gram to \(Q'\). Invertible substitution preserves uniqueness in the complete degree-at-most-two basis.

Consequently the feasible PSD Gram set is a singleton of rank \(n\). Its supporting **factor space** has dimension \(n\). Its minimal face in the PSD cone is isomorphic to \(\mathbb S_+^n\) and has geometric dimension \(n(n+1)/2\), whereas the feasible singleton has affine dimension zero. The source phrase “Gram face has dimension n” needs this distinction. The earlier sign-cube zero count \(2^n\) is correct, and there is no zero enumeration.

## 6. Bit cost, exact replay, priorities and next discriminating step

All input rationals have charged bit length. Metric inversion, the \(N=n(n+1)/2\) rational catalecticant, two matrix products, its columnspace, and normalized joint diagonalization involve polynomial matrix dimensions. Fraction-free elimination bounds their rational heights by determinants/minors of polynomial-size input matrices. The deterministic search has \(O(n^3)\) trials and weights \(t^k\) of \(O(n\log n)\) bits. Characteristic polynomials now have degree \(n\), their rational roots and nullspaces have polynomial height, degree-four variable substitution has only polynomially many monomials, and LDL certificate size and verification are polynomial. Thus a standard exact implementation has polynomial bit complexity in input size. This does not certify SymPy's particular exponent or its performance on arbitrary inputs. No robustness or finite-precision guarantee follows; ill-conditioned transforms and tiny epsilon require exact rational bit costs and can defeat a numerical version.

It receives no hidden fixture matrices and imports none of the author's recovery routines.
