SU(3) WALL BLOCK FOLLOW-UP: CPV HAHN BASIS, COUPLED-SPIN TRANSFORM,
REFINED FIBER PROFILE, AND THE CUBIC-PROXY FAILURE
Date: 2026-10-08

Status. The stable CPV block from `stable_cpv_block_audit.txt` is exact.
CPV's §1.5 identifies its tridiagonal coordinates as an uncoupled Hahn
basis, not the coupled-spin eigenbasis. A finite Clebsch--Gordan change
of coordinates is explicit and its coupled total-spin labels match the
GT spin interval affinely. The source does not identify that algebraic
change with the physical polar map from the multiplicity space to
V_(p,q)[0], so that last basis identification remains open. In the
coupled Hahn basis, the full quartic block is generally not diagonal.
The refined sorted bound has no counterexample in the finite tests below,
but no proof is obtained. The comparison L_S >= M^2/c is decisively too
weak on an exact stable one-dimensional block.

1. EXACT STABLE PARAMETERS AND WHICH INDEX IS WHICH

Let a>=b>=1, N=a+b, and take stable root-lattice labels

  0<=p<=q, q-p=3 delta, p+q<=b,
  Q=(p+2q)/3=p+2 delta, R=(2p+q)/3=p+delta.

The U(2)-GT zero-weight spin list in V_(p,q)[0] is
  j=delta,delta+1,...,R,
and its length is p+1. In CPV's shifted-label convention the six
parameters are

  xi=(N+1-Q, b+1, b+1-delta, b-Q, b-R, 0),
  xi_a=b+1-delta,   xi_b=b-R.

Appendix A, equations (67)--(70), gives the highest-vector basis
omega_m for m=xi_b,...,xi_a-1. Hence m=b-R,...,b-delta and the
index bijection

  j=b-m                                                        (1)

reverses the GT spin list. This is only an index bijection. The vectors
omega_m are highest vectors in the tensor product; the GT vectors are
zero-weight vectors in V_(p,q). Equality of index sets does not identify
these two vector bases.

2. CPV §1.5: OMEGA IS THE UNCOUPLED HAHN BASIS

Use the parameter substitution in CPV Proposition 1.4 with

  eta_1=xi_2=b+1, eta_2=xi_3=b+1-delta,
  eta_5=xi_5=b-R, eta_6=0.

The resulting SU(2) coupling parameters are

  J_1=(eta_1-eta_5-1)/2=R/2,
  J_2=(eta_2-1)/2=(b-delta)/2,
  M_3=(eta_1+eta_5-eta_2)/2=(b-p)/2.

The fixed-M_3 uncoupled basis is indexed by the same interval
eta_b=b-R<=m<eta_a=b+1-delta. Its entries are

  M_1(m)=M_3+J_2-m = j-R/2,
  M_2(m)=m-J_2 = (b+delta)/2-j,   j=b-m,

with M_1+M_2=M_3. The CPV basis omega_m is the corresponding
uncoupled/Hahn basis (up to its source normalization and phase). Thus
j=b-m is the index of an uncoupled magnetic coordinate in this basis;
it is not the coupled total-spin quantum number.

The allowed coupled total spins in this fixed-M_3 sector are

  J_3=M_3,M_3+1,...,J_1+J_2
     =(b-p)/2,...,(b+p)/2.

They can be indexed by the GT integers as

  J_3=gamma+j,  gamma=(b-Q)/2,  j=delta,...,R.              (2)

Indeed gamma+delta=(b-p)/2 and gamma+R=(b+p)/2. Equation (2) is an
exact affine match of spectra/labels in the auxiliary SU(2) model.
It does not, by itself, identify the coupled SU(2) vectors with the
physical U(2)-GT vectors in V_(p,q)[0].

An explicit algebraic intertwiner from the CPV coordinates to the
coupled Hahn coordinates is available. Number the CPV basis positions
r=1,...,p+1, so m=xi_b+r-1. The source H_2 off-diagonal coefficients
after the above substitution are, for r=1,...,p,

  u_r=(H_2)_(r,r+1)=-(R+1-r)(p+1-r),
  v_r=(H_2)_(r+1,r)=-r(r+b-R).

Set D_1=1 and

  D_(r+1)/D_r = sqrt[v_r/u_r]
      = sqrt( r(r+b-R)/((R+1-r)(p+1-r)) ).                 (3)

Then D^{-1}H_2D is symmetric, with off-diagonal
  -sqrt(r(r+b-R)(R+1-r)(p+1-r)).
Let C be the orthogonal SU(2) Clebsch--Gordan matrix whose column j is
the normalized coupled vector |J_1,J_2;J_3=gamma+j,M_3>, expanded in
the uncoupled states indexed by m. Up to the fixed source phase
(-1)^m, the coupled-coordinate change is

  U_Hahn = D C,       H_coupled=C^T D^{-1} H_CPV D C.        (4)

This explicitly gives the Hahn-to-coupled similarity. It is not yet a
proof that U_Hahn is the unitary/polar map for the physical multiplicity
inner product. In particular, the CPV source supplies an algebraic
matrix equivalence; the physical Gram matrix of the omega_m basis has
not been shown here to be D^{-2} or to make (4) unitary.

The CPV §1.5 H_1 diagonal also confirms that its coordinate is uncoupled:
at position r, its eigenvalue is

  j-(b+Q)/4,  with j=R-r+1=b-m.                          (5)

By contrast, the coupled basis diagonalizes H_2, with eigenvalues
  e_j=(gamma+j)(gamma+j+1).

3. FULL QUARTIC BLOCK IN THE COUPLED HAHN COORDINATES

Let A denote H_1 and B denote H_2 after the change (4), so B is diagonal
with B_jj=e_j and H_3=[A,B]. CPV Proposition 1.4 and Appendix B give

  X=z0+z1 A+z2 B+z3[A,B]+z4{A,B},
  Y=z5+z6 A+z7 B+z8[A,B]+z9{A,B}
       +z10 A^2+z11 A^2 B+z12 A B A.

The exact stable substitutions simplify the non-scalar coefficients to

  z1=-(N+delta+2)(N+p+q+4)/4,
  z2=-(a-b+p+q)/4,
  z3=(R+1)/2,                         z4=1/2,
  z6=2 z1 z2/3,
  z7=R(N+Q+4)/4-z2^2/3,
  z8=(3N+2p+5delta+8)z2/6,
  z9=-2z2/3,
  z10=-(N+delta+2)(N+delta+3)/4,
  z11=(N+delta+2)/2,
  z12=-(N+delta+4)/2.                                    (6)

The scalar z0,z5 are exactly those in CPV Appendix B (74b,c), with
the stable xi substitution; they only contribute to the identity below.
Writing c=C2(p,q), alpha=C3(a,b)/C2(a,b), and

  sigma=-c^2/12+(C2(a,b)/3+1/4+alpha^2)c-(4/3)alpha C3(a,b),

the full block is

  Khat=N^2 K^S = tau I
       -(z6+2alpha z1)A -(z7+2alpha z2)B
       -(z8+2alpha z3)[A,B] -(z9+2alpha z4){A,B}
       -z10 A^2-z11 A^2B-z12 ABA,                        (7)

  tau=sigma-z5-2alpha z0.

This is the complete quartic block, not the mixed-cubic proxy. The
physical quadratic form is (1/N^2) x^* G_CPV H_CPV x in the CPV
coordinates, where G_CPV is the positive Gram matrix of omega_m and
H_CPV is the exact matrix from `stable_cpv_block_audit.txt` §3. Under
the algebraic change U_Hahn its coordinate form is

  (1/N^2) z^* G_coupled H_coupled z,
  G_coupled=U_Hahn^*G_CPV U_Hahn,
  H_coupled=U_Hahn^{-1}H_CPV U_Hahn.                       (8)

The unknown in (8) is not H_CPV (known exactly) but the physical Gram
and whether U_Hahn equals the canonical GT-polar map. Replacing G_coupled
by identity without proving that identification would be an invalid
unitary-basis argument.

4. A SYMBOLIC NON-DIAGONALITY TEST IN THE COUPLED HAHN BASIS

Because A is tridiagonal and B is diagonal, the only contributions to
the second off-diagonal of (7) are A^2,A^2B,ABA. Put

  x=gamma+j+2,  A0=N+delta+2.

For every allowed j,j+2,

  (H_coupled)_(j,j+2)
    =-A_(j,j+1) A_(j+1,j+2) C_j,
  C_j=z10+z11 e_(j+2)+z12 e_(j+1)
     =-A0(A0+1)/4+(A0+1)x-x^2.                            (9)

Here A_(j,j+1) is the off-diagonal matrix element of
H_1=(s_z^(1)-s_z^(2))/2 in the normalized coupled SU(2) basis. It is
nonzero whenever both adjacent total spins are allowed: explicitly its
absolute value is

  sqrt( ((J1+J2+1)^2-(J+1)^2)
        ((J+1)^2-(J1-J2)^2)
        ((J+1)^2-M3^2) )
       / (2(J+1)sqrt((2J+1)(2J+3))).                       (10)

As a strict stable example, take a=b=n>=4, (p,q)=(2,2), delta=0.
Then j=0,1,2, gamma=(n-2)/2, A0=2n+2, and (9) gives

  C_0=1/2-n^2/4 != 0.

Both factors in (10) for 0->1 and 1->2 are positive. Consequently
(H_coupled)_(0,2) !=0. Thus the full block is not diagonal in the
coupled Hahn spin basis. This is an exact symbolic calculation. If the
physical U(2)-GT basis is identified with this coupled basis, it also
proves non-diagonality there. The source plus the label match (2) alone
does not establish that final identification; the canonical map/Gram
comparison required to do so is the exact unresolved basis lemma.

5. REFINED SORTED PROFILE: DIAGNOSTICS AND ANCHORS

The refined candidate is for sorted eigenvalues
  kappa_0<=...<=kappa_p of K^S_(p,q):

  kappa_s >= c_* [j+eta j^2],
  j=delta+s, eta=(a-b)/N,                               (11)

except that j=0, delta=0 may be omitted. The earlier positive-floor
claim c_*(j+1)^2 is false by the adjoint family in
`stable_cpv_block_audit.txt` §4. Formula (11) is consistent with that
soft mode and the one-dimensional test below.

Finite CPV-eigenvalue diagnostics found no failure of (11) in the
stable blocks scanned. The smallest ratios kappa_s/(j+eta*j^2) were
at the non-exempt adjoint mode (p,q)=(1,1), j=1: 0.76501875 for
(a,b)=(100,100), 0.80874962 for (200,100), 0.88993209 for (300,100),
0.98800591 for (1000,100), and 0.80217242 for (1000,500). These are
floating-point finite checks only, not evidence for a uniform theorem.
The exact a=b=n adjoint block has eigenvalues

  (2n+1)(2n+3)/(16n^2),
  3(4n^2+8n+1)/(16n^2),

so its j=1 eigenvalue tends to 3/4; any universal c_* in (11) must be
at most 3/4.

For low nonstable labels, CPV's general endpoints are
  xi_b=max(b-Q,b-R,0)=max(0,b-R),
  xi_a=min(N+1-Q,b+1,b+1-delta).
When Q<=a and delta<=b, xi_a=b+1-delta and the interval maps by
j=b-m to the truncated GT list delta,...,min(R,b). Thus the same
CPV tridiagonal formula tests blocks beyond p+q<=b. Low-window scans
with output Casimir c2(p,q)<=0.4N included (a,b)=(50,10),(100,10),
(100,20),(200,10),(100,50),(100,100),(300,100),(1000,10). The minimum
ratios ranged from 0.76501875 to 1.01092963, again at (1,1), j=1;
no counterexample was found. This does not prove (11), and the
physical GT-polar identification is not supplied by dimension counts.

The weakest still-missing spectral statement is a uniform min--max
bound for the full quartic matrix (7), in the physical Gram, namely

  lambda_(s+1)(K^S_(p,q)) >= c_*[(delta+s)+eta(delta+s)^2]

for every low block, except s=0,delta=0, with the truncated endpoint
outside the stable range. CPV tridiagonality in omega coordinates does
not prove this estimate: the full operator includes the quartic Y term,
the basis is nonorthogonal, and no discrete Sturm comparison or
condition-number-free form domination has been established.

6. THE CUBIC COMPARISON K^S >= M^2/c DOES NOT CLOSE (11)

The exact blockwise inequality is

  K^S_(p,q) >= M_(p,q)^2/c,
  M=(X_CPV+2C3(a,b)/3-alpha*c)/N,

where c=C2(p,q). Thus the proxy eigenvalues are
  (x_r+2C3/3-alpha*c)^2/(N^2 c),
with x_r the real eigenvalues of X_CPV, reordered by absolute value
after squaring. Eigenvalue monotonicity says these proxy eigenvalues
lower-bound the correspondingly sorted eigenvalues of K^S, but need not
be positive on every nonzero GT spin.

Exact failure already occurs in dimension one. Let a=b=n>=3 and take
the stable label (p,q)=(0,3), delta=1, whose only GT spin is j=1.
Here c=6, C3=alpha=0. Substituting xi=(2n-1,n+1,n,n-2,n-1,0)
into CPV's diagonal X formula gives X_CPV=0, hence M=0 and

  M^2/c=0.

But the full quartic block gives
  Y_CPV=-2n(n+2),
  sigma=(4n^2+8n-3)/2,
  kappa(K^S_(0,3))=(-Y+sigma)/N^2
       =(8n^2+16n-3)/(8n^2)
       =1+2/n-3/(8n^2)>1.                                (12)

The target in (11) is j+eta*j^2=1. The true block has the required
positive scale, while M^2/c is identically zero. Therefore the
known cubic comparison cannot prove any positive c_* for (11), even
on stable labels and even after excluding only the j=0 channel.

For a wall sequence in the same p=0,delta=1 block, the exact cubic
eigenvalue is

  M=-(a-b)(a+2b+3)(2a+b+3)
       /((a+b)(a^2+ab+3a+b^2+3b)).                          (13)

If b=o(a), (13) tends to -2, so M^2/c tends to 2/3, while the exact
quartic eigenvalue tends to 2 and the right side of (11) tends to 2.
The proxy loses a factor three even away from the symmetric case, and
at a=b it loses the entire positive scalar. Supplementary quartic
channels are essential for this profile.

More generally, the exact one-dimensional p=0 family (p,q)=(0,3r)
has (from the full quartic map, not from the cubic proxy)

  kappa_r = r(r+1)/N^2
       [2 C2(a,b)+5/4+3 alpha^2-r(r+1)],
  1<=r<=min(b,floor(a/3)).                                (14)

This is an exact stable singleton formula and in particular for r=1
it gives (12) at a=b. Along the growing-wall sequence
b=floor(sqrt(a)), a->infinity, (0,3) is stable, eta->1,
kappa_1->2, while M^2/c->2/3. Thus even when both the wall parameter b
and the carrier grow, the cubic comparison supplies only one third of
the required asymptotic singleton scale. Along the symmetric sequence
a=b=n it supplies zero although kappa_1->1. Formula (14) shows that a
positive exact one-dimensional estimate is available from the quartic
operator; it cannot be recovered from M^2/c alone.

7. SOURCE AND SCOPE

Primary source: N. Crampé, L. Poulain d'Andecy, L. Vinet,
“The missing label of su_3 and its symmetry,” arXiv:2110.03521v1,
submitted 2021-10-07, https://arxiv.org/html/2110.03521v1.
The relevant exact statements are §1.3 eq. (18) (simultaneous
tridiagonal X,Y matrices), §1.5 Proposition 1.4 (Hahn/Heun expressions
in H_1,H_2), and Appendix A eqs. (67)--(73) (omega_m highest-vector
basis). Appendix B eqs. (74) gives the z coefficients. The carrier
normalization and exact quartic identity used here are from
`outputs/research/sol_semiclassical_memory/wall_su3/MISSING_LABEL_MAPPING.txt`
§4. No novelty claim is made.

Conclusion: the omega_m index is not the coupled GT spin basis; a
Clebsch--Gordan transform is needed. This transform gives an exact
coupled Hahn matrix and an exact non-diagonality certificate. The
remaining GT step is to identify that transform (including its Gram)
with the physical multiplicity-to-zero-weight polar map. Separately,
the refined sorted profile (11) remains plausible but unproved, and
the mixed-cubic square cannot establish it.
