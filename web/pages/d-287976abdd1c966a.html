# Spin-interface follow-up: precision and resource costs

Worker `c07_l10`; 2026-10-07. This derivation uses my frozen target interface
and moment assumptions recorded in my own work. `INITIAL.txt` remains
unchanged. Status is conditional on the inherited uniform critical-moment and
two-way-channel bounds; no device or sampler runtime is asserted.

## Exact interface

One sample is one independently prepared `N`-qubit Gibbs block

\[
J=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma^{(i)},\qquad
K_N(A,b)=\frac{2J^2}{N}+
\frac{Q_A(J)}{N^{3/2}}+\frac{b\cdot J}{N^{3/4}},\qquad
\rho_{N,A,b}=\frac{e^{K_N(A,b)}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{K_N(A,b)}}.
\]

The family interface supplies fixed `M,B` and ranges over symmetric `A`,
\(\|A\|_{op}\le M\), and \(b\in\mathbb R^3\), \(|b|\le B\). A finite
number of blocks `R` share one unknown parameter. The field parameter in the
exponent is the scaled `b`; the physical coefficient of `J` is
\(h=b/N^{3/4}\). Fixing nonzero physical (h) makes
\(|b|=N^{3/4}|h|\), outside the fixed-(B) theorem window as (N\to\infty\).

For the sensitivity result below, use the supplied uniform critical-sector
moment bounds
\[
\sup_{A,b}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{N,A,b}J^2)\le C_2N^{3/2},\qquad
\sup_{A,b}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{N,A,b}(J^2)^2)\le C_4N^3.
\]
For the experiment comparison, assume the parameter-independent two-way
channels to and from one quartic observation have error
\(\delta_N=C_{M,B}N^{-1/2}\). Those are inherited conditional inputs, not
re-proved here.

## Lemma 1: the Gibbs target is uniformly Lipschitz in scaled parameters

Let \(\theta=(A,b)\), and let a segment \(\theta+t u\), \(0\le t\le1\),
stay inside the admitted compact family. Then
\[
D(\rho_{N,\theta}\|\rho_{N,\theta+u})\le
\frac{C}{2}\bigl(\|u_A\|_{op}^2+|u_b|^2\bigr),\qquad
T(\rho_{N,\theta},\rho_{N,\theta+u})\le
\frac{\sqrt C}{2}\bigl(\|u_A\|_{op}^2+|u_b|^2\bigr)^{1/2},
\]
with `C=C(M,B,C_2,C_4)` independent of `N`. Here
\(T(\rho,\sigma)=\|\rho-\sigma\|_1/2\).

Proof. Write \(\psi_N(\theta)=\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{K_N(\theta)}\).
The exponent increment is
\[
G_u=Q_{u_A}(J)/N^{3/2}+u_b\cdot J/N^{3/4}.
\]
The log-partition Hessian in direction `G_u` is its centered BKM variance.
In an eigenbasis of \(\rho\), the BKM logarithmic-mean coefficient is at
most the arithmetic mean, so
\(\psi_N''[G_u,G_u]\le\operatorname{Tr}(\rho G_u^2)\). In each total-spin
sector, \(|Q_{u_A}(J)|\le\|u_A\|_{op}J^2\) and
\(|u_b\cdot J|\le|u_b|J\). Since these operators commute with (J^2),
\((X+Y)^2\le2X^2+2Y^2\) and the stated moment bounds give
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho G_u^2)\le
2C_4\|u_A\|_{op}^2+2C_2|u_b|^2.
\]
The relative entropy is the Bregman divergence of \(\psi_N\), hence the
integral of this Hessian with weight (1-t), giving the factor (1/2).
Quantum Pinsker gives the trace-distance bound. This proves continuity of the
*target Gibbs state*; it does not require a continuity estimate for any
particular separable decomposition.

## Corollary: parameter rounding for state accuracy

Suppose a mathematical known-rational-parameter routine outputs
\(\sigma_N(\tilde\theta)\) with
\(T(\sigma_N(\tilde\theta),\rho_{N,\tilde\theta})\le C_0e^{-cN}\),
uniformly on the admitted set. Then
\[
T(\sigma_N(\tilde\theta),\rho_{N,\theta})
\le C_0e^{-cN}+L\|\tilde\theta-\theta\|.
\]
This is just the triangle inequality plus Lemma 1. For fixed parameter
dimension (six independent entries of symmetric `A`, three of `b`), a
rational grid with `q` fractional bits per coordinate uses `O(q)` input
bits and achieves rounding error `O(2^{-q})`. Therefore:

- For target trace error \(\varepsilon\gg e^{-cN}\), `q=O(log(1/epsilon))`
  bits per coordinate suffice at the theorem level.
- To preserve an \(e^{-aN}\) target for fixed `a<c`, `q=O(N)` bits
  suffice; this is linear input length, not exponential input length.
- The claimed diffusion theorem gives no guarantee below its own
  `C_0e^{-cN}` error floor. Parameter rounding also does not supply a
  finite-bit SDE solver or bound its discretization, weight, stopping, or
  normalization errors.

If θ is unknown rather than supplied, these bits must be acquired from data;
the lower bound below then applies. Supplying `q` bits and learning `q` bits
are different interfaces.

## Lemma 2: finite-bit outcomes preserve the quartic experiment

The quartic density is
\[
p_\theta(x)=Z_\theta^{-1}\exp(-4|x|^4/3+x^TAx+b\cdot x).
\]
Uniformly on the compact family,
\[
\int|\nabla p_\theta(x)|dx\le L_1<\infty,
\qquad P_\theta(|X|>r)\le C_1e^{-c_1r^4}
\]
for sufficiently large `r`. The first bound follows by differentiating the
log density, whose gradient is
\(-16|x|^2x/3+2Ax+b\), and using the quartic tail. For the second,
\(-4r^4/3+Mr^2+Br\le-c_1r^4\) at large `r`, while \(Z_\theta\) has a
uniform positive lower bound from integration over the unit ball.

Take a cubic grid of side η inside ([-r,r]^3), and use one extra symbol
for its complement. Quantize (X) to its cell index. The reverse kernel
outputs a uniform point in an interior cell, and maps the outside symbol to
the fixed point 0. The reconstructed density is the cell-average of
\(p_\theta\) inside the cube plus an atom at 0. The (W^{1,1}) Poincaré
inequality on cubes bounds the interior (L^1) error by (C\eta L_1); the
outside cell contributes at most its probability to total variation. Hence
the continuous quartic experiment and this finite-alphabet experiment have
Le Cam distance
\[
\varepsilon_{\rm bin}\le C_2'\eta+C_1e^{-c_1r^4}.
\]
Choosing \(\eta=\Theta(\varepsilon)\) and
\(r=\Theta((\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/4})\) gives error at most
\(\varepsilon\) with
\[
\log_2 K=3\log_2(1/\varepsilon)+
\tfrac34\log_2\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(1)
\]
bits per block, where (K=O((r/\eta)^3)) is the alphabet size. The
quantizer/reconstruction are parameter independent.

Compose this finite-alphabet comparison with the inherited spin/quartic
channels. For (R) blocks the Le Cam distance between the spin experiment and
the (R)-fold finite-bit experiment is at most
\[
R(\delta_N+\varepsilon_{\rm bin}).
\]
Thus the (R=o(\sqrt N)) bounded-risk equivalence can use only
\(O(\log N)\) classical output bits per block: take
\(\varepsilon_{\rm bin}=N^{-1/2}\). Total recorded readout is
\(O(R\log N)\) bits. To make additive comparison error negligible relative
to an (O(1/R)) mean-square risk, the inherited \(\delta_N\) requires
\(R=o(N^{1/4})\); choosing \(\varepsilon_{\rm bin}=o(R^{-2})\) costs
\(O(\log R)\) bits per block.

This is a finite *outcome alphabet* bound, not a circuit or analog-detector
cost theorem. The ideal forward map still requires a collective spin/coherent
measurement, whose circuit depth, latency, noise, and energy are unprovided.
The reverse randomization uses ideal continuous uniform draws in its
information-theoretic form; a finite-bit randomizer and its error must be
budgeted separately before claiming a finite-bit implementation.

## Acquisition lower bound and sample units

The same BKM calculation gives
\(D(\rho_{N,\theta}\|\rho_{N,\theta+u})\le C\|u\|^2/2\). For (R)
independent blocks, relative entropy adds; Pinsker bounds trace distance by
\(\sqrt{CR}\|u\|/2\). Choose two interior parameters separated by
\(\|u\|=1/\sqrt{CR}\), so their (R)-block trace distance is at most
(1/2). Any estimator induces a test; the two-point Le Cam bound gives
worst-case squared error at least \(c/R\) for that coordinate, for every
collective measurement.

Consequently, estimating scaled (b) to mean-square accuracy
\(\epsilon_b^2\) needs \(R=\Omega(\epsilon_b^{-2})\) independent
preparations. For the physical coefficient (h=b/N^{3/4}), the lower
mean-square scale is \(c/(N^{3/2}R)\), or
\(R=\Omega(1/(N^{3/2}\epsilon_h^2))\). If the target is a fixed relative
error on a critical-window field (h\asymp N^{-3/4}\), this again costs
\(\Omega(1/\epsilon_{rel}^2)\) blocks, or
\(\Omega(N/\epsilon_{rel}^2)\) qubit exposures. Learning (b) to
\(e^{-aN}\) precision takes \(\Omega(e^{2aN})\) blocks and
\(\Omega(Ne^{2aN})\) qubit exposures. This is acquisition cost, distinct
from the (O(N))-bit representation sufficient to *supply* such a value.

At (A=b=0), the known-(A) limiting classical field Fisher information is
\(0.213523984564 I_3\); its asymptotically efficient one-coordinate
standard-error coefficient is (2.164096722984/\sqrt R\) for scaled (b),
or (2.164096722984/(N^{3/4}\sqrt R)) for (h). These are limiting-model
benchmarks, not exact finite-(N) QFI statements.

## Energy, time, and finite-bit ledger

For one block, the dimensionless exponent obeys
\[
\|K_N(A,b)\|\le N/2+1+M\sqrt N/4+M/(2\sqrt N)+B N^{1/4}/2=O(N).
\]
This uses \(\|J^2\|=(N/2)(N/2+1)\),
\(\|Q_A\|\le M J^2\), and \(\|b\cdot J\|\le B|J|\). The supplied
critical moment bound further gives
\(|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho K_N)|=O(\sqrt N)+O_{M,B}(1)\) in these
dimensionless units. If the physical Hamiltonian is \(H=-K_N/\beta\),
multiply these energy scales by (1/\beta). No inverse temperature or
physical energy conversion is fixed by the statistical theorem. These are
state-energy scales, not the work or energy required to cool, control, or
read out a device.

| Resource | What follows from this interface | What remains unprovided |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied parameters | 9 fixed-dimensional coordinates; (O(q)) input bits for (q)-bit rational precision. Target-state rounding error is (O(2^{-q})), uniformly in (N). | Precision/rounding of computed SDE coefficients and certified arithmetic. |
| Acquired parameters | (\Omega(1/\epsilon_b^2)) blocks for scaled-field MSE (\epsilon_b^2); each block contains (N) spins. | Wall-clock preparation time and detector cadence. |
| Finite classical result | (O(\log(1/\varepsilon))) bits/block approximate the quartic experiment to TV/Le Cam error (\varepsilon); for (R=o(\sqrt N)), (O(\log N)) bits/block suffice. | Cost/noise of realizing the collective POVM; finite-randomness reverse kernel. |
| Quantum output | Any product-state decoder emits (N) one-qubit factors/block, so (RN) output factors for (R) blocks. | Gate count, depth, preparation success, latency, hardware energy. |
| Statistical experiment | Channel comparison error (R C N^{-1/2}); all bounded losses transfer if (R=o(\sqrt N)). | No derivative-level QFI guarantee and no fixed-(h) extrapolation. |
| Thermal energy | \(\|K_N\|=O(N)\); typical exponent energy magnitude (O(\sqrt N)\) under the moment bound. | Physical units, cooling work, total energy use over samples. |

No run-time claim follows from a trace-distance decomposition or a finite
outcome alphabet. For any actual experiment with per-block wall time \(t_N\)
and energy \(e_N\), the resource totals are (R t_N) and (R e_N); the
current theorems supply neither (t_N) nor (e_N).
