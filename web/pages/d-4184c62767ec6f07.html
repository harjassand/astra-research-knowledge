# Independent identifiability attack: paired-daughter damage ratio

**Disposition: conditional algebraic lemma is correct; the proposed 53BP1 mechanism assay is not identified without a calibrated observation model and explicit censoring/cell-cycle handling.** This is a falsification of the assay inference as currently stated, not of the idealized algebra.

## 1. What the ideal model actually identifies

Suppose the *measured* positive signal is exactly

\[
Y_1(t)=g(t)D_0p e^{-k_1t},\qquad
Y_2(t)=g(t)D_0(1-p)e^{-k_2t},
\]

with the same nonzero multiplicative gain across sisters at each time (it may vary over time), no additive background, known times relative to cytokinesis, fixed daughter labels, and a valid exponential law from division onward. Then

\[
r(t)=\log(Y_1/Y_2)=\operatorname{logit}(p)+(k_2-k_1)t.
\]

For two distinct, exactly known times \(t_1,t_2\),

\[
\widehat{\Delta k}=\frac{r(t_2)-r(t_1)}{t_2-t_1},\qquad
\widehat{\operatorname{logit}(p)}=\frac{t_2r(t_1)-t_1r(t_2)}{t_2-t_1}.
\]

Thus the shared gain and \(D_0\) cancel. Only the partition logit and the *difference* \(k_2-k_1\) are identified; neither repair constant separately is identified by the ratio. Swapping daughter labels changes the signs of both contrasts, so a biological direction requires a prespecified daughter identity or a fate/polarity label.

This is a two-parameter line fitted to two ratios. It has no per-pair residual degrees of freedom: any two positive observed signals produce an exact line, so the two-point fit cannot test exponential decay, constant partition, or the assumed signal map.

## 2. Exact counterexamples to the observation claim

### Sister-specific reporter gain confounds partition

With constant but unequal gains,

\[
\log(Y_1/Y_2)=\operatorname{logit}(p)+\log(g_1/g_2)+\Delta k\,t.
\]

The intercept no longer identifies \(p\). For example, if the true \(p=0.75\), \(g_1=1\), and \(g_2=3\), then the observed intercept is \(\log 3+\log(1/3)=0\), which reports \(p_{\rm app}=0.5\). A time-varying sister gain ratio \(g_1(t)/g_2(t)=c e^{\eta t}\) also changes the apparent slope to \(\Delta k+\eta\). A common gain shared by both sisters at each time does cancel; a gain merely shared in expectation does not.

### Common additive background biases both intercept and slope

Even the same background in both channels does not cancel after taking a ratio. Let \(p=0.75\), \(D_0=100\), \(k_1=0.2\,h^{-1}\), \(k_2=0.8\,h^{-1}\), \(g=1\), and common background \(b=5\):

| Time | \(Y_1=gD_1+b\) | \(Y_2=gD_2+b\) | \(\log(Y_1/Y_2)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 h | 80.000 | 30.000 | 0.98083 |
| 1 h | 66.405 | 16.233 | 1.40871 |

The two-point apparent slope is 0.42788 h⁻¹, versus the true \(k_2-k_1=0.6\) h⁻¹. The inferred partition is 0.7273 rather than 0.75. Background subtraction could repair this only if the background is measured on the same scale with adequate precision and the residual signal is positive; log ratios of foci counts also fail when either count is zero, and arbitrary pseudocounts change the estimand.

### Unknown delay confounds the division-time intercept

If recorded time \(s\) starts at the first observation, but the first observation occurs an unknown \(\delta\) after division, then

\[
r(s)=\operatorname{logit}(p)+\Delta k(s+\delta).
\]

The slope remains identifiable, but the observed intercept is \(\operatorname{logit}(p)+\Delta k\delta\); different \((p,\delta)\) pairs give the same data. A delayed first frame alone is **not** a structural failure if its timestamp relative to cytokinesis is known and the exponential model holds throughout the unobserved interval. It does make partition an extrapolation and can make it unstable when the time origin is uncertain or the two times are close.

### Informative death/censoring changes the observed pair population

Complete-pair analysis estimates a survivor-conditioned population if a damaged daughter dies, detaches, exits the field, or becomes untrackable before the second frame. With heterogeneous pairs, this is missing-not-at-random. For example, equal numbers of pair types with labelled \(p=0.9\) and \(p=0.1\) have population mean partition \(E[p]=0.5\); if the probability of retaining a complete pair is 1 for the first type and 0 for the second, the observed mean is 0.9. The direction of selection bias depends on the retention rule. If parameters are truly homogeneous and pair inclusion is independent, censoring reduces precision rather than changing the per-pair identity; that special case does not cover damage-linked death.

## 3. Why a 53BP1 focus trace is not the assumed \(D(t)\)

The equation is about a conserved scalar damage burden split at division and then independently removed with constant first-order rates. A 53BP1 focus count or intensity is a DDR recruitment readout. The cited primary studies establish several mismatches relevant to daughter-cell measurements:

- **A focus is not a one-to-one lesion counter.** Neumaier et al. used live imaging and kinetic modelling to show that nearby DSBs cluster into repair centres; focus yield was dose-dependent and nonlinear (about 15 53BP1 radiation-induced foci/Gy at 2 Gy versus about 64/Gy at 0.1 Gy), and induction and disappearance kinetics varied with dose. Thus a focus signal need not be proportional to lesion burden.
- **The signal can be cell-cycle gated and delayed across mitosis.** Harrigan et al. and Lukas et al. showed that unresolved replication problems can be transmitted through mitosis and appear as large 53BP1 nuclear bodies in daughter-cell G1. In live imaging these bodies arise after mitotic exit and dissipate with S-phase entry. Panagopoulos et al. tracked 53BP1 with PCNA and report that inherited bodies are primarily G1-associated and clear on S-phase entry; perturbing replication changes their appearance and creates sister-cell heterogeneity. That is not a time-homogeneous exponential decay of a fixed post-division lesion pool; replication can change the signal or generate new marked structures.
- **Age can change reporter recruitment per lesion.** Anglada et al. report deficient 53BP1 recruitment to DSB sites in G1 cells from aged human mammary epithelial cells. Because age is central to this project, age-invariant mapping from damage to 53BP1 signal must be measured, not presumed. A common gain cancellation inside one sister pair does not correct a nonlinear or age-dependent recruitment function.
- **Death is part of the proposed biology.** Wang et al. report that in the two-cell-like ESC state, accumulated damage is enriched in a daughter lineage destined for elimination while the sister lineage returns to pluripotency. Tracking only pairs that remain intact at both post-division measurements conditions on a downstream outcome related to damage.

Consequently, the unqualified estimands from the images are the division-time-extrapolated **log signal ratio** and differential **log-signal decay**, conditional on the included lineages. Calling them damage partition and differential DNA-repair rate needs a separately validated map from lesions to the chosen 53BP1 statistic, plus an explicit process model for replication, cell cycle, and attrition. Integrated fluorescence, foci count, focus area, and number of lesions are different observables and should not be interchanged.

## 4. Identifiability gaps and fair controls

1. **Define the target.** Specify what “damage” means (for example, DSB number, a named inherited lesion class, 53BP1 focus count, or integrated reporter signal), the daughter-label convention, and whether \(p\) is per-pair or a population mean. True damage conservation \(D_1(0)+D_2(0)=D_0\) and the absence of new/converted lesions are substantive assumptions.
2. **Calibrate the signal map.** Estimate sister-specific offsets and gains, segmentation/threshold error, and age/cell-cycle dependence. Use background controls and an orthogonal lesion endpoint at matched times. A matched comet or direct DSB assay is preferable to treating another DDR recruitment marker as unquestioned ground truth.
3. **Resolve cell-cycle dynamics.** Record division time and PCNA/EdU or equivalent cell-cycle state; either restrict the exponential window to a justified pre-S interval or fit a state-dependent observation/repair model. The same annotated window must be applied to the proposed model and controls.
4. **Retain the observation process.** Track every division, including single-sister loss, apoptosis, field exit, and tracking failure. Report complete-pair and all-lineage estimates separately, and model death/observation jointly or use a justified censoring adjustment. This matters directly when the damaged lineage is expected to die.
5. **Use equally informed baselines.** Compare with (a) a symmetric-partition null (\(p=1/2\)) and equal-rate null (\(\Delta k=0\)) using the same images, timestamps, cell-cycle covariates, and censoring records; and (b) a raw-signal paired model with measured background/gain terms, rather than comparing against a weaker unpaired or endpoint-only analysis. Label permutation is a useful symmetry control. Evaluate the mechanistic model on at least three time points or on an external held-out/orthogonal measurement; two points alone cannot reject a curved or state-dependent trajectory.
6. **Separate technical feasibility from biological inference.** If the two time points come from fixed-cell staining cohorts, they are not repeated observations of the same daughter pair. A per-pair slope requires live longitudinal measurement; cross-sectional cohorts need a hierarchical model and do not provide the stated per-pair identification automatically.

## 5. Prior-art check (primary research only)

A bounded primary-literature search found substantial adjacent prior art: live multigenerational 53BP1 tracking, replication-linked inheritance and sister heterogeneity, and asymmetric damage/fate segregation in ESC daughters. These papers make live paired-lineage measurement plausible and the biological question timely. I did **not** identify the exact two-time log-ratio decomposition used as a calibrated estimator of initial damage partition separately from differential repair. This bounded search is not a novelty or priority proof.

**Decision for the parent:** retain the ratio result only as a conditional algebraic lemma. For a realistic 53BP1 assay, the present claim has an observation-model gap and a selection/cell-cycle gap. The minimum repair is a calibrated, background-aware measurement model, known cytokinesis times and sister identity, cell-cycle-resolved windows, explicit fate/censoring capture, and a third time point or orthogonal validation.
