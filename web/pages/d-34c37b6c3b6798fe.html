# Independent primality and sampling error reconstruction

## A direct quarter-liar proof

For an odd composite n, write n-1=2^s d with d odd. The strong test accepts base a if a^d=1 modulo n or a^(2^j d)=-1 modulo n for some 0<=j<s. All accepted bases are units. Let n=product_i p_i^e_i, with k distinct odd primes, and write p_i-1=2^v_i u_i with u_i odd. Set g_i=gcd(d,u_i), a=min(s,v_1,...,v_k) and G=product_i g_i.

The unit group of an odd prime-power residue ring is cyclic. Since p_i does not divide n-1, the odd p_i^(e_i-1) factor in its order cannot divide d. Thus a^d=1 has exactly g_i local solutions. For j<v_i, a^(2^j d)=-1 has exactly 2^j g_i solutions; for j>=v_i it has none. The global acceptance alternatives are disjoint, and CRT gives the exact liar count

    L(n)=G [1+sum_(j=0)^(a-1) 2^(jk)].

This formula is a mathematical reconstruction; brute-force base counts in the checker are only a finite cross-check.

If k=1, compositeness means e>=2. The formula gives L<=p-1, hence 4L<=p^e-1=n-1, including the limiting p=3,e=2 case.

For k>=3, G<=product_i (odd part of phi(p_i^e_i)) and sum_i v_i>=ka. Therefore

    L/phi(n) <= [1+sum_(j=0)^(a-1) 2^(jk)]/2^(ka) <= 2^(1-k) <= 1/4.

The middle expression decreases with a for k>=2; its a=1 value is 2^(1-k). Since phi(n)<=n-1, the required bound follows.

For k=2 with unequal v_i, sum_i v_i>=2a+1. Then

    L/phi(n) <= [1+(4^a-1)/3]/2^(2a+1)
               = [1+2/4^a]/6 <= 1/4.

For k=2 with equal v_i=v, first consider a repeated prime factor. The formula gives L<=(p-1)(q-1)/2. Since n>=p^2 q or pq^2, 4L<=2(p-1)(q-1)<=n-1.

It remains to consider squarefree n=pq with equal v. The two odd parts u_p,u_q cannot both divide d: divisibility would imply u_p divides q-1=2^v u_q and u_q divides p-1=2^v u_p; their oddness forces u_p=u_q, hence p=q. At least one gcd g_i is a proper divisor of its odd u_i and loses a factor at least three. Thus G<=u_p u_q/3. Combining this with a<=v gives L/phi(n)<=1/6<1/4.

These cases prove L(n)<=(n-1)/4 for every odd composite n. Bases 1 and n-1 always pass. Sampling uniformly from {2,...,n-2} therefore has acceptance probability at most (L-2)/(n-3)<=1/4. Independent rounds yield at most 4^(-b) false acceptance for b rounds. Primes pass every valid base, by cyclic finite-field units and the fact that a nontrivial square root of one cannot occur in a field.

## Three separately charged sampler events

Let B be the cycle16 upper bound on exceptional primes, including the packing input-rank failures if applicable. For rational total error delta in (0,1), use

    q=ceil(3/delta),
    L=4[bit_length(B)+bit_length(q)+20],
    T=2L*bit_length(q),
    b=min{integer j : 4^j >= Tq}.

Draw at most T independent uniform odd L-bit candidates, including candidates rejected by deterministic small-factor screening in this count. Test each surviving candidate using b independent uniform bases in {2,...,n-2}, stopping at the first acceptance. If none accepts, return UNKNOWN. Test input rank afterward where needed, returning UNKNOWN on rank loss.

Couple this procedure to the exact bounded-prime sampler on the **same candidate stream**. Imagine the unused base randomness also exists for all T candidates. The probability that any composite passes is at most T*4^(-b)<=1/q. On the complementary event, primes are never rejected and no composite is accepted, so the two procedures choose the same candidate or both time out. For the ideal exact sampler, the cycle16 prime-density argument charges timeout at most 1/q, and its accepted genuine prime is uniform among the L-bit primes, with exceptional-prime probability at most 1/q. A union bound gives

    Pr[returned modulus is prime and preserves the full polytope] >= 1-3/q >= 1-delta.

There is no claim that the MR-accepted distribution itself is exactly uniform on primes, nor that the recorded modulus has a deterministic primality certificate. Conditioning on the absence of false positives can bias distributions; the coupling avoids that mistake. Inverse failure over a composite ring must be caught as UNKNOWN, but catching it is not a primality certificate and does not remove the compositeness allocation.

An odd candidate is uniform using exactly L-2 unbiased bits. A uniform base can be generated with rejection from k=bit_length(n-3) unbiased bits, accepting values below n-3 and adding two. Its acceptance probability is greater than one half, so expected random-bit draws per base are below two. This rejection has no deterministic time bound. The outer candidate count and MR rounds are capped; complete entropy acquisition/bit time is **expected** polynomial unless an additional base-draw cutoff and timeout charge are specified. Modulo reduction of random bits is a biased base sampler and cannot silently inherit the quarter-bound as a uniform distribution.

This proof assumes independent unbiased random bits as a mathematical model. Logging OS entropy calls/bases makes the execution replayable but does not empirically prove that hardware/software entropy follows that ideal law. The intended gate is an algorithmic failure bound, not posterior primality certainty from a recorded transcript.

## Source iteration error is another charge

[Hirai2608.13972v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13972v1#S2), Theorem 2.4, controls the minimum full-sweep objective for N completed sweeps. With source D=c_max sqrt(r), Lipschitz bound sqrt(r), and m data subspaces, its bound is

    min_(i=1)^N f(x^i)-tau <= 4(1+log3)(m+1)c_max*r/sqrt(N+2).

The rational source step is c_max/[(m+1)ceil(sqrt(i+1))], starting i=0. An implementation must retain the minimum completed-sweep value or provide an additional bound for its selected output. The asymptotic O(...) convergence statement does not certify an arbitrarily smaller target epsilon for a short run. An independently acquired feasible primal lower bound can instead certify an actual observed gap, but it is a separate output/cost and must not be supplied by a hidden oracle.

For outward arithmetic one may use 4(1+log3)<42/5: the first six terms of exp(11/10) already exceed three. Thus (42/5)(m+1)c_max*r/floor(sqrt(N+2)) is a conservative rational error bound. Arithmetic weights stay rational scalars over both geometric fields. Primal weights, basis coordinates and scalar objective are distinct interfaces.
