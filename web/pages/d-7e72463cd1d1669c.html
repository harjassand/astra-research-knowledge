# Acquired graph matrix spaces: exact boundary and competent comparator

Status: proved conditional acquisition module, with independently implemented exact checks and a rational non-identifiability witness. This is not a claim of novel graph stabilizer theory, a generic polynomial-equivalence algorithm, or an advantage over a competent retaining comparator. Date: 2026-10-06. Worker r06. The recent programme motivated the question; the load-bearing mechanism has older prerequisites.

## Question and legal input

Given only a rational positive-definite matrix M and a rational basis of a symmetric matrix space U, promise that

    U = L^T U_G L,       M = L^T D L,
    U_G = span{E_ij + E_ji : {i,j} is an edge of G},

where G is a simple graph, L is an unknown rational invertible matrix, and D is an unknown positive rational diagonal matrix. Acquire the hidden coordinate axes, or the maximal identifiable coordinate blocks, in polynomial bit complexity. No graph, L, polynomial zero, simple-spectrum generic covariant, or diagonal matrix space is supplied. G need not be triangle-free or have bounded degree. The compatible, co-diagonal metric promise is essential.

The acquired axes are vector lines L^{-1}e_i. Dual coordinate forms are obtained by inverting a matrix with one column on each acquired line. Scaling remains to be fixed from the polynomial if a full face is requested.

## Complete reconstruction of the stabilizer formula

First compute the entire congruence Lie stabilizer

    g(U) = {T : T^T S + S T lies in U for every S in U}.

These are linear equations in the n^2 entries of T. In canonical coordinates write F_ij = E_ij+E_ji. For an edge {i,j}, the forbidden diagonal entries of T^T F_ij + F_ij T are 2T_ji and 2T_ij. For a nonedge {a,j} with a distinct from i,j, its (a,j) entry is T_ia. Therefore T_ia can be nonzero only if each neighbor j of i is a neighbor of a. The case a=j is already forbidden by the diagonal equation. Conversely, if N(i) is a subset of N(a), the elementary matrix E_ia sends every incident edge {i,j} to an allowed edge {a,j}; all other contributions vanish. Linearity proves

    g(U_G) = span{E_ia : N(i) subset N(a)}.                 (1)

The neighborhoods in (1) are OPEN. In particular two adjacent vertices cannot satisfy equality. Isolated vertices have empty neighborhoods, so their rows are unrestricted; this case is included, not silently removed. The input transforms by conjugation:

    g(U) = L^{-1} g(U_G) L.

Intersect with the metric-self-adjoint equations T^T M = M T. Put X=L T L^{-1}; then X^T D=D X, so D_i X_ia=D_a X_ai. A nonzero pair requires both neighborhood containments in (1), hence N(i)=N(a). Conversely all D-self-adjoint matrices within each equal-neighborhood block satisfy the equations. Thus

    J = g(U) intersection {T:T^T M=MT}
      = L^{-1} direct_sum_C [D_C^{-1/2} Sym(C) D_C^{1/2}] L, (2)

where C ranges over false-twin classes, including the class of isolated vertices. Positive square roots appear only in this proof; the algorithm uses rational linear equations.

For Uop=M^{-1}U, (2) equals the proposed Jordan multiplier space

    {T:T^T M=MT, T A + A T in Uop for every A in Uop}.

Indeed T^T S+ST = M(TA+AT). Therefore computing the full ordinary congruence stabilizer and intersecting with self-adjointness is an equally informed baseline for the proposed Jordan method. It has exactly the same legal input and output.

The subspace of J commuting with every element of J is

    Z(J) = L^{-1} span{I_C} L.                            (3)

To prove (3), commute with each block identity to eliminate cross-block entries; within a block, commute with its diagonal self-adjoint elements and its weighted symmetric elementary matrices to force a scalar. The block projectors in (3) give the maximal identifiable decomposition.

## Polynomial bit algorithm without an assumed simple covariant

Represent symmetric matrices by their n(n+1)/2 upper entries. Find a rational annihilator basis of U. For every S in its supplied basis and every annihilator ell, impose ell(T^T S+ST)=0. Gaussian elimination yields g(U). Add T^T M=MT to obtain J, then impose [Z,X]=0 for all basis X of J to obtain Z(J). These systems have polynomially many rows and columns, rational coefficients, and polynomial intermediate/output bit sizes under fraction-free or standard rational linear algebra.

Let C_0,...,C_{c-1} be ANY computed rational basis of the center, where c is its dimension. Consider

    Z(t)=C_0+t C_1+...+t^{c-1} C_{c-1}.

The center acts by one scalar on each hidden block. For any two distinct blocks the difference of their scalar functions of t is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most c-1, since the center contains their individual projectors. Hence at most (c-1) binom(c,2) integer parameters have a collision. Enumerate t=0,...,(c-1) binom(c,2). Factor the rational characteristic polynomial and accept an element with exactly c distinct linear factors. Under the promise its eigenvalues are rational. Nullspaces of Z(t)-lambda I are the acquired blocks. Rational univariate factoring and nullspace computation have polynomial bit algorithms; the parameter powers have O(n log n) bit length. This is deterministic separation, rather than a simple-spectrum assumption. Practical SymPy factorization is a replay implementation, not a proof of its own internal worst-case runtime.

If G has no false twins, each block has size one and this acquires all axes. Every C_n for n>=5, including EVEN C6, satisfies this property. C4 and K3,3 do not. K4 illustrates why closed-neighborhood twins must not be substituted for open-neighborhood twins: it has no false twins and its J is diagonal.

## Exact false-twin information obstruction, even with unequal lower terms

Each false-twin class is an independent set. Between two such classes, adjacency is complete or empty. Therefore any independent D_C-orthogonal changes of coordinates within classes preserve BOTH M=D and U_G. If a block has size at least two, these transformations contain nonmonomial rational matrices, so individual axes are not determined by (M,U) alone.

A concrete exact witness is C4, D=diag(1,2,3,4), and

    R = [[37/38, 0, -15/38, 0],
         [0,     1, 0,      0],
         [5/38,  0, 37/38,  0],
         [0,     0, 0,      1]].

It satisfies R^T D R=D and R^T U_C4 R=U_C4, but R^{-1}e_0 and R^{-1}e_2 are different lines from e_0,e_2. Thus L=I and L=R yield identical legal inputs with different hidden axes. The entries of D are unequal: a nonconstant co-diagonal metric does not fix the ambiguity. It is not a counterexample to recovery from the FULL quartic.

Another essential distinction: an arbitrary positive-definite metric is insufficient. With canonical U_C6 and M=I+11^T, (1) gives g=Diag, while metric self-adjointness forces every diagonal entry equal; J is scalar. This is outside the promised co-diagonal-metric interface, not a failure of (2).

## How additional quartic information resolves a block

Suppose the full homogeneous quartic is also supplied and has the hidden graph form

    p4(x)=sum_i a_i (Lx)_i^4 + sum_edges b_ij (Lx)_i^2 (Lx)_j^2,
    a_i>0.

Restrict to an acquired false-twin block with rational basis B. If C is that hidden class, y=LBz has support only in C; because C is independent, the restriction is

    f_C(z)=sum_{i in C} a_i (R_i z)^4,      R=(LB)|_C invertible.

The restricted metric is R^T D_C R. At a rational v, the metric-contracted Hessian is

    (B^T M B)^{-1} H_f(v)
      =12 R^{-1} diag((a_i/D_i)(R_i v)^2) R.

For each pair i!=j, an eigenvalue collision is a nonzero quadratic polynomial in v: the row forms are independent and cannot be proportional. Sampling coordinates from a finite grid of size K makes total collision probability at most |C|(|C|-1)/K. Distinct eigenvalues are rational under the rational promise; their eigenvectors recover block axes. The derivative and restriction use only the acquired block and the supplied polynomial. This is a conditional extension with additional information, not a generic full-polynomial simple-covariant assumption. Equally informed controls may also apply the older sums-of-powers equivalence algorithm to f_C.

For shifted graph quartics (y_i^2-s_i)^T Q(y_i^2-s_i), compatible metric weights are proportional to (Qs)_i and must be positive. This module tolerates unequal such weights ONCE U is acquired. The original edge eigenspace acquisition need not survive unequal weights: normalized edge eigenvalues vary as 2Q_ij/(3D_iD_j), and separation from the diagonal block requires its own argument. Thus the entire unknown graph quartic is not claimed acquired outside the input promise.

## Prior art and comparator limits

Draisma, Kuhnt and Zwiernik, *Groups acting on Gaussian graphical models*, arXiv1207.5910 (first 25 July2012; v3 29 October2013), Annals of Statistics41(4),1944–1969 (2013). Read primary v3 introduction and complete Lemma2.1/Proposition2.2/Proposition2.5 proofs. They describe the congruence stabilizer of graph-supported concentration matrices, whose diagonal entries are free. Their Lie algebra uses CLOSED-neighborhood inclusion. Our zero-diagonal edge space instead gives OPEN-neighborhood inclusion, so their theorem is a close mechanism comparator rather than a directly applicable identical-input theorem. Neither version warrants presenting the stabilizer approach as a new Lie-theoretic invention.

Li, Qiao, A.Wigderson, Y.Wigderson and Zhang, *Connections between graphs and matrix spaces*, arXiv2206.04815 (first9 June2022; inspected author PDF cover10 June2022). Read §7 Lemmas7.2–7.3 completely. Their spaces use individual matrix units from directed edges. The neighborhood constraints on normalizers are closely related, but their input and hypotheses differ from symmetric edge pairs. They also connect graph isomorphism to matrix-space equivalence; this is not a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary graph-space equivalence.

Kayal, *Efficient algorithms for some special cases of the polynomial equivalence problem*, SODA2011,1409–1421, doi10.1137/1.9781611973082.108. Read §5 and §5.1 completely from author PDF, with publication metadata verified at SIAM. Lemma5.2 identifies the Hessian determinant factors of a sum of independent powers; §5.1 obtains the forms by polynomial factoring and checks coefficients. This is a strong older comparator for our restricted false-twin quartics. It does not solve arbitrary quartic equivalence. SIAM's online posting18December2013 is distinct from the proceedings year2011.
