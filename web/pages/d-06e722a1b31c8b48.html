1. SYMMETRIC LIMIT AND EXACT COHERENT POPULATION INTERFACE
Let |D_{N,k}> be the normalized Dicke vector with k ones. Jz eigenvalue is
k-N/2, up to reversing the sign convention. Set

 p_k = exp[-delta(k-N/2)^2/N], 0<=k<=N,
 S_N(delta) = sum_k p_k |D_{N,k}><D_{N,k}|.

For x in[0,1], phase-average |psi(x,phi)>^tensor N, with
psi=sqrt(1-x)|0>+exp(i phi)sqrt(x)|1>. Its Dicke populations are
B_{N,k}(x)=binom(N,k)x^k(1-x)^(N-k). Therefore a nonnegative integrable density
P(x) satisfying

 integral_0^1 B_{N,k}(x) P(x) dx = p_k                         (1)

is an exact fully separable representation of the UNNORMALIZED S_N(delta).
Its mass is sum_k p_k. Dividing by that mass gives a probability mixture.

Conversely, every pure product vector in the symmetric subspace has identical
factors: swapping sites leaves the product invariant, so the two corresponding
one-site rays must agree. In a positive sum representing a state supported in
the symmetric subspace, every vector lies in that support (test every vector
in its orthogonal complement). Thus full separability of S_N is equivalent to
a probability measure in the Bernstein population interface after normalization.

The numbers q_k=p_k/binom(N,k) are logit-Stieltjes moments, not ordinary moments
of x: q_k=integral x^k(1-x)^(N-k) dmu(x). With t=x/(1-x) they are moments of
(1-x)^N dmu under t. Endpoint x=1 can be retained separately. Positivity of
the relevant two Hankel forms is the necessary symmetric-limit test; finite
diagnostics alone do not prove the theorem. Our density construction bypasses
the extension/boundary issues in that test.

The integrand has no poles in any contour rectangle with |Re u|<=a. We next
prove PT strictly positive with a UNIFORM lower bound, including endpoint limits.

3. SADDLE AND MAIN CONTOUR ESTIMATE
For any real s, define v as the unique solution

 v=(delta L/N)tanh[(s+v)/2].                                  (3)

The right-hand side has derivative at most delta L/(2N)<=1/4, maps the compact
interval[-delta L/N,delta L/N] to itself, and hence has a unique fixed point.
For s>=0, v>=0; for s<=0, v<=0. Set t=tanh[(s+v)/2]. Thus
v=(delta L/N)t, |t|<=1, |v|<=delta L/N<=1/2.

Deform the u integral from[-a,a] to[-a,a]-i v and its two vertical edges.
The horizontal integrand relative to exp[Nv^2/(4delta)]w0(s+v) is

 exp[-Nu^2/(4delta)] c(u)^(-L) exp[iNu v/(2delta)],

Since cos(u/2)>0, its phase is
 phi(u)=L[t u/2-atan(t tan(u/2))].                             (4)
The fixed-point equation exactly cancels the linear phase. The integrand at
-u is the conjugate, so the horizontal integral is real.

For |z|<=3/4,
 cos z>=1-z^2/2>=23/32, |tan z|<=(32/23)|z|.
The derivative of atan(t tan z)-t z is

 |phi(u)| <= L (256/7935)|u|^3 <= L|u|^3/30.                  (5)

g>=(15/23)/(4delta). Since cos phi>=1-phi^2/2 and |c|^(-L)>=1,

5. EXACT POPULATIONS AND THEIR SMALL RESIDUAL

6. EXACT LEGENDRE CORRECTION
Let E be the unique real linear functional on polynomials of degree<=N with
E(B_{N,k})=Delta p_k. These Bernstein polynomials form a basis. Let
R_l(x)=P_l(2x-1), the shifted Legendre polynomial. It satisfies
integral_0^1 R_l R_j dx=1_{l=j}/(2l+1), and |R_l(x)|<=1 on[0,1].
The latter bound follows, for example, from the integral representation
P_l(z)=pi^-1 integral_0^pi [z+i sqrt(1-z^2)cos(theta)]^l dtheta for |z|<=1.

The exact degree-l Bernstein expansion is

 R_l(x)=sum_{j=0}^l (-1)^(l-j) binom(l,j) B_{l,j}(x).

Degree elevation from l to N is a convex average of these coefficients:
its degree-N coefficient at k is
sum_j binom(l,j)binom(N-l,k-j)/binom(N,k) times the degree-l coefficient.
The averaging weights are nonnegative and sum to1 by Vandermonde. Hence each
degree-N coefficient of R_l has absolute value<=2^l. It follows from(14) that

 |E(R_l)| <=2^l sum_k |Delta p_k| <=2^l(N+1)t_N.

Define the REAL degree-N polynomial

 PC(x)=sum_{l=0}^N (2l+1)E(R_l)R_l(x).                        (15)

Orthogonality implies integral f(x)PC(x)dx=E(f) for every polynomial f of
degree<=N. In particular PC corrects each Bernstein population exactly. Also

 ||PC||_infinity <= (N+1)(2N+1)2^(N+1)t_N.                    (16)

8. EXACT FINITE-ALPHA REDUCTION
For t=alpha/N>=0 the isotropic Casimir Gibbs operator G=e^(tJ^2) has an exact
positive rotationally invariant iid-product representation. One explicit heat
identity is

 G=e^(-t/4)(4pi t)^(-3/2) integral_{R^3}
       exp[-|y|^2/(4t)] [sinh(|y|/2)/(|y|/2)] e^(y dot J) dy. (20)

For t=0 use the limiting identity G=I. To verify(20), restrict to any spin-j
irrep. Rotational invariance makes the integral scalar there; its trace is
the character sinh[(j+1/2)r]/sinh(r/2). Multiplying the sinch factor cancels
the denominator. The Gaussian radial integral (differentiate the elementary
cosh Gaussian integral) gives (2j+1)exp[t(j+1/2)^2]. The prefactor e^-t/4
therefore gives (2j+1)exp[tj(j+1)], proving every block identity.

Since e^(y dot J)=tensor_i exp(y dot sigma_i/2), normalizing each one-site
operator gives tau_(r n)=(I+r n dot sigma)/2 with r=tanh(|y|/2) in[0,1).
All scalar trace factors are positive. Thus G is a positive radial mixture of

 K_r=integral_{S^2} tau_(r n)^tensor N dn/(4pi).

For r>=0, tau_(r n)=r |n><n|+(1-r)I/2. Expanding the tensor product gives
an exact nonnegative sum over subsets S:

 K_r=sum_{S subset[N]} r^|S|(1-r)^(N-|S|)
               [Pi_sym,S/(|S|+1)] tensor (I/2)_Sc.           (21)

Here integral |n><n|^tensor k dn/(4pi)=Pi_sym,k/(k+1), by rotational
invariance and trace1. For k0 the block factor is1. No coefficients are
negative. D=exp[-delta Jz^2/N] commutes with each subset symmetrizer and with
the identity on the complement. Condition on a computational-basis vector
of the complement, with Jz complement magnetization m. The remaining k-site
block is, up to its positive scalar,

 exp[-delta(Jz,S+m)^2/N]Pi_sym,S
 = exp[-delta m^2/N] F exp[-(delta k/N)Jz,S^2/k]Pi_sym,S F,
 F=exp[-delta m Jz,S/N]=tensor_{i in S} exp[-delta m sigma_z/(2N)]. (22)

The k0 case is scalar. For k>=1, delta k/N<=delta<=1/4; the middle operator
is S_k(delta k/N), which was proved fully separable above. F is a tensor
product of positive local filters, so conjugating by F preserves full
separability. The fixed complement vector is a product vector. Thus D times
every term in(21) is a fully separable positive operator. Integrating the
positive radial weights from(20) preserves the closed fully separable cone.

Finally G and D commute because J^2 commutes with Jz. Therefore DG is exactly
the unnormalized target exp[(alpha J^2-delta Jz^2)/N]. This proves the theorem
for every finite alpha>=0. It also establishes the equivalence: an all-N floor
for this finite-alpha family implies the symmetric floor by alpha→infinity
and closedness; the symmetric floor implies the finite-alpha family by(20)-(22).
