## Precise stronger statement

Let B be n-1 variables, G in C[B,tau]^n have detJG=kappa in C nonzero, Q in C[B,tau] be SOURCE-only, and sigma be a scalar. Let s be algebraic over K(Z), where K=C(B), satisfying the selected critical equation

    Z dot G_tau(B,s)+Q_tau(B,s)=sigma.

Suppose its selected critical value

    E=Z dot G(B,s)+Q(B,s)-sigma s

belongs to the GLOBAL polynomial ring C[B,Z]. Then s is globally polynomial, and necessarily

    G=h(B)+tau g(B),
    Q_tau=q1 tau+q0(B), q1 in C nonzero,
    s=(sigma-Z dot g-q0(B))/q1.                   (E1)

Conversely(E1) gives a globally polynomial selected section and value. The result does not require a polynomial inverse for G or assume a nonzero scalar Hessian before the proof. It is about a generic algebraic section with a value polynomial on the whole base/dual domain, not isolated fibre values or a locally analytic germ.

## Polynomial Lüroth over K=C(B), without algebraic closure of K

We need the following classical refinement. If K is any characteristic-zero field and

    K subsetneq A=K(f1(tau),...,fn(tau)) subset K(tau),

with the fi polynomials, then A=K(h(tau)) for a nonconstant polynomial h, and all fi=Hi(h) with Hi in K[T]. We use ordinary Lüroth for the existence of a rational generator r in K(tau); the polynomial and composition refinements are proved explicitly here.

Choose a nonconstant fi=Si(r). Regard r as a nonconstant map of projective lines and work over an algebraic closure ONLY to inspect pole fibres. Every pole beta of Si has a nonempty r-fibre, and every point of that fibre is a pole of fi. The polynomial fi has just one pole, the source infinity. Therefore there is a unique pole beta, and its entire fibre is the source infinity. In particular

    beta=r(infinity) belongs to K union {infinity}.

The last assertion is the essential nonclosed-field step: for a rational function with coefficients in K, its projective value at infinity is either infinity or a ratio of leading coefficients in K. A fractional-linear transformation over K sends beta to infinity. Its composite h with r has no finite pole; after reducing its numerator/denominator, a nonconstant denominator would have a finite root over the algebraic closure. Hence h lies in K[tau], is nonconstant, and still generates A.

Now each fj is rational in h. Any finite pole alpha of its rational expression Hj would have a finite preimage under the nonconstant polynomial h: h(tau)-alpha has a root over the algebraic closure. The composite fj would have a finite pole there, impossible. Therefore Hj has only the infinity pole and is a polynomial over K. This proves the refinement, including coefficients over K itself; no generator over a larger constant field was silently substituted.

For completeness, nonemptiness of the rational-map fibres used above follows from homogenizing a reduced numerator/denominator to their common positive degree. Each target point gives a nonzero homogeneous binary polynomial with a root over the algebraic closure, and coprimeness rules out a base point. The pole orders of composition multiply by the positive ramification order, so there is no cancellation of a reduced pole.

## Every Keller scalar curve is generically recoverable when its base is supplied

Over K=C(B), at least one component Gi(tau) is nonconstant: otherwise the Keller tau column is zero. The polynomial Lüroth refinement gives

## The algebraic-branch denominator edge and envelope derivatives

The algebraic branch s cannot be algebraic over K alone. If it were, the free dual variables would remain algebraically independent over K(s). Coefficient comparison in the critical equation would force all Gi_tau(B,s)=0. Substituting into the unit Keller Jacobian gives a zero column and a contradiction. Thus s is transcendental over K.

Consequently evaluation tau↦s is injective on K[tau]. In particular the nonzero denominator D(G(tau)) in(E3) remains nonzero after evaluation. This is necessary: a rational inverse formula cannot simply be evaluated at a special branch where its denominator vanishes.

Characteristic zero makes the finite extension K(Z)(s)/K(Z) separable. Each partial derivative in Zi extends uniquely; differentiating a minimal polynomial defines it because that polynomial's tau derivative is nonzero at s. The envelope identity is therefore valid in the extension:

    E_Zi=Gi(B,s)
       +[Z dot G_tau(B,s)+Q_tau(B,s)-sigma]s_Zi
       =Gi(B,s).

Since E is global polynomial, every Gi(B,s) is polynomial in B,Z. Evaluating(E3) now makes

    s=N(G(B,s))/D(G(B,s)) in K(Z).               (E4)

Every algebraic branch with polynomial critical value has become rational. No assumption about analyticity or the scalar Hessian was used in this step.

## Rational poles and global base denominators

Choose a component Gi of positive tau degree d. If the rational s in(E4) had a pole at an irreducible divisor of K[Z], the highest tau term of Gi(B,s) would have valuation d ord(s), strictly less than every lower term because its leading coefficient is a nonzero element of K and hence a unit for these dual divisors. The sum could not be polynomial. But Gi(B,s)=E_Zi is polynomial. Thus

    s belongs to K[Z].                          (E5)

Apply the source-section degree argument over K. A dual-independent section would kill the Keller column. If G_tau has degree d>=1 and s has dual degree m>=1, cancellation forces m(e-d)=1 for e=deg_tau Q_tau, hence m=1,e=d+1. The transverse dual coefficients then make G_tau=r(tau)ell and give a nonconstant common tau-column factor, contradicting Keller. Therefore G is affine in tau and Q_tau=q1(B)tau+q0(B), with q1 nonzero; the field section is(E1). This is the already independently reconstructed proof in `SOURCE_ONLY_REPAIR_SECTION_GATE.md`, before its global polynomiality step.

Writing Q=q1 tau^2/2+q0 tau+Q0(B), the exact critical value is

    E=Z dot h+Q0(B)-(Z dot g+q0(B)-sigma)^2/(2q1(B)).

The coefficient of Zi^2 is -gi^2/(2q1). Global polynomiality of E gives q1 dividing each gi^2 in C[B]. The g column is unimodular over C[B] by evaluating the Keller adjugate at tau=0. Any irreducible factor of q1 would then divide every gi, a contradiction. Thus q1 is a nonzero constant, and s is in C[B,Z]. This completes(E1) and the converse.
