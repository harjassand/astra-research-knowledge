Cycle 2 follow-up — a path-string obstruction for covariant history connectors
Date: 2026-10-08

RESULT

For the one-round BMVZ path-indexed family, the stationary path law gives a sharper and fully quantified obstruction than the deterministic spaced-sum estimate in RESULT.txt. Every reversible path-label kernel whose transitions alter at most q coordinate labels has ordinary Poincare gap at most C_lambda q/t, with an explicit constant depending only on the base expander eigenvalue. This bound applies to the branch graph inside the covariant history connector by unitary gauge equivalence. Thus constant gap requires changing Omega(t) path coordinates per edge. If every changed coordinate contributes an independent nonmonomial relative Clifford factor, polynomial row support forces only O(log n) such changes, so the connector gap is O(log n/t).

This is an architecture-specific obstruction, not a no-go for all common isometries. BMVZ's final iterated term labels are not established to obey a single stationary length-t Markov path law, and the seed-by-seed nonmonomial condition has not been verified for all candidate edges in the actual iterated label table.

1. Exact source law and theorem

In an expander-backed BMVZ layer, Definition 1.7 of arXiv:2510.01333v1 identifies the one-round labels with all length-t paths in an undirected d-regular lambda-spectral expander G on m vertices, one-to-one. Therefore a uniform path label X=(X_1,...,X_t) has the exact law

    X_1 ~ uniform(V),       X_{i+1} | X_i ~ P_G(X_i, .),

where P_G=A/d. Every path string has probability 1/(m d^(t-1)). Definition 1.9 samples these labels uniformly in the amplified layer. Definition 2.8 bounds the nontrivial adjacency eigenvalues by lambda_G<d, so the normalized transition matrix has absolute eigenvalue bound theta=lambda_G/d<1; in the theorem, lambda denotes this normalized bound. BMVZ handles layers below its expander-size threshold by repeating clauses; the theorem below is stated for an expander-backed layer and does not assume anything about a later iterated distribution.

Theorem. Let G be an undirected regular base walk with stationary distribution nu and absolute nontrivial eigenvalues at most lambda<1. Let mu_t be its stationary length-t path law. Let K be any reversible Markov kernel on the path strings, with stationary law mu_t, such that K(x,y)>0 implies that x and y differ in at most q coordinates. Write

    K_F = 1 + 2 lambda^2/(1-lambda^2),
    C_lambda = max{9, 32 K_F}.

For q>=1, the ordinary Poincare gap satisfies

    gap(K) <= min{2, C_lambda q/t}.

For q=0, gap(K)=0. No bound on the degree of K is needed for this inequality, so it in particular holds for every bounded-degree reversible path graph of this type. The constant is explicit; the asymptotic order O_lambda(q/t) is sharper than O(q^2/t). We do not claim this order is attained by every bounded-degree graph on BMVZ paths.

Proof. Choose A subset V with p=nu(A) in [1/3,2/3] (for the BMVZ regular family, take a subset of floor(m/2) vertices when m>=2), and let g=1_A-p. Then E_nu[g]=0 and sigma^2=E_nu[g^2]=p(1-p)>=2/9. For the path coordinates define the covariance matrix

    Sigma_ij = E_mu_t[g(X_i)g(X_j)] = <g, P_G^|i-j| g>_nu.

The base spectral bound gives |Sigma_ij|<=sigma^2 lambda^|i-j|. Consequently

    ||Sigma||_F^2 <= sigma^4 [ t + 2 sum_{k=1}^{t-1}(t-k)lambda^(2k) ]
                   <= K_F t sigma^4.

Choose independent Rademacher signs epsilon_1,...,epsilon_t and set

    F_epsilon(x) = sum_{i=1}^t epsilon_i g(x_i).

For every sign vector, F_epsilon is mean-zero under mu_t, and

    Var_mu_t(F_epsilon) = epsilon^T Sigma epsilon.

The sign average of this variance is tr(Sigma)=t sigma^2. Its variance over signs is

    4 sum_{i<j} Sigma_ij^2 <= 2 ||Sigma||_F^2 <= 2 K_F t sigma^4.

Chebyshev therefore gives

    Pr_epsilon[ Var_mu_t(F_epsilon) < t sigma^2/2 ] <= 8 K_F/t.

For a reversible kernel K, write its Dirichlet form as

    E_K(F) = (1/2) E_{X~mu_t,Y~K(X,.)}[(F(X)-F(Y))^2].

For any fixed transition x->y, average over the signs. The cross terms vanish, and each changed coordinate contributes at most one because g(x_i)-g(y_i) is in {-1,0,1}; hence

    E_epsilon E_K(F_epsilon) <= q/2.

Markov's inequality gives Pr_epsilon[E_K(F_epsilon)>q]<=1/2. If t>16 K_F, the two good-sign events have total failure probability strictly below one. Some epsilon thus has variance at least t sigma^2/2 and Dirichlet form at most q. The variational characterization yields

    gap(K) <= E_K(F_epsilon)/Var_mu_t(F_epsilon)
            <= 2q/(t sigma^2) <= 9q/t.

For t<=16 K_F, use the universal bound gap(K)<=2; the chosen C_lambda makes min{2,C_lambda q/t}=2 for q>=1. If q=0 every transition leaves the path label unchanged and the gap is zero. This proves the theorem. ∎

2. Transfer to the covariant history isometry

Let U_x be the branch Clifford frame for path x and G=direct-sum_x U_x. For a symmetric normalized path graph Laplacian L_K, the covariant connector is

    C_conn = G (L_K tensor I) G*.

It has exactly the same spectrum and gap as L_K. If C_0 is the branchwise code-space enforcer from RESULT.txt, then C_0 vanishes on the branch-valid subspace. Fix a unit vector xi in the common logical space and put Psi=G(F_epsilon tensor xi). The chosen F_epsilon is orthogonal to the stationary constant vector, so Psi is orthogonal to the common-frame kernel. On this test vector the C_0 energy is zero, and the Rayleigh quotient of (C_0+C_conn)/2 is one half that of L_K. Therefore the full combined enforcer has gap at most gap(K)/2 on the complement of its intended common-history kernel. In particular it cannot have a uniform positive gap if q=o(t).

3. Exact row-support implication and gate accounting

Suppose each edge x~y changes q path-coordinate frames, and the relative Clifford U_x U_y* factors as a tensor product over those disjoint coordinate blocks. A Clifford with Hadamard rank r has exactly 2^r nonzeros in each row. The ranks add across disjoint tensor factors. If at least rho q changed blocks contribute independent nonmonomial factors (rank at least one each), then r>=rho q and that edge block has at least 2^(rho q) nonzeros per row. For a constant-degree path graph and a polynomial row bound n^c, this requires rho q<=c log_2 n+O(1). If rho is a fixed positive constant, q=O(log n), and the gap theorem gives gap O_lambda(log n/t). Constant gap would instead require q>=gamma t/C_lambda, making those connector rows superpolynomial when rho>0 is fixed.

The implication is stated in terms of independent changed-coordinate factors, not an unqualified gate count. It yields s=O(log n) if s counts one such nonmonomial factor per changed seed coordinate. A raw number of local Clifford gates alone is insufficient: multiple gates may compose to a monomial, one gate may touch multiple seed blocks, or the decomposition may include cancellations. An actual gate-count theorem needs a verified relation between the minimal local-circuit cost, changed coordinate count, and Hadamard rank for the concrete seed table.

4. Exact scope and falsifiable next lemma

The Markov covariance calculation is valid for the one-round BMVZ path-label law in a layer whose base graph has the stated spectral bound. It does not automatically cover the final iterated BMVZ clause labels: Theorem 1.4 describes polynomially many O(n)-fold products of constant-local projections, but does not assert that the final labels are sampled as one stationary length-t walk. Until the exact final label generator and its law are reconstructed, treating those labels as such would be an unsupported assumption. Likewise, the row lower bound is conditional on independent nonmonomial coordinate factors; BJSW noncommutativity does not by itself imply this property for every edge of an arbitrary clause expander.

Falsifiable next lemma for the actual iterated construction: for its exact final label law mu_n, exhibit a centered path-string observable F_n with Var_mu_n(F_n)>=a t and prove that every reversible edge kernel changing at most q seed positions has mean Dirichlet cost at most b q, for constants a,b>0 independent of n. This would imply gap<= (b/a)q/t for final labels. Separately, tabulate the exact relative Clifford Hadamard rank for each candidate edge and prove r(x,y)>=rho q(x,y) on the proposed graph. If either statement fails, this one-round theorem does not settle the full family.

The q/t order is not asserted to be tight for every bounded-degree graph on BMVZ paths. It is tight as a general locality-versus-variance scale on independent-coordinate product laws under coordinate-update kernels; a matching lower bound for a bounded-degree BMVZ path-label kernel is not established here.
