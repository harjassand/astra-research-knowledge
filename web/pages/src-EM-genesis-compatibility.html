For an energy density \(F:\mathbb R^{2\times2}\to\mathbb R\), rank-one convexity means
\[ D^2F(A)[\eta\otimes\xi,\eta\otimes\xi]\geq0 \]
for every matrix \(A\) and vectors \(\eta,\xi\).
Quasiconvexity asks for the much stronger conclusion
\[ \int_U F(A+D\varphi(x))\,dx\geq |U|F(A), \qquad \varphi\in W^{1,\infty}_0(U;\mathbb R^2). \]
The distinction is between testing a single compatible wave and controlling arbitrary combinations of compatible deformations. It is central to the stability of vector-valued variational energies. The planar implication is the relevant form of Morrey’s problem. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1)

Define the heat-dissipation density
\[ E_F(A;B_1,B_2) = D^2F(A)[B_1,B_1]+D^2F(A)[B_2,B_2]. \]
When \(A=Dv\), the matrices \(B_i=\partial_iDv\) cannot vary independently. Equality of mixed derivatives gives
\[ B_1= \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ d&e \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B_2= \begin{pmatrix} b&c\\ e&f \end{pmatrix}. \tag{1} \]
Call such a pair compatible.
The correction method seeks a two-form \(\omega\) on matrix space such that
\[ E_F(A;B_1,B_2)+\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\geq0. \tag{2} \]
For a globally exact correction, the added term integrates to zero. A suitable heat-flow argument then gives quasiconvexity. The exact-form/heat-flow interface is prior machinery. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1)
The first useful deduction is stronger than finding a correction for one energy.
For every \(C^2\) rank-one-convex \(F\), and every fixed matrix \(A\), there exists a two-form \(\omega_A\) making (2) nonnegative for every compatible pair.
This does not assert that the choices depend smoothly on \(A\), are polynomial in \(A\), or form a closed differential form.
A pure compatible second derivative has coordinates
\[ (a,b,c,d,e,f) = (u x^2,u xy,u y^2,v x^2,v xy,v y^2). \]
Equivalently,
\[ B_i=\xi_i(\eta\otimes\xi). \]
On these jets,
\[ E_F=|\xi|^2D^2F(A)[\eta\otimes\xi,\eta\otimes\xi]\geq0. \]
This is a nonnegative binary quartic in \(\xi\), whose coefficients form a symmetric matrix acting on \(\eta\). The established sum-of-squares factorization for positive-semidefinite univariate matrix polynomials, followed by homogenization, expresses it as a sum of squares of functions linear in the six jet coordinates. Thus there is a positive-semidefinite quadratic form \(P\) on all six coordinates agreeing with \(E_F\) on pure jets. The factorization is imported machinery, not a new theorem here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08261)
The space of quadratic forms in six variables has dimension \(21\). Their restrictions to these pure jets span the \(15\)-dimensional space of polynomials of bidegree \((2,4)\) in \((\eta,\xi)\). The kernel therefore has dimension six, with basis
\[ ac-b^2,\quad ae-bd,\quad af-be,\quad be-cd,\quad bf-ce,\quad df-e^2. \tag{3} \]
These are precisely the six two-form evaluations on the columns of
\[ \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ b&c\\ d&e\\ e&f \end{pmatrix}. \]
Consequently \(P-E_F=\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\). ∎
What this removes: unrestricted pointwise completion is always possible.

\[ F_p(A)=\big((p-1)s-r\big)R^{p-1}. \tag{4} \]
Work initially on

Let
\[ \omega_z=d(\Re z)\wedge d(\Im z),\qquad \omega_w=d(\Re w)\wedge d(\Im w), \]

\[ K_p=2p(p-1)R^{p-2}, \qquad \Omega_p=K_p(\omega_z+\omega_w). \tag{5} \]
At a given matrix, rotate domain and target so that \(z=r\), \(w=s\), and express the compatible jets in those coordinates as in (1).
\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &E_{F_p}(A;B_1,B_2)+\Omega_p(B_1,B_2)\\ &\quad= p(p-2)R^{p-3} \left[ (p-1)s(a^2+b^2) +\frac{R^2}{4r}\big((c-e)^2+(b-d)^2\big) \right]. \end{aligned}} \tag{6} \]
The right-hand side is nonnegative.
At the diagonal representative,
\[ \begin{aligned} 2z_x&=a+e+i(d-b),& 2z_y&=b+f+i(e-c),\\ 2w_x&=a-e+i(d+b),& 2w_y&=b-f+i(e+c). \end{aligned} \]
The radial Hessian coefficients are
\[ \begin{aligned} F_{rr}&=p(p-1)R^{p-3}\big[-r+(p-3)s\big],\\ F_{rs}&=p(p-1)(p-2)sR^{p-3},\\ F_{ss}&=p(p-1)R^{p-3}\big[r+(p-1)s\big], \end{aligned} \]

\[ \frac{F_r}{r} =pR^{p-2}\frac{-r+(p-2)s}{r}, \qquad \frac{F_s}{s}=p(p-1)R^{p-2}. \]
Substitution and collection of coefficients gives (6). The supplied symbolic script verifies it with \(p\) left as a parameter.

\[ d\Omega_p=dK_p\wedge(\omega_z+\omega_w)\neq0. \]
It would be incorrect to integrate away this correction using Stokes’ theorem.
The next result shows that a more elaborate closed correction cannot repair this.
For every \(p>2\), there is no \(C^1\) closed two-form \(\omega\) on \(\mathcal U\) satisfying
\[ E_{F_p}(A;B_1,B_2)+\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\geq0 \tag{7} \]
for every \(A\in\mathcal U\) and every compatible pair.
The form need not be polynomial, homogeneous, exact, or bounded near the excluded axes.
Suppose such a certificate exists.
The energy is invariant under independent rotations of \(z,w\), induced by domain and target rotations. Average the certificate over this compact symmetry group. This preserves positivity and closedness.
Also average using simultaneous conjugation of \(z,w\). Because the corresponding domain reflection reverses the orientation of the spatial jet pair, the appropriate action on the correction is minus pullback. After this averaging, the general surviving form is
\[ \omega = rU\,dr\wedge d\theta +rV\,ds\wedge d\theta +sW\,dr\wedge d\phi +sZ\,ds\wedge d\phi, \tag{8} \]
where \(z=re^{i\theta}\), \(w=se^{i\phi}\), and \(U,V,W,Z\) depend only on \(r,s\).
Now use a basic property of positive-semidefinite quadratic forms:
A direction with zero quadratic value must have zero mixed coefficient with every other direction.
For the corrected quadratic form in \(a,b,c,d,e,f\), the direction with only \(f\neq0\) has zero quadratic value. Its mixed coefficients force
\[ U-V=K_p,\qquad W-Z=-K_p. \tag{9} \]
After imposing (9), the direction with \(c=e\) and all other coordinates zero is another null direction. Its mixed coefficient with \(a\) forces
\[ Z=K_p,\qquad W=0. \tag{10} \]
These are necessary conditions for any averaged positive completion, not choices made to simplify the calculation.
Closedness of (8) requires
\[ \partial_r(sZ)-\partial_s(sW)=0. \]
But the forced coefficients give
\[ \boxed{ \partial_r(sZ)-\partial_s(sW) = 2p(p-1)(p-2)sR^{p-3}>0. } \tag{11} \]
This contradiction proves the theorem. ∎
The nonzero exterior derivative is forced by positivity along null directions. It is not an accident of the explicit correction in (5).

The quartic setting has additional structure absent from the nonpolynomial Burkholder family.
For a homogeneous quartic \(F\), symmetry of its four-linear polarization gives
\[ \boxed{ D^2F(A)[H,H]=D^2F(H)[A,A]. } \tag{13} \]
Consequently, if \(F\) is rank-one convex and \(R\) has rank one, then
\[ D^2F(R)[H,H]\geq0 \qquad\text{for every }H. \tag{14} \]
Thus the full Hessian is positive semidefinite at every rank-one matrix.
This yields an exact classification.
Suppose \(F\) is a homogeneous quartic polynomial, is rank-one convex, and
\[ F(R)=0 \]
for every rank-one matrix \(R\). Then
\[ \boxed{F(A)=c(\det A)^2,\qquad c\geq0.} \tag{15} \]
Vanishing on the determinant-zero variety implies
\[ F=(\det A)\,Q(A) \]
for a quadratic polynomial \(Q\).
At a nonzero rank-one \(R\), (14) and homogeneity give
\[ D^2F(R)\succeq0, \qquad D^2F(R)[R,R]=12F(R)=0. \]
Positive semidefiniteness therefore gives
\[ D^2F(R)[R,H]=0 \]
for every \(H\). Differentiated Euler homogeneity implies
\[ D^2F(R)[R,H]=3\,DF(R)[H], \]
so \(DF(R)=0\).
But
\[ DF(R)=Q(R)\,D(\det)(R), \]
and \(D(\det)(R)\neq0\). Hence \(Q\) also vanishes on the rank-one variety and is a multiple of \(\det A\). Rank-one convexity along \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,t)\) forces that multiple to be nonnegative. ∎
This does not establish that determinant-square is the only obstruction to closed completion. It establishes the narrower, exact statement that it is the only rank-one-convex quartic invisible on all rank-one matrices.
Write \(\Delta(A)=\det A\). Define
\[ \gamma_*(F) = \inf_{\substack{\operatorname{rank}H=1\\D\Delta(A)[H]\neq0}} \frac{D^2F(A)[H,H]} {2\big(D\Delta(A)[H]\big)^2}. \tag{16} \]
For rank-one-convex quartics this is finite and nonnegative, and, for \(\gamma\geq0\),

The proof is direct: along a rank-one line, \(\Delta(A+tH)\) is affine, so
