# Conditional route sampling without whole-route rejection

This note derives an algorithmic consequence; the source supplies a measurable construction, not this mixing or acquisition claim.

## 1. Question and declared service

At fixed exposed data, a fixed simple route with n distinct vertices, fixed selected accessible labels, and a fixed individually permitted outside-path configuration, let F_i be the source's independent **whole vertex-factor** law. A factor contains one common midpoint and at most one path in each of two copies. The remaining checks are hard avoidance within each copy; there are no checks between copies. The target is

    ν_out(dξ) = Z_out^{-1} J(out,ξ) ∏_{i=1}^n F_i(dξ_i).       (H1)

The outside is held fixed throughout. An exact draw from each F_i and exact tests of its incident checks are declared services with costs C_base and C_test. They are not automatically finite algorithms for continuous Brownian paths or measurable target sets. In particular, accessibility uses a bridge-success probability threshold; deciding that threshold, drawing a uniform point in its target set, and testing whole-path hard collisions remain unacquired. The output below is an ε-accurate draw from (H1) **conditional on those services and that supplied outside sample**.

This does not sample the outside marginal λ_out, the equilibrium Gibbs state, the exposed data, or the route law. It does not turn the imaginary-time construction into a real-time thermal dynamics theorem.

## 2. Uniform premise and the interaction matrix

Write e=e_D, χ=χ_D=C_col M e_D and a=1−χ. The source gives e_D≤C_t' D^{-1/4}, where short bridge time t and M are fixed before D. Its deterministic-opponent estimate integrates the factor's common midpoint. It holds for every fixed regular, confined opponent with its recorded endpoints, whether or not the opponent's label is accessible. It is not valid at a prescribed common midpoint, so the **entire factor** must be updated together.

For every individually permitted assignment of the other factors, even if other pair checks fail, the F_i probability of passing all incident checks is at least a. A pair of distinct factors has at most two checks, one in each copy. Under F_i, failing either check against a fixed value of factor j has probability at most 2e. Only distinct labels are checked within a copy, as required by the source premise.

Let A_i(x) denote the set of values of factor i compatible with the fixed outside and the other factor values x. If x,y differ only at j, the only changed checks are these at most two checks. Therefore

    F_i(A_i(x) Δ A_i(y)) ≤ 4e,
    F_i(A_i(x)), F_i(A_i(y)) ≥ a.                            (H2)

For a probability F and sets A,B of masses at least a, direct integration of the two normalized indicators gives

    TV(F(·|A),F(·|B)) ≤ F(A Δ B)/a.                         (H3)

Here TV is one half of the L1 distance. For example, if F(A)≥F(B), overlap mass of the two densities is F(A∩B)/F(A); hence TV=F(A\B)/F(A), which proves (H3). Thus the heat-bath influence c_ij is at most 4e/a if factors i,j can interact, and zero otherwise.

Every factor path is within distance eleven of its route center; centers at lattice supremum distance greater than thirty cannot interact, as explicitly used in the source. A simple route has at most one factor at each lattice site. Consequently the number of interacting other factors is at most

    Δ = 61^3−1 = 226980,
    max_i ∑_j c_ij ≤ c := 4Δe/a.                            (H4)

The fixed outside affects a and the conditioning kernels but adds no moving coordinate to the influence sum. These bounds are uniform in the outside, route length and scaled volume. Torus identification can reduce the count and never increases it.

Choose D after the fixed t,M so χ≤1/4 and e≤3/(32Δ). Then a≥3/4 and c≤1/2. More generally the theorem only needs a>0 and c<1. An effective numerical D cannot be asserted without acquired constants C_col,C_t'; the source proves their existence rather than affordable calibration.

If those constants and the preceding geometric thresholds are acquired, it suffices additionally to take D≥max{(4C_col M C_t')^4,(32ΔC_t'/3)^4}. This formula makes the quantitative dependency explicit without claiming those constants have been calibrated.

## 3. A legal starting state and exact heat-bath updates

Construct a valid initial configuration in a chosen ordering of factors: repeatedly draw F_i until it passes checks against the fixed outside and previously inserted factors. Missing future checks are deleted. The source's uniform premise still supplies acceptance at least a. Every final pair check was tested when its second endpoint was inserted, so the configuration is valid. This procedure has expected at most n/a base-factor trials; it need not have law ν_out.

At each subsequent update, choose i uniformly from {1,…,n}, keep other factors and the outside fixed, and draw from F_i until all incident checks pass. Conditional acceptance is at least a, so this update costs at most 1/a base-factor trials in expectation and has exactly the ν_out conditional law. It preserves valid configurations. Conditional disintegration directly proves invariance of (H1). Positive Z_out follows from the same sequential insertion bound Z_out≥a^n; the algorithm never evaluates Z_out.

The source's a^n lower bound only licenses a potentially exponential whole-product rejection bound. The present algorithm uses n local acceptance bounds and contracts successive conditional updates; it does not infer that whole-route acceptance is bounded below by a constant.

An equally informed exact baseline draws the complete product and rejects unless J=1. Its expected trial count is 1/Z_out≤a^{−n}; this is only a conservative **upper** bound, not a proved exponential lower bound on that baseline. The present theorem supplies a uniform polynomial service-count guarantee for an approximate conditional draw; it makes no measured speedup claim and does not compare against an uncomputed optimal sampler. Sequential insertion alone is an inexpensive way to obtain a legal state, but generally has the wrong normalized law and is not an exact sampling baseline.

## 4. Mixing proof, including continuous state spaces

Couple the chain X with a stationary chain Y using the same uniformly selected coordinate and maximal coupling of their two one-coordinate conditional distributions. The factor spaces are standard Borel continuous-path spaces, so measurable couplings exist. They are used only in the proof; the algorithm itself uses ordinary local rejection.

If X,Y differ at several coordinates, change them one at a time in the conditional kernel. The intermediate assignments remain individually permitted, although they can be globally forbidden. This is exactly where the uniform quantifier in (H2) is needed. The triangle inequality bounds the disagreement probability after updating i by

    ∑_j c_ij 1{X_j≠Y_j}.

Let p_i(s)=Pr(X_i(s)≠Y_i(s)). Then

    p_i(s+1) ≤ (1−1/n)p_i(s) + (1/n)∑_j c_ij p_j(s).

Since max_i p_i(0)≤1, induction using the row sum (H4) gives

    max_i p_i(s) ≤ [1−(1−c)/n]^s,
    TV(Law(X_s),ν_out) ≤ n exp[−(1−c)s/n].                  (H5)

No column-sum or finite-state assumption is necessary for this maximum-disagreement argument. Thus

    s = ceil{n/(1−c) · log(n/ε)}                            (H6)

updates suffice for 0<ε<1. This is the usual Dobrushin/random-scan coupling mechanism, supplied here with a complete proof rather than a novelty claim.

## 5. Costs and retained boundaries

Including sequential initialization, expected base-factor trials are at most

    (n+s)/a ≤ (4/3)(n+ceil{2n log(n/ε)})                    (H7)

when a≥3/4,c≤1/2. Each trial tests at most C_col M incident collision exclusions, in addition to the charged internal construction of F_i. With uniform service bounds its expected cost is at most

    (n+s)/a · [C_base + C_col M C_test] + O(n+s).            (H8)

Storage includes the supplied exposed data and outside paths, plus n factor values and the local neighbor lists. The table-building, continuous-path representation, random bits, precision and input-reading costs are part of the services and cannot be suppressed by (H8). No finite arithmetic claim is made merely from a measurable kernel.

For a simple route on the K^3-site torus, n≤K^3. Thus the number of update calls is at most O(K^3 log(K^3/ε)) at fixed admitted t,M,D. This is a bound on declared service calls, not a polynomial bit-runtime claim for all paths or a uniform efficient acquisition of the continuous input.

If the exact outside marginal is supplied and (H5) holds uniformly for every permitted outside configuration, integrating the conditional approximate sampler preserves that outside marginal **exactly** and changes the joint law by at most ε in TV. This does not automatically preserve the original likelihood L2 estimate; that estimate belongs to the exact target law. In particular, the source's Gibbs common-measure/affinity theorem remains an analytic result with its exact law unless a separate robust comparison is proved.

The argument applies to the selected, accessible **two-copy route proposal**. It does not license a sampler for all unexposed coordinates under λ_I. Inaccessible labels need not have the source's lower individual bridge-success probability, and their own P-law could lack the uniform collision bound. Fixed inaccessible opponents are allowed; making them update coordinates is a different premise.

## 6. Consequence, prior status and next falsifier

The source's deterministic-opponent comparison is strong enough to remove a route-length global-rejection obstruction at the declared conditional sampling interface. This is a genuine constructive deduction beyond existence of a positive normalizer. It is still not an acquired physical initialization protocol: base bridge/target/check services and the outside equilibrium marginal remain load-bearing.

Nearest prior mechanism is classical Dobrushin uniqueness and heat-bath coupling; the paper's conditional factor representation supplies the specialized hypothesis. Priority of this particular corollary has not been established, and internal checks are not external validation.

Next falsifier: acquire a finite, costed base-factor/target-set/collision service and an exact or quantitatively controlled outside marginal for a concrete model. A failure of the uniform collision bound for a proposed moving factor, or a dependence of its target set on mutable opponents, invalidates (H2) and this sampler. The older passive product-bath initialization problem remains open.
