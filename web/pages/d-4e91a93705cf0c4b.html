# One-sided radial derivative discriminator: independent audit

7 October 2026. **PASS.** The parent's asymptotically linear convex-weight argument is valid in every dimension n≥1, for real bounded C¹ potentials and real or complex solutions. The outer-cutoff limit requires no extra weighted-gradient hypothesis. This is a reconstruction of an established positive-commutator mechanism, with a stronger precise stationary prior identified below. No novelty claim.

## Exact interface

Let H=−Δ+V on Rⁿ, where V∈C¹(Rⁿ;R)∩L∞, n≥1. Suppose, eventually,

    ∂rV(x) ≤ C r^(−1−β),   r=|x|,   β>0.

Let Hu=0 distributionally and e^(Ar)u∈L² for every A>0. Then u=0. These hypotheses imply u∈H² globally: u∈L², Δu=Vu∈L², and the Fourier characterization gives H². Only exterior C¹ regularity is needed for the commutator; boundedness and usual local unique continuation suffice in the interior. A real constant energy can be absorbed into V without changing its radial derivative.

The one-sided upper bound controls the positive part of ∂rV. The negative part and angular derivatives need no global bound beyond local regularity. The conclusion does not follow merely from DᵐV→0 for every fixed m.

## Weight and signs reconstructed directly

Set γ=min(β,1/2), f(r)=r−r^(1−γ), φ=τf. Choose an exterior radius R≥1 so that f'∈[1/2,1]. Then

    f'=1−(1−γ)r^(−γ),   f''=γ(1−γ)r^(−1−γ).

Hess f has radial eigenvalue f'' and tangential eigenvalue f'/r. Both are positive; in n=1 only the radial eigenvalue is present, on each exterior component. The radial formula Δrᵖ=p(p+n−2)r^(p−2) gives

    Δf=(n−1)/r +(1−γ)(γ−n+1)r^(−1−γ),
    Δ²f=−(n−1)(n−3)r^(−3)
      +(1−γ)(γ−n+1)(−1−γ)(n−3−γ)r^(−3−γ).

Consequently |Δ²f|≤C_(n,γ)r^(−3). For compactly supported exterior tests v,

    Pτ=e^φ H e^(−φ)=S+A,
    S=−Δ−|∇φ|²+V,       A=2∇φ·∇+Δφ.

S is symmetric and A is skew symmetric. Expanding the squared norm, with the real part of the complex pairing understood, gives

    ||Pτv||²=||Sv||²+||Av||²+⟨[S,A]v,v⟩.

The direct differential commutator is

    [S,A]=−4∂i(φ_ij ∂j)−Δ²φ
           +4 Hess φ(∇φ,∇φ)−2∇φ·∇V.

Its quadratic form is therefore

    4∫ Hess φ(∇v,∇conj(v))
    +∫[4τ³ f''(f')²−τΔ²f−2τf'∂rV]|v|².

The gradient term is nonnegative. The bracket is at least

    [γ(1−γ)τ³−(C_(n,γ)+2C)τ] r^(−1−γ),

because β≥γ, r≥1 and r^(−3)≤r^(−1−γ). Negative radial derivatives improve this lower bound; using an absolute value of ∂rV would impose an unnecessary condition. Choosing τ≥τ₀ once gives

    cγ τ³ ∫ r^(−1−γ)e^(2τf)|w|²
       ≤ ∫ e^(2τf)|Hw|².                         (1)

Initially w is smooth and compactly supported in r>R. H² approximation extends (1) to compact H² tests there. No second derivative of V occurs. The constants are independent of the support of w.

## Weighted-gradient step, without assuming it

Put ρ=(1+r²)^(1/2), so |∇ρ|≤1 and ρ−r is bounded. With a smooth outer cutoff χT supported in r≤2T, equal to one in r≤T, test Hu=0 against χT²e^(2Aρ)conj(u). Integration by parts and Young's inequality give

    ∫χT²e^(2Aρ)|∇u|²
      ≤ C(A²+||V||∞+1)∫e^(2Aρ)|u|²,

uniformly for T≥1. The cutoff gradient is O(1/T), hence bounded uniformly. The right side is finite by the assumed weighted-u condition. Fatou as T→∞ proves e^(Ar)∇u∈L² for each fixed A. This also follows from unit-ball Caccioppoli, but the test above makes the required integrability explicit.

## Removing cutoffs, with the correct order of limits

Choose R<R₁<R₂ and a fixed inner cutoff η that is zero for r≤R₁ and one for r≥R₂. Choose ζT equal to one for r≤T and zero for r≥2T, with first and second derivatives O(T⁻¹) and O(T⁻²). For T>2R₂ the derivative supports of η and ζT are disjoint. Apply (1) to wT=ηζT u. Since Hu=0,

    HwT=−2∇(ηζT)·∇u−Δ(ηζT)u.

The inner contribution is bounded by

    C e^(2τf(R₂)) B,   B=∫_(R₁<r<R₂)(|u|²+|∇u|²)<∞.

For each fixed τ the outer contribution is at most a constant times

    T⁻²∫_(T<r<2T)e^(2τr)|∇u|²
       +T⁻⁴∫_(T<r<2T)e^(2τr)|u|²,

which tends to zero. Here f≤r and the weighted-gradient conclusion is sufficient. First send T→∞ at fixed τ. The resulting left side is controlled by Fatou, or dominated convergence. For any R₃>R₂, monotonicity of f then yields

    cγ τ³ e^(2τf(R₃))∫_(r≥R₃)r^(−1−γ)|u|²
      ≤ C e^(2τf(R₂))B.

Now send τ→∞. The strict positive gap f(R₃)−f(R₂) forces the weighted tail mass to be zero. A uniform positive lower bound for r^(−1−γ) on an unbounded tail is neither available nor needed. Thus u vanishes on an exterior open set. Standard local unique continuation for bounded potentials, or ODE uniqueness in n=1, gives u=0 globally. Complex solutions are handled by the same Hermitian calculation or their real and imaginary parts.

## Established prior and stronger boundary

[Escauriaza–Kenig–Ponce–Vega, arXiv:1010.1906v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1906v1), submitted 10 October 2010, Theorem 5, equations (1.27)–(1.31), addresses the stationary equation Δw+Ṽw=ζw. The bounded total potential splits into a part decaying like ⟨x⟩⁻¹ᐟ² and a real exterior part whose negative radial derivative is at most c₂/r. A sufficiently large fixed exponential L² weight forces w=0. Theorem 6 and Section 6 provide the linear-weight Carleman proof.

For our equation take ζ=0 and Ṽ=−V. Let χ be smooth, zero on a large ball and one outside a larger ball. Put Ṽ₂=−χV and Ṽ₁=−(1−χ)V. The compact remainder satisfies the prescribed amplitude bound. On the cutoff annulus, |χ'V| is finite and can be bounded by c₂/r; outside it,

    −min(∂rṼ₂,0)=(∂rV)₊.

Thus the established theorem already covers the weaker eventual condition (∂rV)₊≤C/r, namely β=0, and proves more than exclusion of all-exponential decay. The β>0 proof above is complete independently of that attribution. The literal r−r^(1−γ) argument cannot be set to γ=0; importing the prior endpoint requires its additional linear-weight argument.

[Escauriaza–Fanelli–Vega, arXiv:1105.3230v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3230v1), submitted 16 May 2011, Remark 1.3, equation (1.12), and Appendix A are also close repulsive-potential prior. The arXiv HTML's displayed August 2026 date is a conversion artifact, not the source's submission date.

## Independent check of the prior's β=0 Carleman mechanism

The stronger endpoint is also reconstructible without trusting the source's compressed constant estimates. Assume only ∂rV≤C/r for r≥R≥1 and use φ=τr. Write S=−Δ+V−τ², A=τ(2∂r+(n−1)/r), and set

    X=||(S+A)v||₂, F=||r^(−1/2)v||₂,
    D=||r^(−1/2)∇v||₂,
    Dtan=||r^(−1/2)(I−er⊗er)∇v||₂.

The directly reconstructed commutator gives, for τ≥1,

    L:=||Sv||₂²+||Av||₂²+4τDtan² ≤ X²+C_R τF².      (2)

The potential term is −2τ∂rV≥−2Cτ/r, and |Δ²r|≤C_R/r, so both errors enter the last term. From the exact expression for A,

    ||r^(−1/2)∂rv||₂
      ≤ ||Av||₂/(2τ√R)+(n−1)F/(2R).

Consequently (2) implies

    ||Sv||₂≤X+C_R√τ F,
    D²≤C_R τ^(−1)X²+C_R F².                         (3)

Weighted integration by parts in S yields

    τ²F²=D²−(1/2)∫Δ(r⁻¹)|v|²
               +∫Vr⁻¹|v|²−Re∫r⁻¹Sv conj(v)
      ≤D²+C_R F²+R^(−1/2)||Sv||₂F.

Here Δ(r⁻¹)=(3−n)r⁻³, so its absolute contribution is ≤C_R F²; bounded V handles the other term. Substitute (3). Young's inequality bounds C_R XF by ετ²F²+C_(R,ε)τ⁻²X². For sufficiently large τ, the remaining C_R(1+√τ)F² is absorbed by the left side. Thus

    τ³||r^(−1/2)v||₂²≤C_R||(S+A)v||₂².              (4)

This is the source's linear-weight coercivity up to an inessential constant. The cutoff argument already given, now with f=r and weight r⁻¹, proves all-exponential uniqueness under β=0 too. The additional sufficiently-large-single-weight conclusion belongs to the cited theorem and its annular lower-bound argument; it is not claimed to follow by sending τ→∞ when only one fixed exponential weight is known. Equation (4) works in n=1 as well: Dtan=0 and the radial norm is controlled by A.

## Downstream consequence and falsifiable next step

This closes the proposed one-sided relative first-jet target at these strengths through an established theorem. For any nonzero all-exponentially decaying solution with a bounded real C¹ potential, the contrapositive of the established endpoint says

    sup_(r≥R) r(∂rV)₊ = ∞   for every R.

In particular the arbitrary-loss construction's absolute jet decay cannot imply a relative O(1/r) upper bound. A useful next diagnostic is to locate and quantify its positive radial derivative spikes. No necessity claim beyond this logical contrapositive, sharp classification of weaker hypotheses, or new Landis theorem is asserted.
