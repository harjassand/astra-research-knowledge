2. A rigidity theorem: local profile storage cannot exceed polyconvex support
Theorem 1 — First-derivative local-storage equivalence
Let \(F:\mathbb R^{2\times2}\to\mathbb R\) be \(C^2\), and fix an affine background \(B\). The following are equivalent.
There exists a density \(p\in C^3(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2)\) such that
\[
\mathcal P_B(U)=\mathbb E_y\,p(U,U_y)
\]
satisfies (2) for every smooth periodic \(U,V\).
There exists a scalar \(\kappa\) such that
\[
\boxed{
F(B+X)-F(B)-DF(B):X
\ge \kappa\det X
\qquad\text{for every }X.
}
\tag{3}
\]
When (3) holds, an explicit storage is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_B(U)
=
F_1(B)\cdot\mathbb E_y U
+
\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathbb E_y\det(U,U_y),
}
\tag{4}
\]
where \(F_1(B)\) is the derivative with respect to the first matrix column.
This theorem does not assume rotational invariance, homogeneity or even rank-one convexity. The proposed domination property itself forces the conclusion.
Proof: high-frequency velocities eliminate nonlinear derivative dependence
Write \(z=U\), \(q=U_y\). Integration by parts gives
\[
D\mathcal P_B(U)[V]
=
\mathbb E_y
\left[p_z-p_{qz}U_y-p_{qq}U_{yy}\right]\cdot V.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose
\[
U_N(y)=z_0+\frac1Nw(Ny),
\qquad
V_N(y)=v(Ny),
\]
with arbitrary smooth periodic \(w,v\).
The action \(L_B(U_N,V_N)\) remains bounded: it depends on \(w'\) and \(v\), not on \(U_{N,yy}=Nw''(Ny)\). But the proposed storage derivative contains
\[
-N\,\mathbb E_y
\left[p_{qq}(z_0,w')w''\right]\cdot v+O(1).
\]
Applying domination to both \(V_N\) and \(-V_N\) forces
\[
p_{qq}(z_0,w')w''=0.
\]
Periodic \(w\) can have arbitrary prescribed first and second derivatives at a point. Consequently,
\[
p_{qq}=0.
\]
Thus
\[
p(z,q)=A(z)\cdot q+c(z),
\]
and (5) reduces to
\[
D\mathcal P_B(U)[V]
=
\mathbb E_y\left[
\nabla c(U)\cdot V+k(U)\det(V,U_y)
\right],
\]
where
\[
k=\partial_1A_2-\partial_2A_1.
\]
Constant profiles and small constant velocities of both signs force
\[
\nabla c(z)=F_1(B).
\]
Repeat the high-frequency argument around a fixed \(z_0\). It gives an averaged inequality with the constant coefficient \(k(z_0)\). To localize it, take
\[
(V,U_y)=(v_0,q_0)
\]
on a fraction \(\theta\) of a period, and
\[
(V,U_y)=\left(0,-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}q_0\right)
\]
on the remainder. The derivative has zero mean. Divide by \(\theta\) and let \(\theta\downarrow0\). The compensating part contributes the missing term \(F_2(B)\cdot q_0\), giving exactly (3).
Conversely, differentiating (4) and integrating (3) proves domination.
Periodic explicit dependence \(p(y,U,U_y)\) does not enlarge the existence class: averaging a proposed functional over transverse translations preserves domination and produces an autonomous density.
Why this blocks the proposed continuum extension
If such a local storage exists at every \(B\), choose a supporting coefficient \(\kappa_B\). Then
\[
\boxed{

\sup_B
