# Explicit pure-product counterexample to thermal determinant dominance

10 October 2026. Root analytic construction with exact rational sign certificate. This is NOT a counterexample to the amplifier von Neumann entropy bound. No priority claim or external certification is made.

## Statement

Let U be the gain-two two-mode squeezing unitary, with the real sign convention in DERIVATION.md. Let t=1000 and epsilon=1/(100 t^3)=10^(-11). Use both inputs

f_epsilon=(|0>+epsilon |3>)/sqrt(1+epsilon^2).

Let rho_epsilon be either reduced output and tau the gain-two vacuum-output thermal state. Then

det(I+t rho_epsilon) < det(I+t tau).

In particular tau does NOT majorize every output from two arbitrary pure independent inputs: such majorization would imply the opposite log-determinant inequality because log(1+t x) is concave and vanishes at zero. The established Gaussian-channel theorem with vacuum idler is unaffected; the idler here is non-Gaussian.

## Second variation and exact sign

Put d_n=2^(-n-1), c=1/8, s=sqrt(2)/4. Let C_n=binomial(n+3,3), and
P_n=sum_{j=0}^{min(3,n)}(-1)^(3-j)binomial(3,j)binomial(n,j).

The coefficient H of epsilon^2 in log det(I+t rho_epsilon)-log det(I+t tau) is A+s B, where

u_n=t d_n/(1+t d_n),
v_n=t^2 c C_n d_n^2/[(1+t d_n)(1+t c d_n)],
A=sum_n {u_n[binomial(n,3)+c C_n-2]-(1+c)v_n},
B=sum_n {2u_n P_n-2v_n}.

Here binomial(n,3)=0 for n<3. The linear term is zero because the first reduced perturbation has no diagonal entries.

The script certify_negative_determinant_hessian.py uses exact fractions for n=0,...,99 and a rational bracket for sqrt(2) obtained by integer square root. For N=100, the absolute omitted tail is bounded by

(5t/2)(N+3)^3 2^(-N)/[1-(1/2)((N+4)/(N+3))^3]
+ t^2(N+3)^3 4^(-N)/[1-(1/4)((N+4)/(N+3))^3].

This is less than 4.45e-21. The bounds follow from |P_n|<=(n+1)^3, binomial bounds, denominators at least one, and decreasing ratios of the polynomial-geometric summands. All interval comparisons are rational. The resulting upper endpoint is strictly below -2 (numerical display H=-3.168998518588788). The display is not used for the proof.

## Explicit remainder bound

Write u0=U|00>, u1=U(|30>+|03>), u2=U|33>. Their norms are 1,sqrt(2),1 and they are orthogonal. The global vector is

(u0+epsilon u1+epsilon^2 u2)/(1+epsilon^2).

After partial trace, write
rho_epsilon=rho0+epsilon X+epsilon^2 Y+R_epsilon.

Rank-one trace-norm bounds and trace-norm contraction of partial trace give ||X||_1<3 and ||Y||_1<=6. Expanding (1+epsilon^2)^(-2) exactly gives ||R_epsilon||_1<=21|epsilon|^3 for |epsilon|<=1. One way to check this is to use |(1+x)^(-2)-1+2x|<=3x^2 and |(1+x)^(-2)-1|<=2x, for 0<=x<=1, together with the cubic numerator norm <3 and quartic numerator norm 1. For |epsilon|<=1/10, therefore ||rho_epsilon-rho0||_1<=4|epsilon|.

Set R0=(I+t rho0)^(-1) and B_epsilon=t R0^(1/2)(rho_epsilon-rho0)R0^(1/2). Its trace norm is at most 4t|epsilon|. Fredholm determinant multiplicativity gives the log-determinant difference Tr log(I+B_epsilon). For |epsilon|<=1/(8t), ||B_epsilon||<=1/2. The terms of order at least three in the logarithm have absolute trace bounded by

||B_epsilon||_1^3/[3(1-||B_epsilon||)] <= (128/3)t^3 |epsilon|^3.

Replacing B_epsilon by its linear and quadratic terms costs at most 21t|epsilon|^3 in the linear trace and 32t^2|epsilon|^3 in half the quadratic trace. For the latter, use ||epsilon^2 t R0^(1/2)Y R0^(1/2)+t R0^(1/2)R_epsilon R0^(1/2)||_1<=9t epsilon^2 for |epsilon|<=1/10, then expand the square.

At t=1000 the full remainder is strictly less than 44t^3|epsilon|^3. Consequently at epsilon=1/(100t^3),

log det(I+t rho_epsilon)-log det(I+t tau)
< -2 epsilon^2 + 44t^3 epsilon^3
= -(39/25) epsilon^2 <0.

All smallness conditions hold. Thus the witness is explicit, finite-energy and pure-product, without relying on numerical cancellation at epsilon=10^(-11).
