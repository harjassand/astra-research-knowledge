# Cycle 3: state-dependent jump inference beyond iid compound-Poisson marks

**Finding.** A four-channel mass-action moment circuit gives two finite-volume reaction networks with exactly equal drift and CLE covariance at every state, yet unequal state-dependent jump kernels and unequal third jump cumulants. A projection can erase the difference completely: if only the consumed source species is observed, its entire timestamped CTMC law is identical under the two networks, even with zero measurement noise and an arbitrarily long record. I derived a full-path likelihood and a finite-window KL/Fisher bound for the complementary, product-resolved observation. The construction is internally proved and finite-checked; it is not a priority claim or an identified chemical system.

## Prior-art boundary

The likely “Duval et al. (2014)” reference is Comte, Duval, and Genon-Catalot, *Nonparametric density estimation in compound Poisson process using convolution power estimators*, Metrika 77 (2014), 163–183 ([author-hosted full text](https://helios2.mi.parisdescartes.fr/~comte/GenonContribMetrika.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00184-013-0475-3)). Its model is \(X_t=\sum_{i=1}^{N_t}\xi_i\), where \(N_t\) is homogeneous Poisson with unknown constant rate \(c\), and the marks \(\xi_i\) are iid, independent of \(N\), with unknown density \(f\). It exploits independent stationary increments and estimates \(f\) from discretely observed increments; for a sample stopped at a fixed number of nonzero increments, those increments are iid from the corresponding conditional compound-Poisson density. The paper gives an adaptive estimator with minimax \(L^2\) risk over Sobolev balls. This is strong prior art for unknown iid jump-law inference, but its iid-mark/Lévy interface has neither a reaction-network state nor a state-dependent intensity/mark kernel.

There is also a distinct solo Duval article, *When is it no longer possible to estimate a compound Poisson process?*, Electronic Journal of Statistics 8 (2014), 274–301 ([DOI and journal record](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-EJS885), [author publication list](https://sites.google.com/site/celinehduval/research/publications)). Its abstract studies centered finite-variance compound-Poisson data at increasingly coarse discrete sampling, with Fisher-information loss and Gaussian indistinguishability. That is relevant to loss of higher-order jump information under observation, but it is not a result about state-dependent mass-action marks. I verified its scope from the abstract/metadata, not a full proof read; I do not use its exact sampling threshold here.

The strongest direct reaction-network control is Enciso, Erban, and Kim, *Identifiability of Stochastically Modelled Reaction Networks*, arXiv:2006.02272v2 (2020), later *European Journal of Applied Mathematics* 32 (2021), 865–887 ([primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02272v2), [journal DOI 10.1017/S0956792520000492](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792520000492)). Theorem 3.1 assumes a finite set of transition vectors \(\mathcal Z\) and exact transition-rate values \(\lambda^*_z(x)\) on \(S_N=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:\|x\|_1\le N\}\), where \(|S_N|=\binom{N+d}{d}\). In lexicographic order, it recursively computes falling-factorial coefficients
\[
c_z^i=\frac{\lambda_z^*(x^i)-\lambda_z^{i-1}(x^i)}{(x^i)_{(x^i)}},\qquad
\lambda_z^i(x)=\lambda_z^{i-1}(x)+c_z^i x_{(x^i)}.
\]
When all \(c_z^i\ge0\), the theorem constructs the unique mass-action reaction system of order at most \(N\) reproducing those transition-rate maps on \(S_N\) (for directions positive somewhere in that set). Lemma 3.1 gives uniqueness when two order-bounded systems agree on \(S_N\); Theorem 3.3 gives identifiability when the CTMC state space contains \(S_N\). Their acquisition interface is exact transition vectors and event times, with enough state coverage; the paper explicitly says in §6 that the method is not directly applicable to partial observations at discrete times. This is a decisive control boundary: our pair is distinguishable by exact product-resolved transition vectors/rate maps and is not an alternative inference method. The exact zero-information result below applies only after projecting away the product mark and observing X alone. The moment circuit is elementary and the priority of this particular sensor-specific construction is **UNKNOWN**.

The construction also sits beside, rather than beyond, established chemical-reaction-network inference. Zhang et al. maximize the exact fully observed CTMC trajectory likelihood over propensity functions ([SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 2020, DOI 10.1137/19M1265880](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/19M1265880)). Rathinam and Yu give particle filtering and parameter inference from exact partial-state observations ([arXiv:2010.04346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04346)); later guided-simulation work treats conditioning on discrete partial observations, including Gaussian-noisy readouts ([Corstanje et al., DOI 10.1007/s11203-025-09326-9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11203-025-09326-9)). Thus exact path likelihood, filtering, and particle methods are prior art. After the EEK control, the narrow remaining object is not a new reaction-network inference method: it is an exact instance showing that a particular observation map can erase a jump-law contrast even where the complete CTMC maps differ, plus finite-fixture quantification of event-order versus endpoint information. Historical novelty of this observation-specific witness remains **UNKNOWN**.

## Exact multi-source mass-action construction

Use two species, a finite source \(X\) and a measured product \(Y\). For each \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), include two reaction channels

\[
R_{1j}: X\longrightarrow jY,
\qquad
R_{2j}: 2X\longrightarrow X+jY,
\]

with the same net jump \(\nu_j=(-1,j)^\top\) but distinct first- and second-order source propensities. At system-size parameter \(\Omega\), the exact copy-number propensities are

\[
a_{1j}(x)=\alpha_jx,
\qquad
a_{2j}(x)=\beta_j\frac{x(x-1)}{2\Omega}.
\]

The second family makes the conditional jump-size distribution depend on \(x\):

\[
\rho_j(x)=x\left(\alpha_j+\beta_j\frac{x-1}{2\Omega}\right),
\qquad
\Pr(J=j\mid X=x,\text{an event})=
\frac{\alpha_j+\beta_j(x-1)/(2\Omega)}
{\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell+(x-1)\sum_\ell\beta_\ell/(2\Omega)}.
\]

Choose the signed circuit \(w=(-1,3,-3,1)\). Directly,

\[
\sum_jw_j=0,\qquad \sum_jjw_j=0,\qquad
\sum_jj^2w_j=0,\qquad \sum_jj^3w_j=6.
\]

Compare hypotheses \(H_0:(\alpha,\beta)\) and \(H_1:(\alpha+\epsilon w,\beta)\), with \(\epsilon\ne0\) small enough that all rates stay positive. Only first-order channels change. Since the source count rate difference is \(\epsilon xw_j\),

\[
\Delta b=\epsilon x\sum_jw_j\nu_j=0,
\qquad
\Delta C=\epsilon x\sum_jw_j\nu_j\nu_j^\top=0
\]

for every integer \(x\ge0\) and every \(\Omega\). This is stronger than matching only the thermodynamic-limit CLE: the exact finite-volume generator has the same first and second infinitesimal jump moments pointwise. The third jump-cumulant tensor differs; its \(YYY\) entry is \(6\epsilon x\) in copy-number units, or \(6\epsilon z_X/\Omega^2\) for concentration \(z_X=x/\Omega\). Thus the two CTMC generators and path laws differ, despite their exactly identical CLE drift and covariance.

For a small rational fixture, use \(\Omega=5, X_0=5\),

\[
\alpha_0=(0.10,0.10,0.10,0.10)\;\mathrm{s}^{-1},\quad
\epsilon=0.01\;\mathrm{s}^{-1},\quad
\alpha_1=(0.09,0.13,0.07,0.11)\;\mathrm{s}^{-1},
\]

and \(\beta=(0.10,0.20,0.30,0.40)\;\mathrm{concentration}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). The mark law is state-dependent in both models. At \(x=1\), it is \((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\) versus \((9,13,7,11)/40\). At \(x=5\), it is \((7,9,11,13)/40\) versus \((13,21,19,27)/80\). Events consume one \(X\), so a complete batch has exactly five events. The ordered marks are independent conditional on the deterministic sequence of source counts, but non-identically distributed because their conditional law changes as \(X\) is depleted.

No real molecule or transition state is asserted. The parameters are a reproducible synthetic fixture. If \(\alpha_0=0.10\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) were a measured elementary unimolecular rate, its Eyring-equivalent value at \(298.15\,\mathrm K\) with transmission coefficient \(\kappa=1\) would be \(\Delta G^\ddagger=RT\log(k_BT/(h\kappa\alpha_0))=78.73\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol}^{-1}\). The alternative rates would map to 78.99, 78.08, 79.62, and 78.50 kJ/mol under the same assumption. These are conversions of invented rates, not activation barriers, calculated chemistry, or empirical predictions; a measured rate and prefactor/transmission coefficient are prerequisites for a physical barrier claim.

## Projection no-go and exact likelihood

Let \(A_\alpha=\sum_j\alpha_j\), \(B_\beta=\sum_j\beta_j\), and \(c_x=(x-1)/(2\Omega)\). Every reaction reduces \(X\) by one, and its total hazard is

\[
q(x)=\sum_j\rho_j(x)=x(A_\alpha+B_\beta c_x).
\]

Because \(\sum_jw_j=0\), \(q(x)\) is identical under \(H_0,H_1\) at every \(x\). The projected \(X\)-only generator is exactly

\[
(G_Xf)(x)=q(x)[f(x-1)-f(x)].
\]

Therefore the full timestamped \(X\) path laws coincide. Any sensor that observes only \(X\), including arbitrarily frequent exact measurements or any subsequent additive Gaussian/noisy channel, has total-variation distance \(0\) under \(H_0,H_1\) for every finite window and at infinite observation time. Its directional Fisher information for \(\epsilon\) is exactly zero. With unknown reaction rates, the \(X\)-only likelihood depends on \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) only through \(A_\alpha,B_\beta\); every three-dimensional perturbation of either four-vector whose entries sum to zero is unidentifiable from this projection. This is exact observation lumpability, not a small-noise or large-volume approximation.

If the event-resolved \(X,Y\) path is observed, \(\Delta Y=j\) reveals the jump size but not which source-order channel fired. The visible channel intensity is \(\rho_j(x)\), and the path log-likelihood, up to parameter-independent terms, is

\[
\ell(\alpha,\beta)=
\sum_{t_k\le T}\log\rho_{j_k}(X_{t_k-})
-\int_0^Tq(X_t)\,dt.
\]

For the matched pair, \(q\) cancels in the likelihood ratio:

\[
\log\frac{dP_\epsilon}{dP_0}
=\sum_{t_k\le T}\log\frac{\alpha_{j_k}+\epsilon w_{j_k}+\beta_{j_k}c_{X_{t_k-}}}
{\alpha_{j_k}+\beta_{j_k}c_{X_{t_k-}}}.
\]

Define \(p_j^h(x)=\rho_j^h(x)/q(x)\) and \(d_x=D_{\rm KL}(p^1(x)\|p^0(x))\). A finite-window event record satisfies the exact identity

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_1^{\rm path}\|P_0^{\rm path})
=\mathbb E_1\sum_{t_k\le T}d_{X_{t_k-}}.
\]

For a completed batch from \(X_0=n_0\), each \(x=n_0,n_0-1,\ldots,1\) is visited once, so the KL is exactly \(\sum_{x=1}^{n_0}d_x\). For any partial/noisy observation obtained by a Markov observation channel \(K\), data processing and Pinsker give

\[
\|P_1^Y-P_0^Y\|_{\rm TV}
\le \|P_1^{\rm path}-P_0^{\rm path}\|_{\rm TV}
\le \sqrt{\tfrac12\mathbb E_1\sum_{t_k\le T}d_{X_{t_k-}}}.
\]

For example, \(Y_m=H(X_{t_m},Y_{t_m})+\xi_m\), \(\xi_m\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\), is such a channel. Its likelihood is the latent-path integral \(p_\theta(y)=\int\prod_m\phi_\sigma(y_m-Hz_{t_m})P_\theta(dz_{[0,T]})\). The full-path Fisher information for \(\epsilon\), with \(\alpha,\beta\) fixed, is

\[
I^{\rm path}_{\epsilon\epsilon}(T)=
\mathbb E_\epsilon\int_0^T X_t\sum_j
\frac{w_j^2}{\alpha_j+\epsilon w_j+\beta_j(X_t-1)/(2\Omega)}\,dt.
\]

The observed Fisher information is no larger: the observed score is the conditional expectation of the full-data score, and conditional expectation contracts its squared norm. If rates are unknown nuisance parameters, the efficient information is the Schur-complement projection of this matrix and cannot exceed the known-rate information. More sharply, if all four \(\alpha_j\) are unrestricted nuisance rates, \(\epsilon\) is just the reparameterization \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha+\epsilon w\), so a separate network-label parameter is structurally unidentifiable even with product-resolved perfect data. A rate-pattern comparison only makes sense after specifying shared constraints or calibration for the nuisance rates.

As one explicit unknown-rate restriction, set \(\alpha_j=\kappa(1+\theta w_j)\), \(\beta_j=\eta(1,2,3,4)_j\), and treat \(\kappa,\eta\) as unknown shared scales. At \(X_0=\Omega=5,\kappa=\eta=0.1,\theta=0\), the complete-path Fisher matrix for \((\theta,\kappa,\eta)\), including the exponential holding-time scores, has efficient information \(I_{\theta\theta\cdot(\kappa,\eta)}\approx13.378\) for one batch. Any noisy or partial readout has at most this information, and the exact \(X\)-only sensor has zero. This number is an internal computation for the stated finite model, not a general design law.

## Finite fixture, rate and compute costs

For the numerical fixture above, \(q(x)=x[0.4+(x-1)/10]\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). The mean full-batch duration is \(\sum_{x=1}^5q(x)^{-1}=1175/252\approx4.663\,\mathrm{s}\), and the expected integrated source exposure is \(\mathbb E\int X_tdt=743/84\approx8.845\) molecule-seconds. Enumerating all \(4^5=1024\) ordered mark sequences with exact rational probabilities gives exact total-variation values and the following KL divergences (evaluated numerically from those probabilities):

- full ordered event-record \(D_{\rm KL}(H_1\|H_0)=0.0672421\) and exact \(\mathrm{TV}=0.148201\);
- final total-product-count \(Y_T\) has \(D_{\rm KL}=0.000232125\) and exact \(\mathrm{TV}=0.00524719\).

Thus endpoint compression discards most of the available information in this fixture. With *known* simple-hypothesis rate shapes, a test whose sum of type-I and type-II errors is at most 0.2 requires \(\mathrm{TV}\ge0.8\). Pinsker then makes \(N D_{\rm KL}\ge1.28\) necessary: at least 20 independent, fully event-resolved batches, versus at least 5,515 batches if only final integer \(Y_T\) is recorded. These are necessary bounds, not sufficient sample-size guarantees. Unknown rate nuisance parameters, measurement noise, censoring, yield-classification errors, and experimental setup costs increase the real requirement; unrestricted unknown \(\alpha\) vectors erase the hypothesis distinction altogether.

Simulation is cheap here and does not require rare-event acceleration. Direct Gillespie simulation uses at most five reaction events per batch and, with a naive eight-channel propensity scan, at most 40 propensity evaluations. Twenty full-path batches therefore cost at most 100 event updates / 800 propensity evaluations and about 93.3 seconds of sequential reaction time, excluding preparation/readout. The endpoint lower bound corresponds to 27,575 reaction events and about 7.14 hours of sequential reaction time, before product quantification. A dynamic program over reachable \((x,y)\) states has only 51 states at \(X_0=5\), with at most four outgoing mark updates per state; the script instead enumerates 1,024 paths to make the exact TV calculation transparent. At larger \(X_0\), state DP is \(O(X_0^2)\) states, whereas path enumeration is exponential. For noisy discrete snapshots, exact hidden-state likelihood/filtering or a particle method is needed and must charge particles, time discretization/bridge operations, and all propensity evaluations; no such inference code was run here.

## Comparator and falsifier

The equally informed stochastic comparator is the exact CTMC likelihood above, supplied the same initial counts, observation map/noise, and rate constraints as any approximate inference method. Run a matched-moment CLE/LNA fit as a control: it receives the same drift and covariance under both hypotheses, so it cannot legitimately identify which jump law generated the data. Run an iid-mark compound-Poisson fit as an additional misspecification control; it pools mark probabilities across source states, while the exact model predicts the two state-specific vectors listed above. For partial/noisy signals, compare exact finite-state filtering where feasible with a particle/guided-path approximation at the same number of trajectories and propensity calls, and include all latent-path simulation overhead. FFS/TPS is not a needed comparator for this finite five-event construction; it addresses rare-path generation/rate estimation under a different interface.

The finite falsifier is independent reproduction of the rational checks in [the audit script](cycle3_state_dependent_mark_check.py): the first/second moment matrices must coincide for all tested \(x,\Omega\), source-only path TV must be exactly zero, and the full/endpoint KL and TV values must agree. A chemical implementation is falsified if replicated, source-state-conditioned product increments fail the predicted mark likelihood after rate and sensor calibration, or if an \(X\)-only sensor displays a reproducible model difference despite both networks satisfying the stipulated propensities. The latter would point to an omitted reaction, feedback from \(Y\), model misspecification, or a non-Markov observation process.

No physical trajectory, computational chemistry calculation, assay, rare-event transition, or activation barrier was acquired. Rates above are internal synthetic parameters. The next decisive gate is a calibrated finite-volume simulator or a real network with at least four distinguishable product increments and competing source orders, then a preregistered experiment comparing exact event-resolved likelihood, endpoint-only likelihood, matched CLE, and iid-mark decompounding with the same observations and nuisance treatment.
