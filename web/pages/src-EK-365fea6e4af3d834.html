Investigator: Stage III Sol S1. Date: 12 October 2026. Source snapshots remain read-only: Astra `5fc72a144d77c31b400efc0768afd7833c702362`; OpenAI/math `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`. The strongest surviving result is a globally polynomial action predictor whose fibers are exactly the indistinguishability classes for the admitted real controls. It discards half of the root's complex closure, identifies precisely which information an added control can demand, and admits a spectral extension for damping/detuning. ## 1. Model and exact real closure

For `i=1,...,r`, consider complex amplitudes `x_i,y_i,c`, real controls `k=Kv`, and a supplied real `r×d` matrix `K`:

\[
i\dot x_i=k_i\bar y_i c,\qquad i\dot y_i=k_i\bar x_i c,
\qquad i\dot c=\sum_i k_i x_i y_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Write `a_i=|x_i|²`, `e_i=|y_i|²`, `n=|c|²`, `s_i=a_i+e_i`, `b_i=x_i y_i`, and define

\[
z_i=b_i\bar c,\quad p_i=\operatorname{Im}z_i,\quad
S_{ij}=\operatorname{Re}(b_i\bar b_j),\quad
R=SK,\quad P=K^Tp,\quad L=K^T(k\odot s).
\tag{2}
\]

The real state is

\[
\pi_K=(a,e,n,p,R)\in\mathbb R^{3r+1+rd}.
\]

**Theorem 1 (exact polynomial factorization).** For every admitted real control history, the image of (1) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot a_i=\dot e_i&=-2k_i p_i,&
\dot n&=2k^Tp,\\
\dot p_i&=(Rv)_i-k_i s_i n,&
\dot R_{i\alpha}&=-k_i s_i P_\alpha-L_\alpha p_i.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]

All expressions are polynomial in the state and linear in `v`. They remain defined when any amplitudes vanish, including `c=0`. With dense `K`, evaluation and storage cost `O(rd+r)`. The state image has algebraic constraints and is a proper subset of its ambient Euclidean space.

**Proof.** Direct differentiation gives

\[
\dot b_i=-ik_i s_i c,\qquad
\dot z_i=i\sum_jk_jb_i\bar b_j-ik_i s_i n.
\]

Taking imaginary parts gives the equation for `p`. If `B_α=Σ_j K_jα b_j` and `Q_iα=b_i bar B_α`, then

\[
\dot Q_{i\alpha}=-ik_i s_i\overline{\sum_jK_{j\alpha}z_j}
                  +iL_\alpha z_i.
\]

Its real part is `-k_i s_i P_α-L_α p_i`. The action signs follow from `2 Re(bar x_i dot x_i)=-2 k_i Im z_i`; the central sign is the opposite. No division was used. ∎

The root's complex `(z,Q)` closure is therefore correct but excessive for this action task. `Re z` and `Im Q` do not enter (3). The involution

\[
(x,y,c)\longmapsto(\bar x,\bar y,-\bar c)
\tag{4}
\]

preserves (1) under every real control, preserves actions, sends `z→-bar z` and `Q→bar Q`, and leaves `(p,R)` unchanged. Full phase-orbit separation is stronger than necessary here.

An equivalent real geometry is useful. Put `h=-ic`, and identify each complex number with a vector in `R²`. Then

\[
\dot b_i=k_i s_i h,\qquad \dot h=-\sum_i k_i b_i,
\quad p_i=-b_i\cdot h,\quad R_{i\alpha}=b_i\cdot B_\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]

The real subspace `span{h,B_1,...,B_d}⊂R²` is constant along a trajectory: every derivative of these reference vectors lies in their span. Each `b_i` component perpendicular to that subspace is constant. Thus the reduction deliberately forgets components that never influence the admitted action experiment. When both `h` and all `B_α` vanish, the state is an exact equilibrium for every admitted control, even if individual pairs are populated.

## 2. Global existence, realizability, and feedback

**Theorem 2 (global physical factorization).** Let `v∈L¹_loc([0,∞);R^d)`. For every finite initial amplitude vector, (1) has a unique global absolutely continuous solution. The unique solution of (3) initialized at its realizable image equals `π_K` of that solution for all time. In particular, its image remains realizable and cannot escape the physical constraint set.

**Proof.** The amplitude field is polynomial and satisfies the Carathéodory local uniqueness bounds on bounded sets, with integrable envelope proportional to `|v(t)|`. The positive definite quantity

\[
E=\sum_i(a_i+e_i)+2n
\tag{6}
\]

is conserved for all real controls. It bounds every amplitude and prevents finite-time escape. The polynomial reduced field has the same local existence and uniqueness property on bounded sets. The lifted amplitude solution supplies a global reduced solution; uniqueness identifies every reduced solution with it. ∎

This theorem does **not** assert that arbitrary, physically inconsistent ambient coordinates have global solutions. Polynomial vector fields alone do not ensure that. For example, the formal `r=d=1,K=v=1` reduced system has the exact solution

\[
a=e=-(1-t)^{-2},\quad n=(1-t)^{-2},\quad
p=(1-t)^{-3},\quad R=(1-t)^{-4},
\]

which blows up at `t=1` and is unphysical because two actions are negative. Physical constraint preservation is proved by the lift, rather than inferred from arbitrary numerical initial data.

For feedback `v=φ(t,π_K)`, assume the usual Carathéodory bounds: on each compact state set, both its value bound and its state-Lipschitz constant have an integrable time envelope. The amplitude closed-loop field then has the corresponding local existence and uniqueness bounds, (6) still bounds it, and the same proof gives a unique global closed-loop factorization. Autonomous locally Lipschitz feedback satisfies this on every energy shell. Controller internal states can be appended if their own evolution is well posed.

Continuity alone is insufficient. For `r=d=1,K=1`, initialize `x=y=1,c=0` and use `v=√max(p,0)`. Under unit control the ordinary solution has `p(τ)=τ+O(τ³)` near `τ=0`. Reparameterize it by `dτ/dt=√p(τ)`. Besides the stationary solution, nontrivial solutions can depart after any waiting time, because `∫₀^τ du/√p(u)` is finite. The feedback is continuous but not Lipschitz; uniqueness fails. A bang-bang/sign prescription likewise requires an explicit solution concept and cannot inherit the preceding theorem automatically.

## 3. Exact behavioral quotient and dimension lower bound

Discard any row `i` with `K_i=0` from `(p,R)`; its two actions are constant and its phase cannot affect the remaining star. Below, assume every row is nonzero. Controls may be arbitrary piecewise constant `v` in a set containing a nonempty open subset of `R^d`. Defining the admitted class merely by a linear span without enough admissible control values would not justify the following necessity argument.

Call two initial amplitude states behaviorally equivalent when **all modal action trajectories** agree for **every common admitted prescribed control history**, starting from those states.

**Theorem 3 (minimal information, including singular strata).** Two states are behaviorally equivalent if and only if their `π_K` values coincide.

**Proof.** Equality of `π_K` is sufficient by (3) and Theorem 2. Conversely, equality of output histories includes equality of initial actions. For a constant `v`, equality of first action derivatives implies

\[
(K_i\cdot v)\,\Delta p_i=0.
\]

Since the row is nonzero and the control set has interior, `Δp_i=0`. Equality of second derivatives, using equal actions and `p`, then implies

\[
(K_i\cdot v)\,(\Delta R_i\cdot v)=0
\]

for every `v` in that open set. This is a polynomial identity, so either its first linear factor is zero or the second is zero. The first is nonzero by assumption; hence `ΔR_i=0`. The proof uses no nonvanishing amplitude hypothesis. ∎

This is a statement about exact information/fibers, rather than minimal ambient coordinate count. The displayed state is redundant.

**Corollary 3.1 (continuous state-size floor).** Any continuous exact predictor of this full action behavior, taking values in `R^m`, has `m≥3r+1` on a generic unrestricted initial-state set. If the `r` pair differences `a_i-e_i` and total energy (6) are known fixed parameters, the corresponding generic floor is `m≥2r`.

**Proof.** On a local slice fixing the global phase of a nonzero `c`, the initial actions contribute `2r+1` independent real parameters. The `r` pair-product relative phases contribute `r` more. Away from zero cosine/sine derivatives, `(actions,p)` already has full local rank `3r+1`: each `p_i=|b_i||c| sin θ_i` locally determines its own phase. Nonzero rows make these parameters behaviorally distinguishable by first action derivatives. A continuous predictive state must be injective on this local slice; invariance of dimension forbids its embedding into a lower-dimensional Euclidean space. Fixing the `r+1` independent constants removes `r+1` dimensions. ∎

The original amplitude vector has `4r+2` real coordinates. Its phase symmetry has generic dimension `r+1`, leaving the same `3r+1` generic quotient dimension; reflection (4) changes no dimension. For `d=1`, (3) stores `4r+1` real numbers, almost exactly the original amplitude count. It gains regular invariant information structure, not a large memory reduction.

On an open set where `h` and some `B=Σ_i κ_i b_i` are linearly independent, one can reconstruct the full real Gram matrix rationally from their projections. In the `d=1,K=κ` case set `T=κ^TR`, `P=κ^Tp` and

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}n&-P\\-P&T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S_{ij}=(-p_i,R_i)M^{-1}(-p_j,R_j)^T.
\tag{7}
\]

Its denominator `nT-P²` is the squared area of the two reference vectors. It fails at the collinear/zero-reference strata where (3) stays regular. For fixed `κ`, `nT-P²=(Re Σ_i κ_i z_i)²` is conserved; this is the square of the familiar cubic Hamiltonian, consistent with the unobservability of its sign under (4).

## 4. What a newly admitted real control actually requires

It is false that **every** direction outside `col K` necessarily requires new state. Known diagonal coherences can sometimes reconstruct all missing directions. There is a sharp information criterion.

Let

\[
\mathcal D_K=\{D=D^T:\operatorname{diag}D=0,\ DK=0,
\ n_+(D)\le2,\ n_-(D)\le2\},
\tag{8}
\]

where `n_+` and `n_-` count positive and negative eigenvalues. This is a finite-dimensional semialgebraic set; testing it is not claimed inexpensive.

**Theorem 4 (exact added-control information criterion).** With every old row active, `π_K` contains sufficient initial information for all controls in `span(K,ℓ)` if and only if

\[
D\ell=0\qquad\hbox{for every }D\in\mathcal D_K.
\tag{9}
\]

Here “sufficient” means that `π_[K ℓ]` is a single-valued function of `π_K`; initialization of the enlarged polynomial predictor is then possible. A globally polynomial vector field in the **same old coordinate list** is additionally guaranteed whenever the reconstruction `Sℓ=H(π_K)` is polynomial, including the linear reconstruction case below. Condition (9) by itself establishes an information factorization and a continuous reconstruction on compact energy shells, rather than a Lipschitz formula at every singular point.

**Proof.** If old states have equal `π_K`, their two real Gram matrices have equal diagonal `a_i e_i`, equal `SK`, and rank at most two. Their difference belongs to (8): its positive inertia cannot exceed the rank of the first positive semidefinite Gram matrix, and similarly for negative inertia. Thus (9) makes their `Sℓ` equal. Theorem 3 applied to `[K ℓ]` gives sufficiency.

If (9) fails, take `D∈D_K` with `Dℓ≠0`, and its positive/negative spectral parts `S_+,S_-`. Each is positive semidefinite of rank at most two and hence is a Gram matrix of real two-vectors, or complex numbers `b_i^±`. Their diagonals agree because `diag D=0`. Realize each by setting `a_i=e_i=√((S_±)_{ii})`, choosing `x_i,y_i` with product `b_i^±`, and setting `c=0`. The resulting states have equal old actions, `p=0`, and `R=S_±K`, but different new `R` columns `S_±ℓ`. By Theorem 3 they are distinguishable with the enlarged controls. Explicitly, select `q=Kv+ℓ` so that `q_i(Dℓ)_i≠0` for an index where `Dℓ` is nonzero; this is possible because old row `K_i` is nonzero. Their initial `i`th action accelerations differ by `-2q_i(Dℓ)_i`. ∎

If an old row vanishes, a new nonzero coupling in that row usually requires its previously discarded `p_i` already at first order. The active-row assumption is essential.

**A practical sufficient linear test.** Let columns of `N` span `ker K^T`. A symmetric matrix with `DK=0` has the form `D=NAN^T`. If

\[
A\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(NAN^T)
\quad\hbox{is injective on }\operatorname{Sym}(r-d),
\tag{10}
\]

then the known `SK` and `diag S` reconstruct the entire `S` by linear algebra. Every added real control is then supported by the old state, with a polynomial same-coordinate extension. For rank `r-1`, (10) always holds. For `K=(1,1)` it trivially holds; for `K=(1,1,1)`, write `u_i=R_i-S_ii` and recover

\[
S_{12}=(u_1+u_2-u_3)/2,\quad
S_{13}=(u_1+u_3-u_2)/2,\quad
S_{23}=(u_2+u_3-u_1)/2.
\]

Thus even the two-triad example supports arbitrary new real couplings once the old **real** state is acquired.

**First equal-coupling counterexample.** For `r=4,K=(1,1,1,1)^T,c=0`, compare

\[
b^+=(1,1,-1,-1),\qquad b^-=(1,-1,1,-1),\qquad a_i=e_i=1.
\]

Both have `p=0,R=0` and are equilibria under every old control. Add `ℓ=(1,1,0,0)^T`. Then `ℓ^Tb^+=2`, `ℓ^Tb^-=0`, and

\[
\ddot n(0)=2|\ell^Tb|^2=8\quad\hbox{versus}\quad0.
\tag{11}
\]

This is a genuine global control-span barrier. It occurs on the zero-central-mode stratum, while generic rational reconstruction (7) would hide it.

## 5. Acquisition from actions, precision, and concrete control

The coordinates need not be measured using separate coherent sensors: exact action **jets under known probes** suffice. This is an operational mathematical interface, whose finite-precision cost is substantial.

For one fixed nonzero coupling vector `κ`, initialize repeated identical states and use constant unit control. If every `κ_i≠0`,

\[
p_i=-\frac{\dot a_i(0)}{2\kappa_i},\qquad
R_i=-\frac{\ddot a_i(0)}{2\kappa_i}+\kappa_i s_i n.
\tag{12}
\]

For general `K`, choose `d` linearly independent probe vectors `v^(j)` with every component of `Kv^(j)` nonzero. Such vectors exist because a finite union of row hyperplanes cannot contain an open set. The corresponding second derivatives give `R_i·v^(j)` and recover each row `R_i` by a linear solve. This needs `d` distinct identically prepared probe settings, control calibration, all relevant modal-action channels, and a conditioned probe matrix. The symmetric finite-interval implementation below requires fresh forward/reverse copies, hence at least `2d` such probe runs before averaging. The procedure does not acquire a copy's unknown initial state without disturbing it. Repeated preparations, weak-probe extrapolation, or an independently validated reset mechanism are indispensable.

For one scalar action with deterministic measurement error at most `ε_a`, symmetric first/second derivative estimates at `±τ` have errors

\[
|\widehat{\dot a}-\dot a|\le M_3\tau^2/6+\epsilon_a/\tau,
\quad
|\widehat{\ddot a}-\ddot a|\le M_4\tau^2/12+4\epsilon_a/\tau^2,
\tag{13}
\]

assuming known bounds `|a'''|≤M_3, |a''''|≤M_4` on that probe interval. Such bounds can be obtained explicitly from the known energy: for constant coupling with `κ=max_i|k_i|`, conservative choices are

\[
M_3=24\kappa^3 E^{5/2},\qquad M_4=120\kappa^4 E^3.
\tag{13a}
\]

To verify (13a), use the weighted amplitude norm `||u||_E²=Σ_i(|x_i|²+|y_i|²)+2|c|²` and write the quadratic field as a symmetric real-bilinear `F(u)=B(u,u)`. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives `||B(u,w)||_E≤κ||u||_E||w||_E`. Thus `||u'||≤κE`, `||u''||≤2κ²E^{3/2}`, `||u'''||≤6κ³E²`, and `||u''''||≤24κ⁴E^{5/2}` by differentiating the quadratic field. Leibniz differentiation of `|x_i|²` gives the stated scalar constants. They are loose bounds, not assumed unknown sensor inputs.

Reverse-time samples can be implemented by fresh copies with the opposite coupling only in the drift-free model (1). The optimal curvature bound from (13) is `2√(M_4 ε_a/3)`; recovering `R` further amplifies errors by `1/(2|κ_i|)` and probe-matrix inverse conditioning. Finite nonzero `τ` supplies an approximation, not exact derivatives. Statistical averaging has additional sample costs determined by its noise model. No conditioning-independent or sample-efficient sensor theorem follows.

The generic dimension floor in Corollary 3.1 also applies to the number of continuous scalar measurement values in an exact finite sensing transcript: fewer than `3r+1` cannot identify arbitrary generic predictive states. For known constants the floor is `2r`. This statement assumes the preparation/control/measurement transcript depends continuously on the initial state; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary discontinuous encodings or for a single requested scalar output.

Two concrete actuation rules use exactly this state:

* **Instantaneous transfer:** with `||v||₂≤V`, `dot n=2v·P`, so the instantaneous maximum is `2V||P||₂`. The regularized feedback `v=V P/√(η²+||P||²)` is smooth for `η>0` and makes `dot n≥0`. It stalls at `P=0`, including every zero-central-mode initial condition, so it is not a global reachability or optimal-transfer theorem.
* **Starting from `c=0`:** form the symmetric positive semidefinite `d×d` matrix `T=K^TR`, whose entries are `Re(B_α bar B_β)`. Its rank is at most two. The startup curvature is `ddot n=2v^TTv`; a finite initial pulse along a top eigenvector maximizes it at `2V²λ_max(T)`. `T=0` means all admitted collective amplitudes vanish and the entire state is an equilibrium. Eigenvector selection at degeneracy is a prescribed-pulse choice, not an unexamined smooth-feedback claim.

For the original two-triad boundary pair `(b_1,b_2)=(1,1)` versus `(1,-1)`, this controller detects startup curvatures `8` versus `0` under equal couplings. It demonstrates phase-sensitive prediction and a local control choice; preparation of either loop phase and implementation of the coupling signs/strengths are separate physical tasks.

## 6. Damping, detuning, and changing couplings

Add known linear terms

\[
\dot x_i=-(\gamma_{xi}+i\omega_{xi})x_i-ik_i\bar y_i c,
\quad
\dot y_i=-(\gamma_{yi}+i\omega_{yi})y_i-ik_i\bar x_i c,
\]
\[
\dot c=-(\gamma_c+i\omega_c)c-i\sum_i k_i b_i.
\tag{14}
\]

Set `A_i=γ_xi+γ_yi+i(ω_xi+ω_yi)`, `A_c=γ_c+iω_c`. Then

\[
\dot b_i=-A_i b_i-ik_i s_i c,\qquad
\dot z_i=-(A_i+\bar A_c)z_i+i\sum_jk_jb_i\bar b_j-ik_i s_i n.
\tag{15}
\]

Nonnegative damping makes (6) nonincreasing, so the same global existence argument holds under locally integrable coefficients. Bounded negative damping gives a Grönwall energy bound on finite intervals. Arbitrary additive forcing is a different interface and is not covered.

**Real closure failure under even uniform detuning.** If the pair-center detuning is `δ=(ω_x+ω_y)-ω_c`, then `dot p` contains `-δ Re z`. With `r=1`, `a=e=n=1`, `K=1`, the states `z=+1` and `z=-1` have identical old `(actions,p=0,R=1)`. At `δ=1,k=1`, their `dot p` values are `-2` and `0`. Their action accelerations differ. Consequently, the half-sized real closure is not sufficient once this reflection-breaking drift is admitted.

**An exact spectral-hull construction.** For fixed diagonal `D=diag(A_i)`, let the complex linear space

\[
U=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{K,DK,D^2K,\ldots,D^{r-1}K\}
\tag{16}
\]

have dimension `m`, and choose columns of `W` as a basis. Include `z_i` and `Q_iα=b_i overline(B_α)` with `B_α=Σ_j W_jα b_j`. If `DW=WM`, the column vector `B=W^Tb` satisfies `dot B=-M^TB-iLc`. With `Z_α=Σ_jW_jα z_j`, `L_α=Σ_jW_jα k_j s_j`, direct differentiation gives

\[
\dot Q_{i\alpha}=-A_iQ_{i\alpha}
 -\sum_\beta\overline{M_{\beta\alpha}}Q_{i\beta}
 -ik_i s_i\bar Z_\alpha+i\bar L_\alpha z_i.
\tag{17}
\]

Equation (15) uses the `Q` columns expressing each admitted real `k` in `U`. This is an exact globally polynomial closure of size `O(rm)`, including damping/detuning, with no division by an amplitude. For time-dependent diagonal coefficients, use a common space invariant under every admitted diagonal matrix; its joint spectral classes determine the hull. With `g` distinct fixed diagonal values, `m` is the sum of the ranks of `K` restricted to their coordinate groups, at most `min(r,gd)`.

For distinct `A_i` and one column `K` whose entries are all nonzero, the Vandermonde matrix in (16) has rank `r`. Thus generic inhomogeneous linear drift destroys small-`d` savings **within this collective-linear-sketch construction**. This is not a lower bound on every nonlinear predictor: the O(r) amplitude model remains available. Uniform `A_i` makes `m=d`; uniform nonzero pair-center detuning still requires the complex rather than real closure.

**Proposition 6.1 (sharp detuned information).** For fixed coefficients (14), suppose every row of `K` is nonzero and every pair-center detuning `δ_i=Im(A_i+bar A_c)` is nonzero. Then `(actions,z,Q_U)` is exactly the all-action behavioral quotient, where `U` is (16). Its displayed coordinates remain redundant; the claim concerns fibers, not a quadratic dimension lower bound.

**Proof.** Sufficiency follows from (15)–(17) and the damped energy bound. For necessity, action behavior under arbitrary piecewise constant controls determines the values of all iterated Lie derivatives of the outputs under drift `f_0` and the control vector fields `f_α`. To see this directly, concatenate short constant-control segments and differentiate the resulting output with respect to their separate durations at zero; affine combinations of constant controls recover each drift/control word. First, `L_{f_α}a_i=-2K_iα p_i` recovers `p_i` using a nonzero row entry. Then

\[
L_{f_0}p_i=-\operatorname{Re}(A_i+\bar A_c)p_i-\delta_i\operatorname{Re}z_i
\]

recovers `Re z_i`. Writing `Q_iα=R_iα+iJ_iα`, the control derivatives satisfy `L_{f_α}p_i=R_iα-K_iα s_i n` and `L_{f_α}Re z_i=-J_iα`; thus every complex `Q` column for `K` is determined. Finally, for any fixed weight column `w`, the drift alone gives

\[
L_{f_0}Q_{i,w}=-A_iQ_{i,w}-Q_{i,Dw}.
\]

Repeated drift derivatives recover the columns for `DK,D²K,...`, hence the whole hull. Equality of all action responses therefore forces equality of the asserted state, including at zero amplitudes. ∎

This additionally explains why arbitrarily small nonzero detuning can change exact indistinguishability: the reflection symmetry is broken. Estimating the recovered coordinate from a drift derivative divides by `δ_i`, so no uniform finite-precision sensing claim as `δ_i→0` follows.

If `K=K(t)` is differentiable and real, differentiating `Q` adds `b_i overline(Σ_j dot K_jα b_j)`. A moving basis with `dot K=K C(t)` closes with known coefficient mixing. A genuinely moving span generally needs its derivative directions too; instantaneous rank alone is insufficient. More generally, a differentiable basis `W(t)` closes (14) whenever `dot W-DW=W C(t)` and every admitted coupling lies in `span W(t)`. This is a dynamical invariant-subspace condition, not a free way to hold arbitrary real couplings in a small space under arbitrary detuning.

## 7. A constructive extension beyond one shared mode

The same real Gram machinery covers a restricted larger network: private pairs `(x_i,y_i)` coupled to `m` central modes `c_a`, with real matrix `k=Σ_α v_α A_α`:

\[
i\dot x_i=\bar y_i\sum_a k_{ia}c_a,\quad
i\dot y_i=\bar x_i\sum_a k_{ia}c_a,\quad
i\dot c_a=\sum_i k_{ia}b_i.
\tag{18}
\]

Set `h_a=-ic_a`, `B_αa=Σ_i(A_α)_ia b_i`, and retain

\[
P_{ia}=b_i\cdot h_a,\quad
R_{i,\alpha a}=b_i\cdot B_{\alpha a},\quad
T_{ab}=h_a\cdot h_b.
\]

All products are real inner products in `R²`. Let
`U_α=A_α^T P` and `L_α=A_α^T diag(s) k`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot a_i=\dot e_i&=2\sum_a k_{ia}P_{ia},\\
\dot P&=\operatorname{diag}(s)kT-\sum_\alpha v_\alpha R_\alpha,\\
\dot R_\alpha&=\operatorname{diag}(s)kU_\alpha^T+P L_\alpha^T,\\
\dot T&=-k^TP-P^Tk.
\end{aligned}\tag{19}
\]

Central actions are `T_aa`. These equations follow by the product rule from `dot b=diag(s)kh`, `dot h=-k^Tb`; hence they are exact and polynomial on every zero-mode stratum. The conserved positive energy is `Σ_i s_i+2 tr T`. Storage is `O(rmd+m²)`, and straightforward dense evaluation costs `O(rm²d+rm d)`. The original amplitude evaluation costs `O(rm)` once the dense coupling matrix is supplied, so this extension has no general runtime advantage. No output-minimality claim is made for (19).

This is not an arbitrary three-wave hypergraph result. If two nominal private pairs share a passive amplitude, `dot b_i=-ik_i s_i c` no longer holds: for example, a common `x` gives cross terms such as `y_1 bar y_2 c_2`. Additional generators are required. A finite invariant-ring construction can in principle handle any finite compact-torus-equivariant polynomial cluster, but generator counts and syzygies can grow; no linear-size universal closure has been proved here.

* Static and moving detuning-hull derivatives agree with differentiated products to `6.03×10^-14`; the O(r) damped product realization agrees to `1.34×10^-15`. Five symbolic identities verify the explicitly unphysical ambient finite-time blowup.
* The new-control pair (11), uniform-detuning failure, and full-Gram reconstruction at `r=2,3` versus failure at `r=4` are reproduced.

The matched right-hand-side microbenchmark includes `k=Kv` formation in both solvers, gives both the same initial amplitudes, and excludes no acquisition charge from only one solver. In the final local NumPy run, the real closure takes approximately `1.67–4.69` times the amplitude derivative time for tested `(r,d)=(16,1),(64,1),(256,1),(256,4)`; an earlier run gave `1.85–3.05`, illustrating ordinary microbenchmark variability. The first three closures have only one fewer ambient real coordinate than the amplitude vector; the last has 1,793 versus 1,026. These machine/implementation-specific times are evidence against a speed claim, not an optimized performance theorem.

An alternative O(r) globally regular **Lipschitz**, nonpolynomial realization already follows directly from pair products. The constants `δ_i=a_i-e_i` give `s_i=√(δ_i²+4|b_i|²)`, so (5) closes on complex `(b,h)` and the `r` supplied constants, with actions reconstructed as `(s_i±δ_i)/2` and `n=|h|²`. The square-root norm is Lipschitz even at `δ_i=b_i=0`, although not differentiable there. Its one global phase is redundant. Under (14), keep `δ_i` as dynamic variables and use

\[
\dot b_i=-A_i b_i+k_i s_i h,\quad
\dot h=-A_c h-\sum_i k_i b_i,\quad
\dot\delta_i=-(\gamma_{xi}-\gamma_{yi})s_i-(\gamma_{xi}+\gamma_{yi})\delta_i.
\]

This is again an O(r) Lipschitz realization for arbitrary diagonal damping/detuning, with the same action reconstruction and physical energy bound. Thus the spectral-hull growth in (16) is a limitation of the polynomial collective-observable presentation, not of finite-state prediction itself. Comparing only against an O(r²) full Gram baseline would exaggerate the accomplishment.

## 9. Primary-source novelty and applicability audit

The following primary sources were checked live. Their existence rules out broad novelty claims. The narrow exact formula and behavioral theorem above have not been found verbatim; that does not establish priority.

1. [Alber, Luther, Marsden, Robbins (1998), *Geometric Phases, Reduction and Lie-Poisson Structure for the Resonant Three-wave Interaction*](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~marsden/bib/1998/07-AlLuMaRo1998/) explicitly performs invariant-based Poisson reduction of a resonant triad. Invariant products and regular orbit geometry are established machinery.
2. [Alber et al., *Geometry and Control of Three-Wave Interactions*](https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~marsden/bib/1999/09-AlLuMaRo1999/) explicitly treats piecewise continuous control and quasi-phase-matching. Its primary author page and indexed paper text were available; the direct page fetch timed out. Energy-transfer control is therefore not new here. Sign reversal is physically realized only in a concrete quasi-phase-matching/material interface, which (1) does not supply.
3. [Bustamante and Kartashova (2009), *Effect of the dynamical phases on the nonlinear amplitudes’ evolution*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2354) studies triads and two connected triads, and shows major dynamical-phase effects on energy exchange. The PP representative must not be relabeled an AA butterfly.
4. [Bustamante and Kartashova (2011), *Resonance Clustering in Wave Turbulent Regimes: Integrable Dynamics*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.4994) discusses AA/PP butterflies, N-star-A and N-star-P integrability, polynomial conservation laws, and nonintegrable/chaotic cases. The [full arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.4994), Section V.D and equation (62) on PDF page 15, exhibits the very cross products `B1 B2 bar B4 bar B5` used in the initial AA quotient. Thus the loop product itself and AA-star polynomial machinery are not novelty evidence. The publisher's two-page extract alone is incomplete; the full manuscript was checked for this claim.
5. [Harper, Bustamante, Nazarenko (2013), *Quadratic invariants for discrete clusters of weakly interacting waves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3156) identifies quadratic conservation laws with the nullspace of the interaction matrix. That method addresses constants, rather than every sufficient nonconstant observable, but fully preempts claims that finding the quadratic laws is new.
6. [Sontag, *Spaces of Observables in Nonlinear Control*](https://www.sontaglab.org/FTPDIR/icm.pdf) develops observables induced by experiments and the connection to nonlinear realization. Theorem 3 is a concrete closure of this established observation-algebra program. It should be evaluated as a model-specific explicit calculation and singular-stratum result, rather than a new general theory of reduced state.
7. [Wang, Xu, Tang (2011), *A Unique “Nonnegative” Solution to an Underdetermined System: From Vectors to Matrices*](https://people.ece.cornell.edu/atang/pub/11/ToSP11.pdf), Theorem 5, uses nullspace eigenvalue inertia to characterize uniqueness of low-rank positive semidefinite recovery. Its uniqueness contract differs from (9), which asks only for the functional `Sℓ` across rank-at-most-two Gram fibers. Nevertheless, the difference-of-PSD and inertia machinery in Theorem 4 is established, and should not be presented as a new general matrix-recovery principle.

A full invariant ring separates all symmetry orbits, while Theorem 3 separates only admitted-control action behaviors. Its smaller information requirement is mathematically real. The generic local dimension remains the standard phase quotient dimension, and the core construction uses ordinary product differentiation, observation algebras, and low-rank Gram recovery. A potentially publishable incremental contribution would need a careful literature comparison of this exact action equivalence and its particular partial-recovery criterion (9), preferably in a broader model class; the present search does not certify that contribution.

## 10. Charged capability and remaining gates

The proved capability is exact prediction of all modal actions under a fixed admitted real coupling span, through amplitude-zero strata, plus a startup control optimization and a precisely limited network extension. It is not a new physical law, generic integrability theorem, faster simulator, or experimentally demonstrated controller.

Acquisition must charge either coherent complex products (third/fourth order), or the repeated calibrated action probes in (12)–(13). Coherent amplitude acquisition already supplies an O(r) baseline. Action probes require repeatable preparation/reset, multiple controls, finite intervals, precision, and disturbance accounting. Dense supplied `K` has `rd` input entries; learning/calibrating it is not included in one derivative evaluation. Reading every modal-action output itself costs Ω(r) scalars per reported time. Numerical integration time, error control, and algebraic consistency monitoring remain necessary; avoiding coordinate divisions does not make arbitrary noisy products realizable. Coupling sign/phase control, pump energy, actuator bandwidth, delay, mode isolation, and omitted-mode effects require a concrete device and data, none of which are supplied.

There is no indispensable proof gap in Theorems 1–4 or Proposition 6.1 under their stated contracts. The unresolved gates are external novelty, a realistic acquisition/actuation advantage, the smallest globally regular polynomial embedding of the detuned quotient, mixed zero/nonzero-detuning observability, and a general hypergraph result with a useful generator bound. The strongest defensible conclusion is a proved finite-model action quotient with useful sharp boundaries and **no demonstrated asymptotic or experimental advantage over the original amplitude system**.
