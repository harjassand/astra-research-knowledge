# Independent source-to-equation audit

## Finding and scope

Under the literal source mapping—Nature S8's `β` is the same separation fraction `β` in Xiang et al., and fluorescence is proportional to fluorescent ground-state mScarlet—the fitted positive RF increment has the wrong sign. The published RF-on fluorescence change is positive, while the transferred ODE predicts a decrease. This is a narrow specification/interface inconsistency; it does not challenge the measured fluorescence response.

The Nature supplement does not publish its implementation, output map, or exact 520-nm intensity-to-rate conversion. A complement convention, a sign typo in S8, or an unreported readout/mechanism could therefore explain the discrepancy. A sign-corrected separation-fraction increment, with no other model changes, recovers a positive response with about the plotted high-intensity amplitude. The defensible conclusion is conditional, not a finding that an experiment is false.

## Source reconstruction

I retrieved the Nature supplementary PDF directly from the official Springer Nature asset URL, then checked the rendered S8 and Supplementary Data Fig. 6 pages. The bytes match the PDF in the earlier package (SHA-256 in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)). S8 states that `β` changes from `β_i` to `β_i + Δβ B1`, calls `Δβ` the RF-induced change in the magnetic-field-dependent separation fraction, says other kinetic rates come from Xiang et al. Table 1, and uses starting concentrations 50 μM mScarlet, 20 μM FMN, 330 μM FMNH2. The joint fit gives `β_i = 0.68(2)` and `Δβ = 0.117(2) mT^-1`. Supplementary Data Fig. 6 shows positive `ΔF/F` during RF-on windows for `B1 = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 mT` and green intensities `0.5, 1.0, 2.5, 3.8 W cm^-2`.

Xiang et al. explicitly define `β(B)` as the SCRP separation fraction and `1-β(B)` as the recombination fraction. Their Figure 6 mechanism says singlet products preferentially reform fluorescent ground-state mScarlet3; triplet products separate to a nonfluorescent mScarlet radical anion; FMN later reoxidizes that anion. The authors' public MATLAB model independently encodes those meanings: `y(1)` is ground-state mScarlet, `y(3)` is radical anion, and the equations route `β kred H T` into `R` and `(1-β) kred H T` into `G`. Their spin-chemistry code also computes `β = separated/(separated + recombined)`.

The six states are `(G,T,R,F,H,Q)`: fluorescent mScarlet ground state, triplet mScarlet, radical anion, FMN, FMNH2, and FMNH radical. Define

\[
a=\frac{k_{ISC}I\sigma}{k_R^S+k_{ISC}},\quad
u=\beta k_{red}HT,\quad v=k_{ox}FR,\quad w=k_{dis}Q^2.
\]

The source ODE is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot G&=-aG+(k_{tr}+(1-\beta)k_{red}H)T+v,\\
\dot T&=aG-(k_{tr}+k_{red}H)T,\\
\dot R&=u-v,\\
\dot F&=-v+w,\qquad \dot H=-u+w,\\
\dot Q&=u-2w+v.
\end{aligned}
\]

The initial condition used here follows Nature S8 for the three named species and Xiang's stated convention that unlisted tracked states begin at zero:
`(G,T,R,F,H,Q)=(50,0,0,20,330,0) μM`. This gives invariants
`M=G+T+R=50`, `L=F+H+Q=350`, and `J=G+T+2F+Q=90 μM`.

The source model's fluorescence observable is `G` (up to a positive multiplicative factor); Xiang's Supporting Information says the radical anion is nonfluorescent under yellow excitation and uses a constant 3% background offset. Nature S6 reports no significant mean-lifetime change under static-field switching: `10(9) ps` for mScarlet and `10(31) ps` for the blue-pre-excited mScarlet–FMN mixture, with a roughly 3.9 ns mScarlet lifetime. This supports an amplitude/population interpretation. It does not by itself identify the RF-state observation map: S6 uses 0 versus about 50 mT, rather than the resonant RF pulse protocol.

## Immediate-time sign

At a fixed pre-step state, the only direct `β` dependence in `Gdot` is the recombination branch. Therefore

\[
\dot G_{\beta+\Delta\beta}-\dot G_{\beta}
=-\Delta\beta\,k_{red}HT.
\]

For `H,T>0`, an upward step in the separation fraction immediately lowers the ground-state fluorescence. At the fitted steady state with `I520=3.8 W cm^-2`, the exact Table 1 rates give `H T kred` such that the `0.3 mT` fitted increment changes `Gdot` by `-0.3481 μM s^-1` at RF-on.

At the listed S8 starting state, `T(0)=0`, so the first derivative difference is zero at the exact instant of simultaneous green/RF onset. But `Tdot(0)=aG0>0`, and

\[
\Delta\ddot G(0)=-\Delta\beta\,k_{red}H_0aG_0<0
\]

for green illumination and positive `H0,G0`. Thus the non-equilibrated start does not reverse the response; it delays its onset by one derivative order.

## Steady-state sign, derived from the six equations

At a positive steady state, `u=v=w` and `aG=(ktr+kred H)T`. Eliminating states with the three invariants gives

\[
R=2F+Q-40,\quad H=350-F-Q,\quad
F(Q)=\frac{40-Q+\sqrt{(Q-40)^2+8cQ^2}}4,
\quad c=\frac{k_{dis}}{k_{ox}}.
\]

Write `A=50-R` and `C=ktr+kred H`. Then

\[
G(Q)=A\frac{C}{a+C},\qquad
T(Q)=A\frac{a}{a+C},\qquad
\beta(Q)=\frac{k_{dis}Q^2(a+C)}{a k_{red}HA}.
\]

For the physical roots of the fitted interval, `Q > 80/(1+8c)`. With
`D=sqrt((Q-40)^2+8cQ^2)`,

\[
F'(Q)=\frac{-1+(Q-40+8cQ)/D}{4},\quad
(Q-40+8cQ)^2-D^2=8cQ((1+8c)Q-80)>0.
\]

The numerator `Q-40+8cQ` is positive on these roots, so `F'>0`, `R'=2F'+1>0`, and `H'=-F'-1<0`. It follows directly that

\[
G'=-R'\frac{C}{a+C}+A\frac{a k_{red}H'}{(a+C)^2}<0.
\]

The map from `Q` to `β` is increasing as well:

\[
\frac{\beta'}{\beta}
=\frac2Q+\frac{R'}{A}
+H'\left(\frac{k_{red}}{a+C}-\frac1H\right)>0,
\]

because `H'<0` and
`kred/(a+C)-1/H=-(a+ktr)/(H(a+C))<0`. Hence the source model's ground-state fluorescence is strictly decreasing with the literal separation fraction over the fitted roots. This sign proof does not depend on which positive green-light excitation rate `a` is used.

Using JACS Table 1 after unit conversion (`kred=50 μM^-1s^-1`, `kox=0.003375 μM^-1s^-1`, `kdis=5000 μM^-1s^-1`, `ktr=224 s^-1`, `kISC=1.85×10^5 s^-1`, `kR^S=2.5×10^8 s^-1`) and `β: 0.68→0.7151`, the stationary change in `G` is:

| `I520` used in the model | `G(β=.68)` | `G(β=.7151)` | `ΔG/G` | radical-only upper limit `ΔR/R` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 W cm^-2 | 16.9254 μM | 16.4397 μM | −2.870% | +1.469% |
| 1.7 W cm^-2 | 7.2799 μM | 6.9932 μM | −3.938% | +0.671% |
| 3.8 W cm^-2 | 3.6846 μM | 3.5226 μM | −4.396% | +0.350% |

The `I520` values above use a transparent linear scaling of the code/Table 1 reference `Iσ=1652 s^-1` at 1.7 W cm^-2. Nature S8 does not spell out its 520-nm absorption-rate conversion, so these absolute magnitudes are a reproducible source-adjacent check, not an exact reproduction of the private fitting implementation. The sign proof above is independent of that conversion.

## Transient protocol and source-parameter sensitivity

The Nature figure uses RF pulses over 0–5 s and 10–15 s after blue pre-excitation and green-light onset. I integrated those intervals with the ODE above, comparing an RF trace with an otherwise identical `β=.68` control. For the exact S8 initial concentrations and `I520=3.8 W cm^-2`, the literal positive separation increment gives `ΔG/G=-4.411%` at 5 s. Starting instead from the low-β green-light steady state gives `-4.396%`. Repeating after 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, and 10 seconds of green pre-exposure also leaves the first-pulse sign negative. The actual pre-green duration in the schematic is approximate; the endpoint and equilibrium checks bracket its intended regimes.

As a correction/counterexample, changing only the separation-fraction slope from `+0.117` to `-0.117 mT^-1` yields `+4.847%` at 5 s from the stated initial condition; after 2 s green pre-exposure it rises from `+0.87%` at 0.1 s to `+4.82%` at 5 s. The latter is close to the roughly `+4.8%` high-intensity, `B1=.3 mT` trace in Supplementary Data Fig. 6. This is not a refit of the full data panel, but it demonstrates that a sign correction alone can produce the observed response scale under the same reaction network.

The authors' repository default vector differs slightly from Table 1 (`kox=.003675` instead of the Table 1 conversion `.003375`, `kred=50.5406` instead of `50`, and a 0.3% difference in `kISC`). It yields the same sign and a `−4.36%` steady response at 3.8 W cm^-2. I used the exact Table 1 values for the primary calculation because that is what Nature S8 says it uses. The fitted `β_i=.68` is free in the Nature fit; it should not be treated as a new measurement of Xiang's in-vitro `β0=.81`.

## Alternatives attacked

**Complement convention.** If Nature's private variable were actually the recombination fraction `γ=1-β_Xiang`, increasing it would mean a decrease in Xiang's separation fraction and would give a positive fluorescence response. That would resolve the sign direction, but conflicts with the printed S8 description of `β` as a separation fraction. The Nature implementation is not public, so a prose/code convention mismatch remains possible.

**Dark-state fluorescence or normalization.** A fixed observation map `Fdet=G+ρR+B`, with nonnegative constant background `B`, cannot explain the reported high-intensity transient under the literal positive separation step. In the 5-s source-initial-state check, `ΔG≈−0.162 μM` and `ΔR≈+0.163 μM`; in the stationary check they are `−0.1620` and `+0.1620 μM`. Positive signal requires radical brightness at least approximately equal to ground-state brightness (`ρ≈1`). Even if the radical were brighter, the maximum positive fractional signal at 5 s is the radical-only limit, about `0.35%`; ground-state emission or a positive background cannot increase that limit and reduces contrast at finite `ρ`. This is well below the plotted ≈4.8%. The JACS source explicitly calls the radical nonfluorescent and uses a 3% constant background. Dividing by a positive baseline or adding a constant background can scale the signal but cannot reverse its sign.

**Other β-dependent rates.** In the source ODE, β appears only in the separation/recombination branch. Total triplet quenching `kred H T` is β-independent; `a`, `ktr`, `kox`, and `kdis` do not change with β. An RF-dependent change in those other rates could alter the response, but it is not the transferred model described in S8.

**Laser history and transient measurement.** The measured signal is transient rather than a direct equilibrium comparison. The exact ODE gives the wrong sign both from the stated initial concentrations and after green-light equilibration, and across the tested pre-green histories. A positive-kernel low-pass detector model can delay or attenuate a response but cannot turn an everywhere negative input into a positive one. Conversely, the negative-slope counterexample gives a smooth positive transient from a pre-illuminated state.

**Data and parameter mismatch.** Nature measures mScarlet-I with 520-nm green light; Xiang's fitted photocycle is for mScarlet3 and its reference excitation parameter is at 561 nm. Nature calls its simulation semi-quantitative and the radical-pair partners unknown. This limits parameter transfer and the quantitative fit claim, but does not change the branch-level sign if the same β and `G` readout are used.

## Interpretation boundary

The explicit public statements cannot all be true under a literal same-variable transfer: (i) Nature's fitted RF law raises a parameter called the separation fraction; (ii) Xiang's same-named fraction routes triplet products away from fluorescent ground state; and (iii) the resulting model traces reproduce positive RF-on fluorescence. At least one of the variable semantics, slope sign, or output mapping must differ in the implementation. The first two are supported directly by the public sources; the third is Nature's reported fit, but the source packet omits its equations and readout implementation. Thus the **published model specification** contains a conditional sign inconsistency, while whether the private implementation itself is inconsistent is unresolved.

The cited papers already establish the photocycle and RF-sensitive RYDMR observation. This audit claims no new mechanism, capability, experimental discovery, or priority.
