# Exact coefficient-only acquisition by an extactic polynomial

7 October 2026, cycle 8. **New internal derivation, awaiting an independent written gate.** The construction below gives a randomized polynomial-bit acquisition algorithm under the original rational quartic promise, with exact verification of every returned certificate. No historical novelty or broad proof-discovery breakthrough is asserted. The decisive new step within this programme is the universal nonvanishing proof in Section 3. Root, equal_charge_audit and equal_charge_prior have independently reconstructed that step in messages; written gates remain authoritative.

## 1. Exact input and output

Input: the expanded rational coefficients of

    p(x) = ((Lx)^{circ 2}-a)^T Q ((Lx)^{circ 2}-a),

where Q is rational symmetric positive definite, a is entrywise positive rational, b=Qa is entrywise positive, and L is rational invertible. Q,a,b,L are hidden; neither a factor subspace, a zero, a hidden condition number nor a target commuting family is supplied. Let n be the number of variables and B the maximum binary coefficient length of the expanded input.

Output: rational quadratic factors and strictly positive rational weights whose weighted squares expand **exactly** to p. In addition, the algorithm recovers the n rational hidden coordinate rays and the n-dimensional quadratic factor subspace. A declared finite trial budget may return UNKNOWN. Under the promise, unbounded independent retries have expected polynomial bit cost. A bounded run's returned certificate is sound regardless of whether the input satisfied the promise.

This is a structured acquisition theorem, not recognition of arbitrary nonnegative or SOS quartics. It does not improve on an equally informed computer algebra implementation of the same method.

## 2. Observable metric, cubic field and extactic

Acquire from the degree-two homogeneous part

    M = -Hess(p_2)/4 = L^T diag(b) L > 0.

Compute the rational homogeneous cubic vector field

    F(x) = (1/4) M^{-1} grad p_4(x),   D=F dot grad.

Define the polynomial

    E(x) = det [ D^k x_i ]_(0<=k<n,1<=i<=n).             (1)

Every entry in row k is homogeneous of degree 2k+1, so E has degree n^2 if nonzero. A linear coordinate change multiplies this determinant by a nonzero constant and transports D. The hidden real normalization y=diag(sqrt(b)) Lx gives

    D y_i = y_i (Gm)_i,  m_i=y_i^2,
    G=diag(b)^{-1}Q diag(b)^{-1} > 0.                    (2)

Each hidden rational form ell_i(x)=(Lx)_i is a Darboux polynomial: D ell_i is divisible by ell_i. All further D iterates remain divisible by ell_i. Change the column basis of (1) to include ell_i; hence ell_i divides E. This is the standard extactic factor mechanism (Pereira 2001, Proposition 5 in the general formulation), also proved here directly. Merely knowing these factors exist would be useless if E were identically zero.

## 3. Universal nonvanishing under positive definiteness

**Theorem.** For every real symmetric positive-definite G, the extactic determinant for (2) is nonzero.

Proof by induction on n. For n=1 the determinant is y_1. Assume n>1 and choose j with G_jj maximal. Positive definiteness of each 2x2 principal block gives

    G_ij < sqrt(G_ii G_jj) <= G_jj,  i!=j.              (3)

Let B(y) be the (n-1)-square matrix with entries D^k y_i for i!=j and 0<=k<=n-2. Restriction to y_j=0 commutes with D, because the ideal (y_j) is D-invariant. Thus det B restricted to that hyperplane is the extactic polynomial for the positive-definite principal submatrix G_{-j,-j}. By induction it is nonzero; consequently det B is nonzero as a polynomial.

Suppose for contradiction E=0. Over the real rational-function field solve

    D^k y_j = sum_(i!=j) c_i(y) D^k y_i,  0<=k<=n-2,

uniquely using B. The vanishing full determinant says this same relation also holds for k=n-1. Differentiate the first n-1 relations and subtract the next relation. This gives

    sum_(i!=j) (D c_i) D^k y_i = 0,  0<=k<=n-2.

Since B is invertible over the rational-function field, D c_i=0 for every i. In particular

    y_j = sum_(i!=j) c_i(y) y_i                         (4)

is a relation with rational first-integral coefficients. This elementary argument is the needed differential-field Wronskian criterion; it does not assume any higher-dimensional extactic-to-linear-ratio theorem.

Consider real trajectories near the positive j-axis. Write r_i=y_i/y_j, take y_j>0, and rescale time by ds/dt=y_j^2. Then

    dr_i/ds = r_i [G_ij-G_jj
                   + sum_(k!=j) (G_ik-G_jk) r_k^2],
    d(log y_j)/ds = G_jj + sum_(k!=j) G_jk r_k^2.       (5)

By (3), a sufficiently small open cube around r=0 is forward invariant, and |r_i(s)| decreases exponentially to zero. Explicitly choose its radius so the absolute quadratic correction in every bracket is at most half min_(i!=j)(G_jj-G_ij). The second equation has bounded coefficients on that cube, so y_j(s)>0 exists for all finite s. These trajectories form a real open cone.

Choose an initial point in that cone where all rational c_i are defined. Such points form a nonempty open set because det B is a nonzero polynomial. Since D c_i=0, c_i along the trajectory equal their finite initial values wherever the rational representatives are defined. Relation (4), with those fixed initial values, holds near time zero and therefore holds at every finite s by analytic continuation of the analytic ODE solution. This also deals with possible later poles of a rational representative: the fixed-coefficient analytic identity has no poles.

Divide (4) by y_j and let s tend to infinity. Equation (5) gives

    1 = sum_(i!=j) c_i(initial) r_i(s) -> 0,

a contradiction. Thus E is nonzero. QED.

Crucially, the proof never restricts a potentially pole-bearing c_i to y_j=0; only the polynomial minor B is restricted. Tied transverse decay exponents cause no problem. No rate, attraction cone or hidden G is supplied to the algorithm: this argument proves a polynomial is nonzero, and the actual algorithm uses exact arithmetic.

## 4. Recover all hidden factors from one random rational line

No multivariate expansion or arithmetic-circuit factorization is needed. Choose integer vectors u,v uniformly from a grid and compute

    f(t)=E(u+t v),       g_i(t)=(partial_i E)(u+t v).

All degrees are at most d=n^2. Factor f over Q, retaining each rational root r and its multiplicity e. Compute the vector

    h(r,e) = [ (d/dt)^(e-1) g_i(t) at t=r ]_i.         (6)

If E=ell^e H, ell is linear, ell(v)!=0, and H(u+rv)!=0 at r=-ell(u)/ell(v), then differentiating e-1 times along v and once in the ambient coordinates yields

    h(r,e) = e! ell(v)^(e-1) H(u+rv) grad ell.          (7)

Thus (6) is a nonzero rational normal to ell. Multiplicities are essential: using just grad E would lose repeated factors.

For each hidden ell_i write E=ell_i^{e_i} H_i, with H_i not divisible by ell_i. Sufficient good-line conditions are

    ell_i(v)!=0,
    H_i(ell_i(v)u-ell_i(u)v)!=0.                       (8)

The second polynomial in (u,v) is nonzero: its argument ranges over the entire hyperplane ell_i=0 when ell_i(v)!=0, and H_i does not vanish identically on that hyperplane. Its degree is 2(d-e_i), using homogeneity. The product of all conditions in (8) has degree at most n+2n(d-1)<2nd. Consequently a grid with more than 8nd values per coordinate has bad-line probability less than 1/4, by the elementary Schwartz-Zippel counting argument. One good line recovers all n hidden rational normals among at most d candidate normals. Other rational roots may produce extraneous normals; zero vectors are skipped.

The implementation uses 16 n^4+1 values per coordinate, which is more than sufficient. An unlucky line need not be recognized directly: final exact certificate verification ensures soundness. On a good line, the next step is guaranteed to succeed. Independent uniform repeats have failure probability at most 4^{-T} after T trials. The API defaults to system random draws. The saved reproducible fixtures explicitly supply a fixed pseudorandom seed; their success is diagnostic and carries no adversarial-input probability guarantee.

## 5. Select the axes without knowing G

Convert every nonzero candidate normal h into the primal ray w=M^{-1}h. Evaluate its exact rational normalized score

    score(w)=p_4(w)/(w^T M w)^2.

In hidden orthonormal coordinates this is m^T Gm with m>=0 and sum m=1. The identity

    sum_i m_i G_ii - m^T Gm
      = sum_(i<j) m_i m_j (G_ii+G_jj-2G_ij)

is strictly positive whenever m has at least two nonzero coordinates, since G>0. Hence every global maximizer is a hidden axis, and at least one hidden axis attains max_i G_ii. A candidate set containing every hidden axis therefore has an axis as its highest-score element; extraneous normals cannot defeat it.

Select that ray, discard all candidate rays not exactly M-orthogonal to it, and repeat. The remaining true axes are still present, and restriction gives a positive-definite principal G. After n selections the resulting rational matrix V has columns equal to the hidden primal axes up to permutation and nonzero rational scaling.

Substitute x=Vz into the original p. Read Q' from the coefficients of z_i^4 and z_i^2 z_j^2; read b' from -1/2 times the z_i^2 coefficients; solve a'=(Q')^{-1}b'. Check Q'>0 by rational LDL and a'>0, then check the entire expanded identity. Rational LDL gives explicit strictly positive weighted squares in the original coordinates. These checks are unconditional soundness, distinct from promised-class acquisition coverage.

## 6. Polynomial bit cost

The input has at most O(n^4) degree-four monomials. Metric inversion and F construction require polynomially many rational operations with polynomial coefficient growth. To evaluate E and its gradient on a line, solve the formal ODE

    X(s)=u+t v + sum_(k=1)^(n-1) X_k(t) s^k,
    X'(s)=F(X(s)) mod s^(n-1),

by its coefficient recurrence (k+1)X_{k+1}=[s^k]F(X). The polynomials X_k have degree at most 2k+1 in t. Differentiate this recurrence with respect to the initial point to obtain all ambient derivatives. This can be implemented with first-order dual numbers. There are polynomially many truncated univariate products; the denominators introduced by k+1 and the rational input have polynomial total bit length. An n-square polynomial determinant and its cofactors give f and all g_i. Their degrees are at most n^2 and their coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in n and B (standard determinant/coefficient height bounds, with O(log n)-bit sampled line coordinates).

Here is an explicit height bound. Clear a common integer denominator A of F, and let C=max_i ||F_i||_1. For homogeneous degree-r g, ||Dg||_1<=r C ||g||_1. Hence ||D^k x_i||_1<=C^k product_(s<k)(2s+1). The denominator divides A^k. Substitution |u_i|,|v_i|<=S multiplies this bound by at most (2S)^(2k+1). The determinant's common denominator divides A^[n(n-1)/2], and its l1 norm is bounded by n! times the product of these row bounds. This gives coefficient bit lengths polynomial in n, log A, log max(1,C), log S; gradient coefficients add only a polynomial degree factor. Common-denominator formation, C and log A themselves have polynomial size in the expanded input. ODE-series factorial denominators disappear on converting the k-th Taylor coefficient to D^k x_i; retaining them in an implementation also costs only polynomial bits.

Univariate rational polynomial factorization is polynomial bit time by the classical Lenstra-Lenstra-Lovasz theorem. Rational roots, their multiplicities, (6), exact normalized score comparisons, orthogonality, and final substitution/LDL all have polynomial bit cost. In particular any rational root's numerator and denominator have polynomial bit length by the rational-root theorem after clearing denominators; no latent bound on L is assumed. Expected trial count is at most 4/3 for independent uniform sampling.

The implementation is a transparent exact SymPy replay, not an optimized bit-complexity benchmark. In particular, SymPy's `factor_list` backend is **not certified here to have polynomial worst-case bit complexity**. The theorem invokes standard polynomial-time implementations of rational linear algebra, polynomial arithmetic and univariate factorization; it does not infer asymptotic cost merely from SymPy timings. The original Lenstra-Lenstra-Lovasz 1982 primary scan, Introduction and Theorem 3.6 (PDF pages 1 and 18-19), was visually read and archived at `sources/proof_linear/lenstra_lenstra_lovasz1982.pdf`; the prerequisite's statement and stated bit bound were verified, not its entire proof re-audited.
