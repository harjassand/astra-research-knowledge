# Analytic audit of ASTRA full-cone quartic rigidity

## Verdict

The source note's main result is structurally coherent under its stated hypotheses. The crucial constraint for Theorem A is one indefinite quadratic balance, with rank-one directions exactly the null cone in the planar application. Theorem B is a nine-coefficient left-\(\mathrm{SO}(2)\) classification, not a symmetry-restricted test; Theorem C uses that classification to settle every exactly singular averaged-Hessian face. Theorem D rules out only a single fixed constant-coefficient uniformly strongly parabolic linear flow as a universal monotonicity rule.

The claims do not imply full nine-coefficient rank-one-convex \(\Rightarrow\) quasiconvex, do not classify nearly singular gradient sequences, and do not settle the positive-definite averaged-Hessian region. The finite exact checker supports algebraic identities; it does not formalize the measure, Fourier, or variational proofs.

## Reconstructed proof structure

### Theorem A: one-quadric nullspace closure

For a homogeneous quartic, polarization gives
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A].
\]
If \(q\) is indefinite and \(D^2F(A)[R,R]\ge0\) for \(q(R)=0\), the non-strict Finsler lemma supplies \(D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+\lambda_Aq(X)\), \(P_A\succeq0\). Averaging against \(\mathbb E q(X)=0\) yields \(H=\mathbb E D^2F(X)\succeq0\). If \(A\in\ker H\), then \(\mathbb E P_A(X)=0\), so the law is supported on \(U=\ker P_A\).

For \(R\in U\cap\{q=0\}\), the globally nonnegative quadratic \(t\mapsto D^2F(A+tY)[R,R]\) has a zero at \(t=0\), forcing \(D^2C_A(Y)[R,R]=0\), where \(C_A(X)=DF(X)[A]\). If \(q|_U\) is indefinite, a quadratic form vanishing on its null cone is proportional to \(q|_U\); symmetry of the cubic tensor forces that proportionality factor to vanish. Hence \(C_A|_U=0\). If \(q|_U\) is semidefinite, the balanced law supported on \(U\) lies in the radical of \(q|_U\); substituting its support points as the null direction in the preceding identity gives \(C_A(X)=0\) almost surely. This proves the pointwise support closure, not merely \(\mathbb E C_A=0\).

The Finsler lemma requires \(q\) indefinite. Here that holds for \(q=\det\) on \(2\times2\) matrices. The argument makes no gradient or Fourier assumption and applies to all 35 homogeneous quartic coefficients.

### Theorem B: full left-rotation cone and the exact exception

The full invariant quartic space has dimension nine. In complex coordinates \(z,w\) that acquire the same phase under left rotations, a complete basis is
\[
F=\alpha_0|z|^4+\beta_0|z|^2|w|^2+\gamma_0|w|^4+
\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\delta z^2\bar w^2+\epsilon|z|^2z\bar w+\eta|w|^2z\bar w\right).
\]
The exceptional class is
\[
\mathcal E=\left\{\gamma(\det A)^2+|\alpha x_C+\beta y_C|^4:
\gamma\ge0,\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C\right\}.
\]
Its members are polyconvex.

The key algebraic classification is: if for some nonzero \(A\),
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P(X)+\lambda\det X,\qquad P\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{rank}P\le1,
\]
then \(F\in\mathcal E\). When \(A\) has rank one, reciprocity makes \(D^2F(A)\succeq0\); the two Euler/rotation identities rule out \(F(A)>0\) at Hessian rank at most one. Convexity and a zero direction of the rank-one binary restriction reduce it, after an input-coordinate change, to a single fourth power. Two rank-one background Hessian tests then remove all odd coefficients and force the determinant-square coefficient nonnegative.

When \(A\) is invertible, normalize it to \(I\). Homogeneity and left rotation force the first two rows/columns of the Hessian. Comparing them with a rank-one PSD remainder produces three branches. The negative-value branch contradicts the exact cubic closure on an indefinite kernel. The positive branch leaves a \(|w|^4\) term and one cubic invariant; closure kills the cubic term, and rank-one convexity makes the remaining coefficient nonnegative. In the zero-value branch, the transverse Hessian is rank at most one; phase normalization followed by rank-one tests with both signs forces the mixed coefficient and the cubic invariant to vanish, leaving a nonnegative \(|w|^4\) term. These branches exhaust the complete nine-dimensional invariant basis.

Now let \(C=\mathbb E[XX^T]\succeq0\) satisfy the determinant balance \(\operatorname{tr}(J_{\det}C)=0\). If \(\operatorname{rank}C\ge3\) and \(H_F(C)\) is singular, choose \(A\ne0\) in its kernel. The Finsler remainder satisfies \(\operatorname{tr}(P_A C)=0\), so \(\operatorname{ran}C\subseteq\ker P_A\), hence \(\operatorname{rank}P_A\le1\). The classification forces \(F\in\mathcal E\). Conversely, each exceptional member has an independent triple of rank-one directions with singular summed Hessian; the source includes an actual resonant three-direction periodic gradient. Thus, outside \(\mathcal E\), every balanced second moment of rank at least three has strictly positive averaged Hessian.

### Theorem C: Jensen inequality on exactly singular faces

If \(H_F(\mathbb E[GG^T])\) is singular for a compatible gradient \(G=D\phi\), then either \(F\in\mathcal E\), which is already polyconvex, or the covariance has rank at most two by Theorem B. In the latter case \(G(x)\in L=\operatorname{ran}C\) almost everywhere for a fixed matrix subspace \(L\) of dimension at most two.

For any such subspace, the determinant on \(L\) is either identically zero, semidefinite, or an indefinite binary quadratic. In the first case all directions in \(L\) have rank one, so rank-one convexity gives convexity on the affine slice and Jensen applies. In the semidefinite case, zero mean determinant confines the gradient to the null line, again a rank-one-convex line. In the indefinite case the rank-one cone in \(L\) is two lines \(R_j=a_j\otimes b_j\), with independent input directions \(b_1,b_2\). Fourier compatibility gives \(\widehat G(k)=i\widehat\phi(k)\otimes k\); the real and imaginary parts share input direction \(k\). Therefore each nonzero Fourier coefficient lies on one of the two rank-one lines, and its frequency is parallel to that line's \(b_j\). The field is a sum of two one-dimensional profiles. The two independent integer phases push torus Haar measure to product Haar measure, so applying rank-one Jensen in each phase proves the full inequality. Zero-boundary fields follow by zero extension to a larger torus and density.

The conclusion covers arbitrary profiles and no fixed bandwidth, but only on the exact singular face. It does not prove any uniform lower bound for positive eigenvalues or pass automatically to limits of near-singular fields.

### Theorem D: fixed-linear-flow obstruction

For \(F_\eta(A)=(\det A)^2+\eta|A|^4\), \(\eta=1/10000\), the determinant-square and convex fourth-power terms make \(F_\eta\) polyconvex. The pointwise fourth-power identity in the source yields the zero-boundary strict estimate
\[
\int\!\left[F_\eta(A_0+D\phi)-F_\eta(A_0)\right]\ge
\frac{\eta}{3}\int|D\phi|^4.
\]

The explicit smooth potential in the source has exact heat derivatives \(\frac13\) for the determinant-square mean and \(-34621/81\) for the fourth-power mean, giving total derivative \(235379/810000>0\) at \(t=0\). Thus ordinary heat is not universally decreasing for this energy. If any fixed constant-coefficient uniformly strongly parabolic linear operator were universally decreasing, conjugating it by the output and square-torus dihedral symmetries and averaging would preserve the derivative inequality. Output averaging sends every real \(2\times2\) coefficient matrix (including its skew part) to a scalar identity; input averaging then gives a positive scalar multiple of the ordinary Laplacian. The exact heat increase contradicts that. The result says nothing against nonlinear or nonlocal flows, separate-energy methods, or polyconvex Jensen certificates.
