# Canonical quiver optimizer reduction and a certified lifting interface

Worker hessian_check12, cycle17, 7 October 2026. Sol/max handle verified by root; no nested launches. This is an independently reconstructed theorem and a conditional acquisition interface, not a claim that an arbitrary modular flag lifts or that a generic quiver optimizer has been implemented. Primary source and execution metadata are separate.

## Exact question and inherited interface

Let an integer quiver representation V have vertex dimensions alpha_v and integer arrow matrices M_a; arrows may include cycles and loops. Put A=sum_v alpha_v. For a field K write D_K for the attainable subrepresentation dimension vectors and T_K=conv(D_K). For a signed real weight sigma maximize sigma dot dim(W). Coordinate matrices are supplied densely; inactive isolated vertices must be accounted for explicitly rather than hiding binary dimension costs.

Cycle16 independently gated the following prior prerequisite: saturated lattices give D_Q subset D_Fp and hence T_Q subset T_Fp at every prime. Its random-prime arithmetic-Nullstellensatz event gives T_Q=T_Fp simultaneously for every weight with the stated failure probability, not equality of all profiles or all flags. We use that theorem as an inherited prerequisite; this cycle does not rerun its finite controls. Characteristic-zero T_Q=T_C is also inherited. Hirai2608.13972v1 implements a particular fractional-packing dual iteration; it is not by itself an oracle for arbitrary signed quiver support.

The new question is whether choosing a canonical optimizer converts this global polytope event into an actual modular-to-rational acquisition path. The answer below is yes, conditional on acquiring the modular support optimizer and charging its cost. Coordinate height remains a separate quantitative obligation.

## C1. Unique largest optimizer

The collection of subrepresentations is closed under vertexwise intersection and sum. Dimension modularity gives

    score(U)+score(W)=score(U intersection W)+score(U+W).

If U and W both maximize, each term on the right is at most the optimum, so both are maximizers. A maximizer of largest total dimension therefore contains every maximizer: its sum with one cannot increase dimension. Denote it W_K^+(sigma). This proves existence and uniqueness over any field; it does not require enumeration of infinitely many subspaces.

Its dimension vector k_K^+ is the coordinatewise largest point of the sigma-exposed face of T_K. Indeed every attainable point of that face is the dimension of a maximizing subrepresentation contained in W_K^+. Convex combinations remain coordinatewise at most k_K^+, which is itself in the face. Thus the face determines the largest optimizer's dimension vector even though a general face does not determine its constituent subspaces.

## C2. Canonical commutation at a good prime

Assume T_Q=T_Fp. C1 gives k_Q^+=k_Fp^+ for every signed real sigma. Intersect W_Q^+ with the ambient integer lattice vertexwise. This saturated lattice has the same rank and reduces to an Fp subrepresentation with that dimension vector. Its score equals the common support value. It is therefore contained in W_Fp^+ by C1; equal dimensions force equality at every vertex:

    reduction_p(W_Q^+(sigma)) = W_Fp^+(sigma).

This holds simultaneously for all sigma at the one global good-polytope event. It is stronger than support-value preservation but does not identify noncanonical optimizers, intermediate Hirai flags, or an arbitrary modular profile. In particular, it supplies a target only after the canonical selector has been applied.

For an integer sigma, the single tilted weight

    sigma'=(A+1)sigma+(1,...,1)

has W_K^+(sigma) as its unique optimizer. A non-sigma-maximizer has integer score at least one below the maximum; a change in total dimension is at most A, so it cannot win the tilted objective. Among sigma-maximizers, total dimension uniquely selects the largest. For rational sigma first clear its entire common denominator. The weight bit length grows by O(log A), whereas algorithms polynomial in numeric weight size still incur that numeric cost.

This canonical selector is standard prior in quiver stability: see Iwamasa–Oki–Soma2407.06493v3, Theorem3.5 and Section3.3, and its minimum-shrunk-space prerequisites. The commutation consequence of the cycle16 good-prime event is our reconstructed bridge; novelty and priority are unknown.

## C3. An any-prime deterministic acceptance certificate

Suppose a supplied finite-field oracle certifies an upper bound U on the sigma'-support of T_Fp. If a rational subrepresentation W is explicitly checked against every original integer arrow and sigma' dot dim(W)=U, then

    sigma' dot dim(W) <= support_Q(sigma')
                            <= support_Fp(sigma') <= U

forces equality. Thus W is the unique largest original-sigma optimizer over Q, even if p was a bad prime for other objectives. A particular prime is not certified good by this argument; the returned rational optimizer is certified optimal.

The support upper bound must be justified by an actual optimizer/dual-certificate implementation. A bare claimed number, an unchecked modular maximizer, a local full-rank Jacobian, or a Hirai packing bound for a different feasible set is not this oracle. The lifting module accepts U as a declared upstream assumption and marks this explicitly.

## C4. Rigidity of the canonical target

For W=W_K^+(sigma), any quotient I of W satisfies sigma(I)>=0: its kernel K is a subrepresentation of V and sigma(K)<=sigma(W). Conversely every nonzero subrepresentation I of V/W has sigma(I)<0. Its inverse image strictly contains W; a positive score increase contradicts optimality, and a zero increase contradicts maximality of W.

If a quiver morphism W -> V/W were nonzero, its image would be both sorts of I, a contradiction. Therefore

    Hom_quiver(W,V/W)=0.

To identify this with the chart Jacobian, choose pivot rows I_v for each W_v and represent W_v by an alpha_v by k_v matrix Y_v with (Y_v)_{I_v}=identity. The exact arrow-closure equations are

    M_a Y_tail - Y_head (M_a Y_tail)_{I_head}=0.

Only nonpivot rows at the head produce possibly nonzero equations. Their degree in the free entries of Y is at most two. A first-order variation of Y_v is a map W_v -> V_v/W_v. Linearizing the displayed equation says exactly that these maps commute with all induced arrows. Hence the common kernel of the full chart Jacobian is Hom_quiver(W,V/W), so its n=sum_v k_v(alpha_v-k_v) columns are independent at the canonical target.

This argument is characteristic-free. It is a proof of full-column rank for a canonical optimizer, not an optimizer algorithm or a proof for arbitrary modular points. At n=0 the condition is vacuous and all constant arrow residuals must still be tested.

## C5. Good-prime local chart and Newton lift

At the event in C2, choose each chart from an RREF pivot minor of W_Fp^+. In the saturated Q lattice that minor is a unit modulo p, so the Q target lies in this chart over Z_(p). Choosing a modular chart avoids an additional condition that a preselected rational RREF chart remain unchanged.

The full Jacobian modulo p has rank n by C4. Select n independent equation rows. For current chart coordinates x modulo p^s solve the square Newton step

    x_new = x - J_selected(x)^(-1) f_selected(x) modulo p^(2s).

The selected determinant remains a p-adic unit. The genuine Q target is a root of this selected system with the same modular coordinates. Hensel uniqueness therefore makes the Newton lift its reduction at every precision.

Crucial overdetermined-system gate: at a bad prime, an independent subset can have a unique p-adic root while the omitted equations are inconsistent. Check EVERY arrow equation after every doubling and again over Q before accepting. A full-column Jacobian does not supply a characteristic-zero point. The executable controls retain this failure instead of calling it a successful lift.

## C6. Rational reconstruction and costs

If target coordinates have reduced numerator and denominator at most H with denominators coprime to p, residues modulo M=p^s identify each coordinate uniquely once M>2H^2. The implementation may use a declared bound H, or try the symmetric reconstruction window floor(sqrt((M-1)/2)) and double until every exact rational equation and the C3 score certificate pass. A bounded run returns UNKNOWN when precision is exhausted; elapsed time is not an optimality certificate.

One good prime and p-adic doubling suffice; CRT or lifting arbitrary modular flags is unnecessary. Precision has O(log H) bits and O(log(log H/log p)) doublings above p. Each step uses square linear algebra over Z/p^s, not a field except modulo p: invert only known unit pivots. Basic dense elimination costs O(n^3) modular operations per doubling plus evaluating all quadratic closure equations. Rational reconstruction and checking original arrows add their explicit matrix and coordinate-height costs. The code counts declared modular conversion/add/sub/mul/div operations, times construction and lifting, and records maximal modulus bits; it does not equate primitive-call counts with total bit operations.

No polynomial bound in the original binary input height is claimed here for a general supplied modular optimizer. Exact quiver/NCR algorithms already give polynomial rational basis height in explicit matrix input for minimum shrunk subspaces; Iwamasa–Oki–Soma's acyclic canonical algorithm is polynomial in NUMERIC weight size. Those are serious priors and possible acquisition routes, but their expanded/path/operator oracles and numeric-weight costs must be acquired, not silently imported as a generic small executable.

The canonical fixed-dimension locus is a single Q point: any second rational subrepresentation with k_Q^+ would have the same score, be contained in W_Q^+, and hence equal it. Using inherited T_Q=T_C, the same holds over C. Together with C4 it is a reduced isolated point. This explains rigidity, but uniqueness alone is not a polynomial-height theorem; an effective height bound or an implemented structural oracle remains necessary for a binary-input complexity claim.

## Acquisition and remaining obstruction

Input acquisition pipeline: integer arrows and rational sigma -> tilted integer sigma' -> caller-certified prime + justified modular support optimizer/upper value -> canonical modular bases -> full-column chart Jacobian -> p-adic lift with all residuals -> rational reconstruction -> original-Q closure plus matching support score. Conditional on good-prime preservation and sufficient actual precision, the middle lifting stage terminates at the canonical rational optimizer. Unconditional acceptance uses C3.

The decisive improvement over scalar modular output is selection: the unique-largest rule makes an actual target commute with reduction. The unresolved practical bottlenecks are obtaining the generic modular support oracle, bounding or observing canonical coordinate height with complete costs, and scaling beyond the supplied controls. Finite lifting checks are evidence for the implementation, not proofs of a generic oracle, high-probability runtime, or independent external validation.
