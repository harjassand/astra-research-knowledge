Strongest conclusion

The proposed pair-number real-rootedness has a convincing exact reduction to an OLD, explicitly stated matrix theorem: Leake–Ravichandran, arXiv:1609.04195v2 (2018), Theorem 2.12, on expected characteristic polynomials under strongly Rayleigh principal-deletion measures. The potentially useful target-specific step is the two-clone representation of a soft onsite Gutzwiller penalty. The underlying Hermitian determinant, deletion, directional-derivative, and interlacing facts are not new.

The canonical sampling device also has unusually close prior art: Dereziński–Calandriello–Valko, arXiv:1905.13476 (NeurIPS 2019), Section 4/Theorem 4, rescales an ordinary DPP, then rejects all sizes except the requested size, with polynomially bounded rejection cost. The BCS consequence would be a target-specific composition of the cloning lemma, a recent imported counting theorem, that known tilt/rejection device, and a coherent preparation compiler. Neither “fugacity fixes number” nor “avoid rare original number projection by changing the grand-canonical ensemble” is new as a general algorithmic idea.

I did not find an earlier primary source explicitly stating the exact arbitrary-F, arbitrary-sitewise-t charge theorem below. That is LIMITED SEARCH COVERAGE, not evidence establishing novelty. The right current label is “internally derived specialized corollary of established stability theory; physical formulation and algorithmic specialization need specialist priority review.” The uniform preparation theorem remains conditional on Chen–Liu and the N63 finite-bit compiler, and presently covers t_i >= 1/2, not the hard-projection endpoint.

Exact target and quantifiers

Let F be any n-by-n complex matrix; symmetry is optional mathematically and gives the usual spin-singlet restriction physically. Define the unnormalized opposite-spin Gaussian state
  |BCS(F)> = exp(sum_ij F_ij u_i^dagger d_j^dagger)|vac>,
and let the onsite filter have amplitude g_i on double occupancy and amplitude 1 on the other three local states. Write t_i = |g_i|^2. The squared norm in the k-pair sector is
  Z_k = sum_{|U|=|D|=k} |det F[U,D]|^2 product_{i in U intersect D} t_i.
The proposed exact theorem is that
  Z(s) = sum_{k=0}^n Z_k s^k
has only negative finite zeros for ALL real t_i in [0,1]. Here s counts pairs; physical particle number is N=2k. Z_0=1, so zero itself is not a finite root. Degree can drop, especially at t=0; constant cases have the asserted property vacuously. With all t_i>0, the degree is rank(F).

Do not call Z(z^2), the physical-particle-number generating polynomial, real-rooted: even one nonzero pair gives 1+c z^2 with imaginary roots. Do not extend the theorem to arbitrary same-spin/Pfaffian Gaussian states or t_i>1 without proof.

Independent reconstruction of the exact reduction

Make two down-column clones for each site j:
  V_j = sqrt(1-t_j) F_j,  W_j = sqrt(t_j) F_j.
Let Ftilde=[V W], and form its 3n-by-3n Hermitian chiral matrix
  H0 = [[0, Ftilde], [Ftilde^*, 0]].
Scale the U and V vertices by sqrt(2), the W vertices by 1, obtaining H=C H0 C. Choose a retained principal set R by keeping exactly one of U_i,V_i for each i, uniformly and independently, and keeping every W_i. Thus |R|=2n in every case. Equivalently, the deletion-set polynomial is
  P_mu(y)=2^(-n) product_i(y_{U_i}+y_{V_i}),
which is real stable and defines a homogeneous strongly Rayleigh measure.

For equal-size U and cloned-down subsets E, the chiral principal minor is
  det H[U union E] = (-1)^k 2^(k+|E intersect V|) |det Ftilde[U,E]|^2.
It can survive the retained-set choice only when U and E intersect V use disjoint site indices. Its retention probability is exactly 2^(-(k+|E intersect V|)), cancelling the vertex factors. Selecting both clones of a column makes the minor zero because those columns are proportional. For j in D intersect U, only W_j is legal, contributing t_j. For j in D minus U, the sum of V_j and W_j contributions is (1-t_j)+t_j=1. Consequently
  Q(x) := E_R det(x I_(2n)-H[R])
        = sum_k (-1)^k Z_k x^(2n-2k)
        = x^(2n) Z(-1/x^2).

The determinant det(diag(y)-H) is real stable, and the nonnegative directional derivatives product_i(partial_U_i+partial_V_i) preserve stability. On the diagonal y=x it becomes Q(x), hence Q is real-rooted. A nonzero root of Z therefore must be negative. This proof uses a real-analytic matrix identity; the square roots do not themselves furnish a rational counting algorithm.

This is the same mechanism as the worker's clone identity, reconstructed with its probability/vertex cancellation stated explicitly. Those are finite checks of algebra/falsification, not a counting implementation or a proof of the imported algorithm.

Canonical sampling/prior state preparation

6. Dereziński–Calandriello–Valko, Exact sampling of determinantal point processes with sublinear time preprocessing, NeurIPS 2019.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13476
   Local: canonical_bcs_sources/1905.13476.html/.txt, Section 4/Theorem 4/Lemma 4, Appendix B/Proposition 5.
   The explicit reduction samples DPP(alpha L), accepts size k, and tunes alpha to make k a mode. Theorem 4 gives acceptance at least 1/(C sqrt(k)); the paper supplies a way to find alpha. Its analysis uses the Poisson-binomial cardinality law and Darroch's 1964 mode/mean rule. This is a direct prior for changing the ensemble to avoid the original rare size event. Its efficient tuning implementation uses the DPP spectrum/Nyström structure, which the filtered BCS law does not automatically provide.

Our exact root theorem gives Z(s)=product_{ell=1}^d(1+a_ell s), a_ell>0. Thus under tilt s>0, the PAIR COUNT is a sum of independent Bernoullis with probabilities a_ell s/(1+a_ell s), even though physical occupation configurations need not be a DPP. Ordinary log-concavity alone makes k a mode for s in [Z_(k-1)/Z_k, Z_k/Z_(k+1)] when these coefficients are positive, giving probability at least 1/(d+1). Roots need not be computed to use a norm/sampling oracle to tune a mean. Existing Darroch/Poisson-binomial concentration can sharpen this, but do not claim the sharpening as new. The worker's weaker near-integer-mean lower bound 3/[4(n+1)^2] is sufficient for polynomial rejection/amplification.

To retain the exact rational matrix interface, use a rational amplitude tilt F->qF (s=q^2), or explicitly charge finite-precision square-root approximation. Within a k sector every amplitude acquires q^k, so the normalized canonical state is unchanged. Required sample/preparation errors must be small relative to the new polynomial acceptance bound, and trials/amplification must be capped for an every-execution guarantee. Those refinements must be proved in the compiler, not inferred from an expected-runtime rejection statement. No inverse ORIGINAL sector weight is then necessary.

Exact scope/falsification checks (independent deductions)

* Physical strong-Rayleigh extension is FALSE: n=1, F=[1], t=1 gives physical occupation polynomial 1+x_up x_down, which vanishes at x_up=x_down=i. The charge polynomial 1+s is real-rooted. Thus only the charge law gets the latent Bernoulli factorization; generic negative dependence/mixing results for full strongly Rayleigh measures do not follow.
* Attraction extension is FALSE: n=2, F=[[0,1],[1,0]], t_1=t_2=T>1 gives Z(s)=1+2s+T^2 s^2, discriminant 4-4T^2<0. Repulsiveness is load-bearing.
* Hard t=0 is not an empty theorem: it gives real-rootedness for admissible no-double-occupancy pair sectors, but the legal prep region and imported oracle still exclude it.
* The charge theorem neither certifies arbitrary local VMC mixing nor proves exact efficient coefficient evaluation. Prior-art comparisons must maintain these separations.
