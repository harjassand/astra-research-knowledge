# Independent hostile audit: sparse rational-rate endotactic recognition

## Verdict

The claimed ordinary-endotacticity complexity theorem survives this audit after one omitted case is supplied: under the essential-source rule, `NON-ENDOTACTIC` is NP-complete and `ENDOTACTIC` is coNP-complete for the stated dimension-varying, explicitly sparse, positive rational generalized-polynomial input. The 3SAT map is polynomial-size and its universal UNSAT direction argument works in all three slices (z>0,z<0,z=0), including exact ties and neutral arrows. The `PROOF.md` account is incomplete as written in the (z>0) slice because it does not handle neutral clauses when claiming that every clause has a literal at least (z); the child `HARDNESS.md` contains the needed average argument. There is also a harmless guard-score typo: at the satisfying witness each variable's two guards have scores (-1) and (-3), not both (-1).

The result is about ordinary CRNT endotacticity (the weak/essential-source condition) of a sparse rational-rate presentation. It does **not** establish coNP-hardness of standard strong endotacticity. The child report's auxiliary “essential-top-face strong” label is a nonstandard condition and cannot be substituted for GMS strong endotacticity, which retains neutral sources in its additional global-source-max clause.

I found prior art on finite explicit reaction-network recognition and tropical analysis of polynomial/rational kinetics, but no source in the scoped primary search that states this succinct sparse-quotient recognition problem or its complexity. Novelty and priority therefore remain unknown. The broad input class permits rates singular at the boundary and unbounded; the reduction is not shown to lie in bounded rational, canonical Hill, or Michaelis–Menten syntax.

## Exact decision predicate and encoding

The input is a finite list of channels (e), each with a rational jump vector ν_e and rate (P_e/Q_e), where both supports are finite, nonempty, and explicitly listed with rational exponents and strictly positive rational coefficients. On a direction (w), set

\[
  \tau_e(w)=\max_{a\in A_e}a\cdot w-\max_{b\in B_e}b\cdot w,
  \qquad E(w)=\{e:\nu_e\cdot w\ne0\}.
\]

If (E(w)) is empty the test is vacuous. Otherwise, every channel in (E(w)) whose τ-score is maximal over (E(w)) must have ν_e·w<0. Thus an outward channel tied at the active maximum is a counterexample, while neutral channels are removed before taking that maximum. This is the RG-05 / standard essential-source convention. It agrees with the ordinary endotactic condition in CNP and GMS after choosing the maximum-score orientation; it differs from standard strong endotacticity as detailed below.

The common-denominator identity in RG-05 is algebraically sound for positive coefficients: the lifted support of channel (e) is (A_e+\sum_{f\ne e}B_f), and its support height is

\[
 h_{A_e}(w)+\sum_{f\ne e}h_{B_f}(w)
 =h_D(w)+\tau_e(w),\qquad D=\prod_f Q_f.
\]

The common offset preserves strict ordering and exact ties. On tied faces, positive leading coefficients add rather than cancel. This identifies the sparse score predicate with the expanded lift's source-score predicate, but it does not imply that the expanded graph has polynomial size.

## Reduction reconstruction

Take 3CNF with three distinct variables in each clause; this restricted 3SAT remains NP-complete. Use direction coordinates ((u_1,\ldots,u_n,z)). For each variable (i), each clause (C), and the target, define:

| Channel | Rate (P/Q) | Jump |
|---|---|---|
| (T) | (1) | (+e_z) |
| (V_i) | (X_iZ/(X_i^2+1)) | (-2e_z) |
| (C=(i_1,\sigma_1),(i_2,\sigma_2),(i_3,\sigma_3)) | (Z X_{i_1}X_{i_2}X_{i_3}/Q_C) | (\sum_{k=1}^3\sigma_k e_{i_k}-3e_z) |
| (G_i^+) | (X_i/Z^2) | (-e_i) |
| (G_i^-) | (1/(X_iZ^2)) | (+e_i) |

Here

\[
Q_C=\sum_{k:\sigma_k=+1}X_{i_k}^2\!\prod_{j\ne k}X_{i_j}
   +\sum_{k:\sigma_k=-1}\prod_{j\ne k}X_{i_j}.
\]

The rates are positive and denominators do not vanish for (X_i,Z>0). All coefficients are one, and a clause has three distinct denominator exponents, so this restricted reduction has at most three monomials on either side and coefficient one. There are (3n+m+1) channels in dimension (n+1); even with dense vector storage this is polynomial input size. Exponent and jump coordinates have constant bit size.

For a literal occurrence ((i,\sigma)), the associated denominator term has height 
\(\sum_{j\in C}u_j+\sigma u_i\), and the numerator has height (z+\sum_{j\in C}u_j\). Therefore, writing (S_C=\sum_{(i,\sigma)\in C}(\sigma u_i-z)), direct exact support evaluation gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \tau_e(w) & \nu_e\cdot w\\ \hline
T&0&z\\
V_i&z-|u_i|&-2z\\
C&z-\max_{(i,\sigma)\in C}\sigma u_i&S_C\\
G_i^+&u_i-2z&-u_i\\
G_i^-&-u_i-2z&u_i.
\end{array}
\]

The row formulas count literal **occurrences**. For repeated literals, denominator monomials can collide and their coefficients aggregate positively; the maximum is unchanged. Distinct variables are used in the NP-hardness restriction specifically to keep each displayed clause coefficient equal to one.

### SAT direction, including all ties

For a satisfying assignment set (z=1), (u_i=1) for true and (-1) for false. (T) is active, outward, and has score zero. Every (V_i) is active inward with score zero. For a satisfied clause with (r\in\{1,2,3\}) true literal occurrences, its score is zero and its projection is (2r-6\le0); when (r=3) it is exactly neutral and is excluded before maximizing. Thus an all-true clause does not interfere with the witness. The target remains an outward member of the exact active score-zero tie. Each pair of guards has scores (-1) and (-3), in one order or the other; both are strictly below the target. The target tie itself is the refutation, so no perturbation argument is being used.

### UNSAT converse for (z>0)

The target is active outward at score zero. All (V_i) are active inward. Any outward clause has (S_C>0), hence some literal has σu_i>z and its score (z-\max_C\sigma u_i) is strictly negative. (G_i^+) is outward only if (u_i<0), and (G_i^-) only if (u_i>0); either outward guard has score below zero. Therefore every outward active channel is below score zero.

If the target is itself at the active maximum, each variable score is at most zero, so (|u_i|\ge z>0) and the truth assignment given by the signs of the (u_i) is well-defined. For a clause with (S_C<0) that is active and no higher than the target, its score is at most zero, so σu_i\ge z for some literal. If (S_C>0), it would be outward with score below zero and hence cannot be a top tie at zero. **If (S_C=0), the clause is neutral and absent from the active comparison**; however,

\[
  \sum_{(i,\sigma)\in C}\sigma u_i=3z,
\]

so the largest of its three literal values is at least their average (z). Hence this neutral clause also contains a sign-true literal under the sign assignment. This omitted neutral-clause step is required to infer that every clause is satisfied. With it, a top target contradicts UNSAT.

If the target is not at the active maximum, the maximum is strictly positive. Since every outward active score is below zero, all active maximizers are inward. These two cases prove the required predicate for every (z>0) direction.

### UNSAT converse for (z<0)

The target is active inward at score zero. Each variable channel is active (its projection is (-2z>0)) and has score (z-|u_i|<0). An outward clause has (S_C>0), so some σu_i>z and its score is below zero. For an outward (G_i^+), (u_i<0), and the active inward twin (G_i^-) has score greater by (-2u_i>0); for an outward (G_i^-), (u_i>0), and (G_i^+) is its higher-scoring active inward twin. Thus no outward channel can tie the top. The inward target at zero also makes clear that an outward score below zero cannot be maximal.

### UNSAT converse for (z=0)

The target and variable channels are neutral. Put (H=\max_i|u_i|). If (H=0), every channel projection is zero and the condition is vacuous. If (H>0), a coordinate attaining (H) has a guard pointing inward at score (H). All guards have score at most (H), and each clause has score (-\max_C\sigma u_i\le H). An outward clause has (\sum_C\sigma u_i>0), hence (\max_C\sigma u_i>0), so its score is strictly below zero and below the top (H). An outward guard has score (-|u_i|\le0<H). Therefore every active top channel is inward. This explicitly closes the (z=0) hyperplane; a proof restricted to Boolean directions would not suffice.

Together, SAT iff the constructed channel system is not endotactic, giving (3\mathrm{SAT}\le_m\textsf{NON-ENDOTACTIC}). The reduction plus the NP witness argument below yields the claimed completeness.

## Polynomial-bit witness and coNP upper bound

A failure is an outward channel (e) and a direction (w) with ν_e·w>0 and τ_e(w)\ge\u03c4_f(w) for every nonneutral (f). Start with any real failure direction. Fix (i) the positive/negative/zero branch of each ν_f·w and (ii) one maximizing exponent in each numerator and denominator support at that direction. These finite choices make all relevant score comparisons weak rational linear inequalities; neutral labels are equalities and active signs are strict inequalities. No tie is dropped.

Normalize one coordinate that attains ‖w‖∞ to (+1) or (-1), and constrain all coordinates to ([-1,1]). Add a variable δ with (0\le\delta\le1), require the outward witness projection and every branch-declared nonzero projection to be at least δ in its selected signed orientation. The original witness gives a feasible point with δ>0. The resulting rational polytope is bounded, so maximizing δ attains its optimum at a rational basic feasible point. Its bit length is polynomial in the explicit input length by standard determinant bounds for rational linear systems. Thus every real failure has a polynomial-bit rational witness.

Given that witness, exact dot products and support scans verify all scores, neutral labels, and ties in polynomial bit complexity. Hence `NON-ENDOTACTIC` is in NP and `ENDOTACTIC` in coNP. The reduction proves matching hardness. This argument is for dimension supplied as part of the input; it is consistent with an (H^{O(d)}) arrangement algorithm for fixed (d), and it does not establish an FPT bound.

## Standard strong-endotacticity boundary

The reduction proves only the ordinary essential-source predicate. The standard GMS definition adds: for every direction not orthogonal to the stoichiometric subspace, there must be an inward reaction at the **global maximum among all sources**. Neutral reactions are omitted for the ordinary endotactic clause, but neutral source channels are retained for this additional strong clause.

The supplied all-eight-sign-clauses UNSAT formula on three variables is an exact boundary fixture. At (w=(0,0,0,-1)), (T) is inward at score 0; variables and clauses are outward but below 0; both guards for each variable are neutral at score 2. Thus the weak active maximum is inward, but the global all-source maximum consists only of neutral guards. Since (w\cdot e_z\ne0), the standard strong condition fails. The independent `Fraction` verifier reproduces this witness. It falsifies any inference that the ordinary coNP-hardness construction is standard-strong hard.

The child hardness note also says this reduction passes an “essential-top-face strong” test that maximizes only over active reactions. In the form stated there, “every active top is inward and some active reaction exists” is just ordinary endotacticity: a finite nonempty active set has a top element. That is not Definition 3.14(4) of GMS. The formal RG-05 essential-source convention is ordinary endotacticity, not this auxiliary label and not standard strong endotacticity.

## What rates and networks are actually covered

The input model is finite positive (P/Q) with rational supports and coefficients, with the reaction vector separately supplied for each channel. Positivity guarantees no denominator pole in the **open** positive orthant. It does not require the rates to stay bounded near the boundary or at infinity, nor to satisfy a standard kinetic source/product convention. The reduction uses (X_i/Z^2) and (1/(X_iZ^2)); they diverge as (Z\downarrow0) (at fixed positive (X_i)), and are also unbounded in other regimes. Thus the proof does not establish hardness for globally bounded rational rates or canonical saturating Hill/Michaelis–Menten syntax.

The integer supports and jumps in this construction do admit a legal nonnegative-complex mass-action diagram after the RG-05 common-denominator lift: for each (V_i) or clause arrow, the other guard denominators contribute at least (4n) powers of (Z), exceeding the (-2e_z) or (-3e_z) jump; any negative clause coordinate has at least one corresponding (X_i) factor in its numerator; and each (G_i^+) numerator has its (-e_i) source factor. The other coordinates of these sources and all products are nonnegative. But this is an **implicit** rational representation of the expanded diagram, whose Cartesian denominator-support expansion can be exponential. The coNP-completeness claim is not a polynomial-size reduction to an explicitly listed mass-action graph.

Canonical Hill/MM families remain unproved either way. Their bounded/saturating structure may rule out this reduction or admit a more efficient specialized recognizer; neither follows from the broad-class theorem. The same restraint applies to globally bounded rational rates, boundary-regular rates, and additional mass-action-compatible syntax.

## Primary-literature collision and boundary

The sources checked are primary papers, not secondary summaries:

* Craciun–Nazarov–Pantea define the essential subnetwork by retaining reactions whose vector has nonzero projection and give the standard endotactic support test (2011, Definitions 13–14). Their paper proves two-species permanence, not the sparse rational-support recognition complexity here.
* Gopalkrishnan–Miller–Shiu (2013), Definitions 3.9 and 3.14, state ordinary endotacticity via (w)-essential reactions, and standard strong endotacticity with an inward reaction at the all-reactant global maximum. This verifies the strong/weak distinction.
* Johnston–Pantea–Donnell (2014) give MILP recognition for finite, explicit chemical reaction diagrams, including ordinary and strong endotacticity. This is a close algorithmic baseline for the fully materialized lifted graph, but their input is an explicit complex/reaction diagram, not the succinct quotient-support encoding. Their MILP paper does not supply a complexity classification for the latter.
* Samal et al. (2015) treat tropical equilibrations for polynomial or rational rate functions by balancing dominant monomials of opposite signs in ODE components. That is a neighboring tropical-analysis task, not the universal active-source/endotactic predicate audited here.

Targeted primary-source searches for sparse rational-rate endotactic recognition and NP/coNP complexity found no exact collision. This is not an exhaustive literature review; the correct status is **priority unknown**, not novel. The C8 finite tests and exact SMT backend validate fixtures and implementation paths only; they are not proof of the universal implication, and solver UNSAT answers lack a separate proof-checking artifact.
