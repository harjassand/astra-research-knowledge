UNKNOWN BASIS / UNKNOWN RANK: PREFIX-ONLY DISCOVERY
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: conditional algorithm plus impossibility boundary; standard Hankel realization machinery, no novelty or priority claim.

Question
Can one acquire a predictive basis and discover its dimension when neither the feature basis nor the rank is provided? The answer is yes with a finite witness-horizon promise and a separated singular spectrum, but that promise is load-bearing. With only finite-rank membership/prefix access and no rank cap, witness horizon, or equivalence/counterexample oracle, no finite stopping certificate for all future predictions exists.

1. Legal interface and target

Fix a finite alphabet Sigma, |Sigma|=q>=2. Let f(w)=Pr[X_1...X_|w|=w] be the probability of seeing word w as an initial prefix; f(empty)=1 and sum_a f(wa)=f(w). The target is a finite-dimensional linear predictive process: its infinite Hankel matrix

  H_f[u,v] = f(uv),    u,v in Sigma*,

has finite real rank r. Equivalently, f has a representation f(w)=lambda A_w rho with r-dimensional matrices; the minimal possible representation dimension is r.

The prefix-probability oracle PQ(w, eta) returns a number f_tilde(w) with absolute error at most eta. A query names only an observable word; it separately reveals no hidden state, rank, basis, conditional-law oracle answer, or equivalence answer. The exact rational subcase returns f(w) exactly as a reduced rational. A legal sampling alternative is an iid trajectory oracle that returns independent strings of a chosen common length T from the same initial process distribution. These are stronger than one unresettable dependent stream; a single-stream version needs separate mixing assumptions.

A conditional certificate needs a declared witness horizon L. Write W_L=Sigma^{<=L} and N_L=|W_L|=(q^(L+1)-1)/(q-1). Promise C_L says that the full row space and full column space of H_f are generated by prefixes and suffixes in W_L. This supplies neither r nor a feature basis. One sufficient way to obtain C_L is a declared upper bound R on r and L>=R-1: in a minimal r-state representation, the reachable row spaces generated by words of lengths at most ell increase monotonically and, once one step stops increasing, are invariant under every symbol matrix; they can increase at most r-1 times. The dual argument applies to columns. Thus an upper bound on rank is one way to supply a finite horizon, but it is still a real assumption. C_L itself is not checkable from a finite prefix transcript.

The spectral promise is sigma_r(H_L)>=gamma>0 and sigma_(r+1)(H_L)=0, where H_L is H_f restricted to W_L x W_L. Under C_L, rank(H_L)=r. The gap gamma is a numerical identifiability condition, separate from the horizon promise. For stable coefficient recovery from a particular word-indexed minor X=H_f[P,S], also require or measure sigma_min(X)>=lambda>0; gamma for the full block alone does not guarantee that an arbitrary pivot minor is well-conditioned.

2. Adaptive expansion and finite stopping certificate

Expand by word length, starting at W_0={empty}. At level ell, add all length-ell prefixes and suffixes, query the new concatenated words needed for the Hankel table, and update rank-revealing row/column QR or SVD factors. In exact mode, add a direction exactly when it is linearly independent; in noisy mode, use residuals to prioritize basis updates but retain every candidate word for the final rank check. No dimension is set in advance. Intermediate rank plateaus are provisional; continue through L, because a later prefix may create a new direction.

At L form H_L. Also form, for each a in Sigma, the one-symbol-shift block

  S_a[u,v] = f(u a v),    u,v in W_L.

Select word sets P,S subset W_L of size d with X=H_L[P,S] nonsingular. The exact-oracle implementation can find d by rational elimination; the noisy implementation can use the separated singular values described below, then choose a sufficiently conditioned word minor or retain an SVD coordinate basis. Let

  Y_a = S_a[P,S] = (f(p a s))_(p in P,s in S),
  alpha = H_L[empty,S] X^(-1),
  A_a = X^(-1) Y_a,
  rho = H_L[P,empty].

Then for every word w=a_1...a_t,

  f(w) = alpha A_(a_1)...A_(a_t) rho.

Proof: by C_L, the selected rows indexed by P span every Hankel row and the selected columns indexed by S span every Hankel column. Restricting to S therefore gives coordinates c_u=H_f[u,S]X^(-1) for each prefix row, and the restriction is injective on the row space. Right extension by a symbol is a well-defined linear map on the full row space. Its matrix in the P coordinates is A_a, since H_f[P a,S]=X A_a. The empty-prefix coordinate is alpha. Evaluating each row at the empty suffix gives rho, so c_(u a)=c_u A_a and f(u)=c_u rho. Induction proves the formula for all lengths. Only entries f(w) with |w|<=2L+1 are needed.

The stopping certificate is the tuple (L,d,gamma, the finite table, P,S, X, {Y_a}, alpha, {A_a}, rho), with the observed residual and singular-value interval. Under C_L, the gap promise, and exact oracle values, it certifies the exact minimal rank and every future prefix probability. With noisy values, if every queried f(w) has error <=eta, then ||H_tilde_L-H_L||_2 <= ||.||_F <= N_L eta. If N_L eta<=gamma/4, Weyl's inequality gives sigma_d(H_tilde_L)>=3gamma/4 and sigma_(d+1)(H_tilde_L)<=gamma/4. Thresholding at gamma/2 recovers d exactly. This is a proved rank stopping test, not a data-derived proof of C_L: the infinite-horizon conclusion remains conditional on that promise. If the rank gap is not resolved, return UNKNOWN rather than stopping at the apparent rank.

The SVD construction gives an alternative basis without selecting word pivots: if H_L=U Sigma V^T at rank d, set X=U Sigma^(1/2), Y=Sigma^(1/2)V^T, alpha=X[empty,:], rho=Y[:,empty], and A_a=Sigma^(-1/2) U^T S_a V Sigma^(-1/2). It yields the same realization up to a change of coordinates. In finite precision, coefficient/prediction accuracy additionally depends on the stability of the factorization and the growth of products A_w; the rank gap by itself is not an all-horizons numerical error bound.

3. Query, arithmetic, and coefficient-bit costs

Let M_(2L+1)=|Sigma^{<=2L+1}|=(q^(2L+2)-1)/(q-1). A cached prefix oracle needs at most M_(2L+1) distinct word queries to build H_L, all q shift blocks, and the readouts; entries repeat because many u,a,v factorizations name the same word. If charged per table cell rather than per distinct word, the direct dense construction uses at most (q+1)N_L^2 scalar entries. Each eta-precision scalar reply takes O(log(1/eta)) bits because probabilities lie in [0,1], so the oracle reply volume is O(M_(2L+1) log(1/eta)) bits. This is exponential in L in the worst case; adaptivity removes the supplied rank/basis requirement, not the search-depth cost or its worst-case branching.

Dense exact rank revelation on H_L costs O(N_L^3) field operations; computing the q transitions from selected d x d blocks costs O(q d^3) more, plus O(d^2) for the readouts. A thin SVD route costs O(N_L^3) standard arithmetic operations for a dense table and O(q N_L^2 d) for the projected shift blocks. Store H_L in O(N_L^2) scalars, or stream the rank update if query values are cached externally. These are arithmetic-operation counts, not bit-operation counts.

For exact rational queries, suppose every queried probability has numerator and denominator at most B bits. Exact rational elimination has polynomial bit complexity in N_L and B; a coarse common-denominator bound gives intermediate determinant integers of O(N_L^3 B + N_L log N_L) bits. Each output coefficient from an d x d word minor is a quotient of determinants and has a conservative O(d^3 B + d log d) bit bound. Thus storing alpha, rho, and q transition matrices takes at most O((q d^2+2d)(d^3 B+d log d)) output bits. The exact coefficient magnitude can still be large; the lambda condition is what controls numerical stability, not this rational bit-count statement.

For additive-noise queries, ||X_tilde-X||_2<=d eta and ||Y_tilde_a-Y_a||_2<=d eta. If sigma_min(X)>=lambda and d eta<=lambda/2, then ||X_tilde^(-1)||_2<=2/lambda and

  ||A_tilde_a-A_a||_2 <= 2d eta/lambda + 2d^2 eta/lambda^2,
  ||alpha_tilde-alpha||_2 <= 2 sqrt(d) eta/lambda + 2 d^(3/2) eta/lambda^2,
  ||rho_tilde-rho||_2 <= sqrt(d) eta.

In an implementation, an observed sigma_min(X_tilde)>=lambda+d eta certifies the premise sigma_min(X)>=lambda. These follow from the inverse perturbation identity; use them with a finite prediction horizon T. For a0=||alpha||, b0=||rho||, m=max_a||A_a||, eA=max_a||A_tilde_a-A_a||, ealpha and erho the other two errors, the length-t error is bounded by

  ealpha (m+eA)^t (b0+erho)
  + a0 t eA (m+eA)^(t-1) (b0+erho)
  + a0 m^t erho.

So a requested finite-horizon tolerance determines eta; without product-stability or a finite T, no uniform numerical prediction bound follows. If returned coefficients are rounded to b fractional bits, replace ealpha,eA,erho by ealpha+sqrt(d)2^-b, eA+d2^-b, erho+sqrt(d)2^-b. A sufficient b is the smallest integer making the displayed T-horizon bound at most the remaining error budget. The coefficient payload is O((q d^2+2d)b) fractional bits, plus sign and integer-range bits for the measured coefficient magnitudes.

For iid trajectories of length 2L+1, each prefix indicator is in [0,1]. Estimating all M_(2L+1) word probabilities to additive eta by empirical frequencies and a union bound takes

  n_train >= (1/(2 eta^2)) log(2 M_(2L+1)/delta)

independent trajectories with probability at least 1-delta. Setting eta=gamma/(4N_L) gives n_train >= 8 N_L^2 gamma^(-2) log(2M_(2L+1)/delta). The symbol-observation cost is n_train(2L+1); updating all prefix counters costs O(n_train(2L+1)) time and O(M_(2L+1) log n_train) counter bits. This guarantees rank selection; it does not alone certify that the recovered model predicts all longer futures accurately.

A separate held-out finite-horizon check can certify the learned length-T distribution. Put K=q^T, draw n_val independent validation trajectories, and let p_emp be their empirical length-T distribution. Coordinate Hoeffding plus a union bound gives, with probability >=1-delta_val,

  ||p_emp-p||_1 <= K sqrt(log(2K/delta_val)/(2 n_val)).

Thus if the candidate model is a valid distribution and ||p_emp-p_model||_1<=epsilon_obs, then ||p-p_model||_1<=epsilon_obs+epsilon_val except with probability delta_val, provided n_val >= K^2 log(2K/delta_val)/(2 epsilon_val^2). Computing this comparison costs O(K T d^2) model arithmetic and O(K) count storage; the bound is deliberately simple and can be improved with sharper multinomial concentration. The validation observation cost is n_val T symbols. This validates one finite horizon only. Exact infinite-horizon correctness comes from the exact oracle plus C_L, not from holdout agreement.

4. What fails when assumptions fail

No finite stopping with no witness-horizon/rank cap: run any finite adaptive prefix-query algorithm against the deterministic all-zero stream. Let m be the longest queried word. Choose T>m and compare it with the deterministic finite-state clock stream 0^T 1 1 1... . The two processes answer every query in the transcript identically, because their first m symbols are all zero, but their next-symbol predictions after history 0^T differ. Both have finite-dimensional linear representations; the clock process uses finitely many states, with the number allowed to grow with T. Therefore any algorithm that halts on this transcript gives the same output for both and must be wrong on at least one for an all-future claim. A flat singular spectrum/rank on the explored prefix block is not a stopping certificate. This counterexample is removed by a genuine finite witness-horizon or dimension-cap promise, or by a counterexample/equivalence oracle; it is not removed by observing a finite rank plateau.

No uniform sample guarantee without a gap: compare P0, an iid fair bit process of Hankel rank 1, with P_delta, which first chooses Z uniformly from {-1,+1} and then emits iid bits with conditional Pr[1|Z]=1/2+Z delta. For delta>0 the latter has rank 2. On rows/columns {empty,0,1}, its Hankel block has singular values 3/2, 2 delta^2, 0, so the second direction has gamma=2 delta^2. Its length-two outcome probabilities are 1/4+delta^2 on 00,11 and 1/4-delta^2 on 01,10, versus 1/4 each under P0. The KL divergence of one independent length-two trajectory from P_delta to P0 is 8 delta^4+O(delta^8); hence Le Cam/Pinsker gives an Omega(delta^-4)=Omega(gamma^-2) number of independent length-two trajectories for constant-error rank-1-versus-rank-2 discrimination. For an additive-eta prefix oracle, the value p(00) differs by only delta^2; eta>=delta^2 can erase the distinguishing signal. The gamma dependence in the upper bound is therefore necessary in order, even in this two-state example.

If the selected word minor is ill-conditioned, exact rational reconstruction remains defined but small oracle errors are amplified by X^(-1). The lambda requirement or the SVD coordinate route is essential for finite-precision coefficients. Even with stable discovery, long products can amplify coefficient errors unless a finite horizon or a separate product-stability assumption is charged.

5. Source checks and scope

- Fliess, “Séries rationnelles positives et processus stochastiques” (1975), Theorem 2.2 defines the Hankel matrix on all words and gives finite Hankel rank iff a rational/linear representation exists; the proof explicitly builds a representation from a nonsingular minor. This is the exact algebraic foundation used here: [Numdam article and full text](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPB_1975__11_1_1_0/).
- Ho and Kalman, “Effective construction of linear state-variable models from input/output functions” (1966), is the classical Markov-parameter/minimal-realization precedent: [NASA Technical Reports Server record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19670049337). It motivates finite-block realization, but does not supply this task's query interface or remove the need for enough reachability/observability depth.
- Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models” (COLT 2009; JCSS 2012) estimate observable marginal tables from iid sequences, assume the HMM rank condition, and state their routine as LEARNHMM(m,N). They note m may be tuned by cross-validation in practice, while the stated proof uses singular-value conditions. This is a useful acquired-observable comparison, not a proof of a prefix-only, infinite-horizon rank stopping certificate: [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413), [COLT PDF](https://www.learningtheory.org/colt2009/papers/011.pdf).
- Kaznatcheev and Panangaden, “Weighted automata are compact and actively learnable” (2021), use the minimal-adequate-teacher model with membership queries and counterexamples/equivalence queries. That extra teacher channel can certify a candidate in ways prefix membership alone cannot: [author-hosted paper PDF](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~prakash/Pubs/ipl_paper.pdf), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10498).

Decision: conditional advance is the finite-horizon-capped, gap-separated discovery method above. The open interface-level gap is a stopping rule that discovers its own witness horizon from prefix/trajectory access alone with no dimension cap or counterexample oracle. Do not label the conditional construction as a canonical teacher-free solution or a novelty result.
