1. Exact global theorem

Let m >= 2. There is a constant C_m, depending only on m, with the following
property. Let sigma be a nonnegative Radon measure on R^m and A >= 0, and assume

  sigma(B_r(x)) <= A r^(m-1)             for every x in R^m and every r > 0. (1.1)

For every real or complex u in C_c^infinity(R^m), define the extended positive
integral

  J_sigma(u) = int_Rm sigma(dx) int_Rm
                 |u(y)-u(x)|^2 / |x-y|^(m+1) dy.                          (1.2)

The value assigned on x=y is irrelevant: for each x the Lebesgue y-measure of
that point is zero. Then

  J_sigma(u) <= C_m A int_Rm |grad u|^2 dy.                                (1.3)

In particular the integral is finite. Finiteness of sigma is not needed;
finite sigma, as in the assigned question, is included. There is no lower
density, doubling, rectifiability, uniform rectifiability, or supporting-plane
assumption. No extra L^2 term is needed under the global hypothesis (1.1).

We prove the following slightly more general estimate, in the same argument:
if A_1,A_0 >= 0 and

  sigma(B_r(x)) <= A_1 r^(m-1) + A_0 r^m          for every x and r > 0,     (1.4)

then

  J_sigma(u) <= C_m [ A_1 ||grad u||_2^2 + A_0 ||u||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2 ].     (1.5)

Our Fourier convention is unitary:
  uhat(xi) = (2pi)^(-m/2) int exp(-i x.xi) u(x) dx,
  ||u||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2 = int |xi| |uhat(xi)|^2 dxi.
The constants in (1.3)-(1.5) are independent of sigma, its mass, u, and scales.
Balls may be taken open. Under either global growth hypothesis, the same
bound for a closed ball follows by decreasing larger open radii to its
radius. This justifies shell endpoint conventions even if sigma charges
spheres. The local version below uses genuine covers by open balls.

2. Homogeneous dyadic decomposition with specified kernels

Fix a real, even, radial, radially nonincreasing chi in C_c^infinity(R^m), with
0 <= chi <= 1, chi=1 on B_1, and chi=0 outside B_2. All choices in this proof
are fixed once m is fixed. Set

  phi(xi) = chi(xi) - chi(2xi),
  u_j = F^(-1)[phi(2^(-j) xi) uhat(xi)],                         j in Z.    (2.1)

The Fourier support of u_j is contained in
  { 2^(j-1) <= |xi| <= 2^(j+1) }.
Choose a smooth compactly supported multiplier psi that equals 1 on
{1/2 <= |xi| <= 2}. Let

  kappa(x) = (2pi)^(-m) int exp(i x.xi) psi(xi) dxi,
  kappa_j(x) = 2^(jm) kappa(2^j x).

Thus ordinary convolution kappa_j*u_j has the multiplier psi(2^(-j) xi),
and

  u_j = kappa_j*u_j.                                                       (2.2)

Fix an integer M > m, for example M=2m+4, and define

  eta_j(x) = 2^(jm) (1+2^j |x|)^(-M).

Schwartz decay of kappa and its derivatives implies

  |kappa_j(x)| <= C_m eta_j(x),
  int |kappa_j(x)| dx <= C_m,
  int |grad kappa_j(x)| dx <= C_m 2^j.                                     (2.3)

Cauchy-Schwarz against the positive measure |kappa_j(x-y)|dy in (2.2) gives

  |u_j(x)|^2 <= C_m (eta_j*|u_j|^2)(x).                                    (2.4)

For any h with |h| <= 2^(-j), the elementary inequality
  1+2^j|x-y| <= 2(1+2^j|x+h-y|)
also gives

  |grad kappa_j(x+h-y)| <= C_m 2^j eta_j(x-y).

Apply weighted Cauchy-Schwarz to each derivative (or directly to the vector
kernel); using the third bound in (2.3), obtain the pointwise uniform estimate

  sup_(|h|<=2^(-j)) |grad u_j(x+h)|^2
       <= C_m 2^(2j) (eta_j*|u_j|^2)(x).                                  (2.5)

The supremum is legitimate because the same positive convolution on the
right controls every h. This step does not invoke a maximal function theorem
or assume a trace estimate for arbitrary functions.

3. Exact use of upper ball growth

For every y in R^m, (1.4) and a dyadic annulus calculation show

  int eta_j(x-y) sigma(dx) <= C_m (A_1 2^j + A_0).                          (3.1)

Indeed put t=2^(-j). On B_t(y) the integrand is at most t^(-m), while the
mass is at most A_1 t^(m-1)+A_0 t^m. On the annulus
2^ell t < |x-y| <= 2^(ell+1)t, ell>=0, the integrand is at most
C_m t^(-m) 2^(-M ell), and the mass is at most
A_1 (2^(ell+1)t)^(m-1)+A_0(2^(ell+1)t)^m. The resulting two geometric series
converge because M>m. This proves (3.1), also for infinite sigma.

Tonelli applied to (2.4)-(2.5) and (3.1) now proves

  int |u_j|^2 d sigma <= C_m (A_1 2^j + A_0) ||u_j||_2^2,                  (3.2)

  int sup_(|h|<=2^(-j)) |grad u_j(x+h)|^2 sigma(dx)
       <= C_m 2^(2j)(A_1 2^j + A_0)||u_j||_2^2.                           (3.3)

These are frequency-localized bounds derived from the stated upper growth.
They do not imply the desired mixed bound by themselves; the spatial shell
decay in the next step is the additional required mechanism.

4. Mixed shell estimate: both sides of the frequency scale

For k in Z put r_k=2^(-k) and define

  E_k(v) = int sigma(dx) int_(r_k/2<|x-y|<=r_k)
                         |v(y)-v(x)|^2 / |x-y|^(m+1) dy.                 (4.1)

By Tonelli, J_sigma(v)=sum_k E_k(v) for every measurable v.
Write omega_(m-1) for the surface area of the unit sphere in R^m. Precisely,

  int_(r/2<|h|<=r) |h|^(-m-1) dh = omega_(m-1)/r.                        (4.2)

Define

  b_j^2 = (A_1 2^(2j)+A_0 2^j) ||u_j||_2^2.

We claim

  E_k(u_j) <= C_m b_j^2 2^(-|j-k|)                  for every j,k in Z.     (4.3)

Small shells: j<=k means r_k<=2^(-j). For |h|<=r_k, the fundamental theorem
of calculus along the segment gives
  |u_j(x+h)-u_j(x)| <= r_k sup_(|v|<=r_k)|grad u_j(x+v)|.
Use (4.2), then (3.3). The result is

  E_k(u_j) <= C_m r_k 2^(2j)(A_1 2^j+A_0)||u_j||_2^2
             = C_m b_j^2 2^(j-k).                                        (4.4)

Large shells: j>=k means r_k>=2^(-j). Use
  |u_j(y)-u_j(x)|^2 <= 2|u_j(x)|^2+2|u_j(y)|^2.
The x term, by (4.2) and (3.2), is at most
  C_m r_k^(-1)(A_1 2^j+A_0)||u_j||_2^2.
For the y term use the upper kernel bound C_m r_k^(-m-1), reverse the order
of integration, and use (1.4) for B_(r_k)(y). Its value is at most
  C_m (A_1 r_k^(-2)+A_0 r_k^(-1))||u_j||_2^2.
Since r_k>=2^(-j), r_k^(-2)<=2^j/r_k, so the sum of both terms is at most

  C_m [A_1 2^(2j)+A_0 2^j]||u_j||_2^2 2^(k-j).                           (4.5)

Equations (4.4)-(4.5) prove (4.3). Both the source endpoint u_j(x) and the
bulk endpoint u_j(y) have been charged; discarding either is unnecessary.

5. Summing frequencies in the actual mixed norm

Let I be any finite subset of Z and v=sum_(j in I)u_j. For fixed k, E_k(v)
is a squared L^2 norm on the positive pair measure in (4.1). Its triangle
inequality and (4.3) give

  sqrt(E_k(v)) <= C_m sum_(j in I) b_j 2^(-|j-k|/2).                       (5.1)

Put K(h)=2^(-|h|/2). The sequence convolution estimate needed here follows
by Cauchy-Schwarz and Tonelli:

  (sum_j K(k-j)b_j)^2
       <= (sum_j K(k-j)) (sum_j K(k-j)b_j^2),
  sum_k (sum_j K(k-j)b_j)^2 <= ||K||_(ell^1)^2 sum_j b_j^2.

In fact ||K||_(ell^1)=3+2sqrt(2), so its square is 17+12sqrt(2). Thus

  J_sigma(v) <= C_m sum_(j in I)
                   [A_1 2^(2j)+A_0 2^j] ||u_j||_2^2.                     (5.2)

There is no frequency orthogonality assertion in L^2(sigma). The summable
off-diagonal factor from actual physical shells supplies the entire missing
almost orthogonality. The triangle inequality is used before the shell sum,
where its sequence convolution cost is finite.

Ordinary Plancherel and the finite overlap of the annuli in (2.1) give

  sum_j 2^(2j)||u_j||_2^2 <= C_m int |xi|^2 |uhat(xi)|^2 dxi,
  sum_j 2^j ||u_j||_2^2 <= C_m int |xi| |uhat(xi)|^2 dxi.                  (5.3)

To see this directly, at any nonzero xi only a bounded number of phi(2^-j xi)
are nonzero, each has absolute value at most 1, and each corresponding 2^j
is between fixed multiples of |xi|. No measure-specific result enters (5.3).

6. Low-frequency convergence and the exact u, with no trace ambiguity

For L>=1 let v_L=sum_(j=-L)^L u_j. Telescoping gives the multiplier

  vhat_L(xi) = [chi(2^(-L)xi)-chi(2^(L+1)xi)] uhat(xi).                  (6.1)

It tends to uhat(xi) for every xi!=0, and its bracket lies between 0 and 1.
Since u is compactly supported and smooth, uhat is in L^1. Dominated
convergence in Fourier inversion shows

  sup_(x in R^m) |v_L(x)-u(x)| -> 0.                                      (6.2)

The single frequency xi=0 has Lebesgue measure zero and creates no residual
constant. Consequently the pairwise increments converge at every x,y.
Fatou for the positive integral (1.2), combined with (5.2)-(5.3), proves (1.5).
Setting A_1=A,A_0=0 proves (1.3). This completes the global theorem.

All v_L have finite mixed norm by the estimates already proved. No change of
u on sigma-null or Lebesgue-null sets is made. The actual smooth value u(x)
is used throughout, so no unspecified Sobolev trace representative is needed.
Radon sigma is sigma-finite by compact exhaustion, which suffices for all
applications of Tonelli/Fatou. For A=0, (1.1) implies sigma=0 and the result
is immediate; the general proof also covers that case.

7. Growth below a fixed cutoff: exact low-frequency accounting

Suppose instead only that

  sigma(B_r(x)) <= A r^(m-1)          for every x in R^m and 0<r<=r_0,     (7.1)

where r_0>0. An elementary grid cover of B_r(x) by at most C_m(r/r_0)^m
balls of radius r_0, for r>r_0, proves

  sigma(B_r(x)) <= C_m A [r^(m-1)+r^m/r_0]                  for all r>0.  (7.2)

Apply (1.5) with A_1=C_m A and A_0=C_m A/r_0. Thus

  J_sigma(u) <= C_m A [||grad u||_2^2+r_0^(-1)||u||_{dot H^(1/2)}^2]
             <= C_m A [||grad u||_2^2+r_0^(-2)||u||_2^2].                 (7.3)

The last step is the pointwise Fourier inequality
  |xi|/r_0 <= (|xi|^2+r_0^(-2))/2.
Neither total sigma mass nor a dimension-dependent room count is concealed
inside C_m. The sharper first line of (7.3) is also an available conclusion.

This local hypothesis does NOT imply (1.3) uniformly over arbitrarily large
rooms with the same A. Here is an explicit scale obstruction, not a
counterexample to the global theorem. Fix r_0>0 and take L>r_0. Let v_m be
the unit ball volume and let

  sigma_L = [A/(v_m r_0)] 1_(B_L) dy,
  u_L(y) = w(y/L),

with a fixed nonconstant w in C_c^infinity(B_(1/2)). For r<=r_0,
  sigma_L(B_r(x)) <= A r^m/r_0 <= A r^(m-1),
at every center x. Each sigma_L is finite. However, restricting both x,y to
B_L in the mixed integral and rescaling gives

  J_(sigma_L)(u_L) >= [A/(v_m r_0)] L^(m-1) c_w,
  c_w = int_(B_1) dx int_(B_1) dy |w(y)-w(x)|^2/|x-y|^(m+1) > 0,
  ||grad u_L||_2^2 = L^(m-2)||grad w||_2^2.

The fixed c_w is finite by smoothness near the diagonal, and positive because
w is nonconstant. Thus the quotient by A||grad u_L||_2^2 grows at least
c_(m,w)L/r_0. A low-frequency or room-scale charge is necessary if only
(7.1) is assumed and one demands constants independent of the room/cutoff
ratio. The global Frostman constant of this example grows with L/r_0, so
there is no contradiction with (1.3).

8. Inner-room version and balls valid only inside an ambient room

Suppose u is smooth on B_(4R), sigma is carried by B_R, and one asks only
for the integral with y in B_(2R). Fix a cutoff theta=1 on B_(2R), supported
in B_(3R), with |grad theta|<=C_m/R. For any constant c define
  v=theta(u-c), extended by zero to all of R^m.
This is smooth and compactly supported, and for x in B_R,y in B_(2R)
its increment equals u(y)-u(x). Positivity and the global theorem give

  int sigma(dx) int_(B_(2R)) |u(y)-u(x)|^2/|x-y|^(m+1) dy
    <= C_m A [int_(B_(3R))|grad u|^2+R^(-2)int_(B_(3R))|u-c|^2]            (8.1)

under global growth (1.1). Under only (7.1), (7.3) gives instead

    <= C_m A [int_(B_(3R))|grad u|^2
               +(R^(-2)+r_0^(-2))int_(B_(3R))|u-c|^2].                    (8.2)

This is an explicit cutoff accounting, not an assertion about unextended
functions or missing boundary energy. The choice of c is arbitrary because
the mixed increments are invariant under adding a constant.

If the initial growth statement is given only for balls contained in a
larger open room Omega, restrict the measure to a compact inner set K with
distance delta>0 from the complement of Omega. Suppose that every ball
B_t(x) contained in Omega, t<=r_*, obeys the stated bound with A. For any
ball B_r(y) meeting K in positive restricted mass, choose x in K cap B_r(y).
Its restricted part is contained in B_(2r)(x). If
  r <= r_0 := min(r_*,delta)/2,
this larger ball is contained in Omega and has allowed radius. Therefore

  (sigma|K)(B_r(y)) <= 2^(m-1) A r^(m-1)

at every y for 0<r<=r_0. Empty intersections are trivial. One can now apply
(7.3) and (8.2) to sigma|K, with this explicit radius and constant. A strict
smaller r_0 may be used to avoid boundary equality conventions.

If sigma is carried by B_R and (7.1) is known at least up to r_0 comparable
to R, finitely many radius-r_0 balls also give sigma(R^m)<=C_m A R^(m-1),
with a constant charged to R/r_0. For r>r_0 this total mass bound extends
the growth to (1.1), with a constant depending only on m and that ratio.
It is not legitimate to discard that ratio if r_0 is arbitrarily smaller
than R. The local negative control above demonstrates the distinction.

9. Hyperplane calibration by Fourier and one-dimensional Hardy

This section checks the motivating hyperplane directly, rather than citing
a trace theorem as a substitute for the mixed estimate. Let d=m-1 and
sigma=Lebesgue measure on R^d x {0}. It satisfies global growth with
A=volume(B_1 in R^d); infinite mass is permitted by Section 1. A finite
patch has smaller mixed integral. Write x=(x',0), y=(x'+h,t), and
  g(x')=u(x',0),
  K_t(h)=(|h|^2+t^2)^(-(m+1)/2).
Split the increment into
  [u(x'+h,t)-g(x'+h)] + [g(x'+h)-g(x')],
and use the square bound by twice the sum of the two squares.

For t!=0, integration in h gives

  int_(R^d) K_t(h) dh = alpha_m |t|^(-2),
  alpha_m = pi^(d/2)/Gamma((d+2)/2).                                      (9.1)

Thus the vertical part is
  alpha_m int_R ||u(.,t)-g||_(L^2(R^d))^2/t^2 dt.
For a smooth Hilbert-valued F, compactly supported in t, set G(t)=F(t)-F(0).
Integration by parts on (0,infinity), whose boundary terms vanish both at
zero and infinity, and Cauchy-Schwarz yield
  int_0^infinity ||G(t)||^2/t^2 dt
      = 2 Re int_0^infinity <G(t),G'(t)>/t dt
      <= 2 [int ||G||^2/t^2]^(1/2)[int ||F'||^2]^(1/2).
Consequently this integral is at most 4 int_0^infinity ||F'||^2. The same
proof works on the negative half-line. The vertical part is therefore at
most 4 alpha_m ||partial_t u||_2^2. Finiteness is also direct near zero
and infinity, so the integration by parts argument involves no circularity.

For h!=0, integration in t gives

  int_R K_t(h) dt = gamma_m |h|^(-d-1),
  gamma_m=sqrt(pi) Gamma(m/2)/Gamma((m+1)/2).                              (9.2)

Thus the tangential part is gamma_m times
  int dx' int dh |g(x'+h)-g(x')|^2/|h|^(d+1).
Tangential Plancherel shows this equals a positive dimensional constant
times int |xi| |ghat(xi)|^2 dxi. Indeed the relevant inner integral is
  int 2(1-cos(xi.h)) |h|^(-d-1) dh = c_d |xi|;
it is finite by the quadratic bound near zero and integrability at infinity,
and rotation and scaling give the displayed identity.

For completeness, its trace energy is controlled directly by bulk energy.
Let f(xi,t) be the unitary Fourier transform of u in the tangential variables.
It has compact t-support. Since
  |f(xi,0)|^2 = -int_0^infinity partial_t |f(xi,t)|^2 dt,
Cauchy-Schwarz in the form 2ab<=a^2+b^2 gives

  |xi| |f(xi,0)|^2
     <= int_0^infinity [|partial_t f(xi,t)|^2+|xi|^2|f(xi,t)|^2] dt.        (9.3)

The left side is zero at xi=0. Integrate (9.3) in xi and use Plancherel.
Combining (9.1)-(9.3) proves J_sigma(u)<=C_m||grad u||_2^2 directly for
the full hyperplane, including the Hardy normal contribution and the
tangential half-derivative contribution. This matches Section 1.
