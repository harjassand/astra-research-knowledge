## R1. Original definitions and the general wrapper

Let Q:R^m→Sym_r be homogeneous quadratic and PSD for every real input, let n=r+1, and write a symmetric n×n matrix as W=[[a,v^T],[v,T]]. Define

    Phi_y(W) = [[tr(Q(y)T), -v^T Q(y)],[-Q(y)v, a Q(y)]],
    p_Q(X,Z,y) = det((det X) Z - Phi_y(adj X)),
    e=(I_n,I_n,0).

For v,u in R^n, u^T Phi_y(vv^T)u=(v_0 u'-u_0 v')^T Q(y)(v_0 u'-u_0 v'). Thus Phi_y is a positive linear map. For nonreal t in the upper half-plane, Im(tI-X)^-1 is negative definite; positivity gives Im(tI-Z-Phi_y((tI-X)^-1)) positive definite. The determinant cannot vanish. The inverse formula for p_Q and conjugation therefore prove hyperbolicity, of degree n(n+1). This proof does not require a factor of Q jointly polynomial in y.

An alternative pointwise check factors Q(y)=sum_j b_j b_j^T, pads to n columns, and sets B(b)=[[0,b^T],[-b,0]]. The symmetric block determinant with top block I_n⊗X, off-diagonal stack B(b_j), and bottom Z equals p_Q on each line in y. This proves the full closed cone slice test, including singular X. In particular, when X>0 its threshold is Z≥Phi_y(X^-1), and at y=0 the slice is X,Z≥0.

The reduced polynomial is

    q_Q = det([[I_r⊗X,C^T],[C(Q(y)⊗I_n),Z]]),
    C=[K_1 ... K_r], K_i=B(e_i).

Its polynomial identity is p_Q=(det X)q_Q; hence q_Q has degree n² and is hyperbolic. Since q_Q is even in y, convexity sends (X,Z,y) in its cone to (X,Z,0). At y=0 its roots force X,Z≥0. Consequently p_Q and q_Q define the same closed cone. For r=3 this explains the catalogue's degree16 and the original/formalized degree20 without a contradiction. The displayed block matrix defining q_Q is not a real symmetric linear pencil.

## R2. General transport of a weak square-minorant obstruction

Suppose b(z,y)=z^T Q(y)z is nonzero and every bilinear scalar ell with ell(z,y)²≤b(z,y) for all real z,y vanishes identically. Then the closed cone of p_Q, equivalently q_Q, has no finite homogeneous real symmetric pencil in its original coordinates. This is our literal dimension-independent extension of the original source proof; it is not a claim about shadows.

Here is the complete reduction. A hypothetical pencil can be compressed by its common kernel because e is interior. On the y=0 slice its X and Z maps are positive. Splitting by the kernel of the X map at I and making fixed congruences yields positive unital maps D,E and a y-linear off-diagonal block B. The lower y block is zero: its positivity on (I,s²Phi_y(I),sy) for both signs of small s forces this. Rescaling the lower block by s^-1 and taking s→0 gives

    Z≥Phi_y(X^-1) iff E(Z)≥B(y)^T D(X)^-1 B(y),  X>0.

The converse in this limit is justified by adding delta I to Z before passing to the limit, then letting delta→0; no closure of an existential projection is used.

For unit v,u take X_t=vv^T+t(I-vv^T), Z_t=uu^T+t(I-uu^T). Comparing the exact scalar threshold of rZ_t in the preceding equivalence gives

    b(v_0u'-u_0v',y) = ||P(v) B(y) R(u)||_op²,

where P(v) and R(u) project onto ker D(I-vv^T) and ker E(I-uu^T). The inverse-square-root limits follow by diagonalizing the positive matrices. Choose v with v_0≠0 where the rank of E(I-uu^T) is locally maximal. Its kernel projection R is smooth on a neighborhood, as the independent-column formula for the range projection shows. At u=v the right block is zero. Differentiating only R along the normalized path (v+sh)/||v+sh|| gives

    b(v_0h'-h_0v',y) = ||P(v)B(y)dR_v(h)||_op², h⊥v.

The map h↦v_0h'-h_0v' is an isomorphism from v-perp to R^r. Every entry of the resulting matrix is bilinear in z,y and its square is bounded by its operator norm squared, b(z,y). The hypothesis kills every entry, contradicting b≠0. Finite pencil size is unrestricted. An affine original-coordinate representation reduces to a homogeneous one for a closed cone by evaluating at the origin, scaling points, taking limits, and using closedness; alternatively the source theorem states the homogeneous interface explicitly.

For the source's Choi–Lam Q in R³,

    Q(y)=[[y1²+y2²,-y1y2,-y1y3],
          [-y1y2,y2²+y3²,-y2y3],
          [-y1y3,-y2y3,y3²+y1²]],

principal minors and a three-term AM–GM prove positivity. To reconstruct the minorant claim, evaluate ell at b's coordinate zeros and at all equal sign vectors. The cyclic zero coefficients vanish, the remaining off-diagonal coefficients vanish by antisymmetry, and only sum_i a_i z_i y_i remains. The curves z=e_i+s e_(i-1), y=e_(i-1)+s e_i give b=s⁴ and ell=s(a_i+a_(i-1)), so each cyclic sum is zero and all a_i vanish. This is the short weak-extremality proof used by the source, consistent with its cited older Choi–Lam result. No external novelty claim follows.
