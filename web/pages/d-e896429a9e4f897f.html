# Primary-source check and novelty boundary

## Sources inspected

1. Zachary R. Sailer and Michael J. Harms, “Detecting High-Order Epistasis in Nonlinear Genotype-Phenotype Maps,” *Genetics* 205(3), 1079–1088 (2017), [DOI: 10.1534/genetics.116.195214](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/205/3/1079/6066378). The abstract and introduction explicitly say that using a linear epistasis model on a nonlinear genotype-phenotype map creates apparent interactions. Their method fits a power-transform family and back-transforms before decomposing the map (article lines 59–72, 81–99); the paper constructs a five-site additive latent map whose nonlinear transform produces higher-order Walsh coefficients (lines 191–208). They also report accounting for measurement uncertainty in complete landscapes (lines 242–247). This is direct prior art for the biological scale-confounding phenomenon.

2. Juannan Zhou, Mandy S. Wong, Wei-Chia Chen, Adrian R. Krainer, Justin B. Kinney, and David M. McCandlish, “Higher-order epistasis and phenotypic prediction,” *PNAS* 119(39), e2204233119 (2022), [DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2204233119](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9522415/). This studies noisy, partially observed genotype-phenotype maps and uses variance components to encode order-dependent epistasis for prediction (article lines 118–124, 307–316). It is relevant context for noisy/sparse inference, but it does not supply the nuisance-link indistinguishability proof here.

3. “An extension of the Walsh-Hadamard transform to calculate and model epistasis in genetic landscapes of arbitrary shape and complexity,” *PLOS Computational Biology* (2024), [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161127/). It extends background-averaged interaction representations to multiallelic maps and fits sparse regressions to incomplete maps (lines 154–160, 306–322, 324–345). It does not remove the question of which phenotype scale defines the coefficients.

4. Avrim Blum, Adam Kalai, and Hal Wasserman, “Noise-tolerant learning, the parity problem, and the statistical query model,” *JACM* 50(4), 506–519 (2003), [DOI: 10.1145/792538.792543](https://doi.org/10.1145/792538.792543). This is prior art for the initial Walsh/sparse-parity recovery route; hence no novelty is claimed for a noisy sparse parity sample bound translated to epistasis.

## Boundary

The biological phenomenon—apparent epistasis induced by nonlinear scale—is established prior art. Sailer and Harms fit a restricted, data-driven power-transform family and report that some high-order coefficients remain after this correction. The theorem in `PROOF.md` is narrower in biological ambition but exact in its statistical quantifiers: with an unrestricted unknown strictly increasing link, two different latent interaction states induce identical observations for every genotype, so no adaptive query schedule can identify latent-scale interaction. It is a formal impossibility/measurement-contract result, not a claim that the cited works missed scale dependence.

The literature check is bounded, not exhaustive. External proof checking and novelty/priority review remain open. No biological dataset was downloaded or reanalyzed for this note.
