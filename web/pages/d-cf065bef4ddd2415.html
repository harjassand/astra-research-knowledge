# Prime-selective coboundary expansion and the construction-characteristic obstruction

**Status:** internally derived argument, conditional on the infinite-complex identification and acyclicity in the released family 177 proof and on the cited published KMS expansion input. This is not external certification of the release. The elementary transfer, localization, first-jet and code deductions below have been independently reconstructed. No numerical test is used as a theorem premise.

## Interface and primary inputs

Fix a dimension d >= 3. Take a sufficiently large fixed prime q and R = F_q[t]. Two matrix sizes will be useful: N = 2d+1 reproduces the release, while N = 2d uses the stronger surjectivity range below. Let U be the inverse image of upper unitriangular matrices under SL_N(R) -> SL_N(F_q). Put

\[
\Gamma_a(m)=\ker(\mathrm{SL}_a(R)\to\mathrm{SL}_a(R/(t^m))),\quad m\ge2.
\]

For the cyclic simple generators x_i = e_{i,i+1}(1), 1 <= i < N, and x_0 = e_{N,1}(t), set H_i = <x_j : j != i>. Form

\[
L=\operatorname{CC}(U;(H_i)),\quad
T_m=\operatorname{CC}(U/\Gamma_N(m);(\overline H_i)),\quad
X_m=\operatorname{skel}_d T_m.
\]

Faces in CC use common intersection, and vertex types remain tagged. The norm of a cochain is the probability that it is nonzero when a uniformly sampled top face is followed by a uniformly sampled subface of its degree. For odd coefficient characteristic, use oriented simplices and the signed coboundary.

The exact source inputs are:

1. [Released family 177, Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Bounded-degree-coboundary-expanders-in-every-dimension-September-24-2026/paper.pdf): H_i and their intersections are finite q-groups with entries of degree at most one; reduction modulo t^m preserves them. The opposition-complex filtration makes L acyclic below degree N-2. Its attachments use an **integral** cone function, so the same argument works over Z and any coefficient field, not only F_2.
2. [Iwasa, Theorem 0.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04998): for a commutative Tor-unital pro-ring of relative stable range at most r, stabilization of integral H_j is pro-surjective at a >= 2j+r-2, and pro-isomorphic one rank later. The author’s [2019 version, Theorem 0.3 / 4.13](https://ryomei.com/papers/ProStability.pdf) retains that surjectivity range. The release uses only the later isomorphism range.
3. [Morrow, Theorem 0.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.4179): powers of a Noetherian ideal are pro Tor-unital. Here A_m = t^m R. The release’s Chinese-remainder proof gives relative stable range <= 2.
4. [Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth, Theorem 1.2, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05823v2): the (N-2)-skeleton of these sufficiently large-field KMS complexes has uniform cosystolic expansion, including a positive lower bound on nontrivial cosystoles, for every finitely generated Abelian coefficient group. Its definition 4.11 permits any finite quotient that preserves the local groups and intersections; a field quotient is not required.

The particular twin-building identification and its all-attachment argument were read and reconstructed at the release-proof level, but were not independently formalized or checked against every cited building theorem. That remains the external-validation gate.

## 1. Transfer turns pro-surjectivity into finite-level surjectivity

Let Lambda = Z[1/q]. For m' >= m the index [Gamma_a(m):Gamma_a(m')] is a power of q: filter matrices by powers of t and embed each successive factor into an additive matrix group over F_q. Group-homology transfer therefore gives

\[
\operatorname{inc}_*\operatorname{tr}_*=[\Gamma_a(m):\Gamma_a(m')]\operatorname{id}
\]

on H_j(Gamma_a(m); Lambda). The index is a unit in Lambda, so every transition map toward a shallower level is surjective.

The pro-surjectivity from Iwasa means that the cokernel system of stabilization is pro-zero. Localization is flat, so its Lambda-coefficient cokernels are also pro-zero. Their transition maps are surjective because those of their target homology groups are surjective. A pro-zero inverse system with surjective transitions is zero at every level: a sufficiently late map to a chosen term is both zero and onto. Consequently,

\[
H_j(\Gamma_a(m);\Lambda)\twoheadrightarrow H_j(\Gamma_{a+1}(m);\Lambda)
\qquad(a\ge2j,;m\ge1).
\tag{1}
\]

This argument needs only the stabilization cokernel, not its kernel. It also identifies why pro-stability alone is insufficient: transition surjectivity is the extra premise.

## 2. Vanishing away from q

Finite q-group stabilizers make C_*(L;Lambda) projective as Lambda[U]-modules by averaging. The acyclic augmented chains give a partial projective resolution. Since U acts transitively on each face type, the orbit-chain complex is one simplex. Hence

\[
H_j(U;\Lambda)=0\qquad(0<j<N-2).
\tag{2}
\]

If N-2 >= 2j, apply (1) twice. Homology of Gamma_N(m) is then generated by the image of Gamma_{N-2}(m) on **any** chosen N-2 coordinate positions. For an elementary matrix e_ab(f), choose the positions complementary to a,b. It centralizes that subgroup, so its conjugation action fixes a surjective image and is trivial on H_j(Gamma_N(m);Lambda). Elementary matrices generate SL_N(R), therefore its whole conjugation action is trivial in this degree.

The finite quotient G_m = U/Gamma_N(m) is itself a q-group. Averaging makes its coinvariant functor exact. Thus

\[
H_j(U;\Lambda)\cong H_j(\Gamma_N(m);\Lambda)_{G_m}
=H_j(\Gamma_N(m);\Lambda).
\]

Combine this with (2). The Gamma_N(m)-action on L has trivial simplex stabilizers, so the same partial-resolution comparison identifies homology of Gamma_N(m) and T_m. We obtain

\[
H_j(T_m;\Lambda)=0\qquad
1\le j\le d-1
\tag{3}
\]

for both N=2d+1 and N=2d. Finite T_m has finitely generated integral homology, and (3) says that each of its positive homology groups in this range is finite q-primary torsion.

Let A be any finite Abelian group with gcd(|A|,q)=1. Multiplication by q is invertible on A, so A is a Lambda-module. The universal coefficient sequence over the PID Lambda, with H_0(T_m;Lambda)=Lambda, gives H^i(T_m;A)=0 for 1 <= i < d. Cochain spaces and coboundary maps in the relevant degrees agree on T_m and X_m. Connectedness handles reduced degree zero.

The KMS theorem supplies an inequality against cocycles for this A, with constants common to the group and tower. If M = q^{N(N-1)/2}, each d-face lies in between 1 and M chambers. The release’s skeleton-weight comparison has one common normalization Lambda_m in every cochain degree, which cancels. Thus

\[
\|\delta_i f\|_{X_m,i+1}
\ge \frac{\eta}{M}\operatorname{dist}_{X_m,i}(f,B^i(X_m;A)),
\quad 0\le i<d,
\tag{4}
\]

**simultaneously for every finite Abelian A of order coprime to q**, on the same family X_m. The constants do not depend on m or |A|.

## 3. The one-rank refinement

The release sets N=2d+1 because it uses pro-isomorphism at a >= 2j+1. Equation (1) allows N=2d. All geometric and KMS inputs still apply: the ambient complex dimension is then 2d-1 >= 5, and d-1 < N-2.

For fixed q, the displayed degree bound becomes

\[
D_{\mathrm{new}}=q^{d(2d-1)}\binom{2d-1}{d},
\]

instead of D_old = q^{d(2d+1)} binom(2d,d). Therefore D_old / D_new = 2 q^{2d}. The field-threshold and cosystolic constants depend on the ambient dimension, so this does not by itself prove a numerical improvement in the final expansion constant. It is a correct construction/rank refinement rather than a new asymptotic existence class.

## 4. Sharp failure at the construction prime

For every N >= 2 and m >= 1, define the first-jet map

\[
\Phi_m:\Gamma_N(m)\longrightarrow
\mathfrak{sl}_N(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^m)),\qquad
g\longmapsto (g-I)/t^m\pmod{t^m}.
\]

Multiplication is additive modulo t^{2m}, and determinant one implies trace zero there. The kernel is Gamma_N(2m). The map is onto: e_ab(t^m h) supplies every off-diagonal coefficient; conjugating by e_ba(1) supplies diagonal differences as well, because

\[
(I+E_{ba})E_{ab}(I-E_{ba})
=E_{ab}+E_{bb}-E_{aa}-E_{ba}.
\]

The off-diagonal entries and diagonal differences span all trace-zero matrices over F_q[t]/t^m. Hence

\[
\Gamma_N(m)/\Gamma_N(2m)
\cong\bigl(\mathfrak{sl}_N(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^m)),+\bigr),
\quad
\dim_{\mathbb F_q} H_1(\Gamma_N(m);\mathbb F_q)
\ge m(N^2-1).
\tag{5}
\]

The action of Gamma_N(m) on L is free, so its partial-resolution comparison is valid even in characteristic q. The integral cone-function argument gives H_1(L;F_q)=0. Thus (5) passes to T_m and X_m. In particular, H^1(X_m;A) != 0 for every finite Abelian A whose order is divisible by q: compose a nonzero first-jet coordinate with an inclusion F_q -> A. A nonzero cocycle outside B^1 has zero coboundary and positive distance from B^1, so **no positive degree-one coboundary-expansion constant is possible** for such A.

This is an exact coefficient dichotomy. The excluded prime in (4) cannot be removed for this family.

There is also a sharp obstruction to promoting this claim to integral coefficients. By (3), H_1(T_m;Z) is finite q-primary. Equation (5) shows that it is nonzero. The integral universal coefficient sequence injects Ext_Z(H_1(T_m;Z),Z), a nonzero q-primary group, into H^2(T_m;Z). For d>=3 the same H^2 appears on X_m. Therefore degree-two coboundary expansion over Z fails, even though Z itself is torsion-free. Finite prime-to-q coefficients and integer coefficients are different interfaces here.

## 5. Asymmetric q-ary CSS consequence

This section uses the full cosystole conclusion of KMS Theorem 1.2, rather than only the inequality cited in the release.

Put a q-dimensional qudit on each edge of X_m, with CSS matrices H_X = partial_1 and H_Z = partial_2^T, using signed incidence. They commute because partial_1 partial_2 = 0. Let n_c = |X_m(1)|. Fixed local groups and bounded vertex degree give n_c = Theta(|X_m(0)|), and all check weights and qudit incidences are bounded independently of m. Equation (5) gives

\[
k_c=\dim H^1(X_m;\mathbb F_q)\ge m(N^2-1).
\]

A direct count over the local truncated polynomial ring yields

\[
|G_m|=q^{(m-1)(N^2-1)+N(N-1)/2},\qquad
|X_m(0)|=Nq^{(m-1)(N^2-1)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Every nontrivial cohomology representative in the (N-2)-skeleton has weighted norm >= mu. Passing to X_m leaves norm >= mu/M, since Lambda_m >= 1. An edge has weight at most D/n_c, where D bounds its top-face incidence. Therefore the cohomological logical distance is at least mu n_c/(MD) = Omega(n_c).

For homological distance, quotient L -> T_m is a simplicial covering: faces are exactly Gamma-orbits, types are preserved, and a Gamma element fixing a vertex is the identity. This identifies every vertex star with its quotient star. A closed edge path of length ell can be translated so its first edge has representative 1. Successive representatives differ by elements of the H_i. Its deck transformation is a product of ell such elements, including the endpoint correction, hence has polynomial-entry degree <= ell. If ell < m, a matrix congruent to I modulo t^m with degree < m is I. The path consequently has a closed lift to L and is null-homologous, since H_1(L;F_q)=0. Any graph cycle supported on fewer than m edges decomposes into simple cycles on that support. It is therefore also null-homologous. Thus the homological logical distance is >= m.

The resulting internally derived family has

\[
n_c=\Theta(q^{(m-1)(N^2-1)}),\quad
k_c\ge m(N^2-1)=\Omega(\log n_c),\quad
d_{\mathrm{cohom}}=\Omega(n_c),\quad
d_{\mathrm{hom}}\ge m=\Omega(\log n_c).
\]

These are asymmetric distances; the ordinary minimum quantum distance is only guaranteed logarithmic. The result does not improve on known asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes, does not supply a decoder, does not establish geometrical locality, and does not transfer to binary qubits. For A coprime to q the same homological CSS construction has k_c=0, rather than a useful quantum code.

## Costs, falsifications, and validation gates

The theorem fixes d and a valid sufficiently large prime q. Enumerating the finite matrix image, local cosets and faces is polynomial in the full output size, with constants depending strongly on d,q. Breadth-first enumeration by the simple generators retains Theta(n_c log n_c) bits for explicit group/face identifiers, plus working matrices of O(mN^2 log q) bits. A full input cochain costs n_c log |A| bits. There is no acquired small q, practical implementation, efficient nearest-coboundary decoder, or new complexity separation here.

The elementary checks in `check_interfaces.py` verify first-jet spanning ranks for 27 finite cases. They do not verify L acyclicity, the KMS theorem, or the asymptotic code claims. Decisive falsifications are: admitting q-torsion makes (4) false; replacing a pro-zero system by its inverse limit loses the finite-level conclusion; retaining coboundary expansion while claiming nontrivial binary logical qubits contradicts H^1=0; and interpreting the two asymmetric distances as both linear is incorrect.

Prior-art narrowing: [Oppenheim–Valentiner-Branth, 2510.18351v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18351) already proves degree-one prime avoidance, even for non-Abelian coefficient groups, on irreducible-polynomial congruence quotients. The candidate increment here is simultaneous **all-degree** prime-to-q expansion on the t-power tower, the one-rank refinement, and the explicit construction-characteristic obstruction/accounting. No global novelty or priority claim has been established.
