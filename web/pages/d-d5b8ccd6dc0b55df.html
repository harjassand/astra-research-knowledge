# Many-body control: critical complete-graph Heisenberg no-go

## Early lemma and blocker

At the isotropic critical point in Astra N20, the finite-\(N\) state is
\[
\rho_{N,0}=Z_{N,0}^{-1}\exp(2\mathbf J^2/N),
\qquad \mathbf J=\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf S_i,\quad S_i=\boldsymbol\sigma_i/2.
\]
This state is a function of the total-spin Casimir. Every permutation-invariant Hamiltonian commutes with \(\mathbf J^2\). Consequently, even the maximal closed-system controller made of arbitrary time-dependent, permutation-invariant many-spin terms leaves \(\rho_{N,0}\) exactly unchanged. No pulse search, counterdiabatic ansatz, duration, amplitude, or exact parameter knowledge can change that.

The finite-size no-go is exact and self-contained. A useful quantitative separation from a distinct equilibrium target uses Astra N20’s quartic limit; that source record remains source-derived and externally unreviewed. The remaining gate is therefore not pulse optimization. A successful control must leave the closed, permutation-invariant unitary class, and any claimed improvement must charge bath, measurement, symmetry-breaking, work, and preparation costs.

## Model, task, and information boundary

Use the spin-\(\tfrac12\) isotropic Heisenberg ferromagnet on the complete graph,
\[
H_N=-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i<j}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j,
\quad \beta_c=2,\quad \hbar=1.
\]
Since \(\mathbf J^2=3N/4+2\sum_{i<j}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j\), its critical Gibbs density is exactly \(\rho_{N,0}\) above, up to a normalizing scalar. The complete-graph model and \(\beta_c=2\) for spin-\(\tfrac12\) are in the primary Heisenberg-model analysis by Björnberg, Fröhlich, and Ueltschi. The newer Astra N20 record adds the candidate whole-state quartic-vector limit
\[
\mathbf X_N=\mathbf J/N^{3/4}\ \Longrightarrow\
p_b(x)\,dx \propto \exp(-4|x|^4/3+b x_z)\,dx
\]
for the field-tilted family
\[
\rho_{N,b}=Z_{N,b}^{-1}
\exp(2\mathbf J^2/N+bJ_z/N^{3/4}).
\]
The field coefficient \(b\) is dimensionless in the exponent. Under the cited Heisenberg normalization, the corresponding Hamiltonian field is \(b/(2N^{3/4})\).

The control task is to move the critical \(b=0\) Gibbs ensemble to the \(b>0\) tilted critical equilibrium, or at least to create a positive sign-of-magnetization response, using only a closed-system unitary controller that respects all site permutations. The controller is given the exact \(N\), target \(b\), model, and initial state. Its admissible Hamiltonian \(H_c(t)\) is any Hermitian operator satisfying
\[
[H_c(t),U_\pi]=0\quad\text{for every site permutation }\pi
\]
at every time. This includes arbitrary symmetric \(k\)-body interactions for every \(k\leq N\), arbitrary time dependence, and unlimited control strength and runtime. There is no bath, measurement, postselection, or symmetry-breaking input.

This makes the comparator maximally informed and stronger than a restricted pulse family such as double-bang, CRAB, or a finite-order counterdiabatic ansatz. The comparator may optimize over the full permutation-invariant control algebra.

## Exact control lemma

**Lemma.** For every finite \(N\), every piecewise-continuous permutation-invariant \(H_c(t)\), and the initial state \(\rho_{N,0}\), the controlled state satisfies \(\rho_N(t)=\rho_{N,0}\) for all \(t\).

**Proof.** Schur–Weyl decomposition gives
\[
(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes N}
=\bigoplus_j V_j\otimes M_j,
\]
where \(V_j\) carries the spin-\(j\) irreducible representation and \(M_j\) is its multiplicity space. On this decomposition,
\[
\mathbf J^2=\bigoplus_j j(j+1)I_{V_j}\otimes I_{M_j},
\qquad
\rho_{N,0}=\bigoplus_j c_j I_{V_j}\otimes I_{M_j}.
\]
Every permutation-invariant operator has the form
\[
H_c(t)=\bigoplus_j H_j(t)\otimes I_{M_j}.
\]
Thus \([H_c(t),\rho_{N,0}]=0\) for every \(t\), so the von Neumann equation gives \(\dot\rho_N=-i[H_c(t),\rho_N]=0\). Equivalently, the time-ordered unitary commutes with each scalar-\(j\) block. This argument permits arbitrary collective interactions and does not use perturbation theory, a gap, bounded controls, or large \(N\). ∎

The strongest informed pulse optimizer therefore ties the no-pulse protocol exactly: every admissible pulse produces the same density matrix and every collective observable has the same full outcome distribution.

## Quantitative target separation

Let \(O_N=\operatorname{sgn}(J_z)\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\); then \(\|O_N\|_\infty\leq1\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}(O_N\rho_{N,0})=0\). For \(b>0\), N20’s limiting vector law gives
\[
\eta(b)=\mathbb E_{p_b}[\operatorname{sgn}(X_z)]
=\frac{\int_{\mathbb R^3}\operatorname{sgn}(x_z)e^{-4|x|^4/3+bx_z}\,dx}
{\int_{\mathbb R^3}e^{-4|x|^4/3+bx_z}\,dx}>0.
\]
Positivity follows by pairing \(x_z\) with \(-x_z\). The finite-N quantity is also positive for every b>0: in the joint \(|j,m,\alpha\rangle\) basis, the m and -m terms have equal zero-field weight, and their signed tilted contribution is \(e^{bm/N^{3/4}}-e^{-bm/N^{3/4}}>0\) for m>0. If \(\eta_N(b)=\operatorname{Tr}(O_N\rho_{N,b})\), then the trace-distance variational bound and the exact lemma give
\[
\frac12\|\rho_N(t)-\rho_{N,b}\|_1
=\frac12\|\rho_{N,0}-\rho_{N,b}\|_1
\geq \frac12\eta_N(b)
\longrightarrow \frac12\eta(b)>0.
\]
Thus no collective closed-system pulse can even make the field-biased sign statistic, and the full target-state preparation error has a nonzero asymptotic floor.

For small \(b\), symmetry gives \(\eta'(0)=\mathbb E_{p_0}|X_z|\). With \(a=4/3\), the radial integrals are \(\int e^{-ar^4}dx=\pi a^{-3/4}\Gamma(3/4)\) and \(\int |x_z|e^{-a|x|^4}dx=\pi/(2a)\), hence
\[
\eta(b)=
\frac{b}{2(4/3)^{1/4}\Gamma(3/4)}+O(b^3)
=0.379709553995\,b+O(b^3).
\]
The resulting trace-distance floor is \(0.189854776997\,b+O(b^3)\). Direct one-dimensional quadrature of the quartic integral gives \(\eta(0.1)\approx0.03795471\), hence a limiting trace-distance lower bound of about \(0.01897735\), if \(b=0.1\) lies in the chosen public parameter cube. These decimals are numerical evaluations of the integral, not certified interval bounds.

The sign statistic is a one-bit collective readout. Estimating its mean to additive \(\epsilon\) from independent preparations takes \(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) copies by Hoeffding’s inequality. The task assumes access to critical Gibbs preparations; their construction cost and the cost of preparing a re-equilibrated \(b>0\) reference state are not included.

## Strongest control and prior-art boundary

The maximal allowed comparator has complete model knowledge and controls over every permutation-invariant Hamiltonian, not only one- or two-axis pulses. The exact invariant proves that no optimization over this full class can beat doing nothing. For cyclic pulses, the endpoint state and its energy under \(H_N\) are unchanged, so the net system work is zero; peak controller power and device-generation costs are unrestricted by the theorem.

This result concerns a mixed critical Gibbs input. Existing LMG ground-state controls have a different contract. Innocenti et al. optimize double-bang and CRAB trajectories from an easy pure ground state to a critical pure ground state; their reported double-bang scaling beats the inverse-gap reference within that task. Morawetz and Polkovnikov’s September 2026 universal-counterdiabatic sensing preprint likewise starts from an adiabatic ground-state preparation and measures a finite-time response to an unknown field. Those controls are permutation-invariant, but their pure state is not proportional to identity inside each total-spin sector, so the lemma does not apply. In contrast, they would be inert if started from \(\rho_{N,0}\) under the closed-control assumptions here.

The Astra N20 static result already contains the bounded-\((A,b)\) quartic observation law and its \(D^{-2/3}\) total-memory law; neither is claimed as new here. Its record explicitly leaves efficient coherent compilation, growing parameter bounds, local-lattice universality, and external priority open. The latest Astra W–Z packages do not appear to cover this thermal-control contract: W records parity/capacity routes, X conditional positive-kernel Gibbs/replica algorithms, Y quartic fermionic loss, and Z positive-boundary ground-state access and EPR-star obstructions. Z’s N110 targets a selected pure ground vector or a supplied-gap ground-space task; N115 blocks only named counting/gauge routes. The package summaries retain source-reported or candidate status and do not independently certify correctness or priority.

## Evidence, costs, falsifiers, and next gate

- **Finite-\(N\) theorem:** internally reconstructed exact Schur–Weyl argument from the explicit density and control class. It needs no algorithm, numerical data, precision, or runtime bound.
- **Limiting prediction:** conditional on N20’s quartic-vector convergence; the finite-\(N\) invariance theorem and trace-distance inequality do not depend on that convergence.
- **Acquired inputs:** \(N\), the exact Gibbs preparation at \(\beta_c\), target field \(b\), and permutation-invariant pulse access are assumed supplied. No system-identification or temperature-calibration queries are charged.
- **Energy/runtime:** no bound on amplitude, bandwidth, total pulse energy, or duration is needed for the no-go. The controller is unitary and cyclic; any route using rethermalization, nonunital reset, measurement/postselection, or symmetry-breaking interactions must separately charge its bath, work, success probability, and wall time.
- **Decisive falsifier:** for a verified \(\rho_{N,0}\) preparation and genuinely permutation-invariant unitary pulses, any change in a collective outcome distribution contradicts the finite-\(N\) lemma. A nonzero sign response is an especially simple test. Such a signal would first indicate broken assumptions (state-preparation error, a symmetry-breaking term, a bath, or a measurement-conditioned branch).
- **Novelty boundary:** no priority claim. The algebraic core is a standard commutant/Casimir argument. The candidate contribution is the explicit control impossibility and a quartic-law quantitative gap for the thermal-to-tilted task; a systematic literature search for this exact contract remains required.
- **Next gate:** compare a specified symmetry-breaking or nonunital controller against target rethermalization under the same known \(b\), with an acquired finite-\(N\) bath/reset model and explicit work and time budgets. Without that new resource, the present task is completely blocked by an exact invariant.
