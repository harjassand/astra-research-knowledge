## 2. Fixed foundational target T

T is the existential statement that a finite-dimensional bipartite state can have negative partial transpose while remaining undistillable:

\[
\exists d\ge3\ \exists \rho\in M_d(\mathbb C)\otimes M_d(\mathbb C):
\quad \rho\succeq0,\quad \operatorname{Tr}\rho=1,\quad \rho^\Gamma\not\succeq0,
\]
\[
\forall n\ge1\ \forall\psi\text{ with }\operatorname{SR}_{A^n:B^n}(\psi)\le2:
\qquad \langle\psi,(\rho^\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\psi\rangle\ge0.
\]

The rank-two distillation criterion and the Werner partial-trace formulation are imported established formulations; see Costa Rico and the references in the July 2026 papers. The investigation did not alter T to a finite-copy assertion.

A candidate strategy used

\[
\rho_*=(2I_9-F_3)/15,
\]

where \(F_3(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x\). The stronger statement that this particular state is undistillable would prove T. Conversely, finding it distillable would reject this candidate, **not** disprove existential T.

### Elementary checks and the exact remaining obligation

The symmetric and antisymmetric eigenspaces of \(F_3\) have dimensions six and three. Thus \(\rho_*\) has eigenvalues \(1/15\) and \(3/15\), is positive, and has trace one. Set \(\Omega=\sum_{j=0}^2 e_j\otimes e_j\). Since \(F_3^\Gamma=\Omega\Omega^*\),

\[
\rho_*^\Gamma=(2/15)(I-\tfrac12\Omega\Omega^*),
\qquad \rho_*^\Gamma\Omega=-\Omega/15.
\]

It is therefore NPT, without any imported conjecture.

For \(C\in M_{3^n}(\mathbb C)\), use row/column tensor factors indexed by the resource copies, and let \(\operatorname{Tr}_S C\) contract corresponding row and column indices on the subset \(S\). Define

\[
q_n(C)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}(-1/2)^{|S|}\|\operatorname{Tr}_S C\|_F^2.
\]

Vectorize \(C\) as \(\psi_C=\sum_{x,y} C_{xy}e_x\otimes e_y\). Its Schmidt rank is \(\operatorname{rank}C\). Expanding the tensor product and contracting each \(\Omega\Omega^*\) gives exactly

\[
\langle\psi_C,(\rho_*^\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\psi_C\rangle=(2/15)^n q_n(C).
\]

The unproved candidate obligation is therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad q_n(C)\ge0\quad\text{for every }n\ge1\text{ and every complex }C\text{ of rank at most two}.\quad}
\]

At one copy, \(|\operatorname{Tr}C|\le\|C\|_1\le\sqrt{2}\|C\|_F\) proves the inequality. The July two-copy theorem supplies \(n=2\). Neither statement supplies an induction. Partial trace need not preserve matrix rank: for example, the partial trace of a rank-one maximally entangled projector is a full-rank scalar identity.

Wu–Zou prove three-copy inequalities on the normal rank-two sector and specified nonnormal support sectors. Their nuclear-norm/exterior-square estimates and product-reference Schur argument were examined. Their normal-sector conclusion does not cover arbitrary complex rank-two matrices.

## 4. Restricted reconstruction: a product ray in one support plane

This section preserves a usable restriction of the Schur argument, **not a resolution of T**. The proof mechanism comes from Wu–Zou's product-reference calculation. The tensor calculation below applies to any finite number of physical sites. Priority of this formulation was not determined.

Let \(H=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mathbb C^{d_i}\), \(d_i\ge2\), and define

\[
\lambda_i(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I_{d_i}-\tfrac12X,
\qquad \Phi=\bigotimes_i\lambda_i.
\]

**Restricted statement.** The corresponding endpoint form is nonnegative on rank-at-most-two \(C\) whenever \(\operatorname{ran}C\) or \(\operatorname{ran}C^*\) contains a nonzero fully product vector.

### 4.1 Local co-Choi bounds

Normalize a product reference \(u=\bigotimes_i u_i\). By local unitary covariance it suffices to use \(u_i=e_0\). For a single local dimension \(d\), write

\[
A=\lambda(e_0e_0^*)=I-\tfrac12P,\quad P=e_0e_0^*,\quad A^{-1}=I+P,
\]
\[
\ell_a=\lambda(e_ae_0^*)=\delta_{a0}I-\tfrac12e_ae_0^*,
\qquad M(E_{ab})=\ell_aA^{-1}\ell_b^*.
\]

Use the input-first co-Choi convention

\[
\widehat J(\Psi)=\sum_{a,b}E_{ab}\otimes\Psi(E_{ba}).
\]

Put \(R=\widehat J(\lambda)=I-\tfrac12F_d\), \(S=\widehat J(M)\). The following invariant orthogonal pieces give every entry needed to check the inequality \(-R\preceq S\preceq R\).

On \(\mathbb C(e_0\otimes e_0)\), both operators equal \(1/2\). On each span of \(e_0\otimes e_a,e_a\otimes e_0\), \(a>0\),

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here \(R-S=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) and \(R+S=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\), both positive semidefinite. On \(e_0^\perp\otimes e_0^\perp\),

\[
R=I-\tfrac12F_{d-1},\quad S=\tfrac12F_{d-1},\quad
R-S=I-F_{d-1}\succeq0,\quad R+S=I\succeq0.
\]

This proves the local bounds directly.

### 4.2 Tensorization for a fully product reference

For the full product reference, define \(A=\Phi(uu^*)\), \(\ell_a=\Phi(e_au^*)\), and the same \(M_u(E_{ab})=\ell_aA^{-1}\ell_b^*\). These objects factor across sites. After the canonical permutation grouping input and output slots, their co-Choi matrices are \(R=\bigotimes_i R_i\), \(S=\bigotimes_i S_i\).

Each \(R_i\) is positive definite. The Hermitian matrices \(T_i=R_i^{-1/2}S_iR_i^{-1/2}\) have spectra in \([-1,1]\). Consequently \(\bigotimes_iT_i\preceq I\), and congruence gives \(R-S\succeq0\). Thus \(\Phi-M_u\) is completely copositive and, in particular, positive.

For arbitrary \(z\in H\), write \(\ell_z=\Phi(zu^*)\). Positivity gives

\[
\Phi(zz^*)-\ell_zA^{-1}\ell_z^*\succeq0.
\]

Since \(A\succ0\), the Schur complement implies

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\Phi(uu^*)&\Phi(uz^*)\\
\Phi(zu^*)&\Phi(zz^*)
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

These are precisely the two-dimensional input compressions of the Choi operator, with conjugation of the spanning vectors in the input-first convention. Complex conjugation preserves product vectors. The endpoint quadratic form is therefore nonnegative when its column space contains a product ray. The row-space version follows from \(q_n(C^*)=q_n(C)\). This completes the restricted proof.

A general two-dimensional subspace of a multipartite tensor product need not contain a product vector. No decomposition extending this argument to every allowed support plane was proved.

## 5. Exact failure of the entangled-reference tensor certificate

The tempting stronger assertion is that the preceding co-Choi domination remains valid for arbitrary, internally entangled references. It is false. This section supplies an exact finite counterexample to that **sufficient certificate**, not to the foundational target, and not to the valid product-reference argument.

Work first on \(H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\), with

\[
\Phi=(\operatorname{Tr}(\cdot)I_2-\tfrac12\operatorname{id})^{\otimes2},
\qquad u=(1,0,0,1)^T.
\]

The unnormalized reference causes no difficulty: scaling \(u\) by a nonzero scalar leaves \(M_u\) unchanged. Direct contraction gives

\[
A=\Phi(uu^*)=I_4+\tfrac14uu^*,\qquad A^{-1}=I_4-\tfrac16uu^*.
\]

Set \(\ell_a=\Phi(e_au^*)\), \(M_u(E_{ab})=\ell_aA^{-1}\ell_b^*\),

\[
R=\widehat J(\Phi),\qquad S=\widehat J(M_u).
\]

In input-first lexicographic order, choose

\[
v=(3,0,0,1,\ 0,0,2,0,\ 0,2,0,0,\ 1,0,0,3)^T.
\]

Exact multiplication yields

\[
Rv=(3/4,0,0,-3/4,\ 0,0,3/2,0,\ 0,3/2,0,0,\ -3/4,0,0,3/4)^T,
\qquad Sv=-\tfrac{11}{9}Rv.
\]

In particular,

\[
v^*Rv=9,\qquad v^*Sv=-11,\qquad v^*(R+S)v=-2.
\]

Thus the lower bound \(-R\preceq S\) already fails for this entangled reference.

Now pass to four physical qubit sites by using \(\Phi' = \Phi\otimes\Phi\) and reference \(u'=u\otimes u\). Since \(A'=A\otimes A\), the defining formula gives \(M_{u'}=M_u\otimes M_u\). After the canonical factor permutation, the relevant co-Choi matrices are \(R'=R\otimes R\) and \(S'=S\otimes S\). Hence

\[
(v\otimes v)^*(R'-S')(v\otimes v)
=9^2-(-11)^2=-40<0.
\]

The normalized value is \(-40/28^2=-5/98\). Therefore even the upper domination \(R'\succeq S'\) fails at four physical sites. This is an exact rational disproof of the proposed arbitrary-reference certificate.

### Why this is not a Werner counterexample

The local qubit map \(\lambda_2\) is completely positive: its ordinary Choi matrix is \(I_4-\tfrac12\Omega_2\Omega_2^*\succeq0\). Therefore all its tensor powers are completely positive. The actual rank-two positivity condition is true in this qubit example. The negative co-Choi certificate exposes a condition stronger than what positivity requires; it cannot be promoted into a distillation claim.

The standard-library verifier checks all displayed equalities with `fractions.Fraction`, including under `python -O`. A separate SymPy calculation gave the same rational witness. These computations check a finite algebraic counterexample; they are not a formalization or an independent specialist review of the foundational problem.
