              SHARP ONE-THIRD STABILITY OF BRENIER MAPS

                                                 OPENAI


       Abstract. For the uniform probability measure ρ on a compact convex body in Rd , d ≥ 2,
       quadratic optimal transport maps are one-third Hölder continuous in L2 (ρ) with respect to
       the target’s 2-Wasserstein distance. The constant is uniform over all targets supported in a
       fixed compact set, and the exponent is optimal. A three-atom family on a fixed cube disproves
       Letrouit’s conjectured uniform square-root estimate.

1.1. History and significance. Quantitative stability asks how strongly the representation
of a target measure by its optimal map depends on the target. Earlier contributions include
Ambrosio’s local one-half Hölder estimate along target curves near regular data, reported by Gigli
[9, Corollary 3.4], and Berman’s estimates for varying sources against a fixed target whose density
is bounded below on a convex domain [1]. Mérigot, Delalande, and Chazal used dualization to
obtain target-uniform bounds from Berman’s work, with dimension-dependent exponents, and
                                         2/15
then proved a dimension-independent W1        bound for a uniform convex source [17, Corollary 2.4
and Theorem 3.1].
   Potential estimates followed by convex-gradient interpolation became central to the target-
                                                                                      1/6
uniform problem. Delalande and Mérigot proved an L2 -map bound of order W1 for source
densities bounded above and below on a convex domain and targets in a fixed compact set
[7, Theorem 4.2]. Their interpolation inequality [7, Proposition 4.1] is the antecedent of our
last step; the improvement here is the linear W2 estimate for potential differences. Letrouit and
Mérigot extended potential stability to John domains by local-to-global variance arguments, and
obtained the corresponding map estimate when the boundary is rectifiable with finite (d − 1)-
dimensional measure [14, Theorem 1.7].
                                                      1/3
   Divol, Niles-Weed, and Pooladian obtained a W2 -map estimate for regular source densities
and nondegenerate finite targets [8, Theorem 4.3]. Its constant depends on the number of
atoms, a positive lower bound for their masses, and separation and angular geometry, whereas
Theorem 1.1 has one constant for all Borel targets, including degenerating atomic measures. At
the other end of the regularity scale, Lipschitz L2 stability in W2 holds for uniformly positive
Hölder-continuous densities on suitably regular uniformly convex domains [3, Theorem 1.1]. Its
constants depend on nondegeneracy and regularity conditions that exclude our atomic targets.
                                                  1/5
   Cazelles, Pauwels, and Portales obtained a W1 map bound for bounded source densities and
compactly supported marginals in their analysis of Monge-map estimation [5, Corollary 4.9].
                                                          1/4
Han and Zhu [10, Proposition 2.7] report a sharp W1 estimate of Mérigot [16]; see also [6,
Section 2.3]. Since W1 ≤ W2 , a quarter-power estimate in W1 gives at most a quarter-power
estimate in W2 by that comparison alone. In the fixed-cube family of Theorem 2.2, W1 and W2
                                1/3                                             1/4
have different scaling, so its W2 obstruction is compatible with a sharp W1 estimate.
   Letrouit proved an obstruction to exponents above 1/3 for a nonconvex source and formulated
uniform square-root stability in W2 for a uniform convex source as a conjecture [13, Theorem 2
and Conjecture 3]. The fixed-cube three-plane construction proved in Theorem 2.2 disproves
that conjectured exponent and every other exponent above 1/3, even with only three target
atoms. The positive bound in Theorem 1.1 identifies the matching uniform endpoint. Together,
the two parts determine the sharp exponent within the stated uniform-source class.

1.3. Conventions. Lebesgue volume is denoted by | · |, and surface measure on an affine hyper-
plane by dS. All source masses and moments are normalized by |K|. We choose fixed R, L ≥ 1
such that K ⊂ B(0, R) and Y ⊂ B(0, L). A finite maximum of affine functions with slopes
in B(0, L) is convex and L-Lipschitz. Ties between distinct slopes occur on a finite union of
hyperplanes and therefore have zero source measure.

affine functions, with a small change in one slope reassigning source points between the central
and outer cells. The following observation identifies the resulting gradients as optimal maps. It
applies to the general source body K of the introduction.
