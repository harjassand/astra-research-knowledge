N10 — Hidden dissipation under finite-bandwidth observation
Version: v2 (append-only continuation; read after v1)
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived finite-window EP bound; an exact 20-state local-detailed-balance realization has an interval-certified strict witness. The construction is mathematically complete within its stated model class. Historical novelty is unresolved; no empirical confirmation or external proof review.

1. Stronger target and exact claim

The v1 binary three-ring example was detected by a negative covariance at one lag. The target here is harder: construct a stationary locally detailed-balanced jump system whose *entire observed binary path law is time-reversal symmetric* and whose binary pair law at *every individual lag* lies in the closure of equilibrium pair tables, but whose finite multi-time record still forces a strictly positive physical entropy-production rate in every compatible all-even Markov completion.

Candidate theorem. For any stationary finite-state CTMC with all microscopic variables even, stationary law pi, and physical entropy-production rate sigma, a finite scalar statistic F of the observed positive-time half-window with range R obeys

    sigma >= g(4 Delta/R^2)/T,  g(x)=4 sqrt(x) atanh(sqrt(x)),       (A)

whenever Delta = -E[F_- F_+]>0. Here F_- is the same statistic applied to the reflected negative-time observations, F_+ to the positive-time observations, and T is the total span of the two windows. The simpler linear consequence is sigma >= 16 Delta/(R^2 T). The physical EPR is in nats per time; multiply by k_B for entropy per time.

The 20-state model below has a reversible binary output law and positive binary covariance at every lag, so every single-lag binary pair table is completely positive and individually approximable by an equilibrium finite-state CTMC. Nevertheless, a six-sample reflected covariance witness has Delta about 0.0023483030 over T=1.44 time units, giving sigma >= 0.0261744161 nats/time by (A), for every compatible all-even CTMC regardless of its hidden-state count or activity. The example's own EPR is about 69.0084752 nats/time; the infimum over all compatible completions is not determined.

2. Midpoint proof of the bound

Let Q be a stationary finite-state row-generator, pi its stationary distribution, Q* its stationary adjoint, and s=T/2. Conditional on X_0=i, the future half-path law under Q is P_i and the reflected-past half-path law is P_i*, which is the forward law under Q*. Markov conditional independence at X_0 gives

    E[F_- F_+ | X_0=i] = v_i u_i,
    u_i=E_{P_i}F,   v_i=E_{P_i*}F.

The scalar identity

    u_i v_i = (u_i+v_i)^2/4 - (u_i-v_i)^2/4 >= -(u_i-v_i)^2/4

and the range-TV inequality |u_i-v_i| <= R TV(P_i,P_i*) imply

    -E[F_-F_+] <= (R^2/4) sum_i pi_i TV(P_i,P_i*)^2.             (B)

For an all-even stationary process, the mean conditional path KL over the half-window is

    sum_i pi_i D(P_i || P_i*) = s sigma.

Pinsker, TV^2 <= D/2, applied in (B) yields Delta <= R^2 T sigma/16, hence the linear bound.

For the nonlinear bound, write v_i=TV(P_i,P_i*) and Jeffreys divergence J_i=D(P_i||P_i*)+D(P_i*||P_i). Data processing to the set where the first density exceeds the second reduces J_i to binary Jeffreys divergence. If the binary probabilities differ by v_i, minimizing that divergence over their common offset gives

    J_i >= 4 v_i atanh(v_i) = g(v_i^2).

The function g(x)=4 sqrt(x) atanh(sqrt(x))=4 sum_{k>=1} x^k/(2k-1) is increasing and convex on [0,1). Also sum_i pi_i J_i <= 2s sigma = T sigma. Equation (B) gives sum_i pi_i v_i^2 >= 4 Delta/R^2. Jensen therefore gives T sigma >= g(4 Delta/R^2), proving (A). The argument itself ensures 4 Delta/R^2 <=1; finite-rate cases with equality at the endpoint are excluded by infinite Jeffreys divergence.

This bound compares the hidden stationary path to its physical time reversal before observation. Channel-resolved local-detailed-balance EPR is at least the state-path relative-entropy rate by the log-sum inequality, so the same floor holds for physical dissipation. No bound on jump activity or hidden-state number is used. This assumes stationary operation, a time-homogeneous Markov jump process, all state variables even under physical reversal, and an ideal passive observation. If hidden variables have odd parity, the physical reversal involution must be inserted in the construction; that case is not asserted here.

For a reversible completion, P_i=P_i*, so u_i=v_i and E[F_-F_+]=sum_i pi_i u_i^2>=0. More generally, its reflected covariance matrix at times s_i>0 is a Gram matrix. Thus a negative reflected quadratic form rules out every equilibrium all-even Markov completion.

3. Physical 20-state realization

The hidden state is X=(J,S), where J is a continuous-time walk on Z_10 and S is an independent two-state coordinate. The clockwise and counterclockwise rates on J are

    a=10,   b=1/100.

The stationary ring distribution is uniform. Each clockwise jump couples to a fuel reservoir with dimensionless chemical work ln(a/b)=ln(1000), each reverse jump consumes that work, and all ring-state energies are equal. Thus local detailed balance holds edge by edge and the ring EPR is

    sigma_ring=(a-b) ln(a/b)=9.99 ln(1000)=69.0084752370 nats/time.

For S, let L=1_{S=-}, set pi(L=1)=r=1/10, and use rates + to - equal to r gamma=0.005 and - to + equal to (1-r)gamma=0.045, with gamma=1/20. This coordinate is detailed balanced with energy difference E_- - E_+=k_B T_bath ln 9 and has zero EPR. The product chain is irreducible with 20 states and total EPR sigma_ring. All coordinates are even. The readout is instantaneous, deterministic, and passive:

    h(j)=1_{j in {0,1,2,8,9}},    Y=1-L(1-h(J)).

No between-sample jumps are resolved. Sensor work and any back-action are excluded; an actual sensor must either be passive to the accuracy of this model or be included as part of the physical Markov system and its dissipation ledger.

The stationary adjoint Q* reverses the ring rates and leaves the telegraph rates unchanged. The involution Jmap(j,S)=(-j mod 10,S) conjugates Q to Q* and leaves Y unchanged. Jmap is a mathematical symmetry used to prove the output law; it is not the physical odd-parity map. For any finite set of times, the stationary hidden path probability equals that of the reversed Jmap-transformed path, while the emitted symbols are unchanged. Therefore the complete observed process is time-reversal invariant, at continuous observation resolution as well as at every sampled bandwidth:

    P_Y = P_Y^R,    D(P_Y || P_Y^R)=0.

This is a hidden driven system with an exactly reversible observed signal, not a signal whose irreversibility is merely too small for the selected statistic.

4. Exact covariance and all-lag pair compatibility

Set theta=pi/5. The nonzero Fourier coefficients of h, with convention hhat_m=(1/10) sum_j h(j) exp(-i m theta j), are

    c_1=(1+sqrt(5))/10,   c_3=(1-sqrt(5))/10,   c_5=1/10.

For m=1,3 let alpha_m=(a+b)(1-cos(m theta)) and omega_m=(a-b)sin(m theta); for the Nyquist mode alpha_5=2(a+b). The centered ring covariance is exactly

    C_h(t)=2 c_1^2 exp(-alpha_1 t) cos(omega_1 t)
          +2 c_3^2 exp(-alpha_3 t) cos(omega_3 t)
          +c_5^2 exp(-alpha_5 t).

Numerically, alpha_1=1.911739886306776..., alpha_3=13.103260113693224..., alpha_5=20.02, omega_1=5.871974670401807..., omega_3=9.501054597788584.... The coefficient sum is 2c_1^2+2c_3^2+c_5^2=Var(h)=1/4. Since alpha_1 is the smallest decay rate,

    |C_h(t)| <= (1/4) exp(-alpha_1 t) for all t>=0.             (C)

Independence of L and h and the identity Y=1-L(1-h) give p=E Y=1-r/2=0.95, C_L(t)=r(1-r)exp(-gamma t), and

    C_Y(t)=r^2 C_h(t)+r(1-r)exp(-gamma t)(1/4+C_h(t)).          (D)

In fact C_Y(t)>0 for every t>=0. Here is an all-lag proof. Let t0=3/50=0.06. For 0<=t<=t0, |omega_1|,|omega_3| <= a-b < 10, so |omega_m|t<0.6<pi/2. Every cosine in C_h is nonnegative, hence C_h(t)>=0 and (D) is strictly positive.

For t>=t0, (C) and (D) imply

    C_Y(t) >= [r(1-r)exp(-gamma t)/4] [1-F(t)],
    F(t)=exp(-alpha_1 t)[1+(1/9)exp(gamma t)].

The logarithmic derivative F'/F=-alpha_1+gamma x/(1+x), x=exp(gamma t)/9, is strictly negative because alpha_1>1.909>gamma. At t0,

    log F(t0) = -alpha_1(3/50)+log(1+exp(3/1000)/9)
              < -1.909(0.06)+1.004/9 < -0.00298 <0.

The elementary bounds used here are sqrt(5)<2.237, giving alpha_1=(1001/100)(3-sqrt(5))/4>1.909; exp(0.003)<1.004; and log(1+x)<=x. Therefore F(t)<1 for every t>=t0 and C_Y(t)>0. This is not an extrapolation from a finite numerical scan.

For each fixed lag t, the binary pair table has marginal p and covariance eta=C_Y(t), with 0<eta<=p(1-p) by Cauchy-Schwarz. It is a mixture of iid Bernoulli pairs: set lambda=eta/[p(1-p)]; with probability 1-lambda take a latent Bernoulli parameter Theta=p, and with probability lambda draw Theta in {0,1} with P(Theta=1)=p. Conditional on Theta, draw the two endpoints iid Bernoulli(Theta). Then E Theta=p and Var(Theta)=eta, so the mixture is exactly the measured pair table. It is therefore completely positive.

Each such one-lag table is in the closure of pair tables from finite reversible CTMCs. To see this directly, write the mixture as P=sum_r lambda_r p_r p_r^T. Use hidden states (r,y), stationary mass lambda_r p_r(y), deterministic output y, and within component r a reversible reset generator Q_r(y,y')=K p_r(y') for y'!=y. At lag t its pair table is exp(-Kt)diag(p)+(1-exp(-Kt))P, which tends to P as K tends to infinity. A reversible global reset of arbitrarily small positive rate makes the chain irreducible while changing a lag-t table in TV by at most rate times t. Thus every individual lag passes the exact one-lag equilibrium-closure test. The reversible realization can depend on t; this construction does not assert a common generator for several lags.

5. Six-time reflected witness and EPR floor

Let d=3/25=0.12, sample at positive offsets s_i=i d for i=1,...,6, and set

    w=(68,248,184,-5,-193,-302)/1000.

The weights sum to zero, their positive sum is 1/2 and their negative sum is -1/2. Define

    F_+ = sum_i w_i Y_{s_i},     F_- = sum_i w_i Y_{-s_i}.

Then E F_+=E F_-=0 without estimating p, each F has exact range R=1 (and values in [-1/2,1/2]), and

    q=E(F_-F_+)=w^T H w,
    H_ij=C_Y((i+j)d),  i,j=1,...,6.

The full reflected covariance matrix is symmetric because the observed law is time-reversal symmetric. The formula for q is a finite sum of explicit radicals, exponentials, and cosines. Evaluating this sum with 60-decimal outward interval arithmetic in mpmath 1.3.0 gives

    q in [-0.0023483030284429960353137660635815558257277941886848412051856422597,
          -0.0023483030284429960353137660635815558257277941886848412051856036697].

The entire enclosure is strictly negative. Thus H is not positive semidefinite. For any equilibrium all-even Markov completion, H_ij=<P_{s_i}f,P_{s_j}f>_pi with f=Y-p and P_s=exp(sQ), so H must be a Gram matrix and positive semidefinite. The witness excludes every such equilibrium completion, even though the observed law is reversible and every individual pair table lies in the equilibrium closure.

For every compatible stationary all-even CTMC, delta=-q>0, R=1, and T=2s_6=12d=36/25=1.44. The linear and nonlinear midpoint bounds give

    sigma >= 16 delta/T = 0.0260922558715888... nats/time,
    sigma >= g(4 delta)/T = 0.0261744161182282... nats/time.

The nonlinear floor is a little stronger. Neither is asserted to be the exact infimum among CTMC completions. The example's own EPR is 69.0084752370 nats/time. Independently, appending an unobserved driven cycle to any compatible completion leaves the visible law unchanged and can increase total EPR arbitrarily, as derived in v1; there is no finite upper bound without a system-class restriction.

6. Finite-data protocol and costs

For each independent stationary preparation, record the 12 binary values at times +/-0.12, +/-0.24, ..., +/-0.72, then compute Z=F_-F_+. No jump detection or between-sample measurement is used. Because F_-,F_+ lie in [-1/2,1/2], Z lies in [-1/4,1/4]. For N independent stationary windows and confidence 1-alpha, Hoeffding gives

    epsilon_N=sqrt(ln(2/alpha)/(8N)),
    delta_L=max(0,-qhat-epsilon_N),
    sigma >= g(4 delta_L)/1.44

with probability at least 1-alpha. This does not require estimating the stationary mean because sum_i w_i=0.

At alpha=0.05 and the model's delta about 0.0023483, N about 3.35e5 makes the Hoeffding lower confidence bound strictly positive with at least 95% probability under the model; N about 1.34e6 ensures delta_L>=delta/2 on that 95% event, corresponding to a positive linear floor of at least 0.0130 nats/time. These are conservative concentration counts, not an optimized experiment design. The measured windows consume 1.44N system-time units and 12N binary samples. Preparing/resetting independent systems and waiting for stationarity adds time and energy that must be reported separately. A single serial trajectory does not provide independent windows; it requires a mixing/dependence analysis or sufficiently separated blocks. The estimator is a candidate assay, not an empirical result.

7. Exact limitations and next mathematical gap

- The lower bound is valid for every compatible finite-state, time-homogeneous, stationary all-even CTMC, with arbitrary hidden-state count and rates, when its physical transitions obey local detailed balance. It is not a bound for an arbitrary non-Markov hidden process or an unmodeled odd-parity coordinate.
- The hidden involution Jmap only proves equality of output path laws. Treating Jmap as a physical time-reversal parity would be incorrect; all physical states in the model are even.
- The complete positivity of each binary lag table only says each separate table has an equilibrium realization in closure. The rates and hidden realization may differ with lag. This is exactly why single-lag closure does not imply a common equilibrium semigroup.
- Reflection/OS positivity is a necessary equilibrium condition, not a sufficient Markov-realization theorem. The finite witness gives a sufficient EP certificate; it does not characterize all compatible multitime Markov laws or the minimum EPR for this one.
- The exact identified set for full finite-bandwidth data, the sharp infimum over all locally detailed-balanced completions, optimal choice of times/weights, and practical sample-efficiency after correlated-data and reset costs remain unresolved.
- Measurement is assumed passive and noninvasive. If the detector perturbs the system, include the detector and coupling in the physical model and account for their work/entropy.
- Numerical and interval evaluation confirms the strict finite witness for the exact analytic model; it is not a laboratory measurement, independent formal proof, or priority validation.

8. Prior-art boundary (primary sources checked live on 2026-10-09)

1. P. Raux, A. M. Maier, and U. Seifert, “Lower bounds on entropy production from dynamical correlation functions,” arXiv:2608.03619v1 (2026-08-04), https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03619v1, abstract and equations (18), (25), (30). It derives steady-state EP bounds from the antisymmetry of two-observable coarse correlation functions and applies to Markov networks and overdamped Langevin systems. The statistic here is a same-observable, multi-time reflected Gram form; the constructed observed path law is fully reversible, so the Raux correlation-asymmetry statistic vanishes. This paper precludes claiming that correlation-based hidden EPR inference is itself new.
2. A. Dechant, J. Garnier-Brun, and S.-i. Sasa, “Thermodynamic bounds on correlation times,” arXiv:2303.13038v1 (2023), https://arxiv.org/html/2303.13038. Its abstract and equations (20)-(21) infer steady EPR from time-symmetric time-average fluctuations in overdamped diffusions; the paper notes in its physical setup that analogous jump-process bounds are future work. This is close prior art on time-symmetric dissipation inference. The present derivation is for stationary finite-state jump dynamics and a finite reflected sample window; no “first symmetric-observable bound” claim is made.
3. P. Jorgensen and F. Tian, “Reflection positivity and spectral theory,” arXiv:1705.05262v2 (2017), https://arxiv.org/html/1705.05262v2, Section 7, Theorem 7.1, equation (7.18). It gives the standard reflected-sum positivity condition sum_ij <v_i,r(t_i+t_j)v_j> >=0; its introduction (lines 44-47 and 78-80) distinguishes this from the Markov property, which implies but is not implied by reflection positivity. Thus the PSD condition is established mathematics. The candidate contribution is its explicit thermodynamic inference use under partial observation, together with the finite LDB construction for which every individual binary lag is equilibrium-closure compatible while the common multitime witness is not.
4. C. P. Amann, T. Schmiedl, and U. Seifert, “Can one identify nonequilibrium in a three-state system by analyzing two-state trajectories?”, J. Chem. Phys. 132, 041102 (2010), arXiv:1001.0528, https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0528; and B. Wu and C. Jia, “Parameter inference and nonequilibrium identification for Markovian systems based on coarse-grained observations,” arXiv:2406.19586, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19586. These establish qualitative equilibrium-incompatibility criteria from coarse trajectories. The present candidate differs in its reversible output and one-lag-compatible / multitime-incompatible construction, but a full priority search remains required.

These papers are cited as scope constraints, not as validation. The exact minimization problem and whether the finite-window nonlinear bound is historically new remain open.
