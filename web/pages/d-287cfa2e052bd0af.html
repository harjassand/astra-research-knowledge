The multiplier of the quartic constraint must be constant at this order. Its constant is denoted c; this is different from the paper's ellipsoid-lifting constant. The latter can be set to zero here: given any feasible ellipsoid variables M,b,c_ell, average the certificate under x->-x to remove b; its block PSD constraint gives c_ell>=0. Thus the full translated relaxation has the same attainable M as(1), without a supplied latent sign group.

## Exact feasible-set equivalence, valid beyond the QPD class

For every real homogeneous quartic f and positive definite M,

    f-q^2 SOS  <=>  there is c>=0 with cf-q+1-c SOS. (2)

The forward direction has the explicit degree-four certificate

    1-q = (1/2)(1-f) + (1/2)(f-q^2) + (1/2)(q-1)^2. (3)

For the reverse direction, evaluating at zero gives c<=1. At c=0,1-q cannot be nonnegative at infinity. At c=1, f-q is negative at sufficiently small nonzero x. Hence0<c<1. Write a Gram representation of p=cf-q+1-c, and average under the observed global sign x->-x. The resulting PSD Gram is a block on the even features(1,z) and a separate PSD block on the odd linear features x:

    p=(1-c)+2a^T z+z^T C z+h,
    [[1-c,a^T],[a,C]]>=0,
    h=x^T Hx, H>=0.

Coefficient comparison gives2a^T z=-(q+h) and z^T C z=cf. In particular f is SOS. The Schur complement gives

    cf-(q+h)^2/[4(1-c)] SOS.

The polynomial2qh+h^2 is SOS: positive semidefinite quadratic forms are sums of squares of linear forms, and their product is the sum of all pairwise squared products. Adding the appropriate nonnegative multiple therefore yields

    4c(1-c)f-q^2 SOS.

Since beta=4c(1-c)<=1 and f is SOS, adding(1-beta)f proves f-q^2 SOS. This proves(2). It uses the original degree-four SOS certificate, without saturation, hidden coordinates or an uncharged nonlinear search.

Consequently the homogeneous SOS maxdet program

    maximize log det M,
    A(G)=f,
    [[G,m(M)],[m(M)^T,1]]>=0                     (4)

has exactly the same metric feasible set and optimum as the established order-two relaxation(1). Here m(M) is the coefficient vector of q in z. The block in(4) has order N+1 with N=n(n+1)/2; the unreduced Gram in(1) has order1+n+N. This elementary elimination reduces its representation, not its mathematical objective or feasible metrics.
