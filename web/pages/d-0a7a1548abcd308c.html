# An explicit small-cocycle construction with the optimal prefix-count exponent

Status: written derivation by this worker, 7 October 2026. The spectral law is classical, not a new discovery. The quantitative combination and resource ledger below have not undergone external review or formal verification. They do not require the claimed free-factor isomorphism theorem.

## Exact interface

Let Gamma be a group containing freely independent elements p_1,...,p_m, let lambda be its left regular representation on l2(Gamma), and let e=delta_identity. Set

\[
 T_m=I+\sum_{j=1}^m\lambda(p_j),\qquad Q_m=T_mT_m^*.
\]

For a rational 0<eta<=1 we construct a real rational finite-support vector h with

\[
 \|h\|_2<\eta,\qquad\|T_mh-e\|_2<\eta.
\]

The elements p_j, not an arbitrary noisy matrix oracle, are supplied in the abstract spectral theorem. In the free-group implementation below they are acquired explicitly from eta. Nothing is inferred about permutations, characteristic-p ranks, generic matrices, or computationally inexpensive operator flows.

## 1. Exact finite-m spectral law

The spectral measure of Q_m/m at e is

\[
 d\mu_m(x)=\rho_m(x)\,dx,\qquad
 \rho_m(x)=\frac{m(m+1)}{2\pi((m+1)^2-mx)}\sqrt{\frac{4-x}{x}}
 \mathbf 1_{(0,4)}(x).                                      \tag{1}
\]

Also ||Q_m||=4m. These statements hold for every integer m>=1, rather than only in a large-m limit.

Proof. Restrict to the right-coset copy of l2(<p_1,...,p_m>) containing e. Hermitize T_m:

\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&T_m\\T_m^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is the adjacency operator of the universal cover of the multigraph with two vertices and m+1 parallel edges. Choose the identity edge as its spanning tree; the other edges have deck labels p_1,...,p_m. Because these labels freely generate, the covering is the (m+1)-regular tree. Every other right-coset copy has the same operator.

For completeness the tree density follows from a resolvent recursion. At a branch root having m forward neighbors its resolvent F(z) satisfies

\[
 F(z)=\frac1{z-mF(z)},\qquad
 F(z)=\frac{z-\sqrt{z^2-4m}}{2m},
\]

where the square root is selected by F(z)~1/z. The full root resolvent is G(z)=1/(z-(m+1)F(z)). Taking boundary imaginary parts gives

\[
 d\sigma_m(t)=\frac{(m+1)\sqrt{4m-t^2}}
 {2\pi((m+1)^2-t^2)}\mathbf1_{(-2\sqrt m,2\sqrt m)}(t)\,dt.
\]

There are no poles on the physical branch outside this interval; for m=1 the endpoint singularities are integrable. The resolvent asymptotic gives total mass one. Squaring A yields Q_m in its first diagonal block. Push sigma_m forward by t->t^2 and then rescale by m to obtain (1). Its support also gives the norm assertion.

This law is the squared Kesten-McKay law. An equivalent singular-value formula is explicitly recorded in Basak-Dembo, equation (5.8), from Haagerup-Larsen, equation (5.7). Thus the exact density itself has established prior art:

- https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.5100, equation (5.8).
- https://doi.org/10.1006/jfan.2000.3610, Haagerup-Larsen (2000).
- https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/33559, equation (2.4), regular-tree root spectral measure.

## 2. A matching lower bound for the prefix-count scale

If any h in l2(Gamma), of any support and with possibly complex coefficients, satisfies

\[
 \|h\|_2\le\eta,\qquad \|T_mh-e\|_2\le\eta,
\]

then

\[
 m\ge\frac{3}{64\pi^2\eta^4}.                              \tag{2}
\]

Proof. On 0<x<=1, (1) gives rho_m(x)>=sqrt(3)/(4pi sqrt(x)): use m/(m+1)>=1/2 and sqrt(4-x)>=sqrt(3). Put P=1_[0,1](Q_m). Consequently

\[
 \|Pe\|_2^2=\mu_m([0,1/m])\ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2\pi\sqrt m}.
\]

Meanwhile ||PT_m||^2=||PQ_mP||<=1. Therefore ||Pe||<=||P(e-T_mh)||+||PT_mh||<=2eta. Square and rearrange twice to obtain (2).

This is optimality only for the operators T_m in this interface. It is not a lower bound for every possible small-cocycle construction or every free-factor isomorphism.

## 3. Rational finite-support upper construction

Let m be a power of two with

\[
 m\ge2048\eta^{-4},\quad
 d=\eta^4/4096,\quad a=md,\quad b=m(4+d),\quad
 R=(4mI-Q_m)/b.
\]

Choose any integer L>=0 with

\[
 (4/(4+d))^{L+1}\le\eta/4.                                \tag{3}
\]

This integer can be found using exact rational arithmetic. There is also a choice that avoids expanding large rational powers merely to check (3): put q=ceil(4/d), choose the least integer t with 2^t>=4/eta, and set L+1=q*t. Bernoulli's inequality gives (1+d/4)^q>=2, hence (4/(4+d))^(L+1)<=2^(-t)<=eta/4. The integers q,t,L can thus be acquired by integer division, bit lengths, and comparisons on the supplied rational. Define

\[
 P_L(Q_m)=b^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{L}R^k,\qquad
 h_L=T_m^*P_L(Q_m)e.                                      \tag{4}
\]

Every coefficient in (4) is real rational and its group support is finite. No spectral projection, unspecified finite truncation, or spectral-limit convergence rate is needed.

For m>=4, rho_m(x)<=3/(pi sqrt(x)) on 0<x<4, since m(m+1)/(m-1)^2<=20/9<3. First consider h_0=T_m^*(Q_m+a)^(-1)e. Direct functional calculus gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|h_0\|_2^2
 &=m^{-1}\int_0^4\frac{x}{(x+d)^2}\rho_m(x)\,dx
 \le\frac{3}{2m\sqrt d}\le\frac{3\eta^2}{64},\\
 \|T_mh_0-e\|_2^2
 &=\int_0^4\frac{d^2}{(x+d)^2}\rho_m(x)\,dx
 \le\frac32\sqrt d=\frac{3\eta^2}{128}.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{5}
\]

The inequalities use the two elementary beta integrals

\[
 \int_0^\infty\frac{x^{1/2}}{(x+d)^2}\,dx=\frac\pi{2\sqrt d},\qquad
 \int_0^\infty\frac{x^{-1/2}}{(x+d)^2}\,dx=\frac\pi{2d^{3/2}}.
\]

Thus both norms in (5) are less than eta/4. The spectral interval gives 0<=R<=4/(4+d), and the geometric identity is

\[
 P_L(Q_m)=(Q_m+a)^{-1}(I-R^{L+1}).
\]

It follows that ||h_L||<=||h_0||<eta/4. Also

\[
 e-T_mh_L= a(Q_m+a)^{-1}e+Q_m(Q_m+a)^{-1}R^{L+1}e,
\]

so (3) and (5) imply ||T_mh_L-e||<eta/2. This proves the desired strict bounds with room to spare.

One sufficient size is L+1>=5d^(-1)ln(4/eta), hence L=O(eta^(-4)ln(4/eta)). Condition (3), rather than this real-log estimate, is the exact finite stopping test.

## 4. Acquiring short free prefixes in F_3

Write F_3=<a,y,z>, and let m=2^k. On the k-bit strings define two permutations:

\[
 P_y(s_1\ldots s_k)=s_2\ldots s_k s_1,
 \qquad
 P_z(s_1\ldots s_k)=s_2\ldots s_k(1-s_1).
\]

Their Schreier graph is a covering of the two-petal rose. Each step can append either desired bit, so every vertex is reachable from the zero string in at most k positive steps. A breadth-first spanning tree therefore has all root paths of length <=k. The graph has 2m geometric edges and m vertices; deleting the m-1 tree edges gives m+1 standard free generators of its fundamental group. These embed in <y,z> because the graph is a covering. Every resulting Schreier word has length at most 2k+1.

Choose m of these free words and call them b_1,...,b_m. Set

\[
 p_j=(ab_1)\cdots(ab_j),\qquad w=p_ma.
\]

The elements a,b_1,...,b_m freely generate: <a> is free from <y,z>. Replacing b_j by ab_j is a free-basis change, and replacing the free basis u_j=ab_j by its successive products p_j is another invertible free-basis change. Thus the p_j freely generate exactly as required in (1).

For the F_3 cocycle with D_a=h_L and D_y=D_z=0, the subgroup <y,z> has zero cocycle value. The m+1 positive occurrences of a in w have prefixes e,p_1,...,p_m. Therefore

\[
 D_w=T_mh_L,\qquad
 |w|\le2m(k+1)+1.                                        \tag{6}
\]

This yields a fully acquired rational version of the manuscript's small-cocycle lemma, with

\[
 m=\Theta(\eta^{-4}),\quad
 |w|=O(\eta^{-4}\log(4/\eta)).
\]

The same construction works in F_n for every n>=3 by setting the other generator cocycles to zero. The two-generator relative version does not follow: a single unused cyclic generator cannot supply this free Schreier family.

There is also a matching word-length lower bound for this block interface in F_3. Suppose w=a b_1 a b_2 ... a b_m a, with each b_j in <y,z>, and its m prefixes p_j are free. The adjacent differences u_j=p_(j-1)^(-1)p_j=a b_j form a free family by the inverse triangular free-basis change. Thus the b_j are all distinct. There are exactly 2*3^r-1 freely reduced <y,z> words of length at most r. For m>=4 take r=floor(log_3(m/4)); at least m/2 of the b_j then have length greater than r. Since no a letters cancel in the block form,

\[
 |w|=m+1+\sum_j|b_j|
 \ge m+1+\frac m2\bigl(\lfloor\log_3(m/4)\rfloor+1\bigr).
\]

Consequently the O(m log m) word length, and therefore the O(eta^(-4)log(4/eta)) scale as eta->0, are optimal up to constants within this fixed-rank positive-a block interface. This does not constrain words with other occurrence patterns or cocycle architectures.

## 5. Total resource ledger

Assume eta=u/v is supplied in lowest terms. Take the least power of two m>=2048eta^(-4). Then m<4096eta^(-4), k=log2(m), and the covering, its spanning tree, and the b_j require O(mk) symbol work and O(mk) retained bits up to fixed encoding factors.

The scalar choices m,d,q,t,L above can be made in a conservative O(bitlength(eta)^3) bit-operation bound using elementary integer arithmetic. The group-ring expression (4) can be retained as an arithmetic straight-line expression. Build the p_j successively, T_m, Q_m, and R; compute a power and its geometric sum by repeated doubling. This takes O(mk+log(L+1)) arithmetic expression gates. An ordinary pointer encoding retains O((mk+log(L+1))*log(mk+L+2)+bitlength(eta)+k) bits. This descriptor bound does not give that running time for expanding or applying the represented operator.

For explicit coefficient emission, put B_m=(m+1)^2+1 and P_max=2m(k+1). A safe upper bound is

\[
 |\operatorname{supp}h_L|\le(m+1)B_m^L,
 \qquad
 \max_{g\in\operatorname{supp}h_L}|g|\le(2L+1)P_{\max}.
\]

The rational numerator and denominator heights of emitted coefficients have bit length O((L+1)(k+bitlength(eta))). One common denominator is [m(16384v^4+u^4)]^(L+1), before possible cancellations. Hence the worst upper bound for support emission is

\[
 \exp\bigl(O(\eta^{-4}\log^2(4/\eta))\bigr),
\]

and maximal expanded word length is O(eta^(-8)log^2(4/eta)). These are conservative upper bounds; no support-size lower bound is proved. Expression storage, coefficient expansion, an operator-flow integration, and an infinite generating-limit construction are different costs.

## 6. Conditional operator-algebra consequences

The free-factor release gives a polynomial-flow proposition converting ||h||,||D_w-e||<eta into generator displacement and word error at most 3pi eta. If that proposition is adopted, (4)-(6) make its coefficient and word step explicit with the costs above. This result does not independently certify the whole flow or its limiting generation.

Its one-step automorphism fixes A_2,...,A_n exactly, because both its flow and Nielsen correction fix those generators. Consequently its adaptive generation argument, with initial total displacement budget epsilon, would give a normal trace-preserving isomorphism L(F_n)->L(F_(n+1)) that fixes A_2,...,A_n and sends A_1 within epsilon of the canonical A_1. This is a relative consequence of that candidate proof, not a new unconditional resolution of the free-factor problem. The arbitrary finite-m spectral and rational-cocycle results above are independent of this consequence.

No reduced group C*-algebra isomorphism follows. The bounded logarithms used in the flow are measurable functional calculus; they cannot generally be approximated uniformly by continuous functions on the Haar spectrum. Nor does the near-generator estimate imply closeness of whole algebra unit balls.
