# Acquiring ordinary rates and the rare scale in one passive experiment

2026-10-06. Root composition of independently reconstructed graph information bounds, r10's nuisance sensitivity/acquisition proof, and r17's geometric scale search. r17 and r10 checked the load-bearing composition and conditional tail. Final independent full-text gate PASS: `notes/r08_information/cycle5/combined_acquisition_gate.md`, including constants, selected calibration, failed-path stopping costs and numerical margins. ## Result and exact boundary

Supply a finite labeled irreducible directed graph G0, positive bounds qmin<=qmax, a missing edge u->v, and the envelope 0<b<=1 for its added rate. All present ordinary rates are unknown members of[qmin,qmax]. The actual process is a continuous-time Markov chain Q=A+bB, with B=e_u(e_v-e_u)^T. Let L>=2 be the shortest baseline u-to-v path length. Initial state is parameter-independent. Passive endpoint readings give the whole state and calibrated timestamps; no path events, resets, prepared stationary ensembles or hidden clocks are supplied.

For fixed relative error 0<epsilon<=1/4 and confidence1-delta, 0<delta<=.1, there is a complete no-reset adaptive procedure such that, uniformly over the ordinary rates and every b in(0,1],

    Pr(|bhat-b|<=epsilon b) >=1-delta,
    E N <= C b^[-L/(L-1)] [log(1/delta)+log log(e^2/b)],
    E T <= C' b^-1        [log(1/delta)+log log(e^2/b)].

Constants depend on the supplied graph/rate envelope and epsilon, potentially severely. The ordinary rate values and the local b scale are acquired from observations. Known graph support, a positive ordinary-rate gap, full state labels, Markov homogeneity and calibrated timing remain supplied. This is an acquisition theorem within that structural class, not learning an unrestricted physical law.

The known-baseline subfamily gives the independently audited adaptive lower exponent L/(L-1) for readings and1 for physical time, by two-point comparison. Therefore these polynomial orders are optimal; the necessity of the loglog overhead is unresolved. A fixed additive entropy-production error is a different objective because its leading logarithmic coefficient also depends on ordinary rates. No EP extension is silently included.

## Uniform primitives and constants

Use the explicit constants in `notes/r10_probability/cycle6/unknown_baseline_rare_edge.md`: R=dmax qmax, C=2(R+1), D=2dmax, J=C^2/2,

    Astar=D C^(L-1) exp(C)/(L-1)!.

There is a computable eta>0, depending only on the supplied envelope and baseline paths, with every entry of exp(Q) at least eta for all admitted Q. At lag t<=1, collecting M complete return-cycle departures from a desired state, starting from an arbitrary label, costs at most

    [ceil(1/t)+M]/eta

expected endpoint readings. This follows from a uniform block hitting estimate and Kac's mean return identity; self-loops are one-step cycles. First departure outcomes in successive return cycles are iid draws from the sampled row. All intermediate observations and complete returns are charged.

For two admitted ordinary generators A,Ahat with max ordinary-edge error<=Delta, their rare endpoint probabilities satisfy

    |r_A,c(t)-r_Ahat,c(t)|
       <=Delta[Astar t^L+2D c t^2],                 (1)
    r_A,c(t)=[exp(t(A+cB))]_[u,v].

The baseline derivative series vanishes through degree L-1 because every marked walk from u to v needs L baseline factors. The mixed derivative with one nuisance insertion and one B insertion has two Duhamel triangles, each bounded by D Delta t^2. This proves(1); it is not a generic norm bound losing the t^L suppression.

Choose a computable positive Delta satisfying

    Delta<=min{1,1/[64(Astar+3D)],
                   epsilon/[32(Astar+8D)]}.

Choose a positive dyadic kappa small enough that

    kappa<=min{1,1/[4(R+1)],1/(2C),
                  (L!/R^L)^[1/(L-1)],Delta/(2J)}.

For beta_k=2^[-k-1], define the dyadic lag

    t_k=kappa 2^[-ceil((k+1)/(L-1))].

Then(kappa/2)beta_k^[1/(L-1)]<=t_k<=kappa beta_k^[1/(L-1)]. For all true b in[0,1], not only the stage bracket, the uniform bounds are

    r_A,b(t_k)<=b t_k+beta_k t_k,
    partial_b r_A,b(t_k)>=t_k/4.                  (2)

The first uses a baseline jump-count bound and coupling to the added rare clock. The second follows from Duhamel, diagonal survival>=exp(-1/4), and off-diagonal probability<=1/4. It holds for the clipped acquired generator as well.

## Stage k: fresh calibration followed by a scale test

Set delta_k=delta/[16(k+1)^2]. The stage uses only beta_k,t_k, supplied constants and retained data.

First acquire all ordinary rates afresh. For every state i collect

    m0_k=ceil{[8(R+1)+(4/3)Delta]
                 log(2 m_edges/delta_k)/(t_k Delta^2)}

complete return-cycle departures. Divide each ordinary-edge endpoint frequency by t_k, clip into[qmin,qmax], set absent entries to zero and diagonals to minus row sums. Call the resulting generator Ahat_k. It is always an admitted generator, even on a statistical failure. Since

    ||exp(tQ)-I-tQ||_infinity<=Jt^2,

the P/t discretization bias is at most Delta/2. Bernstein plus a union bound gives, conditional on any retained history before this stage,

    Pr(max|Ahat_k-A|>Delta) <=delta_k.            (3)

Next collect a fresh block of

    m_k=ceil[8192 log(1/delta_k)/(beta_k t_k)]

source-u return-cycle departures, with empirical fraction z_k ending at v. Compute a certified threshold c_k approximating r_Ahat_k,1.5beta_k(t_k) to error<=beta_k t_k/64. STOP if z_k>c_k; otherwise start stage k+1. On stopping at K, use the bracket

    [beta_K,min(4beta_K,1)].

Exact rational counts, dyadic lags and a certified uniformization/Taylor evaluator make the threshold decision effective. Precision is increased to the stated tolerance; no unknown b appears in the decision or precision target. Additional rounding of Ahat can consume a fixed fraction of Delta with a corresponding constant sample increase.

## Uniform classification and adaptive bracket coverage

On the calibration event(3), apply(1) at the KNOWN comparison parameter c=1.5beta_k. The threshold model error is at most

    Delta[Astar kappa^(L-1)+3D t_k]beta_k t_k
       <=beta_k t_k/64.

Including numerical threshold error gives at most beta_k t_k/32 total. This avoids inserting the unknown b into a calibration-error test.

If b<=beta_k, equation(2) gives a true probability gap from comparison parameter1.5beta_k of at least beta_k t_k/8. The tested threshold is therefore above the true mean by at least beta_k t_k/16. The true mean is at most2beta_k t_k. Bernstein bounds conditional false stopping by delta_k with the stated8192 constant.

If b>=2beta_k, the true probability exceeds its comparison value by at least b t_k/16. Subtracting the threshold error leaves at least b t_k/32, while the true mean is at most1.5b t_k. Bernstein then gives, on good calibration,

    Pr(continue | calibration data, starting history)
       <=delta_k^[2b/beta_k].

For example, the relevant exponent is at least m_k b t_k/3094, and8192/3094>2. There is no required classification in beta_k<b<2beta_k. Integrating over calibration adds at most delta_k to either error. These statements use fresh second-block outcomes, not a confidence claim conditioned on selected favorable measurements.

A union bound over stage calibration and out-of-indifference decisions fails with probability at most

    2 sum_k delta_k=delta pi^2/48<delta/4.

On this global good event, stopping at K implies b>beta_K. If K>0 the preceding continuation implies b<2beta_(K-1)=4beta_K; if K=0 the envelope b<=1 supplies the upper end. The returned bracket thus contains b. Both rates and scale were acquired rather than used as hidden stage inputs.

## Termination and expected costs on all paths

Let kstar be the first k with beta_k<=b/2. At every later stage, the preceding classification gives the UNCONDITIONAL, history-uniform continuation bound

    Pr(continue stage k | its starting history)
       <=delta_k+delta_k^[2b/beta_k]<=2delta_k.

Hence, by conditional iteration, for j>=1,

    Pr(reach kstar+j)
       <= product_(ell=0)^(j-1) 2delta_(kstar+ell)
       <=(delta/8)^j/(j!)^2.                    (4)

Independence of stage decisions is unnecessary. This factorial tail replaces the stronger tail available with a known baseline and proves almost sure termination despite failed calibrations. Merely conditioning the analysis on successful calibration would not establish expected cost.

Given any stage-start history, all block lengths are deterministic functions of k and fixed constants. Source acquisition and return cycles give expected stage cost at most

    [(n+1)ceil(1/t_k)+n m0_k+m_k]/eta
       <=C beta_k^[-L/(L-1)]
                  [log(1/delta)+log(k+1)+1].

Its expected elapsed time is that cost times t_k. Up to kstar the geometric sum is bounded by its last-scale order. After kstar, (4) dominates the cost growth2^[j L/(L-1)] and the additional log(kstar+j+1). Thus the full scale-and-calibration procedure has the expected read/time orders in the statement, with no uncharged failure paths. The first-hit overhead is included and lower order in b.

## Final estimation with the selected calibration

At the stopping stage retain Ahat_K, beta=beta_K and t=t_K. Collect a NEW source-u return-cycle block of

    mfinal=ceil[32768 log(8/delta)/(epsilon^2 beta t)]

departures and form z. Define bhat by clipping z to the range of r_Ahat_K,c(t) on[beta,min(4beta,1)] and inverting that strictly increasing function. Always return a clipped estimate; an empty confidence-set intersection is not allowed to trigger endless sampling.

On the global good stage event the selected calibration is accurate; selection is covered by the simultaneous union guarantee, not by pretending Ahat_K is unconditionally independent of the search. The final block is fresh conditional on all search history. True success probability is at most5beta t. Bernstein gives |z-r_A,b(t)|<=epsilon beta t/32 with probability at least1-delta/4. Equation(1), b<=4beta and the chosen Delta give

    |r_A,b(t)-r_Ahat_K,b(t)|
       <=Delta(Astar+8D)beta t<=epsilon beta t/32.

The inverse has Lipschitz constant4/t. Projection cannot increase error relative to r_Ahat_K,b(t), so ideal inversion yields |bhat-b|<=epsilon beta/4. Allocate at most epsilon beta/2 to certified numerical inversion, retaining slack inside epsilon beta<=epsilon b. Bisection with probability evaluation errors proportional to epsilon beta t terminates in finitely many operations. Precise fixed constants are conservative and not optimized.

The final block size and calibration are functions only of the random stage index and observed data. Even on a wrong-bracket path, its generator is valid and block size is finite; the same bound(4) controls its unconditional expected cost. Summing over K preserves the stated orders. Failure probability is less than delta/4+delta/4, leaving reserve for the specified arithmetic/enclosure allocations; deterministic arithmetic does not consume a statistical failure budget if properly certified.

## Research consequence and remaining obstacle

Accumulated work changed the legal interface: the original known-generator example became a general-graph minimax theorem, then a sensor-resolution tradeoff, then a nuisance-acquisition estimator, and finally this joint rate-and-scale procedure. It provides a reusable way to decide which information must be measured accurately: graph support suppresses ordinary-rate error at the rare endpoint, so broad ordinary calibration can precede precise rare-rate acquisition.

The closest competent prior is adaptive CTMC endpoint inference, including Barendregt-Webb-Kilpatrick2023's directed-ring experiments. Their approach and ordinary likelihood design can exploit the same timing mechanism. The proposed contribution here is the rigorous topology-dependent lower/upper cost characterization and charged acquisition extension, not the invention of adaptive sampling or likelihood estimation. See `notes/r04_statphys/cycle5/` for the primary-source audit; absence of a located prior theorem does not establish novelty.

The minimum-lag theorem in SENSOR_RESOLUTION_THEOREM.md retains a known baseline and local bracket. This acquired-rates procedure uses lags tending to zero; it cannot be silently composed with a fixed sensor lag, unknown graph support, misclassified labels, multiple vanishing ordinary rates, or a hidden-state process. Fixed additive entropy-production estimation also needs a finer nuisance precision and effective stationary-logarithmic certificate. Those are exact next obligations, not implied corollaries.

Current evidence is a proof composition and independent reconstruction, with separate finite numerical/support diagnostics. This combined adaptive protocol has not yet been implemented end to end. No general scientific discovery engine or foundational breakthrough is established.
