# Cylinder counterexample: finite index with superexponential zero state

7 October 2026. **Independent gate PASS with two explicit notational repairs in the assembly.** Davey–Jeznach arXiv:2607.07639v1, §4 Theorem4.1 yields a real symmetric smooth uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator on T²×R, with smooth real compactly supported potential and a nonzero L² superexponential zero eigenstate. Its negative spectrum is finite by the form proof below. This refutes the unrestricted variable-coefficient/cylinder extension of 'finite index excludes superexponential zero states'. It does **not** settle H=−Δ+V on Euclidean R^n.

## Exact interface supplied by the construction

The cylinder coordinates are (θ,φ,x), with θ,φ periodic of period2π. The matrix has the form A=diag(A_T,1), with A_T a real symmetric2×2 block, and belongs to the source class M_0(2,1,75). Definition2.1 means that A is bounded, uniformly elliptic, symmetric, globally Lipschitz, with no mixed torus/axial entries and with axial entry exactly one. In §4 the cutoffs are smooth, so the constructed A and u are smooth; a uniform bound on every higher derivative is not asserted.

The equation is div(A∇u)=Vu. Thus the selfadjoint operator is H=−div(A∇)+V, with **plus V**, not minus V. The source gives two options:

- For ε∈(0,1], |u|≤C exp(−exp(c|x|^ε)), and |∇A|≤C_ε(1+|x|)^(−1+ε).
- For ε∈(0,1/2], |u|≤C exp(−c_ε|x|^(1/ε)), and |∇A|≤C/[ε(1+|x|)].

Both bounds are uniform in θ,φ and imply decay faster than every fixed exponential in the unbounded coordinate. The torus has finite volume, so u∈L². These are rates in |x| on a cylinder, not radial localization of a lifted periodic function on R³.

Proposition4.2 first gives a half-cylinder solution with V=0, A=I and u=cos(k_0θ)e^(−k_0x) in a neighbourhood of x=1. Lemma4.3 moves one Fourier mode into a mode of twice the frequency in the other periodic coordinate. Smooth cutoffs and coefficient perturbations create the transition while keeping real symmetry and ellipticity. The perturbations in the first two transition matrices are O((kT)^−1) around diag(1,1/4,1); sufficiently large kT preserves positivity. In the phase-change block d=[(ψ')²−ψ''/k]/4, so a monotone cutoff and sufficiently large kT give a positive bounded diagonal entry. This avoids a division by u at its zeros.

The successive modes have k_n=2^n k_0. The coefficient is identity near each interface, and the modes agree on neighbourhoods there, so the countable assembly is locally smooth and has no interface distribution. For the double-exponential version take x_n=(n+1)^(1/ε); for the power version take x_n=(1+γ)^n, with log(1+γ)/log2=ε/(1−ε). Interval lengths are nondecreasing, making a single initial k_0 threshold sufficient.

An independent decay check uses E_n(x)=k_(n−1)x−Σ_(j=0)^(n−2) k_jx_j. At the left endpoint,

E_n(x_(n−1))=k_0[x_0+Σ_(l=1)^(n−1)2^lT_l].

This telescoping identity gives E_n≥k_0 2^(n−1) for the polynomial endpoints (T_1≥1), hence E_n≥(k_0/4) exp[(log2)x^ε]. For geometric endpoints it gives E_n≥c k_0γ x2^n≥c_εx^(1/ε), with an adjusted finite constant on the first interval. Since |u_n|≤2exp(−E_n), both claimed tails follow. The matrix derivative bound O(T_n^−1) gives the corresponding derivative decay. These estimates are reconstructed independently rather than inferred from the abstract.

## Compact smooth potential and the central bridge

On [−1,1] choose a positive smooth even f, with f=e^(k_0x) near the negative endpoint and f=e^(−k_0x) near the positive endpoint. Set u_0=cos(k_0θ)f(x), A=I there, and

V_0=f''/f−k_0².

Then Δu_0=V_0u_0. Because f agrees with exponentials on endpoint neighbourhoods, V_0 vanishes on those neighbourhoods; its extension by zero is actually smooth, not merely a bounded discontinuous cutoff. Its support is contained in T²×[−1/2,1/2]. Division by f is safe because f>0. Reflect the half-cylinder construction to x<−1, matching it to u_0 near ±1. The zero mixed blocks make this reflection valid without introducing sign changes in axial/tangential entries.

The bridge can, if desired, be chosen with V≤0: take a smooth convex even g agreeing with |x| for |x|≥1/2, with |g'|≤1, and f=e^(−k_0g). Then V=k_0²[(g')²−1]−k_0g''≤0. Such g is obtained by integrating a smooth odd monotone approximation of sign(x), then adding the constant needed to match |x|. This is our permissible bridge choice, not a claim that the source fixes that sign.

**Two assembly details must be made explicit.** On even intervals, switching θ and φ in u requires the tensor transformation A↦P A(φ,θ,x)Pᵀ, where P swaps the first two basis vectors. The source writes the argument substitution alone while describing a switch of roles; taken literally, that displayed formula is not enough. For example diag(1,1/4,1) applied to cos(kφ)e^(−kx) gives residual 3k²cos(kφ)e^(−kx)/4. Permutation conjugation gives zero and preserves every matrix bound and endpoint condition. Also choose the cutoff monotone: the source's inequality ψ'≥1 uses this, although the initial list of cutoff properties does not explicitly state it. Both repairs are available within the construction and do not change its conclusion.

## Selfadjoint eigenstate and finite negative count

Define the closed semibounded form on H¹(T²×R),

q(v)=∫[∇vᵀA∇v+V|v|²].

Real symmetry, boundedness and uniform ellipticity give its selfadjoint Friedrichs operator H. The weak solution u lies in the form domain: test the equation with χ_R²u. Cauchy–Schwarz and ellipticity give a uniform global gradient bound in terms of ||u||²_L², ||V||∞, and the cutoff gradient. Let R→∞ to obtain ∇u∈L². The weak identity then extends to every H¹ test, so u∈Dom(H) and Hu=0. No unproved global H² claim is needed.

Cut at x=±1 and allow independent H¹ functions on the compact core and the two exterior pieces. This enlarges the form domain; the decoupled operator imposes natural Neumann boundary conditions. Restricting a global H¹ function to the three pieces preserves its form value and norm. The two exterior forms are nonnegative because V=0 and A is positive. The core has A=I exactly, and

q_core≥q_(−Δ_N,core)−M||v||²,  M=||V_-||∞<∞.

Therefore the maximal dimension of a negative-definite form subspace, and hence the number of negative eigenvalues counted with multiplicity, obeys

N_-(H)≤#{(a,b,j)∈Z²×N_0: a²+b²+(πj/2)²<M}<∞.

The torus multiplicity is exactly represented by the signed integers a,b (equivalently the real sine/cosine multiplicities). A simpler safe bound is (2⌊√M⌋+1)²(⌊2√M/π⌋+1). This is a finite bound in the actual bridge norm M; the source does not supply a numerical M or an exact negative eigenvalue count. Since the core has compact resolvent, no infinite negative accumulation is hidden in the bracketing argument. This proof does not need a generic manifold finite-index theorem.

There is an explicit negative groundstate as well. Extend F(x)=f(x) centrally by F=e^(−k_0|x|) outside the core, and make it constant on the torus. It is smooth, positive and L². Since A_xx=1 and the mixed entries vanish,

HF=−F''+VF=−k_0²F

on the entire cylinder. The positive ground-state identity gives q(v)+k_0²||v||²=∫F²∇(v/F)ᵀA∇(v/F)≥0 for compact smooth v. Consequently infσ(H)=−k_0². The superexponential state u is a different eigenstate at energy zero.

For this particular block structure σ_ess(H)=[0,∞), not merely bottom zero. The finite negative form count excludes essential spectrum below zero. For every λ≥0, use torus-constant axial plane waves e^(i√λx), multiplied by long normalized smooth cutoffs escaping down an end. Outside the core H acts on these functions exactly as −∂_x², so ||(H−λ)v_R||→0 and v_R⇀0. Weyl's criterion puts every λ≥0 in the essential spectrum. This conclusion uses the exact free axial channel, not uniform ellipticity alone.

## Consequence, comparator and remaining boundary

This is a finite-Morse-index, selfadjoint, real smooth uniformly elliptic example with a threshold L² state decaying superexponentially. Exterior positivity here is as strong as possible: V=0, and ψ≡1 is a positive exterior harmonic function. Signed boundary data and variable leading coefficients still allow the high-frequency conversion mechanism. Thus no general proof based only on finite index, exterior groundstate existence, ellipticity and smoothness can exclude superexponential states on such cylinders.

The nodal-domain bound from FINITE_INDEX_BOUNDARY extends to this operator: the same truncation/min–max argument works with symmetric A, and smooth elliptic coefficients have local unique continuation. Hence this example has finitely many global nodal domains despite increasingly numerous components on transverse slices. It also rejects 'finitely many global nodal domains implies no superexponential decay' at this generalized interface. It does not reject that implication for the narrower Euclidean scalar-potential problem.

Lifting the example periodically to R³ makes u nonlocalized in two transverse directions, and makes V supported on an infinite periodic slab rather than a compact Euclidean set. A coordinate change does not convert an arbitrary anisotropic A into the Euclidean Laplacian while retaining this interface. The original question H=−Δ+V on R^n, bounded real V→0, remains UNKNOWN in this audit. The source's authors own the construction; Neumann bracketing and the groundstate deductions here are standard structural consequences, not a novelty claim.
