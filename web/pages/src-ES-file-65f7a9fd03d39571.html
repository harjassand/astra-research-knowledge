1. TARGET AND ACCOUNTING

Given rational A,b,c, solve min c^T x subject to Ax=b, x>=0, including
infeasibility/unboundedness. Required arithmetic-operation count: poly(m,n),
independent of all coefficient encoding lengths. Every intermediate rational
must have encoding length polynomial in the input length. Reading input bits
and rational-arithmetic bit work are not unit-cost claims.

Supplying a feasible start, an improving circuit, a bundle, an optimal support,
or an initial decomposition is a separate acquisition premise. The result in
Section 3 only evaluates an explicitly supplied, verifiable bundle. It does not
find such a bundle, and it does not bound the number of changes between bundles.

2. COMPETING OPERATION A: SUM INDIVIDUALLY MAXIMAL CIRCUIT STEPS

Let 0<a<1, and consider

 A = [[1,0,1,-a], [0,1,-a,1]],  b=(1,1),
 c=(0,0,-(1-a),-(1-a)),          x(0)=(1,1,0,0).

Two elementary kernel vectors are

 g1=(-1,a,1,0),    g2=(a,-1,0,1).

Each support is a three-column circuit: all pairs among its three columns are
independent and all three coefficients in the displayed dependence are nonzero.
Both costs are -(1-a)<0. At any state whose first two coordinates equal q>0,
the individually maximal step in each displayed direction has length q.

Proposed operation: independently compute these maximal step lengths, then add
both resulting moves in one batch. The batch is feasible, since the two directions
have disjoint negative supports and positive cross-contributions. Nevertheless
the exact iteration is

 x1(t)=x2(t)=a^t,
 x3(t)=x4(t)=(1-a^t)/(1-a),
 c^T x(t)=-2(1-a^t).

Proof: adding the two equality constraints gives
 x1+x2+(1-a)(x3+x4)=2. A batch replaces q by aq and adds q to each output.
Thus the displayed recurrence follows by induction. The objective is bounded
below by -2, attained at (0,0,1/(1-a),1/(1-a)). Every finite iterate has objective
strictly larger than -2. Thus this particular exact exchange operation never
terminates, despite strict improvement at every step.

For a=1-2^(-L), Bernoulli's inequality gives a^t>=1-t*2^(-L). Achieving even
c^T x(t)<=-1 requires t>=2^(L-1). The optimal objective remains exactly -2.
The reduced denominator of a^t is 2^(Lt), so the intermediate bit length is
also uncontrolled if this operation is iterated so many times.

Scope: this defeats the stated operation on the stated available directions.
It is not a lower bound for all circuit rules. Other circuits can shortcut this
instance. Claiming otherwise would hide the circuit-selection question.

3. MUTATION B: ALGEBRAIC CLOSURE OF A FIXED CIRCUIT BUNDLE

Let x>=0 satisfy Ax=b. Let G=[g1,...,gk] be rational kernel vectors with
c^T gj<0. They need not actually be circuits for the theorem. Suppose there are
distinct protected coordinates r1,...,rk such that

 gj[rj]=-1,
 gj[i]>=0 for every i!=rj.

Thus every direction has one negative entry, at its own protected coordinate.
Write R=(r1,...,rk), G_R=M-I, where M>=0 and M has zero diagonal. Also G_out>=0.

THEOREM. In O(k^3+nk) rational arithmetic operations after G is supplied, either

 (a) compute alpha>=0 with (I-M)alpha=x_R, so x'=x+Galpha is feasible,
     x'_R=0, and its objective strictly improves if x_R is nonzero; or
 (b) compute v>=0, v!=0, with (I-M)v<=0, so d=Gv>=0 and c^T d<0.
     Then d is a verifiable improving recession direction, hence the LP is
     unbounded below.

No eigenvalue approximation, minimum-circuit oracle, or guessed spectral gap is
used. The two outcomes correspond to rho(M)<1 versus rho(M)>=1, but the exact
algorithm below does not compute rho(M).

PROOF AND ALGORITHM. Put Z=I-M, a Z-matrix (off-diagonal entries nonpositive).
Perform Gaussian Schur elimination in a fixed order, without row exchanges,
while pivots are positive. A positive-pivot Schur complement is again a Z-matrix:
 S_ij <- S_ij - S_ip*S_pj/S_pp. For i!=j, the subtracted quantity is nonnegative.

If every pivot is positive, the upper triangular factor has positive diagonal
and nonpositive strict upper entries. The lower triangular factor has unit
diagonal and nonpositive strict lower entries. The inverses of both factors are
entrywise nonnegative, by forward/back substitution. Thus Z^-1>=0. Solving
Z alpha=x_R yields alpha>=0. Multiplication by G cancels the protected coordinates
and only adds to all other coordinates. If x_R!=0 then alpha!=0, so
c^T G alpha=sum_j alpha_j*c^T gj<0.

If the first nonpositive pivot occurs at position p, use the p-th standard basis
vector in the uneliminated Schur system. The corresponding Schur column is
entrywise nonpositive: its diagonal is the failed pivot and its off-diagonal
entries have the Z signs. Set v_p=1 and v_j=0 for j>p. Back substitute through
the previous positive pivots to make the first p-1 original equations zero.
Each earlier component of v is nonnegative because its triangular row has
positive diagonal and nonpositive upper entries. In block form this is

 v_T=-Z_TT^-1 Z_T,p >=0,
 Z v = (0, Schur_column_p) <=0.

Consequently G_R v=-Zv>=0 and G_out v>=0. Strictly negative costs of all the
supplied directions imply c^T Gv<0. In particular Gv is nonzero.

BIT SIZE. If the supplied data have total binary encoding length L_G, Schur
entries, triangular factors, solves, and witnesses are ratios of minors of
matrices of order at most k, including an augmented right-hand-side column.
Clearing denominators by their product and applying a determinant bound gives
polynomial encoding length in k and L_G. Reduced exact fractions or fraction-
free elimination preserve this bound. If G was computed from a rational basis
of the original A, its coordinates are themselves ratios of minors and also
have polynomial encoding length. Computing and searching for G remains charged.

APPLICATION TO SECTION 2. Here M=[[0,a],[a,0]]. Closure returns
alpha=(1/(1-a),1/(1-a)) in a constant number of arithmetic operations. It jumps
directly to the optimum. For a=1-2^(-L), the result has O(L) bits. The arithmetic
operation count does not depend on L. This removes the feedback repetition for
the supplied block; it says nothing about choosing future blocks on general LPs.

POLICY INTERPRETATION. If each resource has several possible one-negative
directions, selecting one per resource is a policy and the theorem is exact
policy evaluation. Selecting/improving policies is a separate optimization
problem. General Leontief/MDP policy selection cannot be declared solved merely
because each fixed policy can be evaluated by a matrix inverse.

4. ABSOLUTE SIGN OBSTRUCTION: NO ONE-NEGATIVE IMPROVEMENT EXISTS

Let r>=2 and H be a strictly positive r-by-s matrix whose column sums equal 1.
Use A=[I_r,H], c=(0_r,-1_s). Let g=(p,q) be any kernel vector, so p=-Hq.

PROPOSITION. If g has at most one negative coordinate then c^T g>=0.

Proof if the negative coordinate is a resource coordinate: q>=0. If q!=0,
strict positivity of H forces every one of the r resource components of p to be
negative, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus q=0 and there is no improvement.
If the sole negative coordinate is an output coordinate, p>=0. Taking column
sums of H in p+Hq=0 gives sum(q)=-sum(p)<=0, hence c^T g=-sum(q)>=0.
The case with no negative coordinate is included.

This is a statement about every vector in the kernel, not just the circuit
directions that an algorithm happened to inspect. No better search within the
one-negative class can overcome it, including signed circuit recombination.

Small exact instance:

 H=[[1/3,2/3],[2/3,1/3]],  b=(1,1), x0=(1,1,0,0).

The feasible region is bounded, since sum(resource)+sum(output)=2. The optimum
has output=(1,1), resource=0 and cost -2. Yet no improving kernel direction has
one negative coordinate. An orientation reversal turns a consuming move into a
producing move and reverses its objective sign; it does not fix this obstruction.

5. MUTATION C: CHANGE THE POSITIVE REPRESENTATION

5.1 Invertible positive changes are too limited.

LEMMA. If an invertible T satisfies T>=0 and T^-1>=0 then T is a positive
monomial matrix (a permutation times a positive diagonal).

Proof. For i!=j, 0=(TT^-1)_ij is a sum of nonnegative products. For any positive
entry T_ik, the k-th row of T^-1 can have nonzero entries only in column i. Two
positive entries in the same row of T would force two rows of T^-1 to be
supported on the same singleton and would contradict invertibility. Each row
therefore has exactly one positive entry, and invertibility makes those columns
distinct. This proves the claim.

Thus a uniform linear coordinate change preserving the whole nonnegative
orthant in both directions cannot change negative-support cardinality. More
general transformations may help but require a separate feasibility argument.

5.2 Positive reservoirs transfer the difficulty to acquisition.

For resource consumption H alpha<=h, introduce reservoir variables r_j with
column h_j and output variables y_j with the same column h_j. With residual
resources s>=0, the expanded conservation law is

 s+H r+H y=h.

Moving r_j to y_j is now a one-negative, improving circuit (two identical
columns). But the easy initial lift s=h,r=y=0 contains no reservoir mass to
consume. Preparing reservoir mass r requires exactly H r<=h. Maximizing the
amount prepared is the packing problem max 1^T r subject to H r<=h,r>=0.
Calling this preparation free would simply assume an LP solution.

5.3 That packing acquisition contains unrestricted rational feasibility.

First consider arbitrary rational box feasibility

 A x<=b,  0<=x<=1,  x in R^n.

Write A=A^+-A^- with A^+,A^->=0. If some
d_i=b_i+sum_j A^-_ij<0 then box feasibility is impossible, since the minimum of
the i-th left-hand side over the box is -sum_j A^-_ij.

Otherwise form an always-feasible nonnegative packing LP in u,v>=0:

 maximize sum_j(u_j+v_j)
 subject to A^+ u+A^- v<=b+A^- 1,
            u_j+v_j<=1  for all j.

All coefficients and all right-hand sides are nonnegative. Its optimum is at
most n. It equals n if and only if the original box system is feasible.
For the forward direction take u=x,v=1-x. For the reverse direction, attaining
n forces every complement constraint tight, so v=1-u, and substitution gives
Au<=b. An exact packing optimum and equality test therefore decide box
feasibility. The construction needs O(mn) arithmetic operations.

To retain the full unrestricted rational obstruction, reduce general rational
standard-form feasibility Bx=d,x>=0 to a box without a coefficient-size loop.
Clear input denominators by their product D, giving integer B0,d0. Set

 K=max(1, absolute entries of B0 and d0),
 U=1+m! K^m.

If a nonnegative solution exists, a support-reduction argument gives a basic
feasible solution with at most rank(B0)<=m positive entries. Each positive
coordinate is a Cramer ratio. Its denominator is a nonzero integer of absolute
value at least 1 and its numerator has absolute value at most r! K^r<=m! K^m.
Thus there is a solution in [0,U]^n. Set x=Uz and require 0<=z<=1, B0 U z=d0;
replace the equality by two inequalities, then apply the packing construction.
The denominator product, m!, and K^m take polynomially many arithmetic
operations in m,n; their binary lengths are polynomial in the original input
length. No search over a numerical bound is involved.

General inequalities/free variables convert to rational standard form by
slacks and positive/negative variable parts, with a linear dimension increase.
Therefore a strongly polynomial exact reservoir-acquisition optimizer for this
apparently simple nonnegative packing representation would yield a strongly
polynomial algorithm for unrestricted rational LP feasibility. This is a
reduction, not an impossibility theorem and not a bit-complexity lower bound.

5.4 Why uniform inverse positivity cannot rescue pure consumption.

If a square consumption block H>=0 is invertible, cancellation alpha=H^-1 h
works for every h>=0 precisely when H^-1>=0. By the lemma in 5.1, this requires
H to be monomial. A genuinely shared multi-resource consumption matrix cannot
have this uniform guarantee. Testing H^-1 h>=0 for one h is cheap; finding a
column basis that passes that test is the charged compatibility search.
