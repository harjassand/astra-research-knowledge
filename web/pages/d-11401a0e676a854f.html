# Rejected secretary implementation lead and preserved structural boundaries

Status: the proposed general implementation lead is rejected as prior-subsumed at the level of verified author claims. We did not independently execute or reconstruct every step of Huang's complete algorithm. The two algebraic boundaries below are proved independently; neither is a lower bound on compact implicit algorithms.

## 1. Initial question and decisive prior

The initial question was whether the newly established 1/e probability guarantee for linear matroid secretary selection could be implemented from a compact rational representation without enumerating all ambient subspaces. Abdi–Banihashem–Hajiaghayi–Mittal, arXiv:2609.19118v1 (16 September 2026), and Bérczi–Dughmi–Livanos–Soto–Verdugo, arXiv:2609.20797v1 (17 September), give closely related subspace-law arguments. The first source explicitly leaves its strongest guarantee computationally unrestricted; the law update is a linear program over all ambient subspaces and eligible state transitions. Its separate polynomial-oracle constant-factor results do not supply the 1/e implementation.

The tempting task was to compress those laws or replace the ambient subspaces by ground-generated flats. It ceased to be a defensible general open lead after reading Zhiyi Huang, arXiv:2609.30421v1, submitted 24 September 2026 18:17:26 UTC, "Settling the Matroid Secretary Problem." Theorem 1 claims a 1/e ordinal guarantee for all unknown matroids in expected polynomial time and independence-oracle queries. Sections 3–5 give compact latent forward/reverse kernels, rather than storing an ambient-subspace law. This stronger author-claimed comparator must be acknowledged before proposing another implementation as novel.

Reading depth: all Sections 1–5 prose and the preserved TeX algorithms for the online, forward, and reverse procedures. The detailed whole-proof gate and an independently implemented selector remain unperformed. Primary version, authors, dates, and original source are preserved under sources/metric_geometry10/cycle15. Model agreement is not validation of this claimed resolution.

The paper's central latent state contains only one real coordinate per element. The forward and reverse updates have balance identities relating their subprobability kernels and the offline law, and their expected recursive oracle costs are bounded polynomially. The interface uses real uniform arrivals, logarithms, exponentials, and a spanning-time clock. We have not separately established an expected polynomial **bit-cost** simulation from unbiased random bits. That is an uncharged interface here, not a counterexample to the stated paper theorem or a revived novelty claim.

## 2. Ground-generated flats can miss the violating subspace

Work over F_2^3. Let ell=span(e_1), and supply the six nonzero vectors other than e_1, grouped in three pairs:

    (e_2, e_1+e_2), (e_3, e_1+e_3), (e_2+e_3, e_1+e_2+e_3).

Let S_i be the span of pair i, and let mu assign probability 1/3 to each S_i. All S_i are distinct planes through ell. Consider the load inequalities

    E_mu dim(S intersect W) <= beta dim W,    beta=2/3.

Every ground-generated subspace passes. The zero space has load zero. A ground line belongs to exactly one S_i, hence has load 1/3 <= 2/3. If a plane W equals one S_i, its intersection profile is (2,1,1), so its load is 4/3 = beta*2. Every other plane has profile (1,1,1) and load 1 <= 4/3. The whole space has load 2 = beta*3.

The missing line ell is not generated by a ground subset: over F_2 a one-dimensional generated span would require a supplied copy of e_1. But ell has profile (1,1,1) and load 1 > 2/3. It is the unique violation. Thus ground-generated flats cannot replace all ambient constraints for arbitrary laws on generated spans.

Scope: this does not show that this particular law occurs in either secretary algorithm's reachable state space. It does not prove an algorithmic lower bound. It rejects a specific universal constraint-replacement lemma, even when the law itself is supported on ground-generated spans.

## 3. The complete intersection-load profile determines the law

Let L be all nonzero subspaces of F_q^r and define the rational matrix

    K_{S,W} = dim(S intersect W),    S,W in L.

For k>=1 put c_k=product_{h=1}^{k-1}(1-q^h), so c_1=1. For every d>=1,

    d = sum_{k=1}^d [d choose k]_q c_k.                      (1)

Here is a direct derivation. Möbius inversion of the rank function on the subspace lattice gives

    c_k = sum_{j=0}^k [k choose j]_q (-1)^{k-j} q^{(k-j)(k-j-1)/2} j.

Set P_k(t)=product_{h=0}^{k-1}(1-q^h t). The q-binomial theorem identifies the preceding sum with k P_k(1)-P_k'(1). Since P_k(1)=0 and -P_k'(1)=product_{h=1}^{k-1}(1-q^h), this is the claimed c_k, proving (1) by inversion. The empty-product case k=1 is included.

Order L by nondecreasing dimension and let Z_{S,U}=1[U subset S]. Then Z is triangular with diagonal one. By (1),

    K = Z diag(c_dim U) Z^T,
    det K = product_{U in L} c_dim U != 0.                 (2)

All factors are nonzero because q>1. Therefore a full load profile F_mu(W)=sum_S mu(S)dim(S intersect W) determines every mass mu(S) for nonzero S. If total probability is supplied, the remaining zero-space mass is determined too. A distinct smaller-support law cannot preserve the full load profile exactly.

The number of ambient subspaces grows as q^(floor(r^2/4)) up to polynomial/exponential-in-r factors. This proves a full-profile dimension obstruction. It does not prevent succinct implicit samplers, a law with different but sufficient load inequalities, or a compact algorithm such as Huang's. It also does not establish an information-theoretic bit lower bound for matrix inputs.

Prior: factorization (2) is a specialization of the classical meet-matrix determinant mechanism associated with Lindström's "Determinants on semilattices" (Proc. AMS, 1969) and Wilf's related earlier work. The primary AMS/Penn PDF access attempts returned HTTP 403. We preserve that failure and claim neither full reading nor novelty for the determinant mechanism. Formula (1) and its application to this profile are independently reconstructed above; their exact prior priority is UNKNOWN.

## 4. Separation interface and charges

For an explicit law with support S_i and rational masses p_i summing to one, let B_i be the quotient map V -> V/S_i. For beta<1,

    sum_i p_i dim(S_i intersect W) <= beta dim W

is equivalent to

    dim W <= sum_i (p_i/(1-beta)) dim B_i(W).

This resembles the BL dimension inequalities. However, BL's whole-space balance need not hold, and a real operator-scaling theorem is not automatically valid over an arbitrary finite representation field. Replicating rows by the common denominator costs its numeric value, potentially exponentially many rows in its bit length. A submodular/uncrossing reformulation alone does not charge an efficient acquisition of all separating subspaces. These are interface constraints, not a claim that prior numerical or noncommutative methods fail.
