MODEL
Use the same J_-, E=Jz+N/2, R=J_+J_- and D[L] convention as the frozen
COLLECTIVE_DECAY_ENTANGLEMENT.txt. For Gamma>0 and finite nu>0 let
 dot(rho)=Gamma[(nu+1)D[J_-]+nu D[J_+]](rho).
The generator is specified explicitly; no other thermalization model is
silently substituted. The conserved spin weights q_b give
 beta=sum_b bq_b, jbar=sum_b j_bq_b=N/2-beta.
The observables depend only the autonomous j,m populations, so initial
coherences and multiplicity operators do not affect the following witness.

1. EXACT STATIONARY SECTOR LAW
In spin j_b with K=2j_b, write r=m+j_b=0,...,K. Birth and death rates are
 a_r=Gamma nu (r+1)(K-r),
 d_r=Gamma(nu+1)r(K-r+1).
For K>=1 they form an irreducible birth/death chain. Put z=nu/(nu+1) in(0,1).
Detailed balance gives the UNIQUE stationary population law
 pi_K(r)=z^r/S_K(z), S_K(z)=sum_(r=0)^K z^r.
For K0 use pi_0(0)=1. Every sector retains its original weight q_b.
Let mu_K=sum_r r z^r/S_K. Then
 E_infty=beta+sum_b q_b mu_(2j_b)>=beta,
 R_infty=sum_b q_b sum_r pi_K(r)r(K-r+1).                  (1)
The mean equation, derived directly from the two Lindblad terms, is
 d<E>/dt=Gamma[nu N-2nu<E>-<R>].
At stationarity this gives the exact identity
 R_infty=nu(N-2E_infty)<=2nu jbar.                         (2)
The signs follow from J_-J_+=J_+J_--2Jz. The stationary mean is <=N/2,
as is also clear from the decreasing geometric population law.

2. SUFFICIENT ENTANGLEMENT CONDITION
Every fully separable state satisfies <R>>=<E>^2/N by the frozen product/
convexity proof. Define
 Delta=beta^2/N-2nu jbar.
If Delta>0, (1)-(2) give strict stationary violation and <W_beta><=-Delta,
where W_beta=R-(2beta/N)E+(beta^2/N)I is nonnegative on every SEP state.
For an initially SEP input this will be activation, rather than only a
stationary entanglement detection. The criterion is sufficient, not an exact
transition for every initial state. Full-rank thermal inputs have beta>0 and
jbar>0, so EVERY such admitted input has a nonzero guaranteed window
 0<nu<beta^2/(2N jbar).                                   (3)
The scalar q_b,beta and jbar are obtained from the O(N^2) positive thermal
sums in the zero-T note. If jbar0 then all populated sectors are already dark,
and beta>0 gives R0,E=N/2 and entanglement at every time; no positive-window
division by0 is used.

3. EXACT GAP COMPARISON
For K>=1 every nonzero ladder factor r(K-r+1) and(r+1)(K-r) is>=K.
Compare the chain to a reflecting constant-rate walk on0,...,K with births
Gamma K nu and deaths Gamma K(nu+1). It has the SAME pi_K. The Dirichlet
form on every edge of the full chain dominates that of this walk, so its
spectral gap is no smaller.

For constant a,b>0 the reversible tridiagonal matrix has eigenvalues0 and
 a+b-2sqrt(ab)cos[l pi/(K+1)], l=1,...,K.
For completeness: symmetrize by sqrt(pi); the interior diagonal is a+b,
the end diagonals are a and b, and off-diagonals are-sqrt(ab). For
theta=l pi/(K+1), the nonzero eigenvector components
 g_r=sin[(r+1)theta]-sqrt(b/a)sin(r theta)
satisfy the interior equation and both reflecting boundary equations. These
K distinct vectors plus the zero vector sqrt(pi) account for all K+1 eigenvalues.
Consequently the full sector gap gamma_K obeys
 gamma_K>=Gamma K(sqrt(nu+1)-sqrt(nu))^2
          >=Gamma K/(4nu+2).                              (4)
The final reciprocal bound uses2sqrt[nu(nu+1)]<=2nu+1. No numerical spectral
scan, large matrix calculation or infinite-N extrapolation enters (4).

4. EXPLICIT FINITE ONSET TIME
For any initial conditional sector population P0, reversible L2 contraction
and Cauchy-Schwarz give
 TV(P_t,pi_K)<= (1/2)pi_min^(-1/2)exp(-gamma_K t).
Indeed the initial chi-square norm is<=1/pi_min-1 and contracts by the gap.
Here pi_min=z^K/S_K, so
 log(1/pi_min)<=log(K+1)+K log(1+1/nu).
Combining sectors with K<=N, excluding the already stationary K0 sectors,
gives a bound on the full diagonal population TV distance
 epsilon(t)<=(1/2)sqrt(N+1)(1+1/nu)^(N/2)
                    exp[-Gamma t/(4nu+2)].                (5)
This controls R and E even for initially coherent inputs; only populations
are used, not a claimed trace-distance bound on all quantum coherences.

Always E(t)>=beta. Since0<=R<=M_N=floor((N+1)^2/4), (5) implies
 R(t)<=R_infty+M_N epsilon(t)<=2nu jbar+M_N epsilon(t).
If Delta>0, EVERY
 t>(4nu+2)/Gamma * max{0,
       log[M_N/(2Delta)]+(1/2)log(N+1)+(N/2)log(1+1/nu)}    (6)
therefore has R(t)<beta^2/N<=E(t)^2/N and <W_beta><0. The bound is
strict and holds at all later times. Finite-time activation does not rest
solely on a limiting-state argument. Negative logarithms are handled by the
max with0; already entangled/dark inputs do not require a false onset claim.

The rates, physical time and conditioned scalar precision are charged by
(4)-(6). This conservative mixing bound is not uniform as nu->0; the
separate zero-T proof has its stronger finite bound. A huge binary nu or a
tiny margin Delta can make the displayed numerical time/precision expensive.
No polynomial-in-input-bits physical-time claim is made.

5. MAXIMALLY MIXED INPUT: EVERY FIXED FINITE NU AT LARGE N
For I/2^N, E[J^2]=3N/4. Thus jbar<=sqrt(E[j^2])<=sqrt(3N)/2 and
 beta=N/2-jbar>=(N-sqrt(3N))/2.
For N>3 a sufficient positive-bath occupation window is
 nu<(sqrtN-sqrt3)^2/[4sqrt(3N)],                           (7)
asymptotic to sqrtN/(4sqrt3). Equivalently, a sufficient dimension is
 N>3[1+2nu+2sqrt(nu(nu+1))]^2.
The simpler N>48(nu+1)^2 implies this condition. Therefore EVERY fixed finite
nu>0 has an all-sufficiently-large-N maximally mixed input that becomes
entangled at a finite time bounded by (6). The initial state is a product
of maximally mixed qubits, not an unverified separable ansatz.

The root's alternative all-N bound beta>=(N-1)/4 also yields the sufficient
window nu<(N-1)^2/[16sqrt3 N^(3/2)]. These are different conservative bounds;
