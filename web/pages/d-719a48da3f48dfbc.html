# A stronger result: a classical archive built from pair measurements

I obtained a concrete acquisition-and-query protocol, rather than only a memory-scaling theorem. It removes three restrictions from the previous construction: **the spectrum need not be known, acquisition does not require an arbitrary collective angular measurement, and later single-qubit measurement queries can be answered without reconstructing a quantum sample.**

The strongest finite operational statement is:

> **For one million identically prepared qubits with unknown direction and actual Bloch radius \(0.01\), a protocol using singlet–triplet measurements on pairs and individual-qubit readouts can retain just 57 classical bits and achieve global reconstruction error below \(0.078\).**
>
> From that record, the complete outcome distribution of a later specified single-qubit measurement protocol can be approximately sampled classically. No quantum memory is retained after acquisition.

The acquisition cost is explicit: a conservative cap of **41,446,532 pair measurements**, followed by at most one million individual readouts. This is an ideal-primitive theorem with an executable mathematical prototype—not a million-qubit hardware demonstration.

I also obtained a separate, matching upper-and-lower-bound result for **unknown-spectrum compression**. These are substantive extensions, but historical novelty and a field-changing experimental advantage have not been established.

## 1. What changed from the previous result

The supplied research already contained the important starting inequality: a coherent-state measure/prepare channel approximates a thermal spin state with error at most
\[
\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta},
\]
uniformly in the spin size. The original obstruction was turning that into finite storage and an operational protocol without concealing a continuous measurement outcome or difficult reconstruction step. Pasted text

The useful additional connection was a **2025 random-SWAP protocol**, not another asymptotic compression formula. Brahmachari, Hulse, Pfister and Marvian showed that repeated random singlet–triplet measurements can extract Schur information while preserving the relevant quantum state. That extraction mechanism is prior art; it is not something I discovered here. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05046?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The continuation adds the following chain:
\[
\boxed{
\text{unknown weakly polarized ensemble}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{overlapping pair measurements}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{individual readouts}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{finite classical record}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{delayed measurement queries}.
}
\]

The nontrivial mathematical step is showing why the individual readouts preserve the **whole statistical experiment**, rather than merely producing a reasonable estimate of the unknown direction.

## 2. The acquisition protocol and its guarantee

Write
\[
\rho_b=\frac{I+b\cdot\sigma}{2},
\qquad \delta=|b|<1,
\qquad \rho_{n,b}=\rho_b^{\otimes n}.
\]

The input consists of \(n\) copies of the **same unknown qubit state**. This is not compression of an arbitrary \(n\)-qubit state, and it does not preserve a purification.

### Acquisition

First, repeatedly select a random pair of remaining qubits and measure whether it is a singlet or triplet. Remove both qubits only when the outcome is a singlet. Run for
\[
T=\left\lceil 2n\ln(n/\eta)\right\rceil
\]
tests, where \(\eta\) is the extraction-error budget.

Next, measure each remaining qubit individually along an independently chosen random axis. If the chosen axis is \(a_i\) and the measured sign is \(s_i\), form
\[
x_i=s_i a_i.
\]
If \(L\) qubits remain after successful extraction, calculate
\[
Y=\frac3L\sum_{i=1}^{L}x_i,
\qquad
\widehat u=\frac{Y}{|Y|}.
\]

Finally, round \(\widehat u\) to a finite angular grid and retain only the remaining-qubit count and the two grid indices. The measurement-axis sequence, pair-selection history and singlet outcomes are discarded.

For a direction-rounding error budget \(\varepsilon\), take
\[
N_\theta=\left\lceil\frac{\pi\sqrt n}{2\varepsilon}\right\rceil,
\qquad
N_\phi=\left\lceil\frac{\pi\sqrt n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.
\]
The entire retained alphabet has at most
\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm pair}
=
(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1)(N_\theta+1)N_\phi
=
O(n^2\varepsilon^{-2})
}
\]
labels.

Neither the actual polarization \(\delta\) nor its direction is supplied to this encoder.

### General error theorem

Let \(p_L(n,\delta)\) denote the usual spin-sector probabilities, and set
\[
r=\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta},
\qquad a=1-r.
\]

The construction gives
\[
\boxed{
e\le
\eta+\varepsilon+
\sum_L p_L(n,\delta)
\min\!\left\{1,\frac{R_LH_L(r)}4\right\},
}
\]
where \(e\) is **global trace-distance error**,
\[
e=\frac12\left\|
\mathcal D\mathcal E(\rho_{n,b})-\rho_{n,b}
\right\|_1.
\]

The angular-estimation risk satisfies, for every \(0<h<1\),
\[
R_L
\le
\min\!\left\{
\pi^2,\frac{8\pi^2}{L},
\frac{6}{L(1-h)^2}
+
\pi^2\exp\!\left[-\frac{Lh^2}{4+(8/3)h}\right]
\right\},
\]
and the mixing-law curvature is bounded by
\[
H_L(r)=
\frac{(L+1)a}{r(1-r^{L+1})}
\left[
\frac32(1-r^{L+2})
-(L+2)a r^{L+2}
\right].
\]

The spin-zero contribution is zero.

These are finite formulas. They do not require extrapolating a fixed-spectrum asymptotic approximation into a collision regime.

### A finite certificate, not just an asymptotic claim

Use
\[
n=10^6,\qquad
\delta=0.01,\qquad
\eta=0.001,\qquad
\varepsilon=0.005.
\]

The explicit record count is
\[
D_{\rm pair}=98{,}696{,}860{,}072{,}825{,}359<2^{57}.
\]

For the error calculation, separate sectors below \(L=5000\). A concentration bound gives
\[
\Pr(L<5000)\le e^{-12.5}<0.000004.
\]
For all remaining sectors, the risk and curvature estimates above, with \(h=0.2\), give an additional error below \(0.071037\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
e
&<
\underbrace{0.001}_{\text{extraction}}
+
\underbrace{0.005}_{\text{direction rounding}}
+
\underbrace{0.000004}_{\text{low-spin tail}}
+
\underbrace{0.071037}_{\text{local readout}}\\
&<0.078.
\end{aligned}
\]

The high-precision evaluation is approximately \(0.07704065847\); the rounded \(0.078\) bound also has an elementary slack proof, so it does not depend on trusting floating-point output.

Here, \(0.078\) means that **the probability of any event in a later fixed measurement can change by at most 7.8 percentage points**. It is not a claim of predicting individual quantum outcomes with 92.2% accuracy.

This is also not a trivial “forget everything and prepare the maximally mixed state” construction. Over two antipodal inputs, every zero-memory reconstruction has worst-case error at least
\[
\frac{1-(1-\delta^2)^{n/2}}2,
\]
which is essentially \(1/2\) for this example.

## 3. Why the construction works

### Pair extraction preserves the relevant state

Condition on total-spin label \(L\). With \(M\) qubits remaining, let
\[
k=\frac{M-L}{2}
\]
be the number of singlet removals still required.

The probability that a uniformly selected pair is a singlet is
\[
p_{M,L}
=
\frac{(M-L)(M+L+2)}{4M(M-1)}
=
\frac{k(M-k+1)}{M(M-1)}
\ge \frac{k}{2n}.
\]

Thus the expected remaining deficiency contracts:
\[
\mathbb E k_T
\le \frac n2 e^{-T/(2n)}.
\]
This bounds the probability that extraction remains incomplete. Pair projectors act on the multiplicity information rather than disturbing the spin-state factor; successful singlet removal preserves that factor. This is the property exploited by the established random-SWAP method. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05046v2)

Importantly, this uses **overlapping pairs across the sample**. It is not a protocol that permanently divides the input into independent two-qubit blocks.

### The remaining thermal state has an exact positive mixture representation

In a spin-\(L/2\) sector,
\[
\tau_L=
\frac1{Z_L}\sum_{k=0}^{L}r^k|k\rangle\langle k|,
\qquad
Z_L=\sum_{k=0}^{L}r^k.
\]

Let \(v=b/\delta\), \(t=(1+v\cdot u)/2\), and let \(\mu\) be normalized spherical area. Then
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{L,b}
=
\int P_{L,r,v}(u)
\,|u\rangle\langle u|^{\otimes L}\,d\mu(u),
}
\]
with the positive density
\[
P_{L,r,v}(u)
=
\frac{(L+1)r^{L+1}}
{Z_L[1-(1-r)t]^{L+2}}.
\]

This identity is exact. Its \(k\)-th diagonal coefficient follows from
\[
\binom Lk
\int_0^1
\frac{t^{L-k}(1-t)^k}{(1-at)^{L+2}}\,dt
=
\frac{r^{k-L-1}}{L+1}.
\]

The latent direction \(u\) is not revealed to the encoder. The representation is useful because linearity lets us analyze the local readouts as measurements on \(L\) pure copies, conditional on that latent direction.

### The decisive step: preserve the mixture, not every pure component

On a pure input with direction \(u\), the signed-axis observation \(x_i\) has density
\[
1+u\cdot x_i.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb EY=u,\qquad
\mathbb E|Y-u|^2=\frac8L.
\]

A direct attempt to reproduce each pure tensor power from this estimate would incur substantial error. That would not solve the problem.

Instead, compare the **distribution of directions in the mixture** before and after readout. The readout induces a rotation-covariant angular kernel \(K_L\). For any smooth spherical density \(f\),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(K_Lf,f)
\le
\frac{R_L}{4}
\int\|\nabla^2f\|_{\rm op}\,d\mu.
}
\]

The proof is a geodesic Taylor expansion. Averaging over displacement directions cancels the first-order term. The second-order remainder is controlled by the integrated Hessian and mean squared angular displacement.

Applying this to the exact density \(P_{L,r,v}\) yields the displayed \(H_L(r)\). The important scaling is
\[
R_L=O(L^{-1}),
\qquad
H_L(r)=O(L\delta)
\]
for small polarization. Therefore
\[
R_LH_L(r)=O(\delta),
\]
**uniformly in the spin size**.

This is why increasing the sample size does not force an increasing reconstruction error. The additional angular uncertainty from local readout is small compared with the uncertainty already present in the thermal mixture.

In particular, as \(\delta_n\to0\), this pair-measurement construction has vanishing global error when \(\eta_n,\varepsilon_n\to0\). The underlying sample can still contain an increasing amount of distinguishable information when \(n\delta_n^2\to\infty\).

### Reconstruction needs only singles and singlets

Given a record \((L,u)\), define
\[
\Omega_{n,L,u}
=
\mathbb E_{\pi}
\,U_\pi
\left[
(|u\rangle\langle u|)^{\otimes L}
\otimes
(|\psi^-\rangle\langle\psi^-|)^{\otimes(n-L)/2}
\right]
U_\pi^\dagger,
\]
where \(\pi\) is a fresh uniform permutation.

The aligned singles have spin \(L/2\); the singlets have spin zero. Averaging over permutations makes the multiplicity factor maximally mixed while leaving the coherent spin factor unchanged. Thus this is precisely the required embedded spin-state reconstruction.

There is **no inverse Schur transform in this decoder**. The random matching is generated afresh, not retained as hidden input-dependent information.

## 4. The practical capability: delayed queries without quantum reconstruction

The reconstruction above is a mixture of products of single-qubit states and disjoint singlet pairs. That makes a substantial class of later measurements classically simulable.

For a single qubit measured along \(w_i\),
\[
\Pr(s)=\frac{1+s\,u\cdot w_i}{2}.
\]

For a singlet pair measured along \(w_i,w_j\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(a,b)=\frac14(1-ab\,w_i\cdot w_j).
}
\]

A classical decoder samples a fresh matching, samples the single-qubit outcomes, and samples each singlet pair using this formula. It generates the **entire joint outcome vector** in \(O(n)\) arithmetic and random draws.

For a common measurement axis \(w\), the count of plus outcomes is even simpler:
\[
\boxed{
K=\frac{n-L}{2}
+
\operatorname{Binomial}\!\left(
L,\frac{1+u\cdot w}{2}
\right).
}
\]

Thus the record supports a postponed choice of spin-measurement axis or count threshold without storing the original quantum systems or preparing them again.

The global trace-distance guarantee is stronger than guaranteeing accuracy for a selected list of observables. For a fixed statistical decision problem with loss in \([0,1]\), it bounds the loss of achievable statistical performance by the same error. For general entangled measurements, however, efficiently evaluating the required decoder probabilities is not established; the efficient classical query implementation here concerns single-site interrogation.

There are two essential interpretation limits. The distributional guarantee averages over the original encoding randomness; **one realized record is not an exact description of the unknown input state**. Repeated queries to that record also do not manufacture independent experimental data. The comparison covers a later measurement protocol specified independently of the realized encoder record, including adaptive single-site actions within that protocol—not an unrestricted claim about selecting new experiments after examining the record.

Those distinctions prevent an invalid “classical cloning” interpretation.

## 5. A separate theoretical result: optimal storage without knowing the spectrum

The previous leading-rate theorem assumed a known Bloch radius. I extended it to the whole unknown-spectrum family
\[
\mathcal B_R=\{\rho_b^{\otimes n}:|b|\le R\},
\qquad
\kappa=nR^2.
\]

### Matching leading bounds

For \(0<R\le1/2\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), there is a classical code, knowing only the envelope \(R\), satisfying
\[
D\le10^{12}(1+\kappa)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{-6},
\qquad
e\le\frac{2R}{1+R}+\varepsilon.
\]

Every classical, quantum or hybrid code with uniform error at most \(1/8\) satisfies
\[
D\ge\frac{(1+\kappa)^{3/2}}{65536}.
\]

Consequently, when
\[
R_n\to0,\qquad nR_n^2\to\infty,
\]
the optimal leading cost is
\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac32+o(1)\right)\log_2(nR_n^2),
}
\]
and it is attainable **entirely classically**.

The explicit constant in the upper bound is loose; neither its error exponent nor its additive bit overhead is claimed to be optimal.

### The mechanism behind the upper bound

Let \(q_b(L,du)\) be the continuous coherent-measurement law, and take the maximally mixed law \(q_0\) as reference. Its exact likelihood ratio is
\[
\frac{dq_b}{dq_0}(L,u)
=
(1-|b|^2)^{(n-L)/2}(1+b\cdot u)^L.
\]

The vector
\[
X=(L+2)u
\]
satisfies the exact identities
\[
\mathbb E_bX=nb,
\qquad
\mathbb E_b|X-nb|^2
=
3n-n|b|^2+2\mathbb E_bL+4
\le9n.
\]

Therefore the likely records occupy a three-dimensional region of radius roughly \(nR\), with statistical resolution roughly \(\sqrt n\). Its effective cell count is
\[
\left(\frac{nR}{\sqrt n}\right)^3
=
(nR^2)^{3/2}.
\]

That dimensional argument alone would not prove a compression theorem. The needed additional construction is a decoder that, for each cell, **resamples the fine record from a nearby reference model conditioned on that cell**. A uniform log-likelihood Lipschitz bound controls the replacement error. It does not simply prepare a product state at the estimated cell center, which would introduce the wrong reconstruction noise.

### Why quantum memory cannot improve the leading cost

Pack the radius-\(R\) Bloch ball with points separated by \(24/\sqrt n\). There are at least a constant times \(\kappa^{3/2}\) such points. Ordinary Pauli measurements distinguish them with uniformly high success probability.

A good compressor must preserve that success. If \(N\) hypotheses are encoded into a \(D\)-dimensional memory, with encoded states \(\omega_i\) and decoding POVM \(F_i\),
\[
P_{\rm success}
=
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(F_i\omega_i)
\le
\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}F_i
=
\frac DN.
\]
This gives the matching dimension lower bound even for quantum memory.

The extra radial parameter raises the leading cost from the previous known-radius value \(\log_2\kappa\) to \(\tfrac32\log_2\kappa\).

**This optimal-memory construction is separate from the pair-measurement implementation.** I have not shown that the latter attains the optimal leading bit count.

There is also no contradiction with the established impossibility of faithful all-classical compression for fixed noncommuting families: here the family itself shrinks toward the maximally mixed state. The fixed-family distinction is explicit in quantum population-coding research. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03372) Likewise, the 5 October 2026 minimum-description paper studies fixed, distinct positive eigenvalues, not this uniform moving-collision limit. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06742)

## 6. What was implemented and tested

The pack contains both an exact finite version of the original coherent channel and the new pair-measurement construction.

For the **exact collective-angular version**, Gauss–Legendre polar quadrature combined with a uniform azimuthal grid integrates its measure/prepare channel exactly. Its record count is
\[
D_n=\sum_{L\equiv n\pmod2}(L+1)(2L+1)
=\frac{n^3}{3}+O(n^2).
\]

Using the exact spin-sector transition kernel, I calculated the following full-ensemble errors:

| Input copies | Bloch radius | \(n\delta^2\) | Stored bits | Calculated global trace error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 0.10 | 1 | 19 | 0.051515 |
| 1,000 | 0.10 | 10 | 29 | 0.064025 |
| 10,000 | 0.05 | 25 | 39 | **0.034395** |

These are symmetry-reduced calculations of **global** trace distance, not single-qubit marginal errors. They are not measurements on a device. The code tracks analytic truncation allowances, but its floating-point roundoff is not interval-certified. The rigorous channel bound remains \(2\delta/(1+\delta)\).

The pair protocol’s rounded \(0.078\) example instead has the analytic certificate given above. Its checks include **476 exact rational positive-mixture identities**, **4,096 exact singlet-removal probability cases**, numerical Hessian checks, and finite direction-grid checks. The other suite verifies finite POVM completeness, the thermal channel inequalities, packed records, and the singlet reconstruction identity in the full Hilbert space for small systems. All assertions passed.

The small-block investigation also recovered an important limitation: independent single-qubit readout and independent two-qubit-block readout do not recover all simultaneous local information at the same sample cost. The two-copy Fisher-information advantage is established prior art, not a new finding here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06112) The overlapping-pair construction avoids treating those restricted block measurements as sufficient by themselves.

## 7. What this establishes—and what remains unvalidated

The practical implication is an explicit architecture for **acquiring a weak-polarization quantum experiment once, retaining ordinary classical data, and postponing a substantial class of measurement choices**. The retained record preserves more than a chosen estimator or observable list: it approximately preserves the density-operator statistical experiment.

The significance is not merely reducing \(n\) qubits to logarithmic storage. Efficient \(O(\log n)\)-qubit compression was already known. The distinction here is **eliminating retained quantum memory**, removing actual-spectrum knowledge, and giving a pair-measurement acquisition route with finite error accounting. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03542)

The engineering costs remain substantial. The pair protocol needs access to the initial quantum sample, overlapping pair operations, and potentially extensive routing. Its ideal guarantees do not yet establish a favorable device-noise budget. No hardware execution, physical performance advantage, or general application to correlated many-body states is claimed.

So the achieved result is stronger than the previous asymptotic theorem: **a specified acquisition protocol, a 57-bit finite example, an efficient classical query engine, and matching leading storage bounds for the unknown-spectrum problem.** It has concrete operational consequences. Calling it an established groundbreaking discovery would still require independent proof and priority review, and an experimentally credible implementation.
