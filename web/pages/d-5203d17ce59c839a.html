# Cycle 2: multi-context perturbation transport and a finite-sample discriminator

**Status:** primary-method reconstruction completed; transport novelty rejected; randomized design derived with finite-sample error control. No new biological experiment was run and no biological response is claimed.

Cycle 1 established the classical conditional-response transport formula and a no-go SCM pair. This cycle asks whether a recent multi-context cell-perturbation model changes that ceiling, and whether one can make the next recipient experiment precise enough to separate measured response invariance from an actual effect reversal.

## Reconstructed method: pertTF

The primary source is the March 2026 bioRxiv preprint [*pertTF: context-aware AI modeling for genome-scale and cross-system perturbation prediction*](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.12.711379), deposited in PMC as [PMC13015719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13015719/). The deposited full text is saved as [pertTF.xml](source/pertTF.xml). Its journal metadata identify bioRxiv; this report treats it as a preprint, not as peer-reviewed validation.

The study builds a pooled CRISPR knockout dataset with 79 mutant hPSC clonal lines, 30 pancreas-lineage/diabetes-risk genes, five differentiation stages, 14 cell types, and over 87,000 cells. pertTF encodes expression and cell context into cell embeddings; its multi-task objectives include masked-expression reconstruction, cell-type and genotype/perturbation classification, and cell-type/composition prediction. A perturbation module maps a wild-type embedding plus a perturbation representation to a predicted perturbed embedding; expression is decoded with a negative-binomial model and training adds contrastive objectives. Its new-gene representation is supplied by a GEARS GNN trained on Gene Ontology relations and a separate K562 Perturb-seq dataset. Thus the unseen-gene experiment is not source-only extrapolation: it imports external gene/perturbation information.

The methods describe a five-part split: training includes 80% of perturbations and 80% of cell types, with separate validation, unseen-cell-type, unseen-perturbation, and joint unseen-gene/type sets. The regimes have different information boundaries:

| Evaluation | Held out | Still available to fitting or representation | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| New cell type | Perturbed cells from the held-out type | Wild-type cells from that type | Predicting a perturbation response conditional on a measured target-context baseline representation |
| New genotype | All cells with that genotype | Other genotypes, target wild-type cells, and frozen external GEARS embeddings | Prediction for an unseen perturbation using external gene priors |
| New genotype + new type | Perturbed cells for the joint holdout | Target-type wild-type cells plus the external gene representation | A harder prediction split, still supplied with target-context baseline data and an external gene prior |
| iPSC CRISPRi | Most of 50 genes absent from knockout training | A separate iPSC Perturb-seq with >400 guides and >100,000 cells | Cross-perturbation-strategy prediction; the paper says comparisons were restricted to cells with strong predicted knockdown effects, so this is responder-filtered rather than an assignment-level ITT estimate |
| Primary-islet disease cohorts | Primary-cell data used for adaptation/inference | 100–300 labelled primary cells for cell-type annotation; disease-cohort RNA profiles | Cell-type adaptation and latent perturbation-state inference in observational donors; perturbation labels are unknown and no randomized perturbation is performed in this cohort |

The main primary-islet transfer stage fine-tunes masked-expression and cell-type-classification components on labelled primary islet cells while freezing the perturbation tasks. It then infers latent PDX1/HNF4A/NEUROD1 perturbation probabilities in non-diabetic, pre-T2D, and T2D donors. This is a learned-state association with disease context, not a randomized estimate of a primary-islet perturbation effect. The paper also reports a separate validation using a CellOT-associated dataset with siRNA-mediated RFX6 knockdown and concordant signatures. That is a useful, narrow perturbation check; it does not turn the disease-cohort adaptation into randomized cross-system transport or establish broad invariance across tissues/species.

This distinction is consistent with CellOT's own modeling contract: [CellOT](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01969-x) learns a distributional map between unpaired control and perturbed cells using neural optimal transport. Its authors state that out-of-distribution prediction requires both similar observed control samples and training cases with similar perturbation responses, and report failure for patients with unique responses. A learned coupling or embedding alignment can predict under its training regularities; it does not identify the counterfactual target effect when the target response mechanism is unrestricted.

## Result and ceiling

The method reconstruction does not lift the first-cycle causal-identification ceiling. The paper is meaningful recent multi-context predictive work, and it contains perturbation experiments, but its most prominent primary-islet application uses observational disease cohorts plus a small labelled-cell adaptation step. The held-out-cell-type split deliberately retains target-context wild-type cells; the unseen-gene split imports an external GNN prior; and the independent iPSC check filters to cells with strong predicted knockdown. These support scoped prediction claims under those information contracts. They do not provide a theorem that causal effects transport to an unrandomized tissue, species, or recipient model.

The classical positive result remains the conditional-response standardization formula in [PROOF_AND_TRANSPORT.md](../PROOF_AND_TRANSPORT.md). The unrestricted target-mechanism no-go witness remains the two-world SCM in that file. Both are standard transportability territory; neither is advanced as novel. The approximate-shift bound is elementary and leaves the unknown mechanism-drift term unestimated. Cycle 2 therefore rejects an elevated theorem/novelty claim. It contributes a testable experiment contract and exact finite-sample concentration bounds, not a new general transport law.

## What the finite-sample design can decide

The companion [FINITE_SAMPLE_DESIGN.md](FINITE_SAMPLE_DESIGN.md) specifies three contexts: source `S`, candidate recipient `T`, and a genuinely held-out context `H`. It uses randomized target perturbations and matched negative controls in each context, baseline-state strata measured before treatment, common-support standardization, blinded source-model predictions, two independent guides, matched exposure/target engagement, and donor-level blocks. Its bounded-outcome Hoeffding intervals give nonasymptotic, simultaneous coverage for the finite response panel.

The design can certify either (a) equivalence of **predeclared measured causal-response surfaces** within fixed margins over shared baseline-state strata, or (b) an average causal effect reversal for `T` relative to `S`, with `H` showing that the reversal is not shared by the held-out comparator. Other outcomes return `UNKNOWN`/inconclusive. It cannot establish that every latent biochemical mechanism is invariant: finite observed endpoint distributions leave unmeasured pathways unrestricted. Nor can a finite sample make the statistical decision error-free; the stated guarantee is familywise error at most `α` under independent bounded blocks, and margins are required.

## Data that would reopen the question

The causal transfer question becomes empirically testable for a named perturbation and endpoint when the following are acquired together:

1. Randomized perturbation and matched-control wells in `S`, `T`, and a context-level held-out `H`, using the same declared perturbation version, delivery, exposure, and harvest protocol (or a separately declared engagement-matched regimen).
2. Independent donor/clone-level blocks; guide identity; pre-treatment state labels; baseline support; direct target engagement; and the same bounded, predeclared proximal and functional readouts in all contexts.
3. Per-well assignment and raw/processed expression or functional data, enough independent biological blocks for the selected margins, and a separate pilot to calibrate exposure without selecting on post-treatment engagement.
4. For a reanalysis of pertTF's primary-islet claims, the full donor/guide/siRNA assignments, cell-level perturbation status, target-engagement measurements, controls, outcome definitions, and code/splits for its separate RFX6 validation. The observational disease cohort alone cannot resolve causal effect transfer.

For a stronger mathematical claim, one would need a newly specified structural restriction that links unmeasured causal mechanisms across domains, plus a proof that uses that restriction beyond classical transportability. Neither the pertTF preprint nor the retrieved Astra cell-biomedical route supplies such a restriction.
