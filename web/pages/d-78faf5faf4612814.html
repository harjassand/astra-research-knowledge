Cycle 4 primary-literature audit: sparse catalyst-substrate WR family
======================================================================

Checked: 2026-10-08
Status: bounded primary-source audit. Novelty/priority remains UNKNOWN.
Scope: stochastic mass-action CTMC recurrence, geometric ergodicity, the
support-switch exponential Foster function, and common rate-box conclusions.
This report does not independently validate the internal Foster derivations.

Finding in one paragraph
------------------------
The nearest published coverage is more specific than the earlier source map
suggested: Anderson--Cappelletti--Fan--Kim (ACFK, 2025) Corollary 3.5 gives
L2 exponential ergodicity for the target's entire complex-balanced subfamily,
including sparse graphs and arbitrary finite k_ji, by its layered
catalyzed-birth/death hypothesis. This is not all-rate coverage: complex
balance imposes exact rate equalities. For the remaining higher-dimensional,
non-complex-balanced rates, the general stochastic WR results located are
binary, bimolecular, or rank-one; their hypotheses fail on the target's
higher-order catalyst-free boundary. The best explicit high-dimensional
autocatalytic antecedent (Bibbona--Kim--Wiuf, 2020) is a different, open,
total-count-controlled conversion model. No primary source located gives the
same neighborhood-support-dependent exponential corrector or a uniform
mixing theorem over the complete target rate box. That absence is a bounded
search result, not a novelty claim.

1. Exact family and class boundary
---------------------------------
For finite catalyst set J and substrate set I, let E subset J x I. For each
j include 0 <-> A_j. For each (j,i) in E include

  A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i,

with all directed rates strictly positive, finite, k_ji an integer >=1, and
standard falling-factorial stochastic mass-action propensities. For the
first pair the rates are p_ji a_j and q_ji a_j b_i; for the autocatalytic
pair they are u_ji a_j b_i and v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i. The latter reverse
source has molecularity k_ji+2 >=3. Every edge is bidirected, so the
reaction graph is weakly reversible. For a fixed sparse E its graph is a
tree: 0 is joined to every A_j, and each pair (j,i) contributes the path
A_j -- A_j+B_i -- (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i. Its nonisolated-substrate rank is
r+|I_active|; if E is nonempty this is at least two. An isolated B_i is a
conserved class label, not a dynamic coordinate.

The complete-pair case is E=J x I. It has no isolated substrate labels,
rank r+s, one linkage class, and deficiency s(2r-1). The sparse deficiency
is 2|E|-|I_active|. These formulas use the complexes just listed and the
stoichiometric rank; they do not convert weak reversibility into a
recurrence hypothesis.

The separate internally proved candidate functions must not be conflated:

* Complete-pair fixed-rate recurrence uses an all-catalysts-zero correction
  K 1_{A=0} and exponential substrate terms sum_i R_i^(b_i). The cycle-3
  proof claims positive recurrence for every fixed positive finite rate
  vector.
* Complete-pair compact-rate-box work uses V=A+2 sum_i R^(b_i). It claims a
  common geometric Foster drift and common stationary V-moment/tightness.
* Sparse support correction uses
  h_i(a)=1_{sum_{j in N(i)} a_j=0} and
  V=sum_j lambda_j a_j + sum_i R_i^(b_i)(1+epsilon_i h_i(a))
    + K 1_{A=0}.
  It is a different, pointwise-in-rates internal candidate. Each substrate
  gate must be switched when its own neighbor set empties; a non-neighbor
  catalyst may remain present. Its positive last-neighbor-death switch is
  paired with the q_ji a_j b_i substrate-removal drift. The finite rational
  generator checker is a formula check, not an all-state proof. The saved
  sparse argument claims recurrence on every fixed isolated-count class,
  but has not received independent expert review and proves no sparse
  rate-box tightness or mixing statement.

2. Primary results and exact applicability
------------------------------------------

2.1 ACFK: exact exponential-ergodicity coverage on the complex-balanced
    subfamily

Anderson, Cappelletti, Fan, Kim, “A New Path Method for Exponential
Ergodicity of Markov Processes on Z^d, with Applications to Stochastic
Reaction Networks,” arXiv:2309.06970v2, submitted 13 Sep 2023, revised
29 Sep 2023; published in SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems 24(2)
(2025), 1668-1710, DOI 10.1137/24M1665933.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06970v2
         https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06970v2
         https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1665933

Corollary 3.5 (mass-action specialization of Theorem 3.4) assumes: (a) a
positive complex-balanced equilibrium c in R^d_{>0}; (b) a nonempty subset
S_0 of species, each with both 0 -> X_i and X_i -> 0 reactions; and (c) a
partition S_0,S_1,...,S_m of the species such that every species X_i in a
later block S_l has, for some X_j in an earlier block S_0 union ... union
S_(l-1), both reactions

  N^+_ji X_j -> N^+_ji X_j + X_i,
  N^-_ji X_j + X_i -> N^-_ji X_j,

for nonnegative integer N^+_ji,N^-_ji. Under standard stochastic
mass-action kinetics, it concludes L2 exponential ergodicity; for each
fixed epsilon>0 it gives a mixing bound tau_x^epsilon <= C_epsilon ||x||
ln(||x||) for x != 0. It is a per-model statement: the constants are not
asserted uniform over a rate-parameter box.

Exact fit: take S_0={A_j:j in J} and S_1={B_i:i in I_active}. Every A_j
has 0 <-> A_j. For each B_i choose any j in N(i); the two required reactions
are A_j -> A_j+B_i and A_j+B_i -> A_j. Other reactions, including the
higher-order autocatalytic reversible pairs, are permitted by the theorem,
provided the full network has the assumed positive complex-balanced c.
Thus Corollary 3.5 covers the complex-balanced subfamily of both complete
and sparse graphs, even when k_ji+2>2. If B_i is isolated, remove it from
the dynamic network and fix its count; the theorem applies to the remaining
coordinates.

For this tree, complex balance is equivalent to edgewise detailed balance.
The necessary and sufficient positive-c conditions are

  c_Aj = alpha_j/delta_j,
  c_Bi = p_ji/q_ji for every (j,i) in E,
  c_Aj^(k_ji) = u_ji/v_ji for every (j,i) in E.

Hence p_ji/q_ji must be independent of j on each substrate neighborhood,
and the last ratio must match (alpha_j/delta_j)^k_ji. The tree has no
cycle-flux degrees of freedom: a complex-balanced edge current must vanish
on each bridge. These equalities are not implied by weak reversibility or
by positivity of all rates. When they hold, the stationary law is the
product-Poisson law with means c; this also gives uniform moments/tightness
on any compact subset of the complex-balanced parameters on which these
means remain bounded. This product-form fact does not imply geometric
ergodicity for every complex-balanced network: ACFK Example 2.4 gives a
detailed-balanced model without L2 exponential ergodicity. The target's
complex-balanced subfamily is covered by Corollary 3.5's extra layered
birth/death conditions, not by product form alone.

2.2 Anderson--Cappelletti--Kim: WR single-linkage positive recurrence

Anderson, Cappelletti, Kim, “Stochastically Modeled Weakly Reversible
Reaction Networks with a Single Linkage Class,” arXiv:1904.08967v2,
revised 16 Jan 2020; Journal of Applied Probability 57(3) (2020), 792-810,
DOI 10.1017/jpr.2020.28.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08967v2
         https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2
         https://doi.org/10.1017/jpr.2020.28

Theorem 4.1 assumes a weakly reversible, single-linkage, binary
mass-action network and, for every species S_i, at least one of S_i or 2S_i
is a complex. It proves positive recurrence for the stochastic model. The
target has the helpful single linkage and weak reversibility, but fails the
binary condition because the reverse autocatalysis source has order
k_ji+2>=3. It also has no B_i-only or 2B_i complex for any active B_i. The
statement cannot be shortened to “weakly reversible and one linkage class.”

2.3 Anderson--Kim tier conditions: exact catalyst-free boundary failure

Anderson and Kim, “Some Network Conditions for Positive Recurrence of
Stochastically Modeled Reaction Networks,” arXiv:1710.11263v3, revised
22 Aug 2018; SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 78(5) (2018), 2692-2713,
DOI 10.1137/17M1161427.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11263v3
         https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11263v3
         https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1161427

Theorem 3.1 is the classwise continuous-time Foster criterion: if a positive
V and finite K satisfy AV<=-1 off K, every state in each closed irreducible
component is positive recurrent, and the expected time to enter the union
of those components is finite. Theorem 4.2 is a generic tier condition;
Corollary 4.3 requires, for every tier sequence, a descending reaction
whose source is in both the top stochastic-propensity tier and the top
monomial/deterministic tier. These are sufficient conditions, not a
universal WR theorem.

For any active edge (j,i), consider the actual state sequence
a=0, b_i=n -> infinity, with other coordinates fixed. Under falling-factorial
mass action, complexes containing A_j have zero source propensity at every
state on this sequence; the enabled source tier is therefore the zero
complex. But the D-tier monomials (x vee 1)^y for A_j+B_i and
(k_ji+1)A_j+B_i grow like n, even though the actual propensities vanish.
Thus the S-top is {0}, while the D-top contains the substrate-bearing
complexes, and the Corollary 4.3 equality/intersection premise fails on this
reachable catalyst-free ray. This is the precise boundary distinction the
support-switch correction addresses; the tier result does not supply that
correction for this family.

2.4 Anderson--Kim mixing results: fixed-rate binary classes only

Anderson and Kim, “Mixing Times for Two Classes of Stochastically Modeled
Reaction Networks,” arXiv:2209.06988v2, revised 13 Dec 2022; Mathematical
Biosciences and Engineering 20(3) (2023), 4690-4713, DOI 10.3934/mbe.2023217
(published online 28 Dec 2022).
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06988v2
         https://arxiv.org/html/2209.06988v2
         https://doi.org/10.3934/mbe.2023217

Theorem 3.1: start from a weakly reversible, binary, single-linkage network
and add 0 <-> S_i for every species. For each fixed positive choice of all
rates there are C,eta>0 with TV distance <=C(|x|+1)ln(|x|+2)e^(-eta t);
when the original network is conservative, the factor ln(|x|+2) can be
dropped. Theorem 3.2 instead assumes a binary double-full network and that
from each double complex there is a directed path to a unary or zero
complex; it gives exponential TV convergence with C,eta independent of the
initial state within each closed class. Neither theorem is a uniform-in-rate
box theorem. The target fails binary molecularity; its B_i species also
lack direct 0 <-> B_i reactions and double B_i complexes. Theorem 3.2 is
therefore not an analogue for the target's higher-order reverse channels.

2.5 Wiuf--Xu rank-one and Xu second-order results

Wiuf and Xu, “Classification and Threshold Dynamics of Stochastic Reaction
Networks,” arXiv:2012.07954v3, revised 24 Jan 2023:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07954v3
https://arxiv.org/html/2012.07954v3

Their H2 is dim(stoichiometric subspace)=1. Corollary 4.10 says a weakly
reversible mass-action SRN satisfying H2-H3 is positive recurrent on every
positive irreducible component (PIC), with an exponentially ergodic
stationary distribution. Theorem 4.8 gives nonexplosion and ergodicity for
endotactic systems subject to H2-H5. A nontrivial target graph has rank
r+|I_active|>=2; its one-dimensional conclusion does not apply. A network
with E empty is a separate immigration/death process plus fixed B labels.

Xu, “On the Regulary of Reaction Systems,” arXiv:2409.05340v2, revised
9 May 2026:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340v2
https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05340v2

Theorem 4.6(v) states every second-order weakly reversible stochastic
mass-action system is regular (nonexplosive CTMC and globally existing
deterministic system). Theorem 4.13 gives, for bimolecular WR systems,
positive recurrence on every communicating class iff every such class has
a stationary distribution; it does not assert the stationary distribution
exists in every class. The target has a source of order at least three, so
these results do not cover it.

2.6 Higher-dimensional autocatalytic recurrence: close but different model

Bibbona, Kim, Wiuf, “Stationary Distributions of Systems with Discreteness
Induced Transitions,” arXiv:2001.04917v3, revised 28 Mar 2020; Journal of
the Royal Society Interface 17(169) (2020), 20200243,
DOI 10.1098/rsif.2020.0243.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04917v3
         https://arxiv.org/html/2001.04917v3
         https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0243

Their model (14) has arbitrary dimension d, reactions A_i+A_j -> 2A_j and
A_i+A_j -> 2A_i for i != j with nonnegative conversion constants, and
external inflow/outflow 0 -> A_i and A_i -> 0 with lambda_i,delta_i>0.
Theorem 2 quantifies over all such nonnegative conversion parameters and
all positive lambda_i,delta_i: the CTMC on N_0^d is positive recurrent,
has a unique stationary distribution, all moments are finite, and
convergence is exponentially fast. It proves substantial prior art for
arbitrary-dimensional autocatalytic/DIT models. It is not the target: the
conversion steps move one molecule between species and preserve total count;
each species has its own direct immigration and linear death; and the model
is not the target's reversible A_j+B_i <-> (k+1)A_j+B_i catalyst-retaining
amplifier with no direct substrate immigration/death. The total-count
birth-death mechanism prevents the catalyst-free frozen-substrate boundary
that motivates h_i.

2.7 Support-dependent functions and boundary gluing

Laurence and Robert, “Scaling Methods for Stochastic Chemical Reaction
Networks,” arXiv:2310.01949v3, revised 17 Dec 2025; published in Stochastic
Processes and their Applications (2025), 104855,
DOI 10.1016/j.spa.2025.104855.
Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01949v3
         https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01949v3
         https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2025.104855

This source explicitly identifies a general Lyapunov construction problem:
one may find functions decreasing on different large-state regions, but a
jump across a region boundary changes which function applies and changes
the one-step generator. It uses Filonov's stopping-time criterion in
examples to bypass that gluing difficulty. This is a conceptual antecedent
to the support-switch issue, not an exact antecedent to
R_i^(b_i)(1+epsilon_i 1_{N(i) catalysts absent}); it treats fixed-rate
examples and supplies no theorem for this sparse family or common rate box.
No claim of priority is inferred from not locating the exact formula.

3. Common rate-box tightness and mixing: what sources settle
-----------------------------------------------------------

For the complete-pair internal rate-box candidate, every rate parameter is
restricted to a compact interval bounded strictly away from zero and
infinity. The saved proof claims one rate-independent V and constants c,C
with L_theta V<=C-cV, then a uniform stationary V-moment bound and
uniform tightness. It explicitly makes no mixing claim. This is stronger
than the pointwise complete-pair recurrence result but remains an internal
candidate. The sparse support-switch report is pointwise in the fixed
rates; it does not supply common constants over a box.

What the primary literature does supply:

* ACFK Corollary 3.5 supplies L2 exponential ergodicity for each fixed
  complex-balanced target parameter vector satisfying the layered
  catalysis condition. It does not state a uniform lower bound on the
  spectral gap or a shared mixing bound over a parameter box.
* The product-Poisson law yields common stationary moments on a compact
  complex-balanced parameter subset when the Poisson means c remain bounded.
  This is static tightness for that subfamily, not mixing for arbitrary
  rates.
* Anderson--Kim's Theorem 3.2 is uniform over initial states for each
  fixed network/rate vector under its binary-double-full assumptions. It
  does not assert uniformity as rates vary and does not fit this family.

A common geometric drift is not itself the complete uniform-mixing
certificate: a common small-set/minorization bound (or an equivalent
uniform Harris argument) is also required, with constants controlled over
the parameter box. The internal rate-box report has not supplied that
minorization, a uniform spectral gap, or a common TV mixing-time bound.
The compact-positive-rate hypothesis matters; statements here do not extend
to boxes approaching zero rates or unbounded rates.

4. Claim-by-claim novelty boundary
----------------------------------

Claim: complete-pair CTMC is positive recurrent for every fixed positive
finite rate vector and every finite r,s,k_ji.
  External coverage: exact for the complex-balanced subset by ACFK
  Corollary 3.5 (indeed L2 exponential ergodicity); for r+s=2 there is
  also an author/conference-reported all-WR two-dimensional theorem, but
  the prior audit found its paper listed “in preparation” and no inspectable
  proof. For non-complex-balanced rank >=2 parameters, the binary/rank-one
  results above do not cover this family. Internal coverage exists in the
  cycle-3 report, not independently reviewed. Novelty: UNKNOWN.

Claim: sparse E, including multiple distinct catalyst neighborhoods, has
classwise positive recurrence under positive paired rates.
  External coverage: ACFK Corollary 3.5 covers its complex-balanced subset
  when every dynamic substrate has at least one neighbor. No located
  published all-rate theorem covers the remaining higher-order sparse
  family. The support-switch candidate is a new-looking exact construction
  in this bounded comparison, but the 2018 tier theory and Laurence--Robert
  boundary-gluing discussion are genuine methodological antecedents.
  Novelty: UNKNOWN; “not found” is not priority evidence.

Claim: complete-pair compact positive rate box has common geometric drift,
uniform invariant moments/tightness.
  External coverage: product-Poisson formulas imply uniform moments only
  on the compact complex-balanced slice with bounded c. No located primary
  source proves a common V drift or uniform invariant tightness for the
  entire higher-order complete-pair box. Internal candidate coverage is
  specific to complete pairs. Novelty: UNKNOWN.

Claim: uniform mixing across that rate box (or sparse rate box).
  External coverage: no exact-family uniform-in-rate mixing source found.
  The current internal rate-box report explicitly does not claim it, and
  the sparse report is not rate-uniform. Do not infer it from stationary
  tightness or from per-parameter exponential ergodicity. Novelty: no claim
  is available because the statement itself remains unproved here.

Higher-dimensional autocatalytic recurrence as a broad idea is not novel:
Bibbona--Kim--Wiuf proves it for a different all-species-open conversion
model. The exact WR sparse reversible amplifier architecture, the
neighborhood-specific support switch, and arbitrary non-CB rates in that
architecture remain a distinct candidate interface; priority is UNKNOWN.

5. Additional boundary on the 2D claim and search scope
------------------------------------------------------

For r=s=1, the target is a two-species WR network. The earlier primary
source check found Andrea Agazzi's 2022+ slides and later abstracts reporting
2D weakly reversible recurrence, while the ConStRAINeD publications page
listed the proof as “in preparation”; no checkable proof manuscript was
found there. Therefore this report does not promote that announcement to a
formal theorem. References from the earlier source audit:
https://github.com/agazzian/talks/blob/gh-pages/UI_conjecture.html
https://constrained.polito.it/publications/

The literature audit prioritized primary papers and author-hosted versions
named above. It is not exhaustive across every chemistry, queueing,
probability, or biochemical database. No absence result supports a global
novelty/priority conclusion. In all claims keep nonexplosion, positive
recurrence, finite moments, geometric ergodicity, and uniform-in-parameter
mixing distinct.
