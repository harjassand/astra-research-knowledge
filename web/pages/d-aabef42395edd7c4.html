SEMICLASSICAL QUANTUM MEMORY ON A SUPPLIED SYMMETRIC SU(m) CARRIER
2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. External validation and novelty UNKNOWN.
Update: near_full.txt now proves the complete all-Q energy order profile,
including arbitrary kernel subspaces and the logarithmic spin transition.
No iid sector-transport or general-orbit theorem is asserted.

Main conclusion

For the Gibbs orbit exp[(h/N)dΓ(|v><v|)] on H_N=Sym^N(C^m), blind
full-density reconstruction through a quantum register of dimension at most
Q per classical branch has optimal worst-case half trace error comparable
to Λ_N,m(Q)/N^2. Here Λ is the minimum normalized conjugation-Casimir
energy among matrices of rank at most Q. This is an operational equivalence,
proved below with the exact posterior moment, arbitrary-code converse,
positivity replacement, and a full noncommuting trace upper bound.

For every fixed κ<1, the already reconstructed rank-energy profile gives

  e*_Q = Θ_{m,h0,h1,κ}(1/[N Q^(1/(m−1))])

for 1≤Q≤κ dim(H_N), h in a fixed supplied interval 0<h0≤h≤h1<∞.
There is an explicit occupation taper and an exact finite positive cubature
code in this range. The unqualified formula extending uniformly to all Q
is REFUTED for m≥3: at Q=dim(H_N)−1 an explicit coherent-hole filter has
error O(1/[N dim(H_N)]), which is o(N^−2).

The global exact equivalence is existential: optimizing Λ is a nonconvex
rank problem, and its minimizer is not provided by an efficient all-Q
compiler. The supplement near_full.txt provides explicit cap/harmonic
filters achieving the complete ORDER profile at every Q; exact minimizer
acquisition and precision-certified gate costs remain separate.

1. Exact operational contract

Fix m≥2, N≥1 and let s=m−1 and

  H_N=Sym^N(C^m),    d=d_N=binom(N+s,s).

For a projective unit vector v∈CP^(m−1), put P_v=|v><v| and

  n_v=dΓ(P_v),  B_v=n_v/N,  τ_h,v=e^(h B_v)/Z_N(h),
  Z_N(h)=Tr e^(h B_v).

Thus 0≤B_v≤I. The partition function is independent of v. The supplied
data are m,N,Q, the standard occupation realization of H_N, and either h
or the compact positive interval [h0,h1]. The direction v is unknown and
is not a supplied classical label. The encoder receives exactly one state
τ_h,v on this irreducible carrier. This is not N copies of an unknown
fundamental density matrix; H_N and its state are the physical input.

A legal code is a quantum instrument with classical outcome y and an
output register K_y of dimension q_y≤Q, followed by a y-dependent CPTP
decoder K_y→H_N. Independent ancillary states, private/shared classical
randomness and unrestricted computation are allowed. Pre-shared quantum
entanglement or a quantum side channel are not allowed. The classical
alphabet may be arbitrarily large in the converse. Its size is charged
separately in the constructions. Q is the maximum branch dimension, not
an average dimension or the total direct-sum dimension.

The decoder's target carrier is H_N. We require recovery of the whole
density matrix in half trace norm, not merely parameter estimation,
selected observables, visible preparation with v supplied, or preservation
of a reference purification. Define

  e(Φ;h)=sup_v (1/2)||Φ(τ_h,v)−τ_h,v||_1,
  e(Φ;I)=sup_{h∈[h0,h1],v} (1/2)||Φ(τ_h,v)−τ_h,v||_1.

Let e*_Q(h) and e*_Q(I) be the corresponding infima over legal codes. The
same upper construction works for every h in the interval without reading
h. Both the fixed-h and interval risks obey the bounds below.

If a decoder initially produces a larger space with a specified embedding
of H_N, projection onto that embedding and replacement of the complement
by a fixed state is a CPTP map back to H_N and cannot increase target trace
distance. That harmless enlargement is therefore covered. An experiment
whose target state actually occupies several inequivalent carriers is not
covered by this single-carrier theorem.

2. Representation normalization, adjoint multiplicity, and energy

Choose Hermitian fundamental su(m) generators t_a, 1≤a≤m^2−1, with

  Tr_fund(t_a t_b)=δ_ab/2,
  T_a=dΓ(t_a) on H_N.

Use the unnormalized and normalized energies

  E(A)=sum_a ||[T_a,A]||_HS^2,
  Ebar(A)=E(A)/||A||_HS^2 for A≠0,
  Λ_N,m(Q)=min_{rank A≤Q, ||A||_HS=1} E(A).

The minimum exists because this constraint set is compact. The carrier
Casimir and trace index are

  sum_a T_a^2=C_N I,             C_N=N s(N+m)/(2m),        (2.1)
  Tr(T_a T_b)=d N(N+m)δ_ab/[2m(m+1)].                     (2.2)

For completeness, the symmetric highest weight Nω_1 has quadratic Casimir
(Nω_1,Nω_1+2ρ)/2=N(m−1)(N+m)/(2m), using root length squared two;
invariance makes Tr(T_aT_b) scalar in a,b, and summing a fixes (2.2).

The conjugation representation has the multiplicity-free decomposition

  End(H_N)=direct-sum_{k=0}^N H_{k,k},
  c_k=k(k+m−1),
  dim H_{k,k}=binom(k+s,s)^2−binom(k+s−1,s)^2.            (2.3)

Here H_{k,k} has highest weight k(ω_1+ω_{m−1}); for m=2 this means
weight 2kω_1. A derivation uses bihomogeneous (N,N) polynomials and the
Fischer decomposition into |z|^(2(N−k)) times harmonic (k,k) polynomials.
The harmonic dimensions are the difference between consecutive
bihomogeneous dimensions, and the highest-weight Casimir gives c_k.
In particular H_{1,1} is exactly one copy of the adjoint representation,
spanned by the T_a, and the trivial summand is exactly span(I).

This multiplicity statement is used twice, in the posterior moment and in
the upper bound. It would not follow just from irreducibility of an
arbitrary carrier. Section 2 of spectral_geometry/cycle2/RESULT.txt gives
the independent reconstruction of the full spectrum; no such statement
is imported for a reducible or different highest-weight carrier.

For any A, scalar Casimir (2.1) gives the exact identity

  sum_a Tr(T_a A T_a A†)
       =C_N ||A||_HS^2−E(A)/2.                          (2.4)

Each summand on the left is real because it is the trace of a product of
two Hermitian matrices T_a and A T_a A†. Expanding each squared
commutator proves (2.4); it is valid for nonsymmetric, nonnormal matrices.

3. Exact CP posterior moment and a finite-N polarization bound

Put z_a(v)=Tr_fund(t_aP_v). Fundamental completeness gives

  P_v=I/m+2 sum_a z_a(v)t_a,
  B_v=I/m+(2/N)sum_a z_a(v)T_a,
  sum_a z_a(v)^2=s/(2m).                                (3.1)

Normalized uniform projective measure dv has

  integral P_v⊗P_v dv=(I+Swap)/[m(m+1)],
  integral z_a z_b dv=δ_ab/[2m(m+1)].                    (3.2)

The first identity follows because the integral commutes with U(m), is
supported on Sym^2(C^m), and has trace one. Taking traces against t_a⊗t_b
proves the second identity.

Let μ_N(h)=Tr(τ_h,v n_v), independent of v, and define

  r_N(h)=[m μ_N(h)−N]/s.                                (3.3)

At v=e_1, invariance under the stabilizer U(1)×U(m−1) implies that the
one-particle density of τ has eigenvalues μ_N/N on e_1 and
(N−μ_N)/(Ns) on its orthogonal complement. Consequently

  Tr(τ_h,v T_a)=r_N(h) z_a(v).                           (3.4)

The exact operator-valued first harmonic is

  integral z_a(v) τ_h,v dv
        = r_N(h) T_a/[d N(N+m)].                        (3.5)

Proof: the integral is an intertwining map from the adjoint representation
to End(H_N), so adjoint multiplicity one makes it a scalar times T_a.
Pair against T_b. Equations (3.2), (3.4), and (2.2) give the displayed
scalar. The averaged state itself is I/d by carrier irreducibility.

This also supplies the genuine Bayesian posterior first moment. For any
nonzero POVM effect M≥0, the uniform-prior probability is Tr(M)/d and

  E[z_a(v)|M]= r_N(h) Tr(M T_a)/[N(N+m)Tr M].            (3.6)

No qubit Bloch-radius or iid posterior estimate is assumed. The uniform
second moment (3.2) and the operator first moment (3.5) are precisely the
moments needed for the converse; higher posterior moments are not used.
For a normalized effect σ=M/Tr M, its one-particle state ρ_1 has
Tr(σT_a)=N Tr(ρ_1t_a). Since Tr ρ_1^2≤1,

  ||E[z|M]||_2 ≤ [r_N(h)/(N+m)] sqrt[s/(2m)].            (3.7)

A coherent effect attains this vector-norm bound. This is a posterior
check, not a claim that a measure-and-prepare code is optimal for Q>1.

There is no asymptotic uniformity premise in the polarization bound. At
h=0 the occupation basis is uniform over all compositions of N into m
nonnegative parts. For x=n_{e1}/N its mean and variance are

  E_0 x=1/m,
  Var_0 x=s(N+m)/[m^2(m+1)N].                           (3.8)

The identities can be obtained by coefficients of
(1−t)^−m: E_0 n_1=N/m and E_0[n_1(n_1−1)]=2N(N−1)/[m(m+1)].
The tilted occupation law is p_h=p_0 e^(hx)/E_0e^(hx). For h≥0 and
0≤x≤1 it has p_h≥e^(−h)p_0 pointwise. Applying this to two independent
draws and Var(x)=E[(x−x')^2]/2 gives

  Var_h x ≥ e^(−2h) Var_0 x.

Since d(E_hx)/dh=Var_hx, integration from zero to h gives

  r_N(h)/N=(m/s)(E_hx−1/m)
       ≥ h e^(−2h)(N+m)/[m(m+1)N]
       ≥ η := h0 e^(−2h1)/[m(m+1)] >0                 (3.9)

for every N≥1 and h∈[h0,h1]. Also 0<r_N(h)≤N. All constants are fixed
before N and Q. If h=0 alone, all input states are I/d and can be recovered
exactly with Q=1; the positive-h assumption is essential to the lower bound.

4. Arbitrary hybrid codes and the exact full-state score converse

Write encoder Kraus operators E_yα:H_N→K_y and decoder Kraus operators
D_yβ:K_y→H_N. Their composite Kraus operators A_yαβ=D_yβ E_yα have
rank at most q_y≤Q. The trace-preserving identity implies

  w_A=||A||_HS^2/d,       sum_A w_A=1.                   (4.1)

An arbitrary continuous or infinite alphabet does not evade this rank
restriction. Its finite-dimensional Choi operator belongs to the closed
convex cone generated by rank-Q vectors under vectorization, equivalently
the Schmidt-number-at-most-Q cone. The set of normalized such vectors is
compact; finite-dimensional convexity gives a finite decomposition when
needed. Alternatively all calculations below hold under the outcome
integral, with nonnegative weights. No bound on label count is used here.

Conversely, any finite CPTP Kraus family with rank(A_y)≤Q factors into a
legal code: write A_y=V_y S_y W_y† by a thin SVD, use S_yW_y† as the
encoder into C^rank(A_y), and use the isometry V_y as the decoder. Thus
unlimited-classical-label branch dimension is exactly this Kraus-rank
constraint. It is not Choi matrix rank and not ordinary channel Kraus count.

Use the effect 0≤B_v≤I as a witness of full trace error. Its ideal score is

  Tr(B_v τ_h,v)=μ_N(h)/N=1/m+s r_N(h)/(mN).

Using (3.5), the decoded averaged score is

  integral Tr[B_v Φ(τ_h,v)]dv
    =1/m+[2r_N(h)/(d N^2(N+m))]
                  sum_A sum_a Tr(T_a A T_a A†).

Insert (2.4) and (4.1). The exact deficit becomes

  integral Tr[B_v(τ_h,v−Φ(τ_h,v))]dv
    = r_N(h)/[N^2(N+m)] sum_A w_A Ebar(A).              (4.2)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Since expectation differences of
effects are bounded by half trace distance,

  e(Φ;h) ≥ r_N(h) Λ_N,m(Q)/[N^2(N+m)].                 (4.3)

No covariance of the unknown code, no restriction to positive Kraus
operators, no assumption about an estimated direction, and no acquisition
of v is involved. Equation (4.2) is a score identity, but it is a valid
converse for whole-density trace error through the effect inequality.

5. Positivity replacement: the all-Q energy optimizer can be a filter

An all-Q upper based on Λ must not silently assume that its minimizing
matrix is Hermitian or positive. Here that replacement is valid and can be
proved directly. Let

  A=sum_i σ_i |u_i><v_i|,
  L=|A†|=sqrt(AA†)=sum_i σ_i |u_i><u_i|,
  R=|A|=sqrt(A†A)=sum_i σ_i |v_i><v_i|.

All three have the same rank and Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Put
U^a_ij=<u_i,T_a u_j> and V^a_ij=<v_i,T_a v_j>. Then

  sum_a Tr(T_a A T_a A†)
     =sum_a,i,j σ_iσ_j Re[U^a_ij conjugate(V^a_ij)],
  sum_a Tr(T_a L T_a L)=sum_a,i,j σ_iσ_j |U^a_ij|^2,
  sum_a Tr(T_a R T_a R)=sum_a,i,j σ_iσ_j |V^a_ij|^2.

The first expression is real even before taking Re after summation.
Subtracting these expressions using (2.4) gives the exact identity

  E(A)−[E(L)+E(R)]/2
     =sum_a,i,j σ_iσ_j |U^a_ij−V^a_ij|^2 ≥0.

Equivalently, entrywise 2Re(u conjugate(v))≤|u|^2+|v|^2, with
σ_iσ_j≥0, proves

  E(A) ≥ [E(L)+E(R)]/2.                                (5.1)

At least one positive matrix L or R therefore has energy no greater than
A. Applying this to a minimizing A proves

  Λ_N,m(Q)=min_{F≥0, rank F≤Q, ||F||_HS=1} E(F).        (5.2)

This proof uses the scalar Casimir in (2.4); it is not an unsupported
operator-modulus contraction assertion for arbitrary Dirichlet forms.
There is no conclusion that L and R separately always improve energy.

6. Legal covariant filter code and exact adjoint channel coefficient

For any nonzero Hermitian positive F with rank q≤Q put S_F=Tr F^2 and
F_g=U_g F U_g†, with U_g the symmetric-power SU(m) representation. Define

  Φ_F(X)=(d/S_F) integral F_g X F_g dg.                 (6.1)

By irreducibility, integral F_g^2 dg=(S_F/d)I. Thus (6.1) is completely
positive, trace preserving, unital, and covariant. A legal instrument for
outcome g has encoder sqrt(d/S_F) F U_g† into supp(F)≅C^q; its decoder
embeds this register and applies U_g. The final Kraus operator is
sqrt(d/S_F)F_g. This construction does not receive h or v as labels.

Define the Haar-averaged double commutator

  D_F(X)=integral [F_g,[F_g,X]] dg.

Irreducibility and (6.1) give

  Φ_F(X)−X=−d D_F(X)/(2S_F).                            (6.2)

The map D_F is self-adjoint and conjugation-invariant. Adjoint multiplicity
one implies D_F(T_a)=c T_a, with a common scalar c. Pair against T_a and
sum a. Invariance of the sum of generator commutator norms gives

  c=E(F)/(d C_N),
  D_F(B_v)=E(F)(B_v−I/m)/(d C_N).                       (6.3)

In addition,

  integral ||[F_g,B_v]||_HS^2 dg
       =Tr[B_v D_F(B_v)]
       =2E(F)/[m(m+1)N^2].                             (6.4)

For the last equality, trace against the occupation-basis variance (3.8):
Tr(B_v−I/m)^2=d s(N+m)/[m^2(m+1)N]. These equalities are exact for an
arbitrary positive F, not only a stabilizer-invariant taper.

7. Full noncommuting Gibbs trace estimate

Energy alone is not a full-state error bound. The required bound follows
from an ordered-product calculation. Fix v and abbreviate B=B_v. For
each g, the map δ_g=[F_g,·] is a derivation. Apply δ_g twice to B^k.
There are k terms in which both derivations hit the same factor, and
k(k−1) ordered terms in which they hit different factors. After Haar
integration the same-factor terms equal

  k E(F) B^(k−1)(B−I/m)/(d C_N).

Their trace norm is at most kE(F)/C_N because B and B−I/m commute,
||B||op≤1 and ||B−I/m||op≤1. In every cross term there are two factors
[F_g,B], with intervening and exterior powers of B. Schatten Holder and
contraction by those powers bound its trace norm by ||[F_g,B]||_HS^2.
The terms need not commute, and no separation between the two differentiated
factors changes this bound. Using (6.2)–(6.4), for k≥1,

  ||Φ_F(B^k)−B^k||_1
     ≤ d E(F)/(2S_F)
           [k/C_N+2k(k−1)/(m(m+1)N^2)].                (7.1)

The k=0 difference vanishes. Since B≥0 and h≥0, Z_N(h)≥d. Expand
e^(hB) in its absolutely trace-convergent series and use
sum k h^k/k!=h e^h and sum k(k−1)h^k/k!=h^2e^h. Therefore

  e(Φ_F;h) ≤ Ebar(F) e^h/4
              [h/C_N+2h^2/(m(m+1)N^2)]
            ≤ K_{m,h1} Ebar(F)/N^2,                    (7.2)

  K_{m,h1}=e^h1 [m h1/(2s)+h1^2/(2m(m+1))].           (7.3)

Covariance makes the bound uniform in v. The same F and channel work
uniformly for every h∈[0,h1]. This proves whole-density trace error,
without inferring it from a dipole score or assuming that multipole
eigenvalues satisfy an unproved comparison.

8. Global operational–energy equivalence and its acquisition boundary

For every 1≤Q≤d, (3.9), (4.3), (5.2), and (7.2) prove

  [η/(m+1)] Λ_N,m(Q)/N^2
       ≤ e*_Q(h), e*_Q(I)
       ≤ K_{m,h1} Λ_N,m(Q)/N^2                         (8.1)

for h∈[h0,h1]. At Q=d, both sides vanish and the identity code has zero
error. The factor N+m in the exact lower was bounded by (m+1)N; no
large-N approximation is used. For a fixed h>0 replace h0,h1 by h.

The all-Q upper is existential. It requires a positive minimizer in (5.2),
which is a nonconvex rank-constrained matrix optimization on a d-dimensional
carrier. Nothing here establishes efficient acquisition of this minimizer,
an efficient circuit, or a formula for the large-rank profile. These are
distinct from the proof of the infimum relation (8.1).

There is nevertheless no infinite-classical-label loophole in (8.1).
For an arbitrary given F, the unscaled Choi operators
J_g=|vec(F_g)><vec(F_g)| are Hermitian matrices on a d^2-dimensional space.
Their Haar average belongs to the convex hull of the compact group orbit.
Real Caratheodory gives a positive convex combination of at most d^4+1
such J_g. Scaling by d/S_F reproduces Φ_F exactly, including its
trace-preserving identity. Every finite Kraus operator still has rank q.

Thus the global existential upper permits at most d^4+1 labels, max branch
dimension Q, and total direct-sum dimension at most Q(d^4+1). This is a
finite-dimensional existence argument, not an algorithm selecting the
group nodes or weights. Section 10 gives an explicit, smaller cubature
for the supplied taper and coherent-hole filters. Do not conflate these
two statements.

9. Explicit fixed-fraction precision law

The spectral_geometry/cycle2 packet independently proves the following
rank-energy facts with the same normalization. Their relevant mechanism
is recorded here so the operational application is checkable.

Under vectorization a rank-Q unit-HS matrix has Schmidt rank at most Q.
For the invariant Casimir projector Π_K onto k≤K, Schur's lemma gives
Tr_2Π_K=[binom(K+s,s)^2/d]I. If P is the range projection of that matrix,
|vec(A)><vec(A)|≤P⊗I, so its Π_K mass is at most
Q binom(K+s,s)^2/d. Hence

  Ebar(A) ≥ (K+1)(K+m)
            [1−Q binom(K+s,s)^2/d]_+                   (9.1)

for 0≤K<N. Layer-cake integration also gives the exact lower profile

  Λ_N,m(Q)≥sum_{k=0}^{N−1}(2k+m)
                  [1−Q binom(k+s,s)^2/d]_+.            (9.2)

Define

  α_m=s!/[2(2s)^(2s)],
  c_m=(s!/2)^(1/s)/8,
  C_m=s(s+2)/(s!)^(1/s).

For N≥s and 1≤Q≤floor(α_m N^s), the exact integer cutoff in the
spectral packet yields

  Λ_N,m(Q)≥c_m N Q^(−1/s).                              (9.3)

For the upper, in the occupation basis with off-axis occupations β∈Z_+^s
and k=|β|, define the positive cap taper

  F_R|β>=(R+1−k)|β> for k≤R, and zero otherwise,
  R=max{r∈{0,...,N}: binom(r+s,s)≤Q}<N.

Its rank is binom(R+s,s)≤Q. The exact norm and energy are

  S_R=binom(R+s+2,s+2)+binom(R+s+1,s+2),
  W_R=E(F_R)=N s binom(R+s+1,s+1)
               −s(s+1)binom(R+s+1,s+2).                (9.4)

The energy includes the boundary edge from layer R to layer R+1.
Its alternative root-edge expression is
W_R=sum_{k=0}^R (N−k)(k+s)binom(k+s−1,s−1).
The pair of Hermitian generators for a root joining axis 1 to axis i
contributes the ladder coefficient (N−k)(β_i+1); roots inside the
off-axis coordinates and Cartan generators commute with this filter.
This proves the displayed sum without an omitted boundary term.
Binomial estimates give

  Ebar(F_R)=W_R/S_R≤N s(s+2)/(R+s+2)
                  < C_m N Q^(−1/s).                   (9.5)

This upper works for every Q<d, although its order need not be optimal
near full rank.

To extend the lower to every fixed fraction of the carrier, the first
positive Casimir eigenvalue is m. The normalized identity overlap of a
rank-Q matrix is at most Q/d because |Tr A|^2≤Q||A||_HS^2. Therefore

  Λ_N,m(Q)≥m(1−Q/d).                                   (9.6)

Fix 0<κ<1. If Q≤κd and Q>α_m N^s, then NQ^(−1/s)<α_m^(−1/s).
Combine (9.3) and (9.6) to obtain, for N≥s,

  c_{m,κ} NQ^(−1/s)≤Λ_N,m(Q)≤C_m NQ^(−1/s),
  c_{m,κ}=min{c_m, m(1−κ)α_m^(1/s)}.                   (9.7)

The integer floor causes no gap: an integer Q below α_mN^s is below its
floor; otherwise the second case applies. If the small-rank interval is
empty, the second case handles all Q. Inserting (9.7) into (8.1) proves
the stated precision law, with explicit possible constants

  c_prec=η c_{m,κ}/(m+1),      C_prec=K_{m,h1} C_m.      (9.8)

The lower holds despite unlimited classical labels. The upper filter is
known explicitly from m,N,Q, so in this regime no optimizer-oracle for Λ
is needed. Its description and dimension are polynomial in N at fixed m;
this does not by itself assert gate-optimal implementation.

10. Constructive exact finite positive cubature for the explicit filters

The taper is a spectral function of n_{e1}. Interpolating its N+1 scalar
values on {0,...,N} makes it a polynomial of degree at most N in n_{e1}.
After rotating the axis, F_v is the same polynomial of n_v. A matrix
entry of n_v is bilinear in v and conjugate(v). Every entry of F_v can
therefore be homogenized to bidegree (N,N), by multiplying lower-degree
terms by appropriate powers of ||v||^2=1. Every matrix entry of the
superoperator X↦F_v X F_v is consequently a projective polynomial of
bidegree (2N,2N). This holds also for F_v=I−|v^N><v^N| used in Section 11.

An explicit positive cubature for all these moments follows. Write

  v=(sqrt(p_1), sqrt(p_2)e^(iθ_2),...,sqrt(p_m)e^(iθ_m)).

Under uniform projective measure, the relative phases are independent
uniform angles and p is uniform Dirichlet(1,...,1). With stick variables
u_j, 1≤j≤s,

  p_j=u_j product_{i<j}(1−u_i),
  p_m=product_{i=1}^s(1−u_i),
  u_j independently has density (m−j)(1−u_j)^(m−j−1)
       on [0,1].

Use L=2N+1 equally spaced phases in each θ_j, and q=N+1 positive
Gauss-Jacobi nodes for each of the s stick-variable weights above. A
monomial v^α conjugate(v)^β with |α|=|β|=2N has each phase frequency
α_j−β_j in [−2N,2N]. The L-point phase sum kills every nonzero frequency.
Survival forces α=β and leaves p^α. After stick substitution this is
a product of polynomials, of degree at most 2N in each individual u_j.
The q-node Gaussian rule is exact through degree 2q−1=2N+1, so the
product rule integrates every surviving monomial exactly.

The Gaussian-rule fact needs no oracle about this group: for a positive
interval weight its degree-q orthogonal polynomial has q simple interior
roots. Divide a degree≤2q−1 polynomial by it; orthogonality integrates
the quotient part to zero and interpolation handles the remainder.
The node weight equals the integral of the squared Lagrange polynomial,
so it is positive. Normalizing integrates the constant to one.
Here the Jacobi parameters are nonnegative fixed integers, and both
nodes and weights have explicit algebraic descriptions.

It follows that the projective grid has

11. Explicit refutation of the all-Q simple power law

Let Π=|e1^N><e1^N| be a coherent rank-one projector and choose

  F_hole=I−Π,    rank(F_hole)=d−1,    S_F=d−1.

For a coherent highest-weight vector,
sum_a <T_a>^2=N^2s/(2m). Therefore

  E(Π)=2[C_N−N^2s/(2m)]=Ns,
  E(F_hole)=Ns,
  Ebar(F_hole)=Ns/(d−1).                               (11.1)

It is the same finite cubature code of Section 10, with Q=d−1. By (7.2),

  e*_{d−1}(I)≤K_{m,h1} s/[N(d−1)].                    (11.2)

For m≥3, s≥2 and d∼N^s/s!. The right side is O(N^(−s−1)), while
1/[N(d−1)^(1/s)] is Θ(N^−2). Their ratio is O(N^(1−s))→0. Hence no
positive N-independent lower constant can make the simple Q exponent
uniform through Q=d−1 for these m. At Q=d the identity code also gives
zero error. This is an actual legal whole-state reconstruction
counterexample, not just a low-energy matrix.

12. Consequences, strongest controls, and limitations

For a fixed Q and fixed m,h interval, the error is Θ(1/N). A classical-only
code (Q=1) is thus asymptotically faithful in this N-dependent semiclassical
Gibbs family. It cannot achieve o(1/N). Any sequence of legal codes in this
family with error o(1/N) must have Q→∞. This last statement does not require
the fixed-fraction assumption: bounded Q eventually lies in its range.

Within the intermediate precision window in which an optimal required
branch dimension stays below a fixed fraction κd, inversion gives

  Q=Θ((1/(Nε))^s),
  quantum qubits=s log_2(1/(Nε))+O_{m,h0,h1,κ}(1),

with integer clipping at Q=1. A sufficient window is Nε→0 and
N^2ε→∞; then Q→∞ and Q/d→0. Classical label storage of the explicit
code is separately at most 2s log_2 N+O_m(1). No assertion is made that
this label count or total hybrid dimension is optimal.
