STATUS AND STRONGEST CLAIM

For the supplied, known-parameter, all-to-all critical spin-half Gibbs family

  rho_(N,A,b) ∝ exp(2 J^2/N + Q_A(J)/N^(3/2) + b·J/N^(3/4)),
  Q_A(J)=Σ_ik A_ik(J_iJ_k+J_kJ_i)/2,

the saved finite-bit construction is a mathematical existence theorem for a
randomized classical description followed by N local qubit preparations. Under
its rational-data, fixed-public-bound, admitted-size, and native one-qubit
precision assumptions, it gives mean trace error at most
  min{1, E_sep,N + 2 eta + epsilon}
with a conservative polynomial bit-cost upper bound. It is not a runnable,
certified sampler or a gate-level/hardware result. The displayed global moment
constant makes the theorem impractical even for modest bounds; the polynomial
statement alone does not establish usefulness.

I derived a cost refinement that reuses the existing central supersolution but
improves its parameters. It (i) uses a data-dependent nonnegative scalar shift
Lambda with C=A+Lambda I positive semidefinite, replacing the worst-case
quadratic noise mass 6M by S=tr(C); (ii) tunes the field Young inequality with a
positive parameter lambda instead of fixing it to 1; (iii) sharpens the seed
quartic envelope; and (iv) lower-bounds the seed normalizer by a computable
one-dimensional integral instead of only the short interval [1,2]. For the
explicit rational example N=50,000, M=.1, B=.2, A=diag(.10,-.08,.03), this
changes the stated K2* upper bound from about 9.93e108 to about 159.5 and the
seed-attempt upper bound from 67.3 to 2.15. With eta=.049 and numerical budget
epsilon=.001, the resulting bound calls for R=66,400 proposals, m=17 Trotter
steps, about 1.13 million two-by-two update steps, and 50,000 local qubit
emissions. This is a bound, not measured runtime; costs of certified special
function evaluation and a discrete gate set remain open. The numerical example
is generated by cost_audit.py and is not a verified interval computation.

EXACT DERIVATION OF THE COST REFINEMENT

Write s=N^(3/4). Choose a supplied/rationally certified Lambda>=0 so that
C=A+Lambda I is PSD and t0=2/N-Lambda/N^(3/2)>0. For exact spectral data one
can take Lambda=max(0,-lambda_min(A)); a rational upper enclosure works if it
also preserves t0>0. Let S=tr(C). In the three-column square-root factorization
of C, sum_i ||v_i||^2=S. The local path perturbation term in the saved proof can
therefore use
  U = sqrt(3 S m/8) N^(-3/4)
instead of (3/2)sqrt(Mm)N^(-3/4), since the latter is the consequence of
S<=6M. This reduces the certified path radius Q=U L_R+B/(4N^(3/4)) when the
actual anisotropy has a smaller PSD shift than the public worst case.

For the two-replica weight bound, the field is a function of J_u and J^2, which
commute. For any lambda>0,
  B |J_u|/s <= lambda J^2/s^2 + B^2/(4 lambda).
Thus the saved central comparison yields
  Phi_(m,2N)^*(I) <= exp((S+lambda)J^2/s^2 + B^2/(4lambda)),
and its seed-integral step becomes
  E w^2 <= 3 exp(B^2/(4lambda)) E_seed exp(D R^2),
  D=(S+lambda)/2.
The original fixed choice lambda=1 gives D=(6M+1)/2 and the loose public
K2* bound. This change uses the same positive central supersolution; it does
not alter the target density or the D3 importance-resampling argument.

Let delta=1/2-log(cosh 1)>0, y=R/(2N^(1/4)), and f(y)=y^2/2-log(cosh y).
For 0<=y<=1,
  f(y)=integral_0^y (y-t)tanh^2(t)dt
      = y^4 integral_0^1 (1-u)u^2 [tanh(yu)/(yu)]^2 du
      >= delta y^4,
because tanh(z)/z decreases and equality in the last ratio occurs at y=1.
The Euler product for sinh gives log(sinh(y)/y)<=y^2/6. For y>=1,
f(y)/y^2 is nondecreasing: its derivative has the sign of
integral_0^y (2t-y)tanh^2(t)dt>=0, by monotonicity of tanh^2. Hence
f(y)>=delta y^2. These inequalities sharpen the saved 1/48 low-y bound.

For the seed radius law Q_N(R), dropping its nonpositive shift term gives the
pointwise envelopes
  Q_N(R) <= exp(1/6) R^2 exp(-a R^4),              y<=1,
  Q_N(R) <= R^2 exp(-(delta N-1)y^2),              y>=1,
where a=delta/16. The first envelope can be dominated by a Maxwell radial
density h_c=K R^2 exp(-beta R^2), with
  beta=sqrt(3a), K=exp(1/6+beta^2/(4a))=exp(11/12).
This beta minimizes the integral of that Maxwell envelope. The tail is dominated
by h_t=R^2 exp(-alpha_N R^2), alpha_N=(delta N-1)/(4sqrt(N)). Their proposal
mixture requires only three-dimensional normal draws, as in the saved sampler.
Its total envelope mass is
  C_new=exp(11/12)sqrt(pi)/(4 beta^(3/2)) + sqrt(pi)/(4 alpha_N^(3/2)).

For N>=256 and Lambda/sqrt(N)<=1, the seed normalizer Z_N has the public lower
bound
  Z_N >= Z_low(Lambda):=integral_0^8 R^2
           exp(-Lambda R^2/8 - R^4/192)dR.
Indeed, d0=1-Lambda/(2sqrt(N))>=1/2, sinh(y)/y>=1, and f(y)<=y^4/12; on
R<=8 we have y<=1. Therefore the rejection attempt count is at most
C_new/Z_low(Lambda). The integral may be evaluated with outward-rounded
quadrature; the values below used ordinary high-precision mpmath and are not
themselves certified enclosures.

For the tilted second moment, set alpha_N(D)=delta sqrt(N)/4-D-1/(4sqrt(N)).
When this is positive, the same split bounds E_seed exp(D R^2) by
  [ exp(1/6) I_a(D) + sqrt(pi)/(4 alpha_N(D)^(3/2)) ] / Z_low(Lambda),
where
  I_a(D)=integral_0^infinity R^2 exp(-aR^4+DR^2)dR
       <= (1/2) exp(D^2/(4a))
          [sqrt(D/(2a))*sqrt(pi/a) + Gamma(3/4)*a^(-3/4)].
The last inequality follows by setting t=R^2, completing the square in
-a t^2+Dt, and using sqrt(t)<=sqrt(D/(2a))+sqrt(|t-D/(2a)|). Therefore a
valid replacement moment constant is
  K2_new(lambda) = 3 exp(B^2/(4lambda))
       [exp(1/6) I_a(D)+sqrt(pi)/(4 alpha_N(D)^(3/2))]/Z_low(Lambda),
  D=(S+lambda)/2.
Choosing a fixed rational lambda near the numerical minimizer keeps all
certificates rationally selectable. The formula is proved at the envelope level;
the precision of its numerical evaluation still must be certified.

NUMERICAL COST LEDGER AND LIMITS

The bounded high-precision calculation in cost_audit.py gives, for the above
example, delta=.06621917, old Cg/L0=67.2341, new C_new/Z_low=2.14131,
old K2*=9.9259e108, and K2_new=159.424 for lambda≈.03065. The rational finite
sum gives F_N=.816773 and the saved finite separability certificate gives
E_sep,N=5.79e-9. These are numerical evaluations of explicit formulas; they do
not certify the special-function integrals or run the preparation algorithm.

For total error below .1, eta=.049 and numerical epsilon=.001 give m=17,
R=66,400 and Rm=1,128,800 matrix-update steps. Using the improved seed-attempt
bound, K=45 proposals per seed is a conservative exhaustion cap. The saved
inverse-normal implementation then caps scalar normal draws at
3R(K+m)=12,350,400; the corresponding expected bound is about 3,812,950.
For this numerical budget it chooses L≈7.308, path radius L_R≈53.565,
Q≈.02180 and dyadic h<=9.35e-17 (54 precision bits). It uses at most 57 CDF
bisections per normal; each CDF evaluation has a further polynomial-bit series
cost. Randomness is about 110 bits per normal draw at this parameter point.
These costs show why the word “polynomial” hides a material implementation
burden. A clipped finite-bit Box-Muller normal generator is a plausible route to
remove the per-draw inverse-CDF bisection, but it still needs its own coupling,
tail, and interval-arithmetic proof before it can replace the audited routine.

The quantum output cost is at least N emitted systems and the saved construction
meets this with N local one-qubit preparations. Their individual trace error is
charged at O(epsilon/N). The native preparation interface is assumed; the saved
proof supplies no discrete gate-set compilation or hardware cost. The method
does not preserve a purification or an arbitrary external reference.

The known-parameter construction is distinct from the parameter-independent
one-observation decoder, whose whole-state rate is O(N^(-1/4)); no faster rate
may be transferred to that operational task. The O(N^(-3/2)) known-parameter
separable-cone distance is not shown optimal. Neither finite checks nor this
cost refinement establish historical priority.
