## 1. Finite connected circulations and the reset component

For a finite directed multigraph, a nonzero nonnegative integer edge count `n` with balanced incidence `Bn=0` has a directed Euler circuit if its positive-degree support is weakly connected. Balanced indegree/outdegree plus weak connectivity makes that support strongly connected. Requiring the support to contain `z0` gives a circuit based at `z0`. Self-loops and parallel edges cause no exception.

Suggested wording: specify **weakly connected positive-degree support containing z0**. The root's unqualified word “connected” is not mathematically wrong if it means this or the stronger directed condition, but the explicit convention prevents ambiguity.

Every finite closed walk based at `z0` remains in the strongly connected component of `z0`. Conversely, cycles in that component can be connected to `z0` and back by legal paths. A cycle in another component is inaccessible to a restored walk and cannot be admitted by a bare whole-graph circulation LP.

Disconnected positive support inside the reset component is the precise place where the finite and asymptotic claims differ. Such a circulation is not itself one finite Euler walk. The full graph may contain zero-count connectors that permit approximation with small normalized overhead. The root correctly charges those connectors and claims closure only.

## 2. Normalized closure

Let all times be strictly positive and let `f>=0`, `Bf=0`, `t.f=1` inside the reset component. Decompose `f` into finitely many nonnegative real cycle coefficients. For each large integer scale `N`, round `N` times those coefficients to integers. The resulting cycle counts differ by a bounded amount from the desired scaled flow. Visit each cycle from `z0`, execute it the rounded number of times, and return to `z0`.

The number and lengths of connector paths are bounded independently of `N`, since the graph and cycle decomposition are fixed. Normalize by the actual total time. Rounding and connector frequency errors tend to zero, so the legal closed-walk frequencies converge to `f`. This proof works with arbitrary fixed positive real times and rewards; no rationality of those physical labels is needed for closure.

Therefore the closure equals `C={f>=0:Bf=0,t.f=1}` on the reset component. The argument does not establish exact finite equality of a salt row. Intersecting `C` with `s.f=0` is not generally the closure of exactly salt-zero finite closed walks. The root's counterexample proves that distinction.

An idle loop is a physical model addition. The LP claims presuppose that the loop is a legal zero-transfer operation under the same external conditions, or that an equivalent no-operation limit is explicitly allowed. Passive leakage at a developed gradient must be included if it makes “idle” nonzero. This is a receiving-interface qualification, not a graph-duality error.

## 3. LP duality and attainment

After adding the stipulated idle loop, the salt-balanced primal is feasible. Positive times give `0<=f_e<=1/t_e`, so the feasible set is compact and the objective is finite and attained. The dual has variables `u`, `kappa`, `tau`, all unrestricted before edge inequalities:

`min tau`, subject to `B^T u+kappa s+tau t>=x`.

The incidence convention yields `u(head)-u(tail)` with the root's sign. A dual feasible point exists by taking `u=kappa=0` and sufficiently large `tau`. Finite-dimensional LP strong duality gives equal attained optimal values. The unbounded additive gauge of `u` does not prevent dual optimum attainment.

The idle loop forces `tau>=0`; it also gives `eta>=0`. Consequently `eta=0` is equivalent to an attained dual certificate with `tau=0`, exactly as stated. Without an idle/zero feasible output, `eta` need not be nonnegative and “eta=0 iff a tau=0 certificate exists” needs modification. That exception is already avoided by the root's explicit model addition.

If the phrase “strong duality applies when C is nonempty” is retained, read it together with the preceding idle-loop clause. Nonempty `C` by itself does not imply salt-balanced feasibility; a graph whose every cycle has positive salt provides a simple counterexample without the idle loop.

## 4. Exact-zero example passes

Use counts `(a,b,c,d)` for `0->1`, `1->0`, the exchange loop at 1, and idle at 0. Incidence balance gives `a=b`. A realizable closed walk based at 0 with `c>0` must have `a=b>=1`, so its salt is `a>=1`. Exact finite salt zero thus implies `a=b=c=0` and only idle remains.

The normalized circulation `f=(0,0,1,0)` has unit time, salt zero and exchange one. Since exchange is at most total time on every edge, this is the LP optimum `eta=1`. The dual choice `tau=1,kappa=0,u=0` attains the same value. The legal walk `(a,b,c,d)=(1,1,N,0)` has salt 1, exchange `N` and time `N+2`; it approaches the LP frequencies and rates.

The counterexample remains valid for a strongly connected full graph. Strong connectivity alone does not turn a disconnected-support relaxed solution into an exact finite salt-zero schedule. It supplies connectors whose absolute salt can persist.

## 5. Hidden correction, units, and the needed salt allowance

If edge `x_e,s_e` are amounts in moles and `t_e` is time, then:

* `f_e` has units inverse time;
* `eta,tau` have units exchange moles per time;
* `kappa` has units exchange moles per salt mole, numerically dimensionless when both use moles;
* `u`, its span `U`, and any hidden exchange allowance `C_X` have units exchange moles.

The node potential is an LP accounting quantity. It need not be a literal adsorbed-inventory amount or a thermodynamic free energy. Its span is gauge invariant. Do not add a dimensionless potential from a per-site event model to mole-valued storage bounds without the carrier-site mole conversion.

Let certified edge sums be `X,S`, while observed coordinates are `Xobs=X+h_X` and `Sobs=S+h_S`, with `|h_X|<=C_X`, `|h_S|<=C_S`. Include sampling/calibration/source error in these allowances or separately. From the finite-path dual inequality and `|Sobs|<=epsilon`,

`Xobs <= |kappa|(epsilon+C_S) + U + C_X + tau T`.

If the root's `s_e` already equals the corrected observed quantity, take `C_S=0` and the existing formula is valid. Otherwise the missing term is substantive. An exact counterexample to dropping it is a one-state edge with certified `x=s=1`, an idle loop, and `kappa=1,u=tau=0`. Hidden chloride contribution `h_S=-1` makes `Sobs=0`, while `Xobs=1`. The certificate is true, yet the uncorrected bound with `C_S=0` would claim `Xobs<=0`.

For the frozen adversarial packet's antisymmetric both-reservoir exchange coordinate and a conservative total hidden Li/Na amount `B`, the exact storage width is `C_X=B/2`. A one-receiver coordinate instead has width `B` under the corresponding capacity contract. Neither number supplies `C_S`; chloride/electrode storage needs its own bound or a joint polytope. If both an explicit state potential and a residual storage width describe the same inventory, separate their meanings to avoid charging the same uncertainty twice. Double charging is conservative, but it can destroy a useful bound.

## 6. Pure-exchange qualification

The root LP constrains salt and optimizes `X=(Li-Na)/2`. To identify the target `(q,-q,0)`, one also needs `A=Li+Na=0`. In a fully closed three-ion system with neutral external reservoirs and restored charge/electrode inventories, `A=Cl` can supply that implication. It must be part of the acquired interface.

With other transported ions, a neutral restored operation can instead move Li forward and K backward, with Na and Cl unchanged. It has `Cl=0` and `X>0`, yet no Na countertransport. Thus complete internal reset alone is not a substitute for the three-ion/charge premise.

The general repair is to append nuisance rows `R f=0`, with vector multiplier `lambda`:

`x_e <= lambda.R_e + u(head)-u(tail)+tau t_e`.

The same closure and LP proofs apply. Include every claimed auxiliary restoration coordinate or impose independently justified residual bounds. This is exactly the role of the previous cyclic worker's `Q` and “other restoration rows.”

## 7. Robust boxes

For fixed `kappa,u,tau`, maximizing `x_e-kappa s_e` over one rectangular edge box gives

`xhat_e-kappa shat_e+epsx_e+|kappa|epss_e`.

The root's robust inequality is exact for that uncertainty set. The epigraph `a>=kappa,a>=-kappa` gives an equivalent LP because each `epss_e>=0`. Shared uncertainty in x/s can shrink the support term. A Cartesian product of marginal confidence intervals can be used conservatively without probabilistic independence if simultaneous coverage is justified; “independent rectangle” is a geometric uncertainty contract, not by itself a statistical coverage theorem.

Cross-edge correlations can sometimes improve a *schedule-level* robust bound. A single uniform edgewise certificate must nevertheless hold at every edge for every allowed latent parameter; taking the appropriate marginal support for each inequality is valid. Do not silently claim the edgewise robust bound is the optimal robust control value over all correlated model uncertainties.

## 8. Does the previous cyclic construction assume connected flows?

Its first section states an accounting implication for **expected** event counts `z`, with `Dz=p_end-p_start`. For any already realized process, distribution reset gives a circulation. Cycle decomposition of that circulation is valid even if its support is disconnected: different carriers or trajectories can populate different components. This is not a converse controllability theorem from a fixed starting state. The proof explicitly says a signed row-space failure need not yield a nonnegative circulation or legal schedule.

Its occupation-measure section constructs a stationary randomized policy from `mu(x,u)` on occupied states. This is correct, with invariant initial distribution `m(x)=sum_u mu(x,u)`. A stationary occupation measure need not be reachable from a prescribed reset state. For example, two absorbing states and a measure concentrated on state 1 define a stationary policy/distribution, while a device beginning at absorbing state 0 never reaches it. Add reachability/unichain assumptions or charge preparation of the required mixture before calling an arbitrary occupation measure achievable from a fixed reset.

The worker's explicit four-phase antiporter avoids this issue. The phase controls are specified legal kernels, and their period kernel is irreducible and aperiodic. Independent reconstruction verifies the stated periodic distribution, period matrix, exchange `2/17`, and contraction `9/16`. The phase-augmented 12-node graph is strongly connected because each phase has a positive stay probability and the cycle opens both reversible species edges. The periodic initial distribution can be approached by repeated paid preparation cycles, as the worker already states. It is a reset in distribution, not exact restoration of every microscopic carrier state.

The root's deterministic fixed-edge theorem and the worker's stochastic phase-kernel theorem are complementary but have different interfaces. Applying the former directly to measured mean phase rewards gives expectation-level bounds unless actual finite-run deviations are also bounded. Distributional reset and stochastic species outputs must retain those quantifiers.
