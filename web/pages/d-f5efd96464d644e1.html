# Hostile materials audit: Mg/La residue versus impurity-layer contact loss

## Verdict

The cross-paper mechanism is plausible enough to test in LiMgLa|LLZTO, but the present sources do not support a numerical Vema-style capacity cap for that material, or a claim that the LiMgLa seeds form a blocking layer. Ji et al. show interface-adjacent grains after full stripping and Mg/La mapping after partial stripping; they do not chemically identify the full-strip interface grains, quantify their size/fraction/coverage, or measure their Li-ion transport. Their separate liquid-electrolyte control reports Mg and La remaining broadly distributed in Li. Vema et al. study a different impurity population in Li|Al-LLZO, with oxygen-rich residues and a model that assumes insulating particles. The shared garnet/Li setting makes transfer testable, not established.

The meaningful surviving design hypothesis is conditional: if a measured fraction of Mg/La-bearing material is captured as a contact-blocking particle layer, the Vema geometry predicts a pressure/current-dependent contact capacity in addition to Ji's diffusion/void-suppression limit. The captured blocking fraction, actual phase, Li-ion conductance, particle geometry, starting coverage, and same-protocol creep response are all unknown. Consequently `min(VSC/j, C_cr, Q_fatigue)` is a candidate screen, not yet a materials prediction.

## What the two source papers actually establish

Ji et al. use a nominal `Li97.5(Mg–1 wt% La)2.5` composition: about 2.5 wt% master alloy, comprising nominally 2.475 wt% Mg and 0.025 wt% La. Their Supplementary Fig. 21 shows a Materials Project phase diagram and a calculated Wulff shape for a LaMg3 particle; the main-text Figure 3 includes ideal Li|LaMg3 DFT interfaces. Those calculations make LaMg3 a candidate phase, but they do not supply the number density or volume fraction of nonstrippable LaMg3 particles in the operating anode. If every La atom were in LaMg3, stoichiometry permits at most about 0.038 wt% LaMg3 by mass. That is a nominal mass bound, not Vema's volume fraction `f`; it does not bound Mg-rich phases, and phase/density measurements are still needed.

The detail that most changes the transfer argument is in the supplement. Note 4 deliberately switches to 4 M LiFSI/DME to avoid confusion from La-containing LLZTO and reports that Mg and La remain uniformly distributed in deposited Li without obvious segregation and remain in the electrode as nucleation sites. Supplementary Fig. 13's caption places Mg/La EDX maps after stripping to 2 mAh cm−2; its full-strip panels are SEM images only, labeled as showing a small grain at the interface and another in the bulk. Thus morphology supports retention near the interface, while chemistry at that full-strip feature remains inferred rather than mapped there. Neither source image establishes a continuous layer or transport blocking.

Vema et al. test nominally 99.9%-pure Li against Al-substituted LLZO. Their cryogenic EDS maps show an oxygen-enriched layer on the Li side after stripping (their SI Fig. S2); the source does not chemically identify the exact impurity phases. Their mechanistic account treats those impurities as insoluble, insulating particles transported to the interface during stripping. For the simple particle estimate they set `f=0.1%` from the manufacturer's Li purity, infer submicron particles from microscopy, choose `a=130 nm` and `a0=200 nm`, and use the minimum measured starting interface resistance as `Z0`. Those are source-specific estimates, not parameters of LiMgLa. Their SI Eq. S25 also accounts for existing interface coverage; their uniform monolayer is a capacity idealization, while their observed residue deposition is nonuniform.

There is a useful phase-specific prior against inferring behavior from elemental identity alone. Fu et al. report that an Mg metal coating dissolves into molten Li and diffuses into bulk Li, producing low, stable resistance at a Li|garnet interface (DOI 10.1002/ange.201708637). This is not a LaMg3 particle or the Ji alloy, so it is not a prediction for LiMgLa. It establishes that Mg-bearing material can follow a dissolution/redistribution route rather than behaving like Vema's insulating residue. Electronic conductivity would not settle the question either: Li-ion conductance through the particle/interphase or around it is the relevant transport gate.

## Parameter and geometry counterexample

Let `f_seed` denote the true volume fraction of a measured seed phase in the Li, and let `eta` be the fraction of that phase that is both captured at the Li|SSE interface during stripping and present in the Vema-like contact-blocking state. Under the same spherical-particle/creep assumptions, the Vema deposition balance would use `f_eff = eta*f_seed`; its zero-initial-coverage critical height therefore scales as `H_cr(eta)=H_cr(eta=1)/eta`. The same `f_seed`, radius, pressure, current, and creep law produce a tenfold difference for `eta=0.1` and a hundredfold difference for `eta=0.01`; at `eta=0`, the Vema contact-capacity gate disappears. These are sensitivity counterexamples, not estimates of LiMgLa's `eta`. The source does not measure `eta`.

That scalar correction still assumes the inoculant can be represented by the Vema particle layer. A dispersed sphere population, grain-boundary-pinned inclusions, a connected interphase, and particles that dissolve or conduct Li+ do not share a common mapping from bulk mass fraction to active interparticle gap. Equal nominal loading can therefore yield different interface coverage and conductance. The Vema result is not a universal function of nominal alloy content; it is conditional on phase, morphology, transfer, and mechanics.

The reproducible calculation is in [counterexamples.py](counterexamples.py). It computes the LaMg3 stoichiometric ceiling and the `1/eta` sensitivity. The calculation intentionally does not turn a mass composition into a particle volume fraction.

## Decisive experiment and decision rule

Use matched Li, Li97.5Mg2.5, and Li97.5(Mg–1 wt% La)2.5 cells with the same LLZTO batch, surface finish, temperature, nominal area, pressure, current, and matched initial interfacial resistance. Interrupt at zero, preregistered partial-strip capacities, and full strip. At each condition:

1. Use cryo-FIB/STEM with Mg-sensitive EDS/EELS or atom-probe/TEM diffraction to identify phases and quantify interface coverage, particle/feature dimensions, connectivity, and residual bulk distribution. Mg is the cleaner seed tracer because LLZTO already contains La; use matched LLZTO background controls for La.
2. Close a Mg mass balance across remaining Li, interface, and electrolyte and estimate the captured fraction `eta`; do not infer `f` from the nominal weight recipe.
3. Measure Li-ion transport through representative identified phases/interphases, and measure same-condition pressure-dependent Li creep or gap refill. In parallel, record three-electrode voltage and impedance during strip, rest, and restart.
4. Fit geometry/transport parameters on one pressure-current set, then predict held-out pressure/current thresholds without refitting. Include the existing-coverage correction using measured starting impedance/coverage.

Support for transfer requires Mg-bearing material to accumulate at the interface before resistance growth, a measured transport/mechanical route that makes the layer limiting, and out-of-sample capacity/restart-resistance trends following the measured pressure/current and gap-spacing model. The transfer fails for the tested protocol if Mg/La does not enrich at the interface, if the features remain Li+-permeable or are bypassed, or if their descriptors do not predict capacity/resistance after controlling for initial impedance and pressure. If seeds reach the interface but do not reduce Li+ transport, they may still affect mechanics; that would be a different mechanism from Vema's contact-blocking particle gate.
