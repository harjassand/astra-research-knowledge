## What “an actual design algorithm” means in the introduction

Printed pages347–348 distinguish three interfaces: computable real coordinates approximable to any precision, actual coordinates specified by a terminating algorithm, and finite symbolic coordinate formulas. The “very recent” actual-design algorithm refers to reference8, Cui–Xia–Xiang, *Rational designs*, Advances in Mathematics352(2019),541–571. As described by Xiang, existence of almost-rational designs allows enumeration of all finite almost-rational point sets followed by exact design testing; some test eventually succeeds. Almost rational means every coordinate except possibly the first is rational. The resulting coordinates belong to the field generated by square roots of primes.

That terminating enumeration is an exact algebraic acquisition interface. The description supplies no polynomial running-time or variable-degree cardinality guarantee. This worker read Xiang's explanation and bibliography, not the full Cui–Xia–Xiang paper. Its scope is therefore reported through this primary source rather than as an independent audit of the older theorem.

The approximation algorithms mentioned in the introduction are likewise credited as computable constructions. The introduction does not charge their tolerance dependence or prove a uniform polynomial bit budget. References6–7 include Chen–Frommer–Lang2011 and Chen–Womersley2006. A fresh read of the former's Algorithm1 and Lemma2 establishes the more precise finite-certification scope recorded in the main prior gate.

## Reconstructing the prescribed S² cardinality

Let T denote the requested spherical strength; use u for the even parameter called t in §3.4. The construction on printed page349 first obtains a strength T+d−1 weighted H¹₁ semidesign. For d=2, its strength must be at least T+1. Corollary3.12 gives strength u−1, so choose the least even u≥T+2, or any larger even u. Thus u=T+O(1).

Remark3.13 prescribes

    n=(u^(u−1)+1)u=u^u+u,
    m=3u^(u+1).

The n starting abscissas are a_j=(-n+1+2j)/n. Rational-circle rounding uses c_j=(1−sqrt(1−a_j²))/a_j, k_j=floor(m c_j), and rational abscissas 2k_jm/(k_j²+m²), completed by reflection. The rounding error is less than the original separation, so there are n distinct support points. Theorem3.6 assigns positive rational weights chi of total mass exactly n, with weight1 at all but u support points.

For S², Step3 takes s=1 and starts from the same H¹₁ measure; its density multiplier is x_0^(s−1)=1. Step4 is a one-factor product. Neither step changes the total weight n. Step5 multiplies every weight by a positive integer c so that c chi is integer valued; Remark4.12 permits the least common multiple of the reduced weight denominators. The resulting integer-weighted object has total mass c n.

Theorem1.1(i) chooses Y₁ as the regular 4(T+1)-gon. Step6 can be skipped for a=1 as explained at the start of §4.5. Definition4.19 gives the exact mass identity

    |Y₁ twisted-product X|=|Y₁||X|.

Theorem4.20 and Corollary4.25 separate all copies and make the resulting rule unweighted. The map S¹×H¹→S² used in §4.1 is injective because the first hemisphere coordinate is strictly positive. Thus there is no count reduction from identifying copies or from the final embedding. The exact final cardinality is

    N_X(T)=4(T+1)c(u^u+u),   c>=1.                 (1)

In particular N_X(T)>=4(T+1)(u^u+u). Its logarithm is at least u log u+O(log T); the output count already exceeds every polynomial in T, before charging denominator clearing. An explicit output algorithm must at least emit these N_X points, so this recipe cannot match polynomial bit time in T with O(T²) output cardinality.

There is also a certificate limitation before specializing Remark3.13. Corollary3.12 assumes

    epsilon<1/u^(u+1)−1/(4 n u).

Since epsilon>=0, every n certified by that sufficient inequality must satisfy n>u^u/4. A different n within the same corollary therefore cannot reduce the output to O(T²). This is a limitation of this sufficient construction branch, not an intrinsic lower bound for exact designs or for all possible improvements of Xiang's method.

## Bit and field interface

The two initial circle coordinates belong to the totally real part of Q(zeta_(4(T+1))). Twisting uses rational orthogonal matrices and the hemisphere points are rational. Hence the final S² nodes are exact algebraic unit vectors with their third coordinate rational; the first two need not be rational. Xiang's finite-symbol description permits indexed sums/products and roots of unity. A constant number of symbols in that language does not mean a constant or polynomial number of expanded output bits.

Here log m=O(u log u), so the unmodified hemisphere coordinates individually have O(u log u) rational bits. The rational twist coefficients additionally depend on the copy index, whose maximum can be as large as c n. The denominator-clearing factor c is computed from the rational weights or replaced by an explicit multiple; the paper's read statements do not provide the claimed programme's O(log T) rational-coordinate output or a polynomial-in-T total expansion budget. I do not infer an exponential lower bound for each coordinate height: the already-proved count obstruction is sufficient, and the final fields differ as well.

## Comparison and decisive limit

Xiang proves genuine finite explicit exact designs for arbitrary strength, a stronger exactness interface than the programme's approximate equal-weight rational rule. It does not follow that an approximate rational rule is the first computable or actual spherical design algorithm. Conversely, the prescribed explicit recipe has the exact cardinality obstruction (1), and the enumeration described in the introduction has no charged universal polynomial bound there. Neither comparison supplies the complete deterministic polynomial-bit O(T²) approximate-node interface being evaluated in this programme.
