# Rational spectral filtering for the missing certified-basis interface

7 October2026. Root derivation at the cycle17 checkpoint. **AUTHOR CANDIDATE; independent gate pending.** No numerical run, novelty assessment or full small-SDP learner is claimed. This is a concrete first next-cycle audit target; frozen worker reports are not retroactively claimed to adopt it.

## Exact interface

Let S,W be rational symmetric matrices of order N with S positive semidefinite and W positive definite. Let A=SW. A is selfadjoint in the W inner product and similar to the symmetric PSD matrix W^(1/2) S W^(1/2). Suppose an exact certificate supplies r, gamma>0 and epsilon<=gamma/4 such that its top r eigenvalues are >=gamma and its remaining eigenvalues are <=epsilon. In the SOS application r=n-1; rational inertia and the already proved residual bound can supply these inequalities. Acquiring sufficiently accurate primal/dual data remains charged.

For any requested rational eta>0, the proposed algorithm returns rational Y whose range is within eta, in ordinary Euclidean projector norm, of the top-r right eigenspace of A, with I/2<=Y^T Y<=3I/2. No irrational eigenvectors or numerical spectral oracle are supplied.

## A rapidly convergent rational projector filter

Compute Q0=A(A+(gamma/2)I)^(-1) over the rationals. Its low eigenvalues are in[0,1/3] and high eigenvalues in[2/3,1]. Iterate

    Q_(j+1)=3 Q_j^2-2 Q_j^3.

The scalar polynomial f(x)=3x^2-2x^3 preserves[0,1] and obeys f(1-x)=1-f(x). After one step a low eigenvalue is at most7/27. Since f(x)<=3x^2, for j>=1 its distance to the appropriate endpoint is at most

    delta_j=(1/3)(7/9)^(2^(j-1)).

Let Pi be the orthogonal top-r projector of W^(1/2) A W^(-1/2), and P=W^(-1/2) Pi W^(1/2). Then range P is the desired right eigenspace and

    ||Q_j-P||_2 <=sqrt(kappa(W)) delta_j.

Acquire a rational K>=max(1,kappa(W)); for instance (tr W)^N/det W is a valid bound, rounded upward. It has polynomial bit size from exact determinant arithmetic. Choose the iteration count so that

    delta_j <=min(1/(2K), eta*tau/K),
    tau=2^(-2r) K^(-r) N^(-r).

This takes O(log(2+log(1/delta_target))) steps. With one common denominator, the rational matrix update cubes that denominator and uses polynomial matrix arithmetic. Its entry bit bound satisfies b_(j+1)<=3b_j+O(log N), hence is polynomial in the initial bit size and log(1/delta_target): 3^j is a power of that logarithm. This detail matters; merely saying that the iteration count is small would not control exact rational costs.

## Select a well-conditioned rational basis

For Q=Q_j select r columns greedily, maximizing their squared Euclidean residual after exact rational orthogonal projection onto previously selected columns. The squared residuals and projections are rational; no square root is needed for pivoting.

The nonzero singular values of P are at least1/sqrt(K), and ||P||<=sqrt(K). Weyl and delta_j<=1/(2K) imply sigma_r(Q)>=1/(2sqrt(K)) and ||Q||<=2sqrt(K). After k<r columns, the norm of the unselected residual matrix is at least sigma_(k+1)(Q), by the rank-k approximation inequality. Therefore some column residual is at least1/(2sqrt(KN)).

The product of these r residuals bounds the selected matrix B's volume below; its other r-1 singular values are at most2sqrt(K). Consequently

    sigma_min(B) >= [1/(2sqrt(KN))]^r /(2sqrt(K))^(r-1)
                 >= tau.

If E projects orthogonally onto range P, then ||(I-E)B||<=||Q-P||<=K delta_j. Thus the directed gap from range B to range P is at most K delta_j/tau<=eta. Both have dimension r, so this is the ordinary projector-norm bound. The exponential-in-r numeric tau has polynomial logarithmic size and is explicitly charged, rather than hidden as a condition number.

## Rational normalization without changing the range

Compute B^T B=L D L^T by exact positive LDL. Let C=B L^(-T), so C^T C=D is diagonal. Choose each nonzero rational scale s_i with1/2<=D_ii s_i^2<=3/2, using rational interval square-root approximation or elementary dyadic bracketing. Then Y=C diag(s_i) is rational, preserves range B exactly, and has the required approximate orthonormality. Constant relative precision suffices, independent of eta; the acquired LDL heights and scaling bits remain polynomial in B's encoding.

All matrices here have order at most N. Greedy normal equations, inverses, LDL and a polynomial number of rational matrix products have polynomial bit complexity in their input encoding, log(1/eta) and the supplied separation parameters. A complete proof should independently check the common-denominator growth, singular-value/product bound and spectral-certificate composition before adoption.
