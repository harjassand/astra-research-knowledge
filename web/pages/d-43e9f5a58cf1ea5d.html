## Exact extension and interface

Fix a finite signature consisting of `q>=1` relation symbols with positive arities `a_1,...,a_q<=r`, where `r>=2`. On `n>=2` vertices the coordinates are

`J_n = {(t,(v_1,...,v_{a_t})): 1<=t<=q, v_i in [n]}`.

Repeated vertices in a tuple are permitted. Thus the signature can include directed relations, loops, and unary labels. Draw every coordinate independently with the **same** Bernoulli bias `0<p<1`. Let `f:{0,1}^{J_n}->{0,1}` be invariant under the simultaneous action of every permutation of the vertices. Pivotal influence is the disagreement probability between sections at coordinate 0 and 1; it has no extra factor `p(1-p)`.

**Deduction R.** There is a constant `C_{r,q}` such that

`Var_p(f) <= C_{r,q} I_p(f)/(log n)^(r/(r-1))`

for every `n,p,f` in this interface. One may take a constant of order `O_r(q^(1/(r-1)))`, plus a finite small-n term depending only on `r`. For increasing nonconstant `f`, if `p_epsilon,p_{1-epsilon}` are its probability quantiles, then

`p_{1-epsilon}-p_epsilon <= 2 C_{r,q} log((1-epsilon)/epsilon)/(log n)^(r/(r-1))`.

The original simple r-uniform hypergraph result uses unordered, distinct-vertex coordinates rather than the ordered relational coordinates above. Its proof and constants are separately preserved below.

## Reconstruction of the analytic service

Write `sigma=sqrt(p(1-p))` and `chi_i=(x_i-p)/sigma`. For a Boolean `h` on any finite product cube,

`hat h(A union {i})=sigma hat(Delta_i h)(A)`,

where `Delta_i h` is the section difference and takes values in `{-1,0,1}`. Consequently

`sum_S |S| hat h(S)^2 = sigma^2 I(h)` and `Var(h)=sum_{S nonempty}hat h(S)^2`.

Suppose `I_i(h)<=I(h)/m` for every coordinate, and put `k=sigma log m/64`. The following low-level estimate is the source's Lemma 2.1, reconstructed here:

`sum_{1<=|S|<=k} hat h(S)^2 <= m^(-1/4) I(h)`.

For completeness, define `T chi_A=(sigma/4)^|A| chi_A`. In one coordinate, `E chi^3` has absolute value at most `1/sigma` and `E chi^4<=1/sigma^2`. Expanding the fourth moment and using `2|ab^3|<=a^2b^2+b^4` gives

`E(a+(sigma/4)b chi)^4 <= a^4+(13 sigma^2/32)a^2b^2+(9 sigma^2/256)b^4 <= (a^2+b^2)^2`.

The inequality tensorizes: writing `g=a+b chi_i`, the one-coordinate estimate followed by the L2 triangle inequality and induction proves `||Tg||_4<=||g||_2`. Duality then gives

`||P_{<=k}g||_2 <= (4/sigma)^k ||g||_{4/3}`.

Each nonempty Fourier support can be charged to at least one of its coordinates, so its low-level mass is at most

`sigma^2 (4/sigma)^(2k) sum_i I_i(h)^(3/2)`.

Since `sigma log(4/sigma)<=2`, the middle factor is at most `m^(1/16)`. If `I(h)<=m^(1/4)`, then `sqrt(I_i(h))<=m^(-3/8)`, producing at most `sigma^2 m^(-5/16)I(h)<=m^(-1/4)I(h)`. If `I(h)>m^(1/4)`, the claimed right side exceeds 1, whereas the variance is at most 1/4. A nonintegral cutoff causes no issue: only integer Fourier levels are included. The low-level sum is empty when `k<1`.

## The new combinatorial service

Choose a uniform block `B` of `m>=r+1` vertices; keep free every relation coordinate whose tuple meets `B`, and fix the others. All permutations supported inside `B` fix the exterior assignment individually. A free ordered tuple has `j` distinct vertices inside `B`, where `1<=j<=r`. Its orbit has `(m)_j>=m` elements. Thus every restricted individual influence is at most its restricted total influence divided by `m`.

Let `F_B` be the free coordinate set. Averaging the analytic estimate over the exterior assignment gives, by orthogonality of the exterior Fourier characters,

`sum_S Pr_B(1<=|S intersect F_B|<=k) hat f(S)^2 <= m^(-1/4) (r m/n) I_p(f)`.

The factor `r m/n` is only a union bound on the distinct vertices in a coordinate; repeats reduce this probability.

Now take a nonempty Fourier support `S` of `s` relation coordinates. Let `V(S)` be the vertices occurring in its tuples and write `v=|V(S)|`. Distinct coordinates on these vertices number at most

`sum_{t=1}^q v^(a_t) <= q v^r`.

Hence `v>=(s/q)^(1/r)`. The sum of distinct-vertex incidences is at most `rs`. Some occurring vertex `u` therefore has positive support degree

`1<=d_S(u)<=rs/v<=r q^(1/r) s^((r-1)/r)`.

Set `alpha=r/(r-1)`, `D=r q^(1/r)`, and `L=(k/D)^alpha`. Every support `1<=s<=L` has `1<=d_S(u)<=k`. Also `v<=rs<=rL`. Provided

`rL(m-1)/(n-1)<=1/2`,

the probability that `B` contains `u` and no other vertex of `V(S)` is at least

`(m/n)(1-(v-1)(m-1)/(n-1)) >= m/(2n)`.

On that event precisely the `d_S(u)` relation coordinates involving `u` remain free. Each support is bounded separately; the choice of `u` may depend on the support. No simultaneous capture event is asserted. Dividing the averaged estimate by `m/(2n)` gives

`sum_{1<=|S|<=L}hat f(S)^2 <= 2r m^(-1/4) I_p(f)`.

This is the complete change required for the relational extension: the source hypergraph count `s<=v^r` becomes `s<=qv^r`.

## Completing the uniform theorem

Take `m=floor(sqrt n)`. For all sufficiently large `n`, depending only on `r`, we have `m>=r+1`, `log m>=log n/3`, the capture condition, and `m^(-1/4)(log n)^alpha<=1`. This choice can be uniform in `q>=1` because `D>=r` only decreases `L`.

The captured mass is at most `2r I_p(f)/(log n)^alpha`. Weighted Parseval bounds the remaining mass by `sigma^2 I_p(f)/L`. Its coefficient is

`sigma^2/L = (64D)^alpha sigma^(2-alpha)/(log m)^alpha <= (192D)^alpha/(log n)^alpha`.

Here `2-alpha>=0` for `r>=2`, so the estimate is uniform even at small bias. The source's r>=3 proof has a strictly positive exponent, but positivity is not needed at r=2. If `L<1`, the captured sum is empty and the same tail estimate covers the entire variance.

For a large-n threshold `N_r`, take

`C_{r,q}=max{2r+(192 r q^(1/r))^alpha, (log N_r)^alpha/4}`.

The finite small-n range follows from ordinary product Poincare, `Var<=sigma^2 I<=I/4`. This proves Deduction R. For increasing `f`, direct differentiation of the finite product expectation gives `q'(p)=I_p(f)`. The derivative of `log(q/(1-q))` is therefore at least `(log n)^alpha/C_{r,q}`; integrating between the two quantiles yields the stated width.

## A noise-sensitivity consequence with its full assumptions

Let `Y` be formed from `X` by independently retaining each coordinate with probability `1-t` and otherwise drawing a fresh Bernoulli(p) value. This is resampling noise, not adversarial bit flipping. Product Fourier diagonalization gives exactly

`Cov(f(X),f(Y))=sum_{S nonempty}(1-t)^|S|hat f(S)^2`.

For every valid block size and cutoff from the preceding argument,

`0<=Cov(f(X),f(Y))<=2r m^(-1/4)I_p(f)+exp(-tL)Var_p(f)`.

Thus, for a nonconstant family `f_n`, fix `0<a<1` and use `m=floor(n^a)`. If

`I_{p_n}(f_n)/Var_{p_n}(f_n)=O(n^beta)` for some `beta<a/4`,

and `t_n (sqrt(p_n(1-p_n)) log n)^alpha -> infinity`, then the normalized covariance tends to zero. At fixed bias this requires `t_n(log n)^alpha -> infinity`. When `I/Var=n^{o(1)}`, any fixed `a` works. The capture condition holds eventually since `rL n^(a-1)` tends to zero.

This gives an explicit transfer from measured pivotal influence to instability under input resampling. It is applicable to invariant random constraint or relational decision properties meeting the stated influence bound. It is **not** an unconditional noise-sensitivity theorem: a majority of all relation bits has much larger total influence and may remain noise stable at fixed noise.

For a usual sparse random r-ary constraint model with `p_n` of order `n^(-(r-1))`, `sigma_n log n` tends to zero. The stated vanishing-noise condition then cannot hold with `t_n<=1`. Moreover, the absolute logarithmic threshold-width bound can be much larger than the location of that sparse transition. Dense relational models, or separately justified bias regimes, are the direct useful interface; a sparse critical-window improvement would require a new bias-sensitive argument.

## Adversarial boundaries and research use

* Nullary symbols break the theorem. A Boolean function of one nullary bit is invariant, has `Var=1/4` at p=1/2 and `I=1`, contradicting any fixed `C/(log n)^alpha` for large n. Every coordinate must involve at least one vertex.
* Full vertex invariance is essential to the orbit step. A distinguished coordinate also has variance 1/4 and influence 1. Coordinate transitivity alone does not supply the surviving vertex-block group; ordinary tribes give the familiar log-scale barrier.
* A real-valued spectral-gap interpretation is false. For the sum of all independent coordinates, the squared-difference Dirichlet energy equals its variance and its Fourier degree is one. Booleanity is used through `Delta h in {-1,0,1}` and `||Delta h||_(4/3)^2=I_i^(3/2)`.
* Unequal relation biases are not covered by the uniform-bias constant cancellation. Biases in a fixed compact interval admit an adapted proof; arbitrary different biases require a new analytic service. One-hot color constraints violate the product interface and are not obtained by relabeling the theorem.
* The influence law charges no method for computing `f` or its influences. Exact pivotal probabilities can require an exponential sum over configurations. The theorem is structural, not an efficient oracle for discovering a threshold.
