# Cycle 3: no-go for a new plasticity mechanism; PGCC burst risk remains uncalibrated

## Decision

Neither stress-responsive ecDNA segregation nor PGCC burst repopulation is a defensible new mechanism lead on the current record. Direct primary studies already cover ecDNA selection versus active reconfiguration, stress-shifted daughter partition, giant-cell neosis, and intervention against neosis. A three-type branching model can represent PGCC formation, giant-cell death, and burst offspring exactly. Its resistance prediction is explicit, but the primary papers do not jointly estimate the rates and burst-size distribution needed to evaluate it. An equally informed branching baseline therefore subsumes the mechanism; any gain over a mean-rate diffusion or birth–death fit would come from acquiring missing event data, not from a new mathematical prediction.

This is a decisive no-go for novelty and for numerical tumor-risk claims from currently reported measurements. The exact conditional model and a discriminating assay remain useful as an evidence boundary.

## Why ecDNA segregation is closed as a novelty candidate

The proposed bottleneck would have been: under a cytotoxic or targeted stress, cells alter ecDNA partition at mitosis, creating resistant descendants beyond what static selection of pre-existing copy-number variants predicts. That question has now been directly tested in Shibata et al., *Nature Communications* (2026), [“Plasticity of extrachromosomal DNA segregation during drug adaptation”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76566-5). They built a single-cell DNA sequencing plus barcode/clonal tracking platform covering about 11,000 cells. In COLO320DM-derived clones, they distinguished static selection from active reconfiguration under JQ1; the JQ1-surviving EC2 subclones came from higher, more heterogeneous baseline MYC/CDX2 copy distributions, which the authors interpret as active ecDNA reconfiguration. Paclitaxel reduced copy number but did not show the same reconfiguration signal in their tracking analysis. The result is clone- and drug-specific, but the core proposed novelty is already prior art.

The same physical idea is supported by Brückner et al.’s 2025 bioRxiv preprint [“Oncogene Silencing via ecDNA Micronucleation”](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.15.648906): live imaging and paired daughter-cell FISH under hydroxyurea stress, with a mixture model for random versus biased segregation, found increased asymmetry in a 50-pair daughter-cell comparison. This remains a preprint, not peer-reviewed evidence. Earlier peer-reviewed work already modeled random ecDNA inheritance and selection under treatment ([Lange et al., *Nature Genetics* (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01177-x)) and showed coordinated, asymmetric co-inheritance of interacting ecDNA species ([*Nature* (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07861-8)). A new model that merely adds a stress-dependent partition parameter would duplicate an active literature lane.

A precise form of that model is easy to write. If a parent carries n ecDNA copies, a baseline random-partition model has daughter count X distributed as Binomial(n, 1/2). A stress-dependent model may use

\[
X\mid n,z \sim (1-q_z)\operatorname{Binomial}(n,1/2)
+q_z\operatorname{BetaBinomial}(n,a_z,b_z),
\]

where z is the stress state and q_z,a_z,b_z control a clustered or biased partition component; the sister receives n-X copies. For the beta-binomial component, the conditional variance is np(1-p)[1+(n-1)rho], with p=a_z/(a_z+b_z) and rho=1/(a_z+b_z+1). Each daughter’s growth and survival can depend on its ecDNA count, drug, and heritable barcode. The discriminating observable is the daughter-pair partition distribution conditioned on parent copy count and clone identity, measured immediately at cytokinesis, before differential survival can alter the population. Pure selection predicts no stress shift in that event-level conditional distribution; active reconfiguration predicts a shift in p, rho, or q. Bulk post-treatment copy number alone cannot separate partition change from selection.

This is a testable model, but not a new proposal: both the 2026 drug-adaptation paper and the replication-stress micronucleation work already measure or model the relevant family of conditional daughter distributions. No patient-level stress transition rate or transportable treatment rule follows from those cell-line results.

## Strongest remaining quantitative object: a PGCC burst branching model

For a radiation or chemotherapy pulse, use three cell types: ordinary cells S, therapy-induced polyploid giant cells G, and post-burst clonogenic descendants R. The pulse produces n_G candidate G cells; alternatively model their formation from S with a stress-dependent transition hazard and condition on its observed count. S and R have ordinary birth/death rates. A G cell has two competing first events: death at rate δ, or a neosis burst at rate β. At a burst, G is removed and produces K clonogenic R daughters, with probability generating function \(h(s)=E[s^K]\). After treatment, each R daughter follows a linear birth–death process with rates \(b_R,d_R\).

Assumptions: cells reproduce independently conditional on type; rates are constant within a treatment phase; a burst is instantaneous; K counts viable clonogenic daughters, not merely visible buds; and there is no density dependence or further G re-entry. Under continued post-treatment conditions, one R lineage has eventual extinction probability

\[
q_R=\begin{cases}
1,&b_R\le d_R,\\
d_R/b_R,&b_R>d_R.
\end{cases}
\]

Conditioning on which G event occurs first gives the exact probability that one G cell seeds at least one lineage that never goes extinct:

\[
p_G=\frac{\beta}{\beta+\delta}\left[1-h(q_R)\right].
\]

For n_G independent G cells, the probability of at least one established lineage is \(1-(1-p_G)^{n_G}\). If stress creates \(N_G\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)\) giant cells, it is \(1-\exp(-\Lambda p_G)\). This is a tumor resistance probability within the model, not a clinical estimate.

The offspring law matters beyond the mean. As an exact counterexample, set \(q_R=1/2\), \(\beta/(\beta+\delta)=1/2\), and compare two burst laws with the same mean K=4:

- K=4 always: \(h(q_R)=1/16\), so \(p_G=0.46875\).
- K=0 with probability 3/4 and K=16 with probability 1/4: \(h(q_R)=3/4+1/(4\cdot 2^{16})\), so \(p_G=0.1249981\).

Both have the same expected burst output and the same death/burst rates, yet one PGCC’s establishment probability differs by a factor of about 3.75. K=0 means a visible or attempted neosis event yields no viable clonogenic progeny. That is biologically relevant because most therapy-induced PGCCs die and not every observed bud becomes a colony. The numbers above are an identifiability construction, not rates inferred from a paper.

A diffusion or ordinary birth–death approximation using only the same mean output cannot recover this probability. A branching baseline given the complete offspring generating function h, the PGCC transition/death rates, and resistant-daughter birth/death rates recovers it exactly. The mechanism therefore does not survive an equally informed branching baseline; the useful work is measuring the missing quantities.

## What the primary papers establish—and leave unknown

Zhang et al., *Molecular Oncology* (2021), [“Irradiation-induced polyploid giant cancer cells are involved in tumor cell repopulation via neosis”](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.12913), followed irradiated 4T1 cells and tumors. After 10 Gy, time-lapse imaging was collected every two hours for five days, and sorted/single PGCCs were followed by cloning assays. They observed PGCCs die as well as produce Raju-like daughter cells by neosis; the tumor colony repopulated by about day 15. The report describes a PGCC fraction peaking around day 5, and dose-dependent tradeoffs: at day 3, reported PGCC fractions were 47.8% after 14 Gy versus 16.1% after 6 Gy, while day-15 PGCC fractions were 10.6% versus 23.1%, respectively. Those observations support a hidden G state with competing death and repopulation, but do not provide a joint, lineage-linked estimate of β, δ, the clonogenic K distribution, the post-treatment q_R, and the probability a stress-surviving ordinary cell enters G.

Mahaddalkar et al., *Oncogene* (2025), [“Aurora Kinase A and B inhibition abrogates ‘Neosis’, a non-mitotic cell division of GBM residual cells and prevents GBM recurrence”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-025-03372-6), used live-cell imaging in radiation-resistant glioblastoma models and reported MNGC daughter cells emerging by cytoplasmic pinching. Pharmacological and genetic Aurora A/B inhibition reduced neosis and recurrence in vitro and in an orthotopic mouse model. This is strong mechanism and intervention prior art. It still does not supply the full burst offspring law required by the extinction formula, and the perturbation also affects parental mitosis, so recurrence suppression is not by itself a selective estimate of the PGCC-specific causal contribution.

For ecDNA, Shibata et al. (2026) is a direct prior-art stop on the selection-versus-reconfiguration question. Brückner et al. (2025, preprint) report that replication stress changes ecDNA partition asymmetry in daughter pairs and supply a random-versus-biased segregation model. Lange et al. (2022) modeled stochastic ecDNA inheritance and selection under stress, while Hung et al. (2024) showed coordinated inheritance of coexisting ecDNA species and modeled copy-number changes under targeted treatment. A new generic stress-dependent segregation model or “hidden ecDNA state” would not clear this prior-art boundary.

## Matched assay that would identify the missing PGCC rates

If the scientific goal is to estimate the PGCC contribution rather than claim novelty, run one fixed radiation regimen in a pre-registered cell line or patient-derived model and keep density, culture medium, imaging, and recovery time matched. Barcode the parental pool before irradiation. In the treated arm, isolate single PGCCs at the empirically selected post-treatment time; in parallel isolate ordinary diploid survivors from the same treated culture, and include sham-irradiated parental cells. Track individual mothers continuously through death or neosis, record event latency and every daughter, and retain barcode identity through clonogenic expansion and rechallenge. Use an orthogonal ploidy marker and blinded morphology scoring so cell fusion, cell clumps, and senescent cells are not mislabeled as PGCCs.

Estimate separately: (i) the stress-induced count or formation intensity \(\Lambda\); (ii) the competing time-to-death and time-to-burst rates δ and β; (iii) the distribution of clonogenic K per burst, not just mean buds; (iv) post-treatment b_R and d_R from the daughter lineages; and (v) the corresponding values for sorted diploid survivors. If perturbing HMGB1 or Aurora kinases, include a matched perturbation control that measures effects on ordinary parental-cell division and radiation killing. The prior Aurora result targets neosis-associated kinases but also suppresses parent mitosis, so this control is essential.

The quantitative prediction is then \(p_G=\beta[1-h(q_R)]/(\beta+\delta)\), and the tumor-level risk is \(1-\exp(-\Lambda p_G)\) under the stated Poisson formation model. For ecDNA, falsify the stress-reconfiguration component if event-level sister-pair copy distributions show no conditional shift after matching parent copy number and barcode, while selection alone explains the later population change. Falsify the PGCC contribution in this model if the lineage assay yields no clonogenic burst events (β=0 or \(h(q_R)=1\)), or if the observed number of established lineages after conditioning on n_G falls outside the pre-registered binomial prediction. A residual association between polyploid morphology and recurrence without observed parent-to-daughter lineage continuity is insufficient.

## Unknown-rate boundary and final status

The crucial unknowns are not small corrections. The establishment probability is nonlinear in h and q_R, and stress dose changes both PGCC abundance and survival. Existing studies show that giant cells can die, make progeny, and contribute to repopulation in selected systems; they do not establish a transportable estimate of stress-induced PGCC formation, first-event hazards, clonogenic burst size, daughter resistance probability, or tumor-scale independence. The ecDNA papers likewise remain context-specific to cell lines, drugs, and ecDNA amplicons; a 2026 clonal-tracking result should not be converted into a patient treatment schedule.

**Final status: no strong candidate for a new mechanism or quantitative tumor prediction.** PGCC burst branching is the cleanest conditional model and assay target, but its rates remain unknown and the biological mechanism is already prior art. Stress-dependent ecDNA segregation is even more decisively covered by the 2026 direct selection-versus-reconfiguration study. Any numerical resistance risk beyond the symbolic formula above would manufacture parameters. This line should stop as a novelty claim unless a new dataset supplies lineage-linked event rates and an application domain not already covered.
