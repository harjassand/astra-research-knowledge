LEAD INTERFACE: THE EXACT JOINT ROOF IS A SEPARABLE, FIXED-MARGINAL OBJECT

For a finite Hermitian family H=(H_1,...,H_N), define

  I(rho;H) = min_{rho=sum_y p_y pi_y} sum_y p_y sum_j Var_{pi_y}(H_j),
  K(rho;H) = max_{rho=sum_y p_y pi_y} sum_y p_y sum_j Tr(pi_y H_j)^2,

where each pi_y is pure. Since the ensemble barycenter fixes the second-moment sum,

  I(rho;H) = Tr[rho sum_j H_j^2] - K(rho;H).

At rho=tau=I/d and H_j=A_j, this is exactly I(tau;A)=V(Q)-k(Q) in the supplied gate. This identity is about one common ensemble for the entire frame.

Tóth–Pitrik, arXiv:2605.03022v1, SM2.3 Eq. (S17) and SM2.6 Eq. (S28), with proof Eq. (S31), identify K(rho;H) with the two-body correlation support over separable states whose two marginals are each fixed to rho:

  K(rho;H) = max_{omega in SymSep_2(rho)} sum_j Tr[(H_j tensor H_j) omega]
           = max_{omega in Sep_2(rho)} sum_j Tr[(H_j tensor H_j) omega].

The equality of the symmetric-separable and general-separable supports is exact; symmetrizing a feasible separable coupling preserves the objective. No entangled state or general two-extendible Choi state is covered by this reduction. Main-text Table 1(b) gives, for N>=2, the bound K(rho;H) <= sum_j Tr(rho H_j^2) - (1/4)sum_j F_Q[rho,H_j]. The statement has no qubit restriction, but at rho=tau every single-observable QFI vanishes, so this reduces to K<=V and gives no improvement over the trivial support bound.

The 2023 Tóth–Pitrik paper gives the quantum-optimal-transport interpretation: its separable GMPC self-distance obeys D_GMPC,sep(rho,rho)^2=I(rho;H), an optimization over separable couplings / common decompositions (Sec. 5, around lines 614–624). The same paper states the all-N inequality

  I(rho;H) >= (1/4) sum_j F_Q[rho,H_j]                 (Eq. (16)),

and the single-observable formula

  F_Q[rho,H] = 4 min_{rho=sum_y p_y pi_y} sum_y p_y Var_{pi_y}(H) (Eq. (13)).

Yu independently proves the latter as an exact convex-roof theorem for one Hermitian H in arbitrary finite dimension (arXiv:1302.5311v1, Eq. (4)). The common-decomposition inequality is in the direction >=: a single ensemble must serve every H_j, whereas each term's QFI minimum may use a different ensemble. At tau, F_Q[tau,A_j]=0 for every A_j because tau commutes with A_j. Thus the QFI sum does not bound the joint roof away from zero.

Exact check: for a qubit with H=(sigma_x,sigma_y), V=2, each QFI at tau=I/2 is zero, and I(tau;H)=k(tau;H)=1. For every pure Bloch vector r, r_x^2+r_y^2<=1; the equal mixture of the +x and -x states has barycenter tau and attains 1. This rules out substituting the sum of single-observable QFIs for the joint roof.

The fixed-marginal constraint is material. For d=3, A=diag(2,-1,-1), tau=I/3, V=2 and the fixed-barycenter support k(tau;A)=2: the eigenbasis ensemble attains 2, and nonnegative pure-state variance gives the matching upper bound. The unconstrained pure-product score max_{pi,pi'} Tr(pi A)Tr(pi' A) is 4. Thus an unconstrained product optimization cannot be substituted for the common tracial-marginal support.

At tau its skew-information term is zero, so it supplies only the per-observable value V. It does not bound a general frame against a legal two-output Choi state.

TWO-EXTENDIBILITY: EXACT MONOGAMY THEOREM, BUT ONLY ON A SYMMETRIC FAMILY

Johnson–Viola, arXiv:1305.1342v3, Definition II.4 (Eq. (3)) defines 1-n sharability through an n-output extension with every central-to-output marginal equal to the given bipartite state. Theorem III.6, Eq. (25), characterizes when n isotropic marginals with parameters Phi^+_{0,j} are jointly sharable, for arbitrary local dimension d:

  sum_{j=1}^n Phi^+_{0,j} <= (d-1) + (1/(n+d-1))(sum_{j=1}^n sqrt(Phi^+_{0,j}))^2.

For equal parameters this is Theorem III.8: Phi^+ <= 1+(d-1)/n. In particular, the exact 1-to-2 isotropic threshold is Phi^+ <= (d+1)/2. The parameter range 1<Phi^+<=(d+1)/2 is nonempty for every d>=2 and consists of entangled yet two-shareable isotropic states (their Eq. (6) gives positive concurrence for Phi^+>1). After normalized Choi correspondence these are depolarizing-channel examples with tracial marginals and a real, Hilbert–Schmidt-self-adjoint channel; symmetrizing the two outputs gives a legal extension. So two-extendibility is strictly weaker than separability even inside a relevant legal Choi subclass.

This theorem does not give the required arbitrary-frame score bound: isotropic symmetry reduces the state to a single singlet-fraction parameter. It shows why the separable support identity S28 cannot simply be applied to R_AB. It neither proves nor refutes G3.

SIMULTANEOUS MEASUREMENT ERRORS: ALL FINITE FAMILIES, STATE-DEPENDENT OBJECTIVE

Chen–Wang–Yuan, arXiv:2310.11925v2 (2024), Eq. (8), treats one POVM approximating any finite family X_1,...,X_n on a specified input state rho, using a commuting dilation and state-dependent squared errors epsilon_j^2. On its stated nonsingular S_Re domain it gives

  Tr(S_Re^{-1} Q_Re) >= (sqrt(||S_Re^{-1/2} S_tilde,Im S_Re^{-1/2}||_F + 1)-1)^2.

Here Q_Re contains the measurement-dependent errors, while S_Re and S_tilde,Im depend on the observables and the chosen rho (Eq. (8), lines 104–107; the error definition is Eq. (1), lines 62–66). This is an exact all-n measurement-error tradeoff, not a dimension-free upper bound on a channel's broadcast correlations. The theorem constrains measurement error for a selected state and frame; it does not quantify over all positive frame operators and legal two-output Choi extensions, and no conversion to k(Q) or G3 is supplied.

DECISIVE MISSING LEMMA

The exact unresolved import is the supplied G3 itself, a fixed-marginal common-frame monogamy inequality. For every finite d, every positive frame Q=sum_i |A_i><A_i| on traceless Hermitians, and every normalized symmetric tripartite Choi state R with all one-body marginals tau, with R_AB=R_AC the Choi marginal of a unital Hilbert–Schmidt-self-adjoint CPTP channel, prove

  Tr[R W(Q)] <= V(Q)+k(Q),
  W(Q)=sum_i A_i^T tensor (A_i^B+A_i^C).

By the supplied Choi identity this is 2 s_Phi(Q)<=V(Q)+k(Q), equivalent to the sharp C=2 gate. Existing QFI, separable-transport, isotropic-shareability, and state-dependent measurement-error theorems above stop at one of its boundaries: individual observables, separable couplings, isotropic symmetry, or a selected measurement state. None supplies this lemma.
