CYCLE 2 — CAUSAL IDENTIFIABILITY OF CONTROLLED PREDICTIVE STATE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: Exact population theorem proved under the stated finite-state and action-prefix-support assumptions. A constructive raw-trajectory design, finite-sample rank certificate, cost accounting, and nonidentifiability boundaries are given. No claim of minimax-optimal query count, physical hidden-parameter recovery, or external validation.

1. RESULT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

For a K-state partially observed process with unknown reset distribution, unknown action-dependent transition kernels, and an action-independent observation kernel, raw trajectories under chosen open-loop action sequences identify the minimal observable predictive dimension and its action-observation operators up to similarity, provided the action schedules cover a finite complete prefix tree through length 2K-1. The latent state is never set or read: all probabilities average over the reset law and the posterior induced by each observed history. The exact rank is the rank of the infinite history-test Hankel matrix, not necessarily K. It equals K exactly when both the reset-reachable row space and the future-observation column space have dimension K. The exhaustive support guarantee is exponential and is not asserted to be query-minimal. If a needed action is never legally applied, or the observation kernels erase state distinctions, physical action effects may be nonidentifiable even with unlimited trajectories.

2. INTERFACE, CAUSAL LAW, AND COST UNITS

Fix finite hidden states S={1,...,K}, actions A (m=|A|), and observations O (q=|O|). The unknown initial/reset law is a row vector rho on S. Every independent episode starts from the same law rho; reset returns to this distribution, not to a chosen latent state. At step t the learner chooses action a_t, then S_t is drawn from row-stochastic T_{a_t}(S_t|S_{t-1}), then O_t is drawn from E(o_t|S_t). E is action-independent; action affects only T_a. Neither S_t nor rho,T_a,E are supplied. An episode records only its chosen actions and raw observations. A legal intervention is an open-loop finite action sequence, or an adaptive policy whose actions are selected from the same legal interface. The theorem below uses open-loop schedules.

Write D_o=diag(E(o|1),...,E(o|K)), M_(a,o)=T_a D_o, and let X=A×O. A word w=x_1...x_n in X* has action projection act(w) and observation projection obs(w). Define

    p(w) = Pr[O_1...O_n=obs(w) | do(A_1...A_n=act(w))]
         = rho M_(a_1,o_1)...M_(a_n,o_n) 1,       p(empty)=1.

This is a causal interventional law, not a conditional probability given an action sequence selected by a hidden-state-dependent behavior policy. For p(u)>0, the predictive state after the raw history u has coordinates p(uv)/p(u); its underlying hidden posterior, if the model were known, would be rho M_u/(rho M_u 1). The data collector neither observes nor queries that posterior. It is integrated out in every measured p(w).

The conventional conditional history-test entry p(v|u)=p(uv)/p(u) is obtained by dividing row u of H by p(u). Thus, after zero-probability histories are removed, this joint-probability Hankel matrix has the same rank as the conditional PSR prediction matrix.

Costs are charged per episode reset c_R, per selected action c_A(a), and per measured observation c_O. An episode executing a_1...a_n costs c_R + sum_t c_A(a_t) + n c_O. The same accounting is used for active and passive comparisons below. If episodes do not reset independently to a common law, the sampling theorem does not apply without an additional dependence/mixing condition.

3. FINITE HANKEL THEOREM

Let H[u,v]=p(uv), with rows and columns indexed by X*. Let r=rank(H), the minimal real-linear predictive dimension. Suppose the hidden-state cap K is known and all action words in A^* are legal. For W={w in X*: |w|<=K-1}, let H_W=H[W,W]. For each symbol x in X, define the shift block H_x[u,v]=p(uxv), u,v in W. Then:

(i) rank(H_W)=rank(H)=r<=K.
(ii) The raw interventional probabilities p(w) for all |w|<=2K-1 determine r and a minimal r-dimensional linear realization of the entire controlled observable law, up to similarity. H_W uses words of length at most 2K-2; the shifts H_x use length at most 2K-1.
(iii) If B,C subset W each have size r and H_BC is nonsingular, then in row-coordinate convention the realization is recovered by

    H = [p(bc)]_(b in B,c in C),
    h = [p(c)]_(c in C),       f = [p(b)]_(b in B),
    H_x = [p(bxc)]_(b in B,c in C),
    alpha = h H^(-1),          A_x = H_x H^(-1),          beta = f,

and p(x_1...x_n)=alpha A_(x_1)...A_(x_n) beta for every n. Any two minimal r-dimensional realizations of the same interventional word law are related by one invertible similarity transform.
(iv) The physical hidden-state count and kernels are recovered by this predictive realization only if additional assumptions make their stochastic factorization identifiable. In particular, r=K iff both spans

    R = span{rho M_u : u in X*},
    V = span{M_v 1 : v in X*}

have dimension K. More generally,

    r = dim(R) - dim(R intersect V^perp).

The rank can therefore be strictly smaller than K because some latent states are unreachable from rho or because future observations do not separate them. Full-column-rank emission matrix E together with one legal invertible T_a is a sufficient condition for C=R^K, since the one-step future columns T_a E(:,o) then span R^K. It is not necessary: longer action-observation tests can distinguish states even when E is rank-deficient. Full latent-state rank also requires R=R^K.

Proof. Use the K-dimensional representation p(w)=rho M_w 1. Its infinite Hankel factors into rows rho M_u and columns M_v 1, so rank(H)<=K. The unobservable row subspace N={z: z M_v 1=0 for every v} is invariant under right multiplication by every M_x: if z is in N, then z M_x M_v 1=0 for every v. Restrict to the reachable rows and quotient by their intersection with N. This gives a reachable and observable representation of dimension rank(H)=r. In a minimal representation, the spans of rows of length at most j and columns of length at most j start at dimension one. Each strict enlargement raises dimension by at least one; once a span stops growing it is invariant under every symbol operator and cannot grow at a later length. Thus both spans are complete by j=r-1<=K-1. Their product H_W has rank r, proving (i).

For (iii), let R_B have basis rows indexed by B and C_C have basis columns indexed by C, so H=R_B C_C. The extension rows satisfy R_B A_x = A_x^(B) R_B for a unique coordinate matrix A_x^(B); multiplying by C_C gives H_x=A_x^(B) H. Therefore A_x^(B)=H_x H^(-1). The initial row coordinates are h H^(-1), and the terminal column is f. This yields the displayed factorization. For uniqueness, map each reachable history row of one minimal realization to the same history row in the other. A linear combination maps to zero in the first exactly when it annihilates every future-test column, which is equivalent to the same zero Hankel row in the second. The map is therefore well-defined, invertible by symmetry, and intertwines every symbol operator. This is similarity. Finally, the quotient formula for r is the rank of the row-to-future-functional map restricted to R.

4. LEGAL ACTION SUPPORT AND THE EXACT BASIS CONDITION

The target action set must be named. With unrestricted legal sequences the theorem identifies all A_a effects on the observable process. If only a subset A_0 is legal, the theorem applies to the process restricted to A_0^* and its predictive rank; it makes no claim about an excluded action.

For a particular proposed basis B,C, the exact criterion for this finite Hankel basis certificate is: (a) B and C span the full reachable-observable quotient, equivalently rank(H_W)=rank(H_BC)=r under the supplied cap horizon; (b) H_BC is nonsingular; and (c) the legal raw-trajectory interface supplies h, f, H, and every H_x block. Operationally, condition (c) means it can execute the action projections of b, c, bc, and bxc for every b in B, c in C, and symbol x=(a,o) in A_0×O, and measure their observation-sequence probabilities. Together these conditions are necessary and sufficient for the displayed formula to return a minimal r-dimensional realization and every target symbol operator in the selected basis coordinates. Nonsingularity is necessary for B,C to be coordinate bases. If an H_x block is not known, that action-observation operator is not obtained by this certificate. The short schedule set Q(B,C) alone presupposes completeness of the proposed basis; absent that promise, use the full cap-based table to certify it. Other designs can exploit additional model restrictions and may need fewer schedules.

For an arbitrary legal action language L rather than A_0^*, use only target bases whose required action projections are in L. To identify the whole target law through horizon 2K-1 by the exhaustive construction, every target action word of length at most 2K-1 must be a prefix of a legal sampled schedule. If a history-dependent legal interface forbids some basis extension bxc, the corresponding transition effect is outside this certificate. Support alone does not guarantee good conditioning or finite-sample rank recovery; the observable basis block must also be nonsingular with a quantitative margin.

Observation-kernel criterion. The exact condition for K hidden directions to be observable is span{M_v 1 : v in X*}=R^K. The exact condition for K hidden directions to be reached from the reset is span{rho M_u : u in X*}=R^K. These combine to r=K. When either fails, the data may still identify the smaller rank-r predictive quotient, but cannot identify the redundant/unreached physical coordinates. These conditions are on the full action-observation kernels T_a D_o and the reset law, not merely on the one-step matrix rank of E.

5. RAW TRAJECTORY CONSTRUCTION AND QUERY COST

No hidden-state clamp, conditional-history sampler, teacher, or posterior query is used. Let L=2K-1. For every action schedule a_1...a_L in A^L, collect n independent episodes after ordinary reset, execute exactly that schedule, and record all L observations. Each trajectory gives every observation-prefix indicator. For every shorter action word s of length j<L, select any legal length-L extension s a_{j+1}...a_L; the first j observations of its episodes have exactly the interventional law for s, by the causal transition interface. Thus all p(w), |w|<=L, can be estimated from raw events. A full length-L schedule also supplies a single experiment opportunity for every one of its prefixes. This exhaustive full-depth design uses

    N_episode = n m^L,
    N_reset   = n m^L,
    N_action  = n L m^L,
    N_measure = n L m^L.

For action-dependent prices its total cost is

    C = n sum_(a_1...a_L in A^L)
            [c_R + sum_(t=1)^L (c_A(a_t)+c_O)].

With uniform action and observation prices this is n m^L [c_R+L(c_A+c_O)]. A shorter basis-focused design needs only the distinct action schedules

    Q(B,C)={act(b), act(c), act(bc), act(bxc): b in B, c in C, x=(a,o) in A_0×O},

with the required replicate counts. Its exact schedule support is |Q(B,C)| for this reconstruction, but it presupposes that a basis has already been found; it is not a teacher-free discovery complexity bound or a minimax lower bound. The full-depth design is a transparent universal upper bound. No polynomial-in-K guarantee is claimed.

For a finite-sample rank certificate, set d=|A||O| and n_H=sum_(j=0)^(K-1) d^j. There are M=sum_(j=0)^(2K-1) d^j word probabilities in the finite table. Assume a supplied rank-separation margin gamma>0 such that, for the unknown true rank r, sigma_r(H_W)>=gamma and sigma_(r+1)(H_W)=0. Estimate every p(w) from n independent raw trajectories under one legal extension of act(w); for each schedule/word pair the empirical event indicator is Bernoulli. Hoeffding and a union bound imply

    Pr[max_|w|<=L |p_hat(w)-p(w)| > tau]
       <= 2 M exp(-2 n tau^2).

With tau=gamma/(4 n_H), it suffices that

    n >= 8 n_H^2 gamma^(-2) log(2M/delta)

to recover rank by thresholding singular values of H_W at gamma/2 with probability at least 1-delta. The constant follows from 2 n tau^2 = n gamma^2/(8 n_H^2). This is an intentionally simple, exponential, uniform event bound. For a chosen basis block, if sigma_min(H_BC)>=kappa and its estimated block error is <=kappa/2, inversion is well-defined; coordinate errors grow with 1/kappa and 1/kappa^2. Exact rank cannot be certified uniformly from finite samples without a rank cap and nonzero-singular-value margin. Small p(u) additionally makes normalized P(v|u)=p(uv)/p(u) unstable; a lower bound on p(u) is required for that conditional error. No guarantee is made for long-horizon prediction error from noisy recovered matrices without further stability assumptions.

Minimality boundary. The support size |Q(B,C)| is the schedule cost of the stated basis formula once B,C are known. The exhaustive count m^(2K-1) is a sufficient worst-case coverage bound, not a proven minimum over adaptive algorithms. At least every target action whose operator is claimed must occur in some legal experiment: if action a is never selected, changing T_a leaves the entire transcript unchanged. A precise minimax number of schedules for discovering an unknown basis is not established here.

6. MATCHED PASSIVE BASELINE

An exogenous randomized passive assignment that chooses each action independently of the hidden state and records it has, conditional on a realized action schedule a_1...a_L, exactly the same observation law as do(A_1...A_L=a_1...a_L). If such assignment allocates the same number n of raw trajectories to every length-L schedule as the intervention design, the empirical Hankel table has exactly the same sampling distribution and uncertainty bounds. The interventions therefore have no information advantage over a passive randomized design with matched support and allocation. The active design can avoid wasting a fixed N on schedules outside a discovered basis, while iid passive assignment spends an expected N/m^L paths per length-L schedule under uniform actions; that is an allocation advantage over that particular passive policy, not a minimax separation. With same per-action/reset/measurement costs, charge both designs by the same formula. If passive actions are not randomized exogenously, conditioning on an action string may select on earlier observations and is not automatically an interventional law. A behavior policy with zero probability on a necessary schedule is not a matched-support comparator.

For a single-action process, there is no active action choice; the statement reduces to ordinary raw-sequence Hankel/PSR identification. Passive spectral HMM learning can estimate predictive laws under its own separation assumptions, but that fact does not identify kernels for action sequences absent from the passive support.

7. FAILURE WITNESSES AND UNKNOWN

Missing action. Let S={0,1}, rho=(1/2,1/2), and let each state emit through the same binary symmetric channel with 0<eta<1/2. Let the only legal observed action be a with T_a=I in both systems. Let b be a target but excluded action. In model P, T_b=I; in model Q, T_b=Flip. Every legal sequence of a produces identical raw observation laws in P and Q. Under do(b,b), however, the two observation bits have correlation +(1-2eta)^2 in P and -(1-2eta)^2 in Q. No number of legal a-trajectories identifies the b-effect. Here the observation kernel is informative, but action support fails.

Unobservable transition structure. Let every state emit the same Bernoulli(1/2) distribution for every action. Then every observation word has probability 2^(-|w|) under every action schedule, regardless of T_a. The predictive Hankel rank is exactly one, while arbitrary hidden transition matrices yield the same data. This is not a failure to identify predictive rank; it is the correct rank-one observable quotient and a counterexample to recovering physical latent transitions without an observation-separation condition.

Finite plateau. James and Singh give a deterministic five-core-history/test example for which two successive truncated history-test matrices both have rank one although the true system-dynamics rank is five. Thus “rank has stopped increasing on the currently inspected block” is not a sound stopping rule without a supplied finite-state cap or another completeness certificate. Our 2K-1 horizon uses the known K cap and span-stabilization proof; if K is unknown, a finite observed block only gives a rank lower bound and global rank is UNKNOWN.

Other boundaries. If no independent reset is available, if the reset law changes across episodes, if a required basis history is impossible, if action choice depends on hidden state without exogenous randomization, or if the rank/basis singular-value margin is below sampling resolution, the corresponding theorem clause is unavailable. Report UNKNOWN rather than inferring absence of additional predictive rank from finite empirical rank.

8. SOURCE RECONSTRUCTION AND CLAIM STATUS

Primary-source reconstruction:

• Singh, James, and Rudary define the controlled system-dynamics/history-test matrix using probabilities of future action-observation tests given past action-observation histories, identify its rank as linear predictive dimension, and derive finite predictive-state representations from a basis of independent rows/columns. This motivates the target object, not the intervention coverage or sample bound proved here: https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~isbell/reading/papers/uai2004psr.pdf
• James and Singh define finite controlled action/observation histories, discuss reset-based raw access, and explicitly show a rank-stabilization counterexample whose true core size is five. Their reset sampler may have to repeat histories until a particular observation sequence occurs; our construction instead estimates unconditional word probabilities from forced action schedules and uses a known K horizon. https://icml.cc/Conferences/2004/proceedings/papers/117.pdf
• Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang prove a passive raw-HMM spectral learner under a rank/singular-value separation condition. This is a passive single-action predictive-learning comparison, not a theorem for causal identification of action-indexed kernels: https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413

New result here: the controlled finite-state quotient characterization, legal basis-extension condition, raw open-loop intervention schedule, finite 2K-1 Hankel witness, similarity reconstruction, and explicit action-support/observation-kernel failures. The rank/basis proof is internal and elementary; it has not received external review. The finite-sample bound is conservative and exponential in K and alphabet size. No novelty or priority claim is made.
