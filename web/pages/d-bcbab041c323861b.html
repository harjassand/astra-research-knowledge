1. INTERFACE

H is separable, possibly infinite dimensional. States are positive trace-
class operators of trace one, hence normal states on B(H). E is any
nonempty family. All channels are bounded CP maps on trace class, TP;
equivalently their Heisenberg adjoints are normal unital CP maps.
EB means separability of the output against EVERY ancilla, with separability
the trace-norm closed convex hull of product normal states. A general
infinite-dimensional EB map must not be assumed to have a countable
measure/prepare representation.

For a finite prior p with barycenter rho_bar define
 chi(p)=sum_i p_i D(rho_i||rho_bar), C=sup_finite_priors chi(p)<infinity.
This uses relative entropy in nats, not an undefined difference of infinite
entropies. Let T=half trace norm. b is the infimum worst two-output marginal
error of ONE normal full-domain CPTP broadcaster. e is the infimum worst
error of ONE normal full-domain EB channel. The supplied finite-dimensional
theorem is e<=g_C(b), with
 g_C(0)=0, g_C(1)=1,
 g_C(x)=min{1,400sqrt[(C+1)/ln(1/x)]}, 0<x<1.

The independently obtained extension is e<=g_C(b) with exactly the same
function. Moreover, for every eta>0 there is a finite-outcome normal EB map
of error <=g_C(b)+eta, and the infimum over finite-outcome normal EB maps
equals e. The broad normal EB infimum is attained by the separate lemma.
At b=0 a countable projective measure/prepare channel fixes E exactly.

2. STANDARD RELATIVE-ENTROPY FACTS USED

Normal-state relative entropy is nonnegative, lower semicontinuous in both
trace-norm arguments, monotone under normal CPTP maps, and obeys Pinsker
 D(rho||tau)>=(1/2)||rho-tau||_1^2=2T(rho,tau)^2.
For a finite prior, Donald's identity is
 sum_i p_i D(rho_i||tau)=chi(p)+D(rho_bar||tau).
It is valid with extended nonnegative values. In every ensemble mixing
identity used below, the comparison state dominates each component by its
positive prior weight, so all individual quantities are finite. No infinity
minus infinity occurs. These standard identities are explicit in Shirokov's
primary paper, quant-ph/0510073v2, Preliminaries, PDF pp4--6. They are the
infinite-dimensional information-theory inputs to the derivation below.

3. A NORMAL COMMON CENTER AND UNIFORM TIGHTNESS

Choose finite ensembles with chi_n->C and means sigma_n. Mixing two with
equal weights and applying Donald plus Pinsker gives
 C>=(chi_n+chi_m)/2+(1/8)||sigma_n-sigma_m||_1^2.
Thus sigma_n is trace-Cauchy. Its limit sigma is a NORMAL density matrix,
since trace-class states are trace-norm complete. No maximizing prior,
finite-atom optimal measure, compactness of E, or entropy continuity is
assumed to obtain this center.

For arbitrary rho in E and 0<t<1, mix rho into the n-th ensemble. Its mean
sigma_(n,t)=(1-t)sigma_n+t rho satisfies
 t D(rho||sigma_(n,t))<=C-(1-t)chi_n.
First let n increase, using lower semicontinuity; then let t decrease to
zero. This proves D(rho||sigma)<=C for EVERY rho. In particular all states
are supported on P=supp sigma. Sigma lies in the trace closure of conv(E).
This independently reconstructs the needed portion of Shirokov's optimal
average theorem; it is established prior mathematics.

Let P_N be finite-rank spectral projections increasing to P, and write
 q_N=Tr[(I-P_N)sigma]->0,
 p_N(rho)=Tr[(I-P_N)rho].
The binary measurement {P_N,I-P_N} and the binary entropy bound give
 C>=D_bin(p_N(rho)||q_N)>=p_N(rho)ln(1/q_N)-ln 2.
Hence, when q_N>0,
 sup_E p_N(rho)<=(C+ln 2)/ln(1/q_N)->0.
If q_N=0, support inclusion gives p_N=0. Kernel directions outside P cause
no problem. This is a family-dependent spectral cutoff with dimension-free
error control; finite C does not give a uniform cutoff rank or spectral
rate depending only on C.

For completeness, the cutoff bound also proves relative trace compactness
without an imported compactness criterion: finite-dimensional compressed
families are totally bounded, and the uniform compression error below tends
to zero. The trace-norm closure of E is therefore compact.

4. NORMAL PHYSICAL COMPRESSION AND THE SAME MODULUS

Choose any unit vector v in P_1 H and omega=|v><v|. On the full domain use
 F_N(X)=P_N X P_N+Tr[(I-P_N)X]omega,
with codomain T(P_N H), and let J_N be the isometric inclusion. These are
normal CPTP maps. The finite compression has no state-dependent action.
Writing s_N=sup_E p_N(rho), root/gentle continuity gives
 sup_E T(J_N F_N(rho),rho)<=delta_N=sqrt(s_N)+s_N/2->0.
One proof of the full estimate is
 ||rho-P_Nrho P_N||_1<=2sqrt(p_N),
then add the replacement tail of norm p_N. This does not discard output
trace and does not require a lower bound on sigma's positive eigenvalues.

E_N=F_N(E) has capacity C_N<=C by relative-entropy data processing. For an
actual normal broadcaster B of half residual r on E, define
 B_N=(F_N tensor F_N) B J_N.
This is an actual finite-dimensional CPTP broadcaster. On F_N(rho), each
margin has error <=r+delta_N, by comparing J_N F_N(rho) with rho and using
trace contraction. Put u_N=min{1,r+delta_N}.

The supplied finite theorem gives an EB Lambda_N on P_N H with common
error <=g_C(u_N), since g is increasing in capacity. Lift it as
 Lambda_N^H=J_N Lambda_N F_N.
It is normal, full-domain CPTP and EB against every ancilla. Its worst
error on E is <=delta_N+g_C(u_N). A finite-dimensional EB Choi decomposition
gives a FINITE measure/prepare realization (at most rank(P_N)^4 atoms is
a safe bound). Pullback of its effects M_j gives the bounded full-domain
POVM effects
 P_N M_j P_N+Tr(M_j omega)(I-P_N),
which sum to I. Its preparations have finite-dimensional support. Thus
normality and finite outcomes are constructive at every positive slack.

For actual residual r, let N increase. g_C is continuous on [0,1], including
the prescribed endpoints. Hence e<=g_C(r). For the broadcasting infimum,
choose witnesses r<=min{1,b+epsilon} and let epsilon decrease to zero.
This gives e<=g_C(b). The argument first proves an error-infimum inequality;
it does not assume that normal channels form a compact set. The separate
optimizer lemma below supplies an attaining normal EB map if desired.
The finite-outcome lifted maps already prove existence with arbitrary slack.

5. FINITE-OUTCOME INFIMUM VERSUS GENERAL NORMAL EB OPTIMUM

The normal EB optimizer lemma in NORMAL_EB_OPTIMIZER_LEMMA.txt proves that
the broad e is attained. It works for any family, even without finite C,
by retaining an EB trace-nonincreasing weak-star subchannel limit and
replacing its lost trace with one fixed normal preparation. It DOES NOT
assert compactness of the set of trace-preserving normal channels.

For a relatively compact E and any normal EB Lambda, Lambda(E) is also
relatively compact. Choose uniform finite input and output compressions
C_in=J_n F_n and C_out=J_m F_m with respective errors delta and xi on E
and Lambda(E). The channel C_out Lambda C_in differs from Lambda by at
most delta+xi uniformly on E. Its middle map F_m Lambda J_n has finite
input/output dimensions and is EB, so it has a finite Choi measure/prepare
decomposition. Its full-domain lift is therefore finite-outcome normal EB.
Letting delta,xi decrease proves that the finite-outcome infimum equals e.
No uniform outcome count depending only on C,b or requested accuracy is
obtained. Exact finite-outcome attainment can fail, even at b=e=0.

6. EXACT ZERO CASE WITHOUT A NONNORMAL LIMIT

If b=0, the lifted finite-outcome EB maps above have uniform error tending
to zero on E. For any rho,tau in E and ANY finite-outcome EB map with effects
M_j and preparations xi_j, trace duality and HS Cauchy--Schwarz give
 F(rho,tau)=||sqrt(rho)sqrt(tau)||_1
 <=sum_j sqrt[Tr(M_jrho)Tr(M_jtau)]
 <=A(Lambda rho,Lambda tau),
 A(alpha,beta)=Tr[sqrt(alpha)sqrt(beta)].
For the first inequality use the supremum over bounded contractions,
rather than assuming that an infinite-dimensional polar partial isometry
extends to a unitary. The second is finite-sum affinity superadditivity;
our output preparations have finite support, so finite-dimensional affinity
concavity suffices. Root continuity in HS norm extends affinity continuity
to arbitrary normal input states. Taking the error-zero limit gives F<=A,
whereas always A<=F. Thus F=A for every pair.

For a trace-class operator Z, ||Z||_1=Tr Z>=0 implies Z>=0: write
Z=U|Z| with ||U||<=1, and equality in Re Tr(U|Z|)<=Tr|Z| forces U to act
as I on supp |Z|. Applying this to Z=sqrt(rho)sqrt(tau) shows that the
roots commute, hence the states commute. This uses no finite-dimensional
unitary extension or channel compactness premise.

Every rho commutes with the normal center sigma, since sigma is a trace
limit of finite means of states in E. Every positive eigenvalue of sigma
has finite multiplicity. Simultaneously diagonalize the commuting family
in each such finite-dimensional eigenspace; their countable sum is P H.
Add any countable orthonormal basis of the common kernel. The resulting
complete basis {e_k} diagonalizes E. The channel
 Delta(X)=sum_k Tr(|e_k><e_k|X)|e_k><e_k|
is a countable, normal, full-domain CPTP measure/prepare map and fixes E
exactly. On an arbitrary ancilla its output is the trace-norm limit of
separable partial sums, hence separable. The corresponding channel preparing
|e_k><e_k| tensor |e_k><e_k| broadcasts E exactly as well. Thus zero is
attained, not merely an infimum, within the normal legal interface.

7. COUNTEREXAMPLE TO EXACT FINITE OUTCOME IMPLEMENTATION

On l2(N), let P_k=|k><k| and sigma=sum_(k>=1)2^(-k)P_k. For n>=2 set
 rho_n=sigma+(2^(-n)/2)(P_n-P_1), E={sigma,rho_2,rho_3,...}.
These are faithful diagonal normal states, rho_n<=3sigma/2, and
D(rho_n||sigma)<=ln(3/2). Donald's identity gives C(E)<=ln(3/2).
All states commute, so normal countable dephasing gives b=e=0 exactly.
The vectors rho_n-sigma are linearly independent. A finite-outcome
measure/prepare channel has finite-dimensional linear range, and therefore
cannot fix all these states. This is a legal finite-capacity counterexample
only to FINITE-OUTCOME EXACT attainment, not to the modulus or normal EB
attainment. Finite-outcome uniform approximants still exist.
