# Cycle 2 — Quantitative coupon design for disordered conductivity

## Result and scope

There is a sharp finite-correlation-length minimax design theorem for a restricted but exact model: a one-dimensional iid two-phase resistor chain observed through the complete two-terminal boundary-response map. If \(M\) independent cells are acquired, the minimax absolute-error risk for the homogenized conductivity is between constant multiples of \(D_K M^{-1/2}\), and the high-confidence sample count is \(\Theta(D_K^2\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))\) in the stated accuracy regime. Coupon width and coupon count trade exactly through their product \(M\).

This does **not** establish the requested sharp minimax law for general \(d\ge2\) materials with a supplied finite correlation length. For that class I found theorem-backed sufficient RVE designs under more specific statistical and periodization assumptions, and a sharper discrete-iid upper bound. I did not find or construct a matching estimator-independent lower bound for the full local-response interface. The first-wave random-phase stripe lower bound has infinite dependence range and is not a substitute for that missing result.

The complete derivation and arithmetic are in [cycle2_quantitative_design.md](proof/cycle2_quantitative_design.md). The numerical arithmetic audit is [check_cycle2_design_arithmetic.py](scripts/check_cycle2_design_arithmetic.py), run with `python3 work/agents/materials_energy/disorder_homogenization/scripts/check_cycle2_design_arithmetic.py`.

## Local boundary-response interface

For scalar conductivity \(a(x)\) on a cube \(Q_R=(0,R)^d\), an ideal coupon returns its complete Dirichlet-to-Neumann quadratic form
\[
q_a(g)=\langle g,\Lambda_{a,Q_R}g\rangle
       =\min_{u|_{\partial Q_R}=g}\int_{Q_R}\nabla u\cdot a\nabla u.
\]
For each macroscopic direction \(\xi\), minimizing this form over traces of \(u(x)=\xi\cdot x+v(x)\), with \(v\) periodic, gives the periodic-cell energy \(R^{-d}\xi\cdot A_R\xi\). Polarization recovers the cell tensor. Thus an exact continuum DtN map contains the periodic RVE tensor for that coefficient restriction. A finite boundary basis with \(q\) modes requires \(q\) drives and \(q\) measured flux coefficients per drive, or \(q^2\) scalar response values per coupon. Mode truncation, calibration, coefficient imaging where required, and the induced error in the minimized tensor remain charged.

This identity does not make an ordinary crop equal in law to a periodized ensemble. It is the deterministic coupon-to-cell calculation; a probabilistic upper bound still has the source-specific periodization premise. For an open discrete resistor graph, the coupon map also does not reveal conductances on artificial edges added when opposite faces are glued. A discrete periodic-RVE theorem is usable through this interface only if the experiment actually supplies the torus coupon, including its seam edges, or another justified construction of those edges.

## Exact minimax design in a 1D finite-range model

Fix cell length \(\ell>0\), contrast \(K>1\), and unknown phase probability \(p\in[1/4,3/4]\). Each cell independently has conductivity \(K\) with probability \(p\), and conductivity \(1\) otherwise. This stationary model has one-cell dependence range. Let \(\gamma=1-1/K\). The effective conductivity is the harmonic mean
\[
A(p)=\left(\mathbb E[a^{-1}]\right)^{-1}=(1-\gamma p)^{-1}.
\]

An aligned coupon of \(n\) cells returns the DtN matrix
\[
\Lambda=G\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G^{-1}=\ell(n-\gamma B),
\]
where \(B\) is the number of high-conductivity cells. The exact map therefore identifies \(B\). If resistance is measured with worst-case error below \(\gamma\ell/2\), rounding recovers \(B\) exactly. For \(N\) disjoint coupons, the sum \(H=\sum_i B_i\) has law \(\operatorname{Binomial}(M,p)\), where \(M=nN\). No coupon-map estimator has more information about \(p\) than \(M\) iid Bernoulli samples, and \(H\) attains that information.

Define the maximum slope on the admitted parameter interval by
\[
D_K=\max_{p\in[1/4,3/4]}A'(p)=\frac{\gamma}{(1-3\gamma/4)^2}.
\]
For minimax expected absolute error \(\mathcal R_M\), the elementary two-point Le Cam construction and the clipped plug-in estimator give
\[
\frac{D_K}{82\sqrt M}\le\mathcal R_M\le\frac{D_K}{2\sqrt M}.
\]
The constants are deliberately conservative. For \(0<\varepsilon\le D_K/64\) and \(0<\delta\le1/8\), Hoeffding gives the sufficient count
\[
M\ge\frac{D_K^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\log\frac2\delta,
\]
and a two-point Bretagnolle–Huber test gives the necessary threshold
\[
M>\frac{3D_K^2}{1024\varepsilon^2}\log\frac1{4\delta}.
\]
Hence the high-confidence rate matches in order. For fixed coupon width \(R=n\ell\), the width/count frontier is \(Nn\ge M_{\min}\); for fixed count \(N\), the least sufficient width under this model is \(R=\ell\lceil M_{\min}/N\rceil\). With no maximum coupon width, one width-\(M_{\min}\ell\) coupon and \(M_{\min}\) one-cell coupons use the same total sampled length. The statement is exact for the model and measurement precision above, not for arbitrary media.

At \(K=100\), \(D_K=158400/10609\approx14.9307192\). For \(\varepsilon=0.01,\delta=0.05\), the explicit sufficient count is 4,111,743 cells and the lower threshold is about 10,511 cells. These numbers show the constants in the proof are loose; the matching dependence on \(K,\varepsilon,\delta\) is the theorem-level result. The script reproduces these values and the width/count frontier; it checks arithmetic, not the proof or physical model.

## Strongest quantitative upper bounds located

**Continuous-coefficient spectral-gap and periodized-ensemble class.** Fischer and Neukamm, Definition 12–13 and Theorem 14 (published pp. 361–365), define the periodic cell estimator, require the periodic law to satisfy a scale-\(\ell\) spectral-gap condition and to agree with the original field law on central balls of radius (R/4), and note that such a periodization must be proved case by case. With (s=R/\ell\), the random RVE fluctuation has stretched-exponential tail at scale \(s^{-d/2}\). Without their added small-scale regularity assumption \(R\), the bias bound is
\[
b(s)=C_b(d,K,\rho)s^{-(d\wedge4)/2}(\log s)^{p_d},
\quad p_d=(d\wedge4)/2+1/2\ \text{for }d=2,4,
\]
with zero log exponent in other dimensions. With \(R\), the power improves to \(d\wedge4\), with log exponent \(d\wedge4+1/2\) in dimensions 2 and 4. For a single coupon, one sufficient width gate is
\[
s\ge\max\left\{s_{\rm bias}(\varepsilon),\left(\frac{4C_0}{\varepsilon}[\log(2d^2/\delta)]^{1/\nu}\right)^{2/d}\right\},\qquad b(s)\le\varepsilon/4,
\]
plus tensor-response error at most \(\varepsilon/4\). For \(N\) independent coupons, a coordinatewise median-of-means construction gives a sufficient count
\[
N=\left\lceil C\,[1+d^2C_v s^{-d}\varepsilon^{-2}]\log(d^2/\delta)\right\rceil
\]
with a universal numerical factor in \(C\), assuming independent coupons and the theorem’s variance consequence. The sufficient total imaged volume is therefore at most
\[
V\le C\ell^d(s^d+d^2C_v\varepsilon^{-2})\log(d^2/\delta).
\]
The coupon-map cost adds \(Nq^2\) response values for a \(q\)-mode boundary discretization and the per-coupon cell minimizations. Constants \(C_0,C_v,C_b,\nu\) depend on dimension, ellipticity/contrast and the spectral-gap parameter; the source does not give an optimized explicit polynomial in \(K\).

This is not a theorem for every sharp binary finite-range material. Fischer–Neukamm explicitly note that their derivative-form spectral gap does not directly cover random fields taking only discrete values; the corresponding finite-difference/oscillation gap would require an additional corrector-decay argument (published pp. 364–365). Their upper is conditional on that gap and the periodization construction. In particular, merely supplying a number called “correlation length” does not satisfy the theorem unless it comes with the required quantitative inequality and law-level periodization.

**Discrete iid network class.** Gloria, Neukamm and Otto, Theorem 2 in *Quantification of ergodicity in stochastic homogenization* (2015), prove for \(d\ge2\) and iid uniformly elliptic lattice edge conductances that the empirical average of \(N\) independent \(s\)-periodic RVE tensors obeys
\[
\left(\mathbb E|A_{s,N}^{\rm RVE}-A_{\rm hom}|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le C(d,K)\left[N^{-1/2}s^{-d/2}+s^{-d}(\log s)^d\right].
\]
The variance term is the averaging cost and the second term is the bias floor. The theorem is sharper in bias than the general rough-coefficient spectral-gap bound, but applies to an iid discrete model and the periodic-torus coupon interface. Its constants depend on ellipticity and dimension, without an optimized contrast polynomial. Median-of-means can turn the variance term into a high-confidence coupon multiplier (O(C(d,K)^2s^{-d}\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))) once the bias is below the error budget. A physical open-boundary graph does not supply the periodic seam conductances for free.

Fischer’s 2019 finite-range RVE-selection theorem is relevant prior art: it assumes stationarity, finite range and a suitable periodization, then bounds selection using supplied/acquired sample statistics and covariance-conditioning factors. It is not an estimator-independent minimax theorem. In particular, its source constructs finite-range scalar examples where the RVE tensor is uncorrelated with the spatial average, so fixing phase fraction need not reduce RVE variance.

## Why the general sharp match remains open

The first-wave two-laminate construction is stationary and ergodic but has a shared random phase at arbitrarily distant locations. Its dependence range is infinite. It proves that no uniform local-coupon guarantee follows from contrast and phase fraction alone when the correlation scale is unspecified. It does not lower-bound sample width or count in a strict finite-range class with supplied scale.

For \(d\ge2\), the missing object is an estimator-independent two-law testing construction for the same full-DtN observation. It must exhibit two laws with (i) strict dependence range at most the supplied \(L\), (ii) identical supplied phase/contrast statistics, (iii) close joint laws of \(N\) complete coupon response maps at width \(R\), and (iv) a controlled nonzero separation of the homogenized tensors. No such construction, and no lower bound matching the RVE \(\ell^d\varepsilon^{-2}\) volume scale, was established here. The upper bounds above also have unexpanded \(K\)-dependent constants and ensemble/geometry premises. Thus the requested general “least coupon width/count at contrast \(K\) and correlation length \(L\)” remains `UNKNOWN` in this run. The sharp 1D iid theorem is the better completed capability.

## Costs, controls and a discriminating test

For the exact 1D theorem, supplied inputs are \(K,\ell\), the parameter interval \(p\in[1/4,3/4]\), iid cell sampling, coupon alignment, and a worst-case resistance error below \(\gamma\ell/2\). The probability \(p\) is acquired from the maps. Each coupon needs one voltage drive and one scalar current/voltage response; summing the decoded counts costs \(O(N)\) operations. Material cost is \(M\ell\) total sampled length, independent of the width/count split. Calibrating \(K\), \(\ell\), electrode/contact resistance, map precision, and specimen independence adds physical acquisition cost and is not covered by the probability theorem.

The discriminating model check is to measure disjoint aligned coupons and test whether each resistance lies within the declared error of one of the quantized values \(\ell(n-\gamma b)\), \(b=0,\ldots,n\), then compare the pooled decoded count with the \(\operatorname{Binomial}(M,p)\) prediction and the held-out long-chain conductivity with \((1-\gamma p)^{-1}\). Failure of quantization, binomial dispersion, or the predicted conductivity after calibrated measurement error rejects the iid series-resistor model. It does not falsify the minimax theorem conditional on that model. No physical coupon was acquired in this run.

For the multi-D RVE designs, the equally informed comparator is the same coupon budget, same boundary modes, same law/periodization information, and the conventional periodic-cell energy estimator; any selection method must also be charged for its selection statistic and rejection rate. The practical falsifier is held-out coverage: if an independent periodized ensemble under the stated assumptions violates the source tail/bias envelope after numerical response error is included, the implementation, periodization, or assumed law is wrong. An ordinary physical crop that disagrees with the periodized law is a model mismatch, not a contradiction of the source theorem.
