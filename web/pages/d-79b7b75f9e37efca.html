# Cycle 2: omitted-phase coverage and global hull certificates

## Verdict

For a finite admissible competitor universe, global stability is robustly certifiable from energy intervals **if and only if** the target's worst-case upper energy is no larger than the lower convex hull formed from every competitor's certified lower energy. Equivalently, a verifiable affine plane must lie below every competitor lower bound and above the target upper bound. This is the strongest deterministic certificate available from independent interval information: if its LP fails, the allowed interval box contains a completion in which the target is unstable.

This closes the panel-to-global gap only if the universe is complete, or if valid lower bounds cover every omitted phase. With only a finite panel, global stability is not identifiable. A separate randomized audit gives a false-certificate bound only after adding a measurable discovery assumption: every unstable alternative must place at least mass `rho` under a frozen sampling law on phases at least `eta` below the support plane. Then `m` independent, sufficiently accurate energy queries miss with probability at most `(1-rho)^m`. If `rho` may be zero or one phase among a huge universe, there is no useful distribution-free global guarantee.

The hidden A2B example remains decisive. AB is robustly stable on its endpoint-only panel even with 5 meV/atom residual intervals. An omitted A2B at `x_B=1/3`, `E=-0.30 eV/atom` makes `3/4 A2B + 1/4 B` have energy `-0.225 eV/atom`; the exact-point AB margin becomes `+0.125 eV/atom`. On the interval lower bounds in the fixture, the target upper bound is 131.25 meV/atom above the enlarged lower hull. No panel-only calibration removes this failure.

This is a conditional extension and impossibility boundary, not a claim of a new materials-discovery method. The formulas and lower bound are internally reconstructed; the A2B/grid fixture was checked exactly with rational arithmetic. No materials benchmark, DFT campaign, synthesis, or experimental validation was run. Without complete structure search, a verified omitted-phase lower-bound service, or an honestly established `rho,eta` coverage model, output must remain `PANEL-ONLY` or `UNKNOWN`.

## Link to cycle 1 and interface

Fix one thermodynamic state, composition normalization, and energy target (for example, PBE energy per atom at zero temperature). Let target `t` have composition `x_t` in the `q`-component simplex. Let `Omega` be the complete finite universe of admissible competitor phase identities, including all structures/polymorphs and compositions that could compete; exclude `t` itself but include same-composition alternatives. Each phase `z` has composition `x_z` and certified interval `[L_z,U_z]`; the target has `E_t <= U_t`. Assume `x_t` is in the competitors' composition hull. Define

`Lambda_t = {lambda >= 0 : sum_z lambda_z = 1, sum_z lambda_z x_z = x_t}`

and `H_L(x_t) = min_{lambda in Lambda_t} sum_z lambda_z L_z`.

All energies must use a common per-atom basis and one named DFT functional/settings or measured thermodynamic potential. A PBE certificate is a statement about the PBE model. Nature-level claims require bounds that also cover the relevant Gibbs free energies and model error at the declared temperature and pressure.

## Exact deterministic theorem

**Theorem (robust global stability over a finite universe).** Under independent box uncertainty `E_t <= U_t` and `E_z >= L_z` for every `z in Omega`, the target is stable for every allowed completion if and only if

`U_t <= H_L(x_t)`.

By finite-dimensional LP strong duality, this is equivalent to an affine function `ell(x)=a+b·x` with `ell(x_t) >= U_t` and `ell(x_z) <= L_z` for every competitor.

**Proof.** For every feasible mixture, `sum lambda_z E_z >= sum lambda_z L_z`; minimizing shows the true competitor hull is at least `H_L`. Thus `E_t <= U_t <= H_L` implies stability. Conversely, if `U_t > H_L`, choose the allowed completion `E_t=U_t` and `E_z=L_z` for every competitor; its hull is exactly `H_L`, so the target is unstable. For the dual, write the primal with columns `(1,x_z)` and right side `(1,x_t)`. Its dual maximizes `a+b·x_t` subject to `a+b·x_z <= L_z`. Strong duality and attainment give the plane exactly when the primal optimum is at least `U_t`; any such plane also lower-bounds every mass-balanced mixture. ∎

Upper intervals `U_z` are not needed for stable certification; they can certify instability by a decomposition witness, as in the first-wave LP. If this stability LP fails, the result is **UNKNOWN under the supplied intervals**, not proof that the actual target is unstable. It does prove no deterministic method using only those independent intervals can rule out an unstable completion. Any stronger result must add information such as cross-phase error constraints or a prior, and charge its validity/acquisition. For an infinite structure space, a uniform lower envelope `L_c(x) <= E_{c,s}(x)` for every structure `s`, family `c`, and composition `x` is sufficient: an affine plane below all those lower bounds and above `U_t` certifies. Equivalence there needs additional closure/attainment assumptions and a certified global optimizer.

## Random discovery certificate

If a complete LP universe is unavailable, freeze the panel-derived plane `ell` and audit law `Q` before observing audit labels. `Q` samples omitted phase IDs from a declared generator. Define `B_eta(ell)={z:E_z <= ell(x_z)-eta}`. For a fixed `eta>0`, consider only alternatives where (i) the target is unstable by at least `eta`, and (ii) `Q(B_eta(ell)) >= rho`.

The first condition forces at least one `eta`-deep plane violation: a mixture of energy at most `E_t-eta`, with `E_t<=U_t<=ell(x_t)`, has average residual at most `-eta`, so some phase in it has `E_z-ell(x_z)<=-eta`. The second condition is an extra discovery premise; it does not follow from instability.

Draw `m` independent candidates from `Q` and evaluate them at the named fidelity. Assume exact oracle energies or deterministic query error at most `epsilon<eta`; then every draw from `B_eta` is detected as a plane violation. Return `GLOBAL-(rho,eta,delta)-CERTIFIED` only if no violation is found. For every alternative in the declared class,

`Pr(false global-stable output) <= (1-rho)^m`,

so it suffices that `m >= ceil(log(delta)/log(1-rho)) ~= rho^(-1) log(1/delta)`. This is sharp when `Q(B_eta)=rho`. If a physical synthesis/characterization attempt detects a candidate in `B_eta` only with conditional probability at least `p_min`, the miss bound becomes `(1-rho*p_min)^m`. This requires calibrated detection probability for every relevant phase. Failed synthesis is censored non-detection, not evidence of absence or high energy; without credible `p_min`, synthesis does not support a numerical global-coverage claim.

## Sharp finite minimax lower bound

**Lemma (one hidden phase).** Let the known finite universe have `N` IDs and an exact energy-query oracle. Under the null, all candidates have non-destabilizing energies. Under alternative `j`, only ID `j` is changed to a deep destabilizer; choose `j` uniformly from `N`. Any adaptive algorithm querying `m` distinct IDs has transcript total variation `m/N` between the null and the mixture of alternatives. With equal prior odds, the minimum average testing error is exactly `(1-m/N)/2`.

**Proof.** Condition on the algorithm's random seed. Its null query path contains at most `m` distinct IDs. The uniform hidden ID is on that path with probability `m/N`, in which case the changed answer reveals the alternative. Otherwise every answer equals the null transcript. This gives total variation `m/N`; the equal-prior error is `(1-TV)/2`. Querying any `m` distinct IDs attains the bound. ∎

If the method certifies the stable null with probability at least `1-alpha`, its false-stable probability averaged over the hidden ID is at least `(1-m/N)(1-alpha)`. To make this at most `delta` requires `m >= N(1-delta/(1-alpha))` when `delta<1-alpha`. In the common case `alpha=0`, `m >= (1-delta)N`. With `K` bad IDs among `N`, uniform sampling without replacement misses with exact probability `binom(N-K,m)/binom(N,m) <= (1-K/N)^m`. This bound is sharp under its oracle interface: answers on an unqueried ID can change without changing any observations. CAL or a GNoME-style model can beat it only by adding a useful prior or a verified lower-bound theorem.

## Candidate count, composition coverage, and costs

For `q` elements, composition space has dimension `d=q-1`. A denominator-`M` stoichiometric simplex grid

`G_(q,M)={(k_1/M,...,k_q/M): k_i are nonnegative integers, sum_i k_i=M}`

contains exactly `C_q(M)=binom(M+q-1,q-1)` compositions. With `S` structural families/polymorph candidates per composition, the finite universe has `N=S*C_q(M)` IDs (or `N=sum_x S_x` when counts vary). Examples: `q=92,M=2` gives 4,278 compositions; `q=5,M=10` gives 1,001. Ten candidate structures per point on the 92-component denominator-2 grid mean 42,780 energy evaluations before defects, magnetic orderings, or larger stoichiometries. These are combinatorial counts, not a claim every formula is realizable.

Coverage assumptions change the cost:

- **One bad ID among finite `N`, without replacement:** query at least `(1-delta)N` distinct IDs to limit misses to `delta`. At `N=10,000, delta=0.05`, this is 9,500 high-fidelity evaluations. With replacement, `ceil(log(delta)/log(1-1/N))`, about `N log(1/delta)`, draws are needed.
- **Known bad-set prevalence `rho`:** iid sampling uses `ceil(log(delta)/log(1-rho))`. For `rho=0.01, delta=0.05`, this is 299 evaluations. If `rho=1/N` for `N=10,000`, it is about 30,000 with replacement. If `rho=0`, no finite audit works.
- **Family-wise Lipschitz premise:** if each structure family `c` has residual `r_c(x)=E_c(x)-ell(x)` globally `L`-Lipschitz, instability depth `eta` gives some `r_c(x*)<=-eta`; residual stays at most `-eta/2` within radius `r=eta/(2L)`. A deterministic net with fill distance `h<=eta/(2L)` must hit this region in every family; measured queries then need error `epsilon<eta/2` to detect it. One conservative Euclidean simplex-rounding bound is `h<=2(q-1)/M`, so `M>=4(q-1)L/eta` suffices. Grid cost is exactly `S binom(M+q-1,q-1)` and grows like `M^(q-1)` for fixed `q`.
- **Random smooth coverage:** if a family is selected with probability at least `w_min`, composition density is at least `q_min`, and every radius-`r` ball has volume at least `c_q r^(q-1)` in the domain, then `rho>=w_min q_min c_q (eta/(2L))^(q-1)`. Budget is `O(rho^(-1) log(1/delta))`. The family weights, density floor, Lipschitz constant, and margin must be justified. Isolated-stoichiometry polymorphs need not satisfy this premise; then the bound may collapse to zero.

For DFT, charge actual work, not just labels:

`C_DFT = sum_queried_z [structure generation + relaxation + static energy + requested higher-fidelity correction]`.

Record core-hours/wall time, cell sizes, cutoffs, k-point meshes, convergence thresholds, functional, and failed relaxations. Add candidate-generator cost. An r²SCAN pass is extra cost and changes the target. For synthesis, charge every attempt and characterization and estimate `p_min` on blinded positive controls. No universal dollar/core-hour estimate is supplied because it depends on material, cell, laboratory, and protocol.

## CAL and GNoME under equal information

Every method must get the same training/reference data, candidate universe and generator, target functional, phase information, and total high-fidelity budget. Candidate generation, surrogate training, relaxation, and DFT count toward cost. Freeze policies before exposing withheld labels.

| Method | Strength | Scope and fair comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Robust lower-hull LP | Certified `L_z` for every phase gives an exact support-plane witness and deterministic interval guarantee. | Requires complete universe or valid lower bounds; panel bounds give panel-only claims. |
| Random `Q` audit | Explicit false-stable risk under `rho,eta,epsilon`; smoothness can lower-bound `rho`. | Charges generator plus `m` energy calls. Without positive `rho`, no global guarantee. |
| CAL | Novick et al. model each phase-composition energy surface with GPs, induce a posterior over hulls on a finite grid, then query by expected information gain about the whole hull. Their simulated ternary test has 66 compositions and 3 phases (198 pairs); across 40 generated energy surfaces, CAL is near-zero hull-energy MAE around 50 observations, while BASE needs nearly all pairs. Reported EIG uses 200 hull samples and 10 hypothetical outcomes per candidate. | Strongest query-allocation comparator for a fixed set. The example is a finite synthetic grid; no distribution-free guarantee covers unrepresented structures or a misspecified GP. EIG runtime rises with pair count. Match DFT and acquisition compute. |
| GNoME | Large learned graph models, candidate generation/filtering, DFT verification, ensembles and retraining. Reports over 10^9 candidates, 2.2 million stable relative to previous databases, 381,000 entries on updated hull, and 736 independent experimental matches. | Strongest demonstrated large-scale pipeline. Stability is relative to catalogues; the paper notes later discoveries can displace phases and reports displacement of at least 5,000 earlier entries. Its data flywheel and generator are information/cost, not completeness proof. At equal information/oracle calls it cannot beat the hidden-ID bound without a prior/lower-bound service. |

For a matched adversarial test, give all methods the same finite IDs and tell them exactly one ID is uniformly hidden-low. With `m` exact DFT calls, no ranking method exceeds `m/N` detection probability. CAL may do better on average when GP smoothness holds; GNoME may find useful candidates through its learned prior. Those are empirical advantages to test under matched information and costs, not worst-case guarantees.

Primary sources: [CAL, arXiv:2402.15582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15582), including finite-grid EIG, simulation and scaling sections; [GNoME, Nature (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06735-9); [2026 seven-facet preprint v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00254v2). The latter reports 76 meV/atom random-split MAE on 51,068 one-lowest-structure-per-composition entries, says more facets are needed for the exact hull, and does not remove structure search. Results are source-reported, not independently rerun.

## Model versus nature and falsifier

The certificates operate on a named energy function. For PBE, every `L_z,U_t` must bound PBE at the same settings. A PBE certificate is not experimental stability. A nature-level claim needs matched-condition Gibbs free-energy intervals covering DFT error and relevant vibrational, configurational, magnetic, pressure, temperature, defect and disorder terms. Finite calibration against experiments is not automatically a uniform bound over omitted phases.

**Preregistered falsification protocol (not run).** Select a real binary with an AB target and independently established A2B competitor. Fix temperature/pressure; publish `Omega`, structure rules, training data, `Q`, claimed `rho,eta`, and equal cost caps before withheld labels. Hide A2B and its high-fidelity result from every method. Compare robust lower-bound LP where available, CAL EIG ranking, GNoME-style ranking, uniform random and the coverage audit, at equal DFT calls plus charged relaxation/search. If the audit outputs global certification and withheld A2B satisfies `G_AB > (3/4)G_A2B+(1/4)G_B` beyond combined uncertainty, it is false. A fully enumerated challenge with `Q(B_eta)<rho` falsifies the discovery premise. A physical follow-up anneals AB/A2B/B mixtures to equilibrium and uses diffraction/composition analysis plus calorimetry to test that Gibbs inequality. Detecting a lower competitor falsifies the nature-level claim. Failure to synthesize does not validate completeness without measured `p_min` and detection sensitivity.

The A2B values are an exact adversarial fixture, not a nominated material. No physical benchmark or synthesis was executed.
