# Cycle 2: historical exact sequential comparison and effective simulation

Status: scoped literature boundary, not an exhaustive originality certificate. Date: 2026-10-06. Worker: d_prior. Cycle 1 files are preserved. Source metadata and integrity hashes are in `sources/d_prior/cycle2/metadata.json`.

## Exact question and interfaces

The parent candidate concerns a known finite latent state, finite full-support source menu, parameter-independent choice/randomization, positive action costs, and a known fixed iid target experiment G. For every integrable stopping G-policy with finite terminal alphabet, seek an exact reproduction of its state-conditional terminal laws by a controlled-source policy, with a policy/output-cardinality independent remainder and statewise leading factors

`rho_i(q_i) = c(q_i) max_{j != i} D(G_i || G_j) / sum_u q_i(u) D(P_i^u || P_j^u)`.

The source policy may depend on the target policy. The quantifiers are **for every target policy, there exists a source policy**, not one acquired report answering every later target. Source observations are charged; auxiliary randomness and computation are free in Li–Liu's interface. This distinction remains essential when assessing reusable scientific capability.

Controlled target menus would add another interface: actions in the target may depend on its own simulated history, and exact reproduction may concern a terminal answer, a complete finite transcript, or an online interaction. These are different experiments. The parent fixed-target candidate does not itself prove an online controlled-target simulator.

## Closest historical sources and access limits

### Greenshtein 1996 and Greenshtein–Torgersen 1997

*Comparison of Sequential Experiments*, Eitan Greenshtein, Annals of Statistics 24(1), 436–448, February 1996, DOI 10.1214/aos/1033066220. Publisher full and PDF endpoints returned an Incapsula challenge; JSTOR did not expose the article. The failed responses are saved. Purdue's official 1990 technical-report list contains this title as report 19, but supplies no report file and directs readers to contact the department. No contact was made. **Original full definitions/proofs were not read.** The accessible de Oliveira author manuscript below supplies an attributed theorem, but cannot certify every part of the original article's scope.

*Statistical information and expected number of observations for sequential experiments*, Eitan Greenshtein and Erik Torgersen, JSPI 59(2), 229–240, 15 April 1997, DOI 10.1016/S0378-3758(96)00114-0. The primary publisher indexed abstract was readable; direct full/PDF access failed with 403. Its setup uses stopped iid records from the **same** family of equivalent measures. It states that sufficiency of the second stopped experiment for the first requires statewise expected length at least as large, obtained through the Hellinger transform. **No inner definition, theorem number, or proof is verified.** This is adjacent to exact comparison and expected observation cost; it is not evidence that arbitrary controlled source menus have the parent's uniform KL-rate sufficiency theorem.

Additional unresolved prerequisite: Greenshtein–Torgersen, *Ordering of Sequentially Sampled Exponential Experiments*, Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 25(2), 325–329, June 1998, DOI 10.1111/1467-9469.00106. Primary publisher indexed abstract says a class of exponential families admits a necessary stochastic ordering of stopping lengths. ### de Oliveira: actual adapted-garbling definition and theorem

Henrique de Oliveira, *Blackwell's Informativeness Theorem Using Diagrams*, author manuscript dated 30 November 2017; GEB 109, 126–131, May 2018. PDF pp6–8 were read, including proofs of Lemmas 3–4. Definition 2 on p6 requires the prefix marginal of an action-path kernel at date t to depend only on the signal prefix through t. Lemma 3 identifies those kernels with causal strategies; Lemma 4 proves closure under composition. Theorem 5 on p8, attributed to Greenshtein, equates adapted garbling with inclusion of all attainable state-conditional action-path laws and universal preference over dynamic decision problems. Its proof is omitted by reference to the preceding static proof.

This is genuine **exact** sequential comparison prior art, not merely testing. Its finite date grid aligns source and target prefixes. It does not compute a KL leading factor for random source query counts, allow arbitrary expected stopping cost in place of calendar-time alignment, or establish the uniform remainder sought here. Fixed-date adapted garbling is a stricter causal contract than terminal simulation with extra samples.

### Resource theories: approximation cannot be silently turned into exactness

Xin Wang and Mark M. Wilde, *Resource Theory of Asymmetric Distinguishability*, Physical Review Research 1, 033170 (2019). PDF p7 equations (59)–(60) define conversions with approximate reproduction of one state and exact reproduction of the other. Equation (62), p8, gives the asymptotic relative-entropy ratio under that approximation contract. Exact one-shot cost uses max-relative entropy, equation (35). Thus its KL ratio is not an exact finite-output stopping conversion for both states.

Their *Resource Theory of Asymmetric Distinguishability for Quantum Channels*, PRR 1, 033169 (2019), includes sequential strategies. Equation (154), p18, defines an exact sequential cost from fixed n-round strategy boxes; Theorem 5, equation (156), identifies that cost with channel max-relative entropy. Fixed-round distinguishability bits differ from random expected classical source queries. Neither paper read here yields the parent's exact expected-cost universal stopping theorem. Both have broader quantum objects but different resource accounting.

## Exact simulation is already classical; effective costs need a new interface

Nacu–Peres, *Fast Simulation of New Coins from Old*, arXiv math/0309222v5, 31 March 2005; Annals of Applied Probability 15(1A), 93–115. Theorem 2, PDF p2, gives exact simulation of known analytic f(p) from an unknown p-coin on a closed interior interval with exponential input-toss tails; it also gives an analyticity converse for fast simulation on open sets. Definition 1 on p2 specifies the tail requirement. Read pp1–3 and selected later theorem passages, not all proofs. The function f is supplied. This is decisive prior art against claiming exact random-length probability transformation itself as novel, and against a blanket claim that unknown probabilities preclude all exact simulation. It supplies no all-output policy-uniform controlled-menu leading factor.

Saad, Freer, Rinard, Mansinghka, *The Fast Loaded Dice Roller*, PMLR 108, 1036–1046, 26–28 August 2020. Read §§2–5. Algorithm 4 and Theorems 5.1–5.2, PDF p5, exactly sample a rational categorical law from fair bits through a finite representation with rejection; expected random bits are below entropy plus six. Theorem 2.1, p3, recalls Knuth–Yao's entropy lower bound. This supports executable exact rational repair. It charges a different resource, so does not imply a uniform total computation bound for Li–Liu conversion.

### Independent rational construction and separation

The executable `work/d_prior/cycle2/rational_exact_repair.py` checks all matrix identities with exact rational arithmetic. It takes source state laws Bernoulli(1/5), Bernoulli(4/5), and target Bernoulli(1/1000), Bernoulli(1/50). A single-source-sample kernel cannot reproduce target event {1}: its likelihood ratio would be 20, exceeding the largest source event ratio 4. For any nonnegative garbling column k, the output likelihood ratio is a weighted average of source likelihood ratios, hence at most 4. Thus even the date-1 adapted order fails.

Nevertheless, both directed source KL values equal `(3/5) log 4 > .8`; the two target KL values are respectively below `log(.999/.98) < .02` and `(1/50) log 20 < .06`. They satisfy strict directed KL dominance.

Classify the source state using majority of seven observations. Its symmetric error is `e=521/15625`, so the classifier experiment is `A=[[1-e,e],[e,1-e]]`. Let target H have rows `[999/1000,1/1000]`, `[49/50,1/50]`. Direct inversion gives the stochastic repair kernel

```
K = [[3644579/3645750, 1171/3645750],
     [14281441/14583000, 301559/14583000]].
```

Exact `A K = H`, nonnegativity and row sums are checked. One can sample each repair row exactly: draw b fair bits, where `b=ceil(log2 denominator)`, reject integer draws at least the denominator, and compare the accepted integer with the numerator. Conditional on acceptance, the integer is uniform; acceptance probability exceeds one half, so termination is almost sure with exponentially decaying trial count. This supplies an actual exact finite-source-query construction, not Monte Carlo evidence. It is a demonstration of classical classifier/inverse-repair machinery, not a new rate theorem.

### Effective computability versus mathematical probability laws: our deductions

Suppose source probabilities and the target program's transitions are computable, the finite terminal output alphabet is known, and the program halts almost surely in every admitted state. Enumerate its finite halting prefixes and their probabilities. Each terminal probability H_i(z) is lower semicomputable. Since the finitely many probabilities sum to one, `1-sum_{w != z} lower(H_i(w))` supplies decreasing upper bounds. Dovetail until each lower/upper interval has desired width. This computes H uniformly under the halting promise. No uniform runtime bound or finite expected computation cost follows.

For a computable finite law, generate a lazy fair-bit U in [0,1] and successively refine intervals for U and cumulative probabilities. Output a category once the U interval is wholly inside its category interval. Exact output boundaries have probability zero, hence the decision terminates almost surely and has the required law. Probability computation may be arbitrarily expensive. Rational inputs allow the simpler preceding rejection construction.

Consequently finite precision is not by itself an impossibility theorem: variable precision and random runtime can preserve exactness. A bounded fair-bit budget produces only dyadic probabilities, so an exact non-dyadic law requires variable resources. Arbitrary measurable auxiliary randomization and noncomputable supplied laws allowed in a mathematical theorem are broader than the executable interface just proved.

A decisive total-cost obstruction: a target with T=0 can output uniformly among M labels using free auxiliary randomness. The source-observation bound is zero. Its actual exact implementation needs at least log2 M expected fair bits. Therefore policy/output-cardinality independent **source-query** cost cannot become policy/output-cardinality independent **total** acquisition-plus-computation cost without changing assumptions or charging target-side computation too. This does not contradict Li–Liu's resource definition.

## September 2026 note: post-query reuse has stronger quantifiers

Rianne de Heide, *Statistical reduction before the target is known: two boundary results*, arXiv2609.05286v1, 4 September 2026. Full eight-page paper read. Theorem 1, PDF p2, equates preservation of every finite later Bayes risk with sufficiency; Corollary 2, p4, prohibits merging nonproportional likelihood vectors. The author explicitly identifies this as classical finite Blackwell–Bahadur theory. Theorem 3, p5, gives exact symmetric acquisition cost m*C1 for independently informative Gaussian coordinates with coordinatewise validity and an externally specified later query. The proof simulates other coordinates using parameter-independent auxiliary streams, then sums coordinate sample lower bounds. Corollary 4, p6, is the pointwise KL lower bound. These results concern one retained report before the target is known. They neither refute nor supply a different conversion policy chosen after each target policy is supplied.

## Controlled-target lower bound and unresolved theorem

Our independent deduction: for a finite-horizon controlled target with finite alphabets, choose its **whole action/observation transcript** as the terminal output. Under state i versus j its KL is

`sum_v E_i[M_v] D(G_i^v || G_j^v)`.

Exact reproduction by a controlled source implies, by data processing and predictable KL chain rule,

`sum_u E_i[N_u] D(P_i^u || P_j^u) >= sum_v E_i[M_v] D(G_i^v || G_j^v)`

for every j. These inequalities share one source occupation vector; separate pairwise maxima do not suffice (cycle 1 has an exact obstruction). For a terminal answer alone, the target transcript KL may strictly exceed the answer KL, so imposing the transcript bound without specifying output changes the question. The source and target occupation vectors across states are also constrained by implementability of one policy; independent statewise maximization is not automatically valid for arbitrary controlled-target policies.

**Novelty boundary:** the new claim, if independently proved, must be the sharp, statewise, output/policy-uniform expected source-cost conversion for arbitrary finite terminal laws from integrable stopping target policies under controlled source choice. Exact garbling, sequential comparison, exact repair, Bernoulli factories, KL testing allocation, and adaptive-vs-fixed testing gains are prior art. We did not recover a prior theorem with all the parent's quantifiers and costs. Because the closest 1996/1997 originals are not fully accessible, unrestricted novelty remains unresolved rather than certified.

**Concrete downstream use:** such a theorem would permit replacing a known experimental interface while preserving each supplied scientific decision law and quantifying observation cost. It would not acquire unknown source models, supply labeled calibration, make a fixed retained report sufficient for every unknown future goal, or certify scientific breakthroughs. Any consequential application needs a specified experiment menu, calibrated state model, affordable probability computations, and equally informed comparison policy.

**Next falsifiable steps:** (1) obtain and read 1996/1997 original theorem statements through permitted existing library access, with no paid/contact action; (2) independently audit the parent verifier/exact-repair proof and its uniform constants; (3) formulate a controlled-target transcript-versus-answer contract before asserting its extension; (4) give an executable conversion for rational known experiments and a finite encoded target machine while separately accounting for fair bits, probability evaluation, and storage. Failure of any advertised bound in that explicit interface narrows the claim.
