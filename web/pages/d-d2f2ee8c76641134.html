# Reconstructed derivations and adversarial examples

These are our derivations. The first reproduces a known finite-state special case of Dobrushin comparison; it is a baseline, not a novelty claim.

## A. Finite-state local certificate

Let P,Q be strictly positive laws on a finite product space. Let P_i(.|x_-i),Q_i(.|x_-i) be their conditional kernels. Put

    C_ij = sup_{x,z differing only at j} TV(P_i(.|x_-i),P_i(.|z_-i)), i != j; C_ii=0,
    b_i = E_{Y~Q} TV(P_i(.|Y_-i),Q_i(.|Y_-i)).

Assume max_i sum_j C_ij = c < 1. Random-scan Gibbs kernels of P and Q are irreducible and have unique stationary marginals P,Q. Couple them by choosing the same uniformly random coordinate and a maximal coupling of the two conditional draws. The coupled chain is finite, so it has a stationary law nu; its marginals must be P,Q. Write d_i=nu[X_i != Y_i]. Stationarity at coordinate i gives

    d_i = E_nu TV(P_i(.|X_-i), Q_i(.|Y_-i)).

Change X_-i into Y_-i one coordinate at a time. By the triangle inequality and the definition of C,

    d_i <= sum_j C_ij d_j + b_i.

Iterate this inequality k times. Since ||C^k||_infinity <= c^k and 0<=d_i<=1, the remainder C^k d vanishes. Thus d <= (I-C)^-1 b componentwise. For delta_i(f)=sup_{x,z differing only at i}|f(x)-f(z)|, another coordinate telescope gives

    |Pf-Qf| <= E_nu|f(X)-f(Y)| <= sum_i delta_i(f)d_i
             <= delta(f)^T (I-C)^-1 b.

If f depends on a fixed set S, delta_i(f)=0 off S, and b_i<=epsilon, then |Pf-Qf| <= epsilon sum_{i in S}delta_i(f)/(1-c). Neither N nor a hidden total-volume loss enters. Deriving c<1 near a transition is the unresolved application, not a consequence of a learned architecture.

## B. Local error versus global KL

P_N=Bernoulli(1/2)^N and Q_N=Bernoulli(1/2+delta)^N. For f(x)=x_1, |Pf-Qf|=|delta|, independent of N. C=0 and b_i=|delta|, so A gives this exactly. However

    KL(P_N||Q_N) = -(N/2) log(1-4 delta^2).

For delta=.02 the KL coefficient is .00080064068 per coordinate. Global Pinsker grows as sqrt(N) and becomes vacuous while the local error remains .02. This is a direct obstruction to volume independence obtained solely from global-KL decomposition with fixed coordinate losses.

## C. Tiny conditional residual with macroscopic phase bias

On two spins, give P masses (1-epsilon)/2 to each aligned state and epsilon/2 to each anti-aligned state. Give Q masses .1(1-epsilon) to --, .9(1-epsilon) to ++, and epsilon/2 to each anti-aligned state. Take 0<epsilon<1/2, so both laws are positive and the following signs are fixed. A direct conditional calculation gives

    C_12=C_21=1-2epsilon,
    b_1=b_2=.8 epsilon(1-epsilon),
    |P[X_1=+]-Q[X_1=+]|=.4(1-epsilon).

Therefore the A certificate is exact: b/(1-C_12)=.4(1-epsilon). As epsilon tends to zero the local conditional discrepancy goes to zero but local observable bias tends to .4. Any theorem dropping stability would fail already in dimension two. This family separates residual size from relevant/phase-mode response; it does not itself model a thermodynamic critical limit.

## D. Hierarchical KL and conditioning direction

Let each refinement split x_{j-1} orthogonally into (x_j,d_j). If p(x)=p_J(x_J) product_j p_j(d_j|x_j) and q analogously, then expanding log(p/q) and integrating under P gives exactly

    KL(P||Q) = KL(P_J||Q_J) + sum_j E_{x_j~P_j} KL(p_j(.|x_j)||q_j(.|x_j)).

The terms are nonnegative. No contraction or volume independence follows from the algebra. If H is the law of the finite-step conditional sampler, KL(H||Q) averages conditional sampling residuals over x_j~H_j. The direction and prefix law cannot be switched. Pinsker and TV's triangle inequality give the known two-square-root certificate quoted in the audit.

For an observable-specific telescope, enumerate coarse-to-fine factors K_0,...,K_J versus learned factors L_0,...,L_J, and form hybrids H^r=L_0...L_{r-1}K_r...K_J. Adjacent hybrids share a learned prefix and true downstream kernels. If F_r(z)=E[f(final)|prefix and level-r output z; true downstream], their difference is the learned-prefix expectation of (L_r-K_r)F_r. A Wasserstein error can be multiplied by a verified Lipschitz constant of F_r. The sum of these sensitivities, rather than the mere number of scales, determines whether the bound stays finite. Obtaining F_r sensitivities efficiently is a concrete missing construction.

## E. Stein residual has a response factor

Take L_P=Delta + grad log p dot grad and a solution L_P u_f=f-Pf. If Q has a smooth positive density, suitable integration-by-parts tails, and L_Q-invariance, then

    Qf-Pf = Q L_P u_f = Q[(grad log p-grad log q) dot grad u_f].

Hence |Qf-Pf| <= sum_i ||partial_i u_f||_infinity E_Q|partial_i log p-partial_i log q|. A volume-independent *local* bound needs summable spatial/scale derivatives of u_f, or an equally controlled weighted bound. An arbitrary finite list of Stein probes is only a lower bound on the supremum discrepancy unless approximation error of the full test class is controlled. Uniform wavelet covariance does not prove these Poisson sensitivities.

## F. Self-normalized importance sampling is not exactly unbiased

For normalized p,q, ordinary IS has E_q[(p/q)f]=E_p f. Self-normalized IS is sum_n w_n f_n/sum_n w_n. With n=1 it equals f(X_1), so its expectation is E_q f. Let p=(.8,.2),q=(.5,.5),f=(0,1). Then the target is .2 and one-sample SNIS expectation is .5. Exact restriction of a hierarchical q and telescoping control variates cannot remove that finite-n ratio bias. They can preserve the model marginals and give consistency or improved variance; exact unbiasedness needs a different estimator or known normalization.
