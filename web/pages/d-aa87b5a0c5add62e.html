Scope
-----
Independent source scout for an exact bottleneck in hidden-state thermodynamic inference. This excludes the collective-spin bath and chemical-reaction recurrence branches. Search was bounded to three core source families and focused on primary sources. No theorem has been newly proved here; the output is a precise research target and source-grounded mechanisms.

Candidate bottleneck
--------------------
For a stationary finite-state continuous-time Markov chain X with a known observation map Y=phi(X), only a finite, single observed trajectory Y[0,T] is available. Construct a nontrivial one-sided (1-delta) lower confidence bound L_T on the hidden steady-state entropy-production rate sigma(X), valid uniformly over an explicitly specified class of hidden generators and, ideally, simultaneously over all T (anytime validity). The bound should strengthen beyond visible one-step currents by using finite-order path reversal or transition/waiting-time asymmetry. A natural constrained version assumes a known upper bound K on the number of hidden states, known observed labels, bounded rates, and a uniform mixing/spectral-gap bound; the unknown generator topology may remain free.

This is a candidate theorem statement, not a claim that the result is novel or established. Its exact output is a certified lower confidence bound in entropy/time units. The data contract must say whether one long stationary trajectory or independent trajectory replicates are supplied, whether observation timing is exact, and which rate/mixing constants are known. A confidence set C_T for observable path/block statistics suggests the robust value

    L_T = inf { sigma(Q) : Q in Q(K, rate, mixing), observable law of Q lies in C_T }.

Uniform coverage of C_T would imply coverage of L_T. The substantive gate is to make C_T valid under dependent/overlapping observations and to show that this infimum is computable, finite, and informative; for an anytime result, C_t must be a confidence sequence. A block-KL or waiting-time truncation could avoid reconstructing all hidden rates, but the map from finite-sample uncertainty to a population lower bound must be proved, especially when reverse counts are zero or very small.

Why this is a real gap rather than a lack of population estimators
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Ehrich proves that the full stationary Markov entropy production is a path-space time-reversal KL rate and that masking cannot increase it. His observed waiting-time formula is an exact population lower bound. But his inverse hidden-network fit assumes the correct hidden-state count and essentially exact observable jump probabilities; the parameter search is numerical. Fritz, Ertel, and Seifert explicitly document that finite-record empirical waiting-time plug-ins can overshoot their population lower bound. Thus “the estimator is a lower bound” is not a finite-data coverage statement.

Core source 1: exact population route and its bottleneck
--------------------------------------------------------
Jannik Ehrich, “Tightest bound on hidden entropy production from partially observed dynamics,” arXiv:2105.08803v2 (5 July 2021), later J. Stat. Mech. 083214 (2021). Primary version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08803v2

Read/reconstruction depth: downloaded full v2 PDF and read/reconstructed the stationary finite-state masking argument and the four-state realization algebra, pp. 5–14 (PDF pp. numbering starts at the title page; local copy SHA-256 is in SOURCE_LEDGER.json).

Exact result and proof span: Eq. (3), then Eq. (6)–(12), pp. 3 and 6–7 (arXiv PDF, printed pages 4 and 7–8). The steady-state EP per step is expressed as the relative entropy rate between forward and time-reversed full-state paths. Memoryless masking preserves the joint path KL when the observations are adjoined; marginalizing hidden paths yields the data-processing inequality

    EP_rate >= D_rate(P(observed path) || P(time-reversed observed path)).

The displayed chain-rule calculation is at arXiv PDF pp. 6–7, Eqs. (6)–(12). For the two-visible-state masked chain, Eq. (23)–(24), printed pp. 8–9, gives the observed time-series irreversibility as a sum over jump length n of nonnegative terms involving p_ij(n) and visible stationary weights. The text immediately after Eq. (24) says truncating the nonnegative sum at finite n remains a lower bound at the population-statistic level. This is the most directly usable proof mechanism: path reversal + KL data processing, followed by a finite-lag truncation.

The hidden-realization mechanism is Eq. (29), pp. 9–10: P(n) = C H^(n-2) B for hidden-state block H and entrance/exit blocks B,C. For the special 2-visible/2-hidden case, the paper assumes nonsingular P(2), parameterizes B using two free probabilities, obtains C,H from P(2),P(3), and then predicts all longer jump probabilities from the recurrence, Eq. (39)–(47), printed pp. 12–14. It searches over valid free parameters and takes the minimum fitted EP. The source does not supply a global optimization proof for the broad hidden-network inverse problem, and explicitly assumes the correct hidden-state count (printed p. 13, lines preceding Eq. (39)); for other dimensions it says a nonlinear system with potentially many free parameters remains (printed p. 14, Eq. (48) and following text).

Limits: This is an asymptotic/population calculation, not finite-sample coverage. The author calls it a best-case scenario based on accurately determined observable statistics (Discussion, printed p. 16). A compatible realization can have seemingly unbounded high EP as a transition approaches absolute irreversibility, printed pp. 14–15. So minimization may remain meaningful while maximization is ill-posed. The method is conditional on Markovian hidden dynamics, stationarity, finite state space, and reversible support (each nonzero transition has a reverse, setup p. 3).

Core source 2: direct finite-statistics counter-gate
---------------------------------------------------
Jonas H. Fritz, Benjamin Ertel, and Udo Seifert, “Entropy estimation for partially accessible Markov networks based on imperfect observations: Role of finite resolution and finite statistics,” arXiv:2412.04102v2 (8 April 2025); published Phys. Rev. E 111, 044106 (7 April 2025). Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04102v2

Read depth: downloaded full v2 and read Sec. II–III, Eq. (1)–(19), pp. 1–5, plus the stated finite-statistics conclusions; SHA-256 in SOURCE_LEDGER.json.

Exact evidence: Eq. (4), p. 2, states the estimator-level population lower-bound contract. For finite resolution, the log-sum inequality gives an additional population lower bound in Eq. (10), p. 4. In Sec. III, Eq. (16)–(19), pp. 4–5, they model empirical waiting-time-bin counts from finite trajectories and characterize fluctuations. Crucially, the authors state that for finite observation duration T and finite replicate count N, fluctuations can make the empirical mean waiting-time estimator exceed its population value and therefore exceed the true EP (arXiv PDF p. 5, around lines 533–546 in the extracted full text). Their analyses and plots use finite-replicate sampling/variance, not a uniform one-sided confidence procedure.

Limitation: Their “finite statistics” treatment compares several estimators in a four-state network and an augmented Michaelis–Menten scheme, with model-specific simulation results. It is a direct warning that empirical plug-in lower bounds need coverage correction, but is not an impossibility result and does not settle any minimax or anytime-valid formulation.

Core source 3: finite-sample observable HMM mechanism, and exact connection gap
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Daniel Hsu, Sham M. Kakade, and Tong Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” arXiv:0811.4413v6 (6 July 2012), J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 78 (2012), 1460–1480. Primary version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4413v6

Read depth: downloaded full v6 PDF; read model conditions, Lemmas 1–4 and the full Theorem 6 statement/proof route, pp. 3–10 and 15–18; SHA-256 in SOURCE_LEDGER.json.

The useful proof mechanism is observable-operator realization. Under Condition 1 (initial hidden distribution strictly positive and observation and transition matrices both rank m), Lemma 2 proves rank(P_{2,1})=m; the SVD of P_{2,1} identifies an observation subspace. Lemmas 3–4 show that estimated observable operators reproduce any finite observation-word probability. These statements and proofs are pp. 5–7. Theorem 6, p. 10, says that with N independent observation sequences generated from the same initial distribution, a spectral learner gives joint length-t observation probabilities within total variation/L1 error epsilon with probability at least 1-eta, under an explicit sample bound polynomial in t, 1/epsilon, m and inverse singular values sigma_m(O), sigma_m(P_{2,1}) (exact formula at arXiv PDF p. 10, lines 543–568 in extracted text). The proof section pp. 15–18 controls how matrix estimation errors accumulate.

The theorem does not estimate entropy production and does not reconstruct a physically valid transition/observation parameterization: the authors characterize their representation as an improper HMM learner. Its samples are independent sequences; the paper notes that one long dependent sequence would require mixing-aware concentration not included in the theorem (p. 4, lines 159–171). Thus it is a potential confidence-set front end for observable finite blocks, not a solution to the thermodynamic inverse problem. A further exact connection obligation is to turn its finite-block TV guarantee into a one-sided bound on a KL-rate/EP functional. TV alone does not control KL without support or lower-probability conditions.

Named failed proof gate / next decisive obligation
---------------------------------------------------
“Finite observable uncertainty to a thermodynamic lower certificate”: given a dependent finite trajectory and a known class of stationary finite-state hidden CTMCs, construct a uniform (preferably time-uniform) confidence set for the observable trajectory law and prove that minimizing hidden EP over every compatible generator yields a valid, non-vacuous lower confidence bound. Must handle overlapping block counts, reverse-event zeros, hidden-state non-identifiability, and the continuity/compactness of the EP objective. If using only finite-lag observed path KL, prove that the chosen finite-lag statistic itself is below the hidden EP rate in the adopted continuous/discrete observation convention, then propagate uncertainty through the KL minimization. A bare plug-in divergence, a bootstrap interval without coverage theorem, or an asymptotic CLT is not enough.
