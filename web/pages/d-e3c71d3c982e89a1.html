# Independent audit: exact phase-retrieval conditioning, designs, and photon cost

## Verdict

For complex dimension $d\ge2$, I found no counterexample to the claim

\[
\beta^{\ell_2}_{\Psi_A}=2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_j p_j\,\Pi_j^{\otimes2}
 =\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym},
\qquad
p_j=\frac{\|a_j\|^4}{\sum_k\|a_k\|^4},\quad
\Pi_j=\frac{a_ja_j^*}{\|a_j\|^2}.
\]

The proof follows from equality in the published two-dimensional lower-bound argument and compression to every complex two-plane. There is a literal $d=1$ exception: every nonzero scalar sensing system has β=1. The universal-bound paper says “for any matrix” without excluding $d=1$, so its statement needs the standard implicit restriction $d\ge2$, or a correction.

Jasper–Mixon’s 2025 weighted-design construction makes the equality condition attainable in every $d\ge2$ with $d^2+O(d^{1.525})$ rows. Rescaling rows is an immediate consequence of their design, not a new design theorem. A special Bodmann–Haas instance can also be converted into a one-photon POVM and an explicit finite-QFT Naimark implementation. That construction is mathematically exact but has $\Theta(d^2)$ outcome channels and a poor photon-noise direction: two orthogonal coordinate states need $\Theta(d\log(1/\alpha))$ photons to distinguish at error α. The sibling Gaussian-Parseval construction has $O(d)$ outcomes and dimension-independent pairwise Hellinger separation, with a global minimum-distance risk bound $O(d/N)$. Thus β=2 optimizes the stated unweighted additive-ℓ2 intensity condition number; it does not optimize photon cost or statistical risk.

This is an internal proof/prior-art audit, not external validation or a novelty determination.

## Equality characterization audit

Write $S=\sum_j\|a_j\|^4>0$, $a_j=t_ju_j$ for nonzero rows, $p_j=t_j^4/S$, and $\Pi_j=u_ju_j^*$. Let

\[
F=\sum_jp_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2},\qquad
F_0=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}.
\]

For unit $x$, $q(x):=\sum_j|a_j^*x|^4=S\langle x^{\otimes2},F x^{\otimes2}\rangle$. The intensity-map constants in Peng–Han–Huang are $U^2=\max_{\|x\|=1}q(x)$, and $L$ is the infimum of the stated real-part quadratic form over unit $u,v$ with $\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb R$. Their proof in complex dimension two establishes

\[
U^2\ge S/3,\qquad L\le M,\qquad M^2\le S/12.
\]

Consequently $\beta=U/L\ge2$. If β=2, every inequality in this chain is equality: $U^2=S/3$, $L^2=M^2=S/12$. These constants and the compression monotonicity are in the source’s Theorem 3.1 proof (eqs. (17), (21)–(25), and the final estimates in §3.1).

For any two-dimensional complex subspace $V\subset\mathbb C^d$, replace each row by $P_Va_j$. Restricting pairs in the definitions gives $L_A\le L_{A|V}$ and $U_{A|V}\le U_A$, hence $\beta_{A|V}\le\beta_A$. The two-dimensional universal lower bound gives $\beta_{A|V}\ge2$, so it too is exactly 2. Its quartic function on the unit sphere of $V$ has maximum equal to its Haar mean $S_V/3$. Since the function is continuous and nowhere above its mean, it is constant on that sphere. Any two unit vectors lie in a common complex two-plane; therefore $q(x)$ is constant on the full unit sphere.

The Haar mean is $q(x)/S=2/[d(d+1)]$. Hence

\[
\langle x^{\otimes2},(F-F_0)x^{\otimes2}\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }x.
\]

Complex polarization of this bihomogeneous polynomial implies $F=F_0$. Conversely, if $F=F_0$, Haar fourth moments give $U^2=2S/[d(d+1)]$. For unit $u,v$ with $c=\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb R$, the same moment gives

\[
\sum_j\left|\operatorname{Re}(u^*a_ja_j^*v)\right|^2
=\frac{S(1+3c^2)}{2d(d+1)}.
\]

Its minimum is at $c=0$, so $L^2=S/[2d(d+1)]$ and β=2. Zero rows are omitted from $p_j$; $S>0$ is assumed.

### One-dimensional exception

For $d=1$ and any nonzero row family, put $v=(|a_j|^2)_j\ne0$. Then

\[
\Psi_A(x)=|x|^2v,\qquad
\|\Psi_A(x)-\Psi_A(y)\|_2
=\|v\|_2\,\bigl||x|^2-|y|^2\bigr|,
\]

while the nuclear metric is $\bigl||x|^2-|y|^2\bigr|$. Thus $L=U=\|v\|_2$, and β=1. The $d\ge2$ condition is essential for the iff result.

### Row count and exact-minimum case

The moment superoperator $X\mapsto\sum_jp_j\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jX)\Pi_j$ of a 2-design has eigenvalues $1/d$ on the identity and $1/[d(d+1)]$ on all $d^2-1$ traceless Hermitian directions. It has rank $d^2$, while each row contributes rank at most one, so a weighted projective 2-design needs at least $d^2$ rows. Scott’s Theorem 4 proves the stronger equality statement: at $d^2$ rows weights are uniform and off-diagonal overlaps are $1/(d+1)$, exactly a SIC. This is prior art, not a new phase-retrieval minimum-count result. An exact $d^2$-row optimizer exists precisely in dimensions with a SIC; existence in all dimensions remains conjectural.

## All-dimensional row construction from Jasper–Mixon

Jasper–Mixon define a weighted projective 2-design by $\sum_kw_k\Pi_k^{\otimes2}=P_{\rm sym}$, with total weight $N=d(d+1)/2$. Their Theorem 7 says that a Sidon set $S\subset G$ yields a design consisting of $|G|$ character vectors and $d=|S|$ coordinate vectors, with weights

\[
w_\chi=\frac{d^2}{2|G|},\qquad w_r=\frac12.
\]

For the Erdős–Turán parabola Sidon set in $G=\mathbb F_q^2$, choose $q\ge d$ odd prime and $S=\{(r,r^2):0\le r<d\}$. If two pair sums agree, equality of first coordinates gives equal sums and equality of second coordinates gives equal sums of squares; because $q$ is odd, the pair products also agree, so the two unordered pairs coincide. Thus the restricted parabola is Sidon. The support is $m=q^2+d$. Taking $q$ as the next odd prime and using the cited prime-gap estimate $q=d+O(d^{0.525})$ gives $m=d^2+O(d^{1.525})$.

After normalization by $N$, the weights are

\[
\nu_\chi=\frac{d}{q^2(d+1)},\qquad
\nu_r=\frac1{d(d+1)},\qquad
\sum_j\nu_j=1.
\]

Taking sensing rows $a_j=\nu_j^{1/4}u_j$ makes $\|a_j\|^4=\nu_j$, so $F=F_0$ and β=2. This settles an all-$d$ existence sequence at $d^2+O(d^{1.525})$ channels; it does not show that this support is minimal for every $d$, or solve the problem for every prescribed $m$.

## Acquisition interface, exact inverse, and charged costs

### One-photon POVM and Naimark circuit

An arbitrary weighted design with weights $\nu$ does not automatically become a POVM under $a_j=\nu_j^{1/4}u_j$: the effects sum to $\sum_j\sqrt{\nu_j}\Pi_j$, which need not be $I$. The Bodmann–Haas construction has extra layer symmetry. For its normalized character and coordinate layers,

\[
\sum_{\chi}\Pi_\chi=\frac{q^2}{d}I,\qquad
\sum_{r\in S}\Pi_r=I,
\]

so $\sum_j\sqrt{\nu_j}\Pi_j=(q+1)I/\sqrt{d(d+1)}$. Set

\[
\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{d(d+1)}}{q+1},\qquad
E_j=\gamma\sqrt{\nu_j}\Pi_j,\qquad
a_j=\sqrt\gamma\,\nu_j^{1/4}u_j.
\]

Then $E_j=a_ja_j^*\ge0$, $\sum_jE_j=I$, and the probability of outcome $j$ on normalized pure state $x$ is exactly the intensity $p_j(x)=|a_j^*x|^2=\operatorname{Tr}(E_jxx^*)$. Here $S_A=\sum_j\|a_j\|^4=\gamma^2$, giving

\[
L=\frac1{\sqrt2(q+1)},\qquad
U=\frac{\sqrt2}{q+1},\qquad
\beta=2.
\]

The corresponding isometry for a Naimark realization is

\[
Vx=\sqrt{\frac q{q+1}}\,F_{\mathbb F_q^2}E_Sx\;\oplus\;\frac1{\sqrt{q+1}}x,
\]

where $E_S$ embeds the $d$ coordinates at $(r,r^2)\in\mathbb F_q^2$, and $F_{\mathbb F_q^2}$ is the normalized two-coordinate finite-field Fourier transform. Measuring the first branch in the character basis and the second in the coordinate basis realizes the $q^2+d$-outcome POVM. This is an exact isometric specification, not a fabricated optical/hardware demo. It assumes coherent branch control, reversible embedding, two $q$-point QFTs, outcome-resolving detection, ideal calibration, and negligible loss; no gate synthesis, precision, fabrication, readout error, or resource benchmark is supplied.

### Exact inversion and a direct estimator

For $X=xx^*$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(X)=1$ and ideal outcome probabilities $r_j=\operatorname{Tr}(E_jX)$, define

\[
T=\sum_j\sqrt{\nu_j}\,r_j\Pi_j.
\]

The design identity gives $T=\gamma(X+I)/[d(d+1)]$, hence

\[
X=\frac{d(d+1)}\gamma T-I.
\]

For a single outcome $j$, the unbiased linear estimator is

\[
Z_j=\frac{d\sqrt{\nu_j}}\gamma\bigl((d+1)\Pi_j-I\bigr).
\]

Its Frobenius second moment obeys

\[
\mathbb E\|Z\|_F^2\le
\frac{(d^2+d-1)(q+1)}{d+1}<d(q+1),
\]

using $\nu_{\max}=1/[d(d+1)]$, $\sum_j\nu_j\Pi_j=I/d$, and $\operatorname{Tr}(X)=1$. Averaging $N_{\rm shot}$ independent outcomes and projecting to the nearest trace-one rank-one projector gives

\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat X-X\|_F^2\le\frac{4d(q+1)}{N_{\rm shot}},\qquad
\mathbb E\|\widehat X-X\|_*^2\le\frac{8d(q+1)}{N_{\rm shot}}.
\]

This is a valid but not competitive $O(d^2/N_{\rm shot})$ bound when $q\asymp d$; the linear inversion also uses $O(d^2)$ outcomes. It is included to make the acquisition interface concrete, not as an optimal estimator claim.

### Shot-noise counterexample to interpreting β as photon robustness

For coordinate pure states $x=e_r$, $y=e_s$, $r\ne s$, every character outcome has probability $1/[q(q+1)]$ under both states. The only differing probabilities are the two basis outputs $r,s$, each $1/(q+1)$. Therefore

\[
H^2(p_x,p_y):=\sum_j(\sqrt{p_j(x)}-\sqrt{p_j(y)})^2=\frac2{q+1},
\qquad \|xx^*-yy^*\|_*=2.
\]

For independent Poisson counts with mean flux τ and ideal detection, the exact Bhattacharyya coefficient is

\[
\mathrm{BC}(P_x,P_y)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac\tau2H^2(p_x,p_y)\right)
=e^{-\tau/(q+1)}.
\]

The total-variation inequality $\mathrm{TV}\le\sqrt{1-\mathrm{BC}^2}$ implies that any test with both errors at most α<1/2 needs

\[
\tau\ge\frac{q+1}{2}\log\frac1{4\alpha(1-\alpha)}
=\Omega\!\left(d\log\frac1\alpha\right).
\]

For fixed $N_{\rm shot}$, the multinomial affinity is $\bigl(1-1/(q+1)\bigr)^{N_{\rm shot}}$, producing the same linear-in-$d$ order. This is a concrete worst-direction loss despite exact β=2. The channel count $q^2+d$ and incident-copy count are separate costs. Detector inefficiency η multiplies incident flux by at least $1/\eta$; dark counts, saturation, and calibration error are unmodeled.

### Stronger Hellinger comparator

The lead’s cycle-2 report gives a whitened complex-Gaussian Parseval frame with $m=O(d)$ effects $b_jb_j^*$ summing to $I$, and hence also a one-photon POVM. Its outcome Hellinger metric is exactly $\||Bx|-|By|\|_2^2$. Xia–Xu–Xu’s Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 4.2 give a dimension-independent lower bound after whitening, with high probability once $m\gtrsim\log(1/\zeta)\zeta^{-2}d$. The sibling’s cycle-2 derivation then gives a global Hellinger minimum-distance estimator with expected squared phase-distance error $O(m/N_{\rm shot})=O(d/N_{\rm shot})$; exhaustive optimization is exponential in a net-size implementation, so no efficient reconstruction algorithm is established here. For orthogonal states, the Gaussian-Parseval Hellinger separation is bounded below by a constant, versus $2/(q+1)$ for the Bodmann–Haas POVM.

This is not an apples-to-apples comparison of β: the Gaussian theorem controls the square-root probability/magnitude metric, not exact optimality of the raw-intensity ℓ2 condition number. It is the more relevant comparator for Poisson photon cost. It exposes a real tradeoff: exact minimization of the deterministic intensity ratio can be inferior for counts and global statistical risk.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

- The source theorem is Peng–Han–Huang, *The Condition Number in Phase Retrieval from Intensity Measurements*, published in *Information and Inference* 15(2), June 2026, DOI 10.1093/imaiai/iaag019; the accessible primary manuscript is arXiv:2506.22053. It establishes the universal β lower bound and leaves general optimal sensing matrices open in its discussion. Its statement “for any matrix” has the $d=1$ caveat above.
- The exact equality with a weighted projective 2-design is an internal deduction from that proof plus polarization. Projective-design tensor identities and tight-IC interpretations predate it. Scott (2006), Theorems 4 and 12–13, gives the $d^2$ minimum/SIC equality case, the design/POVM equivalence, and a direct reconstruction identity. Jasper–Mixon (2025), Theorem 4 and Theorem 7, supplies the all-dimensional near-minimal design family. These facts make the all-$d$ β=2 existence corollary short once equality is characterized.
- A standard POVM from a weighted design uses effects $d\nu_j\Pi_j$. Its row fourth-power weights are proportional to $\nu_j^2$, not $\nu$, so it generally does not inherit β=2. The special square-root weights $E_j\propto\sqrt{\nu_j}\Pi_j$ work for the Bodmann–Haas layers because both layer projector sums are scalar. A finite countercheck reports a nonzero design-moment discrepancy for this naive POVM at $d=2,4,6,8,9,10$, but zero for the sampled prime dimensions where the normalized weights are uniform; see `verify_bh_phase_povm.py`.
- Direct phase-retrieval condition-number-to-design equivalence may be a useful reframing, but targeted primary-source review did not establish novelty/priority. The mathematical conversion alone does not support a paper-level novelty claim. The root audit should treat novelty as UNKNOWN until a broader search or expert review.

## Reproducibility and evidence scope

`verify_bh_phase_povm.py` checks the formulas for $d=2,\ldots,10$ using the parabola set and floating-point Fourier vectors. The corrected run has POVM-completeness errors $2.2\times10^{-16}$–$8.9\times10^{-16}$, design-moment errors $1.4\times10^{-17}$–$5.6\times10^{-17}$, exact-inverse errors $2.8\times10^{-16}$–$9.0\times10^{-16}$, and coordinate-pair $H^2$ discrepancies at or below $1.1\times10^{-16}$. These are finite algebraic diagnostics, not proof, physical validation, or novelty evidence.
